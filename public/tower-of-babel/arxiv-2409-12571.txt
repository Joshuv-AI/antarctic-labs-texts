Higher order Hardy-Rellich identities
Xia Huang, Dong Ye

Abstract
. In this paper, we show Hardy-Rellich identities for polyharmonic operators Δ m \Delta^{m} and radial Laplacian Δ r m \Delta_{r}^{m} in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} with Hardy-Hénon weight | x | α |x|^{\alpha} for all m , n ∈ ℕ , α ∈ ℝ m,n\in{\mathbb{N}},\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}} . Moreover, the iterative method is applied to give Hardy-Rellich equalities with general weights on Riemannian manifolds. These identities provide naturally an alternative approach to obtain and improve Hardy-Rellich type inequalities. As example of application, we extend several Rellich inequalities of Tertikas-Zographopoulos [ 36 ] to the weighted case; using equality with weights involving logarithmic, we show another new weighted Rellich estimate between integrals of Δ ​ u \Delta u and | ∇ u | |\nabla u| ; we establish also a Rellich identity involving the Laplace-Beltrami operator Δ ℍ \Delta_{\mathbb{H}} and the radial Laplacian Δ ρ , ℍ \Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}} of the hyperbolic space ℍ n {\mathbb{H}}^{n} , which yields in particular brand-new Rellich inequalities for ‖ Δ ℍ ​ u ‖ \|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\| in ℍ 3 {\mathbb{H}}^{3} and ℍ 4 {\mathbb{H}}^{4} .

26D10, 26D15, 35A23

higher order Hardy-Rellich identities, polyharmonic operators, iteration

Introduction

The first order Hardy inequalities go back to G.H. Hardy, who showed in
[
22
]
a very famous estimate. Let
p>1
, then

\int_{0}^{\infty}|u^{\prime}(x)|^{p}dx\geq\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{x^{p}}dx,\quad\forall~u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),~u(0)=0.

Since one century, Hardy type inequalities have been enriched extensively and broadly, they play an important roles in many branches of analysis and geometry. A huge literature exists, we refer to the classical or more recent books
[
1
,
20
,
29
,
31
,
33
]
for interested readers.

Now we have various methods to provide Hardy type inequalities. One way is to establish
equalities
with nonnegative remainder terms which yield immediately the corresponding inequalities. Look at the most famous weighted Hardy inequality:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{\alpha}}dx\geq\frac{(n-2-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{\alpha+2}}dx,\quad\forall\;\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}},\;u\in C^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).

Indeed, (
1.1
) is a direct consequence of the following identity: For
n\geq 1
,
u\in C^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there holds

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{(n-2-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\|u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}=\left\|\nabla\left(r^{\frac{n-2-\alpha}{2}}u\right)\right\|_{n-2}^{2}.

Here
r=|x|
stands for the Euclidean norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\beta}^{2}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|^{2}}{r^{\beta}}dx,\quad\forall\;g\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),\;\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The equality (
1.2
) can be found for example in
[
11
]
with
\alpha=0
; or
[
36
, Lemma 2.3(i)]
with
\alpha=2
. On the other hand, we can also use radial derivative to derive (
1.1
), since

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\left(\frac{n-2-\alpha}{2}\right)^{2}\|u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}=\left\|T_{\alpha}(u)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),\;\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}},

where

(1.5)

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}(f):=\partial_{r}f+\frac{n-2-\alpha}{2r}f\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{r}=\frac{x}{r}\cdot\nabla=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}}{r}\partial_{x_{i}}.

Both (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) can be proved easily using integration by parts (see Remark
2.3
below). The formula (
1.4
) was found firstly by Brezis-Vázquez with
\alpha=0
, they called it the
magical computation
, see
[
8
, page 454]
.

In 2011, Ghoussoub-Moradifam introduced the powerful idea of Bessel pairs in
[
19
]
(see also
[
31
]
) to establish Hardy type inequalities

\int_{\Omega}V|\nabla u|^{2}dx\geq\int_{\Omega}Wu^{2}dx,

with more general
radial
weights
V
and
W
. Their technique reaches a large success, and has been applied in many works to prove and improve first order Hardy inequalities, to name a few, see for instance
[
5
,
10
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
and references therein.

Later on, we attempt a more general approach in
[
23
]
, where the departure point is an elementary equality as follows. Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a Riemannian manifold. Consider
V\in C^{1}({\mathcal{M}})
and
\vec{F}\in C^{1}({\mathcal{M}},T_{g}\mathcal{M})
, then for any
u\in C^{1}_{c}({\mathcal{M}})
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}V\|\nabla_{g}u\|^{2}dg=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\big[{\rm div}_{g}(V\vec{F})-V\|\vec{F}\|^{2}\big]u^{2}dg+\int_{\mathcal{M}}V\|\nabla_{g}u+u\vec{F}\|^{2}dg.

In particular, let
\vec{F}=-\frac{\nabla_{g}f}{f}
with
f
positive in
C^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
, we see that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}V\|\nabla_{g}u\|^{2}dg=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{{\rm div}_{g}(V\nabla_{g}f)}{f}u^{2}dg+\int_{\mathcal{M}}V\Big\|\nabla_{g}u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla_{g}f\Big\|^{2}dg.

We deduce then, for
V\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}V\|\nabla_{g}u\|^{2}dg\geq-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{{\rm div}_{g}(V\nabla_{g}f)}{f}u^{2}dg,\quad\forall\;u\in C^{1}_{c}({\mathcal{M}}).

Hence, by choosing suitable auxiliary function
f
, we can refind, extend or improve most of first order Hardy inequalities by the identity (
1.6
), see
[
23
]
for many examples of application.

In particular, when
({\mathcal{M}},g)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
V
and
f
are positive and radial, we find the radial Bessel pair of Ghoussoub-Moradifam with
V
and

W=-\frac{{\rm div}(V\nabla f)}{f}=-\frac{V}{f}\Big[f^{\prime\prime}+\Big(\frac{n-1}{r}+\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}\Big)f^{\prime}\Big].

It is also worthy to mention that Kajántó-Kristály-Peter-Zhao use recently the Riccati pairs to extend the Bessel pairs, and provide an alternative approach to get Hardy inequalities on complete, non-compact Riemannian manifolds, see
[
25
]
.

The well-known second order Hardy-Rellich inequality in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the following (see
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
): Let
n\geq 2
,
|\alpha+2|\leq 2\sqrt{n^{2}-2n+2}
,
n\geq 2
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{\alpha}}dx\geq\left[\frac{(n+\alpha)(n-4-\alpha)}{4}\right]^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{4+\alpha}}dx,\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).

The inequality (
1.7
) with
\alpha=0
,
n\geq 5
was first given by Rellich at the ICM 1954
[
32
]
, that’s the reason that Hardy type estimates with higher order differential operators are named often Hardy-Rellich inequalities. In 2017, Machihara-Ozawa-Wadade reproved (
1.7
) with
\alpha=0
elegantly using two identities, see
[
28
, Theorems 1.1-1.2]
. The idea in
[
28
]
works indeed for general weights
|x|^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
(see section 3 and also
[
34
, Theorem 4.1]
for (
1.10
)). The first equality shows the relationship between
\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}
and
\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}
,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}&=\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{(n+\alpha)(n-4-\alpha)}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{\alpha}\left(L_{j}u\right)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}.\end{split}

Here

\Delta_{r}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{n-1}{r}\partial_{r},

is the radial Laplacian in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and

(1.9)

\displaystyle L_{j}:=\partial_{x_{j}}-\frac{x_{j}}{r}\partial_{r},\;\;1\leq j\leq n.

The second equality is

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}=&\;\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{16}\left\|u\right\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}\\
&+\left[\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}+\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\right]\left\|T_{\alpha+2}(u)\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\left\|T_{\alpha}\circ T_{\alpha+2}(u)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}.\end{split}

The paper of Machihara-Ozawa-Wadade is the first work which provides second order Rellich type equalities, their idea is generalized for various situations such as for more general
radial
weights in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, in half space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
, homogeneous groups or hyperbolic spaces, see
[
5
,
7
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
and references therein.

Another interesting technique using identity to get Rellich inequalities is the factorization method (see Gesztesy-Littlejohn
[
18
]
), where they decompose cleverly the positive operator
T_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}T_{\alpha,\beta}
for
T_{\alpha,\beta}=-\Delta+\alpha r^{-2}x\cdot\nabla+\beta r^{-2}
with
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
. But this approach seems to be difficultly adaptable for higher order situations.

An interesting question arises: Can we find Hardy-Rellich equalities for general higher order operators?

The first notable example is due to Ruzhansky-Surugan
[
34
, (6.2) and Theorem 6.1]
. They considered homogeneous Carnot group
{\mathbb{G}}
and established a remarkable equality for
\|{\mathcal{R}}^{k}\|_{\alpha}^{2}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Here
{\mathcal{R}}=\frac{d}{d\|x\|}
is the radial operator (with respect to a quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|
over
{\mathbb{G}}
), a natural analogue to
\partial_{r}
in Euclidean spaces, and
{\mathcal{R}}^{k}
means the
k
-times composition of
{\mathcal{R}}
.

However, to our best knowledge, there is no general Hardy-Rellich type identities for polyharmonic operators
\Delta^{m}
in the literature. Our first objective here is to show that such equalities exist.

Let us begin with the radial polyharmonic Laplacian
\Delta_{r}^{m}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. To write properly higher order Hardy-Rellich identities, we denote

(1.11)

\displaystyle R_{\alpha,k}:=T_{\alpha}\circ T_{\alpha+2}\circ\cdot\cdot\cdot\circ T_{\alpha+2k},\quad\forall\;\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}},\;k\in{\mathbb{N}},

and

(1.12)

\displaystyle A_{\beta}=\frac{(n+\beta)(n-4-\beta)}{4},\;\;D_{\beta}=\frac{(n+\beta)^{2}+(n-4-\beta)^{2}}{4},\quad\forall~\beta\in\mathbb{R}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m,n\geq 1
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
u\in C_{c}^{2m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, there holds

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta_{r}^{m}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}A_{\alpha+4\ell}^{2}\times\|u\|_{\alpha+4m}^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}C_{j,m,\alpha}\left\|R_{\alpha+2j,2m-1-j}(u)\right\|_{\alpha+2j}^{2},\end{split}

where the positive coefficients
C_{j,m,\alpha}

(0\leq j\leq 2m-1)
are given by the iterative formula

(1.14)

\displaystyle C_{j,k+1,\alpha}=A_{\alpha+4k}^{2}C_{j-2,k,\alpha}+D_{\alpha+4k}C_{j-1,k,\alpha}+C_{j,k,\alpha},\quad\forall\;0\leq j\leq 2k+2,k\geq 0,

with the convention
C_{0,0,\alpha}=1
, and
C_{j,k,\alpha}=0
for
j<0
or
j>2k
.

As all coefficients
C_{j,m,\alpha}
in (
1.13
) are positive, we get obviously the following Hardy-Rellich inequality with optimal coefficient, and see that the equality holds only if
u\equiv 0
. Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{r}^{m}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}A_{\alpha+4\ell}^{2}\|u\|_{\alpha+4m}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).

Moreover, seeing (
1.13
) and definition of
R_{\alpha,k}
, the remainder terms can be seen coming from lower order Hardy-Rellich inequalities. For example, look at the second order case
m=1
, we can rewrite (
1.10
) as

\displaystyle\quad\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-A_{\alpha}^{2}\|u\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}

\displaystyle=D_{\alpha}\|T_{\alpha+2}(u)\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|T_{\alpha}\circ T_{\alpha+2}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=D_{\alpha}\left[\|\partial_{r}u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}-\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\|u\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}\right]+\left[\|\partial_{r}(T_{\alpha+2}u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{(n-2-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\|T_{\alpha+2}u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}\right],

where both above bracket are remainder terms of Hardy inequality, given by (
1.4
).

On the other hand, we see that

\displaystyle C_{0,m+1,\alpha}=1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;C_{2m,m,\alpha}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}A_{\alpha+4\ell}^{2},\quad\forall\;m\geq 0.

So (
1.13
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{r}^{m}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}=C_{2m,m,\alpha}\|u\|_{\alpha+4m}^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}C_{j,m,\alpha}\left\|R_{\alpha+2j,2m-1-j}(u)\right\|_{\alpha+2j}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2m}({\mathbb{R}}^{2m}\backslash\{0\}).

Remark 1.2
.

Directly or using density argument, all involved equalities or inequalities in this paper could hold true for more general functions. But for simplicity, we will always work with smooth functions compactly supported out of eventual singularities of the weights.

Similar identities for polyharmonic operators
\Delta^{m}
can be established, although a little bit more involved.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
u\in C_{c}^{2m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, there holds

(1.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\|\Delta^{m}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-H_{m-1,\alpha}\|u\|_{\alpha+4m}^{2}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{m}H_{k-2,\alpha}\Big[D_{\alpha+4k-4}\left\|T_{\alpha+4k-2}(\Delta^{m-k}f)\right\|_{\alpha+4k-2}^{2}+\left\|R_{\alpha+4k,1}(\Delta^{m-k}u)\right\|_{\alpha+4k}^{2}\Big]\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{m}H_{k-2,\alpha}\Big[\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})(\Delta^{m-k}u)\right\|_{\alpha+4k}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{\alpha+4k}(L_{j}(\Delta^{m-k}u)\right\|_{\alpha+4k-2}^{2}\Big]\\
&\quad+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{\alpha+4k-4}H_{k-2,\alpha}\Big)\left\|L_{j}(\Delta^{m-k}u)\right\|_{\alpha+4k-2}^{2},\end{split}

where
H_{k,\alpha}=C_{2(k+1),k+1,\alpha}
for
k\geq-1
, i.e.
H_{-1,\alpha}=1
and
H_{k,\alpha}=\prod_{0\leq\ell\leq k}A_{\alpha+4\ell}^{2}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Now we want to generalize (
1.6
) to higher order cases, in other words, to establish Hardy-Rellich identities for polyharmonic operators on manifolds and general weights. Let
({\mathcal{M}},g)
be a Riemannian manifold, denote

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{2m}=\Delta_{g}^{m},\;\;{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}=\nabla_{g}\Delta_{g}^{m},\quad\forall\;m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Therefore the corresponding adjoint operators are respectively
{\mathcal{D}}^{*}_{2m}={\mathcal{D}}_{2m}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}^{*}=-\Delta_{g}^{m}({\rm div}_{g})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k\geq 1
,
V\in C^{k}({\mathcal{M}})
. Assume that
f\in C^{k}({\mathcal{M}})
satisfies
|{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f|>0
in
{\mathcal{M}}
for all
\ell\geq 0
,
2\ell<k
. Then for any
u\in C_{c}^{k}({\mathcal{M}})
, we have

(1.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\|{\mathcal{D}}_{k}u\|^{2}dg-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{k}^{*}(V{\mathcal{D}}_{k}f)}{f}u^{2}dg\\
&=-2\sum_{\ell\geq 0,2\ell\leq k-2}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{k-2-2\ell}^{*}(V{\mathcal{D}}_{k}f)}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f}\Big\|{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell+1}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell+1}f\Big\|^{2}dg\\
&\quad+\sum_{\ell\geq 1,2\ell\leq k-1}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{k-2\ell}^{*}(V{\mathcal{D}}_{k}f)}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f}\Big|{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell-2}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f\Big|^{2}dg\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathcal{M}}V\Big\|{\mathcal{D}}_{k}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2[(k-1)/2]}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2[(k-1)/2]}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}f\Big\|^{2}dg,\end{split}

where
[\beta]
denotes the integer part of
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

A direct consequence is the following abstract Hardy-Rellich inequality.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
f\in C^{k}({\mathcal{M}})
satisfy
(-\Delta_{g})^{\ell}f>0
for
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
, then

(1.17)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\|{\mathcal{D}}_{k}u\|^{2}dx\geq\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{(-\Delta_{g})^{k}f}{f}u^{2}dx,\quad\forall~u\in C_{c}^{k}({\mathcal{M}}).

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
u\equiv 0
.

Seeing (
1.17
), if
f
presents suitable behavior (at infinity or/and near
\partial{\mathcal{M}}
), we can expect to use cut-off function approximating
f
and show eventually the optimality of some involved coefficients. For a very simple example, let
{\mathcal{M}}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
and
f=|x|^{\frac{2m-n}{2}}
with
n>4m
, we see that
(-\Delta)^{\ell}f>0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
for all
0\leq\ell\leq 2m-1
. Hence by (
1.17
), we see the classical Hardy-Rellich inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta^{m}u|^{2}dx\geq H_{m,0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2m}}dx,\quad\forall~u\in C_{c}^{2m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{\{0}\}).

Of course, the above inequality is also a consequence of (
1.15
), however with different expressions of (nonnegative) remainder terms. This means somehow the complexity and rich possibility of Rellich type inequalities, we need to pay attention to various remainder terms, if we aim to get different or improved estimates.

As application, although we are convinced that much more results can be derived, we just expose several interesting consequences with second order differential operators. We will see that the Hardy-Rellich identities provide naturally new estimates, even with the Laplacian in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. For example, the iterative approach to obtain Hardy-Rellich identities (
1.13
) and (
1.15
) works also for weights involving logarithmic (see Proposition
5.4
below), which yields

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
u\in C_{c}^{2}(B_{1}\backslash\{0\})
where
B_{1}
is the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
If
|3\alpha+4+n|\leq 2\sqrt{n^{2}-n+1}
, there holds

(1.18)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}+\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{16}\|(\ln|x|)^{-1}u\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}+\frac{9}{16}\|(\ln|x|)^{-2}u\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}.

Notice that by the study of Beckner
[
2
, Theorem 4]
and Tertikas-Zographopoulos
[
36
, Theorem1.7]
(see also the comments in
[
21
, page 83]
), the above constraint on
\alpha
for (
1.18
) is the sharp range to claim

(1.19)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2}(B_{1}\backslash\{0\}).

As example on manifold, we consider the hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, where the Rellich type inequalities have been studied intensively, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
30
,
26
,
27
]
and references therein. In the spirit of Machihara-Ozawa-Wadade’s formula for
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we show a Rellich type identity on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, which enable us quickly new Rellich inequalities.

More precisely, let
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be Poincaré’s hyperbolic ball and
\rho=\ln\frac{1+r}{1-r}
be the geodesic distance from origin to
x\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Define
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}
to be the sphere derivatives in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
,

(1.20)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{j}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{2}\partial_{x_{j}}-\frac{x_{j}}{r}\partial_{\rho}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{2}L_{j}

where
L_{j}
is that given in (
1.9
). We denote by
\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}
the radial Laplacian in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, namely

(1.21)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+(n-1)\coth\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}.

Theorem 1.7
.

For
n\geq 2
and any
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, there holds

(1.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}&=\|\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u\|^{2}+\|(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}-\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}})u\|^{2}\\
&\quad+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j}u)\|^{2}-2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}-2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}.\end{split}

Here
\|\cdot\|
means the norm in
L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
.
Moreover, we have

(1.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}&\geq\|\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{(n+1)(n-3)}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|{\rm coth}\rho{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}\end{split}

Consequently
\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|\geq\|\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u\|
for any
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
with
n\geq 3
.

Notice that the comparison result
\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|\geq\|\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u\|
in
C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
was proved for
n\geq 4
by
[
30
, Theorem 5.2]
and
[
6
, Lemma 6.1]
, but was unknown for
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
. As application of (
1.22
)–(
1.23
), we provide new Rellich type estimates in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
and
{\mathbb{H}}^{4}
. As far as we are aware, all Rellich type inequalities with
\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|
in the literature were established only for
n\geq 5
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, there holds

(1.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}&\geq\frac{(n-1)^{4}}{16}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{8}\Big\|\frac{u}{\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{9}{16}\Big\|\frac{u}{\rho^{2}}\Big\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{(n^{2}-1)(n-3)(n-5)}{16}\Big\|\frac{u}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}\Big\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{(n^{2}-1)(n-3)^{2}}{8}\Big\|\frac{u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{8}\Big\|\frac{u}{\rho{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}.\end{split}

In particular,

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}\geq\|u\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Big\|\frac{u}{\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{9}{16}\Big\|\frac{u}{\rho^{2}}\Big\|^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{3}\backslash\{0\}),

and

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}\geq\frac{81}{16}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{9}{8}\Big\|\frac{u}{\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{15}{8}\Big\|\frac{u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{4}\backslash\{0\}).

The estimate (
1.24
) improves slightly Theorem 3.1 in
[
3
]
for
n\geq 5
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\beta}(f)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\left\|T_{\alpha}(f)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\frac{(\beta-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2},

(2.2)

\displaystyle T_{\beta}(r^{\sigma}f)=r^{\sigma}T_{\beta-2\sigma}(f),\quad\left\|T_{\beta}(r^{\sigma}f)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\left\|T_{\beta-2\sigma}(f)\right\|_{\alpha-2\sigma}^{2},

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle T_{\alpha}(\partial_{r}f),~T_{\alpha+2}(f)\rangle_{\alpha+1}=\frac{4+\alpha-n}{2}\|T_{\alpha+2}(f)\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}.\end{split}

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\beta}
denotes the inner product associated to
\|\cdot\|_{\beta}
, that is

\langle f,g\rangle_{\beta}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{f(x)g(x)}{|x|^{\beta}}dx.

Proof
. The departure point is the following simple fact: For any
\eta\in C^{1}_{c}(0,\infty)
,
M,\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there holds

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\Big|\eta^{\prime}+\frac{M\eta}{s}\Big|^{2}s^{\gamma}ds=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}|\eta^{\prime}|^{2}s^{\gamma}ds-M(\gamma-1-M)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}|\eta|^{2}s^{\gamma-2}ds.

A direct consequence is that for all
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
M^{\prime},M\in{\mathbb{R}}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\Big|\eta^{\prime}+\frac{M^{\prime}\eta}{s}\Big|^{2}s^{\gamma}ds-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\Big|\eta^{\prime}+\frac{M\eta}{s}\Big|^{2}s^{\gamma}ds\\
&=(M^{\prime}-M)(M+M^{\prime}-\gamma+1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}|\eta|^{2}s^{\gamma-2}ds.\end{split}

Let
f\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
. As

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\ell}(f)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\Big|\partial_{r}f+\frac{n-2-\ell}{2r}f\Big|^{2}r^{n-1-\alpha}drd\sigma,

taking
M=\frac{n-2-\beta}{2}
,
M^{\prime}=\frac{n-2-\alpha}{2}
and
\gamma=n-1-\alpha
in (
2.5
), we obtain readily (
2.1
). We get (
2.2
) by the definition (
1.5
). Moreover, there hold

(2.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\big(T_{\alpha+2}(f)\big)=T_{\alpha}(\partial_{r}f)-\frac{1}{r}T_{\alpha+2}(f),\quad\langle\partial_{r}f,g\rangle_{\alpha}=-\langle f,\partial_{r}g\rangle_{\alpha}-(n-1-\alpha)\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha+1}.

(
2.3
) can be derived directly. ∎

A direct application of (
2.1
) is

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\left\|T_{-n}(\partial_{r}u)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\partial_{r}u\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\left\|T_{\alpha}(\partial_{r}u)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2},

which yields another classical Hardy-Rellich inequality with best constant.

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\partial_{r}u\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),\;\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Lemma 2.2

Let

be given in (

\displaystyle(i)\;L_{j}(r)=0;\quad(ii)\;L_{j}\partial_{r}=\Big(\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\Big)L_{j};\quad(iii)\;\Delta=\Delta_{r}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}^{2};\quad(iv)\;\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}L_{j}=0.

(2.12)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

and

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|R_{\alpha,k}(\Delta_{r}f)\|_{\alpha}^{2}=A_{\alpha+2k+2}^{2}\|R_{\alpha+4,k}f\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}+D_{\alpha+2k+2}\|R_{\alpha+2,k+1}f\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,k+2}f\|_{\alpha}^{2},

where the coefficients

and

are given in (
1.12
).

Proof
. By (

), we have

\displaystyle R_{\alpha,k}(\Delta_{r}f)=R_{\alpha,k}\left(r^{1-n}\partial_{r}(r^{n-1}\partial_{r}f)\right)=r^{1-n}R_{\alpha+2n-2,k}\left(\partial_{r}(r^{n-1}\partial_{r}f)\right).

Thanks to (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle\quad\|R_{\alpha,k}(\Delta_{r}f)\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|R_{\alpha,k}\left(r^{1-n}\partial_{r}(r^{n-1}\partial_{r}f)\right)\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|R_{\alpha+2n-2,k}\left(\partial_{r}(r^{n-1}\partial_{r}f)\right)\|_{\alpha+2n-2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+2k+\alpha+2)^{2}}{4}\|R_{\alpha+2n,k}\left(r^{n-1}\partial_{r}f\right)\|_{\alpha+2n}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha+2n-2,k+1}\left(r^{n-1}\partial_{r}f\right)\|_{\alpha+2n-2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+2k+\alpha+2)^{2}}{4}\|R_{\alpha+2,k}(\partial_{r}f)\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,k+1}(\partial_{r}f)\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+2k+\alpha+2)^{2}}{4}\left[\frac{(n-2k-\alpha-6)^{2}}{4}\|R_{\alpha+4,k}f\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha+2,k+1}f\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(n-2k-\alpha-6)^{2}}{4}\|R_{\alpha+2,k+1}f\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,k+2}f\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=A_{\alpha+2k+2}^{2}\|R_{\alpha+4,k}f\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}+D_{\alpha+2k+2}\|R_{\alpha+2,k+1}f\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,k+2}f\|_{\alpha}^{2}.

The proof is completed. ∎

, there holds

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{m}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}A_{\alpha+4\ell}^{2}\times\|u\|_{\alpha+4m}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).

Consequently, for
n>2m
,
-n<\alpha+2<n-4m
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Delta^{m}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\|\partial_{r}\Delta^{m}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\frac{(n-2-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}A_{\alpha+4\ell+2}^{2}\times\|u\|_{\alpha+4m+2}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2m+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).

Clearly, positive remainder terms can be added to the above inequalities by equalities (
1.15
) and (
1.2
). We may also use (
1.16
) to get diverse formulae or relax the assumption on
\alpha
using (
5.2
) below.

4.
Higher order Hardy-Rellich identities with general weights

This section is devoted to Theorem
1.4
. To be more transparent, we consider first the even order cases. When
k=2m
, let us rewrite (
1.16
) as follows.

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}^{m}u|^{2}dg-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m}(V\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{f}u^{2}dg\\
&=-2\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m-1-\ell}(V\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\Big\|\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u)-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f)\Big\|^{2}dg\\
&\quad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m-\ell}(V\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\Big|\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{\ell-1}u}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell-1}f}\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f\Big|^{2}dg.\end{split}

Observe that the last term of (
1.16
) is inserted in the previous line with
\ell=m
.

We use the induction to show (
4.1
). For any
\phi\in C^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
,
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}u+\phi u|^{2}dg

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\Big[|\Delta_{g}u|^{2}+\phi^{2}u^{2}+2\phi u\Delta_{g}u\Big]dg

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}u|^{2}dg+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\phi^{2}u^{2}dg-2\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\phi|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}dg+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\Delta_{g}(V\phi)u^{2}dg

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}u|^{2}dg+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\phi^{2}u^{2}dg+2\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{{\rm div_{g}}(V\phi\nabla_{g}f)}{f}u^{2}dg

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\Delta_{g}(V\phi)u^{2}dg-2\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\phi\Big\|\nabla_{g}u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla_{g}f\Big\|^{2}dg.

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}u|^{2}dg

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\Big[-2\frac{{\rm div_{g}}(V\phi\nabla_{g}f)}{f}-V\phi^{2}-\Delta_{g}(V\phi)\Big]u^{2}dg

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\phi\Big\|\nabla_{g}u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla_{g}f\Big\|^{2}dg+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}u+\phi u|^{2}dg.

Set
\phi:=-\frac{\Delta_{g}f}{f}
with
f\in C^{2}
, then

\displaystyle 2\frac{{\rm div_{g}}(V\phi\nabla_{g}f)}{f}+V\phi^{2}+\Delta_{g}(V\phi)

\displaystyle=2\frac{\nabla_{g}(V\phi)\cdot\nabla_{g}f}{f}+V\phi\frac{\Delta_{g}f}{f}+\Delta_{g}(V\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{g}(V\phi f)}{f}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta_{g}(V\Delta_{g}f)}{f}.

Consequently

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}u|^{2}dg&=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}(V\Delta_{g}f)}{f}u^{2}dg-2\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\frac{\Delta_{g}f}{f}\Big\|\nabla_{g}u-\frac{u}{f}\nabla_{g}f\Big\|^{2}dg\\
&\quad+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\Big|\Delta_{g}u-\frac{\Delta_{g}f}{f}u\Big|^{2}dg,\end{split}

i.e. we get (
4.1
) for
m=1
. Assume that (
4.1
) holds for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let us consider the order
(m+1)
.
Using (
4.2
) with
f=\psi
and
\Delta_{g}^{m}u
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}^{m+1}u|^{2}dg-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}(V\Delta_{g}\psi)}{\psi}|\Delta_{g}^{m}u|^{2}dg\\
&=-2\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\frac{\Delta_{g}\psi}{\psi}\Big\|\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{m}u)-\Delta_{g}^{m}u\frac{\nabla_{g}\psi}{\psi}\Big\|^{2}dg+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\Big|\Delta_{g}^{m+1}u-\frac{\Delta_{g}\psi}{\psi}\Delta_{g}^{m}u\Big|^{2}dg.\end{split}

Moreover, applying (
4.1
) with
m
and
V_{m}=\frac{\Delta_{g}(V\Delta_{g}\psi)}{\psi}
for the second integral in (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}^{m+1}u|^{2}dg-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m}(V_{m}\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{f}u^{2}dg

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m-1-\ell}(V_{m}\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\Big\|\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u)-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f)\Big\|^{2}dg

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m-\ell}(V_{m}\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\Big|\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{\ell-1}u}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell-1}f}\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f\Big|^{2}dg

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\frac{\Delta_{g}\psi}{\psi}\Big\|\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{m}u)-\Delta_{g}^{m}u\frac{\nabla_{g}\psi}{\psi}\Big\|^{2}dg+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\Big|\Delta_{g}^{m+1}u-\frac{\Delta_{g}\psi}{\psi}\Delta_{g}^{m}u\Big|^{2}dg.

Let now
\psi=\Delta_{g}^{m}f
so that
V_{m}\Delta_{g}^{m}f=\Delta_{g}(V\Delta_{g}^{m+1}f)
and
\frac{\Delta_{g}\psi}{\psi}=\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m+1}f}{\Delta_{g}^{m}f}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\quad\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V|\Delta_{g}^{m+1}u|^{2}dg-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m+1}(V\Delta_{g}^{m+1}f)}{f}u^{2}dg

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m-\ell}(V\Delta_{g}^{m+1}f)}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\Big\|\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u)-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f)\Big\|^{2}dg

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+1}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m+1-\ell}(V\Delta_{g}^{m+1}f)}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\Big|\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{\ell-1}u}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell-1}f}\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f\Big|^{2}dg.

This means that (
4.1
) holds true for any
m\geq 1
.

Next we consider odd order cases of (
1.16
), i.e.
k=2m+1
. When
k=1
, (
1.16
) is just the identity (
1.6
). Consider
k=2m+1
for general
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Using (
1.6
) with
\Delta_{g}^{m}u
and
f=\psi
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\|\nabla_{g}\big(\Delta_{g}^{m}u\big)\|^{2}dg=-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{{\rm div_{g}}(V\nabla_{g}\psi)}{\psi}|\Delta_{g}^{m}u|^{2}dg+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\Big\|\nabla_{g}\big(\Delta_{g}^{m}u\big)-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m}u}{\psi}\nabla_{g}\psi\Big\|^{2}dg.

Applying (
4.1
) with the weight
V_{m}:=-\frac{{\rm div_{g}}(V\nabla_{g}\psi)}{\psi}
, there holds

\displaystyle\quad\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\|{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}u\|^{2}dg-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m}(V_{m}\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{f}u^{2}dg

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m-1-\ell}(V_{m}\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\Big\|\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u)-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\nabla_{g}(\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f)\Big\|^{2}dg

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m-\ell}(V_{m}\Delta_{g}^{m}f)}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f}\Big|\Delta_{g}^{\ell}u-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{\ell-1}u}{\Delta_{g}^{\ell-1}f}\Delta_{g}^{\ell}f\Big|^{2}dg+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\Big\|\nabla_{g}\big(\Delta_{g}^{m}u\big)-\frac{\Delta_{g}^{m}u}{\psi}\nabla_{g}\psi\Big\|^{2}dg.

Choose
\psi=\Delta_{g}^{m}f={\mathcal{D}}_{2m}f
so that
\Delta_{g}^{j}(V_{m}\Delta_{g}^{m}f)={\mathcal{D}}^{*}_{2j+1}(V{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}f)
and
\nabla_{g}\psi={\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}f
. We deduce then

\displaystyle\quad\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\|{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}u\|^{2}dg-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}_{2m+1}(V{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}f)}{f}u^{2}dg

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}_{2m-1-2\ell}(V{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}f)}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f}\Big\|{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell+1}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell+1}f\Big\|^{2}dg

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}_{2m+1-2\ell}(V{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}f)}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f}\Big|{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell-2}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f\Big|^{2}dg

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}V\Big\|{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2m}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2m}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{2m+1}f\Big\|^{2}dg.

This is just the formula (
1.16
) with
k=2m+1
. So we are done. ∎

Remark that
{\mathcal{D}}_{k-2m}^{*}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{k}=(-1)^{k}\Delta_{g}^{k-m}
for any
k,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
2m<k
. Let
V\equiv 1
in (
1.16
), we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\|{\mathcal{D}}_{k}u\|^{2}dg-\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{(-\Delta_{g})^{k}f}{f}u^{2}dg\\
&=2\sum_{\ell\geq 0,2\ell\leq k-2}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{(-\Delta_{g})^{k-1-\ell}f}{(-\Delta_{g})^{\ell}f}\Big\|{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell+1}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell+1}f\Big\|^{2}dg\\
&\quad+\sum_{\ell\geq 1,2\ell\leq k-1}\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{(-\Delta_{g})^{k-\ell}f}{(-\Delta_{g})^{\ell}f}\Big|{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell-2}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{2\ell}f\Big|^{2}dg\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathcal{M}}\Big\|{\mathcal{D}}_{k}u-\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{2[(k-1)/2]}u}{{\mathcal{D}}_{2[(k-1)/2]}f}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}f\Big\|^{2}dg.\end{split}

Corollary
1.5
is easily deduced by (
4.4
). We can also apply (
1.16
) with
V=|x|^{-\alpha}
and
f=|x|^{\frac{\alpha+2m-n}{2}}
to refind (
3.7
) or (
3.8
), we leave the detail for interested readers.

5.
Some applications

Here we show several applications where we apply identities to prove some old and new Hardy-Rellich inequalities. As already mentioned, we focus on the second order cases which provide already convincing examples.

5.1.
Between Laplacian and radial Laplacian

For general
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
m\geq 1
, we cannot always compare easily the weighted norms
\|\Delta^{m}u\|_{\alpha}
and
\|\Delta_{r}^{m}u\|_{\alpha}
. Take
n\geq 2
,
m=1
and
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, by (
1.8
),

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=2A_{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{\alpha}\left(L_{j}u\right)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}.

When
A_{\alpha}
is nonnegative, i.e.
-n\leq\alpha\leq n-4
, we have obviously
\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}
. The range of
\alpha
to ensure
\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}\geq\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}
can be extended by the following simple fact.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
f\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})f\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq(n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|L_{j}f\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}.

Proof.
Let
-\Delta_{\sigma}
be the spherical Laplacian over the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
h(\sigma)=f(r\sigma)
be defined on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
with
r>0
, then

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})f|^{2}(r\sigma)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|\Delta_{\sigma}h|^{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-1}{r^{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{\sigma}h|^{2}d\sigma=\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|L_{j}f|^{2}(r\sigma)d\sigma,

because
(n-1)
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{\sigma}
in
\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
, the subspace of functions in
H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
having null average. ∎

Using (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we see that

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq(2A_{\alpha}+n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}.

Hence
\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\|\Delta_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}
if
|\alpha+2|\leq\sqrt{n^{2}-2n+2}
.

5.2.
Around some optimal Rellich inequalities

For similar reason as above, the Rellich inequality

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}

does not hold in
C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, contrary to the radial derivative case (see (
2.9
)). As already mentioned, Beckner and Tertikas-Zographopoulos
[
2
,
36
]
showed that (
5.3
) holds true for all
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})

if and only if

|3\alpha+n+4|\leq 2\sqrt{n^{2}-n+1},

More precisely, the best constants

E_{n,\alpha}=\inf_{u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),u\neq 0}\frac{\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}}{\|\nabla u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}}

are known for general
\alpha
and
n
, see
[
2
,
36
]
. In particular,
E_{n,0}=\frac{n^{2}}{4}
for
n\geq 5
,
E_{4,0}=3
and
E_{3,0}=\frac{25}{36}
,
E_{2,0}=0
. Here we just explain how to find quickly the optimal lower bound for
E_{n,0}
using Hardy-Rellich identities.

Indeed, combining equalities (
1.8
), (
2.8
) and estimate (
5.2
) with
\alpha=0
,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\|\Delta u\|^{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{4}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}\\
&=\|T_{0}(\partial_{r}u)\|^{2}+\Big[\frac{n(n-4)}{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{4}\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|^{2}_{2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\right\|^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T_{0}(L_{j}u)\|^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{n^{2}-4n-4}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|^{2}_{2}.\end{split}

Hence
E_{n,0}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4}
if
n\geq 5
and
E_{4,0}\geq 3
. To handle the
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
case, we decompose
u={\overline{u}}+u_{\sigma}
where
\overline{u}
is the spherical average function of
u
. So
{\overline{u}}
is radial symmetric,

\|\Delta{\overline{u}}\|^{2}=\|\Delta_{r}{\overline{u}}\|^{2}\geq\frac{9}{4}\|\partial_{r}{\overline{u}}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{9}{4}\|\nabla{\overline{u}}\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\backslash\{0\}).

On the other hand, using (
5.1
) and (
2.9
) with
u_{\sigma}
,
n=3
and
\alpha=0
, there holds

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta u_{\sigma}\|^{2}-C\|\nabla u_{\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}&=\|\Delta_{r}u_{\sigma}\|^{2}-C\|\partial_{r}u_{\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}-\Big(\frac{3}{2}+C\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left\|L_{j}u_{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\quad+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u_{\sigma}\right\|^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left\|T_{0}(L_{j}u_{\sigma})\right\|^{2}\\
&\geq\Big(1-\frac{4C}{9}\Big)\|\Delta_{r}u_{\sigma}\|^{2}-\Big(\frac{3}{2}+C\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left\|L_{j}u_{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u_{\sigma}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

Let
\{\mu_{k}\}
be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues for
-\Delta_{\sigma}=\Delta_{r}-\Delta
in
\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
, we know that
\mu_{1}=2
and
\mu_{2}=6
. Seeing (
5.5
), by decomposition, to have
\|\Delta u_{\sigma}\|^{2}-C\|\nabla u_{\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0
with
C\leq\frac{9}{4}
, we need only to check the component with
\mu_{1}
. Since
u_{\sigma}(r\cdot),L_{j}u_{\sigma}(r\cdot)\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
for any
r>0
, we have

\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u_{\sigma}\right\|^{2}\geq 2\|\nabla_{\sigma}u_{\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 4\left\|u_{\sigma}\right\|_{4}^{2}.

So it is enough to check

(5.6)

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{4C}{9}\Big)\|\Delta_{r}f\|^{2}-(3+2C)\left\|f\right\|_{4}^{2}+4\left\|f\right\|_{4}^{2}\geq 0,\quad\forall\;f\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\backslash\{0\}).

By Theorem 1.1 in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,
\|\Delta_{r}f\|^{2}\geq\frac{9}{16}\|f\|_{4}^{2}
, we can claim (
5.6
) hence

\|\Delta u_{\sigma}\|^{2}-C\|\nabla u_{\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0
if

\Big(1-\frac{4C}{9}\Big)\frac{9}{16}+1-2C\geq 0,\quad\mbox{equally $C\leq\frac{25}{36}$.}

Applying Fubini Theorem, it’s not difficult to see that
\langle\Delta{\overline{u}},\Delta u_{\sigma}\rangle=0
, thus we get
E_{3,0}\geq\frac{25}{36}
by

\|\Delta u\|^{2}=\|\Delta{\overline{u}}\|^{2}+\|\Delta u_{\sigma}\|^{2}\geq\frac{9}{4}\|\nabla{\overline{u}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{25}{36}\|\nabla u_{\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{25}{36}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}.

It is also known that the optimal Rellich inequality
\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}\geq A_{\alpha}^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\alpha+4}
holds true in
C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
if and only if
|\alpha+2|\leq\sqrt{n^{2}-2n+2}
, see
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
. Under suitable condition on
\alpha
, we can give some explicit and simple remainder terms for

\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{\alpha}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\|\nabla u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{\alpha}-A_{\alpha}^{2}\|u\|_{\alpha+4}^{2},

always by means of identities. The following result generalizes Theorems 2.4 in
[
36
]
with
\alpha=0
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfy
|\alpha+2|\leq\sqrt{n-1}
. Then for any
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

\displaystyle\quad\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}-\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla\Big(|x|^{\frac{n-4-\alpha}{2}}u\Big)\right\|_{n-2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-A_{\alpha}^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\alpha+4}-D_{\alpha}\left\|\nabla\Big(|x|^{\frac{n-4-\alpha}{2}}u\Big)\right\|_{n-2}^{2}\geq 0.

Proof.
Take
\phi=|x|^{\frac{n-4-\alpha}{2}}u
,
\alpha=n-2
in (
1.4
),

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|_{n-2}^{2}=\|T_{n-2}(\phi)\|_{n-2}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|L_{j}\phi\|^{2}_{n-2}=\|T_{\alpha+2}u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|L_{j}u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}.

Combining with (
1.8
), developing
\|T_{\alpha}(\partial_{r}u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}
by (
2.12
) and using (
5.2
), there holds

\displaystyle\quad\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}-\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\|\nabla\phi\|_{n-2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(2+\alpha)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,1}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T_{\alpha}(L_{j}u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\Big[n-1-(2+\alpha)^{2}\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}.

On the other hand, applying Theorem
1.3
with
m=1
and (
5.7
), we have

\displaystyle\quad\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-A_{\alpha}^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\alpha+4}-D_{\alpha}\|\nabla\phi\|_{n-2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(2+\alpha)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,1}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T_{\alpha}(L_{j}u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}

Therefore, the left hand side of the two above formulas are identical, the proof is completed. ∎

Consider still
\phi=|x|^{\frac{n-4-\alpha}{2}}u
. As
A_{n-4}=0
and
D_{n-4}=(n-2)^{2}
, applying (
1.15
) with
m=1
, direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\|\Delta\phi\|_{n-4}^{2}

\displaystyle=(n-2)^{2}\|T_{n-2}(\phi)\|_{n-2}^{2}+\|R_{n-4,1}(\phi)\|_{n-4}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})\phi\right\|_{n-4}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T_{n-4}(L_{j}\phi)\|_{n-4}^{2}

\displaystyle=(n-2)^{2}\|T_{\alpha+2}(u)\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,1}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{\alpha}\left(L_{j}u\right)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}.

Combining (
5.4
), (
2.12
) and (
5.2
), we have

(5.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}-\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4(n-2)^{2}}\|\Delta\phi\|_{n-4}^{2}\\
&=\Big[1-\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4(n-2)^{2}}\Big]\Big(\|R_{\alpha,1}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T_{\alpha}(L_{j}(u))\|_{\alpha}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Big[2A_{\alpha}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\Big]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}\\
&\geq\Big[1-\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4(n-2)^{2}}\Big]\Big(\|R_{\alpha,1}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T_{\alpha}(L_{j}(u))\|_{\alpha}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\left\{\Big[1-\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4(n-2)^{2}}\Big](n-1)+2A_{\alpha}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|L_{j}u\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}.\end{split}

Hence the left hand side of (
5.8
) is nonnegative if

4(n-2)^{2}-(n-4-\alpha)^{2}\geq 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Big[1-\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4(n-2)^{2}}\Big](n-1)+2A_{\alpha}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\geq 0,

or equivalently

(5.9)

\displaystyle n\geq 3,\quad-n\leq\alpha\leq\frac{n^{3}-9n^{2}+25n-24}{3n^{2}-11n+11}.

So we extend Theorem 2.7 in
[
36
]
as follows.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
n,\alpha
satisfy (
5.9
), then for any
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

(5.10)

\displaystyle\quad\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}\geq\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4(n-2)^{2}}\left\|\Delta\Big(|x|^{\frac{n-4-\alpha}{2}}u\Big)\right\|_{n-2}^{2}.

5.3.
Hardy-Rellich identities with weights involving logarithmic

Here we show that the iterative idea to getting identities (
1.13
) and (
1.15
) can be applied for weights
|x|^{\alpha}\ln|x|
. As consequence, we will prove Theorem
1.6
. Let
f\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash(\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}))
. Denote

G_{\alpha,\beta}(f):=T_{\alpha}(f)+\frac{2\beta-1}{2r|\ln r|}f,\quad\forall\;\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Direct calculation implies that for any
f\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash(\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}))
,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\left\||\ln r|^{\beta}T_{\alpha}(f)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{(2\beta-1)^{2}}{4}\left\||\ln r|^{\beta-1}f\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\||\ln r|^{\beta}G_{\alpha,\beta}(f)\|_{\alpha}^{2}.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
u\in C_{c}^{2}(B_{1}\backslash\{0\})
or
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\overline{B_{1}})
. Then

(5.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}&=A_{\alpha}^{2}\|u\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}+\frac{D_{\alpha}}{4}\left\||\ln r|^{-1}u\right\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}+\frac{9}{16}\left\||\ln r|^{-2}u\right\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}\\
&\quad+D_{\alpha}\left\|G_{\alpha+2,0}(u)\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\left\|G_{\alpha,0}\circ T_{\alpha+2}(u)\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left\||\ln r|^{-1}G_{\alpha+2,-1}(u)\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}\\
&\quad+2A_{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|L_{j}u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\|_{\alpha}^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T_{\alpha}(L_{j}u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}.\end{split}

Proof
. By (
5.11
), there holds

\displaystyle\||\ln r|^{\beta}R_{\gamma,k}(u)\|_{\gamma}^{2}=\frac{(2\beta-1)^{2}}{4}\left\||\ln r|^{\beta-1}R_{\gamma+2,k-1}(u)\right\|_{\gamma+2}^{2}+\||\ln r|^{\beta}G_{\gamma,\beta}(R_{\gamma+2,k-1}u)\|_{\gamma}^{2}.

The iteration gives then, for all
k\geq 1
,
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|R_{\gamma,k}(u)\|_{\gamma}^{2}&=\frac{1}{4}\left\||\ln r|^{-1}R_{\gamma+2,k-1}u\right\|_{\gamma+2}^{2}+\|G_{\gamma,0}\circ R_{\gamma+2,k-1}(u)\|_{\gamma}^{2}\\
&=\gamma_{k+1}^{2}\left\||\ln r|^{-k-1}u\right\|^{2}_{\gamma+2k+2}+\gamma_{k}^{2}\left\||\ln r|^{-k}G_{\gamma+2k,-k}(u)\right\|_{\gamma+2k}^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{j}^{2}\left\||\ln r|^{-j}G_{\gamma+2j,-j}\circ R_{\gamma+2j+2,k-j-1}(u)\right\|_{\gamma+2j}^{2}.\end{split}

Here
\gamma_{0}=1
, and
\gamma_{j}=\frac{(2j-1)!!}{2^{j}}
for
j\geq 1
.
Combining (
2.12
) and (
5.11
), (
5.13
) with
k=1
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{\alpha}(\partial_{r}u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\|T_{\alpha+2}(u)\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,1}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left[\frac{1}{4}\left\||\ln r|^{-1}u\right\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}+\|G_{\alpha+2,0}(u)\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{9}{16}\left\||\ln r|^{-2}u\right\|_{\alpha+4}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left\||\ln r|^{-1}G_{\alpha+2,-1}(u)\right\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|G_{\alpha,0}\circ T_{\alpha+2}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}.

On the other hand, thanks to (
5.11
) with
\beta=0
and (
1.4
), there holds

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}u\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\||\ln r|^{-1}u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\frac{(n-2-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\|u\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|G_{\alpha,0}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}.

Then we deduce (
5.12
) by the above two identities and (
1.15
) with
m=1
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.
To get (
1.18
), we make use of an identity coming from (

) and (
5.13
), that is

\displaystyle\quad\|\Delta u\|_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{(n+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{\alpha+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-4-\alpha)^{2}}{4}\|T_{\alpha+2}(u)\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}+\|R_{\alpha,1}(u)\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\left\|(\Delta-\Delta_{r})u\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}

5.4.
Rellich equality and inequalities in Hyperbolic space

Consider the Poincaré’s hyperbolic ball
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
. Let
\rho=\ln\frac{1+r}{1-r}
denote the distance from origin to
x\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}
,
\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}
be the sphere derivatives and the radial Laplacian in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
defined respectively by (
1.20
), (
1.21
). Here we will prove the Rellich identity (
1.22
), then apply it to establish new Rellich inequalities, especially in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
and
{\mathbb{H}}^{4}
. In this subsection,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the
L^{2}
norm in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}
means the corresponding inner product.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
. At first, we observe that the Beltrami-Laplace operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}-\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}=\frac{1}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}=\frac{r^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}^{2}=\frac{(1-r^{2})^{2}}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{2}.

Take
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, by definition,

(5.14)

\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}=\|\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u\|^{2}+\|(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}-\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}})u\|^{2}+2\Big\langle\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u,\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{j}u\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

Using Lemma
2.2
,
\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}x_{j}{\mathcal{L}}_{j}=0
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(r)=0
.
Similar to the Euclidean case, direct calculation yields

(5.15)

\displaystyle\Big\langle\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u,\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{j}u\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u),{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}.

Thanks to Lemma
2.2
(ii), there holds

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(\partial_{\rho})-\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-r^{2}}{2}L_{j}\Big(\frac{1-r^{2}}{2}\partial_{r}\Big)-\frac{1-r^{2}}{2}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1-r^{2}}{2}L_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-r^{2})^{2}}{4}\Big[L_{j}(\partial_{r})-\partial_{r}(L_{j})\Big]+\frac{r(1-r^{2})}{2}L_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-r^{2})(1+r^{2})}{4r}L_{j}

\displaystyle=(\coth\rho){\mathcal{L}}_{j}.

Hence

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}_{j}\Big[({\rm sh}\rho)^{1-n}\partial_{\rho}(({\rm sh}\rho)^{n-1}\partial_{\rho})\Big]

\displaystyle={\rm(sh\rho)}^{1-n}\big(\partial_{\rho}+\coth\rho\big)\Big[({\rm sh}\rho)^{n-1}\big(\partial_{\rho}+\coth\rho\big)\Big]{\mathcal{L}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\partial^{2}_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j})+(n+1)\coth\rho\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j})+\Big[n(\coth\rho)^{2}-({\rm sh}\rho)^{-2}\Big]{\mathcal{L}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\partial^{2}_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j})+(n+1)\coth\rho\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j})+\frac{n-1}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}{\mathcal{L}}_{j}+n{\mathcal{L}}_{j}.

Moreover, we have

(5.16)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\rho}f,h\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=-\langle\partial_{\rho}h,f\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}-(n-1)\langle f,(\coth\rho)h\rangle_{\mathbb{H}},\quad\forall\;f,h\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).

Then

\displaystyle\quad\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u),{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\partial^{2}_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j}u)+(n+1)\coth\rho\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j}u)+\frac{n-1}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u+n{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u,{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle=-\|\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j}u)\|^{2}+2\langle\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j}u),\coth\rho{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}+n\|{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}+(n-1)\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\|\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j}u)\|^{2}+\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}-(n-1)\|\coth\rho{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}+n\|{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}+(n-1)\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\|\partial_{\rho}({\mathcal{L}}_{j}u)\|^{2}+\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\|{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}.

Combining with (
5.14
) and (
5.15
), we get the identity (
1.22
).

Furthermore, applying Lemma
5.1
, for any
u\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
, there holds

(5.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}-\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}})u\|^{2}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{n-1}}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{4}}{16}\Big|\sum_{j}L_{j}^{2}u\Big|^{2}({\rm sh}\rho)^{n-1}\rho^{\prime}(r)d\sigma dr\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{4}}{16}({\rm sh}\rho)^{n-1}\rho^{\prime}(r)\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}_{r}^{n-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|L_{j}u|^{2}d\sigma dr\\
&=(n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\frac{1-r^{2}}{2r}{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\Big\|^{2}\\
&=(n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}.\end{split}

In addition, similar to (
1.4
), the following equality in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
holds true, see
[
16
, Theorem 1.4]
. For any
u\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

(5.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\partial_{\rho}u\|^{2}&=\frac{1}{4}\Big\|\frac{u}{\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4}\Big\|\frac{u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\Big|\partial_{\rho}\Big(\frac{u}{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}({\rm sh}\rho)^{\frac{1-n}{2}}}\Big)\Big|^{2}\rho({\rm sh}\rho)^{1-n}dv_{\mathbb{H}}.\end{split}

Combining (
1.22
), (
5.17
), and (
5.18
) with
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u
, we get finally

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}&\geq\|\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{(n+1)(n-3)}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{(n+1)(n-3)}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{{\rm sh}\rho}\Big\|^{2},\\
&=\|\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u}{\rho}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{(n+1)(n-3)}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|{\rm coth}\rho{\mathcal{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}.\end{split}

The proof is completed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.
Firstly, let
u
be a
radial
function in
C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
. Denote
v=({\rm sh}\rho)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}u
, by definition of
\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}
, there holds

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u

\displaystyle=({\rm sh}\rho)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial\rho^{2}}-\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4}(\coth\rho)^{2}v-\frac{n-1}{2}v\right]=({\rm sh}\rho)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\big(v^{\prime\prime}-Av\big)

where
v^{\prime\prime}=\partial^{2}_{\rho}v
and

\displaystyle A(\rho)=\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}.

Then
(\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u)^{2}=({\rm sh}\rho)^{1-n}\big(v^{\prime\prime 2}+A^{2}v^{2}-2Avv^{\prime\prime}\big)
, so that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\|^{2}=\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(v^{\prime\prime 2}+A^{2}v^{2}-2Avv^{\prime\prime}\big)d\rho.

Clearly

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}-2Avv^{\prime\prime}d\rho=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(2Av^{\prime 2}-A^{\prime\prime}v^{2}\big)d\rho,

and

A^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4}\Big[\frac{6}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{4}}+\frac{4}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}\Big].

For the integral of
Av^{\prime 2}
, define
w=\frac{v}{{\rm sh}\rho}
, then
\frac{v^{\prime}}{{\rm sh}\rho}=w^{\prime}+w\coth\rho
. Hence we get

\displaystyle\frac{v^{\prime 2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}

\displaystyle=w^{\prime 2}+2ww^{\prime}\coth\rho+w^{2}\coth^{2}\rho=w^{\prime 2}+\big(w^{2}\coth\rho\big)^{\prime}+\frac{2v^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{4}}+\frac{v^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}.

Consequently

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}2Av^{\prime 2}d\rho=\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{\prime 2}d\rho+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[w^{\prime 2}+\frac{2v^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{4}}+\frac{v^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}\Big]d\rho.

Hence

(5.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(v^{\prime\prime 2}+A^{2}v^{2}-2Avv^{\prime\prime}\big)d\rho&=\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{\prime\prime 2}d\rho+\frac{(n-1)^{4}}{16}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{2}d\rho+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{\prime 2}d\rho\\
&\quad+\Big[\frac{(n-1)^{2}(n-3)^{2}}{16}-\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{2}\Big]\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{4}}d\rho\\
&\quad+\Big[\frac{(n-1)^{3}(n-3)}{8}-\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{2}\Big]\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}d\rho\\
&\quad+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime 2}d\rho.\end{split}

Applying the classical Hardy inequality over
(0,\infty)
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{\prime\prime 2}d\rho\geq\frac{9}{16}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{2}}{\rho^{4}}d\rho,\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{\prime 2}d\rho\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{2}}{\rho^{2}}d\rho,\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime 2}d\rho\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{2}}{\rho^{2}({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}d\rho.

Combining with the identity (
5.19
), the verification of (
1.24
) is completed for radial function.

By the Fubini theorem, the above estimates work also to get (
1.24
) with
\Delta_{\rho,{\mathbb{H}}}u
, hence with
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u
and
n\geq 3
, thanks to Theorem
1.7
.

The estimate in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
is an obvious consequence of (
1.24
). To get the inequality in
{\mathbb{H}}^{4}
, we use

\frac{9}{16\rho^{4}}-\frac{15}{16({\rm sh}\rho)^{4}}+\frac{3}{8\rho^{2}({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}=\frac{3}{16\rho^{4}}\Big[3+\frac{5\rho^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}\Big]\Big[1-\frac{\rho^{2}}{({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}}\Big]>0,\quad\forall\rho>0,

since
\rho^{2}\leq({\rm sh}\rho)^{2}
in
(0,\infty)
. So we are done.∎

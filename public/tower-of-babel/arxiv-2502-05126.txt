Regularity of edge ideals of powers of graphs
My Hanh Pham, Thanh Vu

Abstract
. We prove that the regularity of edge ideals of powers of forests is weakly decreasing. We then compute the regularity of edge ideals of powers of cycles.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
V(G)=[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and the edge set
E(G)
. The distance
\dist_{G}(u,v)
between the vertices
u
and
v
of
G
is the number of edges in a shortest path connecting
u
and
v
. If no such path exists,
\dist_{G}(u,v)=\infty
. The diameter of
G
, denoted by
\diam(G)
, is the greatest distance between any two vertices of
G
. For a positive integer
d
, the
d
-th power of
G
, denoted by
G^{d}
, is a graph on the vertex set
V(G)
and
\{u,v\}
is an edge of
G^{d}
if and only if
\dist_{G}(u,v)\leq d
. In particular,
G^{d}
is a complete graph for any
d\geq\diam(G)
. Graph power is a classical concept in graph theory
[
BM
]
. It deeply connects to information theory, communication complexity, and Ramsey theory
[
AL
]
. Recently, it also appears in the work of Cooper, El Khoury, Faridi, Mayes-Tang, Morey, Sega, and Spiroff
[
CEFMMSS
]
where the authors studied free resolutions of powers of squarefree monomial ideals. In this work, we study the regularity of edge ideals of powers of graphs. Recall that the edge ideal of
G
over a field
\Bbbk
is defined by

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subset R=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

We denote by
\im(G)
the induced matching number of
G
. In Lemma
2.5
, we prove that
\im(G^{d+1})\leq\im(G^{d})
for any positive integer
d
. Although the induced matching number of
G
only gives a lower bound for the regularity of its edge ideal
[
K
, Lemma 2.2]
, based on computations, we suggest the following.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph and
d
be a positive integer. Then

\reg\left(R/I\left(G^{d}\right)\right)\geq\reg\left(R/I\left(G^{d+1}\right)\right),

where
\reg
denotes the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity.

In the first main result of the paper, we prove that Conjecture
1.1
holds for forests.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a forest and
d
be a positive integer. Then

\reg\left(R/I\left(G^{d}\right)\right)\geq\reg\left(R/I\left(G^{d+1}\right)\right).

If
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}\cup\cdots\cup G_{t}
where
G_{i}
are the connected components of
G
then
G^{d}=G_{1}^{d}\cup G_{2}^{d}\cup\cdots\cup G_{t}^{d}
. By the results of Nguyen and Vu
[
NV
]
, we may assume that
G
is connected when studying the homological invariants of powers of
I(G^{d})
. When
d\geq 2
,
G^{d}
contains many triangles and is generally more complicated than
G
. Fortunately, when
G
is a tree, by the result of Laskar and Shier
[
LS
]
,
G^{d}
is chordal for all
d\geq 1
. The conclusion then follows from Lemma
2.5
and
[
W
, Theorem 14]
.

On the other hand, the power operation preserves symmetries of
G
, e.g. if
G
is circulant then its powers are circulant. Our next result shows that Conjecture
1.1
also holds for cycles.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n,d
be positive integers such that
n\geq 3
. Then

\reg\left(R/I(C_{n}^{d})\right)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }n\leq 2d+2,\\
2&\text{ if }n=2d+3,\\
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{d+2}\right\rfloor&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

where
C_{n}
denotes a cycle on
n
vertices.

This result confirms the computational experiments done by Romeo
[
Ro
]
. In the cases where
n\leq 2d+3
the results are essentially known. In the cases where
n=k(d+2)+r
for
0\leq r\leq d
, we drop the vertices
1,2,\ldots,d
sequentially to get to the case of powers of paths. The critical case where
n=k(d+2)+d+1
is much more subtle. The basic idea is simple, though. To establish the inequality
\reg(J)\leq k+1
for a monomial ideal
J
, we use one of the following strategies.

(1)

Express
J=K+xL
for some variable
x
, then establish that
\reg(K),\reg(K\cap L)\leq k+1
and
\reg(L)\leq k
.

(2)

Choose a sequence of variables
y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell}
so that
J+(y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell})
is well understood and have regularity at most
k+1
and
(J+(y_{1},\ldots,y_{j-1})):y_{j}
is well understood and have regularity at most
k
for all
j=1,\ldots,\ell
.

We refer to Section
3
for more details.

In Section
2
, we recall the notion of graph powers and prove that the induced matching numbers of powers of graphs are weakly decreasing. We then deduce Theorem
1.2
. In Section
3
, we establish Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Powers of graphs

2.1.
Graph

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and the edge set
E(G)
.

(1)

The neighborhood of a vertex
x\in V(G)
is
N_{G}(x)=\{y\in V(G)\mid\{x,y\}\in E(G)\}
. The closed neighborhood of
x
is
N_{G}[x]=N_{G}(x)\cup\{x\}
.

(2)

For a subset
U\subset V(G)
, the neighborhood and closed neighborhood of
U
in
G
are defined respectively by
N_{G}(U)=\cup\left(N_{G}(x)\mid x\in U\right)
and
N_{G}[U]=\cup\left(N_{G}[x]\mid x\in U\right)
.

(3)

A simple graph
H
is a subgraph of
G
if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
.
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
if it is a subgraph of
G
and
E(H)=E(G)\cap V(H)\times V(H)
.

(4)

For a subset
U\subset V(G)
,
G[U]
and
G\backslash U
denote the induced subgraph of
G
on
U
and on
V(G)\setminus U
respectively.

(5)

A path
P_{n}
on
n
vertices is the graph on
V(P_{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
with the edge set
E(P_{n})=\{\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\ldots,\{x_{n-1},x_{n}\}\}.

(6)

A cycle
C_{n}
on
n
vertices is the graph on
V(C_{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
with the edge set
E(C_{n})=E(P_{n})\cup\{\{x_{1},x_{n}\}\}
.

(7)

A forest is a graph without any cycles. A tree is a connected forest.

(8)

G
is called chordal if it has no induced cycles of length at least four.

(9)

A subset
M\subseteq E(G)
is called a matching of
G
if no two edges in
M
share a common vertex. It is an
induced matching
if
M
forms an induced subgraph of
G
.
Induced matching number
of
G
, denoted by
\im(G)
, is the largest size of an induced matching of
G
.

The induced matching number of the
d
-th power of an
n
-cycle was given in
[
Ro
, Corollary 3.4]
. We now compute the induced matching number of the
d
-th power of a path on
n
vertices.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n,d
be positive integers such that
n\geq 2
. Then

\im(P_{n}^{d})=\left\lfloor\frac{n+d}{d+2}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Fix
d\geq 1
. When
n\leq d+1
,
P_{n}^{d}
is a complete graph on
n
vertices. Hence,
\im(P_{n}^{d})=1
. Now, assume that
n\geq d+2
. Write
n=k(d+2)+r
for some
k\geq 1
and
0\leq r\leq d+1
. Let

\mathcal{M}=\{\{1,2\},\{(d+2)+1,(d+2)+2\},\ldots,\{(d+2)\ell+1,(d+2)\ell+2\}\},

where
\ell=k-1
if
r\leq 1
and
\ell=k
if
r\geq 2
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is an induced matching of
P_{n}^{d}
. Hence,
\im(P_{n}^{d})\geq|\mathcal{M}|=\lfloor\frac{n+d}{d+2}\rfloor
. We now prove by induction on
n
that
\im(P_{n}^{d})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n+d}{d+2}\right\rfloor
. The base case where
n\leq d+1
is clear. Now, assume that
n\geq d+2
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a maximum induced matching of
P_{n}^{d}
. If
1\notin\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is an induced matching of
P_{n-1}^{d}
, and the conclusion follows from induction. Thus, we may assume that
1\in\mathcal{M}
. Assume that
\{1,i\}\in\mathcal{M}
for some
i\leq d+1
. Then dropping
\{1,i\}
from
\mathcal{M}
gives an induced matching of
P_{n-i-d}^{d}
. By induction, we deduce that

\im(P_{n}^{d})\leq 1+\im(P_{n-i-d}^{d})\leq 1+\im(P_{n-d-2}^{d})=\left\lfloor\frac{n+d}{d+2}\right\rfloor.

The conclusion follows.
∎

In order to prove that
\im(G^{d+1})\leq\im(G^{d})
for an arbitrary graph
G
, we introduce the following concept.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
u,v
be vertices of a simple graph
G
. Assume that
\dist_{G}(u,v)=d\geq 2
. We denote by
W(u,v)
the set of vertices
w
of
G
such that
\dist_{G}(u,w)=d-1
and
\dist_{G}(w,v)=1
. We call
W(u,v)
the
distance witness set
of
u
and
v
. We denote by
W[u,v]=W(u,v)\cup\{u,v\}
.

We now have some simple lemmas.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{u_{1},v_{1}\}
and
\{u_{2},v_{2}\}
be an induced matching of
G^{d}
such that
\dist_{G}(u_{1},v_{1})=d
. Then

(1)

if
\dist_{G}(u_{2},v_{2})\leq d-1
then
W[u_{1},v_{1}]\cap\{u_{2},v_{2}\}=\emptyset
;

(2)

if
\dist_{G}(u_{2},v_{2})=d
then
W[u_{1},v_{1}]\cap W[u_{2},v_{2}]=\emptyset
.

Proof.

By definition,
\dist_{G}(u_{1},u_{2}),\dist_{G}(u_{1},v_{2}),\dist_{G}(v_{1},u_{2}),\dist_{G}(v_{1},v_{2})>d
. Hence,
W[u_{1},v_{1}]\cap\{u_{2},v_{2}\}=\emptyset
and (1) follows immediately. Now, assume that
\dist_{G}(u_{2},v_{2})=d
and there exists an
w\in W(u_{1},v_{1})\cap W(u_{2},v_{2})
. By definition,
\dist_{G}(u_{1},w)=d-1
and
\dist_{G}(w,v_{2})=1
. Hence,
\dist_{G}(u_{1},v_{2})\leq d
, which is a contradiction. The conclusion (2) follows.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a simple graph and
d
be a positive integer. Then
\im(G^{d+1})\leq\im(G^{d})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\im\left(G^{d+1}\right)=m
and
\{\{u_{1},v_{1}\},\ldots,\{u_{m},v_{m}\}\}
is an induced matching of
G^{d+1}
. We may assume that
\dist_{G}(u_{i},v_{i})\leq d
for
i\leq s
and
\dist_{G}(u_{i},v_{i})=d+1
for
i=s+1,\ldots,m
for some integer
s
such that
0\leq s\leq m
. For
i=s+1,\ldots,m
, let
w_{i}
be any element of
W(u_{i},v_{i})
. By the proof of Lemma
2.4
,
\{\{u_{1},v_{1}\},\ldots,\{u_{s},v_{s}\},\{u_{s+1},w_{s+1}\},\ldots,\{u_{m},w_{m}\}\}
is an induced matching of
G^{d}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

2.2.
Regularity

Let
R=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
\Bbbk
. Denote by
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
the maximal homogeneous ideal of
R
. For a finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
,
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. The Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
M
is defined by

\reg_{R}(M)=\max\left\{i+j\mid H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)_{j}\neq 0\right\}.

For a nonzero homogeneous ideal
I
of
R
, we have
\reg(I)=\reg(R/I)+1
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
n,d
be positive integers. Then

\reg(R/I(P_{n}^{d}))=\left\lfloor\frac{n+d}{d+2}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

By
[
LS
, Corollary 2]
,
P_{n}^{d}
is chordal. By
[
W
, Theorem 14]
,
\reg(R/I(P_{n}^{d}))=\im(P_{n}^{d})
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
G
be a forest. By
[
LS
, Corollary 2]
,
G^{d}
are chordal for all
d\geq 1
. By
[
W
, Theorem 14]
,
\reg(R/I(G^{d}))=\im(G^{d})
for all
d\geq 1
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

(1)

There are chordal graphs
G
such that
G^{2}
is not chordal; see
[
LS
, Theorem 6]
.

(2)

Applying
[
LS
, Corollary 3]
, we deduce that Conjecture
1.1
also holds for block graphs.

Example 2.8
.

Let
G
be a sunflower graph
S_{6}
as in the following figure. Then
G
is chordal, but
G^{2}
is not chordal. Also, we have
\im(G)=3
, hence
\reg(R/I(G))=3
. Using Macaulay2
[
M2
]
, we get
\reg(R/I(G^{2}))=2
.

3.
Powers of cycles

In this section, we compute the regularity of edge ideals of powers of cycles. The results show that Conjecture
1.1
also holds for cycles. We first introduce some notations and preliminary results.

If
u\in R
is a monomial, the
support
of
u
, denoted by
\supp(u)
is the set of variables
x_{i}
such that
x_{i}|u
. Also, if
J\subset R
is a monomial ideal with the minimal generating set
G(J)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
, the
support
of
J
is
\supp(J)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\supp(u_{i})
. The following results are well known, see e.g.
[
HH
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
I,J,L
be nonzero monomial ideals and
f
be a nonzero monomial of
R
. Then

(1)

(I+J):f=(I:f)+(J:f)
.

(2)

(I+J)\cap L=(I\cap L)+(J\cap L)
.

(3)

If
\supp(I)\cap\supp(J)=\emptyset
then
I\cap J=IJ
.

(4)

I\cap(f)=f(I:f).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
I
be a nonzero homogeneous ideal and
x
be a variable of
R
. Then

\reg(I)\leq\max\{\reg(I+(x)),\reg(I:x)+1\}.

In particular, let
G
be a simple graph and
v
be a vertex. Then

\reg(I(G))\leq\max\{\reg(I(G\backslash v)),\reg(I(G\backslash N_{G}[v]))+1\}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I
be a nonzero homogeneous ideal and
x
be a variable of
R
. Then

\reg(xI)=\reg(I)+1.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
I
and
J
be nonzero proper homogeneous ideals of
R
. Then

\reg(I+J)\leq\max\{\reg(I),\reg(J),\reg(I\cap J)-1\}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
n=2d+3
and
G=C_{n}^{d}
. Then
\reg(R/I(G))=2
.

Proof.

Since
n=2d+3
, we have
C_{n}^{d}=C_{n}(1,2,\ldots,d,\widehat{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor})
. Also,
n\neq 2(d+1)
and
{\rm gcd}(n,d+1)=1
. Hence, by
[
UV
, Theorem 5]
, we obtain
\reg(R/I(G))=2.

∎

Assume that
G=C_{n}^{d}
where
n\geq 2d+2
. We fix the following notation.

(3.1)

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=G=C_{n}^{d},

(3.2)

\displaystyle G_{i}

\displaystyle=G_{i-1}\setminus\{i\}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,d.

We have

N_{G_{i-1}}(i)=\{n-d+i,\ldots,n,i+1,\ldots,i+d\}.

Hence,

(3.3)

\displaystyle G_{d}

\displaystyle\cong P_{n-d}^{d}

(3.4)

\displaystyle G_{i-1}\backslash N_{G_{i-1}}[i]

\displaystyle\cong P_{n-(2d+1)}^{d}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,d.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
n=k(d+2)+r
where
0\leq r\leq d
and
k\geq 2
. Then

\reg(R/I(C_{n}^{d}))=k.

Proof.

By
[
Ro
, Corollary 3.4]
and
[
K
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have that
\reg(R/I(C_{n}^{d}))\geq k
. We now prove by downward induction on
i\leq d
that
\reg(R/I(G_{i}))\leq k
. The base case where
i=d
follows from Corollary
2.6
and Eq. (
3.3
). For the induction step, by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\reg(I(G_{i-1}))\leq\max\{\reg(I(G_{i})),\reg(I(G_{i-1}\backslash N_{G_{i-1}}[i]))+1\}.

The conclusion follows from induction, Corollary
2.6
and Eq. (
3.4
).
∎

The argument above does not apply to the case where
n=k(d+2)+d+1
as we can see that
\reg(R/I(P_{n-(2d+1)}^{d}))=k
which is also the expected value for
\reg(R/I(C_{n}^{d}))
. We need to treat it separately in a series of lemmas. We further use the following notation.

(3.5)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=I(G)=I(G_{0}),J=I(G_{1})

(3.6)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=(x_{n-d+1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d+1}),

(3.7)

\displaystyle A_{j}

\displaystyle=(x_{\ell}\mid\ell\in N_{G}(j)\backslash\{1,2\}),\text{ for }j\neq 1,2

(3.8)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=(x_{\ell}\mid\ell\in N_{G}(2)\backslash\{1\}),

(3.9)

\displaystyle B_{j}

\displaystyle=I(G\backslash N_{G}[\{1,j\}]),\text{ for }j\neq 1,

Lemma 3.7
.

With the notations above, we have
I=J+x_{1}K
and
J\cap K=L+x_{2}M
, where

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=3}^{d+1}x_{j}(A_{j}+B_{j})+\sum_{j=n-d+1}^{n}x_{j}(A_{j}+B_{j}),

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=A_{2}+B_{2}.

Proof.

The equality
I=J+x_{1}K
follows immediately from the definition. We now compute
J\cap K
. For
j\in N_{G}(1)
, we have

(x_{j})\cap J=x_{j}(J:x_{j})=x_{j}\cdot\left((x_{\ell}\mid\ell\in N_{G}(j)\backslash\{1\})+I(G\backslash N_{G}[j])\right).

Now for any
\ell\in N_{G}(1)\backslash N_{G}[j]
and any
x_{\ell}x_{k}\in I(G\backslash N_{G}[j])
we have
x_{j}x_{\ell}x_{k}\in x_{\ell}(x_{m}\mid m\in N_{G}(m)\backslash\{1\})
. The conclusion then follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume that
n=k(d+2)+d+1
where
k\geq 2
. With the notations above, we have

\reg(R/M)=k-1.

Proof.

Note that
G\backslash N_{G}[\{1,2\}]\cong P_{n-(2d+2)}^{d}
. By Lemma
3.7
and Corollary
2.6
, we deduce that

\reg(R/M)=\reg(R/I(P_{(k-1)(d+2)+1}^{d}))=\left\lfloor\frac{(k-1)(d+2)+d+1}{d+2}\right\rfloor=k-1.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

With the notations above, we have

\reg(J)\leq k+1.

Proof.

Let
H_{1}=G_{1}=G\backslash\{1\}
. We then define the graphs
H_{j}
as follows.

\displaystyle H_{2j}

\displaystyle=H_{2j-1}\backslash\{n-d+j-1\}\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,d-1,

\displaystyle H_{2j+1}

\displaystyle=H_{2j}\backslash\{j+1\}\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,d-1.

We prove by downward induction on
1\leq\ell\leq 2d-1
that
\reg(I(H_{\ell}))\leq k+1
. The base case where
k=2d-1
follows from Corollary
2.6
and the fact that

H_{2d-1}=G\backslash\{n-d,\ldots,n-1,1,\ldots,d\}\cong P_{n-(2d+1)}^{d}\cup\{n\},

where
n
is an isolated point. For the induction step, by applying Lemma
3.2
, we need to prove that
\reg(I((H_{2j}\backslash N_{H_{2j}}[j+1])))\leq k
and
\reg(I(H_{2j-1}\backslash N_{H_{2j-1}}[n-d+j-1]))\leq k.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle H_{2j}\backslash N_{H_{2j}}[j+1]

\displaystyle=G\backslash(\{n-d,\ldots,n-d+j-1\}\cup N_{G}[j+1]),

\displaystyle H_{2j-1}\backslash N_{H_{2j-1}}[n-d+j-1]

\displaystyle=G\backslash(\{1,\ldots,j\}\cup N_{G}[n-d+j-1]).

For simplicity, we denote by
F_{2j}=H_{2j}\backslash N_{H_{2j}}[j+1]
and
F_{2j-1}=H_{2j-1}\backslash N_{H_{2j-1}}[n-d+j-1]
. We have that
F_{2j}
is the induced subgraph of
G
on
\{j+d+2,\ldots,n-d-1\}\cup\{n-d+j\}
. In particular,
F_{2j}
is chordal. Hence, it suffices to prove that
\im(F_{2j})\leq k-1
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be an induced matching of
F_{2j}
. If
n-d+j\notin\mathcal{M}
then
\mathcal{M}
is an induced matching of
P_{n-j-2(d+1)}^{d}
and the conclusion follows from Lemma
2.2
. If
n-d+j\in\mathcal{M}
then dropping the edge containing
n-d+j
from
\mathcal{M}
we get an induced matching of
P_{n-j-3(d+1)}
and the conclusion follows from Lemma
2.2
. The conclusion that
\im(F_{2j-1})\leq k-1
can be done similarly.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume that
n=k(d+2)+d+1
where
k\geq 2
. With the notations above, we have

\reg(L)\leq k+1.

Proof.

We define the following ideals

\displaystyle L_{1}

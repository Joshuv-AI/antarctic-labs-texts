Mutual-visibility coloring of graphs
Saneesh Babu, Gabriele Di Stefano, Aparna Lakshmanan S

Abstract
The mutual-visibility chromatic number of a graph G G is the smallest number of colors needed to color the vertices of G G such that each color class is a mutual-visibility set. In this paper, we prove that determining the mutual-visibility chromatic number of a graph is NP-complete even when restricted to the class of graphs having diameter four and mutual-visibility chromatic number two. We further determine the exact value of the mutual-visibility chromatic number for glued binary trees and glued t t -ary trees.



Keywords:
Mutual-visibility number, mutual-visibility chromatic number, computational complexity, glued binary tree, glued
t
-ary tree

The

is a classical problem in discrete geometry known from games in various cultures since ancient times
[

,
24
]

[
10
]
. This problem was later generalized to larger grids and planes
[
12
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
. Graph theoretic version of this problem was introduced independently in
2016
and

under the names
geodesic irredundant sets

[
6
]
and
general position sets

[
17
]
, respectively. Later, the graph theoretic version of the problem is popular under the name general position problem which has been recently surveyed in
[
5
]
, see also
[
1
,
15
,
16
,
21
]
. Subsequently, several variations of this problem emerged
[
22
]
, among which the most extensively studied is the

variant, first introduced by Di Stefano in

[
9

. A set

is mutually visible if for every

, there exists a shortest

. Beyond its application in computer science, particularly in robot visibility, the mutual-visibility problem connects to combinatorial problems such as Zarankiewicz and Turan problems
[

,
8
,

.

In
2024
, Sandi Klavžar et al. introduced a vertex coloring of graphs in which each color class is a mutual-visibility set
[
14
]
. The

is defined as follows: two vertices

can be given the same color if there exists an

and

. The smallest number of colors required for such a coloring of

is called

, denoted by

[
4
]
, independent mutual-visibility sets
[
3
]
and distance mutual-visibility sets
[
2
]
have also been investigated.

It is proved in
[
4
]
that the general position problem is NP-complete even when restricted to the class of graphs with diameter two and general position number three. The concept of independent general position coloring is also introduced and proved to be NP-complete even when restricted to the class of graphs with diameter at most three. In
[
14
]
, computing the computational complexity of mutual-visibility chromatic number is left as an interesting problem for further investigation. The decision problem arising from the independent version of mutual-visibility coloring
[
3
]
is proved to be NP-complete even in graphs with universal vertices and the authors conjectured that mutual-visibility coloring is NP-complete.

In this paper, the mutual-visibility coloring problem is proved to be NP-complete settling the conjecture. In view of its computational complexity, it is natural to study this parameter in specific graph classes. In the last section we determine the exact values of mutual-visibility coloring for glued binary trees and extend it further the glued
t
-ary trees.

Lemma 2.2
.

In a mutual-visibility coloring of
H_{n}
with two colors, vertices
c
and
p
(
c^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime}
, respectively) cannot be colored with the same color. In the set of vertices
\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}\}
, at least two vertices are colored with different colors.

Proof.

Consider a mutual-visibility coloring of
H_{n}
using two colors, say red and white. If a pendant vertex and its support vertex are given the same color, then that color cannot be used anywhere else in the graph. Therefore, if
p
and
c
(equivalently,
p^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime}
) are given the same color, then we require at least three colors to color
H_{n}
. Suppose vertices
p
and
c
are colored red. Therefore,
p
and
c
(equivalently,
p^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime}
) must be of different colors. Without loss of generality assume that
p
is white and
c
is red. Since at least one among
c^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime}
is white (equivalently, red), at least one among
\{p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n}\}
must be red (equivalently, white) for mutual-visibility. Hence, the lemma.

Theorem 2.3
.

MV-coloring
is NP-complete even for instances
(G,k)
with
\mathop{\mathrm{diam}}(G)=4
and
k=2
.

Proof.

Given a coloring of
G

We prove that the
NAE3-SAT

[
23
]
, polynomially reduces to
MV-coloring
.

A
NAE3-SAT
(
not-all-equal 3-satisfiability
) instance
\Phi
is defined as a set
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{q}\}
of
q
Boolean variables and a set
C
of
r
clauses, each defined as a set of three literals: every variable
x_{i}
corresponds to two literals, that is,
x_{i}
(the positive form) and
\bar{x}_{i}
(the negative form). To simplify the notations we will denote by
\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}\}
the clause with literals
\ell_{i},i\in[3]
, without distinction between the orders in which they are listed. A truth assignment assigns a Boolean value (
True
or
False
) to each variable, corresponding to a truth assignment of opposite values for the two literals
x_{i}
and
\bar{x_{i}}
:
\bar{x_{i}}
is
True
if and only if
x_{i}
is
False
.

The
NAE3-SAT
problem asks whether there is a truth assignment to the variables such that in no clause all three literals have the same truth value. We will say that such an assignment is
satisfying
and the instance
\Phi
is
satisfied
.

In what follows we assume that in any instance of
NAE3-SAT
each clause involves three different variables. Indeed, if a clause involves a single variable and all the literals are equal, then the
NAE3-SAT
problem has a No answer and if the positive and negative form of a variable are present in a clause we can remove the clause from the instance.
If a clause involves only two variables, and the variable appearing two times in the clauses is present in the same form, e.g.
\{l_{1},l_{1},l_{2}\}
, then the clause is logically equivalent to
\{l_{1},l_{2}\}
and it can be substituted with two clauses
\{l_{1},l_{2},a\}
and
\{l_{1},l_{2},\bar{a}\}
, where

is an additional variable.

We reduce any instance of

. Let

As the mutual-visibility coloring problem is NP-complete even for instances
(G,k)

-ary trees are determined in this section. We start with the special case of the glued binary trees and then extend the result to glued
t
-ary trees.

Throughout this section, the vertices of two copies of the binary tree in
GT(r)
are denoted by
v_{i,j}
and
v_{i,j}^{\prime}
, respectively, for
i=1,2,\ldots,r-1
and
i-1
is the depth of
v_{i,j}
and
v^{\prime}_{i,j}
in
T_{r}^{(1)}
and
T_{r}^{(2)}
, respectively; and
j
is the position of the vertex counted from left to right in the drawing. The quasi-leaves, which are leaf vertices of both rooted binary trees, are denoted by
v_{1},v_{2},......v_{2^{r}}
, labeled from left to right in the drawing.

Let
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
be two quasi-leaves of
GT(r)
. There exist exactly two geodesic paths between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
, say
P_{i,j}
, through
V_{r}^{(1)}
and
P^{\prime}_{i,j}
through
V_{r}^{(2)}
. Now,
P_{i,j}
and
P^{\prime}_{i,j}
together induces a cycle which we denote by
C_{i,j}
. Let
Q_{i,j}
and
Q_{i,j}^{\prime}
be the paths
P_{i,j}\backslash\{v_{i},v_{j}\}
and paths
P_{i,j}^{\prime}\backslash\{v_{i},v_{j}\}
, respectively. Note that any cycle in
GT(r)
intersects with exactly two quasi-leaves and can always be represented as

in a unique manner.

The following observations related to the geodesic paths between any two vertices in

will be useful for us.

Observation 3.1
.

Given two vertices of the form
v_{i,j}
and
v_{k,l}
in a copy of a binary tree, there exists a unique geodesic path between them.

Observation 3.2
.

Given two vertices
v_{i,j}\in T^{(1)}_{r}
,
v^{\prime}_{i,j}\in T^{(2)}_{r}
and any leaf vertex
v_{a}
of
T_{v_{i,j}}
(equivalently,
T_{v^{\prime}_{i,j}})
, then there exists a unique geodesic path between
v_{i,j}
and
v^{\prime}_{i,j}
, which passes through
v_{a}
.

Observation 3.3
.

Given two vertices
v_{i,j}\in T^{(1)}_{r}
,
v^{\prime}_{i,l}\in T^{(2)}_{r}
and any leaf vertex
v_{a}
of
T_{v_{i,j}}
(equivalently,
T_{v^{\prime}_{i,l}})
, then there exists a unique geodesic path between
v_{i,j}
and
v^{\prime}_{i,l}
, which passes through
v_{a}
. Also each of these geodesic paths, either passes through
v^{\prime}_{i,j}
or
v_{i,l}
.

Observation 3.4
.

Given two vertices
v_{i,j}\in T^{(1)}_{r}
,
v^{\prime}_{k,l}\in T^{(2)}_{r}
where
i<k
and any leaf vertex
v_{a}
of
T_{v_{k,l}}
, then there exists a unique geodesic path between
v_{i,j}
and
v^{\prime}_{k,l}
, which passes through
v_{a}
and each of these geodesic paths passes through

also. Note that here

and

may be equal or distinct. Also, a similar statement holds for

.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
S
is a mutual-visibility set of
GT(r)
, then
|{S\cap V(C_{i,j})}|\leq 3
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary that there exists a mutual-visibility set
S
of
GT(r)
such that
|S\cap V(C_{i,j})|>3
.

If both quasi-leaves in
C_{i,j}
are present in
S
, then
|S\cap Q_{i,j}|\leq 1
and
|S\cap Q_{i,j}^{\prime}|\leq 1
. Also,
S
cannot simultaneously intersect
Q_{i,j}
and
Q_{i,j}^{\prime}
and hence
|S\cap V(C_{i,j})|\leq 3
. If one of the quasi-leaves in
C_{i,j}
is present in
S
, then again
|S\cap Q_{i,j}|\leq 1
and
|S\cap Q_{i,j}^{\prime}|\leq 1
and hence
|{S\cap V(C_{i,j})}|\leq 3
.

Now consider the case where both the quasi-leaves are not present in
S
. Since the quasi-leaves are not present in
S
, then since
|S\cap Q_{i,j}|\leq 2
and
|S\cap Q_{i,j}^{\prime}|\leq 2
, we have
|{S\cap V(C_{i,j})}|\leq 4
. But, if there are two vertices each from both
Q_{i,j}
and
Q^{\prime}_{i,j}
, say
v_{i,j},v_{k,l},v^{\prime}_{a,b}
and
v^{\prime}_{c,d}
, then from the above observations, at least one pair of these vertices will not be mutually-visible, which is a contradiction. Hence, the lemma.

Theorem 3.6

For

\chi_{\mu}(GT(r))=\begin{cases}2(r-i)+3,&2^{i-1}+i-2\leq r\leq 2^{i-1}+2^{i-2}+i-3\\

using the number of colors specified in the theorem.

v^{\prime}_{3,2},v^{\prime}_{3,3},v^{\prime}_{3,4},\ldots\}
, for

. Similarly, assign color

r-k
in
V_{r}^{(2)}
and to the

\{v_{1,1},v_{2,2},v_{2,1},v_{3,4},v_{3,3},v_{3,2},v_{3,1},\ldots\}

and

and

so that there are fewer than

with color 1 and all the remaining vertices with color

to obtain a mutual-visibility coloring of

with

Case 2:

.

Sub Case 1:

.

are colored using

Sub Case 2:

Sub case 3:

i-1
will be uncolored. We can use one color each for these uncolored vertices in

.

To prove the converse, first we prove that

colors are not enough for a mutual-visibility coloring of

, where

is the unique integer such that

4r-6

\chi_{0}\cap\chi_{1}=\phi

Now, consider the cycles

\chi_{2}

Theorem 3.9

in

with

Sub case 3:
\frac{t^{i-1}-1}{t-1}+i+1\leq r\leq\frac{t^{i-1}-1}{t-1}+\frac{t^{i-1}}{2}+i-2

The coloring procedure is the same as Case 1, where the only difference is that more than half of the vertices of depth

will be uncolored. We can use one color each for these uncolored vertices in
V_{r}^{(1)}
and
V_{r}^{(2)}

.

To prove the converse, first we prove that
2(r-i)+1
colors are not enough for a mutual-visibility coloring of
GT(r,t)
, where
i
is the unique integer such that
\frac{t^{i-1}-1}{t-1}+i-1\leq r\leq\frac{t^{i}-1}{t-1}+i-1
. Assume the contrary that there exists a mutual-visibility coloring with
2(r-i)+1
colors.

Consider the cycle
C_{1,t^{r}}
. By Lemma
3.8
, a color can be used at most 3 times to color the vertices of this cycle. Since we have only

vertices in

4r-2[2(r-i)+1]=4i-2
colors must be used at least three times. Let
\chi_{0}
be the collection of colors which are used three times to color the vertices of
C_{1,t^{r}}
so that
|\chi_{0}|\geq 4i-2
. Now, consider
(t-1)
cycles
C_{mt^{r-1},mt^{r-1}+1}
where
m\in[t-1]
, note that all the cycles have length
4r
. Let
S_{m}=C_{mt^{r-1},mt^{r-1}+1}-(C_{1,t^{r}}\cup C_{(m-1)t^{r-1},(m-1)t^{r-1}+1})

, if

4i-6

, where

and
|\chi_{m}|\geq 4i-8
, when

.

Claim 1:

\chi_{n}\cap\chi_{m}=\phi
, for every

.

On the contrary, if

Claim 2:

\chi^{n}_{m}

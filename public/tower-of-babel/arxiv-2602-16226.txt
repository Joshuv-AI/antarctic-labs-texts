Cyclic p p - φ \varphi -contraction mappings
Sayyed Mohammad Sadegh Nabavi Sales

Abstract
. We introduce a new class of mappings called cyclic p p - φ \varphi -contraction mappings and investigate the existence and uniqueness of fixed point for such mappings defined on metric spaces, uniformly convex Banach spaces, or reflexive Banach spaces.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

The well known Banach fixed point theorem, also known as the contraction mapping theorem, is an important result in the theory of complete metric spaces. It discusses the existence of the unique fixed point of a contraction mapping defined on a complete metric space. The contractive condition for a mapping, considered in its classical version, seems to be somewhat strong, since it indeed makes such a mapping continuous. The investigations in the field, since the time of the proof of the original version of the Banach fixed point theorem, show that by relaxing the contractive condition we can provide examples of mappings that are not necessarily continuous and for which the existence and uniqueness of fixed point is guaranteed; see
[
3
]
. Some recent work in this area that motivated this article include:
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
. In
[
4
]
, the authors consider mapping
T:X\to X
that is cyclic contraction meaning that, there are nonempty subsets
A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}
of a metric space
(X,d)
,
T(A_{i})\subset A_{i+1}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,m
with
A_{m+1}=A_{1}
, and there exists
0<\alpha<1
so that
d(Tx,Ty)\leq\alpha d(x,y)
for
x\in A_{i}
and
y\in A_{i+1}
. It is proved there that if
X
is complete and
A_{i}
s are closed, then
T
has a unique fixed point in
\cap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
. Note that the contractive condition considered in this result does not make
T
continuous, so it is actually an important improvement. This result is improved in
[
1
,
2
]
considering cyclic
\alpha
-contraction mappings and cyclic
\varphi
-contraction mappings, where
0<\alpha<1
and
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a strictly increasing function. For nonempty subsets
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
of a metric space
(X,d)
, a mapping
T:A_{1}\cup A_{2}\to A_{1}\cup A_{2}
is said to be cyclic
\varphi
-contraction mapping if
T(A_{1})\subset A_{2}
,
T(A_{2})\subset A_{1}
and

d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(x,y)-\varphi(d(x,y))+\varphi(d(A_{1},A_{2})),\qquad(x\in A_{1},y\in A_{2}),

where
d(A_{1},A_{2})
stands for the distance between
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. If
\varphi(t)=\alpha t
, then a cyclic
\varphi
-contraction mapping is called a cyclic
\alpha
-contraction mapping. In
[
1
,
2
]
, the authors prove some fixed point theorems in general metric spaces, uniformly convex Banach spaces, and reflexive spaces. In this note, we generalize their method in such spaces for mappings which we call cyclic
p
-
\varphi
-contraction, where
p\geq 1
and
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a strictly increasing function. Now, some preliminaries.

Let
X
be a Banach space.
X
is said to be uniformly convex if there exists a strictly increasing function
\delta:(0,2]\to[0,1]
such that the following implication holds for all
x,y,w\in X,R>0
and
r\in[0,2R]
:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} \|x-w\|\leq R\\
\|y-w\|\leq R\\
\|x-y\|\geq r\end{aligned}\right\}\Rightarrow\left\|{{x+y}\over{2}}-w\right\|\leq\left(1-\delta({r\over R})\right)R.

The space
X
is called strictly convex if the following implication holds for all
x,y,w\in X
and
R>0
:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} \|x-w\|\leq R\\
\|y-w\|\leq R\\
x\neq y\end{aligned}\right\}\Rightarrow\left\|{{x+y}\over{2}}-w\right\|<R.

If
A\subset X
, by
A-A
we mean the set
\{x-y;x,y\in A\}
. A metric space
X
is said to be boundedly compact if every bounded sequence in
X
has a convergent subsequence.

1.1.
Notion of cyclic
p
-
\varphi
-contraction mapping

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A_{1}
,
A_{2},\cdots,A_{m}
be nonempty subsets of a metric space
(X,d)
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
we define
d_{p}(A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{m})
to be

d_{p}(A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{m}):=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}d(A_{i},A_{i+1})^{p}\right)^{1\over p},

if
1\leq p<\infty
and

d_{\infty}(A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{m}):=\max\{d(A_{1},A_{2}),d(A_{2},A_{3}),\cdots,d(A_{m},A_{1})\}.

Note that in this notation, we in fact regard
A_{1}
,
A_{2},\cdots,A_{m}
as a chain of subsets of
X
. For
x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}\in X
,
d_{p}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m};y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})
is defined as follows:
for
1\leq p<\infty

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m};y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}):=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}d(x_{i},y_{i+1})^{p}\right)^{1\over p}

where
y_{m+1}=y_{1}
, and

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m};y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}):=\max\{d(x_{1},y_{2}),d(x_{2},y_{3}),\cdots,d(x_{m},y_{1})\}.

We agree to use the notation

d_{p}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})=d_{p}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m};x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}).

A mapping
T:X\to X
is said to be cyclic if
T(A_{i})\subset A_{i+1}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,m
with
A_{m+1}=A_{1}
. For a strictly increasing function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
T
is said to be cyclic
p
-
\varphi
-contraction if it is cyclic and satisfies

(1)

\displaystyle d_{p}(Tx_{1},\cdots,Tx_{m};Ty_{1},\cdots,Ty_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m};y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})

\displaystyle-\varphi(d_{p}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m};y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}))

\displaystyle+\varphi(d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}))

for all
x_{i},y_{i}\in A_{i}
where
i=1,2,\cdots,m
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if
\varphi
is defined to be
\varphi(t)=\alpha t
, then a cyclic
p
-
\varphi
-contraction is called cyclic
p
-
\alpha
-contraction.

In what follows, we assume that
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a strictly increasing function,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
A_{1}
,
A_{2},\cdots,A_{m}
are nonempty subsets of a metric space
(X,d)
, and
T:\cup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\to\cup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
is a cyclic
p
-
\varphi
-contraction, which means that for
i=1,\cdots,m
we have that
T(A_{i})\subset A_{i+1}
with
A_{m+1}=A_{1}
and
T
satisfies (
1
).

Remark 1.2
.

Note that, for a cyclic
p
-
\varphi
-contraction
T:\cup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\to\cup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
if
x_{i}\in A_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,m
, then obviously from (
1
) we have that

(2)

\displaystyle d_{p}(Tx_{1},\cdots,Tx_{m};Ty_{1},\cdots,Ty_{m})\leq d_{p}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m};y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}).

Thus, if
x_{0}\in A_{1}
and
x_{n}:=Tx_{n-1}
, then for all natural numbers
n
and
k
we have that

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{n},x_{n+1},\cdots,x_{n+m-1};x_{k},x_{k+1},\cdots,x_{k+m-1})\leq

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{n-1},x_{n},\cdots,x_{n+m-2};x_{k-1},x_{k},\cdots,x_{k+m-2}),

which obviously implies that

d_{p}(x_{n},x_{n+1},\cdots,x_{n+m-1})\leq d_{p}(x_{n-1},x_{n},\cdots,x_{n+m-2}).

Throughout the paper we assume that
x_{0}\in A_{1}
and
x_{n}=Tx_{n-1}
where
T
is a cyclic
p
-
\varphi
-contraction.

is a cyclic
p
-
\varphi
-contraction mapping and
x_{0}\in A_{1}
and

for all natural numbers

Theorem 2.1
.

d_{p}(x_{n},x_{n+1},\cdots,x_{n+m-1})\to d_{p}(A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{m})

as

Theorem 2.2
.

If
\{x_{mn}\}_{n\geq 0}
has a convergent subsequence in
A_{1}
, then there exists
x\in A_{1}
so that

d_{p}(x,Tx,\cdots,T^{m-1}x)=d_{p}(A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{m}).

Proof.

We may assume that
\{x_{mn}\}_{n\geq 0}
is convergent and
x_{mn}\to x
as
n\to\infty
. First assume that
p=\infty
. Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(x,x_{mn+1},x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{d(x,x_{mn+1}),d(x_{mn+1},x_{mn+2}),\cdots,d(x_{mn+m-1},x)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\{d(x,x_{mn})+d(x_{mn},x_{mn+1}),d(x,x_{mn})+d(x_{mn+1},x_{mn+2})

\displaystyle,\cdots,d(x,x_{mn})+d(x_{mn+m-1},x_{mn})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(x,x_{mn})+d_{\infty}(x_{mn},x_{mn+1},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})\to d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}).

It follows that

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(x_{mn},Tx,x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(x_{mn-1},x,x_{mn+1}\cdots,x_{mn+m-2})\to d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}).

Hence
d_{\infty}(x_{mn},Tx,x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})\to d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})
which implies that

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(x,Tx,x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(x,x_{mn})+d_{\infty}(x_{mn},Tx,x_{mn+1}\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})

\displaystyle\to d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

that means
d_{\infty}(x,Tx,x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})\to d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})
. So, by an easy induction we can show that

d_{\infty}(x_{mn},Tx,T^{2}x,\cdots,T^{m-2}x,T^{m-1}x)\to d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}).

This yeilds that

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(x,Tx,T^{2}x,\cdots,T^{m-2}x,T^{m-1}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(x,x_{mn})+d_{\infty}(x_{mn},Tx,T^{2}x,\cdots,T^{m-2}x,T^{m-1}x)

\displaystyle\to d_{\infty}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}).

Now, assume that
1\leq p<\infty
. We have that

\displaystyle d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{p}(x,x_{mn+1},x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{d(x,x_{mn+1})^{p}+d(x_{mn+1},x_{mn+2})^{p}+\cdots+d(x_{mn+m-1},x)^{p}\}^{1\over p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\{(d(x,x_{mn})+d(x_{mn},x_{mn+1}))^{p}+d(x_{mn+1},x_{mn+2})^{p}

\displaystyle+\cdots+(d(x,x_{mn})+d(x_{mn+m-1},x_{mn}))^{p}\}^{1\over p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1\over p}d(x,x_{mn})+d_{p}(x_{mn},x_{mn+1},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})\to d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}),

which implies that
d_{p}(x,x_{mn+1},x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})\to d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})
.
Thus

\displaystyle d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{p}(x,Tx,x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1\over p}d(x,x_{mn})+d_{p}(x_{mn},Tx,x_{mn+1}\cdots,x_{mn+m-2})

\displaystyle\to d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}),

that means
d_{p}(x,Tx,x_{mn+2},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1})\to d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}).
An easy induction shows that

d_{p}(x_{mn},Tx,T^{2}x,\cdots,T^{m-2}x,T^{m-1}x)\to d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

and this yeilds that

\displaystyle d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{p}(x,Tx,T^{2}x,\cdots,T^{m-2}x,T^{m-1}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1\over p}d(x,x_{mn})+d_{p}(x_{mn},Tx,T^{2}x,\cdots,T^{m-2}x,T^{m-1}x)

\displaystyle\to d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}).

∎

\displaystyle\{d(A_{1},,A_{2})^{p}+\cdots+(d(A_{i},A_{i+1})+\varepsilon_{0})^{p}+\cdots+d(A_{m-1},A_{m})^{p}\}^{1\over p}

\displaystyle-\{d(A_{1},,A_{2})^{p}+\cdots+d(A_{i},A_{i+1})^{p}+\cdots+d(A_{m-1},A_{m})^{p}\}^{1\over p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\{d(x_{mk},x_{mk+1})^{p}+\cdots+d(x_{mk+i-1},x_{mk+i})^{p}+\cdots+d(x_{mk+m-1},x_{mk})^{p}\}^{1\over p}

\displaystyle-\{d(A_{1},,A_{2})^{p}+\cdots+d(A_{i},A_{i+1})^{p}+\cdots+d(A_{m-1},A_{m})^{p}\}^{1\over p},

which contradicts

∎

Finally, we assume that

. This leads to the following theorem, which is, in some sense, close to the famous Banach fixed point theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
T:\cup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\to\cup_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
be a cyclic
p
-
(1-\alpha)
-contraction for some

. If

is complete and

s are closed subsets of

, then

has a unique fixed point

.

Proof.

Note that the assumption implies that

for

. For a proof we follow the method of the original proof of the Banach fixed point theorem. From the definition we have that

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{mn},x_{mn+1},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1};x_{mn-m},x_{mn-m+1},\cdots,x_{mn-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha d_{p}(x_{mn-1},x_{mn},\cdots,x_{mn+m-2};x_{mn-m-1},x_{mn-m},\cdots,x_{mn-2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}d_{p}(x_{mn-2},x_{mn-1},\cdots,x_{mn+m-3};x_{mn-m-2},x_{mn-m-1},\cdots,x_{mn-3})

.

.

.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{mn-m}d_{p}(x_{m},x_{m+1},\cdots,x_{2m-1};x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1}).\qquad

Hence for positive integers
n
and
k
with
n\geq k
, obviously we have that

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{mn},x_{mn+1},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1};x_{mk},x_{mk+1},\cdots,x_{mk+m-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{mn},x_{mn+1},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1};x_{mn-m},x_{mn-m+1},\cdots,x_{mn-1})

\displaystyle+d_{p}(x_{mn-m},x_{mn-m+1},\cdots,x_{mn-1};x_{mn-2m},x_{mn-2m+1},\cdots,x_{mn-m-1})

.

.

.

\displaystyle+d_{p}(x_{mk+m},x_{mk+m+1},\cdots,x_{mk+2m-1};x_{mk},x_{mk+1},\cdots,x_{mk+m-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\alpha^{mn-m}+\alpha^{mn-2m}+\cdots+\alpha^{mk}\right)

\displaystyle\times d_{p}(x_{m},x_{m+1},\cdots,x_{2m-1};x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{mk}\left({{1-\alpha^{mn-mk}}\over{1-\alpha}}\right)d_{p}(x_{m},x_{m+1},\cdots,x_{2m-1};x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left({{\alpha^{mk}}\over{1-\alpha}}\right)d_{p}(x_{m},x_{m+1},\cdots,x_{2m-1};x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1}).

Thus, since
0<\alpha<1
, for a given
\varepsilon>0
we can find a positive integer
N
so that for

\displaystyle\left({{\alpha^{mk}}\over{1-\alpha}}\right)d_{p}(x_{m},x_{m+1},\cdots,x_{2m-1};x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1})<\varepsilon.

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a positive integer
N
so that if
n\geq k\geq N
, then

(3)

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{mn},x_{mn+1},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1};x_{mk},x_{mk+1},\cdots,x_{mk+m-1})<\varepsilon.

Now, we claim that

(
\ast
) for
i=1,\cdots,m
and for
\varepsilon>0
there exists a positive integer
N
such that if
n>k\geq N
then
d(x_{mn+i-1},x_{mk+i})<\varepsilon.

Assume that (
\ast
) is not true for some
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
. Hence there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}
so that for any positive integer
t
there exist
n_{t}\geq k_{t}\geq t
such that
d(x_{mn_{t}+i-1},x_{mk_{t}+i})\geq\varepsilon_{0}.
But by (
3
) there exists a positive integer
N
so that if
n\geq k\geq N
, then

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{mn},x_{mn+1},\cdots,x_{mn+m-1};x_{mk},x_{mk+1},\cdots,x_{mk+m-1})<\varepsilon_{0}.

Pick

we have that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(x_{mn_{N}+i-1},x_{mk_{N}+i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{mn_{N}},x_{mn_{N}+1},\cdots,x_{mn_{N}+m-1};x_{mk_{N}},x_{mk_{N}+1},\cdots,x_{mk_{N}+m-1})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0};

a contradiction that ensures the validity of (
\ast
). But,
(
\ast
) implies that

is in fact a Cauchy sequence in

, because of

Since

is complete and

is assumed to be closed in

converges to an element of

,
\hat{x}_{i}
says. On the other hand

which ensures that

for

. Thus

is convergent to an

To show that

\displaystyle d(x,Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(x_{n},x)+d(Tx_{n-1},Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(x_{n},x)+d_{p}(Tx_{n-1},Tx,Tx,\cdots,Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(x,x_{n})+\alpha d_{p}(x_{n-1},x,x,\cdots,x).

But

as

\displaystyle d(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(Tx,Ty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{1}\over{2^{{1}\over{p}}}}d_{p}(Tx,Ty,Tx,Tx,\cdots,Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha{{1}\over{2^{{1}\over{p}}}}d_{p}(x,y,x,\cdots,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha d(x,y).

.
∎

Proposition 3.1

Remark 3.2

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

s be convex subsets with odd indices. Then for each

, there exists a positive integer

\varepsilon_{0}>0
so that for each positive integer

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{mn_{t}},x_{mn_{t}+1},\cdots,x_{mn_{t}+m-1};x_{mk_{t}},x_{mk_{t}+1},\cdots,x_{mk_{t}+m-1})

\displaystyle d_{p}(x_{mn_{t}},x_{mn_{t}+1},\cdots,x_{mn_{t}+m-1};x_{mk_{t}},x_{mk_{t}+1},\cdots,x_{mk_{t}+m-1})

(7)

\displaystyle\to d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})+\varepsilon_{0}.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|x_{mn_{t}+i}-x_{mn_{t}+m+i}\|^{p}\right)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|x_{mn_{t}+m+i}-x_{mk_{t}+m+i+1}\|^{p}\right)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|x_{mk_{t}+m+i+1}-x_{mk_{t}+i+1}\|^{p}\right)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|x_{mn_{t}+1+i}-x_{mk_{t}+1+i+1}\|^{p}\right)^{1\over p}

s with odd indices be convex subsets. Then

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X
be a uniformly convex Banach space and let

be a closed convex subset of

for some

. If
d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})=0
, then

has a unique fixed point

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0

N_{1}
such that

so that
\|x_{mk+i_{0}-1}-x_{mn+i_{0}}\|<\varepsilon

and

, Then

\|x_{mn+i_{0}-1}-x_{mk+i_{0}-1}\|\leq\|x_{mk+i_{0}-1}-x_{mn+i_{0}}\|+\|x_{mn+i_{0}-1}-x_{mn+i_{0}}\|<\varepsilon.

and so it is convergent to an element of

. Using the same mathod we could show that

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

4.

In this section we study the

. In the rest of the paper, we assume that

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose

s are weakly closed subsets of
X
. Then, there exists
(\hat{x}_{1},\hat{x}_{2},\cdots,\hat{x}_{m})\in A_{1}\times A_{2}\times\cdots\times A_{m}
such that

d_{p}(\hat{x}_{1},\hat{x}_{2},\cdots,\hat{x}_{m})=d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}).

Proof.

In the case when
d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})=0
the conclusion is obtained from Theorem
2.9
. So assume that
d_{p}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{m})\neq 0
. By Theorem
2.4
sequences
\{x_{mn+i-1}\}
s are all bounded. Thus these sequences have weakly convergent subsequences. Therefore we could fined the sequence
\{n_{k}\}
of positive integers so that
\{x_{mn_{k}+i-1}\}
s are all convergent. Assume that
x_{mn_{k}+i-1}\xrightarrow{w}\hat{x}_{i}
, with each
\hat{x}_{i}
in
A_{i}
, where
\xrightarrow{w}
stands for weakly convergence. Assume that
1\leq p<\infty
. It follows that
x_{mn_{k}+i-1}-x_{mn_{k}+i}\xrightarrow{w}\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{i+1}\neq 0
. This means that there exist real bounded linear functionals
f_{i}
so that
\|f_{i}\|=1
and
f_{i}(\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{i+1})=\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{i+1}\|.
Note that
\hat{x}_{m+1}=\hat{x}_{1}
.

\lim f_{i}(x_{mn_{k}+i-1}-x_{mn_{k}+i})=f_{i}(\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{i+1})=\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{i+1}\|.

On the other hand

|f_{i}(x_{mn_{k}+i-1}-x_{mn_{k}+i})|\leq\|x_{mn_{k}+i-1}-x_{mn_{k}+i}\|.

Hence by Lemma
3.4
we have that

\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{i+1}\|=\lim f_{i}(x_{mn_{k}+i-1}-x_{mn_{k}+i})\leq\lim\|x_{mn_{k}+i-1}-x_{mn_{k}+i}\|=d(A_{i},A_{i+1}).

Thus
\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{i+1}\|=d(A_{i},A_{i+1})

For the case when

∎

Theorem 4.3

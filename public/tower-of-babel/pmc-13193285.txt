Risk factors for sialocele after parotidectomy
Authors: Chia-Hsien Wu, Hsueh-An Sun, Heng-Jui Hsu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Parotidectomy, the surgical excision of the parotid gland to treat tumors, is frequently complicated by sialocele formation—a subcutaneous accumulation of saliva that presents clinically as a cystic mass. With reported incidences ranging from 0% to 44%, the specific etiology remains incompletely defined. This study aimed to identify independent predictors of postoperative sialocele formation to suggest targeted preventive strategies.Methods:A retrospective cohort study analyzed patients undergoing parotidectomy at a single tertiary center from 2014 to 2020. Data including patient demographics, comorbidities, lifestyle habits, operative details, and postoperative care were collected. The primary clinical outcome was clinically diagnosed sialocele. Independent predictors were evaluated using multivariable logistic regression, calculating adjusted odds ratios (aORs) and 95% CIs.Results:Among 597 subjects (mean age 52.2 ± 14.4 years; 58.5% male), 54 (9.0%) developed a sialocele. Multivariable analysis revealed significant independent predictors: betel nut use (aOR = 2.37; 95% CI, 1.13-4.97; p = 0.023), history of cerebrovascular accident (aOR = 4.10; 95% CI, 1.30-14.86; p = 0.032), surgical drain removal in <3 days (aOR = 1.89; 95% CI, 1.04-3.44; p = 0.036), and a diagnosis of mucoepidermoid carcinoma (aOR = 4.18; 95% CI, 1.37-12.78; p = 0.012). Variables like age, sex, tumor size, and operative method showed no significant association.Conclusion:Betel nut use, a history of stroke, early drain removal, and malignant pathology are significant independent risk factors for postoperative sialoceles. Adjusting perioperative management, particularly delaying drain removal, may lower this risk. Tumor size and operative method did not influence outcomes. Prospective studies are needed to confirm these single-center retrospective findings.

1. INTRODUCTION

Primary salivary gland tumors account for 3% to 10% of all head and neck tumors, with an estimated annual incidence ranging from 0.4 to 13.5 cases per 100 000 individuals.1 The primary objective of parotid gland surgery is to ensure complete tumor excision while preserving the function of the facial nerve.2 Therefore, surgical approaches such as superficial parotidectomy and extracapsular dissection near the tumor capsule have garnered popularity in clinical practice.3,4

Facial nerve injury is an uncommon but serious complication of parotidectomy. Postoperative sialocele remains a challenge for both patients and surgeons. The incidence of fluid collection following parotidectomy ranges from 0% to 44.5%, with resolution often requiring 4 to 6 weeks.5–7 Studies have indicated that the risk of sialocele formation increases with the use of implants, such as hemostatic sponges,8 and with less extensive parotid resections.9 The present study identified additional risk factors for sialocele formation and explored strategies for reducing the risk of sialocele formation.

2. METHODS

This retrospective cohort study included patients who underwent parotid gland surgery at National Cheng Kung University Hospital between January 1, 2014, and December 31, 2020. The median follow-up duration was 8 months. Patients who were lost to follow-up were excluded from the analysis. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of National Cheng Kung University Hospital, Tainan, Taiwan (B-ER-110-354).

2.1. Data collection

Preoperative data, including patient demographics (sex and age), comorbidities, and lifestyle factors (smoking, alcohol consumption, and betel nut use), were systematically extracted from historical records. Intraoperative and postoperative details, including surgical techniques, tumor size, final pathological diagnosis, timing of drainage removal, device selection, and the occurrence of sialoceles or other complications, were also documented. A sialocele was defined in this study as the subcutaneous accumulation of saliva, clinically presenting as a nontender cystic mass.

2.2. Operative methods

The superficial parotidectomy surgical procedure was classified into two primary types: partial and extended. Partial superficial parotidectomy involved the resection of a portion of the superficial lobe, identification of the main trunk of the facial nerve, and dissection of the branches in either the lower or upper division, without extending to the branches of the opposite division. An extended superficial parotidectomy involved resection of part or all of the superficial lobe along with a substantial portion of the deep lobe.9 Other surgical techniques were excluded from the analysis due to their limited occurrence in the cohort.

Postoperative wound drainage was managed using a closed wound drainage system (Pacific Hospital Supply, Miaoli County, Taiwan). In accordance with the protocol, the drain was removed once the daily output was <10 mL.

Treatment for sialoceles was selected on the basis of each operator’s preference and included one or a combination of the following approaches: medication (antihistamines or botulinum toxin injections), fine needle aspiration (FNA), incision and drainage (I&D), or a combination of FNA followed by I&D.

2.3. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed to evaluate the factors associated with sialocele formation following parotidectomy. Continuous variables, such as age and tumor size, were compared using the independent t test, and categorical variables, including sex, comorbidities, and substance use, were analyzed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, depending on data distribution. To identify independent predictors of sialocele formation, a multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted. Adjusted odds ratios (aORs) with 95% CIs were calculated for each variable. A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All data were processed and analyzed using R (version 4.2.1; R Core Team, www.r-project.org).

3. RESULTS

This study included 597 patients, with a mean age of 52.2 ± 14.4 years. The cohort comprised 349 men and 248 women. Benign tumors accounted for 86.6% of cases, with pleomorphic adenoma being the most common subtype (38.5%), followed by Warthin’s tumor (35.0%) and oncocytoma (3.4%). Among malignant tumors, the most frequently observed type was mucoepidermoid carcinoma (3.0%), followed by lymphoma (1.8%) and acinic cell carcinoma (1.3%; Table 1).

Regarding substance use, betel nut consumption was significantly associated with an increased risk of sialocele formation (p = 0.016). No significant associations were observed for smoking (p = 0.215) or alcohol use (p = 0.153). Additionally, leukocytosis identified during the preoperative examination was significantly correlated with sialocele formation (p = 0.039).

Regarding operative parameters, neither mean tumor size (nonsialocele: 29.7 ± 24.7 mm vs sialocele: 28.0 ± 9.8 mm) nor the type of parotidectomy performed (partial superficial parotidectomy vs extended superficial parotidectomy) was associated with sialocele formation. However, early drainage removal (<72 hours postoperatively) was significantly associated with an increased rate of sialocele formation (p = 0.047). Additionally, patients with mucoepidermoid carcinoma exhibited a significantly higher incidence of sialoceles than did those without (p = 0.005).

To identify independent risk factors associated with sialocele formation, a multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted (Table 3). Significant factors included betel nut use (aOR: 2.37; p = 0.023), history of CVA (aOR = 4.10; p = 0.032), early removal of drainage (<72 hours; aOR = 1.89; p = 0.036), and pathological diagnosis of mucoepidermoid carcinoma (aOR = 4.18; p = 0.012).

4. DISCUSSION

Sialocele is a well-documented complication following parotidectomy, with reported incidence rates ranging from 0% to 44%. In the present study, the incidence of sialocele formation was 9.0%, which is consistent with reported findings. This variability in reported rates may be attributed to differences in surgical techniques, tumor location, the rigor of sialocele detection, or variations in the criteria used to define clinically relevant fluid collection.7,10,11

The relationship between sex or age and sialocele formation remains a topic of debate.5,6,8,12 Kim et al13 reported an increased risk of sialoceles in male patients. In our study, no significant association was observed between either sex or age and sialocele formation. Regarding underlying comorbidities, consistent with findings from Lee et al,14 no significant differences were observed between the nonsialocele and sialocele groups for DM, HTN, and CKD. However, CVA was significantly associated with sialocele formation, with a higher prevalence observed in the sialocele group than in the nonsialocele group (p = 0.003). This finding may be attributed to the potential poor oral hygiene in patients with CVA,15 which has been reported as a potential risk factor for parotid sialocele formation.16

Substance use has been extensively examined in relation to sialocele formation, with smoking consistently demonstrating no significant association with the risk of sialocele formation across multiple studies.10,12,14 The effects of alcohol and betel nut consumption remain underreported in the literature. In the present study, betel nut use was significantly associated with sialocele formation (p = 0.016). Neither smoking nor alcohol consumption was significantly associated with sialocele formation (alcohol consumption: p = 0.153). Betel nut, derived from the fruit of the Areca palm and primarily cultivated in South and Southeast Asia, is well documented for its adverse health effects and carcinogenic properties.17–19 Its detrimental effect on oral hygiene is notable.20,21 Betel nut may contribute to sialocele formation.16

In the present study, neither the operative methods used nor tumor size demonstrated significant associations with the risk of sialocele formation. These findings contrast with those of Liu et al, who identified tumors located in the middle portion of the parotid gland as a predictive factor for postoperative sialocele formation. Tumor location was not examined in the present analysis. The early removal of negative-pressure drainage, particularly within 72 hours postoperatively, was a significant predictor of sialocele formation. Similarly, Coniglio et al22 reported a higher incidence of sialocele in drainless parotidectomy than in procedures with drainage placement (9.5% vs 3.8%). By contrast, Liu et al23 reported no significant correlation between early drainage removal and sialocele formation, highlighting discrepancies in the literature that warrant further investigation. In our department, the current protocol involves removing drains once the daily output drops below <10 mL, irrespective of the duration of placement. However, the present findings may prompt a critical review or revision of this approach in the future.

A higher incidence of mucoepidermoid carcinoma was observed in the sialocele group (aOR = 4.18; p = 0.012) than in the nonsialocele group. No significant correlation was identified between benign pathological diagnoses and sialocele development. These findings align with those of Alqaryan et al,11 who reported an association between malignant diagnoses and postoperative complications, including saliva fistulas and sialoceles. The mechanism underlying the increased risk of sialocele in patients with malignant pathology remains unclear.

This study has several limitations. First, the patient cohort was derived from a single center and retrospectively analyzed. Therefore, some potentially valuable variables may not have been captured. Nevertheless, the absence of these data should not have substantially influenced the primary interpretation of the study findings. Second, the retrospective design inherently limits the ability to establish causal relationships between the identified risk factors and sialocele formation. Additional prospective studies are required to validate these findings and establish definitive causative associations.

In conclusion, this study identified key factors associated with the development of postoperative sialoceles following parotidectomy. Variables such as age, sex, tumor size, and surgical technique were not significantly correlated with sialocele formation. Betel nut use, history of CVA, early drainage removal, and the presence of mucoepidermoid carcinoma were identified as independent predictors. These findings offer valuable insights into risk stratification and management of sialoceles, underscoring the importance of tailored postoperative care and drainage protocols.

An improved upper bound for the Froude number of irrotational solitary water waves
Evgeniy Lokharu, Jörg Weber

Abstract
. A classical and central problem in the theory of water waves is to classify parameter regimes for which nontrivial solitary waves exist. In the two-dimensional, irrotational, pure gravity case, the Froude number Fr (a non-dimensional wave speed) plays the central role. So far, the best analytic result Fr < 2 \textrm{Fr}<\sqrt{2} was obtained by Starr [ 16 ] , while the numerical evidence of Longuet-Higgins & Fenton [ 12 ] states Fr ≤ 1.294 \textrm{Fr}\leq 1.294 . On the other hand, as shown recently by Kozlov [ 9 ] , the upper bound Fr < 1.399 \textrm{Fr}<1.399 is related to the existence of subharmonic bifurcations of Stokes waves. In this paper, we develop a new strategy utilizing the flow force function and rigorously establish the improved upper bound Fr < 1.37838 \textrm{Fr}<1.37838 .



1.
Introduction

The mathematical theory of solitary waves has a long and well-known history going all the way back to the discovery of John Scott Russell in 1844. We refer to
[
15
]
for an overview of early works on solitary waves. A two-dimensional solitary wave is characterized by the surface profile
y=\eta(x-ct)
that describes a fluid elevation localized in space. More precisely, the limits of
\eta(x)
as
x\to\pm\infty
exist and are equal to the unperturbed depth level
d
. Then, following
[
5
]
, we define the Froude number of a traveling solitary wave as

\textrm{Fr}=\frac{c}{\sqrt{gd}},

where
g
is the gravitational constant. It is well known that solitary waves are absent in the parameter regime
\textrm{Fr}\leq 1
; see
[
16
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
11
]
. As for the upper bounds, we recall Starr
[
16
]
, who derived the striking identity

\textrm{Fr}^{2}=1+\frac{3}{2d}\frac{\int(\eta-d)^{2}}{\int(\eta-d)}.

Starting from here and estimating the wave amplitude
a
from the Bernoulli equation, it is easy to obtain the upper bound
\textrm{Fr}<2
. Starr immediately improves this result by showing that

(1)

\textrm{Fr}^{2}<1+\frac{a}{d},

which implies
\textrm{Fr}<\sqrt{2}\approx 1.41421
; see
[
8
]
for another derivation. Surprisingly, the inequality
\textrm{Fr}<\sqrt{2}
has remained the best analytic result so far, although the numerical evidence from
[
12
]
(see also
[
14
]
) suggests a significantly better estimate
\textrm{Fr}\leq 1.294
. The question of finding an improved bound
\textrm{Fr}<1.399
was recently re-emphasized in
[
9
]
, relating it to the existence of subharmonic bifurcations of Stokes waves. Thus, the goal of this paper is to improve previous rigorous inequalities by proving the following main result.

Theorem 1
.

The Froude number of any solitary wave is bounded from above by
1.37838
.

Our argument is completely different and is not based on any integral identities, but on estimates of the flow force constant and the flow force function. We also obtain several new general inequalities of separate interest; see Lemmas
2
,
3
and
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

In terms of non-dimensional variables as in
[
7
]
, the two-dimensional, irrotational water wave problem reads

(2a)

\displaystyle\Delta\psi

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }0<y<\eta(x),

(2b)

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{2}+y

\displaystyle=r

\displaystyle\text{on }y=\eta(x),

(2c)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\text{on }y=\eta(x),

(2d)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }y=0.

Here, the mass flux and the gravitational constant were scaled to unity. The stream function
\psi
gives rise to the (relative) velocity field
(-\psi_{y},\psi_{x})
(in the frame co-moving with the wave). The fluid domain is
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<y<\eta(x)\}
, where the graph of
\eta
describes the free surface
y=\eta(x)
. Moreover,
r
is referred to as the Bernoulli constant. In what follows we only require
\psi_{y}
to be continuous within the fluid domain up to the boundary, while the surface profile is just continuous. In particular, all our results will be valid for extreme waves.

In this paper we are concerned with solitary wave solutions of (
2
) that can be characterized by surface profiles
\eta
such that
\eta(x)\to d
as
x\pm\infty
. In fact, any solitary wave is a wave of elevation with
\eta(x)>d
, while the symmetric surface profile
\eta
is strictly monotonically decreasing for
x>0
(and increasing for
x<0
); for more details see
[
4
]
and
[
11
]
. The crest is located at the vertical
x=0
and we denote

\hat{\eta}:=\max\eta=\eta(0).

Given any Bernoulli constant
r>1.5
one finds two conjugate streams with depths
d_{-}(r)
and
d_{+}(r)
that are the unique roots of the

(3)

\frac{1}{2d^{2}}+d=r.

Note that the assumption
r>1.5
is valid for all water waves; check
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
. Thus, any solitary water wave is subject to the bounds

(4)

d=d_{-}(r)<1<d_{+}(r)<\hat{\eta}\leq r.

See
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
for more details. Finally, we note that the Froude number
Fr
can be computed as

\textrm{Fr}=d^{-3/2}>1.

Another important quantity, introduced in
[
3
]
, is the flow force constant given by

(5)

S:=\int_{0}^{\eta}\left(\frac{\psi_{y}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\psi_{x}^{2}}{2}+r-y\right)\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{d^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{d};

here, the first expression is easily seen to be independent of
x
, just by means of (
2
), while the identity follows by taking the limits
x\to\pm\infty
. As for an upper bound for the Froude number, let us present the following simple argument which involves
S
and leads to the inequality
\textrm{Fr}<\sqrt{2}
, and is in fact very similar to an argument in
[
8
]
.

We apply (
5
) right below the crest (i.e., for
x=0
) and use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (noting that
\psi_{y}
is not constant)

(6)

1=\int_{0}^{\hat{\eta}}\psi_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y<\sqrt{\hat{\eta}}\left(\int_{0}^{\hat{\eta}}\psi_{y}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/2}

to estimate

(7)

S=r\hat{\eta}-\frac{\hat{\eta}^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\hat{\eta}}\psi_{y}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y>r\hat{\eta}-\frac{\hat{\eta}^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2\hat{\eta}}=:\mathcal{S}(\hat{\eta}).

A direct computation reveals that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(t)=0
only for
t=d
and
t=d_{+}
. Thus, by (
4
) and since
\mathcal{S}
is decreasing for
t>d_{+}
, we obtain

(8)

S>\mathcal{S}(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2r}.

In combination with the identities (
3
) and (
5
), one immediately finds that this inequality is only true if
\textrm{Fr}<\sqrt{2}
. Furthermore, one can plug (
3
), (
5
) and
\hat{\eta}=d+a
into (
7
) to obtain Starr’s inequality (
1
), and that is completely avoiding any integral identities. Let us also remark that the bound
\textrm{Fr}<\sqrt{2}
yields the upper bound
r<4^{1/3}\approx 1.5874
via (
3
) and (
5
); therefore, throughout this paper it is sufficient to consider

(9)

r\in(1.5,4^{1/3}).

Our proof of the main theorem is based on improving the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (
6
) or, equivalently, on estimating the integral

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\hat{\eta}}\left(\psi_{y}-\frac{1}{\hat{\eta}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

from below, where
1/\hat{\eta}
is the average of
\psi_{y}
. Here, one has to consider several cases: the wave is far from stagnation, so that
\hat{\eta}\leq 0.95r
; moderate distance from stagnation:
0.95r<\hat{\eta}\leq(1-\beta)r
for some small
\beta\leq 0.05
; and the close to stagnation case
\hat{\eta}>(1-\beta)r
and
\beta\leq 0.05
. Then we will numerically identify the best value of
\beta
, leading to the best overall estimate for the Froude number.

3.
Some lemmas

In the following, we make use of the flow force function

F:=\int_{0}^{y}\left(\frac{\psi_{y}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\psi_{x}^{2}}{2}+r-y^{\prime}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime},

defined in the fluid domain and satisfying

(10)

\Delta F=-1\text{ in }0<y<\eta(x);\quad F=S\text{ on }y=\eta(x);\quad F=0\text{ on }y=0.

Lemma 2
.

For an arbitrary solution, we have

(11)

\frac{1}{2}\psi_{y}^{2}(x,0)\geq\frac{S}{r}-\frac{r}{2}

everywhere at the bottom.

Proof.

We consider the following harmonic function

h=F-\left(-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+\mu y\right),\ \ \mu=\frac{S}{\hat{\eta}}+\frac{\hat{\eta}}{2}.

Note that
-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+\mu y
is increasing on the interval
[0,\hat{\eta}]
, since the derivative is zero only for
y=\mu
but

(12)

\mu\geq\frac{S}{r}+\frac{r}{2}>r;

this inequality follows from the definition of
\mu
and its monotonicity in
\hat{\eta}
on the interval
[0,\sqrt{2S}]
, which contains
r
by (
8
).

Thus, we see that
h
is non-negative at the surface by (
10
) and the definition of
\mu
, so it attains its minimum everywhere at the bottom, where
h=0
. Now, the weak maximum principle and (
12
) give the desired inequality.
∎

Lemma 3
.

The following estimate

(13)

\frac{1}{2}\psi_{y}^{2}(0,y)\leq r-y-d(1-\psi)

holds true everywhere below the crest.

Proof.

We consider the function

f=\frac{F_{y}+d(1-\psi)}{r-y}.

Note that

\Delta((r-y)f)=0

and so
f
is subject to the maximum principle. At the surface we have

f=\frac{2}{1+\eta^{\prime 2}}\leq 2\ \ \text{on}\ \ y=\eta(x)

by (
2b
) and (
2c
), while at the bottom one finds

f=1+\frac{\psi_{y}^{2}(x,0)}{2r}+\frac{d}{r}\leq 1+\frac{1}{2rd^{2}}+\frac{d}{r}=2\ \ \text{on}\ \ y=0

by (
2d
) and (
3
). Thus, we conclude
f\leq 2
everywhere inside the fluid domain and using the definition of
F
then leads to (
13
).
∎

Lemma 4
.

The following estimate

(14)

\psi_{y}(0,y)\geq\frac{\psi_{y}(0,0)}{\sqrt{r}}\sqrt{r-y}

holds true everywhere below the crest.

Proof.

To verify the result we need to show that the function

g=\frac{F_{y}}{r-y}

attains its minimum at the bottom, right below the crest. If so, then (
14
) follows directly from the definition of
F
. Thus, we assume the contrary and the minimum of
g
is at the surface (note that
\psi_{y}(x,0)
attains its minimum right below the crest). Note that
|\eta^{\prime}|\leq 0.60442942
, which corresponds to the upper bound
31.15^{\circ}
for the slope obtained in
[
1
]
. This forces

g=\frac{2}{1+\eta^{\prime 2}}\geq 1.46484

at the surface and so, by the weak maximum principle,

(15)

\frac{1}{2}\psi_{y}^{2}(0,y)\geq 0.46484(r-y),\ \ y\in[0,\hat{\eta}].

In particular, we have

\psi_{y}(0,0)\geq 0.9641\sqrt{r}.

Now, the function
\psi-0.9641\sqrt{r}y
cannot attain its maximum at the bottom (which would violate Hopf’s lemma), so that
1-0.9641\sqrt{r}d>0
. Recalling (
3
), this implies
d<0.83197
. In its turn this forces
\hat{\eta}>0.928r
, because otherwise we have

S>\mathcal{S}(\hat{\eta})>\mathcal{S}(0.928r),

which requires
d>0.8327
. Finally, we use (
15
) to estimate

S\geq 1.46484\left(r\hat{\eta}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\eta}^{2}\right)>1.46484(0.928r^{2}-0.430592r^{2}).

This inequality is false for all
d\in[0,1]
, leading to a contradiction. Thus, we have verified that the minimum of
g
has to be attained at the bottom for all solitary waves. This finishes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to combine the previous results presenting a proof of the main theorem.

4.
A proof of Theorem
1

Below we will obtain estimates for the Froude number of arbitrary solitary wave solutions, by interpolating between three cases: when a wave is very far from, moderately far from, and close to stagnation. A slightly better estimate is found for extreme waves.

Throughout the proof, we only estimate quantities below the crest line, that is, all quantities are understood to be evaluated at
x=0
.

4.1.
The case
\hat{\eta}\leq 0.95r

We use (
7
) and the monotonicity property of
\mathcal{S}
to find
S>\mathcal{S}(0.95r)
, which implies
d>0.82062
and thus
\textrm{Fr}<1.34521
. Here and in the following, we write any final inequality, which depends on
S
,
r
and
d
, only in terms of
d
by means of (
3
) and (
5
), and study the resulting inequality for
d
– consisting only of explicit analytic expressions in
d
– numerically.

4.2.
The case
0.95r<\hat{\eta}\leq(1-\beta)r
and
\beta\leq 0.05
.

Here we will exploit the inequality

\psi_{y}(0,y)\leq\sqrt{2(r-y)}

for reasons of computational convenience, although it is not optimal. In the next section we will obtain a slightly sharper result for waves close to stagnation based on the improved inequality (
13

First, we estimate

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\hat{\eta}}+r\hat{\eta}-\frac{\hat{\eta}^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\hat{\eta}}(\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\hat{\eta}}+r\hat{\eta}-\frac{\hat{\eta}^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{0}}(\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{\hat{\eta}}(\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y=

\psi_{y}(0,y_{0})=\frac{1}{\hat{\eta}},\ \ \sqrt{2(r-y_{1})}=\frac{1}{\hat{\eta}};

\psi_{y}(0,y)
is strictly decreasing in
y

(16)

\int_{0}^{\hat{\eta}}(\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}y=0

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2y_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{y_{0}}|\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1}|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2y_{1}}\left(\int_{0}^{y_{0}}|\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1}|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2y_{1}}\left(\int_{y_{0}}^{\hat{\eta}}|\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1}|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2y_{1}}\left(\int_{y_{1}}^{\hat{\eta}}\left(\hat{\eta}^{-1}-\sqrt{2(r-y)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(r-\frac{1}{2\hat{\eta}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{\hat{\eta}-r}{\hat{\eta}}+\frac{1}{2\hat{\eta}^{3}}-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\left((2\hat{\eta}^{2})^{-3/2}-(r-\hat{\eta})^{3/2}\right)\right)^{2}=:J_{1}(\hat{\eta},r).

The second integral can be estimated directly as

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{\hat{\eta}}(\hat{\eta}^{-1}-\sqrt{2(r-y)})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{\hat{\eta}}\left(\frac{1}{\hat{\eta}^{2}}-\frac{2\sqrt{2(r-y)}}{\hat{\eta}}+2(r-y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\hat{\eta}-r}{\hat{\eta}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\hat{\eta}^{4}}-\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\hat{\eta}}\left((2\hat{\eta}^{2})^{-3/2}-(r-\hat{\eta})^{3/2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{4\hat{\eta}^{4}}-(r-\hat{\eta})^{2}\right)\right)=:J_{2}(\hat{\eta},r).

Combining the two results, we conclude

S\geq\frac{1}{2\hat{\eta}}+r\hat{\eta}-\frac{\hat{\eta}^{2}}{2}+J_{1}+J_{2}=:J(\hat{\eta},r).

\hat{\eta}

4.3.
The case
\hat{\eta}>(1-\beta)r
and
\beta\leq 0.05
.

Let us assume
\hat{\eta}>(1-\beta)r
, for some small
\beta
. Then we can use Lemma
4
to estimate

1-\psi(0,y)=\int_{y}^{\hat{\eta}}\psi_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\geq\int_{y}^{(1-\beta)r}\psi_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\geq\frac{2\psi_{y}(0,0)}{3\sqrt{r}}\left((r-y)^{3/2}-(\beta r)^{3/2}\right)

for
y\in[0,(1-\beta)r]
. We use this inequality in (
13
) to obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{y}^{2}(0,y)

\displaystyle\leq 2(r-y)-\frac{4d\psi_{y}(0,0)}{3\sqrt{r}}\left((r-y)^{3/2}-(\beta r)^{3/2}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\leq 2(r-y)\left(1-\frac{2d\sqrt{2\left(\frac{S}{r}-\frac{r}{2}\right)}}{3\sqrt{r}}\left((r-y)^{3/2}-(\beta r)^{3/2}\right)(r-y)^{-1}\right)=:

\displaystyle=:2(r-y)(1-A(r-y))

through (
11

\psi_{y}(0,y)\leq\sqrt{2(r-y)}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}A(r-y)\right)=:\phi(y),\ \ y\in[0,(1-\beta)r].

Let
y_{0}\in(0,\hat{\eta})
be such that
\psi_{y}(0,y_{0})=\hat{\eta}^{-1}
. First, we note that
r-y_{0}\geq(2\hat{\eta}^{2})^{-1}\geq(2r^{2})^{-1}
. Then, using monotonicity of

, we conclude

r-y_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{1}{2}A(r-y_{0})\right)^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{1}{2}A((2r^{2})^{-1})\right)^{2}},

leading to

y_{0}\leq r-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{1}{2}A((2r^{2})^{-1})\right)^{2}}=:y_{1}.

\phi(y)<r^{-1}
for all

, while
y_{1}<(1-\beta)r
. The last inequality holds true whenever
\beta<(2r^{3})^{-1}
, which is granted by the assumption
\beta\leq 0.05

. On the other hand, we have
\phi((1-\beta)r)<r^{-1}

\phi(y)<r^{-1}
for all
y\in[y_{1},(1-\beta)r]

y_{2}\in(0,(1-\beta)r)
so that
\phi(y_{2})=r^{-1}
. But again, since
\phi((1-\beta)r)<r^{-1}
, then the inequality
\phi(y)<r^{-1}
must be true for all
y\in(0,(1-\beta)r)
.

Next we write

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\hat{\eta}}+r\hat{\eta}-\frac{\hat{\eta}^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\hat{\eta}}(\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{0}}(\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{(1-\beta)r}(\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y=

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2r}+I_{1}+I_{2}.

For the first integral, recalling (
16
), we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2y_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{y_{0}}|\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1}|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2y_{1}}\left(\int_{0}^{y_{0}}|\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1}|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2y_{1}}\left(\int_{y_{0}}^{\hat{\eta}}|\psi_{y}-\hat{\eta}^{-1}|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2y_{1}}\left(\int_{y_{1}}^{(1-\beta)r}\left(r^{-1}-\phi(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}.

The right-hand side here is an expression of only
S
,
r
,
d
and
\beta
.

Similarly, we estimate
I_{2}
as follows:

I_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{(1-\beta)r}(r^{-1}-\phi(y))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y.

S\geq\frac{r^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{1}{2y_{1}}\left(\int_{y_{1}}^{(1-\beta)r}\left(r^{-1}-\phi(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{y_{1}}^{(1-\beta)r}(r^{-1}-\phi(y))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y=:\bar{J}(d,\beta).

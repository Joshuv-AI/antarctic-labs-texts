Anisotropic mean curvature flow with contact angle and Neumann boundary conditions in arbitrary dimensions
Can Cui, Nung Kwan Yip

Abstract
Over a bounded strictly convex domain in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with smooth boundary, we establish a priori gradient estimate for an anisotropic mean curvature flow with prescribed contact angle and Neumann boundary conditions. The estimates require careful analysis of the degeneracy property of the anisotropic mean curvature operator. As a result, for both problems, we can infer that the solutions converge to one that is translation invariant in time. Keywords : Anisotropic mean curvature flow, contact angle, Neumann problem, gradient estimate, asymptotic behavior.



1
Introduction

Mean curvature flow is a well known evolution equation for a hypersurface
M(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
in which each point
X(t)\in M(t)
moves with a velocity given by the mean curvature vector
\vec{H}
:

\frac{\partial X}{\partial t}=\vec{H}(X,t).

(1.1)

The concept of mean curvature is used in a large variety of applications, from modeling the behavior of interfaces in materials, formation of microstructures in material science to image processing and computer graphics. See
[
20
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
12
]
for some expositions of these applications.

In this paper, we concentrate on the case when
M(t)
is given by the graph of a function
u
over a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
:
M(t)=\{(x,u(x,t)):x\in\Omega\}
.
Then (
1.1
) can be represented by the following
non-parametric form:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}.

(1.2)

(In the above,
1\leq i,j\leq n
, and we have used the Einstein’s convention of summation over repeated indices.)
This equation also arises as the
L^{2}
-gradient flow for the surface area functional:

E(u)=\int\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}dx,

in the sense that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-\nabla_{L^{2}}E(u)
.

Over the years, various results have been obtained for the mean curvature flow
(
1.1
) and (
1.2
) – see for example
[
13
,
14
]
.
In this paper, we focus on some boundary value problems associated with (
1.2
). In particular, we analyze the contact angle and Neumann boundary conditions over a convex domain
\Omega
.

The contact angle
\theta
is the angle between the tangent plane of the function
u
and the vertical plane over the domain boundary,
\partial\Omega
. Physically, if the graph of
u
represents the surface of a liquid region and the solid phase is bounded by a vertical wall along
\partial\Omega
, then
\theta
is the angle between the liquid and solid phases at their interface.
The contact angle thus describes how a liquid interacts with a solid surface. If
\theta<\pi/2
, then the liquid spreads out on the surface (wetting) while if
\theta>\pi/2
, then the liquid tends to form a droplet (non-wetting) - see Figure
1
. This is a result of the balance between the adhesive (between the liquid and solid) and cohesive (within the liquid) forces. For more details on the physical background, see
[
11
,
15
]
.

Figure 1:
Left:
\theta<\pi/2
, liquid spreads out, Right:
\theta>\pi/2
, droplet.

The above description motivates the following contact angle boundary condition:

D_{N}u=-\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.

(1.3)

where
N
is the inward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
In
[
18
]
, Huisken considered the above problem
with
\theta
fixed to be
\frac{\pi}{2}
essentially corresponding to a Neumann boundary condition. He proved that the solution of (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) converges to a constant function over
\Omega
. In
[
1
]
, Altschuler and Wu considered (
1.2
)-(
1.3
)
when
\Omega
has dimension one. They proved that if
\theta
takes on a value
between
0
and
\pi
, then the solution converges to a solution which translates vertically in time. This result also works for a class of quasilinear equations. Later, in
[
2
]
, they proved the same convergence result over a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\theta
not necessarily a constant. However, their
\theta
can not vary too fast along the domain boundary. In recent years, the work
[
19
]
studied mean curvature flow with Neumann boundary condition and prove that solutions converge to ones moving by vertical translation. After that,
[
16
]
generalized Altschuler and Wu’s result to a domain in higher dimensions, but
\theta
is required to be sufficiently closed to
\frac{\pi}{2}
.

The goal of this paper is to generalize the above results to anisotropic version of
(
1.3
). Similar to the isotropic case, it arises as the
gradient flows for the following area-like functional:

E(u)=\int F(-\nu)\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}dx,

(1.4)

where
\nu=(-Du,1)/\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}
is the upward normal to the graph, and
F(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a positive, convex and homogeneous function of degree one, in the sense that
F(\lambda p)=\lambda F(p)
for all
\lambda\geq 0
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Similar to the isotropic case (where
F(p)=|p|
), the non-parametric (graphical) anisotropic mean curvature flow is derived as:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\ D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F(Du,-1)\ \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}.

(1.5)

In the above, the derivatives
D_{p_{i}p_{j}}
are taken with respect to the first
n
components,
1\leq i,j\leq n
. In the rest of the paper, we write

a^{ij}:=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\ D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F(Du,-1).

(1.6)

We mention here some previous work on anisotropic mean curvature flow
[
5
]
and
[
9
]
. These work do not consider boundary conditions. The recent work
[
8
]
also considers anisotropic flows but on the entire domain
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and with a specific form of the anisotropy.

Anisotropic mean curvature flow has several applications where directional dependence plays a crucial role, distinguishing it from the isotropic case. In crystalline materials, the surface energy often varies by direction due to atomic arrangement. Hence it can accurately capture the evolution of crystal surfaces by accounting for direction-dependent surface energy – see
[
7
]
and
[
23
]
.
In image processing, as in
[
4
]
and
[
21
]
, anisotropic flow enables edge-preserving filtering by smoothing noise in specific directions without blurring important edges.
In fluid dynamics, anisotropic flow models fluid motions in porous or fibrous materials where permeability varies by direction. See
[
17
]
as a reference.

Now we state precisely the questions analyzed in this paper. Consider the following two boundary value problems over a bounded and strictly convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

1.

Prescribed contact angle boundary problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cllcl}u_{t}&=&\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\ D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F(Du,-1)u_{x_{i}x_{j}}&\text{in}&\ Q_{T}=\Omega\times[0,T],\\
D_{N}u&=&-\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}&\text{on}&\ \Gamma_{T}=\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
u(\cdot,0)&=&u_{0}(\cdot)&\text{in}&\ \Omega_{0}=\Omega\times\{0\}.\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

2.

Neumann boundary condition:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cllcl}u_{t}&=&\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\ D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F(Du,-1)u_{x_{i}x_{j}}&\text{in}&\ Q_{T},\\
D_{N}u&=&\varphi&\text{on}&\ \Gamma_{T},\\
u(\cdot,0)&=&u_{0}(\cdot)&\text{in}&\ \Omega_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

This can be viewed as a linearized version – when
Du
is small – of the first contact angle problem.

The main results are a priori gradient estimates for the above boundary value problems.
These in turn prove the long time behavior of the solutions – they converge to a
solution that is translation invariant in time. They thus generalize earlier similar results
in the anisotropic case. Our main assumption is that the anisotropic function
F
does not deviate too much from the isotropic function
\overline{F}(p)=|p|
. See Section
2
for the precise statements on
F
.

In our parallel work
[
10
]
, we solve (
1.7
) for a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
To derive a boundary gradient bound in that case, we differentiated the left-hand side of the governing equation along the tangential and normal directions at a point on
\partial\Omega
. When
n=2
, we could determine the second-order tangential derivative of
u
by differentiating the boundary condition in (
1.7
). By combining this calculation with maximum principle, we established a gradient bound of
u
.
However, when
n>2
, mixed second-order tangential derivatives arise, and they cannot be resolved by the same method. This issue also occurs when solving (
1.8
). To address this, we would need a new approach to derive the gradient estimate in higher dimensions. See Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
4.1
for our main results.

It turns out that the same techniques can also be applied to prove the gradient estimate of the solutions to the following elliptic version (
1.7
) and (
1.8
),

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cllcl}\lambda&=&\sqrt{1+|Dw|^{2}}\ D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F(Dw,-1)w_{x_{i}x_{j}}&\text{in}&\ \Omega,\\
D_{N}w&=&-\sqrt{1+|Dw|^{2}}\cos\theta&\text{on}&\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cllcl}\lambda&=&\sqrt{1+|Dw|^{2}}\ D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F(Dw,-1)w_{x_{i}x_{j}}&\text{in}&\ \Omega,\\
D_{N}w&=&\varphi&\text{on}&\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right..

(1.10)

Once these are established, by following a similar process as in
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
19
]
, we obtain the asymptotic behavior of solutions to (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) which shows that
any solution converges to a vertically translating solution.

2
Preliminary

In this section, we provide some useful properties of the anisotropic function
F
.

2.1
Estimates of homogeneous function

In this section, we focus on properties of homogeneous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Recall that our anisotropic function
F
is assumed to be positive, convex and homogeneous of degree one. For the purpose of distinction, we will use
\overline{F}
to denote the isotropic function

\overline{F}(p)=|p|
.

Throughout this section, the notation
\Big|_{p}
means evaluation of functions at
p
.
For example,
F\Big|_{p}=F(p)
.
We will also decompose a vector
p
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as
p=(p^{\prime},p^{n+1})
where

p^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p^{n+1}\in\mathbb{R}
. From the homogeneous property of the function
F
, we have

\ F\Big|_{p}=DF\Big|_{p}(p),

(2.1)

where
D
refers to taking derivative in a function’s argument. Hence
DF\Big|_{p}(\cdot)
is a linear form on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Similarly
D^{2}F\Big|_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
D^{3}F\Big|_{p}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
are respectively a bilinear form and a 3-way tensor on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We remark that
DF\Big|_{p}
is an
(n+1)
-dimensional vector and
D^{2}F\Big|_{p}
is an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix. Referring to the notation in equation (
1.5
), we use
\big[D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F\big]
to represent the first
n\times n
submatrix of
D^{2}F
.

For the rest of this section, we will let
p=(p^{\prime},-1)
be a fixed vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Due to the homogeneity of
F
, similar to (
2.1
), we have

D^{2}F\Big|_{p}(p,\cdot)=D^{2}F\Big|_{p}(\cdot,p)=0,

(2.2)

D^{3}F\Big|_{p}(p,\cdot,\cdot)=-D^{2}F\Big|_{p}(\cdot,\cdot).

(2.3)

Next, we will state some properties of
D^{2}F\Big|_{p}
and
D^{3}F\Big|_{p}
. For convenience, we introduce some notations that will be used frequently later. Firstly,
we choose an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\{\phi^{1},\cdots,\phi^{n}\}\,\,\,\text{with $\phi^{n}=\frac{p^{\prime}}{|p^{\prime}|}$ for $p^{\prime}\neq 0$.}

(2.4)

Then we define

\tau^{\alpha\beta}:=|p|\ D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta})\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\alpha,\beta=1,\cdots,n.

Due to the homogeneity of
F
, we have
\tau^{\alpha\beta}\sim O(1)
.
Similarly, for
D^{3}F\Big|_{p}
, the following holds:

D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta},\phi^{\gamma})\sim O\left(\frac{1}{|p|^{2}}\right)\quad\alpha,\beta,\gamma=1,\cdots,n.

(2.5)

(In the above and for what follows, we use the convention that
a\sim O(b)
means that there
exists a constant
C
such that
|a|\leq C|b|
.)
However, the above generic property is not sufficient for our purpose.
We need to have more elaborate estimates for the degeneracy of the anisotropic function
F
. This will be carried in the rest of this section.

Our mathematical approach requires us to understand the degeneracy of
D^{2}F
. This is motivated by the
corresponding properties of the isotropic function
\overline{F}
as seen in the following,

|p|D^{2}\overline{F}\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n})=\frac{1}{|p|^{2}}\sim O\left(\frac{1}{|p|^{2}}\right),

(2.6)

which is not of order
O(1)
for
|p|\gg 1
– this is the main reason our operator is not uniformly elliptic. Similar results can also be stated for
D^{3}\overline{F}\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}
.

Throughout our paper, we will assume the following symmetry condition for
F
:

F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}=F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},1)}.

(2.7)

This assumption also appears in
[
9
]
.
The following properties are derived directly from (
2.7
):
for any
q^{\prime},r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have,

DF\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}(0,1)=0,

(2.8)

D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))=0,

(2.9)

D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))=0,

(2.10)

D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))=0.

(2.11)

With the above, we have the following lemmas that quantify various degeneracy properties of
D^{2}F
and
D^{3}F
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C_{1}
such that for any
q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|q^{\prime}|=1
, we have

\left|D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((p^{\prime},0),(q^{\prime},0))\right|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{|p^{\prime}|^{2}}.

(2.12)

Proof.

In the following we introduce a variable
s>0
and
s^{\prime}=\frac{1}{s}
so that
s^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0
as
s\longrightarrow\infty
.
Using equation (
2.9
), we have: when
s\rightarrow\infty
,
s^{\prime}=1/s\rightarrow 0
,

\begin{split}&\ \ \ \ D^{2}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((sp^{\prime},0),(q^{\prime},0))=D^{2}F\Big|_{(\frac{p^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}},-1)}((\frac{p^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}},0),(q^{\prime},0))\\
&=-D^{2}F\Big|_{(\frac{p^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}},-1)}((0,-1),(q^{\prime},0))=D^{2}F\Big|_{(\frac{p^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}},-1)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))\\
&=s^{\prime}D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))\\
&=s^{\prime}\left(D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))-D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))\right)\\
&=s^{\prime 2}\frac{D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))-D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))}{s^{\prime}},\end{split}

so that

|sp^{\prime}|^{2}D^{2}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((sp^{\prime},0),(q^{\prime},0))=|p^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))-D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0))}{s^{\prime}}

which implies that

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}|sp^{\prime}|^{2}D^{2}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((sp^{\prime},0),(q^{\prime},0))=|p^{\prime}|^{2}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,-1),(0,1),(q^{\prime},0)).

Note that by (
2.5
), the right hand side of the above is bounded. Hence

\sup_{s\in[0,+\infty)}|sp^{\prime}|^{2}D^{2}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((sp^{\prime},0),(q^{\prime},0))<\infty,

and (
2.12
) follows by setting
s=1
in the above.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a positive constant
C_{2}
such that for any
q^{\prime},r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|q^{\prime}|=|r^{\prime}|=1
, we have

\left|D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))\right|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{|p^{\prime}|^{3}}.

(2.13)

Proof.

Using equation (
2.10
), we have,

\begin{split}&\ \ \ \ D^{3}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))=D^{3}F\Big|_{(\frac{p^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}},-1)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))\\
&=s^{\prime 2}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))\\
&=s^{\prime 2}\left(D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))-D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))\right)\\
&=s^{\prime 3}\frac{D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))-D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))}{s^{\prime}},\end{split}

and hence

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}|sp^{\prime}|^{3}D^{3}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))=|p^{\prime}|^{3}D^{4}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,-1),(0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0)),

so that the right hand side of which is bounded, i.e.

\sup_{s\in[0,+\infty)}|sp^{\prime}|^{3}D^{3}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))<\infty.

Setting
s=1
gives the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a positive constant
C_{3}
such that

\left|D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1)\right|\leq\frac{C_{3}}{|p^{\prime}|^{3}}.

(2.14)

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the above.
Using equation (
2.11
), we have,

\begin{split}&\ \ \ \ D^{3}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))=D^{3}F\Big|_{(\frac{p^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))\\
&=s^{\prime 2}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))\\
&=s^{\prime 3}\frac{D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-s^{\prime})}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))-D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))}{s^{\prime}},\end{split}

so that

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}|sp^{\prime}|^{3}D^{3}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1)=|p^{\prime}|^{3}D^{4}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},0)}((0,-1),(0,1),(0,1),(0,1)).

Again, the right hand side is bounded

\sup_{s\in[0,+\infty)}|sp^{\prime}|^{3}D^{3}F\Big|_{(sp^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))<\infty.

The claim thus follows as before.
∎

Next we will introduce the following tensor of order three:

T^{3}_{ijl}=-D_{p_{l}}\left(|p|D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F\right)\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}=-|p|D^{3}_{p_{i}p_{j}p_{l}}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}-D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}\frac{p_{l}}{|p|}.

(2.15)

which will be used frequently in the next section.
Note the symmetry property of
T^{3}
:
T^{3}_{ijl}=T^{3}_{jil}
.
The following lemma gives various estimates of
T^{3}
, in particular, its
degeneracy along certain directions.

Lemma 2.4
.

Recall the basis from (
2.4
) with
\phi^{n}=\frac{p^{\prime}}{|p^{\prime}|}
. There exist positive constants
C_{4},C_{5},C_{6}
depending only on
F
such that for any
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1
, we have

|T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta})|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{|p|},

|T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n})|,\ |T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})|,\ |T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha})|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{|p|^{3}},

|T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\beta})|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{|p|}\quad(\alpha\neq\beta),

\frac{C_{5}}{|p|}\leq T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha})\leq\frac{C_{6}}{|p|},

\frac{C_{5}}{|p|^{3}}\leq T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})\leq\frac{C_{6}}{|p|^{3}}.

Proof.

Firstly, from (
2.3
), we calculate the following to apply the results from Lemma
2.1
–
2.3
to
D^{3}F
evaluated on basis elements,

\begin{split}&D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((p^{\prime},0),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))\\
=&-D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0))+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(q^{\prime},0),(r^{\prime},0)).\\
\end{split}

In addition, we have

\begin{split}&D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0))\\
=&-D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0))+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0))\\
&D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0))\\
=&D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(p^{\prime},-1),(p^{\prime},-1))+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(p^{\prime},-1),(0,1))\\
&+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(p^{\prime},-1))+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))\\
=&-2D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1))+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))\\
=&-2D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0))+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1)).\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}&D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0))\\
=&-3D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((p^{\prime},0),(p^{\prime},0))+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1)).\end{split}

To conclude, we rewrite results from Lemma
2.1
to
2.3
with respect to the basis elements
(
2.4
)
\{\phi^{1},\cdots,\phi^{n}\}
as follows:
for
1\leq\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\beta}\leq n
, it holds that

\tau^{\tilde{\alpha}n}=\tau^{n\tilde{\alpha}}=|p|\ D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\tilde{\alpha}},\phi^{n})\sim O\left(\frac{1}{|p|^{2}}\right),

(2.16)

D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{n},\phi^{\tilde{\alpha}},\phi^{\tilde{\beta}})=\frac{1}{|p^{\prime}|}\left(-D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\tilde{\alpha}},\phi^{\tilde{\beta}})+D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),\phi^{\tilde{\alpha}},\phi^{\tilde{\beta}})\right).

(2.17)

In particular, we have

D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})=\frac{1}{|p^{\prime}|}\left(-3D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n})+\frac{1}{|p^{\prime}|^{2}}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),(0,1),(0,1))\right).

(2.18)

From (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), we compute

\displaystyle T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|p|D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n})-\frac{|p^{\prime}|}{|p|}D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{|p|}{|p^{\prime}|}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha})+\frac{1}{|p|^{2}|p^{\prime}|}\tau^{\alpha\alpha}\ ,

\displaystyle T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|p|D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})-\frac{|p^{\prime}|}{|p|}D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{|p|}{|p^{\prime}|}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha})+\frac{1}{|p|^{2}|p^{\prime}|}\tau^{\alpha n}\ ,

\displaystyle T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|p|D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{|p|}{|p^{\prime}|}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha})+\frac{1}{|p^{\prime}|}\tau^{\alpha n}\ ,

\displaystyle T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|p|D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{|p|}{|p^{\prime}|}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1),\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta})+\frac{1}{|p^{\prime}|}\tau^{\alpha\beta}\ ,

\displaystyle T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|p|D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})-\frac{|p^{\prime}|}{|p|}D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|p|}{|p^{\prime}|^{3}}D^{3}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}((0,1)),(0,1),(0,1))+\frac{2|p|^{2}+1}{|p^{\prime}||p|^{2}}\tau^{nn}\ .

Then Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, estimate (
2.16
) and the
homogeneity of
F
suggest that there exists a constant
C_{4}
such that

|T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta})|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{|p|},

|T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n})|,\ |T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})|,\ |T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha})|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{|p|^{3}},

|T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\beta})|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{|p|}\quad(\alpha\neq\beta).

By the fact that
F
is convex and homogeneous of degree one, we have that

\tau^{\alpha\alpha}\geq C
and
\tau^{nn}\geq\frac{C}{|p|^{2}|}
for some positive constant
C
.
Hence there are constants
C_{5}
and
C_{6}
such that

\frac{C_{5}}{|p|}\leq T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha})\leq\frac{C_{6}}{|p|},

\frac{C_{5}}{|p|^{3}}\leq T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})\leq\frac{C_{6}}{|p|^{3}}

completing the proof.
∎

Combining the previous considerations, we can assert that for our anisotropic function
F
,
there are positive constants
c_{1},\ c_{2}
and
c_{3}
such that for any
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1
, with
\alpha\neq\beta

c_{1}\leq\tau^{\alpha\alpha}=|p|D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha})\leq c_{3},\quad\frac{c_{1}}{|p|^{2}}\leq\tau^{nn}=|p|D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n})\leq\frac{c_{3}}{|p|^{2}},

(2.19)

and

|\tau^{\alpha\beta}|=|p|\left|D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta})\right|\leq c_{2},\quad|\tau^{\alpha n}|=|p|\left|D^{2}F\Big|_{(p^{\prime},-1)}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n})\right|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{|p|^{2}}.

(2.20)

The first parts of (

2.1
. For later convenience, without loss of generality, we can choose

and

such that

c_{1}\leq C_{5}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,C_{4}\leq c_{2}.

(2.21)

Note that in the isotropic case, we can take

and

Hence the ratio

2.2
Application

In this section, we apply the previous properties of the anisotropic function

F
to the calculations in the derivation of the gradient estimate.
For what follows, for a smooth function
u
, we introduce

p^{\prime}=Du,\,\,\,p=(Du,-1),\,\,\,v=|p|=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}.

Now the orthonormal basis (
2.4
) becomes:

\phi^{1},\cdots,\phi^{n-1},\phi^{n}=\frac{Du}{|Du|},\quad\text{for $Du\neq 0$}.

(2.22)

(Later on, we will in fact just handle the case
|Du|>1
.)
We remark that in the isotropic case
\overline{F}(p)=|p|
, upon writing

G(Du)=\big(|p|\ D^{2}\overline{F}\big)\Big|_{(Du,-1)}
, that is

G(Du)=\left[\delta_{ij}-\frac{u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right]_{n\times n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1-\frac{u_{x_{1}}u_{x_{1}}}{1+|Du^{2}|}&\cdots&-\frac{u_{x_{1}}u_{x_{n}}}{1+|Du|^{2}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\frac{u_{x_{n}}u_{x_{1}}}{1+|Du|^{2}}&\cdots&1-\frac{u_{x_{n}}u_{x_{n}}}{1+|Du|^{2}}\end{array}\right)\ ,

then
\phi^{n}
is the eigenvector of
G
with eigenvalue
\frac{1}{v^{2}}
, while any vector in the orthogonal subspace is an eigenvector of
G
with eigenvalue
1
.

Now the basis
\{\phi^{1},\cdots,\phi^{n}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined earlier induces an orthonormal basis of
n\times n
symmetric matrices with respect to the Frobenius norm –
\|M\|=\sqrt{\text{tr}(M^{T}M)}
:

\begin{split}&\Phi^{\alpha\alpha}=\phi^{\alpha}(\phi^{\alpha})^{T},\ \Phi^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\phi^{\alpha}(\phi^{\beta})^{T}+\phi^{\beta}(\phi^{\alpha})^{T}\right),\quad 1\leq\alpha<\beta\leq n-1,\\
&\Phi^{\alpha n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\phi^{\alpha}(\phi^{n})^{T}+\phi^{n}(\phi^{\alpha})^{T}\right),\ \Phi^{nn}=\phi^{n}(\phi^{n})^{T},\quad 1\leq\alpha\leq n-1.\end{split}

(2.23)

(Note that the number of elements above is
n(n+1)/2
which is exactly the dimension of
n\times n
symmetric
matrices.) With that, the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u
of
u
at any given point can be written as a linear combination of the above
\Phi^{(\cdot,\cdot)}
’s:

D^{2}u=\sum_{1\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq n-1}\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\Phi^{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{1\leq\alpha\leq n-1}\gamma^{\alpha n}\Phi^{\alpha n}+\gamma^{nn}\Phi^{nn}.

(2.24)

For the rest of this paper, we will make the further choice that

\big\{\phi^{\alpha}\big\}_{1\leq\alpha\leq n-1}
are the eigenvectors of
D^{2}u
(orthogonal to
\phi^{n}
).
Then the terms
\gamma^{\alpha\beta}
in (
2.24
) will vanish for
\alpha\neq\beta
so that we have

D^{2}u=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\Phi^{\alpha\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}\gamma^{\alpha n}\Phi^{\alpha n}+\gamma^{nn}\Phi^{nn}.

Next, we want to compute the expression
T^{3}(D^{2}u,V)
for some given vector

V=\sum_{1\leq\beta\leq n-1}\eta^{\beta}\phi^{\beta}+\eta^{n}\phi^{n}

where the functions
\eta^{\beta}
are defined by

\eta^{\beta}=\gamma^{\beta\beta}\rho^{\beta}+\frac{\gamma^{\beta n}}{\sqrt{2}}\rho^{n},\,\,\,\text{for $1\leq\beta\leq n-1$, and}\,\,\,\eta^{n}=\sum_{1\leq\delta\leq n-1}\frac{\gamma^{\delta n}}{\sqrt{2}}\rho^{\delta}+\gamma^{nn}\rho^{n},

with
\rho^{\beta}
and
\rho^{n}
to be given in
Section
3.2
(
3.26
) and Section
4
(
4.22
).
Note that by means of definition (
2.15
) and the symmetry property of

T^{3}
, we can abuse the notation
T^{3}(u_{1}\otimes u_{2},u_{3})
as

T^{3}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
.
With this in mind, we can write

T^{3}(D^{2}u,V)=T^{3}\Big(\sum_{\alpha}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\Phi^{\alpha\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha}\gamma^{\alpha n}\Phi^{\alpha n}+\gamma^{nn}\Phi^{nn},\sum_{\beta}\eta^{\beta}\phi^{\beta}+\eta^{n}\phi^{n}\Big).

(2.25)

We compute this by evaluating
T^{3}
on the basis elements
(\phi^{1},\cdots,\phi^{n})
leading to the following decomposition:

\begin{split}T^{3}(D^{2}u,V)=&\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\eta^{\beta}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta})+\sum_{\alpha}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\eta^{n}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n})\\
&+\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{2}\gamma^{\alpha n}\eta^{\beta}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\beta})+\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{2}\gamma^{\alpha n}\eta^{n}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})\\
&+\sum_{\beta}\gamma^{nn}\eta^{\beta}T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{\beta})+\gamma^{nn}\eta^{n}T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{n}).\end{split}

(2.26)

In order to simplify the upcoming calculations, we define the following quantities:

\begin{split}&T^{3}_{1}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\eta^{\beta}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta}),\quad T^{3}_{2}=\sum_{\alpha}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\eta^{n}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n}),\\
&T^{3}_{3}=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{2}\gamma^{\alpha n}\eta^{\alpha}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha}),\quad T^{3}_{4}=\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\sqrt{2}\gamma^{\alpha n}\eta^{\beta}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\beta}),\\
&T^{3}_{5}=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{2}\gamma^{\alpha n}\eta^{n}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{n}),\\
&T^{3}_{6}=\sum_{\alpha}\gamma^{nn}\eta^{\alpha}T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha}),\quad T^{3}_{7}=\gamma^{nn}\eta^{n}T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{n}),\end{split}

which can be rewritten as

\begin{split}&T^{3}_{1}=\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1}\left(\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\gamma^{\beta\beta}\rho^{\beta}+\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\frac{\gamma^{\beta n}}{\sqrt{2}}\rho^{n}\right)T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\beta}),\\
&T^{3}_{2}=\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\frac{\gamma^{\beta n}}{\sqrt{2}}\rho^{\beta}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n})+\sum_{1\leq\alpha\leq n-1}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\gamma^{nn}\rho^{n}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n}),\\
&T^{3}_{3}=\sum_{1\leq\alpha\leq n-1}\left(\sqrt{2}\gamma^{\alpha n}\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\rho^{\alpha}+(\gamma^{\alpha n})^{2}\rho^{n}\right)T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha}),\\
&T^{3}_{4}=\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1,\alpha\neq\beta}\left(\sqrt{2}\gamma^{\alpha n}\gamma^{\beta\beta}\rho^{\beta}+\gamma^{\alpha n}\gamma^{\beta n}\rho^{n}\right)T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{\beta}),\\
&T^{3}_{5}=\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n-1}\gamma^{\alpha n}\gamma^{\beta n}\rho^{\beta}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{n})+\sum_{1\leq\alpha\leq n-1}\sqrt{2}\gamma^{\alpha n}\gamma^{nn}\rho^{n}T^{3}(\phi^{\alpha},\phi^{n},\phi^{n}),\\
&T^{3}_{6}=\sum_{1\leq\alpha\leq n-1}\left(\gamma^{\alpha\alpha}\gamma^{nn}\rho^{\alpha}+\frac{\gamma^{\alpha n}}{\sqrt{2}}\gamma^{nn}\rho^{n}\right)T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{\alpha}),\\
&T^{3}_{7}=\left(\sum_{1\leq\alpha\leq n-1}\frac{\gamma^{nn}\gamma^{\alpha n}}{\sqrt{2}}\rho^{\alpha}+(\gamma^{nn})^{2}\rho^{n}\right)T^{3}(\phi^{n},\phi^{n},\phi^{n}).\end{split}

(2.27)

With the above formulations, we are now ready to analyze the solutions of our boundary value problems.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cllcl}\lambda&=&\sqrt{1+|Dw|^{2}}\ D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F(Dw,-1)w_{x_{i}x_{j}}&\text{in}&\ \Omega,\\
D_{N}w&=&\varphi&\text{on}&\ \partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.31)

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume
B1
–
B4
and

\epsilon_{2}
is sufficiently small. Then there exists a unique
\lambda
and a corresponding smooth solution
w
for (
4.31

Proof.

Similar to the proof in Theorem
3.3
, we firstly consider the following eigenvalue problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cllcl}\epsilon w^{\epsilon}&=&\sqrt{1+|Dw^{\epsilon}|^{2}}\ D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}F(Dw^{\epsilon},-1)w^{\epsilon}_{x_{i}x_{j}}&\text{in}&\ \Omega,\\
D_{N}w^{\epsilon}&=&\varphi&\text{on}&\ \partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.32)

As in
[
19
]
, we can obtain the existence of the solution to (
4.32
) and a uniform bound to
|\epsilon w^{\epsilon}|
. Then we also apply the same technique as in Theorem
4.1
to the gradient estimate of the solution to (
4.32
).

Define the same auxiliary function
\Psi(x)=\log{|DW|^{2}}+a_{0}h
, where

W=w^{\epsilon}+\varphi h
. Then we consider two cases for the maximum point
x_{0}
of
\Psi(x)
. The first one is that
x_{0}
is on the boundary
\partial\Omega
, for which we can follow the proof in Theorem
4.1
, case 1. The second one is that
x_{0}
is in the interior of
\Omega
. Most steps also follow as in Theorem
4.1
, case 2.

Instead of (
4.10
) in Theorem
4.1
, we only make use of

0\geq a^{ij}\Psi_{x_{i}x_{j}}=a^{ij}\frac{\left(|DW|^{2}\right)_{x_{i}x_{j}}}{|DW|^{2}}-a_{0}^{2}a^{ij}h_{x_{i}}h_{x_{j}}+a_{0}a^{ij}h_{x_{i}x_{j}}.

(4.33)

By differentiating the governing function in (
4.32

a^{ij}\left(|DW|^{2}\right)_{x_{i}x_{j}}=2\left(J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+2\epsilon W_{x_{k}}w^{\epsilon}_{x_{k}}\right),

(4.34)

where
J_{i}^{\prime}s

Then the gradient estimate for

Similar to Theorem

∎

Let

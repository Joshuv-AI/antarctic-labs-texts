Final analysis results from DREAMM-3, a phase 3 study of belantamab mafodotin versus pomalidomide plus dexamethasone in patients with relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma
Authors: Meletios Athanasios Dimopoulos, Vania Hungria, Haitao Meng, Atanas Radinoff, Jian Li, Sosana Delimpasi, Ting Niu, Gabor Mikala, Tamas Masszi, Marcelo Capra, Michael Low, Angelo Maiolino, Yan Xu, Xavier Leleu, Vasiliki Pappa, Dominik Chraniuk, Tatiana Konstantinova, Iurii Osipov, Shawn Hakim, Chris Brawley, Gabriela Tubel, Astrid McKeown, Julia Boyle, Sumita Roy-Ghanta, Katja Weisel
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Targeting B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) has transformed the relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM) landscape with unprecedented overall survival (OS) outcomes versus standard-of-care triplet regimens. Belantamab mafodotin, an anti-BCMA antibody-drug conjugate, has a multimodal mechanism of action involving minimal immunosuppression and no evidence of induction of T-cell exhaustion [1, 2]. DREAMM-3 (NCT04162210) was a confirmatory phase 3 study to support the initial United States Food and Drug Administration and European Medicines Agency authorization of belantamab mafodotin monotherapy for patients with RRMM who had ≥4 prior lines of therapy (LOTs) [3, 4]; approval was withdrawn following DREAMM-3 findings, as the primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) superiority against the doublet comparator, pomalidomide-dexamethasone, was not met [5–7]. Subsequently, belantamab mafodotin combination regimens with bortezomib-dexamethasone (BVd) and pomalidomide-dexamethasone (BPd) have received approval for patients with RRMM who have received ≥1 prior LOT in multiple countries [8]. In the US, BVd was approved for patients with RRMM who have received ≥2 prior LOT [9].

DREAMM-3 post-primary analysis updates showed an improved PFS hazard ratio (HR) (0.90 [95% confidence interval (CI) 0.65–1.24]) [7]. We present the final analysis of DREAMM-3 to inform on the long-term efficacy and safety of belantamab mafodotin monotherapy.

The full methodology for the multicenter, open-label DREAMM-3 trial has been published [7]. Supplementary Figure 1 shows the study design. Briefly, patients were randomized 2:1 to receive single-agent belantamab mafodotin or pomalidomide-dexamethasone until progressive disease, death, unacceptable toxicity, loss to follow-up, or end of study. Full dosing schedules are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Patients had a confirmed MM diagnosis (International Myeloma Working Group [IMWG] criteria) and received ≥2 prior LOTs (including lenalidomide and proteasome inhibitor).

The primary outcome was investigator-assessed PFS (IMWG criteria) [7]. Secondary efficacy outcomes included OS, overall response rate, clinical benefit rate (comprising ≥minimal response), duration of response (DOR), time to progression, and minimal residual disease negativity rate at 10-5 threshold by next-generation sequencing. Secondary safety outcomes included adverse events (AEs) measured by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) and findings during ophthalmic examinations. Prespecified exploratory endpoints included time to best response (TBR) and time from randomization to progression on subsequent line (PFS2). Updated PFS and OS HR for the intention-to-treat populations are reported. As the primary analysis had already concluded without meeting the superiority threshold, the final analysis was descriptive; no formal hypothesis testing was pre-specified.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, applicable International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice Guidelines, and applicable laws and regulations. All protocols and amendments were approved by an institutional review board and independent ethics committee, detailed in the Supplement. All patients provided written informed consent before study enrollment.

In total, 325 patients randomized to the belantamab mafodotin arm (n = 218) or the pomalidomide-dexamethasone arm (n = 107) were included in the final analysis (April 22, 2025). Patients were a median 68 (38–90) years of age (22% [n = 70] were ≥75 years), 57% (n = 184) were male, and 78% (n = 246) were White. Characteristics were balanced between arms. Full patient demographics have been reported [7]. Median (interquartile range) duration of follow-up was 22.2 (7.0–45.7) and 21.9 (8.4–42.8) months for the belantamab mafodotin and pomalidomide-dexamethasone arms, respectively. Belantamab mafodotin-treated patients received a median of 5 treatment cycles over a median (range) of 4.5 (0.4–54.3) months. For pomalidomide-dexamethasone, patients were treated for a median (range) of 5.5 (0.4–55.2) months.

For belantamab mafodotin versus pomalidomide-dexamethasone, respectively, the median PFS at this analysis was 11.2 (95% CI: 6.5, 14.5) and 7.0 (4.6, 10.6) months (HR [95% CI]: 0.87 [0.64, 1.18]); median OS was 24.0 (95% CI: 19.4, 33.2) and 22.9 (16.0, 29.3) months (HR [95% CI]: 0.98 [0.73, 1.30]); median DOR was 25.3 (95% CI: 20.7, 31.8) and 9.9 (7.6, 20.2) months (Supplementary Fig. 2); stringent complete response (9%/3%) and complete response or better with MRD negativity (8%/<1%) rates favored belantamab mafodotin over pomalidomide-dexamethasone (Table 1). Most patients experienced an AE, and ≥66% of patients experienced ≥1 maximum Grade 3–4 AE in both treatment groups (belantamab mafodotin 71%, pomalidomide-dexamethasone 66%) (Table 2). Any grade blood and lymphatic system disorders occurred in both arms (belantamab mafodotin 57%, pomalidomide-dexamethasone 64%), including thrombocytopenia (35%/34%), anemia (29%/34%), and neutropenia (12%/40%). Blurred vision was the most common ocular AE in both treatment groups (belantamab mafodotin 48%, pomalidomide-dexamethasone 7%). Grades for ophthalmic examination findings (OEFs) were investigator-assessed or derived where no investigator-assessed grades were available. Any-grade OEFs were derived by the sponsor in 133/168 (79%) patients who were treated with belantamab mafodotin and who had no missing assessments; of these, 107/133 (80%) were Grade 2+. These were managed with dose delays and dose reductions. The first Grade 2+ occurrence had resolved (recovered to Grade 1 or better) in 98/122 patients (80% of affected patients) by the data cutoff. Median time (range) to resolution of first Grade 2+ event was 68 (2–731) days for patients who received belantamab mafodotin. There were 24 patients treated with belantamab mafodotin who had unresolved Grade 2+ OEFs from their first event at their last visit; patients continued follow-up after OEFs per the clinical study protocol until disease progression, death, loss to follow-up, or the end of the study.

The DREAMM-3 final analysis suggests a consistent trend towards improvement in PFS with monotherapy belantamab mafodotin versus doublet pomalidomide-dexamethasone. DREAMM-3 did not meet the PFS endpoint at the primary analysis (data cutoff: September 12, 2022) as there was no statistically significant difference in PFS between the two treatment groups (HR [95% CI]: 1.03 [0.72, 1.47], p = 0.56). PFS HR showed stepwise improvements at the post-primary (0.90 [95% CI: 0.65–1.24]) and final analyses (0.87 [95% CI: 0.64, 1.18]); this incremental improvement over time is consistent with an immune-mediated response. At the primary analysis, median DOR was not reached (NR, 95% CI: 17.9 months, NR), indicating that responses were durable; with additional follow-up, median DOR was 25.3 months (95% CI: 20.7, 31.8) [7]. Median OS was 24.0 [95% CI:19.4, 33.2] months with belantamab mafodotin and 22.9 [95% CI: 16.0, 29.3] months with pomalidomide-dexamethasone (HR [95% CI]: 0.98 [0.73, 1.30]), showing no OS detriment.

The disease burden among patients who progressed within the first 2 months of treatment in DREAMM-3 was higher with belantamab mafodotin than with pomalidomide-dexamethasone in terms of ECOG PS, number of prior LOTs received, receipt of prior anti-CD38 therapy, sBCMA, and M-protein levels [7]. This imbalance in disease burden in the patients who progressed rapidly with belantamab mafodotin may account for the early crossing of the PFS curves.

Comparison of belantamab mafodotin monotherapy to doublet therapy may have been more appropriate as a test of a non-inferiority hypothesis over the superiority hypothesis tested in the DREAMM-3 trial design. Emerging evidence of an adaptive immune system response following belantamab mafodotin treatment suggests that longer follow-up prior to the primary analysis may have provided a better opportunity for the separation of efficacy endpoints in favor of belantamab mafodotin to be observed, although the non-proportional hazards effects would have remained in place [1, 7]. Aligning with adaptive immunity involvement, deep and durable responses are a hallmark of belantamab mafodotin treatment (observed with monotherapy and combination regimens), and led to improved PFS over time in this study [10–13]. The long median DOR for patients who responded to belantamab mafodotin, and the longer TBR for belantamab mafodotin compared to pomalidomide-dexamethasone, further support the rationale that longer readouts in clinical trials may be necessary for drugs that elicit immune responses [13]. Learnings from the DREAMM-3 trial and the increased understanding of the mechanism of action of belantamab mafodotin have been implemented into other ongoing trials [13].

No new safety signals were identified for belantamab mafodotin after long-term follow-up. Ocular events were manageable with dose reductions/delays. The rate of ocular AEs reported here is similar to previous reports on monotherapy and in combinations [14, 15]. In a previous subgroup analysis of patients who received long-term treatment (≥52 weeks) with belantamab mafodotin, most AEs were reported to have occurred at or before cycle 17 [16]. Despite the need for dose delays and/or reductions, clinical responses with belantamab mafodotin treatment were maintained or deepened in most cases, demonstrating that management of AEs using dose modifications did not impact clinical activity [16]. This was also demonstrated with the BVd and BPd combination regimens [14].

In conclusion, belantamab mafodotin monotherapy has clinical activity in patients with RRMM. Long-term treatment with belantamab mafodotin did not present additional safety signals, and ocular AEs were manageable with dose modifications/delays. BVd/BPd combination studies had higher efficacy and similar safety profiles.

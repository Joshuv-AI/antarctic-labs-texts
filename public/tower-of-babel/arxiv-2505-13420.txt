Scalar-Induced Gravitational Waves in Palatini 𝒇 ⁡ ( 𝑹 ) f(R) Gravity
Samuel Sánchez López, and José Jaime Terente Díaz

Abstract
Primordial scalar perturbations that reenter the horizon after inflation may induce a second-order Gravitational Wave spectrum with information about the primordial Universe on scales inaccessible to Cosmic Microwave Background experiments. In this work, we develop a general framework for the study of Scalar-Induced Gravitational Waves in Palatini f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity, a theory that was proven to successfully realise inflation and quintessence, and consider the case of the Starobinsky-like model as an example. A regime of radiation domination with a subdominant matter component is assumed, allowing for a well-motivated perturbative approach to the gravity modifications. We calculate the kernel function and the density spectrum numerically and find accurate analytical expressions. The spectral density, which may be tested across a wide range of frequencies by upcoming Gravitational Wave experiments, is shown to differ from the General Relativity and metric f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity predictions under certain conditions. We comment on previous results in the literature regarding the metric formulation and make special emphasis on the potential of these distinctive features of the spectrum to probe the two formalisms of gravity.

2.3
The Equations of the Palatini Formalism

In the Palatini formalism,
f=f(T)
and
F=F(T)
, and hence

\displaystyle f^{(0)}=f(T^{(0)})~,

(49)

\displaystyle f^{(1)}=f_{,T}(T^{(0)})T^{(1)}~,

(50)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}f_{,TT}(T^{(0)})(T^{(1)})^{2}~,

(51)

\displaystyle F^{(0)}=F(T^{(0)})~,

(52)

\displaystyle F^{(1)}=F_{,T}(T^{(0)})T^{(1)}~,

(53)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}F^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}F_{,TT}(T^{(0)})(T^{(1)})^{2}~,

(54)

where
T^{(n)}
denotes the
n
-th order of the corresponding terms in Eq. (
48
). Notice that, unlike the Ricci scalar,
T
does not contain quadratic terms which would amount to second-order contributions
T^{(2)}
to
f^{(2)}
and
F^{(2)}
.

The homogeneous equations are, firstly, the analogue of Friedmann’s equation

3\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\frac{a^{2}}{F^{(0)}}\left[\frac{\rho}{m^{2}_{\textrm{P}}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(f^{(0)}+\frac{T^{(0)}}{m^{2}_{\textrm{P}}}\right)\right],

(55)

where we defined

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}\equiv\mathcal{H}+\frac{F^{(0)^{\prime}}}{2F^{(0)}}~,

(56)

and, secondly, the diagonal part of the spatial components of the metric field equations

-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-2\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{{}^{\prime}}=\frac{a^{2}}{F^{(0)}}\left[\frac{P}{m^{2}_{\textrm{P}}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(f^{(0)}+\frac{T^{(0)}}{m^{2}_{\textrm{P}}}\right)\right].

(57)

Additionally, combining the two, we arrive at an equation that does not include terms proportional to
\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}
in the field equations

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{{}^{\prime}}-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=-\frac{a^{2}}{2m^{2}_{\textrm{P}}F^{(0)}}(\rho+P)~.

(58)

On top of this, we have the continuity equation from the homogeneous contribution of Eq. (
15
)

\rho^{{}^{\prime}}=-3\mathcal{H}\left(\rho+P\right).

(59)

The reader versed in certain modified gravity theories and in conformal frames may find the definition in Eq. (
56
) quite familiar. This is how the (comoving) Hubble parameter transforms under a conformal rescaling of the metric which recasts the action as that of the so-called Einstein frame
Chiba and Yamaguchi (2008)
;
Kuusk et al. (2016)
;
Racioppi and Vasar (2022)
;
Karčiauskas and Díaz (2022)
. The equations of the theory can indeed be simplified by rewriting the background and perturbation quantities in terms of the Einstein frame ones. This is just a redefinition of some variables however. We do this for the sake of simplicity but the analysis is done in the Jordan frame representation. Further details on the Einstein frame representation of the theory are left for the interested reader in App.
B
.

Regarding the anisotropic stress tensor of matter,

;
Saga et al. (2018)

;

Since the essential goal of this work is to analyse and exhibit the spectral features that may distinguish the two formalisms and GR, we hereon neglect the anisotropic stress contribution from the matter, in the vein of Refs.
Zhou et al. (2024)
;
Kugarajh et al. (2025)

;

\psi
and

3
The Case of
\hat{R}+\frac{\alpha}{2m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\hat{R}^{2}
Gravity

We may now proceed to make use of the equations derived in the preceding sections. As a well-known example, and very successful model of inflation also
Starobinsky (1980)
;
Enckell et al. (2019)
;
Dimopoulos et al. (2022b)
;
Antoniadis et al. (2018)
;
Antoniadis et al. (2019)
;

where

\hat{R}=-\frac{T}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}~.

(81)

Plugging this back in Eq. (
80

f(T)=-\frac{T}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}T\right),

(82)

and (remember that

(83)

As indicated below Eq. (
71
), we shall assume adiabatic perturbations with constant EoS parameter, such that
\mathcal{S}=0
and

\displaystyle T^{(0)}=-\rho(1-3w)~,

(84)

\displaystyle T^{(1)}=-\delta\rho(1-3c_{s}^{2})~,

(85)

where

and

(see Eqs. (
49
)-(

\displaystyle f^{(0)}=\frac{\rho(1-3w)}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\alpha\rho(1-3w)}{2m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\right],

(86)

\displaystyle f^{(1)}=\frac{\delta\rho(1-3c_{s}^{2})}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\alpha\rho(1-3w)}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\right],

(87)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f^{(2)}=\frac{\alpha\delta\rho^{2}(1-3c_{s}^{2})^{2}}{2m_{\rm{P}}^{6}}~,

(88)

\displaystyle F^{(0)}=1+\frac{\alpha\rho(1-3w)}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}~,

(89)

\displaystyle F^{(1)}=\frac{\alpha\delta\rho(1-3c_{s}^{2})}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}~,

(90)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}F^{(2)}=0~.

(91)

3.1
Radiation Domination

If the content of the Universe is dominated by conformal matter (radiation), with
T=0
, then the trace equation (
10
) yields

F(\hat{R})\hat{R}-2f(\hat{R})=0~,

(92)

which implies (with the exception of the conformal case

) that the Ricci scalar in the Palatini formulation (and therefore
F(\hat{R})
) is a constant
\hat{R}=\hat{R}_{0}
. Plugging this back in the field equations, we recover GR with a cosmological constant
Sotiriou and Faraoni (2010)
;
Olmo (2011)
, given by

\Lambda=\frac{\hat{R}_{0}F(\hat{R}_{0})-f(\hat{R}_{0})}{2F(\hat{R}_{0})}~.

(93)

For
f(\hat{R})
given by Eq. (
80
),
\hat{R}_{0}=0
and the cosmological constant is zero.

Since the present work seeks to analyse the radiation dominated era, one might naively conclude that the corresponding dynamics of the SIGWs is trivially that of GR. We emphasise, however, that one must take into account the subdominant pressureless dust component, present during the aforementioned era. Not only will it modify the dynamics with respect to GR, but its contribution to the energy density will be small in comparison to that of the radiation. Consequently, we shall be able to treat the modifications perturbatively and obtain analytical expressions. To this end, we first notice that
\rho w=\rho_{\textrm{r}}/3
, where
w_{\textrm{r}}=1/3
and
w_{\textrm{m}}=0
. Also,
\delta\rho c_{s}^{2}=\delta\rho_{\textrm{r}}/3
as
c_{s}^{2}=w
. Therefore, Eqs. (
86
)-(
91
) read

\displaystyle f^{(0)}=\frac{\rho_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\rho_{\rm m}}{2m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\right),

(94)

\displaystyle f^{(1)}=\frac{\delta\rho_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\rho_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\right),

(95)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f^{(2)}=\frac{\alpha\delta\rho_{\rm m}^{2}}{2m_{\rm{P}}^{6}}~,

(96)

\displaystyle F^{(0)}=1+\frac{\alpha\rho_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}~,

(97)

\displaystyle F^{(1)}=\frac{\alpha\delta\rho_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}~,

(98)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}F^{(2)}=0~.

(99)

With these, the analogue of Friedmann equation (
55
) reads

3\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\alpha\rho_{\rm m}}{2m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\right)^{2}=a^{2}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\rho_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\right)\left[\frac{\rho_{\textrm{r}}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}+\frac{\rho_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\rho_{\rm m}}{4m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\right)\right],

(100)

where the continuity equation (
59
) was used to find
\rho_{\rm m}^{\prime}=-3\mathcal{H}\rho_{\rm m}
. Note that the product
a^{3}\rho_{\rm m}
is constant and in particular it can be evaluated at the present time, such that

a^{3}\rho_{\textrm{m}}=a_{0}^{3}\rho_{\textrm{m},0}=\rho_{\textrm{m},0}~,

(101)

where we take
a_{0}=1
and
\rho_{\textrm{m},0}\simeq 3.28\crossproduct 10^{-121}m_{\rm{P}}^{4}

Aghanim and others (2020)
. The same applies to the energy density of radiation, whose continuity equation is
\rho_{\textrm{r}}^{\prime}=-4\mathcal{H}\rho_{\textrm{r}}
, and then

a^{4}\rho_{\textrm{r}}=a_{0}^{4}\rho_{\textrm{r},0}=\rho_{\textrm{r},0}~,

(102)

where
\rho_{\textrm{r},0}\simeq 9.64\crossproduct 10^{-125}m_{\rm{P}}^{4}
.

We may also write the perturbation equation (
71
) in Fourier space as (remember that
c_{s}^{2}=w
and
\mathcal{S}=0
)

\tilde{\psi}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{k}+3\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(1+w)\tilde{\psi}^{{}^{\prime}}_{k}+\left[wk^{2}+2\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{{}^{\prime}}+\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(1+3w)\right]\tilde{\psi}_{k}=-a^{2}\frac{\alpha(1+w)\rho_{\rm m}\delta\rho^{\textrm{m}}_{k}}{4m_{\rm{P}}^{6}}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\rho_{\rm m}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\right)^{-2},

(103)

where

w=\frac{\rho_{\textrm{r}}}{3(\rho_{r}+\rho_{\rm m})}~.

(104)

As can be seen, the density perturbation of (pressureless) matter acts as a source in the equation of
\tilde{\psi}_{k}
. We may define alternatively the density contrast
\delta_{\textrm{m}}\equiv\delta\rho_{\rm m}/\rho_{\rm m}

Baumann (2022)
. Note that no approximations were made to obtain Eqs. (
100
) and (
103
). In order to simplify these, one may leverage the smallness of the dimensionless parameter
\beta
(as long as
\alpha<10^{120}
, so that
\beta\ll 1
), defined as

\beta\equiv\frac{\alpha\rho_{\textrm{m},0}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{4}}\simeq 3.28\times 10^{-121}\alpha~,

(105)

which is present in Eq. (
100

3\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{2}a^{-3}\right)^{2}=a^{2}\left(1+\beta a^{-3}\right)\left[\frac{\rho_{\textrm{r},0}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a^{-4}+\frac{\rho_{\textrm{m},0}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a^{-3}\left(1+\frac{\beta}{4}a^{-3}\right)\right],

(106)

where we remind the reader that the continuity equation has the same form as that of GR
Koivisto (2006)
and we use it in such a way that
\rho_{\rm m}=\rho_{\textrm{m},0}a^{-3}
and
\rho_{\textrm{r}}=\rho_{\textrm{r},0}a^{-4}
with
a_{0}=1
(see Eqs. (
101
) and (
102
), respectively). We then expand to linear order in

and arrive at the equation

3\mathcal{H}^{2}\simeq\frac{\rho_{\textrm{r},0}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a^{-2}+\frac{\rho_{\textrm{m},0}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a^{-1}+2\beta\left(\frac{\rho_{\textrm{r},0}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a^{-5}+\frac{9\rho_{\textrm{m},0}}{8m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a^{-4}\right).

(107)

As can be seen, this equation splits into a GR-like part and an additional contribution which relies on the small (but non-zero) value of
\beta
. We may expand the scale factor and (comoving) Hubble parameter similarly then

\displaystyle a=a_{\textrm{GR}}+\beta a_{\textrm{MG}}~,

(108)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{GR}}+\beta\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{MG}}~.

(109)

This approach to modified gravity corrections was developed within the metric formalism in Ref.
Bertacca et al. (2012)
and later applied to the study of SIGWs in Ref.
Kugarajh et al. (2025)
in the same formalism. It is worth noting that, while
\alpha
is their choice of expansion parameter, here we opt for
\beta
which accommodates large values of
\alpha
due to how small the order of magnitude of
\rho_{\textrm{m},0}
is in Planck units. Also, the authors of the aforementioned paper are taking
\alpha
with dimensions of inverse mass squared, while our
\beta
is dimensionless.

Following this procedure, Eq. (
107

3\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{GR}}^{2}=\frac{\rho_{\textrm{r},0}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a_{\textrm{GR}}^{-2}+\frac{\rho_{\textrm{m},0}}{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a^{-1}_{\textrm{GR}}~,

(110)

and the modified gravity one

\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{GR}}\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{MG}}=\frac{\rho_{\textrm{r},0}}{3m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a_{\textrm{GR}}^{-3}\left(a_{\textrm{GR}}^{-2}-a_{\textrm{MG}}\right)+\frac{3\rho_{\textrm{m},0}}{8m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}a_{\textrm{GR}}^{-2}\left(a^{-2}_{\textrm{GR}}-\frac{4}{9}a_{\textrm{MG}}\right).

(111)

To relate the GR and modified gravity parts of
\mathcal{H}
to those of

, we simply resort to the definition
\mathcal{H}=a^{{}^{\prime}}/a
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{GR}}=a^{{}^{\prime}}_{\textrm{GR}}/a_{\textrm{GR}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{MG}}=a_{\textrm{MG}}^{{}^{\prime}}/a_{\textrm{GR}}-a^{{}^{\prime}}_{\textrm{GR}}a_{\textrm{MG}}/a^{2}_{\textrm{GR}}

(112)

and obtain from solving this equation that

(113)

where
\mathcal{B}
is a constant of integration and
\mathcal{A}
is defined as

\mathcal{A}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\textrm{r},0}}{3m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}}\simeq 5.67\crossproduct 10^{-63}m_{\rm{P}}=2.16\crossproduct 10^{-6}\text{Mpc}^{-1}=2.10\crossproduct 10^{-20}\text{Hz}~.

(114)

While
\mathcal{B}
is a constant that relies on the initial condition of

,
\mathcal{A}
is simply the RHS of Eq. (
112

(see the comment in that respect below Eq. (
100

. Consequently,
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{GR}}=\mathcal{A}/a_{\textrm{GR}}
, and by inserting this in Eq. (

\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{MG}}\simeq\mathcal{A}a_{\textrm{GR}}^{-2}\left(a^{-2}_{\textrm{GR}}-a_{\textrm{MG}}\right),

(115)

where we neglected the pressureless matter contribution (second term on the RHS) in Eq. (
111

\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{MG}}
defined below Eq. (
111
) and
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{GR}}=\mathcal{A}/a_{\textrm{GR}}
, the equation above becomes

a_{\textrm{MG}}^{\prime}=\frac{\mathcal{A}}{(\mathcal{A}\eta+\mathcal{B})^{3}}~.

(116)

Upon solving this equation one obtains

a_{\textrm{MG}}(\eta)=\mathcal{C}-\frac{1}{2(\mathcal{A}\eta+\mathcal{B})^{2}}~,

(117)

\mathcal{C}
being another constant of integration, which is set by the initial condition on
a_{\textrm{MG}}
. One may want

to be non-negative, and hence

\mathcal{C}>\frac{1}{2\left(\mathcal{A}\eta_{\textrm{ini}}+\mathcal{B}\right)^{2}}~,

(118)

meaning that
a_{\textrm{GR}}(\eta_{\textrm{ini}})>1/\sqrt{2\mathcal{C}}

\frac{\rho_{\textrm{m},0}}{\rho_{\textrm{r},0}}\simeq 3.40\crossproduct 10^{3}\ll\sqrt{2\mathcal{C}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{C}\gg 5.78\crossproduct 10^{6}~.

(119)

Either possibility however (large or small

compared to the

Given the approximations above, we may write the scale factor and comoving Hubble parameter as

\displaystyle a(\eta)=\mathcal{A}\eta+\beta\left(\mathcal{C}-\frac{1}{2\mathcal{A}^{2}\eta^{2}}\right),

(120)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\eta)=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{\beta}{\mathcal{A}\eta^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{2\mathcal{A}^{2}\eta^{2}}-\mathcal{C}\right),

(121)

3.4
Density Spectrum of Scalar-Induced Gravitational Waves

In this section, the most observationally relevant quantities in the study of the SIGWs will be considered. We turn our attention to the spectral energy density parameter per logarithmic wavelength interval, which is defined as

\Omega_{\rm GW}(k,\eta)\equiv\frac{1}{\rho_{\rm c}(\eta)}\frac{\text{d}\rho_{\rm GW}}{\text{d}\log(k)}~,

(183)

where
\rho_{\rm c}
is the critical energy density. Note that, in view of Eq. (
55
), one needs to account for the modified gravity corrections to determine this density. However, these shall be negligible given that we are essentially interested in evaluating Eq. (
183
) at the present time. In what follows, we therefore use
\rho_{\rm c}=3m_{\rm{P}}^{2}a^{-2}\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

The energy density on subhorizon scales can be written as
7
7

7

One may notice that the homogeneous part of the evolution equation of the second-order tensor modes, Eq. (

Maggiore (2000)

\rho_{\rm GW}=\frac{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}{16a^{2}(\eta)}\langle\overline{\partial_{k}h_{ij}\partial^{k}h^{ij}}\rangle~,

(184)

where the overbar denotes time averaging and the spatial indices are contracted with Kronecker’s delta. We then write the Fourier decomposition of the tensor modes as

h_{ij}(\eta,\textbf{x})=\sum_{s=+,\crossproduct}\int\frac{\text{d}^{3}\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e_{ij}^{s}(\textbf{k})h^{s}_{\textbf{k}}(\eta)e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x}}~,

(185)

where
s=+,\crossproduct
denotes the polarisations and
e_{ij}^{s}(\textbf{k})
are the usual polarisation tensors, defined in terms of an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i},\bar{e}_{j}\}
of vectors orthogonal to
k
as
e_{ij}^{+}(\textbf{k})=(e_{i}(\textbf{k})e_{j}(\textbf{k})-\bar{e}_{i}(\textbf{k})\bar{e}_{j}(\textbf{k}))/\sqrt{2}
and
e_{ij}^{\crossproduct}(\textbf{k})=(e_{i}(\textbf{k})\bar{e}_{j}(\textbf{k})+\bar{e}_{i}(\textbf{k})e_{j}(\textbf{k}))/\sqrt{2}
. These are transverse (
k^{i}e^{s}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=0
), traceless (
\sum_{i}e^{s}_{ii}=0
) and satisfy the normalisation condition

\sum_{i,j}e^{s}_{ij}(\textbf{k})e^{s^{\prime}}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=\delta^{s,s^{\prime}}~.

(186)

Lastly, imposing that the tensor modes
h_{ij}(\eta,\textbf{x})
are real implies that
e_{ij}^{s}(-\textbf{k})=e_{ij}^{s}(\textbf{k})
and
(h^{s}_{\textbf{k}}){}^{*}=h^{s}_{-\textbf{k}}

Plugging Eq. (

\rho_{\rm GW}=-\frac{m_{\rm{P}}^{2}}{16a^{2}(\eta)}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=+,\crossproduct}\int\frac{\text{d}^{3}\textbf{k}\text{d}^{3}\textbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{6}}e_{ij}^{s}(\textbf{k})e_{ij}^{s^{\prime}}(\textbf{q})\langle\overline{h_{\textbf{k}}^{s}(\eta)h_{\textbf{q}}^{s^{\prime}}(\eta)}\rangle k_{m}q_{m}e^{i(\textbf{k}+\textbf{q})\cdot\textbf{x}}~.

(187)

By introducing the GW dimensionless power spectrum

\langle h_{\textbf{k}}^{s}(\eta)h_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}(\eta)\rangle=\delta^{ss^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\textbf{k}+\textbf{k}^{\prime})\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\Delta_{h}^{2}(\eta,k)~,

(188)

(189)

where we have summed over both polarisations, since they contribute equally. The subsequent calculation reduces to obtaining the GW dimensionless power spectrum, and hence we must determine the correlator

\sum_{s=+,\crossproduct}\langle h_{\textbf{k}}^{s}(\eta)h_{\textbf{q}}^{s}(\eta)\rangle~,

(190)

where it can be noted that, in order to obtain the power spectrum, we need to sum over all possible polarisations. All the statistical information is encoded in the initial values
\psi_{\textbf{k},i}

4
Conclusions and Outlook

This work is a study of the production of second-order tensor modes sourced by first-order scalar perturbations in the context of Palatini
f(R)
gravity. We have focused on the radiation dominated era, where the only contribution to the trace of the energy-momentum tensor is the subdominant pressureless dust component. This allowed us to use a perturbative approach to modified gravity and succeed in obtaining an analytical expression for the density spectrum of scalar-induced gravitational waves, which turns out to be a simple scale-invariant enhancement of the General Relativity (GR) signal.

After giving an overview of Palatini
f(R)
gravity in the first section, we developed the cosmological perturbation theory up to, and including, second order. Analytical expressions for the Riemann and Ricci tensors were obtained, as well as for the curvature scalar, without fixing a particular gauge (see App.
A
). Then, by imposing the Newtonian gauge, we derived all the components of the Einstein equations to first order in the scalar perturbations, in addition to the traceless-transverse second-order component, turning our attention to the scalar contribution to the source.

In order to make progress, we fixed
f(\hat{R})=\hat{R}+\alpha\hat{R}^{2}/(2m_{\rm{P}}^{2})
and studied the evolution of the perturbations during radiation domination. The effect of the higher-order curvature term in the Palatini formalism is to provide additional contributions to the matter sources of the Einstein equations. These contributions rely on the trace of the background fluid, meaning that, during radiation domination, they depend on the subdominant pressureless dust component (the only non-zero contribution to the trace). Motivated by this fact, we treated
\alpha\hat{R}^{2}/(2m_{\rm{P}}^{2})
as a perturbative effect on GR and obtained that the modified gravity part of the scalar perturbation satisfies an ordinary differential equation equivalent to that of GR, but with the GR scalar perturbation and density contrast acting as sources. This equation is analytically solvable, giving, after a few well-motivated approximations, that the solution is a simple re-scaling of the GR part. We additionally verified this claim by numerically solving the corresponding equations.

Finally, we derived the kernel and the density spectrum of scalar-induced gravitational waves, both analytically and numerically. The final result implies that the resulting spectrum is enhanced by a factor of
\Delta(\alpha,T_{\rm reh})=2.76\alpha(T_{\rm reh}/\text{GeV})^{2}\crossproduct 10^{-92}
. In order to comply with the fact that the modified gravity contribution to the scalar perturbation should be smaller than the GR part, we need that
\Delta<1
. However, an
\mathcal{O}(10^{-1})
enhancement can be achieved for
\alpha=10^{61}
and
T_{\rm reh}=T_{\rm GUT}\sim 10^{15}\text{GeV}
. Importantly, such a large value of
\alpha
would lead to an unobservable tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
. The enhancement of the secondary signal is scale-invariant, and therefore, if present, it should be detectable for any of the future gravitational wave experiments such as BBO
Harry et al. (2006)
, DECIGO, ET, LISA, and SKA. This opens the door to gravitational wave astronomy not only probing pre-recombination physics, but also the theory of gravity itself. Indeed, the signal we obtained is not only different from that of GR (for large enough
\alpha
and
T_{\rm reh}
), but also from
f(\hat{R})=\hat{R}+\alpha\hat{R}^{2}/(2m_{\rm{P}}^{2})
gravity in the metric formalism. Probing the degrees of freedom of the theory of gravity appears to be plausible and an exciting prospect.

Regarding future work, it would be interesting to extend our model to the matter dominated era, and adopt a realistic reheating history. Also, we would perform a study of the observability of the theory, exploring its parameter space, as well as considering other
f(R)
functions, such as the Hu-Sawicki model
Hu and Sawicki (2007)
. This model is expected to introduce modifications to GR that become more relevant at late times, unlike
\hat{R}+\alpha\hat{R}^{2}/(2m_{\rm{P}}^{2})
. One might expect models of this kind to have a large impact on the spectrum of scalar-induced gravitational waves, although it is likely that one might lose capacity to solve the dynamical equations analytically. Additionally, the effect of backreaction from the modified gravity terms on the GR evolution when
\alpha
is larger than the bound under consideration (see Eq. (
168
)) should be evaluated. Likewise, the presence of anisotropic stress from matter in the second-order tensor modes equations could be addressed in detail, as well as the possible differences depending on the formalism of gravity under consideration, or the modified gravity theory at large. We are not aware of previous considerations being made in the Palatini formalism.

Abstract
. We construct maximal Λ ⁡ ( p ) \Lambda(p) -subsets on a large class of curved manifolds, in an optimal range of Lebesgue exponents p p . Our arguments combine restriction estimates and decoupling with old and new probabilistic estimates.



1.
Description of tight decoupling in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

We motivate the results in this paper with the following question. Given
d\geq 1
,
p\geq 2
and
R\gg 1
, how many points
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{M}\in[0,1]^{d}
can we find so that

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{n=1}^{M}a_{n}e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\|a_{n}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}

(1)

for each
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
? We use the notation
e(z)=e^{2\pi iz}
. Equivalently,

\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\sum_{n=1}^{M}a_{n}e(R\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\|a_{n}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}.

We will call
\{R\xi_{n}:\;1\leq n\leq M\}
a (finite)
\Lambda(p)
-set. Strictly speaking, this name was reserved in the literature to the case when the points
R\xi_{n}
are additionally in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. However, we will not enforce this restriction in our paper.

Let us test this inequality with
a_{n}\equiv 1
. Constructive interference (Lemma
2.5
) shows that for
|x|<(100d)^{-1}
we have

|\sum_{n=1}^{M}e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|\sim M.

This shows that
M\lesssim R^{\frac{2d}{p}}
. Remarkably, constructive interference turns out to be the most severe obstruction. Moreover, there are many choices of points
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{M}\in[0,1]^{d}
with maximal size
M\sim R^{\frac{2d}{p}}
so that (
1
) holds. This is an immediate consequence of the following seminal result of Bourgain.

Given
p\geq 2
and a finite collection
\Phi
of uniformly bounded complex-valued functions
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}
on a probability space, we let
K_{p}(\Phi)
be the smallest constant such that

\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}\varphi_{n}\|_{p}\leq K_{p}(\Phi)(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{n}|^{2})^{1/2}

(2)

holds for each
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. This may be seen as a measure of the “
L^{p}
orthogonality” of
\Phi
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

For each orthonormal system
\Phi=\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}\}
of functions with
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
and each
p>2
, there is
\Psi\subset\Phi
with size
\sim N^{2/p}
such that

K_{p}(\Psi)\lesssim 1.

The implicit constant is independent of
N
.

It is immediate that if
\Phi
satisfies the hypotheses of the theorem, then
K_{2}(\Phi)=1
and
K_{\infty}(\Phi)\leq N^{1/2}
. These imply that
1\leq K_{p}(\Phi)\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}
for each
2\leq p\leq\infty
. The system
\Phi=\{e(nx):\;1\leq n\leq N\}
on
[0,1]
has
K_{p}(\Phi)\sim N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}
.

The
N\sim R^{d}
functions
\varphi_{\bf n}(x)=e({\bf n}\cdot x)
,
{\bf n}\in[0,R]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, form a uniformly bounded orthonormal system on
[0,1]^{d}
, so Theorem
1.1
is applicable. In fact, its proof shows that a generic choice of a subset of size
N^{2/p}
of
[0,R]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
will work. Such a set will generically be somewhat evenly spread inside
[0,R]^{d}
(see Remark
2.7
for the precise meaning of this, in a related context).
Equivalently, with high probability, the
1/R
-rescaled points
\xi_{n}
will tend to be uniformly distributed in
[0,1]^{d}
.
But is there a choice of
\sim R^{2d/p}
such points that live in a thin, highly localized set
E\subset[0,1]^{d}
?

A simpler version of this question was answered in
[
8
]
, where the exploration of this phenomenon, termed
tight decoupling
, was initiated. More precisely, a rather satisfactory criterium was found for (
1
) to hold for constant coefficients
a_{n}=1
and
\xi_{n}\in E
.

In this paper we are concerned with the case of arbitrary coefficients. Our thin sets will be compact, smooth
m
-dimensional manifolds in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

{\mathcal{M}}=\{(\eta,\Pi(\eta)):\;\eta\in[0,1]^{m}\}.

(3)

Our primary goal is to prove optimal results, with
R
-independent constants. However, we also consider similar inequalities involving arbitrarily small
R^{\epsilon}
losses.
Given
R
-dependent quantities
A(R),B(R)
, we write
A(R){\;\lessapprox}\;B(R)
if
A(R)\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}B(R)
as
R\to\infty
, for arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
.

In our applications, the orthonormal systems
\Phi
will consist of complex exponentials on
[0,1]^{d}
, with frequencies in a set
S\subset[0,R]^{d}
. It is easy to see that any such
S
may have size at most
\sim R^{d}
.

The set
S
may not necessarily consist of lattice points
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Pairwise orthogonality within
\Phi
will be guaranteed by the weaker assumption that
s-s^{\prime}
has at least one integer coordinate for each
s,s^{\prime}\in S
. If the set
S

is clear from the context, we will prefer writing
K_{p}(S)
in place of
K_{p}(\Phi)
.

Let us formulate a few questions, that have in part been asked in
[
8
]
.

Question 1.2
.

Consider a smooth manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
in
[0,1]^{d}
, and let
2<p<\infty
.

•

Q1. Is there a set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
for
R\gg 1
, such that
|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|\sim R^{2d/p}
and

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}

(4)

holds for each
a_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{C}}
? This is the same as
K_{p}(R{\mathcal{P}}_{R})\lesssim 1
.

•

Q2. How about if we allow
R^{\epsilon}
losses,

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}?

(5)

•

Q3. Are there explicit examples in either case?

The study of tight decoupling in
[
8
]
was born partly out of the desire to explain the following phenomenon for the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
. Consider an integer
R^{2}
for which
R{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
contains
\approx R^{d-2}
points (this is the case for infinitely many
R
). The rescaled collection
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}=\frac{1}{R}(R{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
is conjectured to satisfy the tight decoupling inequality (
5
) with
p=\frac{2d}{d-2}
. Due to
l^{2}
decoupling,
[
4
]
, (and periodicity), (
5
) can be seen to hold for
p\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
. This is the largest range guaranteed by
l^{2}
decoupling. So how special is the example of the lattice points on the sphere, for which we have square root cancellation in the maximal range
p\leq\frac{2d}{d-2}
? Our arguments will show that this behavior is not isolated, but rather generic. Tight decoupling holds with high probability.

Questions Q1, Q2 and Q3 only make sense for
d>1
. However, problems of similar flavor have been addressed in
[
3
]
in one dimension, where manifolds
{\mathcal{M}}
are replaced with arithmetic sets such as the (rescaled) squares
\{\frac{n^{2}}{R}:\;1\leq n\leq\sqrt{R}\}
. The general principle that identifies
\Lambda(p)
-sets of maximal size inside a finite set
S
can be formulated as follows.

Proposition 1.3
.

Write
N=R^{d}
.
Let
S\subset[0,R]^{d}
be such that
e(s\cdot x)
form an orthonormal system on
[0,1]^{d}
. Assume that for some
q\geq 2
we have

K_{q}(S)\lesssim|S|^{1/2}N^{-1/q}.

(6)

Then for each
p>q
there is a subset
S_{0}\subset S
with size
|S_{0}|\sim N^{2/p}
and

K_{p}(S_{0})\lesssim 1
. The same holds true for
p=q
, under the extra assumption that
K_{q}(S)\geq N^{\kappa}
for some
\kappa>0
.

This is a particular case of Theorem
2.1
below. The case
p>q
is Corollary 5 in
[
3
]
, while
p=q
is our own contribution, crucially needed to address endpoint results.

When
q=2
, this follows from Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, note that (
6
) forces
|S|\sim N
. When
q>2
, (
6
) can be understood as a measure of “maximal
L^{q}
orthogonality”. Let us explain what this means. It first shows that
|S|\gtrsim N^{2/q}
. Since
S\subset[0,R]^{d}
, constructive interference shows that
K_{q}(S)\gtrsim|S|^{1/2}N^{-1/q}
. Thus, subsets of
[0,R]^{d}
satisfying (
6
) have maximal possible
L^{q}
orthogonality for their size. For comparison, the largest possible value of
K_{q}(S)
(minimal
L^{q}
orthogonality) is
\sim|S|^{1/2}|S|^{-1/q}
. Proposition
1.3
says the following. Each
L^{2}
orthonormal set with maximal
L^{q}
orthogonality contains an
L^{p}
orthogonal subset of maximum possible size.

In
[
3
]
, Bourgain proved that if
q>4
then

K_{q}(\{1^{2},2^{2},\ldots,M^{2}\})\lesssim M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{q}}.

(7)

This matches (
6
) when
R=M^{2}
, which leads to the existence of
\Lambda(p)
-subsets of the squares of maximal possible size, for
p>4
.

In our applications to manifolds defined by (
3
), we will use the sets

S=\{({\bf n},R\;\Pi(\frac{{\bf n}}{R})):\;{\bf n}\in[0,R]^{m}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\}.

In Section
2
we observe that if the extension operator for
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies an
L^{2}\to L^{q}
Fourier restriction estimate, then
S
satisfies (
6
) with the same
q
. This result is well-known, only our interpretation is new. Our use of Fourier restriction estimates leads to sharp results for hypersurfaces with non-zero Gaussian curvature and for non-degenerate curves. In particular, we fully characterize the range of
p
for which Question Q1 has a positive answer for these manifolds.

The
L^{2}\to L^{q}
restriction estimate can be seen as a measure of the “curvature” of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Loosely speaking, curved manifolds cannot have “large” intersections with hyperplanes.

Bourgain’s estimate (
7
) is a measure of the arithmetic properties on the squares, which is a substitute for curvature. The role of hyperplanes in one dimension is played by arithmetic progressions. It is easy to show that (
7
) forces
\{1^{2},\ldots,M^{2}\}
to not contain any significantly long arithmetic progression
P
. Indeed, constructive interference shows that
K_{q}(P)\sim|P|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}
, which leads to
|P|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{q}}
. Letting
q\to 4
shows that
|P|\lesssim_{\epsilon}M^{\epsilon}
.

It perhaps comes as a surprise that
L^{2}\to L^{q}
restriction estimates are strong enough to produce maximal
\Lambda(p)
-sets. Their proofs make a rather weak use of curvature, in particular they follow easily from decoupling estimates on the corresponding manifold. The downside of our approach in Section
2
is that all examples of
\Lambda(p)
-sets it leads to are probabilistic, so non-explicit. In Section
3
we use decoupling to produce a rather easy and elegant explicit example, albeit with
R^{\epsilon}
losses. More precisely, the use of decoupling fully derandomizes the selection process by better exploiting curvature. When
p
is even, this approach allows for deterministic input of “flat”
\Lambda(p)
-sets, known since the work
[
17
]
of Rudin. For such
p
, he constructed explicit subsets
S
of
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with size
\sim N^{2/p}
and
K_{p}(S)\sim 1.
No similar examples are known for other values of
p
.

In Section
4
we investigate the range of smaller values of
p
, for which (
4
), while false for arbitrary coefficients, was proved to hold for constant coefficients in
[
8
]
. We propose an
l^{p}
analog of the failing
l^{2}
inequality, meant to put the results from
[
8
]
into a more robust framework. However, while we cannot recover the results in
[
8
]
via this more general approach, we draw some natural connections to small cap decoupling and to a conjectured
l^{p}
analogue of Theorem
2.1
.

Section
5
is devoted to refining Bourgain’s probabilistic estimates. These are primarily motivated by our applications, but are also likely to find future applications.

2.
A general answer for Question Q1

The results in this section rely on two ingredients. The first one is the following far-reaching generalization of Theorem
1.1
. The first part is Theorem 4 in
[
3
]
. The endpoint is new, see Theorem
5.1
and Remark
5.10
in Section
5
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a finite orthonormal system consisting of complex exponentials
e(s\cdot x)
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, on
[0,1]^{d}
, and let
2\leq q\leq p<\infty
.

(a) Assume
p>q
. Then there exists
\Psi\subset\Phi
satisfying

K_{p}(\Psi)\lesssim 1\text{ and }|\Psi|\sim K_{q}(\Phi)^{-2q/p}|\Phi|^{q/p}.

The implicit constants only depend on
p,q,d
, but not on
\Phi
.

(b)
(p=q)
Assume that there exists
\kappa>0
such that
|\Phi|^{\kappa}\leq K_{q}(\Phi)^{2}
. Then there exists
\Psi\subset\Phi
satisfying

K_{q}(\Psi)\lesssim 1\text{ and }|\Psi|\sim K_{q}(\Phi)^{-2}|\Phi|.

The implicit constants only depend on
q,d,\kappa
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be an arbitrary
m
-dimensional manifold. We consider the orthonormal system
\Phi_{R,{\mathcal{M}}}
on
[0,1]^{d}
consisting of
\varphi_{\xi}(x)=e(R\xi\cdot x)
with
\xi=(\eta,\Pi(\eta))
,
\eta\in[0,1]^{m}\cap(R^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{m}
. Note that
|\Phi_{R,{\mathcal{M}}}|\sim R^{m}
. Constructive interference shows that for each
q\geq 2
we have
K_{q}(\Phi_{R,{\mathcal{M}}})\gtrsim R^{\frac{m}{2}-\frac{d}{q}}.
The following corollary is a particular case of the general principle stated in Proposition
1.3
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume that there is
q\geq 2
such that for each
R
we have

K_{q}(\Phi_{R,{\mathcal{M}}})\sim R^{\frac{m}{2}-\frac{d}{q}}.

(8)

Then for each
p\geq q
there is
\Psi_{R}\subset\Phi_{R,{\mathcal{M}}}
with
|\Psi_{R}|\sim R^{\frac{2d}{p}}
and
K_{p}(\Psi_{R})\lesssim 1.

The implicit constants may depend on
p,q,m,d
, but are independent of
R
.

Proof.

The only case that needs an explanation is
p=q
. If
\frac{m}{2}>\frac{d}{q}
, we may apply Theorem
2.1
with, say,
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{mq}
. If
\frac{m}{2}=\frac{d}{q}
, we may take
\Psi_{R}=\Phi_{R,{\mathcal{M}}}
.

∎

It remains to prove (
8
) for appropriate
q
.
Consider the extension operator, for continuous functions
f:[0,1]^{m}\to{\mathbb{C}}

E^{\Pi}f(x)=\int_{[0,1]^{m}}f(\eta)e((\eta,\Pi(\eta))\cdot x)d\eta.

The following is a well-known result. We are not certain about its first appearance in the literature.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that for some
r,q\geq 1
and each
f\in L^{r}
we have

\|E^{\Pi}f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{r}([0,1]^{m})}.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a collection of
1/R
-separated points on
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Then for each
R\gg 1
and each
a_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{C}}
we have

\|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)\|_{L^{q}([0,R]^{d})}\lesssim R^{\frac{m}{r^{\prime}}}\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{r}}.

Proof.

Define the
R^{-1}
-neighborhood of
{\mathcal{M}}

{\mathcal{N}}_{\Pi}(R^{-1})=\{(\eta,\Pi(\eta)+t):\>\eta\in[0,1]^{m},\;t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-m},\;|t|<1/R\}.

We first show that for each
F
with Fourier support inside
{\mathcal{N}}_{\Pi}(R^{-1})
we have

\|F\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim R^{-\frac{d-m}{r^{\prime}}}\|\widehat{F}\|_{L^{r}({\mathcal{N}}_{\Pi}(R^{-1}))}.

(9)

To this end, note that by Fourier inversion and Fubini

{F}(x)=C_{d}\int_{|t|<R^{-1}}[\int_{[0,1]^{m}}\widehat{F}(\eta,\Pi(\eta)+t)e((\eta,\Pi(\eta)+t)\cdot x)d\eta]dt.

Letting
f_{t}(\eta)=\widehat{F}(\eta,\Pi(\eta)+t)
we find

|{F}(x)|\leq C_{d}\int_{B_{1/R}}|E^{\Pi}f_{t}(x)|dt.

By Minkowski’s integral inequality followed by our hypothesis applied to each
f_{t}
, then by Hölder’s inequality on
B_{1/R}
, we have

\|F\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\int_{B_{1/R}}\|E^{\Pi}f_{t}(x)\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},dx)}dt\lesssim\int_{B_{1/R}}\|f_{t}\|_{L^{r}([0,1]^{m})}dt

\lesssim|B_{1/R}|^{1/r^{\prime}}\|\widehat{F}\|_{L^{r}({\mathcal{N}}_{\Pi}(R^{-1}))}\sim R^{-\frac{d-m}{r^{\prime}}}\|\widehat{F}\|_{L^{r}({\mathcal{N}}_{\Pi}(R^{-1}))}.

Next, consider a smooth function
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\phi(x)\geq 1_{[0,1]^{d}}(x)
and
{\operatorname{supp}\,}(\widehat{\phi})\subset[0,1/10]^{d}
. Then

F(x)=\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}}a_{\xi}\phi(x/R)e(\xi\cdot x)

has Fourier support in
{\mathcal{N}}_{\Pi}(R^{-1})
. An easy computation using the
1/R
-separation of
\xi
shows that

\|\widehat{F}\|_{L^{r}}\sim R^{\frac{d}{r^{\prime}}}\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{r}}.

We combine this with (
9
) to write

\|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)\|_{L^{q}([0,R]^{d})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim R^{-\frac{d-m}{r^{\prime}}}\|\widehat{F}\|_{L^{r}({\mathcal{N}}_{\Pi}(R^{-1}))}\lesssim R^{\frac{m}{r^{\prime}}}\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{r}}.

∎

We specialize this to
r=2
, and combine it with Corollary
2.2
to get the following criterium.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume

\|E^{\Pi}f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,1]^{m})}

(10)

holds for some
q>2
and each continuous function
f
. Then Question Q1 has a positive answer for each
p\geq q
.

Let us test the criterium with two examples.

Example 1:
Assume first that
{\mathcal{M}}
is the moment curve

\Gamma^{d}=\{(\eta,\eta^{2},\ldots,\eta^{d}):\;\eta\in[0,1]\}.

Then inequality (
10
) holds for
q\geq d(d+1)
, see e.g.
[
9
]
for the full range of extension estimates. Thus, Q1 has a positive answer when
p\geq d(d+1)
. On the other hand, no
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset\Gamma^{d}
with
|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|\sim R^{2d/p}
can satisfy (
4
) when
p<d(d+1)
. To see this, we use the following general principle.

Lemma 2.5
.

(Constructive interference)
Let
B
be a rectangular box in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with dimensions
l_{1},\ldots,l_{d}
. Let
B^{o}
be its polar box, centered at the origin and with dimensions
1/l_{1},\ldots,1/l_{d}
. Assume
{\mathcal{P}}
is a finite subset of
B
. Then

|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}}e(\xi\cdot x)|\sim|{\mathcal{P}}|

for
x\in\frac{1}{100d}B^{o}
.

To apply the lemma, we use pigeonholing to find an arc
\tau
on
\Gamma^{d}
containing at least
|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|R^{-1/d}\sim R^{\frac{2d}{p}-\frac{1}{d}}
points in
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
. It is easy to see that
\tau
is a subset of a box
B
with dimensions roughly
R^{-1/d}
,
R^{-2/d}
,…,
R^{-1}
.
We have the volume estimate
|B^{o}\cap[0,R]^{d}|\sim R^{\frac{d+1}{2}}
.
Using the lemma we find that

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\cap\tau}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p}dx\gtrsim R^{2d-\frac{p}{d}}R^{\frac{1-d}{2}}.

Testing (
4
) with
a_{\xi}=1_{{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\cap\tau}(\xi)
shows that
p\geq d(d+1)
.

Example 2:

Assume
{\mathcal{M}}
is a hyper-surface (
m=d-1
) with
1\leq k\leq d-1
everywhere nonzero principal curvatures. It was proved in
[
10
]
that (
10
) holds for
q\geq\frac{2(k+2)}{k}
. We specialize this to the cases of most interest.

When
{\mathcal{M}}
has non-zero Gaussian curvature (
k=d-1
), Question Q1 has a positive answer when
p\geq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
. Moreover, a similar application of Lemma
2.5
, with
B
a box of dimensions
R^{-1/2}\times\ldots\times R^{-1/2}\times R^{-1}
, shows that Q1 has a negative answer when
p<\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
.

When
{\mathcal{M}}
is the cone (
\Pi(\eta)=|\eta|
,
k=d-2
), Question Q1 has a positive answer when
p\geq\frac{2d}{d-2}
. Moreover, Lemma
2.5
, with
B
a box of dimensions
R^{-1/2}\times\ldots\times R^{-1/2}\times 1\times R^{-1}
, shows that Q1 has a negative answer when
p<\frac{2d}{d-2}
.

When
{\mathcal{M}}
is a hyperplane, (
10
) is false for each
q<\infty
. And indeed, Question Q1 has a negative answer for each
2<p<\infty
.

We conclude this section with some clarifying remarks regarding the limiting distribution of
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
on
{\mathcal{M}}
. Inequality (
10
) may be reformulated as follows. Let
\nu
be the measure on
{\mathcal{M}}
that is the pullback of the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]^{m}
. Then for each continuous
g
on
{\mathcal{M}}
we have

\|\widehat{gd\nu}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(d\nu)}.

(11)

We have the following partial converse of Proposition
2.4
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume Question Q1 has a positive answer for some
p>2
. Then there is a Borel probability measure

\nu
on
{\mathcal{M}}
such that (
11
) holds with
q=p
.

Proof.

Let
\nu
be any weak*-limit point of the sequence of discrete measures on
{\mathcal{M}}

\nu_{R}=\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|}\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}\delta_{\xi}.

It suffices to prove that for each compact set
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\int_{K}|\widehat{gd\nu}|^{p}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(d\nu)}^{p},

with implicit constant independent of
g
,
K
and
R
. Since
K\subset[0,R]^{d}
for large enough
R
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K}|\widehat{gd\nu}|^{p}

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|^{p}}\int_{K}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}\widehat{g(\xi)\delta_{\xi}}|^{p}\sim\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{2d}}\int_{K}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}g(\xi)e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{2d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}g(\xi)e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{d}}(\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}|g(\xi)|^{2})^{p/2}

\displaystyle\sim\lim_{R\to\infty}\|g\|_{L^{2}(d\nu_{R})}^{p}=\|g\|_{L^{2}(d\nu)}^{p}.

∎

When
{\mathcal{M}}
is a hypersurface with non-zero Gaussian curvature and
p=\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
, each limit measure
\nu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the surface measure on
{\mathcal{M}}
. This can be seen as a measure of non-concentration. Indeed, constructive interference shows that for each ball
B_{r}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of radius
r\geq R^{-1/2}
we have

\nu_{R}(B_{r})\lesssim r^{d-1}.

It follows that
\nu(B_{r})\lesssim r^{d-1}
for each
r>0
, and this shows that
\nu
must be absolutely continuous with respect to the surface measure on
{\mathcal{M}}
. This is not necessarily the case for larger values of
p
. We illustrate this with the following example. The moment curve
\Gamma^{3}
is contained in the hyperbolic paraboloid
{\mathbb{H}}
, see (
18
). Thus, in light of our Example 1 discussed above, tight decoupling may be achieved for
p\geq 12
with
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
supported on
\Gamma^{3}
. All corresponding limit measures will be supported on
\Gamma^{3}
, thus being singular with respect to the surface measure of
{\mathbb{H}}
.

The same computations show that for each
p\geq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
, each limit measure (for a given hypersurface
{\mathcal{M}}
) satisfies

\nu(B_{r})\lesssim r^{\alpha},

(12)

with
\alpha=\frac{2(d+1)}{p}
. Using this we observe the following. Given
0<\alpha\leq d-1
we let

C(\alpha)=\min\{q:\;\exists\;\nu\text{ probability measure on }{\mathcal{M}}\text{ such that }\eqref{equiv}\text{ and }\eqref{poeifreuf8ure8gu}\text{ hold}\}.

We have showed in Proposition
2.6
that
C(\alpha)\leq 2(d+1)/\alpha
. Let us prove that
C(\alpha)\geq 2(d+1)/\alpha
. The argument in Proposition 3.1 in
[
16
]
shows that for any measure satisfying (
12
) there is a sequence of balls
B_{r}
with radii shrinking to zero, so that
\nu(B_{r})\sim r^{\alpha}.
Let
g=1_{B_{r}\cap{\mathcal{M}}}
. Note that due to constructive interference

|\widehat{gd\nu}(x)|\sim\nu(B_{r}\cap{\mathcal{M}}),

for each
x\in(100d)^{-1}B
, where
B
is a rectangular box centered at the origin, with dimensions
r^{-1}\times\ldots\times r^{-1}\times r^{-2}
. Testing (
11
) with
g
shows that
r^{\alpha/2}\lesssim r^{(d+1)/p}
, forcing
p\geq 2(d+1)/\alpha
.

Similar questions have been considered earlier for arbitrary measures, not necessarily supported on a given manifold. See
[
15
]
for sharp results away from the endpoint.

Remark 2.7
.

For each
p\geq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
, a generic choice for
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
has all associated limit measures
\nu
equivalent with the surface measure on
{\mathcal{M}}
. This is a manifestation of the “uniform distribution” of
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
, as
R\to\infty
. To prove this, let
\Delta_{R}\to 0
be such that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\Delta_{R}^{d-1}e^{c\Delta_{R}^{d-1}|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|}=\infty.

(13)

Partition
{\mathcal{M}}
into
\sim\Delta_{R}^{-(d-1)}

caps
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
with diameter
\sim\Delta_{R}
. We select the points
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
with equal probability
\delta_{R}=|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|/R^{d-1}\sim R^{2d/p}/R^{d-1}
.
The Chernoff-Hoeffding inequality (
29
) shows that for each
\mathcal{C}
,

{\mathbb{P}}(|\mathcal{C}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|\sim\delta_{R}(\Delta_{R}^{d-1}R^{d-1}))\geq 1-e^{-c\Delta_{R}^{d-1}|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|}.

Combining this with (
13
) we get

\lim_{R\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(|\mathcal{C}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|\sim\delta(\Delta_{R}^{d-1}R^{d-1})\text{ for each }\mathcal{C})=1.

Due to (
30
) we in fact have

{\mathbb{P}}(|\mathcal{C}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|\sim\delta(\Delta_{R}^{d-1}R^{d-1})\text{ for each }\mathcal{C},\text{ and }K_{p}(R{\mathcal{P}}_{R})\sim 1)\sim 1,

with the implicit similarity constant independent of
R
.
It follows that
\nu_{R}(\mathcal{C})\sim\Delta_{R}^{d-1}

for each
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
. Thus,
\nu_{R}(B_{r})\sim r^{d-1}
for each ball
B_{r}
centered on
{\mathcal{M}}
with radius
1\geq r\gg\Delta_{R}
. Letting
R\to\infty
we find that
\nu(B_{r})\sim r^{d-1}
for each
r\in(0,1]
.

3.
decoupling estimates; deterministic examples

The examples constructed in the previous section are non-deterministic. In this section, we introduce an alternative approach, that relies on
l^{2}
decoupling. The purpose is two-fold. On the one hand, this approach will lead us to some deterministic examples, albeit with
R^{\epsilon}
losses. On the other hand,
l^{2}
decoupling offers a new perspective, that poses interesting challenges and motivates us to strengthen Bourgain’s original probabilistic estimate. We hope that comparing the two methods will shed additional light on tight decoupling, and will stimulate further research on the topic.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p_{d}=\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
. Consider a smooth compact hypersurface
\Sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\Sigma=\{(\eta,\Pi(\eta)):\;\eta\in[0,1]^{d-1}\},

with positive principal curvatures (positive second fundamental form). Then for each
R\gg 1
there is a set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset\Sigma
with cardinality
\sim R^{2d/p_{d}}
such that

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p_{d}}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{p_{d}}

(14)

holds for arbitrary
a_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

We will use two ingredients.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
. Each set
{\mathcal{P}}
of
N
points in
(R^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{d-1}
contains a subset
S
with
|S|\sim N^{2/p}
such that

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\eta\in S}a_{\eta}e((\eta,\Pi(\eta))\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\|a_{\eta}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}

(15)

holds for arbitrary
[0,R]^{d}
and
a_{\eta}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. The implicit constant is independent of
R
,
N
,
a_{\eta}
.

Proof.

Write
x=(\bar{x},x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
. The functions
(e(R\eta\cdot\bar{x}))_{\eta\in{\mathcal{P}}}
form an orthonormal system on
[0,1]^{d-1}
. We use Theorem
1.1
to find
S
with
|S|\sim N^{2/p}
such that for each
b_{\eta}\in{\mathbb{C}}

\frac{1}{R^{d-1}}\int_{[0,R]^{d-1}}|\sum_{\eta\in S}b_{\eta}e(\eta\cdot\bar{x})|^{p}d\bar{x}\lesssim\|b_{\eta}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}.

We then use this inequality with
b_{\eta}=a_{\eta}e(\Pi(\eta)x_{d})
and integrate over
x_{d}\in[0,R]
. This gives (
15
).

∎

We also need the
l^{2}
decoupling for
\Sigma
proved in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
R\gg 1
. Consider a partition of
\Sigma
into square-like caps
\theta
with diameter
\sim R^{-1/2}
. Given a (complex) measure
\mu
supported on
\Sigma
we denote by
\mu_{\theta}
its restriction to
\theta
.

We have

\|\widehat{\mu}\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}([0,R]^{d})}{\;\lessapprox}\;(\sum_{\theta}\|\widehat{\mu_{\theta}}\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}([0,R]^{d})}^{2})^{1/2}.

In our application,
\mu
will be a linear combination of Dirac deltas.

Proof.

(of Theorem
3.1
)

Let
\Theta
be a partition of
[0,1]^{d-1}
into cubes
\theta
with side length
\sim R^{-1/2}
. Since
|\theta\cap(R^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{d-1}|\sim R^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
, we may use Lemma
3.2
to create a set
S_{\theta}\subset\theta
with size
\sim(R^{\frac{d-1}{2}})^{2/p_{d}}

such that, writing
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\theta)=\{(\eta,\Pi(\eta)):\;\eta\in S_{\theta}\}
, we have for each
b_{\xi}

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\theta)}b_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p_{d}}dx\lesssim\|b_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}({\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\theta))}^{p_{d}}.

(16)

We let

{\mathcal{P}}_{R}=\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\theta).

Note first that our choice for
p_{d}
forces

|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|\sim|\Theta|R^{\frac{d-1}{p_{d}}}\sim R^{\frac{2d}{p_{d}}}.

Fix
a_{\xi}
. Then Theorem
3.3
implies that

\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p_{d}}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\left(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}(\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\theta)}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p_{d}}dx)^{2/p_{d}}\right)^{p_{d}/2}.

(17)

Now (
14
) follows by first applying (
17
), then (
16
) with
b_{\xi}=a_{\xi}
.

This argument relies crucially on the fact that
p=p_{d}
in two ways. First, this guarantees the right size for
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
. Second, (
17
) is no longer true for
p>p_{d}
.

∎

The critical index
p_{d}
is an even integer when
d=2
and
d=3
. For all even integers
p
, Rudin
[
17
]
has constructed explicit examples of
\Lambda(p)
sets with maximal size. To keep things simple and self-contained, and since our use of decoupling inevitably introduces
R^{\epsilon}
-losses, we will instead use an elegant, explicit construction of an almost
\Lambda(4)
-set of maximal size in two dimensions.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have for each
a_{n,m}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and each
\epsilon>0

\|\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}a_{n,m}e(n^{2}x+m^{2}y)\|_{L^{4}([0,1]^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}\|a_{n,m}\|_{l^{2}}.

Proof.

This is a well-known argument. We raise to the forth power, and analyze the 4-linear expression. The only contributing terms are those for which

\begin{cases}n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}=n_{3}^{2}+n_{4}^{2}\\
m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}=m_{3}^{2}+m_{4}^{2}\end{cases}.

Given
n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2}
, the values of
n_{3},n_{4},m_{3},m_{4}
are determined up to
N^{\epsilon}
many choices, due to the classical divisor bound.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Consider a smooth compact hypersurface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

\Sigma=\{(\eta,\Pi(\eta)):\;\eta\in[0,1]^{2}\}

with positive principal curvatures. For each
R\gg 1
consider the subset of
\Sigma

{\mathcal{P}}_{R}=\{(\eta,\Pi(\eta)),\;\eta=(\frac{i}{\sqrt{R}}+\frac{n^{2}}{R},\frac{j}{\sqrt{R}}+\frac{m^{2}}{R}):\;0\leq i,j<\sqrt{R}-1,\;1\leq n,m<R^{1/4}\}

with size
|{\mathcal{P}}_{R}|\sim R^{3/2}
. Then

\frac{1}{R^{3}}\int_{[0,R]^{3}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{4}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{4}

holds for arbitrary
a_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

The best known examples of such
\Sigma
are the sphere and the elliptic paraboloid.

An interesting contrast is provided by the hyperbolic paraboloid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

{\mathbb{H}}=\{(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{1}\eta_{2}):\;(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in[0,1]^{2}\}.

(18)

Its two principal curvatures have opposite signs.
The method in this section fails to produce a deterministic example of an almost
\Lambda(4)
-set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset{\mathbb{H}}
of maximal size
\sim R^{3/2}
, and we will shortly see why. Nevertheless, we are able to produce non-explicit examples. The argument combines an interesting decoupling recently proved in
[
12
]
with a refinement of Bourgain’s probabilistic estimate, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 3.6
.

For each
R\gg 1
there is a set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset{\mathbb{H}}
with cardinality
\sim R^{3/2}
such that

\frac{1}{R^{3}}\int_{[0,R]^{3}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{4}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{4}

holds for arbitrary
a_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

It was observed in
[
5
]
that
l^{2}(L^{p})
decoupling into square-like caps
\theta
(the analog of Theorem
3.3
) is false for each
p>2
. The reason is that
{\mathbb{H}}
contains lines. Each measure
\mu
supported on such a line would fail
l^{2}
decoupling, due to the lack of curvature.

In fact, through each point on
{\mathbb{H}}
there are two lines on
{\mathbb{H}}
. Their projections onto the
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
plane are parallel to either the
\eta_{1}
or the
\eta_{2}
axis. Remarkably, there is an almost partition of
{\mathbb{H}}
which leads to a successful
l^{2}
decoupling on the critical space
L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. Let us describe it.

For each dyadic number
R^{-1}\leq 2^{l}\leq 1
, we consider a partition
{\mathcal{U}}_{l}
of
[0,1]^{2}
into axis-parallel rectangles
U
with dimensions roughly
(2^{l},R^{-1}2^{-l})
. We call
{\mathcal{S}}_{l}
the collection of vertical projections
S
of these rectangles onto
{\mathbb{H}}
, and write
{\mathcal{S}}=\cup_{l}{\mathcal{S}}_{l}
. While
{\mathcal{S}}
is not a partition, it is immediate that each
\xi\in{\mathbb{H}}
is contained in only
\sim\log R
many
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

The following is Theorem 2.5 in
[
12
]
. It will be one of the main ingredients in our proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
R\gg 1
. Given a (complex) measure
\mu
supported on
{\mathbb{H}}
we denote by
\mu_{S}
its restriction to the set
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

We have

\|\widehat{\mu}\|_{L^{4}([0,R]^{3})}{\;\lessapprox}\;(\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}}\|\widehat{\mu_{S}}\|_{L^{4}([0,R]^{3})}^{2})^{1/2}.

The following proposition records the second ingredient needed in the proof of Theorem
3.6
. Since the sets in
{\mathcal{S}}
have some overlap, the selection of one exponential
e(\xi\cdot x)
will affect the
K_{p}
constant of all the sets in
{\mathcal{S}}
containing
\xi
. Addressing this needs some extra care.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
p>2
. Consider an orthonormal system
\Phi
consisting of
M
complex exponentials
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{M}
on
[0,1]^{d}
. For each subset
S
of
\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
we denote by
\Phi_{S}
the set
(\varphi_{i})_{i\in S}
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a collection of subsets of
\{1,\ldots,M\}
, each having cardinality
N
. Assume that, say,
|{\mathcal{S}}|\leq N^{2}
. Then there is a subset
G\subset\{1,\ldots,M\}
with cardinality
\sim MN^{\frac{2}{p}-1}
such that

K_{p}(\Phi_{G\cap S})\lesssim\log N

for each
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

We consider
\{0,1\}
-valued i.i.d.’s
\delta_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq M
, with
{\mathbb{P}}(\delta_{i}(\omega)=1)=N^{\frac{2}{p}-1}
. We let
G(\omega)=\{i:\;\delta_{i}(\omega)=1\}
and note the expected value
{\mathbb{E}}_{\omega}(|G(\omega)|)=MN^{\frac{2}{p}-1}
.
Then we have (see (
29
))

{\mathbb{P}}(|G(\omega)|\sim MN^{\frac{2}{p}-1})>\frac{1}{2}.

(19)

On the other hand, Theorem
5.2
(a) with
q=2
implies that
{\mathbb{P}}(K_{p}(\Phi_{G(\omega)\cap S})\geq\log N)\lesssim N^{-C_{p}\log N}
for each
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
and some
C_{p}>0
. Thus,

{\mathbb{P}}(K_{s}(\Phi_{G(\omega)\cap S})\leq\log N,\;\forall S\in{\mathcal{S}})\gtrsim 1-|{\mathcal{S}}|N^{-C_{p}\log N}>\frac{1}{2}.

Combining this with (
19
) leads to the desired conclusion.

∎

Proof.

(of Theorem
3.6
)

We consider the orthonormal system
\Phi
on
[0,1]^{3}
consisting of the functions

\varphi_{n,m}(x)=e(R(\frac{n}{R},\frac{m}{R},\frac{nm}{R^{2}})\cdot x),\;1\leq n,m\leq R.

Proposition
3.8
applied to
\Phi
with
p=4
,
M=R^{2}
,
N=R
delivers a set
G\subset\{1,\ldots,R\}^{2}
with cardinality
\sim R^{3/2}
such that

K_{4}(\Phi_{G\cap U})\leq\log R

(20)

for each
U\in\cup_{l}{\mathcal{U}}_{l}
. The
\log R
loss is harmless, as it is absorbed in the notation
{\;\lessapprox}\;
.

We define

{\mathcal{P}}_{R}=\{(\frac{n}{R},\frac{m}{R},\frac{nm}{R^{2}}):\;(n,m)\in G\}.

Then (
20
) means that for each
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
a_{\xi}

\frac{1}{R^{3}}\int_{[0,R]^{3}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\cap S}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{4}dx\leq\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}({\mathcal{P}}_{R}\cap S)}^{4}.

On the other hand, Theorem
3.7
implies that

\int_{[0,R]^{3}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{4}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\left(\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}}(\int_{[0,R]^{3}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\cap S}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{4}dx)^{1/2}\right)^{2}.

We combine the last two inequalities, and use the fact that each
\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
appears in
\sim\log R
many sets

, to conclude that

\frac{1}{R^{3}}\int_{[0,R]^{3}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{4}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{4}.

∎

It would be interesting to find an explicit set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
satisfying Theorem
3.6
. Also, rather intriguingly, the most natural analog of Theorem

[

A similar question may be asked for the moment curve
\Gamma^{d}
at the critical exponent

, for

. Even though

is an even integer, we do not know how to construct explicit examples. In order to help the reader understand the difficulty of this task, we present a decoupling proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 3.9
.

For each
p\geq p_{d}
and
R\gg 1
there is a set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset\Gamma^{d}
with
\sim R^{2d/p}
points satisfying

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}.

(21)

We will use the following
l^{2}
decoupling for the moment curve proved in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
R\gg 1
. Consider a partition of
\Gamma^{d}
into arcs
\tau
with length
\sim R^{-1/d}
. Given a (complex) measure
\mu
supported on
\Gamma^{d}
we denote by
\mu_{\tau}
its restriction to
\tau
.

We have

\|\widehat{\mu}\|_{L^{\gamma_{d}}([0,R]^{d})}{\;\lessapprox}\;(\sum_{\tau}\|\widehat{\mu_{\tau}}\|_{L^{\gamma_{d}}([0,R]^{d})}^{2})^{1/2}.

The key step is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.11
.

Each arc
\tau\subset\Gamma^{d}
with length
\sim R^{-1/d}
contains a set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\tau)
with cardinality
\sim R^{\frac{2d}{\gamma_{d}}-\frac{1}{d}}
such that for each
a_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{C}}

\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{[0,R]^{d}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\tau)}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{\gamma_{d}}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{\gamma_{d}}.

(22)

Moreover the points in
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\tau)
have the first coordinate in the set
R^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\pi_{d}:\Gamma^{d+1}\to\Gamma^{d}
the bijection

\pi_{d}(t,\ldots,t^{d+1})=(t,\ldots,t^{d}).

We use induction on
d
. The case
d=2
follows by using Theorem
1.1
, as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Indeed, there are
N\sim R^{1/2}
points on
\tau
with first coordinate in
R^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}
. It suffices to note that
N^{\frac{2}{\gamma_{2}}}\sim R^{\frac{4}{\gamma_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Assume we have verified the conclusion for some
d
, and let us prove it for
d+1
. Consider
\tau\subset\Gamma^{d+1}
with length
\sim R^{-\frac{1}{d+1}}
. We partition
\tau
into arcs
\tau^{\prime}
with length
\sim R^{-\frac{1}{d}}
.

Step 1.
The arc
\pi_{d}(\tau^{\prime})
on
\Gamma^{d}
has length
\sim R^{-\frac{1}{d}}
. Using our induction hypothesis and Fubini we may find
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\tau^{\prime})\subset\tau^{\prime}
with cardinality
\sim R^{\frac{2d}{\gamma_{d}}-\frac{1}{d}}
such that for each
a_{\xi}

\frac{1}{R^{d+1}}\int_{[0,R]^{d+1}}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\tau^{\prime})}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{\gamma_{d}}dx{\;\lessapprox}\;\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{\gamma_{d}}.

(23)

More precisely, the set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\tau^{\prime})
is the preimage
\pi_{d}^{-1}
of the set on
\pi_{d}(\tau^{\prime})
guaranteed by the induction hypothesis.
Let

{\mathcal{S}}_{R}(\tau)=\bigcup_{\tau^{\prime}\subset\tau}{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\tau^{\prime}).

Note first that

|{\mathcal{S}}_{R}(\tau)|\sim R^{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{d+1}}R^{\frac{2d}{\gamma_{d}}-\frac{1}{d}}.

Also, the points in
{\mathcal{S}}_{R}(\tau)
have the first coordinate in
R^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Step 2. We use Theorem
3.10
to separate the contribution from
\tau
into the contributions from various
\tau^{\prime}
. Via Fubini, this naturally extends to
l^{2}(L^{\gamma_{d}})
decoupling (sometimes referred to as

) for
\Gamma^{d+1}
, since
\pi_{d}(\Gamma^{d+1})=\Gamma^{d}

In order to provide a better context to the results in this section, we start by recalling the classical Chernoff-Hoeffding estimate for random selectors. Let
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}

\{0,1\}

(29)

We prove two theorems.

, but not on

(b)

We start by recalling the setup in
[
20
]
. Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a real Banach space. Its dual
X^{*}
is a Banach space equipped with the canonical dual norm

\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}=\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leq 1}|x^{*}(x)|.

Alternatively, we may recover the norm on
X
from its dual norm via the identity

\|x\|_{X}=\sup_{\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\leq 1}|x^{*}(x)|.

The closed unit ball of
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})

X_{1}=\{x\in X:\;\|x\|_{X}\leq 1\}

is a convex set with the property that for each
x\in X\setminus\{0\}

\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}:\;\lambda x\subset X_{1}\}

is a compact interval of positive length, centered at the origin. Conversely, given any
B\subset X
that is convex and such that
\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}:\;\lambda x\subset B\}
is a compact, symmetric interval of positive length for each
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
, the Minkowski functional of
B

p_{B}(x)=\inf\{\alpha>0:\;x\in\alpha B\}

defines a norm on
X
whose closed unit ball is
B
.

We use this strategy to introduce a family of alternative norms on
X^{*}
as follows. Fix some distinct
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
. Then for each
C>0
, we consider the set

X_{1,C}^{*}=\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:\;\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\leq 1\text{ and }\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x^{*}(x_{n})|^{2}\leq C\}.

This set can be seen to coincide with the closed unit ball of
X^{*}
when equipped with the norm

\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}_{C}}=\inf\{\alpha>0:\;x^{*}\in\alpha X_{1,C}^{*}\}.

This is the dual norm of the norm on
X
given by

\|x\|_{X_{C}}=\sup\{|x^{*}(x)|:\;\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\leq 1\text{ and }\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x^{*}(x_{n})|^{2}\leq C\}.

We call
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})

2
-convex
if there exists a constant
\eta>0
such that

\left\|\frac{x+y}{2}\right\|_{X}\leq 1-\eta\|x-y\|_{X}^{2}

(31)

for any

.

In our application, we will consider
X=L^{t}([0,1]^{d})
, for

Lemma 5.5

\mathcal{C}_{2}=\{\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}\beta_{n}\varphi_{n}:\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}|\beta_{n}|^{2}\leq C^{-1}\}
.

Let us now prove Lemma
5.6
.

Proof of Lemma
5.6
.

Note that

{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{p}([0,1]^{d})}^{p}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|f|\geq t\})dt

where
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure restricted to
[0,1]^{d}
. We choose a constant
D
such that
D^{p-2}=C_{1}
(this is possible since
p\geq q>2
) and split
{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{p}([0,1]^{d})}^{p}
according to the range of
t
as follows

{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{p}([0,1]^{d})}^{p}\approx\int_{0}^{1}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|f|\geq t\})dt+\int_{1}^{D}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|f|\geq t\})dt+\int_{D}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|f|\geq t\})dt.

It is easy to see that

\int_{0}^{1}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|f|\geq t\})dt\lesssim 1.

We turn to the second term. Since
{\left\|f\right\|}_{C_{1}}\leq 1
, Lemma
5.7
implies that
f=u_{1}+u_{2}
where
{\left\|u_{1}\right\|}_{L^{p_{1}}([0,1]^{d})}\leq 1
and
u_{2}=\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}\beta_{1,n}\varphi_{n}
such that
\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}|\beta_{1,n}|^{2}\leq C_{1}^{-1}
. In particular, we have
{\left\|u_{2}\right\|}^{2}_{L^{2}([0,1]^{d})}\leq C_{1}^{-1}
. Thus

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{D}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|f|\geq t\})dt&\leq\int_{1}^{D}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|u_{1}|\geq t/2\})dt+\int_{1}^{D}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|u_{2}|\geq t/2\})dt\\
&\leq\int_{1}^{D}t^{p-p_{1}-1}dt+C_{1}^{-1}\int_{1}^{D}t^{p-3}dt\lesssim 1.\end{split}

Lastly, since
{\left\|f\right\|}_{C_{2}}\leq\sqrt{\log N}
, Lemma
5.7
imply that
f=v_{1}+v_{2}
such that
{\left\|v_{1}\right\|}_{L^{p_{1}}([0,1]^{d})}\leq\sqrt{\log N}
and
v_{2}=\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}\beta_{2,n}\varphi_{n}
such that
\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}|\beta_{2,n}|^{2}\leq C_{2}^{-1}\log N
. Similarly,

\int_{D}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|f|\geq t\})dt\leq\int_{D}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|v_{1}|\geq t/2\})dt+\int_{D}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|v_{2}|\geq t/2\})dt.

Note that
C_{1}\gtrsim N^{c}
, where the constant
c
depends on
p
,
q
and
\kappa
. If
p=q
, then we may take
c=\kappa/2
. Therefore, we get

\int_{D}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|v_{1}|\geq t/2\})dt\lesssim(\log N)^{p_{1}/2}\int_{D}^{\infty}t^{p-p_{1}-1}dt\lesssim 1.

We find

{\left\|v_{2}\right\|}_{L^{q}([0,1]^{d})}\lesssim K_{q}(\Phi)\left(\sum_{n}|\beta_{2,n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim K_{q}(\Phi)(\log N)^{1/2}C_{2}^{-1/2}.

Also, we have that

{\left\|v_{2}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}([0,1]^{d})}\leq N^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n}|\beta_{2,n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim N^{1/2}(\log N)^{1/2}C_{2}^{-1/2}.

Since

C_{2}^{-1}\log N\lesssim K_{q}(\Phi)^{-\frac{2q}{p}}N^{1-\frac{q}{p}},

we interpolate the inequalities above to get

{\left\|v_{2}\right\|}_{L^{p}([0,1]^{d})}\leq{\left\|v_{2}\right\|}_{L^{q}([0,1]^{d})}^{q/p}{\left\|v_{2}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}([0,1]^{d})}^{1-q/p}\lesssim 1.

Finally,

\int_{D}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(\{|v_{2}|\geq t/2\})dt\lesssim{\left\|v_{2}\right\|}_{L^{p}([0,1]^{d})}^{p}\lesssim 1.

We conclude that
{\left\|f\right\|}_{p}\lesssim 1
.

∎

Corollary 5.8
.

For each set
S\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
we have

K_{p}(S)\lesssim 1+\|U_{S}\|_{C_{1}}+\frac{\|U_{S}\|_{C_{2}}}{\sqrt{\log N}}.

The implicit constant is independent of
N
.

Proof.

Let
f(x)=\sum_{n\in S}a_{n}\varphi_{n}
such that
\sum_{n\in S}|a_{n}|^{2}\leq 1
. Lemma
5.5
shows that

{\left\|f\right\|}_{C}\leq{\left\|U_{S}\right\|}_{C}.

Consider

\tilde{f}(x)=\frac{f(x)}{1+{\left\|U_{S}\right\|}_{C_{1}}+({\left\|U_{S}\right\|}_{C_{2}}/\sqrt{\log N})}.

Since
{\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|}_{C_{1}}\leq 1
and
{\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|}_{C_{2}}\leq\sqrt{\log N}
,
we may apply Lemma
5.6
to
\tilde{f}
and conclude that
\|\tilde{f}\|_{p}\lesssim 1
.

∎

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We invoke Corollary
5.8

to write

K_{p}(\Psi(\omega))\lesssim 1+\|U_{\Psi(\omega)}\|_{C_{1}}+\frac{\|U_{\Psi(\omega)}\|_{C_{2}}}{\sqrt{\log N}}.

Corollary
5.4
and Hölder implies that
{\mathbb{E}}(K_{p}(\Psi))\lesssim_{p,q}1
if
p>q
and
{\mathbb{E}}(K_{p}(\Psi))\lesssim\kappa^{-1/2}
if
p=q
. Since
|\Psi(\omega)|\sim K_{q}(\Phi)^{-2q/p}|\Phi|^{q/p}
with probability close to 1, there is
\Psi\subset\Phi
with desired properties.

∎

Let us turn to the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
\delta
,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be the constants in Corollary
5.4
. For sufficiently large
v
, we have the estimates

{\mathbb{P}}\left({\left\|U_{\Psi}\right\|}_{C_{1}}^{2}\geq v\right)\lesssim_{p,q}\exp(-C_{p,q}v)\qquad\text{if}\ q>p,

{\mathbb{P}}\left({\left\|U_{\Psi}\right\|}_{C_{1}}^{2}\geq\frac{v}{\kappa}\right)\lesssim_{q}\exp(-C_{q}v)\qquad\text{if}\ q=p,

and

{\mathbb{P}}({\left\|U_{\Psi}\right\|}_{C_{2}}^{2}\geq\log(N)v)\lesssim_{p,q}\exp(-C_{p,q}v).

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Corollary
5.4
. For sufficiently large
N
, we have
B_{1}=C_{1}
and
B_{2}=C_{2}
. We let
\epsilon_{1}=1/2(1-q/p)
if
q>p
and
\epsilon_{1}=\kappa/2
if
q=p
and let
\epsilon_{2}=(100K(\eta)\log N)^{-1}
. Then, (
33
) in Theorem
5.3
implies the desired inequalities.

∎

We can now prove Theorem
5.2
using a very similar argument.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

By Corollary
5.8
, we can write

K_{p}(\Psi(\omega))\lesssim 1+\|U_{\Psi(\omega)}\|_{C_{1}}+\frac{\|U_{\Psi(\omega)}\|_{C_{2}}}{\sqrt{\log N}}.

If
q>p
, there exists a constant
C
such that

{\mathbb{P}}(K_{p}(\Psi(\omega)\geq u)\leq{\mathbb{P}}({\left\|U_{\Psi(\omega)}\right\|}\geq Cu)+{\mathbb{P}}({\left\|U_{\Psi(\omega)}\right\|}\geq C\log(N)u).

Thus, Corollary
5.9
implies that
{\mathbb{P}}(K_{p}(\Psi(\omega)\geq u)\lesssim_{p,q}\exp(-C_{p,q}u^{2})
.

If
q=p
, there exists a constant
C
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(K_{q}(\Psi(\omega)\geq\frac{u}{\kappa}\right)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left({\left\|U_{\Psi(\omega)}\right\|}\geq C\frac{u}{\kappa}\right)+{\mathbb{P}}\left({\left\|U_{\Psi(\omega)}\right\|}\geq C\log(N)u\right).

Using Corollary
5.9
again, we obtain that
{\mathbb{P}}(K_{q}(\Psi(\omega)\geq u/\kappa)\lesssim_{q}\exp(-C_{q}u^{2})
.

∎

Remark 5.10
.

We presented our arguments in the context of real-valued systems, and with constants
K_{p}
defined using real coefficients
a_{n}
. To emphasize this distinction, let us denote this constant by
K_{p}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Given a complex-valued system, we may define the associated constant
K^{{\mathbb{C}}}_{p}
by allowing complex coefficients.

Let us consider an orthonormal system
\Phi

of complex exponentials

\phi_{n}(x)=e(s_{n}\cdot x)=\cos(2\pi s_{n}\cdot x)+i\sin(2\pi s_{n}\cdot x),

with
s_{n}\in S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. It is easy to see that this forces
s_{n}-s_{m}
to have at least one integer coordinate, for each
n,m\in S
.
Thus, the (appropriately normalized) systems
\Phi_{1}=\{\cos(2\pi s_{n}\cdot x):\;s_{n}\in S\}
and
\Phi_{2}=\{\sin(2\pi s_{n}\cdot x):\;s_{n}\in S\}

are themselves orthonormal systems, consisting of real-valued functions. The arguments we presented show that for each
i\in\{1,2\}
, a random selection of a subset
\Phi_{i}(\omega)
of
\Phi_{i}
will satisfy the specific requirements with probability very close to 1. So, in fact, such a generic selection will simultaneously work for both systems. Since

K_{p}^{{\mathbb{C}}}(\Phi(\omega))\sim K_{p}^{{\mathbb{R}}}(\Phi_{1}(\omega))+K_{p}^{{\mathbb{R}}}(\Phi_{2}(\omega)),

all the probabilistic results we presented extend to systems of complex exponentials.

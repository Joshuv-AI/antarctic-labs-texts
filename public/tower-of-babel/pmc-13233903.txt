Structure-aware fusion learning and intelligent decision support based on dynamic flight parameter hypergraphs in flight test
Authors: Tianchang Liu, Chen Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the bottlenecks in fusion analysis and decision support caused by strongly coupled dynamics, significant non-stationary disturbances, and cross-condition distribution shifts under high-frequency sampling conditions of multi-source flight parameters in flight test experiments, this paper proposes a structural perception fusion representation learning framework based on a dynamic flight parameter hypergraph. The method employs dual-stream gated multi-scale encoding in the temporal dimension to achieve scale decoupling and adaptive fusion, which respectively characterize transient mutations and low-frequency trends while suppressing noise-induced spurious correlations, thereby generating robust channel-level representations. In the structural dimension, flight parameter channels are modeled as nodes, enabling end-to-end learning of hypergraph associations that vary with window contents, and achieving cross-channel collaborative fusion through high-order message passing and normalized propagation across nodes, hyperedges, and nodes. A structural fingerprint is also constructed to support traceable analysis of key channel groups and collaborative mechanisms. At the geometric representation level, supervised contrastive learning constraints are introduced to compress intra-class variance and enlarge inter-class separation, enhancing metric consistency and cross-domain robustness of the embedding space, allowing the same representation to simultaneously adapt to state recognition, similar segment retrieval, and retrieval-enhanced prediction. Evaluated on complex engineering benchmarks and multiple random seeds, the proposed method outperforms sequence baselines such as LSTM and TCN in metrics such as F1 and PR-AUC. Ablation experiments further validate the critical contributions of dynamic hypergraph structural modeling and multi-scale encoding; changes in gated statistics and hyperedge allocation entropy reveal structural reconstruction patterns triggered by collaborative events. Results indicate that this framework provides a stable, reusable, and interpretable foundation for fusion representations in flight state assessment.Keywords: Intelligent decision support, Dynamic hypergraph, Structural awareness, Fusion learning, Temporal modelingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the continuous expansion of advanced aircraft flight test missions and the ongoing upgrades of airborne testing and telemetry systems, flight test data have shown significant growth in sampling frequency, number of channels, and information dimensions, exhibiting typical characteristics such as multi-source heterogeneity, strong coupling, and pronounced non-stationarity1,2. Modern flight testing is no longer confined to conventional verification of flight quality, control law effectiveness, and key performance indicators but increasingly emphasizes the proactive identification of potential risks, situational assessment, and rapid response capabilities under complex operating conditions and uncertain disturbances3–5. Consequently, the structural perception-based fusion analysis and intelligent decision support for flight test big data have gradually become essential foundational elements in the digitization and intelligence of flight test engineering. The evolution of flight test states exhibits high variability and multi-morphology, which can manifest as short-term spikes, localized oscillations, abrupt offsets, and transient drifts, or evolve covertly as slow degradation, bias accumulation, long-period fluctuations, and multi-channel collaborative mismatches6,7. Meanwhile, flight test data generally exhibit domain differences and distribution drift. Variations in aircraft platform types, payload configurations, meteorological conditions, sensor calibration states, and control strategies can induce systematic changes in marginal statistics and inter-channel dependency structures, leading to performance degradation and confidence distortion of models established within the training domain when applied to the test domain8,9. Under practical constraints such as high labeling costs, class imbalance, and strong noise interference, the key challenge for both academic research and engineering applications is how to learn stable, transferable, and reusable fusion representations from multi-source flight parameter time series and further support highly reliable situational recognition, similar segment retrieval, and auxiliary decision-making10.

In recent years, deep models such as recurrent neural networks, temporal convolutional networks, and Transformers have performed outstandingly in multivariate time series learning, capable of capturing time dependencies from local to long-range and supporting state recognition, trend prediction, and anomaly detection11,12. For instance, the Transformer, when applied to the remaining useful life (RUL) prediction of aircraft engines, combines Fourier-enhanced inverted Transformers and graph-enhanced spatiotemporal modeling to effectively capture global spatiotemporal dependencies and improve prediction performance13. Other studies have proposed a multi-scale hourglass-Transformer to address the challenges of capturing degradation trends across different time scales and extracting long-term dependencies for aircraft engine RUL prediction14. Furthermore, for sensor fault prediction and classification in drones, Transformers have also been utilized to develop innovative real-time diagnostic and preventive strategies15. Meanwhile, recent studies have increasingly emphasized transferable representation learning under heterogeneous operating conditions. For instance, fine-grained feature decoupling with multi-source domain adaptation has been shown effective for separating domain-invariant semantics from condition-specific interference in rotating machinery fault diagnosis16. Similarly, ConvFormer-based biconditional domain adaptation further demonstrates that combining convolutional inductive bias with attention-based contextual modeling can improve the extraction of informative and highly transferable features under cross-machine shifts17. These studies suggest that, beyond temporal dependency modeling, robust multivariate time series learning also requires improved transferability and resistance to distribution shifts. However, test flight data contain multi-source flight parameters characterized by strong coupling, strong non-stationarity, and cross-condition drift, making it difficult to achieve robust generalization by directly treating multi-channel inputs as independent dimensional sequences18. The underlying reason is the existence of complex physical coupling and control loop feedback among flight parameters, where the cross-channel dependency structure itself forms a critical part of the risk situational semantics. Ignoring this structure may cause models to rely on superficial waveform similarities rather than mechanism-consistent discriminative cues19. Test flight risk events are often presented as subsystem-level coordinated deviations, where multiple channels deviate from the baseline or exhibit cascading responses during specific periods. Detection and discrimination based on the assumption of channel independence are prone to false positives and missed detections20. Meanwhile, test flight signals exhibit significant multi-scale characteristics, with coexisting high-frequency transients and low-frequency drifts. A single receptive field cannot balance transient sensitivity with trend robustness, leading to unstable thresholds and confidence distortions under cross-task and cross-platform conditions21. For example, the ODDformer model addresses non-stationarity, global time factors, and the neglect of critical trends and seasonal characteristics in RUL prediction of aircraft engines by combining odd-even de-stationarization and decomposition techniques with Transformer structures.

To explicitly model channel relationships, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are used to learn adjacency and propagate information22,23, for example, the MDGNN model can capture hidden spatiotemporal dependencies among flight parameters, enabling simultaneous prediction of multiple flight safety events. In turbofan engine RUL prediction, GNNs have also been proposed to address the limitations of capturing spatial correlations among sensors24. However, GNNs mainly describe binary relationships, making it difficult to express higher-order synergies triggered by multichannel co-occurrences25, and graph construction often relies on static priors or heuristic measures, which can lead to structural mismatches and cumulative propagation noise under strong noise and nonstationary backgrounds26. To address this issue, the MGCL model employs multi-order graph neural networks and a cross-learning mechanism to capture higher-order dependencies among variables in multivariate time series and enhance anomaly detection performance. Furthermore, recent advancements have increasingly highlighted the critical role of graph-based approaches in tackling domain adaptation and distribution shifts, which are highly relevant to the cross-condition challenges in flight testing. For instance, hierarchical graph structures have been proposed to learn local-to-global topologies, effectively aligning feature distributions across source and target domains in semi-supervised settings27. Similarly, cross-domain knowledge distillation frameworks leveraging Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have shown significant promise in driving the generalization capabilities of multi-layer perceptrons under severe distribution shifts28. These pioneering works demonstrate that explicitly modeling structural topologies can significantly enhance cross-domain robustness. At the same time, recent studies on adaptive decision support and imbalanced risk prediction have further highlighted the importance of robustness under heterogeneous data conditions. For example, dynamic ensemble selection based on algorithm Shapley values provides an effective way to adaptively weight predictive evidence under changing data regimes29. In addition, iterative under-sampling methods for delisting risk prediction have shown that reliable decision support in practical systems must jointly consider class imbalance, uncertainty, and distribution heterogeneity30. Although these studies were developed in different application domains, they collectively reinforce the need for robust and transferable representation learning in complex flight test scenarios. However, applying these general domain adaptation methods directly to multi-source flight test data requires further consideration of the highly dynamic, multi-scale temporal nature and the high-order synergistic coupling (rather than just pairwise relations) inherent in complex aerodynamic responses.

Hypergraphs, which connect multiple nodes through hyperedges, are better suited to represent higher-order linkages, but still face challenges in dynamic correlation generation, higher-order propagation stability, information fidelity, task-coordination optimization, and verifiability of structural evidence in test flight scenarios31,32. For example, the STHFD model utilizes spatiotemporal hypergraphs to deal with the heterogeneous data characteristics and complex spatiotemporal dependencies in the fault diagnosis of aero-engine bearings. Furthermore, the graph-Mamba method based on hypergraphs has also been proposed for turbofan engine RUL prediction to integrate multi-sensor information and address challenges posed by time-varying and nonlinear signal characteristics. In addition to structural factors, cross-operating-condition distribution shifts introduce difficulties in representation alignment, as the appearance differences of the same semantic state are significant and interclass boundaries may overlap, making reliance on cross-entropy supervision prone to learning shortcuts to operating conditions. To address this, contrastive learning can enhance distribution alignment and discriminability through multi-view consistency and sample separation33. For example, the CAROTS model incorporates causal relationships into contrastive learning to improve the robustness of anomaly detection in multivariate time series34. The GDCMAD model proposes a graph-based dual contrastive representation learning method to address limited representation and discriminative capabilities in anomaly detection for multivariate time series35. Graph contrastive learning further aligns the relation patterns induced by structural design, but in the absence of temporal multi-scale decoupling and higher-order structural modeling, enhancements may disrupt semantics and weaken sensitivity to synergistic structural changes. Beyond the time-series domain, the broader efficacy of geometric representations and advanced contrastive learning in handling complex structural dependencies has been extensively validated, providing a more comprehensive perspective for resolving these structural bottlenecks. For instance, in knowledge-aware applications, multi-space interaction learning has been effectively utilized to disentangle complex structural semantics and optimize the geometric properties of latent spaces36. Furthermore, advanced contrastive paradigms, such as mask diffusion-based37 and hierarchy-aware knowledge-enhanced models38, have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in extracting robust, invariant structural features from highly coupled relational graphs. These cross-domain successes highlight the immense potential of geometric representation alignment in modeling complex structural data.

Therefore, it is urgently needed to introduce a unified framework with complementary inductive biases at the levels of temporal modeling, structural modeling, and representation geometry constraints to achieve stable structure-aware fused representation under complex operating conditions and support consistent measurement for situational awareness, similarity retrieval, and decision support39,40.

This study proposes a structure-aware fusion learning framework for multi-source flight test parameter time series, integrating dual-stream attention multi-scale encoding, dynamic flight parameter hypergraph convolution, and supervised contrastive learning into an end-to-end unified system. The method employs a fixed-length sliding window as a unit, following a design approach of temporal modeling first, structural fusion second, and geometric alignment last. The temporal module uses a fast pathway and a slow pathway to respectively capture high-frequency transients and low-frequency trends, achieving time-varying gated adaptive fusion to maintain sensitivity to bursts while enhancing robustness against noise and non-stationary disturbances, thereby providing a more stable channel-level node feature for relational learning. The structural module models flight parameter channels as nodes and dynamically learns hypergraph associations, achieving cross-channel collaborative fusion through high-order propagation from nodes to hyperedges and back to nodes, explicitly representing subsystem-level interactions and generating structural fingerprints to support the visual audit of key channel groups and collaborative structures. The geometric module introduces a supervised contrastive objective to align the distribution of similar samples under perturbations and cross-condition drifts, compressing intra-class variance and expanding inter-class separations, thus obtaining semantically consistent and measurable embedding representations that cater to state recognition, similarity retrieval, and retrieval-augmented prediction. The framework emphasizes the synergistic coupling of the three modules, learning disturbance-insensitive yet semantic-sensitive fused representations by employing contrastive gradient reverse constraints on temporal scale selection and dynamic structure reconstruction.

The innovation of this study lies in the synergistic and integrated design of temporal modeling, structural modeling, and representation geometric constraints, constructing an end-to-end structure-aware fusion learning framework that incorporates multi-scale temporal decoupling, high-order relational propagation, and embedding distribution alignment into a unified optimization target for joint training. The framework utilizes dual-stream gated encoding to generate robust channel-level features, where fast and slow pathways respectively depict high-frequency transients and low-frequency drifts, achieving adaptive fusion via time-varying gates to suppress the effects of non-stationary noise. At the structural level, it models flight parameter channels as nodes and dynamically learns hypergraph adjacency matrices, explicitly characterizing subsystem-level collaborative changes through high-order message passing from nodes to hyperedges and back to nodes with residual aggregation, while inducing structural fingerprints via the adjacency matrix to provide auditable evidence. At the representation level, supervised contrastive constraints are introduced to achieve alignment of similar instances and separation of dissimilar ones under noise and cross-condition drifts, enhancing cross-domain robustness and retrieval consistency. Associated module toggles and graph construction mode switches support rigorous ablation and reproducible experimental validations.

Models and methods

Overall architecture: structure-aware fusion encoder with three-module coordination

In flight test scenarios, multi-source flight parameters under high-frequency sampling conditions are simultaneously affected by control loop closed-loop feedback, strong coupling across subsystems, and external random disturbances, resulting in signals exhibiting significant non-stationarity, strong correlation, and phase dependency. The core challenge arising from this is that the observational representation of risk situations exhibits obvious inter-domain differences across different test subjects, load configurations, and flight phases, while different situations may show morphologically similar waveform segments in local time periods, thereby causing data-driven models to face problems such as difficulty in balancing multi-scale temporal morphology, high-order cross-channel synergistic relationships that dynamically evolve with phases, and representation misalignment caused by cross-condition distribution drift. Meanwhile, flight test engineering analysis not only focuses on situation discrimination conclusions but also emphasizes traceable evidence chains, requiring the ability to locate key moments and key channel linkage mechanisms, and support similar segment retrieval based on historical data to complete comparative verification and review decisions. Based on the above engineering constraints, this study unifies risk situation recognition and similar segment retrieval as a representation learning problem with sliding windows as basic units, learning discriminative, alignable, retrievable, and interpretable fusion representations for each fixed-length window to provide a consistent metric foundation for classification decisions, nearest neighbor retrieval, and retrieval-enhanced analysis. Around window-level input , this study constructs an end-to-end fusion encoder , introducing inductive biases in three complementary dimensions of temporal modeling, structural modeling, and representation geometric constraints to enhance cross-condition robustness and engineering auditability. The overall approach adopts a serial coordination paradigm of temporal-first, structural-second, and geometric-third, enabling temporal scale selection, dynamic structure reconstruction, and embedding space shaping to jointly converge under the same optimization objective, avoiding objective mismatch and error accumulation caused by modular design, as shown in Fig. 1. The engineering implementation takes structured modeling with sensors and flight parameter channels as nodes as the main thread, ensuring that model expression remains consistent with the synergistic mechanisms of flight test systems.

In the temporal dimension, targeting the characteristics of flight test signals with coexisting high-frequency transients and low-frequency trends accompanied by non-stationary disturbances, dual-stream gated multi-scale encoding is adopted to achieve scale decoupling and adaptive fusion. The fast stream focuses on characterizing short-term changes such as control inputs, transient coupling responses, and spike disturbances, while the slow stream emphasizes representing trend terms such as trim drift, gradual condition changes, and long-period oscillations. In the representation geometric dimension, to alleviate representation misalignment caused by cross-condition distribution drift and enhance measurement consistency, supervised contrastive learning constraints are introduced to explicitly promote alignment of similar samples under perturbation augmentation and condition changes while expanding intervals between dissimilar samples, thereby compressing intra-class variance and improving inter-class separability, making the embedding space possess more stable semantic consistency under metrics such as cosine similarity, balancing classification discrimination and nearest neighbor retrieval. Overall, this framework provides verifiable temporal and structural evidence interfaces while outputting situation discrimination results, and naturally supports similar segment retrieval and comparative analysis, better meeting the comprehensive requirements of flight test decision support for reliability, interpretability, and traceability.

Submodule 1: dual-stream attention

Multi-source flight parameters in test flights exhibit significant time-frequency heterogeneity and statistical non-stationarity under sliding window representation, arising from the combined effects of control input injection, control law or mode switching, and closed-loop coupled dynamics. Short-term strong transients and local mutations contain high-information-density risk clues that benefit situation discrimination and early warning, but are susceptible to contamination from sensor noise, quantization errors, and outlier spikes, inducing false correlations and false alarms. In contrast, trim drift, accumulated environmental disturbances, and gradual operating condition changes correspond to low-frequency trend components with stronger stability, yet may exhibit delayed response during sudden risk phases and weaken discrimination sensitivity. When a single pathway uniformly encodes full-band information, models often fall into the trade-off between transient sensitivity and noise robustness, manifesting as overfitting to high-frequency disturbances leading to degraded generalization or excessive smoothing that erases critical transients. To address this, a dual-stream attention temporal encoding module is introduced, as shown in Fig. 2, employing scale decoupling and gating fusion for multi-scale modeling. Two branches extract fast-varying and slow-varying representations with different temporal receptive fields respectively, and temporal attention generates time-varying gating coefficients for moment-by-moment adaptive weighting, thereby preserving transient discriminability while suppressing noise-induced unstable correlations, and providing more stable node features for higher-order relationship learning in subsequent structural modules. The gating sequence serves as an explicit intermediate variable that can locate critical moments and align control events with phase transitions, enhancing interpretability and analyzability.

Submodule 2: dynamic flight parameter hypergraph convolution

The discrimination of flight test risk situations typically does not arise from amplitude anomalies in a single channel but is more often reflected in the collaborative deviation and cascading response of multiple channels within specific time slices. For instance, attitude and angular velocity changes drive control surface deflection via closed-loop control, which further couples to the interactive evolution of engine parameters and structural loads. Such cross-subsystem associations exhibit high-order characteristics and phase dependencies. High-order characteristics signify that a situation is often triggered by the joint participation of multiple channels, making it difficult to be adequately expressed by pairwise correlations or binary edges. Phase dependency implies that under changes in flight phases, control law modes, and operating conditions, the coupling intensity and collaborative topology of the same group of channels can be restructured. Static graphs or fixed-topology GNNs tend to solidify phase-specific correlations as global priors, leading to structural mismatches across operating conditions. Meanwhile, heuristic graph construction based on correlation coefficients is difficult to align end-to-end with downstream recognition or retrieval targets. Therefore, this study introduces dynamic flight parameter hypergraph convolution on the channel node representations output by the temporal module. This involves constructing hypergraphs that adaptively change with the window content through learnable soft assignments and employing Node–Hyperedge–Node high-order propagation to fuse collaborative information in the structural dimension. Simultaneously, verifiable structural evidence is output to support engineering review. Dynamic graph construction and propagation can be collectively expressed as a unified mapping, as shown in Eq. This module models flight parameter channels as nodes and parameterizes potential synergistic events or linkage patterns as E hyperedges, using a soft association matrix to probabilistically assign nodes to hyperedges. Compared to binary edges in ordinary graphs, hyperedges can simultaneously connect multiple nodes, naturally characterizing multi-channel co-occurrence and synergistic triggering mechanisms.

To achieve end-to-end dynamic hypergraph construction, the incidence matrix is synthesized directly from the temporal node representations . The hyperedge cardinality serves as a structural hyperparameter denoting the maximum capacity of latent synergistic patterns, such as subsystem-level coupling modalities within the flight test system. Instead of relying on static physical priors, this capacity is empirically optimized through validation performance grid search, enabling the architecture to adaptively capture the optimal number of collaborative modes. In relatively clean conditions, a smaller is more favorable for emphasizing key collaborative structures, whereas in noisier or more strongly disturbed environments, a moderately larger helps avoid unstable structural activation caused by spurious local responses.

Although continuous soft assignments are imperative for preserving end-to-end differentiability during backpropagation, fully dense topological connections inherently risk introducing spurious correlations under high-noise operational environments. To mitigate this vulnerability, the temperature parameter is constrained to a fractional value to induce a sharper pseudo-sparse probability distribution. This regularization forces individual nodes to establish strong affiliations with a strictly limited subset of highly relevant hyperedges. Furthermore, during the extraction of the structural fingerprint A AT for interpretability analysis, a hard thresholding mask is implemented to systematically prune weak connectivities, thereby enforcing explicit structural sparsity and isolating only the most statistically significant collaborative frameworks. Crucially, the dynamic evolution of this hypergraph topology is governed at the temporal window resolution. Within any given sliding window, the temporal representation module aggregates the multivariate sequence into a unified node embedding , which subsequently dictates a single stable incidence matrix for that specific temporal slice. Consequently, the topological configuration remains invariant within individual windows to accurately characterize the dominant synergistic state, while dynamically reconstructing across consecutive windows. This inter-window evolutionary mechanism empowers the model to seamlessly track the transient migration of coupling relationships precipitated by flight phase transitions, control law reconfigurations, and operating condition drifts.

Subsequently, normalized hypergraph convolution is employed to complete high-order propagation, aggregating node features to hyperedge space through A and then back-propagating to node space, while introducing symmetric normalization of node degree and hyperedge degree to alleviate representation bias caused by uneven degree distribution, obtaining structurally enhanced node representations . To form a stable structural evidence interface, a structural fingerprint is further defined, which reflects the channel co-occurrence intensity induced by high-order synergy within the window and can be used for cross-window comparison of synergistic structural consistency and support visualization analysis of key channel groups.

This dynamic hypergraph design has advantages in mechanistic expression, cross-domain robustness, and interpretability. The hypergraph can explicitly model multi-channel high-order co-occurrence, enabling the model to maintain discriminative stability when similar situational waveforms have large differences but consistent synergistic relationships. The dynamically learned enables topology to adaptively reconstruct according to window content, covering relationship migration under phase switching, control law changes, and working condition drift while reducing structural mismatch, achieving integrated alignment of structural learning and task objectives. As intermediate quantities, reveals the membership structure from channels to synergistic patterns, and provides channel-level co-occurrence networks, facilitating mutual validation with linkage chain analysis in flight testing. Combined with the temporal module’s front-end suppression of non-stationarity and spike noise, the structural module learns high-order relationships on more stable node inputs, which can reduce noise-induced pseudo-synergy and improve the credibility of structural evidence.

Submodule 3: graph contrastive learning

Flight test risk situation recognition exhibits significant cross-condition distribution drift, where different subjects and configurations, weight and center of gravity, meteorological disturbances, and flight phases synchronously alter the marginal statistics of flight parameters and channel coupling structures. This causes the same semantic situation to present strong appearance differences in the observation space, while similar maneuvers and responses may lead to waveform confusion among different semantic situations. Relying solely on discriminative supervision such as cross-entropy is prone to inducing shortcut feature learning, resulting in insufficient intra-class aggregation and fragile inter-class margins in the embedding space, thereby weakening cross-condition generalization and similar segment retrieval consistency. Therefore, this study introduces graph contrastive learning on the hypergraph node representations output by the structural module, with the module architecture shown in Fig. 3. Through contrastive loss, semantic constraints are imposed in the embedding geometry to pull semantically consistent samples closer and separate semantically different samples, achieving feature space alignment and discriminative sharpening, while simultaneously improving classification robustness and cosine similarity-based retrieval measurability.

Graph contrastive learning module.

Diverging from conventional methodologies that contrast raw temporal vectors, the formulated contrastive objective is directly applied to the graph-level embeddings derived from the hypergraph readout function. This architectural adaptation guarantees that the optimization process enhances the discriminability of topological synergistic patterns rather than superficial numerical fluctuations. Through the projection of these structured representations into a contrastive latent space, the network is constrained to identify consistent subsystem coupling behaviors across disparate temporal windows, thereby fully exploiting the high-order relational inductive biases intrinsically provided by the dynamic hypergraph topology. Furthermore, this contrastive learning strategy provides profound robustness against the complex domain discrepancies frequently encountered in aerospace environments. Flight test data typically exhibits pronounced distribution shifts precipitated by fluctuating operational parameters including altitude variations, Mach number transitions, and dynamic flight maneuvers. While traditional cross-entropy objectives tend to establish fragile decision boundaries that deteriorate under these domain shifts, the supervised contrastive loss fundamentally optimizes the latent geometric space. By aggregating samples of identical system states collected under heterogeneous flight conditions as positive pairs, the objective function forces the network to filter out condition-specific perturbations and distill condition-invariant structural representations. The resultant tight intra-class clustering alongside maximized inter-class margins establishes a robust buffer against continuous distribution drifts, ultimately ensuring stable generalization capabilities across diverse and previously unseen flight phases.

At the implementation level, the graph contrastive learning module consists of three mutually coupled key operators. First, graph-level readout is applied to the node representations after hypergraph convolution, aggregating channel-level information into window-level global embeddings. Through attention pooling or weighted aggregation, the contribution of key channels is enhanced while suppressing discriminative bias caused by single-channel dominance, thereby forming holistic representations that better conform to system coupling mechanisms. Second, a projection head is introduced with normalization to map global representations to a contrastive embedding space more suitable for metric learning, improving optimization conditions and gradient scale consistency for contrastive objectives, enhancing training stability and maintaining consistency between similarity computation and cosine metrics. Finally, through graph view generation, two randomly perturbed views are constructed for the same sample, including augmentation strategies such as node feature masking, node or hyperedge dropout, association matrix perturbation, and slight temporal segment jittering. This enables the model to learn robust invariance and necessary equivariance under structural noise and local missing conditions, thereby enhancing domain generalization capability under cross-condition distribution drift. To ensure overall coherence of expression, projection mapping and supervised contrastive objectives can be jointly formulated and optimized in a unified end-to-end manner. Compared to using cross-entropy alone, this strategy better suppresses incidental correlations related to specific conditions and enhances the stability of semantic discrimination; compared to unsupervised contrast, it leverages label structure to reduce the risk of same-class samples being erroneously repelled, particularly suitable for risk situation identification scenarios with fine class boundaries and significant appearance overlap. Furthermore, given that inputs derive from structural representations after dynamic hypergraph fusion, contrastive learning essentially aligns samples at the structural consistency level, helping maintain the transferability of channel interaction mechanisms across different flight phases, thereby improving cross-domain robustness and retrieval reliability.

Engineering discussions: robustness and transferability

Robustness to telemetry degradation

Beyond the architectural contributions validated above, the proposed framework also exhibits strong robustness in harsh flight test environments.

In real-world flight test operations, data streams are frequently subjected to severe degradation, including sensor failures, missing telemetry channels, and environmental noise. To evaluate the fault tolerance and robustness of the proposed framework, we conducted supplementary experiments under simulated degraded conditions.

Theoretically, the proposed framework possesses inherent resilience to such anomalies. First, the dynamic hypergraph topology provides significant structural redundancy. Unlike simple pairwise graphs, higher-order hyperedges capture multi-variable correlations; thus, the failure of a single sensor node does not collapse the regional topological semantics, as the remaining nodes within the hyperedge sustain the structural integrity. Second, the temporal attention mechanism adaptively down-weights the influence of corrupted channels. Finally, the contrastive learning objective inherently acts as a denoising regularizer by enforcing representation invariance across augmented and perturbed views.

To quantitatively assess the robustness of the proposed model, its performance was further examined on a severely corrupted test set constructed to emulate realistic flight-test uncertainties. Specifically, sensor channels were randomly masked at rates of 10%, 20%, and 30% to simulate telemetry loss and sensor malfunction, while additive Gaussian noise was introduced to represent signal quality degradation and measurement contamination. The experimental results indicate that the proposed framework exhibits strong resistance to data corruption and maintains stable predictive performance even under substantial input degradation. In particular, when the channel missing rate reached 20%, the decline in the primary evaluation metric, represented by the F1 score, remained limited to approximately 15.1%, while the overall classification accuracy was still preserved above 86.0%. By comparison, baseline models without dynamic hypergraph modeling experienced markedly more severe performance deterioration under the same perturbation settings, with the corresponding reduction exceeding 27.2%. This substantial discrepancy highlights the effectiveness of the proposed framework in preserving discriminative feature representations under incomplete and noisy observations. These results provide strong quantitative evidence that the model possesses superior reliability, fault tolerance, and robustness, thereby demonstrating its suitability for deployment in harsh, uncertain, and operationally variable flight-test environments.

The outstanding robustness demonstrated by the model is mainly attributed to the synergistic gain between the dynamic hypergraph convolutional network and the contrastive learning mechanism. Although no specific modeling for sensor fault parameters was explicitly introduced during training, the model still maintains high recognition stability and prediction reliability when faced with severe data loss, signal distortion, and random noise interference. Mechanistically, this is primarily due to the higher-order relational modeling capabilities inherent in the hypergraph structure. Unlike traditional CNNs or LSTMs, which mainly rely on local receptive fields or fixed sequence structures for feature extraction, dynamic hypergraphs incorporate multiple sensors with similar functional attributes or coupled behaviors into the same hyperedge through a “node-hyperedge-node” message-passing approach, thus preventing a single sensor failure from directly causing an information link disruption. Even when a node experiences missing data or anomalies, its associated hyperedge can still aggregate and share semantic information from other related nodes and further propagate this group-level representation back to the anomalous node, achieving an implicit compensation and reconstruction of the locally failed features. At the same time, the learning process of the dynamic correlation matrix endows the model with significant adaptive noise reduction capabilities. Since the association strength between nodes and hyperedges is dynamically generated based on current features, when a sensor is subject to strong noise interference, its feature distribution deviates from the normal structural pattern, resulting in lower weights during similarity computation and Softmax normalization, thereby mitigating its adverse impact on the overall representation learning. In other words, the model can automatically suppress the interference of abnormal nodes through the dynamic reweighting mechanism driven by structural consistency. Moreover, the introduction of Gaussian noise augmentation and InfoNCE contrastive loss during training further enhances the encoder’s tolerance to disturbances, enabling the model to learn discriminative representations in the latent space that are insensitive to small input perturbations while maintaining stability in semantic structures. Therefore, when encountering previously unseen sensor failures during testing, the model can still map the damaged samples to the correct semantic neighborhood based on overall structural relationships, without being easily misled by local outliers. In summary, the proposed method achieves feature-level information bridging and self-recovery through the global connectivity of the hypergraph, and enhances structural invariance in the representation space through contrastive learning, thereby demonstrating robustness and generalization capabilities superior to traditional baseline models under complex noise environments and conditions of severe data loss. At the same time, we also want to clarify that in cases of severe degradation of telemetry quality, such as prolonged loss of frame synchronization, a large amount of flight parameter and engineering data will be filled with padding words, making decision-making impossible. Naturally, under such circumstances, ground monitoring will contact the crew to confirm the status of the telemetry signal terminal and pause the flight test.

Transferability across mission profiles and aircraft types

In practical flight-test engineering, the cost of large-scale data annotation and repeated model development for every new scenario is often considerable. Therefore, beyond within-dataset accuracy, it is also important to examine whether the proposed framework can remain effective across different mission profiles and different aircraft types.

For cross-mission profiles, like different flight sorties or maneuver conditions of the same aircraft model, the proposed framework is expected to exhibit relatively strong adaptability. Although variations in mission execution, environmental disturbance, and operating conditions may lead to changes in local signal morphology, the underlying aerodynamic configuration, control architecture, and major inter-parameter coupling mechanisms remain essentially unchanged for the same aircraft platform. Under such conditions, the dynamic hypergraph is able to preserve structurally meaningful associations among flight parameters, while the dual-stream temporal encoder captures both short-term transient responses and long-term variation trends. As a result, the learned representation is less sensitive to mission-specific waveform fluctuations and more focused on stable dynamic patterns that are repeatedly manifested across sorties of the same aircraft.

For different aircraft types, the modeling task becomes more challenging because variations in aerodynamic layout, inertial characteristics, and control response can lead to noticeable differences in multi-parameter dynamic behavior. In particular, aircraft with different tail configurations often exhibit distinct coupling patterns and response characteristics under similar operating conditions. Therefore, two representative test platforms, namely a T-tail test aircraft and a conventional-tail test aircraft, were considered in this study, as shown in Fig. 9. Owing to their evident differences in configuration and aerodynamic response characteristics, these two aircraft provide a suitable basis for evaluating the applicability of the proposed framework across heterogeneous aircraft types.

Conclusion

This study proposed a structure-aware fusion learning framework for multi-source flight-test parameter time series, integrating dual-stream gated temporal encoding, dynamic hypergraph-based structural modeling, and supervised contrastive representation learning within a unified architecture. The framework was designed to address strong non-stationarity, multi-scale temporal variation, high-order cross-channel coupling, and cross-condition distribution shifts in complex flight-test scenarios. Experimental results on the benchmark considered in this work show that the proposed method achieves consistently improved average performance over representative sequential and Transformer-based baselines across multiple random seeds, particularly in F1 and PR-AUC. Ablation analysis further suggests that dynamic hypergraph modeling is a major contributor to the observed gains, while dual-stream temporal encoding and supervised contrastive learning improve representation robustness and stability. In addition, the learned embeddings support not only state recognition but also similar-segment retrieval and retrieval-assisted analysis in the evaluated scenarios, and the temporal gating and structural analysis provide interpretable cues for examining key intervals and channel collaboration patterns.

The limitations of this study are reflected in three aspects. First, the retrieval-assisted prediction results demonstrate feasibility in the current setting, but additional validation is required before the proposed framework can be considered a stand-alone predictive tool for safety-critical operational deployment. Second, while the interpretability analyses provide meaningful structural and temporal evidence, these outputs should be understood as model-derived explanatory cues rather than direct verification of underlying physical causality. Finally, as with many data-driven approaches in flight-test analysis, the proposed framework remains constrained by practical factors such as limited labeled samples, class imbalance, annotation uncertainty, and the potential degradation of performance under severely compromised telemetry quality.

Overall, the present results suggest that combining temporal multi-scale modeling, dynamic high-order structural learning, and geometric representation alignment is a promising direction for flight-test fusion analysis and intelligent decision support. Future work will focus on broader validation and more rigorous assessment under real-world operational constraints.

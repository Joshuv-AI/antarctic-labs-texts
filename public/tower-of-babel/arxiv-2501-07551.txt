P P -trivial MMP, Zariski decompositions and minimal models for generalised pairs
Zhengyu Hu

Abstract
. We develop P P -trivial MMPs whose steps are trivial with respect to a given nef divisor P P , and an inductive method for generalised pairs without the NQC assumption. For such an MMP, rational approximation with fixed nef data controls extremal intersection numbers and scaling numbers. Under a rationality hypothesis on K X + B + M − P K_{X}+B+M-P , we establish finiteness of adjunction coefficients under divisorial adjunctions, yielding a special termination theorem. This includes the case P = 0 P=0 . As applications, we prove that a projective lc g-pair ( X , B + M ) (X,B+M) of dimension 3 3 has a minimal model if K X + B + M K_{X}+B+M birationally admits a Nakayama–Zariski decomposition with nef positive part and either K X + B + M ≡ D ≥ 0 K_{X}+B+M\equiv D\geq 0 or M ≡ D ≥ 0 M\equiv D\geq 0 . The same conclusion holds for klt g-pairs in arbitrary dimension under these hypotheses, and for projective lc g-pairs of dimension 4 4 with a birational Nakayama–Zariski decomposition with nef positive part and log abundant nef data. These results extend a theorem of Birkar and Hu to generalised pairs under the above hypotheses.

1.
Introduction

We work over an algebraically closed uncountable field
k
of characteristic zero. All varieties are
quasi-projective over
k
and a divisor refers to an
\mathbb{R}
-Weil divisor unless stated otherwise.

Generalised pairs arise naturally in the canonical bundle formula and play a central role in birational geometry
[
BZh16
,
Bir21b
]
. They are used in the proofs of the Borisov–Alexeev–Borisov conjecture
[
Bir19
,
Bir21a
]
and the M
c
Kernan–Shokurov conjecture
[
Bir23
]
. Much of their minimal model theory assumes that the nef data are NQC, that is, a finite non-negative real linear combination of nef
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors; see
[
CLX23
,
LX23a
,
LX23b
,
TX24
,
Xie24
]
. Without this assumption, numerical nonvanishing can fail, and the usual control of coefficients under adjunction is no longer available from a rational decomposition of the nef part. Non-NQC generalised pairs are also related to the birational geometry of Kähler varieties
[
DH23
,
DHY23
,
DH24
,
HP24
]
and to foliations
[
CHLX23
]
.

The main contribution of this paper is an inductive method for studying lc generalised pairs without the NQC assumption. The method has two ingredients. First, rational approximation of auxiliary divisors and boundaries gives control of extremal intersections and scaling numbers. This allows us to construct
P
-trivial MMPs. Second, along suitable MMPs of this type, we obtain finite sets of divisorial adjunction coefficients. The induced maps on lc strata then lift to lower-dimensional MMPs, allowing us to apply lower-dimensional termination results.

If a projective lc g-pair
(X,B+M)
has a minimal model, then
K_{X}+B+M
birationally admits a Nakayama–Zariski decomposition with nef positive part. We consider the converse.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a projective lc g-pair. Suppose there is a birational model
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
such that
P_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M))
is nef. Then
(X,B+M)
has a minimal model.

I do not know whether the conjecture should be expected without any positivity assumption.

For usual pairs, Conjecture
1.1
was proved by Birkar and the author
[
BH14
]
, using an MMP which is trivial on the nef positive part
P
. Related results for NQC generalised pairs appear in
[
TX24
]
; the case where either the nef data or
P
is NQC is discussed in Remark
3.19
. Here neither is required to be NQC. The additional numerical hypotheses in our results provide the effectivity needed for perturbation; they are not automatic, as the following example shows.

The following example is due to Han and Liu
[
HL20
, Example 1.3]
, based on a construction of van Dobben de Bruyn
[

]
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
E
be a non-CM elliptic curve and
X=E\times E
. Fix
p\in E
and write
F_{1}=\{p\}\times E
,
F_{2}=E\times\{p\}
and
\Delta
for the diagonal. The divisor

M=F_{1}+\sqrt{2}F_{2}+(\sqrt{2}-2)\Delta

is nef, satisfies
M^{2}=0
, and spans an irrational extremal ray of the nef cone. If
M\equiv D\geq 0
, every prime component of

is nef. By extremality, their numerical classes must lie on this ray, which is impossible since they are nonzero integral classes. Moreover, the positive intersections
M\cdot C
, for curves

, are not bounded away from zero
[
vDdB18
]
. Thus
(X,0+M)
is a klt g-pair with nef log canonical divisor for which numerical nonvanishing fails.

In this example the curves are
K_{X}
-trivial. Example

exhibits positive intersections tending to zero even along

-negative extremal curves. Thus the positive lower bounds used in a

-trivial MMP require additional hypotheses.

Control of intersection numbers without NQC.

The following lemma is the basic tool, extending the argument of
[
BH14
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem 1.3

(
=Lemma
3.8
)
.

Let
(X/Z,B+M)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt g-pair and
P
be a nef
/Z
divisor.
Suppose that

{\operatorname{Supp}}\{P\}_{ir}\subseteq{\operatorname{Supp}}\{B\},

where

is

-trivial for all sufficiently large

The point is to approximate

P
-intersections on negative extremal curves of minimal length; rational independence preserves this control along
P
-trivial steps. Theorem

With suitable representatives, an ample boundary perturbation

The following theorem establishes Conjecture

The inductive method.

Write
L=K_{X}+B+M=P+N
, with
P
nef and
N
a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. A
P
-trivial MMP preserves the b-divisor determined by
P
. Using Lemmas
3.8
and
4.1
, we construct such a sequence with scaling of a birationally ample divisor
A
by successively running finite MMPs for

L+\lambda_{i}A+\alpha_{i}P,\qquad\lambda_{i}\downarrow 0,

where
\alpha_{i}
may increase as
\lambda_{i}
decreases (Definition
4.7
).

The main difficulty is to control the induced maps on lc strata without an NQC decomposition of the nef data. Our approach uses the rationality of the remainder
N
: since the b-divisor determined by
P
is fixed, discrepancy differences are governed by
N
. Theorem
4.16
combines this control with local Cartier indices to place the adjoint boundary coefficients in finite sets. Its last assertion preserves the required form of the coefficients of
N
under restriction, so the argument can be repeated on lower-dimensional strata.

Using this finiteness, we follow Fujino’s special termination argument
[
Fuj07
]
to obtain the following inductive step.

Theorem 1.6

(
A special termination, =Theorem
4.18
)
.

Let
(X/Z,B+M)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt g-pair with

K_{X}+B+M=P+N
, where
P
is nef
/Z
and
N
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor whose support contains no lc centres. Consider a
P
-trivial MMP with scaling of a birationally ample
/Z
divisor
A
, and an lc centre
S
which is not contracted. If the induced maps
S^{i}\dashrightarrow S^{i+1}
are isomorphisms on
\left\lfloor{B_{S^{i}}}\right\rfloor
for
i\gg 0
, then after discarding finitely many steps, they lift to a
P^{i}|_{\widetilde{S}^{i}}
-trivial MMP on small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorialisations
\widetilde{S}^{i}
, with scaling of the induced divisors
A^{i}|_{\widetilde{S}^{i}}
.

The theorem makes induction on the dimension of lc strata available in this non-NQC setting. Lower-dimensional termination results then give termination near
\left\lfloor{B}\right\rfloor
. A further step is needed to obtain an MMP on
L+\alpha P
for a fixed
\alpha
, with the same scaling numbers. We call this degeneration (Definition
4.19
). Under its numerical hypotheses, Theorem
4.21
(3) obtains degeneration from termination near the lc centres and degenerations on the lc centres. Example
4.22
illustrates the distinction between these two requirements. When
P=P_{\sigma}(L)
, degeneration yields a minimal model by Lemma
4.28
.

When
P=0
, the adjoint divisor
K_{X}+B+M=N
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. In subsequent joint work, Hu and Liu
[
HL26
, Section 6]
extend this special-termination method to linearly decomposable generalised pairs, using convex decompositions into rational adjoint divisors.

Higher-dimensional and related results.

Theorems
4.29
and
4.32
give higher-dimensional versions of the lc results. Under log effectivity of the nef data or the positive part, the former reduces the problem in dimension
d
to the restricted termination conjecture
4.23
in dimension
d-1
. Under log abundance of the nef data, the latter assumes termination of MMPs for usual dlt pairs in dimensions at most
d-1
.

Without a lower-dimensional termination assumption, we obtain weak minimal models. This notion, recalled in Definition
3.16
, is weaker than that of a minimal model. Their existence is equivalent for NQC g-pairs (Proposition
3.17
), but this is not known for non-NQC g-pairs.

Theorem 1.7

(
=Theorem
4.36
)
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a projective lc g-pair with data
M^{\prime}
. Assume
K_{X}+B+M
birationally has a Nakayama–Zariski decomposition with nef positive part. Suppose either
M^{\prime}
or the positive part is log effective. Then
(X,B+M)
has a weak minimal model.

If, in addition,
\mathbf{B}_{-}(K_{X}+B+M)
is disjoint from the lc centres, a minimal model exists. We also prove an anticanonical analogue under a klt generalised
\mathbb{R}
-complement and numerical effectivity assumptions (Theorem

Boundedness of intersection numbers.

We record bounds for intersection numbers with extremal curves, which will be used to run the MMP for non-NQC g-pairs.

Definition 3.4

(Extremal curves of minimal length)
.

Let
X\to Z
be a projective morphism from a normal variety and
\Gamma
be a curve on
X
. We say
\Gamma
is
extremal of minimal length
if

•

\Gamma
generates an extremal ray of
\overline{NE}(X/Z)
; and

•

H\cdot\Gamma
is minimal for all curves in the extremal ray, where
H
is an ample
/Z
Cartier divisor.

We use the length bound in the following form.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X\to Z
be a projective morphism from a normal variety and
\Gamma
be an extremal curve of minimal length. Then for any boundary
B
and data
M^{\prime}
such that
(X,B+M)
is lc, we have
(K_{X}+B+M)\cdot\Gamma\geq-2\dim X
.

Lemma 3.6

(Boundedness of intersection numbers)
.

Let
(X/Z,B+M)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt g-pair and
D
be a divisor. Suppose
(X,(B+tD)+M)
is lc for some real number
t>0
. Then there is a number
l>0
depending on
t
and
\dim X
such that, for any
(K_{X}+B+M)
-negative extremal curve
\Gamma
of minimal length,
D\cdot\Gamma>-l
.

Proof.

Since
(X,B+tD+M)
is lc, we have
(K_{X}+B+tD+M)\cdot\Gamma\geq-2\dim X
, hence
D\cdot\Gamma>-2\dim X/t
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

With the notation above, if a birational contraction
X\dashrightarrow X^{i}
is
(K_{X}+B+tD+M)
-non-positive (for example, an MMP on
K_{X}+B+tD+M
), then the lower bound
l
is preserved for the birational transform
D^{i}
of
D
.

We immediately obtain the next lemma.

Lemma 3.8

(
cf.
[
BH14
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
(X/Z,B+M)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt g-pair and
P
be a nef divisor. Suppose that
{\operatorname{Supp}}\{P\}_{ir}\subseteq{\operatorname{Supp}}\{B\}
, where
\{\cdot\}_{ir}
denotes the irrational fractional part (the fractional part with irrational coefficients). Then any MMP on
K_{X}+B+M+\alpha P
is
P
-trivial for all
\alpha\gg 0
.

Proof.

Let
P=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}P_{i}
be a convex combination of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors such that the coefficients
\alpha_{j}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent and
\|P_{j}-P\|\ll 1
. Let
N:=K_{X}+B+M-P
. Since there are boundaries
B_{j}
such that
(X,B_{j}+M)
is dlt and

, for any
(K_{X}+B+M)
-negative extremal curve

of minimal length,

is bounded from below by Lemma

and

is bounded away from zero. Pick

so that

Let

are birational transforms. Inductively, suppose every step is

-trivial step is also

4.

-trivial MMP and its applications

4.1.
P
-trivial MMP

The following lemma allows us to run an MMP with scaling of a prescribed divisor, such as an ample divisor, for non-NQC g-pairs.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X/Z,B+M)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt g-pair. Let
C
be a divisor such that
(X,(B+C)+M)
is dlt and
K_{X}+(B+C)+M
is nef. Suppose that
{\operatorname{Supp}}\{M\}_{ir}\subseteq{\operatorname{Supp}}\{B+C\}
. Let

\lambda=\inf\{t\geq 0|K_{X}+B+tC+M\text{ is nef}\}.

If
\lambda>0
, then there exists a
(K_{X}+B+M)
-negative extremal curve
\Gamma
such that
(K_{X}+B+\lambda C+M)\cdot\Gamma=0
. Moreover, we can run an MMP
/Z
on
K_{X}+B+M
with scaling of
C
.

Proof.

After a small perturbation, we may assume that
{\operatorname{Supp}}\{M\}_{ir}\subseteq{\operatorname{Supp}}\{B+C\}\subseteq{\operatorname{Supp}}\{B\}
. Let
\Gamma_{i}
be extremal curves of minimal length generating the
(K_{X}+B+M)
-negative rays, and define
\lambda_{i}>0
by
(K_{X}+B+\lambda_{i}C+M)\cdot\Gamma_{i}=0
. Then
\lambda=\sup_{i}\lambda_{i}
and

\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}=\frac{(K_{X}+B+C+M)\cdot\Gamma_{i}}{-(K_{X}+B+M)\cdot\Gamma_{i}}+1.

Choose convex decompositions
B=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}B_{j}
and
B+C=\sum_{k}\beta_{k}\Delta_{k}
with
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent coefficients, such that
B_{j}+M,\Delta_{k}+M
are
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors and the corresponding g-pairs are dlt. For some integer
m>0
, the length bound gives

(K_{X}+B_{j}+M)\cdot\Gamma_{i}=\frac{n_{i,j}}{m},\qquad(K_{X}+\Delta_{k}+M)\cdot\Gamma_{i}=\frac{n^{\prime}_{i,k}}{m},

where
n_{i,j},n^{\prime}_{i,k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-2m\dim X}
. Thus

\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}=\frac{\sum_{k}\beta_{k}n^{\prime}_{i,k}}{-\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}n_{i,j}}+1,\qquad 0<-\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}n_{i,j}\leq 2m\dim X,

so the supremum is attained.

The same argument applies on subsequent models. At
X^{n}
, the pair with boundary
B^{n}+\lambda_{n-1}C^{n}
is lc. If
\lambda_{n}<\lambda_{n-1}
, use
tC^{n}
with
\lambda_{n}<t<\lambda_{n-1}
. Otherwise, let
m
be the first index with
\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{n}
and choose the above decomposition of
B^{m}+\lambda_{m}C^{m}
. The intervening steps are trivial for its rational adjoints, so their transforms on
X^{n}
remain lc.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

With the notation and assumptions of Lemma
4.1
, let
P
be a nef divisor with
{\operatorname{Supp}}\{P\}_{ir}\subseteq{\operatorname{Supp}}\{B\}
and

\lambda(\alpha)=\inf\{t\geq 0|K_{X}+B+tC+M+\alpha P\text{ is nef}\}.

Then there exists a number

such that
\lambda(\alpha)
is independent of
\alpha
for
\alpha\geq l
. Moreover, any MMP
/Z
on
K_{X}+B+M+lP
with scaling of
C
is
P
-trivial and is also an MMP
/Z
on
K_{X}+B+M+\alpha P
with scaling of
C
for every
\alpha\geq l
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.8
there exists a number
l>0
such that
P\cdot\Gamma=0
for any
(K_{X}+B+M+lP)
-negative extremal curve
\Gamma
of minimal length. For
\alpha\geq l
, nefness of
P
gives
\lambda(\alpha)\leq\lambda(l)
. Suppose the inequality is strict and choose an extremal curve
\Gamma
on which
K_{X}+B+\lambda(\alpha)C+M+lP
is negative. Since
K_{X}+B+C+M+lP
is nef and
0\leq\lambda(\alpha)<\lambda(l)\leq 1
, the ray is also
(K_{X}+B+M+lP)
-negative. Thus

, contradicting the nefness of

∎

Definition 4.3

(
P
-nefness)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety projective over
Z
and
P
be a nef divisor. An
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor

is said to be
P
-nef (resp.

-pseudo-effective)
if for some ample
/Z

, for any sufficiently small number

, there exists a number
\alpha>0
such that

is nef (resp. pseudo-effective).

Note that the above definition is independent of the choice of the ample divisor

. An
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor

is

-nef if and only if

D\cdot\gamma\geq 0\quad\text{for every }\gamma\in\NEbar(X/Z)\cap P^{\perp}.

Indeed, on the compact slice
A\cdot\gamma=1
, the divisor

is positive near

, while
P\cdot\gamma
is bounded away from zero on the complement. The converse follows by letting
\epsilon

Example 4.4

(

Let

We apply the preceding induction to lc g-pairs with weak Zariski decompositions, generalising Corollary

Weak Zariski decompositions.

Given a pseudo-effective divisor

is nef and

. We propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.23
.

Let
(X/Z,B+M)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt g-pair and

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Given a

0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and every prime divisor
D
over
W
with
\sigma_{D}(L_{W}+\alpha P_{W}+\epsilon A)>0
,

a(D,W,B_{W}+\epsilon A+M_{W})+\sigma_{D}(L_{W}+\alpha P_{W}+\epsilon A)>1.

Proof.

Replacing
(X,B+M)
, assume it is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt and
K_{X}+B+M=P+N
is its Nakayama–Zariski decomposition with
P
nef. Take a log resolution
\pi:(W,\Delta_{W}+M_{W})\to X
of
(X,(B+N)+M)
such that the components of
\{\Delta_{W}^{>0}\}
are disjoint, where
K_{W}+\Delta_{W}+M_{W}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M)
. Write

\Delta_{W}-\pi^{*}N=B_{W}-N_{W},

where
B_{W},N_{W}\geq 0
have no common components. Then
K_{W}+B_{W}+M_{W}=\pi^{*}P+N_{W}
is its Nakayama–Zariski decomposition, and a minimal model of
(W,B_{W}+M_{W})
is a weak minimal model of
(X,B+M)
. Increasing the coefficients of
B_{W}
and
N_{W}
by the same sufficiently small amounts makes
N_{W}
rational and preserves
\mu_{D}(B_{W}-N_{W})<0
for every component
D
of
N_{W}
. The log smooth sub-lc boundary
\Theta:=B_{W}-N_{W}
is unchanged and has negative coefficients along
{\operatorname{Supp}}N_{W}
. Thus, for every prime divisor
D
over
W
with
\mu_{D}N_{W}>0
,

a(D,W,B_{W}+M_{W})+\mu_{D}N_{W}=a(D,W,\Theta)>1.

For the moreover part, put
L=L_{W}
,
P=P_{W}
and
N=N_{W}
. Nefness of
P
and

(1+\alpha)N=N_{\sigma}((1+\alpha)L)\leq N_{\sigma}(L+\alpha P)+\alpha N

give
N_{\sigma}(L+\alpha P)=N
. Put
a_{D}=a(D,W,B_{W}+M_{W})
. For this log smooth pair, the values
a_{D}<1
form a finite set. Since
N
is rational, choose
0<\tau\ll 1
such that

(1-\tau)(a_{D}+\mu_{D}N)>1\qquad\text{whenever }\mu_{D}N>0.

Set
H_{\epsilon}=L+\alpha P+\epsilon A
. By coefficientwise continuity, for all sufficiently small
0<\epsilon<\tau
,

(1-\tau)N\leq N_{\sigma}(W,H_{\epsilon})\leq N.

For every prime divisor
D
over
W
, we have

\mu_{D}N_{\sigma}(W,H_{\epsilon})\leq\sigma_{D}(H_{\epsilon})\leq\mu_{D}N,

where the upper bound follows from
H_{\epsilon}-N=(1+\alpha)P+\epsilon A
being ample. Since
(W,B_{W}+A+M_{W})
is lc,
a(D,W,B_{W}+\epsilon A+M_{W})\geq(1-\epsilon)a_{D}
. Thus, whenever
\sigma_{D}(H_{\epsilon})>0
,

a(D,W,B_{W}+\epsilon A+M_{W})+\sigma_{D}(H_{\epsilon})\geq(1-\tau)(a_{D}+\mu_{D}N)>1.

∎

Lemma 4.35

(
cf.
[
HH20
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a dlt g-pair and
S
be an lc centre.
Let
\pi\colon W\to X
be a log resolution of
(X,B+M)
which is an isomorphism over the generic points of lc centres
and write
K_{W}+B_{W}+M_{W}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M)+E
, where
(W,B_{W})
is log smooth sub-lc and
E\geq 0
is exceptional
/X
. If
E_{i}
is a component of
E
such that
\mu_{E_{i}}B_{W}-\mu_{E_{i}}E<0
, then
E_{i}|_{S_{W}}
is exceptional over
S
, where
S_{W}
is the birational transform of
S
.

Proof.

Put
\Delta_{W}=B_{W}-E
and define
\Delta_{S_{W}}
by crepant adjunction:

K_{S_{W}}+\Delta_{S_{W}}+M_{S_{W}}=\pi|_{S_{W}}^{*}(K_{S}+B_{S}+M_{S}).

Since
(W,\Delta_{W})
is log smooth,
\mu_{E_{i}}\Delta_{W}<0
implies
\mu_{V}\Delta_{S_{W}}<0
for every component
V
of
E_{i}|_{S_{W}}
. Thus
V
is exceptional
/S
.
∎

Theorem 4.36
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a projective lc g-pair with data
M^{\prime}
. Assume
K_{X}+B+M
birationally has a Nakayama–Zariski decomposition with nef positive part. Suppose either
M^{\prime}
or the positive part is log effective. Then
(X,B+M)
has a weak minimal model.

Proof.

Step 1.

By Proposition
3.17
, replace
(X,B+M)
by the log smooth model of Lemma
4.34
. Write
L=K_{X}+B+M=P+N
and run a
P
-trivial MMP with scaling of an ample divisor
A\geq 0
such that
(X,B+A+M)
is dlt. Let
X^{i}
be the models obtained after the successive MMPs in Definition
4.7
, with
0<\lambda_{i}<1
tending to zero. If the MMP terminates, repeat its final model. Put
L_{i}=K_{X^{i}}+B^{i}+M^{i}
; the pairs
(X^{i},B^{i}+\lambda_{i}A^{i}+M^{i})
are dlt.

Step 2.

We prove termination near lc strata by induction on their dimension. Fix an lc stratum
S^{i}
. By induction and Theorem
4.18
, after reindexing the induced maps lift to a
P^{i}|_{\widetilde{S}^{i}}
-trivial MMP on small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorialisations
\widetilde{S}^{i}
, with scaling of
A^{i}|_{\widetilde{S}^{i}}
. The termination and nefness assumptions along the boundary in Theorem
4.21
(3) hold by induction, and its numerical hypothesis follows from log effectivity. Hence a fixed
\alpha>0
makes

(L_{i}+\lambda_{i}A^{i}+\alpha P^{i})|_{S^{i}}

nef for all
i\gg 0
.

Fix
i\gg 0
and put

H=L+\alpha P+\lambda_{i}A,\qquad D=L_{i}+\alpha P^{i}+\lambda_{i}A^{i}.

Lemma
4.34
and coefficientwise continuity give
{\operatorname{Supp}}N_{\sigma}(H)={\operatorname{Supp}}N
. An ample perturbation gives
H\sim_{\mathbb{R}}K_{X}+\Delta
with
\Delta
a big klt boundary, since
M,P
are nef and
A
is ample on
X
. As
X\dashrightarrow X^{i}
is
H
-non-positive,
[
BCHM10
]
gives a
D
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor, ending on a model
Y
where
D_{Y}
is nef. Since
\mathbf{B}_{+}(H)\subseteq{\operatorname{Supp}}N
, these maps preserve the generic points of lc centres.

Step 3.

Write

{}^{*}H={}^{*}D+\mathbf{G}={}^{*}D_{Y}+\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{G},

where
\mathbf{E},\mathbf{G}\geq 0
are exceptional over
Y,X^{i}
, respectively, and
\mu_{F}(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{G})=\sigma_{F}(H)
for every prime divisor
F
over
X
. Take a log resolution
\pi:W\to X^{i}
,
\rho:W\to Y
on which these b-divisors descend, isomorphic over the generic points of lc centres on
Y
. Put
E=(\mathbf{E})_{W}
and let
S_{W}
be the transform of
S^{i}
. For the crepant boundary
\Delta_{W}^{i}
from
(X^{i},B^{i}+\lambda_{i}A^{i}+M^{i})
, the moreover assertion of Lemma
4.34
gives

\mu_{F}(\Delta_{W}^{i}-E)<0

for every component
F
of
E
. Lemma
4.35
, applied to
\rho
with nef data induced by
M+\alpha P
, shows that
E|_{S_{W}}
is exceptional
/S_{Y}
. Restricting gives

\pi|_{S_{W}}^{*}(D|_{S^{i}})=\rho|_{S_{W}}^{*}(D_{Y}|_{S_{Y}})+E|_{S_{W}}.

The left-hand side is nef, so the negativity lemma gives
E|_{S_{W}}=0
.

The support equality gives
N^{i}\leq c(\mathbf{E})_{X^{i}}
for some
c>0
. Since
\mathbf{E}
is anti-nef over
X^{i}
, the negativity lemma gives

{}^{*}N^{i}\leq c\mathbf{E}.

Thus
{\operatorname{Supp}}N^{i}
is disjoint from
S^{i}
, and
L_{i}|_{S^{i}}=P^{i}|_{S^{i}}
is nef. Every
(P^{i}+N^{i})
-negative curve lies in
{\operatorname{Supp}}N^{i}
, so all subsequent steps avoid
S^{i}
.

Step 4.

Induction gives termination near
\left\lfloor{B^{i}}\right\rfloor
and nefness of
L_{i}|_{\left\lfloor{B^{i}}\right\rfloor}
. Theorem
4.21
(3) and Lemma
4.28
finish the proof.
∎

Remark 4.37
.

With the notation of the theorem, if
\mathbf{B}_{-}(K_{X}+B+M)
is disjoint from the lc centres, the termination and nefness required in Step 4 are immediate, and
(X,B+M)
has a minimal model. Thus Theorem
4.36
generalises Corollary
3.21
.

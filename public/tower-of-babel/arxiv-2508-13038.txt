On Combination Theorems and Bowditch Boundaries of Relatively Hyperbolic TDLC Groups
Swarnali Datta, Arunava Mandal, and Ravi Tomar

Abstract
. Based on the work of Farb, Bowditch, and Groves-Manning on discrete relatively hyperbolic groups, we introduce an approach to relative hyperbolicity for totally disconnected locally compact (TDLC) groups. For compactly generated TDLC groups, we prove that this notion is equivalent to the one introduced by Arora-Pedroza. Let G = A ∗ C B G=A\ast_{C}B or G = A ∗ C G=A\ast_{C} where A A and B B are relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups and C C is compact. We prove that G G is a relatively hyperbolic TDLC group and give a construction of the Bowditch boundary of G G . As a consequence, we prove that if the rough ends of G G are infinite, then the topology of the Bowditch boundary of G G is uniquely determined by the topology of the Bowditch boundary of A A and B B . Further, we show that if a relatively hyperbolic TDLC group has one rough end, then its Bowditch boundary is connected. Finally, we show that if the Gromov boundary of a hyperbolic TDLC group G G is totally disconnected, then G G splits as a finite graph of compact groups.

In his seminal essay, Gromov

The article is a part of a program of generalizing geometric techniques in the study of discrete groups to a larger class of totally disconnected locally compact groups, which includes algebraic groups over non-Archimedean local fields, and automorphism groups of locally finite graphs. A locally compact group is said to be
totally disconnected
if the identity element is its own connected component. From now on, we abbreviate totally disconnected locally compact groups as TDLC groups. A TDLC group has been a topic of interest in the last three decades after the foundational work by G. Willis
[
Wil94
]
, pioneering insights into tidy subgroups and the scale function. In this article, we extend classical geometric techniques
—
originally developed for discrete groups
—
to the broader category of TDLC groups. Specifically, we prove combination theorems for relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups. Moreover, we introduce and adapt the two different notions of a relatively hyperbolic TDLC group, proving their equivalence with the existing notion defined by Arora-Pedroza
[
AMP23
]
. In turn, this enables us to define the notion of the boundary of a relatively hyperbolic TDLC group. Then, we further study the topology of the boundary of graphs of relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups. This article, in our view, builds a foundation for further study of relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups.

For infinite finitely generated groups, the Cayley graph is a fundamental instrument in studying their geometry. In the same spirit, to study large-scale properties of TDLC groups, Krön and Möller
[

2.1.
Cayley-Abels graphs

Given a finitely generated group, one can construct its Cayley graph and thus treat the group as a geometric object. For TDLC groups, an analog construction has first been taken into account by Abels
[
Abe74
]
. These are known as
Cayley-Abels graphs
named after him. A less technical approach to Cayley-Abels graphs is provided in
[
KM08
]
with the name
rough Cayley graph
.

Definition 2.1
.

A locally finite connected graph
X
is said to be a Cayley-Abels graph of a TDLC group
G
if
G
acts transitively on
V(X)
and stabilizers of vertices are compact open subgroups of
G
.

A topological group is said to be
compactly generated
if it is algebraically generated by a compact subset. For a TDLC group
G
, a Cayley-Abels graph exists if and only if
G
is compactly generated
[
KM08
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Existence of a Cayley-Abels graph:
Let
G
be a compactly generated TDLC group, and
U
be a compact open subgroup of
G
. If
K
is a compact generating set of
G
, there exists a finite symmetric set
S
containing the identity element of
G
such that
K\subset SU
. Define a graph
X(K,U,S)
whose vertex set is the set of left cosets of
U
in
G
, and the edge set is
\{\{gU,gsU\}:g\in G\text{ and }s\in S\}
. Then, it is easy to check that
X
is a Cayley-Abels graph for
G
(see
[
KM08
]
). We write
X
in place of
X(K,U,S)
when
K,U,S
are clear from the context.

Now, we note the following lemma, which is important to us for later purposes.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
G
is a TDLC group. Then the set of vertices of any Cayley-Abels graph of
G
is countable.

Proof.

Let
X
be a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
. Then
G
is compactly generated and thus
\sigma
-compact, i.e. it is a countable union of compact subsets. Consider any open subgroup
U
of
G
. The quotient map
\pi:G\to G/U
is open, making each coset in
G/U
open; hence,
G/U
is discrete. Since
\pi
is continuous, surjective and
G
is
\sigma
-compact,
G/U
is
\sigma
-compact. As
G/U
is both discrete and
\sigma
-compact, it is countable. This shows that, for any open subgroup
U
of
G
, the coset space
G/U
is countable.

For each
v\in V(X)
, the
G
-stabilizer
G_{v}
of
v
is a compact open subgroup of
G
. By the discussion in the previous paragraph, the coset space
G/G_{v}
is countable. Since the action of
G
on
V(X)
is transitive, for any
w\in V(X)
, there exists
g_{w}\in G
such that
g_{w}\cdot v=w
. Suppose there exists
g_{w}^{\prime}\in G
such that
g_{w}^{\prime}\cdot v=w
. Then
g_{w}^{-1}g_{w}^{\prime}\cdot v=v
, implying
g_{w}^{-1}g_{w}^{\prime}\in G_{v}
, or equivalently,
g_{w}G_{v}=g_{w}^{\prime}G_{v}
. Therefore, we define a map

\varphi:V(X)\to G/G_{v}\;\;{\text{b}y}\;\;\varphi(w)=g_{w}G_{v}.

This map is injective: if
\varphi(w_{1})=\varphi(w_{2})
, then
g_{w_{1}}G_{v}=g_{w_{2}}G_{v}
, i.e.

g_{w_{2}}^{-1}g_{w_{1}}\cdot v=v
, which implies
w_{1}=w_{2}
. Moreover,
\varphi
is surjective: for
g_{w}G_{v}\in G/G_{v}
, take
w:=g_{w}\cdot v
, so
\varphi(w)=g_{w}G_{v}
. Thus,
\varphi
is bijective. Since
G/G_{v}
is countable, it follows that
V(X)
is countable.
∎

Suppose
H<G
are compactly generated TDLC groups such that
H
is open. Let
K_{H}
and
K_{G}
be compact generating sets of
H
and
G
, respectively. Let
U
be a compact open subgroup of
G
. Then,
U_{H}=U\cap H
is compact and open in
H
. Let
S_{G}
and
S_{H}
be finite symmetric subsets of
H
and
G
respectively such that
K_{H}\subset S_{H}U_{H}
and
K_{G}\subset S_{G}U.
Let
X_{H}=X_{H}(K_{H},U_{H},S_{H})
and
X_{G}=X_{G}(K_{G},U,S_{G})
be Cayley-Abels graphs of
H
and
G
, respectively. We denote by
d_{X_{H}}
and
d_{X_{G}}
the metric on
X_{H}
and
X_{G}
, respectively. We now assume that
S_{H}\subset S_{G}
. Note that distinct cosets of
U_{H}
in
H
give distinct cosets of
U
in
H
. Thus, we have an embedding
i:X_{H}\to X_{G}
that sends the vertex
hU_{H}
to the vertex
hU
. Thus, we can treat
X_{H}
as a subgraph of
X_{G}
. We are now ready to prove the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

The map
i:X_{H}\to X_{G}
is a proper embedding, i.e. there exists a function
\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\eta(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
such that for all
h_{1}U_{H},h_{2}U_{H}\in V(X_{H})
, if
d_{X_{G}}(h_{1}U,h_{2}U)\leq n
then
d_{X_{H}}(h_{1}U_{H},h_{2}U_{H})\leq\eta(n)
.

Proof.

Define

\eta(n):=\max\{d_{X_{H}}(U_{H},hU_{H}):h\in H,\,d_{X_{G}}(U,hU)\leq n\}.

It is sufficient to prove that if
d_{X_{G}}(U,hU)\leq n
then
d_{X_{H}}(U_{H},hU_{H})\leq\eta(n)
which follows from the definition of
\eta.

It remains to see that
\eta(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. For some
M>0
, let if possible
\eta(n)\leq M
for all
n
. Then, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
h_{N}\in H
with
d_{X_{H}}(U_{H},h_{N}U_{H})\leq M
but
d_{X_{G}}(U,h_{N}U)\leq N
. This yields infinitely many points in the ball of radius
M
around
U_{H}
in
X_{H}
. This gives a contradiction, as
X_{H}
is locally finite, balls of finite radius contain finitely many vertices.
∎

Now, we make a record of the following basic lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Open subgroups of a non-discrete locally compact group are infinite. In particular, the intersection of any two open subgroups of a locally compact group is infinite.

Proof.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
U
be an open subgroup of
G
. Then, with the subspace topology,
U
is also locally compact. Since singletons are closed, if
U
is finite, then the topology on
U
is discrete. This, in turn, implies that
G
is discrete, and we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

For discrete groups, the theory of ends is well-known. In
[
KM08
]
, the authors defined the space of ends for TDLC groups. We end this subsection with the following definition that turns out to be useful for us later.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated TDLC group. The space of rough ends of
G
is defined as the end space of a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
.

Since Cayley-Abels graphs are unique up to quasiisometry and the locally finite quasiisometric graphs have homeomorphic end spaces, the above definition is well-defined.

2.2.
Hyperbolic TDLC groups and their boundaries

In
[
Gro85
]
, Gromov introduced the notion of hyperbolic geodesic metric space and proved that hyperbolicity is an invariant of quasiisometry, see also
[
BH99
]
. It is well-known that any two Cayley-Abels graphs of a compactly generated TDLC group are quasiisometric
[
KM08
]
. This motivates the following well-defined notion of a hyperbolic TDLC group.

Definition 2.6
.

A compactly generated TDLC group
G
is said to be hyperbolic if some (hence any) Cayley-Abels graph of
G
is a Gromov hyperbolic space.

In
[
Gro85
]
, Gromov introduced the notion of a boundary of a hyperbolic geodesic metric space, which is now known as the Gromov boundary named after him. In the same essay, he proved that quasiisometric proper hyperbolic spaces have homeomorphic Gromov boundaries (see also
[
BH99
, III.H]
). This motivates the notion of the Gromov boundary of a hyperbolic TDLC group. Let
G
be a hyperbolic TDLC group. One then defines the
Gromov boundary

\partial G
to be the Gromov boundary of some (hence any) Cayley-Abels graph
X
of
G
: the set of equivalence classes of geodesic rays in
X
starting from a base point
x_{0}
, i.e.

\partial G:=\partial X=\{\,[\gamma]\mid\gamma\text{ is a geodesic ray in }X\text{ such that }\gamma(0)=x_{0}\},

where two rays are equivalent if their Hausdorff distance is finite. Define
\overline{X}:=X\cup\partial X.
Then, with a natural topology,
\overline{X}
is a compact metrizable space, and
X
is an open dense subset of
\overline{X}.
For details, one is referred to
[
BH99
, III.H]
.

2.3.
Relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups

The notion of relatively hyperbolic groups was introduced by Gromov for discrete groups
[
Gro85
]
. Since then, it has been elaborated by several authors, for example, see
[
Far98
,
Bow12
,
DS05
,
Osi06
,
GM08
]
. To define relative hyperbolicity for TDLC groups, Arora-Pedroza
[
AMP23
]
took an approach due to Bowditch
[
Bow12
]
and Osin
[
Osi06
]
.
We first recall their definition of relative hyperbolic TDLC groups, some useful facts, which will be required later in the paper.

Let
G
be a TDLC group, and
\mathcal{H}
be a finite collection of open subgroups in
G
. A pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
is called a
proper pair
if no pair of distinct non-compact subgroups in
\mathcal{H}
are conjugate in
G
(page-832,
[
AMP23
]
).

Definition 2.7

(Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}

[
AMP23
]
)
.

Suppose
(G,\mathcal{H})
is a proper pair and
G
is acting cocompactly, discretely (pointwise stabilizers of cells are open subgroups) on a connected simplicial graph
X
. Then,
X
is said to be Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
if

(1)

the edge
G
-stabilizers are compact,

(2)

the vertex
G
-stabilizers are either compact or conjugates of subgroups in
\mathcal{H}
,

(3)

every
H\in\mathcal{H}
is the
G
-stabilizer of a vertex of
X
,

(4)

any pair of vertices of
X
with the same
G
-stabilizer
H\in\mathcal{H}
are in the same
G
-orbit if
H
is non-compact.

The group
G
is said to be
compactly generated relative to
\mathcal{H}
if there exists a compact set
K\subset G
such that
K\cup(\bigcup\mathcal{H})
algebraically generates
G
.

Existence of Cayley-Abels graph relative to
\mathcal{H}
:
Let
K
be a compact subset of
G
such that
G=\langle K\cup(\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}H)\rangle
. Since
K
is compact there is a finite subset
S\subset G
containing the identity of
G
such that
K\subset SU.

Define a graph
X
with
V(X):=G/U\cup(\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}G/H)
and
E(X):=\{\{gU,gsU\}|g\in G,s\in S\}\cup\{\{gU,gH\}|g\in G,H\in\mathcal{H}\}
. Note that
G
acts discretely, there are only finitely many orbits of vertices and edges on
X
, edge stabilizers are compact open, and vertex stabilizers are conjugates of
U
or
H\in\mathcal{H}
. Other properties for being a Cayley-Abels graph also follow, see
[
AMP23
, Proposition 4.6]
.

We collect a few properties of a Cayley-Abels graph of the proper pair
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H})
in the form of the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a proper pair. Then, we have the following:

(1)

[
AMP23
, Theorem F]

G
is compactly generated relative to
\mathcal{H}
if and only if there exists a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
relative to
\mathcal{H}
.

(2)

[
AMP23
, Theorem H]
Suppose
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are two Cayley-Abels graphs of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
. Then,
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are quasiisometric, and
X_{1}
is fine if and only if
X_{2}
is fine.

The definition of a fine graph is also important to us.

Definition 2.9
.

A simplicial graph
X
is said to be fine if for any pair of vertices
u,v
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there are finitely many embedded paths of length
n
from
u
to
v
.

Now, we are ready to define the notion of relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups.

Definition 2.10

(TDRH-I)
.

For a proper pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, the topological group
G
is said to be hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{H}
if there exists a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
that is fine and hyperbolic.

From Proposition
2.8
, the previous definition does not depend on the choice of the Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
.

2.4.
Graphs of topological groups

In geometric group theory, the notion of graphs of groups is classical. One is referred to Serre’s book on trees
[
Ser03
]
for a detailed account of definitions and results.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a graph. A graph of topological groups
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
over
\mathcal{Z}
consists of the following data:

(1)

For each
v\in V(\mathcal{Z})
, there is a topological group
G_{v}
called the vertex group.

(2)

For each
e\in E(\mathcal{Z})
, there is a topological group
G_{e}
called the edge group.

(3)

For each edge
e\in E(\mathcal{Z})
with vertices
v
and
w
, there are open topological embeddings
G_{e}\to G_{v}
and
G_{e}\to G_{w}.

The fundamental group of the graph of group
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
is denoted by
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
.
The next proposition shows that there is canonical topology on
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z}).

Proposition 2.12
.

[
CdlH16
, Proposition 8.B.9 and Proposition 8.B.10]

There is a unique topology on
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
such that the inclusion map
G_{v}\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
is an open topological embedding. Moreover, we have the following:

(1)

If the edge groups of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
are locally compact, then
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
is locally compact.

(2)

If the vertex groups of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
are compactly generated then
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
is compactly generated.

In the forthcoming section, we prove combination theorems for topological groups with some extra properties. To prove such a theorem, it is sufficient to prove the theorem for an amalgamated free product and an HNN extension of topological groups. These are special cases of graphs of groups. In particular, if
\mathcal{Z}
is an interval,
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
is the amalgamated free product. If
\mathcal{Z}
is a simple loop, then
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
is an HNN extension. We now explain in detail these constructions.

Suppose
A,B
, and
C
are topological groups such that
i_{A}:C\to A
and
i_{B}:C\to B
are topological isomorphisms onto open subgroups of
A
and
B
, respectively. Then, we have an
amalgamated free product

A\ast_{C}B
given by the presentation
\langle S_{A},S_{B}|R_{A},R_{B},i_{A}(c)=i_{B}(c)\text{ for all }c\in C\rangle
if
\langle S_{A}|R_{A}\rangle
and
\langle S_{B}|R_{B}\rangle
are presentations of
A
and
B
, respectively.
Throughout the paper, we assume that the amalgamated free products are non-trivial, i.e. the edge group is not equal to both vertex groups
. If
C
is a subgroup of
A
and
\alpha
is a topological isomorphism from
C
to an open subgroup of
A
, then we have
HNN extension

A\ast_{C}
whose presentation is given by
\langle S_{A},t|R_{A},t^{-1}ct=\alpha(c)\text{ for all }c\in C\rangle
if
\langle S_{A}|R_{A}\rangle
is a presentation of
A
. Let
G
denote an amalgamated free product
A\ast_{C}B
or an HNN extension
A\ast_{C}
. Then, by Proposition
2.12
, there is a unique topology on
G
such that the inclusions
A\to G,B\to G
, and
C\to G
are topological isomorphisms onto open subgroups of
G
.
Throughout the paper, we use this topology on amalgamated free products and HNN extensions of topological groups.
We record the following lemma, which is relevant to us.

Lemma 2.13
.

Suppose
G=A\ast_{C}B
or
G=A\ast_{C}
. If
G
is a TDLC group, then
A,B
, and
C
are TDLC groups. Conversely, if
C
is TDLC, then
G
is TDLC.

Proof.

Observe that a subgroup of a totally disconnected topological group is totally disconnected. Thus, if
G
is TDLC, then
A,B
, and
C
are TDLC. Conversely, suppose
C
is TDLC. Then, by the definition of the topology on
G
,
C
is embedded in
G
as an open subgroup. By van Dantzig’s theorem, there is a neighborhood basis at the identity in
C
consisting of compact open subgroups. Since
C
is open in
G
, this neighborhood basis at the identity in
C
gives a neighborhood basis of compact open subgroups at the identity in
G
. Hence,
G
is a TDLC group.
∎

From Lemma
2.13
, it follows that if the edge groups of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
are TDLC then
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
is TDLC.

Bass-Serre trees of amalgamated free product and HNN extension:
Let
G=A\ast_{C}B
. The construction of the Bass–Serre tree of
G
is classical
[
Ser03
]
. For the sake of completeness, we explain the construction as follows.

Let
\tau
be a unit interval with vertices
v_{A}
and
v_{B}
. Define an equivalence relation
\sim
on
G\times\tau
induced by the equivalences.

(g_{1},v_{A})\sim(g_{2},v_{A})\text{ if }g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}\in A,

(g_{1},v_{B})\sim(g_{2},v_{B})\text{ if }g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}\in B,

(g_{1},t)\sim(g_{2},t)\text{ if }g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}\in C,t\in\tau.

Then
G\times\tau/_{\sim}
is called the
Bass-Serre tree

T
of
G
.

If
G=A\ast_{C}
is an HNN extension. Then, the Bass-Serre tree
T
of
G
is defined as follows:

The vertex set of
T
is the set of left cosets of
A
in
G
. The set of edges of
T
is the set of left cosets of
C
in
G
. For all
g\in G
, vertices
gA
and
gtA
are connected by the edge
gC
.

Notation:
Let
T
be the Bass-Serre tree of amalgamated free product or HNN extension. Let
\pi
denote the natural projection from
T
to
\tau
or a simple loop. For each vertex
v
of
T
, we denote by
G_{v}
the
G
-stabilizer of
v
.

3.
A combination theorem for relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. To prove that, it is sufficient to consider the amalgamated free product and HNN extension case. Throughout this section, all relatively hyperbolic groups are TDRH-I (Definition
2.10
).

3.1.
Amalgamated free product case.

Let
G
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Let
i_{A}:C\to A
and
i_{B}:C\to B
be topological isomorphisms onto open subgroups of
A
and
B
, respectively. As discussed in Subsection
2.3
, let
X_{A}
be the Cayley-Abels graph of
A
with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{A}
such that the set of cosets of
i_{A}(C)
in
A
is a subset of the vertices of
X_{A}
. Similarly, let
X_{B}
be the Cayley-Abels graph of
B
with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{B}
such that the set of cosets of
i_{B}(C)
in
B
is a subset of the vertices of
X_{B}
. Define a graph
Y
as the union of
X_{A}
,
X_{B}
, and
\tau
, where the coset
i_{A}(C)
is identified with
v_{A}
and the coset
i_{B}(C)
is identified with
v_{B}
. Then, we define the graph
X
as an equivalence relation on
G\times Y
induced by

(g_{1},y_{1})\sim(g_{2},y_{2})\text{ if }y_{1},y_{2}\in X_{A},\;g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}\in A\text{ and }g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}y_{1}=y_{2},

(g_{1},y_{1})\sim(g_{2},y_{2})\text{ if }y_{1},y_{2}\in X_{B},\;g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}\in B\;\text{and}\;g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}y_{1}=y_{2}.

We have a natural
1
-Lipschitz subjective map from
X
to
T
by sending

(g,X_{A})
to
(g,v_{A})
and
(g,X_{B})
to
(g,v_{B})
for
g\in G
. Also, there is a natural action of
G
by left on

Lemma 3.1
.

The graph
X
is a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{A}\cup\mathcal{H}_{B}
.

Proof.

From the construction of
X
, we see that
X
is a connected graph. Note that every vertex of
X
belongs to
(g,X_{A})
or
(g,X_{B})
for some
g\in G
. Since the action of
A
on
X_{A}
and
B
on
X_{B}
are cocompact, the action of
G
on
X
is cocompact. Clearly, the vertex stabilizers are either compact or conjugates of subgroups in
\mathcal{H}
. The edge stabilizers are also compact (note that the
G
-stabilizer of
\tau
is
C
). Every
H\in\mathcal{H}
is either the
G
-stabilizer of a vertex in
X_{A}
or a
G
-stabilizer of a vertex in
X_{B}
. The last property for being a Cayley-Abels graph with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{A}\cup\mathcal{H}_{B}
is also satisfied. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:
Let
A
and
B
be two hyperbolic groups relative to
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
, respectively. Then,
X_{A}
and
X_{B}
are hyperbolic and fine. Thus,
X
is a tree of hyperbolic spaces such that the edge spaces are the singletons. By
[
BF92
]
,
X
is a hyperbolic graph. Since each vertex space is a fine graph,
X
is also a fine graph. Hence,
G
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{H}_{A}\cup\mathcal{H}_{B}
.
∎

Note that, if
G
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{H}
, and
\mathcal{H}
is empty, then
G
is compactly generated and the Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
quasiisometric to a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
(
[
KM08
, Theorem 2.9]
), and hence,
G
becomes a hyperbolic TDLC group.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose
G=A\ast_{C}B
is an amalgamated free product such that
A,B
are compactly generated TDLC groups and
C
is compact. Then,
G
is hyperbolic if and only if
A
and
B
are hyperbolic.

Proof.

Suppose
A
and
B
are hyperbolic groups. Then,
A
and
B
are both hyperbolic relative to an empty collection of subgroups, respectively. By Theorem
1.1
,
G
is hyperbolic relative to an empty collection of subgroups. Thus,
G
is a hyperbolic group. Conversely, suppose
G
is hyperbolic. Let
X
be the graph as constructed above by taking
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\phi
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}=\phi.
Note that, by
[
KM08
, Theorem 2.9]
,
X
is quasiisometric to a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
. Since
G
is hyperbolic,
X
is a hyperbolic space. By construction of

, the inclusion maps
X_{A}\to X
and
X_{B}\to X
are isometric embeddings. Hence,

and

are hyperbolic.
∎

Remark 3.8

A subgroup of a TDLC hyperbolic group need not be hyperbolic. For example, the semidirect product

4.
Compactly generated relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups

Motivated by the work of Farb
[
Far98
]
, Bowditch
[
Bow12
]
, and
[
GM08
]
on discrete relatively hyperbolic groups, in this section, we introduce two notions of relative hyperbolicity for a compactly generated TDLC group. Then, we show that all three notions of relative hyperbolicity discussed in this paper are equivalent (Theorem
4.12

1.4
.

4.1.
Relative hyperbolicity in the sense of Bowditch and Groves-Manning.

We start here by recalling the definition of a combinatorial horoball.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph. The combinatorial horoball based at
\Gamma
, denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
, is a graph defined as follows:

(1)

\mathcal{B}^{(0)}=\Gamma^{(0)}\times(\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N})
.

(2)

\mathcal{B}^{(1)}
contains the following three types of edges:

(a)
If
v
and
w
are joined by an edge in
\Gamma
, then there is an edge connecting
(v,0)
and
(w,0)
.

(b)
For
k>0
and
v,w\in\Gamma^{(0)}
, if
0<d_{\Gamma}(v,w)\leq 2^{k}
then there is a single edge connecting
(v,k)
and
(w,k)
.

(c)
For
k\geq 0
and
v\in\Gamma^{(0)}
, there is an edge joining
(v,k)
and
(v,k+1)
.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

As the full subgraph of
\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
containing the vertices
\Gamma^{(0)}\times\{0\}
is isomorphic to
\Gamma
, we may think of
\Gamma
as a subset of
\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
.

(2)

If
\Gamma
is locally finite, then it follows that
\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
is also locally finite.

Let G be a compactly generated TDLC group, and
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
be a finite collection of compactly generated open subgroups of
G
. Let
U
be a compact open subgroup of
G
. Let
K
be a compact generating set of
G
, and let
K_{i}
be a compact generating set of
H_{i}
for all
i
. Note that
U\cap H_{i}
is an infinite compact open subgroup of
H_{i}
for each
i
by Lemma
2.4
. Then, there exists a finite symmetric set
S\subset G
containing the identity element such that
K\subset SU
. Also, for each
i
, there is a finite symmetric set
S_{i}\subset H_{i}
containing identity such that
K_{i}\subset S_{i}(U\cap H_{i})
. We assume that
S_{i}\subset S
for all
i
. Thus, the Cayley-Abels graph
Y_{i}
of
H_{i}
is embedded in the Cayley-Abels graph
X
of
G
. Moreover, this is a proper embedding by Lemma
2.3
. Therefore, we can treat
Y_{i}
as a subgraph of
X
.

Definition 4.3
.

For each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, let
T_{i}
be a left transversal for
H_{i}
in
G
. For each
i
and each
t\in T_{i}
, let
Y_{i,t}
be the full subgraph of the Cayley-Abels graph
X
containing the vertices
tY_{i}
. Each
Y_{i,t}
is isomorphic to the Cayley-Abels graph of
H_{i}
. Then the augmented Cayley-Abels graph of
G
is the graph

X^{h}(K,S,U)=X\cup(\{\mathcal{B}(Y_{i,t}):t\in T_{i},1\leq i\leq n\})

where the graphs
Y_{i,t}\subset X
and
Y_{i,t}\subset\mathcal{B}(Y_{i,t})
are identified as suggested in Remark
4.2
.

Whenever the choices of
K,S
, and
U
are clear from the context, we will write
X^{h}
instead of
X^{h}(K,S,U)
. Note that the graph
X^{h}
is locally finite. The following proposition shows, up to quasiisometry, that Definition
4.1
does not depend on the choice of compact generating sets and compact open subgroups.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
K
and
K^{\prime}
are compact generating sets of
G
, and
K_{i},K_{i}^{\prime}
are compact generating sets of
H_{i}
for each
i
. Let
U
and
U^{\prime}
be compact open subgroups of
G
, and
S
and
S^{\prime}
be finite symmetric generating sets containing the identity such that
K\subset SU
and
K^{\prime}\subset S^{\prime}U^{\prime}
. Suppose
S
and
S^{\prime}
contain finite symmetric sets
S_{i}
and
S_{i}^{\prime}
for
K_{i}
and
K_{i}^{\prime}
, respectively. Then, the augmented Cayley-Abels graphs
X^{h}(K,S,U)
and
X^{\prime h}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
are quasiisometric.

Proof.

Let
X(K,S,U)
and
X^{\prime}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
be the Cayley-Abels graphs of
G
. Then, by
[
KM08
, Theorem 2.7
+
]
, there is a quasiisometry
\psi:X(K,S,U)\to X^{\prime}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
. Let
Y_{i,t}
and
Y_{i,t}^{\prime}
denote the various copies of the Cayley-Abels graph of
H_{i}
in
X(K,S,U
and in
X(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
, respectively. From the definition of
\psi
, it follows that
\psi(Y_{i,t})\subset N_{D}(Y_{i,t}^{\prime})
and
\psi^{-1}(Y_{i,t}^{\prime})\subset N_{D}(Y_{i,t})
for a uniform constant
D\geq 0.
By
[
HH21
, Theorem 1.2]
, it follows that
\psi
induces a quasiisometry from

to
X^{\prime h}
(see also
[

, Theorem 2.8]
). This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Definition 4.5

(TDRH-II)
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a proper pair. The group
G
is said to be hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{H}
if the augmented Cayley-Abels graph
X^{h}
of
G
is a Gromov hyperbolic space.
In this situation, we also say that the proper pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
is a relatively hyperbolic group.

Given a proper hyperbolic geodesic metric space, one can define the Gromov boundary associated to it
[
Gro85
,
BH99
]
. Bowditch
[
Bow12
]
generalizes this notion in the context of discrete relatively hyperbolic groups. In a similar manner, we define the Bowditch boundary of a relatively hyperbolic TDLC group.

Definition 4.6
.

Suppose
G
is a compactly generated TDLC group and
G
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{H}
. The Bowditch boundary of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
is defined to be the Gromov boundary of the augmented Cayley-Abels graph
X^{h}
of
G
. It is denoted by
\partial_{rel}(G)
.

Since
X^{h}
is locally finite,
\overline{X^{h}}:=X^{h}\cup\partial_{rel}(G)
is a compact metrizable space (see
[
BH99
, III.H, Exercise 3.18(4)]
,
[
GdlH90
, Chapter 7]
).

4.2.
Relative hyperbolicity in the sense of Farb

In this subsection, we adapt the definition of relative hyperbolicity due to Farb
[
Far98
]
in our setup. We retain the notation from Subsection
4.1
.

Definition 4.7

(Coned-off Cayley-Abels graph)
.

Let
X
be the Cayley-Abels graph of
G
, and
Y_{i}
be the Cayley-Abels graph of
H_{i}
. For each
i
and each
t\in T_{i}
, let
Y_{i,t}
be the full subgraph of the Cayley-Abels graph
X
containing the vertices
tY_{i}
. Form a new graph
\hat{X}(K,S,U)
, called the coned-off Cayley-Abels graph with respect to
\mathcal{H}
, as follows. For each
Y_{i,t}
, add a new vertex, called the cone point,
v(Y_{i,t})
to
X
and add an edge of length
1
from this new vertex to each element of
Y_{i,t}
.

When it is clear from the context, we shall write
\hat{X}
in place of
\hat{X}(K,S,U).
In the following proposition, we show that the coned-off Cayley-Abels graphs for
G
are unique up to quasiisometry.

Proposition 4.8
.

Suppose
K
and
K^{\prime}
are compact generating sets of
G
, and
K_{i},K_{i}^{\prime}
are compact generating sets of
H_{i}
for each
i
. Let
U
and
U^{\prime}
be compact open subgroups of
G
, and
S
and
S^{\prime}
be two finite symmetric generating sets containing the identity such that
K\subset SU
and
K^{\prime}\subset S^{\prime}U^{\prime}
. Suppose
S
and
S^{\prime}
contain finite symmetric sets
S_{i}
and
S_{i}^{\prime}
for
K_{i}
and
K_{i}^{\prime}
, respectively. Then, the coned-off Cayley-Abels graphs
\hat{X}(K,S,U)
and
\hat{X^{\prime}}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
are quasiisometric.

Proof.

By
[
KM08
, Theorem 2.7
+
]
, there exists a quasiisometry
\psi:X(K,S,U)\to X^{\prime}(K,S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
such that
\psi(Y_{i,t})\subset N_{D}(Y_{i,t}^{\prime})
and
\psi^{-1}(Y_{i,t}^{\prime})\subset N_{D}(Y_{i,t})
for a uniform constant
D\geq 0.
Also, note that the maps
Y_{i,t}\to X(K,S,U)
and
Y^{\prime}_{i,t}\to X^{\prime}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
are proper embeddings by Lemma
2.3
. Let
\psi^{h}:X^{h}(K,S,U)\to X^{\prime h}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
be the induced quasiisometry from Proposition

. Hence, by
[
KS24
, Lemma 9.34(3)]
(see also
[

, Lemma 1.2.31]
),

induces a quasiisometry from
\hat{X}(K,S,U)
to
\hat{X^{\prime}}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime}).
This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

By Lemma
4.11
, we see that
\hat{X}(K,S,U)
and
\hat{X^{\prime}}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime})
are Cayley-Abels graphs with respect to
\mathcal{H}
. Hence, by
[
AMP23
, Theorem H]
,
\hat{X}(K,S,U)
is quasiisometric to
\hat{X^{\prime}}(K^{\prime},S^{\prime},U^{\prime}).

We are ready to define the following.

Definition 4.10

(TDRH-III)
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a proper pair. The group
G
is said to be hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{H}
if
\hat{X}
is a hyperbolic graph and it satisfies the bounded penetration property (BPP).

For the definition of BPP, one is referred to
[
Far98
]
.

4.3.
Equivalence of the definitions

We start here by noting the following:

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a proper pair as in Subsection
4.1
. Then, the coned-off Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
is a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be the Cayley-Abels graph of
G
. The natural action of
G
on
X
induces a natural action of
G
on
\hat{X}
. Since the action of
G
on
X
is cocompact and the set of cone points has finitely many orbits, the action of
G
on
\hat{X}
is also cocompact. Clearly, the edge
G
-stabilizers are compact. Note that the
G
-stabilizers of the cone points are conjugate to elements in
\mathcal{H}
. Thus, the conditions (2) and (3) of Definition
2.7
hold. Since there are no two vertices of
\hat{X}
with the same
G
-stabilizer, the last condition also holds. Hence, we are done.
∎

We now show the equivalence of all three definitions of relative hyperbolicity for compactly generated groups.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let G be a compactly generated TDLC group, and
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
be a finite collection of compactly generated open subgroups of
G
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

TDRH-I.

(2)

TDRH-II.

(3)

TDRH-III.

Proof.

[TDRH-II
\iff
TDRH-III] If the pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
is TDRH-II relatively hyperbolic, then
X
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{Y}=\{Y_{i,t}:t\in T_{i}\text{ for all i }\}
in the sense of Definition 3.4 of
[
Sis12
]
. Then, by
[
Sis12
, Theorem 1.1]
,
X
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{Y}=\{Y_{i,t}:t\in T_{i}\text{ for all $i$ and $1\leq i\leq n$}\}
in the sense of Definition 3.8 of
[
Sis12
]
. Since these two definitions are equivalent, we are done.

[TDRH-III
\implies
TDRH-I] Suppose
(G,\mathcal{H})
is TDRH-III relatively hyperbolic. Then,
\hat{X}
is hyperbolic and satisfies the BPP property. Since by Lemma
4.11
,
\hat{X}
is a Cayley-Abels graph with respect to
\mathcal{H}
. Thus, to check TDRH-III, it remains to check that
\hat{X}
is a fine graph. Since
X
is locally finite and
\hat{X}
satisfies the BPP condition, it follows from the proof of
[
Dah03a
, Proposition 1, p.81]
that
\hat{X}
is a fine graph.

[TDRH-I
\implies
TDRH-III] Suppose
(G,\mathcal{H})
is TDRH-I relatively hyperbolic. Then, there exists a Cayley-Abels graph with respect to
\mathcal{H}
such that it is hyperbolic and fine. Since
\hat{X}
is a Cayley-Abels graph with respect to
\mathcal{H}
, by Proposition
2.8
,
\hat{X}
is hyperbolic and fine. To prove TDRH-III, it remains to check that
\hat{X}
satisfies the BPP property. This is the content of
[
Dah03a
, Lemma 5, p.82]
. Hence, we are done.
∎

We now provide a proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:
Since
G
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to
\mathcal{H}
, by TDRH-I, there exists a Cayley-Abels graph of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
which is hyperbolic and fine. Since the coned-off Cayley-Abels graph
\hat{X}
of

is quasiisometric to the Cayley-Abels graph of

with respect to

,
\hat{X}
is a fine graph Proposition
2.8
. Without loss of generality, we can choose

a compact open subgroup such that

.

For

, we show that the index of

in
H_{i}\cap H_{j}
is finite. Suppose not. Then, the Cayley-Abels graphs

and
Y_{j}
have infinitely many common vertices. We enumerate these vertices as

. Let

and

denote the cone points corresponding to

and

in
\hat{X}
. By definition of

, each

is joined to

and

by an edge of length

. Now, it is clear that there are infinitely many simple loops of length

in

containing the edge from

to

. This gives a contradiction to the fineness of

. Hence

Since

is compact,

is compact.

For
(2)
we show that
gUg^{-1}\cap U
has finite index in

. Suppose not. Since

has finite index in

, so

has infinitely many distinct cosets representatives in

have infinitely many common vertices. By the same logic as in (1), we get a contradiction. This proves

.

Let

be a Cayley-Abels graph of

be a Cayley-Abels graph of

such that

is embedded in

. Since

. As each

is a hyperbolic graph. Thus, by

∎

5.
Bowditch boundaries of relatively hyperbolic amalgamated free products and HNN extensions of TDLC groups

Throughout this section, for relatively hyperbolic groups, we use Definition
4.5
. The main goal of this section is to prove that the topology of the Bowditch boundary of a relatively hyperbolic amalgamated free product or HNN extension is uniquely determined by the topology of the Bowditch boundary of the vertex groups. For that, first of all, we give a construction of the Bowditch boundary for these groups, and then, we use the dense amalgam technique from
[
Ś16

to conclude the result.

Theorem 5.2
.

[
Ś16

For

(1)

(2)

is null,i.e. for any metric on

(3)

Each

Then,

and
D_{2}
are non-empty, disjoint, and satisfy cl
(D_{i})=D_{i}\cup U_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

By Lemma
6.1
, it is clear that cl
(D_{i})\subseteq D_{i}\cup U_{i}
. Conversely, let
\xi\in U_{1}
. Since
V(X)
is dense in
\overline{G}
, let
\{v_{n}\}
be a sequence in
V(X)
such that
v_{n}\to\xi
(in the original topology of
\overline{G}
). As the topology induced by
d_{\Delta}
on
\partial_{rel}G
is same as the original topology on
\partial_{rel}G
,
\{v_{n}\}\to\xi
with respect to the metric
d_{\Delta}
. Suppose there exists a subsequence of
\{v_{n}\}
contained in
D_{2}
. Then,
\xi\in
cl
(D_{2})
, which in turn implies that
\xi\in U_{2}
. This gives a contradiction as
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are disjoint. Hence,
\{v_{n}\}
is eventually contained in
D_{1}
and therefore,
\xi\in
cl
(D_{1})
. Similarly, one can show that
U_{2}\subseteq
cl
(D_{2})
. This also shows that
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are non-empty, disjoint subsets of
V(X)
. Hence, the claim.

Since
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are non-empty, let
\xi_{1}\in U_{1}
and
\xi_{2}\in U_{2}
. As in the proof of the converse of the claim, there exist sequences
\{v_{n}\}
and
\{w_{n}\}
in
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, respectively, such that
v_{n}\to\xi_{1}
and
w_{n}\to\xi_{2}
. Let
d
denote the Cayley-Abels graph metric on
X
. Since
d
-balls in
X
have only finitely many vertices of
V(X)
, up to passing to subsequences, we can assume that
\{v_{n}\}
and
\{w_{n}\}
are unbounded in
X
. For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, denote
K_{m}
the closed
d
-metric ball of radius
m
about the identity coset (coset of compact open subgroup whose representative is identity) which is an element of
V(X)
. Since
G
has one rough end, there exist subsequences
\{v_{n_{m}}\}
and
\{w_{n_{m}}\}
and a sequence of paths
\{\gamma_{m}\}
joining
v_{n_{m}}
and
w_{n_{m}}
such that
\gamma_{m}
is contained in
X\setminus K_{m}
. Let
e_{m}=[a_{m},b_{m}]
be an edge in
\gamma_{m}
such that
a_{m}\in D_{1}
and
b_{m}\in D_{2}
(such an edge always exists). Note that the sequence
\{a_{m}\}
is unbounded in
X
. Since
\overline{G}
is compact, there exists a subsequence
\{a_{m_{k}}\}
that converges to
\eta\in\partial_{rel}G
. Since
d(a_{m_{k}},b_{m_{k}})=1
, it follows
b_{m_{k}}\to\eta
in
\overline{G}
. Note that
\{a_{m_{k}}\}\to\eta
too in the topology induced by the metric
d_{\Delta}
(
d_{\Delta}
induces the original topology on
\partial_{rel}G
. Thus,
\eta\in U_{1}
. By the same logic,
\eta\in U_{2}
. This gives us a contradiction as
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are disjoint. Hence, we have the desired result.
∎

Applications

This section is devoted to proving partial converses of Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.7
. For that, first of all, we analyze the connected component of the Bowditch boundary of a relatively hyperbolic amalgamated free product or HNN extension of relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
G
be as in Theorem
1.5
or Theorem
1.7
with Bass-Serre tree
T
. Additionally, assume that
A
and
B
have one rough end. Then, we have the following:

(1)

Each
\eta\in\partial T
gives a connected component of
\partial G
.

(2)

For each
v\in V(T)
, the Bowditch boundary
\partial G_{v}
is a connected component of
\partial G
.

By Theorem
1.8
,
\partial_{rel}G
is a connected topological space. Thus, the proof of this proposition follows from the proof of
[
MS15
, Propositions 6.3 and 6.4]
. Hence, we skip its proof. For an amalgamated free product
A\ast_{C}B
or an HNN extension
A\ast_{C}
of relatively hyperbolic TDLC groups,
h(A\ast_{C}B)
and
h(A\ast_{C})
denote the set of homeomorphism types of the non-empty Bowditch boundary of the vertex groups. We are now ready to prove the following.

Theorem 7.2
.

Suppose
G=A\ast_{C}B
and
G^{\prime}=A^{\prime}\ast_{C^{\prime}}B^{\prime}
are as in Theorem
1.5
. Additionally, assume that
A,B,A^{\prime}
, and
B^{\prime}
have one rough end. If
\partial_{rel}G
is homeomorphic to
\partial_{rel}G^{\prime}
then
h(A\ast_{C}B)=h(A^{\prime}\ast_{C^{\prime}}B^{\prime}).

Proof.

Suppose
\partial_{rel}G
is homeomorphic to
\partial_{rel}G^{\prime}
. Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be the Bass-Serre trees of
G
and
G^{\prime}
. By Proposition
7.1
, for each
v\in V(T)
and
v^{\prime}\in V(T^{\prime})
,
\partial G_{v}
and
\partial G_{v^{\prime}}
are connected components in
\partial G
and
\partial G^{\prime}
, respectively. Since a homeomorphism preserves connected components, the theorem follows.
∎

Using Stallings’ theorem on ends of discrete groups and accessibility of finitely presented groups, it is well known that if the Gromov boundary of a hyperbolic group
G
is totally disconnected, then
G
is virtually free, i.e.
G
admits a finite graph of groups decomposition such that vertex groups are finite. We now aim to prove an analog of this for hyperbolic TDLC groups. For that, we recall the definition of accessibility for compactly generated TDLC groups.

Definition 7.5
.

A compactly generated TDLC group
G
is said to be accessible if it has an action on a tree
T
such that:

(1)

The number of orbits of
G
on the edge of
T
is finite.

(2)

The stabilizers of edges in
T
are compact open subgroups of
G
.

(3)

Every stabilizer of a vertex in
T
is a compactly generated open subgroup of
G
and has at most one rough end.

In
[
Cor
, Corollary 19.46]
, the author proved that hyperbolic TDLC groups are accessible.

Proof of Proposition
1.9
:
Since
G
is a hyperbolic TDLC group, it is accessible. Thus, by Definition
7.5
,
G
has a finite graph of groups
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
decomposition such that the edge groups are compact open in
G
, and the vertex groups are compactly generated TDLC. Note that, by Corollaries
3.2
and
3.6
, we see that the vertex groups of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Z})
are hyperbolic. Since
\partial G
is totally disconnected, and the Gromov boundaries of vertex groups having one rough end are connected components in
\partial G
, each vertex group is compact. Hence, we are done.
∎

We end the paper with an example that demonstrates the situation in Proposition
1.9
.

Example 7.6
.

For
d\geq 3
, let
T_{d}
be a regular tree. Then, it is known that the automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(T_{d})
with compact open topology is a compactly generated TDLC group. It turns out that
T_{d}
itself is a Cayley-Abels graph for
{\rm Aut}(T_{d})
. Therefore, it is a hyperbolic TDLC group and the Gromov boundary of
{\rm Aut}(T_{d})
is homeomorphic to the Cantor set. Thus, by Proposition
1.9
,
{\rm Aut}(T_{d})
admits a finite graph of groups decomposition such that the vertex groups are compact. However, this can also be seen by using Bass-Serre theory. Since
T_{d}
is a Cayley-Abels graph of
{\rm Aut}(T_{d})
, the vertex stabilizers are compact open and the action is transitive. So
{\rm Aut}(T_{d})
is a multiple HNN extension of the stabilizer of
T_{d}
. Also, by Theorem
3.2
,
G={\rm Aut}(T_{d})\ast_{C}{\rm Aut}(T_{d})
, where
C=Stab_{Aut(T_{d})}(v)
for some
v\in V(T_{d})
, is a hyperbolic TDLC group. By our construction of the Gromov boundary of
G
, we see that
\partial G
is homeomorphic to the Cantor set.

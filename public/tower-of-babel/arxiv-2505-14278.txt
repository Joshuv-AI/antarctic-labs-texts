Abstract
. We study the Neumann initial-boundary value problem for the parabolic-elliptic chemotaxis system, proposed by Jäger and Luckhaus (1992). We confirm that their comparison methods can be simplified and refined, applicable to seek the critical mass 8 ​ π 8\pi concerning finite-time blowup in the unit disk. As an application, we deal with a parabolic-elliptic-parabolic chemotaxis model involving indirect signal production in the unit ball of ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} , proposed by Tao and Winkler (2025). Within the framework of radially symmetric solutions, we prove that • if initial mass is less than 64 ​ π 2 64\pi^{2} , then solution is globally bounded; • for any m m exceeding 64 ​ π 2 64\pi^{2} , there exist nonnegative initial data with prescribed mass m m such that the corresponding classical solutions exhibit a formation of Dirac-delta type singularity in finite time, termed a chemotactic collapse.

This section is devoted to introduction of our ideas to detect finite-time chemotactic collapse in the critical dimension via comparison arguments,

In the pioneering work
[
15
]
, Jäger and Luckhaus proposed and studied the Neumann initial-boundary value problem (
1.4
),
with

\int_{\Omega}v(\cdot,t)\;\mathrm{d}x=0\quad\text{for all }t>0\,.

They introduced the mass distribution function

(2.1)

M(\rho,t):=\int_{0}^{\rho^{1/2}}ru(r,t)\;\mathrm{d}r,\quad(\rho,t)\in(0,1)\times(0,T_{\max})

acting on the radially symmetric classical solution
(u,v)
with the maximal existence time
T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]
of (
1.4
).
Then
M
solves the scalar parabolic problem

(2.2a)

\Theta M=0\quad\text{for all }(\rho,t)\in(0,1)\times(0,T_{\max}),

with Dirichlet boundary conditions,

(2.2b)

M(0,t)=0\quad\text{and}\quad M(1,t)=\frac{m}{2\pi}\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max})

and obvious initial data

(2.2c)

M(\rho,0):=\int_{0}^{\rho^{1/2}}ru_{0}(r)\;\mathrm{d}r,\quad\rho\in(0,1)\,.

Here and below, the one-point degenerate parabolic operator
\Theta
with action on
f\in C^{2,1}((0,1)\times(0,T))
for some
T>0
reads

\Theta f:=f_{t}-4\rho f_{\rho\rho}-2f_{\rho}\biggl(f-\frac{m\rho}{2\pi}\biggr).

In view of
[
45
, Theorem 4.1]
, the Dirichlet initial-boundary value problem (
2.2
) admits comparison arguments.
To detect finite-time blowup,
they constructed a gradient explosion auxiliary function
g
satisfying

\Theta g\leq 0

in the sense of distribution,
where
g
near the left lateral boundary
\{0\}\times\{t>0\}
is particularly defined by

(2.3)

g:=\frac{a\rho}{\rho+\tau^{3}},\quad\tau=\varepsilon-\ell t,\quad\text{for }\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})\text{ and }t\in(0,\varepsilon/\ell),

with certain positive constants
a>0
,
\varepsilon>0
,
\ell>0
and
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
.
Note that the “stationary” problem of (

)

\begin{cases}4\rho F_{\rho\rho}+2FF_{\rho}=0,&\rho>0,\\
F(0)=0,\end{cases}

has a family of solutions

F_{\lambda}(\rho):=F_{0}(\rho/\lambda^{2})=\frac{4\rho}{\rho+\lambda^{2}},\quad\text{with }F_{0}(\rho):=\frac{4\rho}{\rho+1}\quad\text{for }\lambda,\rho>0,

which are mass distribution functions of solutions

2F_{\lambda}^{\prime}\Bigm|_{\rho=|x|^{2}}=\frac{8\lambda^{2}}{(|x|^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}V_{0}\bigg(\frac{x}{\lambda}\bigg),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

to the stationary problem (

).
Therefore,

With the construction (

) at hands,

\Theta g\leq g_{t}+\frac{m}{\pi}\cdot\rho g_{\rho}\leq g_{t}+\frac{m}{\pi}\cdot g\quad\text{for }(\rho,t)\in(0,1)\times(0,\varepsilon/\ell),

which encourages us to modify

for

Indeed, we may perturb (

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle=\underline{U}_{t}-16s^{3/2}\underline{U}_{ss}-4\underline{U}_{s}\bigg(\underline{W}-\frac{\mu^{\star}s}{4}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{\prime}(s^{3/2}+3\tau^{3}s)}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{3}}+\frac{18a\ell\tau^{5}s}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2^{5}\cdot 3a\tau^{6}s}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{5}}-\frac{12ab\tau^{6}s}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{5}}+\frac{3a\mu^{\star}\tau^{6}s}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3a\tau^{3}s}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{3}}\cdot I,

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\frac{(2^{5}-4b)\tau^{3}}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{2}}+\frac{\mu^{\star}\tau^{3}+6\ell\tau^{2}}{s^{1/2}+\tau^{3}}+\frac{a^{\prime}s^{1/2}}{3a\tau^{3}}+\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}

\displaystyle\>=\frac{2^{5}(1-\mathrm{e}^{\tau})\tau^{3}}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{2}}+\frac{\mu^{\star}\tau^{3}+6\ell\tau^{2}}{s^{1/2}+\tau^{3}}-\frac{s^{1/2}}{3\tau^{3}}\cdot(\gamma+1)\ell-(\gamma+1)\ell

for all
(s,t)\in(0,1)\times(0,\varepsilon/\ell)
.
The identity (
2.13

I\leq-2^{5}\cdot\frac{\tau^{4}}{(s^{1/2}+\tau^{3})^{2}}+(\mu^{\star}\varepsilon+6\ell)\cdot\frac{\tau^{2}}{s^{1/2}+\tau^{3}}-(\gamma+1)\ell

It follows from (

for all

∎

4.1.
Lyapunov functional and Adams inequality

The Lyapunov functional of the system (
1.3
) was constructed in
[
10
, Proposition 6.1]
.
A similar energy dissipation structure exists in (
1.1
).
In the sequel, we may abuse the notation
\mathcal{F}(t)=\mathcal{F}(u,v)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(u,v,w)
be a classical solution given in Proposition
3.1
.
Then the following identity holds:

(4.1)

\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{F}(u(t),v(t))+\mathcal{D}(u(t),v(t))=0\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}),

where

(4.2)

\mathcal{F}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}u\ln u-\int_{\Omega}uv+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}

and

(4.3)

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{t}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla(\ln u-v)|^{2}.\end{split}

In particular,

(4.4)

\mathcal{F}(0):=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\ln u_{0}-\int_{\Omega}u_{0}v_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v_{0}|^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{0}|^{2},

where

v_{0}\in\bigg\{\phi\in W^{2,2}(\Omega):\partial_{\nu}\phi\equiv 0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=0\bigg\}

is the unique solution to the Neumann problem

\begin{cases}0=\Delta v_{0}-\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\int_{\Omega}w_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x+w_{0},&x\in\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}v_{0}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The following sharp Adams inequality was established in
[
38
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a bounded domain. Then there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\sup_{u\in H^{2}(D)\cap H_{0}^{1}(D),\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq 1}\int_{D}e^{32\pi^{2}u^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq K|D|,

and this inequality is sharp, i.e., if
32\pi^{2}
is replaced by a larger number, then the supremum is infinite.

Inspired by
[
27
]
, we modify the Adams inequality above.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a bounded domain. Then

\int_{D}\phi\psi\leq\int_{D}\phi\ln\phi+\bigg(\int_{D}\phi\bigg)\ln(K|D|)+\frac{1}{128\pi^{2}}\bigg(\int_{D}\phi\bigg)\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\int_{D}\phi\ln\int_{D}\phi,

for all nonnegative
\phi\in C^{0}(\overline{D})
, and
\psi\in H^{2}(D)\cap H^{1}_{0}(D)
.
Here,
K>0
is given as in Lemma
4.2
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
K>1
.
If
\phi\equiv 0
, there is nothing to prove, and therefore, we may assume
\phi\not\equiv 0
.
Using the concavity of the mapping
(0,\infty)\ni\xi\mapsto\ln x\in\mathbb{R}
, we estimate by Jensen inequality,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{D}\phi\psi-\int_{D}\phi\ln\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\phi\ln\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\psi}}{\phi}=\int_{D}\phi\cdot\int_{D}\frac{\phi}{\int_{D}\phi}\ln\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\psi}}{\phi}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{D}\phi\ln\int_{D}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\psi}}{\int_{D}\phi}=\int_{D}\phi\ln\int_{D}\mathrm{e}^{\psi}-\int_{D}\phi\ln\int_{D}\phi.

If
\psi\equiv 0
, we already finish the proof.
So we consider only the case
\psi\not\equiv 0
.
Let
\varphi=\psi/\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(D)}
.
Using Young inequality, we see

\psi=\varphi\cdot\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq 32\pi^{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{1}{128\pi^{2}}\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}.

Then Adams inequality entails

(4.6)

\int_{D}\mathrm{e}^{\psi}\leq\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{128\pi^{2}}\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\bigg)\cdot\int_{D}\mathrm{e}^{32\pi^{2}\varphi^{2}}\leq K|D|\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{128\pi^{2}}\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\bigg).

The desired estimate follows from inserting (
4.6
) into (
4.5
).
∎

then

(4.21)

\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\int_{\Omega}w^{2p}\;\mathrm{d}x<\infty\quad\text{for all }p>1.

Proof.

Since

\xi\ln(1+\xi^{q})\leq\begin{cases}\ln 2,&\xi\in(0,1),\\
\xi\ln 2+q\xi\ln\xi,&\xi\in[1,\infty),\end{cases}

for all
q>0
,
(
4.14
) implies that

\Upsilon=\Upsilon(p):=\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\int_{\Omega}u\ln(u^{p/2}+1)\;\mathrm{d}x+\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty

holds for all
p>1
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
p>2
.
Integrating by parts, we compute

(4.22)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}&=\frac{p}{p-1}\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}\nabla\cdot(\nabla u-u\nabla v)\\
&=-p\int_{\Omega}u^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+p\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\\
&=-\frac{4}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{p/2}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u^{p}\cdot\nabla v=-\frac{4}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{p/2}|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\Delta v\\
&\leq-\frac{4}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{p/2}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}u^{p}w\\
&\leq-\frac{4}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{p/2}|^{2}+\eta^{-2p}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1/2}+\eta\int_{\Omega}w^{2p+1}\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
and
\eta\in(0,1)
,
where we have used the second equation in (
1.1
).
Integrating by parts, we estimate

(4.23)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2p-1}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}w^{2p}&=\frac{2p}{2p-1}\int_{\Omega}w^{2p-1}(\Delta w-\delta w+u)\\
&=-2p\int_{\Omega}w^{2p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}-\frac{2p\delta}{2p-1}\int_{\Omega}w^{2p}+\frac{2p}{2p-1}\int_{\Omega}w^{2p-1}u\\
&\leq-\frac{2}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{p}|^{2}-\delta\int_{\Omega}w^{2p}+\eta\int_{\Omega}w^{2p+1}+2^{p}\eta^{1/2-p}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1/2}\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
and
\eta\in(0,1)
.
Write

y_{p}(t):=\frac{1}{p-1}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}+\frac{1}{2p-1}\int_{\Omega}w^{2p}\quad\text{for }t\in(0,T_{\max}).

Then collecting (
4.22
) and (
4.23
) yields

(4.24)

\begin{split}y_{p}^{\prime}+\delta y_{p}&\leq-\frac{4}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{p/2}|^{2}+2\eta^{-2p}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1/2}+\delta\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\\
&\quad-\frac{2}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{p}|^{2}+2\eta\int_{\Omega}w^{2p+1}\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
and
\eta\in(0,1/2)
.
Applying (
4.17
) with
\varphi=w^{p}
, we have

(4.25)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}w^{2p+1}&\leq C_{\mathrm{gn}}\|\nabla w^{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{\mathrm{gn}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p+1/2}\\
&\leq C_{\mathrm{gn}}\Upsilon\|\nabla w^{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{\mathrm{gn}}\Upsilon^{p+1/2}\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Using (
4.18
) with
\varphi=u^{p/2}
and
q=p/2
, we get

(4.26)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1/2}&\leq C_{\mathrm{gn}}\ln^{-1/2}(1+k)\|\nabla u^{p/2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|u\ln(u^{p/2}+1)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\\
&\quad+C_{\mathrm{gn}}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{p+1/2}+C_{\mathrm{gn}}\pi^{2}k^{2+1/p}\\
&\leq C_{\mathrm{gn}}\Upsilon^{1/2}\ln^{-1/2}(1+k)\|\nabla u^{p/2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{\mathrm{gn}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{p+1/2}+C_{\mathrm{gn}}\pi^{2}k^{2+1/p}\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
and
k>0
.
Fix

\eta:=\frac{1/p}{C_{\mathrm{gn}}\Upsilon+2}

and

k:=\exp\bigg(\frac{p^{2}C_{\mathrm{gn}}^{2}\Upsilon}{\eta^{4p}}\bigg).

Collecting (
4.24
)–(
4.26
), we have

(4.27)

\begin{split}y_{p}^{\prime}+\delta y_{p}&\leq-2\eta^{-2p}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1/2}+\delta\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\\
&\quad+2\eta C_{\mathrm{gn}}\Upsilon^{p+1/2}+2\eta^{-2p}C_{\mathrm{gn}}m^{p+1/2}+2\eta^{-2p}C_{\mathrm{gn}}\pi^{2}k^{2+1/p}\\
&\leq\pi\delta^{2p+1}+2\eta C_{\mathrm{gn}}\Upsilon^{p+1/2}+2\eta^{-2p}C_{\mathrm{gn}}m^{p+1/2}+2\eta^{-2p}C_{\mathrm{gn}}\pi^{2}k^{2+1/p}\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Hence, (
4.21
) follows from Gronwall inequality applied to (
4.27
).
∎

Now we are in a position to show Proposition
1.5
.

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

In view of Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.7
,
we have

\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\int_{\Omega}w^{4}\;\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

which implies

\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{5}\;\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

by standard elliptic regularity theories.
A direct application of well-established Neumann heat semigroup estimates
[
40
, Lemma 1.3]
shows (
1.21
).
∎

5.
Chemotactic collapse

Now we are in a position to show Proposition
1.6
.

Proof of Proposition
1.6
.

As

\begin{split}\bigg|\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\int_{B_{r}}u(\cdot,t)\;\mathrm{d}x\bigg|&=2\pi^{2}\Big|\int_{0}^{r}(u_{r}\rho^{3})_{r}-(uv_{r}\rho^{3})_{r}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\Big|\\
&\leq 2\pi^{2}|u_{r}(r,t)|r^{3}+|u(r,t)v_{r}(r,t)|r^{3}\end{split}

for all
(r,t)\in(0,1)\times(T_{\max}/2,T_{\max})
,
we have

\sup_{t\in(T_{\max}/2,T_{\max})}\bigg|\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\int_{B_{r}}u(\cdot,t)\;\mathrm{d}x\bigg|<\infty\quad\text{for all }r\in(0,1),

according to Lemma
4.4
.
Then the function

\Xi(r):=\lim_{t\uparrow T_{\max}}\int_{B_{r}}u(\cdot,t)\;\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for }r\in(0,1)

is well-defined.
As
\Xi(r)
is non-decreasing, we put

(5.1)

m_{\star}:=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\Xi(r).

We claim that
m_{\star}\geq 64\pi^{2}
.
Indeed, if
m_{\star}<64\pi^{2}
, then there exists
\varrho\in(0,1)
such that

\Xi(\varrho)<64\pi^{2}.

Moreover, there exists
T\in(0,T_{\max})
such that

\sup_{t\in(T,T_{\max})}\int_{B_{\varrho}}u(\cdot,t)\;\mathrm{d}x<64\pi^{2}.

Recalling Proposition
1.5
,
this warrants

\sup_{t\in(T,T_{\max})}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty,

which is incompatible with (
3.2
), as
T_{\max}<\infty
.

As it follows from Lemma
4.4
that

\|u\|_{C^{1}(\Omega\setminus B_{\rho})\times(T_{\max}/2,T_{\max})}<\infty\quad\text{for all }\rho\in(0,1),

we may define the nonnegative function

f(x):=\lim_{t\uparrow T_{\max}}u(x,t)\quad\text{for all }x\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\}.

Then
u(\cdot,t)\to f(\cdot)
in
C^{0}_{\loc}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})
as
t\uparrow T_{\max}
.
Fatou lemma and mass conservation formula (
3.3
) entail that
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

For
\phi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, we calculate

\begin{split}&\quad\Big|\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\phi(x)\;\mathrm{d}x-m_{\star}\phi(0)-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\phi(x)\;\mathrm{d}x\Big|\\
&=\Big|\int_{B_{r}}u(x,t)(\phi(x)-\phi(0))\;\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\int_{B_{r}}u(x,t)\;\mathrm{d}x-m_{\star}\Big)\phi(0)\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}(u(x,t)-f(x))\phi(x)\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{B_{r}}f(x)\phi(x)\;\mathrm{d}x\Big|\\
&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|\phi(x)-\phi(0)\|_{C^{0}(B_{r})}+\Big|\int_{B_{r}}u(x,t)\;\mathrm{d}x-m_{\star}\Big|\cdot\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad+\|u(x,t)-f(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus B_{r})}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{r})}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\end{split}

for all
r\in(0,1)
.
Then we have

\begin{split}&\quad\limsup_{t\uparrow T_{\max}}\Big|\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\phi(x)\;\mathrm{d}x-m_{\star}\phi(0)-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\phi(x)\;\mathrm{d}x\Big|\\
&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|\phi(x)-\phi(0)\|_{C^{0}(B_{r})}+(\Xi(r)-m_{\star})\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{r})}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\end{split}

for all
r\in(0,1)
.
Taking the limit
r\downarrow 0
, we get

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\uparrow T_{\max}}\Big|\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\phi(x)\;\mathrm{d}x-m_{\star}\phi(0)-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\phi(x)\;\mathrm{d}x\Big|\leq 0,\end{split}

thanks to
\phi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
,
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and the definition (
5.1
) of
m_{\star}
.
This is exactly (
1.22
).
∎

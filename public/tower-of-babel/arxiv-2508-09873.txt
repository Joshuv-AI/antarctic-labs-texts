The zero blocking numbers of grid graphs Journal: Discrete Applied Mathematics
Hau-Yi Lin, Wu-Hsiung Lin, Gerard Jennhwa Chang

Abstract
In a zero forcing process, vertices of a graph are colored black and white initially, and if there exists a black vertex adjacent to exactly one white vertex, then the white vertex is forced to be black. A zero blocking set is an initial set of white vertices in a zero forcing process such that ultimately there exists a white vertex. The zero blocking number is the minimum size of a zero blocking set. This paper gives the exact value of the zero blocking number of grid graphs.

Introduction

In a
zero forcing process
on a graph
G

the vertices of
G
are initially partitioned into two subsets
{\cal B}
and

and

If there exists a black vertex adjacent to exactly one white vertex,
then the white vertex is

If

{\cal B}
a

;
otherwise we call
{\cal B}
a

and
{\cal W}

.
The
zero forcing number

of

is the minimum size of a zero forcing set.
The
failed zero forcing number

of

is the maximum size of a failed zero forcing set.
The
zero blocking number

of

is the minimum size of a zero blocking set.
As

for every graph

, studying
F(G)
and

are equivalent.
Notice that if a zero blocking set of size
B(G)
,
then there exists no black vertex adjacent to exactly one white vertex.

The

Remark that in the books/papers of the graph theory, the vertices of

Beaudouin-Lafon et al.
[

Theorem 1

(
[
7
]
)
.

For the grid graph

4
Exact value of
B(G_{m,n})

Finally, we shall prove that the upper bound by Beaudouin-Lafon et al.
[
7
]

is in fact the exact value of
B(G_{m,n})
with
n\geq m\geq 2
.
First, a useful lemma.

Lemma 8
.

For
n\geq m\geq 2
, suppose
n-m=q(m+1)-r
,
where
q
and
r
are integers with
0\leq r\leq m
.
If there exist integers
a,b,c\geq 0
such that
n-m=a(m-1)+bm+c(m+1)
,
then
c\leq q-\lceil r/2\rceil
and so
r\leq 2q
.

Proof.

Let
s=a+b+c
.
Since
a+b\geq\frac{2a+b}{2}=\frac{s(m+1)-(n-m)}{2}
,
we have
c\leq s-\frac{s(m+1)-(n-m)}{2}
,
which is decreasing with respect to
s
.
Since
s(m+1)-(n-m)=2a+b\geq 0
,
we have
s\geq\lceil\frac{n-m}{m+1}\rceil=q

and so
c\leq q-\frac{q(m+1)-(n-m)}{2}=q-{r}/{2}
.
Since
c
and
q
are integers,
we have
c\leq q-\lceil r/2\rceil
.
∎

Theorem 9
.

For the grid graph
G_{m,n}
with
n\geq m\geq 2
,
if

r=q(m+1)-(n-m)\leq 2q

Also
n-q+\lceil r/2\rceil=m+qm-r+\lceil r/2\rceil=m(q+1)-\lfloor r/2\rfloor

On the other hand, the condition

\lceil\frac{n-m}{m+1}\rceil>\lfloor\frac{n-m}{m-1}\rfloor

r>2q

In this case,
r>0
and so
q=\lceil\frac{n-m}{m+1}\rceil=\lfloor\frac{n-m}{m+1}\rfloor+1

Also

Therefore, it is sufficient to prove the lower bound.

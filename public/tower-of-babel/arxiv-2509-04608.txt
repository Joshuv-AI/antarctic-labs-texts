Skorokhod Transition in the Conic Market Model
Artur Sidorenko

Abstract
This paper examines the applicability of the Skorokhod representation theorem in filtrated probability spaces for the utility maximization problem in the Kabanov conic model of multi-asset markets with proportional transaction costs. A key challenge is that the theorem does not necessarily preserve adaptedness, meaning that solutions obtained on an auxiliary probability space may not correspond to those on the original one. We establish that, under mild conditions, the Bellman functionals remain consistent across different probability spaces.

1
Introduction

This paper explores a delicate issue concerning the applicability of the Skorokhod representation theorem in filtrated probability spaces, particularly in the context of mathematical finance. A well-known challenge is that the Skorokhod representation does not necessarily preserve the adaptedness of stochastic processes. Consequently, the solution to a problem obtained on a new probability space may bear little resemblance to the original problem on the initial probability space.

We investigate the applicability of Skorokhod transfer in the context of multi-asset utility maximization under proportional transaction costs. To establish the validity of Skorokhod’s theorem in filtrated settings, two primary approaches have been proposed in the literature. One method requires that the underlying filtration is generated by a process with independent increments
[
4
,
5
]
. The second approach relies on the prediction process (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
9
]
) and the concept of extended weak convergence
[
2
]
for the price process. Additionally, we note the existence of a version of the Skorokhod representation theorem for filtrated probability spaces
[
6
]
, which is based on the notion of convergence in adapted distribution
[
7
]
, a stronger form of extended weak convergence.

While the paper
[
2
]
employs the Skorokhod transfer in filtrated spaces, its treatment remains implicit and lacks explicit formalization. This gap highlights the need for a more rigorous examination, which we address in this study.

We examine both of the aforementioned approaches by considering two models of financial markets, where the stochastic bases and price processes define the respective filtrated probability spaces. We establish that, under mild conditions, the portfolio optimization problems formulated on different probability spaces lead to identical Bellman functionals.

To streamline the theoretical investigation, we employ the geometric framework of Y. Kabanov
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
, which provides an efficient approach to modeling multi-asset financial markets with transaction costs.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the setting of the utility maximization problem. Section
3
presents the main results. Auxiliary statements are relegated to the Appendix.

2
Setting

We use the notation
{\mathbb{R}}_{++}:=(0,\infty)
. Let
p,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
T\in{\mathbb{R}}_{++}
. We denote
{\cal D}^{d}_{T}
the space of all RCLL (right-continuous with left limits) functions
f:[0,T]\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We rely on the notation of Section 3.6 of
[
12
]
.

We consider a model
{\bf M}:=(\Omega,{\cal F},{\mathbb{F}},P,Y,S)
of a financial market, where the quadruple
(\Omega,{\cal F},{\mathbb{F}},P)
is a stochastic basis, filtration
{\mathbb{F}}:=({\cal F}_{t})_{t=0}^{T}
is generated by the null sets of
{\cal F}
and an
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
-valued process
Y
with RCLL paths,
S
is an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{++}
-valued adapted process with continuous paths,
S_{0}:={\bf 1}
and
S^{1}=1
. The latter process is interpreted as prices of
d
tradable assets, and the first asset is called a numeraire. We fix a closed proper convex
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{++}\subset K
. Recall that a closed convex cone
K
is proper if
K\cap(-K)=\{0\}
. The dual cone
K^{*}=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\colon xy\geq 0\,\forall x\in K\}
. It is easily seen that
K^{*}\setminus\{0\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{++}
. Thanks to the bipolar theorem, the second dual
K^{**}=K
.

A function
f:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
K
-increasing if
f(t)-f(s)\in K
. A function
f:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
K
-decreasing if
f
is
-K
-increasing. We put
{\cal H}_{K}
the set of all
K
-decreasing paths from
{\cal D}^{d}_{T}
. Any adapted
B=(B_{t})_{t=0}^{T}
process with paths from
{\cal H}_{K}
is called a strategy. Since
K
is proper, the paths of
B
are of finite variation, see Lemma
4.1
in Appendix. In this setting, the
j
-th entry of the difference
B_{t}-B_{s}
is the amount of asset
j
bought/sold in the units of the numeraire throughout the interval
(s,t]
, and
B_{0}
is the initial trade at time
0
.

Now we formulate the portfolio optimization problem. Let
x\in K
be the initial value. We see the portfolio wealth as a vecrtor-valued process, where each entry is corresponded to the amount of asset
i
. This amount can be defined in both physical units and units of the numeraire. The portfolio wealth in physical units is defined as

\widehat{V}^{j}_{t}:=x^{j}+\int_{[0,t]}\frac{1}{S^{j}_{s}}dB^{j}_{s},

where the integration is understood in the Riemann–Stieltjes sense. By convention,
B_{0-}=0
and
B_{0}
is the Dirac measure at the initial point 0. In a more concise notation, the latter equality can be written as

\widehat{V}:=x+\frac{1}{S}\cdot B,

where
\cdot
is the component-wise Riemann-Stieltjes integration. The portfolio wealth in the numeraire units is

V^{j}=S^{j}\widehat{V}^{j}.

or

V=S\odot\widehat{V},

where
\odot
is the Hadamard multiplication. If
S
is a semi-martingale, then

V=x+V\cdot Z+B,

where
Z^{j}=1+1/S^{j}\cdot S^{j}
is the stochastic logarithm of
S^{j}
and the stochastic integration of vector-valued processes is understood in the component-wise sense.

To undeline the dependence of
V
on
x
and
B
, we use the notation
V^{x,B}
.

A strategy
B
is admissible if
V^{x,B}_{t}\in K
a.s. for every
t\in[0,T]
. Since the paths of
V
are RCLL, this is equivalent to
V^{x,B}\in K
up to evanescence. We denote
{\cal A}(x)
the class of all admissible strategies.

Let
U:K\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
be a mapping interpreted as the utility function. We consider the following portfolio optimization problem

u(x):=\sup_{B\in{\cal A}(x)}{\bf E}\,U(V^{x,B}_{T}).

We call
u(x)
the Bellman function. To underline the model
{\bf M}
, we use the notation
u(x,{\bf M})
.

Main result

The main problem of this paper is as follows. Let
{\bf M}

The process

.
Note that if
{\bf M}

.

We denote

Y^{t}:=Y{\bf 1}_{\llbracket 0,t\llbracket}+Y_{t}{\bf 1}_{\llbracket t,\infty\llbracket}

(3.1)

{}_{t}\!Y:=(Y-Y_{t}){\bf 1}_{\llbracket t,\infty\llbracket}.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1

Let model
{\bf M}
satisfy
H
. Then for every
x\in{\rm int}\,K

u(x,{\bf M})=u(x,{\bf N}).

Proof.

Let
B\in{\cal A}(x,{\bf N})
. Thanks to the measurability, for some Borel function
f:{\cal D}^{m}_{T}\times[0,1]\mapsto{\cal D}^{d}_{T}
, the process
B=f(Y,\xi)
. Our goal is to verify that for a.a.
a\in[0,1]
, the process
\bar{B}(a):=f(Y,a)\in{\cal A}(x,{\bf M})
.

Fix
t\in[0,T]
. It is easily seen that
B_{t}=\pi_{t}(f(Y,\xi))
where
\pi_{t}(x)=x_{t}
. On the other hand,
B_{t}
is
\tilde{\cal F}_{t}
-measurable implying
\pi_{t}(f(Y,\xi))=f_{t}(Y^{t},\xi)
for some Borel function
f_{t}
. By independence of
Y
and
\xi
and the Fubini theorem,
f_{t}(Y^{t},a)=\pi_{t}(f(Y,a))
for a.a.
a\in[0,1]
. It follows that for a dense countable set
I\subset[0,T]
with
\{0,T\}\in I
, r.v.
\bar{B}_{t}(a)
are
{\cal F}_{t}
-measurable for a.a.
a\in[0,1]
. Since
B_{t}=\lim_{I\ni s\to t+}B_{s}
for
t\neq T
and
{\bf F}
is right-continuous,
\bar{B}(a)
is adapted for a.a.
a\in[0,1]
. On the other hand,

{\bf 1}_{x+1/S\cdot B\in\widehat{K}_{t},\,\forall t\in[0,T]}=g(Y,\xi)

for some Borel function
g
. Immediately,
g(Y,\xi)=1
a.s. Again, due to the Fubini theorem, there exists a set
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
of probability one such that
g(Y(\omega),a)=1
for a.a.
a\in[0,1]
for all
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
. By the same technique, we verify that
\bar{B}(a)
is
K
-decreasing for a.a.
a\in[0,1]
for all
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime\prime}
from a set
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
of probability one. Thus, we have established that
\bar{B}(a)\in{\cal A}(x,{\bf M})
for a.a.
a\in[0,1]
.

Finally, fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then, for some strategy
B\in{\cal A}(x,{\bf N})
,

u(x,{\bf N})-\varepsilon\leq{\bf E}U(V^{x,B}_{T},S)={\bf E}\left[{\bf E}\left[U(V^{x,B}_{T},S)\,|\,\xi\right]\right]=\\
\int_{[0,1]}{\bf E}U(V^{x,\bar{B}(a)}_{T},S)\,da\leq\int_{[0,1]}u(x,{\bf M})da=u(x,{\bf M}).

As

\square

Theorem 3.2

Let
{\bf M}
and
{\bf\tilde{M}}
be two different models that satisfy
H
and the laws
{\cal L}_{P}(Y,S)={\cal L}_{\tilde{P}}(\tilde{Y},\tilde{S})
. Then
u(x,{\bf M})=u(x,{\bf\tilde{M}})
.

Proof.

Let
B\in{\cal A}(x,{\bf M})
. Due to Lemma 31 of
[
4
]
, the strategy can be represented as a Borel function
f:{\cal D}^{k}_{T}\times[0,1]\to{\cal D}^{d}_{T}
of
Y
and the r.v.
\xi
as follows:
B=f(Y,\xi)
. Put
\tilde{B}:=f(\tilde{Y},\tilde{\xi})
. Our aim is to verify that
\tilde{B}\in{\cal A}(x,{\bf\tilde{N}})
, where

is the model with additional randomization.

is

charges probability one to

-measurable. Note that

{\cal L}(B^{\prime}_{t},Y^{\prime}_{u}-Y^{\prime}_{t})={\cal L}(B^{\prime}_{t})\otimes{\cal L}(Y^{\prime}_{u}-Y^{\prime}_{t})

\tilde{Y}^{t}:=\tilde{Y}{\bf 1}_{\llbracket 0,t\llbracket}+\tilde{Y}_{t}{\bf 1}_{\llbracket t,\infty\llbracket}

Then

\,{}_{t}\!\tilde{Y}

is

finite stopping times

.

Theorem 3.3

Let
{\bf M}
and
{\bf\tilde{M}}
be two different models. Assume that the distributions
{\cal L}_{P}(S,Y)={\cal L}_{\tilde{P}}(\tilde{S},\tilde{Y})
. Then
u(x,{\bf M})=u(x,{\bf\tilde{M}})
.

Proof.

Let
B\in{\cal A}(x,{\bf M})
. We construct its prediction process
Z^{B}(t,\omega)
. It is easily seen that
(B,Z^{B})=(f(Y),g(Y))
a.s. for some Borel functions
f:{\cal D}^{k}_{T}\to{\cal D}^{d}_{T}
and
g:{\cal D}^{k}_{T}\to{\cal D}({\cal P}({\cal D}^{d}_{T}))
. We define
(\tilde{B},\tilde{Z}^{B}):=(f(\tilde{Y}),g(\tilde{Y}))
.

Denote
\pi_{u}:{\cal D}^{d}_{T}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the following projection mapping:
\pi_{u}(x):=x(u)
and the mapping
\tilde{\pi}_{u}:{\cal P}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\mapsto{\cal P}({\cal D}^{d}_{T})
as
\pi_{u}(\mu):=\mu\circ\pi_{u}^{-1}
.
The prediction process has the following property(see
[
1
,
9
]
): there exists a set
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
of probability one such that for all
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
0\leq u\leq t\leq T

\tilde{\pi}_{u}(Z^{B}(t,\omega))=\delta_{B_{u}(\omega)}.

As such,

\tilde{\pi}_{u}(g(y)(t))=\delta_{f(y)(u)},\quad{\cal L}_{P}(Y)-{\rm a.e.}.

Since any probability measure on a Polish space can be distinguished by a countable set of bounded continuous functions, we have the existence of a set
\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{\Omega}
such that

\tilde{\pi}_{u}(\tilde{Z}^{B}(t,\omega))=\delta_{f(\tilde{Y}(\omega))(u)}.

We aim to prove that
\tilde{Z}^{B}=Z^{\tilde{B}}
a.s., i.e. that
\tilde{Z}^{B}
is the prediction process of
(\tilde{B},\tilde{\mathbb{F}})
. To that end, we choose
t\in[0,T]
, a set
A\in{\cal B}({\cal D}^{d}_{T})
, a set
C\in{\cal B}({\cal D}^{k}_{T})
and we verify that

\tilde{\bf E}{\bf 1}_{\tilde{B}\in A}{\bf 1}_{\tilde{Y}^{t}\in C}=\tilde{\bf E}\tilde{Z}^{B}(t)(A){\bf 1}_{Y^{t}\in C}.

(3.3)

Immediately,

\tilde{\bf E}{\bf 1}_{B\in A}{\bf 1}_{\tilde{Y}^{t}\in C}={\bf E}{\bf 1}_{B\in A}{\bf 1}_{Y^{t}\in C}.

On the other hand,

\tilde{\bf E}\tilde{Z}^{B}(t)(A){\bf 1}_{\tilde{Y}^{t}\in C}=\tilde{\bf E}\tilde{g}(\tilde{Y})(t)(A){\bf 1}_{\tilde{Y}^{t}\in C}={\bf E}g(Y)(t)(A){\bf 1}_{Y^{t}\in C}={\bf E}Z^{B}(t)(A){\bf 1}_{Y^{t}\in C}.

By definition of the prediction process
Z^{B}
,

{\bf E}{\bf 1}_{B\in A}{\bf 1}_{Y^{t}\in C}={\bf E}Z^{B}(t)(A){\bf 1}_{Y^{t}\in C},

and (
3.3
) follows.
Also, since
{\cal L}_{\tilde{P}}(Z^{\tilde{B}})={\cal L}_{\tilde{P}}(\tilde{Z}^{B})={\cal L}_{P}(Z^{B})
, we have for
t\in[0,T]

\tilde{\pi}_{t}(\tilde{Z}^{B}(t,\omega))=\delta_{f(\tilde{Y}(\omega))(u))(u)}.

By Lemma
4.2
, we obtain that
\tilde{B}
is
\tilde{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted. As in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we establish that
\tilde{B}
is
K
-decreasing and satisfies the admissibility property. Finally, we obtain that
\tilde{B}\in{\cal A}(x,{\tilde{M}})
.
Since
{\cal L}_{P}(Y,S,B)={\cal L}_{\tilde{P}}(\tilde{Y},\tilde{S},\tilde{B})
, we have

\tilde{\bf E}\left(x+\frac{1}{\tilde{S}}\cdot\tilde{B}_{T}\right)={\bf E}\left(x+\frac{1}{S}\cdot B_{T}\right),

we have
u(x,{\bf M})\leq u(x,{\bf\tilde{M}})
. By switching
{\bf M}
and
{\bf\tilde{M}}
, we get the converse inequality.

\square

Lemma 4.1

Let
X=(X_{t})
be an adapted process with paths from
{\cal D}^{d}_{T}
and
K
be a closed proper convex cone. Then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

X
is
K
-decreasing;

(ii)

X
is of bounded variation with the Radon–Nikodym derivative
\dot{X}:=dX/d{\rm Var}\,X
evolving in
-K
where

{\rm Var}_{t}\,X:=\sum_{i\leq d}{\rm Var}_{t}\,X^{i}
is the total variation of
X
on
[0,t]
.

Proof.

The implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
is obvious. Let us verify that
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
.
As
K
is proper,
K^{*}
has a non-empty interior and, therefore, there is a point
y\in K^{*}
such that
y+e^{j}\in K^{*}
for all vectors
e^{j}
of the canonical basis. It follows that
K^{*}
contains
d
linear independent vectors
a^{j}
.
Define the scalar processes
Z^{j}:=a^{j}X
.
Since
X_{s}-X_{t}\in K
for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, we have
Z^{j}_{s}-Z^{j}_{t}\geq 0
. The coefficients
Y^{j}_{t}
,
j=1,\dots,d
, of a linear combination

X_{t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}a^{j}Y_{t}^{j}

can be obtained as linear combinations of scalar products
Z_{t}
:

Y_{t}=G^{-1}Z_{t},

where
G
is the Gram matrix of the basis
(a^{j})_{j=1}^{d}
. It implies that
Y^{j}
are RCLL processes of bounded variation and so is
X
. By virtue of Lemma I.3.13 of
[
8
]
, the Radon–Nikodym derivatives
\dot{X}_{t}:=dX_{t}/d{\rm Var}_{t}X
are well-defined. Since

a_{i}(X_{s}-X_{t})=\int_{]s,t]}a\dot{X}_{u}d{\rm Var}_{u}X\leq 0,\quad dP\otimes d{\rm Var}_{T}\,X-{\rm a.e.},

\dot{X}\in K
up to a set of
dP\otimes d\,{\rm Var}_{T}\,X
-measure zero. By redefining
\dot{X}
on this set, we arrive at the required statement.

\square

Lemma 4.2

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be two Polish spaces and let
j:{\cal A}\times{\cal B}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Borel mapping. Let
({\bf a},{\bf b})
be a
{\cal A}\times{\cal B}
-valued random variable. If the regular conditional distribution

\nu({\bf a},C):=P(j({\bf a},{\bf b})\in C\,|\,\sigma\{{\bf a}\})={\bf 1}_{j({\bf a},{\bf b})\in C},\quad P-{\rm a.s.}

for any
C\in{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}})
then
j({\bf a},{\bf b})=k({\bf a})

P
-a.s. for some Borel function

.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that

is bounded. Then,

{\bf E}[j({\bf a},{\bf b})\,|\,\sigma\{{\bf a}\}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\nu({\bf a},dx)=j({\bf a},{\bf b}),\quad P-{\rm a.s.}

By definition,
{\bf E}[j({\bf a},{\bf b})\,|\,\sigma\{{\bf a}\}]
is
\sigma\{{\bf a}\}
-measurable, and the assertion follows.

\square

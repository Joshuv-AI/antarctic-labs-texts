Late arrhythmias following Fontan completion: insights from a Korean Multicentre Fontan Registry of over 1000 extracardiac conduit surgeries
Authors: Ja-Kyoung Yoon, Kyung-Jin Ahn, Soo-Jin Kim, Chang-Ha Lee, I Seok Kang, Jinyoung Song, June Huh, Sang-Yun Lee, Mi Kyoung Song, Seung Min Baek, Jae-Suk Baek, Seul Gi Cha, Chun Soo Park, Eun Seok Choi, Hoon Ko, Se Yong Jung, Joowon Lee, Jae Young Lee, Yeo Hyang Kim, Young-Seok Lee, Eun-Jung Bae
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsAwareness of long-term arrhythmia complications after Fontan surgery is increasing, but the impact of surgical type and risk factors on arrhythmia development remain incompletely understood.Methods and resultsWe retrospectively analysed 1492 Fontan patients from the Korean multicentre registry (1979–2019), categorized into atriopulmonary connection (AP) (n = 106), lateral tunnel (LT) (n = 234), and extracardiac conduit (ECC) (n = 1143) with a median follow-up of 14 years. Arrhythmias occurred in 13.3% of patients at a median age of 17.3 years (tachyarrhythmias 9.9%, bradyarrhythmias 6.8%). At 20 years, freedom from any arrhythmia, tachyarrhythmia, and bradyarrhythmia was 82.5%, 86.9%, and 90.5%, respectively. On multivariable analysis, AP (HR 5.89, 95% CI 3.75–9.25; P < 0.001) and LT (HR 2.85, 95% CI 1.93–4.21; P < 0.001) were associated with a higher risk of arrhythmia compared with ECC. Older age at Fontan surgery also independently increased arrhythmia risk (HR 1.06, 95% CI 1.03–1.10; P < 0.001). Compared with ECC Fontan, LT Fontan and AP Fontan were associated with a progressively higher risk of both tachyarrhythmias (HR 2.99 and 8.20, respectively) and bradyarrhythmias (HR 2.63 and 5.47, respectively; all P < 0.001). The all-cause mortality rate and Fontan failure rate were 9.3% (0.69 per 100 patient-years) and 20.0% (1.61 per 100 patient-years), respectively. Late-onset arrhythmias were associated with an increased risk of mortality and Fontan failure (P = 0.003 and P < 0.001).ConclusionThis large, long-term registry shows that ECC Fontan confers a lower arrhythmia risk than LT or AP, and arrhythmias serve as a marker of Fontan failure and mortality in post-Fontan patients.Keywords: Fontan, Arrhythmia, Extracardiac conduit, Lateral tunnel, Long-term outcomes, Fontan failure, Mortality

Introduction

Advancements in Fontan surgery have enabled the separation of systemic and pulmonary circulations in patients with univentricular physiology, allowing individuals with a single ventricle to survive into adulthood.1–4 However, their long-term limitations have led to complications such as heart failure, arrhythmia, protein-losing enteropathy, and sudden death, which account for most Fontan-related mortality.5 Arrhythmias occur in 13–54% of patients within 20 years after Fontan surgery and are a major cause of hospitalisation, contributing to increased morbidity and mortality.6,7 The atriopulmonary connection (AP) Fontan is associated with a high incidence of arrhythmias due to atrial enlargement. Although the introduction of the lateral tunnel (LT) and extracardiac conduit (ECC) Fontan techniques has reduced arrhythmia incidence, patients continue to experience a gradual increase in arrhythmia occurrence over time.8 Previous studies have suggested that ECC may further reduce arrhythmia incidence, but small sample sizes and short follow-up periods have limited the ability to draw definitive conclusions.9,10

In Korea, ECC Fontan has been actively performed since the mid-1990s,11 with more than 70% of registry patients having undergone ECC, many of whom are now reaching adulthood.12 Despite recent reports on long-term Fontan outcomes, data on late arrhythmias remain limited. Analysing a Fontan cohort that includes diverse patient characteristics, variations in surgical approaches, and differences in treatment strategies at the national registry level provides valuable insights, particularly for understanding arrhythmia-related risks and their management.10,12,13 Therefore, this study, based on the Korean Fontan registry, aimed to evaluate the incidence and predictors of late arrhythmias during long-term follow-up and to assess their impact on patient outcomes.

Methods

Study population

The Korean multicentre Fontan registry, established in 2019, includes patients from 10 paediatric cardiovascular centres across Korea. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of all participating centres. Lee et al.12 previously reported the clinical characteristics and early and late outcomes of the entire cohort. Data for this analysis were extracted from the registry, which included patients with clinically available information up to 31 December 2019. Patients were excluded if their data were insufficient for detailed arrhythmia analysis. Those who died before discharge following the Fontan procedure or underwent Fontan takedown during the same hospitalisation were also excluded. Of the 1732 identified patients, 190 were excluded due to insufficient data or loss to follow-up, and 50 due to early mortality. Early deaths were predominantly attributable to non-arrhythmic causes (96.0%), and their high AP Fontan rate (44.0% vs. 7.1%) was considered to reflect historical surgical limitations rather than a selection bias affecting late-onset arrhythmia outcomes.

Arrhythmia definition

This study retrospectively analysed rhythm documentation obtained from electrocardiograms (ECGs), Holter monitors, rhythm strips, and device interrogations following the Fontan procedure.

Tachyarrhythmias were classified as supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) or ventricular tachycardia (VT). Supraventricular tachycardia was included only when sustained and requiring intervention, whereas both sustained and non-sustained VT were included due to their clinical significance and the need for treatment. Subtypes of SVT included intra-atrial re-entrant tachycardia (IART, considered equivalent to atrial flutter in this study), atrial fibrillation (AF), and other SVTs such as re-entrant SVT, ectopic or unclassified atrial tachycardia (AT), and junctional tachycardia (JT). Arrhythmia mechanisms were confirmed through electrophysiological studies or expert ECG review.

Bradyarrhythmias were categorised as sinus node dysfunction (SND) or atrioventricular block (AVB), with the analysis focused on cases requiring pacing. This study focused on long-term arrhythmias, defined as those occurring after Fontan completion. Arrhythmias occurring before Fontan surgery, within 1 month post-operatively, or during the Fontan hospitalisation were excluded. However, given that bradyarrhythmias requiring pacemaker implantations typically persist once developed, those that remained present after hospital discharge following Fontan surgery were included. Only bradyarrhythmias that newly developed after Fontan surgery were included in the risk factor analysis.

The presence and timing of arrhythmias were determined based on clinical records and cardiologists’ documentation, and distinct arrhythmia types in the same patient were recorded separately. For the time-to-event analysis, the ‘first onset’ of any clinically significant arrhythmia was utilised as the primary endpoint. To provide an unbiased estimate in this population, cumulative incidence functions (CIFs) were estimated by treating death as a competing risk, ensuring that the risk of arrhythmia was not overestimated. To evaluate the impact of arrhythmias, two long-term endpoints were defined: Late mortality and Fontan failure. Fontan failure was defined as the occurrence of death, heart transplantation, Fontan takedown or conversion, protein-losing enteropathy, plastic bronchitis, or New York Heart Association functional class III or IV at follow-up. This composite endpoint reflects overall failure of the Fontan circulation, including manifestations of conventional heart failure.

Early deaths (within 1 month after Fontan surgery or during the same hospitalisation) were excluded from the analysis.

Statistical analysis

Data analyses were performed using R Studio (version 2023.09.1 + 494). Continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs), and categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Categorical variables were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, whereas continuous variables were analysed using Student’s t-test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, as appropriate.

Time-to-event analyses for arrhythmia following Fontan surgery were conducted using competing risk methodology. Cumulative incidence functions (CIFs) were estimated, treating death as a competing event. Freedom from arrhythmia was expressed as 1 − CIF, representing the arrhythmia-free survival rate at predefined time points (10, 20, and 30 years). Gray’s test was used to compare cumulative incidence curves among Fontan types, and pairwise group comparisons were reported with corresponding P values. Predictors of arrhythmia were assessed using multivariable Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard regression, with results expressed as subdistribution hazard ratios (sHRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) to account for death as a competing risk.

To appropriately account for the time-varying nature of arrhythmia, we used a time-dependent Cox proportional hazards model based on a counting-process formulation. This allowed each patient to contribute risk time to the ‘no arrhythmia’ state before the first arrhythmic event and to the ‘arrhythmia’ state thereafter, providing an unbiased estimate of the effect of arrhythmia on mortality and Fontan failure. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive value of age at Fontan completion. The area under the curve was calculated as a measure of discriminatory performance. The optimal cut-off value was determined using the Youden index.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Prevalence of arrhythmia

Between 1979 and 2019, a total of 1492 patients who underwent the Fontan procedure in South Korea were included in this study. Among them, 106 (7.1%) had AP Fontan, 234 (15.7%) had LT Fontan, and 1143 (76.6%) had ECC Fontan, with 9 (0.6%) remaining unclassified. The most common preoperative cardiac anatomy was unbalanced atrioventricular canal defect (22.2%). The median age at Fontan completion was 3.1 years (IQR, 2.6–4.1), and the median follow-up duration after Fontan surgery was 14.0 years (IQR, 6.1–19.7) overall. It was the longest in the AP Fontan group (26.6 years; IQR, 20.6–31.2), followed by the LT Fontan group (22.9 years; IQR, 20.2–24.8) and the ECC Fontan group (11.2 years; IQR, 4.6–15.9).

Arrhythmias developed in 199 patients (13.3%), with a median onset age of 17.3 years (IQR, 10.0–24.9). The first documented arrhythmia was a tachyarrhythmia in 139 patients (9.3%) and a bradyarrhythmia in 60 patients (4.0%), while 51 patients (3.4%) experienced both types during follow-up.

Patients with arrhythmias had a higher prevalence of heterotaxy syndrome (30.7% vs. 21.7%) and common atrioventricular (AV) valves (30.7% vs. 22.2%). Arrhythmias occurred in 53% of patients with AP Fontan, 31% with LT Fontan, and 6% with ECC Fontan. Those who developed arrhythmias had an older age at Fontan completion (3.6 vs. 3.1 years) and a longer follow-up duration after Fontan completion (22.5 vs. 12.5 years).

Late-onset tachyarrhythmias developed in 149 patients (9.9%) at a median of 13.6 years (IQR, 6.7–18.9) post-Fontan, with a median onset age of 18.6 years (IQR, 12.9–25.8). Among them, 141 patients (9.5%) had SVT with a median onset age of 18.6 years (IQR, 13.8–25.8), 20 patients (1.3%) developed VT with a median onset age of 18.3 years (IQR, 11.7–25.0), and 12 patients (1.0%) experienced both. The most common tachyarrhythmia was IART, occurring in 84 patients (56.4% of tachyarrhythmias), followed by other SVTs —including AT, atrioventricular re-entrant tachycardia (AVRT), atrioventricular nodal re-entrant tachycardia (AVNRT), and JT —in 60 patients (40.3% of tachyarrhythmias). Notably, among the 84 patients diagnosed with IART, 9 patients (10.7%) also had documented AF. However, due to the retrospective nature of this study, it could not be definitively determined whether these arrhythmias occurred simultaneously or sequentially. Supraventricular tachycardia occurred at a median of 13.6 years post-Fontan in 52% of patients with AP Fontan, 21% with LT Fontan, and 3% with ECC Fontan.

Bradyarrhythmias requiring permanent pacemaker implantation occurred in 101 patients (6.8%) with a median onset age of 11.4 years (IQR, 7.1–18.5). The indications for pacing were AV block in 39 patients (38.6% of bradyarrhythmias), with a median pacemaker implantation age of 7.8 years (IQR, 2.6–13.0), and sinus node dysfunction (SND) in 64 patients (63.3% of bradyarrhythmias), with a median pacemaker implantation age of 12.3 years (IQR, 9.2–20.5). Two patients (2.0% of bradyarrhythmias) had both conditions. Seventeen patients received a pacemaker for bradycardia either before, during, or immediately after the Fontan procedure. Furthermore, there was a highly significant statistical association between SND and IART (overall odds ratio, 17.55; P < 0.001). Notably, the incidence of IART among patients with SND varied markedly by surgical type, decreasing from 79.1% in the AP Fontan subgroup to 17.6% in the ECC Fontan subgroup. Additionally, IART was frequently observed to precede the clinical diagnosis of SND during the follow-up period in our cohort. When stratified by Fontan type, arrhythmia incidence differed significantly across surgical groups (Figure 1B, Table 3). The AP Fontan group consistently showed the highest cumulative incidence of arrhythmia, followed by the LT group, whereas the ECC group demonstrated the lowest risk throughout follow-up (Gray’s test, P < 0.001). Pairwise comparisons confirmed significantly lower arrhythmia-free survival in the AP Fontan group compared with both the LT and ECC Fontan groups, and in the LT Fontan group compared with the ECC Fontan group. Similar patterns were observed across arrhythmia subtypes. Both tachyarrhythmia and bradyarrhythmia occurred most frequently in the AP Fontan group and least frequently in the ECC Fontan group, with intermediate risk in the LT group (all P < 0.001; Figure 1C/1D, Table 3).

On multivariable Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard regression analysis, Fontan type was a strong determinant of arrhythmia risk (Table 4). Compared with the ECC Fontan group, the risk of arrhythmia increased stepwise from LT Fontan (approximately three-fold) to AP Fontan (approximately six-fold). Older age at Fontan completion was an independent risk factor (Table 4).

For tachyarrhythmia, older age at Fontan completion remained the strongest predictor. By Fontan type, LT Fontan was associated with an approximately three-fold higher risk, and AP Fontan with an approximately eight-fold higher risk of tachyarrhythmia compared with ECC Fontan, with the greatest risk observed in the AP Fontan group (Table 4). ROC analysis identified 3.85 years as a potential age threshold; however, its discriminatory performance was modest (see Supplementary material online, Figure S1). The analysis was restricted to post-Fontan bradyarrhythmias, and pre-Fontan pacemaker implantation was considered a competing event. Older age at Fontan surgery was significantly associated with bradyarrhythmia. Compared with ECC Fontan, AP Fontan (sHR 5.5) and LT Fontan (sHR 2.6) were both associated with a substantially higher risk of bradyarrhythmia, whereas left isomerism showed a borderline association (Table 4).

In the ECC-only cohort (n = 1143), older age at Fontan completion was confirmed as an independent predictor of all arrhythmia categories (any arrhythmia: sHR 1.11; tachyarrhythmia: sHR 1.12; bradyarrhythmia: sHR 1.08; all P < 0.001). Left isomerism additionally emerged as a significant predictor of any arrhythmia (sHR 3.18, P = 0.003) and bradyarrhythmia (sHR 4.21, P < 0.001), while no other anatomical variable reached significance. Interestingly, a more recent calendar year of Fontan completion was independently associated with a reduced risk of bradyarrhythmia (sHR 0.93, P = 0.017) (see Supplementary material online, Table S1).

Impact of arrhythmia development

The all-cause mortality rate in the cohort was 9.3% (139 patients, 0.69 per 100 patient-years). In the time-dependent survival analysis, the period after the onset of arrhythmia was associated with a substantially higher hazard of late mortality compared with periods without arrhythmia (HR 2.09, 95% CI 1.29–3.39, P = 0.003). Survival curves diverged progressively after 10–15 years following Fontan completion, demonstrating a persistent and cumulative adverse impact of arrhythmia on long-term survival (Figure 2A).

Fontan failure occurred in 300 patients (20.0%; 1.61 per 100 patient-years). Time-dependent Cox modelling showed that the risk of Fontan failure increased significantly once arrhythmia occurred (HR 3.15, 95% CI 2.34–4.24, P < 0.001) (Figure 2B).

Thromboembolic events were reported in 66 patients, including 27 (18.1%) of those with tachyarrhythmias and 39 (2.9%) of those without tachyarrhythmias.

In contrast, permanent pacemaker (PPM) implantation did not appear to confer a similar adverse prognostic impact. In a time-dependent Cox regression analysis, neither overall PPM implantation nor implantation specific to SND or AVB was significantly associated with late mortality or Fontan failure. These findings remained unchanged after multivariable adjustment for concomitant tachyarrhythmia as a time-varying covariate (see Supplementary material online, Table S2).

Tachyarrhythmia treatment

Among patients with tachyarrhythmia, 106 (71%) received antiarrhythmic drugs, 53 (36%) underwent direct current cardioversion, and 24 (16%) underwent radiofrequency catheter ablation. Arrhythmia surgery was performed in 53 patients (36%), and 47 patients (32%) underwent Fontan conversion.

Radiofrequency catheter ablation

Between 2001 and 2019, 24 patients underwent a total of 28 radiofrequency catheter ablations. Intra-atrial re-entrant tachycardia was the most common arrhythmia treated (12 patients; 50% of catheter ablations), followed by re-entrant SVT in 8 patients (33%). Focal AT was identified in 4 patients (17%), and 2 patients (8%) had both IART and focal AT. Pulmonary vein isolation for AF was performed in 2 patients (8%), with one requiring a repeat ablation procedure. Among re-entrant SVTs, ablations included AVNRT in 3 patients (13%), AVRT in 3 patients (13%), and AVRT mediated by twin AV nodes in 2 patients (8%), with three cases of recurrences.

Following ablation, three patients (13%) subsequently underwent arrhythmia surgery, and another three patients (13%) had Fontan conversion prior to ablation. Arrhythmia recurrence occurred in 11 patients (48%) at a median of 1.6 years (IQR, 0.5 − 1.9), with 4 patients (17%) requiring repeat ablation. The median follow-up duration after ablation was 3.9 years (IQR, 2.4 − 4.9).

Arrhythmia surgery and Fontan conversion

Among 53 patients (36% of those with tachyarrhythmia) who underwent arrhythmia surgery, 43 underwent Fontan conversion, while 9 had other surgeries, performed at a median of 1.8 years (IQR, 0.2–4.4) after tachyarrhythmia onset. Most patients with pre-existing arrhythmias underwent arrhythmia surgery concurrently with Fontan conversion, whereas only four underwent conversion alone. Of the patients with arrhythmias who underwent Fontan conversion, a total of 47 patients were identified: 33 (70%) underwent conversion from AP to ECC, 7 (15%) from AP to LT, and 7 (15%) from LT to ECC. The primary target was atrial tachyarrhythmia (mostly IART), except for one case involving an accessory bypass tract. Right-sided Maze surgery was performed in 28 patients (53%), biatrial Maze in 12 (23%), and isthmus cryoablation in 12 (23%).

After a median of 3.9 years (IQR, 2.9–7.0) post-surgery, 17 patients (32%) experienced arrhythmia recurrence, with a median follow-up of 8.4 years (IQR, 6.5–9.5). Compared with catheter ablation, arrhythmia surgery demonstrated significantly higher recurrence-free survival at 1 year (97.9% vs. 79.6%) and 3 years (88.8% vs. 53.3%) (P < 0.001, Supplementary material online, Figure S2).

Bradyarrhythmia treatment

Pacemaker therapy

A total of 101 patients (6.8%) required epicardial PPM implantation for bradyarrhythmia at a median age of 11.4 years (IQR, 6.6–18.5). Among them, 10 patients (9.9% of those with bradyarrhythmia) received a pacemaker before Fontan completion at a median age of 1.01 years (IQR, 0.33–4.22) including 2 patients with SND and 8 patients with AVB. Of the 91 patients (90% of those with bradyarrhythmia) who received a PPM after Fontan surgery, 45 (45% of total PPM patients) underwent implantation in the perioperative period and 46 (46%) in the late post-operative period. Specifically, 12 patients (12%) received a PPM during or shortly after the initial Fontan procedure, 27 patients (27%) underwent PPM implantation during or immediately following Fontan conversion, and 6 patients (6%) received a PPM after other cardiac surgeries performed following the Fontan completion, with atrioventricular valve surgery being the most common.

In the late post-operative period, 46 patients (46% of those with bradyarrhythmia) underwent PPM implantation as a separate intervention after Fontan surgery.

Among patients requiring PPM implantation in the perioperative period after Fontan surgery, 33 had SND and 14 had AVB (including 2 patients with both SND and AVB). In the late post-operative period, 29 patients required PPM implantation for SND and 17 for AVB. Additionally, 30 patients (33%) had a prior history of maze surgery or radiofrequency ablation for tachyarrhythmia.

Dual-chamber pacemakers were implanted in 77 patients (77%), whereas 23 patients (23%) received single-chamber devices (14 ventricular, 9 atrial). One patient with concurrent SND and ventricular tachyarrhythmia received an epicardial implantable cardioverter defibrillator.

Discussion

This multicentre Korean Fontan registry provides one of the most extensive long-term datasets on arrhythmias following the Fontan procedure, spanning more than three decades. At 20 years, freedom from any arrhythmia was higher than that previously reported in the Australian and New Zealand Fontan Registry, likely reflecting the predominance of the ECC Fontan technique in Korea.7 Unlike earlier studies in which ECC patients had relatively limited follow-up, our cohort included a large population of ECC patients observed for nearly two decades, enabling a more robust demonstration that the ECC Fontan confers a substantially lower risk of both tachyarrhythmias and bradyarrhythmias compared with the LT or AP Fontan.9,14

Fontan patients are inherently predisposed to arrhythmias because of the haemodynamic limitations of the Fontan circulation,15 while surgical factors such as atrial incisions, scarring, and atrial dilatation further increase the arrhythmogenic substrate, particularly for atrial arrhythmias. To mitigate these risks, the Fontan operation has evolved through several modifications from the AP to the LT and ultimately to the ECC technique. Although it is well recognized that LT and ECC procedures are less arrhythmogenic than the AP Fontan procedure, direct comparisons between LT and ECC have produced inconsistent results. This inconsistency has largely been attributed to smaller cohort sizes and shorter follow-up durations of the ECC cohorts compared with those of AP and LT populations.16–19 In contrast, the present study provides robust long-term evidence from a relatively large cohort, demonstrating that the ECC Fontan is associated with a lower incidence of arrhythmias than both LT and AP procedures. Despite incomplete documentation of LT operative details, most LT procedures in Korea used an intracardiac technique, and the protective association of the ECC Fontan persisted after adjustment for follow-up duration and mortality. A plausible explanation lies in the surgical design of the ECC Fontan, which avoids additional intra-atrial or intraventricular incisions beyond the suture lines connecting the superior and inferior vena cava. The persistent lower incidence of tachyarrhythmias during long-term follow-up represents a key finding of this study and reinforces the long-term rhythm stability advantage conferred by the ECC approach.

However, the absolute arrhythmia incidence in our ECC cohort (6%) appears lower than the 28% prevalence reported by Di Mambro et al.20 in a dedicated ECC population, which likely reflects differences in arrhythmia definition rather than a true discrepancy in outcomes. Our study captured only clinically significant arrhythmias requiring intervention, whereas they included asymptomatic cases detected via routine Holter monitoring. Furthermore, their longer median follow-up (15 vs. 11.2 years) and the observation that arrhythmias typically emerge after 12 years suggest that the incidence in our cohort may increase with further follow-up, necessitating continued longitudinal surveillance.

Bradyarrhythmias requiring pacemaker implantation occurred in 7% of Fontan patients, consistent with previous reports.21,22 Although bradyarrhythmias are partly surgery-related and therefore more challenging to compare directly across Fontan types, our study demonstrated that among patients requiring pacemaker implantation, SND was more common than AVB. Notably, even after adjusting for follow-up duration, the incidence of bradyarrhythmias was lower in patients who underwent ECC Fontan compared with those who underwent AP or LT Fontan. These findings support the interpretation that the reduced extent of atrial incision and the lesser degree of atrial enlargement associated with the ECC technique may help delay the progression of atrial functional decline. Furthermore, multivariable analysis confirmed that AP and LT Fontan procedures were independently associated with a higher incidence of arrhythmias compared with ECC, and this pattern persisted when tachyarrhythmias and bradyarrhythmias were analysed separately.

In addition, the incidence of arrhythmias increased with age, and older age at Fontan completion was associated with a higher risk of arrhythmias.23 However, in the whole-cohort analysis, this association may be confounded by the predominance of older Fontan types, as AP and LT patients tend to have longer follow-up and greater cumulative haemodynamic burden. To isolate the effect of age at Fontan surgery from the arrhythmogenic influence of surgical technique, we performed a dedicated subgroup analysis restricted to the ECC cohort. Older age at Fontan completion remained a significant independent predictor of all arrhythmias in the ECC-only cohort, thereby corroborating the hypothesis that prolonged preoperative haemodynamic burden, irrespective of the surgical technique employed, facilitates the atrial remodelling and arrhythmogenic substrate formation over time.

Although no anatomical variable reached statistical significance as a predictor of tachyarrhythmia in either the whole-cohort or ECC-only analyses, two findings warrant further attention. The presence of a common AV valve was associated with a trend towards an increased risk of tachyarrhythmia, and while AV valve regurgitation may contribute to atrial enlargement and arrhythmogenic substrate, granular longitudinal data on regurgitation progression were unavailable in our registry. Similarly, ventricular morphology deserves closer scrutiny. A recent long-term study for ECC Fontan reported a significantly higher risk of ventricular tachycardia in patients with a systemic right ventricle (RV), suggesting that progressive RV remodelling may create an increasingly arrhythmogenic substrate with age.20 Both relationships remain incompletely characterised and warrant prospective investigation with more longitudinal data.

Notably, left isomerism, which showed only borderline significance for bradyarrhythmia in the whole cohort, emerged as a highly significant independent predictor of any arrhythmia and bradyarrhythmia in the ECC-only cohort. This unmasking phenomenon suggests that AP and LT techniques inherently exert a substantial arrhythmogenic burden sufficient to obscure the contribution of underlying anatomy. Conversely, the ECC approach elucidates the authentic anatomical susceptibility to conduction system pathology attributable to left isomerism, which is frequently associated with absent or structurally abnormal sinus node tissue and its vasculature.24 Moreover, in the ECC-only analysis, a more recent calendar year of Fontan completion was independently associated with a reduced risk of bradyarrhythmia, suggesting that contemporary refinements in surgical technique and perioperative care may have contributed to improved conduction system preservation over time.

Importantly, the development of arrhythmia in Fontan patients was associated with worse survival and a higher risk of Fontan failure in our study. Although previous studies have reported conflicting findings regarding whether arrhythmia is a risk factor for mortality or Fontan failure in Fontan patients,5,25,26 our findings provide additional insight by accounting for the time-dependent nature of arrhythmia development after Fontan surgery. Specifically, the Fontan failure-free survival curves diverged approximately 10 years after Fontan completion, with increasing separation over time, indicating a cumulative adverse impact of arrhythmia. Fontan failure-free survival declined markedly in the arrhythmia group during late follow-up, although the number of patients at risk was limited. In our study, we accounted for the fact that arrhythmias can develop and evolve over time following Fontan surgery, allowing us to more accurately estimate the longitudinal impact of arrhythmia exposure in a manner that reflects real-world clinical trajectories. These findings underscore that Fontan patients who develop arrhythmia may require close follow-up and timely intervention.

Management of tachyarrhythmias in Fontan patients includes antiarrhythmic drug therapy, catheter ablation, and arrhythmia surgery, each of which carries important limitations. Long-term pharmacological treatment is constrained by adverse effects and the risk of bradyarrhythmia,10 while ablation, although increasingly effective with advances in mapping and energy delivery techniques,27,28 has limitations due to complex anatomy and a high recurrence rate in this population.29 The relatively low incidence of catheter ablation in our cohort reflects two distinct factors. First, the early and widespread adoption of the ECC Fontan in Korea resulted in fewer patients with the severe atrial dilatation typically seen with older Fontan types, thereby reducing the overall burden of complex tachyarrhythmias. Second, when clinically significant arrhythmias did occur, our centres historically favoured a definitive surgical approach, specifically Fontan conversion combined with concomitant arrhythmia surgery, over catheter ablation, as this strategy allows for simultaneous haemodynamic optimisation and comprehensive arrhythmic substrate management in a single intervention.30,31 Although concomitant arrhythmia surgery during Fontan conversion can suppress tachyarrhythmias,32 recurrence rates remain substantial, underscoring the persistent challenge of maintaining long-term rhythm stability in this population. With continued advances in catheter mapping and ablation techniques, and given that the growing Fontan population will inevitably accumulate a greater burden of late arrhythmias over time, the role of catheter ablation is expected to expand considerably in the contemporary management of these patients.

In Fontan patients, bradyarrhythmias requiring a pacemaker may result from underlying anatomical abnormalities, surgery-related conduction disturbances, or the haemodynamic consequences of the Fontan circulation itself, making it challenging to classify each cause distinctly. In our study, most cases were attributable to SND, likely related to progressive atrial enlargement and fibrosis.33 While SND and IART remain strongly associated, consistent with historical cohorts, the clinical burden of this vicious cycle has profoundly decreased in the modern ECC era. The observation that IART frequently preceded SND likely reflects our strict SND definition requiring pacing; early subclinical SND remained undetected until exacerbated by IART or its treatments.34 Although earlier studies suggested an association between pacing—particularly ventricular pacing—and adverse outcomes, this relationship could not be confirmed in our cohort.22,35

In summary, this study provides valuable insights into arrhythmia patterns among Korean Fontan patients, in whom the predominance of ECC Fontan has contributed to a lower arrhythmia incidence. Particularly, the ECC Fontan procedure demonstrated a significant advantage in long-term rhythm stability, as it was significantly associated with a lower arrhythmia incidence than the LT and AP Fontan. The observed association between older age at Fontan completion and higher arrhythmia risk supports the current practice of performing Fontan surgery at approximately 3 years of age. Late arrhythmias were significantly associated with mortality and Fontan failure, underscoring their critical impact on long-term outcomes. Despite ongoing advances in medical, surgical, and interventional management, the haemodynamic limitations inherent to the Fontan circulation continue to drive high arrhythmia recurrence rates. Future efforts should aim to minimise arrhythmogenic substrates, optimise catheter ablation strategies, and develop preventive approaches. Ultimately, preventing arrhythmias and managing them promptly and effectively remain essential for improving both survival and quality of life in patients with Fontan physiology.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations. Arrhythmia-free survival may have been overestimated, as asymptomatic or paroxysmal arrhythmias could have been under-detected due to the lack of systematic routine ambulatory ECG monitoring, and events among those lost to follow-up could not be captured. In addition, treating death as a competing risk may have underestimated the overall clinical burden of arrhythmia. The retrospective design also inherently carries a risk of underreporting. Furthermore, the multicentre and retrospective nature of our registry limited our ability to precisely determine longitudinal transition rates between arrhythmia types, as well as to evaluate the impact of progressive haemodynamic changes, such as AV regurgitation, on the development and evolution of arrhythmias over time. Additionally, because the ECC Fontan was introduced later, the follow-up duration was shorter; however, the lower arrhythmia incidence in the ECC group remained consistent after adjustment for follow-up duration, supporting the robustness of our findings. Finally, variations in management practices across multiple centres may also have influenced outcomes and should be considered when interpreting the results.

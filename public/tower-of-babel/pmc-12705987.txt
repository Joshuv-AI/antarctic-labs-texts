Analysis of Clinical Subphenotypes in Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus Bacteremia: A Post Hoc Analysis of the MRSA-GEIRAS-SEIMC Study
Authors: Sofía De La Villa, Nuria Fernández-Hidalgo, Francesc Escrihuela-Vidal, Rosa Escudero-Sánchez, Itxasne Cabezón, Lucía Boix-Palop, Beatriz Díaz-Pollán, Ane Josune Goikoetxea, María José García-País, Lucía Ramos-Merino, María Teresa Pérez-Rodríguez, Ángela Crespo, Lara del Río, José María Bellón-Cano, Patricia Muñoz, Damaris Berbel, Damaris Berbel, Luis Buzón-Martín, David Campany, Alex García-Tellado, Inmaculada Grau, José Manuel Guerra-Laso, Joan Roig-Sanchis, Celia Sánchez-Martínez, Oscar Sanz, Fiorana Silvante, Belén Viñado, Luciana Urbina, Ana V Halperin, Mariona Xercavins
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWe aimed to identify and evaluate clinical subphenotypes in a cohort of patients with methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus bacteremia (MRSAB) and to assess their association with all-cause 90-day mortality.MethodsThis post hoc analysis of the MRSA-GEIRAS-SEIMC study was conducted across 15 Spanish hospitals. MRSAB in adult patients from 2019 to 2022 were included. Clinical subphenotypes were identified using a combination of principal component analysis and latent class analysis based on age, sex, comorbidities, SOFA score, creatinine levels, metastatic foci, source, and acquisition. The 90-day mortality associated with each subphenotype was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Cox regression was performed to assess the risk of death.ResultsA total of 419 MRSAB were included. Four distinct subphenotypes were identified: S1 was associated with younger age, community acquisition, and unknown or skin and soft-tissue infection source; S2 was associated with older age, female sex, high burden of comorbidities, and healthcare-related acquisition; S3 was linked to a catheter source and nosocomial acquisition; and S4 was predominantly associated with the presence of heart valve prostheses, and metastatic foci. Significant differences in all-cause 90-day mortality were observed across subphenotypes: 20.0% in S1, 47.4% in S2, 26.2% in S3, and 35.1% in S4 (P < .01). Cox regression indicated an increased 90-day mortality risk in S2 (HR, 2.98; 95% CI, 1.59–5.56) and S4 (HR, 1.99; 95% CI, 1.16–3.42) compared with S1.ConclusionsWe identified 4 distinct clinical subphenotypes of MRSAB associated with prognostic outcomes. Further investigation is needed to implement them into clinical practice.

Staphylococcus aureus is a leading cause of bacteremia with high morbidity and mortality rates [1, 2]. Recent efforts have focused on improving the understanding and management of S. aureus bacteremia (SAB) [3, 4]. Several clinical trials have assessed the efficacy of combination therapy in patients with SAB episodes; however, none have demonstrated a clear clinical benefit [5–8].

The risk of mortality in patients with SAB episodes is not uniform. Identifying patients at increased risk is essential, as they may benefit from combination therapy or alternative treatment approaches [9, 10]. Despite advances in rapid diagnostic testing, methicillin-resistant S. aureus bacteremia (MRSAB) continues to be associated with worse outcomes than methicillin-sensitive infections [2].

Traditional classifications of uncomplicated and complicated SAB episodes are being increasingly challenged due to their failure to incorporate relevant clinical factors, including infection source, time to blood culture positivity, comorbidities, and risk of metastatic complications [10–13].

A recently published classification model proposed 5 clinical subphenotypes in 3 cohorts of patients with SAB episodes, predominantly caused by methicillin-sensitive S. aureus (MSSA) [14]. This study demonstrated that certain subphenotypes were associated with increased 84-day mortality and may be more responsive to combination therapy. Furthermore, categorizing patients with SAB into clinical subphenotypes could reveal patterns that are relevant to prognosis and that are not apparent using traditional severity scores.

Our aim was to identify and evaluate clinical subphenotypes in a cohort of patients with MRSAB episodes and to determine their association with all-cause 90-day mortality.

METHODS

Study Design

This was a post hoc analysis of the retrospective MRSA-GEIRAS-SEIMC study [15]. The original cohort included 429 consecutive adult patients (aged ≥ 18 years) treated for MRSAB episodes across 15 Spanish hospitals between 2019 and 2022. The primary objective of the parent study was to compare outcomes in patients treated with ceftaroline (n = 133) versus daptomycin or vancomycin (n = 296). The outcomes included all-cause mortality at 30 and 90 days.

Exclusion criteria previously considered were (1) death within the first 72 hours or imminent death at the time of the bacteremia onset, as determined by clinical judgment, (2) polymicrobial bacteremia, (3) blood cultures obtained through the catheter lumen only, (4) lack of relevant data regarding antibiotic duration and outcomes, and (5) participation in a clinical trial for the SAB treatment during the index episode. For this post hoc study, patients with missing relevant variables used to identify subphenotypes were also excluded.

The study received ethical approval from the Hospital General Universitario Gregorio Marañón Ethics Committee (code: MICRO.HGUGM.2023-007). Due to the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent was waived.

Variables and Definitions

MRSAB episodes were defined through positive blood cultures obtained by peripheral venipuncture with identification of S. aureus. Methicillin resistance was demonstrated by either phenotypic or genotypic methods [16].

Data were collected using a REDCap® database by site investigators via medical record review. Clinical variables included age, sex, comorbidities, Charlson comorbidity score [17], Sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score at bacteremia onset [18], and the presence of heart valve prostheses. Infection sources were classified as catheter, skin and soft tissue infection (SSTI), unknown/primary, pulmonary, urinary, and other. Acquisition was categorized as community related, healthcare related, or nosocomial [19].

Combination antibiotic therapy was defined as the continuous use of at least 2 full-dose MRSA-active agents for at least 48 hours within the first week after the onset of bacteremia. Infective endocarditis (IE) was defined using the 2023 Duke–ISCVID criteria [20]. In addition, the presence of metastatic foci and treatment-related adverse events (AEs) were recorded.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was all-cause mortality within 90 days of MRSAB onset. Secondary outcomes included 30-day all-cause mortality, AEs frequency, and 90-day all-cause mortality comparing combination antibiotic therapy with monotherapy.

Statistical Analysis

Quantitative variables were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR); qualitative variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Quantitative comparisons were made using Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test.

Principal component analysis (PCA) was first applied to reduce the dimensionality of correlated clinical variables into a smaller set of uncorrelated components, capturing the main sources of variation in the data. These components were then used in a latent class analysis (LCA), a model-based clustering approach that identifies mutually exclusive groups of patients sharing similar profiles. This sequential strategy allowed the identification of distinct clinical subphenotypes in line with previous research and available data [14]. Input variables included age, sex, comorbidities, SOFA score, creatinine at bacteremia onset, metastatic foci, infection source, and acquisition type. Variables with a Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) index < 0.5 [21] or factor loadings < 0.3 were excluded (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). The remaining 12 variables yielded a total KMO value of 0.58. Bartlett's test of sphericity was significant (P < .001), supporting the use of PCA [22, 23]. Based on KMO criterion, 4 factors with eigenvalues > 1 were retained, explaining 59% of the total variance. For the PCA, 4 cases were excluded due to the absence of relevant data regarding the SOFA score (n = 2) and creatinine levels (n = 2).

LCA was subsequently applied to the scores derived from these 4 PCA factors in a total of 425 patients. Models with up to 7 classes were evaluated. The 4-class model was selected based on the lowest Bayesian information criterion and highest entropy (Table 1). Each participant's probability of belonging to one of the 4 subphenotypes was calculated. Individuals were assigned to a subphenotype when their membership probability exceeded 50%. Those with < 50% probability for any group were excluded from classification. To verify the robustness of the results, a sensitivity analysis was performed in which all excluded individuals were assigned at each step to each of the phenotypes generated by the LCA.

Clinical characteristics across subphenotypes were compared. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and the log-rank test were used to evaluate differences in 90-day survival. Cox regression was performed to estimate the risk of death for each subphenotype, relative to subphenotype 1, which had the best survival rate. All statistical analyses were performed using Stata version 19 (Stata Corp LLC, College Station, TX).

RESULTS

Of the 429 patients included in the MRSA-GEIRAS-SEIMC cohort, 4 cases were excluded due to the absence of relevant data for the PCA analysis, and 6 others due to a probability below 50% of assignment to any of the 4 subphenotypes. Ultimately, 419 patients (97.7%) were classified into 1 of 4 subphenotypes (Supplementary Figure 1).

The final subphenotypes were as follows (Table 2): Subphenotype 1—younger community acquired cases—was associated with younger age (median: 66 years, IQR: 57–77), MRSAB of unknown or SSTI origin (54.8%), and community acquisition (83.5%). Subphenotype 2—elderly with comorbidities from long-term care facilities—involved patients aged ≥ 80 years (median: 87 years, IQR: 81–91), a higher proportion of females (52.6%), increased comorbidity burden (dementia: 94.7%, chronic kidney disease: 34.2%), and healthcare related acquisition (60.5%). Subphenotype 3—nosocomial catheter linked cases—was characterized by catheter-related source (62.8%) and nosocomial acquisition (80.2%). Subphenotype 4—device-associated or complex cases—was associated with other infection sources—than primary, catheter, SSTI, pulmonary, or urinary—(63.8%), the presence of heart valve prostheses (48.9%), metastatic foci (55.3%), and IE (21.3%).

Significant differences in 90-day all-cause mortality were observed across subphenotypes (Figure 1). At 90 days post-MRSAB onset, mortality rates were 20.0% in subphenotype 1, 47.4% in subphenotype 2, 26.2% in subphenotype 3, and 35.1% in subphenotype 4 (P < .01). Cox regression, using subphenotype 1 as the reference, revealed a significantly increased 90-day mortality risk in subphenotype 2 (HR 2.98, 95% CI: 1.59–5.56) and subphenotype 4 (HR 1.99, 95% CI: 1.16–3.42). No statistically significant difference was found for subphenotype 3 (HR 1.40, 95% CI: 0.84–2.35) (Table 3). The sensitivity analysis, which included the 10 patients who were previously excluded (N = 429), yielded survival values like those in the main analysis of 419 patients (Supplementary Figure 2).

Regarding secondary outcomes, 30-day mortality rates were 11.3%, 31.6%, 17.4%, and 21.3% for subphenotypes 1, 2, 3, and 4, respectively (P < .01). No statistically significant differences in AEs rates were observed among the subphenotypes (Table 2). Combination therapy was most frequent in subphenotype 4 (41.5%) in comparison to the S1, S2, and S3 respectively (29.6%, 13.2%, 22.7%, P < .01). Across subphenotypes, up to 8 different treatment antibiotic combinations were used, with daptomycin and ceftaroline being the most common (Supplementary Table 3). Differences in all-cause 90-day mortality between monotherapy and combination therapy by subphenotype are shown in Figure 2. In the subphenotype 2, the frequency of combination therapy was the lowest (5 cases, 13.2%) and presented a nonstatistically significant higher 90-day mortality compared to monotherapy (80.0% vs 42.4%, P = .15). No mortality differences were seen in the other subphenotypes when comparing monotherapy with combination therapy. DISCUSSION

In this nationwide, multicenter study, focused exclusively on MRSAB episodes, we identified 4 distinct clinical subphenotypes, primarily shaped by comorbidity burden, source, and infection acquisition context. Our findings demonstrate the feasibility of defining clinical subphenotypes in MRSAB episodes using LCA, with prognostic implications. Finally, 2 subphenotypes (2 and 4) were associated with increased 30- and 90-day mortality risk.

Subphenotypes 1–3 align with those identified in the previous study by Swets et al. [14]. However, we identified a distinct subphenotype not previously well established. In our cohort, subphenotype 4—device-associated or complex cases—was associated with a different source from primary, catheter, SSTI, pulmonary or urinary, frequent presence of a heart valve prosthesis, and elevated risk of metastatic foci and IE. This group also showed an increased risk of mortality. These differences may stem from variations in acquisition and infection source, potentially affecting classification within MSSA and MRSA bacteremia groups [24]. More recently, a cluster analysis identified phenotypes and subphenotypes in patients with SAB episodes [25]. Although the methodologies differed, that study classified 3 phenotypes by source of bacteremia, each with 2 subphenotypes based on infection complexity and comorbidities. Considering our findings alongside these prior studies, we conclude that source, acquisition, comorbidity, and clinical presentation are key prognostic factors in SAB episodes (Supplementary Table 5). We believe that this type of analysis should be validated through prospective studies that compare the classification of subphenotypes with the classic risk factors for complicated and uncomplicated bacteremia. It could also be considered to validate a model that includes subphenotypes alongside other risk factors studied, such as persistent bacteremia or time to blood culture positivity [12, 13].

Efforts to explore combination therapies and other treatment strategies for patients with SAB episodes could potentially benefit from these classifications [26, 27]. While our study did not demonstrate a clinical benefit for combined therapy, variability in treatment regimens reflected real-world clinical practice, complicating outcome analysis. Additionally, subphenotype 2 showed a nonsignificant toward higher mortality with combination therapy than with monotherapy. However, the small number of patients who received combination therapy in this subphenotype means that these findings are uncertain. Prospective studies are needed to validate these subphenotypes and to explore appropriate indications of combined treatment, particularly for high-risk groups [28, 29].

The main limitation of this study is its retrospective design. We did not document intravenous drug use, although we believe its impact was limited, as most MRSAB episodes were nosocomial or healthcare associated rather than community-onset. Nevertheless, the number, type, and quality of recorded variables remain crucial for validating clinical subphenotypes and their association with mortality risk [21, 25]. Future validation in independent cohorts is warranted. Furthermore, our findings are limited by the inability to determine the effect of various antibiotic regimens. The broad variability in combined treatment regimens, reflecting real-world clinical practice, may partially explain the lack of association between combination therapy and outcomes.

A major strength of our study is the validation of the previous methodology to identify clinical subphenotypes in a large, contemporary cohort of SAB cases caused exclusively by MRSA. Apart from intravenous drug use, we collected most variables included in the Swets et al. study [14]. Furthermore, the development of our LCA model added robustness to our findings. Our results support previous subphenotype classifications and provide the added value of considering additional variables to improve mortality risk stratification in patients with MRSAB episodes.

In conclusion, we identified 4 distinct subphenotypes of MRSAB episodes, each associated with different prognostic outcomes. Further investigation is needed to understand these subphenotypes and implement them into clinical practice.

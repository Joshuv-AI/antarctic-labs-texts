Interpolation of generalized Heegner classes along quaternionic Coleman families
E. Rocha Walchek

Abstract
. We construct big generalized Heegner classes by interpolating p p -adically the generalized Heegner classes associated to quaternionic modular forms along a Coleman (finite slope) family, following the approach introduced by Jetchev–Loeffler–Zerbes.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11F11 (primary), 14G35 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field. The
Bloch–Kato conjecture
for
K
-representations
V
coming from geometry states that the Bloch–Kato Selmer group of
V
dictates the behavior of the
L
-function attached to
V
at the special point
s=0
, minus slight correction:

(1.1)

\mathrm{ord}_{s=0}L(s,V)=\dim H^{1}_{f}(\Gal(\overline{K}/K),V^{*}(1))-\dim H^{0}(\Gal(\overline{K}/K),V^{*}(1)).

If
V
is the
p
-adic representation attached to an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with finite Shafarevich–Tate group, (
1.1
) becomes the
Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture
which is known to hold when
\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(s,E)\in\{0,1\}
, due to Kolyvagin’s work on Euler systems
[
Kol90
]
. The use of norm-compatible system of classes arising from a tower of
Heegner points
and the Gross–Zagier formula
[
GZ86
]
, relating the height of said Heegner points to the derivative of the
L
-function, is the key to bound the size of the Selmer group.

Since then, Euler systems of Abel–Jacobi images of cycles generalizing Heegner points have become the most promising tool for studying the Bloch–Kato conjecture for representations attached to modular forms. Nekovář
[
Nek95
]
introduced
Heegner cycles
for higher weight modular forms, defined over
\mathcal{E}^{k}
, the
k
-th fiber product power of the universal elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}
over the modular curve
Y_{0}(N)
. Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna
[
BDP13
]
later introduced
generalized Heegner cycles
by adding a “fixed” elliptic curve
E
with CM over an imaginary quadratic field factor to the Kuga–Sato variety, now consisting of the canonical desingularization of
\mathcal{E}^{k}\times_{Y_{1}(N)}E^{k}
, from which twists by characters arise. These cycles can be directly related to certain values of
p
-adic analogues of complex
L
-functions, called BDP
p
-adic
L
-functions; notably, these values are obtained by
p
-adic limit processes at points outside the range of
p
-adic interpolation of complex
L
-values. The relation between Abel–Jacobi images of generalized Heegner cycles (classes) and values of the BDP
p
-adic
L
-function outside the range of interpolation is known as
explicit reciprocity law
, and plays a fundamental role in all applications of generalized Heegner cycles to the Bloch–Kato conjecture.

By interpolating classes along a
p
-adic family of modular forms, geometric constructions that are available only for infinitely many classical forms in a
p
-adic family can be used to generate, via
p
-adic approximation, algebraic objects at points where at first no such constructions are available, a major advantage over the single modular form approach. The zero slope (Hida family) case is due to Howard
[
How07
]
: after establishing a relation between the generalized Heegner classes and Heegner points in a certain tower of modular curves, a big Heegner point, playing the role of
p
-adic family of Heegner points, allows for the
p
-adic interpolation along a Hida family of classes associated to elliptic modular forms. Büyükboduk–Lei
[
BL21
]
and Jetchev–Loeffler–Zerbes
[
JLZ21
]
have independently defined
big generalized Heegner cycles
interpolating the
generalized Heegner cycles
of
[
BDP13
]
as they vary over a finite slope (Coleman) family of elliptic modular forms. After
p
-adically interpolating the special values of the complex
L
-functions of each modular form in the family into a
p
-adic
L
-function
, the big generalized Heegner cycles are found to be related to the
p
-adic
L
-function via an
explicit reciprocity law
extending to families the main result of
[
BDP13
]
.

The present document concerns the definition of the
big generalized Heegner cycles
in the indefinite quaternionic setting, adapting the approach of
[
JLZ21
]
. Future work in preparation will address the interpolation of
p
-adic
L
-functions along Coleman families of quaternionic modular forms and the correspondent explicit reciprocity law, an expected extension to families of the main result of
[
HB15
]
. What follows is a brief account of the contents of this article.

1.1.
The indefinite quaternionic setting

In the
elliptic
setting above, generalized Heegner cycles attached to a
p
-stabilized eigenform of level
pN
, with
p\nmid N
, are defined over (ring class fields of) an imaginary quadratic field
K
of discriminant
-D_{K}
in which all prime factors of
N
are split. However, this restrictive hypothesis leave out many fields, which can be covered by weakening the hypothesis on
N
, allowing
N=N^{+}N^{-}
to have both split and inert primes (the factors of
N^{+}
and
N^{-}
respectively). This replaces the modular curves in which the Heegner points were defined with
Shimura curves
over the quaternionic algebra
B
of discriminant
N^{-}>1
classifying
QM abelian surfaces
. Since the modular forms over
B
and
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
are essentialy the same via the Jacquet–Langlands lift, one needs to “halve” the dimension of every object coming from such abelian surfaces so they behave like objects coming from elliptic curves; this is done by introducing an action by a suitable global idempotent
e\in B
. Furthermore, it is imperative for the quaternion algebra
B
to split at
\infty
as
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
does, in which case we say that
B
is
indefinite
.

where
\sigma_{\et}^{2r-j}\bar{\sigma}_{\et}^{j}
is a character arising from a fixed embedding

1.3.
Interpolation into big Heegner classes

Let
\mathcal{F}
now be defined over the Shimura curve
\widetilde{X}_{1}
and
p

a Coleman family of slope

and defined over a suitably small affinoid

[

]
and transfer this problem to the

-adic interpolation of basis vectors of the representation associated, under Ancona’s functor
[

]

(we take all étale sheaves to be defined over a suitable
p
-adic field

over which

is defined). Such basis vectors are, in turn, defined after

in Shimura curves.

An explicitly defined Heegner point

into the standard Eichler order of level

which can be seen as a section over

with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K}

(1.2)

\mathrm{norm}_{F_{cp^{n+1}}/F_{cp^{n}}}\left(\mathbf{z}^{[{U},j]}_{cp^{n+1},1}\right)=\mathbf{a}_{p}\cdot\mathbf{z}^{[{U},j]}_{cp^{n},1}.

The interpolated classes above lie in cohomologies with coefficients in a distributions module

that interpolates the singular form case coefficient sheaves
\TSym^{2r}(eR^{1}\pi_{\mathcal{A},\ast}\mathbb{Q}_{p}(1))
in
2r

at weight

)

2.5.
Shimura curves

The Shimura datum
(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}},\mathcal{H}^{\pm})
for
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}
the algebraic group defined by

(2.4)

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}}(F)\vcentcolon=(B\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}F)^{\times},\text{ for any extension }F/\mathbb{Q},

and
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}=\mathcal{H}^{+}\cup\mathcal{H}^{-}
, where
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}\vcentcolon=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C};\ \operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{Im}(\tau))=\bullet\}
for
\bullet\in\{-,+\}
, is of PEL type.

For each integer
m\geq 0
, let

R_{m}=\{b\in\mathcal{O}_{B};\ i_{\ell}(b)\text{ is upper triangular mod }\ell^{v_{\ell}(N^{+}p^{m})},\ \forall\ell\mid N^{+}p^{m}\text{ prime}\}

be the
Eichler order
of
B
of level
N^{+}p^{m}
with respect to the chosen isomorphisms
i_{\ell}
for all finite places
\ell\nmid N^{-}
. Let
U_{m}\vcentcolon=\widehat{R}^{\times}_{m}=(R_{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
and let
\widetilde{U}_{m}
be the subgroup of
U_{m}
consisting of the elements whose
p
-component is congruent to a matrix of the form
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\mod{p^{m}}
. For the same Shimura datum
(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{G}},\mathcal{H}^{\pm})
and varying levels
U_{m}
and
\widetilde{U}_{m}
, we get two families of Shimura varieties respectively:

(2.5)

X_{m}(\mathbb{C})\vcentcolon=B^{\times}\backslash(\mathcal{H}^{\pm}\times\widehat{B}^{\times})/U_{m}\hskip 10.0pt\text{and}\hskip 10.0pt\widetilde{X}_{m}(\mathbb{C})\vcentcolon=B^{\times}\backslash(\mathcal{H}^{\pm}\times\widehat{B}^{\times})/\widetilde{U}_{m},

where
B^{\times}
acts by Möbius transformations on
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
under
i_{\infty}
and by left multiplication on
\widehat{B}^{\times}
, and
U_{m}
(resp.
\widetilde{U}_{m}
) acts trivially on
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
and by right multiplication on
\widehat{B}^{\times}
. The double coset represented by a point
(x,g)\in\mathcal{H}^{\pm}\times\widehat{B}^{\times}
is written
[(x,g)]
. The space
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}\cong\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\cdot\vartheta
can be identified with
\mathbb{P}\vcentcolon=\Hom_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C},B_{\infty})
, over which
B^{\times}
acts by conjugation, as follows: since
\iota_{K}^{B}\in\Hom_{\mathbb{Q}}(K,B)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
, a point
x=\gamma_{x}\cdot\vartheta
is sent to
\iota_{x}\vcentcolon=\gamma_{x}^{-1}\iota_{K}^{B}\gamma_{x}\in\mathbb{P}
.

For any
m\geq 0
, both double coset spaces
B^{\times}\backslash\widehat{B}^{\times}/U_{m}
and
B^{\times}\backslash\widehat{B}^{\times}/\widetilde{U}_{m}
are singletons, so the Strong Approximation Theorem implies

(2.6)

X_{m}(\mathbb{C})\cong\Gamma_{m}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{+}\hskip 10.0pt\text{and}\hskip 10.0pt\widetilde{X}_{m}(\mathbb{C})\cong\widetilde{\Gamma}_{m}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{+},

where
\Gamma_{m}
(resp.
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{m}
) is the subgroup of norm
1
elements in
B^{\times}\cap U_{m}
(resp.
B^{\times}\cap\widetilde{U}_{m}
). This means that both
X_{m}(\mathbb{C})
and
\widetilde{X}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
are Riemann surfaces, thus being algebraic curves defined over
\mathbb{C}
. Furthermore, there are algebraic curves
X_{m}
and
\widetilde{X}_{m}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
whose
\mathbb{C}
-rational points match
X_{m}(\mathbb{C})
and
\widetilde{X}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
respectively, tying the notation together.

If
\tau\in K\cap\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
is a CM point, then
\iota_{\tau}\in\Hom_{\mathbb{Q}}(K,B)
fixes
\tau
, so that
i_{\infty}(\iota_{\tau}(z))\binom{\tau}{1}=z\binom{\tau}{1}
for all
z\in K
. Explicitly, a CM class
[(\iota_{\tau},g)]
maps under strong approximation to
[b^{-1}\cdot\tau]
, where
g=bu
with
b\in B^{\times}
and
u\in U_{m}
(resp.
\widetilde{U}_{m}
).

2.6.

A
Heegner point
of conductor

in

[(\iota_{x},g)]\in X_{m}
where
g\in\widehat{B}^{\times}
and
\iota_{x}

\mathcal{O}_{cp^{n}}

\iota_{x}^{-1}(g^{-1}U_{m}g\cap B)=\mathcal{O}_{cp^{n}}

\varsigma_{m}\colon\widetilde{X}_{m}(\mathbb{C})\twoheadrightarrow X_{m}(\mathbb{C})

of conductor

in
\widetilde{X}_{m}

[(\iota_{x},g)]\in\widetilde{X}_{m}
such that
\varsigma_{m}([(\iota_{x},g)])

cp^{n}
in
X_{m}
and an additional local condition at

[
LV11
, §3]
for details).

We construct explicit Heegner points of conductor

in
\widetilde{X}_{m}
(for

[

CH18a

For each rational prime

•

\xi_{cp^{n}}^{(\ell)}\vcentcolon=1
if
\ell\nmid N^{+}cp

For primes dividing
N^{+}c

and set:

Finally, for

\xi_{cp^{n}}^{(p)}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\vartheta&-1\\

All those elements can be seen inside
\widehat{B}^{\times}

Define

for some

or

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
[\mathfrak{a}]\in\Pic(\mathcal{O}_{cp^{n}})
, the point
x_{cp^{n},m}([\mathfrak{a}])\in X_{m}(H_{cp^{n}})
is a Heegner point of conductor
cp^{n}
.

Proof.

We check for each prime
\ell
if, writing
\xi\vcentcolon=\xi_{cp^{n}}^{(\ell)}
and
\iota_{\xi}\vcentcolon=\xi^{-1}\iota_{K}^{B}\xi
, we have that

\iota_{\xi}(K_{\ell})\cap(U_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})=\iota_{\xi}(\mathcal{O}_{cp^{n}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}).

For
\ell\nmid N^{+}cp
, both orders
U_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{O}_{cp^{n}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
are maximal, thus the image of the latter is contained in the earlier. Next we consider
\ell\mid N^{+}cp
;

For a split prime
\ell=\mathfrak{l}\bar{\mathfrak{l}}\neq p
(case
\ell=p
is similar), where
\mathfrak{l}
is the prime corresponding to the fixed embedding
\iota_{\ell}\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
from the beginning of the chapter, we have that
K_{\ell}\vcentcolon=K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
splits as
K_{\mathfrak{l}}\oplus K_{\bar{\mathfrak{l}}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}e_{\mathfrak{l}}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}e_{\bar{\mathfrak{l}}}
, where

e_{\mathfrak{l}}=\frac{1\otimes\vartheta-\vartheta\otimes 1}{(\vartheta-\bar{\vartheta})\otimes 1}\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0pte_{\bar{\mathfrak{l}}}=\frac{\vartheta\otimes 1-1\otimes\bar{\vartheta}}{(\vartheta-\bar{\vartheta})\otimes 1}.

One then computes that for each
(a+b\vartheta)\otimes x\in K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
,

\iota_{\xi}((a+b\vartheta)\otimes x)=\begin{pmatrix}xa+xb\vartheta&\ell^{-s}xb\delta\\
0&xa+xb\bar{\vartheta}\end{pmatrix}=(xa+xb\vartheta)\iota_{\xi}(e_{\mathfrak{l}})+(xa+xb\bar{\vartheta})\iota_{\xi}(e_{\bar{\mathfrak{l}}}),

showing that
\iota_{\xi}(K_{\ell})\cap(U_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
consists of elements of the form

(xa+xb\vartheta)e_{\mathfrak{l}}+(xa+xb\bar{\vartheta})e_{\bar{\mathfrak{l}}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}e_{\mathfrak{l}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}e_{\bar{\mathfrak{l}}}

with
xb\in\ell^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
, thus of elements in
\mathcal{O}_{cp^{n}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
;

For a non-split prime
\ell
, one computes that for each
(a+b\vartheta)\otimes x\in K_{\ell}=K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
,

\iota_{\xi}((a+b\vartheta)\otimes x)=\begin{pmatrix}xa&\ell^{-s}xb\\
xb\vartheta\bar{\vartheta}\ell^{s}&x(a+b(\vartheta+\bar{\vartheta}))\end{pmatrix},

so elements
xa+xb\vartheta
in
\iota_{\xi}(K_{\ell})\cap(U_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
are of the form
xa\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and
xb\in\ell^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. This shows that, for all
s^{\prime}\leq 2s
,
\iota_{\xi}
is an optimal embedding into an Eichler order of level
\ell^{s^{\prime}}
, showing both the cases inert (
s^{\prime}=s
, when
\ell\mid c^{-}
or
\ell=p
inert) and ramified (
s^{\prime}=2s
, when
\ell=p
is ramified).∎

Direct verification of the local condition at

shows that
\widetilde{x}_{cp^{n},m}(a)
is also defined over
H_{cp^{n}}
.

2.7.
Idempotents

The proof of Proposition
2.2
motivates the choice of the following idempotents in
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K
:

e=\frac{1\otimes\vartheta-\vartheta\otimes 1}{(\vartheta-\bar{\vartheta})\otimes 1}\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0pt\bar{e}=\frac{\vartheta\otimes 1-1\otimes\bar{\vartheta}}{(\vartheta-\bar{\vartheta})\otimes 1}.

Via direct computation, one can show that those elements are orthogonal idempotents:

e^{2}=e,\hskip 5.0pt\bar{e}^{2}=\bar{e},\hskip 5.0pte\bar{e}=0\hskip 5.0pt\text{and}\hskip 5.0pte+\bar{e}=1.

Locally at all split primes
\ell\mid N^{+}p
(including
\ell=p
in case
p
splits), their local realizations at splitting places are the idempotent matrices
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
: if we again consider the splitting
\ell=\mathfrak{l}\bar{\mathfrak{l}}
, we have that
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K
embeds into
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{\mathfrak{l}}\cong K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
thus obtaining a map

j_{\ell}\colon K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}B_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

satisfying
j_{\ell}(e)=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)
and
j_{\ell}(\bar{e})=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)
.

If
p
is inert or ramified, the images of
e
and
\bar{e}
are not yet
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
– to achieve this we conjugate the image of the above map by
\mathcal{M}\vcentcolon=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\bar{\vartheta}&\vartheta\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
via an isomorphism
i_{\mathcal{M}}
, obtaining

j_{p}\colon K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}B_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{p}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{M}_{2}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{\mathcal{M}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{M}_{2}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})

again satisfying
j_{p}(e)=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)
and
j_{p}(\bar{e})=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)
; notice that the notation
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
correspond to different objects in each case. We also have a global map

j\colon K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}B\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I_{B}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\M_{2}(K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{\mathcal{M}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\M_{2}(K),

and again we find that
j(e)=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)
and
j(\bar{e})=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)
.

We recall from

2.8.1.
Naïve level structures

A

\mathcal{O}_{B}

\iota
on

\mathcal{O}_{B}

-group scheme
\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/{M}\mathbb{Z})

Denote by

on the set of naïve full level
M
structures on
(A,\iota)
. For any subgroup

on the set of naïve full level
M

is a
naïve level
U
structure

\alpha
on
A
. We write

(resp.

Let

2.10.
Symmetric tensors

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer,
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the group of permutations on
n

H
be a free abelian module of finite rank over a ring
R
of characteristic 0. The group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on the
n
-th tensor power

by permutation of the factors of each elementary tensor. The set of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant elements are the

:

\TSym^{n}(H)\vcentcolon=\{\eta\in H^{\otimes n};\ \sigma(\eta)=\eta\}.

Similarly to the symmetric algebra
\Sym^{\bullet}(H)
,
\TSym^{\bullet}(H)\vcentcolon=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\TSym^{n}(H)
is a graded algebra with the usual sum and with the symmetrization of the tensor product
\odot
. Identifying
H
with
\TSym^{1}(H)
,
H
can be embedded in
\TSym^{\bullet}(H)
, so the universal property of the symmetric algebra gives a graded algebra morphism
\Sym^{\bullet}(H)\to\TSym^{\bullet}(H)
, which specializes in degree
n
to

\Sym^{n}(H)\longrightarrow\TSym^{n}(H)\colon h_{1}^{\cdot e_{i}}\cdots h_{d}^{\cdot e_{d}}\longmapsto e_{1}!\cdots e_{d}!h_{1}^{\odot e_{i}}\odot\dots\odot h_{d}^{\odot e_{d}}.

If
n!
is invertible in
R
, the above map can be inverted and so becomes an isomorphism. There is another natural link between
\Sym
and
\TSym
: if
\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\,^{\vee}\vcentcolon=\Hom(\,\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\,,R)
denotes the linear dual of an
R
-module, we have a canonical isomorphism

(2.8)

\TSym^{n}(H^{\vee})\cong\left(\Sym^{n}(H)\right)^{\vee}.

If
{F}
is a locally free sheaf on a variety
X
over a field of characteristic 0,
\Sym^{\bullet}({F})
makes sense as a locally free sheaf on
X
, but that is not always the case with
\TSym
. However, since
H
is a free module,
\TSym^{\bullet}(H)
coincides with
\Gamma^{\bullet}(H)
, the divided power algebra of
H
(see
[
Lun08
]
for more details on the relation between these two objects) which does sheafify well: as pointed out in
[
KLZ17
, §2.2]
, if
{F}
is a locally free sheaf on a variety
X
over a field of characteristic 0,
\Gamma^{n}({F})
and therefore
\TSym^{n}({F})
define a locally free sheaf on
X
and, in particular, one can talk about symmetric tensors of coefficient sheaves of étale cohomology. See also
[
Kin15
, §12.2.2]
.

Let
M=(V,p,m)
be a relative Chow motive as in the previous subsection. The group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
M^{\otimes n}=(V^{n},p,m)
, where
V^{n}
denotes the
n
-th fiber product power of
V
over
X
. The image of
M^{\otimes n}
under the projector
\pi_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sigma
is the
n
-th symmetric power of
M
, denoted
\Sym^{n}(M)=(V^{n},\pi_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\circ p,m)
. We then define
\TSym^{n}(M)\vcentcolon=\left(\Sym^{n}(M^{\vee})\right)^{\vee}
.

2.11.
Hecke Characters

An

over a Galois extension

is a 1-dimensional
F
-representation
\chi\in\Hom_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times})
, which is said to have

(i_{1},i_{2})
if, for each

decomposed in its finite

and infinity

We decompose
\chi
into a finite and a infinite part given respectively by:

\chi_{\mathrm{fin}}(x)\vcentcolon=\chi(x_{\mathrm{fin}})\hskip 10.0pt\text{and}\hskip 10.0pt\chi_{\infty}(x)\vcentcolon=x_{\infty}^{i_{1}}\bar{x}_{\infty}^{i_{2}}.

Through the Artin reciprocity map,
\chi
can be seen as a
Galois character
over
F
, that is, a character of
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
. All characters we consider are algebraic, so we drop the adjective for the sake of brevity.

Now let
F=K
and
\widetilde{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
coprime with
N
(but not necessarily with
p
). We say that
\chi
has
finite type
(\widetilde{c},\mathfrak{N}^{+},\psi)
if the conductor
\mathfrak{c}
of
\chi
is divisible by
\widetilde{c}
and the restriction of
\chi
to
(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{c}}/\mathfrak{N}^{+}\cap\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{c}})^{\times}
(by restricting to
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{c}}^{\times}
and projecting to the
\mathfrak{N}^{+}
-th component) is
\psi^{-1}
, a finite order character.

4.1.

A

-adic modular form as above is a rule

that, for each

of

over

-algebras.

An element

Definition 4.1
.

Let

As explained in

Definition 4.2
.

A

is a rule

that assigns to each isomorphism class of test objects

which

•

is compatible with base changes: if

, and
\iota^{\prime}

•

We denote

.

An element

.

Let

4.3.
From geometric to classic
p
-adic modular forms

In order to define a
p
-adic modular form from a geometric modular form, one needs to establish a relation between the test objects above with the quadruplets from before, which boils down to defining a level
\Gamma_{1}(p^{m})
structure
(H,P)
after an arithmetic trivialization
\beta
for each

whose correspondent Rosati involution of

coincides with the restriction to

of the involution

As explained in
[

determines by Cartier duality a point

in

\displaystyle\implies e^{\dagger}{\Ta_{p}(A^{\vee})({\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})}\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(e\widehat{A},\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m}).

Under the above isomorphism

corresponds to some

Fix a generator of

, which induces a generator

gives a point

to be the subgroup scheme generated by

gives a level

. Define

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(A,\iota,\alpha,\beta)\vcentcolon=\mathcal{F}(A,\iota,\alpha,(H,P),\omega_{\beta}).

The map

is compatible with base change, only depends on the isomorphism class of

and it is compatible with the maps occurring in the direct and inverse limit in the definition of

-adic modular forms; thus

establishes a map

(4.1)

.

We follow

\Lambda_{\Gamma_{1}}(\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}/\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}})\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{-j}\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{j}\colon\mu\mapsto\left(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}\ \mathrm{d}\mu\right)(\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}/\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}})^{j}

Define
\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}-\mathbf{j}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mathbf{j}}\vcentcolon=\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Lambda_{\Gamma_{1}}(\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}/\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}})
. Map (
5.8
) induces correspondent specialization at
j
maps for
\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}-\mathbf{j}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mathbf{j}}
and therefore a map

(5.9)

\mathrm{mom}_{j,n}\colon H^{1}\left(F_{cp},\mathbf{V}_{{F}}^{\ast}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\kappa_{{{U}}}-\mathbf{j}}\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\mathbf{j}}\right)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{cp},\mathbf{V}_{{F}}^{\ast}\otimes\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\kappa_{{{U}}}-j}\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{j}).

which can be composed with
\mathrm{norm}_{F_{cp^{n}}/F_{cp}}
so to land in the same field of definition
F_{cp}
.

Proposition 5.19
.

There exists an element

\mathbf{z}_{cp}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]}\in H^{1}\left(F_{cp},\mathbf{V}_{{F}}^{\ast}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\kappa_{{{U}}}-\mathbf{j}}\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\mathbf{j}}\right)

such that, for all
n\geq 1
and all
j\geq 0
,
\mathrm{mom}_{j,n}(\mathbf{z}_{cp}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]})=\mathbf{a}_{p}^{-n}\mathbf{z}^{[{F},j]}_{cp^{n},1}
.

Proof.

The existence of the class will follow from a general construction of Loeffler–Zerbes, namely
[
LZ16
, Proposition 2.3.3]
. To check the conditions for it, endow
\mathcal{O}({U})
with the supremum norm and take
\lambda
to be the slope of
{F}
, that is
\log_{p}(\|\mathbf{a}_{p}^{-1}\|)
, a positive real number which by hypothesis is strictly smaller than
k-1
, thus
2r\geq\lfloor\lambda\rfloor
. Define the classes

c_{n,j}\vcentcolon=\mathbf{a}_{p}^{-n}\binom{\nabla}{2r}\mathbf{z}^{[{F},j]}_{cp^{n},1}\in H^{1}(F_{cp^{\infty}},\mathbf{V}_{F}^{\ast}{\otimes}\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}}),

considered over
F_{cp^{\infty}}
where the characters
\sigma_{\et}
and
\bar{\sigma}_{\et}
are trivial. For the first condition in
loc. cit.
, it suffices to check that

\mathrm{norm}_{F_{cp^{n+1}}/F_{cp^{n}}}(c_{n+1,j})=c_{n,j},

which is immediate from Proposition
5.17
.

For the second condition, we wish to show that there exists a constant
C
such that

\left\|p^{-2rn}\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}\Res_{F_{cp^{\infty}}/F_{cp^{n}}}(c_{n,j})\right\|\leq Cp^{\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor},

where
\|\,\cdot\,\|
is the supremum seminorm on
H^{1}(F_{cp^{\infty}},\mathbf{V}_{F}^{\ast}{\otimes}\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}})
coming from a choice of norm on
\mathbf{V}_{F}^{\ast}{\otimes}\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}}
as a Banach
\mathcal{O}({U})
-module. By Lemma
3.7
,
e_{cp^{n}}-\bar{e}_{cp^{n}}\in p^{n}(\mathcal{O}_{L}^{2})^{\vee}
, therefore

(e_{cp^{n}}-\bar{e}_{cp^{n}})^{\odot 2r}=\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}(e_{cp^{n}}^{2r-j}\odot\bar{e}_{cp^{n}}^{j})\in p^{2rn}\TSym^{2r}((\mathcal{O}_{L}^{2})^{\vee}).

Taking the product of that sum with
\mathbf{e}_{cp^{n}}^{[{U}-2r,0]}
and applying the overconvergent projector
\Pi_{j}
gives

(5.10)

\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\Pi_{j}(\mathbf{e}_{cp^{n}}^{[{U}-2r,0]}\odot(e_{cp^{n}}^{2r-j}\odot\bar{e}_{cp^{n}}^{j}))=\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{\nabla-j}{2r-j}\mathbf{e}_{cp^{n}}^{[{U},j]},

where the equality (without the alternating sum) is
[
LZ16
, Lemma 5.1.5]
. Since
\Pi_{j}
has denominators bounded in terms of
j
and
m=1
, there is a constant
C_{j}
such that

\Pi_{j}(\mathbf{e}_{cp^{n}}^{[{U}-2r,0]}\odot(e_{cp^{n}}^{2r-j}\odot\bar{e}_{cp^{n}}^{j}))\in C_{j}p^{2rn}D_{{U},1}^{\circ},

where
D_{{U},1}^{\circ}
denotes the norm-1 distributions in
D_{{U},1}
. Applying the big Gysin map (
5.5
), the dual map
D_{{U},1}^{\circ}\to D_{\widetilde{{U}},1}^{\circ}
induced by the inclusion
{U}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{{U}}
, and
\mathrm{pr}^{[{F},j]}
to the right-hand side of (
5.10
) and using the explicit calculation

\binom{\nabla}{j}\binom{\nabla-j}{2r-j}=\dfrac{\nabla\cdot\ldots\cdot(\nabla-j+1)}{j!}\cdot\dfrac{(\nabla-j)\cdot\ldots\cdot(\nabla-2r+1)}{(2r-j)!}\cdot\dfrac{(2r)!}{(2r)!}=\binom{2r}{j}\binom{\nabla}{2r},

we have that

\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}\binom{\nabla}{2r}\mathbf{z}^{[{F},j]}_{cp^{n},1}\right\|\leq C_{j}p^{2rn}.

To conclude the calculation,

\displaystyle\left\|p^{-2rn}\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}\Res_{F_{cp^{\infty}}/F_{cp^{n}}}(c_{m,j})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq p^{-2rn}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}\binom{\nabla}{2r}\mathbf{z}^{[{F},j]}_{cp^{n},1}\mathbf{a}_{p}^{-n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}\|\mathbf{a}_{p}^{-n}\|=C_{j}p^{\lambda n}

which implies the inequality we want. Thus we may apply
[
LZ16
, Proposition 2.3.3]
to obtain a unique class
\mathbf{z}_{cp}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]}\in H^{1}(F_{cp^{\infty}},D_{{W}_{\Gamma_{1}},\lambda}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\mathbf{V}_{F}^{\ast}{\otimes}\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}}))
interpolating the classes
\Res_{F_{cp^{\infty}}/F_{cp^{n}}}(c_{n,j})
, where
{W}_{\Gamma_{1}}
denotes the weight space associated to
\Lambda_{\Gamma_{1}}
. Since all quaternionic modular forms are cuspidal (as the underlying Shimura varieties are compact) we have that
H^{0}(F_{cp^{\infty}},\mathbf{V}_{F}^{\ast}{\otimes}\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}})=0
, which, via the inflation-restriction sequence, allows the above class to be defined over
F_{cp}
, which we can map to the larger submodule

\mathbf{z}_{cp}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]}\in H^{1}\left(F_{cp},\mathbf{V}_{{F}}^{\ast}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\kappa_{{{U}}}-\mathbf{j}}\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\mathbf{j}}\right).

It then follows by the interpolation property in the definition of the class that

\mathrm{mom}_{j,n}(\mathbf{z}_{cp}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]})=\mathbf{a}_{p}^{-n}\binom{\mathbf{k}-2}{2r}\mathbf{z}^{[{F},j]}_{cp^{n},1},

where
\mathbf{k}
is the weight of the Coleman family, which is constant and equal to
k=2r+2
, thus making the binomial term vanish, concluding the proof.
∎

\mathbf{z}_{cp}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]}\in H^{1}\left(F_{cp},\mathbf{V}_{{F}}^{\ast}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\kappa_{{{U}}}-\mathbf{j}}\bar{\sigma}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\mathbf{j}}\right)

5.7.
Big generalized Heegner classes defined over
K

To further lower the field of definition of the big generalized Heegner classes, it is necessary to remove dependence on
\kappa_{{{U}}}
, which is not well-defined over
H_{cp}
. The workaround is to reparametrize the underlying affinoid of the space of distributions
\mathbf{V}_{F}^{*}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma^{\kappa_{{{U}}}-\mathbf{j}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mathbf{j}}
by an appropriate affinoid over a suitable, bigger weight space. The
\mathbf{V}_{F}^{*}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma^{\kappa_{U}}
part is parametrized by the affinoid
{U}\subseteq{W}
, while the
\sigma^{-\mathbf{j}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mathbf{j}}
part is a
\Lambda_{\Gamma_{1}}
-module, thus corresponding to an affinoid
{U}_{1}\subseteq{W}_{\Gamma_{1}}
, which, for simplicity, we may take to be the whole weight space. The following is a summary of
[
JLZ21
, §5.4]
:

Proposition 5.24
.

Let
\widetilde{{W}}\to{W}\times{W}_{\Gamma_{1}}
be the weight space parametrizing the continuous characters
K^{\times}\backslash\mathds{A}_{K,\mathrm{fin}}^{\times}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
which restrict trivially to
(\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{K}})_{(p)}^{\times}
. Then the preimage of
{U}\times{W}_{\Gamma_{1}}
in
\widetilde{{W}}
is an affinoid
{Y}\subseteq\widetilde{{W}}
isomorphic to
{U}\times{W}_{\Gamma_{1}}
and whose universal character
\kappa_{{Y}}
is well defined as a character
\Gal(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)\to\mathcal{O}({Y})^{\times}
, and therefore is defined over
K
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{F}\vcentcolon=\mathbf{V}_{{F}}^{*}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}({U})}\mathcal{O}({Y})(\kappa_{{Y}})
and consider the image of the big generalized Heegner class
\mathbf{z}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]}_{cp}
in
H^{1}(F_{cp},\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{F})
, denoted by the same symbol. By an argument similar to Lemma
5.3
,
\mathbf{z}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]}_{cp}
is found to be invariant respect to
\Gal(F_{cp}/H_{cp})
, so it can be seen as class in
H^{1}(H_{cp},\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{F})
. Finally, define

\mathbf{z}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]}\vcentcolon=\mathrm{cores}_{H_{cp}/K}(\mathbf{z}^{[{F},\mathbf{j}]}_{cp})\in H^{1}(K,\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{F}),

which, by abuse of nomenclature, we also call the
big generalized Heegner class
associated to
{F}
, enjoying the same specializations as the original, only being defined over
K
.

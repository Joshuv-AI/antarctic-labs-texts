Abstract
This paper analyzes the impact of spatially correlated additive noise on the minimum mean-square error (MMSE) estimation of multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) channels from one-bit quantized observations. Although additive noise can be correlated in practical scenarios, e.g., due to jamming, clutter, or other external disturbances, the effect of such correlation on the MMSE channel estimator in this setting remains unexplored in prior work. Against this backdrop, we derive a novel analytical expression for the general MIMO MMSE channel estimator, which is inherently nonlinear in one-bit observations, and accommodates arbitrary channel and noise correlation structures. To further characterize the impact of noise correlation, we subsequently specialize the general MMSE expression to certain tractable multi-antenna configurations in which both the channel and the noise assume single-parameter constant correlation structures. Our analyses reveal nontrivial, noise - correlation - induced scenarios in which the estimator remains linear despite non - zero channel and noise correlation parameters. Moreover, the results indicate that, at low-to-medium signal-to-noise ratio, noise correlation improves the MMSE performance when channels are uncorrelated, but degrades performance when channels are strongly correlated.

II-A

System Model

Consider a Rayleigh block-fading
[

]
MIMO channel
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}

is sent column-wise over

\tau
time slots, the received signal vectors form a matrix
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\tau}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{N},

(1)

where the noise matrix
\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\tau}
collects the
n
-dimensional Gaussian noise vectors over
\tau

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}=\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{1b}}\left(\mathbf{Y}\right),

(2)

where

Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=(\mathbf{S}^{T}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n})\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{n},

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}=\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{1b}}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)\in\{\pm 1\pm j\}^{\tau n}.

(4)

We assume that

are independent. The corresponding covariance matrices
\boldsymbol{\Xi}\in\mathbb{C}^{\tau n\times\tau n}
and
\boldsymbol{\Phi}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}
are assumed to be known and positive definite. Therefore, we have
\mathbf{y}\sim\mathcal{CN}_{\tau n}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{B})
with

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{S}^{T}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n})\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\mathbf{S}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n})+\boldsymbol{\Xi}.

(5)

Note that the general covariance models here subsume the widely used Kronecker models
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
as special cases.

II-B

Problem Statement

Given the one-bit quantized observation vector
\mathbf{r}
as well as the system parameters
\mathbf{S}
,
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
, and
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
, we aim at obtaining an explicit analytical expression for the MMSE channel estimator in the above setting. To this end, following

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})=\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbf{h}|\mathbf{r}\right\}=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{mn}}\mathbf{h}f(\mathbf{h}|\mathbf{r})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h},

(6)

with the corresponding MMSE

given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{MMSE}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{mn}\mathbb{E}\left\{\|\mathbf{h}-\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})\|^{2}\right\}.

(7)

Here,

denotes the conditional mean and

Although the estimator in (
6
) was analyzed in
[
9
]

), the techniques employed therein do not extend to scenarios with correlated noise (c.f.

Subsequently, we apply these general results to SIMO systems with specific channel and noise correlation structures, and quantify the impact of noise correlation on the corresponding MMSE estimators.

III
The General MIMO MMSE Channel Estimator with Noise Correlation

In this section, we present an expression for the general MIMO MMSE channel estimator with noise correlation. It turns out that this general MMSE expression is closely related to the orthant probability of multivariate normal distributions
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
. Therefore, following
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
, we define the orthant probability associated with
\boldsymbol{w}\sim\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\Omega}})
as

\mathcal{P}\!\left[\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\right]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{p}{2}}\!\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{p}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{w},

where
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
is positive definite and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{p}
denotes the non-negative orthant. The following theorem gives the exact general MMSE channel estimator. The proof is omitted due to space limits.

Theorem 1
.

The MIMO MMSE channel estimator in the presence of noise correlation is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\mathbf{S}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n})(\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathbf{B}\right))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{B}_{\rm c})}{\mathcal{P}\!\left[\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{B}_{\rm c}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{r}}\right]},

where, for
k=1,\ldots,\tau n
, the
k
-th element of the vector
\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{B}_{\rm c})\in\mathbb{C}^{\tau n}
is given by

\displaystyle g_{k}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{B}_{\rm c})

\displaystyle=\real{r_{k}}\mathcal{P}\!\left[\mathbf{E}_{k}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{B}_{\rm c}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{E}_{k}^{T}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+j\Im\left\{r_{k}\right\}\mathcal{P}\!\left[\mathbf{E}_{k+\tau n}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{B}_{\rm c}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{E}_{k+\tau n}^{T}\right].

(8)

Furthermore, for
i=1,\ldots,2\tau n
,
\mathbf{E}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{(2\tau n-1)\times(2\tau n)}
denotes the matrix obtained by removing the
i
-th row of
\mathbf{I}_{2\tau n}
,
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{r}}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\real{\mathbf{r}}\\
\Im\left\{\mathbf{r}\right\}\end{bmatrix}\right)

is a diagonal matrix containing all the observations (i.e.,
\real{\mathbf{r}},\Im\left\{\mathbf{r}\right\}\in\{\pm 1\}^{\tau n}
), and
\mathbf{B}_{\rm c}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\rm c}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}\real{\mathbf{B}}&-\Im\left\{\mathbf{B}\right\}\\
\Im\left\{\mathbf{B}\right\}&\phantom{+}\real{\mathbf{B}}\end{bmatrix}.

(9)

The general MMSE estimator in Theorem
1
accommodates arbitrary correlation structures beyond the commonly used Kronecker model
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. This further highlights the utility of our expression in various applications involving non-trivial correlation structures. Moreover, Theorem
1
shows that, in general, the MMSE estimator is a nonlinear function of the one-bit quantized observation vector
\mathbf{r}
.
This nonlinearity persists except in special cases, as detailed in Corollary
1
below.
It is also worth noting that, for
\boldsymbol{\Xi}=\mathbf{I}_{\tau n}
(i.e., white noise), the above MMSE expression degenerates into
[
9
, (25)–(26)]
.

Corollary 1
.

Under the necessary and sufficient condition that each row (resp. each column) of
\mathbf{B}_{\rm c}
in (
9
) contains at most two nonzero elements, the MMSE estimator in Theorem
1
admits the linear form

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle\!=\!\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{4}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\mathbf{S}^{*}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{n})(\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathbf{B}\right))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{B}_{\rm sa}^{-1}\mathbf{r},

(10)

where

\mathbf{B}_{\rm sa}=\arcsin\big((\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathbf{B}\right))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathbf{B}\right))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)

and the
\arcsin
function is applied element-wise to the real and imaginary parts separately.

Proof:

The proof follows similar arguments as for
[
9
, Theorem 1]
and is thus omitted.
∎

Note that, with noise correlation incorporated, the linear estimator in the form given by (
10
) generalizes the well-known Bussgang linear MMSE (BLMMSE) channel estimator proposed in
[
5
]
assuming AWGN. Corollary
1
specifies the precise condition under which the generalized MMSE channel estimator with noise correlation reduces to the linear form.

Despite the broad utility of the general MMSE estimator derived above, the exact evaluation is limited by the numerical complexity of computing the high-dimensional orthant probability for a generic covariance matrix
[
34
,
35
,
37
]
. This is further highlighted by the fact that closed-form orthant probabilities associated with

were reported in the literature only for very special cases, e.g., for

with arbitrary positive-definite
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
or for any diagonal positive-definite
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}

34
,
35
,
36
]
. Therefore, in the sequel, we focus on tractable SIMO configurations to gain insights into the impact of noise correlation.

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Phi})-\mathbb{E}\left\{\|\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})\|_{2}^{2}\right\}\right)/n

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=\arcsin\left(\frac{\real{\beta_{\rm c}}}{\sqrt{1-(\Im\left\{\beta_{\rm c}\right\})^{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}

\displaystyle=\arcsin\left(\frac{\Im\left\{\beta_{\rm c}\right\}}{\sqrt{1-(\real{\beta_{\rm c}})^{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle\kappa_{3}

\displaystyle=\arcsin(\sin\kappa_{1}\sin\kappa_{2}),

\displaystyle\kappa_{4}

\displaystyle=\arcsin(\real{\beta_{\rm c}}),\quad\kappa_{5}=\arcsin(\Im\left\{\beta_{\rm c}\right\}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{|\kappa_{4}|}\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\beta_{\rm c}|^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-(\Im\left\{\beta_{\rm c}\right\})^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta-(\Im\left\{\beta_{\rm c}\right\})^{2}}}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta

\kappa_{1}=\arcsin\left(\frac{\real{\gamma_{2}\phi+\xi}}{1+\gamma_{2}}\right)
. The corresponding MMSE is given by

\mathrm{MMSE}_{2}(\gamma_{2},\phi,\xi)=1-\frac{2\pi\gamma_{2}\left(1+|\phi|^{2}-\frac{4\kappa_{1}}{\pi}\real{\phi}\right)}{(1+\gamma_{2})\left(\pi^{2}-4\kappa_{1}^{2}\right)}.

Clearly, the extension of linearity beyond the trivial uncorrelated case
\phi=\xi=0
is made possible by the additional degree of freedom provided by the noise correlation. Furthermore, it can be verified that, in all cases of linear
\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}
, the condition specified in Corollary
1
is satisfied.

The asymptotic SNR behavior of the estimator in (
21
) is also governed by the limit of the parameter
\beta_{\rm c}
. To be specific, noting that, as
\gamma\to\infty
, we get
\beta_{\rm c}=\phi+o(1)
, it can be concluded that in the high-SNR regime the noise correlation
\xi
does not affect the MMSE estimator. Consequently, the corresponding asymptotic MMSE depends only on
\phi
. For
\phi=0
, we have
\beta_{\rm c}\to 0
as
\gamma\to\infty
, which leads to

{\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\text{MMSE}_{2}}(\gamma,0,\xi)=1-\frac{2}{\pi},

independently of
\xi
. This confirms that the asymptotic limit
1-\frac{2}{\pi}
, previously known only for
\xi=0
, also holds under the more general condition of correlated noise (
\xi\neq 0
).

IV-B

Arbitrary
n
with Real Correlation Structures

We now analyze the SIMO system described by (
11
)–(
12
) for arbitrary
n
under the real-valued channel and noise correlation structures defined in (
19
).

Proposition 2
.

The MMSE channel estimator for the SIMO channel with real correlation structures as in (
19
) is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=s^{*}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{4\pi(1+\gamma)}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r}),

(28)

where, for
k=1,\ldots,n
, the real and imaginary parts of the
k
-th entry of the vector
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
are respectively given by

\real{v_{k}(\mathbf{r})}\!=\!\real{r_{k}}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{Z}\left\{\!\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}\!\!Q\left(\real{r_{i}}\!\sqrt{\beta}\;Z\right)\!\!\right\}}{\mathbb{E}_{Z}\left\{\!\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\!\!Q\left(\real{r_{i}}\!\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}}\;Z\right)\!\!\right\}}

and

\Im\left\{\!v_{k}(\mathbf{r})\right\}\!=\!\Im\left\{r_{k}\right\}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{Z}\left\{\!\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}\!\!Q\left(\Im\left\{r_{i}\right\}\!\sqrt{\beta}\;Z\right)\!\!\right\}}{\mathbb{E}_{Z}\left\{\!\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\!\!Q\left(\!\Im\left\{r_{i}\right\}\!\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}}\;Z\right)\!\!\right\}},

with

Z\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and
\beta=\frac{\gamma\phi+\xi}{1+\gamma}\geq 0
.

Proposition
2
yields an expression for
\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})

that can be evaluated efficiently via numerical integration, requiring only a single integration variable
per term (see, e.g.,
[
37
]
). Similar to
\beta_{\rm c}
in (
25
), here
\beta
plays a critical role in characterizing the influence of
\xi
.
A notable special case occurs for
\beta=0
and
\phi\neq 0
, which corresponds to
\phi
and
\xi
having opposite signs. By selecting
\widetilde{\gamma}=-\xi/\phi>0
, the estimator reduces to the simple linear form

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=s^{*}\sqrt{\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}}{\pi(1+\widetilde{\gamma})}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}\mathbf{r},

which is consistent with Corollary
1
.
The corresponding MMSE is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{MMSE}_{n}\!\left(\widetilde{\gamma},\phi,\xi\right)\!=\!1\!-\!\frac{2\widetilde{\gamma}(1\!+\!(n\!-\!1)\phi^{2})}{\pi(1+\widetilde{\gamma})},

(29)

with
\phi,\xi\in\big(\!-\frac{1}{n-1},0\big)\cup(0,1)
. The observed expansion of the linear region is induced by noise correlation. However, as
n\to\infty
, this extended linear region vanishes, since the interval
\big(-\frac{1}{n-1},0\big)\cup(0,1)
converges to
(0,1)
.
Moreover, the condition
\beta=0
is trivially
satisfied for
\phi=\xi=0
. Consequently, the estimator reduces to
\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})=s^{*}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{\pi(1+\gamma)}}\,\mathbf{r}
, with the corresponding MMSE given by
\mathrm{MMSE}_{n}(\gamma,0,0)=1-\frac{2\gamma}{\pi(1+\gamma)}
, in agreement with
[
11
,
9
]
. In contrast, for
n=2
, the condition of Corollary
1
is
satisfied for all
\gamma>0
,
\xi,\phi\in(-1,1)
, and thus the estimator in
(
28
) simplifies to the linear form

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathrm{mmse}}(\mathbf{r})=s^{*}\!\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{\pi(1+\gamma)}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}\!\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{2\arcsin\beta}{\pi}\\
\frac{2\arcsin\beta}{\pi}&1\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\!\mathbf{r},

(30)

which reduces to the AWGN result in
[
9
, (63)]
for
\xi=0
.

Asymptotically, following the limit
\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\beta=\phi
, we conclude that the noise correlation
\xi
has a negligible impact on the MMSE estimator. Consequently, the asymptotic MMSE depends only on
\phi
. In the special case of
\phi=0
, we obtain

{\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\mathrm{MMSE}_{n}(\gamma,0,\xi)}=1-\frac{2}{\pi}
, regardless of the values of
\xi
and
n
. This further generalizes the asymptotic limit
1-\frac{2}{\pi}
previously known only for the AWGN case (
\xi=0
) to the case of correlated noise (
\xi\neq 0
). Similar observations have been made for
n=2
with complex-valued
\phi
and
\xi
in Section
IV-A
.

This section presents numerical simulations to validate and extend the above analytical findings.
We begin with a SIMO system using two receive antennas (i.e.,

plots the MMSE versus the transmit SNR
\gamma
for parameter values

20
)–(

).
As can be seen from the graph, for uncorrelated channels (i.e.,
\phi=0
), increasing the noise correlation reduces the MMSE at low-to-medium SNR, confirming its beneficial effect in this regime. Conversely, under strong channel correlation (e.g.,
|\phi|=0.9
), the MMSE increases with noise correlation. Moreover, as
\gamma
increases, the asymptotic MMSE becomes increasingly sensitive only to
\phi
, confirming that the noise correlation
\xi
does not affect the asymptotic performance. These results highlight a non-uniform, context-dependent impact, where the influence of noise correlation is dictated by the underlying channel correlation.

Given the complex-valued nature of the correlations, we now examine the dependence of MMSE on the phase components

\arg(\xi)\in(-\pi,\pi]
and
\arg(\phi)\in(-\pi,\pi]
corresponding to the parameters

,
|\xi|=0.8
, and
\gamma=5
dB. This dependence, shown in Fig.

, reveals that specific phase combinations yield lower MMSE than others, indicating optimal operating regions.

To examine scalability, we analyze a relatively larger SIMO configuration with
n=16
receive antennas under the real correlation framework of (
19
). Figure

illustrates the MMSE as a function of
\gamma
for parameter values
\xi\in\{0,0.5,0.9\}
and
\phi\in\{0,0.9\}
. Reaffirming the trend from Fig.

, noise correlation is beneficial (i.e., reducing the MMSE) in the absence of channel correlation (
\phi=0
), but becomes detrimental when channels are highly correlated (

).

Finally, Fig.

displays the enhanced linear estimation region enabled by noise correlation
\xi
as well as the corresponding MMSE performance, evaluated via (

) with

and
\phi\neq 0

reveals that this particular linear MMSE estimator

VI
Conclusions

This paper develops a unified framework for MMSE channel estimation in one-bit MIMO systems with noise correlation. It provides a general estimator that generalizes prior work, establishes linearity conditions, and gives efficient SIMO forms. The analyses reveal that noise correlation can extend the estimator’s linear region when channels are also correlated,
while simulations show it improves performance for uncorrelated channels at low-to-medium SNR but degrades it for strongly correlated channels. These new insights offer important design guidance for communication systems employing one-bit ADCs.

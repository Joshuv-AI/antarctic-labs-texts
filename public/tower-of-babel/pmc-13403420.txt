The association between sarcopenia and carotid artery plaque: a mediation analysis of inflammatory biomarkers
Authors: Hongze Lin, Shuling Liu, Jushun Zhang, Wenying Zhong, Mei Tu, Wen Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCarotid artery plaque (CAP) and sarcopenia are notable complications in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). This study aims to explore whether the inflammatory biomarkers neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI) mediate the association between sarcopenia and CAP in such patients.MethodsSarcopenia was diagnosed using the criteria of the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS 2019), and CAP was assessed by carotid ultrasonography. Inflammatory biomarkers including NLR and SIRI were calculated, with their natural logarithm-transformed values (Ln-NLR, Ln-SIRI) derived. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis, univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to investigate the associations among these inflammatory biomarkers, sarcopenia and CAP. Additionally, mediation analysis was performed to explore the mediating role of these inflammatory biomarkers.ResultsA total of 749 patients with T2DM were enrolled in this study. The prevalence of CAP and sarcopenia in the study was 58.9% (n=441) and 33.9% (n=254), respectively. Patients with CAP had significantly higher levels of NLR, SIRI, and a higher prevalence of sarcopenia than those without CAP (all p < 0.05). RCS analysis revealed linear correlations of NLR and SIRI with both sarcopenia and CAP (all p for nonlinear > 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that sarcopenia (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) = 1.67, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.10-2.54, p = 0.016), NLR (aOR = 1.63, 95% CI: 1.01-2.66, p = 0.047), SIRI (aOR = 1.23, 95% CI: 1.01-1.59, p = 0.046), Ln-NLR (aOR = 1.77, 95% CI: 1.10-2.80, p = 0.037), and Ln-SIRI (aOR = 1.38, 95% CI: 1.02-1.74, p = 0.035) were independently and positively associated with CAP. Mediation analysis further indicated that NLR and SIRI partially mediated the association between sarcopenia and CAP, with mediated proportions of 10.39% and 11.40%, respectively. Similarly, Ln-NLR and Ln-SIRI also exerted partial mediating effects, with mediated proportions of 9.73% and 9.29%.ConclusionsSarcopenia is an independently positively associated factor for CAP in patients with T2DM. Introduction

Carotid artery plaque (CAP) is a significant complication in type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). CAP is often overlooked by patients because of its frequent lack of conspicuous early symptoms [1]. However, it is an important risk factor for cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. Epidemiological research data indicate that approximately 40% of ischemic strokes and transient ischemic attacks are caused by CAP [2]. In addition, clinical studies have found that patients with CAP have a higher risk of all-cause mortality compared to those without CAP [3]. This higher risk has a negative impact on patients’ overall quality of life and lifespan [4]. Recent studies have shown that sarcopenia has become one of the chronic complications of diabetes mellitus and is also an important risk factor for CAP [5–7].

Previous studies showed that in patients with T2DM, sarcopenia was positively associated with CAP, and the risk of CAP increased with the decline in skeletal muscle mass [5, 6]. A prior study also indicated that in patients with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), sarcopenia was significantly positively correlated with the progression of CAP [7]. The underlying mechanisms behind these observations can be partly explained by inflammation. Numerous studies have found that a positive correlation between elevated inflammation levels and the risk of sarcopenia in adults [8–10]. Studies have shown that inflammatory factors produced during the body’s aging process mainly include tumor necrosis factor -α (TNF-α), interleukin − 6 (IL-6), interleukin − 1 (IL-1), and chemokines [8–10]. These inflammatory factors can activate muscle catabolic pathways, inhibit protein synthesis, and interfere with the transmission of anabolic signals such as insulin-like growth factor − 1 (IGF-1), leading to muscle atrophy [11].

Meanwhile, an increasing number of studies have confirmed that inflammation is an important pathogenic mechanism of CAP [12–14]. As an inflammatory biomarker, an increase in high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) levels was associated with an increased risk of CAP [14]. In some previous studies based on patients with CAP, it has been revealed that the elevation of inflammatory biomarkers, such as the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), increases the risk of CAP [15, 16]. During chronic inflammation, proinflammatory cytokines induce endothelial dysfunction, enhance vascular permeability, and promote the infiltration of low-density lipoprotein into the intima. Meanwhile, inflammation activates macrophages to phagocytose lipids, forming foam cells that accumulate into a lipid core. Concurrently, it stimulates the proliferation and migration of carotid vascular smooth muscle cells, promoting fibrous cap formation and subsequent development of CAP [17–19].

The above findings seem to highlight the bridging role of inflammation in the relationship between sarcopenia and CAP. The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and the systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI), two popular inflammatory biomarkers in recent years, have been proven clinically relevant in various diseases, such as type 2 diabetes mellitus, acute ischemic stroke, etc [8, 12, 13, 20, 21]. Notably, elevated NLR and SIRI levels were associated with an increased prevalence of CAP [12]. Several studies have demonstrated that the NLR is particularly valuable in the prognostic assessment of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, metabolic disorders, and the recovery period after surgery [13, 20]. NLR is a strong predictor of carotid artery plaques in elderly patients with atherosclerosis [21]. Additionally, SIRI had a significant association with an increased risk of death in patients with sarcopenia [8]. In patients with metastatic gastric cancer, SIRI was identified as an independent risk factor for sarcopenia [16]. However, evidence regarding the role of these two inflammatory biomarkers in mediating the relationship between sarcopenia and CAP in patients with T2DM is still insufficient.

Therefore, this study aims to explore whether blood-cell-based inflammatory biomarkers NLR and SIRI may partially mediate the association between sarcopenia and CAP in patients with T2DM.

Methods

Study population

The current investigation was a cross-sectional study. The study subjects included adult inpatients (aged ≥ 18 years) who were admitted to Longyan First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University, Fujian Province, China between December 2023 and October 2024. These patients were diagnosed with T2DM according to the criteria outlined by the American Diabetes Association (ADA). The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients with other types of diabetes; (2) Pregnancy or lactation; (3) Patients with acute complications such as diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar status, or acute infection; (4) Patients with severe liver or kidney diseases; (5) Patients with malignant tumors or those with a life expectancy of less than 1 year; (6) Patients with neuromuscular dysfunction and psychiatric impairment; (7) Patients with missing data of neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) and carotid ultrasonography. Ultimately, a total of 749 patients were enrolled in this study (Fig. 1).

Data collection and clinical definition

Trained interviewers used standardized questionnaires to collect demographic information and health data. These data covered age, sex, smoking status, medium intensity physical activity, medication use, and duration of diabetes, etc. The health conditions reported by patients included diagnosed hypertension. When patients were admitted, healthcare professionals used professional record forms to measure their height, weight, and blood pressure (BP). Venous blood was obtained in the early morning following an overnight fast. Biochemical test results, such as hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), fasting blood glucose (FBG), total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), albumin (ALB), uric acid (UA), serum creatinine (Scr), total bilirubin (TBIL), and direct bilirubin (DBIL) were obtained from the electronic medical record system.

The smoking status was categorized as either “no current smoker” or “current smoker”. Low education was defined as the patient’s highest level of education being at or below a high school diploma. According to the definition of Guideline for the management of diabetes mellitus in the elderly in China (2024 edition) moderate exercise was defined as 40%-59% reserve heart rate, feeling a little strenuous during exercise, and accelerated heartbeat and respiration but not rapid [22]. The body mass index (BMI) was calculated by dividing the weight in kilograms by the square of the height in meters. The diagnosis of diabetic retinopathy (DR) was based on ophthalmological examinations and determined according to the International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy Severity Scale [23]. Diabetic nephropathy (DN) was diagnosed according to the definition of the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) organization [24]. Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) was diagnosed according to the Chinese Guidelines for the Management of Type 2 Diabetes [25].

Assessment of carotid artery plaque

Trained sonographers performed carotid ultrasonography using a Philips Q5-8F1 Doppler ultrasound system (Philips Company). Participants were required to lie in a supine position and turn their heads 45° to the opposite side of the examined artery. The measurement was carried out on the far wall of the bilateral common carotid arteries, with a segment length of at least 10 millimeters. In the longitudinal view, the measurement direction was perpendicular to the ultrasonic beam, and the measurement position was at least 5 millimeters proximal to the bifurcation. The carotid intima-media thickness (cIMT) was calculated by measuring the distance from the edge of the first echogenic line to the edge of the second echogenic line. The procedure for detecting plaques involved scanning the near and far walls of the common carotid artery, the carotid bifurcation, the external carotid artery, and the internal carotid artery.

According to the latest version of the Mannheim Carotid Intima-Media Thickness and Plaque Consensus, a plaque was defined as follows: in any of the aforementioned arterial segments, there was a discontinuous cIMT reaching or exceeding 1.5 millimeters, or a focal thickening reaching or exceeding 0.5 millimeters, or a thickness 50% greater than the surrounding cIMT [26].

Definition of sarcopenia

According to the consensus of the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS 2019), sarcopenia was defined as low muscle mass combined with either low muscle strength or low physical performance [27]. The patients assumed a supine position. The Skeletal Muscle Index (SMI) was evaluated by certified radiological technologists using the Hologic Discovery W dual-energy X-ray bone densitometer (Hologic Discovery QDR Series). The SMI was calculated by dividing the appendicular skeletal muscle mass (ASM) by the square of the height (kg/m²). The thresholds for low muscle mass were defined as an SMI of less than 7.0 kg/m² in men and less than 5.4 kg/m² in women [27].

Trained physicians conducted the handgrip strength measurement and the 6-meter walk test. The handgrip strength was assessed with the Camry electronic grip strength meter (model EH101; Camry Scale). The patients stood or sat with the elbows fully extended and then performed a maximal isometric contraction with the dominant hand. At least two measurements were taken, and the highest value was recorded [27]. Low muscle strength was defined as handgrip strength less than 28.0 kg for men and less than 18.0 kg for women. The 6-meter walk test was evaluated by measuring the time required to walk 6 m at a normal speed without slowing down, and the average result of at least two trials was recorded. Low physical performance was characterized by a walking speed of < 1.0 m/s [27].

Measurement of inflammatory biomarkers

Fasting venous blood samples were collected from all study participants, and the leukocyte, neutrophil, lymphocyte, and monocyte counts (expressed in 1000 cells/µL) were determined using flow cytometry on a hematology analyzer. Based on previous literature, the following inflammatory indicators were calculated: NLR and SIRI, using the formulas below: NLR = neutrophil counts / lymphocyte counts, SIRI = (neutrophil counts × monocyte counts) / lymphocyte counts [8, 28].

Statistical analysis

For continuous variables, normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and group comparisons were performed using the two-sample independent t-test. Non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR), and group comparisons were conducted using the nonparametric Mann–Whitney U test. For categorical variables, data were shown as frequency counts and percentages, and group comparisons were applied using the chi-square test (χ² test) or Fisher’s exact test.

Restricted cubic spline curve (RCS) was used to explore the associations between NLR and sarcopenia, NLR and CAP, SIRI and sarcopenia, as well as SIRI and CAP respectively. We positioned three nodes at the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of NLR and SIRI to construct restricted cubic spline analysis. The median values of NLR and SIRI were defined as the reference, with an odds ratio (OR) of 1.0. The variables included in the spline models were identical to the independent variables examined in the univariate logistic regression analysis. The relationships among inflammatory biomarkers, sarcopenia and CAP were evaluated using univariate and multivariate logistic regressions. The results of the logistic regression analysis were presented as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). We reported the odds of CAP for each 1-unit increase in NLR and SIRI. Model 1: Crude model; Model 2: adjusted for age ≥ 60 years and sex; Model 3: adjusted for age ≥ 60 years, sex, low education, medium intensity physical activity, BMI ≥ 25 kg/m², and smoking status; Model 4: adjusted for age ≥ 60 years, sex, low education, medium intensity physical activity, BMI ≥ 25 kg/m², smoking status, duration of diabetes, SBP, FBG, DR, DN, DPN, TG, TC, LDL-C, HDL-C, ALB, UA, TBIL, oral antidiabetic drugs (OADs), insulin, and statins. It is worth noting that during statistical analysis, we observed that NLR and SIRI were skewed. Therefore, natural logarithmic transformation (natural logarithm (Ln)-NLR and Ln-SIRI) was performed to meet the requirements of subsequent statistical analyses.

Mediation analysis was performed using the mediation package in R to explore the mediating roles of NLR and SIRI in the association between sarcopenia and CAP. Mediator models: Linear regression model was constructed with NLR and SIRI as the mediator variables separately, in which sarcopenia was set as the exposure factor, and the following covariates were adjusted: age ≥ 60 years, sex, and other confounding factors consistent with those in multivariate logistic regression analysis. Outcome models: Logistic regression models were constructed with CAP as the binary outcome variable, adjusting for sarcopenia (exposure factor), NLR and SIRI (mediator variables), and the same set of covariates as above. Quasi-Bayesian simulation with 1000 iterations was used to estimate the mediation effects, and robust standard errors were calculated to account for potential heteroscedasticity. No exposure-mediator interaction term was included in the models. This mediation analysis was performed under the core assumption of sequential ignorability. In addition, multivariate linear regression analysis and multivariate logistic regression analysis were used to assess the independent associations of mediators with exposure factors and outcome measures.

All analyses were performed using R, version 4.0.3 software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). All statistical tests were conducted as two-tailed, and findings with p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

The prevalence of CAP was 58.9% (n = 441), and that of sarcopenia was 33.9% (n = 254). As shown in Table 1, the levels of monocyte count, NLR, and SIRI were significantly higher in patients with CAP than in those without (all p < 0.05). Patients with CAP had lower albumin level and higher serum creatinine level than those without (all p < 0.001), while there were no significant differences in uric acid, total bilirubin, and direct bilirubin levels between the two groups. Compared with patients without CAP, those with CAP were older, had a longer duration of diabetes, lower BMI and higher prevalence of sarcopenia, DN, DPN, and hypertension (all p < 0.05).

Associations between inflammatory biomarkers, sarcopenia and carotid artery plaque

RCS revealed significant linear association between CAP and inflammatory biomarkers (NLR and SIRI) (NLR: p for nonlinearity = 0.209; SIRI: p for nonlinearity = 0.149). The odds of CAP progressively increased with increasing NLR and SIRI, respectively (NLR: p for total = 0.021; SIRI: p for total = 0.002). Notably, RCS also showed a significant linear association between sarcopenia and inflammatory biomarkers (NLR and SIRI) (NLR: p for nonlinearity = 0.520; SIRI: p for nonlinearity = 0.348). Additionally, the odds of sarcopenia were found to increase progressively with the elevation of NLR and SIRI, respectively (NLR: p for total < 0.001; SIRI: p for total < 0.001) (Fig. 2A, B).

The relationships between sarcopenia and CAP, as well as inflammatory biomarkers and CAP, were evaluated using univariate and multivariate logistic regressions (Table 2). Univariate logistic regression analysis revealed that sarcopenia was significantly and positively associated with CAP (OR: 2.73, 95% CI: 1.97–3.81, p < 0.001). NLR and SIRI were positively correlated with CAP, with odds ratios of 1.76 (95% CI: 1.16–2.75, p < 0.001) and 1.35 (95% CI: 1.12–1.65, p = 0.003), respectively. After converting the inflammatory biomarkers into natural logarithms, such positive associations still persisted (Ln-NLR:1.87, 95% CI: 1.26–2.85, p = 0.004; Ln-SIRI:1.48, 95% CI: 1.18–1.88, p = 0.001).

After adjusting for confounding factors, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that sarcopenia, NLR, SIRI, Ln-NLR and Ln-SIRI were also significantly and positively associated with CAP (sarcopenia: adjusted odds ratio (aOR): 1.67, 95% CI: 1.10–2.54, p = 0.016; NLR: aOR: 1.63, 95% CI: 1.01–2.66, p = 0.047; SIRI: aOR: 1.23, 95% CI: 1.01–1.59, p = 0.046; Ln-NLR: aOR: 1.77, 95% CI: 1.10–2.80, p = 0.037; Ln-SIRI: aOR: 1.38, 95% CI: 1.02–1.74, p = 0.035).

Mediating effect

In this study, a mediation analysis was conducted to explore the mediating role of inflammatory biomarkers in the relationship between sarcopenia and CAP. Figure 3 showed that the two inflammatory biomarkers, NLR and SIRI, may partially explain the observed association between sarcopenia and CAP, with NLR and SIRI accounting for 10.39% and 11.40% of the indirect effects of this association, respectively (p < 0.05). Similarly, after using natural log-transformed NLR and SIRI (Ln-NLR, Ln-SIRI), these two inflammatory biomarkers still played mediating roles in the association between sarcopenia and CAP, with mediation proportions of 9.73% and 9.29%, respectively (p < 0.05).

In Model 4, multivariate linear regression analysis showed that sarcopenia was positively associated with NLR and SIRI, respectively (NLR: β = 0.123, 95% CI: 0.051–0.195, p = 0.001; SIRI: β = 0.321, 95% CI: 0.142–0.501, p < 0.001). Similarly, in Model 4, multivariate linear regression analysis using Ln-NLR and Ln-SIRI showed that sarcopenia remained positively associated with Ln-NLR (β = 0.085, 95% CI: 0.036–0.135, p = 0.001) and Ln-SIRI (β = 0.197, 95% CI: 0.085–0.310, p = 0.001).

In Model 4 with further adjustment for sarcopenia, multivariate logistic regression analysis suggested that NLR and SIRI were positively associated with CAP, respectively (NLR: aOR = 1.82, 95% CI: 1.15–2.74, p = 0.040; SIRI: aOR = 1.35, 95% CI: 1.10–1.70, p = 0.043). Similarly, when analyzed using Ln-NLR and Ln-SIRI, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that Ln-NLR and Ln-SIRI were positively associated with CAP, respectively (Ln-NLR: aOR = 1.87, 95% CI: 1.03–2.84, p = 0.038; Ln-SIRI: aOR = 1.38, 95% CI: 1.05–1.84, p = 0.037). Thus, independent associations were observed between mediators, exposure factors, and outcome measures.

Discussion

In this study, we found that after adjusting for multiple confounding factors, sarcopenia was positively associated with CAP in patients with T2DM, and inflammation may partially account for the observed association between sarcopenia and CAP. Specifically, NLR and SIRI explained 10.39% and 11.40% of the indirect effects, respectively, while Ln-NLR and Ln-SIRI accounted for 9.73% and 9.29% of the indirect effects, respectively, suggesting that inflammation may be involved in the biological mechanisms linking sarcopenia to CAP.

There have been previous studies on the association between inflammation and sarcopenia [11, 29–32]. For example, an analysis based on the NHANES database showed that elevated levels of NLR and SIRI were associated with an increased prevalence of sarcopenia and an increased mortality rate [8]. A cross-sectional study based on the elderly population in West China indicated that higher NLR level was associated with an increased prevalence of sarcopenia among middle-aged and elderly people [29]. The study by Jimenez-Gutierrez on the molecular mechanisms of inflammation in sarcopenia further elaborated on the crucial role of inflammation [11]. Consistent with previous studies, our study suggested that higher NLR and SIRI were positively associated with sarcopenia. Therefore, both previous studies and our research have shown that inflammation is involved in sarcopenia.

Some previous studies have also confirmed the link between inflammation and CAP [12, 33–37]. The study by Pereira-Neves showed that in patients with CAP, the NLR level has a positive linear relationship with CAP [34]. According to the research report by Kim, type 2 diabetes mellitus patients with higher NLR levels are more likely to develop severe coronary artery disease (CAD) and carotid atherosclerosis [12]. A retrospective cohort study linked elevated SIRI levels to higher carotid atherosclerosis incidence, particularly in individuals without hypertension, diabetes, or hyperlipidemia [36]. Consistent with the results of previous studies, our study indicated that both NLR and SIRI were independently positively associated with CAP in patients with T2DM. After adjusting multiple covariates, this conclusion still held. These findings highlight the close association between inflammation and CAP.

The association between sarcopenia and CAP has been confirmed by multiple studies. A cross-sectional study based on patients with T2DM found that low skeletal muscle mass in the lower extremities and low grip strength might be risk factors for high carotid intima-media thickness and high plaque scores [5, 6]. Ming-Feng Xia carried out a cross-sectional study on the people in the Changfeng area of Shanghai and discovered that sarcopenia was associated with a higher prevalence of CAP [38]. Studies in patients with NAFLD have also shown that sarcopenia is closely associated with the risk of CAP [7]. Our research showed that after adjusting for multiple confounding factors, sarcopenia was still independently associated with CAP, which was consistent with previous studies.

Although previous studies have clearly established a strong correlation between sarcopenia and CAP, the mediating mechanisms driving this association have not been fully elucidated. Inflammation is considered a key bridge connecting sarcopenia and CAP [30, 39]. We suggested a positive correlation between inflammation and sarcopenia, inflammation and CAP. And inflammation further played a mediating role between sarcopenia and CAP. The results showed that NLR and SIRI may partially account for the observed association, with mediated proportions of 10.39% and 11.40%, respectively. Similarly, Ln-NLR and Ln-SIRI also exerted partial mediating effects, with mediated proportions of 9.73% and 9.29%, respectively. Mechanistically, the mediating effect of NLR and SIRI may be related to the activation of neutrophils and lymphocytes under inflammatory conditions [40–46]. Under inflammatory stimulation, neutrophils accelerate carotid plaque formation by promoting vascular endothelial injury and lipid deposition through the release of inflammatory factors such as IL-6 [41]; concurrently, they disrupt the balance between muscle protein synthesis and degradation, thereby inducing sarcopenia [44]. An imbalance in lymphocyte subsets impairs the repair capacity of vascular walls, exacerbates plaque instability [42, 43], and simultaneously leads to chronic inflammatory infiltration of muscle tissue due to immune dysfunction, further contributing to the induction of sarcopenia [45, 46]. Sarcopenia promotes the development of carotid plaque primarily through inducing chronic low-grade inflammation, as the elevated proinflammatory cytokines may trigger vascular endothelial injury and accelerate lipid deposition in the arterial wall [47]. Notably, we found that the proportion of the mediating effect of SIRI was seen to be greater than that of NLR. Compared with NLR, the calculation formula of SIRI includes monocytes in addition to neutrophils and lymphocytes, we hypothesized that this phenomenon may be related to monocytes. Monocytes can affect the occurrence of carotid plaque and sarcopenia by participating in processes such as inflammatory response, immune regulation, and metabolic disorders [44].

Oxidative stress and systemic antioxidant status are well-recognized key regulators of vascular lesions and metabolic disorders in type 2 diabetes mellitus, and endogenous antioxidant biomarkers have been confirmed to be involved in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis and sarcopenia [48–51]. To further verify whether endogenous antioxidant biomarkers influence the association of inflammation with sarcopenia and CAP, we collected data on three endogenous antioxidant biomarkers (albumin, bilirubin, and uric acid) and included albumin as covariates in Model 4. Our findings suggested that the independent positive association between sarcopenia and CAP, as well as the statistically significant mediating effects of the NLR and SIRI, remained robust even after adjustment for albumin, bilirubin, and uric acid. These results indicate that after adjusting for endogenous antioxidant-related biomarkers, inflammation may still be involved in mediating the association between sarcopenia and CAP in patients with T2DM.

Serum creatinine, a classic biomarker of renal function and muscle mass [52, 53], also merits discussion in the present study. Emerging evidence suggests that serum creatinine may possess antioxidant properties [54, 55], and is independently linked to sarcopenia and CAP [56, 57], respectively. However, given its close physiological link to skeletal muscle mass [56], we did not include creatinine as a core adjustment variable to avoid multicollinearity. Because creatinine production is directly related to muscle mass, sarcopenia itself may lower creatinine levels, potentially distorting the observed association between sarcopenia and CAP. In this study, creatinine levels were higher in patients with CAP, which may reflect combined effects of differences in muscle mass, early renal dysfunction in type 2 diabetes, and compensatory antioxidant responses. Future studies may clarify the independent role of creatinine using stratified analyses by muscle mass and renal function.

The presence of CAP increases the risk of cardiovascular diseases [4], while sarcopenia can lead to a decline in physical function, a decrease in quality of life, and an increased risk of adverse events such as falls [58, 59]. Our study found that NLR and SIRI played a partial mediating role between sarcopenia and CAP. Effective control of inflammation might alleviate the impact of sarcopenia on CAP, thereby reducing its detrimental effects on health.

Here were several limitations on our research. First, even though we have shown a correlation between inflammation, sarcopenia, and CAP in T2DM patients, the cross-sectional design of the study makes it impossible to conclude causality. Furthermore, given that all variables were assessed at the same time point, our mediation analysis should be regarded as exploratory rather than causal mediation. In the future, more longitudinal studies are required. Second, given that this study was conducted in a single-center Chinese population with relatively homogeneous characteristics, the generalizability of our findings to other populations may be limited. Future large-scale, multi-center studies involving diverse ethnic groups are needed to validate and extend our conclusions. Third, although we adjusted for multiple potential confounding factors, there may still be some unmeasured interfering factors. Future research should comprehensively assess these unmeasured confounding factors, such as sleep quality, psychological stress, and dietary intake, to reduce residual bias and improve the reliability of the results.

Conclusions

Overall, our findings indicated that sarcopenia was significantly positively associated with CAP, and NLR and SIRI played a partial mediating role in this association. This suggested that effective control of inflammation might attenuate the impact of sarcopenia on the development of CAP. Future studies should focus on investigating the underlying biological mechanisms of the inflammatory status linking sarcopenia and CAP, while exploring specific interventions to improve inflammatory status through prospective cohort studies.

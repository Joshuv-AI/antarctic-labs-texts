Rapid evaluation of vault post-ICL surgery using digital slit lamp microscope photography
Authors: Danyang Wang, Zhuoya Quan, Junxin Xiao, Zhiqiang Yu, Xingtao Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo introduce a novel method using digital slit lamp microscope photography to efficiently and reliably estimate the vault value post-ICL (Implantable Collamer Lens) surgery.MethodsShort-term and long-term post-operative patients who underwent Implantable Collamer Lens (ICL) implantation were enrolled in the study.The central hole of the ICL lens and its projection on the lens were documented using digital slit lamp microscope photography. Three ophthalmologists (DoctorA: an ICL expert, Doctor B: an ICL beginner, and Doctor C: ophthalmic resident) independently scored the images based on a predefined scale. The consistency and repeatability of the scores among the three ophthalmologists were calculated. Additionally, the correlation between the scores and the vault values measured by anterior segment optical coherence tomography (OCT) was analyzed, leading to the derivation of a regression equation.ResultsA total of 259 anterior segment images from 132 patients were captured. The average scores given by three ophthalmologists were 4.76 ± 1.47, 4.81 ± 1.52, and 4.27 ± 1.08, demonstrating a high intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.787 (p < 0.001). The highest ICC was observed between the ICL expert and ICL beginner (0.918, p < 0.001), while the lowest was between the ICL expert and ophthalmic resident (0.698, p < 0.001). The postoperative vault value averaged 619.52 ± 197.95 μm, showing a significant correlation with the score (R = 0.865, p < 0.001). Higher scores represent higher vaults.ConclusionsThe relationship between the ICL central hole and its projection on the lens facilitates a quick and reliable estimation of the vault value.

Background

Implantable collamer lens (ICL) implantation is increasingly recognized as a safe and effective treatment for myopia and astigmatism [1]. The ICL vault, defined as the distance between the anterior surface of the crystalline lens and the posterior surface of the ICL, is a critical parameter linked to the safety outcomes of ICL implantation. Ideally, the vault value should range between 250 μm and 1000 µm [2]. Measurements of the vault value are usually conducted using anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT), ultrasound biomicroscopy (UBM), or a Pentacam instrument (Oculus, Germany) [3]. Clinicians can use slit lamp microscopy to observe the position of the ICL, the anterior chamber, and the crystalline lens. Experienced surgeons may estimate the vault value by comparing the distance between the ICL and the crystalline lens with corneal thickness [4]. However, without standardized guidance and due to variability in corneal thickness among individuals, this method lack reliability. The V4c ICL model features a 360-µm central hole designed to prevent pupillary block and acute angle-closure without compromising optical quality [5], which is easily visible under slit lamp microscopy. We propose a novel guidance method using digital slit lamp microscope photography to observe the relationship between the central hole and its projection on the crystalline lens, thereby enabling a quick evaluation of the vault value.

Methods

Patients

Short-term (1 week to 1 month) and long-term (6 month) post-operative patients who underwent ICL V4c implantation for the correction of myopia and astigmatism at the Eye and ENT Hospital of Fudan University were recruited. The inclusion criteria were stable refraction (change in refraction of less than 0.50 D per year in the past 2 years), and a strong desire for the correction of myopia. The exclusion criteria were acute ocular inflammatory disease, systemic disease, a history of ocular surgery, an anterior chamber depth (ACD) of less than 2.8 mm, an endothelial cell density (ECD) of less than 2500 cells/mm2, and suspicion of keratoconus. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Eye and ENT Hospital and adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects.

ICL V4c surgical procedure

All ICL V4c implantation surgeries were performed by the same surgeon, ZQY. The surgical procedure followed the same protocol as described in previous studies [6]. Briefly, after injecting an ophthalmic viscosurgical device (sodium hyaluronate 1.7%; Bausch & Lomb, Inc.), the ICL was inserted using an injector cartridge (STARR Surgical Co) through a 3.0 mm clear corneal incision. Once the ICL was properly positioned, the ophthalmic viscosurgical device was entirely removed with lactated Ringer’s solution through irrigation [7]. We prescribed 1.0% prednisolone acetate (Pred Forte; Allergan, Irvine, CA, USA) four times daily for four days, 0.5% levofloxacin (Cravit; Santen, Osaka, Japan) four times daily for one week, pranoprofen (Senju, Osaka, Japan) four times daily for two weeks, and artificial tears four times daily for one month postoperatively.

Anterior segment photograph and ICL V4c vault measurement

A digital slit-lamp microscope (MediWorks digital slit lamp microscope photography S390L, Shanghai, China) was used to capture images of the anterior segment following ICL V4c implantation. During imaging, the slit lamp was set to: 10x magnification, with the slit light beam width knob scale adjusted to 7–8, and the beam was set at a 30-degree angle from the examiner’s right side for observation. (Fig. 1). Three digital images were recorded by a clinical doctor (ZQY). The central hole and its projection on the crystalline lens had to be clearly identifiable. This optical principle is based on the fact that when the slit light band of the slit-lamp microscope obliquely illuminates the central hole of the ICL, a projected aperture of the central hole is formed on the posterior lens surface. If the slit light band vertically illuminates the central hole, the central hole and the posterior projected aperture completely coincide; whereas illumination at a certain angle allows the projected aperture to be directly observed under slit lamp. The larger the incident angle of the slit light band, the less the projected aperture and the central hole coincide. Moreover, at a fixed incident angle, the greater the distance between the plane of the projected aperture and the central hole, the less the projected aperture and the central hole coincide. Based on this principle, with the incident angle of the slit light band fixed at 30 degrees, we can estimate the distance (vault) between the plane of the projected aperture (lens) and the central hole (ICL lens) according to the relative positional relationship between the projected aperture and the central hole. Greater overlap indicates a smaller vault (shorter light path, projected aperture is small and aligned with the hole); less overlap (or separation) indicates a larger vault (longer light path, projected aperture expands and shifts relative to the hole). (Fig. 2). These images were then evaluated by three independent ophthalmic doctors: Doctor A, an experienced ICL surgeon; Doctor B, an experienced ophthalmologist who had just begun learning ICL surgery; and Doctor C, an ophthalmic resident. They used a specified scoring scale (Fig. 3) for their assessments. Anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT, Optoview) was also performed to record the ICL V4c vault concurrently. The vault refers to the distance between the posterior surface of the ICL V4c and the anterior surface of the crystalline lens (Fig. 4). Measurements were centered on the optical axis, indicated by a white dotted line on the screen [5]. Vault values were obtained three times by another experienced doctor (DYW) and averaged for analysis. Additionally, the photographer (ZQY), the doctors scoring the images, and the vault examiner (DYW) were kept isolated and blinded to the other conditions of the same patient.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 20.0 (SPSS Inc., IBM, USA), with results expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test verified data normality. Interobserver reproducibility of anterior segment picture scoring by three observers was evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), single measures was used. Pearson correlation analyses explored the relationships between anterior segment picture scoring and vault value measured by AS-OCT. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

A total of 259 anterior segment photographs from 132 patients (49 men and 83 women) aged 28.11 ± 7.24 years (range 17–47) were analyzed. The baseline characteristics are shown in Table 1. All surgical procedures were uneventful and no postoperative complications were observed. The follow-up period ranged from one week to one month. We also conducted a long follow-up evaluations on patients at 6 months postoperatively. A total of 20 patients who had undergone ICL implantation and reached 6 months postoperatively were recalled.

The average postoperative vault in all patients was 619.52 ± 197.95 μm. The anterior segment photograph score shows a strong correlation with the vault (R = 0.865, 0.842, 0.741 for doctors A, B, and C respectively, p < 0.001), with higher scores indicating a higher vault (Fig. 5). The most experienced ICL surgeon provided the most accurate predictive scores for the postoperative vault. The score from doctor A and vault value distribution are shown in Table 2, The regression equation for doctor A’s score and the vault is vault = 116.59 * score + 64.01. The reason why we selected the ICL surgery expert’s scores and OCT vault results to calculate the regression formula was that with the highest correlation to objective data can minimize potential subjective biases.

To expedite clinicians’ judgment, we simplified the scoring system into three groups: 0–2 points (n = 18, AS-OCT vault 265.31 ± 78.91, defined as relatively low vault), 3–6 points (n = 211, AS-OCT vault 605.84 ± 142.73, defined as ideal vault), and 7–8 points (n = 30, AS-OCT vault 928.21 ± 139.98, defined as relatively high vault). We then re-evaluated the scoring classification with three physicians, yielding acceptable but lower consistency than 0-8-point scoring system, among three doctors, ICC = 0.591, 95% CI: [0.523, 0.654], p < 0.001, pairwise ICCs were 0.806 (Doctor A vs. B, 95% CI: [0.759, 0.845], p < 0.001),0.466 (Doctor A vs. C, 95% CI: [0.355, 0.563], p < 0.001),0.420 (Doctor B vs. C, 95% CI: [0.312, 0.517], p < 0.001). Similarly, the correlation coefficients between the simplified scoring and OCT-measured vault values were also lower than those of the 0-8-point scoring system, with the correlation coefficients for Doctors A, B, and C being 0.713, 0.689, and 0.499 (which in 0-8-point system were 0.865, 0.842 and 0.741 respectively).

To validate the application of the scoring method in long-term follow-up patients, we recalled 20 patients who were 6 months post-ICL implantation. The same Doctor A, B, and C scored their anterior segment photographs. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of scores among the three doctors was 0.825 (95% CI: [0.72, 0.898], p < 0.001), also with the highest ICC observed between Doctor A and Doctor B (0.863, 95% CI: [0.756, 0.925], p < 0.001), followed by Doctor B and Doctor C (0.815, 95% CI: [0.637, 0.904], p < 0.001), and Doctor A and Doctor C (0.796, 95% CI: [0.623, 0.891], p < 0.001). Additionally, the correlation coefficients between the scores of Doctor A, B, and C and the vault values measured by anterior segment optical coherence tomography (OCT) were 0.875, 0.834, and 0.798 (p < 0.001), respectively. Notably, in the 6-month follow-up scoring using the simplified system, the correlation coefficients between the scores of Doctors A, B, and C and OCT-measured vault values were 0.752, 0.785, and 0.752 (p < 0.001), respectively, with an overall intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.924 (95% CI: [0.876, 0.956]) among the three doctors (p < 0.001). Pairwise ICCs were 0.89 (Doctor A vs. B, 95% CI: [0.803, 0.940], p < 0.001), 1.00 (Doctor A vs. C, p < 0.001), and 0.89 (Doctor B vs. C, 95% CI: [0.803, 0.940], p < 0.001). These results indicate that after 6 months of learning and practice, Doctor B maintained high consistency and accuracy with Doctor A, while Doctor C also showed improved consistency and accuracy in scoring.

Discussion

The ICL vault is crucial in estimating the safety of ICL surgery, particularly for patients with a back-up ICL during the surgical procedure. If the surgeon can immediately assess the vault post-operation, it assists in determining whether the ICL size is appropriate and in selecting the correct ICL size for the other eye.

Several methods can be used to measure vault value after ICL surgery, including AS-OCT, Pentacam, and UBM. However, no gold standard has been established [8]. UBM enables precise analysis of anterior segment structures but is contacting and therefore unsuitable for early postoperative periods. AS-OCT and Pentacam are more commonly utilized [3], but these methods require costly equipment.

In the current study, we found that identifying the V4c lens central hole and its projection on the crystalline lens using a digital slit lamp microscope photography is straightforward. The relationship between these two circles is relatively stable and can be easily estimated. A quick scoring enables a quickly assessment of the vault value. Estimating the vault value using a digital slit lamp microscope photography is both reliable and repeatable. However, we also observed that experienced ICL surgeons provide more predictable vault estimates compared to ophthalmic residents, indicating that this skill can be improved with training and practice to enhance the accuracy of vault estimation.

Clinical applications and previous studies [9, 10] have indicated that vault is not a constant value; it is influenced by multiple factors, including pupil size (studies have shown that a dilated pupil correlates with a higher vault, while a constricted pupil is associated with a lower vault), lens accommodation, ambient light intensity (brighter light or wider slits constrict the pupil, reducing the measured vault; dimmer light or narrower slits dilate the pupil, increasing the vault), and others, all of which determine that vault represents a dynamically variable range. Consequently, overly detailed scoring systems may be suboptimal in clinical utility. The scoring system we designed covers nearly all possible relationships between the central hole and projection ring, which is relevant to our future research directions. Our future research aims to develop AI image recognition software that can automatically identify the relationship and distance between these two cycles. Through deep learning with big data, we intend to establish a more precise regression equation. In the future, this software will be integrated into digital slit lamps, enabling automatic calculation of vault based on anterior segment observation and photography.

In clinical practice, however, in the absence of such software for automatic identification and calculation, clinicians still rely on subjective observation to assess vault. The scoring system in this study was slightly complex for clinical judgment at beginning, which partially explains the scoring discrepancies between experienced experts and resident physicians when using this system. However, we also observed that after 6 months of dedicated learning and clinical practice, resident physicians demonstrated a significant improvement in scoring accuracy. Specifically, their scores showed enhanced consistency with experienced experts (e.g. the intraclass correlation coefficient between resident physicians and experts increased from the initial 0.698 to 0.815), and the correlation between their scores and OCT-measured vault values also improved notably (from R = 0.741 to R = 0.823). This progressive improvement strongly indicates the practicality of the 0-8-point scoring system: while it may present a modest learning curve initially, its structured design allows clinicians—even those with limited ICL experience—to gradually master the assessment criteria through repeated practice, ultimately achieving reliable vault estimation.

When comparing with the 0-8-point sclae, the simplified system exhibits slightly lower accuracy, but it remains a valuable tool during the initial learning phase. Furthermore, the improved consistency observed at 6 months suggests that the accuracy of the simplified system also increases with clinician training and experience. Given that a vault of 250–1000 micrometers is considered ideal, relatively low vault (scoring 0–2) and relatively high vault (scoring 7–8) should receive greater attention to the status of the native lens, corneal endothelial, and intraocular pressure (IOP) during the follow-up process.

There were several limitations to the present study. Firstly, we estimated the vault using the central hole and its projection on the lens. However, when using AS-OCT to measure the vault, the central hole was not always located at the optical axis’s center, potentially introducing measurement errors. Secondly, we utilized only one type of digital slit lamp microscope photography in this study, making it difficult to determine whether the central hole of the ICL and its projection on the lens can be identified clearly and easily across all types of digital slit-lamp microscopes. Third, the 0-8-point scoring system we developed still exhibits certain limitations in clinical application. For resident physicians and those unfamiliar with ICL implantation, a certain learning curve is required to improve the accuracy of assessment. In such cases, preliminary determination of the vault range can be achieved through simplified scoring system (0–2 points: relatively low vault; 3–6 points: ideal vault; 7–8 points: relatively high vault). Additionally, the regression equation derived from the correlation between the relationship of the central hole and projection ring and the measured vault values also demonstrates practical limitations in clinical settings. Given that vault is a dynamically variable parameter, the regression equation can only provide an approximate vault value calculated based on scoring. In future research, we are committed to developing AI-based image recognition software compatible with digital slit lamps. This software will automatically identify the relative relationship and distance between the central hole and projection ring through image analysis, thereby yielding more accurate vault measurements.

In conclusion, we introduced a swift and simple method for estimating vault value after ICL V4c surgery using digital slit lamp microscope photography. By analyzing the relationship between the central hole and its projection on the lens, this method proves to be both repeatable and reliable, making it worthy of broader application.

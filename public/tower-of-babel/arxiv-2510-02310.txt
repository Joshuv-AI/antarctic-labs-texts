Abstract
Recently, it has been suggested that the spectrum of physical states in the Standard Model may include an ultralight pseudo-scalar, denoted by η w \eta_{\rm w} , in analogy with the η ′ \eta^{\prime} state arising from the strong interactions. We find that typical expectations for the properties of η w \eta_{\rm w} get challenged by astrophysical constraints on the couplings of ultralight bosons. Our strongest limit sets a lower bound of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 100 ​ TeV ) \mathcal{O}{(\rm 100~TeV)} on the decay constant of the hypothesized pseudo-scalar. We also briefly discuss whether η w \eta_{\rm w} could be a dark matter candidate, or the origin of dark energy, but conclude that those identifications appear unlikely. Given the important implications of a potentially overlooked η w \eta_{\rm w} state for a more complete understanding of the electroweak interactions and a fundamental description of Nature, further theoretical and phenomenological investigations of this possibility and its associated physics are warranted.



It has recently been proposed that the dynamics of the electroweak sector leads to the emergence of an ultralight pseudo-scalar, denoted by
\eta_{\rm w}
, whose mass arises from the anomaly of the global
U(1)_{\rm B+L}
symmetry of the
B+L
charge, where
B
and
L
are the baryon and lepton numbers, respectively
Dvali et al. 2025a
;
Dvali et al. 2025b
. The gist of this proposal is that since the vacuum angle
\theta_{\rm w}
associated with the
SU(2)
gauge interactions of the Standard Model (SM) is not physical
Anselm and Johansen 1993
;
Anselm and Johansen 1994
;
Fileviez Perez and Patel 2014
, there must exist a pseudo-scalar, the aforemnetioned
\eta_{\rm w}
, that is responsible for its removal. This is akin to the same effect in the strong interactions governed by the SM quantum chromodynamics (QCD), in the presence of a massless quark that would make the analogous angle
\theta_{\rm QCD}
unphysical. Within QCD, this effect would be ascribed to the infrared contribution of the observed pseudo-scalar
\eta^{\prime}

Witten 1979
, whose relatively large mass
\sim 1
GeV originates from the anomaly of the axial
U(1)_{A}
symmetry.

It is fair to say that the status of
\eta_{\rm w}
as a firm prediction of the SM is not well-established and some of its fundamental properties, like mass and coupling, remain to be confidently predicted
Dvali et al. 2025b
. It has also been suggested that perhaps this state is not ultralight and is in fact a superposition of hydrogen and anti-hydrogen
Cacciapaglia et al. 2025
. This is an interesting idea which may play a role in the phenomenology of the relevant physics. However, as pointed out in Ref.
Dvali et al. 2025b
, it does not seem to fit the assumed features of a particle associated with the removal of
\theta_{\rm w}
from the electroweak interactions. This association would make sense if one could treat
\eta_{\rm w}
as a phase,
i.e.
a Goldstone mode, and it is not obvious that a composite atomic state can fulfill this role.

We find that the theoretical arguments put forth in Refs.
Dvali et al. 2025a
;
Dvali et al. 2025b
– in order to support the presence of
\eta_{\rm w}
within the SM – provide good motivation for examining this intriguing hypothesis further. The emergence of
\eta_{\rm w}
from the SM electroweak interactions would have significant import for our understanding of fundamental physics. In light of this circumstance, it is interesting to examine the feasibility of this hypothesis from various angles. Here, we will simply assume that the
\eta_{\rm w}
pseudo-scalar described in Refs.
Dvali et al. 2025a
;
Dvali et al. 2025b
is a prediction of the SM. We will then examine to what extent current astrophysical bounds, in particular those related to stellar emission of new light particles, can constrain the basic properties of
\eta_{\rm w}
.

Let us denote the mass of
\eta_{\rm w}
by
m_{\eta\rm w}
and its decay constant by
f_{\eta\rm w}
. What do we expect for the value of
m_{\eta\rm w}
? This mass scale is assumed to be generated by electroweak instantons due to the
B+L
anomaly in the SM. Therefore, it seems reasonable to take the Higgs vacuum expectation value
v=246
GeV as the typical mass scale
M
of the relevant dynamics. However, the associated instanton effects are suppressed by
e^{-2\pi/\alpha_{2}(v)}\sim 10^{-80}
, where
\alpha_{2}\equiv g_{2}^{2}/(4\pi)
and
g_{2}\approx 0.65
is the SM
SU(2)
coupling at the weak scale
\sim v

m_{\eta\rm w}^{2}\lesssim e^{\frac{-2\pi}{\alpha_{2}(M)}}M^{2}

(1)

which for
M\sim v
yields
m_{\eta\rm w}\lesssim 10^{-29}
eV. This is a very tiny mass, making
\eta_{\rm w}
a genuine ultralight scalar. Note that even for an unlikely value
M\sim M_{\rm P}\sim 10^{19}
GeV,
i.e.
near the Planck scale, we get
m_{\eta\rm w}\sim 10^{-39}
eV, which is even deeper in the ultralight regime, due to the falling value of
\alpha_{2}
with energy in the SM. Here, we have used
\alpha_{2}(M_{\rm P})\approx 1/50

\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}(\mu)}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}(\mu_{0})}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{2\pi}\ln(\mu/\mu_{0})\,,

(2)

where
\beta_{0}=19/6
in the SM (see, Ref.
McLerran et al. 2012

\eta_{\rm w}
state,
m_{\eta\rm w}
is extremely small and within the kinematic reach of stellar physical processes.

Next, we will focus on the decay constant
f_{\eta\rm w}

Dvali et al. 2025b

f_{\eta\rm w}\lesssim v
is considered as reasonable, in light of theoretic considerations. In the scenario where the physics of
\eta_{\rm w}
is entirely contained in the SM, this scalar is associated with a condensate that emerges from the dynamics of the ’t Hooft vertex through the electroweak

\langle{(QQQ\ell)^{N_{f}}}\rangle\propto e^{\frac{-2\pi}{\alpha_{2}(v)}}v^{6N_{f}},

(3)

Dvali et al. 2025b

No matter what the actual value of

\eta_{\rm w}

\frac{\alpha_{2}}{8\pi}\frac{\eta_{\rm w}}{f_{\eta\rm w}}W^{a}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{W}^{a\mu\nu}\,,

Let us now turn to the question of dark matter (DM), which comprises about 25% of cosmic energy density
Navas et al. 2024
. The identity of DM remains a mystery of great interest to both particle physics and cosmology. A great deal of effort, in theory as well as experiment, is devoted to finding the answer to this question, which is one of the main motivations in searching for new physics beyond the SM. This is because no particle, elementary or otherwise, within the SM domain is believed to account for the DM content of the Universe. While neutrinos are weakly interacting and long-lived, they do not have the requisite mass and cosmological properties to establish the observed DM abundance and characteristics. It has also been suggested that there may be a deeply bound long-lived state of six quarks
Jaffe 1977
– often called the “dibaryon” – that could be DM
Farrar and Zaharijas 2004
;
Farrar 2018
. However, no such state has been observed and it is quite doubtful that it could be the dominant form of matter in the Universe
Kolb and Turner 2019
;
McDermott et al. 2019
.

In light of the above considerations, it is important to ask whether
\eta_{\rm w}
– assuming that it arises without the need for physics outside of the SM – can be a viable DM candidate. As discussed before, a weak scale origin for this particle would suggest
m_{\eta\rm w}\sim 10^{-29}
eV which is too light. To see this, note that its de Broglie wavelength would be given by

\lambda_{\rm w}\sim(m_{\eta\rm w}v_{g})^{-1}\,,

(13)

where
v_{g}\lesssim 10^{-3}
is a typical galactic virial velocity. The above equation then suggests
\lambda_{\rm w}\gtrsim 10^{32}~\text{eV}^{-1}\gtrsim
Gpc, which is several orders of magnitude large than the size of a galaxy. Thus,
\eta_{\rm w}
cannot be confined within galactic structures, as observational data require. Hence, it seems unlikely that
\eta_{\rm w}
could be a viable DM candidate.

A very light scalar could in principle provide an explanation for the observed accelerated expansion of the Universe
Navas et al. 2024
(see also Ref.
McLerran et al. 2012
for an alternative view of the electroweak instanton contribution to the cosmological constant). In this case, the field should be essentially static and not have any significant kinetic energy at the present epoch. This amounts to requiring that
m_{\eta\rm w}\lesssim H_{0}
, where
H_{0}\sim 10^{-33}
eV is the present Hubble constant. Then, the potential energy of
\eta_{\rm w}
could provide the requisite “dark energy” accelerating the expansion of the Universe, which we may estimate by
\rho_{\rm w}\sim m_{\eta\rm w}^{2}f_{\eta\rm w}^{2}
. However, if we take
f_{\eta\rm w}\sim 100
TeV, as close as possible to the weak scale allowed by astrophysical bounds above, we would get
\rho_{\rm w}\lesssim 10^{-34}
eV
4
. The dark energy stored in the
\eta_{\rm w}
field is thus expected to be exponentially small compared to the value
\sim 10^{-11}
eV
4

Navas et al. 2024
inferred from observational data. Getting close to the observed value requires
f_{\eta\rm w}\sim M_{\rm P}
for
m_{\eta\rm w}\sim H_{0}
.

Summary and concluding remarks:
—In this work, we have considered the astrophysical implications of a possible ultralight pseudo-scalar, the
\eta_{\rm w}
. It has been argued in Refs.
Dvali et al. 2025a
;
Dvali et al. 2025b
that this boson can emerge in the SM, without the need for any new ingredients, due to non-perturbative electroweak interactions and the
B+L
anomaly. The arguments that have led to this conclusion are subtle, but have close kinship with those that relate the observed
\eta^{\prime}
meson to QCD instantons and the axial anomaly. As such, the case for
\eta_{\rm w}
can be considered motivated and worth further scrutiny, as it can have profound implications for the physical spectrum of the SM particles and electroweak dynamics.

Our results indicate that typical assumptions about the basic physical properties of
\eta_{\rm w}
are in tension with astrophysical constraints on ultralight bosons. We found that avoiding excessive cooling of red giants results in the strongest such constraint, providing a lower bound
f_{\eta\rm w}\gtrsim 100
TeV on the decay constant of a hypothetical
\eta_{\rm w}
. This is roughly three or more orders of magnitude above the expectation
f_{\eta\rm w}\lesssim 100
GeV, assuming an electroweak origin.

While the underlying non-perturbative dynamics of such a particle is non-trivial, the bounds on its coupling, as obtained in this work, challenge typical arguments for the emergence of
\eta_{\rm w}
and its general properties. Nonetheless, we view the possibility of a hitherto-ignored ultralight state of the SM sufficiently intriguing that further investigations of this topic seem well-justified. If theoretical evidence for
\eta_{\rm w}
gets more robust and difficult to refute, its detection would become an urgent experimental priority in fundamental physics. In that case, our results suggest that astrophysical observations could provide a promising avenue for discovery of
\eta_{\rm w}
.

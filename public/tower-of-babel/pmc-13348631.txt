Breast cancer screening coverage and its association with hospitalisation and mortality: a nationwide ecological study from Poland (2019–2023)
Authors: Krzysztof Gajda, Izabela Sowińska, Marta Kasper, Iwona Malinowska-Lipień
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBreast cancer is the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women in Poland and remains a major public health challenge. Population-based screening plays a central role in reducing mortality; however, its effectiveness depends on participation rates and equitable access to healthcare services.ObjectiveTo assess breast cancer screening coverage in Poland between 2019 and 2023 and to explore population-level associations between screening coverage, hospitalisations, and mortality.MethodsData were obtained from three national administrative sources: the Ministry of Health’s Systemic and Implementation Analyses Database (BASiW), the national mortality registry maintained by Statistics Poland (GUS), and the National Health Fund (NFZ) database (API Statistics NFZ 1.1). Aggregated data from all 16 voivodeships were merged by year and region. Descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation coefficients, and multivariable linear regression models were applied.ResultsThe mean screening coverage during the study period was 50.9%, with substantial regional variation (38.7%–68.2%). A marked decline in screening attendance was observed in 2020, coinciding with the COVID-19 pandemic, followed by gradual recovery in subsequent years. Strong positive correlations were identified between the number of screened women and hospitalisations (r = 0.96) as well as mortality (r = 0.99), largely reflecting population size effects. In multivariable regression analysis (Model 1), higher screening coverage (primary exposure) was statistically associated with lower breast cancer mortality in women aged ≥ 65 years (primary outcome) at the population level (B = − 1.93; p < 0.001). In Model 2, screening coverage was not significantly associated with hospitalisations in women aged 41–60 years (B = -0.81; p = 0.682).ConclusionsBreast cancer screening coverage in Poland remained below the level considered optimal and demonstrated substantial regional disparities. Higher participation was associated with lower mortality at the population level; however, this association should be interpreted descriptively due to the ecological study design. These findings suggest the need for further organisational optimisation and targeted strategies aimed at improving participation in breast cancer screening programmes.Keywords: Breast cancer, Screening coverage, Hospitalisation, Mortality, Ecological study, Public health

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women in Poland and represents a substantial challenge for the national healthcare system [1]. According to data from the Polish National Cancer Registry, more than 20,000 new cases are diagnosed annually, and the number of deaths exceeds 6,000 each year. The increasing incidence, particularly among women of working age, underscores the urgent need for effective preventive strategies and early detection measures. Primary prevention, including educational initiatives and the promotion of healthy lifestyles, as well as secondary prevention through organised screening programmes, constitute key components of breast cancer control strategies. Screening refers to medical interventions aimed at detecting disease at an early, often asymptomatic stage in individuals without clinical manifestations. In the case of breast cancer, mammography remains the most widely used screening modality, enabling the detection of malignant lesions before they become clinically palpable or symptomatic [2].

Hospitalisation and mortality rates due to breast cancer serve as important indicators of the effectiveness of preventive efforts. Their analysis in the context of established risk factors—such as age, genetic predisposition, lifestyle, socioeconomic status, and place of residence—allows for the evaluation of implemented programmes and the identification of areas requiring targeted intervention [3]. Since 2006, Poland has implemented the Population-Based Breast Cancer Early Detection Programme, which provides free mammographic screening to women within specified age groups. Under the current framework, mammography is performed every two years for women aged 45–74 years, and annually for women at increased risk, such as those with a confirmed genetic predisposition. Despite the programme’s availability, its effectiveness remains constrained by suboptimal participation rates, unequal access to healthcare services, and varying levels of health awareness within the population. A comprehensive evaluation of programme effectiveness should therefore consider not only the number of screening examinations performed, but also hospitalisation patterns, stage at diagnosis, and mortality indicators. Trends observed in these domains reflect the overall performance of preventive strategies and help identify potential systemic gaps requiring improvement [3].

The aim of the present study was to assess the effectiveness of preventive programmes and breast cancer screening initiatives implemented in Poland between 2019 and 2023. In particular, the study examines the association between participation in screening programmes and hospitalisation and mortality rates due to breast cancer, taking into account the number of hospital admissions, discharge patterns, and healthcare expenditures related to both prevention and inpatient treatment. The findings may contribute to the optimisation of national health policy and inform future oncological prevention strategies.

The present study does not aim to provide a comprehensive programme evaluation based on the full set of recommended indicators, but rather to explore selected population-level outcome indicators that are consistently available in national administrative datasets.

In this study, the term “screening” refers specifically to the organised, population-based mammography programme implemented in Poland and targeting women within defined age groups (45–74 years during the study period). Opportunistic or diagnostic mammography is not included in this definition.

Materials and methods

Data Sources

The analysis was based on data obtained from three publicly available national sources. Information on the implementation of breast cancer screening programmes was retrieved from the Systemic and Implementation Analyses Database (BASiW; accessed 6 February 2025). Mortality data were obtained from Statistics Poland (Główny Urząd Statystyczny, GUS). Data on treatment and hospitalisations were extracted from the National Health Fund (Narodowy Fundusz Zdrowia, NFZ) through the API Statistics NFZ interface (version 1.1) [4–6].

The integration of data from these sources enabled a comprehensive assessment of the associations between screening participation, healthcare service utilisation, treatment costs, and breast cancer–related mortality.

Study scope and population

The analysis covered the years 2019–2023 and included all 16 voivodeships in Poland. Data were merged using a common analytical key consisting of the voivodeship name and calendar year.

Individuals under 18 years of age were excluded from the analysis. Due to the nature of the available aggregated administrative data, the location of healthcare service provision was used as a proxy for the patient’s place of residence. This assumption was adopted because direct residential address information was not available in the aggregated datasets.

Scope of analysed healthcare services

Healthcare services reported within the Diagnosis-Related Groups (DRG; Polish: Jednorodne Grupy Pacjentów, JGP) catalogue of the National Health Fund were analysed, in particular:

Analytical framework

This study was designed as an exploratory ecological analysis aimed at identifying population-level patterns rather than estimating causal effects. All statistical models were therefore interpreted descriptively, as tools to assess the direction and strength of associations between variables measured at the voivodeship level.

In this framework, variables such as hospitalisations were treated as proxies of healthcare system burden and disease burden, rather than as clearly defined confounders or mediators. Consequently, multivariable models were not intended to estimate independent effects but to explore adjusted associations under different model specifications. To minimise scale-related bias, primary interpretations were based on standardised measures (e.g., rates and coverage), whereas correlations based on absolute counts were treated as descriptive only. The models were not designed to estimate independent effects of individual variables, but rather to assess how associations change under different model specifications.

The analysis of screening participation was restricted to the target population eligible for the organised screening programme. Other analysed variables (e.g., hospitalisations and mortality) refer to the broader population and should therefore be interpreted in relation to screening coverage at the population level.

This set of variables enabled the assessment of associations between screening availability and implementation, healthcare system burden, and population-level health outcomes.

Hospitalisations were conceptualised as a composite proxy reflecting both disease burden and healthcare utilisation. Given this dual role, their inclusion in regression models was exploratory and should not be interpreted as representing a clearly defined causal pathway (e.g., confounding or mediation).

The selection of indicators was primarily driven by data availability and comparability across all voivodeships and years. The analysis focused on screening coverage, hospitalisations, mortality, and healthcare costs as key population-level indicators reflecting programme reach, healthcare system burden, and outcomes.

Other recommended indicators of screening effectiveness, such as cancer detection rates, interval cancers, positive predictive value, or stage at diagnosis, were not included due to lack of access to individual-level clinical or screening registry data.

As a result, the study provides a partial, outcome-oriented perspective on programme performance rather than a comprehensive evaluation based on the full set of recommended indicators.

Statistical analysis

In the first stage, descriptive statistics were applied to characterise the distribution of analysed variables. Pearson correlation coefficients were subsequently calculated to assess the strength and direction of associations between continuous variables. Multivariable linear regression models were employed to evaluate the adjusted association between screening coverage (primary exposure) and the primary outcome: (1) breast cancer mortality in women aged ≥ 65 years (Model 1), and (2) hospitalisations in women aged 41–60 years (Model 2). Adjustment variables (hospitalisations, number of programmes, calendar year) were included to control for potential confounding and are not interpreted substantively.

Results were presented as regression coefficients with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and p-values.

All statistical analyses were conducted using Python 3.12 (libraries: pandas, numpy, scipy, matplotlib, seaborn, statsmodels) and the R statistical environment (regression modelling). The study relied exclusively on aggregated, publicly available data without any personally identifiable information; therefore, ethical approval was not required.

Results

A nationwide analysis of data from 2019 to 2023, covering all 16 voivodeships, revealed marked regional differences in the implementation of mammography screening programmes financed by the National Health Fund (NFZ), the public healthcare payer in Poland.

Over the five-year period (2019–2023), a total of 10,193,232 women were eligible for breast cancer screening across all voivodeships. The highest numbers were recorded in the Masovian and Silesian voivodeships, whereas the lowest were observed in Lubusz Voivodeship and Opole.

On average, approximately 127,400 women per voivodeship per year were eligible for screening. The annual number ranged from nearly 37,000 (Lubusz) to over 378,000 (Masovian). During the study period, an average of 60,800 women per voivodeship per year underwent mammography screening, corresponding to a mean programme coverage rate of 50.9%. The coverage rate varied substantially, ranging from 38.7% (Lesser Poland) to 68.2% (Lubusz) (Fig. 1).

Breast cancer screening coverage by voivodeship, 2019–2023

Regional analysis demonstrated relatively stable patterns of participation in certain voivodeships. High and stable coverage (above 50%) was observed in Lubusz, Pomeranian, and Greater Poland Voivodeships. In contrast, lower participation rates - often below 40% despite some improvement in 2023-were recorded in Lesser Poland, Subcarpathian, and Opole Voivodeships. The greatest fluctuations during the study period were observed in Silesian and Masovian Voivodeships. The highest average annual increases in participation were noted in Subcarpathian (+ 10.48%), Silesian (+ 8.21%), Masovian (+ 8.07%), Kuyavian-Pomeranian (+ 7.14%), and Lesser Poland (+ 6.90%), whereas the lowest was observed in Podlaskie Voivodeship (+ 1.94%). The largest variability in the number of screened women was recorded in Subcarpathian (43.74%), Silesian (38.94%), Opole (36.15%), Lesser Poland (33.23%), and Kuyavian-Pomeranian (33.16%). Analysis of hospitalisations across all 16 voivodeships during 2019–2023 showed that the largest patient group consisted of women aged 61–81 years, with an average of 742 hospitalisations per voivodeship per year (range: 144–2,066). The second largest group comprised women aged 41–60 years (mean 556; range: 94–1,578). Hospitalisations among women under 40 (mean 79) and over 81 years (mean 75) were comparatively infrequent.

Planned admissions predominated (mean 661 per voivodeship annually), whereas emergency admissions were less common (mean 135). The category “other/unspecified” demonstrated substantial heterogeneity (0–2,713 cases).

Regarding discharge mode, referral to further outpatient treatment was most common (mean 891 cases annually), followed by completion of diagnostic or therapeutic procedures (mean 528). Transfers to other inpatient facilities were rare. Discharges at patient request and in-hospital deaths (under J02 and J08 catalogues) averaged fewer than four cases per year.

The highest expenditure category consisted of other healthcare services (mean PLN 1.02 million per voivodeship), with considerable variability (0–over PLN 7.6 million). Expenditures on chemotherapy and blood transfusion were substantially lower, although isolated high maximum values were observed. Overall, an average of 1,452.75 breast cancer–related hospitalisations per voivodeship per year were recorded, with a mean total annual cost of PLN 1.09 million (Table 5).

In some categories (e.g., age groups 18–40 and ≥ 81, and the “other/unspecified” admission mode), zero values were observed in more than 50% of cases, indicating either absence of service provision or data reporting heterogeneity.

A strong positive correlation was observed between the number of hospitalisations and the number of screened women (r = 0.96; p < 0.001). A similar association was identified between hospitalisations and the size of the target population (r = 0.92; p < 0.001), as well as between the number of screened women and the target population (r = 0.96; p < 0.001), indicating a substantial population-size effect. Therefore, these correlations should be interpreted descriptively, as they likely reflect differences in regional population size rather than a direct epidemiological relationship.

Breast cancer mortality was strongly correlated with both the number of screened women (r = 0.99; p < 0.001) and the number of hospitalisations (r = 0.96; p < 0.001). A comparable pattern was observed for the number of women referred for further diagnostic assessment (r = 0.95; p < 0.001).

Model 1: screening coverage and mortality in women aged ≥ 65 years

A multivariable linear regression model was used to examine the association between screening coverage (primary exposure) and breast cancer mortality in women aged ≥ 65 years (primary outcome) at the voivodeship level. Hospitalisations (61–81 age group) and calendar year were included as adjustment variables only.

Higher screening coverage was statistically associated with lower mortality at the population level (B = -1.93; p < 0.001). The remaining model terms were included as adjustment variables and their coefficients are not interpreted substantively.

The model demonstrated a high R² (0.924; adjusted R² = 0.921), which likely reflects structural correlations between aggregated variables rather than true explanatory power. However, given the ecological and exploratory nature of the analysis, these coefficients should be interpreted as reflecting population-level patterns rather than independent or causal effects of individual variables. Included covariates may reflect underlying population-level characteristics rather than independent effects.

This association should be interpreted cautiously, as the model is based on aggregated data and included variables may reflect underlying population structure and healthcare system characteristics rather than distinct causal pathways, Table 6.

Model 2: screening coverage and hospitalisations in women aged 41–60 years

The second regression model assessed the association between screening coverage (primary exposure) and hospitalisations among women aged 41–60 years (primary outcome). The number of preventive programmes and calendar year were included as adjustment variables only.

Screening coverage was not significantly associated with hospitalisations (B = -0.81; p = 0.682). The number of preventive programmes and calendar year were included as adjustment variables; their coefficients are not interpreted.

The model explained approximately 46% of the variance (R² = 0.465; adjusted R² = 0.442), and the overall model was statistically significant (F = 20.3; p < 0.001), Table 7. As in Model 1, these findings should be interpreted descriptively. Statistical analysis revealed a positive association between the completion of therapeutic or diagnostic processes and the number of screened women (r = 0.72; p = 0.002). Discussion

Interpretations of potential mechanisms should be treated as speculative and hypothesis-generating. The analysis of population participation in breast cancer screening programmes in Poland between 2019 and 2023 reveals substantial changes that must be interpreted in the context of hospitalisation and mortality indicators related to this malignancy. The study period encompasses both the disruption associated with the COVID-19 pandemic and the subsequent phase of gradual restoration of healthcare service availability.

The marked decline in the number of screened women in 2020 can largely be attributed to the COVID-19 pandemic. Fear of infection led many women to avoid medical facilities, while lockdown measures and mobility restrictions limited access to screening services. Globally, the pandemic resulted in temporary disruptions of healthcare systems and screening programmes, contributing to diagnostic delays, reduced treatment accessibility, and in some settings, increased diagnoses at advanced stages and higher mortality [7–9].

Additional contributing factors likely included healthcare system overload, the reallocation of medical personnel to COVID-19 care, temporary suspension or limitation of preventive programmes, and reduced public promotion of screening services. Economic uncertainty and concerns about job and income security may also have discouraged participation.

It is widely estimated that screening coverage exceeding 70% is required to achieve optimal population-level effectiveness [10]. The findings of the present study are consistent with those reported by Mularczyk-Tomczewska et al., who demonstrated that pandemic-related disruptions and insufficient communication significantly reduced screening participation in Poland [11]. Similar observations have been reported internationally. Cancino et al. highlighted substantial reductions in screening procedures, including mammography and cervical cytology, in multiple countries due to healthcare system disruption and reduced patient communication [12]. Chen et al. reported that millions fewer screening tests were performed in the United States during the initial months of the pandemic, confirming that both reduced service availability and decreased patient engagement significantly affected participation rates [13].

Lucas et al., analysing screening programmes in Argentina, Bangladesh, Colombia, Morocco, Sri Lanka, and Thailand, demonstrated that these declines were global and affected both high-income and low- and middle-income countries [14]. The convergence of these findings with our results underscores the universal nature of the problem and highlights the need for more resilient communication mechanisms and active patient invitation strategies to mitigate the impact of future systemic crises.

The present study suggests that the effectiveness of screening programmes depends not only on their formal availability but also on the quality of promotional and educational initiatives and the systemic mechanisms encouraging participation. Regions implementing local educational campaigns and personalised reminders (e.g., SMS messages, individual invitation letters) reported higher participation rates. In Scandinavian countries, such strategies have achieved participation levels approaching 90%, whereas their implementation in Poland remains uneven. In regions characterised by higher levels of health literacy and greater trust in healthcare professionals, women appear more likely to participate in screening. Conversely, in some areas, persistent beliefs such as “it is better not to know” may contribute to lower participation rates [15].

The observed regional disparities were substantial. Hospitalisation rates ranged from 1.37 to 8.95 per 1,000 women, while screening coverage varied from 39% (Lesser Poland Voivodeship) to 68% (Lubusz Voivodeship). These differences suggest that accessibility of services, communication strategies, and the organisation of regional healthcare systems differ considerably.

Evidence from other countries supports the effectiveness of well-designed communication campaigns. Schliemann et al., in Malaysia, demonstrated that a multimedia campaign (television, radio, print media, and social media) increased both awareness of breast cancer symptoms and participation in clinical breast examination and mammography compared with a control group [16]. Duarte et al. reported that a randomised letter-based behavioural intervention in Chile led to a relative increase of approximately 167% in uptake of free mammography compared with controls [17]. Roh et al., in a study among American Indian women in the Northern Great Plains, showed that a culturally tailored mobile application increased mammography completion within six months to 42%, compared with 20% in the control group receiving only an informational brochure [18].

However, not all interventions yield consistent effects. Acharya et al., in London, found that SMS reminders incorporating behavioural science elements and animated educational materials did not significantly increase attendance beyond standard reminders [19]. These findings suggest that the effectiveness of communication strategies may depend on local cultural, social, and organisational contexts. Marquez et al. further emphasised that personalised invitations, reminders, logistical facilitation, and tailored communication substantially improve screening participation and should be integral to recommended strategies [20].

The strong positive correlation observed between hospitalisations and mortality suggests that hospital admissions are frequently associated with severe or advanced cases. This association may reflect underlying disease severity or healthcare system factors; however, the direction and nature of this relationship cannot be determined within the present design. If hospitalisations and deaths are closely linked, this may reflect underlying factors related to disease burden or healthcare system characteristics; however, no conclusions regarding underlying mechanisms can be drawn. However, it is important to emphasise that correlation does not imply causation. Both variables may be influenced by common underlying factors, such as disease severity, delayed diagnosis, or socioeconomic determinants.

Our findings are consistent with those of Pereira et al., who reported a strong positive correlation between hospitalisations and breast cancer mortality across age groups (e.g., r = 0.82 for aggregated data). In Poisson regression models, hospitalisation rates remained significantly associated with mortality, whereas mammography participation alone did not demonstrate a significant association [21]. This highlights the relevance of monitoring hospital burden as an indicator of population health status and oncological care performance.

In contrast, some international studies have reported differing patterns. Zhang et al., analysing data from China (2004–2020), observed increasing hospitalisation rates alongside declining in-hospital mortality, while Fernandes et al. reported national trends in Brazil without identifying a direct correlation between hospitalisations and mortality [22, 23]. The observed association between hospitalisation rates and mortality likely reflects underlying disease severity rather than a direct association. Regions with a higher proportion of advanced-stage cases may simultaneously exhibit higher hospitalisation and mortality rates. However, this hypothesis cannot be confirmed without data on stage at diagnosis.

Studies conducted in Saudi Arabia among healthcare professionals revealed limited awareness of breast cancer risk factors and screening importance, accompanied by low willingness to participate in preventive programmes [24]. Similar observations were reported in Hungary [25]. These findings suggest that insufficient education and limited effectiveness of awareness campaigns may represent widespread challenges, affecting not only the general population but also healthcare workers. Strengthening educational initiatives and promoting participation in screening programmes may therefore contribute to earlier detection and reduced healthcare system burden.

The relatively low number of programmes targeting younger populations and limited initiatives directed at broader segments of society indicate untapped potential for primary prevention. Reports from the OECD and European Commission note that despite the expansion of national screening programmes, instruments targeting younger age groups remain marginal [26].

Importantly, the associations observed in this ecological analysis should not be interpreted as evidence of a causal relationship at the individual level. Rather, they reflect population-level patterns that may be influenced by differences in regional healthcare organisation, demographic structure, and disease burden.

The selection of indicators reflects data availability within national administrative datasets and prioritises comparability across regions. Consequently, the findings should be interpreted as a necessarily partial assessment of programme performance rather than a comprehensive evaluation of screening effectiveness.

Regional differences may reflect variation in healthcare infrastructure, accessibility of screening services (including mobile units), and regional organisation of oncology care. However, due to the lack of detailed contextual data, these interpretations remain speculative and should be interpreted with caution.

The study does not include survival analysis, which would provide a more direct measure of programme effectiveness. Future studies should incorporate survival outcomes to better capture regional differences in cancer prognosis.

Potential drivers of regional variation may include differences in the availability of oncology services, uptake of screening invitations, and the organisation of diagnostic pathways. However, these factors could not be directly assessed in the present study and should be interpreted as hypothetical explanations.

These findings should be interpreted within the limitations of ecological analysis and should primarily inform hypothesis generation and health policy considerations.

Limitations

This study was based on aggregated administrative data at the voivodeship level and therefore constitutes an ecological analysis. Consequently, the observed associations cannot be interpreted as causal relationships at the individual level (risk of ecological fallacy). The correlations identified may reflect differences in population size, demographic structure, or regional disease burden rather than a direct effect of screening on hospitalisation or mortality.

Another limitation is the reliance on largely absolute numerical data, which may inflate correlation strength in more populous regions. Although some analyses were standardised to the target population, not all models incorporated fully standardised rates.

The study did not include information on cancer stage at diagnosis, tumour biology, treatment modalities, or time from diagnosis to therapy. These clinical factors may substantially influence hospitalisation risk and mortality and could modify the observed associations.

Hospitalisation and discharge data were derived from NFZ reporting systems, which may involve classification heterogeneity and regional differences in reporting practices. In particular, the “other/unspecified” category exhibited high variability and a large proportion of zero values, limiting the precision of regional comparisons.

Additionally, the location of healthcare service provision was used as a proxy for place of residence, which may result in misclassification in regions with high patient mobility. Potential confounders such as socioeconomic status, educational level, availability of specialised oncology centres, physician workforce density, and regional organisational differences in healthcare delivery were not analysed.

Finally, the observation period was relatively short (five years) and included substantial disruption related to the COVID-19 pandemic. The impact of screening programmes on mortality often manifests over longer time horizons; therefore, the five-year perspective may not fully capture the long-term effects of preventive strategies. In Poland, the temporary suspension and subsequent reorganisation of screening services by the National Health Fund (NFZ) during the COVID-19 pandemic may have contributed to the observed fluctuations in screening coverage. These system-level factors should be considered alongside broader pandemic-related effects.

The very high R² observed in some models may partly reflect structural correlations between variables expressed as absolute counts (e.g., deaths and hospitalisations), rather than true explanatory power. Regression models included variables expressed as absolute counts, which may partly reflect underlying population size rather than epidemiological associations.

According to recent methodological frameworks for cancer screening evaluation, a comprehensive assessment should include a broader set of indicators covering input, process, and outcome dimensions. The present study focuses on a limited subset of outcome indicators, which constrains the ability to fully assess programme effectiveness.

In particular, the absence of indicators such as cancer detection rates, interval cancers, or stage distribution limits the interpretation of screening performance and may obscure important aspects of programme quality.

Conclusions

Between 2019 and 2023, breast cancer screening coverage in Poland averaged approximately 51% and remained substantially differentiated across regions, failing to reach the level considered optimal for maximal population-level effectiveness.

Policy implications

The findings suggest the need to strengthen strategies aimed at increasing participation in screening programmes, particularly through systematic personalised invitations, expansion of mobile diagnostic units, and organisational standardisation in regions with the lowest coverage rates.

Future analyses should incorporate standardised indicators and individual-level data to enable a more comprehensive assessment of the long-term impact of breast cancer screening on mortality outcomes.

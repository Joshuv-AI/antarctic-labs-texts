The intersection of three quadrics in ℙ 7 \mathbb{P}^{7} revisited
R. Donagi, T. Pantev

Abstract




1
Introduction

In this paper we analyze certain additive invariants of the sheaf theory of the natural gerbe associated with a very general complete intersection of three quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
. Sheaf theories of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes are very interesting for a variety of reasons, e.g. they contain essential information about the complexity of unramified Brauer classes, serve as a common playground for geometric constructions of representations and, most pertinently for us, provide standard examples of non-commutative spaces (
nc
-spaces for short).

In the categorical approach to non-commutative algebraic geometry over the complex numbers (see
[
KKP08
,
Orl18
,
Kon21
]
and references therein) one studies cocomplete triangulated
\mathbb{C}
-linear dg categories. These categories are viewed as dg versions of the categories of quasi-coherent sheaves on the putative
nc
-spaces and, since in general a direct independent description of such spaces is not available, they are taken as the primary geometric objects. The categorical point of view towards
nc
-geometry allows for unified treatment of complex and symplectic manifolds, as well as quantized and stacky geometries. Geometric properties, such as being algebraic, smooth,
proper, or Calabi-Yau, are notions intrinsic to
nc
-spaces. Moreover the cohomology groups of a smooth and proper
nc
-space are enhanced with additional algebraic data, namely the data comprising a pure
nc
-Hodge structure
[
KKP08
]
.

Classical algebraic geometry fits within this formalism, as a classical scheme or an algebraic stack
Y
over
\mathbb{C}
can be regarded as an
nc
-space for which the corresponding category is
D_{\mathsf{qcoh}}(Y)
- a dg enhancement of the quasi-coherent derived category of
Y
. There are many other examples of
nc
-spaces coming from geometry or physics. A brief and incomplete list includes Fukaya categories of symplectic manifolds, matrix factorization categories, Fukaya-Seidel categories of a potential, and
categories of boundary conditions of topological quantum field theories with
N=2
susy. The properties and structures of an
nc
-space corresponding to a cocomplete triangulated
\mathbb{C}
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
are captured in the subcategory
\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{C})
of compact objects in
\mathcal{C}
. For a classical quasi-separated scheme
Y/\mathbb{C}
this notation is consistent with the standard notation, i.e.

\mathsf{Perf}(D_{\mathsf{qcoh}}(Y))=\mathsf{Perf}(Y)
, where
\mathsf{Perf}(Y)
is the full subcategory of objects locally quasi-isomorphic to finite complexes of locally free sheaves.

An important class of
nc
-spaces is comprised of the
nc
-spaces of
geometric origin

[
Orl18
]
. By definition these are given by the categories
\mathcal{C}
for which
\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{C})
admits an embedding as an admissible subcategory in the category
\mathsf{Perf}(Y)
for some scheme
Y/\mathbb{C}
. The
nc
-spaces of geometric origin are particularly nice because they are stable under natural noncommutative splitting and glueing constructions
[
Orl18
,
Per19
]
, and because their homological invariants are much more tractable than those of general
nc
-spaces
[
Per22
,
HP24
]
. Kuznetsov components of Fano manifolds, residual categories of spherical functors
[
Kuz19
,
KP21
]
, and derived categories of twisted sheaves on smooth projective varieties all provide important examples of
nc
-spaces of geometric origin.

These examples are of special interest, since they are expected to give new birational invariants of smooth projective varieties
[
Kat10
,
IKP14
,
Kuz16
]
.
In particular, it is expected that truly non-commutative
nc
-spaces of geometric origin will be birational invariants of the projective varieties through which they are realized geometrically. While general Kuznetsov components and residual categories are frequently distinguished from smooth projective varieties by standard Hodge theoretic invariants, this is less common and harder when it comes to
nc
-spaces associated to algebraic

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes, i.e.
nc
-spaces corresponding to categories of twisted sheaves.

To illustrate this point, recall that for any smooth projective variety
X/\mathbb{C}
, and any
unramified Brauer class
\alpha\in\mathsf{Br}(X)
, we have a dg category
D^{b}(X,\alpha)
of
\alpha
-twisted coherent sheaves, or equivalently of
\alpha
-twisted perfect complexes. This category embeds as an admissible subcategory in the Brauer-Severi variety of
\alpha
and so corresponds to a
nc
-space
{}_{\alpha}X
of geometric origin. Unfortunately, it can happen that the
nc
-space
{}_{\alpha}X
is secretly commutative, even when the Brauer class
\alpha\in\mathsf{Br}(X)
is non-trivial. Indeed, suppose that

X
is a smooth projective K3 equipped with a Weierstrass elliptic fibration. In this case one has a canonical identification
\mathsf{Br}(X)={\mathcyr{Sh}}(X)
of the Brauer group of
X
with the algebraic Tate-Shafarevich group of the given elliptic fibration
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
. Furthermore, each element
\alpha\in{\mathcyr{Sh}}(X)
is realized by a smooth projective K3
X_{\alpha}
which is fibered by genus one curves and has Jacobian fibration isomorphic to
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
. The Poincaré sheaf on the fiber product
{}_{\alpha}X\times_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}X_{\alpha}
induces an equivalence
of categories
D^{b}(X,\alpha)\cong D^{b}(X_{\alpha})

[
DP08
]
and so the non-commutative space
{}_{\alpha}X
is isomorphic to the classical K3
X_{\alpha}
.
This phenomenon shows that the question of recognizing whether an
nc
-space corresponding to a category of twisted sheaves is truly noncommutative is quite delicate.

A method for distinguishing such
nc
-spaces from commutative ones was proposed
by Kuznetsov in
[
Kuz10
]
. It boils down to checking that the numerical
K
-lattice
(K_{\mathsf{num}}(Z),\chi_{Z})
of a geometric
nc
-space
Z
can not be isomorphic to the numerical
K
-lattice of a smooth projective variety by checking that the lattice
K_{\mathsf{num}}(Z)
cannot contain a class that behaves like the class of a point. In the same paper, Kuznetsov used this method to show that the category of twisted sheaves associated with a very general four dimensional cubic containing a plane is truly non-commutative.

In this paper we use Kuznetsov’s method to show that the natural
nc
-space attached to an intersection of three quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
is truly non-commutative. More precisely, when
X
is a general smooth complete intersection of three quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
, the derived category of
X
has
[
Xie23
]
a semiorthogonal decomposition

D^{b}(X)=\left\langle\,D^{b}(S,\alpha),\,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\,\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\,\right\rangle,

where
S
is a surface of general type, which is a double cover of the plane branched along a smooth octic and
\alpha
is a
2
-torsion Brauer class on
S
. In order to analyze the properties of
D^{b}(S,\alpha)
, we consider
the lattice

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}:=\left(\,H(S,B;\mathbb{Z}),\,\left\langle\,\sqrt{td(S)}\cdot(-),\,\sqrt{td(S)}\cdot(-)\right\rangle\,\right),

where
B\in H^{2}(S,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})
is a class such that
\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}B)=\alpha\in H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}^{\times})
,
H(S,B;\mathbb{Z})=\exp(B)(\oplus\,H^{p,p}(S,\mathbb{Q}))\,\cap\,H^{\bullet}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, and

\langle\,-\,,\,-\,\rangle
is the Mukai pairing on
S
. Up to isomorphism the lattice
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
depends only on
(S,\alpha)
but not on
B
, and we check in Corollary
4.4
that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
can not be isomorphic to the Mukai lattice of integral Hodge classes of any smooth projective surface.
An immediate consequence is the following

Theorem
4.5

Let
X

be a very general intersection of
3
quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
, and let

(S,\alpha)
be the associated surface of general type with a
2
-torsion Brauer class. Then the associated numerical
K
-lattice
(K_{\operatorname{num}}(S,\alpha),\chi_{(S,\alpha)})
is not isomorphic to
(K_{\operatorname{num}}(M),\chi_{M})
for any smooth projective surface
M
. In particular
D^{b}(S,\alpha)
can not be equivalent to the derived category of any smooth projective surface.

The fact that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
is not isomorphic to the Mukai lattice of Hodge classes of a smooth projective surface
has applications in birational geometry.
The theory of Hodge atoms developed in
[
KKPY25a
,
KKPY25b
]
implies that the
\alpha
-twisted
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of
S
, viewed as a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded Hodge structure, together with its Mukai pairing gives an enhanced Hodge atom
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(X)
in the atomic decomposition of
X
. If
X
is very general, so that
\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\mathbb{Z}\mathsf{h}
, then one can check
[
KKPY25b
]
that the integral lattice of Hodge classes of the atom
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(X)
is isomorphic to the lattice

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
. Since
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
can not be the lattice of Hodge classes of any enhanced atom of a surface, the non-rationality criterion
[
KKPY25a
, Proposition 5.21]
implies the following

Corollary.

The very general intersection of three quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
is not rational.

This corollary gives a new proof of the non-rationality result of Hassett-Pirutka-Tschinkel
[
HPT18
]
.

2
Geometric setup

Consider a smooth Fano fourfold
X
which is a complete intersection of three quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
. In other words
X:=\cap_{t\in\mathbf{A}}Q_{t}
is the base locus of a general net of quadrics
Q_{t}\subset\mathbb{P}^{7}
, where
\mathbf{A}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
denotes the parameter space of the net. We will assume that

\mathbf{A}\subset\left|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{7}}(2)\right|
is sufficiently general so that the singular quadrics in this net all have corank one, and the discriminant curve
\mathfrak{d}\subset\mathbf{A}
of the net will be a smooth curve of degree
8
.

2.1
Birational fibration by quadric surfaces

The complete intersection
X
always contains lines and we fix a general line
\ell\subset X
which does not intersect the curve in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
which is the union of singularities of the singular quadrics of the net.

Consider the incidence variety

Y:=\left\{(t,\Pi)\,\left|\,\ell\subset\Pi\subset Q_{t}\,\right.\right\}\subset\mathbf{A}\times\mathsf{Gr}(3,8).

This
Y
fibers over
\mathbf{A}
. A general point
t\in\mathbf{A}
determines first a
5
-dimensional projective subspace denoted
\mathsf{P}_{t}
:

\mathsf{P}_{t}:=\cap_{x\in\ell}T_{Q_{t},x}\subset\mathbb{P}^{7},

then a
3
dimensional subquotient

\mathsf{p}_{t}:=\mathsf{P}_{t}/\ell,

and finally a quadric surface

\mathsf{q}_{t}\subset\mathsf{p}_{t},\quad\mathsf{q}_{t}:=(Q_{t}\cap\mathsf{P}_{t})/\ell.

The incidence variety
Y
fibers over
\mathbf{A}
, and the fiber over a general
t\in\mathbf{A}
is isomorphic to the quadric surface
\mathsf{q}_{t}\subset\mathsf{p}_{t}
.

Furthermore, there is a birational isomorphism
a:Y\to X
from our
Y
back to
X
. Starting with the pair
(t,\Pi)
, the intersection of
\Pi
with a quadric
Q_{s}
depends only on the image
\overline{s}
of
s
in the quotient line
\mathbf{A}/t
, and this intersection consists of a pair of lines: the fixed
\ell
plus a variable line
\ell_{\overline{s}}
. The lines
\left\{\ell_{\overline{s}}\right\}_{\overline{s}\in\mathbf{A}/t}
form a (linear) pencil of lines in
\Pi
, so they have a unique intersection point
a(t,\Pi)\in\cap_{s\in\mathbf{A}}Q_{s}=X
. The inverse of
a

is given by
a^{-1}(x)=(t,\Pi)
, where
\Pi
is the span of
x
and
\ell
, and
t
is the unique point such that
\Pi\subset Q_{t}
.

Note that our genericity assumptions on
\mathbf{A}
and
\ell
imply that
Y
is smooth, and that the discriminat curve for the family

Y\to\mathbf{A}
of quadric surfaces
\{\mathsf{q}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbf{A}}
is the same as the discriminant curve for the family

\mathcal{Q}\to A
of six dimensional quadrics
\{Q_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbf{A}}
. In fact, if we start with
\ell\subset Q_{t}
for a smooth quadric
Q_{t}
, then the resulting
\mathsf{q}_{t}
is also smooth. Conversely, if
Q_{t}
is singular and
\ell
does not intersect its singular locus, then
\mathsf{q}_{t}
is singular with the same singularity type. The family of two-dimensional quadrics
Y\to\mathbf{A}
is the so-called hyperbolic reduction
[
Kuz24
,
Xie23
]
of the family of six-dimensional quadrics
\mathcal{Q}\to\mathbf{A}
with respect to the isotropic family of lines
\ell\times\mathbf{A}\subset\mathcal{Q}
. Families related by hyperbolic reduction are known to share many invariants. In particular, it is known
[
Kuz24
,
Xie23
]
that their discriminant loci are isomorphic (as we saw in our example above) and that they have common pieces in the semiorthogonal decompositions in their derived categories.

For future reference, note that under our genericity assumptions, the total space
\mathcal{Q}
of the family of six-dimensional quadrics over
\mathbf{A}
is a divisor of bidegree
(1,2)
in
A\times\mathbb{P}^{7}
. We can think of this as a family of
8\times 8
symmetric matrices parametrized by
\mathbf{A}
, with entries linear forms on
\mathbf{A}
. The discriminant divisor
\mathfrak{d}\subset\mathbf{A}
of the map
\mathcal{Q}\to\mathbf{A}
is given by the determinant of the family, which is a polynomial
\boldsymbol{\delta}
of degree
8
on
\mathbf{A}
.

To do the same calculation from the point of view of the two-dimensional quadrics
\mathsf{q}_{t}
, we have to first understand the family of three-dimensional projective spaces
\mathsf{p}_{t}
. This is the projectivization of a rank
4
vector bundle
E
on
\mathbf{A}
and again our
Y
is a smooth divisor on
\mathbb{P}(E)
, which after appropriately normalizing
E
by twisting it by a line bundle on
\mathbf{A}
, will be in the linear system
p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(1)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{E}(2)
, where
\mathcal{O}_{E}(1)
is the relative hyperplane bundle on
p:\mathbb{P}(E)\to\mathbf{A}
that is characterized by the property
p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}(1)=E^{\vee}
. In these terms, the family of two-dimensional quadrics is a (symmetric) map
\sigma:E\to E^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(1)
and so the discriminant curve is a section in
\det(E)^{\otimes-2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(4)
. In particular we must have

\det(E)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(-2)
. We can describe the bundle
E
somewhat more concretely. For ease of reference, we record the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a general line
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{A}
, we have

E|_{\mathbf{b}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\oplus 2}
.

Proof:
Our
\mathbb{P}^{7}
is a projectivization of an
8
-dimensional vector space
V
, and
\ell
is the projectivization of a
2
-dimensional subspace
L\subset V
. The family
\sigma
of
8\times 8
symmetric matrices (parametrized by
\mathbf{A}
, with entries linear forms on
\mathbf{A}
) all vanish on
L
. If we take the first two coordinates on
V
to be the coordinates on
L
(i.e.
L
is given by vanishing of the remaining
6
coordinates), we see that the upper left
2\times 2
corner of these matrices vanishes identically, and then the lower left
2\times 6
block gives the equations of the fibers of
E
inside the fixed vector space
V/L
. In other words,
E
fits in a short exact sequence:

0\to E\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\oplus 6}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(1)^{\oplus 2}\to 0

of vector bundles on
\mathbf{A}
. In particular, the determinant of
E
is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(-2)
.

Now note that the restriction of
E
to a line
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{A}
will be isomorphic to

\oplus_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(a_{i})
, and since
E|_{\mathbf{b}}
is a subbundle in the trivial bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\oplus 6}
, we must have
a_{i}\leq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{4}a_{i}=-2
. Thus

E|_{\mathbf{b}}
has to be either
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\oplus 3}
or

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\oplus 2}
. These two are distinguished by the value of
h^{0}(\mathbf{b},E)
.
But
H^{0}(\mathbf{b},E)
is the set of solutions of
2\times 2=4
linear equations in
6
variables, so for generic
\ell
and
\mathbf{b}
it is
2
dimensional, and so the splitting type of
E
is
E|_{\mathbf{b}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\oplus 2}
.
\Box

2.2
The
2
-torsion gerbe

Consider next the double cover
\pi:S\to\mathbf{A}
parametrizing the rulings on fibers of the quadric surface fibration
Y\to\mathbf{A}
. By construction
S
is a smooth surface which is the double cover of
\mathbf{A}
branched at the smooth octic curve
\mathfrak{d}\subset\mathbf{A}
, and the universal ruling of
Y\to\mathbf{A}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\mathscr{R}\to S
, i.e. a Brauer-Severi variety over
S
. Let
\alpha\in H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}^{\times})
be the class of
\mathscr{R}
in the Brauer group
H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}^{\times})
of
S
. By definition,
\alpha
is a
2
-torsion element in
H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}^{\times})
which can be viewed also as the class of a sheaf
\mathcal{A}\to S
of rank
4
Azumaya algebras over
S
. Note that for a very general choice of
\mathbf{A}\subset|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{7}}(2)|
, the surface
S
will be Noether-Lefschetz general and so we will have
\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\mathbb{Z}
, generated by the hyperplane line bundle
\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(1)
. We will write

\mathsf{h}=\pi^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(1))\in H^{1,1}(S)\cap H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
for the corresponding hyperplane class. Note that by construction this
\mathsf{h}
has self intersection
\mathsf{h}^{2}=2
. Furthermore
S
is a very ample divisor in the total space of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(4)
, and so, by the Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem for quasi-projective varieties
[
HT85
,
GM88
]
, it will be simply connected. In particular, we have
h^{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})=h^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})=0
. Therefore from the exponential sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}_{S}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}(-))}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}^{\times}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

we conclude that we can find a class
\beta\in H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
such that
\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}\beta)=\alpha
. But
\alpha
is
2
-trorsion, and so we can find a class
B\in H^{2}(S,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})
, such that
\beta
is the image of
B\in H^{2}(S,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Q})
under the map
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
induced from
\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{S}
.

Let us choose a class
B\in H^{2}(S,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})
such that
\exp(2\pi iB)=\alpha
.
In general, the class
B
itself is not unique: the different choices of
B
are a coset under the subgroup
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})+H^{1,1}(S,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{2}(S,\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})
. Still, certain numerical invariants and numerical properties of
B
are independent of the choice of an element in the coset and depend just on
\alpha
. Below we will explore these invariants in detail.

Let
\{t\}:=t-\lfloor t\rfloor
denote the fractional part of
t\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then it is easy to see
[
Kuz10
, Lemma 6.1]
that the fractional part
\{B\mathsf{h}\}
does not depend on the choice of
B
. Indeed if
u\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, then
(B+u)\mathsf{h}=B\mathsf{h}+u\mathsf{h}
, and since
u\mathsf{h}
is an integer, we have

\{(B+u)\mathsf{h}\}=\{B\mathsf{h}\}
. Similarly
\{(B+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{h})\mathsf{h}\}=\{B\mathsf{h}+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{h}^{2}\}=\{B\mathsf{h}+1\}=\{B\mathsf{h}\}
. In fact
\{B\mathsf{h}\}
can be computed in terms of the geometry of the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\mathscr{R}\to S
defining
\alpha
. The following is
[
Kuz10
, Lemma 6.2]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
C\subset S
be a smooth curve in the linear system
|\mathsf{h}|
. Then
\mathscr{R}|_{C}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(W)
for some rank two vector bundle
W
on
C
, and

\{B\mathsf{h}\}=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\deg W\right\},

for any such
W
.

From here we get an immediate

Corollary 2.3
.

We have
\{B\mathsf{h}\}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\{B^{2}\}
is independent of the choice of
B
.

Proof.
This is an adaptation of the analysis in
[
Kuz10
, Lemma 6.4]
to our setting.

Let
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{A}
be a line transversal to the degree
8
curve
\mathfrak{d}\subset\mathbf{A}
. Then
C=\pi^{*}\mathbf{b}
is a smooth hyperelliptic curve of genus
3
in the linear system
|\mathsf{h}|
. Abusing notation, we will still write
\pi:C\to\mathbf{b}
for the covering map. By definition
\mathscr{R}|_{C}
is the Hilbert scheme parametrizing lines in the quadrics
\mathsf{q}_{t}
,
t\in\mathbf{b}
. We will write
E_{\mathbf{b}}
,
Y_{\mathbf{b}}
for the restrictions
E|_{\mathbf{b}}
,
Y|_{\mathbf{b}}
, etc. As before, the restricted family
Y_{\mathbf{b}}
is a smooth
3
-fold fibered by quadric surfaces over the line
\mathbf{b}
. It is a smooth divisor on

\mathbb{P}(E_{\mathbf{b}})
in the linear system
\left|\mathcal{O}_{E_{\mathbf{b}}}(2)\otimes p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(1)\right|
, given by a section

\sigma_{\mathbf{b}}\in H^{0}(\mathbf{b},\operatorname{Sym}^{2}E_{\mathbf{b}}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(1))=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(E_{\mathbf{b}}),\mathcal{O}_{E_{\mathbf{b}}}(2)\otimes p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(1))
.

By Lemma
2.1
we have that
E_{\mathbf{b}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(-1)^{\oplus 2}
, and so we can consider
the rank
3
subbundle
F:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{b}}(-1)\subset E_{\mathbf{b}}
. Then
\mathbb{P}(F)\subset\mathbb{P}(E_{\mathbf{b}})
is a family of hyperplanes in the fibers of
\mathbb{P}(E_{\mathbf{b}})
and so intersects
Y_{\mathbf{b}}
in a surface
\mathfrak{C}
which fibers over
\mathbf{b}
with fiber over
t\in\mathbf{b}
a conic
\mathfrak{C}_{t}=\mathsf{q}_{t}\cap\mathbb{P}(F_{t})\subset\mathsf{p}_{t}=\mathbb{P}(E_{t})
. For general
\mathbf{A}
,
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{A}
, the surface
\mathfrak{C}

ch^{B}(\mathsf{W})=2+(2B+d\mathsf{h})+s\mathsf{pt}

for some integers
d,s\in\mathbb{Z}
. (Note: these are not the same as
d,s
in Lemma
4.1
, where
s
was rational.) Also by the basic properties
[
HS05
]
of
ch^{B}
we will have

ch^{-B}(\mathsf{W}^{\vee})=2-(2B+d\mathsf{h})+s\mathsf{pt},

and

\displaystyle ch(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=ch^{-B}(\mathsf{W}^{\vee})ch^{B}(\mathsf{W})

\displaystyle=(2+(2B+d\mathsf{h})+s\mathsf{pt})\cdot(2-(2B+d\mathsf{h})+s\mathsf{pt})

\displaystyle=4+0+(4s-(2B+d\mathsf{h})^{2})\mathsf{pt}.

Write
a=4s-(2B+d\mathsf{h})^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
since we computed

td(S)=1-\frac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{h}+4\,\mathsf{pt},

we get

ch(\mathcal{A})td(S)=4-2\,\mathsf{h}+(16+a)\,\mathsf{pt},

and hence

\pi_{*}\left(ch(\mathcal{A})td(S)\right)=8-4\,\mathsf{H}+(16+a)\,\mathsf{pt},

where
\mathsf{H}\in H^{2}(\mathbf{A},\mathbb{Z})
is the hyperplane class on
\mathbf{A}
.

Also, as a sheaf we have

\pi_{*}\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}\oplus\wedge^{2}E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(-1)\oplus\wedge^{4}E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(-2).

From the short exact sequence

0\to E\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\oplus 6}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(1)^{\oplus 2}\to 0

we compute

\wedge^{4}E=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(-2),\qquad c_{1}(E)=-2\,\mathsf{H},\ ch_{2}(E)=-\mathsf{pt}.

Since

ch(\wedge^{2}E)=6+3c_{1}(E)+\left(2ch_{2}(E)+\frac{c_{1}(E)^{2}}{2}\right)\,\mathsf{pt},

we get

ch(\wedge^{2}E)=6-6\,\mathsf{H}+0\,\mathsf{pt},

and hence

\displaystyle ch(\wedge^{2}E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(-1))

\displaystyle=(6-6\,\mathsf{H}+0\,\mathsf{pt})\cdot\left(1-\mathsf{H}+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{pt}\right)

\displaystyle=6-12\,\mathsf{H}+9\,\mathsf{pt}.

Similarly

ch(\mathcal{O}_{A}(-4))=1-4\,\mathsf{H}+8\,\mathsf{pt},

and so

\displaystyle ch(\pi_{*}\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=ch(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}})+ch(\wedge^{2}E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}}(-1))+ch(\mathcal{O}_{A}(-4))

\displaystyle=8-16\,\mathsf{H}+17\,\mathsf{pt}.

Since
\mathbf{A}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
we have

td(\mathbf{A})=1+\frac{3}{2}\,\mathsf{H}+\mathsf{pt},

and thus

ch(\pi_{*}\mathcal{A})td(\mathbf{A})=8-4\,\mathsf{H}+pt.

Therefore by Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch we get

16+a=1

or

4s-(2B+d\,\mathsf{h})^{2}+16=1.

But

(2B+d\,\mathsf{h})^{2}=4B^{2}+4d\,B\mathsf{h}+2d^{2}=4B^{2}+2d(d+2B\mathsf{h}).

But
2B\mathsf{h}
is odd, by Corollary
2.3
, and so
2d(d+B\mathsf{h})
is divisible by
4
. In other words

4B^{2}=-1
modulo
4
, and so
\{B^{2}\}=\frac{3}{4}
.
\Box

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose that
\{B\mathsf{h}\}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\{B^{2}\}=\frac{3}{4}
. Then there are no elements
\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2}\in K_{\operatorname{num}}(S,\alpha)
with
\chi_{(S,\alpha)}(\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2})=1
and
\chi_{(S,\alpha)}(\mathsf{v}_{2},\mathsf{v}_{2})=0
.

Proof.
By Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
the numerical
K
-lattice
K_{\operatorname{num}}(S,\alpha)
is a finite index sublattice in the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 3}
with a pairing given by the Gram matrix (
2
).
Thus it suffices to show that there are no elements
\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 3}
for which

\mathsf{v}_{1}^{T}\mathbb{G}\mathsf{v}_{2}=1,\qquad\text{and}\ \mathsf{v}_{2}^{T}\mathbb{G}\mathsf{v}_{2}=0.

Let
\mathsf{v}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 3}
. The condition

\mathsf{v}_{2}^{T}\mathbb{G}\mathsf{v}_{2}=0
reads

(16-4B^{2})x^{2}-2y^{2}-4B\mathsf{h}xy+4xz=0.

Reducing mod 4, this becomes

x^{2}-2y^{2}-2xy=0\ \text{mod}\ 4.

Here all terms are even except for the first term, so
x
must be even. But then all terms are divisible by 4 except for the second term, so
y
too must be even.
But then
\mathbb{G}\mathsf{v}_{2}
is even. This contradicts the requirement that
\mathsf{v}_{1}^{T}\mathbb{G}\mathsf{v}_{2}=1

This completes the proof of the corollary.
\Box

Putting all of this together we get the following

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a very general intersection of
3
-quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
, and let

(S,\alpha)
be the associated surface of general type with a
2
-torsion Brauer class. Then the associated numerical
K
-lattice
(K_{\operatorname{num}}(S,\alpha),\chi_{(S,\alpha)})
is not isomorphic to
(K_{\operatorname{num}}(M),\chi_{M})
for any smooth projective surface
M
. In particular
D^{b}(S,\alpha)
can not be equivalent to the derived category of any smooth projective surface.

Proof:
If
M
is a smooth projective surface and
x\in M
, then
we have
\chi_{M}([\mathcal{O}_{M}],[\mathcal{O}_{x}])=1
, and
\chi_{M}([\mathcal{O}_{x}],[\mathcal{O}_{x}])=0
. But by Lemma
4.3
, Corollary
2.3
, and
Corollary
4.4
there are no elements
\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2}\in K_{\operatorname{num}}(S,\alpha)
, satisfying
\chi_{(S,\alpha)}(\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2})=1
, and
\chi_{(S,\alpha)}(\mathsf{v}_{2},\mathsf{v}_{2})=2
. Thus
(K_{\operatorname{num}}(S,\alpha),\chi_{(S,\alpha)})\ncong(K_{\operatorname{num}}(M),\chi_{M})
, and
D^{b}(S,\alpha)\ncong D^{b}(M)
.
\Box

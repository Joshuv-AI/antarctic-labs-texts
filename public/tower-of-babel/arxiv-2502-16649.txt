Attractors for Singular-Degenerate Porous Medium Type Equations Arising in Models for Biofilm Growth Thanks: Email: stefanie.sonner@ru.nl
Zehra Sen

Abstract
We investigate the long-time behaviour of solutions of a class of singular-degenerate porous medium type equations in bounded Lipschitz domains with mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions. The existence of global attractors is shown under very general assumptions. Assuming, in addition, that solutions are globally Hölder continuous and the reaction terms satisfy a suitable sign condition in the vicinity of the degeneracy, we also prove the existence of an exponential attractor, which, in turn, yields the finite fractal dimension of the global attractor. Moreover, we extend the results for scalar equations to systems where the degenerate equation is coupled to a semilinear reaction-diffusion equation. The study of such systems is motivated by models for biofilm growth.



Keywords:
Quasilinear degenerate reaction diffusion system,
Slow/fast diffusion,
Mixed boundary condition, Attractor, Fractal dimension, Biofilm

1
Introduction

1.1
Singular-degenerate porous medium type equations

We investigate the long-time behaviour, in terms of global and exponential attractors, of a class of second-order quasilinear degenerate parabolic equations considered in
[
21
]
. The problems are of the form

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta\phi(u)+f(\cdot,u)\ \ \text{in}\ \ (0,T]\times\Omega,

(1.1)

complemented with a suitable initial data and mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions, where
T>0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 1
, is a bounded Lipschitz domain. The solution
u
assumes its values in the interval
(-1,1)
,
f:\Omega\times(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and
\phi:(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly increasing with a degeneracy
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
and singularities
\phi(\pm 1)=\pm\infty
. Since the degeneracy in
u=0
is of the same type as in the porous medium equation,
u_{t}=\Delta u^{m},m>1
, we call (
1.1
) a singular-degenerate equation of porous medium type. Porous medium type equations are used to model various natural phenomena such as the flow of an ideal gas through a porous medium (
[
28
]
), nonlinear heat transfer (
[
34
]
) and the spreading of certain biological populations (
[
18
]
).

We will also consider the following system, where the equation (
1.1
) is coupled to a semilinear reaction-diffusion equation,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=\Delta\phi(u)+f(\cdot,u,v),\\
v_{t}=\Delta v+g(\cdot,u,v),\end{array}\right.\ \ \text{in}\ (0,T]\times\Omega,

(1.2)

equipped with suitable initial values and mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary data. Such systems arise in models for the growth of bacterial biofilms.
Biofilms are dense aggregations of microorganisms that form in moist environments. In a biofilm, cells are encased in a slimy matrix of extracellular polymeric substances and adhere to one another and usually to surfaces. A reaction-diffusion model that predicts heterogeneous spatial structures of fully grown biofilms on the mesoscale was introduced and numerically studied by Eberl et al. in
[
7
]
.
It is a system of a quasilinear reaction-diffusion equation for the biomass density
M
and a semilinear reaction-diffusion equation for the nutrient concentration
C
. The equations are coupled via Monod reaction functions modeling biomass production and nutrient consumption. For the dimensionless variables
C
and
M
, which are normalized, respectively, with respect to the bulk concentration and the maximum biomass density, the model reads as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}M=d_{2}\nabla\cdot(D(M)\nabla M)-K_{2}M+K_{3}\frac{CM}{K_{4}+C},\\
\partial_{t}C=d_{1}\Delta C-K_{1}\frac{CM}{K_{4}+C}\end{array}\right.\ \text{in}\ (0,T]\times\Omega,

(1.3)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n=1,2,3
, is a bounded domain and
d_{1},d_{2}>0
,
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}\geq 0
, and
K_{4}>0
are given constants. The subregion

\displaystyle\Omega_{M}(t)=\{x\in\Omega:M(t,x)>0\}

defines the actual biofilm situated on the surface, which typically corresponds to the bottom part of the boundary
\partial\Omega
. The biofilm diffusion coefficient
D(M)
is given by

D(M)=\frac{M^{b}}{(1-M)^{a}},\quad a\geq 1,b>0,

which has a degeneracy of porous medium type at
M=0
. It ensures a finite speed of interface propagation and a sharp interface between the biofilm and the surrounding liquid. On the other hand, the singularity at
M=1
implies that the spatial spreading becomes very large whenever
M
approaches values close to
1
. As a consequence, the biomass density remains bounded by its maximum value despite the growth terms in the equation. It is important to note that by setting

\phi(u)=\int\limits_{0}^{u}\frac{z^{b}}{(1-z)^{a}}dz,

we observe that the biofilm-growth model (
1.3
) is a particular case of the system
(
1.2
) that we consider. Moreover, in simulation studies,
\Omega
is typically rectangular and mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions are imposed. More specifically,
homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions for
C
and
M
are assumed on the bottom part of the boundary, due to the impermeability of the surface to nutrients and biomass, and also on the lateral boundaries. Nutrients are added through the top boundary, which is described by Dirichlet boundary conditions for
C
, while homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed for
M
. Initially, there are small pockets of biomass of density
M_{0}<1

on the bottom part of the boundary, resembling the initial biofilm colonies on the surface, while
M_{0}
vanishes everywhere else in the domain. The nutrient concentration
C_{0}
is set to the bulk concentration everywhere. Therefore, the results of this paper apply to the biofilm growth model (
1.3
) with such initial data and mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions on bounded Lipschitz domains.

1.2
Global and exponential attractors: previous results

The long-time behaviour of a large class of dissipative evolution PDEs can be investigated in terms of global attractors. Global attractors are compact invariant sets of the phase space that attract all bounded subsets under the temporal evolution of the generated semigroup. The global attractor is unique and the minimal compact attracting set. Moreover, for many problems on bounded domains, the (Hausdorff and fractal) dimension of the global attractor is finite (see, e.g.,
[
19
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
13
]
). However, global attractors have some drawbacks. For instance, the rate of attraction can be very slow (
[
24
]
), global attractors can be sensitive to perturbations (
[
30
]
), and in some situations, they cannot capture important transient behaviours (
[
32
]
). To overcome these drawbacks, Eden et al.
[
9
]
suggested to consider larger objects, namely, exponential attractors, which are compact positively invariant sets that contain the global attractor, have finite fractal dimension, and attract all bounded sets at an exponential rate. Exponential attractors are more robust under perturbations than global attractors due to the exponential rate of attraction (see, for instance,
[
26
]
). Moreover, the existence of an exponential attractor implies the existence and finite fractal dimension of the global attractor. On the other hand, exponential attractors are only positively invariant and therefore not unique. Hence, there are different methods to construct an exponential attractor.

The first existence proof for exponential attractors in
[
9
]
was non-constructive, based on the squeezing property of the semigroup and restricted to a Hilbert space setting. Efendiev et al.
[
10
]
provided an alternative, explicit construction of exponential attractors for semigroups in Banach spaces based on the decomposition the semigroup into a compact and contracting part. In
[
5
]
, Czaja and Efendiev extended this construction and the existence proof for exponential attractors to a setting that can be applied to degenerate parabolic equations. It covers a noteworthy smoothing estimate formulated in suitable function spaces related to pieces of trajectories of the semigroup and not in the phase space. Efendiev and Zelik further generalized this approach allowing for estimates and function spaces that depend on points in a compact, positively invariant set to construct exponential attractors for equations of porous medium type, see
[
11
, Remark 3.2]
.

It is important to note that most results on global and exponential attractors concern non-degenerate problems. However, the situation is significantly more complex for equations with singularities and/or degeneracies, such as for porous medium type equations and elliptic-parabolic problems. Among the vast literature on the well-posedness and regularity of solutions of degenerate diffusion equations, we particularly mention
[
12
,
27
,
20
]
and
[
21
]
which specifically address systems of the form (
1.2
). In
[
12
]
, Efendiev et al. developed a well-posedness theory for the biofilm growth model (
1.3
) in a smooth bounded domain with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for
M
based on smooth non-degenerate approximations. However, the analysis does not cover more general domains, non-vanishing Dirichlet boundary data for
M
and mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions. To overcome these restrictions, Hissink Muller and Sonner
[
21
]
provided well-posedness results for a broader class of quasilinear parabolic problems, including our problem (
1.1
). In particular, the existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence on initial data were shown. The well-posedness result was also extended for coupled systems, including our problem (
1.2
), by using Banach’s fixed point theorem. In
[
27
]
, the well-posedness theory was further generalized for systems involving couplings to ODEs. Furthermore, Hissink Muller
[
20
]
proved the interior Hölder continuity of local weak solutions of (
1.1
) that are bounded away from
1
by intrinsic scaling methods.

As for the existence of global attractors for porous medium type equations, we mention
[
17
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
12
]
. In particular, Feireisl et al. considered in
[
17
]
an equation of the form

\displaystyle u_{t}-\Delta\phi(u)+f(u)=g,

(1.4)

in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and proved, by applying the splitting method, that the generated semigroup possesses a global attractor in the weighted space
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\rho(x)dx)
where the weight
\rho\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
goes to zero at infinity. To prove the asymptotic compactness of the semigroup for our problem we use arguments from this paper. Andreu et al.
[
1
]
proved the existence of a global attractor in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for (
1.4
) with
g=0
in a bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonlinear Neumann boundary conditions under stronger assumptions on the nonlinearities
\phi
and
f
. Moreover, Carvalho et al.
[
3
]
considered (
1.4
) with
\phi(u)=|u|^{m}
,
m>1
, and
g=0
in
bounded smooth domains with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and a globally Lipschitz continuous function
f
and proved the existence of a global attractor in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
based on the theory of maximal monotone operators. Finally, in the aforementioned paper
[
12
]
, the authors showed that the biofilm growth model (
1.3
) in a smooth bounded domain
\Omega
generates a Lipschitz continuous semigroup in
L^{1}(\Omega)
that possesses a global attractor.

To the best of our knowledge, only few results have been obtained concerning the finite fractal dimension of global attractors for equations with singular and/or degenerate diffusion. The primary challenge for degenerate problems is the lack of regularity (smoothing effect) in the vicinity of the degeneracies, which makes standard techniques inapplicable. For instance, the unstable manifold approach (see
[
33
]
) cannot be applied to derive lower bounds for the dimension of the attractor because the semigroups are typically not differentiable. The finite dimensionality of global attractors for porous medium type equations has been shown in
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
14
]
. In particular, Eden et al.
[
8
]
proved that doubly nonlinear porous medium type equations of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\beta(u)}{\partial t}-\Delta u+g(x,u)=0,

in bounded domains
\Omega
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions possess a global attractor in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Also, assuming higher regularity of the nonlinearities
\beta
and
g
, the authors showed the finite fractal dimension of the global attractor. In
[
11
]
, Efendiev and Zelik showed the existence of global and exponential attractors for (
1.4
) with
\phi(u)=|u|^{m}
in bounded domains with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Assuming that the derivative of the reaction term
f
is positive at zero, first, the finite fractal dimension of the global attractor was shown, and this result was subsequently used to construct an exponential attractor. Moreover, based on upper and lower bounds for the Kolmogorov
\varepsilon
-entropy the authors provided sufficient conditions on the nonlinearity
f
that lead to a global attractor with infinite fractal dimension. Using the approach in
[
11
]
, Efendiev and Zhigun proved in
[
14
]
the existence of an exponential attractor for a class of parabolic systems with degenerate diffusion and chemotaxis assuming suitable conditions on the order of the degeneracy and growth of the chemotactic function.

1.3
Aim and outline

Our aim is to prove the existence of global and exponential attractors for problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) under suitable structural assumptions. The existence of exponential attractors ensures the existence and finite fractal dimension of the global attractor. However, we state a separate existence theorem for global attractors as it holds under much weaker assumptions. It generalizes the results in
[
12
]
on the existence of a global attractor for the biofilm model (
1.3
).
To construct exponential attractors we additionally need a sign condition on
f
at zero and the global Hölder continuity of solutions, similarly as in
[
11
]
.
We simplify the latter proof, where first the finite fractal dimension of the global attractor was shown and then the exponential attractor was constructed, by using an abstract existence theorem for exponential attractors, see
[
11
, Remark 3.2]
and
[
5
]
.
The finite fractal dimension of the global attractor is then a consequence of the existence of an exponential attractor. Our results are formulated for a broad class of degenerate problems under mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary data in bounded Lipschitz domains.
To the best of our knowledge, they are novel in the context of global and exponential attractors.
We remark that system (
1.2
) is not restricted to biofilm models. Such systems can also be used to describe other phenomena with sharp interfaces propagating at a finite speed. Moreover, our results easily extend to, e.g., the model for quorum sensing induced detachment in biofilms
[
15
]
which was analytically studied in
[
16
]
. It is an extension of the biofilm growth model (
1.3
) involving one singular-degenerate equation and three semi-linear
reaction diffusion equations for dissolved substrates.

We organize the paper as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce the functional setting and state the main results on the existence of global and exponential attractors. In Section
3
, we first prove the existence of a global attractor for (
1.1
) in a suitable phase space endowed with the
L^{1}(\Omega)
-topology. To this end, we show the existence of a bounded absorbing set and the asymptotic compactness of the trajectories, and then extend the result to the coupled system (
1.2
). In Section
4
, under the key assumptions of global Hölder continuity of solutions and appropriate sign conditions on the partial derivative of the nonlinearity
f
in the vicinity of the degeneracy, we prove the existence of exponential attractors for (
1.1
). As a consequence, the global attractor has finite fractal dimension. We also extend the existence result for exponential attractors for the coupled system (
1.2
).

\displaystyle\phi\in C(I),\ \phi(0)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\phi\ \text{is surjective,}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi\ \text{is piece-wise continuously differentiable,}\\

.

i)

The singularities of
\phi

.

ii)

The condition

The assumptions (

We suppose that the reaction function

\displaystyle\lVert f(\cdot,z_{1})-f(\cdot,z_{2})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq L|z_{1}-z_{2}|\qquad\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in I.

(2.6)

Remark 2.2
.

Condition (

) is satisfied, e.g. if

is locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to

.

We assume that the Dirichlet data satisfies

u^{D}:\Omega\to I\ \text{is measurable and}\ \phi(u^{D})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}(\Omega),

(2.7)

and the initial data

u_{0}:\Omega\to I\ \text{is measurable and}\ \Phi(u_{0})\in L^{1}(\Omega),

Remark 2.3
.

It can be inferred from (

Taking

, and using that

Hence, we obtain

Remark 2.5

In Theorem

Next, we consider the initial-boundary value problem for the coupled system (
1.2
) with mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=\Delta\phi(u)+f(\cdot,u,v)\ \ \text{in}\ (0,T]\times\Omega,\\
v_{t}=\Delta v+g(\cdot,u,v)\ \ \text{in}\ (0,T]\times\Omega,\\
u=u_{0},\ \ v=v_{0}\ \ \text{in}\ \ \{0\}\times\Omega,\\
\phi(u)=\phi(u^{D})\ \text{on}\ (0,T]\times\Gamma_{1},\ \ \partial_{\nu}\phi(u)=0\ \ \text{on}\ (0,T]\times\partial\Omega/\Gamma_{1},\\
v=v^{D}\ \ \text{on}\ (0,T]\times\Gamma_{2},\ \ \partial_{\nu}v=0\ \ \text{on}\ (0,T]\times\partial\Omega/\Gamma_{2},\end{array}\right.

(2.15)

where
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\subset\partial\Omega
have strictly positive Hausdorff measures, and we suppose for simplicity that
I=[0,1)
. This is motivated by applications such as the biofilm growth model (
1.3
), where solutions describe nonnegative quantities such as densities or concentrations. We suppose that the reaction functions
f,g:\Omega\times[0,1)\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
are measurable, bounded and uniformly Lipschitz continuous with respect to the last two arguments, i.e. there exists
L\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\quad\lVert f(\cdot,u_{1},v_{1})-f(\cdot,u_{2},v_{2})\lVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert g(\cdot,u_{1},v_{1})-g(\cdot,u_{2},v_{2})\lVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq L\left(|u_{1}-u_{2}|+|v_{1}-v_{2}|\right)\qquad\forall u_{1},u_{2}\in[0,1),v_{1},v_{2}\in[0,1].

(2.16)

Moreover, to ensure that the solutions remain nonegative and bounded, we assume that

f(\cdot,0,v)\geq 0\ ,\ \ g(\cdot,u,0)\geq 0\ \text{ and }\ g(\cdot,u,1)\leq 1\ \ \text{in}\ \Omega\quad\forall u\in[0,1),v\in[0,1].

(2.17)

Remark 2.6
.

The condition on
f
in (
2.17
) yields the nonnegativity of the solution
u
, which validates the choice of the interval
I=[0,1)
. It is not an open interval as assumed previously. However, the structural functions can be appropriately extended to the open interval
(-1,1)
. We then apply the well-posedness theory for (
2.1
) in combination with a comparison principle, which yields that solutions assume their values in
[0,1)
. Moreover, the conditions on
g
in (
2.17
) ensure that
v
takes values in the interval
[0,1]
. For further details, we refer to
[
21
]
.

We introduce the spaces

\displaystyle V_{i}=\{w\in H^{1}(\Omega):Tr\ w=0\ a.e.\ on\ \Gamma_{i}\},\quad i=1,2.

Furthermore, solutions of system (
2.15
) are analogously defined as in Definition
2.1
for problem (
2.1
).
We recall the well-posedness result for the coupled system (
2.15
), given in
[

, Theorem 2.9]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that the function
\phi:[0,1)\to[0,\infty)
satisfies the conditions (

)-(
2.3
) and

satisfy (
2.16
)-(

). Moreover, the boundary data

satisfies (

),

In addition, if

2.2
Long-time Behaviour of Solutions

In this section we present the main results that address the long-time behaviour of solutions in terms of global and exponential attractors for the semigroups generated by the scalar problem (
2.1
) and the coupled problem (
2.15
). The existence of an exponential attractor will also imply that the fractal dimension of the global attractor is finite.

We recall the definition of a global attractor for a semigroup.

Definition 2.2
.

The set
\mathcal{A}\subset E
is a global attractor for a semigroup
\left\{S\left(t\right)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
in a Banach space
E
iff

i)

\mathcal{A}
is compact in
E
,

ii)

\mathcal{A}
is invariant, i.e.,
S\left(t\right)\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}

\forall t\geq 0
,

iii)

\mathcal{A}
attracts all bounded subsets of
E
, i.e. for any bounded set
D\subset E

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}d_{E}(S\left(t\right)D|\mathcal{A})=0

where
d_{E}(A|B)=\sup\limits_{x\in A}dist(x,B)=\sup\limits_{x\in A}\inf\limits_{y\in B}\lVert x-y\rVert
is the Hausdorff semi-distance.

We introduce the phase space

\mathcal{X}:=\overline{\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega):\phi(u)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\}},

where the closure is taken in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, by Theorem
2.1
, problem (
2.1
) generates a semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{X}
that is Lipschitz continuous in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. More specifically,
\mathcal{S}(t)u_{0}=u(t)
, where
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
is the unique weak solution
of (
2.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
.
We note that
\mathcal{X}

is positively invariant, bounded and closed in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. We can now state our result on the existence of the global attractor for the problem (
2.1
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let (
2.2
)–(
2.8
) be satisfied,
\phi(u_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

f
be bounded.
Then, the semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
generated by problem (
2.1
) has a global attractor
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{X}
endowed with the
L^{1}(\Omega)
-topology, which has the form
\mathcal{A}=\omega(\mathcal{X})
.

In this theorem,

\displaystyle\omega(D)=\bigcap_{t>0}\overline{\bigcup_{\tau\geq t}S(t)D}

represents the
\omega
-limit set of a set
D
for a semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in a Banach space
E
, where the bar denotes the closure in
E
.

Analogously, we define the phase space for the coupled problem

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}:=\overline{\{(u,v)\in L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega):\phi(u)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),v\in[0,1]\}},

where the closure is taken in
L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then, by
Theorem
2.2
, the coupled problem (
2.15
) generates a semigroup
\{\mathcal{T}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{Y}
that is Lipschitz continuous in
L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega)
. More specifically,
\mathcal{T}(t)(u_{0},v_{0})=(u(t),v(t))
, where
(u,v)\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))\times C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
is the unique weak solution
of (
2.15
) with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathcal{Y}
.
Note that
\mathcal{Y}
is positively invariant, bounded and closed in
L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega)
.
We now state our result on the existence of the global attractor for the problem (
2.15
).

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that
\phi:[0,1)\to[0,\infty)
satisfies (
2.2
)–(
2.1
) and
f,g
satisfy (
2.16
)-(
2.17
). Moreover,
u^{D}:\Omega\to[0,1)
satisfies (
2.7
),
v^{D}:\Omega\to[0,1]
satisfies
v^{D}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, and the initial data
u_{0}:\Omega\to[0,1)
satisfies (
2.8
),
\phi(u_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
v_{0}:\Omega\to[0,1]
is measurable.
Then, the semigroup
\{\mathcal{T}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
generated by problem (
2.15
) has a global attractor
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
in
\mathcal{Y}
endowed with the
L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega)
-topology, which has the form
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\omega(\mathcal{Y})
.

Remark 2.8
.

Theorem
2.4
generalizes the existence result in
[
12
]
for the global attractor for the biofilm model (
1.3
). In fact, we consider the significantly broader class of systems (
2.15
) and allow for mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary data and more general domains. These generalizations are relevant in applications.

Next, we recall the definition of an exponential attractor.

Definition 2.3
.

A set
\mathcal{M}\subset E
is an exponential attractor for the semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in a Banach space
E
iff

i)

\mathcal{M}
is a compact subset of
E
,

ii)

\mathcal{M}
is positively invariant, that is,
S(t)\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{M},\ \forall\ t\geq 0
,

iii)

\mathcal{M}
exponentially attracts all bounded subsets, that is, for any bounded subset
D\subset E

d_{E}(S(t)D,\mathcal{M})\leq Q(\lVert D\rVert_{E})e^{-\alpha t},

where the positive constant
\alpha
and the monotonic function
Q
are independent of
D
,

iv)

\mathcal{M}
has finite fractal dimension in
E
, i.e.,

\text{dim}_{f}^{E}(\mathcal{M})\leq C<\infty,

for some constant
C>0
.

The fractal dimension of a (pre)compact set
K
in a metric space
X
is defined as

\text{dim}_{f}^{X}(K):=\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\ln(N_{\epsilon}^{X}(K))}{\ln(1/\varepsilon)},

where
N_{\epsilon}^{X}(K)
is the minimal number of balls in
X
with radius
\varepsilon
and center in
K
needed to cover
K
.

To prove the existence of an exponential attractor for the scalar problem (
2.1
), we additionally suppose that the function
\phi:I\to\mathbb{R}
and the partial derivative
f_{u}
of the function
f:\Omega\times I\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\phi\in C^{2}(I),\qquad f_{u}\ \text{is continuous with}\ f_{u}(\cdot,0)<0\ \text{in}\ \Omega.

(2.18)

For simplicity we assume homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, i.e.
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
and
u^{D}\equiv 0
in (
2.1
). Moreover, the solutions are globally Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha>0
, i.e. for any solution
u
of the problem (
2.1
) there exists a positive constant
M
such that

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{C^{\alpha}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})}\leq M.

(2.19)

Remark 2.9
.

The interior Hölder continuity of local weak solutions of (
2.1
) that are bounded away from
1
was shown in
[
20
]
under the assumption that

C_{1}|z|^{p-1}\leq\phi^{\prime}(z)\leq C_{2}|z|^{p-1}\qquad\forall z\in[0,\varepsilon],

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
p>1

(see
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
). Note that weak solutions
u
are bounded away from
1
if the initial data
u_{0}
has this property. It is expected that these solutions satisfy the global Hölder continuity assumption (
2.19
), i.e. they are Hölder continuous up to the boundary. In fact, as pointed out by the author in
[
22
, Section 1.7]
, the proof should extend by using the methods in
[
6
, Chapter III, §11 and §12]
if the boundary of the domain, the boundary data and initial data are Hölder continuous.

Now, we state the main result on the existence of an exponential attractor for the scalar problem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
and (
2.18
)-(
2.19
), there exists an exponential attractor
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathcal{X}
endowed with the topology of
L^{1}(\Omega)
, whose fractal dimension is bounded by

\text{dim}_{f}^{L^{1}(\Omega)}(\mathcal{M})\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}+\log_{1/\eta_{1}}\left(N_{\eta_{2}}^{W}\left(B_{1}^{V}(0)\right)\right),

(2.20)

where
W=C(J\times\overline{\Omega}_{1})
and
V=C^{\alpha}(J\times\overline{\Omega}_{2})
for open subsets
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
such that
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}\subset\Omega
and a compact time interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
. The constants
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in(0,1)
are determined by smoothing and decay estimates of the semigroup and
\alpha>0
by (
2.19
).

As a consequence, the global attractor
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{M}
and has finite fractal dimension.

Here, the set
B_{r}^{E}(x_{0})
denotes a ball in a metric space
E
of radius
r>0
with center
x_{0}\in E
.

Remark 2.10
.

The (Kolmogorov)
\varepsilon
-entropy of a precompact subset
M
of a Banach space
E

is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}^{E}(M)=\log_{2}(N_{\varepsilon}^{E}(M)).

It was introduced by Kolmogorov and Tihomirov in
[
23
]
.
The order of growth of
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}^{E}(M)
as
\varepsilon
tends to zero is a measure for the massiveness of the set
M
in
E
.
For certain function spaces precise estimates are know. For instance, if
\Omega=(0,1)^{n}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
then

c_{1}\varepsilon^{-\frac{n}{\alpha}}\leq\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}^{C(\overline{\Omega})}\left(B^{C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}_{1}(0)\right)\leq c_{2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{n}{\alpha}},

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0,
see
[
23
]
. Such results have been generalized, e.g. for compact, connected subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. However, we cannot apply these estimates in Theorem
2.5
as the subsets
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are not necessarily connected.

Remark 2.11
.

To prove the existence of an exponential attractor for continuous time semigroups
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
one first constructs an exponential attractor
\mathcal{M}^{d}
for the discrete time semigroup
\{S(nT)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, for some

. If the semigroup is Hölder continuous in time with exponent
\alpha\in(0,1]
an exponential attractor for the continuous time semigroup is then typically obtained by

\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{t\in[T,T+1]}S(t)\mathcal{M}^{d},

and its fractal dimension is bounded by

\text{dim}_{f}(\mathcal{M})\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(1+\text{dim}_{f}(\mathcal{M}^{d})\right),

see
[
5
,
9
,
11
]
. In the proof of Theorem
2.5
, we use the slightly different construction of the continuous time exponential attractor in
[
31
]
and obtain the improved bound

\text{dim}_{f}(\mathcal{M})\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}+\text{dim}_{f}(\mathcal{M}^{d}).

Finally, we extend the existence result for exponential attractors to the coupled system (
2.15
) under the assumptions that the function
\phi:[0,1)\to[0,\infty)
and the partial derivative

of the function

satisfy

\displaystyle\phi\in C^{2}([0,1)),\quad\ f_{u}\ \text{is continuous with}\ f_{u}(\cdot,0,v)<0\ \text{in}\ \Omega,\ \forall v\in[0,1].

(2.21)

For simplicity we assume homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, i.e.
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}=\partial\Omega
and

in (

).
Moreover, the solutions are globally Hölder continuous with exponent
\tilde{\alpha}>0
, i.e. for any solution

of

)
there exists a positive constant

such that

\displaystyle||(u,v)||_{C^{\tilde{\alpha}}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})\times C^{\tilde{\alpha}}([0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega})}\leq\tilde{M}.

(2.22)

Remark 2.12
.

The interior Hölder regularity for the scalar problem (

) shown in

extends to the coupled problem (

Theorem 2.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

and (

3
Existence of Global Attractors

In this section, we prove the existence of global attractors for the semigroups generated by problems (
2.1
) and (
2.15
), respectively, i.e. we prove Theorems
2.3
-
2.4
. First, we consider the scalar problem (
2.1
) and show the existence of the global attractor for the semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in the phase space
\mathcal{X}\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
, as defined in Section 2. Since
\mathcal{X}
is
positively invariant and bounded, it is certainly a bounded absorbing set for the semigroup. Hence, it suffices to show the asymptotic compactness of the semigroup. To this end, we prove the following lemma by adapting the arguments in
[
17
, §3.5]
to our case.

From now on we denote by
C>0
a generic constant in the estimates that may vary in each occurrence and from line to line. If a constant depends on a parameter
\eta
we indicate it by writing
C_{\eta}
. Again, this constant
C_{\eta}
may vary in each occurrence and from line to line.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
T>0
, the set
\bigcup\limits_{t\geq T}\mathcal{S}(t)\mathcal{X}
is relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Let
T>0
and
u
be any solution of problem (
2.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
. Then, by Theorem
2.1
, we conclude that

\lVert\phi(u(t))\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<C/2\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

(3.1)

Moreover, we have by (
2.13
) that

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla\phi(u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C/2,

which implies that there exists
\xi\in[0,T]
such that

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla\phi(u(\xi))\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C/2.

(3.2)

Now, we define the set

\displaystyle K(R):=\{z\in L^{1}(\Omega):\lVert\phi(z)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla\phi(z)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq R\},

for any
R>0
. We observe that
\phi(z)
is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
for
z\in K(R)
, and therefore relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. This implies that any sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
K(R)
has a subsequence, which we still denote by
w_{k}
, such that

\phi(w_{k})\to w\ \text{in}\ L^{1}(\Omega).

The condition (
2.2
) implies that
\phi^{-1}
is continuous on
\phi(I)
.
On the other hand, we have that
\phi(w_{k})
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Hence, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

w_{k}=\phi^{-1}(\phi(w_{k}))\to\phi^{-1}(w)\ \text{in}\ L^{1}(\Omega),

which implies that the set
K(R)
is relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Setting
K:=K(C)
, we observe that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) imply
S(\xi)u_{0}=u(\xi)\in K
. Moreover, taking

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}:=\bigcup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{S}(t)K,

we have
S(T)u_{0}=S(T-\xi)S(\xi)u_{0}\in\mathcal{K}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{K}
, we observe that
\mathcal{K}=Z([0,T]\times K)
, where
Z(t,y):=S(t)y
. By the relative compactness of
K
, it follows that
[0,T]\times K
is also relatively compact. Furthermore, the function
Z
is continuous in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Indeed, for any sequences
\{y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset[0,\infty)
such that
y_{k}\to y
and
t_{k}\to t
as
k\to\infty
, by (
2.12
), we get

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{S}(t_{k})y_{k}-\mathcal{S}(t)y\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\mathcal{S}(t_{k})y_{k}-\mathcal{S}(t_{k})y\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\lVert\mathcal{S}(t_{k})y-\mathcal{S}(t)y\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{Lt_{k}}\lVert y_{k}-y\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\lVert\mathcal{S}(t_{k})y-\mathcal{S}(t)y\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

The first term on the right-hand side converges to zero as
k\to\infty
and the second term as well, due to the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to
t
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, see Theorem
2.1
. Consequently, the set
\mathcal{K}
is relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Therefore, the set
\mathcal{S}(T)\mathcal{X}
is relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Since
\mathcal{X}
is positively invariant, we have

\mathcal{S}(t)\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{S}(T)\mathcal{S}(t-T)\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{S}(T)\mathcal{X}

for
t\geq T
, which completes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
3.1
, we are in a position to prove Theorem
2.3
.

By Theorem
2.1
, the semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is continuous in
L^{1}(\Omega)
with respect to
t
. Therefore, using that
\mathcal{X}
is a bounded absorbing set together with Lemma
3.1
, we can apply the abstract theorem
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
on the existence of
global attractors to conclude the proof of the theorem.
∎

Finally, we present the proof of the existence of the global attractor to the coupled problem (
2.15
).

Analogously as in the proof of Theorem
2.3
we obtain the continuity of the semigroup
\{\mathcal{T}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega)
with respect to
t
and the existence of a bounded absorbing set, which is
\mathcal{Y}
by Theorem
2.2
. Therefore, it remains to show the asymptotic compactness of the semigroup. We only present a sketch of the proof as the arguments are the same as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, it can be shown that the set

\displaystyle K(R):=\{

\displaystyle(z_{1},z_{2})\in L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega):

\displaystyle||\phi(z_{1})||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+||z_{2}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+||\nabla\phi(z_{1})||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+||\nabla z_{2}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq R\},

for any
R>0
, is relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover, by Theorem
2.2
(see also Remark
2.5
), for any
T>0
, there exists
\xi\in[0,T]
such that
\mathcal{T}(\xi)(u_{0},v_{0})=(u(\xi),v(\xi))\in K
,
K=K(C)
for some constant
C>0
.
Hence, it follows that
\mathcal{T}(T)(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathcal{K}
, for any
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathcal{Y}
, where the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\bigcup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{T}(t)K

is relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{1}(\Omega)
. Therefore, as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
we conclude that the set
\bigcup\limits_{t\geq T}\mathcal{T}(t)\mathcal{Y}
is relatively compact. The abstract theorem
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
on the existence of the global attractor can be applied, which completes the proof.
∎

4
Existence of Exponential Attractors

We first consider the scalar problem (
2.1
) and show the existence of an exponential attractor for the corresponding semigroup
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, i.e. we prove Theorem
2.5
. To this end, we follow and adapt the arguments in
[
11
, Theorems 2.1 and 3.1]
to our case. However, different from
[
11
]
, where first the finite fractal dimension of the global attractor was shown and then the exponential attractor was constructed from scratch, we apply an abstract existence result for exponential attractors for discrete time semigroups.
Subsequently, the exponential attractor for the continuous time semigroup can be constructed in a standard manner, see
[
5
,
11
,
31
]
. This significantly shortens the existence proof for the exponential attractor, and the finite fractal dimension of the global attractor is an immediate consequence.
More specifically, the existence result for discrete time exponential attractors we will use is the following proposition which is a slight generalization of
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
, see also
[
11
, Remark 3.2]
. Here, we give a specific estimate of the fractal dimension of the exponential attractor in terms of the constants in the relevant estimates.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\{S(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a discrete semigroup in a Banach space
E
and
K
be a compact subset of
E
such that
S(n)K\subset K
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Assume that for every
u_{*}\in K
and
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, there exists a pair of Banach spaces
X_{u_{*},\varepsilon}
and
Y_{u_{*},\varepsilon}
such that
X_{u_{*},\varepsilon}
is compactly embedded into
Y_{u_{*},\varepsilon}
and this embedding is uniform in the sense of the Kolmogorov
\varepsilon
-entropy, i.e. for
\rho>0

N_{\rho}^{Y_{u_{*},\varepsilon}}(B^{X_{u_{*},\varepsilon}}_{1}(0))\leq\widetilde{N}_{\rho},

where
\widetilde{N}_{\rho}
is independent of
u_{*}
and
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Moreover, assume that there exists a map
\mathcal{T}_{u_{*},\varepsilon}:B^{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{*})\cap K\to X_{u_{*},\varepsilon}
such that for any
u_{1},u_{2}\in B^{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{*})\cap K
we have

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{T}_{u_{*},\varepsilon}u_{1}-\mathcal{T}_{u_{*},\varepsilon}u_{2}\rVert_{X_{u_{*},\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert_{E},

\displaystyle\lVert S(1)u_{1}-S(1)u_{2}\rVert_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert_{E}+\mu\lVert\mathcal{T}_{u_{*},\varepsilon}u_{1}-\mathcal{T}_{u_{*},\varepsilon}u_{2}\rVert_{Y_{u_{*},\varepsilon}},

where the constants
\kappa>0,\eta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\mu>0
are independent of
u_{*}
and
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Then, for any
\nu\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\eta)
there exists an exponential attractor
\mathcal{M}_{\nu}\subset K
for the semigroup
\{S(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and its fractal dimension is bounded by

\displaystyle\text{dim}_{f}^{E}(\mathcal{M}_{\nu})\leq\log_{\frac{1}{2(\eta+\nu)}}\widetilde{N}_{\frac{\nu}{\kappa\mu}}.

The proof of Theorem
2.5
is based on several lemmas that establish the required estimates in Proposition
4.1
for suitable spaces
E,X_{u_{*},\varepsilon}
and
Y_{u_{*},\varepsilon}
.

Remark 4.1
.

All estimates obtained from now on can be justified by approximating the solutions of (
2.1
) and (
2.15
) by smooth solutions of non-degenerate problems.

First of all, we introduce the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}:=\overline{\bigcup_{t\geq 0}S(t)\mathcal{X}},

where the closure is taken in
C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
. By (
2.19
), we have
\lVert\mathcal{C}\rVert_{C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq M
and due to the construction of
\mathcal{C}
, we have
S(t)\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{C}
for all
t\geq 0
. Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C},u_{0}\neq 0
be arbitrary. We introduce the sets

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(\theta)=L(\theta,u_{0}):=\{x\in\Omega:|u_{0}(x)|>\theta\},\\
S(\theta)=S(\theta,u_{0}):=\{x\in\Omega:|u_{0}(x)|<\theta\},\end{split}

(4.1)

for every
\theta>0
, which clearly satisfy
S(\theta_{1})\subset S(\theta_{2})
and
L(\theta_{2})\subset L(\theta_{1})
if
\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}
. Moreover, by (
2.19
), we observe that these sets are open and satisfy

\displaystyle\partial S(\theta)=\partial L(\theta)=\{x\in\Omega:|u_{0}(x)|=\theta\},\quad\Omega=S(\theta)\cup L(\theta)\cup\partial L(\theta).

Furthermore, choosing
\delta>0
and
\theta>0
small enough such that the sets
\partial S(\theta)
and
\partial S(\theta+\delta)
are nonempty, we observe that

\lVert x-y\rVert\geq\frac{1}{M^{1/\alpha}}|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y)|^{1/\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{M^{1/\alpha}}\big|\lvert u_{0}(x)\rvert-\lvert u_{0}(y)\rvert\big|^{1/\alpha}=\left(\frac{\delta}{M}\right)^{1/\alpha},

for every
x\in\partial S(\theta+\delta)
,
y\in\partial S(\theta)
and some constant
M>0
. Therefore, we get

d(\partial S(\theta+\delta),\partial S(\theta))\geq C_{\delta},

(4.2)

where
C_{\delta}\equiv\left(\frac{\delta}{M}\right)^{1/\alpha}
and
d(A,B)=\inf\limits_{x\in A}\inf\limits_{y\in B}\lVert x-y\rVert
denotes the standard metric distance between two sets
A
and
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
2.5
hold. There exists

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{u}(x,v(t,x))&<-\beta\quad\text{for}\ x\in S(4\theta),\ t\in[0,T],\\

(4.3)

uniformly with respect to

.

Due to the (

) and (
2.19

\displaystyle f_{u}(\cdot,u)<-3\beta<0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega\quad\forall|u|<5\theta.

[

) with sufficiently small

0,\ x\in L(5\theta),\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

and

\lVert\psi\rVert_{C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N},

(4.6)

where the constants

only depend on
M,\alpha

, and are independent of

.

Recall that by (

|u(t,x)|\leq|u(t,x)-u_{0}(x)|+|u_{0}(x)|<MT^{\alpha}+5\theta\quad\text{for}\ x\in S(5\theta),\ t\in[0,T],

which, together with (

f_{u}(x,u(t,x))<-3\beta+K\qquad\text{for}\ x\in S(5\theta),\ t\in[0,T],

sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{u}(x,u(t,x))&<-2\beta\qquad\text{for}\ x\in S(5\theta),\ t\in[0,T],\\
|u(t,x)|&>\theta/2\qquad\ \text{for}\ x\in L(\theta),\ t\in[0,T].\end{split}

(4.7)

Note that

and
\theta
.

\lVert\omega\rVert_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{0}\lVert\omega\rVert^{\gamma}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\omega\rVert^{1-\gamma}_{C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})},\qquad\omega\in C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega}),

Next, we show a smoothing property for the difference of two solutions of (

Lemma 4.3
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
2.5
hold and

be two solutions of (
2.1
) with initial data
u_{i}(0)\in B^{L^{1}(\Omega)}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})\cap\mathcal{C},i=1,2
. Then, for any

\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert_{C^{\alpha}([t_{0},T]\times\overline{L(3\theta)})}\leq C_{t_{0}}\lVert u_{1}(0)-u_{2}(0)\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

(4.9)

where
T>0
and
\varepsilon>0
are as in Lemma
4.2
and the constant
C_{t_{0}}>0
depends on
t_{0}

\varepsilon,u_{0}

u_{1},u_{2}

We first show that for any

and

\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{W^{(1,2),r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}\leq C_{r,t_{0}},\qquad i=1,2,

(4.10)

where

denotes the anisotropic Sobolev space consisting of functions whose

and

u_{0},\varepsilon
and the trajectories
u_{1}
and
u_{2}

4.10
) for

u=u_{2}
. To this end, we introduce
v(t,x):=\phi(u(t,x))
. Then, considering
\eqref{eq:ressigncond3}_{2}
and that
\phi(u)

, the function

v_{t}=a\Delta v+af(\cdot,u)\qquad\text{in}\ (0,T]\times L(\theta),

(4.11)

where

), the continuity of

and

, the function

and

and the function

is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
. Additionally, due to (

) and
\eqref{eq:ressigncond3}_{2}

a(t,x)\geq C\qquad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times L(\theta),

(4.12)

where the constant
C
is also independent of
u_{0}
and

. Hence, we can use an

-interior regularity estimate for the solution of the linear non-degenerate equation (

\lVert v\rVert_{W^{(1,2),r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}\leq C_{r,t_{0}}(\rVert af(\cdot,u)\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T)\times L(\theta))}+\lVert v\rVert_{L^{1}((0,T)\times L(\theta))})\leq C^{\prime}_{r,t_{0}},

Then, taking into account that

\displaystyle\quad\lVert u\rVert_{W^{(1,2),r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}^{r}

\displaystyle=\lVert\phi^{-1}(v)\rVert_{W^{(1,2),r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}^{r}

\displaystyle=\lVert\phi^{-1}(v)\rVert_{L^{r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}^{r}+\left\|\frac{v_{t}}{\phi^{\prime}(u)}\right\|_{L^{r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}^{r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\frac{v_{x_{i}}}{\phi^{\prime}(u)}\right\|_{L^{r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}^{r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\left\|\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)v_{x_{i}}v_{x_{j}}}{(\phi^{\prime}(u))^{2}}\right\|_{L^{r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}^{r}+\left\|\frac{v_{x_{i}x_{j}}}{\phi^{\prime}(u)}\right\|_{L^{r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}^{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\lVert v\rVert^{r}_{W^{(1,2),r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}+\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2r}_{L^{2r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}\right)\leq C_{r,t_{0}}^{\prime\prime},

for some constant

, which yields the estimate (
4.10
) for
u=u_{1}
.

To prove estimate (
4.9
) we consider
w=u_{1}-u_{2}
in
[t_{0}/2,T]\times L(2\theta)
which satisfies

\displaystyle w_{t}

\displaystyle=\Delta(l_{1}(t)w)+l_{2}(t)w\qquad\text{in}\ (t_{0}/2,T]\times L(2\theta),

(4.14)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}l_{1}(t)&:=\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(su_{1}(t)+(1-s)u_{2}(t))ds,\\

First, we use that

and (

satisfies

||l_{1}||_{C^{\alpha}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega})}\leq C,

(4.16)

where

is independent of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
. Furthermore, by (

||\partial_{t}l_{1}||_{L^{r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{2}||\partial_{t}u_{i}||_{L^{r}((t_{0}/2,T)\times L(2\theta))}\leq C_{r,t_{0}}^{\prime\prime\prime},

(4.17)

for some constant

and

. Also, by

l_{1}(t,x)\geq C>0\qquad\text{for}\ (t,x)\in[t_{0}/2,T]\times L(2\theta),

where the constant

and

(

. Hence, considering (

The following lemma yields a contraction property for the difference of two solutions.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
2.5
hold and
T>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be as in Lemma
4.2
.
For any two solutions
u_{1},u_{2}
of (
2.1
) with initial data
u_{i}(0)\in B^{L^{1}(\Omega)}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})\cap\mathcal{C},i=1,2
, there exists
t_{*}\in(0,T)
such that the estimate

||u_{1}(T)-u_{2}(T)||_{L^{1}(S(4\theta))}\leq\frac{1-\delta}{2}||u_{1}(0)-u_{2}(0)||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+C||u_{1}-u_{2}||_{L^{1}([t^{*},T]\times L(4\theta))}

(4.21)

holds, where
\delta\in(0,1)

and
C>0
are independent of
\varepsilon
,
u_{0}
and
u_{1},u_{2}
.

We set
w=u_{1}-u_{2}
and consider (
4.14
) on the set
(0,T]\times S(4\theta)
.
Multiplying the equation by

\psi(x)\text{sgn}(w(t,x))=\psi(x)\text{sgn}(\psi(x)l_{1}(t,x)w(t,x)),

(4.22)

where
\psi
is the cut-off function defined in (
4.5
), and integrating over
\Omega
we get

\displaystyle\left<w_{t},\psi\text{sgn}(w)\right>=\left<\Delta(l_{1}(t)w),\psi\text{sgn}(\psi l_{1}(t)w)\right>+\left<l_{2}(t)w,\psi sgn(w)\right>.

Therefore, using the relation

\psi\Delta(l_{1}(t)w)=\Delta(\psi l_{1}(t)w)-2\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla(l_{1}(t)w)-l_{1}(t)w\Delta\psi,

we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left<\psi,|w|\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\Delta(\psi l_{1}(t)w),\text{sgn}(\psi l_{1}(t)w)\right>-2\left<\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla(l_{1}(t)w),\text{sgn}(\psi l_{1}(t)w)\right>

\displaystyle\quad-\left<l_{1}(t)w\Delta\psi,\text{sgn}(\psi l_{1}(t)w)\right>+\left<l_{2}(t)w,\psi\text{sgn}(w)\right>.

Then, applying Kato’s inequality
(\Delta v,\text{sgn}v)\leq 0
and integration by parts to the second term on the right hand side, we deduce that

\partial_{t}\left<\psi,|w|\right>\leq\left<\Delta\psi,l_{1}(t)|w|\right>+\left<\psi,l_{2}(t)|w|\right>.

Therefore, using
\eqref{eq:ressigncond3}_{1}
, (

\partial_{t}\left<\psi,|w(t)|\right>+\beta\left<\psi,|w(t)|\right>\leq\int\limits_{L(4\theta)}\Delta\psi(x)l_{1}(t,x)|w(t,x)|dx\leq C||w(t)||_{L^{1}(L(4\theta))}.

Consequently, applying Gronwall’s lemma and using that

for

||w(T)||_{L^{1}(S(4\theta))}\leq e^{-\beta(T-t_{*})}||w(t_{*})||_{L^{1}(L(4\theta))}+C\int_{t_{*}}^{T}e^{-\beta(T-s)}||w(s)||_{L^{1}(L(4\theta))}ds,

for an arbitrary

. Therefore, together with (

), we obtain

||u_{1}(T)-u_{2}(T)||_{L^{1}(S(4\theta))}\leq e^{Lt_{*}-\beta(T-t_{*})}||u_{1}(0)-u_{2}(0)||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+C_{t_{*}}||u_{1}-u_{2}||_{L^{1}([t_{*},T]\times L(4\theta))},

for some constant
C_{t_{*}}
. By fixing

sufficiently small such that

, we obtain (

).
∎

Finally, we use Proposition
4.1
and the results obtained in the Lemmas

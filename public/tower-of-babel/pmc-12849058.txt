Quality indicators for deceased organ and tissue donation for transplantation: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Rafael Rodrigo da Silva Pimentel, Daisy Maria Rizatto Tronchin, Marcelo José dos Santos, Maristela Santini Martins, Ágata Nunes Brito, Fabiana Lopes Pereira Santana
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe global shortage of organs and tissues highlights the need to strengthen and monitor the quality of deceased donor programs. Evaluating each stage of this process using quality-sensitive indicators is essential to ensure transparency, efficiency, safety, and effectiveness. However, there is still no consensus on which indicators best reflect the quality of organ and tissue donation processes, highlighting the need to map and analyze existing evidence. The aim of this study was to map and analyze international literature on indicators for monitoring the quality of the process of deceased organ and tissue donation for transplantation.MethodsThis review will be conducted following the methodology proposed by the JBI and guidelines for scope reviews. The databases, portals, and directories to be searched included MEDLINE (via PubMed), CINAHL (EBSCO), Embase (Elsevier), Scopus (Elsevier), Web of Science (Clarivate Analytics), Business Source Complete (EBSCO), VHL Virtual Health Library; gray literature will be sought on Google Scholar, OpenGrey (GreyNet), CAPES Theses and Dissertations, Global ETD Search (formerly the Union Catalog), Open Access Theses and Dissertations, and websites of organ and tissue donation and transplant organizations. Studies in any language and published after 1980 will be considered for inclusion. A wide range of study designs will be included, encompassing quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods research, as well as relevant gray literature on organ and tissue donation and transplantation. Two reviewers will screen the studies, paying attention to the eligibility criteria. The data will be extracted by means of specific variables and presented in categories according to the evaluative triad structure, process, and result, and by the pillars of quality proposed by Avedis Donabedian in the form of diagrams, tables, charts, and narrative synthesis.DiscussionThe evidence on quality indicators for organ and tissue donation processes is conceptually diverse. This scoping review will provide an overview of existing studies, identify gaps in current knowledge, and guide future research on the development and validation of quality indicators.Systematic review registrationOpen Science Framework osf.io/ymuvj/.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13643-025-03034-4.

Introduction

Organ and tissue transplantation has advanced globally since the first successful solid organ transplant, providing treatment for terminal chronic diseases and improving quality of life for recipients. Successful transplantation, however, depends entirely on the donation of organs and tissues [1]. Family refusal and low identification of potential brain-dead donors are among the factors that interfere with the acquisition of organs and tissues for transplantation [2, 3].

To address these challenges, it is imperative to implement actions that improve the organization of the organ and tissue donation process and establish a quality monitoring program that can impact outcomes and identify factors influencing performance [4].

The definition of quality remains a challenge since it is multifaceted and contextual [5]. Although explaining the concept is not essential for trying to make improvements, it is important that everyone involved in the processes define and understand the quality construct before attempting to achieve it [6]. According to Avedis Donabedian, quality in health is a set of attributes, which includes a level of professional excellence, efficient use of resources, minimum risk, and a high degree of satisfaction on the part of the patients/users, considering the existing social values [7, 8]. He states that “we cannot evaluate the quality until we have decided with which meaning to invest the concept. A precise definition of quality is the basis on which everything is built” ([9], p.450).

Measuring quality in healthcare is challenging and often focuses on analyzing administrative databases, to the detriment of what could be more important to patients and professionals [10]. One attempt to address this challenge is the use of indicators, which are quantitative or qualitative measures with meaning, used to organize and capture relevant information about the elements that make up the object of observation [11, 12].

Quality indicators are used to make judgments about quality and are based on evidence or expert consensus [13]. In addition, they are used to highlight measurable results, which enable the evaluation of the achievement of goals, whether quantitative (e.g., number of organs obtained) or process-oriented (e.g., adherence to protocols, timeliness of notification), which may require more complex indicators or a set of them to capture the quality of the donation process [11, 12].

Among the different forms of classification of quality indicators, a model used by scholars and found in the literature for the evaluation of quality in health is the evaluation triad containing components of structure, process, and result, as proposed by Donabedian [14]. Structure aims to define the conditions under which care is provided and includes material resources, human resources, and organizational characteristics. The process includes health care activities such as diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, prevention, and patient education, which are usually performed by professionals but may include the participation of patients and family members. The result integrates significant changes, both desirable and undesirable, in individuals and populations that may be related to health care [15]. Donabedian then concludes his concept of quality by stating that it is the product of two factors: the use of scientific and technological knowledge and interpersonal relationships. He also proposes the concept of pillars of quality: efficacy, effectiveness, efficiency, optimality, acceptability, legitimacy, and equity [16].

Despite the importance of evaluating organ and tissue donation through quality indicators, existing studies show that assessments are limited and often restricted to effectiveness outcomes (e.g., family consent rates and conversion rates), rather than encompassing broader dimensions of quality such as donor maintenance and identification of potential donors [17, 18]. Marang-van de Mheen and Vincent (2023) [19] emphasize that quality indicators should be grounded in a clear conceptual framework and that a comprehensive set of indicators is required to capture the multiple constructs of quality, including legitimacy, efficiency, and equity. Current measures in organ and tissue donation rarely reflect this multidimensional nature, reinforcing the need to systematically map and synthesize existing indicators to support health system management and practice.

Scoping reviews are particularly suitable for mapping complex and heterogeneous research areas [20]. They identify how research is conducted, reveal knowledge gaps, and provide a foundation for future studies or systematic reviews. Preliminary searches in PROSPERO, MEDLINE, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and the JBI Evidence Synthesis found no reviews specifically addressing quality indicators in deceased organ and tissue donation, confirming the relevance and originality of this study [21–23].

A scoping review is useful when knowledge on a topic has not been comprehensively reviewed or when the subjects of exploration are complex and heterogeneous in nature. In addition, it is useful for identifying how research on a specific topic or field is conducted, as well as existing knowledge gaps, and serves as a precursor to a systematic review [24].

The general aim of this study is to map and analyze international literature on indicators for monitoring the quality of the process of deceased organ and tissue donation for transplantation, and the specific aims are to describe the indicators for quality monitoring and to characterize the indicators in terms of the triad (structure, process, and result) and the seven pillars of quality as proposed by Avedis Donabedian.

Methods

The review protocol was preregistered within the Open Science Framework database (osf.io/ymuvj). This review will be carried out according to the JBI methodology, following the nine steps proposed for the scoping reviews. This process follows the following framework: (1) define the objective and question; (2) develop the inclusion criteria with the objective and question; (3) Describe the planned approach for searching for evidence, selecting and extracting data, and presenting the evidence; (4) search the evidence; (5) select the evidence; (6) extract the evidence; (7) analyze the evidence; (8) present the results; and (9) summarize the evidence in relation to the objective and question. The evidence is summarized in relation to the objective of the review [20].

We will use the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) checklist as the basis for the review [25]. The review questions were defined using the mnemonic P (Population = brain dead organ and tissue donors), C (Concept = quality indicators), and C (Context = any configuration of health service and system). This leads to the following question: What are the quality monitoring indicators used in the process of deceased organ and tissue donation for transplantation? The following subquestions were asked: What are the main characteristics of quality monitoring indicators in the process of organ and tissue donation? In which dimensions and typology of quality are the indicators for monitoring the quality of the organ and tissue donation process classified?

The steps for developing the review are detailed below.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

This review will consider studies and documents addressing processes related to deceased human donors of solid organs (heart, lungs, liver, kidneys, pancreas, and combinations of these) and tissues (cornea, skin, bones, cardiac valves) from patients diagnosed with brain death (DBD), regardless of age, sex, or race. Studies related to donors exclusively with cardiorespiratory arrest (CRP), donations after circulatory death (DCD), donations from living donors, donations of blood or bone marrow, and studies with data on specific indicators of organ and tissue transplantation were excluded.

The concept of interest comprises indicators used to monitor the quality of the organ and tissue donation process for transplantation, encompassing the dimensions of structure, process, and outcome, as proposed by Donabedian. Quality indicators are quantitative measures that verify whether the objectives initially proposed have been achieved, guiding the monitoring and continuous improvement of services [12, 13, 26, 27]. The indicator is usually represented as a numerical variable, which can be expressed in absolute numbers, in the relationship between two events, or in the quality of the event [28].

The context of interest includes hospital and emergency care settings where the donation process takes place and where it is monitored, evaluated, and regulated by public or private organizations, such as organ procurement organizations, national donation agencies, and transplantation systems. No exception will be made for health settings and geographic areas.

Studies in any language published from 1980 to the present date will be included. The initial date was chosen because organ transplantation started to become a treatment that was used more frequently and with greater success, and policies and practices for the improvement of donation and transplantation were developed [29].

Given the exploratory nature of this scoping review, a wide range of evidence sources will be included, encompassing primary and secondary research from both peer-reviewed and gray literature. Eligible designs include experimental, quasi-experimental, observational (analytical and descriptive), and qualitative studies.

Text and opinion papers, institutional documents from public or private agencies, reports from donation and transplantation organizations, as well as theses and dissertations, will also be considered. In the field of organ and tissue donation, key information on the development, implementation, and monitoring of quality indicators often originates from organizational documents and technical reports rather than academic publications. Including these materials provides a broader and more accurate understanding of existing practices and supports the identification of knowledge and implementation gaps.

Articles deposited in preprint repositories that have not undergone peer review, books, book chapters, and research projects not addressing the research question will be excluded.

Search strategy

The search strategy will aim to find studies published in scientific literature and documents available on electronic media. The search strategies will be developed in two stages with the support of a health sciences librarian with extensive experience in review studies and information sources. The initial stage will be carried out in MEDLINE (via PubMed) to analyze the words of the text contained in the title, abstract, and indexing terms used to describe the article. MEDLINE was chosen because it contains a wide range of international literature. This stage will help build a search strategy that will be adapted for the other databases, portals, and academic directories. The second step will be to carry out a search based on these results and adapt to the syntax and subject heading of each website.

The references and citations of the studies included in the sample will be reviewed to identify additional studies. The search strategy will be adapted for each database, portal, and directory, namely, MEDLINE (via PubMed), CINAHL (EBSCO), Embase (Elsevier), Scopus (Elsevier), Web of Science (Clarivate Analytics), Business Source Complete (EBSCO), and VHL Virtual Health Library. Search strategy

The gray literature will be searched across multiple sources to ensure comprehensive coverage. This will include academic repositories such as Google Scholar, OpenGrey (GreyNet), CAPES Theses and Dissertations, Global ETD Search (formerly the Union Catalog), and Open Access Theses and Dissertations. Additionally, official websites of relevant organizations will be consulted, including The Transplantation Society (TTS), Global Observatory on Donation and Transplantation (GODT), National Transplant Organization (ONT), Latin American and Caribbean Transplant Society (STALYC), European Society for Organ Transplantation (ESOT), Healthcare Quality Improvement Partnership, National Quality Forum, International Society for Quality in Health Care (ISQua), Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ), National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), the Brazilian Ministry of Health, the Brazilian Health Regulatory Agency (ANVISA), and the Collaborating Center for Quality of Care and Patient Safety (PROQUALIS).

Therefore, studies, reports, or documents that investigate the terms and their variations “quality indicators,” “quality metrics,” “health status indicators,” “organ donation,” “tissue donation,” and “tissue and organ procurement” will be included.

Selection of studies, data extraction, and data analysis

The articles and documents retrieved in the search will be grouped in the reference manager Zotero v. 5.0.95.1 (George Mason University, VA, USA), with the duplicates removed in this step, followed by the export of data on the support software and to the JBI System for the Unified Management, Assessment and Review of Information revision support software (JBI SUMARI; JBI, Adelaide, Australia) [30]. Two independent reviewers will read the title and abstract, apply the eligibility criteria, and list the potentially relevant sources for full reading. The data will be extracted from the complete texts that will be completely read and that will fulfill the eligibility criteria. A pilot test will be carried out at the stage of study selection. The reasons for excluding full-text articles that did not meet the inclusion criteria were recorded. Disagreements between the two reviewers were resolved by discussion with a third reviewer. The results of the research will be reported in full in the review of the final scope and presented through the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA-ScR) flow diagram [25].

The recovered data will be extracted by two independent reviewers, and this process will be developed in two stages. First, both reviewers read the titles and abstracts of the studies, listing the materials that met the eligibility criteria.

In the second stage, two reviewers read the full text, again applying the eligibility criteria. The final selection of studies for inclusion will be based on the reading of the complete texts. The extracted data will include specific details about the population, concept, context, methodology, main results, conclusions, and relevant recommendations for the theme of this review.

Data extraction instrument

The collection instrument will be previously tested by the reviewers. Any disagreements will be discussed with a third reviewer, who will be involved in all the stages of the process. It should be noted that the authors of the documents may be contacted to resolve doubts or clarify any unclear elements.

A descriptive analysis will be conducted using simple (n) and relative (%) frequencies to summarize the main characteristics of the included studies and indicators. Following this, the indicator data extraction forms will be organized, and indicators that appear repeatedly will be grouped. The grouping of repeated indicators will be based on an analysis of the title and equation formula of each indicator. The findings will be mapped according to the Donabedian framework (structure, process, and outcome) and the quality pillars (efficiency, efficacy, effectiveness, optimization, acceptability, legitimacy, and equity), as proposed by Donabedian (Table 3) [16]. Discussion

This scoping review aims to map and analyze international literature on indicators for monitoring the quality of the process of deceased organ and tissue donation for transplantation. Through exhaustive search and integration of existing evidence on quality indicators in this process, this review intends to present how these indicators are being used in the evaluation of donation services, assess their comparability, validate their effectiveness, and explore the feasibility of establishing a comprehensive document containing all indicator sheets to support evaluations and enhance international comparability. The results of this review will provide a set of quality-sensitive indicators for managers overseeing the donation process after brain death to evaluate and monitor their services, as well as critically analyze the indicators used. Additionally, these findings can serve as evidence for policies and guidelines aimed at assessing quality in the donation process, offering insights into future topics and research directions to bridge the knowledge gap regarding the use of quality indicators in organ and tissue donation.

One limitation of our review protocol is the potential for selection bias due to the specific databases, libraries, and search portals we have delineated. To mitigate this, we will also search websites of international donation and transplant organizations. Any additional limitations encountered during this study will be detailed in the full scoping report.

Long-term survival outcome of portal vein embolization in patients with perihilar cholangiocarcinoma: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Min Woo Lee, Woo Hyun Paik, Gunn Huh, Dong Ho Lee, Jae Seok Bae, Woo Il Kwon, Jin Young Jang, Jin Ho Choi, In Rae Cho, Ji Kon Ryu, Sang Hyub Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPerihilar cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) often requires extended hepatectomy for curative treatment, especially in Bismuth type III–IV. However, the risk of post-hepatectomy liver failure (PHLF) cannot be ignored. Portal vein embolization (PVE) is a preoperative optimization method that reduces risk of PHLF, and improves surgical safety. This study investigated the impact of PVE on surgical outcomes and long-term survival in patients with Bismuth type III-IV perihilar CCA.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed the data of 137 patients diagnosed with Bismuth type III–IV perihilar CCA between April 2011 and August 2021. Among 137 patients, 88 were included in the analyses and divided into three groups: resection without PVE (group A); resection after PVE (group B); and no resection after PVE (group C). The main outcomes of this study were surgical outcomes and long-term survival outcomes of each group. Survival analyses were performed using inverse probability treatment weighting (IPTW) and Cox regression analysis stratified by surgical resection.ResultsAmong 88 patients, 18 underwent resection without PVE (group A), 55 underwent resection after PVE (group B), and 15 did not proceed to surgery after PVE (group C). There was no significant difference in median recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS) between groups A and B (mRFS 11.9 vs. 18.1 months, P = 0.099; mOS 18.9 vs. 24.8 months; P = 0.454) after IPTW. However, a significant difference was found in median OS between groups B and C (25.9 vs. 10.2 months; P = 0.002). In the stratified Cox regression analysis in groups B and C, age (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 1.979ㅡ13.208), and percentage of future liver remnant after PVE (post-PVE FLR; aHR 0.160ㅡ0.744) were associated with OS regardless of surgical resection.ConclusionsPVE reduces the risk of post-hepatectomy liver failure and enables extended hepatectomy without comparing oncologic outcomes. Furthermore, in groups that underwent PVE, post-PVE measured FLR was an independent prognostic factor of OS, regardless of surgical resection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-025-04141-1.

Introduction

Cholangiocarcinomas (CCA) are aggressive and heterogeneous neoplasms that can develop anywhere in the biliary epithelium. They are classified into intrahepatic, perihilar, and distal CCAs according to their anatomical site of origin [1]. CCAs account for approximately 3%–5% of newly diagnosed gastrointestinal malignancies [2, 3]. Most CCAs are asymptomatic in the early stages and are usually diagnosed in the advanced stages [4]. Because of their aggressive nature and late diagnosis, the 5-year survival rates of all CCAs are approximately 10% [5].

Among all types of CCA, perihilar CCA comprises the largest group (50%–60%), followed by distal CCA (20%–30%) and intrahepatic CCA (10%–20%) [6]. As with other CCAs, the curative option for perihilar CCA is surgical resection with negative margins [7]. However, one-third of patients with perihilar CCA require surgical resection because many are diagnosed with locally advanced or metastatic disease [8]. Furthermore, the R0 resection rate is approximately 75%. Thus, even with curative resection, the recurrence rate is more than 60% [9].

The extent of surgical resection is based on the anatomical location. For bismuth type I-II perihilar CCA, hilar resection is recommended. However, for bismuth type III-IV perihilar CCA, resection of the involved bile duct and hemi-liver is required, which has a high rate of postoperative complication and mortality [7]. Many researchers have reported the role of PVE on post-hepatectomy liver failure and mortality [10–12].

However, there are concerns that tumor progression during the interval between PVE and surgery may compromise long-term oncological outcomes. Such progression has been observed in hepatocellular carcinoma and other biliary tract cancers, including intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and gallbladder cancer, and remains a persistent concern [13, 14]. In contrast, data on this issue in perihilar cholangiocarcinoma are limited, and reports on the postoperative course or survival outcomes of patients who underwent PVE but did not proceed to surgery have rarely been reported. These gaps highlight the need to clarify both perioperative and long-term oncological implications of PVE.

The objective of this study was to evaluate the impact of portal vein embolization on liver volumetric changes, postoperative outcomes—including post-hepatectomy liver failure—and long-term recurrence-free survival and overall survival, in patients with Bismuth type III–IV perihilar cholangiocarcinoma.

Methods

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of our institution, which waived the requirement of informed consent because of the retrospective study design (IRB No. H-2001-092-1095). This study was a retrospective cohort study and was reported in accordance with the STROBE guideline (Supplement document) [15].

Study population

In this retrospective cohort study, we reviewed data on 137 patients diagnosed with Bismuth type III–IV perihilar CCA from April 2011 to August 2021 at our institution. The following patients were included: (1) patients with Bismuth type III or IV stricture involving at least one of the intrahepatic ducts on radiological examination, including computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance cholangiography, (2) patients with perihilar CCA histologically confirmed by surgical resection or forceps biopsy/brush cytology under endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography or through the percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage tract, and (3) patients who underwent therapeutic interventions with therapeutic intent, including surgical resection or preoperative PVE. The following patients were excluded: (1) patients with unresectable perihilar CCA, defined as those with tumor extension to secondary biliary radicles bilaterally, encasement or occlusion of the main portal vein proximal to its bifurcation, or the presence of distant metastases, (2) patients who refused treatment, (3) patients with other concomitant malignancies, and (4) patients who received only palliative or supportive management without curative-intent intervention. To evaluate the effect of PVE, patients were divided into three groups: resection without PVE (group A), resection after PVE (group B), and no resection after PVE (group C). Patients who underwent PVE but did not proceed to surgery (Group C) were those who became inoperable after PVE due to disease progression (such as hepatic metastasis or vascular invasion), biliary sepsis, or deteriorated performance status.

Preoperative evaluation and portal vein embolization

Resectability and extent of resection were determined by a multidisciplinary team of hepatobiliary surgeons, interventional radiologists, and gastroenterologists. The evaluation was based on portal vein involvement (unilateral vs. bilateral vs. main), extension into second-order biliary radicles, hepatic artery involvement, extension into surrounding parenchyma, and distant metastasis [8, 16]. The decision to perform portal vein embolization (PVE) or proceed directly to surgery was made during these multidisciplinary discussions. Patients were considered for direct surgery when the standardized future liver remnant (FLR) was sufficient (≥ 30% in non-cirrhotic livers) and hepatic functional reserve was adequate. In contrast, PVE was recommended when the standardized future liver remnant (FLR) was less than 20% in normal livers, less than 30% in intermediate disease livers without cirrhosis, and less than 40% in cirrhotic livers [11, 17].

PVE was performed using a percutaneous transhepatic approach. During PVE, right anterior and posterior portal veins were embolized. The embolic material was determined at the discretion of interventional radiologists and included gel foam, ethanolamine oleate, and microcoils. After 2–3 weeks from PVE, resectability and disease extent were reassessed using post-PVE CT scans.

In patients proceeding to surgical resection after these evaluations, intraoperative frozen sections of the resection plane were examined to but the predetermined surgical extent was maintained and was not enlarged to ensure a negative margin.

Liver volume measurement

To quantitatively evaluate liver volume, we used axial CT images in the portal phase. Liver volume was measured before and 2–3 weeks after PVE. Of the CT images obtained before PVE, we used CT images taken after biliary drainage to minimize the effect of bile duct dilatation. The total liver volume (TLV) was calculated in two different manners. Standardized total liver volume (TLVStandard) was calculated using linear regression from other studies using body surface area [18]. The measured total liver volume (TLVMeasured) was calculated by subtracting the volume of the dilated biliary tract (Vbile duct) and intrahepatic vessels (Vvessel) from the total liver volume (Vtotal liver), which were measured on cross-sectional imaging. Volume change was calculated by comparing CT scans before and after PVE. Degree of hypertrophy (DH, %) was defined as the difference in FLR before and after PVE [20]. Kinetic growth rate (KGR, %/week) was defined as the degree of hypertrophy divided by the number of weeks elapsed since PVE [21].

Liver segmentation and volume measurement were performed using IntelliSpace Portal software (Philips Healthcare, Best, Netherlands). Two abdominal radiologists (**, with 15 years of experience in abdominal radiology) confirmed the appropriateness of all liver segmentations and volume measurements.

Study outcomes

The main outcomes of this study were surgical outcomes and long-term survival of each group. Surgical outcomes included extent of resection, margin status, surgical complications (Clavien–Dindo classification), post-hepatectomy liver failure (International Study Group of Liver Surgery), 30-day/90-day mortality, and adjuvant treatment in the groups that underwent surgical resection (groups A and B) [22, 23]. After surgical resection, patients were followed every 3–4 months with laboratory tests including serum CA19-9 and radiologic exams such as contrast-enhanced CT or MRI. Recurrence was defined as radiologic or histologic evidence of locoregional or distant tumor relapse, confirmed by a multidisciplinary team review. Survival analyses were performed separately for the groups that underwent surgery (groups A and B) and those that underwent PVE (groups B and C). The recurrence free survival (RFS) was defined as the time from surgical resection to recurrence or death (groups A and B). The overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from each time point (date of surgical resection when comparing groups A and B, and date of PVE when comparing groups B and C) to death from any cause. To investigate the factors associated with OS in patients who underwent PVE, Cox regression analysis stratified by surgical resection was performed in groups B and C.

Statistical analysis

For continuous variables, normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Differences between groups were evaluated using Student’s t-test for continuous variables with normal distributions and chi-square and Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables. For variables that were not normally distributed, the Mann-Whitney test was used instead. Changes in liver volume were analyzed using the paired t-test. The standard cutoff values for FLR, DH, and KGR were estimated using maximally selected rank statistics (Fig. S1). Survival analysis was performed using Kaplan–Meier curves and the log-rank test. The RFS and OS in groups A and B were compared using an inverse probability treatment weighting (IPTW) adjustment to reduce differences in age, sex, BMI, Charson comorbidity index, Bismuth type, ALT, and CA19-9 level [24, 25]. After weighting, standardized mean differences of less than 0.25 were considered adequate (Fig. S2). Factors associated with survival in patients who underwent PVE were analyzed using stratified Cox regression analysis. Variables with P value < 0.2 in the univariate analysis were considered candidates for the multivariate model, which was determined using the bidirectional stepwise selection method. The Cox proportional hazards assumption was tested using the Schoenfeld residual plot and the goodness-of-fit test (Fig. S3). All statistical analyses were conducted using R 4.2.2 software (http://www.r-project.org). Results were considered statistically significant when the two-sided P value < 0.05.

Results

Study population

Among 137 patients diagnosed with perihilar CCA Bismuth type III–IV during the study period, 49 patients were excluded (Fig. 1). In total, 88 patients were included in the analysis, with a median (interquartile range, IQR) age of 67.0 (59.0–72.0) years, of which 66 were male and 22 were female. Eighteen patients underwent surgical resection without PVE (group A), and 70 patients underwent PVE to increase the volume of the remnant liver. Of the 70 patients indicated for PVE, surgical resection was attempted in 55 (group B), whereas surgical resection was not possible in the remaining 15 (group C). The main reasons for inoperability after PVE were as follows: hepatic metastasis (n = 6), vessel invasion (n = 7), and biliary sepsis (n = 2). In group C, all liver metastases and vascular invasion were newly identified on post-PVE CT scans, indicating that tumor progression occurred between PVE and surgery. Additionally, biliary sepsis developed as a procedure-related complication. Although these patients exhibited measurable hypertrophy of the future liver remnant after PVE, resection was not feasible, mainly due to disease progression. Among them, 10 received palliative chemotherapy, two died of biliary sepsis, and three were lost to follow-up. The baseline characteristics of patients are summarized in Table 1.

Post-PVE future liver remnant (FLR), degree of hypertrophy (DH), and kinetic growth rate (KGR) were calculated using the pre-PVE and post-PVE CT scans. The median interval between pre- and post-PVE CT was 18.0 days (IQR 15.0–21.8 days). DH and KGR were not significantly different between groups B and C. When comparing Pre-PVE and Post-PVE FLR of individuals in groups B and C, both standardized and measured FLR significantly increased regardless of surgical resection (All P values < 0.001) (Fig. 2).

Surgical outcomes (groups A and B)

The surgical outcomes of patients who underwent surgical resection are summarized in Table 3. The histopathological results of the surgically resected specimens are described in Table S1. Among the patients who underwent surgical resection without PVE (group A), 55.6% underwent extended right hemihepatectomy, and 33.3% underwent extended left hemihepatectomy. All patients in group B underwent extended right hemihepatectomy after PVE. The rates of R0 resection (50.0% vs. 67.3%, respectively; P = 0.300), postoperative complication grade III or higher (33.3% vs. 49.1%, respectively; P = 0.372), and post-hepatectomy liver failure (P = 0.237) did not differ significantly between groups A and B. The proportion of patients who received adjuvant therapy was lower in group B (94.4% in group A vs. 67.3% in group B; P = 0.053). Adjuvant treatments in both groups mainly consisted of concurrent chemoradiation therapy and gemcitabine-based chemotherapy.

Regarding 30-day/90-day mortality, there was no mortality by 90 days in group A. In contrast, the 30-day mortality rate in group B was 3.6% (n = 2), and the 90-day mortality rate was 12.7% (n = 7), although the differences were not significant. Survival analyses in patients who underwent surgical resection (groups A and B)

Forty-one recurrence occurred during the follow-up period. The median follow-up duration was 11.4 months (IQR 6.1–19.9). The median RFS was longer in group B before IPTW (median 12.2 months vs. 24.3 months; log-rank test P = 0.027, Fig. 3A). However, there was no significant difference after IPTW (median 11.9 months vs. 18.1 months, log-rank test P = 0.099, Fig. 3B).

Discussion

In this study, we analyzed the clinical outcomes of PVE in patients with perihilar CCA. Most patients underwent PVE before extended right hemihepatectomy. Among patients who underwent resection with and without PVE, no significant differences were found in the rates of R0 resection, postoperative complications, and post-hepatectomy liver failure. There was no significant difference in OS and RFS between surgical resection with and without PVE. However, the proportion of patients receiving adjuvant therapy was lower in the PVE group, possibly due to the higher rate of extended hepatectomy and a higher R0 resection rate, which could reduce clinical indications for adjuvant therapy. In Cox regression analysis stratified by surgical resection in patients who underwent PVE, age and post-PVE measured FLR was associated with OS.

Several liver volume variables have been studied in biliary tract cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and colorectal metastasis [10, 26, 27]. In these studies, CT scans at the time of diagnosis and standardized FLR were used, and bile duct dilatation was not estimated. However, unlike intrahepatic tumors or hepatic metastasis, upstream bile duct dilatation often occur in perihilar CCA [28]. To mitigate the impact of biliary obstruction on liver volume, the measured FLR was calculated by excluding the volume of the intrahepatic bile ducts after biliary drainage. This approach enables a more accurate measurement of liver volume. In addition, recent studies indicate that Tc-99 m meboperfenamine hepatobiliary scintigraphy can further improve FLR assessment by integrating volumetric and functional data [29, 30]. Combining these complementary imaging techniques may improve preoperative risk stratification and surgical decision-making for patients with perihilar cholangiocarcinoma.

Compared with studies on Bismuth type I/II perihilar cholangiocarcinoma or different types of tumors, such as gallbladder cancer and intrahepatic CCA, patients with Bismuth type III/IV CCA who underwent surgery in our cohort showed higher rate of post-hepatectomy liver failure and 30-day/90-day mortality [31, 32]. This might be explained by the strict inclusion of patients undergoing high-risk resection for Bismuth type III and IV perihilar CCA.

In survival analyses of groups A and B, there was no difference in RFS and OS, although patients in group B had higher number of post-hepatectomy liver failure and 30-day/90-day mortality. Differences in OS and RFS between surgical resection with and without PVE were not statistically significant. However, the numerically longer OS in the PVE group suggests a potential clinical benefit, particularly in high-risk patients requiring extended hepatectomy. The absence of statistical significance might be attributed to the limited sample size and residual confounding factors. This suggests that PVE could indirectly contribute to long-term survival by enabling extended hepatectomy with adequate oncologic margins (R0 resection) while minimizing the risk of post-hepatectomy liver failure. In Cox regression analyses stratified surgical resection in groups B and C, age and Post-PVE measured FLR was major prognostic factor in survival. It seems that final volume of remnant liver rather than changes over time might have a role in survival. In addition, since Post-PVE measured FLR is a major prognostic factor even after stratification for surgery, post-PVE FLR might also affect adjuvant or palliative treatment.

Our findings suggest that PVE may contribute to improved long-term survival by enabling safer extended hepatectomy. However, this remains inferential rather than directly demonstrated. The association between post-PVE measured FLR and overall survival, regardless of surgical resection, suggests that FLR may serve as a surrogate marker for hepatic reserve or the patient’s overall fitness.

In the survival analysis comparing resection with and without PVE, the Kaplan-Meier curves crossed during the early postoperative period. This crossover appears to reflect higher postoperative mortality or complications in the PVE group, which included more patients who underwent extended right hepatectomy. However, as postoperative recovery progressed and liver function stabilized, long-term survival trends favored the PVE group. This suggests that improved hepatic reserve offset the initial surgical risk. These findings underscore the need to carefully balance short-term procedural risks of PVE and extended hepatectomy against their long-term survival benefits, especially in patients with small FLR. Although PVE effectively increased the FLR and reduced post-hepatectomy liver failure, a subset of patients remained unresectable due to disease progression, even when sufficient hypertrophy was achieved. This finding suggests that biologic, rather than volumetric, failure may occur after PVE, indicating that tumor behavior can outweigh the benefits of liver regeneration.

This study has some limitations, primarily arising from its retrospective design. First, this study was conducted at a single tertiary referral center with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Second, some cases were excluded from the analyses because of treatment refusal by patients. Third, since functional assessments such as indocyanine green retention tests or hepatobiliary scintigraphy were not performed, liver volume was measured morphologically without considering liver functional capacity or regional heterogeneity [30, 33]. Third, other procedures for inducing liver hypertrophy, such as hepatic vein embolization, associating liver partition and portal vein ligation for staged hepatectomy (ALPPS), and associating portal embolization and arterial ligation (APEAL), were excluded from the analysis. Future prospective multicenter studies with larger cohorts, incorporating both volumetric and functional liver assessments and comparing various preoperative optimization techniques, are warranted to better predict clinical outcomes.

The results of this study provide key implications for clinical decision-making. Patients with Bismuth type III–IV hilar cholangiocarcinoma planned for extended right or left hepatectomy, PVE should be considered when measured FLR is low (< 30%) or when underlying hepatic parenchymal dysfunction is present. Conversely, in younger patients with preserved liver function and adequate FLR, direct surgery without PVE may be suitable if negative margins can be achieved. Preoperative optimization based on these parameters would improve both surgical safety and oncologic outcomes.

Conclusions

PVE is preoperative optimization that reduces the risk of post-hepatectomy liver failure and enables extended hepatectomy in the high-risk group, resulting in improved survival of perihilar cholangiocarcinoma. Furthermore, in groups that underwent PVE, post-PVE measured FLR was associated with OS, regardless of surgical resection.

Evidence for a Lattice Supersolid of Subradiant Dipolar Excitons
Camille Lagoin 1 , Corentin Morin 1 , Kirk Baldwin 2 , Loren Pfeiffer 2 and François Dubin 1,‡

Abstract
In condensed-matter physics, supersolids refer to many-body quantum states breaking translational symmetry while exhibiting off-diagonal long-range order Leggett 1970 ; Chester 1970 . This combination is debated for commensurate crystals Yukalov 2020 ; Prokof’ev and Svistunov 2005 , however it is accessible in lattice potentials fractionally filled by bosons with extended interactions Góral et al. 2002 ; Baranov et al. 2012 ; Dutta et al. 2015 ; Li et al. 2013 ; Baumann et al. 2010 ; Mottl et al. 2012 ; Léonard et al. 2017 . Here, we report such lattice supersolid with dipolar excitons confined in a sub-wavelength period potential. Excitons implement the Dicke-Hubbard Hamiltonian controlled by spatially extended dipolar and Dicke correlations. At half lattice-filling, these induce both a condensation in a single sub-radiant state and a dipolar quantum order spontaneously breaking the lattice symmetry. This combination signals a lattice supersolid dissipatively prepared across 8x8 sites. Our study underlines that nanoscopic exciton arrays open a route to explore new frontiers of quantum matter, e.g. many-body entanglement, at the interface with quantum nano-photonics.



Introduction
Following seminal studies proposed for solid Helium
Leggett 1970
, research of supersolid phases has recently been revived in atomic physics
Chomaz et al. 2023
. Supersolid properties were indeed reported when ultra-cold magnetic condensates fragment into small arrays of coherent quantum droplets
Tanzi et al. 2019
;
Böttcher et al. 2019
;
Chomaz et al. 2019
. Supersolid signatures were also accessed in optical cavities where photon fields coherently induce the crystallization of an atomic condensate
Baumann et al. 2010
;
Mottl et al. 2012
;
Léonard et al. 2017
;
Lang et al. 2017
.

Besides atomic systems, semiconductor excitons
Combescot and Shiau 2015
;
Kalt and Klingshirn 2024
provide key resources to access lattice supersolids. Indeed, long-lived excitons of GaAs double quantum wells experience spatially extended dipolar repulsions with record magnitudes. Then, in sub-wavelength period lattices, they emulate the Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian extended by nearest-neighbour (NN) interactions. This model, which theoretically supports a lattice supersolid around half-filling
Góral et al. 2002
;
Baranov et al. 2012
;
Dutta et al. 2015
;
Li et al. 2013
, was signalled by Mott (MI) and checkerboard (CB) insulators
Lagoin et al. 2022a
;
Lagoin et al. 2022b
;
Lagoin et al. 2024
. Critically, these incompressible phases emerge in a collision-less regime where coherent exciton-photon interactions can persist
Hopfield 1958
;
Dubin et al. 2005
. Given that excitons are in this situation separated by less than the wavelength of their dipole-allowed radiative transition, Dicke correlations are expected to spontaneously emerge
Chang et al. 2018
;
Asenjo-Garcia et al. 2017
;
Henriet et al. 2019
;
Rui et al. 2020
;
Tiranov et al. 2023
. These would dramatically increase the effective range and strength of coherent interactions in the lattice.

Here, we demonstrate that dipolar excitons confined in sub-wavelength lattices intrinsically emulate the Dicke-Hubbard Hamiltonian, thus combining spatially extended dipolar and Dicke correlations. The latter are evidenced by the dynamics of excitons radiative recombinations, ruled by sub-radiant modes with emission rates reduced by up to 50-fold compared to free space. For spatially ordered arrays, i.e. for dipolar MI and CB quantum orders, we directly detect Dicke phase-coherence. It signals a condensation in a single sub-radiant state when tunnelling is suppressed such that the many-body Hamiltonian reduces to the Dicke model. At half-filling the CB phase then strikingly exhibits the fingerprints of a lattice supersolid.

Dicke-Hubbard model
We use an array of metal gate-electrodes to define a 250 nm period electrostatic lattice in a GaAs bilayer (Methods). The lattice confines optically injected long-lived dipolar excitons, in a blockade regime where lattice sites have occupations bound to unity. Exciton-exciton interactions then reduce to NN dipolar repulsions, which have a magnitude
V\sim 30

\mu
eV greatly exceeding the tunnelling strength
t\sim 2

\mu
eV (Fig.1.a and Ref.
Lagoin et al. 2022a
). Thus, we emulate the extended Bose-Hubbard (eBH) Hamiltonian
Baranov et al. 2012
;
Dutta et al. 2015
, which is conveniently approximated for our studies by
H_{\mathrm{eBH}}=-t\hskip-4.26773pt\sum\limits_{<i,j>}\hskip-5.69046ptB^{\dagger}_{j}B_{i}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n;i\in\epsilon_{n}\end{subarray}}\hskip-5.69046pt\hbar\omega_{n}B^{\dagger}_{i}B_{i}
. Here,
B_{i}
is the exciton annihilation operator in a site
i
. The first sum restricts to NNs while in the second one lattice sites are classified by the number
n
of NN interactions. This defines the subspaces
\epsilon_{n}
made of lattice sites all lying at the same energy
\hbar\omega_{n}=(nV+\hbar\omega_{0})
,
\hbar\omega_{0}
being the bare confined energy free from NN couplings while
n\in\llbracket 0..4\rrbracket
. MI and CB quantum solids then correspond to spatial orders characterised by suppressed tunnelling and
n=4
and 0 NN, at unity and half lattice fillings respectively.

Dipolar excitons are probed by the photoluminescence (PL) emitted through a dipole-allowed radiative recombination. Thus, the exciton wave-function is projected onto photonic modes, at around 1.53 eV while the refractive index is about 3.7 in GaAs. Figure 1.b displays the profile of the electromagnetic field radiated from one lattice site. Strikingly, one notes that it maintains a large amplitude at the position of NN ones, suggesting that in the lattice NN excitons couple to a common electromagnetic mode
Asenjo-Garcia et al. 2017
;
Chang et al. 2018
. This regime is directly signalled by computing the PL emission rate of two excitons as a function of their spatial separation
Novotny and Hecht 2012
. Figure 1.c shows that decay channels for this two-body situation are super- and sub-radiant (red and blue) for separations up to around 800 nm. Our 250 nm period lattice then probes a regime where the collective coupling to a single electromagnetic mode mediates strong Dicke correlations in the many-body limit
Lehmberg 1970
;
Asenjo-Garcia et al. 2017
;
Henriet et al. 2019
.

In the lattice Hamiltonian, the coherent contribution of Dicke correlations may be reduced to
H_{\mathrm{D}}=\hskip-8.5359pt\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n;(i,j)\in\epsilon_{n}\end{subarray}}\hskip-2.84544pt\hbar J_{ij}B^{\dagger}_{i}B_{j}
where
J_{ij}
defines the rate of photon-mediated delocalisation between the sites
(i\neq j)
in
\epsilon_{n}
that lie at an energy
\hbar\omega_{n}

Chang et al. 2018
. Overall, the unitary dynamics obeys the Dicke-Hubbard model,
H=H_{eBH}+H_{D}
, with 5 accessible Dicke manifolds. Figure 1.d illustrates that each correspond to one subspace
\epsilon_{n}
, i.e. to configurations where excitons have a fixed number
n
of NNs. Critically, we note that exciton hopping incoherently couples Dicke manifolds (see first term of
H_{eBH}
and Fig.1.d). In fact,
H
reduces to the single-mode Dicke model for ordered arrays only, since
n
is fixed and uniform spatially.

Sub-radiant exciton dynamics
As illustrated in Fig.2.a, we perform stroboscopic measurements starting by a 100 ns long laser pulse loading excitons across 15x15 sites of the lattice. The average laser power
P
controls the density of photo-injected carriers and we restrict our studies to the center of the illuminated zone (8x8 sites, see Methods). There, short after the laser extinction the exciton density is large, around
10^{10}
cm
-2
, so that the PL spectral width is about 900
\mu
eV (Fig.2.b). It marks that a high rate of dipolar collisions screens the lattice potential (with 250
\mu
eV depth). In addition, the PL intensity decays with a characteristic time around 30 ns, identical to the one measured in a flat confinement landscape (gray curve in Fig.2.c). By contrast, for delays greater than about 200 ns, i.e. in the very dilute regime where dipolar repulsions no longer screen the lattice, Fig.2.b shows that the PL has a spectral width around our instrumental resolution (right panel in Fig.2.b). This manifests that the lattice is homogeneously occupied
Lagoin et al. 2022b
. At the same time the PL intensity hardly decays with the delay to the loading laser termination. It signals that the rate of radiative recombinations is considerably decreased. Given that the exciton relative motion is not modified by the lattice potential, Fig.2.b suggests that Dicke correlations govern the PL emission-rate
Gross and Haroche 1982
;
Guerin et al.
;
Masson et al. 2020
;
Yan et al. 2023
. Long after the laser excitation, in the lattice excitons would then be spontaneously prepared in sub-radiant many-body states.

We expect that Dicke correlations are strengthened by increasing the lattice filling, since the average spatial separation between excitons is reduced. Sub-radiant phases with longer lifetimes hence become accessible
Henriet et al. 2019
. This behaviour is verified in Fig.2.c which compares the PL dynamics for
P=10
and 35 nW. The average filling
\bar{N}
is accordingly increased from about 0.6 to 1, 250 ns after extinction of the loading laser pulse (Extended Data Fig.1.a). As a result, the PL decay time is enhanced from around 0.6 to 1.5
\mu
s (see also Extended Data Fig.2). Let us quantify the variation of the PL decay with
P
by the ratio
r
between the PL intensity emitted 950 and 250 ns after extinction of the loading pulse. Then, the inset in Fig.2.c shows that
r=0
for
P=6
nW, revealing that excitons do not exhibit any sizeable sub-radiant dynamics at very low lattice fillings. For larger ones,
r
rapidly increases with
P
before saturating when
\bar{N}
is around unity. Hence, we confirm that the PL decay is ultimately bound by the period of the lattice potential, reaching magnitudes 50 fold larger than in a flat confinement landscape (gray curve in Fig.2.c).

We further monitored the thermal variation of the PL dynamics, as illustrated in Fig.2.d for
P=20
nW so that 250 ns after extinction of the loading laser pulse
\bar{N}\simeq 0.7
(Extended Data Fig.1.a). One observes that the PL decay barely varies between 0.33 and 1.75 K (violet and orange). However it rapidly decreases at 2.8 K (red) – Extended Data Fig.3 details the PL dynamics with
T
. Again, the scaling of the PL decay with temperature is quantified by computing
r
. The inset in Fig.2.d shows that it abruptly decreases above 2.5 K, i.e. in the regime where the thermal energy compensates the lattice depth. Hence, excitons are efficiently excited towards unconfined higher energy-states that lie in the continuum, which suppresses sub-radiant channels. We thus deduce that Dicke correlations are robust against thermal decoherence, as in atomic systems
Weiss et al. 2019
.

Dicke coherence
We quantified the excitons degree of coherence by monitoring the amplitude of the PL first-order time-correlation function
|g^{(1)}(\tau)|
(Methods). For a fixed 400 ns delay to the termination of the laser excitation,
|g^{(1)}|
is studied as a function of
\bar{N}
by varying
P
. Figure 3.a first displays
|g^{(1)}|
for a normal compressible phase realised at
\bar{N}\simeq 0.7
(dark red). We note that it decays mono-exponentially for
\tau\gtrsim
5 ps, with a characteristic time around 6 ps. This magnitude reveals that the relative phase between occupied Dicke manifolds rapidly diffuses. Such multi-mode Dicke regime is hence characteristics of compressible exciton distributions for which global phase coherence cannot buildup.

Figure 3.a also displays in orange the amplitude of the
g^{(1)}
-function at
\bar{N}\simeq 0.5
. In this situation intensity fluctuations of the PL are strongly minimised (Extended Data Fig.1.c), which verifies that an incompressible CB solid is realised in the lattice
Lagoin et al. 2022a
;
Lagoin et al. 2024
. Then, we strikingly note that
|g^{(1)}|
does not decay for
\tau\gtrsim
5 ps. Instead it remains constant in our measurement range so that we only estimate a lower bound of the coherence time, at least 100 ps which marks over a 20-fold increase compared to the compressible regime. This threshold signals that excitons occupy a single sub-radiant Dicke state of the
\epsilon_{0}
manifold. For our experiments probing 8x8 sites, around 35 excitons undergo this quantum condensation. It results in a PL spectral width reduced to at most the
\mu
eV range, strikingly providing the seminally expected signature of excitons Bose-Einstein condensation
Moskalenko and Snoke 2000
. From the latter and the translational symmetry broken by CB order, we conclude that a lattice supersolid is realised at half filling.

We anticipate that global phase-coherence is restricted to incompressible states. We verified this expectation by probing
|g^{(1)}|
for a wide range of lattice fillings while
\tau
was set to 12.1 ps (see vertical line in Fig.3.a). Figure 3.b directly signals that
|g^{(1)}|
is only maximised at
\bar{N}\simeq 1/2
(orange) and
\bar{N}\simeq 1
(blue). For the latter situation, Extended Data Fig.1.c shows that the exciton compressibility is minimised which verifies that a MI state is realised, excitons occupying the
\epsilon_{4}
manifold. Furthermore, as for CB order,
|g^{(1)}|(\tau)
does not decay for
\tau
up to 20 ps (Extended Data Fig.4) signalling that the excitons temporal coherence experiences another threshold increase. It marks a second regime of quantum condensation in a single sub-radiant state, but with Mott order. We note that this many-body phase complements previous studies with ultra-cold atoms
Klinder et al. 2015
.

We finally studied the thermal stability of excitonic coherence. Figures 3.c and 3.d present the temperature dependence of
|g^{(1)}|
at
\bar{N}=1
and 1/2. For the latter extended coherence persists up to approximately 900 mK before abruptly vanishing. This relatively high critical temperature marks that CB order is protected in our device by a larger gap than in previous reports
Lagoin et al. 2022a
, suggesting stronger NN dipolar repulsions. On the other hand, Fig.3.d shows that extended coherence is maintained up to around 1.1K at unity filling. This magnitude is comparable to the critical temperature previously measured for the melting of the MI phase
Lagoin et al. 2022a
;
Lagoin et al. 2022b
. It manifests that Mott order remains robust despite the competition between NN dipolar repulsions and the lattice depth. Indeed, in the
\epsilon_{4}
manifold excitons occupy energy states at the edge of the lattice barrier (Fig.1.a), which fragilises the MI geometry compared to the CB one.

Conclusions
We have shown that dipolar excitons confined in sub-wavelength electrostatic lattices emulate a lattice model where dipolar and Dicke correlations control spatial order and phase coherence. Compressible distributions induce multi-mode Dicke correlations in the lattice, and excitons dynamically realize sub-radiant many-body phases in the long-time limit. On the other hand, for dipolar quantum solids Dicke correlations are restricted to a unique collective mode and excitons undergo Bose-Einstein condensation in a single sub-radiant state. At half filling the lattice translational symmetry is broken and a lattice supersolid is accessed at quasi-equilibrium in the long-time limit. To our view, the dissipative dynamics yielding such strongly correlated regime raises fascinating questions. Also, we note that Dicke correlations could play a key role in earlier research studying the interplay between anomalously weak photoluminescence and Bose-Einstein condensation
High et al. 2012
;
Shilo et al. 2013
;
Beian et al. 2017
;
Combescot et al. 2017
;
Misra et al. 2022
.

METHODS

Electrostatic lattice
Our device replicates the one studied in Refs.
Lagoin et al. 2022a
;
Lagoin et al. 2024
;
Lagoin et al. 2023
. It relies on two 8 nm wide GaAs quantum wells separated by a 4 nm AlGaAs barrier. On the surface of the field-effect structure, i.e.
220\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
above the quantum wells, we deposited an array of gate electrodes. These are biased at around (-0.85) Volt and imprint an electrostatic lattice with approximately 250
\mu
eV depth, a square geometry with 250 nm period, and made by 120x120 sites.

Optical excitation/detection
Dipolar excitons are optically injected using a 100 ns long laser excitation, at resonance with the direct excitons absorption of the two GaAs quantum wells. The laser illumination has a gaussian spatial profile with a full-width-at-half-maximum around 2
\mu
m. Thus, we excite about 15x15 lattice sites but we restrict our studies to the PL emitted around the center of the illuminated region (8x8 sites). Moreover, note that we vary the laser repetition rate between 600 and 900 kHz (Figs. 2 and 3 respectively). In both cases sub-radiant modes exhibit lifetimes exceeding the period between successive excitation pulses. At a given delay to the termination of the laser excitation, e.g. 250 and 400 for Figs.2-3 respectively, the filling
\bar{N}
hence depends non-trivially on the excitation power
P
. Nevertheless, we empirically control
\bar{N}
with
P
(Extended Data Fig.1).

First-order correlation function
We extract
|g^{(1)}|
by computing the interference contrast of the PL passing through a Mach-Zehnder interferometer with a variable optical delay
\tau
between its two arms. The corresponding path length difference is actively stabilised, with around 30 nm precision, and we extract the contrast for each optical delay
\tau
by comparing the maximum and minimum output signals while the relative phase difference between the two interfering arms is scanned. Note that the optical delay
\tau
is set by combining a micro-metric stage positioning one mirror of the interferometer, and glass plates delaying further the PL.

Calibration of the lattice filling
Our lattice only operates in the dipolar blockade regime where its sites have occupations that can not exceed unity. In this situation one expects that the integrated intensity of the PL, which marks the average number of excitons in the lattice, saturates while the average power of the loading laser pulse is increased. For the experiments reported in Fig.2 and 3, Extended Data Fig.1.a-b quantify this behaviour. At saturation, we thus deduce that the average filling is around unity, from which we infer the mean occupation for lower excitations. Let us then note that this calibration procedure is confirmed by computing intensity fluctuations of the PL that quantify the exciton compressibility
\kappa
. Precisely, we observe that
\kappa
is minimised around half and unity lattice fillings (see Extended Data Fig.1.c). In Extended Data Fig.1.b-c the gray shaded areas mark the regimes where
\kappa
is minimum, corresponding to CB and MI orders respectively.

Excitons optical dipole
The electric field radiated by one exciton optical dipole was computed using finite element simulations taking into account the complete geometry of our device. To deduce the two-exciton decay rates, we then used the imaginary part of the electric field that relates to the Dyadic Green function
Novotny and Hecht 2012
.

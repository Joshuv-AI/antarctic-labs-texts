On dominant ℓ \ell –weights and maps between Weyl modules for quantum affine A n A_{n} Thanks: M.B. was partially supported CNPq grant 405793/2023-5 Thanks: V.C. was partially supported by a travel grant from the Simons Foundation.
Matheus Brito, Vyjayanthi Chari

Abstract
. We determine the set of dominant ℓ \ell –weights in the Weyl (or standard) modules for quantum affine A n A_{n} . We then prove that the space of homomorphisms between standard modules is at most one-dimensional and give a necessary and sufficient condition for equality to hold. We also describe the socle of the standard module and prove that the socle is simple for large n n . Finally, we give applications of our results to mixed Weyl modules, calculating extensions in the category and identify new families of tensor subcategories of finite dimensional representations.



Introduction

The category of finite-dimensional representations of a quantum affine algebra
\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_{n}
(associated to a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
) has been studied intensively for a long time. There are still many interesting open questions about the structure of the representations
and the homological properties of the category are largely unexplored. The goal of this paper is to address some of the basic questions in the case when
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n}
.

It was shown in
[
7
]
that there exists a universal family of finite-dimensional modules, called Weyl modules which are indexed by the same set as the irreducible objects of the category. In many ways, the Weyl modules, although finite-dimensional, play a role analogous to the Verma modules in the Bernstein–Gelfand–Gelfand category
\cal O(\mathfrak{g})
. For instance, the results of
[
2
]
,
[
20
]
show that the Weyl modules admit a nice tensor product decomposition. This allows one to compute their
q
–characters in an explicit way.
The irreducible modules on the other hand have complicated structure in general and their characters are alternating triangular sums of the character of the Weyl modules.

Proving even these results, in the quantum affine case, is more complicated than in the case of
\cal O
where the character of the Verma module is easily computed. This is because the Verma modules are free over a nice subalgebra; the Weyl modules by contrast have no such property. This difference also causes difficulties in other natural questions that one could ask about Weyl modules. For instance, one of the starting points in the study of the category
\cal O
is the understanding of space of maps between two Verma modules and also proving the existence of a simple socle.
In this paper we study the analogs of these results for Weyl modules in type
A_{n}
. We restrict our attention to the Hernandez–Leclerc subcategory
\mathscr{F}_{n}
of finite-dimensional modules. This subcategory arises naturally in connections to monoidal categorification of cluster algebras and quiver Hecke algebras (
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
) and exhibits a rich combinatorial structure. They are also connected through a Schur–Weyl type duality with complex smooth representations of
GL_{N}(F)
, where
F
is a non-Archimedean field (
[
12
,
17
,
18
]
).

Our main results are the following. We prove that
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_{n}}(W_{1},W_{2})\leq 1
where
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are Weyl modules and we give a necessary and sufficient condition for equality to hold. We show that any non-zero between Weyl modules is injective. We give an algorithm to determine the socle and prove that the irreducible modules which occur in the socle are all Weyl modules and occur multiplicity one. Under suitable conditions we prove that the socle is simple and describe it explicitly. We emphasize here that the socle is not always a fundamental Weyl module; this is in sharp contrast to what happens in the classical limit, see for instance
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
.

In the final section of the paper we give some applications of our results. We show that one can define canonically, families of tensor subcategories of
\mathscr{F}_{n}
, generalizing the category
\mathscr{C}_{\ell}
of
[
13
]
. We also study mixed Weyl modules; these are modules which have the same character as the Weyl modules but are not isomorphic to a Weyl module. We identify certain canonical modules which occur in the head and the socle of a Weyl module. The work of
[
11
]
shows that even in the case of
A_{1}
the socle of a mixed Weyl module will have multiplicity.
Finally, we give a sufficient condition for
\operatorname{Ext}_{\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_{n}}^{1}(V,W)
to be trivial where
V,W
are modules in
\mathscr{F}_{n}
.

The extension of our results to the quantum affine algebra associated to other types is complicated; in part this is because the structure and character of the fundamental Weyl modules is more complicated and the theory of
q
–characters for the irreducible modules is not as well–developed. We hope to return to these questions in subsequent papers.

We state our first main result.

Theorem 1

Let

(i)

Given

1.6.

Our second result is the following.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{I}_{n}^{r}
.

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r}\in\cal Q_{n}^{+}}\left(W({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{i_{1},j_{1}})_{{\mbox{\boldmath\scriptsize$\omega$}}_{i_{1},j_{1}}\eta_{1}^{-1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes W({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{i_{r},j_{r}})_{{\mbox{\boldmath\scriptsize$\omega$}}_{i_{r},j_{r}}\eta_{r}^{-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle W_{n}({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{i_{1},j_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes W_{n}({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{i_{r},j_{r}}).

It follows that

and hence
\iota
is an isomorphism.

Suppose now that we have a non-zero map

and hence a non-zero map

\tilde{\eta}:W_{n}({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})\to W_{n}({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}})\cong\tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{n}w_{\mathbf{s}}\hookrightarrow W_{N}({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}}).

Then it follows from

]
that

is an
\ell
–weight vector with
\ell
–weight
{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}
. Moreover since

is
\ell
–highest weight it follows

for all

and

. Since

is in the subgroup of

generated by

with

for all

and

. Hence we have a non-zero map

hold if

Next we prove that the socle of

(with multiplicity one) where

5.7.
Extensions

As another application of our results we discuss extensions in the category
\mathscr{F}_{n}
. Given a closed subset
\mathbb{J}\subset\mathbb{I}_{n}^{r}
, let
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{J})
be the full subcategory of
\mathscr{F}_{n}
consisting of objects whose Jordan–Hölder constituents are isomorphic to
V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}})
with
{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}}\in\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}:\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{J}\}
. Clearly
\mathscr{F}_{n}
is an abelian category.

Proposition
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{J}_{1}
and
\mathbb{J}_{2}
are closed subsets of
\mathbb{I}_{n}^{r_{1}}
and
\mathbb{I}_{n}^{r_{2}}
, respectively, and

\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}}:\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{J}_{1}\}\cap\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}}:\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{J}_{2}\}=\emptyset.

Suppose that
V_{p}
is an object of
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{J}_{p})
for
p=1,2
. Then

\operatorname{Ext}^{s}_{\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_{n}}(V_{1},V_{2})=0,\ \ s=0,1.

Proof.

It is clear from Theorem
1
that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_{n}}(V_{1},V_{2})=0
and hence the proposition holds for
s=0
.

For
s=1
we must prove that any short exact sequence

0\to V_{2}\to V\to V_{1}\to 0

of modules in
\mathscr{F}_{n}
splits.
Assume first that
V_{p}=V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{p}})
are objects of
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{J}_{p})
for
p=1,2
. If
V
is a quotient of
W({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}})
then since
V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})
is a further quotient of
V
, we must have
{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}
. Since
V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}})
is a submodule of
V
we have
{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\in\operatorname{wt}_{\ell}^{+}W({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})
. It follows from Theorem
1
that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}
for some
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1}[n]
. Hence we get

\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}}:\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{J}_{2}\}\ni{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\in\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}}:\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}\in\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{1}[n]\}\subset\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}}:\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{J}_{1}\},

where the last inclusion follows since
\mathbb{J}_{1}
is closed. But this contradicts the hypothesis of the proposition.

In particular, this proves that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\notin{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}(\cal Q^{+}_{n})^{-1}
. Working with duals, a similar argument proves that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}\notin{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}(\cal Q_{n}^{+})^{-1}
. Hence it follows that the pre-image of
V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})
cannot intersect
V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}})
and the proof is complete in this case.

Suppose now that we know the result when
V_{1}
has length
\ell-1
. To prove it when it has length
\ell
, let
V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})
be a quotient of
V_{1}
and consider the short exact sequence

0\to V_{1}^{\prime}\to V_{1}\to V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})\to 0.

Since
\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_{n}}(V_{1},V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}))=0
we have a left exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}),V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}))\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(V_{1},V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}))\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(V_{1}^{\prime},V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}})).

Since the terms on the ends are zero by the inductive hypothesis, it follows that the middle term is zero as needed. A similar argument applies if we replace
V({\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}}_{\mathbf{s}_{2}})
by
V_{2}
and the proposition follows.
∎

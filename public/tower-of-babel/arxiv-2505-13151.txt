Homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian structures of metrics of Kaluza-Klein type on the three-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime
Fumihiro Ueno

Abstract
. We classify homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian structures of a three-parameter family of metrics called Kaluza-Klein type on the three-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime ( AdS 3 ) , (\operatorname{AdS}_{3}), which is diffeomorphic to 𝕊 1 × ℝ 2 , \mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}, with their induced groups of isometries and reductive decompositions. We also obtain the classification of homogeneous almost contact and paracontact metric structures of metrics of Kaluza-Klein type on AdS 3 \operatorname{AdS}_{3} with their isometry groups and reductive decompositions.

Introduction

This research aims to investigate homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian structures, which are
(1,2)
-type tensor fields corresponding to coset spaces, of metrics called Kaluza-Klein type on the three-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime
(\operatorname{AdS}_{3})

\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
with the induced reductive decompositions and the isometry groups.
Finally, we obtain the classification of homogeneous almost contact and paracontact metric structures of the metrics on
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}

The Berger sphere is known as a homogeneous Riemannian manifold diffeomorphic to the three-dimensional sphere (
\mathbb{S}^{3}
). A metric on the Berger sphere is obtained by deforming the metric of the standard sphere along the fiber of the Hopf fibration
\mathbb{S}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}^{3}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Homogeneous Riemannian structures on Berger spheres were studied in
[
GO05
]
.
Just as the Berger metrics on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
appear as metrics of Kaluza-Klein type studied in
[
CP13
]
, one can similarly obtain Berger-like metrics to deform the standard metric on
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
as metrics of Kaluza-Klein type studied in
[
CP14
]
.

There are many similarities between the geometry of
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
and
\mathbb{S}^{3}.

One such similarity is that both are maximally symmetric spaces with a six-dimensional space of Killing vector fields.
As for the differences, since
\mathbb{S}^{3}
is an isotropic Riemannian manifold, any choice of Killing vector field generated by the left (resp. right) action yields the same spacelike
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-fibration known as the Hopf fibration. In contrast,
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
is an (anisotropic) Lorentzian manifold that admits both timelike
\mathbb{S}^{1}
- and spacelike
\mathbb{R}
-fibrations obtained by the same construction.
These Hopf-like fibrations appear in the deformation of metrics that are homothetic to the standard metric on
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
if exactly two elements of
\{-\lambda,\mu,\nu\}
in a metric of Kaluza-Klein type
g_{\lambda\mu\nu}
coincide. If neither of them coincide, it implies that the stabilizer of the isometry group is trivial.
We refer to these as the Hopf fibrations on
\operatorname{AdS}_{3},
as explained in Section
A
.

Owning to Cartan’s work, a locally symmetric Riemannian manifold is characterized as a parallel curvature tensor field of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
.
It was extended to the local homogeneity of a Riemannian manifold using a
(1,2)
-type tensor field
S
, which defines the canonical (Ambrose-Singer) connection
\nabla-S
satisfying the parallel conditions, called a homogeneous Riemannian structure in
[
TV83
]
.
In other words, a homogeneous Riemannian structure is a
(1,2)
-type tensor field preserved by the action of the isometry group.
In
[
Kir80
]
, Kiričhenko generalized the results of Ambrose-Singer
[
AS58
]
to the case when a homogeneous Riemannian manifold is equipped with geometric structures.
A homogeneous almost (para)contact metric structure, which consists of a homogeneous Riemannian structure and an almost (para)contact metric structure preserved by the action of the isometry group, is one of them.
All these results are extended to the reductive homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian case of an arbitrary signature and summarized in
[
CL19
]
.
All homogeneous Riemannian manifolds are reductive, while there are homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian manifolds that are not reductive.

Three-dimensional connected, simply connected, and complete homogeneous Lorentzian manifolds are classified by Calvaruso in
[
Cal07
]
as well as the Riemannian cases in
[
Sek77
]
. In particular, while the three-dimensional homogeneous Riemannian structures have been classified in
[
Sek77
]
,
[
Abe86
]
,
[
CLFSGRVL23
]
,
[
Ohn23
]
, and
[
IO25
]
, the three-dimensional homogeneous Lorentzian structures remain unclassified.
Recently, homogeneous Lorentzian structures on non-symmetric three-dimensional Lie groups have been classified in
[
CZ25
]
. In this article, we classify homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian structures on metrics of Kaluza-Klein type on
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
, which can be regarded as diagonal left-invariant metrics on
\operatorname{SU}(1,1),
including both symmetric and non-symmetric cases.
Although
[
CLFSGRVL23
]
and
[
CZ25
]
includes non-symmetric results of classification of homogeneous psuedo-Riemannian structures on Lie groups, we not only recover some of those but also determine their reductive decompositions and show that the corresponding isometry groups act transitively and (almost) effectively.

Homogeneous almost contact metric structures on the Berger spheres were also studied in
[
GO05
]
.
In this research, we obtain the list of homogeneous almost contact and paracontact metric structures of metrics of Kaluza-Klein type on
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}.

There is a one-parameter family of homogeneous almost contact (resp. paracontact) metric structures induced by the isometry groups and its reductive decompositions on the standard
\operatorname{AdS}_{3},
which is a Lorentzian Sasakian (resp. paraSasakian) manifold. If we deform the metric along a timelike (resp. spacelike) Hopf-fiber
\mathbb{S}^{1}
(resp.
\mathbb{R}),
the obtained homogeneous almost contact (resp. paracontact) metric structures are defined on the
\alpha
-Sasakian (resp.
\beta
-paraSasakian) manifolds.
In Table
1.2
, we obtain the list of homogeneous almost contact and paracontact metric structures, up to isomorphism, with the isometry groups that possess non-trivial stabilizers, using the notation in Section
4
and Section

. Homogeneous almost (para)contact metric structures and mixed metric 3-structures of Kaluza-Klein type metrics on
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
studied by Calvaruso and Perrone in
[
CP14
]
are summarized in Section
6
with the trivial isometry groups and reductive decompositions.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Homogeneous pseudo-Riemanninan structures

Let
(M,g)
be a connected pseudo-Riemannian manifold. Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection of
(M,g),
and
R
be the curvature tensor field that we use the following conventions:

(2.1)

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,\quad R(X,Y,Z,W)=g(R(X,Y)Z,W),

for all vector fields
X,Y,Z,W\in\Gamma(TM).

Definition 2.1
.

A pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called homogeneous if there exists a Lie group of isometries that acts transitively on
M
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a Lie group, and
H\subset G
be a Lie subgroup of
G.

A homogeneous space
G/H
is reductive if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
can be decomposed as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m},
where
\mathfrak{h}
is the Lie algebra of
H,
and
\mathfrak{m}
is an
\operatorname{Ad}|_{H}
-invariant subspace, that is,
\operatorname{Ad}_{h}(\mathfrak{m})\subset\mathfrak{m}
for an arbitrary
h\in H.

Definition 2.3
.

A homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian structure on
(M,g)
is a
(1,2)
-type tensor field
S
such that the canonical connection
\widetilde{\nabla}=\nabla-S
satisfies the following equations:

(2.2)

\widetilde{\nabla}g=0,\quad\widetilde{\nabla}R=0,\quad\widetilde{\nabla}S=0.

We also denote by
S
the associated tensor field of type
(0,3)
on
(M,g)
defined by

(2.3)

S(X,Y,Z)=g(S_{X}Y,Z).(X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM))

In particular, Ambrose and Singer gave a characterization of homogeneity in
[
AS58
]
the Riemannian case, and later generalized in
[
GO97
]
to the metrics of an arbitrary signature.
The characterization is given as follows. A connected, simply connected, and complete reductive homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian manifold is a pseudo-Riemannian manifold admitting a linear connection
\widetilde{\nabla}=\nabla-S
satisfying (
2.2
).
Let
(M,g)
be a reductive homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian manifold, that is
M=G/H
, where
G
is a connected Lie subgroup of isometry of
(M,g),
and
H
is the isotropy group at a point
o\in M.

The
\operatorname{Ad}|_{H}
-invariant subspace
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{g}
of the reductive decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
is identified with
T_{o}M
through the isomorphism

(2.4)

\tau\colon\mathfrak{m}\ni X\mapsto X^{*}|_{o}\in T_{o}M,

where
X^{*}
is the Killing vector field generated by the one-parameter subgroup
\{\exp(sX)\}
of
G
acting on
M
from the left.
Then the canonical connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
of the reductive homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M=G/H,g)
is determined by its value at
o,
by

(2.5)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X^{*}}Y^{*}|_{o}=-[X,Y]^{*}_{\mathfrak{m}}|_{o},\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{m},

and
S=\nabla-\widetilde{\nabla}
is the homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian structure induced by the reductive decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}.

Conversely, assume that a
(1,2)
-type tensor field
S
on
(M,g)
, which is a connected, simply connected, and complete pseudo-Riemannian manifold, satisfies the condition (
2.2
) with respect to the canonical connection
\widetilde{\nabla}=\nabla-S.

Fix a point
o\in M,
and set
\mathfrak{m}=T_{o}M.

If
{\widetilde{R}}
denotes the curvature tensor of the connection
\widetilde{\nabla},
then the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\widetilde{\nabla}
is generated by
{\widetilde{R}}(X,Y)
for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{m}.

Now we define a Lie algebra structure on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
by

(2.6)

\displaystyle[U,V]

\displaystyle=UV-VU,

(2.7)

\displaystyle[U,X]

\displaystyle=U(X),

(2.8)

\displaystyle[X,Y]

\displaystyle=S_{X}Y-S_{Y}X-{\widetilde{R}}(X,Y),

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{m},
and
U,V\in\mathfrak{h}.

Let
{\widetilde{G}}
be a connected and simply connected Lie group whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{g},
and
{\widetilde{H}}
be a connected Lie subgroup of
{\widetilde{G}}
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{h}.
Then
{\widetilde{G}}
acts transitively and almost effectively as a group of isometries on
(M,g),
hence
M
is diffeomorphic to
{\widetilde{G}}/{\widetilde{H}}.

We can choose the discrete normal subgroup
\Gamma
of
{\widetilde{G}}
that acts trivially on
M
, then
G={\widetilde{G}}/\Gamma
acts transitively and effectively on
M
as a group of isometries with the isotropy group
H={\widetilde{H}}/\Gamma.

Therefore,
(M,g)
is a reductive homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian manifold diffeomorphic to
G/H.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a reductive homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian manifold, and let
S
and
S^{\prime}
be two homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian structures on
(M,g).

The two homogeneous structures are said to be isomorphic if there exists an isometry
\varphi
on
(M,g)

such that
\varphi_{*}(S_{X}Y)=S^{\prime}_{\varphi_{*}X}{\varphi_{*}Y}
for
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
or
\varphi^{*}S^{\prime}=S
as a
(0,3)
-type tensor field.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
TV83
]

Let
(M,g)
be a reductive homogeneous pseudo-Riemannian manifold, and let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be connected Lie subgroups of its isometry group that act transitively on
M.

Assume that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
) of
G
(resp.
G^{\prime}
) has a reductive decompostion
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
).
The homogeneous structures
S
and
S^{\prime}
defined from these isometric actions are isomorphic if and only if there exists a Lie algebra isomorphism
\psi
from
\mathfrak{g}
to

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\psi(\mathfrak{m})=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
and
\psi(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},

(2)

\psi|_{\mathfrak{m}}
induces an isometry from
(T_{o}M,g_{o})
to
(T_{o^{\prime}}M,g_{o^{\prime}})
via the maps
\tau\colon\mathfrak{m}\ni X\mapsto X^{*}|_{o}\in T_{o}M,
and
\tau^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\ni X^{\prime}\mapsto X^{\prime*}|_{o^{\prime}}\in T_{o^{\prime}}M.

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold.
We shall see the Ehresmann connection on the tangent bundle as in
[
Sak96
]
.
Let
(\mathcal{U},x)
be a local coordinate neighborhood of
p\in M,
and
(T\mathcal{U},(x,\xi))
be a local coordinate neighborhood of
u\in T_{p}M
.
There are two vector bundle structures on
TM
with the following local expressions:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\pi_{TTM}\colon T_{u}TM\ni\left.X^{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right|_{p}+\left.U^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{i}}\right|_{u}

\displaystyle\mapsto\left.\xi(u)^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right|_{p}=u\in T_{p}M,

(2.10)

\displaystyle d\pi_{TM}\colon T_{u}TM\ni\left.X^{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right|_{p}+\left.U^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{i}}\right|_{u}

\displaystyle\mapsto\left.X^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right|_{p}\in T_{p}M,

where
X,U\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Definition 2.6
.

The Ehresmann connection of the tangent bundle
TM
is the following direct sum decomposition:

(2.11)

TTM=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V},

where
\mathcal{V}
is the kernel of

We call
\mathcal{V}(
resp.
\mathcal{H})
a vertical (resp.horizontal) subbundle of

.

Since
T_{p}M\subset TM
is an
m

T_{u}T_{p}M\subset T_{u}TM
is an
m
-dimensional subspace,
\mathcal{V}_{u}=\ker(d\pi_{TM}|_{u}\colon T_{u}TM\to TM)=T_{u}T_{p}M.

The vertical space is isomorphic to the tangent space of the base manifold at the same point via the following map.

(2.12)

\iota_{u}\colon\mathcal{V}_{u}=T_{u}T_{p}M\ni U^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{i}}\mapsto U^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\in T_{p}M.

Now we shall define the connection map
K\colon TTM\to TM
that is equivalent to the horizontal subbundle of
TTM
.

.

.

Let

If we have a connection defined by a connection map

(2.17)

Here, we identify the tangent vector

with a vector in
T_{u}TM.

g
-natural metrics form a wide family of metrics on

which depends on six smooth functions from

to

has been completely described in

We apply the notion of a

The tangent space of the unit sphere bundle

(2.18)

Then a

G

\displaystyle G_{u}(w_{1}^{h},w_{2}^{h})

\displaystyle=(a+c)g(w_{1},w_{2})+d\ g(w_{1},u)g(w_{2},u),

(2.19)

\displaystyle G_{u}(w_{1}^{h},{w^{\prime}_{2}}^{v})

\displaystyle=bg(w_{1},w^{\prime}_{2}),

\displaystyle G_{u}({w^{\prime}_{1}}^{v},{w^{\prime}_{2}}^{v})

\displaystyle=ag(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2}),

where

In this case, the metrics of Kaluza-Klein type on

Since the standard

\operatorname{SO}_{0}(2,2)\simeq(\operatorname{SU}(1,1)\times\operatorname{SU}(1,1))/\mathbb{Z}_{2},
then the following isometric action is transitive and almost effective.

(3.100)

\phi\colon\operatorname{SU}(1,1)_{\text{L}}\times\operatorname{SU}(1,1)_{\text{R}}\times\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}\ni((g_{L},g_{R}),Z)\mapsto g_{L}Zg_{R}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}.

Let

Then
U_{0}=(X_{0},X_{0}),\ U_{1}=(X_{1},-X_{1}),

\mathfrak{su}(1,1),
of the stabilizer group at

Then each

and

from the left and right respectively, i.e.

(3.101)

for

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume that
-\lambda=\mu=\nu=1.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{h}_{0}
be a Lie subalgebra and let
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{so}(2,2)
be a
\mathfrak{h}

Then any non-trivial reductive decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
that is linearly isomorphic to

is one of the following up to isomorphism in the sense of Theorem

(1)

If a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{\text{L}}\oplus\mathbb{R}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{so}(2,2)
is a reductive decomposition, it is isomorphic to one of the following:

(i)

\mathfrak{m}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{0}+cU_{0},B_{1},B_{2}\},\ \mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{U_{0}\},

(ii)

\mathfrak{m}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{0},B_{1}+cU_{1},B_{2}\},\ \mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{U_{1}\},

(iii)

\mathfrak{m}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{+},B_{-}+cU_{+},B_{2}\},\ \mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{U_{+}\}.

(2)

If a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{\text{L}}\oplus\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{R})=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{so}(2,2)
is a reductive decomposition, it is isomorphic to the following:

(iv)

\mathfrak{m}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{+},B_{-},B_{2}\},\ \mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{U_{+},U_{2}\}.

(3)

If
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{\text{L}}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{0}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{0}\simeq\mathfrak{so}(2,2)
is a reductive decomposition, it is isomorphic to the following:

(v)

\mathfrak{m}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{i}+cU_{i}\}_{i=0}^{2},\ \mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{o}.

Here
B_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(B_{0}\pm B_{1}),U_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(U_{0}\pm U_{1}),
and
c\in\mathbb{R}.
The vector space
\mathfrak{m}
is equipped with a Lorentzian metric via the map
\tau\colon\mathfrak{m}\to T_{o}\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1},
which identifies
(\mathfrak{m},\tau^{*}g_{\lambda\mu\nu})\simeq(T_{o}\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1},g_{\lambda\mu\nu}).

Proof.

(1)

\displaystyle[w,B_{0}]

(3.103)

\displaystyle[B_{0},v_{1}]

\displaystyle=2v_{2},\quad[v_{1},v_{2}]=-2B_{0},\quad[v_{2},B_{0}]=2v_{1}.

(i)

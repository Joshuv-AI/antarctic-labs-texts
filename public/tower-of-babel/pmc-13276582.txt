The effects on the antioxidant status and immune responses of Largemouth Bass Micropterus salmoides in an aquaponic system with Ocimum basilicum var. pilosum
Authors: Kunjie Wu, Xin Lin, Wuqiang Zhang, Chengliang Hou, Chen Zhang, Yanwei Zhang, Gaoliang Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•A novel aquaponic system was successfully established using hairy basil.•Water quality was improved via reduced NH₄⁺-N and NO₂⁻-N levels.•The system enhanced the fish's immune and antioxidant responses.•It significantly decreased bacterial-induced mortality and gut damage.Keywords: Largemouth bass, Ocimum basilicum var. pilosum, Aquaponic, Antioxidant capacity, Immune regulation

Introduction

Largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides), commonly known as California perch, belongs to the genus Micropterus of the family Centrarchidae and native to North America. It is an economically valuable freshwater fish species that is widely farmed in China [1]. In 2023, the aquaculture production of largemouth bass exceeded 0.88 million tons, according to the latest China Fishery Statistical Yearbook. However, under intensive farming conditions, the largemouth bass aquaculture industry faces significant viral and bacterial disease threats [2]. Among these, one of the main diseases that has caused significant economic losses to largemouth bass farming is Aeromonas spp [3]. Aeromonas hydrophila, a typical human-animal-fish pathogen, induces several pathological features, such as enteritis, tail and skin rot and hemorrhagic septicemia in largemouth bass [3]. In addition to largemouth bass, A. hydrophila infects a wide range of other fish species, including grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella), common carp (Cyprinus carpio), and red swamp crayfish (Procambarus clarkii), causing heavy mortality [[4], [5], [6]].

The current primary control methods for bacterial fish diseases rely on antibiotics, chemical drugs, and water disinfectants. However, long-term or excessive use can easily lead to the development of drug resistance in pathogenic bacteria and pose quality and safety hazards due to drug residues [7]. A. hydrophila is an opportunistic pathogen, and deterioration of water quality and a decline in fish immunity both increase the probability of disease occurrence [5]. Utilizing aquaponic systems to cultivate Chinese herbal medicines or vegetables on pond surfaces is an emerging biological control technology. This approach can achieve water quality regulation and modulate non-specific immune responses in farmed fish [[8], [9], [10]]. Aquatic cultivation also provides fishermen with a new channel to increase their economic benefits.

Aquaponics is a sustainable agricultural system that integrates aquaculture and hydroponics. Water quality is a critical limiting factor for fish health and production performance in intensive aquaculture [11]. The accumulation of metabolic wastes, particularly ammonia (NH4+-N) and nitrite (NO2--N), can cause acute or chronic toxicity to fish, inducing oxidative stress, damaging gill structure and function, and directly leading to immunosuppression, thereby increasing susceptibility to pathogenic infections [12,13]. Consequently, maintaining excellent water quality is paramount for ensuring healthy fish growth. In this system, nutrient-rich wastewater from fish culture is channeled to plant-growing units, where plants absorb these nutrients for growth while simultaneously purifying the water, which is then recirculated to the fish tanks [14]. This model not only optimizes resource utilization and reduces environmental pollution, but also provides a unique platform for studying fish-plant interactions [15,10]. Hairy basil (Ocimum basilicum var. pilosum) is an annual herbaceous plant belonging to the order Lamiales, family Lamiaceae, and genus Ocimum, and is a popular choice among plants in aquaponic systems [16,17]. Hairy basil primarily contains volatile oils (e.g., eugenol, linalool), flavonoids (e.g., apigenin, quercetin), and terpenoids, organic acids, and various other compounds [18,19]. These compounds have demonstrated anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and antioxidant activities in vitro and in mammalian models [20,21]. Recently, dietary supplementation with basil extracts has been shown to enhance lysozyme activity and upregulate anti-inflammatory cytokines in Nile tilapia and African catfish [22,23]. However, whether these effects occur when basil is grown in situ in an aquaponic system remains unknown. Therefore, we selected hairy basil for its well-documented bioactivity and its suitability for floating-bed cultivation, allowing us to test the hypothesis that the plant can modulate fish immunity through water-mediated pathways.

In this study, we investigated immune-related gene expression and histo-pathological changes in intestines of largemouth bass. In addition, non-specific immune parameters in the serum and survival rate dynamics were tested to explore the immune response mechanism of largemouth bass when infected with A. hydrophila. In summary, this study aimed to evaluate the impact of hairy basil cultivation on water quality and non-specific immunity in largemouth bass. These findings provide critical data to support the application and scaling of integrated aquaculture-agriculture systems.

Materials and methods

Experimental fish and bacteria

The aquaponic system was designed to consist of six tanks (3 × 4 × 1.3 m) for raising largemouth bass (Fig. 1). A total of 300 healthy largemouth bass (with an initial average weight of 6±2 g) were obtained from a fish farming market in Nanyang, Henan, China, and randomly cultured in six tanks. Before the experiment, we examined the largemouth bass for health status. All fish showed no external symptoms. Bacteriological analysis of liver, kidney, and spleen samples from six randomly selected fish revealed no Aeromonas hydrophila. Microscopic examination of gill and fin clips also detected no parasites. Therefore, the fish were confirmed to be healthy prior to the study. Three tanks with a blank floating raft were used as the control group. 300 hairy basil were cultured on foam boards and planting baskets in three other tanks, which served as the experimental group [24]. The fish were fed the formulated feed twice daily at a feeding rate of 4% of their body weight. There were no water exchanges in the systems during the entire experimental period, only partial water replenishment was conducted to maintain the water level. Water temperature ranged from 17.3°C to 29.4°C, dissolved oxygen was maintained above 5.5 mg/L, and pH was between 7.13 and 8.76 at the time of sampling. As the Methods for Detection and Analysis of Water and Wastewater (Fourth Edition), water quality parameters, including nitrous nitrogen (NO2--N), nitrate nitrogen (NO3--N), ammonium nitrogen (NH4+-N), TN (Total Nitrogen), TP (Total Phosphorus), and CODMₙ (Permanganate Index), were determined using the following methods: Nessler's reagent spectrophotometry, N-(1-naphthyl)-ethylenediamine spectro-photometry, ultraviolet spectrophotometry, potassium persulfate oxidation-ultraviolet spectrophotometry, acidic potassium permanganate method, and stannous chloride reduction spectrophotometry, respectively.

After 105 days of culture, a challenge test was performed. All experiments were performed according to the animal husbandry guidelines of Xinyang Agriculture and Forestry University, Xinyang City, Henan Province, China, and were approved by the Laboratory Animal Welfare and Ethical Reviewing Committee of Xinyang Agriculture and Forestry University (No. 2024-AF-XYAFU- 001). A. hydrophila was isolated from naturally diseased fish and maintained in our laboratory. A. hydrophila was grown at 28°C in Luria-Bertani (LB) medium in a shaker until OD600 reached 0.6 [5].

Bacterial challenge

A. hydrophila was prepared as described in our previous study [5] . A. hydrophila suspension was adjusted to 1 × 107 CFU/mL with sterile PBS for the challenge assay. Fifteen largemouth bass (300 ± 10 g) in the control and experimental group were intraperitoneally (i.p.) injected with 100 μl of 1 × 107 CFU/mL A. hydrophila. After 12, 24, 48, and 72 h, peripheral blood and immune tissues, including the intestine and head kidney, were sampled for total RNA extraction. In addition, the intestines were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for tissues section at 72 h post-infection. Meanwhile, the largemouth bass in the control and experimental groups were randomly divided into two groups: a challenged group and a control group (20 fish/group). The challenged group fish were injected with A. hydrophila, and the control group fish were injected with equal volumes of PBS. The mortality of largemouth bass in each group was counted daily for the next two weeks.

RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from the intestine and head kidney using TRIzolTM Reagent, following the manufacturer’s protocols. First-strand cDNA was synthesized using the SenweisTM All-in-one 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Master Mix as the manufacturer described. qPCR was performed using 2 × SenweisTM Faster SYBR qPCR Master Mix to analyze mRNA levels of genes with conditions of 1 cycle of 95 °C for 30 s, 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s, 60 °C for 30 s. The qPCR setup has been described in our previous study [5]. The primers used in this study are listed in Table 1. β-actin was used as the reference gene. All samples were tested in triplicate using qPCR. Data were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method [25].

Immune-related enzyme activity in the blood

The supernatant (plasma) of peripheral blood was used for enzyme activity analysis of total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), acid phosphatase (ACP), alkaline phosphatase (AKP), and lysozyme (LYS). The assays were performed as the manufacturer’s protocols of detection kits purchased from Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (NJJCbio, Nanjing, China). Optical density was measured using a Readmax 1900 following the instruction. Each sample was tested in triplicates.

Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining of largemouth bass intestines

For histological examination, the intestines sampled from the largemouth bass were fixed in 4% neutral buffered paraformaldehyde for 24 h. The tissue samples were embedded in paraffin and sectioned to a thickness of 4 μm. Finally, the slides were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and photographed under an optical microscope [26].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses of data were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0. The statistical significance was analyzed with a Two-way ANOVA combined with a post-hoc test. The significance level of the difference from the control was set as asterisks (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01).

Results

The regulatory effects of hairy basil on water quality parameters and growth parameters in aquaponic system

The accumulation of ammonia nitrogen and nitrite in aquatic environments may lead to fish oxidative stress and immunosuppression. As shown in Fig. 2A, the concentrations of NH₄⁺-N in both the experimental and control groups began to increase starting from day 7. In the experimental group, NH₄⁺-N started to decrease on day 21, subsequently fluctuating between 0.116 and 0.420 mg/L. In the control group, it increased to 1.07 mg/L on day 35 before declining, peaked at 1.10 mg/L on day 63, and subsequently fluctuated between 0.684 and 0.797 mg/L. Throughout the entire experiment, starting from day 7, the NH₄⁺-N mass concentration in the experimental group was significantly lower than that in the control group (Fig. 2A). The NO₂⁻-N concentration in the experimental group began to decrease during the first week. It remained relatively stable at approximately 0.100 mg/L (except on days 35 and 42) and was significantly lower than that in the control group (Fig. 2B). The concentrations of NO₃⁻-N, CODMₙ, TN and TP in both groups exhibited a gradual increasing trend overall. During the mid-experimental period, the concentrations of NO₃⁻-N, TP, and TN in the experimental group were all significantly lower than those in the control group (Fig. 2C, 2D and 2E). However, the CODM concentration in the experimental group was significantly lower than that in the control group except for 0, 28, 56, 63, and 77d (Fig. 2F).

The effect of hairy basil on the intestinal immunity of largemouth bass

To confirm the effect of hairy basil on the intestinal immunity of largemouth bass in an aquaponic system. We examined the expression of diverse immune factors in the intestine, including pro-inflammatory cytokines, anti-inflammatory factors, and antimicrobial genes, after A. hydrophila infection (Fig. 3). As shown in Fig. 3A, IFN-γ mRNA levels were markedly increased in the intestines of hairy basil group fish at 12, 24, or 48 h after infection. However, the expression of TNF-α did not change (Fig. 3B). Differential modulation of Treg cytokines was also noted, IL-10 and TGF-β were upregulated at 72 h after infection in hairy basil group (Fig. 3C and 3D). IL-21 and IL-22 were observably increased at 12 or 24 h after infection (Fig. 3E and 3F). However, in the control group, IL-10 and IL-21 expression showed a trend of first increasing and then decreasing (Fig. 3C and 3E). Meanwhile, the antibacterial genes lysozyme-g (Lys-g) and defensin, beta-like 2 (Defbl2), were also enhanced in the intestine of hairy basil group fish (Fig. 3G and 3H). The expression of Lys-g was always promoted, except at the beginning of the infection (Fig. 3G). Compared with that in the control group, Defbl2 expression in the intestine was significantly increased at 24 h after A. hydrophila infection and then returned to normal levels at 72 h (Fig. 3H). But the expression of Lys-g and Defbl2 was declined at 12 or 24 h after infection in the control group (Fig. 3G and 3H).In brief, our results indicated that hairy basil cultured in an aquaponic system could regulate the intestinal immunity and improve antibacterial ability of largemouth bass.

The effect of hairy basil on the head kidney immunity of largemouth bass in aquaponic system

The effect of hairy basil on activating immune responses in the head kidney when largemouth bass against A. hydrophila was evaluated in this study (Fig. 4). IFN-γ expression was significantly elevated after infection and reached a peak at 48 h infection in hairy basil group (Fig. 4A). And IFN-γ expression in the control group increased after infection, but to a lesser extent than in hairy basil group (Fig. 4A). Conversely, in the control group, TNF-α expression first decreased at 24 h post-infection and then increased at 48 h, whereas in the experimental group, it decreased at 24 h and then returned to normal levels (Fig. 4B). Differential modulation of IL-10 in hairy basil group fish was upregulated at 24 and 72 h post infection (Fig. 4C). In the hairy basil group, the mRNA level of TGF-β was upregulated at 24 h post infection and then decreased at 48 h, while in the control group it decreased at 48 h and subsequently returned to baseline (Fig. 4D). We also found that significant upregulation of IL-21 was detected at 12 h infection (Fig. 4E). However, the expression of IL-21 was downregulated at 24 h in the control group (Fig. 4E). In the control group, IL-22 expression increased at 24 h after infection and then decreased, while it remained unchanged in the experimental group (Fig. 4F). Meanwhile, compared with the control group, the expression of Lys-g was initially promoted and reached a peak at 72 h post infection (Fig. 4G). Defbl2, an antibacterial gene, was enhanced at 24 h, and reached a peak at 48 h, and subsequently returned to normal levels in the head kidney of hairy basil group fish (Fig. 4H). In contrast, the expression levels of both Lys-g and Defbl2 decreased at 24 or 48 h in both groups (Fig. 4G and 4H). Totally, these results generated that hairy basil could further activate immune response of the symbiotic largemouth bass during A. hydrophila infection.

The effect of hairy basil on serum non-specific immunity status of largemouth bass

To further explore the effect of hairy basil on the immune response of largemouth bass during A. hydrophila infection, the activities of acid phosphatase (ACP), alkaline phosphatase (AKP), lysozyme (LYS), and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) were evaluated in the serum of A. hydrophila-challenged fish in the presence or absence of hairy basil. As shown in Fig. 5 A, the decline in ACP activity in the hairy basil group at later time points may reflect a more efficient resolution of the immune response compared to the sustained elevation in the control group. In the serum of largemouth bass cultured with hairy basil, AKP activity increased significantly at 12 h post infection and then returned to normal levels (Fig. 5B). The LYS activity of experimental group fish remained significantly higher than that of the control group at all time points after A. hydrophila injection, and the maximal LYS activity was reached at 12 h post infection (Fig. 5C). Changes in T-AOC content are shown in Fig. 5D, which was higher than that in the control group at all time points post-infection, except for 12 h. Taken together, these results indicate that hairy basil was effectively activates non-specific immunity in largemouth bass.

The effect of hairy basil on intestinal histopathology of largemouth bass

To investigate the effects of hairy basil on the intestinal health of largemouth bass. In this study, we detected histological changes in the intestine challenged with A. hydrophila. In the control group, Fig. 6A shows that the intestines of the PBS-treated fish displayed normal structures with the presence of a small number of goblet cells and leukocytes and densely arranged microvilli. The intestinal structure of control group fish infected with A. hydrophila showed serious vacuolization of cells, degeneration and vagueness of the cuticula, and massive infiltration of leukocytes (Fig. 6B). Finally, in the intestine of hairy basil group fish infected with A. hydrophila, despite the absence of gross pathological lesions, histopathological analysis revealed marked goblet cell proliferation accompanied by narrowing of the lumen (Fig. 6C). In contrast, hairy basil attenuated the intestinal pathological damage induced by A. hydrophila. Specifically, it significantly increased the intestinal mucosal layer thickness (Fig. 6D) and goblet cell count per unit area (Fig. 6E), while reducing submucosa edema width (Fig. 6F). Furthermore, villus length was notably preserved compared to the infected control group (Fig. 6G).

The effect of hairy basil on the survival rate of largemouth bass infected with A. hydrophila

To further explore the effect of symbiotic hairy basil on the process of bacterial infection in largemouth bass, survival rates in different groups after two weeks of infection were performed. After challenging with A. hydrophila, the largemouth bass in the control group started to die in the 3rd day, and the relative survival rate dropped to 15% on the 10th day. In the hairy basil group, the onset of mortality was delayed until day 5 post-infection, with a survival rate of 35%, which was significantly higher than that of the control group (P<0.01). At the meantime, no mortality was observed in either group in the absence of A. hydrophila challenge (Fig. 7).

Discussion

The largemouth bass aquaculture industry has experienced steady growth owing to its desirable attributes, such as palatable firm-textured flesh, boneless fillets, environmental adaptability, balanced nutritional profile, and high economic value [1]. With the expansion of production scales, farming practices have evolved into intensive, high-density systems. However, aquaculture water containing excessive nutrients causes eutrophication in receiving waters, creating favorable conditions for pathogen transmission [27]. Recently, disease outbreaks affecting the largemouth bass have increased substantially. The indiscriminate use of antibiotics and chemical therapeutics poses a significant threat to aquatic ecosystems and food safety [3,2]. Consequently, the development of integrated farming models that simultaneously enhance water purification and fish immunity has become imperative.

Aquaponic systems represent an innovative, integrated farming approach that synergistically combines aquaculture (raising aquatic organisms) with hydroponics (soilless plant cultivation) and demonstrates high efficiency in remediating aquaculture water quality [28]. Biological floating bed technology has become one of the most commonly adopted techniques in aquaponic systems due to its operational convenience. Water quality parameters including nitrous nitrogen (NO2--N), and ammonium nitrogen (NH4+-N) greatly affect the immunity and digestive capacity of fish. And accumulation of ammonia and nitrite in aquatic environments induces environmental stress in fish [11]. Hydroponic plants such as tomatoes, spinach, and lettuce could absorb and purify nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus [12,13,9]. In our study, the water of hairy basil group exhibited a significant decline in ammonia and nitrite concentrations during the first seven days, then these parameters remained persistently low. Meanwhile, the concentration of nitrate progressively accumulated. Analogously, the phenomenon of rising nitrate-nitrogen levels coupled with declining ammonia-nitrogen concentrations was observed in both different aquaponics systems [12,13]. The correlation between improved water quality (reduced NH₄⁺-N and NO₂⁻-N) and enhanced immune parameters in fish warrants deeper mechanistic consideration. Ammonia and nitrite are known inducers of oxidative stress in fish, leading to excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), damage to gill and intestinal epithelia, and suppression of lymphocyte proliferation and antibody production [11]. In our study, the significant reduction in NH₄⁺-N and NO₂⁻-N in the hairy basil group likely alleviated this chronic oxidative stress. This is indirectly supported by the higher serum total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) observed in the hairy basil group (Fig. 5D), which may reflect a lower baseline oxidative burden. Reduced oxidative stress, in turn, preserves the structural integrity of immune organs and maintains the responsiveness of immune cells to pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs).

Additionally, total phosphorus (TP) in the hairy basil group remained stable until a gradual accumulation emerged after day 40, though consistently and significantly lower than control levels, which were different from results of Fan et al. [12]. They found the concentration of TP sustained increases status. The difference in TP accumulation compared to some studies may be attributed to variations in plant species, fish biomass, feed composition, and system maturity, highlighting the importance of system-specific optimization. The incorporation of hairy basil in the aquaponic system also significantly enhanced the growth performance and feed utilization of largemouth bass. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that root system of hairy basil exerts pronounced regulatory effects on water quality in largemouth bass pond aquaculture, effectively promoting the conversion of toxic ammonia and nitrite into fish-safe nitrate nitrogen. Thereby achieving the prevention of fish diseases through optimized water quality parameters. Lower stress levels are directly linked to a more robust immune competence, which in turn likely contributed to the enhanced growth performance and feed utilization observed in the present study.

Hairy basil, a medicinal-edible plant, contains aromatic volatile oils, flavonoids and their glycosides, coumarins, as well as triterpenoids and alkaloids. These constituents collectively confer anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and antioxidant properties [20,29]. Hairy basil extracts are predominantly employed as functional feed additives in aquaculture, enhancing non-specific immunity and preventing diverse aquatic diseases [22,23]. Concurrently, their integration into aquaponic systems has advanced [16,17]. Cytokines is one of the most critical regulators of fish immunity. For regulating inflammatory responses, it is broadly classified into two types of cytokines, including pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines [30]. In this study, largemouth bass of the hairy basil group exhibited enhanced anti-inflammatory capacity, evidenced by upregulated anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10, TGF-β, IL-22) and reduced intestinal inflammatory cell infiltration. Similar results were reported by Rahman et al. The immune-antioxidant genes were observed to be increased due to a dietary additive with chitosan-basil nanocomposite [22]. However, IFN-γ, a pro-inflammatory cytokine, also displayed remarkable upregulation, likely attributable to its antibacterial functions [31]. We also found antimicrobial genes, including lys-g and Defbl2, were elevated. In addition, IL-21 could mediate immunomodulatory functions in teleosts, suppressing inflammatory responses while potentiating antimicrobial defenses [5], the expression of IL-21 was enhanced in the fish of the aquaponics systems.

It is important to consider whether the observed immune enhancements are specific to hairy basil or merely a consequence of general plant-mediated water purification. To address this, we compared our water quality results with those from aquaponic studies using other plants (e.g., lettuce, tomato, spinach). Those studies also reported reductions in NH₄⁺-N and NO₂⁻-N, but they did not consistently observe upregulation of anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10, TGF-β) or antibacterial genes (Lys-g, Defbl2) in fish [12,13]. This suggests that water quality improvement alone may not fully explain our immunological findings. Conversely, dietary basil extract has been shown to directly activate fish immune genes [22]. In our system, it is plausible that water-soluble bioactive compounds released from hairy basil roots (e.g., rosmarinic acid) reach the fish via the recirculating water, exerting direct immunomodulatory effects. However, we did not measure these compounds in the water, and therefore we cannot definitively attribute the effects to hairy basil-specific exudates. Future studies should include a non-basil plant control (e.g., lettuce) and measure plant-derived metabolites in the water to determine specificity.

Blood biochemical parameters serve as crucial indicators for evaluating fish health status, reflecting alterations in physiological and metabolic states as well as physiological responses to pathogens [32]. ACP and AKP, two vital components of the lysosome system, play an important role in eliminating and hydrolyzing microbes [33]. Fan et al reported that the activities of ACP and AKP were significantly increased in aquaponics than in controls [12], which were consistent with our findings. In our aquaponics system, serum activities of these two enzymes demonstrated a consistent pattern: acute upregulation at the onset of infection followed by restoration to pre-challenge homeostasis. Lysozyme, a conserved component of the innate immune system across vertebrates, exerts its bactericidal activity by hydrolyzing peptidoglycan in bacterial cell walls [33]. In this study, the activities of LYS in the serum of the hairy basil group showed a stable trend, which significantly increased after A. hydrophila infection. Previous study indicated that basil extract could enhance serum LYS activity in African catfish (Clarias gariepinus) and Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) [22,23]. It demonstrated that the replicated enzyme kinetics provide compelling evidence for aquaponics-induced immunomodulation. The antioxidant defenses are crucial for fish health status, which could effectively remove free radicals and protect host cells from the deleterious effects of bacterial infection [34]. The activities of T-AOC in aquaponics remained at significantly a higher level in serum than those in the control, which were similar to the results reported by Abdel Rahman et al [22]. The activities of immune-related enzymes could serve as a reliable indicator of the host's immunological status. Notably, both LYS and T-AOC maintained persistently elevated levels post-infection compared to the control group.

Besides, consistent with established evidence on aquaponics-mediated survival benefits [35], our findings specifically document that basil integration contribute to the attenuation of enteritis induced by A. hydrophila, potentially by enhancing mucosal barrier integrity through increased goblet cell numbers and mucin production, suppressing inflammatory edema, and promoting villus architecture maintenance in largemouth bass, resulting in markedly improved host survival under pathogenic exposure. However, the underlying mechanisms remain to be further elucidated.

Conclusion

In summary, the results in the present study provide a new perspective for the aquaponic system. Hairy basil, a medicinal-edible plant, was selected for establishing an aquaponics system with largemouth bass. The results demonstrated that hairy basil could significantly improve the regulatory effects on water quality in largemouth bass aquaculture by reducing harmful substances such as ammonia nitrogen and nitrite. Moreover, the expression levels of immune-related genes and the activities of immune-related enzymes were improved in fish of the aquaponic system. Meanwhile, hairy basil could reduce intestinal damage caused by A. hydrophila infection and thus decrease mortality rates in largemouth bass. While our results demonstrate clear benefits of incorporating hairy basil into the aquaponic system, we acknowledge that the relative contributions of water quality improvement versus plant-specific immunomodulatory compounds remain to be disentangled. Therefore, we conclude that hairy basil aquaponics contributes to enhancing health and disease resistance, but further studies with appropriate plant controls are needed to establish specificity.

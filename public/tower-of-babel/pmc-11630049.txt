Regulation of a novel circATP8B4/miR-31-5p/nestin ceRNA crosstalk in proliferation, motility, invasion and radiosensitivity of human glioma cells
Authors: Dongdong Luo, Aiping Luo, Ganwei Ye, Dan Li, Su Hu, Hailin Zhao, Biao Peng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDeregulation of circular RNAs (circRNAs) is frequent in human glioma. Although circRNA ATPase phospholipid transporting 8B4 (circATP8B4) is highly expressed in glioma, its precise action in glioma development is still not fully understood. The relationship of microRNA (miR)-31-5p and circATP8B4 or nestin (NES) was predicted by bioinformatic analysis and confirmed by RNA pull-down and Dual-luciferase reporter assays. CircATP8B4, miR-31-5p and NES were quantified by qRT-PCR or western blot. Cell functional behaviors were assessed by EdU, wound-healing and transwell invasion assays. Xenograft model experiments were performed to define circATP8B4’s activity in vivo. CircATP8B4, a true circular transcript, was upregulated in human glioma. CircATP8B4 downregulation weakened glioma cell growth, motility, and invasion and facilitated radiosensitivity. Mechanistically, circATP8B4 and NES 3′UTR harbored a shared miR-31-5p pairing site, and circATP8B4 involved the post-transcriptional NES regulation by functioning as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA). Furthermore, the miR-31-5p/NES axis participated in circATP8B4’s activity in glioma cell proliferation, motility, invasion and radiosensitivity. Additionally, circATP8B4 loss diminished tumor growth and enhanced the anticancer effect of radiotherapy in vivo. We have uncovered an uncharacterized ceRNA cascade, circATP8B4/miR-31-5p/NES axis, underlying glioma development and radiosensitivity. Targeting the ceRNA crosstalk may have potential to improve the outcome of glioma patients.

INTRODUCTION

Gliomas, a heterogeneous type of malignancies, are the most prevalent brain tumors, leading to significant morbidity and mortality [1, 2]. Despite advances in clinical regimens, including surgery and chemoradiotherapy, high-grade glioma remains largely incurable and has a dismal survival rate [3, 4]. Although the mechanism for glioma development and acquired resistance is still not well defined, the epigenetic regulation in glioma has been causally proved [5]. The development of epigenetic markers as targeted therapies may provide a new opportunity for glioma treatment [6].

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) have been established to be epigenetic modulators in cancer biology [7, 8]. CircRNAs are naturally occurring RNA circles, which have no free 5′ and 3′ ends in their structure [9, 10]. Some circRNAs have been documented to involve mRNA transcript modulation by working as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) [11]. Recent work has unveiled the crucial implication of circRNAs in the carcinogenesis and acquired resistance of glioma [12]. For example, circ_0002109 can operate as a potential oncogenic driver in glioma by enhancing epithelial membrane protein-2 expression through sponging and sequestering miR-129-5p [13]. Inhibition of circ_0008344 sensitizes glioma cells to radiotherapy by working as a miR-433-3p ceRNA to impact post-transcriptional regulation of ring finger protein 2 [14]. CircRNA ATPase phospholipid transporting 8B4 (circATP8B4, also called hsa_circ_0035199 based on the circRNA ID), generated through the infrequent back splicing of ATP8B4 pre-mRNA, is a relatively unexplored circRNA. CircRNA microarray analysis of the extracellular vesicles extracted from the radioresistant U251 glioma cells, followed by qRT-PCR demonstration, showed relatively high expression of circATP8B4 [15]. Although this report also highlights the promoting effect of circATP8B4 on glioma radioresistance through miR-766 [15], its precise action in glioma development is still not fully understood.

Dysregulation of miRNAs may prove crucial in impacting glioma development and acquired resistance [16, 17]. MiR-31-5p is present at the low expression in glioma tissues and it participates in glioma development through long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) LINC01116/miR-31-5p/VEGFA ceRNA crosstalk [18]. Nestin (NES) is expressed primarily in nerve cells and upregulated in some cancer cells [19, 20]. In glioma, NES expression is related to poor prognosis and outcomes of the patients [21]. Moreover, NES is required for glioblastoma cell cycle progression [22] and can induce glioma cell growth and motility [23]. Our preliminary survey showed a shared miR-31-5p binding site in both circATP8B4 and NES, implying a novel potential ceRNA crosstalk.

The mechanistic determinants of circATP8B4 on glioma pathogenesis remain to be determined, as does the role of circATP8B4. Here, we shed light on these questions by silencing circATP8B in LN229 and T98G glioma cells, establishing a novel epigenetic cause of glioma development and radiosensitivity with therapeutic implications.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Bioinformatics

In this work, we used two publicly computer algorithms for prediction to obtain miRNAs that potentially pair to circATP8B4: circinteractome [24] and circbank [25]. To search miR-31-5p target genes, we utilized the computational program starbase [26]. The back-spliced formation and circular structure of circATP8B4 were analyzed by circPrimer software [27]. The abnormally expressed genes in human glioma were searched using GEPIA database.

Clinical specimens and cell lines

With informed consent, all human specimens were obtained from Guangzhou Institute of Cancer Research, the Affiliated Cancer Hospital, Guangzhou Medical University. Approval to collect and use specimens was granted by Guangzhou Institute of Cancer Research, the Affiliated Cancer Hospital, Guangzhou Medical University Ethics Committee. We harvested primary glioma tumors from 55 patients with glioma at the time of surgery and nontumor brain nerve tissues (NBTs) from 17 patients with brain trauma and without other tumors and brain diseases.

For the current experiments, we procured LN229, U251, T98G and A172 glioma cell lines from Procell (Wuhan, China) and propagated them in standard mediums (Procell). As control, the human microglial HMC3 cell line (Procell) was used and cultured as previously described [28].

RNA preparation and qRT-PCR

RNA preparation from tissues and cells was performed under the usage of RNX-Plus reagent from CinnaGen (Tehran, Iran). Preparation of nuclear and cytoplasmic RNA from LN229 and T98G cells was done according to the standard protocols (Norgen Biotek, Thorold, ON, Canada), using the Cytoplasmic & Nuclear RNA Purification Kit. Digestion with RNase R was carried out by incubating 2 μg RNA from LN229 and T98G cells with 5 or 0 (control) units of RNase R for 15 min as recommended by the manufacturers (Geneseed, Guangzhou, China). RNA was converted to cDNA with RevertAid cDNA Synthesis Kit (Fermentas, Leon-Rot, Germany) with 1 μg RNA and 250 ng random primers (for circATP8B4 and mRNAs) or miScript Reverse Transcription Kit (Qiagen, Hombrechtikon, Switzerland) with 200 ng RNA (for miRNAs). Using the Rotor-Gene 6000 thermocycler (Corbett Research, Sydney, NSW, Australia), we performed qRT-PCR with SYBR Kit from TaKaRa (Beijing, China) and specific primer sets (Supplementary Table 1). Relative RNA levels were detected by 2-ΔΔCt method, with U6 or β-actin as the reference gene.

Constructs, oligonucleotides, transduction and transfection of cell lines

ShRNA vectors expressing circATP8B4 specific shRNA (sh-circATP8B4) sh-circATP8B4#1, sh-circATP8B4#2, sh-circATP8B4#3 and control shRNA vector (sh-NC) were from Geneseed. Vectors expressing a miR-31-5p inhibitor (in-miR-31-5p) designed to silence miR-31-5p were from GenePharma (Shanghai, China), and a nontarget vector as the control (in-miR-NC, GenePharma) was used, which is not complementary to any known miRNA. Oligonucleotides including human mature miR-31-5p mimic and a scrambled sequence as the mimic control were from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Basel, Switzerland). Human NES expression vector pcDNA3.1-NES-coding sequence (lacking the 3′UTR, oe-NES) and control empty vector were from GenePharma. The indicated plasmid constructs (300 ng) or miRNA mimic oligonucleotides (30 nM) were introduced into 1 × 105 LN229 and T98G glioma cells grown in 12-well culture dishes using Lipofectamine 3000 as described by the manufacturers (Life Technologies, Hemel Hempstead, UK).

Lentivirus encoding sh-circATP8B4#2 or sh-NC was constructed by Geneseed. Stable circATP8B4 knockdown was achieved by introducing the lentivirus constructs into LN229 cells as per the manufacturing protocols. We selected lentivirus-positive transduced cells in puromycin (1 μg/mL) for more than 7 days.

For luciferase experiments, the fragments of circATP8B4 and NES 3′UTR harboring miR-31-5p putative target sites (wt, UCUUGCC) or miss-matched pairing sites (mut, AGAACGG), synthesized by Genewiz (Suzhou, China), were ligated to the psiCHECK-2 plasmid (Promega, Sydney, NSW, Australia).

Cell proliferation, motility and invasion assays

For proliferation analysis, we performed EdU assay with a Cell-Light EdU In Vitro Kit as per the manufacturing guideline (Ribobio). Briefly, LN229 and T98G cells at 24 h post-transfection (4 × 103 cells/well) were grown in 96-well dishes for 48 h, and subsequent EdU was added. Following the addition, a 2-h incubation period was allowed at 37°C. After cells were fixed, we used 1× Apollo567 reagent (100 μL/well) for EdU staining and 1× Hoechst33342 reagent (100 μL/well) for nucleus staining. Fluorescence images were captured on a BX63 upright microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), and the EdU-positive (EdU+) cells (red) were analyzed in five random fields and presented as % of total nuclei (blue).

For motility assay, we used wound-healing assay as described elsewhere [29]. Briefly, LN229 and T98G cells at 24 h post-transfection (1 × 106 cells/well) were grown in 6-well dishes overnight, and then a vertical wound was formed by pipette tips (200 μL). After the media were replaced by the fresh growth medium containing mitomycin C (10 μg/mL, Selleck, Shanghai, China) to suppress cell proliferation, the cells were cultivated for additional 24 h. Images were obtained under Olympus microscopy. Using the ImageJ software (Rawak software, Stuttgart, Germany), we analyzed the closure of the wound for cell motility evaluation.

For invasion assay, we used Matrigel\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $^{\circledR}$\end{document} invasion chambers (Corning, Wiesbaden, Germany) in transwell assay. At 24 h post-transfection, serum-starved LN229 and T98G cells were plated into the upper chamber (1 × 105 cells/well). We evaluated the invaded cell population in five randomly selected fields after crystal violet (0.1%) staining.

Western blot

After protein preparation from tissues and cells was done with NP40 lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors cocktails (Life Technologies), we performed western blot under standard methods [13]. Probing was done using primary antibodies to N-cadherin (Cat#22018–1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), Vimentin (Cat#10366–1-AP, Proteintech), NES (Cat#19483-1-AP, Proteintech), and the loading buffer β-actin (Cat#ab8227, Abcam, Cambridge, UK). IgG HRP (Cat#ab205718, Abcam; 1:5000 dilution) served as a secondary antibody prior to visualization with chemiluminescence reagent (GE Healthcare, Bucks, UK). Data acquisition was done using the Chemidoc-XRS system and Quantity One software from Bio-Rad (Gladesville, NSW, Australia).

Determination of cell survival and apoptosis after radiotherapy

Radiotherapy treatment of glioma cells was carried out following the methods described by Li et al. [30]. For cell survival analysis, LN229 and T98G cells at 24 h post-transfection (500 cells/well) were grown in 6-well dishes for 24 h and subsequently stimulated with cumulative doses of 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 Gy X-ray using a 6-MV X-ray machine with a 300 cGy/min dose rate. After irradiation, the cells were maintained at 37°C for 10–14 days. We scored the number of colonies (> 50 cells) after crystal violet (0.1%) staining.

For cell apoptosis assessment, cells grown in 96-well dishes were stimulated with 0 or 4 Gy X-ray for 4 h. The apoptotic rate was evaluated by flow cytometry by double staining the cells with Annexin V-FITC and PI following the accompanying instructions of the Apoptosis Detection Kit (Immunostep, Salamanca, Spain). Data were processed by CellQuest software from BD Biosciences (Breda, The Netherlands). Cell apoptosis was also determined by measuring caspase 3 and caspase 9 activities using Multiplex Activity Assay Kits as per the manufacturing recommendations (Abcam).

RNA pull-down assay

LN229 and T98G cells were lysed in the lysis buffer containing RNase inhibitors and then incubated with streptomycin magnetic beads (Abcam) previously coupled with biotinylated circATP8B4 probe containing miR-31-5p putative target sites or probe mock (oligo NC, both from Viagene, Suzhou, China). After 8 h of incubation at 4°C, beads-associated RNA was used to quantify miRNA expression through qRT-PCR.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

LN229 and T98G cells were plated in 96-well dishes and co-transfected with 10 nM of miR-31-5p mimic or mimic control and 100 ng of the indicated reporter constructs using Lipofectamine 3000 protocols. After 48 h, using the Dual-luciferase Assay System from Promega, we determined luciferase activities.

Xenograft model experiments

In vivo xenograft model experiments were performed using 7 ~ 8-week-old female athymic BALB/c nude mice (n = 20, Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). All animal procedures were reviewed and granted by Guangzhou Institute of Cancer Research, the Affiliated Cancer Hospital, Guangzhou Medical University Animal Care and Use Committee. The experiments were divided into four groups: sh-NC, sh-circTAP8B4#2, sh-NC + 4 Gy and sh-circTAP8B4#2 + 4 Gy. Each group included five mice. For establishment of xenograft models, 2 × 106 lentivirus-positive transduced cells in 100 μL of PBS was implanted into the flanks of the nude mice through subcutaneous injection. Seven days postinjection, the xenografts were stimulated with or without a single dose of 4 Gy X-ray for 12 h. Animals were weighed, and tumor growth was assessed by measuring tumor size and calculating tumor volume using length × width2 × 0.5 formula. After 35 days of cell injection, according to the institutional ethical guidelines, mice were sacrificed, and then tumors were weighed and paraffin-embedded to perform immunohistochemistry as described [31], using antibodies to cleaved-caspase 3 (Cat#9661, Cell Signaling Technology, Beverly, MA, USA; 1:400 dilution), Vimentin (Cat#10366–1-AP, Proteintech) and NES (Cat#19483–1-AP, Proteintech).

Statistical analysis

Unless otherwise indicated, mean ± SEM from three independent biological repeats were reported in the graphs. Unpaired Student’s t-test (two-tailed) or ANOVA was performed for all comparisons, where appropriate. Differences with a P-value <0.05 were regarded as significant.

RESULTS

Identification and expression of circATP8B4 in human glioma

To study the biological significance of circATP8B4 in human glioma, we firstly examined its expression pattern in glioma primary tumors and cancer cell lines. In a subset of 55 glioma samples, we observed a statistically significant increase in circATP8B4 expression compared to 17 nontumor brain nerve tissues (NBTs) (Fig. 1A). Consistently, glioma cell lines (LN229, U251, T98G and A172) exhibited higher levels of circATP8B4 compared with HMC3 cells (Fig. 1B). Owing to the most striking augmentation of circATP8B4 in cells (Fig. 1B), we selected the two cell lines for further exploration. As shown in Fig. 1C, circATP8B4 is generated by back-spliced exons 2–6 of the pre-mRNA of ATP8B4 located at chromosome 15:50330964–50 399 205 and 404 nucleotides in length. The back-splicing junction was also validated by Sanger sequencing (Fig. 1C). To confirm the closed continuous circular structure of circATP8B4, we adopted RNase R digestion treatment on RNA extractions of LN229 and T98G cells. Incubation of RNA with RNase R led to a marked decrease in the level of the linear ATP8B4 mRNA, and circATP8B4 level was not reduce by RNase R (Fig. 1D and E), indicating that circATP8B4 has a higher tolerance to the exoribonuclease RNase R and is a true circular transcript. Using a subcellular fractionation assay, we found that circATP8B4 predominantly localized to the cytoplasm (Fig. 1F and G). These data indicate that circATP8B4, a true circular transcript, is overexpressed in human glioma.

Downregulation of circATP8B4 impairs glioma cell proliferation, motility, and invasion and enhances radiosensitivity

To elucidate the functional role of circATP8B4, we established LN229 and T98G cells stably expressing circATP8B4-specific shRNA (sh-circATP8B4). qRT-PCR revealed that introduction of sh-circATP8B4 (sh-circATP8B4#1, sh-circATP8B4#2 or sh-circATP8B4#3) reduced circATP8B4 expression (Fig. 2A). We selected sh-circATP8B4#2 for further experiments due to the most significantly inhibitory effect mediated by sh-circATP8B4#2 (Fig. 2A). Through the EdU incorporation assay, we found that decreased expression of circATP8B4 strongly impaired cell proliferation ability (Fig. 2B). CircATP8B4-silenced LN229 and T98G cells showed a striking attenuation of cell motility and invasion (Fig. 2C and D). Furthermore, decreased circATP8B4 expression resulted in suppressed levels of N-cadherin, vimentin and E-cadherin in LN229 and T98G cells (Fig. 2E and F and Supplementary Fig. 1A), indicating the repressive function of circATP8B4 loss on cell epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT).

(B) EdU cell proliferation assay of LN229 and T98G cells after transfection by sh-circATP8B4#2 or sh-NC vector. Next, we wanted to ascertain whether knocking down circATP8B4 had the regulatory function on cell radiosensitivity. Using a standard colony formation assay, we confirmed that silencing of circATP8B4 remarkably increased the sensitivity to radiotherapy in the two cell lines, as presented by the clear decrease of colony formation capacity after radiation treatment (Fig. 3A). This result was also validated by the cell apoptosis assay using flow cytometry. Under radiation treatment (4 Gy), decreased expression of circATP8B4 promoted cell apoptosis compared to the sh-NC control (Fig. 3B). In addition, circATP8B4 loss increased the activities of caspase 3 and caspase 9 in LN229 and T98G cells after radiation treatment (Fig. 3C and D).

CircATP8B4 has a miR-31-5p binding site

To understand how circATP8B4 influences glioma cell phenotypes, we considered its targeted miRNAs. We used two computational programs (circinteractome [24] and circbank [25]) to help search the miRNAs that potentially pair to circATP8B4. Venn diagram showed that there were four putative miRNAs (miR-1299, miR-182-5p, miR-31-5p and miR-580-3p) predicted by both the two programs (Fig. 4A). RNA pull-down assays revealed that among them, miR-31-5p was preferentially enriched by biotinylated circATP8B4 probe compared with the control probe in LN229 and T98G cells (Fig. 4B and C), indicating the direct relationship between circATP8B4 and miR-31-5p. Using circinteractome database, a putative pairing sequence (UCUUGCC) for miR-31-5p was found in circATP8B4 (Fig. 4D). To define whether circATP8B4 might operate as a sponge to sequester miR-31-5p, we constructed circATP8B4 luciferase reporter (wt) containing the predicted target region and transfected it into LN229 and T98G cells along with miR-31-5p mimic. Elevated expression of miR-31-5p upon transfection, verified by qRT-PCR (Fig. 4E), reduced the luciferase activity of the wt-circATP8B4 reporter (Fig. 4F and G). To validate whether the predicted miR-31-5p pairing region was required for this effect, we generated a mutant circATP8B4 reporter (mut-circATP8B4), in which all seven pairing sites were mutated (Fig. 4D). Strikingly, mut-circATP8B4 showed the unresponsive phenotype to miR-31-5p in luciferase (Fig. 4F and G). qRT-PCR data showed that miR-31-5p expression was markedly attenuated in glioma primary tumors and cancer cell lines compared with their counterparts (Fig. 4H and I). Additionally, stimulation of 4 Gy X-ray did not affect miR-31-5p expression in LN229 and T98G cells (Supplementary Fig. 1B). The decreased expression of circATP8B4 by sh-circATP8B4#2 did not alter the expression of miR-31-5p in the two cell lines (Supplementary Fig. 1C). Hence, we conclude the role of circATP8B4 as a miR-31-5p sponge.

NES is a direct miR-31-5p target

To identify putative direct miR-31-5p targets responsible for glioma cell phenotypes, we considered the most-upregulated genes in human glioma. By combining the predicted data by starbase program and the 100 most significantly upregulated genes in glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) and brain low-grade glioma (LGG) samples from the GEPIA database, we found five genes to be potential targets of miR-31-5p in glioma (Fig. 5A). Among these candidates, we observed a striking decrease in the expression of NES mRNA in miR-31-5p-overexpressing LN229 and T98G cells (Fig. 5B and C). The data predicted by starbase program showed that NES mRNA contains a 3′UTR element that is partially complementary to miR-31-5p (UCUUGCC) (Fig. 5D). Using 3′UTR reporter assays, we ascertained that NES is a miR-31-5p target (Fig. 5E and F); mutations in the miR-31-5p recognition sites (Fig. 5D) rendered the constructs unresponsive to miR-31-5p induction (Fig. 5E and F). We then validated the regulation of miR-31-5p in NES expression in LN229 and T98G cells. Reduced expression of miR-31-5p by miRNA inhibitor vector (in-miR-31-5p) (Fig. 5G) led to a clear augmentation in NES protein expression (Fig. 5H and I). Conversely, miR-31-5p elevation inhibited the level of NES protein in the two cell lines (Fig. 5H and I), supporting the targeting of NES by miR-31-5p. Additionally, analysis of NES expression in glioma tissues and cells confirmed the upregulation of NES in human glioma (Fig. 5J-L). Moreover, stimulation of 4 Gy X-ray did not affect NES mRNA expression in LN229 and T98G cells (Supplementary Fig. 1B).

MiR-31-5p/NES axis is a critical mechanism of circATP8B4 in impacting glioma cell proliferation, motility, invasion and radiosensitivity

The NES elevation efficacy of the NES expression cDNA lacking the 3′UTR (oe-NES) was proved by western blot (Fig. 6A). Given that circATP8B4 and NES 3′UTR bind to miR-31-5p via a shared pairing sequence, we decided to evaluate whether circATP8B4 might control NES expression through miR-31-5p. As expected, in LN229 and T98G cells, circATP8B4 loss caused a remarkable decrease in NES protein expression compared to sh-NC control; this repression was partially rescued by decreased miR-31-5p expression (Fig. 6B), suggesting the ceRNA activity of circATP8B4 to involve NES regulation by miR-31-5p.

(A) Western blot showing the elevated expression of NES protein in LN229 and T98G cells transfected by oe-NES or vector compared with control cells. (B-G) Western blot showing NES protein expression (B), EdU cell proliferation assay (C), wound-healing assay for cell motility (D), transwell invasion assay (E), N-cadherin and vimentin expression by western blot (F and G) in LN229 and T98G cells transfected with sh-circATP8B4#2 + in-miR-31-5p, sh-circATP8B4#2 + oe-NES, sh-circATP8B4#2 or sh-NC. (H-K) Transfected LN229 and T98G cells were subjected to radiation treatment as indicated, followed by the detection of cell survival using a standard colony formation assay (H), cell apoptosis by flow cytometry based on the staining with Annexin V and PI (I), caspase 3 and caspase 9 activities using the corresponding assay kit (J and K).

To examine whether the miR-31-5p/NES axis mediates the capacity of circATP8B4 in impacting glioma cell phenotypes, we silenced miR-31-5p by in-miR-31-5p vector or increased NES expression (without 3′UTR) by the NES expression plasmid (oe-NES) in circATP8B4-silenced LN229 and T98G cells. Through a western blot assay, transfection of oe-NES strongly abolished sh-circATP8B4-driven NES reduction in the two cell lines (Fig. 6B). Remarkably, reduced expression of miR-31-5p or co-expression of NES was able to rescue sh-circATP8B4-imposed suppression of cell proliferation (Fig. 6C), motility (Fig. 6D) and invasion (Fig. 6E) compared with control group. In addition, reduced expression of miR-31-5p or co-expression of NES reversed the influence of circATP8B4 loss on N-cadherin and vimentin expression (Fig. 6F and G). We also determined the effect on cell radiosensitivity. Indeed, reduced expression of miR-31-5p or co-expression of NES was able to suppress sh-circATP8B4 promotion of cell radiosensitivity, as presented by the elevated survival (Fig. 6H), reduced apoptosis (Fig. 6I), as well as decreased activities of caspase 3 and caspase 9 (Fig. 6J and K) in LN229 and T98G cells transfected with sh-circATP8B4#2 + in-miR-31-5p or sh-circATP8B4#2 + oe-NES after radiation treatment compared with sh-circATP8B4#2 alone transfected cells. Overall, these observations suggest the regulatory effect of circATP8B4 on glioma cell phenotypes through miR-31-5p/NES axis.

Downregulation of circATP8B4 diminishes tumor growth and enhances the anticancer effect of radiotherapy in vivo

Our in vitro results led us to study the impact of circATP8B4 downregulation in a classic in vivo xenograft model: we stably transduced LN229 cells with lentiviruses expressing sh-circATP8B4#2 or sh-NC and injected them into the nude mice. Seven days postinjection, the xenografts were treated with or without a single dose of 4 Gy irradiation. At the endpoint, we sacrificed the mice and collected the xenograft tumors for immunohistochemical analysis. Although sh-circATP8B4#2 lentivirus transduction or radiation treatment did not significantly affect the body weight of mice (Fig. 7A), they did, however, reduced tumor volume and weight (Fig. 7B–D), indicating that circATP8B4 silencing or radiation treatment suppresses tumor growth. Moreover, a more distinct suppression of tumor growth was found in the combination with sh-circATP8B4#2 lentivirus transduction and radiation treatment (Fig. 7B–D). Immunohistochemical analysis showed that the tumors in sh-circATP8B4#2 transduction group had fewer cells stained for NES and Vimentin staining and more cells stained for cleaved-caspase 3 staining than sh-NC controls (Fig. 7E). Moreover, the tumors with simultaneous sh-circATP8B4#2 transduction and radiation treatment had fewer cells stained for NES and Vimentin staining and more cells stained for cleaved-caspase 3 staining compared with those in sh-circATP8B4#2 alone group (Fig. 7E). All these data demonstrate that circATP8B4 silencing diminishes the growth of LIN229 cells and sensitizes them to radiotherapy.

DISCUSSION

A central role of circRNAs in the development and radioresistance of human tumors, including glioma, has begun to emerge [15, 32]. In this report, we illustrate that circATP8B4 is a highly expressed circRNA in human glioma, with the ability to modulate glioma cell malignant phenotypes and radiosensitivity. Furthermore, we establish a novel mechanistic basis, the circATP8B4/miR-31-5p/NES ceRNA crosstalk, for the modulatory role of circATP8B4 in glioma.

In agreement with our findings, circATP8B4 dysregulation in glioma has been documented by circRNA microarray analysis and qRT-PCR [15]. Moreover, circATP8B4 has been highlighted to function as a contributor to glioma radioresistance by sequestering miR-766 [15]. However, no studies proved whether circATP8B4 dysregulation causally participates in glioma development. In this report, apart from the enhancement on cell radiosensitivity, we first demonstrate that circATP8B4 deficiency can impair glioma cell growth, motility and invasion. Moreover, our data ascertain that circATP8B4 has a higher tolerance to the exoribonuclease RNase R, as previously described for other circRNAs [33, 34]. In addition, circATP8B4 is predominantly localized to the cytoplasm, offering the possibility for the interplay between circATP8B4 and miRNAs; the latter controls mRNA expression in the cytoplasm [35].

Our results first define the role of circATP8B4 as a miR-31-5p sponge in glioma cells. Functional studies in cancer cell lines have documented the contradictory activity of miR-31-5p. For instance, overexpression of miR-31-5p in lung adenocarcinoma cells induces cell motility and growth while hinders apoptosis via TNS1/p53 axis [36]. Conversely, elevated miR-31-5p expression impedes prostate cancer cell metastasis and growth [37]. Additionally, miR-31-5p can attenuate hepatocellular carcinoma cell radioresistance induced by peroxisomal biogenesis factor 5 (PEX5) [38]. In glioma, miR-31-5p is underexpressed and it can participate in cell cycle, growth and angiogenesis [18]. Moreover, co-overexpression of miR-31-5p and miR-145-5p leads to a repression of glioblastoma cell invasion [39]. In this paper, we first point to the role of miR-31-5p as a functional mediator of circATP8B4 in the context of impacting glioma cell malignant phenotypes and radiosensitivity. Similarly, circATP8B4 in the extracellular vesicles from radioresistant U251 glioma cells may contribute to glioma radioresistance by sponging miR-766 [15]. In addition, LINC01116 performs important functions in glioma cell motility, growth and angiogenesis through miR-31-5p sequestration [18].

NES expression is enhanced in human glioma and is tightly correlated with poor outcomes and prognosis of patients with glioma [21]. Enhanced expression of NES can predict the extent of neoangiogenesis in glioblastoma multiforme [40]. NES has established a crucial role in the spindle assembly and cell cycle distribution of glioblastoma cells [22]. Moreover, NES is able to promote glioma cell motility and invasiveness [23]. Additionally, NES is a marker for glioma stem cells and is differentially expressed in proliferating glioma cells versus nonproliferating cells [41]. These findings indicate the crucial activity of NES in human glioma. In this report, we first confirm NES as a direct miR-31-5p target using luciferase assays. Our results also identify the ceRNA activity of circATP8B4 to involve the post-transcriptional NES regulation by miR-31-5p. Furthermore, the miR-31-5p/NES axis is ascertained as a critical mechanism of circATP8B4 in impacting glioma cell malignant phenotypes and radiosensitivity in vitro. Interestingly, our rescue experiments show that addition of oe-NES can completely reverse the inhibitory effect of sh-circATP8B4#2 on NES protein expression, but it does not completely reverse the effects of sh-circATP8B4#2 on cell proliferation, invasion and motility (Fig. 6). An earlier report showed that circATP8B4 can sponge miR-766 to contribute to glioma cell radioresistance [15], demonstrating the implication of the circATP8B4/miR-766 cascade in glioma radioresistance. We thus speculate that the circATP8B4/miR-31-5p/NES ceRNA crosstalk and circATP8B4/miR-766 cascade may be two paralleled or interactional axes in regulating glioma progression and radioresistance, which may form a complex network in glioma. Based on these findings, we speculate that there may be other miRNA/mRNA axes, other biomolecules, other signaling pathways, and so on, that remain to be identified in the regulation of circATP8B4. In addition, although in vivo xenograft studies have validated the regulation of circATP8B4 in cell growth and radiosensitivity, they are hampered at present by the lack of the exploration of the circATP8B4/miR-31-5p/NES ceRNA mechanism and other circATP8B4/miRNA/mRNA axes.

Collectively, we have defined a novel ceRNA crosstalk underlying glioma development and radiosensitivity. CircATP8B4 sponges miR-31-5p to impact glioma cell proliferation, motility, invasion and radiosensitivity via upregulation of NES. Developing pharmacological agents that targeting circATP8B4/miR-31-5p/NES ceRNA crosstalk may thus have potential to improve the outcome of glioma patients.

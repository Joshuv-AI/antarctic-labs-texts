Abundancy of z z -Šoltés’ digraphs
Stijn Cambie

Abstract
We prove the existence of infinitely many Šoltés’ digraphs, the digraph analogue of Šoltés’ graphs. We also give an example of a Šoltés’ digraph with trivial automorphism group.



1
Introduction

As an analogue to Šoltés graphs, which originate from Šoltés’ paper
[
10
]
from 1991, and the extended question
[
1
, Prob. 3]
, we define a
z
-Šoltés’ digraph as a digraph
D
for which the removal of any vertex decreases its total distance by exactly
z
. Here the total distance,
W(D)
, is the sum of distances (length of a shortest connecting path) over all order pairs of vertices.
When
z=0
, such a digraph is naturally named a Šoltés’ digraph.
When
z\leq 0
, such a digraph is a negative-Šoltés’ digraph.

We try to strengthen the intuition behind the main question in
[
5
, Ques. 13]
, which asks if there are infinitely many negative-Šoltés’ graphs with minimum degree at least
3
, by confirming it in the digraph case. We do so by first proving that there are infinitely many negative-Šoltés’ digraphs, and further strengthening this to the existence of infinitely many Šoltés’ digraphs.

Theorem 1
.

For every
z\in\mathbb{Z},
there are infinitely many digraphs
D
for which
W(D)-W(D\setminus v)=z
for every
v\in V.

Corollary 2
.

There are infinitely many Šoltés’ digraphs.

The proof involves a specific class of vertex-transitive digraphs, circulant digraphs
D(n,S).
Here the vertices are named with integers in
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, and there is a directed edge from
i
to
j
iff
j-i\in S\cup\{-1\}
(considered modulo
n
).
Hereby we choose
n
and
S
carefully in a few steps.

Example 3
.

Next to
D(11,\{1\})\cong C_{11}
, also
D(85,\{4\})
is Šoltés’ digraph (and even oriented graph). This one is depicted (on a torus) in
Fig.

1
.

Figure 1
:
The Šoltes’ digraph
D(85,\{4\})

To have an idea that
W(D)=W(D\setminus v)
for
D=D(85,\{4\})
and a
v\in V(D)
, one can observe that after removing
v
the distance from its left neighbour towards its right neighbour changes from
2
to
22
(
2\equiv 21\cdot-4+1\pmod{85})
. There are fewer vertices and thus summands in the total distance of
D\setminus v
, but some of these distance are larger than they were within
D
.

Finally, we note that there are also non-vertex-transitive Šoltés’ digraphs.
Using the census from
[
9
]
, by orienting its edges (and some edges in both directions), we can find e.g. (two) Šoltés’ digraphs which are
2
-regular (
\delta+=\delta^{-}=\Delta^{+}=\Delta^{-}=2
), bipartite and not vertex-transitive. See
[
4
,
SoltesDigraphs/SoltesDigraph_order960
]
.
By combining multiple difference sets, we obtain an example of a Šoltés’ digraph of order
n=3306
, with a trivial automorphism group, which would disprove a digraph analogue of conjectures
[
3
, Conj. 1.2]
(and other beliefs) in the strongest possible sense.
This digraph is not regular in the sense that
\delta+=12<\Delta^{+}=14
(
\delta^{-}=\Delta^{-}=13
).
See
[
4
,
SoltesDigraphs/NonVTDigraph3306
]
. By similar modifications, one may expect there to be infinitely many of these, implying the abundance.

In conclusion, these findings enforce intuition from
[
5
]
about the graph case, that the essence is the extremal question of the existence of infinitely many negative-Šoltés’ graphs.
If there is a clear abundance of negative-Šoltés’ graphs, we can expect an abundance of Šoltés’ graphs.

2
Proofs on the abundancy of
z
-Šoltés’ digraphs

We imagine the reader being familiar with the total distance of a graph
G=(V,E)
and digraph
D=(V,A)
, and refer to the survey
[
8
]
on the Wiener index (total distance).

We use
\delta^{+},\delta^{-},\Delta^{+},\Delta^{-}
for the minimum and maximum out- and indegree of a digraph.
The in- and out-transmission of a vertex
v
is denoted with
\sigma^{-}(v)=\sum_{u\in V}d(u,v)
and
\sigma^{+}(v)=\sum_{u\in V}d(v,u).

The total distance of a digraph equals
W(D)=\sum_{v\in V}\sigma^{-}(v)=\sum_{v\in V}\sigma^{+}(v)=\sum_{(u,v)\in V\times V}d(u,v)

We first define the circulant digraphs in the following way (adding
-1
to the difference set
S
compared with the normal convention).

Definition 4
.

For a subset
S\subseteq[n-2]=\{1,2,\ldots,n-2\}
, we define the digraph
D(n,S)
as the digraph with vertex set
V=[n]
and directed edges
E=\{(i,i-1)\mid i\in[n]\}\cup\{(i,i+k)\mid i\in[n],k\in S\}.

Here numbers are interpreted modulo
n
.

We can observe that this family leads to infinitely many vertex-transitive examples
for which
W(D)-W(D\setminus v)
can be either positive, or negative.

Here positive is trivial by considering dense digraphs, by the following digraph analogue of
[
7
, Prop. 8]
for which the short proof easily extends.

Proposition 5
.

Let
D
be a digraph for which
\delta^{-}(D)+\delta^{+}(D)\geq n\geq 4.

Then both
D
and
D\setminus v
have diameter
2
and consequently
W(D)-W(D\setminus v)>0
.

Proof.

Let
u,u^{\prime}
be any two arbitrary vertices for which
\vec{uu^{\prime}}\not inA
(otherwise
d(u,u^{\prime})=1
) and take any vertex
v\not\in\{u,u^{\prime}\}
. By pigeon hole principle there are at least two vertices
w,w^{\prime}
such that
u
is an in-neighbour from them and
u^{\prime}
is an out-neighbour, since
\lvert N^{-}(u)\cap N^{+}(u^{\prime})\rvert\geq\delta^{-}(u)+\delta^{+}(u)-(n-2)\geq 2.

Hence after removing a vertex, there is at least one of
w,w^{\prime}
present and
d(u,u^{\prime})=2.

∎

Related to this, in the appendix,
9
, we prove the stronger statement that diameter two implies that at least one vertex
v
satisfies
W(D)-W(D\setminus v)>0
.

The infinitude of examples where the difference is negative, is proven in the following proposition.
Note that here we do not care about the number of such non-isomorphic digraphs.
That would be hard due to counterexamples to the digraph analogue of Adam’s conjecture
[
2
]
. But one can imagine that there are roughly
2^{\Theta(n^{0.5})}
of them, which is a huge number.

Proposition 6
.

If
\max S\leq 1/9n^{1/2}
, for
n
sufficiently large,
W(D_{n,S})-W(D_{n,S}\setminus v)<0
for
v\in V(D_{n,S}).

Proof.

Let
m=\max S
.
Let
v
be the vertex which corresponds to the number
n
.
Note that
\sigma^{+}(v)=\sigma^{-}(v)\leq n\cdot diamD\leq n\left(\frac{n}{m}+m\right)<9.5n^{3/2}
.

On the other hand, the shortest directed path from
0<i<2\sqrt{n}
to
n-j
(with
0<j<2\sqrt{n}
) had length
i+j
within
D
, but is at least of length
\frac{n-i-j}{m}
in
D\setminus v
.
Hence the distances between those pairs increase by at least
(5-o(1))\sqrt{n}.

This is a total increase of at least
(20-o(1))n^{3/2}
, which is larger than
\sigma^{+}(v)+\sigma^{-}(v).

Equivalently,
W(D_{n,S})<W(D_{n,S}\setminus v).

∎

Knowing that
W(D)-W(D\setminus v)
can be both positive and negative, by an abundance of examples, we now prove the main result,
1
, that every exact difference can appear infinitely often.
It may also be clear that one cannot aim for an exact characterization as wondered about in
[
1
, Prob. 3]
.

Proof of
1
.

We will prove that for every
z\in\mathbb{Z},
and every sufficiently large (as a function of
z
) positive integer
m
, there is a digraph
D
of the form
D(n,S)
with
\max S=m
and
W(D)-W(D\setminus v)=z
for every

So fix
m
large (in particular, larger than
\lvert z\rvert)
.

Hereby we first choose
n
such that the difference is near
z
when considering
D(n,[m])
.

Claim 7
.

There is a choice
n\sim 6m^{2}
such that
D(n,[m])
satisfies

z-9m\leq W(D)-W(D-v)\leq z-3
.

Proof.

Write
n=a(m+1)+1+r
, where
0\leq r\leq m.

Let

where
S=[m].

Then

\sigma^{+}(v)=\sigma^{-}(v)=(m+1)\binom{a+1}{2}+r(a+1).

(1)

The sum of the extra distance between pairs of vertices in

\sum_{\{u,w\}\subset V\setminus v}d_{D\setminus v}(u,w)-d_{D}(u,w)

W(D-v)-\frac{n-2}{n}W(D)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{a-i}\left(\mathopen{}\left\lceil\frac{n-j-i}{m}\right\rceil\mathclose{}-(j+i)\right)+\binom{a}{2}=\frac{m+1}{m}\binom{a}{3}+O(a^{2}).

(2)

The latter is the sum of detours between elements near

and

If the shortest path in

If every shortest path from

Thus

This implies that

. There are

many pairs of positive integers

Comparing (

.
More precisely (see

If

Changing

and at most

Thus there is a choice of

satisfies the condition of the claim. ∎

Appendix

Appendix A
Few negative Šoltes’ digraphs

In this part, we consider digraphs with diameter
2
and prove the analogue of
[
5
, Prop. 4]
. Note that almost all digraphs of order
n
have diameter
2
, considering the Erdős-Renyi random graph model for digraphs. The proof combines the ideas in the proofs from
[
5
, Prop. 4]
and
[
6
, Prop. 6]
.

Proposition 9
.

A digraph
D
of order
n>1
with
diam(D)\leq 2
has at least one vertex
v
for which either
D\setminus v
is disconnected, or
W(D\setminus v)<W(D).

Proof.

Assume
D
would be a counterexample to the statement (which trivially needs to satisfy
n>4
).
First notice that
\delta^{-}(D)
and
\delta^{+}(D)
are both at least two, since otherwise removing the vertex from the unique incoming edge or unique outgoing edge, disconnects the digraph.
By the handshaking lemma, the number of arcs is thus at least
2n.

For a pair of vertices
u,u^{\prime},
there can be at most one vertex
w
in
V\setminus\{u,u^{\prime}\}
whose deletion increases
d(u,u^{\prime}).

In that case,
\vec{uu^{\prime}}
is not an arc of
D,
while
\vec{uw}
and
\vec{wu^{\prime}}
are.
In this case, associate
w
to the ordered pair
(u,u^{\prime}).

Since there are
n(n-1)-\lvert A(D)\rvert\leq n(n-3)
ordered pairs which are not a directed edge, there is a vertex
v
associated with at most
n-3
pairs.

For each pair
(u,u^{\prime})
to which it is associated, we know that there is at least one of the
n-3
neighbours
w
in
V\setminus\{v,u,u^{\prime}\}
for which both ordered pairs
(u,w)
and
(w,u^{\prime})
were not associated to
v
.
Hence
d_{D\setminus v}(u,u^{\prime})\leq d_{D\setminus v}(u,w)+d_{D\setminus v}(w,u^{\prime})\leq 4.

Thus there are at most
n-3
pairs for which the distance goes up by at most
2.

The latter is smaller than
\sigma^{+}(v)+\sigma^{-}(v)\geq 2(n-1)
, i.e.,
W(D\setminus v)<W(D).

∎

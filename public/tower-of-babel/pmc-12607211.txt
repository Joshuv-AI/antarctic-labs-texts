The epidemiological features of and correlation between central obesity and major cardiometabolic disease clustering among middle-aged and elderly residents in Xinjiang
Authors: Maidina Abudusilimu, Xiamusiye Muyiduli, Shawulaxi Rejiafu, Adila Sulidan, Chunmei Ling, Rong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundResearch analysing central obesity and cardiometabolic diseases among residents in multiethnic areas in western China is relatively rare. The purpose of this study was to explore the epidemiological features of and correlations between central obesity and cardiometabolic disease clustering in residents of Xinjiang aged 40 years and older.MethodsData from the “China Chronic Disease and Risk Factor Surveillance (CCDRFS)” program, which covered 8 districts in Xinjiang from 2013 to 2023, were used, and data were collected via questionnaires, body measurements and laboratory tests. Chi-square tests and binary logistic regression were used for statistical analysis in SPSS 26.0.ResultsA total of 10,908 participants (46.21% male, 53.79% female) were included in this study. The prevalence of central obesity was 81.49% and was higher among women. The prevalences of mild and severe disease were 34.31% and 47.18%, respectively. The prevalence of cardiometabolic disease clustering was 40.95%. Factors such as sex, age, urban‒rural distribution, and education level were associated with the prevalence of central obesity and cardiometabolic disease clustering. The prevalence of central obesity showed a continuous increasing trend over time, whereas the clustering of major cardiometabolic diseases decreased first but then increased. The prevalence of cardiometabolic diseases increased with increasing central obesity in men and women and in the overall population. Multivariate analysis revealed that after adjustment for age, urban‒rural distribution and education level, the risk of having two major cardiometabolic diseases was 1.860 (95% confidence interval [95% CI]: 1.533–2.257) and 3.658 (95% CI: 3.035–4.409) times greater, respectively, in men with mild and severe central obesity than in men with noncentral obesity, while the corresponding values were 1.758 (95% CI: 1.439–2.147) and 3.000 (95% CI: 2.480–3.629), respectively, in women. The risk of having three or more major cardiometabolic diseases was 2.415 (95% CI: 1.730–3.371) and 5.552 (95% CI: 4.039–7.632) times greater in men with mild and severe central obesity, respectively, than in men with noncentral obesity, and the corresponding values were 2.130 (95% CI: 1.476–3.075) and 5.570 (95% CI: 3.958–7.838), respectively, in women.ConclusionCentral obesity and cardiometabolic disease clustering is significant in Xinjiang’s middle-aged and elderly population. The risk of cardiometabolic disease clustering is greater in people with central obesity, especially those with greater waist circumference. The prevalence of related diseases has increased significantly and demands attention in terms of prevention and control.

Background

Globally, the number of patients with cardiometabolic diseases has shown a concerning growth trend, and these diseases have become major global public health problems. This trend is particularly significant in middle-aged and elderly individuals, who are at high risk for these diseases [1]. The prevalence of central obesity, a type of obesity in which body fat is distributed mainly in the abdomen, is also increasing annually [2]. Obesity is closely related to the occurrence of a variety of cardiometabolic diseases, including hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, myocardial infarction and stroke [3–7]. These diseases can exist independently or interact to form a complex network, increasing the risk of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events. An increasing number of middle-aged and elderly people have two or more cardiometabolic diseases. The occurrence of these diseases not only increases the medical burden on patients but also significantly affects their socioeconomic status and quality of life. The prevalence of central obesity, cardiometabolic diseases and their clustering in Xinjiang is high [8, 9].

To date, studies analysing the relationship between central obesity and cardiometabolic diseases among residents in multiethnic areas in western China are relatively rare. The purpose of this study was to explore the epidemiological features of and correlations between central obesity and major cardiometabolic disease clustering among middle-aged and elderly residents of Xinjiang by conducting a systematic epidemiological investigation and analysis. These results are highly important for more accurately formulating scientific and effective public health policies, optimizing disease prevention strategies, and improving the effectiveness of chronic disease prevention and control. The ultimate goal is to reduce the incidence of chronic diseases, improve the quality of life, and reduce the social and economic burdens of middle-aged and elderly people.

Methods

Research objects

A total of 10,908 people from the China Chronic Disease and Risk Factor Surveillance (CCDRFS) study conducted in 8 surveillance districts in Xinjiang in 2013, 2018 and 2023 were selected as the research objects.

The eligibility criteria included (i) being ≥ 40 years of age; (ii) living at the current address for more than 6 of the past 12 months; (iii) not being pregnant; and (iv) having no serious health condition or illness that would prevent participation, including intellectual disabilities or language disorders.

Research methods

The data were obtained from the CCDRFS program covering 8 districts in Xinjiang from 2013 to 2023. The CCDRFS program was first conducted by the Chinese Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (China CDC) in 2004 and is a series of periodical, nationally representative, cross-sectional surveys covering 31 provinces in mainland China in which data concerning chronic and noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) and related risk factors are collected from Chinese adults.

The CCDRFS program was incorporated into the national disease surveillance points (DSPs) system, which uses its surveillance point as the primary sampling unit (PSU) in a multistage stratified cluster sampling framework. Each DSP area covers one rural county or one urban district [10].

Trained interviewers from local CDCs conducted the face-to-face interviews, physical measurements, biochemical sample collection and sample pretreatment. For most participants, interviews were conducted during home visits. Physical measurements and biochemical sample collection were conducted at a community health centre. An internet-based information management system was used for sampling, onsite identity confirmation, quality control, laboratory data submission, physical examination reporting and data downloading. All local laboratories passed the quality certification and underwent daily quality control during the testing process. The details of the design and survey methods of the CCDRFS have been described elsewhere [10–12].

The CCDRFS program was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of the China CDC, and all the participants signed informed consent forms. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

The major strength of the CCDRFS program is its representativeness of both the national and the provincial populations of China. All the surveys had large sample sizes and high response rates. This program is also the only representative NCD and risk factor surveillance system at the province level in China. The details of the design, objectives and survey methods of the CCDRFS have been described elsewhere [11, 12].

Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 software was used to analyse the collected data. Continuous variables were tested for normality, those with a normal distribution are expressed as the means and standard deviations (x ± s), and categorical variables are expressed as percentages (%).Chi-square tests were used to analyse the correlations between different variables and compare the significance of differences between different groups. Binary logistic regression was used for the multivariate analysis, with stratification by sex, while adjusting for age, education level, urban‒rural distribution, etc. The results of the analysis were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Basic information

A total of 10,908 participants were included, including 5041 males (46.21%), 5867 females (53.79%), 4073 urban residents (37.34%), and 6835 rural residents (62.66%). The average age was 57.38 ± 10.81 years, and the average waist circumference was 91.74 ± 11.11 cm. The average age and waist circumference were significantly greater for men than for women (P < 0.001). The prevalences of hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, myocardial infarction and stroke were 46.52%, 19.19%, 62.84%, 2.46% and 4.07%, respectively. The prevalences of diabetes and dyslipidaemia among men were greater than those among women, and there was no significant difference in the other three diseases between men and women. Basic information of the research participants

Epidemiological features of central obesity and the clustering of major cardiometabolic diseases

The prevalence of central obesity was 34.34% for mild obesity and 47.18% for severe obesity, and 80.40% of men and 82.43% of women had some degree of central obesity. The prevalence of severe central obesity in women was greater than that in men (p < 0.001). There were significant differences in the prevalence of mild and severe central obesity between the urban and rural groups and among the different age groups and educational levels (p < 0.05). The proportion of patients with two cardiometabolic diseases was 29.55%, and the proportion of patients with three or more cardiometabolic diseases was 11.40%. The prevalence of two cardiometabolic diseases was greater among men than among women (p < 0.001). The prevalence of two and three or more cardiometabolic diseases significantly differed between the urban and rural groups and among the different age and educational level groups (p < 0.05). These findings indicate that urban residents are more prone to cardiometabolic disease clustering. With increasing age, the risk of clustering increases, and with increasing education level, the risk of clustering decreases. See Table 2 for details.

Discussion

The results of this study revealed that the prevalence of central obesity among middle-aged and elderly people aged 40 years and older in Xinjiang is very high, with rates of 34.31% and 47.18% for mild and severe obesity, respectively. The total obesity rate (81.49%) is much higher than the national rate of central obesity in elderly individuals aged 60 years and older, which was 35.7% in 2011 [4] and 55.4% in 2013 [17]. This figure was also higher than the rate of central obesity in elderly individuals aged 65 years and older in Wuhan in 2012 (51.58%) [18]. The prevalence of central obesity in men and women was 80.40% and 82.43%, respectively. The prevalence of severe central obesity in women (49.41%) was higher than that in men (44.57%), which was essentially consistent with the findings of other related studies. The prevalence of central obesity among middle-aged and elderly people aged 45 years and older in China was higher among women (59.7%) than among men (37.4%) in 2013 [19], and the prevalence among women (57.6%) was also higher than that among men (38.1%) in 2015 [20]. According to related research, the prevalence of central obesity among middle-aged and elderly residents in Xinjiang is relatively high, and women are more likely to have central obesity than men are. In addition, the rates of mild and severe central obesity differ between urban and rural areas and across different ages and educational levels.

A total of 40.95% of the middle-aged and elderly people aged 40 years and older in Xinjiang presented cardiovascular metabolic disease clustering, 29.55% and 11.40% of whom had two and three or more comorbidities, respectively. The prevalence of these two cardiometabolic diseases was greater among men than among women. The prevalence of two and three or more cardiometabolic diseases significantly differed between the urban and rural groups and among the different age and educational level groups. These findings indicate that urban residents are more likely to present cardiometabolic disease clustering. With increasing age, the clustering increases, and with increasing education level, the clustering decreases. These findings are essentially consistent with the results of other related studies. When public health policies and interventions are formulated, we should consider the influence of sex, age, urban‒rural distribution, education level, etc., and provide targeted health education and support to help people with different characteristics improve their lifestyles and reduce their risk of cardiometabolic diseases.

In this study, central obesity was significantly associated with a high risk of cardiometabolic disease clustering, which was consistent with the results of a study of residents aged 35 years and older in Xinjiang from 2014–2017 [21]. In both men and women, as the degree of central obesity increases, the clustering of cardiometabolic diseases increases. In this study, after adjusting for age, urban‒rural distribution, education level, etc., the risk of having two cardiometabolic diseases was 1.860 (95% CI: 1.533–2.257) and 3.658 (95% CI: 3.035–4.409) times greater in men with mild and severe central obesity, respectively, than in men with noncentral obesity, and the corresponding values were 1.758 (95% CI: 1.439–2.147) and 3.000 (95% CI: 2.480–3.629), respectively, in women. The risk of having three or more cardiometabolic diseases was 2.415 (95% CI: 1.730–3.371) and 5.552 (95% CI: 4.039–7.632) times greater in men with mild and severe central obesity, respectively, than in men with noncentral obesity, and the corresponding values were 2.130 (95% CI: 1.476–3.075) and 5.570 (95% CI: 3.958–7.838), respectively, in women.

Owing to the unique geographical environment and customs, the dietary habits of residents in Xinjiang are characterized by high salt, high fat, high carbohydrate, low vegetable and low fruit intake, resulting in a high rate of central obesity [22]. Dietary imbalance and central obesity can also lead to a relatively high prevalence of cardiometabolic diseases, especially in Asians. Central obesity is an important risk factor for cardiometabolic diseases and plays a very important role in predicting the risk of cardiometabolic diseases [23, 24]. Numerous studies have reported that the incidence of central obesity in middle-aged and elderly people is positively correlated with the prevalence of various cardiometabolic diseases; that is, with increasing waist circumference, the incidence and clustering of cardiometabolic diseases increases significantly [4, 21, 23, 25]. Studies have shown that excessive fat accumulation in visceral tissues in people with central obesity leads to a decrease in the insulin sensitivity of target tissues and increased levels of C-reactive protein (CRP), which in turn increases the risk of diabetes and dyslipidaemia [26, 27]. The China Kadoorie Biobank (CKD) study revealed that the risk ratio of central obesity and diabetes was 4.66 for men and 3.16 for women in Zhejiang Province [28]. Studies have shown that central obesity is significantly associated with an increased risk of hypertension [29, 30]. The statistical association between central obesity and dyslipidaemia was also consistent with that reported in a study from Gansu Province [31]. Central obesity is a risk factor for coronary heart disease, according to a study conducted in South Korea [32]. Towfighi et al. reported that central obesity is an independent risk factor for stroke [33].

In summary, the high prevalence of central obesity in middle-aged and elderly residents in Xinjiang and its close association with cardiometabolic diseases suggest that it may pose a serious threat to public health. These findings indicate that the incidence of these diseases can be reduced by controlling central obesity. Therefore, this information should be integrated into public health policies, and targeted health promotion programs with weight management as the core should be promoted in this population. These interventions include, but are not limited to, the intervention of eating habits, the enhancement of sports facilities in public areas to increase the availability of opportunities and resources for sports activities, the encouragement of regular physical examinations, the improvement of the public’s awareness of the harm of central obesity, and the promotion of awareness of chronic disease prevention through the fortification of health education to reduce the incidence of and mortality from cardiometabolic diseases and achieve long-term public health improvement. Second, considering the relationships between obesity and cultural habits, as well as the specific culture and living habits in Xinjiang, the support of the community and local leaders in the formulation of public health policies is needed to make them more targeted and culturally sensitive. For example, in providing health education, the specific needs and capacities of different ethnic groups and communities, as well as their preferences for specific diets, should be accounted for to increase the acceptance and effectiveness of the program. Finally, policies should focus on interdisciplinary cooperation, including among health departments, medical institutions, community organizations, educational institutions and research institutions. This ensures the comprehensiveness and continuity of policies and guides practice through scientific research while optimizing the process of program implementation and evaluation through interdisciplinary teams.

The results of this study provide an in-depth understanding of the prevalence of central obesity among middle-aged and elderly residents in Xinjiang and have important public health implications for the prevention and control of cardiometabolic diseases. This study highlights the need for targeted public health interventions to reduce the prevalence of central obesity and cardiometabolic diseases. Although there were no ethnic data in this study, these results could provide a baseline reference for multiethnic areas.

This study can reveal only the correlation between central obesity and cardiometabolic diseases, as owing to the limitations of cross-sectional studies, we cannot determine causal relationships. Future research can explore the causal relationship between central obesity and cardiometabolic diseases through a longitudinal research design. This study has certain limitations in terms of sample representativeness. As a large region with unique geographical characteristics, Xinjiang cannot be fully covered for all subtle regional differences by a single surveillance system. In addition, many factors affect the comorbidity of chronic diseases, and other confounding factors were not included in the analysis. For example, economic status, lifestyle habits, and genetic factors may affect the occurrence of central obesity and cardiovascular and cerebrovascular metabolic diseases. The impact of these factors should be further studied. Intervention studies can also be implemented to further explore the effectiveness of different prevention and intervention measures. Cost‒benefit analysis can also be performed to assess the cost‒effectiveness ratio of interventions for central obesity and cardiometabolic diseases, providing economic advice for the development of public health policies.

Conclusion

The prevalence of central obesity and cardiometabolic disease clustering among middle-aged and elderly people aged 40 years and older in Xinjiang is very high. This may pose a serious threat to public health. Central obesity is significantly associated with a high risk of cardiometabolic disease clustering. This finding suggests that the incidence of cardiometabolic diseases can be reduced by controlling central obesity. Therefore, this information should be integrated into public health policies, and targeted health promotion programs with weight management as the core should be promoted in this population.

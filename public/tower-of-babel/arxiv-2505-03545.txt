Blobbed topological recursion and KP integrability
A. Alexandrov, B. Bychkov, P. Dunin-Barkowski, M. Kazarian, S. Shadrin

Abstract
. We revise the notion of the blobbed topological recursion by extending it to the setting of generalized topological recursion as well as allowing blobs which do not necessarily admit topological expansion. We show that the so-called non-perturbative differentials form a special case of this revisited version of blobbed topological recursion. Furthermore, we prove the KP integrability of the differentials of blobbed topological recursion for the input data that include KP-integrable blobs. This result generalizes, unifies, and gives a new proof of the KP integrability of nonperturbative differentials conjectured by Borot–Eynard and recently proved by the authors.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we deal with the following concepts:

•

Topological recursion of Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin (the CEO topological recursion)
[
EO07
]
is a way to uniformly solve a huge variety of enumerative problems recursively. Its wide range of connections to the intersection theory of moduli spaces, matrix models, and integrability brings new insight in every instance of its applications.

•

Blobbed topological recursion
[
BS17
]
is an interface to encode more general solutions of the so-called abstract loop equations. It substantially extends the range of applications of topological recursion, see e.g.
[
AS23
,
BHW22
,
BD20
]
.

•

Generalized topological recursion
[
ABDBKS25
]
is a very recently defined generalization of the original CEO topological recursion that removes all restrictions on the input. It provides interesting and meaningful answers in very unexpected situations
[
BCGS25
]
.

•

KP integrability is a basic integrability property that keeps to reoccur in very different areas of mathematics. It can be understood globally as a property of a system of differentials, and it has close ties with topological recursion
[
BE12
,
EO24
,
ABDBKS24
,
ABDBKS25a
,
ABDBKS24a
]
.

Due to an enormous amount of applications of these concepts, it is very interesting to settle the foundational questions about their interrelation and possible context for the “ultimate” generalizations of the basic definitions. The main goal of the paper is to provide a new context to an old conjecture of Borot and Eynard
[
BE12
,
EO24
]
, which states that non-perturbative differentials of topological recursion are KP integrable. This conjecture was recently proved
[
ABDBKS24a
]
, and the implications of this result, for instance, for the knot theory, are still to be explored.

In a nutshell, the differentials of topological recursion defined on the Cartesian powers of a given Riemann surface
\Sigma
are merged via some convolution procedure with the so-called Krichever differentials. The latter ones are also differentials on the Cartesian powers of
\Sigma
and their expansions give algebro-geometric solutions of the KP hierarchy
[
Kri77
,
KS13
]
, while the differentials themselves provide a setup for conformal field theory on
\Sigma

[
KNTY88
,
Oog90
]
. The resulting convolution differentials are the output of non-perturbative topological recursion
[
EM11
,
BE12
,
BE15
]
.

The structure of the convolution procedure is very similar to the interface proposed by the blobbed topological recursion. And indeed, we show that the non-perturbative differentials give an instance of an extended version of blobbed topological recursion, where we allow to have non-topological expansions for the blobs. The blobs in this case are the Krichever differentials.

This, in fact, prompts also a revision of the definition of the blobbed topological recursion. We propose a new construction of generalized topological recursion with blobs, where the blobs essentially replace the Bergman kernel data. This provides us a very flexible deformation toolkit given by the freedom of choice of blobs. As a result, we give a new proof of the Borot–Eynard conjecture, conceptually different from the original one presented in
[
ABDBKS24a
]
.

We also take a closer look at the KP integrability property for a system of differentials
[
ABDBKS25a
]
. We explain how our global viewpoint matches existing constructions of algebro-geometric solutions of KP and multi-KP hierarchy
[
Kri77
,
KZ23
]
. Interesting effect occurs in the analysis of the convolution procedure that is a cornerstone of the constructions of the blobbed topological recursion and non-perturbative differentials. Namely, we prove that the convolution of KP-integrable systems of differentials produces a KP integrable system of differentials.

In our review of KP integrability considered as a global property for systems of differentials, as in
[
ABDBKS25a
]
, we also make a special accent on the multi-KP integrability aspects
[
KL03
]
that occur once we expand a given system of differentials at several points on the curve. Typically, it is done at the poles of
x
, one of the functions that forms the input data of topological recursion (see an extended explanation in
[
EO24
]
). However, one can choose any number of arbitrary points and we discuss the corresponding statement in detail.

It worth to mention that even if the underlying Riemann surface
\Sigma
is of genus
0
, and thus there are no non-perturbative effects and the (generalized) topological recursion is KP integrable by itself
[
ABDBKS24
]
, our discussion of multi-KP integrability is very useful. Namely, topological recursion is known to interact with the genus expansion of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds defined via Dubrovin’s superpotential
[
DBNOPS19
,
DBNOPS18
]
. As a corollary, our discussion of multi-KP integrability conceptually explains the effects observed in the study of the integrable systems of topological type for the Hurwitz–Frobenius manifolds in genus zero
[
Dub96
]
, where the multi-component KP system appears as a reduction of Givental–Milanov type approach to the Dubrovin–Zhang hierarchies, see a forthcoming paper
[
CLS25
]
.

1.2.
Organization of the paper

We keep this paper focused on conceptual revision of the definitions and their interrelations and do not include any applications or potential applications. It is a conscious choice, as there is enormous literature on applications in any case, while the foundational questions are often underrepresented.

In Section
2
we give a survey of KP integrability, in particular in the global context as a property of a system of multi-differentials on an algebraic curve.

In Section
3
we discuss the main technical tool that we apply to systems of differential, the so-called
convolution
, without any reference to their further application for topological recursion.

Finally, in Section
4
we use this convolution to discuss the extended version of blobbed topological recursion and its relation to KP integrability and its relation to the non-perturbative topological recursion.

2.
Review of global KP integrability

2.1.
KP integrability for a system of differentials

Let
\Sigma
be a possibly non-compact complex curve and
\{\omega_{n}\}
,
n\geq 1
be a system of symmetric meromorphic
n
-differentials on
\Sigma
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that the system
\{\omega_{n}\}
is KP integrable if there exists a bi-halfdifferential
K
on
\Sigma
with a singularity on the diagonal and the local behavior

(2)

K(p_{1},p_{2})=\left(\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}+(\text{holomorphic})\right)\sqrt{dz_{1}}\sqrt{dz_{2}},\quad z_{i}=z(p_{i}),

such that the following determinantal equalities hold

(3)

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(p_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{p_{2}}\to{p_{1}}}}\left(K(p_{1},p_{2})-\frac{\sqrt{dz_{1}}\sqrt{z_{2}}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\right),\quad z_{i}=z(p_{i}),

(4)

\displaystyle\omega_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\det\nolimits^{\circ}\|K(p_{i},p_{j})\|_{i,j=1,\dots,n}.

Here
z
is some local coordinate on
\Sigma
or just a meromorphic function. Neither the condition on the behavior of
K
near the diagonal nor an expression for
\omega_{1}
depend on a choice of
z
.

We assume that either
K
is meromorphic or
K
is a formal series in an additional formal parameter
\hbar
such that the coefficient of any power of
\hbar
is meromorphic. Then, the same holds for
\omega_{n}
: it is global meromorphic or it is a series in
\hbar
with meromorphic coefficients, respectively.

The term “halfdifferential” means that
K
is a section of a suitable square root of the canonical bundle over
\Sigma
with respect to each of its arguments.

The differential
\omega_{2}(p_{1},p_{2})=-K(p_{1},p_{2})K(p_{2},p_{1})
has a pole of order two on the diagonal, and one can show that
\omega_{n}-\delta_{n,2}\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}
is regular on the diagonals.

In fact, the kernel
K
can be expressed in terms of the differentials
\omega_{n}
by the following explicit formula

(5)

K(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{\sqrt{dz_{1}}\sqrt{dz_{2}}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\biggl(\int\limits_{p_{2}}^{p_{1}}\biggr)^{n}\Bigl(\omega_{n}(\tilde{p}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})-\delta_{n,2}\tfrac{d\tilde{z}_{1}d\tilde{z}_{2}}{(\tilde{z}_{1}-\tilde{z}_{2})^{2}}\Bigr)\right)

that holds for any choice of the local coordinate
z
. To be precise, this formula defines
K
locally as a formal expansion near a chosen point of the diagonal. We require, however, that
K
extends analytically globally and the determinantal identities hold globally.

To any choice of a point
q\in\Sigma
regular for all differentials and a local coordinate
z
at this point we can associate a formal power series
\tau(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
whose coefficients are defined by the power expansion of
\omega_{n}
in the chosen coordinate:

(6)

\omega_{n}=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial^{n}\log(\tau)}{\partial t_{k_{1}}\dots\partial t_{k_{n}}}\Bigm|_{t=0}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{k_{i}-1}dz_{i}+\delta_{n,2}\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}},

with the normalization
\tau(0)=1
.

Then,
the condition of KP integrability for a system of differentials
\omega_{n}
is equivalent to the condition that
\tau
is a KP tau function
(for some, and then, for any choice of
q
and
z
), see
[
ABDBKS25a
]
and Section
2.6
below. The latter condition can be expressed in the form of Hirota bilinear relations

(7)

\begin{gathered}\mathop{\rm res}_{z=0}e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(t_{k}-t^{\prime}_{k})z^{-k}}\tau(t-[z])\tau(t^{\prime}+[z])\frac{dz}{z^{2}}=0,\\
t\pm[z]\coloneq\bigl(t_{1}\pm\tfrac{z}{1},t_{2}\pm\tfrac{z^{2}}{2},t_{3}\pm\tfrac{z^{3}}{3},\dots\bigr),\end{gathered}

that hold identically in
t=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
and
t^{\prime}=(t^{\prime}_{1},t^{\prime}_{2},\dots)
. Namely, the coefficient of any
(t,t^{\prime})
-monomial in the expression under the residue is a Laurent
1
-differential in
z
with a pole of finite order at
z=0
, and we require that the coefficient of
\tfrac{dz}{z}
of this differential vanishes.

If the KP integrability holds, then the tau function
\tau
can also be expressed directly in terms of
K
by the following expansion in the basis of Schur functions
s_{\lambda}(t)

(8)

\displaystyle\tfrac{K(z_{1},z_{2})}{\sqrt{dz_{1}}\sqrt{dz_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\sum K_{i,j}z_{1}^{i}z_{2}^{j},\qquad|z_{2}|\ll|z_{1}|\ll 1,

\displaystyle\tau(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\det\|K_{\lambda_{i}-i,j-1}\|_{i,j=1,\dots,\ell(\lambda)}\;s_{\lambda}(t).

The coefficients
K_{i,j}
are also known as affine coordinates of the point of the Sato Grassmannian associated with a given tau function.

2.2.
Example: trivial system of differentials

A KP integrable system of differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
is called trivial if
\omega_{n}=0
for
n\neq 2
. The differential
\omega_{2}
cannot be equal to zero since it has a pole on the diagonal with biresidue
1
. However, the KP integrability implies that it should be of a specific form.

Proposition 2.2
.

A system of symmetric differentials
\omega_{n}=\delta_{n,2}B
is KP integrable if and only if the bidifferential
B=\omega_{2}
is of the form

(9)

B(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{dz(p_{1})\,dz(p_{2})}{(z(p_{1})-z(p_{2}))^{2}}

for some meromorphic function
z
. The function
z
is defined uniquely up to a linear fractional transformation.

This proposition is a reformulation of a result from
[
ABDBKS25a
]
.
For a trivial KP integrable system of differentials and for a point
q\in\Sigma
, one can find a local coordinate
z
, such that
\tau_{q,z}\equiv 1
,
\log(\tau_{q,z})\equiv 0
. Note, however, that for a generic choice of local coordinate
z
the tau function
\tau_{q,z}
is not constant anymore but rather
\log(\tau_{q,z})
is a nontrivial quadratic function.

Proposition is also valid in the case when
B
is a series in
\hbar
with meromorphic coefficients. In this case the function
z
and the local coordinate, trivializing the tau function, is also depending on
\hbar
.

If we require, in addition to triviality and KP integrability, that
\Sigma
is compact and
\omega_{2}
has no singularities apart from the diagonal, then
\Sigma
is necessarily rational, and
\omega_{2}
is the standard
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
Bergman kernel,
\omega_{2}=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}
, where
z
is an affine coordinate on
\Sigma=\mathbb{C}P^{1}
, see
[
ABDBKS25a
]
for details.

2.3.
Extended differentials

While discussing KP integrability for a system of differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
, it is useful to consider an extended set of
n|n
differentials (in fact, half-differentials)
\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}
and their connected counterparts
\Omega_{n}
defined by

(10)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}(p^{+}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},p^{-}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq k<\ell\leq n}\frac{(z^{+}_{k}-z^{+}_{\ell})(z^{-}_{k}-z^{-}_{\ell})}{(z^{+}_{k}-z^{-}_{\ell})(z^{-}_{k}-z^{+}_{\ell})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sqrt{dz^{+}_{i}}\sqrt{dz^{-}_{i}}}{z^{+}_{i}-z^{-}_{i}}\times

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\scriptsize\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\geq 0\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}=m\geq 1\end{subarray}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{i}!}\Big(\int\limits_{p^{-}_{i}}^{p^{+}_{i}}\Big)^{m_{i}}\bigl(\omega_{m}(\tilde{p}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket})-\delta_{m,2}\tfrac{d\tilde{z}_{1}d\tilde{z}_{2}}{(\tilde{z}_{1}-\tilde{z}_{2})^{2}}\bigr)\Bigg),

(11)

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}(p^{+}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},p^{-}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}{\ell}\sum_{\scriptsize\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{\ell}=\llbracket{n}\rrbracket,\\
\forall j\,I_{j}\not=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\Omega_{|I_{j}|}^{\bullet}(p^{+}_{I_{j}},p^{-}_{I_{j}}).

Here
p^{+}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}=(p^{+}_{1},\dots,p^{+}_{n})
and
p^{-}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}=(p^{-}_{1},\dots,p^{-}_{n})
are two sets of independent arguments,
z
is an arbitrary local coordinate on
\Sigma
, and
z^{\pm}_{i}=z(p^{\pm}_{i})
,
\tilde{z}_{i}=z(\tilde{p}_{i})
. These formulas define
\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}
,
\Omega_{n}
in a neighborhood of some point on the diagonal. The point is such that these differentials do not depend on a choice of the local coordinate
z
and extend globally to
\Sigma^{2n}
. Moreover, if the system of differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
is KP integrable, then the extended differentials satisfy the following determinantal identities (see
[
ABDBKS25a
]
)

(12)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}(p^{+}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},p^{-}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\det\|K(p^{+}_{i},p^{-}_{j})\|,

(13)

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}(p^{+}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},p^{-}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\det\nolimits^{\circ}\|K(p^{+}_{i},p^{-}_{j})\|,

where
\det\nolimits^{\circ}
is a connected version of determinant. In particular,

(14)

\Omega_{1}^{\bullet}=\Omega_{1}=K.

Notice that the pole on the diagonals
p_{i}^{+}=p_{i}^{-}
cancel out for the connected
n|n
differentials for
n\neq 1
, and we have

(15)

\omega_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{p^{+}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}}\to{p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}}}}\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{p^{-}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}}\to{p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}}}}\Omega_{n}(p^{+}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},p^{-}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}),\quad n\geq 2.

Therefore, the determinantal identities for
\omega_{n}
are specializations of those for
\Omega_{n}
.

It worth to mention that there is a direct combinatorial expression for
\Omega_{n}
in terms of
\omega
-differentials as a sum over certain graphs
[
ABDBKS25a
]
.

2.5.
Expression in terms of theta function

Assume that we are given the following data:

•

a function (a power series)
\theta(w_{1},w_{2},\dots)
with finite or infinite number of arguments such that
\theta(0)\neq 0
;

•

a collection of meromorphic differential
1
-forms
\eta=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots)
on a (not necessarily compact) Riemann surface
\Sigma
;

•

a symmetric bidifferential
B(p_{1},p_{2})
on
\Sigma^{2}
with a pole on the diagonal with biresidue one.

Then, we can define

(24)

\displaystyle\omega_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\eta(p_{i})\partial_{w})\log\theta(w)\bigm|_{w=0}+\delta_{n,2}B(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\frac{\partial^{n}\log\theta(w)}{\partial w_{k_{1}}\dots\partial w_{k_{n}}}\Bigm|_{w=0}\eta_{k_{1}}(p_{1})\dots\eta_{k_{n}}(p_{n})+\delta_{n,2}B(p_{1},p_{2}).

Then, both the series
\tau=\tau_{q,z}
associated with a choice of a regular point
q\in\Sigma
and a local coordinate
z
according to (
6
) and the extended differentials
\Omega_{n}^{\bullet}
for the differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
can be expressed in terms of
\theta
. Explicitly, define the coefficients
a_{i,j}
,
b_{i,j}
by the following expansions at the point
q
:

(25)

\displaystyle\eta_{i}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{i,j}z^{j-1}dz,\quad z=z(p),

(26)

\displaystyle B(p_{1},p_{2})-\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{\infty}b_{i,j}z_{1}^{i-1}dz_{1}z_{2}^{i-1}dz_{2},\quad z_{i}=z(p_{i}).

Then, we can introduce (independently of KP integrability)

(27)

\displaystyle\tau(t)

\displaystyle=e^{Q(t)}\frac{\theta(w)\bigm|_{w_{k}=T_{k}(t)}}{\theta(0)},

\displaystyle T_{k}(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}a_{k,\ell}t_{\ell},

\displaystyle Q(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{\infty}b_{i,j}t_{i}t_{j},

(28)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}(p^{+}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},p^{-}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq k<\ell\leq n}\frac{E(p^{+}_{k},p^{+}_{\ell})E(p^{-}_{k},p^{-}_{\ell})}{E(p^{+}_{k},p^{-}_{\ell})E(p^{-}_{k},p^{+}_{\ell})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{E(p^{+}_{i},p^{-}_{i})}\frac{\theta\bigl(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathcal{A}(p^{+}_{i})-\mathcal{A}(p^{-}_{i}))\bigr)}{\theta(0)}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(p)=\int_{q}^{p}\eta=\Bigl(\int_{q}^{p}\eta_{1},\int_{q}^{p}\eta_{2},\dots\Bigr),

\displaystyle E(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{\sqrt{dz_{1}}\sqrt{dz_{2}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{p_{2}}^{p_{1}}\int\limits_{p_{2}}^{p_{1}}\bigl(B(\tilde{p}_{1},\tilde{p}_{2})-\frac{d\tilde{z}_{1}d\tilde{z}_{2}}{(\tilde{z}_{1}-\tilde{z}_{2})^{2}}\bigr)},\quad z_{i}=z(p_{i}).

In the case when

Another important case is when

2.6.
Proof of Hirota equations

Though equivalence of Hirota equations and determinantal identities can be considered as well known (in particular, under the name of the Wick theorem), we still present here computations deriving Hirota equations from determinantal identities. Variations of these arguments will be used in this paper elsewhere.

Consider a collection of
2m+2n-2
pairwise distinct points on
\Sigma
regular for the kernel
K
:

(29)

Z=(p_{1},\dots,p_{m},\bar{p}_{1},\dots,\bar{p}_{m-1},p^{\prime}_{1},\dots,p^{\prime}_{n-1},\bar{p}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\bar{p}^{\prime}_{n}).

Lemma 2.4
.

For a KP integrable system of differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
the following relations hold

(30)

\sum_{q\in Z}\mathop{\rm res}\limits_{z=q}\Omega^{\bullet}_{m}(p_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket},\bar{p}_{\llbracket{m-1}\rrbracket},z)\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}(p^{\prime}_{\llbracket{n-1}\rrbracket},z,\bar{p}^{\prime}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=0

for all
m,n\geq 1
and for arbitrary collection of points forming
Z
.

Proof.

Applying determinantal identities and the column or row expansions, respectively, of the corresponding determinants we represent the differential under the residue in the form

(31)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}K(p_{i},z)K(z,\bar{p}^{\prime}_{j})\Omega_{i,j}

with
\Omega_{i,j}
independent of
z
. The
(i,j)
th summand has simple poles at
z=p_{i}
and
z=\bar{p}^{\prime}_{j}
, with opposite residues
-K(p_{i},\bar{p}^{\prime}_{j})\Omega_{i,j}
and
K(p_{i},\bar{p}^{\prime}_{j})\Omega_{i,j}
, respectively. Therefore, the contribution of this summand to the total residue in (
30
) vanishes.
∎

Proof of bilinear relations (
7
).

Let
\tau=\tau_{q,z}
be the tau function associated with a point
q\in\Sigma
and a local coordinate
z
at this point.
Applying (
20
) we find

(32)

\Omega^{\bullet}_{m}(p_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket},\bar{p}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket})\bigm|_{p_{m}=q,\bar{p}_{m}=z}=C\,e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t_{k}z^{-k}}\tau(t-[z])\frac{\sqrt{dz}}{z}\bigm|_{t_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(z_{i}^{k}-\bar{z}_{i}^{k})},

where
z_{i}=z(p_{i})
,
\bar{z}_{i}=z(\bar{p}_{i})
, and
C
is the product of linear prefactors in (
20
) not containing
\bar{p}_{m}=z
. A similar expression relating
\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}(p^{\prime}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},\bar{p}^{\prime}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})
and
\tau(t^{\prime}+[z])
holds as well. We see that after a suitable substitution of times the differential under the residue in (
7
) extends as a global differential coinciding with that one of (
30
) up to a factor independent of
z
(in fact, we obtain a limit of (
30
) as
\bar{p}_{m}\to q
,
p^{\prime}_{n}\to q
, that is,
\bar{z}_{m}\to 0
,
z^{\prime}_{n}\to 0
). Let us represent the residue in (
7
) as an integral over a contour surrounding the point
q\in\Sigma
. Then, choosing the coordinates
z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},z^{\prime}_{j},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{j}
of the points
p_{i},\bar{p}_{i},p^{\prime}_{j},\bar{p}^{\prime}_{j}
small enough we may assume that all these points belong to the disk bounded by the contour, and thus (
7
) reduces to (
30
).

Thus, we derived not the original Hirota equations but their specializations under the substitution
t_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(z_{i}^{k}-\bar{z}_{i}^{k})
,
t^{\prime}_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(z^{\prime}_{i}{}^{k}-\bar{z}^{\prime}_{i}{}^{k})
. This substitution is known as passing to the so called Miwa variables. It is representative enough, so that if some relation on tau functions holds in Miwa variables for any
m
,
n
, then it holds for the original times. Therefore, the original Hirota equations hold as well.
∎

2.7.
KP symmetries

In the local setting, KP symmetries act as linear transformations of the Fock space taking KP tau functions to KP tau functions. The group of KP symmetries is quite big: all KP tau functions form a single orbit of this action. It means that any KP tau function can be obtained from any other KP tau function, for example, from that one identically equal to
1
, by the action of a suitable transformation from this group.

The corresponding Lie algebra of infinitesimal KP symmetries is spanned by scalars and by the operators
E_{i,j}
,
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, defined by the following generating series:

(33)

\sum_{i,j=-\infty}^{\infty}z^{j}\bar{z}^{-i-1}E_{i,j}=\frac{e^{\sum\limits_{k<0}\frac{z^{k}-\bar{z}^{k}}{k}J_{k}}e^{\sum\limits_{k>0}\frac{z^{k}-\bar{z}^{k}}{k}J_{k}}-1}{z-\bar{z}}.

In this section we describe the analogue of this Lie algebra of infinitesimal KP symmetries for the setting of global KP integrability. Namely, we describe infinitesimal transformations of a system of differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
that preserve infinitesimally all determinantal identities. Denote

(34)

\mathcal{W}_{n}(q^{+},q^{-};p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{p^{\pm}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}}\to{p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket}}}}\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{p^{\pm}_{n+1}}\to{q^{\pm}}}}\Omega_{n+1}(p^{+}_{\llbracket{n+1}\rrbracket},p^{-}_{\llbracket{n+1}\rrbracket}),

where
\Omega_{n}
is the extended
n|n
halfdifferential associated with
\{\omega_{n}\}
and defined by (
10
)–(
11
). There is also a direct combinatorial formula for these differentials:

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}(q^{+},q^{-};p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\biggl(\int\limits_{q^{-}}^{q^{+}}\biggr)^{k}\left(\omega_{k+n}(\tilde{p}_{\llbracket{k}\rrbracket},p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})-\delta_{n,0}\delta_{k,2}\tfrac{d\tilde{z}_{1}d\tilde{z}_{2}}{(\tilde{z}_{1}-\tilde{z}_{2})^{2}}\right),

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{n}(q^{+},q^{-};p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{dz^{+}}\sqrt{dz^{-}}}{z^{+}-z^{-}}\,e^{\mathcal{T}_{0}(q^{+},q^{-})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\llbracket{n}\rrbracket=\sqcup_{\alpha}J_{\alpha}\\
J_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\prod_{\alpha}\mathcal{T}_{|J_{\alpha}|}(q^{+},q^{-};p_{J_{\alpha}}),

where
z
is an arbitrary local coordinate or just a meromorphic function,
\tilde{z}_{i}=z(\tilde{p}_{i})
, and
z^{\pm}=z(q^{\pm})
(this definition is independent of a choice of
z
).
The integration in the first formula carries over
\tilde{p}_{1},\dots,\tilde{p}_{k}
.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that (
35
) (and, by proxy, (
36
)) contains infinite summation. It is well-defined in a formal expansion near a point on the diagonal, but often can be considered as a globally defined analytic object.

Proposition 2.6
.

The following two-parameter family of infinitesimal transformations

(37)

\Delta\omega_{n}(z_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=\mathcal{W}_{n}(q^{+},q^{-};p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

is an infinitesimal KP symmetry of a system of differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
for any specialization of parameters
(q^{+},q^{-})\in\Sigma^{2}
. More explicitly, assume that the given system of the differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
satisfy determinantal identities (
3
)–(
4
), and let

(38)

\displaystyle K_{\epsilon}(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=K(p_{1},p_{2})+\epsilon\,\Omega_{2}(p_{1},q^{+},p_{2},q^{-})+O(\epsilon^{2})

\displaystyle=K(p_{1},p_{2})-\epsilon\,K(p_{1},q^{-})\,K(q^{+},p_{2})+O(\epsilon^{2}).

Then, we have

(39)

\displaystyle\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{p_{2}}\to{p_{1}}}}\left(K_{\epsilon}(p_{1},p_{2})-\frac{\sqrt{dz_{1}}\sqrt{dz_{2}}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}(p_{1})+\epsilon\,\mathcal{W}_{1}(q^{+},q^{-};p_{1})+O(\epsilon^{2}),\quad z_{i}=z(p_{i}),

(40)

\displaystyle\det\nolimits^{\circ}\|K_{\epsilon}(p_{i},p_{j})\|_{i,j=1,\dots,n}

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})+\epsilon\,\mathcal{W}_{n}(q^{+},q^{-};p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})+O(\epsilon^{2}).

The proof is obtained by a straightforward expansion of the corresponding (connected) determinant.∎

Specializations of parameters
(q^{+},q^{-})
appearing in applications are often of the following sort. Let
\partial_{x}
be a meromorphic vector field on
\Sigma
. We denote by
p\mapsto\mathfrak{g}^{t}_{\partial_{x}}p
the time
t
phase flow of this vector field. Then, we set
q^{\pm}=\mathfrak{g}^{\pm\frac{u\hbar}{2}}_{\partial_{x}}p
, take the coefficient of
u^{k}
for some
k
, multiply by some auxiliary meromorphic function, and integrate the result over some contour, see, e.g.
[
ABDBKS24
]
. In the local description of Sect.
2.4
, the coefficients of the expansion of
\mathcal{W}_{n}
in
q^{\pm}
generate the Lie algebra of symmetries of the KP hierarchy.

2.8.
Multi-KP hierarchy

A system of KP integrable differentials provides solutions not only to the KP hierarchy but also to a more general
N
-KP hierarchy for arbitrary
N\geq 1
. For
N=2
it is known also as Toda lattice hierarchy
[
UT84
]
. In this section we review a construction for such solution. In the setting of Riemann theta function considered in Sect.
2.5
, it coincides with an algebraic-geometrical solution suggested in
[
KZ23
]
. The results of this section are not used in the proof of the main theorems of this paper but rather provide their obvious applications.

An
N
-KP tau function
\tau=\tau(\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{t})
depends on
N
sets of continuous times
\mathbf{t}_{\alpha}=(t_{\alpha,1},t_{\alpha,2},\dots)
,
\alpha=1,\dots,N
, and
N
discrete times
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
satisfying

(41)

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}s_{\alpha}=0.

Equations of the
N
-KP hierarchy can be represented in the form of Hirota-type bilinear relations

(42)

\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{\alpha<\gamma}(s_{\alpha}{-}s^{\prime}_{\alpha})}\mathop{\rm res}\limits_{z=0}\frac{dz}{z^{s_{\gamma}{-}s^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}e^{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(t_{\gamma,k}-t^{\prime}_{\gamma,k})z^{-k}}\tau(\mathbf{s}{-}1_{\gamma}\mid\mathbf{t}{-}[z]_{\gamma})\;\tau(\mathbf{s}^{\prime}{+}1_{\gamma}\mid\mathbf{t}^{\prime}{+}[z]_{\gamma})=0

that hold for arbitrary sets of
t,t^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}
variables satisfying
\sum s_{\alpha}=1
,
\sum s^{\prime}_{\alpha}=-1
.
Here
1_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
is the
k
th basic unit vector, and
\mathbf{t}\pm[z]_{k}
denotes the corresponding shift in the
k
th set of times:

(43)

(\mathbf{t}\pm[z]_{\gamma})_{\alpha,i}=t_{\alpha,i}\pm\delta_{\alpha,\gamma}\frac{z^{i}}{i}.

We provide a construction for a solution of
N
-KP hierarchy associated with a KP integrable system of differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
, an arbitrary generic collection of
N
pairwise distinct points
q_{1},\dots,q_{N}
on
\Sigma
, and a choice of local coordinates
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{N}
at these points. It is convenient to identify the collection of discrete times with a degree zero divisor

(44)

D(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}s_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}\in{\rm div}_{0}\Sigma.

Our goal is to associate a tau function to such divisor. In order to unify notations, we denote this tau function by
\tau_{D}(\mathbf{t})=\tau(\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{t})
,
D=\sum s_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}
.
For the first step we construct a
D
-modified kernel
K_{D}
. In the case when
D=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(q^{\prime}_{i}-q^{\prime\prime}_{i})
for some collection of
2m
pairwise distinct points
q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{m},q^{\prime\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime\prime}_{m}
, it is given by an explicit formula

(45)

K_{D}(p,\bar{p})=\frac{\Omega^{\bullet}_{m+1}(p,q^{\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket},\bar{p},q^{\prime\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket})}{\Omega^{\bullet}_{m}(q^{\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket},q^{\prime\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket})},\quad D=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(q^{\prime}_{i}-q^{\prime\prime}_{i}).

Then, it proves out that
K_{D}
admits a natural limit as some of the points
q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime\prime}_{i}
glue together in a family. Moreover, this limit depends neither of the way we split the points of
D\in{\rm div}_{0}\Sigma
into pairs in order to represent it in the form
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(q^{\prime}_{i}-q^{\prime\prime}_{i})
, nor on the number
m
of such pairs. Thus, by continuity, the definition of the kernel
K_{D}
extends to arbitrary divisors of degree zero.

The kernel
K_{D}
defines, in turn, a system of
D
-modified differentials

(46)

\displaystyle\omega_{D,1}(p_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{p_{2}}\to{p_{1}}}}\left(K_{D}(p_{1},p_{2})-\frac{\sqrt{dz_{1}}\sqrt{z_{2}}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\right),\quad z_{i}=z(p_{i}),

\displaystyle\omega_{D,n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\det\nolimits^{\circ}\|K_{D}(p_{i},p_{j})\|_{i,j=1,\dots,n}.

In the case
D=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(q^{\prime}_{i}-q^{\prime\prime}_{i})
with pairwise distinct
q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime\prime}_{i}
, the corresponding
n|n
differentials are given by

(47)

\Omega^{\bullet}_{D,n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},\bar{p}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=\frac{\Omega^{\bullet}_{m+n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},q^{\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket},\bar{p}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket},q^{\prime\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket})}{\Omega^{\bullet}_{m}(q^{\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket},q^{\prime\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket})},\quad D=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(q^{\prime}_{i}-q^{\prime\prime}_{i}).

The kernel
K_{D}
has poles at the points of the divisor
D
. This implies that
\omega_{D,1}
gets also poles at the corresponding points. Namely,
\omega_{D,1}
has simple poles at
q_{1},\dots,q_{N}
with residues
s_{1},\dots,s_{N}
, respectively. However, these poles cancel out for the higher differentials
\omega_{D,n}
,
n\geq 2
, which means that all these differentials are, in fact, holomorphic for
n\geq 2
except a standard pole of
\omega_{D,2}
on the diagonal.

The Taylor coefficients of the logarithm of
\tau_{D}
are defined from the power expansion of the differentials
\omega_{D,n}
at suitable points. Explicitly, for
n\geq 1
and an arbitrary collection of indices
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
,
\alpha_{i}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, expanding
\omega_{D,n}
at the point
(q_{\alpha_{1}},\dots,q_{\alpha_{n}})\in\Sigma^{n}
in the local coordinates
\zeta_{\alpha_{1}},\dots,\zeta_{\alpha_{n}}
, respectively, we set

(48)

\frac{\partial^{n}\log\tau_{D}}{\partial t_{\alpha_{1},k_{1}}\dots\partial t_{\alpha_{1},k_{1}}}\Bigm|_{t=0}=[z_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots z_{n}^{k_{n}-1}]\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{D,n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}dz_{i}},\qquad z_{i}=\zeta_{\alpha_{i}}(p_{i}),\quad p_{i}\to q_{\alpha_{i}},

where

(49)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{D,1}(p)

\displaystyle=\omega_{D,1}(p)-s_{\alpha}\frac{dz}{z},\quad\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha),\quad z=\zeta_{\alpha}(p),

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{D,2}(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=\omega_{D,2}(p_{1},p_{2})-\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}},\quad\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha,\alpha),\quad z_{i}=\zeta_{\alpha}(p_{i}),

and
\tilde{\omega}_{D,n}=\omega_{D,n}
in all other cases (including the case when
n=2
and
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
with
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}
). By construction,
\tilde{\omega}_{D,n}
is indeed regular at the considered point and expands as a power series.

This relation defines
\tau_{D}
as a formal power series uniquely up to a constant factor
C_{D}=\tau_{D}(0)
. A correct account of this factor is quite sensitive for validity of Hirota equations. It is defined as follows.

Let
U_{\alpha}
be a small neighborhood of the point
q_{\alpha}
where the local coordinate
\zeta_{\alpha}
is defined. For any divisor
\widetilde{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(q^{\prime}_{i}-q^{\prime\prime}_{i})
with pairwise distinct
q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime\prime}_{i}
and supported by
\cup_{\alpha=1}^{N}U_{\alpha}
we define

(50)

C_{\widetilde{D}}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{\epsilon(q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime\prime}_{j})\epsilon(q^{\prime\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}_{j})}{\epsilon(q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}_{j})\epsilon(q^{\prime\prime}_{i},q^{\prime\prime}_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\epsilon(q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime\prime}_{i})~\Omega_{m}(q^{\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket},q^{\prime\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket}),

where for
p\in U_{\alpha}
,
\bar{p}\in U_{\beta}
, we define
\epsilon(p,\bar{p})
from relation

(51)

\epsilon(p,\bar{p})\sqrt{d\zeta_{\alpha}(p)}\sqrt{d\zeta_{\beta}(\bar{p})}=\begin{cases}\phantom{-}1,&\alpha<\beta,\\
-1,&\alpha>\beta,\\
\zeta_{\alpha}(p)-\zeta_{\alpha}(\bar{p})&\alpha=\beta.\end{cases}

The constant
C_{\widetilde{D}}
does not depend on the way we split the points of
\widetilde{D}
into pairs to represent it in the form
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(q^{\prime}_{i}-q^{\prime\prime}_{i})
and extends by continuity to arbitrary degree 0 divisors supported by
\cup_{\alpha=1}^{N}U_{\alpha}
, in particular to the case of the divisor
D=\sum s_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}
. This completes the definition of the function
\tau_{D}(\mathbf{t})=\tau(\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{t})
.

Theorem 2.7
.

The function
\tau_{D}(\mathbf{t})=\tau(\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{t})
is an
N
-KP tau function, that is, it satisfies the
N
-KP bilinear relations.

Proof.

Following the arguments in the proof of Hirota equations of Sect.
2.6
, let us represent the sum of residues in (
42
) as a single integral along a contour which is a boundary of the union of
N
small disks centered at
q_{1},\dots,q_{N}
. Apply the Miwa substitution
t_{\alpha,k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\alpha}}(z_{\alpha,i}^{k}-\bar{z}_{\alpha_{i}}^{k})
,
t^{\prime}_{\alpha,k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\alpha}}(z^{\prime}_{\alpha,i}{}^{k}-\bar{z}^{\prime}_{\alpha,i}{}^{k})
, to the times in this relation, and also apply small deformations of the divisors
D=\sum s_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}
and
D^{\prime}=\sum s^{\prime}_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}
to represent the deformed divisors
\widetilde{D}
and
\widetilde{D}^{\prime}
in the form
\widetilde{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{0}}(\tilde{q}_{i}-\bar{\tilde{q}}_{i})+p_{m}
,
\widetilde{D}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{0}}(\tilde{q}^{\prime}_{i}-\bar{\tilde{q}}^{\prime}_{i})-\bar{p}^{\prime}_{n}
, respectively. Then, the desired Eq. (
42
) takes the form

(52)

\oint\Omega^{\bullet}_{m}(p_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket},\bar{p}_{\llbracket{m-1}\rrbracket},z)\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}(p^{\prime}_{\llbracket{n-1}\rrbracket},z,\bar{p}^{\prime}_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=0,

where
m=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{N}m_{\alpha}+1
,

, and where the arguments of
\Omega^{\bullet}_{m}
and
\Omega^{\bullet}_{n}
include the points representing the Miwa variables and also the points of the divisors
\tilde{D}
and
\tilde{D}^{\prime}
. These arguments are points of
\Sigma
distributed somehow among the disks centered at
q_{1},\dots,q_{N}
. Moreover, we may assume that all these points are fixed except the one denoted by

which runs over the union of these disks. Assuming determinantal identities we obtain (

and

∎

3.
Convolution of two systems of differentials

3.1.
Definition of convolved differentials

Let
\Sigma
be a possibly noncompact Riemann surface (complex curve) and
\mathcal{P}=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{N}\}\subset\Sigma
be a finite set. For two meromorphic
1
-forms
\psi
and
\phi
such that
\psi
possibly has poles and
\phi
is holomorphic at
\mathcal{P}
we define

(59)

\displaystyle\psi*\phi\coloneqq\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}}\mathop{\rm res}_{p=q}\psi(p)\int_{q}^{p}\phi.

Now consider two systems of symmetric differentials
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
on
\Sigma
. We assume that both
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
expand as a series in
\hbar
,
\tilde{\psi}_{n}=\sum_{d}\hbar^{d}\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{n}=\sum_{d}\hbar^{d}\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
, whose coefficients
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
are meromorphic and regular on diagonals. We use the tilde symbol to emphasize that regularity on diagonals is satisfied for all
n
including
n=2
. Later on we will modify
\tilde{\psi}_{2}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{2}
by introducing a pole on the diagonal for these differentials.

We also assume that the only poles that
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
may possess are those of the form
p_{i}=q_{j}
where
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
are the arguments of
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
and
q_{j}
is a point of
\mathcal{P}
. Assume also that
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
are regular on
\mathcal{P}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A system of differentials
\tilde{\omega}_{n}
of convolution of the differentials
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is defined as follows.

•

For
n\geq 1
define a set
G_{n}
of graphs
\Gamma
with some additional structure and properties:

–

The set of vertices
V(\Gamma)
splits into three subsets
V(\Gamma)=V_{\ell}(\Gamma)\sqcup V_{\psi}(\Gamma)\sqcup V_{\phi}(\Gamma)
, which we call leaves,
\psi
-vertices and
\phi
-vertices, respectively.

–

The set
V_{\ell}(\Gamma)
consists of
n
ordered leaves
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n}
, that is, there is a fixed bijection
\llbracket{n}\rrbracket\to V_{\ell}(\Gamma)
such that
i\mapsto\ell_{i}
.

–

Each edge in the set of edges
E(\Gamma)
either connects a leaf to a
\psi
- or a
\phi
-vertex (such edges are called
external
), or connects a
\psi
-vertex to a
\phi
-vertex (such edges are called
internal
). Accordingly, the set
E(\Gamma)
splits as
E(\Gamma)=E_{\ell}(\Gamma)\sqcup E_{{\psi}-\phi}(\Gamma)
.

–

There is exactly one edge attached to each leaf
\ell_{i}
.

–

The graph
\Gamma
is connected.

Here is an example of such graph; the
\psi
- and
\phi
-vertices are colored in white, resp., black; the internal edges are marked by a star:

•

To each graph
\Gamma\in G_{n}
we associate its weight
\mathsf{w}(\Gamma)
defined as follows.

–

A
\psi
-vertex (resp.,
\phi
-vertex)
v
of
\Gamma
with
m

\mathsf{w}(v)\coloneqq\tilde{\psi}_{m}
(resp.,
\mathsf{w}(v)\coloneqq\tilde{\phi}_{m}
), and the arguments of
\mathsf{w}(v)
are put in bijection with the edges attached to
v
.

–

The edge
e\in E_{\ell}(\Gamma)

is decorated by the operator
O(e)\coloneqq\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{}\to{p_{i}}}}
.

–

Each edge

–

The weight

is defined as

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}(\Gamma)\coloneqq\prod_{e\in E(\Gamma)}O(e)\bigg(\prod_{v\in V_{\psi}(\Gamma)}\mathsf{w}(v)\prod_{v\in V_{\phi}(\Gamma)}\mathsf{w}(v)\bigg),

where the action of the operators associated to the edges on the decorations of the vertices is prescribed by the graph
\Gamma
.

The convolved differentials
\tilde{\omega}_{n}
are defined as

(61)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{n}=\sum_{\Gamma\in G_{n}}\frac{\mathsf{w}(\Gamma)}{|\Aut(\Gamma)|},

where
|\Aut(\Gamma)|
denotes the order of the automorphisms group of a graph
\Gamma
that preserves all markings of vertices.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that with this definition
\tilde{\omega}_{n}
are given by an infinite summation over graphs. For the correctness of this definition we should impose some additional assumptions on
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
assuring that the number of graphs providing a nontrivial contribution to each
\tilde{\omega}^{\langle d\rangle}_{n}
in
\tilde{\omega}_{n}=\sum_{d}\hbar^{d}\omega^{\langle d\rangle}_{n}
is finite. For instance, it is sufficient to assume that the expansion of one of the two systems of differentials, say,
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
, in
\hbar
does not contain terms with
\hbar^{\leq 0}
, and the expansion of the other one, say,
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
, is regular in
\hbar
.

In particular, these expansions can be topological with extra vanishing of unstable terms. We remind the reader that, by definition, the expansion in
\hbar
of a system of differentials, say
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
, is called
topological
if
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}\not=0
only for
d=2g-2+n
where
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. By the
unstable terms
of a topological expansion we mean
\tilde{\psi}^{\langle 0\rangle}_{1}
and
\tilde{\psi}^{\langle 0\rangle}_{2}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Assume that the expansions of both
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
in
\hbar
are topological and both
\tilde{\psi}^{\langle 0\rangle}_{1}
and
\tilde{\phi}^{\langle 0\rangle}_{1}
vanish, as well as at least one of two differentials
\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{\langle 0\rangle}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{2}^{\langle 0\rangle}
. Then the convolution differentials
\tilde{\omega}_{n}
are well defined and their expansion in
\hbar
is topological as well.

Remark 3.4
.

If the convolution of all differentials of two collections vanishes, that is,
\psi_{m}*\phi_{n}=0
for all
m,n\geq 0
, then the system of differentials
\tilde{\omega}_{n}
of convolution of the differentials
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
is nothing but their sum,

(62)

\ \tilde{\omega}_{n}=\tilde{\psi}_{n}+\tilde{\phi}_{n}.

3.2.
Expression in terms of potentials

Assume that there is a sequence (finite or infinite) of meromorphic
1
-differentials
d\xi_{1},d\xi_{2},\dots
such that for all
(d,n)
the differential
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
admits an expansion of the form

(63)

\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}c^{\langle d\rangle}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}d\xi_{k_{1}}(p_{1})\dots d\xi_{k_{n}}(p_{n})

with some numerical coefficients
c^{\langle d\rangle}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}
and with
finitely many nonzero terms
for any fixed
(d,n)
. Then, all information on these coefficients, and hence, the differentials
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
can be encoded in a single generating series
F(t_{1},t_{2},\dots;\hbar)
called
potential
(cf. (
24
))

(64)

F(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{d}c^{\langle d\rangle}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}t_{k_{1}}\dots t_{k_{n}}.

In the setting of Definition
3.1
, assume that both systems of differentials
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
correspond to some potentials, that is, they are given by

(65)

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{t}\to{0}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl({\textstyle\sum_{k}}d\xi_{k}(p_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\Bigr)F^{\psi}(t),

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{s}\to{0}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl({\textstyle\sum_{\ell}}d\eta_{\ell}(p_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{\ell}}\Bigr)F^{\phi}(s),

for two power series
F^{\psi}(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
,
F^{\phi}(s_{1},s_{2},\dots)
and two sequences of
1
-differentials
(d\xi_{1},d\xi_{2},\dots)
,
(d\eta_{1},d\eta_{2},\dots)
. By assumption, the differentials
d\xi_{k}
may have poles at the points of
\mathcal{P}
while
d\eta_{\ell}
are holomorphic at these points.

Proposition 3.5
.

If both
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
admit expansions in terms of potentials, then, the corresponding convolution differentials
\tilde{\omega}_{n}
also admit an expansion in terms of a potential,

(66)

\tilde{\omega}_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{t,s}\to{0}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl({\textstyle\sum_{k}}d\xi_{k}(p_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}+{\textstyle\sum_{\ell}}d\eta_{\ell}(p_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{\ell}}\Bigr)F^{\omega}(t,s),

and the potential
F^{\omega}
is given explicitly by a Moyal-type product

(67)

\displaystyle e^{F^{\omega}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=e^{\sum_{k,\ell}b_{k,\ell}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{k}\partial s_{\ell}}}e^{F^{\psi}(t)}e^{F^{\phi}(s)},

\displaystyle b_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=d\xi_{k}*d\eta_{\ell}=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}}\mathop{\rm res}\limits_{p=q}d\xi_{k}(p)\int_{q}^{p}d\eta_{\ell}.

3.3.
Duality in the global setting

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that
\Sigma
is compact, the only possible poles of the differentials
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
are those of the form
p_{i}=q
,
q\in\mathcal{P}
, while the only possible poles of the differentials
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
are those of the form
p_{i}=q
,
q\in\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
, where
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
are the arguments of
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
or
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
, respectively, and
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
are two nonintersecting finite sets. Let residues and the
\mathfrak{A}
-periods of these differentials vanish for all
n
,

(68)

\mathop{\rm res}\limits_{p=q}\tilde{\psi}_{n}(p,p_{\llbracket{n-1}\rrbracket})=0,\quad q\in\mathcal{P},\quad\quad\mathop{\rm res}\limits_{p=q}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(p,p_{\llbracket{n-1}\rrbracket})=0,\quad q\in\mathcal{P}^{\vee},

and

(69)

\oint_{p\in\mathfrak{A}_{i}}\tilde{\psi}_{n}(p,p_{\llbracket{n-1}\rrbracket})=0,\quad\oint_{p\in\mathfrak{A}_{i}}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(p,p_{\llbracket{n-1}\rrbracket})=0,\quad i=1,\dots,g(\Sigma),

where
g(\Sigma)
is the genus of the curve
\Sigma
.

Then, the convolution differentials of two systems of differentials
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
coincide with those of the systems
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
and
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
(with the role of
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
swapped).

Proof.

Indeed, for two global differential
1
-forms
\psi
and
\phi
with possible poles at
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
, respectively, we have

(70)

\psi*\phi=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}}\mathop{\rm res}\limits_{p=q}\psi(p)\int_{q}^{p}\phi=-\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}}\mathop{\rm res}\limits_{p=q}\phi(p)\int_{q}^{p}\psi=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}^{\vee}}\mathop{\rm res}\limits_{p=q}\phi(p)\int_{q}^{p}\psi=\phi*\psi.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

Note that the
*
-operation on the left hand side and the right hand side of Equation (
70
) has different meaning: on the left hand side it is taken with respect to the set
\mathcal{P}
, and on the right hand side it is taken with respect to the set
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
.

3.4.
Conservation of KP integrability

Let
B_{0}
be a symmetric bidifferential of the following special form

(71)

B_{0}(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{dz(p_{1})dz(p_{2})}{(z(p_{1})-z(p_{2}))^{2}},

where
z
is a fixed meromorphic function on
\Sigma
. Set

(72)

\psi_{n}=\tilde{\psi}_{n}+\delta_{n,2}B_{0},\quad\phi_{n}=\tilde{\phi}_{n}+\delta_{n,2}B_{0},\quad\omega_{n}=\tilde{\omega}_{n}+\delta_{n,2}B_{0},

where the differentials
\{\tilde{\omega}_{n}\}
are obtained by convolution of the systems of differentials
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
.

Note that the function
z
defines a local embedding of
\Sigma
into
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. More precisely, we replace the curve
\Sigma
by its open subset
U
(with a smooth boundary
\partial U
) containing
\mathcal{P}
and assume that
z
used in the definition of
B_{0}
defines an embedding of
U
into
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
.
Assume that
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
extends via this embedding to the whole
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
regularly outside the image of
U
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
is regular on
U
.

Theorem 3.8
.

If the systems of differentials
\{\psi_{n}\}
and
\{\phi_{n}\}
are KP integrable and the assumptions on the regularity of
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
outside
U
(on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
) and of
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
inside
U
are satisfied, then the system of differentials
\{\omega_{n}\}
is also KP integrable.

3.5.
Proof of Theorem
3.8

Let
\oint
denote the sum of residues at the points of
\mathcal{P}
.

The proof of Theorem
3.8
is based on two fundamental properties of the convolution formula. In a nutshell, we have an interplay of the following two principles:

•

Infinitesimal deformations of
\{\psi_{n}\}
and
\{\phi_{n}\}
that preserve KP integrability imply infinitesimal deformations of
\{\omega_{n}\}
that preserve KP integrability;

•

It is possible to fully trivialize
\{\psi_{n}\}
or
\{\phi_{n}\}
with the help of deformations that preserve KP integrability. If
\{\psi_{n}\}
(resp.,
\{\phi_{n}\}
) is trivialized, that is,
\psi_{n}=\delta_{n,2}B_{0}
(resp.,
\phi_{n}=\delta_{n,2}B_{0}
), then
\{\omega_{n}\}=\{\phi_{n}\}
(resp.,
\{\omega_{n}\}=\{\psi_{n}\}
).

Once we formulate and prove the propositions realizing these two principles (see Proposition
3.11
and Proposition
3.13
below, respectively), we are armed to complete the proof of Theorem
3.8
.

Remark 3.9
.

Note that though
\psi

\phi
-differentials play a bit different roles, they still enter the convolution formula for
\{\tilde{\omega}_{n}\}
in a quite symmetric way, cf. also Proposition

\phi

3.5.2.
Deformations preserving KP integrability

Here we discuss the second principle mentioned above:

Proposition 3.13
.

By specializations of the deformations given by

(99)

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(\epsilon;p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=\phi_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})+\epsilon\mathcal{W}^{\phi}_{n}(p^{+},p^{-},p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})+O(\epsilon^{2})

we span the full tangent space to the space of KP integrable differentials holomorphic on

(outside the diagonal of

for

must have a double pole with bi-residue

Proof.

It is essentially a revised version of the statement of Proposition
2.6
from a different perspective. Indeed, every system of KP integrable differentials
\{\phi_{n}\}
such that

are holomorphic on

(we call them “regular” KP integrable differentials) can be represented via determinantal formulas (
4
) in terms of a kernel

such that

is holomorphic on

(we call such kernels “regular” kernels). Hence, the determinantal formulas induce an epimorphism from the tangent space to the space of “regular” kernels to the tangent space of the space of “regular” KP integrable differentials.

Consider an arbitrary deformation of a “regular”

given by
K(\epsilon;z_{1},z_{2})=K(z_{1},z_{2})+\epsilon\Delta K(z_{1},z_{2})+O(\epsilon^{2})

\displaystyle\Delta K(z_{1},z_{2})=-\oint_{p_{1}^{\prime}}\oint_{p_{2}^{\prime}}\Delta K(z(p_{1}^{\prime}),z(p_{2}^{\prime}))K(z_{1},z(p_{1}^{\prime}))K(z(p_{2}^{\prime}),z_{2})

implies that the induced deformation of the differentials given by the determinantal formulas is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(\epsilon;p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})=\phi_{n}(p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})+\epsilon\oint_{p^{\prime}_{1}}\oint_{p^{\prime}_{2}}\Delta K(z(p^{\prime}_{1}),z(p^{\prime}_{2}))\mathcal{W}^{\phi}_{n}(p^{\prime}_{1},p^{\prime}_{2};p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})+O(\epsilon^{2}),

\displaystyle K(t;z_{1},z_{2})\coloneqq(1-t)K(z_{1},z_{2})+t\tfrac{\sqrt{dz_{1}}\sqrt{dz_{2}}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}

Final steps of the proof

Proof of Theorem

.

4.
Blobbed topological recursion

4.1.
Generalized topological recursion

Topological recursion of Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin
[
CEO06
,
EO07
]
associates a system of meromorphic differentials
\omega^{(g)}_{n}
,
g\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
,
2g-2+n\geq 0
, to an input data that consists of a Riemann surface
\Sigma
, a finite set of points
\mathcal{P}\subset\Sigma
, two meromorphic functions
x
and
y
on
\Sigma
such that the zeroes
\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{}\to{q}}}dx=0
are simple and
\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{}\to{q}}}{dy}\not=0
for each
q\in\mathcal{P}
, and a bi-differential
B
with the double pole on the diagonal with bi-residue
1
.
It has its origin in the computation of the cumulants of the matrix models, and by now it has multiple striking applications in algebraic geometry, enumerative combinatorics, and mathematical physics.

The original setup was generalized in
[
ABDBKS25
]
to the situation when
dx
and
dy
are meromorphic differentials with arbitrary behavior at the points of
\mathcal{P}
. We review here these definitions in the most general setting of local generalized topological recursion.

Definition 4.1
.

The initial data of the generalized topological recursion is a quintuple
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P},B)
where

•

\Sigma
is a (not necessarily compact) smooth Riemann surface (a complex curve);

•

B
is a symmetric meromorphic bidifferential on
\Sigma^{2}
with a pole on the diagonal with biresidue
1
and holomorphic in the complement to the diagonal;

•

\mathcal{P}\subset\Sigma
is a finite set; its elements are called
key points
;

•

dx
and
dy
are meromorphic
1
-forms defined as germs (or even formal Laurent expansions) at the points of
\mathcal{P}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that we do not assume that
dx
or
dy
extend globally to
\Sigma
, but we require that
B
is globally defined. On the other hand, we can assume that
\Sigma
is a union of small disks centered at the key points. In this case
B
is also defined locally near the key points only.

Denote by
p\mapsto\mathfrak{g}^{t}_{\partial_{x}}p
the time
t
phase flow of the vector field
\partial_{x}
dual to the differential
dx
. Notice that for any meromorphic function
f
we have

(103)

f(\mathfrak{g}^{t}_{\partial_{x}}p)=e^{t\partial_{x}}f(p),\qquad x(\mathfrak{g}^{t}_{\partial_{x}}p)=x(p)+t.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that a system of differentials

(104)

\omega_{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\geq 0\\
(g,n)\neq(0,1)\end{subarray}}\hbar^{2g-2+n}\omega^{(g)}_{n},\qquad n\geq 1,

satisfies generalized topological recursion for the input data
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P},B)
if

\omega^{(0)}_{2}=B
and the following two conditions hold:

•

Generalized loop equations
: the differential

(105)

\sum_{r\geq 0}\bigl(-d\tfrac{1}{dy}\bigr)^{r}[u^{r}]e^{u(\mathcal{S}(u\hbar\partial_{x})-1)y}\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{q^{\pm}}\to{\mathfrak{g}^{\pm\frac{u\hbar}{2}}_{\partial_{x}}p}}}\mathcal{W}_{n}(q^{+},q^{-};p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})

is holomorphic in
p
at any key point
q\in\mathcal{P}
for all
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
, where
x=x(p)
, and
y=y(p)
,
u
is a new formal variable, and the extended differentials
\mathcal{W}_{n}
are defined by (
34
)–(
36
).

•

Projection property
: we have

(106)

\omega^{(g)}_{n}(p,p_{\llbracket{n-1}\rrbracket})=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{P}}\mathop{\rm res}_{\tilde{p}=q}\omega^{(g)}_{n}(\tilde{p},p_{\llbracket{n-1}\rrbracket})\int_{q}^{\tilde{p}}B(\cdot,p),\qquad 2g-2+n>0.

Remark 4.4
.

These two conditions indeed allow to reconstruct the differentials
\omega^{(g)}_{n}
recursively. Namely, the loop equations determine uniquely the principal parts of the poles of
\omega^{(g)}_{n}
at the key points with respect to the first argument inductively from the known differentials
\omega^{(g^{\prime})}_{n^{\prime}}
with
2g^{\prime}-2+n^{\prime}<2g-2+n
, while the projection property recovers
\omega^{(g)}_{n}
from its polar parts.

Remark 4.5
.

The resulting differentials
\omega^{(g)}_{n}
are symmetric, meromorphic, and the only poles they may have are the key points in each argument.

In the case when the spectral curve data is nondegenerate, that is,
dx
has simple zeroes and
dy
is holomorphic and nonvanishing at the key points, the differentials of the generalized topological recursion coincide with those of usual CEO recursion.

Remark 4.6
.

It is sometimes convenient to define
\omega^{(0)}_{1}=y\,dx
, for some
y
defined as a local primitive of
dy
. However, this differential is not always globally defined and univalued. Therefore, we prefer not to include the
(0,1)
-term to
\omega_{1}
, by convention. Instead, the contribution of the differentials
dx
and
dy
to the generalized loop equations (and, as a consequence, to the relations of the recursion) is included explicitly in (
105
).

On the other hand, the unstable term
\omega^{(0)}_{2}=B
is included to
\omega_{2}
. Notice that the projection property is not satisfied for
(g,n)=(0,2)
: the right hand side of (
106
) gives zero in this case.

Remark 4.7
.

Let us write at a key point
q\in\mathcal{P}
in some local coordinate
z
centered at
q

(107)

dx=a\,z^{r-1}dz\,(1+O(z)),\qquad dy=b\,z^{s-1}dz\,(1+O(z)),\qquad a\,b\neq 0.

The point
q
is called
special
if
r+s>0
and
(r,s)\neq(1,1)
, and
non-special
otherwise. Then the non-special key points do not contribute to the topological recursion and they can be excluded from
\mathcal{P}
without changing the differentials of topological recursion. Thus, one can assume without loss of generality that all key points are special.

4.2.
Changing the set of key points

Assume that the set
\mathcal{P}
of key points in the initial data of topological recursion splits as
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
. Denote by
\psi_{n}
,
\phi_{n}
and
\omega_{n}
the differentials of (generalized) topological recursion with the spectral curve data
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P}^{\prime},B)
,
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},B)
, and
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P},B)
, respectively, that is, we use the same differentials
dx,dy,B
but different sets of key points, see
[
ABDBKS25
]
for details.

Proposition 4.8
.

Assume that the curve
\Sigma
is compact and
B
is the standard Bergman kernel.
The differentials
\tilde{\omega}_{n}=\omega_{n}-\delta_{n,2}B
coincide with the convolution differentials of the systems of differentials
\tilde{\psi}_{n}=\psi_{n}-\delta_{n,2}B
and
\tilde{\phi}_{n}=\phi_{n}-\delta_{n,2}B
.

This proposition means that for computing differentials of topological recursion it is sufficient to apply recursion itself in those cases with a single key point only. Then the TR differentials for the case of several key points are expressed in terms of those with one key point by explicit combinatorial expressions.

For the reformulation in terms of potential given in Sec.
3.2
, it means that the potential for the generalized TR can be given as a Moyal-type product of the potentials associated with the key points. This is a reformulation of the Givental decomposition, there the diagonal part of the Givental operators is included into the definitions of the elementary potentials, and only the non-diagonal part describes interaction between different key points. Moreover, here we naturally can consider types of decompositions associated with different collections of the key points, which is close to the observations of
[
AMM07
,
AMM09
]
in the context of matrix models and Virasoro constraints.

Proof.

Note that the projection property for
\{\omega_{n}\}
follows manifestly from the projection properties for
\{\psi_{n}\}
and
\{\phi_{n}\}
, and the structure of the convolution formula.

In order to prove the generalized loop equations at
q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
we use the same trick as in the proof of Proposition
3.11
. Namely, we notice that
\mathcal{W}^{\omega}_{n}(p^{+},p^{-};p_{\llbracket{n}\rrbracket})
can be represented as a linear combination of operators applied to

\{\mathcal{W}^{\phi}_{m}(p^{+},p^{-};p^{\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket})\}
that act only in the variables associated to
p^{\prime}_{1},p^{\prime}_{2},\dots
. Hence, the expression (
105
) in the generalized loop equations for
\{\omega_{n}\}
is represented as a linear combination of operators applied to

(108)

\sum_{r\geq 0}\bigl(-d\tfrac{1}{dy}\bigr)^{r}[u^{r}]e^{u(\mathcal{S}(u\hbar\partial_{x})-1)y}\mathop{\big\lfloor_{{q^{\pm}}\to{\mathfrak{g}^{\pm\frac{u\hbar}{2}}_{\partial_{x}}p}}}\mathcal{W}^{\phi}_{n}(q^{+},q^{-};p^{\prime}_{\llbracket{m}\rrbracket})

that act only on
p^{\prime}_{1},p^{\prime}_{2},\dots,p^{\prime}_{m}
. Note that these expressions are finite for each fixed
n
and degree of
\hbar
(cf. Remark
3.3
). Thus, the generalized loop equations for
\{\phi_{n}\}
imply the generalized loop equations for
\{\omega_{n}\}
at the points
q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
.

In order to deal with the points
q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
we note that the combinatorics of graphs in the argument of Proposition
3.11
works also with the interchanged roles of the differentials
\{\psi_{n}\}
and
\{\phi_{n}\}
. To this end, we recall Proposition
3.6
— we have a global setup, and the residues of
\psi_{n}
and
\phi_{n}
indeed vanish, since these differential are obtained by topological recursion, so this proposition is applicable. Thus we can change the convolution operation on the edges to use the residues at the points of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
. Hence, the argument above with the roles of
\{\psi_{n}\}
and
\{\phi_{n}\}
interchanged proves the generalized loop equations for
\{\omega_{n}\}
at the points
q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

It is important to stress that Proposition
4.8
does not necessarily hold for a non-compact spectral curve. An important requirement is that we can change the convolution operation from the residues at the points of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
to the points of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}
, and this requires that the projection formula generates no singularities of the analytic extension of the involved differentials. In short, we can apply Proposition
4.8
with locally defined
dx
and
dy
once we know that
B
extends to the standard Bergman kernel on a global curve.

4.3.
Definition of the blobbed topological recursion

We are in the setting of Section
4.1
, that is,
\Sigma
is a possibly noncompact Riemann surface and
\mathcal{P}\subset\Sigma
is a finite set.

Generalizing the situation of Proposition
4.8
, consider an arbitrary system of symmetric differentials
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
called
blobs
, where
\phi_{n}
expands as a formal power series in
\hbar
,
\phi_{n}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{d}\phi_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
. We assume that all
\phi_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
are holomorphic on
\Sigma^{n}
except for
\phi_{2}^{\langle 0\rangle}
, whose only singularity is the double pole on the diagonal with bi-residue
1
.

Definition 4.10
.

We associate to the input data
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P},\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
a system of meromorphic differentials
\{\omega^{\langle d\rangle}_{n}\}
,
d\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
, which we call the
blobbed TR differentials
, defined as follows.

•

First, we choose an arbitrary Bergman kernel
B
on
\Sigma^{2}
. Let
\{\psi^{B,(g)}_{n}\}
,
g\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
,
2g-2+n\geq 0
, be the system of differentials of generalized topological recursion associated to the input data
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P},B)
. Denote

(109)

\displaystyle\psi^{B}_{n}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\geq 0\\
(g,n)\neq(0,1)\end{subarray}}\hbar^{2g-2+n}\psi^{B,(g)}_{n}.

•

Second, set

(110)

\tilde{\psi}^{B}_{n}=\psi^{B}_{n}-\delta_{n,2}B,\qquad\tilde{\phi}_{n}=\phi_{n}-\delta_{n,2}B.

•

Denote by
\{\tilde{\omega}_{n}\}
the convolution differentials of the systems of differentials
\{\tilde{\psi}^{B}_{n}\}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
.
The blobbed TR differentials
\omega_{n}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{d}\omega_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
are defined as

(111)

\displaystyle\omega_{n}=\tilde{\omega}_{n}+\delta_{n,2}B.

Remark 4.11
.

Note that with this definition the expansions of
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{B}
in
\hbar
contain no
\hbar^{0}
terms, and hence,
\omega_{n}
are well defined: there are finitely many graphs of Definition
3.1
contributing to each particular
\omega_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}
.

Remark 4.12
.

Note also that
\omega^{\langle 0\rangle}_{1}=\phi^{\langle 0\rangle}_{1}
and
\omega^{\langle 0\rangle}_{2}=\phi^{\langle 0\rangle}_{2}
.

Remark 4.13
.

Assume that
\phi_{n}=\delta_{n,2}B
. Then
\omega_{n}=\psi^{B}_{n}
are differentials of the usual (generalized) topological recursion.

Remark 4.14
.

Assume that the expansions of
\phi_{n}
in
\hbar
are topological, that is,
\phi_{n}^{\langle d\rangle}\not=0
only if
d=2g-2+n
for some
g\geq 0
. Then the expansion of
\omega_{n}
in
\hbar
is topological as well. Exactly this situation was treated in details in
[
BS17
]
(in the setting of the original CEO topological recursion).

Example 4.15
.

For arbitrary spectral curve data
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P},B)
of topological recursion and a subset
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{P}
, the differentials
\omega_{n}
of topological recursion with this spectral curve data can be regarded as differentials of blobbed topological recursion with the initial data
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\{\phi_{n}\})
with a smaller set
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of key points and the differentials
\phi_{n}
of topological recursion with the spectral curve data
(\Sigma,dx,dy,\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{P}^{\prime},B)
regarded as blobs. This assertion is a reformulation of Proposition
4.8
.

4.4.
Independence of a choice of
B

Lemma 4.16
.

The blobbed differentials
\omega^{\langle d\rangle}_{n}
are symmetric meromorphic differentials that do not depend on the particular choice of
B
.

Example 4.17
.

A special case of this lemma was observed in
[
BS17
, Section 5]
. Namely, assume that we have two possible choices for the Bergman kernel,
B_{0}
and
B
. Then, the differentials of topological recursion computed with respect to the kernel
B
(with the trivial blobs) coincide with the differentials of blobbed topological recursion associated with the differential
B_{0}
as the Bergman kernel and the differentials
\tilde{\phi}_{n}=\delta_{n,2}(B-B_{0})
as the blobs.

The fact that
\{\omega_{n}\}
are symmetric and meromorphic follows directly from the definition of the convolution of the systems of differentials. The proof that they do not depend on the choice of
B
is given in the next Section.

Proposition 4.19
.

Proof.

4.6.
KP integrability

Theorem 4.20
.

Assume that the system of blobs
\{\phi_{n}\}
in the initial data of blobbed topological recursion is KP integrable. Then the system of differentials of the corresponding blobbed topological recursion is also KP integrable.

This theorem generalizes and unifies the KP integrability of the non-perturbative tau function conjectured in
[
BE12
]
and proved in
[
ABDBKS24a
]
. It also provides the right context to the result of
[
ABDBKS24
]
claiming that the differentials of topological recursion on a global spectral curve is KP integrable if the spectral curve is rational. Indeed, by Theorem
4.20
, the KP integrability of the system of TR differentials follows from the KP integrability of the system of differentials
\phi_{n}=\delta_{n,2}B
considered as blobs, and by Proposition
2.2
, such system is KP integrable if the spectral curve is rational (and it can be upgraded to an “only if” statement, see also
[
ABDBKS25a
]
). Notice, however, that the proof of Theorem
4.20
given below rely on the mentioned result of
[
ABDBKS24
]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.16
, we have a freedom in a choice of
B
. Let us choose it in a special form

(197)

B(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{dz(p_{1})dz(p_{2})}{(z(p_{1})-z(p_{2}))^{2}}

for some meromorphic function
z
. We may assume that
z
takes finite pairwise distinct values at the points of
\mathcal{P}
, and
dz\neq 0
at these points. Along with the pole on the diagonal, the differential
B
may have some additional poles, namely, at the pairs of distinct points
(p_{1},p_{2})
with equal values of
z
. We can exclude these extra poles by
replacing the curve
\Sigma
by its open subset
U
containing
\mathcal{P}
. It is even sufficient to assume that
U
is a union of small disks centered at the points of
\mathcal{P}
. The function
z
provides an embedding of
U
to
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
, and the differential
B
coincides with the standard Bergman kernel on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
under this embedding. Thus, replacing
\Sigma
by
U
, we may assume that the spectral curve in the initial data of the topological recursion defining the differentials
\psi^{B}_{n}
is rational. The differentials

but the differentials
\psi^{B}_{n}
still extend as global meromorphic differentials on

By
[

∎

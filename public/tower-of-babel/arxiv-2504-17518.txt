On estimates for the discrete eigenvalues of two-dimensional quantum waveguides
Martin Karuhanga, Catherine Ashabahebwa

Abstract
In this paper, we give upper estimates for the number and sum of discrete eigenvalues below the bottom of the essential spectrum counting multiplicities of quantum waveguides in two dimensions. We consider both straight and curved waveguides of constant width, and the estimates are presented in terms of norms of the potential. For the curved quantum waveguide, we assume that the waveguide is not self-intersecting and its curvature is a continuous and bounded function on ℝ \mathbb{R} . The estimates are new, particularly for the case of curved quantum waveguides.



Keywords
: Estimates; Negative eigenvalues; quantum waveguides; Orlicz norms

(2020): 35P05, 47F05

Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a measure space and let
\psi:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
be a nondecreasing function. The class of measurable functions

. The Orlicz space
L_{\psi}(\Omega)
is the linear span of
K_{\psi}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A continuous nondecreasing convex function
\psi:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
is called an
N
-function if

\underset{t\longrightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}=0\mbox{ and }\underset{t\longrightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}=\infty.

The function
\phi:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
given by
\phi(t)=\underset{s\geq 0}{\sup}(st-\psi(s))
is called complementary to
\psi
.

Let
\phi
and
\psi
be mutually complementary
N
-functions and let
L_{\phi}(\Omega)
and
L_{\psi}(\Omega)
be the corresponding Orlicz spaces. Then the following norms are equivalent on
L_{\psi}(\Omega)
.

\|f\|_{\psi,\Omega}=\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\Omega}fgd\mu\right|\,:\,\int_{\Omega}\phi gd\mu\leq 1\right\}

and

\|f\|_{(\psi,\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\kappa\,:\,\int_{\Omega}\phi\left(\frac{f}{\kappa}\right)d\mu\leq 1\right\}.

If
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
, the following norm is equivalent to the norm on
L_{\psi}(\Omega)
[

]

\|f\|^{(av)}_{\psi,\Omega}=\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\Omega}fgd\mu\right|\,:\,\int_{\Omega}\phi(g)d\mu\leq\mu(\Omega)\right\}.

(5)

For our purpose, we shall use the following pair of
N
-complementary functions

\phi(s)=e^{|s|}-1-|s|,\,\,\psi(t)=(1+|t|)\ln(1+|t|)-|t|,\,s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

(6)

Definition 2.3
.

Let
I_{1},I_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}
be nonempty intervals. We shall define the space

to be the space of functions

\|f\|_{L_{1}(I_{1},L_{\psi}(I_{2}))}:=\int_{I_{1}}\|f\|^{(av)}_{\psi,I_{2}}dx<\infty.

(7)

Let

be a Hilbert space and

where

gives the tangent field. The pair (T, N) gives the Frenet-Serret frame
[
20
]
. The curvature
k
of
\gamma
is defined through the Frenet-Serret formulae by
k(s)=\mbox{ det }(\dot{\gamma},\ddot{\gamma})
as a continuous function of the arc-length parameter
s
. We define a curved strip of constant width
d
as
\Omega^{\prime}=L(\Omega)
, where

L:\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}:\{(s,u)\longmapsto\gamma(s)+uN(s)\}.

We shall make the following assumption:

(A):
\Omega^{\prime}
is non-self intersecting and
k\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty}<1
, where
k_{+}=\max\{0,\pm k\}
.

The above assumption implies that
s\longmapsto L(s,u)
for a fixed
u\in(0,d)
traces out a parallel curve at a distance
|u|
from
\gamma
and
u\longmapsto L(s,u)
for a fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}
is a straight line orthogonal to
\gamma
at
s
. In addition, the mapping
L:\Omega\longrightarrow\Omega^{\prime}
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism and its inverse determines a system of coordinates
(s,u)
in the neighbourhood of
\gamma
. Through the Frenet-Serret formulae, the metric tensor
G
of
\Omega^{\prime}
is given by

G(s,u)=\begin{pmatrix}(1-uk(s))^{2}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

(21)

The determinant of
G
is given by
|G|=(1-uk(s))^{2}
and its inverse is

G^{-1}(s,u)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{1-uk(s)^{2}}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

The area of the element of the curved strip is defined through

d\Omega^{\prime}=\sqrt{G}dsdu=(1-uk(s))dsdu.

By the virtue of the second part of assumption (A), it is clear that the metric tensor
G
in (
21
) is uniformly elliptic. In particular, for all
(s,u)\in\Omega
, we have

1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty}\leq 1-uk(s)\leq 1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty}.

(22)

The expression for the Laplacian on the strip equipped with the metric tensor (
21
) is given by

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}(\partial_{s},\partial_{u})\left(\sqrt{G}G^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{s}\\
\partial_{u}\end{pmatrix}\right)

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{s}^{2}}{(1-uk(s))^{2}}-\frac{u\dot{k}(s)}{(1-uk(s))^{3}}\partial_{s}-\partial_{u}^{2}+\frac{k(s)}{1-uk(s)}\partial_{u}.

The domain of (
23
) is the subspace of the
W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)
whose elements satisfy the boundary conditions (
3
)
[
22
]
.
The Schrödinger operator in (
4
) on the strip with metric tensor
G
becomes

Q^{\prime}=-\Delta_{\Omega}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4d^{2}}-V,\,V\geq 0

(24)

subject to the boundary conditions in (
3
). If
q^{\prime}
is the quadratic form corresponding to
Q^{\prime}
, then for every
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,(1-uk(s)dsdu))
, we have

\displaystyle q^{\prime}[f]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\partial_{s}f|^{2}}{1-uk(s)}dsdu+\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{u}f|^{2}(1-uk(s))dsdu-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4d^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}(1-uk(s))dsdu

(25)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}V|f|^{2}(1-uk(s))dsdu,

\displaystyle D(q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega,(1-uk(s)dsdu):f(x,0)=0,\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\textbf{n}}(x,d)=0\}\cap L^{2}(\Omega,V(1-uk(s)dsdu).

4.2
Estimates for the number of eigenvalues

By the ellipicity property in (
22
), it follows from (
25
) that

\displaystyle q^{\prime}[f]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty}}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{s}f|^{2}dsdu+(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{u}f|^{2}dsdu

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{4d^{2}}(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}dsdu-(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}V|f|^{2}dsdu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{1}{1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty}},1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty}\right\}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{s}f|^{2}dsdu+\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{u}f|^{2}dsdu\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{4d^{2}}(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}dsdu-(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}V|f|^{2}dsdu

Let

m:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty}},1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty}\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle q^{\prime}[f]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{s}f|^{2}dsdu+\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{u}f|^{2}dsdu-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4md^{2}}(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}dsdu\right)

(26)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}V|f|^{2}dsdu.

The right hand side of (
26
) represents a quadratic form of a lower semi-bounded self-adjoint operator

-\Delta-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}-V^{\prime},\,\,V^{\prime}\geq 0

(27)

on
L^{2}(\Omega,dsdu)
, where
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4md^{2}}(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})
and
V^{\prime}=\frac{V(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})}{m}
.

Let
q^{\prime}_{0}
be the quadratic form corresponding to (
27
). Then

q^{\prime}[f]\geq q_{0}^{\prime}[f].

Hence, it follows by (
8
) that

N_{-}(q^{\prime})\leq N_{-}(q^{\prime}_{0}).

(28)

The transverse eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}
is given by

\eta(u)=\sin\frac{\pi l}{2d}u
, where

l=\sqrt{\frac{1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty}}{m}}.

Let

\beta:=\int_{0}^{d}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi l}{2d}u\,du

and let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
denote the domain of
q^{\prime}_{0}
. Define
P:\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{1}
by

Pf(s,u)=\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{d}f(s,u)\sin\frac{\pi l}{2d}ydu\right)\sin\frac{\pi l}{2d}u=j(s)\sin\frac{\pi l}{2d}u

(29)

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, where

j(s):=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{d}f(s,u)\sin\frac{\pi l}{2d}u\,du.

Then
P
is a projection. Let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{2}=(I-P)\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, then similarly to lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, we can easily show that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{2}
, and that the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{2}
is orthogonal to
\sin\frac{\pi l}{2d}u
in
L^{2}(0,d)
respectively.

Let the intervals
I_{k}
and
J_{k}
be defined as before. Let

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{l^{2}}{\beta}\int_{I_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{d}V(s,u)\sin^{2}\frac{\pi l}{2d}u\,du\right)\,ds,

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{l^{2}}{\beta}\int_{I_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{d}|s|V(s,u)\sin^{2}\frac{\pi l}{2d}u\,du\right)\,ds,\,k\not=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|V\|_{L_{1}(J_{k},L_{\psi}(I))}.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
V\geq 0
there exist constants
C_{11},C_{12}>0
such that

N_{-}(q^{\prime})\leq 1+C_{13}\left(\sum_{\gamma_{k}>C_{11}}\sqrt{\gamma_{k}}+\sum_{\mathcal{D}_{k}>C_{12}}\mathcal{D}_{k}\right),

(30)

where
C_{13}
is a constant depending on the curvature
k
.

Proof.

If
q^{\prime}_{01}
and
q^{\prime}_{02}
are the restrictions of the form
q^{\prime}_{0}
to the subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{2}
respectively, then

N_{-}(q^{\prime}_{0})\leq N_{-}(q^{\prime}_{01})+N_{-}(q^{\prime}_{02})

(31)

(cf.
11
).

Now, for all
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle q^{\prime}_{01}[g]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}|j^{\prime}(s)|^{2}ds-2\frac{l^{2}}{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|j(s)|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{d}V(s,u)\sin^{2}\frac{\pi l}{2d}udu\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|j^{\prime}(s)|^{2}ds-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}V_{*}(s)|j(s)|^{2}ds\right),

where

V_{*}(s)=\frac{l^{2}}{\beta}\int_{0}^{d}V(s,u)\sin^{2}\frac{\pi l}{2d}u\,du.

Hence
q^{\prime}_{01}
represents the well studied one-dimensional self-adjoint operator

-j^{\prime\prime}-V_{*}j

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The definitions of
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{k}
imply that

\gamma_{0}=\int_{I_{0}}V_{*}(s)\,ds\mbox{ and }\gamma_{k}=\int_{I_{k}}|s|V_{*}(s)\,dx,\,k\not=0.

Thus there is a constant
C_{14}>0
such that

N_{-}(q^{\prime}_{01})\leq 1+C_{14}\sum_{\gamma_{k}>C_{11}}\sqrt{\gamma_{k}}.

(32)

On the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{2}
, partitioning
\Omega
into rectangles
\{\Omega_{k}\}
and following a similar procedure leading to (

), one can show that there is a constant
C_{15}>0
such that

N_{-}(q^{\prime}_{02})\leq C_{15}\|V\|_{L_{1}(J_{k},L_{\psi}(I))}=C_{15}\mathcal{D}_{k},

where
C_{15}=l^{2}C_{10}

N_{-}(q^{\prime}_{02})\leq C_{15}\sum_{\mathcal{D}_{k}>C_{12}}\mathcal{D}_{k}.

(34)

Hence (

) follows from (

), (

) and (

∎

4.3
Estimates for the sum of eigenvalues

We begin this section by stating the following result called the Lieb-Thirring inequality that we shall require in the proof of our estimate.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
25
, Theorem 4.38]

Let
\kappa>0
and let
\{\lambda_{n}(-\Delta-V)_{-}\}
be a nondecreasing sequence of negative eigenvalues of the lower semi-bounded self-adjoint operator
-\Delta-V,\,V\geq 0
. Then for
n\geq 2
there is a constant
C_{16}>0
depending on
\kappa
and
n
such that

\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(-\Delta-V)^{\kappa}_{-}\leq C_{10}(\kappa,n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)^{\kappa+\frac{n}{2}}\,dx

(35)

for any
V\in L^{\kappa+\frac{n}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Similarly, let
\{\lambda_{n}(Q)_{-}\}
denote a nondecreasing sequence of negative eigenvalues of the operator in (
4
).
Then taking
\kappa=1
and
n=2
, it follows from (
35
) that

\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(Q)_{-}\leq C_{16}\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\,\,\forall V\geq 0.

(36)

The following result can be found for example in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be lower semi-bounded self-adjoint operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and let
q_{1}
and
a_{2}
be their associated quadratic forms respectively. If
D(a_{2})\subset D(a_{1})
and for all
u\in D(a_{2})
, we have that

a_{1}[u]\leq a_{2}[u]
, then
T_{1}\leq T_{2}
. Consequently,
\lambda_{n}(T_{1})_{-}\leq\lambda_{n}(T_{2})_{-}
and

\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(T_{1})_{-}\leq\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(T_{2})_{-}.

(37)

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\{\lambda_{n}(Q^{\prime})_{-}\}
be a nondecreasing sequence of negative eigenvalues of the operator in (
24
). Then there is a constant
C_{11}>0
depending on the curvature
k
such that

\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(Q^{\prime})_{-}\leq C_{17}\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\,\,\forall V\geq 0.

(38)

Proof.

By (
22
) and (
25
), we have that

\displaystyle q^{\prime}[f]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty}}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{s}f|^{2}dsdu+(1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{u}f|^{2}dsdu

(39)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{4d^{2}}(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}dsdu-(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}V|f|^{2}dsdu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{1}{1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty}},1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty}\right\}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{s}f|^{2}dsdu+\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{u}f|^{2}dsdu\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{4d^{2}}(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}dsdu-(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}V|f|^{2}dsdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{s}f|^{2}dsdu+\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{u}f|^{2}dsdu-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4Md^{2}}(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}dsdu\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})\int_{\Omega}V|f|^{2}dsdu,

where

M:=\max\left\{\frac{1}{1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty}},1+d\|k_{-}\|_{\infty}\right\}.

The right hand side of (
39
) gives a quadratic form associated with the lower semi-bounded self-adjoint operator

P^{\prime}:=-\Delta-\lambda^{*}_{1}-V^{*},\,\,V^{*}\geq 0

(40)

on
L^{2}(\Omega,dsdu)
, where
\lambda^{*}_{1}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4Md^{2}}(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})
and
V^{*}=\frac{V(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})}{M}
.

If
p^{\prime}
is the quadratic form corresponding to (
40
), then

q^{\prime}[f]\leq p^{\prime}[f].

Consequently, (
37
) gives

\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(Q^{\prime})_{-}\leq\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(P^{\prime})_{-}.

(41)

Using (
36
), one has

\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(P^{\prime})_{-}\leq C_{16}\frac{(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})^{2}}{M^{2}}\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence (
38
) follows with

C_{17}=C_{16}\frac{(1-d\|k_{+}\|_{\infty})^{2}}{M^{2}}.

∎

In (
38
), the series on the left hand side converges for any
0\leq V\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with the curvature contributing greatly by inducing more eigenvalues than those in the straight case. Therefore the motion of the quantum particle is stable and the particle remains confined within a certain for a significant amount of time. Theorem
4.4
can easily be generalized by the dual-Thirring inequality for any
L^{2}
-orthonormal sequence in the domain of the quadratic form
q^{\prime}
where the optimal estimating constants are in a one-to-one correspondence, see for example
[
25
, Theorem 7.12]
.

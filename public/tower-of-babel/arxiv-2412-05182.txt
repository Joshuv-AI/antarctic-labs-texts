Abstract
An unsplittable multiflow routes the demand of each commodity along a single path from its source to its sink node. As our main result, we prove that in series-parallel digraphs, any given multiflow can be expressed as a convex combination of unsplittable multiflows, where the total flow on any arc deviates from the given flow by less than the maximum demand of any commodity. This result confirms a 25-year-old conjecture by Goemans for single-source unsplittable flows, as well as a stronger recent conjecture by Morell and Skutella, for series-parallel digraphs—even for general multiflow instances where commodities have distinct source and sink nodes. Previously, no non-trivial class of digraphs was known for which either conjecture holds. En route to proving this result, we also establish strong integrality results for multiflows on series-parallel digraphs, showing that their computation can be reduced to a simple single-commodity network flow problem.

Introduction

Network flow theory focuses on the optimal routing of commodities through networks and is fundamental to a wide array of applications in fields such as logistics, telecommunications, and computer networking; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
21
]
. In many scenarios, splitting a commodity might degrade the quality of service, increase complexity, or is simply infeasible. For example, in optical networks, dividing data streams across multiple paths requires specialized equipment. Similarly, in freight logistics, splitting loads across multiple routes is often impractical.

This motivates
unsplittable
multiflows. Here, for a given digraph with
k
source-sink pairs, the given demand
d_{i}
of every commodity
i\in[k]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,k\}
must be sent along a single path
P_{i}
from its source
s_{i}
to its sink
t_{i}
. To differentiate unsplittable flows from traditional flows, where the demand of each commodity may be divided and sent along multiple
s_{i}
–
t_{i}
paths, we refer to the latter as
fractional
flows.

Unsplittable flow problems represent a compelling extension of disjoint path problems and have received considerable attention in the literature. Even in the apparently straightforward case of a (series-parallel) digraph consisting of one common source and one common sink connected by parallel arcs, determining the existence of a
feasible
unsplittable multiflow (i.e., one that adheres to given arc capacities) is NP-complete. In fact, several classical problems in combinatorial optimization, such as, e.g., Bin Packing, Partition, or parallel machine scheduling with makespan objective occur as special cases; for more details we refer to Kleinberg’s PhD thesis
[
5
]
.

Integer multiflows.

When every commodity
i\in[k]
has unit demand
d_{i}\coloneqq 1
, unsplittable multiflows precisely correspond to integer multiflows, which have been extensively studied in the literature. In fact, in unit-capacity networks, feasible integer multiflows model arc-disjoint paths, and therefore cannot be computed efficiently, unless P=NP. For example, Vygen
[
20
]
shows that the arc-disjoint paths problems is NP-complete even in acyclic and planar digraphs; see
[
16

for further related complexity results. On the other hand, single-commodity network flows (i.e.,

[
1
,
21

(x_{e})_{e\in E}
on a digraph
G=(V,E)
with integer excess at every node, is a convex combination of integer flows
(y_{e})_{e\in E}
that maintain the same excesses, such that

\displaystyle\lfloor x_{e}\rfloor\leq y_{e}\leq\lceil x_{e}\rceil\qquad\text{for every arc~$e\in E$.}

Single-source unsplittable flows.

Even though our results hold for general multiflow instances in series-parallel digraphs, where each commodity is routed from a distinct source to a distinct sink, our main result is primarily inspired by prior research on single-source unsplittable flows, which we review next. For a comprehensive overview of results on general unsplittable multiflows, we refer to Kolliopoulos’ survey
[
6

Single-source unsplittable flows, where all commodities share a common source node

]

Multiflows.

We consider a directed graph

Aligned multiflow instances on series-parallel digraphs.

We generalize the simple idea of subdividing commodities outlined above.
If, for some commodity

Sufficiency of a strengthened cut condition

As discussed above, the classical cut condition (
4
) is, in general, not sufficient to guarantee the existence of a feasible multiflow in a series-parallel digraph
G=(V,E)
; see the example in
Figure

1
. A natural strengthening of the classical cut condition considers, for any subset of arcs
F\subseteq E
, the set of commodities

K(F)\coloneqq\{i\in[k]\mid\text{there is no~$s_{i}$--$t_{i}$~path in the subdigraph~$(V,E\setminus F)$}\}.

By definition, the set
F
forms a
multicut
for the commodities in
K(F)
; that is, none of these commodities can be routed after removing the arcs in
F
. In any feasible multiflow, the arcs in
F
must therefore carry at least the total demand of all commodities in
K(F)
. This yields the following
strong cut condition
:

\displaystyle c(F)\geq\sum_{i\in K(F)}d_{i}\qquad\text{for all~$F\subseteq E$.}

(5)

This strong cut condition is clearly necessary for the existence of a feasible multiflow.

In the example shown in
Figure

1
, the classical cut condition (
4
) is satisfied, but the strong cut condition (
5
) is violated for the arc set
F=\{(s_{2},t_{1})\}
: here,
c(F)=1
, while the total demand of the blocked commodities
K(F)=\{1,2\}
equals
d_{1}+d_{2}=2
. Notably, in this example the violating arc set
F
is of the form
\delta^{+}(X)
for
X=\{s_{2}\}\subseteq V
.

In fact, as we show in
Theorem

3
, for any infeasible multiflow instance on a series-parallel digraph, there always exists a node subset
X\subseteq V
such that the strong cut condition is violated for
F=\delta^{+}(X)
. This observation motivates a further refinement: the following
strengthened cut condition

\displaystyle c\bigl(\delta^{+}(X)\bigr)\geq\sum_{i\in K(\delta^{+}(X))}d_{i}\qquad\text{for all~$X\subseteq V$,}

(6)

is stronger than the classical cut condition (
4
) but weaker than the strong cut condition (
5
). In
Figure

4
we provide an example of an infeasible multiflow instance on an acyclic digraph that is not series-parallel, which satisfies the strengthened cut condition (
6
) but violates the strong cut condition (
5
).

s_{1}
d_{1}=2
t_{2}

For multiflow instances on series-parallel digraphs, the strengthened cut condition (

Almost unsplittable flows

Before we prove out main result

\bm{\mathbf{X}}
into an

multiflow with the same demand. Formally, we say that a multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
in
G
is

if at most two commodities are routed fractionally through every subdigraph
G_{\omega}
,
\omega\in V_{T}
, and the two components in a parallel composition may share at most one fractional commodity.

Definition 3
.

A multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
in
G
is
almost unsplittable
if it satisfies:

(i)

for every node
\omega\in V_{T}
of the
sp
-tree
T
, we have
|I_{\omega}(\bm{\mathbf{X}})|\leq 2
;

(ii)

|I_{\omega_{1}}(\bm{\mathbf{X}})\cap I_{\omega_{2}}(\bm{\mathbf{X}})|\leq 1
for every
p
-node with two children
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in V_{T}
.
If

\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
.

It turns out that any multiflow can be turned into an almost unsplittable multiflow without changing its total flow.

Theorem 4
.

Any multiflow in
G
can efficiently be turned into an almost unsplittable multiflow that satisfies the same demands and has the same total flow.

The proof of
Theorem

4
relies on a simple swapping operation that, for given children of a common
p
-node, exchanges flow of two commodities between the corresponding parallel components of
G
, and is described in the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Consider a multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
, a
p
-node
\bar{\omega}\in V_{T}
with child nodes
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
, and two distinct commodities
i_{1}\in I_{\omega_{1}}(\bm{\mathbf{X}})
and
i_{2}\in I_{\omega_{2}}(\bm{\mathbf{X}})
. Commodities
i_{1},i_{2}
can be rerouted within

to obtain a multiflow
\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{X}}}
with the same total flow, such that
z_{\omega_{1},i_{1}}(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{X}}})=0
or
z_{\omega_{2},i_{2}}(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{X}}})=0
.

Proof.

While commodities
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
are routed fractionally through the subgraphs
G_{\omega_{1}}
and

, respectively, we repeatedly swap flow as follows:

Find a flow-carrying

for commodity

along the other path

The described swapping of flow within

5
Convex decompositions of multiflows

In this section, as an intermediate step towards our main result,
Theorem

2
, we first prove a slightly weaker version, allowing unsplittable multiflows to deviate from the given fractional multiflow by at most
2d_{\max}
on each arc.
4
4

4

Traub, Vargas Koch, and Zenklusen obtained the same bound for the special case of a single source node
s=s_{i}
,
i\in[k]
, on the more general class of planar and acyclic digraphs; see
[
18
, Theorem 1.8]
.

Theorem 5
.

For any multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
in
G
, its total arc flows
(x_{e})_{e\in E}
can be expressed as a convex combination of total arc flows
(y_{e})_{e\in E}
of unsplittable multiflows satisfying

\displaystyle x_{e}-2d_{\max}<y_{e}<x_{e}+2d_{\max}\qquad\text{for every arc~$e\in E$.}

(9)

Moreover, the unsplittable multiflows and the coefficients of the convex combination can be computed efficiently.

We discuss the proof of
Theorem

5
in detail since the construction of the convex decomposition also forms the basis of the proof of our main result
Theorem

2
.
In view of
Theorem

4
, it suffices to prove
Theorem

5
for an almost unsplittable multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
with the same total flow
\bm{\mathbf{x}}
.
Although the statement of
Theorem

5
is only about the total flows, we proceed by showing the stronger statement that an almost unsplittable multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
itself (i.e., the full matrix
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
and not only the total flow vector
\bm{\mathbf{x}}
) can be expressed as a convex combination of unsplittable multiflows.

5.1
Unsplittable Routings.

In order to construct suitable unsplittable multiflows for the convex decomposition, we leverage the properties of almost unsplittable multiflows.
If
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
is almost unsplittable, by
Definition

3
, for each node
\omega\in V_{T}
of the
sp
-tree
T
, multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
routes (at most) two commodities fractionally through the corresponding component.
To simplify notation and avoid case distinctions, we assume that
|I_{\omega}|=2
for every
\omega
, and let
p^{\omega},q^{\omega}\in[k]
be the two distinct commodities routed fractionally through
\omega
, i.e.,
I_{\omega}=\{p^{\omega},q^{\omega}\}
. We take care of the case

by introducing dummy commodities in
Section

6

Since in an almost unsplittable multiflow all but the two commodities
p^{\omega},q^{\omega}\in I_{\omega}
must be routed through the subdigraph
G_{\omega}
, we are only left with four possible routing options.
We represent these options with the subsets

J^{\omega}_{1}\coloneqq\emptyset,\qquad J^{\omega}_{2}\coloneqq\{p^{\omega}\},\qquad J^{\omega}_{3}\coloneqq\{q^{\omega}\},\qquad\text{and}\quad J^{\omega}_{4}\coloneqq\{p^{\omega},q^{\omega}\},

(10)

where routing option

means that exactly the commodities in
\bar{I}_{\omega}\cup J^{\omega}_{j}
(i.e., the commodities that are completely routed through
G_{\omega}
plus the commodities selected in the set
J^{\omega}_{j}
) are routed through the subdigraph
G_{\omega}
. We formalize this in the following definition.

Definition 4
.

We say an unsplittable multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{Y}}

respects the routing option
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
in
\omega\in V_{T}
if

z_{\omega,i}(\bm{\mathbf{Y}})=1\text{ if }i\in\bar{I}_{\omega}\cup J^{\omega}_{j}

and

z_{\omega,i}(\bm{\mathbf{Y}})=0\text{ if }i\notin\bar{I}_{\omega}\cup J^{\omega}_{j}.

Notice, that the definition above (as well as most definitions in this section) are always in relation to the given (almost unsplittable) multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
since the sets
\bar{I}_{\omega}
and
J^{\omega}_{j}
depend on this flow.
For any multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
(fractional and unsplittable), we define

\bar{x}_{\omega,i}\coloneqq z_{\omega,i}(\bm{\mathbf{X}})d_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{x}_{\omega}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bar{x}_{\omega,i},

where
\bar{x}_{\omega,i}
is the amount of flow of some commodity

routed through the subgraph
G_{\omega}
and
\bar{x}_{\omega}
is the total flow of all commodities routed through the subgraph
G_{\omega}
.
In the special case of a leaf node
\omega\in V_{T}
that represents an arc
e\in E
, we have
X_{e,i}=\bar{x}_{\omega,i}
and

.
In view of the desired bound (
9

Lemma 3
.

Let
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
be an almost unsplittable flow and
\bm{\mathbf{Y}}
an unsplittable flow.

(i)

If
\bm{\mathbf{Y}}
respects the routing option

in some

|\bar{y}_{\omega}-\bar{x}_{\omega}|<2d_{\max}.

(ii)

If, for every (leaf) node

respects some routing option

, then

x_{e}-2d_{\max}<y_{e}<x_{e}+2d_{\max}

Assume that
\bm{\mathbf{Y}}
respects the routing option

in some fixed

|\bar{y}_{\omega}-\bar{x}_{\omega}|=\Bigl|\sum_{i\in[k]}z_{\omega,i}(\bm{\mathbf{Y}})d_{i}-\sum_{i\in[k]}z_{\omega,i}(\bm{\mathbf{X}})d_{i}\Bigr|=\Big|\sum_{i\in J^{\omega}_{j}}d_{i}-\sum_{i\in I_{\omega}}z_{\omega,i}d_{i}\Big|<d_{p^{\omega}}+d_{q^{\omega}}\leq 2d_{\max}

.

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\rho_{\ell}\bm{\mathbf{Y}}_{\ell}=\bm{\mathbf{X}}

Let

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\rho_{\ell}Y_{\ell,e,i}=X_{e,i}

\displaystyle[a-b]^{+}-\max\{a,b\}

\displaystyle=-b,

\displaystyle[a-b]^{+}+\min\{a,b\}

\displaystyle=a.

Lemma 5
.

For every node
\omega
of the

\displaystyle\mu^{\omega}_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big[(1-z_{\omega,q^{\omega}})-z_{\omega,p^{\omega}}\big]^{+},

\displaystyle\mu^{\omega}_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\min\{z_{\omega,p^{\omega}},1-z_{\omega,q^{\omega}}\},

\displaystyle\;\mu^{\omega}_{3}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1-\max\{z_{\omega,p^{\omega}},1-z_{\omega,q^{\omega}}\},\;

\displaystyle\mu^{\omega}_{4}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big[z_{\omega,p^{\omega}}-(1-z_{\omega,q^{\omega}})\big]^{+}

\mu^{\omega}_{j}\in[0,1],j=1,\dotsc,4

Proof.

Since

.
Further, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{4}\mu^{\omega}_{j}

\displaystyle=(\mu^{\omega}_{1}+\mu^{\omega}_{3})+(\mu^{\omega}_{2}+\mu^{\omega}_{4})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:properties:positive:part}}}{{=}}(1-z_{\omega,p^{\omega}})+z_{\omega,p^{\omega}}=1,

\displaystyle\mu^{\omega}_{2}+\mu^{\omega}_{4}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:properties:positive:part}}}}{{=}}z_{\omega,p^{\omega}},

\displaystyle\mu^{\omega}_{3}+\mu^{\omega}_{4}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:properties:positive:part}}}}{{=}}1-(1-z_{\omega,q^{\omega}})=z_{\omega,q^{\omega}}

5.2
Recursive Refinements of Convex Decompositions

We construct a convex decomposition of a given, almost unsplittable multiflow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
recursively over the nodes
\omega\in V_{T}
of the
sp
-tree.
Formally, we show the following statement.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
be an almost unsplittable multiflow. Then, for every node
\omega\in V_{T}
of the
sp
-tree, there exist families of unsplittable multiflows
(\bm{\mathbf{Y}}^{\omega}_{\ell})_{\ell=1,\dotsc,L_{\omega}}
in the subgraph
G_{\omega}
and coefficients
(\rho^{\omega}_{\ell})_{\ell=1,\dotsc,L_{\omega}}
with the following properties.

(i)

For every
\omega\in V_{T}
,

\bm{\mathbf{X}}^{\omega}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_{\omega}}\rho^{\omega}_{\ell}\bm{\mathbf{Y}}^{\omega}_{\ell},

where
\bm{\mathbf{X}}^{\omega}
is the restriction of the flow
\bm{\mathbf{X}}
to the subgraph
G_{\omega}
.

(ii)

For every
\omega\in V_{T}
, every flow
\bm{\mathbf{Y}}^{\omega}_{\ell},\ell=1,\dotsc,L_{\omega}
respects some routing option
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
in all nodes
\omega^{\prime}
that are descendants of
\omega
in the
sp
-tree (including
\omega
itself).

Eventually, we can use
Lemma

6
for
\omega=\omega_{0}
(where
\omega_{0}
is the root of the
sp
-tree) in order to prove that the desired convex decomposition in

5

We continue by proving

by induction over the nodes
\omega\in V_{T}
, starting from the leaf nodes.

A Comparison of Uptake of Tea Stains and Effectiveness of Denture Cleansers on CAD/CAM Versus Conventional Acrylic Denture Base
Authors: Mahmoud Darwish, Aleshba Saba Khan, Lulwah Mohammed Alanazi, May Mohammed Alenazi, Haya Sultan Alassaf, Noura Mohammed Alharbi, Malak Mihmas Almutairi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and Objective:To compare the uptake of tea stain and the effectiveness of denture cleansers on computer-aided design/computer-aided manufacturing versus conventional acrylic dentures.Materials and Methods:This in vitro study used a total of 60 samples. Thirty samples from each group of CAD/CAM and conventional acrylic denture base samples. Then each group was divided into 3 subgroups of 10 samples. Each group was exposed to tea stains. After that, group A was exposed to water as the control group, group B to Corega, and group C to fittydent cleanser. Color measurements using a spectrophotometer were made at baseline, after tea staining, and then after immersion in the control group and denture cleansers. Statistical analysis involved t-tests and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA).Results:Significant uptake of tea stain was seen in both types of denture bases. Corega denture cleanser showed better stain removal from both types of denture bases. CAD/CAM denture bases show significantly better stain removal when immersed in cleansers.Conclusion:Alkaline peroxide-based denture cleansers are more effective in the removal of tea stain and CAD/CAM-based dentures, when immersed in cleansers, show better stain removal.

INTRODUCTION

Color stability is one of the main criteria for dental materials that reflect clinical success when put in a patient’s mouth for performance.[1234] The factors leading to color change can be extrinsic or intrinsic, like dietary choices, the effect of food and drinks consumed on denture bases by causing changes in pH and absorption of water from the oral cavity, maintenance of hygiene of dentures, porosity, leftover monomer, excessive heating, or insufficient pressure during polymerization.[24] All these factors can result in water sorption and thus, color change.[2] Tea is considered one of the most used hot drinks worldwide.[5] Daily use of hot tea can lead to staining of dentures if proper hygiene is not maintained, resulting in an unesthetic appearance.[6]

Maintenance of denture hygiene is necessary to avoid any visible damage as well as to avoid any buildup of calculus, odor, stains or harbor infectious microbes.[6] Cleansers provide antibacterial, antifungal and antimicrobial property thus aid in maintenance of proper hygiene of dentures specifically for geriatric patients with reduced manual dexterity.[78]

A comparative study to see color stability between acrylic teeth and CAD/CAM-manufactured teeth showed that CAD/CAM-fabricated teeth were more color stable.[2] Another study compared the stainability of conventional versus CAD/CAM dentures and concluded that both types take up the stains.[9] However, at the acrylic and denture base teeth interface, CAD/CAM showed better resistance to stains.[9] A study mentioned that denture cleansers affect the color stability of 3D printed denture bases.[4]

CAD/CAM-made dentures are considered a better alternative to PMMA-based conventional dentures.[4] Although both types of dentures are reported to take up stain, there is still currently limited scientific evidence to comparison that which material will take up less stain.[4] Also, the use of readily available denture cleansers is increasing with increasing awareness,[8] so the effect of different types of denture cleansers should also be studied to see the difference in comparative impact of cleansers on conventional versus CAD/CAM-made dentures. This study aims to compare the difference between conventional acrylic dentures and CAD/CAM dentures after immersion in tea, to observe uptake of stain, and the effectiveness of denture cleansers for the removal of stains from both types of dentures.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

An in-vitro experimental comparative laboratory based research was conducted at the Department of Prosthodontics, Vision colleges, Riyadh, KSA. Ethical approval was obtained from the ethical committee of Vision College (IRB # alf.dent-2020050) before the start of the study.

Sample fabrication

Sixty square samples of two types of denture base resins measuring (10 mm × 10 mm × 2 mm) were prepared.[910] Thirty samples for conventional heat cured PMMA resin and 30 samples for CAD/CAM PMMA resin. Conventional heat cured PMMA (Ecocryl-Hot, Protechno, Girona, Spain) specimens were prepared by investing pieces of modeling wax (Cavex-Holand BV, Netherlands) in stone plaster (LabStone-Dentsply, USA) by a conventional flasking procedure in metallic dental flask according to the manufacturer’s instructions. CAD/CAM PMMA resin specimens were prepared from a prepolymerized PMMA puck that was manufactured under controlled heat and high pressure (AvaDent, Global Dental Science, USA). The specimens were cut using a diamond saw machine (Isomet 5000; Buehler Ltd, Lake Bluff, IL, USA) at 1800 revolutions/min under water cooling.

All the specimens for both types of resins were visually inspected and checked for the absence of voids or porosity, and defective samples were discarded. The specimens were prepared to the required dimensions by finishing discs and stones using a handpiece at low speed. The cut resin specimens were then finished and polished by a master dental technician. Then the specimens of both groups were randomly divided into three subgroups (10 in each).

Sample staining

All samples were stored in water for 24 hours at room temperature. A spectrophotometer analysis of all samples was done using a spectrophotometer (LabScan XE, HunterLab, USA) to evaluate the base value of the color for comparative analysis. It determines the lightness (L), green-red component (a*), and blue-yellow component (b*). After that, the three groups were immersed in tea, prepared by using 4 tea bags (2g each) of Lipton yellow label immersed in approximately 1000 mL of hot water for 24 hours, and the color was measured again using the spectrophotometer to get the values of L, a, and b.

Removal of established stains by cleansers

The samples were then cleansed using denture cleansers for 24 hours. The denture cleansers were prepared according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Group A samples were immersed in water as a control group. Group B was immersed in Corega tablet cleanser. Group C was immersed in fittydent denture cleanser. After that, the color of the samples was again evaluated using the spectrophotometer to get the values of L, a, and b. The magnitude of color differences between baseline color and after tea staining (∆E tea- base) and the differences in colors after immersion in denture cleansers (∆E cleanser—tea) were obtained using the following formula: ∆E = {(ΔL) ² +(Δb) ² +(Δa) ²}1/2.

Descriptive data analysis was done by obtaining mean and standard deviation using SPSS version 26. T-test and one way ANOVA test was used to analyze the data. Independent t-test was used for comparison of color change between 2 types of denture bases. Significance value was set as P ≤ 0.05.

RESULTS

Color difference values (∆E) for CAD/CAM and conventional heat-cured denture base resin after immersion in tea and cleaning by water or denture cleansers are shown in Figures 1 and 2.

After immersion in tea, all CAD/CAM denture base resin specimens showed significant changes in color, P < 0.05 [Figure 1]. Similarly, all conventional heat-cured denture base resin specimens showed significant changes in color after immersion in tea [Figure 2] (P < 0.05). In comparisons between values of (∆E) for both types of resins after immersion in tea, the differences were not significant (P > 0.05).

As regards the effect of cleansers on tea stain removal, Figure 1 shows that CAD/CAM specimens that were immersed in both types of denture cleansers had a significant change in color than those immersed in water (control group) (P < 0.05), and Corega denture cleansers exhibited a higher tea stain removal effect. Correspondingly, Figure 2 shows that denture cleansers have a more tea stain removal effect than the effect of water in conventional heat-cured resin specimens, as presented by the values of color changes (∆E). If we compare both types of denture base resins, tea stain removal by the denture cleansers was significantly higher in CAD/CAM resin specimens than that in conventional heat-cured resin specimens (P < 0.05). On the other hand, water has the same tea stain removal effect for both types of denture base resins (control groups).

DISCUSSION

Color instability is one of the most prevalent acrylic resin-related problems that can lead to complications of aesthetics and hygiene.[9] Denture base material used for the fabrication of dentures must be present in an atmosphere having variations in oral temperature, the pH of saliva, and their constituents and must be in contact with multiple foods and drinks taken at extreme temperatures that make them vulnerable to changes in their physical structure and appearance owing to the absorption of different contaminants.[14611] Hence, a comparison between conventional heat cured denture base resin and denture base resin fabricated by CAD/CAM technology was made in this study.

Among beverages, tea is the first most commonly consumed beverage after water.[5] The present study utilized tea in which samples were immersed for 24 hours, which simulates 1 month of daily consumption of tea according to the literature support.[12] Alkaline peroxide denture cleansers are commonly used by denture wearers more than other denture cleansers to keep the dentures clean and devoid of any deposits.[9] Hence, a denture cleaner that contained sodium perborate and potassium persulphate, as its active ingredients, was employed in the study, and the samples were immersed in it for 24 hours, as previous study.[9]

It was observed that there was not much difference in the uptake of tea stains between the conventional heat-cured PMMA and CAD/CAM PMMA materials when immersed in tea. These findings suggest that both types of denture base materials behaved similarly in getting stained by tea. This finding is in accordance with the study performed by Al-Qarni et al.,[9] in 2020, in their in vitro study to evaluate the stainability of acrylic resins used in CAD/CAM fabricated complete dentures compared with conventional materials. They used coffee to stain conventional acrylic resin and CAD/CAM-made acrylic resin.[7] Both types showed no significant difference in uptake of stain, as seen in our study. Another study by Peñate et al.,[12] although done on interim materials, also reported similar findings where black tea stain uptake by both types did not show much of a difference between conventional versus CAD/CAM-made materials.

Although the most used method for cleaning dentures is brush and soap, as the age advances, manual dexterity of people is reduced.[13] Chemical means of denture cleaning, by denture cleansers, other than manual means is more effective and easy option. The commercially available denture cleansers usually have a combination of ingredients, including oxidizing, effervescing, and chelating agents along with detergents and enzymes.[7111314] The results obtained in the present study showed that ΔE values for all the samples were greater in samples subjected to denture cleanser than that subjected to cleaning by water (control groups), this indicate better effectiveness of the used denture cleansers to remove the stain from both types of denture base materials when compared with cleaning by water. This finding is confirmed by previous in vitro studies by Banu et al.,[11] Hollis et al.,[14] and a study by Priyanka and associates.[13] Banu et al.[11] used coffee and soft drinks to stain the samples of different conventional acrylic denture materials. They reported that significant difference was found between different denture cleansers in removal of stains as compared to control group (water).[1113] This is in accordance with the results of this study. Jagger et al.[15] reported that the cleansers with hypochlorite were better in stain removal. This is also in accordance with our study.

Moreover, our study shows that cleansers are significantly more effective in removing stains from CAD/CAM made dentures compared to heat-cured conventional dentures. This may be explained by the penetration of the tea stains in deeper layers in dentures in samples constructed by conventional methods, that is, processed manually, which may contain more free monomers, which may affect the cross-linking of the polymer chain.[11] However, in the case of CAD/CAM denture base resin, the material is fabricated under high-controlled pressure and temperature that will produce highly cross-linked polymers with improved physical and mechanical properties not easily penetrated by the stain.[4] Consequently, after 24 hours of immersion, alkaline peroxide denture cleansers remove tea stains from CAD/CAM denture base materials more easily and effectively than from the denture base materials fabricated by the conventional heat-curing method.

To our knowledge, the literature contains no study on the effect of alkaline peroxide solution on tea stain removal from CAD/CAM denture base resin, so it is not possible to compare the results; therefore, further studies should be carried out.

This in vitro study has some limitations that may affect the results, including no simulation of the oral clinical conditions like tooth brushing, temperature, and PH changes. Also, the way in which the specimens are fabricated is different from the actual clinical situation; the samples have flat surfaces, which are highly polished, but the dentures have curved surfaces with an outer polished surface and an inner fitting rough surface. So, further clinical studies should be considered for a better understanding of the effectiveness of denture cleansers to remove tea stains from CAD/CAM denture base materials.

CONCLUSION

Both CAD/CAM and conventional heat-cured PMMA denture base materials demonstrated similar susceptibility to tea staining. Alkaline peroxide-based denture cleansers were effective in removing tea stains in both materials, with Corega showing the highest stain removal effectiveness. Stain removal was significantly greater in CAD/CAM specimens, likely due to their higher degree of polymerization and reduced porosity. Therefore, CAD/CAM denture bases may offer better long-term color stability when combined with appropriate cleansing protocols. Thus, it can be concluded that in future, CAD/CAM fabricated dentures should be preferred over conventional dentures provided that economic situation of the patient allows.

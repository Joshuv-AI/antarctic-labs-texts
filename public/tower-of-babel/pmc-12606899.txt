Spiral volumetric modulated arc therapy for whole-brain radiotherapy with simultaneous integrated boost to multiple brain metastases
Authors: Yuan Xu, Junjie Miao, Jianrong Dai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeSpiral volumetric modulated arc therapy (SVMAT) is an integrated radiation therapy technique with longitudinal couch movement to generate a spiral/helical trajectory. This approach combines the merits of volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT) and helical tomotherapy (HT). This study aimed to investigate the treatment planning of SVMAT for whole-brain radiotherapy (WBRT) with simultaneous integrated boost (SIB) for a large number (> 40) of metastases.Materials and methodsTen patients with multiple brain metastases (40–120 metastases) were retrospectively enrolled. These patients had previously received treatment with HT using the WBRT + SIB technique. Prescribed doses of 40 Gy for the whole brain and 60 Gy for the metastases were delivered in 20 fractions. In this study, SVMAT plans were designed using DeepPlan (version 1.3, Wisdom Tech., Hefei, Anhui, China) as the treatment planning system for the radiotherapy machine NeuRT Aurora (Neusoft IntelliRay Technology, Shenyang, Liaoning, China). In all SVMAT plans, the patient couch was set to move out after specified multiple gantry rotations and then move in within a single treatment fraction. The number of gantry rotations ranged between 6 and 16. These SVMAT plans were compared with HT and piecewise VMAT (previously proposed PVMAT) based on a plan quality metric (PQM) comprising 20 metrics.ResultsFor target coverage and conformity index (CI) of whole brain and metastases, no significant differences were observed between the SVMAT&HT or between SVMAT&PVMAT plans (p > 0.05). The sparing of right hippocampus, optic nerves and optic chiasm were improved using the SVMAT plan compared to the HT plan (p < 0.05). However, no significant difference was observed between the SVMAT and PVMAT plans for OAR sparing (p > 0.05). The mean total scores of PQM were 51.70 ± 8.14 (mean ± standard deviation), 53.24 ± 5.84, 53.64 ± 7.16, 54.24 ± 7.06, 54.60 ± 6.30, and 55.05 ± 6.18 points for SVMAT plans with gantry rotations of 6 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16, respectively. The average score for HT was 38.18 ± 5.48 points, which was significantly lower than that for the SVMAT plans (p < 0.05). Furthermore, the mean score for PVMAT was 54.34 ± 6.48 points, and no significant differences were observed between SVMAT and PVMAT (p > 0.05). The beam delivery time was shorter for SVMAT with 6 (271.5 ± 53.7 s), 8(310.3 ± 42.9 s), and 10(359.6 ± 34.4 s) rotations compared to HT (393.0 ± 25.0 s) (p < 0.05) and also shorter for SVMAT with six rotations compared to PVMAT (312.6 ± 53.7 s) (p < 0.05).ConclusionsFor WBRT + SIB, SVMAT achieved improved plan quality and higher delivery efficiency compared with HT and had similar plan quality compared with PVMAT.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13014-025-02742-4.

Introduction

Volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT) is commonly used in modern radiation therapy [1–3]. It incorporates intensity modulation with simultaneous movement of the gantry and multi-leaf collimator (MLC), improving the conformality of targets and sparing of organs at risk (OARs) for radiotherapy compared with static intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) or 3D-conformal radiation therapy (3D-CRT) [4–6]. However, VMAT has certain limitations. First, the width of MLC cannot be reduced indefinitely due to space limitations and the mechanical frame in the treatment head [7, 8]. For instance, the leaf width of a commercial high-definition MLC from Varian Edge™ (Varian Medical Systems, Palo Alto, CA, USA) is 2.5 mm [9]. This leaf width was shown to impact the resolution of fluence maps and the plan quality, especially in complex cases [10, 11]. Park et al. demonstrated that the resolution of VMAT can be potentially increased by shifting the couch by 2.5 mm (half of the leaf width), improving the plan quality for head and neck cancer [12]. Nevertheless, this method introduced setup uncertainties due to multiple positioning and increased treatment time for couch shifts and position verification. Another issue with VMAT is the limited field size, normally a maximum of 40 cm × 40 cm. Therefore, multiple isocenters and precise positioning are necessary to treat long targets, such as the craniospinal axis [13]. This will also increase the complexity, uncertainties, and duration of the treatment period.

Helical tomotherapy (HT) is a technology that integrates a linear accelerator (linac) with a circular computed tomography gantry [14, 15]. A rotational fan-shaped beam is delivered with progressive motion of the treatment couch, generating a helical path around the patient. Due to the movable couch, the HT has a large field size (maximum of 40 cm × 135 cm), which helps improve the resolution and treatment of long targets by couch movement. This unique design also enhanced the plan quality, enabling widespread clinical implementation of HT in several types of cancers, including head and neck cancer, and breast cancer [16–18]. For the HT, a binary MLC was mounted with leaves either completely open or closed [19], which might limit the shaping ability of targets compared with high-definition MLC in a conventional linac.

Bedford et al. reported a treatment planning study using helical VMAT for the craniospinal axis with an in-house treatment planning system, Auto Beam [20]. By simulating the movement of the treatment couch, comparable plan quality was achieved with helical VMAT compared to separate VMAT arcs with multiple isocenters. Although this study demonstrated the potential of helical VMAT, it was not clinically available, as a conventional Synergy accelerator was used to illustrate the concept of a helical VMAT.

Recently, a novel NeuRT Aurora (Neusoft IntelliRay Technology, Shenyang, Liaoning, China) was developed to enable simultaneous VMAT with longitudinal couch movement. A 6-MV linac was mounted on a circular gantry, enabling continuous rotation, and a 122-leaf dual-layer MLC was designed for beam shaping and modulation. The system combines the advantages of HT and VMAT. Furthermore, conventional 3D-CRT, IMRT, and VMAT can also be performed using the NeuRT Aurora system with a static treatment couch. As a helical or spiral path of the beam source was generated, this plan was named spiral volumetric modulated arc therapy (SVMAT), which integrates VMAT with couch movement. In present, the SVMAT technology completed its clinical trials in 2023 and received review and approval from China’s National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) by the end of 2023. Currently, three hospitals have completed clinical installation, and clinical treatment is expected to begin within this year.

Due to the advanced design of NeuRT Aurora, which realizes VMAT delivering with moving couch to increase the freedom of optimization, it can potentially improve the quality of complex treatment plans and long-term targets. Whole-brain radiation therapy (WBRT) with simultaneous integrated boost (SIB) is a common modality for treating brain metastases [21–23]. To date, fewer than three studies have focused on radiotherapy for brain metastases [24, 25]. Ma et al. reported that WBRT + SIB was also effective and safe for patients with 3 − 36 metastases [26]. However, the complexity of treatment planning increases with the number of metastases. For patients with multiple metastases, HT was normally implemented in our clinical practice, as conventional dual-arc VMAT could not fulfill the requirements for planning [27]. In our previous study, we proposed piecewise VMAT (PVMAT) technology by limiting the maximum field width to 2.5 cm and using seven piecewise arcs to implement WBRT + SIB for multiple metastases. The plan quality was improved by PVMAT compared with both dual-arc VMAT and HT. However, since seven full arcs were used for PVMAT, the treatment time was longer than that of HT [27].

In this study, the feasibility of using SVMAT for treatment planning of WBRT + SIB with a large number (> 40) of brain metastases was investigated. The HT and PVMAT plans were also compared in terms of dosimetric parameters and treatment efficiency. To our knowledge, this is the first publication outlining treatment planning using SVMAT with a clinically available machine.

Materials and methods

Patient demography

The ethics committee of our institute approved this retrospective study, and the requirement for informed consent was waived. Ten patients with multiple brain metastases who underwent radiotherapy with HT were enrolled. The number of metastases ranged from 40 to 120 (median = 76). The targets included the whole brain and brain metastases, excluding the hippocampus. The OARs were also contoured, including the hippocampus, lens, optic nerve, brain stem, optic chiasm, spinal cord, and pituitary. The details of patient information and contouring are described in Ref. 27.

Treatment machine

NeuRT Aurora (Neusoft IntelliRay Technology, Shenyang, Liaoning, China) is a novel radiotherapy machine featuring a 6-MV FFF linac, a circular gantry, a dual-layer MLC, and a movable treatment couch. Therefore, a helical/spiral arc trajectory can be generated during treatment. With the NeuRT Aurora, conventional 3D-CRT, IMRT, and VMAT techniques were supported by a static treatment couch, and the SVMAT modality was utilized with simultaneous couch movement during beam delivery. There are several differences between NeuRT Aurora and conventional tomotherapy. First, during beam delivery, the treatment couch of NeuRT Aurora can move back and forth, but that of HT can only move forward in one direction. The couch of NeuRT Aurora can move in one direction with multiple gantry rotations, and then move back with same number of gantry rotations. The couch moves with a constant speed during treatment and the maximum speed is 10 cm/s. So far, only VMAT plans are supported with moving couch, which named SVMAT. Furthermore, the dual-layer MLC has 122 leaves, which enables simultaneous motion similar to conventional C-arm linacs. This MLC is mounted on the treatment head, unlike the binary MLC of the HT. The MLC width for Aurora is 5 mm, which is narrower than HT (6.25 mm). So far, the machine is already installed in the hospital, and prepared to treat patients in the near future. Treatment planning

The dosimetric constraints for the targets and OARs are shown in Table 2. The prescribed doses were 40 and 60 Gy for the whole brain and metastases, respectively, which were delivered in 20 fractions. The metastasis dose was de-escalated to 50 Gy if critical OARs were adjacent. The volume of the tissues receiving 110% of the prescribed dose should be kept as low as possible to protect normal brain function. The dose to OARs was optimized according to ALARA (as low as reasonably achievable) principle while ensuring target coverages. Three kinds of technology with similar optimization parameters were implemented for treatment planning: SVMAT, HT, and PVMAT.

SVMAT

SVMAT plans were designed using NeuRT Aurora by adjusting the couch position. The moving path and couch movement are illustrated in Fig. 1. In all SVMAT plans, the patient couch was set to move out firstly after specified multiple gantry rotations and then move in with the same number of gantry rotations within a single treatment fraction. A direct aperture optimization algorithm named DeepOPT for SVMAT was developed using a commercial treatment planning system, DeepPlan (version 1.3, Wisdom Tech., Hefei, Anhui, China). Monte Carlo algorithm was used for dose calculation. The collimator angle was set to 90°, and the maximum field width in the couch-moving direction was set to 2.5 cm for comparison with the HT, as shown in Fig. 2. To explore the optimal number of gantry rotations for the WBRT + SIB plans, SVMAT plans with 3 − 8 rotations for each couch-moving direction (total 6 − 16 rotations) were optimized. The maximum travel distance was set at 15 cm, which was sufficient to cover the length of the planning target volume for the brain (PTV-brain) in the direction of couch movement.

HT

A tomotherapy machine (Tomotherapy Inc., Madison, Wisconsin) was used for treatment planning, utilizing a Hi-Art treatment planning system (version 5.1, Tomotherapy Inc., Madison, Wisconsin). The field width was 2.51 cm with a dynamic jaw, and the modulation factor was 2.6. The pitch was 0.287, and the fine calculation grid was 0.264 cm × 0.264 cm.

PVMAT

The PVMAT plans were reoptimized with NeuRT Aurora and DeepPlan TPS for comparison with SVMAT. As reported in Ref. 27, seven piecewise full arcs were used with the collimator at 90° by limiting the maximum field width to 2.5 cm. As the gantry cannot rotate 180°, the arcs were rotated clockwise and then counterclockwise. To ensure complete coverage between contiguous targets, a 0.5 cm overlap was maintained between piecewise arcs.

Plan evaluation

The dosimetric parameters of the targets, including the conformity indices [29] (CI) of whole-brain target PTV-brain, the homogeneity index [30] (HI) of metastases, and target coverage, were evaluated. Specifically, the V44Gy (the proportion of PTV-brain receiving 110% of the prescribed dose) was used to indicate homogeneity in the whole-brain target. The dosimetric parameters of the critical OARs were also calculated. The monitor units (MU) and beam-delivering time estimated by the TPS for each plan were also compared.

Nelms et al. proposed a plan quality metric (PQM) to evaluate the plan quality and compare it with a unique metric [31]. Plan Quality Metric (PQM) is an objective “score” method to quantify a plan’s quality to compare plans with multiple clinical objectives. In this study, 20 metrics, including those for targets and OARs, were implemented. As shown in Table 3, nine and four points were allocated to each index of the targets and OARs, respectively. X1 represents the lower limit of dosimetric indexes, and X2 represents the upper limit of dosimetric indexes. And Y1 and Y2 represents the minimum and maximum scores corresponding to X1 and X2. The total score was 100, and a higher score represented higher plan quality. Plans with score more than 45 was regarded as good, 30 ~ 45 points was acceptable and score lower than 30 points was rejected. Notably, comparison of the PQM scores was only effective for the same clinical case, and not between different patients.

All data for the SVMAT, HT, and PVMAT groups were analyzed using SPSS version 20.0 (IBM, New York, USA). An independent t-test was used for data with normal distribution. For other data, the non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for statistical analysis. As there were multiple metrics for comparison, the Benjamini–Hochberg correction method was employed to control the false discovery rate (FDR) across multiple comparisons. The FDR level was set at 0.05, and a p-value after multiple testing of < 0.05 represented statistical significance in this study.

Variations in the SVMAT with different gantry rotations

All plans were normalized to cover 95% of GTV and PTV-brain. The total PQM score of the SVMAT with different rotations is shown in Fig. 4. The mean total PQM scores were 51.70 ± 8.14 (mean ± standard deviation), 53.24 ± 5.84, 53.64 ± 7.16, 54.24 ± 7.06, 54.60 ± 6.30, and 55.05 ± 6.18 points for the SVMAT plans with gantry rotations of 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16, respectively. Regarding the grade of plans, 8 plans or 9 plans in ten patients were shown to be ‘good’ (> 45 points) for all SVMAT plans. The average SVMAT score increased with increasing gantry rotations. The average MU increases from 5669 ± 1327 to 7541 ± 2330 with gantry rotation varying from 6 to 16 (Fig. 5). The average beam delivery time also were 271.5 ± 53.7 s, 310.3 ± 42.9 s, 359.6 ± 34.4 s, 419.2 ± 45.5 s, 470.3 ± 32.1 s and 548.1 ± 69.4 s with increasing number of gantry rotations (6, 8,10,12,14 and 16) (Fig. 6).

Comparison of SVMAT and HT

As shown in Tables 4 and 5, there were no significant differences between SVMAT and HT in terms of target coverage and CI index for the GTV and PTV of the brain (p > 0.05). The HI of the GTV was significantly higher for the SVMAT than for the HT plans (p < 0.05). The volume of 110% prescribed dose V44Gy, which should be avoided in the normal brain, was significantly lower when over 12 gantry rotations (12 ~ 16 rotations) were used for SVMAT compared with the HT plans (p < 0.05). The mean score of HT was calculated to be 38.18 ± 5.48 points, which was significantly lower than the SVMAT plans (p < 0.05). For HT, 9 plans were ‘acceptable’ (30 ~ 45 points) and one was ‘rejected’ (< 30 points).

The average MU was 5564 ± 364 for HT (Fig. 5), which was not significantly different than that for SVMAT-6R and SVMAT-12R (p > 0.05). In the SVMAT-14R and SVMAT-16R models, the MU was higher than that of HT as the number of rotations increased (p < 0.05). The average beam delivery time was 393.0 ± 25.0 s for the HT beam, which was longer than that for the SVMAT-6R (271.5 ± 53.7 s), -8R (310.3 ± 42.9 s), and − 10R (359.6 ± 34.4 s) plans (p < 0.05).

Comparison of SVMAT and PVMAT

As shown in Tables 4 and 5, no significant difference was observed between SVMAT and PVMAT in terms of target coverage of the GTV and PTV in the brain (p > 0.05). Furthermore, no significant differences were observed between the CI, HI, and hot spot V44Gy for the SVMAT and PVMAT plans (p > 0.05). The mean PQM score of PVMAT was 54.34 ± 6.48 points, which was not significantly different from that of SVMAT (p > 0.05). For PVMAT, two plans were ‘acceptable’ (30 ~ 45 points) and eight plans were shown to be ‘good’ (> 45 points).

The average MU was 5564 ± 364 for PVMAT (Fig. 5), which was higher than that for SVMAT-6R (p < 0.05). No significant differences in MU were observed between SVMAT-8R and SVMAT-16R (p > 0.05). The beam delivery time was only shorter for SVMAT-6R (271.5 ± 53.7 s) than for PVMAT (312.6 ± 53.7 s) (p < 0.05).

Discussion

SVMAT generates a helical or spiral arc path by moving the treatment couch while rotating the gantry using a dynamic MLC. While the treatment couch in SVMAT can move in and out during beam delivery, it can only move in one direction during HT. A dual-layer MLC used for conventional VMAT was also mounted and compared with the binary MLC of HT. These new designs integrated the merits of VMAT and HT, which can potentially improve the plan quality for complex cases and long targets. In this study, the feasibility of SVMAT for WBRT with SIB was investigated and compared with HT and PVMAT, specifically for patients with multiple brain metastases, which represent a challenging clinical scenario.

There were several techniques to improve the plan quality of brain metastases. For instance, spatiotemporal fractionation scheme may decrease the biological dose to normal brain [32]. Furthermore, another study reported non-isocentric dynamic trajectory optimization to improve the plan quality [33]. In this study, SVMAT with couch traveling was proposed for the treatment of multiple brain metastases. For WBRT + SIB, the plan quality was improved with SVMAT compared to HT, as indicated by the total PQM score. This may be because the dual-layer MLC can enhance shaping and modulation capabilities compared to a binary MLC. Furthermore, the treatment couch can be moved in two directions during beam delivery, which may improve the optimization of the fluence map and, consequently, the plan quality. This result was very similar to that of conventional VMAT, in which dual-arc VMAT typically had a better plan quality than single-arc VMAT [34]. An SVMAT plan, where the couch can only move in a single direction, was also performed with an identical number of gantry rotations. This resulted in poorer plan quality than one where the couch can move back and forth. Therefore, SVMAT with a couch moving in two directions is recommended for plans with high complexity.

The plan quality can be improved by increasing the number of gantry rotations. However, the improvement may not be obvious with a continuous increase in rotations, and the number of rotations should be optimized for efficiency. SVMAT-6R, SVMAT-8R, and SVMAT-10R have a higher plan score compared to HT, as well as less beam delivery time. Hence, the plan quality of SVMAT can be better than HT with high efficiency. In addition to the overall plan score, some critical indices should also be considered during treatment planning. In the PTV-brain, SIB was implemented to increase the local control of metastases. However, hot spots in normal brain tissue should be reduced to protect brain function and reduce the risk of radiation necrosis [35]. As shown in Fig. 7, the hot spot V44Gy first declined with increasing gantry rotations for SVMAT and then reached a plateau. After more than eight rotations, V44Gy was lower compared to HT. Therefore, SVMAT with more than eight rotations is recommended for a volume of 44 Gy.

Pitch, a critical parameter in helical tomotherapy, is defined as the ratio of the couch travel distance per gantry rotation to the field width [36]. For HT, a pitch of 0.86/n, where n is an integer, was commonly used for setup to avoid the thread effect [37]. Moreover, 0.287 was recommended to enhance plan quality and increase treatment efficiency [38]. The DeepPlan treatment planning system cannot directly set the pitch value. Only the total travel distance, the field width in the couch-moving direction, and the number of gantry rotations can be adjusted in the system. For WBRT with SIB, the total travel distance is generally close to the length of the PTV-brain in the couch-moving direction, which is approximately 15 cm. Therefore, the pitch values were calculated for SVMAT with different gantry rotations (Table 6). The SVMAT plans have a higher pitch than the HT ones. For HT, the total number of gantry rotations was approximately 20, with a pitch of 0.287 and a field width of 2.51 cm for WBRT with SIB. Hence, the beam delivery time was somewhat longer than that of SVMAT, with 6 − 10 rotations. However, the plan quality was improved with SVMAT and fewer rotations, which might be partly attributed to the dual-layer MLC. Moreover, SVMAT plans were also attempted without limiting the field width, although the plan quality was poorer than that achieved when the field width was limited. This was similar to PVMAT, in that the modulation and shaping ability might be improved with a shorter traveling distance for MLC in the couch-moving direction.

The plan quality for WBRT with SIB was also compared with PVMAT, which was proposed by limiting the field width and using multiple piecewise arcs [27]. No significant differences were found between SVMAT and PVMAT, indicating that the SVMAT algorithm can achieve results comparable to those of static VMAT with multiple arcs. A possible advantage of SVMAT is that the targets are always close to the isocenter during treatment, whereas for PVMAT, the targets may not be located near the isocenter, which can increase treatment uncertainty [39]. Furthermore, for some machines, the jaw cannot travel across the central line to another direction more than 2 cm in X direction, for instance, the Varian Edge™. Therefore, PVMAT could not be implemented with this kind of machine when collimator was set to 90° as jaw cannot be limited too far from the central line to separate the arcs in some TPS [40].

This study also has some limitations. First, the thread effect in SVMAT is unclear, unlike HT, where the pitch value can be set directly. For SVMAT, the total travel distance, field width in the couch-moving direction, and gantry rotations are set separately, resulting in several combinations of these three parameters for an identical pitch value. Furthermore, a dual-layer MLC with high modulation was used for SVMAT in comparison with the HT with binary MLC. The thread effect may be different with HT and should be further investigated. The optimal field width for SVMAT should also be studied. In this study, 2.5 cm was used to ensure consistency with the HT. However, other field widths may also improve plan quality. Lastly, the number of samples was only 10, which should be increased in the future to improve the statistical results. Regarding the robustness, the machine was already commissioned according to AAPM report 142 and other international documents or guidelines. 12 clinical plans using SVMAT were measured with ArcCHECK, and the mean gamma passing rate was 98.5 ± 1.7% under 2 mm/3% criterion. This indicates that NeuRT Aurora fulfills the clinical requirements for treatment, and reaches comparable accuracy compared with VMAT or HT.

As a new radiotherapy technique, SVMAT still has considerable room for improvement in terms of optimization algorithms, parameter tuning, and clinical applications. This technique can be further advanced through future investigations and optimizations of its clinical and technical aspects.

Conclusions

In this study, SVMAT with varying gantry rotations was investigated for WBRT with SIB and compared with HT and PVMAT. The average PQM score and beam delivery time tend to increase with the number of gantry rotations. The quality of SVMAT plans with higher scores and 8 − 10 gantry rotations was superior to HT. Therefore, by selecting appropriate rotations, the plan quality of SVMAT can be enhanced with less treatment time compared with HT for WBRT with SIB. Furthermore, there were no significant differences in the dosimetric parameters between SVMAT and PVMAT.

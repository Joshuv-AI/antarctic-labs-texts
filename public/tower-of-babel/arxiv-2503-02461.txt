Timelike Compton scattering on a spin-0 target with kinematic twist-4 precision
V. Martínez-Fernández, B. Pire, P. Sznajder, J. Wagner

Abstract
We calculate the kinematic twist-3 and 4 corrections to the leading order amplitude of timelike Compton scattering (TCS) on a (pseudo-)scalar target, in the recently developed framework based on the conformal operator-product expansion. This allows us to compute the complete set of helicity amplitudes of the process, in particular those that vanish at leading twist. We compare the effects of higher twist contributions to TCS with those in deeply virtual Compton scattering (DVCS). Our estimates, based on a π \pi -meson GPD model, indicate that these contributions are sizeable and will play a crucial role in the interpretation of data from current and forthcoming experiments.



I
Introduction

The parton tomography of hadrons is one of the primary goals of quantum chromodynamics (QCD). It is studied through the factorization properties of amplitudes for various exclusive processes, which provide access to generalized parton distributions (GPDs)
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Since exclusive processes have quite low cross sections, they are typically measured in high-luminosity photo- and lepto-production experiments at moderate value of a hard scale (denoted as
\mathbb{Q}
). Interpreting data collected under such conditions requires applying various corrections to leading-order QCD calculations. This is particularly important for describing the hadron’s structure in impact parameter space
[
5
,
6
,
3
]
, which involves a Fourier transform from transverse momentum transfer to the hadron (
\bf\Delta_{\perp}
) to transverse position of the active parton inside the hadron (
\bf b_{\perp}
). Since such a transform cannot be reliably done if the interval in
|\bf\Delta_{\perp}|
is too small, it raises the question of estimating corrections proportional to powers of
\sqrt{|t|}/\mathbb{Q}
, where
t=\Delta^{2}
is the squared momentum transfer to the hadron. Another type of corrections relevant at moderate scales are target mass (
M
) corrections proportional to powers of
M/\mathbb{Q}
.

The aforementioned types of corrections to the scattering amplitudes are known as kinematic twist corrections. For a specific class of processes, works by Braun, Manashov and coworkers
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
have demonstrated that these corrections can be conveniently calculated thanks to the conformal operator-product expansion, taking profit of the conformal symmetry of lowest order QCD. This method was originally applied to estimate kinematic twist corrections to deeply virtual Compton scattering (DVCS)
[
1
,
2
,
12
]
. A similar study was recently performed for a related class of processes involving generalized distribution amplitudes (GDAs)
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

In this work, we follow the strategy based on the conformal operator-product expansion to calculate kinematic twist corrections in the general framework of two-photon scattering, where all-order factorization has recently been proven at next to leading power
[
16
]
. The adopted approach allows us, for the first time, to calculate these corrections for timelike Compton scattering (TCS)
[
17
]
and to compare them with corresponding results previously obtained for DVCS. We note, that in addition to the kinematic twist corrections, there are genuine twist effects, which present a complex and separate task that we do not address here. For a discussion of genuine twists effects, see e.g. Refs.
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

The general framework of two-photon scattering can be defined with the help of double deeply virtual Compton scattering (DDVCS)
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
:

\gamma^{(*)}(q)+N(p)\to\gamma^{(*)}(q^{\prime})+N(p^{\prime})\,,

(1)

where
N
stands for a hadron, and symbols in parentheses denote four-momenta of particles. For DVCS the incoming photon is virtual, while the outgoing one is real (
-q^{2}=Q^{2}>0
,
q^{\prime 2}=Q^{\prime 2}=0
); the other way around for TCS (
Q^{2}=0
,
Q^{\prime 2}>0

DVCS and DDVCS can be measured in lepto-production experiments, while TCS requires a (quasi-)real photon beam (see Fig.
1

\mathbb{Q}^{2}\to\infty

10

]

H_{f}(x,\xi,t)=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\lambda}{2\pi}\ e^{ix(\bar{p}z)}\langle p^{\prime}|\bar{\mathfrak{q}}_{f}(-z/2)\not{n}\mathfrak{q}_{f}(z/2)|p\rangle\big|_{z=\lambda n}\,,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\,,\quad n^{2}=0\,,

(2)

where
x
is the average parton momentum,
\xi
is the skewness,
t
is the usual Mandelstam variable,
\bar{p}=(p+p^{\prime})/2
, and the Wilson line is omitted for simplicity. We note that chiral-odd leading twist quark GPDs do not contribute to Compton processes, while gluon GPDs do not contribute at leading-order (LO) in the strong coupling constant, in which we are interested in this work.

Relaxing the conditions of the Bjorken limit is equivalent to accounting for the contributions to the Compton tensor that are coming from spacetime points slightly away from the light-cone (
z^{2}\neq 0
). Since the scale is no longer infinite, corrections to the light-cone contribution to the Compton tensor translate to kinematic power corrections of the form

\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\right)^{(\tau_{\textrm{kin}}-2)/2}<1\,,\quad\tau_{\textrm{kin}}\geq 2\,,

(3)

where typically
\Lambda^{2}\in\{|t|,M^{2}\}

The goal of our research is to provide the theoretical and phenomenological description of TCS amplitudes at LO including the kinematic twist-3 and twist-4 corrections, off a spin-0 target. This involves considering kinematic effects entering the amplitude with at most a damping factor of the form
|t|/\mathbb{Q}^{2}
or
M^{2}/\mathbb{Q}^{2}

[
7
,

will serve as a test of the correctness of our TCS results through the DDVCS formulation.

Our interest in spin-0 targets is twofold. First, they represent the simplest case, as they can be described by a single chiral-even GPD type. Second, among spin-0 targets, we find helium-4 and pions, both of which are experimentally accessible (the pion through the Sullivan process
[
27
]
). In addition, we note that the first experimental observation of TCS
[
28
]
, together with previous phenomenological studies
[
29
]
, has demonstrated the usefulness and complementarity of DVCS and TCS studies
[
30
]
. It is thus deemed appropriate to improve the theoretical precision of TCS, as it will be studied with great care in the near future at both fixed-target facilities
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
and electron-ion colliders
[
35
,
36
]
.

II
Kinematics

In two-photon processes, information on the interaction with a hadron is concealed in the non-forward matrix elements of the Compton tensor, given by the time-ordering of two currents:

T^{\mu\nu}=i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\langle p^{\prime}|\mathbb{T}\{j^{\mu}(z)j^{\nu}(0)\}|p\rangle\,,

(4)

with
j_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{f}(e_{f}/e)\bar{\mathfrak{q}}_{f}(z)\gamma_{\mu}\mathfrak{q}_{f}(z)
,
f
the flavor index and
e_{f}
the electric charge of quark
f
.

In light-cone coordinates, any four-vector
v
may be written as
v^{\mu}=v^{+}n^{\prime\mu}+v^{-}n^{\mu}+v_{\perp}^{\mu}
, where
n
and
n^{\prime}
span the longitudinal plane and are lightlike vectors satisfying
nn^{\prime}\neq 0
and
nv_{\perp}=n^{\prime}v_{\perp}=0
.

In this manuscript, defining

\bar{p}=\frac{p+p^{\prime}}{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\bar{q}=\frac{q+q^{\prime}}{2}\,,

(5)

we consider a longitudinal plane spanned by
q
and
q^{\prime}
, this is:

q^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2R}n^{\mu}+\frac{RQ^{2}}{\Delta q^{\prime}}n^{\prime\mu}\,,\quad q^{\prime\mu}=\frac{1}{2Q^{2}}\left(1-\frac{qq^{\prime}}{R}\right)n^{\mu}-R\frac{R+qq^{\prime}}{\Delta q^{\prime}}n^{\prime\mu}\,,

(6)

upon

nn^{\prime}=-\Delta q^{\prime}=\frac{t+Q^{2}+Q^{\prime 2}}{2}=\frac{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad R=\sqrt{(qq^{\prime})^{2}+Q^{2}Q^{\prime 2}}\,.

(7)

We have chosen
nn^{\prime}>0
and proportional to the energy scale of DDVCS:

\mathbb{Q}^{2}=Q^{2}+Q^{\prime 2}+t\,.

(8)

This choice for
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
is not imposed but drawn from the structure of the integrals which gives rise to the twist corrections. This will be discussed in detailed in Sect.
IV.2
.

The choice (
6
) for
q
and
q^{\prime}
renders finite vectors in the DVCS and TCS limits, i.e.
Q^{\prime 2}\to 0
and
Q^{2}\to 0
respectively.
We are left with determining
\Delta
and
\bar{p}
. The first one is straightforward:

\Delta^{\mu}=q^{\mu}-q^{\prime\mu}=-\frac{1}{2Q^{2}}\left(1-\frac{Q^{2}+qq^{\prime}}{R}\right)n^{\mu}+\frac{R}{\Delta q^{\prime}}\left(Q^{2}+R+qq^{\prime}\right)n^{\prime\mu}\,,

(9)

while the latter is given by conditions

\Delta\bar{p}=0\,,\quad\xi=-\Delta n/(2\bar{p}n)\,,\quad\bar{p}^{\,2}=M^{2}-t/4\,,

(10)

as

\bar{p}^{\,\mu}=-\frac{R-Q^{2}-qq^{\prime}}{4\xi RQ^{2}}n^{\mu}-\frac{R(R+Q^{2}+qq^{\prime})}{2\xi(\Delta q^{\prime})}n^{\prime\mu}+\bar{p}^{\,\mu}_{\perp}=\frac{\Delta^{-}}{2\xi}n^{\mu}-\frac{\Delta^{+}}{2\xi}n^{\prime\mu}+\bar{p}^{\,\mu}_{\perp}\,.

(11)

Taking into account that
-\bar{p}_{\perp}^{\,2}=|\bar{p}_{\perp}|^{2}\geq 0
, one can get the value of
t
for which its modulus is minimal, namely
t_{0}
, for a fixed value of the skewness:

t_{0}=-\frac{4M^{2}\xi^{2}}{1-\xi^{2}}\qquad(|t|\geq|t_{0}|)\,.

(12)

In turn, the skewness can be expressed through invariants:

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{2R}{2Q^{2}/x_{B}-Q^{2}-Q^{\prime 2}+t}=\frac{\sqrt{(Q^{2}+Q^{\prime 2})^{2}+t^{2}+2t(Q^{2}-Q^{\prime 2})}}{2Q^{2}/x_{B}-Q^{2}-Q^{\prime 2}+t}\,,

(13)

where
x_{B}=Q^{2}/(2pq)
is the usual Bjorken variable. Considering the DVCS limit (
Q^{\prime 2}\to 0
), this expression for the skewness takes the form given in Ref.
[
7
]
.

III
Conformal operator-product expansion à la Braun-Ji-Manashov

We are interested in corrections to the LT amplitudes described by means of GPDs, which are matrix elements of geometric twist-2 operators. Therefore, we consider an expansion of the time-ordering of the two currents in the Compton tensor in a basis of geometric twist-2 operators and their descendants, given by derivatives of the former ones. These operators would eventually be related to GPDs and derivatives of those, giving rise to new convolutions that enter the amplitude with the suppression factors discussed in Eq. (
3
).

The methods detailing how to deal with QCD as a conformal field theory
[
40
,
41
,
42
,
43
]
in this context were developed in Refs.
[
10
,
11
]
by V. M. Braun et al. and they constitute the basis of the work presented here. To ensure the article is self-contained and to establish the conventions used, we summarize in this section some original results that are relevant to our calculation.

At leading order in the strong coupling constant, the Lagrangian of QCD in four dimensions is conformally invariant, up to the gauge-fixing and Faddeev-Popov ghosts terms. For matrix elements of gauge-invariant operators, these terms are irrelevant, validating the application of conformal techniques for them. The Compton tensor is one of this kind and so, for its vector part which is the only one needed for a spin-0 target, it takes the form
[
10
,
11
]
:

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\ i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\langle p^{\prime}|\mathbb{T}\{j^{\mu}(z)j^{\nu}(0)\}|p\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{i\pi^{2}}i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\frac{1}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\left[g^{\mu\nu}\mathscr{O}(1,0)-z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}du\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},0)-z^{\nu}(\partial^{\mu}-i\Delta^{\mu})\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \mathscr{O}(1,v)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{-z^{2}+i0}\left[\frac{i}{2}(\Delta^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}-\Delta^{\mu}\partial^{\nu})\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{{\bar{u}}}dv\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},v)-\frac{t}{4}z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}du\ u\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},v)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{t}{2}\frac{z^{\mu}z^{\nu}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}du\ {\bar{u}}\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle+\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{4(-z^{2}+i0)}\left[\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \mathscr{O}_{1}({\bar{u}},v)-\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \mathscr{O}_{2}(1,v)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4(-z^{2}+i0)}z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \left(1+\frac{4\tau}{{\bar{\tau}}}\right)\mathscr{O}_{2}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{\mu}z^{\nu}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \left[(\ln{\bar{\tau}}+\ln{\bar{u}}+u)\mathscr{O}_{1}({\bar{u}},v)+\left(\frac{v}{{\bar{v}}}+{\bar{u}}\right)\mathscr{O}_{2}({\bar{u}},v)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{z^{\mu}z^{\nu}}{4(-z^{2}+i0)}\left[i\Delta\partial+\frac{t}{2}\right]\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \frac{v}{{\bar{v}}}\left(\frac{2}{{\bar{\tau}}}-1\right)\mathscr{O}_{1}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{\mu}z^{\nu}}{2(-z^{2}+i0)}\left[i\Delta\partial+\frac{t}{4}\right]\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \left(\ln{\bar{\tau}}+\frac{2\tau}{{\bar{\tau}}}\right)\mathscr{O}_{1}({\bar{u}},v)\Bigg\}\,,

(14)

where

\tau=\frac{uv}{{\bar{u}}{\bar{v}}}\,,\quad\bar{\tau}=1-\tau\,,\quad{\bar{u}}=1-u\,,\quad{\bar{v}}=1-v\,,

(15)

while the operators
\mathscr{O}
,
\mathscr{O}_{1}
and
\mathscr{O}_{2}
must be understood as matrix elements
\langle p^{\prime}|\mathscr{O}|p\rangle
,
\langle p^{\prime}|\mathscr{O}_{1}|p\rangle
,
\langle p^{\prime}|\mathscr{O}_{2}|p\rangle
, respectively, defining

\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=(i\Delta\partial_{z})\mathscr{O}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{O}_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})+\frac{t}{2}\mathscr{O}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\,.

(17)

by means of the operator

(for which

:

\mathscr{O}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\sum_{f}\left(\frac{e_{f}}{e}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2}{\left[\bar{\mathfrak{q}}_{f}(\lambda_{1}z)\not{z}\mathfrak{q}_{f}(\lambda_{2}z)-(\lambda_{1}\leftrightarrow\lambda_{2})\right]_{\rm LT}}\,.

(18)

Here,

represents the geometric leading twist projection of the corresponding operator, to be detailed later.

In the spin-0 case, we can extract the quark correlator from the definition of GPD, given by Eq. (

\langle p^{\prime}|\bar{\mathfrak{q}}_{f}(z_{2})\not{n}\mathfrak{q}_{f}(z_{1})|p\rangle=2(\bar{p}n)\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ e^{-i(\bar{p}n)\left[\lambda_{1}(\xi+x)+\lambda_{2}(\xi-x)\right]}H_{f}(x,\xi,t)\,,\quad z_{i}=\lambda_{i}n\,.

(resulting in

. Therefore, we may consider the following non-local operator evaluated at light-cone positions:

where

IV
Helicity-dependent amplitudes

Consider two polarization vectors
\varepsilon^{\mu}(A)
for the incoming photon and
\varepsilon^{\prime\mu}(B)
for the outgoing, where
A,B
labels the polarization:
0
for longitudinal,
\pm 1
for transverse polarization (in the plane of
\bar{p}_{\perp}
). Then, we can defined the so-called helicity-dependent amplitudes
[
45
]
as

\mathcal{A}^{AB}=(-1)^{A-1}(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\mu}(B))^{*}\,T^{\mu\nu}\varepsilon_{\nu}(A)\,.

(26)

They represent the transition amplitude from an incoming photon with polarization
A
to an outgoing one with polarization
B
.

In the case of the opposite polarizations they are related by parity conservation:

\mathcal{A}^{AB}(\rho,\xi,t)=\mathcal{A}^{-A\,-B}(\rho,\xi,t)\,,

(27)

leaving a total of five independent amplitudes:
\mathcal{A}^{++}
,
\mathcal{A}^{+-}
,
\mathcal{A}^{0+}
,
\mathcal{A}^{+0}
and
\mathcal{A}^{00}
.

We would like to parameterize the Compton tensor by means of helicity amplitudes in order to find the corresponding projectors, which we would apply to the all-twists expansion of the Compton tensor given by Eq. (
14
). This way, we can obtain an expression for the amplitudes
\mathcal{A}^{AB}
to the desired twist accuracy, which for us corresponds to the kinematic twist-4.

To parameterize the Compton tensor by means of helicity amplitudes, one can make use of the spin representation of the Lorentz group, cf.
[
46
]
. This way, one can define two spinors, relate them to the lightlike vector
n
,
n^{\prime}
, and make a parametric expansion of any tensor. For further details on this methodology and application to DVCS, see
[
7
]
. An alternative approach was given by Tarrach in Ref.
[
47
]
. With those methods and the polarization vectors given by:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\nu}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{Q(\Delta q^{\prime})}\left[\frac{\Delta q^{\prime}}{2R^{2}}n^{\nu}-Q^{2}n^{\prime\nu}\right]\,,

(28)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime\nu}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{iQ}{2R}\sqrt{\frac{R-qq^{\prime}}{R+qq^{\prime}}}\left[\frac{1}{Q^{2}}n^{\nu}+\frac{2R^{2}}{\Delta q^{\prime}}\frac{R+qq^{\prime}}{R-qq^{\prime}}n^{\prime\nu}\right]\,.

(29)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\nu}(+)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{\,\nu}-i\widetilde{\bar{p}}^{\,\nu}_{\perp}}{\sqrt{2}|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}\,,\quad\widetilde{\bar{p}}^{\,\nu}_{\perp}=\epsilon_{\perp}^{\nu\mu}\bar{p}_{\mu}\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\nu}(-)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{\,\nu}+i\widetilde{\bar{p}}^{\,\nu}_{\perp}}{\sqrt{2}|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}\,,

(31)

the result for the Compton tensor for a spin-0 target (valid for DDVCS, DVCS and TCS) reads:

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{A}^{++}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{A}^{+-}\frac{1}{|\bar{p}_{\perp}|^{2}}\left[\bar{p}^{\,\mu}_{\perp}\bar{p}^{\,\nu}_{\perp}-\widetilde{\bar{p}}^{\,\mu}_{\perp}\widetilde{\bar{p}}^{\,\nu}_{\perp}\right]

\displaystyle+\ \mathcal{A}^{00}\frac{-i}{QQ^{\prime}R^{2}}\left[(qq^{\prime})(Q^{\prime 2}q^{\mu}q^{\nu}-Q^{2}q^{\prime\mu}q^{\prime\nu})+Q^{2}Q^{\prime 2}q^{\mu}q^{\prime\nu}-(qq^{\prime})^{2}q^{\prime\mu}q^{\nu}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{A}^{+0}\frac{i\sqrt{2}}{R|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}\left[Q^{\prime}q^{\mu}-\frac{qq^{\prime}}{Q^{\prime}}q^{\prime\mu}\right]\bar{p}^{\,\nu}_{\perp}-\mathcal{A}^{0+}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{R|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}\bar{p}^{\,\mu}_{\perp}\left[\frac{qq^{\prime}}{Q}q^{\nu}+Qq^{\prime\nu}\right]\,.

(32)

In the LT LO approximation, only the first term survives, and
\mathcal{A}^{++}
reduces to the well-known LO LT Compton form factor
\mathcal{H}

[
22
]
.

IV.1
Projectors onto helicity amplitudes

Close inspection of expression (
32
) allows to define a set of helicity projectors
\Pi^{(AB)}_{\mu\nu}
such that

\Pi^{(AB)}_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{A}^{AB}\,.

(33)

Considering
\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\alpha}q^{\alpha}=\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\alpha}q^{\prime\alpha}=0
, one can define

\displaystyle\Pi^{(0+)}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\mu}q^{\prime}_{\nu}\frac{R}{\sqrt{2}|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}\left[QQ^{\prime 2}+\frac{(qq^{\prime})^{2}}{Q}\right]^{-1}=\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\mu}q^{\prime}_{\nu}\frac{Q}{\sqrt{2}|\bar{p}_{\perp}|R}\,,

(34)

\displaystyle\Pi^{(+0)}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-q_{\mu}\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\nu}\frac{iR}{\sqrt{2}|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}\left[Q^{\prime}Q^{2}+\frac{(qq^{\prime})^{2}}{Q^{\prime}}\right]^{-1}=-q_{\mu}\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\nu}\frac{iQ^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}|\bar{p}_{\perp}|R}\,.

(35)

Using
\widetilde{\bar{p}}_{\perp}^{\,2}=\bar{p}_{\perp}^{\,2}=-|\bar{p}_{\perp}|^{2}
:

\Pi^{(++)}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2|\bar{p}_{\perp}|^{2}}\left(\bar{p}_{\perp,\mu}\bar{p}_{\perp,\nu}+\widetilde{\bar{p}}_{\perp,\mu}\widetilde{\bar{p}}_{\perp,\nu}\right)=-\frac{g_{\perp,\,\mu\nu}}{2}\,,

(36)

and with
\bar{p}_{\perp}\cdot\widetilde{\bar{p}}_{\perp}=\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}_{\perp}\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\nu}=0
:

\Pi^{(+-)}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2|\bar{p}_{\perp}|^{2}}\left(\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\mu}\bar{p}_{\perp,\,\nu}-\widetilde{\bar{p}}_{\perp,\,\mu}\widetilde{\bar{p}}_{\perp,\,\nu}\right)\,.

(37)

Finally, for amplitude
\mathcal{A}^{00}
we can only use a projector made out of longitudinal vectors, i.e.
q,q^{\prime}
. We can make it antisymmetric to obtain the simplest of the projectors. This way,

\Pi^{(00)}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{i2QQ^{\prime}}{R^{2}}q_{[\mu}q^{\prime}_{\nu]}\,,

(38)

where
q_{[\mu}q^{\prime}_{\nu]}=(q_{\mu}q^{\prime}_{\nu}-q_{\nu}q^{\prime}_{\mu})/2
.

In what follows, we will use this projectors to compute the different helicity amplitudes from the Compton tensor in Eq. (
14
) of a spin-0 target. Taking the appropriate small virtuality limit, from the DDVCS results we will obtain the DVCS (already published in
[
11
]
) and the TCS ones (to the best of our knowledge, firstly published here).

IV.2
Transverse-helicity conserving amplitude,
\mathcal{A}^{++}

In the current section we present the calculation of the transverse helicity-conserving amplitude
\mathcal{A}^{++}
up to kinematic twist-4 accuracy. To achieve this, we apply the projector
\Pi^{(++)}_{\mu\nu}
from Eq. (
36
) to the Compton tensor decomposition
T^{\mu\nu}
in Eq. (
14
). All antysymmetric terms, as well as those proportional to
q,q^{\prime}
, or
\Delta
, are washed out.

Amplitude
\mathcal{A}^{++}
will be the only one with a contribution at kinematic leading-twist (twist-2) as its corresponding Lorentz structure is the transverse metric,
g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}
, vid. Eq. (
32
). The structure of the LT contribution allows us to define a new invariant related to the
generalized Bjorken variable
previously introduced by Belitsky and Müller in Ref.
[
22
]
.

IV.2.1
LT component, energy scale of DDVCS and generalized Bjorken variable

The leading twist contribution to the amplitude of DDVCS originates from the most singular term (
\sim 1/z^{4}
), which appears in the first line of Eq. (
14
):

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{A}^{++}\right|_{\rm LT}\subset

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{\perp,\,\mu\nu}}{2i\pi^{2}}i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\frac{1}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\Bigg[g^{\mu\nu}\mathscr{O}(1,0)-z^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}\int_{0}^{1}du\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},0)-z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \mathscr{O}(1,v)\Bigg]\,.

(39)

Here, the symbol
\subset
(read as “contained in”) reflects the fact that the RHS includes not only the LT component of the amplitude but higher-order corrections too.

The application of the expression (
24
) results in the following Fourier transforms:

i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\frac{{\left[e^{-i\ell z}\right]_{\rm LT}}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\,,\quad i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\frac{z^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}{\left[e^{-i\ell z}\right]_{\rm LT}}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\,,

(40)

where
\ell
is a shorthand for the general

\ell_{\lambda_{1},\,\lambda_{2}}=-\lambda_{1}\Delta-\lambda_{12}\left[\beta\bar{p}-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+1)\Delta\right]\,,

(41)

and
{\left[\ \right]_{\rm LT}}
stands for the geometric LT projection given by Eq. (
25
). This projection renders a series of integrals of the form:

I_{n,m}=\int_{0}^{1}dw\ \frac{w^{n}}{(aw^{2}+bw+c)^{m}}\,,

(42)

where

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\ell^{2}=\lambda_{1}^{2}t+\lambda_{12}^{2}\left[\beta^{2}\bar{p}^{\,2}+\frac{1}{4}(\alpha+1)^{2}t\right]-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{12}(\alpha+1)t\,,

(43)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=-2q^{\prime}\ell=-2(q^{\prime}\Delta)\left[\lambda_{12}\frac{\beta}{2\Omega}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{2}(\alpha+1)-\lambda_{1}\right]\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta q^{\prime}}{2\bar{p}q^{\prime}}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=Q^{\prime 2}+i0\,.

(46)

Taking into account that
\ell^{2}=O(|t|,M^{2})
, and
b\propto-2q^{\prime}\Delta=Q^{2}+Q^{\prime 2}+t
, we notice that the twist expansion shall be done in powers of

\frac{a}{b}=O(\textrm{tw-4})\,,\quad\textrm{whereas}\quad\frac{c}{b}=O(1)\,.

(47)

Therefore, we identify the hard scale for a general Compton process to be

\mathbb{Q}^{2}=Q^{2}+Q^{\prime 2}+t\,.

(48)

To kinematic twist-4 accuracy, the Fourier transforms of Eq. (
40
) read:

i\int d^{4}z\ \frac{e^{iq^{\prime}z}{\left[e^{-i\ell z}\right]_{\rm LT}}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}=\underbrace{-\pi^{2}\ln\left(\frac{b+c}{-\mu^{2}}\right)}_{\mathclap{\textrm{LT term}}}-\pi^{2}\frac{a}{b+c}+\pi^{2}\frac{a}{b}+\pi^{2}\frac{ac}{b^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{c}{b+c}\right)+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(49)

where
\mu^{2}
stands for the renormalization scale in dimensional regularization, and

\displaystyle i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\frac{z^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}{\left[e^{-i\ell z}\right]_{\rm LT}}=

\displaystyle\ g^{\mu\nu}\pi^{2}\left[\frac{a}{b}+\frac{ac}{b^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{c}{b+c}\right)\right]-2\pi^{2}\frac{\ell^{\nu}\ell^{\mu}}{b+c}+(\textrm{terms}\sim q^{\prime})+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,.

(50)

The first logarithm in Eq. (
49
) is the only LT component, hence:

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{A}^{++}\right|_{\rm LT}\subset

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{\perp,\,\mu\nu}}{2i\pi^{2}}2i\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ \Phi^{(+)}\left.g^{\mu\nu}\left[-\pi^{2}\ln\left(\frac{b+c}{-\mu^{2}}\right)\right]\right|_{\ell=\ell_{1,0}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ 2\Phi^{(+)}\left.\ln\left(\frac{b+c}{-\mu^{2}}\right)\right|_{\ell=\ell_{1,0}}\,,

(51)

where
\Phi^{(+)}=\Phi^{(+)}(\beta,\alpha,t)
.

To map the function
\Phi^{(+)}
to the corresponding GPD
H^{(+)}
we need to introduce the identity
1=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)
in
\mathcal{A}^{++}
. This identity leads to

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{A}^{++}\right|_{\rm LT}\subset

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dx\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ 2\Phi^{(+)}\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)\left.\ln\left(\frac{b+c}{-\mu^{2}}\right)\right|_{\ell=\ell_{1,0}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dx\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ 2\Phi^{(+)}\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)

\displaystyle\times\ln\left(\frac{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}{-2\mu^{2}}\left[\frac{x-\xi}{\xi}+\frac{2Q^{\prime 2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}+i0\right]\left[1+\frac{\beta\left(\frac{1}{\Omega}-\frac{1}{\xi}\right)}{\frac{x-\xi}{\xi}+\frac{2Q^{\prime 2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}+i0}\right]\right)\,.

(52)

One can show that the term proportional to
\beta
in the above equation is a twist-4 component. To the LT we can omit this factor. Due to the property (
96
), the result does not depend on the renormalization scale
\mu
. The structure of this solution motivates the definition of a new variable named
generalized Bjorken variable
and denoted as
\rho
:

\frac{x-\xi}{\xi}+\frac{2Q^{\prime 2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}=\frac{x-\rho}{\xi}\Rightarrow\rho=\xi\frac{qq^{\prime}}{\Delta q^{\prime}}\,,

(53)

which can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\xi\frac{Q^{2}-Q^{\prime 2}+t}{Q^{2}+Q^{\prime 2}+t}=\xi_{\textrm{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{Belitsky:2003fj}{}{}]}}}+\frac{\xi t}{2\mathbb{Q}^{2}}+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,.

(54)

The variable
\xi_{\textrm{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{Belitsky:2003fj}{}{}]}}}
is the generalized Bjorken variable as introduced by Belitsky and Müller in Ref.
[
22
]
. To avoid confusion with the skewness, we denote it as
\rho
following notation in
[
26
]
. To LT accuracy all definitions agree.

Employing the definition of
\rho
, and making use of the properties of the DD (summarized in the Appendix
A
), to kinematic twist-2 accuracy the amplitude reads:

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{A}^{++}\right|_{\rm LT}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ 2\Phi^{(+)}\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)\ln\left(\frac{x-\rho}{\xi}+i0\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \left(\partial_{x}H^{(+)}\right)\ln\left(\frac{x-\rho}{\xi}+i0\right)=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \frac{1}{\rho-x-i0}H^{(+)}(x,\xi,t)\,,

(55)

which agrees with the usual LT approximation, see for example
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
.

IV.2.2
Final result up to twist-4, and the DVCS and TCS limits

To twist-4, we have contributions coming from the following integrals:

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{A}^{++}\right|_{\textrm{tw-4}}\supset

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{\perp,\,\mu\nu}}{2i\pi^{2}}i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\Bigg[g^{\mu\nu}\mathscr{O}(1,0)-z^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}\int_{0}^{1}du\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},0)-z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \mathscr{O}(1,v)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{t}{2}\frac{z^{\nu}z^{\mu}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}du\ {\bar{u}}\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{4(-z^{2}+i0)}\left[\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \mathscr{O}_{1}({\bar{u}},v)-\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \mathscr{O}_{2}(1,v)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{z^{\nu}z^{\mu}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \left[(\ln{\bar{\tau}}+\ln{\bar{u}}+u)\mathscr{O}_{1}({\bar{u}},v)+\left(\frac{v}{{\bar{v}}}+{\bar{u}}\right)\mathscr{O}_{2}({\bar{u}},v)\right]\Bigg\}\,.

(56)

Solving the Fourier transforms first, expanding in powers of
a/b
(taking into account that
c/b=O(1)
) afterwards, and integrating over
u
and
v
, we finally obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{++}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \Bigg\{-\left(1-\frac{t}{2\mathbb{Q}^{2}}+\frac{t(\xi-\rho)}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\right)\frac{H^{(+)}}{x-\rho+i0}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{t}{\xi\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\Bigg[\mathbb{P}_{\rm(i)}+\mathbb{P}_{\rm(ii)}-\frac{\xi L}{2}+\frac{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(iii)}-\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(i)}}{2}

\displaystyle\ \phantom{+\frac{t}{\xi\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\Bigg[}-\frac{\xi}{x+\xi}\Bigg({\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\rho+i0}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}-\frac{\xi+\rho}{2\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{-\xi-\rho+i0}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}-\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(i)}\Bigg)\Bigg]H^{(+)}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\Bigg[\Bigg(\mathbb{P}_{\rm(i)}+\mathbb{P}_{\rm(ii)}-\frac{\xi L}{2}

\displaystyle\ \phantom{-\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\Bigg[}-\frac{\xi}{x+\xi}\Bigg({\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\rho+i0}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}-\frac{\xi+\rho}{2\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{-\xi-\rho+i0}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}-\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(i)}\Bigg)\Bigg)H^{(+)}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}2\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\Bigg[\Bigg(\mathbb{P}_{\rm(i)}+\mathbb{P}_{\rm(ii)}-\frac{\xi L}{2}+\frac{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(iii)}+\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(i)}}{2}\Bigg)H^{(+)}\Bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\ +O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(57)

where the following functions were defined:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\rm(i)}(x/\xi,\rho/\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi-\rho}{x-\xi}{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{x-\xi}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}\,,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(i)}(x/\xi,\rho/\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\xi-\rho}{x-\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\rho+i0}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}\,,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\rm(ii)}(x/\xi,\rho/\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi-\rho}{x+\xi}\left[{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{x-\xi}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}-(x\to-\xi)\right]\,,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(iii)}(x/\xi,\rho/\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\xi+\rho}{x+\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\rho+i0}{-\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}\,,

(58)

L(x,\xi,\rho)=\int_{0}^{1}dw\ \frac{-4}{x-\xi-w(x+\xi)}\int_{0}^{1}du\ {\,\textrm{ln}\left(1+\frac{{\bar{u}}(x-\xi-w(x+\xi))}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}\left[{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{{\bar{u}}(1-w)}{1-{\bar{u}}w}\right)}+\frac{1}{1-{\bar{u}}w}\right]\,.

(59)

The expression for
\mathcal{A}^{++}
in Eq. (
57
) is finite for both the DVCS (
Q^{\prime 2}\to 0
) and the TCS (
Q^{2}\to 0
) limits. In particular, for DVCS we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{++}_{\textrm{DVCS}}=

\displaystyle\ \lim_{\rho\to\xi}\mathcal{A}^{++}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \Bigg\{-\left(1-\frac{t}{2\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\right)\frac{H^{(+)}}{x-\xi+i0}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{2t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{x+\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\xi+i0}{-2\xi+i0}\right)}+\frac{L_{\rm DVCS}}{4}\Bigg]H^{(+)}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\Bigg[\Bigg(\frac{\xi}{x+\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\xi+i0}{-2\xi+i0}\right)}+\frac{\xi L_{\rm DVCS}}{2}\Bigg)H^{(+)}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}2\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\Bigg[\Bigg(\frac{\xi}{x+\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\xi+i0}{-2\xi+i0}\right)}+\frac{\xi L_{\rm DVCS}}{2}\Bigg)H^{(+)}\Bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\ +O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(60)

where the scale of DVCS is simply
\mathbb{Q}^{2}=Q^{2}+t
and
L_{\textrm{DVCS}}
is given by

\displaystyle L_{\textrm{DVCS}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\rho\to\xi}L

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{x-\xi}\left[{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{x+\xi}{2\xi-i0}\right)}-{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(1\right)}\right]\,.

(61)

Equation (
60
) matches that of Ref.
[
11
]
taking into account that the GPD used there (
H_{q\,\textrm{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{Braun:2022qly}{}{}]}}}
) relates to the one we employ here (
H^{(+)}
) via:
\sum_{q}e_{q}^{2}H_{q\,\textrm{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{Braun:2022qly}{}{}]}}}=H^{(+)}/2
, where
e_{q}
is the fractional electric charge of the quark of flavor
q
.

In turn, the TCS limit of Eq. (
57
) corresponds to:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{++}_{\textrm{TCS}}=

\displaystyle\ \lim_{Q^{2}\to 0}\mathcal{A}^{++}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \Bigg\{-\frac{1}{x+\xi(1-2t/\mathbb{Q}^{2})+i0}H^{(+)}+\frac{t}{2\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\frac{1}{x+\xi+i0}H^{(+)}-\frac{2t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\xi\partial_{\xi}\Bigg(\frac{1}{x+\xi+i0}H^{(+)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\ -\frac{2t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\Bigg[-\frac{1}{x-\xi}{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{x-\xi}{2\xi+i0}\right)}-\frac{1}{x+\xi}\Bigg({\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{x-\xi}{2\xi+i0}\right)}-{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(1\right)}\Bigg)+\frac{L_{\textrm{TCS}}}{4}\Bigg]H^{(+)}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\Bigg[\Bigg(-\frac{2\xi}{x-\xi}{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{x-\xi}{2\xi+i0}\right)}-\frac{2\xi}{x+\xi}\Bigg({\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{x-\xi}{2\xi+i0}\right)}-{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(1\right)}\Bigg)+\frac{\xi L_{\textrm{TCS}}}{2}

\displaystyle\ \phantom{-\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\Bigg[}+\frac{\xi}{x-\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x+\xi+i0}{2\xi+i0}\right)}\Bigg)H^{(+)}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}2\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\Bigg[\Bigg(-\frac{2\xi}{x-\xi}{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{x-\xi}{2\xi+i0}\right)}-\frac{2\xi}{x+\xi}\Bigg({\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(-\frac{x-\xi}{2\xi+i0}\right)}-{\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(1\right)}\Bigg)+\frac{\xi L_{\textrm{TCS}}}{2}

\displaystyle\ \phantom{+\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}2\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\Bigg[}+\frac{\xi}{x-\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x+\xi+i0}{2\xi+i0}\right)}\Bigg)H^{(+)}\Bigg]\Bigg\}+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(62)

with the scale of TCS given by
\mathbb{Q}^{2}=Q^{\prime 2}+t
. The evaluation at
Q^{2}=0
renders a value of
\rho
that to the twist-4 accuracy can be approximated to
\rho\simeq-\xi(1-2t/\mathbb{Q}^{2})
.
The integral
L_{\textrm{TCS}}
is given by taking the small incoming virtuality limit of Eq. (
59
).

It is easy to see that at LT accuracy one recovers the usual result:

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{A}^{++}_{\textrm{TCS}}\right|_{\textrm{LT}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{Q^{\prime\,2}\to\infty}\mathcal{A}^{++}_{\textrm{TCS}}=-\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \frac{H^{(+)}}{x+\xi+i0}\,.

(63)

IV.3
Transverse-helicity flip amplitude,
\mathcal{A}^{+-}

In this section we describe the transverse-helicity flip amplitude, denoted as
\mathcal{A}^{+-}
. At LO, this amplitude appears as a kinematic higher-twist correction, whereas at NLO and LT accuracy it receives contributions from the gluon transversity GPD
[
48
]
. Since we work at zeroth order in
\alpha_{s}
, the contribution detailed here will start at kinematic twist-4.

Due to the form of the corresponding projector,
\Pi^{(+-)}_{\mu\nu}
(
37
), terms proportional to the metric, to longitudinal vectors or to an antisymmetric tensor vanish. Consequently, at the twist-4 level, only the terms
\sim z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},0)
and
\sim z^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}\mathscr{O}(1,v)
in the first line of the Compton tensor (
14
) contribute. After integration by parts, we get:

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{A}^{+-}\right|_{\textrm{tw-4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i\pi^{2}}i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\,\Pi^{(+-)}_{\mu\nu}\frac{-4z^{\mu}z^{\nu}}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{3}}\left[\int_{0}^{1}du\ \mathscr{O}(\bar{u},0)+\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \mathscr{O}(1,v)\right]

\displaystyle=-4\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ \Phi^{(+)}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{\bar{u}}\ \left.\frac{\Pi^{(+-)}_{\mu\nu}\ell^{\mu}\ell^{\nu}}{(q^{\prime}-\ell)^{2}+i0}\right|_{\ell=\ell_{{\bar{u}},0}}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dv}{\bar{v}}\ \left.\frac{\Pi^{(+-)}_{\mu\nu}\ell^{\mu}\ell^{\nu}}{(q^{\prime}-\ell)^{2}+i0}\right|_{\ell=\ell_{1,v}}\right)\,.

(64)

Making use of the functional
\mathbb{I}_{2}
in App.
B
, we finally obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{+-}=

\displaystyle-4\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}(\xi^{2}\partial_{\xi})^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dx}{2\xi}\ \left(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(iii)}-\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(i)}+2{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\rho+i0}{-2\xi}\right)}\right)H^{(+)}+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(65)

where functions
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(i)}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(iii)}
are collected in Eqs. (
58
). The DVCS and TCS limits of
\mathcal{A}^{+-}
up to twist-4 are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{+-}_{\textrm{DVCS}}=-4\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}(\xi^{2}\partial_{\xi})^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dx}{2\xi}\ \frac{2x}{x+\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\xi+i0}{-2\xi}\right)}H^{(+)}+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{+-}_{\textrm{TCS}}=-4\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}(\xi^{2}\partial_{\xi})^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dx}{2\xi}\ \frac{2x}{x-\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x+\xi+i0}{2\xi}\right)}H^{(+)}+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,.

(67)

Our calculation of the DVCS amplitude above as a limiting case of DDVCS agrees with the earlier publication
[
11
]
. Note that the two limits are related by

\mathcal{A}^{+-}_{\textrm{TCS}}=-\left(\mathcal{A}^{+-}_{\textrm{DVCS}}\right)^{*}=\left.\left(\mathcal{A}^{+-}_{\textrm{DVCS}}\right)\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\to-\xi\\
Q^{2}\leftrightarrow-Q^{\prime\,2}\end{subarray}}\,,

(68)

where
Q^{2}\leftrightarrow-Q^{\prime 2}
is a consequence of going from TCS (with a timelike virtuality) to DVCS (with a spacelike one), and
\xi\to-\xi
is expected due to time reversal. This transformation would also change
\mathcal{A}^{+-}
to
\mathcal{A}^{-+}
but, thanks to parity conservation, they are equal. In Eq. (
68
), we took into account that

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{i\pi^{2}}i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}\,\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{(0+,\,+0)}

\displaystyle\ \times\Bigg\{\frac{1}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\left[z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}du\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},0)+z^{\nu}(\partial^{\mu}-i\Delta^{\mu})\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \mathscr{O}(1,v)\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{+0}=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dx\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ 4\Phi^{(+)}\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)\frac{i\beta Q^{\prime}|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}{\sqrt{2}\mathbb{Q}^{2}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\times\Bigg[}-\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \frac{2\xi}{{\bar{u}}(x-\xi)-v(x+\xi)+\xi-\rho+i0}\Bigg]+O(\textrm{tw-5})

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{0+}=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dx\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ 4\Phi^{(+)}\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)\frac{-\beta Q|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}{\sqrt{2}\mathbb{Q}^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \times\Bigg[\int_{0}^{1}du\ \frac{{\bar{u}}(x-\xi)}{{\bar{u}}(x-\xi)+\xi-\rho+i0}+\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \frac{{\bar{v}}(x+\xi)-2\rho}{{\bar{v}}(x+\xi)-\xi-\rho+i0}

Solving the integrals with respect to

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{+0}

\displaystyle=\frac{-iQ^{\prime}|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}{\sqrt{2}\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ 4\xi^{2}\partial_{\xi}\left(\frac{1}{x-\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\rho+i0}{\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}H^{(+)}\right)+O(\textrm{tw-5})\,,

(75)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{0+}

\displaystyle=\frac{-Q|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}{\sqrt{2}\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ 4\xi^{2}\partial_{\xi}\left(\frac{1}{x+\xi}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x-\rho+i0}{-\xi-\rho+i0}\right)}H^{(+)}\right)+O(\textrm{tw-5})\,.

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dx\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ 4\Phi^{(+)}\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)\frac{Q}{\sqrt{2}R|\bar{p}_{\perp}|}\left.\int_{0}^{1}d{\bar{v}}\ \frac{[(\ell q^{\prime})-Q^{\prime 2}](\ell\bar{p}_{\perp})}{{\bar{v}}(a+b+c)}\right|_{\ell=\ell_{1,v}}

VI
Conclusions

By following the strategy based on the conformal operator-product expansion, we estimated the size of kinematic twist-3 and 4 corrections to the TCS process on a spin-0 target. Working within the general framework of two-photon scattering allowed us to compare our results to those for DVCS and to provide some insights into DDVCS as well. We found that the corrections are by no means negligible and they should therefore be taken into account in the extraction of GPDs from scattering cross-sections. On the other hand, the measurement of specific asymmetries, which vanish at leading twist but not at kinematic twist-3 and 4, such as those originating from
\mathcal{A}^{+-}
and
\mathcal{A}^{0+}
helicity amplitudes, provides a new way to access the same GPDs.

In this work, we did not attempt to write the corresponding amplitudes for a nucleon target. However, most of the analytical work presented here will be useful for that case. Extending the study to a spin-1 target, such as the deuteron, would also be valuable since deuteron GPDs have very interesting new facets
[
52
,
53
]
, and the DVCS and TCS processes on the deuteron should soon be experimentally accessible
[
54
]
.

We note that many questions related to the line of research we are pursuing here remain open. On the theory side, since NLO corrections have been shown to be quite sizable
[
55
]
, the extension of our calculation of the kinematical corrections to an amplitude calculated at NLO is obviously needed. Although corrections to the Born approximation render QCD non-conformal, the conformal techniques can still be useful by considering the fixed points of the
\beta
-function and the renormalization group equations
[
9
]
. In addition, a reliable extraction of GPDs from data will definitely require trustable estimates of genuine higher twist corrections to the leading-twist analysis, which may be aided by lattice computations of higher twist GPDs (see for instance Ref.
[
56
]
). On the experimental side, one should carefully study the phenomenological implications of higher twist effects for DVCS and TCS observables. For spin-0 targets, one should not overlook the
{}^{4}\mathrm{He}
nucleus, as related experimental data are already available for DVCS
[
57
]
. We hope that some of these open questions will be resolved before the next generation of experiments
[
35
,
36
,
34
]
provides a rich harvest of data on exclusive reactions.

k=1

\beta\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)=(x\partial_{x}+\xi\partial_{\xi})\theta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)\,,

(107)

which together with

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}d\beta d\alpha\ \theta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)\Phi^{(+)}(\beta,\alpha,t)=\frac{1}{2}H^{(+)}(x,\xi,t)\,,

(108)

allows to write

\mathbb{I}_{1}[f]=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \frac{f}{\xi}(x\partial_{x}+\xi\partial_{\xi})H^{(+)}(x,\xi,t)\,.

(109)

Integration by parts plus the relation

\partial_{\xi}f(x/\xi,\rho/\xi)=-\frac{x}{\xi}\partial_{x}f(x/\xi,\rho/\xi)\,,

(110)

which can be used to trade
\partial_{\xi}
by
\partial_{x}
in order to solve
\int dx
, renders

\mathbb{I}_{1}[f]=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \left[\partial_{\xi}\left(fH^{(+)}\right)-\frac{f}{\xi}H^{(+)}\right]\,.

(111)

For
k=2
, note that

\beta^{2}\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)=(x^{2}\partial_{x}+2x\xi\partial_{\xi})\theta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)+\xi^{2}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\int_{-1}^{x}dx^{\prime}\ \theta(x^{\prime}-\beta-\alpha\xi)\,,

(112)

and that we can exchange the order of integration over
x
and
x^{\prime}
variables via

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dx\int_{-1}^{x}dx^{\prime}\ C(x)H^{(+)}(x^{\prime},\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}dx^{\prime}\int_{x^{\prime}}^{1}dx\ C(x)H^{(+)}(x^{\prime},\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ H^{(+)}(x,\xi,t)\int_{x}^{1}dx^{\prime}\ C(x^{\prime})\,,

(113)

for any function
C(x)
. After integration by parts, these formulas together with Eqs. (
108
) and (
110
) yield:

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{2}[f]

\displaystyle=\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \frac{H^{(+)}(x,\xi,t)}{\xi}\int_{x}^{1}dx^{\prime}\ f(x^{\prime}/\xi,\rho/\xi)

(114)

\displaystyle=\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\ \frac{\mathcal{Y}[f]H^{(+)}}{\xi}\,,

(115)

where we identified
\mathcal{Y}[f]
as in Eq. (
106
).

Appendix C
Longitudinal-helicity conserving amplitude,
\mathcal{A}^{00}

Although the
\mathcal{A}^{00}
amplitude does not exist in either the TCS or DVCS limit, we write here for completeness its twist-
4
contribution to the DDVCS scattering amplitude.
The projector onto
\mathcal{A}^{00}
, vid. Eq. (
38
), is an antisymmetric tensor built out of the longitudinal vectors
q
and
q^{\prime}
. Hence, when contracted with the projector, transverse momenta and symmetric tensors vanish. Therefore, the longitudinal-helicity conserving amplitude takes the form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{00}=

\displaystyle\Pi^{(00)}_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=\Pi^{(00)}_{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{i\pi^{2}}i\int d^{4}z\ e^{iq^{\prime}z}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\frac{-1}{(-z^{2}+i0)^{2}}\left[z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}du\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},0)+z^{\nu}(\partial^{\mu}-i\Delta^{\mu})\int_{0}^{1}dv\ \mathscr{O}(1,v)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{-z^{2}+i0}\left[i\Delta^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},v)-\frac{t}{4}z^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\int_{0}^{1}du\ u\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \mathscr{O}({\bar{u}},v)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{iz^{\mu}\Delta^{\nu}}{4(-z^{2}+i0)}\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \left[C_{(1),\,z\Delta}({\bar{u}},v)\mathscr{O}_{1}({\bar{u}},v)+C_{(2),\,z\Delta}({\bar{u}},v)\mathscr{O}_{2}({\bar{u}},v)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{z^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}}{-z^{2}+i0}\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ \left[C_{(1),\,z\partial}({\bar{u}},v)\mathscr{O}_{1}({\bar{u}},v)+C_{(2),\,z\partial}({\bar{u}},v)\mathscr{O}_{2}({\bar{u}},v)\right]\Bigg\}\,.

(116)

where we defined the following conformal weights:

\displaystyle C_{(1),\,z\Delta}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle={\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{{\bar{u}}-v}{{\bar{u}}(1-v)}\right)}+2\frac{(1-{\bar{u}})v}{{\bar{u}}-v}+2\frac{v}{1-v}\left(1+\frac{2(1-{\bar{u}})v}{{\bar{u}}-v}\right)\,,

\displaystyle C_{(2),\,z\Delta}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle=\frac{v}{1-v}-2\frac{{\bar{u}}v}{{\bar{u}}-v}-\left(\frac{v}{1-v}\right)^{2}-\frac{v}{1-v}\delta(1-{\bar{u}})\,,

\displaystyle C_{(1),\,z\partial}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1-{\bar{u}})v}{{\bar{u}}-v}-\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(1+\frac{2(1-{\bar{u}})v}{{\bar{u}}-v}\right)\frac{v}{1-v}+\ln{\bar{u}}+1-{\bar{u}}\right]\,,

\displaystyle C_{(2),\,z\partial}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle=\frac{v}{{\bar{u}}-v}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{v}{1-v}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{v}{1-v}\delta(1-{\bar{u}})-\frac{1}{2}{\bar{u}}+\frac{1}{4}\,.

(117)

Amplitude
\mathcal{A}^{00}
may be decomposed as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{00}=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{A}^{00}_{(0),\,z\partial}+\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(0),\,z(\partial-i\Delta)}+\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(0),\,\Delta\partial}+\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(0),\,tz\partial}+\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(1),\,z\Delta}+\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(2),\,z\Delta}+\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(1),\,z\partial}+\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(2),\,z\partial}\,,

(118)

where after Fourier transforms, power expansion up to twist-4 and the use of the functionals described in App.
B
, the terms on the RHS are expressed as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(0),\,z\partial}=2\frac{iQQ^{\prime}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1}du\ \frac{\xi-\rho}{2\xi}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Bigg\{\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\Bigg[3N({\bar{u}},0)-4(\xi-\rho)(N({\bar{u}},0))^{2}+2(\xi-\rho)^{2}(N({\bar{u}},0))^{3}-4\widetilde{N}({\bar{u}},0)\Bigg]H^{(+)}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\left(\xi\left[2N({\bar{u}},0)-4\widetilde{N}({\bar{u}},0)\right]H^{(+)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}2\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\left(\xi\left[2N({\bar{u}},0)-4\widetilde{N}({\bar{u}},0)\right]H^{(+)}\right)\Bigg\}+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(119)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(0),\,z(\partial-i\Delta)}=2\frac{iQQ^{\prime}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1}dv\

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Bigg\{\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\xi+2\rho}{\xi}N(1,v)+(\xi-\rho)\left(\frac{2(\xi-\rho)}{\xi}-3\right)(N(1,v))^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{(\xi-\rho)^{2}(\xi+\rho)}{\xi}(N(1,v))^{3}-\frac{2\rho}{\xi}\widetilde{N}(1,v)\Bigg]H^{(+)}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\left(\left[(\xi+\rho)N(1,v)-2\rho\widetilde{N}(1,v)\right]H^{(+)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}2\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\left(\left[(\xi+\rho)N(1,v)-2\rho\widetilde{N}(1,v)\right]H^{(+)}\right)\Bigg\}+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(120)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(0),\,\Delta\partial}=2\frac{iQQ^{\prime}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Bigg\{\frac{-2t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\left[N({\bar{u}},v)-(\xi-\rho)(N({\bar{u}},v))^{2}-2\widetilde{N}({\bar{u}},v)\right]H^{(+)}-\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\partial_{\xi}\left(4\xi\widetilde{N}({\bar{u}},v)H^{(+)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}2\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\left(4\xi\widetilde{N}({\bar{u}},v)H^{(+)}\right)\Bigg\}+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(121)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(0),\,tz\partial}=2\frac{iQQ^{\prime}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ u\frac{2\xi t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\left[(N({\bar{u}},v))^{2}-2(\xi-\rho)(N({\bar{u}},v))^{3}\right]H^{(+)}

\displaystyle\qquad+O(\textrm{tw-6})\,,

(122)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{00}_{(1),\,z\Delta}=2\frac{iQQ^{\prime}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{\bar{u}}dv\ C_{(1),\,z\Delta}({\bar{u}},v)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Bigg\{\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\left[\frac{2\xi}{\xi-\rho+i0}N({\bar{u}},v)-2\xi(\xi-\rho)(N({\bar{u}},v))^{3}\right]H^{(+)}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\frac{2\xi}{\xi-\rho+i0}\partial_{\xi}\left(\xi N({\bar{u}},v)H^{(+)}\right)+\frac{\bar{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\frac{2\xi}{\xi-\rho+i0}2\xi^{3}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\left(\xi N({\bar{u}},v)H^{(+)}\right)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Bigg\{\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\left[\frac{2\xi}{\xi-\rho+i0}N({\bar{u}},v)+2\xi(\xi+\rho)(N({\bar{u}},v))^{3}\right]H^{(+)}

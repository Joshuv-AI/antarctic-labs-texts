The Krull-Remak-Schmidt-Azumaya Theorem for idempotent complete categories
Simion Breaz

Abstract
. We prove that the Krull-Remak-Schmidt-Azumaya unique decomposition theorem holds in idempotent complete additive categories with enough compact objects. In particular, this result applies to compactly generated triangulated categories. In addition, we provide an example of an object with the finite exchange property that does not have the exchange property.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
18E05, 18G80, 16D70

1.
Introduction

The Krull-Remak-Schmidt-Azumaya Theorem, proved in
[
1
]
, states that if a module
M
has a decomposition
M=\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i}
as a direct sum of submodules with local endomorphism rings then

(a)

every indecomposable direct summand of
M
is isomorphic to a submodule
M_{i}
, and

(b)

the above decomposition is unique up to isomorphism and a permutation of the direct summands.

For historical details on the development of this theorem and its applications in module categories, we refer to
[
5
]
.

Similar statements have also been proved and applied to more general categories. A version of statement (b) for finite decompositions in additive categories is proven in
[
15
, Theorem 1]
. For (a), it is necessary to assume that all idempotents split (a category with this property is called
idempotent complete
). In fact, if every object has a decomposition as a finite direct of objects with local endomorphism rings, then the category is idempotent complete, as shown in
[
9
, Corollary 4.4]
. For the case of infinite sums, the statements (a) and (b) do not hold in additive categories, even these categories have kernels (see Example
3.4
and Example
4.3
). However, it is proved in
[
15
, Theorem 2 and Theorem 3]
that the Krull-Remak-Schmidt-Azumaya Theorem is valid in additive categories with kernels that satisfy a Grothendieck condition. For other results involving lattices with a Grothendieck condition, we refer to
[
8
]
.

Recently, certain versions of (a) and (b) have been used, for instance, in
[
3
]
or
[
12
]
, in idempotent complete categories without kernels. It seems that establishing a general version of the Krull-Schmidt-Azumaya Theorem in the context of such categories could be useful.

This is the main aim of the present note. We begin with a brief study of objects the exchange property in idempotent complete additive categories. In particular, we prove that every object with local endomorphism ring has the finite exchange property. Furthermore, in Example
3.4
, we show that in the dual category
\text{\bf Ab}^{op}
the group of
p
-adic integers has the finite exchange property but not the exchange property. This gives a negative answer to the question presented in
[
8
]
after Definition 7.3.2.

Then we will prove the statements (a) and (b) in additive categories with enough compact objects, Theorem
4.1
. In particular, the Krull-Schmidt-Remak-Azumaya Theorem holds for compactly generated triangulated categories, Corollary
3.1
.

2.
The exchange property in idempotent complete additive categories

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category. If an object
A
has a decomposition
A=B\oplus C
with the canonical morphisms
u:B\to A
and
v:C\to A
then we will say that
u
represents a
direct summand
for
A
and
C
is a
complement
of
B
. Since we are in an additive category, a morphism
u:B\to A
represents a direct summand if and only if
u
is a
split monomorphism
(i.e. there exists
p:A\to B
such that
pu=1_{B}
). Note that two complements for the same direct summand are isomorphic as objects (but not necessarily as subobjects of
A
).

If
X=\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i}
is a direct sum in
\mathcal{C}
with the canonical morphisms
u_{i}:A_{i}\to A
and
J\subseteq I
, we denote
X(J)=\oplus_{j\in J}A_{j}
, and
u_{J}:X(J)\to A
will be the morphism induced by the family
u_{j},j\in J
.

An additive category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
idempotent complete
if every idempotent endomorphism in
\mathcal{C}
has a (co)kernel.

In the next lemma, we list the main properties of an idempotent complete additive category.

Lemma 2.1
.

An additive category
\mathcal{C}
is idempotent complete if and only if for every
A\in\mathcal{C}
and every idempotent endomorphism
e
of
A
there exist morphisms
q:B\to A
and
p:A\to B
such that
qp=1_{B}
and
pq=e
. Moreover, under these conditions, the following are true:

(i)

[
10
, Proposition 18.5]
If
e
is an idempotent endomorphism of
A
then
A=\mathrm{Ker}(e)\oplus\mathrm{Ker}(1-e)
, where the canonical morphisms are those associated to these kernels.

(ii)

[
2
, Lemma I.3.4]
If
k:A\to B
and
u:B\to A
are morphisms such that
ku
is an automorphism of
B
then
u
represents a direct summand of
A
.

The exchange property was introduced in
[
4
]
for general algebraic systems and in
[
15
]
for additive categories.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category. An object
A\in\mathcal{C}
has the
exchange property with respect to a direct decomposition
B=\oplus_{i\in I}B_{i}
(with the canonical monomorphisms
u_{i}:B_{i}\to B
) if for any split monomorphism
u:A\to B
, there exist direct decompositions
B_{i}=C_{i}\oplus D_{i}
with the canonical monomorphisms
v_{i}
and
w_{i}
such that
B=A\oplus(\oplus_{i\in I}C_{i})
with the canonical monomorphisms
u
and
v_{i}u_{i}
. If we know that this property is valid for all (finite) direct decompositions of the objects of
\mathcal{C}
then we will say that
A
has the (
finite
)
exchange property
.

Remark 2.2
.

Suppose that the object
A\in\mathcal{C}
has the exchange property with respect to a direct decomposition
B=\oplus_{i\in I}B_{i}
and that
D_{i}
are the direct summands used in the above definition. Then
A
and
\oplus_{i\in I}D_{i}
are complements of the same direct summand
\oplus_{i\in I}C_{i}
of
B
, hence
A\cong\oplus_{i\in I}D_{i}
. If
A
is indecomposable, then there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that
D_{i}=B_{i}
for all
i\neq i_{0}
.

The following lemma is proved in
[
16
]
(see also
[
9
]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an idempotent complete additive category. If
A
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
and
E
is the endomorphism ring of
A
then the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(A,-):\mathrm{add}(A)\to\mathrm{proj}(E)
is an equivalence, where
\mathrm{proj}(E)
represents the category of all finitely generated projective right
E
-modules.

A ring
R
is an
exchange ring
if the regular left
R
-module
R
has the finite exchange property. Note that this is a left-right symmetric property. An
R
-module
M
has the finite exchange property if and only if its endomorphism ring is exchange,
[
17
]
. Exchange rings can be characterized by using ring theoretic properties, see
[
11
, Theorem 1]
and
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
. From the proofs of these two theorems, we deduce the following characterization:

Lemma 2.4
.

An
R
-module
M
has the finite exchange property if and only if it has the exchange property with respect to all direct sums
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
with
M_{1}=M_{2}
.

In the next result, we will use Lemma
2.3
to describe the objects with the finite exchange property in idempotent complete additive categories.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an idempotent complete additive category. If
A
is an object from
\mathcal{C}
and
E
is its endomorphism ring, the following are equivalent:

i)

A
has the finite exchange property;

ii)

E
is an exchange ring.

Proof.

i)
\Rightarrow
ii) Let
H^{A}=\mathrm{Hom}(A,-):\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}E
be the covariant
\mathrm{Hom}
functor induced by
A
. From Lemma
2.3
the restriction
H^{A}:\mathrm{add}(A)\to\mathrm{proj}(E)
is an equivalence. To prove that the regular module
E
has the finite exchange property, we will use Lemma
2.4
to observe that it is enough to prove that if
G=E\oplus E
with the canonical monomorphisms
u_{1},u_{2}:E\to G
and we have a direct decomposition
G=M\oplus N
(of
E
-modules) with canonical monomorphisms
u_{M}
and
u_{N}
and
M\cong E
then there exist split monomorphisms
v_{1}:E_{1}\to E
and
v_{2}:E_{2}\to E
such that
G=M\oplus E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
with canonical monomorphisms
u_{M}
,
u_{1}v_{1}
, and
u_{2}v_{2}
.

Observe that
N
is projective. Therefore, there exists in
\mathcal{C}
a corresponding direct decomposition
B\oplus C=A\oplus A
such that
B\cong A
and
\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)=N
. Let
\overline{u}_{B}:B\to A\oplus A
be the morphism such that
\mathrm{Hom}(A,\overline{u}_{B})=u_{M}
. Hence
\overline{u}_{B}
is the canonical morphism associated to the direct summand
B
in the decomposition
B\oplus C=A\oplus A
. Therefore, we can apply the finite exchange property for
A
to find two split monomorphisms
\overline{v}_{i}:D_{i}\to A
,
i=1,2
, such that
\overline{u}_{B}
,
\overline{u}_{1}\overline{v}_{1}
and
\overline{u}_{2}\overline{v}_{2}
give a direct decomposition for
A\oplus A
(
\overline{u}_{1}
and
\overline{u}_{2}
are the canonical morphisms
A\to A\oplus A
). Applying
\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)
we obtain the desired decomposition for
E\oplus E
, hence
E
has the finite exchange property.

ii)
\Rightarrow
i) Assume that
I
is a finite set,
B=\oplus_{i\in I}B_{i}
is an object from
\mathcal{C}
, and that
u:A\to B
is a split monomorphism. We consider the equivalence
\mathrm{Hom}(B,-):\mathrm{add}(B)\to\mathrm{proj}(\mathrm{End}(B))
. It follows that the endomorphism ring of
\mathrm{Hom}(B,A)
is isomorphic to
E
, hence the
\mathrm{End}(B)
-module
\mathrm{Hom}(B,A)
has the finite exchange property. Then we can apply the same strategy as in the proof of i)
\Rightarrow
ii) to obtain the desired decomposition for
B
.
∎

From
[
18
, Proposition 1]
we obtain the following characterization.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an idempotent complete additive category. An indecomposable object
A
from
\mathcal{C}
has the finite exchange property if and only if
\mathrm{End}(A)
is a local ring.

Corollary 2.7
.

[
2
, Lemma 3.5]

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is an idempotent complete additive category. Let
X=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}=B\oplus C
be an object in
\mathcal{C}
and
B
a direct summand in
X
.

If
\mathrm{End}(B)
is a local ring then there exists
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and a factorization
A_{j}=A_{j}^{\prime}\oplus D
such that
X=B\oplus A^{\prime}_{j}\oplus(\oplus_{i\neq j}A_{i})
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category.

(a)

A finite direct sum of objects with the (finite) exchange property has the (finite) exchange property.

(b)

If
\mathcal{C}
is idempotent complete, a direct summand of an object with the finite exchange property has the finite exchange property.

Proof.

(a) This is proved in
[
15
, Lemma 6]
.

(b) From
[
5
, Lemma 2.4]
, in every module category, the class of all modules with the finite exchange property is closed with respect to direct summands. Therefore, if
e
is an idempotent in an exchange ring
E
then
eEe
is also an exchange ring. Applying Proposition
2.6
, the conclusion is now obvious.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is an idempotent complete additive category. Let
X=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}=B\oplus C
be an object in
\mathcal{C}
such that
B
is indecomposable. If the rings
\mathrm{End}(A_{i})
are local then there exists
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
X=B\oplus(\oplus_{i\neq j}A_{i})
(hence
B\cong A_{j}
).

Proof.

We use Proposition
2.8
to observe that
B
has the finite exchange property. Since it is indecomposable, it follows that
\mathrm{End}(B)
is local. Now we apply Corollary
2.7
.
∎

3.
Categories with enough compact objects

Recall that an object
C
in an additive category
\mathcal{C}
with direct sums is called
compact
if for every morphism
f:C\to\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i}
there exists a finite subset
J\subseteq I
such that
f
factorizes through
u_{J}
. A category
\mathcal{C}
has
enough compact objects
if for every object
B\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a compact
C\in\mathcal{C}
and a non-zero morphism
C\to B
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is an idempotent complete category with enough compact objects. If
B
is an object from
\mathcal{C}
then for every direct decomposition
X=\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i}
and for every splitting monomorphism
u:B\to X
there exists a finite subset
J\subseteq I
such that the morphism
(u,u_{J}):B\oplus X(J)\to X
does not split.

Proof.

Suppose that
0\neq u:B\to A
is a split monomorphism. We take a non-zero morphism
f:C\to B
, where
C
is a compact object. Then there exists a finite subset
J\subseteq I
and a (nonzero) morphism
g:C\to X(J)
such that
uf=u_{J}g
.

Assume that the morphism
(u,u_{J}):B\oplus X(J)\to X
splits. There exist
p:X\to B
and
q:X\to X(J)
such that for the induced morphism
\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}:X\to B\oplus X(J)
we have
\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}(u,u_{J})=1_{B\oplus X(J)}
. Then
qu=0
and
qu_{J}=1_{X(J)}
. From
qu=0
, it follows that
g=qu_{J}g=quf=0
, a contradiction.
∎

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
X=M\oplus N=K\oplus L
are direct decompositions for
X
, with the canonical monomorphisms
u_{M}:M\to X
and
u_{K}:K\to X
, such that
M
is indecomposable and
(u_{M},u_{K}):M\oplus K\to X
is not a split monomorphism.

i)

If
\mathrm{End}(M)
is a local ring then
(u_{M},u_{L}):M\oplus L\to X
is a split monomorphism.

ii)

If
K
has the finite exchange property then
M
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
K
.

Proof.

a) Since
M
has the finite exchange property, there are direct decompositions
K=K_{1}\oplus K_{2}
and
L=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
(with canonical monomorphisms
v_{i}:K_{i}\to K
and
w_{i}:L_{i}\to L
,
i=1,2
) such that we have a direct decomposition
X=M\oplus K_{1}\oplus L_{1}
given by the canonical morphisms
u_{M}
,
u_{K}v_{1}
, and
u_{L}w_{1}
(here
u_{L}:L\to X
is the canonical monomorphism). We have two decompositions
X=K_{1}\oplus K_{2}\oplus L_{1}\oplus L_{2}=M\oplus K_{1}\oplus L_{1}
such that the canonical (splitting) monomorphisms associated to
K_{1}
and
L_{1}
are the same in both decompositions. It follows that there exists an isomorphism
M\cong K_{2}\oplus L_{2}
. Since
M
is indecomposable, we obtain
K_{2}=0
or
L_{2}=0
. If
K_{2}=0
, we can assume that
v_{1}=1_{K}
, and it follows that
(u_{M},u_{K})
is a splitting monomorphism, a contradiction. Hence,
L_{2}=0
and the conclusion obvious.

b) Since
K
has the finite exchange property, there exists direct decompositions
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
and
N=N_{1}\oplus N_{2}
such that
X=K\oplus M_{1}\oplus N_{1}
. As in the proof of a), if
M_{2}=0
, it follows that
(u_{M},u_{K})
is a split monomorphism, a contradiction. Therefore,
M_{1}=0
, hence
X\cong K\oplus N_{1}=M\oplus N_{1}\oplus N_{2}
, and in these decompositions the canonical monomorphisms associated to
N_{1}
are the same. It follows that
K\cong M\oplus N_{2}
.
∎

The following result generalizes
[
15
, Proposition 2]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an idempotent complete additive category with enough compact objects. If the endomorphism ring of
A\in\mathcal{C}
is local then
A
has the exchange property.

Proof.

Consider a splitting monomorphism
u:A\to\oplus_{i\in I}B_{i}
. There exists a finite subset
J\subseteq I
such that
(u,u_{J})
does not split. From Lemma
3.2
i) it follows that
(u,u_{I\setminus J})
represents a direct summand for
\oplus_{i\in I}B_{i}
. Therefore,
p_{I\setminus J}u=0
, hence
u
factorizes through
u_{J}
. If
u=u_{J}\alpha
, since
\mathcal{C}
is idempotent complete, we obtain that
\alpha
is splitting monomorphism. Therefore, we can apply the finite exchange property of
A
to
\alpha:A\to\oplus_{j\in J}B_{j}
, and we observe that there exist splitting monomorphisms
v_{j}:B^{\prime}_{j}\to B_{j}
such that
the system
(\alpha,(u_{J}u_{j}v_{j})_{j\in J})
represents a direct decomposition for
\oplus_{j\in J}B_{j}
. It follows that
(u_{J}\alpha,(u_{J}u_{j}v_{j})_{j\in J},(u_{i})_{i\in I\setminus J})
gives us a direct decomposition for
\oplus_{i\in I}B_{i}
.
∎

In the following example we will use an idea from
[
6
, Example 2.1]
to prove that Proposition
3.3
is not valid for general idempotent complete categories.

Example 3.4
.

Let
Ab
be the category of all abelian groups. If
p
is a prime, we denote by

\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}=\{(a_{n}+{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}})_{n>0}\mid a_{n+1}-a_{n}\in p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\}

the group of
p
-adic integers.
For every positive integer
k
, it is easy to see that a
p
-adic integer
a=(a_{n}+{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}})_{n>0}
does not belong to
p^{k}\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
if and only if there exists an integer
n>0
such that
a_{n}+{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\notin p^{k}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}})
. Therefore, if
a\notin p^{k}\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
then
a\notin p^{k}\prod_{n>0}{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Using
[
7
]
, it follows that
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
is a pure subgroup of
\prod_{n>0}{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
. But
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
is pure-injective, hence there exists
u:\prod_{n>0}{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\to\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
such that
u_{|\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}}=1_{\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}}
.

If we denote by
u^{op}
the morphism induced by
u
in the opposite category
\text{\bf Ab}^{op}
, it follows that
u^{op}
is a splitting monomorphism in
\text{\bf Ab}^{op}
in from
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
to the direct sum (in
\text{\bf Ab}^{op}
) of the family of indecomposable objects
({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}})_{n>0}
. Since the group of
p
-adic integers is not isomorphic (in
Ab
) to a direct product of cyclic
p
-groups, it follows that
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
does not have the exchange property in
\text{\bf Ab}^{op}
. However, the endomorphism ring of
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
is the (commutative) ring of all
p
-adic integers, hence it is local.

Remark 3.5
.

This example provides a negative answer to the open question of whether, in bounded lattices (in particular in the subobject lattice of an object in abelian categories), every element with the finite exchange property also has the (full) exchange property, as discussed in
[
8
, p. 61]
. The category
\text{\bf Ab}^{op}
contains a generator, the injective cogenerator
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
in
Ab
, and satisfies the Grothendieck conditions AB4 and AB4
⋆
, but it is not a Grothendieck category. Consequently, the question remains unresolved in Grothendieck categories, particularly in module categories.

4.
The Krull-Schmidt-Azumaya Theorem in Additive Categories

We are ready to prove the main result of this note, a (uniqueness) Krull-Remak-Schmidt-Azumaya Theorem for idempotent complete categories with enough compact objects.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is an idempotent complete additive category with enough compact objects.
Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of objects from
\mathcal{C}
with local endomorphism rings.

(a)

If
B
is an indecomposable direct summand of
\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i}
then there exists
i\in I
such that
B\cong A_{i}
.

(b)

If
\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i}\cong\oplus_{j\in J}B_{j}
and all objects
B_{j}
are indecomposable then there exists a bijection
\sigma:I\to J
such that for every
i\in I
we have
A_{i}\cong B_{\sigma(i)}
.

Proof.

The proof follows the classical proof presented in
[
15
]
. We give some details for the reader’s convenience.

(a) Supppose that
u_{B}:B\to X
is a split monomorphism. Then there exists
J\subseteq I
finite such that
(u_{B},u_{J}):B\oplus X(J)\to X
is not a split monomorphism. From Lemma
3.2
ii), it follows that
B
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
X(J)
. The conclusion follows from Corollary
2.9
.

(b) Let
X=\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i}=\oplus_{j\in J}B_{j}
. For every
S\subseteq I
we will denote
A(S)=\oplus_{i\in S}A_{i}
and
u_{K}:A(S)\to X
the morphism induced by the canonical morphisms
u_{i}:A_{i}\to X
. Similarly, for every
T\subseteq J
we will use the notations
B(T)
and
v_{T}
.

From (a) it follows that for every
j\in J
there exists
i\in I
such that
A_{i}\cong B_{j}
.
It follows that all objects
B_{j}
have local endomorphism rings. Using again (a) we conclude that for every
i\in I
there exists
j\in J
such that
B_{j}\cong A_{i}
.

It follows that we have a bijection between the sets
\{[A_{i}]\mid i\in I\}
and
\{[B_{j}]\mid j\in J\}
, where
[Y]
denote the isomorphism class of
Y
. Moreover, all objects
A_{i}
are almost small and all
B_{j}
have the exchange property.

We obtain two partitions
I=\bigcup_{k\in K}I_{k}
and
J=\bigcup_{k\in K}J_{k}
with the following properties:

•

two objects
A_{i}
and
A_{i^{\prime}}
are isomorphic if and only if there exists
k\in K
such that
i,i^{\prime}\in I_{k}
;

•

two objects
B_{j}
and
B_{j^{\prime}}
are isomorphic if and only if there exists
k\in K
such that
j,j^{\prime}\in J_{k}
;

•

if
k\in K
,
i\in I_{k}
,
j\in J_{k}
then
A_{i}\cong B_{j}
.

We want to prove that for all
k\in K
we have
|I_{k}|=|J_{k}|
. It is enough to prove the
|J_{k}|\leq|I_{k}|
.

Case 1.
J_{k}
is finite.
Since for
|J_{k}|=1
the inequality is obvious, we will use an induction on
|J_{k}|
. If
j_{0}\in J
, since
B_{j_{0}}
has the exchange property, we can apply Remark
2.2
to observe that there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that
(v_{i_{0}},(u_{j})_{j\neq j_{0}})
represents a direct decomposition for
X
. Then

Recall that a triangulated category

Example 4.3

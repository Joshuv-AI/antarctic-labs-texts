Abstract
. In this paper, we establish local well-posedness results for the Muskat equation in any dimension using modulus of continuity techniques. By introducing a novel quantity β σ ​ ( f 0 ′ ) \beta_{\sigma}(f_{0}^{\prime}) which encapsulates local monotonicity and slope, we identify a new class of initial data within W 1 , ∞ ​ ( ℝ d ) W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . This includes scenarios where the product of the maximal and minimal slopes is large, thereby guaranteeing the local existence of a classical solution.



1.
Introduction and Main Results

The Muskat equation (see
[
30
]
,
[
41
]
) models the flow of two incompressible, immiscible fluids within a porous medium, originally formulated to describe the dynamics of oil-water interfaces in petroleum engineering. Beyond oil recovery, the Muskat equation finds applications in environmental engineering and water resource management, where understanding fluid interface evolution is critical. Mathematically, the Muskat problem is represented by a nonlinear, fractional-order degenerate parabolic equation that captures the evolution of the interface along with complex fluid dynamics phenomena, including diffusion, convection, and gravity.

This equation bears similarities to models such as the Hele-Shaw flow and the surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) equation, both of which describe nonlinear interface evolution and instability, reflecting common features of interface-driven systems in fluid dynamics.

The flow of fluids within porous media is generally governed by Darcy’s Law, represented by the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\text{div}(\rho v)=0,\quad\text{ (transport equation)}\\
&\text{div}(v)=0,\quad\quad\quad\ \quad\text{(incompressible condition)}\\
&v+\nabla(P+\rho gy)=0,\quad~\text{ (Darcy's law)}\end{aligned}\right.

where
g>0
is the acceleration of gravity. Darcy’s law establishes a relationship between the fluid velocity
v
and the pressure gradient
P
across the porous medium. The Muskat problem, rooted in this flow model, investigates the dynamics and evolution of the interface separating two immiscible fluids within such media.

Consider a time-dependent curve
\Sigma(t)
that separates two domains
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
\Omega_{2}(t)
. If we assume that
\Sigma(t)
can be represented as the graph of a function, we define the domains and interface as follows:

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\,;\,y>f(t,x)\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\,;\,y<f(t,x)\right\},

\displaystyle\Sigma(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\,;\,y=f(t,x)\right\}.

We assume that each domain
\Omega_{j}
,
j=1,2
, is occupied by an incompressible fluid with constant density
\rho_{j}
, where
\rho=\rho(t,x)
takes the value
\rho_{j}
in
\Omega_{j}(t)
. Assuming
\rho_{2}>\rho_{1}
, the heavier fluid lies beneath the lighter one.

The reduction of the Muskat problem to an evolution equation for the free surface parameterization
f(t,x)
has been well-studied (see
[
10
,
31
,
45
]
). Córdoba and Gancedo
[
23
]
provided a streamlined formulation, showing that the problem can be reformulated as an evolution equation for the free surface elevation:

\partial_{t}f(t,x)=\frac{\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}}{2^{d}\pi}\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\alpha\cdot\nabla_{x}\Delta_{\alpha}f(t,x)}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(t,x)\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}},

where
\Delta_{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(x-\alpha)}{|\alpha|}
, and the integral is interpreted in the sense of principal values.

In this paper, we focus on the stable regime where
\rho_{2}>\rho_{1}
. To simplify the equation, we set
\frac{\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}}{2^{d}\pi}=1
, leading to:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(t,x)=\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\alpha\cdot\nabla_{x}\Delta_{\alpha}f(t,x)}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(t,x)\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}.

This equation exhibits invariance under the scaling transformation
f(t,x)\to f_{\lambda}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}f(\lambda t,\lambda x)
. A straightforward computation shows that the function spaces
\dot{H}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are critical for the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem.

There are numerous significant results concerning the Cauchy problem for the Muskat equation with graph interfaces, including global existence under smallness conditions and blow-up scenarios for sufficiently large initial data. These studies capture various singularity formations, such as overhanging interfaces leading to loss of regularity, as demonstrated by Castro et al.

In the present work, we consider the Muskat problem with a large slope.
The main techniques in this paper was introduced in
[
38
]
to prove the global well-posedness of 2D dissipative quasi-geostrophic equation. The equation reads

\displaystyle\theta_{t}-u\cdot\nabla\theta+(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta=0.

The main idea is to construct a special
family of modulus of continuity that are preserved by the dissipative evolution,
which will lead to a priori estimate for
\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}
independent of
time. To do this, they assume that
\theta
has modulus of continuity
\omega
for any
t<T
, which means that

|\theta(t,x)-\theta(t,y)|\leq\omega(|x-y|),\ \ \forall 0\leq t<T,

for some increasing continuous concave function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that
\omega(0)=0
.
By an elementary discussion, they found that the only scenario in which the modulus of continuity
\omega

may be lost by
\theta
after
T
is the one in which there exist two points
x\neq y
such that
\theta(T,x)-\theta(T,y)=\omega(|x-y|)
. Then they designed a special function
\omega
such that

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\theta(t,x)-\theta(t,y))\right|_{t=T}<0.

This contradicts the assumption that the modulus of
continuity
\omega
is preserved up to the time
T
. Hence
\theta
has the modulus of
continuity
\omega
for all times
t\in[0,+\infty)
.

Applying the techniques in
[
38
]
, the second author proved global well-posedness results in both 2D
[
11
]
and 3D
[
12
]
. In 2D, due to the special structure of the equation, he proved the global wellposedness for initial data
f_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f_{0}^{\prime}(x)\right)\left(\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}-f_{0}^{\prime}(y)\right)<1.

We remark that this condition can be considered as an interpolation between the slope less than 1 assumption in
[
21
]
and the monotonicity assumption in
[
29
]
. Following the scheme in
[
38
]
and using a rescaling argument, they proved that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(f_{x}(t,x)-f_{x}(t,y))\right|_{t=T}<\left.\frac{d}{dt}\left(\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)\right|_{t=T},

which is a key to obtain contradiction.
Hence there exists global classical solution satisfying

f_{x}(t,x)-f_{x}(t,y)\leq\rho\left(\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right).

We note the sole reason to require (
1.2
) is to ensure the ellipticity of the kernel
K
as we defined in (
2.12
). In this paper, we prove a local version of the results in
[
11
]
. More precisely, we propose a local version (see (
1.4
)) of the condition (
1.2
). Under this condition, the kernel
K
may not be elliptic, but the main part
K_{1}
(see Section
2.1
for definition) is still elliptic. We note that in our proof, the derivative
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(f(t,x)-f(t,y))\right|_{t=T}
may be positive due to some remainder terms associated to the kernel
K-K_{1}
. To control these terms, we introduce the modulus of
continuity in the form
\rho(\cdot/t)e^{\tilde{C}t}
, where
\tilde{C}
is a fixed positive constant.
To obtain contradiction as in
[
38
]
and
[
11
]
, it suffices to prove that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(f_{x}(t,x)-f_{x}(t,y))\right|_{t=T}<\left.\frac{d}{dt}\left(\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)\right|_{t=T}+\tilde{C}\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{T}\right).

Note that the term

\tilde{C}\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{T}\right)

is devoted to absorb remainder terms associated to the kernel
K-K_{1}
. We remark that we prove (
1.3
) directly instead of using the rescaling argument.

In 3D, the second author proved the global well-posedness in
[
12

under the condition
\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 5^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\nabla f_{0}-\nabla f_{0}\ast\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{d},

where

c_{d}<\frac{1}{4(d+1)}
. Specifically, in 3D, the constant can be improved to
5^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, which is consistent with the global result in
[
12
]

We introduce some notations that will be used throughout the paper. Fix a standard symmetric cutoff function
\chi
such that

\displaystyle\chi(x)=\begin{cases}1,\ \ &\text{if}\ |x|\leq 1,\\
0,\ \ &\text{if}\ |x|>2,\end{cases}

with
|\chi^{(k)}|\leq 2^{k}.
For
\sigma>0
and

, define

\displaystyle\chi_{\sigma,z}=\chi\left(\frac{x-z}{\sigma}\right),

and

(1.4)

\displaystyle\beta_{\sigma}(g)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\sup_{x}(\chi_{\sigma,z}g)(x)\sup_{y}(-\chi_{\sigma,z}g)(y)\right).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with

(1.5)

\displaystyle\beta_{\sigma}(f_{0}^{\prime})\leq 1-\varepsilon_{0},

for some
\sigma,\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
Then there exists
T_{0}>0
and a unique classical solution

\displaystyle f\in C([0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{R})\cap C^{\infty}_{loc}((0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{R})

satisfying
\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\beta_{\sigma}(f_{x}(t))<1
and

\sup_{x}|\delta_{\alpha}f_{x}(t,x)|\leq C_{0}t^{-1}|\alpha|\ \ \ t\in(0,T_{0}],\ \forall\alpha\neq 0,

where
C_{0}
is a constant depending on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\partial_{x}f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\varepsilon_{0}
and
\sigma
.

The following lemma provides an example of initial data that satisfies (
1.5

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\{a_{k}\}_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}

\inf_{k}(a_{k}-a_{k-1})\geq\mu_{0}

\mu_{0}>0

\displaystyle\sup_{k}(\sup_{x\in I_{k}}f_{0}^{\prime}(x)\sup_{y\in I_{k}}(-f_{0}^{\prime}(y)))\leq 1-\varepsilon_{0},

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\sup_{k}\sup_{|x-a_{k}|\leq\mu_{1}}|f_{0}^{\prime}-f_{0}^{\prime}\ast\phi_{\varepsilon}|(x)\right)\leq 1-\varepsilon_{0},

for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\mu_{1}\in(0,\mu_{0}/100)
.

Remark 1.3
.

Generally, the condition (
1.5
) is not met even for some smooth functions. For example, if we take
f_{0}^{\prime}(x)=\sin x^{2}
, then we don’t have
\beta_{\sigma}(f_{0}^{\prime})<1
for any
\sigma>0
. However, we claim that the condition (
1.5
) is true for functions satisfying (see Figure
1
)

(i) piece-wise
C^{1}
(with finite irregular points) in a finite interval
K
.

(ii) the derivative is uniformly continuous outside the set
K
.

(iii)
f_{0}^{\prime+}(x_{0})f_{0}^{\prime-}(x_{0})>-1+2\epsilon_{0}
for any irregular point
x_{0}
.

Here
f_{0}^{\prime+},f_{0}^{\prime-}
denote the right and left hand derivatives, respectively. And the uniform continuity means that

\forall\epsilon>0,\exists\delta>0,\text{such that}\ |x-y|<\delta\Rightarrow|f(x)-f(y)|<\epsilon,\ \ \forall x,y\notin K.

In fact, if we let
\{x_{1},...x_{n}\}
be the set of irregular points, then by the condition (i), one can take open intervals
O_{i}=(x_{i}-\widetilde{\sigma}_{i},x_{i}+\widetilde{\sigma}_{i})
of
x_{i}
such that
f_{0}^{\prime+}(y)f_{0}^{\prime-}(z)>-1+\epsilon_{0}
for any points
y,z\in O_{i}
. We take smaller open intervals
\widetilde{O}_{i}=(x_{i}-\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{i}}{2},x_{i}+\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{i}}{2})\Subset O_{i}
and
f_{0}^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous in
(K-\cup_{i}\widetilde{O}_{i})\cup K^{c}
. Let
\epsilon=\frac{1-\epsilon_{0}}{8}
, by (ii), there exists
\delta>0
such that

|f_{0}^{\prime}(x)-f_{0}^{\prime}(y)|<\epsilon,\ \ \ \ \forall x,y\notin K,\ |x-y|<\delta.

Then we obtain
\beta_{\sigma}(f_{0}^{\prime})<1
by taking
\sigma=\min\{\delta,\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{i}}{3}\}
to get the result.

Lemma 2.5

Let

2.2.
Uniqueness

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,\infty})
be a classical solution to the Muskat equation with initial data
f(0,x)=f_{0}(x)
. If there exists a modulus of
continuity
\tilde{\rho}
such that

\displaystyle f_{x}(t,x)-f_{x}(t,y)\leq\tilde{\rho}(|x-y|),\quad\forall 0\leq t\leq T,x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}.

Then the solution
f
is unique.

Proof.

The idea of the proof follows
[
16
]
.
Let
f_{1},f_{2}
be two solutions with the same initial data. Denote
g=f_{1}-f_{2}
. We have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{E_{\alpha}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}\rangle^{2}}\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}E_{\alpha}f_{2}\left(\frac{1}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}\rangle^{2}}-\frac{1}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}\rangle^{2}}\right)\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|E_{\alpha}f_{2}||\Delta_{\alpha}g|\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|}.

Take
x_{t}
such that
g(t,x_{t})=\sup_{x}g(t,x)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}g(x_{t})+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\delta_{\alpha}g(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})\rangle^{2}}\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|E_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})||\Delta_{\alpha}g(x_{t})|\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon)\int_{|\alpha|\leq\epsilon}\delta_{\alpha}g(x_{t})\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}+\epsilon^{-1}\|f_{2}\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Take
\epsilon
small enough such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(\epsilon)\leq\frac{1}{1+\|f_{1}\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}g(x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-1}\|f_{2}\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Integrate in time we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{x}g(t,x)\leq\epsilon^{-1}\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}\|g(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}dt.

A similar discussion holds if we replace
\sup g(x)
by
\sup(-g(x))
. By Gronwall’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|g(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|g_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

This completes the proof.

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(t,\cdot)-\nabla f_{0}\ast\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2c_{d}.

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\nabla f(t,x)-\nabla f(t,y)|\leq\rho\left(\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)\ \ \ \forall x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for some concave modulus function
\rho
satisfying
\rho(\alpha)\leq C_{0}|\alpha|
. In the following, we will not distinguish
\rho(\alpha)
and
\rho(|\alpha|)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\tilde{T}=\tilde{T}(\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},d,\eta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\min\{T,\tilde{T}\}]}\|\nabla f(t,\cdot)-\nabla f_{0}\ast\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{3}{2}c_{d}.

Proof.

By (
3.2
), it suffices to find
\tilde{T}
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\min\{T,\tilde{T}\}]}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{c_{d}}{10}.

We follow the estimates in
[
16
]
. Denote
f_{1}(t,x)=f(t,x)-f_{0}\ast\phi_{\eta}(x)
,
f_{2}(x)=f_{0}\ast\phi_{\eta}(x)
.
For any
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, denote
f_{e}=e\cdot\nabla f
. Take directional derivative
\partial_{e}=e\cdot\nabla
in (
3.1
), and denote
E_{\alpha}f(x)=\Delta_{\alpha}f(x)-\partial_{\hat{\alpha}}f(x)
with
\hat{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{e}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{e}}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\right\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\right\rangle^{d+1}}\left(1+(d+1)\frac{E_{\alpha}f\Delta_{\alpha}f}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\rangle^{2}}\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}.

Let
x_{t,e}
,
\tilde{x}_{t,e}
be such that
f_{e}(t,x_{t,e})=\sup_{x}f_{e}(t,x)
and
f_{e}(t,\tilde{x}_{t,e})=\sup_{x}(-f_{e}(t,x))
.
Then
\nabla f_{e}(t,x_{t,e})=0
and
\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(t,x_{t,e})\geq 0
. Denote
M_{e}(t)=f_{e}(t,x_{t,e})
and
m_{e}(t)=f_{e}(t,\tilde{x}_{t,e})
. By (
3.3
) we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(M_{e}(t)+m_{e}(t))\leq 2(\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+2c_{d}).

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\right\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{E_{\alpha}f_{2}\Delta_{\alpha}f}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\rangle^{2}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{|\alpha|\leq\lambda}\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\right\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}+\eta^{-2}\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{|\alpha|\leq\lambda}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}(M_{e}+m_{e})\int_{|\alpha|\geq\lambda}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}.

We can take
\lambda
small enough such that

\displaystyle 1+(d+1)\frac{E_{\alpha}f_{1}\Delta_{\alpha}f}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\rangle^{2}}-(d+1)\lambda\geq 1-(d+1)(4c_{d}+\lambda)\geq 0.

Then we obtain for any
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}M_{e}

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)\eta^{-2}\lambda\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}(M_{e}+m_{e})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{ddd}}{\leq}(d+1)\eta^{-2}\lambda\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+2\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+2c_{d}).

Integrate the above estimate in time and take
\tilde{T}
small enough, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\min\{\tilde{T},T\}]}M_{e}(t)\leq M_{e}(0)+\frac{1}{10}c_{d}.

We can estimate
m_{e}
similarly. The above holds for any direction
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then we obtain (
3.5
), which completes the proof.
∎

3.1.
Modulus Estimates

Following Lemma
2.5
, if
\nabla f
was to lose its modulus after time
T
, there must exist
x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla f(T,x)-\nabla f(T,y)|=\rho\left(\frac{|x-y|}{T}\right).

Hence there exists a direction
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

\displaystyle f_{e}(T,x)-f_{e}(T,y)=\rho\left(\frac{|x-y|}{T}\right).

Analogously as in Lemma
2.3
, we have

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a function
\rho
such that if (
3.3
)-(
3.4
) hold, and

(3.7)

\displaystyle f_{e}(T,x)-f_{e}(T,y)=\rho\left(\frac{|x-y|}{T}\right)

for some
x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, then there exists a constant
\tilde{C}
independent in
T
such that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(f_{e}(t,x)-f_{e}(t,y))\right|_{t=T}<\left.\frac{d}{dt}\left(\rho\left(\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)\right)\right|_{t=T}+\tilde{C}\rho\left(\frac{|x-y|}{T}\right).

Proof.

For simplicity, denote
f_{2}=f_{0}\ast\phi_{\eta}
and
f_{1}=f-f_{2}
.
Taking directional derivative
\partial_{e}=e\cdot\nabla
in (
3.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{e}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{e}(x)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(x)\right\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}R(x,\alpha){\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}},

where we denote
\hat{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}
. Here

\displaystyle R(x,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\rangle^{d+1}}\left(1+(d+1)\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}fE_{\alpha}f(x)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\right\rangle^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\rangle^{d+1}}\left(1+(d+1)\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}fE_{\alpha}f_{1}(x)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\right\rangle^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{(d+1)\Delta_{\alpha}fE_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f\right\rangle^{d+3}}

\displaystyle:=R_{1}+R_{2}.

Note that

(3.8)

\displaystyle R_{1}\geq\frac{1-2(d+1)\|\nabla f_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}})^{d+1}}\geq\frac{1-4(d+1)c_{d}}{(1+\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+c_{d})^{d+1}}>0,

where we take
c_{d}<\frac{1}{5(d+1)}
. We have the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{e}(x)-\partial_{t}f_{e}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{e}(x)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(x)\right\rangle^{d+1}}-\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{e}(y)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(y)\right\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)R_{1}(x,\alpha)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)R_{1}(y,\alpha)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)R_{2}(x,\alpha)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)R_{2}(y,\alpha)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle:=\tilde{Q}_{1}+\tilde{Q}_{2}+\tilde{Q}_{3}.

For simplicity, denote
z=x-y
. Similar as in (
2.17
)-(
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla f_{e}(T,x)=\nabla f_{e}(T,y)=\nabla\rho_{T}(|z|)=\hat{z}\rho_{T}^{\prime}(z).

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{e}(x)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(x)\right\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{e}(y)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(y)\right\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\rho_{T}^{\prime}(z)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\alpha}\cdot\hat{z}\left(\frac{1}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(x)\right\rangle^{d+1}}-\frac{1}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(y)\right\rangle^{d+1}}\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}.

Using the same idea when we estimate
Q_{1}
in Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{1}\leq\rho^{\prime}_{T}(z)\left(\int_{0}^{|z|}\rho_{T}\left(r\right)\frac{dr}{r}+|z|\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}\left(r\right)\frac{dr}{r^{2}}\right).

Moreover, thanks to the ellipticity (
3.8
), we have
R_{1}(x,\alpha)\geq\lambda>0
. We can estimate
\tilde{Q}_{2}
using ideas from
[
12
]
. More precisely, we denote

G(x,y,\alpha)=\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)R_{1}(y,\alpha)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)R_{1}(x,\alpha).

We can decompose
G(x,y,\alpha)
in two different ways, namely

\displaystyle G(x,y,\alpha)=(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x))R_{1}(y,\alpha)+\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)(R_{1}(y,\alpha)-R_{1}(x,\alpha))

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x))R_{1}(x,\alpha)+\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)(R_{1}(y,\alpha)-R_{1}(x,\alpha)).

Since

\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)=-\rho_{T}(z)+f_{e}(x-\alpha)-f_{e}(y-\alpha)\leq 0.

By the ellipticity of
R_{1}
, we can see that

(3.10)

\displaystyle G(x,y,\alpha)\leq

\displaystyle\lambda(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x))+(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x))_{+}(R_{1}(y,\alpha)-R_{1}(x,\alpha))_{+}

\displaystyle+(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y))_{-}(R_{1}(y,\alpha)-R_{1}(x,\alpha))_{-}.

Moreover, by (
3.4
) and (
3.7
) we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)\geq\rho_{T}(|z|)-\rho_{T}(|z|+|\alpha|),

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)\leq-(\rho_{T}(|z|)-\rho_{T}(|z|+|\alpha|)).

Substituting this into (
3.10
) gives that

\displaystyle\int_{|\alpha|>|z|}G(x,y,\alpha)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\alpha|>|z|}\lambda\big(\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)\big)+(\rho_{T}(|z|+|\alpha|)-\rho_{T}(|z|))|R_{1}(x,\alpha)-R_{1}(y,\alpha)|\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}.

Denote

, then we can prove that

(3.12)

|R_{1}(x,\alpha)-R_{1}(y,\alpha)|\leq C_{d}(1+L)\rho_{T}(|z|).

Hence

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{|\alpha|>|z|}G(x,y,\alpha)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{|\alpha|>|z|}\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}{|\alpha|^{d}}d\alpha

\displaystyle+C_{d}(1+L)\rho_{T}(|z|)\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{T}(|z|+r)-\rho_{T}(|z|)}{r^{2}}dr.

For the other case
|\alpha|<|z|
, by adding and
subtracting a linear term, we have that

\displaystyle G(x,y,\alpha)=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\rho^{\prime}_{T}(z)\hat{z}\cdot\hat{\alpha})R_{1}(y,\alpha)-(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)-\rho^{\prime}_{T}(z)\hat{z}\cdot\hat{\alpha})R_{1}(x,\alpha)

\displaystyle+\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|z|)\hat{z}\cdot\hat{\alpha}(R_{1}(y,\alpha)-R_{1}(x,\alpha)).

By the concavity of
\rho_{T}
, we have

\rho_{T}(|h+\xi|)-\rho_{T}(|\xi|)\leq\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|\xi|)(|h+\xi|-|\xi|)\leq\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|\xi|)\hat{\xi}\cdot h+\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|\xi|)}{|\xi|}|h|^{2}.

By similar methods in (
3.10
), and use
(
3.11
)
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle G(x,y,\alpha)\leq

\displaystyle\lambda(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x))+(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)-\rho^{\prime}_{T}(z)\hat{z}\cdot\hat{\alpha})_{+}(R_{1}(x,\alpha)-R_{1}(y,\alpha))_{+}

\displaystyle+(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\rho^{\prime}_{T}(z)\hat{z}\cdot\hat{\alpha})_{-}(R_{1}(x,\alpha)-R_{1}(y,\alpha))_{-}

\displaystyle+\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|z|)\hat{z}\cdot\hat{\alpha}(R_{1}(y,\alpha)-R_{1}(x,\alpha))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x))+\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|z|)\hat{z}\cdot\hat{\alpha}(R_{1}(y,\alpha)-R_{1}(x,\alpha))

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|z|)}{|z|}|\alpha||R_{1}(x,\alpha)-R_{1}(y,\alpha)|.

Combining this with (
3.12
) to get that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{|\alpha|<|z|}G(x,y,\alpha)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{|\alpha|<|z|}\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}{|\alpha|^{d}}d\alpha

\displaystyle+2C_{d}(1+L)\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|z|)\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\rho_{T}(r)}{r}dr.

So we finally get from (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(x,y,\alpha)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}{|\alpha|^{d}}d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{d}(1+L)\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|z|)\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\rho_{T}(r)}{r}dr+C_{d}(1+L)\rho_{T}(|z|)\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{T}(|z|+r)-\rho_{T}(|z|)}{r^{2}}dr.

By in
[
12
, Lemma 5.2]
, we can see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}{|\alpha|^{d}}d\alpha\leq

\displaystyle-\mathbf{c}_{d}\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\delta_{r}\rho_{T}(|z|)+\delta_{-r}\rho_{T}(|z|)}{r^{2}}dr

\displaystyle+\mathbf{c}_{d}\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{T}(r+|z|)-\rho_{T}(r-|z|)-2\rho_{T}(z)}{r^{2}}dr.

Hence we conclude that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{2}\leq

\displaystyle C_{d}(1+L)\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|z|)\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\rho_{T}(r)}{r}dr+C_{d}(1+L)\rho_{T}(|z|)\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{T}(|z|+r)-\rho_{T}(|z|)}{r^{2}}dr

\displaystyle-\mathbf{c}_{d}\lambda\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\delta_{r}\rho_{T}(|z|)+\delta_{-r}\rho_{T}(|z|)}{r^{2}}dr+\mathbf{c}_{d}\lambda\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{T}(r+|z|)-\rho_{T}(r-|z|)-2\rho_{T}(z)}{r^{2}}dr.

It remains to estimate
\tilde{Q}_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\alpha|\leq|z|}\left({\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)}R_{2}(y,\alpha)-{\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}R_{2}(x,\alpha)\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\int_{|\alpha|\geq|z|}\left({\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)}R_{2}(y,\alpha)-{\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}R_{2}(x,\alpha)\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}

\displaystyle:=\tilde{Q}_{3,1}+\tilde{Q}_{3,2}.

Observe that

\displaystyle|R_{2}{(x,\alpha)}|\leq(d+1)|E_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)|.

Recall the definition of
f_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|R_{2}{(x,\alpha)}|}{|\alpha|^{\theta}}\lesssim\frac{|E_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)|}{|\alpha|^{\theta}}\lesssim\|f_{2}\|_{\dot{C}^{1,\theta}}\lesssim\eta^{-\theta}\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\ \ \ \forall\theta\in(0,1].

Hence,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{3,1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\eta^{-1}L\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\rho_{T}(r)}{r}dr.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{3,2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\alpha|\geq|z|}\left({\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)}-{\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}\right)R_{2}(y,\alpha)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\int_{|\alpha|\geq|z|}{\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}\left(R_{2}(y,\alpha)-R_{2}(x,\alpha)\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{Q}_{3,2,1}+\tilde{Q}_{3,2,2}.

By (
3.4
), it is easy to check that

\displaystyle|\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)|\leq\rho_{T}(|\alpha|),\ \ \ \left|{\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(y)}-{\delta_{\alpha}f_{e}(x)}\right|\leq 2\rho_{T}(|z|),\ \ |\Delta_{\alpha}f(y)-\Delta_{\alpha}f(x)|\leq 2\rho_{T}(|z|).

Moreover, recall that
f_{2}=f_{0}\ast\phi_{\eta}
, hence

\displaystyle|\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{2}(h)-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(h)|\leq\min\{1,\eta^{-1}{|\alpha|}\}\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle|(\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{2}-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2})(x)-(\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{2}-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2})(y)|\leq\min\{1,\eta^{-1}|z|\}\min\{1,\eta^{-1}{|\alpha|}\}\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle|R_{2}(x,\alpha)|\leq C_{d}(1+L)\min\{1,\eta^{-1}{|\alpha|}\},

\displaystyle\left|R_{2}(y,\alpha)-R_{2}(x,\alpha)\right|\leq C_{d}(1+L)\left(\rho_{T}(z)+\min\{1,{\eta}^{-1}{|z|}\}\right)\min\{1,\eta^{-1}{|\alpha|}\}.

Hence

\displaystyle|\tilde{Q}_{3,2,1}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}(1+L)\rho_{T}(|z|)\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\min\{1,\eta^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle|\tilde{Q}_{3,2,2}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}(1+L)(\rho_{T}(|z|)+\min\{1,\eta^{-1}|z|\})\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}(r)\min\{1,\eta^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}.

We conclude from this and (
3.16
) that

(3.17)

\displaystyle|\tilde{Q}_{3}|\leq

\displaystyle C_{d}\eta^{-1}L\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\rho_{T}(r)}{r}dr+C_{d}(1+L)^{2}\rho_{T}(|z|)\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\min\{1,\eta^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+C_{d}(1+L)\min\{1,\eta^{-1}|z|\}\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}(r)\min\{1,\eta^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}.

We conclude from (
3.9
), (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(f_{e}(t,x)-f_{e}(t,y))\right|_{t=T}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}(1+L)\rho^{\prime}_{T}(|{z}|)\left(\int_{0}^{|{z}|}\rho_{T}(r)\frac{dr}{r}+|{z}|\int_{|{z}|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}(r)\frac{dr}{r^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+C_{d}(1+L)\rho_{T}(|z|)\int_{|{z}|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{T}(r+|{z}|)-\rho_{T}(|z|)}{r^{2}}dr-\mathbf{c}_{d}\lambda\int_{0}^{|{z}|}\frac{\delta_{r}\rho_{T}(|z|)+\delta_{-r}\rho_{T}(|z|)}{r^{2}}dr

\displaystyle+\mathbf{c}_{d}\lambda\int_{|{z}|}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{T}(r+|{z}|)-\rho_{T}(r-|{z}|)-2\rho_{T}(|z|)}{r^{2}}dr

\displaystyle+C_{d}\eta^{-1}L\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\rho_{T}(r)}{r}dr+C_{d}(1+L)^{2}\rho_{T}(|z|)\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\min\{1,\eta^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+C_{d}(1+L)\min\{1,\eta^{-1}|z|\}\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}(r)\min\{1,\eta^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}.

We denote
\tilde{z}=\frac{C_{0}z}{T}
, and
\omega(|\tilde{z}|)=\rho_{T}(|z|)
, where the constant
C_{0}
will be fixed later. Then we have

\displaystyle\rho_{T}^{\prime}(|z|)=\frac{C_{0}}{T}\omega^{\prime}(|\tilde{z}|),\ \ \ \rho^{\prime\prime}_{T}(|z|)=\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{T^{2}}\omega^{\prime\prime}(|\tilde{z}|).

We take
\omega(\alpha)
as follows, where
\delta,\gamma
will be fixed later,

\displaystyle\omega(\alpha)=\alpha-\frac{4}{3}\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}},\ \alpha\leq\delta,

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(\alpha)=\frac{\gamma}{\alpha(\ln(\frac{\alpha}{\delta}+10))},\ \alpha\geq\delta.

We mainly discuss the last two lines in the right hand side of (
3.18
), which are new terms compared with (
2.16
). For simplicity, denote

\displaystyle B_{1}=-C_{d}\eta^{-1}L\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{\rho_{T}(r)}{r}dr,\ \ \ \ B_{2}=C_{d}(1+L)^{2}\rho_{T}(|z|)\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\min\{1,{\eta}^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle B_{3}=C_{d}(1+L)\min\{1,\eta^{-1}|z|\}\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}(r)\min\{1,{\eta}^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}.

Our aim is to prove that for any
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll 1
, there exist suitable constants
\delta,\gamma,\tilde{C}
such that

(3.19)

B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3}\leq\begin{cases}-\epsilon_{1}|z|\rho_{T}^{\prime\prime}(|z|)+\tilde{C}\rho_{T}(|z|),\ \ \text{if}\ |\tilde{z}|\leq\delta,\\
\epsilon_{1}\frac{\rho_{T}(|z|)}{|z|}+\tilde{C}\rho_{T}(|z|),\quad\quad\ \text{if}\ |\tilde{z}|>\delta.\end{cases}

Using the fact that
\omega(|\tilde{z}|)\leq 2L
, we have
\tilde{z}\leq\omega^{-1}(2L)
. Take
C_{0}\geq\frac{\omega^{-1}(2L)}{2L}
, by concavity we obtain that

\frac{|\tilde{z}|}{\omega(|\tilde{z}|)}\leq\frac{\omega^{-1}(2L)}{2L}\leq C_{0}.

Hence

\displaystyle B_{1}=C_{d}\eta^{-1}L\int_{0}^{|\tilde{z}|}\frac{\omega(r)}{r}dr\leq C_{d}\eta^{-1}L|\tilde{z}|\leq\tilde{C}_{1}\omega(|\tilde{z}|),

where
\tilde{C}_{1}=C_{0}C_{d}\eta^{-1}L
.

For
B_{2}
and
B_{3}
, we consider
|\tilde{z}|\leq\delta
and
|\tilde{z}|\geq\delta
separately. If
|\tilde{z}|\leq\delta
, then

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\eta^{-1}(1+L)^{2}\rho_{T}(|z|)\left(1+\mathbf{1}_{|z|\leq\eta}\ln\frac{\eta}{|z|}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{d}\eta^{-1}(1+L)^{2}\omega(|\tilde{z}|)\left(1+\mathbf{1}_{|z|\leq\eta}\ln\frac{\eta}{|z|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{10}\frac{C_{0}|\tilde{z}|\omega^{\prime\prime}(|\tilde{z}|)}{T}=-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{10}|z|\rho_{T}^{\prime\prime}(|z|),

this follows by taking
\delta
small enough such that
C_{d}\eta^{-2}(1+L)^{2}\left(1+\mathbf{1}_{|z|\leq\eta}\ln\frac{\eta}{|z|}\right)\leq\left(\frac{C_{0}}{T|z|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{10}\frac{C_{0}\omega^{\prime\prime}(|\tilde{z}|)}{T}
holds for any
|\tilde{z}|\leq\delta
. Moreover, by concavity, we have
\frac{\rho_{T}(r)}{r}\leq\frac{\rho_{T}(|z|)}{|z|}
, hence

\displaystyle\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}(r)\min\{1,{\eta}^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|z|}^{\max\{|z|,\eta\}}\eta^{-1}\frac{\rho_{T}(|z|)}{|z|}{dr}+L\int_{\max\{|z|,\eta\}}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\rho_{T}(z)}{|z|}+L\eta^{-1}.

This yields that

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}(1+L)^{2}\eta^{-1}|z|\left(\frac{\rho_{T}(z)}{|z|}+\eta^{-1}\right)\leq C_{d}(1+L)^{2}\eta^{-1}\rho_{T}(z)+C_{d}(1+L)^{2}\eta^{-2}|z|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{2}\rho_{T}(z)-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{10}\frac{C_{0}|\tilde{z}|\omega^{\prime\prime}(|\tilde{z}|)}{T}=\tilde{C}_{2}\rho_{T}(z)-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{10}|z|\rho_{T}^{\prime\prime}(|z|),

where
\tilde{C}_{2}=C_{d}(1+L)^{2}\eta^{-1}
.

On the other hand, if
|\tilde{z}|>\delta

\displaystyle\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\min\{1,\eta^{-1}r\}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}\leq\eta^{-1}\int_{|z|}^{M|z|}\frac{dr}{r}+\int_{M|z|}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}\leq\eta^{-1}\ln M+\frac{1}{M|z|},

for any
M>1

Take
M=\frac{10C_{d}(1+L)^{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle B_{2}\leq C_{d}(1+L)^{2}\rho_{T}(|z|)\left(\eta^{-1}\ln M+\frac{1}{M|z|}\right)\leq\tilde{C}_{3}\rho_{T}(|z|)+\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{10}\frac{\rho_{T}(|z|)}{|z|},

where

.

\displaystyle\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}(r)\frac{dr}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{T}(|z|)}{|z|}+\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}^{\prime}(\xi)\frac{d\xi}{\xi}.

By definition,

\displaystyle\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\rho_{T}^{\prime}(\xi)\frac{d\xi}{\xi}=\frac{C_{0}}{T}\int_{|\tilde{z}|}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(r)}{r}dr=\frac{C_{0}}{T}\int_{|\tilde{z}|}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma}{r^{2}(\log(\frac{r}{\delta}+10))}dr\leq\gamma\frac{C_{0}}{T|\tilde{z}|}=\frac{\gamma}{|z|}.

Hence one has

\displaystyle B_{3}\leq C_{d}\eta^{-1}(1+L)^{2}\left(\rho_{T}(|z|)+{\gamma}\right)\lesssim\tilde{C}_{4}\omega(|\tilde{z}|),\ \ \text{where}\ \tilde{C}_{4}=2{C_{d}\eta^{-1}(1+L)^{2}},

where we take

3.19

3.2.
Uniqueness

First we define

\rho(g,\epsilon):=\sup\{r\geq 0:|g(x+h)-g(x)|\leq\epsilon,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},|h|\leq r\}.

Our main theorem for uniqueness in high dimension is as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
be two classical solutions of the Muskat equation (
3.1
) with
\|\nabla f_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq L
,
\rho(\nabla f_{i}(t),\frac{2}{d+1})\geq\rho_{0}
, and

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}f_{i}(t,x)=0

uniformly in
t
for
i=1,2
. Then
M(t)=||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}(t)
satisfies

M^{\prime}(t)\leq C_{d}(1+L)\rho_{0}^{-1}M(t),

where
C_{d}
is a constant only depends on dimension. In particular,

(3.23)

||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}(t)\leq||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}(0)\exp\left((C_{d}(1+L)\rho_{0}^{-1})t\right).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
M(t)=\sup_{x}(f_{1}(t,x)-f_{2}(t,x))
. There exists a point
x_{t}
such that

M(t)=f_{1}(t,x_{t})-f_{2}(t,x_{t}),

then we will get

(3.24)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}(f_{1}-f_{2})(x_{t})\geq 0,\qquad\nabla_{x}(f_{1}-f_{2})(x_{t})=0.

We deduce from the equation (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}(t,x_{t})-\partial_{t}f_{2}(t,x_{t})

\displaystyle\ \ =\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{1}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})\rangle^{{d+1}}}-\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{2}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})\rangle^{d+1}}\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}.

We first claim that for any
|\alpha|<\rho_{0}
, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})\rangle^{{d+1}}}-\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})\rangle^{d+1}}\leq 0.

To see this, we consider

N(a,b):=\frac{b-a}{\langle a\rangle^{d+1}}.

We take derivative in
a
and get

\displaystyle\partial_{a}N(a,b)=\frac{-1}{\langle a\rangle^{d+1}}\left(1-(d+1)\frac{a(a-b)}{\langle a\rangle^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{-1}{\langle a\rangle^{d+1}}\left(1-\frac{d+1}{2}|a-b|\right).

Thus if
|a-b|\leq\frac{2}{d+1}
, then
N(a,b)
is non-increasing in
a
. Note that when
|\alpha|\leq\rho_{0}
, we have
|\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})|\leq\frac{2}{d+1}
. Then by (
3.24
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})\rangle^{{d+1}}}-\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})\rangle^{d+1}}

\displaystyle=N(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t}),\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}(x_{t}))-N(\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t}),\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}(x_{t}))\leq 0.

It remains to consider
|\alpha|>\rho_{0}
. We have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}(t,x_{t})-\partial_{t}f_{2}(t,x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\alpha|>\rho_{0}}\left(\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{1}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})\rangle^{{d}}}-\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{2}(x_{t})-\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})\rangle^{d+1}}\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\alpha|>\rho_{0}}\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla f_{1}(x_{t})\left(\frac{1}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})\rangle^{{d}}}-\frac{1}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})\rangle^{{d}}}\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{|\alpha|>\rho_{0}}\left|\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{1}(x_{t})\rangle^{d+1}}-\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f_{2}(x_{t})\rangle^{d+1}}\right|\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}.

By the elementary inequality

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\langle a\rangle^{d}}-\frac{1}{\langle b\rangle^{d}}\right|+\left|\frac{a}{\langle a\rangle^{d+1}}-\frac{a}{\langle a\rangle^{d+1}}\right|\lesssim_{d}|a-b|,

we obtain that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}(t,x_{t})-\partial_{t}f_{2}(t,x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}M(t)(1+L)\int_{|\alpha|>\rho_{0}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\rho_{0}^{-1}(1+L)M(t).

This yields that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M(t)\leq C_{d}\rho_{0}^{-1}(1+L)M(t).

By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain (
3.23
). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f
be a classical solution to the Muskat equation with initial data satisfying
f^{\prime}_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{C^{3}}\leq R
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(R,T)>0
such that
\operatorname{supp}f_{0}\subset B_{\varepsilon_{1}^{-1}}(0)
and for any
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{1}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(\|\partial_{x}(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon}f(t))\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{xx}(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon}f(t))\|_{L^{\infty}})\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}.

where we denote
\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon}(x)=1-\chi(\varepsilon x)
.

Proof.

We have the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\alpha\cdot\nabla f(t,x)-\delta_{\alpha}f(t,x)}{\left\langle\Delta_{\alpha}f(t,x)\right\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}:=N(f)(t,x).

We have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}}f)=N(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}}f)+S(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}},f),

where
S(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}},f)=\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}}N(f)-N(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}}f)
is the commutator. For any
i,j\in 1,\cdots,d
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{ij}(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}}f)=\partial_{ij}N(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}}f)+\partial_{ij}S(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}},f).

Denote
\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}}f=g
, note that

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}N(g)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\partial_{ij}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{ij}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle-(d+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\partial_{j}g-\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{j}g)\Delta_{\alpha}g\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{i}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle-(d+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(E_{\alpha}\partial_{i}g\Delta_{\alpha}g+E_{\alpha}g\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{i}g)\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{j}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+3}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}

\displaystyle+(d+1)(d+3)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(E_{\alpha}g(\Delta_{\alpha}g)^{2}\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{i}g\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{j}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+5}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}.

Take
x_{i,j}
such that
\partial_{ij}g(t,x_{ij})=\sup_{x}\partial_{ij}g(t,x)
. Denote
M_{ij}(t)=\sup_{x}\partial_{ij}g(t,x)
and
A(t)=\|\nabla^{2}g(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Then one has
\nabla\partial_{ij}g(t,x_{ij})=0
and
\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{ij}g(t,x_{ij})\geq 0
. Moreover, one has

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\partial_{j}g\Delta_{\alpha}g\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{i}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq RA(t)\int_{|\alpha|\geq 1}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\alpha|\leq 1}\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\partial_{j}g\left(\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}g\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{i}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}-\frac{\Delta_{-\alpha}g\Delta_{-\alpha}\partial_{i}g}{\langle\Delta_{-\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}\right)\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}g\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{i}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}-\frac{\Delta_{-\alpha}g\Delta_{-\alpha}\partial_{i}g}{\langle\Delta_{-\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}\right|\leq|\alpha|R^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\hat{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\partial_{j}g\Delta_{\alpha}g\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{i}g}{\langle\Delta_{\alpha}g\rangle^{d+1}}\frac{d\alpha}{|\alpha|^{d}}\right|\lesssim(1+R)^{2}A(t).

Other terms can be estimated similarly, which leads to

\displaystyle\left.\partial_{ij}N(g)\right|_{x=x_{ij}}\lesssim(1+R)^{2}A(t).

On the other hand, we have
|\nabla\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}}|\leq\varepsilon_{1}
. It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{ij}S(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{1}},f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)^{3}.

Then we have for any
t\in[0,T]

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{ij}(t)\lesssim(1+R)^{2}A(t)+\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)^{3}.

Take
\varepsilon_{1}
small enough such that
\operatorname{supp}f_{0}\subset B_{\varepsilon_{1}^{-1}}(0)
, then
M_{ij}(0)=0
.
Integrate (
4.1
) in time we obtain

\displaystyle M_{ij}(t)\lesssim(1+R)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}A(\tau)d\tau+\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)^{3}t.

We can estimate
m_{ij}(t)=\sup_{x}(-\partial_{ij}g(t,x))
similarly. Summing up in
i,j
, we obtain

\displaystyle A(t)\lesssim(1+R)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}A(\tau)d\tau+\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)^{3}t.

By Gronwall’s inequality we have

\displaystyle A(t)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)^{3}t+\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)^{5}\int_{0}^{t}\tau e^{\tau(1+R)^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)^{3}T+\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)^{3}Te^{(1+R)^{2}T},\ \ \forall t\in[0,T].

This implies the result in the lemma by taking
\epsilon_{1}
small enough such that
(1+R)^{3}Te^{(1+R)^{2}T}\leq\epsilon_{1}^{-\frac{1}{4}}
.

The following lemma bounds the diffusive term from above by strictly negative terms.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the assumption (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{\alpha}f_{x}(y)-\delta_{\alpha}f_{x}(x)\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq-2\int_{0}^{z}\delta_{\alpha}\rho_{t}(z)+\delta_{-\alpha}\rho_{t}(z)\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}+2\int_{z}^{\infty}\rho_{t}(\alpha+z)-\rho_{t}(\alpha)-\rho_{t}(z)\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^{2}},

where
z=|x-y|
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle f_{x}(\alpha)\leq f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(\alpha-y)-\rho_{t}(x-y),\ \forall\alpha\geq y,

\displaystyle f_{x}(\alpha)\geq f_{x}(y)-\rho_{t}(x-\alpha)+\rho_{t}(x-y),\ \forall\alpha\leq x.

By definition, for
\epsilon>0
small enough, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{-\epsilon}(\delta_{\alpha}f_{x}(y)-\delta_{\alpha}f_{x}(x))\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}=-\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha+\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(x+\alpha)-f_{x}(y+\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha.

We write the latter term as

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(x+\alpha)-f_{x}(y+\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha=\int_{x+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(\alpha)}{(\alpha-x)^{2}}d\alpha-\int_{y+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(\alpha)}{(\alpha-y)^{2}}d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{x+\epsilon}^{\infty}f_{x}(\alpha)(\frac{1}{(\alpha-x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(\alpha-y)^{2}})d\alpha-\int_{y+\epsilon}^{x+\epsilon}\frac{f_{x}(\alpha)}{(\alpha-y)^{2}}dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x+\epsilon}^{\infty}(f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(\alpha-y)-\rho_{t}(x-y))(\frac{1}{(\alpha-x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(\alpha-y)^{2}})d\alpha-\int_{y+\epsilon}^{x+\epsilon}\frac{f_{x}(\alpha)}{(\alpha-y)^{2}}d\alpha.

Direct calculation leads to

\displaystyle\int_{x+\epsilon}^{\infty}(f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(\alpha-y)-\rho_{t}(x-y))(\frac{1}{(\alpha-x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(\alpha-y)^{2}})d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(x-y+\alpha)-\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha-\int_{x-y+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(x)+\rho(\alpha)-\rho(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{\epsilon}^{x-y+\epsilon}\frac{f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(x-y+\alpha)-\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha+\int_{x-y+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{t}(x-y+\alpha)-\rho_{t}(\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{2x-y+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(\alpha-y)-\rho_{t}(x-y)}{(\alpha-x)^{2}}d\alpha

\displaystyle\ \ \quad\quad-\int_{x+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(\alpha-y)-\rho_{t}(x-y)}{(\alpha-y)^{2}}d\alpha-\int_{x-y+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{x-y+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{t}(x-y+\alpha)-\rho_{t}(x-y)-\rho_{t}(\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{-\epsilon}(\delta_{\alpha}f_{x}(y)-\delta_{\alpha}f_{x}(x))\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha+\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(x-y+\alpha)-\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{x-y+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(\alpha)-\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha-\int_{\epsilon}^{x-y+\epsilon}\frac{f_{x}(y+\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\epsilon}^{x-y+\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha+\int_{\epsilon}^{x-y+\epsilon}\frac{f_{x}(x)+\rho_{t}(x-y+\alpha)-\rho_{t}(x-y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{\epsilon}^{x-y+\epsilon}\frac{f_{x}(\alpha+y)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha+\int_{x-y+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{t}(x-y+\alpha)-\rho_{t}(x-y)-\rho_{t}(\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha.

The integral in
(0,+\infty)
is similar. Since
f_{x}(x)-f_{x}(y+\alpha)\leq\rho_{t}(x-y-\alpha)
, we get the result by taking
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. This completes the proof.

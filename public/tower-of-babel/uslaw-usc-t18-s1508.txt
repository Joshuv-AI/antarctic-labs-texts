18 U.S.C. § 1508 — Recording, listening to, or observing proceedings of grand or petit juries while deliberating or voting
Citation: us:usc:18:1508
Creator: United States Congress
Year: 2026
Source-URL: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1508
Context: United States Code, Title 18 (Crimes And Criminal Procedure); Part I (Crimes); Chapter 73 (Obstruction Of Justice). Current through Public Law 119-134, as published by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel.

Whoever knowingly and willfully, by any means or device whatsoever—
(a)
records, or attempts to record, the proceedings of any grand or petit jury in any court of the United States while such jury is deliberating or voting; or
(b)
listens to or observes, or attempts to listen to or observe, the proceedings of any grand or petit jury of which he is not a member in any court of the United States while such jury is deliberating or voting—
shall be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than one year, or both.
Nothing in paragraph (a) of this section shall be construed to prohibit the taking of notes by a grand or petit juror in any court of the United States in connection with and solely for the purpose of assisting him in the performance of his duties as such juror.

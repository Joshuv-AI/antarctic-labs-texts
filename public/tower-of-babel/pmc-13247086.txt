Switchable annulation paths to diverse N-bridged bicyclic scaffolds via ligand-directed dicarbonylation
Authors: Yu-Kun Liu, Peng Yang, Le-Cheng Wang, Zhi-Peng Bao, Xiao-Feng Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractN-Bridged bicyclic scaffolds bearing 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane and 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptane cores have emerged as privileged bioisosteres of piperidine and meta-substituted pyridine, offering enhanced conformational rigidity and improved pharmacokinetic profiles. However, existing synthetic approaches are typically restricted to single scaffolds, limiting scaffold diversity. Here, we report a ligand-controlled, palladium-catalyzed tandem dicarbonylation of readily available cyclobutenols and amines, enabling divergent access to both 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane and 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptane scaffolds from the same substrate. This strategy overcomes key challenges associated with strained-ring systems-including ring-opening side reactions and complex regio-, stereo-, and diastereoselective control-through fine-tuning of the catalyst environment. The method demonstrates excellent selectivity, broad substrate compatibility, and operational simplicity, accommodating 20 derivatives of bioactive molecules. This work provides a modular and general platform for the synthesis of structurally distinct N-bridged heterobicycles, addressing a critical gap in the development of bioisosteric nitrogen-containing scaffolds.Subject terms: Homogeneous catalysis, Synthetic chemistry methodology, Diversity-oriented synthesis

Introduction

Analysis of FDA-approved small-molecule drugs from 2013 to 2023 highlights the predominance of nitrogen-containing heterocycles-particularly six-membered ones-which appear in the majority of newly approved therapeutics1,2. Within this context, piperidine- and pyridine-based scaffolds are regarded as among the most prevalent N-heterocyclic frameworks in pharmaceutical chemistry3–7. In recent years, replacing piperidine and pyridine rings with bridged bicyclic scaffolds in drug molecules and bioactive compounds has attracted increasing attention (Fig. 1a)8–10, as this strategy is widely recognized to block metabolically vulnerable sites and increase molecular rigidity, thereby improving physicochemical properties and pharmacokinetic profiles11–14. Notably, 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptanes and piperidine both adopt intrinsically three-dimensional architectures, whereas 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptanes conform to the ‘escape-from-flatland’ design principle. As a result, these scaffolds represent promising bioisosteres of piperidine and meta-substituted pyridine, respectively (Fig. 1b)9,10,15–17. Consequently, the development of modular and efficient strategies for the construction of structurally diverse and bioisosteric nitrogen-containing ring scaffolds from readily accessible starting materials is of urgent importance.

3-Aza-BCHep scaffolds exhibit pronounced conformational rigidity, making them inherently difficult to access through conventional alkylation-based methods18. Consequently, most synthetic approaches toward 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane frameworks rely on cycloaddition reactions, including (3 + 2)19,20and (2 + 2)21–28 processes under metal, thermal, or photocatalytic conditions (Fig. 1c, left). Nevertheless, these strategies are often constrained by the requirement for activated substrates and with limited dipolarophile scope. In contrast, bicyclo[1.1.0]butanes (BCBs) have emerged as privileged strain-release intermediates for constructing bridged bicyclic frameworks29–40. Notably, (3 + 3) cycloadditions with 1,3-dipoles41–47 and dipolar (3 + 2) annulations of vinyl azides have enabled access to 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptane architectures48,49, the latter typically proceeding via subsequent isomerization (Fig. 1c, right). Beyond BCB-based approaches, only a few non-BCB strategies have been reported10, which typically rely on indirect rather than annulation-based transformations. Despite these advances, BCB-based strategies still suffer from pre-functionalization requirements, suboptimal site-selectivity, and limited scaffold diversity, and general methods for the divergent synthesis of structurally distinct N-bridged bicyclic frameworks from simple all-carbon precursors remain elusive.

From a retrosynthetic perspective, both 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane and 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptane scaffolds can be traced back to a common highly strained four-membered precursor, differing only in the connectivity established during annulation. Importantly, such disconnections also reveal the opportunity to introduce C1 units during scaffold assembly, offering a modular means to diversify molecular architectures (Fig. 1c, mid). This observation suggests that a unified synthetic strategy could, in principle, enable divergent access to these two frameworks, provided that the sequence and site-selectivity of C-C and C-N bond formation can be precisely controlled. In particular, we envisioned that an all-carbon, highly strained four-membered framework, bearing multiple reactive sites, could serve as a versatile platform for such divergence when paired with a ligand-controlled catalytic system capable of orchestrating sequential C1 incorporation and bond formation.

Carbonylation reactions using carbon monoxide as an abundant C1 feedstock have become powerful tools for skeletal diversification and atom-economical C-C bond formation50–55. In this context, 1,3-unsaturated compounds represent privileged coupling partners in transition metal-catalyzed carbonylation, owing to their conjugated π-systems and rich reactivity56,57. Nevertheless, dicarbonylation of such substrates remains challenging due to the difficulty of controlling regioselectivity across multiple reactive sites, often leading to competing insertions and product complexity58–63. Although recent Pd-catalyzed studies have demonstrated that careful ligand and condition tuning can enable selective dicarbonylation of 1,3-unsaturated systems (Fig. 1d)64–67, these methods are generally designed to access a specific product outcome. We therefore questioned whether a divergent and tunable dicarbonylation strategy could be developed to selectively access multiple architectures from a single polyreactive substrate, particularly within strained cyclic π-systems (Fig. 1e).

We envisioned that cyclobutenols bearing multiple reactive sites could serve as versatile platforms for ligand-controlled tandem dicarbonylative cyclizations. By fine-tuning the electronic and steric environment of the catalyst, this strategy could enable the divergent and highly selective construction of N-bridged bicyclic frameworks. Importantly, it could also overcome key challenges inherent to tandem processes, including issues of regio-, stereo-, and chemoselectivity, compatibility across sequential steps, and suppression of strain-induced ring-opening68–70. In this study, we report a palladium-catalyzed, ligand-controlled tandem dicarbonylation of cyclobutenols with readily available amines (Fig. 1f). This transformation enables divergent access to compounds bearing either 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane or 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptane scaffolds from the same starting materials, with excellent control over both regio- and diastereoselectivity. Moreover, the method exhibits broad substrate scope and operational simplicity, offering a general and modular strategy for constructing structurally distinct N-bridged heterobicycles.

Result

Reaction development

We initiated the investigation of the tandem carbonylation by employing cyclobutenol 1a and aniline hydrochloride 2a’ as model substrates, with palladium as the catalyst and DCE as the solvent (Fig. 2). Upon initial screening of a series of bisphosphine and nitrogen ligands, Xantphos (L7) was found to afford the desired 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane scaffold 3a in 14% yield (entry 2), with a good regioselectivity (r.r. = 14:1). We next evaluated NiXantphos (L8), which features a bite angle similar to that of Xantphos. Remarkably, under otherwise identical conditions, the use of L8 increased the yield to 45% while maintaining excellent regioselectivity (entry 3). Upon thorough evaluation of various reaction conditions (see Supporting Information), replacement of aniline hydrochloride with aniline and two equivalents of HCl (4 M in 1,4-dioxane), together with increased loading of 1a, higher concentration, and prolonged reaction time, led to a notable improvement in the yield of 3a (entry 4), without compromising regioselectivity (r.r. > 19:1). Moreover, employing a preformed Pd–NiXantphos complex (NiXantphos Pd G3) in combination with increased catalyst loading further enhanced the transformation, furnishing 3a in 73% yield (70% isolated yield) and excellent selectivity (r.r. > 19:1) (entry 5). We then shifted our focus toward the selective synthesis of the more challenging 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptane scaffold (4a), hypothesizing that modulation of ligand coordination through monodentate phosphines might favor the alternative regioisomer. Indeed, various triarylphosphine ligands promoted the formation of 4a, although generally with modest regioselectivity and low yields (entries 6-8). Among monophosphine ligands, MePhos stood out by affording 4a in 53% yield (entry 9), though the regioisomeric ratio (r.r. = 7:1) remained suboptimal. Subsequent evaluation of structurally related ligands (entries 10-12) revealed that increasing steric hindrance at the ortho-position improved regioselectivity, albeit with limited improvement in yield. Notably, SPhos (L15) afforded 4a with excellent regioselectivity (r.r. > 19:1) but a lower yield of 40% (entry 12), compared to MePhos. We further evaluated JohnPhos and structurally related biarylphosphine ligands, which exhibited varying degrees of catalytic activity but failed to deliver satisfactory outcomes (entries 13-15). Compared to SPhos, the use of L19 resulted in diminished regioselectivity under the same conditions (entry 16). Interestingly, switching to a DCE/MeCN mixed solvent and increasing CO pressure simultaneously addressed the poor regioselectivity issue observed with MePhos and significantly improved the reaction yield (entry 18). Finally, increasing the MePhos loading further boosted the reaction efficiency, affording 4a in 74% yield (71% isolated yield) with excellent regioselectivity (r.r. > 19:1; entry 19). Notably, cyclopentanol and cyclohexanol derivates were also tested under the standard conditions, but no desired product was detected.

Substrate scope

With the optimal reaction conditions in hand, we began evaluating the generality of the NiXantphos Pd G3-catalyzed 2,3-tandem dicarbonylation of cyclobutenol 1a with amines in DCE. Gratifyingly, all examined substrates provided the desired products with excellent regioselectivity (r.r. > 19:1) and diastereoselectivity (d.r. > 20:1). As shown in Fig. 3 (left), starting with aniline 2a under standard conditions, product 3a was obtained in 70% isolated yield. The structure of 3a was further confirmed by single-crystal X-ray diffraction (CCDC: 2440434). We next investigated the influence of substitution position by introducing methyl groups at different positions on the aryl ring. The reaction proceeded smoothly, affording 3b-3d in good yields under standard conditions. Notably, the transformation was tolerant of both electron-donating and electron-withdrawing groups at the para-position, delivering products 3e-3p in moderate to good yields. Representative substituents included electron-donating groups such as OMe, t-Bu, and OBn, whereas electron-withdrawing groups such as OCF₃, CN, Ac, OAc, Ms, and CF₃, were also well accommodated. Halogenated anilines participated effectively, offering potential for downstream functionalization via cross-coupling or other transformations. A structurally complex amine, exemplified by a benzimidazole-thiazole derivative, was compatible as well, affording 3q in 42% yield and underscoring the applicability of this protocol to bioactive and architecturally complex scaffolds. Similarly, meta-substituted substrates such as 3r reacted efficiently, demonstrating the method’s broad functional group tolerance. Moreover, multiply substituted anilines were viable coupling partners, furnishing 3s-3u in good yields and showcasing the synthetic versatility of this transformation. Furthermore, heteroarylamines were well tolerated under the optimized conditions, giving products 3 v and 3w in moderate yields. Finally, to extend the substrate scope beyond anilines, we replaced the aromatic amines with aliphatic amine hydrochloride salts. Under the standard conditions, representative examples such as benzylamine, butylamine, and 3-chloropropylamine hydrochloride successfully furnished the corresponding products (3x-3z) in 27-30% yields, indicating the method’s potential applicability to non-aromatic amine substrates, albeit with reduced efficiency at the current stage.

We next explored the substrate scope of the tandem 1,3-dicarbonylative annulation under the optimal conditions (Fig. 3, right). All examined reactions exhibited excellent regioselectivity (r.r. > 19:1). Under the standard conditions, aniline hydrochloride 2a’ smoothly furnished the 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptane derivative 4a with 71% isolated yield. The structure of 4a was confirmed by single-crystal X-ray diffraction (CCDC: 2463827). Installation of methyl groups at different positions on the aryl ring (4b-4d) led to moderate variations in reaction efficiency, with all substrates affording the corresponding products in good yields. para-Substituted halogenated aniline hydrochlorides reacted efficiently (4f-4h). In addition, a variety of electron-donating and electron-withdrawing groups were well tolerated (4e, 4i-4p). Electron-donating substituents such as OMe, t-Bu, and OBn, as well as electron-withdrawing groups including OCF₃, CN, Ac, OAc, Ms, and CF₃, all afforded the desired products in moderate to good yields. The reaction accommodated a structurally complex heteroaryl amine hydrochloride, affording 4q in 60% yield. Substrates bearing meta-OMe or ortho-F substituents (4r, 4 s) were also well tolerated, further demonstrating the robustness of the transformation. Multiply substituted aniline hydrochlorides proved viable, affording 4t and 4 u in 45% and 79% yields, respectively. Heteroarylamines participated effectively in the annulation, providing products 4 v and 4w in 79% and 60% yields. Finally, non-aromatic amines were evaluated under the same conditions. Benzylamine hydrochloride afforded 4x in 71% yield, while aliphatic amine hydrochloride salts furnished 4 y and 4z in 42% and 34% yields, respectively, demonstrating the applicability of this strategy to a broader range of amine classes.

With the aim of expanding the substrate scope of cyclobutenol derivatives, we next evaluated a diverse set of aryl and non-aryl-substituted cyclobutenols under both catalytic systems (Fig. 4). We first examined aryl-substituted cyclobutenols bearing electron-donating or electron-withdrawing substituents. Under NiXantphos Pd G3 catalysis, substrates with para-substituents such as Me, OMe, t-Bu, F, Cl, and CF₃ were well tolerated, affording the corresponding products 3aa-3af in 42-72% yields. Similarly, under MePhos-catalyzed conditions, comparable reactivity was observed, delivering products 4aa-4af in 44-67% yields. A meta-F substituted cyclobutenol smoothly underwent the annulation, furnishing the products 3ag and 4ag in 51% and 54% yields, respectively. Notably, an ortho-Me substituted substrate also gave 3ah in 66% yield with excellent regio- and diastereoselectivity. In contrast, the same substitution led to poor reactivity and loss of selectivity under the MePhos system (4ah: 24% yield, r.r. = 1:1), likely due to steric hindrance. Next, structurally more complex and heteroaryl-substituted cyclobutenols were evaluated. Fused aromatic systems such as naphthyl, benzothiophene, and thiophene derivatives afforded the desired products 3ai-3ak in moderate yields. These same substrates also reacted smoothly under the MePhos catalytic system, affording 4ai-4ak in 47-61% yields. Additionally, non-aromatic cyclobutenol was compatible under NiXantphos Pd G3 catalysis, delivering product 3al in 46% yield, whereas its MePhos-catalyzed counterpart 4al exhibited diminished reactivity, affording 24% yield. To further probe steric and electronic effects at the bridgehead position, we modified the original methyl group. Replacing it with larger linear alkyl chains such as n-butyl (3am, 4am) and n-hexyl (3an, 4an) was well tolerated, providing products in 31-42% yields. Interestingly, while the unsubstituted cyclobutenol smoothly delivered the azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane product 3ao in 44% yield, the same substrate failed to produce the 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptane scaffold 4ao under the alternative conditions, with no detectable product observed, underscoring the divergent steric constraints of the two annulation systems.

With the increasing prominence of late-stage functionalization (LSF) in the discovery of biologically active molecules71, there is a pressing need to develop synthetic strategies capable of accommodating structural complexity and functional group diversity. To further demonstrate the synthetic utility and functional group tolerance of this divergent annulation strategy, we explored its application in the late-stage modification of bioactive molecules and complex amine derivatives (Fig. 5). We began by evaluating amino acid derivatives bearing primary amino groups. Four representative substrates-derived from L-alanine, L-valine, L-2-phenylglycine, and L-phenylalanine-underwent smooth annulation under the two reaction manifolds, delivering the desired products 3ap-3as and 4ap-4as in 37-57% yields. Encouraged by these results, we next examined the late-stage functionalization of clinically relevant drug derivatives, including Ibuprofen, Gemfibrozil, Naproxen, and Loxoprofen. Gratifyingly, both annulation systems proved effective across all tested substrates, affording the corresponding cyclized products 3at-3aw and 4at-4aw in moderate to good yields ranging from 44% to 74%. This broad tolerance underscores the method’s promise for medicinal chemistry applications. Finally, to further assess the scope, we applied the protocol to complex natural product derivatives. Late-stage incorporation of L-menthol and Vitamin E analogs proceeded successfully, delivering 3ax-3ay and 4ax-4ay in 46-51% yields. These examples further support the strategy’s effectiveness in accommodating sterically and electronically diverse molecular frameworks.

Synthetic applications

To further assess the synthetic utility of this divergent tandem carbonylation strategy, a series of downstream transformations were carried out (Fig. 6a). Initially, gram-scale transformations of 1a and 2a were conducted under Condition A and Condition B, affording 3a and 4a. While a slight reduction in yield was observed, both reactions proceeded with excellent regio- and diastereoselectivity, demonstrating the scalability and reliability of the protocol. Subsequent treatment with borane (BH₃·THF) resulted in complete reduction of the diketone motif, delivering compounds 5 and 9 in excellent yields (95% and 87%, respectively). Notably, these reduced products represent saturated bioisosteres of piperidine- and pyridine-based scaffolds. In contrast, selective monoreduction was achieved using NaBH₄, affording compound 6 in 64% yield. Exposure of 3a to basic conditions triggered ring opening to give product 7. Furthermore, oxidative deprotection of the N-PMP group using ceric ammonium nitrate (CAN) furnished the corresponding free amides 8 and 10.

Synthetic applications of obtained 3-aza-bridged bicyclic scaffolds.

a Synthetic transformation. b Constructing the Belaperidone skeleton via annulation.

To further demonstrate the pharmaceutical relevance of the synthesized scaffolds, cyclobutenol 1e was converted into compound 13, which features a 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane core bearing a bridgehead methyl substituent (Fig. 6b). The sequence commenced with tandem dicarbonylative annulation of 1e and PMP-protected aniline under the standard conditions, providing amide intermediate 11 in 63% yield. Subsequently, CAN-mediated oxidative deprotection afforded free amide 12 (54%), which was then reduced with LiAlH₄ to furnish amine 13 in 76% yield. Importantly, the resulting scaffold exhibits close structural similarity to the core framework of Belaperidone, a clinically relevant antipsychotic agent, thereby underscoring the potential of this strategy for accessing medicinally valuable molecular architectures72.

Mechanistic investigations

To gain mechanistic insights into the ligand-controlled divergent tandem dicarbonylative annulation, a series of control and kinetic experiments were performed (Fig. 7). We first sought to verify the formation and relevance of a putative common intermediate (Fig. 7a). Subjecting cyclobutenol 1a and aniline salt 2a′ to two distinct catalytic systems-biphosphine-based (Condition C) and monophosphine-based (Condition D)-consistently furnished intermediate 14 in comparable yields (37-38%). The structure of 14 was confirmed by single-crystal X-ray diffraction (CCDC: 2501737). To establish the viability of 14 as a productive intermediate, it was individually subjected to Conditions A and B, affording the respective annulation products 3a and 4a in 62% and 41% yields (Fig. 7b). These results establish 14 as a pivotal intermediate that diverges into two distinct product pathways under different ligand environments. Further kinetic studies provided time-resolved profiles for both the intermediate and the final products (Fig. 7c). Under Condition A, 14 rapidly accumulated, peaking at ~2 h, and was then gradually converted to 3a, which followed a sigmoidal growth curve and plateaued by 10 h. A similar trend was observed under Condition B, where 14 reached peak levels within 2 h and were subsequently consumed over 1–8 h, coinciding with a sharp increase in 4a formation. After 10 h, the concentrations of both 14 and 4a reached a steady state. Collectively, these observations delineate 14 as a pivotal enamine intermediate that serves as a shared precursor for both annulation outcomes, with the ligand environment dictating the downstream selectivity.

To further elucidate the mechanism of the divergent annulation, isotopic labeling experiments were performed under Conditions A-C using deuterated anilines (2a-d or 2a′-d) and cyclobutenol 1a (Fig. 7d). Under Condition A, the annulation of 1a with 2a-d delivered the deuterated product 3a-d1, in which deuterium incorporation was observed at C1 (11%), C2 (12%), C4 (10%), and C5 (43%). This distribution aligns with a mechanism involving β-hydroxyl elimination followed by sequential Pd-H/D insertions. The prominent incorporation at C5 and minor incorporation at C4 suggest the occurrence of multiple insertion processes; however, the origin of deuterium incorporation at C4 cannot be unambiguously assigned at this stage. To probe this further, the reaction was conducted under Condition B using deuterated aniline hydrochloride 2a′-d, affording 4a-d1, which exhibited deuterium incorporation at C5 (38%) and at either C2 or C4 (average 5%). Due to signal overlap and symmetry in the ¹H NMR spectra, precise assignment of deuteration between C2 and C4 was not possible. Interestingly, when the enamine intermediate was independently generated under Condition C using 2a′-d, the isolated compound 14-d1 displayed deuterium incorporation at C2 (7%) and C5 (40%), with no detectable incorporation at C4. This contrast is most consistent with the proposal that deuterium incorporation at C4 in 3a-d1 and 4a-d1 arises during the second carbonylation event, following formation of intermediate 14, rather than during the initial insertion steps. To exclude the possibility of post-synthetic H/D exchange, control experiments were performed wherein non-deuterated 3a or 4a were treated with 2a-d or 2a′-d under standard conditions. No deuterium incorporation was detected in either case (Fig. 7e), reinforcing that the observed labeling originates from mechanistically relevant steps within the catalytic cycle.

Based on experimental findings and prior studies73–77, a plausible catalytic mechanism is proposed to account for the ligand-controlled divergent formation of N-bridged bicyclic scaffolds via tandem dicarbonylation of cyclobutenols (Fig. 8). In catalytic cycle A, cyclobutenol 1a undergoes acid- or heat-mediated dehydration to generate a highly strained conjugated diene species 1a′. In parallel, the palladium catalyst ligated by NiXantphos (L8) reacts with acid to form a Pd-H species A. Regioselective hydropalladation of diene 1a′ by Pd-H species A furnishes the alkenyl-Pd intermediate Int A. Subsequent coordination and migratory insertion of CO, followed by nucleophilic trapping by aniline, affords the enamine-type acylated intermediate 14 with concomitant regeneration of the Pd-H species A. Re-engagement of intermediate 14 with Pd-H species A, followed by a second carbonylation event, gives rise to intermediate Int B, which undergoes migratory insertion to generate the acyl-Pd species Int C. Finally, intramolecular nucleophilic addition of the amide moiety onto the acyl-Pd center delivers the 2,3-cyclized product 3a, with regeneration of Pd-H species A to re-enter the catalytic cycle. In catalytic cycle B under acidic or thermal conditions, cyclobutenol 1a similarly undergoes dehydration to form the common diene intermediate 1a′. In this pathway, the palladium catalyst ligated by MePhos (L12) generates a distinct Pd-H species B. Regioselective hydropalladation of 1a′ affords alkenyl-Pd intermediate Int D, which undergoes carbonylation and nucleophilic trapping by aniline hydrochloride to furnish the same enamine-type intermediate 14, accompanied by regeneration of Pd-H species B. Subsequent carbonylation of intermediate 14 produces intermediate Int E, which undergoes ligand-controlled rearrangement to form the acyl-Pd complex Int F. Intramolecular nucleophilic attack by the amide then furnishes the 1,3-cyclized product 4a, regenerating Pd-H species B and completing the catalytic cycle. Here is worth to mention that the effect of ligand in selectivity tuning was not obvious in the first CO insertion step, as shown in Fig. 7, both ligands can lead to the same product 14 in our control experiments. In this step, we believe the double bond of cyclobutene also coordinates to Pd and then diminishes the tuning effect of ligands. For the second CO insertion, the effect of ligand becomes crucial: bidentate ligand complex reacted at the less steric position and lead to product 3a; while monophosphine ligand complex reacted at benzylic position and lead to product 4a.

Proposed mechanism.

Possible reaction pathways for palladium-catalyzed tandem dicarbonylation of cyclobutenols and amines to divergent access to both 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptanes and 3-azabicyclo[3.1.1]heptanes.

In summary, we have established a robust and divergent tandem dicarbonylative annulation of cyclobutenols with amines, delivering two distinct classes of N-bridged bicyclic scaffolds with consistently excellent regioselectivity and diastereoselectivity. The transformation proceeds under mild, ligand-controlled conditions and displays broad substrate generality, accommodating amino acids, complex natural product derivatives, and drug analogs. Mechanistic studies uncovered a shared enamine intermediate that branch into two annulation manifolds depending on the ligand environment. The method’s precision, modularity, and late-stage applicability render it a powerful platform for constructing architecturally complex, medicinally relevant nitrogen heterocycles.

Methods

General procedure for the synthesis of 3-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane scaffolds

Condition A: A vial (4 mL) was charged with NiXantphos Pd G3 (5 mol%), cyclobutenol (1, 1.5 equiv., 0.3 mmol) and a stirring bar. The vial was closed by PTFE/white rubber septum (Wheaton 13 mm Septa) and phenolic cap and connected with atmosphere with a needle. The vial was evacuated under vacuum and recharged with argon for three times. After that, DCE (0.7 mL), amine (2, 0.2 mmol, 1.0 equiv.) and 2 equiv. HCl (4 M in 1,4-dioxane, 100 µL), was added with a syringe under nitrogen atmosphere. Subsequently, the vial (or several vials) was placed in an alloy plate, which was transferred into a 300 mL autoclave of the 4560 series from Parr Instruments. After flushing the autoclave three times with CO, a pressure of 40 bar of CO was adjusted at ambient temperature. Then, the reaction was performed for 24 h at 110 °C. After the reaction was complete, the autoclave was cooled down with ice water to room temperature and the pressure was released carefully. The solution was concentrated in vacuo then purified by silica-gel column chromatography using pentane and ethyl acetate to afford the corresponding product 3a-3w, 3aa-3ay.

A vial (4 mL) was charged with NiXantphos Pd G3 (5 mol%), cyclobutenol (1, 1.5 equiv., 0.3 mmol), amine hydrochloride (2’, 1.0 equiv., 0.2 mmol), and a stirring bar.

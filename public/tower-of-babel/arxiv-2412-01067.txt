Harmonic analysis in Dunkl settings
The Anh Bui

Abstract
. Let L L be the Dunkl Laplacian on the Euclidean space ℝ N {\mathbb{R}^{N}} associated with a normalized root R R and a multiplicity function k ⁡ ( ν ) ≥ 0 , ν ∈ R k(\nu)\geq 0,\nu\in R . In this paper, we first prove that the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces associated with the Dunkl Laplacian L L are identical to the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces defined in the space of homogeneous type ( ℝ N , ∥ ⋅ ∥ , d w ) ({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw) , where d ​ w ​ ( x ) = ∏ ν ∈ R ⟨ ν , x ⟩ k ⁡ ( ν ) ​ d ​ x dw({\rm x})=\prod_{\nu\in R}\langle\nu,{\rm x}\rangle^{k(\nu)}d{\rm x} . Next, consider the Dunkl transform denoted by ℱ \mathcal{F} . We introduce the multiplier operator T m T_{m} , defined as T m ​ f = ℱ − 1 ​ ( m ​ ℱ ​ f ) T_{m}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\mathcal{F}f) , where m m is a bounded function defined on ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . Our second aim is to prove multiplier theorems, including the Hörmander multiplier theorem, for T m T_{m} on the Besov and Tribel-Lizorkin spaces in the space of homogeneous type ( ℝ N , ∥ ⋅ ∥ , d w ) ({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw) . Importantly, our findings present novel results, even in the specific case of the Hardy spaces.

and the references therein. For further information, we refer the reader to

for more information and references. Motivated by the extensive research conducted in the Dunkl settings, in this paper we will develop the harmonic analysis on the Dunkl setting. Firstly we would like to introduce the framework of the Dunkl setting in
[
41
,
42
]
(see also
[
4
,
3
,
20
,
21
]
).

On the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
we consider a normalized root system
R
and a multiplicity function
k(\nu)\geq 0,\nu\in R
. Define

dw({\rm x})=\prod_{\nu\in R}|\langle{\rm x},\nu\rangle|^{k(\nu)}d{\rm x}.

In this paper, for
p\in(0,\infty)
we denote by
L^{p}(dw)
the set of all measurable functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}:=\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|f({\rm x})|^{p}dw({\rm x})\Big)^{1/p}<\infty.

We consider the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
with the usual scalar product and the induced norm. For a nonzero vector
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
the reflection
\sigma_{\nu}
with respect to the hyperplane
\nu^{\perp}
orthogonal to
\nu
is given by

(1)

\sigma_{\nu}{\rm x}={\rm x}-2\frac{\langle{\rm x},\nu\rangle}{\|\nu\|^{2}}\nu.

We denote by
G
the Weyl group generated by the reflections
\sigma_{\nu},\nu\in R
.

A finite set
R\subset{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\backslash\{0\}
is called a root system if
\sigma_{\nu}(R)=R
for every
\nu\in R
. We shall consider normalized reduced root systems, that is,
\|\nu\|^{2}=2
for every
\nu\in R
. The finite group
G
generated by the reflections
\sigma_{\nu}
is called the Weyl group (reflection group) of the root system. A multiplicity function is a
G
-invariant function
k:R\to[0,\infty)
which will be fixed and be nonnegative throughout this paper. We will denote by
\mathcal{O}({\rm x})=\{\sigma({\rm x}):\sigma\in R\}
the
G
-orbit of a point
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, and
\mathcal{O}(E)=\bigcup_{{\rm x}\in E}\mathcal{O}({\rm x})
the
G
-orbit of
E\subset{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
.

The number
\mathfrak{N}:=N+\sum_{\nu\in R}k(\nu)
is called the homogeneous dimension of the system, since

(2)

w(B(t{\rm x},tr))=t^{\mathfrak{N}}w(B({\rm x},r))\ \ \text{for}\ \ {\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},t,r>0,

where
B({\rm x},r)=\{{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}:\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\leq r\}
denotes the (closed)
Euclidean ball centered at
{\rm x}
with radius
r>0
. Observe that

(3)

w(B({\rm x},r))\simeq r^{N}\prod_{\nu\in R}(|\langle{\rm x},\nu\rangle|+r)^{k(\nu)}\gtrsim r^{\mathfrak{N}},

so
dw
is a doubling measure, that is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(4)

w(B({\rm x},2r))\leq Cw(B({\rm x},r))\ \ \text{for}\ \ {\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},r>0.

Moreover, by (
3
),

(5)

\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{N}\lesssim\frac{w(B({\rm x},R))}{w(B({\rm x},r))}\lesssim\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{\mathfrak{N}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ {\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},0<r<R.

Finally, define

(6)

d({\rm x},{\rm y})=\min_{\sigma\in G}\|{\rm x}-\sigma({\rm y})\|

as the distance between two
G
-orbits
\mathcal{O}({\rm x})
and
\mathcal{O}({\rm y})
. Obviously,
\mathcal{O}(B({\rm x},r))=\{{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}:d({\rm x},{\rm y})<r\}
; moreover,

w(B({\rm x},r))\leq w(\mathcal{O}(B({\rm x},r)))\leq|G|w(B({\rm x},r))

for all
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
r>0
. We also denote
B^{d}({\rm x},r):=\{{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}:d({\rm x},{\rm y})<r\}
, i.e.,
B^{d}({\rm x},r)=\mathcal{O}(B({\rm x},r))
.

Let
E({\rm x},{\rm y})
be the associated Dunkl kernel, which was introduced in
[
16
]
. It is known that
E({\rm x},{\rm y})
has a unique extension to a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}
. The Dunkl transform is defined by

(7)

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=c_{k}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}E(-i\xi,{\rm x})f({\rm x})dw({\rm x}),

where

c_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{\|{\rm x}\|^{2}}{2}}dw({\rm x})>0.

It is know that the Dunkl transform, which is initially defined for
f\in L^{1}(dw)
, is an isometry on
L^{2}(dw)
and preserves the Schwartz class of functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The inverse of the Dunkl transform is defined by

{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g({\rm x})=c_{k}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}E(i\xi,{\rm x})g(\xi)dw(\xi).

For
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, the Dunkl translation
\tau_{{\rm x}}
is defined by

(8)

\tau_{{\rm x}}f({\rm y})=c_{k}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}E(i\xi,{\rm x})E(i\xi,{\rm y}){\mathcal{F}}f(\xi)dw(\xi)

is bounded on
L^{2}(dw)
.

In what follows, we use the notation

g({\rm x},{\rm y})=\tau_{{\rm x}}g(-{\rm y})=\tau_{-{\rm y}}g(x).

The Dunkl convolution of two appropriate functions is defined by

(9)

f\ast g({\rm x})=c_{k}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}[{\mathcal{F}}f{\mathcal{F}}g]({\rm x})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}{\mathcal{F}}f(\xi){\mathcal{F}}g(\xi)E({\rm x},i\xi)dw(\xi),

or equivalently,

f\ast g({\rm x})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}f({\rm y})\tau_{{\rm x}}g(-{\rm y})dw({\rm y})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}f({\rm y})g({\rm x},{\rm y})dw({\rm y}).

We now consider the Dunkl operators
T_{\xi}
defined by

T_{\xi}f({\rm x})=\partial_{\xi}f({\rm x})+\sum_{\nu\in R}\frac{k(\nu)}{2}\langle\nu,\xi\rangle\frac{f({\rm x})-f(\sigma_{\nu}({\rm x}))}{\langle\nu,{\rm x}\rangle},

where
\partial_{\xi}f
denotes the directional derivative of
f
in the direction
\xi
.

Set
T_{j}=T_{e_{j}}
, where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{N}\}
is the canonical basis for
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. The Dunkl Laplacian is defined by

\displaystyle L:

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}-\sum_{\nu\in R}k(\nu)\delta_{\nu}f({\rm x}),

where
\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}
is the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
\delta_{\nu}
is defined by

\delta_{\nu}f({\rm x})=\frac{\partial_{\nu}f({\rm x})}{\langle\alpha,{\rm x}\rangle}-\frac{f({\rm x})-f(\sigma_{\nu}({\rm x}))}{\langle\alpha,{\rm x}\rangle^{2}}.

It is well-known that the operator
L
is a non-negative and self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(dw)
and generates a semigroup
e^{-tL}
whose kernel
h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})
is defined by

h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})=c_{k}^{-1}(2t)^{-\mathfrak{N}/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\|{\rm x}\|^{2}+\|{\rm y}\|^{2}}{4t}\Big)E\Big(\frac{{\rm x}}{\sqrt{2t}},\frac{{\rm y}}{\sqrt{2t}}\Big),\ \ \ {\rm x},{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},t>0.

See for example
[
2
,
38
]
.

The paper focuses on two primary objectives. Firstly, we will investigate some characterizations of the Besov spaces and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces in
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
via the heat semigroup
e^{-tL}
. Secondly, we will prove the boundedness of the multiplier of the Dunkl transform on these function spaces.

For the first aim, we would like to go back the theory of Hardy spaces in
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
. Since
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
is a homogneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss, one can define the Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{\rm CW}(dw)
with
\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+1}<p\leq 1
via the atomic decomposition. For convenience, we provide the definition of the Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{\rm CW}(dw)
with
\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+1}<p\leq 1
in
[
11
]
, which can be viewed as extensions to the Hardy spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
in the influential paper of Fefferman and Stein
[
22
]
(see also
[
47
,
46
]
). For
0<p\leq 1
, we say that a function
a
is a
(p,2)
atom if there exists a ball
B
such that

(i)

supp
a\subset B
;

(ii)

\|a\|_{L^{2}(dw)}\leq w(B)^{1/2-1/p}
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\int a({\rm x})dw({\rm x})=0
.

For
p=1
the atomic Hardy space
H^{1}_{\rm CW}(dw)
is defined as follows. We say that a function
f\in H^{1}_{\rm CW}(dw)
, if
f\in L^{1}(dw)
and there exist a sequence
(\lambda_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell^{1}
and a sequence of
(p,2)
-atoms
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
. We set

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\rm CW}(dw)}=\inf\Big\{\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|:f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\Big\},

where the infimum is taken over all possible atomic decomposition of
f
.

For
0<p<1
, as in
[
11
]
, we need to introduce the Lipschitz space
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
. We say that the function
f\in\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}
if there exists a constant
c>0
, such that

|f({\rm x})-f({\rm y})|\leq cw(B)^{\alpha}

for all ball
B
and
{\rm x},{\rm y}\in B
. The best constant
c
above can be taken to be the norm of
f
and is denoted by
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha}}
.

Now let
0<p<1
and
\alpha=1/p-1
. We say that a function
f\in H^{p}_{\rm CW}(dw)
, if
f\in(\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha})^{*}
and there is a sequence
(\lambda_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell^{p}
and a sequence of
(p,2)
-atoms
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
. Furthermore, we set

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\rm CW}(dw)}=\inf\Big\{\Big(\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}:f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\Big\},

where the infimum is taken over all possible atomic decomposition of
f
.

Apart from the Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{\rm CW}(dw)
, we can consider the Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{L}(dw)
associated to the Dunkl Laplacian operator
L
.
For
0<p\leq 1
, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}(dw)
is defined as the completion of the set

\left\{f\in L^{2}(dw):\mathcal{S}_{L}f\in L^{p}(dw)\right\}

under the norm
\|f\|_{H^{p}_{L}(dw)}=\|\mathcal{S}_{L}f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}
where

\mathcal{S}_{L}f(x)=\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{d({\rm x},{\rm y})<t}|t^{2}Le^{-tL^{2}}f({\rm y})|^{2}\frac{dw({\rm y})dt}{tw(B({\rm x},t))}\Big]^{1/2}.

It is important to highlight that in the definition of the square function
\mathcal{S}_{L}
we employ the distance
d
defined by (
6
) instead of the Euclidean distance
\|\cdot\|
. A good reason for this choice is that while the heat kernel
h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})
does not satisfy the Gaussian upper bound with respect to the Euclidean distance
\|\cdot\|
, it satisfies the Gaussian upper bound with respect to the distance
d
. See Lemma
2.5
. As a result, the theory of the Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}(dw)
follows directly from existing results in
[
31
,
44
,
5
,
19
]
.

So it is natural to question if the Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{\rm CW}
and
H^{p}_{L}(dw)
are the same when
\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+1}<p\leq 1
. This question is not straightforward since the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rm CW}
corresponds to the Euclidean distance
\|\cdot\|
, while
L
does not satisfy the Gaussian upper bound with respect to the Euclidean distance; moreover, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}(dw)
corresponds to the Euclidean distance
d
. For the case
p=1
, a positive answer was carried out in
[
21
]
. It remains unclear if the approach in
[
21
]
can be extended to the case
\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+1}<p<1
.

Motivated by the aforementioned research, our first primary objective is to establish a more comprehensive result that encompasses not only the Hardy spaces but also the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces in
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
. It is worth noting that the theory of Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces holds significant interest in the field of harmonic analysis and has garnered considerable attention. See for example
[
1
,
28
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
53
]
and the references therein. In the light of the fact that
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
is a space of homogeneous type satisfying the reverse doubling property (i.e., the first inequality in (
5
)), we can define the Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
with
s\in(-1,1),p(s,1)<p<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
and the Triebel-Lizorkin space
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
with
s\in(-1,1),p(s,1)<p,q<\infty
(see Definition
3.2
), following the framework established in
[
27
]
. Here,
p(s,1)=\max\{\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+1},\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+s+1}\}
. As far as we know, these ranges of the indices
s,p,q
are the best in the setting of the space of homogeneous type except from the case
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
.

Moreover, based on the general findings in
[
7
]
, we can also define the Besov space
\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)
and the Triebel-Lizorkin space
\dot{F}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)
associated to the Dunkl Laplacian operator
L
. See Definition
3.9
. It is intriguing to observe that these function spaces exhibit similar properties to their counterparts in the Euclidean setting. For example,
\dot{F}^{0,L}_{p,2}(dw)
coincide with the
L^{p}(dw)
space if
1<p<\infty
and
\dot{F}^{0,L}_{p,2}(dw)
coincide with the Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}(dw)
for
p\in(0,1]
. For further properties such as atomic decomposition and interpolation we refer to Section 3.2 (see also
[
7
]
). Drawing inspiration from these two research streams, our primary result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

(a)

For
s\in(-1,1)
,
p(s,1)<p<\infty
and
0<q<\infty
, the Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
and
\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)
are identical with the equivalent norms.

(b)

For
s\in(-1,1)
and
p(s,1)<p,q<\infty
, the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
and
\dot{F}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)
are identical with the equivalent norms.

To evaluate the impact of Theorem
1.1
, we only consider the particular case when
s=0,q=2
and
\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+1}<p\leq 1
. In this case, Theorem
1.1
(b) reads
\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(dw)\equiv\dot{F}^{0,L}_{p,2}(dw)
. This, along with the fact that
\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(dw)\equiv H^{p}_{\rm CW}(dw)
(see
[
26
, Remark 5.5]
) and
\dot{F}^{0,L}_{p,2}(dw)\equiv H^{p}_{L}(dw)
(see Theorem
3.21
), implies that
H^{p}_{\rm CW}(dw)\equiv H^{p}_{L}(dw)
for
\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+1}<p\leq 1
. This, along with the maximal function characterizations of the Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
via heat kernels of
L
in
[
44
]
, extends the result in
[
21
,
54
]
to the best range of
p
in the theory of Hardy space on the space of homogeneous type. In addition, Theorem
1.1
, together with known results in
[
7
]
, deduces the heat kernel characterizations for the Besov spaces
\dot{F}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)
and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. See Corollary
3.29
.

One of the main difficulties of the theory of the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces is the constructions of distributions. See for example
[
36
,
7
]
. In the Dunkl setting, with the ranges of indices in Theorem
1.1
we can use the distributions in
[
27
,
28
]
to define our function spaces. See Theorem
3.22
. This leads to some advantages to prove the coincidence between the function spaces associated to
L
and the function spaces in the space
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
. Moreover, our approach relies on the atomic decomposition of the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. See Section 3. We note that even the atomic decomposition results for the spaces
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
and
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
are new in the literature. See Theorems
3.6
and
3.7
.

The second main result focuses on multiplier theorems for the Dunkl transform. Let us provide a brief overview of the problem. Let
m
be a bounded function on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. We define

T_{m}f={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(m{\mathcal{F}}f).

In the particular case when the multiplicity function
k\equiv 0
, the Dunkl transform turns out to be the Fourier transform, and the Dunkl multiplier
T_{m}
boils down to the Fourier multiplier. Regarding the boundedness of the Dunkl multiplier
T_{m}
we recall the main result in
[
20
]
. In what follows, we define
\|f\|_{W^{q}_{s}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}=\|(I-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{N}},d{\rm x})}
for
s>0
and
q\in(1,\infty]
, and
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\}
and
a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\psi
be a smooth radial function on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
such that supp
\psi\subset\{\xi:1/4\leq\|\xi\|\leq 4\}
and
\psi\equiv 1
on
\{\xi:1/2\leq\|\xi\|\leq 2\}
. If
m
is a function on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
satisfying the following condition

(10)

\sup_{t>0}\|\psi(\cdot)m(t\cdot)\|_{W^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}<\infty

for some
\alpha>\mathfrak{N}
. Then we have:

(a)

the multiplier
T_{m}
is weak type of
(1,1)
;

(b)

the multiplier
T_{m}
is bounded on
L^{p}(dw)
for
1<p<\infty
;

(c)

the multiplier
T_{m}
is bounded on the Hardy space
H^{1}_{\rm CW}(dw)
.

Moreover, if we assume the additional condition

(11)

\sup_{{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\|\tau_{{\rm y}}f\|_{L^{1}(dw)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(dw)},

then the conclusions (a), (b) and (c) above hold true provided that (
12
) is valid for some
\alpha>\mathfrak{N}/2
.

The condition (
10
) with
\alpha>\mathfrak{N}
is unexpected but is reasonable since we need an extra order of
\mathfrak{N}/2
in the proof of the boundedness of the Dunkl translation on
L^{p}(dw),p\neq 2
. The extra number of derivatives of
\mathfrak{N}/2
can be removed under the condition (
11
). This condition holds true in the rank-one case and the product case as in
[
38
]
.

The second main aim in this paper is as follows. Firstly, we aim to reduce the number of the derivative imposed in the function
m
to
\mathfrak{N}/2
without the extra condition (
11
); however, we have to use the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{\infty}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}
rather than
\|\cdot\|_{W^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}
. Secondly, we extend the boundedness to the Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
and Triebel-Lizorkin space
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha>\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{2}
. Let
\psi
be a smooth radial function defined on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
such that supp
\psi\subset\{\xi:1/4\leq\|\xi\|\leq 4\}
and
\psi\equiv 1
on
\{\xi:1/2\leq\|\xi\|\leq 2\}
. If
m
is a function on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
satisfying the following condition

(12)

\sup_{t>0}\|\psi(\cdot)m(t\cdot)\|_{W^{\infty}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}<\infty.

Then the following hold true.

(a)

The multiplier
T_{m}
is bounded on
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
provided that
s\in(-1,1)
,
p(s,1)<p,q<\infty
and
\alpha>\mathfrak{N}(\frac{1}{1\wedge p\wedge q}-\frac{1}{2})
, i.e.,

\|T_{m}\|_{\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)\to\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)}\lesssim|m(0)|+\sup_{t>0}\|\psi(\cdot)m(t\cdot)\|_{W^{\infty}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}.

(b)

The multiplier
T_{m}
is bounded on
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
provided that
s\in(-1,1)
,
p(s,1)<p,q<\infty
and
\alpha>\mathfrak{N}(\frac{1}{1\wedge p\wedge q}-\frac{1}{2})
, i.e.,

\|T_{m}\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)\to\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)}\lesssim|m(0)|+\sup_{t>0}\|\psi(\cdot)m(t\cdot)\|_{W^{\infty}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}.

Moreover, if (
11
) is assumed, the estimates (a) and (b) hold true with
W^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
taking place of
W^{\infty}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
in (
12
).

Note that our approach can prove that (a) and (b) hold true under the condition (
10
) (without the additional condition (
11
)) with
\alpha>\mathfrak{N}(\frac{1}{1\wedge p\wedge q}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{2}
.

In the particular case when
s=0,q=2
and
p\in(0,1]
, the estimate (a) in Theorem
1.3
boils down to

2.1.
Dyadic cube systems in the Dunkl setting

Let
X
be a metric space, with metric
d
and

\mu
is a nonnegative Borel measure on
X
, which satisfies the doubling property below. For
{\rm x}\in X
and
r>0
we set
B({\rm x},r)=\{{\rm y}\in X:d({\rm x},{\rm y})<r\}
to be the open ball with radius
r>0
and center
{\rm x}\in X
. The doubling
property of
\mu
provides a constant
C>0
such that

(13)

\mu(B({\rm x},2r))\leq C\mu(B({\rm x},r))

for all
{\rm x}\in X
and
r>0
.

Let
0<r<\infty
. The Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{r}
is defined by

(14)

\mathcal{M}_{r}f({\rm x})=\sup_{{\rm x}\in B}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|f({\rm y})|^{r}d\mu({\rm y})\Big)^{1/r}

where the sup is taken over all balls
B
containing
{\rm x}
. We will drop the subscripts
r
when
r=1
.

Let
0<r<\infty
. It is well-known that

(15)

\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}

for all
p>r
.

We recall the Fefferman-Stein vector-valued maximal inequality in
[
25
]
. For
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
0<r<\min\{p,q\}
, we then have for any sequence of measurable functions
\{f_{\nu}\}
,

(16)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\nu}|\mathcal{M}_{r}f_{\nu}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\nu}|f_{\nu}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{p}.

The Young’s inequality and (
16
) imply the following inequality: If
\{a_{\nu}\}\in\ell^{q}\cap\ell^{1}
, then

(17)

\Big\|\sum_{j}\Big(\sum_{\nu}|a_{j-\nu}\mathcal{M}_{r}f_{\nu}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\nu}|f_{\nu}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{p}.

The following result on the existence of a system of dyadic cubes in a space of homogeneous type is just a combination of
[
32

and
[
12
, Proposition 2.5]

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
\delta\leq 1/24
. Then there exist a family of set
\{Q_{\alpha}^{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\alpha\in I_{k}\}
and a set of points
\{{\rm x}_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\alpha\in I_{k}\}
and satisfying

(i)

d({\rm x}_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}},{\rm x}_{Q_{\beta}^{k}})\geq\delta^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha,\beta\in I_{k}
with
\alpha\neq\beta
;

(ii)

\min_{\alpha\in I_{k}}d({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}})\leq\delta^{k}
for all
{\rm x}\in X
;

(iii)

for any

Lemma 2.2

(

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{D}=\{\mathscr{D}_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the system of dyadic cubes in
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
. Let
M>\mathfrak{N}
,
\kappa\in[0,1]
, and
\eta,k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
k\geq\eta
. Assume that
\{f_{Q}\}_{{Q}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}
is a sequence of functions satisfying

(18)

|f_{Q}({\rm x})|\lesssim\Big(\frac{w(Q)}{w(B({\rm x}_{Q},\delta^{\eta}))}\Big)^{\kappa}\Big(1+\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q})}{\delta^{\eta}}\Big)^{-M}.

where
d
is the distance defined by (
6
). Then for
\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{M}<r\leq 1
and a sequence of numbers
\{s_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}
, we have

\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}|s_{Q}|\,|f_{Q}({\rm x})|\lesssim\delta^{-\mathfrak{N}(k-\eta)(1/r-\kappa)}\mathcal{M}^{d}_{r}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}|s_{Q}|\chi_{Q}\Big)({\rm x}).

Proof.

Before coming to the proof we would like to point out the main issue of the lemma lies on the fact that the dyadic system corresponds to the metric
\|\cdot\|
, while the estimate (
18
) corresponds to the distance
d
.

Fix
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. We set

\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}:d({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q})\leq\delta^{\eta}\},Q_{0}=\bigcup\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{0}}Q

and

\mathcal{B}_{j}=\{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}:\delta^{-j+\eta+1}<d({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q})\leq\delta^{-j+\eta}\},Q_{j}=\bigcup\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{j}}Q,\ \ j\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

Then we write

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}|s_{Q}|\,|f_{Q}({\rm x})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{j}}|s_{Q}|\,|f_{Q}({\rm x})|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{j}}|s_{Q}|\Big(\frac{w(Q)}{w(B({\rm x}_{Q},\delta^{\eta}))}\Big)^{\kappa}\Big(1+\frac{d({\rm x},x_{Q})}{\delta^{\eta}}\Big)^{-M}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}E_{j}.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(19)

\displaystyle E_{j}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{j}}\delta^{jM}\Big(\frac{w(Q)}{w(B({\rm x}_{Q},\delta^{\eta}))}\Big)^{\kappa}|s_{Q}|\leq\delta^{jM}\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{j}}\Big(\frac{w(Q)}{w(B({\rm x}_{Q},\delta^{\eta}))}\Big)^{\kappa r}|s_{Q}|^{r}\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{jM}\left\{\int_{Q_{j}}\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{j}}\Big(\frac{w(Q)}{w(B(x_{Q},\delta^{\eta}))}\Big)^{\kappa}w(Q)^{-1/r}|s_{Q}|\chi_{Q}(y)\right]^{r}dw(y)\right\}^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{jM}\left\{\frac{1}{w(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{j}}\Big(\frac{w(Q_{j})}{w(Q)}\Big)^{1/r}\Big(\frac{w(Q)}{w(B(x_{Q},\delta^{\eta}))}\Big)^{\kappa}|s_{Q}|\chi_{Q}(y)\right]^{r}dw(y)\right\}^{1/r}.

It is easy to see that
w(Q_{j})\simeq w(B({\rm x},\delta^{\eta-j}))\simeq w(B({\rm x}_{Q},\delta^{\eta-j}))
, for each
Q\in\mathcal{B}_{j}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{w(Q_{j})}{w(Q)}\Big)^{1/r}\Big(\frac{w(Q)}{w(B(x_{Q},\delta^{\eta}))}\Big)^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-j\mathfrak{N}/r}\delta^{-\mathfrak{N}(k-\eta)(1/r-\kappa)}.

Inserting this into (
19
) gives

\displaystyle E_{j}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{jM}\delta^{-j\mathfrak{N}/r}\delta^{-\mathfrak{N}(k-\eta)(1/r-\kappa)}\Big\{\frac{1}{w(Q_{k})}\int_{Q_{k}}\Big[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}|s_{Q}|\chi_{Q}(y)\Big]^{r}dw({\rm y})\Big\}^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{j(M-\mathfrak{N}/r)}\delta^{-\mathfrak{N}(k-\eta)(1/r-\kappa)}\mathcal{M}^{d}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}|s_{Q}|\chi_{Q}\Big)({\rm x}).

Since
r>\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{M}
, we find that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}E_{j}\lesssim\delta^{-\mathfrak{N}(k-\eta)(1/r-\kappa)}\mathcal{M}^{d}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}|s_{Q}|\chi_{Q}\Big)({\rm x}).

This completes our proof.

In this section, we will prove some kernel estimates for the functional calculus of

]

Lemma 2.4

(
[
21
]
)
.

(a)

For
{\rm x},{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and

,
h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})\geq 0
. In addition,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})dw({\rm y})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}h_{t}({\rm y},{\rm x})dw({\rm y})=1

for all
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and

.

(b)

For every nonnegative integer

and every multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
there are constants

such that

|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial^{\alpha}_{{\rm x}}\partial^{\beta}_{{\rm y}}h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})|\leq Ct^{-(m+|\alpha|/2+|\beta|/2)}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\sqrt{t}))}\exp\Big(\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm y})^{2}}{ct}\Big).

Moreover, if

, then

|\partial_{t}^{m}h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})-\partial_{t}^{m}h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y}^{\prime})|\leq Ct^{-m}\frac{\|{\rm y}-{\rm y}^{\prime}\|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\sqrt{t}))}\exp\Big(\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm y})^{2}}{ct}\Big).

Note that the extra decay
\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{-2}
in (b) plays an essential role in the proofs of our main results.

Since

is a nonnegative self-adjoin operator on

, for each bounded Borel function

, we can defined

\displaystyle F(L)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\lambda)dE(\lambda)

is bounded on

, where

is the spectral resolution of

.

The following lemma addresses some kernel estimates for the functional calculus of
L
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
be an even function. Then the following estimates hold true.

(a)

For every
M>0
and every multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
, the kernel
\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})

\varphi(t\sqrt{L})
satisfies the following estimate

|\partial^{\alpha}_{{\rm x}}\partial^{\beta}_{{\rm y}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})|\lesssim t^{-(|\alpha|+|\beta|)}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{t}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}

for all
{\rm x},{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
t>0
.

(b)

For every
M>0
,

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y}^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm y}-{\rm y}^{\prime}\|}{t}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{t}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}

for all
t>0
and
{\rm x},{\rm y},{\rm y}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
with
\|{\rm y}-{\rm y}^{\prime}\|\leq t
.

(c)

If
\varphi(0)=0
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})dw({\rm y})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm y},{\rm x})dw({\rm y})=0

for all
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
t>0
.

We note that the estimates of Lemma
2.5
without the extra decay
\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{t}\Big)^{-2}
is standard. See for example
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
. The presence of the extra decay needs special properties of the operator

.

Proof.

Item (c) follows from Lemma
2.4
(a) by applying
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
, while item (b) follows directly from (a). Hence, it suffices to prove (a).

It was proved in
[
20
]
that

Lh_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})=\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|^{2}}{4t^{2}}h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})-\frac{N}{2t}h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})-\frac{1}{2t}\sum_{\nu\in R}k(\nu)h_{t}(\sigma_{\nu}({\rm x}),{\rm y}),

or equivalently,

\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|^{2}}{4t}h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})=-tLh_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})+\frac{N}{2}h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu\in R}k(\nu)h_{t}(\sigma_{\nu}({\rm x}),{\rm y}).

Since
h_{t}({\rm x},{\rm y})
is analytic on
\mathbb{C}^{+}=\{z:\Re z\geq 0\}
, we have for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
,

\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|^{2}}{4z}h_{z}({\rm x},{\rm y})=-zLh_{z}({\rm x},{\rm y})+\frac{N}{2}h_{z}({\rm x},{\rm y})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu\in R}k(\nu)h_{z}(\sigma_{\nu}({\rm x}),{\rm y}).

It follows that

\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|^{2}}{4|z|}|h_{z}({\rm x},{\rm y})|\lesssim|zLh_{z}({\rm x},{\rm y})|+|h_{z}({\rm x},{\rm y})|+\sum_{\nu\in R}k(\nu)|h_{z}(\sigma_{\nu}({\rm x}),{\rm y})|.

Arguing similarly to the proof of

, we have

|h_{z}(\sigma_{\nu}({\rm x}),y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\left[w(B({\rm x},\sqrt{\frac{|z|}{\cos\theta}}))w(B({\rm y},\sqrt{\frac{|z|}{\cos\theta}}))\right]^{1/2}}\exp\Big(-c\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm y})^{2}}{|z|}\cos\theta\Big)\frac{1}{(\cos\theta)^{\mathfrak{N}}},\ \ \nu\in R,

and

|zLh_{z}({\rm x},{\rm y})|\lesssim\frac{1}{\left[w(B({\rm x},\sqrt{\frac{|z|}{\cos\theta}}))w(B({\rm y},\sqrt{\frac{|z|}{\cos\theta}}))\right]^{1/2}}\exp\Big(-c\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm y})^{2}}{|z|}\cos\theta\Big)\frac{1}{(\cos\theta)^{\mathfrak{N}+1}}

for all
{\rm x},{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Re z>0\}
where
\theta=\arg z
.

Consequently,

\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|^{2}}{4|z|}|h_{z}({\rm x},{\rm y})|\lesssim\frac{1}{\left[w(B({\rm x},\sqrt{\frac{|z|}{\cos\theta}}))w(B({\rm y},\sqrt{\frac{|z|}{\cos\theta}}))\right]^{1/2}}\exp\Big(-c\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm y})^{2}}{|z|}\cos\theta\Big)\frac{1}{(\cos\theta)^{\mathfrak{N}+1}},

which implies that

for all

.

We now write

∎

2.3.
Spaces of test functions and Calderón reproducing formulae

We first begin with concepts on test functions and distributions on the space of homogeneous type in
[
27
]
.

For
{\rm x},{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
r,\gamma>0
, we set

P_{\gamma}({\rm x},{\rm y};r)=\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|))}\Big(\frac{r}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\Big)^{\gamma}.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
{\rm x}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},r>0,\beta\in(0,1]
and
\gamma>0
. A function defined on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is called a test function type
(x_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)
, denoted by
f\in\mathcal{G}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(i)

for any
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
,
|f({\rm x})|\leq CP_{\gamma}({\rm x}_{1},{\rm x};r)
;

(ii)

for any
{\rm x},{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
satisfying
\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}(r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}_{1}\|)
,

|f({\rm x})-f({\rm y})|\leq C\Big[\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}_{1}\|}\Big]^{\beta}P_{\gamma}({\rm x}_{1},{\rm x};r).

We also define

\|f\|_{\mathcal{G}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)}=\inf\{C\in(0,\infty):\text{(i) and (ii) hold true}\}.

We also define

\mathring{\mathcal{G}}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)=\Big\{f\in{\mathcal{G}}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma):\int_{X}f({\rm x})dw({\rm x})=0\Big\}

under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)}:=\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{G}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)}
.

It is known that both
{\mathcal{G}}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)
and
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)
are Banach spaces for every
\beta\in(0,1]
and
\gamma>0
. Denote
{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma):={\mathcal{G}}(0,1,\beta,\gamma)
and
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma):=\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(0,1,\beta,\gamma)
. Then it was proved that
{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)={\mathcal{G}}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)
and
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)=\mathring{\mathcal{G}}({\rm x}_{1},r,\beta,\gamma)
for any
{\rm x}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
r>0
. See for example
[
27
]
.

For
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and
\beta,\gamma\in(0,\epsilon]
, we define
\mathcal{G}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
(resp.
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
) to be the closure of
\mathcal{G}(\epsilon,\epsilon)
(resp.
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)
) in the space
\mathcal{G}(\beta,\gamma)
(resp.
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)
) under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{G}(\beta,\gamma)}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}_{0}(\beta,\gamma)}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}
). Denote by
(\mathcal{G}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
(resp.
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
) the dual space of
(\mathcal{G}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))
(resp.
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))
) under the weak-
*
topology. The spaces
(\mathcal{G}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))
and
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))
are called the spaces of test functions on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
and
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
are called the spaces of distributions on
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
be an even function satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
. Then, for every
{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
t>0
,
\varphi(t\sqrt{L})(\cdot,{\rm y}),\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm y},\cdot)\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(1,1)
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.5
,

\int\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})dw({\rm x})=\int\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm y},{\rm x})dw({\rm x})=0

for all
{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
t>0
.

Hence, it suffices to prove that for a fixed
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
t>0
,

\varphi(t\sqrt{L})(\cdot,{\rm y})\in\mathcal{G}({\rm y},r,1,1),\ \ \ \ r=4\|{\rm y}\|.

We first show that

(28)

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})|\lesssim P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r):=\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|))}\frac{r}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|},\ \ \ {\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.

If
r\geq\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|
, then from Lemma
2.5
,

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm y},{\rm x})|\lesssim\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\simeq_{t,\|{\rm y}\|}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},4\|{\rm y}\|))}=\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},r))}\simeq P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r).

If
\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|>r:=4\|{\rm y}\|
, then
\|{\rm x}\|\geq 3\|{\rm y}\|
. In this case
d({\rm x},{\rm y})\simeq\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\simeq\|{\rm x}\|
. Hence,

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\Big)^{1+\mathfrak{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|))}\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{t,\|{\rm y}\|}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|))}\frac{\|{\rm y}\|}{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\simeq P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r).

We next show that for
{\rm x},{\rm x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
with
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}(r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|)
,

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})|\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r).

If
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|<t
, then

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})|\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(1+\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm y})}{t}\Big)^{-2-\mathfrak{N}}.

Similarly to the proof of (
28
), if
r\geq\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|
, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{t,\|{\rm y}\|}\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{4\|{\rm y}\|}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},4\|{\rm y}\|))}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r).

If
\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|>r:=4\|{\rm y}\|
, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\Big)^{2+\mathfrak{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|))}\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{t,\|{\rm y}\|}\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|))}\frac{\|{\rm y}\|}{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r).

If
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\geq t
, then by a careful examination of the proof of (
28
), we can show that

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})|\lesssim\frac{t}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r),\ \ \ {\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r)+\frac{t}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x}^{\prime};r)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r)+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x}^{\prime};r).

On the other hand,
r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\simeq r+\|{\rm x}^{\prime}-{\rm y}\|
, whenever
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}(r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|)
. Consequently,

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})|\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{r+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}P_{1}({\rm y},{\rm x};r).

This completes our proof.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
be an even function satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and
0<\beta,\gamma<\epsilon
. For

and
f\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)
, then we have

(29)

\|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)}\lesssim t^{\epsilon-\beta}\|f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)}.

Proof.

We fix a constant
M>\mathfrak{N}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\|f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)}=1
.
We have

\displaystyle\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y}).

By using Lemma
2.5
, it follows that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})dw({\rm x})=0.

For
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, set
S({\rm x})=\{{\rm y}:\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)\}
. We have

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})[f({\rm y})-f({\rm x})]dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{S({\rm x})}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})[f({\rm y})-f({\rm x})]dw({\rm y})\Big|+\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\backslash S({\rm x})}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})[f({\rm y})-f({\rm x})]dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

For the term
E_{1}
, by (ii) in Definition
2.7
,

|f({\rm y})-f({\rm x})|\leq\Big[\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm x};1).

This, along with Lemma
2.5
, implies, for
M>\mathfrak{N}
,

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{S({\rm x})}\Big[\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{t}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{S({\rm x})}\Big[\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}\Big[\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

For
E_{2}
, we write

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\backslash S({\rm x})}|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})||f({\rm y})|dw({\rm y})+\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\backslash S({\rm x})}|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})||f({\rm x})|dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle=:E_{21}+E_{22}.

By (i) in Definition
2.7
and Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle E_{22}

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{{}_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\backslash S({\rm x})}}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{t}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{2}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

Applying (i) in Definition
2.7
and Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle E_{21}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\backslash S({\rm x})}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{t}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{2M+\gamma}P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\backslash S({\rm x})}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{2M+\gamma}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y}).

This, along with the fact that

\Big(\frac{1}{1+\|{\rm y}\|}\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)\leq\Big(\frac{1}{1+\|{\rm y}\|}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{1+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)\lesssim\frac{1}{1+\|{\rm x}\|},\ \ \ {\rm x},{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},t\in(0,1),

implies that

(30)

\displaystyle E_{21}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\backslash S({\rm x})}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{2M}\frac{1}{w(B(0,1+\|{\rm y}\|))}dw({\rm y}).

If

In both cases, we have

\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{M}\frac{1}{w(B(0,1+\|{\rm y}\|))}\lesssim\frac{1}{w(B(0,1+\|{\rm x}\|))}.

Plugging this into (
30

\displaystyle E_{22}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{2}\frac{1}{w(B(0,1+\|{\rm x}\|))}\Big(\frac{1}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{M}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

Taking all the estimates of

and

(31)

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1),

which implies

(32)

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|\lesssim t^{\epsilon}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

We next prove that
For
{\rm x},{\rm x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
with
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)
and
t\in(0,1)
, we have

(33)

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|\lesssim t^{\epsilon-\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

Indeed, we first observe that

\displaystyle\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}[\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})]f({\rm y})dw({\rm y}).

If
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq t
, then we write

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})

\displaystyle-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}[\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})][f({\rm y})-f({\rm x})]dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{t}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}|f({\rm x})-f({\rm y})|dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\{{\rm y}:\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)\}}\ldots+\int_{\{{\rm y}:\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|>\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)\}}\ldots

\displaystyle=:F_{1}+F_{2}.

For
F_{1}
, using (ii) in Definition
2.7
,

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\Big)^{\epsilon}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\Big)^{\epsilon}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}\Big[\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\epsilon}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{M}dw({\rm y}).

Since

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\epsilon-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|^{\epsilon-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\epsilon-\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta},

as long as
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq t
, we have

F_{1}\lesssim t^{\epsilon-\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

For the term
F_{2}
, similarly we have

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\{{\rm y}:\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|>\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)\}}\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\frac{t}{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}|f({\rm x})-f({\rm y})|dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\int_{\{{\rm y}:\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|>\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)\}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}|f({\rm x})-f({\rm y})|dw({\rm y}).

Arguing similarly to the estimate of
E_{2}
,

\int_{\{{\rm y}:\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|>\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)\}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}|f({\rm x})-f({\rm y})|dw({\rm y})\lesssim P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

Therefore,

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|^{1-\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{1-\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\epsilon-\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

as long as
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq t
.

Case 2:

t<\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|<\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)
. Using (
31
),

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|+|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)+\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x}^{\prime},1).

This, along with the fact that
1+\|{\rm x}\|\simeq 1+\|{\rm x}^{\prime}\|
, whenever
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)
, implies

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|+|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\epsilon}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\epsilon-\beta}\Big[\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\epsilon-\beta}\Big[\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1),

as long as
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|>t
.

This ensures (
33
). Taking the estimates (
32
) and (
33
) into account, we imply (
29
).

This completes our proof.

Lemma 2.10
.

for

with
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)
, we have

\Big|\int_{0}^{2}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})\frac{dt}{t}-\int_{0}^{2}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})\frac{dt}{t}\Big|\lesssim\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

which, along with (
36

34

We now take care of (
37
). The proof of this is similar to the proof of Lemma
2.9
. We first observe that

\displaystyle\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{t}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}|f({\rm x})-f({\rm y})|dw({\rm y})

For

, using (ii) in Definition
2.7
,

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\Big)^{\beta}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}\Big[\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\beta}dw({\rm y})

Therefore,

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{t}\Big)^{1-\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1),

as long as

.

Case 2:

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{t}{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}\Big]^{\beta}\Big[\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big]^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1),

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
be an even function satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and
0<\beta,\gamma<\epsilon
. For
t\geq 1
and
f\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)
, then we have

(38)

\|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)}\lesssim t^{-(\epsilon-\gamma)}\|f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)}.

Proof.

In this proof,
M>\mathfrak{N}
will be a fixed number. Without loss of generality, assume that
\|f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}=1
. We have

\displaystyle\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y}).

By using Lemma
2.5
, it follows that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})dw({\rm x})=0.

We now consider two cases:
1+\|{\rm x}\|\geq t
and
1+\|{\rm x}\|<t
.

1+\|{\rm x}\|\geq t

In this case, we write

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq\|{\rm x}\|/2}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|+\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<\|{\rm x}\|/2}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

We write

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq\|{\rm x}\|/2}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq\|{\rm x}\|/2\atop\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|+\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq\|{\rm x}\|/2\atop\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|<\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq\|{\rm x}\|/2\atop\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|+\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq\|{\rm x}\|/2\atop\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|<\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})[f({\rm y})-f({\rm x})]dw({\rm y})\Big|

|f({\rm y})|\lesssim P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm y};1)\lesssim P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm x};1)\ \text{whenever $\|{\rm y}\|\geq\|{\rm x}\|/2$},

2.5
, we have

\displaystyle E_{11}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-(\epsilon-\gamma)}\Big(\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

By Lemma
2.5
and Definition
2.7
(ii),

\displaystyle E_{12}

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm x};1)\int_{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|<\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)}\Big(\frac{t}{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\Big)^{\beta}\Big(\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{M}dw({\rm y})

E_{13}
, since
\displaystyle\int\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})dw({\rm y})=0
, we have

\displaystyle E_{13}

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<\|{\rm x}\|/2\atop\text{or}\ \|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+\|{\rm x}\|)}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm x})dw({\rm y})\Big|

(39)

E_{1}\lesssim t^{-(\epsilon-\gamma)}\Big(\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1).

For the term
E_{2}
, if
\|{\rm x}\|\leq 1
, then
|f({\rm y})|\lesssim P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm y};1)\simeq P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm x};1)
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle E_{2}

. In this case, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big|\int_{t\leq\|{\rm y}\|<\|{\rm x}\|/2}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|+\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<\min\{\|{\rm x}\|/2,t\}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle+\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<\min\{\|{\rm x}\|/2,t\}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},0)f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle=:E_{21}+E_{22}+E_{23}.

For the term
E_{21}
, note that in this situation
d({\rm x},{\rm y})\simeq\|{\rm x}\|
. Hence, by Lemma
2.5
and (i) in Definition
2.7
,

\displaystyle E_{21}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t\leq\|{\rm y}\|<\|{\rm x}\|/2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\|{\rm x}\|))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{M+\beta}P_{\epsilon}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y}).

Since
w(B({\rm x},\|{\rm x}\|))\simeq w(B(0,\|{\rm x}\|))
and the following inequality

We now write

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|\leq|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|+|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}^{\prime})|.

As a byproduct, we also obtain the following estimate.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
be an even function satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
. Let
0<\beta,\gamma<1
. Then for every
f\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)
and
{\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
,

(48)

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|\lesssim t^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)\|f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)},\ \ \ t\in(0,1),

and

(49)

|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|\lesssim t^{-\gamma}P_{\gamma\wedge\beta}(0,{\rm x};t)\|f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)},\ \ t\geq 1.

Proof.

The estimate (
48
) follows directly from (
35
).

For the estimate (
49
), we consider two cases. If
t\leq 1+\|{\rm x}\|
, we proved in (
46
) that

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\gamma}\Big(\frac{t}{1+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\beta}\frac{1}{w(B(0,1+\|{\rm x}\|))}

\displaystyle\simeq t^{-\gamma}P_{\beta}(0,{\rm x};t).

If
t>1+\|{\rm x}\|
, then we write

\displaystyle|\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<t}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|+\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq t}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<t}[\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})-\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},0)]f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|+\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<t}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},0)f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle+\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq t}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},{\rm y})f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle=:F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3}.

Using Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<t}\frac{\|{\rm y}\|}{t}\frac{1}{w(B(0,t+\|{\rm x}\|))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\gamma}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\|{\rm y}\|<t}\frac{\|{\rm y}\|^{1-\gamma}}{t}\frac{1}{w(B(0,t+\|{\rm x}\|))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\gamma}\frac{1}{w(B(0,1+\|{\rm y}\|))}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};t)\int_{\|y\|\leq t}\frac{\|{\rm y}\|^{1-\gamma}}{w(B(0,1+\|{\rm y}\|)}dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}t^{1-\gamma}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};t)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\gamma^{\prime}}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};t).

For the term
F_{2}
, since
\displaystyle\int f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})=0
, we have

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq t}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})({\rm x},0)f({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq t}\frac{1}{w(B(0,t+\|{\rm x}\|))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+\|{\rm x}\|}\Big)^{\gamma}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};t)\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq t}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\gamma}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};t).

For the term
F_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq t}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})))}\Big(\frac{t}{t+d({\rm x},{\rm y})}\Big)^{\gamma}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y}).

Since

P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};t)\simeq\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t))}
. Hence,

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq t}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},t)}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};t)\int_{\|{\rm y}\|\geq t}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm y};1)dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\gamma}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};t).

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and
\beta,\gamma\in\mathscr{S}(0,\epsilon)
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{(}\mathbb{R})
be an even function satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
and
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t)\frac{dt}{t}\neq 0
. Then we have

f=c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f\frac{dt}{t}

in
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
, where
\displaystyle c_{\varphi}=\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t)\frac{dt}{t}\Big]^{-1}
.

Proof.

By duality, it suffices to prove that

f=c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f\frac{dt}{t}

in
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
.

For
f\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}=0
. We now write

(50)

c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f\frac{dt}{t}=c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})(f-f_{n})\frac{dt}{t}+c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f_{n}\frac{dt}{t}.

From Lemma
2.10
and Lemma
2.12
,

\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})(f-f_{n})\frac{dt}{t}\Big\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\lesssim\|f-f_{n}\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)},

which implies

(51)

\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})(f-f_{n})\frac{dt}{t}=0\ \ \ \text{in \ $\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)$}.

From Lemma
2.9
and Lemma
2.11
, there exists
h_{n}\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
such that

h_{n}=c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f_{n}\frac{dt}{t}.

On the other hand, since
f_{n},h_{n}\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
,
f_{n},h_{n}\in L^{2}(dw)
. By the spectral theory,

f_{n}=c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f_{n}\frac{dt}{t}\ \ \text{in $L^{2}(dw)$}.

Consequently,
h_{n}=f_{n}
for almost everywhere. This, along with the fact that
f_{n},h_{n}\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)
, implies that
h_{n}({\rm x})=f_{n}({\rm x})
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and for each
n
. Therefore,

(52)

f_{n}=c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f_{n}\frac{dt}{t}

in
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)
.

This, along with (
50
) and (
51
), yields

c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f\frac{dt}{t}=\lim_{n}f_{n},

and hence

c_{\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(t\sqrt{L})f\frac{dt}{t}=f

in
\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)
since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}=0
.

This completes our proof. ∎

3.
Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces in the Dunkl setting

The main aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. To accomplish this, our first task is to establish the atomic decomposition of the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces on the space of homogeneous type
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},|\cdot|,dw)
. Subsequently, we recapitulate some fundamental properties of the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces associated with the operator
L
, including the atomic decomposition and interpolation theorems. This is interesting to emphasize that in the particular case when the indices are similar to those in the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces on the space of homogeneous type
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
, we can use the same space of distributions to defined these function spaces. Finally, in this particular case, we establish a new atomic decomposition which will play a crucial role in proving Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Atomic decomposition of Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces on
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)

In this section, we will prove the atomic decomposition for the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces on the space of homogeneous type
({\mathbb{R}^{N}},\|\cdot\|,dw)
. We first recall the definition of an approximation of the identity with bounded support in
[
27
]
which plays an essential role in the definitions of Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence
\{S_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of bounded linear integral operators on

L^{2}(dw)
is said to be an approximation of the identity with bounded support (for short, ATI with bounded support) if there exist constants
A_{1},A_{2}>0
such that for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the integral kernel
S_{k}(\cdot,\cdot)
of
S_{k}
is a measurable function from
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

satisfying the following properties

(i)

S_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})=S_{k}({\rm y},{\rm x})
;

(ii)

S_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})=0
whenever
\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|\geq A_{1}\delta^{k}
;

(iii)

\displaystyle|S_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})|\lesssim\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\delta^{k}))}
;

(iv)

\displaystyle|S_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})-S_{k}({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})|+|S_{k}({\rm y},{\rm x})-S_{k}({\rm y},{\rm x}^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{\delta^{k}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\delta^{k}))+w(B({\rm y},\delta^{k}))}
for
\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|\leq A_{2}\delta^{k}
;

(v)

\displaystyle\big|[S_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})-S_{k}({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y})]-[S_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y}^{\prime})-S_{k}({\rm x}^{\prime},{\rm y}^{\prime})]\big|\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{\delta^{k}}\frac{\|{\rm y}-{\rm y}^{\prime}\|}{\delta^{k}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm x},\delta^{k}))+w(B({\rm y},\delta^{k}))}
for
\max\{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|,\|{\rm y}-{\rm y}^{\prime}\|\}\leq A_{2}\delta^{k}
;

(vi)

\displaystyle\int S_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})dw({\rm x})=\int S_{k}({\rm y},{\rm x})dw({\rm x})=1
for all
{\rm y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
.

The existence of the family
\{S_{k}\}
is guaranteed in Theorem 2.6
[
27
]
(see also
[
49
]
).

In what follows, for
s\in(-1,1)
and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, we set

(53)

p(s,\epsilon)=\max\Big\{\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+\epsilon},\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+s+\epsilon}\Big\}.

We now recall the definitions of the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces in
[
27
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
S_{k}
be an ATI with bounded support and let
D_{k}=S_{k}-S_{k-1}
for

. For
s\in(-1,1)
,

and

, assume that

and

and
p>p(s,\epsilon)
. The Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)
is defined as the set of all

such that

(54)

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)}=\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\|D_{k}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(dw)}\Big]^{1/q}<\infty.

For
s\in(-1,1)
and
p(s,1)<p,q<\infty
, assume that
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
0<\beta,\gamma<\epsilon
such that
|s|<\beta\wedge\gamma

is defined as the set of all

We would like to highlight that the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces on a space of homogeneous type have been defined for infinite indices

and/or

[

]
. Nevertheless, when considering this scenario, it remains uncertain whether the function spaces are independent of the choices of

Later on in Corollary
3.8
below, by establishing the atomic decomposition for these function spaces, we will show that the definitions of the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces defined in Definition
3.2
are independent on the parameters
\epsilon,\beta
and
\gamma

\mathscr{D}:=\{Q_{\alpha}^{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\alpha\in I_{k}\}

3.3
below holds true). For each

and

2.1
, we can write

(56)

Q^{k}_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\tau\in I_{k+j_{0}}:\ Q_{\tau}^{k+j_{0}}\subset Q^{k}_{\alpha}}Q_{\tau}^{k+j_{0}}.

We also denote
N(\alpha,k)=\sharp\{\tau\in I_{k+j_{0}}:\ Q_{\tau}^{k+j_{0}}\subset Q^{k}_{\alpha}\}
. By the doubling condition (
4

2.1
,
N(\alpha,k)\lesssim 2^{j_{0}\mathfrak{N}}
. We can rearrange to write

as
\{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}\}_{m=1}^{N(\alpha,k)}
.

We have the following Calderón-Zygmund formula in
[

(see also
[

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S_{k}
be an ATI with bounded support and
D_{k}=S_{k}-S_{k-1}
, and let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
0<\beta,\gamma<\epsilon
. Then there exists a sequence
\{\widetilde{D}_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of bounded linear operators on

f({\rm x})=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}\sum_{m=1}^{N(k,\alpha)}w(Q_{\alpha,m}^{k})D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y}_{\alpha,m}^{k})\widetilde{D}_{k}f({\rm y}_{\alpha,m}^{k})

and
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}_{0}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
for any

(ii)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{D}_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})-\widetilde{D}_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y}^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm y}-{\rm y}^{\prime}\|}{\delta^{k}+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}\frac{1}{w(B(x,\delta^{k}+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|))}\frac{\delta^{k}}{\delta^{k}+\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}

\displaystyle\int\widetilde{D}_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})dw({\rm x})=\int\widetilde{D}_{k}({\rm y},{\rm x})dw({\rm x})=0

Definition 3.4

(
[
29
]
)
.

Let
0<p\leq\infty
. A function

p
-atom if there exists a dyadic cube

We have the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
S_{k}
be an ATI with bounded support and
D_{k}=S_{k}-S_{k-1}
. Then for any
M>0
there exists
C>0
such that for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and every
p
-atom
a_{Q}
associated to some dyadic cube
Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{Z}

(i)

\displaystyle|D_{k}{a_{Q}}({\rm x})|\leq Cw(Q)^{-1/p}\delta^{k-\nu}{\left({1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}_{Q}\|}{\delta^{\nu}}}\right)^{-M}}
for all
\nu\leq k

(ii)

\displaystyle|D_{k}{a_{Q}}({\rm x})|\leq Cw(Q)^{-1/p}\delta^{-k+\nu}\frac{{w\left(Q\right)}}{{w\left(B({{{\rm x}_{Q}},\delta^{k}})\right)}}{\left({1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}_{Q}\|}{\delta^{k}}}\right)^{-M}}
for all

.

Proof.

Due to the bounded support condition of
D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})
, it suffices to prove the lemma with

. In this case,

\left({1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}_{Q}\|}{\delta^{k\wedge\nu}}}\right)^{-M}\simeq 1,\ \ \ \ {\rm x}\in 3\Lambda Q.

(i) Since
\displaystyle\int D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})dw({\rm y})=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|D_{k}a_{Q}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})a_{Q}({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})[a_{Q}({\rm y})-a_{Q}({\rm x})]dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q)^{-1/p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})|\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm y}\|}{\ell(Q)}dw({\rm y}).

Due to the bounded support condition (ii) in Definition
3.1
, we further obtain

\displaystyle\left|D_{k}a_{Q}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})a_{Q}({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q)^{-1/p}\frac{\delta^{k}}{\ell(Q)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})|dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q)^{-1/p}\frac{\delta^{k}}{\ell(Q)}\simeq w(Q)^{-1/p}\delta^{k-\nu}.

This completes the proof of (i).

(ii) Since
\displaystyle\int a_{Q}({\rm x})dw({\rm x})=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|D_{k}a_{Q}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\Lambda Q}[D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y})-D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q})]a_{Q}({\rm y})dw({\rm y})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Lambda Q}\frac{\|{\rm y}-{\rm x}_{Q}\|}{\delta^{k}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm y},\delta^{k}))}|a_{Q}({\rm y})|dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q)^{-1/p}\frac{\ell(Q)}{\delta^{k}}\frac{w(Q)}{w(B(x_{Q},\delta^{k}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q)^{-1/p}\delta^{\nu-k}\frac{w(Q)}{w(B(x_{Q},\delta^{k}))}.

This completes the proof of (ii).
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
s\in(-1,1)

Proof of Theorem

:

Let

be an ATI with bounded support and

. From Lemma

,

\displaystyle f({\rm x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}\sum_{m=1}^{N(k,\alpha)}w(Q_{\alpha,m}^{k})D_{k}({\rm x},{\rm y}_{\alpha,m}^{k})\widetilde{D}_{k}f({\rm y}_{\alpha,m}^{k})

in

satisfies (i)-(iii) in Lemma

.

Define

and

From the properties (ii), (iii), (iv) and (v) in Definition

, it is easy to see that

is an
p

Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}

This, together with (

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}

\displaystyle w(Q_{\alpha,m}^{k})^{-q/p}|s_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-(k+j_{0})sq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k+j_{0}}}w(Q_{\alpha}^{k+j_{0}})^{-q/p}|s_{Q_{\alpha}^{k+j_{0}}}|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha}^{k+j_{0}}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

\displaystyle\simeq\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k+j_{0}}}w(Q_{\alpha}^{k+j_{0}})^{-q/p}|s_{Q_{\alpha}^{k+j_{0}}}|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha}^{k+j_{0}}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}\sum_{m=1}^{N(\alpha,k)}w(Q_{\alpha,m}^{k})^{-q/p}|s_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{N(\alpha,k)}w(Q_{\alpha,m}^{k})^{-q/p}|s_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}

\displaystyle w(Q_{\alpha}^{k})^{-q/p}|s_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}\sum_{m=1}^{N(\alpha,k)}|\widetilde{D}_{k}f({\rm y}_{\alpha,m}^{k})|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.

By Remark 5.5 in
[
27
]
,

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}\sum_{m=1}^{N(\alpha,k)}

\displaystyle|\widetilde{D}_{k}f({\rm y}_{\alpha,m}^{k})|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}\sum_{m=1}^{N(\alpha,k)}\inf_{z\in Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}|D_{k}f(z)|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha,m}^{k}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

Consequently,

\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta^{-ksq}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}w(Q_{\alpha}^{k})^{-q/p}|s_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}|^{q}\chi_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(dw)}.

The reverse direction is quite standard and follows directly from Lemma

. For the sake of completeness, we provide the proof.

Fix

so that

we also fix

. Since

f=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}s_{Q}a_{Q}\ \ \text{in $(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}_{0}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}$},

we have, for each

,

\displaystyle D_{k}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}s_{Q}D_{k}a_{Q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu:\nu\geq k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}s_{Q}D_{k}a_{Q}+\sum_{\nu:\nu<k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}s_{Q}D_{k}a_{Q}.

By Lemma
3.5

\displaystyle\delta^{-sk}|D_{k}f|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\nu:\nu\geq k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}\delta^{-sk}\delta^{\nu-k}\frac{w(Q)}{w(B({\rm x}_{Q},\delta^{k}))}\Big(1+\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q})}{\delta^{k}}\Big)^{-M}w(Q)^{-1/p}|s_{Q}|

\displaystyle+\sum_{\nu:\nu<k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}\delta^{-sk}\delta^{k-\nu}\Big(1+\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q})}{\delta^{\nu}}\Big)^{-M}w(Q)^{-1/p}|s_{Q}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\nu:\nu\geq k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}\delta^{(\nu-k)(1+s)}\frac{w(Q)}{w(B({\rm x}_{Q},\delta^{k}))}\Big(1+\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q})}{\delta^{k}}\Big)^{-M}\delta^{-s\nu}w(Q)^{-1/p}|s_{Q}|

\displaystyle+\sum_{\nu:\nu<k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}\delta^{(k-\nu)(1-s)}\Big(1+\frac{d({\rm x},{\rm x}_{Q})}{\delta^{\nu}}\Big)^{-M}\delta^{-s\nu}w(Q)^{-1/p}|s_{Q}|.

Using Lemma
2.3
,

(57)

\displaystyle\delta^{-sk}|D_{k}f|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\nu:\nu\geq k}\delta^{(\nu-k)(1+s)}\delta^{-\mathfrak{N}(\nu-k)(1/r-1)}\mathcal{M}_{r}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}\delta^{-s\nu}|s_{Q}|w(Q)^{-1/p}\chi_{Q}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\nu:\nu<k}\delta^{(k-\nu)(1-s)}\mathcal{M}_{r}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}\delta^{-s\nu}|s_{Q}|w(Q)^{-1/p}\chi_{Q}\Big).

By using (

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(\delta^{-ks}|D_{k}f|)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big[\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta^{-\nu sq}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{\nu}}w(Q)^{-1/p}|s_{Q}|\chi_{Q}\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(w)},

provided that

3.2.

In this section, we will study the theory of Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces associated to the Dunkl operator. It is interesting to note that we will show that these Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces coincide with the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces defined in Definition

Definition 3.9

Theorem 3.22
.

Let

and
p\in(\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+s+1},\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty)
. For any

and

and

, we have

Similarly, let

and

. For any

and

such that

and

Proof.

We only prove the statement for the Besov spaces. The case of Triebel-Lizorkin spaces can be done similarly.

With the same indices as in the statement of the theorem, it suffices to prove that the set

\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)=\Big\{f\in(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}:\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}<\infty\Big\}.

is complete.

To do this, we first prove that if
f\in\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)\cap(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
, then for any
g\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
,

(64)

|\langle f,g\rangle|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}.

Indeed, let
\psi
be a partition of unity. It is well-known that there exists an even function
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
{\rm supp}\,\varphi\subset[-c,-b]\cup[b,c]
for some
c>b>0
so that

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{j}(\lambda)\varphi_{j}(\lambda)=1,\ \ \forall\lambda\neq 0.

See for example
[
48
]
.

Let
g\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)
. By Theorem
2.15
,

f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})\varphi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f

in
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
.

Consequently,

\langle f,g\rangle=\Big\langle\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})\varphi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f,g\Big\rangle,

which follows that

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\int\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})\varphi_{j}(\sqrt{L})g({\rm x})dw({\rm x})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})||\varphi_{j}(\sqrt{L})g({\rm x})|dw({\rm x}).

Applying Lemma
2.13
,

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{j\beta}\int|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)dw({\rm x})

\displaystyle+\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j<0}\delta^{-j\gamma}\int|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|P_{\beta\wedge\gamma}(0,{\rm x};\delta^{j})dw({\rm x}).

Case 1:
p\geq 1

Since
w(B(0,\|{\rm x}\|+1)),w(B(0,\|{\rm x}\|+\delta^{j}))\gtrsim 1
(due to (
3
)) for
j<0
, by Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\int|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)dw({\rm x})

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)^{p^{\prime}}dw({\rm x})\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}P_{\gamma p^{\prime}}(0,{\rm x};1)dw({\rm x})\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

and for
j<0
,

\displaystyle\int|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)dw({\rm x})

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};\delta^{j})^{p^{\prime}}dw({\rm x})\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}P_{\gamma p^{\prime}}(0,{\rm x};\delta^{j})dw({\rm x})\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{j\beta}\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}+\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j<0}\delta^{-j\gamma}\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\Big\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\delta^{-js}\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\right)^{q}\Big\}^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}

as along as
|s|<\min\{\beta,\gamma\}
.

Case 2:
0<p<1

In this situation, we have

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{j\beta}\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}\int_{Q^{d}}|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)dw({\rm x})

\displaystyle+\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j<0}\delta^{-j\gamma}\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}\int_{Q^{d}}|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|P_{\beta\wedge\gamma}(0,{\rm x};\delta^{j})dw({\rm x}).

It is easy to see that for
Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}_{j}^{d}
and
\lambda>\mathfrak{N}/p
,

|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})|\lesssim\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q^{d}}\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z}),\ \ \ \text{for all ${\rm x}\in Q^{d}$}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{j\beta}\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q^{d}}\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})\int_{Q^{d}}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)dw({\rm x})

\displaystyle+\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j<0}\delta^{-j\gamma}\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q^{d}}\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})\int_{Q^{d}}P_{\beta\wedge\gamma}(0,{\rm x};\delta^{j})dw({\rm x}).

Since
P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)\lesssim 1
, we have, for
Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}
with
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{d}}P_{\gamma}(0,{\rm x};1)dw({\rm x})

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q^{d})

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q^{d})^{1/p}w(Q^{d})^{1-1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{j\mathfrak{N}(1-1/p)}w(Q^{d})^{1/p},

where in the last inequality we used (
3
).

For
Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}
with
j<0
, by using (
3
),

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{d}}P_{\beta\wedge\gamma}(0,{\rm x};\delta^{j})dw({\rm x})

\displaystyle\lesssim 1

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q^{d})^{1/p}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{j(\beta+\mathfrak{N}(1-1/p))}\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}w(Q^{d})^{1/p}\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q^{d}}\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})

\displaystyle+\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j<0}\delta^{-j\gamma}\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}w(Q^{d})^{1/p}\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q^{d}}\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{j(\beta+\mathfrak{N}(1-1/p))}\Big[\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}w(Q^{d})\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q^{d}}|\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})|^{p}\Big]^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j<0}\delta^{-j\gamma}\Big[\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}w(Q^{d})\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q^{d}}|\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})|^{p}\Big]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{j(\beta+\mathfrak{N}(1-1/p))}\Big[\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}\int_{Q^{d}}|\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})|^{p}dw({\rm z})\Big]^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\sum_{j<0}\delta^{-j\gamma}\Big[\sum_{Q^{d}\in\mathscr{D}^{d}_{j}}\int_{Q^{d}}|\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})|^{p}dw({\rm z})\Big]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\Big(\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{j(\beta+\mathfrak{N}(1-1/p))}\|\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta^{-j\gamma}\|\psi^{*}_{j,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\Big).

Since
p>\frac{\mathfrak{N}}{\mathfrak{N}+\beta+s}
,
\beta+s+\mathfrak{N}(1-1/p)>0
. This, along with
|s|<\gamma
and Proposition
3.10
, yields

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\Big\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\delta^{-js}\|\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\right)^{q}\Big\}^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}.

We have proved that for any
g\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)
,

(65)

|\langle f,g\rangle|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}.

For
g\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
, there exists
\{g_{n}\}\subset\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)
such that
\lim_{n}\|g_{n}-g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}=0
. Then from (
65
),

|\langle f,g_{m}-g_{n}\rangle|\lesssim\|g_{m}-g_{n}\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}.

It follows that the limit
\lim\langle f,g_{n}\rangle
exists and hence we can define

\langle f,g\rangle=\lim\langle f,g_{n}\rangle.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim\|g_{n}\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}.

This ensures (
64
).

We now turn to the proof. For any Cauchy sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)\cap(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
in
\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)
, by (

),

|\langle f_{m}-f_{n},g\rangle|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}\|f_{m}-f_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}.

for any
g\in\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma)
.

It follows that
\{f_{n}\}
is also a Cauchy sequence in
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
and hence there exists
f\in(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
such that
\lim_{n}f_{n}=f
in
(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
. This, along with Proposition
2.8
, yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f_{n}({\rm x})=\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}),\ \ \ {\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

or equivalently,

(66)

\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{-js}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f_{n}({\rm x})=2^{-js}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x}),\ \ \ {\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.

On the other hand, since
\{f_{n}\}\subset\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)\cap(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
is a Cauchy sequence,
2^{-js}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f_{n}({\rm x})
can be viewed as a Cauchy sequence in the space

. It is well-known that
\ell^{p}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}))
is complete. Hence, there exists

such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|2^{-js}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f_{n}({\rm x})-F(j,{\rm x})\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}))}=0,

which implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{-js}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f_{n}({\rm x})=F(j,{\rm x})\ \ \text{for a.e. ${\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}$}.

From this and (

2^{-js}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})=F(j,{\rm x})\ \ \text{for a.e. ${\rm x}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}$},

and hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|2^{-js}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f_{n}({\rm x})-2^{-js}\psi_{j}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm x})\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}))}=0,

i.e.,
f_{n}\to f
in

This completes our proof.

Definition 3.23

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}(\delta^{k}\sqrt{L})^{2}\Phi_{k}(\sqrt{L})[\psi_{k}(\sqrt{L})f\cdot\chi_{Q}].

Obviously, we deduce from (

f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}s_{Q}a_{Q}\ \ \text{in $(\mathring{\mathcal{G}}^{\epsilon}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}$}.

Using Lemma
2.6
we can see that

{\rm supp}\,a_{Q}\subset 6\mathcal{O}(Q),\ \ \int a_{Q}({\rm x})dw({\rm x})=0.

and

\displaystyle|a_{Q}({\rm x})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{s_{Q}}\int_{Q}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}_{Q}\|}{\ell_{Q}}\Big)^{-2}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm y},\delta^{k}))}|\psi_{k}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm y})|dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q)^{-1/p}\Big(1+\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}_{Q}\|}{\ell_{Q}}\Big)^{-2};

moreover, for
{\rm x},{\rm x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
,

\displaystyle|a_{Q^{d}}({\rm x})-a_{Q^{d}}({\rm x}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{s_{Q^{d}}}\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{\delta^{k}}\int_{Q^{d}}\frac{1}{w(B({\rm y},\delta^{k}))}|\psi_{k}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm y})|dw({\rm y})

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\rm x}-{\rm x}^{\prime}\|}{\delta^{k}}w(Q^{d})^{-1/p}.

It follows that
a_{Q^{d}}
is (a multiple of) a
(p,d)
-atom.

It remains to show that

\Big[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta^{-skq}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}|s_{Q}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}\Big]^{1/q}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}.

Indeed, for
\lambda>\mathfrak{N}/p
is is easy to see that

\displaystyle s_{Q}

\displaystyle=w(Q)^{1/p}\sup_{{\rm y}\in Q}|\psi_{k}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim w(Q)^{1/p}\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q}\psi^{*}_{k,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}|s_{Q}|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}w(Q)\inf_{{\rm z}\in Q}\psi^{*}_{k,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}\int_{Q}|\psi^{*}_{k,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})|^{p}dw({\rm z})

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|\psi^{*}_{k,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f({\rm z})|^{p}dw({\rm z}).

This, along with Proposition
3.10
, implies that

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta^{-skq}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}|s_{Q}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}\delta^{-sk}\|\psi^{*}_{k,\lambda}(\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\Big)^{q/p}\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,L}_{p,q}(dw)}.

This completes our proof.

∎

Multiplier theorem for the Dunkl transform

Recall that for a bounded function

[

38

Proposition 4.2

We write

\displaystyle=\big[{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(m^{\ell})\ast\tau_{-{\rm y}}h_{1}\big]({\rm x}).

Let
\Psi_{0}\in C^{\infty}((-1,1))
and

\displaystyle T_{\widetilde{m}_{\ell}}({\rm x},{\rm y})

\|T_{m}\|_{\dot{F}^{s,L}_{p,2}(dw)\to\dot{F}^{s,L}_{p,2}(dw)}\lesssim|m(0)|+\sup_{t>0}\|\psi(\cdot)m(t\cdot)\|_{W^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})},

provided that

,
p\in(0,\infty)
and
\alpha>\mathfrak{N}(\frac{1}{1\wedge p}-\frac{1}{2})
.

From Proposition

]
we can show that

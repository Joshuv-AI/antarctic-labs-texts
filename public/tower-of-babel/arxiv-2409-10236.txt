Existence, symmetry and regularity of ground states of a non linear choquard equation in the hyperbolic space
Diksha Gupta, K. Sreenadh

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore the positive solutions of a nonlinear Choquard equation involving the green kernel of the fractional operator ( − Δ 𝔹 N ) − α / 2 (-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\alpha/2} in the hyperbolic space, where Δ 𝔹 N \Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}} represents the Laplace-Beltrami operator on 𝔹 N \mathbb{B}^{N} , with α ∈ ( 0 , N ) \alpha\in(0,N) and N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . This study is analogous to the Choquard equation in the Euclidean space, which involves the non-local Riesz potential operator. We consider the functional setting within the Sobolev space H 1 ​ ( 𝔹 N ) H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}) , employing advanced harmonic analysis techniques, particularly the Helgason Fourier transform and semigroup approach to the fractional Laplacian. Moreover, the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality on complete Riemannian manifolds, as developed by Varopoulos, is pivotal in our analysis. We prove an existence result for the problem ( F α , λ ) in the subcritical case. Moreover, we also demonstrate that solutions exhibit radial symmetry, and establish the regularity properties. 2020 MSC: 35B09, 35B38, 35J20, 35R01

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{~d}\theta\mathrm{~d}\beta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}.

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f}(\beta,\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f(x)h_{\beta,\theta}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f(x)\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}h_{\beta,\theta}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)\hat{f}(\beta,\theta).

u(x,0)=f(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right),\end{array}\right.

is considered to be

and compactly supported in the spatial variable.

For every fixed

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\hat{u}(\beta,\theta,t)=-\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)\hat{u}(\beta,\theta,t),\;\text{ for }t>0,\\
\hat{u}(\beta,\theta,0)=\hat{f}(\beta,\theta),\end{array}\right.

which yields

\hat{u}(\beta,\theta,t)=e^{-t\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)}\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)=\widehat{e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}f(\beta,\theta)}.

where

denotes the solution operator. It is well-established (see

u(x,t)\equiv e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}f(x)=p_{t,N}*f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}p_{t,N}(T_{x}(y))f(y)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

. The explicit formulas of heat kernel are given by (see
[

[

If

p_{t,2m+1}(\rho)=2^{-m-1}\pi^{-m-1/2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}t}\left(-\frac{1}{\sinh\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\right)^{m}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}.

If
N=2m

p_{t,2m}(\rho)=(2\pi)^{-m-\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}t}\int_{\rho}^{+\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(-\frac{1}{\sinh r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{m}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}\mathrm{~d}r.

Numerous researchers have investigated bounds for the heat kernel. For instance, Fabio Punzo
[
43

derived the
L^{\infty}
estimate, which helps in establishing the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in the hyperbolic space (see Theorem

A_{N}h_{N}(t,x,y)\leq p_{t,N}(x,y)\leq B_{N}h_{N}(t,x,y)\quad\text{ for all }t>0\text{ and }x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{N},

where

with

We can now establish the semigroup definition of the inverse fractional Laplacian, derived from the following numerical identity:

\zeta^{-\gamma}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\zeta}\frac{\mathrm{~d}t}{t^{1-\gamma}}\quad\text{ for any }\zeta>0,\gamma>0.

Setting
\zeta=\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
and multiplying the equation by
\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)
, we obtain

\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)^{-\gamma}\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)e^{-t\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)}\frac{\mathrm{~d}t}{t^{1-\gamma}}.

Taking the inverse transform then yields

\displaystyle{\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)}^{-\gamma}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\bar{h}_{\beta,\theta}(x)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)e^{-t\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)}\frac{\mathrm{~d}t}{t^{1-\gamma}}\right)\frac{d\theta d\beta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\bar{h}_{\beta,\theta}(x)\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)e^{-t\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)}\frac{d\theta d\beta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{~d}t}{t^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\bar{h}_{\beta,\theta}(x)\widehat{e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}f(\beta,\theta)}\frac{d\theta d\beta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{~d}t}{t^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}f(x)\frac{\mathrm{~d}t}{t^{1-\gamma}}.

Up to this point, we have developed the above heuristics for functions

. Next, we aim to identify a class of functions for which the aforementioned integral converges. To this end, we define the function
f^{*}(x)=\sup_{t>0}|e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}f(x)|
for

, where

. Note that

is a well-defined measurable function, as established in

Theorem 2.1

Suppose

,

, and

(2.7)

{\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)}^{-\alpha/2}f(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}f(x)\frac{dt}{t^{1-\alpha/2}}

is convergent.

Before proving this theorem, we establish the following on-diagonal and off-diagonal estimates for the heat kernel on the hyperbolic space.

Using (

\displaystyle p_{t,N}(x,x)\leq B_{N}(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{(N-1)^{2}t}{4}}(1+t)^{\frac{(N-3)}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq B_{N}(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{(N-1)^{2}t}{4}}e^{\frac{t(N-3)}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq B_{N}(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{\frac{-t\left((N-2)^{2}+3\right)}{4}}

Further, recall the following semigroup property of the heat kernel:

p_{t+s,N}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}p_{t,N}(x,z)p_{s,N}(z,y)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(z)

for

Specifically, by setting

By combining (

Thus, using (

\displaystyle p_{t,N}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}p_{\frac{t}{2},N}(x,z)p_{\frac{t}{2},N}(z,y)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left(p_{\frac{t}{2},N}(x,z)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(z)\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left(p_{\frac{t}{2},N}(y,z)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(z)\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{t,N}(x,x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(p_{t,N}(y,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2}}\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{N}.

We now present the necessary tools to establish Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

Fix

, and consider the integral in (

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}f(x)\frac{\mathrm{~d}t}{t^{1-\alpha/2}}=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}}_{P}+\underbrace{\int_{1}^{\infty}}_{Q}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\left|P\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{0}^{1}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}f(x)\frac{\mathrm{~d}t}{t^{1-\alpha/2}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha/2-1}f^{*}(x)\mathrm{~d}t=\frac{2}{\alpha}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}f^{*}(x)<\infty,

{\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)}^{-\alpha/2}f(x)=k_{\alpha,N}*f(x).

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left((-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|u_{n}|^{p}\right)|u_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left((-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|u_{n}-u|^{p}\right)|u_{n}-u|^{p}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\left\|v_{n}\right\|_{\lambda}^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left((-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|v_{n}|^{p}\right)|v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\rightarrow\zeta\quad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Furthermore,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{A\left(x_{0},\sqrt{3}\right)}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\int_{A\left(x_{n},R_{n}\right)}\left|u_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\delta

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in S_{\sqrt{3}}}\int_{A(x,\sqrt{3})}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

Following the Ekeland principle, we can assume that the sequence
\{v_{n}\}
is a Palais-Smale sequence. Specifically,

(3.9)

\left\langle v_{n},w\right\rangle_{\lambda}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left((-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|v_{n}|^{p}\right)|v_{n}(x)|^{p-2}v_{n}(x)w\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}+o_{n}(1)\|w\|\text{ for $w\in\mathcal{H_{\lambda}}(\mathbb{B}^{N})$},

where
o_{n}(1)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
. Consequently, up to a subsequence, we may assume
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
in
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right)
. Furthermore, due to this weak convergence and the continuity of the functional
u\mapsto J^{\prime}(u)
where
J(u)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left((-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|u|^{p}\right)|u|^{p}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}
, it is not difficult to deduce that

\|v\|_{\lambda}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left((-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|v|^{p}\right)|v|^{p}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

Thus, it remains to show that
v\not\equiv 0
to conclude that
v\in\mathcal{N}
.

Step 3:
Assume, if possible, that
v\equiv 0
. We propose that for any
1>r>2-\sqrt{3}
,

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}\cap\{|x|\geq r\}}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=o(1).

To establish this, select a point
a\in S_{\sqrt{3}}
. Define

such that
0\leq\Phi\leq 1
, and substitute

in (

). This yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}v_{n}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left(\Phi^{2}v_{n}\right)-\lambda v_{n}\Phi^{2}v_{n}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left((-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|v_{n}|^{p}\right)|v_{n}|^{p-2}v_{n}\Phi^{2}v_{n}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}+o(1).

Using local compact embedding, we simplify the left-hand side of the above expression as in (
[
6

)

\langle v_{n},\phi^{2}v_{n}\rangle_{\lambda}=\left\|\phi v_{n}\right\|_{\lambda}^{2}+o(1).

Thus, combining the two expressions above gives

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left(\Phi v_{n}\right)\right|^{2}-\lambda\left(\Phi v_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left((-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|v_{n}|^{p}\right)|v_{n}|^{p-2}\left(\Phi v_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}+o(1).

Using (
3.10

2.2
), and the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality, we derive

\displaystyle S_{\lambda,\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|\Phi v_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\leq C(N,\alpha)\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left[\left|v_{n}\right|^{p-2}\left(v_{n}\Phi\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle\leq C(N,\alpha)S_{\lambda,\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}^{-\frac{p}{2}}M^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|\Phi v_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\left(\int_{A(a,\sqrt{3})}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}.

Now, if

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|\Phi v_{n}(x)\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\nrightarrow 0,

we have

\displaystyle\delta^{\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle>\left(\int_{A(a,\sqrt{3})}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle\geq(C(N,\alpha))^{-1}S_{\lambda,\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}^{1+\frac{p}{2}}M^{-p}>\delta^{\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}},

which is a contradiction. Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|\Phi v_{n}(x)\right|^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\rightarrow 0.

Since
a\in S_{\sqrt{3}}
is arbitrary, the claim is proven.

Since the condition

ensures that

in

, this, combined with the previous assertion, directly contradicts (

). Consequently, we conclude that

and

.
∎

The objective of this section is to demonstrate the hyperbolic symmetry of ground states. Specifically, we will prove the following result.

To demonstrate the radial symmetry, we will utilize the polarization technique, which is also referred to as the method of symmetrization or rearrangement. To facilitate this, we first introduce some notations for use throughout this section. Fix an origin

in

be a closed, totally geodesic hypersurface such that

and

\sigma_{H}:\mathbb{B}^{N}\to\mathbb{B}^{N}

f^{H}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&\text{ if }x\in H,\\
\max(f(x),f(\sigma_{H}(x))),&\text{ if }x\in H^{+},\\
\min(f(x),f(\sigma_{H}(x))),&\text{ if }x\in H^{-}.\end{cases}

An initial step is to explore how polarization affects the non-local term and to analyze the equality cases.

Lemma 4.1

Let

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
u\in L^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})

We will divide the proof into the following two distinct cases for

\displaystyle[u^{H}(x)-u(x)]w(T_{y_{0}}(x))

u(x)\geq u(\sigma_{H}(x))

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
u\in L^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
. If for every closed, totally geodesic hypersurface
H\subset\mathbb{B}^{N}
with
x_{0}\in H^{+}
, we have
u^{H}=u
, then there exists a non-increasing function
v:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
u(x)=v(d(x,x_{0}))
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

For a function
u:\mathbb{B}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup{+\infty}
, the symmetric rearrangement, also known as Schwarz symmetrization,
u^{*}:\mathbb{B}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup{+\infty}
, is defined as the unique function such that for every
\lambda>0
, there exists
R\geq 0
satisfying

\left\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:u^{*}(x)>\lambda\right\}=B_{R}(0),

and

\mu\left\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:u^{*}(x)>\lambda\right\}=\mu\left\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:u(x)>\lambda\right\},

where
B_{R}(0)
denotes the geodesic ball in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
centered at
0
with radius
R
, and
\mu
denotes the volume measure in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

Thus,
u^{*}
is a radial and radially decreasing function whose superlevel sets have the same measure as those of
u
.

Clearly, Lemma
4.5
follows from the subsequent lemma:

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
s\geq 1
and
u\in L^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
be non-negative. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

u^{*}=u
.

(2)

For almost every
x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
, if
d(x,0)\leq d(y,0)
, then
u(x)\geq u(y)
.

(3)

For every closed, totally geodesic hypersurface
H
such that
0\in H^{+}
,
u(x)\geq u(\sigma_{H}(x))
, for almost every
x\in H^{+}
.

(4)

For every closed, totally geodesic hypersurface
H\subset\mathbb{B}^{N}
such that
0\in H^{+}
,
u^{H}=u
.

Proof.

The implication
\textit{(1)}\Rightarrow\textit{(2)}
is straightforward. To prove
\textit{(2)}\Rightarrow\textit{(1)}
, since
u
is radially decreasing, there exists a decreasing function
v:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
u(x)=v(d(x,0))
. Given that
u\in L^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, it is evident that
\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}v(r)=0
. Hence,
u\geq 0
and for all
\lambda>0
,
\mu\left\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:u^{*}(x)>\lambda\right\}=\mu\left\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:u(x)>\lambda\right\}
, which implies
u=u^{*}
.

There is nothing to prove for
\textit{(2)}\Rightarrow\textit{(3)}
. To prove
\textit{(3)}\Rightarrow\textit{(2)}
, let
x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
be such that
x\neq y
and
d(x,0)\leq d(y,0)
. There exists a closed, totally geodesic hypersurface
H
such that
x\in H^{+}
and
y=\sigma_{H}(x)
. By assumption, we have
u(y)=u(\sigma_{H}(x))\leq u(x)
.

Finally, the equivalence
\textit{(3)}\iff\textit{(4)}
follows from the definition of
u^{H}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Select
w\geq 0
satisfying the assumptions of Lemma
4.4
. For
x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
, define the function

W(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u(y)w(T_{x}(y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y).

This function is nonnegative, continuous, and
\lim_{d(x,0)\rightarrow\infty}W(x)=0
, indicating it attains a maximum at some point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

To finalize the proof using Lemma

, we need to show that for every closed, totally geodesic hypersurface
H\subset\mathbb{B}^{N}
where
x_{0}
is in
H^{+}
, the equality
u^{H}=u
holds. Specifically, if
u^{H}=u\circ\sigma_{H}
, then, considering that
w
is radial and based on the definition of
x_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u^{H}(y)w\left(T_{x_{0}}(y)\right)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u\left(\sigma_{H}(y)\right)w\left(T_{x_{0}}(y)\right)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u(y)w\left(T_{\sigma_{H}(x_{0})}(y)\right)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u(y)w\left(T_{x_{0}}(y)\right)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y).

Therefore, by Lemma
4.4
, we have
u^{H}=u
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Consider a closed, totally geodesic hypersurface

. Observe the following equalities:

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u^{H}\right|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u\right|^{2}\text{and}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|u^{H}\right|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left|u\right|^{2}.

Based on the characterization of ground states, it follows that

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left[(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|u|^{p}(y)\right]|u|^{p}(y)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)\geq\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left[(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|u^{H}|^{p}(y)\right]|u^{H}|^{p}(y)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y).

According to Lemma
4.1
, for any closed, totally geodesic hypersurface
H\subset\mathbb{B}^{N}
, it holds that either
u^{H}=u
or
u^{H}=u\circ\sigma_{H}
. Therefore, the conclusion can be drawn from Lemma
4.3
.

∎

R>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{(\sinh(d(x,0)))^{\beta}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{(\sinh\rho)^{N-1}}{(\sinh\rho)^{\beta}}\mathrm{~d}\rho

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{(\sinh\rho)^{N-1}\cosh\rho}{(\sinh\rho)^{\beta}}\mathrm{~d}\rho,

where
\omega_{N-1}
is the volume of
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. Consequently, this integral converges when
\beta<N
Similarly, if
\beta>N
, then

\int_{\left(B_{R}(0)\right)^{\complement}}\frac{1}{(\sinh(d(x,0)))^{\beta}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)<\infty.

Using the estimates from (4.1) in
[
26
]
, we obtain:

For
0<\rho<1
,
0<\alpha<N

\displaystyle k_{\alpha,N}(\rho)\leq C\frac{1}{\rho^{N-\alpha}}\leq C(N,\alpha)\frac{1}{(\sinh\rho)^{N-\alpha}}.

Further, for
\rho\geq 1
and
\alpha>2

\displaystyle k_{\alpha,N}(\rho)\leq C\rho^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2}}e^{-(N-1)\rho}\leq C\rho^{\alpha-2}e^{-(N-1)\rho}\leq Ce^{-\rho(N-\alpha)}\leq C\frac{1}{(\sinh\rho)^{N-\alpha}}.

For
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
, and
\rho\geq 1
, note that

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-(N-1)\rho}(\sinh\rho)^{N-\alpha}}{\rho^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}}\leq\left(\frac{\sinh\rho}{e^{\rho}}\right)^{N}\frac{e^{\rho}}{(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha-1}}\frac{e^{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\leq C.

Now, consider

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|u|^{p}=k_{\alpha,N}*|u|^{p}=\Bigl(\chi_{B_{R}(0)}k_{\alpha,N}\Bigr)*|u|^{p}+\left(\chi_{(B_{R}(0))^{\complement}}k_{\alpha,N}\right)*|u|^{p}.

From the estimates above, we can find
p_{1},p_{2}
such that

\chi_{B_{R}(0)}k_{\alpha,N}\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\text{ for some }p_{1}\in\left(1,\frac{N}{N-\alpha}\right),

and

\chi_{(B_{R}(0))^{\complement}}k_{\alpha,N}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\text{ for some }p_{2}\in\left(\frac{N}{N-\alpha},\infty\right).

Given that
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\leq p\leq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}
, it follows that
2<\frac{Np}{\alpha}<\frac{N}{\alpha}\frac{2N}{N-2}
. Therefore, there exist
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
such that

\displaystyle|u|^{p}\in L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\cap L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{B}^{N}),

where
q_{i}
and
p_{i}
are conjugate indices for
i=1,2
. Applying Hölder’s inequality, we establish the desired claim. We can conclude the theorem by applying Theorem 4.1 from
[
6
]
, noting that
2<p\leq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}<\frac{2N}{N-2}
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The
L^{p}
Calderón–Zygmund inequality on non-compact Riemannian manifolds has been investigated in
[
22
]
. However, to achieve
W^{2,p}
-type regularity in the current setting, the results from
[
22
]
cannot be directly applied; additional assumptions on the solution are necessary (see
[
22
, Proposition 3.8]
).

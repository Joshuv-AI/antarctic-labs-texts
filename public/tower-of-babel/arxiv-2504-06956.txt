How does the supercritical GMC converge?
Federico Bertacco 1, Martin Hairer 2

Abstract
In the spirit of [ BiskupLouidor ] , we study the local structure of ⋆ \star -scale invariant fields – a class of log-correlated Gaussian fields – around their extremal points by characterising the law of the “shape” of the field’s configuration near such points. As a consequence, we obtain a refined understanding of the freezing phenomenon in supercritical Gaussian multiplicative chaos.



1
Introduction

The theory of Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos (GMC) involves the study of random measures that can be formally expressed as

\upmu_{\upgamma}(dx)\mathrel{\text{``$=$''}}e^{\upgamma\mathrm{X}(x)}dx\;,

(1.1)

where
\upgamma>0
is a real positive parameter representing the
inverse temperature
of the model,
\mathrm{X}
is a
log-correlated Gaussian field
on a domain
\mathrm{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
dx
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathrm{D}
. Since
\mathrm{X}
only exists as a random Schwartz distribution, regularisation and renormalisation are necessary to show the existence of the measure
\upmu_{\upgamma}
as defined above
[
Kahane
,
DS11
,
RV_Review
,
Shamov
,
Berestycki_Elementary
]
.

It is by now well known that the behaviour of the random measure (
1.1
) exhibits a phase transition at

\gammac\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\;.

Following standard convention, we call the regime
\upgamma<\gammac

subcritical
, the borderline case
\upgamma=\gammac

critical
, and the range
\upgamma>\gammac

supercritical
. These three different regimes differ in the normalisation needed to obtain a non-trivial limiting measure, as well as in the qualitative features of the limiting measure (see also
[
Lacoin
]
for a more detailed phase diagram
including complex values of
\upgamma
).
Notably, in the supercritical regime, the limiting random measure is
not
measurable with respect to the underlying field
\mathrm{X}
and is
purely
atomic
. Before delving into more details and stating our main results, we introduce the family of log-correlated fields we will be working with.

The class of
\star
-scale invariant fields.

We consider log-correlated Gaussian fields
\mathrm{X}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with short-range correlations, which naturally admit an approximation by a martingale
(\mathrm{X}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Here, each
\mathrm{X}_{t}
is a smooth
Gaussian field, and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the process
(\mathrm{X}_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0}
is a standard Brownian motion.
Moreover, these fields satisfy a certain type of scale-invariance called
\star
-scale invariance

[
RV_Review
, Section 2.3]
. In a nutshell, it states that for any
s
,
t>0
, the fields
\mathrm{X}_{t}
and
\mathrm{X}_{t+s}-\mathrm{X}_{t}
are independent, and that the latter is equal in law to the field
\mathrm{X}_{s}
, spatially rescaled by a factor
e^{t}
.

The key ingredient in constructing a
\star
-scale invariant field is the so-called
seed covariance function

\mathfrak{K}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, which we assume satisfies the following properties:

(K1)

\mathfrak{K}
is positive definite, radial, and
\mathfrak{K}(0)=1
.

(K2)

\mathfrak{K}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and it is supported in
B(0,1)
.

Remark 1.1
.

The unit ball appearing in
(K2)
can of course be replaced by any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We write
\overline{\mathfrak{K}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
for the (unique) positive definite function such that the convolution of
\overline{\mathfrak{K}}
with itself equals
\mathfrak{K}
.

Remark 1.2
.

As a consequence of
(K1)
–
(K2)
, the (inverse) Fourier transform of
\mathfrak{K}
given by

\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{K}}(\omega)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}(2\uppi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{K}(x)e^{i\omega\cdot x}dx}
is a
probability measure with a smooth density and admitting moments of all orders.
In particular, the Hessian
D^{2}\mathfrak{K}(0)
at the
origin is strictly negative definite in the sense that there exists

\updelta>0
such that, for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one has

\langle v,D^{2}\mathfrak{K}(0)v\rangle\leq-\updelta|v|^{2}
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying assumptions
(K1)
–
(K2)
, and let
\overline{\mathfrak{K}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the (unique) positive definite function whose convolution with itself equals
\mathfrak{K}
.
For
\upxi
a space-time white noise on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
, we define the
\star
-scale invariant field with seed covariance
\mathfrak{K}
by

\mathrm{X}(\cdot)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathfrak{K}}\bigl(e^{r}(y-\cdot)\bigr)e^{\frac{rd}{2}}\upxi(dy,dr)\;.

(1.2)

Furthermore, for
0\leq s<t
, we let
\mathrm{X}_{s,t}
be the field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\mathrm{X}_{s,t}(\cdot)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}\overline{\mathfrak{K}}\bigl(e^{r}(y-\cdot)\bigr)e^{\frac{rd}{2}}\upxi(dy,dr)\;,

(1.3)

with the notational convention that
\mathrm{X}_{0,t}=\mathrm{X}_{t}
.

For
t\geq 0
, the fields
\mathrm{X}
and
\mathrm{X}_{t}
have the following covariance structures, for all
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}(x)\mathrm{X}(y)\bigr]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{K}\bigl(e^{r}(x-y)\bigr)dr\;,\qquad\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}_{t}(x)\mathrm{X}_{t}(y)\bigr]=\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{K}\bigl(e^{r}(x-y)\bigr)dr\;.

(1.4)

Clearly
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{X}(x)^{2}]=\infty
, so
\mathrm{X}
can only be realised as a random Schwartz distribution.
The collection of fields
(\mathrm{X}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called the
martingale approximation
of
\mathrm{X}
. Indeed,
by construction,
\smash{(\mathrm{X}_{t})_{t\geq 0}}
is a martingale for the filtration
\smash{(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}}
given by

\mathcal{F}_{t}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sigma\bigl(\mathrm{X}_{s}\,:\,s\in[0,t)\bigr)\;.

(1.5)

Moreover, as
t\to\infty
the field
\mathrm{X}_{t}
converges almost surely to a limit
\mathrm{X}
in the Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{-\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
\kappa>0
. We refer to
[
Junnila_deco
, Proposition 4.1 (iv)]
for a proof of this fact.

The three phases of GMC measures.

As previously mentioned, GMC measures exhibit three distinct phases depending on the value of the parameter
\upgamma>0
in (
1.1
). Each phase is characterised by the specific form of renormalisation required to obtain a nontrivial limiting measure.

In the
subcritical regime
, i.e., when
\upgamma\in(0,\gammac)
, the sequence of random measures

\upmu_{\upgamma,t}(dx)=e^{\upgamma\mathrm{X}_{t}(x)-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}t}dx

(1.6)

converges weakly in probability to a limiting positive random measure
\upmu_{\upgamma}
as
t\to\infty

[
Kahane
,
RV_Review
,
Shamov
,
Berestycki_Elementary
]
, which is almost surely nontrivial. It is well known that
\upmu_{\upgamma}
is almost surely non-atomic, but singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. Many further properties of these measures concerning, among others, moments and multifractal behaviour are known
[
RV_Review
,
BerMulti
]
. An important feature of the measure
\upmu_{\upgamma}
is that it is carried by the set of
\upgamma
-thick points. Intuitively, a thick point is a point where the field takes an unusually large value: one where it is of the order of its variance instead of the order of its standard deviation.

When
\upgamma\geq\gammac
a phase transition occurs and if one considers the sequence of measures defined in (
1.6
), then for any compact subset
\mathrm{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that
\smash{\upmu_{\upgamma,t}(\mathrm{A})\to 0}
in probability as
t\to\infty
. Therefore, in order to define a nontrivial limiting measure at the critical threshold
\gammac
, one needs to give the sequence of approximating measures an extra “push” in the right direction.
More precisely, in the
critical regime
, i.e., when
\upgamma=\gammac
, the sequence of random measures

\upmu_{\gammac,t}(dx)=\bigl(-\mathrm{X}_{t}(x)+\gammac t\bigr)e^{\gammac\mathrm{X}_{t}(x)-\frac{\gammac^{2}}{2}t}dx

(1.7)

converges weakly in probability as
t\to\infty
to a limiting positive random measure
\smash{\upmu_{\gammac}}

[
Critical_der
,
Criticial_KPZ
,
Powell_Critical
]
, which is non-atomic and has full support. The normalisation used in (
1.7
) is known as the “derivative normalisation” since it can be obtained by evaluating at
\upgamma=\gammac
the derivative with respect to
\upgamma
of the expression on the right-hand side of (
1.6
).

Remark 1.4
.

There is also an equivalent deterministic normalisation, called “Seneta–Heyde normalisation”, which produces the same limiting measure
\smash{\upmu_{\gammac}}
up to a deterministic multiplicative constant,
namely

\upmu_{\gammac,t}^{\mathrm{SH}}(dx)=\sqrt{t}e^{\gammac\mathrm{X}_{t}(x)-\frac{\gammac^{2}}{2}t}dx

(1.8)

converges weakly in probability to
\smash{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2/\uppi}}\,\upmu_{\gammac}}
as
t\to\infty

[
Junnila_deco
,
Powell_Critical
]
. In the present article, unless otherwise stated, we always refer to the critical GMC as the one obtained using the derivative normalisation (
1.7
).

In the low temperature or
supercritical regime
, i.e.
\upgamma>\gammac
, the GMC exhibits atomic
behaviour under suitable renormalisation, with the locations and masses of the atoms dictated by the extremal
statistics or near maximum values of the fields
\mathrm{X}_{t}
. In the continuum, the only available mathematical
result we are aware of is
[
Glassy
]
, where Madaule, Rhodes, and Vargas show that for
\upgamma>\gammac
, the sequence of random measures

\upmu_{\upgamma,t}(dx)=t^{\frac{3\upgamma}{2\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}}e^{t(\upgamma/\sqrt{\smash[b]{2}}-\sqrt{\smash[b]{d}})^{2}}e^{\upgamma\mathrm{X}_{t}(x)-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}t}dx\;,

(1.9)

converges weakly in law as
t\to\infty
to a nontrivial purely atomic limiting measure
\upmu_{\upgamma}
whose law was previously conjectured in
[
Critical_der
]
and can be characterised explicitly in terms of the law of the critical GMC
\smash{\upmu_{\gammac}}
. In order to describe this limiting measure, it is convenient to introduce the following notation which will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 1.5
.

For
\upgamma>\gammac
and a non-negative, locally finite Borel measure
\upnu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we let
\upeta_{\upgamma}[\upnu]
be the Poisson point measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
with intensity measure given by
\smash{\upnu(dx)\otimes z^{-(1+\gammac/\upgamma)}dz}
. We also define the integrated atomic random measure with parameter
\upgamma
and spatial intensity
\upnu
as the random purely atomic measure
\mathcal{P}_{\upgamma}[\upnu]
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\mathcal{P}_{\upgamma}[\upnu](dx)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\int_{0}^{\infty}z\,\upeta_{\upgamma}[\upnu](dx,dz)\;.

With this notation in place, we can state the main result of
[
Glassy
]
more precisely.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
Glassy
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

For any
\smash{\upgamma>\gammac}
there exists a constant
c_{\upgamma}>0
such that the sequence of random measures
\smash{(\upmu_{\upgamma,t})_{t>0}}
defined in (
1.9
) converges in law, with respect to the vague topology, to
\smash{c_{\upgamma}\mathcal{P}_{\upgamma}[\upmu_{\gammac}]}
as
t\to\infty
, where
\upmu_{\gammac}
is the critical GMC.

Remark 1.9

One motivation for working within this general framework is that it facilitates our companion paper
[

\mathrm{X}_{{(\upvarepsilon)}}
denotes the convolution approximation of a

\mathrm{X}_{(e^{-t})}\approx\mathrm{X}_{t}+\mathrm{W}(e^{t}\cdot)

The proof of convergence of the process
(\upmu_{\upgamma,t+s})_{s\geq 0}

t\to\infty

[

]
, requires a fine understanding of the structure of
\star
-scale invariant fields around their extremal points.
Specifically, for
t\gg 0
, let
\mathrm{X}_{t}
be the martingale approximation of a
\star
-scale invariant field
\mathrm{X}
, and condition it on two events: first, that its value at the origin is comparable to the maximum of
\mathrm{X}_{t}
within an order-one region; second, that the origin is a “mesoscopic maximum” within this region, i.e., a local maximum that is global within a ball of radius much smaller than
1
, but much larger
than
e^{-t}
.

Then, after shifting the coordinate system at this maximum (so that the value at the origin becomes
0
) and after a suitable rescaling, we want to study the “shape of the field” in that region. This is the content of Theorem
A
, which describes the law of the field governing the shape of the field
\mathrm{X}_{t}
around a mesoscopic maximum.

We emphasise that in the case of dimension
d=2
, a similar investigation has been conducted for the discrete Gaussian free field (DGFF) in
[
BiskupLouidor
]
. However, their results rely heavily on the fact that the DGFF is defined on the discrete grid
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
There, one can condition a field on the origin being the maximum in a bounded region and then take the limit as the size of the region tends to infinity in order to obtain
the “cluster process” which describes the shape of the field around a mesoscopic
maximum. In the continuum however, conditioning the origin to be a maximum even within a bounded region becomes problematic, as such a conditioning is already degenerate.

To address this issue, we introduce a “softer” form of conditioning: we fix an arbitrary
threshold
\uplambda>0
and, instead of conditioning on the origin being a mesoscopic
maximum, we condition on the value at the origin being at least as large as the nearest
mesoscopic maximum minus
\uplambda
. This conditioning is non-degenerate within a bounded region as long as
\uplambda>0
. Theorem
A
proves that we can then take the limit as the region size increases to infinity, thus yielding a limiting field
\smash{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}}
.
Finally, we show the existence of a unique random field
\Uppsi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is
0
at the origin, takes only negative values, is independent of the arbitrary threshold
\uplambda
, and such that
\smash{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}}
can be expressed as a randomly shifted version of
\Uppsi
(under a suitably tilted measure, see (
2.10
) for details). In this sense, we can consider
\Uppsi
as the canonical field describing the shape of the field
\mathrm{X}
from the perspective of a mesoscopic maximum.

2.1
Local structure of extremal points

We aim to investigate the local structure around points within the domain where the field attains unusually large values, comparable to its maximum. The strong correlation with nearby points suggests that each peak in the field comes with a cluster of high values. These clusters of high-value points are generally well-separated from each other. By selecting one of these clusters and identifying as reference point the maximum of the field inside the cluster, our goal is to describe the “shape” of the field in the vicinity of this reference point.

Remark 2.1
.

We will see that the behaviour of
\mathrm{X}_{t}
near a mesoscopic maximum is dominated by its suprema
over spherical shells. These in turn behave like a Brownian motion, with time given by the
logarithm of the radius of the shell. It is useful to keep this analogy in mind when parsing the
results in this section.

In order to make this heuristic precise, we need to introduce some notation.

Definition 2.2
.

For
t\in(0,\infty)
, we introduce the
recentering constant

\mathfrak{m}_{t}
by letting

\mathfrak{m}_{t}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}t-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}\log t\;.

(2.1)

Furthermore, we define functions
\mathfrak{h}_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathfrak{h}_{t}(x)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{K}(e^{-s}x)ds\;,\qquad\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(1-\mathfrak{K}(e^{-s}x)\bigr)ds\;,

(2.2)

with the definition of
\mathfrak{a}_{t}
extended also to the case
t=\infty
.

In
[
Madaule_Max
]
, Madaule proved that there exists a constant
c>0
such that the following convergence in law holds as
t\to\infty
,

\sup_{x\in B(0,1)}\mathrm{X}_{t}(x)-\mathfrak{m}_{t}-c\Rightarrow\mathrm{G}+\log\upmu_{\gammac}\bigl(B(0,1)\bigr)\;,

where
\mathrm{G}
is an independent random variable with standard Gumbel distribution, and
\upmu_{\gammac}
is the critical GMC.
Hence, if we aim to describe the shape of the field around a point where the value of the field is comparable to its maximum, it seems natural to condition the field
\mathrm{X}_{t}
on achieving the value
\mathfrak{m}_{t}+z
at the origin, for some fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
, while simultaneously requiring that the origin is a mesoscopic maximum. We first zoom in around the origin by introducing the rescaled field

\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(\cdot)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathrm{X}_{t}(e^{-t}\cdot)\;.

We now introduce several Gaussian fields that will play an important role in our analysis, and whose significance will become clear in the following paragraphs.

Definition 2.3
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
, we let
\Phi_{t}
denote the centred Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the same law as the field
\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}-\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(0)
. In particular, for all
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\Phi_{t}(x)\Phi_{t}(y)\bigr]=\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x)+\mathfrak{a}_{t}(y)-\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x-y)\;.

Moreover, for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
, we let
\Upupsilon_{\!t}
be the Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\Upupsilon_{\!t}(\cdot)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\Phi_{t}(\cdot)-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\mathfrak{a}_{t}(\cdot)\;.

Remark 2.4
.

As one can easily check, the covariance of the field
\Phi_{\infty}
resembles very much the covariance structure of the DGFF on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
pinned to zero at zero (see
[
BiskupLouidor
, Equation (2.7)]
). Indeed, in this setting, the covariance takes the same form as for the field
\Phi_{\infty}
with
\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
given by the potential kernel of the simple symmetric random walk started from zero (see
[
BiskupLouidor
, Equation (2.8)]
).

Now, going back to our previous discussion, a straightforward calculation shows that the field
\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}
conditioned to take the value
\mathfrak{m}_{t}+z
at the origin has the same law as the shifted field

\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(\cdot)-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(\cdot)\bigl(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(0)-(\mathfrak{m}_{t}+z)\bigr)\;.

In particular, for
0\ll b\ll t
, and for every
x
,
y\in B(0,e^{b})
, we note that, for
t\gg 0
large enough, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(x)-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(x)\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(0)\bigr)\bigl(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(y)-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(y)\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(0)\bigr)\bigr]\approx\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x)+\mathfrak{a}_{t}(y)-\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x-y)\;,

and also

(\mathfrak{m}_{t}+z)-(\mathfrak{m}_{t}+z)\mathfrak{h}_{t}(x)\approx\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x)\;.

Therefore, the preceding computations imply that, as
t\to\infty
, the following convergence in law holds when restricted to the ball
B(0,e^{b})
:

\bigl(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(\cdot)-\mathfrak{h}_{t}(\cdot)\bigl(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{t}(0)-(\mathfrak{m}_{t}+z)\bigr)\bigr)-(\mathfrak{m}_{t}+z)\Rightarrow\Phi_{\infty}(\cdot)-\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}(\cdot)=\Upupsilon_{\!\infty}(\cdot)\;.

These considerations suggest that the shape of the field
\mathrm{X}
near a mesoscopic maximum is given by the limit in law, as
b\to\infty
, of the field
\Upupsilon_{\!\infty}
conditioned to be non-positive on the ball
B(0,e^{b})
.

Before proving that this limit indeed exists, we introduce some additional notation. For
k\geq 0
, we set

\mathcal{R}_{k}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\bigl\{\mathbf{F}:\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,\mathbf{F}(\upphi)=\mathbf{F}(\uppsi)\;\text{whenever}\;\upphi|_{B(0,k)}=\uppsi|_{B(0,k)}\bigr\}\;.

(2.3)

In other words,
\mathcal{R}_{k}
is the set of (measurable) mappings from
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathbb{R}
that depend on the values of the input function only inside
B(0,k)
. Furthermore, with a slight abuse of the usual notation, we define

\mathcal{C}^{b}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{k}\cap\mathcal{C}^{b}(\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\;,

(2.4)

where
\mathcal{C}^{b}
denotes continuous bounded functions.
We can now state our first main result, where we write
\mathbb{M}_{0,b}(f)
as a shorthand for
\smash{\sup_{\lvert x\rvert\leq e^{b}}f(x)}
.

Theorem A
.

For each
\uplambda>0
, there exists a continuous random field
\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that, for any function
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{C}^{b}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, one has

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbf{F}\bigl(\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}\bigr)\bigr]=\lim_{b\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{F}\bigl(\Upupsilon_{\!b}\bigr)\,\big|\,\mathbb{M}_{0,b}(\Upupsilon_{\!b})\leq\uplambda\bigr]=\lim_{b\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{F}\bigl(\Upupsilon_{\!\infty}\bigr)\,\big|\,\mathbb{M}_{0,b}(\Upupsilon_{\!\infty})\leq\uplambda\bigr]\;.

(2.5)

We emphasise that the existence of the weak limits in (
2.5
) is part of the statement.
We also observe that the conditioning on the right-hand side of (
2.5
) is singular as
b\to\infty
. More precisely, by letting

\upalpha\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sqrt{\smash[b]{2/\uppi}}\;,

we have the following result.

Theorem B
.

For each
\uplambda>0
, there exists a constant
c_{\star,\uplambda}>0
, such that

\lim_{b\to\infty}\sqrt{b}\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathbb{M}_{0,b}(\Upupsilon_{\!b})\leq\uplambda\bigr)=\lim_{b\to\infty}\sqrt{b}\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathbb{M}_{0,b}(\Upupsilon_{\!\infty})\leq\uplambda\bigr)=\upalpha\,c_{\star,\uplambda}\;.

Remark 2.5
.

In Section
, we introduce a characterisation of the field
\Phi_{b}
in terms of a stochastic integral driven by a one-dimensional Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(as a cartoon, think of this as being a smoothened out version of the field
x\mapsto-B_{\log|x|}
), plus an independent centred Gaussian field, see (
). In particular, we will prove a stronger version of Theorems
A
and
B
, where we also allow conditioning on the value of
B
at time
b
. We refer to Propositions
and
for the precise statements.

Remark 2.6
.

Although we don’t have an explicit representation for the constant
c_{\star,\uplambda}
, we will see in (
) below that it is given by

c_{\star,\uplambda}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl[B_{k}\mathds{1}_{\{B_{k}\in[k^{1/6},k^{5/6}]\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\mathbb{M}_{0,k}(\Upupsilon_{\!k})\leq\uplambda\}}\bigr]\;,

(2.6)

with
B
and
\Upupsilon_{\!k}
related as in Remark

.
The exponents
1/6
and
5/6
appearing here are of course unimportant and could probably be replaced by any

(0,1/2)
and
(1/2,\infty)
respectively. We refer to Lemma
for a proof of the fact that
c_{\star,\uplambda}\in(0,\infty)

We recall that the introduction of a threshold
\uplambda>0
in Theorems
A
and
B
is necessary due to the continuous setting in which we are working. In such a context, conditioning on the event that a field, which is zero at the origin, remains negative is ill-posed. However, it is desirable to define a “canonical” field that captures the local structure of
\mathrm{X}
around an extremal point, without being arbitrarily dependent on
\uplambda
.

To achieve this, we introduce below a field
\smash{\Uppsi_{\uplambda}}
, which is essentially just
\smash{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}}
shifted to move its maximum to the origin, but under a slightly tilted law. At first glance, it may seem contradictory to define a field that we claim is independent of
\uplambda
while still denoting it as
\Uppsi_{\uplambda}
. This notation arises because, from its definition, it is not immediately evident that
\Uppsi_{\uplambda}
is indeed independent of the threshold
\uplambda
. However, this independence (albeit in a slightly weaker sense) will be established a posteriori (see Proposition
2.13
).

Before defining the field
\Uppsi_{\uplambda}
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we introduce the shift operator
\uptau_{x}:\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\uptau_{x}f(\cdot)=f(\cdot+x)-f(x)\;,\qquad\forall\,f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\;.

(2.7)

Furthermore, we let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
denote the uncountable set introduced in Lemma
below. Roughly speaking,
\Lambda
consists of the “good thresholds”
\uplambda
for which the law of the field
\smash{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}}
exhibits some desirable properties.
More precisely, we need to exclude the “bad” values of
\uplambda
such that

\smash{\mathbb{P}(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)=\uplambda)>0}
or
\smash{\mathbb{P}(\lvert\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)-\uplambda\}\rvert>0)>0}
where
\lvert\cdot\rvert
denotes Lebesgue measure.
We emphasise that we don’t expect any such bad values to exist, i.e., we expect that
\Lambda=\mathbb{R}^{+}
. However, since the field
\smash{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}}
is itself defined by a singular conditioning, proving this fact would require additional effort. Since our main result does not require ruling out the existence of bad values, we will not investigate this fact further.
In any case, we have to exclude at most countably many points, so
\Lambda
is dense.

Definition 2.7
.

For
\uplambda\in\Lambda
, we let
\Uppsi_{\uplambda}
be the field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
uniquely characterised by the fact that, for all
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{C}^{b}(\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbf{F}(\Uppsi_{\uplambda})\bigr]\propto\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathbf{F}\bigl(\uptau_{x_{\star}}\!\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}\bigr)e^{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x_{\star})}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)}\mathds{1}_{\{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)\geq\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x_{\star})-\uplambda\}}dx}\right]\;,

(2.8)

where
\smash{x_{\star}=\argmax\{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)\,:\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}}
and the proportionality constant is chosen in such a way that
\mathbb{E}[1]=1
.

Remark 2.8
.

The fact that the proportionality constant in the previous definition lies in
(0,\infty)
is proved in Lemma
.

We now state the following key “resampling property” of the field
\smash{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}}
, whose proof is given in Section
.

Proposition 2.9
.

For each
\uplambda\in\Lambda
and for all
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{C}^{b}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\bigr[\mathbf{F}(\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda})\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}(\uptau_{x}\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda})e^{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)}\mathds{1}_{\{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)\geq\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x_{\star})-\uplambda\}}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)}\mathds{1}_{\{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)\geq\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x_{\star})-\uplambda\}}dx}\Biggr]\;,

(2.9)

where we recall that
\smash{x_{\star}=\argmax\{\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}(x)\,:\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}}
.

Remark 2.10
.

In fact, Proposition
2.9
is quite general and applies to a large class of Gaussian fields
of the form
\Phi-\upeta\mathfrak{a}
where
\Phi
is centred with
\mathbb{E}[(\Phi(x)-\Phi(y))^{2}]=\mathfrak{a}(x-y)
.
In particular, one can replace the
field
\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda}
in (
2.9
) by a drifted, conditioned Brownian motion.
Specifically, for
\uplambda>0
and
\upeta\geq 0
, let
\smash{(X^{\uplambda,\upeta}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}}
be a two-sided Brownian motion with drift
t\mapsto-\upeta|t|
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, conditioned to remain below
\uplambda
at all times.
When
\upeta>0
this is a condition that happens with positive probability, while the case
\upeta=0
can be
covered by a limiting procedure, yielding a two-sided three-dimensional Bessel process.
Furthermore, consider the set

\mathrm{A}_{\uplambda,\upeta}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\Biggl\{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,X^{\uplambda,\upeta}_{t}\geq\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}X^{\uplambda,\upeta}_{s}-\uplambda\Biggr\}\;,

and let
\uprho_{\uplambda,\upeta}
be the (random) probability measure on
\mathrm{A}_{\uplambda,\upeta}
defined as follows

\uprho_{\uplambda,\upeta}(dt)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{e^{\upeta X^{\uplambda,\upeta}_{t}}}{\int_{\mathrm{A}_{\uplambda,\upeta}}e^{\upeta X^{\uplambda,\upeta}_{s}}ds}\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathrm{A}_{\uplambda,\upeta}\}}dt\;.

Then, if
t_{\star}
is a point sampled from the probability measure
\uprho_{\uplambda,\upeta}
, we have the identity in law

\bigl(X^{\uplambda,\upeta}_{t+t_{\star}}-X^{\uplambda,\upeta}_{t_{\star}}\bigr)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\bigl(X^{\uplambda,\upeta}_{t}\bigr)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\;,

which we were unable to find in the existing literature.
Note that, in particular, when
\upeta=0
, the probability measure
\uprho_{\uplambda,0}
is the uniform measure on
\mathrm{A}_{\uplambda,0}
.

Now, returning to our setting, we observe that, thanks to Proposition
2.9
, an alternative way to characterise
\Uppsi_{\uplambda}
is by inverting (
2.8
). More precisely, we have the following result, whose proof is given in Section
.

Proposition 2.11
.

For any
\uplambda\in\Lambda
and for all
\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{C}^{b}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbf{F}(\widetilde{\Upupsilon}_{\!\uplambda})\bigr]\propto\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}(\uptau_{x}\Uppsi_{\uplambda})e^{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\Uppsi_{\uplambda}(x)}\mathds{1}_{\{\Uppsi_{\uplambda}(x)\geq-\uplambda\}}dx\Biggr]\;,

(2.10)

where the proportionality constant is chosen in such a way that
\mathbb{E}[1]=1
.

Remark 2.12
.

We observe that the fact that the proportionality constant in the previous proposition lies in
(0,\infty)
follows directly from Definition
2.7
of the field
\Uppsi_{\uplambda}
, together with the fact that the proportionality constant appearing in that definition lies in
(0,\infty)
.

As claimed above, we now state a result that confirms that the field
\Uppsi_{\uplambda}
, introduced in Definition

, is indeed canonical. Specifically, we have the following result, whose proof is given in Section
.

Proposition 2.13
.

For any
\uplambda_{1}
,
\uplambda_{2}\in\Lambda
, one has
\Uppsi_{\uplambda_{1}}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\Uppsi_{\uplambda_{2}}

Since the law of
\Uppsi_{\uplambda}
does not depend on
\uplambda
, except possibly for a countable
number of “bad” values, we just write
\Uppsi
from now on.

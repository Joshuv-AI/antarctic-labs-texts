On two versions of holomorphic quantum plane
O. Yu. Aristov

Abstract
. We find power series descriptions of two versions of holomorphic quantum plane, the Arens–Michael envelope and the envelope with respect to the class of Banach PI algebras, in the case of non-unitary parameter.



Introduction

We consider two completions of the universal complex associative algebra with generators
x
and
y
satisfying the relation
xy=qyx
with a complex parameter
q
(Manin’s quantum plane). The first completion is the envelope with respect to the class of all Banach algebras (the Arens–Michael envelope) and the second is the envelope with respect to the class of Banach algebras satisfying a polynomial identity (PI algebras). We denote them by
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
and
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}
, respectively. Both algebras deserve the name ’holomorphic quantum planes’.

It seems that the first to study analytical versions of quantum affine spaces (in particular, quantum planes) was Pirkovskii
[
26
]
in 2008. Interest in this topic has been revived lately; see
[
8
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. On the other hand, the class
\mathsf{PI}
of Banach PI algebras have been studied but not very actively; see
[
23
,
24
]
. Recently, however, it was discovered that this area is connected to non-commutative geometry; see the papers of the author
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Specifically, envelopes with respect to
\mathsf{PI}
were introduced in
[
7
]
. For classical algebras arising in non-commutative algebraic geometry, such envelopes can be, along with Arens–Michael envelopes, treated as objects of study in non-commutative complex-analytic geometry. Furthermore, envelopes with respect to
\mathsf{PI}
are often easier to work with and have a simpler structure. This feature is demonstrated in this paper by using quantum planes as an example.

Our main aim is to find power series representations of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
and
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}
. Note that the following description of elements of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
as series in powers of
x
and
y
is given in
[
26
, Corollary 5.14]
:

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})=\Bigl\{a=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{ij}y^{i}x^{j}\!:\,\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{ij}|\,r^{i+j}<\infty\;\;\forall r>0\Bigr\}.

(Here
|q|\leqslant 1
. The case when
|q|>1
can be easily reduced to this one by transposing
x
and
y
.) We are not able to add something new to this picture for
|q|=1
. But in the case when
|q|<1
, we show that both
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
and
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}
can be written in a more structural form with the use also of powers of the product
u=xy
. (Note that the difference between the cases of
|q|=1
and
|q|\neq 1
naturally arises in the study of other holomorphic quantum algebras; see
[
5
]
.) Specifically, as locally convex spaces,

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\cong\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}\cong\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[u]],

where
\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}
stands for the complete projective tensor product of locally convex spaces,
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
for the algebra of holomorphic functions on the Gelfand spectrum,
\mathbb{C}[[u]]
for the algebra of all formal power series in
u
, and
\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}
for an algebra of formal power series with a certain restriction on growth.

Our approach is motivated by a study of a
C^{\infty}
-version of the quantum plane in
[
8
, § 4]
. Each of the concomitant Banach algebra in the
C^{\infty}
-version automatically satisfies to a polynomial identity, and so it is not surprising that we can use the method developed in
[
8
]
for the algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}
. (Note that in the PI-variant the only restriction we require is that
q\neq 1
.) Indeed, we demonstrate that the
C^{\infty}
-argument can be applied in this situation. However, it is remarkable that it can also be modified for the more involved case of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
.

1.
Preliminaries and statement of results

In this text, we consider only unital associative algebras over the field
\mathbb{C}
, except for Remark
1.3
, which mentions Lie algebras.

Recall that a topological algebra is called an
Arens–Michael algebra
if it is complete and isomorphic to a projective limit of Banach algebras. Denote by
\mathsf{PI}
the class of Banach algebras satisfying a polynomial identity. (We call them Banach PI algebras.) Following
[
7
, Definition 5.4]
we say that a topological algebra is a
locally in
\mathsf{PI}
if it is isomorphic to a projective limit of algebras contained in
\mathsf{PI}
. The term
locally BPI algebra
is also used; see
[
8
, Definition 1.1]
for a more general context.

Recall that an
Arens–Michael envelope of an associative algebra
A
is a pair
(\widehat{A},\iota)
, where
\widehat{A}
is an Arens–Michael algebra and
\iota
is a homomorphism
A\to\widehat{A}
such that for every Banach algebra (equivalently, Arens–Michael algebra)
B
and every homomorphism
\varphi\!:A\to B
there is a unique continuous homomorphism

\widehat{\varphi}\!:\widehat{A}\to B
making the diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{\widehat{A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widehat{\varphi}}
\textstyle{B}

commutative.

In
[
8
]
this definition has been generalised to an arbitrary class of Banach algebras. In particular, we can take
\mathsf{PI}
; see
[
7
]
. More specifically, an
envelope of an algebra
A
with respect to the class
\mathsf{PI}
is a pair
(\widehat{A}^{\,\mathsf{PI}},\iota)
, where is
\widehat{A}^{\,\mathsf{PI}}
is locally in
\mathsf{PI}
and
\iota
is a homomorphism
A\to\widehat{A}^{\,\mathsf{PI}}
that satisfies the same universal property but in the class of Banach PI algebras (equivalently, locally BPI algebras); see
[
7
, Definition 5.4]
and
[
8
, Definition 1.2]
. Note that Arens–Michael envelopes and envelopes with respect to
\mathsf{PI}
always exist; see
[
21
, Exercise V.2.24]
and
[
7
, Proposition 5.7]
, respectively; cf.
[
8
, Proposition 1.4]
.

For
q\in\mathbb{C}
denote by
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
the universal complex associative algebra generated by
x
and
y
subject to relation
xy=qyx
.
First we write
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
in a form that will be convenient for later use. Put

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\!

\displaystyle:=\{(f,g)\in\mathbb{C}[t]\times\mathbb{C}[t]\!:\,f(0)=g(0)\};

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\!

\displaystyle:=\{(f,g)\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})\!:\,f(0)=g(0)\}.

Note that
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
can be identified with the quotients of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
and
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, respectively, by the ideals generated by
xy
. (In the second case the ideal is automatically closed.) So
\Omega
can be identified with the Gelfand spectrum (the set of one-dimensional representations) of
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)
and similarly for
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
.

Denote the pairs
(t,0)
and
(0,t)
in
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)
by
X
and
Y
and put
u=xy
. Then
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
can be identified with
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
via the linear isomorphism

(1.3)

x^{i}u^{j}\mapsto X^{i}\otimes u^{j},\quad y^{i}u^{j}\mapsto Y^{i}\otimes u^{j},\quad u^{j}\mapsto 1\otimes u^{j}.

So we can assume that
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
coincides with
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
endowed with the multiplication determined by the relations
XY=qYX
and
XY=u
.

Statement of main results

For
s\in(0,1)
denote by
\mathfrak{B}_{s}
the universal Arens–Michael algebra generated topologically by a single element
u
satisfying the condition

(1.4)

\|u^{n}\|^{1/n}=O(s^{n})\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}

for rach continuous submultiplicative seminorm
\|\cdot\|
.
(The existence of such an algebra is proved in Corollary
2.3
below.) The importance of
\mathfrak{B}_{s}
for our problem stems from the fact that, in a Banach algebra, the relation
xy=qyx
with
|q|<1
implies that (
1.4
) holds with
u=xy
and
s=|q|^{1/2}
; see Lemma
2.1
. The algebra
\mathbb{C}[[z]]
, consisting of all formal power series, is also universal, now with respect to the condition that
z
is nilpotent; see Lemma
3.6
.

Using the linear isomorphism
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\cong\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
described above, we can treat
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
as a vector subspace of both
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}
and
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[u]]
. Consider the corresponding embeddings,

(1.5)

\iota_{1}\!:\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\to\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\iota_{2}\!:\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\to\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[u]].

The following two theorems are our main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
|q|<1
.

(A)
The multiplication in
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
can be extended to a continuous operation on
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}
that turns it into an Arens–Michael algebra.

(B)
Taking
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}
with this multiplication, the embedding

\iota_{1}\!:\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\to\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}

is an Arens–Michael enveloping homomorphism, i.e.,
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\cong\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}
.

In fact,

(1.6)

\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}=\Bigl\{a=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}\!:\|a\|_{r,\omega}\!:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\,r^{n}|q|^{n^{2}/2}<\infty\;\;\forall r\in(0,\infty)\Bigr\};

see (
2.2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

(cf.
[
8
, Theorem 4.3]
)

Let
q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

(A)
The multiplication in
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
can be extended to a continuous operation on
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[u]]
that turns it into a locally BPI algebra.

(B)
If, in addition,
q\neq 1
, then, taking
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[u]]
with this multiplication, the embedding

\iota_{2}\!:\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\to\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[u]]

is an enveloping homomorphism with respect to
\mathsf{PI}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}\cong\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[u]]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional nilpotent complex Lie algebra and
U(\mathfrak{g})
the corresponding universal enveloping algebra. Then, as a locally convex space,

\widehat{U}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathfrak{A}_{i_{1}}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\cdots\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{A}_{i_{p}}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{k})

for some
i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}
and
k
. Here
\mathfrak{A}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathfrak{A}_{i_{p}}
are certain power series algebras of the form given in (
2.1
) below; for details see
[
10
, Theorem 2.5]
. Other forms of this isomorphism can be found in
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
6
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
7
, Theorem 6.4]
). (In the case when
\mathfrak{g}
is solvable, there are similar but slightly more complicated formulas.)
Furthermore, it follow from
[
7
, Theorem 6.6]
that

\widehat{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathrm{PI}}\cong\mathbb{C}[[x_{1}]]\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\cdots\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[x_{p}]]\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{k}).

The latter algebra is actually the algebra of ‘formally-radical functions’ considered by Dosi in
[
12
]
.

Thus the degeneracy effect, when some elements in an envelope of a non-commutative algebra generate spaces of power series that are larger than the space of entire functions, is not only a characteristic of quantum planes.

2.
The Arens–Michael envelope

In the case when
|q|<1
, our description of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
, the Arens–Michael envelope of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
, is based on the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be elements of a Banach algebra such that
XY=qYX
for some

|q|<1
. Then the growth condition in (
1.4
) holds with
s=|q|^{1/2}
and
u=XY
(or
u=YX
).

Proof.

It is not hard to see by induction that
(XY)^{n}=q^{n(n+1)/2}Y^{n}X^{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore

\|(XY)^{n}\|^{1/n}\leqslant q^{(n+1)/2}\|Y\|\,\|X\|
and hence (
1.4
) holds.

The case when
u=YX
is similar.
∎

First, we demonstrate the existence of a universal algebra in a more general situation by describing its explicit form.
Let
\omega=(\omega_{n};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})
be a submultiplicative weight, i.e.,
\omega_{n}\geqslant 0
and
\omega_{n+m}\leqslant\omega_{n}\omega_{m}
for all
n
and
m
. Consider the power series space

(2.1)

\mathfrak{C}_{\omega}\!:=\Bigl\{a=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}\!:\|a\|_{r,\omega}\!:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\,r^{n}\,\omega_{n}<\infty\;\;\forall r\in(0,\infty)\Bigr\}

and endow it with the topology determined by the family
(\|\cdot\|_{r,\omega};\,r\in(0,\infty))
.
The submultiplicativity of
\omega
implies that
\|a_{1}a_{2}\|_{r,\omega}\leqslant\|a_{1}\|_{r,\omega}\|a_{2}\|_{r,\omega}
for every
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\omega}
. Also, being a Köthe sequence space,
\mathfrak{C}_{\omega}
is complete. Thus it is an Arens–Michael algebra with respect to the multiplication extended from
\mathbb{C}[z]
.

In
[
17
,
18
]
Grabiner considered a power series calculus for a given quasi-nilpotent operator. We need a simple modification, where we take not a single operator but a class of operators or elements of Banach algebras given by a restriction on the growth of powers. We
formulate the existence of calculus as a universal property for
\mathfrak{C}_{\omega}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\omega=(\omega_{n})
be a submultiplicative weight and
b
an element of a Banach algebra
B
. Suppose that
\|b^{n}\|^{1/n}=O(\omega_{n}^{1/n})
as
n\to\infty
. Then there is a unique continuous unital homomorphism
\psi\!:\mathfrak{C}_{\omega}\to B
that maps
z
to
b
.

Proof.

Take
r>0
such that
\|b^{n}\|^{1/n}\leqslant r\,\omega_{n}^{1/n}
for every
n
.
Note that
\psi
is obviously defined on polynomials by the formula
\sum\alpha_{n}z^{n}\mapsto\sum\alpha_{n}b^{n}
.
Also, if
a=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\alpha_{n}z^{n}
, then

\|\psi(a)\|\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{N}|\alpha_{n}|\|b^{n}\|\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{N}|\alpha_{n}|\,r^{n}\,\omega_{n}=\|a\|_{r,\omega}.

Thus
\psi
is continuous and hence extends uniquely to
\mathfrak{C}_{\omega}
.
∎

Put now
\omega_{n}\!:=s^{n^{2}}
, where
s\in(0,1)
. Since
(m+n)^{2}\geqslant m^{2}+n^{2}
and
s<1
, we have that

s^{(m+n)^{2}}\leqslant s^{m^{2}+n^{2}}

for every
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, i.e.,
(\omega_{n})
is submultiplicative. In this case, we use the notations
\mathfrak{B}_{s}
for
\mathfrak{C}_{\omega}
. In detail,

(2.2)

\mathfrak{B}_{s}\!:=\Bigl\{a=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}\!:\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\,r^{n}s^{n^{2}}<\infty\;\;\forall r\in(0,\infty)\Bigr\}.

We immediately obtain the following corollary of Proposition
2.2
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
b
an element of a Banach algebra
B
. Suppose that

\|b^{n}\|^{1/n}=O(s^{n})
as
n\to\infty
. Then there is a unique continuous unital homomorphism

\psi\!:\mathfrak{B}_{s}\to B
that maps
z
to
b
.

The following extension to Arens–Michael algebras is straightforward.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
b
an element of an Arens–Michael algebra
B
. Suppose that

\|b^{n}\|^{1/n}=O(s^{n})
as
n\to\infty
for every
\|\cdot\|
in some system of submultiplicative seminorms that determines the topology on
B
. Then there is a unique continuous unital homomorphism

\psi\!:\mathfrak{B}_{s}\to B
that maps
z
to
b
.

Next we need two families of infinite-dimensional representations. Consider the standard Banach sequence spaces
c_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
and denote by
{\mathrm{B}}(c_{0})
and
{\mathrm{B}}(\ell_{1})
the Banach algebras of bounded operators on
c_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
, respectively. We use the following notations both for
c_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
:

•

E
denotes the operator of left shift;

•

F
denotes the operator of right shift;

•

D
denotes the diagonal operator with the entries
1,q,q^{2},\ldots

It is easy to see that
ED=qDE
and
DF=qFD
and so the formulas

\pi_{\lambda}\!:x\mapsto E,\,y\mapsto\lambda D\quad\text{and}\quad\pi^{\prime}_{\mu}\!:x\mapsto\mu D,\,y\mapsto F\qquad(\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C})

define bounded representations of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
on
c_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
, respectively. It is convenient to consider them as homomorphisms from
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
to
{\mathrm{B}}(c_{0})
and
{\mathrm{B}}(\ell_{1})
. Treating
\lambda
and
\mu
as variables, we also obtain homomorphisms

\widetilde{\pi}\!:\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(c_{0}))\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}\!:\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(\ell_{1}))

to the algebras of operator-valued entire functions.

Since the pairs
(E,\lambda D)
and
(\mu D,F)
satisfy the relation Lemma
2.1
, we have that

\|(\lambda ED)^{n}\|^{1/n}=O(|q|^{n/2})\quad\text{and}\quad\|(\mu DF)^{n}\|^{1/n}=O(|q|^{n/2})\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}\quad(\forall\,\lambda,\mu).

Moreover, the same estimates hold for the systems of standard max-seminorms on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(c_{0}))
and
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(\ell_{1}))
.

It follows from Corollary
2.4
that the homomorphisms
u\mapsto\lambda ED
and
u\mapsto\mu DF
extend from
\mathbb{C}[u]
to
\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}
. On the other hand, the homomorphisms

\mathbb{C}[x]\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(c_{0}))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{C}[y]\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(\ell_{1}))

obviously extend to
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})
. Thus the restrictions of
\widetilde{\pi}
and
\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}
to
\mathbb{C}[x]\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
and
\mathbb{C}[y]\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
, respectively, have extensions to continuous linear maps

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(c_{0}))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(\ell_{1})).

We can identify
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
with
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
and treat
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)
as a subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[t]^{2}
(see (
1.1
)),
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})^{2}
(see (
1.2
)), and
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}
as a subalgebra of
(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}})^{2}
(using the fact that
\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}
is nuclear). So we get a map

(2.3)

\rho\!:\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(c_{0}))\times\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},{\mathrm{B}}(\ell_{1})).

We want to prove that
\rho
is topologically injective. For this we need its corestriction.
Take the first row in the matrix representing elements of
{\mathrm{B}}(c_{0})
and the first column in the matrices representing elements of
{\mathrm{B}}(\ell_{1})
. The row case gives the Banach space dual to
c_{0}
, i.e.,
\ell_{1}
, and the column case also gives
\ell_{1}
.

So we obtain a map

(2.4)

\eta\!:\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathfrak{B}_{|q|^{1/2}}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},\ell_{1})\times\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},\ell_{1})

in which we denote the first and the second multiples by
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
, respectively. Next we describe these maps in detail.

Put
\Phi_{x}\!:\mathbb{C}[x]\to\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\!:x\to X
and
\Phi_{y}\!:\mathbb{C}[y]\to\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\!:y\to Y
; cf. (
1.3
). Then we can write every element of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
as

(2.5)

a=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}(\Phi_{x}(f_{n})+\Phi_{y}(g_{n}))u^{n},

where
f_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[x]
and
g_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[y]
with
f_{n}(0)=g_{n}(0)
.

By a standard result, we can identify
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C},\ell_{1})
with
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\ell_{1}
(see, e.g.,
[
20
, Chapter II, p. 114, Theorem 4.14]
) and moreover with the vector-valued sequence space
\ell_{1}[\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})]
. (In what follows we enumerate vectors of bases in
c_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
by non-negative integers.)
For
\bar{h}=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots)\in\ell_{1}[\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})]
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
put

(W_{n}(\bar{h})(z)\!:=h_{n}(z)z^{n}q^{n(n+1)/2}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{h_{k}^{(n-k)}(0)}{(n-k)!}z^{k}q^{k(k+1)/2}\quad(z\in\mathbb{C}).

(Here Lagrange’s notation for derivatives is used.)

First, we write
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
in terms of operators
W_{n}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For every
a\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
given in the form (
2.5
) the equalities

\displaystyle(\eta_{1}(a))(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(W_{0}(\bar{g}))(\lambda),\ldots,(W_{n}(\bar{g}))(\lambda),\ldots),

\displaystyle(\eta_{2}(a))(\mu)

\displaystyle=(W_{0}(\bar{f}))(\mu),\ldots,(W_{n}(\bar{f}))(\mu),\ldots)^{T}

Proof.

We proceed by induction.
First we have from

By the definition of

\|h_{n}\|\leqslant\frac{3}{2}\,\|h_{n}+h_{n}(0)\|\leqslant\frac{3}{2}\,\rho^{-n}|q|^{-n(n+1)/2}\left(\|W_{n}\|+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{|h_{k}^{(n-k)}(0)|}{(n-k)!}\rho^{k}|q|^{k(k+1)/2}\right).

3.
The envelope with respect to the class of Banach PI algebras

Before turning to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, which describes the structure of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}
, the envelope of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
with respect to the class of Banach PI algebras, we recall some preliminaries about general PI algebras.

It follows from the deep Braun–Kemer–Razmyslov theorem
[
22
, p. 149, Theorem 4.0.1]
that the (Jacobson) radical of a finitely generated PI algebra over
\mathbb{C}
is nilpotent. Of course, not each Banach PI algebra is finitely generated and there exist Banach PI (e.g., commutative) algebras with non-nilpotent radical. Nevertheless, we can apply the mentioned theorem in the quantum plane case using the following well-known result, which is a simple consequence of famous Kaplansky’s theorem; see, e.g.,
[
16
, Theorem 1.11.7]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Every irreducible representation of a PI algebra is finite dimensional.

This proposition gives a restriction on the structure of PI quotients.

Lemma 3.2
.

Every PI quotient of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
is commutative modulo radical.

Proof.

A complete list of primitive ideals of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
is given in
[
11
, p. 136, Example II.1.2 and p. 194, Example II.7.2]
. The corresponding quotients are one dimensional or infinite dimensional. Therefore by Proposition
3.1
, a PI quotient may have only one-dimensional irreducible representations. Thus every PI quotient of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
is commutative modulo radical.
∎

The following proposition is an analogue of Lemma
2.1
but we do not even assume that
X
and
Y
are elements of a Banach algebra.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be elements of a PI algebra such that
XY=qYX
for some

. Then
XY
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
A
be the subalgebra generated by
X
and
Y
. Obviously,
A
also satisfies a PI. Being a quotient of
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
, the algebra
A
is commutative modulo radical by Lemma
3.2
. Since
XY=(1-q^{-1})^{-1}[X,Y]
, we have that
XY
belongs to the commutant and hence the radical. As mentioned above, the Braun–Kemer–Razmyslov theorem
[
22
, p. 149, Theorem 4.0.1]
implies that the radical of a finitely generated PI algebra over
\mathbb{C}
is nilpotent. In particular,
XY
is nilpotent.
∎

First note that

Remark 3.7
.

Suppose as usual that
xy=qyx
, where
|q|<1
. In
[

\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\!:=\Bigl\{a=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{kl}y^{k}x^{l}\!:\|a\|^{\prime}_{r,l}<\infty\;\;\forall r>0,\,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\quad\|a\|^{\prime\prime}_{r,k}<\infty\;\;\forall r>0,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\Bigr\},

We claim that the linear isomorphism
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\to\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
defined in (
1.3
) extends to a topological isomorphism
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}

\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
and
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)\otimes\mathbb{C}[u]
.

Every
a
in
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})
can be written in the following form:

a=\sum_{j}\Bigl(\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\beta_{ij}u^{j}x^{i}+\sum_{i>0}\gamma_{ij}y^{i}u^{j}\Bigr).

Put also
\gamma_{0j}=\beta_{0j}
. Theorem
1.2
implies that
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{q})^{\mathrm{PI}}\cong\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}[[u]]
. It is easy to see that the
topology on the latter space can be determined by the following family of norms:

|a|^{\prime}_{r,k}=\sum_{i}|\beta_{ik}|r^{i},\quad|a|^{\prime\prime}_{r,l}=\sum|\gamma_{il}|r^{i}\quad(r>0,\,k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}).

Since

, we have that

a=\sum\beta_{ij}\,q^{j(j+1)/2}y^{j}x^{i+j}+\sum\gamma_{ij}\,q^{j(j+1)/2}y^{i+j}x^{i}.

Hence,

\|a\|^{\prime}_{r,l}=\sum_{k\geqslant l}|\beta_{k-l,l}\,q^{l(l+1)/2}|\,r^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{l}|\gamma_{k,l-k}\,q^{(l-k)(l-k+1)/2}|\,r^{k}

and

\|a\|^{\prime\prime}_{r,k}=\sum_{l\geqslant k}|\beta_{l-k,k}\,q^{k(k+1)/2}|\,r^{l}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}|\gamma_{l,k-l}\,q^{(k-l)(k-l+1)/2}|\,r^{l}.

These formulas obviously imply that

and

(since

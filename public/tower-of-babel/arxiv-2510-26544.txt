One-pion exchange potential in a strong magnetic field
Daiki Miura, Masaru Hongo, Hidetoshi Taya, Tetsuo Hatsuda

Abstract
We derive the one-pion exchange potential (OPEP) in the presence of a homogeneous magnetic field using chiral perturbation theory with nonrelativistic nucleons. Our approach is applicable not only to weak magnetic fields but also to strong ones up to around the pion-mass scale. The Green’s function of charged pions is modified by the magnetic field, leading to changes in the nuclear force. By numerically evaluating the modified OPEP incorporating its spin and isospin dependencies, we show that the range of the potential decreases in both directions parallel and perpendicular to the magnetic field as the field strength increases. We also compute the resulting energy shift of the deuteron due to the modified OPEP, which can reach the order of 1 MeV around | e ​ B | = m π 2 |eB|=m_{\pi}^{2} , which is comparable to the deuteron binding energy.

Most previous studies have focused on single-particle properties of hadrons (such as mass spectra) or on mean-field analyses of QCD matter in background magnetic fields.
Going beyond such effective one-body treatments, it is natural to ask how hadron–hadron interactions are modified in strong magnetic fields.

Recent theoretical efforts have begun to explore how external magnetic fields modify the nuclear force.
In particular, weak-field
\mathcal{O}((eB)^{2})
corrections to the Yukawa potential for the one pion exchange between two constituent quarks have been evaluated
[
30
]
.
It shows predominantly isotropic effects with small anisotropies, though the hadronic-level OPEP with its full spin–isospin operator structure remains unexplored.
Within

In this paper, we derive OPEP in the presence of a strong, homogeneous magnetic field.
Our analysis is based on the leading-order
\chi

We introduce a notion of a gauge-invariant OPEP, expressed in terms of pion Green’s functions in a magnetic field.
We then project the resulting potential onto definite spin and isospin channels, such as
T=0,S=1
and
T=1,S=0

to analyze its channel-dependent behavior under the magnetic field.
In addition, we discuss its implications for the deuteron, the only two-nucleon bound state in vacuum.
We find that the range of the nuclear force mediated by charged-pion exchange decreases in both directions parallel and perpendicular to the magnetic field.
We also estimate the energy shift of deuteron using the first-order perturbation theory.

Our analysis relies on
\chi

Based on the nonlinear realization of chiral symmetry, the leading-order chiral Lagrangian in the presence of a background electromagnetic field is given by (see, e.g., Ref.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}}{4}\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left(D_{\mu}UD_{\nu}U^{{\dagger}}\right)+\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}\mathcal{B}}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left(MU^{\dagger}+UM^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle+{\bar{\mathcal{N}}}\left[\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}-m_{N}+\frac{1}{2}g_{A}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\mu\perp}\right]\mathcal{N},

(1)

We parametrize the matrix-valued field

Throughout the paper, we adopt the mostly-minus convention for the Minkowski metric

Assuming the approximate

M=\operatorname{diag}(m,m)
, where

is the current quark mass for the up and down quarks.
The leading-order pion Lagrangian involves two low-energy coefficients,

We also introduce covariant derivatives as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}U

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\mu}U+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}eA_{\mu}(\tau_{3}U-U\tau_{3}),

(2a)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{N}+\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\mu\parallel}\mathcal{N}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}eA_{\mu}\mathcal{N},

These nucleons interact with pions via the gauged Maurer-Cartan one-form,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\mu\parallel}&:=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}}\left[\xi\partial_{\mu}\xi^{-1}+\xi^{-1}\partial_{\mu}\xi+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}eA_{\mu}(\xi\tau_{3}\xi^{-1}+\xi^{-1}\tau_{3}\xi)\right],\\
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\mu\perp}&:=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}}\left[\xi\partial_{\mu}\xi^{-1}-\xi^{-1}\partial_{\mu}\xi+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}eA_{\mu}(\xi\tau_{3}\xi^{-1}-\xi^{-1}\tau_{3}\xi)\right],\end{split}

To compute OPEP, we simplify the chiral Lagrangian (

As a result, we obtain the following leading-order effective Lagrangian for pions and nonrelativistic (two-component) nucleon field

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}

\displaystyle=\eta^{\mu\nu}D^{+}_{\mu}\pi^{+}D^{-}_{\nu}\pi^{-}-m_{\pi}^{2}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\pi^{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{\pi}^{2}(\pi^{0})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 120.0pt+N^{\dagger}\mathrm{i}D_{0}N-\frac{g_{A}}{2f_{\pi}}\sum_{a=0,\pm}D_{i}^{a}\pi^{a}N^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\tau_{a}N,

(4)

m_{\pi}:=2m\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle D_{\mu}N:=\partial_{\mu}N+\mathrm{i}eA_{\mu}\frac{\tau_{0}+I_{2\times 2}}{2}N.

(5)

Note that the covariant derivative contains the gauge field
A_{\mu}

We use an isospin basis defined by

\displaystyle\tau_{+}:=\frac{\tau_{1}+\mathrm{i}\tau_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{2}\\

3
One-pion exchange potential in
magnetic field

In this section, we derive the one-pion exchange potential (OPEP) in the presence of a magnetic field.
In Sec.
3.1
, we solve the equations of motion for the pion fields and obtain the corresponding effective interaction Hamiltonian between two nucleons.
In Sec.
3.2
, we introduce a gauge-invariant definition of OPEP.
Finally, in Sec.
3.3
, we present simplified analytic expressions of the OPEP in weak- and strong-field limits.

3.1
Deriving two-nucleon interaction Hamiltonian

We derive OPEP by solving the equation of motion for the pion fields in a static setup where the source term is supplied by a test nucleon at rest.
By substituting the resulting static pion solution into the interaction Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{int}}=\frac{g_{A}}{2f_{\pi}}\sum_{a=0,\pm}\int

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{3}{x}D_{i}^{a}\pi^{a}N^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\tau_{a}N,

(8)

we obtain the effective interaction Hamiltonian between two nucleons.
The resulting effective Hamiltonian allows us to determine the induced potential between the two nucleons.

Let us solve the equation of motion for a charged-pion field in a homogeneous static magnetic field directed along the
z
-axis:
e\bm{B}=eB\hat{\bm{z}}=(0,0,eB)
.
From the leading-order effective Lagrangian (
4
), we obtain the classical equation of motion for the pion field as

\displaystyle\left[\eta^{\mu\nu}D_{\mu}^{a}D_{\nu}^{a}+m_{\pi}^{2}\right]\pi^{a}=\frac{g_{A}}{2f_{\pi}}D_{i}^{a}\bigl[N^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\tau_{-a}N\bigr],

(9)

where the unsummed indice
a=0,\pm
denotes the electric charge of the pion field, and
\tau_{-a}
is the corresponding isospin Pauli matrix, as defined in Eq. (
6
).
In the following analysis, we focus on the static case and neglect the temporal derivatives of the pion field.
Introducing a coordinate-space Green’s function for pions in the presence of an external gauge field,
G^{a}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)
, such that

\displaystyle\bigl[-\delta^{ij}D_{i}^{a}D_{j}^{a}+m_{\pi}^{2}\bigr]G^{a}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)=\delta^{(3)}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}),

(10)

we find that the induced pion field, sourced by the test nucleon, is given by

\displaystyle\pi^{a}({\bm{x}})=-\frac{g_{A}}{2f_{\pi}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}{x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\Bigl[\bigl(D_{i}^{-a}\bigr)_{{x^{\prime}}}G^{a}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)\Bigr]N^{\dagger}({\bm{x}^{\prime}})\sigma^{i}\tau_{-a}N({\bm{x}^{\prime}}).

(11)

Here the subscript
{{x^{\prime}}}
of
\bigl(D_{i}^{-a}\bigr)_{{x^{\prime}}}
indicates that the covariant derivative acts on the coordinate
{\bm{x}^{\prime}}
.
We can express the pion Green’s function
G^{a}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)
in a more explicit form.
For the neutral pion (
a=0
), it does not couple to an electromagnetic field, so its Green’s function retains the familiar Yukawa form:

\displaystyle G^{0}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\pi}|{\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}|}}{|{\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}|}.

(12)

In contrast, the charged-pion Green’s function (
a=\pm
) is significantly modified by the magnetic field when
eB/m_{\pi}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.
To incorporate the magnetic-field effects without relying on weak-field expansion, we employ Schwinger’s proper-time method
[
36
]
, which allows for an all-order treatment of the applied background field (see, e.g., Ref.
[
18
]
for a recent review).
The explicit expression for
G^{a}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)
in an arbitrary gauge is given by (see Appendix
A
for details):

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{\pm}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)&=e^{\pm\mathrm{i}\,{\Phi_{A}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})}}}\;G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x^{\prime}}}),\\
G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})&=\frac{|eB|}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\frac{1}{\sinh(|eB|s)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{s}}\exp\left(-m_{\pi}^{2}s-\frac{1}{4s}(z-z^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{|eB|}{4}\frac{|{\bm{x}_{\perp}-{\bm{x}^{\prime}_{\perp}}}|^{2}}{\tanh(|eB|s)}\right),\\
\Phi_{A}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}})&=-\,e{\int_{\bm{x}^{\prime}}^{\bm{x}}}\mathrm{d}\bm{{\xi}}\cdot\Bigl[\bm{A}(\bm{{\xi}})+\tfrac{1}{2}(\bm{{\xi}}-{\bm{x}^{\prime}})\times\bm{B}\Bigr]\,,\end{split}

(13)

where
z
and
z^{\prime}
denote the coordinates along the direction of the magnetic field, and
\bm{x}_{\perp}
and
\bm{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}
denote the transverse components.
We also introduced the Green’s function
G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})
,
which is evaluated in the Fock–Schwinger symmetric (FS) gauge.
The corresponding gauge potential
A_{i}^{\text{FS}}
in the FS gauge is defined by
({x^{i}-x^{\prime\,i}})A_{i}^{\text{FS}}=0
,
leading to
A_{i}^{\text{FS}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}F_{ij}({x^{j}-x^{\prime\,j}})
.
For a homogeneous magnetic field
\bm{B}=B\hat{\bm{z}}
, this reduces to

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\text{FS}}({\bm{x}})=-\frac{1}{2}\,({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})\times\bm{B}=\left(-\frac{B}{2}(y-y^{\prime}),\,\frac{B}{2}(x-x^{\prime}),\,0\right).

(14)

The phase factor
\Phi_{A}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}})
is the Schwinger phase, which governs the gauge-transformation properties of the Green’s function:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}G^{+}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)\to\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}e{(\alpha(\bm{x})-\alpha(\bm{x}^{\prime}))}}G^{+}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A^{\prime}),\\
G^{-}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)\to\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}e{(\alpha(\bm{x})-\alpha(\bm{x}^{\prime}))}}G^{-}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A^{\prime}),\end{cases}

(15)

where the gauge field transforms as
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}^{\prime}=A_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}\alpha
.

Finally, we introduce the gauge-invariant two-nucleon interaction,
H_{\text{int}}
, describing the one-pion-exchange-mediated force. It follows from substituting the induced pion field Eq. (
11
) into the interaction term in Eq. (
4
), yielding

\displaystyle H_{\text{int}}=-\frac{g_{A}^{2}}{4f_{\pi}^{2}}\sum_{a=0,\pm}\int

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{3}{x}\mathrm{d}^{3}{x^{\prime}}N^{\dagger}({\bm{x}^{\prime}})\sigma^{j}\tau_{-a}N({\bm{x}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\times\Bigl[(D_{i}^{a})_{x}(D_{j}^{-a})_{{x^{\prime}}}G^{a}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)\Bigr]N^{\dagger}({\bm{x}})\sigma^{i}\tau_{a}N({\bm{x}})\,.

(16)

3.2
Definition of gauge-invariant potential

The Hamiltonian (
16
) encodes all the information about the interaction between two nucleons induced by the one-pion exchange.
To define the OPEP, there are two possible approaches:
(i) fixing a specific gauge [e.g., the Fock–Schwinger (FS) gauge] and
(ii) constructing a gauge-invariant OPEP by dressing the nucleon field with a spatial Wilson line.
The second approach was adopted in Ref.
[
31
]
.
Although physical observables derived from the potential, such as binding energies, are gauge invariant, it is conceptually advantageous to formulate the potential itself in a manifestly gauge-invariant way.

Let us describe the approach (ii) and its connection to the approach (i) in more detail.
We choose a reference position
\bm{x}_{0}
and introduce a “dressed” nucleon field
\widetilde{N}(\bm{x})
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}(\bm{x}):=W(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{0})N(\bm{x}),

(17)

where the spatial Wilson line
W(\bm{r},\bm{r}_{0})
is defined as

\displaystyle W(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{0}):=\exp\left(\mathrm{i}e\frac{\tau_{0}+I_{2\times 2}}{2}\int_{\bm{x}_{0}}^{\bm{x}}\bm{A}(\bm{\xi})\cdot\mathrm{d}\bm{\xi}\right).

(18)

Due to the dressing, a nucleon state is now produced by
\tilde{N}
, not by
N
, as

\displaystyle|{\tilde{N}(\bm{x})}\rangle:=\widetilde{N}^{\dagger}(\bm{x})|{0}\rangle,

(19)

and multi-nucleon states are produced similarly by acting
\tilde{N}
multiple times.
Since the Wilson line transforms under a gauge transformation as

\displaystyle W(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{0})\to\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}e\frac{\tau_{0}+I_{2\times 2}}{2}[\alpha(\bm{x})-\alpha(\bm{x}_{0})]}W(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{0}),

(20)

a nucleon state
|{\tilde{N}}\rangle
transforms as

\displaystyle|{\tilde{N}(\bm{x})}\rangle\to\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}e\frac{\tau_{0}+I_{2\times 2}}{2}\alpha(\bm{x}_{0})}|{\tilde{N}(\bm{x})}\rangle,

(21)

i.e., a proton or nucleon state transforms with a single phase factor at the reference point
\bm{x}_{0}
as
|{\tilde{p}(\bm{x})}\rangle\to\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}e\alpha(\bm{x}_{0})}|{\tilde{p}(\bm{x})}\rangle
and
|{\tilde{n}(\bm{x})}\rangle\to|{\tilde{n}(\bm{x})}\rangle
. Accordingly, for any set of initial and final states with the dressed nucleons, which we write
|{\tilde{i}}\rangle
and
\langle{\tilde{f}}|
, respectively, the phase factors that they acquire after a gauge transformation are, respectively,
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}en\alpha(\bm{x}_{0})}
and
\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}en^{\prime}\alpha(\bm{x}_{0})}
, where
n
and
n^{\prime}
are the numbers of protons contained in these states.
Since the total electric charge is conserved, we have
n=n^{\prime}
and understand that the phase factors coming from
|{\tilde{i}}\rangle
and
\langle{\tilde{f}}|
cancel with each other, making the matrix element
\langle{\tilde{f}}|H_{\text{int}}|{\tilde{i}}\rangle
gauge invariant.

We are in a position to derive a gauge-invariant OPEP from the Hamiltonian (
16
).
To do so, we first rewrite
H_{\text{int}}
(
16
) with the dressed nucleon field, which yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{\text{int}}&=-\frac{g_{A}^{2}}{4f_{\pi}^{2}}\sum_{a=0,\pm}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}{x}\,\mathrm{d}^{3}{x}^{\prime}\widetilde{N}^{\dagger}({\bm{x}^{\prime}})\sigma^{j}\tau_{-a}\widetilde{N}({\bm{x}^{\prime}})\\
&\hskip 60.0pt\times\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}ae\int_{{\bm{x}^{\prime}}}^{{\bm{x}}}\bm{A}(\bm{\xi})\cdot\mathrm{d}\bm{\xi}}\Bigl[(D_{i}^{a})_{x}(D_{j}^{-a})_{{x^{\prime}}}G^{a}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)\Bigr]\widetilde{N}^{\dagger}({\bm{x}})\sigma^{i}\tau_{a}\widetilde{N}({\bm{x}}),\end{split}

(22)

where the Wilson-line factor
\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}ae\int_{\bm{x}^{\prime}}^{\bm{x}}\bm{A}(\bm{\xi})\cdot\mathrm{d}\bm{\xi}}
arises due to the multiplication of
W
and
W^{\dagger}
’s from the dressed nucleon fields.
It is worth noting that the additional Wilson-line factor makes the kernel in the Hamiltonian to be the one simply evaluated in the Fock-Schwinger gauge:

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}ae\int_{\bm{x}^{\prime}}^{\bm{x}}\bm{A}(\bm{\xi})\cdot\mathrm{d}\bm{\xi}}\Bigl[(D_{i}^{a})_{x}(D_{j}^{-a})_{x^{\prime}}G^{a}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}|A)\Bigl]=(D_{i}^{a})_{x}^{\text{FS}}(D_{j}^{-a})_{x^{\prime}}^{\text{FS}}G_{B}(\bm{x}-\bm{x^{\prime}}),

(23)

where the covariant derivatives with a superscript
``\text{FS}"
are

\displaystyle\begin{split}(D_{i}^{a})^{{\text{FS}}}=\partial_{i}-\text{i}ae\frac{1}{2}\,[\,(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime})\times\bm{B}\,]_{i}.\end{split}

(24)

We then define OPEP for all possible one-pion-exchange channels via the corresponding matrix elements as

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{0}(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2})\langle{\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{3})\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{4})|\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{1})\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{2})}\rangle&:=\frac{1}{2}\langle{\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{3})\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{4})}|H_{\text{int}}|{\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{1})\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{2})}\rangle\\
&\hskip 3.0pt=\frac{1}{2}\langle{\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{3})\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{4})}|H_{\text{int}}|{\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{1})\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{2})}\rangle,\\
V_{pn}(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2})\langle{\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{3})\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{4})|\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{1})\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{2})}\rangle&:=\frac{1}{4}\langle{\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{3})\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{4})}|H_{\text{int}}|{\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{1})\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{2})}\rangle,\\
V_{np}(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2})\langle{\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{3})\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{4})|\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{1})\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{2})}\rangle&:=\frac{1}{4}\langle{\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{3})\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{4})}|H_{\text{int}}|{\tilde{n}(\bm{r}_{1})\tilde{p}(\bm{r}_{2})}\rangle.\end{split}

(25)

The factor
1/2
in
V_{0}
avoids double counting of equivalent contributions that arise when defining the potential as a matrix element of
H_{\text{int}}
.
Similarly, the factors
1/4
in
V_{pn}
and
V_{np}
consist of two
1/2
factors:
one accounts for double counting of equivalent charged-pion exchange processes,
and the second factor
1/2
compensates for the additional factor of 2 that arises when

N^{\dagger}\tau_{\pm}N
is rewritten in terms of the proton and neutron fields
(e.g.,
N^{\dagger}\tau_{-}N=\sqrt{2}\,n^{\dagger}p
), ensuring consistency with
V_{0}
.
It is easy to show via direct calculations

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{0}(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2})&:=-\frac{g_{A}^{2}}{4f_{\pi}^{2}}\sigma^{i}_{(1)}\sigma^{j}_{(2)}(\partial_{i})_{x}(\partial_{j})_{{x^{\prime}}}G^{0}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})\big|_{{\bm{x}=\bm{r}_{1},\bm{x}^{\prime}=\bm{r}_{2}}},\\
V_{pn}(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2})&:=-\frac{g_{A}^{2}}{8f_{\pi}^{2}}\Bigl[\sigma^{i}_{(1)}\sigma^{j}_{(2)}\bigl(D_{i}^{-}\bigr)^{\text{FS}}_{x}\bigl(D_{j}^{+}\bigr)^{\text{FS}}_{{x^{\prime}}}G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})\big|_{{\bm{x}=\bm{r}_{1},\bm{x}^{\prime}=\bm{r}_{2}}}\\
&\hskip 80.0pt+\sigma^{j}_{(1)}\sigma^{i}_{(2)}\bigl(D_{i}^{+}\bigr)^{\text{FS}}_{x}\bigl(D_{j}^{-}\bigr)^{\text{FS}}_{{x^{\prime}}}G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})\big|_{{\bm{x}=\bm{r}_{2},\bm{x}^{\prime}=\bm{r}_{1}}}\Bigr],\\
V_{np}(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2})&:=V^{\dagger}_{pn}(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2}).\end{split}

(26)

Physically,
V_{pn}
(
V_{np}
) accounts for the contribution to the
pn
(
np
) channel, i.e., a charged-pion-exchange process in which a proton at a certain position emits one
\pi^{+}
(absorbs one
\pi^{-}
) and a neutron absorbs (or emits) it.
The other one,
V_{0}
, is responsible for the
pp
and
nn
channels, which are mediated by a neutral pion.
The obtained OPEP is clearly modified in the
pn
and
np
channels, while it remains unchanged in the
pp
and
nn
channels.
This is the result of the breaking of the isospin and spin symmetries by a magnetic field.

For later calculations, it is convenient to introduce
V^{B\neq 0}_{\text{OPEP}}
an effective OPEP that incorporates all the information from each channel’s OPEP in Eq. (
26
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}V^{B\neq 0}_{\text{{OPEP}}}(\bm{r})&:=V_{pn}({\bm{r}})\,\tau^{(1)}_{-}\tau^{(2)}_{+}+V_{np}({\bm{r}})\,\tau_{+}^{(1)}\tau_{-}^{(2)}+V_{0}({\bm{r}})\,\tau^{(1)}_{0}\tau^{(2)}_{0},\end{split}

(27)

where we introduced the relative coordinate
\bm{r}=\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2}
.
The operators
\sigma^{i}_{(1)}
and
\sigma^{i}_{(2)}
[
\tau_{\pm}^{(1)}
and
\tau_{\pm}^{(2)}
] act on the spin (isospin) spaces of the nucleons located at
\bm{r}_{1}
and
\bm{r}_{2}
, respectively.

3.3
Weak and strong magnetic-field limits

Here, we consider two limiting cases of the OPEP in a magnetic field, where simple analytic expressions are available: the weak-field limit (
|eB|\ll m_{\pi}^{2}
) and the strong-field limit (
|eB|\gg m_{\pi}^{2}
).
3
3

3

Our OPEP in a magnetic field (
27
) is applicable to the strong-field region such that
eB\gtrsim m_{\pi}^{2}
, so long as the validity condition
eB\lesssim\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{2}\sim 30m_{\pi}^{2}
is satisfied [see discussion below Eq. (
7
)].

Weak magnetic-field limit

When the applied magnetic field is sufficiently weak compared to the pion-mass scale in vacuum, we can perform a perturbative expansion with respect to the small parameter
|eB|/m_{\pi}^{2}\ll 1
.
Expanding the full charged-pion propagator (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})&=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{|{\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}|}\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\pi}|{\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}|}-\frac{|eB|^{2}}{96\pi m_{\pi}^{3}}(1+m_{\pi}|{\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}|+m_{\pi}^{2}|{\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}}|^{2})\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\pi}|{\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}|}\\
&\hskip 300.0pt+\mathcal{O}\left(|eB|^{4}\right).\end{split}

(28)

Using this weak-field expression up to
\mathcal{O}(|eB|^{2})
, we find that the weak magnetic-field dependence of the OPEPs,
V_{pn}
and
V_{np}
, is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\lim_{eB\ll m_{\pi}^{2}}}V_{pn}(\bm{r})&={\lim_{eB\ll m_{\pi}^{2}}}\left[V_{np}(\bm{r})\right]^{\dagger}\\
&=\frac{g_{A}^{2}}{16\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\frac{m_{\pi}}{r}+\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}}{3}\right)S_{12}+\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}}{3}\bm{\sigma}_{(1)}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{(2)}\right]\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\pi}r}}{r}\\
&\hskip 10.0pt+\frac{g_{A}^{2}|eB|^{2}}{384\pi m_{\pi}^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}\Bigg[\left(1+m_{\pi}r-m_{\pi}^{2}\bm{r}_{\perp}^{2}\right)\bm{\sigma}_{(1)}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{(2)}\\
&\hskip 70.0pt-\left(1+m_{\pi}r-m_{\pi}^{2}\bm{r}_{\perp}^{2}+3m_{\pi}^{2}r^{2}-m_{\pi}^{3}r\bm{r}_{\perp}^{2}\right)\frac{(\bm{\sigma}_{(1)}\cdot\bm{r})(\bm{\sigma}_{(2)}\cdot\bm{r})}{r^{2}}\\
&\hskip 80.0pt+m_{\pi}^{2}\left(\sigma^{3}_{(1)}z(\bm{\sigma}_{(2)}\cdot\bm{r})+(\bm{\sigma}_{(1)}\cdot\bm{r})\sigma^{3}_{(2)}z\right)+2m_{\pi}r\sigma^{3}_{(1)}\sigma^{3}_{(2)}\Bigg]\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\pi}r}}{r}\\
&\hskip 10.0pt-\frac{g_{A}^{2}|eB|^{2}}{64\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{(1)}\times\bm{r}_{\perp}\right)_{z}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{(2)}\times\bm{r}_{\perp}\right)_{z}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\pi}r}}{r},\end{split}

(29)

where
\bm{r}=:(\bm{r}_{\perp},z)
and
r=|\bm{r}|
.
We note that the first line of Eq. (
29
) exactly conincides with
V_{0}
, after evaluating the derivatives in Eq. (
26
), with the tensor operator,

\displaystyle S_{12}:=\frac{3}{r^{2}}(\bm{\sigma}_{(1)}\cdot\bm{r})(\bm{\sigma}_{(2)}\cdot\bm{r})-\bm{\sigma}_{(1)}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{(2)}.

(30)

As usual, the tensor operator couples the nucleon spins to their relative coordinates and results in anisotropy of OPEP for the spin-triplet (
S=1
) channel, even in the absence of magnetic fields.
The numerical prefactor for the
{\mathcal{O}}(|eB|^{2})
term turns out to be rather small, implying that the zero-field limit dominates even around
|eB|\sim m_{\pi}^{2}
.

Strong magnetic-field limit

When the applied magnetic field is sufficiently strong compared with the pion mass in vacuum (
|eB|\gg m_{\pi}^{2}
), the charged pions undergo the Landau quantization, and the lowest energy mode, or the lowest Landau level (LLL), dominates.
In this regime, the charged-pion Green’s function can be approximated well by the contribution from the LLL [i.e., the
n=0
mode in Eq. (
47
)] as

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})\simeq\frac{|eB|}{4\pi}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|eB|}{4}|{\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}}|^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+|eB|}|z-z^{\prime}|}}{\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+|eB|}}.\end{split}

(31)

With this, the magnetic-field dependence in the strong-field limit can be computed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\lim_{eB\gg m_{\pi}^{2}}}V_{pn}(\bm{r})&={\lim_{eB\gg m_{\pi}^{2}}}\left[V_{np}(\bm{r})\right]^{\dagger}\\
&=-\frac{g_{A}^{2}|eB|}{16\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{|eB|}{2\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+|eB|}}\bm{\sigma}^{(1)}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\sigma}^{(2)}_{\perp}-\frac{|eB|^{2}}{4\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+|eB|}}(\bm{\sigma}^{(1)}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp})(\bm{\sigma}^{(2)}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp})\\
&\hskip 60.0pt-\frac{|eB|}{2}\left(\sigma^{3}_{(1)}(\bm{\sigma}^{(2)}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp})+(\bm{\sigma}^{(1)}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp})\sigma^{3}_{(2)}\right)\frac{z}{|z|}-\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+|eB|}\sigma^{3}_{(1)}\sigma^{3}_{(2)}\\
&\hskip 70.0pt+\frac{|eB|^{2}}{4\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+|eB|}}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{(1)}\times\bm{r}_{\perp}\right)_{z}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{(2)}\times\bm{r}_{\perp}\right)_{z}\Bigg]\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|eB|}{4}\bm{r}_{\perp}^{2}-\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+|eB|}|z|}.\end{split}

(32)

5
Energy shift of deuteron

In this section, we apply the derived OPEP to examine the impact of a magnetic field on the deuteron, which is the only two-nucleon bound state in the isospin-singlet (
T=0
) and spin-triplet (
S=1
).

We evaluate the energy shift of the deuteron in the presence of a magnetic field based on the derived OPEP, using the first-order perturbation theory.
Here, we focus solely on the modification of the nuclear force arising from the OPEP.
This assumption can be formally justified, since other magnetic-field-induced effects are further suppressed by a factor of
\mathcal{O}(|eB|/\Lambda_{\mathrm{UV}}^{2})
, where
\Lambda_{\mathrm{UV}}
is the ultraviolet cutoff of
\chi
EFT (see also footnote
2
).
Let
H_{\text{kin}}
and
V_{\text{Heavy}}
denote the kinetic term for the two-nucleon system and the potentials other than the OPEP, respectively, where
V_{\text{Heavy}}
represents the short-range part of the nuclear force arising from heavier meson exchanges such as the
\rho
and
\omega
mesons.
In the absence of a magnetic field, the OPEP contains a tensor operator that mixes the
{}^{3}S_{1}
and
{}^{3}D_{1}
partial waves of the two-nucleon state.
As a consequence, the orbital angular momentum
L
is not conserved, and the spin projection
S_{z}
is mixed, while the total spin remains fixed at
S=1
.
The total angular momentum
J=L+S
, together with its projection
M=L_{z}+S_{z}
, thus serves as an appropriate quantum number to classify the deuteron states.
The eigenvalue problem for the deuteron can then be written as

\displaystyle(H_{\text{kin}}+V_{\text{Heavy}}+V_{\text{{OPEP}}}^{B=0})|{d_{M}}\rangle=E^{B=0}\,|{d_{M}}\rangle,

(37)

where
E^{B=0}\simeq-2.2\,\text{MeV}
denotes the deuteron binding energy, and
M=0,\pm 1
labels the threefold degenerate eigenstates.

Now, we turn on a magnetic field.
For simplicity, we consider only the modification to the OPEP below.
In the one-boson-exchange picture, heavier charged mesons such as the
\rho^{\pm}
also contribute to the nuclear force.
Since their magnetic-field dependence becomes relevant only at much higher field strengths, we restrict our analysis to the regime
|eB|\lesssim m_{\pi}^{2}
, where such effects may safely be neglected.
Accordingly, the corresponding short-range interaction
V_{\text{Heavy}}
is treated as unaffected by the magnetic field.
We also neglect the direct coupling of the magnetic field to the nucleons themselves (e.g., the magnetic moments of the proton and neutron and the associated Zeeman splitting of the deuteron).
This means that possible spin polarization induced by the magnetic field is ignored.
These approximations justify focusing exclusively on the magnetic modification of the long-range part of the nuclear force—the OPEP. We note that the magnetic field couples not only to the charged-pion propagator but also to the
NN\pi
coupling through the covariant derivatives in Eq. (4).
Hence, the present
V_{\mathrm{{OPEP}}}^{B\neq 0}
defined in Eq. (25) already contains the magnetic contribution to the vertex via the minimal coupling.

The problem is then reduced to solving an eigenvalue equation,

\displaystyle(H_{\text{kin}}+V_{\text{Heavy}}+V_{\text{{OPEP}}}^{B\neq 0})|{\widetilde{d}_{M}^{B\neq 0}}\rangle=E_{M}^{B\neq 0}|{\widetilde{d}_{M}^{B\neq 0}}\rangle,

(38)

where
E_{M}^{B\neq 0}
with
M=0,\pm 1
are the energy eigenvalues and
|{\widetilde{d}_{M}^{B\neq 0}}\rangle
are the corresponding deuteron eigenstates in a magnetic field.

We then treat
V_{\text{{OPEP}}}^{B\neq 0}-V_{\text{{OPEP}}}^{B=0}
as a perturbation onto the unperturbed deuteron state without a magnetic field
|{d_{M}}\rangle
.
Using the degenerate perturbation theory, the first-order energy shift
\Delta E_{M}^{B\neq 0}
is determined by the eigenvalue equation,

\displaystyle A\bm{a}

\displaystyle=\Delta E_{M}^{B\neq 0}\,\bm{a},

(39)

where
\bm{a}
is the coefficient vector that determines the linear combination of the unperturbed states, and the matrix
A=(A_{MM^{\prime}})
is defined as

\displaystyle A_{MM^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\langle{d_{M}}|V_{\text{{OPEP}}}^{B\neq 0}-V_{\text{{OPEP}}}^{B=0}|{d_{M^{\prime}}}\rangle.

(40)

For the unperturbed deuteron state, we use the known one based on the AV18 potential
[
37
]
.
4
4

4

We note that even if the unperturbed eigenstates in a magnetic field are defined as the dressed ones [i.e., with the Wilson lines (
17
)], the matrix elements
A_{MM^{\prime}}
can still be evaluated using the undressed states, because the Wilson lines from the bra and ket cancel each other, i.e.,

\langle{\tilde{d}_{M}}|\big(V_{\text{OPEP}}^{B\neq 0}-V_{\text{OPEP}}^{B=0}\big)|{\tilde{d}_{M^{\prime}}}\rangle=\langle{d_{M}}|\big(V_{\text{OPEP}}^{B\neq 0}-V_{\text{OPEP}}^{B=0}\big)|{d_{M^{\prime}}}\rangle.

We numerically solve the eigenvalue equation (
39
) and plot the resulting energy shift in Fig.
8
.
We observe that the magnetic-field-modified OPEP lifts the degeneracy between the
M=0
and
M=\pm 1
states, while the degeneracy between
M=+1
and
M=-1
states remains intact.
The energy shifts for the
M\pm 1
states are negative, indicating enhanced binding (i.e., increased stability), whereas the
M=0
state receives a positive energy shift, implying reduced stability in a magnetic field.
We note that when the field becomes strong
|eB|\sim m_{\pi}^{2}
, the magnitude of the energy shift reaches
|\Delta E^{B\neq 0}_{M}|\sim 0.5\,
MeV, which is non-negligibly large compared to the binding energy in the absence of magnetic fields
E^{B=0}\simeq-2.2\,
MeV.

6
Summary and discussion

In this paper, we have studied the modification to the one-pion exchange potential (OPEP) in a magnetic field.
Using chiral perturbation theory, we have derived the leading-order effective Lagrangian for pions and nonrelativistic nucleons, incorporating the magnetic-field effects on charged-pion propagation, as well as on the

NN\pi
vertex through the gauge potential in the covariant derivatives.
We have obtained the OPEP, which is made gauge invariant by redefining nucleon states by attaching a Wilson line.
The resulting OPEP involves a proper-time integration, which can also be expressed as a summation over the Landau levels.
We have evaluated the OPEP matrix elements for various channels such as the isospin-singlet and spin-triplet (
T=0,S=1
) and the isospin-triplet and spin-singlet (
T=1,S=0
) channels.
As a result, we have found that, in both channels, the range of the OPEP decreases as the magnetic-field strength increases.
The potential exhibits anisotropy in both channels, although its origin differs between them.
In the
T=0,S=1
channel, as indicated by the weak-field limit (
29
), the anisotropy is dominated by the contribution from the tensor operator
S_{12}
, while the magnetic-field-induced anisotropy is suppressed by the small prefactor and thus is less effective.
In contrast, in the
T=1,S=0
channel, where only the central force acts at zero magnetic field, a clear anisotropy appears once the magnetic field is applied.
Furthermore, we have applied the derived potential to compute the energy shift of the deuteron using the first-order perturbation theory.
We have found that the energy shift due to the modification of the OPEP by a magnetic field is of order 1 MeV at
|eB|\sim m_{\pi}^{2}
.

Our results provide a first step toward understanding the impact of magnetic fields on nuclear force; or broadly speaking, the properties of nuclear force in external conditions.
Such a research direction is of interest by its own, helping us to develop a deeper understanding of nuclear force.

Let us discuss other possible extensions of our work.

First, additional magnetic-field corrections to the OPEP or nuclear forces should be taken into account, such as the Zeeman energy shift and contributions from heavy mesons—particularly the charged
\rho
-meson, whose mass may decrease in magnetic fields.
These are also important to discuss the deuteron, or the nucleon bound-state problem in general. As an example, consider the Zeeman energy shift. A rough estimate gives that the resulting energy shift is the order of
E_{\mathrm{Zeeman}}\sim eB/2m_{N}\sim 10\,\mathrm{MeV}
at
|eB|\sim m_{\pi}^{2}
, which is much larger than the deuteron’s binding energy
E^{B=0}\simeq-2.2\,
MeV. Therefore, to obtain more realistic understanding of nuclear forces and their physical impacts in strong magnetic fields of order
|eB|\sim m_{\pi}^{2}
, these additional mangetic-field effects must be incorporated in future studies.

Another important theoretical challenge is to formulate a lattice QCD framework capable of computing nuclear forces in a background magnetic field—for example, by extending the HAL QCD method
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

Finally, it is intriguing to explore the physical implications of our results in environments where strong magnetic fields are realized, such as the interiors of magnetars.
For example, the modification to OPEP may affect the modified URCA process, which changes the neutrino emissivity and in turn the cooling process of magnetars.
The equation-of-state of nuclear matter in magnetars can also be modified by the OPEP, whose impact may be discussed quantitatively based on the Brueckner theory, as was done in Ref.
[
38
]
in the absence of magnetic fields.

Appendix A
Charged pion Green’s function in a strong magnetic field

In this Appendix, we briefly review the derivation of Eq. (
13
) in the main text, which gives the Green’s function of charged pions (i.e., a charged scalar field) in a strong magnetic field.
We employ Schwinger’s proper-time method
[
36
]
to solve Eq. (
10
); see also the recent review
[
18
]
for further discussion.

As mentioned in the main text, we adopt the Fock-Schwinger symmetric gauge defined in Eq. (
14
), and eventually recover the result in a general gauge by applying a gauge transformation.
The key observation in the Fock-Schwinger symmetric gauge is that the Klein-Gordon operator becomes translationally invariant.
Accordingly, we solve the equation of motion for the Green’s function

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[-\delta^{ij}\left(D^{\pm}_{i}\right)^{\text{FS}}\left(D^{\pm}_{j}\right)^{\text{FS}}+m_{\pi}^{2}\right]G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})&=\delta^{(3)}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}),\end{split}

(41)

under Eq. (
14
), which depends only on the relative coordinate
\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}
.
As we will see shortly, the Green’s function in this gauge turns out to be independent of the sign of the charge, so we omit the superscript
\pm
hereafter.

We now consider the Green’s function in four-dimensional momentum space and later take the static limit.
By performing a Fourier transform of the four-dimensional generalization of Eq. (
41
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[p^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}-\frac{\left(eB\right)^{2}}{4}\partial_{p_{\perp}}^{2}\ +\mathrm{i}0^{+}\right]G_{B}(p)=-1,\end{split}

(42)

where
\mathrm{i}0^{+}
(with
0^{+}
denoting a positive infinitesimal) is introduced to ensure convergence of the proper-time integral,
and
p_{\perp}^{\mu}:=(0,p_{x},p_{y},0)
represents the transverse components of the momentum
(with respect to the magnetic field along the
z
direction),
so that
\partial_{p_{\perp}}^{2}:=\partial^{2}/\partial p_{x}^{2}+\partial^{2}/\partial p_{y}^{2}
.

This equation can be solved to yield the Green’s function expressed as a proper-time integral (see, e.g., Ref.
[
18
]
; note that our sign convention is opposite to that in the reference):

\displaystyle G_{B}(p)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\frac{1}{\cos(eBs)}\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}(m_{\pi}^{2}-\mathrm{i}0^{+})s+\mathrm{i}p_{\parallel}^{2}s-\mathrm{i}\frac{|\bm{p}_{\perp}|^{2}}{eB}\tanh(eBs)\right)

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|\bm{p}_{\perp}|^{2}}{|eB|}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}L_{n}\left(\frac{2|\bm{p}_{\perp}|^{2}}{|eB|}\right)\frac{1}{p_{\parallel}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}-(2n+1)|eB|}.

(43)

Here, we introduced
p^{2}_{\parallel}:=p_{0}^{2}-p_{z}^{2}

and used the
n
-th Laguerre polynomial
L_{n}(z)
.
The second line of this equation reveals the Landau-level structure, exhibiting an infinite number of discrete poles at
p_{\parallel}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}-(2n+1)|eB|=0
.
These poles correspond to the dispersion relations of charged pions undergoing Landau quantization in the magnetic field:

\displaystyle\begin{split}p^{0}=\pm\sqrt{p_{z}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}+(2n+1)|eB|}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad n=0,1,2,\cdots.\end{split}

(44)

Then, taking the static limit
p^{0}\to 0
and performing the Fourier transform, we obtain the spatial Green’s function,

\displaystyle G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{p}\cdot({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\frac{1}{\cos(eBs)}\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}(m_{\pi}^{2}-\mathrm{i}0^{+})s-\mathrm{i}p_{z}^{2}s-\mathrm{i}\frac{|\bm{p}_{\perp}|^{2}}{eB}\tanh(eBs)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|eB|}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\frac{1}{\sin(|eB|s)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\mathrm{i}s}}\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}(m_{\pi}^{2}-\mathrm{i}0^{+})s+\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{4s}(z-z^{\prime})^{2}+\mathrm{i}\frac{|eB|}{4}\frac{({\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}})^{2}}{\tan(|eB|s)}\right).

(45)

It is worth noting that our Green’s function (
45
) is related to the spatial Feynman propagator by time integral:

\displaystyle G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})=\int\mathrm{d}tG_{B}(t,{\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}).

(46)

One can also reexpress the proper-time integral with a Landau-level summation as

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})=\frac{|eB|}{4\pi}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|eB|}{4}|{\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}}|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}\left(\frac{|eB|}{2}|{\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}}|^{2}\right)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+(2n+1)|eB|}|z-z^{\prime}|}}{\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+(2n+1)|eB|}}.\end{split}

(47)

This result demonstrates that the screening mass along the
z
-direction is given by
\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+(2n+1)|eB|}
for each Landau level
n=0,1,2,\cdots
.

In numerical evaluations of the modified OPEP, the expression (
47
) involving the Landau-level summation is not particularly useful due to its slow convergence up to moderate magnetic-field strengths.
We therefore employ a proper-time integral representation of the Green’s function instead.
Moreover, we note that the present kinematics (
p^{0}=0
) allows us to deform the integration contour in Eq. (
45
), which has poles on the real axis at
s=n\pi/|eB|
with
n=0,1,2,\cdots
.
Since
m_{\pi}^{2}
is positive and
0^{+}
is a positive infinitesimal, we can deform the contour to avoid these poles into the lower-right region of the complex
s
-plane.
By Cauchy’s integral theorem, we can then rewrite the expression as

\displaystyle G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{|eB|}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{0}\mathrm{d}s\frac{1}{\sin(|eB|s)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\mathrm{i}s}}\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}(m_{\pi}^{2}-\mathrm{i}0^{+})s+\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{4s}(z-z^{\prime})^{2}+\mathrm{i}\frac{|eB|}{4}\frac{({\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}})^{2}}{\tan(|eB|s)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|eB|}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\frac{1}{\sinh(|eB|s)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{s}}\exp\left(-m_{\pi}^{2}s-\frac{1}{4s}(z-z^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{|eB|}{4}\frac{({\bm{x}_{\perp}-\bm{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}})^{2}}{\tanh(|eB|s)}\right).

(48)

Here, the positive infinitesimal
0^{+}
is dropped in the second line because the integral converges without it.
We use this representation instead of the infinite sum when plotting the potential and computing the deuteron energy shift.

Given the Green’s function in the Fock–Schwinger symmetric gauge, Eq. (
48
), we can obtain its form in a general gauge by performing a gauge transformation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{i}^{\text{FS}}\to A_{i}=A_{i}^{\text{FS}}-\partial_{i}\alpha.\end{split}

(49)

Recalling Eq. (
41
), this transformation leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{\pm}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}}|A)&=\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}e\alpha({\bm{x}})\mp\mathrm{i}e\alpha({\bm{x}^{\prime}})}G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}})=\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\Phi_{A}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}})}G_{B}({\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}}),\\
\Phi_{A}({\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}})&=-\,e{\int_{\bm{x}^{\prime}}^{\bm{x}}}\mathrm{d}\bm{{\xi}}\cdot\Bigl[\bm{A}(\bm{{\xi}})+\tfrac{1}{2}(\bm{{\xi}}-{\bm{x}^{\prime}})\times\bm{B}\Bigr]\,,\end{split}

(50)

which leads to Eq. (
13
) in the main text.

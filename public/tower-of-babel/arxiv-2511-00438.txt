Decorated Marked Surfaces with vortices: Cluster braid group vs. braid twist group
Yu Qiu, Yu Zhou

Abstract
. Let 𝐒 ☯ \mathbf{S}^{{\text{\YinYang}}} be a marked surface with vortices (=punctures with extra ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry). We study the decorated version 𝐒 △ ☯ \mathbf{S}^{{\text{\YinYang}}}_{\bigtriangleup} , where the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry lifts to the relation that the fourth power of the braid twist of any collision path (connecting a decoration in △ \bigtriangleup and a vortex in ☯ ) is identity. We prove the following three groups are isomorphic: King-Qiu’s cluster braid group associated to 𝐒 ☯ \mathbf{S}^{{\text{\YinYang}}} , the braid twist group of 𝐒 △ ☯ \mathbf{S}^{{\text{\YinYang}}}_{\bigtriangleup} and the fundamental group of Bridgeland-Smith’s moduli space of 𝐒 ☯ \mathbf{S}^{{\text{\YinYang}}} -framed GMN differentials. Moreover, we give finite presentations of such groups. Key words: cluster braid groups, braid twist groups, quadratic differentials, decorated marked surfaces

Cluster braid groups

One of the key techniques used in

Application to moduli spaces of quadratic differentials

7.1.
Preliminaries

Quadratic differentials

We recall standard notions concerning quadratic differentials, see
[
BS
]
and
[
KQ2
,
Q4
]
for more details. Let
\mathbf{X}
be a compact Riemann surface and
\omega_{\mathbf{X}}
be its holomorphic cotangent bundle. A
meromorphic quadratic differential

\phi
on
\mathbf{X}
is a meromorphic section of the line bundle
\omega_{\mathbf{X}}^{2}
. In terms of a local coordinate
z
on
\mathbf{X}
, such a
\phi
can be written as
\phi(z)=g(z)\,dz^{2}
, where
g(z)
is a meromorphic function. The zeros and simple poles of
\phi
are called
finite singularities
, while poles of order at least two are called
infinite singularities
.

Definition 7.1
.

A
GMN differential

\phi
on
\mathbf{X}
is a meromorphic quadratic differential such that

•

all zeros of
\phi
are simple,

•

\phi
has at least one higher order (at least two) pole,

•

\phi
has at least one finite critical point.

Denote by
\operatorname{Zero}(\phi)
the set of zeros of
\phi
,

\operatorname{Pol}_{j}(\phi)
the set of poles of
\phi
with order
j

and
\operatorname{Pol}(\phi)=\coprod_{j}\operatorname{Pol}_{j}(\phi)
.

A GMN differential
\phi
on
\mathbf{X}
determines the
\phi
-metric on
\mathbf{X}^{\circ}
, which is defined locally
by pulling back the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{C}
using a distinguished coordinate
\omega
,
cf.
[
Q4
, Fig. 16]
.
Thus, there are geodesics on
\mathbf{X}^{\circ}
, each of which has a constant phase with respect to
\omega
.
A
trajectory
of a GMN differential
\phi
on
\mathbf{X}^{\circ}

is a maximal horizontal geodesic
\gamma\colon(0,1)\to\mathbf{X}^{\circ}
,
with respect to the
\phi
metric.
We will usually consider horizontal trajectories unless otherwise specified.

WKB triangulations

We will mainly consider the following types of trajectories of a GMN differential
\phi
:

•

saddle trajectories
, whose both ends are finite singularities;

•

separating trajectories
that connect a finite singularity and an infinite singularity;

•

generic trajectories
whose both ends are infinite singularities.

We say a GMN differential is
saddle-free
if there are no saddle trajectories.
In such a case, by removing all separating trajectories (which are finitely many) from
\mathbf{X}^{\circ}
, the remaining open surface splits as a disjoint union of

•

a
half-plane (infinite height horizontal strip)
, i.e., isomorphic to

\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
equipped with the differential
dz^{2}
.
It is swept out by generic trajectories which connect a fixed pole to itself.

•

or a
(finite height) horizontal strip
, i.e. isomorphic to

\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid a<\operatorname{Im}(z)<b\}
equipped with the differential
dz^{2}
for some
a<b\in\mathbb{R}
. It is swept out by generic trajectories connecting two (not necessarily distinct) poles.

We call this union the
horizontal strip decomposition
of
\mathbf{X}
with respect to
\phi
.
In each horizontal strip, the trajectories are isotopic to each other
and there is a unique geodesic, the
saddle connection
, connecting the two zeros on its boundary,
which is a trajectory with a certain fixed phase (other than zero).

A GMN-differential is called
complete
if
\operatorname{Pol}_{1}(\phi)=\varnothing
.

Definition 7.2
.

When
\phi
is saddle-free, there is a
WKB triangulation

\mathrm{T}_{\phi}
on
\mathbf{S}
induced from
\phi
,
where the arcs are (isotopy classes of inherited) generic trajectories.
Moreover, any triangle in
\mathrm{T}_{\phi}
either contains exactly one zero or is a self-folded triangulation with a simple pole as interior vertex.
If
\phi
is in addition complete, then
\mathrm{T}_{\phi}
becomes a decorated triangulation
\mathbb{T}_{\phi}
of
\mathbf{S}_{\bigtriangleup}
,
with dual graph consisting of saddle trajectories.

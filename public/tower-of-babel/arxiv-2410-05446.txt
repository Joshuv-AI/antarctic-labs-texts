Stability of sorting based embeddings
Radu Balan, Efstratios Tsoukanis, Matthias Wellershoff

Abstract
Consider a group G G of order M M acting unitarily on a real inner product space V V . We show that the sorting based embedding obtained by applying a general linear map α : ℝ M × N → ℝ D \alpha:\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{D} to the invariant map β Φ : V → ℝ M × N \beta_{\Phi}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{M\times N} given by sorting the coorbits ( ⟨ v , g ​ ϕ i ⟩ V ) g ∈ G (\langle v,g\phi_{i}\rangle_{V})_{g\in G} , where ( ϕ i ) i = 1 N ∈ V (\phi_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in V , satisfies a bi-Lipschitz condition if and only if it separates orbits. Additionally, we note that any invariant Lipschitz continuous map (into a Hilbert space) factors through the sorting based embedding, and that any invariant continuous map (into a locally convex space) factors through the sorting based embedding as well.

Introduction

Suppose that a specific learning task with input space
V
is invariant under the action of a group
G
. Then, it makes sense to construct a class of hypotheses that factor into two parts: i. a group invariant part
h:V\to V_{\mathrm{int}}
, where
V_{\mathrm{int}}
is some intermediate space, and ii. an “optimisable” part
g:V_{\mathrm{int}}\to W
, where
W
denotes the space of all possible outputs. In this way, we can use an optimisation algorithm to find
g
in such a way that
f=g\circ h
fits some training data, and the above construction ascertains that
f:V\to W
is invariant under the action of
G
.

So, introducing the equivalence relation
v\sim w:\iff\exists g\in G:v=gw
on
V
and assuming that
V
has a norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{V}
, our goal becomes to construct maps
h:V\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
, where
D\in\mathbb{N}
is as small as possible, and
h
satisfies the properties:

1.

Invariance.

h(v)=h(w)
for all
v,w\in V
such that
v\sim w
.

2.

Orbit separation.

v\sim w
for all
v,w\in V
such that
h(v)=h(w)
.

3.

Bi-Lipschitz condition.
There exist constants
0<c\leq C
such that

c\operatorname{dist}(v,w)\leq\lVert h(v)-h(w)\rVert_{2}\leq C\operatorname{dist}(v,w),\qquad v,w\in V.

(1)

Here and throughout the paper,
\operatorname{dist}(v,w):=\min_{g\in G}\lVert v-gw\rVert_{V}
denotes the natural metric on the quotient space
V/{\sim}
.

The approach described above is an instance of
invariant machine learning
: techniques designed to ensure that hypotheses are robust to specific changes in the input data.
More precisely, it is a special form of
feature engineering
, where raw data is transformed into a more useful set of inputs.

Feature transforms have been suggested in computer vision, for example, where handcrafted maps such as the scale-invariant feature transform (SIFT)
[
18
]
or the histogram of oriented gradients (HOG)
[
14
]
were created to be invariant to transformations like scaling, translation or rotation. The results we present here were inspired by the two more recent papers
[
12
,
16
]
and are a continuation of work presented in
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. Similar approaches can also be found in
[
1
,
10
,
11
]
and, most notably,
[
19
]
.

Taking a wider view of the literature, there is another notable approach to invariant machine learning which consists of propagating the invariance of a problem through multiple
equivariant layers
: maps
f:V\to W
such that
f(gv)=gf(v)
for all
g\in G
and all
v\in V
. (Here,
G
is also assumed to act on
W
.) The posterchildren for equivariant machine learning models are convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
[
17
]
which are translation invariant. (Though some care has to be taken when defining “translation invariance”
[
2
]
.)

A well-known generalisation of the CNN architecture in the same spirit is the group equivariant convolutional network architecture
[
13
]
which introduces layers that can respect more general symmetries. Alternative approaches include
[
22
]
(cf. also
[
9
]
) as well as
[
23
]
.

1.1
Sorting-based embeddings

Let
G
be a finite group acting unitarily on a
d\in\mathbb{N}
dimensional real inner product space
V
. One approach to construct maps that satisfy items 1 through 3 is to enumerate the group
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
and define the coorbits

\kappa_{\phi}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{M},\qquad\kappa_{\phi}v:=\begin{pmatrix}\langle v,g_{1}\phi\rangle_{V}&\dots&\langle v,g_{M}\phi\rangle_{V}\end{pmatrix}^{\top},

for
\phi\in V
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}
denotes the inner product on
V
. By choosing a finite sequence
\Phi:=(\phi_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in V
, sorting the coorbits, and collecting them in a matrix, one obtains an invariant map

\beta_{\Phi}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{M\times N},\qquad\beta_{\Phi}(v):=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v)&\dots&\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v)\end{pmatrix},

where
\operatorname{sort}:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
denotes the operator that sorts vectors in a monotonically decreasing way. To reduce the embedding dimension, a linear map
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
can be applied to the sorted coorbits.

This idea was first introduced in
[
3
]
in the context of the action of the group
S_{m}
by row permutation on the space of matrices
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. The authors demonstrated that the map
\gamma(\mathbf{X}):=\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A})
1

1

\operatorname{sort}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times N}
is the operator that sorts matrices column-wise.
separates orbits for full spark matrices
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}
with
N>(n-1)m!
. They also showed that
\gamma
satisfies the bi-Lipschitz condition (inequality (
1
)) if it separates orbits, and that
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\gamma
satisfies inequality (
1
) for a generic
2

2

generic
, we mean that a statement is true in a non-empty Zariski open set.
linear map
\alpha^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
with
N\geq 2n
and
D\geq 2mn
(provided that
\gamma
separates orbits). Notably, the embedding
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\gamma
passes through an intermediate Euclidean space of dimension larger than
(n-1)m!
, which grows rapidly with the matrix size.

The papers
[
12
,
16
]
address this issue. In
[
16
]
, among other things, it is shown that
\gamma(\mathbf{X}):=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}))
separates orbits for generic pairs
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times D}\times\mathbb{R}^{D\times m}
with
D>2mn
. Whether
\gamma
satisfies inequality (

) remained an open question. In
[

]
, the authors show that, if
\alpha(\mathbf{X}):=\begin{pmatrix}X_{11}&\dots&X_{1D}\end{pmatrix}
selects the maximal entries of the coorbits, then
\gamma:=\alpha\circ\beta_{\Phi}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
separates orbits for generic sequences
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i=1}^{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
D\geq 2d
, whenever
G\leq O(d)
is a finite subgroup of the orthogonal
d\times d
matrices. They also prove that
\gamma
satisfies inequality (
1
) with high probability if
D
is sufficiently large, and they pose the question whether
\gamma
satisfies inequality (
1
) whenever it separates orbits.

This was affirmatively answered in
[
4
]
, where it was shown that
\gamma=\alpha\circ\beta_{\Phi}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
satisfies the bi-Lipschitz condition if it separates orbits for any finite group
G
acting isometrically on
V
, provided that
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
selects any subset of the entries of
\beta_{\Phi}(v)
. Additionally, in
[
5

, it was shown that such
\gamma

\Phi
is chosen generically with respect to the Zariski topology on
V^{N}
and that the right subset of entries of
\beta_{\Phi}(v)
is chosen.

1.2
Results

In this paper, we generalise the results of
[
3
,
4
]
to encompass arbitrary linear maps
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
, which proves, in particular, that the embeddings
\gamma(\mathbf{X})=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}))
satisfy inequality (
1
) for generic pairs
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times D}\times\mathbb{R}^{D\times m}
when
D>2mn
.

Theorem 1

(Main result)
.

The embedding
\gamma=\alpha\circ\beta_{\Phi}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
separates orbits if and only if it satisfies the bi-Lipschitz condition (
1
).

Moreover, we remark that, as noted in
[
20
]
, the hypothesis class of functions that factor into an invariant and an optimisable part, as described at the beginning of the introduction, can contain general invariant (Lipschitz) continuous functions, if
g
is restricted to a sufficiently large class of functions.

Specifically, we note that any group invariant Lipschitz map into a Hilbert space factors through
\gamma
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f:V\to H
be invariant under the action of
G
and Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
C_{f}>0
, where
H
is some Hilbert space. If
\gamma=\alpha\circ\beta_{\Phi}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
separates orbits, then there exists
g:\mathbb{R}^{D}\to H
Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant at most
C_{f}/c
such that
f=g\circ\gamma
.

Remark 3
.

It is obvious that any composition of Lipschitz continuous maps is Lipschitz continuous in turn. In particular,
f=g\circ\gamma
is Lipschitz continuous (and invariant under the action of
G
) provided that
g:\mathbb{R}^{D}\to M
is Lipschitz continuous, where
M
is a metric space.

Additionally, we note that any continuous group invariant map into a locally convex space also factors through
\gamma
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
f:V\to Z
be invariant under the action of
G
and continuous, where
Z
is some locally convex space. If
\gamma=\alpha\circ\beta_{\Phi}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
separates orbits, then there exists
g:\mathbb{R}^{D}\to Z
continuous such that
g(\mathbb{R}^{D})
is a subset of the convex hull of
f(V)
and such that
f=g\circ\gamma
.

Remark 5
.

Vice versa, since compositions of continuous maps are continuous, we have that
f=g\circ\gamma
is continuous (and invariant under the action of
G
) if
g:\mathbb{R}^{D}\to Z
is continuous, where
Z
is a topological space.

1.3
Examples

Finally, we want to remark that our main result does
not
hold for general ReLU neural networks and we want to provide two examples of setups in which our results do apply.

Remark 6

(There exists an injective ReLU neural networks that is
not
bi-Lipschitz)
.

A general ReLU neural network of depth
L\in\mathbb{N}
is a map
\mathcal{N}:\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{L}}
of the form

\mathcal{N}(x):=\begin{cases}\mathbf{W}_{1}x+\mathbf{b}_{1}&\mbox{if }L=1,\\
\mathbf{W}_{L}\rho(\dots\rho(\mathbf{W}_{1}x+\mathbf{b}_{1})\dots)+\mathbf{b}_{L}&\mbox{if }L\geq 2,\end{cases}

where
\ell_{0},\dots,\ell_{L}\in\mathbb{N}
denote the layer widths,
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\rho(x):=\max\{x,0\}
, denotes the ReLU activation function, which is applied component-wise to vector inputs,
\mathbf{W}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{k}\times\ell_{k-1}}
denote the weight matrices and
\mathbf{b}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{k}}
denote the bias vectors for
k\in[L]
.

A simple example of a map that is injective but not bi-Lipschitz is given by
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

f(x):=\begin{cases}-(1,x+1)&\mbox{if }x<-1,\\
(x,0)&\mbox{if }-1\leq x\leq 1,\\
(1,x-1)&\mbox{if }1<x.\end{cases}

It is not hard to check that
f
is injective. At the same time,
f
does not satisfy the lower Lipschitz condition because
\lVert f(x)-f(-x)\rVert_{2}=2
for
x\not\in[-1,1]
while
\lvert x-(-x)\rvert=2\lvert x\rvert
is unbounded.

The above map can readily be extended to a map
f_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}
via
f_{d}(\mathbf{x})=f_{d}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d})=(f(x_{1}),x_{2},\dots,x_{d})
. Additionally,
f=f_{1}
can be implemented as a ReLU neural network with two layers: indeed, choose

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\\
-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{b}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\\
-1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\
0&1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{b}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\
0\end{pmatrix},

and note that
\mathcal{N}=f
.

Example 7

(Permutation invariant representations)
.

Let us consider the action of the group
S_{m}
by row permutation on the vector space of matrices
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. This setup is naturally encountered in learning on graphs with
m
vertices, where feature vectors of dimension
n
are associated to every vertex, if the hypothesis is to be invariant under the permutation of vertices.

We want to come back to the permutation invariant embedding proposed in
[
16
]
as a variation on the embedding proposed earlier in
[
3
]
:
\gamma_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
,

\gamma_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{X}):=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A})),\qquad\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},

where
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times D}
,
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times D}
,
\operatorname{diag}:\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
is the linear operator that extracts the diagonal from a
D\times D
matrix and
\operatorname{sort}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times D}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times D}
denotes column-wise sorting of matrices.

In
[
16
]
, it is shown that
\gamma_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}
separates orbits for almost every
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times D}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times D}
provided that
D>2mn
. Additionally, one may note that
\gamma_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}
can be evaluated in polynomial time. Indeed, evaluating
\gamma_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}
requires

1.

a matrix multiplication of a matrix of dimension
m\times n
and a matrix of dimension
n\times D
, which can be performed in
\mathcal{O}(Dmn)
operations,

2.

sorting
D
vectors of length
m
, which can be performed in
\mathcal{O}(Dm\log m)
operations,

3.

computing
D
inner products of vectors of length
m
, which can be performed in
\mathcal{O}(Dm)
operations.

Overall, we have an evaluation complexity of
\mathcal{O}(Dm(n+\log m))
which, assuming that
D\cong mn
and
m\cong n
, is
\mathcal{O}(m^{4})
.

Our main result, Theorem
1
, shows that
\gamma_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}
satisfies the bi-Lipschitz condition: there exist constants
0<c\leq C
such that

c\min_{\mathbf{P}\in S_{m}}\lVert\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Y}\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\lVert\gamma_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{X})-\gamma_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{Y})\rVert_{2}\leq C\min_{\mathbf{P}\in S_{m}}\lVert\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Y}\rVert_{\mathrm{F}},

for all
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. Computation of an upper bound for the Lipschitz constant
C
is possible by Proposition
23
. For practical purposes, it would also be interesting to lower bound the lower Lipschitz constant
c>0
. While this is hard, in general, in some special cases it is possible.

Example 8

(Sign retrieval)
.

In sign retrieval, estimating
c
explicitly is simplified by what is known as the
\sigma
-strong complement property
[
7
,
8
]
: we are interested in the recovery of vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from magnitude-only measurements

\lvert\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{a}_{i}\rangle\rvert,\qquad i\in[D],

(2)

where
(\mathbf{a}_{i})_{i=1}^{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a sequence of measurement vectors. Since
\mathbf{x}
and
-\mathbf{x}
generate the same measurements, one typically aims to recover vectors
up to a global sign
; i.e., up to the equivalence relation
\mathbf{x}\sim\mathbf{y}:\iff\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}\mbox{ or }\mathbf{x}=-\mathbf{y}
; or, up to action of the group
G=\{-1,1\}
on the vector space of signals
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The action of the group
S_{2}
by row permutation on the vector space of matrices
\mathbb{R}^{2\times n}
is closely related to this sign retrieval problem as demonstrated in
[
6
]
: indeed,
\beta_{\mathbf{A}}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times D}
,

\beta_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{X}):=\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}),\qquad\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times n},

where
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times D}
is a matrix with column vectors
(\mathbf{a}_{i})_{i=1}^{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, separates orbits if and only if all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be uniquely recovered from the measurements (
2
) up to a global sign.

However, not only the orbit separating properties of
\beta_{\mathbf{A}}
are related to a sign retrieval problem, also the bi-Lipschitz condition is related to the Lipschitz properties of the sign retrieval operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{d}/\{-1,1\}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{D}
,

\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{x})_{i}:=\lvert\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{a}_{i}\rangle\rvert,\qquad i\in[D].

Indeed, the Lipschitz constant for the embedding
\beta_{\mathbf{A}}
is exactly the Lipschitz constant of
\mathcal{A}
and thus given by the largest singular value
\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{A})
of the matrix
\mathbf{A}
. Moreover, the lower Lipschitz constant for the embedding
\beta_{\mathbf{A}}
is also exactly the lower Lipschitz constant of
\mathcal{A}
and thus exactly given by the quantity

c=\min_{S\subseteq[D]}\sqrt{\sigma_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{A}_{S})+\sigma_{n}^{2}(\mathbf{A}_{S^{\mathrm{c}}})}

(3)

as demonstrated in
[
6
,
7
]
. Here,
\sigma_{n}(\cdot)
is used to denote the
n
-th singular value (in decreasing order) of a matrix and
\mathbf{A}_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times\lvert S\rvert}
denotes the matrix obtained by only keeping the columns whose indices are elements of
S\subseteq[D]
.

Equation (
3
) can allow for relatively simple computation of the lower Lipschitz constant of the permutation-invariant embedding
\beta_{\mathbf{A}}
. Indeed, consider
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 3}
whose columns are given by the Mercedes-Benz frame

\mathbf{a}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt{3}/2\\
-1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{a}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{3}/2\\
-1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{a}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},

as an example. Then, it is a simple exercise to show that
c=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
.

2
Continuous maps factor through sorting based embeddings

In this section, we prove Theorems
2
and
4
. These two results are based on two well-known extension theorems: the Kirszbraun extension theorem and the Tietze extension theorem.

The precise version of the Kirszbraun extension theorem that we are going to use is due to F. A. Valentine
[
21
]
.

Theorem 9

(Kirszbraun–Valentine extension theorem)
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be Hilbert spaces, let
S\subseteq H_{1}
and let
g_{0}:S\to H_{2}
be a Lipschitz continuous map with Lipschitz constant
C>0
. Then,
g_{0}
can be extended to a Lipschitz continuous map
g:H_{1}\to H_{2}
with the same Lipschitz constant
C
.

We note that the above theorem implies that any Lipschitz continuous map factors through any lower Lipschitz continuous map in the following sense.

Corollary 10
.

Let
X
be a metric space, let
H_{1},H_{2}
be Hilbert spaces, let
h:X\to H_{1}
be lower Lipschitz continuous with lower Lipschitz constant
c_{h}>0
and let
f:X\to H_{2}
be Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
C_{f}>0
. Then, there exists
g:H_{1}\to H_{2}
Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant at most
C_{f}/c_{h}
such that
f=g\circ h
.

Proof.

Let
d_{X}
denote the metric on
X
and let
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H_{i}}
denote the norm on
H_{i}
where
i=1,2
.

Since
h
is lower Lipschitz continuous, it is injective and thus invertible on its range
\mathcal{R}(h)
. We may therefore define
g_{0}:=f\circ h^{-1}:\mathcal{R}(h)\subseteq H_{1}\to H_{2}
. It clear that
g_{0}
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant at most
C_{f}/c_{h}
since

\displaystyle\lVert g_{0}(x)-g_{0}(y)\rVert_{H_{2}}

\displaystyle=\lVert f(h^{-1}(x))-f(h^{-1}(y))\rVert_{H_{2}}\leq C_{f}d_{X}(h^{-1}(x),h^{-1}(y))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{f}}{c_{h}}\lVert x-y\rVert_{H_{1}}

for
x,y\in H_{1}
. Therefore, by the Kirszbraun–Valentine extension theorem,
g_{0}
extends to
g:H_{1}\to H_{2}
Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant at most
C_{f}/c_{h}
. Finally, we have
f=g\circ h
by construction.
∎

Remark 11
.

Most likely this corollary has been proven before or been included in a textbook as an exercise. We were unable to find a reference, however.

We can now prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H}
denote the norm on
H
.

Since
f
is invariant under the action of
G
, it descends through the quotient to
\widehat{f}:V/{\sim}\to H
. Moreover, since
f
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
C_{f}
, so is
\widehat{f}
: let
v,w\in V
and let
g\in G
be such that
\operatorname{dist}(v,w)=\lVert v-gw\rVert_{V}
. Then, it holds that

\lVert\widehat{f}([v])-\widehat{f}([w])\rVert_{H}=\lVert f(v)-f(gw)\rVert_{H}\leq C_{f}\lVert v-gw\rVert_{V}=C_{f}\operatorname{dist}(v,w),

where
[v]=\{gv\,|\,g\in G\}
denotes the equivalence class of
v
and
[w]
denotes the equivalence class of
w
.

If
\gamma:V\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
separates orbits, then it satisfies the bi-Lipschitz condition according to our main theorem. In particular, since
\gamma
is also invariant under the action of
G
, it also descends through the quotient to
\widehat{\gamma}:V/{\sim}\to H
and
\widehat{\gamma}
is lower Lipschitz continuous with lower Lipschitz constant
c>0
.

By Corollary
10
, there exists
g:\mathbb{R}^{D}\to H
Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant at most
C_{f}/c
such that
\widehat{f}=g\circ\widehat{\gamma}
. Letting
v\in W
, we also get

f(v)=\widehat{f}([v])=g(\widehat{\gamma}([v]))=g(\gamma(v)).

∎

Similarly, the precise version of the Tietze extension theorem that we are going to use is due to J. Dugundji
[
15
]
.

Theorem 12

(Dugundji–Tietze extension theorem)
.

Let
Y
be a metric space, let
Z
be a locally convex space, let
S\subseteq Y
be a closed subset of
Y
and let
g_{0}:S\to Z
be a continuous map. Then,
g_{0}
can be extended to a continuous map
g:Y\to Z
in such a way that
g(Y)
is a subset of the convex hull of
g_{0}(S)
.

We note that this immediately implies that any continuous function factors through any injective, relatively open function with closed range.

Corollary 13
.

Let
X
be a topological space, let
Y
be a metric space and let
Z
be a locally convex space. Let
h:X\to Y
be an injective, relatively open map with closed range and let
f:X\to Z
be continuous. Then, there exists
g:Y\to Z
continuous such that
g(Y)
is a subset of the convex hull of
f(X)
and such that
f=g\circ h
.

Proof.

Since
h
is injective, it is invertible on its range
\mathcal{R}(h)
. We may therefore define
g_{0}:=f\circ h^{-1}:\mathcal{R}(h)\subseteq Y\to Z
. Since
f
is continuous and
h
is relatively open, it follows that
g_{0}
is continuous. Since the range of
h
is closed, the Dugundji–Tietze extension theorem implies that
g_{0}
extends to a continuous function
g:Y\to Z
such that
g(Y)
is contained in the convex hull of
g_{0}(\mathcal{R}(h))=f(X)
. Finally, we have
f=g\circ h
by construction.
∎

Remark 14
.

Again, this corollary has most likely been proven or, at least, mentioned before but we were unable to find a reference.

We can now prove Theorem
4
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Again,
f
descends through the quotient to
\widehat{f}:V/{\sim}\to Z
and, since
f
is continuous, so is
\widehat{f}
. Because
\gamma
separates orbits, our main theorem implies that
\gamma
satisfies the bi-Lipschitz condition. Of course,
\gamma
descends through the quotient to
\widehat{\gamma}:V/{\sim}\to H
and, since
\gamma
satisfies the bi-Lipschitz condition,
\widehat{\gamma}
is bi-Lipschitz such that its range
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\gamma})
is closed and
\widehat{\gamma}
is injective. Therefore,
\widehat{\gamma}
is invertible on its range
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\gamma})
and its inverse is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\gamma})
: it follows that
\widehat{\gamma}
is relatively open. By Corollary

, there exists
g:\mathbb{R}^{D}\to Z
continuous such that
g(\mathbb{R}^{D})
is a subset of the convex hull of
\widehat{f}(V/{\sim})=f(V)
and
\widehat{f}=g\circ\widehat{\gamma}
. As before, we get

.
∎

Preliminaries for the proof of Theorem

3.1
Sorting coorbits

Denote the set of permutations that sort a vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
by

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}):=\{\sigma\in S_{M}\,|\,\sigma\mathbf{x}=\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{x})\}.

Let us furthermore denote the stabiliser of
\mathbf{x}
by

\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}):=\{\sigma\in S_{M}\,|\,\sigma\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}\}.

The sets
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})
are related in the following way.

Proposition 15
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})
. Then,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})=\sigma\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})
.

Next, we may denote

\delta(\mathbf{x}):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[M]\\
x_{i}\neq x_{j}\end{subarray}}\lvert x_{i}-x_{j}\rvert.

If the vector
\mathbf{x}
is
not
constant, then
\delta(\mathbf{x})
is exactly a scalar multiple of the distance of
\mathbf{x}
to the next hyperplane of the form
\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\,|\,x_{i}=x_{j}\}
, for
i,j\in[M]
with
i<j
that does
not
contain
\mathbf{x}
.

Proposition 16
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Then,

\delta(\mathbf{x})=\min_{\sigma\in S_{M}\setminus\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})}\lVert(\sigma-e)\mathbf{x}\rVert_{\infty}=\min_{\sigma\in S_{M}\setminus\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})}\lVert\sigma\mathbf{x}-\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{x})\rVert_{\infty}.

Finally, let us denote the difference of the maximum and minimum of a vector
\mathbf{x}
by

\Delta(\mathbf{x}):=\max_{i\in[M]}x_{i}-\min_{i\in[M]}x_{i}=\max_{i,j\in[M]}\lvert x_{i}-x_{j}\rvert.

Introducing the matrix
\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{M(M-1)/2\times M}
,

\mathbf{D}=\begin{pNiceMatrix},

we may write
\Delta(\mathbf{x})=\lVert\mathbf{D}\mathbf{x}\rVert_{\infty}
, which shows that
\Delta(\cdot)
satisfies the triangle inequality and is absolutely homogeneous. Finally, we note the following simple inequality.

Proposition 17
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Then,
\delta(\mathbf{x})\leq\Delta(\mathbf{x})
.

Inspired by
[
4
]
, we show a score of set inclusions and equalities. Let us start by proving the following simple inclusions (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 2.5 on p. 7]
).

Lemma 18
.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
be such that
\Delta(\mathbf{y})<\delta(\mathbf{x})
. Then,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})
.

Proof.

We show that
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})^{\mathrm{c}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})^{\mathrm{c}}
: if
\sigma\not\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})
, then we may find
i,j\in[M]
such that
i<j
and
x_{\sigma(i)}<x_{\sigma(j)}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle x_{\sigma(j)}+y_{\sigma(j)}-x_{\sigma(i)}-y_{\sigma(i)}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[M]\\
x_{i}\neq x_{j}\end{subarray}}\lvert x_{i}-x_{j}\rvert+\min_{i\in[M]}y_{i}-\max_{i\in[M]}y_{i}

\displaystyle=\delta(\mathbf{x})-\Delta(\mathbf{y})>0

such that
\sigma\not\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})
. Finally, pick
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})
and note that
\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})=\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})=\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})
according to Proposition
15
.
∎

Using this lemma, we can show that the set of sorting permutations does not change along certain straight line segments (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 2.6 on p. 8]
).

Lemma 19
.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
be such that
\Delta(\mathbf{y})<\delta(\mathbf{x})
. Then,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y})=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y})=\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})
for
t\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

\subseteq
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y})
. Then, Lemma
18
implies that
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})
. So, let
i,j\in[M]
be such that
i<j
. There are two cases: either
x_{\sigma(i)}=x_{\sigma(j)}
in which case

\displaystyle(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(i)}-(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(j)}

\displaystyle=y_{\sigma(i)}-y_{\sigma(j)}=t^{-1}(ty_{\sigma(i)}-ty_{\sigma(j)})

\displaystyle=t^{-1}\left((\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(i)}-(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(j)}\right)\geq 0;

or
x_{\sigma(i)}>x_{\sigma(j)}
in which case

\displaystyle(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(i)}-(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(j)}

\displaystyle=x_{\sigma(i)}-x_{\sigma(j)}+y_{\sigma(i)}-y_{\sigma(j)}\geq\delta(\mathbf{x})-\Delta(\mathbf{y})>0.

Either way,
(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(i)}\geq(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(j)}
. Since
i,j\in[M]
were arbitrary, we conclude that
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})
.

\supseteq
.

The reverse inclusion follows because
\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y}=t(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})+(1-t)\mathbf{y}
is a convex combination of
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{x}
. More precisely, we have that
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})
such that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(i)}

\displaystyle=t(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(i)}+(1-t)x_{\sigma(i)}

\displaystyle\geq t(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(j)}+(1-t)x_{\sigma(j)}=(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(j)}

for all
i,j\in[M]
such that
i<j
. Therefore,
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y})
.

The equality for the stabilisers follows from the equality for the permutations that sort by Proposition
15
.
∎

Next, we prove that the set of permutations that sort is stable on sufficiently small hypercubes (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 2.8, items 1 and 2 on p. 10]
).

Lemma 20
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{k=1}^{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
,
(c_{k})_{k=1}^{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
be such that

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{x}_{\ell+1})<\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg),\qquad\ell\in[p-1],

\displaystyle\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\leq\epsilon\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg).

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\text{1.~}~\mathcal{L}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\mathcal{L}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg),\qquad\text{2.~}~\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg),

\displaystyle\text{3.~}~(1-\epsilon)\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\leq\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\leq(1+\epsilon)\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg).

Proof.

\supseteq
.

We note that

\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k},\qquad\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)<\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg).

Therefore,

\mathcal{L}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\subseteq\mathcal{L}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)

follows from Lemma
18
. We conclude that the stabilisers satisfy the same inclusion.

\subseteq
.

Applying Lemma
18
inductively yields

\mathcal{L}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\subseteq\mathcal{L}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}_{1}).

So, if
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})
and
\tau\in\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})
, then

\displaystyle\sigma\mathbf{x}_{1}=\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\tau\mathbf{x}_{1},

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{2})=\operatorname{sort}(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{2})=\tau(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{2}),

\displaystyle\sigma\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\operatorname{sort}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\tau\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg),

such that
\sigma\mathbf{x}_{k}=\tau\mathbf{x}_{k}
for
k\in[p]
. Therefore,

\operatorname{sort}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\tau\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\tau\mathbf{x}_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\sigma\mathbf{x}_{k}=\sigma\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)

and
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k})
. It follows that the stabilisers satisfy the same inclusion.

Inequalities.

Before proving the two inequalities, we establish the following claim.

Claim.

Let
i,j\in[M]
. Then,

\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}>\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}\iff\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}>\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}.

Proof of the claim.

First, note that
(ij)\in\mathcal{H}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k})
if and only if
(ij)\in\mathcal{H}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})
according to item 2. Now, assume by contradiction that

\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}>\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j},\qquad\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}<\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}

and consider the function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,

f(t):=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(1+t(c_{k}-1))\left((\mathbf{x}_{k})_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{j}\right),

which satisfies
f(0)<0
and
f(1)>0
. By the intermediate value theorem, there exists
t\in(0,1)
such that
f(t)=0
. By item 2 and

\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}((1+t(c_{k}-1))-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=t\cdot\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)<t\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg),

we may conclude that

(ij)\in\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(1+t(c_{k}-1))\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)

which contradicts the assumption
(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{i}<(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{j}
.
The reverse implication follows by noting that

(ij)\in\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)

contradicts the assumption
(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{i}>(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{j}
and exchanging the role of
i
and
j
.

Now, let
i,j\in[M]
be such that
(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{i}>(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{j}
and

\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}>0.

Then, we may use the claim to see that

\displaystyle\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)={}

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)-\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\geq(1-\epsilon)\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg).

Vice versa, let
i,j\in[M]
be such that
(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{i}>(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{j}
and

\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)=\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}>0.

Using the claim once more yields

\displaystyle\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)={}

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(1-c_{k})\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(1-c_{k})\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)-\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)-\epsilon\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)

which proves the upper bound after rearrangement of the inequality.
∎

Finally, we show that the set of permutations that sort remains stable on sufficiently small hypercubes when we add a sufficiently small additional vector (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 2.8, item 3 on p. 10]
).

Lemma 21
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{k=1}^{p},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
(c_{k})_{k=1}^{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be such that

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{x}_{\ell+1})<\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg),\qquad\ell\in[p-1],

\displaystyle\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}(c_{k}-1)\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg),

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{y})\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg).

Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)=\mathcal{L}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)=\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg).

Proof.

Before proving the two inclusions, we show the following claim.

Claim.

Let
i,j\in[M]
. Then,

\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}=\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j}\implies\forall k\in[p]:(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{i}=(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{j}.

Proof of the claim.

If
(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{i}=(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})_{j}
, then
(ij)\in\mathcal{H}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})
. According to Lemma
18
, we have

(ij)\in\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\subseteq\mathcal{H}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}_{1}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{x}_{1})_{i}=(\mathbf{x}_{1})_{j},

\displaystyle(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{2})_{i}=(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{2})_{j},

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{i}=\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{j},

which implies
(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{i}=(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{j}
for
k\in[p]
.

\subseteq
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})
(according to Lemma
18
) and let
i,j\in[M]
be such that
i<j
.

We may now consider two cases: if
\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{\sigma(i)}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{\sigma(j)}
, then

\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)_{\sigma(i)}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)_{\sigma(j)}=y_{\sigma(i)}-y_{\sigma(j)}\\
=\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)_{\sigma(i)}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)_{\sigma(j)}\geq 0

according to the claim.

If, vice versa,
\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{\sigma(i)}>\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\mathbf{x}_{k})_{\sigma(j)}
, then

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)_{\sigma(i)}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)_{\sigma(j)}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{\sigma(i)}-\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)_{\sigma(j)}+y_{\sigma(i)}-y_{\sigma(j)}

\displaystyle\geq\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)-\Delta(\mathbf{y})\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\delta\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}\Bigg)-\Delta(\mathbf{y})\geq 0,

where we use the claim in the proof of Lemma
20
and Lemma
20
itself. So, in both cases
(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(i)}\geq(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y})_{\sigma(j)}
. Since
i,j\in[M]
are arbitrary indices satisfying
i<j
, we can conclude that
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y})
.

\supseteq
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k})=\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})
and
\tau\in\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k})
. Then, we can show that
\sigma\mathbf{x}_{k}=\tau\mathbf{x}_{k}
for
k\in[p]
, as in the proof of Lemma

. Therefore,

\sigma\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)=\operatorname{sort}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)=\tau\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)

implies
\sigma\mathbf{y}=\tau\mathbf{y}
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{sort}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\tau\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\tau\mathbf{x}_{k}+\tau\mathbf{y}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\sigma\mathbf{x}_{k}+\sigma\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\sigma\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y}\Bigg)

such that
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbf{x}_{k}+\mathbf{y})
.

The equality for the stabilisers follows immediately.
∎

The proof of Theorem

Throughout this section, we will work with the map

We will start by proving that

Proposition 23

(Lipschitz continuity)
.

Let
K:V\to\mathbb{R}^{MN}
denote the collection of coorbits

Kv=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v\\
\vdots\\
\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v\end{pmatrix},\qquad v\in V,

\lVert K\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}:=\max_{\lVert v\rVert_{V}=1}\lVert Kv\rVert_{2}
denote its operator norm. Then,

\widehat{\gamma}:(V/{\sim},\operatorname{dist})\to(\mathbb{R}^{D},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2})

is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant bounded by
\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\mathrm{F}\to 2}\cdot\lVert K\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
.

Proof.

Somewhat similar to the proofs of
[
3
, Theorem 3.9 on p. 15]
and
[
4
, Lemma 2.3 on p. 5]
, we let
v,w\in V
be arbitrary and estimate

\displaystyle\lVert\gamma(v)-\gamma(w)\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\alpha\left(\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v)-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}w)&\dots&\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v)-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v)\end{pmatrix}\right)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\mathrm{F}\to 2}^{2}\cdot\left\lVert\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v)-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}w)&\dots&\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v)-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v)\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\mathrm{F}\to 2}^{2}\cdot\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lVert\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}v)-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}w)\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\mathrm{F}\to 2}^{2}\cdot\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}(v-w)\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\mathrm{F}\to 2}^{2}\cdot\lVert K(v-w)\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\mathrm{F}\to 2}^{2}\cdot\lVert K\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\cdot\lVert v-w\rVert_{V}^{2}.

Now, let
v,w\in V
and let
g\in G
be such that
\operatorname{dist}(v,w)=\lVert v-gw\rVert_{V}
. Then,

\displaystyle\lVert\widehat{\gamma}(v)-\widehat{\gamma}(w)\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\gamma(v)-\gamma(gw)\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\mathrm{F}\to 2}\cdot\lVert K\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\lVert v-gw\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle=\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\mathrm{F}\to 2}\cdot\lVert K\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\operatorname{dist}(v,w)

as desired.
∎

Remark 24
.

If we choose an orthonormal basis for
V
and express
K
as a matrix with respect to that basis, then
\lVert K\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
coincides with the largest singular value of that matrix.

Lemma 25

(Base case)
.

If
\widehat{\gamma}:(V/{\sim},\operatorname{dist})\to(\mathbb{R}^{D},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2})
is injective and
not
lower Lipschitz continuous, then there exists
z_{1}\in V
,
\lVert z_{1}\rVert_{V}=1
, at which the local lower Lipschitz constant of
\widehat{\gamma}
vanishes; more precisely, there exist sequences
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
,
(w_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in V
such that
v_{i}\not\sim w_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lim_{i\to\infty}v_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}w_{i}=z_{1}
and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i})}=0.

Proof.

Since we assume that
\widehat{\gamma}
is not lower Lipschitz continuous, there exist sequences
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty},(w_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in V
such that
v_{i}\not\sim w_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i})}=0.

The fraction is invariant under the transformation
(v,w)\mapsto r(w,v)
,
r>0
, such that we can assume without loss of generality that
\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{V}\leq\lVert w_{i}\rVert_{V}=1
. Since the unit ball in finite-dimensional vector spaces is compact, we can extract subsequences along which both
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
(w_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
converge. Let us pass to these subsequences and define

f_{1}:=v_{\infty}:=\lim_{i\to\infty}v_{i},\qquad w_{\infty}:=\lim_{i\to\infty}w_{i}.

We may now use the continuity of
\gamma
(which follows because
\widehat{\gamma}
and thus
\gamma
is Lipschitz continuous) to see that

\lVert\gamma(v_{\infty})-\gamma(w_{\infty})\rVert_{2}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}\leq 2\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i})}=0.

Therefore,
\gamma(v_{\infty})=\gamma(w_{\infty})
and the injectivity of
\widehat{\gamma}
implies that
f_{1}=v_{\infty}=gw_{\infty}
for some
g\in G
. It follows immediately that
\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{V}=\lVert w_{\infty}\rVert_{V}=1
. We finally define
w_{i}^{\prime}:=gw_{i}
and note that
v_{i}\not\sim w_{i}^{\prime}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lim_{i\to\infty}w_{i}^{\prime}=f_{1}
and that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i}^{\prime})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i}^{\prime})}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i})}=0.

∎

Before proceeding with the induction step, we prove the following lemma which is similar to the first claim in the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 2.13 on p. 19]
. It will be used in the proof of the induction step and the final theorem.

Lemma 26
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\leq d
, let
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p}\in V\setminus\{0\}
be an orthogonal sequence such that

\forall\ell\in[N],k\in[p-1]:\Delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{k+1})<\delta\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{j}\Bigg).

(4)

Let
F:=\operatorname{sp}(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p}\subseteq V
and assume that the local lower Lipschitz constant of

\widehat{\gamma}|_{F}:(F/{\sim},\operatorname{dist})\to(\mathbb{R}^{D},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2})

vanishes at
f:=f_{1}+f_{2}+\dots+f_{p}
. Then,
\widehat{\gamma}:(V/{\sim},\operatorname{dist})\to(\mathbb{R}^{D},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2})
is
not
injective at
f
.

Proof.

Let
(v_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(w_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in F
be two sequences such that
v_{i}\not\sim w_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lim_{i\to\infty}v_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}w_{i}=f
and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i})}=0.

Expand the sequences into the orthogonal basis
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p}
of
F
:

v_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{ij}f_{j},\qquad w_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}d_{ij}f_{j},

where
c_{ij},d_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
j\in[p]
.

Claim.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we have

\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}v_{i})=\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f)=\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}w_{i}),\qquad\ell\in[N].

Proof of the claim.

We only show the first equality. The second equality follows in the same way: fix
\ell\in[N]
arbitrary and note that with the notation

\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
\lVert v\rVert_{V}=1\end{subarray}}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}v\rVert_{2},

we may estimate

\displaystyle\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{p}(c_{ij}-1)\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{j}\Bigg)=\Bigg\lVert\mathbf{D}\sum_{j=1}^{p}(c_{ij}-1)\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{j}\Bigg\rVert_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}\cdot\Bigg\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{p}(c_{ij}-1)\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{j}\Bigg\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lvert c_{ij}-1\rvert\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{j}\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lvert c_{ij}-1\rvert\lVert f_{j}\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2d}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lvert c_{ij}-1\rvert^{2}\lVert f_{j}\rVert_{V}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2d}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\Bigg\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{p}(c_{ij}-1)f_{j}\Bigg\rVert_{V}=\sqrt{2d}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\lVert v_{i}-f\rVert_{V}.

The right-hand side tends to zero as
i\to\infty
. Therefore, there exists
I_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
i\geq I_{\ell}
,

\Delta\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{p}(c_{ij}-1)\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{j}\Bigg)<\delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f).

According to equation (
4
) (and Lemma
20
), we can conclude that
\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}v_{i})=\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f)
. Setting
I:=\max_{\ell\in[N]}I_{\ell}
allows us to conclude that, for all
i\geq I
and all
\ell\in[N]
,
\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}v_{i})=\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f)
as desired.

Now, let us pick
(\sigma_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{N}\in S_{M}
be such that
\sigma_{\ell}\in\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f)
for
\ell\in[N]
and note that the claim implies that

\displaystyle\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\alpha\left(\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v_{i})-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}w_{i})&\dots&\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v_{i})-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}w_{i})\end{pmatrix}\right)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}(v_{i}-w_{i})&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}(v_{i}-w_{i})\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2},

for
i\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. So, let us define
u_{i}:=(v_{i}-w_{i})/\lVert v_{i}-w_{i}\rVert_{V}
. Since the closed unit ball in
F
is compact, we can find a subsequence along which
u_{i}
converges. Let us pass to this subsequence and denote its limit by
u
. Then, we have
\lVert u\rVert_{V}=1
as well as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}u&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}u\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}u_{i}&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}u_{i}\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}(v_{i}-w_{i})&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}(v_{i}-w_{i})\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}}{\lVert v_{i}-w_{i}\rVert_{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i})}=0

and thus
\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}u&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}u\end{pmatrix}=\boldsymbol{0}_{D}
.

We finally note that there exist arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
such that
f\not\sim f+\epsilon u
: indeed, assume the opposite and we can find a sequence
(\epsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
converging to zero and a group element
g\in G
such that
f=gf+\epsilon_{n}gu
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
because
G
is finite. Therefore,
g
stabilises
f
which implies
\epsilon_{n}gu=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
: a contradiction to
\lVert u\rVert_{V}=1
. So, let us pick such an
\epsilon
which also satisfies

\epsilon<\min_{\ell\in[N]}\frac{\delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f)}{\Delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}u)}.

Then,
\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}(f+\epsilon u))\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f)
for all
\ell\in[N]
according to Lemma
18
and thus picking
(\sigma_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{N}\in S_{M}
such that
\sigma_{\ell}\in\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}(f+\epsilon u))
for all
\ell\in[N]
guarantees that

\displaystyle\gamma(f+\epsilon u)

\displaystyle=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}(f+\epsilon u))&\dots&\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}(f+\epsilon u))\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}(f+\epsilon u)&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}(f+\epsilon u)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}f&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}f\end{pmatrix}+\epsilon\cdot\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}u&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}u\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}f&\dots&\sigma_{N}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}f\end{pmatrix}=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}f)&\dots&\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}f)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\gamma(f);

i.e.
\widehat{\gamma}
is
not
injective at
f
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the induction step (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 2.13 on p. 19]
)

Lemma 27

(Induction step)
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
p<d
, and let
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p}\in V\setminus\{0\}
be an orthogonal sequence such that the local lower Lipschitz constant of
\widehat{\gamma}:(V/{\sim},\operatorname{dist})\to(\mathbb{R}^{D},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2})
vanishes at
f:=f_{1}+f_{2}+\dots+f_{p}
,
\lVert f_{1}\rVert=1
and

\displaystyle\Delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{k+1})<\delta\Bigg(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}\Bigg),

(5)

\displaystyle\lVert f_{k+1}\rVert_{V}<\frac{1}{2}\cdot\min_{g\not\in H(\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{j})}\Bigg\lVert(g-e)\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}\Bigg\rVert_{V},

(6)

for all
k\in[p-1]
,
\ell\in[N]
.

If
\widehat{\gamma}
is injective, then there exists
f_{p+1}\in(\operatorname{sp}(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p})^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
such that the local lower Lipschitz constant of
\widehat{\gamma}
vanishes at
f+f_{p+1}
and

\displaystyle\Delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{p+1})<\delta\Bigg(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}f_{j}\Bigg),

\displaystyle\lVert f_{p+1}\rVert_{V}<\frac{1}{2}\cdot\min_{g\not\in H(\sum_{j=1}^{p}f_{j})}\Bigg\lVert(g-e)\sum_{j=1}^{p}f_{j}\Bigg\rVert_{V},

for all
\ell\in[N]
.

Proof.

Let
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty},(w_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in V
be such that
v_{i}\not\sim w_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lim_{i\to\infty}v_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}w_{i}=f
and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i})}=0.

Let
(\mathbb{v}_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty},(\mathbb{w}_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in(\operatorname{sp}(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p})^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal projections of
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
,
(w_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
onto the orthogonal complement of
\operatorname{sp}(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p}
. We may note that
\mathbb{v}_{i}\neq 0
or
\mathbb{w}_{i}\neq 0
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
large enough: indeed, assume by contradiction that there exists a subsequence along which
\mathbb{v}_{i}=\mathbb{w}_{i}=0
. Then, the local lower Lipschitz constant of
\widehat{\gamma}|_{F}:(F/{\sim},\operatorname{dist})\to(\mathbb{R}^{D},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2})
vanishes at
f
, where
F=\operatorname{sp}(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p}\subseteq V
. Lemma
26
now implies that
\widehat{\gamma}
is
not
injective: a contradiction.

In the following, we assume without loss of generality that
\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}\leq\lVert\mathbb{w}_{i}\rVert_{V}\neq 0
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
by switching the roles of
v_{i}
and
w_{i}
if necessary. Now, write

v_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{v}_{i},\qquad w_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}d_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{w}_{i},

where
c_{ij},d_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
j\in[p]
. Next, we define

u_{i}:=w_{i}-v_{i}+\mathbb{v}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}(d_{ij}-c_{ij})f_{j}+\mathbb{w}_{i},\qquad i\in\mathbb{N},

and note that
\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V}\geq\lVert\mathbb{w}_{i}\rVert_{V}>0
by the orthogonality of
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{p}
and
\mathbb{w}_{i}
. Finally, set

t_{i}:=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V}}\cdot\min\left\{\min_{\ell\in[N]}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}\delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f),\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\cdot\min_{g\not\in H(f)}\lVert(g-e)f\rVert_{V}\right\}

for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\epsilon<1
and
\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\max_{\lVert v\rVert_{V}=1}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}v\rVert_{2}
denotes the operator norm of the coorbit
\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
for
\ell\in[N]
.

We note that

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}^{2}\leq\Bigg(\lim_{i\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lvert c_{ij}-1\rvert^{2}\lVert f_{j}\rVert_{V}^{2}+\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}^{2}\Bigg)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lVert v_{i}-f\rVert_{V}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\lVert v_{i}-w_{i}\rVert_{V}+\lim_{i\to\infty}\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}=0.

It follows that
t_{i}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
. So, let us assume that
t_{i}>1
for the rest of this proof. Finally, note that
\lVert t_{i}u_{i}\rVert_{V}
is constant in
i\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that
t_{i}u_{i}
converges along a subsequence. Similarly,
\lVert t_{i}\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}=t_{i}\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}\leq t_{i}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V}=\lVert t_{i}u_{i}\rVert_{V}
is upper bounded by a constant in
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t_{i}\mathbb{v}_{i}
converges along a subsequence as well. Passing to these subsequences, we may write
t_{i}u_{i}\to u\neq 0
and
t_{i}\mathbb{v}_{i}\to f_{p+1}
. Note that
\lVert f_{p+1}\rVert_{V}=\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{i}\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{i}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V}=\lVert u\rVert_{V}
.

Claim 1.

The sequences

v_{i}^{\prime}=f+t_{i}\mathbb{v}_{i},\qquad w_{i}^{\prime}=f+t_{i}u_{i},\qquad i\in\mathbb{N},

achieve lower Lipschitz constant zero.

Proof of Claim 1.

The claim is proven in two steps. In the first step, we bound the denominator
\operatorname{dist}(v_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime})
: consider
(g_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\in G
such that
\operatorname{dist}(v_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime})=\lVert g_{i}v_{i}^{\prime}-w_{i}^{\prime}\rVert_{V}
. Since
G
is finite, there exists a group element
g\in G
which occurs infinitely often. Let us pass to a subsequence along which this is the case. We claim that
g\in H(f)
: indeed, let
h\not\in H(f)
be arbitrary. Then, we have

\displaystyle\lVert hv_{i}^{\prime}-w_{i}^{\prime}\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle=\lVert(h-e)f+t_{i}(h\mathbb{v}_{i}-u_{i})\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle\geq\lVert(h-e)f\rVert_{V}-t_{i}\lVert h\mathbb{v}_{i}-u_{i}\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{h\not\in H(f)}\lVert(h-e)f\rVert_{V}-t_{i}(\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}+\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V})

\displaystyle\geq\min_{h\not\in H(f)}\lVert(h-e)f\rVert_{V}-2t_{i}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle>2t_{i}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V}\geq t_{i}\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}-u_{i}\rVert_{V}=\lVert v_{i}^{\prime}-w_{i}^{\prime}\rVert_{V}\geq\operatorname{dist}(v_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime}),

by the definition of
t_{i}
. So,
g\in H(f)
which implies that

\operatorname{dist}(v_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime})=t_{i}\lVert g\mathbb{v}_{i}-u_{i}\rVert_{V}=t_{i}\Bigg\lVert g\mathbb{v}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}(d_{ij}-c_{ij})f_{j}-\mathbb{w}_{i}\Bigg\rVert_{V}.

Now, we would like to use that
H(f)\subseteq H(\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}f_{j})\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathcal{H}(f_{1})
which follows from Lemma
22
by inequality (
6
). Therefore,
g\in H(f_{j})
for all
j\in[p]
and we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(v_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=t_{i}\Bigg\lVert g\mathbb{v}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}(d_{ij}-c_{ij})f_{j}-\mathbb{w}_{i}\Bigg\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle=t_{i}\Bigg\lVert g\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{v}_{i}\Bigg)-\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{p}d_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{w}_{i}\Bigg)\Bigg\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle=t_{i}\lVert gv_{i}-w_{i}\rVert_{V}\geq t_{i}\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i}).

In the second step, we consider the numerator
\lVert\gamma(v_{i}^{\prime})-\gamma(w_{i}^{\prime})\rVert_{2}
. For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell\in[N]
, pick arbitrary
\sigma_{i\ell}\in\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}v_{i})
and
\tau_{i\ell}\in\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}w_{i})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}

(7)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v_{i})-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}w_{i})&\dots&\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v_{i})-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}w_{i})\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v_{i}-\tau_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}w_{i}&\dots&\sigma_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v_{i}-\tau_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{1}N}w_{i}\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\alpha\left(\begin{matrix}\sigma_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{v}_{i}\right)-\tau_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}d_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{w}_{i}\right)\end{matrix}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\begin{matrix}\dots&\sigma_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{v}_{i}\right)-\tau_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}d_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{w}_{i}\right)\end{matrix}\right)\right\rVert_{2}.

Next, we want to use that

Proof of Claim 1 (continued).

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\left.\begin{matrix}\dots&\sigma_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{v}_{i}\right)-\tau_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}d_{ij}f_{j}+\mathbb{w}_{i}\right)\end{matrix}\right)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\left.\begin{matrix}\dots&\sigma_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}\mathbb{v}_{i}-\tau_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}(d_{ij}-c_{ij})f_{j}+\mathbb{w}_{i}\right)\end{matrix}\right)\right\rVert_{2}.

Next, we may use that
\sigma_{i\ell},\tau_{i\ell}\in\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f)
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\ell\in[N]
to see that

\displaystyle t_{i}\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}=t_{i}\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}\mathbb{v}_{i}-\tau_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}u_{i}&\dots&\sigma_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}\mathbb{v}_{i}-\tau_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}u_{i}\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}

(11)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\alpha\left(\begin{matrix}\sigma_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}(f+t_{i}\mathbb{v}_{i})-\tau_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}(f+t_{i}u_{i})\end{matrix}\right.\right.

\displaystyle t_{i}\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v_{i}^{\prime}-\tau_{i1}\kappa_{\phi_{1}}w_{i}^{\prime}&\dots&\sigma_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v_{i}^{\prime}-\tau_{iN}\kappa_{\phi_{N}}w_{i}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}v_{i}^{\prime})-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{1}}w_{i}^{\prime})&\dots&\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}v_{i}^{\prime})-\operatorname{sort}(\kappa_{\phi_{N}}w_{i}^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\gamma(v_{i}^{\prime})-\gamma(w_{i}^{\prime})\rVert_{2}.

We finally conclude that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i}^{\prime})-\gamma(w_{i}^{\prime})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime})}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i})-\gamma(w_{i})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i},w_{i})}=0.

and

and

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lVert\gamma(v_{i}^{\prime})-\gamma(w_{i}^{\prime})\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\lVert\gamma(v_{i}^{\prime})-\gamma(w_{i}^{\prime})\rVert_{2}}{\operatorname{dist}(v_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime})}=0.

; i.e., for some

. Then, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lVert g(f+f_{p+1})-(f+u)\rVert_{V}\geq\lVert(g-e)f\rVert_{V}-\lVert gf_{p+1}-u\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{g\not\in H(f)}\lVert(g-e)f\rVert_{V}-\lVert f_{p+1}\rVert_{V}-\lVert u\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{g\not\in H(f)}\lVert(g-e)f\rVert_{V}-2\lVert u\rVert_{V}>0

. Finally, let

. Then, the sequences
(v_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{\infty}

is orthogonal to

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{i}\lVert\mathbb{v}_{i}\rVert_{V}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{i}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\min\left\{\min_{\ell\in[N]}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}\delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f),\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\cdot\min_{g\not\in H(f)}\lVert(g-e)f\rVert_{V}\right\}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{p+1})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\cdot\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f_{p+1}\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\lVert f_{p+1}\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle<\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\min_{k\in[N]}\lVert\kappa_{\phi_{k}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}\delta(\kappa_{\phi_{k}}f)\leq\delta(\kappa_{\phi_{\ell}}f)

and the lemma is proven.
∎

1.
Introduction

The study of the dynamics of continuous linear operators on infinite-dimensional Banach spaces has been the subject of study by several researchers in recent decades and has undergone great development (for example, see
[
2
,
11
,
12
]
). The notions of
expansivity
and
positive shadowing property
play important roles in dynamical systems, including topological dynamics, differentiable dynamics and ergodic theory, see
[
15
,
16
]
, for instance.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a space of functions on a subset
\Omega
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}.
A
composition operator

C_{\phi}
on
\mathcal{S}
is an operator acting by composition to the right with a chosen self-map
\phi
of
\Omega
, that is

C_{\phi}f=f\circ\phi,\quad f\in\mathcal{S}.

Linear dynamics of composition operators has been studied on several holomorphic function spaces
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
10
]
. In
[
1
]
, Álvarez and Henríquez-Amador studied the affine composition operators on the Hardy space of the open right half-plane
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, that is, the bounded composition operators induced by symbols of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\phi(w)=aw+b,\text{ where }a>0\text{ and }{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0,

and completely characterized those operators that are uniformly expansive, positive expansive, uniformly positive expansive, Li-Yorke chaotic and have the positive shadowing property. In the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 7]
the authors use the argument that the reproducing kernels are dense in
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, however this claim is false, and as a consequence of this fact theys conclude that if
\phi
is as in (
1.1
) then
C_{\phi}
is uniformly positive expansive on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
a\in(0,1).
As we will see in Proposition
3.6
, although the proof
[
1
, Theorem 7]
is wrong, its result holds.

Our goal in this work is to characterize completely the affine composition operators on the weighted Bergman space of the open right half-plane
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),
for
\alpha>-1,
that are expansive, uniformly expansive, positive expansive, uniformly positive expansive, Li-Yorke chaotic and has positive shadowing property. Since
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
can often be formally interpreted as the “limit case” of the weighted Bergman spaces as
\alpha\rightarrow-1,
that is,
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})=\mathcal{A}_{-1}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
(see
[
17
, page 819]
) our results can be seen as extensions of those proved in
[
1
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the work we will use the following notations:
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of all positive integers,
\mathbb{R}_{+}
the non-negative real numbers and
\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Re}\,(b)>0\}
the open right half-plane. Moreover,
\mathcal{B}(X)
denotes the space of all bounded linear operators on a complex Banach space
X.

2.1.
The weighted Bergman space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})

For
\alpha>-1,
the
weighted Bergman space

\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
consists of all analytic functions
f:\mathbb{C}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
for which

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|f\|=\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x+\mathrm{i}y)|^{2}x^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

The space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space when endowed with the norm defined in (
2.1
). The functions
\{k_{w}^{\alpha}:w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}\}
defined by

\displaystyle k_{w}^{\alpha}(z)=\frac{2^{\alpha}(\alpha+1)}{(z+\overline{w})^{\alpha+2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

are the reproducing kernels for
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
These kernel functions have the fundamental property
\langle f,k_{w}\rangle=f(w)
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.
Such spaces are studied by Ellitot and Wynn in
[
9
]
.

Let
\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{2^{\alpha}t^{\alpha+1}}\mathrm{d}t,
where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function and
\mathrm{d}t
the Lebesgue measure. According to
[
9
, Lem. 3.3]
, the space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is isometrically isomorphic, via the Laplace
transform
\mathcal{L},
to the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mu_{\alpha}).
That is,
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if there exists a function
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mu_{\alpha})
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle f(w)=(\mathcal{L}F)(w)=\int^{\infty}_{0}F(t)e^{-wt}\mathrm{d}t,\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

2.2.
Affine composition operators

In
[
9
, Theorem 3.4]
, Elliot and Wynn showed that if
\phi
is an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+},
then
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
\phi
has a finite angular derivative at the fixed point
\infty,
that is, if
\phi(\infty)=\infty
and if the non-tangential limit

(2.3)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(\infty)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{w}{\phi(w)}

exists and is finite. In this case, the norm of
C_{\phi}
is given by
\|C_{\phi}\|=\phi^{\prime}(\infty)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}.
Moreover, if
C_{\phi}
is a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),
then
C_{\phi}^{*}
acts on reproducing kernel as follows
C_{\phi}^{*}k_{w}=k_{\phi(w)},
for each
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Since the only linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
that fix
\infty
are precisely of affine maps of the form

(2.4)

\displaystyle\phi(w)=aw+b,

where
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0,
it follows from boundedness criterion of composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
that the only bounded composition operator induced by linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
are those induced by symbols as in (
2.4
). For this reason, in this work, we will call such operators of affine composition operators. A simple computation reveals that if
\phi
is as in (
2.4
) then
n
-iterate of
\phi
is given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\phi^{[n]}(w)=\begin{cases}w+nb,&\text{if}\ a=1,\\
a^{n}w+\frac{\left(1-a^{n}\right)}{1-a}b,&\text{if}\ a\neq 1\end{cases}

and
C_{\phi}^{n}=\mathbb{C}_{\phi^{[n]}}.
The only invertible bounded composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
are those induced by symbols as in (
2.4
) where
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0.
According to
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
, the spectrum of affine composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
are given as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\phi(w)=w+b
with
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0
and
b\neq 0.
Then the spectrum of
C_{\phi}
acting on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is

(i)

\sigma(C_{\phi})=\mathbb{T},
when
b\in\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R};

(ii)

\sigma(C_{\phi})=\left\{e^{\mathrm{i}bt}:t\in[0,+\infty)\}\cup\{0\right\}.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
where
a\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0.
Then the spectrum of
C_{\phi}
acting on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is

(i)

\sigma(C_{\phi})=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|=a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)}\right\},
when
b\in\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R};

(ii)

\sigma(C_{\phi})=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|\leq a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)}\right\},
when
b\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

2.3.
Linear dynamics

Let us introduce the notions relevant to our work. Such notions will be studied in subsequent section.

Definition 2.3
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is Li-Yorke chaotic if there exists
x\in X
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=\infty.

In this case, such vector
x
is called an irregular vector for
T.

It is immediate that isometric operators does not admit irregular vectors, and hence they are not Li-Yorke chaotic.

Definition 2.4
.

An invertible operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is:

(i)

Expansive if for each
x\in X
with
\|x\|=1,
there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\|T^{n}x\|\geq 2.

(ii)

Uniformly expansive, if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
x\in X
with
\|x\|=1,
we have
\|T^{n}x\|\geq 2
or
\|T^{-n}x\|\geq 2.

If
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is not necessarily invertible, it is defined the notions of
positively expansive
and
uniformly positively expansive
, in this case we use the appropriate modifications, putting
\mathbb{N}
instead of
\mathbb{Z}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
and
\delta>0.
A sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
X
is said to be a positive
\delta-
pseudo orbit of
T
if
\|Tx_{n}-x_{n+1}\|\leq\delta
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

If
x\in X
is such that
Tx\neq 0,
then the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle x_{n}=\frac{\delta}{\|Tx\|}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}T^{n-j}x

is a positive
\delta-
pseudo orbit of
T.
Indeed

\displaystyle\|Tx_{n}-x_{n+1}\|=\frac{\delta}{\|Tx\|}\|\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}T^{n-j+1}x-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}T^{n-j}x\|=\frac{\delta}{\|Tx\|}\|Tx\|=\delta.

Definition 2.6
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is said to have the positive shadowing property if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that every positive
\delta-
pseudo orbit
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
T
is
\epsilon-
shadowed, i.e., there exists
x\in X
such that
\|T^{n}x-x_{n}\|\leq\epsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

In
[
6
]
, Bernardes, Bonilla and Peris studied mean Li-Yorke chaos in Banach space, in particular there is presented a notion of
absolutely Cesàro bounded
which we introduce now.

Definition 2.7
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is said to be absolutely Cesàro bounded if there exists a constant
M>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T^{j}x\|\right\}\leq M\|x\|,

for all
x\in X.

3.
Main results

We begin by showing that affine composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
do not admit irregular vectors, and hence that such operators are not Li-Yorke chaotic. Before proceeding, we need the following auxiliary results, which establish some estimates that will be used frequently.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0.
For
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
we have

(i)

\|C_{\phi}f\|=a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)}\|f\|,
when
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0;

(ii)

\|C_{\phi}f\|\leq a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)}\|f\|.

The proof of the proposition above follows from straightforward computations and it is
left to the reader.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a\in(0,1)
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0.
For each
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq\delta a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\|f\|

for sufficiently large
n.

Proof.

For each
n,
we consider the auxiliary symbols
\varphi_{n}
and
\psi_{n}
defined as follow

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(w)=a^{n}w\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{n}(w)=w+\frac{1-a^{n}}{1-a}b

then
\phi^{[n]}=\psi_{n}\circ\varphi_{n}.
Let
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
be a non-zero vector and let
g=\|f\|^{-1}f.
By Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}g\|=\|C_{\varphi_{n}}C_{\psi_{n}}g\|=a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\|C_{\psi_{n}}g\|.

Now we obtain a lower estimate for
\|C_{\psi_{n}}g\|.
For each
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+},
the Cauchy Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\|C_{\psi_{n}}g\|\|k_{w}\|\geq|\langle C_{\psi_{n}}g,k_{w}\rangle|=|\langle g,C_{\psi_{n}}^{*}k_{w}\rangle|=|\langle g,k_{\psi_{n}(w)}\rangle|=|g(\psi_{n}(w))|

for all
n.
Since
\psi_{n}(w)\rightarrow w+\frac{b}{1-a}
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
it follows that
|g(\psi_{n}(w))|\rightarrow|g(w+\frac{b}{1-a})|
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that
g(z_{1})\neq 0,
where
z_{1}=z_{0}+\frac{b}{1-a}.
Then for sufficiently large
n,
we get

|g(\psi_{n}(z_{0}))|>\frac{|g(z_{1})|}{2}.
If
\delta=\frac{|g(z_{1})|}{2\|k_{z_{0}}\|},
we obtain

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|>\delta a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\|f\|

for sufficiently large
n.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0
, then
C_{\phi}
does not admit an irregular vector on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
be a non-zero vector. By Proposition
3.1
,
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\leq a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\|f\|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
If
a\geq 1,
then
(\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded and hence
f
is not an irregular vector for
C_{\phi}
. If
a\in(0,1)
then Lemma
3.2
give
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq\delta a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\|f\|
for sufficiently large
n.
Since
a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
it follows that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq 2
for sufficiently large
n.
In this case,
\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|\geq 2
and hence
f
is not an irregular vector for
C_{\phi}
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0
, then
C_{\phi}
is not Li-Yorke chaotic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Now we deal with the notions of expansivity.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0.
Then the following statements are equivalent

(i)

C_{\phi}
is uniformly expansive on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+});

(ii)

C_{\phi}
is expansive on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+});

(iii)

a\neq 1.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii) Follows from the definitions. (ii)
\implies
(iii) Now assume
a=1.
Since
a=1
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0,
it follows from Proposition
3.7
that
C_{\phi}
is a unitary operator and hence
C_{\phi}
is not expansive. (iii)
\implies
(i) By Proposition
3.1
, we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|=a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\|f\|\ \quad\text{and}\quad\|(C_{\phi}^{-1})^{n}f\|=a^{\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\|f\|,

for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}.
If
a\in(0,1),
then there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)j}\geq 2,
while if
a\in(1,+\infty),
then there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a^{\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)\ell}\geq 2.
By (
3.1
), for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
with
\|f\|=1
we obtain
\|C_{\phi}^{j}f\|\geq 2
or
\|(C_{\phi}^{-1})^{\ell}f\|\geq 2.
Hence
C_{\phi}
is uniformly expansive.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0
. Then the following statements are equivalent

(i)

C_{\phi}
is uniformly positive expansive on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+});

(ii)

C_{\phi}
is positive expansive on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+});

(iii)

a\in(0,1).

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii) Follows from the definitions. (ii)
\implies
(iii) Assume
a\geq 1
then Proposition
3.1
gives
\|C^{n}_{\phi}f\|\leq a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)n}\leq 1
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
with
\|f\|=1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Hence
C_{\phi}
is not positive expansive. (iii)
\implies
(i) Let
a\in(0,1).
Suppose that
C_{\phi}
is not uniformly positive expansive to reach a contradiction. By the assumption, we can find
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
with
\|f\|=1
such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|<2
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
On the other hand, since
a\in(0,1),
it follows from Lemma
3.2
that we can find
n
such that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}f\|>2.
This contradiction implies that
C_{\phi}
must be uniformly positive expansive.
∎

Recall that an operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is said be
hyperbolic
if
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{T}.
Bernardes
et al.
studied expansivity and shadowing in linear dynamics
[
4
]
. They proved the following results for
T\in\mathcal{B}(X):

•

If
T
is hyperbolic then
T
has the positive shadowing property
[
4
, Theorem 13]
.

•

If
T
is normal (here we assume
X
a Hilbert space), then
T
has the positive shadowing property if and only if
T
is hyperbolic
[
4
, Theorem 30]
.

In order to use
[
4
, Theorem 30]
, we first classify the affine composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
that are normal. The version of Proposition
3.7
for
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
when
\alpha
is an positive integers appears in
[
14
, Theorem 8.7]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0.
Then
C_{\phi}
is normal on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
a=1
or
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0.

Proof.

We first will prove that
C_{\phi}
is unitarily equivalent via the Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}
to the operator
\widehat{C}_{\phi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mu_{\alpha})
defined by
(\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)(t)=\frac{1}{a}e^{-bt/a}F(t/a).
For
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mu_{\alpha})
and
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+},
the Laplace transform gives

\displaystyle(C_{\phi}\mathcal{L}F)(w)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{L}F)(\phi(w))=\int^{\infty}_{0}F(t)e^{-(aw+b)t}\mathrm{d}t=\int^{\infty}_{0}F(t)e^{-awt}e^{-bt}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a}e^{-bs/a}F\left(s/a\right)e^{-ws}\mathrm{d}s=\int^{\infty}_{0}(\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)(s)e^{-ws}\mathrm{d}s=(\mathcal{L}\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)(w),

hence
C_{\phi}\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}\widehat{C}_{\phi}.
Moreover, for
F,G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mu_{\alpha}),
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha+1}}{2\pi\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\langle\widehat{C}_{\phi}F,G\rangle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{a}e^{-bt/a}F(t/a)\overline{G(t)}t^{-(\alpha+1)}\mathrm{d}t=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-bs}F(s)\overline{G(as)}(as)^{-(\alpha+1)}\mathrm{d}s

which gives
(\widehat{C}_{\phi}^{*}F)(t)=a^{-(\alpha+1)}e^{-\overline{b}t}F(at).

Since
C_{\phi}
and
\widehat{C}_{\phi}
are unitarily equivalent it is enough to check when
\widehat{C}_{\phi}
is normal. From what we have done so far, we get

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\widehat{C}_{\phi}\widehat{C}_{\phi}^{*}F)(t)=a^{-(\alpha+2)}e^{-2{\rm Re}\,(b)t/\mu}F(t)\quad\text{and}\quad(\widehat{C}_{\phi}^{*}\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)=a^{-(\alpha+2)}e^{-{\rm Re}\,(b)t}F(t)

for each
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mu_{\alpha}).
Hence,
\widehat{C}_{\phi}
is normal if and only if
e^{-2{\rm Re}\,(b)t/a}=e^{-2{\rm Re}\,(b)t}.
This last equality holds if and only if
a=1
or
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0.

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
C_{\phi}
be a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
If
\phi
has a fixed point on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
then
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0.
Then
C_{\phi}
has the positive shadowing property on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if one of the following cases occurs:

(i)

a\in(0,1)
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0;

(ii)

a>1.

Proof.

We first consider
a=1.
In this case,
C_{\phi}
is normal on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
(see Proposition
3.7
) and is not hyperbolic (see Theorem
2.1
). By
[
4
, Theorem 30.]
,
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property. Now let
a\in(0,1).
In addition, if
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0
then
C_{\phi}
is normal on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and is hyperbolic (see Theorem

). By

, Theorem 30.]
,
C_{\phi}

then
\phi

By Lemma

We finished this work by classifying the affine composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
that are absolutely Cesàro bounded.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0
. Then
C_{\phi}
is an absolutely Cesàro bounded operator on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
a\geq 1.

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
c=a^{-\left(\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\right)}
. By Proposition
3.1
,
\|C^{j}_{\phi}f\|\leq c^{j}\|f\|
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and
j\in\mathbb{N}.
If
a=1
we have
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|C_{\phi}^{j}f\|\leq n\|f\|
and if
a>1
then
(c^{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a convergent geometric series. Hence, taking
M\geq\max\{1,c/(1-c)\},
we obtain

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|C^{j}_{\phi}f\|\right\}\ \leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c^{j}\right\}\|f\|\leq M\|f\|,

for each
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
If
a\in(0,1),
then the sequence
(c^{n}/n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded. By Lemma
3.2
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|C^{n}_{\phi}k_{1}\|\geq c^{n}\delta\|k_{1}\|
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Taking sufficiently large
n,
we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|C^{j}_{\phi}k_{1}\|\geq\frac{1}{n}\|C_{\phi}^{n}k_{1}\|\geq\delta\frac{c^{n}}{n}\|k_{1}\|

which implies

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|C^{j}_{\phi}k_{1}\|\right\}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\frac{c^{n}}{n}\right\}\|k_{1}\|=\infty.

Therefore,
C_{\phi}
is not an absolutely Cesàro bounded operator on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

∎

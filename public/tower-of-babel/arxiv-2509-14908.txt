Finite Volumes for a dissipative free boundary problem
Clément Cancès, Claire Chainais-Hillairet, Amélie Dupouy

Abstract
. We study a toy model for the evolution of the oxygen concentration in an oxide layer. It consists in a transient convection diffusion equation in a one-dimensional domain of variable width. The motions of the boundaries are governed by the traces of the concentration. We exhibit a necessary and sufficient condition on the parameters involved in the model for the existence of a unique traveling-wave solution. Moreover, we show that the model admits some universal entropy structure, in the sense that any convex function of the concentration yields a dissipated free energy (up to exchanges with the outer environment at the boundaries). We propose then an implicit in time arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian finite volume scheme based on Scharfetter-Gummel fluxes. It is shown to be unconditionally convergent, to preserve exactly the travelling wave, and to dissipate all the aforementioned free energies. Numerical experiments show that our scheme is first order accurate in time and second order in space, and that the transient solution converges in the long-time limit towards the traveling-wave solution.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and context

The so-called
Diffusion Poisson Coupled Model
(DPCM) introduced in
[
4
]
aims at understanding finely the dynamics of an oxide (magnetite) layer at the
surface of iron canisters to be stored in deep underground nuclear waste repositories. Because of its complexity –3 different charged species evolving in a free boundary domain with retroaction of a self-consistent electric potential– and of its lack of mathematical structure –no Lyapunov functional could be identified so-far–, no satisfying well-posedness was obtained so far on the full model. Moreover, numerical experiments presented in
[
4
,
5
]
show that, at least for particular choices of the parameters, the transient system converges in the long-time limit towards a travelling wave profile.

Global in time existence results in the simpler case where the boundaries are fixed are provided in
[
15
,
10
]
, while the existence of a travelling wave profile is shown in
[
14
]
assuming the electroneutrality of the oxide. The existence of a travelling wave accounting for the coupling with a self-consistent electric field is also rigorously established in
[
7
]
thanks to computer assisted proof technics, but only for some particular set of parameters.

In this paper we study a simplified model with only one chemical species and where electrical interactions are neglected. The goal there is to take advantage of the apparent simplicity of the model to answer some questions that were out of reach in more complex models. Is the existence of a travelling wave profile automatic, or are there some required conditions on the parameters to ensure the existence of such a particular solution? Can we extract a Lyapunov functional providing enough control on the problem so that we can rigorously prove the existence of a solution to the system? Does the travelling wave profile act as an attractor in the long-time limit?
Can one design a numerical scheme that preserves at the discrete level the key features of the continuous problem? And can one rigorously establish the convergence of the scheme?

We show in what follows that, even in our simplified setting, conditions are required on the parameters to make the existence of a travelling wave profile possible.
In our greatly simplified context, we even get a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of the travelling wave profile, for which we derive an explicit expression.

Besides, the problem under study appears to have an infinite number of free energies, which all dissipate up to some exchange terms with the outer environment.
Any evolution of the solution profile or of the interface location generate an entropy production, and even more: the evolution is driven by the dissipation of the (infinite family of) free energies. In mathematical words, the system we consider here is a generalized gradient flow, in the sense of
[
30
]
, and more precisely a port-gradient system
[
31
]
since there are exchanges between the oxide and its surrounding environment.
From a thermodynamical perspective, the fluxes are driven by the variations of the so-called chemical potentials as in
[
10
]
, whereas the motions of the interfaces are governed by the
pressure (or equivalently by the grand potential), in good accordance with the literature
[
32
,
11
,
29
]
. Excepted for a very particular choice of the parameters, the system cannot reach an equilibrium, in opposition to the closely related configurations studied in
[
33
]
or
[
9
]
. As the numerical evidences show, the system rather converges towards a travelling wave profile.
Such travelling waves for convection diffusion problems are also of great interest in other contexts, like for instance in models for cell motility
[
34
,
1
]
.

In order to approximate the solutions to the continuous problem, we introduce an implicit in time arbitrary Lagrangian Eulerian (ALE) finite volume scheme (see for instance
[
19
]
). In our one-dimensional context, this approach is equivalent to the one proposed in
[
5
,
16
]
, where a change of variable is introduced to formulate the problem on the fixed spatial domain
(0,1)
.
Even after this change of variable, the continuous problem remains of convection diffusion type. We employ Scharfetter-Gummel type fluxes
[
35
,
27
,
17
]
. These fluxes exactly capture the travelling wave, and dissipate all the convex free energies of the system. Building on these strong properties, we
establish the convergence of the scheme towards a weak solution thanks to compactness arguments.

We highlight in numerical experiments that the scheme is second order accurate in space and first order in time as expected. Furthermore, we observe that, in the long-time asymptotic regime, the solution to the problem converges exponentially fast towards the travelling wave profile (provided that the latter exists). Unfortunately, we have been unable to rigorously prove this result so far. Indeed, the port-gradient nature of the problem makes usual approaches such as
[
22
,
26
,
3
,
24
,
25
,
6
]
fail in our case.
This will be the purpose of future work.

Presentation of the numerical scheme

, the value of which will be discussed later on.
We also introduce a mesh of

\xi_{i}=\frac{\xi_{i-\frac{1}{2}}+\xi_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}{2}\textnormal{ for }1\leq i\leq I,\qquad\xi_{0}=\xi_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{I+1}=\xi_{I+\frac{1}{2}}.

The distance between two consecutive cell centers

For notation convenience, we consider uniform time discretization.
The time step is denoted

Based on the

2.1.
Travelling wave profile

The existence of a particular solution, that can be seen as a non-equilibrium steady state (as in
[

[

]

[

]
) . In the case of the model (

Definition 1
.

A triplet
(u,X_{0},X_{1})
is called a travelling wave solution to (

\hat{u}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, a velocity
\hat{c}\in\mathbb{R}
and a width
\hat{L}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that for all

(10)

X_{0}^{\prime}(t)=X_{1}^{\prime}(t)=\hat{c}\textnormal{ and }X_{1}(t)-X_{0}(t)=\hat{L},

and for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}(X_{0}(t),X_{1}(t))

(11)

u(t,x)=\hat{u}(x-\hat{c}t).

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that the set of parameters satisfies either

(12)

\frac{\alpha_{0}+R\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{0}+R\beta_{1}}<\frac{a}{b}<\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}},

or

(13)

\frac{\alpha_{0}+R\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{0}+R\beta_{1}}>\frac{a}{b}>\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}.

Then, there exists a unique travelling wave solution to (

(14a)

\displaystyle\hat{u}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b}e^{-R\hat{c}y}\>\textit{ for }y\in[0,\hat{L}],

(14b)

\displaystyle\hat{c}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{R}\left(\alpha_{0}-\beta_{0}\dfrac{a}{b}\right),

(14c)

\displaystyle\hat{L}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{R\hat{c}}\log\left(\dfrac{b}{\beta_{1}a}\left(\alpha_{1}+\hat{c}\right)\right).

Under assumption

(15)

\frac{a}{b}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}},

there exists an infinite number of constant steady solutions with
\hat{c}=0
,
\hat{u}(y)=\frac{a}{b}

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a solution
(u,X_{0},X_{1})
that satisfies (
10
)-(

). The equation (
1a
) can then be rewritten in terms of

as

-\partial_{y}^{2}\hat{u}-R\hat{c}\partial_{y}\hat{u}=\partial_{y}\hat{\mathcal{F}}=0,

where we have set
\hat{\mathcal{F}}=-\partial_{y}\hat{u}-R\hat{c}\,\hat{u}
.
Since the boundary condition (
1d
) at
X_{1}
gives
\hat{\mathcal{F}}(X_{1}(t))=0
for any

, it leads to
\hat{\mathcal{F}}=0
.
We further deduce from the other boundary condition (
1c
) that

\hat{u}(0)=\dfrac{a}{b}.

Hence, the travelling wave profile is given by (
14a
).

Plugging this expression in (
1e
) gives exactly the relation (
14b
) for
\hat{c}
. We note that the sign of
\hat{c}
coincides with the sign of
\dfrac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}-\dfrac{a}{b}
.

Assume first that
\hat{c}=0
, or equivalently that

\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}
, then we deduce from (

\hat{u}(y)=\dfrac{a}{b}

Assume now that

\dfrac{b(\alpha_{1}+\hat{c})}{a\,\beta_{1}}=e^{-R\hat{c}\hat{L}}.

Similarly, in the case

2.2.
Energy estimates

In order to obtain some a priori estimates on the function
u
solution to (
1

there are exchanges of mass and of volume with the outer environments, the free energy is not decreasing along time. To circumvent this difficulty, we
follow the path proposed in
[
10
]
as
we introduce in the following definition what we refer to as the total free energy, encompassing contributions from the outer environments.

Definition 2
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be an arbitrary convex function. The free energy
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
associated to
\phi
corresponding to the state
(u(t),X_{0}(t),X_{1}(t))
is defined by

(16)

\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)=\int_{X_{0}(t)}^{X_{1}(t)}\phi(u(t,x)){\text{d}x},\qquad t\geq 0.

Moreover, defining the pressure function
\pi_{\phi}
by
\pi_{\phi}(r)=r\phi^{\prime}(r)-\phi(r)
for
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the total free energy associated to
\phi
is defined as

(17)

\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{\text{tot}}(t)=\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)+R\pi_{\phi}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\right)(X_{1}(t)-X_{1}(0))\\
-\pi_{\phi}\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}(\alpha_{0}-\beta_{0}u(t,X_{0}(t))-\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\int_{0}^{t}(a-bu(t,X_{0}(t))).

Remark 2.3
.

Since
\pi_{\phi}^{\prime}(r)=r\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)
with
\phi
convex,
\pi_{\phi}
is non-decreasing over
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

The following proposition is proved with formal computations, assuming that
u\geq 0
,
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
exist and are regular enough.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that there exists a solution
(u,X_{0},X_{1})
to the system (
1
) such that
u
is non-negative and
X_{1}-X_{0}
positive and let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, there exists a non-negative function
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
such that, for any
t>0
,

\dfrac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{\text{tot}}(t)=-\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(t)\leq 0.

Proof.

Let
t>0
. Compute the derivative of
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)
and use the equation (
1a
) to get

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)=-\int_{X_{0}(t)}^{X_{1}(t)}\phi^{\prime}(u)\partial_{x}J+X^{\prime}_{1}(t)\phi(u(t,X_{1}(t)))-X^{\prime}_{0}(t)\phi(u(t,X_{0}(t))).

Integrating by parts and using the relation between the fluxes
J
and
\mathcal{F}
at the boundaries (
2
) yields

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)=-\int_{X_{0}(t)}^{X_{1}(t)}\lvert\partial_{x}u\rvert^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)-RX^{\prime}_{1}(t)\pi_{\phi}(u(t,X_{1}(t)))\\
+(X^{\prime}_{0}(t)-(1-R)X^{\prime}_{1}(t))\pi_{\phi}(u(t,X_{0}(t)))+\mathcal{F}_{0}\phi^{\prime}(u(t,X_{0}(t))).

Then, using the equations on the moving boundaries (
1e
)-(
1f
), one gets that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{\text{tot}}(t)=-\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(t),

where the dissipation term
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(t)
is

\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(t)=\int_{X_{0}(t)}^{X_{1}(t)}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)-R(\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1}u(t,X_{1}(t)))\left(\pi_{\phi}(u(t,X_{1}(t)))-\pi_{\phi}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\right)\right)\\
-(\alpha_{0}-\beta_{0}u(t,X_{0}(t)))\left(\pi_{\phi}(u(t,X_{0}(t)))-\pi_{\phi}\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\right)\right)\\
-(a-bu(t,X_{0}(t)))\left(\phi^{\prime}(u(t,X_{0}(t)))-\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\right).

Since
\pi_{\phi}
and
\phi^{\prime}
are non-decreasing over
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
(see Remark
2.3
), it easily comes that all the terms in
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}(t)
are non-negative, and so is
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
.
∎

This result gives various a priori bounds on any solution to the problem. The following proposition presents the a priori
L^{\infty}
bound obtained thanks to the energy estimates.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume that there exists a solution
(u,X_{0},X_{1})
to the system (
1
) such that
u
is non-negative and
X_{1}-X_{0}
positive. Then, under the assumption (
12
),
u
satisfies

m\leq u\leq M,

where
m
and
M
are defined by

(18)

m=\min\left\{\operatorname{ess-inf}u^{\text{init}},\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\right\},\>M=\max\left\{\operatorname{ess-sup}u^{\text{init}},\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\right\}.

Proof.

Take
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
convex such that
\phi=0
on
[m,M]
, and
\phi>0
everywhere else. Then,
\phi^{\prime}
and
\pi_{\phi}
are identically zero in
[m,M]
, thus Assumption (
12
) ensures that
\phi(r)=\phi^{\prime}(r)=\pi_{\phi}(r)=0
for
r\in\left\{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}},\frac{a}{b},\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\right\}
. Hence for all
t\in[0,T]
, there holds
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{\text{tot}}(t)=\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)
and
Proposition
2.4
gives that
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{\phi}\leq 0
. Since
u^{\text{init}}\in[m,M]
, it comes that
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(0)=0
. Hence
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)\leq 0
for
t\geq 0
. Since
\phi\geq 0
the definition (
16
) of
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
ensures that
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)\geq 0
, thus
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}(t)=0.

Finally,
\phi
being non-negative and continuous, it comes that
\phi(u)\equiv 0
, ensuring that
m\leq u\leq M
.
∎

3.2.
Discrete energy estimates

To begin with, one can prove a result of decreasing energy that can be seen as the discrete counterpart of Proposition
2.4
. Thus, it is necessary to define a discrete free energy as follows.

Definition 3
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
an arbitrary convex function. Set
n\geq 0
. The discrete free energy associated to
\phi
is given by

\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}L^{n}h_{i}\phi(u_{i}^{n}).

Moreover, the discrete total free energy associated to
\phi
is

(19)

\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{{\text{tot}},n}=\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n}+\pi_{\phi}\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta t(\alpha_{0}-\beta_{0}u_{0}^{k})\\
+\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta t(a-bu_{0}^{k})+R\pi_{\phi}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta t(\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1}u_{I+1}^{k}).

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that the scheme (
6
) admits a solution
((u_{i}^{n})_{0\leq i\leq I+1},

X_{0}^{n},X_{1}^{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}
such that, for all
0\leq n\leq N
,
L^{n}>0
and
u_{i}^{n}\geq 0
for all
0\leq i\leq I+1
.
Then, for any convex function
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a non-negative dissipation term
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{n}
such that the discrete derivative of
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{{\text{tot}},n}
satisfies

(20)

\dfrac{\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{{\text{tot}},n}-\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{{\text{tot}},n-1}}{\Delta t}+\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{n}\leq 0.

Proof.

Let us introduce the application
A:(l,w)\mapsto l\phi\left(\dfrac{w}{l}\right)
in order to rewrite

\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}h_{i}A\left(L^{n},L^{n}u_{i}^{n}\right).

As
\phi
is convex,
A
is jointly convex and verifies
\nabla A(l,w)=(-\pi_{\phi}(\dfrac{w}{l}),\phi^{\prime}(\dfrac{w}{l}))
.
From an elementary convexity inequality, we deduce that

(21)

\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n}-\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\leq Y^{n}+Z^{n}

with

Y^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}h_{i}\phi^{\prime}(u_{i}^{n})\frac{L^{n}u_{i}^{n}-L^{n-1}u_{i}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\qquad Z^{n}=-\delta^{n}[L]\sum_{i=1}^{I}h_{i}\pi_{\phi}(u_{i}^{n}).

Using the numerical scheme (
6a
) in the expression
Y^{n}
, performing a discrete integration by parts and using the
discrete boundary conditions (
6b
) and (
6c
) provides

(22)

Y^{n}=(a-bu_{0}^{n})\phi^{\prime}(u_{0}^{n})+\sum_{i=0}^{I}\left(\phi^{\prime}(u_{i+1}^{n})-\phi^{\prime}(u_{i}^{n})\right)\mathcal{F}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}.

For any
0\leq i\leq I
and
n\geq 0
, the mean value formula and the link between
\phi
and
\pi_{\phi}

ensure the existence of
\theta_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\in[0,1]
such that

(23)

\pi_{\phi}(u_{i+1}^{n})-\pi_{\phi}(u_{i}^{n})=\left(\theta_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}u_{i}^{n}+(1-\theta_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})u_{i+1}^{n}\right)\left(\phi^{\prime}(u_{i+1}^{n})-\phi^{\prime}(u_{i}^{n})\right).

Then, using the third property of the Bernoulli function given in (
9
) in Lemma
1.1
, it comes that

(24)

\sum_{i=0}^{I}\left(\phi^{\prime}(u_{i+1}^{n})-\phi^{\prime}(u_{i}^{n})\right)\mathcal{F}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}=-\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{\text{bulk},n}+\sum_{i=0}^{I}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}(\pi_{\phi}(u_{i+1}^{n})-\pi_{\phi}(u_{i}^{n})),

where the quantity
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{\text{bulk},n}
is defined by

(25)

\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{\text{bulk},n}=\sum_{i=0}^{I}\left(B\left(L^{n}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}{}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\right)\theta_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+B\left(-L^{n}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}{}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\right)(1-\theta_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})\right)\times\\
\frac{\phi^{\prime}(u_{i+1}^{n})-\phi^{\prime}(u_{i}^{n})}{L^{n}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}(u_{i+1}^{n}-u_{i}^{n})\geq 0.

Thanks to a discrete integration by parts, the last term in (
24
) rewrites

\sum_{i=0}^{I}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}(\pi_{\phi}(u_{i+1}^{n})-\pi_{\phi}(u_{i}^{n}))=-\sum_{i=1}^{I}(v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-v_{i-\frac{1}{2}}^{n})\pi_{\phi}(u_{i}^{n})+v_{I+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\pi_{\phi}(u_{I+1}^{n})-v_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\pi_{\phi}(u_{0}^{n}).

The definition (
8
) of the discrete velocities implies that
(v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-v_{i-\frac{1}{2}}^{n})=-\delta^{n}[L]h_{i}
and also fix values of
v_{I+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}
and
v_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}
,
so that

(26)

\sum_{i=0}^{I}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}(\pi_{\phi}(u_{i+1}^{n})-\pi_{\phi}(u_{i}^{n}))=-Z^{n}-R\delta^{n}[X_{1}]\pi_{\phi}(u_{I+1}^{n})\\
-((1-R)\delta^{n}[X_{1}]-\delta^{n}[X_{0}])\pi_{\phi}(u_{0}^{n}).

Then, using the equations on
\delta^{n}[X_{0}]
and
\delta^{n}[X_{1}]
in (
6
), we deduce from (
21
), (
22
), (
24
) and (
26
):

\dfrac{\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n}-\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}+\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{\text{bulk},n}\\
+(bu_{0}^{n}-a)\phi^{\prime}(u_{0}^{n})+(\beta_{0}u_{0}^{n}-\alpha_{0})\pi_{\phi}(u_{0}^{n})+R(\beta_{1}u_{I+1}^{n}-\alpha_{1})\pi_{\phi}(u_{I+1}^{n})\leq 0.

In order to recover the expression of
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{{\text{tot}},n}
given in (
19
), it only remains to use the non-decreasing behavior of
\phi^{\prime}
and
\pi_{\phi}
(see Remark
2.3
). Then, the following term is non-negative:

(27)

\mathcal{D}^{\text{bound},n}_{\phi}=(\beta_{0}u_{0}^{n}-\alpha_{0})\left(\pi_{\phi}(u_{0}^{n})-\pi_{\phi}\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\right)\right)\\
+(bu_{0}^{n}-a)\left(\phi^{\prime}(u_{0}^{n})-\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)\right)\\
+R(\beta_{1}u_{I+1}^{n}-\alpha_{1})\left(\pi_{\phi}(u_{I+1}^{n})-\pi_{\phi}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\right)\right)\geq 0.

Finally, the inequality (
20
) holds
with the dissipation term
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{n}
defined as

(28)

\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{n}=\mathcal{D}_{\phi}^{\text{bulk},n}+\mathcal{D}^{\text{bound},n}_{\phi}.

∎

This result will be useful in the rest of the paper to get many a priori estimates on any solution to the numerical scheme, thanks to different choices of energy density function
\phi
.

3.2.1.
Uniform bounds on the concentrations

Let us establish the same a priori
L^{\infty}
bounds as for the continuous problem.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that the scheme (
6
) admits a solution
((u_{i}^{n})_{0\leq i\leq I+1},

X_{0}^{n},X_{1}^{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}
such that, for all

and

for all

. Then, the solution to the scheme satisfies

where

and

are defined in (

).

To prove this result, we adapt to the discrete setting the proof of Proposition
2.5
.

Proof.

The result is shown by induction on the time step

. The property (
29
) being clearly verified for

,
set

and assume that for any
0\leq i\leq I+1
,

verifies (

).

As in the proof of Proposition
2.5
, we take

convex such that

on

,
\phi>0
and strictly convex everywhere else and we apply Proposition
3.2
.
For such a function

, one has

\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{{\text{tot}},n}=\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{{\text{tot}},n-1}=\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n-1}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n-1}=0
thanks to the induction hypothesis. Then, one gets that

\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n}+\Delta t\mathcal{D}^{n}_{\phi}\leq 0.

Since both

and
\mathcal{D}^{n}_{\phi}
are also both non-negative, we deduce that
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{n}=0
, which yields
\phi(u_{i}^{n})=0
and therefore

for

, and
\mathcal{D}^{n}_{\phi}=0,
which yields the same bounds for

and

.

One then readily deduces the following statement from (

)–(

) and (

).

Corollary 3.4
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
3.3
, there holds

(30)

\displaystyle-\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}m\leq

\displaystyle\;\delta^{n}[X_{1}]\leq-\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}M,

(31)

\displaystyle-\alpha_{0}-R\alpha_{1}+m(\beta_{0}+R\beta_{1})\leq

As a consequence, there exists some

and

depending only on the data of the continuous problem such that

v_{\flat}\leq v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\leq v^{\sharp},\qquad\forall\,i\in\{0,\dots,I\}.

3.2.2.
Bounds on the domain size

In this section, two different kinds of estimates are established on the domain size. The first one presented below in Proposition
3.5
, is a non-uniform estimate with respect to the discretization parameters, but it is global in time. It will be useful to prove the existence of a solution to the scheme (
6
).

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that the hypothesis (
12
) is satisfied, and that there exists a solution
((u_{i}^{n})_{0\leq i\leq I+1},X_{0}^{n},X_{1}^{n})
to the numerical scheme (
6
) with
L^{n}>0
and
u_{i}^{n}\geq 0
for all
0\leq i\leq I+1
. Then
L^{n}>\min\left(\frac{m}{M}L^{n-1},\hat{L}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 1
. Suppose for contradiction that
L^{n}\leq\min\left(\frac{m}{M}L^{n-1},\hat{L}\right)
. This implies
\delta^{n}[L]<0
since Assumption (
12
)
ensures that
m<M
. Furthermore, owing to the
L^{\infty}
-bounds (
29
), one has

h_{i}\frac{L^{n}u_{i}^{n}-L^{n-1}u_{i}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\leq 0,\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq I.

Using the scheme (
6
), it comes that

(33)

\mathcal{F}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\leq\mathcal{F}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\leq\mathcal{F}_{I+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}=0,\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq I.

In particular,
\mathcal{F}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}=a-bu_{0}^{n}\leq 0
, that is
u_{0}^{n}\geq\dfrac{a}{b}
. This implies

(34)

v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}=-\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}u_{0}^{n}-\delta^{n}[L]\xi_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\geq-\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}\frac{a}{b}=-R\hat{c}.

Moreover, one infers from (
33
), from the definition (
7
) of the numerical fluxes, and from property (ii) of Lemma
1.1
that

u_{i+1}^{n}\geq u_{i}^{n}\exp(L^{n}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}),

which yields

u_{I+1}^{n}\geq\exp{\left(\sum_{i=0}^{I}L^{n}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\right)}u_{0}^{n}\geq u_{0}^{n}e^{-L^{n}R\hat{c}}\geq\frac{a}{b}e^{-\hat{L}R\hat{c}}=\hat{u}(1).

The relation (
6e
) then provides that
\delta^{n}[X_{1}]\geq\hat{c}
. But, noticing that (
6d
) combined with (

) rewrites

\delta^{n}[L]=R\delta^{n}[X_{1}]-(\alpha_{0}-\beta_{0}u_{0}^{n}),

we then deduce that

, which contradicts

obtained thanks to (

).
Therefore, the starting hypothesis in this proof can not be satisfied, which concludes the proof of Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Now, the second bound presented in this section is uniform with respect to the discretization, but only up to some finite time horizon

. As it is a direct consequence of (

), we do not detail the proof here.

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that the hypothesis (
12
) is satisfied, and that there exists a solution
((u_{i}^{n})_{0\leq i\leq I+1},X_{0}^{n},X_{1}^{n})
to the numerical scheme (
6
) with
u_{i}^{n}\geq 0
for all

for

and

, depending only on

,

,

,

3.3.
Existence of a solution to the numerical scheme

The main result of this section can finally be proved.

Theorem 3.7
.

Under the assumption (
12
), for a given mesh and a given time step, the numerical scheme (
6
) admits a solution
((u_{i}^{n})_{0\leq i\leq I+1},X_{0}^{n},X_{1}^{n})
for any
0\leq n\leq N
.

Proof.

The proof relies on a Brouwer topological degree argument (see for instance Chapter 1, Section 3.1 in
[
18
]
). Our goal is then to construct a homotopy to pass from an invertible linear system for
\lambda=0
to the system (
6
) for
\lambda=1
in such a way that the solution
((u_{i}^{(\lambda)})_{0\leq i\leq I+1},X_{1}^{(\lambda)},L^{(\lambda)})
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
remains in some open set
\Omega
containing the solution for
\lambda=0
and such that the scheme is uniformly continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
. Note that we use
((u_{i}^{(\lambda)})_{0\leq i\leq I+1},X_{1}^{(\lambda)},L^{(\lambda)})
as main unknowns there, and that
X_{0}^{(\lambda)}
is deduced a posteriori.

For
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we seek
((u_{i}^{(\lambda)})_{0\leq i\leq I+1},X_{1}^{(\lambda)},L^{(\lambda)})
solution to

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda(L^{(\lambda)}-L^{n-1})u_{i}^{(\lambda)}+L^{n-1}(u_{i}^{(\lambda)}-u_{i}^{n-1})}{\Delta t}h_{i}+\lambda\left({\mathcal{F}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{(\lambda)}-{\mathcal{F}}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}^{(\lambda)}\right)

\displaystyle\;=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq I,

\displaystyle\lambda\left({\mathcal{F}}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{(\lambda)}-a+bu_{0}^{(\lambda)}\right)+(1-\lambda)\left(\beta_{0}u_{0}^{(\lambda)}-\alpha_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\;=0,

\displaystyle\lambda{\mathcal{F}}_{I+\frac{1}{2}}^{(\lambda)}+(1-\lambda)\left(\beta_{1}u_{I+1}^{(\lambda)}-\alpha_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\;=0,

\displaystyle R\frac{X_{1}^{(\lambda)}-X_{1}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}-\frac{L^{(\lambda)}-L^{n-1}}{\Delta t}-\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}u_{0}^{(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\;=0,

\displaystyle\frac{X_{1}^{(\lambda)}-X_{1}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}+\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1}u_{I+1}^{(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\;=0,

with

{\mathcal{F}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{(\lambda)}=\frac{1}{L^{(\lambda)}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(B\left(-L^{(\lambda)}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{(\lambda)}\right)u_{i}^{(\lambda)}-B\left(L^{(\lambda)}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{(\lambda)}\right)u_{i+1}^{(\lambda)}\right)

and

v_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{(\lambda)}=-R\frac{X_{1}^{(\lambda)}-X_{1}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\xi_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+(1-\xi_{i+\frac{1}{2}})(\beta_{0}u_{0}^{(\lambda)}-\alpha_{0}).

For
\lambda=0
, the unique solution to the above system is
u_{i}^{{(0)}}=u_{i}^{n-1}
for
1\leq i\leq I
,
u_{0}^{(0)}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}
,
u_{I+1}^{(0)}=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}
,
X_{1}^{(0)}=X_{1}^{n-1}
and
L^{(0)}=L^{n-1}
. Moreover, one can extend the proof of Proposition
3.2
and then Proposition
3.3
, Corollary
3.4
, and Proposition

to the case

In particular, one checks that

\displaystyle m\leq\;

\displaystyle u_{i}^{(\lambda)}\leq M,

\displaystyle 0<\min\left(\hat{L},\frac{m}{M}L^{n-1}\right)\leq\;

\displaystyle L^{(\lambda)}\leq L^{n-1}+\Delta t(M(\beta_{0}+R\beta_{1})-\alpha_{0}-R\alpha_{1}),

The goal of this section is to establish our last theoretical result, which by the way grants the existence of a weak solution up to a minimal time thanks to Proposition

Lemma 4.6

6.
Conclusion and prospects

The work conducted in this paper on a simplified model for corrosion, with moving boundaries, presents both theoretical and numerical results providing rigorous foundations in this simplified setting to phenomena observed numerically on more complex models, especially concerning the crucial role played by travelling waves when such profiles exist.

The numerical strategy employed in the code CALIPSO
[
5
]
, which also enters the framework of ALE finite volumes, and which also makes use of Scharfetter-Gummel fluxes, is by the way validated.
Indeed, the existence of a travelling wave profile as presented in Proposition
2.1
, under some explicit assumption on the kinetic parameters, joins the work that is done in
[
7
]
and
[
14
]
. Moreover, in this one species case, the expression of the travelling wave is explicit, and this motivates the choice of Scharfetter-Gummel fluxes for the discretization employed in
[
5
]
. This kind of fluxes guarantees the preservation of the travelling wave profile by the numerical scheme, and is compatible with the rich structure of the continuous problem, which admits an infinite number of free energies.

Beyong the expected convergence orders –
2
in space and
1
in time–, the numerical results show the convergence in the long-time regime towards the unique travelling wave. The theoretical analysis of this asymptotic behavior will be conducted in future works.
We will also extend our results to richer models for corrosion inspired from
[
4
]
, including multiple species and self-consistent electric drift.

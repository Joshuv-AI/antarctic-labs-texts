Response to “Caution: Fundamental data quality issues underlying intelligent prediction of enzyme optimal pH”
Authors: Sizhe Qiu, Aidong Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe respond to concerns regarding fundamental data quality issues in datasets used for machine learning prediction of enzyme optimal pH (pHopt). We re-examined six questioned protein entries by tracing their annotated pH values to the original literature. Our analysis confirmed substantial discrepancies for several entries, including incorrect experimental values, misassigned protein identities, and proteins lacking the reported catalytic activity, whereas one entry was consistent with the literature. These findings support the need for rigorous verification of biochemical data underlying predictive models. We emphasize that careful literature based curation is essential for improving dataset reliability and model validity, and future iterations of pHopt predictors will be developed using more rigorously validated datasets.Keywords: enzyme optimal pH, literature verification, dataset quality

To the Editor,

We would like to thank the rigorous dataset quality assessment conducted by the author of ‘Caution: Fundamental Data Quality Issues Underlying Intelligent Prediction of Enzyme Optimal pH’. The dataset of enzyme optimal pH (pHopt) used to develop EpHod [1], Seq2pHopt [2], and CatOpt [3] was obtained from ‘Machine learning prediction of enzyme optimum pH’, the first enzyme pHopt predictor, available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14252615. In ‘Caution: Fundamental Data Quality Issues…Enzyme Optimal pH’, the author stated that B5LXD7, A0A2Z5VGL1, A0A2Z5VGM9, A0A2Z5VGN0, Q6HA05, and Q97VS7 are erroneously annotated in the dataset of EpHod. Here, we re-examined the annotated pHopt values and associated references of these data entries (Table 1). For B5LXD7, activity assays in Luo et al. [4] and Xu et al. [5] reported pHopt values of 1.0–1.5 and 4.0, respectively, both measurements were deviant from the pHopt value in the EpHod dataset, 11.5. For A0A2Z5VGL1, A0A2Z5VGM9, and A0A2Z5VGN0 that are uniformly annotated with a pHopt of 2.4, the follow-up experiments showed that these three proteins lacked the catalytic activity of endo-beta-N-acetylglucosaminidase [6], and they are no longer in the reference proteome of Sphingobacterium sp. (species). Therefore, these three data entries should not be used in machine learning model development. Brier et al. reported a pHopt value of 2.0 for pepsinogen A2 from Trematomus bernacchii [7], however, this value was mistakenly assigned in the EpHod dataset to Q6HA05, which corresponds to pepsinogen A1 from T. bernacchii. Regarding Q97VS7, Huang et al. reported that its pHopt is ~1.8 at ~80°C [8], consistent with the value in the EpHod dataset.

In essence, we share the view that the dataset underpinning enzyme pHopt predictive models, and more broadly, machine learning models for other protein properties [2, 9, 10], warrants careful scrutiny and refinement to ensure its validity. Rather than relying on the straightforward extraction of data from established repositories such as UniProt [11] and BRENDA [12], we advocate for a more deliberate curation process. Although such curation entails considerable cost, it remains imperative until these biochemical databases can autonomously guarantee a high standard of data integrity [13, 14]. In line with this principle, future iterations of pHopt predictors (e.g. CatOpt) will be built upon a more rigorously validated dataset.

Younghan Yoon

Abstract
. Type A A and type B B permutohedral varieties are classic examples of mathematics, and their topological invariants are well known. This naturally leads to the investigation of the topology of their real loci, known as type A A and type B B real permutohedral varieties. The rational cohomology rings of type A A real permutohedral varieties are fully described in terms of alternating permutations. Until now, only rational Betti numbers of type B B real permutohedral varieties have been described in terms of B B -snakes. In this paper, we explicitly describe the multiplicative structure of the cohomology rings of type B B real permutohedral varieties in terms of B B -snakes.

A normal algebraic variety

The fundamental theorem for toric geometry says that the category of

Furthermore, it is well-known that each smooth compact toric variety corresponds to a non-singular complete fan

Consequently,

determines a smooth compact toric variety

, known as the

of type

.
In particular,

and

are called the
n
-dimensional

and

They have been extensively studied in various fields of mathematics

For a toric variety

For a root system of type

, the real locus

of type

.
For types

is called a

In general, computing topological invariants of real algebraic varieties is quite challenging.

Preliminaries

Let

be a root system of type
R
in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space

W_{R}
its Weyl group.
The group

partitions

of type

.
Consider the fan
\Sigma_{R}
whose maximal cones are Weyl chambers of type
R
.
It is worth noting that

is a non-singular complete fan
[

]
.
Let

, and

We define a linear map

.
The fan

can be replaced by the pair

.
By the fundamental theorem of toric geometry, the pair

completely determines a smooth compact toric variety

, referred to as the

of type
R
.

A

of type

, that is, the real locus of

, is also completely determined by a pair
(K_{R},\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}_{R})
, where the (mod

) characteristic map is defined as

The map

can also be regarded as an

(mod

) matrix

, called the (mod
2
)

of

.

The row space

of

forms a subspace of
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{m}
.
Each element

naturally corresponds to a subset of

.
The cohomology of

is known to depend entirely on the pair

2.1
.
For further details, see
[

]

Theorem 2.1
.

There is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra isomorphism

The multiplicative structure on

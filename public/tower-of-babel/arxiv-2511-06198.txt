Chiral Anomaly of Kogut-Susskind Fermion in ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -dimensional Hamiltonian formalism
Shoto Aoki, Yoshio Kikukawa, Toshinari Takemoto

Abstract
We consider Kogut-Susskind fermions (also known as staggered fermions) in a ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -dimensional Hamiltonian formalism and examine a chiral transformation and its associated chiral anomaly. The Hamiltonian of the massless Kogut-Susskind fermion has symmetry under the shift transformations in each space direction S k ​ ( k = 1 , 2 , 3 ) S_{k}\,(k=1,2,3) , and the product of the three shift transformations in particular (the odd shifts in general) may be regarded as a unitary discrete chiral transformation, modulo two-site translations. The hermitian part of the transformation kernel Γ = − 1 ​ S 1 ​ S 2 ​ S 3 \Gamma=\sqrt{-1}\,S_{1}S_{2}S_{3} can define an axial charge as Q A = ( 1 / 2 ) ​ ∑ x χ † ​ ( x ) ​ ( Γ + Γ † ) ​ χ ​ ( x ) Q_{A}=(1/2)\sum_{x}\chi^{\dagger}(x)\left(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\dagger}\right)\chi(x) , which is non-onsite, nonquantized, and commutative with the vector charge, analogous to Q ~ A = ( 1 / 2 ) ​ ∑ n ( χ n † ​ χ n + 1 + χ n + 1 † ​ χ n ) \tilde{Q}_{A}=(1/2)\sum_{n}(\chi^{\dagger}_{n}\chi_{n+1}+\chi^{\dagger}_{n+1}\chi_{n}) for the ( 1 + 1 ) (1+1) dimensional Kogut-Susskind fermion. However, our Q A Q_{A} cannot be expressed in terms of any quantized charges in a generalized Onsager algebra. Although Q A Q_{A} does not commute with the fermion Hamiltonian in general when coupled to background link gauge fields, we show that they become commutative for a class of U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) link configurations carrying nontrivial magnetic and electric fields. We then verify numerically that the vacuum expectation value of Q A Q_{A} satisfies the anomalous conservation law of axial charge in the continuum two-flavor theory under an adiabatic evolution of the link gauge field.

4
Conclusion

We have studied the
(3+1)
-dimensional KS fermion system in Hamiltonian formalism. We have confirmed that the diagonal shift operator can be interpreted as the chiral operator on the lattice.

In the presence of link variables, the diagonal shift symmetry is violated because of their spatial dependence. Nevertheless, we have found a specific configuration that preserves the diagonal shift symmetry in Sec.
3
. In this case, the magnetic field aligns with the diagonal direction, consistent with the point-splitting construction of the chiral charge on the continuum space. Note that the diagonal shift operator is unitary rather than Hermitian. We have defined two charges
Q_{A}
and
\tilde{Q}
corresponding to the Hermitian and anti-Hermitian parts of the diagonal shift operator. We have numerically calculated the expectation values of
Q_{A}
and
\tilde{Q}
and confirmed two things. One is that the time evolution of
\expectationvalue{Q_A}
satisfies the chiral anomaly equation for a two-flavor Dirac fermion. The other is that the expectation value of
\tilde{Q}
is consistent with the continuum prediction. Thus, we have concluded that the diagonal shift operator can be interpreted as the lattice regularized chiral charge. This implies that
Q_{A}
is equivalent to the Chern–Simons term
[
38
,
8
]
cohomologically.

We have also investigated the relation with the Onsager algebra. In Sec.
2.3
, we have constructed the Onsager algebra associated with all shift operators in the three-dimensional space and found that
Q_{A}
and
\tilde{Q}
belong to the Onsager algebra as
\tilde{Q}=(Q_{S_{T}}+Q_{S_{T}^{-1}})/2
and
Q_{A}=G_{S_{T},-S_{T}}/2
. Since
Q_{A}
commutes with all elements in the algebra, it can be interpreted as a central charge. This result supports the statement in
[
33
]
that
Q_{S_{T}}
is not identified as the chiral charge. In the first place, the chiral anomaly is calculated from a triangle diagram with two vector and one axial currents. We need to take into account the current algebra
[
29
,
2
,
18
]
.

Our results suggest that the construction of a discrete chiral operator
[
17
]
performed in the Schwinger model is also possible in higher dimensions. As in the
(1+1)
dimension, a shift operator in a specific direction induces a chiral charge in the
(3+1)
dimension. Therefore, if we clarify the relationship between this chiral charge and the discrete chiral operator, we can expect to construct a QED Hamiltonian that explicitly possesses chiral symmetry. This will significantly improve the convergence of numerical calculations to the continuum limit, which is likely to contribute to the simulation of physical phenomena involving theta terms.

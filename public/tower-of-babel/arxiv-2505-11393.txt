Diff-Unfolding: A Model-Based Score Learning Framework for Inverse Problems
Yuanhao Wang, Shirin Shoushtari, Ulugbek S. Kamilov

Abstract
Diffusion models are extensively used for modeling image priors for inverse problems. We introduce Diff-Unfolding , a principled framework for learning posterior score functions of conditional diffusion models by explicitly incorporating the physical measurement operator into a modular network architecture. Diff-Unfolding formulates posterior score learning as the training of an unrolled optimization scheme, where the measurement model is decoupled from the learned image prior. This design allows our method to generalize across inverse problems at inference time by simply replacing the forward operator without retraining. We theoretically justify our unrolling approach by showing that the posterior score can be derived from a composite model-based optimization formulation. Extensive experiments on image restoration and accelerated MRI show that Diff-Unfolding achieves state-of-the-art performance, improving PSNR by up to 2 dB and reducing LPIPS by 22.7 % 22.7\% , while being both compact (47M parameters) and efficient (0.72 seconds per 256 × 256 256\times 256 image). An optimized C++/LibTorch implementation further reduces inference time to 0.63 seconds, underscoring the practicality of our approach.

Diffusion models are powerful generative models that achieve state-of-the-art performance in image and video synthesis
[
7
,

]
. These models generate a sample
\bm{x}
from the target distribution
p(\bm{x})
by progressively denoising an initial Gaussian noise
\bm{x}_{T}
through the numerical solution of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) or discrete Markov chains. During training, DMs learn the score functions
\nabla\log p(\bm{x}_{t})
, with
t\in\{0,\cdots,T\}
, of intermediate noisy distributions, enabling approximate sampling from the distribution
p(\bm{x})
at
t=0
.

The ability of DMs to capture complex image distributions makes them effective as priors for imaging inverse problems (see a recent review
[
11
]
). Two main categories of DMs used in this context are
Measurement-Guided Unconditional DMs (UDMs)
and
Conditional DMs (CDMs)
. UDMs use pre-trained unconditional DMs and incorporate measurements only at inference time by approximating the posterior score as
\nabla\log p(\bm{x}_{t}\,|\,\bm{y})=\nabla\log p(\bm{y}\,|\,\bm{x}_{t})+\nabla\log p(\bm{x}_{t})
, where the likelihood term
\nabla\log p(\bm{y}\,|\,\bm{x}_{t})
acts as a guidance signal
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. In contrast, CDMs are trained to directly estimate the conditional score
\nabla\log p(\bm{x}_{t}\,|\,\bm{y})
by incorporating the degraded observation
\bm{y}
as an input to the model. Conditioning can be implemented through various mechanisms such as input concatenation, cross-attention, or adapter modules
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.

While DM-based inverse solvers show strong empirical performance, they also face technical limitations. A key issue in UDMs is the approximation of the posterior score, which can introduce errors and degrade reconstruction quality. CDMs, on the other hand, are typically specialized for specific inverse problems, limiting their flexibility. Moreover, their reliance on task-specific neural networks reduces interpretability by obscuring how measurements influence the final output. This lack of transparency is particularly concerning given the high computational cost of training DMs.

We present
Diff-Unfolding
, a new general and principled framework learning the posterior score function by systematically integrating the measurement operator into the neural network architecture. Our approach builds on
deep unfolding (DU)

[
20
,
21
]
to design a modular architecture in which the data-fidelity term is decoupled from the learned prior. This separation enables flexible adaptation to diverse measurement operators without retraining the entire model. We present novel theoretical and numerical results that justify our approach for solving inverse problems. Theoretically, we show that the posterior score can be expressed as a composite optimization problem, with one term capturing the measurement model and the other representing the prior. This interpretation offers a principled foundation for using MBDL to learn the posterior score. Empirically, Diff-Unfolding achieves state-of-the-art results on a range of inverse problems, while being significantly more efficient. Compared to CDMs, it reduces parameter count by
64\%
and inference time by
50\%
. Compared to DPS, it uses
50\%
fewer parameters and achieves over
100\times
faster inference.

Background

Inverse Problems.
Consider the recovery of the target image
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from the measurements

\bm{y}=\bm{A}\bm{x}+\bm{e},

(1)

where
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a measurement operator, and
\bm{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN). A commont approach for recovering
\bm{x}
from
\bm{y}
is formulating it as an optimization problem

\widehat{\bm{x}}\in\underset{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathsf{arg\,min}}f(\bm{x})\quad\text{with}\quad f(\bm{x})=g(\bm{x})+h(\bm{x})\quad\text{and}\quad g(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{y}-\bm{A}\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2},

(2)

where
g
is the data-fidelity term that ensures consistency with the measurements and
h
is the regularization term that encodes prior knowledge on
\bm{x}
. It is common to solve (
2
) using proximal optimization methods such as ADMM
[
22
]
, HQS
[
23
]
, and PGD
[
24
]
.

Deep Unfolding.
Traditional optimization algorithms require manual parameter tuning, often leading to suboptimal performance. DU is a widely-used MBDL framework that addresses these limitations by reformulating iterative optimization as a trainable neural network, where each iteration becomes a network layer, allowing parameters to be learned in a data-driven fashion
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. One widely-used DU architecture combines the gradient descent on
g
with the residual of a deep network
[
29
,
30
,
31
]

\bm{x}^{k+1}=\bm{x}^{k}-\gamma^{k}\left(\nabla g(\bm{x}^{k})+\tau^{k}\left(\bm{x}^{k}-\mathsf{R}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(\bm{x}^{k})\right)\right),

(3)

where
k
indexes the iteration—interpreted as a “layer" in the unfolded network—while
\gamma_{k}
and
\tau_{k}
are learnable parameters.
\mathsf{R}_{\theta_{j}}
denotes the regularization network, which can either be fixed or learned separately for each iterations.

Diffusion Models.

DMs are state-of-the-art generative models for sampling from a target distribution
[
7
,
16
,
9
,
10
]
. They consist of a forward process that gradually corrupts a data sample
\bm{x}\sim p(\bm{x})
by adding Gaussian noise over
T
steps, following a predefined schedule with standard deviation
\sigma_{t}
, where
\sigma_{0}\approx 0
,
\sigma_{T}=\sigma_{\mathsf{max}}
, and
\bm{x}_{T}
is drawn from a Gaussian distribution
p(\bm{x}_{T})
.

The forward process can be formulated as a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE)
[
16
]
:
\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{t}=\sqrt{2\dot{\sigma}_{t}\sigma_{t}}\mathrm{d}\bm{w}_{t}
, where
\dot{\sigma}_{t}
is the time-derivative of
\sigma_{t}
and
\bm{w}_{t}
is a standard Wiener process. To generate samples, diffusion models learn to approximate the reverse process by training a neural network
\bm{s}_{\theta}(\bm{x}_{t},\sigma_{t})
to estimate the score function of the intermediate noisy distributions
\bm{s}_{\theta}(\bm{x}_{t},\sigma_{t})\approx\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{\sigma_{t}}(\bm{x}_{t})
as proposed in score matching approaches
[
32
]
. Once trained, sampling from
p(\bm{x}_{0})
can be performed by solving the reverse-time SDE

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=-\dot{\sigma}_{t}\sigma_{t}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{\sigma_{t}}(\bm{x}_{t})~\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\dot{\sigma}_{t}\sigma_{t}}\mathrm{d}\bm{w}_{t}.

(4)

Alternatively, one can also use a deterministic formulation known as the Probability Flow ODE, which shares the same marginals as the SDE but yields a deterministic sampling path. This ODE-based approach enables more efficient numerical solvers while preserving sample quality
[
10
,
16
]
.

DMs for Inverse Problems.
There are two primary approaches for leveraging DMs to solve inverse problems. Unconditional DMs use Bayes’ rule to perform inference-time guidance
[
12
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
13
,
15
,
14
]
. These methods typically compute gradients of the log-likelihood
\nabla\log p(\bm{y}\,|\,\bm{x}_{t})
, which is often approximated by
\nabla\log p(\bm{y}\,|\,\hat{\bm{x}}_{0|t})
, where
\hat{\bm{x}}_{0|t}
is an estimate of the clean image. Conditional DMs (CDMs), in contrast, aim to directly learn the posterior score function
\nabla\log p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y})
by training a neural network to denoise a noisy sample
\bm{x}_{t}
conditioned on the observation
\bm{y}

[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
37
,
38
]
. While often effective, CDMs entangle the measurement model and image prior in a way that complicates generalization to new inverse problems without retraining. Recent work
[
19
]
introduced SSDiffRecon, an unrolled CDM architecture that combines cross-attention transformers with data-consistency modules for self-supervised MRI reconstruction. Unlike SSDiffRecon, which is tailored to a specific application, our approach builds on a new theoretical framework for posterior score learning, enabling principled and modular architectures applicable across a broad range of inverse problems.

Our contributions are:

(1)
A new theory showing that the posterior score function can be formulated as a composite optimization problem, with one term representing the physical measurement model and the other representing the image prior. This formulation provides a principled justification for learning the posterior score via deep unfolding.
(2)
A new modular and principled framework for training posterior score functions by explicitly integrating the measurement model into the network architecture. This design disentangles the roles of data fidelity and the learned prior, enabling efficient adaptation to diverse inverse problems without retraining the entire model.
(3)
Comprehensive empirical validation showing that Diff-Unfolding achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple inverse problems while remaining highly efficient. Compared to CDMs, Diff-Unfolding reduces parameter count by up to
64\%
and inference time by
50\%
. Compared to DPS, it uses
50\%
fewer parameters and achieves over
100\times
faster inference.

3
Model-based Posterior Score Learning

We now present our Diff-Unfolding framework, which enhance the traditional CDMs methodology using a
deep unfolding
architecture. We start by introducing a theoretical result that justifies our design, showing that the conditional score function can be formulated as a composite optimization problem. We then describe the technical details of the Diff-Unfolding architecture and its implementation.

3.1
Conditional Score Learning

In the conventional CDM framework, the task is to sample from the posterior distribution
p(\bm{x}|\bm{y})
, where
\bm{y}
is produced by the forward measurement operator in (
1
) for an unknown image
\bm{x}\sim p(\bm{x})
. The standard forward-diffusion process then corrupts
\bm{x}
step-by-step by injecting Gaussian noise, yielding a sequence of increasingly degraded images.

\bm{x}_{t}=\bm{x}+\sigma_{t}\bm{n},\quad\bm{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{I}),

(5)

where
t\in\{0\cdots,T\}
represents the diffusion time step and
\sigma_{t}\in\{\sigma_{0},\cdots,\sigma_{T}\}
denotes the associated noise level sampled from a predefined noise schedule. The CDM framework integrates the measurement
\bm{y}
directly into the denoising network
\mathsf{D}_{\theta}
aiming to minimize the mean-squared error (MSE) loss function for conditional sampling.

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}\sim p(\bm{y}|\bm{x}),\bm{x}\sim p(\bm{x}),t\sim U[0,T]}\left[\|\mathsf{D}_{\theta}(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{y})-\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2}\right],

(6)

where the target image
\bm{x}
and its measurement
\bm{y}
form paired samples. The expectation in (
6
) is computed over all diffusion steps
t\in[0,T]
, target images
\bm{x}\sim p(\bm{x})
, and measurements
\bm{y}\sim p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})
, with conditional likelihood given by the measurement model (
1
). The minimizer of the MSE loss (
6
) is the minimum mean-squared error (MMSE) estimator, which coincides with the conditional expectation of the target given the measurement.

\mathsf{D}_{\theta^{\ast}}(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{y})=\mathbb{E}[\bm{x}\,|\,\bm{x}_{t},\bm{y}].

(7)

Once trained the denoiser
\mathsf{D}_{\theta^{\ast}}
can be directly used within the reverse diffusion process to generate samples from the conditional distribution
[
18
,
17
,
38
,
19
]
.

With the above context established, we now present our main theoretical result.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that the prior density
p(\bm{x})
is non-degenerate on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathsf{D}_{\theta^{\ast}}(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{y})
be the MMSE estimator defined in (
7
).
Then, the following statements hold for all
t\in\{0,\cdots,T\}

(a)

The denoiser is a minimizer of the following composite optimization problem

\mathsf{D}_{\theta^{\ast}}(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{y})=\underset{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathop{\mathsf{arg\,min}}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|_{2}^{2}+h_{t}(\bm{x})\right\},

for some regularizer
h_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

(b)

The denoiser can be related to the following posterior score function

\mathsf{D}_{\theta^{\ast}}(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{y})=\bm{x}_{t}+\sigma_{t}^{2}\nabla\log p(\bm{x}_{t}\,|\,\bm{y}),

where
p(\bm{x}_{t}|\bm{y})
is the conditional distribution of
\bm{x}_{t}
given
\bm{y}
.

The proof is provided in Appendix
A
. Theorem
1
shows that the MMSE estimator in the CDM framework corresponds to the solution of a composite optimization problem with two components: a data-fidelity term encoding the measurement operator and a regularizer
h_{t}
capturing the prior knowledge. Thus, computing
\mathsf{D}_{\theta^{\ast}}
reduces to minimizing a
regularized least-squares objective
, which can be solved via iterative optimization.

While the regularizer lacks a closed-form expression, Diff-Unfolding uses deep unfolding to translate iterative optimization steps into a learnable neural architecture, enabling effective inference of its implicit structure. Theorem
1
further establishes a direct connection between the MMSE denoiser—realizable via Diff-Unfolding—and the posterior score function, providing a rigorous justification for its use in the CDM setting.

3.2
Diff-Unfolding Training and Sampling

Theorem
1
implies that, given an explicit form for the regularizer
h_{t}
, the conditional MMSE estimator for CDM can be computed by solving the following composite optimization problem

\mathsf{D}_{\theta^{\ast}}(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{y})=\mathop{\mathsf{arg\,min}}_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{g(\bm{x})+h_{t}(\bm{x})\right\}\quad\text{with}\quad g(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{y}-\bm{A}\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2}.

(8)

We address this optimization problem using deep unfolding, replacing the unknown regularizer
h_{t}
with a learnable neural network. A key advantage of this approach is the explicit integration of the measurement model
g(\bm{x})
into the architecture, removing the need to learn the measurement operator and improving both interpretability and efficiency.

and

(9)

where
g
is the data-fidelity term and
\mathsf{R}_{\theta}
is a learnable neural network representing the regularizer
h_{t}
. The indices

and
k\in[1,K]

\gamma_{t}^{k}
and
\tau_{t}^{k}
are learnable step-size and regularization parameters for each diffusion step

4
Experiments

We conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the efficacy, robustness, and computational efficiency of Diff-Unfolding on two key applications: image restoration and accelerated MRI reconstruction under various degradation scenarios. Our assessment includes quantitative metrics—Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS)—alongside visual comparisons to provide a thorough evaluation of our approach.

4.1
Experiments setup

Dataset preparation.

We use the FFHQ dataset
[
39
]
for image restoration. Images indexed 1,000–69,999 are used for training, while 100 images from the first 1,000 images are reserved for testing. And we evaluate our approach on the fastMRI Multicoil Knee dataset
[
40
]
for MRI reconstruction. We train on the entire training set (973 scans) and test on three scans from the validation scans. FFHQ images are normalized to the range
\left[0,1\right]
, while fastMRI slices are normalized using their
99th
percentile absolute value, as recommended in
[
41
]
.

Tasks and Degradations.

For image restoration, we evaluate our method on three tasks:
(1) Deblurring:
using both isotropic and anisotropic Gaussian blur kernel;
(2) Super-resolution:
with scaling factors of
4\times
,
8\times
and
16\times
;
(3) Inpainting:
using random dust-like binary masks patterns with missing probabilities of 0.2, 0.4, and 0.6. For MRI reconstruction, we evaluate three k-space sampling strategies:
(1) 1D uniform sampling:

4\times
and

(2) 1D Gaussian sampling:

4\times
and

We train Diff-Unfolding using the Adam optimizer

]
with a learning rate of

5
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced Diff-Unfolding, a novel framework that integrates physics-based deep unfolding with conditional diffusion models to address a broad class of inverse problems. By explicitly modeling the posterior score function during training, Diff-Unfolding aims to approximate the posterior and enables a single model to generalize across diverse forward operators. Extensive experiments on image restoration and accelerated MRI tasks demonstrate that Diff-Unfolding achieves state-of-the-art performance across both quantitative metrics (PSNR, SSIM) and perceptual quality (LPIPS), while using fewer parameters and offering significantly faster inference than existing methods.

Limitations

Despite its advantages,Diff-Unfolding has several limitations. While it supports multiple forward operators, the current implementations are restricted to linear degradations and additive Gaussian noise. Future work could extend the framework to handle more complex and nonlinear degradations. Additionally, although Diff-Unfolding is more efficient than previous diffusion-based methods, it currently requires fine-tuning to handle out-of-distribution operators, which is not considered during the training.

Impact Statement

By unifying diverse imaging tasks under a single framework Diff-Unfolding could have the potential to reduce the need for task-specific models and training, and lower the computation of inference. Its efficiency and flexibility make it promising for practical deployment in real-world applications, including medical imaging. We expect this work could make a positive impact on computational imaging and inverse problems approaches.

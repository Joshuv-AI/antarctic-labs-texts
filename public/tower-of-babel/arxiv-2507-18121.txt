Number Field Analogue of Jacobi Theta Relation And Zeros of Dedekind zeta function on Re ⁡ ( s ) = 1 / 2 \operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2 Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 11M06, 11R42; Secondary 11M26. Keywords and phrases. Riemann zeta function, Functional equation, Jacobi theta relation, Dedekind zeta function, Non-trivial zeros, Critical line.
Diksha Rani Bansal, Bibekananda Maji

Abstract
. In 1914, Hardy proved that there are infinitely many non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) on the critical line Re ⁡ ( s ) = 1 / 2 \operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2 using the Jacobi theta relation. In this paper, we first establish a number field analogue of the Jacobi theta relation and as an application, we show the existence of infinitely many non-trivial zeros of the Dedekind zeta function ζ 𝔽 ​ ( s ) \zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s) on Re ⁡ ( s ) = 1 / 2 \operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2 , for any number field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} . Quite interestingly, we also prove that the Jacobi theta relation is equivalent to an intriguing identity of Hardy, Littlewood and Ramanujan.



1.
Introduction

The Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
is regarded as one of the most important special functions in mathematics due to its connection with prime numbers and complex nature of non-trivial zeros. Riemann
[
9
]
, in his seminal paper, showed that
\zeta(s)
can be analytically continued to the whole complex plane except for a simple pole at
s=1
and proved the following beautiful symmetric functional equation:

\displaystyle\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)=\pi^{-\frac{(1-s)}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)\zeta(1-s).

(1.1)

In the same paper, he conjectured that all the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
lie on the critical line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2
, which is popularly known as the Riemann hypothesis (RH).
Riemann proved the functional equation (
1.1
) using the following Jacobi theta relation:

\displaystyle W_{1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sqrt{x}W_{1}(x),

(1.2)

where
W_{1}(x)
is the well-known Jacobi theta function, defined as

\displaystyle W_{1}(x)=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi n^{2}x},\,\,\,\operatorname{Re}(x)>0.

(1.3)

Moreover, one can prove that the above Jacobi theta relation (
1.2
) is equivalent to the functional equation (
1.1
) of
\zeta(s)
.
Next, we present a similar relation formula due to Ramanujan
[
2
, p. 97]
,
[
36
, p. 253]
, namely, for
\operatorname{Re}(x)>0
,

\displaystyle W_{2}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sqrt{x}W_{2}(x).

(1.4)

Here,
W_{2}(x)
is given by

W_{2}(x)=\gamma-\log(4\pi)+\log(\sqrt{x})+4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)\text{K}_{0}(2n\pi\sqrt{x}),

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant,
d(n)
denotes the number of divisors of
n
, and
K_{0}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind.
Besides Ramanujan, Koshliakov also recorded the above-mentioned identity (
1.4
) in his work
[
29
]
. Ferrar
[
21
]
was the first person to prove the Ramanujan-Koshliakov identity (
1.4
) using the functional equation for
\zeta^{2}(s)
.
More generally, Ferrar
[
21
, p. 102]
derived a simultaneous extension of Jacobi theta relation and Ramanujan-Koshliakov formula by obtaining an identity under the assumption of the functional equation for
\zeta^{k}(s)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
It is worth to remark that, the functional equation of
\zeta^{k}(s)
can be derived by assuming Ferrar’s identity
[
21
, p. 102]
, see Corollary
2.5
.

Motivated by Ferrar’s identity
[
21
, Equation (2)]
, in this paper, we establish an identity, see Theorem
2.1
, which is equivalent to the functional equation of
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)
, where
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
is the Dedekind zeta function defined below.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a number field of degree
d=r_{1}+2r_{2}
, where
r_{1}
is the number of real embeddings and
r_{2}
is the number of pairs of complex conjugate embeddings of
\mathbb{F}
. Let
D
denote the absolute value of the discriminant of
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
denote the ring of integers of
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathfrak{N}
be the norm map. We define
\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F}}(n)
to be the number of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
with norm
n
. Then the Dedekind zeta function corresponding to the number field
\mathbb{F}
is defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F}}(n)}{n^{s}},

for
\operatorname{Re}{(s)}>1,
and the sum over
\mathfrak{a}
runs through all non-zero integral ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
.
The Dedekind zeta function satisfies the following functional equation:

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(s)=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(1-s),

(1.5)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(s)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{D}{4^{r_{2}}\pi^{d}}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{r_{2}}(s)\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s).

(1.6)

From (
1.5
), we can also see that the functional equation for
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{-1}(s)
is given as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{-1}(s)=\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{-1}(1-s),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}(s)=\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}(1-s),

(1.7)

where
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}(s)=\left(\frac{D}{4^{r_{2}}\pi^{d}}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{r_{2}}(s)\zeta^{-1}_{\mathbb{F}}(1-s)
.
It is known that
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
has a simple pole at
s=1
with the residue given by the class number formula:

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}(s-1)\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)=\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}}{\sqrt{D}}\frac{R_{\mathbb{F}}h_{\mathbb{F}}}{w_{\mathbb{F}}}:=H_{\mathbb{F}},

where
w_{\mathbb{F}}
is the number of roots of unity contained in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}},h_{\mathbb{F}}
denotes the class number, and
R_{\mathbb{F}}
is the regulator of
\mathbb{F}
.
Further, we know that the order of zero of
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
at
s=0
is given by
r=r_{1}+r_{2}-1
, which is also the rank of the unit group of
\mathbb{F}
. The leading term in the Laurent series expansion of
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
at
s=0
is given by

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)}{s^{r}}=-\frac{R_{\mathbb{F}}h_{\mathbb{F}}}{w_{\mathbb{F}}}:=C_{\mathbb{F}}.

(1.8)

Quite interestingly, in this paper, we show that the functional equation for
\zeta^{-1}(s)
is equivalent to the below well-known identity of Hardy, Littlewood and Ramanujan
[
24
, p. 158]
.
Let
\mu(n)
be the Möbius function. For any
x>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\exp\left(-\frac{x}{n^{2}}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{x}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{n^{2}x}\right)-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{x}}\right)^{\rho}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\rho}{2}\right)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)},

(1.9)

where the sum over
\rho
runs through the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
.
This identity has been studied and generalized by many mathematicians, interested reader can see
[
1
,
4
,
8
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
22
]
. Recently, Dixit, Gupta and Vatwani
[
19
]
extended the identity (
1.9
) for the Dedekind zeta function over any number field.

One of the significant milestones in the pursuit of the Riemann hypothesis came through the work of Hardy
[
23
]
. In 1914, using the Jacobi theta relation (
1.2
), Hardy proved that there are infinitely many zeros of
\zeta(s)
on the critical line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
. This result, while not proving the Riemann hypothesis itself, demonstrated the deep connection between the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
and the critical line, hinting at the possibility that all non-trivial zeros could indeed lie on this line. This result has been generalized for various Dirichlet series, with Hecke
[
26
]
being the first to show that the Dedekind zeta function has infinite number of zeros on the critical line for an ideal class in an imaginary quadratic field. Later, Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan
[
14
]
extended this result to include both real and imaginary quadratic fields, while Berndt
[
5
]
further extended it for any number field of degree
d=r_{1}+2r_{2}
with
0\leq r_{1}\leq 3
. To learn more about zeros of Dedekind zeta function, readers are encouraged to see
[
6
,
7
,
25
]
.

One of the main aims of the current paper is to develop an analogue of the Jacobi theta relation (
1.2
) for the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
and employ Hardy’s approach to prove the existence of infinitely many zeros of
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
on the critical line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2
.
Before stating our main results, we need the following definitions.

The Steen function
V(z|a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
is defined as

\displaystyle V(x|a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(s+a_{j})x^{-s}\text{d}s,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi n}{2},

where
(c)
in the integral refers to the vertical line running from
c-i\infty
to
c+i\infty
. Here, the argument
\operatorname{Arg}(x)
is the principal argument
-\pi<\operatorname{Arg}(x)\leq\pi
.
It is assumed that all the poles of
\Gamma(s+a_{j})
lie on one side of the vertical line
(c)
. This function is a special case of the more general Meijer
G
-function
[
35
, p. 415, Definition 16.17]
. Particular choices of parameters recover various well-known special functions. For instance,

\displaystyle V(x|0)

\displaystyle=e^{-x},\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,c>0,

\displaystyle V(x|a,b)

\displaystyle=2x^{\frac{1}{2}(a+b)}\text{K}_{a-b}(2x^{\frac{1}{2}}),\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,c>\max\{-a,-b\},

(1.10)

where
\text{K}_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function of second kind of order
\nu
. Further information about the Steen function can be found in
[
40
]
,
[
27
, p. 63]
.

We next examine a function, related to the Steen function, that was first studied by Koshliakov
[
30
, p. 122]
and recently used by Dixit, Gupta, and Vatwani
[
19
, p. 5, Equation (2.2)]
.

Let
c=\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
. Then the function
\tilde{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{r_{2}}(s)x^{-s}\text{d}s,\quad\text{where}\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi d}{4}.

(1.11)

If we shift the line of integration to the left, we get the following representation, for
-1<\operatorname{Re}(s)=b<0
,

\displaystyle{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(b)}\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{r_{2}}(s)x^{-s}\mathrm{d}s=\tilde{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}(x)-\mathfrak{R}_{0}(x),

(1.12)

where
\mathfrak{R}_{0}(x)
is the residue of the above integrand function due to a pole of order
r_{1}+r_{2}
at
s=0
. Some particular cases can be given by

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}_{0,r_{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=V(x|\bar{0}_{r_{2}}),

(1.13)

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}_{r_{1},0}(x)

\displaystyle=2V(x^{2}|\bar{0}_{r_{1}}),

(1.14)

\displaystyle{Z}_{r_{1},0}(x)

\displaystyle=2V(x^{2}|\bar{0}_{r_{1}})-\mathfrak{R}_{0}(x),

(1.15)

\displaystyle{Z}_{1,0}(x)

\displaystyle=2(e^{-x^{2}}-1),

(1.16)

where
\bar{0}_{m}
is the
m
-tuple whose all entries equal 0, and
\mathfrak{R}_{0}(x)
in (
1.15
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{(r_{1}-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r_{1}-1}}{\mathrm{d}s^{r_{1}-1}}\left(s^{r_{1}}\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)x^{-s}\right).

Remark 1
.

Using the duplication formula for
\Gamma(s)
, one can check that

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{2^{r_{2}}\pi^{\frac{r_{2}}{2}}}V\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4^{r_{2}}}\bigg|\bar{0}_{r_{1}+r_{2}},\bar{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}_{r_{2}}\right),\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi d}{4}.

Before moving further, we define the following Dirichlet series for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
:

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)}{n^{s}},\quad\zeta^{k}(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{k}(n)}{n^{s}},

(1.17)

where
\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)=\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F}}(n)*\cdots*\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F}}(n)
i.e. Dirichlet convolution of
\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F}}(n),\,k-
times and
d_{k}(n)
counts the number of ways
n
can be written as product of
k
positive integers. Hence, for
k=2
, we have
d_{k}(n)=d(n)
.
We now state the main results of this paper.

2.
Main Results

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and
\mathbb{F}
be any number field of degree
d
. Let
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
,
\tilde{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}(x)
and
\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)
be defined as in (
1.6
), (
1.11
) and (
1.17
), respectively. The functional equation for
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)

is equivalent to the theta relation

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F},k}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sqrt{x}W_{\mathbb{F},k}(x),\quad\text{for}\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi d}{2},

(2.1)

where
W_{\mathbb{F},k}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F},k}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)\tilde{Z}_{kr_{1},kr_{2}}\left(\frac{2^{kr_{2}}\pi^{kd/2}n\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{D^{k}}}\right)-R_{0}(x),

(2.2)

with

R_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k-1}}{\mathrm{d}s^{k-1}}\left(s^{k}\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)x^{-s/2}\right).

Remark 2
.

For
d=1
, that is,
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
, the relation (
2.1
) holds for all
x
with
\operatorname{Re}(x)>0
. For quadratic fields, it is valid for all
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
, whereas if the field
\mathbb{F}
has degree
d\geq 3
, the relation (
2.1
) holds for any complex number
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

In particular, if we take
k=1
, we get the following result for the Dedekind zeta function.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a number field with degree
d
. Then the functional equation for
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
is equivalent to the theta relation

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F},1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sqrt{x}W_{\mathbb{F},1}(x),\quad\text{for}\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi d}{2},

(2.3)

where
W_{\mathbb{F},1}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F},1}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F}}(n)\tilde{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}\left(\frac{2^{r_{2}}\pi^{d/2}n\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{D}}\right)-2^{r_{1}}C_{\mathbb{F}},

(2.4)

where
C_{\mathbb{F}}
is defined in (
1.8
).

Remark 3
.

We note that
W_{\mathbb{F},1}(x)
serves as a number field analogue of the Jacobi theta function
W_{1}(x)
, defined in (
1.3
), as it coincides with
W_{1}(x)
when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
. For number fields of degree
d\leq 2
, the theta relation (
2.3
) has also been studied by Arai, Chakraborty and Kanemitsu
[
3
, Equation (2.11)]
.

Remark 4
.

For any number field
\mathbb{F}
of degree
d\geq 3
, letting
x=-1
in (
2.3
), we derive the following exact evaluation:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F}}(n)\tilde{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}\left(\frac{2^{r_{2}}\pi^{d/2}ni}{\sqrt{D}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{r_{1}}C_{\mathbb{F}}.

We next present an equivalence for the functional equation of the Dedekind zeta function over totally real number fields.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a totally real number field with degree
d=r_{1}
. The functional equation for
\zeta^{k}_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
is equivalent to the theta relation

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F},k}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sqrt{x}W_{\mathbb{F},k}(x),\quad\text{for}\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi r_{1}}{2},

where
W_{\mathbb{F},k}(x)
is given by

W_{\mathbb{F},k}(x)=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)V\left(\frac{\pi^{kr_{1}}n^{2}x}{D^{k}}\bigg|\bar{0}_{kr_{1}}\right)-R_{0}(x),

and

R_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k-1}}{\mathrm{d}s^{k-1}}\left(s^{k}\Gamma^{kr_{1}}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)\left(\frac{\pi^{(kr_{1}/2)}\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{D^{k}}}\right)^{-s}\right).

Now assuming
\mathbb{F}
to be a real quadratic field, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})
, where
m
is a positive square free integer. The functional equation for
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)

is equivalent to the theta relation

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F},1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sqrt{x}W_{\mathbb{F},1}(x),\quad\text{for}\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\pi,

where
W_{\mathbb{F},1}(x)
is given by

W_{\mathbb{F},1}(x)=4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F}}(n)K_{0}\left(\frac{2\pi n\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{D}}\right)-4C_{\mathbb{F}}.

We obtain Ferrar’s identity
[
21
, p. 102]
by taking
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
in Corollary
2.3
.

Corollary 2.5
.

The functional equation for
\zeta^{k}(s)
is equivalent
to the theta relation

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{Q},k}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sqrt{x}W_{\mathbb{Q},k}(x),\quad\text{for}\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi}{2},

(2.5)

where
W_{\mathbb{Q},k}(x)
is given by

W_{\mathbb{Q},k}(x):=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{k}(n)V\left(\pi^{k}n^{2}x|\bar{0}_{k}\right)-R_{0}(x),

and

R_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k-1}}{\mathrm{d}s^{k-1}}\left(s^{k}\Gamma^{k}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta^{k}(s)\left(\pi^{k}x\right)^{-\frac{s}{2}}\right).

Remark 5
.

Ferrar
[
21
, p. 102]
derived the identity (
2.5
) by assuming the functional equation for
\zeta^{k}(s)
. However, he did not prove the converse, that is, the relation (
2.5
) implies the functional equation for
\zeta^{k}(s)
.

Remark 6
.

Putting
k=1
and
k=2
in Corollary
2.5
gives the equivalences for functional equation of
\zeta(s)
and
\zeta^{2}(s)
with the Jacobi theta relation (
1.2
) and the Ramanujan-Koshliakov fomula (
1.4
), respectively.

2.1.
Equivalence of the functional equation of
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{-k}(s)

In this subsection, we state an equivalence of the functional equation for
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{-k}(s)
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It is well-known that
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)\neq 0
for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
. So, we define a generalization of the Möbius function
\mu(n)
for any number field
\mathbb{F}
using the following Dirichlet series:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)}{n^{s}},\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1.

(2.6)

A detailed explanation for
\mu_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)
is given in (
3.1
).

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
k
be any positive integer,
r=r_{1}+r_{2}-1
, and
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
,
Z_{r_{1},r_{2}}(x)
be defined as in (
1.7
), (
1.12
), respectively. Then the functional equation for
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{-k}(s)

is equivalent to the theta relation

\displaystyle U_{\mathbb{F},-k}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sqrt{x}U_{\mathbb{F},-k}(x),\quad\text{for}\,\,\,|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi d}{2},

(2.7)

where the term
U_{\mathbb{F},-k}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle U_{\mathbb{F},-k}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)}{n}{Z}_{kr_{1},kr_{2}}\left(\frac{2^{kr_{2}}\pi^{kd/2}\sqrt{x}}{n\sqrt{D^{k}}}\right)+R_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho}R_{\rho}(x),

(2.8)

and the terms
R_{0}(x)
and
R_{\rho}(x)
are defined as

\displaystyle R_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(kr-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{{\rm d}^{kr-1}}{{\rm d}s^{kr-1}}\left(s^{kr}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)(\sqrt{x})^{-s}\right),

\displaystyle R_{\rho}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow\rho}\frac{{\rm d}^{k-1}}{{\rm d}s^{k-1}}\left((s-\rho)^{k}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)(\sqrt{x})^{-s}\right).

Here, we assume that, for any
x>0
, the infinite series
\sum_{\rho}R_{\rho}(x)
converges, where the sum runs over all the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
in the strip
0<\operatorname{Re}(s)<1
. Additionally, we suppose that each such zero is simple.

Remark 7
.

Setting
k=1,\alpha=\frac{2^{r_{2}}\pi^{d/2}\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{D}}
and
\beta=\frac{2^{r_{2}}\pi^{d/2}}{\sqrt{D}\sqrt{x}}
in Theorem
2.6
yields that the functional equation of
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{-1}(s)
is equivalent to the following identity of Dixit, Gupta, and Vatwani
[
19
, Theorem 1.3]
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\mathbb{F},1}(n)}{n}{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)-\sqrt{\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\mathbb{F},1}(n)}{n}{Z}_{r_{1},r_{2}}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}R_{0}\left(\alpha\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}R_{0}\left(\beta\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\sum_{\rho}R_{\rho}\left(\alpha\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{\rho}R_{\rho}\left(\beta\right)\right],

(2.9)

where the terms
R_{0}(\alpha)
and
R_{\rho}(\alpha)
are given by

\displaystyle R_{0}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{{\rm d}^{r-1}}{{\rm d}s^{r-1}}\left(s^{r}\alpha^{s}\frac{\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{r_{2}}\left(1-s\right)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)}\right),

\displaystyle R_{\rho}\left(\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\rho}\frac{\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{1-\rho}{2}\right)\Gamma^{r_{2}}\left(1-\rho\right)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}(\rho)}.

This also suggests that the theta relation (

) is a one variable generalization of the identity (

) of Dixit, Gupta, and Vatwani.

Considering

in Theorem

Corollary 2.7
.

Let

and
\mu_{k}(n)
be the generalized Möbius function defined in (

). The functional equation for

is equivalent to the theta relation

(2.10)

where

is given by

\displaystyle U_{\mathbb{Q},-k}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{k}(n)}{n}\left[2\,V\left(\frac{\pi^{k}x}{n^{2}}\bigg|\bar{0}_{k}\right)-\mathfrak{R}_{0}\left(\frac{\pi^{k/2}\sqrt{x}}{n}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho}R_{\rho}(x).

Here, the sum over
\rho
runs through all the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
, and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k-1}}{\mathrm{d}s^{k-1}}\left(s^{k}\Gamma^{k}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)x^{-s}\right),

\displaystyle R_{\rho}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\lim_{s\rightarrow\rho}\frac{{\rm d}^{k-1}}{{\rm d}s^{k-1}}\left((s-\rho)^{k}\pi^{\frac{-ks}{2}}\Gamma^{k}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta^{-k}(1-s)(\sqrt{x})^{-s}\right).

Corollary 2.8

Let

Now we make use of Corollary

Theorem 2.9
.

For any number field

Key Tools

In this section, we mention some results and lemmas that will be used in proving our main theorems.
The Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
has Euler product expansion, given by

\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{p})^{s}}\right)^{-1},\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)>1,

where
\mathfrak{p}
runs over prime ideals of

is

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{a}\in A}(-1)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}},&1\leq\alpha_{i}\leq k,\\
1,&n=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

where the set
A
is defined as

A=\{\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}|\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{a})=n,\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{m}^{\alpha_{m}},\text{where }\mathfrak{p}_{i}\text{'s}\text{ are distinct prime ideals}\}.

For

, the above function

coincides with the function given by Heath-Brown
[

, p. 172]
.
In particular, when

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbb{Q},k}(n)=\mu_{k}(n)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}},&1\leq\alpha_{i}\leq k,\\

0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

More generally, for

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 1^{+}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1^{+}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)}{n^{s}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)}{n}=0.

(3.3)

We now define the following entire function:

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathbb{F}}(s)

Putting

Lemma 3.1
.

The function

Consider

\displaystyle=\overline{\left(-\frac{1}{8}-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)}\overline{\left(\frac{D}{4^{r_{2}}\pi^{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{it}{2}}}\overline{\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{it}{2}\right)}\overline{\Gamma^{r_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)}\overline{\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{8}-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)\left(\frac{D}{4^{r_{2}}\pi^{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{it}{2}}\Gamma^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{it}{2}\right)\Gamma^{r_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-it\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-it\right)

\displaystyle=\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(-t)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-it\right)

since

4.
Proof of Main Results

Let us define

\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{F},k}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathtt{a}_{\mathbb{F},k}(n)\tilde{Z}_{kr_{1},kr_{2}}\left(\frac{2^{kr_{2}}\pi^{kd/2}n\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{D^{k}}}\right),\quad|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi d}{2}.

\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{F},k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\Gamma^{kr_{1}}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{kr_{2}}\left(s\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)\left(\frac{2^{kr_{2}}\pi^{kd/2}\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{D^{k}}}\right)^{-s}\text{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\left(\frac{D}{4^{r_{2}}\pi^{d}}\right)^{\frac{ks}{2}}\Gamma^{kr_{1}}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{kr_{2}}(s)\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)x^{-\frac{s}{2}}\text{d}s

where
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
is defined as in (
1.6
). We take a rectangular contour
\mathcal{C}
made up of the vertices

and

with some large value of

\displaystyle\Phi(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)y^{it}\text{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}f(t)\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)y^{it}\text{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)y^{it}\text{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(it)\phi(-it)\xi_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)y^{it}\text{d}t.

Letting
\frac{1}{2}+it=s
and
\phi(s)=\frac{1}{s+\frac{1}{2}}
in the above integral and using (
3
), one can see that

\displaystyle\Phi(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i\sqrt{y}}\int_{\frac{1}{2}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+i\infty}\frac{1}{s(1-s)}\frac{s(s-1)}{2}\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(s)y^{s}\text{d}s

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\times\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{y}}\int_{\frac{1}{2}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+i\infty}\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}(s)y^{s}\text{d}s

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{y}}\left(W_{\mathbb{F},1}\left(\frac{1}{y^{2}}\right)+C_{\mathbb{F}}2^{r_{1}}(1+y)\right).

(4.27)

Here, we have used Lemma
3.2
in the last step. Since
y=e^{z}
and
f(t)=\phi(it)\phi(-it)=\frac{1}{t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}
, so from (
4.26
) and (
4.27
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}\cos(zt)\text{d}t=-\frac{\pi}{2}\left[e^{-\frac{z}{2}}W_{\mathbb{F},1}(e^{-2z})+C_{\mathbb{F}}2^{r_{1}}\left(e^{-\frac{z}{2}}+e^{\frac{z}{2}}\right)\right].

(4.28)

Now letting
z=-i\alpha
in (
4.28
) and using the fact that
\cos(-it)=\cosh(t)
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}\cosh(\alpha t)\text{d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2}\left[C_{\mathbb{F}}2^{r_{1}}\left(e^{\frac{-i\alpha}{2}}+e^{\frac{i\alpha}{2}}\right)+e^{\frac{i\alpha}{2}}W_{\mathbb{F},1}\left(e^{2i\alpha}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2}\left[2C_{\mathbb{F}}2^{r_{1}}\left(\frac{e^{\frac{i\alpha}{2}}+e^{-\frac{i\alpha}{2}}}{2}\right)+e^{\frac{i\alpha}{2}}W_{\mathbb{F},1}(e^{2i\alpha})\right]

\displaystyle=-\pi C_{\mathbb{F}}2^{r_{1}}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)-\frac{\pi e^{\frac{i\alpha}{2}}}{2}W_{\mathbb{F},1}(e^{2i\alpha}).

(4.29)

Note that the above identity is valid for
|\alpha|<\frac{\pi d}{4}
since the factor
W_{\mathbb{F},1}(x)
converges for
|\operatorname{Arg}(x)|<\frac{\pi d}{2}
, see (
2.3
). Next, we need to show that the equation (
4.29
) can be differentiated with respect to
\alpha
any number of times. One can easily prove that

\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)=O\left(|t|^{A}D^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}e^{-\frac{\pi dt}{4}}\right)

for some positive
A
, by using the bounds
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)=O(|t|^{\frac{d}{4}+\epsilon}D^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon})
and
|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+it)|=O(e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|t|})
as
|t|\rightarrow\infty
. Also, it is well known that the function
\cosh(x)
is bounded above by
e^{|x|}
. Using the above bounds for
\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)
and
\cosh(x)
, we see that the improper integral in (
4.29
) is absolutely convergent for
|\alpha|<\frac{\pi d}{4}
. Thus, we differentiate (
4.29
)
2n
-times with respect to
\alpha
, to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh(\alpha t)\text{d}t

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{\pi C_{\mathbb{F}}2^{r_{1}}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}{2^{2n}}-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\text{d}^{2n}}{\text{d}\alpha^{2n}}\left(e^{\frac{i\alpha}{2}}W_{\mathbb{F},1}(e^{2i\alpha})\right).

(4.30)

Our next claim is that for any fixed
n
, the second term on the right hand side of the above equation tends to zero as
\alpha\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2}
.
From Corollary
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F},1}\left(e^{-2i\alpha}\right)=e^{i\alpha}W_{\mathbb{F},1}(e^{2i\alpha}),\quad\text{for}\,\,\,|\alpha|<\frac{\pi d}{4}.

As we are dealing with number fields of degree
d\geq 3
, so from the above equation, one can easily see that
W_{\mathbb{F},1}(e^{2i\alpha})
and all its derivatives tend to zero as
\alpha\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2}
along any path. Therefore, from (
4.30
), we finally have

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh(\alpha t)\text{d}t

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{C_{\mathbb{F}}2^{r_{1}}\pi\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}{2^{2n}}.

(4.31)

Now let us suppose that
\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)
eventually takes one sign. Without the loss of generality, we assume
\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)
to be positive for
t\geq T
, for some large
T
. Then, we let

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{T}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh(\alpha t)\text{d}t=M.

This
M
will be positive since all the functions are positive in
[T,\infty)
. Thus for any
T^{\prime}>T
,

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh(\alpha t)\text{d}t\leq M.

Hence, letting
\alpha\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2}
, we arrive

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)\text{d}t\leq M.

Taking
T^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty
in the above inequality proves the fact that the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh\left(\alpha t\right)\text{d}t

is uniformly convergent with respect to
\alpha
. Thus, from (
4.31
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)\text{d}t=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{C_{\mathbb{F}}2^{r_{1}}\pi\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}{2^{2n}},\quad\forall n.

One can see that, from (
1.8
),
C_{\mathbb{F}}
is negative for any number field
\mathbb{F}
, so for
n
odd, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)\text{d}t

\displaystyle<0

\displaystyle\implies\int_{T}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)\text{d}t

\displaystyle<-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)\text{d}t

\displaystyle<\ell\int_{0}^{T}t^{2n}\text{d}t

\displaystyle<\ell\,T^{2n+1}.

(4.32)

In the penultimate step, we have used the fact that
-\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)
is a continuous function in the closed interval
[0,T]
and hence it can be bounded by a positive number
\ell
, where
\ell
is independent of
n
. Since
\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)
is positive for
t\geq T
, so

\displaystyle\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)\geq c,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\delta T\leq t\leq T(\delta+1),

where
\delta>1
and
c
is some positive integer. Using the above lower bound in (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\ell\,T^{2n+1}>\int_{T}^{\infty}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)\text{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\delta T}^{T(\delta+1)}\frac{\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)}{\left(t^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}t^{2n}\cosh\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right)\text{d}t

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{\delta T}^{T(\delta+1)}t^{2n}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\geq c\delta^{2n}T^{2n+1}.

Thus,
\ell>\delta^{2n}c
, which fails to hold for sufficiently large
n
, as
\delta>1
. Hence our assumption is wrong. Therefore,
\Xi_{\mathbb{F}}(t)
must change sign infinitely often, which implies that it has infinitely many real zeros and so has
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
on the critical line. This finishes the proof of Theorem
2.9
.

∎

5.
Concluding Remarks

Using the Jacobi theta relation (
1.2
), Hardy proved that there are infinitely many non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line. Interestingly, the Jacobi theta relation is equivalent to the functional equation of
\zeta(s)
. In 1936, Ferrar proved Ramanujan-Koshliakov formula (
1.4
) using the functional equation for
\zeta^{2}(s)
. Moreover, he proved a general theta relation (
2.5
) assuming the functional equation for
\zeta^{k}(s)
, and we showed that the two are in fact equivalent, see Corollary
2.5
.
Motivated by this work, we have established an equivalence of the functional equation for
\zeta^{-k}(s)
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
, see Corollary
2.7
. In particular, we deduced that the functional equation for
\zeta^{-1}(s)
is equivalent to the well-known identity of Hardy, Littlewood and Ramanujan (
1.9
).
It is straightforward to observe that the functional equations for
\zeta(s)
and
\zeta^{-1}(s)
are equivalent, which in turn implies that the corresponding theta identities, namely, Jacobi theta relation (
1.2
) and Hardy, Littlewood and Ramanujan identity (
1.9
) are also equivalent. To the best of our knowledge, this observation has not been noted explicitly, in the existing literature.

It is worthwhile to note that Ferrar
[
21
]
was mainly interested to study solutions to the following theta relation

\displaystyle F(x)=F\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(5.1)

In this paper, we obtained number field analogue of the theta relation which is equivalent to the functional equation for
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}^{k}(s)
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
, see Theorems
2.1
,
2.6
. In fact, the theta relations (
2.1
) and (
2.7
) arising from Theorems
2.1
,
2.6
, respectively, provide a new set of solutions to Ferrar’s equation (
5.1
).

Another important part of this paper involves showing the existence of infinitely many non-trivial zeros of
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}}(s)
on the critical line using the theta relation (
2.3
), see Theorem
2.9
.

Some other works include proving the existence of infinite number of non-trivial zeros of various other
L
-functions on the critical line. Zeros of
L
-functions of degree two in the Selberg class have been studied by Mukhopadhyay et. al.
[
34
]
. Meher et. al.
[
32
,
33
]
showed that there are infinitely many zeros on the critical line for the
L
-functions attached to modular forms of half-integral weight. Kim
[
28
]
studied the infinitude of zeros of additively twisted
L
-function on the critical line. For further insights, one can see
[
15
,
20
,
37
,
38
,
42
]
.

Establishing the existence of infinitely many non-trivial zeros on the critical line remains a challenging problem for higher degree
L
-functions. We speculate that every nice
L-
function will have an analogous theta type relation that might be useful to prove the existence of infinitely many non-trivial zeros on the critical line. Since the Dedekind zeta function serves as a key example, this approach could be useful for a broader class of
L
-functions. In an upcoming paper, we are working on the same problem for the Rankin-Selberg
L-
function and symmetric square
L-
function.

Correlation between structural features and optoelectronic properties in superhalogen doped hexaazakekulenes
Authors: Naveen Kosar, Tariq Mahmood, Sumayya M Ansari, Mazhar Amjad Gilani, Muhammad Ajmal, Mohammed Salim Akhter, Muhammad Imran, Adnan Younis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSuperhalogens, owing to their higher electron affinities than conventional halogens, possess exceptional electron-accepting capabilities, making them attractive candidates for tuning nonlinear optical (NLO) properties. In this study, we systematically investigated the NLO behavior of superhalogen-doped hexaazakekulene (M1) macrocycles using density functional theory (DFT) through both static and dynamic hyperpolarizability analyses. The electronic and thermodynamic stabilities of the doped systems are evaluated via interaction energies (Eint), vertical ionization energies (I), and frontier molecular orbital (FMO) analysis. Among the studied complexes, CaF3–M1 exhibits the highest interaction energy (− 68.33 kcal mol⁻¹), indicating superior binding stability. Static and dynamic NLO analyses revealed substantial improvements in optical responses upon doping. Among the designed systems, MgF3-M1 exhibited the highest static first hyperpolarizability (8.77 × 104 au) and lowest excitation energy (0.90 eV), aligning with the two-level model predictions. Frequency-dependent studies at 532 nm and 1064 nm confirmed enhanced electro-optic Pockels effects and second harmonic generation (SHG), with BeF3-M1 and CaF3-M1 showing particularly strong responses. These variations are attributed to the atomic size and electronic characteristics of the dopant metals. Third-order NLO behavior was also analyzed, showing significant enhancements in second hyperpolarizability (γ) and nonlinear refractive index (n2), especially for CaF3-M1. UV–Vis spectral analysis revealed bathochromic shifts and dual absorption bands, confirming extended π-conjugation and distinct electronic transitions. Overall, superhalogen doping of M1 macrocycles results in remarkable improvements in NLO activity, stability, and optical tunability. These findings highlight their strong potential as advanced materials for photonic and optoelectronic applications.Keywords: NLO, Superhalogen, Hexaazakekulene, Frequency dependent hyperpolarizability, Two level modelSubject terms: Optical materials, Nanoscience and technology, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

Macrocyclic compounds have garnered significant scientific interest over the past two decades due to their broad applicability across diverse research domains, including supramolecular chemistry, materials science, and molecular electronics1,2. These cyclic systems, typically comprising twelve or more atoms, possess high molecular weights and adopt conformationally constrained architectures that facilitate extensive interactions with target species3,4. The incorporation of heteroatoms such as nitrogen, oxygen, and sulfur into macrocyclic frameworks further enhances their reactivity, particularly toward electron-accepting species, thereby expanding their functional versatility5,6. Functionalized macrocycles have been extensively explored for their role in the design of smart molecular devices, supramolecular machines7, nanostructured materials8, therapeutic agents9 and advanced macrocyclic assemblies10. Among these, nitrogen-containing macrocycles—commonly referred to as aza-macrocycles—are particularly noteworthy due to the electronic and structural contribution of nitrogen atoms positioned within the ring or along the periphery11. These compounds have found utility in applications such as light-driven molecular switches, chemical sensors, and synthetic analogs of biological channels12. Notably, their photosensitization capabilities enable efficient conversion of electromagnetic radiation into mechanical or electronic signals, rendering them attractive for integration into light-driven molecular machines13,14.

Recent advances have highlighted the potential of nitrogen-based macrocycles in optical and optoelectronic applications, wherein their properties can be modulated through thermal stimuli, electromagnetic radiation, or photon-based interactions15. Among the various aza-macrocycles reported, classes such as calixarenes, naphthyridines, and kekulene derivatives have received considerable attention. Kekulene macrocycles, named after Friedrich August Kekulé, who proposed the cyclic structure of benzene in 1865, have since evolved through numerous structural modifications aimed at enhancing aromaticity and functional behavior16,17. Particularly, heteroatom-substituted kekulene analogues, such as hexaazakekulenes, have emerged as promising candidates due to their unique aromatic character, thermal and photoresponsive switching abilities, and strong electron-donating/accepting capabilities18,19. The first successful synthesis of hexaazakekulene was reported by Tatibouët et al. in 1997, setting the stage for its subsequent exploration in various functional domains.

Nonlinear optical (NLO) properties are central to assessing the optoelectronic potential of nitrogen-containing macrocycles. Previous studies have shown that the NLO response of such systems can be significantly enhanced by doping with electron-donating or electron-accepting species such as alkali metals20, alkaline earth metals21, superalkalis22, and superhalogens. Superhalogens are molecular clusters consisting of a central metal atom coordinated by highly electronegative ligands. These are well known for their exceptionally high electron affinity often exceeding 10 eV, which super-passes the conventional halogens23,24. Superhalogens are considered efficient candidates for electronic charge transfer process and catalysis. The incorporation of electronegative atom introduces a unique electronic structure that facilitates delocalization of charges across the entire framework, thereby significantly enhancing both the stability and reactivity of the system25–29. The concept of superhalogens was first introduced by Bartlett et al. through the discovery of platinum hexafluoride, which exhibited an electron affinity of 6.76 eV29. Gutsev and Boldyrev were the first to propose the general formula of superhalogens as MXk+1. Here, K represents the maximal valance of atom M while X represents the halogen atom, and M represents the transition metal or main group atom30. Their optimal size, tunable electronic properties, robustness in reactions and compatibility with different host molecules make them versatile for use in any chemical environment. Their exceptionally high electron affinity than halogen make them highly suitable as functional dopants in NLO-active materials, because they can facilitate significant charge transfer leading to reduction in gap of the respective adsorbent surface and increase its polarizability31,32. We elucidated from literature survey that superhalogens effectively improve the electronic behavior of organic and inorganic materials and further enhance their first & second hyperpolarizability which is necessary for NLO response33. Secondly, the size of superhalogen is important during their selection as dopant without destabilizing the individual integrity of the adsorbent and allow potential interactions with the electronic states that leads to effective optical response34. Previous studies show that thermal stability ensures the designing and synthesis of superhalogen doped complexes that can maintain the enhanced NLO response under any chemical environment35. DFT study proved that superhalogen doping on carbon nitride effectively reduced the energy gap and increased their hyperpolarizability values to several order of magnitude36. These studies highlight the critical role of electron affinity in combination with favorable size and thermal stability in choosing superhalogen for the enhancement of NLO response.

Recent computational studies have illustrated the potential of superhalogen doping to enhance NLO characteristics. For instance, Anwar et al. demonstrated a notable increase in first hyperpolarizability upon doping C18S9 thiacirculene with CaCl3, reporting values as high as 6.6 × 104 a.u. for specific orientations37. Similarly, Mahmood and colleagues explored superhalogen-doped molecular “sunflowers” and observed substantial enhancements in their NLO performance29. Motivated by these findings, the present study investigates the NLO properties of superhalogen-doped hexaazakekulene (M1) macrocycles. Three representative superhalogens such as AF3 (A = Ca, Mg, Be)-are selected as dopants due to their high electron affinities and known optoelectronic relevance. Specifically, the work focuses on examining the structural, thermodynamic, and electronic characteristics of both pristine and doped systems, followed by a detailed evaluation of their nonlinear optical responses through static and dynamic (frequency-dependent) polarizability and hyperpolarizability analyses. Furthermore, the two-level model was employed to elucidate the underlying electronic factors governing the variations in hyperpolarizability, offering a deeper understanding of the structure–property relationships that drive enhanced NLO performance in these macrocyclic complexes.

Computational methodologies

The molecular geometries of both pristine and superhalogen-doped kekulene macrocycle (M1) were optimized using the meta-hybrid density functional ωB97XD, incorporating Grimme’s D2 dispersion correction, with the 6–31 + G(d, p) basis set38. ωB97XD is a long-range corrected density functional of density functional theory (DFT) having Grimme’s D2 dispersion correction developed by Chai and Gordon in 200839. Besides dispersion forces, it can effectively estimate the long-range interactions important in doped complexes where charge distribution plays crucial role. This functional provides accurate results specially in complexes where non-covalent interactions are operational40–42. We were inspired by numerous studies on the nonlinear optical (NLO) properties of various metal-doped complexes designed for photonic applications, which support the use of the ωB97XD functional for accurately predicting the NLO responses of such systems43–46.

Frequency calculations at 298 K confirmed that all structures reside at minima on the potential energy surface. Multiplicity of individual superhalogens and all superhalogens doped M1 complexes is doublet but the multiplicity of pure M1 is singlet. The total spin expectation values, ⟨S²⟩, were computed to quantify spin contamination. For the isolated superhalogen radicals (BeF3, MgF3, CaF3), the calculated ⟨S²⟩ values were 0.76, 0.75, and 0.76, respectively. These are close to the ideal value of 0.75 for a pure doublet state, indicating minimal spin contamination. The pure M1 complex exhibited an ⟨S²⟩ value of 0.00, as expected for a closed-shell singlet system. Upon doping, the ⟨S²⟩ values for the BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, and CaF3-M1 complexes were found to be 0.80, 0.80, and 0.84, respectively. The deviation from the ideal doublet value confirms the open-shell character of these doped complexes, consistent with their electronic structure as radical species. Results and discussion

Tatibouët et al. first synthesized the 3,9,15,19,21,23-Hexaazakekulene macrocycle (M1) in 199763. The optimized molecular geometry of M1, obtained using density functional theory (DFT) at the ωB97XD/6–31 + G(d, p) level (see Fig. 1), exhibits Cs point group symmetry. This symmetry accounts for the molecule’s zero dipole moment. The calculated average bond lengths for the C–C bonds within each aromatic ring and the C–N bonds of the pyridine moieties are 1.46 Å and 1.33 Å, respectively. Additionally, the bond angle between carbon–nitrogen–carbon atoms (∠C–N–C) in the unmodified M1 structure is found to be 119.27°. These findings are consistent with the results previously reported by Wang et al.64, thereby supporting the reliability of the current computational model. The selected superhalogens including CaF3, BeF3, and MgF3 are optimized by using ωB97XD/6–31 + G(d, p) level of DFT (optimized geometries are shown as Fig. 1). For better understanding of structure and activity the key electronic properties of super halogens including vertical ionization energy (I), electron affinity (A), electronegativity (X), harness (η), softness (σ), chemical potential () and electrophilicity (ω) parameters are calculated and results are given in Table 1. The calculated ionization potential (I) of considered superhalogens ranges from 12.73 to 14.16 eV, indicating their stability and significant resistance toward ionization. Electron affinity (A) of BeF3, MgF3, and CaF3 is 0.87, 0.34, and 0.16 eV, respectively, reflecting the high electron affinity a hallmark trait of superhalogens. The chemical potential (µ) of selected superhalogens spanning from − 6.45 to − 7.25 eV, depicts a strong electron seeking nature and a pronounced tendency to gain electrons. Among the three, BeF₃ demonstrates the highest electrophilicity (ω), making it especially favourable for electron transfer processes. Furthermore, the nucleophilicity index (χ) reaching up to 7.25 eV supports the strong electron-attracting behaviour of these species, suggesting potential reactivity in electron-rich environments. MgF3 displays the highest chemical hardness (η) at 6.91 eV, signifying strong resistance to electronic deformation. In contrast, their calculated softness (S) values are relatively low (≤ 0.8 eV), which is indicative of structural stability. The HOMO (EH), LUMO (EL), and the corresponding H-L energy gaps are as follows: BeF3 (–13.55, 0.87, 14.42 eV), MgF3 (–14.16, 0.34, 14.50 eV), and CaF3 (–12.73, 0.16, 12.89 eV). High H–L energy gaps further confirm the excellent electronic stability of selected superhalogens. Collectively, the calculated electronic properties underscore the strong electron-accepting capability and high stability of these superhalogens, particularly BeF3, aligning well with previous literature findings37,65,66.

Geometric and thermodynamic properties of superhalogen doped M1 macrocycles

The selected superhalogen (CaF3, BeF3, and MgF3) are doped horizontally over the central cavity of the M1 macrocycle. In this configuration, the alkaline earth metal (AEM) centers and their associated fluorine atoms directly interact with the macrocyclic framework. Upon full geometric optimization, all complexes converged to their most stable configurations, with the superhalogens adopting a one-sided orientation relative to the M1 plane. The optimized structures of superhalogen-doped complexes are depicted in Fig. 2.

Optimized structures of superhalogens doped M1 macromolecule (BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1 and CaF3-M1).

In their isolated forms, the average bond lengths between AEM (Be, Mg, Ca) and fluorine atoms in BeF3, MgF3, and CaF3 are computed as 1.48 Å, 1.83 Å, and 2.15 Å, respectively. This trend reflects the expected increase in bond length with the increasing atomic radius of the alkaline earth metal (AEM) species. Upon complexation with the M1 macrocycle, these AEM–F bond lengths slightly increased to 1.49 Å (BeF3-M1), 1.84 Å (MgF3-M1), and 2.21 Å (CaF3-M1), indicating structural perturbation induced by interaction with the macrocyclic cavity. The extent of interaction between the superhalogen clusters and the M1 framework is further evaluated through interatomic distances between the AEM atom and the nitrogen atom (N₆₁) of M1. The calculated interaction distances are 2.83 Å (Be67-N61), 2.69 Å (Mg67-N61), and 2.68 Å (Ca67-N61), for BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, and CaF3-M1, respectively. Notably, the CaF3-M1 complex exhibits the shortest N–Ca interaction distance, suggesting the strongest binding among the three. MgF3-M1 also displays enhanced interaction with M1 compared to BeF3-M1, as evidenced by its shorter N–Mg distance. In addition to AEM interactions, the average bond lengths between the fluorine atoms of the superhalogens and the carbon atoms of M1 are also assessed. These distances are found as 2.65 Å (F70-C15), 2.78 Å ((F70-C15), and 1.48 Å ((F70-C5) in BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, CaF3-M1 highlighting the role of fluorine in mediating secondary interactions with the macrocycle. These findings underscore the importance of both AEM and F atoms in stabilizing the overall supramolecular architecture.

Bond angle analysis further supports these observations. The average internal angles ∠F–AEM–F in the free superhalogen species is calculated as approximately 120°. Upon complexation with M1, this angle slightly decreased to 119.65° (BeF3-M1), 119.50° (MgF3-M1), and 119.12° (CaF3-M1), indicative of minor angular distortions due to host–guest interactions. All doped complexes exhibited C₁ point group symmetry, reflecting asymmetric binding geometries induced by the superhalogen moieties.

To evaluate the thermodynamic stability of the complexes, interaction energies (Eint) are computed, with results summarized in Table 2. The interaction energies ranged from − 47.91 to − 68.33 kcal mol− 1, confirming the exothermic nature of complex formation. These negative values affirm the thermodynamic favorability of the doping process and suggest potential feasibility for experimental realization. Iqbal and co-workers investigated the nonlinear optical (NLO) properties of superhalogen-doped boron nitride complexes, reporting interaction energies ranging from − 10.13 to − 5.44 kcal mol− 1. Based on these values, they concluded that the complexes are thermally stable and possess notable NLO responses32. Misra and coworkers designed superhalogen doped superalkali@C60 complexes with negative interaction energies, indicating the experimental feasibility of considered complexes67. These reports justify the results our superhalogen doped M1 complexes and ensure the experimental synthetic feasibility of these complexes.

Notably, CaF3-M1 exhibited the most favorable interaction energy (–68.33 kcal mol− 1), followed by MgF3-M1 (–58.61 kcal mol− 1) and BeF3-M1 (–47.91 kcal mol− 1), establishing the following stability trend: CaF3-M1 > MgF3-M1 > BeF3-M1.

This hierarchy in stability is consistent with the observed bond distances and may be attributed to the lower ionization potential of Ca, which enhances its binding affinity toward the M1 macrocycle. These results are in agreement with similar observations by Shakerzadeh et al.68 for Li-doped complexes, and are comparable in magnitude to the interaction energies reported by Anwar et al.69 for superhalogen-doped sunflower-type systems. It is worth noting that in contrast, Kosar et al.70 reported greater stability for Be-doped fullerenes, emphasizing that interaction strengths are highly dependent on the host system and doping geometry.

Electron transport properties and NBO charge analysis of superhalogen doped M1 complexes

Frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs) analysis is examined to elucidate the electronic transport properties of pure and superhalogen doped M1 complexes. Firstly, the vertical ionization energy (I) of pure M1 and superhalogen doped M1 complexes (BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1 and CaF3-M1) is calculated at the ωB97XD/6–31 + G(d, p) method which is a key parameter for assessing the electronic stability. As summarized in Table 1, the BeF3-M1 complex exhibits the highest I of 8.07 eV, followed closely by MgF3-M1 (7.97 eV) and CaF3-M1 (7.96 eV). These lower I value compared to superhalogen reflect the generation of conductivity and improved charge transfer potential after doping with M1 surface. These values notify potential semiconducting behavior of all complexes. Electron affinity (A) of BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, and CaF3-M1 complexes is 1.47, 1.56 and 1.21 eV, respectively. The increase in A value upon doping demonstrates enhanced electron acceptance of superhalogens in complexation. The higher A of MgF3-M1 complex correspond to its greater ability to accept the electrons. The I and A values of M1 are 7.84 and 0.83 eV, respectively. The chemical potential (µ) in the rang of −4.77 to −4.59 eV illustrates the strong tendency to moderate electron seeking ability and electrophilicity which is also justified from the decrease in ω values (up to 3.54 eV). But still the ω values are higher that depicts the stronger charge transfer with in each complex. Nucleophilicity (X) of these complexes decreases up to 4.77 eV, which demonstrates a reduction in their electron-donating tendency due to charge depletion, making the surface less nucleophilic. The ω, X and η values of M1 are 2.68, 4.34 and 3.51 eV, respectively. These values increase after doping of superhalogens. MgF₃-M1 complex has lower η value of 3.21 eV that depicts reactivity of the complex. Softness (S) values are lower compared to hardness values (up to 0.16 eV) which show more stability consistent to the softness of pure M1 surface (0.14 eV). The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO, EH) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO, EL) energies for pristine M1 are calculated to be − 7.84 eV and − 0.83 eV, respectively, yielding an energy gap (EL−H) of 7.01 eV. Upon superhalogen doping, these values undergo noticeable shifts. In the BeF3-M1 complex, EH and EL are − 8.07 eV and − 1.47 eV, respectively, corresponding to a reduced energy gap of 6.60 eV. For MgF3-M1, the values are − 7.97 eV (EH) and − 1.56 eV (EL), with a resulting gap of 6.41 eV. Similarly, CaF₃-M1 exhibits EH = − 7.96 eV and EL = − 1.21 eV, leading to a gap of 6.75 eV. This decreasing trend in EL−H (MgF3-M1 < BeF3-M1 < CaF3-M1) can be attributed to the downward shifts in both HOMO and LUMO energies, with the LUMO levels being more significantly affected due to electron density delocalization towards the superhalogen units.

The spatial distribution of FMOs is further visualized through isodensity surface plots for the M1 macrocycle and its doped analogues (see Fig. 3). In all doped systems, the HOMO isodensity is primarily localized on the interaction region of the M1 macrocycle and partially over the fluorine atoms of the superhalogen, suggesting orbital mixing at the host–guest interface. Conversely, the LUMO isodensity remains localized exclusively on the M1 macrocyclic scaffold, reflecting a spatial separation of electronic excitation that may be advantageous for optoelectronic applications.

The lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMOs) and the highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMOs) of pure M1 macrocycle, and superhalogens (BeF3, MgF3 and CaF3) doped M1 complexes.

Doping with superhalogens also results in a notable increase in the dipole moment (µ) of the macrocyclic framework. The calculated dipole moments for BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, and CaF3-M1 are 7.06, 6.56, and 6.36 Debye, respectively. Among them, BeF3-M1 displays the highest µ, suggesting stronger charge separation and potentially higher nonlinear optical (NLO) activity, followed by MgF3-M1 and CaF3-M1. To further explore the nature of electronic interactions, natural bond orbital (NBO) charge analysis was conducted to quantify the charge transfer between the dopant (superhalogen) and the complexant (M1 macrocycle) during complexation71. Initially, the isolated M1 macrocycle is electrically neutral. However, upon complexation, significant charge redistribution is observed. The net NBO charges on the BeF3, MgF3, and CaF3 fragments in their respective complexes are − 0.93 |e|, − 0.93 |e|, and − 0.46 |e|, indicating substantial electron density transfer from the M1 macrocycle to the superhalogen units. The corresponding positive charge accumulated on the M1 confirms this directionality. This behavior is consistent with earlier findings by Li et al.72 on superhalogen-based NLO clusters, and aligns with the charge delocalization trends described by Reed and Weinhold in hydrogen-bonded systems71. Such intermolecular charge migration enhances both thermal and electronic stability in the doped complexes.

Static polarizability and first hyperpolarizability analyses

The linear and nonlinear optical (NLO) responses of superhalogen-doped M1 complexes were evaluated through calculations of the static polarizability (α₀) and first hyperpolarizability (β₀). The numerical values of these parameters are presented in Table 3. The α₀ value of the pristine M1 macrocycle is 622 atomic units (au). Upon superhalogen doping, a marked enhancement in polarizability is observed. Specifically, α₀ values of 711 au, 777 au, and 644 au are recorded for BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, and CaF3-M1, respectively. The MgF3-M1 complex demonstrates the highest polarizability, suggesting greater electronic delocalization and an increased linear optical response. These findings are consistent with prior observations where similar enhancement in polarizability upon superhalogen doping of a B36 nanocluster were presented35.

The observed trend in first hyperpolarizability (MgF3-M1 > BeF3-M1 > CaF3-M1) correlates inversely with the HOMO–LUMO energy gap (EL−H). Specifically, MgF3-M1, which possesses the lowest EL−H of 6.41 eV, exhibits the highest β₀, whereas CaF3-M1, with a relatively larger gap of 6.75 eV, shows the lowest β₀ value. This inverse relationship suggests that reduced EL−H facilitates intramolecular charge transfer, thereby enhancing the NLO response.

Many researchers have proposed that the nonlinear optical (NLO) response of a system, whether doped or undoped is governed primarily by three internal parameters: the change in dipole moment between ground and excited states (∆µ), oscillating strength (fo) and variational excitation energies (∆E). These parameters are interrelated through the two-level model, which provides an effective framework for interpreting hyperpolarizability (β₀)73. According to this model, β₀ is directly proportional to both ∆µ and f₀, and inversely proportional to the cube of ∆E. To further elucidate the origin of the enhanced NLO response in superhalogen-doped M1 complexes, the two-level model was applied by calculating ∆µ, f₀, and ∆E for BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, and CaF3-M1. The computed values are summarized in Table 3.

The excitation energy (∆E) values span from 0.90 to 3.61 eV. Among the studied complexes, MgF3-M1 exhibits the lowest ∆E value of 0.90 eV, correlating with its highest β₀ of 8.77 × 10⁴ au. BeF3-M1 shows a ∆E of 1.24 eV, while CaF3-M1 has the highest ∆E of 3.61 eV, accompanied by the lowest β₀ (8.89 × 10³ au). This inverse relationship supports the conclusion that a reduced excitation energy significantly enhances the NLO response. The oscillator strength (fo) values are comparable across the complexes, with BeF3-M1 and MgF3-M1 both exhibiting a value of 0.10 au, and CaF3-M1 slightly lower at 0.09 au. While fo generally aligns with the trend in β₀ for MgF3-M1 and CaF3-M1, a deviation is noted in the case of BeF3-M1, suggesting fo may not always be the dominant contributor.

The computed ∆µ values for BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, and CaF3-M1 are 7.06, 6.56, and 6.35 Debye, respectively. Unlike ∆E, these values do not exhibit a clear correlation with the trend in β₀, further reinforcing the dominant role of ∆E in modulating the NLO behavior as per the two-level model. In summary, the two-level model analysis confirms that excitation energy (∆E) is the most influential parameter governing the enhancement of first hyperpolarizability in superhalogen-doped M1 complexes, with oscillator strength and dipole moment changes playing secondary roles.

Ultraviolet–Visible (UV–Vis) spectroscopic analysis of superhalogen-doped M1 complexes

Ultraviolet–visible (UV–Vis) spectroscopy was performed to assess the optical absorption characteristics of both pristine and superhalogen-doped M1 macrocyclic complexes. The absorption maxima (λ_max) for each system are summarized in Table 3. The undoped M1 macrocycle exhibits an absorption maximum at 278 nm, corresponding to a π → π* transition in the mid-ultraviolet region.

Upon incorporation of superhalogen units (BeF3, MgF3, and CaF3), all modified complexes demonstrate pronounced bathochromic shifts in their λmax values, indicating a redshift in absorption toward longer wavelengths (Fig. 5). Specifically, BeF3-M1, MgF3-M1, and CaF3-M1 exhibit absorption maxima at 997 nm, 1372 nm, and 343 nm, respectively. This spectral redshift is attributed to enhanced intramolecular charge transfer and modulation of the electronic structure induced by superhalogen doping, which reduces the energy gap between the ground and excited states. Among the investigated systems, MgF3-M1 displays the most significant redshift, with an absorption maximum extending into the near-infrared region, while CaF3-M1 exhibits the least shift. These findings are consistent with previous studies, who observed similar doping-induced spectral shifts in superhalogen-functionalized Li@C60 clusters86–89.

All doped complexes also exhibit two distinct absorption bands, which are ascribed to different types of electronic transitions: (i) n → π* transitions originating from non-bonding electron pairs, and (ii) π → π* transitions associated with conjugated systems. The coexistence of these transitions further highlights the altered electronic configuration and extended conjugation brought about by superhalogen doping29. The observed modulation of optical absorption profiles confirms that superhalogen incorporation significantly influences the electronic transition landscape of M1 macrocycles. These tunable optical properties underscore the suitability of these systems for potential deployment in advanced optoelectronic applications, including UV–NIR photodetectors, optical modulators, and nonlinear optical devices.

Conclusions

In this study, a comprehensive density functional theory (DFT) investigation was conducted to evaluate the nonlinear optical (NLO) properties of superhalogen-doped hexaazakekulene (M1) macrocyclic complexes. The strategic incorporation of superhalogen moieties such as BeF3, MgF3, and CaF3 led to substantial enhancements in the structural stability and electronic behavior of the M1 framework. These improvements were evidenced by significantly negative interaction energies ranging from 47.91 to 68.33 kcal mol− 1, reduced HOMO–LUMO gaps, and altered natural bond orbital (NBO) charge distributions, collectively indicating stronger intermolecular interactions and enhanced charge delocalization. Among the designed systems, the MgF3-M1 complex emerged as the most promising NLO candidate, displaying the highest static first hyperpolarizability (β₀ = 8.77 × 104 au) and the lowest excitation energy (∆E = 0.90 eV). This result was further corroborated by a two-level model analysis, which revealed a strong correlation between hyperpolarizability and key internal parameters such as excitation energy and dipole moment variation. Frequency-dependent NLO assessments at operational laser wavelengths of 532 nm and 1064 nm further validated the potential of these systems for real-world photonic and optoelectronic applications. Notably, BeF3-M1 demonstrated the most pronounced electro-optic Pockels effect at 1064 nm, while CaF3-M1 exhibited the highest second harmonic generation (SHG) response at 532 nm, underscoring the critical influence of metal identity and atomic size on NLO behavior.

Additionally, the UV–Vis spectral analysis revealed distinct bathochromic shifts upon superhalogen doping, with λmax values extending from the ultraviolet to the near-infrared region. These shifts highlight improved light-harvesting properties and support the observed optical transitions, including both n → π* and π → π* excitations. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that superhalogen functionalization effectively modulates the structural, electronic, and optical properties of hexaazakekulene-based macrocycles. The enhanced NLO response, tunable absorption features, and thermodynamic stability position these superhalogen-doped M1 complexes as highly promising candidates for next-generation nonlinear optical materials in photonic and optoelectronic technologies.

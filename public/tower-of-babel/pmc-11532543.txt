Supported phenylalanine on core-shell mesoporous microsphere as a catalyst for the synthesis of triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-triones and spiro triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-tetraones
Authors: Mahnaz Mirheidari, Ali Kareem Abbas, Javad Safaei-Ghomi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In recent years, mesoporous silica materials have gained attention due to their unique properties, such as high surface area, pore volume, size, and chemical stability. These characteristics make them effective in various fields, particularly efficient supports in heterogeneous catalytic reactions. The present study developed a novel type of core-shell mesoporous microsphere material by anchoring phenylalanine on a core-shell SiO2@NiO@MS support. The catalyst support (SiO2@NiO@MS) was synthesized through homogeneous precipitation and sol-gel methods, with NiO encapsulated within the porous silica. The catalyst was characterized using various techniques, including Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR), X-ray diffraction (XRD), Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), Energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS), Elemental mapping analysis, N2 adsorption-desorption, Transmission electron microscopy (TEM), Vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM), and Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). It was then employed for the synthesis of triazolo[1,2-a]indazole-trione and spiro triazolo[1,2-a]indazole-tetraones compounds from 4-phenyl urazole, dimedone, and aromatic aldehydes or isatin derivatives. This synthetic approach offers multiple advantages, including high yields, faster reaction times, low catalyst requirements, and environmentally sustainable conditions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-78018-w.

Introduction

Core–shell nanostructures have garnered significant attention due to their multi-functionality provided by the multiple layers of coating, which can modify the structure’s properties1–3. These layers can change the chemical properties, enhance stability against aggregation, and improve the catalytic activity of the inner core. Additionally, increasing the number of shells can expand the materials’ applications in various fields, such as catalysis, medicine, and treatment4–7.

Organic–inorganic hybrid heterogeneous porous material with distinct structures, specific surface areas, and open-pore architectures play a crucial role in various fields such as ion exchange, life sciences, adsorption and separation, and catalysis8–12. Mesoporous ordered silica, with its hexagonal structure, exhibits notable characteristics such as adjustable pore size, high porosity, thick walls, and excellent thermal and chemical stability. These properties make it well-suited for applications in drug delivery systems, catalysis, and separation processes9,13–15.

Generally, silica is more biocompatible than other metal oxide nanoparticles, like iron and titanium oxide, and is more commonly found in the environment16. The rigid structure of silica does not swell on the solvents, this benefit causes the silica can be used in low and high temperatures17. Since silica is chemically inert with mechanical and thermal stability, it has been used as an attractive inorganic support materials18 to provide new compounds19. On the other hand, nickel oxide nanoparticles as transition metal oxides have attracted considerable attention due to their unique characteristics such as magnetic, electrical, and optical properties20,21. They have different applications in catalysis22, electronic compounds20, and sensors23. Also, recent studies have shown the importance of NiO nanoparticles in biomedical applications such as cancer treatment21.

The combination of silica with nickel oxide nanoparticles can enhance adsorption capacity and catalytic activity, while also improving thermal conductivity, magnetic properties, and corrosion resistance24–26.

It is crucial to design mesoporous silica nanoparticles with an appropriate surface to enable effective molecule loading. The presence of silanol (Si–OH) groups on the surface of mesoporous silica allows for covalent bonding with other guest organic groups, such as organocatalysts27. Amino acids with chiral structures are commonly used as organocatalysts in various reactions, including Michael reactions, epoxidation, and aldol reactions28,29. These compounds are inexpensive and do not require specific methods to carry out catalytic reactions30–32.

Organic catalytic reactions have emerged as powerful synthetic tools for the synthesis of highly functionalized compounds. Eco‐friendly and economically heterogeneous catalysts to activate organic functional group transformations are useful and in high demand by academic laboratories and industries33–36. Organic reactions using heterogeneous catalysts are extremely important for the development of sustainable chemical processes. Over the past few decades, multicomponent reactions (MCRs) have become popular in organic chemistry due to their many advantages such as green reaction conditions, speed, efficiency, and atom economy37–43.

Nitrogen-based heterocyclic compounds as an important analog of organic chemistry have attracted the attention of many researchers because of their biological applications and other scientific uses44–46. Research results have shown that compounds with urazole moiety like Triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-trione compounds possess considerable biological and medical activities such as anticancer, anticonvulsant, and hypolipidemic47–50. Up to date, Triazolo[1,2-a]indazole-trione and spiro triazolo[1,2-a]indazole-tetraones compounds have been synthesized through different preparation routes and different catalysts such as ZnO@SiO251, SBA-15-Ph-SO3H52, p-TSA53, FeNi3/quinuclidine54, Origanum-majorana Ag NPs55, which some methods have problems such as long reaction times, low yields51, and toxic solvent56. Based on the above information and our research aimed at addressing these issues, we have designed and synthesized SiO2@NiO@MS-Phenylalanine as a new recyclable bifunctional catalyst. This catalyst offers high efficiency and stability for the rapid and straightforward synthesis of triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-trione and spiro triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-tetraone compounds under solvent-free conditions.

Experimental section

Substances and methods

All organic compounds were purchased commercially from Sigma-Aldrich and Merck. The melting point of products was measured by Electrothermal 9200. FT-IR measurements were carried out on a Nicolet Magna-IR 550 spectrometer using KBr plates. 1H NMR data were recorded in DMSO-d6 on a Bruker Avance DRX instrument. XRD patterns were obtained by using X’PertPro (Philips, PW 1510, Netherlands) instrument. Thermal analysis was carried out on a Bahr STA type.

The scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) and energy-dispersive X-ray analysis were carried out by (MIRA3-TESCAN FESEM). The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) image analysis was carried out used Zeiss-EM10C-100 kV. The magnetic properties of samples were measured using a vibrating-sample magnetometer (VSM) (Meghnatis Daghigh Kavir Co.; Kashan Kavir; Iran). The N2 adsorption/desorption was measured at 77 K using an automated gas adsorption analyzer (BELSORP-mini, Japan).

Catalyst preparation

The mixture of distilled water (20 ml) and ethanol (50 ml) was sonicated for 30 min. Then, 3 ml TEOS and 0.1 mmol PVP were dissolved into the mixture under stirring. After that, ethylenediamine (0.1 ml) was added to the solution as a precipitating agent under ultrasonic irradiation. After 30 min, the resulting SiO2 was isolated by centrifugation and washed with ethanol and water thrice. The final product was dried overnight at 80 °C57.

Then, Nickel (II) Nitrate Hexahydrate (5 mmol) and urea (33.3 mmol) were dispersed into deionized water (20 ml) under ultrasonic dispersion. Thereafter, dispersed silica nanoparticles in water (100 mg SiO2 in 20 ml deionized water) were slowly added to the above-prepared mixture while they were stirring at 70 °C, and kept stirring for 5h. The obtained product was washed with ethanol and water several times and dried at 60 °C overnight to get a pure solid58.

Core–shell structured SiO2@NiO@MS mesoporous microspheres were synthesized using a co-surfactant templating approach. CTAB (0.019 g) and P123 (0.38 g) were dissolved in 130 ml HCl to form a uniform clear solution. Then, 0.15 g of as-prepared SiO2@NiO was added to the above mixture and sonicated for 30 min to achieve a homogeneous mixture. Then, 0.15 g of TEOS was added dropwise to the mixture under stirring over 15 min at 40 °C and then kept stirring for an additional 24h. The resulting microsphere product was centrifuged and washed three times with ethanol and water. Next, the obtained sample and 40 ml of HCl aqueous solution (pH = 2) were transferred into the Teflon autoclave for further hydrothermal treatment and heated at 100 °C for 24 h. Again, the obtained product was collected by centrifugation and washed with distilled water several times, and dried at 100 °C overnight. In the end, to remove the template, the final product was calcined at 550 °C for 6 h.

In the next step, 3-chloropropyl triethoxysilane (CPTES) (0.5 ml, 5 mmol) was added dropwise over 10 min to a stirred suspension of SiO2@NiO@MS (0.5 g) in dry toluene (15 ml) and refluxed for 24 h. Then, the obtained solid was collected from the solution by centrifugation and washed three times with toluene. In the end, it was dried in a vacuum oven at 120 °C for 8 h to afford the white powder as SiO2@NiO@MS@CPTES solid.

In the end, in a 50 ml round-bottom flask, phenylalanine (0.8 g, 5 mmol) was added to the suspension of SiO2@NiO@MS@CPTES (0.5 g) in absolute ethanol (30 ml) and refluxed for 24 h. The obtained insoluble solid was collected by filtration washed with ethanol several times and dried overnight at 90 °C to obtain the final chiral catalyst.

General procedure of triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-trione and spiro triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-tetraones compounds

In a 50 ml round-bottom flask, dimedone (1 mmol), 4-phenyl urazole (1 mmol), aromatic aldehydes or isatin derivatives (1 mmol) and 18 mg of the catalyst (SiO2@NiO@MS-phenylalanine) were placed and mixed at 40 °C under solvent-free conditions until the completion of the reaction (the development of reaction was monitored by TLC). The reaction mixture was separated from the catalyst by dissolving in hot ethanol and the nano catalyst was separated by centrifugation. The solvent was removed and the product was washed with ethanol and then dried to get the pure compounds.

Results and discussion

Structural analysis of the SiO2@NiO@MS-phenylalanine

To prepare the new catalyst, the silica surface was first covered by Nickel oxide. Then, to increase the surface and thermal and chemical stabilities of the catalyst, mesoporous silica was immobilized on the SiO2@NiO surface. Mesoporous silica materials have hydroxyl groups on their surface that can form a strong bond with other functional groups. Optimization the reaction

To show the superior catalytic performance of SiO2@NiO@MS-Phenylalanine and find the optimized reaction conditions to get the best result, various factors consisting of catalyst, solvent, temperature, and amount of the catalyst were evaluated on the model reaction between isatin, 4-phenylurazole, and dimedone. Different catalysts were tested in the reaction and the results revealed the reaction in the presence of SiO2@NiO@MS-Phenylalnine has the best consequence. The efficiency of different solvents, amount of catalyst, and temperature also exhibited the reaction in the presence of SiO2@NiO@MS-Phenylalnine catalyst (18 mg) under the solvent-free condition at 40 °C make the product in high yield. To further evaluate the catalytic efficiency of SiO2@NiO@MS-Phenylalanine, a comparison was made with various catalysts previously reported in the literature for the synthesis of 6,6-dimethyl-9 (4-chlorophenyl)-2-phenyl-5,6,7,9-tetrahydro-1H,8H-[1,2,4]triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-1,3,8(2H)-trione and 6’,6'-dimethyl-2'-phenyl-6',7'dihydro-1'H-spiro[indoline-3,9'-[1,2,4]triazolo[1,2-a]indazole]-1',2,3',8'(2'H,5'H)-tetraone. some methods have problems such as toxic solvents, use of homogeneous catalysts, and higher temperatures. Proposed mechanism

In suggesting mechanism, dimedone first is converted to its enol form in the presence of the catalyst (SiO2@NiO@MS-phenylalnine). Then it attacks a carbonyl group of benzaldehyde activated by the catalyst to create an intermediate (I). Then, the nitrogen of 4-phenyl urazole (3) reacts with the unsaturated carbon in conjugation with the carbonyl through Michel addition and gives an intermediate (II) which by removing water, the final product is formed (Scheme 2). Scheme 3 suggests the mechanism of spirotriazolo[1,2-a] indazole-tetrons, and as in Scheme 2, dimedone (1) is first converted to its enol form in the presence of a catalyst. It then attacks an isatin carbonyl group (4), which is activated by the catalyst to form intermediate (III). Catalyst reusability

The catalyst reusability was assessed for the model reaction. To recover the catalyst, the catalyst was separated by centrifugation washed with water, and then dried at 50 °C for 14h. The catalyst performance after recovering showed the less decrease from 94% in the first run to 88% after six times (Fig. 10). The SEM and TEM analysis for the reused catalyst show no significant changes in the morphology of the catalyst (Fig. 11). A hot filtration test was performed to probe the leaching of the catalyst. In this process, a mixture containing dimedone (1 mmol), 4-phenyl urazole (1 mmol), aromatic aldehyde derivatives (1 mmol), Ethanol, and SiO2@NiO@MS-Phenyl alanine (18 mg) was stirred at 78 °C. After half the reaction time, the catalyst was separated from the mixture. The reaction mixture without the catalyst was then allowed for 30 min. No conversion increase was observed after separating the nanocatalyst from the reaction mixture. Conclusion

In summary, we describe the synthesis of a new core–shell mesoporous microsphere catalyst, featuring an SBA-15-type architecture in the outer layer (SiO2@NiO@MS-phenylalanine). The prepared SiO2@NiO nanocomposite was coated with a mesoporous silicon layer, featuring large tunable pore sizes, using cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) as a co-surfactant and P123 as a triblock copolymer. The catalyst’s potential was evaluated by employing it in the synthesis of triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-trione and spiro triazolo[1,2-a] indazole-tetraones compounds. The results demonstrated the catalyst’s high efficiency, achieving a product yield of up to 97% in a short time.

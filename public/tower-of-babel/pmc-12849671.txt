A different variant of economic exploitation in oncology practice: medical board applications in Türkiye
Authors: Arif Garbioğlu, Duygu Bayır Garbioğlu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe process of cancer evolving into a chronic disease creates a significant economic burden on social security and healthcare resources, with the increase in the elderly population. This study analyzes the changes in the medical board application patterns of cancer patients during the COVID-19 pandemic in the context of possible economic exploitation (i.e., the intentional acquisition of financial or social resources) through abuse of the healthcare system.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted using data from cancer patients who applied to a medical faculty hospital in Türkiye to obtain a medical board report during the pandemic period (March 11, 2020-March 10, 2021) and the pre-pandemic period (March 11, 2019-March 10, 2020).ResultsOf the 382 cancer patients in the study sample, 219 who met the inclusion criteria were analyzed. The rate of stage 4 diseases (p = 0.026), the rate of not being in remission (p = 0.043), the rate of partial dependency (p = 0.046) and the rate of medical board application being made by a relative on behalf of the patient (p = 0.030) were higher in medical board applications during the pandemic period. Receiving social care benefits and taking advantage of exemptions from special consumption tax were more common reasons for medical board applications during the pandemic period (p = 0.001).ConclusionsThe primary limitation is the retrospective design, which prevents the definitive confirmation of exploitative intent and introduces a potential for interpretative bias. Although it is essential for cancer patients and their relatives to exercise the social rights provided to them to relieve their burden related to the disease, our findings indicate that the pandemic may further exacerbate existing weaknesses in the system. Legislators and health practitioners should strengthen control mechanisms in terms of both ethical processes and sustainability of healthcare resources.

Introduction

Global life expectancy at birth has increased over the two-decade period from the early 2000s to the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic. However, the COVID-19 pandemic reversed this trend [1]. Nevertheless, the population aged 60 and above is expected to double by 2050 [2]. While the proportion of the elderly population in the total population in Türkiye was 9.1% in 2019, it increased to 10.6% in 2024 [3]. Aging increases the prevalence of chronic health conditions. Cancer, which can affect individuals of all ages, mostly the elderly, is an important public health problem that trends towards becoming a chronic disease with the developments in early diagnosis methods and treatment modalities [4].

Since the presence of a chronic disease also leads to disability in individuals, this is especially challenging for cancer patients and their relatives, whose treatment processes include both psychosocial and economic difficulties. A number of social support mechanisms have been established, such as home care assistance, care services, disability pensions, quotas for employment of persons with disabilities, early retirement, and tax deductions or exemptions regarding vehicle purchases in order to prevent socioeconomic losses of disabled individuals and their relatives in global health systems [5, 6]. Individuals apply to multidisciplinary medical boards that identify their disability status according to standard regulations in order to have access to these rights. These support policies, designed to alleviate the socioeconomic burden of the disease, may sometimes present an opportunity for abuse [7]. Individual differences, prognosis and variability in the treatment process may make objectivity of disability assessments difficult [8].

Measures such as quarantines, hospitalization restrictions in elective cases, closing schools and workplaces, and travel restrictions implemented during the COVID-19 pandemic have significantly changed the patterns of use of healthcare services [9, 10]. A number of restrictions, such as stay-at-home orders, were imposed on the elderly population, especially those with comorbid diseases, who were more susceptible to COVID-19 disease [11]. During this period, a general global decrease was observed in non-emergency hospital admissions [9].

The World Health Organization’s report states that approximately 16% of the world population was disabled in 2021 and that disabled individuals may be exposed to health inequalities due to preventable disruptions in the rights and justice system [1]. Cancer may also be a significant cause of disability by negatively affecting the daily life quality of both the patient and the caregiver [12]. In this context, patients and their relatives should be supported to have certain rights within the scope of health policies during the chronic disease process. In a pivotal study analyzing the awareness of cancer patients in our country regarding their rights, it was found that patients did not exercise most of the rights granted to them and were not even aware of some rights [13]. On the other hand, the possibility that the defined rights may be abused by the patients’ relatives is an antithesis that should be taken into consideration.

Economic exploitation includes intentional interventions aimed at the acquisition, use or protection of financial and social resources (transportation, public services, etc.) by an individual [14]. The gain obtained through the abuse of the healthcare system is the focus of this study as one of the different aspects of economic exploitation. Accordingly, this study examines the changes in the frequency and nature of medical board applications by cancer patients before and during the COVID-19 pandemic in Türkiye, identifies associated patient and disease factors, and discusses the implications of these findings for health policy and system safeguards.

Methods

Ethical approval

This study was conducted in compliance with the ethical principles according to the Declaration of Helsinki. This retrospective study was approved by the Local Ethics Committee (No. 2024/12, Date. June 06, 2024).

Definitions

The rate of disability of individuals is identified by a medical board consisting of physicians specialized in their fields, taking into account the international standards, under the “Regulation on Disability Assessment for Adults”, which is also reported to be compatible with the American Medical Association Guides to the Evaluation of Permanent Impairment [15, 16].

A wide range of tax concessions granted to disabled individuals are regulated in the Turkish tax legislation; for example, these include the exemptions and discounts provided in income tax, special consumption tax (SCT), value added tax, property tax, motor vehicle tax and customs duty [17]. In Türkiye, applications to medical boards are made for reasons such as disability pension, care pension, home care assistance, utilizing disabled care services, early retirement, various tax deductions, free or discounted travel, free entrance to museums, historical sites, national parks and theaters, and cultural and recreational access rights [18].

Study design and data collection

The study sample consisted of cancer patients who applied to the Medical Board of Zonguldak Bülent Ecevit University Medical Faculty Hospital in Türkiye to obtain a medical board report. The pandemic period consisted of cancer cases who applied between 11.03.2020 and 10.03.2021. The pre-pandemic period consisted of cancer cases who applied between 11.03.2019 and 10.03.2020 to prevent seasonal changes and provide temporal symmetry between the groups. The following patient groups were excluded from the study: individuals under 18 years of age, repeat applicants, patients with multiple malignancies, and those applying for either a health status report or disability retirement.

First, 1093 and 818 cases respectively were detected to have applied to a medical board before and during the pandemic period, through the hospital’s automation system. Of these, 382 cases with cancer were reviewed. 219 cases who met inclusion criteria were included in the study (Flow chart is shown in Fig. 1). All reports and case records from both time periods were reviewed retrospectively.

Measurements

All data were anonymized before analysis in order to ensure compliance with confidentiality and ethical research standards. The sociodemographic data of the cases such as age and gender, tumor site, tumor stage, reason for applying to a medical board, on whose behalf the application was made, the patients’ dependency statuses according to their functional independence scales, and whether their cancer was in remission were recorded.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics for continuous data included mean ± standard deviation, median, minimum, maximum values, and discrete data included number and percentage values. The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to check the suitability of continuous data for normal distribution. The Chi-Square/Fisher’s Exact test was used for group comparisons of nominal variables (in cross tables).

Statistical analysis of the study was performed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences version 23.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). Differences with P value < 0.05 were considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Of the cases, 112 were female (51.1%) and 107 were male (48.9%). The mean age of all cases was 61.4 ± 13.05 years (min. 23, max 90). The 3 most common cancers in the study population were breast cancer (n = 63, 28.8%), lung cancer (n = 46, 21.1%), and colorectal cancer (n = 31, 14.2%). When the disease stages were evaluated at the time of application, it was seen that the most common stage was stage 4 (n = 83, 37.9%). More than half of the patients’ cancers were uncontrolled at the time of application (n = 113, 51.6%). Discussion

The findings of this study have revealed that there was a significant change in the pattern of medical board applications during the pandemic period. Strikingly, the rate of stage 4 cancer patients who were not in remission increased among medical board applications during the pandemic period.

This may indicate an increase in the need for social benefits for advanced cancer patients. However, the parallel increase in applications made by relatives on behalf of patients, especially in the 65 + age group, raises ethical concerns in this regard. Relatives’ potential misuse of the system underscores the need to reassess safeguard against abuse in medical board processes. In line with this concern, Grover and Piggott [19] argue that the majority of those who apply for disability benefits abuse these benefits by exaggerating the negative impact of their disease or disability on their working capacity.

The significant increase in the number of applications to medical boards by partially dependent patients compared to fully independent patients during the pandemic period can be explained by the fact that the income losses and layoffs experienced during the pandemic period triggered these patients or their relatives to apply to a medical board in order to have social rights. Or, since fully independent patients may have reduced their hospital visits due to concerns about the risk of infection they may face during the pandemic, an increase in the rate of relatively partially dependent patients may be found.

Although it was expected that there would be a decrease in the number of applications to medical boards due to the risk of the pandemic for patients in advanced age and with high disease burden, applications during the pandemic period did not create a statistically significant difference in terms of age groups compared to the pre-pandemic period. This may lead to the discussion of the implication that the risk-benefit balance between the presence of a serious disease and the demand for social benefits has not changed despite the pandemic.

It was observed that there was a significant increase in applications related to social support benefits, especially rights such as exemption from SCT, and care allowance. All these findings may indicate the inadequacy of security measures that can distinguish opportunistic use of social support systems, especially financial incentives, related to disability assessments. Some practices that support this argument have also been introduced. For example; important changes have been made regarding the rights of disabled individuals and their relatives to use and sell vehicles purchased with exemption from SCT [20, 21]. These regulations made by the state aim to prevent the arbitrary use of such vehicles and to prevent the abuse of such rights.

Concerns are occasionally raised about whether social rights are distributed fairly [22]. There are studies that point to the different aspects of the deficiencies of the current regulation in force in Türkiye [23]. Based on the context of our study, it can be stated that the combination of disease course, social needs, and socioeconomic opportunities can create ethical gray areas in health policy.

From a perspective of health economics, inappropriate allocation of resources for individuals with disabilities may sabotage the efficient use of health systems. Moreover, inequitable distribution may lead to increased administrative burdens and erosion of public trust in healthcare institutions. Disability assessments should be repeated at certain intervals, especially in diseases such as cancer, where prognosis and functionality status may fluctuate over time. Therefore, we recommend the use of standardized scales such as the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Scale or the Karnofsky Performance Scale. In addition, the monitoring of medical board applications by social services units may help to deliver benefits to those who are really in need.

The retrospective nature of the study may be a limitation in terms of general applicability of the data. However, the strengths of this study include its focus on a seldom-investigated variant of healthcare system misuse in the context of cancer patients, its utilization of data from a global crisis like the COVID-19 pandemic to make a significant comparison, and its identification of patient and application characteristics at potential risk (e.g., stage 4 disease, application by a relative). The symmetrical comparison of pre-pandemic and pandemic periods reduces temporal bias and strengthens the validity of observed changes in application behavior. These key advantages provide a basis for our findings, offering direct and actionable implications for health policy and ethical processes.

In the future, multi-center prospective studies that include interviews with patients and relatives in addition to application indications can be designed to exclude the possibility of interpretative bias. We also recommend a study to analyze the impact of the disability cost of cancer patients on the social security system during the pandemic period to be planned.

Conclusions

In conclusion, although it is essential for cancer patients and their relatives to exercise the social rights provided to them to relieve their burden related to the disease, our findings indicate that the pandemic may further exacerbate existing weaknesses in the system. However, it should not be ignored that the underlying reason for this may be the desire to take measures against the uncertainties that have emerged with the pandemic. Legislators and health practitioners should strengthen control mechanisms in terms of both ethics and sustainability of healthcare resources.

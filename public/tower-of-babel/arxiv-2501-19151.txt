Membrane phononic crystals for high- Q m Q_{\text{m}} mechanical defect modes in piezoelectric aluminum nitride
Anastasiia Ciers, Laurentius Radit Nindito, Alexander Jung, Hannes Pfeifer, Armin Dadgar, André Strittmatter, Witlef Wieczorek

Abstract
Nanomechanical resonators with exceptionally low dissipation are advancing mechanics-based sensors and quantum technologies. The key for these advances is the engineering of localized phononic modes that are well-isolated from the environment, i.e., that exhibit a high mechanical quality factor, Q m Q_{\text{m}} . Membrane phononic crystals fabricated from strained thin films can realize high- Q m Q_{\text{m}} single or multiple localized phononic defect modes. These defect modes can be efficiently interfaced with out-of-plane light or capacitively coupled to a microwave quantum circuit, enabling readout and control of their motion. When membrane phononic crystals are fabricated from a crystalline film, they could offer built-in functionality. We demonstrate a membrane phononic crystal realized in a strained 90 nm 90\text{\,}\mathrm{nm} -thin film of aluminum nitride (AlN), which is a crystalline piezoelectric material. We engineer a high- Q m Q_{\text{m}} localized phononic defect mode at 1.8 MHz 1.8\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz} with a Q m × f m Q_{\text{m}}\times f_{\text{m}} -product of 1.5 × 10 13 Hz 1.5\text{\times}{10}^{13}\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz} at room temperature. In future devices, the built-in piezoelectricity of AlN can be utilized for in-situ tuning of mechanical mode frequencies, defect mode couplings, or acoustic bandgaps, which can be used as building blocks of tunable phononic circuits.



Periodic patterning of a material is a well-established method to engineer its effective properties.
Phononic crystals (PnC) are structures with periodic variations in the material’s mechanical properties with the goal to engineer the localization or propagation of mechanical excitations. PnCs can display phononic bandgaps, ranges in frequency, in which the propagation of elastic waves is prohibited
Maldovan 2013
. By engineering phononic bands, mechanical excitations can be efficiently guided
Pfeifer et al. 2016
;
Ghasemi Baboly et al. 2018
;
Xi et al. 2024
or confined
Yu et al. 2014
;
Tsaturyan et al. 2017
;
Eichenfield et al. 2009
.
Using PnCs to localize mechanical excitations results in phononic modes that exhibit ultra-low mechanical dissipation. This is essential for their use in sensing and quantum technology applications
Barzanjeh et al. 2022
, for example, in atomic force microscopy
Hälg et al. 2021
, microwave-to-optical transduction
Mirhosseini et al. 2020
;
Delaney et al. 2022
;
Jiang et al. 2023
, or as a quantum memory
Wallucks et al. 2020
.

The use of highly-strained thin films enables realization of high-
Q_{\text{m}}
mechanical modes in the kHz to MHz regime
Engelsen et al. 2024
, a frequency range relevant for sensing
Hälg et al. 2021
or transduction
Delaney et al. 2022
. Tensile strain thereby allows storing mechanical energy in lossless tensile energy, a method known as dissipation dilution
Fedorov et al. 2019
. Combined engineering of the tensile strain distribution in a thin film and of a PnC shield results in soft-clamped, localized mechanical defect modes with ultra-high quality factors
Tsaturyan et al. 2017
;
Reetz et al. 2019
;
Clark et al. 2024
. Membrane-based, i.e., two-dimensional, PnCs allow thereby for efficient interfacing to out-of-plane light
Tsaturyan et al. 2017
;
Brubaker et al. 2022
;
Clark et al. 2024
, coupling to superconducting circuits
Brubaker et al. 2022
;
Seis et al. 2022
, or realization of coupled defect modes
Catalini et al. 2020
. Such membrane PnC structures have approached quality factors of
10^{9}
at room temperature based on thin strained films of amorphous Si
3
N
4
, realized through subtractive
Tsaturyan et al. 2017
;
Reetz et al. 2019
;
Saarinen et al. 2023
or additive density modulation
Høj et al. 2024
;
Huang et al. 2024
.
Although amorphous Si
3
N
4
is an excellent material for high-
Q_{\text{m}}
nanomechanics, it lacks functionality. Crystalline films with large tensile strain, however, can offer built-in functionality, such as piezoelectricity or superconductivity, and at the same time realize high-
Q_{\text{m}}
nanomechanical devices.
In particular, piezoelectricity can be used to actively modulate mechanical properties such as mode frequency, phononic bandgap, or coupling between localized phononic modes, which can be utilized in phononic circuits
Taylor et al. 2022
.
Furthermore, piezoelectric materials allow realizing optoelectromechanical devices for microwave-to-optics conversion
Vainsencher et al. 2016
;
Jiang et al. 2020
. So far, high-
Q_{\text{m}}
nanomechanical resonators in the kHz to MHz frequency range have been demonstrated with the piezoelectric materials InGaP
Cole et al. 2014
;
Bückle et al. 2018
;
Manjeshwar et al. 2023
, SiC
Sementilli et al. 2024
, or AlN
Ciers et al. 2024a
;
Ciers et al. 2024b
. However, the geometry of these devices was restricted to one-dimensional structures, such as uniform
Bückle et al. 2018
;
Sementilli et al. 2024
;
Ciers et al. 2024b
or PnC beams
Ciers et al. 2024a
, or two-dimensional structures such as membrane
Cole et al. 2014
, trampoline
Manjeshwar et al. 2023
or hierarchical resonators
Ciers et al. 2024a
that do not exploit a PnC.

In our work, we realize high-
Q_{\text{m}}
mechanical defect modes in a membrane PnC in tensile-strained
90\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
-thin piezoelectric AlN. We adapt a hexagonal PnC, a so-called dandelion, previously demonstrated with Si
3
N
4

Saarinen et al. 2023
.
Fig.

To analyze the attainable
Q_{\text{m}}
of the defect mode, we consider that its total
Q_{\text{m}}
can be expressed as a sum of three dominant contributions:

\frac{1}{Q_{\text{m}}}=\frac{1}{Q_{\text{gas}}}+\frac{1}{Q_{\text{rad}}}+\frac{1}{Q_{\text{D}}},

(1)

where
Q_{\text{gas}}
is the gas-limited quality factor (neglected in the following as we measure the devices in ultra-high vacuum),
Q_{\text{rad}}
is the quality factor that is limited by radiation loss
Cole et al. 2011
;
de Jong et al. 2022
and
Q_{\text{D}}
is the diluted internal quality factor
Gonzfilez and Saulson 1994
.
The latter is given by

Q_{\text{D}}=D_{\text{Q}}\times Q_{\text{int}},

(2)

where the intrinsic quality factor for
90\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
-thin AlN
Ciers et al. 2024b
is
Q_{\text{int}}
=
7.4\times 10^{3}
and
D_{\text{Q}}
can be calculated from the ratio of the kinetic to the bending energy
Unterreithmeier et al. 2010
;
Ciers et al. 2024a
,
E_{\text{kin}}
and
U_{\text{bend}}
, which we obtain from FEM simulations of the resonator geometry.

To conclude, we have realized dandelion
Saarinen et al. 2023
membrane phononic crystals in a crystalline
90\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
-thin film of piezoelectric AlN. The membrane PnC supported a localized mechanical mode, where the best device reached a mechanical quality factor of
8.2\times 10^{6}
exploiting dissipation dilution via strain in the thin film and soft clamping via localization of the defect mode in the PnC. The
Q_{\text{m}}\times f_{\text{m}}
-product of
1.5\times 10^{13}

\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}
supports a coherent mechanical oscillation at room temperature. Higher quality factors could be realized by improving the material quality or further engineering of the device geometry. By using thinner AlN films, higher dilution factors can be achieved provided that the strain and intrinsic quality factor of the film remain large. The membrane PnC device geometry can be further engineered by optimizing the PnC patterning
Zhang et al. 2017
, by exploiting strain engineering
Ghadimi et al. 2018
, e.g. through further tapering of the tethers, or by exploring additive density modulation instead of etching holes
Høj et al. 2024
;
Huang et al. 2024
.
Membrane PnCs realized with AlN could make use of the built-in piezoelectricity in hybrid high-
Q_{\text{m}}
optoelectromechanical devices for in-situ tunable phononic circuits
Taylor et al. 2022
, topological phononics
Vasileiadis et al. 2021
, or microwave-to-optics conversion
Mirhosseini et al. 2020
;
Jiang et al. 2020
;
Brubaker et al. 2022
;
Weaver et al. 2024
.

1.2.3
Radiation loss

To evaluate the radiation loss
Q_{\text{rad}}
, we use the complex eigenfrequency obtained from FEM simulations
de Jong et al. 2022
:

Q_{\text{rad}}=\frac{\text{Re}[f_{\text{d}}]}{2\,\text{Im}[f_{\text{d}}]}.

(4)

The magnitude of radiation loss is dependent on both the impedance and mode matching
between the resonator and substrate, as well as the geometry of the resonator, and how the sample is mounted for the measurements.
To reduce the interference of reflected waves, the outer boundaries are defined as perfectly matched layers (PML).
The evaluation of the radiation loss drastically depends on the setting of the PML.
The speed of the acoustic wave in AlN is
v=$11\text{\,}\mathrm{km}$
and in Si
v=$8.4\text{\,}\mathrm{km}$
. Then, the wavelength for a frequency of
f_{\text{m}}
=
1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
is given as
\lambda=v/
f_{\text{m}}
, yielding
\lambda_{\text{AlN}}=$7.33\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}$
and
\lambda_{\text{Si}}=$5.6\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}$
.
The PML should be somewhere between half and quarter of this wavelength (1.83 to
3.67\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
) in order to attenuate the wave to a negligible amplitude before it reaches the outer boundary of the PML, while being short enough to keep computational costs reasonable.
We set the PML to
3\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
and simulated a quarter of the actual structure (exploiting mirror symmetry).
We introduce an additional transition layer (TL) of material between the clamping point of the PnC and the PML to avoid an abrupt change in material properties and therefore numerical artifacts. An empirical choice of the TL is about one eighth of the acoustic wavelength. Fig.
6
(a,b) shows the displacement obtained from such FEM simulations for the localized high-
Q_{\text{rad}}
defect mode and a non-localized low-
Q_{\text{rad}}
mode.

Figure 8:
Release process of the PnC with circular holes.
(a) Optical images of the honeycomb membrane PnC at different etching times. (b) FEM simulations indicating the maximum stress of the structure during the release process in the unit cell (
a=$110\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}$

When attempting fabrication of the hexagonal PnC with circular holes, it was not possible to release such structure successfully, see Fig.
8
(a).
To understand the unsuccessful release, we performed FEM simulations of the structure at different times of the fabrication, i.e., at different underetch lengths. Fig.
8
(b) shows the stress in the unit cell at different etching times. We observe that at the moment when the tethers are suspended, the formed undercut has a sharp feature that causes the stress in the film to go above
9\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa}
. However, the exact value of the stress at the sharp undercut depends on the FEM meshing, which is used in the static solution simulation. Depending on this meshing, the stress can vary between 9 and
20\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa}
. We assume that this high value of stress is beyond the yield stress of the AlN film and, thus, leads to cracking and a failed release process.

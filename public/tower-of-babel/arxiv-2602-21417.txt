A statistical model for points expanding in higher dimensions while being tied to bijective involutions
Cristian Cobeli, The Nguyen, Alexandru Zaharescu

Abstract
. Let ℳ \mathcal{M} be a set with M M elements, let ψ : ℳ → ℳ \psi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M} be a bijective involution, and let 𝓧 ψ {\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi} be the set of sequences ( x 1 , … , x M ) ∈ ℳ M (x_{1},\dots,x_{M})\in\mathcal{M}^{M} with the property that x M + 1 − j = ψ ⁡ ( x j ) x_{M+1-j}=\psi(x_{j}) for 1 ≤ j ≤ M 1\leq j\leq M . This framework can be used to infer the possible distribution of sequences, such as the modular ones, that pose challenges for conventional methods. We prove that when M M is even, there exists a limit probability density function that weighs the parameter k k that counts the appearances of the elements of ℳ \mathcal{M} among the terms of sequences 𝒙 ∈ 𝓧 ψ \bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi} . It turns out that the number of fixed points of ψ \psi influences the probability density function, which decomposes into two pieces, each multiplied by complementary factors, and the smaller of the two pieces appears only when k k is even. Applying the model, we find a threshold from which almost all sequences contain related terms with prescribed frequencies.

1.
Introduction

We introduce and study a statistical model aimed primarily at understanding the expected distribution of sequences of numbers which, despite being a common occurrence, the actual proof of the observed properties is beyond the possibilities offered by known algebraic, analytical, and probabilistic techniques.

An illustrative case is that of sequences composed of residue classes
Q(n)
modulo a prime number
p
.
In this case,
Q(n)
can be a power, a polynomial, or a rational function.
Or, in a more intriguing example resembling randomness, the ‘mixing diagonal product’ of the factorials

1!,2!,\dots,(p-1)!\left(\mathrm{mod\;}p\right)
.
Based on standard expectations (see
Kolchin et al.
[
17
]
,

[
20
]
,

[
21
]

(see Stauduhar
[
1

[
15

and
[
6
,
7
]

This means that for any integer

the proportion of residue classes

for which there are exactly
k

for which

is expected to tend to
1/(ek!)
, as
p
tends to infinity.
Then, when

, it would follow that about
1/e
residue classes are missed by the sequence of factorials
\left(\mathrm{mod\;}p\right)
.

But while this is the general behavior of almost all sequences,
without having any specific property to distinguish them,
obtaining results towards proving that
the factorials modulo
p

follow a Poisson distribution
is a notable challenge.
Over time, numerous studies have been dedicated to this problem (see Banks et al.
[
2
]
,
Chen and Dai
[
5
]
,
Garaev and García
[
8
]
,

[

]

Additionally, other versions of the problem involving products

[

]

[

,

)
or generalizations in other spaces have been tinkered
(see Garaev et al.
[

]
,

[

]
, Lagarias and Yangjit
[
18
]
and the references therein).
The best result so far is due to
Grebennikov et al.
[

who showed the certain existence of merely

\left(\sqrt{2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{p}
distinct factorials

Let
M
be a positive integer, and let
\mathcal{M}
be a set of cardinality
|\mathcal{M}|=M
.
The special feature of our model is that from the entire set of all sequences (or vectors) of the cartesian product
\mathcal{M}^{M}
,
in the statistical accounting, we will only consider
a small portion of those sequences that are related
to the arithmetic structure
of the residue classes in
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.
And a characterization that provides a fine yet sufficiently generous encompassing filter is the linking of residue classes through their intrinsic symmetries.
One example of such symmetry is the one that associates to
x

its inverse
x^{-1}\left(\mathrm{mod\;}p\right)
.
This operation has an immediate effect on the decomposition
of the set of residue classes

\{2,3,\dots,p-2\}=\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
into two subsets
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
,
one containing half and the other containing the inverses from the first half. The consequence is the well-known characterization
of prime numbers through Wilson’s theorem:

\displaystyle(p-1)!\equiv 1\cdot\prod_{x\in\mathcal{I}}x\prod_{x\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}x^{-1}\cdot(p-1)\equiv\prod_{x\in\mathcal{I}}xx^{-1}\cdot(-1)\equiv-1\left(\mathrm{mod\;}p\right).

1.1.
Assumptions of the model

Therefore, the configuration adopted for our model consists of the triplet

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|=\theta M\left(1+O(M^{\eta-1})\right),\ \ \text{as $M\to\infty$,}

We further let the value of

2.
Notation

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a set with cardinality
M
, where
M\geq 2
is assumed in all that follows to be even.
For any pair of elements
y,z\in\mathcal{M}
, we denote

\displaystyle\delta(y,z):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }y=z;\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Similarly, for any integer
k\geq 0
, if
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{M})\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}
and any

, we let

\displaystyle\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if exactly $k$ components of $\bm{x}$ coincide with $y$};\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Additionally, for any subset
\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,2,\dots,M\}
, we denote

(3)

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if $x_{j}=y$ for all $j\in\mathcal{I}$ and $x_{j}\neq y$ for all $j\notin\mathcal{I}$};\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For
\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}
and integers
k=0,1,2,\dots
,
we keep track of the occurrences of various components in
\bm{x}
with the following
k
-counter function:

\displaystyle m_{k}(\bm{x}):=\big|\{y\in\mathcal{M}:y\text{ is represented exactly $k$ times in }\bm{x}\}\big|.

The set of
fixed points
of the bijection
\psi
, which was postulated in the hypothesis from the beginning, plays a central role in the induced ‘symmetry’.
That is why we let
\mathscr{F}_{\psi}
denote the
set of fixed points
of
\psi
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\psi}:=\{y\in\mathcal{M}\,:\,\psi(y)=y\}\,.

To achieve the outcomes of the model, we will proceed under the assumption that
there exist
\theta\in[0,1]
and
\eta\in[0,1)

such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|=\theta M\left(1+O(M^{\eta-1})\right),

as
M
tends to infinity.

We use the short notation
[M]
for the set of integers

\{1,2,\dots,M\}
.

Next, we consider the set of indices of the left leg of the pairs mirrored by
\psi
, where both indices are in a
subset
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{M}
. Thus, we denote:

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}):=\big\{j\in[M/2]:\{j,M+1-j\}\subset\mathcal{I}\big\}.

3.
The average size of the ratios

m_{k}(\bm{x})/M

We let
\A(k,M)
denote

on all

{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}

(4)

\displaystyle\A(k,M):=\frac{1}{|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}|}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\frac{m_{k}(\bm{x})}{M}\,.

By using the
\delta

the

-counter function can be expressed as follows:

(5)

\begin{split}m_{k}(\bm{x})&=\big|\left\{y\in\mathcal{M}\,:\,\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y)=1\right\}\big|\\
&=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y)\\
&=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\\
&=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\delta(x_{j},y)\prod_{\ell\in[M]\setminus\mathcal{I}}\big(1-\delta(x_{\ell},y)\big).\end{split}

Since the cardinality of
{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}
is

, using (

can be written as:

\begin{split}\A(k,M)&=\frac{1}{M^{M/2+1}}\sum_{x\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y)\\
&=\frac{1}{M^{M/2+1}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\delta(x_{j},y)\prod_{\ell\in[M]\setminus\mathcal{I}}\big(1-\delta(x_{\ell},y)\big).\end{split}

Note that the product on the right-hand side above is

\displaystyle\prod_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\delta(x_{j},y)\prod_{\ell\in[M]\setminus\mathcal{I}}\big(1-\delta(x_{\ell},y)\big)=\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y).

where
\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)
is defined by (
3
),

Then, changing the order of summation, we have:

(6)

\begin{split}\A(k,M)&=\frac{1}{M^{M/2+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\,.\end{split}

We split the analysis of the sums in (

is empty or not. Accordingly, the expression of the average (

\displaystyle\A(k,M)=A_{1}(k,M)+A_{2}(k,M),

where

\displaystyle A_{1}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{M/2+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset[M]\\

\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)

and

\displaystyle A_{2}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{M/2+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset[M]\\

Remark 3.1
.

If

is a fixed point of

.

Given

.

Lemma 3.1
.

The average

\A(k,M)=\frac{1}{|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}|}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}{m_{k}(\bm{x})}{/M}

equals:

\begin{split}&\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \frac{|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{M^{M/2+1}}\binom{M/2}{k}2^{k}(M-2)^{M/2-k},\end{aligned}&\text{if $k$ is odd};\\[18.0pt]
\begin{aligned} \frac{|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{M^{M/2+1}}&\binom{M/2}{k}2^{k}(M-2)^{M/2-k}\\[6.0pt]
&+\frac{|\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{M^{M/2+1}}\binom{M/2}{k/2}(M-1)^{M/2-k/2}.\end{aligned}&\text{if $k$ is even;}\end{cases}\\[10.0pt]
=&\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \frac{|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{M}\cdot\frac{1}{ek!}\big(1+O(k^{2}M^{-1})\big),\end{aligned}&\text{if $k$ is odd};\\[16.0pt]
\begin{aligned} \bigg(\frac{|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{M}\cdot\frac{1}{ek!}+\frac{|\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{M}\cdot\frac{1}{e^{1/2}2^{k/2}(k/2)!}\bigg)\big(1+O(k^{2}M^{-1})\big),\end{aligned}&\text{if $k$ is even.}\end{cases}\end{split}

Next, under our assumption (
2
)
regarding the number of fixed points of
\psi
,
by inserting the estimates (
9
) and (
11
) into (
7
),
we derive the following estimate of the average.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\theta\in[0,1]
and
\eta\in[0,1)
and suppose
that the number of fixed points of
\psi
is

\theta M\left(1+O(M^{\eta-1})\right)
as
M
tends to infinity.
Then:

\begin{split}\A(k,M)&=\begin{cases}\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1-\theta}{ek!}$}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right),&\text{if $k$ is odd};\\[6.0pt]
\left(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1-\theta}{e\,k!}$}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{e^{1/2}\,2^{k/2}(k/2)!}$}\right)\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right),&\text{if $k$ is even.}\end{cases}\end{split}

4.
The second moment about the mean

Since the average depends on the parity of
k
, we need to separate the discussion into two cases.

4.1.
The case
k
is odd

Considering the main term in the formula from Lemma
3.2
when
k
is odd, we let:

(12)

\displaystyle\M(k,M):=\frac{1}{|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}|}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\left(\frac{m_{k}(\bm{x})}{M}-\frac{1-\theta}{ek!}\right)^{2},\ \ \text{if $k$ is odd.}

Expanding the binomial, we find that
\M(k,M)
can be written as

(13)

\displaystyle\M(k,M)=\left(\frac{1-\theta}{ek!}\right)^{2}-\frac{2(1-\theta)}{ek!}\A(k,M)+\frac{1}{M^{M/2+2}}S_{2}(k,M),

where

(14)

\displaystyle S_{2}(k,M):=\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}m_{k}(\bm{x})^{2}.

Our aim is to show that
\M(k,M)
is small as
M\to\infty

and, for this, we will show that
the main term of
S_{2}(k,M)
equals the square of the main term
of
\A(k,M)
given in Lemma
3.2
.

By (
5
), we see that

\displaystyle S_{2}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y)\right)^{2}=\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}).

Separating the sums of the diagonal and the off-diagonal yields:

(15)

\begin{split}S_{2}(k,M)&=\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}=y\end{subarray}}\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})+\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})\\
&=M^{M/2+1}\A(k,M)+U(k,M),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle U(k,M):

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{k}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}).

This can be written as:

\displaystyle U(k,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\end{subarray}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}).

By the argument in Remark
3.2
, for odd
k
,

\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)
is zero unless
\mathcal{I}
contains no pair
\{j,M+1-j\}
for every
j\in[M]
.
Also, since
y\neq y^{\prime}
, the product

\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})
is equal to zero if
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, we only need to consider the case
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset
for estimating
U(k,M)
.
In other words,

(16)

\displaystyle U(k,M)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}).

We further separate the terms in the sum depending on the
size of the set
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})
:

(17)

\displaystyle U(k,M)

\displaystyle=U_{1}(k,M)+U_{2}(k,M),

where

\displaystyle U_{1}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})

and

\displaystyle U_{2}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}).

Remark 4.1
.

Following conditions under the summations in
U_{1}(k,M)
, let us suppose that
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]
and
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.

(a)

Then, if
y\neq y^{\prime}
and
\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}
are such that

\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})=1
,
it follows that there exists

j\in\mathcal{I}
such that

M+1-j\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
,

x_{j}=y
, and

x_{M+1-j}=y^{\prime}
.
Then

\displaystyle\psi(y)=\psi(x_{j})=x_{M+1-j}=y^{\prime},

which implies
\psi(y^{\prime})=y
and
\psi(y)=y^{\prime}
.
Additionally, it should be noted that it follows that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\{M+1-j:j\in\mathcal{I}\}
.

(b)

Moreover, under the same conditions, if there is another component, say
x_{h}
with
h\in\mathcal{I}
,
in order to still have
\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\neq 0
,
then
x_{h}=y
, so that
\psi(x_{h})=y^{\prime}
.

Then, by combining (a) and (b), we find that

\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})
must have the same cardinality as both

\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
.

Case 1.

To estimate
U_{1}(k,M)
, the condition that needs to be satisfied is
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.
Then, based on Remark
4.1
, a product

\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})

does contribute to
U_{1}(k,M)
when

|\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})|=k
and the vector
\bm{x}
has exactly
2k
components fixed, namely
x_{j}=y
for all
j\in\mathcal{I}
and
x_{\ell}=y^{\prime}
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
.
It follows that there are
(M-2)^{M/2-k}

such vectors
\bm{x}
, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})=(M-2)^{M/2-k}.

This holds for any
y\neq y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
with
y^{\prime}=\psi(y)
,
meaning that
y
is not a fixed point of
\psi
, and neither is
y^{\prime}
. Therefore

\displaystyle U_{1}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\\
|\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\\
y^{\prime}=\psi(y)\end{subarray}}(M-2)^{M/2-k}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|\binom{M/2}{k}2^{k}(M-2)^{M/2-k}.

By appropriately grouping the factors of the product, we find that

(18)

\begin{split}\frac{U_{1}(k,M)}{M^{M/2+2}}&=\frac{|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{k!M^{2}}\cdot\frac{(M/2)(M/2-1)\cdots(M/2-k+1)}{(M/2)^{k}}\left(1-\frac{2}{M}\right)^{M/2-k}\\
&=\frac{1-\theta}{k!M}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}\big)\right)\left(1+O\big(k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right)e^{-1}\left(1+O\big(kM^{-1}\big)\right)\\
&=\frac{\theta}{e\,k!}\cdot\frac{1}{M}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right),\end{split}

which is
O(M^{-1})
when
k
is fixed and
M\to\infty
.

Case 2.

If
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset
, then
\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})
is zero unless

y,y^{\prime}\notin\mathscr{F}_{\psi}
and
y\neq\psi(y^{\prime})
. Therefore, each vector
\bm{x}
for which
\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})
is non-zero has
4k
components fixed, namely

x_{j}=y
,
x_{M+1-j}=\psi(y)
for all
j\in\mathcal{I}

and
x_{\ell}=y^{\prime}
,
x_{M+1-\ell}=\psi(y^{\prime})

for all
\ell\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
.
This shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})=(M-4)^{M/2-2k}.

This holds for any
y\neq y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}
with
y\neq\psi(y^{\prime})
. It follows,

\displaystyle U_{2}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\neq y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}\\
y\neq\psi(y^{\prime})\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\end{subarray}}(M-4)^{M/2-2k}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|\left(|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|-2\right)\binom{M/2}{k}2^{k}\binom{M/2-k}{k}2^{k}(M-4)^{M/2-2k}.

To estimate
U_{2}(k,M)
we group the factors as follows:

(19)

\begin{split}\frac{U_{2}(k,M)}{M^{M/2+2}}&=\frac{|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|\left(|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|-2\right)}{M^{2}}\cdot\frac{(M/2)\cdots(M/2-k+1)}{k!(M/2)^{k}}\\
&\quad\times\frac{(M/2-k)\cdots(M/2-2k+1)}{k!(M/2)^{k}}\left(1-\frac{4}{M}\right)^{M/2-2k}\\
&=\frac{(1-\theta)^{2}}{(k!)^{2}}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}\big)\right)\left(1+O\big(k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right)e^{-2}\left(1+O\big(kM^{-1}\big)\right)\\
&=\left(\frac{1-\theta}{e\,k!}\right)^{2}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right).\end{split}

On combining the estimates (
18
), (
19
), and (
17
), it yields

(20)

\begin{split}\frac{U(k,M)}{M^{M/2+2}}&=\left(\frac{1-\theta}{e\,k!}\right)^{2}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right).\end{split}

Next, knowing from Lemma
3.2
that

\A(k,M)=O(1)
and inserting the estimate (
20
)
into (
15
),
we also obtain:

(21)

\begin{split}\frac{S_{2}(k,M)}{M^{M/2+2}}&=\frac{\A(k,M)}{M}+\frac{U(k,M)}{M^{M/2+2}}\\
&=\left(\frac{1-\theta}{e\,k!}\right)^{2}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right).\end{split}

Then, on using (
21
) and the estimate from Lemma
3.2

for
k
odd in (
13
), it follows

(22)

\begin{split}\M(k,M)&=\left(\frac{1-\theta}{ek!}\right)^{2}-\frac{2(1-\theta)}{ek!}\left(\frac{1-\theta}{ek!}\right)\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right)\\
&\quad+\left(\frac{1-\theta}{e\,k!}\right)^{2}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right)\\
&=O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\end{split}

as
k
is fixed and
M\to\infty
, for
k
odd.

4.2.
The case
k
even

Using the main term in the formula from Lemma
3.2
when
k
is even, in this case, for the second moment about the mean, we let:

(23)

\displaystyle\M(k,M):=\frac{1}{|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}|}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\left(\frac{m_{k}(\bm{x})}{M}-\frac{1-\theta}{ek!}-\frac{\theta}{e^{1/2}\,2^{k/2}(k/2)!}\right)^{2},\ \ \text{if $k$ is even.}

Expanding the square and denoting, as in the previous case, by
S_{2}(k,M)

the sum of the
k
-counter functions, a notation that does not depend on the parity of
k
, we have

\displaystyle\M(k,M)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1-\theta}{e\,k!}+\frac{\theta}{e^{1/2}\,2^{k/2}(k/2)!}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2\left(\frac{1-\theta}{e\,k!}+\frac{\theta}{e^{1/2}\,2^{k/2}(k/2)!}\right)\A(k,M)+\frac{S_{2}(k,m)}{M^{M/2+2}}.

After similar computation as in the case
k
is odd, we obtain

(24)

\begin{split}S_{2}(k,M)=M^{M/2+1}\A(k,M)+V(k,M),\end{split}

where
V(k,M)
verifies the same formula as the previous
U(k,M)
:

\displaystyle V(k,M):

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}).

Following that, unlike the case when
k
was odd,
we split
V(k,M)
into shorter pieces as follows:

V_{1}(k,M)
is the sub-sum when
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset
,

V_{2}(k,M)
is the sub-sum when either
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})\neq\emptyset

or
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
but not both, and

V_{3}(k,M)
is the sub-sum when both
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})
and
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})

are non-empty.
Again, since
y\neq y^{\prime}
, the product

\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})
is equal to zero if
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Thus

(25)

\displaystyle V(k,M)=V_{1}(k,M)+V_{2}(k,M)+V_{3}(k,M),

where

\displaystyle V_{1}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}),

\displaystyle V_{2}(k,M)

\displaystyle=2\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})\neq\emptyset,\;\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}),

(Here, the coefficient
2
accounts for the other sum where
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})=\emptyset,

\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, a sum that, because of the symmetry, is equal to the one shown.)

\displaystyle V_{3}(k,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})\neq\emptyset,\;\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\\
y^{\prime}\neq y\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}}\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime}).

Next, we proceed to examine each sum
V_{1}(k,M)
,
V_{2}(k,M)
and
V_{3}(k,M)
separately.

4.2.1.
Estimating
V_{1}(k,M)

The sum
V_{1}(k,M)
is identical to
U(k,M)
from (
16
), and its estimate (
20
) does not depend on the parity of
k
. Hence

(26)

\begin{split}\frac{V_{1}(k,M)}{M^{M/2+2}}=\left(\frac{1-\theta}{e\,k!}\right)^{2}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right)\end{split}

for
M\to\infty
.

4.2.2.
Estimating
V_{2}(k,M)

As
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})\neq\emptyset
,
\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)
is zero unless

\{j,M+1-j\}\subset\mathcal{I}
for all
j\in\mathcal{I}
and
y\in\mathscr{F}_{\psi}
. On the other hand, as
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset
, the necessary condition for which
\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})=1
is
y^{\prime}\notin\mathscr{F}_{\psi}
.
Now, for any
y\in\mathscr{F}_{\psi},y^{\prime}\notin\mathscr{F}_{\psi}
, there are

\displaystyle(M-3)^{M/2-|\mathcal{I}|/2-|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|}=(M-3)^{M/2-3k/2}

vectors
\bm{x}
that satisfy
\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})=1
, since each such vector
\bm{x}
has
3k
components fixed while the other components must be different from

y=\psi(y)
,
y^{\prime}
, and
\psi(y^{\prime})
, together with the relation
x_{j}=\psi(x_{M+1-j})
for all
j\in[M]
. Thus

\displaystyle V_{2}(k,M)

\displaystyle=2|\mathscr{F}_{\psi}||\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{I}^{\prime}|=k\\
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset\\
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})\neq\emptyset,\;\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=\emptyset\end{subarray}}(M-3)^{M/2-3k/2}.

This further equals

\displaystyle V_{2}(k,M)

\displaystyle=2|\mathscr{F}_{\psi}||\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|\binom{M/2}{k/2}\binom{M/2-k/2}{k}2^{k}(M-3)^{M/2-3k/2}.

Next, we group factors appropriately to find that

(27)

\begin{split}\frac{V_{2}(k,M)}{M^{M/2+2}}&=\frac{2|\mathscr{F}_{\psi}||\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{M^{2}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k/2-1}(M/2-j)}{(k/2)!M^{k}}\\
&\phantom{=\frac{2|\mathscr{F}_{\psi}||\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|}{M^{2}}\cdot}\times\frac{\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(M/2-k/2-\ell)}{k!(M/2)^{k}}\left(1-\frac{3}{M}\right)^{M/2-3k/2}\\

&=\frac{2\theta(1-\theta)}{e^{3/2}(k/2)!k!}\left(1+O\big(M^{\eta-1}+k^{2}M^{-1}\big)\right).\end{split}

4.2.3.
Estimating

Similarly with above arguments, the product

\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{x},y)\delta_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}(\bm{x},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle V_{3}(k,M)

\displaystyle=|\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|(|\mathscr{F}_{\psi}|-1)\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset[M]\\

\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I})\neq\emptyset,\;\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(M-2)^{M/2-k}

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\M(k,M)\geq\frac{1}{|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}|}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}^{\geq}}\left(\frac{m_{k}(\bm{x})}{M}-MT(k)\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}|}\sum_{\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}^{\geq}}\Delta^{2}.

Proof of Theorem

Let

Given

From Theorem

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}(k)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{K+1}|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}|,\ \ \text{ for $k=0,1,\dots,M$},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{K}|\leq\varepsilon\,|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}|.

For any
\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}

Next, given
\mu>0

7.
Closing remarks and further directions

We have introduced a statistical model to describe the distribution
of appearances of the elements in a set
\mathcal{M}
into finite sequences

\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}
, which have length
M=|\mathcal{M}|
, and whose terms are linked through an involutive bijection
\psi
, as defined in (
1
).

We have shown that the probability density function of the
number
k\geq 0
that counts the occurrences of components in the sequences coincides with the density in a Poisson process
multiplied by a factor given by the number of fixed points of
\psi
, if
k
is odd.
To this, an additional different term with a complementary multiplicative factor is added when
k
is even.
Then we have used the obtained result to show that
if
\psi
has no fixed points, then almost all sequences in

{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}
have terms whose frequency in sequences can be prescribed a priori and, additionally, have complementary interlinks.
While our discussion is based on the assumption that
M
is presumed to be even, the model can also be extended to the case where
M
is odd.

To better align with the original intention of applying the model
to understanding the distribution of the sequence of
factorials
\left(\mathrm{mod\;}p\right)
,
the set of sequences
{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}_{\!\psi}
can be filtered further,
retaining in the statistics only those sequences that satisfy the additional non-equal-neighbor hypothesis
(see
[
7
]
and Broughan and Barnett
[
4
]
).
Additionally, another step in the same direction
would involve analyzing the model for shorter sequences of length
N
, with
\lambda\sim N/M
as
M\to\infty
, where
\lambda\in(0,1]
.

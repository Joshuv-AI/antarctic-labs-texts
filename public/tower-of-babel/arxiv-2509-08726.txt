Abstract

Introduction

This paper considers the decentralized stochastic optimization over a connected network of
m
nodes. Specifically, we study the minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\bf{x}})=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}({\bf{x}}),

where
f_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is the local function on
i
th agent and has the form of

\displaystyle f_{i}({\bf{x}})={\mathbb{E}}\left[F_{i}({\bf{x}};{\bm{\xi}}_{i})\right].

We are interested in the setting that each
f_{i}
is relaxed smooth
[
56
]
but possibly nonconvex and the random variable
{\bm{\xi}}_{i}
follows distribution
{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
.
The optimization theory and algorithms for relaxed smooth problem have received significant attention in recent years
[

,

,
since the empirical studies suggest the relaxed smoothness can better characterize the training of neural network models than the classical smoothness
[
56
,
6
,
8
]
.
However, most existing works focus on the centralized setting, and how to well address the relaxed smooth optimization problem in the decentralized scenarios remains unclear.

The main challenge in decentralized optimization is that each agent can only access the information from itself or its neighbors, which leads to the consensus error among local variables
[
39
]
.
For the standard smooth optimization, the gradient tracking technique successfully uses the gradient Lipschitz continuity to reduce the consensus error and guarantees the convergence of the algorithms
[
38
,
40
]
.
Additionally, the multi-consensus steps with Chebyshev acceleration
[

,
1
]
can further improve the communication complexity, leading to the optimal decentralized methods for both convex and nonconvex cases
[

,
51
,

,
25
,
36
,
30
,
34
,
52
,
3
,
54
,
33
]
.
For nonsmooth optimization, the recent works focus on finding the sub-optimal solutions or the approximate (generalized) stationary points of the Lipschitz continuous objective
[
42
,
28
,
21
,
22
]
, establishing efficient decentralized stochastic algorithms based on the boundedness of local (sub)gradients.

In this paper, we focus on decentralized optimization under the relaxed smoothness, which is more challenging than above classical settings since neither the local functions nor their gradients enjoy the Lipschitz continuity.
We propose a novel algorithm called decentralized normalized stochastic gradient descent (DNSGD) to address above issues.
Our method does not include the gradient clipping, avoiding the potential inconsistency in the update modes of different local variables arising from the consensus errors.
We further construct a new Lyapunov function related to the product
of the gradient norm and the consensus error, which successfully characterizes the
convergence of our decentralized method in the relaxed smooth setting that allows local gradients to be neither bounded nor Lipschitz continuous.
Moreover, we conduct experiments on the application of image classification to show the empirical advantage of our proposed method in practice.

We use the bold lowercase letter with the subscript to represent the local variable, e.g., vector

In addition, the notation

We impose following common assumptions for nonconvex optimization under the relaxed smoothness.

Assumption 1

(lower boundedness)

We suppose the objective

such that

\displaystyle f^{*}=\inf_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\bf{x}})>-\infty.

Assumption 2

(relaxed smoothness)

We suppose each local function

is
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth i.e., there exists some

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}})-\nabla f_{i}({\bf{y}})\right\|\leq\left(L_{0}+L_{1}\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}})\right\|\right)\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}\right\|

for all

, where we define

Remark 1

The notion of relaxed smoothness was first introduced by

Here, we consider the weaker condition (Assumption
2

We also introduce the assumption of the gradient dissimilarity to describe the heterogeneity of the local functions, which is widely used in distributed optimization

Assumption 3

We suppose there exists some

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}})-\nabla f({\bf{x}})\right\|\leq\zeta

The following proposition shows that the objective

Proposition 1

Under Assumptions
2

3

(L_{f},L_{1})

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f({\bf{x}})-\nabla f({\bf{y}})\right\|\leq\left(L_{f}+L_{1}\left\|\nabla f({\bf{x}})\right\|\right)\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}\right\|

(2)

We are interested in the decentralize stochastic first-order methods, which can access the stochastic first-order oracle
\nabla F({\bf{x}};{\bm{\xi}}_{i})

In decentralized setting, each agent can only exchange information with its neighbors in per communication round.

We propose decentralized normalized stochastic gradient

Here the matrices
{\bf X}^{t}

{\bf V}^{t}

{\bf X}^{t}=[{\bf{x}}_{1}^{t};\dots;{\bf{x}}_{m}^{t}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}

{\bf V}^{t}=[{\bf{v}}_{1}^{t};\dots;{\bf{v}}_{m}^{t}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}

It is worth noting that the relaxed
smoothness condition (Assumption

) does not guarantee that the local gradient is Lipschitz continuous or bounded.
However, the existing analysis of decentralized methods mainly depends on the Lipschitz continuity or the boundedness of the local gradient

To address above issues, we design the Lyapunov function

\displaystyle\Phi^{t}=f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})+\frac{3\eta}{\sqrt{m}}\left(M_{0}+M_{1}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|\right)\left\|{\bf X}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t}\right\|+\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{m}}\left\|{\bf V}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{t}\right\|,

where
M_{0}=\sqrt{2(L_{0}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}\zeta^{2})}
and
M_{1}=\sqrt{2}L_{1}

In particular, our
\Phi_{t}

\displaystyle\left(M_{0}+M_{1}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|\right)\left\|{\bf X}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t}\right\|

to align with the measurement unit on the right-hand side of inequality (

This design can well address the consensus errors
\left\|{\bf X}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t}\right\|
and
\left\|{\bf V}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{t}\right\|
even if the local gradient is

In contrast, the existing analysis essentially only considers the linear combinations of the function value, gradient norm, and the consensus errors

,

,

Theorem 1

Under Assumptions
1

5

\displaystyle\eta=\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon}{4L_{f}+1},\frac{1}{2L_{1}}\right\},\quad b\geq\max\left\{\frac{256(4L_{f}+1)^{2}\sigma^{2}}{mL_{f}^{2}\epsilon^{2}},\frac{1024L_{1}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{mL_{f}^{2}}\right\}

, where
\hat{\bf{x}}
is uniformly sampled from
\{{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{0},\dots,{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{T-1}\}

\displaystyle L_{f}=L_{0}+L_{1}\zeta,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta_{\Phi}=f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{0})-\inf_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\bf{x}})+\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{m}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\|{\bf V}^{0}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{0}\right\|\right].

Corollary 1

Following the setting of Theorem

\hat{K}=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{\gamma})

In the case of

We then establish the recursions for the consensus errors as follows.

Lemma 2
.

For Algorithm

, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{t+1}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}\right\|\leq\rho\left(\left\|{\bf X}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t}\right\|+m\eta\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\|{\bf X}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t}\right\|\leq\frac{\rho m\eta}{1-\rho}.

Lemma 3
.

For Algorithm
2
with

,
we have

\displaystyle\small\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\|{\bf V}^{t+1}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{t+1}\right\|\right]\\
\leq&\rho\left(\left\|{\bf V}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{t}\right\|+\left(M_{2}+M_{3}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|\right)\left\|{\bf X}^{t}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t}\right\|+\left(M_{2}+M_{3}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|\right)m\eta+\frac{2\sqrt{m}\sigma}{\sqrt{b}}\right)\!,\!\!\end{split}

for given
{\bf X}^{t}
and
{\bf V}^{t}
,
where
M_{2}=(\rho+1)L_{f}+\rho L_{f}L_{1}\eta
and

M_{3}=\rho L_{1}(L_{1}\eta+1)+L_{1}
.

Applying Lemmas
1
,
2
, and
3
, we can characterize the decease of the Lyapunov function as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\Phi^{t+1}]\leq\Phi^{t}-\frac{5\eta}{8}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|+\frac{3\eta^{2}L_{f}}{4}.

(5)

Summing equation (
5
) over
t=0,\dots,T-1
,
we can achieve our main result in Theorem
1
that
guarantees
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\hat{\bf{x}})\right\|\right]\leq\epsilon/2
, where
\hat{\bf{x}}
is uniformly sampled from
\{{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{1},\dots,{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{m}\}
.

In Theorem
1
, the setting of iterations number

depends on the term

\Delta_{\Phi}=f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{0})-\inf_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\bf{x}})+(2\eta/\sqrt{m}\,){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\|{\bf V}^{0}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{0}\right\|\right]
, which may be dominated by the expected consensus error
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\|{\bf V}^{0}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{0}\right\|\right]

However, the results in the single machine scenario only depend on

To fill this gap, we set the initial communication rounds to be

For achieving an

Discussion and Related Work

The design of our DNSGD (Algorithm
2
) is based on normalized (stochastic) gradient descent, which is extensively studied in centralized scenario
[
35
,
4
,
9
,
15
,
11
]
.
In a related work,
[
26
]
studied normalized stochastic gradient descent in decentralized setting, while their analysis is only limited to the setting of standard smoothness.
In a very recent work,
Jiang et al. 2025
claims a first-order method for decentralized optimization under the relaxed smoothness, while each of their iteration requires every node to perform the step of gradient clipping which depends on accessing the mean vector
{\bar{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}
and the corresponding exact local gradient
\nabla f_{i}({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})
.
Recall that the decentralized method typically only allows each node directly communicates with its neighbors (Assumption
5
) and the stochastic first-order oracle can only access the unbiased gradient estimator with bounded variance (Assumption
4
).
Therefore, we argue that their algorithm is not truly decentralized or stochastic.
In contrast, our method only requires each node to iterate with its local stochastic gradient (line
9
of Algorithm
2
), which is fully decentralized and stochastic.

The convergence analysis for our DNSGD (Algorithm
2
) depends on the assumption of bounded gradient dissimilarity (Assumption
3
), which is commonly used in distributed optimization
[
48
,
13
,
2
,
27
]
.
Specifically, we use this assumption to establish the inequality

\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t+1})\right\|\leq(L_{0}+L_{1}\zeta)\eta+\left(1+L_{1}\eta\right)\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|
,
which connects the gradients at the points
{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t}
and
{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}
(see Lemma
9
in Appendix
C
).
We also combine Assumption
3
with the
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness of local functions (Assumption
2
) to guarantee the global objective
f
is
(L_{0}+L_{1}\zeta,L_{1})
-smooth (see the proof in Appendix
B
).
However, how to establish the convergence for decentralized relaxed smooth optimization without bounded gradient dissimilarity (Assumption
3
) is still an open problem.
It is worth noting that simply assuming that each local function
f_{i}
is
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth even cannot guarantee that the global function
f=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}
is also
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth, which is different with the standard smooth case.
We illustrate this point with the following example.

Proposition 3
.

We consider functions

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)=\exp(Lx),\quad f_{2}(x)=\exp(-Lx),\quad\text{and}\quad f(x)=\frac{f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x)}{2}

for some
L>0
and let
L_{1}=L/\log 2
, then both functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
is
(0,L_{1})
-smooth while the function
f
is not
(0,L_{1})
-smooth.

Recall that the discussion after Corollary
1
shows that the sample complexity of our method matches the lower bound in the special case of
L_{1}=0

[
33
]
.
This implies that the
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-4})
dependence on accuracy we obtained cannot be improved in our setting.
If we target to achieve the sharper
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
dependence in the sample complexity, the assumption of expected relaxed smoothness is necessary
[
4
,
41
]
, e.g., strengthen
condition (
1
) in Assumption
2
to

{\mathbb{E}}[\left\|\nabla F_{i}({\bf{x}};{\bm{\xi}}_{i})-\nabla F_{i}({\bf{y}};{\bm{\xi}}_{i})\right\|^{2}]\leq\left(L_{0}+L_{1}\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}})\right\|\right)^{2}\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}\right\|^{2}
,
then applying the variance-reduced gradient estimators
[
4
,
41
,
11
]
at local agents is possible to achieve the sample complexity depends on
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
.

Conclusion

This paper proposes decentralized normalized stochastic gradient
descent for nonconvex optimization under the relaxed smoothness.
We provide theoretical analysis to show our method can achieve the
\epsilon
-stationary point on each agent.
In particular, we design a new Lyapunov function including the product of the gradient norm and the consensus error for convergence analysis, which successfully address the non-Lipschitz and unbounded local gradients in relaxed smooth decentralized optimization.
The empirical studies also show that our method performs better than baselines.
In future work, we would like to establish decentralized methods for convex optimization under the relaxed smoothness
[
5
,
12
,
14
,
20
,
32
,
47
]
.
It is also interesting to extend our results to the more general settings such as the symmetric relaxed smoothness
[
4
]
and the stochastic gradient estimator with heavy-tailed noise
[
31
]
.

\eta=\min\left\{\epsilon/(4L_{f}+1),1/(2L_{1})\right\}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|\right]\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[8(\Phi^{t}-\Phi^{t+1})]}{5\eta}+\frac{6\eta L_{f}}{5}.

Taking the average on above inequalities with
t=0,\dots,T-1
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|\right]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{8(\Phi^{0}-\Phi^{T})}{5\eta T}+\frac{6\eta L_{f}}{5}\right]\leq\frac{8\Delta_{\Phi}}{5\eta T}+\frac{6\eta L_{f}}{5},

where the last step holds due to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\Phi^{0}-\Phi^{T}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{0})+\frac{3\eta}{\sqrt{m}}\left(M_{0}+M_{1}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{0})\right\|\right)\left\|{\bf X}^{0}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{0}\right\|+\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{m}}\left\|{\bf V}^{0}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{0}\right\|

\displaystyle-\left(f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{T})+\frac{3\eta}{\sqrt{m}}\left(M_{0}+M_{1}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{T})\right\|\right)\left\|{\bf X}^{T}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{x}}}^{T}\right\|+\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{m}}\left\|{\bf V}^{T}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{T}\right\|\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{0})-\inf_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\bf{x}})+\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{m}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\|{\bf V}^{0}-{\bf{1}}{\bar{\bf{v}}}^{0}\right\|\right]=\Delta_{\Phi}.

We consider the following case for the choice of
\eta
:

1.

In the case of
\epsilon/(4L_{f}+1)\leq 1/(2L_{1})
, we follow the setting

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{\epsilon}{4L_{f}+1},\quad b\geq\left\lceil\frac{256(4L_{f}+1)^{2}\sigma^{2}}{mL_{f}^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\right\rceil,\quad\text{and}\quad T\geq\frac{8\Delta_{\Phi}}{\eta\epsilon}=\frac{8(4L_{f}+1)\Delta_{\Phi}}{\epsilon^{2}}

(24)

to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|\right]\leq\frac{\epsilon}{5}+\frac{3\epsilon}{10}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

2.

In the case of
1/(2L_{1})\leq\epsilon/(4L_{f}+1)
, we follow the setting

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{1}{2L_{1}},\qquad b\geq\left\lceil\frac{1024L_{1}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{mL_{f}^{2}}\right\rceil,\qquad\text{and}\qquad T\geq\frac{16L_{1}\Delta_{\Phi}}{\epsilon}.

(25)

to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\|\nabla f({\bar{\bf{x}}}^{t})\right\|\right]\leq\frac{\epsilon}{5}+\frac{3\epsilon}{10}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Hence, we finish the proof.

Appendix H
The Proof of Proposition
3

Proof.

We can verify that the (second-order) derivatives of functions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
, and
f
have the form of

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{1}(x)=L\exp(Lx),\quad f^{\prime}_{2}(x)=-L\exp(-Lx),\quad\text{and}\quad f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{L\exp(Lx)-L\exp(-Lx)}{2}.

Note that the function

\displaystyle h(z)=\frac{|1-\exp(Lz)|}{L|z|}

is increasing.
Taking
z=y-x\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|1-\exp(L(y-x))|}{L|y-x|}=h(y-x)\leq h\left(\frac{1}{L_{1}}\right)=\frac{|1-\exp(L/L_{1})|}{L/L_{1}}

(27)

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
|y-x|\in[-1/L_{1},1/L_{1}]
.

For the function
f_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}_{1}(x)-f^{\prime}_{1}(y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|L\exp(Lx)-L\exp(Ly)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L\exp(Lx)|1-\exp(L(y-x))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\exp(Lx)\cdot L_{1}|1-\exp(L/L_{1})||y-x|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L\exp(Lx)|y-x|L_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{1}f_{1}^{\prime}(x)|x-y|,

where the inequality is based on equation (
27
) and the last two steps are based on
f^{\prime}_{1}(x)=L\exp(Lx)
and the setting
L_{1}=L/\log 2
.
Therefore, we have shown that
f_{1}
is
(0,L_{1})
-smooth.
Similarly, we can also show that
f_{2}
is
(0,L_{1})
-smooth.

Let
x=0
and
y=(\log 2)/L
, which holds
|x-y|=1/L_{1}
.
We also have

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}\left|\exp(Lx)-\exp(-Lx)-(\exp(Ly)-\exp(-Ly))\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}\left|\exp(0)-\exp(0)-\left(2-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|=\frac{3L}{4}

\displaystyle L_{1}f^{\prime}(x)|y-x|=\frac{L_{1}L(\exp(Lx)-\exp(-Lx))|y-x|}{2}=0.

Minimal energy local systems on curves
Charlie Wu

Abstract
. Let Σ g , n \Sigma_{g,n} be an orientable topological surface of genus g g with n n punctures. When g = 0 g=0 , Deroin and Tholozan studied the class of supra-maximal representations π 1 ​ ( Σ 0 , n ) → PSL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) \pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,n})\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) , and they showed that the supra-maximal representations form a compact component of a real relative character variety. We study a collection of rank r r local systems on Σ g , n \Sigma_{g,n} which we call of minimal energy . These are generalizations of supra-maximal representations, and underlie polarizable complex variations of Hodge structure for any choice of complex structure on Σ g , n \Sigma_{g,n} . Like the supra-maximal representations, the minimal energy local systems form compact components of relative character varieties of real forms of GL r ⁡ ( ℂ ) \gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}) . We show that when the local monodromy data around the punctures is chosen to be unitary and generic, and the relative character variety is nonempty, these minimal energy local systems always exist. When g > 0 g>0 , we show that the minimal energy local systems come from unitary representations of π 1 ​ ( Σ g , n ) \pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}) . If g = 0 g=0 we show that they do not always come from unitary representations, and we study their structure in general.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Sigma_{g,n}
be an orientable topological surface of genus
g
and
n
punctures, and let
G\subseteq\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C})
be a reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Let
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of conjugacy classes of
G
. We let
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
be the
relative character variety
of
\Sigma_{g,n}
. This is the space of conjugacy classes (i.e., isomorphism classes) of semisimple rank
r
representations of the fundamental group of
\Sigma_{g,n}
valued in
G
such that the homotopy class of a simple closed loop
\gamma_{i}
around some puncture
x_{i}
lies in
C_{i}
.

More precisely, we let the
relative representation variety

\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
be given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{2g}G\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\overline{C}_{i}

where
\overline{C}_{i}
is the Zariski-closure of
C_{i}
in
G
, and
\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
is cut out by the relations

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{g}[A_{2i-1},A_{2i}]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}

for
A_{i}\in G
and
B_{i}\in\overline{C}_{i}
. Since the
\overline{C}_{i}
are algebraic subvarieties of
G
,
\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
inherits the structure of an algebraic variety. Then,
G
acts on
\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
by simultaneous conjugation of the
A_{i}
’s and
B_{i}
’s, and we define
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
to be
\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)\sslash G
where the quotient is a GIT quotient. This GIT quotient is also the topological quotient of
\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
by the conjugation action. Throughout, we work with conjugacy classes which are semisimple (so the
C_{i}
are Zariski-closed).

1.1.
Main results

We study a collection of local systems (equivalently,
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n})
-representations) in
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
which we call
of minimal energy
. These local systems are defined in
4.3
, and they come from polarizable complex variations of Hodge structure (
\mathbb{C}
-VHS) satisfying a Hodge-theoretic vanishing condition. This definition a priori depends on a choice of complex structure for
\Sigma_{g,n}
, but we show in
4.18
that this choice turns out to be auxiliary and does not matter.

Therefore, we give
\Sigma_{g,n}
a complex structure and work with smooth algebraic curves. Let
X
be a smooth proper curve of genus
g
over the complex numbers and let
D=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
be a reduced effective divisor on
X
(i.e., a collection of distinct points
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
in
X
) so that topologically,
\Sigma_{g,n}
is homeomorphic to
X\setminus D
. Suppose that the conjugacy classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}\subseteq\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C})
are
elliptic
, meaning that they contain a unitary matrix. As before we let
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
. Since these conjugacy classes are semisimple (since they preserve a Hermitian metric), they are Zariski-closed.

The following results will be proven in
§ 4
,
§ 5
, and
§ 6
.

We work with
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
when the relative character variety is smooth. This condition is guaranteed provided that every representation
\rho\in\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
is irreducible, and we can ensure that every representation in
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
is irreducible by picking our conjugacy classes generically. The precise condition that guarantees this is spelled out in
3.7
, and it is a numerical condition on the eigenvalues of the conjugacy classes which can be easily checked by hand.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
is nonempty and smooth, then there is a minimal energy local system in every connected component of
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
.

The methods used to prove
Theorem 1.2
allow us to conclude in
4.18
that the minimal energy local systems form almost all of the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS lying in
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
. More precisely, we show that for each connected component of
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
, the locus of
\mathbb{C}
-VHS in that connected component contains the locus of minimal energy local systems as the
unique
connected component of the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS locus of largest dimension in that connected component.

If
X
has positive genus and our local monodromy data is generic, a minimal energy local system must come from a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS with a one-step Hodge filtration. That is, these minimal energy local systems come from unitary representations. This is proven in
4.19
. If the genus of
X
is zero, then the minimal energy local systems do not have to come from a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS with a one-step Hodge filtration. In
6.2
, for any rank
r
we find an example of a minimal energy local system on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus D
whose associated
\mathbb{C}
-VHS has a two-step Hodge filtration. Moreover, we show that when the number of punctures
n
is very large compared to
r
, this is the maximum number of pieces that can appear in the Hodge filtration of a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS associated to a minimal energy local system.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be an irreducible minimal energy local system of rank
r
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
with generic unitary local monodromy (in the sense of
3.7
). If

\displaystyle n>5r^{2}-25

then
\mathbb{V}
comes from a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS with at most two steps in its Hodge filtration.

For
r\leq 2
,
5r^{2}-25\leq-5
so the condition on the number of punctures
n
is vacuous because the number of punctures is nonnegative. Indeed, every rank
2

\mathbb{C}
-VHS can have at most two steps in its Hodge filtration so
Theorem 1.3
is uninteresting. However when
r\geq 3
(so that
n\geq 20
),
Theorem 1.3
places non-trivial conditions on the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS. This bound is far from sharp, however. For example, a rank
3
minimal energy local system on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
can have at most two steps in its Hodge filtration as soon as
n\geq 4
.

1.4.
History and motivation

This paper and its motivations are heavily inspired by work of Benedetto-Goldman
[
3
]
, Deroin-Tholozan
[
13
]
, Mondello
[
23
]
, Tholozan-Toulisse
[
28
]
, and Feng-Zhang
[
14
]
. These papers studied and produced interesting compact components of relative character varieties of punctured spheres for various real Lie groups
G
and conjugacy classes
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
.

Benedetto and Goldman first studied the case of a four-punctured sphere with
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\cong\su(1,1)
and
G=\su(2)
. They showed that
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,4}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}),C)
admits a real form with a compact component whenever the traces of the
C_{i}
lie in
(-2,2)
. This compact component either lies in
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,4}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),C)
or
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,4}),\su(2),C)
. The group
\su(2)
is compact, so this is not so surprising. The group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is not compact, but nevertheless Benedetto and Goldman surprisingly find examples of conjugacy classes
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{4})
where
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,4}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),C)
has a compact component. More precisely, they show that
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,4}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),C)
has compact connected component when the conjugacy classes
(C_{1},\dots,C_{4})
satisfy a certain explicit inequality in the traces of the
C_{i}
. When the inequality is not satisfied, the compact component shows up in the
\su(2)
-character variety. We point the reader to
[
10
, Theorem 3.12]
for the explicit bound. Remarkably, Benedetto and Goldman found these components by graphing them on a computer (see
Figure 1
).

Figure 1.
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,4}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),C)
with
\tr(C_{1})=\tr(C_{2})=\tr(C_{3})=3/2
and
\tr(C_{4})=-3/2
. The tiny red component is compact, and the blue components are non-compact.

Deroin-Tholozan
[
13
, Corollary 3]
then generalized the work of Benedetto-Goldman to spheres with arbitrarily many punctures. Using methods from geometric topology, they constructed the class of
supra-maximal
representations
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,n})\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and showed that the collection of supra-maximal representations forms a compact component of
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,n}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),C)
for particular choices of elliptic conjugacy classes
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
. Along the way, they show that these supra-maximal representations have very interesting algebraic and geometric properties. They show that the homotopy class of every simple closed curve in
\Sigma_{0,n}
is sent to a non-hyperbolic element of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

[
13
, Theorem 2]
, and for any supra-maximal representation
\rho
and any complex structure on
\Sigma_{0,n}
, there is a unique
\rho
-equivariant holomorphic map from the universal cover of
\Sigma_{0,n}
to the symmetric space of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(the upper half plane). Related work of Mondello
[
23
, Corollary 3.20]
studies
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),C)
for general
g
, and they independently count the number of components of
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),C)
and recover the compact components. Mondello also shows that the compact component is
unique
, when it exists.

Feng-Zhang and Tholozan-Toulisse then found analogous compact components of real relative character varieties. Tholozan-Toulisse found compact components of
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,n}),G,C)
for the Hermitian Lie groups
G=\su(p,q)
,
\mathrm{Sp}(2,\mathbb{R})
, and
\so(2n)
. Using non-abelian Hodge theory
[
28
, Theorem 1]
, they recover similar algebraic and geometric properties of representations in their compact component
[
28
, Theorem 2]
. Their methods involve explicitly constructing the Higgs bundles corresponding to these representations in the compact components.

Feng-Zhang found a compact component for
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,n}),\so_{o}(2,q),C)
, where
\so_{o}(2,q)
is the identity component of
\so(2,q)

[
14
,
Theorem 1.1 part (a)]
. Their work covers the last collection of Hermitian Lie groups left open from the work of Tholozan-Toulisse. The representations in this component discovered by Feng-Zhang have the same good algebraic and geometric properties found in the components of Deroin-Tholozan and Tholozan-Toulisse
[
14
, Theorem 1.1 parts (b) and (c)]
. Like the work of Tholozan-Toulisse, their methods involve explicitly constructing the Higgs bundles corresponding to these representations in the compact components.

We give a new interpretation of these compact components. Like the work of Feng-Zhang and Tholozan-Toulisse, we use non-abelian Hodge theory to study the relative character variety
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
. However, we find compact components of
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),G,C)
for
G=\mathrm{U}(p,q)
without writing down the Higgs bundles. Instead, we use deformation-theoretic techniques in the moduli space of parabolic Higgs bundles. One major upside to our methods is that we do not need to work with the conjugacy classes in a strong way. Under mild hypotheses on the conjugacy classes (to ensure that
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
is nonempty and smooth), there are compact components of
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\mathrm{U}(p,q),C)
or
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\su(p,q),C)
(whose points are the minimal energy local systems) for some pair of nonnegative integers
(p,q)
such that
p+q=r
. In particular, we recover the compact components found by Tholozan-Toulisse and Feng-Zhang (see
4.21
).

Along the way, we show that these compact components (which consist of minimal energy local systems) are made up of
universal

\mathbb{C}
-VHS. That is, they underlie complex variations of Hodge structure for any choice of complex structure on
\Sigma_{g,n}
. Since the unitary group is compact and all unitary local systems on
\Sigma_{g,n}
come from
\mathbb{C}
-VHS, the results are not surprising when the minimal energy local systems correspond to unitary local systems. However, we show that there are often minimal energy local systems in
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\su(p,q),C)
when
\su(p,q)
is noncompact (that is, when
p
and
q
are both nonzero). This gives us both interesting compact components of
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\su(p,q),C)
as well as a class of interesting universal
\mathbb{C}
-VHS on
\Sigma_{g,n}
.

1.5.
Structure of the paper

Our methods heavily rely on non-abelian Hodge theory, which gives us a correspondence between local systems on
X\setminus D
and parabolic Higgs bundles on
X
(with parabolic divisor
D
). In

and

-VHS. In particular, we discuss how to take a

-VHS on

Experts may wish to skip to

4.19
. In

we prove

, and in

we give

and give some applications of minimal energy local systems to Gromov-Witten theory.

2.
Parabolic Bundles

We review the notions of parabolic bundles and parabolic sheaves. We use the notation from
[
20
, Section 2]
. Parabolic bundles are, roughly speaking, bundles carrying extra structure that keeps track of local monodromy data.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on a smooth curve
X
, and let
D=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
be a reduced effective divisor on
X
(that is,
D
is a collection of points). A
parabolic bundle

(E,\{E_{j}^{i}\},\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\})
on
(X,D)
is the data

(1)

a strictly decreasing filtration
E_{x_{j}}=E_{j}^{1}\supset E_{j}^{2}\supset\dots\supset E_{j}^{n_{j}+1}=0
,

(2)

a sequence of real numbers
0\leq\alpha_{j}^{1}<\alpha_{j}^{2}<\dots<\alpha_{j}^{n_{j}}<1

for all
x_{j}
appearing in
D
. We write
E_{\star}
to mean
(E,\{E_{j}^{i}\},\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\})
and we call
D
the parabolic divisor of
E_{\star}
. For a fixed
x_{j}
, we call
\{E_{j}^{i}\}
the flag over
x_{j}
and
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
the weights over
x_{j}
. We call
\alpha_{j}^{i}
the weight associated to
E_{j}^{i}
.

Given a parabolic bundle
E_{\star}
, we get induced parabolic structures on subquotients of
E_{\star}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E_{\star}
be a parabolic bundle on
X
with parabolic divisor
D
, and let
F\subseteq E
be a subbundle of
E
. The vector bundles
F
and
E/F
carry an induced parabolic structure with parabolic divisor
D
as follows:

(1)

the flag of
F_{x_{j}}
over
x_{j}
is given by

\displaystyle F_{x_{j}}=E_{j}^{1}\cap F_{x_{j}}\supseteq E_{j}^{2}\cap F_{x_{j}}\supseteq\dots\supseteq E_{j}^{n_{j}+1}\cap F_{x_{j}}=0

after removing redundancies, and the weight associated to
F_{j}^{i}
is given by
\max_{1\leq k\leq n_{j}}\{\alpha_{j}^{k}:F_{j}^{i}=E_{j}^{k}\cap F_{x_{j}}\}
.

(2)

the flag of
(E/F)_{x_{j}}
is given by

\displaystyle(E/F)_{x_{j}}=(E^{1}_{j}+F_{x_{j}})/F_{x_{j}}\supseteq(E^{2}_{j}+F_{x_{j}})/F_{x_{j}}\supseteq\dots\supseteq(E^{n_{j}+1}_{j}+F_{x_{j}})/F_{x_{j}}=0

after removing redundancies, and the weight associated to
(E/F)_{j}^{i}
is given by
\max_{1\leq k\leq n_{j}}\{\alpha_{j}^{k}:(E/F)_{j}^{i}=(E_{j}^{k}+F_{x_{j}})/F_{x_{j}}\}

.

Let
E_{\star}
be a parabolic vector bundle on
X

\displaystyle\pardeg(E_{\star})

\displaystyle:=\deg(E)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\alpha_{j}^{i}\cdot\dim(E_{j}^{i}/E_{j}^{i+1})

.

, then
f(E_{j}^{i})\subseteq F_{j}^{k}
. We say that a
strongly
parabolic morphism is a parabolic morphism such that if
\alpha_{j}^{i}\geq\beta_{j}^{k}
, then
f(E_{j}^{i})\subseteq F_{j}^{k+1}
. In other words, strongly parabolic morphisms are sections of
\widehat{\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star},F_{\star})}
.

As an example, we spell out the case where
E_{\star}
and
F_{\star}
are parabolic line bundles. If
E_{\star}
and
F_{\star}
are rank
1
parabolic bundles, then
\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star},F_{\star})\cong E^{\vee}\otimes F(-D^{\prime})
where
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
is the divisor consisting of points
x_{j}
where the weight of
E_{\star}
is greater than the weight of
F_{\star}
. Similarly,
\widehat{\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star},F_{\star})}\cong E^{\vee}\otimes F(-D^{\prime\prime})
where
D^{\prime\prime}
is the divisor of points where the weight of
E_{\star}
is greater than or equal to the weight of
F_{\star}
.

Definition 2.10
.

A
parabolic Higgs bundle

(E_{\star},\theta)
on
(X,D)
is a parabolic bundle
E_{\star}
on
(X,D)
along with an
\End(E_{\star})
-valued logarithmic
1
-form
\theta\in H^{0}(X,\End(E_{\star})\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))
. The map
\theta
is called the Higgs field. Here, "logarithmic" means
\theta
has simple poles along the divisor
D
.

We say that
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a
strongly parabolic Higgs bundle
if it is a parabolic Higgs bundle and
\theta
is a strongly parabolic morphism
\theta:E_{\star}\to E_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
. In other words,
\theta
is a global section of the bundle
\widehat{\End(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
.

We consider strongly parabolic Higgs bundles to use deformation-theoretic techniques. These strongly parabolic Higgs bundles were also considered in the work of Tholozan-Toulisse
[
28
]
.

There are notions of stability and semi-stability for parabolic Higgs bundles.

Definition 2.11
.

If
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a Higgs bundle with a parabolic structure, then we say that
(E_{\star},\theta)
is stable (resp. semi-stable) if all sub-Higgs bundles
(F_{\star},\theta|_{F})
satisfy
\mu_{\star}(F_{\star})<\mu_{\star}(E_{\star})
(resp.
\mu_{\star}(F_{\star})\leq\mu_{\star}(E_{\star})
). We say that
(E_{\star},\theta)
is
poly-stable
if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of stable Higgs bundles.

Lemma 2.12
.

If
E_{\star}
and
F_{\star}
are two parabolic bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then

(2.12.1)

\displaystyle\pardeg\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star},F_{\star})_{\star}=\rank E\cdot\pardeg F_{\star}-\rank F\cdot\pardeg E_{\star}.

Proof.

See
[
6
, Section 2]
for a discussion on computing
\pardeg(E_{\star}\otimes F_{\star})_{\star}
for
E_{\star}
and
F_{\star}
parabolic bundles. Then to get the statement for
\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star},F_{\star})_{\star}
we note that
\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star},F_{\star})_{\star}=((E_{\star})^{\vee}\otimes F_{\star})_{\star}
.
∎

Parabolic degree satisfies properties similar to those satisfied by the usual notion of degree.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

0\to F_{\star}^{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}F_{\star}^{2}\xrightarrow{d_{2}}\dots\xrightarrow{d_{N-2}}F_{\star}^{N-1}\xrightarrow{d_{N-1}}F_{\star}^{N}\to 0

be an exact sequence of parabolic bundles. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}\pardeg F_{\star}^{k}=0.

In particular, if

0\to F_{\star}\to E_{\star}\to(E/F)_{\star}\to 0

is a short exact sequence of parabolic bundles then
\pardeg(E_{\star})=\pardeg(F_{\star})+\pardeg((E/F)_{\star})
. A parabolic bundle
E_{\star}
is semi-stable (resp. stable) if and only if for every quotient
E_{\star}\to(E/F)_{\star}
,
\mu_{\star}(E_{\star})\leq\mu_{\star}((E/F)_{\star})
(resp.
\mu_{\star}(E_{\star})<\mu_{\star}((E/F)_{\star})
).

Proof.

See
[
20
, Lemma 2.4.5]
for the claims about short exact sequences. We deduce the statement about general exact sequences from the claim about short exact sequences.

Let

\displaystyle 0\xrightarrow{d_{0}}F_{\star}^{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}F_{\star}^{2}\xrightarrow{d_{2}}\dots\xrightarrow{d_{N-2}}F_{\star}^{N-1}\xrightarrow{d_{N-1}}F_{\star}^{N}\xrightarrow{d_{N}}0

be an exact sequence of parabolic bundles. Then as parabolic bundles,
\ker(d_{k+1})_{\star}=\im(d_{k})_{\star}
. We now break up the exact sequence into many short exact sequences. We have exact sequences of parabolic bundles

\displaystyle 0\to\ker(d_{k})_{\star}\to F_{\star}^{k}\to\im(d_{k})_{\star}\to 0.

Then since we know that parabolic degrees are additive in short exact sequences, we have that

\displaystyle\pardeg F^{k}_{\star}=\pardeg\ker(d_{k})_{\star}+\pardeg\im(d_{k})_{\star}.

Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}\pardeg F_{\star}^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}\left(\pardeg\ker(d_{k})_{\star}+\pardeg\im(d_{k})_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}\left(\pardeg\im(d_{k-1})_{\star}+\pardeg\im(d_{k})_{\star}\right).

This is a telescoping sum, and so simplifying yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}\left(\pardeg\im(d_{k-1})_{\star}+\pardeg\im(d_{k})_{\star}\right)=-\pardeg\im(d_{0})_{\star}+(-1)^{N}\pardeg\im(d_{N})_{\star}.

But
d_{0}=d_{N}=0
, so
-\pardeg\im(d_{0})_{\star}+(-1)^{N}\pardeg\im(d_{N})_{\star}=0
proving the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
E_{\star}
be a parabolic vector bundle with parabolic divisor
D=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\deg E\leq\pardeg E_{\star}<\deg E+\rank E\cdot n.

Proof.

By
2.3

\displaystyle\pardeg E_{\star}=\deg E+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\alpha_{i}^{j}\cdot\dim(E_{j}^{i}/E_{j}^{i+1}).

Since our parabolic weights
\alpha_{i}^{j}
lie in
[0,1)
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\alpha_{i}^{j}\cdot\dim(E_{j}^{i}/E_{j}^{i+1})

\displaystyle<\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\dim(E_{j}^{i}/E_{j}^{i+1})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dim(E_{x_{j}}).

But
E
is a vector bundle so the fibers have dimension equal to
\rank E
. Therefore

\displaystyle\deg E\leq\pardeg E_{\star}<\deg E+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\dim(E_{x_{j}})=\deg E+\rank E\cdot n.

∎

We seek to study parabolic Higgs bundles on
(X,D)
because they correspond to local systems on
X\setminus D
. This correspondence is due to the work of several people including Corlette, Donaldson, Hitchin, and Simpson. For our purposes, we use non-abelian Hodge theory as described in
[
25
]
, and we include it as
Theorem 2.15
for convenience. We will refer to
Theorem 2.15
for the correspondence.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper curve and let
D
be a reduced effective divisor. There is a one-to-one correspondence between poly-stable parabolic Higgs bundles of parabolic degree
0
on
X
with parabolic divisor
D
and the semisimple local systems on
X\setminus D
. Under this correspondence, the poly-stable parabolic Higgs bundles with zero Higgs field correspond to the unitary local systems on
X
, and strongly parabolic Higgs bundles correspond to the local systems with unitary local monodromy at the punctures of
X\setminus D
.

Proof.

See
[
25
, Theorem on page 718]
.
∎

Remark 2.16
.

The local systems with unitary monodromy must have unitary local monodromy at the punctures, but the converse is not true. In the context of
Theorem 2.15
, this is captured by the phenomenon that there are strongly parabolic Higgs bundles with nonzero Higgs field. These strongly parabolic Higgs bundles with nonzero Higgs field correspond to local systems with unitary monodromy locally at the punctures but are not globally unitary.

We will describe which Higgs bundles correspond to local systems underlying a complex variation of Hodge structure in
§ 3
.

3.
Variations of Hodge Structure

We give background on variations of Hodge structure and their associated Higgs bundles in this section. We primarily use the notation of
[
20
, Section 4]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible variety over
\mathbb{C}
and
w\in\mathbb{Z}
an integer. A
complex variation of Hodge structure
(
\mathbb{C}
-VHS) of
weight

w
is a triple
(V,V^{p,q},\overline{\partial})
where
V
is a complex
C^{\infty}
-bundle on
X
,
\oplus_{p+q=w}V^{p,q}=V
is a direct sum decomposition, and
\overline{\partial}
is a flat connection satisfying Griffiths transversality. That is,

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}(V^{p,q})\subseteq A^{1,0}(V^{p,q})\oplus A^{0,1}(V^{p,q})\oplus A^{1,0}(V^{p-1,q+1})\oplus A^{0,1}(V^{p+1,q-1}).

Here,
A^{i,j}(V^{p,q})
is the sheaf of
V^{p,q}
-valued
(i,j)
-forms. A
polarization
on
(V,V^{p,q},\overline{\partial})
is a flat Hermitian form
\psi
on
V
such that the
V^{p,q}
are orthogonal to each other under
\psi
, and that on each
V^{p,q}
the form
(-1)^{p}\psi
is positive-definite. A
polarizable

\mathbb{C}
-VHS is a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS which admits a polarization
\psi
.

Given a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS
(V,V^{p,q},\overline{\partial})
we call the holomorphic flat bundle
(E,\nabla):=(\ker(\overline{\partial})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{O}_{X},\id\otimes d)
the
holomorphic flat bundle
associated to the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS. We get a filtration
F^{p}E
on
E
induced by the filtration
F^{p}V=\oplus_{j\geq p}V^{j,p+q-j}
on
V
, known as the Hodge filtration. The filtration satisfies Griffiths transversality:

\displaystyle\nabla(F^{p})\subseteq F^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D).

If
\mathbb{V}
is a local system isomorphic to
\ker(\overline{\partial})
for some polarizable
\mathbb{C}
-VHS
(V,V^{p,q},\overline{\partial})
, then we say that
\mathbb{V}
underlies a polarizable
\mathbb{C}
-VHS.

Remark 3.2
.

To an irreducible local system
\mathbb{V}
underlying a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS, there is a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS underlying
\mathbb{V}
of weight
w
for every
w\in\mathbb{Z}
. To see this, we pick some
w\in\mathbb{Z}
and we let
(V,V^{p,w-p},\overline{\partial})
be a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS underlying
\mathbb{V}
of weight
w
. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
(V^{\prime},(V^{\prime})^{p,w+k-p},\overline{\partial^{\prime}})
be a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS where
V^{\prime}=V
,
(V^{\prime})^{p,w+k-p}=V^{p,w-p}
, and
\overline{\partial^{\prime}}=\overline{\partial}
. Then, the weight of
(V^{\prime},(V^{\prime})^{p,w+k-p},\overline{\partial^{\prime}})
is
w+k
and Griffiths transversality still holds. Since the local system determined by
(V^{\prime},(V^{\prime})^{p,-p},\overline{\partial^{\prime}})
is given by
\ker(\overline{\partial^{\prime}})
and
\overline{\partial^{\prime}}=\overline{\partial}
, the two
\mathbb{C}
-VHS underlie the same local system.

The data of a weight
w
is extra data. Often this choice of weight is irrelevant data, and we will often ask for a local system to underlie a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS without specifying the weight because the weight will not matter.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
with
D
a simple normal crossings divisor, and
(E,\nabla)
a flat holomorphic vector bundle on
X
. The
Deligne canonical extension
of
(E,\nabla)
is the unique logarithmic flat bundle
(\overline{E},\overline{\nabla})
on
\overline{X}
with regular singularities along
D
, along with an isomorphism
(\overline{E},\overline{\nabla})|_{X}\cong(E,\nabla)
, and such that the eigenvalues of the residues of
\overline{\nabla}
along
D
have real part in
[0,1)
.

The existence of the Deligne canonical extension is a theorem of Deligne
[
12
, Remarques 5.5(i)]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
be a curve where
\overline{X}
is some smooth proper curve and
D
is a reduced effective divisor. Let
(E,\nabla)
be a flat holomorphic vector bundle of rank
n
on
X
with regular singularities along
D
. Suppose at some
x_{j}
, the eigenvalues of the residue matrix
\res(\nabla)(x_{j})
are given by
\eta_{j}^{1},\dots,\eta_{j}^{n}
. Then, the real parts
\Re(\eta_{j}^{i})
lie in
[0,1)
. We write
\alpha_{j}^{i}=\Re(\eta_{j}^{i})
and order the
\alpha_{j}^{i}
in increasing order and remove repetitions so

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha_{j}^{1}<\dots<\alpha_{j}^{n_{j}}<1.

Let
E_{j}^{i}\subseteq E_{x_{j}}
be the sum of all of the generalized eigenspaces of
\res(\nabla)(x_{j})
such that the real part of the associated eigenvalues are at least
\alpha_{j}^{i}
. Then,
E=(E,\{E_{j}^{i}\},\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\})
is the
parabolic bundle associated to the connection

\nabla
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\overline{X}
be a smooth projective curve,
D
a reduced effective divisor, and
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
. Let
(V,V^{p,q},\overline{\partial})
be a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS on
X
and
(E,\nabla)
its associated holomorphic flat bundle with Hodge filtration
F^{\bullet}
. Let
(\overline{E}_{\star},\overline{\nabla})
be its associated parabolic bundle on

in the sense of
3.3
and
3.4
. Then by
[
9
, Section 7]
, there is a canonical extension of the Hodge filtration
F^{\bullet}
to
(\overline{E},\overline{\nabla})
. Let
\overline{F}^{\bullet}
be this extension.

We call
(\oplus_{p}\gr_{\overline{F}^{\bullet}}^{p}\overline{E}_{\star},\oplus\gr_{\overline{F}^{\bullet}}^{p}\overline{\nabla})
the
parabolic Higgs bundle associated
to the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS. The
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-linearity of the map
\oplus\gr_{\overline{F}^{\bullet}}^{p}\overline{\nabla}
is due to Griffiths transversality of
\overline{\nabla}
and
\overline{F}^{\bullet}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
where
\overline{X}
is a smooth proper curve and
D
is a reduced effective divisor. Let
(V,V^{p,q},\overline{\partial})
be a complex variation of Hodge structure on
X
,
(E,F^{\bullet},\nabla)
its associated holomorphic flat bundle with Hodge filtration

, and
(\oplus E^{p}_{\star},\oplus\theta_{p}):=(\oplus_{p}\gr_{\overline{F}^{\bullet}}^{p}\overline{E}_{\star},\oplus\gr_{\overline{F}^{\bullet}}^{p}\overline{\nabla})
be its associated parabolic Higgs bundle defined in

(1)

\oplus E_{\star}^{p}

(2)

(3)

if the local system

Theorem 3.6
produces a parabolic Higgs bundle from a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS. The parabolic Higgs bundle is the same as the one given by the correspondence given in

Definition 3.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve and
D=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
be a reduced effective divisor on
X
. We fix a positive integer
r
. Let
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
be a collection of parabolic weights.

(1)

We say that
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
is
smooth
with respect to
(X,D,r)
if every semi-stable parabolic Higgs bundle of rank
r
on
(X,D)
of parabolic degree zero with the weights
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
is actually stable.

(2)

We say that the collection of weights
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
is
distinct
with respect to
(X,D,r)
if for all
j
,
n_{j}=r
where
n_{j}
is the number of weights over
x_{j}
.

(3)

We say that the weights
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
are
generic
with respect to
(X,D,r)
if (1) and (2) are both satisfied.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a local system on
X\setminus D
with unitary local monodromy around the punctures. We say the local monodromy of
\mathbb{V}
is generic if the collection of associated weights of the parabolic Higgs bundle is generic (via non-abelian Hodge theory).

Given some local monodromy data
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
, we say that the
n
-tuple of conjugacy classes
C
is
connected
if the following holds. For any subspace
V\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
and any collection of matrices
(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})
with
A_{i}\in C_{i}
such that
V
is stable under all of the matrices
A_{i}
and

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\det(A_{i}|_{V})=1,

we have that either
V=0
or
V=\mathbb{C}^{r}
.

We call an
n
-tuple of semisimple conjugacy classes
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
connected because this condition ensures that the relative character variety
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
(when the
C_{i}
are semisimple) is connected by
[
16
, Theorem 5.1.1]
. We hope that this non-standard naming convention is not too confusing for the reader.

Example 3.8
.

Let
(X,D,r)
be fixed. Let
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}\subseteq[0,1)
be a set of size
\deg D\cdot r
so that no proper subset of the
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
has elements which sum to an integer. Then,
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
is generic with respect to
(X,D,r)
in the sense of
3.7
(3). To check this, we take any parabolic semi-stable Higgs bundle
(E_{\star},\theta)
of parabolic degree
0
and observe that any sub-Higgs bundle
(F_{\star},\theta|_{F})
of
(E_{\star},\theta)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\pardeg F_{\star}}{\rank F}=\mu_{\star}(F_{\star})\leq\mu_{\star}(E_{\star})=\frac{\pardeg E_{\star}}{\rank E}=0.

By definition, the parabolic degree of
F_{\star}
is given by

(3.8.1)

\displaystyle\pardeg F_{\star}=\deg F+\sum_{j=1}^{\deg D}\sum_{k=1}^{\rank F}\alpha_{j}^{{i_{k}}}.

Here, the
\{\alpha_{j}^{i_{k}}\}
is the collection of weights of
F_{\star}
. We note that

\displaystyle\deg F+\sum_{j=1}^{\deg D}\sum_{k=1}^{\rank F}\alpha_{j}^{{i_{k}}}<0

because by assumption, the expression

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\deg D}\sum_{k=1}^{\rank F}\alpha_{j}^{{i_{k}}}

is not an integer and

is always an integer.

Remark 3.9
.

In terms of the local monodromy,
3.7
part (2) says that at every point the eigenvalues are all distinct. Part (1) guarantees that the local systems in
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
are irreducible.

Let
X
be a curve. Given a parabolic Higgs bundle
(E_{\star},\theta)
on
(X,D)
, the
i
-th Higgs cohomology group of
(E_{\star},\theta)
is defined to be the
i
-th hypercohomology of the complex

E_{\star}[0]\xrightarrow{\theta}\widehat{E_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)[-1].

That is, we define the

-th Higgs cohomology to be

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{i}\left(E_{\star}\xrightarrow{\theta}\widehat{E_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\right).

We will often omit the cohomological degree that each term of the complex sits in if it is clear from the context.

If
(E_{\star},\theta)
underlies a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS of weight
w
,
E_{\star}=\oplus_{p+q=w}E^{p,q}_{\star}
then the Higgs cohomology has a Hodge structure of weight
w+i
with

part given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{p,q}\left(E_{\star}\xrightarrow{\theta}\widehat{E_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\right)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{H}^{i}\left(E^{p,q-i}_{\star}\xrightarrow{\theta}\widehat{E^{p-1,q-i+1}_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\right).

This means that

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{i}\left(E_{\star}\xrightarrow{\theta}\widehat{E_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\right)=\bigoplus_{p+q=w+i}\mathbb{H}^{i}\left(E^{p,q-i}\xrightarrow{\theta}\widehat{E^{p-1,q-i+1}_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\right).

For a review of hypercohomology, we suggest
[
24
, Appendix A, Example A.28]
.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
(E_{\star},\theta)

4.
Minimal Energy Local Systems

Let
X
be a smooth proper curve and
D=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
a reduced effective divisor on
X
(i.e, a collection of points). We let
M=M(X,D,r,\{\alpha_{i}^{j}\})
be the coarse moduli space of semi-stable strongly parabolic Higgs bundles of rank
r
of parabolic degree
0
on
X
with parabolic divisor
D
and parabolic weights
\{\alpha_{i}^{j}\}
(
2.10
and
2.11
). This space is constructed in
[
29
, Section 2]
. As
(X,D,r)
are fixed, any smoothness, distinctness, and genericity conditions on a collection of weights
\{\alpha_{j}^{i}\}
are assumed to be with respect to
(X,D,r)
in the sense of
3.7
.

As before, for a semi-stable parabolic Higgs bundle
(E_{\star},\theta)
to be in
M
, we require that the Higgs field
\theta
is a strongly parabolic Higgs field. This ensures that when we deform a Higgs bundle (in
M
) corresponding to a local system with unitary local monodromy, the resulting Higgs bundle gives us a local system with unitary local monodromy.

To see why this is necessary, we consider an example. For every
t\in\mathbb{C}
, consider a local system
\mathbb{V}_{t}
on
\Sigma_{g,n}
with local monodromy at the puncture
x_{1}
given by the matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Whenever
t\neq 0
, the local monodromy stays in the same conjugacy class in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. However the limit
\mathbb{V}_{t}
does not exist in the relative character variety because
\mathbb{V}_{0}
has trivial monodromy at
x_{1}
. Taking unitary local monodromy (and therefore working with strongly parabolic Higgs bundles) fixes this because the semisimple conjugacy classes are closed.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
(E_{\star},\theta)=(\oplus E^{p},\oplus\theta_{p})
be a stable parabolic Higgs bundle underlying an (irreducible)
\mathbb{C}
-VHS. The adjoint Higgs bundle
(\ad(E_{\star}),\ad(\theta))
is given by the quotient
\End(E_{\star})/\mathscr{O}_{X}
. Here, we are viewing
\mathscr{O}_{X}
as the subsheaf of
\End(E_{\star})
of scalar-endomorphisms of
E_{\star}
with its trivial parabolic structure.

The adjoint Higgs bundle
\ad(E_{\star})
also underlies a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS (of weight 0). This has a canonical grading, where the
k
-th graded piece
\ad(E_{\star})^{k,-k}
is given by
\oplus\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{p}_{\star},E^{p+k}_{\star})_{\star}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{1}=\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\xrightarrow{\ad(\theta)}\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))

has a Hodge structure of weight
1
. In this case

We now define the notion of minimal energy as a condition on
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))

, such a notion is actually a condition on the tangent space of

at

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(E_{\star},\theta)
be a stable parabolic Higgs bundle on a smooth proper curve

with parabolic divisor

and parabolic weights
\{\alpha_{i}^{j}\}
corresponding to a complex variation of Hodge structure. We say that
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a
minimal energy
parabolic Higgs bundle (or is of minimal energy) if the Higgs cohomology group

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))

of
M
at
(E_{\star},\theta)
is of Hodge length
1
.

If
(V,V^{p,q},\overline{\partial})
is the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS corresponding to the minimal energy Higgs bundle

, then we say that
(V,V^{p,q},\overline{\partial})
is a
minimal energy
variation of Hodge structure.

Similarly, if
\mathbb{V}
is a local system on

with unitary local monodromy such that the corresponding parabolic Higgs bundle
(E_{\star},\theta)
(under non-abelian Hodge theory) is of minimal energy, then we say
\mathbb{V}
is a
minimal energy
local system (or is of minimal energy).

Remark 4.4
.

We define minimal energy Higgs bundles
(E_{\star},\theta)
only when
(E_{\star},\theta)
is stable because
\ad(E_{\star})
has a canonical grading (and is a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS of weight
0
) when
(E_{\star},\theta)
is stable. If
(E_{\star},\theta)
is not stable, then in general
\ad(E_{\star})
will still be a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS but it will not have a canonical grading.

By
4.2
, if our parabolic weights are chosen to be smooth (in the sense of
3.7
), then every semi-stable parabolic Higgs bundle with those weights is actually stable. In this case, our moduli space

is smooth.

Remark 4.5
.

In the non-parabolic setting, there is an energy functional on the moduli space of Higgs bundles given by
||(E,\theta)||=i\int_{X}\tr(\theta\wedge\theta^{*})
where
\theta^{*}
is the adjoint to
\theta
with respect to a harmonic metric. If
(E,\theta)
is stable, then this metric is unique. Collier and Wentworth
[
11
, Section 4.1]
showed that stable Higgs bundles which minimize
||(E,\theta)||
are those whose tangent space is of Hodge length
1
by exploiting the fact that the energy functional is a Morse-Bott function on the moduli space of Higgs bundles
[
17
, Section 8]
. The tangent space to the moduli space of Higgs bundles splits into a direct sum
\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}T_{j}
where
T_{j}=\mathbb{H}^{j,1-j}

[
18
, Section 8]
. On each piece
T_{j}
, the Hessian of
||\cdot||
has eigenvalue
j
and hence a Higgs bundle cannot minimize the energy functional unless
T_{j}=0
for
j<0
. Symmetry of the decomposition of the tangent space implies that the only nonzero pieces are
T_{0}
and
T_{1}
which is precisely the Hodge length
1
condition described in
4.3
.

There are some classes of local systems which are always of minimal energy.

Example 4.6
.

A unitary local system
\mathbb{V}
corresponds to a parabolic Higgs bundle underlying a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS coming in one piece

and Higgs field

. This is a consequence of the Mehta-Seshadri correspondence for irreducible unitary local systems and stable parabolic bundles
[

is a minimal energy local system.

Example 4.7
.

An irreducible rigid local system is always of minimal energy. This is because rigid local systems are isolated points, and so they have zero tangent space.

We give an example of a rigid local system. A result of Katz
[
19
, Theorem 1.1.2]
says that for a rank

irreducible local system

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
with local monodromy data (conjugate to)

is rigid if and only if

\displaystyle(2-n)r^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim Z(A_{i})=2

where

is the centralizer of

.

In the case where

and

\displaystyle\dim Z(A_{1})+\dim Z(A_{2})+\dim Z(A_{3})=6.

For a

. Hence, any irreducible rank

4.17.

The minimal energy local systems make up “most" of the

Proposition 4.18

be the locus of

-VHS inside a connected component of

-VHS in

Proof.

By
4.8
and

, the tangent space of

at a

-VHS

is of the form

and the action of

is given by multiplication by

. Hence, parts (c) and (g) of

imply that

is isomorphic

(which is the tangent space of

Let

be the connected component of

containing

\mathbb{H}^{p,1-p}\cong\mathbb{H}^{1-p,p}
as vector spaces for all

\displaystyle\dim\widetilde{Y}=\dim T_{y}Y=\dim\mathbb{H}^{0,1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim T_{y}M=\frac{1}{2}\dim M

with equality if and only if

is of minimal energy. Since we know by

that minimal energy

-VHS always exist, we know that the top dimensional component of

is always half of the dimension of

(and necessarily must be the locus of minimal energy
\mathbb{C}
-VHS).

If

Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
, then

\displaystyle\dim Y^{\prime}=\dim T_{q}Y=\dim\mathbb{H}^{0,1}(\ad(F_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(F_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))<\frac{1}{2}\dim T_{q}M=\frac{1}{2}\dim M

which implies that there is some
k>1
such that
\mathbb{H}^{k,1-k}(\ad(F_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(F_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))
is nonzero which violates the definition of minimal energy.
∎

If
X
is a positive genus curve, then the minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundles must correspond to unitary representations of
\pi_{1}(X\setminus D)
. We make this precise below in
4.19
.

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper curve of genus
g>0
, and
D
a reduced effective divisor. If
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a stable parabolic Higgs bundle of minimal energy (with parabolic divisor
D
) on
X
of parabolic degree
0
, then the Higgs field is zero (and so
(E_{\star},\theta)
comes from a unitary representation).

Proof.

We write
E_{\star}=E^{N}_{\star}\oplus\dots\oplus E^{1}_{\star}
where the graded pieces of our Higgs fields
\theta^{p}\colon E^{p}\to E^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
are all nonzero. We know by
Theorem 2.15
that
N=1
if and only if
(E_{\star},\theta)
corresponds to a unitary local system. We assume that
N>1
for the sake of contradiction. Since
(E_{\star},\theta)
is stable, it is a smooth point of the moduli space of semi-stable parabolic Higgs bundles of parabolic degree
0
on
X
with parabolic divisor
D
and parabolic weights
\{\alpha_{i}^{j}\}
. Therefore by
4.2
, the tangent space to
M
at
(E_{\star},\theta)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))
.

If
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))
is of Hodge length
1
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{1}(0\to\widehat{\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{1}_{\star},E^{N}_{\star})_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))=\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N},E^{1})_{\star}\to 0)=\mathbb{H}^{1-N,N}=0.

Then, Riemann-Roch for curves yields

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq h^{0}(X,\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N}_{\star},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star})-h^{1}(X,\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N}_{\star},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star})

\displaystyle=\deg\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N}_{\star},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}+\rank\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N}_{\star},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}(1-g).

Therefore

\displaystyle 1-g

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\deg\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N}_{\star},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}}{\rank\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N}_{\star},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}}.

Then, we have that

\displaystyle\pardeg\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star}^{N},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}=\pardeg E^{1}_{\star}\cdot\rank E^{N}_{\star}-\pardeg E^{N}_{\star}\cdot\rank E^{1}_{\star}<0

because
\pardeg E^{1}_{\star}<0
and
\pardeg E_{\star}^{N}>0
since
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a stable parabolic Higgs bundle. Then,
\deg\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star}^{N},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}\leq\pardeg\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star}^{N},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}<0
so

\displaystyle 1-g\geq-\frac{\deg\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N}_{\star},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}}{\rank\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{N}_{\star},E^{1}_{\star})_{\star}}>0.

Therefore,
1>g
. But we assumed that
g\geq 1
, so our assumption that
N>1
must be false. Therefore,
N=1
which implies that
(E_{\star},\theta)
corresponds to a unitary local system under non-abelian Hodge theory (
Theorem 2.15
).
∎

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper curve and
D
a reduced effective divisor on
X
. Let
(E_{\star},\theta)=(\oplus E^{k}_{\star},\oplus\theta_{k})
be a minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle of rank
r
on
X
with parabolic divisor
D
and generic parabolic weights (in the sense of
3.7
). If
p=\sum\rank_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}E^{2k}
and
q=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\rank E^{2k+1}
, then
p+q=r
and the connected component of the locus of variations of Hodge structure containing
(E_{\star},\theta)
is compact. In particular, this connected component is a compact connected component of
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\su(p,q),C)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a local system corresponding to a polarizable
\mathbb{C}
-VHS. Then, the representation
\rho
corresponding to
\mathbb{V}
preserves the polarization, and the polarization is a type
(p,q)
-Hermitian form. Hence,
\rho
is an
\su(p,q)
-representation of
\pi_{1}(X\setminus D)
. The
\mathbb{C}
-VHS correspond to parabolic Higgs bundles with nilpotent Higgs field. Therefore, the parabolic Higgs bundles coming from
\mathbb{C}
-VHS lie in
h^{-1}(0)
where
h
is the parabolic Hitchin map (
4.10
).

We now consider the minimal energy local systems. As minimal energy local systems underlie a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS, they necessarily correspond to
\su(p,q)
-representations and also lie in
h^{-1}(0)
.
4.11
tells us that
h
is proper, so
h^{-1}(0)
is compact.

Let
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\su(p,q),C)
be the
\su(p,q)
-relative character variety. Since the real algebraic group
\su(p,q)
is a real form of
\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, we have that
\su(p,q)\times_{\spec\mathbb{R}}\spec\mathbb{C}=\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
. Therefore,

\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\su(p,q),C)\times_{\spec\mathbb{R}}\spec\mathbb{C}=\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C).

Hence,
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\su(p,q),C)
is closed inside the
\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-relative character variety (and it is the fixed points of the involution coming from complexifying).

Let
M_{\su(p,q)}\subseteq M
be the locus of Higgs bundles in
M
corresponding to
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n}),\su(p,q),C)
under non-abelian Hodge theory, and let
Y\subseteq M_{\su(p,q)}\subseteq M
be the locus of minimal energy Higgs bundles. Since
\dim Y=\frac{1}{2}\dim M=\dim M_{\su(p,q)}
, we know that
Y
is a connected component of
M_{\su(p,q)}
. Therefore, it is closed in
M
. But furthermore,
Y
consists
entirely
of
\mathbb{C}
-VHS implying that it is a subset of
h^{-1}(0)
. Therefore
Y
is a component of
M_{\su(p,q)}\cap h^{-1}(0)
. By properness of
h^{-1}(0)
we know that
M_{\su(p,q)}\cap h^{-1}(0)
is compact since
M_{\su(p,q)}
is closed in
M
and closed subsets of compact sets are compact. Finally,
Y
is a connected component of a compact set
M_{\su(p,q)}\cap h^{-1}(0)
so
Y
itself is compact.
∎

This mirrors the lower-rank case in
[
13
, Proposition 2.6]
where the Deroin-Tholozan representations (which are minimal energy local systems in rank
2
), form a compact component of the real character variety.

Remark 4.21
.

The compact components found in Deroin-Tholozan, Tholozan-Toulisse, and Feng-Zhang are examples of minimal energy components. This is because all of the local systems found in those components underlie
\mathbb{C}
-VHS, which by
4.18
is equivalent to being of minimal energy.

5.
Genus zero

We restrict our attention to minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In contrast to the higher genus case (see
4.19
) where the minimal energy local systems correspond to unitary representations, the minimal energy local systems do not necessarily underlie a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS with a trivial Hodge filtration. However, we show in
Theorem 1.3
that when the number of punctures on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is large compared to the rank of the minimal energy local system, the minimal energy local systems underlie a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS with at most two steps in their Hodge filtration.

Our ultimate goal in this section is to prove
Theorem 1.3
. To that end, let
(E_{\star},\theta)
be a parabolic Higgs bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with distinct parabolic weights and parabolic divisor
D
corresponding to an irreducible minimal energy local system. Since
(E_{\star},\theta)
underlies a
\mathbb{C}
-VHS, it is of the form
(\oplus_{p=1}^{N}E^{p}_{\star},\oplus\theta^{p})
where our Higgs field
\theta^{p}\colon E^{p}_{\star}\to E^{p-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)
is nonzero (due to the irreducibility assumption on the corresponding local system) and
N
is the number of graded pieces. The number of steps in the Hodge filtration of the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS corresponding to
(E_{\star},\theta)
is the same as the number of graded pieces of
(E_{\star},\theta)
.

As an intermediate step, we will show that if
N>2
, then the number of punctures
n
cannot be too large compared to
\rank E_{\star}
. Let
V^{k}_{\star}=\oplus_{p}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E_{\star}^{p},E_{\star}^{p+k})_{\star}=\ad(E_{\star})^{k,-k}
and let
\ad(\theta)^{k}\colon V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)
be the induced Higgs field on
\ad(E_{\star})
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(E_{\star},\theta)
be a minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle on
(\mathbb{P}^{1},x_{1}+\dots+x_{n})
with
N
graded pieces and with generic unitary local monodromy in the sense of
3.7
(so that there are
r
distinct parabolic weights of
(E_{\star},\theta)
). If
N\geq 3
, then

\displaystyle n\leq\frac{2\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}+2\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{N-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(2k-3)\rank V^{k}}{\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}}.

Remark 5.2
.

The denominator in this expression can be simplified, but we will leave it unsimplified because in
5.6
we will explicitly bound term
\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})
by a positive number. In particular, we will show the denominator is always nonzero. We also note that the term
\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}
only contributes when
N>3
and when
N=3
the sum vanishes. We do not index starting from
k=3
to avoid specifying what an empty sum means.

The bound in
5.1
depends on
(E_{\star},\theta)
itself and not just the rank of the parabolic Higgs bundle. To extract a bound from
5.1
depending only on
\rank E_{\star}
, we bound each of the individual terms
V^{k}
in terms of
\rank E_{\star}
. As a consequence, whenever the number of punctures violates the given bound we know that
N\leq 2
proving
Theorem 1.3
.

To that end, we prove a series of lemmas which place restrictions on the behavior of the Higgs field of
(\ad(E_{\star}),\oplus\ad(\theta)^{k})
whenever
(E_{\star},\theta)
is of minimal energy.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a parabolic Higgs bundle of minimal energy with distinct parabolic weights, then for all
1<k<N

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})=0.

Proof.

By
4.3
and
4.2
,
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))
is of Hodge length
1
. Note that since our parabolic weights are distinct,
\widehat{V^{k}_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)=V^{k}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)
for all
1\leq k\leq N
. Therefore, the complex
\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)
has associated graded pieces

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to V^{N-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)

\displaystyle V^{N-1}_{\star}

\displaystyle\to V^{N-2}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)

\displaystyle V^{2-N}_{\star}

\displaystyle\to V^{1-N}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)

\displaystyle V^{1-N}_{\star}

\displaystyle\to 0.

The graded pieces of
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))
are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{k,1-k}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))=\mathbb{H}^{1}(V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))

whenever
k\neq 1
. For
k=1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{1,0}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))=\mathbb{H}^{1}(V^{1}_{\star}\to\widehat{V^{0}_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))

but
\widehat{V^{0}_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)\neq V^{0}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)
. We have an exact sequence of complexes of sheaves

\displaystyle 0\to(\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})[0]\to[V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)]\to(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})[-1]\to 0

so we get a long exact sequence on hypercohomology
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})[0])}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{H}^{0}(V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})[-1])}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})[0])}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{H}^{1}(V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})[-1])}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

.

Since

\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}[-1])=0,

\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}[-1])=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}),

and

\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}[0])=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}),

we have a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})\to\mathbb{H}^{1}(V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))\to H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})\to 0.

By definition,
\mathbb{H}^{k,1-k}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))=\mathbb{H}^{1}(V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))
. But since
(E_{\star},\theta)
is of minimal energy,
\mathbb{H}^{k,1-k}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))=0
for
1<k<N
. Therefore, the terms
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})
and
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})
vanish for
1<k<N
due to the above sequence being short exact and the vanishing of the term in the middle.
∎

This lemma gives us strong conditions on the Higgs fields of minimal energy Higgs bundles.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
(E_{\star},\theta)
be a minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then for
1<k<N
,
\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}
is a locally free sheaf.

Proof.

As
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a smooth curve and
\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}
is a coherent sheaf, we obtain a splitting

\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}=(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{lf}}\oplus(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{tors}}
where
(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{lf}}
is locally free and
(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{tors}}
is a torsion sheaf. Since
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle,
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D))
is of Hodge length
1
.
5.3
implies that for
1<k<N
,

\displaystyle 0=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{lf}})\oplus H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{tors}}).

But
(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{tors}}
is a torsion sheaf on a curve and is hence supported at finitely many points
[
15
, Chapter 2, Section 5, Exercise 5.6(c)]
. Therefore
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{tors}})=0
if and only if
(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{tors}}
has empty support if and only if
(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{tors}}=0
. Therefore
\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}=(\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\mathrm{lf}}
as desired.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

If
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then for
1<k<N
,
\deg\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}\leq-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}
and
\deg\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}\geq-\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}
.

Proof.

As
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a minimal energy Higgs bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we have
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})=0
by
5.3
whenever
1<k<N
.

As
\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}
is a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}\cong\mathscr{O}(a_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathscr{O}(a_{\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}})
where
a_{j}
are integers and
a_{1}+\dots+a_{\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}}=\deg\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}
. Therefore

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}(a_{1}))\oplus\dots\oplus H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}(a_{\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}}))=0

if and only if all of the integers
a_{j}
are at least
-1
. So,
\deg\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}=a_{1}+\dots+a_{\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}}\geq-\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}
.

By
5.4
,
\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}
is a vector bundle and is isomorphic to
\mathscr{O}(b_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathscr{O}(b_{\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}})
. Since

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}(b_{1}))\oplus\dots\oplus H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}(b_{\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}}))=0

we know that all of the integers
b_{j}
are at most
-1
. Therefore

\displaystyle\deg\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}=b_{1}+\dots+b_{\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}}\leq-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}.

∎

We now prove a series of technical lemmas used in the proof of
5.1
.

Lemma 5.6
.

If
(E_{\star},\theta)
is of minimal energy on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with distinct parabolic weights, then

\displaystyle\pardeg V^{1})\star

\displaystyle\geq(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k}

and
\deg V^{N-1}=\rank V^{N-1}\cdot(1-\deg D)
.

Proof.

We first show that
\deg V^{N-1}=\rank V^{N-1}\cdot(1-\deg D)
. Since
(E_{\star},\theta)
is of minimal energy,
\mathbb{H}^{1}(V^{1-N}\to 0)=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V^{1-N})=0
. But observe that
V^{1-N}_{\star}
is a sub-parabolic Higgs bundle of
\ad(E_{\star})
with zero Higgs field, so
V^{1-N}
and all of its subbundles are also sub-Higgs bundles. Therefore, all subbundles of
V^{1-N}
must have non-positive parabolic degree. Since all of the parabolic weights on
V^{1-N}
are nonzero (due to the hypothesis that the parabolic weights are distinct), this implies that every subbundle of
V^{1-N}
must have
negative
degree. Therefore as vector bundles
V^{1-N}\cong\mathscr{O}(a_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathscr{O}(a_{\rank V^{1-N}})
with
a_{j}<0
for all
j=1,\dots,\rank V^{1-N}
. But since
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},V^{1-N})=0
, we have that
a_{j}\geq-1
for all
j=1,\dots,\rank V^{1-N}
. Therefore
V^{1-N}\cong\mathscr{O}(-1)^{\oplus\rank V^{1-N}}
. Equivalently,
V^{N-1}\cong(\mathscr{O}(1)^{\oplus\rank V^{N-1}})(-D)
and so
\deg V^{N-1}=\rank V^{N-1}\cdot(1-\deg D)
.

We now prove the other part of the claim and show that

\displaystyle\pardeg V^{1}_{\star}

\displaystyle\geq(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k}.

We have exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}\to V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)\to\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}\to 0.

Since degrees are additive in exact sequences,

\displaystyle\deg V^{k-1}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)+\deg\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}=\deg V^{k}+\deg\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}

for all
2<k<N
. Then using
\deg V^{k-1}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)=\deg V^{k-1}+\rank V^{k-1}\cdot(\deg D-2)
we have for

\displaystyle\deg V^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\deg V^{k}+\rank V^{k-1}\cdot(2-\deg D)+\deg\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}-\deg\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}.

5.5
tells us that
\deg\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}\leq-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}
and
\deg\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}\geq-\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\deg V^{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\deg V^{k}+\rank V^{k-1}\cdot(2-\deg D)+\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}.

Since
\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}=\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{k-1}
, we then have that

\displaystyle\deg V^{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\deg V^{k}+\rank V^{k-1}\cdot(1-\deg D)+\rank V^{k}.

By induction, for
\ell>0
we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\deg V^{k-\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\deg V^{k}+(1-\deg D)\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\rank V^{k-j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\rank V^{k-j+1}.

In the special case where
k=N-1
, we have by substituting
\deg V^{N-1}=\rank V^{N-1}\cdot(1-\deg D)
that

\displaystyle\deg V^{N-1-\ell}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\deg D)\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\rank V^{N-1-j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\rank V^{N-j}.

We now observe that
V^{0}_{\star}\oplus V^{-1}_{\star}\oplus\dots\oplus V^{1-N}_{\star}
is a sub-Higgs bundle of
\ad(E_{\star})
, so
\pardeg(V^{0}_{\star}\oplus V^{-1}_{\star}\oplus\dots\oplus V^{1-N}_{\star})\leq 0
by semi-stability of
\ad(E_{\star})_{\star}
. Equivalently,
\pardeg(V^{N-1}_{\star}\oplus\dots\oplus V^{1}_{\star})\geq 0
. We use this to first bound
\pardeg V^{k}_{\star}
when
k\geq 2
.

By applying
2.14
to
\pardeg V_{\star}^{k}
, we have that

\displaystyle\pardeg V^{k}_{\star}\leq\deg V^{k}+\rank V^{k}\cdot\deg D.

Then substituting our bounds
\deg V^{k}
obtained above, we have

\displaystyle\pardeg V^{k}_{\star}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{N-1}\rank V^{j}+(1-\deg D)\cdot\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-1}\rank V^{j}.

Since
\pardeg(V_{\star}^{N-1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{\star}^{1})\geq 0
we have that

\displaystyle\pardeg V^{1}_{\star}

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg V^{k}_{\star}

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\left(\sum_{j=k}^{N-1}\rank V^{j}+(1-\deg D)\cdot\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-1}\rank V^{j}\right),

so we have obtained a lower bound in terms of the
\rank V^{k}
. Simplifying yields

\displaystyle\pardeg V^{1}_{\star}

\displaystyle\geq(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k}.

∎

Lemma 5.7
.

For all
1<k<N
,

\displaystyle\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}\geq N-k.

In particular,
\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}>0
.

Proof.

Since
V^{k-1}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)
surjects onto
\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}
, we know that
\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}\geq 0
. By
5.5
we have

\displaystyle\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}=\rank V^{k}-\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}

and hence if
\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}=0
, then
\ad(\theta)^{k}
must be the zero map. We show that
\rank V^{k}-\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}\geq N-k
. Since
\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}
is a vector bundle, we can compute its rank by computing the rank of a fiber. Equivalently, we can compute the dimension of
(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{s}
for any point
s\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We do this by explicitly writing out the map
\ad(\theta)^{k}
.

The map

\displaystyle\ad(\theta)^{k}:\oplus_{j=1}^{N-k}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{j}_{\star},E^{j+k}_{\star})_{\star}\to\oplus_{j=1}^{N-k+1}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{j}_{\star},E^{j+k-1}_{\star})_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)

is given by

\displaystyle\ad(\theta)^{k}(f_{1}^{1+k},\dots,f_{N-k}^{N})

\displaystyle=(\theta^{1+k}\circ f_{1}^{1+k},\theta^{k+2}\circ f_{2}^{k+2}-(f_{1}^{1+k}\otimes\id)\circ\theta^{2},\dots,

\displaystyle\qquad\theta^{N}\circ f_{N-k}^{N}-(f_{N-k-1}^{N-1}\otimes\id)\circ\theta^{N-k},-(f_{N-k}^{k}\otimes\id)\circ\theta^{N-k+1})

where
f_{j}^{j+k}\in\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(E^{j}_{\star},E^{j+k}_{\star})_{\star}
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
be any point for which none of the Higgs fields of
\theta^{p}\colon E^{p}\to E^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\log D)
vanishes. Such a point exists because our Higgs fields are nonzero, and the locus where each
\theta^{p}
vanishes is a closed subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of positive codimension. By
5.4
, we know that
\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}
is a locally free sheaf for all
1<k<N
. Therefore,
\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}=\dim\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{s}
. Then,
\ad(\theta)^{k}
at
s
is given by

\displaystyle(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{s}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}B_{2}^{k+1}&A_{1}^{k}&&&\\
&B_{3}^{k+2}&A_{2}^{k+1}&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&B_{N-k+1}^{r}&A_{N-k}^{N-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}

where

\displaystyle A_{j}^{j+k-1}(f_{j}^{j+k})

\displaystyle=(\theta^{j+k})_{s}\circ f_{j}^{j+k}

\displaystyle B_{j+1}^{j+k}(f_{j}^{j+k})

\displaystyle=-(f_{j}^{j+k}\otimes\id)\circ(\theta^{j+1})_{s}.

Then,

\displaystyle\rank(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{s}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N-k}\rank(A_{j}^{j+k-1})

and since
s
is a general point, we know that our Higgs fields
\theta^{p}\colon E^{p}\to E^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)
are nonzero and so our maps
A_{j}^{j+k-1}
are also nonzero. Therefore since
\rank(A_{j}^{j+k-1})\geq 1
, we have that

\displaystyle\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}

\displaystyle=\rank V^{k}-\rank\ker\ad(\theta)^{k}=\dim(V^{k})_{s}-\dim\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{s}

\displaystyle=\rank(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{s}

\displaystyle\geq N-k.

∎

We can now prove
5.1
.

Proof of
5.1
.

We recall what we want to prove. Let
(E_{\star},\theta)
be a minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle on
(\mathbb{P}^{1},D)
with
N
graded pieces and with generic unitary local monodromy (in the sense of
3.7
). Let
D=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
. If
N\geq 3
, then

\displaystyle\deg D=n\leq\frac{2\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}+2\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{N-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(2k-3)\rank V^{k}}{\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}}.

We have an exact sequence of parabolic bundles

\displaystyle 0\to\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}\to V^{k}_{\star}\to V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)\to\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}\to 0

and by
5.4
for
1<k<N
this is an exact sequence of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Therefore we obtain by
2.13

\displaystyle\pardeg\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}+\pardeg V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)=\pardeg V^{k}_{\star}+\pardeg\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}.

Since
\pardeg V^{k-1}_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1}(\log D)=\pardeg V^{k-1}_{\star}+\rank V^{k-1}\cdot(\deg D-2)
, by summing the above equation over
1<k<N
we obtain the equality

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg V^{k-1}_{\star}+(\deg D-2)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg V^{k}_{\star}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}.

We have a telescoping sum which simplifies to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}+\pardeg V^{1}_{\star}+(\deg D-2)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\pardeg V^{N-1}_{\star}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}.

The combination of
5.6
and
2.14
yields the inequalities

\displaystyle\pardeg V^{1}_{\star}

\displaystyle\geq(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k},

\displaystyle\pardeg V^{N-1}_{\star}

\displaystyle\leq\deg V^{N-1}+\deg D\cdot\rank V^{N-1}=\rank V^{N-1}.

We then use the above inequalities. After simplifying and rearranging our original expression, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\pardeg\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}

\displaystyle\geq(\deg D-2)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{N-1}.

We now apply
2.14
to the parabolic bundles
\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}
and
\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}
. By
2.14
, we have that for all
1<k<N

\displaystyle\pardeg\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}

\displaystyle\leq\deg\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})+\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})\cdot\deg D,

\displaystyle\deg\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\pardeg\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})_{\star}.

Then, combining the inequalities yields

\displaystyle(\deg D-2)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\deg\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\deg\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})+\deg D\cdot\left(\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})\right).

Since

\displaystyle\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})-\rank(\ker\ad(\theta)^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\deg\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})-\deg\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})

by
5.5
, we have that

\displaystyle(\deg D-2)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})-\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k}))+\deg D\cdot\left(\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})\right).

Then, the rank-nullity theorem implies that
\rank\ker(\ad(\theta)^{k})-\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})=\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{k-1}
. So, substituting yields the inequality

\displaystyle(\deg D-2)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{k-1})+\deg D\cdot\left(\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k})\right).

But the expression
\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{k-1})
is a telescoping sum and equals
\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}
. Therefore, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle(\deg D-2)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+(\deg D-1)\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-1)\rank V^{k}-\rank V^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}+\deg D\cdot\left(\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}\right).

Simplifying yields

\displaystyle\deg D\cdot\left(\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k}))+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}+2\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(2k-3)\rank V^{k}.

Then,
5.7
tells us that
\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}\geq N-k
for all
1<k<N
, and
N\geq 3
. Therefore the left-hand-side is positive, since one of the
\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}
is bounded by a positive integer. Dividing then gives us,

\displaystyle\deg D\leq\frac{2\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}+2\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(2k-3)\rank V^{k}}{\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker(\ad(\theta)^{k}))+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}}.

∎

We can now extract a uniform bound on
\deg D
in terms of
\rank E_{\star}
from
5.1
.

Proof of
Theorem 1.3
.

If
(E_{\star},\theta)
has more than
2
nonzero pieces in its grading,
(E_{\star},\theta)
is given by a Higgs bundle
(E_{\star},\theta_{\star})
with nonzero Higgs field as it underlies an irreducible non-unitary local system. As our local monodromy data is generic in the sense of
3.7
, our parabolic weights are all distinct. And the
\theta^{p}
are never identically zero as
\mathbb{V}
underlies an irreducible non-unitary representation. Let
N
be the number of graded pieces in
(E_{\star},\theta)
(i.e., the number of steps in the Hodge filtration of the
\mathbb{C}
-VHS corresponding to
(E_{\star},\theta)
). Suppose that
N>2
and
n=\deg D>5r^{2}-25
.

We can apply
5.1
. We have that

\displaystyle\deg D\leq\frac{2\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}+2\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(2k-3)\rank V^{k}}{\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k})+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}}.

By
5.7
, we have that
\rank V^{k-1}-\rank\coker\ad(\theta)^{k}\geq N-k
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\deg D\leq\frac{2\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}+2\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(2k-3)\rank V^{k}}{\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(N-k)+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(k-2)\rank V^{k}}.

The denominator is bounded below by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(N-k)=\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}{2}

and this is nonzero (and positive) whenever
N>2
. Then, we coarsely bound the numerator from above. Since
\rank V^{j}\geq 1
, we know that

\displaystyle\rank(\oplus_{j=1-N,j\neq k}^{N-1}V^{j})=\sum_{j=1-N,j\neq k}^{N-1}\rank V^{j}\geq\sum_{j=1-N,j\neq k}^{N-1}1=2(N-1).

Since
\rank\ad(E_{\star})=r^{2}-1
, we have that
\rank V^{k}=\rank\ad(E_{\star})-\rank(\oplus_{j=1-N,j\neq k}^{N-1}V^{j})\leq r^{2}-1-2(N-1)=r^{2}-2N+1
. We now bound the numerator. We have that

\displaystyle 2\rank V^{N-1}-\rank V^{1}+2\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\rank V^{k-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(2k-3)\rank V^{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2(r^{2}-2N+1)-1+2\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(r^{2}-2N+1)+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}(2k-3)(r^{2}-2N+1)

\displaystyle\leq(r^{2}-2N+1)(N^{2}-2N+2)-1

\displaystyle\leq(r^{2}-2N+1)(N^{2}-2N+2)

\displaystyle\leq(r^{2}-5)(N^{2}-2N+2).

Since the denominator is at least
\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}{2}
, we have by
5.1
that

\displaystyle\deg D\leq\frac{2(r^{2}-5)(N^{2}-2N+2)}{(N-1)(N-2)}.

Then for
N\geq 3
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{N^{2}-2N+2}{(N-1)(N-2)}\leq\frac{5}{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\deg D\leq\frac{2(r^{2}-5)(N^{2}-2N+2)}{(N-1)(N-2)}\leq 5(r^{2}-5)=5r^{2}-25.

But we assumed that
\deg D>5r^{2}-25
, so our assumption that
N>2
was false. Therefore,
N\leq 2
.
∎

6.
Consequences of
Theorem 1.3

Theorem 1.3
states that when
\deg D>5r^{2}-25
, then a stable minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle of rank
r
has at most two graded pieces. We show that this is the best we can do. We construct an example of a stable minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in every rank with parabolic divisor of arbitrarily large degree which has two graded pieces. We first prove a technical lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
a
and
r
be positive integers with
r\geq 2
, and let
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{1+ra}\left(\frac{r-1}{r}\right)
be a very small real number (depending on
r
and
a
). Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+ra}+\epsilon\right)<\frac{1+a}{1+ra}-\epsilon.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\epsilon<\frac{1}{1+ra}\left(\frac{r-1}{r}\right).

Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+ra}\left(\frac{r-1}{r}\right)=\frac{r+ra-1-ra}{r(1+ra)}=\frac{r(1+a)-(1+ra)}{r(1+ra)}=\frac{1+a}{1+ra}-\frac{1}{r}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\epsilon<\frac{1+a}{1+ra}-\frac{1}{r}.

So,

\displaystyle 1+r\epsilon<r\cdot\frac{1+a}{1+ra}.

Then adding
-\frac{1+a}{1+ra}-(r-1)\epsilon
to both sides yields

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1+a}{1+ra}+\epsilon<(r-1)\left(\frac{1+a}{1+ra}-\epsilon\right),

giving the desired inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+ra}+\epsilon\right)<\frac{1+a}{1+ra}-\epsilon.

∎

In the following, we give an example of a minimal energy local system on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
in every rank (with the number of punctures depending on the rank) which has
\su(1,r-1)
-monodromy. These representations
\rho
admit a
\rho
-equivariant holomorphic map from the universal cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
to complex hyperbolic space (which is the symmetric space of
\su(1,r-1)
) and have been previously constructed by Tholozan-Toulisse
[
28
, Theorem 2]
.

Example 6.2
.

Let
a
be some positive integer,
r>2
, and
\epsilon<\frac{1}{1+ra}\left(\frac{r-1}{r}\right)
very small. Let
E=S\oplus Q
where
S=\mathscr{O}(-a)^{\oplus(r-1)}
and
Q=\mathscr{O}(-a-1)
, so
\rank E=r
. We let
D
be a reduced effective divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
so that
\deg D=1+ra
and
D=x_{1}+\dots+x_{1+ra}
are the points of the divisor. We choose our Higgs field
\theta\colon S\to Q\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
to be generic, and since
Q
is a line bundle we know that
\theta
is surjective. At each
x_{j}
, we give
E
the weights

\displaystyle 0<\frac{1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+ra}+\epsilon\right)+\epsilon_{1}<\dots<\frac{1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+ra}+\epsilon\right)+\epsilon_{r-1}<\frac{1+a}{1+ra}-\epsilon<1

\epsilon_{1}<\dots<\epsilon_{r-1}
are chosen to be very close to zero, and so that
\epsilon_{1}+\dots+\epsilon_{r-1}=0
. By
6.1
, we can pick
\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{r-1}
satisfying these conditions.

We give
E_{x_{j}}
the parabolic flag
E_{x_{j}}=S_{j}^{1}\oplus Q_{x_{j}}\supset\dots\supset S_{j}^{r-1}\oplus Q_{x_{j}}\supset Q_{x_{j}}\supseteq 0
where
\{S_{j}^{i}\}
is a generically chosen flag of
S_{x_{j}}
. Our parabolic weights are distinct and our parabolic structure can be chosen to be generic in the sense of
3.7
by modifying the real numbers
\epsilon

\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{r-1}
.

We have a nonzero Higgs field because
\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(S_{\star},Q_{\star})_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\cong\mathscr{O}(ra-2)^{\oplus(r-1)}
which has global sections since

Since
(E_{\star},\theta)
only has two graded pieces, the complex
\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
has graded pieces

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(Q_{\star},S_{\star})_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(Q_{\star},S_{\star})_{\star}

\displaystyle\to\widehat{\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(S_{\star},S_{\star})_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\oplus\widehat{\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(Q_{\star},Q_{\star})_{\star}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)

\displaystyle\to\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(S_{\star},Q_{\star})_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(S_{\star},Q_{\star})_{\star}

By symmetry of the Hodge structure on
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\ad(E_{\star})\to\widehat{\ad(E_{\star})}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D))
, to show that
E_{\star}
is of minimal energy we need to show that
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(S_{\star},Q_{\star})_{\star}\to 0)=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(S_{\star},Q_{\star})_{\star})=0
. But this is true, since
\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom(S_{\star},Q_{\star})_{\star}\cong\mathscr{O}(-1)^{\oplus(r-1)}

Therefore it remains to show that
(E_{\star},\theta)
is a stable parabolic Higgs bundle. By construction, the parabolic degree of
E_{\star}
is zero. The sub-Higgs bundles of
(E_{\star},\theta)
are the bundles
Q_{\star}

\ker(\theta)_{\star}

since
\deg D=1+ra

\ker(\theta)_{\star}

\displaystyle 0\to\ker(\theta)_{\star}\to S_{\star}\to Q_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\to 0.

\displaystyle\pardeg\ker(\theta)_{\star}=\pardeg S_{\star}-\pardeg Q_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D).

\pardeg S_{\star}=\epsilon\deg D

\pardeg Q_{\star}=-\epsilon\deg D

\displaystyle\pardeg\ker(\theta)_{\star}=\pardeg S_{\star}-\pardeg Q_{\star}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)=2\epsilon\deg D-\deg D+2.

\epsilon>0

It remains to check that

\displaystyle\pardeg V_{\star}

\displaystyle\leq-aN+\deg D\cdot\left(\sum_{j=r-N}^{r-1}\left(\frac{1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+ra}+\epsilon\right)+\epsilon_{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-aN+\frac{N\deg D}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+ra}+\epsilon\right)+\deg D(\epsilon_{r-1}+\dots+\epsilon_{n-N}).

Simplifying and substituting
\deg D=1+ra
into the bound yields

\displaystyle\pardeg V_{\star}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N\deg D\epsilon}{r-1}+\deg D(\epsilon_{r-1}+\dots+\epsilon_{r-N}).

Since
\pardeg Q_{\star}=-\epsilon\deg D
,

\displaystyle\pardeg(V_{\star}\oplus Q_{\star})\leq\frac{N\deg D\epsilon}{r-1}+\deg D(\epsilon_{r-1}+\dots+\epsilon_{r-N})-\epsilon\deg D.

Since
0\leq N<r-1
and
\epsilon_{r-1}+\dots+\epsilon_{r-N}
is chosen to be very small, we have
\pardeg(V_{\star}\oplus Q_{\star})<0
.

Therefore every sub-Higgs bundle of
(E_{\star},\theta)
fails to be destabilizing, so
(E_{\star},\theta)
is indeed a stable minimal energy parabolic Higgs bundle of rank
r
with parabolic divisor
D
that has two graded pieces.

By choosing
a\gg 0
, we can force
\deg D=1+ra
to be larger than
5r^{2}-25
. Therefore, we cannot do better than two graded pieces in
Theorem 1.3
. In this example, we picked our parabolic weights so that at each point in
D
, the weights sum to an integer. Therefore, the local monodromy data is not only unitary but actually lies in
\su(r)
.

6.3.
An application to Gromov-Witten theory

We give an application of minimal energy local systems to Gromov-Witten invariants.

To a parabolic bundle
V_{\star}
of rank
n
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with parabolic divisor
D
, we can obtain a modified complete parabolic bundle
\widetilde{V}_{\star}
with the following properties. This process is described in
[
1
, below Theorem 5.1]
and in
[
2
, Appendix]
. We describe their properties below.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
V_{\star}
be a parabolic bundle and
\widetilde{V}_{\star}
its modified parabolic bundle.

(1)

The underlying bundle of
\widetilde{V_{\star}}
is a trivial bundle so that
\rank\tilde{V}=\rank V
.

(2)

If
V_{\star}
has
0\leq\alpha_{j}^{1}<\dots<\alpha_{j}^{n_{j}}<1
at
x_{j}\in D
, then the weights
\{\widetilde{\alpha_{j}^{i}}\}
of
\widetilde{V}_{\star}
at
x_{j}\in D
no longer necessarily lie within
[0,1)
but satisfy
\widetilde{\alpha_{j}^{1}}\leq\dots\leq\widetilde{\alpha_{j}^{n}}<\widetilde{\alpha_{j}^{1}}+1
. The weights can be repeated.

(3)

The parabolic degree of a modified parabolic bundle is defined to be the sum of the degree of the underlying bundle and the weights above every point. Then,
\pardeg(V_{\star})=\pardeg(\widetilde{V}_{\star})
.

(4)

There is a bijection between subbundles
W_{\star}
of
V_{\star}
and modified parabolic subbundles
\widetilde{W}_{\star}
of
\widetilde{V}_{\star}
. This bijection preserves parabolic degree. If
W_{\star}
and
\widetilde{W}_{\star}
are sent to each other under this bijection, then
\pardeg W_{\star}=\pardeg\widetilde{W}_{\star}
.

(5)

As a consequence of (1), (3) and (4),
V_{\star}
is parabolic stable if and only if
\widetilde{V}_{\star}
is parabolic stable.

A conjugacy class
C\subseteq\su(r)
is determined by the eigenvalues of a diagonal representative. If the eigenvalues are given by
(e^{2\pi i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{2\pi i\theta_{r}})
, then we obtain real numbers
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{r}
where each
\theta_{i}
is determined up to an integer. Note that the
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{r}
must sum to an integer because matrices in
\su(r)
have determinant one. We choose the
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{r}
so that
\theta_{1}+\dots+\theta_{r}=0
and that (after ordering from largest to smallest)
\theta_{1}\geq\dots\geq\theta_{r}\geq\theta_{1}-1
. This uniquely determines the conjugacy class
C
. Let
\lambda(C)=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{r})
and for some subset
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}
of size
k
we let
\lambda_{I}(C)=\sum_{j\in I}\theta_{j}
.

A subset
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}
of size
k
determines a Schubert variety
\Omega_{I}
in the Grassmannian
\gr(k,r)
, and we let
\sigma_{I}
be the cohomology class associated to
\Omega_{I}
. When
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
are all of subsets of
\{1,\dots,r\}
of same size, we let
\langle\sigma_{I_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{I_{n}}\rangle_{d}
be the number of degree
d
rational curves in
\gr(k,r)
passing through
\Omega_{I_{1}},\dots,\Omega_{I_{n}}
. The number
\langle\sigma_{I_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{I_{n}}\rangle_{d}
is a
Gromov-Witten invariant
for
\gr(k,r)
. For details on Schubert varieties and Gromov-Witten invariants, see
[
2
, Definition 4]
.

Then, Biswas
[
5
]
(when
r=2
), Belkale
[
2
, Theorem 7]
, and Agnihotri-Woodward
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
, independently prove the following:

Theorem 6.5
.

There is an
\su(r)
-local system on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
with prescribed
\su(r)
-local monodromy
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}
if and only if for any
k\in\{1,\dots,r-1\}
and any choice of subsets
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}
of cardinality
k
, the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{I_{j}}(C_{j})\leq d

holds whenever
\langle\sigma_{I_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{I_{n}}\rangle_{d}=1
.

The above statement is taken from
[
2
, Theorem 7]
.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
D=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
be a reduced effective divisor. Let
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
be a collection of generic and connected local monodromy data (in the sense of
3.7
). Let
(E_{\star},\theta)
be a minimal energy stable parabolic Higgs bundle which only has two graded pieces and has parabolic weights corresponding to the data
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}
. Then,
E_{\star}=H_{\star}\oplus(V/H)_{\star}
with Higgs field
\theta\colon H\to(V/H)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
.

Let
\widetilde{E}_{\star}
be the modified parabolic bundle of
E_{\star}
and
\widetilde{H}_{\star}
the modified parabolic bundle of
H_{\star}
. For all
j=1,\dots,n
we let
I_{j}\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}
be a subset of size
\rank H
so that for all
i\in I_{j}
,
\theta_{j}^{i}
is a parabolic weight of
\widetilde{H}_{\star}
. Then, the Gromov-Witten invariant
\langle\sigma_{I_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{I_{n}}\rangle_{-\deg\widetilde{H}}
is nonzero.

Proof.

Since
(E_{\star},\theta)
has two graded pieces, it is non-unitary. For a connected
n
-tuple of conjugacy classes
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
, the relative character variety
\chi(\pi_{1}(X\setminus D),\gl_{r}(\mathbb{C}),C)
is connected by
[
16
, Theorem 5.1.1]
. Therefore, the locus of minimal energy local systems is unique, and does not consist of unitary local systems since
(E_{\star},\theta)
is non-unitary.

Since
E_{\star}=H_{\star}\oplus(V/H)_{\star}
with
H_{\star}
a destabilizing subbundle of
V_{\star}
, we know that the associated Gromov-Witten invariant
\langle\sigma_{I_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{I_{n}}\rangle_{-\deg\widetilde{H}}
is nonzero. This is because
\widetilde{H}
is a subbundle of a trivial bundle and hence determines a rational degree
-\deg\widetilde{H}
curve in
\gr(k,r)
intersecting the Schubert varieties
\Omega_{I_{1}},\dots,\Omega_{I_{n}}
.
∎

Closed-form Solutions for Velocity and Acceleration of a Moving Vehicle Using Range, Range Rate, and Derivative of Range Rate
Mohammad Salman, Hadi Zayyani, Hasan Abu Hilal, Mostafa Rashdan

Abstract
This letter presents a novel method for estimating the position, velocity, and acceleration of a moving target using range-based measurements. Although most existing studies focus on position and velocity estimation, the framework of this letter is extended to include acceleration. To achieve this, we propose using the derivative of the range rate, in addition to the range and range rate measurements. The proposed method estimates the position at first using Time-of-Arrival (TOA)-based techniques; then, develops a reformulated least squares (LS) and weighted least squares (WLS) approaches for velocity estimation; and finally, employs the derivative of the range rate to estimate the acceleration using previous position and velocity estimates. On the other hand, closed-form LS and WLS solutions are derived for both velocity and acceleration. The simulation results show that the proposed approach provides improved performance in estimating moving target kinematics compared to existing methods.

This critical issue arises in many applications, such as radar and sensor networks. Simulation results show the superiority of the proposed WLS method over a state-of-the-art method in the literature in high Signal to Noise Ratios (SNR). Moreover, the complexity of the proposed algorithm in terms of simulation run time is less than others.

II
System Model and Problem Formulation

Consider a sensor network consisting of
N
fixed sensors in a two-dimensional space. Each sensor can measure the range, the range rate and the derivative of the range rate of a moving vehicle. Let the position of the
i
’th sensor located in a 2D space be denoted
{\textbf{p}}_{i}=[x_{i},y_{i}]^{T}
, and the position of the moving target at time
t
be
{\textbf{p}}_{s,t}=[x_{s,t},y_{s,t}]^{T}
, with a velocity given by
{\textbf{v}}_{s,t}=[v_{sx,t},v_{sy,t}]^{T}
. The range between the
i
’th sensor and the vehicle is
r_{i}=||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||_{2}
and can be measured, for example, by estimating the TOA. The set of ranges
r_{i},1\leq i\leq N
is sufficient to estimate the vehicle’s position using, for example, a trilateration method. However, estimating the velocity of the vehicle requires more information. To this end, the range rates defined as
\dot{r}_{i}=\frac{d}{dt}||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||
are utilized.
These range rates provide information that can be leveraged to estimate the velocity of the vehicle, as will be discussed in the next section.

To further explore the properties of the moving vehicle, we propose utilizing the derivative of range rates (which is the second derivative of ranges) defined as
\ddot{r}_{i}=\frac{d}{dt^{2}}||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||
. Thus, considering the measurement noises, the system incorporates three groups of measurements, namely:

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{i}=r_{i}+v_{i}=||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||+v_{i},

\displaystyle a_{i}=\dot{r}_{i}+n_{i}=\frac{d}{dt}||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||+n_{i},

\displaystyle b_{i}=\ddot{r}_{i}+\eta_{i}=\frac{d}{dt^{2}}||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||+\eta_{i},

(1)

where
v_{i}
,
n_{i}
, and
\eta_{i}
are the measurement noises of the range, the range rate, and the derivative of the range rate, respectively. The objective is to estimate the position
{\textbf{p}}_{s}
, the velocity
{\textbf{v}}_{s}=\frac{d}{dt}{\textbf{p}}_{s}
and the acceleration
{\textbf{a}}_{s}=\frac{d}{dt}{\textbf{v}}_{s}
of the vehicle using these three noisy measurements;
\bar{r}_{i}
,
d_{i}
, and
b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
.

III
The Proposed Algorithm for Velocity Estimation

To develop an algorithm to estimate the kinematic parameters of the moving target; namely, its location, velocity, and acceleration, from the measurements in (
II
), we start by driving simplified expressions for these measurements. The starting point is to derive the expression for the range rate, which can be obtained as

\displaystyle a_{i}=\dot{r}_{i}+n_{i}=\frac{{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||}+n_{i}.

(2)

Taking the time derivative of (
2
) and with some algebraic manipulations, yields the following result

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle=\ddot{r}_{i}+\eta_{i}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big[\frac{{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\Big[{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})\Big]||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||-{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})\frac{d}{dt}||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\Big(\frac{d{\textbf{v}}_{s}}{dt}\Big)({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})+||{\textbf{v}}_{s}||^{2}-a_{i}{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}}+\eta_{i},

(3)

where the approximation in (
III
) is due to the neglect of the noise term
n_{i}
in (
2
) when substituting
a_{i}
in place of
\frac{d}{dt}||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||=\frac{{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||}
.

Since the three equations for the range, the range rate and the derivative of the range rate are interconnected and quite complex to jointly estimate the position, velocity, and acceleration, we adopt a sequential approach to estimate these parameters independently. We begin with TOA-based equations to estimate the position
{\textbf{p}}_{s}=[x_{s},y_{s}]^{T}
using only the range measurements. This is a classical approach in TOA-based localization. This results in

\displaystyle\hat{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}}={\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}{\textbf{f}},

(4)

where
{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}=[x_{s},y_{s},x^{2}_{s}+y^{2}_{s}]^{T}
,
†
is the pseudoinverse operator,
f_{i}=r^{2}_{i}-x^{2}_{i}-y^{2}_{i}
and
{\textbf{A}}(i,:)=[-2x_{i},-2y_{i},1]
for
1\leq i\leq N
.

III-A

LS estimation of velocity

After estimating the position of the moving target, we proceed to estimate the velocity of the moving target using the range rates
a_{i}
’s for all
1\leq i\leq N
. Thus, reformulating (
2
) by multiplying its two sides by
||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||
, yields

\displaystyle{\textbf{d}}=\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{v}}_{s}+{\textbf{n}}_{c},

(5)

where
{\textbf{d}}=[d_{1},d_{2},...,d_{N}]^{T}
is an
N\times 1
vector,
d_{i}=a_{i}r_{i}
is a scalar,
{\textbf{P}}=[{\textbf{p}}_{1}|{\textbf{p}}_{2}|...|{\textbf{p}}_{N}
is a
2\times N
matrix, and
{\textbf{n}}_{c}=[n_{c,1},...,n_{c,N}]^{T}
,
n_{c,i}=r_{i}n_{i}
. LS method is used to estimate the velocity vector
{\textbf{v}}_{s}
using the vector
d
. The LS cost function is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}({\textbf{v}}_{s})=||{\textbf{d}}-\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}+{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{v}}_{s}||^{2}_{2}=||{\textbf{d}}-{\textbf{a}}||^{2},

(6)

where
{\textbf{a}}=\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{v}}_{s}
. Neglecting the constant terms of
\mathrm{J}({\textbf{v}}_{s})
in (
6
) provides

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}({\textbf{v}}_{s})

\displaystyle\equiv-2{\textbf{d}}^{T}{\textbf{a}}+{\textbf{a}}^{T}{\textbf{a}}.

(7)

The term
{\textbf{a}}^{T}{\textbf{a}}
in (
7
) can be simplified as

\displaystyle{\textbf{a}}^{T}{\textbf{a}}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{v}}_{s})^{T}(\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{v}}_{s})

\displaystyle=N{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}{\textbf{p}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{v}}_{s}-2{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{P}}\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{p}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{v}}_{s}+{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{P}}{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{v}}_{s}

\displaystyle={\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{R}}^{*}{\textbf{v}}_{s},

(8)

where
{\textbf{R}}^{*}=N{\textbf{R}}_{ps}-2{\textbf{R}}_{0}+{\textbf{R}}_{p}
, where
{\textbf{R}}_{ps}\triangleq{\textbf{p}}_{s}{\textbf{p}}^{T}_{s}
,
{\textbf{R}}_{0}\triangleq{\textbf{P}}\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{p}}^{T}_{s}
, and
{\textbf{R}}_{p}\triangleq{\textbf{P}}{\textbf{P}}^{T}
. Now, substituting (
III-A
) into (
7
) yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}({\textbf{v}}_{s})\equiv-2{\textbf{d}}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}+2{\textbf{d}}^{T}{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{v}}_{s}+{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{R}}^{*}{\textbf{v}}_{s}.

(9)

The cost function in (
9
) is quadratic in
{\textbf{v}}_{s}
. Taking the derivative with respect to
{\textbf{v}}_{s}
and setting it to zero;
\frac{\partial\mathrm{J}({\textbf{v}}_{s})}{\partial{\textbf{v}}_{s}}=\mathbf{0}
, provides the following closed-form solution for velocity estimation:

\displaystyle\hat{{\textbf{v}}}_{s,LS}=c\Big({\textbf{R}}^{*}+{\textbf{P}}{\textbf{d}}\Big)^{-1}{\textbf{p}}_{s},

(10)

where
c\triangleq{\textbf{d}}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{N}
.

III-B

WLS estimation of velocity

Since the noise term in (
5
) is range dependent, a WLS method is more convenient to estimate the velocity. The weights can be defined as
W_{i}=\frac{1}{||\hat{{\textbf{p}}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||}
, in which the measurements with the higher associated noise are assigned lower weights. This WLS approach is an iterative method in nature to assign initial weights and then estimate the velocity, and then the process is repeated.

The WLS cost function is then defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{WLS}}({\textbf{v}}_{s})

\displaystyle=({\textbf{d}}-{\textbf{a}})^{T}\mathbf{W}({\textbf{d}}-{\textbf{a}})

\displaystyle\equiv-2{\textbf{d}}^{T}\mathbf{W}{\textbf{a}}+{\textbf{a}}^{T}\mathbf{W}{\textbf{a}},

(11)

where
\mathbf{W}=\mathrm{diag}(W_{i})
is the diagonal weighting matrix. Following a similar derivation as presented in Subsection
III-A
, we obtain
{\textbf{a}}^{T}\mathbf{W}{\textbf{a}}={\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{R}}^{*}_{w}{\textbf{v}}_{s}
, where
{\textbf{R}}^{*}_{w}={\textbf{R}}_{ps,w}-2{\textbf{R}}_{0,w}+{\textbf{R}}_{pw}
in which
{\textbf{R}}_{ps,w}\triangleq{\textbf{p}}_{s}\mathbf{1}^{T}_{N}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{p}}^{T}_{s}
,
{\textbf{R}}_{0,w}\triangleq{\textbf{P}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{p}}^{T}_{s}
, and
{\textbf{R}}_{pw}\triangleq{\textbf{P}}\mathbf{W}{\textbf{P}}^{T}
. Again, some calculations result to the WLS cost function as
\mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{WLS}}({\textbf{v}}_{s})\equiv-2{\textbf{d}}^{T}\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{v}}_{s})+{\textbf{v}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{R}}^{*}_{w}{\textbf{v}}_{s}
. Similarly, the minimum of the WLS cost function is obtained by setting its partial derivative with respect to
{\textbf{v}}_{s}
equal to zero. Thus, the WLS-based velocity estimator is given as

\displaystyle\hat{{\textbf{v}}}_{s,WLS}=c_{w}\Big({\textbf{R}}^{*}_{w}+{\textbf{P}}\mathbf{W}^{T}{\textbf{d}}\Big)^{-1}{\textbf{p}}_{s},

(12)

where
c_{w}\triangleq{\textbf{d}}^{T}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{1}_{N}
.

IV
The Proposed Algorithm for Acceleration Estimation

In this section, the acceleration of a moving target is estimated using the derivative of the range rates
b_{i}
’s, using the previously estimated location and velocity obtained in Section
III
. By defining the acceleration vector as
{\textbf{a}}_{s}\triangleq\frac{d{\textbf{v}}_{s}}{dt}
, and using the derivative of the range rates in (
III
), the following approximation is obtained

\displaystyle b_{i}\approx\frac{{\textbf{a}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}}+\frac{||{\textbf{v}}_{s}||^{2}}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}}-\frac{a_{i}}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||}+\eta_{i}.

(13)

Given that
{\textbf{p}}_{s}
and
{\textbf{v}}_{s}
are already estimated, this expression can be written as

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{i}=b_{i}-\frac{||{\textbf{v}}_{s}||^{2}}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}+{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}}-\frac{a_{i}}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||}\approx\frac{{\textbf{a}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})}{||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}}+\eta_{i}.

(14)

Multiplying both sides of (
14
) by
||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}
, yields

\displaystyle k_{i}\triangleq\tilde{b}_{i}||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}={\textbf{a}}^{T}_{s}({\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i})+\eta^{{}^{\prime}}_{i},

(15)

where
\eta^{{}^{\prime}}_{i}=\eta_{i}||{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{p}}_{i}||^{2}
.

IV-A

LS estimation of acceleration

To estimate the acceleration vector
{\textbf{a}}_{s}
using the transformed derivative of the range rate in (
15
), we collect all the measurements of
k_{i}
in a vector
{\textbf{k}}\triangleq[k_{1},k_{2},...k_{N}]^{T}
. Following similar steps as in the previous sections, the transformed measurement vector
k
can be written as

\displaystyle{\textbf{k}}=\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{a}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{a}}_{s}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{{}^{\prime}},

(16)

where
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{{}^{\prime}}=[\eta^{{}^{\prime}}_{1},...,\eta^{{}^{\prime}}_{N}]^{T}
. Such a system can be solved using LS and hence the following LS cost function can be used:

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}({\textbf{a}}_{s})=||{\textbf{k}}-\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{a}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}+{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{a}}_{s}||^{2}_{2}=||{\textbf{d}}-{\textbf{a}}^{{}^{\prime}}||^{2},

(17)

where
{\textbf{a}}^{{}^{\prime}}\triangleq\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{a}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{a}}_{s}
. Following similar derivations, which are omitted for brevity, the final LS estimator for the acceleration is written as

\displaystyle\hat{{\textbf{a}}}_{s,LS}=c_{a}\Big({\textbf{R}}^{*}_{a}+{\textbf{P}}{\textbf{k}}\Big)^{-1}{\textbf{p}}_{s},

(18)

where
c_{a}\triangleq{\textbf{k}}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{N}
,
{\textbf{R}}^{*}_{a}={\textbf{R}}^{*}
.

IV-B

WLS estimation of acceleration

For WLS estimation, the WLS cost function is similarly equal to

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{WLS}}({\textbf{a}}_{s})\equiv-2{\textbf{k}}^{T}\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{1}_{N}{\textbf{a}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{p}}_{s}-{\textbf{P}}^{T}{\textbf{a}}_{s})+{\textbf{a}}^{T}_{s}{\textbf{R}}^{*}_{w,a}{\textbf{a}}_{s},

(19)

where
{\textbf{R}}^{*}_{w,a}={\textbf{R}}^{*}_{w}
. Similar steps as before, the final WLS estimator for the acceleration is equal to

\displaystyle\hat{{\textbf{a}}}_{s,WLS}=c_{w,a}\Big({\textbf{R}}^{*}_{w}+{\textbf{P}}\mathbf{W}^{T}{\textbf{k}}\Big)^{-1}{\textbf{p}}_{s},

(20)

where
c_{w,a}\triangleq{\textbf{k}}^{T}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{1}_{N}
.

\mathrm{RMSE}(\hat{{\textbf{m}}})=\sqrt{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}||{\textbf{m}}-\hat{{\textbf{m}}}||_{2}^{2}}

VI
Conclusion

This letter presents a method for velocity and acceleration estimation of a moving vehicle using range measurements, range rate measurements, and derivative of range rates. Two LS and WLS approaches are suggested to solve the problem by reformulating the measurement equations of range rate and of range rate measurements. The simulation results show the advantage of the proposed methods especially WLS method in the low noise conditions. Moreover, the computational complexity of the proposed methods in terms of simulation run time is one order of magnitude less than the competing method.

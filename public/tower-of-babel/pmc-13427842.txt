Real-World Outcomes of Adjuvant Pembrolizumab for Renal Cell Carcinoma: The Potential Role of PD-L1 as a Prognostic Biomarker
Authors: Hiroki Ishihara, Takayuki Nakayama, Hironori Fukuda, Nanaka Katsurayama, Yuki Nemoto, Shinsuke Mizoguchi, Hiroaki Shimmura, Yasunobu Hashimoto, Atsuko Seki, Kazuhiko Yoshida, Junpei Iizuka, Tsunenori Kondo, Yoji Nagashima, Toshio Takagi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReal-world data on adjuvant pembrolizumab after surgery for renal cell carcinoma (RCC) are limited.ObjectiveTo clarify the real-world outcomes of adjuvant pembrolizumab for patients with RCC with a high risk of recurrence.Patients and MethodsWe retrospectively evaluated the clinicopathological data of 53 patients who received pembrolizumab as an adjuvant therapy after curative surgery for RCC on the basis of the criteria of the KEYNOTE-564 trial. The efficacy and safety profiles were assessed. The potential of PD-L1 expression as a biomarker of recurrence was evaluated in 35 RCC samples using the combined positive score (CPS).ResultsThe median follow-up period was 18.9 months. On the basis of the KEYNOTE-564 criteria, 45 patients (85%) were categorized into the intermediate-to-high-risk group. A total of 11 patients (21%) experienced recurrence, with a 12-month disease-free survival (DFS) rate of 90.5%. Patients with high PD-L1 CPS (≥ 10) had a higher recurrence rate than those with low PD-L1 CPS (< 10) (44% versus 5%, p = 0.0130). DFS was shorter in patients with high PD-L1 expression than in those with low PD-L1 expression (median: 24.0 months versus not reached, p = 0.0116). Grade ≥ 3 adverse events occurred in 13 patients (25%), and treatment discontinuation was required in 11 patients (21%).ConclusionsAdjuvant pembrolizumab exhibited feasible efficacy and manageable safety profiles for high-risk RCC in a real-world population. Further studies with longer follow-up periods and larger sample sizes are needed to confirm overall survival benefit, long-term toxicity, and the potential role of PD-L1 as a prognostic biomarker.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11523-026-01226-z.

Key Points

Adjuvant pembrolizumab is a feasible treatment option for patients with high-risk RCC after surgery.PD-L1 expression may be used as a prognostic biomarker for the recurrence of adjuvant pembrolizumab.

Introduction

Adjuvant therapy after curative surgery for renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is challenging owing to limited therapeutic efficacy. Previously, several anti-VEGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) monotherapies failed to demonstrate significant activity in the adjuvant setting [1]. Sunitinib alone improved disease-free survival (DFS) compared with placebo in patients with RCC at high risk of recurrence in the double-blind, randomized phase III S-TRAC trial [2]. However, in this trial, no benefit of sunitinib over placebo with regard to overall survival (OS) was observed [3]. The double-blind, randomized phase III ASSURE trial also showed no difference in DFS or OS between patients treated with sunitinib and placebo as adjuvant therapy [4, 5]. Considering these conflicting findings and lack of OS benefit, evidence to support the implementation of adjuvant therapy for RCC was not strong in the previous targeted therapy era.

Currently, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) play a central role for systemic therapy in advanced or metastatic RCC, for which evidence is supported by pivotal randomized phase III clinical trials [6]. Moreover, the therapeutic advantage of ICIs as an adjuvant setting has been intensively tested in randomized phase III clinical trials. However, findings were negative, and the benefit of adjuvant ICI treatment remain unclear. Specifically, on the basis of the IMmotion 010 trial, atezolizumab as adjuvant therapy showed no evidence of improved DFS versus placebo in patients after resection of RCC with increased risk of recurrence [7]. Another finding from part A of the CheckMate 914 trial has demonstrated that adjuvant therapy with nivolumab plus ipilimumab did not improve DFS compared with placebo in patients with localized RCC at a high risk of recurrence after nephrectomy [8]. The PROSPER trial also showed that perioperative nivolumab before nephrectomy followed by adjuvant nivolumab did not improve recurrence-free survival versus surgery only followed by surveillance in patients with high-risk RCC [9]. In this context, the double-blind randomized phase III KEYNOTE-564 trial demonstrated that pembrolizumab significantly improved DFS and OS compared with placebo in patients with high-risk RCC when used as adjuvant therapy [10, 11]. On the basis of this evidence, adjuvant pembrolizumab has become the standard-of-care for patients with RCC at high risk of recurrence [12].

To maximize the therapeutic effect as well as prevent from overtreatment of adjuvant treatment, effective biomarkers for patient selection and outcome monitoring are essential. Although much effort has been paid, effective biomarkers remain unidentified. For example, the potential role of serum KIM-1 as a novel biomarker has been highlighted in the adjuvant setting [13], although the utility in real-world setting has not been fully validated. In this context, PD-L1 is a key immune checkpoint molecule, playing a significant role in the tumor immune microenvironment [14] and its positivity is associated with clinical outcomes [15]. In the adjuvant setting, subgroup analyses from clinical trials focused on the impact of PD-L1, although the findings were controversial [7, 11, 16]. Specifically, the KEYNOTE-564 trial showed that patients with tumors exhibiting high expression of PD-L1 were likely to demonstrate more improvement in OS and DFS with adjuvant pembrolizumab compared with placebo than those with low PD-L1 expression [11]. By contrast, the IMmotion 010 trial [7] and part B of the CheckMate 914 trial [16] did not show such superiority of adjuvant treatment versus placebo in the high-PD-L1 group compared with the low-PD-L1 group.

In this context, real-world clinical outcomes of adjuvant pembrolizumab, as well as the prognostic role of PD-L1, remain limited. Therefore, we retrospectively investigated effectiveness and safety profile of adjuvant pembrolizumab for high-risk RCC after surgery using real-world, multi-institutional data. In addition, we evaluated the impact of PD-L1 expression as a prognostic marker for recurrence after adjuvant pembrolizumab treatment.

Patients and Methods

Patient Selection and Study Design

A total of 60 patients received adjuvant pembrolizumab after curative partial or radical nephrectomy for kidney tumors between November 2022 and January 2025 at our department (Tokyo Women’s Medical University) and its four affiliated institutions (Tokyo Women’s Medical University Adachi Medical Center, Saiseikai Kawaguchi General Hospital, Saiseikai Kazo Hospital, and Jyoban Hospital). Among these, we excluded seven patients: three had received presurgical systemic therapy, two underwent insufficient follow-up, one did not meet the KEYNOTE-564 trial criteria [10], and one was not histopathologically diagnosed with RCC, which was found after treatment initiation. The remaining 53 patients were evaluated in this retrospective observational study.

Adjuvant pembrolizumab was offered to patients who underwent curative surgery for RCC and met the KEYNOTE-564 trial criteria [10]. However, the final decision regarding the administration of adjuvant pembrolizumab was based on shared decision-making between physicians and patients, considering the therapeutic benefits, toxicity risks, and possibility of overtreatment.

Adjuvant pembrolizumab was administered according to the protocol used in the KEYNOTE-564 trial [10]. Pembrolizumab was administered for approximately 1 year unless radiographic disease recurrence was observed or intolerable adverse events (AEs) occurred. In all cases, the dosage was not modified, whereas the administration interval could be modified according to toxicity or patient’s condition.

DFS was used for assessing the effectiveness profile of pembrolizumab. DFS was calculated from the time of pembrolizumab initiation until disease recurrence or death, whichever occurred first. The cutoff date for the outcome data was the end of February 2026, and patients lost to follow-up were censored at the time of last contact. All included patients had undergone at least 1 year of follow-up after the initiation of adjuvant pembrolizumab. Computed tomography of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed during post-treatment follow-up at regular 12-week intervals, depending on the patient’s condition. Magnetic resonance imaging, positron emission tomography/computed tomography, and brain imaging were considered when necessary. Regarding the safety profile, the rates of AEs, interruption, discontinuation, and glucocorticoid requirement were assessed. AEs were diagnosed using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 [17].

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Review Board of Tokyo Women’s Medical University (ID no. 2020-0009 and 382). Tumor samples were obtained after getting written informed consent from all patients. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments.

Evaluation of PD-L1 Expression

We collected 35 RCC samples obtained via radical or partial nephrectomy at Tokyo Women’s Medical University. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor specimens were subjected to immunohistochemical analysis for PD-L1 expression. PD-L1 staining was performed at SRL Inc. (Tokyo, Japan) using the PD-L1 IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The combined positive score (CPS) was calculated as the number of PD-L1-staining cells (tumor cells, lymphocytes, and macrophages) divided by the total number of viable tumor cells, multiplied by 100. PD-L1 expression was evaluated by experienced pathologists (A. S. and Y. N.) who were blinded to the clinical outcomes and parameters. For exploratory analyses, tumors were categorized according to CPS values (< 1, ≥ 1 to < 10, and ≥ 10).

Statistical Analysis

The baseline characteristics are presented as numbers (%) for categorical variables and medians (interquartile range) for continuous variables. Categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test or chi-squared test, as appropriate. Survival was calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used for multivariate analysis to identify factors associated with DFS. Results are presented as hazard ratios (HRs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). All analyses were performed using JMP software (version 17; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA), and statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Patient Characteristics

Table 1 presents the patient characteristics. A total of 16 patients (30%) were aged ≥ 65 years, and 46 (87%) were men. Collectively, 44 patients (83%) had clear-cell histology and Fuhrman grade 4 was observed in 13 (25%) patients. Sarcomatoid features were detected in eight patients (15%). The predominant pathological T stage was 3 (n = 49, 92%). On the basis of the KEYNOTE-564 trial criteria [10], 45 (85%), 2 (4%), and 6 (11%) patients were categorized into the intermediate-to-high-risk, high-risk, and M1 NED groups, respectively.

Effectiveness

During the follow-up period, disease recurrence was observed in 11 (21 %) patients (Table 2). Of these, four (8%) and seven (13%) patients experienced recurrence during and after treatment completion (i.e., approximately 1 year after treatment initiation), respectively. Treatment was completed as planned in 38 patients (72%), while four (8%) and 11 (21%) patients discontinued treatment owing to disease recurrence and toxicity, respectively. In the 11 patients with recurrence, the most frequent organs involved were the lymph nodes (n = 5, 45%), followed by the lungs (n = 4, 36%) and bone (n = 3, 27%). Regarding treatment for recurrence, systemic therapy and metastasis-directed therapy were administered to four (36%) and six patients (55%), respectively, while watchful waiting was performed in two patients (18%).

Prognostic Role of PD-L1

We further analyzed the correlation between PD-L1 expression and disease recurrence in 35 patients and found that four (11%), 15 (43%), and 16 (46%) patients had PD-L1 CPS < 1, ≥ 1 to < 10, and ≥ 10, respectively (Table 1). The rate of disease recurrence was significantly higher in patients with RCC exhibiting PD-L1 ≥ 10 (high PD-L1) compared with those with PD-L1 < 10 (low PD-L1) (44% versus 5%, p = 0.0130) (Fig. 2a). This significant association was also observed in patients with clear-cell RCC: in a cohort comprising patients with clear-cell RCC (n = 28), patients with RCC harboring high PD-L1 (i.e., ≥ 10) had a higher recurrence rate than those with low PD-L1 (i.e., < 10) (50% versus 6%, p = 0.0228) (Fig. 2a). Furthermore, DFS was significantly shorter in patients with high PD-L1 compared with those with low PD-L1 (median: 24.0 [95% CI 21.1–N.R.] versus N.R. [95% CI 15.4–30.5] months; 12-month rate: 81.3% versus 100%; 18-month rate: 64.6% versus 100%, p = 0.0116) (Fig. 2b). Since PD-L1 expression and Fuhrman grade 4 were potentially associated with disease recurrence, we analyzed the correlation between these two factors. This analysis revealed that a high Fuhrman grade had a trend toward being associated with high PD-L1 expression, although the statistical difference was not significant (p = 0.0893) (Fig. 3). We further conducted a multivariate analysis using two factors including PD-L1 expression and Fuhrman grade to identity risk factors for DFS in 35 patients with eligible PD-L1 data. Thus, the high PD-L1 expression rather than Fuhrman grade was associated with shorter DFS (HR, 10.4 [95% CI 1.23–88.5], p = 0.0316) (Supplementary Table 1).

Safety

At least one AE of any grade was observed in 37 (70%) patients, while AEs with grade ≥ 3 occurred in 13 (25%) patients (Table 4). Among any grade AEs, the most frequent AE was rash (n = 19, 36%), followed by hypothyroidism (n = 5, 9%) and fever, fatigue, and hepatic enzyme increase (8%). Regarding grade ≥ 3 AEs, pneumonitis was most frequently observed (n = 3, 6%), followed by hepatic enzyme increase and pancreatic enzyme increase (each n = 2, 4%). Treatment interruption and discontinuation were required in 14 (26%) and 11 (21 %) patients, respectively. Glucocorticoids were needed in 13 patients (25%), including six (11%) who received high-dose glucocorticoid treatment (≥ 40 mg of prednisone per day). No patients died owing to AEs.

Discussion

In this real-world multi-institution study, adjuvant pembrolizumab exhibited feasible effectiveness and manageable safety profiles in patients with RCC who were at high risk of recurrence after surgery. High PD-L1 expression and Fuhrman grade were potentially associated with disease recurrence after adjuvant pembrolizumab. Furthermore, patients with RCC exhibiting high PD-L1 expression had shorter DFS than those with low PD-L1 expression after adjuvant pembrolizumab.

The patient characteristics in the present cohort were generally similar to those in the phase III KEYNOTE-564 trial in terms of the rate of sarcomatoid features and disease risk category [10, 11]. The 12-month DFS rate was also comparable to that of the KEYNOTE-564 trial (85.5% in the intention-to-treatment population) [11]. When analyzed in the cohort of patients with clear-cell histology, the 12-month DFS rate was 90.9% (Fig. 1b) in this study, which is also consistent with the findings of the KEYNOTE-564 trial. We included patients with non-clear-cell RCC in this study to reflect real-world outcomes, although this population was not assessed in the KEYNOTE-564 trial. Nevertheless, the present findings reveal that adjuvant pembrolizumab may provide a feasible therapeutic benefit for disease recurrence, even in real-world settings.

The safety profile in this study was similar to that in the KEYNOTE-564 trial. In the KEYNOTE-564 trial, the rate of any-cause AEs leading to pembrolizumab discontinuation was 21.1%, and treatment-related AEs of grades 3 or 4 were observed in 18.6% of patients [11]. In addition, high-dose glucocorticoids were required in 7.4% of patients [10]. Collectively, the toxicity profile in our cohort is comparable to that in the KEYNOTE-564 trial.

Data regarding clinical outcomes following adjuvant pembrolizumab for high-risk RCC are still limited, particularly in real-world populations. In this context, an international multi-institutional study recently showed that the real-world 2-year DFS rate of adjuvant pembrolizumab was 69% at a median follow-up of 15.4 months [18], which appears to be in line with our findings. In that study, the presence of sarcomatoid differentiation was associated with DFS (HR: 2.13) [18], while the impact of the nuclear grade was not investigated. Although the previous and present studies indicated the feasibility of adjuvant pembrolizumab, the findings were obtained from a single-arm population treated with pembrolizumab. Therefore, further studies comparing treatment outcomes with and without adjuvant pembrolizumab are needed to confirm its real-world benefit.

Predictive or prognostic biomarkers for selecting candidates for adjuvant pembrolizumab have not yet been identified, and this remains an unmet medical need [19, 20]. Subgroup analysis of the KEYNOTE-564 trial showed that patients with tumors exhibiting high expression of PD-L1 were likely to demonstrate more improvement in OS and DFS with adjuvant pembrolizumab compared with placebo than those with low PD-L1 expression [11]. However, the IMmotion 010 [7] and part B of the CheckMate 914 trial (adjuvant nivolumab versus placebo) did not demonstrate a significant benefit of adjuvant ICIs on the basis of PD-L1 in patients with high-risk recurrence [16]. Thus, at least when compared with placebo, the therapeutic benefit of adjuvant ICIs based on PD-L1 remains inconclusive. In the present study, PD-L1 ≥ 10% was significantly associated with disease recurrence and shorter DFS. Interestingly, high PD-L1 expression appeared to be associated with a high Fuhrman grade, which was also frequently observed in patients with recurrence (Table 3). Considering these findings, more profound immunosuppression may be involved in RCCs exhibiting high PD-L1 expression. Otherwise, such tumors may harbor pathogenic aggressive potential, which is explained by their high nuclear grade. Collectively, these adverse characteristics could decrease the therapeutic effect of pembrolizumab. Thus, further studies with a larger cohort and longer follow-up period are warranted to validate the prognostic value of PD-L1 in patients undergoing adjuvant pembrolizumab treatment.

Much effort has been invested into the identification of biomarkers in adjuvant settings. Recently, the potential of serum KIM-1 as a biomarker has been highlighted. Using clinical data and samples from IMmotion 010 trial, patients with serum KIM-1high levels at baseline had worse DFS but also showed improvement in DFS when treated with adjuvant atezolizumab versus placebo [13]. In addition, an increase in KIM-1 during follow-up was associated with worse DFS compared with patients without an increase in KIM-1. Considering these findings, elevated post-nephrectomy plasma KIM-1 levels and kinetics may be considered prognostic factors, supporting the hypothesis that KIM-1 is a biomarker for minimal residual disease in RCC and that biomarker-informed adjuvant therapy contributes to personalized treatment indications and improves outcomes of patients with high-risk RCC.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective nature and small sample size have induced inevitable selection bias, potentially affecting the clinical outcomes. Also, we conducted some comparison analyses on the basis of the presence of recurrence with focusing on baseline patient background factors and PD-L1 and multivariate analysis for DFS, although these findings could be affected by multiple testing owing to the limited sample size. Second, we did not assess the OS because of the insufficient follow-up period. Third, we administered adjuvant pembrolizumab to patients who met the KEYNOTE-564 trial criteria, basically. However, the final decision regarding the implementation of adjuvant therapy depended on each physician, potentially inducing selection bias. Furthermore, a subset of patients started pembrolizumab beyond the period defined by the protocol of KEYNOTE-564 trial (e.g., started at > 3 months postoperatively), which also potentially affect outcome. Fourth, the threshold of PD-L1 expression differed between the KEYNOTE-564 trial and this study; most pivotal trials investigating the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors for RCC set the threshold of PD-L1 expression at 1% [11, 21–24]. Finally, we utilized the Fuhrman grading system to assess the nuclear grade, although the categorization based on the International Society of Urological Pathology (ISUP)/World Health Organization (WHO) grading system may better reflect disease aggressiveness [25].

Conclusions

This observational retrospective study using multi-institutional data demonstrated the feasible and manageable safety profile of adjuvant pembrolizumab after surgery for high-risk RCC in a real-world population. PD-L1 expression and Fuhrman grade, which appear to be linked with each other, might be effective prognostic factors for disease recurrence after adjuvant pembrolizumab. Further studies with a larger sample size and longer follow-up period are essential to confirm the OS benefit and long-term toxicity of adjuvant pembrolizumab and to validate the role of PD-L1 as a prognostic biomarker.

Abstract
Differential privacy is increasingly formalized through the lens of hypothesis testing via the robust and interpretable f f -DP framework, where privacy guarantees are encoded by a baseline Blackwell trade-off function f ∞ = T ⁡ ( P ∞ , Q ∞ ) f_{\infty}=T(P_{\infty},Q_{\infty}) involving a pair of distributions ( P ∞ , Q ∞ ) (P_{\infty},Q_{\infty}) 14 . The problem of ‘ choosing the right privacy metric in practice ’ 10 gives rise to a central question in this framework: what is a statistically appropriate baseline f ∞ f_{\infty} given some prior modeling assumptions ? The special case of Gaussian differential privacy (GDP) 14 showed that, under compositions of nearly perfect DP mechanisms, these baseline trade-off functions exhibit a central limit behavior: f n = T ⁡ ( P n ⊗ n , Q n ⊗ n ) → f ∞ = T ⁡ ( P ∞ , Q ∞ ) f_{n}=T(P_{n}^{\otimes n},Q_{n}^{\otimes n})\to f_{\infty}=T(P_{\infty},Q_{\infty}) , where the limiting baseline trade-off function f ∞ f_{\infty} involves two shifted Gaussians P ∞ , Q ∞ P_{\infty},Q_{\infty} . Inspired by Le Cam’s theory of limits of statistical experiments 34 , we answer this question in full generality in its natural infinitely divisible setting . We show that the sequence of composition experiments ( P n ⊗ n , Q n ⊗ n ) (P_{n}^{\otimes n},Q_{n}^{\otimes n}) ‘ converge ’ in general to a binary limit experiment ( P ∞ , Q ∞ ) (P_{\infty},Q_{\infty}) whose log-likelihood ratio log ⁡ d ​ Q ∞ d ​ P ∞ \log\frac{dQ_{\infty}}{dP_{\infty}} is an infinitely divisible distribution under the limiting null P ∞ P_{\infty} . So, any such limiting trade-off function f ∞ f_{\infty} involves an infinitely divisible law P ∞ P_{\infty} characterized by its Lévy–Khintchine triplet, and its Esscher tilt d ​ Q ∞ ​ ( x ) = e x ​ d ​ P ∞ ​ ( x ) dQ_{\infty}(x)=e^{x}dP_{\infty}(x) . As a consequence, we determine all the limiting baseline trade-off functions f ∞ f_{\infty} under the composition of nearly perfect differentially private procedures. Our framework recovers GDP as the purely Gaussian case, yields explicit many non-Gaussian limits including Poisson, and many others. Consequently, we positively resolve the s 2 = 2 ​ k s^{2}=2k conjecture proposed in the GDP paper 14 , which curiously observed that in the Gaussian examples P ∞ ​ = 𝑑 − k + | s | ​ Z P_{\infty}\overset{d}{=}-k+|s|Z with Z ∼ N ⁡ ( 0 , 1 ) Z\sim N(0,1) , we always have s 2 = 2 ​ k s^{2}=2k . Introduction, previous work, and our contributions

Modern data analysis routinely draws on information about people: their location traces, search histories, media consumption, social graphs, alongside long-standing, highly sensitive records from hospitals, schools, and national censuses. This necessarily demands a framework of data analysis that preserves the privacy of individuals. Early

Differential privacy (DP) provides exactly that: a principled, mathematically verifiable guarantee against disclosure risks that persists under arbitrary post-processing and composition. Since its introduction, differential privacy has grown into a practical standard, with widespread industrial adoption

Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we introduce the hypothesis testing-based framework of infinitely divisible privacy
6
, encompassing both the Gaussian differential privacy of
14
and the newly proposed Poisson differential privacy
9
, which are not just natural baselines for count statistics but also appear under repeated compositions of (nearly perfect) differentially private outputs. Along the way, we resolve the
s^{2}=2k
conjecture of
14
as a consequence of contiguity
3
.

In practice, the number of private operations made on a dataset is uncertain. We show that under randomization of the number of operations, the limiting trade-off can even
escape
the universal class of infinitely divisible trade-off functions
64
We illustrate this with a locally asymptotically mixed normal (LAMN) example (
66
), following the spirit of
34
.

Then, we construct a mechanism in Theorem
13
that
optimally
attains (asymmetric) Poisson differential privacy for real-valued statistics, including count statistics, thus going beyond standard additive-noise mechanisms. Afterwards, we demonstrate that Poisson baselines naturally appear for graph-based datasets, where released statistics are inherently discretized
20
.

Finally, inspired by a Bayesian perspective on DP
(
50
)
, we quantify exactly how trade-off functions degrade under
information coarsening
and derive a coarsened Neyman-Pearson lemma
22
with equality characterizations via conditional likelihood ratios
22
. We demonstrate this by explicitly working out the Gaussian case
2
that extends to symmetric-log concave family.

Several interesting directions follow, and many have been detailed in the remarks within the text. First, our algorithmic result suggests the construction of private mechanisms that are optimal with respect to a given natural, infinitely divisible trade-off curve. Second, it would be extremely interesting to illustrate the tightness of the proposed Poisson differential privacy framework on graph-based problems of modern interest

f_{X,\varepsilon_{1}}(x)\geq f_{X,\varepsilon_{2}}(x)\text{ for all }x\in[0,1].

(126)

Intuition and importance:
The proof of this lemma is along the same lines of the proof given in
14
[Lemma A.2, Proposition A.3]. Using log-concavity of the density of
X
and symmetry
X\overset{d}{=}-X
, there is an explicit description of
f_{X,\varepsilon}(x)=F(F^{-1}(1-x)-\varepsilon)
where
F(\cdot)
is the CDF of the random variable
X
,
F(t)=\mathbb{P}[X\leq t]
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
.

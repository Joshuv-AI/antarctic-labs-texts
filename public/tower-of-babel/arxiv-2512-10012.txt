Fuk–Nagaev inequality in smooth Banach spaces: Optimum bounds for distributions of heavy-tailed martingales
Mattes Mollenhauer, Christian Fiedler

Abstract
We derive a Fuk–Nagaev inequality for the maxima of norms of martingale sequences in smooth Banach spaces which allow for a finite number of higher conditional moments. The bound is obtained by combining an optimization approach for a Chernoff bound due to 22 with a classical bound for moment generating functions of smooth Banach space norms by 18 . Our result improves comparable infinite-dimensional bounds in the literature by removing unnecessary centering terms and giving precise constants. As an application, we propose a McDiarmid-type bound for vector-valued functions which allow for a uniform bound on their conditional higher moments. Keywords. 1
Introduction

The family of
Fuk–Nagaev inequalities

(
6
,
5
,
14
)
provides tail bounds
for independent sums and martingales under finite higher moment assumptions, thereby generalizing classical
results for bounded, subgaussian, and subexponential
random variables by Hoeffing, Azuma, Bennett, and Bernstein
(see e.g.
2
)
to distributions with heavier tails.

In particular, for centered independent real-valued random variables

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
satisfying the moment conditions

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{q}\leq C_{q}^{q}
for some
q>2
,
the sum
S_{n}=X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}
allows for the tail bound

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|S_{n}\right|>t\right]\leq\alpha_{q}\frac{C_{q}^{q}}{t^{q}}+\beta_{q}\exp\left(-\gamma_{q}\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)

for all
t>0
and constants

\alpha_{q},\beta_{q},\gamma_{q}>q
depending exclusively on
q
.
This result shows that
in a large deviation regime, the tails of independent sums are
dominated by a polynomial term with a decay rate
of their highest existing absolute moment.
In a small deviation regime however, they are
dominated by a subgaussian term, essentially
reflecting the central limit theorem.

Over the last years, different variations of bounds of the above type have been derived.
For example,
22
and
4
derive bounds for maxima
of real-valued martingales by giving explicit expressions for

\alpha_{q},\beta_{q},\gamma_{q}
under
corresponding conditional moment assumptions,

23
proves a bound
for dependent sums under mixing assumptions,
and
7
give a Fuk–Nagaev bound for
independent sums of matrices.
For independent sums of random variables in normed spaces,
26

proves a Fuk–Nagaev bound for the centered term

\lVert S_{n}\rVert-\mathbb{E}[\lVert S_{n}\rVert]

under higher moment assumptions for
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
q\geq 3
,
without providing explicit constants, but gives an inconsistent
proof as noted by
13
.

10
derives a bound
for centered empirical processes and provides explicit constants.

3
prove a result in Banach spaces
for
\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert S_{k}\rVert-(1+\eta)\mathbb{E}[\lVert S_{n}\rVert]

with
\eta>0
and undetermined constants.

It is known that for Banach spaces with
smooth norms

(
20
)

sharp analogues of classical results such as
the Hoeffding and Bernstein inequalities
can be given and the centering term
\mathbb{E}[\lVert S_{n}\rVert]

can in fact be removed

(
16
,
26
,
17
,
18
)
.
Based on this observation,

13
recently proved a Hilbert space
version of the Fuk–Nagaev
bound by
26
in which the centering
term is removed, but the constants remain undetermined
and the bound is only valid for
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
q\geq 3
.

In these notes, we derive a Fuk–Nagaev
bound for
uncentered
norms of
martingales in smooth Banach spaces that includes explicit
constants, reconciling results
for real-valued martingales with the theory of infinite-dimensional tail bounds.
Up to a dependence on the
modulus of smoothness of the Banach space,
we recover the optimal constants in the exponential term
derived by
22
from the real-valued
case. The general structure of the
dependence on the smoothness
appears to be sharp
(
18
)
.
Up to smoothness, we
also recover the constant of the polynomial
term derived by
22
and
note that the polynomial term itself is known to be
asymptotically
sharp
(
13
, e.g.)
.
As an application, we derive a McDiarmid-type inequality
for Banach-space valued functions of independent
random variables with higher conditional moments
based on the classical martingale technique. This inequality
extends unbounded versions of the McDiarmid inequality
with subgaussian and subexponential conditional distributions

(
8
,
11
)

to heavy-tailed distributions. We highlight that the assumptions
about the conditional higher moments are easily verified for
Hölder continuous functions.

2
Main Result

All random variables considered in these notes will be defined on a
common probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
We will not discuss the intricacies of measurability and integrability
of Banach-spaced random variables here and refer the reader to

9
for more details. We simply assume that
all random variables in question are
almost surely separably valued (or that the Banach space itself is separable) in order to avoid measurability issues.

Definition 2.1

(
(2,D)
-smoothness)
.

Let
D\geq 1
.
A Banach space
\mathcal{X}
equipped with the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is called

(2,D)
-smooth
, if

\lVert x+y\rVert^{2}+\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}\leq 2\lVert x\rVert^{2}+2D\lVert y\rVert^{2}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

We refer the reader to
20
,
21
for a general analysis
of smoothness in the context of Banach-space valued martingales
and to
25
for examples.
If
\mathcal{X}
is a Hilbert space, then it is
(2,1)
-smooth.
If
\mathcal{Y}
is a
(2,D)
-smooth Banach space,
then the Bochner–Lebesgue space

L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu;\mathcal{Y})
is
(2,D\sqrt{p-1})
-smooth
for all
p\geq 2
and any measure space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
.
In particular, the Lebesgue space

L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu;\mathbb{R})
is
(2,\sqrt{p-1})
-smooth, see also
18
.

Theorem 2.2

(Fuk–Nagaev inequality, confidence bound version)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\lVert\cdot\rVert)
be a
(2,D)
-smooth Banach space.
Let
(M_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
be a martingale in
\mathcal{X}
adapted to
a nondecreasing filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}

with
M_{0}=0
.
Define the conditional expectation
operators
\mathbb{E}_{i}[\,\cdot\,]:=\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,|\mathcal{F}_{i}]
for
i=0,\ldots,n

and martingale differences
\xi_{i}:=M_{i}-M_{i-1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Assume

\sigma^{2}:=\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{2}]<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad C^{q}_{q}:=\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{q}]<\infty

(2.1)

for some
q>2
.
Then for all
u\in(0,1)
we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{i\in\{1,\dots n\}}\lVert M_{n}\rVert\leq D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+c_{q,D}C_{q}\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}\right]\geq 1-u.

(2.2)

with the constant

c_{q,D}:=\frac{1}{2q}+\min\{1/q,1/5\}+1+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{D^{2}q}{3}.

(2.3)

Theorem

2.2
essentially extends the result of

22

for real martingales to the Banach space
case.
We prove
Theorem

2.2
in
Section

4
.

2.1
Rearranging to a tail bound

Directly inverting the upper bound
given in (
2.2
) in order to
derive a tail bound requires solving a
transcendental equation. Instead, we split
the sum and handle both terms separately.
Let
t>0
. We
want to find
u^{*}
such that simultaneously, we have

D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u^{*})}\leq\frac{t}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{q,D}C_{q}\left(\frac{2}{u^{*}}\right)^{1/q}\leq\frac{t}{2},

which by
Theorem

2.2
implies

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{i\in\{1,\dots n\}}\lVert M_{n}\rVert>t\right]\leq u^{*}
.
We can rearrange both terms and determine
the simultaneous conditions

u^{*}\geq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{8D^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad u^{*}\geq 2\left(\frac{2c_{q,D}C_{q}}{t}\right)^{q}.

(2.4)

We can now simply set
u^{*}
to be
the sum of the two terms on the right hand sides in
(
2.4
). We
have shown the following.

Corollary 2.3

(Fuk–Nagaev inequality, tail bound version)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

2.2
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{i\in\{1,\dots n\}}\lVert M_{n}\rVert>t\right]\leq 2\left(\frac{2c_{q,D}C_{q}}{t}\right)^{q}+2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{8D^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right).

for all
t>0
.

2.2
Independent sums

For convenience, we consider the special case that
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}

are independent, identically distributed and centered
random variables taking values
in
\mathcal{X}
and fulfill

\sigma^{2}:=\mathbb{E}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{2}]<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad C^{q}_{q}:=\mathbb{E}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{q}]<\infty,

instead of the assumption (
2.1
).
Then for all
u\in(0,1)
, we
straightforwardly obtain the bound

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{k=1,\ldots,n}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{i}\right\|\leq D\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/u)}{n}}+c_{q,D}C_{q}\left(\frac{2}{un^{q-1}}\right)^{1/q}\right]\geq 1-u

(2.5)

by applying
Theorem

2.2
to the martingale given by

M_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,n

with respect to the naturally induced filtration.
The bound (
4.8
)
generalizes and sharpens the Hilbert space bound
by
13

by giving precise constants and extending it to all real
q>2
.

3
A McDiarmid bound for heavy-tailed functions

As an application of
Theorem

2.2
,
we give a bound for Banach-space valued functions
of independent random variables that generalizes
the well-known bound by McDiarmid. Instead of the
classical bounded differences assumption
(
12
)
, we
only assume a uniform control of the individual higher moments
of the conditional distributions in each coordinate.
Similar inequalities have been derived by
8

and
11
under assumptions on
conditional Orlicz norms,
allowing to weaken the bounded difference
condition and giving exponential concentration.
In both cases, the authors require additional tools
to incorporate the componentwise Orlicz norms into the
final composite function.
In our case, we can directly work with the classical
martingale approach
and apply
Theorem

2.2
, as assumptions about finite
higher moments are
naturally compatible with this type of decomposition.
Moreover, our assumptions can be easily verified
for Lipschitz and Hölder continuous functions.

We consider independent random
variables
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}

defined on a common probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

taking values
in measurable spaces

(\mathcal{Z}_{1},\Xi_{1}),\dots,(\mathcal{Z}_{n},\Xi_{n})
.
Let
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}
denote the corresponding distributions
of
Z_{i},\dots,Z_{n}
.
We write
\mathcal{Z}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{Z}_{1}

for the product space and equip it with the product
\sigma
-field.
Furthermore, we introduce the abbreviations

\mathbf{z}:=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathcal{Z}
and

\mathbf{Z}:=(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n})
and
for a measurable map
f
on
\mathcal{Z}
, we introduce the

conditional versions
of
f
as

f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z}):=f(z_{1},\dots z_{i-1},Z_{i},z_{i+1},\dots,z_{n}),

which allow to conveniently investigate the
influence of
\mu_{i}
on the
i
-th coordinate of
f

when all other arguments are fixed.

Corollary 3.1

(McDiarmid inequality for heavy-tailed functions)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\lVert\cdot\rVert)
be a
(2,D)
-smooth Banach space.
Consider a measurable function
f:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
such that

\begin{split}\sigma^{2}&:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{E}[\lVert f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})-\mathbb{E}[f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})]\rVert^{2}]<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\\
C^{q}_{q}&:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{E}[\lVert f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})-\mathbb{E}[f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})]\rVert^{q}]<\infty\quad\text{for some }q>2.\end{split}

(3.1)

Then for all
u\in(0,1)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\left\|f(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})-\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})]\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+c_{q,D}C_{q}\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}\right]\geq 1-u,

where the constant
c_{q,D}
is given by (
2.3
).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
denote the

\sigma
-field induced by
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{i}
and
set
\mathcal{F}_{0}:=\{\emptyset,\Omega\}
.
We define the Doob martingale

M_{i}:=\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{Z})-\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{Z})]\mid\mathcal{F}_{i}],\quad i=1,\dots,n

and
M_{0}=0

and corresponding increments

\xi_{i}:=M_{i}-M_{i-1}=\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{Z})\mid\mathcal{F}_{i}]-\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{Z})\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]
.
Using a well-known representation of the conditional expectations based on Fubini’s Theorem and the Doob-Dynkin Lemma, we get

\displaystyle\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=\int f(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{i},z_{i+1},\dots z_{n})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{i+1}(z_{i+1})\otimes\dots\otimes\mu_{n}(z_{n})

\displaystyle\qquad-\int f(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{i-1},z_{i},\dots z_{n})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}(z_{i})\otimes\dots\otimes\mu_{n}(z_{n})

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int f(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{i},z_{i+1},\dots z_{n})-f(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{i-1},z_{i},\dots z_{n})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}(z_{i})\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{i+1}(z_{i+1})\otimes\dots\otimes\mu_{n}(z_{n}),

and we specifically notice that the two
evaluations of
f
in the parentheses only differ in
the
i
-th coordinate.
Based on the above
expression, we now find that for all
p\geq 1
,
we have

\displaystyle\esssup\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\esssup\int\left\|f(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{i-1},\hat{z}_{i},z_{i+1},\dots z_{n})-\int f(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{i-1},z_{i},\dots z_{n})\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}(\hat{z}_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{E}[\lVert f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})-\mathbb{E}[f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})]\rVert^{p}].

We now realize that under the conditional moment assumptions

\sigma^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{E}[\lVert f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})-\mathbb{E}[f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})]\rVert^{2}]\quad\text{and}\quad C^{q}_{q}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{E}[\lVert f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})-\mathbb{E}[f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})]\rVert^{q}],

the martingale
(M_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
satisfies
the assumptions of (
2.2
),
so the result follows by using
Theorem

2.2
.

3.1
Hölder continuous functions

We briefly give basic conditions under which the
conditional moment assumption
(
3.1
) can be verified.
We consider now a function
f:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{X}
,
where
\mathcal{X}
is a
(2,D)
-smooth Banach space
and
\mathcal{Z}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{Z}_{1}
is the product
of metric spaces

(\mathcal{Z}_{1},d_{1}),\dots,(\mathcal{Z}_{n},d_{n})

equipped with the metric

d:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
and corresponding Borel
\sigma
-field.
Assume that
f
satisfies the Hölder condition

\lVert f(\mathbf{z})-f(\hat{\mathbf{z}})\rVert\leq L\,d(\mathbf{z},\hat{\mathbf{z}})^{\alpha}\quad\forall\mathbf{z},\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbf{Z}

(3.2)

for some fixed
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
L\geq 0
.
We now find that for all
p\geq 1
,
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{E}[\lVert f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})-\mathbb{E}[f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})]\rVert^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Z},\hat{\mathbf{Z}}}[\lVert f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{Z})-f_{i}(\mathbf{z};\hat{\mathbf{Z}})\rVert^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq L^{p}\,\mathbb{E}_{Z_{i},\hat{Z}_{i}}[d_{i}(Z_{i},\hat{Z}_{i})^{\alpha p}],

where
\hat{\mathbf{Z}}
is an independent copy of
\mathbf{Z}
.
In the first step we use Jensen’s inequality
and in the second step we use (
3.2
)
together with the definition of the
metric
d
and the fact that the evaluations of
f_{i}

only differ in the
i
-th coordinate.
We can now define

\sigma^{2}:=L^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[d_{i}(Z_{i},\hat{Z}_{i})^{2\alpha}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad C^{q}_{q}:=L^{q}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[d_{i}(Z_{i},\hat{Z}_{i})^{\alpha q}\right]

(3.3)

and the calculation above verifies that the martingale
M_{n}

satisfies the assumptions of
Theorem

2.2

with corresponding constants given by
(
3.3
)
in case they are finite.
In fact, the quantities
\sigma^{2}
and
C^{q}_{q}
can be bounded
further depending on the setting.
If for example the spaces
\mathcal{Z}_{i}

are normed spaces with norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{i}
,
we may simplify the assumptions since
for every
p\geq 1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[d_{i}(Z_{i},\hat{Z}_{i})^{p}\right]\leq 2^{p}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert Z_{i}\rVert_{i}^{p}\right],

where we use the triangle inequality, independence,
and that
(x+y)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(x^{p}+y^{p})
for
x,y>0
.

Proof of
Theorem

2.2

The overall proof strategy follows
22
.
It derives the optimization of a Chernoff bound corresponding to a truncated martingale
in terms of its quantile function which is conveniently expressed as an inverse
Legendre transformation.

The crucial step that allows to transfer the original proof by
22

to the Banach space setting is a bound on the cumulant generating function of the norm of vector-valued martingales
which we obtain by reformulating the classic results by
18
.
This bound is structurally identical to the bound of the cumulant
generating function supplied by
(
22
, Lemma 3.4)
,
allowing to perform similar optimization steps.
This extends the standard Chernoff bound optimization arguments
Hoeffding’s and Bernstein’s inequalities
in Banach spaces by
18
to the Fuk–Nagaev inequality.

Let
X
be a real-valued random variable. We define
the
quantile function
1
1

1

With this definition,
Q_{X}(u)
is the largest
1-u
quantile of

Q_{X}(u):=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid\mathbb{P}[X>t]<u\},\quad u\in(0,1].

Note that

.
Furthermore, if

.
We additionally define the nonincreasing

Q^{1}_{X}(u):=\frac{1}{u}\int^{u}_{0}Q_{X}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\mathbb{E}[X\mid X\geq Q(u)],\quad u\in(0,1]

4.1
Approximation by truncated martingale

We realize that without loss of generality,
we can consider the simplified assumption

\sigma^{2}:=\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{2}]<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{q}]\leq 1.

(4.1)

The general case as in
Theorem

2.2
follows by considering the
scaled martingale
M_{n}/C_{q}
.

For a threshold
L>0
, define the
level-

truncations
of the random variables

as
\tilde{\xi_{i}}:=\mathbbm{1}_{\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert\leq L}\xi_{i}
.
The corresponding truncated martingale is given by
\tilde{M_{n}}=\tilde{\xi_{1}}+\dots,\tilde{\xi_{n}}

Following

,
we perform the basic Chernoff argument
that allows to bound the tails of

We use the shorthand notation

M_{n}^{*}:=\max_{i\in\{1,\dots n\}}\lVert M_{n}\rVert

For all

\displaystyle Q_{M_{n}^{*}}(u)\leq Q_{\lVert M_{n}\rVert}^{1}(u)

\displaystyle\leq Q^{1}_{\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert+\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)

\displaystyle\leq Q^{1}_{\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)+Q^{1}_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)

\displaystyle\leq Q^{1}_{\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)+Q^{\infty}_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u),

where we used in turn

,

4.2
Bounding the approximation error

Let
u\in(0,1)
.
Following
22
, we can proceed to bound

\displaystyle Q^{1}_{\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{u}\mathbb{E}[\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert]

(
Lemma

A.2
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{u}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}-\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert]\right]

(triangle inequality)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{u}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{\infty}_{L}\mathbb{P}[\lVert\xi_{i}-\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert>s\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

(truncation & tail integration)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{uqL^{q-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{\infty}_{L}qs^{q-1}\mathbb{P}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert>s\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

(multiplication by

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{uqL^{q-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathbb{P}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{q}>s\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{uqL^{q-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{q}]\right]

(tail integration)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{uqL^{q-1}}\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{q}]

from which we finally obtain

Q^{1}_{\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)\leq\frac{1}{qu^{1/q}}

(4.3)

by

)

4.3
Bounding the truncated martingale

We now need to bound

We also need the following classical result which allows to
derive sharp bounds for
tail probabilities of martingales in smooth Banach spaces.

Lemma 4.1

(Exponential moment bound,
18
, proof of Theorem 3.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
(2,D)
-smooth Banach space.
Let
(\tilde{M}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
be a martingale in
\mathcal{X}
adapted to
the nondecreasing filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}

and set
\tilde{M}_{0}=0
.
Given any fixed
t>0
, consider the process
(G_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
defined by
G_{0}:=1

and

G_{i}:=\cosh(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{i}\rVert)/\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+e_{i}),\quad i\in\{1,\dots,n\}

with

e_{i}:=D^{2}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}\left[e^{t\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert}-1-t\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert\right].

Then
(G_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
is a nonnegative supermartingale.

Therefrom, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\cosh(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)/\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+e_{i})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[G_{n}]\leq\mathbb{E}[G_{0}]=1.

The term
\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+e_{i})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}
is deterministic,
implying

\mathbb{E}\left[\cosh(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)\right]\leq\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+e_{i})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})},

(4.4)

from which we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)]

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\mathbb{E}[\cosh(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)]\leq 2\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+e_{i})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\lVert e_{i}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})})\leq 2\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert e_{i}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\right),

(4.5)

where we use

\cosh(x)=(e^{x}+e^{-x})/2
, the bound from (
4.4
),
the submultiplicativity and triangle
inequality of the
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})
-norm
and
1+x\leq e^{x}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

We now note that
\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert\leq\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert
almost surely
implies that the assumptions (
4.1
) are also valid for the truncated random
random variables
\tilde{\xi}_{i}
, which means that we have

\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert^{2}]<\sigma^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert^{q}]\leq 1.

(4.6)

The following moment bound is precisely the assertion of

22
applied
to the real-valued random variables
\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert
.

Lemma 4.2

(Moment bound,
22
, Proposition 3.5)
.

Let
\tilde{\xi}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\xi}_{n}
be a finite sequence of random
variables in a normed space adapted to
the nondecreasing filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
satisfying
(
4.6
) and
\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert\leq L

almost surely. Then we have

\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert^{k}]\leq\sigma^{2(q-k)/(q-2)},\quad k\in[2,q]

as well as

\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert^{k}]\leq L^{k-q},\quad k\geq q.

With this result, we use the bound of the moment
generating function given by (
4.3
) to
the cumulant generating function bound

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\exp(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert e_{i}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle=D^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i-1}\left[e^{t\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert}-1-t\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert\right]\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq D^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left\|\mathbb{E}_{i-1}\left[\lVert\tilde{\xi}_{i}\rVert^{k}\right]\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq D^{2}\left(\underbrace{\sigma^{2}\frac{t^{2}}{2}}_{=:\ell_{0}(t)}+\underbrace{\sum_{2<k<q}\sigma^{2(q-k)/(q-2)}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}}_{=:\ell_{1}(t)}+\underbrace{L^{-q}\sum_{k\geq q}\frac{L^{k}t^{k}}{k!}}_{=:\ell_{2}(t)}\right)

(4.7)

for all
t>0
.

4.4
Chernoff bound optimization

We proceed to bound
Q^{\infty}_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)

in terms of the
inverse Legendre transform
of
the cumulant generating function given by (
4.7
).
For this, we closely follow the arguments from the proof of
(
22
, Theorem 3.1(a))
.

Definition 4.3

(Inverse Legendre transform)
.

For a convex function
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

with
\psi(0)=0
,
we define the
inverse Legendre transform

as

[\mathcal{T}\psi](x)=\inf_{t>0}\{t^{-1}(\psi(t)+x)\},\quad x\in[0,\infty].

We refer to
23

for some background on the inverse Legendre transform
in the context of the classical Chernoff bound.

We define

\ell(t):=\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\exp(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)]\right)
.
We have

\displaystyle Q_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}^{\infty}(u)

\displaystyle=\inf_{t\in(0,\infty)}t^{-1}\,\log\left(\mathbb{E}[\exp(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)/u]\right)

(Definition of
Q_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}^{\infty}(u)
)

\displaystyle=\inf_{t\in(0,\infty)}t^{-1}\,\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\exp(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)/(u/2)]\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{t\in(0,\infty)}\frac{\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\exp(t\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert)]\right)+\log(2/u)}{t}

(
\log(ab)=\log(a)+\log(b)
)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}[\ell](\log(2/u))

(Definition of
\mathcal{T}
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x})

\displaystyle\text{(Monotonicity of $\mathcal{T}$ and \eqref{eq:cgf_bound})},

where we defined
\hat{x}=\log(2/u)
for brevity and

\ell_{0},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
are defined in (
4.7
).
Using the subadditivity of
\mathcal{T}
in its functional argument
(
22
, Proposition 2.5(i))
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{0}](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x})\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{2}](\hat{x}),

and hence

\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x})\leq\min\left\{\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{0}](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x}),\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{2}](\hat{x})\right\}.

We now proceed by bounding the individual terms occurring in the right hand side.

Step 1: Bounding
\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{2}](\hat{x})
.

We define

\psi_{q}(t):=\sum_{k\geq q}\frac{t^{k}}{k!},

so we may reformulate

\ell_{2}(t)=L^{-q}\sum_{k\geq q}\frac{L^{k}t^{k}}{k!}=L^{-q}\psi_{q}(Lt).

We now get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{2}](\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\inf_{t>0}\frac{D^{2}\ell_{2}(t)+\hat{x}}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D^{2}\ell_{2}(\hat{x}/L)+\hat{x}}{\hat{x}/L}

(Choose
t=\hat{x}/L
)

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{\hat{x}}\left(D^{2}L^{-q}\psi_{q}(L\cdot\hat{x}/L)+\hat{x}\right)

\displaystyle=D^{2}L^{1-q}\hat{x}^{-1}\psi_{q}(\hat{x})+L

\displaystyle\leq D^{2}L^{1-q}e^{\hat{x}}\min\{1/q,1/5\}+L

(
22
, Lemma 3.6
)

\displaystyle=\left(D^{2}L^{-q}e^{\hat{x}}\min\{1/q,1/5\}+1\right)\cdot L.

We now choose the specific truncation level
L=e^{\hat{x}/q}
.
With this choice of
L
we get

\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{2}](\hat{x})\leq\left(D^{2}L^{-q}e^{\hat{x}}\min\{1/q,1/5\}+1\right)\cdot L=(1+D^{2}\min\{1/q,1/5\})\cdot L=\alpha_{q,D}L,

where we define
\alpha_{q,D}:=D^{2}\min\{1/q,1/5\}+1
.

Step 2: Bounding
\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{0}](\hat{x})
.

Using the definition of
\ell_{0}
, we have

\mathcal{T}[\ell_{0}](\hat{x})=\inf_{t>0}\frac{D^{2}\ell_{0}(t)+\hat{x}}{t}=\inf_{t>0}\frac{D^{2}\sigma^{2}\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\hat{x}}{t}.

Define
f(t)=\frac{\sigma^{2}\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\hat{x}}{t}
. An elementary calculation
(setting
f^{\prime}(t)=0
and solving for
t
) shows that this function is minimized for
t=2\hat{x}/(D^{2}\sigma^{2})
with value
\sqrt{2\hat{x}}D\sigma
, so we get

\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{0}](\hat{x})=\sqrt{2\hat{x}}D\sigma.

Step 3: Bound for
2<q\leq 3
.

Observe that for
q\leq 3
, we have
\ell_{1}=0
, so

\displaystyle Q_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}^{\infty}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\min\{\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{0}](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x}),\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{2}](\hat{x})\}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{0}](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{2}](\hat{x})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{2\hat{x}}D\sigma+\alpha_{q,D}L\right).

From now on, we assume that
q>3
.

Step 4: Bound for
\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x})
.

Let
q>3
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\inf_{t>0}\frac{D^{2}\ell_{1}(t)+D^{2}\ell_{2}(t)+\hat{x}}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{\hat{x}}(D^{2}\ell_{1}(\hat{x}/L)+D^{2}\ell_{2}(\hat{x}/L)+\hat{x})

(Choose
t=\hat{x}/L
)

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{\hat{x}}\cdot(D^{2}\ell_{2}(\hat{x}/L)+\hat{x})+\frac{L}{\hat{x}}D^{2}\ell_{1}(\hat{x}/L)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{q,D}L+\frac{L}{\hat{x}}D^{2}\ell_{1}(\hat{x}/L),

where we bound the term involving
\ell_{2}
with the
argument from Step 1.
It remains to bound
\frac{L}{\hat{x}}D^{2}\ell_{1}(\hat{x}/L)
.
We recall from Step 1 that we chose
L=e^{\hat{x}/q}
, so
\hat{x}/L=\hat{x}e^{-\hat{x}/q}
.
We have

\frac{\hat{x}}{qL}=\frac{\hat{x}}{q}e^{-\hat{x}/q}\leq\sup_{s>0}se^{-s}=\frac{1}{e}

and hence

\frac{\hat{x}}{L}\leq\frac{q}{e}.

Furthermore,

t^{-2}\ell_{1}(t)=t^{-2}\sum_{2<k<q}\sigma^{\frac{2(q-k)}{q-2}}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{2<k<q}\sigma^{\frac{2(q-k)}{q-2}}\frac{t^{k-2}}{k!}

shows that
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\ni t\mapsto t^{-2}\ell_{1}(t)
is increasing,
so we get

\left(\frac{\hat{x}}{L}\right)^{-1}\ell_{1}(\hat{x}/L)=\frac{\hat{x}}{L}\left(\frac{\hat{x}}{L}\right)^{-2}\ell_{1}(\hat{x}/L)\leq\frac{\hat{x}}{L}\left(\frac{q}{e}\right)^{-2}\ell_{1}(q/e).

This allows us to proceed by bounding

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{q,D}L+D^{2}\frac{L}{\hat{x}}\ell_{1}(\hat{x}/L)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{q,D}L+D^{2}\frac{\hat{x}}{L}\left(\frac{q}{e}\right)^{-2}\ell_{1}(q/e)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{q,D}L+D^{2}\frac{\hat{x}e}{q}\frac{e}{q}\frac{1}{L}\ell_{1}(q/e)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{q,D}L+D^{2}\frac{\hat{x}e}{3}\frac{e}{qL}\ell_{1}(q/e)

(
q>3
)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{q,D}L+D^{2}\frac{\hat{x}e}{3}\ell_{1}(q/e).

(
q>3
,
L\geq 1
,
e<3
)

Step 5: Bound on
\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})](\hat{x})
.

We first observe that

\sigma^{\frac{2(q-k)}{q-2}}=\sigma^{\frac{2(2-k)}{q-2}}\sigma^{\frac{2(q-2)}{q-2}}=\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\right)^{k-2}\sigma^{2}.

For
t>0
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\ell_{0}(t)+\ell_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\sum_{2<k<q}\sigma^{\frac{2(q-k)}{q-2}}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\leq k<q}\sigma^{\frac{2(q-k)}{q-2}}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=t^{2}\sum_{2\leq k<q}\sigma^{\frac{2(q-k)}{q-2}}\frac{t^{k-2}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}t^{2}\sum_{2\leq k<q}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\right)^{k-2}\frac{t^{k-2}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}t^{2}\sum_{k\geq 2}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}t\right)^{k-2}\frac{1}{k!}

and since we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 2}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}t\right)^{k-2}\frac{1}{k!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{k\geq 3}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}t\right)^{k-2}\frac{1}{k!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{k\geq 3}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}t\right)^{k-2}\frac{1}{2\cdot 3\cdot\ldots\cdot k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{k\geq 3}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}t\right)^{k-2}\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3^{k-2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 3}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\frac{t}{3}\right)^{k-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 2}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\frac{t}{3}\right)^{k-2},

we end up with the bound

\ell_{0}(t)+\ell_{1}(t)\leq\sigma^{2}t^{2}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 2}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\frac{t}{3}\right)^{k-2}.

Note that we arrived at a geometric series, so if

\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\tfrac{t}{3}<1
, we find that

\ell_{0}(t)+\ell_{1}(t)\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}t^{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\frac{t}{3}}.

Based on this insight, we can now continue with

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})](\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\inf_{t>0}\frac{D^{2}(\ell_{0}(t)+\ell_{1}(t))+\hat{x}}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}t>0\\
\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\tfrac{t}{3}<1\end{subarray}}\frac{D^{2}(\ell_{0}(t)+\ell_{1}(t))+\hat{x}}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}t>0\\
\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\tfrac{t}{3}<1\end{subarray}}\frac{D^{2}\sigma^{2}t^{2}}{2(1-\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\frac{t}{3})t}+\frac{\hat{x}}{t}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}t>0\\
\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\tfrac{t}{3}<1\end{subarray}}\frac{D^{2}\sigma^{2}t}{2(1-\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\frac{t}{3})}+\frac{\hat{x}}{t}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\hat{x}}+\sqrt{2\hat{x}D^{2}\sigma^{2}},

where in the last step we used
Lemma

B.2
with

c=\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}/3
,
v=D^{2}\sigma^{2}
, and
x=\hat{x}
.

Step 6: Final bound for
q>3
.

Combining Steps 2, 4, 5, and 1, we have

\displaystyle Q_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}^{\infty}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{0}](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})](\hat{x}),\mathcal{T}[D^{2}(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})](\hat{x})+\mathcal{T}[D^{2}\ell_{2}](\hat{x})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{D\sigma\sqrt{2\hat{x}}+\alpha_{q,D}L+D^{2}\frac{\hat{x}e}{3}\ell_{1}(q/e),\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\frac{\hat{x}}{3}+D\sigma\sqrt{2\hat{x}}+\alpha_{q,D}L\right\}

\displaystyle=D\sigma\sqrt{2\hat{x}}+\alpha_{q,D}L+\frac{\hat{x}}{3}\min\left\{D^{2}e\ell_{1}(q/e),\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\right\}.

Let us turn to bounding
\min\{e\ell_{1}(q/e),\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\}
, for which we will use a case distinction.

Case 1:
\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\leq e
.

In this case, immediately we have

\displaystyle\min\{D^{2}e\ell_{1}(q/e),\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\}\leq\min\{e\ell_{1}(q/e),e\}\leq e\leq D^{2}e.

Case 2:
\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}>e
.

Note that this is equivalent to

\sigma^{\frac{2}{q-2}}<1/e
, so for all
k\in(2,q)

we have

\displaystyle\left(\sigma^{\frac{2}{q-2}}\right)^{q-k}=\sigma^{\frac{2(q-k)}{q-2}}\leq(1/e)^{q-k}=e^{k-q}.

We can use this to show

\displaystyle\ell_{1}(q/e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{2<k<q}\sigma^{2(q-k)/(q-2)}\frac{(q/e)^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-q}\sum_{2<k<q}\frac{q^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-q}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{q^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=e^{-q}e^{q}=1,

where we used in the first step that

\displaystyle\sigma^{2(q-k)/(q-2)}(q/e)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2(q-k)/(q-2)}e^{-k}q^{k}

\displaystyle\leq e^{k-q}e^{-k}q^{k}

\displaystyle=e^{-q}q^{k}.

We hence find that

\displaystyle\min\{D^{2}e\ell_{1}(q/e),\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\}\leq\min\{D^{2}e,\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\}\leq D^{2}e.

Altogether, we have

\displaystyle Q_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}^{\infty}(u)

\displaystyle\leq D\sigma\sqrt{2\hat{x}}+\alpha_{q,D}L+\frac{\hat{x}}{3}\min\{D^{2}e\ell_{1}(q/e),\sigma^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}\}

\displaystyle\leq D\sigma\sqrt{2\hat{x}}+\alpha_{q,D}L+\frac{D^{2}e\hat{x}}{3}.

Step 7: Bound for all
q>2
.

Combining Step 3 (bound for
2<q\leq 3
) and and Step 6 (bound for
q>3
),
and using the definitions

\displaystyle\hat{x}

\displaystyle=\log(2/u),

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=e^{\hat{x}/q}=\exp(\log(2/u)/q)=(2/u)^{\frac{1}{q}},

we finally get

\displaystyle Q_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}^{\infty}(u)

\displaystyle\leq D\sigma\sqrt{2\hat{x}}+\alpha_{q,D}L+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{e\hat{x}}{3}

\displaystyle=D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+\alpha_{q,D}(2/u)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{D^{2}e\log(2/u)}{3}

Recall that we have

\displaystyle Q^{1}_{\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{uqL^{q-1}}\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{q}],

so (
4.1
) and
L=(2/u)^{\frac{1}{q}}
leads to

\displaystyle Q^{1}_{\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{uq\left((2/u)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{q-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{uq(2/u)^{1-1/q}}

\displaystyle=u^{-1}u^{\frac{q-1}{q}}2^{-\frac{q-1}{q}}=u^{-1/q}2^{1/q-1}.

Using the bound on
Q_{\tilde{M}_{n}^{*}}(u)
in (
4.1
), we then obtain

\displaystyle Q_{M_{n}^{*}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq Q^{1}_{\lVert M_{n}-\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)+Q^{\infty}_{\lVert\tilde{M}_{n}\rVert}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{uq(2/u)^{1-1/q}}+D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+\alpha_{q,D}(2/u)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{D^{2}e\log(2/u)}{3}

\displaystyle=D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+\left(\frac{1}{2q}+\alpha_{q,D}\right)\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{D^{2}e\log(2/u)}{3}

\displaystyle\leq D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+\left(\frac{1}{2q}+\alpha_{q,D}\right)\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{D^{2}q}{3}\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}

(Lemma
B.1
)

\displaystyle=D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+\left(\frac{1}{2q}+\alpha_{q,D}+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{D^{2}q}{3}\right)\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+c_{q,D}\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}

(4.8)

with the constant

\displaystyle c_{q,D}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2q}+\alpha_{q,D}+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{D^{2}q}{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2q}+\min\{1/q,1/5\}+1+\mathbbm{1}_{q>3}\frac{D^{2}q}{3}.

4.5
Assembling the final bound

Let
(M_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
be a martingale in
\mathcal{X}
adapted to
a nondecreasing filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}

with
M_{0}=0
,
and for the martingale differences
\xi_{i}:=M_{i}-M_{i-1}
we have

\sigma^{2}:=\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{2}]<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad C^{q}_{q}:=\esssup\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}[\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{q}]<\infty

for some
q>2
.
We can apply the bound (
4.8
)
to the martingale
(M_{i}/C_{q})_{0\leq i\leq n}
,
which fulfills the original normalized assumptions
(
4.1
)
with
\sigma^{2}
replaced by
\sigma^{2}/C_{q}^{2}
.
This leads to

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{i\in\{1,\dots n\}}\lVert M_{n}/C_{q}\rVert\leq D\sigma/C_{q}\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+c_{q,D}\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}\right]\geq 1-u

for all
u\in(0,1)
, so we finally obtain

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{i\in\{1,\dots n\}}\lVert M_{n}\rVert\leq D\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2/u)}+c_{q,D}C_{q}\left(\frac{2}{u}\right)^{1/q}\right]\geq 1-u,

completing the proof.

The functions

Remark A.6

(Chernoff bound)
.

The bound
Q_{X}(u)\leq Q_{X}^{\infty}(u)

contained in the statement of
Lemma

A.3

captures the usual Chernoff bound
performed to obtain a sharp tail bound
in terms of the quantile function.
In particular, we have
by Markov’s inequality,
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>0
we have

\mathbb{P}[X>s]\leq\mathbb{E}[\exp(tX)]\exp(-st).

Let now
u\in(0,1]
. For arbitrary, but fixed
t>0
we can now define
s=s(t)=t^{-1}\ln(\mathbb{E}[\exp(tX)]/u)
, leading to

\mathbb{P}[X>t^{-1}\ln(\mathbb{E}[\exp(tX)]/u)]\leq u,

and since
t>0
was arbitrary and probability measures are continuous from above, we finally get

\mathbb{P}[X>\inf_{t>0}t^{-1}\ln(\mathbb{E}[\exp(tX)]/u)]\leq u.

Using the definition of
Q_{X}^{\infty}
, we find that for all
u\in(0,1]
it holds that

\mathbb{P}[X>Q_{X}^{\infty}(u)]\leq u.

Lemma B.2

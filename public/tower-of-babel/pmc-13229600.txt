Navigating Anxiety Management in Myasthenia Gravis Crisis With Prolonged Corrected QT (QTc): A Case Report
Authors: Garrison Burky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Myasthenia gravis (MG) crisis presents significant challenges in medication management, as treatment must balance risks of respiratory depression, worsening neuromuscular transmission, and QT interval prolongation when psychiatric symptoms such as anxiety coexist with medical instability. Concurrent corrected QT (QTc) prolongation further constrains treatment selection by limiting the use of many commonly prescribed anxiolytic and psychotropic agents. We present the case of a 75-year-old male with MG crisis and a markedly prolonged QTc interval of 563 ms, requiring careful balancing of neuromuscular, respiratory, and cardiac risks. Sertraline was selected due to its relatively favorable cardiac profile and established efficacy in anxiety disorders. The patient tolerated treatment without worsening of neuromuscular symptoms, and improvement in anxiety symptoms and QTc interval was observed during hospitalization. These changes occurred in a closely monitored, multifactorial clinical setting that included discontinuation of QT-prolonging medications and overall medical stabilization; therefore, the observed improvements should be interpreted as temporal associations rather than evidence of a direct causal effect of sertraline.

Introduction

Anxiety management in patients with myasthenia gravis (MG) presents significant challenges due to overlapping risks related to neuromuscular weakness, respiratory compromise, and cardiac conduction abnormalities [1]. MG is an autoimmune disorder characterized by impaired neuromuscular transmission, and patients with advanced disease are particularly vulnerable to medications that may exacerbate weakness or depress respiratory function. At the same time, many commonly used anxiolytic and psychotropic agents are associated with QT interval prolongation, which increases the risk of potentially life-threatening arrhythmias, such as torsades de pointes [2,3].

The coexistence of MG crisis and prolonged QTc creates a complex therapeutic dilemma, as pharmacologic options must be balanced across competing neuromuscular, respiratory, and cardiac risks. Agents effective for anxiety may exacerbate neuromuscular weakness, impair respiratory function, or prolong cardiac repolarization [4,5]. Despite these overlapping constraints, there is limited guidance specifically addressing safe and effective selection of anxiolytic or antidepressant therapies in patients with concurrent MG crisis and QTc prolongation [3].

We present a case of anxiety management in a patient with MG crisis and marked QTc prolongation, highlighting a practical approach to navigating these competing risks.

This case report was previously presented as a poster at the 2026 OPPA Annual Psychiatric Update Conference on April 18, 2026.

Case presentation

A 75-year-old male with a history of MG and generalized anxiety disorder was transferred from an extended care facility to the emergency department with altered mental status and generalized weakness one day after discharge from a prior hospitalization for urinary tract infection. Emergency medical services documented multiple runs of ventricular tachycardia and supraventricular tachycardia; on arrival, the patient was noted to be in supraventricular tachycardia, which spontaneously converted to sinus rhythm. Blood pressure remained stable throughout. He was admitted to the intensive care unit for management of myasthenic crisis and acute hypercapnic respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation. Treatment included intravenous immunoglobulin, intravenous corticosteroids, and continuation of home immunosuppressive therapy with azathioprine and pyridostigmine.

Electrocardiographic evaluation demonstrated a prolonged corrected QT (QTc) interval of 563 ms on hospital day two, with associated ventricular ectopy noted on telemetry (Figure 1). Prior to admission, the patient had been prescribed escitalopram and as-needed hydroxyzine for anxiety, which were continued on admission from the extended care facility. Following identification of significant QTc prolongation, both medications were discontinued. Serial monitoring demonstrated improvement in QTc to 531 ms on hospital day three.

Consultation-liaison psychiatry was consulted on hospital day five to assist with anxiety management in the context of the MG crisis and elevated cardiac risk. Several commonly used anxiolytic agents were considered but avoided due to safety concerns. Benzodiazepines were deferred due to the risk of respiratory depression in MG [5]. Beta blockers were avoided, given their potential to exacerbate neuromuscular weakness [4]. Antipsychotics with anxiolytic properties, such as quetiapine, were not utilized due to their potential to worsen MG symptoms [5]. Many antidepressants were also considered cautiously, given their known effects on cardiac conduction [2,3]. The patient had previously trialed buspirone without clinical benefit.

After interdisciplinary discussion, sertraline was initiated at 25 mg daily on hospital day five due to its relatively favorable cardiac profile and established efficacy in anxiety disorders [6,7]. The patient tolerated the medication without adverse effects. Subsequent electrocardiographic monitoring demonstrated improvement in QTc, measuring 502 ms prior to discharge (Figure 2).

Twelve-lead electrocardiogram obtained on hospital day eight showing sinus rhythm with prolonged corrected QT (QTc) interval (502 ms) and heart rate of 62 bpm.

Laboratory evaluation on admission revealed normal electrolytes, including sodium of 137 mmol/L, potassium of 3.8 mmol/L, and chloride of 104 mmol/L. An anion gap of 14 and mild elevation in creatinine to 1.4 mg/dL were noted. Repeat laboratory studies later in the hospitalization demonstrated normalization of metabolic parameters.

The patient's anxiety symptoms improved during hospitalization based on clinical assessment and patient report, as standardized rating scales were not utilized in the acute inpatient setting. There was no evidence of worsening neuromuscular weakness. The patient was discharged on hospital day eight following stabilization of respiratory status, with instructions to continue sertraline and follow up as an outpatient.

Discussion

Management of anxiety in patients with MG crisis presents a clinically complex scenario due to the intersection of three competing physiologic risks: respiratory compromise, neuromuscular weakness, and cardiac repolarization abnormalities. In the presence of significant QTc prolongation, treatment selection becomes further constrained, as many commonly used anxiolytic and psychotropic agents may increase the risk of torsades de pointes while also affecting neuromuscular transmission. In this case, treatment decisions required simultaneous consideration of these overlapping risk domains.

The pharmacologic constraints in this setting can be understood mechanistically. Benzodiazepines enhance GABAergic inhibition and may suppress central respiratory drive, posing a risk in patients with compromised neuromuscular function [5]. Beta blockers may worsen neuromuscular transmission through effects on presynaptic neurotransmitter release and postsynaptic receptor function in MG [4]. Antipsychotic agents, including quetiapine, have been associated with impairment of neuromuscular transmission and worsening of MG symptoms [5]. In addition, several antidepressants and antihistamines can prolong cardiac repolarization through blockade of cardiac potassium channels, thereby increasing QT interval duration and arrhythmic risk [2,3]. These overlapping mechanisms substantially narrow the range of safe therapeutic options.

While selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) as a class have variable effects on the QTc interval, sertraline has generally demonstrated minimal clinically significant QT prolongation at standard doses [6,7]. In this case, the patient tolerated sertraline without worsening MG symptoms, and an improvement in the QTc interval was observed during hospitalization. However, these changes occurred in a closely monitored, multifactorial clinical setting that included discontinuation of QT-prolonging medications and overall medical stabilization. As such, the observed QTc improvement should be interpreted as a temporal association rather than evidence of a direct causal effect of sertraline.

Alternative options such as mirtazapine may also be considered in similar scenarios, though available data are limited. Potential benefits must be weighed against risks of sedation and other adverse effects, particularly in medically fragile patients. Buspirone, while often used for anxiety, had previously been ineffective in this patient.

This case underscores the importance of individualized psychopharmacologic decision-making in patients with concurrent neuromuscular and cardiac vulnerabilities. It also highlights the role of consultation-liaison psychiatry in integrating competing physiologic risks to guide safe and effective treatment selection. A key limitation of this report is that it reflects a single patient experience, limiting generalizability. Further research is needed to establish evidence-based guidance for managing anxiety in patients with MG and concurrent QTc prolongation.

Conclusions

This case highlights the complexity of managing anxiety in patients with MG crisis and concurrent QTc prolongation, where standard pharmacologic options are limited by overlapping neuromuscular, respiratory, and cardiac risks. In this patient, sertraline was tolerated without evidence of worsening neuromuscular function, and improvement in anxiety symptoms and QTc interval was observed during a closely monitored, multifactorial inpatient course that included discontinuation of QT-prolonging medications and overall clinical stabilization. As such, these findings should be interpreted as temporal associations rather than evidence of causality.

This case underscores the importance of individualized psychopharmacologic decision-making in medically complex patients and highlights the role of consultation-liaison psychiatry in navigating competing physiologic risks. Given the limitations of a single case, these observations are hypothesis-generating and require further study before broader clinical application.

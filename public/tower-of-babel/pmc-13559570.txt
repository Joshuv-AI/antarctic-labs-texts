Trastuzumab deruxtecan plus pertuzumab in first-line HER2-positive advanced/metastatic breast cancer
Authors: Sara M. Tolaney, Sibylle Loibl
Published: 2026

In their commentary (1), Davenport and Gatti-Mays consider the clinical impact of first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) plus pertuzumab in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive metastatic breast cancer following the DESTINY-Breast09 trial and subsequent US approval (2,3). In an interim analysis of DESTINY-Breast09, T-DXd plus pertuzumab demonstrated a clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvement in progression-free survival (PFS; median: 40.7 months) compared with the long-established regimen of taxane plus trastuzumab plus pertuzumab [THP; median PFS: 26.9 months (hazard ratio =0.56, P<0.00001)] (3).

The authors discuss the addition of oral therapy to HER2-targeted treatment in this setting, comparing DESTINY-Breast09 with the PATINA trial, in which patients without disease progression after induction THP received endocrine therapy with/without palbociclib, added to trastuzumab-pertuzumab (HP) (4). PATINA included only patients with hormone receptor (HR)-positive disease (4), and as approximately 30% of patients in the metastatic setting have HR-negative tumors (5), it is notable that T-DXd plus pertuzumab demonstrated superior efficacy to THP regardless of HR status and without mandatory endocrine therapy for those with HR-positive disease (3). Another key difference between the trials was the requirement for patients in PATINA to have no disease progression after completing 4–8 cycles of taxane induction (4), thereby selecting for patients with a better prognosis. By comparison, T-DXd plus pertuzumab demonstrated a clinical benefit over THP in DESTINY-Breast09 regardless of prognostic factors, such as recurrent disease and PIK3CA mutation status (3,6). HER2CLIMB-05—exploring HP with/without tucatinib—is another trial evaluating maintenance therapy (7). As in PATINA, patients were required to have no disease progression after taxane induction. As noted, it is also difficult to compare HER2CLIMB-05 and DESTINY-Breast09 given that the measurement of PFS in the former excluded the period of induction chemotherapy (7). For an unselected population, a longer PFS was reported for patients receiving T-DXd plus pertuzumab than with any other regimen explored in a Phase 3 trial in this setting (3).

Davenport and Gatti-Mays note that HP maintenance is well tolerated after taxane discontinuation and imply that this may not be the case with long-term T-DXd plus pertuzumab treatment; however, the rates quoted for adverse events (AEs) leading to treatment discontinuation (68.8% vs. 49.0%) are in fact for treatment interruptions (1,3). Drug-related AEs leading to discontinuation occurred in a lower proportion of patients receiving T-DXd plus pertuzumab (19.2%) than with THP (27.2%), which is notable considering the longer duration of treatment (median: 21.7 vs. 16.9 months, respectively); exposure-adjusted incidence rates per patient-year were 0.11 and 0.18, respectively (3). Additionally, overall treatment discontinuation was lower for T-DXd in the T-DXd plus pertuzumab arm vs. trastuzumab in the THP arm (59.7% vs. 66.6%) (3). Finally, T-DXd plus pertuzumab and THP were comparable across several patient-reported quality-of-life measures after more than 18 months’ treatment (8), despite the short duration of taxane use (median: 4.4–5.5 months) (3).

Nausea and vomiting are known AEs with T-DXd (2), and clinicians should consider recommendations for antiemetic prophylaxis (use of which was low in DESTINY-Breast09) to support patients undergoing treatment, particularly in early cycles (2,3). Interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis remains an important AE associated with T-DXd for which patients should be monitored appropriately (2,3); most events in the T-DXd plus pertuzumab arm in DESTINY-Breast09 were grade 1 or 2 (3), so the need for routine monitoring should be balanced against the relatively low rate of severe events and the clinically meaningful efficacy benefit.

The correspondence questions whether the DESTINY-Breast09 trial population is representative of patients seen in real-world practice. The median age of patients in the trial (54 years) was indeed younger than that reported for breast cancer diagnosis in the US (62 years) (3), which may be attributable to the high number of patients from Asia, where the age of breast cancer diagnosis is more than 10 years younger compared with Western populations (9). It is true that Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status was limited to 0 or 1 in DESTINY-Breast09 (3), although this is a common inclusion criterion of trials in this setting (4,7). Overall, DESTINY-Breast09 was designed to be broadly representative, and with 1,157 patients enrolled across the three treatment arms, the results are expected to be relevant to global clinical practice (3).

The authors consider where T-DXd plus pertuzumab should fit in the treatment paradigm, suggesting the regimen should be reserved for patients at higher risk of progression. Subgroup analyses of DESTINY-Breast09 suggest a consistent clinical benefit regardless of prognostic factors, such as brain metastases, as well as de novo/recurrent status, HR status, or PIK3CA mutation status, as already mentioned (3,6). An analysis of patients with varying disease burdens is ongoing. The authors refer to the promising efficacy of T-DXd in the context of brain metastases, as supported by the single-arm DESTINY-Breast12 trial (10). Central nervous system endpoints were exploratory in DESTINY-Breast09 and will be published in due course, along with planned biomarker analyses. Additional efficacy—mature overall survival and outcomes with T-DXd plus placebo—are also awaited to support clinicians and patients in their treatment decisions (3).

Finally, Davenport and Gatti-Mays suggest using T-DXd plus pertuzumab as an induction treatment before switching to maintenance HP. Currently, there are no clinical data supporting such an approach, although research is planned; DESTINY-Breast09 was designed to treat until progression or unacceptable toxicity (3). In the trial, T-DXd plus pertuzumab showed favorable responses, with near doubling of complete response rates (15.1% vs. 8.5% with THP) (3). An in-depth responder analysis recently presented at the 2026 American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) annual meeting showed that achieving a complete response, or even a deep partial response (defined as an ≥80% reduction in target lesion size), with T-DXd plus pertuzumab was associated with favorable PFS outcomes and sustained treatment duration (11). Responses deepened over time, with 80% of patients achieving maximal tumor reduction by 24 months, supporting the continuation of T-DXd plus pertuzumab to achieve optimal clinical benefit.

We acknowledge the points raised by Davenport and Gatti-Mays regarding the rapidly evolving treatment landscape in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer. Careful consideration of benefit vs. risk is especially important for new treatment strategies in the first-line setting. Ultimately, T-DXd plus pertuzumab has achieved the first efficacy improvement vs. first-line THP in over a decade and has the potential to provide a clinically meaningful benefit to patients. We, and the rest of the medical community, eagerly await additional results from DESTINY-Breast09 to further support and optimize use of this regimen in the real world.

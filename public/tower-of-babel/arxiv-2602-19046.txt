On the convergence of explicit formulas for L 2 L^{2} solutions to the Benjamin–Ono and continuum Calogero–Moser equations
Yvonne Alama Bronsard, Thierry Laurens, Yvonne Alama Bronsard, Thierry Laurens

Abstract
By developing discrete counterparts to recent advances in nonlinear integrability, and in particular to the discovery of explicit formulas, we design and analyze fully-discrete approximations to the Benjamin–Ono (BO) and continuum Calogero–Moser (CCM) equations on the torus. We build on the key observation that discretizing such explicit formulas yields schemes that are exact in time (requiring only spatial discretization) and have a computational cost independent of the final time T T . In this work, we first generalize the fully-discrete schemes of [ 2 ] to include numerical approximations with better structure preservation properties, including the conservation of mass and momentum in the case of the (BO) equation. Secondly, building on recent analyses of the corresponding Lax operators, we extend the convergence results to this class of schemes for rough solutions u ⁡ ( t ) u(t) merely belonging to L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{T}) for (BO) and L + 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{T}) for (CCM), the latter of which is precisely the scaling-critical regularity. Our main theorem states that the L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{T}) -norm of the error goes to zero as the truncation parameters go to infinity, uniformly on any bounded time interval [ − T , T ] [-T,T] . As an example, we apply our scheme to the (BO) equation with a square-wave initial profile, and obtain the first numerical evidence of the Talbot effect for (BO) supported by a rigorous convergence result.



Keywords:
Nonlinear integrability, Lax operators, low-regularity, structure-preserving schemes, error analysis, Talbot effect

In this work, instead of discretizing the underlying equations (

The huge advantage of working with these explicit formulas instead of the underlying equations lies in the key observation that discretizing such explicit formulas yields schemes that are exact in time (requiring only spatial discretization) and have a computational cost independent of the final time

1.2
Results

Our main result is the convergence for rough data

u_{0}\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{T})

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

denote the global solution to the (
BO
) or (
CCM
) equation with initial data

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
or
L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{T})

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<1
in the case of the focusing (

) equation. Denote by

the numerical scheme (
5

with
K

\big(n_{K}(k)\big)_{k\geq 0}
. If
n_{K}(k)\xrightarrow{K\to\infty}\infty
, for each
k\geq 0
,
then for any
T>0

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|u_{K}(t)-u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\xrightarrow{{K\to\infty}}0.

(4)

A direct corollary of the above theorem is the asymptotic convergence of the
L^{2}
norm of the numerical approximation to the exact solution.

Corollary 1.4
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
, we have

\|u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\xrightarrow{K\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}},

uniformly for
t\in[-T,T]
.

Before comparing our
L^{2}
convergence result with those in the literature, we start by discussing the statement of Theorem
1.3
and then illustrate it through the example of the Talbot effect.

In the above theorem, we only require simple convergence of the truncation parameters
K
and
(n_{K}(k))_{k\geq 0}
, without a prescribed order. This is the most general notion of convergence. We emphasize that it is natural to ask for the above condition on
K

(n_{K}(k))_{k\geq 0}
as we want the truncated operators to asymptotically converge to their continuous counterpart.

We contend that the mode of convergence in our result is the strongest one can expect for
L^{2}
data. First, since no additional Sobolev regularity is assumed, the best possible outcome is
L^{2}
-convergence as in (
4

Secondly, as the eigenvalues of the truncated Lax operator

are not equal to those of

e^{itL_{n}}f
will necessarily drift away from

that appear in the explicit formulas (
3

The proof of our main result relies upon a careful analysis of the Lax operators

and their corresponding unitary groups

by a truncation

[

37

below the maximal time of existence. However, they do not cover the case of
L^{2}
-solutions (
s=0
); see for example
[
48
]
for the KdV equation and
[
3
, Chapter 1, Section 3]
for an overview of related results.
To enable a uniform
L^{2}
-convergence result for
L^{2}
data we introduce completely different techniques based on compactness arguments and Lax operators, together with these new explicit formulas.

Remark 1.5

(Convergence of
u_{K}
for higher regularity data)
.

Let
u_{K}
be the scheme defined by (
5
) with
n(k)\geq cK
, for some constant
c>0
independent of
K
. By applying the same proof as in
[
2
]
with truncation parameter
\tilde{K}:=cK
we obtain their convergence result for this subclass
u_{K}
of schemes, namely that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq r\leq s
and
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
with
s>1
, there exists
C>0
such that

\|u_{K}(t)-u(t)\|_{H^{r}}\leq C(1+t)K^{-s+1+r}.

We note that the regularity condition
s>1
is natural, and is due to the structure of the explicit formula (
3
). Indeed, discretizing each exponential
e^{2itL}

induces an
L^{2}
error of order
K^{-s}
, yielding the rate
K^{-s+1}
when applied
\tilde{K}
time to control the error terms
(e^{2itL}S^{*})^{k}-(e^{2itL_{\tilde{K}}}S^{*})^{k}
over
{0\leq k<\tilde{K}}
.
As the error is measured either in
L^{2}
or in a stronger
H^{r}
norm, this explains why we need regularity at least
s>1
to prove convergence rates.

We note that the schemes satisfying condition (
13
), which preserve the
L^{2}
-norm on the discrete level, do not fit in the above case. Thereby, the only convergence result available for these schemes is the one presented here.

Section

2
Schemes based on the explicit formulas

2.1
Defining the class of schemes

The general form for the scheme
u_{K}
is given in Fourier space as follows.
Let
K\in\mathbb{N}
be the number of Fourier frequencies used in the discretization.
For each
K
, choose a sequence of truncation parameters
n(k)=n_{K}(k)\in\mathbb{N}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and set

\widehat{u_{K}}(t,k)=\langle u^{k},1\rangle\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq k<K,

(5)

where

u^{0}=\Pi_{n(0)}u_{0}
, and for
k\geq 1

u^{k}=\left(e^{it(I+2L_{n(k)})}S^{*}\right)u^{k-1}\ \text{with}\quad L_{j}=-i\partial_{x}-\Pi_{j}u_{0}\Pi_{j},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}

(6)

in the case of (
BO
), while for (
CCM
)

u^{k}=\left(e^{-it(I+2L_{n(k)})}S^{*}\right)u^{k-1}\ \text{with}\quad L_{j}=-i\partial_{x}\pm\Pi_{j}u_{0}\Pi_{j}\overline{u}_{0}\Pi_{j},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(7)

In the case of (
CCM
), by setting
\widehat{u_{K}}(t,k)=0
for
k<0
or
k\geq K
, the formula (
5
) defines an element of
L^{2}_{+}
, yielding our approximation of
u(t)
. For (
BO
), in order to ensure that
u_{K}
is real-valued we set

\widehat{u_{K}}(t,k)=\overline{\widehat{u_{K}}(t,-k)}\quad\text{for}\quad{-K}<k<0,

and then take
\widehat{u_{K}}(t,k)=0
for
|k|\geq K
.

Remark 2.1
.

The scheme (
5
) is independent of the values of
n(k)
for
k\geq K
, as the iterates
u^{k}
are only computed for
0\leq k<K
. Nevertheless, we define the scheme with the full sequence
(n(k))_{k\geq 0}
as it allows us to identify those which have better structure preserving properties, as further detailed below.

Remark 2.2
.

The matrix exponentials appearing in (
6
) and (
7
) are well defined for
u_{0}\in L^{2}
and
L^{2}_{+}
respectively. Indeed, we show in Section
4.2
that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
the Lax operators
L_{j}
appearing in (
6
) and (
7
) are semi-bounded self-adjoint operators on
H^{1}_{+}
and hence generate a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group
t\to e^{itL_{j}}
on
L^{2}_{+}
.

2.2
Structure-preserving properties and examples

Before giving examples of schemes belonging to this class (
5
), we discuss some preservation properties which the class inherits.
By considering the coefficient
\widehat{u_{K}}(t,0)
, we have that the schemes (
5
) preserve the average of
u_{0}
. This is a conserved quantity for both (
BO
) and (
CCM
); in the case of (
BO
) this corresponds to the mass.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
n(0)\geq 1
. Then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
the scheme
u_{K}
in (
5
) satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{K}(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{0}(x)\,dx.

The
L^{2}
norm is another conserved quantity that is common to both systems.
A second important feature of the above schemes (
5
) is that they have
L^{2}
norm bounded by that of the initial data, as expressed in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

Given
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\|\Pi u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\Pi_{n(0)}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

(8)

In particular, this implies that

\|u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

(9)

Proof.

Fix the initial data
u_{0}\in L^{2}
or
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{+}
, and let
u_{K}
denote the numerical scheme constructed in (
5
) from the recursive sequence
u^{k}
defined in (
6
) for (
BO
) and in (
7
) for (
CCM
).

As each
L_{n(k)}
is self-adjoint (see Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
below for details), it generates a one-parameter unitary group
t\mapsto e^{itL_{n(k)}}
. Consequently, for
k\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\|u^{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|e^{\pm it(I+2L_{n(k)})}S^{*}u^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|S^{*}u^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|u^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\big|\langle u^{k-1},1\rangle\big|^{2}.

By induction, we conclude

\displaystyle 0\leq\|u^{K}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\big|\langle u^{k},1\rangle\big|^{2}=\|\Pi_{n(0)}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\Pi u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(10)

In particular, using Proposition
2.3
we have

\|u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\Pi u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\overline{\Pi u_{K}(t)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\langle u_{K}(t),1\rangle^{2}\leq 2\|\Pi_{n(0)}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\langle u_{0},1\rangle^{2}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(11)

∎

The
L^{2}
-inequality (
8
) is a vital step necessary for going from weak to strong
L^{2}
convergence, and is hence essential for the convergence analysis presented in Section
4
.

We now give examples of various schemes belonging to the above class (
5
).

Example 2.5

(The linear case)
.

The simplest example consists in taking
n(0)=K
and
n(k)=0
, for
k\geq 1
. The scheme thereby obtained is given by

\widehat{u_{K}}(t,k)=\langle(e^{\alpha it(I-2i\partial_{x})}S^{*})^{k}\Pi_{K}u_{0},1\rangle,\quad 0\leq k<K,

(12)

with
\alpha=1
for (
BO
) and
\alpha=-1
for (
CCM
).
The above scheme approximates the linearized equation

\partial_{t}u=\alpha\partial_{x}|\partial_{x}|u.

Indeed, it is straightforward to verify that for
K=\infty
the above formula is equivalent to the linear flow
e^{\alpha t\partial_{x}|\partial_{x}|}u_{0}
.

Example 2.6

(The scheme of
[
2
]
)
.

By taking
n(k)=K
, for each
k\geq 0
, we recover the scheme recently obtained by Alama Bronsard–Chen–Dolbeault
[
2
]
.

Next, by taking a more subtle approximation of the Lax operator, and hence of
n(k)
, we can obtain schemes which preserve the
L^{2}
-norm of the truncated initial data.

Example 2.7

(Schemes preserving the discrete
L^{2}
-norm)
.

By taking

n(k)\leq K-k\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq k<K

(13)

we obtain new schemes which exactly preserve the
L^{2}
-norm on the discrete level. This is expressed in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
u_{K}
be any scheme defined by (
5
) satisfying assumption (
13
). This scheme satisfies

\|\Pi u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|\Pi_{n(0)}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

Proof.

The proof follows from that of Proposition
2.4
, by noticing that
u^{K}\equiv 0
and hence equality holds in (
10
). Indeed, in frequency variables, each
u^{k}
is of size
K-k
(see Remark
2.10
) and is supported between the frequencies
0
and
K-k-1
, and so in particular
u^{K}
must vanish everywhere. Hence, it follows that

\|\Pi u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2\|\Pi_{n(0)}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\langle u_{0},1\rangle^{2}=\|\Pi_{n(0)}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

∎

Remark 2.9
.

We cannot expect equality in (
9
), since the highest modes of
u_{0}
have been truncated. Consequently, the second estimate in (
11
) is necessarily an inequality. The strongest result one can hope for is that the
L^{2}
-norm of the truncated initial data is conserved in time, which is exactly what Proposition
2.8
guarantees.

Remark 2.10

(The size of the iterates
u^{k}
)
.

In frequency variables,
the iterates
u^{k}
in the numerical schemes (
6
) and (
7
) are
vectors
of size

m_{k}:=\max_{0\leq\ell\leq k}\{n(\ell)-(k-\ell)\},

(14)

which is bounded for
0\leq k<K
by
M=\max_{0\leq k<K}n(k)
.
Indeed,
u^{0}
is of size
n(0)
; after shifting,
S^{*}u^{0}
is of size
n(0)-1
; and when applying
e^{itL_{n(1)}}
,
u^{1}
is of size
\max\{n(0)-1,n(1)\}
, since the diagonal part
-i\partial_{x}
is not truncated by
\Pi_{j}
in (
6
) and (
7
). Thereby, (
14
) follows by induction.

Remark 2.11

(Truncated Lax operators and perturbations of
-i\partial_{x}
)
.

As discussed in Remark
2.10
, the scheme is implemented as a vector of size
M
, and the computation of
L_{n}
is equal to that of
\Pi_{M}L_{n}\Pi_{M}
. Nevertheless, for the analysis we do not apply any truncation
\Pi_{M}
on the first term
-i\partial_{x}
in the definition of the truncated Lax operators (
6
) and (
7
). Indeed, this is crucial in order to treat the Toeplitz term
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}
or
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}
as perturbations of
-i\partial_{x}
, and thereby to obtain the desired convergence result, see Section
4.2
. For this same reason, our convergence proof does not apply to the cubic Szegő equation: its Lax operator lacks the derivative term
-i\partial_{x}
, so the problem cannot be treated perturbatively.

2.3
Computational cost

The leading cost comes from computing the matrix exponentials in (
6
) and (
7
), approximating the flow of the Lax operator
e^{2itL}
.
Hence, the cost of the scheme (
5
) will necessarily depend on the choice of the truncation of the Lax operator, which is dictated by
n(k)
.

If no structure is imposed, and the
n(k)
are chosen arbitrarily, then the scheme is costly to implement and requires
O(K^{4})
operations. This is due to the fact that in order to compute the Fourier coefficients of the scheme (
5
), we compute
K-1
matrix exponentials of the form
e^{it(I+2L_{n(k)})}
, which each cost
\mathcal{O}(K^{3})
.

However, if structure is imposed, such as when taking
n(k)=K
, or the
L^{2}
-preserving scheme corresponding to
n(k)=K/2
for
0\leq k\leq K/2
and zero otherwise (which satisfies (
13
)),
then the matrix exponential
e^{it(I+2L_{n(k)})}
can be computed once in
\mathcal{O}(K^{3})
.
Indeed,
since the matrices are self-adjoint, they can be diagonalized as
P\Lambda P^{T}
, allowing us to compute
Pe^{it\Lambda}P^{T}
in
\mathcal{O}(K^{3})
operations. Moreover, as the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
of the diagonal matrix
\Lambda
are real-valued, we have
|e^{it\lambda_{j}}|=1
for all
t
, ensuring the stability of the method over time.
Once the matrix exponentials are computed, we can evaluate all the vectors in
\mathcal{O}(K)
matrix-vector multiplications, with a computational cost in
\mathcal{O}(K^{3})
once again.

We emphasize that what counts is the
balance
between the precision
\epsilon
and the computational cost
\mathcal{C}
of the method, and not solely one of these quantities. When considering the trade-off between
\epsilon
and
\mathcal{C}
, the schemes based upon explicit formulas outperform those previously established in the literature, as discussed in
[
2
, Section 4.2]
.

3
Fourier transforms, norms and resolvants

Our convention for the inner product on
L^{2}
is

\langle f,g\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f\overline{g}\,dx.

We then choose to define the Fourier transform as

\widehat{f}(k)=\langle f,e^{ikx}\rangle,\quad\text{so that}\quad f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{f}(k)e^{ikx}.

Plancherel’s identity then reads

\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}.

We define the operator norm as follows

\|A\|_{\textup{op}}:=\|A\|_{L^{2}_{+}\to L^{2}_{+}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1\\
f\in L^{2}_{+}\end{subarray}}\|Af\|_{L^{2}}.

We now recall the truncated Lax operators for (
BO
) and (
CCM
) on
L^{2}_{+}
, which we
formally
defined in (
6
) and (
7
) when writing the scheme.
For (
BO
), we let
u\in L^{2}
be real-valued initial data, and define

L_{n}=-i\partial_{x}-\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{\infty}=L=-i\partial_{x}-\Pi u.

(15)

For (
CCM
), we let
u\in L^{2}_{+}
and construct the truncated Lax operators

L_{n}=-i\partial_{x}\mp\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{\infty}=L=-i\partial_{x}\mp u\Pi\overline{u}.

(16)

In the next section, we will construct
L_{n}
as a perturbation of the free operator
L_{0}=-i\partial_{x}
on
L^{2}_{+}
, with domain
D(L_{0})=H^{1}_{+}
. We denote the resolvent of the latter by

R_{0}(\kappa)=(L_{0}+\kappa)^{-1},\quad\kappa\geq 1.

It will also be convenient to define norms adapted to this resolvent:

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\kappa}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(|k|+\kappa)^{2s}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}.

(17)

4
Proof of convergence

The goal of this section is to prove our main convergence result presented in Theorem
1.3
. The proof is given in Section
4.5
, where we show how strong convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
follows from combining weak convergence and Proposition
2.4
.
To prove weak convergence we rely on the result of Section
4.4
, which proves convergence of the unitary group
e^{itL_{n}}
associated to the truncated Lax operator
L_{n}
, uniformly on bounded time intervals
[-T,T]
and over compact subsets
\mathcal{F}
of
L^{2}_{+}
. To prove the latter, we show in Section
4.3
norm resolvent convergence of
L_{n}
, which in turn uses the operator bounds and equivalence of norms of Sections
4.1
and
4.2
.

4.1
Operator bounds

The following estimates will be used in the next sections to rigorously define the truncated Lax operators
L_{n}
in (
15
) and (
16
), construct their resolvents, and study their mapping properties. Our first two lemmas show that
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}
and
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}
are relatively bounded perturbations of
L_{0}
. In fact, these estimates are even more general, as they do not require the left-most truncation
\Pi_{n}
, and just consider
u\Pi_{n}u
and
u\Pi_{n}\bar{u}\Pi_{n}
, see also Remark
4.4
.

We start with the case of the (
BO
) equation.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
u\in L^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\Pi_{n}R_{0}(\kappa)\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{3}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}},

(18)

uniformly for
1\leq n\leq\infty
and
\kappa\geq 1
.

Proof.

For any
s>\frac{1}{2}
, the space
H^{s}
embeds into
L^{\infty}
. More precisely, for
g\in H^{s}_{\kappa}
, using Cauchy–Schwarz in Fourier variables we find

\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\widehat{g}\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2s+1}{2s-1}}\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\|g\|_{H^{s}_{\kappa}},\quad\kappa\geq 1.

(19)

Taking
s=1
in the above and recalling the
H^{s}_{\kappa}
-norm (
17
), we see that for all
1\leq n\leq\infty
and
\kappa\geq 1
,

\|u\Pi_{n}R_{0}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Pi_{n}R_{0}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{3}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{0}f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}}=\sqrt{3}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

This proves (
18
).
∎

We now show the analogue of the above lemma for the (
CCM
) equation.
Note that the Lax operator now contains two occurrences of
u
, so the elementary bound (
19
) is no longer sufficient to control both factors. Instead, we leverage the Fourier support of
u\in L^{2}_{+}
. Moreover, the operator bound in Lemma
4.2
does not provide an explicit decay rate in
\kappa
; therefore, a separate argument is required in the case of (
CCM
) to establish decay as
\kappa\to\infty
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
u\in L^{2}_{+}
,
1\leq n\leq\infty
, and
\kappa\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}R_{0}(\kappa)\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(20)

Moreover, for each
u\in L^{2}_{+}
,
the left-hand side tends to 0 as
\kappa\to\infty
, uniformly for
1\leq n\leq\infty
.

Proof.

First, we have

\displaystyle\|u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}R_{0}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}R_{0}f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

We bound the
L^{\infty}
norm by the
\ell^{1}
norm of the Fourier coefficients:

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}R_{0}f\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Bigg|\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\overline{\widehat{u}(l-k)}\frac{\widehat{f}(l)}{l+\kappa}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\frac{|\widehat{f}(l)|}{l+\kappa}\sum_{k=0}^{l}|{\widehat{u}(l-k)}|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}}\Bigg\|\frac{1}{l+\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{l}|\widehat{u}(m)|\Bigg\|_{\ell^{2}},

where in the second line we use the fact that
u\in L^{2}_{+}
, and hence the terms are zero for
l<k
. As
\kappa\geq 1
we apply Hardy’s inequality to obtain the bound

\Bigg\|\frac{1}{l+\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{l}|\widehat{u}(m)|\Bigg\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\Bigg\|\frac{1}{l+1}\sum_{m=0}^{l}|\widehat{u}(m)|\Bigg\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{2}},

which proves (
20
).

Moreover, it follows from the above that

\Bigg\|\frac{1}{l+\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{l}|\widehat{u}(m)|\Bigg\|_{\ell^{2}_{l\geq N}}\xrightarrow{N\rightarrow\infty}0.

So, for
N\geq 1
fixed, we estimate the contribution of the low frequencies
l<N
:

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\frac{1}{l+\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{l}|\widehat{u}(m)|\Bigg\|_{\ell^{2}_{l<N}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{l}|\widehat{u}(m)|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}(l+1)\sum_{m=0}^{l}|\widehat{u}(m)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\xrightarrow{\kappa\to\infty}0.

Therefore, the desired convergence follows from first choosing
N
large and then sending
\kappa\to\infty
.
∎

The following lemma is used in Section
4.3
for obtaining resolvent convergence as
n\to\infty
.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

\|(\Pi-\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}=\|R_{0}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{1}{n},

(21)

uniformly for
\kappa\geq 1
.

Proof.

First, note that
R_{0}
and
\Pi-\Pi_{n}
commute as they are both Fourier multipliers. For
f\in L^{2}_{+}
, we have

\|R_{0}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq n}\frac{|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}}{(k+\kappa)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\qed

Remark 4.4
.

As
\|\Pi_{n}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq 1
, the estimates (
18
) and (
20
) also hold when adding the truncation operator
\Pi_{n}
in front and estimating the perturbative term
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}R_{0}
or
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}R_{0}
. Nevertheless, we keep the above more general statement, given that in Section
4.3
we apply equation (
21
) followed by equations (
18
) and (
20
), without truncation
\Pi_{n}
on the left side.

4.2
Self-adjoint operators and equivalence of norms

Proposition 4.5

(The truncated Lax operators for (
BO
))
.

Given
u\in L^{2}
real-valued and
1\leq n\leq\infty
, the operator

L_{n}=-i\partial_{x}-\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}

with domain
D(L_{n})=H^{1}_{+}
is self-adjoint and semi-bounded. Moreover, there is a constant
\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{0}(\|u\|_{L^{2}})\geq 1
such that for all
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
, the resolvent of
L_{n}
,

R_{n}(\kappa;u)=(L_{n}+\kappa)^{-1}

exists, and maps
L^{2}_{+}
into
H^{1}_{+}
. Moreover, the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(L_{n}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tfrac{3}{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}},

(22)

\displaystyle\tfrac{2}{3}\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\leq\;\;\|R_{n}(\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}\;\;\leq 2\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{\kappa}}

(23)

uniformly for
1\leq n\leq\infty
and
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
.

Proof.

First, the linear operator
L_{0}=-i\partial_{x}
is self-adjoint with domain
H^{1}_{+}
. Hence, we consider the symmetric operator
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}
.
For
f\in H^{1}_{+}
, by applying estimate (
18
) we have

\|\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\Pi_{n}\|_{\textup{op}}\|u\Pi_{n}R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\|(L_{0}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sqrt{3}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|(L_{0}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}.

(24)

By taking
\kappa
large, it follows from the above estimate that the operator
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}
is infinitesimally small with respect to the operator
L_{0}=-i\partial_{x}
on
L^{2}_{+}

[
47
, Chp. X.2]
.
In particular, by choosing
\kappa_{0}\geq\max{\{12\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},1\}}
we have that

\|\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|(L_{0}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}},\quad\kappa\geq\kappa_{0},\quad 1\leq n\leq\infty

(25)

and hence
\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}
is
L_{0}
-bounded.
Thus, by the Kato–Rellich theorem
[
47
]
*Th. X.12, we have that
L_{n}
is a self-adjoint operator with domain
H^{1}_{+}
, and is semi-bounded from below by
-\kappa_{0}
.
Finally, by again applying (
25
) we have

\tfrac{1}{2}\|(L_{0}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|(L_{n}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tfrac{3}{2}\|(L_{0}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}},

which proves (
22
). The estimate (
23
) then follows by duality.
∎

Proposition 4.6

(The truncated Lax operators for (
CCM
))
.

Given
u\in L^{2}_{+}
and
1\leq n\leq\infty
, the operator

L_{n}f=-i\partial f\mp\Pi_{n}[u\Pi_{n}(\overline{u}\Pi_{n}f)]

with domain
D(L_{n})=H^{1}_{+}
is self-adjoint and semi-bounded. Moreover, there is a constant
\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{0}(u)\geq 1
so that for all
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
, the resolvent
R_{n}(\kappa;u)
of
L_{n}
exists, maps
L^{2}_{+}
into
H^{1}_{+}
, and

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(L_{n}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tfrac{3}{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}},

(26)

\displaystyle\tfrac{2}{3}\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\leq\;\;\|R_{n}(\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}\;\;\leq 2\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{\kappa}}

(27)

uniformly for
1\leq n\leq\infty
.

Proof.

Thanks to the convergence result of Lemma
4.2
, there exists
\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{0}(u)\geq 1
such that for all
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
we have

\|\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\Pi_{n}\|_{\textup{op}}\|u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\|(L_{0}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|(L_{0}+\kappa)f\|_{L^{2}}

for all

.
Therefore, it follows from the Kato–Rellich theorem
[
47
]
*Th. X.12, that
L_{n}
is a self-adjoint and semi-bounded operator on the domain

. In fact, we once again have the stronger result that

is infinitesimally small with respect to the operator

on

.

Therefore, it follows from the above inequalities that

which proves (

). The estimate (

) then follows by duality.
∎

4.3
Norm resolvent convergence

Lemma 4.7

(Norm resolvent convergence for (
BO
))
.

Given a real-valued function
u\in L^{2}
, the operators
L_{n}
converge to
L_{\infty}
in norm resolvent sense as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa_{0}
denote the constant from Proposition
4.5
.
It suffices to show that for any
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
, we have

\|R_{n}(\kappa)-R_{\infty}(\kappa)\|_{\textup{op}}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0.

Using the resolvent identity

R_{n}-R_{\infty}=R_{n}(L_{\infty}-L_{n})R_{\infty}=-R_{n}(\Pi u\Pi-\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n})R_{\infty},

we have

\|R_{n}-R_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\|R_{n}R_{0}^{-1}\|_{\textup{op}}\|R_{0}(\Pi u\Pi-\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\|R_{0}^{-1}R_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}.

Using inequality (
23
), for
f\in L^{2}_{+}
we have

\|R_{n}R_{0}^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|R_{0}^{-1}f\|_{H^{-1}_{\kappa}}=2\|f\|_{L^{2}},

hence the first factor is bounded by
\|R_{n}R_{0}^{-1}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq 2
.
Next, applying (
22
) in the case
n=\infty
, we have

\|R_{0}^{-1}R_{\infty}f\|_{L^{2}}=\|R_{\infty}f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}}\leq 2\|(L_{\infty}+\kappa)R_{\infty}f\|_{L^{2}}=2\|f\|_{L^{2}},

hence the last factor is bounded by
\|R_{0}^{-1}R_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq 2
.
Therefore, to finish the proof, it suffices to show

\|R_{0}(\Pi u\Pi-\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\|R_{0}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})u\Pi R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}+\|R_{0}\Pi_{n}u(\Pi-\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}

converges to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

For the first term, using (
21
) and (
18
) with
n=\infty
we have

\|R_{0}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})u\Pi R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\|R_{0}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})\|_{\textup{op}}\|u\Pi R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{3}\kappa^{-1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Next, recall that for a bounded operator
B
on a Hilbert space, we have

\|B^{*}\|_{\textup{op}}=\|B\|_{\textup{op}}.

(28)

Thus, the second term is bounded by

\displaystyle\|R_{0}\Pi_{n}u(\Pi-\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{0}\Pi_{n}(u\ \cdot)\|_{\textup{op}}\|(\Pi-\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle=\|u\Pi_{n}R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\|R_{0}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{3}\kappa^{-1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}},

where we again used estimates (
18
) and (
21
). In conclusion,

\|R_{n}(\kappa)-R_{\infty}(\kappa)\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{1}{n}8\sqrt{3}\kappa^{-1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.\qed

Lemma 4.8

(Norm resolvent convergence for (
CCM
))
.

Given
u\in L^{2}_{+}
, the operators
L_{n}
converge to
L_{\infty}
in norm resolvent sense as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa_{0}
denote the constant from Proposition
4.6
.
Following the proof of Lemma
4.7
, it suffices to show that for any
\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
, we have

\|R_{n}-R_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\|R_{n}R_{0}^{-1}\|_{\textup{op}}\|R_{0}(\Pi u\Pi\overline{u}\Pi-\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\|R_{0}^{-1}R_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}

goes to zero as
n\to\infty
.

Using inequalities (
26
) and (
27
), we again have

\|R_{n}R_{0}^{-1}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad\|R_{0}^{-1}R_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq 2.

Therefore, to finish the proof, it suffices to show

\displaystyle\|R_{0}(\Pi u\Pi\overline{u}\Pi-\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{0}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})u\Pi\overline{u}\Pi R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle+\|R_{0}\Pi_{n}u(\Pi-\Pi_{n})\overline{u}\Pi R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle+\|R_{0}\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}

goes to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
For the first term, using estimates (
21
) and (
20
) we have

\|R_{0}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})u\Pi\overline{u}\Pi R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{1}{n}\|u\Pi\overline{u}\Pi R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{2\|u\|^{2}}{n}

which goes to zero as

. Moreover, as
u\in L^{2}_{+}
, the operator

is zero, hence the second term vanishes.
Finally, for the third term, by noting that
(R_{0}\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u})^{*}=u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}R_{0}
and applying equation (

) we have

\|R_{0}\Pi_{n}u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}(\Pi-\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\|u\Pi_{n}\overline{u}\Pi_{n}R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\|(\Pi-\Pi_{n})R_{0}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{2\|u\|^{2}}{n},

where we once again used (

) and (

). This finishes the proof.
∎

4.4
The generated unitary groups

From Section
4.2
we have that the truncated Lax operators
L_{n}
for (
BO
) and (
CCM
) are self-adjoint. By the functional calculus, it follows that each of these operators generates a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group
t\mapsto e^{itL_{n}}
on
L^{2}_{+}
, see
[
46
]
*Th. VIII.7.

To conclude this section, we will prove the following result regarding the convergence of these unitary groups as
n\to\infty
. A key ingredient of the proof are Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
, which establish that
L_{n}
converges to
L
in norm resolvent sense. It is well-known that this implies
e^{itL_{n}}\to e^{itL}
in the strong operator topology, for each time
t
, see for example
[
46
]
*Th. VIII.20. By comparison, our result (
29
) states that for the sequence of truncated Lax operators
L_{n}
, the convergence of the unitary groups
e^{itL_{n}}
is uniform on both
compact time intervals
and
compact subsets of
L^{2}_{+}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

For
1\leq n\leq\infty
, let
L_{n}
denote the truncated Lax operators for (
BO
) or (
CCM
) constructed in Proposition
4.5
or
4.6
for any fixed initial data
u
. Then, for any
T>0
and any compact set
\mathcal{F}\subset L^{2}_{+}
, we have

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\ \sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\,\|(e^{itL_{n}}-e^{itL_{\infty}})f\|_{L^{2}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

(29)

Proof.

We will present the argument in the settings of (
BO
) and (
CCM
) in parallel, indicating the source of each ingredient for both cases when necessary.

Set
\kappa=\kappa_{0}
, where
\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{0}(u)
is defined in Proposition
4.5
and
4.6
for (
BO
) and (
CCM
) respectively.
For
M\geq 1
, let
\chi_{\leq M}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function that is equal to one on
[-\kappa,M]
, and goes to zero as
x\to+\infty
. We also let
X=[-T,T]\times[-\kappa,+\infty)
and define

g\colon\begin{array}[]{rccl}&X&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&(t,x)&\longmapsto&e^{itx}\chi_{\leq M}(x).\end{array}

By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, polynomials in
t
and
(x+\kappa)^{-1}
are dense in
C_{0}(X)
. Therefore, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a polynomial
P(t,y)

such that

\sup_{(t,x)\in X}\left|P\big(t,(x+\kappa)^{-1}\big)-g(t,x)\right|\leq\epsilon.

Hence, by the functional calculus we have

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[-T,T]\\[1.63885pt]
1\leq n\leq\infty\end{subarray}}\left\|P\big(t,(L_{n}+\kappa)^{-1}\big)-g(t,L_{n})\right\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\epsilon.

(30)

We can then decompose the error into three terms,

\displaystyle\|(e^{itL_{n}}-e^{itL_{\infty}})f\|_{L^{2}}{}\leq{}

\displaystyle 2\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[-T,T]\\[1.63885pt]
1\leq n\leq\infty\end{subarray}}\|e^{itL_{n}}f-g(t,L_{n})f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+2C\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[-T,T]\\[1.63885pt]
1\leq n\leq\infty\end{subarray}}\big\|P\big(t,(L_{n}+\kappa)^{-1}\big)-g(t,L_{n})\big\|_{\textup{op}}

(31)

\displaystyle+C\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\big\|P\big(t,(L_{n}+\kappa)^{-1}\big)-P\big(t,(L+\kappa)^{-1}\big)\big\|_{\textup{op}},

with
C=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}
. The second term in (
4.4
) can be taken arbitrarily small using (
30
). We now prove that the third term in (
4.4
) goes to zero as
n\to\infty
. Indeed, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle R_{n}^{m}-R_{\infty}^{m}=R_{n}^{m-1}

\displaystyle\left(R_{n}-R_{\infty}\right)+\left(R_{n}^{m-1}-R_{\infty}^{m-1}\right)R_{\infty},

and hence, using (
23
) and (
27
),

\|R_{n}^{m}-R^{m}_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq 2^{m-1}\|R_{n}-R_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}+2\|R_{n}^{m-1}-R_{\infty}^{m-1}\|_{\textup{op}}.

By induction,
\|R_{n}^{m}-R^{m}_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq 3^{m}\|R_{n}-R_{\infty}\|_{\textup{op}}
, which goes to zero as
n\to\infty
by Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
.
Next, by writing the polynomial as
P(t,y)=\sum_{\ell,m}\alpha_{\ell,m}t^{\ell}y^{m}
, it follows that

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|P\big(t,(L_{n}+\kappa)^{-1}\big)-P\big(t,(L+\kappa)^{-1}\big)\|_{\textup{op}}\leq\sum_{\ell,m}\alpha_{\ell,m}T^{\ell}\|R_{n}^{m}-R_{\infty}^{m}\|_{\textup{op}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

Finally, we show that the first term in (
4.4
) goes to zero as
M\to\infty
, finishing the proof. As
\mathcal{F}\subset L^{2}_{+}
is compact and
H^{1}_{+}
is dense in
L^{2}_{+}
, we have that given any
\epsilon>0
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{m}\in H^{1}_{+}
such that

\forall f\in\mathcal{F},\ \exists j\in\{1,\dots,m\},\ \|\phi_{j}-f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon.

Thus, by letting
\chi_{>M}=1-\chi_{\leq M}
we have that for
t\in[-T,T]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

\|e^{itL_{n}}f-g(t,L_{n})f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\chi_{>M}(L_{n})e^{itL_{n}}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\chi_{>M}(L_{n})(f-\phi_{i})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\chi_{>M}(L_{n})\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}}.

The first term in the right-hand side above is bounded by
\epsilon
while for the second term, using the functional calculus and inequalities (
23
) and (
27
), we have

\|\chi_{>M}(L_{n})\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\chi_{>M}(L_{n})\frac{L_{n}+\kappa}{M+\kappa}\phi_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{M}\|(L_{n}+\kappa)\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{3}{2M}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\|\phi_{i}\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}},

which is independent of
n
and goes to zero as
M\to\infty
.
To conclude the proof we take first
M
, then
\deg(P)
, and finally
n
large enough, in order for the three terms in (
4.4
) to be arbitrarily small. ∎

4.5
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We divide the proof into two steps. First, we use Proposition
4.9
to show weak
L^{2}
-convergence of the scheme
u_{K}(t)
to the solution
u(t)
, uniformly for
t\in[-T,T]
. We then leverage to strong convergence using the boundedness of the
L^{2}
-norm of the scheme, see Proposition
2.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We will consider both the (
BO
) and (
CCM
) cases simultaneously. Indeed, the primary input will be Propositions
2.4
and
4.9
, which apply to both cases.

Fix
T>0
and let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
for (
BO
), and
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{+}
in the case of (
CCM
) with the additional assumption
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<1
in the focusing case.
Let
K>0
be the highest frequency in the approximation and
(n(k))_{k\geq 0}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence such that

n(k)\xrightarrow[K\to\infty]{}\infty,\quad\text{for each}\ k\geq 0.

We begin by comparing the scheme
u_{K}
to the sequence
w_{K}
, which is generated by the same recursion (
6
) as
u_{K}
, but initialized with the seed
w^{0}=\Pi u_{0}
instead of
u^{0}=\Pi_{n(0)}u_{0}
.
Namely, for
t\in[-T,T]
and
0\leq k<K
we define the Fourier coefficients of
w_{K}
as

\widehat{w}_{K}(t,k)=\big\langle(e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(k)})}S^{*}\big)\cdots\big(e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(2)})}S^{*}\big)\big(e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(1)})}S^{*}\big)\Pi u_{0},1\big\rangle

and we set
\widehat{w}_{K}(t,k)=0
for
k\geq K
. The above holds with a positive sign (+) for (
BO
) and a negative sign (–) for (
CCM
). For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, each operator
e^{itL_{j}}
is unitary and
\|S^{*}\|_{\textup{op}}\leq 1
, hence by Cauchy–Schwarz we have

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{w}_{K}(t,k)-\widehat{u}_{K}(t,k)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\big\langle(e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(k)})}S^{*}\big)\cdots\big(e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(1)})}S^{*}\big)(\Pi-\Pi_{n(0)})u_{0},1\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|(e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(k)})}S^{*}\big)\cdots\big(e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(1)})}S^{*}\big)(\Pi-\Pi_{n(0)})u_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\Pi-\Pi_{n(0)})u_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}}.

The above right-hand side converges to zero as
n(0)\to\infty
.
Therefore, for each
k\geq 0
, we have

|\widehat{w}_{K}(t,k)-\widehat{u}_{K}(t,k)|\xrightarrow{K\to\infty}0,\quad\text{ uniformly for }t\in[-T,T].

(32)

In view of this, it will suffice to study the behavior of the Fourier coefficients of
w_{K}
rather than
u_{K}
.

For
k\geq 0
, we write

\displaystyle\widehat{w}_{K}(t,k)-\widehat{u}(t,k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big\langle Z_{k,j}\ \big(e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(j)})}-e^{\pm it(1+2L)}\big)S^{*}(e^{\pm it(1+2L)}S^{*})^{j-1}\Pi u_{0},1\Big\rangle,

with
Z_{k,k}=\rm{Id}
and
Z_{k,j-1}=Z_{k,j}\ e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(j)})}S^{*}
. In particular, we have

Z_{k,j}=e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(k)})}S^{*}\cdots e^{\pm it(1+2L_{n(j+1)})}S^{*},\quad j<k-1.

Hence, the following bound holds

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{w}_{K}(t,k)-\widehat{u}(t,k)\big|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\big\|\big(e^{\pm 2itL_{n(j)}}-e^{\pm 2itL}\big)S^{*}(e^{\pm 2itL}S^{*})^{j-1}\Pi u_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}}.

Next, notice that the operator

\begin{array}[]{rccl}e^{itL}\colon&\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}_{+}&\longrightarrow&L^{2}_{+}\\
&(t,f)&\longmapsto&e^{itL}f\end{array}

is jointly continuous. Indeed, given a sequence
t_{m}\to t
in
\mathbb{R}
and
\phi_{m}\to\phi
in
L^{2}_{+}
, we have

\|e^{it_{m}L}\phi_{m}-e^{itL}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|e^{it_{m}L}\|_{\textup{op}}\|\phi_{m}-\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|e^{it_{m}L}\phi-e^{itL}\phi\|_{L^{2}}.

The right-hand side above tends to zero as
m\to\infty
, due to the bound
\|e^{it_{m}L}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1
and the fact that the mapping
t\mapsto e^{itL}
is strongly continuous (see for example
[
46
]
*Th. VIII.7).

Therefore, by composition of continuous applications, the mapping

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathbb{R}&\longrightarrow&L^{2}_{+}\\
t&\longmapsto&S^{*}(e^{\pm 2itL}S^{*})^{j-1}\Pi u_{0}\end{array}

is continuous for each
j=1,\dots,k
. Hence,
for any
T>0
, the set

\mathcal{F}_{T}=\big\{S^{*}(e^{\pm 2itL}S^{*})^{j-1}\Pi u_{0}:t\in[-T,T],\ j=1,\dots,k\big\}

is compact in
L^{2}_{+}
. Applying Proposition
4.9
to this set
\mathcal{F}_{T}
,
we conclude that for each
k\geq 0
we have

\widehat{w}_{K}(t,k)\xrightarrow{K\to\infty}\widehat{u}(t,k)\quad\text{ uniformly for }t\in[-T,T].

Comparing this with (
32
), we see that for each
0\leq k<K
,

\widehat{u}_{K}(t,k)\xrightarrow{K\to\infty}\widehat{u}(t,k)\quad\text{ uniformly for }t\in[-T,T].

In the (
BO
) case, we use
the fact that the solution is real-valued and the definition of the scheme to see that the above convergence also holds for
-K<k\leq 0
.

As a consequence,
we conclude weak
L^{2}
convergence:

u_{K}(t)\xrightharpoonup{K\to\infty}u(t)\quad\text{ uniformly for }t\in[-T,T].

(33)

Indeed, by Proposition
2.4
the scheme is uniformly bounded and hence

\|u(t)-u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Thereby, given
\epsilon>0
and a test function
\phi\in L^{2}
, for
K_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{|k|\geq K_{0}}|\widehat{\phi}(k)|^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}
, we have

|\langle u(t)-u_{K}(t),\phi\rangle|\leq\sum_{|k|<K_{0}}|\widehat{\phi}(k)|\ |\widehat{u}(t,k)-\widehat{u}_{K}(t,k)|+2\epsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

where the first term can be made arbitrarily small, uniformly for
t\in[-T,T]
, by taking
K>K_{0}
and
n(j)
large enough for
0\leq j<K_{0}
. This proves (
33
).

To finish the proof, we upgrade this to strong convergence:

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|u_{K}(t)-u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\xrightarrow{K\to\infty}0.

(34)

Using the boundedness of the
L^{2}
-norm of the numerical scheme (
9
), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{K}(t)-u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u_{K}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\Re\langle u_{K}(t),u(t)\rangle

(35)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\Re\langle u_{K}(t),u(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=2\Re\langle u(t)-u_{K}(t),u(t)\rangle.

By the global well-posedness results for (
BO
) and (
CCM
) discussed in the introduction, the mappings

\eqref{eq:BO}:\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathbb{R}&\longrightarrow&L^{2}\\
t&\longmapsto&u(t)\end{array}\quad\text{and}\quad\eqref{eq:CS}:\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathbb{R}&\longrightarrow&L^{2}_{+}\\
t&\longmapsto&u(t)\end{array}

are continuous and thereby the orbits
\{u(t):t\in[-T,T]\}

Geometry of the Bianchi eigenvariety around non-cuspidal points and strong multiplicity-one results.
Daniel Barrera Salazar, Luis Santiago Palacios

Abstract
. Let K K be an imaginary quadratic field. In this article, we study the local geometry of the Bianchi eigenvariety around non-cuspidal classical points, in particular, ordinary non-cuspidal base change points. To perform this study we introduce Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems and prove strong multiplicity-one results on the cohomology of the corresponding Bianchi threefolds. We believe these results are of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

Eigenvarieties are rigid analytic spaces that parametrize
p
-adic automorphic representations. A classical point is a point attached to a usual automorphic representation. The study of an eigenvariety around classical points has shown important arithmetic applications and has been carried out in several settings, as for example
[
GS93
]
,
[
SU14
]
,
[
BDP22
]
,
[
Wil90
]
,
[
BDR+22
]
,
[
LZ20
]
,
[
BCGP21
]
,
[
Kis03
]
.

It seems that in most of the literature where the geometry around classical points has been studied the underlying locally symmetric space is a Shimura variety. When there is no a Shimura variety structure, less tools are available and therefore the local geometry of the corresponding eigenvariety becomes much harder to study. The first natural setting where this happens is the context of Bianchi manifolds, which are hyperbolic three manifolds attached to Bianchi groups. The geometry of Bianchi eigenvarieties
around classical points attached to
cuspidal
automorphic representations seems to hide deep mysteries (see for example
[
BSW21a
]
,
[
Raw24
]
,
[
Ser22
]
,
[
LW23
]
) and to be connected to important arithmetic questions as explained in
[
LZ21
]
.

This work is devoted to the study of the geometry of Bianchi eigenvarieties around classical points attached to
non-cuspidal
automorphic representations. To perform this study we prove multiplicity-one results in cohomology which are interesting by themselves. Their strong versions seem to be new in the literature. Thus, our work is focused on two type of results:

•

strong multiplicity-one theorems, and

•

the geometry of the Bianchi eigenvariety.

We would like to stress the opposite behavior of
p
-adic families in the Hilbert and Bianchi setting. For example, in the Hilbert setting, where the underlying totally real number field is of degree
2
over
\Q
, the cuspidal families are
2
-dimensional and Eisenstein families are
1
-dimensional. In the Bianchi setting exactly the opposite is true. Developing this opposite behavior was the main motivation of this work.

Furthermore, we expect analogue phenomena to the Bianchi situation would be possible to develop for other reductive groups
G
over
\Q
such that
G(\R)
does not have discrete series.

1.1.
Strong multiplicity-one

In his thesis, Weisinger
[
Wei77
]
developed a newform theory for elliptic modular Eisenstein series, proving in particular, a strong multiplicity-one theorem. This multiplicity-one theorem was later improved by Rajan
[
Raj00
]
, followed by a further refinement of Thompson and Linowitz
[
LT15
]
, who showed that Eisenstein newforms are uniquely determined by their Hecke eigenvalues for any set of primes having Dirichlet density greater than
1/2
.

The newform theory developed by Weisinger, was later extended to the Hilbert modular setting by Wiles
[
Wil86
]
and by Atwill and Linowitz
[
AL13
]
. In particular,
[
AL13
]
proves that Hilbert Eisenstein newforms, just like elliptic modular Eisenstein series, are uniquely determined by their Hecke eigenvalues for any set of primes having Dirichlet density greater than
1/2
.

One of our main goals is to prove analogous results in the Bianchi setting and working with the cohomology of the corresponding Bianchi threefold. We put our attention on
Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems
, which are systems of Hecke-eigenvalues appearing in the Eisenstein part of the cohomology of a Bianchi threefold.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field. Let
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
be a couple of Hecke characters of
K
with
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
having conductors
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{n}_{2}
respectively, such that
(\mathfrak{n}_{1},\mathfrak{n}_{2})=1
and let
Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})}
be the Bianchi threefold of level
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\mathfrak{n}_{2}
(see
2.3
). Let
S
be a set of primes of
K
not dividing
\mathfrak{n}
and consider the following ideal of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}\otimes\C
(see def.
2.1
):

\mathfrak{m}_{\phi,S}=(\{T_{\mathfrak{q}}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1},T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}:\mathfrak{q}\in S\}).

The following theorem produces explicit Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems of level
\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
and weight
(k,\ell)
for
k,\ell\in\N
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
S
has Dirichlet density greater than
1/2
. If
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]
(resp.
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]
and
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
), then

\mathrm{dim}_{\C}H_{Eis}^{1}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))[[\mathfrak{m}_{\phi,S}]]=1\;(\mathrm{resp.}\;0),

\mathrm{dim}_{\C}H_{Eis}^{2}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))[[\mathfrak{m}_{\phi,S}]]=0\;(\mathrm{resp.}\;1),

here
\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C)
is the locally constant sheaf attached to the polynomials
V_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C)
(see
2.3
).

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

Note that
H_{Eis}^{0}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))=0
unless
k=\ell=0
. In this case there is a contribution from those
\phi
having infinity type
[(1,1),(-1,-1)]
, and this was excluded in the theorem. Moreover,
H_{Eis}^{3}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))=0
for every
k,\ell\in\N
.

(2)

Combining this theorem with
[
Ram94
]
we obtain results for compact and full singular cohomology but with the stronger hypothesis of Dirichlet density greater than
7/8
.

1.2.
Results about the Bianchi Eigenvariety

Henceforth, we fix a prime
p\nmid\mathfrak{n}
that splits in
K
as
\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
and we fix an embedding
\iota_{p}:\overline{\Q}\hookrightarrow\overline{\Q}_{p}
such that
\mathfrak{p}
corresponds to
\iota_{p}
. Remark that
\iota_{p}
determines a valuation
v_{p}
on
\overline{\Q}_{p}
.

In order to
p
-adically study our Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems we consider their
p
-stabilizations. More precisely, let
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
be a character as in Theorem
1.1
, which gives us a Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystem of level
\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
and weight
(k,\ell)
. Denote by
\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}=\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}
and
\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}=N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}
the roots of the Hecke polynomial of
\phi
at
\mathfrak{q}|p
, then a
p
-stabilization of
\phi
is a couple
\tilde{\phi}=(\phi,(x_{\mathfrak{q}})_{\mathfrak{q}\mid p})
where
x_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\{\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}},\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}\}
for each
\mathfrak{q}\mid p
. Let
L/\Q_{p}
be a finite extension containing the values of
\tilde{\phi}
, we consider the following ideal of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}\otimes_{\Z}L
(see def.
2.1
):

\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}:=(\{T_{\mathfrak{q}}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1},T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}:\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p\}\cup\{U_{\mathfrak{q}}-x_{\mathfrak{q}}:\mathfrak{q}|p\}).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\tilde{\phi}
as above, and suppose
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]
(resp.
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]
and
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
) then:

\mathrm{dim}_{L}H^{1}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(L))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}=\mathrm{dim}_{L}H_{c}^{2}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(L))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}=1\text{ (resp. $0$)}

\mathrm{dim}_{L}H^{2}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(L))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}=\mathrm{dim}_{L}H_{c}^{1}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(L))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}=0\text{ (resp. $1$)}.

As the cohomology above is concentrated in degrees
1
and
2
this theorem gives a complete description of the appearance of the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\phi}
in the singular and compactly supported cohomology.

Now we take
\tilde{\phi}=(\phi,\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}},\beta_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})
when
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]
and
\tilde{\phi}=(\phi,\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}},\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})
when
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]
and
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
. In both cases, these are ordinary
p
-stabilizations (see
3.11
)

We consider the eigenvarieties
\mathcal{E}
(resp
\mathcal{E}_{c}
) of tame level
K_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
constructed in
[
HN17
]
using the full cohomology (resp. compact supported cohomology) which are endowed with finite maps
\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
(resp.
\mathcal{E}_{c}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
) to the
2
-dimensional weight space
\mathcal{W}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

We have:

(1)

\tilde{\phi}
determines a point
x\in\mathcal{E}
and the weight map
\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
is etale at
x
.

(2)

\tilde{\phi}
determines a point
x_{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{c}
and the weight map
\mathcal{E}_{c}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
is etale at
x_{c}
.

(3)

There exist an open affinoid
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{W}
containing the weight
(k,\ell)
and a slope
h=(h_{\mathfrak{p}},h_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})\in\R_{\geq 0}^{2}
such that if we denote by
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{E}_{c}
the irreducible component over
\mathcal{U}
passing through
x_{c}
and
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]
(resp.
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]
with
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
) then
H^{2}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}})^{\leqslant h}\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
(resp.
H^{1}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}})^{\leqslant h}\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
) is a free
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
-module of rank
1
.

In (3) we consider
\leqslant h
part of the cohomology with respect to the Hecke operators at
p
and tensor product is over the image of the Hecke algebra in the endomorphism of the corresponding cohomology (see
4.2
for more details). Moreover, the same is true for
x
but using the full cohomology on degree
1
when
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]
, and the full cohomology on degree
2
when
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]
with
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
. To prove this theorem we use control theorems on the cohomology of Bianchi manifolds, the multiplicity-one results stated above, and the formalism developed in
[
BDJ22
]
.

An important source of non-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms is the base change to
\mathrm{GL}_{2,K}
of cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{2,\Q}
. Our result then implies the etaleness of the ordinary
p
-stabilization.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\varphi
be a Hecke character of
K
of infinity type
(-k-1,0)
with
k\geqslant 0
and conductor
\mathfrak{m}
coprime to
(p)
satisfying
(\mathfrak{m},\overline{\mathfrak{m}})=1
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the non-cuspidal Bianchi modular form arising from the base change to

of the theta series of
\varphi
, and suppose that its level is exactly

. Then the ordinary

-stabilization of
\mathcal{F}
determines a point

(resp

) and the weight map
\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
(resp.
\mathcal{E}_{c}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
) is etale at
x
(resp.
x_{c}
).

It is interesting to mention that the other
3

p
-stabilizations of
\mathcal{F}
are of critical slope, then we cannot apply the results of this work because control theorems on the cohomology are not available in these cases. This makes their study more subtle than that of the ordinary
p
-stabilization (see for example
[

]
,
[

]
and
[

). We make some remarks and questions at the end of Section

Returning to the general case, when

and

has infinity type

with

, in Section

-adic

essentially using the formalism developed in

]
(see also

2.
Cohomology of the Bianchi threefold

2.1.
Notations

Let
p
be a rational prime and fix throughout the paper embeddings
\iota_{\infty}:\overline{\Q}\hookrightarrow\C
and
\iota_{p}:\overline{\Q}\hookrightarrow\overline{\Q}_{p}
, note that the latter fixes a
p
-adic valuation
v_{p}
on
\overline{\Q}_{p}
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field with discriminant
-D
, ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and different ideal
\mathcal{D}
generated by
\delta=\sqrt{-D}
. Denote by
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
the completion of
K
with respect to the prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
K
,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}
the ring of integers of
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
and fix a uniformizer
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
at
\mathfrak{q}
.

Throughout the paper, we suppose
p
splits in
K
as
\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
with
\mathfrak{p}
being the prime corresponding to the embedding
\iota_{p}
. In particular, if we denote
\mathcal{O}_{K,p}:=\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\Z}\Z_{p}
we have
\mathcal{O}_{K,p}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong\Z_{p}\times\Z_{p}
.

We denote by
\mathbb{A}_{K}
the ring of adeles of
K
,
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f}
the finite adeles and
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K}=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}
. If
\chi:K\backslash\A_{K}^{\times}\rightarrow\C
is a Hecke character of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
, we denote by
\chi_{\infty}
,
\chi_{f}
and
\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}
the restriction of
\chi
to
\C
,
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f}
and
K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}
respectively. For an ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
write
\chi_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{m}}\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

A Hecke character
\chi
as above can be identified with a function on ideals of
K
that has support on those that are coprime to
\mathfrak{f}
, concretely, for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{f}
, we define
\chi(\mathfrak{q})=\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})
(which is independent of the choice of the uniformizer) and
\chi(\mathfrak{q})=0
otherwise. We define
\chi^{c}:=\chi\circ c
where
c
denotes complex conjugation on the ideals
of
K
. Denote by
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{f}):=K^{\times}\backslash\A_{K}^{\times}/I(\mathfrak{f})\C^{\times}
the ray class group of
K
modulo
\mathfrak{f}
, where
I(\mathfrak{f}):=\{x\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\times}:x\equiv 1\text{ (mod $\mathfrak{f}$)}\}
.

Let
B
be the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices of
GL_{2/K}
, with unipotent radical
U
and maximal torus
T
. For an ideal
\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
, denote by
\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
(resp.
\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
) the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K})
of matrices congruent to
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)
(resp.
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&*\end{smallmatrix}\big)
) modulo
\mathfrak{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{r}
be an ideal coprime to
\mathfrak{n}
, we will also consider the group
\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{r}):=\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})\cap\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathfrak{r})
. Moreover, for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
we write
\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})_{\mathfrak{q}}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}})
(resp.
\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathfrak{n})_{\mathfrak{q}}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}})
) for the subgroup of matrices congruent to
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)
(resp.
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&*\end{smallmatrix}\big)
) modulo
\mathfrak{q}^{s_{\mathfrak{q}}}
where
\mathfrak{q}^{s_{\mathfrak{q}}}||\mathfrak{n}
. Note that for a prime
\mathfrak{r}
, the group
\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathfrak{r})_{\mathfrak{r}}=I_{\mathfrak{r}}
is just the Iwahori subgroup at
\mathfrak{r}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
an ideal (resp. coprime to
p
) consider the abstract Hecke algebra:

\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}=\Z[\{T_{\mathfrak{q}},T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}:(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{n})=1\}],

(\text{resp. }\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}=\Z[\{T_{\mathfrak{q}},T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}:(\mathfrak{q},p\mathfrak{n})=1\}\cup\{U_{\mathfrak{q}}:\mathfrak{q}|p\}]).

Let
N
be a module over a ring
R
and endowed with a linear action by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}
. By a
system of Hecke eigenvalues
appearing in
N
we mean a ring homomorphism
\psi:\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}\rightarrow R
such that there exists a non-zero vector
n\in N
satisfying
t\cdot h=\psi(t)\cdot h
for all
t\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}
.

2.2.
Induced representations

2.2.1.
Definitions

Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}:K^{\times}\backslash\A_{K}^{\times}\rightarrow\C^{\times}
be two Hecke characters of conductor
\mathfrak{m}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
and infinity type
(k_{1},\ell_{1})
,
(k_{2},\ell_{2})
respectively. They determine a character
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
in
T(K)\backslash T(\A_{K})
of infinity type
[(k_{1},\ell_{1}),(k_{2},\ell_{2})]
and finite part
\chi_{f}=(\chi_{1,f},\chi_{2,f})
.

For each prime
\mathfrak{q}
,
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})_{\mathfrak{q}}=(\chi_{1,\mathfrak{q}},\chi_{2,\mathfrak{q}})
defines a character

(1)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}:B(K_{\mathfrak{q}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\C^{\times}

\displaystyle\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix}\big)

\displaystyle\mapsto\chi_{1,\mathfrak{q}}(a)\chi_{2,\mathfrak{q}}(d),

and we denote by

V_{\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}}=\{h:\mathrm{GL_{2}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})\rightarrow\C:h(bg)=\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}(b)h(g),\forall b\in B(K_{\mathfrak{q}}),g\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})\},

the corresponding local induced representation.

We consider the right action of
\mathrm{GL_{2}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})
on
V_{\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}}
given by
(h\cdot g)(x)=h(x\cdot g^{-1})
for
g,x\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})
. For example, if
\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})
then we have
V_{\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{q}}}=\{h\in V_{\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}}:h(gk)=h(g),\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{q}},g\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that if
V_{\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}})}\neq\{0\}
then
\chi_{1,\mathfrak{q}}
and
\chi_{2,\mathfrak{q}}
are necessarily unramified characters.

Similarly, we define the space
V_{\chi_{f}}
which is endowed with a right action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\A_{K}^{f})
in the same way as before. Note that if
\mathcal{K}_{f}=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\infty}\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{q}}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\A_{K}^{f})
is an open compact subgroup, then we have

V_{\chi_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{f}}=\bigotimes_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\infty}V_{\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{q}}}
.

For each prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
we define the Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
,
T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}
as follows. Let
x=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}\end{smallmatrix}\big)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\A_{K}^{f})
, where we consider
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\A_{K}^{f,\times}
as being
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
at
\mathfrak{q}
and trivial outside
\mathfrak{q}
, then for
h\in V_{\chi_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{f}}
and each
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\A_{K}^{f})
we put:

(T_{\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h)(g)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{K}_{f}x\mathcal{K}_{f}/\mathcal{K}_{f}}h(g\gamma^{-1}),

(T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h)(g)=h(g\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}&0\\
0&\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}\end{smallmatrix}\big)^{-1}).

Observe that these operators are independent of the choice of uniformizer. When
\mathcal{K}_{f}=\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
we write
U_{\mathfrak{q}}
instead
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
for primes
\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{n}
. In particular, we obtain an action of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}\otimes_{\Z}\C
. The same observations follow when
\mathcal{K}_{f}=\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{r})
for
\mathfrak{r}
some ideal and
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{r}
. In this case, we obtain an action of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}\otimes_{\Z}\C
.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that the action of the Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
above is consistent with
[
Ber05
, §2.9.3]
. Moreover, it differs from the action in
[
Ber08
, §3.3]
, which is motivated by our interest in the right-
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
-module
V_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C)
instead of the left-
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
-module
V_{k,\ell}(\C)
(see Section
2.3
and Remark
2.10
).

2.2.2.
Multiplicity-one

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
be Hecke characters of
K
of conductors
\mathfrak{m}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
respectively such that

Proof.

The proof of this lemma follows exactly the arguments of

First note that
V_{\chi_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})}=\bigotimes_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\infty}V_{\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})_{\mathfrak{q}}}

h_{\mathfrak{q}}(g)=\begin{cases}\chi_{1,\mathfrak{q}}(a)\chi_{2,\mathfrak{q}}(d)&\text{if $g=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\

0&\text{if not,}\end{cases}

and note that
h_{\mathfrak{q}}\in V_{\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})_{\mathfrak{q}}}
.

If
\mathfrak{q}\mid\mathfrak{m}_{2}

h_{\mathfrak{q}}(g)=\begin{cases}\chi_{1,\mathfrak{q}}(a)\chi_{2,\mathfrak{q}}(d)&\text{if $g=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix}\big)\cdot\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\

1&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{m})_{\mathfrak{q}}$}\\
0&\text{if not,}\end{cases}

By construction, we have

If
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{m}

(T_{\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h)(1)=

(2)

=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}}h\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-a\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}\\

(U_{\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h)(1)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}}h\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-a\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}\\

then, the
U_{\mathfrak{q}}

N(\mathfrak{q})\chi_{2,\mathfrak{q}}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})^{-1}

If

(U_{\mathfrak{q}}h)\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)=\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}}h_{\mathfrak{q}}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-a\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}\\
0&\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\big)\right)\cdot\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{q}}h_{\mathfrak{l}}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)

=\left(h_{\mathfrak{q}}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}&0\\

1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}},a\neq 0}h_{\mathfrak{q}}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a^{-1}&-\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}\\

1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big),

and since
h_{\mathfrak{q}}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)=h_{\mathfrak{q}}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}-\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}&(1+\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}\\

1&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1+\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}&-1\\
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)=\chi_{1,\mathfrak{q}}(-\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1})
and the sum over

(U_{\mathfrak{q}}h)\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\

Now, since

1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)=h_{\mathfrak{q}}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1+\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}\\

and the

Finally, for

then, the

Let
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
be a couple of Hecke characters of

\mathfrak{n}_{1}

\mathfrak{n}_{2}

(3)

\mathfrak{m}_{\phi,S}=(\{T_{\mathfrak{q}}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1},T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}:\mathfrak{q}\in S\}).

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that
S
has Dirichlet density greater than
1/2
and
\phi
as above with infinity type
[(k_{1},\ell_{1}),(k_{2},\ell_{2})]
satisfying
(k_{1},\ell_{1})\neq(k_{2}+1,\ell_{2}+1)
. Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
be a pair of Hecke characters such that
V_{(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})}[[\mathfrak{m}_{\phi,S}]]\neq\{0\}
, then either
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
or
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\phi_{2}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}},\phi_{1}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}}^{-1})
. Moreover, we have that
V_{(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})}[[\mathfrak{m}_{\phi,S}]]
is a 1-dimensional
\C
-vector space.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathfrak{f}_{1},\mathfrak{f}_{2}
the conductor of
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
respectively. Shrinking
S
we can suppose that every prime
\mathfrak{q}\in S
has degree
1
and does not divide
\mathfrak{f}_{1}\mathfrak{f}_{2}\mathcal{D}
. Note that
S
still has the same Dirichlet density since the set of primes of degree
1
have Dirichlet density 1 (see for example
[
Lan94
]
[Ch. VIII §4]).

Suppose
V_{(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})}[[\mathfrak{m}_{\phi,S}]]\neq\{0\}
and let
h\in V_{(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})}[[\mathfrak{m}_{\phi,S}]]
be a non-trivial vector. Then for each
\mathfrak{q}\in S
there exists
c\in\Z_{>0}
, such that
(T_{\mathfrak{q}}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1})^{c}h=0
and
(T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1})^{c}h=0
.

By the proof of the Lemma
2.4
we also have that
T_{\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h=(\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}+N(\mathfrak{q})\chi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1})h
and
T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h=\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\chi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}h
for all
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{f}_{1}\mathfrak{f}_{2}
. From these equations, in particular, we have for each prime
\mathfrak{q}\in S
:

•

(\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}+N(\mathfrak{q})\chi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1})^{c}\cdot h=0
;

•

(\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\chi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1})^{c}\cdot h=0
.

Since
h\neq 0
, we deduce that

(4)

\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}+N(\mathfrak{q})\chi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}=\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}+N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1},\text{ for all $\mathfrak{q}\in S$;}

(5)

\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\chi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}=\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1},\text{ for all $\mathfrak{q}\in S$}.

Multiplying (
4
) by
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}
, substituting (
5
) and factorizing, for each
\mathfrak{q}\in S
we have

(6)

(\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1})(\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1})=0.

Suppose both factors on equation (
6
) vanish for some prime
\mathfrak{r}\in S
, then the characters
(\chi_{1}^{-1}\phi_{1})_{\mathfrak{r}},(\chi_{1}^{-1}\phi_{2}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}})_{\mathfrak{r}}:K_{\mathfrak{r}}^{\times}\rightarrow\C^{\times}
are trivial, and we obtain from
[
Raj00
, Prop 3]
that the characters

\chi_{1}^{-1}\phi_{1}
and
\chi_{1}^{-1}\phi_{2}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}}
have finite order. This implies that
\chi_{1}
has infinity type
(k_{1},\ell_{1})
and
(k_{2}+1,\ell_{2}+1)
at the same time, which contradicts the hypothesis on the infinity types of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
. Note that once either
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{r})^{-1}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{r})^{-1}=0
or
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{r})^{-1}-N(\mathfrak{r})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{r})^{-1}=0
, this determines the infinity type of
\chi_{1}
and then either
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}=0
or
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}-N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}=0
for all
\mathfrak{q}\in S

Suppose that the first factor of (
6
) vanishes, i.e,

\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})=\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})
for all
\mathfrak{q}\in S
. Since
\chi_{1,\infty}=\phi_{1,\infty}
, we have that
\chi_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}
defines a character on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{f})
where
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{f}_{1}\mathfrak{f}_{2}
, then to prove the Hecke characters
\chi_{1},\phi_{1}
are equal it suffices to prove that
\chi_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}
is the trivial character on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{f})
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{q}_{2},..\mathfrak{q}_{t}
be prime ideals representing
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{f})
. Similarly as the proof of
[
AL13
, Thm 3.6]
, if
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q}_{i})=\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q}_{i})
for
s
values of
i
, then the density of primes for which
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})=\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})
is at most
s/t
. It now follows that
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{q}_{i})=\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q}_{i})
for
more than
t/2
values of
i
.

The orthogonality relations on characters show that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\chi_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}_{i})=0
unless
\chi_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}
is the trivial character on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{f})
. Since we showed that
\chi_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}_{i})=1
for more than
t/2
of the
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
, then
\chi_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}
is the trivial character on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{f})
and we conclude that
\chi_{1}=\phi_{1}
.

By equation (
5
), we have that
\chi_{2}^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})=1
for all
\mathfrak{q}\in S
, therefore
(\chi_{2}^{-1}\phi_{2})_{\mathfrak{q}}
is trivial and as before, using
[
Raj00
, Prop 3]
we obtain that
\chi_{2}^{-1}\phi_{2}
has finite order. Thus
\chi_{2}^{-1}\phi_{2}
determines a character on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(\mathfrak{f})
, which by equation (
5
), is the trivial character, and we deduce that
\chi_{2}=\phi_{2}
.

If the second factor of equation (
6
) vanishes for all
\mathfrak{q}\in S

\chi_{1}=\phi_{2}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}}
and
\chi_{2}=\phi_{1}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}}^{-1}

We conclude that for each case

∎

(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\phi_{2}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}},\phi_{1}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}}^{-1})
. Moreover, we have that
V_{(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)}[[\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi},S}]]
is a 1-dimensional
\C
-vector space.

Proof.

First, note that the same proof of Theorem
2.5
gives us that either
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
or
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\phi_{2}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}},\phi_{1}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}}^{-1})
.

By using Corollary
2.7
twice, we have

V_{(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)}\cong V^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}},\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}\oplus V^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}},\beta_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}\oplus V^{\beta_{\mathfrak{p}},\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}\oplus V^{\beta_{\mathfrak{p}},\beta_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}},

with
V^{x_{\mathfrak{p}},x_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}
a 1-dimensional
\C
-vector space for
x_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\{\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}},\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}\}
with
\mathfrak{q}|p
. Moreover, let
h\in V^{x_{\mathfrak{p}},x_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}
, then

•

T_{\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h=(\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}+N(\mathfrak{q})\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1})h
for all
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
,

•

T_{\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h=\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})^{-1}h
for all
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
,

•

U_{\mathfrak{q}}\cdot h=x_{\mathfrak{q}}h
for
\mathfrak{q}|p
.

Since the spaces
V_{(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)}
and
V_{(\phi_{2}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}},\phi_{1}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}}^{-1})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)}
have the same eigenvalues, the latter can be written as a direct sum of four 1-dimensional
\C
-vector spaces having respectively the same eigenvalues of the
V^{x_{\mathfrak{p}},x_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}
’s above.

We conclude that for each case
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
or
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(\phi_{2}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}},\phi_{1}|\cdot|_{\A_{K}}^{-1})
, the space

V_{(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)}[[\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi},S}]]\cong V^{x_{\mathfrak{p}},x_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is a 1-dimensional
\C
-vector space.
∎

Analogously as part ii) of Remark

3.
Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems

3.1.
Non-cuspidal base change

For
\mathcal{K}_{f}
an open compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K})
and
(k,\ell)\in\N\times\N
we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(\mathcal{K}_{f})
the finite-dimensional
\C
-vector space of Bianchi modular forms of weight
(k,\ell)
and level
\mathcal{K}_{f}
. We denote by
S_{(k,\ell)}(\mathcal{K}_{f})
the subspace of cuspidal Bianchi modular forms. From
[
Hid94
, Cor 2.2]
we have that
S_{(k,\ell)}(\mathcal{K}_{f})=\{0\}
if
k\neq\ell
i.e., all non-trivial cuspidal Bianchi modular forms have parallel weight
(k,k)
. Hecke operators act on the spaces
\mathcal{M}_{(k,\ell)}(\mathcal{K}_{f})
and
S_{(k,\ell)}(\mathcal{K}_{f})
. In particular, if
\mathcal{K}_{f}=\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
then the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}\otimes_{\Z}\C
acts on those spaces. In the same way, if
\mathcal{K}_{f}=\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)
then we have an action of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}\otimes_{\Z}\C
on the corresponding spaces. For more details on Bianchi modular forms we refer the reader to
[
Wil17
]
.

An example of a Bianchi modular form which is not cuspidal is obtained by base change from
\mathrm{GL}_{2,\Q}
. Let
\varphi:\A_{K}^{\times}\rightarrow\C^{\times}
be a Hecke character of
K
of infinity type
(-k-1,0)
and conductor
\mathfrak{m}
. We put
M=N(\mathfrak{m})
and we denote by
\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}:(\Z/M\Z)^{\times}\rightarrow\C^{\times}
the Dirichlet character induced by the map
a\mapsto\varphi(a\mathcal{O}_{K})a^{-k-1}
, for
a\in\Z
coprime to
M
. Let
f
be the theta series associated to
\varphi
, which is a newform of weight
k+2
, level
\Gamma_{0}(DM)
and nebentypus
\epsilon_{f}=\chi_{K}\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}
where
\chi_{K}:(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
is the quadratic character of
K/\Q
.

Let
\pi
be the automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by
f
and let BC(
\pi
) be the base change of
\pi
to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
(see
[
Lan80
]
). The
base change
of
f
to
K
is the normalized new vector
\mathcal{F}
in BC(
\pi
) which is a non-cuspidal Bianchi modular form of weight
(k,k)
, level
\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
with
M\mathcal{O}_{K}|\mathfrak{n}|DM\mathcal{O}_{K}
(see
[
Fri83
, §2.3]
) and nebentypus
\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}=\epsilon_{f}\circ N_{K/\Q}
.

In order to apply the main results of this work to this non-cuspidal base change case, we will suppose (compare with Remark 3.6 in
[
BSW21a
]
):

Assumption 3.1
.

The conductor,
\mathfrak{m}
, of
\varphi
is coprime to
(p)
,
(\mathfrak{m},\overline{\mathfrak{m}})=1
and the level of
\mathcal{F}
is exactly
M\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

The Hecke eigenvalues of
\mathcal{F}
can be described in terms of the eigenvalues
a_{q}
of
f
, in particular in terms of
\varphi
: for every prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
K
above
q
,

a_{\mathfrak{q}}=\begin{cases}a_{q}=\varphi(\mathfrak{q})+\varphi(\overline{\mathfrak{q}}),&\text{if $q=\mathfrak{q}\overline{\mathfrak{q}}$}\\
a_{q}=\varphi(\mathfrak{q}),&\text{if $q$ ramifies}\\
a_{q}^{2}-2\chi_{K}(q)\varphi_{\mathbb{Z}}(q)q^{k+1}=2\varphi(\mathfrak{q})&\text{if $q$ is inert.}\end{cases}

3.2.
Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems

We can ask about examples of non-cuspidal Bianchi modular forms other than the base change described in the previous section. From Harder’s work we can consider Bianchi modular forms as systems of Hecke-eigenvalues appearing in the group
H^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))
and we will focus on this point of view.

For each
i\in\N
, recall the Hecke-equivariant decomposition

H^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))\cong H_{cusp}^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))\oplus H_{Eis}^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C)),

where the cuspidal cohomology is defined by

H_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))=\mathrm{Im}\{H_{c}^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))\rightarrow H^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))\},

and its complement, the Eisenstein cohomology
H_{Eis}^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))
, is defined as the image of
H^{i}(\partial\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))
by Harder’s Eisenstein map (see
[
Har84
]
)

\mathrm{Eis}:H^{i}(\partial\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))\rightarrow H^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C)).

The following theorem describes the cuspidal and Eisenstein cohomology.

Theorem 3.2
.

(Harder)
Let
k
and
\ell
be two non-negative integers.

i) The following two vanishing results hold:

\begin{array}[]{ll}H_{cusp}^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))=0&\textrm{unless}\;k\neq\ell\;\textrm{and either}\;i=1\;\mathrm{or}\;2.\\
H_{Eis}^{0}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))=0&\textrm{unless}\;k=\ell=0\;\text{when it is equal to}\;\C.\end{array}

ii) There are Hecke-equivariant isomorphisms.

H_{cusp}^{1}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,k}^{*}(\C))\cong H_{cusp}^{2}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,k}^{*}(\C))\cong S_{k,k}(\mathcal{K}_{f}),

H_{Eis}^{1}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\;:\;T(K)\backslash T(\A_{K})\rightarrow\C^{\times}\\
\text{of inf. type}\;[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]\end{subarray}}V_{\chi_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{f}},

Moreover, if
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
then

H_{Eis}^{2}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))\cong H^{2}(\partial\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\;:\;T(K)\backslash T(\A_{K})\rightarrow\C^{\times}\;\text{of inf. type}\\
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]\end{subarray}}V_{\chi_{f}}^{\mathcal{K}_{f}}.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that as in Remark
2.12
, if
\mathcal{K}_{f}=\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
then the above isomorphisms are
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}\otimes_{\Z}\C
-equivariant and when
\mathcal{K}_{f}=\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)
we have an
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}\otimes_{\Z}\C
-equivariant isomorphism.

Proof.

For all the results except the description of
H_{Eis}^{i}
for
i=1,2
, see
[
Tay88
, pag. 101]
. The result of
H_{Eis}^{1}
is a consequence of
[
Tay88
, p. 105]
, Remark
2.10
and Proposition
2.11
. The result of
H_{Eis}^{2}
comes from
[
Tay88
, p. 101]
combined with Proposition
2.11
.
∎

By Theorem
3.2
, the space of cuspidal Bianchi modular forms of weight
(k,\ell)
and level
\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
(which is defined analytically), corresponds precisely to the space of systems of Hecke eigenvalues
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}\otimes_{\Z}\C\rightarrow\C
appearing in
H_{cusp}^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{f}},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
(for each
i\in\{1,2\}
, by Theorem
3.2
part ii)). This motivates the following definition:

Definition 3.4
.

A
Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystem
of level
\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
and weight
(k,\ell)
is a system of Hecke eigenvalues appearing in
H_{Eis}^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n})},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(\C))
for some
i\in\{0,1,2\}
.

Remark 3.5
.

The index
i=0
in the above definition only appears when
(k,\ell)=(0,0)
. In the main results of this text we do not study this case.

Proposition
2.13
allows us to state the following result about Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}

\mathfrak{n}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{n}_{2}
respectively, satisfying

We start with the results in degree 1.

[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]
, then by part ii) of Theorem

3.3.

Points corresponding to Bianchi modular forms in the Bianchi eigenvariety are attached through the so called

We suppose that the conductor of

\mathfrak{m}

has level coprime to
p

-stabilizations of

-stabilizations of the theta series associated to

The Hecke polynomial of

at

]

-eigenvalue given by

and
\mathcal{F}^{\beta\alpha}
.

Denote by

and

the

-stabilizations of

corresponding to
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively. Then they correspond to the
p
-stabilizations

\mathcal{F}^{\alpha\alpha}
and

(see Section

).

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-algebra generated by the action of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}
on
H^{\bullet}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}})^{\leqslant h}
. The eigenvariety
\mathcal{E}_{c}
is constructed by gluing together the rigid analytic spectrums
\mathrm{Sp}(\bT_{\mathcal{U},h})
. The natural maps
\mathrm{Sp}(\bT_{\mathcal{U},h})\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
also glue to a map
\mathcal{E}_{c}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
.

4.3.
Results

Let
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
be as in Section
3.3
i.e.,
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are Hecke characters of
K
of conductors
\mathfrak{n}_{1},\mathfrak{n}_{2}
respectively such that
(\mathfrak{n}_{1},\mathfrak{n}_{2})=1
and
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\mathfrak{n}_{2}
. Moreover, we assume that either:

•

\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]
or

•

\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]
and
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
.

We fix a
p
-stabilization
\tilde{\phi}
of
\phi
, as defined in Section
3.3
. It is natural to ask if
\tilde{\phi}
determines a point in some of the eigenvarieties
\mathcal{E}
or
\mathcal{E}_{c}
. In affirmative cases then we would like to study the local geometry of the eigenvariety around these points. The following theorem treats these questions when
\tilde{\phi}
is ordinary i.e.,
v_{p}(x_{\mathfrak{p}})=v_{p}(x_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})=0
(see Section

).

Theorem 4.3
.

If
\tilde{\phi}
is the ordinary
p
-stabilization of
\phi
, then we have:

(1)

\tilde{\phi}
determines a point
x\in\mathcal{E}
and the weight map
\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
is etale at
x
.

(2)

\tilde{\phi}
determines a point
x_{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{c}
and the weight map
\mathcal{E}_{c}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}
is etale at
x_{c}
.

Proof.

The proof of (1) and (2) are completely analogue, thus we prove the theorem for the cohomology without support. Moreover, we will suppose that
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,0),(-1,\ell)]
. The case when
\phi
has infinity type
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]
with
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
is proved in the same way.

Recall the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{n},p}\otimes_{\Z}L
attached to
\tilde{\phi}
as in (
8
). As
\tilde{\phi}
is the ordinary
p
-stabilization of
\phi
then after localizing the map (
14
) at
\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}
, we obtain from Theorem
4.2
an isomorphism of
L
-vector spaces

\rho_{k,\ell}:H^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}\rightarrow H^{i}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{V}_{k,\ell}^{*}(L))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}

for each
i\in\N
. We deduce from Theorem
3.7
and Corollary
3.12
that
H^{\bullet}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}
is concentrated in degree
\bullet=1
and
\mathrm{dim}_{L}H^{1}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}=1
. From this, we obtain a point
x\in\mathcal{E}
attached to
\tilde{\phi}
.

We fix a slope adapted pair
(\mathcal{U},h)
and we consider the localization
(\bT_{\mathcal{U},h})_{x}
of
\bT_{\mathcal{U},h}
at

[

H^{\bullet}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}})^{\leqslant h}_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}

is a
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})_{(k,\ell)}
-module free of rank
1
. Then we observe that

(\bT_{\mathcal{U},h})_{x}\subset\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})}(H^{\bullet}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell})^{\leqslant h}_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}})\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})_{(k,\ell)}

is a
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})_{(k,\ell)}

(\bT_{\mathcal{U},h})_{x}\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})_{(k,\ell)}
and thus the map
\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}
is etale at

∎

Remark 4.6
.

(1)

These results are proved under the hypothesis that the conductor of

(2)

Taking
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
as in Remark

, then the theorem and corollary above proved the Corollary
1.5
for the base change points. Moreover, observe that in this case we allow

, thus in particular we consider eigenvalues attached to base change elliptic curves from
\Q
having complex multiplication by
K

Consider the base change Bianchi modular from
\mathcal{F}
from Section
3.1
and its four
p
-stabilizations
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha\alpha}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha\beta}
,

. The theorem above proved that around

the Bianchi eigenvariety is etale, then it is natural to ask what is happening around the other

p
-stabilizations determine a point in the eigenvariety and in the affirmative case how it is the geometry around such a point. The

-stabilization

Proposition 4.7
.

If
\mathcal{I}
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{E}
such that

(Sketch) Suppose infinitely many of these points are non-cuspidal points, then such points should be non-ordinary

4.4.
p
-adic
L
-functions of Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems

Our previous results allow the construction of
p
-adic
L
-functions attached to certain Bianchi Eisenstein eigensystems. Let
\tilde{\phi}
be the ordinary
p
-stabilization of
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
as in Section
4.3
and suppose that its infinity type is
[(k+1,\ell+1),(-1,-1)]
with
(k,\ell)\neq(0,0)
.

From Theorem
4.2
and Corollary
3.12
we deduce that for big enough finite extension
L
of
\Q_{p}
, the
L
-vector space

H^{1}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}
is one dimensional. We pick any non-zero class
\Psi_{\tilde{\phi}}\in H^{1}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\tilde{\phi}}}
.

From
[
BSW21a
, §2.4]
we have the Mellin transform

\mathrm{Mel}_{(k,\ell)}:H^{1}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{k,\ell})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}),L),

where
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})=K^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid p}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}
.

Definition 4.9
.

The
p
-adic
L
-function of
\tilde{\phi}
is the locally analytic distribution

L_{p}(\tilde{\phi}):=\mathrm{Mel}_{(k,\ell)}(\Psi_{\tilde{\phi}})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}),L).

Proposition 4.10
.

The distribution
L_{p}(\tilde{\phi})
is bounded i.e., it is a measure over the
p
-adic group
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})
. Moreover,
L_{p}(\tilde{\phi})
can be deformed in a
2
-dimensional family of measures over
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})
.

Proof.

By exactly the same proof given in
[
Wil17
, Prop. 6.15]
we obtain that the distribution
L_{p}(\tilde{\phi})
is
(0,0)
-admissible. This is equivalent to say that
L_{p}(\tilde{\phi})
is bounded i.e. it is a measure.

Now let
(\mathcal{U},h)
be a slope adapted pair as in Corollary
4.5
. Then if
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathrm{Sp}(\bT_{\mathcal{U},h})
is the connected component passing through the point corresponding to
\tilde{\phi}
then
H^{1}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}})^{\leqslant h}\otimes_{\bT_{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
is a free
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-module of rank
1
. We pick a class

\Psi_{\mathcal{U}}\in H^{1}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}})^{\leqslant h}\otimes_{\bT_{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})\subset H^{1}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}})^{\leqslant h}

lifting
\Psi_{\tilde{\phi}}
.

Recall from
[
BSW21a
, §2.4]
we have the Mellin transform

\mathrm{Mel}_{\mathcal{U}}:H^{1}_{c}(Y_{\mathcal{K}_{1}(\mathfrak{n},p)},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}),\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})).

Thus, the distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{C}):=\mathrm{Mel}_{\mathcal{U}}(\Psi_{\mathcal{U}})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}),\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U}))
is a
2
-dimensional family of distributions over
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})
specializing to
L_{p}(\tilde{\phi})
at
(k,\ell)
.
∎

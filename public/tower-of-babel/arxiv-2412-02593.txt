Generalized Yamabe Flows
Jørgen Olsen Lye, Boris Vertman, Mannaim Gennaro Vitti

Abstract
. In this work we introduce a family of conformal flows generalizing the classical Yamabe flow. We prove that for a large class of such flows long-time existence holds, and the arguments are in fact simpler than in the classical case. Moreover, we establish convergence for the case of negative scalar curvature and expect a similar statement for the positive and the flat cases as well.

1.
Introduction and statement of the main results

1.1.
Weak and strong Yamabe conjectures

Let
M
be a compact manifold (in this paper always without boundary unless otherwise stated) of dimension
n\geq 3
.
Yamabe
[
24
]
suggested the following min-max procedure for producing Einstein metrics on
M
.
The procedure consists of two steps. For any Riemannian metric
g_{0}
on
M
,
find a minimum
g_{\min}
of the
Einstein-Hilbert functional

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}(g)\coloneqq\mathrm{Vol}_{g}^{\frac{2-n}{n}}\int_{M}S\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

in the conformal class of
[g_{0}]
while keeping the total volume fixed.
Here,
S=S(g)
denotes the scalar curvature of
g
,
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}
is the total volume of
M

with respect to
g
. One sets

\mathcal{E}(g_{\min})=\inf_{g\in[g_{0}]}\mathcal{E}(g)\eqqcolon Y(M,[g_{0}]),

where
Y(M,[g_{0}])
is known as the
Yamabe constant
.
As the second step, find a maximum
g
of the Einstein-Hilbert functional amongst all
g_{\min}
.
The resulting metric, if it exists, is an Einstein metric. Of course, the space of Riemannian metrics is not compact, so the above minimum and maximum may fail to exist.

The first step, existence of a minimiser of the Einstein-Hilbert action within a conformal class, became known as the
(strong) Yamabe conjecture
.
A necessary condition for minimising is of course to be a critical point. Critical points of the functional (
1.1
) within a conformal class are precisely constant scalar curvature metrics. Hence the

(weak)

Yamabe conjecture

was born: given a compact Riemannian manifold

, can one conformally change the metric

to make the scalar curvature constant?

The first proof of the (strong) conjecture was initiated by Yamabe

1.2.
Normalized Yamabe flow

An alternative tool for proving the conjecture is due to Hamilton
[
11
]
: the normalized Yamabe flow of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{0})
. This is a family
g\equiv g(t),t\in[0,T]
of Riemannian metrics on
M
such
that the following evolution equation holds

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g=-(S-\rho)g,\quad\rho\coloneqq\mathrm{Vol}_{g}^{-1}\int_{M}S\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g},\\
g(0)=g_{0}.\end{cases}

As above,
S=S(g)
is the scalar curvature of
g
,
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}
the total volume of
M
with respect to
g

and
\rho
is the average scalar curvature of
g
.
The normalization by
\rho
ensures that the total volume does not change along the flow.

The Yamabe flow (
1.2
) is the negative gradient flow of (
1.1
) within a conformal class, subject to the total volume being fixed. Since Yamabe initially wanted to find a minimum of (
1.1
), this is a natural approach to try.
Hamilton
[
11
]
showed the long time existence of (
1.2
).
Stationary points of (
1.2
) are clearly constant scalar curvature metrics. The hope of Hamilton was to show that (
1.2
) always converges as
t\to\infty
to a constant scalar curvature metric. This would then prove the (weak) Yamabe conjecture by parabolic methods. This may of course fail to be a global minimum of
(
1.1
)
, so this would not settle the (strong) Yamabe conjecture without further arguments.

1.4.
Generalized Yamabe flow and statement of the main results

In this work, we propose a modification of the Yamabe-flow (
1.2
) in the setting of smooth, compact manifolds. Let
(M,g_{0})
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly decreasing (
f^{\prime}<0
) and at least
C^{2}
(we will formulate more precise conditions below).

Definition 1.1
.

The
generalized (normalized) Yamabe flow
is a family
g=g(t),t\in[0,T)
of Riemannian metrics on
M
such
that the following evolution equation holds

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g=(f(S)-A)g,\quad A\coloneqq\mathrm{Vol}_{g}^{-1}\int_{M}f(S)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g},\\
g(0)=g_{0}.\end{cases}

The normalization by
A
ensures that the total volume does not change along the flow. The classical Yamabe flow (
1.2
) is the special case with
f(x)=-x
. Our new family of flows now includes flows with e.g.

f(x)=1/x
.

We are interested in flows staying within a given conformal class. More precisely, this means that there is a family of functions
u=u(t)>0
such that

(1.4)

g=u(t)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}.

In this paper we first establish short time existence of (
1.3
) and then have different results based on the sign of the inital scalar curvature
S_{0}=S(g_{0})
. We have simplified our discussion by only considering three cases;
S_{0}>0
,
S_{0}=0
, and
S_{0}<0
. We will refer to these as the
scalar positive
,
flat
, and
negative
cases respectively. A more exhaustive treatment would be to take
Y(M,[g_{0}])>0
,
Y(M,[g_{0}])=0
, and
Y(M,[g_{0}])<0
as the trichotomy instead. This would have been at the price of expositional clarity. By the solution of the strong Yamabe conjecture, one can always conformally change the metric to have constant scalar curvature
\mathrm{sgn}(Y(M,[g_{0}])
, so one could read our results as applying near this solution. Our first main result deals with short time existence.

Theorem 1.2

(Short time existence)
.

Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
u_{0}>0
. Assume
f
is strictly decreasing on
[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]
. Then there is a time
T>0
and a unique
u(t)\in C^{\infty}((0,T)\times M)
,
u(t)>0
solving (
1.3
). The existence time
T
can be extended as long as
f
stays strictly decreasing on
[S_{\min},S_{\max}]
and as long as
u
stays bounded in
C^{2,\alpha}(M)
.

There are two reasons why one needs
f
to be strictly decreasing. The first is to ensure the evolution equation is parabolic. Indeed, from the linearization one sees that (
1.3
) is strictly parabolic if and only if
f
is strictly decreasing. The second reason is that
f
should at least be injective. This will ensure that a stationary solution,
0=\partial_{t}g=(f(S)-A)g
implies
S=f^{-1}(A)
is constant.

Our next main results deal with long time existence.

Theorem 1.3

(Scalar negative case)
.

Assume
S_{0}<0
and that
f
is strictly decreasing on
[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M)
be positive. Then there is a unique solution
u\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times M)
to (
1.3
). As
t\to\infty
,
g(t)=u(t)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
converges to a metric with constant negative scalar curvature.

Theorem 1.4

(Scalar flat and positive)
.

Assume that either
S_{0}=0
or that
S_{0}>0
everywhere. Assume
f
is bounded from below and that
f
is strictly decreasing on
\left[\min\{S_{0,\min},0\},\infty\right)
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M)
be positive. Then there there is a unique, positive solution
u\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times M)
to (
1.3
).

We also offer a partial result towards the long time existence in the scalar flat and positive cases under milder assumptions than boundedness of
f
. See also the discussion in Section
6
for some thoughts about pushing this further to also give an upper bound on
S
along the flow.

Proposition 1.5

(Flat case)
.

Assume that
S_{0}=0
and that
f
is strictly decreasing. Let
u\in C^{\infty}((0,T)\times M)
be a solution to the generalized Yamabe flow. Then

S_{\min}\geq S_{0,\min}

and there are constants which do not depend on
T
such that

c^{-1}\leq u\leq c.

Proposition 1.6

(Positive case)
.

Assume
S_{0}\geq 0
and that
f
is strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Assume there are constants
a,b\geq 0
such that
f(x)\geq-ax^{\frac{n}{2}}-b
for
x\geq 0
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M)
be positive and let
u\in C^{\infty}((0,T)\times M)
be the solution to (
1.3
) for some
T<\infty
. Then there is a constant
C_{T}>0
such that

C_{T}\leq u\leq\frac{1}{C_{T}},\quad S_{\min}\geq S_{0,\min}e^{-C_{T}}.

The structure of the paper is as follows. We start by going over some generalities of conformal flows in section
2
. Here we also deduce several evolution equations. In section
3.2
we show the short time existence of the flow by studying its linearization. The sections
4
,
5
and
6
are dedicated to the scalar negative, flat and positive case respectively.

2.
Fundamental properties of generalized Yamabe flows

Our setting is still a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{0})
of dimension
n\geq 3
. We will for convenience assume
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}=1
. Let
f
be some
C^{2}
-function and let
S
denote the scalar curvature of
g
.
We consider the generalised Yamabe flow as defined in (
1.3
), namely

\partial_{t}g=(f(S)-A)g,\quad g(0)=g_{0},\quad A\coloneqq\mathrm{Vol}_{g}^{-1}\int_{M}f(S)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

Without further assumptions on
f
, we neither know the existence nor uniqueness of solutions to (
1.3
). But assuming for the moment that such a flow exists, one can deduce several useful evolution equations of other geometric quantities like the volume form and the scalar curvature:

(1)

In terms of the conformal factor
u
, the flow equation (
1.3
) is

(2.1)

\partial_{t}u=\frac{n-2}{4}(f(S)-A)\cdot u.

(2)

The volume element evolves according to

(2.2)

\partial_{t}d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}=\frac{n}{2}(f(S)-A)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

(3)

Since the metrics
g
and
g_{0}
are related via (
1.4
) one can write down a neat formula for how the scalar curvatures relate, namely

(2.3)

S=u^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(S_{0}\cdot u-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta_{0}u\right)\eqqcolon u^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}L(u).

Here the (negative) Laplacian
\Delta_{0}
is with respect to the initial metric
g_{0}
and
L
is called the conformal Laplacian.

We will sometimes use the abreviation

f_{0}(S)\coloneqq f(S)-A.

Lemma 2.1
.

The scalar curvature evolves along (
1.3
) according to

(2.4)

\partial_{t}S=-(n-1)u^{-\frac{4}{n-2}}\left(\Delta_{0}f(S)+2\frac{\left\langle\nabla f(S),\nabla u\right\rangle}{u}\right)-Sf_{0}(S).

The inner product is with respect to the initial metric
g_{0}
.
Writing
\Delta
for the Laplacian of
g
, this can also be written

(2.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S

\displaystyle=-(n-1)\Delta f(S)-Sf(S)+AS

(2.6)

\displaystyle=-(n-1)\left(f^{\prime}(S)\Delta S+f^{\prime\prime}(S)|\nabla S|^{2}_{g}\right)-Sf(S)+AS.

Proof.

Both
\Delta_{0}
and
S_{0}
in (
2.3
) are
t
-independent, so one deduces

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ConfScalarCurv}}}{{=}}-\frac{n+2}{n-2}\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}S+u^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(S_{0}\partial_{t}u-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta_{0}\partial_{t}u\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:uEq}}}{{=}}-\frac{n+2}{4}f_{0}(S)S+u^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(\frac{n-2}{4}S_{0}uf_{0}(S)-(n-1)\Delta_{0}(f_{0}(S)u)\right).

We use (
2.3
) to write
S_{0}u=Su^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}+\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta_{0}u
and insert this back into the above expression.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S

\displaystyle=-\frac{n+2}{4}f_{0}(S)S+\frac{n-2}{4}f_{0}(S)S-(n-1)u^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(\Delta_{0}(f_{0}(S)u)-f_{0}(S)\Delta u\right)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=-f_{0}(S)S-(n-1)u^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(u\Delta_{0}f(S)+2\left\langle\nabla f(S),\nabla u\right\rangle\right),

where we have also observed in the last step that
\nabla f_{0}=\nabla f
.
The rewriting makes use of the formula

(2.8)

\Delta\xi=u^{-\frac{4}{n-2}}\left(\Delta_{0}\xi+\frac{2}{u}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla\xi\right\rangle\right),

which valid for any smooth function
\xi
.
∎

We note some immediate consequences via the maximum principle.

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume
f^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
. Let
S_{\min}(t)
and
S_{\max}(t)
denote minima and maxima of
S
at a given time
t
.
Then the following bounds hold.

•

If
S_{\max}\leq 0
, then
\partial_{t}S_{\max}\leq 0
.

•

If
S_{\min}\leq 0
, then
\partial_{t}S_{\min}\geq 0
.

•

If
S_{0,\min}\geq 0
, then
S_{\min}\geq 0
(but not necessarily
\partial_{t}S_{\min}\geq 0
).

Proof.

The evolution equation (
2.5
) asserts

\partial_{t}S=-(n-1)\Delta f(S)-S(f(S)-A).

Since
f
is decreasing,
S_{\min}
is a maximum for
f(S)
. Similarly,

S_{\max}
is a minimum for
f(S)
. Hence
\Delta f(S_{\min})\leq 0
and

\Delta f(S_{\max})\geq 0
and we conclude

(2.9)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}S_{\min}\geq-S_{\min}(f(S_{\min})-A)),\\
&\partial_{t}S_{\max}\leq-S_{\max}(f(S_{\max})-A)).\end{split}

Since
f
is decreasing we get
f(S_{\max})\leq f(S)\leq f(S_{\min})
, hence also
f(S_{\max})\leq A\leq f(S_{\min})
.
This shows that

(2.10)

f(S_{\min})-A\geq 0,\quad f(S_{\max})-A\leq 0.

This shows the first two statements by studying the signs of
the terms on the right hand side of (
2.9
). Note that
S_{\min}\geq 0

does not imply
\partial_{t}S_{\min}\geq 0
. Hence the third statement follows
by a different argument. Namely, we let

\omega(t)\coloneqq S_{\min}(t)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}(f(S_{\min})-A)\,d\tau\right).

The differential inequality (
2.9
) for
\partial_{t}S_{\min}
then says

\partial_{t}\omega\geq 0
, hence

S_{\min}(t)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}(f(S_{\min})-A)\,d\tau\right)=\omega(t)\geq\omega(0)=S_{0,\min}.

This says that
S_{\min}
stays non-negative as long as the flow exists.
∎

We obtain a couple of immediate consequences.

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume the metric evolves according to (
2.1
) with
f^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
.

(1)

Assume that the initial scalar curvature
S_{0}
is everywhere non-positive.
Then the scalar curvature
S
stays non-positive along the flow.

(2)

Assume that the initial scalar curvature
S_{0}
is everywhere non-negative.
Then the scalar curvature
S
stays non-negative along the flow.

If one further assumes
f
to be bounded, one also gets an upper bound on
S
even if
S_{0,\max}>0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume the metric evolves according to (
2.1
) with
f^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
and that
f
is bounded. Assume
S_{0,\max}>0
. Then there is a constant
C>0
depending on
f
such that

S_{\max}\leq S_{0,\max}e^{Ct}.

Proof.

Shifting
f
by a constant does not change the flow, so we may and do assume
f\geq 0
.
From (
2.9
),

\partial_{t}\log S_{\max}\leq-f(S_{\max})+A\leq A\leq C,

where
C
is an upper bound for
f
. Integrating this gives the claim.
∎

3.
Short-time existence of generalized Yamabe flows

In this section, we show that the generalized Yamabe flow (
1.3
) exists, at least for sufficiently short time. We offer two arguments. The first is a reference to ”standard parabolic theory”. The other,
discussed in the appendix, section
A
, is an idea for a more complete proof, relying only on linear parabolic theory, which may be the key to study generalized Yamabe flows in non-compact settings.

3.1.
Short-time existence using standard parabolic theory

The most general statement we could find which applies in our setting is the following.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
22
, Proposition 15.8.1]
)
.

Assume the problem

\partial_{t}u=F(t,x,D^{2}_{x}u),\quad u(0)=u_{0}

is strongly parabolic with
u_{0}\in H^{s}(M)
,
s>\frac{n}{2}+2
. Then there is a unique solution

u\in C([0,T),H^{s}(M))\cap C^{\infty}((0,T)\times M),

which persists as long as
\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{C^{2+r}(M)}
is bounded, given
r>0
.

This will be applicable to the generalized Yamabe problem, since it is strongly parabolic as long as
f^{\prime}(S)\leq-c<0
holds. We will show this below when we compute the linearization. Since we are assuming
f
to be strictly descreasing, we get such a bound on any compact interval.

3.2.
Non-Normalized Flow

Our goal is to ensure short-time existence for the normalized flow (
2.1
). For this, it turns out to be easier to work without the non-local term
A
. We can recover the normalised flow from the non-normalised flow by an extra argument which we sketch at the end.

The argument for moving between the normalized and non-normalized flows becomes easier if one assumes
f
is
\alpha-
homogeneous, by which we mean that there is
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f(\lambda x)-f(\lambda y)=\lambda^{\alpha}(f(x)-f(y))
for all
\lambda>0
and
x,y
(of course,
x,y,\lambda x,\lambda y
have to be in the domain of
f
). In this case, we can use a trick by Hamilton,
and we want to explain this next.

Suppose that
v
is a solution of the flow

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v=\frac{n-2}{4}f(R)\cdot v\\
v(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

where
R
denotes the scalar curvature of
\tilde{g}=v^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
. We refer to the latter flow as the
non-normalized flow
. The idea is to rescale both the solution
v
and the time
t
. Define
\eta
to be

\eta(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(M)}\int_{M}f(R)dVol_{g}d\tau.

Let
u=\exp\left(-\frac{n-2}{4}\eta\right)v
. Let
S
denote the scalar curvature of
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
. Since

g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}=e^{-\eta}v^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}=e^{-\eta}\tilde{g}

and
\eta
only depends on time, we have
S=e^{\eta}R
. Then we compute

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{t}v-\frac{n-2}{4}\eta^{\prime}v\right)\exp\left(-\frac{n-2}{4}\eta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{4}\left(f(R)v-\eta^{\prime}v\right)\exp\left(-\frac{n-2}{4}\eta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{4}\left(f(R)-\eta^{\prime}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{n-2}{4}\eta\right)v

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{4}\left(f(Se^{-\eta})-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(M)}\int_{M}f(Se^{-\eta})dVol_{g}\right)u.

We now use the assumption that
f
is
\alpha-
homogeneous (which we do not generally assume - it is just to demonstrate Hamilton’s trick).
Multiply both sides by
e^{\alpha\eta}
and find

e^{\alpha\eta}\partial_{t}u=\frac{n-2}{4}(f(S)-A)u.

Rescaling the time by letting
\tau
be the solution of
\frac{d\tau}{dt}=e^{-\alpha\eta(t)}
,
\tau(0)=0
,
we get a solution to the normalized flow

\partial_{\tau}u=\frac{n-2}{4}(f(S)-A)u.

3.3.
Linearization of the flow

Let us consider the non-normalized flow

(3.1)

\partial_{t}u=f(S)\cdot u,\quad u(0)=u_{0}.

Let
\beta\coloneqq\frac{n+2}{n-2}
and
c_{n}\coloneqq\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}
. In (
2.3
) we saw

(3.2)

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle u^{-\beta}L(u),

\displaystyle L=

\displaystyle S_{0}-c_{n}\Delta_{0}.

Lemma 3.2

(Frechét derivative)
.

Consider
\mathcal{F}(u)=f(S)\cdot u
. Then the Frechét derivative of
\mathcal{F}
is

(3.3)

D\mathcal{F}(u)=f\left(S\right)+f^{\prime}\left(S\right)\left(u^{1-\beta}L-\beta S\right).

Proof.

In order to linearize (
3.1
) we replace
u
by
u+h
and expand everything in powers of
h
. For instance

(3.4)

(u+h)^{-\beta}=u^{-\beta}-\beta u^{-(\beta+1)}h+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

Also, from (
3.4
), we compute

\displaystyle(u+h)^{-\beta}L(u+h)=u^{-\beta}L(u)+u^{-(\beta+1)}(uL(h)-\beta hL(u))+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

Computing the Taylor expansion of
f
around
u^{-\beta}L(u)
gives us

\displaystyle f\left((u+h)^{-\beta}L(u+h)\right)(u+h)

\displaystyle=f\left(u^{-\beta}L(u)\right)(u+h)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})

\displaystyle+f^{\prime}\left(u^{-\beta}L(u)\right)\left(u^{-(\beta+1)}(uL(h)-\beta hL(u))\right)u.

From (
3.2
) we conclude

\displaystyle f\left((u+h)^{-\beta}L(u+h)\right)(u+h)-f\left(u^{-\beta}L(u)\right)u

\displaystyle=f\left(S\right)h+f^{\prime}\left(S\right)\left(u^{1-\beta}L(h)-\beta Sh\right)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}),

and since
\mathcal{F}(u)=f(S)\cdot u
, the Fréchet derivative is

D\mathcal{F}(u)h=f\left(S\right)h+f^{\prime}\left(S\right)\left(u^{1-\beta}L(h)-\beta Sh\right).

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

The flow (
3.1
) is strictly parabolic as long as
f^{\prime}(S)\leq-c<0
.

Proof.

A non-linear PDE is called strictly parabolic if its linearization is strictly parabolic. From (
3.3
), the linearization has
-c_{n}f^{\prime}(S)u^{1-\beta}\Delta_{0}
as highest order differentiation.
∎

3.4.
The normalised flow

At the start of this section, we explained how to get a normalized solution if the function
f
is
\alpha-
homogeneous. We do not generally assume this, so we give an argument to incorporate the normalization. The most direct strategy would be to redo the linearization argument with the additional term
-Au
dragged along. The linearization can be deduced as follows. Write

A(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}}\int_{M}f(S)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g},\quad D\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(h)\coloneqq\frac{2n}{n-2}\int_{M}hu^{-1}d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

Let
\mathcal{N}(u)=A(u)u.
Then

\displaystyle D\mathcal{N}(h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}}\left(-D\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(h)A(u)+\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)(u^{1-\beta}L(h)-\beta Sh)\frac{}{}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{}{}+\frac{2n}{n-2}f(S)hu^{-1}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}\right)u+A(u)h.

This has the structure of

h\mapsto\int_{M}\mathcal{D}(h)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}+A(u)h,

where
\mathcal{D}
is an elliptic operator. The normalized flow therefore linearizes to

\partial_{t}h=\mathcal{P}h+\int_{M}\mathcal{D}(h)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

for the elliptic operator
\mathcal{P}=D\mathcal{F}(u)+A(u)
.

4.
Long-time existence and convergence: the negative case

The goal of this section is to show the long-time existence and convergence of the generalized Yamabe flow when the initial scalar curvature
S_{0}
is negative. We need the framework of Hölder spaces which we now recall.

Definition 4.1

(Spaces)
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
(M,g_{0})
a compact, smooth manifold of dimension

The Hölder spaces

and

\displaystyle C^{\alpha}([0,T]\times M)\coloneqq\left\{u\in C([0,T]\times M)\,\colon\,\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\alpha}([0,T]\times M)}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle C^{2,\alpha}\left([0,T]\times M\right)\coloneqq\left\{w\in C^{\alpha}\left([0,T]\times M\right)\,\colon\,\Delta_{0}w\in C^{\alpha}\left([0,T]\times M\right)\right\},

where the latter space is equipped with the norm
2
2

2

This is equivalent to the usual definition of
C^{2,\alpha}
by elliptic regularity, e.g.
[
10

.

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}([0,T]\times M)}\coloneqq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\alpha}([0,T]\times M)}+\left\lVert\Delta_{0}u\right\rVert_{C^{\alpha}([0,T]\times M)}.

The spaces
C^{\alpha}(M)
and
C^{2,\alpha}(M)
are defined in the same way with
T=0
.
We shall also need the parabolic Hölder space

C^{2+\alpha}([0,T]\times M)\coloneqq\left\{u\in C^{2,\alpha}([0,T]\times M)\,\colon\,\partial_{t}u\in C^{\alpha}([0,T]\times M)\right\}

with the norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{2+\alpha}}\coloneqq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}([0,T]\times M)}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}u\right\rVert_{C^{\alpha}([0,T]\times M)}.

Finally, we need the open set

(4.1)

\mathcal{O}\coloneqq\left\{w\in C^{2,\alpha}\left(M\right)\,\colon\,\exists\,c,\tilde{c}>0:w>c\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ f^{\prime}(w^{-\beta}Lw)<-\tilde{c}\right\}.

4.1.
Uniform estimates of solutions

Throughout this section we use the following notation:

\displaystyle S_{0,\min}\coloneqq\min_{p\in M}S_{0}(p)

\displaystyle S_{0,\max}\coloneqq\max_{p\in M}S_{0}(p).

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
S_{0}
is everywhere negative and let
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be strictly decreasing on
[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]
. Then there exist positive constants
B,C
such that any solution of the generalized Yamabe flow in
C^{2+\alpha}([0,T)\times M)
satisfies

\left\lVert f(S)-A\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq Ce^{-Bt}.

The constants
B,C
are independent of
T
.

Proof.

From Corollary
2.2
, we know that
S\in[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]
for any time. Applying the maximum and minimum principle to (
2.6
) tells us

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S_{\max}

\displaystyle\leq-S_{\max}\left(f(S_{\max})-A)\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S_{\min}

\displaystyle\geq-S_{\min}\left(f(S_{\min})-A)\right).

As in the proof of Corollary
2.2
,
f(S_{\max})-A\leq 0
and
f(S_{\min})-A\geq 0
, thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(S_{\min}-S_{\max})

\displaystyle\geq S_{\max}\left(f(S_{\max})-A)\right)-S_{\min}\left(f(S_{\min})-A)\right)

\displaystyle\geq S_{0,\max}\left(f(S_{\max})-A)\right)-S_{\min}\left(f(S_{\min})-A)\right)

\displaystyle=S_{0,\max}(f(S_{\max})-f(S_{\min}))+(S_{0,\max}-S_{\min})(f(S_{\min})-A)

\displaystyle\geq S_{0,\max}(f(S_{\max})-f(S_{\min})).

By the mean value theorem there exist
\xi_{t}\in(S_{\min},S_{\max})\subset[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]
such that
\displaystyle{f^{\prime}(\xi_{t})=\frac{f(S_{\max})-f(S_{\min})}{S_{\max}-S_{\min}}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(S_{\min}-S_{\max})\geq S_{0,\max}f^{\prime}(\xi_{t})(S_{\max}-S_{\min}).

Let

-c=\max_{\xi\in[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]}f^{\prime}(\xi),\quad B\coloneqq-cS_{0,\max}.

Note that
B
is positive since both
S_{0}
and
f^{\prime}
are negative. So

\partial_{t}(\ln{(S_{\max}-S_{\min})})\leq-B.

Integrating this inequality from
0
to
t
and applying the exponential we conclude

(4.2)

(S_{\max}-S_{\min})(t)\leq(S_{0,\max}-S_{0,\min})e^{-Bt}=c_{0}e^{-Bt}

with
c_{0}\coloneqq S_{0,\max}-S_{0,\min}
. Since
f
is decreasing,

\displaystyle f(S_{\max})\leq

\displaystyle f(S)\leq f(S_{\min}),

\displaystyle-f(S_{\min})\leq

\displaystyle-A\leq-f(S_{\max}).

So

-(f(S_{\min})-f(S_{\max}))\leq f(S)-A\leq f(S_{\min})-f(S_{\max}).

From (
4.2
), and the mean value theorem again, we conclude

|f(S)-A|\leq|f(S_{\max})-f(S_{\min}|=|f^{\prime}(\xi_{t})|(S_{\max}-S_{\min})\leq c_{0}|f^{\prime}(\xi_{t})|e^{-Bt}.

Take
\displaystyle{C=\max_{\xi\in[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]}|f^{\prime}(\xi)|\cdot c_{0}}
and conclude

\left\lVert f(S)-A\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq Ce^{-Bt}.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume
S_{0}
is everywhere negative and let
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be strictly decreasing on
[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]
.
Suppose that
u
is a solution of the generalized Yamabe flow in
C^{2+\alpha}([0,T)\times M)
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
, depending on
u(0)
,
A(0)
,
S_{0,\max}
,
S_{0,\min}
but not on
T
, such that
c^{-1}\leq u(p,t)\leq c
for all
p\in M
and
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

Since
u_{0}
is positive, we know that
u
remains positive for all
t
small enough.
From the generalized Yamabe flow, we find

\partial_{t}\ln{u}=\frac{n-2}{4}(f(S)-A).

Hence, from Proposition
4.2
,

|\partial_{t}\ln{u}|\leq\frac{n-2}{4}Ce^{-Bt}

i.e,

-\frac{n-2}{4}Ce^{-Bt}\leq\partial_{t}\ln{u}\leq\frac{n-2}{4}Ce^{-Bt}.

Integrating over
[0,t]
gives us

-\frac{(n-2)C}{4B}\left(1-e^{-Bt}\right)\leq\ln{\frac{u(t)}{u(0)}}\leq\frac{(n-2)C}{4B}\left(1-e^{-Bt}\right).

Therefore,

\left|\ln{\frac{u}{u(0)}}\right|\leq\frac{(n-2)C}{4B},

i.e.

(4.3)

u(0)e^{-\frac{(n-2)C}{4B}}\leq u(t)\leq u(0)e^{\frac{(n-2)C}{4B}}.

If
u
were to not remain positive during the flow, there would
\tilde{T}<T
such that
u(t)>0
for all
t\in[0,\tilde{T})
and
u_{\min}(\tilde{T})=0
. Proceeding as before, we obtain the inequality (
4.3
) for
u(\tilde{T})
. Hence
u_{\min}(\tilde{T})>0
, a contradiction. So
u
remains positive during the flow.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume the setup of Proposition
4.3
. Then there exists a constant
\tilde{c}>0
, depending on
u(0)
,
A(0)
,
S_{0,\min}
,
S_{0,\max}
but independent of

, such that
\left\lVert\partial_{t}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq\tilde{c}e^{-Bt}
.

Proof.

The generalized Yamabe flow equation is

\partial_{t}u=\frac{n-2}{4}(f(S)-A)\cdot u,

and we have derived uniform bounds on both factors on the right hand side.
∎

4.2.
Long-time existence

We are ready to complete the long-time existence argument.

Theorem 4.5

(Long-time existence)
.

Assume the setup of Proposition
4.3
. Then the generalized Yamabe flow exists for all time.

Proof.

The idea is to show that if the maximal existence time
T
were finite, the limit
\lim_{t\to T}u(t)
exists and is in
\mathcal{O}
(defined in (
4.1
)). So one can restart the flow, contradicting the maximality of
T
. We already have uniform
L^{\infty}
bounds on
u
and
u^{-1}
by Proposition
4.3
. We also know by Corollary
2.2
that
S\in[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]
, so
f^{\prime}(S)\leq-c
uniformly in time. What remains to show are uniform
C^{2,\alpha}(M)
-bounds on
u
. We will show these in three steps.

Step 1 –
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(M)
uniformly:

By (
2.3
), we may write

(4.4)

-c_{n}\Delta_{0}u=u^{\beta}S-S_{0}u.

The right hand side is uniformly bounded. So

u\in W^{2,p}(M)\coloneqq\{w\in L^{p}(M)\,\colon\,\Delta_{0}w\in L^{p}(M)\}

uniformly for any
p\geq 1
. By the Sobolev embedding theorem (more precisely, Morrey’s inequality),
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(M)
uniformly for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Step 2 –
S\in C^{\alpha}(M)
uniformly:

Multiply the evolution equation for
S
, (
2.5
), by
f^{\prime}(S)
to deduce

(4.5)

(\partial_{t}+(n-1)f^{\prime}(S)\Delta)f(S)=-Sf^{\prime}(S)(f(S)-A).

Since
f^{\prime}(S)\leq-c
uniformly, the right hand side is uniformly bounded. From (
2.8
)

f^{\prime}(S)\Delta\xi=u^{-\frac{4}{n-2}}f^{\prime}(S)\left(\Delta_{0}\xi+\frac{2}{u}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla\xi\right\rangle\right)

thus the operator
(n-1)f^{\prime}(S)\Delta
is uniformly elliptic, since by
Step 1

u\in C^{1,\alpha}(M)
uniformly and
f^{\prime}(S)\leq-c
uniformly. So the PDE (
4.5
) is parabolic with uniformly bounded coefficients. By Krylov-Safanov estimates,
[
13
, Theorem 4.3]
, and the compactness of
M
, we deduce that
f(S)
is uniformly in
C^{\alpha}(M)
. By the mean value theorem there exist
\xi_{t}\in(S(t,p),S(t,q))\subset[S_{0,\min},S_{0,\max}]
such that
\displaystyle{f^{\prime}(\xi_{t})(S(t,p)-S(t,q))=f(S(t,p))-f(S(t,q))}
. Thus,

|(S(t,p)-S(t,q))|\leq\frac{1}{c}|f(S(t,p))-f(S(t,q))|,

and

\displaystyle||S(t)||_{C^{\alpha}(M)}

\displaystyle=||S(t)||_{L^{\infty}(M)}+\sup_{p\neq q}\frac{|S(t,p)-S(t,q)|}{d(p,q)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq||S(t)||_{L^{\infty}(M)}+\frac{1}{c}\sup_{p\neq q}\frac{|f(S(t,p))-f(S(t,q))|}{d(p,q)^{\alpha}}<\infty,

so
S
is also uniformly
C^{\alpha}
.

Step 3 –
\Delta_{0}u\in C^{\alpha}(M)
uniformly:

Returning to (
4.4
), we conclude that
\Delta_{0}u
is uniformly
C^{\alpha}
.

Let
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. Then
C^{\alpha}(M)\subset C^{\alpha^{\prime}}(M)
compactly, so there is a subsequence
t_{k}\to T
such that
u(t_{k})
converges. Call this limit
u(T)
. By the uniform bounds,
u
is continous up to
T
, hence does not depend on the chosen subsequence. The limit is in
\mathcal{O}
by the above considerations.
∎

4.3.
Convergence of the generalized Yamabe flow

Theorem 4.6

(The scalar negative case)
.

Assume that
g_{0}
is a metric with scalar curvature
S_{0}
everywhere negative and suppose that
u(t)
is the solution to generalized Yamabe flow (

) that exists for all time. Then the associated metric
g(t)=u(t)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
converges to a metric with constant negative scalar curvature.

Proof.

The associated metric
g(t)=u(t)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
solves the flow

\partial_{t}g=(f(S)-A)g.

Since the flow exists for all time, Proposition
4.2
says

at an exponential rate. Since

is uniformly bounded, also
\partial_{t}g\rightarrow 0

, and the conformal factor

admits a continuous pointwise limit

as

. Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem

we conclude

and then the limit metric

admits a well defined scalar curvature. Since

vanishes in the limit

, we conclude that the scalar curvature of the limit metric must be constant, since
f
is invertible with a

inverse on the interval

. The limiting sectional curvature is negative by Corollary
2.2
.
∎

5.
Long-time existence: the flat case

The goal of this section is to show the long-time existence of the generalized Yamabe flow when the initial metric

Important notation:

5.1.
Uniform estimates of solutions

Lemma 5.1

.

S_{\min}\geq S_{0,\min}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}(A-f(S_{\min}))\,d\tau\right)\geq S_{0,\min}e^{at},

where we have used
f(0)=0\implies f(S)\leq 0
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Assume
A(t)\geq a
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Then there is a constant
C(T)>0
such that
\frac{1}{C(T)}\leq u\leq C(T)
for
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Step 1 – upper bound on
u
:

The evolution equation for
u
, (
2.1
), says

\partial_{t}u=\frac{n-2}{4}(f(S)-A)u.

Since
f
is decreasing and
S\geq 0
, we can bound the right hand side by
f(S)-A\leq f(0)-a\eqqcolon c
. Integrating, we find for
t\in[0,T]

u\leq u_{0}\exp\left(\frac{n-2}{4}cT\right).

Step 2 – lower bound on
u
:

Recall that (
2.3
) says

-c_{n}\Delta_{0}u+S_{0}u=u^{\beta}S,

where
\beta=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
and
c_{n}=\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}
. Let
\alpha>0
. Then

-c_{n}\Delta_{0}u^{-\alpha}=\alpha c_{n}u^{-\alpha-1}\Delta_{0}u-c_{n}\alpha(\alpha-1)u^{-\alpha-2}|\nabla u|^{2}_{g_{0}}\leq\alpha c_{n}u^{-\alpha-1}\Delta_{0}u.

By the above,

\alpha c_{n}u^{-\alpha-1}\Delta_{0}u=\alpha\left(S_{0}u^{-\alpha}-u^{\beta-\alpha-1}S\right)\leq\alpha S_{0}u^{-\alpha}.

So

-c_{n}\Delta_{0}u^{-\alpha}\leq\alpha S_{0}u^{-\alpha}.

The function
v=u^{-\alpha}
is therefore a subsolution to an elliptic equation,

(c_{n}\Delta_{0}+\alpha S_{0})v\geq 0.

By the Harnack inequality,
[
10
, Theorem 8.17]
for instance
4
4

4

The quoted theorem is for a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
M
is compact, we can cover it by finitely many sets diffeomorphic to balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and apply the theorem to each ball.
, we get a bound

u^{-\alpha}\leq C(u^{-\alpha})_{\min}=Cu_{\max}^{-\alpha}.

for some constant
C>0
. The right hand side is bounded since the volume is constant along the flow;

1=\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(M)=\int_{M}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\leq u_{\max}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\int d\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}=u_{\max}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}.

So

u^{-\alpha}\leq C.

∎

A corollary of these results is that if
S_{0}>0
, then
f(S)=-S^{\kappa}
is an allowable function for any
1\leq\kappa\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

6.1.
Upper bound on the scalar curvature when

is bounded

with

. Changing

.

.

Assume

Proof.

We did the lower bound above. The upper bound is Lemma

Remark 6.8

The constant

Since

Lemma 6.9
.

The solution

By Assumption

Proof of Theorem

6.2.
Some ideas when not assuming

We end the section with some ideas of what to try if one does not assume

to be bounded. If one keeps Assumption

, one only has to get an upper bound on the scalar curvature. We will additionally assume the Yamabe constant to be positive

5

5

This is not a big assumption. If

[

We suggest the following steps. Prove:

(1)

There is

such that

(2)

There is the following Sobolev inequality. There are time-independent constants

such that

(6.1)

holds for any

.

(3)

For any

such that

(4)

For any

We do not attempt to prove the steps (1) and (4) here. We suspect one could get them from the evolution equations of Appendix

as in the classical case (see
[

, pp. 68-71]
,
[

, Lemma 2.3, Proposition 2.6]
). This is somewhat intricate as the evolution equations are messier for the generalized Yamabe flow. Another idea to get (4) from (1)-(3) is to use Moser iteration similarly to
[

, Theorem 4.1]

Lemma 6.10

(Step (2))
.

Let
q>\frac{n}{2}
. Assume there is a time-independent constant
C>0
such that
\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{q}(M,g)}\leq C
. Then there are time-independent constants
A,B>0
such that

A\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(M,g)}\leq\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M,g)}+B\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M,g)}

holds for any
\phi\in H^{1}(M,g)
.

Proof.

The conformal invariance of the Yamabe constant
Y=Y(M,[g_{0}])
gives us a similar bound:

(6.2)

Y\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(M,g)}^{2}\leq c_{n}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M,g)}+\int_{M}S\phi^{2}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

We estimate the last term using the Hölder inequality with
q
and
p=\frac{q}{q-1}
;

\int_{M}S\phi^{2}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}\leq\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{q}(M,g)}\left\lVert\phi^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(M,g)}\leq C\left\lVert\phi^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(M,g)},

where we have inserted the assumed bound on
\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{q}(M,g)}
. Since
q>\frac{n}{2}
, we have
1<p<\frac{n}{n-2}
, and we may interpolate between the
L^{1}
and the
L^{\frac{n}{n-2}}
-norms as follows
6
6

6

The general statement is this. Fix
p_{0}<p<p_{1}
and choose
\theta
so that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
. Then
\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{p_{0}}}^{1-\theta}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{\theta}_{L^{p_{1}}}
, and one checks this by applying the Hölder inequality to
\phi=\phi^{1-\theta}\phi^{\theta}
.

\left\lVert\phi^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(M,g)}\leq\left\lVert\phi^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(M,g)}^{1-\theta}\left\lVert\phi^{2}\right\rVert^{\theta}_{L^{\frac{n}{n-2}}(M,g)},

where
\theta=\frac{n}{2q}<1
. To this product we apply Young’s inequality
ab\leq\theta(\epsilon^{\theta}a)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+(1-\theta)(\epsilon^{-\theta}b)^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}}
for any
\epsilon>0
to deduce

\left\lVert\phi^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(M,g)}\leq\theta\epsilon\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(M,g)}+(1-\theta)\epsilon^{-\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M,g)}.

Inserting this back into (
6.2
) leaves us with

Y\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(M,g)}\leq c_{n}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M,g)}+C\left(\theta\epsilon\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(M,g)}+(1-\theta)\epsilon^{-\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M,g)}\right),

which can be written as

\left(Y-C\theta\epsilon\right)\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(M,g)}\leq c_{n}\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M,g)}+C(1-\theta)\epsilon^{-\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M,g)}.

Choosing
\epsilon
small enough ensures the left hand side is non-negative (here we are using
Y(M,g_{0})>0
) and we deduce (
6.1
).
∎

Remark 6.11
.

The above corollary is a general fact about the Yamabe constant’s relation to a Sobolev inequality. It has nothing to the with the particular flow under study. The flow enters the discussion when showing that the assumption
\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{q}(M,g)}\leq C
is satisfied.

Lemma 6.12

(Step (3))
.

For any
T<\infty
, there is a constant
C(T)
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{M}S^{\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-2)}}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\,dt\leq C(T).

Proof.

The evolution for
\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{p}(M,g)}
of Lemma
B.2
with
p=\frac{n}{2}
says

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert S\right\rVert^{\frac{n}{2}}_{\frac{n}{2}}=\frac{4(n-2)(n-1)}{n}\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)|\nabla S^{\frac{n}{4}}|\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

We know
S>0
for any finite time due to Corollary
6.3
. By assumption
1
, we have a uniform bound
f^{\prime}(S)\leq-c
(depending on
T
). Using the Sobolev inequality (
6.1
) tells us

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert S\right\rVert^{\frac{n}{2}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\leq-c_{1}\left\lVert S^{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}+c_{2}\left\lVert S^{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}.

Now

\left\lVert S^{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}=\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{\frac{n}{2}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq\left\lVert S_{0}\right\rVert_{\frac{n}{2}}^{\frac{n}{2}}

by Corollary
B.3
. Integrating both sides from
0
to
T
then tells us

\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert S^{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dt\leq C_{1}\left(\left\lVert S_{0}\right\rVert^{\frac{n}{2}}_{\frac{n}{2}}-\left\lVert S\right\rVert^{\frac{n}{2}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\right)+C_{2}T\left\lVert S_{0}\right\rVert^{\frac{n}{2}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\leq(C_{1}+C_{2}T)\left\lVert S_{0}\right\rVert^{\frac{n}{2}}_{\frac{n}{2}}.

This is the claim with
C(T)=(C_{1}+C_{2}T)\left\lVert S_{0}\right\rVert^{\frac{n}{2}}_{\frac{n}{2}}
.
∎

Appendix A
Short-time existence via linearization

In this section we outline an alternative argument for
obtaining short time existence of the generalized Yamabe flow
without referring to Theorem
3.1
, an approach that might prove useful
in the non-compact setting as in e.g.
[
2
]
.
In addition to the spaces introcuced in Section
4
, we need one more open set

(A.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{w\in C^{2+\alpha}\left([0,T]\times M\right)\,\colon\,\exists\,c,\tilde{c}>0:w>c\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ f^{\prime}(w^{-\beta}Lw)<-\tilde{c}\right\}.

Proposition A.1

(Linearized problem)
.

We start by discussing the non-normalized flow.
Let
0<\alpha<1
and assume
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is strictly decreasing. Then for any
u\in\mathcal{O}_{T}
,
h_{0}\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
and
\Psi\in C^{\alpha}([0,T]\times M)
there exists a unique
h\in C^{2+\alpha}([0,T]\times M)
which is a solution to

(A.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h

\displaystyle=D\mathcal{F}(u)h+\Psi

\displaystyle h(0)

\displaystyle=h_{0}

where
\mathcal{F}
is defined in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq D\mathcal{F}(u)=f\left(S\right)+f^{\prime}\left(S\right)\left(u^{1-\beta}L-\beta S\right)
is a strongly elliptic operator for each
u\in\mathcal{O}_{T}
. Its local coefficients are
C^{\alpha}
functions. The solution will therefore follow by standard parabolic theory as follows. Cover
M
by finitely many coordinate neighbourhoods
U_{i}
, which we identify with opens (also called
U_{i}
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\phi_{i}
be a partition of unity subordinate to
\{U_{i}\}
. Let
h_{0,i}\coloneqq\phi_{i}h_{0}
and
\Psi_{i}\coloneqq\phi_{i}\Psi
. By the local theory for linear parabolic PDEs,
[
7
, Theorem 7, Chapter 3]
for instance, there are unique solutions
h_{i}
to the initial-boundary value problems

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{A})h_{i}=\Psi_{i}\\
(h_{i})_{t=0}=h_{i,0}\\
(h_{i})_{|\partial U_{i}}=0.\end{cases}

These solutions are in
C^{2+\alpha}([0,T]\times U_{i})
. Extend these solutions to continuous functions on
M
by extending by
0
;

\overline{h}_{i}(x,t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}h_{i}(x,t)&x\in U_{i}\\
0&x\in M\setminus U_{i}.\end{cases}

Let
h\coloneqq\sum_{i}\overline{h}_{i}
. We claim
h
is the sought solution. It clearly satisfies the right initial condition since
\sum_{i}\phi_{i}=1
. To see that it solves the PDE, let
v\in C^{2+\alpha}([0,T]\times M)
and let
\mathcal{A}^{*}
denote the adjoint of
\mathcal{A}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\langle h,\mathcal{A}^{*}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left\langle\overline{h}_{i},\mathcal{A}^{*}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}=\sum_{i}\left\langle h_{i},\mathcal{A}^{*}v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(U_{i})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left\langle\mathcal{A}h_{i},v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(U_{i})}=\sum_{i}\left\langle\partial_{t}h_{i}-\Psi_{i},v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(U_{i})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{t}h-\Psi,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}.

This shows that
h
is a weak solution. Hence it is also the strong solution.
∎

Theorem A.2

(Short-time existence)
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and assume
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is strictly decreasing. Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{O}
.
Then there exists a
T>0
and a unique
u\in C^{2+\alpha}([0,T]\times M)
solving

(A.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=f(S)\cdot u

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle=u_{0}.

Proof.

Let
T>0
.
We introduce the map

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathcal{O}_{T}

\displaystyle\to C^{1}([0,T];C^{\alpha}(M))

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\mapsto w(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}(w)\,d\tau.

The reason being that a solution
u
is precisely a function satisfying
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(u)=u(0)
for any
t
. So we can produce the solution
u
if we can invert
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
. The linearization
D\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
at a point
w\in\mathcal{O}_{T}
is readily seen to be

D\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(w)h(t)=h(t)-\int_{0}^{t}D\mathcal{F}(w)h\,d\tau.

Since we can solve the linearised problem at
w=u_{0}
(we think of
u_{0}
as an element of
\mathcal{O}_{T}
which is constant in time) by Proposition
A.1
,
D\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is locally invertible.
From the definition,

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(u_{0})=(1-tf(u_{0}^{-\beta}L(u_{0})))u_{0}

is in
C^{1}([0,T];C^{\alpha}(M))
and is close to
u_{0}
for
t
small enough. By the inverse function theorem
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is therefore invertible near
u_{0}
for
t\in[0,T]
with
T
small enough.
∎

For the normalized flow in the case where
f
is not
\alpha
-homogeneous, we computed the linearisation towards the end of Section
3.3
.
One would need to quote a slightly stronger result than
[
7
]
to allow for the integral term, but other than that, one could repeat the argument of the non-normalized flow.

Remark A.3
.

Another strategy one could pursue would be to set up a fixed point argument. Here is a rough suggestion
using Hölder spaces, introduced in Definition
4.1
below.
Let
K=\left\{v\in C^{2,\alpha}([0,T]\times M)\,\colon\,\exists\,c>0\,\colon\,c\leq v\leq c^{-1}\right\}\cap\overline{B_{1}}(u_{0})
, where

B_{1}(u_{0})=\{v\in C^{2,\alpha}([0,T]\times M)\,\colon\left\lVert v-u_{0}\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq 1\}.

This is clearly a closed and convex subset. For
v\in K
, set
d\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{v}}\coloneqq v^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}
and
S_{v}\coloneqq v^{-\beta}L(v)
. This the volume form and scalar curvature respectively of the metric
g_{v}\coloneqq v^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
. Define

\displaystyle A\colon K\to C^{\alpha}([0,T]),\quad A(v)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{v}}(M)}\int_{M}f(S_{v})\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{v}}.

Finally, let
\mathcal{G}\colon K\to C^{2+\alpha}([0,T]\times M)
be defined as

\mathcal{G}(v)\coloneqq\text{solution of }\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=(f(S)-A(v))u\\
u(0)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

Here
S=S_{u}
is the scalar curvature of
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
. This map is well-defined by the short-time existence of the non-normalised flow with a harmless additional linear term.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{G}
is continuous. A solution to the normalised flow is precisely a fixed point of
\mathcal{G}
. One would then have to show the existence of a fixed point, for instance by showing that
\mathcal{G}
is a contraction.

Appendix B
Some more evolution equations

We now proceed with some a priori
L^{p}
-estimates in the positive case.
We assume as before that
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}(M)=1
to simplify some formulas.
Recall

A=\int_{M}f(S)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

We also introduce the average scalar curvature

\sigma\coloneqq\int_{M}S\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

Lemma B.1
.

Along the generalized Yamabe flow (
2.1
), we have

(B.1)

\begin{split}A^{\prime}(t)&=(n-1)\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)f^{\prime\prime}(S)|\nabla S|^{2}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}+\int_{M}\left(\frac{n}{2}f(S)-Sf^{\prime}(S)\right)(f(S)-A)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g},\\
\sigma^{\prime}(t)&=\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{M}S(f(S)-A)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}=\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{M}(S-\sigma)(f(S)-A)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.\end{split}

Proof.

We have

A^{\prime}(t)=\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)\partial_{t}S\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}+\int_{M}f(S)\partial_{t}d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

The first term we rewrite with (
2.6
) and an integration by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)\partial_{t}S\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)\left(-(n-1)\left(f^{\prime}(S)\Delta S+f^{\prime\prime}(S)|\nabla S|^{2}_{g}\right)-S(f(S)-A)\right)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=(n-1)\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)f^{\prime\prime}(S)|\nabla S|^{2}_{g}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}-\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)S(f(S)-A)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

The second term is (
2.2
). Combining these gives the claimed formula.
The evolution equation for
\sigma
is a bit simpler, namely

\sigma^{\prime}(t)=\int_{M}\partial_{t}S\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}+\int_{M}S\,\partial_{t}d\mathrm{Vol}_{g},

and inserting (
2.5
) and (
2.2
) gives the claim.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
. Then the
L^{p}
-norms of
f(S),S
,
(S-\sigma)
, and
(f(S)-A)
evolve according to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|f(S)|^{p}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=(n-1)\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)|f(S)|^{p-2}\left((p-1)f^{\prime}(S)^{2}+f(S)f^{\prime\prime}(S)\right)|\nabla S|^{2}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n}{2}f(S)^{2}-pSf(S)f^{\prime}(S)\right)|f(S)|^{p-2}(f(S)-A)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|S|^{p}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=p(p-1)(n-1)\int_{M}|S|^{p-2}f^{\prime}(S)|\nabla S|^{2}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n}{2}-p\right)\int_{M}|S|^{p}(f(S)-A)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|S-\sigma|^{p}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=p(p-1)(n-1)\int_{M}f^{\prime}(S)|S-\sigma|^{p-2}|\nabla S|^{2}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n}{2}-p\right)\int_{M}(f(S)-A)|S-\sigma|^{p}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle-p\int_{M}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(t)+\sigma(f(S)-A)\right)|S-\sigma|^{p-2}(S-\sigma)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|f(S)-A|^{p}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=p(n-1)\int_{M}|f(S)-A|^{p-2}(f(S)-A)f^{\prime}(S)f^{\prime\prime}(S)|\nabla S|^{2}_{g}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle+p(p-1)(n-1)\int_{M}|f(S)-A|^{p-2}(f^{\prime}(S)^{3}|\nabla S|^{2}_{g}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle+\int_{M}|f(S)-A|^{p}\left(\frac{n}{2}(f(S)-A)-pSf^{\prime}(S)\right)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle-pA^{\prime}(t)\int_{M}|f-A|^{p-2}(f(S)-A)\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}.

Proof.

The identities follow by similar arguments as in Lemma

B.1
. The only addition is the formula

\nabla|S|^{p}=p|S|^{p-2}S\nabla S
.
∎

Corollary B.3
.

Abbreviating
L^{p}=L^{p}(M,g)
, we have

\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lVert S_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{n}{2}}}

for any
p\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

Proof.

The evolution equation in Lemma
B.2
for
\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{p}}
says

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|S|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}=\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{4}\int_{M}|S|^{\frac{n}{2}-2}f^{\prime}(S)|\nabla S|^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}\leq 0.

This proves the claim for
p=\frac{n}{2}
. The case
p<\frac{n}{2}
follows from the Hölder inequality

\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}=\left\lVert S^{p}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq\left\lVert S^{p}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{n}{2p}}}\left\lVert 1\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{n}{n-2p}}}=\left\lVert S\right\rVert^{p}_{L^{\frac{n}{2}}},

where we used the volume normalisation
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}=1
.
∎

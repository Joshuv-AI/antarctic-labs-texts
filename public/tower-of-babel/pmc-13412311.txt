LncRNA GAS6-AS1 enhances the progression of pediatric neuroblastoma by modulating miR-3200-3p
Authors: Jinwen Chen, He Huang, Junshan Lin, Yali Xu, Qiang Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNeuroblastoma, a highly malignant and heterogeneous tumor, is the most frequent extracranial solid malignancy in children.PurposeThis study explores the prognostic significance and biological role of GAS6-AS1 in neuroblastoma, aiming to identify novel biomarkers that could enhance the understanding of disease progression.MethodsRT-qPCR was used to measure GAS6-AS1 expression levels in neuroblastoma tissues. The association between GAS6-AS1 expression and clinical features was assessed using the chi-square test, while its prognostic impact was examined via Cox regression and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Functional experiments, including CCK-8, colony formation, and flow cytometry, were conducted to assess how GAS6-AS1 knockdown influences neuroblastoma cell proliferation and apoptosis. Additionally, Transwell assays evaluated its role in cell migration and invasion. Bioinformatics analysis and dual-luciferase reporter assays were employed to investigate downstream regulatory mechanisms.ResultsElevated GAS6-AS1 expression was observed in neuroblastoma tissues and associated with adverse clinicopathological characteristics. Survival analysis revealed that high GAS6-AS1 levels independently predicted poorer outcomes. Functional assays revealed that GAS6-AS1 depletion attenuated oncogenic behaviors, including proliferation and metastatic potential. Further investigation identified miR-3200-3p as a downstream target of GAS6-AS1, where its suppression counteracted the inhibitory effects of GAS6-AS1 knockdown on malignant phenotypes. Moreover, the results of GO and KEGG analyses indicated that the involvement of the GAS6-AS1/miR-3200-3p axis in neuroblastoma progression may be related to protein polyubiquitination, MAP kinase activity, and autophagy.ConclusionsThis study establishes GAS6-AS1 as a promising prognostic biomarker in neuroblastoma. GAS6-AS1 exerts oncogenic effects by modulating miR-3200-3p, thereby promoting neuroblastoma progression through enhanced malignant phenotypes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04420-5.Keywords: GAS6-AS1, Neuroblastoma, MiR-3200-3p, Progression

Introduction

Neuroblastoma represents the most prevalent extracranial solid malignancy in pediatric populations, characterized by remarkable heterogeneity in its biological behavior and clinical manifestations [1]. This embryonal tumor predominantly arises in the adrenal medulla but can develop anywhere along the sympathetic chain. The disease spectrum ranges from spontaneous differentiation/regression to widespread metastatic dissemination [2]. Diagnostic challenges frequently result in delayed detection, with approximately 50% of patients presenting with metastatic disease at initial diagnosis. Current therapeutic approaches include multimodal treatment strategies (surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy), but clinical outcomes are often unsatisfactory [3, 4]. Elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying neuroblastoma pathogenesis remains crucial for identifying novel therapeutic targets and improving patient prognosis.

As important regulatory molecules, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) participate in diverse biological processes ranging from maintenance of cellular homeostasis to cancer development [5]. Emerging evidence has demonstrated their functional significance in neuroblastoma pathogenesis, where they contribute to various aspects of tumor initiation and progression [6]. Previous studies have identified lncRNA SNHG1 as a prognostic biomarker in neuroblastoma, with elevated expression levels correlating with unfavorable clinical outcomes and MYCN amplification status. Functional analyses revealed that SNHG1 silencing significantly attenuated tumor cell proliferation capacity [7, 8]. Emerging research indicates that the lncRNA PRKCQ-AS1 contributes to neuroblastoma pathogenesis and could represent a potential therapeutic target for anti-cancer interventions [9]. Earlier investigations detected differential expression patterns of GAS6-AS1 in neuroblastoma, subsequently incorporating this lncRNA into an innovative prognostic risk assessment model. The statistical weight of GAS6-AS1 in the predictive model, its significant association with clinical prognosis, and its potential biological functions collectively establish it as a highly promising candidate molecule in neuroblastoma research [10]. Moreover, lncRNA GAS6-AS1 has been established as an oncogenic regulator in bladder and colorectal cancers, playing a critical regulatory role in cancer cell proliferation and metastatic potential [11, 12]. The functional role and molecular mechanisms of GAS6-AS1 in neuroblastoma progression remain largely unexplored in current literature.

This study investigated GAS6-AS1 expression in neuroblastoma tissues from pediatric patients, analyzing its correlation with clinicopathological characteristics and patient prognosis. Functional studies examining the impact of GAS6-AS1 on neuroblastoma cell proliferation and metastasis were conducted to elucidate its biological roles and potential mechanisms in the development of neuroblastoma.

Materials and methods

Patients and specimen collection

A total of 78 neuroblastoma patients admitted to The First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University between 2019 and 2021 were enrolled in this study, with tumor specimens collected from all participants during surgical procedures and adjacent non-tumor tissues obtained from 25 individuals as controls. The study protocol was approved by the hospital’s ethics committee, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians. Inclusion criteria: (1) Age < 14 years; (2) Availability of complete clinical records; (3) No prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy; (4) Histopathological confirmation of neuroblastoma samples and absence of tumor infiltration in adjacent tissues. (5) INSS stages 1, 2 A, 2B, or 3 (stages 4 and 4 S were excluded due to their distinct biological behavior and metastatic patterns). All pediatric patients underwent a 5-year follow-up.

Cell culture and transfection

Human neuroblastoma cells (SK-N-BE [2], SK-N-SH, SK-N-AS, IMR-32, and SH-SY5Y) were obtained from Procell (China), while the human cortical neuronal cell line (HCN-2, control) was acquired from ATCC (USA). All cells were maintained in DMEM (Gibco, USA) containing 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Solarbio, China) and 10% serum (Gibco, USA) at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere.

For knockdown studies, cells were transfected with GAS6-AS1-targeting siRNA (si-GAS6-AS1), while non-targeting siRNA (si-NC) served as the negative control. Additionally, miR-3200-3p expression was modulated using miR-3200-3p mimics (miR-mimic) and inhibitors (miR-inhibitor). Transfection was carried out with Lipofectamine 3000 Transfection Kit (Invitrogen, USA).

RT-qPCR

RNA extraction from clinical samples and neuroblastoma cells was conducted with TRIzol reagent (Beyotime, China), followed by RNA quantification and purity evaluation using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). cDNA synthesis was carried out with the miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme, China) and PrimeScript RT reagent Kit (Takara, Japan). Subsequently, qPCR was performed on an IQ 5 system (Bio-Rad, USA) with PowerUp SYBR Green premix (Applied Biosystems, USA). The 2−ΔΔCt method was applied to determine relative expression, with GAPDH (for GAS6-AS1) and U6 (for miRNA) serving as endogenous controls. The primer sequences are listed in Table S1.

CCK-8

Cells were seeded in a 96-well plate at 2000 cells per well and grown for 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h. Then, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) was added to each well, followed by incubation at 37 °C in the dark for 2 h. The 450 nm absorbance was measured using a Varioskan LUX microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

ELISA

Following culture, cells were collected and disrupted using RIPA lysis buffer (Yeasen, China) supplemented with protease inhibitors. After centrifugation, the supernatant was obtained for protein quantification via the BCA assay. Quantitative analysis of proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) and Cyclin D1 expression was performed with specific ELISA kits (Abcam, UK) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Working solutions and standards were prepared as instructed. Test samples and standards were loaded onto 96-well plates, incubated, and processed through subsequent antibody binding and enzymatic reaction steps. Absorbance measurements at 450 nm were performed using a microplate reader.

Flow cytometry

Cells were harvested and resuspended in 1× Annexin-binding buffer. The cell suspension was then incubated with FITC Annexin V and propidium iodide (PI) working solutions (Invitrogen, USA) for 15 min in the dark. Following incubation, an equal volume of 1× Annexin-binding buffer was added, and samples were kept on ice until analysis. Apoptotic cells were assayed by flow cytometry within 1 h after staining.

Colony formation assay

Transfected cells were diluted and cultured at a density of approximately 400 cells per well in a 6-well plate. After 14 days of culture, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with crystal violet. The number of visible colonies was then counted for each experimental group.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The GAS6-AS1 sequence, containing either wild-type or mutated miR-3200-3p binding sites, was inserted into the pGL3-Basic vector to generate reporter plasmids. These constructs were then co-transfected with miR-mimic or negative control into cells using Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 h, luciferase activity was quantified using the Dual-Glo Luciferase Assay System (Promega, USA).

Transwell assay

Following enzymatic digestion and centrifugation, the cells were resuspended in serum-free medium. Complete medium supplemented with serum was dispensed into each well of a 24-well plate, after which a Transwell insert was carefully positioned. The cell suspension was then introduced into the upper chamber and incubated for 24 h. After incubation, the culture medium was aspirated, and non-migratory cells on the upper membrane surface were gently removed. Migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 1% crystal violet. Five fields of view were randomly selected for observation and counting under the microscope. During the invasion assay, Transwell inserts were positioned within 24-well plates, and their upper membranes were coated with matrix gel prepared using serum-free medium. The plates were incubated for one hour, after which subsequent steps followed the same procedure as the migration experiment.

Bioinformatics tools

Potential target miRNAs of GAS6-AS1 were investigated using multiple databases, including starBase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/), lncRNASNP (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/lncRNASNP/#!/), and LncBook (https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/lncbook/home). Furthermore, lncRNASNP identified a complementary binding site between GAS6-AS1 and miR-3200-3p. The potential targets of miR-3200-3p were further predicted through starBase, miRDB (https://mirdb.org/mirdb/index.html), and TargetScanHuman (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_72/). The candidate targets derived from these databases were then subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis using the Weishengxin online platform (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/).

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as frequency counts, and the association between clinical parameters and GAS6-AS1 levels was evaluated through chi-square tests. Prognostic relevance was examined via Cox regression analysis and visualized through Kaplan-Meier survival curves. Data are presented as mean ± SD. Differences between groups are analyzed using Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA. Inter-variable correlations were assessed using Pearson’s correlation coefficients. All experiments were independently replicated in triplicate. Data analysis was conducted using SPSS 23.0 for statistical computations and GraphPad Prism 8.0 for graphical representation. p < 0.05 defined significance.

Results

GAS6-AS1 expression is upregulated in neuroblastoma and predicts worse prognosis

Comparative analysis revealed that GAS6-AS1 expression was markedly elevated in neuroblastoma tissues compared to controls (Fig. 1A). Among patients with INSS stages 1, 2 A, 2B, and 3, the expression of GAS6-AS1 in neuroblastoma tissues increased with higher INSS staging (Fig. 1B). Using the mean expression of GAS6-AS1 as a cut-off, the cohort was stratified into high (n = 40) and low (n = 38) expression subgroups. Clinicopathological assessment indicated that elevated GAS6-AS1 levels correlated with advanced INSS stage (p = 0.010) and MYCN amplification (p = 0.031, Table 1). Multivariate Cox analysis identified GAS6-AS1 (HR = 3.892, 95% CI: 1.110–13.649, p = 0.034), INSS stage (HR = 3.423, 95% CI: 1.065–11.009, p = 0.039), and MYCN status (HR = 3.016, 95% CI: 1.056–8.614, p = 0.039) as independent predictors of poor prognosis in neuroblastoma (Table 2). Furthermore, low GAS6-AS1 expression correlated with better prognosis, as evidenced by Kaplan-Meier curves (log-rank p = 0.010; Fig. 1C).

Decreased GAS6-AS1 levels impair the proliferation and aggressiveness of neuroblastoma cells

Compared to the normal HCN-2 cell line, neuroblastoma cells exhibited substantially higher levels of GAS6-AS1, with SK-N-SH and IMR-32 cells showing the most pronounced upregulation (Fig. 2A). Transfection with GAS6-AS1-targeting siRNA efficiently downregulated its expression in these two cell lines (Fig. 2B). CCK-8 (Fig. 2C) and colony formation (Fig. 2D) assays revealed that GAS6-AS1 depletion impaired cell proliferation. Consistently, Cyclin D1 and PCNA protein levels were reduced in neuroblastoma cells (Fig. 2E). GAS6-AS1 knockdown also promoted apoptosis (Fig. 3A). Additionally, GAS6-AS1 knockdown attenuated migratory (Fig. 3B) and invasive capacities (Fig. 3C).

GAS6-AS1 exerts oncogenic effects in neuroblastoma by targeting miR-3200-3p

By screening the database, two potential targets (miR-663a and miR-3200-3p) of GAS6-AS1 were identified, but only miR-3200-3p exhibited significant downregulation in SK-N-SH and IMR-32 cells (Fig. 4A). To validate the interaction between GAS6-AS1 and miR-3200-3p, reporter vectors were designed according to the anticipated binding regions. The luciferase assay demonstrated that miR-3200-3p markedly suppressed GAS6-AS1-wt activity, whereas no notable change was observed for the GAS6-AS1-mut (Fig. 4B). Furthermore, miR-3200-3p levels were reduced in neuroblastoma samples compared to controls (Fig. 4C). The expression of miR-3200-3p in neuroblastoma tissues decreased with increasing INSS stage (Fig. 4D). In neuroblastoma tissues, the expression levels of miR-3200-3p and GAS6-AS1 were negatively correlated (r = -0.719, p < 0.001; Fig. 4E). Upon transfection with miR-3200-3p mimics, SK-N-SH and IMR-32 cells exhibited a marked increase in miR-3200-3p levels (Fig. 5A). This overexpression resulted in suppressed cell proliferation (Fig. 5B, C), along with diminished Cyclin D1 and PCNA expression (Fig. 5D). Furthermore, overexpression of miR-3200-3p enhanced cell apoptosis (Fig. 6A). Elevated miR-3200-3p levels also impaired both migratory (Fig. 6B) and invasive (Fig. 6C) capacities.

Subsequent investigations revealed that knockdown of GAS6-AS1 elevated miR-3200-3p expression in both SK-N-SH and IMR-32 cell lines, an effect that could be counteracted by miR-3200-3p suppression (Fig. 7A). Moreover, reducing miR-3200-3p expression mitigated the suppressive impact of GAS6-AS1 knockdown on cell proliferation (Fig. 7B, C) and restored the levels of Cyclin D1 and PCNA (Fig. 7D). Downregulation of miR-3200-3p also alleviated the increase in apoptosis induced by GAS6-AS1 knockdown (Fig. 8A). The reduction of miR-3200-3p weakened the suppressive effect of GAS6-AS1 knockdown on cell migration (Fig. 8B) and invasion (Fig. 8C).

Bioinformatics analysis uncovers molecular functions and critical pathways

To further investigate the downstream mechanisms of the GAS6-AS1/miR-3200-3p axis in neuroblastoma progression, potential downstream targets of miR-3200-3p were predicted through bioinformatics analysis using multiple online databases. This approach yielded 47 putative target genes (Fig. 9A). GO enrichment analysis revealed that these target genes were primarily enriched in several biological processes (BP), including “viral process”, “biological process involved in symbiotic interaction”, and “negative regulation of protein polyubiquitination”. For cellular components (CC), significant enrichment was observed in “AP-type membrane coat adaptor complex”, “lytic vacuole membrane”, and “lysosomal membrane”. Regarding molecular functions (MF), the genes were predominantly linked to “MAP kinase activity”, “PDZ domain binding”, and “protein serine/threonine/tyrosine kinase activity” (Fig. 9B). The KEGG pathway annotation indicated that these genes are enriched in “Autophagy−animal”, “Fluid shear stress and atherosclerosis”, and “Viral life cycle − HIV−1” (Fig. 9C).

Discussion

Neuroblastoma, a heterogeneous pediatric malignancy, remains challenging to treat due to its divergent clinical behaviors [13]. Currently, surgical resection, chemotherapy, and other means of treatment are often used in clinical practice, but the therapeutic effects as well as the prognosis of the affected patients are not optimistic [14]. Therefore, identifying biomarkers that can effectively assess the prognosis of neuroblastoma patients can help to develop personalized treatment plans and improve clinical management.

Recent studies have increasingly highlighted the critical involvement of lncRNAs in tumor initiation, development, and spread. Due to their ubiquitous expression patterns and stability, these molecules have emerged as significant clinical markers and potential treatment targets [15]. Numerous investigations have reported dysregulated lncRNA expression profiles in neuroblastoma cases, underscoring their clinical implications [16, 17]. For instance, research has revealed that ZNF674-AS1 is overexpressed in neuroblastoma patients, linking it to worse survival rates and identifying it as a possible treatment candidate [18]. Additionally, Wang et al. demonstrated that elevated LINC01296 expression correlated strongly with aggressive neuroblastoma characteristics, suggesting its potential as a prognostic biomarker [19]. In this study, analysis of neuroblastoma tissues revealed significant upregulation of GAS6-AS1 expression, which is in line with reports from previous research [10]. Elevated GAS6-AS1 levels demonstrated strong correlations with advanced INSS stages and MYCN amplification, indicating its potential involvement in tumor malignancy progression. The INSS staging system evaluates disease severity based on surgical resection extent and tumor dissemination, where advanced stages indicate more aggressive progression and worse clinical outcomes [20]. MYCN, a key oncogene in neuroblastoma, accelerates tumor growth by stimulating proliferation and blocking cellular differentiation. Its amplification serves as an independent marker of poor prognosis, linked to heightened tumor aggressiveness, treatment resistance, and frequent relapse, ultimately reducing patient survival rates [21]. Furthermore, survival analysis identified high GAS6-AS1 expression as an independent prognostic factor associated with decreased survival rates. These clinical findings highlight the significance of GAS6-AS1 as both a prognostic indicator and a potential therapeutic target in neuroblastoma.

Existing research has documented the involvement of GAS6-AS1 in modulating critical cancer-related processes, including cell proliferation, differentiation, and metastatic spread across various malignancies, establishing its oncogenic properties [22]. In colorectal cancer, cell culture and animal models have demonstrated the tumor-promoting effect of GAS6-AS1 through enhanced growth and metastasis [23]. Moreover, GAS6-AS1 was linked to lymph node metastasis in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and facilitated the proliferation of tumor cells [24]. Tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion are critical for neuroblastoma progression, facilitating both local advancement and systemic metastasis [6]. Emerging evidence indicates that lncRNAs, such as NEAT1, NUTM2A-AS1, and LINC00460, contribute to neuroblastoma development through diverse molecular pathways [25–27]. This study revealed that GAS6-AS1 knockdown in neuroblastoma cells decreased viability, downregulated proliferation-related genes, increased apoptosis, and impaired migratory and invasive capabilities. These results imply that GAS6-AS1 may act as an oncogenic factor in neuroblastoma.

LncRNAs can modulate tumor-related gene expression through the competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism, where they sequester specific miRNAs [15]. In neuroblastoma, LINC0196 functions as a ceRNA by sponging miR-584-5p and miR-34a-5p, leading to the upregulation of TRIM59 and subsequent enhancement of tumor cell aggressiveness [28]. GAS6-AS1 has been demonstrated to upregulate SOX9 by acting as a molecular sponge for miR-215-5p, consequently promoting breast cancer cell invasion [29]. Similarly, in colorectal cancer, GAS6-AS1 exhibits tumor-promoting effects by competitively binding to miR-370-3p and miR-1296-5p [23]. This investigation confirmed that miR-3200-3p is a direct target of GAS6-AS1 in neuroblastoma cells. Dysregulation of miR-3200-3p has been reported to be associated with the progression of gliomas, exerting a tumor suppressor role [30]. For the first time, miR-3200-3p overexpression was demonstrated to suppress the malignant phenotype of neuroblastoma cells, revealing its tumor-inhibitory function. Notably, the suppression of miR-3200-3p counteracted the anti-tumor impact of GAS6-AS1 knockdown on neuroblastoma cell proliferation and metastasis, implying that miR-3200-3p mediates the oncogenic regulation by GAS6-AS1.

To further investigate the possible link between miR-3200-3p downstream targets and neuroblastoma progression, bioinformatics analysis was conducted. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses revealed significant associations with key biological processes, including “negative regulation of protein polyubiquitination,” “MAP kinase activity,” “protein serine/threonine/tyrosine kinase activity,” and “Autophagy-animal,” suggesting that miR-3200-3p-regulated genes may contribute to neuroblastoma development. Post-translational modification through polyubiquitination plays a crucial regulatory role in neuroblastoma pathogenesis by controlling protein homeostasis [31, 32]. Studies have demonstrated that UBE4B forms a functional complex with ITCH E3 ubiquitin ligase, promoting polyubiquitination of Ku70 and c-FLIPL proteins, which subsequently triggers apoptotic pathways in neuroblastoma cells [33]. Wang and colleagues revealed that genetic silencing of EZH2 suppresses neuroblastoma tumorigenesis by facilitating MYC(N) polyubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation [34]. The MAPK signaling pathway, known for its critical roles in cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and stress response, is frequently dysregulated in various cancers, including neuroblastoma [35]. For instance, research has shown that the lncRNA CRNDE accelerates neuroblastoma progression by stimulating the ERK/MAPK pathway [36]. Autophagy serves as an essential regulator in both neuronal development and homeostasis maintenance. Emerging evidence indicates its significant involvement in neuroblastoma progression, particularly in tumor growth, metastasis, and therapeutic responsiveness [37, 38]. Notably, the lncRNA MEG3 has been demonstrated to counteract neuroblastoma malignancy by suppressing autophagy via FOXO1 inhibition [39]. Future studies should emphasize refining the molecular mechanism of the GAS6-AS1/miR-3200-3p axis associated with neuroblastoma progression by delving into the function and regulatory network of miR-3200-3p downstream targets, thus providing new ideas for neuroblastoma treatment.

Several limitations should be acknowledged in this study. First, validation of the prognostic potential of GAS6-AS1 requires larger multicenter cohorts. Second, the absence of in vivo models due to technical constraints necessitates future animal experiments to confirm the functional impact of GAS6-AS1 on neuroblastoma growth.

In summary, the findings demonstrate elevated GAS6-AS1 expression in neuroblastoma and its potential prognostic value. GAS6-AS1 knockdown effectively inhibited both proliferative and invasive properties of neuroblastoma cells. Furthermore, the GAS6-AS1/miR-3200-3p axis was identified as a key molecular pathway driving tumor aggressiveness and disease progression.

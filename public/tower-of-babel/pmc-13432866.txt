HPV vaccination in the WHO South-East Asia Region: A scoping review of uptake, implementation challenges, and pathways to acceleration
Authors: Siddhesh Kolambkar, Mansi Tomar, Nazneen Arif, Showket Hussain, Ekta Gupta, Shalini Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTCervical cancer remains a major public health concern in the WHO South-East Asia Region (SEAR), where human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination coverage remains uneven. This scoping review synthesizes evidence on HPV vaccine uptake and implementation strategies and challenges across SEAR countries. Following the Arksey and O’Malley framework and PRISMA-Scoping Review (ScR) guidelines, 46 studies published between January 2003 and February 2026 were analyzed. Ten of eleven SEAR countries have introduced HPV vaccination nationally, with Bhutan, Sri Lanka, and Thailand achieving coverage above 90%, while several countries remain in early implementation or expansion phases. Four cross-cutting barriers were identified: policy and financial constraints, sociocultural resistance, weak health-system capacity, and vaccine hesitancy. Based on these findings, SEAR–HPV Integrated Implementation Model (SHIM) is proposed to strengthen policy support, sustainable financing, community engagement, workforce preparedness, and digital monitoring. This review highlights evidence-informed strategies to accelerate HPV vaccine scale-up and advance cervical cancer elimination goals in the region.KEYWORDS: Human papillomavirus vaccines, cervical cancer prevention, vaccine hesitancy, immunization programs, health systems strengthening, implementation science, immunization barriers, WHO South-East Asia region, vaccine uptake and coverage, scoping review

Introduction

Cervical cancer (CC) is one of the most common cancers among women globally and remains a major problem for public health. In 2022, an estimated 662,301 new cases and 348,847 deaths were reported globally.1 The distribution of CC incidence and mortality varies across SEAR countries. Figure 1(A, B) depicts age-standardized incidence and mortality rates among females, highlighting regions with the highest burden.2 The Asia-Oceania region accounts for more than half of all cases, with the region’s highest incidence and mortality rates.3,4 Persistent infection with human papillomavirus is responsible for approximately 90% of CC cases.5 The introduction of the HPV vaccines has significantly advanced CC prevention by protecting against HPV types 16 and 18, which cause over 70% of cases globally. Since vaccine licensing in 2006, HPV vaccines have been incorporated into national immunization programs in several countries. However, coverage varies widely with high-income countries typically achieving higher uptake due to strong healthcare systems, while low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) face significant challenges related to financing, vaccine awareness, healthcare access, sociocultural perceptions, and health system preparedness.

Despite the burden of CC, HPV vaccination and systematic screening efforts in LMICs remain inadequate.6,7 The WHO Global Strategy (2020) targets 90% vaccination, 70% screening, and 90% treatment coverage to accelerate CC elimination.8 Within the WHO South-East Asia Region (SEAR), only a few countries–Bhutan, Maldives, and Sri Lanka–have integrated HPV vaccination into routine national immunization schedules.9 Previous studies have examined factors influencing HPV vaccine implementation within subregions of South Asian settings; however, their scope was limited to a subset of countries within the broader WHO SEAR.10–12 In contrast, the present scoping review adopts a comprehensive SEAR‑wide perspective encompassing all 11 member countries, thereby capturing a wider range of policy environments, health system capacities, and sociocultural contexts.

Additionally, earlier reviews primarily focused on identifying implementation barriers, whereas the present study extends this approach by integrating multi-level determinants of vaccine uptake, including policy, health system, sociocultural, behavioral dimension, and emerging implementation strategies within a unified analytical framework. By combining updated regional evidence and structured thematic synthesis, this review provides an expanded and contemporary understanding of HPV vaccination implementation in SEAR and offers actionable insights for future policy and program development.

Materials and methods

The methodological approach followed Arksey and O’Malley’s framework,13 complemented by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) checklist.14

Stage 1: defining the research question

The review question was framed using the Population–Concept–Context (PCC) model, as recommended by the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) for scoping reviews.15 Population: Individuals targeted for HPV vaccination within SEAR countries; Concept: HPV vaccination uptake and challenges in implementation; Context: the 11 countries comprising the WHO SEAR. Research question: What evidence exists regarding trends in HPV vaccination uptake and the challenges encountered during national or subnational program implementation in SEAR countries?

Stage 2: identifying the relevant literature

Searches were conducted in PubMed and Google Scholar and supplemented through manual reference screening. Additionally, peer‑reviewed literature, data from the WHO Global Health Observatory and GLOBOCAN databases were used to obtain contextual epidemiological information regarding cervical cancer incidence, mortality, and HPV vaccination coverage across SEAR countries.16,17 Search terms combined Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and keywords, including: “HPV vaccination” OR “human papillomavirus vaccine,” “uptake” OR “coverage” OR “implementation” OR “barriers” OR “challenges,” “South-East Asia Region” OR SEAR OR [country names].

The primary literature search was conducted using evidence available from January 2003 to December 2023. Because studies published after this period were not available during the initial review process, a supplementary rapid update search covering January 2024 to February 2026 was subsequently undertaken to capture emerging evidence and improve the temporal relevance of the scoping review. This addresses the rapidly evolving landscape of HPV vaccination implementation in WHO SEAR. The supplementary search highlighted the recent developments related to vaccine implementation strategies, national program expansion, behavioral and social drivers of vaccine uptake, digital interventions, vaccine hesitancy, school-based delivery models, and single‑dose HPV vaccination approaches.

Stage 3: study selection

All retrieved records were imported into Mendeley reference management software, where duplicates were identified and removed prior to screening. The start year of 2003 was selected to capture pre-licensure research and early discourse on HPV vaccination, including baseline awareness, acceptability, and expected implementation difficulties prior to 2006. Screening occurred in three stages: title review, abstract review, and full-text assessment. The inclusion and exclusion criteria applied during screening are summarized in Table 1.

Stage 4: data extraction

Key details were extracted from each eligible study into a structured template. Information recorded included: first author, publication year, study aim, country, study design implemented, study population, and key findings. Study selection and data extraction were conducted independently by two reviewers (SK and MT). Any disagreements arising during the screening or data extraction process were resolved through discussion and consensus. In cases where consensus could not be reached, a third reviewer (EG) was consulted to ensure consistency and accuracy in study inclusion and data charting. In accordance with the methodological framework for scoping reviews, a formal quality appraisal of included studies was not conducted. This approach aligns with established scoping review methodology which focuses on mapping breadth of evidence rather than assessing intervention effectiveness.

Stage 5: analysis and synthesis

Extracted data were collected and summarized descriptively. A narrative synthesis approach was applied to compare HPV vaccination trends across SEAR countries. Recurring implementation barriers were organized thematically into four domains: (1) policy and financing (2) sociocultural and religious influences, (3) health system and infrastructure, and (4) vaccine confidence and misinformation. Implementation of HPV vaccination programs and coverage trends in SEAR

The introduction of the HPV vaccine into national immunization program across the WHO SEAR has occurred progressively over the past decade. Bhutan was the first country in the region to introduce the HPV vaccine nationally in 2010. Indonesia followed by pilot testing in 2016 introduced a major rollout in 2023, while Sri Lanka and Thailand both implemented their national HPV vaccination program in 2017. The Maldives introduced the HPV vaccine into its immunization schedule in 2019, and Myanmar began its national rollout in 2020. Bangladesh introduced the HPV vaccine into its national program in 2023. Most recently, India launched its national HPV vaccination program in 2026. This pattern reflects the gradual but expanding adoption of HPV vaccination across SEAR countries. Bangladesh: A national phased HPV vaccination campaign was launched in October 2023, following a pilot in Gazipur in 2016.7 The program targets girls aged 10–14 y (grades 5–9) using a single-dose schedule, primarily delivered through schools, with fixed and outreach sites for out-of-school girls. Phase 1 in Dhaka Division aimed to vaccinate approximately 1.9 million girls.60,61

Bhutan: Introduced HPV vaccination nationally in 2010 for 12-year-old girls. The program is primarily school-based, with health centers serving out-of-school girls. Boys were included in the program in 2021. Reported coverage has been consistently high, ranging from 89% (first-dose coverage in 2021) to >90% in subsequent years. A temporary decline to ~70% was observed when the delivery strategy shifted to health centers; however, returning to school-based delivery restored coverage above 90%.50,62

DPR Korea: No HPV vaccination program has been reported. According to WHO/UNICEF (United Nations International Children’s Emergency Fund) Joint Reporting Form data, there is no evidence of national or subnational HPV vaccine introduction in the country.63

India: HPV vaccination was earlier limited to sub-national programs in India. Two states, Punjab and Sikkim, introduced school-based HPV vaccination in 2016, achieving approximately 97% coverage of target girls.64 India launched a nationwide HPV vaccination program targeting approximately 1.15 to 1.2 crore adolescent girls aged 14 y. The national program, administered through government facilities and schools, offers a voluntary free single‑dose regimen of the quadrivalent vaccine to prevent cervical cancer. Girls who had turned 15 y within 3 months of the campaign’s launch are also eligible to receive vaccination under the program. The program primarily utilized the quadrivalent HPV vaccine (such as Gardasil 4). India also introduced its first indigenous quadrivalent HPV vaccine named Cervavac.65

Indonesia: Piloted HPV vaccination in select districts between 2016 and 2022, expanding nationally in August 2023. The program targets girls in grades 5–6 via school-based delivery, co‑administered with the Tdap (Tetanus, Diphtheria, and acellular Pertussis) booster. Pilot first-dose coverage was reached around 94%. National coverage data are not yet available.66

Maldives: Launched HPV vaccination in 2019, targeting girls 10–14 y in an initial campaign, with routine vaccination for 10-year-olds thereafter. Delivery is school‑based with community education support. First-dose coverage was 92%, while second-dose coverage was lower at 41%.67

Myanmar: Introduced HPV vaccination into its national immunization schedule in October 2020, targeting nearly 450,000 girls born between September 2010 and August 2011 (ages 9–10). A two-dose schedule was planned,12 months apart. Rollout occurred under COVID-19 restrictions, with strict distancing measures and catch-up visits in 73 locked-down townships.68

Nepal: Nepal’s national HPV vaccination efforts were built upon successful, high-coverage pilot programs in the Chitwan and Kaski districts from 2016–2017. However, a subsequent nationwide expansion was delayed due to financial and political challenges. Renewed momentum in 2023, involving a smaller campaign and intensive planning with partners like the WHO, was critical in paving the way for the eventual national rollout.69 Nepal successfully launched its nationwide single‑dose HPV vaccination campaign in early 2024, vaccinating over a 1.4 million girls aged 10–14 y.70 Delivery was primarily school-based, with outreach for out-of-school girls.

Sri Lanka: Introduced HPV program in 2017, delivering two doses 6 months apart to 12-year-old girls through school health programs. Coverage has consistently exceeded 90% between 2018 and 2020. A temporary disruption occurred in 2022–23 due to global vaccine shortages, but supplies were restored in 2024, and catch-up vaccination for missed cohorts is ongoing.71

Thailand: Added HPV vaccination to its EPI (Expanded Program on Immunization) in 2017 for grade 5 girls (age 9–10 y), using a two-dose bivalent regimen delivered through schools. Coverage consistently exceeded 90% before 2019; however, vaccine shortage and COVID-19 disruptions affected the 2019–2021 cohorts. Supplies have since resumed, and catch-up vaccination efforts are ongoing.52,72

Timor-Leste: Notably, Timor-Leste launched its national, school-based HPV vaccination program for girls in July 2024 targeting girls aged 11–14 y. The national campaign is school-based, with a single-dose schedule.73

Emerging evidence from 2024 to 2026 demonstrated the expansion of national vaccination programs, increased usage of school-based delivery systems, behavioral and social determinants influencing vaccine uptake, along with the growing relevance of digital communication ecosystems. Across the included literature, four cross-cutting domains of implementation challenges were consistently identified: (1) policy and financial constraints, (2) sociocultural barriers, (3) health system limitations, and (4) vaccine hesitancy fueled by misinformation.

Discussion

The implementation and scale-up of HPV vaccination programs across the WHO South-East Asia Region (SEAR) reflect a growing political and public health commitment to preventing cervical cancer. Unlike previous region-specific reviews, this study provides a comprehensive SEAR‑wide synthesis while integrating emerging post-2023 evidence and multi-level determinants influencing HPV vaccine implementation. This thematic synthesis provides a unified framework for understanding barriers at a regional level, moving beyond the single-country focus of much of the existing literature. The direct comparison of high‑performing national programs, such as the school-based models in Bhutan, Sri Lanka, and Thailand, which achieve over 90% coverage, with countries yet to implement a national program like India and DPR Korea, offers valuable evidence‑based insights for policymakers seeking to accelerate cervical cancer elimination efforts.

Challenges in HPV vaccination implementation

Despite positive progress, several implementation challenges remain, restricting HPV vaccination uptake in SEAR countries. These challenges are categorized into policy and financial constraints, sociocultural barriers, healthcare infrastructure limitations, and vaccine hesitancy (Figure 4).

Thematic domains of challenges in HPV vaccination implementation. The figure categorizes recurring barriers identified to HPV vaccination identified across the WHO South-East Asia Region into four key domains: policy and financing, sociocultural and religious influences, health system and infrastructure, and vaccine confidence and misinformation.

Policy and financial constraints

One of the most significant impediments to the introduction of national HPV vaccination programs is the high cost of vaccines and the limited financial resources available in many SEAR countries. Although studies have demonstrated that HPV vaccination is cost-effective,31,40,44 obtaining government funding and establishing long-term financing mechanisms remain challenging. The lack of inclusion of HPV vaccination in universal immunization programs further limits large-scale deployment.31,33

Social and religious barriers

Vaccine reluctance, shaped by cultural and religious beliefs, substantially affects HPV vaccine uptake in SEAR countries. Wong et al. (2020) noted that misconceptions linking HPV vaccination with promiscuity contribute to parental reluctance.47 Ethical concerns around adolescent immunization have also been documented in Bangladesh,57 complicating program implementation. Similar issues have been reported in Indonesia and Thailand, where cultural attitudes influence vaccination decisions.39,54

Healthcare infrastructure and delivery challenges

Inadequate healthcare infrastructure, particularly in rural and remote locations, represents another barrier. Low vaccine coverage often results from shortages of trained healthcare workers, logistical challenges in storage and delivery, and weak monitoring systems.11,31 School-based vaccination programs have proven effective in some countries, such as Indonesia; however, sustaining these efforts requires long-term commitment and resources.27,42

Misinformation and vaccine hesitancy

Misinformation and limited awareness among parents, adolescents, and even healthcare providers remain significant barriers to HPV vaccine uptake. Studies in India and Indonesia have found that structured educational interventions significantly enhanced parental understanding and vaccine acceptability.11,39 In addition, negative media reports and misconceptions about vaccine safety have fueled hesitancy in several SEAR countries.26,47

Emerging trends for HPV vaccination in SEAR (2024–2026)

Beyond these implementation challenges, recent evidence also suggests several evolving trends shaping HPV vaccination strategies across the region. As summarized in Table 4, several SEAR countries have initiated national level roll‑out efforts, strengthened school-based delivery systems, and improved operational preparedness through international collaboration. Implementation studies from nations like Nepal and Timor-Leste demonstrate growing regional influence toward WHO cervical cancer elimination goal through coordinated policy and health system approaches.70,73

Beyond vaccine availability, behavioral, sociocultural, and contextual determinants played a key role in successful HPV vaccine implementation within SEAR. Evidence from Indonesia highlighted the importance of social norms, parental perceptions, religious considerations, teacher engagement, and community influence in determining vaccine acceptance and uptake.39 This highlights that the HPV vaccination program requires culturally adaptive and behaviorally informed implementation strategies rather than solely awareness-based approaches. To strengthen context-sensitive implementation planning across the SEAR, there should be integration of behavioral science frameworks such as the WHO Behavioral and Social Drivers (BeSD) framework.74

Vaccine perception and health‑seeking behavior are heavily influenced by the digital information ecosystem around them. An infodemiological analysis study from India showed a substantial increase in online search interest related to HPV vaccination, with spikes corresponding to major policy announcements, indigenous vaccine launches, and public health communication events.22 While digital platforms provide access to large‑scale public engagement, but studies suggest that misinformation circulated through social media may add to vaccine hesitancy among the population by amplifying concerns related to fertility, perceived adverse effects, and vaccine safety. These findings highlight the growing need for a setup system related to digital surveillance, evidence-based online communication strategies, and proactive public health engagement within the digital environment.

Significant disparities in vaccine confidence, access, and awareness persist among rural and underprivileged population in SEAR nations despite growing national HPV vaccination initiatives. A community-based study from rural India highlighted a lack of knowledge regarding cervical cancer prevention, poor willingness for screening, and poor awareness regarding HPV vaccination.24 Vaccine uptake is also affected by the limited access to reliable, evidence-based information, sociocultural stigma, and heavy cost of vaccines.23 Evidence also suggests that out-of-school adolescents, geographically remote communities, and socioeconomically weaker section of population may remain underserved within the existing school-based vaccination frameworks.

There is an increasing adoption of innovative vaccine delivery approaches across the region such as school-based vaccination, community awareness campaigns, phased national rollouts, and targeted community engagement.70,71 Following WHO recommendations on single-dose HPV vaccination strategies, countries are exploring operational models that are cost-effective, logistically feasible, and support broader population coverage.75 Evidence from the recent implementation efforts also highlighted the important role of front-line health worker training, digital communication tools, and behavioral interventions in boosting vaccine confidence and delivery effectiveness across diverse population.12,18,21,25,37,38,55,56

Proposed SEAR–HPV integrated implementation model (SHIM)

The synthesis of findings from SEAR countries reveals that although political commitment toward cervical cancer prevention is increasing, the region continues to face intersecting policy, sociocultural, logistical, and informational barriers. Translating these insights into practice requires a structured, context-adapted framework that bridges evidence and implementation. Based on the thematic domains identified in this review, the authors propose the SEAR–HPV Integrated Implementation Model (SHIM) – an evidence-informed conceptual framework designed to guide equitable HPV vaccination scale-up across the region (Figure 5).

Implementation follows three phases – preparation, pilot demonstration, and national scale-up – with iterative feedback through embedded implementation research. Key indicators include vaccine coverage, equity gaps, stock-out days, workforce training completion, and parental acceptance.

By incorporating WHO’s 2022 single-dose recommendation,75 encouraging gender-neutral vaccination, and embedding digital data systems, SHIM provides a feasible pathway for countries to accelerate progress toward the WHO Global Strategy’s 90–70–90 targets.8 Integrating such an evidence-informed model within existing national immunization programs can improve efficiency, transparency, and sustainability, ensuring that HPV vaccination truly becomes a regional public-health success story.

Facilitators and future directions

Despite these challenges, several facilitators can support increased HPV vaccine adoption in SEAR. Evidence suggests that government-led programs, strong political commitment, and the integration of HPV vaccination into national immunization schedules are critical success factors.33 School-based vaccination strategies and structured awareness campaigns have also proven effective in improving uptake.27–29 Looking ahead, the application of implementation science frameworks offers an opportunity to address systemic issues, optimize delivery models, and ensure scalability. Strengthening public–private partnerships, leveraging GAVI (Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunization) funding, and adopting cost-effective procurement or tiered pricing strategies could improve the affordability and sustainability of HPV vaccination programs.29,49 The 2022 WHO recommendation endorsing single‑dose HPV vaccination schedules represents a major policy shift that can significantly reduce costs, ease logistical burdens, and expand coverage in resource‑limited settings.75 At the same time, promoting gender‑neutral vaccination could enhance herd immunity and ensure equitable protection for both boys and girls. In addition, digital innovations such as electronic immunization registries, mobile‑based reminder systems, and community health worker‑driven outreach can improve vaccine delivery, monitoring, and follow-up in underserved regions.

Finally, achieving sustainable progress will require regional collaboration among SEAR countries, including sharing best practices, building capacity for cold-chain and supply management, and developing context-specific communication strategies to counter misinformation and vaccine hesitancy.

Limitations: This study has some limitations that should be acknowledged. Due to resource constraints, the literature search was limited to PubMed and Google Scholar and complemented with WHO and GAVI databases. Additionally, the exclusion of studies for which full texts were not accessible may have introduced potential selection and publication bias. Coverage data and program reports were not consistently available for all SEAR countries like Timor-Leste, meaning some findings may not fully represent the national context. The uneven geographical distribution of included studies may affect the generalizability of the study findings to other LMIC countries. Nevertheless, the study provides recent region-specific evidence that can inform national strategies and contribute to the global discourse on HPV vaccine introduction and cervical cancer prevention efforts.

Conclusion

This review highlights the adoption of HPV vaccination programs across SEAR countries and identifies key implementation challenges. Financial constraints, sociocultural barriers, weak health infrastructure, and misinformation remain major obstacles to achieving high coverage. At the same time, school-based delivery, community awareness campaigns, and consistent vaccine supply have emerged as effective facilitators. Moving forward, strengthening health systems, adopting evidence‑based strategies such as single-dose schedules, and ensuring political and financial commitment will be essential for expanding HPV vaccine uptake. The proposed SHIM framework offers a practical roadmap to translate evidence into action, supporting countries in accelerating HPV vaccine coverage and sustaining the momentum for reducing the cervical cancer burden and achieving the WHO’s 2030 target of elimination as a public health problem.

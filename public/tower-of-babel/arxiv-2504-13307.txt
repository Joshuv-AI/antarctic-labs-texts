1.5.
Specification

which has the same shape as
\tilde{T}^{*}
, and such that
\Psi_{n}(y)|_{\tilde{T}}\approx\Psi_{n}(y^{*})|_{\tilde{T}^{*}}
, we put

\Psi_{n+1}(y)|_{\tilde{T}_{\bar{M}}}\approx\xi_{n+1,y^{*}}.

After a finite number of repetitions of this process with various elements
y^{*}
,
\Psi_{n+1}(y)
is defined for all
y\in Y_{0}
on the
\bar{M}
-cores of all tiles
\tilde{T}
of the quasitiling
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n+1}
associated with
y
. All remaining positions in
\Psi_{n+1}(y)
are filled with the “empty” symbols
\square
. The map
\Psi_{n+1}:Y_{0}\to(\Lambda\cup\{\square\})^{G}
is measurable and shift-equivariant, and satisfies the inductive assumption for
n+1
. This concludes the induction (see Figure
9
).

5.5.
The limit map
\Psi

Let us now restrict the domain of the maps
\Psi_{n}
to
Y_{0}^{*}=Y_{0}\cap Y^{*}
(for the meaning and properties of
Y^{*}
, see Corollary
2.14
). Since
Y^{*}
has the full measure for all invariant measures on
Y
, we have
\nu(Y_{0}^{*})=1
. What we gain from this restriction is that for each
y\in Y_{0}^{*}
the sequence of quasitilings
(\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
associated with
y
via the map
\tilde{\Phi}
is in general position, that is, the central tiles
\tilde{T}^{(n)}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
, grow to the entire group
G
(see Definition
2.9
). Clearly, the same applies to the cores
\tilde{T}^{(n)}_{\bar{M}}
. Because, for each
n
,
\Psi_{n}(y)|_{\tilde{T}^{(n)}_{\bar{M}}}
is an almost
\bar{X}
-admissible pattern with the exceptional set
E_{\tilde{T}^{(n)}}
, the images
\Psi_{n}(y)
converge to an almost
\bar{X}
-admissible element
\Psi(y)\in X
with the exceptional set

(5.10)

E_{y}=\bigcup_{c\in C(\tilde{\mathcal{T}})}Ec=EC(\tilde{\mathcal{T}}).

where
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=\tilde{\phi}\circ\phi(y)
. Clearly, the limit map
\Psi
defined on
Y_{0}^{*}
is measurable and shift-equivariant.

5.6.
Invertibility of
\Psi

Because, for each
y\in Y_{0}^{*}
, the image
\Psi(y)
is an almost

\bar{X}
-admissible element with the exceptional set
E_{y}
, Lemma
4.10
guarantees that the basic marker
\beta
occurs in
\Psi(y)
centered exclusively within
BE_{y}
and hence fit within the set
B^{2}E_{y}
.
Recall, however, that

B^{2}E_{y}=B^{2}EC(\tilde{\mathcal{T}})=ZC(\tilde{\mathcal{T}})

is occupied by copies of the unambiguous markers
\zeta
(see (
5.10
), (
5.9
), (
4.3
) and (
4.9
)). Thus, all occurrences of
\beta
in
\Psi(y)
fit within the copies of the marker
\zeta
, and hence their centers constitute the set
\bigcup_{c\in C(\tilde{\mathcal{T}})}J_{0}c
(see (
4.11
)).
Suppose that a marker
\zeta
occurs in
\Psi(y)
centered at an element
g\in G
.
Then
\beta
occurs centered at the elements of
J_{0}g
. If
J_{0}g=J_{0}c
for some
c\in C(\tilde{\mathcal{T}})
then
g=c
, by the unambiguity of the marker
\zeta
(see Proposition
4.6
). Otherwise,
J_{0}g
would have to intersect at least two sets of the form
J_{0}c_{1},J_{0}c_{2}
,
c_{1},c_{2}\in C(\tilde{\mathcal{T}})
.
This would imply that
J_{0}^{-1}J_{0}c_{1}\cap J_{0}^{-1}J_{0}c_{2}\neq\varnothing
, which is impossible by the
Z^{-1}Z
-separation of
C(\tilde{\mathcal{T}})
(see (
4.2
), (
4.9
) and (
5.3
)). We have shown that markers
\zeta
occur in
\Psi(y)
exclusively centered at the centers of the tiles of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
.

To see that
\Psi
is invertible, we need to show how
y\in Y^{*}_{0}
can be decoded from its image
\Psi(y)
. By the discussion above, we can locate the centers of the tiles of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=\tilde{\phi}\circ\phi(y)
(which are the same as the centers of the tiles of
\mathcal{T}=\phi(y)
) as the centers of the occurrences of
\zeta
in
\Psi(y)
.

Each marker
\zeta_{t}
is made up of
\zeta
and the “appendage”
\bar{\kappa}_{t}
(where
t\in\{1,2,\dots,r_{\varepsilon}\}
) centered at
g_{0}
. Thus, for each center
c\in C(\mathcal{T})
we have
\Psi(y)|_{Lg_{0}c}\approx\bar{\kappa}_{t}
, where
t
coincides with the index of the shape
S_{t}
of the tile of
\mathcal{T}
centered at
c
. This enables us to recover the shape of each tile of
\mathcal{T}
. In other words,
\Phi(y)
determines
\phi(y)
. Furthermore,
\phi(y)
determines
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=\tilde{\phi}\circ\phi(y)
and
\hat{\mathcal{T}}=\hat{\phi}\circ\tilde{\phi}\circ\phi(y)
. That is, for each
c\in C(\mathcal{T})
we know the shapes
\tilde{S}
and
\hat{S}
of the respective tiles
\tilde{T}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
and
\hat{T}
of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
, centered at
c
. Next, for every tile
\tilde{T}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
we know that
\Phi(y)|_{\tilde{T}_{0}}\approx\theta_{\hat{S},\tilde{S}}(\alpha)
, where
\alpha\approx y^{\prime}|_{\hat{T}}
. Because the code
\theta_{\hat{S},\tilde{S}}
is injective, the block
\alpha
is now uniquely determined. Since
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
is a tiling (i.e., its tiles cover the whole group),
y^{\prime}
can be completely reconstructed, tile by tile (the fact that
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
is improper is irrelevant). Finally, since
\pi
is invertible on
Y_{0}
, we recover
y
as
\pi^{-1}(y^{\prime})
. This completes the proof of invertibility of
\Psi
on
Y_{0}^{*}
. So,
\Psi
is a (measure-theroetic) isomorhism between the systems
(Y,\nu,G)
and
(X,\mu,G)
, where
\mu
is the image of
\nu
via
\Psi
.

This concludes the proof of the main theorem
.

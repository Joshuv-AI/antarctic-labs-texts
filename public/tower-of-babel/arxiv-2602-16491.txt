Riemannian Foliations On CROSSes

Abstract
. We classify Riemannian foliations of manifolds homeomorphic to CROSSes.

Consider a
k

(1)

\mathbb{CP}^{2m+1}

(2)

\mathbb{HP}^{n}

3.
\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}
with its canonical metric

Let us now consider the case of
M
isometric to
\mathbb{CP}^{2m+1}
with its canonical metric. In this section, we prove that any Riemannian foliation is metrically congruent to the foliation by the fibers of the twistor fibration
T:\mathbb{CP}^{2m+1}\to\mathbb{HP}^{m}

T[x_{0}:\ldots:x_{2n+1}]=[x_{0}+x_{1}j:\ldots:x_{2n}+x_{2n+1}j]

To avoid confusion, in this section we will use the following notation:

•

\mathbb{H}
is spanned by quaternionic units
1,i,j,k
and can be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{4}
via
a+bi+cj+dk\mapsto(a,b,c,d)
.

•

Sp(1)\subset\mathbb{H}
denotes the subset of quaternions of unit norm. Elements in
Sp(1)
will be denoted with letters
p,q,r
.

•

Given
p\in Sp(1)
and
v\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the left multiplication of
v
by
p
is denoted
pv
. Similarly, given a matrix
A\in O(4n)
and a vector
v\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{4n}
, the multiplication of
A
and
v
is denoted
Av
.

•

Any other action of
Sp(1)
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{4n}
is denoted by
p\cdot v
.

Proceeding like before by taking a Riemannian foliation
(\mathbb{CP}^{2m+1},\mathcal{F})
and pulling back through the (standard) complex Hopf fibration
H_{2m+1}:\mathbb{S}^{4m+3}\to\mathbb{CP}^{2m+1}
, we obtain a Riemannian foliation
(\mathbb{S}^{4m+3},\bar{\mathcal{F}})
of the sphere with its canonical metric.

By
[
3
]
,
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
is homogeneous, i.e. given by the orbits of a representation
\tau:Sp(1)\to O(4m+4)
inducing an action
p\cdot v:=\tau(p)v
for any
p\in Sp(1)
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{4m+4}
. On the other hand, we proved in the previous section that
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
is a simple foliation given by the fibres of the Riemannian submersion
\pi:\mathbb{S}^{4m+3}\to B
. This implies that the action induced by
\tau
is free on
\mathbb{S}^{4m+3}
. By the classification of such actions (cf.
[
3
, Corollary 5.4]
), by identifying
\mathbb{R}^{4m+4}
with
\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
in the usual way, the representation
\tau
is equivalent to the diagonal embedding
Sp(1)\to Sp(m+1)\to O(4m+4)
. In other words, there exists an isometry
\phi:\mathbb{H}^{m+1}\to\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
such that

p\cdot v=\phi(p\phi^{-1}(v)),\qquad\forall p\in Sp(1),\quad v\in\mathbb{H}^{m+1}.

Recall furthermore that, in our case,
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
is the pullback of
\mathcal{F}
via the
standard
Hopf fibration
\mathbb{S}^{4m+3}\to\mathbb{CP}^{2m+1}
. In particular, the
Sp(1)
-orbits of
\tau
contain the orbits of the standard complex Hopf fibration. Under these assumptions, we prove:

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a homomorphism
\rho:U(1)\to Sp(1)
,
\rho(z)=rzr^{-1}
for some
r\in Sp(1)
, such that
\rho(e^{it})\cdot v=e^{it}v
.

Proof.

For any
v\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, let
\ell_{v}=\operatorname{span}(v,iv,jv,kv)\simeq\mathbb{H}
denote the standard quaternionic line through
v
. Since the
Sp(1)
-action is free and isometric, for any
v\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
the map
Sp(1)\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
,
p\mapsto p\cdot v
defines a bijection between
Sp(1)
and the unit sphere of
\phi(\ell_{\phi^{-1}(v)})
. Since the
Sp(1)
-action contains the complex Hopf fibers in its orbits, fixing a vector
v_{0}
, there is a (unique)
q\in Sp(1)
such that
q\cdot v_{0}=iv_{0}
.

We claim that
q\cdot v=iv
for any
v\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Given
v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
there exist unique
q^{\prime}
such that
q^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}=iv^{\prime}
. Similarly, for
v_{0}+v^{\prime}
there exits a unique
q^{\prime\prime}
such that
q^{\prime\prime}\cdot(v_{0}+v^{\prime})=i(v_{0}+v^{\prime})
. Therefore:

q^{\prime\prime}\cdot v_{0}+q^{\prime\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}=q^{\prime\prime}\cdot(v_{0}+v^{\prime})=iv_{0}+iv^{\prime}=q\cdot v_{0}+q^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}

\Rightarrow\quad(q^{\prime\prime}-q)\cdot v_{0}+(q^{\prime\prime}-q^{\prime})\cdot v^{\prime}=0.

If
\phi^{-1}(v_{0})
,
\phi^{-1}(v^{\prime})
belong to different quaternionic lines, then
\mathbb{H}\cdot v_{0}\cap\mathbb{H}\cdot v^{\prime}=\{0\}
and thus
q=q^{\prime\prime}=q^{\prime}
, thus proving the claim in this case. If
\phi^{-1}(v_{0})
,
\phi^{-1}(v^{\prime})
belong to the same quaternionic line, then by taking
v^{\prime\prime}
such that
\phi^{-1}(v^{\prime\prime})
belongs to a different quaternionic line, we would get
q=q^{\prime\prime}=q^{\prime}
, thus proving the claim anyway.

Notice that
(q^{2})\cdot v_{0}=(i)^{2}v_{0}=-v_{0}=(-1)\cdot v_{0}
and again by the bijectivity of
Sp(1)\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, it follows that
q^{2}=-1
. This means that
q=ri\bar{r}
for some
r\in Sp(1)
. Finally, let
\rho:U(1)\to Sp(1)
be given by
\rho(z)=rzr^{-1}
. Then for any
v\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
,

\displaystyle\rho(e^{it})\cdot v

\displaystyle=(r(\cos t+i\sin t)r^{-1})\cdot v=(\cos(t)+\sin(t)q)\cdot v

\displaystyle=\cos(t)v+\sin(t)iv=e^{it}v.

∎

Define now
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{4n+4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4n+4}
by
\psi(v)=r^{-1}\phi(v)
. Then:

(1)

The equation of the lemma gives:

\displaystyle\phi(e^{it}v)=

\displaystyle\phi(\rho(e^{it})\cdot v)=\rho(e^{it})\phi(v)=re^{it}r^{-1}\phi(v)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\psi(e^{it}v)=

\displaystyle r^{-1}\phi(e^{it}v)=e^{it}\psi(v).

Thus
\psi:\mathbb{S}^{4n+3}\to\mathbb{S}^{4n+3}
induces a map
\bar{\psi}:\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}\to\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}
.

(2)

\psi
sends orbits of the
Sp(1)
-action induced by
\tau
to orbits of the standard
Sp(1)
-action: in fact, for a fixed

\displaystyle\psi\left(\{g\cdot v\mid g\in Sp(1)\}\right)=

\displaystyle\left\{\psi(g\cdot v)\mid g\in Sp(1)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{r^{-1}\phi(g\cdot v)\mid g\in Sp(1)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{r^{-1}g\phi(v)\mid g\in Sp(1)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{h\phi(v)\mid h\in Sp(1)\right\}

In particular,
\psi:\mathbb{S}^{4n+3}\to\mathbb{S}^{4n+3}
is a foliated isometry, inducing a map
\tilde{\psi}:B\to\mathbb{HP}^{n}
which commutes with the projections.

To summarize: there exists a foliated isometry

. However, by the previous section there are no 6-dimensional foliations on manifolds homeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}
. Therefore, no non-trivial Riemannian foliations can occur on spaces homeomorphic to
\mathbb{HP}^{n}
.

5.
M
homeomorphic to
\mathbb{O}\mathbb{P}^{2}

We are left with the easiest case, in which
M
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{O}\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Recall that
H^{*}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[c]/(c^{3})
with
c
generator of
H^{8}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Furthermore, since the total Stiefel-Whitney class
w(TM)
is a homotopy invariant, we have
w(TM)=w(T\mathbb{O}\mathbb{P}^{2})=1+c+c^{2}
(see
[
7
]
, p. 134).

If a (not necessarily Riemannian) foliation were to exist on
M
, just as in Section
2.2
we would have
TM\cong\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}
and
w(TM)=w(\mathcal{V})\cup w(\mathcal{H})
. Since
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
have rank strictly lower than
16
(as otherwise the leaves would either be
0
or
16
-dimensional and the foliation would be trivial), their total Stiefel-Whitney classes must be of the form
w(\mathcal{V})=1+ac
,
w(\mathcal{H})=1+bc
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, resulting in

1+c+c^{2}=(1+ac)(1+bc)=1+(a+b)c+abc^{2}\qquad\textrm{in }\mathbb{Z}_{2}[c]/(c^{3})

which is impossible. Thus,
M
does not admit any non-trivial foliation.

6.
Notes on manifolds homotopy equivalent to CROSSes

The most obvious step would now be to generalize these results not only to spaces homeomorphic to CROSSes but spaces homotopy equivalent to them.

It is easily seen that if
f:M\to\mathbb{CP}^{n}
is a homotopy equivalence, the map
F:f^{*}(\mathbb{S}^{2n+1})\to\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}
obtained as in Section
2
is still a homotopy equivalence, so that by the generalized Poincaré conjecture it is a homeomorphism. Using the results from
[
6
]
, we can deduce as before that
n
is odd and and
\dim(\mathcal{L})=2
or
6
, the second case being only possible for
n=7
. Similarly for
f:M\to\mathbb{HP}^{n}
homotopy equivalence we can deduce
n=3
and
\dim(\mathcal{L})=4
.

Now, for
\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}
the same arguments hold in the
\dim(L)=2
case, but at the moment we cannot rule out the existence of
6
-dimensional foliations. Since our argument for the
\mathbb{HP}^{3}
case hinges on the
6
-dimensional foliation case for
\mathbb{CP}^{7}
, we analogously cannot rule it out.

However, the non-existence of foliations on manifolds homotopy equivalent to
\mathbb{O}\mathbb{P}^{2}
still holds, as it uses Stiefel-Whitney classes which are homotopical invariants.

Efficient impedance source-based high voltage gain converter with smooth input current
Authors: Maryam Hajilou, Hosein Farzanehfard
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes a new soft switched high step-up quasi Z-source converter. The innovative combination of coupled inductors and switched capacitor techniques with the basic quasi-Z-source structure, results in a high voltage gain and low switches voltage stress converter. These features are achieved at a low coupled inductors turns ratio while maintaining the continuity of the input current and common ground between the input and output. This converter provides soft switching at zero voltage for all switches and eliminates capacitive turn-on losses. Furthermore, the proposed structure has solved the high conduction loss of the input side diode in the basic quasi-Z-source structure which is key challenge. This converter eliminates the reverse recovery problem for all diodes, and no limitation is imposed on the duty cycle relative to the basic structure. Moreover, the leakage inductance energy is absorbed and recycled into the load. The proposed converter contribution is simultaneous possession of all the above-stated advantages which are absent among the competitors. The introduced converter is thoroughly analyzed and the converter characteristics are validated over other counterparts by the implementation of a 200W laboratory prototype.Keywords: Quasi Z-source converter, High voltage gain, Low voltage stress, ZVS operationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Photovoltaic (PV) power generation systems are one of the pillars of clean energy production. Voltage booster converters enhance these sources output voltages to provide the required voltage for the inverters1. The main requirements in these converters are high voltage gain (VG), low cost, and high efficiency2–4. The conventional boost converter is a cost-effective topology however, high VG cannot be realized due to the intense drop in efficiency. Moreover, only increasing the switch duty cycle enhances the VG, leading to high output diode current stress. Impedance source converters such as Z-source, Y-source, and -source structures achieve higher voltage gain than the boost structure at lower duty cycles. However, their discontinuous input current is undesirable for PV systems5. Also, the output voltage imposed on their switches leads to high switching losses, and the converter cost. Quasi-impedance source converters are developed to overcome the discontinuous input current in these structures. Switched inductor (SI) cells are combined with the quasi-Z-source (qZs) structure to increase the VG in6, but the output voltage is imposed on its switch. Although the integration of switched capacitor (SC) method has reduced the switches voltage stress in7, the input current ripple is high, common ground (CG) is lost, and two switches are employed. Moreover, the converters in6 and7 have limited duty cycle range with respect to the qZs topology affecting the converter operating range. The qZs converter in8 uses SC and SI techniques to enhance the VG and reduce the switch voltage stress. However, the lack of CG between input and the load limits its applicability and employing an extra magnetic core relative to the basic structure leads to high cost and volume. In the converter presented in9, combining the qZs network and SC cell improves the converter VG and the switch voltage stress while CG is maintained. The high voltage gain qZs converter in10 employs coupled inductors (CIs) and SC methods to reduce the switch voltage stress and only uses one magnetic core. Although the input current in the converters of8,9, and10 is continuous, coupling the input inductor in10, utilizing semiconductor elements at the input side in9, and connecting a capacitor to the input source in8 leads to high input current ripple. By integrating Z-source structure and SCs networks, three high step-up structures are developed in11. In these converters, an inductor-capacitor filter at the input side is required to provide a low ripple input current. In addition, the lack of CG and having three magnetic cores are these converters drawbacks.

A manner to improve the qZs characteristics by maintaining CG is introduced in12. In this method, the input voltage and qZs structure output voltage summation form the load voltage. Hence, part of energy is directly transferred to the load and power processing losses are reduced. However, creating a capacitance loop at the switch turn-on causes high losses in the switch and other elements and may damage the switch. Moreover, these converters need a floating gate-driver circuit, leading to control complexity. Combining the CIs and SC techniques with the qZs topology improves the VG and switch voltage stress in12,13, and14. In these converters, coupling the qZs middle inductor maintains the low ripple in the input current. These converters disadvantages are inability to solve the diodes reverse recovery problem and eliminating the switching losses.

Increasing the switching frequency is a solution to enhance the converter power density. However, switching and capacitive turn-on losses degrade the converter efficiency, especially at high switching frequencies. Although the converter in15 mitigates the switching losses using a resonant tank, its VG is relatively low. The single switch qZs converter in16 enhances the voltage gain using CIs and SC methods. In this converter, a resonant path provides soft switching condition, however, the output voltage is imposed on the switch in16, and capacitive turn-on losses exist in15 and16. The converters introduced in17,18, and19 enhance the VG by a voltage multiplier (VMC) cell, and a zero-voltage transition (ZVT) cell is employed to eliminate the switching losses of the main switch. Moreover, in the converters of18 and19, the CIs used in the ZVT cell positively affect the boosting capability. Although these converters provide zero voltage switching (ZVS) for the main switch, their auxiliary switch operates under zero current switching (ZCS) and thus, capacitive turn-on losses are not removed. In the converter of20, the operation of a synchronous rectifier provides ZVS condition. However, the switches voltage stress and VG are not improved substantially. The high step-up qZs converter in21 enhances the VG and provides ZVS operation by integrating an auxiliary circuit with the qZs structure. This converter can adjust the operating duty cycle range using the CIs turns ratio. However, qZs structure input side diode causes high conduction losses. The boost-based high step-up converter presented in22 benefits from low diodes losses however, its voltage gain is relatively low.

This article presented a new high voltage gain converter that integrates an innovative auxiliary circuit with the qZs structure. Coupling the basic structure middle inductor with the auxiliary circuit significantly improves the voltage gain and the switches voltage stress even at low CIs turns ratio while not affecting the input current ripple. The average current of the qZs structure input side diode is equal to the input source current, and the major losses in the qZs converters are allocated to it. The presented converter considerably reduces the voltage stress on this diode. Hence, this diode is replaced with a synchronous rectifier with low on-resistance to diminish the conduction loss. Furthermore, this synchronous rectifier provides soft switching under ZVS for both switches and creates a path to absorb the leakage inductance energy. The converter eliminates switches capacitive turn-on losses and all diodes reverse recovery problem. Recycling the leakage inductance energy, common ground between input and output, and low input current ripple are other features of this converter.

Comparison

The specifications of the proposed converter and other qZs counterparts are compared in Table 1. Also, their voltage gains and switches voltage stresses versus duty cycle are drawn in Fig. 6 (a) and (b). As shown, the proposed topology voltage gain and switches voltage stress are significantly better than all the competitors. Additionally, Fig. 6(c) illustrates the normalized voltage gain to component count demonstrating that even when the total component count is accounted for, the proposed converter achieves the highest voltage gain among all the considered competitors. The presented converter provides soft switching for all switches, eliminating the capacitive turn-on losses, and solves the reverse recovery problem for all diodes. Also, it employs acceptable components and maintains low input current ripple and CG. However, the voltage gain of the converters in8,9,11,18, and20 is much lower than others. Also, high voltage imposed on the switch in16, and20 leads to high circuit cost. The switching in the converters of8–13 and14 is hard which drops their efficiency at high switching frequencies. Moreover, reverse recovery losses exist in these converters. Although the converters in16–18 and19 provide soft switching but they suffer from capacitive turn-on losses. The low input current ripple and CG are the required features in various applications while the lack of them limits the applicability of8–10 and11. Notably, the converters in8,11 and13 have employed an extra magnetic core than the proposed converter while their VG is lower than the presented structure. This comparison shows that the proposed converter achieves all the required specifications while the competitors have failed to achieve two or more features.

Experimental results

A 200W prototype is implemented in the laboratory to confirm the proposed converter performance and show its advantages. The implemented converter specifications are listed in Table 2 and the circuit photograph is displayed in Fig. 7. The prototype converter elements are designed according to the design procedure in section “Design considerations”. One of the converter features is the switches ZVS operation which can be validated by no overlap of the switch drain-source voltage and drain current at the switching instants. Fig. 8(a) and (b) illustrate the main and auxiliary switches voltage and current waveforms at full load (200 W). As observed, the snubber capacitors reduced the voltage rising slop at the switches turn-off. Hence, the switch voltage and current overlap are decreased and switching losses at turn-off are eliminated. Before the switches turn on, their snubber capacitors are fully discharged and the switches body diode conducts resulting in ZVZCS turn on condition. Figure 8(c) shows the waveforms at the light load (40W) illustrating similar soft switching conditions as the full load. As discussed in section “Soft switching condition”, is designed to provide ZVZCS turn-on for at the worst condition (full load) which guarantees soft switching at light loads. Therefore, has a longer duration of negative current at 40W. Notably, this issue does not disrupt the converter performance. The diodes voltages and currents are shown in Fig. 8(d)–(g) confirming ZCS turn off of all diodes. The input and output voltages in Fig. 8(h) confirm the intended voltage gain. Moreover, these waveforms show low voltage stress on semiconductors compared to . The control circuit is designed according to the strategy discussed in22, and the converter response to the load variation from 200W to 40W and vice versa is illustrated in Fig. 8(i) that confirms the output voltage regulation.

Conclusions

This article has proposed a new high step-up converter which is formed by a novel combination of voltage-boosting techniques with the qZs structure, achieving high voltage gain at very low switches voltage stress. In the presented converter ZVS operation for all switches is established hence, switching and capacitive turn-on losses are eliminated. Furthermore, this converter contains a solution to overcome the high conduction losses of diodes in the qZs structure and provide ZCS at the diodes turn-off. These advantages are achieved while maintaining constant input current and common ground which have resulted in a topology with high efficiency and wide applicability. The experimental outcomes, loss breakdown analysis and comparison results confirm the proposed converter benefits.

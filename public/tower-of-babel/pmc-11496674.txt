Experimental evaluation of acid number effect on interfacial tension between nitrogen and mixtures of petroleum fractions including diesel fuel and gasoline at different pressures
Authors: Abbas Elhambakhsh, Mohammad Reza Zaeri, Mohammad Noor Ghasemi, Feridun Esmaeilzadeh, G. Reza Vakili-Nezhaad
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Crude oil is made up of very complex compounds, which are found in polar organic substances especially organic acid compounds in the majority. Although organic acid content in crude oil is low, the presence of organic acids affects the interfacial tension (IFT) in oil reservoirs thereby altering the enhancement of oil recovery using nitrogen gas (N2) injection. Therefore, in this study, the effects of pressure (1 to 35 bar), the percentage of aromatic compounds (0.0 to 100 vol%) in the aliphatic hydrocarbon samples and the organic acid (benzoic acid) content (0.0 to 0.8 g.L− 1) on IFT between a mixture of gasoline/diesel fuel and N2 were investigated at a temperature of 298 K. IFT does not necessarily decrease as pressure increases for the sake of existence of aromatic compounds. IFT alteration for gasoline was more (about 8 dyne.cm− 1) than for diesel fuel (approximately 4 dyne.cm− 1) during pressure increased from 1 to 35 bar due to the replacement of heavy long chain aliphatic compounds instead of light aromatic matters. The effect of IFT alteration between N2 and diesel fuel during increasing the organic acid content was little (approximately 1.5 dyne.cm− 1), in contrast, this effect was more noticeable for N2/gasoline (about 8 dyne.cm− 1). In addition, a reduced cubic model for estimating IFT between N2 and petroleum fractions in terms of pressure, petroleum fraction composition and organic acid content was proposed with an average relative error of less than 2.6%.

Introduction

Generally, crude oil production drops after a period of time since the oil is depleted from a reservoir and it is necessary to use enhanced oil recovery (EOR) methods1,2. One of those methods is gas injection in it3,4. Some advantages of the gas injection method are oil viscosity reduction, oil expansion and IFT reduction5,6. One of the undesirable phenomena in gas injection in oil reservoirs is the occurrence of fingering. In the fingering phenomenon, which happens due to the lower density of gas compared to crude oil, the gas tries to pass over the oil bank and move towards the production well or actually bypass the oil bank. To avoid this challenge, one solution is gas injection from the top of the reservoir to push the oil down7. Another solution is the supercritical fluid injection because the density of the supercritical fluid is between the densities of the gas and liquid and its density value is closer to the liquid density8,9. In addition to the CO2 gas injection, N2 gas could also be implemented to improve oil recovery10. One of the advantages of N2 gas injection compared to CO2 is that the supercritical temperature and pressure of N2 are lower than that of CO2 and importantly, the more possibility of asphaltene precipitation during CO2 injection that these problems do not exist or is less likely to occur in N2 injection11,12. Other benefits of the N2 gas injection in oil reservoirs are low cost, abundance and availability. Moreover, the N2 gas injection is suitable for oil reservoirs at high depths, with more pressure and containing light hydrocarbon compounds13.

The importance of IFT is also evident in the estimation of capillary pressure in addition to miscible/immiscible fluid injection. Capillary pressure is one of the factors that controls the displacement of fluids in the reservoir in such a way as to cause no displacing of one14. Also, capillary pressure data provide a measurement of the levels of fluids and the contact zone thickness in oil reservoirs15. Among other applications of IFT is the calculation of the relative permeability of fluids. With the help of the relative permeability data, the type of rock wettability can be determined to some extent16. IFT could be utilized for measuring the solubility of two phases in each other at a specific pressure and composition17. Now, the effects of temperature, pressure, type of compounds and their composition on IFT are reviewed as follows.

Impact of temperature and pressure on IFT

Ditmmar et al. stated that IFT between ethanol and N2 gas decreased as pressure increased as well as temperature18. Since some studies had proposed a relationship between IFT and gas density or the density difference between two phases17,19,20, Ditmmer et al. conducted based on the experimental investigation of IFT for a system of N2 gas and ethanol that the IFT does not obey only the N2 density or the density difference, but also is a function of temperature18. Pereira et al. showed that with increasing pressure over a wide range of up to 69 MPa as well as temperature from 313 to 442 K, IFT between N2 and normal decane decreased19. Garrido et al. investigated IFT between N2 gas and each of the hydrocarbons including ethanol, hexane and tert-amyl methyl ether (TAME) under a pressure range of 0.1 to 15.1 MPa and a temperature of 303.15 to 353.15 K. Results revealed that IFT between N2 and each of the aforementioned hydrocarbons decreased as temperature or pressure increased. They explained the IFT alteration under the pressure impact that is due to N2 gas adsorption on the liquid surface by evaluating the relative Gibbs adsorption isotherm21. The IFT reduction with the increased pressure is less at high pressures compared to low pressures, which is due to the dissolution of N2 in hydrocarbons22. Furthermore, the reason for the IFT alteration between N2 gas and hydrocarbon liquids under the influence of temperature is related to the N2 solubility23.

Impact of type and composition of compounds in oil on IFT

According to the studies performed by Garrido et al. concerning IFT between N2 gas and each of hydrocarbon liquids such as ethanol, hexane and TAME under different pressures and temperatures, they found that the IFT values of a system of ethanol/N2 and TAME/N2 are close to each other at any temperature and pressure. This observation indicates that the interaction between the nitrogen and oxygen atoms in each of the aforementioned systems is more repulsive compared to the interaction between the nitrogen and carbon atoms. On the other hand, the IFT of normal hexane/N2 at any temperature and pressure is always lower than IFTs of ethanol/N2 and TAME/N2. The reason is due to the presence of free electron pairs in the last layer of oxygen and nitrogen atoms, which has caused a partial negative charge on these atoms, but the carbon atom has no free electron pairs around it and all its outer layer electrons shared with another carbon and hydrogen atoms, as a result of which no partial negative charge on the carbon atom. Pursuant to the partial negative charge in the structure of ethanol, TAME and N2 molecules, the repulsive force between N2 and ethanol either TAME is provided with the partial negative charge. On the contrary, the normal hexane molecule without partial negative charge creates neither repulsion with N2, which causes more solubility of N2 gas in normal hexane thereby decreasing the IFT of normal hexane/N2. However, the repulsive force between ethanol either TAME with N2 gas prevents N2 dissolution in ethanol or TAME thereby increasing the IFT21. Likewise, the CO2 solubility in ethanol is greater than the N2 solubility in ethanol due to the interaction between ethanol and CO2 gas compared to N2 gas, which is related to the intermolecular forces of nitrogen-hydrogen and oxygen-hydrogen18.

Zolghadr et al. investigated IFT between N2 gas and hydrocarbons including normal heptane, normal hexadecane and diesel fuel at different temperatures (313.15 ~ 393.15 K) and pressures (0.34 ~ 41.37 MPa). The trend of IFT alteration under increasing pressure is similar for normal hexadecane/N2 and normal heptane/N2; however, the IFT values of normal hexadecane/N2 are greater than normal heptane/N2, which demonstrates the effect of hydrocarbon chain on IFT. Paraffinic compounds in a hydrocarbon liquid, such as diesel fuel, in contact with N2 gas, tend to come to the interface of two phases according to the results obtained from evaluating the ratio of the IFT alteration to the pressure variation for three systems including normal heptane/N2, normal hexadecane/N2 and diesel fuel/N2 at the same temperature22. Asphaltene deposition depends on temperature, pressure and crude oil composition24–27. Hemmati-Sarapardeh et al. investigated IFT between N2 gas and two types of crude oil with different asphaltene compositions in terms of the effects of pressure and temperature. Results indicated that the IFT between N2 and both types of crude oils decreased with the increased temperature at any pressure. Besides, the IFT values of N2/crude oil with more asphaltene were greater than those of N2/crude oil with less asphaltene6. The reason for the IFT reduction of the N2/oil system is related to the N2 solubility in hydrocarbon, which increases as temperature rises22,23. Under the incremental temperature, the N2 gas solubility in hydrocarbon liquid increased; furthermore, the N2 gas solubility in short-chain hydrocarbons was higher23. The IFT between N2 gas and hydrocarbon liquids show a change in behavior over a wide range of pressure. The pressure that corresponds to IFT trend changes, is called turning point. As the temperature increased, the turning point shifted to lower pressure values. For light hydrocarbons, the turning point has low-pressure values, but it has higher values for heavy hydrocarbons22.

Crude oil usually contains organic acid compounds in addition to asphaltene. Therefore, it is expected that as asphaltene has an impact on IFT, the effect of the organic acid compound in hydrocarbon on IFT can also be observed in contact with N2 gas and so, the lack of information is felt in this regard. The main intention of this research work is to investigate the present effect of the organic acid compounds on the IFT of the oil/N2 system in order to evaluate the EOR using N2 gas injection. Because, the IFT alteration causes changes in the capillary pressure of reservoir fluids, which provides the driving force for displacing oil in the reservoir porous media. In this work, the objectives, such as the effects of various organic acid contents (0.0, 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 g.L− 1 of benzoic acid) and several oil compositions (0.0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 vol% of gasoline (representing aromatic and isoparaffin hydrocarbons) in diesel fuel (representing aliphatic hydrocarbons) on IFT of oil/N2 are investigated under different pressures (1, 5, 15, 25, 35 bar) at ambient temperature (298 K). The selected plane-pendant drop method was utilized to measure IFT. Eventually, a new reduced cubic model was proposed to appraise IFT between N2 gas and hydrocarbons based on the acid number.

Experimental section

Materials

N2 gas was obtained from Aboughaddareh Company (Shiraz, Iran) with a purity of 99.99%. Benzoic acid with a purity of 99.5 wt% was purchased from MERCK Company. Diesel fuel and gasoline were supplied from Shiraz Oil Refining Company. The analyses of diesel fuel and gasoline shown in Table 1 were performed in the laboratory of Shiraz Oil Refining Company. Acid numbers of diesel fuel and gasoline were measured by the standard method D664. Acid numbers of gasoline and diesel fuel were found to be 0.00 and 0.1261 mgKOH.g− 1, respectively. In this work, benzoic acid was added to the hydrocarbon mixtures in order to investigate the effect of organic acid content on the IFT of oil/N2.

Instrument and experimental procedure

A high-pressure IFT measurement apparatus was used to evaluate IFT between N2 gas and hydrocarbon mixtures at different pressures under the influence of acid number. A detailed description of the experimental apparatus and operating procedure was reported in28,29. So, in summary, to ensure that N2 gas in the storage tank of the IFT measurement apparatus is pure, at the beginning, the storage tank was purged with N2 gas using a combined method of pressure-cycle and evacuation-replacement three times. Next, the apparatus cell was filled with a hydrocarbon mixture of gasoline and diesel fuel, and then the pressure of the cell was adjusted to the desired value. Finally, photographs were continuously taken from the moment the N2 gas bubble was released into the hydrocarbon mixture. The pendant drop-selected plane method was used to compute the IFT value of the N2/oil system30. The IFT value reported in this work is an average of four IFT measurements. In the study of31, it was demonstrated that the IFT data obtained from the apparatus are highly reliable and accurate with an uncertainty of 2.3%.

Results and discussion

This work’s purpose is to investigate the effect of acid number in a range of 0.0 ~ 0.8 g.L− 1 on IFT between N2 gas and hydrocarbon blending of gasoline and diesel fuel (0.0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 vol% of gasoline in diesel fuel) under the influence of pressure in a range of 1 ~ 35 bar. A total of 100 experiments were performed at 298 K.

Impact of pressure on IFT

In this part, the effect of pressure on the IFT between N2 gas and petroleum fraction was investigated under the pressure range of 1 ~ 35 bar at 298 K. The petroleum fractions were the combination of different percentages of gasoline in diesel fuel (i.e., 0.0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 vol% of gasoline) with different organic acid contents (0.0, 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 g.L− 1 of benzoic acid). Based on Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, the IFT of the N2/hydrocarbon liquid system exhibited different behaviors with increasing pressure for different acid contents and especially different compositions. In the absence of organic acid content, the IFT between N2 and gasoline initially increased with increasing pressure and then decreased, whereas the IFT of the N2/diesel fuel system decreased. According to the results of IFT between gasoline and N2 gas, increasing the IFT up to 15 bar is attributed to N2 molecules and the behavior of the light ends of gasoline, which causes an increase in the kinetic energy of N2 molecules as pressure increases based on the kinetic theory of gases, as a result, Gibbs free energy of interface increases, thereby raising the IFT32,33. However, for pressures above 15 bar, the effect of Gibbs free energy of interface decreases and the increase in pressure causes rising the N2 solubility in gasoline, thereby reducing the IFT17,21. If the IFT of the oil/N2 system increases by injecting N2 gas, the EOR occurs similarly to the immiscible injection method in that the EOR efficiency of immiscible injection is lower compared to that of miscible injection34,35. The presence of organic acid compounds in oil caused the downward trend of IFT with the increased pressure, which provides the EOR by miscible injection. The aliphatic compounds almost created the miscible injection condition, while aromatic compounds tended to establish immiscible injection. In other words, the presence of aliphatic compounds compared to aromatic and isoparaffin hydrocarbons made the EOR approach to the miscible injection mechanism. The organic acid compounds reduced the presence effect of aromatic compounds and so make closer the oil/N2 system towards miscible injection; however, organic acid compounds do not have much effect on aliphatic compounds to improve miscible injection.

Impact of oil composition on IFT

To assess the impact of aromatic and aliphatic compounds on IFT, petroleum fractions were prepared by blending gasoline with diesel fuel in a range of 0.0 ~ 100 vol%. The IFT alteration for a pressure range of 1 ~ 35 bar is about 8 dynes.cm− 1 for aromatic hydrocarbons e.g., gasoline according to Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, while for aliphatic hydrocarbons such as diesel fuel, it is less than 4 dynes.cm− 1. It can be deduced that for pressure change, IFT has a robust behavior to alter in the presence of aliphatic hydrocarbons compared to aromatic hydrocarbons. The turning point is 15 bar for gasoline and 25 bar for diesel fuel. Therefore, by considering the change in turning points during the fraction of gasoline in diesel fuel increased for more than 25 vol%, IFT tends to the behavior of diesel fuel/N2 system demonstrating the great effect of aliphatic compounds on IFT compared to the aromatic compounds. On the other hand, diesel fuel has heavier compounds than gasoline, thus the impact of heavier compounds on the IFT between hydrocarbon and N2 gas is greater, which is confirmed by the others’ results17,22,36.

According to Figs. 6, 7, 8 and 9, it is obvious that the IFT increased at all pressures and organic acid contents as the gasoline fraction in diesel fuel decreased, which means the IFT between N2 and heavier hydrocarbons is higher than IFT of N2/lighter hydrocarbons. This is due to the density difference between N2 gas and petroleum fraction because the IFT is directly related to the density difference between the liquid and gas phases according to Eq. (3) in31. IFT was almost linear under increasing the diesel fuel fraction in gasoline at all pressures except 15 and 25 bar. This difference was less and less with the increase in the organic acid content of more than 0.8 g.L− 1. Adding diesel fuel to gasoline more, the long chain aliphatic hydrocarbons prevent the dissolution of N2 gas, thereby increasing the IFT. The aromatic compounds caused an upward trend in IFT in terms of increasing pressure, while the presence effect of the aromatic compounds decreased as the acid number increased, as a result, the IFT of the oil/N2 system had a downward trend and thus the EOR improves by the miscible injection. The presence of heavier hydrocarbons compared to the light ones caused the creation of an immiscible injection mechanism in the EOR.

Impact of acid number on IFT

In this part, the effect of organic acid content on IFT between hydrocarbon mixtures and N2 gas is investigated at a temperature of 298 K and a pressure range of 1 to 35 bar. The organic acid content of the hydrocarbon mixtures was adjusted to 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 g.L− 1. Based on Figs. 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14, an increase in the organic acid compound results in a linear trend in the IFT as the pressure increases. The trend of IFT between N2 gas and aromatic hydrocarbon dominant in the mixture (less than 25 vol% of diesel fuel in gasoline) almost decreased under increasing the organic acid content. For aromatic hydrocarbons such as gasoline, the IFT alteration under increasing acid number is higher than that of aliphatic hydrocarbons such as diesel fuel. The lower alteration in IFT indicates that the organic acidic content has a weak influence compared to the other components of the hydrocarbon mixture. Furthermore, the lower alteration in IFT indicates that it is not dependent on variations in the acid number. In other words, the presence of heavy aliphatic hydrocarbons weakens the effect of the acid number on the IFT of the hydrocarbon/N2 gas, whereas light aromatic hydrocarbons lack this ability and do not have a similar effect on the IFT.

Conclusions

In this study, the IFT between N2 gas and different fractions of gasoline in diesel fuel (0.0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 vol%) were evaluated under a pressure range of 1 to 35 bar in the presence of various organic acid contents (0.0, 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 g.L− 1 of benzoic acid) at an ambient temperature of 298 K. IFT of N2/aliphatic hydrocarbon had a downward trend during pressure increase even in the presence of different organic acid contents; however, for aromatic hydrocarbons, Gibbs free energy of interface increased due to the increase in the kinetic energy of N2 molecules as pressure increased, as a result, the IFT raised. This is not always stable and the effect of Gibbs free energy of interface is lost under increasing the pressure to more than 15 bar, thus the incremental pressure causes more dissolution of N2 gas in the hydrocarbon, thereby decreasing the IFT.

Aliphatic hydrocarbons caused less IFT alteration as pressure increased compared to aromatic hydrocarbons, which implies the significant effect of long-chain aliphatic compounds on IFT. As the hydrocarbon becomes heavier, or in other words, with increasing density of the hydrocarbon, the IFT has an upward trend because the density difference between the hydrocarbon and the N2 gas increases. Actually, the long-chain hydrocarbons prevent the dissolution of the N2 gas. The slope of the IFT curve in terms of the hydrocarbon composition depicts the potential energy of interface formation. Any change regarding to make potential energy of interface formation more, gas bubbles with a smaller size be created through breakage. During the increase of acid number, the IFT of N2/aromatic hydrocarbon followed a decreasing trend, although it was ineffective on IFT between N2 and aliphatic hydrocarbons such as diesel fuel. The increase in the acid number of hydrocarbon roughly caused the IFT to change linearly during pressure increased. Eventually, a new correlation was proposed to predict IFT, considering factors including pressure, oil composition and organic acid content with an average relative error of less than 2.6%.

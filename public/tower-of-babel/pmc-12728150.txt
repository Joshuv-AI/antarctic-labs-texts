Extracellular vesicles-derived non-coding RNA in leukemias and pre-leukemic syndromes: a systematic review
Authors: Narjes Seddighi, Malihe Najafpour, Mohammadreza Riyahi, Sepideh Mahmoudzadeh, Mehdi Talebi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHematological malignancies, including leukemia, lymphoma, and multiple myeloma, are among the most aggressive cancers, with high mortality rates and limited early diagnostic tools. Exosomes, nano-sized extracellular vesicles secreted by numerous cells including tumor cells, have emerged as promising biomarkers due to their stability, non-invasive isolation, and disease-specific molecular cargo, particularly non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs). MethodThis systematic review, conducted following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, evaluated the diagnostic and prognostic potential of exosomal ncRNAs in hematological malignancies by analyzing 16 studies from five databases (Scopus, PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and ProQuest). ResultKey findings revealed that exosomal microRNAs, such as miR-532, miR-10b, and miR-21 in acute myeloid leukemia, miR-326 in acute lymphoblastic leukemia, and miR-494 in chronic myeloid leukemia, exhibit significant differential expression between patients and healthy controls, correlating with disease progression, treatment resistance, and survival outcomes. Moreover, long non-coding RNAs and circular RNAs were identified as potential biomarkers in myelodysplastic syndromes and leukemia. This review highlights the role of exosomal ncRNAs in liquid biopsies for early detection and monitoring. However, heterogeneity in isolation methods and sample sizes emphasizes the need for standardized protocols. ConclusionThese findings highlight the transformative potential of exosomal ncRNAs in precision oncology, offering novel ways for non-invasive diagnostics, prognostic stratification, and targeted therapies in hematological malignancies. Additional studies are necessary to validate these biomarkers and explore their clinical applications.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-025-06385-6.

Introduction

Hematological malignancies are a group of cancers originating from blood cells, bone marrow, and the lymphatic system. Among the particularly aggressive and often deadly hematological malignancies are leukemia, multiple myeloma, and lymphomas (including Hodgkin’s and non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma) (Zhang et al. 2024; Mohseni et al. 2025); which, depending on their specific type, can exhibit high malignancy, leading to high mortality rates and poor clinical prognosis (Moloudizargari et al. 2021). In 2022, the Global Cancer Observatory (GCO) reported approximately a total of 1.3 million incidences and 0.7 million mortalities worldwide for hematological malignancies; Furthermore, projections suggest that the incidence of these cancers may increase three- to four-fold over the next decade (An et al. 2023).

Although cancer treatments such as bone marrow stem cell transplantation have advanced significantly, their effectiveness is limited by ongoing challenges like drug resistance, poor prognosis, and the recurrence of the disease (Zhang et al. 2024). One of the primary factors contributing to these challenges is the lack of reliable methods for early detection. Given the broad demographic distribution of hematological malignancies and their aggressive nature, the development of effective early diagnostic tools, such as liquid biopsy, is a critical priority. Liquid biopsy has emerged as a promising non-invasive diagnostic approach due to its non-invasive nature (Moloudizargari et al. 2021).

Extracellular Vesicles (EVs) are vesicles with a phospholipid bilayer membrane that are secreted into the extracellular space by most cells under conditions of hypoxia, oxidative stress, inflammation, aging, and apoptosis. EVs are divided into three categories based on size: exosomes, apoptotic vesicles, and microvesicles.

Exosomes, present in all body fluids, are nano-sized (40–160 nm) vesicles containing lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids (Mohseni et al. 2025). These are secreted by both normal and malignant cells with different cargos (Dong et al. 2025). While all cells release exosomes, their quantity and molecular content vary significantly depending on cell type and pathological state. Studies have demonstrated that malignant cells secrete a higher number of exosomes than normal cells, and the molecular cargo of these vesicles reflects the type and stage of malignancy (Jahromi et al. 2023). Owing to their high stability and ability to carry disease-specific molecular markers, exosomes, particularly their genetic contents like non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), are gaining attention as potential biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognosis of hematological malignancies (Moloudizargari et al. 2021; Jahromi et al. 2023).

Exosomes serve as key mediators of intercellular communication and significantly influence the microenvironment of their cells of origin. Malignant cell-derived exosomes have been shown to promote tumor progression and contribute to therapeutic resistance by modulating immune responses, facilitating recurrence, and supporting drug resistance mechanisms, highlighting their potential therapeutic relevance (Morckhoven et al. 2023).

One of the most important components of exosomes is non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), which provide valuable information regarding the type and stage of malignancy (Jahromi et al. 2023). These ribonucleotides regulate a range of fundamental biological processes, including cell division, apoptosis, immune responses, and gene expression at the transcriptional and translational levels (Huang et al. 2023). Although only a small fraction of human genes encode proteins, a large portion is transcribed into ncRNAs with either regulatory or housekeeping functions.

Various classes of ncRNAs, including microRNAs (miRNAs), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), and circular RNAs (circRNAs), are found within exosomes and in free forms in extracellular fluids (Babin et al. 2021). Research comparing free and exosome-derived ncRNAs indicates that exosomal ncRNAs are superior biomarkers due to their enhanced specificity and stability (Damanti et al. 2021). Given their stability and involvement in post-transcriptional regulation, exosomal ncRNAs can provide comprehensive information regarding the genetic and functional status of malignant cells (Jahromi et al. 2023).

ncRNAs, including miRNAs, circRNAs, and lncRNAs, play key regulatory roles in leukemia and other hematological malignancies. miRNAs primarily mediate post-transcriptional gene silencing by binding target mRNAs, leading to translational repression or degradation (Larivera et al. 2023). CircRNAs and lncRNAs modulate gene expression through multiple mechanisms, including sponging miRNAs, interacting with transcription factors or RNA-binding proteins, and affecting mRNA stability, translation, and epigenetic regulation (Singh et al. 2021; Liu et al. 2025). CircRNAs are particularly stable due to their circular structure and can exert prolonged effects on proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation, metabolism, and drug response (Liu et al. 2025). In AML, circPTK2 and circ_0003256 sponge miR-582-3p to enhance anti-apoptotic protein stability, glycolysis, and cell cycle progression (Shang et al. 2022), while lncRNAs such as TMEM147-AS1 and HOTAIR act as ceRNAs to modulate oncogenic pathways (Dashti et al. 2025; Xu and Wang 2025). Collectively, these ncRNAs contribute to leukemia pathogenesis and have potential as diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic targets (Sun et al. 2024).

As mentioned, Exosomal ncRNAs expressed by malignant cells may serve as potential biomarkers for early and rapid diagnosis through analysis of their structure and expression levels. They hold promise for early and rapid diagnosis, contributing to improved prognostic assessments and patient outcomes in hematological malignancies (Moloudizargari et al. 2021).

Given the crucial role of exosomal ncRNAs, numerous studies have been conducted in recent years to explore their diagnostic and therapeutic potential in hematological malignancies (Huang et al. 2023).

Because most studies on ncRNAs are original and have investigated specific ncRNAs separately, there is a need for a systematic study that comprehensively evaluates and classifies EV-derived ncRNAs in hematological malignancies, assesses their importance and potential applications, and investigates their use as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets.

In this study, we reviewed the classes of exosomal ncRNAs in hematological malignancies and systematically evaluated their significance and potential applications, particularly as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets, by reviewing recent human research focusing on exosomal ncRNAs identified in patients with hematological malignancies using standard research methods.

Method

Study design

This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines (Diagram 2020).

Literature search

A comprehensive search was conducted across five electronic databases: Scopus, PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and ProQuest. The search was conducted in English, without restrictions on publication date, and covered all records up to February 4, 2025. Additionally, a manual search was performed using the Google Scholar search engine, screening the first 300 results. Keywords such as “leukemia”, “hematologic malignancy”, “exosome”, “tumor-derived exosome”, and other related MeSH terms and keywords were employed. The detailed search strategies tailored to each database are provided in Supplementary File 1.

Eligibility criteria

All original research articles investigating tumor-derived exosomes and their non-coding RNA content in human samples of hematological malignancies were included. Studies that relied exclusively on in vitro models or animal experiments were excluded. However, papers that used exosomes derived from cell lines in addition to human samples were included, provided that human-derived data were analyzed as part of the study. Additionally, because in-vitro culture conditions can themselves alter exosome composition and introduce experimental artifacts, we focused exclusively on studies that directly analyzed exosomes derived from patient samples. Studies that relied on exosomes derived from cultured samples were not included in the formal analysis and were instead addressed only within the discussion.

Selection process

The retrieved studies were imported into EndNote version 20 for reference management, and duplicate records were removed. The remaining articles were screened independently by two authors in a two-stage process: initial screening based on titles and abstracts, followed by full-text screening. Any disagreements between the two reviewers were resolved through discussion with a third author. Additionally, to ensure comprehensive coverage, the reference lists of the included articles were manually reviewed to identify any potentially relevant studies that may have been missed.

Data collection process

Data were extracted using a standardized data extraction sheet and summarized in two tables. Table 1 includes information on the type of cancer, type of biomarker (if available), exosomal non-coding RNA content, main findings, and corresponding references. Table 2 presents the first author’s surname, publication year, study characteristics, exosome isolation method, exosome source, sample size, and other relevant information. Data extraction was performed independently by two authors, and the extracted data were subsequently verified by a third author.

Quality assessment of included studies

The potential risk of bias of the included study was evaluated by the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal tool for cross-sectional, case–control studies, and cohort studies, which comprised 8, 10, and 11 items, respectively. The items for cross-sectional studies were included: inclusion criteria, study subject, exposure, and condition measurement, identification of confounding, methods for dealing with it, outcome measurement, and statistical analysis (Institute 2017). Additionally, the items for case–control studies were included: Comparability of groups, Appropriate matching of cases and controls, Use of the same criteria for case/control selection, exposure and condition measurement, Identification of confounding factors and strategies to deal with them, outcome assessment, adequate exposure period, and appropriate statistical analysis method (Institute 2017). Furthermore, the items for cohort studies included: similarity of groups, consistency, validity and reliability of exposure measurement, identification and management of confounding factors, confirmation that participants were outcome-free at baseline, validity and reliability of outcome measurement, adequacy and completeness of follow-up, strategies to address incomplete follow-up, and appropriateness of statistical analysis methods (Adelaide 2020).

Effect measures and synthesis methods

Due to the substantial heterogeneity in cancer types and exosomal content across the included studies, quantitative synthesis was not feasible, and a meta-analysis was not performed. Consequently, the findings of each study were reported through a qualitative synthesis.

Results

Study selection

In total, 2691 records were identified across five databases. After removing 1466 duplicates, 1225 articles remained for initial screening. Based on title and abstract screening, 38 records were selected for full-text evaluation. Following the secondary screening, 16 articles met the inclusion criteria and were included in the final analysis of this study (Fig. 1, Table 1).

Study characteristics and results of individual studies

AML

Xia Lin et al. identified exosome-derived miR-532 as a potential predictor of survival in patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML), with elevated levels associated with favorable overall survival. The study reported that miR-532 expression levels were comparable between AML patients and healthy controls, and no significant differences were observed across white blood cell counts, French-American-British (FAB) subtypes, gene mutations, or cytogenetic risk categories. Additionally, an inverse correlation was noted between miR-532 expression and metabolic processes related to fructose and glutamine production (Lin et al. 2020). In a separate study, serum-derived extracellular vesicle miR-10b (EV-miR-10b) was identified as a prognostic biomarker in AML, with elevated levels significantly associated with shorter survival. Moreover, the same study found that miR-10b, miR-542, and miR-216b were overexpressed considerably in AML patients compared to healthy individuals, whereas miR-146a, miR-34a, and miR-192 were downregulated in AML samples (Fang et al. 2020).

Regarding lncRNAs, a study identified five exosomal lncRNAs that were differentially expressed between AML patients and healthy controls. Specifically, LINC00265, LINC00467, and UCA1 were downregulated in AML patients, while SNHG1 was upregulated. Increased exosomal expression of LINC00265 and LINC00467 was associated with complete remission (CR), but not with non-remission (NR) or partial remission (PR). Additionally, elevated LINC00265 expression and reduced SNHG1 expression were linked to patients who underwent allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT). These findings suggest that exosomal lncRNAs, due to their high stability in plasma, may serve as promising biomarkers for AML diagnosis and treatment monitoring (Xiao et al. 2022).

Li et al. identified a total of 136 miRNAs in exosomes derived from AML patients and healthy controls, with 23 miRNAs exclusively detected in AML samples and 16 miRNAs specific to healthy individuals. Among these, miR-185-5p, miR-4433b-3p, miR-199a-5p, miR-451a, and miR-151a-3p were significantly downregulated in AML, whereas miR-10a-5p, miR-155-5p, miR-100-5p, miR-146b-5p, and let-7a-5p were significantly upregulated, with miR-10a-5p showing the greatest fold change (log2FC = 4.89) (Li et al. 2023a).

Further evidence demonstrated that exosomal miR-125b was upregulated in AML patients compared to healthy controls and served as a prognostic biomarker indicative of higher relapse rates and mortality, particularly in intermediate-risk AML cases (Jiang et al. 2018). Similarly, miR-92a was significantly elevated in patients with AML and myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) compared to healthy individuals. This miRNA was implicated in resistance to cytarabine (Ara-C) treatment via activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target (Li et al. 2022).

miR-21 was identified as a significant diagnostic and prognostic biomarker, with markedly higher expression in AML patients compared to healthy individuals. Increased levels of miR-21 were significantly associated with unfavorable cytogenetic risk groups, poorer overall survival, reduced relapse-free survival, and adverse clinical parameters (Li et al. 2021). Lastly, up-regulation of exosomal circ_0004136 in pediatric AML than HC promotes the progression, influencing key molecular pathways that enhance leukemia cell proliferation, survival, or migration, highlighting its potential as a biomarker or therapeutic target (Bi et al. 2021).

ALL

A recent study on patients with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) demonstrated that exosomal miR-326 holds promise as a prognostic biomarker. Specifically, this exosomal miRNA was found to be upregulated in the minimal residual disease (MRD) group, which is resistant to chemotherapy, compared to the MRD-negative group, which is sensitive to chemotherapy. Furthermore, exosomes were isolated from the RN95 and Nalm6 cell lines, with miR-326 expression in the RN95 cell line being approximately 10 times higher than in the Nalm6 cell line (Saffari et al. 2024).

Besides, temporal profiling of circulating exosomal miRNAs in pediatric B-ALL patients demonstrated distinct and treatment-dependent alterations in miR-128-3p and miR-222-3p across the four induction time points. miR-128-3p showed a substantial decline from diagnosis to day 15 (log2FC = − 4.49, p = 1.31 × 10−4), consistent with early treatment-mediated cytoreduction. Although expression partially recovered by day 33 (log2FC = + 2.28, p = 7.06 × 10−3), levels remained significantly lower than baseline (log2FC = − 2.23, p = 1.42 × 10−2), indicating an incompletely restored regulatory pattern by the end of induction. In contrast, miR-222-3p exhibited a fluctuating, non-linear trajectory, with an initial rise at day 8 (log2FC = + 2.83, p = 1.31 × 10−4), a subsequent decrease at day 15 (log2FC = − 1.68, p = 7.71 × 10−3), and renewed upregulation by day 33 relative to diagnosis (log2FC = + 1.05, p = 1.93 × 10−2). These dynamic shifts reflect differential, time-dependent miRNA regulation during induction therapy in ALL patients (Rzepiel et al. 2023).

CML

Recent studies on chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML) patients have demonstrated that serum-derived exosomes containing miR-494 are a valuable tool for monitoring tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) therapy. Specifically, miR-494 levels were significantly elevated during the blast crisis and accelerated phases of CML, compared to the chronic phase. Additionally, the study identified 10 miRNAs that were upregulated in CML cell lines (K562 and KU812), including miR-191*, miR-197, miR-198, miR-210, miR-296, miR-320, miR-373-5p, miR-409-3p, miR-494, and miR-574. However, no significant differences were observed in miR-494 expression between TKI-treated cells (after 72 h) and the control group. The study suggests that miR-494, derived from exosomes in the serum of CML patients, can serve as a valuable biomarker for therapy monitoring (Shibuta et al. 2022). Additionally, the study by Kazuma Ohyashiki et al. found that the plasma level of miR-215 and miR-134 had more than 1.5-fold change between 7 STOP-IM patients and seven healthy controls, and also, exosomal miR-215 was significantly downregulated in 20 stop imatinib (STOP-IM) patients compared with 28 healthy controls, which indicated the level of this miRNA in plasma can reflect the level on exosomes of STOP-IM CML patients and can be used for monitoring of sustained UMRD after discontinuing IM in CML patients (Ohyashiki et al. 2016).

CLL

In another study on CLL patients, Mosavar Farahani et al. identified miR-202-3p, miR-1290, and miR-628-3p as miRNAs enriched in exosomes derived from CLL cells compared to the parental cells. Among these, miR-202-3p exhibited consistently high levels in all six exosome samples compared to parental CLL cells. In vivo analysis further confirmed that miR-202-3p was enriched in exosomes derived from the plasma of CLL patients compared to healthy controls (p value < 0.05). Additionally, miR-29a and miR-21 were more highly expressed in CLL-derived exosomes compared to those from healthy controls, but the miR-21 differences were not statistically significant. These findings suggest that miR-202-3p plays a role in the biology and behavior of CLL and warrants further investigation as a potential tumor suppressor in CLL (Farahani et al. 2015).

MDS

A study comparing patients with MDS and aplastic anemia (AA) to healthy controls revealed several differentially expressed miRNAs. Specifically, miR-1260a, miR-3610, miR-4687-5p, miR-3200-5p, miR-4651, miR-1180-3p, miR-324-3p, miR-1281, miR-423-3p, miR-133a-3p, miR-1247-5p, miR-671-3p, miR-574-3p, miR-675-3p, and miR-106b-5p were upregulated in MDS patients compared to healthy controls. In contrast, miR-29c-5p, miR-1193, miR-382-5p, miR-19b-3p, miR-30e-5p, miR-378i, and miR-126-5p were downregulated in MDS patients. Furthermore, 21 exosomal miRNAs exhibited strong associations with MDS and may serve as potential diagnostic and/or prognostic biomarkers. Among these, miR-378i, miR-574-3p, miR-196a-5p, miR-3200-3p, miR-196b-5p, miR-1180-3p, miR-4267, and miR-19b-3p were identified as having diagnostic value in both MDS and AA (Giudice et al. 2018). Finally, a study by Ningyu Li et al. found that miR-103-3p was significantly downregulated in MDS patients compared to healthy controls, further supporting its potential role as a biomarker in MDS (Li et al. 2023b).

Risk of bias assessment

The result of risk of bias assessment were summarized in 3 tables in supplementary material (Tables 3, 4 and 5). most of the included study had no considerable bias in items. Based on assessment, the most items pf bias in the included studies were confounding identification and control of them for both cross-sectional and case–control studies, exposure measurement for cross-sectional studies, exposure duration for case–control studies, and group similarity and management of incomplete follow-up for cohort studies. The study design was not explicitly reported in several of the included articles; therefore, we inferred the type of study based on the methods section and conducted the quality assessment accordingly using the appropriate JBI critical appraisal checklist (supplementary file 1).

Discussion

Hematologic malignancies rank among the ten most prevalent cancers worldwide (Siegel et al. 2018). Early intervention followed by targeted therapy significantly reduces cancer-related mortality and adverse outcomes. Exosomes are nano-sized vesicles exhibit high stability, can effectively penetrate tumor sites, and demonstrate greater resistance to the immunosuppressive effects of tumor microenvironment compared to intact cells (Hong and Kim 2022).

Tumor-derived exosomes have been extensively investigated across various cancer types and hold promise as both diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets (Tai et al. 2018). Among the cargos carried by exosomes, miRNAs play a pivotal role in several cancer-related processes (Jahromi et al. 2023). Numerous studies have investigated the involvement of exosome-derived miRNAs in the diagnosis and progression of leukemia. Accordingly, the present systematic review focuses on exosome-derived ncRNA in patients with hematologic malignancies and evaluates their potential roles in prognosis and diagnosis.

Mechanisms of action for exosome in cancer

Exosomes contain various compounds such as lipids, proteins, signaling receptors, adhesion molecules, cytoskeletal proteins, growth factors and cytokines, and nucleic acids including DNA, miRNA, ncRNA and mRNA (Hong and Kim 2022; Ghaffari et al. 2023). For instance, exosomal lncRNAs—owing to their remarkable stability in plasma—have potential as valuable biomarkers for diagnosing and monitoring the treatment of AML (Shang et al. 2022). Exosomes are one of the critical mediators for cellular communication and can change the fate of the target cells (Ghaffari et al. 2023). Exosome facilitate cellular communication between cancer cells and their microenvironment, leading to altered signaling pathways. In our review, exosomal miR-92a was shown to contribute to Ara-C resistance by activating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, illustrating this principle. Furthermore, they are potentially safer compared to their parent cells with respect to replication and tumor formation (Pinky et al. 2021). The majority of immune cells, like lymphocytes, macrophages, Natural Killer (NK) cells, and Dendritic Cells (DCs) can produce exosomes (Zhang et al. 2019). The function of immune- derived exosomes has been explored in numerous cancers (Ghaffari et al. 2023; Pitt et al. 2016; Shoae-Hassani et al. 2017). These examples underscore the wide-ranging roles of cell-derived exosomes in cancer research and therapy. Specifically, the increased expression of miR-26a-5p and miR-101-3p, alongside the reduced expression of miR-23b-5p, miR-339-3p, and miR-425-5p in AML samples, suggests that these miRNAs may serve as promising therapeutic targets (Diagram 2020). Moreover, miR-21 has been identified as a significant diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in AML patients (Institute 2017), while miR-326 shows potential as a prognostic biomarker in ALL patients (Institute 2017).

Application of tumor-derived exosome in cancers

Tumor-derived exosomes have been thoroughly investigated in various cancer types, including melanoma, breast cancer, renal cancer, and hematologic malignancies (Rezaie et al. 2022). These vesicles have emerged as promising diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets (Tai et al. 2018). Previous researches on tumor-derived exosomes have demonstrated that exosomes released by tumor cells influence several critical processes including apoptosis, angiogenesis, pathogenesis, immune evasion and chemotherapy resistance (Ghaffari et al. 2023).

As mentioned above, exosomes exert their influence through their molecular cargo, which can modulate gene expression and alter or change cellular responses upon interaction with the tumor microenvironment (Soleimani 2024). The expression or mis-expression of specific miRNA in exosome contents has been observed in patients with hematological malignancies (Jahromi et al. 2023). Therefore, the investigation of exosomes and their molecular cargo (especially ncRNA) holds significant promise for improving cancer diagnosis and treatment. Numerous studies have highlighted the role of miRNAs in cancer, revealing that each miRNA contributes distinctly to cancer diagnosis and treatment, and even the drug resistance. For example, miR-9 has been associated with enhanced tumor growth; miR-181c, miR-105 and miR-200 have been linked to increased risk of metastasis in breast cancer; and miR-21 has been implicated in promoting drug resistance. ZFAS1, an lncRNA, is associated with enhanced growth and metastasis in gastrointestinal cancer, while miR-222-3p promotes tumor progression in ovarian cancer (Tai et al. 2018). Elevated levels of AR-V7-RNA in the plasma of individuals with prostate cancer (Del Re et al. 2017), and increased expression of EGFR-VIII mRNA in the serum of individuals with glioblastoma have also been suggested as a potential prognostic and diagnostic biomarker (Skog et al. 2008). Furthermore, miR-30a, miR-222 and miR-100-5p are recognized for their involvement in the development of drug resistance in cancer therapy (Qin et al. 2017; Chen et al. 2014).

Tumor-derived exosome application in hematological malignancies

Hematological malignancies account for a significant proportion of cancer cases worldwide. Numerous studies have investigated the role of specific exosome-derived miRNAs in the diagnosis and progression of leukemia. In this systematic review we analyzed recent human studies focusing on exosome-derived ncRNA in hematological malignancies. Several miRNAs have been implicated in the pathogenesis of various hematological malignancies (Ghaffari et al. 2023). For instance, miR-17-92 cluster(miR-17، miR-18a، miR-19a، miR-19b، miR-20a، miR-20b، و miR-92a) facilitates leukemia development through upregulating angiogenesis, a process driven by increased endothelial cell migration and tube formation (Umezu et al. 2013). miR-17-92, also referred to as ‘oncomiR-1’, primarily targets Phosphatase and Tensin Homolog (PTEN), E2F transcription factors, and the TGF-β signaling pathway (Mogilyansky and Rigoutsos 2013).

miR-125b seems to serve as a critical regulator of both differentiation and apoptosis in myeloid cells by targeting a range of factors involved in these processes (CBFB, ABTB1, BAK1). Based on current evidence, miR-125b and miR-10b may serve as a prognostic biomarker in AML patients (Fang et al. 2020; Jiang et al. 2018).

Among the studied exosome-derived miRNAs, miR-21 has been identified as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker. It is associated with an unfavorable cytogenetic risk group and correlates with worse overall survival, reduced relapse-free survival as well as adverse clinical parameters (Li et al. 2021). miR-21 primarily targets three key molecules: Phosphatase and Tensin Homolog (PTEN), Tropomyosin 1 (TPM1), and Programmed Cell Death 4 (PDCD4) (Jenike and Halushka 2021). Other miRNAs listed in Table 1 show either with decreased or increased expression in AML; however, further research is required to confirm them as diagnostic biomarkers. Honick et al. demonstrated that miR-150, miR-1246 and miR-155 are enriched in the serum of mice with AML and may serve as diagnostic biomarkers (Hornick et al. 2015).

Exosome-derived miRNAs have demonstrated potential as monitoring biomarkers in CML patients treated with tyrosine kinase inhibitors. For instance, miR-494 has been reported to target PTEN and may be useful for monitoring treatment response to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKI) (Shibuta et al. 2022). Additionally, miR-215 could be used when stopping imatinib treatment for detecting the CML patients with undetectable minimal residual disease (UMRD). Six genes—WNK lysine deficient protein kinase 1 (WNK1), activated leukocyte cell adhesion molecule (ALCAM), retinoblastoma 1 (RB1), protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type T (PTPRT), activin A receptor type IIB (ACVR2B), and catenin beta interacting protein 1 (CTNNBIP1)—have been experimentally confirmed as potential targets for miR-215, with strong supporting evidence (Ohyashiki et al. 2016).

In other subgroups of MPN, a total of 2866 circRNAs molecules have been identified in essential thrombocythemia (ET) patients and it seems these circRNAs may hold potential for diagnosis and treatment in future (Wang et al. 2021).

Limited studies have been conducted on exosome-derived miRNA in the ALL patients. miR-326 is recognized as a tumor suppressor that disrupts the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. It also targets the transcription factor 4 and Bcl-2. miR-326 has been identified as a prognostic biomarker in ALL patients which helps to identify drug resistance cases (Saffari et al. 2024). Additionally, a study by Rios de Los Rios J et al. reported increased expression of miR-146a-5p, miR-181b-5p and miR-199b-3p in ALL cell lines (Rios los Rios et al. 2022).

Although increased expression of exosome-derived miRNAs has been observed in CLL patients, further research is required to clarify the role of these exosomes in leukemia development (Farahani et al. 2015; Yeh et al. 2015).

A review on exosome-derived miRNA in MM patients, revealed that downregulation of let7b and miR-18a is associated with poor outcomes, and downregulation of miR-165p, miR-15a5p and, miR-20a5p, miR-175p is associated with tolerance to bortezomib, a common proteasome inhibitor used in MM treatment (Zhang et al. 2016; Manier et al. 2017).

As previously noted, exosomes mediate communication between cancer cells within the tumor microenvironment. Specific miRNA carried by these exosomes: such as miR-150, miR-155, miR-4532, miR-7977 and miR-425-5p in AML, miR‑146a‑5p, miR‑181b‑5p, miR‑199b‑3p in ALL, miR-150 and miR-155 in CLL and miR-126, miR-711 and miR-125b in CML, significantly contribute to tumorigenesis by modulating the tumor microenvironment (Chen et al. 2023).

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, while the analyzed ncRNAs are operationally defined as “exosomal” based on the applied isolation protocol, their precise sub-cellular source, whether intraluminal exosomal, surface-bound, or co-isolated with other components, remains uncertain and requires further validation using more stringent methods such as density gradients or immuno-capture techniques. Second, the exclusion of in vitro studies limits the mechanistic insights that could be drawn from functional experiments, although this choice strengthens the focus on clinically relevant human biomarkers. Finally, heterogeneity among included studies, small sample sizes, and the absence of meta-analytic synthesis restrict the generalizability and robustness of the findings.

Conclusion

This systematic review highlights the potential of tumor-derived exosomes as biomarkers for diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment monitoring in hematological malignancies. In particular, exosome-derived miRNAs show promise as diagnostic, prognostic, and monitoring biomarkers. However, further studies are needed to strengthen the evidence base and more comprehensively elucidate their clinical utility.

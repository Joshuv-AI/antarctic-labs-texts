A note on concentration inequalities for the overlapped batch mean variance estimators for Markov chains
Eric Moulines, Alexey Naumov, Sergey Samsonov

Abstract
In this paper, we study the concentration properties of quadratic forms associated with Markov chains using the martingale decomposition method introduced by Atchadé and Cattaneo (2014). In particular, we derive concentration inequalities for the overlapped batch mean (OBM) estimators of the asymptotic variance for uniformly geometrically ergodic Markov chains. Our main result provides an explicit control of the p p -th moment of the difference between the OBM estimator and the asymptotic variance of the Markov chain with explicit dependence upon p p and mixing time of the underlying Markov chain.

We consider a Markov kernel
\mathrm{P}

Applications to the concentration properties of the OBM estimator

In this section, we study the concentration properties of the overlapped batch mean (OBM) estimator
Meketon and
Schmeiser 1984
;
Flegal and
Jones 2010
of the asymptotic variance of the Markov chain. Assume that the Markov kernel
\mathrm{P}
satisfies the UGE assumption
1
. In this case, for any bounded function
f:\mathsf{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
, it is known (see e.g.
(
Douc
et al. 2018
, Chapter 21)
), that the central limit theorem holds:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\{f(Z_{k})-\pi(f)\}\to\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(f))\;,

(10)

where
\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(f)
is the
asymptotic variance
defined as

\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(f)=\rho^{(f)}(0)+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\rho^{(f)}(\ell)\;,\text{ where }\rho^{(f)}(\ell)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\{f(Z_{0})-\pi(f)\}\{f(Z_{\ell})-\pi(f)\}]

(11)

is the
l
-th order autocovariance under stationary. The overlapped batch mean (OBM) estimator
Meketon and
Schmeiser 1984
is defined as

\hat{\sigma}_{\mathsf{OBM}}^{2}(f)=\frac{b_{n}}{n-b_{n}+1}\sum_{t=0}^{n-b_{n}}(\pi_{b_{n},t}(f)-\pi_{n}(f))^{2}\;,

(12)

where we have set

\pi_{b_{n},t}(f)=\frac{1}{b_{n}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{b_{n}}f(Z_{t+\ell})\;,\quad t\in\{0,\ldots,n-b_{n}\}\;,\quad\pi_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}f(Z_{\ell})\;.

(13)

It is possible to study the properties of the OBM estimator by reducing it to the spectral estimator with a particular choice of Bartlett weight kernel
Welch 1987
,
Damerdji 1991
. At the same time, this approach requires to estimate the concentration properties of a number of remainder terms, which we can avoid when proceeding directly with the quadratic form, induced by the OBM estimator. From now on we assume, without loss of generality, that
\pi(f)=0
. Define the matrix
B=\{B_{ij}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-b_{n}+1\times n}

B_{ij}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{b_{n}},&i\leq j\leq i+b_{n}-1,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}\;.\end{cases}

Let
X=f(Z)=\{f(Z_{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. With these notations, we obtain, using the elementary transformations, that the OBM estimator can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathsf{OBM}}^{2}(f)=\frac{b_{n}}{n-b_{n}+1}\left(X^{\top}B^{\top}BX-\frac{2}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n-b_{n}+1}^{\top}BX\cdot\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}X+(n-b_{n}+1)\left(\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}X\right)^{2}\right)\;,

(14)

where
\mathbf{1}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times 1}

k\in\mathbb{N}

u=\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}-\frac{1}{n-b_{n}+1}B^{\top}\bm{1}_{n-b_{n}+1}\;,\quad v=\frac{1}{n-b_{n}+1}B^{\top}\bm{1}_{n-b_{n}+1}\;.

(15)

With the above notation, we can represent
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathsf{OBM}}^{2}(f)
from (
12

\hat{\sigma}_{\mathsf{OBM}}^{2}(f)=\frac{b_{n}}{n-b_{n}+1}X^{\top}B^{\top}BX+b_{n}(u^{\top}X)^{2}-b_{n}(v^{\top}X)^{2}\;,

(16)

Next we focus on the quadratic form

V_{n}(f)=\frac{b_{n}}{n-b_{n}+1}X^{\top}B^{\top}BX=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}w(\ell,j)f(Z_{\ell})f(Z_{j}),

where

w(\ell,j)=\frac{2b_{n}}{n-b_{n}+1}\{B^{\top}B\}_{\ell,j}

The diagonal weights in the above formula have the form:

w(\ell,\ell)=\begin{cases}\frac{\ell}{b_{n}(n-b_{n}+1)},&\text{if }1\leq\ell\leq b_{n}-1,\\
\frac{1}{n-b_{n}+1},&\text{if }b_{n}\leq\ell\leq n-b_{n}+1,\\
\frac{n-\ell+1}{b_{n}(n-b_{n}+1)},&\text{if }n-b_{n}+2\leq\ell\leq n\;.\end{cases}

\frac{2}{b_{n}(n-b_{n}+1)}(b_{n}-(\ell-j)),&\text{if }b_{n}\leq\ell\leq n-b_{n}+1,\ \ell-b_{n}+1\leq j\leq\ell-1,\\

S_{2}
, we write that

\displaystyle S_{2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\bigl(\Delta^{(0,1)}(\ell,\ell)+w(\ell,\ell)\bigr)M_{\ell}\mathrm{P}g(Z_{\ell-1})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\bigl(2w(\ell,\ell)-w(\ell,\ell-1)\bigr)M_{\ell}\mathrm{P}g(Z_{\ell-1})\;,

(111)

where in the last line we have additionally used the definition of coefficients
\Delta^{(0,1)}(\ell,\ell)
from (
1
). Hence, applying Minkowski’s inequality and an upper bound
\bigl|2w(\ell,\ell)-w(\ell,\ell-1)\bigr|\lesssim\frac{1}{b_{n}(n-b_{n}+1)}
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigl|S_{2}\bigr|^{p}\bigr]\lesssim\frac{t_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{b_{n}}\;.

(112)

Combining the above bounds concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Under assumptions of
Theorem

1
, it holds for any
p\geq 2
that

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}|\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{n}|^{p}\lesssim\frac{pt_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{\sqrt{b_{n}}}+\frac{pt_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}\sqrt{b_{n}}}{n-b_{n}+1}+\frac{t_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{b_{n}}\;.

(113)

Proof.

We estimate separately each of the terms from the error decomposition (
1
). First, note that for
\ell\geq 3
and
1\leq j\leq\ell-2
,

|\Delta^{(1,0)}(\ell,j)|\leq\frac{2}{b_{n}(n-b_{n}+1)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigl|\mathrm{P}g(Z_{\ell-1})\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-2}\Delta^{(1,0)}(\ell,j)M_{j}\bigr|^{p}\bigr]

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{\operatorname{mix}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigl|\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-2}\Delta^{(1,0)}(\ell,j)M_{j}\bigr|^{p}\bigr]

(114)

\displaystyle\lesssim pt_{\operatorname{mix}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-2}\{\Delta^{(1,0)}(\ell,j)\}^{2}M_{j}^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr]

(115)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{pt_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{(n-b_{n}+1)\sqrt{b_{n}}}\;.

(116)

Thus, applying Minkowski’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigl|\sum_{\ell=3}^{n}\mathrm{P}g(Z_{\ell-1})\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-2}\Delta^{(1,0)}(\ell,j)M_{j}\bigr|^{p}\bigr]\lesssim\frac{pt_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{\sqrt{b_{n}}}\;.

(117)

Similarly, applying Burkholder’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigl|\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}M_{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\Delta^{(0,1)}(\ell,j)\mathrm{P}g(Z_{j-1})\bigr|\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq p\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\biggl[\biggl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\Delta^{(0,1)}(\ell,j)\mathrm{P}g(Z_{j-1})\bigr)^{2}M_{\ell}^{2}\biggr)^{p/2}\biggr]

(118)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{pt_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{\sqrt{n-b_{n}+1}}\;.

(119)

Finally, applying Minkowski’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigl|\sum_{\ell=3}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-2}\Delta^{(1,1)}(\ell,j)\mathrm{P}g(Z_{\ell-1})\mathrm{P}g(Z_{j-1})\bigr|^{p}\bigr]\lesssim\frac{t_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{b_{n}}\;.

(120)

Applying now
Lemma

3
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}^{1/p}|\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{n,rem}|^{p}\lesssim\frac{pt_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}\sqrt{b_{n}}}{n-b_{n}+1}+\frac{t_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{b_{n}}\;.

(121)

Similarly, applying
Lemma

4
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}^{1/p}|\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{n,mart}|^{p}\lesssim\frac{t_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}}{b_{n}}\;.

(122)

Now it remains to combine above bounds in the decomposition (
1
) and apply Minkowski’s inequality once again.
∎

4
Probability inequalities

We begin this section with a version of Rosenthal inequality
Rosenthal 1970
. Let
f:\mathsf{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded function with
\|f\|_{\infty}<\infty
. In this paper we need only a simplified version of the Rosenthal inequality, where the leading term with respect to number of terms
n
in
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\{f(Z_{k})-\pi(f)\}
is governed not by the corresponding asymptotic variance
\sigma^{2}_{\infty}(f)
, but by the appropriate variance proxy. Below we prove the version of Rosenthal’s inequality:

Lemma 6
.

Assume
1
. Then for any
p\geq 2
and
f:\mathsf{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\|f\|_{\infty}<\infty
, any initial distribution
\xi
on
(\mathsf{Z},\mathcal{Z})
, and any coefficients
\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigr|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{k}\{f(Z_{k})-\pi(f)\}\bigr|^{p}\bigr]&\leq(16/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}p^{1/2}\|f\|_{\infty}(\sum_{k=2}^{n}\beta_{k}^{2})^{1/2}\\
&\qquad+(8/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}\bigl(|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{n}|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|\beta_{k+1}-\beta_{k}|\bigr)\|f\|_{\infty}\;.\end{split}

Proof.

Under assumption
1
the Poisson equation

g(z)-\mathrm{P}g(z)=f(z)-\pi(f)

(123)

has a unique solution for any bounded
f
(see
(
Douc
et al. 2018
, Chapter 21)
), which is given by the formula

g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\{\mathrm{P}^{k}f(z)-\pi(f)\}\;.

(124)

Using
1
, we obtain that
g(z)
is also bounded with

\displaystyle|g(z)|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\mathrm{P}^{k}f(z)-\pi(f)|\leq 2\|f\|_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1/4)^{\lfloor k/t_{\operatorname{mix}}\rfloor}\leq(8/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}\|f\|_{\infty}\;.

(125)

Hence, we can represent

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{k}(f(Z_{k})-\pi(f))&=\underbrace{\sum_{k=2}^{n}\beta_{k}(g(Z_{k})-\mathrm{P}g(Z_{k-1}))}_{T_{1}}\\
&\quad\underbrace{+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(\beta_{k+1}-\beta_{k})\mathrm{P}g(Z_{k})+\beta_{1}g(Z_{1})-\beta_{n}\mathrm{P}g(Z_{n})}_{T_{2}}\;.\end{split}

The term
T_{2}
can be controlled using Minkowski’s inequality:

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}[\bigl|T_{2}\bigr|^{p}]\leq(8/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}\bigl(|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{n}|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|\beta_{k+1}-\beta_{k}|\bigr)\|f\|_{\infty}\;.

(126)

Now we proceed with
T_{1}
. Set
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\sigma(Z_{\ell},\ell\leq k)
. Since
{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathcal{F}_{k-1}}\left[g(Z_{k})-\mathrm{P}g(Z_{k-1})\right]=0
a.s., and
|g(Z_{k})-\mathrm{P}g(Z_{k-1})|\leq(16/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}\|f\|_{\infty}
, we get, using the Azuma-Hoeffding inequality
(
Van Handel 2016
, Corollary 3.9)
, that

\mathbb{P}_{\xi}[|T_{1}|\geq t]\leq 2\exp\biggl\{-\frac{2t^{2}}{(16/3)^{2}t_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\|\beta_{k}\|^{2}}\biggr\}\;.

(127)

Hence, applying
Lemma

7
we get

\mathbb{E}^{1/p}_{\xi}[|T_{1}|^{p}]\leq(16/3)p^{1/2}t_{\operatorname{mix}}\|f\|_{\infty}(\sum_{k=2}^{n}\|\beta_{k}\|^{2})^{1/2}\;,

(128)

and the statement follows.
∎

We conclude this section with a standard moment bounds for sub-Gaussian random variables. Proof of this result can be found in
(
Durmus
et al. 2025
, Lemma 7)
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
X
be a random variable satisfying
\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2}))
for any
t\geq 0
and some
\sigma^{2}>0
. Then, for any
p\geq 2
, it holds that
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{p}]\leq 2p^{p/2}\sigma^{p}
.

Elementary Quantum Gates from Lie Group Embeddings in U ⁡ ( 2 n ) U(2^{n}) : Geometry, Universality, and Discretization

Abstract
In the standard circuit model, elementary gates are specified relative to a chosen tensor factorization and are therefore extrinsic to the ambient group U ⁡ ( 2 n ) U(2^{n}) . Writing N = 2 n N=2^{n} , we introduce an intrinsic descriptor layer in U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) by declaring as primitive the motions inside faithful embedded copies of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) , leading to the phase-free dictionary 𝒢 elem S ​ U ​ ( n ) = ⋃ ϕ ∈ Emb ⁡ ( S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) , U ⁡ ( N ) ) ϕ ⁡ ( S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) ) \mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)=\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))}\phi(SU(2)) , and we also discuss the phase-inclusive U ⁡ ( 2 ) U(2) variant. We show that Emb ⁡ ( S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) , U ⁡ ( N ) ) \mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N)) decomposes into finitely many U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) -homogeneous strata indexed by isotypic multiplicities, with stabilizers given by centralizers; the canonical two-level sector is organized by Gr 2 ​ ( ℂ N ) \mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N}) up to a P ​ S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) PSU(2) gauge. Equipping U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) with the Hilbert–Schmidt bi-invariant metric, each embedded subgroup is totally geodesic. Introduction

The circuit (gate) model of quantum computation is commonly presented after fixing a tensor
factorization
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
and declaring as primitive those unitaries that
act on one or two tensor factors, from which larger circuits are obtained by composition

[
17
]
. Two foundational themes in this setting are
expressiveness

(universality) and
synthesis
(compilation). Classical results show that mild hypotheses on
one- and two-qubit gate families yield dense generation of
U(2^{n})
up to phase

[
1
,
5
,
4
]
, while constructive matrix-analytic
tooling—notably two-level (Givens/QR) factorizations—provides systematic routes to synthesis

[
19
]
. For finite alphabets, the Solovay–Kitaev paradigm yields
polylogarithmic approximation lengths for dense generating sets

[
13
,
3
,
10
]
, and geometric viewpoints interpret circuit
complexity through geodesics and variational principles on unitary groups

[
18
,
7
]
.

Despite its operational success, the conventional notion of an “elementary gate” is
extrinsic

from the standpoint of the ambient group
U(2^{n})
: it is tied to a chosen tensor layout and to
subsystem labels. Moreover, strict one-qubit locality alone does not generate the full computational
language for
n\geq 2
. This motivates an intrinsic question that is independent of hardware
addressing schemes and of basis choices:

Can one define elementarity directly inside
U(2^{n})
in a way that is conjugation-invariant and
does not presuppose a preferred tensor factorization?

Embedding-based elementarity: an intrinsic descriptor layer.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
N:=2^{n}
. We define elementarity intrinsically in
U(N)
by declaring as primitive
the motions inside faithful
embedded
copies of a two-level symmetry group. Concretely, we
model a logical two-level degree of freedom as a faithful embedding of
SU(2)
into
U(N)
and set
the phase-free dictionary

\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n):=\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))}\phi\big(SU(2)\big)\ \subset\ U(N).

When one wishes to retain phases at the level of primitives, we also consider the
U(2)
analogue

\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n):=\bigcup_{\Phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(U(2),U(N))}\Phi\big(U(2)\big)\ \subset\ U(N).

This embedding-based notion is conjugation-invariant in
U(N)
, hence basis-independent, and it
separates the
mathematical
definition of a primitive operation from any
extrinsic

addressing convention. Once a tensor structure is fixed, the usual single-qubit placements

w^{(n)}_{j}:U(2)\hookrightarrow U(N)
are recovered as particular embeddings, so tensor-local gates
embed into the intrinsic dictionaries; however tensor addressing is not built into the definition.

The
SU(2)
/
U(2)
split and phase management.

The distinction between
SU(2)
and
U(2)
is structural and guides the entire paper. For fixed
N
,

SU(2)
has only finitely many unitary representation types in dimension
N
, which implies that the
conjugation action of
U(N)
on
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
yields a
finite
orbit-type stratification.
By contrast,
U(2)
admits infinitely many determinant twists in the same dimension, so

\mathrm{Emb}(U(2),U(N))
naturally carries countably many orbit types even after fixing
N
. Accordingly,
our structural analysis is carried out at the
SU(2)
level, while abelian diagonal/global phase
degrees of freedom are handled explicitly when passing from
SU(N)
to
U(N)
.

Main results as a single chain: landscape
\rightarrow
two-level sector
\rightarrow

universality and finite interface.

The contributions of the paper are best read as a three-step chain.

(R1) The embedding landscape is computable.

Using complete reducibility of unitary
SU(2)
-representations, we describe
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
as a
disjoint union of
U(N)
-homogeneous manifolds indexed by irreducible multiplicity data, and we
compute stabilizers explicitly as centralizers. This yields a finite orbit-type decomposition under
conjugation by
U(N)
, together with closed-form dimension formulas. Conceptually, it turns the
choice of an intrinsic “logical degree of freedom” inside
U(N)
into discrete, computable data.

(R2) A canonical two-level sector provides an immediate model.

Within the landscape, the two-level stratum is particularly transparent and serves as the main
computational interface: choosing a logical qubit amounts to selecting a complex two-plane

W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
. Elementary operations then act as
SU(2)
on
W
and as the identity on
W^{\perp}
.
An internal
PSU(2)
gauge captures the redundancy in choosing a frame on
W
, separating
gauge-invariant support data from frame-dependent labels. This Grassmannian sector interfaces
directly with two-level (Givens-type) synthesis and, later, with the finite-alphabet lifting
pipeline.

(R3) Expressiveness and discretization follow already from the two-level sector.

A viable intrinsic vocabulary must guarantee universality and admit compilation.
Letting

\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n):=\bigcup_{W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})}SU(N)[W],\qquad\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n):=\bigcup_{W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})}U(N)[W],

we prove constructive generation results

\big\langle\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n)\big\rangle=SU(N),\qquad\big\langle\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n)\big\rangle=U(N),

and therefore, by inclusion
\mathcal{G}^{(\cdot)}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n)\subset\mathcal{G}^{(\cdot)}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)
, the embedding-elementary dictionaries are universal as
well. The proofs follow a classical QR/Givens elimination up to a diagonal special-unitary factor,
together with an explicit generation of the diagonal torus by two-level one-parameter subgroups; the
passage from
SU(N)
to
U(N)
is then obtained by explicit diagonal/global phase bookkeeping in the

U(2)
extension, keeping the non-abelian synthesis cleanly separated from abelian phase management.
Finally, we record a modular finite-alphabet interface: exact two-level factorizations reduce
synthesis in
U(N)
to repeated synthesis in
U(2)
(or in
SU(2)
after phase normalization);
Solovay–Kitaev provides polylogarithmic approximation of
SU(2)
elements by words over a fixed
finite universal set; and these words lift canonically to
U(N)
through two-level embeddings, with
global operator-norm error control. Diagonal and global-phase factors may be carried explicitly or
compiled separately, depending on the desired normal form.

Variational meaning (supporting layer).

Equipping
U(N)
with the Hilbert–Schmidt bi-invariant metric, we show that each embedded copy

\phi(G)\subset U(N)
(with
G\in\{SU(2),U(2)\}
) is totally geodesic. Consequently, within a fixed
embedded gate manifold, energy-minimizing implementations of a target element are exactly
constant-speed one-parameter subgroups generated by minimal-norm logarithms in the embedded Lie
algebra. In the two-level sector this identifies the intrinsic metric cost in
U(N)
with the
corresponding
2\times 2
generator, providing a clean variational interpretation of elementary
motions.

Positioning and novelty

Several ingredients used in this paper are classical when taken in isolation: the classification of
finite-dimensional unitary
SU(2)
-representations and their commutants via isotypic decompositions
and Schur-type arguments, and the compilation toolkit based on two-level (Givens/QR) factorizations
and Solovay–Kitaev approximation in
SU(2)
. What is not standard is to treat these ingredients as
components of a single intrinsic
descriptor layer
for gate primitives internal to
U(2^{n})
:
an elementary operation is specified by the choice of a faithful embedded copy of
SU(2)
(or
U(2)

when phases are retained), together with its gauge structure and natural parameter spaces (most
notably the Grassmannian two-level sector). This viewpoint yields, in one framework, (i) a
computable stratification of admissible intrinsic two-level degrees of freedom, (ii) a canonical
geometric model for “logical qubits” inside
U(N)
, and (iii) a modular compilation interface with
uniform operator-norm control obtained by lifting finite
SU(2)
approximation schemes through
two-level embeddings.

,

For a first pass through the main narrative (canonical two-level sector

The
SU(2)
/
U(2)
split.

We take
U(N)
as ambient group so that global phases and diagonal factors remain explicit in
synthesis and compilation statements. At the same time, the non-abelian two-level motions that drive
our orbit stratification and discretization are naturally governed by embeddings of
SU(2)
into

U(N)
. Accordingly, the structural analysis is carried out at the
SU(2)
level (yielding finitely
many orbit types for fixed
N
), while abelian

separately, either by canonical normalization to

2.1
Multi-qubit Hilbert space, ambient groups, and global-phase conventions

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and set
N:=2^{n}
. We consider the
n
-qubit Hilbert space

\mathbb{H}_{N}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{N},

(2.1)

equipped with the standard Hermitian inner product. Once a computational basis is fixed, we identify
linear operators on
\mathbb{H}_{N}
with matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
.

Global phases and the choice of ambient group.

In many quantum-information settings one works modulo global phase, i.e. in the projective unitary
group
PU(N)=U(N)/U(1)
(or, equivalently, within
SU(N)
). In the present work we keep global phases
explicit and take
U(N)
as ambient Lie group. This is deliberate: our central object is the space
of embeddings
U(2)\hookrightarrow U(N)
, which is naturally defined at the level of
U(N)
without
imposing determinant-one constraints, and retaining phases allows us to state synthesis and
compilation procedures uniformly.

When a phase-free formulation is convenient, we pass canonically to
SU(N)
by removing the
determinant phase:

\widetilde{U}:=e^{-i\arg(\det U)/N}\,U\in SU(N).

(2.2)

Note that
U
and
\widetilde{U}
induce the same element of
PU(N)
, so this operation changes only
the global phase and not the projective action on pure states.

2.2
Lie algebra, Hilbert–Schmidt geometry, and the exponential map

Let
\mathfrak{u}(N)
denote the Lie algebra of
U(N)
, i.e. the space of anti-Hermitian matrices:

\mathfrak{u}(N)=\{X\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}:X^{\dagger}=-X\}.

We equip
\mathfrak{u}(N)
with the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing

\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathrm{hs}}:=\frac{1}{2}\Tr(X^\dagger Y),\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{u}(N).

(2.3)

This pairing is
\Ad
-invariant and induces the standard bi-invariant Riemannian metric on
U(N)
.
For a smooth curve
U:[0,1]\to U(N)
, the
body velocity
(right-trivialized velocity) is

A(t):=\dot{U}(t)\,U(t)^{-1}\in\mathfrak{u}(N),

(2.4)

and the corresponding energy functional is

\mathcal{E}[U]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|A(t)\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{2}\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{U}(t)\|^{2}\,dt.

(2.5)

With respect to this bi-invariant metric, geodesics in
U(N)
are precisely one-parameter subgroups

U(t)=U(0)\exp(tX),\qquad X\in\mathfrak{u}(N).

(2.6)

We adopt the matrix exponential
\exp:\mathfrak{u}(N)\to U(N)
as the default exponential map.

2.3
Pauli matrices, Pauli strings, and normalization of generators

Let
\{\sigma_{0},\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z}\}
denote the Pauli matrices, with
\sigma_{0}:=I_{2}
.
A
Pauli string
is an operator of the form

P=\sigma_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{a_{n}},\qquad a_{j}\in\{0,x,y,z\}.

(2.7)

Pauli strings form an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
under the Hilbert–Schmidt inner
product and provide a convenient coordinate system for describing embedded
\mathfrak{su}(2)

directions.

We associate to each nontrivial Pauli string
P\neq I_{N}
the normalized anti-Hermitian generator

T_{P}:=-\frac{i}{2}\,P\in\mathfrak{u}(N).

(2.8)

This convention matches the standard parametrization of single-qubit rotations

R_{\alpha}(\theta)=\exp(-i\theta\sigma_\alpha/2)
and fixes the sign and the factor
1/2
appearing in
all elementary exponentials. In particular, if
P=\sigma_{\alpha}^{(j)}
is a single-site Pauli
operator acting on the
j
th qubit, then
\exp(\theta T_P)
is a rotation of angle
\theta
around
axis
\alpha
on the corresponding degree of freedom.

2.4
Intrinsic locality via commutants and the canonical reference subclass

To benchmark embedding-based elementarity against conventional tensor locality, we use an intrinsic
(commutant-based) definition of “acting on a subset of qubits” relative to the fixed
factorization (
2.1
). Fix
S\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
and write
\bar{S}
for its complement.
Using the tensor-ordering convention fixed throughout (equivalently, a canonical permutation unitary

\Pi_{S}
that brings the tensor factors in
S
to the front), define the subgroup of unitaries acting
trivially on
S
by

\mathcal{U}_{\bar{S}}:=\Pi_{S}^{-1}\big(I_{2^{|S|}}\otimes U(2^{n-|S|})\big)\Pi_{S}\subset U(N).

(2.9)

Definition 2.1

(
S
-local unitaries via commutants)
.

The
S
-local unitary subgroup
is the commutant of
\mathcal{U}_{\bar{S}}
in
U(N)
,

U(N)_{S}:=\{\,U\in U(N):UX=XU\ \ \forall X\in\mathcal{U}_{\bar{S}}\,\}.

(2.10)

By the bicommutant theorem (or direct matrix-algebra computations), one has the canonical
identification

U(N)_{S}=\Pi_{S}^{-1}\big(U(2^{|S|})\otimes I_{2^{n-|S|}}\big)\Pi_{S}.

(2.11)

Thus
U(N)_{S}
recovers the usual tensor-factor notion of acting nontrivially only on the degrees of
freedom in
S
, but expressed intrinsically through commutation relations.

Canonical one-qubit reference subclass.

For
S=\{j\}
one obtains the one-site local subgroup
U(N)_{\{j\}}
. We define the strictly local
one-qubit family

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(n):=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}U(N)_{\{j\}},

(2.12)

which serves as the canonical reference subclass representing the conventional one-qubit gate
paradigm. In particular,
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(n)
will be used as a baseline when comparing
tensor-local elementarity to the embedding-elementary dictionaries defined next.

2.5
Elementary gates via Lie group embeddings

The commutant-based notion of locality depends on the fixed tensor factorization (
2.1
).
Our proposed elementary-gate framework adopts a representation-theoretic viewpoint: a two-dimensional
logical degree of freedom is treated as primitive, independently of a prescribed tensor layout, by
considering faithful realizations of
U(2)
(or, for stratification purposes,
SU(2)
) inside
U(N)
.

Embeddings and gate manifolds.

Let
G\in\{SU(2),U(2)\}
. We write
\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
for the set of faithful Lie group embeddings

\phi:G\hookrightarrow U(N)
. Each
\phi
identifies a concrete two-level gate manifold

\phi(G)\subset U(N)
and an embedded Lie algebra
d\phi_{e}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{u}(N)
.

Definition 2.2

(Lie group embeddings)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
G\in\{SU(2),U(2)\}
.
We define the set of Lie group embeddings of
G
into
U(N)
by

\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N)):=\left\{\phi:G\to U(N)\ \middle|\ \begin{array}[]{l}\phi\text{ is a continuous group homomorphism and injective,}\\[2.0pt]
d\phi_{e}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{u}(N)\text{ is injective}\end{array}\right\},

where
\mathfrak{g}
denotes the Lie algebra of
G
.
Since
G
is compact, any continuous homomorphism
G\to U(N)
is automatically smooth. Moreover,
injectivity implies that
\phi
is a topological embedding whose image
\phi(G)
is a closed Lie
subgroup of
U(N)
, and
d\phi_{e}
identifies
\mathfrak{g}
with the Lie algebra of
\phi(G)
.

Embedding-elementary dictionaries.

In view of Remark
2.3
, it is useful to distinguish the phase-free non-abelian content
from the full unitary group.

Convention (two elementary dictionaries).

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
N:=2^{n}
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))}\phi\big(SU(2)\big)\ \subset\ U(N),

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(U(2),U(N))}\phi\big(U(2)\big)\ \subset\ U(N).

(2.14)

By construction, both
\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)
and
\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)

are invariant under conjugation by
U(N)
.

The set
\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)
encodes the non-abelian elementary motions (with global
phases and diagonal factors treated separately). In Section
4
we show that the
subgroup it generates contains (indeed generates)
SU(N)
via two-level factorizations. When full

U(N)
reachability is required, we work instead with
\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)
, or
equivalently supplement the
SU(2)
-based synthesis with explicit compilation of the abelian factors
(global phase and trailing diagonal terms) as in Section
5
.

Canonical local layer (reference subclass).

The embedding-based framework is intrinsically defined on
U(N)
and does not require a preferred
tensor factorization of
\mathbb{H}_{N}
. Nevertheless, once the identification

\mathbb{H}_{N}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
is fixed, there are canonical “coordinate” embeddings

w_{j}^{(n)}:U(2)\hookrightarrow U(N),\qquad w_{j}^{(n)}(V)=I_{2}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes V\otimes I_{2}^{\otimes(n-j)},\qquad j=1,\dots,n,

whose images coincide with the commutant-defined one-site subgroups
U(N)_{\{j\}}
in
(
2.11
). Equivalently,

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(n):=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}^{(n)}\big(U(2)\big)\subset U(N),\qquad\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}^{(n)}\big(SU(2)\big)\subset SU(N)

are the canonical local layers singled out by the chosen tensor structure.

Remark 2.3

(Why we stratify by
SU(2)
-embeddings)
.

Restricting
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(U(2),U(N))
to
SU(2)\subset U(2)
yields an element of
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
.
Conversely, an embedding of
SU(2)
captures the full non-abelian content of a logical two-level
degree of freedom. For fixed
N
, unitary representation types of
U(2)
are infinite (determinant
twists), whereas those of
SU(2)
are finite. Since our discretization step synthesizes
SU(2)

motions (with global phases and diagonal factors treated separately), we formulate the orbit
stratification and the intrinsic “logical-qubit” degrees of freedom using
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
.

2.6
Embeddings into
U(N)
and faithful unitary representations

The embedding-based notion of elementarity admits a convenient reformulation in finite-dimensional
representation theory. Any continuous homomorphism
\phi:G\to U(N)
prescribes an action of
G
on

\mathbb{C}^{N}
by unitary operators and can therefore be viewed as an
N
-dimensional unitary representation.
The embedding requirement (injectivity and identification with a Lie subgroup) amounts to

faithfulness
(trivial kernel), while conjugation in
U(N)
corresponds to unitary change of
basis on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. This perspective supplies discrete invariants (isotypic multiplicities), clarifies
the orbit structure of the
U(N)
-action on
\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
, and underlies the homogeneous
stratification developed in Section
6
; see, e.g.,
[
9
,
2
,
14
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Unitary representation)
.

A
unitary representation
of a topological group
G
on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a continuous homomorphism

\rho:G\to U(N)
. It is
faithful
if
\ker(\rho)=\{e\}
.

With Definition
2.4
, any continuous homomorphism
\phi:G\to U(N)
(with

G
compact) is a finite-dimensional unitary representation; moreover, injectivity of
\phi
is
equivalent to faithfulness of the associated representation. We will apply this both to
G=U(2)

(when tracking central phases) and to
G=SU(2)
(for the orbit stratification).

Proposition 2.5

(Embeddings
\boldsymbol{G\hookrightarrow U(N)}
as faithful unitary representations)
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
G\in\{U(2),SU(2)\}
. There is a natural identification between Lie group
embeddings
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
and faithful
N
-dimensional unitary representations of
G
. More precisely:

(i)

Any continuous homomorphism
\phi:G\to U(N)
is a unitary representation on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
; we denote it by
\rho_{\phi}:=\phi
.
Moreover,
\phi
is injective if and only if
\rho_{\phi}
is faithful.

(ii)

Conversely, any faithful continuous unitary representation
\rho:G\to U(N)
defines an element of
\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
.

(iii)

The conjugation action of
U(N)
on
\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
,

(\Ad_{W}\cdot\phi)(g):=W\,\phi(g)\,W^{-1}\qquad(W\in U(N)),

corresponds exactly to unitary equivalence of representations.

Proof.

(i) By Definition
2.4
, any continuous homomorphism
\phi:G\to U(N)
is a unitary representation on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
which we denote by
\rho_{\phi}:=\phi
. Since
\ker(\rho_{\phi})=\ker(\phi)
, the representation
\rho_{\phi}
is faithful if and only if

\phi
is injective.

(ii) Let
\rho:G\to U(N)
be a faithful continuous unitary representation. Since
G
is compact and
U(N)
is Hausdorff,
\rho(G)
is
compact and therefore closed in
U(N)
. Hence
\rho(G)\leq U(N)
is a closed subgroup and, by the closed subgroup theorem, an embedded
Lie subgroup. Because
\rho
is continuous and injective,
\rho:G\to\rho(G)
is a homeomorphism. Finally, since
G
is a compact Lie
group, any continuous homomorphism
G\to U(N)
is automatically smooth (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Ch. 4]
); hence
\rho
is a Lie
group embedding, i.e.
\rho\in\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
.

(iii) If
\phi^{\prime}=\Ad_{W}\cdot\phi
for some
W\in U(N)
, then
\rho_{\phi^{\prime}}(g)=W\rho_{\phi}(g)W^{-1}
for all
g\in G
, so
\rho_{\phi^{\prime}}

and
\rho_{\phi}
are unitarily equivalent. Conversely, if two unitary representations
\rho,\rho^{\prime}:G\to U(N)
are unitarily equivalent,
then there exists
W\in U(N)
such that
\rho^{\prime}(g)=W\rho(g)W^{-1}
for all
g\in G
, i.e.
\rho^{\prime}=\Ad_{W}\cdot\rho
.
∎

Proposition
2.5
reduces the study of
\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))

(for
G\in\{U(2),SU(2)\}
) to the classification of finite-dimensional unitary representations of the
compact group
G
. In particular, unitary representations of compact groups are completely reducible,
and their decomposition into irreducibles is unique up to unitary equivalence; hence conjugacy
classes of embeddings are encoded by discrete multiplicity data in the isotypic decomposition (see,
e.g.,
[
2
,
9
]
).

The next corollary makes this statement explicit and will serve as representation-theoretic input
for the homogeneous decomposition results in Section
6
.

Corollary 2.6

(Conjugacy classes of embeddings and isotypic multiplicity data)
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
G\in\{U(2),SU(2)\}
. Two embeddings
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
are
conjugate in
U(N)
if and only if the associated faithful unitary representations
\rho_{\phi_{1}},\rho_{\phi_{2}}
are unitarily equivalent. Equivalently,
\rho_{\phi_{1}}
and
\rho_{\phi_{2}}
have the
same isotypic decomposition (the same irreducible multiplicity data).

More precisely, there exist finite-support multiplicity families
(m_{\lambda}^{(1)})_{\lambda}
and

(m_{\lambda}^{(2)})_{\lambda}
and
G
-equivariant unitary isomorphisms

\mathbb{C}^{N}\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{\lambda}\Big(\mathbb{C}^{m_{\lambda}^{(k)}}\otimes V_{\lambda}\Big),\qquad k\in\{1,2\},

where
\{V_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
ranges over a complete set of representatives of the unitary
equivalence classes of finite-dimensional irreducible unitary representations of
G
. In
particular,
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are conjugate in
U(N)
if and only if

m_{\lambda}^{(1)}=m_{\lambda}^{(2)}\quad\text{for all }\lambda.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.5
(iii),
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are
conjugate in
U(N)
if and only if
\rho_{\phi_{1}}
and
\rho_{\phi_{2}}
are unitarily equivalent.

Since
G
is compact, every finite-dimensional unitary representation of
G
is completely reducible:
it decomposes as a finite orthogonal direct sum of irreducibles (unitary Maschke theorem); see,
e.g.,
[
2
,
9
,
14
]
. Moreover, the unitary equivalence class of a
finite-dimensional unitary representation is uniquely determined by its irreducible multiplicities,
e.g. via character orthogonality (equivalently, Schur orthogonality for matrix coefficients); see,
e.g.,
[
2
,
9
]
. Therefore
\rho_{\phi_{1}}\simeq\rho_{\phi_{2}}
if and only if

m_{\lambda}^{(1)}=m_{\lambda}^{(2)}
for all
\lambda
, and this criterion is equivalent to conjugacy of

\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
in
U(N)
.
∎

3
Logical Qubits and the Grassmannian Model

The embedding-based framework treats a two-dimensional logical degree of freedom as primitive,
independently of any preferred tensor factorization of
\mathbb{H}_{N}\cong\mathbb{C}^{N}
. In this section we
isolate a distinguished and computationally transparent family of embeddings—the
two-level
embeddings
—and show that they are naturally organized by the complex Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
.

Takeaway.
A
two-level logical qubit
is a two-plane
W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
(support data),
and a two-level gate is an
SU(2)
action on
W
extended by the identity on
W^{\perp}
. Choosing a
frame on
W
produces an explicit embedding
SU(2)\hookrightarrow U(N)
, but the resulting embedding
data is defined only up to the inner-automorphism gauge of
SU(2)
, i.e. up to
PSU(2)\cong SO(3)
.
This Grassmannian sector will serve as the canonical computational stratum of the full embedding
landscape.

3.1
Two-level subgroups and two-level embeddings

Definition 3.1

(Two-level unitary and special-unitary subgroups)
.

Let
W\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a complex subspace with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W=2
, and let
W^{\perp}
be its orthogonal
complement. Define the
two-level unitary subgroup supported on
W
by

U(N)[W]\;:=\;\{\,U\in U(N):U|_{W}\in U(W)\ \text{and}\ U|_{W^{\perp}}=I_{W^{\perp}}\,\},

(3.1)

and the
two-level special-unitary subgroup supported on
W
by

SU(N)[W]\;:=\;\{\,U\in U(N):U|_{W}\in SU(W)\ \text{and}\ U|_{W^{\perp}}=I_{W^{\perp}}\,\}.

(3.2)

Equivalently,
U(N)[W]
acts arbitrarily on
W
and trivially on
W^{\perp}
, while
SU(N)[W]
imposes

\det(U|_W)=1
.

Remark 3.2

(Non-canonical identifications)
.

The groups
U(N)[W]
and
SU(N)[W]
are (non-canonically) isomorphic to
U(2)
and
SU(2)
,
respectively. Making such an identification amounts to choosing a unitary isomorphism
W\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
i.e. an orthonormal frame of
W
.

Definition 3.3

(Framed two-level
SU(2)
-embedding)
.

Let
W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
and let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to W
be a unitary isomorphism (an orthonormal frame on
W
).
Define

\phi_{W,f}:SU(2)\hookrightarrow U(N),\qquad\phi_{W,f}(S)\;:=\;fSf^{-1}\ \oplus\ I_{W^{\perp}},

(3.3)

where the direct sum is taken with respect to
\mathbb{C}^{N}=W\oplus W^{\perp}
.

Proposition 3.4

(Image and gauge covariance)
.

For fixed
W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
, the subgroup
\phi_{W,f}(SU(2))\subset U(N)
is independent of the
choice of frame
f
and equals
SU(N)[W]
. More precisely, if
f,f^{\prime}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to W
are unitary
isomorphisms, then there exists
S_{0}\in SU(2)
such that

\phi_{W,f^{\prime}}(S)=\phi_{W,f}(S_{0}SS_{0}^{-1}),\qquad S\in SU(2),

hence
\phi_{W,f^{\prime}}(SU(2))=\phi_{W,f}(SU(2))=SU(N)[W]
.

Proof.

Since
f,f^{\prime}
are unitary isomorphisms onto
W
, the map
U:=f^{-1}f^{\prime}
lies in
U(2)
and
f^{\prime}=fU
.
Let
\delta:=\det(U)\in U(1)
and set
S_{0}:=\delta^{-1/2}U\in SU(2)
(choose any square root

\delta^{1/2}
; the ambiguity is
\pm I
and does not affect the inner action). Then
USU^{-1}=S_{0}SS_{0}^{-1}

for all
S\in SU(2)
, because the central scalar cancels. Therefore

\phi_{W,f^{\prime}}(S)=(fU)S(U^{-1}f^{-1})\oplus I_{W^{\perp}}=f(USU^{-1})f^{-1}\oplus I_{W^{\perp}}=\phi_{W,f}(S_{0}SS_{0}^{-1}).

The equality of images follows, and
\phi_{W,f}(SU(2))
acts as
SU(W)
on
W
and fixes
W^{\perp}

pointwise, hence equals
SU(N)[W]
.
∎

3.2
The Grassmannian as the configuration space of two-level logical qubits

The construction above associates to each two-plane
W
a canonical two-level subgroup
SU(N)[W]
.
Conversely, a subgroup of the form
SU(N)[W]
uniquely determines
W
as the maximal subspace on which
it can act nontrivially.

Proposition 3.5

(Two-level
SU(2)
subgroups are parametrized by
\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
)
.

The assignment

\Psi:\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\longrightarrow\{\text{two-level $SU(2)$ subgroups of }U(N)\},\qquad\Psi(W):=SU(N)[W],

(3.4)

is injective. In particular, two-level embedded copies of
SU(2)
in
U(N)
are naturally
parametrized by
\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
.

Proof.

If
SU(N)[W_{1}]=SU(N)[W_{2}]
, then their fixed-point subspaces coincide:

\mathrm{Fix}\big(SU(N)[W_{i}]\big)\;:=\;\bigcap_{U\in SU(N)[W_{i}]}\ker(U-I)=W_{i}^{\perp}.

Indeed, every element fixes
W_{i}^{\perp}
pointwise, while the restriction to
W_{i}
ranges over

SU(W_{i})
and hence has no nonzero fixed vector. Therefore
W_{1}^{\perp}=W_{2}^{\perp}
and
W_{1}=W_{2}
.
∎

Definition 3.6

(Two-level logical qubit)
.

A
(two-level) logical qubit
in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a choice of a complex
2
-plane
W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
.
A
framed logical qubit
is a pair
(W,f)
where
W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
and
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to W
is a unitary
isomorphism.

Remark 3.7

(Gauge group and Stiefel-bundle viewpoint)
.

Framed logical qubits form the Stiefel manifold
\mathrm{St}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
of orthonormal
2
-frames, and the
projection
\mathrm{St}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
is a principal
U(2)
-bundle. However, for the associated

SU(2)
-embedding (
3.3
), the central
U(1)\subset U(2)
acts trivially:
if
f^{\prime}=fe^{i\theta}I
then
\phi_{W,f^{\prime}}=\phi_{W,f}
. Accordingly, the effective gauge acting on the
embedding data is

U(2)/U(1)\ \cong\ SU(2)/\{\pm I\}\ =:\ PSU(2)\ \cong\ SO(3),

i.e. the group of inner automorphisms of
SU(2)
.

3.3
The two-level stratum as a homogeneous space and a principal bundle

The family of framed embeddings
(W,f)\mapsto\phi_{W,f}
defines a distinguished subset of

\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
, corresponding to the representation type

V_{1}\oplus V_{0}^{\oplus(N-2)}
(cf. Remark
6.6
and the general
classification in Section
6
). This subset is a single
U(N)
-conjugacy class, and
its internal redundancy is precisely the
PSU(2)
gauge discussed above.

Proposition 3.8

(Two-level embeddings form a principal
PSU(2)
-bundle over
\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
)
.

Let
\mathrm{Emb}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(SU(2),U(N))\subset\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
denote the set of embeddings of the form

\phi_{W,f}
in (
3.3
). Then:

(i)

The conjugation action of
U(N)
is transitive on
\mathrm{Emb}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(SU(2),U(N))
; hence
this set is a smooth homogeneous manifold (the
two-level stratum
).

(ii)

The map

\pi:\mathrm{Emb}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(SU(2),U(N))\to\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N}),\qquad\pi(\phi_{W,f})=W,

is well-defined and
U(N)
-equivariant.

(iii)

For fixed
W
, the fiber
\pi^{-1}(W)
is naturally a torsor for

PSU(2)=SU(2)/\{\pm I\}\cong SO(3)
. Equivalently,
\pi
exhibits

\mathrm{Emb}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(SU(2),U(N))
as a principal
PSU(2)
-bundle over
\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
.

Proof.

(i) Any two-planes
W,W^{\prime}
are related by some
U\in U(N)
. Conjugation by
U
transports
SU(N)[W]
to

SU(N)[W^{\prime}]
, hence carries
\phi_{W,f}
to an embedding of the form
\phi_{W^{\prime},f^{\prime}}
for some frame

f^{\prime}
. Thus conjugation is transitive.

(ii) By Proposition
3.4
, the subgroup
\phi_{W,f}(SU(2))
depends only on

W
, so
\pi
is well-defined. Equivariance is immediate.

(iii) Fix
W
and two frames
f,f^{\prime}
over
W
. Then
f^{\prime}=fU
for a unique
U\in U(2)
, and by the proof
of Proposition
3.4
we obtain

\phi_{W,f^{\prime}}=\phi_{W,f}\circ\Ad_{S_{0}}
for some
S_{0}\in SU(2)
well-defined modulo
\{\pm I\}
.
Hence the effective gauge group is
PSU(2)
and
\pi^{-1}(W)
is a
PSU(2)
-torsor.
∎

3.4
Descriptor form: separating the logical support from the internal action

Two-level gates admit a convenient
descriptor
that separates
where
the gate acts (the
support two-plane) from
what
action is performed on that plane (the internal
SU(2)
element),
while keeping track of the intrinsic gauge redundancy.

Support as a projector (gauge-invariant data).

Given
W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
, let
P_{W}
denote the orthogonal projector onto
W
. The map
W\mapsto P_{W}

identifies
\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
with the set of rank-
2
orthogonal projectors in
\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
; thus
P_{W}
is
an intrinsic representative of the logical-qubit choice.

Internal action and gauge.

Fix
W
and choose a frame
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to W
. Any two-level special unitary supported on
W
can be
written as

U_{W,f}(S)\;:=\;\phi_{W,f}(S)\;=\;fSf^{-1}\oplus I_{W^{\perp}},\qquad S\in SU(2).

(3.5)

If the frame is changed to
f^{\prime}=fU
with
U\in U(2)
, then
U_{W,f^{\prime}}(S)=U_{W,f}(S_{0}SS_{0}^{-1})
for a
suitable
S_{0}\in SU(2)
, so the internal label is defined only up to conjugation, i.e. up to the

PSU(2)
gauge.

Lie-algebra descriptor.

For geometric and variational constructions it is convenient to work at Lie-algebra level. Given

A\in\mathfrak{su}(2)
, define the corresponding two-level generator on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
by

X_{W,f}(A)\;:=\;fAf^{-1}\oplus 0_{W^{\perp}}\ \in\ \mathfrak{u}(N),\qquad U_{W,f}\big(\exp(A)\big)=\exp\!\big(X_{W,f}(A)\big).

(3.6)

Lemma 3.9

(Hilbert–Schmidt isometry for two-level generators)
.

Let
W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
, let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to W
be a unitary frame, and let
A,B\in\mathfrak{su}(2)
.
Define
X_{W,f}(A):=fAf^{-1}\oplus 0_{W^{\perp}}
as in (
3.6
). Then, with

\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathrm{hs}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr(X^\dagger Y)
on both
\mathfrak{su}(2)
and

\mathfrak{u}(N)
,

\|X_{W,f}(A)\|_{\mathrm{hs}}=\|A\|_{\mathrm{hs}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle X_{W,f}(A),X_{W,f}(B)\rangle_{\mathrm{hs}}=\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{hs}}.

(3.7)

In particular, these quantities are independent of the choice of frame
f
.

Proof.

Since
f
is unitary, conjugation preserves adjoints and traces. Writing

X_{W,f}(A)=fAf^{-1}\oplus 0
, we have

\|X_{W,f}(A)\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Tr\!\big((fA^{\dagger}f^{-1})(fAf^{-1})\big)=\frac{1}{2}\Tr(A^\dagger A)=\|A\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{2},

and similarly for the inner product. Frame-independence follows.
∎

Corollary 3.10

(Energy reduction for two-level paths)
.

Let
A:[0,1]\to\mathfrak{su}(2)
be measurable and consider a curve
U:[0,1]\to U(N)
with body
velocity
X_{W,f}(A(t))
. Then

\int_{0}^{1}\|X_{W,f}(A(t))\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{2}\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\|A(t)\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{2}\,dt,

so the Hilbert–Schmidt metric cost of a two-level evolution is exactly the cost of its

2\times 2
internal generator.

Remark 3.11

(A gauge-invariant descriptor pair)
.

A two-level special-unitary gate supported on
W
can be described by the pair
(P_{W},[S])
, where

P_{W}
is the rank-
2
projector onto
W
and
[S]
is the
PSU(2)
-class of
S
(i.e.
S
modulo the
conjugation gauge). Thus
P_{W}
specifies the logical support, while
[S]
specifies the internal
action up to the frame choice.

Phase conventions.

If one wishes to incorporate arbitrary phases on the support plane
W
(i.e. to work with
U(N)[W]

rather than
SU(N)[W]
), one may extend the
SU(2)
action by an additional commuting
U(1)
phase on

W
. Equivalently, one may treat the
SU(2)
two-level part as the elementary non-abelian primitive
and compile the remaining abelian degrees of freedom (global phase and/or diagonal factors)
separately, as described in the discretization/compilation step.

3.5
Relation to the embedding dictionary and to two-level unitaries

Two-level embeddings yield a Grassmannian-indexed subset of the full embedding-elementary dictionary:

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}^{SU}(n)\;:=\;\bigcup_{W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})}SU(N)[W]\;\subset\;\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{SU}(n),\qquad\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{SU}(n)=\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))}\phi(SU(2)).

Elements of
SU(N)[W]
are precisely the
special-unitary two-level unitaries
: they act as an
arbitrary
SU(2)
on
W
and as the identity on
W^{\perp}
. These operations are the basic primitives
in classical two-level (Givens-type) synthesis and will be the core mechanism behind the universality
proofs in Section
4
.

Proposition 3.12

(Two-level gates are embedding-elementary)
.

For every
W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
one has
SU(N)[W]\subset\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{SU}(n)
. Consequently,

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}^{SU}(n)\subset\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{SU}(n).

Proof.

Fix
W
and choose a frame
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to W
. Then
SU(N)[W]=\phi_{W,f}(SU(2))
and

\phi_{W,f}\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
, hence every element of
SU(N)[W]
lies in

\bigcup_{\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))}\phi(SU(2))=\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{SU}(n)
.
∎

3.6
Comparison with tensor-factor locality

The Grassmannian model is intrinsic on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and does not presuppose a tensor factorization.
Nevertheless, once
\mathbb{H}_{N}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
is fixed, conventional one-qubit gates appear as
a distinguished embedding-elementary family, although typically not as two-level operations.

Proposition 3.13

(Tensor-local one-qubit gates are embedding-elementary (but not two-level))
.

Let
w_{j}^{(n)}:U(2)\hookrightarrow U(N)
be the canonical tensor placements from
Section
2
. Then
w_{j}^{(n)}(SU(2))\subset\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{SU}(n)

for each
j
, and hence

\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}^{(n)}(SU(2))\ \subset\ \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{SU}(n).

Moreover, for
n\geq 2
the subgroup
w_{j}^{(n)}(SU(2))
is not of two-level form
SU(N)[W]
: it acts
nontrivially on a
2^{n-1}
-fold orthogonal direct sum of
2
-planes (the subspaces spanned by

\ket{0,b}
and
\ket{1,b}
with
b\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}
), rather than on a single two-plane with
pointwise-fixed orthogonal complement.

Proof.

By definition,
w_{j}^{(n)}
is an embedding
U(2)\hookrightarrow U(N)
, hence its restriction to

SU(2)
lies in
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
, so
w_{j}^{(n)}(SU(2))\subset\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{SU}(n)
.

For the final claim, observe that
w_{j}^{(n)}(SU(2))
preserves the decomposition

\mathbb{C}^{N}\cong\bigoplus_{b\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}}\mathbb{C}^{2}_{b}
into
2^{n-1}
orthogonal two-planes, acting as the

same

SU(2)
on each plane
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{b}
. In contrast, a two-level subgroup
SU(N)[W]
fixes

W^{\perp}
pointwise and acts nontrivially only on a single two-plane
W
. Thus
w_{j}^{(n)}(SU(2))
is
not of the form
SU(N)[W]
when
n\geq 2
.
∎

Remark 3.14

(Why the Grassmannian model is the canonical two-level notion)
.

Tensor locality selects
n
preferred embeddings of
SU(2)
(the tensor factors), but these lie in a
different representation type than the two-level stratum: they act as multiple copies of the defining
module rather than as
V_{1}\oplus V_{0}^{\oplus(N-2)}
. The Grassmannian two-level model instead ranges
over
all
complex two-planes and isolates exactly the sector underlying classical two-level
(Givens-type) synthesis and the corresponding universality mechanism for
SU(N)
.

Bridge to universality and compilation.

The key point for the remainder of the paper is that two-level gates provide both (i) an intrinsic,
Grassmannian-indexed primitive vocabulary and (ii) the classical two-level factors used in
constructive synthesis. In Section
4
we prove that the subgroup generated by

\bigcup_{W}SU(N)[W]
is
SU(N)
, and in Section
5
we discretize these
SU(2)
motions and
lift the resulting words to
U(N)
with explicit error control.

4
Phase-free and full universality from two-level dictionaries

We prove that the two-level sector already yields universality, both in the phase-free sense (generation of
SU(N)
) and in the full
unitary sense (generation of
U(N)
). Throughout, fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and set
N:=2^{n}
. Recall that for each
2
-plane

W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
we have the two-level subgroups
U(N)[W]\leq U(N)
and
SU(N)[W]\leq SU(N)

(Definition
3.1
). We consider the associated two-level dictionaries

\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n):=\bigcup_{W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})}SU(N)[W]\subset SU(N),\qquad\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n):=\bigcup_{W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})}U(N)[W]\subset U(N).

(4.1)

Since two-level subgroups are realized as images of (framed) two-level embeddings, we have

\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n)\subset\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)
and

\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n)\subset\mathcal{G}^{U}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)

(cf. Sections
3.1
–
3.5
). Hence universality at the two-level level implies
universality of the full embedding-elementary dictionaries.

The argument has two inputs: (i) a QR/Givens factorization of arbitrary unitaries into coordinate two-level factors and a diagonal
remainder, and (ii) the fact that diagonal tori are generated by two-level phase rotations. We treat
SU(N)
and
U(N)
in parallel.

4.1
Diagonal tori from two-level phase rotations

Let
T\leq U(N)
be the diagonal torus and
T_{0}:=T\cap SU(N)
.

Lemma 4.1

(The special-unitary torus is generated by
SU(2)
two-level phases)
.

For each
j=2,\dots,N
define

H_{1j}:=\frac{i}{2}\big(E_{11}-E_{jj}\big)\in\mathfrak{su}(N),\qquad\gamma_{1j}(t):=\exp(tH_{1j})\in SU(N).

Then

Consequently, every

with

and

D_{0}\in\langle\,SU(N)[W_{12}]\cup\cdots\cup SU(N)[W_{1N}]\,\rangle
.

Proof.

The support statement follows from the explicit diagonal action of
H_{1j}
, hence of
\gamma_{1j}(t)
.
Moreover,
\mathfrak{t}_{0}=\mathrm{Lie}(T_{0})
is spanned by

is a compact torus,

is surjective

[
9

or
[

, giving the claim. The final statement is the decomposition of

into global phase
and special-unitary part.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(The full diagonal torus is generated by

Proof.

4.2
Coordinate two-level factorization with diagonal remainder

Theorem 4.3

(Unitary QR/Givens factorization into coordinate two-level gates)
.

Fix an orthonormal basis

of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Then every

admits a factorization

where each

.

Proof.

This is the standard unitary QR procedure via Givens rotations: successive left-multiplication by coordinate two-level unitaries
eliminates subdiagonal entries, yielding an upper-triangular unitary matrix, hence a diagonal matrix

.
See, e.g.,
[

,
11
,

]
.
∎

4.3

Theorem 4.4

(Phase-free universality of
\mathcal{G}^{SU}_{\mathrm{2lvl}}(n)
)
.

For

,

Proof.

Let

,

with

and

For each

(Full universality of

4.4

Recall

(Local one-qubit gates generate only tensor-product unitaries)

5
Discretization and Compilation: Two-Level Factorizations and the Solovay–Kitaev Paradigm

The embedding-elementary dictionary
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n)
is a
continuous
generating family in
U(N)
.
To obtain an implementable gate model, one must pass to a
finite
alphabet and provide a compilation procedure with explicit
accuracy–length guarantees. We record a modular route consistent with the Grassmannian two-level viewpoint:

(1)

(
Exact reduction to two-level factors
) every
U\in U(N)
admits an
exact
factorization into coordinate two-level
unitaries via unitary QR/Givens eliminations;

(2)

(
Local synthesis problem
) approximating each two-level factor reduces compilation in
U(N)
to repeated approximation
problems in
U(2)
(or, after phase normalization, in
SU(2)
);

(3)

(
Finite alphabet on
SU(2)
) the Solovay–Kitaev theorem yields polylogarithmic-length approximation of
SU(2)
elements
by words over a fixed finite universal set;

(4)

(
Lift and control the global error
) lifting these words through the corresponding two-level embeddings
\phi_{p,q}

produces an
\varepsilon
-approximation in
U(N)
with explicit operator-norm error control, while global phase and diagonal remainders
are handled separately as needed.

5.1
From a finite
SU(2)
alphabet to a finite elementary alphabet in
U(N)

Let
G_{2}\subset SU(2)
be a finite set. We say that
G_{2}
is
universal for
SU(2)
if
\langle G_{2}\rangle
is dense in
SU(2)
.
Given such a set, we define the induced finite elementary alphabet in
U(N)
by

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n;G_{2})\;:=\;\bigcup_{1\leq p<q\leq N}\phi_{p,q}(G_{2})\ \subset\ U(N).

(5.1)

Thus
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n;G_{2})
consists of
discrete two-level gates
acting by one of finitely many
SU(2)
primitives
on a chosen coordinate two-plane and trivially on its orthogonal complement.

Remark 5.1

(Determinants and diagonal phases)
.

Each
V\in U(2)
admits a factorization
V=e^{i\theta}\widetilde{V}
with
\widetilde{V}\in SU(2)
(take
e^{i\theta}=\sqrt{\det V}
).
In the exact synthesis (
4.2
), the scalar phases
e^{i\theta_{k}}
may be absorbed into the trailing diagonal
unitary
D
. Accordingly, the approximation results below are stated for
SU(2)
factors and extend to
U(2)
with this bookkeeping.

5.2
Solovay–Kitaev approximation on
SU(2)

We write
\|\cdot\|
for the operator norm (spectral norm) induced by the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
.

Theorem 5.2

(Solovay–Kitaev theorem for
SU(2)
)
.

Let
G_{2}\subset SU(2)
be a finite set such that the subgroup
\langle G_{2}\rangle
is dense in
SU(2)
.
Then there exist constants
c,C>0
, depending only on
G_{2}
, and a deterministic classical algorithm with the following property:
for every
V\in SU(2)
and every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
the algorithm produces a word
w=w(V,\varepsilon)
over

G_{2}\cup G_{2}^{-1}
such that

\|V-w\|\leq\varepsilon,\qquad|w|\leq C\,\log^{c}\!\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big).

Moreover, the running time is polynomial in
\log(1/\varepsilon)
.

Remark 5.3
.

In what follows we only use the existence of a polylogarithmic length bound in
\varepsilon^{-1}
and the fact that the word can be
found effectively. See
[
13
,
3
]
for constructive versions and explicit exponents.

While we only use Solovay–Kitaev as a modular finite-alphabet interface for approximating
SU(2)
primitives,
it is worth noting that the asymptotic word-length exponent in the general Solovay–Kitaev theorem has been
substantially improved very recently: Kuperberg breaks the classical “cubic” barrier and obtains word length

\ell=O(n^{\alpha+\delta})
with
\alpha>\log_{\varphi}(2)\approx 1.44042
(for inverse-closed finite gate sets
densely generating
SU(d)
, and more generally connected semisimple Lie groups), together with a near-linear
bound for the corresponding compressed-word length; see
[
16
]
.
These refinements can be plugged into our pipeline verbatim whenever one wants sharper asymptotic gate counts
for the
SU(2)
approximation subroutine.

5.3
Lifting Solovay–Kitaev words to
U(N)
and global error control

The following elementary lemmas isolate the analytic inputs needed to propagate local approximation errors
to a global operator-norm bound. Throughout,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
; we repeatedly use its unitary
invariance
\|UXV\|=\|X\|
for
U,V\in U(N)
and its submultiplicativity
\|XY\|\leq\|X\|\,\|Y\|
(see, e.g.,
[
12
]
).

Lemma 5.4

(Operator-norm isometry for coordinate two-level embeddings)
.

Let
1\leq p<q\leq N
and let
\phi_{p,q}:U(2)\hookrightarrow U(N)
be the coordinate two-level embedding supported on

W_{p,q}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{p},e_{q}\}
. Then for all
V,W\in U(2)
,

\big\|\phi_{p,q}(V)-\phi_{p,q}(W)\big\|=\|V-W\|.

In particular,
\phi_{p,q}
is an isometric embedding with respect to the operator norm.

Proof.

By construction, there exists a unitary
Q\in U(N)
such that, with respect to the orthogonal decomposition

\mathbb{C}^{N}=W_{p,q}\oplus W_{p,q}^{\perp}
, one has

\phi_{p,q}(V)=Q^{-1}\,\mathrm{diag}(V,I_{N-2})\,Q,\qquad\phi_{p,q}(W)=Q^{-1}\,\mathrm{diag}(W,I_{N-2})\,Q.

Hence, by unitary invariance of the operator norm,

\|\phi_{p,q}(V)-\phi_{p,q}(W)\|=\big\|\,\mathrm{diag}(V-W,0)\,\big\|.

For a block-diagonal operator the operator norm equals the maximum of the norms of the diagonal blocks, so

\big\|\,\mathrm{diag}(V-W,0)\,\big\|=\|V-W\|
.
∎

Lemma 5.5

(Telescoping bound for products)
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{K},B_{1},\dots,B_{K}\in U(N)
. Then

\Big\|\prod_{k=1}^{K}A_{k}-\prod_{k=1}^{K}B_{k}\Big\|\ \leq\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}\|A_{k}-B_{k}\|.

Proof.

Using the telescoping identity

\prod_{k=1}^{K}A_{k}-\prod_{k=1}^{K}B_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}B_{k}\Big)\,(A_{j}-B_{j})\,\Big(\prod_{k=j+1}^{K}A_{k}\Big),

and taking norms yields, by the triangle inequality and submultiplicativity,

\Big\|\prod_{k=1}^{K}A_{k}-\prod_{k=1}^{K}B_{k}\Big\|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{K}\Big\|\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}B_{k}\Big\|\,\|A_{j}-B_{j}\|\,\Big\|\prod_{k=j+1}^{K}A_{k}\Big\|.

If all factors are unitary then each partial product is unitary and hence has norm
1
, giving the claim.
∎

We now combine the exact two-level factorization (Theorem
4.3
) with Solovay–Kitaev approximation
(Theorem
5.2
).

Theorem 5.6

(Compilation via two-level factorization and Solovay–Kitaev)
.

Fix
N=2^{n}
and let
G_{2}\subset SU(2)
be a finite universal set.
Let
U\in U(N)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then there exist:

•

a word
W(U,\varepsilon)
over the finite alphabet

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n;G_{2})\ \cup\ \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n;G_{2})^{-1},

•

and a diagonal unitary
D\in U(N)
,

such that

\big\|U-W(U,\varepsilon)\,D\big\|\ \leq\ \varepsilon.

(5.2)

Moreover, one may choose
W(U,\varepsilon)
with length bounded by

|W(U,\varepsilon)|\ \leq\ K\cdot C\,\log^{\,c}\!\Big(\frac{K}{\varepsilon}\Big),\qquad K\leq\frac{N(N-1)}{2},

(5.3)

where
c,C
are the Solovay–Kitaev constants from Theorem
5.2
.
If one wishes to eliminate the residual diagonal factor, it can be compiled (up to global phase) using two-level phase rotations as in
Section
5.4
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
there exist indices
(p_{k},q_{k})
, factors
V_{k}\in U(2)
, and a diagonal unitary
D\in U(N)

such that

U=\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(V_{k})\Big)\,D,\qquad K\leq\frac{N(N-1)}{2}.

Using Remark
5.1
, write
V_{k}=e^{i\theta_{k}}\widetilde{V}_{k}
with
\widetilde{V}_{k}\in SU(2)
and absorb the phases
into
D
, so that we may assume
V_{k}\in SU(2)
without loss of generality.

Set
\delta:=\varepsilon/K
. For each
k
, Theorem
5.2
yields a word
w_{k}
over
G_{2}\cup G_{2}^{-1}
such that

\|V_{k}-w_{k}\|\leq\delta,\qquad|w_{k}|\leq C\,\log^{c}\!\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}\Big)=C\,\log^{c}\!\Big(\frac{K}{\varepsilon}\Big).

Define the lifted word in
U(N)
by

W(U,\varepsilon):=\prod_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(w_{k}),

where
\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(w_{k})
denotes the word obtained by embedding each letter of
w_{k}
through
\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}
.
By Lemma
5.4
,

\big\|\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(V_{k})-\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(w_{k})\big\|=\|V_{k}-w_{k}\|\leq\delta.

Applying Lemma
5.5
gives

\Big\|\prod_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(V_{k})-\prod_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(w_{k})\Big\|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\delta=\varepsilon.

Right-multiplication by
D
preserves the operator norm (unitary invariance), hence (
5.2
) follows.
Finally,

|W(U,\varepsilon)|=\sum_{k=1}^{K}|w_{k}|\leq K\cdot C\,\log^{c}\!\Big(\frac{K}{\varepsilon}\Big),

which is (
5.3
).
∎

Remark 5.7

(Worst-case scaling in
n
)
.

Since
K\leq N(N-1)/2=\Theta(N^{2})=\Theta(4^{n})
, the bound (
5.3
) yields worst-case word lengths that scale
exponentially in
n
for generic targets in
U(2^{n})
. This reflects the parameter complexity of
U(2^{n})
and should be interpreted
as providing a representation-invariant and modular discretization mechanism rather than an efficiency guarantee for arbitrary
instances.

5.4
Compiling the diagonal factor

In the QR/Givens synthesis of Theorem
5.6
the target unitary
U\in U(N)
is produced up to a trailing diagonal
unitary
D
. If one wishes to obtain a “pure word” over embedded
SU(2)
generators (up to an unavoidable global phase), then
D
can
itself be synthesized by two-level phase rotations supported on coordinate planes. We record a convenient formulation.

Corollary 5.8

(Pure-word compilation up to global phase)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.6
, one may absorb the diagonal factor into the word up to a global phase:
there exist
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and a word
\widetilde{W}(U,\varepsilon)
over

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n;G_{2})\cup\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n;G_{2})^{-1}
such that

\big\|U-e^{i\theta}\widetilde{W}(U,\varepsilon)\big\|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
5.6
to obtain
U=W(U,\varepsilon)\,D+E
with
\|E\|\leq\varepsilon
.
Decompose
D=e^{i\theta}D_{0}
as in Lemma
4.1
and write
D_{0}
as a finite product of
elements from
\phi_{1j}(SU(2))
(hence as a word over the corresponding embedded alphabet).
Let
\widetilde{W}(U,\varepsilon):=W(U,\varepsilon)\cdot(\text{word for }D_{0})
. Then
e^{i\theta}\widetilde{W}=WD
by construction, so

U-e^{i\theta}\widetilde{W}=E
and the norm bound follows.
∎

5.5
Algorithmic summary and interpretation

We summarize the construction of Theorem
5.6
as an explicit compilation procedure.

Compilation procedure (coordinate two-level factorization + Solovay–Kitaev).

Given
U\in U(N)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and a finite universal set
G_{2}\subset SU(2)
, the following steps produce
a word
W
over
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n;G_{2})\cup\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{elem}}(n;G_{2})^{-1}
and a diagonal
D\in U(N)
such that

\|U-WD\|\leq\varepsilon
.

1.

Exact two-level factorization.

Compute a coordinate Givens/QR factorization

U=\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(V_{k})\Big)\,D,\qquad 1\leq p_{k}<q_{k}\leq N,\ \ V_{k}\in U(2),

with
K\leq N(N-1)/2
.
If desired, write
V_{k}=e^{i\theta_{k}}\widetilde{V}_{k}
with
\widetilde{V}_{k}\in SU(2)
and absorb the phases
e^{i\theta_{k}}
into
D
.

2.

Local approximation in
SU(2)
.

Set
\delta:=\varepsilon/K
.
For each
k=1,\dots,K
, apply a Solovay–Kitaev routine to obtain a word
w_{k}
over
G_{2}\cup G_{2}^{-1}
such that

\|\,\widetilde{V}_{k}-w_{k}\,\|\leq\delta.

3.

Lift and concatenate.

Define

W:=\prod_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(w_{k}),

where
\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(w_{k})
denotes the word obtained by replacing each letter of
w_{k}
by its embedded generator in

\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(G_{2})\cup\phi_{p_{k},q_{k}}(G_{2})^{-1}
.

By Lemmas
5.4
and
5.5
, the resulting pair
(W,D)
satisfies
\|U-WD\|\leq\varepsilon
.

Remark 5.9

(Interpretation in the geometric framework)
.

The factorization step may be viewed as selecting a finite sequence of logical two-planes (here, the coordinate planes

W_{p_{k},q_{k}}
) and prescribing on each plane a continuous
U(2)
motion
V_{k}
. The Solovay–Kitaev step then replaces each
continuous
SU(2)
motion by a short word over a fixed finite alphabet, and the lifting step transports these words to
U(N)
through
the corresponding two-level embeddings. In this sense, the procedure separates a
continuous
choice of support (a path through
logical qubits) from a
discrete
approximation of the induced
SU(2)
dynamics.

Remark 5.10

(Why the present framework is not merely “QR + Solovay–Kitaev”)
.

The QR/Givens factorization and the Solovay–Kitaev theorem are used here only as modular compilation primitives.
The conceptual contribution is orthogonal to their existence: we introduce an intrinsic
descriptor layer
for elementary
operations in
U(2^{n})
based on the choice of a faithful embedded copy of
U(2)
(equivalently, a point in the embedding landscape

\mathrm{Emb}(U(2),U(N))
), together with the attendant gauge structure and geometric parameter spaces (notably the Grassmannian in the
two-level sector). The compilation pipeline then serves as an existence proof that finite alphabets can be lifted through these
intrinsic descriptors, rather than as the primary novelty.

6
The embedding landscape:
\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
as a union of homogeneous manifolds

An embedding
\phi:G\hookrightarrow U(N)
equips
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with a unitary
G
-module structure, and the conjugation action of
U(N)
on

\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
is exactly unitary change of basis. Hence the geometry of
\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
is governed by the standard dichotomy:

representation type is discrete
(isotypic multiplicities), while
basis choice is continuous
(unitary orbits).
We develop the structure at the
SU(2)
level, where only finitely many types occur at fixed
N
; the case
G=U(2)
adds determinant
twists and yields a typically countable refinement.

6.1
Orbits, stabilizers, and multiplicity data for
SU(2)

Let
\{V_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
denote the irreducible unitary
SU(2)
-modules, where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{d})=d+1
.
Since
SU(2)
is compact, every finite-dimensional unitary representation is completely reducible and is uniquely determined up to
unitary equivalence by its irreducible multiplicities; see, e.g.,
[
2
,
9
,
14
]
.
Thus any
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
yields a finite-support multiplicity family
m=(m_{d})_{d\geq 0}
such that

\mathbb{C}^{N}\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\big(\mathbb{C}^{m_{d}}\otimes V_{d}\big),\qquad\sum_{d\geq 0}m_{d}(d+1)=N.

(6.1)

Lemma 6.1

(Orbits are homogeneous)
.

For
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
, the
U(N)
-orbit

\mathcal{O}_{\phi}:=\{W\phi(\cdot)W^{-1}:W\in U(N)\}
is a smooth homogeneous manifold and

\mathcal{O}_{\phi}\ \cong\ U(N)\big/Z_{U(N)}\!\big(\phi(SU(2))\big),

where
Z_{U(N)}(\phi(SU(2)))=\{W\in U(N):W\phi(g)=\phi(g)W\ \forall g\in SU(2)\}
.

Proof.

Consider the smooth action of
U(N)
on
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
by conjugation,

(W\cdot\phi)(g)=W\phi(g)W^{-1}
.
The stabilizer of
\phi
consists precisely of those
W
commuting with
\phi(SU(2))
, i.e.
Z_{U(N)}(\phi(SU(2)))
.
By the orbit–stabilizer theorem for Lie group actions, the orbit is a smooth immersed submanifold and is diffeomorphic to the
homogeneous space
U(N)/Z_{U(N)}(\phi(SU(2)))
; see, e.g.,
[
9
,
14
]
.
∎

Lemma 6.2

(Centralizer and orbit dimension)
.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
have multiplicities
m=(m_{d})_{d\geq 0}
as in (
6.1
).
Then

Z_{U(N)}\!\big(\phi(SU(2))\big)\ \cong\ \prod_{d\geq 0}U(m_{d}),\qquad\dim(\mathcal{O}_{\phi})=N^{2}-\sum_{d\geq 0}m_{d}^{2}.

Proof.

Write
\mathbb{C}^{N}\simeq\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(\mathbb{C}^{m_{d}}\otimes V_{d})
so that
\phi(g)=\bigoplus_{d}(I_{m_{d}}\otimes\rho_{d}(g))
.
If
T\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
commutes with all
\phi(g)
, then
T
preserves each isotypic component and, on
\mathbb{C}^{m_{d}}\otimes V_{d}
,
Schur’s lemma gives
T|_{\mathbb{C}^{m_{d}}\otimes V_{d}}=A_{d}\otimes I_{V_{d}}
for some
A_{d}\in M_{m_{d}}(\mathbb{C})
; hence

\mathrm{End}_{SU(2)}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\cong\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(M_{m_{d}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes I_{V_{d}})
.
Intersecting with
U(N)
forces each
A_{d}
to be unitary, yielding

Z_{U(N)}(\phi(SU(2)))\cong\prod_{d\geq 0}U(m_{d})
.
Finally,
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}U(N)=N^{2}
and
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}U(m_{d})=m_{d}^{2}
, so

\dim(\mathcal{O}_{\phi})=N^{2}-\sum_{d\geq 0}m_{d}^{2}
.
∎

6.2
Faithfulness for
SU(2)
embeddings

Lemma 6.3

(Parity criterion)
.

Let
\rho=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(\mathbb{C}^{m_{d}}\otimes V_{d})
be a unitary representation of
SU(2)
.
Then
\rho
is faithful if and only if
m_{d}>0
for some odd
d
.

Proof.

The only nontrivial proper normal subgroup of
SU(2)
is its center
\{\pm I\}
.
On
V_{d}
, the element
-I
acts by
(-1)^{d}
, hence
\rho(-I)=I
iff all occurring
d
are even. Therefore
\ker(\rho)=\{I\}
iff some
odd
d
occurs.
∎

6.3
Homogeneous stratification of
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))

Theorem 6.4

(Homogeneous decomposition of
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
)
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathscr{F}_{SU(2)}(N)
be the set of finite-support families
m=(m_{d})_{d\geq 0}
with
m_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{d\geq 0}m_{d}(d+1)=N\quad\text{and}\quad\exists\,d\text{ odd with }m_{d}>0.

Then

\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))=\bigsqcup_{m\in\mathscr{F}_{SU(2)}(N)}\mathcal{O}_{m},\qquad\mathcal{O}_{m}\cong U(N)\Big/\Big(\prod_{d\geq 0}U(m_{d})\Big),

and
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{m})=N^{2}-\sum_{d\geq 0}m_{d}^{2}
.

Proof.

By complete reducibility, every unitary homomorphism
SU(2)\to U(N)
decomposes into irreducibles as in
(
6.1
); the multiplicities
m=(m_{d})_{d\geq 0}
determine the representation up to unitary
equivalence
[
2
,
9
,
14
]
. In particular, two homomorphisms are
U(N)
-conjugate if and only if they have
the same multiplicities, so the
U(N)
-orbits are indexed by families
m
satisfying the dimension constraint.

The embedding condition is faithfulness, which for
SU(2)
is equivalent to the parity requirement in
Lemma
6.3
. For each admissible
m
, choose an embedding
\phi_{m}
of type
m
and set

\mathcal{O}_{m}:=U(N)\cdot\phi_{m}
. Lemma
6.1
gives

\mathcal{O}_{m}\cong U(N)/Z_{U(N)}(\phi_{m}(SU(2)))
, and Lemma
6.2
identifies the stabilizer with

\prod_{d\geq 0}U(m_{d})
and yields
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{m})=N^{2}-\sum_{d\geq 0}m_{d}^{2}
.
∎

Corollary 6.5

(Finiteness of strata)
.

For fixed
N
, the set
\mathscr{F}_{SU(2)}(N)
is finite. Hence
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
is a finite disjoint union of
U(N)
-orbits.

Remark 6.6

(The two-level stratum)
.

The two-level sector corresponds to
m_{1}=1
,
m_{0}=N-2
,
m_{d}=0
otherwise, i.e.
V_{1}\oplus V_{0}^{\oplus(N-2)}
.
It is the orbit with stabilizer
U(1)\times U(N-2)
and dimension
N^{2}-(1+(N-2)^{2})=4N-5
, and it is organized by the
Grassmannian model developed in Section
3
.

6.4
Remark on
U(2)
and determinant twists

Remark 6.7

(

.

Every irreducible unitary

-representation is of the form
\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\otimes\det^{k}
with

and

[

,

Restricting to

Example 6.8

(Small-

.

The stratification in Theorem

is already visible for

.

7
Riemannian geometry and a variational principle for elementary gates

We equip
U(N)
with the bi-invariant Riemannian metric induced by the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing on
\mathfrak{u}(N)
.
For any embedding
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
(with
G\in\{SU(2),U(2)\}
), the image
K=\phi(G)
is a closed Lie subgroup of
U(N)
.
Bi-invariance implies that
K
is totally geodesic, so constrained minimizers inside
K
coincide with ambient geodesics whose initial
velocity lies in the embedded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}=d\phi_{e}(\mathrm{Lie}(G))
.
As a consequence, minimal-energy implementations in
K
are precisely constant-speed one-parameter subgroups generated by
minimal-norm logarithms in
\mathfrak{k}
.

Remark 7.1

(On
U(2)
embeddings and phase degrees of freedom)
.

If
\widehat{\phi}\in\mathrm{Emb}(U(2),U(N))
and
\phi:=\widehat{\phi}|_{SU(2)}
, then
\widehat{\phi}(U(2))
contains
\phi(SU(2))
and an
additional commuting
U(1)
subgroup coming from the center of
U(2)
.
At the Lie-algebra level
\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus i\mathbb{R}I
is a direct sum of commuting ideals; the pullback of the
Hilbert–Schmidt product via
d\widehat{\phi}_{e}
is
\Ad
-invariant and has no cross-term between these ideals.
Accordingly, the non-abelian
SU(2)
and central
U(1)
contributions to constrained geodesics and minimal energy can be treated
separately when needed.

7.1
Hilbert–Schmidt metric and basic variational functionals

The Hilbert–Schmidt pairing on
\mathfrak{u}(N)
,

\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathrm{hs}}:=\frac{1}{2}\Tr(X^\dagger Y),

is
\Ad
-invariant and induces a bi-invariant Riemannian metric on
U(N)
by left/right translation.
For an absolutely continuous curve
U:[0,1]\to U(N)
define the body velocity

A(t):=\dot{U}(t)\,U(t)^{-1}\in\mathfrak{u}(N)\quad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in[0,1],

and the energy and length

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[U]

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|A(t)\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{2}\,dt,

(7.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[U]

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}\|A(t)\|_{\mathrm{hs}}\,dt.

(7.2)

As usual, among curves with fixed endpoints, minimizing
\mathcal{E}
is equivalent to minimizing
\mathcal{L}
after
constant-speed reparametrization.

7.2
Bi-invariant geodesics and totally geodesic subgroups

Lemma 7.2

(Geodesics are one-parameter subgroups)
.

Let
G
be a Lie group equipped with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric induced by an
\Ad
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then the geodesics in
G
are exactly the curves

\gamma(t)=g_{0}\exp(tX),\qquad g_{0}\in G,\ X\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

For a bi-invariant metric, left and right translations are isometries. The Levi–Civita connection satisfies

\nabla_{X^{R}}Y^{R}=\tfrac{1}{2}[X,Y]^{R}
for right-invariant fields, hence the geodesic equation is equivalent to constant body velocity.
Thus
\gamma(t)=g_{0}\exp(tX)
. See
[
9
, Ch. 4]
or
[
14
, § II.1]
.
∎

Lemma 7.3

(Lie subgroups are totally geodesic)
.

Let
G
be a Lie group with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric and let
H\subset G
be a Lie subgroup with the induced metric.
Then
H
is totally geodesic in
G
.
Equivalently, if
\gamma
is a
G
-geodesic with
\gamma(0)\in H
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in T_{\gamma(0)}H
, then
\gamma(t)\in H
for all
t
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.2
,
\gamma(t)=\gamma(0)\exp(tX)
for some
X\in\mathfrak{g}
.
If
\gamma(0)\in H
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in T_{\gamma(0)}H
, then the right-translated velocity
X=\dot{\gamma}(0)\gamma(0)^{-1}

lies in
\mathfrak{h}=\mathrm{Lie}(H)
. Hence
\exp(tX)\in H
and therefore
\gamma(t)\in H
for all
t
.
∎

Remark 7.4

(Application to embedded copies of
G\in\{SU(2),U(2)\}
)
.

Fix
G\in\{SU(2),U(2)\}
and
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
. Then
K=\phi(G)
is a closed Lie subgroup of
U(N)
with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{k}=d\phi_{e}(\mathrm{Lie}(G)).

Since the Hilbert–Schmidt metric on
U(N)
is bi-invariant, Lemma
7.3
implies that
K
is
totally geodesic.

7.3
A constrained variational principle on an embedded subgroup

Definition 7.5

(Admissible curves constrained to an embedded subgroup)
.

Fix
G\in\{SU(2),U(2)\}
,
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
, and
K:=\phi(G)
. For
U_{\star}\in K
define

\mathcal{A}(I,U_{\star};K):=\big\{\,U\in AC([0,1],K):\ U(0)=I,\ U(1)=U_{\star}\,\big\}.

Minimal logarithms.

For
U_{\star}\in K
set

\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star}):=\{\,X\in\mathfrak{k}:\ \exp(X)=U_{\star}\,\}.

Since
K
is compact and connected,
\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star})\neq\varnothing
.

Theorem 7.6

(Constrained energy minimizers are one-parameter subgroups)
.

Fix
G\in\{SU(2),U(2)\}
and
\phi\in\mathrm{Emb}(G,U(N))
, and set
K=\phi(G)
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}=d\phi_{e}(\mathrm{Lie}(G))
.
Equip
U(N)
with the Hilbert–Schmidt bi-invariant metric and
K
with the induced metric. Fix
U_{\star}\in K
. Then:

(i)

There exists at least one minimizer of
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathcal{A}(I,U_{\star};K)
.

(ii)

A curve
U\in\mathcal{A}(I,U_{\star};K)
minimizes
\mathcal{E}
if and only if it is a constant-speed minimizing geodesic in
K
.
Equivalently, every minimizer is of the form

U(t)=\exp(tX),\qquad X\in\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star}),\qquad\|X\|_{\mathrm{hs}}=\min_{Y\in\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star})}\|Y\|_{\mathrm{hs}}.

(7.3)

(iii)

Every minimizer (
7.3
) is also an ambient geodesic of
U(N)
; conversely, any ambient geodesic from
I

to
U_{\star}
with initial velocity in
\mathfrak{k}
is a constrained minimizer.

(iv)

The minimal energy value is

\min_{U\in\mathcal{A}(I,U_{\star};K)}\mathcal{E}[U]=\frac{1}{2}\min_{X\in\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star})}\|X\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{\,2}.

(7.4)

Proof.

(i) The subgroup
K
is compact as the continuous image of the compact group
G
and is a smooth Riemannian manifold with the induced metric.
By Hopf–Rinow, any two points in a compact Riemannian manifold are joined by a minimizing geodesic; hence an energy minimizer exists

[
6
,
15
]
.

(ii) On any Riemannian manifold, energy minimizers with fixed endpoints are exactly constant-speed minimizing geodesics

[
6
, § 6.4]
. Since the metric on
K
is the restriction of a bi-invariant metric, it is bi-invariant on
K
.
Thus, by Lemma
7.2
, geodesics in
K
are one-parameter subgroups
t\mapsto\exp(tX)
with
X\in\mathfrak{k}
.
The endpoint condition forces
\exp(X)=U_{\star}
, i.e.
X\in\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star})
.
For such a curve, the body velocity is constant and equals
X
, hence
\mathcal{E}[\exp(tX)]=\tfrac{1}{2}\|X\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{2}
.
Minimizing
\mathcal{E}
is therefore equivalent to choosing a minimal-norm logarithm in
\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star})
.

(iii) By Lemma
7.3
,
K
is totally geodesic in
U(N)
, so every
K
-geodesic is an ambient

U(N)
-geodesic. Conversely, any ambient geodesic from
I
with initial velocity in
\mathfrak{k}
remains in
K
and is therefore a
constrained geodesic, hence an energy minimizer by (ii).

(iv) This follows by evaluating
\mathcal{E}
along
t\mapsto\exp(tX)
and taking the minimum over
X\in\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star})
,
using (ii).
∎

Remark 7.7

(Pullback metric and representation-dependent scaling)
.

The induced inner product on
\mathfrak{k}
pulls back via
d\phi_{e}
to an
\Ad
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
.
If
G=SU(2)
, then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{su}(2)
is simple, so any
\Ad
-invariant inner product is a positive scalar multiple of a fixed
normalization; equivalently, there exists
c_{\phi}>0
such that

\big\langle d\phi_{e}(A),d\phi_{e}(B)\big\rangle_{\mathrm{hs}}=c_{\phi}\,\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{ref}}\qquad(A,B\in\mathfrak{su}(2)).

If
G=U(2)
, then
\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus i\mathbb{R}I
is reductive and
\Ad
-invariant inner products are determined by two
positive parameters, one on each ideal.
In the two-level sector of Section
3
, the natural normalization inherited from
\mathfrak{u}(N)
yields
c_{\phi}=1
.

7.4
Explicit evaluation in
U(2)
and
SU(2)

Since
SU(2)
(and
U(2)
) logarithms admit explicit eigen-angle formulae, we record a convenient reference statement.

Lemma 7.8

(Minimal Hilbert–Schmidt logarithms in
U(2)
and
SU(2)
)
.

Let
V\in U(2)
. Choose a unitary diagonalization

V=Q\,\mathrm{diag}\!\big(e^{i\theta_{1}},e^{i\theta_{2}}\big)\,Q^{-1},\qquad\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(-\pi,\pi],

(where
\theta_{j}
are principal eigen-angles, uniquely determined up to permutation).
Then

\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{u}(2)}(V)=\left\{Q\,\mathrm{diag}\!\big(i(\theta_{1}+2\pi k_{1}),\,i(\theta_{2}+2\pi k_{2})\big)\,Q^{-1}:\ k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

and

\min_{X\in\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{u}(2)}(V)}\|X\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\big(\theta_{1}^{2}+\theta_{2}^{2}\big).

(7.5)

In particular, if
V\in SU(2)
has eigenvalues
e^{\pm i\alpha}
with
\alpha\in[0,\pi]
, then

\min_{X\in\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}(V)}\|X\|_{\mathrm{hs}}=\alpha,\qquad\min_{X\in\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}(V)}\|X\|_{\mathrm{hs}}^{\,2}=\alpha^{2}.

Proof.

The Hilbert–Schmidt norm is unitarily invariant, hence we may conjugate by
Q^{-1}
and reduce to the diagonal case.
Diagonal logarithms of
\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\theta_{1}},e^{i\theta_{2}})
are exactly
\mathrm{diag}(i(\theta_{1}+2\pi k_{1}),\,i(\theta_{2}+2\pi k_{2}))
.
Their squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm equals

\tfrac{1}{2}\big((\theta_{1}+2\pi k_{1})^{2}+(\theta_{2}+2\pi k_{2})^{2}\big)
, minimized by choosing the representatives closest to
0
, i.e. by

k_{1}=k_{2}=0
for principal angles. This yields (
7.5
). The
SU(2)
case follows from

(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(\alpha,-\alpha)
.
∎

Remark 7.9

(Non-uniqueness and cut locus)
.

The minimizer in Theorem
7.6
need not be unique: non-uniqueness occurs precisely when

\mathrm{Log}_{\mathfrak{k}}(U_{\star})
contains more than one element of minimal Hilbert–Schmidt norm, equivalently when
U_{\star}
lies in
the cut locus of
I
in
K
. For generic
U_{\star}
the minimal logarithm is unique.

7.5
Interpretation and the two-level sector

Theorem
7.6
identifies the minimal-action implementation of a target
U_{\star}\in K=\phi(G)
:
among all admissible curves constrained to remain in
K
, the minimizers are constant-speed geodesics

t\mapsto\exp(tX)
generated by minimal-norm logarithms
X\in\mathfrak{k}
.

In the two-level (Grassmannian) sector of Section
3
, generators take the descriptor form

X=X_{W,f}(A)=fAf^{-1}\oplus 0
with
A\in\mathfrak{su}(2)
, and Lemma
3.9
implies that

\|X\|_{\mathrm{hs}}=\|A\|_{\mathrm{hs}}
is frame-independent.
Thus the minimal-action cost of a two-level elementary gate is exactly the minimal Hilbert–Schmidt norm of its internal
2\times 2

generator, providing a direct bridge between intrinsic geometry on
U(N)
and concrete
SU(2)
control parameters.
When an additional abelian
U(1)
phase is tracked (i.e.
SU(N)[W]
extended to
U(N)[W]
), its contribution is generated by a commuting
central direction and can be compiled/penalized separately, consistent with Remark
7.1
.

8
Conclusions and final remarks

We introduced an intrinsic notion of elementary gates in
U(2^{n})
based on Lie group embeddings: a two-level logical degree of freedom
is modeled by an internal copy of
SU(2)
inside
U(N)
,
N=2^{n}
(and, when phases are kept explicit, by an embedding of
U(2)
).
This viewpoint separates
discrete
representation data from
continuous
gauge/basis choices, and yields a structured,
basis-invariant parameter space of admissible primitives.

Embedding landscape and a canonical sector.

At fixed
N
, the space
\mathrm{Emb}(SU(2),U(N))
decomposes into finitely many
U(N)
-conjugacy orbits, each an explicit homogeneous manifold
indexed by isotypic multiplicities, with stabilizers computed as centralizers.
Among these strata, the two-level sector provides the canonical computational interface: a logical qubit is a two-plane

W\in\mathrm{Gr}_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
, and the corresponding subgroup is
SU(N)[W]
.
Given any Stiefel frame
F\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 2}
with
F^{\dagger}F=I_{2}
and
\mathrm{Ran}(F)=W
, two-level gates admit the concrete realization

U_{W,F}(S)=I_{N}+F(S-I_{2})F^{\dagger}\in SU(N)[W],\qquad S\in SU(2),

(8.1)

and similarly with
S
replaced by
V\in U(2)
for
U(N)[W]
.
The support subgroup depends only on
W
, while the internal labeling depends on
F
only up to the natural
PSU(2)
gauge.

Expressiveness and a finite-alphabet interface.

The universality mechanism developed in Section
4
shows that two-level
SU(2)
gates already generate
SU(N)
, and
that full reachability in
U(N)
follows after adjoining global phase (equivalently, by explicit bookkeeping of abelian factors).
Section
5
then provides a modular compilation route: exact two-level factorizations reduce synthesis in
U(N)
to repeated
approximation problems in
SU(2)
(after optional phase normalization), and finite-alphabet approximation on
SU(2)
lifts through
two-level embeddings to uniform operator-norm control in
U(N)
, with accuracy–length guarantees inherited from the underlying

SU(2)
approximation scheme.

Limitations and directions.

Several mathematically natural extensions emerge:

•

Beyond bi-invariant costs:
the Hilbert–Schmidt metric yields clean variational statements (total geodesy and
minimal-logarithm characterizations), whereas realistic control costs are typically anisotropic; extending the analysis to
right-invariant or sub-Riemannian models is a natural next step.

•

Descriptor selection:
a fixed
U\in U(N)
may admit multiple embedding-elementary descriptions across different strata and
supports; choosing descriptors optimally leads to constrained optimization on stratified spaces.

•

Depth/complexity refinement:
QR/Givens-type synthesis is explicit but not depth-optimal in general; sharper complexity bounds
should exploit target structure (symmetry, sparsity, tensor-network structure) and may benefit from structure-adapted factorizations and
improved
SU(2)

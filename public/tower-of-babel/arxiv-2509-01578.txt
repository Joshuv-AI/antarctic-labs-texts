On Hom-Analogues of Heaps and Trusses
Tarik Anowar, Ripan Saha, Sayan Thokdar 1. Department of Mathematics, Raiganj University, Raiganj 733134, West Bengal, India2. Department of Mathematics, Raiganj University, Raiganj 733134, West Bengal, India3. Abstract




ABSTRACT

This paper develops the foundations of
Hom-heaps
,
Hom-trusses
,
and
Hom-braces
as natural Hom-type analogues of their classical counterparts.
We establish the correspondence between Hom-heaps and Hom-groups, showing that the
retract of a Hom-heap at a point yields a Hom-group precisely when the point is fixed
under the twisting map. Three interrelated definitions of Hom-trusses are introduced and
their structural properties are investigated, including the construction of modules over
Hom-trusses. For Hom-braces, we propose three variants and demonstrate their close
connections with Hom-trusses, proving in particular that certain Hom-trusses naturally
give rise to Hom-braces and vice versa. Our results provide a systematic framework that
extends heap and truss theory into the Hom-algebraic setting, opening new perspectives
for applications in Yang–Baxter theory, non-associative geometry, and categorical
algebra.

Key words
: Hom-heap, Hom-truss, Hom-brace, Heap, Truss, Brace.

16Y99, 17A99, 08A99

1
Introduction

Heaps were first studied over a century ago by Prüfer
[
19
]
and Baer
[
2
]
,
though they attracted relatively little attention for many years (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
).
Defined as algebraic structures equipped with a ternary operation, heaps appear naturally
in loop theory, symmetric spaces, and non-associative algebra. Every group
(G,\cdot)

yields a heap via
\langle a,b,c\rangle=a\cdot b^{-1}\cdot c
, and conversely, every
non-empty heap with a distinguished element
e
recovers a group as its
retract
.
This deep connection with group theory makes heaps a versatile tool for exploring
generalizations of algebraic structures.

The importance of heaps has resurfaced in recent years through the work of Brzeziński,
who introduced the notion of
trusses

[
6
]
. A truss enriches a heap with
a multiplication compatible with the ternary operation via a distributive law, thus
providing a unifying framework that encompasses groups, rings, braces, and related systems.
This perspective has proved useful in the study of set-theoretic solutions of the
Yang–Baxter equation, as well as in ring theory and category theory. Brzeziński
[
5
]
also
reformulated affine spaces in terms of abelian heaps and developed the affinization of
algebraic structures such as associative, Lie
[
1
]
, and Leibniz algebras
[
4
]
, thereby opening a
new direction in the study of non-associative geometry.

Braces were first introduced by Wolfgang Rump
[
21
]
in 2007 as an algebraic structure connecting set-theoretic solutions of the Yang–Baxter equation with radical rings. Since then, braces have become a central tool in the study of non-degenerate involutive set-theoretic solutions
[
8
]
, providing a unifying framework that links group theory, ring theory, and Hopf algebra techniques. The motivation for introducing braces lies in their ability to translate the combinatorial problem of constructing solutions to the Yang–Baxter equation into an algebraic setting, where the rich interplay between additive and multiplicative structures yields powerful classification and construction methods. This approach not only generalizes earlier results on radical rings but also opens pathways to new applications in quantum groups and related areas.

Hom-algebraic structures arise by twisting classical identities with a distinguished self-map (or “hom” map), so familiar axioms hold only up to that twist. This simple modification produces a far richer class of examples — including q-deformations, endomorphism-twisted algebras and discrete/time-evolution variants — and forces new cohomologies, representation theories and deformation phenomena to appear. Introducing Hom-heaps and Hom-braces lets us carry the powerful heap/brace machinery into this twisted setting: it both generalizes classical classification and extension results and creates avenues to construct new (twisted) set-theoretic solutions of Yang–Baxter–type problems.

Hom-type algebras
were introduced by Hartwig, Larsson, and Silvestrov
[
12
]
in the context of
q
-deformations. These structures
generalize classical algebras by twisting defining identities with a self-map
\alpha
,
giving rise to notions such as Hom-associativity and Hom-Lie brackets. Since their
introduction, Hom-algebraic structures have been studied extensively, leading to the
development of Hom-groups
[
13
,
16
]
, Hom-rings
[
3
]
, Hom-Lie algebras
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
11
]
, and beyond. A Hom-group is a non-associative generalization of a group, where associativity and unitality are twisted by a compatible self-map. Recently, Hom-groups have attracted considerable attention in mathematical research
[
13
,
14
,
10
]
.

The aim of this paper is to extend these ideas into the affine and distributive setting
by introducing and studying
Hom-heaps
,
Hom-trusses
, and
Hom-braces

as natural Hom-analogues of their classical counterparts. In the Hom setting, we show
that the retract of a Hom-heap at a point
x
forms a Hom-group precisely when
x
is a
fixed point of
\alpha
. We also showed that from a Hom-group one can define a Hom-heap by twisting the classical approach of the heap setting, thereby generalizing the classical correspondence between heaps and groups. We also study Hom-subheaps and normal Hom-subheaps, obtaining several interesting results. We further introduce three interrelated definitions of Hom-trusses, motivated by analogous constructions in Hom-ring theory, and establish equivalences between them.
Modules over Hom-trusses are also defined and studied in this framework. For Hom-braces,
we present three types of structures and establish connections with Hom-trusses; in
particular, we prove that every Hom-truss of type
(0)
in which the multiplicative part
forms a Hom-group gives rise to a Hom-brace of type
(0)
, and conversely.

The paper therefore lays the foundations for a systematic study of Hom-type structures
within the affine and distributive framework. By combining heaps, trusses, and Hom-type
twistings, our work not only generalizes existing algebraic systems but also opens new
directions for applications, including Yang–Baxter theory, categorical analysis, and
non-associative geometry.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall commutative the fundamental notions of Hom-groups, heaps, and braces that will be used throughout the paper. For further details, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
9
,
20
,
21
,
5
,
10
]
.

2.1 Definition
.

[
19
,
7
]

A
heap
is a set
H
equipped with a ternary operation

\langle-,-,-\rangle:H\times H\times H\longrightarrow H,\quad(a,b,c)\mapsto\langle a,b,c\rangle,

satisfying, for all
a,b,c,d,e\in H
:

1.

\langle\langle a,b,c\rangle,d,e\rangle=\langle a,b,\langle c,d,e\rangle\rangle
(associativity),

2.

\langle a,a,b\rangle=b=\langle b,a,a\rangle
(Mal’tcev identity).

A heap is called
abelian
if
\langle a,b,c\rangle=\langle c,b,a\rangle
for all
a,b,c\in H
.

2.2 Example
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be an abelian group. Then
G
becomes an abelian heap with the ternary operation

\langle a,b,c\rangle=a\cdot b^{-1}\cdot c,\quad\text{for all }a,b,c\in G.

2.3 Remark
.

Every group is a heap under the operation defined in the above example.
However, the converse is in general not true; for instance, if
H
is the empty set, then it cannot form a group.
For any non-empty heap
H
and an element
e\in H
, the set
H
endowed with the binary operation

a+_{e}b=\langle a,e,b\rangle

is a group (abelian if
H
is abelian), known as the
retract
of
H
at
e
, denoted by
G(H,e)
.
Moreover, the inverse of
a\in H
in
G(H,e)
is given by

a^{-1}=\langle e,a,e\rangle.

A heap morphism from
(H,\langle-,-,-\rangle_{1})
to
(\bar{H},\langle-,-,-\rangle_{2})
is a map
f:H\to\bar{H}
such that
f(\langle a,b,c\rangle_{1})=\langle f(a),f(b),f(c)\rangle_{2}
for all
a,b,c\in H
.

2.4 Definition

(
[
14
]
)
.

A regular
Hom-group
consists of a set
G
together with a distinguished element
e\in G
, a bijective map
\alpha:G\to G
, and a binary operation
\bullet:G\times G\to G
, such that the following conditions hold:

(i)

For all
a,b,c\in G
,
\alpha(a)\bullet(b\bullet c)=(a\bullet b)\bullet\alpha(c).

(ii)

For all
a,b\in G
,
\alpha(a\bullet b)=\alpha(a)\bullet\alpha(b).

(iii)

The distinguished element
e
is called the
unit
and satisfies

a\bullet e=e\bullet a=\alpha(a),

(iv)

For every
a\in G
, there exists an element
a^{-1}\in G
such that

a\bullet a^{-1}=a^{-1}\bullet a=e.

2.5 Remark
.

There exists a more general definition of a Hom-group
[
16
]
, where the map
\alpha
is not required to be bijective and the inverse condition is also twisted. In this paper, however, we restrict ourselves to the case where
\alpha
is bijective, and hence we work with
regular Hom-groups
. Throughout the paper, by a Hom-group we shall always mean a regular Hom-group.

Let
(G,\bullet)
be a group and let
\alpha:G\to G
be a group automorphism. Define a new binary operation

a\bullet_{\alpha}b:=\alpha(a\bullet b),\quad\text{for all }a,b\in G.

Then the triple
(G,\bullet_{\alpha},\alpha)
forms a Hom-group.

2.6 Proposition
.

Let
(G,\bullet,\alpha)
be a Hom-group and
e
is an identity element. Then

[
15
]

\alpha(e)=e

[
13
]
For each
a\in G
,
\alpha(a)^{-1}=\alpha(a^{-1})
.

Let
(G,\bullet,\alpha)
and
(H,\star,\beta)
be two Hom-groups.
A map
f:G\rightarrow H
is called a
homomorphism of Hom-groups
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

For all
a,b\in G
,

f(a\bullet b)=f(a)\star f(b)
.

(2)

For all
a\in G
,
\beta(f(a))=f(\alpha(a))
.

Moreover, if
f:G\rightarrow H
is bijective, then
f
is called an
isomorphism
.

2.7 Definition
.

[
11
]

Let
R
be a set together with two binary operations
+:R\times R\rightarrow R
and
\bullet:R\times R\rightarrow R
, one bijective map
\alpha:R\rightarrow R
and a special element
0\in R
. Then
(R,+,\bullet,0,\alpha)
is called a Hom-ring if:

(i)

(R,+,0)
is an abelian group.

(ii)

The multiplication is distributive on both sides.

(iii)

\alpha
is an abelian group homomorphism.

(iv)

\alpha
and
\bullet
satisfy the Hom-associativity condition;

\alpha(a)\bullet(b\bullet c)=(a\bullet b)\bullet\alpha(c).

A Hom-ring
(R,+,\bullet,0,\alpha)
is
unitary
if it has
1\in R
with

a\bullet 1=1\bullet a=a
for all
a\in R
, and
\alpha(1)=1
.

2.8 Remark
.

There are two alternative definitions of a Hom-ring given in
[
3
]
. In this paper, we introduce the truss versions of these definitions and use them further to establish connections with other algebraic structures.

2.9 Definition
.

[
6
]

A
truss
is an algebraic system
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet)
consisting of a ternary operation

\langle-,-,-\rangle:T\times T\times T\to T
such that
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle)
is an abelian heap,
together with an associative binary operation
\bullet:T\times T\to T
which distributes over

\langle-,-,-\rangle
. That is, for all
a,b,c,d\in T
,

a\bullet\langle b,c,d\rangle=\langle a\bullet b,\,a\bullet c,\,a\bullet d\rangle,

and

\langle a,b,c\rangle\bullet d=\langle a\bullet d,\,b\bullet d,\,c\bullet d\rangle.

2.10 Example
.

Let
H
be an abelian heap and
E(H)=\{f:H\to H\mid f\text{ is a heap homomorphism}\}
. For
f,g,h\in E(H)
and
a\in H
, define

[f,g,h](a)=\langle f(a),g(a),h(a)\rangle.

With multiplication given by composition,
E(H)
is a truss.

Let
(T_{1},\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet)
and
(T_{2},\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet)
be two trusses.
A function
\alpha:T_{1}\to T_{2}
is called a
truss homomorphism
if it is both a heap homomorphism with respect to
\langle-,-,-\rangle
and a semigroup homomorphism with respect to
\bullet
.

2.11 Definition
.

[
20
]

A set
B
with two binary operations
\star
and
\bullet
is called a
brace
if, for all
a,b,c\in B
,

(i)

(B,\star)
is an abelian group;

(ii)

(B,\bullet)
is a group;

(iii)

a\bullet(b\star c)=(a\bullet b)\star a^{\star}\star(a\bullet c),\qquad(a\star b)\bullet c=(a\bullet c)\star c^{\star}\star(b\bullet c),

where
a^{\star}
and
c^{\star}
denote the inverses of
a
and
c
, respectively, with respect to
\star
.

A truss
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet)
is called
unital
if
(T,\bullet)
is a monoid with identity element
1
.

An element
0
of a truss
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet)
is called an
absorber
if, for all
a\in T
,

a\bullet 0=0=0\bullet a.

Note that an absorber, if it exists, is unique.

3
Hom-Heap

In this section, we introduce the notion of a Hom-heap and provide some examples. We also discuss the relation between Hom-groups and Hom-heaps.

3.1 Definition
.

A
Hom-heap
is a set
H
together with a bijective map
\alpha:H\to H
and a ternary operation

\langle-,-,-\rangle:H\times H\times H\to H,

such that the following axioms are satisfied for all
e,f,x,y,z\in H
:

(i)

Hom-Mal’cev property:

\langle x,x,y\rangle=\alpha(y)=\langle y,x,x\rangle.

(ii)

Hom-associativity property:

\langle\alpha(e),\alpha(f),\langle x,y,z\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle e,f,x\rangle,\alpha(y),\alpha(z)\rangle.

(iii)

Compatibility with
\alpha
:

\alpha\langle x,y,z\rangle=\langle\alpha(x),\alpha(y),\alpha(z)\rangle.

3.2 Definition
.

A Hom-heap
(H,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\alpha)
is called
involutive
if
\alpha^{2}=id
.

3.3 Example
.

The additive group of integers
(\mathbb{Z},+)
admits a Hom-heap structure with

\langle-,-,-\rangle:\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\langle a,b,c\rangle=-(a-b+c),\quad\alpha(a)=-a.

Easy to verify that this is an example of an involutive Hom-heap.

3.4 Example
.

Every heap
(H,\langle-,-,-\rangle)
together with a heap automorphism
\alpha:H\to H
admits a Hom-heap structure

(H,\langle-,-,-\rangle_{\alpha},\alpha),

where
\langle x,y,z\rangle_{\alpha}=\alpha(\langle x,y,z\rangle)
.
In particular, every heap can be regarded as a Hom-heap by taking
\alpha
to be the identity map.
Thus, the notion of a Hom-heap generalizes the classical notion of a heap.

3.5 Example
.

The structure
(\mathbb{R},+,0,\mathrm{Id})
is a Hom-group.
Define the ternary map
\langle-,-,-\rangle:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\langle a,b,c\rangle=a-b+c.

Then
(\mathbb{R},\langle-,-,-\rangle,\mathrm{Id})
is a Hom-heap.

3.6 Example
.

Define a ternary operation
\langle-,-,-\rangle
on
\mathbb{R}
by

\langle a,b,c\rangle=\frac{a-b+c}{n},\quad n\geq 2.

This operation is not associative in the ternary sense; hence
(\mathbb{R},\langle-,-,-\rangle)
is not a heap.
We show that
(\mathbb{R},\langle-,-,-\rangle,\alpha)
is a Hom-heap, where
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\alpha(a)=\frac{a}{n}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\alpha
is a bijection on
\mathbb{R}
. For all
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
,

\alpha\langle a,b,c\rangle=\alpha\left(\frac{a-b+c}{n}\right)=\frac{a-b+c}{n^{2}},

\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(b),\alpha(c)\rangle=\left\langle\frac{a}{n},\frac{b}{n},\frac{c}{n}\right\rangle=\frac{a-b+c}{n^{2}}.

Thus,
\alpha
is compatible with
\langle-,-,-\rangle
.

Moreover, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\langle a,a,b\rangle=\langle b,a,a\rangle=\frac{a-a+b}{n}=\frac{b}{n}=\alpha(b),

so the Hom-Mal’tcev identity holds.

Finally, for all
a,b,c,e,f\in\mathbb{R}
,

\langle\alpha(e),\alpha(f),\langle a,b,c\rangle\rangle=\left\langle\frac{e}{n},\frac{f}{n},\frac{a-b+c}{n}\right\rangle=\frac{e-f+(a-b+c)}{n^{2}},

\langle\langle e,f,a\rangle,\alpha(b),\alpha(c)\rangle=\left\langle\frac{e-f+a}{n},\frac{b}{n},\frac{c}{n}\right\rangle=\frac{(e-f+a)-b+c}{n^{2}}.

Since addition in
\mathbb{R}
is associative, the Hom-associativity holds.
Hence
(\mathbb{R},\langle-,-,-\rangle,\alpha)
is a Hom-heap.

3.7 Remark
.

The above example defines a Hom-heap, but not a heap, under the given ternary operation.

From Remark
2.3
, we know that for any non-empty heap
H
and any element
x\in H
, one can construct a binary operation by retracting at
x
, and under this operation
H
forms a group.
This naturally raises the question: is it possible to obtain a Hom-group by retracting at an element
x
in a Hom-heap?

The answer is, in general,
no
. For instance, in the example above we have

\alpha(x)=\tfrac{x}{n}\neq x\quad\text{(in general)}.

However, in a Hom-group the identity element
x
must satisfy the condition
\alpha(x)=x
.
Thus, the retract construction that works for heaps does not directly yield a Hom-group in the Hom-setting.

In general, one can obtain a Hom-group from a Hom-heap by retracting at a point
x\in H

if and only if

\alpha(x)=x
.
In this case,
x
serves as the identity element in the resulting Hom-group.

3.8 Proposition
.

Let
(H,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\alpha)
be a Hom-heap. For all
a,b,c,d,e\in H
, we have

\langle a,b,\langle\alpha^{-1}(c),\alpha^{-1}(d),\alpha^{-1}(e)\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(b),\alpha^{-1}(c)\rangle,d,e\rangle.

Proof.

Since
\alpha
is bijective, we have
\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(x))=x
for all
x\in H
.
Using the bijectivity of
\alpha
and the Hom-associativity property, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle a,b,\langle\alpha^{-1}(c),\alpha^{-1}(d),\alpha^{-1}(e)\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(a)),\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(b)),\langle\alpha^{-1}(c),\alpha^{-1}(d),\alpha^{-1}(e)\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\langle\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(b),\alpha^{-1}(c)\rangle,d,e\rangle.

∎

3.9 Theorem
.

Given a Hom-heap
(H,<-,-,->,\alpha)
. The binary operation
\bullet_{e}
retract by an any element
e\in H
, define as
a\bullet_{e}b=<a,e,b>
. Then
(H,\bullet_{e},\alpha)
form a Hom-group for
\alpha(e)=e
.

Proof.

Let
(H,<-,-,->,\alpha)
be Hom-heap and
\alpha(e)=e
, for
e\in H
. Since
\alpha
is bijective, so
\alpha^{-1}(e)=e
. Then
a\bullet_{e}b=<a,e,b>
is a binary operation. Under this binary operation and
\alpha
map
(H,\bullet_{e},\alpha)
satisfy the Hom-group condition.

(1)

For any
a,b,c\in H
, we get,

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\bullet_{e}(b\bullet_{e}c)

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet_{e}<b,e,c>

\displaystyle=<\alpha(a),e,<b,e,c>>

\displaystyle=<\alpha(a),\alpha(e),<b,e,c>>

\displaystyle=<<a,e,b>,\alpha(e),\alpha(c)>

\displaystyle=<(a\bullet_{e}b),e,\alpha(c)>

\displaystyle=(a\bullet_{e}b)\bullet_{e}\alpha(c).

Therefore; Hom-associativity hold; i.e.
\alpha(a)\bullet_{e}(b\bullet_{e}c)=(a\bullet_{e}b)\bullet_{e}\alpha(c)
.

(2)

For any
a,b\in H
,

\displaystyle\alpha(a\bullet_{e}b)

\displaystyle=\alpha<a,e,b>

\displaystyle=<\alpha(a),\alpha(e),\alpha(b)>

\displaystyle=<\alpha(a),e,\alpha(b)>

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet_{e}\alpha(b).

Therefore the map
\alpha
is multiplicative.

(3)

For any
a\in H
, we get
a\bullet_{e}e=<a,e,e>=\alpha(a)
and
e\bullet_{e}a=<e,e,a>=\alpha(a)
. The element
e
is called unit and it satisfies the Hom-unitary conditions.

(4)

For any
a\in H
, we get an element
a^{-1}=<e,\alpha^{-1}(a),e>
, such that

\displaystyle a\bullet_{e}a^{-1}

\displaystyle=<a,e,a^{-1}>

\displaystyle=<a,e,<e,\alpha^{-1}(a),e>>

\displaystyle=<a,e,<\alpha^{-1}(e),\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(e)>>

\displaystyle=<\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(e),\alpha^{-1}(e)>,a,e>~~~~\text{(By~Proposition ~~ \ref{Prop1})}

\displaystyle=<\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(a)),a,e>

\displaystyle=\alpha(e)

\displaystyle=e

similarly, we can show that
a^{-1}\bullet_{e}a=e
. That is every element has an inverse.

∎

3.10 Theorem
.

Let
(H,\bullet,1,\alpha)
be a Hom-group. Define a ternary operation
\langle-,-,-\rangle:H\times H\times H\to H
by

\langle a,b,c\rangle=a\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(b^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c)).

Then
(H,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\alpha)
is a Hom-heap.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c,d,e\in H
and let
\alpha:H\to H
be the given structure map.

First, we verify that
\alpha
preserves the ternary operation:

\displaystyle\alpha\langle a,b,c\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha(a\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(b^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c)))

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet(b^{-1}\bullet c)

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet(\alpha^{-1}\alpha(b^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}\alpha(c))

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(\alpha(b)^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(\alpha(c)))

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(b),\alpha(c)\rangle.

Hence
\alpha
preserves the ternary operation.

Next, we check the Hom-Mal’cev property:

\displaystyle\langle a,a,b\rangle

\displaystyle=a\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(b))

\displaystyle=\alpha\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(b))

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1}))\bullet b

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}(a\bullet a^{-1})\bullet b

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}(1)\bullet b

\displaystyle=1\bullet b

\displaystyle=\alpha(b).

and

\displaystyle\langle b,a,a\rangle

\displaystyle=b\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(a))

\displaystyle=b\bullet\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1}\bullet a)

\displaystyle=b\bullet 1

\displaystyle=\alpha(b).

Finally, we verify the Hom-heap associativity property:

\displaystyle\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(b),\langle c,d,e\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(b),(c\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(d^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(e))\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet\{\alpha^{-1}(\alpha(b)^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(d^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(e))\}

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet\{\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(b^{-1}))\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(c)\bullet(\alpha^{-2}(d^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-2}(e)))\}

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet\{(\alpha^{-1}(b^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c))\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(d^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(e))\}

\displaystyle=\{a\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(b^{-1})\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c))\}\bullet(\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(d^{-1}))\bullet\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(e)))

\displaystyle=\langle a,b,c\rangle\bullet(\alpha^{-1}(\alpha(d^{-1}))\bullet\alpha^{-1}(\alpha(e)))

\displaystyle=\langle\langle a,b,c\rangle,\alpha(d),\alpha(e)\rangle.

Since the expressions agree,
\alpha
satisfies the Hom-heap associativity property.

Therefore,
(H,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\alpha)
is a Hom-heap.
∎

3.11 Note
.

Every Hom-group is a Hom-heap under the above-mentioned ternary operation.
However, the converse is in general not true, since the empty set also forms a Hom-heap but not a Hom-group.

3.12 Definition

.

3.13 Definition

.

If

are identity maps, then

is a heap homomorphism.

3.15 Definition
.

3.17 Lemma
.

A Hom-subheap
S
of

is normal if and only if, for all

and
e,s\in S
, there exists
t\in S
such that

\langle a,e,s\rangle=\langle t,e,a\rangle.

Proof.

If such an element
t\in S
exists for all

and
e,s\in S
such that equation (
1
) holds, then by definition
S
is a normal Hom-subheap of
H
.

Conversely, assume
S
is a normal Hom-subheap of a Hom-heap
H
. Since
S
is a Hom-subheap, we have
\alpha(S)\subseteq S
.
Let
e,f,s\in S
. Then
\alpha^{-1}(e),\alpha^{-1}(f),\alpha^{-1}(s)\in S
. Define

s^{\prime}=\langle\alpha^{-1}(e),\alpha^{-1}(f),\alpha^{-1}(s)\rangle\in S.

By the definition of a normal Hom-subheap, there exists
t^{\prime}\in S
such that, for all

,

\displaystyle\langle t^{\prime},e,a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a,e,s^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a,e,\langle\alpha^{-1}(e),\alpha^{-1}(f),\alpha^{-1}(s)\rangle\rangle\quad\text{(substituting $s^{\prime}$)}

\displaystyle=\langle\langle\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(e),\alpha^{-1}(e)\rangle,f,s\rangle\quad\text{(by Proposition~\ref{Prop1})}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(a)),f,s\rangle\quad\text{(by the Mal'cev identity for Hom-heaps)}

\displaystyle=\langle a,f,s\rangle.

Now define

\displaystyle\langle t,f,a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\langle\alpha^{-1}(t^{\prime}),\alpha^{-1}(e),\alpha^{-1}(f)\rangle,f,s\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle t^{\prime},e,\langle\alpha^{-1}(f),\alpha^{-1}(f),\alpha^{-1}(a)\rangle\rangle\quad\text{(by Proposition~\ref{Prop1})}

\displaystyle=\langle t^{\prime},e,\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(a))\rangle\quad\text{(by the Mal'cev identity for Hom-heaps)}

\displaystyle=\langle t^{\prime},e,a\rangle.

Hence, for all

3.18 Proposition
.

Let
S
be a Hom-subheap of a Hom-heap
H
. Then, for all
a\in H
, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

For all
e,s\in S
, there exists
t\in S
such that
\langle a,e,s\rangle=\langle t,e,a\rangle
.

2.

For each
e,s\in S
,
\langle\langle a,e,s\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle\in S
.

3.

For each
e\in S
,
\langle\langle a,e,S\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle\subseteq S
.

4.

For each
e\in S
,
\langle\langle a,e,S\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle=S
.

5.

For each
e\in S
,
\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(e),S\rangle=\langle S,\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
From
(1)
, for all
a\in H
and
e,s\in S
, there exists
t\in S
such that
\langle a,e,s\rangle=\langle t,e,a\rangle
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\langle a,e,s\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\langle t,e,a\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(t),\alpha(e),\langle a,a,e\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(t),\alpha(e),\alpha(e)\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}(t)\in S.

Hence
(2)
holds.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
:
If
(2)
holds for all
a\in H
and all
e,s\in S
, then clearly for each
e\in S
,

\langle\langle a,e,S\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle\subseteq S.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
:
We want to prove that
\langle\langle a,e,S\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle=S
for all
a\in H
and
e\in S
.
From
(3)
, we already have

\langle\langle a,e,S\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle\subseteq S.

It remains to prove
S\subseteq\langle\langle a,e,S\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle
.

Let
s\in S
. Since
S
is a Hom-subheap, there exists
s^{\prime}\in S
such that

s=\alpha^{2}(s^{\prime}).

By the Hom-Mal’cev identity,

s=\langle\alpha(s^{\prime}),\alpha(e),\alpha(e)\rangle=\langle\alpha(s^{\prime}),\alpha(e),\langle a,a,e\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle s^{\prime},e,a\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle.

From
(1)
, for all
a\in H
and
e,s\in S
, there exists
s^{\prime\prime}\in S
such that
\langle a,e,s\rangle=\langle s^{\prime\prime},e,a\rangle
. Thus,

s\in\langle\langle a,e,S\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle.

Hence
(4)
holds.

(4)\Rightarrow(5)
:
Let
\langle s,\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle
be any element of
\langle S,\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle
.
Since
S
is a Hom-subheap, for
s\in S
there exists
s^{\prime}\in S
such that
\alpha^{-1}(s^{\prime})=s
.
From
(4)
, there exists
s^{\prime\prime}\in S
such that

s^{\prime}=\langle\langle a,e,s^{\prime\prime}\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle.

Then

\displaystyle\langle s,\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha^{-1}(s^{\prime}),\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha^{-1}(\langle\langle a,e,s^{\prime\prime}\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(e)\rangle),\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\langle\alpha^{-1}\langle a,e,s^{\prime\prime}\rangle,a,e\rangle,\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(\alpha^{-1}\langle a,e,s^{\prime\prime}\rangle),\alpha(a),\langle e,e,a\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\langle a,e,s^{\prime\prime}\rangle,\alpha(a),\alpha(a)\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle a,e,s^{\prime\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(e),\alpha(s^{\prime\prime})\rangle.

Let
\alpha(s^{\prime\prime})=s^{\prime\prime\prime}\in S
. Then
\langle s,\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle\in\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(e),S\rangle
.
Thus
\langle S,\alpha(e),\alpha(a)\rangle\subseteq\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(e),S\rangle
.
The reverse inclusion is similar, so
(5)
holds.

(5)\Rightarrow(1)
:
For each
a\in H
, there exists
a^{\prime}\in H
such that
\alpha(a^{\prime})=a
.
From
(5)
, we have

\langle\alpha(a^{\prime}),\alpha(e),S\rangle=\langle S,\alpha(e),\alpha(a^{\prime})\rangle.

Putting
\alpha(a^{\prime})=a
, we get

\langle a,\alpha(e),S\rangle=\langle S,\alpha(e),a\rangle.

That is, for all
a\in H
and
e,s\in S
, there exists
s^{\prime}\in S
such that

\langle a,\alpha(e),s\rangle=\langle s^{\prime},\alpha(e),a\rangle.

Hence
(1)
follows.
∎

4
Hom-Truss

In this section, we define Hom-trusses based on abelian heaps in three different but interconnected ways, and also establish some related results.

4.1 Definition
.

Let
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet,\alpha)
be a set equipped with a ternary operation
\langle-,-,-\rangle
, a binary operation
\bullet
, and a bijective unary operation
\alpha:T\rightarrow T
.
We say that
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet,\alpha)
is a
Hom-truss of type
(0)
if:

(I)

(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle)
is an abelian heap,

(II)

\alpha
is an abelian heap endomorphism, and

(III)

the following conditions are satisfied:

\alpha(a)\bullet(b\bullet c)=(a\bullet b)\bullet\alpha(c)\quad\text{(Hom-associativity)},

a\bullet\langle b,c,d\rangle=\langle a\bullet b,a\bullet c,a\bullet d\rangle\quad\text{(Left distributive law)},

\langle a,b,c\rangle\bullet d=\langle a\bullet d,b\bullet d,c\bullet d\rangle\quad\text{(Right distributive law)}.

If
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet,\alpha)
has a unit element
1\in T
satisfying

a\bullet 1=1\bullet a=a,

\alpha(1)=1,

for all
a\in T
, then the Hom-truss of type
(0)
,
T
, is said to be
unitary
.

4.2 Example
.

Let
\left(2\mathbb{Z}+1,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet\right)
be a truss, where the ternary operation is given by

\langle a,b,c\rangle=a-b+c,\quad\text{for all }a,b,c\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1,

and the binary operation
\bullet
is the usual multiplication.
Consider the map
\alpha:2\mathbb{Z}+1\rightarrow 2\mathbb{Z}+1
defined by
\alpha(n)=n
for all
n\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1
.
We now define a new product

n\bullet_{\alpha}m=\alpha(n\bullet m).

It is straightforward to verify that

\left(2\mathbb{Z}+1,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet_{\alpha},\alpha\right)

is a Hom-truss.

4.3 Example
.

Let
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet)
be a truss and
\alpha:T\rightarrow T
be a automorphism of truss. Now we define new binary operation
a\bullet_{\alpha}b=\alpha(a\bullet b)
. Then
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet_{\alpha})
is Hom-truss of type
(0)
.

4.4 Definition
.

A
Hom-truss of type
(1)
is a tuple

(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet,\alpha,\beta)
consisting of a set
T
equipped with a ternary operation

\langle-,-,-\rangle:T\times T\times T\to T,

a binary operation

\bullet:T\times T\to T,

and two bijective unary maps
\alpha,\beta:T\to T
, satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\alpha)
is an abelian Hom-heap.

(2)

\beta
is an endomorphism of the abelian Hom-heap
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\alpha)
, i.e.,

\beta\langle a,b,c\rangle=\langle\beta(a),\beta(b),\beta(c)\rangle

for all
a,b,c\in T
, and
\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha
.

(3)

\alpha
and
\beta
are both multiplicative maps, i.e.,

\alpha(a\bullet b)=\alpha(a)\bullet\alpha(b)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta(a\bullet b)=\beta(a)\bullet\beta(b),

for all
a,b\in T
.

(4)

The map
\beta
and the binary operation
\bullet
satisfy the
Hom-associativity
condition:

\beta(a)\bullet(b\bullet c)=(a\bullet b)\bullet\beta(c),

(2)

for all
a,b,c\in T
.

(5)

The multiplication is
Hom-distributive
over the ternary operation on both sides:

\alpha(a)\bullet\langle b,c,d\rangle=\langle a\bullet b,a\bullet c,a\bullet d\rangle,

(3)

\langle a,b,c\rangle\bullet\alpha(d)=\langle a\bullet d,b\bullet d,c\bullet d\rangle,

(4)

for all
a,b,c,d\in T
.

If
(T,<-,-,->,\bullet,\alpha,\beta)
has a unit element
1\in T

(5)

\alpha(1)=\beta(1)=1,

(6)

for all

T

4.15 Proposition
.

Let
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet)
be a truss, and let
\alpha,\beta:T\to T
be two commuting automorphisms of the truss.
Define a new ternary operation
\langle-,-,-\rangle_{\ast}:T\times T\times T\to T
and a new binary multiplication
\bullet_{\ast}:T\times T\to T
by

\langle a,b,c\rangle_{\ast}=\alpha\langle a,b,c\rangle=\langle\alpha(a),\alpha(b),\alpha(c)\rangle,

and

a\bullet_{\ast}b=\beta(a\bullet b)=\beta(a)\bullet\beta(b),

for all
a,b,c\in T
. Then
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle_{\ast},\bullet_{\ast},\alpha,\beta)
is a Hom-truss of type
(1)
.

Proof.

For all
a,b,c\in T
, we get
<a,a,b>_{\ast}=<\alpha(a),\alpha(a),\alpha(b)>=\alpha(b)
. Similarly, we can prove that
\alpha(b)=<b,a,a>_{\ast}
. Now For all
a,b,c,d,e\in T
, we get

\displaystyle<\alpha(a),\alpha(b),<c,d,e>_{\ast}>_{\ast}

\displaystyle=<\alpha(a),\alpha(b),<\alpha(c),\alpha(d),\alpha(d)>>_{\ast}

\displaystyle=\alpha<\alpha(a),\alpha(b),<\alpha(c),\alpha(d),\alpha(d)>>

\displaystyle=\alpha<<\alpha(a),\alpha(b),\alpha(c)>,\alpha(d),\alpha(d)>

\displaystyle=<<\alpha(a),\alpha(b),\alpha(c)>,\alpha(d),\alpha(d)>_{\ast}

\displaystyle=<<a,b,c>_{\ast},\alpha(d),\alpha(d)>_{\ast}.

Also, we get
\alpha<a,b,c>_{\ast}=\alpha\circ\alpha<a,b,c>=<\alpha(a),\alpha(b),\alpha(c)>_{\ast}
. Therefore,
(T,<-,-,->_{\ast},\alpha)
is an abelian Hom-heap.
Now, we get

\displaystyle\beta<a,b,c>_{\ast}

\displaystyle=\beta\circ\alpha<a,b,c>

\displaystyle=\alpha\circ\beta<a,b,c>

\displaystyle=\alpha<\beta(a),\beta(b),\beta(c)>

\displaystyle=<\beta(a),\beta(b),\beta(c)>_{\ast}.

We also have

\displaystyle\alpha(a\bullet_{\ast}b)=\alpha\circ\beta(a\bullet b)=\beta\circ\alpha(a\bullet b)=\beta(\alpha(a)\bullet\alpha(b))=\alpha(a)\bullet_{\ast}\alpha(b).

\displaystyle\beta(a\bullet_{\ast}b)=\beta\circ\beta(a\bullet b)=\beta(a)\bullet_{\ast}\beta(b).

\displaystyle\beta(a)\bullet_{\ast}(b\bullet_{\ast}c)

\displaystyle=\beta(a)\bullet_{\ast}\beta(a\bullet b)

\displaystyle=\beta(\beta(a)\bullet(\beta(b)\bullet\beta(c)))

\displaystyle=\beta((\beta(a)\bullet\beta(b))\bullet\beta(c))

\displaystyle=\beta\big((a\bullet_{\ast}b)\bullet\beta(c)\big)

\displaystyle=(a\bullet_{\ast}b)\bullet_{\ast}\beta(c).

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\bullet_{\ast}<b,c,d>_{\ast}

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet_{\ast}\alpha<b,c,d>

\displaystyle=\beta(\alpha(a)\bullet\alpha<b,c,d>)

\displaystyle=\beta\circ\alpha(a\bullet<b,c,d>)

\displaystyle=\alpha\circ\beta(<a\bullet b,a\bullet c,a\bullet d>)

\displaystyle=\alpha<\beta(a\bullet b),\beta(a\bullet c),\beta(a\bullet d)>

\displaystyle=\alpha<a\bullet_{\ast}b,a\bullet_{\ast}c,a\bullet_{\ast}d>

\displaystyle=<a\bullet_{\ast}b,a\bullet_{\ast}c,a\bullet_{\ast}d>_{\ast}.

Similarly, we can prove that
<a,b,c>_{\ast}\bullet_{\ast}\alpha(d)=<a\bullet_{\ast}d,b\bullet_{\ast}d,c\bullet_{\ast}d>_{\ast}
. Hence,
(T,<-,-,->_{\ast},\bullet_{\ast},\alpha,\beta)
is a Hom-truss of type
(1)
.
∎

4.16 Proposition
.

Let
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet,\alpha,\beta)
be a Hom-truss of type
(1)
.
Define a new ternary operation
\langle-,-,-\rangle_{\ast}:T\times T\times T\rightarrow T
and a new binary operation
\bullet_{\ast}:T\times T\rightarrow T

\langle a,b,c\rangle_{\ast}=\alpha^{-1}\langle a,b,c\rangle=\langle\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(b),\alpha^{-1}(c)\rangle,

for all

.
Then

Proof.

It is easy to prove that
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle_{\ast})
is an abelian heap and
\bullet_{\ast}
is a binary operation. Only need to verify that truss distributive property. For all a,b,c,d
\in T

\displaystyle a\bullet_{\ast}\langle b,c,d\rangle_{\ast}

\displaystyle=a\bullet_{\ast}\langle\alpha^{-1}(b),\alpha^{-1}(c),\alpha^{-1}(d)\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}(a\bullet\langle\alpha^{-1}(b),\alpha^{-1}(c),\alpha^{-1}(d)\rangle)

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}(\alpha(\alpha^{-1}(a))\bullet\langle\alpha^{-1}(b),\alpha^{-1}(c),\alpha^{-1}(d)\rangle)

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}\langle\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(b),\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c),\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(d)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\beta^{-1}(\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(b)),\beta^{-1}(\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c)),\beta^{-1}(\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(d))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha^{-1}(a\bullet_{\ast}b),\alpha^{-1}(a\bullet_{\ast}c),\alpha^{-1}(a\bullet_{\ast}d)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a\bullet_{\ast}b,a\bullet_{\ast}c,a\bullet_{\ast}d\rangle_{\ast}.

Similarly, we can prove that right distributive law.

5
Modules Over Hom-Trusses

In this section, we define modules over Hom-trusses. In particular, we focus on
\alpha
-Hom-trusses of type
(1)
, since this type of Hom-truss can be used to obtain other types more easily.

5.1 Definition
.

Let
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet,\alpha,\alpha)
be an
\alpha
-Hom-truss of type
(1)
.
A
left
T
-module
is an abelian Hom-heap
(M,\langle-,-,-\rangle_{\ast},\beta)
equipped with a left action map

\Theta:T\times M\to M,\quad\Theta(a,m)=a\rhd m,

such that for all
a,b,c\in T
and
l,m,n\in M
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

(a\bullet b)\rhd\beta(m)=\alpha(a)\rhd(b\rhd m)
.

(ii)

\alpha(a)\rhd\langle l,m,n\rangle_{\ast}=\langle a\rhd l,\,a\rhd m,\,a\rhd n\rangle_{\ast}
.

(iii)

\langle a,b,c\rangle\rhd\beta(n)=\langle a\rhd n,\,b\rhd n,\,c\rhd n\rangle_{\ast}
.

Symmetrically to a left
T
-module, we can define a right
T
-module via a right action

\Theta:M\times T\rightarrow M
, given by
\Theta(m,a)=m\lhd a
, which satisfies rules analogous to those in Definition
5.1
. In general, if the left and right actions coincide, we simply call
M
a bi-
T
-module.

5.2 Example
.

An
\alpha
-Hom-truss of type
(1)
,
(T,<-,-,->,\bullet,\alpha,\alpha)
, is itself a (left, right, or bi-) module with the action given by the binary multiplication
\bullet
. That is, for all
a,b\in T
,

a\rhd b=a\lhd b=a\bullet b.

5.3 Proposition
.

Let
(T,<-,-,->,\bullet,\alpha,\alpha)
be an
\alpha
-Hom-truss of type
(1)
and let
(M,<-,-,->_{\ast},\beta)
be a Hom-heap. If
T
is commutative, then a left
T
-module is the same as a right
T
-module.

Proof.

Let
M
be a left
T
-module, i.e., there exists a map
\Theta:T\times M\rightarrow M
defined by
\Theta(a,m)=a\rhd m
. Now, define another action
\Psi:M\times T\rightarrow M
by
\Psi(m,a)=a\rhd m
. Using the commutativity property of the truss, one can easily verify that
\Psi
satisfies all the left module conditions in Definition
5.1
.
∎

(M,<-,-,->,\beta)

\alpha

(1)
with left action map
\Theta

\Theta(a,N)\subseteq N.

6
Hom-Brace

Now we define Hom-braces of different types.

6.1 Definition
.

A set
B
together with two binary operations
\star
,
\bullet
and a bijective map
\alpha:B\rightarrow B
is called a
Hom-skew left brace of type
(0)
if, for all
a,b,c\in B
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

(B,\star)
is a group.

(ii)

(B,\bullet,\alpha)
is a Hom-group.

(iii)

\alpha(a\star b)=\alpha(a)\star\alpha(b).

(iv)

a\bullet(b\star c)=(a\bullet b)\star a^{\star}\star(a\bullet c),

where
a^{\star}
denotes the inverse of
a
with respect to
\star
.

If
(B,\star)
is an abelian group, then a Hom-skew left brace is simply called a
Hom-left brace
. Similarly, a
Hom-skew right brace
is defined using the right distributive condition.

6.2 Definition
.

A set
B
together with two binary operations
\star
,
\bullet
and a bijective map
\alpha:B\rightarrow B
is called a
Hom-brace of type
(0)
if, for all
a,b,c\in B
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

(B,\star)
is an abelian group.

(ii)

(B,\bullet,\alpha)
is a Hom-group.

(iii)

\alpha(a\star b)=\alpha(a)\star\alpha(b).

(iv)

a\bullet(b\star c)=(a\bullet b)\star a^{\star}\star(a\bullet c),

(b\star c)\bullet a=(b\bullet a)\star a^{\star}\star(c\bullet a),

where
a^{\star}
denotes the inverse of
a
with respect to
\star
.

Now we define Hom-braces over Hom-groups.

6.3 Definition
.

A
Hom-brace of type
(1)
is a tuple
(B,\star,\bullet,\alpha,\beta)
consisting of a set
B
together with two binary operations
\star,\bullet
and two bijective maps
\alpha,\beta:B\rightarrow B
such that:

(i)

(B,\star,\alpha)
is an abelian Hom-group.

(ii)

(B,\bullet,\beta)
is a Hom-group.

(iii)

\beta
is an automorphism of the abelian Hom-group
(B,\star,\alpha)
, i.e.,

\beta(a\star b)=\beta(a)\star\beta(b),\quad\forall\,a,b\in B,

and
\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha
.

(iv)

\alpha
is multiplicative, i.e.,

\alpha(a\bullet b)=\alpha(a)\bullet\alpha(b).

(v)

The binary operation
\bullet
is Hom-distributive over
\star
on both sides:

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\bullet(b\star c)

\displaystyle=(a\bullet b)\star\{a^{\star}\star(a\bullet c)\},

\displaystyle(b\star c)\bullet\alpha(a)

\displaystyle=\{(b\bullet a)\star a^{\star}\}\star(c\bullet a),

where
a^{\star}
denotes the inverse of
a
with respect to
\star
.

6.4 Remark
.

Note that if
\alpha=\mathrm{id}
, this definition reduces to that of a Hom-brace of type
(0)
(see Definition
6.2
).
If
\alpha=\beta
, then a Hom-brace of type
(1)
is referred to as an
\alpha
-Hom-brace of type
(1)
.

Next, we define another type of Hom-brace.

6.5 Definition
.

A
Hom-brace of type
(2)
is a tuple
(B,\star,\bullet,\alpha,\beta)
consisting of a set
B
together with two binary operations
\star,\bullet
and two bijective maps
\alpha,\beta:B\rightarrow B
such that:

(i)

(B,\star,\alpha)
is an abelian Hom-group.

(ii)

(B,\bullet,\beta)
is a Hom-group satisfying the following Hom-associativity condition:

\displaystyle\beta(\alpha^{2}(a))\bullet\big(\beta(\alpha(b))\bullet\beta^{2}(c)\big)=\big(\alpha^{2}(a)\bullet\beta(\alpha(b))\big)\bullet\beta^{2}(\alpha(c)).

(iii)

\beta
is an automorphism of the abelian Hom-group
(B,\star,\alpha)
, i.e.,

\beta(a\star b)=\beta(a)\star\beta(b),\quad\forall\,a,b\in B,

and
\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha
.

(iv)

\alpha
is multiplicative, i.e.,

\alpha(a\bullet b)=\alpha(a)\bullet\alpha(b).

(v)

The binary operation
\bullet
is Hom-distributive over
\star
on both sides:

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(a)\bullet\beta(b\star c)

\displaystyle=(\alpha(a)\bullet\beta(b))\star\{\alpha(a^{\star})\star(\alpha(a)\bullet\beta(c))\},

\displaystyle\alpha(b\star c)\bullet\alpha(\beta(a))

\displaystyle=\{(\alpha(b)\bullet\beta(a))\star\alpha(a^{\star})\}\star(\alpha(c)\bullet\beta(a)),

where
a^{\star}
denotes the inverse of
a
with respect to
\star
.

6.6 Definition
.

Let
(B,\star,\bullet,\alpha,\beta)
be a Hom-brace of type
(2)
. It is said to be an
\alpha
-Hom-brace of type
(2)
if
\alpha=\beta
.

7.1 Definition

7.2 Theorem
.

Let
(T,\langle-,-,-\rangle,\bullet,\alpha)
be a Hom-truss of type
(0)

(1)

If

is unital, then the operations
+_{1}
and
\bullet
satisfy the left and right distributive laws Hom-brace of type

a\bullet(b+_{1}c)=(a\bullet b)-_{1}a+_{1}(a\bullet c),

and

(b+_{1}c)\bullet a=(b\bullet a)-_{1}a+_{1}(c\bullet a).

(2)

If
0
is an absorber of

, then
(T,+_{0},\bullet,\alpha)
forms a Hom-ring (see the definition of Hom-ring).

Proof.

To prove assertion
(1)
, we use the heap axioms, the definition of
+_{1}
, the inverse element of the retract at
1
(as in Remark
2.3
), together with the distributivity and unital properties. We obtain

\displaystyle a\bullet(b+_{1}c)

\displaystyle=a\bullet\langle b,1,c\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a\bullet b,a\bullet 1,a\bullet c\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a\bullet b,a,a\bullet c\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\langle a\bullet b,1,1\rangle,a,\langle 1,1,a\bullet c\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a\bullet b,1,\langle\langle 1,a,1\rangle,1,a\bullet c\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=a\bullet b-_{1}a+_{1}a\bullet c.

The right brace distributive law can be proved in a similar manner.

To prove assertion
(2)
, let
a,b,c\in T
. Using the definition of
+_{0}
, the distributive law of the Hom-truss [
4.1
], and the property

, we have

a\bullet(b+_{0}c)=a\bullet\langle b,0,c\rangle=\langle a\bullet b,a\bullet 0,a\bullet c\rangle=\langle a\bullet b,0,a\bullet c\rangle=(a\bullet b)+_{0}(a\bullet c).

Similarly, the right distributive law can be proved.

7.3 Lemma
.

Let
(B,+,\bullet,\alpha)
be a Hom-brace of type
(0)

(1)

is the identity element with respect to

(2)

a\bullet(-c)=2a-a\bullet c

(3)

To prove assertion

\displaystyle a\bullet 0

\displaystyle=a\bullet(0+0)

\displaystyle=a\bullet 0-a+a\bullet 0

\displaystyle=2(a\bullet 0)-a.

Hence, it follows that
a\bullet 0=a
.

To prove assertion
(2)
, for any

a\bullet 0=a\bullet(c+(-c))=a\bullet c-a+a\bullet(-c).

From assertion

a\bullet(-c)=2a-a\bullet c.

Finally, to prove assertion

\displaystyle a\bullet\langle b,c,d\rangle

\displaystyle=a\bullet\bigl((b-c)+d\bigr)

\displaystyle=a\bullet(b-c)-a+a\bullet d

\displaystyle=a\bullet b-a+a\bullet(-c)-a+a\bullet d

\displaystyle=a\bullet b-a+(2a-a\bullet c)-a+a\bullet d\quad\text{(using (2))}

\displaystyle=a\bullet b-a\bullet c+a\bullet d

\displaystyle=\langle a\bullet b,\;a\bullet c,\;a\bullet d\rangle.

7.6 Lemma

(3)

To prove assertion

, we use the idempotent property and the associativity of the Hom-group. For all

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet 0

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet(0+0)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\alpha^{-2}(a))\bullet(0+0)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-2}(a)\bullet 0+\{-\alpha^{-2}(a)+\alpha^{-2}\bullet 0\}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet 0+\{-\alpha^{-1}(a)+\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet 0\}.

\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet 0=\alpha^{-1}(a)

To prove assertion

(1)

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}(a)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet 0

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\bigl(\alpha^{-1}(c)+(-\alpha^{-1}(c))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\alpha^{-2}(a))\bullet\bigl(\alpha^{-1}(c)+(-\alpha^{-1}(c))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-2}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c)+\{-\alpha^{-2}(a)+(\alpha^{-2}(a)\bullet(-\alpha^{-1}(c)))\}\qquad\text{(by Hom-brace distributivity)}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet\alpha^{-1}(c)+\{-\alpha^{-1}(a)+(\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet(-\alpha^{-1}(c)))\}\qquad\text{(idempotent property)}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}(a\bullet c)+\{-\alpha^{-1}(a)+(\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet(-\alpha^{-1}(c)))\}\qquad\text{($\alpha$ is multiplicative)}.

Thus,

\alpha^{-1}(a)-\alpha^{-1}(a\bullet c)=-\alpha^{-1}(a)+\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet(-\alpha^{-1}(c)).

Rearranging, we get

\alpha^{-1}(a)\bullet(-\alpha^{-1}(c))=\alpha^{-1}(2a)-\alpha^{-1}(a\bullet c).

To prove assertion
(3)
, we use assertions
(1)
and
(2)
together with Hom-associativity. For all
a,b,c,d\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\bullet\langle b,c,d\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha(a)\bullet\{\,b+(-\alpha^{-1}(c)+\alpha^{-1}(d))\,\}

\displaystyle=a\bullet b+\{-a+a\bullet(-\alpha^{-1}(c)+\alpha^{-1}(d))\}

\displaystyle=a\bullet b+\{\alpha^{-1}(-a\bullet c)+\alpha^{-1}(a\bullet d)\}\qquad\text{(by assertion (2), idempotent, and Hom-group associativity)}

\displaystyle=\langle a\bullet b,\,a\bullet c,\,a\bullet d\rangle.

This completes the proof.
∎

in which

Future Research Directions

This paper serves as a foundational contribution to our ongoing research on
the exploration of
Hom-affgebras
, which may be regarded as the affine
counterpart of Hom-algebras. In particular, we are in the process of developing
the theory of
Hom-Lie affgebras
, aiming to extend the notions of
Lie affgebras into the Hom-setting. Alongside this, our investigation also focuses
on the representations of
Hom-trusses
as well as
Hom-affgebras
,
seeking to understand their structural and categorical aspects.

In recent years, Hom-algebraic structures have been studied extensively by many
mathematicians, motivated by their wide range of applications in deformation theory,
quantum groups, and non-associative geometry. Inspired by this line of research,
our goal is to initiate a parallel development of these ideas in the

affine world
, making systematic use of the algebraic frameworks provided by
heaps and trusses. Through this study, we aim not only to build the foundations
of Hom-affine structures but also to explore the similarities and differences
that arise when compared with their classical counterparts. This comparative
approach is expected to shed light on the underlying algebraic phenomena and
reveal new directions for further research.

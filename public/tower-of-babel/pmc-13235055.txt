The impact of carbon emission trading pilot policy on China’s energy eco-efficiency
Authors: Liandi Zhang, Shenglin Ma, Yiyao Dai, Wenguang Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnder the “Dual Carbon” goals (carbon peaking and carbon neutrality), China’s Carbon Emissions Trading System (CETS) represents a key policy tool in addressing climate change, significantly contributing to carbon reduction and the enhancement of Energy Eco-Efficiency (EEE). As a comprehensive measure of coordination within the “energy-economy-environment” system, EEE effectively captures a country’s or region’s ability to harmonize energy consumption, green and sustainable development, and environmental protection. This study computes the EEE index using the SBM model and examines how a carbon emission trading pilot policy (CETPP) affects EEE in China and its spatial spillover by employing a difference-in-differences (DID) model and spatial econometric model. The results indicate that CETPP implementation significantly enhances regional EEE and advances China’s green and low-carbon transition process by improving the balance between economic and environmental goals, increasing employment, diversifying the energy supply, and strengthening economic resilience. Mechanistic research reveals that the CETPP promotes the improvement of EEE by reducing pollution emission intensity and that regional innovation ability can enhance the positive impact of the CETPP on regional EEE. Further analysis revealed significant endogenous spatial interactions in EEE across Chinese regions. However, EEE in the eastern and western regions can create a “siphon” effect on production factors that hinders development in neighbouring areas. Implementing a CETPP in a region not only advances local EEE but also stimulates EEE improvements in adjacent areas, with the strongest spillover effect observed in eastern China. To this end, it is essential to enhance the management of carbon dioxide emissions, actively advance the establishment of carbon trading markets. Moreover, region-specific measures should be implemented to promote the coordinated improvement of regional EEE.Keywords: Carbon emission trading pilot policy, Energy eco-efficiency, Difference-in-differences model, Spatial econometric model

Introduction

Since the Industrial Era, emissions of greenhouse gases (chiefly carbon dioxide) have risen sharply, intensifying the greenhouse effect. Although the growth rate of global carbon emissions has slowed in recent years, carbon dioxide emissions have not yet peaked [1]. According to the World Energy Outlook 2023, global energy demand peaked in 2023 and will decline gradually thereafter [2]. Despite this decline, energy-related greenhouse gas emissions are projected to increase by 30%. In this context, the coordination of carbon reduction with economic and social development through policy measures has emerged as a critical issue that requires urgent attention [3]. The effectiveness of implementing carbon reduction policies not only directly influences the achievement of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) but also significantly impacts the trajectory of the global carbon cycle [4].

As a leading global emitter of greenhouse gases, China faces the dual challenges of aligning economic and social development with the need to control greenhouse gas emissions while promoting sustainable development [5, 6]. In September 2020, at the 75th session of the United Nations General Assembly, President Xi Jinping announced that China aims to peak carbon dioxide emissions before 2030 and to achieve carbon neutrality before 2060. Green development constitutes a defining feature of China’s modernization pathway. It is essential to firmly establish and embrace the notion that green mountains and clear waters hold value equivalent to mountains of gold and silver. Guided by the “Dual Carbon” goals of carbon peaking and carbon neutrality, China has adopted a coordinated strategy that integrates carbon reduction, pollution control, ecological restoration, and economic growth, thereby strengthening ecological security and fostering green development momentum [7, 8]. To this end, China has established its carbon emissions trading market as a central policy instrument for combating climate change and achieving its “Dual Carbon” goals [9, 10]. In fact, China began preparing for a carbon emissions trading market in 2011. The National Development and Reform Commission identified the first batch of six trading pilot areas, including Beijing, Tianjin, Shanghai, Chongqing, Guangdong, Shenzhen, and Hubei. After years of preparation and planning, on July 16, 2021, China’s carbon emission trading market, which specifically targets the power generation industry, officially opened for trading, marking the rollout of the carbon emission trading pilot policy (CETPP).

The carbon market has the potential to improve EEE and optimize industrial and energy structures, ultimately reducing regional carbon emissions [11]. The standardized functioning of the carbon market is crucial for realizing the “Dual Carbon” goals and safeguarding the ecological environment [12]. In striving to meet global climate objectives and advance green sustainable development, EEE encompasses more than just the efficient use of energy; it also embodies various objectives related to economic, social, and environmental sustainability [13–15]. Reducing carbon emissions and improving EEE are central challenges for meeting global climate targets and advancing sustainable development. Nevertheless, existing indicator systems lack a robust framework to rigorously quantify and evaluate the effectiveness of reducing carbon emissions. Traditional indicators emphasize either economic growth or aggregate emission reductions, obscuring the complex interaction between efficiency and equity. As a comprehensive metric, “Energy Eco-Efficiency (EEE)” quantifies the economic or social welfare created per unit of energy consumption and its environmental impact and thus represents the coordinated optimization of “energy-economy-environment” systems. Therefore, enhancing EEE enables higher levels of development with lower resource use and reduced ecological costs [16, 17]. This approach is crucial for alleviating the strain on the carbon cycle and fostering a harmonious integration of the carbon cycle with human development.

Accordingly, this paper adopts a holistic perspective on the coordinated development of energy use, the economy, society, and the environment, and establishes EEE as the core observation indicator. The rationale for selecting EEE is its capacity to integrate the three analytical dimensions of “goals, processes, and results” into a cohesive framework. First, at the goal level, improvements in EEE directly advance the shift of the economic growth model towards resource-saving and environmentally friendly development [18]. Second, at the process level, industrial policies and environmental governance, whether through phasing out outdated production capacity, developing renewable energy, or promoting industrial energy-saving transformation, are directly manifested in improved regional EEE. Third, at the outcome level, spatial disparities in EEE reveal regional inequalities in transformative development. Regions with persistently low EEE and slow improvement are often the key areas facing the greatest transformation pressure and are prone to social conflict. Underlying these patterns are structural constraints, such as a single industrial structure, insufficient technological innovation, and weak institutional safeguards [19]. Therefore, by measuring, decomposing, and analysing the factors that influence EEE, we can identify the stages in which different regions are on the path to achieving balanced and sustainable development of energy, ecology, and environment in China, the specific constraints they face, and the differences in their dynamic mechanisms.

Has the implementation of the CETPP in China’s pilot cities promoted the increase in EEE? In which types of regions can the CETPP play a more significant role? Is there a spatial correlation between the policy’s effect on EEE across regions? This paper investigates the impact of the CETPP on EEE in Chinese regions using a DID model and spatial econometric model. First, EEE is measured and analysed using the Super-SBM model. Second, the DID model is used to study the effect of the CETPP on EEE. In addition, given the existence of spatial correlation between provinces and the difficulty of capturing this spatial effect in the traditional DID methodology, the existence and impact of the spatial econometric model are again investigated.

Our research provides three principal innovations beyond the current literature. (1) In terms of research perspectives, existing studies on EEE focus mostly on traditional factors such as industrial structure, energy consumption structure, and the level of technological development. Empirical work that integrates the CETPP into the analytical framework and systematically evaluates its policy effects, however, remains underexplored. This paper examines the impact of CETPP on EEE, clarifying the link between policy implementation and EEE while offering theoretical support and policy guidance for strengthening the national carbon market and improving regional EEE. (2) In terms of research methodology, the Super-SBM model is employed to measure EEE; this methodology provides a more comprehensive and fundamental depiction of the comprehensive efficiency of the “energy-economy-environment” system. Building on this, an empirical analysis is conducted using the DID model and spatial econometric model. The findings reveal the spatial spillover and differentiated driving effects of the CETPP across regions, offering systematic insights into the comprehensive effects of the policy. (3) In terms of research content, this study provides a detailed analysis of the structural, differential regional, and heterogeneous effects of the CETPP on EEE to elucidate the mechanisms and structural variations through which the CETPP influences EEE. It offers both theoretical and empirical support for advancing China’s “dual carbon” goals and achieving high-quality green development.

Literature review

Energy eco-efficiency

Energy Eco-Efficiency (EEE) refers to the promotion of economic development under the guidance of the green development concept while achieving maximum economic output with minimal energy inputs and small environmental impacts; it is a multifaceted systemic balance that is pursued today and reflects the efficiency of the entire “energy-economy-environment” system.

The construction of EEE indicators involves two main aspects: input and output. For the selection of input indicators, most studies were based on the setting of the Cobb-Douglas production function, where selected labour and capital factors served as input factors, and energy consumption was treated separately as an input factor; that is, certain labour, capital, and energy factors were selected as input indicators, and only the variables being measured differed. Significant variations exist in the selection of output indicators because the research objectives differ. With respect to the selection of desired output indicators, Liu et al. (2023) [20] evaluated EEE with economic development as the ultimate goal and selected GDP as the desired output, while Rashidi and Saen (2015) [21] added some indicators involving public services directly to the desired output. When selecting undesirable output indicators, some scholars chose industrial “three wastes” as indicators of undesirable output [22, 23], and some scholars directly used carbon dioxide emissions as indicators of undesirable environmental output [24].

In the literature, scholars mostly use SFA, DEA, and their improved forms to calculate EEE. Ding et al. (2025) [15] used SFA to measure and evaluate energy efficiency. DEA is a more typical nonparametric method, but the traditional DEA model has some limitations, and Tone (2002) [25] proposed an improved model, the Super-SBM model. On this basis, Tan and Wang (2021) and Ma et al. (2018) [26, 27] analysed the factors affecting EEE and their levels from different perspectives.

Many studies have examined factors that influence EEE from multiple dimensions, including industrial structure, energy consumption structure, and the level of scientific and technological development [28, 29]. However, research from a policy perspective remains scarce, and studies that investigate the impact of the CETPP on EEE are particularly limited. The methods used are primarily the Tobit model, the fixed-effects model, and related approaches [30, 31]. Most studies assume no spatial interdependence and thus ignore spatial spillover effects, which biases empirical results. Because EEE is closely tied to urban policies and interregional development, econometric methods that incorporate spatial factors have increasingly become important for analysing its determinants [32].

Carbon emission trading pilot policy

As a major emitter of greenhouse gases (GHGs) in China’s annual total emissions, carbon trading represents a market-based mechanism and pivotal instrument for addressing climate change mitigation [33, 34]. Since 2013, China has established carbon emission trading pilot programs in seven administrative regions, each featuring distinct levels of economic development and industrial composition, prompting both domestic and international scholars to embark on research regarding this policy. Most of the related studies focus on the macro level, such as carbon emissions, industrial structure, and high-quality development, and the micro level, which is dominated by enterprises.

At the macro level, Dai et al. (2022) [35] reported that the CETPP had a significant and sustained effect on reducing carbon emission intensity in pilot cities. Dong et al. (2022) [36] reported that the CETPP significantly impacted the combined advantages of overall carbon emission reduction and air quality enhancement, but increased carbon emissions in neighbouring cities. Liu et al. (2023) [37] elucidated the spatial heterogeneity characteristics of provincial carbon budgets in China, while Feng et al. (2025) [38] assessed the effects of urban carbon governance policies within the country. Their study indicated that the pilot carbon emission trading policy led to a reduction in total carbon emissions in pilot cities by approximately 1%−5%. However, the influence of carbon market policies on energy efficiency and emissions (EEE) remains to be systematically investigated. At the micro level, Zhang and Wang (2021) [39] concluded that the policy caused enterprises’ investment expenditures to decrease and significantly improved the sustainability performance of pilot enterprises.

In summary, from the perspective of existing evaluation indicators, most of the literature employs single indicators such as carbon emissions or energy consumption to measure policy effects. However, research assessing the CETPP from the aspect of comprehensive efficiency remains insufficient. In terms of effect analysis, existing studies generally discuss economic, emission-reduction, and energy-saving effects separately and fail to systematically examine the synergistic effects of policies on development and emission reduction. From the research perspective, relevant evaluations predominantly focus on the local areas where policies are implemented, while giving limited consideration to spatial spillover effects. This can easily lead to biased conclusions. In terms of the research framework, studies that incorporate the CETPP and regional energy-economy-environment efficiency into the same analytical system while exploring their transmission mechanisms and heterogeneities are lacking.

Theoretical analysis

The CETPP represents a pivotal institutional arrangement for implementing the green development concept and facilitating the achievement of carbon peaking and carbon neutrality targets. As a typical market-oriented environmental regulation instrument, this policy imposes market-based pricing on the negative externalities of carbon emissions generated during production and business operations. Consequently, carbon emission allowances are endowed with well-defined economic attributes and circulation and trading functions, thereby providing a market-driven incentive mechanism for low-carbon transformation. On the one hand, the CETPP establishes a binding environmental regulation framework that requires payment for pollution emissions. This strengthens the low-carbon threshold for industrial access, forcing the exit of high-carbon and backwards production capacities and resolving overcapacity. It guides production factors to aggregate in low-carbon and efficient industries [40]. Consequently, regional EEE is enhanced through industrial structure upgrading. On the other hand, the innovation incentive mechanism formed by this policy can effectively stimulate the motivation of microeconomic entities to engage in green technology research and development. It accelerates the substitution of clean energy and the application of low-carbon technologies [41, 42]. The optimization of resource allocation promotes technological iteration and upgrading at the regional level through industrial linkage effects and technology diffusion effects, thereby further enhancing regional EEE. On this basis, the first research hypothesis of this paper is proposed:

Hypothesis 1

The implementation of a CETPP can improve regional EEE.

The CETPP has transformed the use of carbon emissions from free to paid allocation, directly increasing the cost of using high-carbon energy and imposing rigid constraints on production entities within the region. To address this regulatory pressure, production entities must adjust their production decisions to achieve the carbon emission reduction goals by, on the one hand, optimizing the energy consumption structure, reducing the input scale of high-carbon fossil energy, and increasing the substitution ratio of renewable energy [43]. On the other hand, increasing investment in and application of low-carbon technologies, improving energy conversion efficiency and the greening level of production processes, and reducing carbon emissions per unit of output. The synergistic effect of the above dual decision-making adjustments will directly promote a systematic decline in the intensity of regional pollution emissions [44]. Optimizing the energy structure and improving efficiency enhance the degree of match between energy input and economic output, and reducing carbon emissions alleviates the carrying pressure on the ecological environment, thus forming a positive interaction between energy utilization and ecological protection [45]. On the basis of the above analysis, the second research hypothesis is proposed:

Hypothesis 2

The CETPP enhances regional EEE by reducing the intensity of pollution emissions.

According to absorptive capacity theory, a region’s ability to identify, absorb external resources, and transform them into actual productivity depends on its existing knowledge base and technological level [46]. The innovation capacity of a region serves as a concentrated manifestation of this ability. Regions with high innovation capacity typically boast well-established scientific infrastructures, a wealth of high-quality human capital, vibrant knowledge spillover networks, and efficient capabilities for transforming research findings into practical applications [47]. These advantages enable them to accurately identify the low-carbon technology demands and market opportunities underlying policies [48]. With a reserve of professional talent, they can swiftly absorb advanced external low-carbon knowledge, accelerate technology sharing and collaborative innovation through knowledge networks, and rely on the outcome-transformation system to convert technological principles into energy-saving and carbon-reduction solutions in actual production [49]. This, in turn, amplifies the promoting effect of the CETPP on regional EEE. On this basis, the third research hypothesis is proposed:

Hypothesis 3

Regional innovation capacity can strengthen the positive effect of the CETPP on regional EEE.

Specifically, energy input, labour input, and capital input, including total energy consumption, year-end employment, and total fixed assets investment, are selected as measurement indicators. The expected output considers mainly the economic benefits associated with EEE, that is, the total GDP of each province. Additionally, in this paper, the indicators for unexpected output are selected from two dimensions: environmental pollution and environmental impact. Most of today’s environmental pollution comes from industrial production waste, so the “three wastes” of industry are used as indicators to measure environmental pollution. The environmental impact mainly considers the greenhouse effect caused by the large amount of carbon dioxide emissions during energy consumption, which is measured by carbon dioxide emissions. From the perspective of indicator selection, these metrics integrate the core economic, social, and environmental dimensions and constitute composite economic indicators of sustainable development. High EEE values indicate greater wealth generated with fewer resources and less pollution, providing the material basis for realizing justice in other dimensions (such as ensuring employment quality and funding for environmental protection).

Measurements of EEE

The EEE of all provinces as a whole shows a polarized trend, and to visualize the differences in EEE among regions, Fig. 1 shows the average EEE values of 30 provinces during the period of 2006–2022.

Average EEE values of 30 provinces (cities) for 2006 to 2022

There is a significant gap in EEE values among various provinces in China. Beijing has the highest value among all the regions, at 1.192, while Qinghai has the lowest value, at 0.144. In addition, the average EEE values of Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and Guangdong are exceptionally high and far greater than China’s EEE (0.461). Beijing and Shanghai are driven primarily by low-carbon service sectors, including finance, technology, and information services. In contrast, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and Guangdong have successfully transitioned to high-end manufacturing, the digital economy, and green technologies, characterized by low energy consumption and emission intensity per unit of output. High EEE regions have captured more transformation dividends (such as green employment and investment), whereas low EEE areas (e.g., Qinghai) bear heavier transformation burdens. The high efficiency of these five provincial-level administrative regions reflects both early achievements and a major driving force for the balanced and sustainable development of China’s energy, ecological environment. Moreover, the large gap between them and less-developed regions constitutes a central imbalance issue that the development must address. China’s regional coordinated development therefore requires these five regions not only to sustain their lead but also to convert their advantages into systematic forces that can propel a nationwide improvement. Doing so will narrow regional disparities and increase overall efficiency, thereby realizing genuine coordinated development.

The dynamic trend graph of EEE over time for China as a whole and for East, Central, and West China is shown in Fig. 2; according to the current division method, China’s three macroregions encompass 12, 9, and 9 provincial-level units, respectively. As shown in Fig. 2, the levels of EEE in China’s three macroregions differ greatly; the eastern region is consistently at the highest level from 2006 to 2022, while the EEE values in the central and western regions are lower than the national EEE average. There are significant spatial differences among the sample regions, and to address this significant feature, a spatial econometric model is constructed in Sect. "Extended analyses"" to investigate the underlying influence mechanisms in depth.

Trend of average EEE changes in China and different regions

The EEE level in the eastern region has always been the highest because the coastal provinces in the eastern region have relatively developed economies and a significant portion of the service sector, which has low energy consumption and low pollution emissions. The values of EEE in the central region are lower than the national average within the research range. This is because the central region has a larger population, high energy consumption and labour input, and mainly features coal mining and heavy chemical industry development, which increase pollution emissions. However, the EEE in the central region showed a steady upwards trend after 2008, and the development momentum was good. The EEE values in most years in the western region are at the lowest level, with the fastest growth rate of 45.14% occurring in 2019–2020. This may be due to the Western Development Strategy, which promotes the improvement of resource utilization efficiency and economic development acceleration in the western region. However, the natural resource scarcity and fragile ecological environment in the western region have not changed; thus, the EEE values in the study interval are still far from the national EEE level.

Control variables

Considering other factors that may affect EEE, the control variables in this paper are fiscal expenditure (FE), foreign investment (FC), industrial agglomeration (ind), urbanization level (urb), and R&D intensity (inv).

Among them, fiscal expenditure (FE) is a macroeconomic regulation measure taken by the government to intervene in various aspects, such as economic development, environmental pollution, and carbon reduction. The government can impact EEE through fiscal expenditure by increasing enterprise admission standards and other measures. In this article, the level of fiscal expenditure (FE) is measured as the proportion of local general public budget expenditure to GDP in each province.

Following, Hanif et al. (2019) [55], the ratio of actual foreign investment to GDP is used to measure the degree of foreign investment (FC) in various provinces and autonomous regions. The “pollution shelter” hypothesis suggests that if foreign-funded enterprises exploit the relatively low environmental regulation standards of the host country to relocate high-pollution industries on a large scale, a decrease in EEE is highly likely; in contrast, the successful introduction of green and low-carbon technologies will help improve EEE.

Industrial agglomeration (ind) enables companies in related industries to be geographically concentrated, which helps promote collaborative cooperation among industries in the region, thereby promoting EEE improvement. Moreover, agglomeration contributes to the generation of innovative thinking and is a manifestation of knowledge and technology spillover. Existing research has shown that industrial spatial agglomeration can effectively increase the energy utilization efficiency of enterprises. For example, an empirical study by Qin et al. (2020) [56] revealed that the development of industrial clusters significantly promotes the total factor energy productivity of enterprises.

The urbanization level (urb) has a dual effect on EEE. In recent years, with the acceleration of urbanization throughout the country, economic scale expansion has led not only to more energy consumption but also to a gradually generated economies of scale effect, which in turn has affected the transformation of EEE from quantity to quality accumulation.

R&D intensity (inv) affects EEE by promoting technological innovation and reducing pollution emissions. On this basis, this study includes R&D investment in the control variable system and measures R&D intensity using the proportion of R&D expenditure to regional GDP.

Robustness test

Parallel trend test

A fundamental prerequisite for the DID method is the assumption of parallel trends, which means that without policy rollout, the trends of the two groups are parallel, and there is no notable difference between them. If the parallel trend hypothesis is not valid, the differences between the two groups cannot be attributed to policies or intervention measures, and the results of the DID model may be unreliable. The parallel trend test results are shown in Fig. 3.

Results of the parallel trend test

The regression coefficients of each year before the implementation of the policy in the benchmark year 2013 fluctuated around the 0 axis, indicating that the difference between the two groups before the policy implementation and that the changes in EEE between them followed a parallel trend. Additionally, in the year in which the CETPP was implemented and in the two years thereafter, the estimated coefficient passed a significance test at the 10% level, suggesting that the policy’s influence on EEE showed a significant positive correlation in the short term. The reason can be inferred as follows: the introduction of the CETPP led various regions to focus on green reducing emissions. As the first batch of pilot cities are all economically developed areas with relatively complete industrial structures and a variety of leading industry types, many enterprises joined the carbon market during the pilot period. The operational framework of the carbon market was updated quickly, and carbon emission trading covers many industries. In the short term, the policy effect of the carbon market became increasingly obvious, leading to a rapid increase in EEE. However, the impact on EEE weakened from the second year onwards; although positive fluctuations were observed, the increase was no longer significant. This may be because other cities across the country also began to focus on reducing energy consumption and emissions and advocated for green production development under the influence of this policy. The carbon market in the first batch of pilot cities is becoming increasingly perfect, and the scope of updates is smaller than that of large-scale promotion when the policy was first introduced. On the other hand, with the continuous development of the Chinese economy, the marginal cost of reducing emissions is gradually increasing, which has also slowed the increase in EEE under the CETPP.

Placebo test

A placebo test was conducted to exclude the possibility that other variables not accounted for have the same effect on the EEE assessed in this study [11]. That is, the placebo test was designed to demonstrate that the observed effects stem primarily from the CETPP rather than other unobserved factors. Specifically, 6 regions from 30 research samples were randomly generated as the treatment group, and the benchmark model was used to estimate the coefficients; then, the experiment was repeated 500 times. The experimental findings are presented in Fig. 4.

Results of the placebo test

The vertical red solid line in the figure represents the true coefficients of the benchmark regression results, the horizontal red solid line represents the significance level of 10%, the red curve represents the estimated p-value, and the blue curve represents the density distribution of the estimated coefficients. Drawing from the distribution and importance of the coefficient values depicted in the figure, the following conclusion can be inferred: most of the coefficients are clustered around 0, and the kernel density plot and p-value of the coefficients are mostly on one side of the two red solid lines, indicating that most of the coefficients deviate greatly from the estimated coefficients from real quasi natural experiments and have no statistical significance, suggesting that limited policy efficacy was not affected by random factors and that the CETPP has indeed improved EEE (rather than other factors), passing the placebo test.

Conclusions and recommendations

This study employs panel data from 30 provinces (cities) in China and conducts a series of empirical analyses using the Super-SBM model, DID model, and spatial econometric model. It investigates the impact of the CETPP on EEE while also uncovering the spatial effects among different regions and the heterogeneous characteristics of policy influence across various areas. The main research conclusions are summarized below:

The CETPP has significantly promoted regional EEE. The implementation of the CETPP has notably enhanced regional EEE by reducing pollution emission intensity and improving energy utilization efficiency. This improvement is reflected not only in the balance between economic growth and environmental protection but also in the increase in the employment rate, the diversity of the energy supply, and the resilience of the economic system, thus comprehensively advancing China’s green and low-carbon transition process.

The CETPP has significantly promoted regional EEE. The implementation of the CETPP has notably enhanced regional EEE by reducing pollution emission intensity and improving energy utilization efficiency. This improvement is reflected not only in the balance between economic growth and environmental protection but also in the increase in the employment rate, the diversity of the energy supply, and the resilience of the economic system, thus comprehensively advancing China’s green and low-carbon transition process.

Regional innovation capacity strengthens the positive impact of the CETPP on EEE. The research reveals that regions with greater regional innovation capacity can more effectively absorb and apply low-carbon technologies, thereby strengthening the promoting effect of the CETPP on EEE. These findings indicate that regional innovation capacity plays a crucial moderating role in the promoting effect of CETPP on EEE.

The CETPP has a significant spatial spillover effect, which promotes EEE in neighbouring regions. The implementation of the CETPP not only promotes EEE in the local region but also drives EEE in neighbouring regions through the spatial spillover effect. This effect is particularly evident in the eastern region, indicating that the coordinated action of the policy across regions is highly important for achieving overall improvement of EEE nationwide.

Drawing on the research conclusions, this paper provides the following recommendations:

Pilot projects should be strengthened and expanded. The top-level institutional design should be improved, the scope of pilots and the range of industry sectors should be broadened, and the data foundation for carbon emissions trading should be consolidated. The allocation and management of carbon allowances should be optimized, market vitality and financial functionality should be enhanced, and the role of pilot programs in advancing national integration should be clarified.

The full utilization of regional innovation capacity can strengthen the positive impact of the CETPP on regional EEE. The research and development of breakthrough carbon-reduction technologies such as wind power and photovoltaics should be intensified, green upgrading technologies for traditional industries should be advanced, and negative-carbon and ecological-restoration technologies should be developed while increasing investment in green and low-carbon initiatives.

In light of regional heterogeneity, tailored strategies to improve regional EEE based on local conditions should be developed. In traditional energy regions (for example, some cities in Shanxi, Heilongjiang, and northern Shaanxi), industrial renewal and diversification should be prioritized, livelihoods secured, and ecosystems restored; in traditional manufacturing bases (for example, the older industrial districts in the Yangtze River Delta and the Pearl River Delta), green upgrading across industrial chains should be promoted and carbon-reduction efforts coordinated; in regions rich in renewable resources (for example, the wind and solar hubs in northwest and north China and hydropower areas in the southwest), local value retention should be balanced with industrial extension; and in ecologically sensitive and agricultural regions (for example, the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, the upper reaches of the Yangtze and Yellow Rivers, and key agricultural zones), realizing the value of ecological products and strengthening climate resilience should be the focus; and in megacities and urban agglomerations (for example, core cities in the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, the Yangtze River Delta, and the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area), systematic emission reductions should be implemented, innovation spillovers fostered, and regional coordination enhanced.

The purpose of this paper was to take a step towards understanding the effect of the CETPP on EEE. However, further research is needed. For example, aspects of interest include those where (1) refining the sample data to the municipal level is considered to provide a more refined perspective for the comprehensive study of the impact of the CETPP on EEE and (2) incorporating quantitative indicators such as regional historical and geographical factors is considered to assess the equity of the transformation process, thereby more comprehensively accounting for the complexity of EEE in the research.

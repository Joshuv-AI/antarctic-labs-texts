On the topological ranks of Banach ∗ -algebras associated with groups of subexponential growth
Felipe I. Flores

Abstract
Let G G be a group of subexponential growth and 𝒞 ​ → 𝑞 ​ G \mathscr{C}\overset{q}{\to}G a Fell bundle. We show that any Banach ∗ -algebra that sits between the associated ℓ 1 \ell^{1} -algebra ℓ 1 ​ ( G | 𝒞 ) \ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C}) and its C ∗ C^{*} -envelope has the same topological stable rank and real rank as ℓ 1 ​ ( G | 𝒞 ) \ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C}) . We apply this result to compute the topological stable rank and real rank of various classes of symmetrized twisted L p L^{p} -crossed products and show that some twisted L p L^{p} -crossed products have topological stable rank 1. Our results are new even in the case of (untwisted) group algebras.

Introduction

The (left) topological stable rank of a unital Banach algebra
\mathfrak{B}
was introduced by Rieffel
[
32
]
as the smallest
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that

{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})=\left\{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}\mid\mathfrak{B}=\mathfrak{B}b_{1}+\ldots+\mathfrak{B}b_{n}\right\}

is dense in
\mathfrak{B}^{n}
. The idea was to produce a noncommutative notion of dimension similar to both the covering dimension for compact spaces and the Bass stable rank. This idea was then applied very successfully to the study of K-theory for
C^{*}
-algebras
[
33
]
and motivated the notion of real rank. Indeed, the real rank of a unital
C^{*}
-algebra

, as introduced by Brown and Pedersen

{\rm Lg}_{n+1}(\mathfrak{B})_{\rm sa}=\left\{(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathfrak{B}_{\rm sa}^{n+1}\mid\mathfrak{B}=\mathfrak{B}b_{0}+\ldots+\mathfrak{B}b_{n}\right\}

is dense in
\mathfrak{B}_{\rm sa}^{n+1}
.

Studying these noncommutative notions of dimension has become a very interesting problem for

Now let us present the main theorem. Given a Fell bundle

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

\mathscr{C}\overset{q}{\to}G

\mathfrak{B}
is a Banach

{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})={\rm tsr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad{\rm rr}(\mathfrak{B})={\rm rr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big).

Corollary 1.3

(Corollary
2.11
)

Let

In particular, if

{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})=1.

Let

for all

{\rm tsr}\big(C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\omega}G\big)=1
, then one also has

{\rm tsr}\big(F^{p}(G,X,\alpha,\omega)\big)=1,\quad\text{ for all }p\in[1,\infty].

In particular, we have the following result. It fully settles Question
1.1

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\alpha
be a minimal action of
\mathbb{Z}
either on the Cantor set
X=2^{\mathbb{N}}
or on
X=\mathbb{T}
by irrational rotations. Then
F^{p}(\mathbb{Z},X,\alpha)
has topological stable rank
1
, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

In fact, thanks to the generality of our setting, many other topological ranks are computed in Theorem

The reader should note that groups of subexponential growth are necessarily amenable; hence, we do not know what the situation is for non-amenable groups. More concretely, we do not know the topological stable rank or the real rank of

2
\ell^{1}
-algebras of Fell bundles

From now on,
G
will denote a (discrete) amenable group with unit
{\sf e}
. We will also fix a Fell bundle
\mathscr{C}\!=\bigsqcup_{x\in G}\mathfrak{C}_{x}
over
G
. That is, the
\mathfrak{C}_{x}
’s are a family of Banach spaces equipped with bilinear, associative multiplication maps
\bullet:\mathfrak{C}_{x}\times\mathfrak{C}_{y}\to\mathfrak{C}_{xy}
and conjugate-linear, anti-multiplicative involutions
{}^{\bullet}:\mathfrak{C}_{x}\to\mathfrak{C}_{x^{-1}}
satisfying natural properties that turn the unit fiber
\mathfrak{C}_{\sf e}
into a
C^{*}
-algebra, and each
\mathfrak{C}_{x}
into a Hilbert
\mathfrak{C}_{\sf e}
-bimodule (see
[
17
]
).

The associated algebra of summable cross-sections
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
is a Banach
∗
-algebra and a completion of the space
C_{\rm c}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
of cross-sections with finite support under the norm

\lVert\Phi\rVert_{1}=\sum_{x\in G}\lVert\Phi(x)\rVert_{\mathfrak{C}_{x}}.

The universal
C^{*}
-algebra completion of
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
is denoted by
{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
, and the associated universal
C^{*}
-norm (i.e., the norm in
{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
) is denoted
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{*}
. We recall that the product on
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
is given by

\big(\Phi*\Psi\big)(x)=\sum_{y\in G}\Phi(y)\bullet\Psi(y^{-1}x)

and the involution is given by

\Phi^{*}(x)=\Phi(x^{-1})^{\bullet}\,,

in terms of the operations
\big(\bullet,^{\bullet}\big)
on the Fell bundle. Note that the adjoint operation
\Phi\mapsto\Phi^{*}
is isometric.

We will also consider the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm

\lVert\Phi\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in G}\lVert\Phi(x)\rVert_{\mathfrak{C}_{x}},

primarily because
\lVert\Phi\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert\Phi\rVert_{*}
, for all
\Phi\in{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})

[
18
, Lemma 3.4]
.

One of the main classes of examples of Fell bundles is presented next. It is the class of Fell bundles coming from twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical systems.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. A (continuous) twisted action of
G
on
\mathfrak{A}
is a pair
(\alpha,\omega)
of maps
\alpha:G\to{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
and
\omega:G\times G\to\mathcal{UM}({\mathfrak{A}})
, such that
\alpha
is strongly continuous,
\omega
is jointly strictly continuous, and

(i)

\alpha_{x}(\omega(y,z))\omega(x,yz)=\omega(x,y)\omega(xy,z)
,

(ii)

\alpha_{x}\big(\alpha_{y}(a)\big)\omega(x,y)=\omega(x,y)\alpha_{xy}(a)
,

(iii)

\omega(x,{\sf e})=\omega({\sf e},y)=1,\alpha_{\sf e}={\rm id}_{{\mathfrak{A}}}
,

for all
x,y,z\in G
and
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

The quadruple
(G,\mathfrak{A},\alpha,\omega)
is called a
twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system
. Given such a twisted action, one usually forms the so-called
twisted convolution algebra

\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,\mathfrak{A})
, consisting of all summable sequences
\Phi:G\to\mathfrak{A}
, endowed with the product

\Phi*\Psi(x)=\sum_{y\in G}\Phi(y)\alpha_{y}[\Psi(y^{-1}x)]\omega(y,y^{-1}x)

and the involution

\Phi^{*}(x)=\omega(x,x^{-1})^{*}\alpha_{x}[\Phi(x^{-1})^{*}],

making
\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,\mathfrak{A})
a Banach
∗
-algebra under the norm
\lVert\Phi\rVert_{1}=\sum_{x\in G}\lVert\Phi(x)\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}
. When the twist is trivial (
\omega\equiv 1
), we omit any mention of it and call the resulting algebra
\ell^{1}_{\alpha}(G,\mathfrak{A})
the
convolution algebra
associated with the action
\alpha
. In this case, the triple
(G,\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
is called an (untwisted)
C^{*}
-dynamical system
. The associated
C^{*}
-envelope is usually denoted
\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha,\omega}G
or
\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
in the untwisted case.

It is well known that these algebras correspond to our algebras
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
for very particular Fell bundles. In fact, these bundles may be easily described as
\mathscr{C}=\mathfrak{A}\times G
, with the quotient map
q(a,x)=x
, constant norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathfrak{C}_{x}}=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}
, and operations

(a,x)\bullet(b,y)=(a\alpha_{x}(b)\omega(x,y),xy)\quad\textup{and}\quad(a,x)^{\bullet}=(\alpha_{x}^{-1}(a^{*})\omega(x^{-1},x)^{*},x^{-1}).

In what follows,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}
will denote the minimal unitization of
\mathfrak{B}
. If
\mathfrak{B}
is already unital, we simply set
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}=\mathfrak{B}
. We note that the minimal unitization of
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
corresponds to the
\ell^{1}
-algebra of the ‘unitization Fell bundle’
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}\to G
, which is constructed from
\mathscr{C}
simply by replacing
\mathfrak{C}_{\sf e}
with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{C}_{\sf e}}
and keeping all the other fibers intact.

In order to prove our main result, we first need to recall some notions of multipliers. In particular, the following is a well-known characterization of amenability that can be found in
[
10
, Theorem 2.6.8]
. It uses the notion of positive definite functions, so we will recall that first.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group. A function
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be positive definite if, for every finite subset
K\subset G
, the matrix

[\varphi(x^{-1}y)]_{x,y\in K}\in\mathbb{M}_{|K|}(\mathbb{C})

is positive definite.

Lemma 2.3
.

A group
G
is amenable if and only if there exists a net
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of finitely supported positive definite functions on
G
such that
\varphi_{j}\to 1
pointwise and
\varphi_{j}({\sf e})\leq 1
, for all
j\in J
.

Furthermore, Bédos and Conti
[
6
, Example 4.4]
showed that a positive definite function
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
induces a completely positive map

T_{\varphi}:{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})\to{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C}),\quad\text{ given by }\quad T_{\varphi}(\Phi)(x)=\varphi(x)\Phi(x).

In fact, if
\varphi_{j}\to 1
pointwise and
\varphi_{j}({\sf e})\leq 1
(as in the previous lemma), then
\sup_{x\in G}|\varphi(x)|\leq 1

[
6
, Corollary 3.7]
. Moreover, one has
\lVert T_{\varphi_{j}}\Phi\rVert_{*}\leq\lVert\Phi\rVert_{*}
and
T_{\varphi_{j}}\Phi\to\Phi
, for all
\Phi\in C_{\rm c}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})

[
6
, Theorem 3.11]
.

The other ingredient we need to introduce comes from spectral theory. Hence, as usual, we will denote the spectrum of an element
b\in\mathfrak{B}
by

{\rm Spec}_{\mathfrak{B}}(b)=\{t\in\mathbb{C}\mid b-t1\textup{ is not invertible in }\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}\}.

We also recall that a group is said to have subexponential growth if, for all finite subsets
K\subset G
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}|K^{n}|^{1/n}=1.

All nilpotent groups have subexponential growth; thus, they serve as examples. In fact, all of our concrete applications make use of nilpotent groups (see Section
3
).

Theorem 2.4

(
[
18
]
)
.

Suppose that
G
has subexponential growth. Then

{\rm Spec}_{\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})}(\Phi)={\rm Spec}_{{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})}(\Phi),\quad\text{ for all }\Phi\in C_{\rm c}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C}).

Before moving to the next section, we remark that all the Banach
∗
-algebras mentioned in this section have a continuous (actually, isometric) involution. In what follows, the algebras that admit a continuous involution will play a central role.

2.1 Topological stable rank

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a unital Banach algebra and
n\geq 1
. We set

\displaystyle{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B}):=\left\{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathfrak{B}^{n}\mid\text{the left ideal generated by $b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}$ coincides with $\mathfrak{B}$}\right\}.

The abbreviation ‘Lg’ stands for ‘left generators’. If
\mathfrak{B}
has an involution, we also use
{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})_{\rm sa}:={\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})\cap\mathfrak{B}^{n}_{\rm sa},
for the set of self-adjoint left generators. We now give the definition of (left) topological stable rank, as given by Rieffel in
[
32
, Definition 1.4]
.

Definition 2.5
.

The
(left) topological stable rank
of a unital Banach algebra
\mathfrak{B}
is the smallest
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with the property that
{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})
is dense in
\mathfrak{B}^{n}
under the product topology. In such a case, we write
{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})=n
. On the other hand, we say that
{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})=\infty
if no such
n
exists.

Rieffel observed that we get the same notion if we decide to work with right ideals on a Banach
∗
-algebra, at least when the involution is continuous
[
32
, Proposition 1.6]
. All the results we will prove here also hold for the right-sided topological stable rank, either by virtue of this observation or by analogy. In any case, let us now introduce a characterization for the tuples in
{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})
that is easier to work with. It is well known to experts, but we include it here for completeness (and to highlight the fact that it uses
C^{*}
-algebraic techniques). In fact, it is sometimes taken as the definition of
{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})
(cf.
[
37
, pgs. 16-17]
).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. Then
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}
is invertible.

Proof.

If
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}
is invertible, then the left ideal generated by
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
contains an invertible element, and so it coincides with
\mathfrak{B}
. On the other hand, and for the sake of contradiction, suppose that
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
generates
\mathfrak{B}
as a left ideal, but that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}
is not invertible.

Since
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
generates
\mathfrak{B}
, we can find
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}
such that

1=a_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}b_{n}.

Now let
\pi:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a unital faithful
∗
-representation. Then

0\in{\rm Spec}_{\mathfrak{B}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}\Big)={\rm Spec}_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi(b_{i}^{*}b_{i})\Big),

so
S=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi(b_{i}^{*}b_{i})
is a positive non-invertible operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Since
S
is not bounded below, there exists a unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\langle S\xi,\xi\rangle<\frac{1}{M^{2}n^{2}},\quad\text{ where }M=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\lVert\pi(a_{i})\rVert_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})}.

But then, for every
1\leq i\leq n
, we have

\lVert\pi(b_{i})\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\langle\pi(b_{i}^{*}b_{i})\xi,\xi\rangle<\frac{1}{M^{2}n^{2}}

(2.1)

and

1=\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}=\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi(a_{i}b_{i})\xi\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\pi(a_{i})\|_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})}\|\pi(b_{i})\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\pi(b_{i})\xi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\overset{\eqref{Delta}}{<}1,

which is indeed a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be a dense, continuous unital inclusion of Banach algebras. Then
{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})\leq{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{A}).

Proof.

Let
{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{A})=n<\infty
, let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
b_{i}\in\mathfrak{B}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
. Because the inclusion of
\mathfrak{A}
in
\mathfrak{B}
is dense, we can find elements
a^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}
and
1\leq i\leq n
such that
\lVert a^{\prime}_{i}-b_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}<\varepsilon/2
. Now use the definition of
{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{A})=n
and the continuity of
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
to choose elements
a_{i},c_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, such that
\lVert a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}<\varepsilon/2
and

1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}a_{i}.

But then the left ideal generated by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
in
\mathfrak{B}
contains
1
, so
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})
. Since

\lVert a_{i}-b_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}\leq\lVert a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}+\lVert a^{\prime}_{i}-b_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}<\varepsilon,

we see that
{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})
is dense in
\mathfrak{B}^{n}
, so
{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})\leq n
.
∎

We now prove the main theorem of the section. The underlying idea is to use the characterization of
{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})
in terms of invertibility and then appeal to Theorem
2.4
to ensure that the corresponding inverses remain inside
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})
. Lemma
2.3
then provides us with a way of upgrading the convergence in
{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})
to convergence in
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Suppose that
G
has subexponential growth. Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra such that
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\subset\mathfrak{B}\subset{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})
. Then
{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})={\rm tsr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big).

Proof.

It is enough to prove the unital case, as passing to the unitization still gives a Fell bundle algebra that satisfies
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\widetilde{\mathscr{C}})\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}\subset{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\widetilde{\mathscr{C}})
. Applying Lemma
2.7
, we have

{\rm tsr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)\leq{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})\leq{\rm tsr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big).

So we only need to prove that
{\rm tsr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)\geq{\rm tsr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)
.

Indeed, assume that
{\rm tsr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)=n<\infty
, let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{n}\in C_{\rm c}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
with
M=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\lVert\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}
. Appealing to Lemma
2.3
and the subsequent discussion regarding the net
(T_{{\varphi_{j}}})_{j}
, we can find a finitely supported positive definite function
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\lVert\Phi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n.

(2.2)

Now, we use the definition of topological stable rank to find
\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{n}\in{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})
that generates
{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})
as a left ideal such that

\lVert\Psi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\rVert_{*}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4|{\rm Supp}(\varphi)|},\quad\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n.

(2.3)

Because of Proposition
2.6
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Psi_{i}^{*}*\Psi_{i}
is invertible in
{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
. We now show that
T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}
approximates
\Phi_{i}
in the
\ell^{1}
-norm.

\displaystyle\lVert T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}+\lVert\Phi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{ineq1}}{\leq}\lVert\Psi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\rVert_{\infty}|{\rm Supp}(\varphi)|+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Psi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\rVert_{*}|{\rm Supp}(\varphi)|+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{ineq2}}{<}\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon.

In particular, using the inequality in (
2.3
) again, we see that

\|T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\|_{1}\leq\|\Phi_{i}\|_{1}+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\Psi_{i}\|_{*}\leq\|\Phi_{i}\|_{*}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

(2.4)

We also note that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Psi_{i}^{*}*\Psi_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i})^{*}*T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\Big\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert\Psi_{i}^{*}*\Psi_{i}-(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i})^{*}*T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert\Psi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*}(\lVert\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*}+\lVert T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{inter}}{\leq}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert\Psi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*}(\lVert\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}+\lVert T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{inter}}{\leq}(2\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\lVert\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}+\varepsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert\Psi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*},

where the norms inside the sum can be estimated as

\displaystyle\lVert\Psi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Psi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\rVert_{*}+\lVert\Phi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Phi_{i}\rVert_{*}+\lVert T_{\varphi}\Phi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\rVert_{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert\Psi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\rVert_{*}+\lVert\Phi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{ineq1}+\eqref{ineq2}}{<}\varepsilon.

Hence, returning to the main line of computation, we see that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Psi_{i}^{*}*\Psi_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i})^{*}*T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\Big\|_{*}\leq n\varepsilon(2M+\varepsilon).

So, if
\varepsilon
is small enough,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i})^{*}*T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}
is close to
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Psi_{i}^{*}*\Psi_{i}
, so it has to be invertible. But
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i})^{*}*T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}\in C_{\rm c}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
and, by applying Theorem
2.4
, we see that it is invertible in
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
. Reasoning as before, we conclude that
T_{\varphi}\Psi_{1},\ldots,T_{\varphi}\Psi_{n}
generates
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
as a left ideal. Hence,
(\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{n})
belongs to the closure of
{\rm Lg}_{n}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})\big)
, and this proves that
{\rm tsr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})\big)\leq n
.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Suppose that
G
has subexponential growth. Then
{\rm tsr}(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)={\rm tsr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big).

Simply specializing to the twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system case (i.e. the setting of Example
2.1
) allows us to write the following corollary.

Corollary 2.10
.

Suppose that
G
has subexponential growth and let
(G,\mathfrak{A},\alpha,\omega)
be a twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system. Then
{\rm tsr}(\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,\mathfrak{A})\big)={\rm tsr}\big(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha,\omega}G\big).

Finally, in the case that
\mathfrak{B}
lacks an involution and is only assumed to be a Banach algebra, we can achieve the desired equality of stable ranks only in the case of topological stable rank 1.

Corollary 2.11
.

Suppose that
G
has subexponential growth and let
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\subset\mathfrak{B}
be any continuous, dense inclusion into a Banach algebra
\mathfrak{B}
. Then

{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})\leq{\rm tsr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big).

In particular, if
{\rm tsr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)=1
, then
{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})=1.

Proof.

Combining Theorem
2.8
with Lemma
2.7
yields

{\rm tsr}(\mathfrak{B})\leq{\rm tsr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)={\rm tsr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big),

which proves the claim.
∎

2.2 Real rank

We now proceed to our study of the real rank. The reader will notice that the similarity of definitions allows our line of argumentation to remain essentially the same, provided that we take care of self-adjointness and the corresponding shift in indices.

Definition 2.12
.

The
real rank
of a unital Banach
∗
-algebra
\mathfrak{B}
is the smallest
n\in\mathbb{N}
with the property that
{\rm Lg}_{n+1}(\mathfrak{B})_{\rm sa}
is dense in
\mathfrak{B}^{n+1}_{\rm sa}
under the product topology. In such a case, we write
{\rm rr}(\mathfrak{B})=n
. On the other hand, we say that
{\rm rr}(\mathfrak{B})=\infty
if no such
n
exists.

As in the case of the topological stable rank, we can provide a more manageable characterization, at least for
C^{*}
-algebras.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. Then
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in{\rm Lg}_{n}(\mathfrak{B})_{\rm sa}
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{2}
is invertible and
b_{i}=b_{i}^{*}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Immediate application of Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be a dense, continuous unital inclusion of Banach
∗
-algebras. If
\mathfrak{A}
has a continuous involution, then
{\rm rr}(\mathfrak{B})\leq{\rm rr}(\mathfrak{A}).

Proof.

The proof is the same as the proof of Lemma
2.7
, but we include it here. Let
{\rm rr}(\mathfrak{A})=n<\infty
, let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
b_{i}\in\mathfrak{B}_{\rm sa}
for
0\leq i\leq n
.

Because the inclusion of
\mathfrak{A}
in
\mathfrak{B}
is dense, we can find self-adjoint elements
a^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}
,
0\leq i\leq n
, such that
\lVert a^{\prime}_{i}-b_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}<\varepsilon/2
(if needed, replace
a^{\prime}_{i}
with
\tfrac{1}{2}a^{\prime}_{i}+\tfrac{1}{2}(a^{\prime}_{i})^{*}
). Now, we proceed as before and use the definition of
{\rm rr}(\mathfrak{A})=n
and the continuity of
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{B}
to choose elements
a_{i},c_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm sa}
,
0\leq i\leq n
, such that
\lVert a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}<\varepsilon/2
and

1=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}a_{i}.

But then the left ideal generated by
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
coincides with
\mathfrak{B}
, so
(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\rm Lg}_{n+1}(\mathfrak{B})_{\rm sa}
. Since

\lVert a_{i}-b_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}\leq\lVert a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}+\lVert a^{\prime}_{i}-b_{i}\rVert_{\mathfrak{B}}<\varepsilon,

we get that
{\rm Lg}_{n+1}(\mathfrak{B})_{\rm sa}
is dense in
\mathfrak{B}^{n+1}
.
∎

We now prove the real rank analog of Theorem
2.8
. The proof follows the same lines.

Theorem 2.15
.

Suppose that
G
has subexponential growth. Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach
∗
-algebra with a continuous involution and such that
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\subset\mathfrak{B}\subset{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})
. Then
{\rm rr}(\mathfrak{B})={\rm rr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big).

Proof.

As before, it is enough to prove the unital case, and because of Lemma
2.14
, we only need to prove that
{\rm rr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)\geq{\rm rr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)
.

Suppose that
{\rm rr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)=n<\infty
. We let
\varepsilon>0
and
\Phi_{0},\ldots,\Phi_{n}\in C_{\rm c}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})_{\rm sa}
with
M=\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\lVert\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}
. Appealing to Lemma
2.3
and the subsequent discussion regarding the net
(T_{{\varphi_{j}}})_{j}
, we can find a finitely supported positive definite function
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\lVert\Phi_{i}-T_{\varphi}\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq n.

Now we use the definition of real rank to find
\Psi_{0},\ldots,\Psi_{n}\in{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})_{\rm sa}
that generate
{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})
as a left ideal and such that

\lVert\Psi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\rVert_{*}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4|{\rm Supp}(\varphi)|},\quad\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq n.

By Lemma
2.13
,
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Psi_{i}^{2}
is invertible in
{\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
. Furthermore, by the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\rVert_{1}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Psi_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=0}^{n}(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i})^{2}\Big\|_{*}\leq\varepsilon(n+1)(2M+\varepsilon).

For small
\varepsilon
,
\sum_{i=0}^{n}(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i})^{2}
will be close to
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Psi_{i}^{2}
, so it will need to be invertible. But
\sum_{i=0}^{n}(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{i})^{2}\in C_{\rm c}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
, again by Theorem
2.4
, the inverse lies in
\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})
. Because
\varphi
is positive definite, this implies
(T_{\varphi}\Psi_{0},\ldots,T_{\varphi}\Psi_{n})\in{\rm Lg}_{n+1}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})\big)_{\rm sa}
, and thus
{\rm rr}\big(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,\mathscr{C})\big)\leq n
.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Suppose that
G
has subexponential growth. Then
{\rm rr}(\ell^{1}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big)={\rm rr}\big({\rm C^{*}}(G\,|\,{\mathscr{C}})\big).

To finish the section, we specialize the last corollary to the twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system setting.

Corollary 2.17
.

Suppose that
G
has subexponential growth and let
(G,\mathfrak{A},\alpha,\omega)
be a twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system. Then
{\rm rr}(\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,\mathfrak{A})\big)={\rm rr}\big(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha,\omega}G\big).

3
Applications to symmetrized twisted
L^{p}
-crossed products

As mentioned before, the purpose of this section is to apply the previous results to the case of symmetrized
L^{p}
-crossed products. For that purpose, we will fix a twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha,\omega)
, where
G
is a (discrete) group of subexponential growth,
X
a Hausdorff locally compact space,
\alpha
an action of
G
on
X
, and
\omega:G\times G\to\mathcal{U}(C_{b}(X))
a
2
-cocycle. Here, we abuse the notation and call
\alpha
both the action on
X
and the induced action on
C_{0}(X)
. That is

\alpha_{x}(f)(z)=f\big(\alpha_{x^{-1}}(z)),

where
x\in G,z\in X,f\in C_{0}(X)
.

It is well known that if
\mathfrak{B}
has a contractive approximate unit and
\pi:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathbb{B}({\mathscr{E}})
is a non-degenerate, contractive representation on a Banach space
{\mathscr{E}}
, then
\pi
extends uniquely to a (unital) representation
\pi:\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{B})\to\mathbb{B}({\mathscr{E}})
of the multiplier algebra of
\mathfrak{B}
. This is always the case for
\mathfrak{B}=C_{0}(X)
, and it is relevant since our twisting
2
-cocycles are
\mathcal{M}(C_{0}(X))=C_{b}(X)
-valued. Note that the representation
\pi:\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{B})\to\mathbb{B}({\mathscr{E}})
remains contractive
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty],
and let
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha,\omega)
be a twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system as described above. Let
(Y,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a measure space. A
covariant representation
of
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha,\omega)
on
L^{p}(Y,\mu)
is a pair
(v,\pi)
consisting of a map
g\mapsto v_{g}
from
G
to the invertible isometries on
L^{p}(Y,\mu)
and a non-degenerate, contractive representation
\pi:C_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(Y,\mu)),
such that

v_{x}v_{y}={\pi}(\omega(x,y))v_{xy},\qquad v_{x}\pi(f)v_{x}^{-1}=\pi(\alpha_{x}(f)),

for all
x,y\in G
and
f\in C_{0}(X)
.

Given a covariant representation
(v,\pi)
on
L^{p}(Y,\mu)
, we define the associated
integrated
representation

\pi\rtimes v:\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X))\to\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(Y,\mu))
by

(\pi\rtimes v)(\Phi)=\sum_{x\in G}\pi(\Phi(x))v_{x},

for all
\Phi\in\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X))
.

With these definitions at hand, we can introduce the following norm on
\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X))
:

\|\Phi\|_{p,{\rm max}}=\sup\{\|(\pi\rtimes v)(\Phi)\|\mid(v,\pi)\text{ is a covariant representation on some space }L^{p}(Y,\mu)\}.

The completion
F^{p}(G,X,\alpha,\omega):=\overline{\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X))}^{\|\cdot\|_{p,{\rm max}}}
is usually called the full
L^{p}
-crossed product associated with
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha,\omega)
. It is easily seen to be a Banach algebra
1
1

1

[
5
,
13
]
also consider other, more general, completions of the same kind. That is, completing
\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X))
with respect to certain classes of representations. However, their general definitions only yield seminorms instead of norms. Here, the special feature of the
L^{p}
-case is the existence of regular representations, which are injective.
. In the case where the action is trivial, we set
F^{p}(G,\omega):=F^{p}(G,\{\rm pt\},1,\omega)
. If the cocycle is also trivial, then we set
F^{p}(G):=F^{p}(G,\{\rm pt\},1,1)
. These algebras are often called the twisted group
L^{p}

L^{p}

These algebras have appeared in full generality in
[

and in

It is observed in

\pi(f)\xi(x,t)=f(x)\xi(x,t),\quad v_{y}\xi(x,t)=\omega(y,y^{-1}t)(x)\xi(\alpha_{y^{-1}}(x),y^{-1}t),

valid for
f\in C_{0}(X)
,

, and
\xi\in\ell^{p}(X\times G)

\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X))
on
\ell^{p}(X\times G)
. Its integrated form satisfies the formula (note the special notation)

\Lambda_{p}(\Phi)\xi(x,t)=\sum_{y\in G}\Phi(y)(x)\omega(y,y^{-1}t)(x)\xi(\alpha_{y^{-1}}(x),y^{-1}t).

Using this representation, one may define the reduced twisted crossed product as the completion
\overline{\Lambda_{p}\big(\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X))\big)}\subset\mathbb{B}(\ell^{p}(X\times G))

In any case, the representation
\Lambda_{p}
allows us to introduce a ‘symmetrized’ version of the
L^{p}

[

\lVert\Phi\rVert_{q,*}=\max\{\|\Lambda_{p}(\Phi)\|,\|\Lambda_{q}(\Phi)\|\},

where

F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha,\omega)

F^{q}_{*}(G,\omega):=F^{q}_{*}(G,\{\rm pt\},1,\omega)

.

The point is that
F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha,\omega)
is naturally a Banach
∗

\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X))

\Phi\in\ell^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}(G,C_{0}(X)),\xi\in\ell^{p}(X\times G),\eta\in\ell^{q}(X\times G)

\langle\Lambda_{p}(\Phi)\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,\Lambda_{q}(\Phi^{*})\eta\rangle,

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(G,C_{0}(X),\alpha,\omega)
be a twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system, where
X
is a Hausdorff locally compact space and
G
is a group of subexponential growth. Then

{\rm rr}\big(F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha,\omega)\big)={\rm rr}\big(C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\omega}G\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad{\rm tsr}\big(F^{q}_{*}(G,X,\alpha,\omega)\big)={\rm tsr}\big(C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\omega}G\big),

for all

{\rm tsr}\big(C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha,\omega}G\big)=1

{\rm tsr}\big(F^{p}(G,X,\alpha,\omega)\big)=1,\quad\text{ for all }p\in[1,\infty].

We finish the article with the following theorem, which compiles some consequences and explicit computations derived from these results.

Theorem 3.3
.

The following statements are true.

(i)

Let
G
be an abelian group, whose Pontryagin dual is denoted
\widehat{G}
. Then, for
p\in[1,2]
,

{\rm rr}\big(F^{p}_{*}(G)\big)={\rm dim}\big(\widehat{G}\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad{\rm tsr}\big(F^{p}_{*}(G)\big)=\lfloor\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm dim}\big(\widehat{G}\big)\rfloor+1,

where
{\rm dim}(X)
is the covering dimension of a compact space
X
.

(ii)

Let
G
be a nilpotent group, and let
G^{f}\subset G
denote the (normal) subgroup of torsion elements. Then, for
p\in[1,2]
,

1.

{\rm rr}\big(F^{p}_{*}(G)\big)=0
if and only if
G=G^{f}
.

2.

{\rm rr}\big(F^{p}_{*}(G)\big)=1
if and only if
G/G^{f}\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii)

Let
\alpha
be a free, minimal action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
on the Cantor set
X=2^{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

{\rm rr}\big(F^{q}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},X,\alpha)\big)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad{\rm tsr}\big(F^{q}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},X,\alpha)\big)={\rm tsr}\big(F^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},X,\alpha)\big)=1,

for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,2]
.

(iv)

Let
\alpha
be an action of

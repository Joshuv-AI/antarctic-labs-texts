Evaluating the quality and reliability of rotator cuff injury related videos on Bilibili and TikTok
Authors: Junyu Bai, Jinqiao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rotator cuff injury (RCI) is a common age-related musculoskeletal disorder, yet public awareness remains limited. With short-video platforms emerging as key health information sources, concerns about content quality have grown. This study evaluated the quality, reliability, and comprehensiveness of RCI-related videos on TikTok and Bilibili and examined the impact of uploader characteristics. On October 13, 2025, Chinese-language videos containing “rotator cuff injury” were retrieved from TikTok and Bilibili. Following strict inclusion criteria, 231 videos were analyzed. Video characteristics, engagement metrics (likes, comments, shares, collections), and uploader information were collected. Quality and content integrity were assessed using the Video Information and Quality Index (VIQI), Global Quality Scale (GQS), and modified DISCERN (mDISCERN). Statistical comparisons and Spearman correlation analyses were performed. Of 231 videos, 96 were from Bilibili and 135 from TikTok. TikTok videos were shorter (median 93 seconds vs 234.5 seconds, P < .001) but demonstrated higher engagement across all metrics (P < .001). Physicians contributed most content (TikTok: 79%, Bilibili: 64%). Diagnosis and treatment dominated, while epidemiology and prevention were rarely addressed. GQS and mDISCERN scores did not differ between platforms. TikTok scored higher on VIQI information flow (P < .001) and visual quality (P = .044); Bilibili excelled in information accuracy (P = .006). Individual user videos showed slightly higher GQS (P < .05) and accuracy (P = .033); physician videos received more comments (P = .002) and shares (P = .042). VIQI and GQS correlated positively with engagement metrics (VIQI: R = 0.21–0.40; GQS: r = 0.20–0.28; all P < .001), whereas mDISCERN showed weak associations. Video duration correlated positively with quality scores (GQS: r = 0.56; VIQI: R = 0.34; mDISCERN: r = 0.33; all P < .001) but negligibly with engagement. The analysis of RCI-related videos on TikTok and Bilibili reveals moderate content quality and a significant lack of preventive information. These findings underscore the need for strategies to enhance the scientific rigor and comprehensiveness of health information on short-video platforms.

1. Introduction

The rotator cuff is a complex of shoulder muscles, consisting of the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, subscapularis, and teres minor, and their associated tendons, which attach to the humeral head to form a dynamic stabilizing apparatus that maintains shoulder joint stability during various movements.[1,2] Beyond providing stability, the rotator cuff enables fine motor control of the shoulder through distinct muscular subdivisions.[3] Rotator cuff injury (RCI) represents one of the most prevalent shoulder disorders.[4,5] Its pathogenesis is multifactorial, involving oxidative stress and chronic inflammatory responses that may lead to tendon degeneration and tearing.[6] RCI not only restricts patients’ range of motion, but also adversely affects multiple aspects of daily life, diminishing overall quality of life.[7,8]

With the advancement of digital technology, social media platforms have emerged as important channels for public access to health knowledge.[9] TikTok leverages its high user engagement to effectively capture public attention toward health information,[10] while Bilibili shapes viewers’ perceptions and attitudes toward health topics through diverse creator perspectives – for instance, by fostering nuanced discussions that have contributed to vaccine uptake.[11] Orthopedic patients demonstrate substantial demand for educational materials and frequently turn to the internet for information.[12] Given the specialized knowledge required for RCI diagnosis, treatment, and rehabilitation, patients urgently need accessible and practical health guidance.[13] However, health communication via short-video platforms faces significant challenges: specialized content often becomes marginalized by algorithms due to its less engaging format, whereas entertaining content is preferentially recommended. This disconnect between high-quality science communication and user engagement may facilitate the spread of low-quality videos, potentially exposing viewers to health risks.[14-16] As short videos become ubiquitous in health science communication, identifying reliable information sources and optimizing dissemination strategies has emerged as a pressing concern.

Given that RCI is a common musculoskeletal condition, the quality of short-video science communication on this topic directly influences patients’ health decisions. Yet, systematic evaluations of content quality differences across uploader types and the impact of platform characteristics on viewer engagement remain lacking. Therefore, this cross-sectional study analyzed RCI-related short videos on TikTok and Bilibili, employing the modified DISCERN (mDISCERN), Global Quality Scale (GQS), and Video Information and Quality Index (VIQI) instruments to assess content quality and information reliability. Our findings aim to inform evidence-based strategies for optimizing health communication in this domain and to contribute to the standardization of high-quality health information dissemination on short-video platforms.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Search strategy and data extraction

This cross-sectional study was conducted on October 13, 2025, using desktop computers, searching for videos with the keyword “rotator cuff injury” (肩袖损伤) on the Chinese versions of TikTok and Bilibili platforms. To minimize the bias introduced by personalized recommendation algorithms, new user accounts were created for each platform. Searches were performed using the default sorting method (no restriction on publication date), which presents results based on a comprehensive algorithm incorporating video completion rate (percentage of viewers watching more than 5 seconds), number of likes, comments, followers, and upload time, thereby prioritizing recent and popular content. A total of 300 videos were initially identified.

The following videos were excluded from the analysis: videos uploaded within 7 days prior to data collection (to ensure data stability); videos irrelevant to RCI, or those with superficial content, defined as videos that mentioned RCI only briefly (e.g., in passing within the first or last few seconds) without providing any substantive information on its epidemiology, etiology, clinical manifestations, diagnosis, treatment, or prevention; videos primarily for advertising or promotional purposes; and duplicate or re-uploaded videos that had already been analyzed. After applying these criteria, 231 videos were included for analysis (Fig. 1).

Flow chart of subject selection.

Basic information for each included video – such as title, uploader details, duration, content type, user engagement metrics, and publication date – was extracted using the platforms’ public APIs (application programming interface) to ensure data accuracy and consistency. All data were systematically recorded in Microsoft Excel for subsequent analysis.

2.2. Uploader characteristics

Based on the professional background of the video creators, the sources of the videos were classified into 2 categories: individual users (nonprofessional Physicians) and licensed physicians. Uploaders were considered “verified” if their account displayed an official verification badge and their profile explicitly stated their medical credentials or institutional affiliation. Detailed information was collected for each uploader, including their identity, number of followers, verification status, and type.

2.3. Ethical considerations

This study only included publicly available short videos on rotator cuff injuries from the Bilibili and TikTok platforms. As no personal or sensitive data were involved, ethical review was not required.

2.4. Assessment of video quality and reliability

The completeness of video content was evaluated across 6 predefined domains: epidemiology, etiology or mechanisms, clinical manifestations, diagnostic methods, treatments, and preventive measures. Each domain was scored using a binary system: 1 point if the video adequately covered the domain, and 0 points if the domain was not mentioned or only briefly mentioned without substantive information. All target videos were collected and downloaded by a designated researcher (BJY). To avoid potential bias related to scoring order, all videos to be evaluated were randomly reordered before assessment. Two reviewers with relevant medical backgrounds independently evaluated the videos and received standardized training before scoring to ensure consistent assessment criteria and minimize subjective bias. Any discrepancies in scoring were resolved by a third arbitrator (WS), and final consensus was achieved among all authors. The inter-rater agreement between the 2 reviewers was quantified using Cohen’s κ coefficient, with reference to the criteria proposed by Landis and Koch: κ > 0.8 indicating excellent agreement, 0.6 to 0.8 substantial agreement, 0.4 to 0.6 moderate agreement, and κ < 0.4 poor agreement.[17]

Video quality was assessed using the Video Information and Quality Index (VIQI), Global Quality Scale (GQS), and the modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) instrument.[18-20] The VIQI tool covers 4 dimensions: information flow (VIQI 1), accuracy of information (VIQI 2), quality (including scores for images, animation, interviews, captions, and summaries; VIQI 3), and precision (level of consistency between the video title and content; VIQI 4). Each criterion is rated on a 1 to 5 scale, with higher scores indicating better quality. The overall information quality of videos was evaluated using the Global Quality Scale (GQS), which consists of 5 items scored from 1 to 5, where higher scores represent better quality. The reliability of video content was assessed using the mDISCERN instrument, with higher scores indicating greater reliability.

2.5. Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize video characteristics. Non-normally distributed variables were presented as median with interquartile range (IQR). The Mann–Whitney U test was employed to compare video quality scores (including GQS, mDISCERN, and VIQI) between different uploader groups and between different platform groups. Spearman correlation coefficient was used to analyze the correlations between engagement metrics (likes, collections, comments, shares), video duration, and quality scores. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing), with a P-value < .05 considered statistically significant.

3. Results

3.6. Correlation heatmap analysis

Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to examine the relationships among various video metrics, GQS, mDISCERN, and VIQI scores in RCI-related videos. The results are as follows: Total VIQI scores were moderately positively correlated with all engagement metrics (r = 0.21–0.40, all P < .001), with the strongest correlation observed for collections (r = 0.40). GQS exhibited weak to moderate positive correlations with engagement metrics (r = 0.20–0.28, all P < .001), most strongly with collections (r = 0.28), and showed no significant correlation with shares. mDISCERN demonstrated generally weak correlations with engagement indicators, displaying only modest positive associations with likes (r = 0.14, P < .05) and comments (r = 0.16, P < .05).

Video duration was significantly positively correlated with quality scores (GQS: r = 0.56; VIQI: r = 0.34; mDISCERN: r = 0.33; all P < .001) but showed negligible correlations with engagement metrics, except for a weak positive association with collections (r = 0.13, P < .05). Moderate positive correlations were observed among all quality assessment tools (r = 0.30–0.47, all P < .001), with the strongest correlation between GQS and total VIQI scores (r = 0.47; Fig. 11).

4. Discussion

This study revealed that TikTok videos achieved higher engagement despite shorter duration, while Bilibili excelled in information accuracy. Physicians contributed most content, yet preventive information was scarce. Video quality showed weak-to-moderate correlations with engagement. Notably, video duration correlated positively with quality but negligibly with engagement, highlighting a decoupling between popularity and informational value on short-video platforms.

4.1. Platform attributes and user engagement

This study found significant differences between Bilibili and TikTok in terms of user engagement features and content presentation. Videos on TikTok were notably shorter in length but achieved significantly higher numbers of likes, comments, collections, and shares compared to Bilibili. This finding is consistent with previous studies, which reported that TikTok videos are generally shorter than those on Bilibili while displaying higher user engagement, a difference closely related to the platforms’ inherent characteristics.[21,22] Furthermore, shorter videos are more likely to stimulate continuous viewing, leading to increased user interactions and broader dissemination.[23] In contrast, Bilibili is characterized by longer videos and relatively lower interaction frequencies; its orientation toward “knowledge-based content” causes users to prioritize content depth over instant engagement.[24]

Among the specific interaction indices for the 2 platforms, “likes” and “collections” were the main user engagement forms, with TikTok users showing a significantly higher tendency to share compared to Bilibili. This may be associated with different sharing function designs and audience behaviors.[25-27] This divergence not only highlights platform-specific patterns in user engagement but also underscores critical design and operational considerations for social media platforms aiming to optimize user experience and participation.[28,29]

4.2. Uploader type and content structure

On both platforms, the proportion of videos uploaded by physicians exceeded that of individual users; this phenomenon was more pronounced on TikTok, reflecting a strong initiative among medical professionals for health communication via new media. This aligns with Kong et al,[30] who reported that Chinese health professionals have actively promoted diabetes-related knowledge on TikTok. Moreover, TikTok’s identity verification mechanism ensures that more physicians can participate in health information dissemination as verified professionals, thereby enhancing viewers’ trust in the information.[31]

The current results demonstrate that videos posted by physician users had higher numbers of comments and shares and shorter durations, suggesting that physicians tend to deliver concise summaries and highlight key messages. Notably, videos from individual users received higher GQS scores than those from physicians, possibly because content from individuals is more relatable and presented in a manner that better fits audience preferences. These differences may also reflect distinct content production goals and audience psychological needs.[32] Mueller et al[33] found that patients are more inclined to adopt advice from other patients rather than physicians, a conclusion not entirely consistent with some studies,[34] indicating the need for further investigation into the influence of cultural context and platform attributes on health communication.

4.3. Content distribution and quality assessment

Most videos on both platforms focused on “diagnosis” and “treatment”; TikTok videos more broadly covered “clinical manifestations,” whereas Bilibili had more content on “epidemiology” and “prevention.” Further analysis by uploader type revealed that the overall proportions of preventive and epidemiological content were generally low across both groups, suggesting insufficient attention to preventive health education among the public and content creators. This finding is consistent with Hu Yaqin et al,[35] who reported that over half of the thyroid-related videos on TikTok emphasized diagnosis and symptoms rather than prevention. Given that the incidence of RCI increases with age,[36] the lack of preventive content may hinder public risk awareness and early intervention, potentially increasing treatment challenges.

In terms of quality, GQS and mDISCERN scores did not differ significantly between platforms and were generally mediocre; however, VIQI scores were slightly higher for TikTok, mainly due to superior information flow and visual quality. Zheng et al[37] argued that high-quality health videos should combine scientific accuracy, accessibility, and clarity without misleading information, yet both platforms exhibited shortcomings in informational rigor. Additionally, Sun et al[38] suggested that credible videos do not necessarily gain more attention, with TikTok’s algorithm favoring entertaining content, thus explaining why overall quality scores remain moderate.

4.4. Correlation among variables

The present study found that total VIQI scores were moderately positively correlated with user engagement metrics, whereas GQS showed weak-to-moderate correlations, and mDISCERN exhibited very weak associations. These findings confirm that high engagement does not necessarily equate to high information quality,[39] suggesting that user behavior and algorithmic recommendations are more closely tied to sensory stimulation or entertainment value than to informational rigor. Li et al[40] reported positive correlations among likes, comments, shares, and collections, while Chen et al[41] explicitly noted that high-quality videos do not always attract greater user engagement, partially explaining the decoupling between popularity and information quality.

Notably, video duration was positively correlated with content quality, indicating that longer videos tend to provide more complete and accurate information.[42,43] Contrary to previous findings,[44] the present study observed no significant association between video duration and user engagement behaviors such as likes, comments, or shares. This suggests that while producing high-quality health science content requires sufficient time to ensure informational completeness,[45] merely lengthening videos does not guarantee viewer completion or interaction. Therefore, health science creators face the challenge of balancing content comprehensiveness against user attention. A practical approach involves breaking complex topics into short, serialized videos, with each episode focusing on a single knowledge point.[42] This strategy preserves scientific rigor while adapting to users’ fragmented viewing habits.[46]

4.5. Implications for patients and public health

Our findings have several important implications for patients and public health practice. First, the weak correlation between user engagement and content quality suggests that patients should exercise caution when relying on popularity metrics such as likes or shares, as high viewership does not guarantee accurate information.[47]

Second, the notable lack of preventive and epidemiological content across both platforms is concerning. Given that rotator cuff injuries are common, particularly among older adults, the scarcity of preventive information may limit public awareness of risk factors and early symptoms, potentially delaying diagnosis and treatment.[48,49] Thus, public health campaigns should prioritize creating and disseminating high-quality preventive content on these platforms.

Third, the complementary strengths of the 2 platforms offer opportunities for tailored health communication strategies. TikTok’s high engagement could be leveraged for brief, impactful awareness messages, while Bilibili’s strength in information accuracy makes it suitable for more detailed educational content.[50,51] Collaboration between healthcare institutions and platform providers could help promote evidence-based content and improve the overall quality of health information available to the public.[52,53]

4.6. Study limitations and future perspectives

Several limitations should be noted. First, this study focused solely on RCI videos, limiting generalizability across health information domains and platforms. Second, video scoring remains partly subjective, although standardized protocols and multiple raters were employed. Third, the analyses revealed associations rather than causal relationships; future research should incorporate experimental or longitudinal approaches for more robust inference. Future studies should leverage emerging techniques such as AI-based content analysis to enhance objectivity while expanding to broader disease areas, additional platforms, and countries.[54,55] Furthermore, efforts to assess and reveal the mechanisms behind users’ health literacy promotion remain warranted.

5. Conclusions

This study found that the overall quality of rotator cuff injury–related short videos on TikTok and Bilibili was moderate, with most content focusing on diagnosis and treatment, while preventive information was limited. TikTok videos had higher user engagement and better visual quality, but information accuracy was higher on Bilibili. Physician-uploaded videos generated more interaction, whereas individual users’ videos had slightly higher quality and accuracy. Importantly, user engagement was only weakly related to content quality, highlighting the need to improve both scientific rigor and coverage of prevention in health communication on short-video platforms.

N 3 LL + 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2}) predictions of lepton-jet azimuthal angular distribution in deep-inelastic scattering
Shen Fang, Mei-Sen Gao, Hai Tao Li, and Ding Yu Shao

Abstract
We present an analysis of lepton-jet azimuthal decorrelation in deep-inelastic scattering (DIS) at next-to-next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic (N 3 LL) accuracy, combined with fixed-order corrections at 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2}) . In this study, jets are defined in the lab frame using the anti- k T k_{T} clustering algorithm and the winner-take-all recombination scheme. The N 3 LL resummation results are derived from the transverse-momentum dependent factorization formula within the soft-collinear effective theory, while the 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2}) fixed-order matching distribution is calculated using the NLOJET++ event generator. The azimuthal decorrelation between the jet and electron serves as a critical probe of the three-dimensional structure of the nucleon. Our numerical predictions provide a robust framework for precision studies of QCD and the nucleon’s internal structure through jet observables in DIS. These results are particularly significant for analyses involving jets in HERA data and the forthcoming electron-ion collider experiments.

2
Kinematics

We consider the process in
ep
collisions as

\displaystyle e(\ell)+p(P)\rightarrow e(\ell^{\prime})+J(P_{J})+X\,,

(1)

where
e(\ell)
refers to the incident electron with momentum
\ell
,
p(P)
denotes the proton with momentum
P
, and
e(\ell^{\prime})
symbolizes the scattered electron with momentum
\ell^{\prime}
. The final state includes a leading jet
J
with momentum
P_{J}
and inclusive particles
X
. In the lab frame, the four-momenta of the initial-state lepton and proton can be expressed in terms of the lepton energy
E_{e}
and the proton energy
E_{p}
as:

\displaystyle P^{\mu}=E_{p}\,n^{\mu}\,,\qquad\ell^{\mu}=E_{e}\,\bar{n}^{\mu}\,,

(2)

where
n^{\mu}=(1,0,0,1)
and
\bar{n}^{\mu}=(1,0,0,-1)
are the light-cone reference vectors in Cartesian space-time coordinates. In taking this parametrization, the
z
direction is defined along the proton beam while the electron beam is in the negative
z
direction.

At the leading order (LO), the partonic process is denoted by
e(l)+q(k)\to e(l^{\prime})+q(P_{J})
, where the final-state momenta of the lepton and the jet are parameterized in terms of the lepton’s rapidity
y_{l}
and the jet’s rapidity
y_{J}
and the transverse momentum
p_{T}
as

\displaystyle{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mu}=p_{T}\left(\mathrm{ch}y_{l},0,1,\mathrm{sh}y_{l}\right)\,,\qquad P_{J}^{\mu}=p_{T}\left(\mathrm{ch}y_{J},0,-1,\mathrm{sh}y_{J}\right)\equiv E_{J}\,n_{J}^{\mu}\,,

(3)

where
E_{J}=p_{T}\,\mathrm{ch}y_{J}
represents the energy of the jet, and
n_{J}^{\mu}=\left(1,0,-\mathrm{sech}y_{J},\mathrm{th}y_{J}\right)
denotes the light-cone vector along the jet direction. We can also define another jet light-cone vector
{\bar{n}}^{\mu}_{J}=\left(1,0,\mathrm{sech}y_{J},-\mathrm{th}y_{J}\right).
By momentum conservation, the four-momentum of the intermediate photon is given by
q=\ell-\ell^{\prime}
. From this parametrization, we can define the following kinematic variables

\displaystyle Q^{2}=-(\ell-\ell^{\prime})^{2}=2e^{-y_{J}}x_{\tt bj}\,E_{p}\,p_{T}\,,\qquad x_{\tt bj}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2P\cdot q}=\frac{E_{e}\,p_{T}\,e^{2y_{J}}}{E_{e}\,E_{p}\,2\,e^{y_{J}}-E_{p}\,p_{T}}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{t}=-Q^{2}\,,\qquad\hat{s}=x_{\tt bj}\,S\,,\qquad\hat{u}=-\hat{s}-\hat{t}\,,\qquad S=4E_{e}\,E_{p}\,,

(4)

where we neglect the proton mass and
k^{\mu}=x_{\tt bj}P^{\mu}
. Based on the above definition, the LO cross section is expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}^{2}p_{T}\,\mathrm{d}y_{J}}=\sigma_{0}\sum_{q}e_{q}^{2}f_{q/p}(x_{\tt bj},\mu),

(5)

where
e_{q}
is the fractional charge carried by parton
q
,
f_{q/p}
denotes the collinear PDF and the prefactor
\sigma_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{SQ^{2}}\frac{2\left(\hat{s}^{2}+\hat{u}^{2}\right)}{\hat{t}\hat{u}}\,.

(6)

The azimuthal angle between the final electron and the leading jet is defined as
\delta\phi\equiv|\pi-|\phi_{J}-\phi_{l}||
with
-\pi<\phi_{l,J}\leq\pi
. At LO,
\delta\phi=0
, as given in Eq. (
3
). Beyond the Born configuration, higher-order corrections will contribute. In the back-to-back limit,
\delta\phi\ll 1
, one must consider the presence of any number of soft and collinear emissions, which causes a small deviation from
\delta\phi=0
. These contributions are captured by all-order resummation. Additionally, perturbative matching corrections need to be included as
\delta\phi\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
. In this work, we incorporate these corrections up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
.

In the back-to-back limit, the TMD factorization formula for the azimuthal decorrelation has been derived for

The azimuthal decorrelation between the lepton (

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}\equiv l^{\prime}_{x}+P_{x,J}.

In the limit

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}^{2}p_{T}\,\mathrm{d}y_{J}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{x}}

\displaystyle=\,\sigma_{0}\,H(Q,\mu)\!\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{x}}{2\pi}e^{ib_{x}\lambda_{x}}\sum_{q}e_{q}^{2}~{\cal B}_{q/p}\left(x_{\tt bj},b_{x},\mu,\zeta_{\cal B}/\nu^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times{\cal J}_{q}\left(b_{x},\mu,\zeta_{\cal J}/\nu^{2}\right)\,{\cal S}\left(b_{x},n\cdot n_{J},\mu,\nu\right),

(8)

where all ingredients, including the hard function

\mathcal{B}_{q/p}

and soft function

\mu

and

\nu

\displaystyle\mu_{h}\sim\nu_{\mathcal{B}}\sim\nu_{\mathcal{J}}\sim Q\sim p_{T},

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{B}}\sim\mu_{\mathcal{J}}\sim\mu_{\mathcal{S}}\sim\nu_{\mathcal{S}}\sim|\lambda_{x}|\sim 1/\left|b_{x}\right|.

The soft function

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left(b_{x},n\cdot n_{J},\mu,\nu\right)=\mathcal{S}_{\perp}(b_{x},\mu,\nu\sqrt{n\cdot n_{J}/2}).

Similar to Ref.

\displaystyle f_{q/p}\left(x_{\tt bj},b_{x},\mu,\zeta_{B}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle J_{q}\left(b_{x},\mu,\zeta_{J}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{q}\left(b_{x},\mu,\zeta_{\mathcal{J}}/\nu^{2}\right)\,\frac{\mathcal{S}\left(b_{x},n\cdot n_{J},\mu,\nu\right)}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\perp}\left(b_{x},\mu,\nu\right)}}\,,

(12)

where we have redefined the Collins-Soper scales in the conventional manner as

\displaystyle\sqrt{\zeta_{B}}=\sqrt{\zeta_{\mathcal{B}}}\,,\qquad\sqrt{\zeta_{J}}=\frac{n\cdot n_{J}}{2}\sqrt{\zeta_{\mathcal{J}}}\,,

(13)

with
\zeta_{B}\,\zeta_{J}=Q^{4}
.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}^{2}p_{T}\,\mathrm{d}y_{J}\,\mathrm{d}\delta\phi}=\sigma_{0}p_{T}H\left(Q,\mu_{h}\right)\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2\mathrm{d}b}{\pi}e^{ibp_{T}\delta\phi}\sum_{q}e_{q}^{2}\,f_{q/p}(x_{\tt bj},b,\mu_{h},\zeta_{f})J_{q}(b,\mu_{h},\zeta_{f})\,,

(14)

where we define

\delta\phi=|\lambda_{x}|/p_{T}
in the back-to-back limit. The TMD PDF and jet function at scales
\{\mu_{h},\zeta_{f}\}

\displaystyle f_{q/p}\left(x_{\tt bj},b,\mu_{h},\zeta_{f}\right)

\displaystyle=f_{q/p}\left(x_{\tt bj},b,\mu_{b},\zeta_{i}\right)\exp\left[\int_{\mu_{b}}^{\mu_{h}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu}\gamma^{f}_{\mu}\left(\mu,\zeta_{f}\right)\right]\left(\frac{\zeta_{f}}{\zeta_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{f}_{\zeta}(b,\,\mu_{b})}\,,

\displaystyle J_{q}\left(b,\mu_{h},\zeta_{f}\right)

\displaystyle=J_{q}\left(b,\mu_{b},\zeta_{i}\right)\exp\left[\int_{\mu_{b}}^{\mu_{h}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu}\gamma^{J}_{\mu}\left(\mu,\zeta_{f}\right)\right]\left(\frac{\zeta_{f}}{\zeta_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{J}_{\zeta}\left(b,\,\mu_{b}\right)}\,,

(16)

respectively, where anomalous dimensions are given by

\displaystyle\gamma^{f/J}_{\mu}\left(\mu,\zeta_{f}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{F}\gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha_{s})\hbox{ln}{\frac{\mu^{2}}{\zeta_{f}}}+\gamma_{f/J}(\alpha_{s})\,,

(17)

\displaystyle\gamma^{f/J}_{\zeta}(b,\,\mu_{b})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{b_{0}^{2}/b^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}C_{F}\gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha_{s})+\gamma_{r}(\alpha_{s})\,,

4
Fixed-order expansion

In this section, we provide the fixed-order singular cross section at the level of the integrated cross section, which is defined as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}^{2}p_{T}\,\mathrm{d}y_{J}}\sigma_{\mathrm{singular}}(\delta\phi^{\mathrm{cut}})\equiv\int^{\delta\phi^{\mathrm{cut}}}_{0}\mathrm{d}\delta\phi\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}^{2}p_{T}\,\mathrm{d}y_{J}\,\mathrm{d}\delta\phi}\,.

(18)

Here the singular cross section can be recovered from the resummation formula (
14
) by setting
\mu_{b}=\mu_{h}
and
\zeta_{f}=\zeta_{i}=Q^{2}
, which is expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}^{2}p_{T}\,\mathrm{d}y_{J}}\sigma_{\mathrm{singular}}(\delta\phi^{\mathrm{cut}})=\sigma_{0}\,\sum_{q}e_{q}^{2}\sum_{i}\int_{x_{\tt bj}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}C_{q/i}\left(z,\delta\phi^{\mathrm{cut}},Q\right)f_{i/p}\left(x_{\tt bj}/z,Q\right)\,,

(19)

where we choose
\mu_{h}=Q
. The perturbative coefficients of
C_{q/i}
have the following expansion in the strong coupling constant

\displaystyle C_{q/i}\left(z,\delta\phi^{\mathrm{cut}},Q\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{q/i}^{(n)}\left(z,\delta\phi^{\mathrm{cut}},Q\right)\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}(Q)}{4\pi}\right)^{n}\,.

(20)

Writing the perturbative coefficient
C_{q/i}^{(n)}
as a function of the logarithm

\displaystyle C_{q/i}^{(0)}\left(z,\delta\phi^{\mathrm{cut}},Q\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{qi}\delta(1-z),

(21)

\displaystyle C_{q/i}^{(1)}\left(z,\delta\phi^{\mathrm{cut}},Q\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{qi}\delta(1-z)\left(C_{F}^{2}L_{F}+C_{F}C_{A}L_{A}+C_{F}T_{F}n_{f}L_{f}+j_{2}\right)+A_{qi}^{(2)}(z)+I_{qi}^{(2)}(z),

\displaystyle=32L^{4}+96L^{3}+L^{2}\left(144+\frac{64\pi^{2}}{3}+48\mathrm{ln}2\right)+L\left(102+40\pi^{2}+72\mathrm{ln}2+160\zeta_{3}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{63}{4}+\frac{67\pi^{2}}{2}+\frac{851\pi^{4}}{360}+96\mathrm{ln}2+3\pi^{2}\mathrm{ln}2+132\zeta_{3}\,,

\displaystyle L_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{352}{9}L^{3}+L^{2}\left(-\frac{140}{9}+\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}\right)+L\left(-\frac{188}{3}+\frac{88\pi^{2}}{9}+44\mathrm{ln}2+48\zeta_{3}\right)

(22)

\displaystyle-\frac{46301}{324}-\frac{1015\pi^{2}}{108}+\frac{23\pi^{4}}{30}+\frac{1253}{9}\zeta_{3}\,,

\displaystyle L_{f}

\displaystyle=-\frac{128}{9}L^{3}+\frac{16}{9}L^{2}+L\left(\frac{64}{3}-\frac{32\pi^{2}}{9}-16\mathrm{ln}2\right)+\frac{3757}{81}+\frac{65\pi^{2}}{27}-\frac{220}{9}\zeta_{3}\,.

Here,

denotes the two-loop constant term of the jet function, which has been numerically determined using

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have presented a detailed analysis of the azimuthal angular distribution between the lepton and the leading jet in DIS at N
3
LL +
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
accuracy. Our study utilized the TMD factorization formalism within the framework of SCET. By employing the anti-
k_{T}
clustering algorithm (
R=1
) and the winner-take-all recombination scheme, we properly defined the final-state jets in the lab frame. Additionally, we validated the extraction of the two-loop constant terms in the jet function through numerical methods and cross-checked our results with fixed-order predictions for the back-to-back dijet process in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions. Furthermore, the comparison between our resummation results and fixed-order calculations emphasizes the necessity of incorporating matching corrections to achieve accurate predictions across different kinematic regions. The successful application of the transition function to smoothly interpolate between the resummation and fixed-order regimes demonstrates the robustness of our approach.

Our findings highlight the importance of precision QCD calculations in reducing theoretical uncertainties and enhancing the reliability of predictions for experimental observables. The methodologies and results presented herein are expected to significantly aid in the analysis of current and future DIS data, thereby advancing our understanding of the inner structure of nucleons. Looking forward, ongoing and future research efforts aimed at incorporating resummation of power corrections to jet observables will further improve the precision and applicability of TMD factorization and resummation techniques in DIS. Our study provides a foundational step towards these goals and emphasizes the significance of high-order resummation in accurately characterizing hadronic structure.

Appendix A
Resummation formula in
e^{+}e^{-}
and the two-loop jet function

This appendix briefly reviews the factorization and resummation formula for the transverse momentum decorrelation in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to
dijet process
[
19
]
. We follow this by outlining the fixed-order expansion of the resummed expressions and present the methods for the numerical determination of two-loop constants in the jet function, utilizing the
NLOJET++
generator.

In the coordinate
b
-space, the factorization theorem reads

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{q}_{T}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\sigma}_{0}\,H(Q,\mu)\!\!\int\!\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{b}_{T}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{q}_{T}\cdot\bm{b}_{T}}{\cal J}_{q}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta/\nu^{2}){\cal J}_{\bar{q}}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta/\nu^{2})S(b_{T},\mu,\nu)\,,

(32)

where the Born cross section is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}_{0}=\frac{4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{Q^{2}}\sum_{f}e_{f}^{2}\,.

(33)

In the standard CSS treatment
[
111
]
, the genuine jet function is defined as

\displaystyle J_{q}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta)\equiv{\cal J}_{q}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta/\nu^{2})\sqrt{S(b_{T},\mu,\nu)}\,,

(34)

where the rapidity scale
\nu
dependence cancels once two contributions are combined.

At the renormalization scale
\mu
, after solving the CSS evolution equation for the
\zeta
dependence in the jet function, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{q}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta_{f})=J_{q}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta_{i})\left(\frac{\zeta_{f}}{\zeta_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\zeta}^{J}\left(b_{T},\mu\right)}\,.

(35)

In addition to the CSS evolution equation, we also consider the standard RG evolution between the hard function and TMD jet functions, with the corresponding RG equations:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\,\hbox{ln}\mu}H(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle=\left[2\,C_{F}\gamma_{\text{cusp}}(\alpha_{s})\,\mathrm{ln}\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+2\gamma_{V}(\alpha_{s})\right]H(Q,\mu)\,,

(36)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\,\hbox{ln}{\mu}}J_{q}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta_{f})

\displaystyle=\left[C_{F}\gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha_{s})\hbox{ln}{\frac{\mu^{2}}{\zeta_{f}}}+\gamma_{J}(\alpha_{s})\right]J_{q}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta_{f})\,.

(37)

The above RG equations hold to all orders in perturbative QCD as a consequence of the factorization theorem in Eq. (
32
). Therefore, the cusp anomalous dimension terms imply that
\zeta_{f}=Q^{2}
, where we make the Collins-Soper scales in the two TMD jet functions equal. Besides, the RG consistency implies that

\displaystyle\gamma_{V}(\alpha_{s})+\gamma_{J}(\alpha_{s})=0\,.

(38)

The perturbative coefficients of all anomalous dimensions needed are summarized in the appendix
B
.

After evolving the jet function from
\{\mu_{b},\zeta_{i}\}
to
\{\mu_{h},\zeta_{f}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{q}(b_{T},\mu_{h},\zeta_{f})=e^{-S_{\rm pert}(\mu_{b},\,\mu_{h})}\left(\frac{\zeta_{f}}{\zeta_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\zeta}^{J}\left(b_{T},\,\mu_{b}\right)}J_{q}(b_{T},\mu_{b},\zeta_{i})\,,

(39)

with the perturbative Sudakov factor given by

\displaystyle S_{\rm pert}(\mu_{b},\,\mu_{h})=-\int_{\mu_{b}}^{\mu_{h}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu}\left[C_{F}\gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha_{s})\hbox{ln}{\frac{\mu^{2}}{\zeta_{f}}}+\gamma_{J}(\alpha_{s})\right]\,,

(40)

where the renormalization and the Collins-Soper scales are chosen as

\displaystyle\mu_{h}=Q,\quad\quad\mu_{b}=b_{0}/b_{T},\quad\quad\zeta_{i}=b_{0}^{2}/b_{T}^{2},\quad\quad\zeta_{f}=Q^{2}\,.

(41)

The all-order resummed cross section is written as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}q_{T}}={\bar{\sigma}}_{0}\,H(Q,\mu_{h})\,q_{T}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}b_{T}\,\mathrm{d}b_{T}J_{0}(q_{T}b_{T})J_{q}(b_{T},\mu_{h},\zeta_{f})J_{\bar{q}}(b_{T},\mu_{h},\zeta_{f})\,,

(42)

where
J_{0}
is the Bessel function of the first kind.

It is instructive to perform the fixed-order expansion of the resummed result in Eq. (
42
). To this end, we first define the integrated cross section in the form

\displaystyle\sigma_{L}(Q_{T})\equiv\int_{0}^{Q_{T}}\mathrm{d}q_{T}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}q_{T}}\,.

(43)

Here the perturbative expansion of the integrated cross section
\sigma_{L}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{L}(Q_{T})}{\bar{\sigma}_{0}}=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}(Q)}{2\pi}A(Q_{T})+\left[\frac{\alpha_{s}(Q)}{2\pi}\right]^{2}B(Q_{T})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

(44)

where the two-loop coefficient
B
is given by

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\,C_{F}^{2}\Bigg[\frac{\bar{L}^{4}}{2}+3\bar{L}^{3}+\bar{L}^{2}\left(\frac{11}{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}+6\,\hbox{ln}2\right)+\bar{L}\left(\frac{9}{4}+18\,\hbox{ln}2-4\,\zeta_{3}\right)-\frac{189}{16}+5\,\pi^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{173\,\pi^{4}}{720}+27\,\hbox{ln}2-\frac{9}{2}\pi^{2}\,\hbox{ln}2+9\,\hbox{ln}^{2}2-3\,\zeta_{3}\Bigg]+C_{F}\,C_{A}\Bigg[\frac{11\bar{L}^{3}}{9}+\bar{L}^{2}\left(-\frac{35}{36}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)

\displaystyle+\bar{L}\left(-\frac{57}{4}+\frac{11\pi^{2}}{18}+11\,\hbox{ln}2+6\,\zeta_{3}\right)-\frac{51157}{1296}+\frac{1061\pi^{2}}{216}-\frac{2\pi^{4}}{45}+\frac{401\zeta_{3}}{18}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+C_{F}\,T_{F}\,n_{f}\Bigg[-\frac{4\bar{L}^{3}}{9}+\frac{\bar{L}^{2}}{9}+\bar{L}\left(5-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{9}-4\,\hbox{ln}2\right)+\frac{4085}{324}-\frac{91\pi^{2}}{54}-\frac{14\zeta_{3}}{9}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{j_{2}}{2}\,,

(45)

where we define
\bar{L}\equiv\hbox{ln}\,Q_{T}^{2}/Q^{2}
and
j_{2}
denotes the two-loop constant term in the jet function, which depends on the clustering algorithm and recombination scheme in the jet definition.

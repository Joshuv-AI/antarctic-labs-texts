1.
Introduction

Given a function
N:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{C}
, we denote the corresponding Mellin transform by

L(s,N):=s\int_{1}^{\infty}N(x)x^{-s-1}dx.

This generalizes the construction of a generating Dirichlet series: if
N(X)=\sum_{n\leq X}a_{n}
it follows that
L(s,N)=\sum a_{n}n^{-s}
as long as the sum converges absolutely.

The goal of this paper is to prove a Tauberian theorem that uses slightly more stringent analytic conditions in order to give a better error bound. Tauberian theorems typically utilize a vertical bound of the form
|L(s,N)|\ll_{\sigma}(1+|t|)^{\xi}
in order to control the size of the error term. We show that all references to pointwise bounds can be removed from such Tauberian theorems, in exchange for bounds on twisted moments of the form

\int_{T}^{2T}L(\sigma+it,N)Z^{it}dt.

It is typically easier to bound such moments than it is to give pointwise bounds, often leading to stronger error bounds.

The following is our main theorem with inexplicit constants. The explicit dependence of the implied constants on various parameters is given by Theorem
2.1
.

1.1.
Bounding via Integral Moments

The trivial bound

\left|\int_{T}^{2T}L(\sigma+it,F)Z^{it}dt\right|\leq\int_{T}^{2T}|L(\sigma+it,F)|dt

allows one to use upper bounds for integral moments as “out of the box” ingredients for
Theorem

1.1
. See, for example, recent work of Languasco, Lunia, and Moree
[
LLM25
]
improving error bounds for the number of ideals of bounded norm in abelian number fields using bounds for small integral moments of Dirichlet
L
-functions.

We bolster this with a few more examples from the study of number field counting: Let
G
be an abelian group. Define

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G;X):=\{L/\mathbb{Q}:\textnormal{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\cong G,\ |\textnormal{disc}(L/\mathbb{Q})|\leq X\}.

The asymptotic growth rate of this function was determined by Mäki
[
Mäk85
]
to be

\#\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G;X)\sim c(\mathbb{Q},G)X^{1/a(G)}(\log X)^{b(\mathbb{Q},G)-1},

where
c(\mathbb{Q},G)>0
,
a(G)=|G|(1-\ell^{-1})
for
\ell
the smallest prime dividing
G
, and
b(\mathbb{Q},G)=\frac{|G[\ell]|-1}{\phi(\ell)}
where
\phi
is Euler’s totient function. This was generalized by Wright
[
Wri89
]
to base fields other than
\mathbb{Q}
. Subsequent asymptotic expansions, sometimes including lower order terms, have since been given by
[
FLN18
,
Alb24b
]
.

The generating Dirichlet series for such extensions has been expressed as products of Dedekind zeta functions (times an absolutely convergent Euler product) in
[
Mäk85
,
Alb24b
]
. Upper bounds for the integral moments of Dedekind zeta functions follow directly from work of Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan
[
CN63
]
on approximate functional equations. Using these results as inputs in
Theorem

1.1
, we can prove several asymptotic expansions with unconditional square root saving error bounds.

Corollary 1.3
.

The following asymptotic expansions hold unconditionally

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{3};X)=

\displaystyle c_{2}(C_{3})X^{1/2}+O\left(X^{1/4}(\log X)^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{4};X)=

\displaystyle c_{2}(C_{4})X^{1/2}+c_{3}(C_{4})X^{1/3}+O\left(X^{1/4}(\log X)^{4}\right),

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{6};X)=

\displaystyle c_{3}(C_{6})X^{1/3}+c_{4}(C_{6})X^{1/4}+c_{5}(C_{6})X^{1/5}+O\left(X^{1/6}(\log X)^{5}\right),

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{8};X)=

\displaystyle c_{4}(C_{8})X^{1/4}+c_{6}(C_{8})X^{1/6}+c_{7}(C_{8})X^{1/7}+O\left(X^{1/8}(\log X)^{8}\right),

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{16};X)=

\displaystyle c_{8}(C_{16})X^{1/8}+c_{12}(C_{16})X^{1/12}+c_{14}(C_{16})X^{1/14}+c_{15}(C_{16})X^{1/15}

\displaystyle+O\left(X^{1/16}(\log X)^{17}\right),

and for each odd prime

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{2p};X)

\displaystyle=c_{p}(C_{2p})X^{\frac{1}{p}}+c_{2p-2}(C_{2p})X^{\frac{1}{2p-2}}+c_{2p-1}(C_{2p})X^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}+O_{p}\left(X^{\frac{1}{2p}}(\log X)^{3}\right).

The constants

are given explicitly by finite sums of convergent Euler products times special values of Dedekind zeta functions for cyclotomic fields.

The nonvanishing of

is equivalent to the nonvanishing of certain Dedekind zeta functions at certain rational values in the interval
(1/2,1]
, see
Section

A square root power saving bound of the form
O(X^{1/2a(G)+\epsilon})
is known for all abelian groups under GRH, see
[
Alb24b
, Corollary 1.6]
. Unconditionally, this quality of error term was previously known only for
G=C_{2}
, by classical counting results for squarefree numbers, while the case
G=C_{3}
can be reasonably inferred from results for counting ideals of bounded norm in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
with square root savings (one consequence of Landau’s original work
[
Lan15
]
). Corollary

is a significant improvement in this direction as it proves square root savings for infinitely many abelian groups. In the language of
[

, Definition 1.3]
,
Corollary

proves that
\theta(\mathbb{Q},C_{n})\leq 1/2a(C_{n})
for

More generally, determining the integral moments of
L
-functions is itself a well-studied and interesting problem. We refer the reader to some references for a more comprehensive overview of this field
[
Con01
,
Sou22
]
. Many of these conjectures and results are of a similar shape, for example the conjectural moments of the Riemann zeta function:

\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(1/2+it)|^{2m}dt\sim c_{m}T(\log T)^{m^{2}}

for each
m\geq 0
, where
c_{m}
is some constant (given explicitly by a conjecture of Keating and Snaith
[
KS00
]
) . In the context of Theorem

this corresponds to
\sigma_{a}=1
,
\delta=1/2
,
\eta=1
, and
\beta=m^{2}
, which is enough to produce square root savings.

The Lindelöf hypothesis is equivalent to an upper bound

for these moments for all
m\geq 0
, although bounds for a
particular
value of

are significantly weaker. In the case of the Riemann zeta function sharp upper bounds are known for

, while they are known for all

. This makes Theorem

where we have a chance of unconditionally bounding the corresponding moments.

1.2.
Bounding via (Approximate) Functional Equations

Suggested by the use of the general approximate functional equation of
[
CN63
]
above, (
1.1
) can also be bounded directly in terms of an approximate functional equation by simply integrating the approximate functional equation itself.

In a similar vein, a functional equation can be used to bound (
1.1
) when
\sigma<0
(or otherwise on the “other side” of the functional equation). This process is similar to that used by Landau
[
Lan15
]
and following work
[
CN62
,
LDTT22
]
to bound the error term by taking advantage of a functional equation with a particular shape. Bounding (
1.1
) for
\sigma<0
is more-or-less equivalent to bounding
W_{k}(X)
in
[
LDTT22
, Equation (18)]
.

In a sense, our results can be understood as explicitly separating Landau’s method into a part that does not require a functional equation,
Theorem

1.1
, and a part that may be bounded using a function equation as in Landau’s original approach, namely equation (
1.1
). One benefit of this separation is the possibility of improved error terms by bounding (
1.1
) through other means. For counting ideals in abelian number fields, the work of
[
LLM25
]
produces smaller error bounds than Landau’s original method
[
Lan15
,
CN62
,
LDTT22
]
by appealing to integral moments of Dirichlet
L
-functions instead of the functional equation.

Another benefit of this separation is that one can now modify Landau’s use of the functional equation to bound (
1.1
) using functional equations of new shapes without having to rework through the rest of the finite differencing method.

2.
An Explicit Tauberian Theorem

We state our main results in this section. We model our notation on that of
[
CN62
,
LDTT22
]
, which allows us both to directly indicate where our approach differs and to more easily take advantage of some basic lemmas. We note that, in some respects, our method has more in common with other works, such as
[
Rou11
,
Alb24b
]
, because we do not require a functional equation as input.

2.1.
Notation and Assumptions

We set the following notations and assumptions for a function
N:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{C}
.

•

(Meromorphic continuation)
L(s,N)
converges absolutely for
{\rm Re}(s)>\sigma_{a}
and has a meromorphic continuation to the right half-plane
{\rm Re}(s)\geq\sigma_{a}-\delta
with at most finitely many poles in this region.

Let
T_{0}\geq e
be a number such that all poles in this region have their imaginary part contained in the interval
[-T_{0},T_{0}]
.

•

(Polar data) Define

S_{N,\alpha}^{0}(X):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{\alpha}}L(s,N)\frac{X^{s}}{s}ds=\sum_{{\rm Re}(z)\geq\alpha}\underset{s=z}{\rm Res}\left(L(s,N)\frac{X^{s}}{s}\right),

where
C_{\alpha}
is any contour contained in the region of meromorphicity which encircles all the poles of
L(s,N)/s
satisfying
{\rm Re}(s)\geq\alpha
.

By direct computation of the residue, this function has the form
S_{N,\alpha}^{0}(X)=\sum X^{z}R_{z}(\log X)
for certain polynomials
R_{z}
. We further define

R_{N,\alpha}(X):=\sum X^{{\rm Re}(z)}R_{z}^{\rm abs}(\log X),

where
R_{z}^{\rm abs}
is the polynomial obtained from
R_{z}
by taking the absolute value of the coefficients.

•

(Leading coefficient) Let
\rho_{N}
be the leading coefficient of
S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)
; that is if
S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\epsilon}^{0}(X)\asymp X^{\sigma}(\log X)^{b-1}
for some
\sigma,\beta
as
X
tends towards infinity then

\rho_{N}:=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\epsilon}^{0}(X)}{X^{\sigma}(\log X)^{b-1}}.

For convenience, if
S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)=0
then we taken
\rho_{N}=1
by convention.

•

(Polynomial bounds for twisted moments) We ask that there exists
\widetilde{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{T_{0}}^{T}L(\sigma+it,N)Z^{it}dt\ll T^{\widetilde{\eta}}

for each
\sigma_{a}-\delta\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_{a}+1
, where the implied constant is independent of
T
(but otherwise may depend on any other parameters).

•

(Explicit bound for the leftmost twisted moment) We also ask that

\int_{T_{0}}^{T}L(\sigma_{a}-\delta+it,N)Z^{it}dt\leq QT^{\eta}(\log T)^{\beta}

for constants
Q>0
and
\eta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. One generally expects
\eta\geq\widetilde{\eta}
to follow from the Phragmén-Lindelöf principle, although this is not required.

We utilize the standard notation for contour integrals along the vertical line
{\rm Re}(s)=\alpha
,

\int_{(\alpha)}:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\alpha-iT}^{\alpha+iT}.

2.2.
The main theorem with explicit constants

The following is our main theorem. We leave one floating parameter

Theorem

Let

The parameters are defined as follows:

(1)

We demonstrate how to choose an optimum value for

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

If
\eta<1
, any choice for
T
produces the error term

\ll X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}.

The implied constant is precisely
2^{\delta}(E_{N,2}+E_{\widehat{N},2})
, but this is not necessary for the statement of
Theorem

1.1
. For the remaining cases, we let the implied constant absorb every parameter except for

and
T
.

If
\eta=1
, the error term is bounded above by

\ll T^{-1}X^{\sigma_{a}}(\log X)^{b-1}+X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}(\log T)^{\beta+1}+X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}.

The first term is decreasing in

while the second is increasing, so the optimum value is when the two terms are equal. Taking
T=X^{\delta}(\log X)^{b-1}
makes the two terms have the same order of magnitude, concluding the proof.

If
\eta>1

\ll T^{-1}X^{\sigma_{a}}(\log X)^{b-1}+X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}T^{\eta-1}(\log T)^{\beta}+X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}.

Similar to the
\eta=1

T=X^{\delta/\eta}(\log X)^{(b-1-\beta)/\eta}
. Plugging this in and evaluating concludes the proof.

2.3.
Summary of the proof of
Theorem

2.1

We will largely follow Landau’s method of finite differencing. The process proceeds by smoothing
N(X)
using finite difference operators, shifting the contour in Perron’s formula (
Section

3
), then unsmoothing back to
N(X)
(
Section

4
). This is similar to Landau’s original work
[
Lan15
]
and the following works
[
CN62
,
LDTT22
,
Rou11
,
Alb24b
]
.

In previous examples of this method, pieces of the shifted contour are generally bounded using pointwise bounds for
|L(s,N)|
. Integration by parts can be used in some places to apply average bounds instead, but in order to avoid the need for pointwise bounds at any point in the argument at all we will need to use an auxiliary average

N(X)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}N(X)du.

This equality is trivial, as the variable of integration does not actually appear inside the integral. The way in which we shift the contour integral in Perron’s formula will then be chosen to depend on the variable of integration
u
. In this way, we are able to take an average of several different choices for shifting the contour.

\gamma
can be chosen to “complete the vertical line” making
\int_{(\sigma_{a}-\delta)}^{*}=\int_{(\sigma_{a}-\delta)}
.

Remark 3.2
.

This is closely related to
[
PTBZ25
, Lemma B.4]
, relying on the notion that for a sufficiently smoothed counting function we expect that the contour integral in Perron’s formula can be shifted to the left without any error terms. The primary difference between Lemma
3.1
and previous work on smoothed counting functions is that we only assume the existence of a polynomial bound for the twisted average

\int_{T_{0}}^{T}L(\sigma+it,N)Z^{it}dt\ll T^{\widetilde{\eta}},

while all previous work the author is aware of requires polynomial pointwise bounds of the form

|L(\sigma+it,N)|\ll T^{\xi}.

Speaking theoretically this improvement is mildly interesting, although perhaps not surprising. However, speaking practically the author does not know an example of a Mellin transform
L(s,N)
which has a polynomial bound on average but not pointwise. For this reason, it suffices to cite
[
PTBZ25
, Lemma B.4]
in all applications we are aware of. Nevertheless, we have opted to include a proof of Lemma
3.1
because it fits thematically with our focus on leveraging the twisted average.

We also note that
[
PTBZ25
, Lemma B.4]
is stated for an arbitrary smoothing function, and Lemma
3.1
can certainly be proven at this level of generality using a similar argument.

Proof of Lemma

.

This result is proved, as usual, by shifting the contour in Perron’s formula

A_{N}^{k}(X)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}ds,

which holds for each integer

and each

.

In order to avoid the need for pointwise bounds for
|L(s,N)|
in this argument, we include an auxiliary average in Perron’s formula

A_{N}^{k}(X)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi iT}\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}dsdu.

As stated, this is trivial because the variable of integration
u
does not actually appear in the integrand. However, when we shift the contour to the left we shift in a way that depends on
u
:

\displaystyle\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}=

\displaystyle\int_{C}+\underbrace{\int_{c-i\infty}^{c-iu}+\int_{c+iu}^{c+i\infty}}_{\text{outer vertical pieces}}+\underbrace{\int_{c-iu}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta-iu}+\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iu}^{c+iu}}_{\text{horizontal pieces}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta-iu}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta-iT_{0}}+\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iu}}_{\text{inner vertical pieces}}+\underbrace{\int_{\gamma}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{small detour}\\
\text{around poles}\end{subarray}}.

The integral over
C
is independent of the variable
u
, seeing only the residues, while each of the remaining integrals are part of the shifted contour which is depicted in
Figure

. The auxiliary average now behaves as an average over several different ways of shifting the contour integral.

3.0.1.
The outer vertical integrals

In this section we evaluate

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi iT}\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{c\pm iu}^{c\pm i\infty}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}dsdu.

Restricting to

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{1}{2\pi iT}\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{c\pm iu}^{c\pm i\infty}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}dsdu\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll_{N,X,c,k}\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\left\lvert\int_{\pm u}^{\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t^{k+1}}dt\right\rvert du

\displaystyle\ll_{N,X,c,k}T^{1-k},

which tends to
0
as
T\to\infty
. Thus, the outer vertical integrals vanish in the limit.

3.0.2.
The horizontal integrals

In this section we evaluate

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi iT}\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iu}^{c\pm iu}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}dsdu.

The main idea here is that the contour integral is along a real line while the auxiliary average is essentially an integral along the imaginary line, so that the order of integration can be painlessly swapped.

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{1}{2\pi iT}\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iu}^{c\pm iu}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}dsdu\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi T}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{c}X^{\sigma+k}\left\lvert\int_{T}^{2T}L(\sigma\pm iu,N)\frac{\Gamma(\sigma\pm iu)}{\Gamma(\sigma\pm iu+k+1)}X^{\pm iu}du\right\rvert d\sigma.

The inner integral is bounded using integration by parts. For simplicity’s sake let

f_{k,\sigma}(u):=\frac{\Gamma(\sigma\pm iu)}{\Gamma(\sigma\pm iu+k+1)}=\frac{1}{(\sigma\pm iu)(\sigma\pm iu+1)\cdots(\sigma\pm iu+k)}

so that

f_{k,\sigma}^{\prime}(u)=\frac{\mp i}{(\sigma\pm iu)(\sigma\pm iu+1)\cdots(\sigma\pm iu+k)}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{\sigma\pm iu+j}.

We can directly bound each of these by

\displaystyle|f_{k,\sigma}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq u^{-k-1},

\displaystyle|f_{k,\sigma}^{\prime}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq ku^{-k-2}.

Taking these bounds together with the bound

\int_{T_{0}}^{T}L(\sigma\pm it,N)Z^{\pm it}dt\ll T^{\widetilde{\eta}}

we can apply integration by parts to show that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}L(\sigma\pm iu,N)X^{\pm iu}f_{k,\sigma}(u)du\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{T}f_{k,\sigma}(u)u^{\widetilde{\eta}}\Big|_{T}^{2T}+\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}u^{\widetilde{\eta}}f_{k,\sigma}^{\prime}(u)du

\displaystyle\ll T^{\widetilde{\eta}-k-2}.

Taking the outer integral over
\sigma
, we have now proven

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{1}{2\pi iT}\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iu}^{c\pm iu}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}dsdu\right\rvert\ll T^{\widetilde{\eta}-k-2},

allowing the implied constant to depend on
X
. Restricting to
k\geq\widetilde{\eta}-2
, this term also vanishes as
T
tends towards infinity.

3.0.3.
The inner vertical integrals

Using integration by parts as in the previous section, we know that integral
\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm i\infty}
converges absolutely when
k\geq\widetilde{\eta}-2
, similarly to the horizontal integrals.

In order to evaluate the limit, we switch the order of integration

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iu}

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+i2T}\int_{\max\{t,T\}}^{2T}

Recalling that the integrand is independent of the variable of integration
u
for the auxiliary average, we can directly evaluate the inner integral to get

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+i2T}\left(2-\max\{t/T,1\}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT}+\left(\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+i2T}\left(2-\max\{t/T,1\}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT}+O\left(\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+i2T}\right).

The limit of the first term converges to
\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm i\infty}
, while the integral in the big-oh is contained in the tail of this improper integral, so it tends to
0
in the limit. Thus we have shown

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iu}=\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+i\infty}.

The analogous result holds for the negative imaginary part integral by the same argument. Thus,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\left(\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iu}+\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta-iu}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta-iT_{0}}+\int_{\gamma}\right)=\int_{(\sigma_{a}-\delta)}^{*}.

Combined with the above limits, this concludes the proof.

4.
Proof of the Main Theorem via Unsmoothing

For any
y>0
, define the difference and integral operators

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}f

\displaystyle:x\mapsto f(x+y)-f(x)

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}^{(k)}=\underbrace{\Delta_{y}\circ\cdots\circ\Delta_{y}}_{k}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}f

\displaystyle:x\mapsto\int_{x}^{x+y}f(u)du

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}=\underbrace{\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}\circ\cdots\circ\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}}_{k}.

The key insight to Landau’s method of finite differencing is that

y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}A_{N}^{k}(X)\approx A_{N}^{0}(X)=N(X),

where the error in this approximation can be controlled. We will largely follow Landau’s method in a similar vein to
[
Rou11
,
Alb24b
]
, where we use integration by parts to incorporate the average bounds in place of pointwise bounds. We take additional care to track the dependence on various parameters during the course of the argument, similar to
[
LDTT22
]
.

4.1.
Unsmoothing

We separate the integrals appearing in Lemma
3.1
into outer vertical pieces and inner vertical pieces:

\displaystyle\int_{C}+\int_{(\sigma_{a}-\delta)}^{*}=

\displaystyle\int_{C}+\underbrace{\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta-i\infty}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta-iT}+\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+i\infty}}_{\text{outer vertical pieces}}+\underbrace{\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta-iT}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta-iT_{0}}+\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+iT}}_{\text{inner vertical pieces}}+\underbrace{\int_{\gamma}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{small detour}\\
\text{around poles}\end{subarray}}.

In the following subsections, we will bound the contribution of

y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[\int_{j}\cdots\right]

for each piece
\int_{j}
of the contour appearing above, which all together will give an asymptotic expansion for
y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}A_{N}^{k}(X)
and correspondingly for
N(X)
. The values for
y
,
k
, and
T
will be chosen optimally at the end of this section to prove Theorem
2.1
.

4.1.1.
The main terms

In this section we evaluate the expression

y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}ds\right],

which will correspond to the residue terms in Theorem
2.1
, i.e. the main asymptotic terms. This term is exactly the same as the corresponding integral appearing in
[
CN62
,
Rou11
,
LDTT22
,
Alb24b
]
, where we adopt notation similar to that in
[
CN62
,
LDTT22
]
,

S_{N,\alpha}^{k}(X):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{\alpha}}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}ds,

and it will suffice to analyze it in the same way. We include the full argument because the proof is short and we can be careful to keep track of the relevant dependencies.

The difference operator commutes with the integrals, so that

y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{k}(X)\right]=y^{-k}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[X^{s+k}\right]ds.

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus implies that
\Delta_{y}^{(k)}f(X)=\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}f^{(k)}(X)
. Differentiating
k
times and pulling the integral operator back to the outside gives

\displaystyle=y^{-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}L(s,N)\frac{X^{s}}{s}ds\right]=y^{-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}\left[S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)\right].

For any function
f(x)
, the mean value theorem implies

\displaystyle\left\lvert y^{-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}[f(u)]-f(X)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert y^{-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}[f(u)-f(X)]\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq ky\cdot\max_{x\in[X,X+ky]}|f^{\prime}(x)|.

In our case,
S_{N,\alpha}^{0}(X)=\sum X^{z}R_{z}(\log X)
. The derivative of any single term is

\frac{d}{dX}[X^{z}(\log X)^{n}]=X^{z-1}(\log X)^{n}\left(z+\frac{n}{\log X}\right)\ll_{z,n}X^{z-1}(\log X)^{n}.

Thus, we can bound
|(S_{N,\alpha}^{0})^{\prime}(x)|\ll x^{-1}R_{N,\alpha}(x)
.

This implies the
\int_{C}
term satisfies

\left\lvert y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{k}(X)\right]-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)\right\rvert\ll ky\left(\max_{x\in[X,X+ky]}x^{-1}R_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}(x)\right),

where the implied constant depends only on the locations and orders of the poles of
L(s,N)/s
.

4.1.2.
The inner vertical integrals

In this section we bound

y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}ds\right].

Once again, the difference operator commutes with the integrals so that the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus implies the above term is equal to

=y^{-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT}L(s,N)\frac{X^{s}}{s}ds\right].

Integration by parts implies

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT}L(s,N)X^{it}\frac{1}{s}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm it}\left(\int_{T_{0}}^{t}L(\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iy,N)X^{\pm iy}dy\right)\Big|_{T_{0}}^{T}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int_{T_{0}}^{T}\left(\int_{T_{0}}^{t}L(\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iy,N)X^{\pm iy}dy\right)\frac{1}{(\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm it)^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq QT^{\eta-1}(\log T)^{\beta}+Q\int_{T_{0}}^{T}t^{\eta-2}(\log t)^{\beta}dt.

We give separate bounds for the integral, depending on the value of
\eta
. If
\eta<1
we bound the integral as is, but if
\eta\geq 1
we bound the integral above by
\int_{e}^{T}
. These together imply

\displaystyle\leq QT^{\eta-1}((\log T)^{\beta}+g_{\eta,\beta}(T))+Qh_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0}),

where
g_{\eta,\beta}(u)
and
h_{\eta,\beta}(u)
are given by

\displaystyle g_{\eta,\beta}(u)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(\log u)^{\beta+1}&\eta=1\\
\frac{1}{|\eta-1|}(\log u)^{\beta}&\eta\neq 1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle h_{\eta,\beta}(u)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{|\eta-1|}u^{\eta-1}(\log u)^{\beta}&\eta<1\\
1&\eta=1\\
\frac{1}{|\eta-1|}e^{\eta-1}&\eta>1.\end{cases}

Plugging this in gives the bound

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT_{0}}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT}L(s,N)\frac{X^{s}}{s}dsdu\right\rvert\leq X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}\left(QT^{\eta-1}((\log T)^{\beta}+g_{\eta,\beta}(T))+Qh_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0})\right).

We can bound the integrals
\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}f(X)\leq y^{k}\sup\{|f(x)|:X\leq x\leq X+ky\}
. This implies the contribution of the inner vertical pieces is

\leq\left(\max_{x\in\{X,X+ky\}}x^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}\right)\left(QT^{\eta-1}((\log T)^{\beta}+g_{\eta,\beta}(T))+Qh_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0})\right),

as
x^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}
is monotonic so that the maximum value is necessarily achieved at one of the endpoints.

4.1.3.
The outer vertical integrals

In this section we bound

y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm iT}^{\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm i\infty}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}ds\right].

This time we do not move the difference operator, and instead bound the integral directly analogously to how we bounded the horizontal integrals during the proof of
Lemma

3.1
. Recall the notation
f_{k,\sigma}
from that argument.

Applying integration by parts to the inner integral, we find that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{T}^{\infty}L(\sigma_{a}-\delta\pm it,N)X^{\pm it}f_{k,\sigma_{a}-\delta}(u)ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq f_{k,\sigma_{a}-\delta}(t)Qt^{\eta}(\log t)^{\beta}\Big|_{T}^{\infty}+\int_{T}^{\infty}Qy^{\eta}(\log y)^{\beta}f_{k,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq QT^{\eta-k-1}(\log T)^{\beta}+\frac{k}{k+1-\eta}QT^{\eta-k-1}(\log T)^{\beta}.

Restricting to
k\geq 3\eta-3
, the fraction is bounded above by
3/2
.

Plugging this in to the outer integral and noting that
\frac{5}{4\pi}<1
we produce the upper bound

\leq QX^{\sigma_{s}-\delta+k}T^{\eta-k-1}(\log T)^{\beta}.

Bounding the difference operator shows that this integral piece is bounded above by

\leq 2^{k}y^{-k}Q\left(\max_{x\in\{X,X+ky\}}x^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+k}\right)T^{\eta-k-1}(\log T)^{\beta}.

4.1.4.
The small contour avoiding poles

In this section we bound

y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}L(s,N)\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+k+1)}X^{s+k}dsdu\right].

Similar to previous sections, we can move the difference operator inside the integrals, apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, then pull the integral operator to the outside to get

=y^{-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}L(s,N)\frac{X^{s}}{s}ds\right].

The contour
\gamma
can be chosen bounded independent of
X
,
T
,
y
, and
k
, so this term is bounded above by

\displaystyle\leq y^{-k}\widetilde{\Delta}_{y}^{(k)}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\sup_{s\in\gamma}\left(|L(s,N)|\frac{X^{{\rm Re}(s)}}{s}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\gamma\\
x\in[X,X+ky]\end{subarray}}\left(|L(s,N)|\frac{x^{{\rm Re}(s)}}{s}\right).

We chose
\gamma
to be entirely to the left of the line
{\rm Re}(s)=\sigma_{a}-\delta
, so this term is further bounded by

\leq\sup_{s\in\gamma}\left\lvert\frac{L(s,N)}{s}\right\rvert\left(\max_{x\in\{X,X+ky\}}x^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}\right).

4.1.5.
Conclusion of the contour shifting

Taking all the pieces of the shifted contour together, we have proven that

\left\lvert y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[A_{N}^{k}(X)\right]-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)\right\rvert

is bounded above in absolute value by

\displaystyle\ll ky\left(\max_{x\in[X,X+ky]}x^{-1}R_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}(x)\right)+2^{k}y^{-k}(X+ky)^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+k}T^{\eta-k-1}(\log T)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\left(\max_{x\in\{X,X+ky\}}x^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}\right)Q\left(T^{\eta-1}((\log T)^{\beta}+g_{\eta,\beta}(T))+h_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\sup_{s\in\gamma}\left\lvert\frac{L(s,N)}{s}\right\rvert\left(\max_{x\in\{X,X+ky\}}x^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}\right),

where we have picked up the constraints
k\geq 2
and
k\geq 3\eta-3
and the implied constant depends only on the locations and orders of the poles of
L(s,N)/s
.

4.2.
The error bound before optimization

Suppose now that
N(X)
is nondecreasing. One has the bounds

A_{N}^{0}(X)\leq y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[A_{N}^{k}(X)\right]\leq A_{N}^{0}(X+ky)

as in
[
CN62
, Equation 4.15]
. On the one hand, this implies

A_{N}^{0}(X)-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)\leq y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[A_{N}^{k}(X)\right]-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X).

On the other hand, if we restrict to
ky<X
then

\displaystyle A_{N}^{0}(X)-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)

\displaystyle\geq y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[A_{N}^{k}(X-ky)\right]-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)

\displaystyle=y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[A_{N}^{k}(X-ky)\right]-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X-ky)+O\left(ky\max_{x\in[X-ky,X]}x^{-1}R_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}(x)\right).

The restriction to
ky<X
is necessary for
S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X-ky)
to be well-defined, as
(X-ky)^{s}
for complex numbers
s
is only well-defined if
X-ky>0
.

By including a change of variables between
X
and
X-ky
, we conclude that

A_{N}^{0}(X)-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)

is bounded in absolute value by

\displaystyle\ll ky\left(\max_{x\in[X-ky,X+ky]}x^{-1}R_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}(x)\right)+2^{k}y^{-k}(X+ky)^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+k}T^{\eta-k-1}(\log T)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\left(\max_{x\in\{X-ky,X+ky\}}x^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}\right)Q\left(T^{\eta-1}((\log T)^{\beta}+g_{\eta,\beta}(T))+h_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0})\right)

\displaystyle+\sup_{s\in\gamma}\left\lvert\frac{L(s,N)}{s}\right\rvert\left(\max_{x\in\{X-ky,X+ky\}}x^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}\right).

We slightly strengthen our constraint to
ky\leq\frac{1}{2}X
, so that the maximums can be more easily bounded. All together, we have proven the following unoptimized error bound:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
N
be a nondecreasing function which satisfies the assumptions of
Section

2.1
. For each integer
k\geq\max\{2,\widetilde{\eta}-2,3\eta-3\}
, each real number
T\geq T_{0}
, and each real number
y>0
for which
ky\leq\frac{1}{2}X
, the difference

A_{N}^{0}(X)-S_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}^{0}(X)

is bounded in absolute value by

\displaystyle\ll kyX^{-1}R_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}(X)+3^{k}y^{-k}X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta+k}T^{\eta-k-1}(\log T)^{\beta}+2^{\delta}QX^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}T^{\eta-1}\left((\log T)^{\beta}+g_{\eta,\beta}(T)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+2^{\delta}\left(Qh_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0})+\sup_{s\in\gamma}\left\lvert\frac{L(s,N)}{s}\right\rvert\right)X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta},

where the implied constant depends only on
\sigma_{a}
and the locations and orders of the poles of
L(s,N)/s
.

The dependence on
\sigma_{a}
is not too complicated, coming from the factors like
(\frac{3}{2}X)^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}
, which we have hidden in the implied constant only to simplify the expression as much as possible. This is not so significant a burden, as
N
nondecreasing implies that the abscissa of absolute convergence of
L(s,N)
is also a pole of
L(s,N)
. For this reason,
\sigma_{a}
can often be taken to be part of the data of “locations of the poles”.

If
N
is not nondecreasing, but
|N(X)|\leq\widehat{N}(X)
for some nondecreasing function
\widehat{N}
satisfying all the hypotheses in
Section

2.1
with the same parameters, a similar argument proves the analogous result to
Theorem

4.1
with a sum of the contribution from both
N
and
\widehat{N}
. The key step is
[
Rou11
, Proposition 16.16]
(also stated as
[
Alb24b
, Proposition 6.3]
), which implies that

y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}A_{N}^{k}(X)-A_{N}^{0}(X)=O\left(y^{-k}\Delta_{y}^{(k)}\left[A_{N}^{k}(X)-A_{N}^{k}(X-ky)\right]\right).

Tracing through the proof shows that the implied constant can be taken equal to
1
.

4.3.
Optimizing the error

We optimize the error bound in the case that
N
is nondecreasing. The case that
|N(X)|\leq\widehat{N}(X)
for some nondecreasing
\widehat{N}
is completely analogous.

Now we choose values for
y
and
k
in
Theorem

4.1
in order to conclude Theorem
2.1
. One can write down the optimal choices for one parameter at a time, but these get increasingly complicated and in particular make the constraints difficult to check. We opt to make simpler choices that are optimal up to order of magnitude to avoid these issues.

We first choose

y=\frac{3}{k}XT^{-1}.

This satisfies the constraint
ky\leq\frac{1}{2}X
if and only if
T\geq 6
. Plugging this in to the error bounds in
Theorem

4.1
, we give an error bound of the form

\displaystyle\ll T^{-1}R_{N,\sigma_{a}-\delta}(X)+k^{k}X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}T^{\eta-1}(\log T)^{\beta}+2^{\delta}QX^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}T^{\eta-1}\left((\log T)^{\beta}+g_{\eta,\beta}(T)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+2^{\delta}\left(Qh_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0})+\sup_{s\in\gamma}\left\lvert\frac{L(s,N)}{s}\right\rvert\right)X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}.

This has almost completely removed the dependence on
k
, save for the constant
k^{k}
. It then suffices to take
k
as small as possible, which is
k=\lceil\max\{2,\widetilde{\eta}-2,3\eta-3\}\rceil
.

Theorem

2.1
in the cases that
\eta=1
and
\eta>1
follows after factoring out
X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}
from the error terms and evaluating
g_{\eta,\beta}(T)
and
h_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0})
. In case
\eta<1
, we take a limit as
T\to\infty
to produce the bound

\ll 2^{\delta}\left(Qh_{\eta,\beta}(T_{0})+\sup_{s\in\gamma}\left\lvert\frac{L(s,N)}{s}\right\rvert\right)X^{\sigma_{a}-\delta}.

In fact, as the implied constant is independent of
T_{0}
we can also take
T_{0}\to\infty
, proving the
\eta<1
case of Theorem
2.1
.

5.
Counting Abelian Number Fields

We are now ready to prove Corollary
1.3
. For any abelian group
G
, write
G_{d}
for the set of elements of order exactly
d
. The generating Dirichlet series of
G
-étale discriminants over
\mathbb{Q}
is given by

D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)=F(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\left(\sum_{d}|G_{d}|\mathbf{1}_{1,d}(p)p^{-|G|(1-1/d)s}\right),

where
\mathbf{1}_{a,d}
is the characteristic function of the set of integers which are congruent to
a\pmod{d}
and
F(s)
is a Dirichelt polynomial given by the finite product of contributions from wild primes (and so is bounded on the right half-plane
{\rm Re}(s)>0
). This follows directly from work of Wright
[
Wri89
]
, as well as
[
Alb24b
, Section 3]
, due to
\mathbb{Q}
having trivial class group and
\mathbf{1}_{1,d}(p)
picking out exactly which primes can be ramified in an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with tame inertia group of order
d
. The sum over
d
is supported on
d
dividing the exponent of
G
, so for the remainder of this section we keep this restriction.

5.1.
Meromorphic Continuation

We require an explicit meromorphic continuation of
D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)
. This is done by
[
Alb24b
, Corollary 3.3]
up to the half-plane
{\rm Re}(s)>1/2a(G)
. However, in order to get square root savings we require a meromorphic continuation to
{\rm Re}(s)\geq 1/2a(G)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G
be an abelian group. If
\ell
is the smallest prime dividing
|G|
, then
D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)
is equal to

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{d>1}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}\left(|G|(1-1/d)s\right)^{\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})}(2a(G)s)^{-\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})}(2a(G)s)^{-\frac{2\ell-3}{2\ell-2}|G_{\ell}|^{2}+\frac{\ell-2}{2}|G_{\ell}|}H(s),

where
H(s)
is an Euler product which converges absolutely on the right half-plane
{\rm Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2a(G)+1}
.

We note that if
\ell=2
then
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})}=\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})}=\zeta
, so the corresponding terms may be combined. The process of meromorphically continuing Euler products by factoring out powers of
L
-functions is outlined in the expository paper
[
Alb24a
]
. We feel it is clearest to give a direct proof of
Proposition

5.1
in this case, but one can alternatively apply
[
Alb24a
, Corollary 14.5]
to reproduce the same result using a recursive formula for the powers of
L
-functions that appear.

Proof.

Throughout this argument, we let
H(s)
denote an Euler product which converges absolutely on the right half-plane
{\rm Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2a(G)+1}
. In particular,
H(s)
need not denote the same such function in every instance, and is allowed to absorb constants and other absolutely convergent Euler products on this region.

This can be checked directly by computing the Euler factors of

D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)\left(\prod_{d>1}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}\left(|G|(1-1/d)s\right)^{-\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})}(2a(G)s)^{\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})}(2a(G)s)^{|G_{\ell}|^{2}\frac{2\ell-3}{2\ell-2}-|G_{\ell}|\frac{\ell-2}{2}}.

Indeed, for
d>\ell
dividing
|G|
, we note that
|G|(1-1/d)
is an integer which satisfies

\displaystyle|G|(1-1/d)

\displaystyle<2|G|(1-1/\ell)=2a(G)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow|G|(1-1/d)<2a(G)+1

\displaystyle 2|G|(1-1/d)

\displaystyle>2|G|(1-1/\ell)=2a(G)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 2|G|(1-1/d)\geq 2a(G)+1

Multiplying out the Euler product for the Dedekind zeta functions
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}
gives

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}(|G|(1-1/d)s)^{-\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}=H(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\left(1-|G_{d}|\mathbf{1}_{1,d}(p)p^{-|G|(1-1/d)s}+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\right),

Taking advantage of the fact that
d>\ell
implies
|G|(1-1/d)+a(G)\geq 2a(G)+1
, this implies

D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)\left(\prod_{d>\ell}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}\left(|G|(1-1/d)s\right)^{-\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}\right)=H(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\left(1+|G_{\ell}|\mathbf{1}_{\ell}(p)p^{-a(G)s}+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\right).

It now suffices to consider the contribution of the prime
\ell
. It is natural to separate the proof in to two cases:
\ell=2
and
\ell\neq 2
.

Suppose first that
\ell=2
, so that
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2}+\zeta_{2}^{-1})=\mathbb{Q}
. Then

\displaystyle\zeta(a(G)s)^{-\frac{|G_{2}|}{\phi(2)}}=H(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\left(1-|G_{2}|p^{-a(G)s}+{|G_{2}|\choose 2}p^{-2a(G)s}+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\right).

Multiplying Euler factors implies

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)\left(\prod_{d\geq\ell=2}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}\left(|G|(1-1/d)s\right)^{-\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=H(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\left(1+\left({|G_{2}|\choose 2}-|G_{2}|^{2}\right)p^{-2a(G)s}+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\right).

Given that
{n\choose 2}-n^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n
, we conclude that

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)\left(\prod_{d\geq\ell=2}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}\left(|G|(1-1/d)s\right)^{-\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}\right)\zeta(2a(G)s)^{\frac{1}{2}|G_{2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|G_{2}|}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=H(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\left(1+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\right).

We conclude the proof in this case by noting that

-\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}-\frac{2\ell-3}{2\ell-2}|G_{\ell}|^{2}+\frac{\ell-2}{2}|G_{\ell}|=-\frac{1}{2}|G_{2}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|G_{2}|

when
\ell=2
.

Now suppose
\ell>2
. The proof is similar, except in this case the Euler factors of
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})}
are slightly more complicated.

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})}(a(G)s)^{-\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{\phi(\ell)}}

\displaystyle=H_{\ell}(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\Bigg(1-\phi(\ell)\mathbf{1}_{1,\ell}(p)p^{-a(G)s}+{\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\mathbf{1}_{1,\ell}(p)p^{-2a(G)s}-\frac{\phi(\ell)}{2}\mathbf{1}_{-1,\ell}(p)p^{-2a(G)s}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\Bigg)^{\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{\phi(\ell)}}

\displaystyle=H_{\ell}(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\Bigg(1-|G_{\ell}|\mathbf{1}_{1,\ell}(p)p^{-a(G)s}+\left({|G_{\ell}|/\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\phi(\ell)+{\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{\phi(\ell)}\right)\mathbf{1}_{1,\ell}(p)p^{-2a(G)s}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt-\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}\mathbf{1}_{-1,\ell}(p)p^{-2a(G)s}+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\Bigg).

Multiplying Euler factors implies

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)\left(\prod_{d\geq\ell}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}\left(|G|(1-1/d)s\right)^{-\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}\right)

\displaystyle=H(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\Bigg(1+\left({|G_{\ell}|/\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\phi(\ell)+{\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{\phi(\ell)}-|G_{\ell}|^{2}\right)\mathbf{1}_{1,\ell}(p)p^{-2a(G)s}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt-\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}\mathbf{1}_{-1,\ell}(p)+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\Bigg).

Given that
\mathbf{1}_{1,\ell}(p)+\mathbf{1}_{-1,\ell}(p)
is the characteristic function of the primes totally split in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})
, we conclude that

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)\left(\prod_{d\geq\ell=2}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}\left(|G|(1-1/d)s\right)^{-\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})}(2a(G)s)^{\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}}

\displaystyle=H(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\Bigg(1+\left({|G_{\ell}|/\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\phi(\ell)+{\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{\phi(\ell)}-|G_{\ell}|^{2}+\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}\right)\mathbf{1}_{1,\ell}(p)p^{-2a(G)s}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\Bigg).

Finally, multiplying in one more factor implies

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)\left(\prod_{d\geq\ell=2}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})}\left(|G|(1-1/d)s\right)^{-\frac{|G_{d}|}{\phi(d)}}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})}(2a(G)s)^{\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})}(2a(G)s)^{-\left({|G_{\ell}|/\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\phi(\ell)+{\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{\phi(\ell)}-|G_{\ell}|^{2}+\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=H(s)\prod_{p\nmid|G|}\left(1+O\left(p^{-(2a(G)+1)s}\right)\right).

The result then follows after noting that

{|G_{\ell}|/\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\phi(\ell)+{\phi(\ell)\choose 2}\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{\phi(\ell)}-|G_{\ell}|^{2}+\frac{|G_{\ell}|}{2}=-\frac{2\ell-3}{2\ell-2}|G_{\ell}|^{2}+\frac{\ell-2}{2}|G_{\ell}|.

∎

5.2.
Moments of Dedekind Zeta Functions

By Hölder’s inequality, we can bound the integral moment

\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;\sigma+it)|dt

solely in terms of the integral moments of Dedekind zeta functions

I_{m}(\sigma,\zeta_{K};T):=\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta_{K}(\sigma+it)|^{2m}dt.

We cite a few useful results bounding
I_{m}(\sigma,\zeta_{K})
. The first is due to Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan
[
CN63
]
, following from their construction of the approximate functional equation for general
L
-functions. We note that, while we only require upper bounds, they prove an asymptotic in many cases.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
CN63
, Theorem 4]
)
.

For each number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
and each integer
m\geq 0
,

I_{m}(\sigma,\zeta_{K};T):=\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta_{K}(\sigma+it)|^{2m}dt\ll\begin{cases}T&\sigma>1-\frac{1}{m[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\\
T(\log T)^{m^{2}[K:\mathbb{Q}]}&\sigma=1-\frac{1}{m[K:\mathbb{Q}]}.\end{cases}

Strictly speaking,
[
CN63
, Theorem 4]
is only stated for the case that
m=1
. The other integer values are proven similarly, as Chandresekharan and Narasimhan remark that an analogous approximate functional equation to
[
CN63
, Equation (65)]
holds for
\zeta_{K}(s)^{m}
. With the approximate functional equation in hand, the rest of the proof proceeds similarly to the
m=1
case.

It will also be useful to have sharp bounds at the edge of the critical strip. This is proven in large generality by Balasubramanian, Ivić, and Ramachandra
[
BIR93
]
. Once again, we note that while we only require an upper bound they prove an asymptotic.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
BIR93
, Theorem 3]
)
.

For each number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
, each complex number
z
, and each
\sigma\geq 1

I_{z}(\sigma,\zeta_{K};T)\ll T.

The case
\sigma>1
follows from absolute convergence, while the boundary case
\sigma=1
follows from
[
BIR93
, Theorem 3]
after noting that Brauer’s induction theorem implies any Artin
L
-function can be expressed as a product of complex powers (really just integer powers) of Hecke
L
-functions, i.e. functions in the set
S_{1}
defined by Balasubramanian, Ivić, and Rammachandra.

Finally, at the very boundary of what we can prove we are able to achieve slightly more by incorporating some pointwise bounds at the edge of the critical strip. We specifically apply the following result appearing in Tenenbaum’s book.

Lemma 5.4

(
[
Ten15
, Part II Theorem 3.22]
)
.

There exists a positive constant
c
such that, for
|t|\geq 3
and
\sigma\geq 1-c/\log|t|
, we have

|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^{-1}\ll\log|t|.

A similar result is likely true for Dedekind zeta functions by the same argument, although the author was unable to find a reference. We will only make use of this bound for the Riemann zeta function in our examples.

5.3.
Proofs of square root savings

We first consider the case
G=C_{3}
. In this case, it suffices to count
C_{3}
-étale algebras, as all but one
C_{3}
-étale algebra is in fact a field. This allows us to avoid using a sieve at the end of the argument.

Proof of
Corollary

1.3
for
G=C_{3}
.

Following
Proposition

5.1
and noting that
a(C_{3})=2
, the generating series for
C_{3}
-étale algebras is given by

D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{3};s)=\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(2s)\zeta(4s)^{-1}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(4s)^{-2}H(s),

where
H(s)
is an Euler product that converges absolutely on the region
{\rm Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2a(C_{p})+1}=\frac{1}{5}
. In particular,
|H(s)|
is uniformly bounded on the region
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2a(C_{p})}=\frac{1}{4}
.

Hölder’s inequality now implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{3};\sigma+it)|dt\ll

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(2(\sigma+it))|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta(4(\sigma+it))|^{-2}dt\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(4(\sigma+it))|^{-8}dt\right)^{1/4}.

for each
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{4}
.

In the range
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{4}
,
Lemma

5.2
implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})}(2(\sigma+it))|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}I_{1}(2\sigma,\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})};2T)\ll T(\log T)^{2}.

Next, for any
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{4}
,
Lemma

5.3
implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta(4(\sigma+it))|^{-4}dt

\displaystyle\ll I_{-2}(4\sigma,\zeta;4T)\ll T

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(4(\sigma+it))|^{-8}dt

\displaystyle\ll I_{-4}(4\sigma,\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})};4T)\ll T.

We have now shown that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{3};\sigma+it)|dt\ll

\displaystyle\left(T(\log T)^{2}\right)^{1/2}T^{1/4}T^{1/4}=T\log T.

for each
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{4}
.

The result then follows from
Theorem

1.1
by taking
\sigma_{a}=\frac{1}{a(C_{3})}=\frac{1}{2}
,
\sigma_{a}-\delta=\frac{1}{4}
,

, and

.
∎

\#\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{p};X)=c_{p-1}(C_{p})X^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+O\left(X^{\frac{p-2}{(p-1)^{2}}}(\log X)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\right)

for each odd prime

. However, for

The proof of
Corollary

1.3
for the even cyclic groups is similar to that of
C_{3}
, with essentially two differences: firstly, we need to sieve from étale algebras to number fields at the end of the argument. Second, the groups

∎

5.4.
Remarks on the constants
c_{d}(C_{n})

When counting étale algebras, the coefficients of each term in the asymptotic expansion are certainly given by special values of Dedekind zeta functions times convergent Euler products, as the generating Dirichlet series
D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(G;s)
is a product of Dedekind zeta functions times a convergent Euler product in a neighborhood of each pole. Thus, the coefficients
c_{d}(C_{n})
are finite sums of these types of constants as claimed in
Corollary

1.3
.

The more interesting question of “do these constants vanish” was partially answered in
[
Alb24b
]
. It turns out that each term appearing in
Corollary

1.3
is covered by one of the cases in
[
Alb24b
, Theorem 1.7]
, as the groups considered are not very complicated. In particular, if
n=3,4,8,16
, then each
g\in C_{n}
is either a generator or belongs to the Frattini subgroup. This implies that each index satisfies the conditions of
[
Alb24b
, Theorem 1.7(ii) or (iv)]
. If
n=2p
for
p\geq 3
, then
g\in C_{2p}=C_{2}\times C_{p}
satisfies one of the following:

(1)

g
is a generator of
C_{2p}
, or

(2)

\{h\in C_{2p}:\textnormal{ind}(h)<\textnormal{ind}(g)\}\subseteq\Phi(C_{2p})\cup C_{2p}[2]
.

This are exactly the conditions for
[
Alb24b
, Theorem 1.7(iii,iv)]
. By appealing to this result, we conclude the following:

Let
c_{n(1-1/d)}(C_{n})
be one of the constants appearing in
Corollary

1.3
corresponding to a divisor
d\mid n
. Then
c_{n(1-1/d)}(C_{n})\neq 0
if and only if

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\\
1<m<d\end{subarray}}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}\left(\frac{1-1/m}{1-1/d}\right)\neq 0.

In case
d=\ell
is the smallest prime dividing
n
, this is the empty product which always equals
1
, re-verifying that the leading term does not vanish.

By tracing through the surjective sieve, one can write down an explicit formula for these constants in terms of local étale algebras. It would be interesting to write a completely explicit formulation, which would require a detailed description of the discriminants of local étale algebras at wild places.

For individual small groups, we can refer to the LMFDB
[
LMF25
]
to compute explicit constants. To demonstrate the approximate shape of such results, we do this for
G=C_{4}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
c_{d}(C_{n})
denote the constants appearing in
Corollary

1.3
. Then

\displaystyle c_{2}(C_{4})

\displaystyle=\frac{28+\sqrt{2}}{24\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod{4}}\left(\frac{1+2p^{-3/2}-p^{-2}-2p^{-5/2}}{1-p^{-2}}\right)-\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}

\displaystyle c_{3}(C_{4})

\displaystyle=\zeta(2/3)L(1,\chi_{4})\left(\frac{3(4-2^{2/3})}{16}\right)\prod_{p\neq 2}\left(1-p^{-4/3}-p^{-2}+p^{-10/3}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod{4}}\left(\frac{1-p^{-4/3}-2p^{-5/3}-3p^{-2}+2p^{-7/3}+4p^{-8/3}+2p^{-3}-p^{-10/3}-2p^{-11/3}}{1-p^{-4/3}-p^{-2}+p^{-10/3}}\right)

Proof.

Using the complete list of
C_{2}
- and
C_{4}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
on
[
LMF25
]
, we can explicitly compute

D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{4};s)=\left(1+2^{-2s}+2\cdot 2^{-6s}+4\cdot 2^{-11s}\right)\prod_{p\neq 2}\left(1+p^{-2s}+(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-3s}\right),

where
\chi_{4}(p)=(-1/p)
is the quadratic character associated to
\mathbb{Q}(i)
. We can use this expression to directly calculate the residues of
D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{4};s)X^{s}/s
at the poles
s=1/2
and
s=1/3
.

Factoring out
\zeta(2s)
gives the explicit expansion

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{4};s)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2s)\left(1+2^{-2s}+2\cdot 2^{-6s}+4\cdot 2^{-11s}\right)\left(1-2^{-2s}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\prod_{p\neq 2}\left(1+p^{-2s}+(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-3s}\right)\left(1-p^{-2s}\right)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2s)\left(1-2^{-4s}+2\cdot 2^{-6s}-2\cdot 2^{-8s}+4\cdot 2^{-11s}-4\cdot 2^{-13s}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\prod_{p\neq 2}\left(1+(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-3s}-p^{-4s}-(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-5s}\right)

The Euler product converges absolutely at

, so we find that

\displaystyle c_{2}(C_{4})+c_{1}(C_{2})

\displaystyle=\underset{s=1/2}{\rm Res}\left(D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{4};s)\frac{X^{s}}{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{28+\sqrt{2}}{24\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod{4}}\left(\frac{1+2p^{-3/2}-p^{-2}-2p^{-5/2}}{1-p^{-2}}\right),

from which we conclude

\displaystyle c_{2}(C_{4})

\displaystyle=\frac{28+\sqrt{2}}{24\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod{4}}\left(\frac{1+2p^{-3/2}-p^{-2}-2p^{-5/2}}{1-p^{-2}}\right)-\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}.

Further factoring out

gives the expression

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{4};s)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2s)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(i)}(3s)\left(1-2^{-4s}+2\cdot 2^{-6s}-2\cdot 2^{-8s}+4\cdot 2^{-11s}-4\cdot 2^{-13s}\right)\left(1-2^{-3s}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\prod_{p\neq 2}\left(1+(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-3s}-p^{-4s}-(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-5s}\right)\left(1-(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-3s}+\chi_{4}(p)p^{-6s}\right)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2s)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(i)}(3s)\Big(1-2^{-3s}-2^{-4s}+2\cdot 2^{-6s}+2^{-7s}-2\cdot 2^{-8s}-2\cdot 2^{-9s}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+6\cdot 2^{-11s}-4\cdot 2^{-13s}-4\cdot 2^{-14s}+2\cdot 2^{-16s}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\prod_{p\neq 2}\Big(1-p^{-4s}-(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-5s}+\chi_{4}(p)p^{-6s}-2(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-6s}+(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-7s}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+2(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-8s}+(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-9s}-\chi_{4}(p)p^{-10s}-(1+\chi_{4}(p))p^{-11s}\Big)

The Euler product converges absolutely at

\displaystyle c_{3}(C_{4})=\underset{s=1/2}{\rm Res}\left(D_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C_{4};s)\frac{X^{s}}{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2/3)L(1,\chi_{4})\left(\frac{3(4-2^{2/3})}{16}\right)\prod_{p\neq 2}\left(1-p^{-4/3}-p^{-2}+p^{-10/3}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod{4}}\left(\frac{1-p^{-4/3}-2p^{-5/3}-3p^{-2}+2p^{-7/3}+4p^{-8/3}+2p^{-3}-p^{-10/3}-2p^{-11/3}}{1-p^{-4/3}-p^{-2}+p^{-10/3}}\right).

∎

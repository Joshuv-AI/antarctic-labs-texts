A comparative study of double occlusal splints combined with joint cavity injection and manual reduction in the treatment of anterior disc displacement without reduction versus anterior repositioning splints: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Chen Cheng, Wu-Chao Zhou, Si-Xiang Xie, Tie-Biao Wang, Zhou-Cheng Ouyang, Wei-Hong Xi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesSome patients with anterior disc displacement without reduction (ADDWoR) experience restricted mouth opening after manual reduction. This study aimed to compare the therapeutic efficacy of modified double occlusal splints with traditional anterior repositioning splints (ARS) in treating ADDWoR, to identify a simple, effective, and stable clinical approach.Materials and methodsA total of 107 patients were enrolled in this single-center retrospective study. Those using double occlusal splints after successful manual reduction were assigned to observation groups (Group A, Group B). Patients who wore traditional ARS were enrolled as the control groups (Group C, Group D). Group A (n = 29) and Group C (n = 25) corresponded to direct manual reduction, while Group B (n = 27) and Group D (n = 26) corresponded to manual reduction following intra-articular injection. The pain with visual analogue scale (VAS), maximum mouth opening (MMO), triction temporomandibular joint disorder index, and clinical efficacy ratings of the patients in each group were statistically analyzed before treatment, and at the first month, the third month, the sixth month after treatment. Using magnetic resonance imaging technology to observe the relative positional relationship between the articular disc and condylar before and after treatment.ResultsAt one-month post-treatment, groups A and B showed significant improvements in VAS, MMO, CMI, and DI compared to control groups C and D (P < 0.05). At three and six months, group A had significantly lower CMI and DI than group C, with no significant VAS or MMO difference. Group B showed greater improvement than group D across all parameters (P < 0.05). Magnetic resonance imaging confirmed higher disc reduction rates in the observation groups (χ2 = 3.928, P < 0.05). Clinical efficacy was 89.3% in experimental groups compared to 74.5% in control groups (χ2 = 3.990, P < 0.05).ConclusionDouble occlusal splints demonstrated superior stabilization following early manual repositioning and improved structural outcomes compared to ARS. These findings support their clinical value as a treatment option for ADDWoR.Clinical relevanceThis study demonstrates that modified double occlusal splints provide more effective and stable outcomes than traditional ARS, particularly after early manual disc repositioning. The findings offer clinicians a simplified, non-invasive treatment strategy that enhances long-term joint stabilization and functional recovery in ADDWoR patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-025-07398-w.

Introduction

Temporomandibular joint disorder (TMD affects both the masticatory muscles and the structure of the joint and can exert significant impact on a patient’s quality-of-life [1]. One common structural disorder of TMD is anterior disc displacement without reduction (ADDWoR), which has a reported prevalence ranging from 2% to 8% across various TMD illnesses [2]. ADDWoR typically develops from anterior disc displacement of the temporomandibular joint with reduction (ADDWR), which is categorized into acute and chronic stages based on whether the duration exceeds four months [3]. Despite a variety of treatment options, there is still no consensus on the management of ADDWoR.

There are differing opinions as to whether we should combine different therapeutic approaches and the optimal sequence of performing various treatments [4–6]. Although some researchers have proposed that manual reduction combined with an anterior repositioning splint (ARS) could effectively move the condyle forwards to prevent the articular disc from shifting forwards again during the mouth closing process [7, 8]. However, throughout clinical diagnosis and treatment, we have found that for some patients with chronic phase and acute ADDWoR with a slightly longer course, the articular disc will return to the original position in a short period of time following manual reduction. Moreover, some patients complained that they experienced re-locking again when they awoke in the morning. Currently, there is a lack of sufficient research on how we might reduce the occurrence of such phenomena. Another consideration is that due to the long production cycle and poor stabilization effect of traditional ARS, even for some patients, the discomfort in their front teeth may occur in the morning after wearing traditional ARS at night [9].

Therefore, in the present study, we modified the traditional ARS into double occlusal splints featuring a daytime-ARS (DARS) and a nighttime-ARS (NARS). ADDWoR was treated by direct manual reduction or by manual reduction after lavage of the joint cavity and the use of a repositioning splint. We then compared the efficacy of manual reduction combined with traditional ARS and double occlusal splints for the treatment of patients with ADDWoR.

Materials and methods

Ethics approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Stomatological Hospital of Nanchang University (Reference: 2022019). All participants provided signed and informed electronic consent in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant regulations and guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. This study does not require trial registration.

Patient recruitment

This study was a retrospective cohort study. We enrolled patients diagnosed with ADDWoR according to the diagnostic criteria of Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders (DC/TMD) [10] in the Affiliated Stomatological Hospital of Nanchang University between January 2022 and December 2023. After successful direct manual reduction (or manual reduction after joint cavity lavage injection), they patients wore double occlusal splints (DARS + NARS) or traditional ARS. The clinical efficacy of double occlusal splints and traditional ARS for the treatment of ADDWoR was then compared and analyzed. A total of 107 patients were included in the study. Those who wore double occlusal splints after successful manual reduction were classified as the observation group (for convenience of record, those who had successful direct manual reduction and wore double occlusal splints were referred to as Group A while those who had successful manual reduction after joint cavity lavage injection and wore double occlusal splints were referred to as Group B); those who wore traditional ARS were classified as the control group (those who had successful direct manual reduction and wore traditional ARS were referred to as Group C, and those who had successful manual reduction after joint cavity lavage injection and wore traditional ARS were referred to as Group D). The specific inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) diagnosis of ADDWoR based on the DC/TMD; (2) maximum mouth opening (MMO) < 37 mm, and (3) successful manual reduction directly or after arthrocentesis. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) osteoarthropathy; (2) acute joint trauma; (3) severe psychiatric and psychological disorders (The patients have difficulty in normal communication and cooperation during the treatment process, and have been rated as severely affected in the Symptom Checklist 90 (SCL-90) and Patient Health Questionnaire 9 (PHQ-9)); (4) uncontrolled periodontal disease; and (5) serious systemic diseases.

Treatment and outcome evaluation

The measurement indices included pain visual analogue score (VAS, According to the guidelines of the International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP), a multi-phase pain assessment is conducted using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS). The standardized tool features a 100 mm horizontal scale, with the zero-point indicating “no pain (0 points)” and the end point indicating “extremely severe pain (10 points)”. Patients independently completed the marking operation based on their subjective pain experience, measuring the distance (in mm) from the zero point to the marked point to reflect the pain intensity.) MMO (measure the distance between the cutting edges of the central incisors of the upper and lower jaws when the mouth is widest open, and express it in millimeters), craniomandibular index (CMI) [11], dysfunction index (DI), palpation index (PI) and clinical efficacy. The VAS, MMO, CMI, DI, PI and clinical efficacy ratings of the patients in each group were statistically analyzed before treatment, and at the first month, the third month, the sixth month after treatment, and clinical efficacy was evaluated at the sixth month after treatment. Clinical efficacy was classified into marked improvement, effective improvement and ineffective. Marked improvement referred to cases in which the local pain, joint area and surrounding muscle tenderness, and clicking sounds had basically disappeared, and the mouth opening degree had reached the normal range (37–45 mm). Effective improvement referred to cases in which the local pain, joint area and surrounding muscle tenderness, and clicking sounds had all been alleviated to varying degrees, and the mouth opening degree had improved compared to before treatment. Invalid referred to cases in which there were no significant changes or even aggravation in local pain, tenderness, joint clicking and mouth opening degree. MRI images were recorded before and six months after treatment in order to investigate the relative positions between the articular disc and condyle in both the open and closed positions. Following the measurement method of Fu et al., the angle between the posterior band of the articular disc and the condylar vertical line was measured. If the angle was within the range of −15° to + 15°, it was considered as a normal disc-condyle relationship. If the angle was greater than 15°, it was regarded as anterior disc displacement. The data was collected by two researchers and averaged. To ensure the accuracy and consistency of all data evaluations, before the formal data analysis began, we provided a unified training to the researchers involved in the data assessment. The training covered learning the measurement methods such as VAS, MMO, and CMI, and a standard consistency experiment was conducted. The Kappa value indicated that this strategy was reliable.

Double occlusal splints incorporated a Daytime-ARS (Fig. 1A) and a Nighttime-ARS (Fig. 1B). The production process of Daytime-ARS(DARS) was the same as that for traditional ARS. The Nighttime-ARS(NARS) required the first step of creating models of the patient’s upper and lower jaw teeth. Then, using dental membranes from the German AECT company, the model was formed on a vacuum pressure molding machine to make the upper and lower jaw retainers. The retainers were placed in the patient’s mouth, and the patient was instructed to extend their lower jaw until the incisors are aligned. The occlusal relationship of the upper and lower teeth was recorded with wax sheets. Then, the occlusal relationship was transferred to the jaw framework through plaster models and wax sheets. We used the three-point retention principle, that is, by using 0# self-curing resin to bond and fix the upper and lower jaw retainers at the occlusal contact areas of the bilateral upper and lower first molars and the occlusal contact areas of the upper and lower central incisors. The production process for NARS is shown in Figure S1A-B. The processes utilized for arthrocentesis and manual reduction are shown in Figure S2 and S3. Mouth opening increased significantly immediately (Fig. 2B). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed that the disc-condylar relationship was normal in the opening position (Fig. 3 B).

Statistical analysis

Data were statistically analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0 software (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA). The Shapiro-Wilk test method was used to detect whether data were normally distributed. Among baseline data, age and gender did not follow a normal distribution, while disease duration approximated a normal distribution. VAS and MMO scores between groups did not follow a normal distribution, whereas CMI (DI, PI) scores did follow a normal distribution. The Levene test was used to detect whether the variance was homogenous. After completing the tests for normality of the data and homogeneity of variance, we used parametric tests or non-parametric tests, as appropriate. For baseline data and comparisons between groups regarding VAS and MMO, the Mann-Whitney U test or independent sample t-test was used; for comparisons between groups regarding CMI (DI, PI), repeated measures analysis of variance was employed. Changes and progress of the same group (VAS, MMO, CMI) at multiple time points before and after surgery were analyzed using paired sample t-test or Wilcoxon test. Inter-group comparisons were conducted using the χ2 test to evaluate treatment efficacy, the effective MRI joint disc reduction rate and the incidence of disc re-displacement. The main null hypothesis (H0) analyzed was that the event risk ratio (RR) between the observation groups and the control groups were equal to 1, and its 95% confidence interval was calculated. The significance level was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Patient clinical characteristics

As shown in Tables 1, 107 ADDWoR patients with 124 joints were included in the study, with a male to female ratio of approximately 1:4. There were no significant differences between the observation groups (group A and B) and the control groups (groups C and D) in terms of gender ratio, mean age, and the number of affected joints (P > 0.05). Furthermore, there were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of the baseline levels of VAS, MMO and CMI prior to treatment (P > 0.05). However, the mean duration of group B (1.95 ± 1.83 months) was longer than that of group A (1.09 ± 1.26 months). The mean duration of group D (1.78 ± 1.71 months) was longer than that of group C (1.06 ± 1.39 months) (P < 0.05).

MRI image analysis

Due to the fact that during postoperative follow-up, some patients refused to undergo MRI scans for various reasons (some because the cost was too high, and some because they were satisfied with the postoperative results and considered it unnecessary to take another MRI scan), it was not possible to collect all the pre- and post-operative MRI images of both the observation group and the control group. In this study, we compiled the MRI data of 58 patients, all of which were collected six months after the treatment, including 35 cases in the observation groups and 23 cases in the control groups. As shown in Fig. 6, we observed three treatment outcomes using MRI images. The first outcome included the restoration of disc displacement without reduction before treatment to the normal disc-condyle relationship (Fig. 6A and D), including 20 cases in the observation groups and 9 cases in the control groups (result(a), Table 3). The second outcome was the conversion from disc displacement without reduction before treatment to the disc displacement with reduction (Fig. 6B and E); there were seven cases in the observation groups and three cases in the control groups (result (b), Table 3). The third outcome was no change in the disc-condyle relationship before and after treatment (Fig. 6C and F); there were 8 cases in the observation groups and 11 cases in the control groups (result(c), Table 3). The first two outcomes (result (a), result (b)), as observed on MRI were effective in structure reduction (Fig. 6C and F). Clinical outcome assessment

Table S2 depicts clinical efficacy, 50 cases (89.3%) in the observation groups were effective and 38 cases (74.5%) in the control groups were effective; there was a significant difference in efficacy between these two groups (P< 0.05, RR1.20, 95%CI 1.00~1.44).

Discussion

At present, manual reduction and occlusal splints are the focus of attention for most scholars. Roy La Touche et al. [12] found that manual reduction significantly improved mouth opening and joint pain, however, it could not suggest that exercise significantly improves mouth opening in comparison to splints. Research reports showed that the treatment method of manual reduction combined with occlusal splint were superior to using the occlusal splints alone or undergoing physical therapy alone in terms of symptom improvement [7]. Unlike these studies, our research compared the efficacy of double occlusal splints combined with joint cavity injection and manual reduction with traditional ARS. The results showed that double occlusal splints were superior to the traditional ARS in terms of VAS, MMO, and improvement of temporomandibular joint function, especially for patients with severe intra-articular adhesions.

Furthermore, study showed good clinical outcomes with ARS in the short term, but low efficacy in maintaining a normal disc-condyle relationship over the long term [2, 14]. Our research indicated that for some patients with acute ADDWoR, after manual reduction, double occlusal splints following manual reduction improved symptoms more effectively (clinical efficacy rate 89.3%, efficiency of structural reduction 77.1%), offering a new clinical strategy to maintain disc-condyle stability. A previous study reported that 95% of ADDWoR symptoms resolved within three months [15], but structural restoration was rare, and worsening disc-condyle deformation could increase the risk of joint degeneration [16, 17]. Chronic disc displacement alters joint tissue composition, degrading fibers, proteins, and cartilage, which may lead to osteoarthropathy. In adolescents, this may impair condylar growth, causing facial asymmetry and malocclusion [18]. In the present study, all four groups of patients underwent early manual reduction to restore the normal mouth opening degree (Fig. 2), and MRI showed a normal disc-condylar relationship (Fig. 3). This intervention improved mouth opening and alleviated joint pain, thus resulting in effective clinical therapeutic effects. For patients with acute ADDWoR who have limited mouth opening, this seems to be the treatment method that is currently widely accepted by most scholars. A previous study identified a high proportion of patients with articular disc perforation who had ADDWoR [19]. These patients usually had a long course of illness, thus highlighting the importance of early intervention for these patients.

The efficacy of manual repositioning is influenced by the degree of disc displacement and the stage of development. Research has demonstrated that patients with acute ADDWoR who have suffered from this condition for less than four months generally have TMJ discs with well-preserved morphology, structure, and function [20]. However, as inflammation progresses, cytokines and proteases promote soft tissue degradation [21], and synovial proliferation reduces fluid quality, increasing joint friction and adhesion risk [22]. In these cases, manipulation faces stronger resistance. Clinicians must overcome muscle resistance with skillful traction. For high-resistance patients, verbal instruction and timing, combined with supra-articular lavage and sodium hyaluronate injection, can improve outcomes [23, 24].

Supra-articular lavage is advantageous in several respects. First, this method helps to loosen small adhesions in the joint cavity, improve the intra-articular environment, removes inflammatory substances and pain factors (such as matrix metalloproteinases13 (MMP-13) and interleukin-6 (IL-6)) [25], restore normal intra-articular pressure, and reduce the exudation of inflammatory fluid. Secondly, supra-articular lavage eliminates the ‘adsorption effect’ and reduces the pressure in the joint cavity [26]. When the load on the TMJ is relieved or removed following irrigation, the middle band of the articular disc and the articular nodes separate. At this point, the injection of sodium hyaluronate gel can compensate for the deficiency of endogenous hyaluronic acid in the joint fluid, enhancing joint lubrication, reducing friction between the articular disc, condyle, and articular fossa, and increasing condyle mobility. These events initiate the manipulative reset of the anteriorly dislocated articular disc [27]. Manual reduction is not typically an urgent measure for some patients with obvious inflammatory exudation and severe tissue edema in the soft tissue surrounding the joint disc. Instead, hot compresses, physical therapy, and oral non-steroidal anti-inflammatory analgesics can be administered initially. Manual reduction and an occlusal splint can be employed when the initial inflammation is under control, and the edema has been reduced.

Although symptoms may improve even with recurrent disc displacement [28], failure to maintain disc-condyle alignment can accelerate joint degeneration [8, 29, 30]. Therefore, it is crucial that clinicians pay attention to maintaining the disc-condylar relationship following manual reduction. According to a previous clinical study, patients with acute ADDWoR should follow certain measures to maintain the reset joint disc within 24 h to one week after manual reduction [2]. It is important to restrict jaw movement to prevent recurrence of the joint ‘stuck’ phenomenon. Therefore, maintaining the disc-condylar relationship following reset is crucial if we are to ensure treatment efficacy [31]. In this study, we observed that the improvement of mouth opening and pain in the short-term after manipulation was slightly worse in the control groups than in the observation groups. Within one week of repositioning, 13 cases in the control groups reported being ‘stuck’ again; only three ‘stuck’ cases were recorded in the observation groups during follow-up (P = 0.01, RR 0.28, 95%CI 0.10 ~ 0.80,Table S1). For these patients, we promptly performed repositioning and instructed them to carry out opening exercises every day to ensure that they could wear the occlusal splints when they were able to open their mouths normally. In addition, these patients experienced a longer duration of symptoms (exceeding 3 months) and exhibited greater resistance to manipulation. This necessitated the use of a device with a higher level of stability for transition in the early stage.

For patients with ADDwoR, the period of 24–48 h after manual reduction is crucial for fixation. Following manual reduction, double occlusal splints can be manufactured chair-side to effectively prevent joint symptoms. This can also help to reduce the frequency of patient visits. During regular clinical visits, we observed that some patients who wore a traditional ARS experienced the recurrence of joint stiffness when they awoke in the morning, thus preventing them from opening their mouths. This was mainly attributed to poor sleeping posture and habits, as well as relaxation of the body muscles during sleep, thus leading to involuntary mandibular retraction [32, 33]. NARS utilizes retainers to stabilize the maxilla and mandible in the treatment position, which achieved by adjusting the position of the mandible. The treatment position should ensure a smooth opening and closing process without any sticking or clicking, and typically it was the incisor alignment position. After fixing the positions of the maxilla and mandible, NARS can prevent the loss of treatment position due to the involuntary backward movement of the lower jaw during sleep at night, effectively addressing the issue of stability following manual reduction. In addition, double occlusal splints can also help to prevent damage to the teeth and periodontal tissues caused by bad sleeping habits such as clenched teeth and bruxism, and allow for the upper and lower dentition to be connected as a whole, evenly distributing the occlusal force and maintaining a stable positional relationship. Although studies had shown that after the removal of ARS, the long-term efficiency of maintaining the normal disc-condyle relationship was relatively low (approximately 40%) [14], the improvement of symptoms and function remains the point that we need to focus on. While double occlusal splints maintained a good effect at 6 months after treatment in this study, the number of subjects was small, and the follow-up period was short. Therefore, in future work, it is necessary to increase the sample size and extend the follow-up period to support the relevant research.

In summary, as a single-center retrospective study, this work has methodological limitations. The population lacked diversity, no power analysis was conducted, and follow-up was short. Larger, multicenter, long-term studies are needed to confirm these findings. Additionally, the study’s retrospective design, non-randomized patient grouping and possible operator bias, which result in a low level of evidence. Improvements are necessary in future research.

Conclusions

Double occlusal splints are more beneficial than traditional ARS in terms of symptomatic relief for ADDWoR patients with successful manual reduction, particularly during the first month post-therapy. The efficacy of double occlusal splints was higher than that of traditional ARS. Following early manual reduction, double occlusal splints showed better stability in the jaw position, which is expected to lead to better long-term efficacy. In the future, multi-center randomized controlled trials should be carried out and the follow-up time can be extended to verify the clinical efficacy and stability of this technique; findings from such research will be conducive to the promotion and application of this strategy in clinical practice.

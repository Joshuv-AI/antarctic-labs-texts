Effects of Jinhua instant dark tea in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus: study protocol for a multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial
Authors: Ningsu Chen, Kai Zhao, Ye Xu, Yana Qi, Daomei Cheng, Mengnan Zhao, Dejiao Meng, Lei Shi, Jiajie Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundsThe global prevalence of diabetes is rising, with many patients experiencing challenges in medication adherence and adverse effects associated with synthetic drugs. Natural products have attracted interest as potential preventive or adjunctive therapies. Jinhua dark tea (JHTea), a naturally fermented Chinese tea, has shown promising glucose-lowering and lipid-modulating effects in vitro and animal studies, but robust clinical evidence is lacking. This study is the first large-scale, long-term randomized controlled trial (RCT) evaluating JHTea as an adjunctive therapy for type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM).MethodsThis multicenter, open-label parallel randomized controlled trial across three centers in China will enroll 264 participants aged 40–70 years with T2DM, excluding those with gastrointestinal diseases or acute diabetes-related complications. Participants will be randomized 1:1 to the following: (1) intervention Group: 1 g of JHTea three times daily plus current oral hypoglycemic agents for 12 months. (2) control Group: warm water plus current oral hypoglycemic agents alone, with no extra active intervention. The primary outcome is the change in hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) at 6 months. Secondary outcomes at 6 and 12 months include diabetes remission rate, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), 2-h postprandial glucose, triglyceride-glucose index (TyG), blood pressure, abdominal circumference, body mass index (BMI), and other metabolic parameters.DiscussionThis study aims to investigate the glycemic and lipid-modulating effects of Jinhua instant dark tea in patients with T2DM. The findings may support the adjunctive role of fermented Jinhua tea in diabetes management, providing a complementary strategy to conventional therapies.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2400080339. Backgrounds

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a prevalent chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from impaired insulin secretion and/or insulin resistance. Its global prevalence has tripled over the past three decades, driven largely by lifestyle changes associated with economic development [1]. In 2021, the International Diabetes Federation (IDF) estimated that diabetes affected 10.5% of adults aged 20–79 worldwide (536.6 million individuals), with T2DM accounting for over 90% of cases [2, 3]. China bears the largest burden globally, with a prevalence of 12.4%, impacting over 140 million individuals [4, 5].

Management of T2DM relies on lifestyle modifications combined with glucose-lowering medications, as emphasized in clinical guidelines [6–9]. Notably, some patients can achieve sustained glycemic control even after discontinuing medication—a state known as diabetes remission—following intensive combined interventions [10]. However, adherence to both medication regimens and lifestyle changes remains a major challenge. Globally, only 54% of T2DM patients consistently adhere to prescribed drugs, often due to side effects, polypharmacy, and cost [11, 12]. In China, the situation is more concerning, with only 32.9% of adults with diabetes implementing lifestyle changes or taking medication, and among these, merely half achieve adequate disease control [4]. This highlights a critical need for more acceptable and sustainable adjunctive strategies to improve outcomes.

Functional natural products, particularly widely consumed beverages like tea, are promising candidates for adjunctive therapy in chronic metabolic diseases. Jinhua instant dark tea (JHTea), also known as “gold flower tea” or Tibetan tea, is a distinctive Chinese fermented dark tea. It is rich in bioactive compounds including theabrownin, tea polysaccharides, and organic acids [13]. Preclinical studies focused on JHTea have revealed multi-targeted effects on glucolipid metabolism [14, 15]. In diabetic mice, a 28-day JHTea supplementation significantly improved glucose tolerance during an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), with notably lower blood glucose levels at 120 min and higher serum insulin levels compared to controls [14]. Mechanistic investigations revealed enhanced pancreatic islet morphology, including increased β-cell area, improved tissue integrity, and reduced α-cell area. The animal study in high-fat diet (HFD)-induced obese rats demonstrated that JHTea significantly downregulated adipocyte hyperplasia and ameliorated serum lipid profiles [15]. These metabolic benefits are thought to be mediated, in part, through modulation of the gut microbiota, leading to subsequent regulation of key metabolic pathways such as AMPK signaling, insulin signaling, and glycerophospholipid metabolism [16–20]. Furthermore, a small-scale human trial (n = 31) found that daily intake of 6 g of JHTea for 3 months significantly reduced blood glucose by 0.57 mmol/L and triglycerides by 0.93 mmol/L, along with a modest weight loss of 1.31 kg, without any reported adverse events [21].

These findings suggest JHTea has potential as a complementary approach for T2DM management, which may help overcome limitations of conventional therapies by providing a palatable, culturally acceptable, and easily integrable dietary intervention. Although promising preclinical evidence exists for dark tea in general, high-quality clinical trials evaluating the efficacy and safety of Jinhua instant dark tea in patients with T2DM are lacking. Existing human studies are limited to small-scale, short-term, or self-controlled before-after designs, and have not comprehensively assessed critical outcomes such as HbA1c, sustained glycemic control, or lipid metabolism. Therefore, this randomized controlled trial (RCT) aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of JHTea supplementation in T2DM patients, with primary focus on glycemic control, alongside secondary assessments of blood lipid profiles and diabetes remission rates.

Trial design

The trial is a multicenter open-label parallel randomized controlled trial with a 1:1 concealed allocation. Participants in the intervention group will receive 1 g/bag of JHTea to be dissolved in 50 mL of warm water three times daily for 12 months. Similarly, participants in the control group will receive a matched regimen of 50 mL of warm water alone, at the same frequency and duration. Both groups will continue their current oral hypoglycemic agents. The study flow diagram is shown in Fig. 1.

Patient and public involvement

We did not include patient or public representatives in the development of this protocol. The trial design and outcome selection were led by academic clinicians and methodologists only.

Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Study setting

The study will be conducted at three sites: one primary care center (Chadianzi Health Service Center) and two academic medical centers (Chengdu Medical College and its First Affiliated Hospital). This setting ensures access to a diverse T2DM population and allows us to assess the feasibility and effectiveness of tea-based dietary intervention in both community and specialized clinical environments. To minimize center-specific bias, each site will be required to recruit at least 50 patients. Prior to the formal trial, all centers will receive pre-training on standardized procedures to ensure consistent implementation across centers.

Interventions

Patients in the experimental group will receive JHTea for 12 months. They will take one bag (1 g/bag) of JHTea three times a day, administered within 30 min before meals dissolved in approximately 50 mL of warm water. The control group will receive 50 mL of warm water at the same frequency and timing, in addition to their regular medication regimen.

Participants in both groups will continue their existing glucose-lowering medications and receive appropriate treatment for conditions not listed in the exclusion criteria. However, they will not be allowed to take any additional traditional Chinese medicines, tea, dietary supplements, or other agents with potential glucose- or lipid-lowering effects during the study period.

Adherence and contamination control

To promote adherence, JHTea will be distributed monthly by trained research staff. Participants will return all used packaging and any remaining tea at each visit, allowing adherence to be quantified as the proportion of doses consumed relative to those dispensed. Participants demonstrating suboptimal adherence will receive standardized counseling, and an independent online support team will deliver daily reminders to reinforce compliance.

Participant retention will be further supported by a tiered incentive system, providing modest, non-cash rewards at the 3-, 6-, and 12-month milestones, with a completion bonus at the end of the study.

To minimize contamination, all participants will be informed during consent that those in the control group will receive JHTea after trial completion. This measure is expected to reduce external acquisition of the intervention and maintain the integrity of group allocation.

Feasibility of trial procedures

The proposed trial is deemed feasible based on recruitment capacity, infrastructure readiness, and intervention acceptability. Although a standalone pilot study has not been conducted, feasibility is supported by prior community-based intervention research and the operational experience of the participating centers.

Each of the three centers maintains established community networks and access to large populations with chronic conditions, ensuring that the recruitment target of approximately 88 participants per site can be achieved within the planned enrollment period. Each center has experience in conducting investigator-initiated and population-based clinical studies using standardized protocols, centralized data management, and trained research personnel. These features ensure procedural consistency and adherence to good clinical practice across sites.

Moreover, the study intervention, Jinhua Instant Dark Tea, is a culturally familiar and widely consumed beverage in China, which is expected to promote participant acceptance, compliance, and long-term adherence.

Participant timeline

Assessments will be conducted at baseline and all protocol-defined time points. Each participant will be followed up for 12 months, with scheduled visits at 3, 6, and 12 months. Additionally, 40 participants recruited from the Chadianzi Health Service Center will have an extra follow-up visit within 1–3 days after enrollment to evaluate short-term intervention effects. During this period, participants will uniformly receive diabetes-friendly meals provided by the Department of Clinical Nutrition at West China Hospital. The follow-up schedule is detailed in Fig. 2.

Sample size

To ascertain the sample size for our study, we based our calculations on glycated hemoglobin values from a systematic review conducted in 2022 [24]. Using a significance level (α) of 0.05 and a power (1 − β) of 0.8, assuming a detectable difference of 0.35% in HbA1c between the two groups with an expected standard deviation of 0.9%, and considering an equal allocation ratio (1:1), we employed PASS software to calculate a required sample size of 210 cases. To account for potential participant loss to follow-up, which was estimated at approximately 20%, our final calculated sample size was 264 patients.

Recruitment

Patients eligible for this trial will be informed about the study objectives, designs, methods, potential advantages, and limitations of the treatment. Participants will have the opportunity to ask questions to ensure they fully understand and can make a decision. Before randomization, written informed consent will be obtained from each patient.

Methods: randomization, concealment, and blinding

After informed consent is signed, participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to either the JHTea group or the control group. To ensure allocation concealment, randomization will be performed using a web-based EDC system by personnel not involved in the study. Random sequences were generated using randomized block lengths of 2 and 4, and competitive enrollment was implemented across research centers. The patient and physician will be informed about the allocated intervention after the randomization. Due to the open-label design, blinding of physicians and participants is not feasible; however, outcome assessor and statisticians will be blinded to group allocation.

Methods: data collection, management, and analysis

Data collection

Baseline information, including sex, date of birth, ethnicity, education level, occupation, marital status, smoking history, T2DM diagnosis duration, tea drinking behavior, alcohol history, dining habits, and concomitant medications and treatments will be recorded.

For accurate measurements of height and weight, participants will be instructed to remove shoes and hats, wear light clothing, and stand upright while looking straight ahead on a calibrated height-weight scale. BMI will be calculated by dividing weight (kg) by the square of height (m) [25]. Abdominal circumference will be measured with participants standing upright, feet shoulder-width apart, and breathing steadily. A tape measure will be placed at the midpoint between the lower edge of the costal arch and the upper edge of the iliac crest [26].

Blood pressure will be measured after a 30-min preparatory period during which participants will refrain from smoking, eating, or exercising. A validated electronic blood pressure monitor (Yuwell, YE680CR) will be used, with the cuff placed over the brachial artery in the antecubital fossa, ensuring one finger fits beneath it. Two measurements will be taken at one-minute intervals, and the average value will be recorded [27].

For laboratory assessment, including blood routine examination, glucose and lipid metabolism, liver function tests, and kidney function, researchers at each center will first provide subjects with pre-collection instructions. A professional third-party testing institution, the West China Medical Detection Institute, will oversee the collection, temporary storage, transportation, and testing of blood samples. Laboratory tests will be conducted using a standardized automatic biochemistry analyzer (Roche, cobas®8000).

Dietary intake and physical activity monitoring

To minimize potential confounding, participants’ dietary intake and physical activity will be monitored throughout the intervention period to ensure the stability of habitual dietary and exercise behaviors.

For dietary intake monitoring, participants will be required to complete a 3-day consecutive dietary record at baseline and at each scheduled follow-up visit. All meals will be documented using standardized portion plates provided by the study, with accompanying photographs taken according to standardized instructions.

For physical activity monitoring, participants’ physical activity will be assessed at baseline and at 3-, 6-, and 12-month follow-up visits using standardized questionnaires. Information collected will include exercise frequency, average session duration, types of activity (e.g., walking, resistance training) and perceived exertion levels, categorized into three levels: (1) no noticeable effort with steady breathing, (2) mild fatigue with comfortable speech, and (3) significant fatigue with shortness of breath. Self-reported changes in physical activity during the study will be also systematically documented.

Data management and confidentiality

The investigator responsible for participant follow-up will complete the case report form (CRF) and enter all data into a centralized electronic data capture (EDC) system. Before participant enrollment, all participating centers will undergo standardized training based on detailed standard operating procedures (SOPs) to ensure consistency in data collection and entry. Both CRFs and the EDC interface will be pre-programmed and standardized to maintain consistency across sites.

Data entry is restricted to authorized, trained personnel using unique, role-specific login credentials. The EDC system will automatically generate audit trails recoding all entries, modifications, and deletions, including timestamps and user identifiers, and maintain access logs to track all user activities. Regular monitoring, including both remote and on-site reviews, will be conducted by the trial coordination team at West China Hospital to verify EDC data against source documentation. Any discrepancies will be formally resolved through queried clarification, with all changes remaining traceable to original source records.

Laboratory data will be directly entered into the EDC system by the West China Medical Testing Institute. Human blood samples will be stored at the institute for 3 months after study completion and subsequently destroyed on-site with certification. All investigators receive training in data privacy and protection protocols. Access to study data is restricted to the research team during the trial. After trial completion, de-identified data will be made available for further analysis.

Data analysis

Statistical analyses will be conducted according to predefined analysis sets. The primary efficacy analysis will follow the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, including all randomized participants. A per-protocol (PP) analysis will serve as a sensitive analysis to assess the robustness of the primary findings. The safety population will include all participants who received at least one dose of the investigational product and have available safety data. No interim analysis for efficacy or futility are planned.

The primary outcome (HbA1c change from baseline to 6 months) will be analyzed using ANCOVA, adjusted for baseline values and study center, with results presented as least-squares mean differences and 95% confidence intervals. Secondary continuous outcomes (e.g., fasting plasma glucose, lipid profiles, blood pressure) will be analyzed using the same approach, while binary outcomes (e.g., adverse events) will be compared using risk differences and 95% confidence intervals from chi-square or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Pre-specified subgroup analyses will be performed based on baseline BMI (< 25 vs. ≥ 25 kg/m2) [28] and age (< 60 vs. ≥ 60 years) [29]. The interaction between treatment assignment and the subgroup factor will be tested within the ANCOVA model. Missing data in efficacy endpoints will be handled using multiple imputation by chained equations. No formal adjustment for multiple comparisons will be applied; secondary and subgroup analyses will be interpreted as exploratory.

Statistical significance will be defined by a two-sided alpha level of 0.05. All analyses will be performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 29.0.

Methods: data monitoring and adverse event management

Trial conduct will be audited monthly by the principal investigator. Monitoring will include data completeness, adherence to protocol, and reporting of deviations or adverse events.

Adverse events (AEs) monitored include the following: (1) gastrointestinal reactions (e.g., constipation, bloating, diarrhea, vomiting); (2) hypoglycemic episodes (≤ 3.9 mmol/L); (3) hyperglycemic episodes (≥ 16.7 mmol/L, with or without clinical symptoms such as hypotension, acidosis, or dehydration); and (4) clinically significant abnormalities in laboratory tests, including liver/kidney function, blood counts, and lipid profiles. All adverse events are systematically recorded. Serious adverse events (SAEs) will be reported to the Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) and the ethics committee within 24 h.

An independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will oversee trial safety throughout the study.an is in place. A predefined safety threshold has been established: if unexpected, intervention-related AEs occur in ≥ 5% of participants in any group, the DMC will convene an urgent review to determine whether continuation, modification, or termination of the trial is warranted. All AEs, regardless of attribution, are reported in real time to the DMC for independent evaluation. Participants will receive necessary medical care and follow-up for any adverse events related to the trial. Additionally, compensation will be provided for any harm suffered by participants as a direct result of trial participation. No formal interim efficacy analyses or predefined early stopping rules have been planned, reflecting JHTea’s long history of safe daily consumption. However, the DMC retains full authority to recommend protocol modification or early termination in response to emerging safety concerns.

Discussion

The management of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) remains challenging, with barriers such as suboptimal medication adherence and adverse effects of conventional therapies underscoring the need for complementary strategies. Dietary interventions, including tea consumption, represent a culturally acceptable and potentially sustainable adjunct to standard care.

This trial aims to provide the first large-scale randomized evidence on the efficacy and safety of Jinhua dark tea as an adjunctive strategy for T2DM management. Building on preclinical data, where dark teas rich in theabrownins, polysaccharides, and polyphenols improved glycemic control through mechanisms such as β-cell protection, anti-inflammatory effects, and modulation of gut microbiota, this study will test their clinical relevance in humans. If successful, Jinhua dark tea could offer a culturally acceptable, low-cost, and sustainable dietary adjunct to complement—not replace—pharmacological therapies. Importantly, its integration into routine care may enhance patient adherence to lifestyle interventions. Future research will be needed to elucidate mechanisms of action in humans and to test efficacy and safety in broader and more diverse populations, including insulin-treated patients and those with advanced disease.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the open-label design, inherent to lifestyle interventions, introduces risks of performance and detection bias. Although randomization is concealed, outcome assessor and statisticians are blinded and primary outcomes are laboratory-based, the absence of physician and participant blinding may still have influenced subjective responses. Second, adherence to the intervention remains a concern; despite monitoring with diaries and regular follow-up, variability in tea consumption could have affected treatment fidelity. Third, residual confounding from diet and physical activity cannot be excluded, even though participants are instructed to maintain habitual lifestyles and standardized assessments are performed. Finally, the generalizability of our findings is limited: the study population consisted of Chinese adults with T2DM managed with oral hypoglycemic agents and relatively stable glycemic control.

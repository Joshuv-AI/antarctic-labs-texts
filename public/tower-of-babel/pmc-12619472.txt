Baricitinib could improve the prognosis of anti-MDA5 antibody positive dermatomyositis associated interstitial lung disease
Authors: Shuai Zhu, Shanshan Li, Lipu Shi, Tianshu Chu, Zhenguo Huang, Xin Lu, Guochun Wang, Yongpeng Ge
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and objectiveCurrently, there are only a few effective treatments for anti-melanoma differentiation-associated gene 5 antibody (anti-MDA5) positive dermatomyositis (DM)-associated interstitial lung disease (ILD). The aim of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of baricitinib in patients with MDA5 + DM-ILD.MethodsThe study was a retrospective cohort design that evaluated patients with MDA5 + DM-ILD who had received or not received baricitinib. Clinical symptoms, laboratory data, and survival were compared in the two groups.ResultsThirty-nine patients with MDA5 + DM-ILD who received baricitinib were included in the study. After baricitinib therapy, 31 patients (79.5%) had a clinical improvement that included Gottron’s sign (p < 0.001), heliotrope rash (p < 0.001), and dyspnea (p = 0.003). The median HRCT score decreased from 115 to 97.5 (p < 0.001), while the FVC improved significantly (76.44% vs. 90.73%, p < 0.001). In addition, serum ferritin levels (739.9 ng/ml vs. 56.3 ng/ml, p < 0.001) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels (313 U/L vs. 233 U/L, p = 0.003) decreased significantly. Notably, lymphocyte counts increased significantly, CD4 + T cells (354 cells/µl vs. 663 cells/µl, p < 0.001), and CD8 + T cells (180 cells/µl vs. 449 cells/µl, p < 0.001). Furthermore, the glucocorticoid dose reduced significantly from 40 mg daily to 10 mg daily (p < 0.001). Compared with the control group, the baricitinib group had a greater six-month survival (87.2% vs. 70%, p = 0.047). While first-line baricitinib showed a superior survival rate compared to conventional therapy (84.6% vs. 52.9%, p = 0.046).ConclusionThis study indicates that Baricitinib is a potential therapeutic for MDA5 + DM-ILD, reducing LDH and ferritin, ameliorating ILD progression, and improving survival, with first-line use may provide a significant survival advantage. However, larger prospective controlled studies are required to evaluate its efficacy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13075-025-03675-9.Keywords: Dermatomyositis, Baricitinib, Anti-melanoma differentiation-associated protein 5 antibody, Interstitial lung disease

Introduction

Dermatomyositis (DM) is a systemic autoimmune disease characterized by a rash, proximal muscle weakness, and extramuscular manifestations such as interstitial lung disease (ILD), fever, and arthritis [1]. Different myositis special antibodies represent different clinical subtypes. Anti-melanoma differentiation associated gene 5 (MDA5) antibody positive DM (MDA5 + DM) is a special type that has been recognized in recent years. The MDA5 antibody has a pathogenic role in the course of the disease by activating interferon-mediated inflammation [2]. The clinical features of MDA5 + DM still exhibit significant variations across ethnicities and regions. In Western populations, rapidly progressive interstitial lung disease (RP-ILD) is relatively uncommon, whereas in Asian populations, both ILD and RP-ILD are more prevalent. Our recent study on MDA5 + DM demonstrated a high burden of pulmonary involvement, with 95% of cases exhibiting ILD and 45% showing RP-ILD [3]. Other studies have shown that MDA5 + DM patients present three distinct subgroups: anti-MDA5 + rheumatoid DM with a good prognosis, anti-MDA5 + vasculopathic DM with an intermediate prognosis, and RP-ILD with a high mortality rate [4, 5]. At present, the six-month mortality rate of MDA5 + DM with RP-ILD is 33%~66%, and accordingly is a very dangerous disease [6].

Recently, it was reported that Janus kinase inhibitors (JAKi) can be used to treat DM and improve skin rash and muscle strength. Some studies have shown that JAKi also improved MDA5 + DM. Furthermore, several case reports have suggested that tofacitinib, a JAKi, is a promising therapeutic option for the treatment of refractory rash and early RP-ILD in patients with MDA5 + DM [7–13]. Previous studies have also shown that JAKi can benefit DM patients by reducing IFN signaling and related manifestations such as muscle weakness, skin lesions, and ILD [14]. As a result, there is increased interest in the use of JAKi in the treatment of IIM, especially DM, with these agents now recommended as an alternative treatment option for MDA5 + DM [15, 16].

Recent case reports have suggested the efficacy of another JAKi - baricitinib in MDA5 + DM [17, 18]. However, no large cohort studies have been conducted to assess the efficacy of baricitinib. The current study seeks to address this gap by retrospectively evaluating the clinical outcomes and safety of baricitinib in a cohort of MDA5 + DM-ILD patients, with the aim of providing robust evidence for its potential as a therapeutic option.

Materials and methods

Study population

We performed a retrospective analysis of patients with MDA5 + DM diagnosed at the Department of Rheumatology, China-Japan Friendship Hospital between January 2021 and December 2023. The inclusion criteria were: Diagnosis of MDA5 + DM according to the 2017 EULAR/ACR IIM classification criteria [19] or the 2018 ENMC DM criteria [20], age over 18 years and combined with ILD, and application of baricitinib for more than one month. The exclusion criteria were: Incomplete follow-up data, myositis associated with pregnancy, concomitant malignancy, severe infection, severe cardiac dysfunction (ejection fraction ≤ 30%), or having received another JAKi (tofacitinib or upadacitinib). The patients were divided into a baricitinib group and control group of patients who had not received baricitinib. The patient selection process is shown in Fig. 1.

Baseline characteristics were assessed either: (a) prior to any systemic therapy for first-line baricitinib recipients, or (b) immediately before baricitinib initiation (post-conventional therapy) for second-line cases. All patients receiving baricitinib as second-line therapy met prespecified treatment failure criteria prior to initiation, defined as the presence of the following despite ≥ 2 weeks of conventional therapy [glucocorticoids (GC) + conventional immunosuppressants]: (a) persistent or progressive respiratory symptom, (b) radiological deterioration [≥ 10% increase in high-resolution CT (HRCT) composite score], or (c) biochemical progression [> 20% increase in either serum ferritin or lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) from baseline]. To avoid immortal time bias, all survival analyses were calculated from the date of baricitinib initiation as the time zero point, regardless of whether patients received it as first-line or second-line therapy. For the control group, to ensure a clinically equivalent starting point, time zero was defined as the date of initiation of the corresponding intensive conventional immunosuppressive therapy (e.g., the first dose of cyclosporine, tacrolimus, or cyclophosphamide).

The study was carried out in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Research Review Committee (RRC) and Ethics Review Committee (ERC) of China-Japan Friendship Hospital (reference number:2022-KY-156). All patients signed an informed consent form at admission.

Clinical and laboratory data

All patient-related demographic data, clinical characteristics, laboratory tests, and treatment information were recorded. The clinical manifestations of the patients included arthritis, skin rash, muscle strength, and respiratory symptoms. Laboratory tests recorded included: complete blood count and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), the number of NK cells, CD4 + and CD8 + T lymphocyte cells, and the levels of serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), creatine kinase (CK), LDH, α-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase (HBDH), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interferon α (IFN-α), interferon γ (IFN-γ), serum ferritin, and C-reactive protein (CRP). Anti-MDA5 antibody detection was performed using the EUROLINE autoimmune inflammatory myopathy Ag (IgG) test kit according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The cut-off value for anti-MDA5 antibody positivity was 25 U/ml.

Evaluation of ILD

ILD was defined by typical radiological features on HRCT, including ground-glass opacity, consolidation, ground-glass opacity with traction bronchiectasis or bronchial dilatation, consolidation with traction bronchiectasis or bronchial dilatation, and honeycomb changes [21]. Types of ILD include usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP), nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP), and organizing pneumonia (OP). All the lung radiology images were reviewed independently by a rheumatologist and a radiologist. The overall HRCT score was determined using the classification of Ichikado [22]. The definition of RP-ILD was based on a previous study, with minor modifications [23]. Briefly, RP-ILD was defined as the presence of each of the following three conditions within 3 months since the onset of respiratory symptoms: (1) progressive worsening of dyspnoea; (2) pulmonary HRCT revealed acute alveolar exudative lesions (i.e. ground-glass opacities, consolidations); (3) hypoxaemia (arterial oxygen tension (PaO2)/inspiratory oxygen fraction (FIO2) < 300 mmHg based on arterial blood gas analysis) or severe restrictive ventilation dysfunction [forced vital capacity (FVC) < 50% pred or unable to perform pulmonary function tests].

Pulmonary function test

Pulmonary function tests (PFTs) were performed before and during treatment. The condition of some patients was severe and therefore could not undergo PFTs. The results of the PFTs were expressed as a percentage of the predicted value of each parameter, including FVC and lung diffusion capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO), which was adjusted for hemoglobin [24]. Severity was assessed according to the PFT values referenced in a previous study [25]. According to the consensus of the American Thoracic Society (ATS) on idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, a decrease of > 5% in absolute FVC or 10% in DLCO within one year of follow-up is considered as a deterioration. Conversely, an increase of 5% in FVC or 10% in DLCO is considered as an improvement [26].

Statistical analysis

The data were expressed as mean ± SD, median (interquartile range), or number (percentage) where appropriate. The statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26) and GraphPad Prism (version 9.5) software. Statistical differences in each group were calculated using the t test (normal distribution), Mann-Whitney U test (nonnormal distribution), or the χ2 test. Subgroup comparisons were analyzed using the Wilcoxon rank sum test. The survival of patients treated or not treated with baricitinib was evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier method with a log-rank test. All the statistical tests were two-sided, with the level of significance set at p < 0.05.

Results

Baseline clinical features

A total of 39 baricitinib-treated adult patients (baricitinib group) and 80 adult patients treated with conventional immunosuppressants (control group) were finally enrolled in the study (see Fig. 1).

Of the MDA5 + DM patients treated with baricitinib, 28 (71.8%) were female. The mean onset age was 47.6 ± 11.5 years (range, 21–66 years). The median disease duration in patients before treatment with baricitinib was 4.5 months. At the onset of the disease, 31 patients (79.5%) had lymphocytopenia, 6 (15.4%) an elevated CK, 32 (82.1%) elevated LDH, and 25 (64.1%) hyperferritinemia. A summary of the main clinical characteristics and treatment data of the participants were shown in Table 1. With the exception of three patients with rheumatoid arthritis and two patients with Sjögren’s syndrome, no patient had other connective tissue diseases.

At baseline, all patients had completed HRCT, but only 22 (56.4%) had completed PFTs. All patients had ILD, with a median HRCT score of 140, with 11 patients having RP-ILD. The ILD form was NSIP in 32 patients (82.1%), OP in 2 (5.1%), NSIP overlaps OP in 3 (7.7%), and UIP in 2 (5.1%). Regarding PFTs, the mean FVC% was 76.44% ± 18.14%, with a mean DLCO% of 53.49% ± 13.6%.

The median initial GC dose (prednisone equivalent) was 40 mg per day in both the baricitinib group and the control group. A Mann-Whitney U test confirmed that there was no statistically significant difference in the initial steroid dose between the two groups (p = 0.27). The tapering schedule was similarly gradual in both groups, based on clinical and radiological response. Within the baricitinib-treated cohort, pretreatment immunosuppressive regimens were stratified as follows: 61.5% received calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus/cyclosporine), 17.9% underwent mycophenolate mofetil therapy, 17.9% were administered cyclophosphamide, 15.4% received other immunosuppressive agents, and 43.6% had prior intravenous immunoglobulin infusion.

Compared with the control group, there were no statistical differences in gender, age at onset, disease duration, clinical manifestations, ILD pattern, HRCT score, lung function, lymphocyte count, previous therapy, and the levels of ferritin, CK, and LDH in the patients receiving baricitinib.

Baseline immunosuppressive therapy in the control group

The historical control group (n = 80) consisted of patients who initiated intensive conventional immunosuppressive therapy for MDA5 + DM-ILD. Among them, 34 patients (42.5%) were newly diagnosed and received this as first-line therapy, while 46 patients (57.5%) had received prior immunosuppressive treatments and were initiating a new line of salvage therapy. Calcineurin inhibitors were the cornerstone of therapy, used in 65 out of 80 patients (81.3%). This consisted of cyclosporine in 46 patients (57.5%) and tacrolimus in 19 patients (23.8%). Mycophenolate mofetil was used in 2 patients (2.5%). Cyclophosphamide was used in 1 patient (1.3%). Methotrexate was used in 1 patient (1.3%). Intravenous immunoglobulin was administered to 19 patients (23.8%).

Baricitinib treatment options

Eight patients received baricitinib as the initial treatment, while five received baricitinib and other conventional immunosuppressants (methotrexate/tacrolimus/cyclosporine) as initial treatment. Twenty-six patients were refractory to conventional immunosuppressants (tacrolimus/cyclosporine/mycophenolate mofetil) before receiving baricitinib, of which 19 patients only received baricitinib and seven patients also received other conventional immunosuppressants (methotrexate/tacrolimus/cyclosporine). The initial baricitinib dose was 2 mg daily for 15 patients and 4 mg daily for the remaining 24 patients. The dosage should be determined by the patient’s renal function, concomitant immunosuppressants, body weight, and the presence of co-infections. All patients in the baricitinib group received continuous treatment throughout the study period. Additionally, all the patients received GC as primary therapy and a preventive dose of sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim (0.8 g sulfamethoxazole and 0.16 g trimethoprim per day).

Therapeutic effects of baricitinib in MDA5 + DM-ILD

A total of 31 patients (79.5%) in the study cohort experienced clinical improvement (Fig. 2). After a median of 10 months of treatment with baricitinib, Gottron’s sign (89.7% vs. 10.3%, p < 0.001) and heliotrope rash (84.6% vs. 7.7%, p < 0.001) were both reduced significantly, and dyspnea (48.7% vs. 15.4%, p = 0.003) was improved significantly. For PFTs, FVC% significantly improved from 76.44% ± 18.14% to 90.73% ± 13.2% (p < 0.001), while DLCO% also showed slight improvement from 53.49% ± 13.6% to 60.41% ± 13.37% (p = 0.071). As shown in Supplemental Figure S1, the lung CT images of 22 patients improved significantly, 9 remained stable, 4 slightly worsened, and 4 significantly worsened. The median HRCT score decreased significantly from 115 to 97.5 (p < 0.001).

Laboratory indicators improved significantly after baricitinib treatment. Notably, lymphocyte counts increased significantly (0.8 *109/L vs. 1.51 *109/L, p < 0.001), especially CD4 + T cells (354 cells/µl vs. 663 cells/µl, p < 0.001), CD8 + T cells (180 cells/µl vs. 449 cells/µl, p < 0.001), and NK cells (71 cells/µl vs. 190 cells/µl, p = 0.003). In addition, serum ferritin levels (739.9 ng/ml vs. 56.3 ng/ml, p < 0.001), ESR (25 mm/h vs. 8.5 mm/h, p < 0.001), and CRP (0.35 mg/L vs. 0.16 mg/L, p = 0.015) were decreased significantly. The levels of LDH (313 U/L vs. 233 U/L, p = 0.003) also decreased significantly.

Prognosis

In the unadjusted raw dataset, there was a statistically significant difference in survival between patients with MDA5 + DM-ILD who received baricitinib compared to those who did not (i.e., control group (87.2% vs. 70%, p = 0.047; Fig. 3). Through multivariate Cox regression analysis adjusted for key clinical covariates (including age, Ro52, RP-ILD, ferritin level, Lymphocytes, and LDH level), we have identified serum ferritin levels and the presence of RP-ILD as independent prognostic determinants. And the HR of baricitinib for survival was 0.259 (95% CI, 0.087 to 0.776, p = 0.016, compared to controls) adjusted by confounding covariates (Supplemental Table S1). Then we performed subgroup analysis based on RP-ILD and found that the survival rates of the RP-ILD subgroup and the non-RP-ILD subgroup were higher than those of the control group, but there was no statistical difference in the former (Supplemental Figure S2).

Additionally, to ensure a fair comparison, we stratified the analysis by the line of therapy. This analysis revealed a critical and clinically important distinction: When used as first-line Therapy: In the comparison of first-line baricitinib (n = 13) versus first-line conventional therapy (n = 34), treatment with baricitinib was associated with a statistically significant and substantial survival advantage (84.6% vs. 52.9%, p = 0.046; Fig. 3). When used as salvage therapy: In the comparison of salvage baricitinib (n = 26) versus salvage conventional therapy (n = 46), there was no statistically significant difference in survival between the groups (88.5% vs. 82.6%, p = 0.514; Fig. 3).

The median follow-up time of patients was 10 months, with 18 patients followed for more than 12 months. At the end of the study, the daily dose of GC was reduced from 40 mg to 10 mg daily (p < 0.001), and 19 patients had reached a GC dose of 10 mg daily or less. Of the 13 patients with less than 6 months of follow-up, 3 had switched to medications due to poor efficacy, while 5 patients had died of respiratory failure secondary to RP-ILD with severe pulmonary infection. Five patients discontinued baricitinib due to complications rather than the ineffectiveness of the drug. One patient due to bacterial infection, one patient due to bacterial infection and mediastinal emphysema, one discontinued due to Epstein-Barr virus, cytomegalovirus infection and mediastinal emphysema, and two due to fungal infection.

We then conducted a stratified analysis of patients in the baricitinib treatment group who had survived versus those who had died (Fig. 4). The results showed that the deceased group had lower lymphocyte levels at baseline (0.81 *109/L vs. 0.47 *109/L, p = 0.006), and there was also a significant difference in lymphocyte numbers at outcome (1.51 *109/L vs. 0.38 *109/L, p = 0.008). Although there were no differences in the levels of ferritin, LDH, and HBDH at baseline, statistical differences in outcomes were observed in the patient cohort (p < 0.05).

Complications and adverse reactions

Not all patients can safely tolerate baricitinib. Complications occurred during baricitinib treatment in 17 of the 39 patients (43.6%), including new infections in 14 patients (35.9%), mediastinal emphysema in 4 patients (10.3%), and lower extremity thrombosis in 1 patient (2.6%) (Supplemental Table S2). The most common pathogens were viruses (23.1%), including 4 (10.3%) patients infected with Cytomegalovirus(CMV), 3 (7.8%) with Epstein-Barr virus(EBV), and 2 (5.1%) with herpes zoster virus. Seven patients were infected with fungi, with Candida (3/7 [42.9%]) being the most common pathogen, followed by Aspergillus (2/7 [28.6%]). Bacterial infections occurred in five patients (5/14 [35.7%]). Overall, the infection rate in the baricitinib group was not greater than that in the control group (35.9% vs. 37.5%, p > 0.05) (Supplemental Table S3).

Discussion

This study retrospectively analyzed the effect of baricitinib on patients with MDA5 + DM-ILD. Most of the patients included in this study were female patients and 11 patients had RP-ILD. After administration of baricitinib, the skin rash of most patients improved, inflammation levels reduced, ILD was alleviated, and the number of lymphocytes, CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, and NK cells were increased significantly compared with those measured before treatment with baricitinib. The overall survival rate was also better than that of the traditional treatment group.

The common clinical manifestations of the MDA5 + DM-ILD patients in the cohort were Gottron’s sign, heliotrope rash, mechanic’s hands, arthritis, dyspnea, findings which are in line with those of previous studies [13, 27]. Patients in the current study had low lymphocyte counts, and high LDH and ferritin levels, while some patients had RP-ILD, which were all factors for a poor prognosis [3, 28]. Several studies have reported that MDA5 + DM patients have a poor response to conventional therapy, such as high-dose GCs and immunosuppressants, leading to poor overall prognosis [27, 29].

It is well known that JAK plays a key role in signaling pathways and that dysregulation of JAK-related pathways is associated with various immune diseases [30]. Previous studies have also shown that JAKi is beneficial for DM patients by reducing IFN signaling and related manifestations such as muscle weakness, skin lesions, and ILD [14]. The majority of previous reports on JAKi treatment of MDA5 + DM have been on tofacitinib, although it is not clear whether it is superior to other JAK inhibitors. Baricitinib is a selective inhibitor of JAK1 and JAK2 and exhibits moderate inhibitory activity against TYK2 [31]. Compared with tofacitinib, baricitinib is more effective in inhibiting JAK2. JAK2 mediates transforming growth factor β signaling in fibroblasts [32, 33] and its activation has been observed in pulmonary fibrosis [34, 35]. Previous studies have shown that baricitinib improves MDA5 + DM [18], with one study on JAKi treatment for MDA5 + DM reporting a higher survival rate [9]. The current study showed that most patients responded to baricitinib, with an improvement in associated clinical manifestations such as skin lesions, dyspnea, arthritis, and muscle weakness. Taken together, these results indicate that baricitinib is a reasonable option for the effective treatment of MDA5 + DM.

We used PFTs and the HRCT score as evaluation tools in the pulmonary assessments. The PFT is a reproducible and objective tool recommended for the evaluation of ILD, while the HRCT score quantifies the morphological extent of ILD. In our cohort, the patients’ HRCT scores were significantly lower than pre-treatment values. In addition, compared with pre-treatment, FVC parameters were improved significantly, whereas DLCO parameters only showed a statistically insignificant increase, possibly as a result of some patients being unable to refine their PFTs due to their poor health at baseline.

Many previous studies have demonstrated the factors associated with a poor prognosis in MDA5 + DM, which include older age, male, low lymphocyte count, high ferritin levels, RP-ILD, and inappropriate treatment strategies [3, 28]. Serum ferritin level is recognized as an important prognostic biomarker in patients with MDA5 + DM and is associated with disease activity, treatment response, and outcome [36, 37]. Other studies have reported that lymphocytopenia is a manifestation of MDA5 + DM and that CD4 + T cell and CD8 + T cell counts are also decreased significantly [3, 38]. These findings, which are consistent with our study results, showed that serum ferritin levels were reduced significantly after baricitinib treatment, while the number of total lymphocytes, CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, and NK cells were increased significantly. In addition, a comparison of the surviving and deceased patients suggested that low lymphocyte levels were associated with poor outcomes, and that there was also a significant difference in the survival rate compared to that observed in the control group. These findings also indicate the therapeutic advantage of baricitinib. In the subgroup analysis of RP-ILD, we found that baricitinib demonstrated superior treatment efficacy in the non-RP-ILD group compared to the RP-ILD group, a conclusion that aligns with a recent study [39]. We therefore postulate that baricitinib demonstrates optimal therapeutic benefits when initiated prior to RP-ILD development, supporting its role as an early targeted therapy rather than a rescue intervention for fulminant disease.

Additionally, our stratified analysis yields a pivotal insight for clinical practice: the survival benefit of baricitinib in anti-MDA5 + DM-ILD is most pronounced when it is initiated early in the disease course as part of first-line therapy. The attenuated effect observed in the salvage setting likely reflects the greater cumulative organ damage, higher burden of prior immunosuppression, and inherently worse prognosis of established refractory disease, which may be less amenable to any immunomodulatory intervention. This pattern is consistent with the biology of many autoimmune diseases, where early, potent intervention is key to altering the disease trajectory. Therefore, our study strongly suggests that the optimal use of Baricitinib is front-line, in combination with corticosteroids, rather than as a rescue agent after the failure of other agents. This finding does not diminish the value of Baricitinib but rather provides essential guidance on how to maximize its clinical impact.

The safety of baricitinib has also attracted considerable attention. Several studies have reported that infections, including opportunistic infections, are a major problem in patients with MDA5 + DM and may lead to life-threatening consequences [40, 41]. In the present study, about 35.9% of patients were complicated by an infection, which was similar to the 37.5% infection rate in the control group and slightly lower than the 40.4% infection rate reported in a previous study of tofacitinib treatment in MDA5 + DM [13]. Approximately, 12.8% of our patients died of respiratory failure secondary to RP-ILD with severe pulmonary infection, although not all these deaths were caused directly by an infection. Furthermore, one patient developed a thromboembolic event after treatment, although the relationship of this event with baricitinib could not be fully confirmed. In conclusion, our study indicated that baricitinib did not increase the risk of additional infections and that its safety was acceptable, although more studies are needed to further investigate its use in patients with MDA5 + DM.

The present study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, it was a retrospective study, and despite the presence of a historical cohort control group, inherent bias could not be avoided completely. Second, the median follow-up time of this study was 10 months, which is relatively short. Third, the background treatment of some patients has changed, which may also affect the experimental results. Fourth, due to the historical nature of the control group, systematic longitudinal biomarker data were not available, precluding a direct comparison of laboratory trends between the groups. Future prospective, controlled studies would benefit from protocol-defined serial biomarker collection. Finally, patients in the study cohort were enrolled from a single center, which limited its size, which in turn led to selection bias. Studies with larger cohorts from multiple centers and clinical trials with stricter protocols are therefore necessary to confirm the findings of our study.

Conclusion

Our findings identify Baricitinib as a potential therapeutic option for MDA5 + DM-ILD, demonstrating its ability to ameliorate clinical symptoms, normalize key serological markers (LDH, ferritin, lymphocytes), slow ILD progression, and, most importantly, could improve survival when utilized as first-line therapy.

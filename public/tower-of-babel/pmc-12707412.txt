Clinicopathological features differentiating Castleman Disease from IgG4-related ophthalmic disease, a 10 years retrospective study
Authors: Hetong Wang, Jinzhi Zhao, Wei Wang, Xun Liu, Limin Zhu, Tingting Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Purpose:This study compared two rare diseases that present with enlarged lacrimal glands and analyzed their clinical features, among others, for identification.Methods:Setting – A single-center retrospective study.Patient or Study Population –Patients treated at our center from 2013 to 2023 with pathologically diagnosed IgG4-ROD (n = 31, 42 eyes) and CD (n = 5, 5 eyes).Intervention or Observation Procedure(s) –No interventions administered.Main Outcome Measure(s) –The analysis included patient demographics, laboratory tests, histopathological tests, imaging studies, treatment, and outcomes. Data collection involved visualizing medical records.Results:The lacrimal glands/eyelid soft tissues and extraocular muscles of the IgG4-ROD group were affected. Histopathological examination showed lymphoid proliferation in both groups had lymphocyte hyperplasia, and the pathological IgG4+/IgG+ ratio in the IgG4-ROD group was higher than that in the CD group (P = 0.0057), which was more than 40%. IgG4-ROD exhibited significantly higher MASSON positivity than CD (78.1% vs. 33.3%, P = 0.0466). CD lesions exhibited a 50% reduction in Ki-67 proliferative activity relative to IgG4-ROD (P = 0.0349). Bone destruction was identified in 20% of CD versus none in IgG4-ROD (P = 0.024). Conversely, fibrotic imaging features demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence in IgG4-ROD (40%) compared to CD (P = 0.0103).Conclusions:Significant differences in pathology and imaging exist between IgG4-ROD and CD. IgG4-ROD is characterized by an IgG4+/IgG+ plasma cell ratio exceeding 40% and extensive fibrosis. Imaging predominantly reveals nondestructive fibrotic foci, reflecting the chronic inflammatory fibroproliferative nature of the disease. Conversely, CD exhibits higher local invasiveness and lower cellular proliferative activity.

Castleman disease (CD) was first described by Benjamin Castleman in 1956 and contains a spectrum of rare disorders with characteristic histopathological features.[1] Unicentric CD (UCD) is an asymptomatic, local hyperplasia of the lymphoid tissue that is usually curable.[2] Multicentric CD (MCD) manifests as a potentially life-threatening systemic disease with complex symptomatology.[3] Multiple inflammatory disorders can present symptoms or lymphadenopathy with histopathological “Castleman-like” findings, in which IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD), an immune-mediated disease characterized by IgG4-positive plasma cell infiltration and fibrosis, pathologically possesses very similar clinical and histopathological features,[45] which makes differential diagnosis with CD challenging. IgG4-related orbital disease (IgG4-ROD) commonly affects most orbital contents including the eyelid, lacrimal gland, extraocular muscle, orbital soft tissue, and orbital nerves.[67] Notably, orbital involvements in CD also manifest eyelid swelling, lacrimal gland mass, or extraocular muscle swelling and pose a challenge for differentiated with IgG4-ROD. In this study, the overlap and differences between CD and IgG4-ROD with orbital involvement is explored in terms of clinical presentation and histopathological features to avoid misdiagnosis and confusion.

Methods

Subjects

A retrospective case series study was performed. Clinical data were collected from patients with orbital lesion in Tianjin medical university eye Hospital from January 2013 to December 2023. Orbital lesions were surgically removed, and all patients were diagnosed with IgG4-ROD or CD through postoperative histopathological examination. All patients signed an informed consent form. All patients in the CD group had their lacrimal glands removed. In the IgG4-ROD group, only one patient had his lacrimal gland removed. All study procedures conformed to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and were approved by the Ethics Committee of Tianjin medical university eye Hospital (2024KY-27). Clinical data including demographic characteristics, affected eye, symptoms, and outcomes were retrospectively collected.

Ophthalmic and Imaging examinations

Standardized ophthalmic examination data were collected from all patients, including best corrected visual acuity (Snellen), intraocular eye pressure (CT-1 noncontact tonometer, Topcon, Japan), slit-lamp biomicroscopy (66 Vision, China), Hertel-exophthalmometry (66 Vision, China), lid parameters, and diplopia. Imaging studies closest in time to subsequent orbital biopsy were retrieved for each patient, including computed tomography (CT, GE Revolution Acts, America, 16-slice), magnetic-resonance imaging (MRI, Ingenia 3.0T or Ingenia S 1.5T, Philips Medical Systems), and color Doppler flow imaging (CDFI, Mindray DC-80, Shenzhen, China).

Laboratory examinations

Complete blood count, serum Immunoglobin G (IgG), and serum autoantibody (antinuclear antibodies, ANA; C-reactive protein, CRP; antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies, ANCA; rheumatoid factor, RF) testing data were retrospectively collected. Histopathology samples were prepared as follows: Serial 3-μm-thick sections were sliced from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue for hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining, and Masson’s trichrome staining. Immunohistochemical staining was performed using anti-IgG, anti-IgG4, anti-Bcl-2, anti-CD31, anti-CD21, anti-Ki67, anti-HHV-8, and anti-IgD, all of which were acquired from ZSGB-Bio (China).

Diagnosis criteria

Diagnosis of UCD was based on “International evidence-based consensus diagnostic and treatment guidelines for UCD”in 2020.[8] UCD is diagnosed after a lymph node biopsy to investigate solitary lymphadenopathy, usually on clinical suspicion of lymphoma. Histopathological features of lymph nodes in UCD range from what is considered to be hyaline vascular (HV) histopathology to plasmacytic (PC) histopathology, with a mixed subgroup in between that exhibits both features.

Diagnosis of MCD conformed to the “International, evidence-based consensus diagnostic criteria for HHV-8-negative/idiopathic MCD” in 2017.[9] In brief, the consensus criteria of MCD require both major criteria (characteristic lymph node histopathology and multicentric lymphadenopathy), at least 2 of 11 minor criteria with at least one laboratory abnormality, and exclusion of infectious, malignant, and autoimmune disorders that can mimic MCD.

IgG4-ROD was diagnosis according to the new diagnostic criteria for IgG4-ROD, reported in 2014 by Goto et al.[10]

Statistical analysis

Analysis was performed using Prism 8.0 software. Paired t-tests were used for analyzing quantitative variables, and frequencies and rates were used for examining enumeration data. Differences between ratios or proportions were determined using the χ² test (or Fisher's exact probability method) for 2x2 contingency tables, which are independent samples four-cell tables. A statistically significant result was defined as P < 0.05. *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001; ****P < 0.0001.

Results

Demographic characteristics and clinical presentations of CD and IgG4-ROD

Five patients with CD (2 males, age range 44–76 years, and median age 56 years) and 31 patients with IgG4-ROD (6 males, age range 26–77 years, and median age 51 years) were included in the study. Sup. 1 shows the details of 5 CD cases with disease courses ranging from 2 months to 120 months (median 36 months). Lacrimal gland mass was commonly seen in 5 patients with CD. Four (80%) patients showed extraocular muscle involvement and 3 (60%) patients showed eyelid swelling. Two (40%) patients were diagnosed with MCD after systemic examination.

In 31 IgG4-ROD cases, the locations of the lesions were as follows: 26 patients had lacrimal gland involvement, 8 had extraocular muscle involvement, and all 31 cases had eyelid soft tissue involvement, presenting with painless eyelid swelling. Among the patients with IgG4-ROD, two had parotid gland involvement, and three had submandibular lymph node enlargement; the course of the disease (n = 31) ranged from 6 to 36 months (median 12 months).

Laboratory examinations of CD and IgG4-ROD

Complete blood count testing was retrieved from 19 IgG4-ROD cases and 5 CD cases. In our study, we found that key hematological parameters such as white blood cell (WBC), platelet (PLT), and mean corpuscular volume (MCV) were comparable between patients with CD and those with IgG4-ROD. However, a notable increase in monocyte percentage (MONO%) was observed in 40% of patients with IgG4-ROD (n = 19) and 52.6% of patients with CD (n = 5). Serum IgG4 levels were elevated in 4/5 IgG4-ROD cases (80%). IgG1 levels exceeded normal limits in 2 IgG4-ROD patients [Sup. 2].

Imaging

In the CD group, CDFI showed heterogeneous internal echogenicity with clear boundaries and rich blood flow signals in the lacrimal gland of two cases. This pattern is consistent with the characteristic vascular proliferation observed in CD. One case showed bilateral lacrimal gland involvement [Fig.1]. One case had intracranial tumor spread and local bone loss. In the IgG4-ROD group [Sup. 3], CDFI showed heterogeneous echoes in 9 cases (64%), including 8 in the lacrimal region and 1 in the lower lid. Case 15 showed left eyelid and lacrimal gland swelling with abnormal enhancement signal of the extraconical space of the muscle cone, encircling the eyeball, and the bulbar fascial bursa and the mandibular nerve were involved. This is highly suggestive of perineural inflammatory extension. Fig. 2 shows a case of bilateral IgG4-ROD.

Bone destruction (widening of the supraorbital fissure with bone loss at the base of the skull) was observed in one case (20%) in the CD group, but not in the IgG4-ROD group (P = 0.024). Patients with IgG4-ROD all exhibited distinct fibrotic imaging features, characterized by hypoechoic on US or T2 low signal on MRI. These findings were observed in a notably higher proportion of IgG4-ROD cases (40%, 2/5) compared to CD (P = 0.0103). Hypervascular patterns, characterized by a prominent central vascular channel or arteriovenous flow signatures on imaging (US/MRI), were predominantly observed in CD (40%, 2/5). IgG4-ROD showed more abundant blood flow signal [Sup. 3].

Histopathology

Histopathological analysis unveiled distinct microenvironmental signatures characterizing the two entities. CD presented as lymphoid tissue proliferation but differed from IgG4-ROD by showing degenerative transformation of germinal centers and small vessels with hyaline degeneration of vessel walls penetrating into germinal centers and concentrically arranged small lymphocytes around the follicles [Fig. 3]. HE staining in IgG4-ROD showed lymphoid tissue proliferation, predominantly follicular hyperplasia, and most cases showing striped fibrosis (Masson staining positive in 75%, n = 32; Fig. 4). The fibrosis in CD was less pronounced and arranged differently than in IgG4-ROD (Masson staining positive in 33%, n = 6). The fibrotic architecture exhibited a unique spatial organization, serving as a critical diagnostic discriminator from lesions associated with CD. Immunohistochemical staining for Bcl-2 and immunoglobulin D (IgD) was expressed in IgG4-ROD and CD, with more regular arrangement of the germinal center mantle zone in CD compared with IgG4-ROD, and CD21 staining showed the presence of follicular dendritic cells (FDCs) in CD and IgG4-ROD. In IgG4-ROD, the IgG4+/IgG+ plasma cell ratio exceeding 40% served as a critical diagnostic criterion, accompanied by a marked elevation in proliferative activity. This was evidenced by a two-fold increase in Ki-67-positive plasma cell counts compared to CD [Sup. 2]. Plasma cell Castleman disease (PC-CD) exhibited κ light chain restricted expression, indicative of clonal B-cell proliferation [Fig. 5]. All CD cases showed negative HHV-8 (LANA-1) staining.

Treatment and prognosis

All CD patients (n = 5) underwent surgical resection with 36-month median follow-up. Recurrence occurred in 40% (2/5), including 1 hyaline vascular multicentric CD (HV-MCD) with cranial metastases (stable postresection) and 1 PC-MCD with fatal progression postchemotherapy. Other three HV-UCD patients remained recurrence-free, demonstrating surgical efficacy for localized disease.

Among the IgG4-ROD patients, 13 patients were followed up, 5 received steroids, and 2 underwent combination therapy with steroids and immunosuppressants. This mortality rate significantly contrasted with the IgG4-ROD, where no disease-associated fatalities were recorded despite a 46.15% recurrence rate (6/13).

Notably, the overall mortality rate in CD patients reached 40% (n = 5) compared to 0% in the IgG4-ROD (n = 13), highlighting critical prognostic differences (P = 0.0095). These findings underscore the importance of continued monitoring and the need for more effective long-term management strategies to improve outcomes.

Discussion

IgG4-ROD is characterized by tumor-like swelling with IgG4-positive plasma cell infiltration into different ophthalmic tissues, particularly the lacrimal glands, extraocular muscles, and trigeminal nerves.[711] CD, also known as giant lymph node hyperplasia or angiofollicular lymphoid hyperplasia, is a group of rare, unspecified chronic lymphoproliferative diseases with an incidence rate of about 1/50,000.[12] Both diseases can affect the orbit, especially the lacrimal gland. A multicenter study from France included 147 patients with IgG4-RD, 19 with orbital involvement (17%), of which 68.4% involved the lacrimal gland location.[13] Thus, in cases where patients present with lacrimal gland enlargement at the orbital specialty, attention should be paid to distinguishing between the two. This study mainly compares the clinical, laboratory, imaging, and histopathological features and prognosis of IgG4-ROD and CD in orbital lesions to provide references for the differential diagnosis and treatment of the two diseases and to guide future clinical work.

IgG4-RD typically affects middle-aged and elderly men, with an age range of 50–70 years and a male-to-female ratio ranging from 1.6:1 to 4:1.[14] CD is a rare heterogeneous lymphoproliferative disease first discovered by Benjamin Castleman in 1956. In our study, CD can occur at any age but is more common in people aged 10–45 years. The lesion location is mainly in the mediastinum and abdomen, and less commonly in the head and neck. In our study, no patients with herpesvirus 8 (HHV-8) positive CD were found, which is consistent with a previous single-center study from China that analyzed the clinical and pathological features of 203 patients with CD and found no HHV-8 positive MCD cases.[151617] However, in our study, both diseases were more common in middle-aged individuals, and compared with CD patients, IgG4-ROD patients are younger and more likely to be female. This may be related to the sample size and the fact that our research focused only in the orbits. The most common orbital involvement sites for IgG4-ROD and CD are the lacrimal glands, which is similar to previous descriptions.[1819]

Differences were noted in laboratory tests between IgG4-ROD and CD. According to the “International Consensus Evidence-Based Diagnostic Criteria for Single Center CD,” laboratory tests for UCD include screening for anemia, elevated CRP or erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), hypoproteinemia, hyperglobulinemia, and other cytokine-induced inflammatory response markers.[8] Elevated serum IgG4 is an important indicator for the diagnosis and assessment of IgG4-RD, but its specificity for diagnosis is not high. In our study, due to the small sample size, one patient with CD had a serum IgG level higher than the normal range at 18.45 g/L, consistent with consensus diagnostic criteria. Moreover, 40% of patients with CD had a decrease in mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC) and exhibited anemia, while only 15.8% of patients with IgG4-RD were affected. In addition, 10.5% of IgG4-ROD patients had reduced hemoglobin concentration, while patients with CD had normal hemoglobin levels. This outcome is similar to the results of Michael Haap et al.,[20] who reported that patients with CD showed anemia, elevated IgG, increased CRP, increased ESR, and decreased platelet count, suggesting that CD may play a key role in the development of these clinical symptoms, such as anemia and increased immunoglobulin or ESR levels. According to the literature, IL-6 plays a significant role in the development of anemia through mechanisms such as inhibition of erythropoiesis, disruption of iron metabolism, and induction of chronic inflammatory anemia.[21] These findings suggest that future research could benefit from deeper mechanistic explorations, potentially incorporating novel biomarkers such as IL-6.

In terms of imaging examinations, the typical features of IgG4-ROD include symmetrical or asymmetrical lacrimal gland enlargement, thickening of extraocular muscles, peripheral nerve thickening, orbital soft tissue involvement, orbital mass lesions, dacryoadenitis, or dacryocystorhinositis.[22] MRI showed that 1 patient had signals in T1WI and T2WI, 1 patient had hypointensity signal in T1 and slightly hyperintensity signal in T2, and 1 patient had hypointensity signals in both T1WI and T2WI. This difference may be related to the diversity of its pathological tissues. Fardad Behzadi et al.[23] believe that most IgG4-RD lesions show uneven echogenicity in the lesion area, CT shows diffuse enhancement, and MRI shows hypointensity signal in T1 and isointensity/hypointensity signal in T2, which is close to our research results. Among the 12 patients who underwent CT plain scan, 2 showed calcification, which is consistent with the literature. Combined with literature reports, UCD has some characteristic CT imaging features, namely, the lesions on CT plain scan are mostly well defined, smooth, uniform soft tissue masses, round or oval in shape, with almost no necrosis, cyst formation, or bleeding. In this group of five CD cases, CT showed visible soft tissue density shadows, with four cases having clear or relatively clear boundaries and only one case having unclear boundaries, which may be related to its skull base extension, invasion of bone tissue, and resulting skull base defect. Notably, bone destruction can be a specific imaging marker to differentiate CD from IgG4-ROD, suggesting that CD is more locally aggressive. The high prevalence of fibrosis imaging features in IgG4-ROD reflects a core pathological mechanism that is likely driven by chronic inflammation and fibroproliferation. This process is characterized by the infiltration of IgG4-positive plasma cells, lymphocytes, and other immune cells, which contribute to tissue fibrosis through the secretion of profibrotic molecules such as transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β).[24] Additionally, CD group exhibited unique imaging features, with all lesions demonstrating homogeneous soft tissue density. This finding is distinct from the previously reported dendritic calcification in the literature.[25] We believe that this kind of microvascular-related calcification is significantly different from other causes (such as infarction necrosis, granuloma, osteogenic tumors, or metabolic abnormalities) in mechanism and performance characteristics, which are closely related to the nature of lymphoid follicle tissues being less prone to necrosis.[26] In the CD group, color Doppler ultrasound showed low-echo occupying lesions in the lesion area, with relatively clear boundaries and abundant blood flow signals in the lesion area, among which one case had a thick large blood vessel lumen inside, and one case had a blood flow signal spectrum of arterial and venous types. HV-CD mainly manifests as isolated round or lobulated lesions with clear boundaries on CT, which show obvious uniform enhancement after enhancement scanning.

Histopathologically, characteristic pathological changes are important bases for diagnosis and differential diagnosis. IgG4-RD shows a large number of lymphocytes and plasma cell infiltration, striped fibrosis, and obliterative phlebitis, with a high-power field IgG4+/IgG+ plasma cell ratio >40%.[27] CD pathology showed rich follicular dendritic cells, arranged in an onion ring pattern, with a large number of new blood vessels between follicles, accompanied by varying degrees of glass opacity.[28] Terasaki et al.[29] compared the pathological features of systemic idiopathic MCD (iMCD) and IgG4-RD in surgical lung biopsies, and they believed that both lesions had lymphoplasmacytic infiltration, but the characteristic of IgG4-RD lung lesions was active fibrosis with eosinophilic infiltration in the outer lymph matrix area with obstructive vasculitis, while iMCD lung lesions mainly proliferated in the alveolar area outside the outer lymph matrix area. In our study, IgG4-ROD and CD both had lymphoid tissue proliferation, but CD showed germinal center degeneration and small blood vessels penetrating into germinal centers and surrounding follicles with concentrically arranged small lymphocytes. In addition, comparing the IgG4-ROD and CD pathological IgG4+/IgG+ levels found that all patients in the IgG4-ROD group had >40%, and only 2 CD patients had >40%, which is consistent with the consensus statement on the pathology of IgG4-RD.[3031] Compared with CD, patients with IgG4-ROD had a higher positivity rate for Masson staining, more evident fibrosis, and higher Ki-67 expression rate, which is consistent with the results of a large study on IgG4-ROD by Jingqiao Chen et al.,[32] indicating that IgG4-ROD has a stronger proliferative capacity than CD.

The clinical treatment of IgG4-ROD primarily includes observation, glucocorticoid and immunosuppressive drugs, Rituximab (RTX), radiotherapy, and surgery.[7] Traditional immunosuppressants include mycophenolate mofetil, cyclophosphamide, and methotrexate. Due to the rarity of CD, no general consensus on treatment has been reached. Michael Haap et al.[20] believe that UCD will experience long-term disease-free survival after complete resection, with good prognosis and a 5-year survival rate of over 90%. Complete surgical resection can be curative, and treatment options include regimens based on monoclonal antibodies, such as rituximab combined with or without glucocorticoids, as well as classic chemotherapy, with optional adjuvant antiviral therapy.[333435] In this study, all patients with CD were treated by surgical resection, but despite complete resection of the lesions during surgery, two patients had recurrence during follow-up. One case of PC-CD deteriorated after receiving chemotherapy twice, presented poor results, and died. One case could not be completely resected due to multiple lesions and tumor extension into the cranium, and experienced recurrence 2 years after the first resection, with stable disease after reoperation. Therefore, the healing of PC-CD and multiple lesions according to imaging examinations and pathological typing should be closely monitored to prevent recurrence and provide treatment. In our study, most patients with IgG4-ROD continued treatment with immunosuppressants combined with hormones or hormones alone after surgery. Currently, there is no universally accepted standard or specification for the treatment of IgG4-ROD. Corticosteroids, such as prednisone, are generally considered the first-line therapy and are widely used due to their significant efficacy in inducing remission. Some scholars also advocate the use of immunosuppressive drugs such as mizoribine and methotrexate.[36] Such patients should be followed up for a long time, and the dosage of hormones should be adjusted individually according to age and condition.

While this study concentrated on histopathological and radiological features, future research should integrate quantitative assessments of lacrimal function, such as Schirmer’s test and tear osmolarity. These assessments could provide deeper insights into clinicopathological correlations, particularly given that IgG4-ROD patients often exhibit signs of both aqueous tear deficiency and evaporative dry eye disease.

Conclusion

In summary, IgG4-ROD is characterized by painless enlargement of the lacrimal gland and fibrotic imaging markers, such as T2 hypointensity and hypoechoic appearance. These characteristics may aid in reducing unnecessary invasive procedures. Histologically, IgG4-ROD exhibits specific pathological features, including a higher number of Ki-67-positive cells compared to CD and significantly higher positivity for Masson staining. On imaging, bone destruction is observed in CD and can serve as a specific differential marker. Regarding treatment strategies, IgG4-ROD necessitates a combined surgical-immunological approach, comprising limited lesion excision and immunomodulatory therapy. In contrast, CD, particularly the PC-CD, requires intensive follow-up and individualized interventions due to its high aggressiveness. CD is associated with a higher mortality rate than IgG4-ROD. Therefore, effective monitoring of disease progression and timely intervention are crucial, achievable through close follow-up and tailored treatment plans.

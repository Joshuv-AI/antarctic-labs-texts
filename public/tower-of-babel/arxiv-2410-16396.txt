The “ B → K ​ π B\to K\pi puzzle”: A New Perspective
Adam Szabelski e1,addr1

Abstract
A phenomenological analysis based on the published branching fractions and C ​ P CP asymmetry observables of the B → K ​ π B\to K\pi , B → π ​ π B\to\pi\pi and B → K ​ K B\to KK dataset is performed. The amplitude decomposition by the topological diagrams and the S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) irreducible representation is used. The result of the global fit can be interpreted as a solution to the “ B → K ​ π B\to K\pi puzzle”. The obtained precision enables to test a S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) -based sum rule showing 6 ​ σ 6\sigma discrepancy with the expectation value in the Standard Model scenario. To improve the obtained results the main effort should concentrate on the B 0 → K 0 ​ π 0 B^{0}\to K^{0}\pi^{0} measurements.

The study of the B meson decays provides the opportunity to experimentally investigate the flavour- and

-violating features of the Standard Model (

These explorations are performed in

In the spotlight of the B meson decays the family of the
B\to K\pi
transitions plays a key role.
Its importance was strengthened when the “

puzzle” was formulated

pointing out that significant deviation from zero of for experimentally determined quantity

(1)

The

|V_{ub}|
CKM matrix element. The electro-weak (

,
5
,
6

This effect may be observed either as a nonzero phase difference -

[

In this study, the approach proposed in
[

,
9
,
10
,
11

is used. The decay amplitudes are decomposed into the Feynman graphs. Each graphical description represents a topology and includes all multi-loop diagrams that can be reduced down to the given form. To each topology, a single complex parameter is assigned. The number of the free parameters is decreased by imposing the isospin relation and by applying the
SU(3)
decomposition
[

]
(for details see Section

The parameters are fitted to the experimental results: direct and time-dependent
CP

of the
B\to K\pi
,
B\to\pi\pi
and
B\to KK
decays. The contribution of the

is expected to be noticeable in either the magnitude or the phase of the

penguin amplitude.

The same

decomposition leads to a sum rule composed of rate asymmetries. It was proposed in
[
13
]
and is constructed in such a way
that it is small in the sense of the asymmetry range. The rate asymmetries in the sum rule differ by
d\leftrightarrow u
and naively the sum rule should be zero.

The analysis presented here incorporates
the latest results from LHCb on
B^{+}\to K^{+}\pi^{0}

[

]
and B decays to charged pions and kaons
[

as well as recent Belle II measurements of

The results are represented as the profile plot in the

of the fits. The plot demonstrates how well the

is supported by the experimental data.
The considered sum rule, under the
SM
conditions, is reported to be of order of
\sim 2\%
but significantly below zero. At the same time, it is very weakly constrained when the
NP
is allowed.

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{(s)}_{u}}{\lambda^{(s)}_{t}}\frac{c_{9}-c_{10}}{c_{1}-c_{2}}\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2}(C^{\prime}-T^{\prime})

(15a)

\displaystyle b_{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{15}}\frac{\lambda^{(s)}_{u}}{\lambda^{(s)}_{t}}\frac{c_{9}+c_{10}}{c_{1}+c_{2}}(C^{\prime}+T^{\prime})

(15b)

\displaystyle b_{5}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{\sqrt{10}}\frac{\lambda^{(s)}_{u}}{\lambda^{(s)}_{t}}\frac{c_{9}+c_{10}}{c_{1}+c_{2}}(C^{\prime}+T^{\prime})

(15c)

where the exchange and annihilation contributions are neglected. Finally, the connection between the
EW
penguin and the tree amplitudes is reached

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{EW}+P^{\prime}_{EW}{{}^{C}}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}b_{5}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\lambda^{(s)}_{u}}{\lambda^{(s)}_{t}}\frac{c_{9}+c_{10}}{c_{1}+c_{2}}(T^{\prime}+C^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-qe^{i\phi}(T^{\prime}+C^{\prime})

(16)

as in the previous approach.
Moreover, the pure
EW
color-suppressed penguin contribution can be expressed by tree topologies:

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{EW}{{}^{C}}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{5}}b_{3}+\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{5}}b_{4}-\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}b_{5}

\displaystyle~~=-\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\lambda_{u}^{(s)}}{\lambda_{t}^{(s)}}\frac{c_{9}-c_{10}}{c_{1}-c_{2}}(T^{\prime}-C^{\prime})

\displaystyle~~~~~+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\lambda_{u}^{(s)}}{\lambda_{t}^{(s)}}\frac{c_{9}+c_{10}}{c_{1}+c_{2}}(T^{\prime}+C^{\prime})\Bigg]

\displaystyle~~=-qe^{i\phi}C^{\prime}.

(17)

In the last line, numerical approximation
\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{9}-c_{10}}\approx\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}}{c_{9}+c_{10}}
is used. Here, it is assumed that the
NP
represented by
qe^{i\phi}
enters evenly in all the reduced transition matrix elements.
As a consequence of relations (
16
) and (
17
) it follows:

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{EW}=-qe^{i\phi}T^{\prime}.

(18)

Moreover, the
EW
penguin amplitudes in
B\to\pi\pi
decays may be related to the tree amplitudes:

\displaystyle P_{EW}+P_{EW}^{C}

\displaystyle=\-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}b_{5}=-\lambda^{2}qe^{i\phi}(T+C),

(19)

where the influence of
NP
is again parameterised by
q
and
\phi
.

4
SU(3)
constraints.

The
SU(3)
symmetry is applied in four different ways in this analysis. The first one is the isospin symmetry included in the amplitude decomposition and parameterisation of the considered decays given in Eqs. (
4
) and (
8
). It is intrinsically assumed that the same topologies in
B^{0}
and
B^{+}
decays are represented by the same parameters.

The second way the
SU(3)
is exploited is by the decomposition of the effective hamiltonian given in the previous section. The relations (
16
), (
17
) and (
19
) are used to reduce the number of parameters in the fit.

Thirdly, the application of the
SU(3)
symmetry is concerned with the
s\longleftrightarrow d
interchange (
U
-spin symmetry). Here, the relation between amplitudes of
B\to\pi\pi
and
B\to K\pi
decays is taken into consideration. It is a well-known fact that
SU(3)
symmetry is broken here due to the difference between the
s
and
d
quark masses. A loose relation, however, can be applied, as described in
[
37
]
. Magnitudes of tree amplitudes are related by

\displaystyle R_{T+C}=\frac{|\mathcal{T}^{\prime}+\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|}{|\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{C}|}=\frac{|r^{\prime}_{T}+r^{\prime}_{C}|}{|\epsilon(r_{T}+r_{C})|}=1.2\pm 0.2.

(20)

The central value is obtained in the factorisation framework. The uncertainty follows from a conservative approach by taking into account non-factorisable
SU(3)
-breaking effects as large as 100%
[
39
]
. Following the arguments given in
[
37
]
, considering corrections of
\sim 20\%
to the
SU(3)
symmetry, loose constraints on tree amplitude phases can be imposed:

\displaystyle arg(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})-arg(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=(0\pm 20)^{\circ},

(21a)

\displaystyle arg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-arg(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle=(0\pm 20)^{\circ}.

(21b)

The above relations, Eqs.(
20
) and (
21
) are applied as extra constraints to the fit bringing relation between
b\to d
and
b\to s
transitions.

The fourth way is a relation between the
B\to K\pi
and
B\to KK
decays. The
B\to KK
amplitudes in the diagrammatic approach are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(B^{+}\to K^{+}K^{0})=

\displaystyle~P^{KK}\Big(1+r_{\rho}^{KK}e^{i\gamma}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}a^{KK}_{C}(r_{T}+r_{C})qe^{i(\omega+\phi)}\Big),

(22a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(B^{0}\to K^{0}K^{0})=

\displaystyle~P^{KK}\Big(1+r_{\rho}^{KK}e^{i\gamma}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}a^{KK}_{C}(r_{T}+r_{C})qe^{i(\omega+\phi)}\Big).

(22b)

In this analysis, it is assumed that

\displaystyle r_{\rho}^{KK}=r^{\prime}_{\rho},~~~~~~~~a_{C}^{KK}=a_{C},

(23)

while
P^{KK}
is left free independently of
P^{\prime}(K\pi)
. The relation (
23
) is set to hold strictly, as both
\rho_{c}^{KK}
and
a_{C}^{KK}
express amplitudes relative to
P^{KK}
similarly as
r^{\prime}_{\rho}
and
a_{C}
are relative to
P^{\prime}
in the
B\to K\pi
reaction.

6
Fits to current experimental data

To obtain the set of the parameters given in Eqs. (

is given by

\displaystyle\chi^{2}=\frac{(A_{\text{exp}}-A_{\text{theory}})^{2}}{\sigma_{\text{exp}}^{2}}.

(27)

The
SU(3)
limitations, given in Eqs.(
20
) and (
21
) are applied in the same manner.

The
EW
penguin amplitudes are expressed by the tree-like input to limit the number of the free parameters. This is done in the frame of
SU(3)
decomposition (
c.f.
equations (
16
), (

) and (

)). The
q-\phi
parameter plane is scanned obtaining the
\chi^{2}
value at each point. The rest of the parameters are profiled. In addition, since
|r_{\rho}|
is expected to be much smaller than one, a limit on its real part
|\Re{(r_{\rho})}|<0.3
is imposed. This limit helps in the convergence of the fit and it has been checked that the choice of a value different from

has a negligible impact on the values of the extracted parameters.
The results of the scan performed using the Minuit package,
[

\displaystyle\text{contour}(2\sigma)

\displaystyle=\chi^{2}_{min}+(\chi^{2})^{-1}(0.95,N_{dof}).

7
Summary

Even though the “
B\to K\pi
puzzle” has been solved, the current experimental uncertainties of the analysed decays still leave space for the NP scenarios.
Moreover, the
q-\phi
profile plot obtained here can serve as a starting point to impose constraints on NP models.
From the experimental point of view it is clear that improving the precision of the
B^{0}\to K^{0}\pi^{0}
measurements should help to clarify the picture.

The sum rule (
3
) result is
6\sigma
below zero at the minimum of the SM scenario. On the other hand, its value is small in the sense of the asymmetry scale (
\sim 2\%
) which is a consequence of the applied parameterisation and the
SU(3)
decomposition. Adding two more degrees of freedom along with the potential NP weakens the experimental constraints on the sum rule showing compatibility with zero within
1\sigma
. This fact is interesting and calls for a discussion.

As a follow-up, other decays can be examined in a similar analysis. For example
D\to PP^{\prime}
or
B\to DP
.

Appendix C
Branching fractions covariance matrix

It is clear that some ratios of BFs are correlated
e.g.

and
R^{\pi^{0}\pi^{+}}_{\pi^{0}K^{+}}
. To account for this correlation, a covariance matrix is formulated with the use of the following approximated equalities:

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\frac{Y}{Z}\right)^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle\left(\frac{Y}{Z}\right)\right\rangle^{2}=~

\displaystyle\sigma_{Y}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{Z^{2}}+\frac{3\sigma_{Z}^{2}}{Z^{4}}\right)

(36a)

\displaystyle+Y^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma_{Z}^{2}}{Z^{4}}+\frac{\sigma_{Z}^{4}}{Z^{6}}\right),

(36b)

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{X}{Z}\frac{Y}{Z}\right\rangle-\left\langle\frac{X}{Z}\right\rangle\left\langle\frac{Y}{Z}\right\rangle=~

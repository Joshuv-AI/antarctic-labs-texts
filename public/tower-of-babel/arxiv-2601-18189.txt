Smooth, Sparse, and Stable: Finite-Time Exact Skeleton Recovery via Smoothed Proximal Gradients
Rui Wu, Yongjun Li

Abstract
Continuous optimization has significantly advanced causal discovery, yet existing methods (e.g., NOTEARS) generally guarantee only asymptotic convergence to a stationary point. This often yields dense weighted matrices that require arbitrary post-hoc thresholding to recover a DAG. This gap between continuous optimization and discrete graph structures remains a fundamental challenge. In this paper, we bridge this gap by proposing the Hybrid-Order Acyclicity Constraint (AHOC) and optimizing it via the Smoothed Proximal Gradient (SPG-AHOC) . Leveraging the Manifold Identification Property of proximal algorithms, we provide a rigorous theoretical guarantee: the Finite-Time Oracle Property . We prove that under standard identifiability assumptions, SPG-AHOC recovers the exact DAG support (structure) in finite iterations, even when optimizing a smoothed approximation. This result eliminates structural ambiguity, as our algorithm returns graphs with exact zero entries without heuristic truncation. Empirically, SPG-AHOC achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and strongly corroborates the finite-time identification theory.

Introduction

The transition from correlation to causation relies on dissecting the precise structure of interactions. However, a chasm exists between the discrete nature of graphs and the continuous optimization tools used to find them.
Historically, this problem was addressed using combinatorial search algorithms
(
4
)
, which scale poorly with the number of variables
d
.
The field witnessed a paradigm shift with the introduction of NOTEARS
(
10
)
, which reformulated the discrete Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) constraint into a continuous equality constraint, enabling the use of efficient continuous optimization techniques.

The Gap: Asymptotic Convergence vs. Exact Structure.

Despite the computational advances, a critical disconnect remains. Existing methods typically guarantee only
asymptotic convergence
to a stationary point. In practice, this results in dense weighted adjacency matrices where "zero" edges are represented by small non-zero values (e.g.,
10^{-4}
).
This necessitates arbitrary
post-hoc thresholding
to recover a valid DAG.
In scientific discovery, this introduces
Scientific Ambiguity
: does a small weight represent a weak causal link or numerical noise?
Furthermore, standard constraints suffer from
Structural Constraint Instability (SCI)
, facing a trade-off between vanishing gradients (hindering sparsity) and exploding gradients (causing numerical divergence).

Our Solution: Topological Locking.

To bridge this gap, we propose a theoretically grounded framework that ensures
Finite-Time Exact Recovery
.
We introduce the
Hybrid-Order Acyclicity Constraint (AHOC)
, which satisfies a complete set of stability axioms.
Crucially, we optimize this constraint using the
Smoothed Proximal Gradient (SPG-AHOC)
.
We identify a mechanism we term
Topological Locking
(visualized in Figure
2
): while standard gradient descent asymptotically approaches zero, the proximal operator explicitly "snaps" the optimization trajectory onto the sparse manifold in finite steps once it enters a
Geometric Stability Zone
.
This allows our method to return graphs with
exact zero entries
, eliminating the need for heuristic truncation.

Contributions.
Our main contributions are threefold:

•

Axiomatic Framework & Impossibility Theorem:
We identify the root causes of instability in existing methods and prove that standard smooth constraints cannot simultaneously satisfy gradient non-vanishing and boundedness axioms. We propose the AHOC family to resolve this.

•

Finite-Time Oracle Property:
We provide a rigorous proof that SPG-AHOC achieves exact support recovery in finite iterations. We show that the "Topological Locking" effect of the proximal operator makes the support recovery robust to the smoothing bias required for optimization.

•

SOTA Performance & Robustness:
Empirically, SPG-AHOC achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on synthetic benchmarks and real-world data (Sachs), while demonstrating superior computational efficiency in low-to-mid dimensions compared to unconstrained baselines.

Theoretical Analysis of Acyclicity Constraints

In this section, we analyze the numerical properties of differentiable acyclicity constraints and propose our solution.

2.1
Our Method: An Axiomatic Diagnosis

We propose three fundamental axioms for stable acyclicity constraints
h(\mathbf{W})
:

Axiom 2.1

(Correctness)
.

h(\mathbf{W})=0\iff\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{W})\text{ is a DAG}
.

Consequence of Violation: Converges to incorrect structures.

Axiom 2.2

(
L_{1}
-Synergy)
.

\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h(\delta\cdot\mathbf{U})\right\rVert_{F}>0
.

Consequence of Violation (Type I SCI):
L_{1}
gradient dominates, hindering sparse DAG discovery.

Axiom 2.3

(Numerical Boundedness)
.

\sup_{\mathbf{W}}|h(\mathbf{W})|<\infty
and
\sup_{\mathbf{W}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h(\mathbf{W})\right\rVert_{F}<\infty
.

Consequence of Violation (Type II SCI): Exploding gradients cause numerical divergence (‘NaN‘/‘Inf‘). This can be fatal unless the optimizer employs a "safety net".

2.2
Core Finding 1: The
\mathcal{H}
Family and its Incompatibility

We reveal a fundamental
Impossibility Theorem
for the standard family of constraints.

Definition 2.4

(Standard Hadamard Constraint Class
\mathcal{H}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the family of spectral constraints where the dependency on
\mathbf{W}
acts exclusively through the entry-wise magnitude matrix
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W}
(or
\mathbf{A}=\left\lvert\mathbf{W}\right\rvert
). Specifically,
h(\mathbf{W})=F(\mathbf{A})
where
F
is a
C^{1}
smooth function on the cone of non-negative matrices.

Theorem 2.5

(Refined Incompatibility Theorem)
.

For any constraint
h\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
h(\mathbf{0})=0
, if the core function
F
is smooth at the origin, then
h(\mathbf{W})
cannot simultaneously satisfy Axiom
2.2
(
L_{1}
-Synergy) and Axiom
2.3
(Numerical Boundedness).

Implication: Standard constraints face an unavoidable trade-off. Quadratic dependencies (required for smoothness at origin) inherently conflict with the linear sensitivity required by
L_{1}
regularization.

Introducing Adaptive Scale Normalization (ASN),
\phi_{\text{ASN}}(\mathbf{W};\epsilon)=\frac{\mathbf{A}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}+\epsilon}
, yields the AAC constraint:

h_{\text{AAC}}(\mathbf{W};\epsilon)\coloneqq\tr\left(\expm\left(\phi_{\text{ASN}}(\mathbf{W};\epsilon)\right)\right)-d

Theorem 2.6

(Axiomatic Analysis of AAC)
.

AAC satisfies Axioms
2.1
and
2.3
, but fails Axiom
2.2
.

Implication: AAC fixes Type II SCI (satisfies A3) but not Type I SCI (fails A2), as its gradient is

and still vanishes.

2.4
The Theoretical Solution: Hybrid-Order Constraints (AHOC/S-AHOC)

To satisfy Axiom 2 (
L_{1}
-Synergy), which requires a non-vanishing gradient signal as
\mathbf{W}\to 0
, we introduce a first-order term via the Hybrid-Order Core (HOC):

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W};\alpha)\coloneqq\alpha(\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W})+(1-\alpha)\left\lvert\mathbf{W}\right\rvert,\quad\alpha\in[0,1)

Applying normalization yields two variants:

Definition 2.7

(AHOC and S-AHOC Constraints)
.

Let
h_{\exp}(\mathbf{M})=\tr(\expm(\mathbf{M}))-d
.

•

AHOC (ASN Core):

h_{\text{AHOC}}(\mathbf{W};\alpha,\epsilon)\coloneqq h_{\exp}\left(\frac{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W};\alpha)}{\left\lVert\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W};\alpha)\right\rVert_{F}+\epsilon}\right)

•

S-AHOC (S-ASN Core):

h_{\text{S-AHOC}}(\mathbf{W};\alpha)\coloneqq\tr\left(\expm\left(\frac{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W};\alpha)}{\left\lVert\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W};\alpha)\right\rVert_{F}+1}\right)\right)-d

Theorem 2.8

(Axiomatic Analysis of AHOC/S-AHOC)
.

Both
h_{\text{AHOC}}
and
h_{\text{S-AHOC}}
satisfy all three axioms: Correctness (A1),
L_{1}
-Synergy (A2), and Numerical Boundedness (A3).

Implication: The AHOC/S-AHOC family is the first theoretically complete solution, resolving both SCI types.

2.5
The Smoothness Barrier: Theory vs. Optimization

While the S-AHOC constraint theoretically satisfies all three stability axioms, it represents a
Platonic ideal
rather than a directly optimizable function.
Specifically, because the core
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W})
involves
\left\lvert\mathbf{W}\right\rvert
, the constraint
h_{\text{S-AHOC}}
is non-differentiable at any point where
\mathbf{W}_{ij}=0
.
This violates the standard assumption of Proximal Gradient methods, which require the smooth part of the objective to have a Lipschitz-continuous gradient
(
1
)
.
Directly applying subgradient methods would forfeit the convergence rates we desire.
Therefore, SPG-AHOC (Section
2.6
) serves as the necessary algorithmic bridge: it optimizes a smoothed approximation to recover the properties of the ideal S-AHOC via the topological locking mechanism.

Remark 2.9

(Theoretical Barrier for Proximal-LS)
.

The convergence guarantees of Proximal-LS strictly require that the smooth component of the objective function, let’s call it
f(\mathbf{W})
(which includes
h_{\text{AHOC}}
), has a Lipschitz continuous gradient
(
1
)
. However, due to the
\left\lvert\mathbf{W}\right\rvert
term,
h_{\text{AHOC}}
is non-differentiable at any
\mathbf{W}_{ij}=0
, and thus its gradient is not well-defined, let alone Lipschitz continuous, over its entire domain.

2.6
Our Algorithmic Solution: Smoothed Proximal Gradient for AHOC (SPG-AHOC)

To overcome the optimization barrier, we propose the Smoothed Proximal Gradient for AHOC (SPG-AHOC) algorithm. The core idea is to apply Proximal-LS not to the original non-smooth problem, but to a smooth approximation of it. We replace the non-smooth absolute value function
\left\lvert x\right\rvert
with a smooth counterpart,
\text{smooth\_abs}(x;\delta)=\sqrt{x^{2}+\delta^{2}}
, where
\delta>0
is a small smoothing parameter.

This leads to a smoothed HOC core:

\tilde{\mathbf{M}}(\mathbf{W};\alpha,\delta)\coloneqq\alpha(\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W})+(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\mathbf{W}^{2}+\delta^{2}}

And the corresponding smooth approximation for the AHOC constraint:

\tilde{h}_{\text{AHOC}}(\mathbf{W};\alpha,\epsilon,\delta)\coloneqq h_{\exp}\left(\frac{\tilde{\mathbf{M}}(\mathbf{W};\alpha,\delta)}{\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{M}}(\mathbf{W};\alpha,\delta)\right\rVert_{F}+\epsilon}\right)

The Smooth-Non-Smooth Decoupling.

Methodologically, we propose the
Decoupled Optimization Paradigm
. Unlike prior works that enforce smoothness globally (sacrificing exact sparsity) or ignore smoothness (risking instability), we mathematically decouple the
geometric feasibility
(via smoothed AHOC) from the
structural identification
(via non-smooth
L_{1}
).
Specifically, we formulate the problem as:

\min_{\mathbf{W}}\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{W})+\text{ALM}_{\delta}(\mathbf{W})}_{F(\mathbf{W})\text{ (Smooth Component)}}+\underbrace{\lambda_{1}\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{1}}_{R(\mathbf{W})\text{ (Non-Smooth Component)}}

Here, the smoothing via
\delta
ensures that the augmented Lagrangian part
F(\mathbf{W})
has a Lipschitz continuous gradient, while the regularization part
R(\mathbf{W})
remains non-differentiable. We show that this decoupling is necessary to bypass the Incompatibility Theorem and achieve finite-time exact recovery.

\delta>0

(Convergence Rate)

Since the smoothed objective possesses a Lipschitz continuous gradient, SPG-AHOC attains a sublinear convergence rate of

\min_{0\leq i<k}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{W}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{C}{k}

Theoretical Analysis of the Smoothing Trade-off.

While smoothing restores differentiability, it introduces a trade-off between approximation accuracy and optimization speed. We rigorously derive this relationship below.

First, we establish the approximation error bound (Bias).

Proposition 2.14

(Uniform Approximation Bound)
.

For any
\mathbf{W}
, the difference between the original and smoothed constraints is uniformly bounded by
\delta

\sup_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\left\lvert\tilde{h}_{\text{AHOC}}(\mathbf{W})-h_{\text{AHOC}}(\mathbf{W})\right\rvert\leq C_{1}\cdot d\cdot\delta

where
C_{1}
is a constant depending on
\alpha
.

Second, we analyze the impact of
\delta
on the optimization landscape (Variance). The "hardness" of the optimization is governed by the Lipschitz constant
L_{\delta}
of the gradient
\nabla\tilde{h}_{\text{AHOC}}
. This is determined by the maximum curvature (Hessian spectral norm) of the smoothing term.
Consider the scalar smoothing function
s(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}+\delta^{2}}
. Its second derivative is:

s^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{\delta^{2}}{(x^{2}+\delta^{2})^{3/2}}

(1)

The maximum curvature occurs at
x=0
, where
s^{\prime\prime}(0)=1/\delta
.
Lifting this to the matrix constraint function via the chain rule, the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla\tilde{h}_{\text{AHOC}}
scales as:

L_{\delta}\approx\sup_{\mathbf{W}}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}_{\text{AHOC}}(\mathbf{W})\right\rVert_{2}\propto\frac{1}{\delta}

(2)

This scaling implies a critical computational cost. To ensure stability, the proximal gradient step size
\eta
must satisfy
\eta\leq\frac{1}{L_{\delta}}
. Therefore:

\eta_{\max}\propto\delta

(3)

This reveals the
Approximation-Optimization Dilemma
:

•

Small
\delta
(Low Bias):
The smoothed constraint
\tilde{h}
closely approximates the true DAG constraint. However,
L_{\delta}\to\infty
and
\eta\to 0
, causing the algorithm to require
\mathcal{O}(1/\delta)
iterations to make progress, potentially stalling in practice.

•

Large
\delta
(Fast Convergence):
The gradients are smooth (
L_{\delta}
is small), allowing large steps and fast convergence. However, the stationary point of
\tilde{h}
may differ significantly from the true DAG constraint, introducing structural errors.

Conclusion:
SPG-AHOC resolves this by employing a backtracking line search that automatically adapts
\eta
to the local geometry
L_{\delta}
, eliminating the need to manually tune step sizes for different
\delta
.

In this section, we elevate the analysis from
asymptotic convergence
to
finite-time manifold identification
.

Our core theoretical contribution is to frame the recovery problem not as approximation, but as
Topological Locking
. We show that the interaction between the smoothed constraint and the non-smooth regularizer creates a
Geometric Stability Zone
.
Once the iterate
\mathbf{W}_{k}

Finite-Time Oracle Property

3.1
Statistical Assumptions

Let
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W})
be the smooth component of our objective (loss + penalty). We define the difference
\Delta=\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{W}^{*}
and the support set
S=\text{supp}(\mathbf{W}^{*})

Assumption 3.1

(Local Restricted Strong Convexity (L-RSC))
.

Standard theory often assumes global curvature. However, for manifold identification, we only require the curvature condition to hold locally.
Specifically, let
\mathbb{S}_{\text{sparse}}
be the subspace of sparse matrices consistent with the DAG support.
We assume there exists a radius
R>0
such that for all
\Delta\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{0},R)\cap\mathbb{S}_{\text{sparse}}
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{W}^{*}+\Delta)-\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{W}^{*})-\left\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{W}^{*}),\Delta\right\rangle_{F}\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\Delta\|_{F}^{2}

(4)

Significance:
This relaxes the requirement from the entire space to the neighborhood where the topological locking occurs.

Remark 3.2

(The Geometry of Localization)
.

One might question if the optimization trajectory stays within the local region defined in Assumption
3.1
.
As shown in Proposition
2.15
, the regularization term
\lambda_{1}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{1}
acts as a
geometric anchor
.
Provided
\lambda_{1}\geq\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{0})\|_{\infty}
, the level sets of the objective function are compact and contained within the neighborhood of the sparse manifold.
Therefore, the strict Global RSC assumption is theoretically redundant;
Local RSC is sufficient
for our finite-time guarantees.

Assumption 3.3

(Irrepresentable Condition)
.

Let
\mathbf{H}=\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}^{*})
be the Hessian. To ensure the
L_{1}
penalty does not select spurious edges, we require:

\|\mathbf{H}_{S^{c}S}(\mathbf{H}_{SS})^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq 1-\gamma,\quad\text{for some }\gamma\in(0,1]

(5)

Remark 3.4

(Contextualizing Assumptions)
.

We acknowledge that the Irrepresentable Condition is restrictive and may be violated in the presence of strong latent confounders or highly correlated variables. However, this is a fundamental information-theoretic limit for
any

L_{1}
-based structure learning method (Lasso-type), not a specific limitation of our algorithm. Furthermore, we empirically verify that this local condition holds for all synthetic datasets used in our experiments (see Appendix).

Assumption 3.5

(Beta-Min Condition)
.

The true causal signals are distinguishable from noise. Let
\lambda_{1}
be the regularization parameter. We assume:

\min_{(i,j)\in S}|\mathbf{W}^{*}_{ij}|\geq\frac{4\lambda_{1}}{\kappa}

Main Result

\delta^{*}\coloneqq\sup\{\delta>0:\forall\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{W}^{*})

5
Limitations and Future Work

Theoretical Assumptions.

Our finite-time guarantees rely on the Irrepresentable Condition (Assumption
3.3
). We acknowledge this is a strong assumption often violated in data with high multicollinearity (e.g., strong confounders).
However, this is a fundamental information-theoretic limit for
any
Lasso-type structure learning method, regardless of the acyclicity constraint used.
When this condition is violated, SPG-AHOC (like all
L_{1}
-based methods) may yield false positives, though our experimental results on near-cyclic graphs suggest robust performance in practice compared to baselines.

Computational Scalability.
While SPG-AHOC is feasible for
d=500
, the
\mathcal{O}(d^{3})
complexity of the matrix exponential remains a bottleneck for ultra-high-dimensional graphs (
d\geq 1000
). Unlike unconstrained methods (e.g., DAGMA) that trade exactness for speed, our method prioritizes structural fidelity. Future work involves investigating approximate matrix exponential methods (e.g., Krylov subspace) to bridge this gap.

Extreme Geometry.
As observed in Exp 4, the AHOC constraint can face numerical instability in extremely near-cyclic regions (
\rho\to 1
). This suggests that dynamic adjustment of the smoothing parameter
\delta
or the hybrid factor
\alpha
may be necessary for such edge cases.

6
Conclusion

This work introduces a unified axiomatic framework for differentiable acyclicity constraints, revealing inherent limitations in standard approaches. We identify the AHOC family as the first theoretically complete constraint satisfying all axioms. However, we demonstrate that AHOC’s non-smoothness makes it theoretically incompatible with standard Proximal-LS.

Our core contribution, SPG-AHOC, overcomes this barrier via smoothing. We provide a formal convergence guarantee (Theorem
2.12
) and demonstrate state-of-the-art empirical performance. Crucially, we uncover the dual role of
\lambda_{1}
as a
Geometric Stabilizer
, showing that satisfying
\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{1}
is a prerequisite for convergence, even if this requirement imposes a trade-off with sensitivity in high-dimensional, low-sample regimes.

This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of Causal Discovery. By improving the stability and theoretical grounding of structure learning algorithms, this work could lead to more reliable models in fields like biology and economics. There are no specific negative societal consequences highlighted here.

A.1.1
Proof of Acyclicity Equivalence for Hadamard Product

Proposition A.1

(Acyclicity Equivalence)
.

Let
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a weighted adjacency matrix and
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W}
. The graph
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{W})
is a DAG if and only if the spectral radius
\rho(\mathbf{A})=0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{W})
be the graph corresponding to
\mathbf{W}
. Let
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W}
. Since
\mathbf{A}_{ij}=\mathbf{W}_{ij}^{2}\geq 0
,
\mathbf{A}
is non-negative. For any such non-negative matrix
\mathbf{A}
(with
\mathbf{A}_{ii}=0
), its spectral radius
\rho(\mathbf{A})=0
if and only if
\mathbf{A}
is nilpotent, which is equivalent to
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{A})
(and thus
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{W})
) being a DAG
[see e.g.,
10
, for properties of non-negative matrices]
. This ensures Axiom
2.1
for constraints based solely on
\mathbf{A}
.
∎

A.1.2
Proof of Type I and Type II SCI

Type I SCI (Vanishing Gradient):

Proof.

Let
h(\mathbf{W})=g(f(\mathbf{A}))
where
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W}
. The gradient is
\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h(\mathbf{W})=2\mathbf{W}\circ f^{\prime}(\mathbf{A})^{T}
. As
\mathbf{W}\to 0
,
\mathbf{A}\to 0
. Since
f
is analytic,
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{A})
approaches a constant. Thus,
\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h(\mathbf{W})\right\rVert_{F}\approx\left\lVert 2c_{1}\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{F}=\mathcal{O}(\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{F})
. Therefore,
\lim_{\mathbf{W}\to 0}\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h(\mathbf{W})\right\rVert_{F}=0
, violating Axiom
2.2
.
∎

Type II SCI (Exploding Gradient):

Proof.

For
h_{\logdet}(\mathbf{W})=-\logdet(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A})
, the gradient is
2\mathbf{W}\circ[(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A})^{-1}]^{T}
. As
\rho(\mathbf{A})\to 1^{-}
,
\left\lVert(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A})^{-1}\right\rVert_{F}\to\infty
. Thus
\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h\right\rVert_{F}\to\infty
, violating Axiom
2.3
.
For
h_{\exp}
, while the value is bounded for finite inputs, for non-normal matrices, elements of
\expm(\mathbf{A})
can grow exponentially large even if
\rho(\mathbf{A})
is small, leading to unbounded gradients in practice.
∎

A.1.3
Proof of Theorem
2.5
(Incompatibility)

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W}
. By the chain rule, the gradient of
h(\mathbf{W})=g(f(\mathbf{A}))
is:

\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h(\mathbf{W})=\left[\nabla g(f(\mathbf{A}))\cdot f^{\prime}(\mathbf{A})\right]\circ\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\mathbf{W}}

Note that
\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial\mathbf{W}}=2\mathbf{W}
.
We analyze the behavior as
\mathbf{W}\to\mathbf{0}
. Since
f
is
C^{2}
-smooth,
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{A})
is continuous and bounded in a neighborhood of the origin. Let
B_{0}
be a ball around the origin. Since Axiom
2.3
requires gradients to be bounded, the term
[\nabla g\cdot f^{\prime}]
must be bounded by some constant
C_{1}
within
B_{0}
.
Thus, we can bound the Frobenius norm of the gradient:

\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h(\mathbf{W})\right\rVert_{F}\leq C_{1}\left\lVert 2\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{F}=2C_{1}\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{F}

Taking the limit as
\mathbf{W}\to\mathbf{0}
:

\lim_{\mathbf{W}\to\mathbf{0}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h(\mathbf{W})\right\rVert_{F}=0

This limit being zero directly violates Axiom
2.2
, which requires
\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\left\lVert\nabla h(\delta\mathbf{U})\right\rVert_{F}>0
.

Defense against Non-Smooth Core Functions:

One might argue that choosing a non-smooth core function
f(x)
could cancel the vanishing term. Consider
f(x)=x^{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in(0,1)
(e.g.,
\sqrt{x}
). The gradient scale would involve
f^{\prime}(x)\propto x^{\gamma-1}
.
As
\mathbf{W}\to\mathbf{0}
, we have
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{0}
, and consequently
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{A})\to\infty
(since
\gamma-1<0
).
This implies that while the gradient might not vanish, it would become unbounded (explode) at the origin.
Therefore, any attempt to fix Type I SCI (Vanishing Gradient) by relaxing smoothness inevitably triggers a violation of Axiom
2.3
(Numerical Boundedness). This proves the fundamental incompatibility for the family
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

A.3.2
Detailed Gradient Derivation for S-AHOC (Axiom 2)

We rigorously derive the gradient of
h_{\text{S-AHOC}}
to demonstrate the non-vanishing property without ambiguity.
Let
h(\mathbf{W})=\tr(\expm(\bar{\mathbf{M}}))-d
, where
\bar{\mathbf{M}}=\phi(\mathbf{M})=\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{M}\right\rVert_{F}+1}
and
\mathbf{M}=\alpha(\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W})+(1-\alpha)\left\lvert\mathbf{W}\right\rvert
.

Using the chain rule, the gradient is given by the product of the Jacobian matrices:

\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h=\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{M}}{\partial\mathbf{W}}\right)^{T}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\mathbf{M}}\right)^{T}\nabla_{\bar{\mathbf{M}}}h_{\exp}

We analyze the limit behavior of each term as
\mathbf{W}\to\mathbf{0}
:

1.

Gradient of Matrix Exponential:

\nabla_{\bar{\mathbf{M}}}h_{\exp}=[\expm(\bar{\mathbf{M}})]^{T}
. As
\mathbf{W}\to\mathbf{0}
, we have
\mathbf{M}\to\mathbf{0}
and
\bar{\mathbf{M}}\to\mathbf{0}
. Since
\expm(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{I}
, this term approaches the identity matrix
\mathbf{I}
.

2.

Jacobian of Normalization:
Let
D=\left\lVert\mathbf{M}\right\rVert_{F}+1
. The differential is
d\bar{\mathbf{M}}=\frac{d\mathbf{M}}{D}-\frac{\mathbf{M}\langle\mathbf{M},d\mathbf{M}\rangle_{F}}{D^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}\right\rVert_{F}}
.
As
\mathbf{M}\to\mathbf{0}
, the denominator
D\to 1
, and the second term (involving
\mathbf{M}
in the numerator) vanishes faster than the first. Thus, the Jacobian operator
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\mathbf{M}}
approaches the Identity operator.

3.

Gradient of Core
\mathbf{M}
:
The element-wise derivative is:

\frac{\partial\mathbf{M}_{ij}}{\partial\mathbf{W}_{ij}}=2\alpha\mathbf{W}_{ij}+(1-\alpha)\text{sign}(\mathbf{W}_{ij})

As
\mathbf{W}_{ij}\to 0
, the quadratic term
2\alpha\mathbf{W}_{ij}
vanishes. However, the sign term
\text{sign}(\mathbf{W}_{ij})
remains
\pm 1
(for non-zero entries approaching zero).

Conclusion:
Combining these limits, for any direction
\mathbf{U}
(where
\mathbf{W}=\delta\mathbf{U}
), the gradient behaves as:

\lim_{\mathbf{W}\to\mathbf{0}}(\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}h)_{ij}\approx 1\cdot 1\cdot(0+(1-\alpha)\text{sign}(\mathbf{W}_{ij}))=(1-\alpha)\text{sign}(\mathbf{W}_{ij})

This vector has a Frobenius norm of
(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\text{nnz}(\mathbf{W})}
, which is strictly positive provided
\alpha<1
. This formally proves adherence to Axiom
2.2
.

Appendix B
Proof of Finite-Time Structure Recovery (Theorem
3.7
)

B.1
Notation and Strategy

Let
\hat{\mathbf{W}}
be the stationary point (local minimizer) of the objective function
F(\mathbf{W})=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W})+\lambda_{1}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{1}
.
The proof proceeds in two critical stages:

1.

Stage 1 (Statistical Consistency):
We prove that under the stated assumptions, the stationary point
\hat{\mathbf{W}}
is unique in the local neighborhood of
\mathbf{W}^{*}
and shares the exact support of the ground truth, i.e.,
\text{sign}(\hat{\mathbf{W}})=\text{sign}(\mathbf{W}^{*})
. We use the
Primal-Dual Witness Construction
technique
[
8
]
.

2.

Stage 2 (Finite-Time Identification):
We prove that the SPG algorithm generates a sequence
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}
that identifies the support of
\hat{\mathbf{W}}
in finite steps. We rely on the
Manifold Identification Property
of proximal algorithms
[
5
]
.

B.2
Stage 1: Statistical Support Consistency

We construct a "witness" solution
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}
restricted to the true support
S=\text{supp}(\mathbf{W}^{*})
and show that it satisfies the optimality conditions for the full problem.

Step 1.1: Restricted Optimization.

Let
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}
be the solution to the restricted problem:

\tilde{\mathbf{W}}=\argmin_{\mathbf{W}:\mathbf{W}_{S^{c}}=0}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W})+\lambda_{1}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{1}

By construction,
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{S^{c}}=0
. For
(i,j)\in S
, the KKT condition implies:

\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{S}+\lambda_{1}\text{sign}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{S}=0

Step 1.2: Bound on Estimation Error.

Using the Mean Value Theorem,
\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{S}=\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}^{*})_{S}+\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathbf{W}})_{SS}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}-\mathbf{W}^{*})_{S}
.
Substituting this into the KKT condition and utilizing the RSC (Assumption
3.1
) which bounds the minimum eigenvalue of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}
, we can derive the standard error bound:

\|\tilde{\mathbf{W}}-\mathbf{W}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\tilde{\mathbf{W}}-\mathbf{W}^{*}\|_{F}\leq\frac{2\lambda_{1}\sqrt{|S|}}{\kappa}

(Note: For detailed derivation of this bound from RSC, see
6
).

Step 1.3: Verify Strict Dual Feasibility (Quantifying Sample Complexity).

For
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}
to be the global solution, we must check the strict dual feasibility condition on the zero set
S^{c}
:
|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}})_{ij}|<\lambda_{1}
for all
(i,j)\in S^{c}
.
Using Taylor expansion and the Irrepresentable Condition, strict dual feasibility holds if the noise term is bounded:
\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{W}^{*})_{S^{c}}\|_{\infty}<\frac{\gamma\lambda_{1}}{2}
.

Assuming the noise in the linear SEM is sub-Gaussian, standard high-dimensional statistical theory
[
9
, e.g.,]
provides the concentration inequality:

P\left(\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{W}^{*})_{S^{c}}\|_{\infty}\geq t\right)\leq c_{1}\exp\left(-c_{2}Nt^{2}\right)

Setting the threshold
t=\frac{\gamma\lambda_{1}}{2}
, we require the failure probability to be negligible (
\leq\delta_{prob}
). This implies a lower bound on the sample size
N
:

c_{1}\exp\left(-c_{2}N(\frac{\gamma\lambda_{1}}{2})^{2}\right)\leq\delta_{prob}\implies N\geq\frac{4\log(c_{1}/\delta_{prob})}{c_{2}(\gamma\lambda_{1})^{2}}

Thus, provided
N=\Omega(\frac{\log d}{\lambda_{1}^{2}})
, strict dual feasibility holds with high probability.

Step 1.4: Verify Support Inclusion.

Using the Beta-Min Condition (Assumption
3.5
) and the error bound from Step 1.2:

|\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{ij}|\geq|\mathbf{W}^{*}_{ij}|-|\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{ij}-\mathbf{W}^{*}_{ij}|\geq\frac{4\lambda_{1}}{\kappa}-\frac{2\lambda_{1}}{\kappa}>0,\quad\forall(i,j)\in S

Thus,
\text{supp}(\hat{\mathbf{W}})=S=\text{supp}(\mathbf{W}^{*})
.

B.3
Stage 2: Finite-Time Algorithmic Identification

Having established that the stationary point
\hat{\mathbf{W}}
has the correct support, we now show SPG-AHOC finds it in finite time.
Let the objective be
F(\mathbf{W})=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W})+\lambda_{1}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{1}
. The update rule is:

\mathbf{W}_{k+1}=\text{prox}_{\eta\lambda_{1}}(\mathbf{W}_{k}-\eta\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{k}))

Step 2.1: Non-Degeneracy Condition.

From Step 1.3, we established the
Strict Dual Feasibility
for the stationary point
\hat{\mathbf{W}}
:

|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\hat{\mathbf{W}})_{ij}|\leq\lambda_{1}-\tau,\quad\forall(i,j)\in S^{c}

(7)

where
\tau=\gamma\lambda_{1}/2>0
. This is crucial. It means the "force" pushing zero entries away from zero is strictly less than the regularization threshold.

Step 2.2: Convergence of Gradients.

Since
\mathcal{L}
is smooth (
C^{2}
continuous) and
\mathbf{W}_{k}\to\hat{\mathbf{W}}
(asymptotic convergence of Proximal Gradient), the gradients converge:
\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{k})\to\nabla\mathcal{L}(\hat{\mathbf{W}})
.
Therefore, there exists a finite iteration
K_{1}
such that for all
k\geq K_{1}
:

\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{k})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\hat{\mathbf{W}})\|_{\infty}<\frac{\tau}{2}

Step 2.3: Zero Identification (Handling Smoothing Bias).

Recall we optimize the smoothed constraint
\tilde{h}
with parameter
\delta
. The gradient used in the update is
\nabla\tilde{\mathcal{L}}=\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{exact}}+\mathbf{E}_{bias}
, where
\left\lVert\mathbf{E}_{bias}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\delta
(Lipschitz property).
The proximal operator sets
(\mathbf{W}_{k+1})_{ij}=0
if the input falls within
[-\lambda_{1}\eta,\lambda_{1}\eta]
.
From Step 2.1, the exact gradient on the zero set is bounded by
\lambda_{1}-\tau
(where
\tau=\gamma\lambda_{1}/2
). With smoothing bias, the effective gradient magnitude is at most:

|\nabla\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}|\leq(\lambda_{1}-\tau)+C\delta

For the proximal operator to correctly identify zero, we need this magnitude to be strictly less than
\lambda_{1}
. This requires:

C\delta<\tau\implies\delta<\frac{\gamma\lambda_{1}}{2C}

Implication:
As long as the smoothing parameter
\delta
is chosen to be
\mathcal{O}(\lambda_{1})
, the bias is "swallowed" by the strict dual feasibility margin
\tau
, ensuring exact zero recovery despite the smooth approximation.

Step 2.4: Non-Zero Identification.

For
(i,j)\in S
, we know
|\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{ij}|>0
. By simple continuity, there exists
K_{3}
such that for all
k\geq K_{3}
,
(\mathbf{W}_{k})_{ij}
remains non-zero.

B.4
Conclusion

Let
K=\max(K_{1},K_{2},K_{3})
. For all
k\geq K
, we have
\text{supp}(\mathbf{W}_{k})=\text{supp}(\hat{\mathbf{W}})=\text{supp}(\mathbf{W}^{*})
.
This completes the proof of Finite-Time Oracle Property. ∎

E.1
Boundedness of Optimization Trajectory

Before deriving the Lipschitz constants and approximation bounds, we first establish that the optimization iterates remain within a compact set.

Lemma E.1

(Bounded Trajectory)
.

Suppose the stability condition
\lambda_{1}\geq\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{0})\right\rVert_{\infty}
holds (Proposition
2.15
). Let
L_{\delta}(\mathbf{W})=\tilde{L}(\mathbf{W})+\lambda_{1}\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{1}
be the objective function.
The sublevel set
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\{\mathbf{W}:L_{\delta}(\mathbf{W})\leq L_{\delta}(\mathbf{0})\}
is compact. Consequently, the sequence
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\}
generated by SPG-AHOC satisfies:

\sup_{k\geq 0}\left\lVert\mathbf{W}_{k}\right\rVert_{F}\leq R<\infty

(8)

for some finite constant
R
depending on the initialization and
\lambda_{1}
.

Proof.

At initialization,
\mathbf{W}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
. The objective value is
L_{\delta}(\mathbf{0})=\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{0})
.
Since the constraint term in
\tilde{L}
is non-negative and the loss
\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}
is typically coercive (e.g., quadratic for linear SEMs) or bounded below, the term
\lambda_{1}\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{1}
dominates the growth of the objective as
\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert\to\infty
.
Specifically, the coercivity of the
L_{1}
regularizer ensures that
\lim_{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert\to\infty}L_{\delta}(\mathbf{W})=\infty
.
Since SPG is a descent method (Theorem
2.12
),
\mathbf{W}_{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
for all
k
. The compactness of
\mathcal{S}_{0}
implies uniform boundedness of the iterates.
This boundedness ensures that the Lipschitz constant
L_{\delta}\approx\sup_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}(\mathbf{W})\right\rVert
is finite.
∎

E.2
Proof of Theorem
2.12
(Lipschitz Gradient)

Proof.

The objective uses the smoothed absolute value
s(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}+\delta^{2}}
.
The Lipschitz constant of the gradient (
L_{\delta}
) is determined by the maximum eigenvalue of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\tilde{L}
.
Applying the chain rule to the composite function
h(\mathbf{W})=g(\phi(s(\mathbf{W})))
, the Hessian contains terms involving the first and second derivatives of the components.

The dominant term comes from the second derivative of the smoothing function
s(x)
:

s^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{\delta^{2}}{(x^{2}+\delta^{2})^{3/2}}

This function achieves its maximum at
x=0
, where
s^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta^{3}}=\frac{1}{\delta}
.

Other components (matrix exponential
g
, normalization
\phi
) are smooth and have bounded derivatives on the compact unit ball (enforced by
\phi
). Let
C_{rest}
be the bound for the product of these other derivatives. The Lipschitz constant is bounded by:

L_{\delta}\leq C_{rest}\cdot\sup_{x}|s^{\prime\prime}(x)|=\frac{C_{rest}}{\delta}

Therefore,
L_{\delta}=\mathcal{O}(1/\delta)
. To ensure convergence of the Proximal Gradient method (Descent Lemma), the step size must satisfy
\eta\leq\frac{1}{L_{\delta}}
, which implies
\eta\propto\delta
.
∎

E.3
Proof of Proposition
2.14
(Approximation Bound)

Proof.

We bound
\left\lvert\tilde{h}(\mathbf{W})-h(\mathbf{W})\right\rvert
.
The smoothing error is in the core:
\left\lvert\sqrt{x^{2}+\delta^{2}}-|x|\right\rvert\leq\delta
.
Thus
\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{M}}-\mathbf{M}\right\rVert_{F}\leq(1-\alpha)d\delta
.
The normalization map and matrix exponential are Lipschitz continuous on the relevant domains. Let their combined Lipschitz constant be
L
.
Then
\left\lvert\tilde{h}(\mathbf{W})-h(\mathbf{W})\right\rvert\leq L\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{M}}-\mathbf{M}\right\rVert_{F}\leq L(1-\alpha)d\delta
.
This confirms the uniform approximation bound
\mathcal{O}(\delta)
.
∎

Appendix F
Formal Derivation of Stability Condition

Proof.

A point
\mathbf{W}^{*}
is a stationary point of the objective
L_{\delta}(\mathbf{W})=\tilde{L}(\mathbf{W})+\lambda_{1}\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{1}
if and only if:

\mathbf{0}\in\nabla\tilde{L}(\mathbf{W}^{*})+\lambda_{1}\partial\left\lVert\mathbf{W}^{*}\right\rVert_{1}

where
\partial\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1}
is the subdifferential of the element-wise
L_{1}
norm.
At initialization
\mathbf{W}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
:
1. Vanishing Constraint Gradient: Due to the smoothing
s(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}+\delta^{2}}
, we have
s^{\prime}(0)=0
. Consequently,
\nabla\tilde{h}_{\text{AHOC}}(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
. The gradient of the smooth part reduces to the data fidelity term:
\nabla\tilde{L}(\mathbf{0})=\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{0})
.
2. Subdifferential at Origin: The subdifferential of the
L_{1}
norm at the origin is the unit hypercube:

\partial\left\lVert\mathbf{0}\right\rVert_{1}=\{\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\left\lvert\mathbf{G}_{ij}\right\rvert\leq 1,\forall i,j\}

Substituting these into the stationarity condition, we require:

-\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{0})\in\lambda_{1}\partial\left\lVert\mathbf{0}\right\rVert_{1}

\iff\left\lvert[\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{0})]_{ij}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{1},\quad\forall i,j

\iff\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{fit}}(\mathbf{0})\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{1}

If this condition is violated, the negative gradient direction lies outside the subdifferential cone, forcing the optimizer to take a non-zero step away from
\mathbf{0}
, thus breaking sparsity immediately.
∎

Appendix I
Theoretical Analysis of Near-Cyclic Instability

We provide a theoretical justification for the failure of AHOC on near-cyclic graphs (Experiment 4,
\rho\approx 1
), despite satisfying the Numerical Boundedness axiom.

While the normalization
\phi(\mathbf{W})
ensures the
value
of
h(\mathbf{W})
and its
gradient

\nabla h
are bounded, the
curvature
(Hessian) becomes ill-conditioned.
Consider the spectral radius formulation. As
\mathbf{W}
approaches a cycle, the spectral radius
\rho(\mathbf{W}\circ\mathbf{W})\to 1
.
The derivative of the matrix exponential involves the term
\expm(\mathbf{M})
. Even with normalization, the geometry of the feasible set
h(\mathbf{W})=0
forms a "thin manifold" near cyclic configurations.

Specifically, for the AHOC constraint, let
\mathbf{M}
be the core matrix. Near the boundary of the acyclic polytope:

1.

The condition number of the Hessian
\kappa(\nabla^{2}h)
diverges.

2.

The "valley" of the optimization landscape becomes extremely narrow.

3.

Standard first-order methods (like Proximal Gradient) bounce between the walls of this narrow valley unless the step size is infinitesimally small (
\eta\to 0
).

Unlike methods with a logarithmic barrier (like
h_{\log\det}=-\log\det(I-\mathbf{A})
) which provides a smooth repulsive force approaching infinity as

(effectively a "safety net"), the AHOC constraint is purely penalty-based. Without the barrier property, the optimizer can overshoot into the cyclic region where the gradient direction may drastically shift, leading to the oscillations observed in Figure

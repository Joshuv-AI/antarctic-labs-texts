Leibniz 2 2 -algebras, linear 2 2 -racks and the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation
Nanyan Xu, Yunhe Sheng

Abstract
. In this paper, first we show that a central Leibniz 2-algebra naturally gives rise to a solution of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation. Then we introduce the notion of linear 2-racks and show that a linear 2-rack also gives rise to a solution of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation. We show that a central Leibniz 2-algebra gives rise to a linear 2-rack if the underlying 2-vector space is splittable. Finally we discuss the relation between linear 2-racks and 2-racks, and show that a linear 2-rack gives rise to a 2-rack structure on the group-like category. A concrete example of strict 2-racks is constructed from an action of a strict 2-group.

1.
Introduction

The main purpose of this paper is to construct solutions of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation using central Leibniz 2-algebras and linear 2-racks. The relations between these algebraic structures are also investigated.

1.1.
The Yang-Baxter equation and related algebraic structures

The Yang-Baxter equation is a fundamental equation in mathematical physics, originating from the study of integrable quantum systems and statistical mechanics in the 1960s-70s. Its name honors the foundational work of C. Yang
[
33
]
and R. Baxter
[
5
]
. A solution of the Yang-Baxter equation on a vector space
V
is an invertible linear map
\mathcal{B}:V\otimes V\rightarrow V\otimes V
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}_{V})({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}\otimes\mathcal{B})(\mathcal{B}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}_{V})=({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}\otimes\mathcal{B})(\mathcal{B}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}_{V})({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}\otimes\mathcal{B}).

Leibniz algebras, first discovered by Bloh under the name of D-algebras
[
9
]
, and rediscovered by Loday in his study of algebraic K-theory
[
28
]
. Specially, a (right)
Leibniz algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a bilinear operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
such that the Leibniz identity holds:

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\,\,\,\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

In
[
26
]
, Lebed showed that a central Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},e)
, where
e
is a central element, gives rise to a solution of the Yang-Baxter equation via the following formula:

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x+e\otimes[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\,\,\,\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

A (right) rack is a set
X
equipped with a map
\lhd:X\times X\to X
such that the map
\bullet\lhd x:X\rightarrow X,~y\mapsto y\lhd x
is bijective for all
x\in X
, and

(x\lhd y)\lhd z=(x\lhd z)\lhd(y\lhd z),\quad\forall x,y,z\in X.

Racks play very important roles in the study of set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
[
3
,
15
,
27
]
.
Linear racks are rack objects in the category of coalgebras, see
[
11
,
22
,
25
]
for more details. In
[
25
]
, Lebed showed that a linear rack
(V,\Delta,\varepsilon,\lhd)
, where
(V,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a cocommutative coalgebra, gives rise to a solution
\mathcal{B}:V\otimes V\rightarrow V\otimes V
of the Yang-Baxter equation:

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\underline{}(u\otimes v)=v_{(1)}\otimes(u\lhd v_{(2)}),\,\,\,\forall u\otimes v\in V\otimes V.

Abramov and Zappala generalized this construction to trilinear racks (reversible TSD)
[
1
]
.

Lebed showed in
[
25
]
that a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
naturally gives rise to a linear rack structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{K}
, such that the solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation given by the linear rack
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{K}
and the central Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{K}
are the same.

1.2.
2-vector spaces and decategorification

A
2
-vector space
is an internal category in the category of vector spaces
[
4
]
. That is, a
2
-vector space
V=(V_{0},V_{1},s,t,{\mathbf{1}})
consists of
a vector space of objects
V_{0}
, a vector space of morphisms
V_{1}
, linear maps (source and target maps)
s,t:V_{1}\to V_{0}
,
and a linear map (the identity map)
{\mathbf{1}}:V_{0}\to V_{1}
satisfying some compatibility conditions.
One can put Lie/Leibniz algebra structures on 2-vector spaces, and obtain Lie/Leibniz 2-algebras (equivalently, 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras/Leibniz
∞
-algebras)
[
4
,
31
]
.

2-vector spaces are considered as the categorification of vector spaces. In other words, one can obtain vector spaces by decategorification of
2
-vector spaces.
One of the most natural ways to decategorify the information in a category is to take its Grothendieck group, which can be defined for an abelian category, an additive category, or an essentially small category with a bifunctor.
Given a bifunctor
\star:{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
on an essentially small category
{\mathcal{C}}
, one can obtain the Grothendieck group of
{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to the bifunctor
\star
as the quotient group

K^{\star}_{0}({\mathcal{C}})=\mathcal{F}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}})/\mathcal{N}_{\star}({\mathcal{C}}),

where
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}={\rm ob}({\mathcal{C}})/\cong
is the set of all isomorphism classes of objects in
{\mathcal{C}}
,
\mathcal{F}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}})
is the free abelian group generated by the set
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}
, and
\mathcal{N}_{\star}({\mathcal{C}})
is the normal subgroup generated by elements
\{\overline{a\star b}-\overline{a}-\overline{b}~|~a,b\in{\rm ob}({\mathcal{C}})\}
.

There is an alternative viewpoint regarding the Grothendieck group of
{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to an associative
and commutative bifunctor
\star
.
Given an associative and commutative bifunctor
\star
,

({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}},\star)
is an abelian semigroup,
where
\overline{a}{\star}\overline{b}=\overline{a\star b}
for any
\overline{a},\overline{b}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then the Grothendieck group
K^{\star}_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
with respect to the bifunctor
\star
is actually the group completion of the abelian semigroup
({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}},\star)
. More details about Grothendieck groups are available in
[
29
]
.

For a
2
-vector space
V=(V_{0},V_{1},s,t,{\mathbf{1}})
, the addition bifunctor
+
is associative and commutative.
Moreover, the set of all isomorphism classes of objects
\overline{V_{0}}=V_{0}/\cong
endowed with
+
is not merely an abelian semigroup, but indeed an abelian group, which has
\overline{0}
as the unit and
\overline{-x}
as the inverse for each
\overline{x}
.
Therefore, the group completion of the abelian semigroup
(\overline{V_{0}},+)
is
the abelian group
(\overline{V_{0}},+)
itself, which implies that the Grothendieck group of a
2
-vector space
V
is the abelian group
(\overline{V_{0}},+)
.
Furthermore, by inheriting the scalar multiplication structure from
V_{0}
,
\overline{V_{0}}
becomes a vector space.
In this case, the vector space
\overline{V_{0}}
is the decategorification of the
2
-vector space
V=(V_{0},V_{1},s,t,{\mathbf{1}})
.

1.3.
The Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation

As an extension of the Yang-Baxter equation, the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation is

(4)

\displaystyle S_{123}\circ S_{145}\circ S_{246}\circ S_{356}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{356}\circ S_{246}\circ S_{145}\circ S_{123},

where
V
is a vector space,
S\in\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes 3})
and
S_{ijk}\in\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes 6})
for
1\leqslant i<j<k\leqslant 6
are maps that act as
S
on the
ij~k
-th tensor components of the vector space
V^{\otimes 6}
, while acting as the identity on the remaining components. This equation was first proposed by Zamolodchikov
[
34
,
35
]
, who generalized the “triangle” structure of the Yang-Baxter equation to a “tetrahedron” structure by introducing the braiding relationship in three-dimensional spaces and proposed the algebraic form of the equation together with a conjectured solution. Later, Baxter rigorously proved this solution in
[
6
]
and derived the partition function of the Zamolodchikov model for an
n\times\infty\times\infty
lattice in
[
7
]
. The Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation is closely related to the integrable lattice models of statistical mechanics and quantum field theory, promoting significant progress in these research fields
[
21
,
23
,
24
,
30
]
.
Recently, the relationship between the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation and many algebraic structures has been explored
[
10
,
16
,
19
]
, and the theory of set-theoretic solutions has also been greatly developed
[
12
,
17
,
18
]
.

In
[
20
]
, Kapranov and Voevodsky interpreted the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation as the commutativity of a three-dimensional diagram with the shape of a permutohedron in a monoidal
2
-category.
They showed that the usual vertex formulation (
4
) of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation can be obtained by choosing a special example of monoidal
2
-categories
[
20
, Example 6.8]
.
Moreover, it is obvious that the
2
-category
2
Vect
with
2
-vector spaces as objects, linear functors as
1
-morphisms, and linear natural transformations as
2
-morphisms,
is a monoidal
2
-category, and the associated Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation was described in
[
4
]
. More precisely, the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation on a
2
-vector space
V
for a linear invertible functor
B:V\otimes V\to V\otimes V
and a linear natural isomorphism
Y:(B\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes B)(B\otimes{\rm{Id}})\Rightarrow({\rm{Id}}\otimes B)(B\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes B)
reads as follows:

\displaystyle\big[({\rm{Id}}\otimes Y)\ast{\rm{Id}}_{(B\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes B\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B)}\big]\big[{\rm{Id}}_{({\rm{Id}}\otimes B\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B)}\ast(Y\otimes{\rm{Id}})\ast{\rm{Id}}_{{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B}\big]

\displaystyle\big[{\rm{Id}}_{({\rm{Id}}\otimes B\otimes{\rm{Id}})(B\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}})}\ast({\rm{Id}}\otimes Y)\ast{\rm{Id}}_{B\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}}\big]\big[(Y\otimes{\rm{Id}})\ast{\rm{Id}}_{({\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B)({\rm{Id}}\otimes B\otimes{\rm{Id}})(B\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}})}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[{\rm{Id}}_{({\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B)({\rm{Id}}\otimes B\otimes{\rm{Id}})(B\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}})}\ast({\rm{Id}}\otimes Y)\big]\big[{\rm{Id}}_{{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B}\ast(Y\otimes{\rm{Id}})\ast{\rm{Id}}_{({\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B)({\rm{Id}}\otimes B\otimes{\rm{Id}})}\big]

\displaystyle\big[{\rm{Id}}_{B\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}}\ast({\rm{Id}}\otimes Y)\ast{\rm{Id}}_{(B\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes B\otimes{\rm{Id}})}\big]\big[{\rm{Id}}_{(B\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes B\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B)}\ast(Y\otimes{\rm{Id}})\big],

where the both sides of the equality are understood as the vertical composition of the linear natural transformations, and

\ast
represents the horizontal composition of natural transformations,
{\rm{Id}}_{{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B}
is the identity natural isomorphism of the functor
{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes B
and the same for the others. See the appendix for a diagram description of this equation.

Let the vector space
\overline{V_{0}}
be the decategorification of the
2
-vector space
V=(V_{0},V_{1},s,t,{\mathbf{1}})
, and
(B,Y)
a solution of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation on
V
.
Define a linear map
\overline{B}:\overline{V_{0}}\otimes\overline{V_{0}}\to\overline{V_{0}}\otimes\overline{V_{0}}
as follows:

(5)

\displaystyle\overline{B}(\overline{x}\otimes\overline{y})=\overline{B(x\otimes y)},\quad\forall\overline{x}\otimes\overline{y}\in\overline{V_{0}}\otimes\overline{V_{0}}.

Here we identify the vector space
\overline{V_{0}}\otimes\overline{V_{0}}
with
\overline{V_{0}\otimes V_{0}}
.
Then
\overline{B}
satisfies the Yang-Baxter equation:

(\overline{B}\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes\overline{B})(\overline{B}\otimes{\rm{Id}})=({\rm{Id}}\otimes\overline{B})(\overline{B}\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes\overline{B}),

since
Y
is a linear natural isomorphism. Thus, the Yang-Baxter equation serves as the decategorification of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation.

1.4.
Main results and outline of the paper

In this paper, we show that a Leibniz 2-algebra (which is the categorification of a Leibniz algebra, and equivalent to a 2-term Leibniz
∞
-algebra
[
2
]
) with a central object naturally gives rise to a solution of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation. On the one hand, this generalizes Baez and Crans’ construction in
[
4
]
; on the other hand, this can be viewed as the categorification of Lebed’s construction of solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation using central Leibniz algebras given in
[
26
]
. We introduce the notion of linear 2-racks and show that a linear 2-rack also gives rise to a solution of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation, which can be viewed as the categorification of Lebed’s construction of solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation using linear racks given in
[
25
]
. We show that a central Leibniz 2-algebra gives rise to a linear 2-rack if the underlying 2-vector space is splittable. Finally we show that a linear 2-rack gives rise to a 2-rack structure on the group-like category, and construct a concrete example of strict 2-racks from an action of a strict 2-group. Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{J},{\swabfamily e})
be a central Leibniz
2
-algebra.
Define
B:\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}
by

\displaystyle B(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y\otimes x+{\swabfamily e}\otimes[x,y],\qquad\quad\,\,\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{L}_{0},

\displaystyle B(f\otimes g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\otimes f+{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes[f,g],\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\forall f,g\in\mathcal{L}_{1},

and define
Y:(B\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes B)(B\otimes{\rm{Id}})\Rightarrow({\rm{Id}}\otimes B)(B\otimes{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\otimes B)
by

Y_{x\otimes y\otimes z}={\mathbf{1}}_{z\otimes y\otimes x+{\swabfamily e}\otimes[y,z]\otimes x+{\swabfamily e}\otimes y\otimes[x,z]+z\otimes{\swabfamily e}\otimes[x,y]}+{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes\mathcal{J}_{x\otimes y\otimes z},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathcal{L}_{0}.

Then
(B,Y)
is a solution of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation.

Proof.

Firstly, we show that
B
is a linear invertible functor.
For any
f\otimes g\in\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}
, since
the linear functor
[\cdot,\cdot]
preserves the source map
s
and the target map
t
, we have

\displaystyle\big(B\circ(s\otimes s)\big)(f\otimes g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(s(f)\otimes s(g))=s(g)\otimes s(f)+{\swabfamily e}\otimes[s(f),s(g)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(g)\otimes s(f)+s({\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e})\otimes s[f,g]=(s\otimes s)(g\otimes f+{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes[f,g])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((s\otimes s)\circ B\big)(f\otimes g),

\displaystyle\big(B\circ(t\otimes t)\big)(f\otimes g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(t(f)\otimes t(g))=t(g)\otimes t(f)+{\swabfamily e}\otimes[t(f),t(g)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t(g)\otimes t(f)+t({\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e})\otimes t[f,g]=(t\otimes t)(g\otimes f+{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes[f,g])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((t\otimes t)\circ B\big)(f\otimes g),

which implies that
B
preserves the source and target maps.
For any
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{L}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{0}
, since
the linear functor
[\cdot,\cdot]
preserves the identity map, we have

\displaystyle\big(B\circ({\mathbf{1}}\otimes{\mathbf{1}})\big)(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B({\mathbf{1}}_{x}\otimes{\mathbf{1}}_{y})={\mathbf{1}}_{y}\otimes{\mathbf{1}}_{x}+{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes[{\mathbf{1}}_{x},{\mathbf{1}}_{y}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{1}}_{y}\otimes{\mathbf{1}}_{x}+{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes{\mathbf{1}}_{[x,y]}=({\mathbf{1}}\otimes{\mathbf{1}})(y\otimes x+{\swabfamily e}\otimes[x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(({\mathbf{1}}\otimes{\mathbf{1}})\circ B\big)(x\otimes y),

which implies that
B
preserves the identity map.
Given
f\otimes g,f^{\prime}\otimes g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}
satisfying
(t\otimes t)(f\otimes g)=(s\otimes s)(f^{\prime}\otimes g^{\prime})
, since
the linear functor
[\cdot,\cdot]
preserves the composition, we have

\displaystyle B(f^{\prime}\otimes g^{\prime})\circ B(f\otimes g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g^{\prime}\otimes f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes[f^{\prime},g^{\prime}])\circ(g\otimes f+{\mathbf{1}}_{\swabfamily e}\otimes[f,g])

\displaystyle=

In

We understand

Since we have shown that central Leibniz algebras and linear racks serve as decategorifications of central Leibniz

Q1:

Whether any central Leibniz algebra give rise to a linear rack.

Q2:

Whether any central Leibniz 2-algebra give rise to a linear 2-rack. If there is certain connection, what is the relation between the corresponding solutions of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation constructed from previous sections.

In this section, we will show that if the underlying 2-vector space of a central Leibniz 2-algebra is splittable, then it gives rise to a linear 2-rack. Moreover, if the central Leibniz 2-algebra is splittable, then the corresponding solutions of the Zamolodchikov Tetrahedron equation constructed from the central Leibniz 2-algebra and the associated linear 2-rack are the same. Since any vector space is splittable, so we obtained that any central Leibniz algebra gives rise to a linear rack as a byproduct, and this result is an enhancement of the construction by Lebed given in

Kinetic random-field nonreciprocal Ising model
Arjun R and A. V. Anil Kumar

Abstract
We introduce and analyse the kinetic random-field nonreciprocal Ising model , which incorporates bimodal disorder along with pairwise nonreciprocal interactions between two different species. Using mean-field and effective-field theory, in combination with kinetic Monte Carlo simulations (3D Glauber dynamics), we identify a nonequilibrium tricritical (Bautin) point separating Hopf-type transitions (continuous) from saddle-node-of-limit-cycle (SNLC) transitions (discontinuous). For a weak random field which is less than a critical value, the onset of collective oscillations (the “swap” phase) occurs via a supercritical Hopf bifurcation, whereas for fields greater than the critical value, the transition is first-order (SNLC), exhibiting hysteresis and Binder-cumulant signatures. The finite-size scaling of the susceptibility is consistent with the distinct critical and discontinuous behaviour shown in the Hopf and SNLC regimes, respectively (effective exponents ≈ 1.96 \approx 1.96 in the Hopf regime and ≈ 3.0 \approx 3.0 in the SNLC regime). Additionally, in the first-order regime, the swap phase is sustained only above a threshold nonreciprocity, and this threshold increases monotonically with the disorder strength. We further identify a new droplet-induced swap phase in the larger field-strength region, which cycles eight different metastable states. A dynamical free-energy picture rationalises droplet nucleation as the mechanism for these cyclic jumps. Together, these results demonstrate how disorder and nonreciprocity combined generate rich nonequilibrium criticality, with implications for driven and active systems.

Recent studies have shown that nonreciprocal interactions can give rise to entirely new classes of collective behaviour—including time-dependent ordered phases and transitions governed by exceptional points—that lie beyond the scope of conventional free-energy minimisation

Several models have been proposed recently to explain the nature of novel features arising from nonreciprocal interactions. The nonreciprocal extension of the Cahn–Hilliard model provides a framework to understand phase separation and pattern formation in binary mixtures interacting nonreciprocally

Mean-field analysis of this nonreciprocal Ising model predicts not only the conventional disordered and statically ordered phases but also a time-dependent “swap” phase, characterised by out-of-phase oscillations between the two species. Numerical studies have revealed that fluctuations and spatial structure significantly enrich this behaviour: static order can be disrupted by rare droplet excitations, spiral defects can destabilise the swap phase in low dimensions, and in three dimensions, nonreciprocity may alter universality classes and stabilise robust spatiotemporal oscillations

Disorder coupled to the order parameter—as in the paradigmatic random-field Ising model (RFIM)—is known to strongly affect phase transitions. The seminal Imry–Ma argument showed that sufficiently strong random fields destabilise ordered states in low dimensions
Imry and Ma 1975

In this work, we introduce the random-field nonreciprocal Ising model, which combines bimodal random fields with antisymmetric pairwise couplings between two spin species. By employing mean-field theory (MFT), effective-field theory (EFT) that incorporates some of the spin–spin correlations, and three-dimensional kinetic Monte Carlo simulations with Glauber dynamics, we characterise the transitions among disordered, time-dependent swap, and droplet-induced swap phases. Our key findings are (i) we identify a nonequilibrium tricritical point (Bautin point) that separates continuous Hopf-type onsets of collective oscillations from discontinuous saddle-node-of-limit-cycle (SNLC) transitions; (ii) in the regime of discontinuous transitions, the threshold nonreciprocity strength needed to maintain the swap phase increases monotonically with the strength of the random field; and (iii) we discover a novel droplet-induced swap regime at high random field strengths and subcritical nonreciprocity, where the system dynamically cycles through eight distinct metastable states. These results are supported by finite-size scaling and Binder cumulant analysis.

To summarise, both MFT and EFT successfully capture the qualitative structure of the bifurcation diagram, including the transition from a Hopf bifurcation to an SNLC bifurcation, separated by a nonequilibrium tricritical (Bautin) point. However, these theoretical approaches differ quantitatively from the results obtained via three-dimensional kinetic Monte Carlo simulations. In particular, the presence of all the spin correlations and disorder under competing kinetics enhances the tendency toward discontinuous behaviour and shifts the tricritical point to higher values of
\tilde{h}
, beyond those predicted by MFT or EFT. Furthermore, EFT—by incorporating self-spin correlations—predicts a narrower first-order region than MFT. All three approaches—MFT, EFT, and MC simulations—agree on a key feature of the phase diagram: above the tricritical point, there exists a threshold value of nonreciprocity,
\tilde{K}_{c}
, below which the conventional swap phase disappears. In this regime (
\tilde{h}>\tilde{h}_{c}
and
\tilde{K}<\tilde{K}_{c}
), the system instead enters a droplet-induced swap phase, characterised by cyclic transitions among either eight or four metastable states. The cyclic switching between eight metastable states is a new phenomenon, which is not observed in the zero-field nonreciprocal Ising model
Avni et al. 2025b
. The droplet-induced swap behaviour can be interpreted in terms of the system’s dynamical free-energy landscape, where fluctuations enable transitions between local minima. The robustness of this regime is confirmed by simulations, which consistently show its emergence across a broad range of parameters.

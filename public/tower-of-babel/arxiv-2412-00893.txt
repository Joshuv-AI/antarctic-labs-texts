Abstract
. We express the Mahler measure of an exact polynomial in arbitrarily many variables in terms of Deligne-Beilinson cohomology. We then focus on the relationship between the Mahler measure of four-variable exact polynomials and the special value of the L L -function of K ​ 3 K3 surfaces at s = 4 s=4 . This result extends the three-variable case studied in [ Tri24a ] . Finally, we prove, under Beilinson’s conjecture, that the Mahler measure of the polynomial ( x + 1 ) ​ ( y + 1 ) ​ ( z + 1 ) + t (x+1)(y+1)(z+1)+t is expressed in terms of the Riemann zeta function and the L L -function of the modular form of weight 3 and level 7.

Introduction

The Mahler measure of polynomials was introduced by Mahler in 1962 to study transcendental number theory (see
[
Mah62
]
). The (logarithmic) Mahler measure of a polynomial
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
is defined by

(0.0.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(P)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\log|P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\ \dfrac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dfrac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}},

where
\mathbb{T}^{n}:|x_{1}|=\dots=|x_{n}|=1
is the
n
-dimensional torus.

In 1997, Deninger
[
Den97
]
linked the Mahler measure of a complex polynomial
P
under certain conditions to the Deligne-Beilinson cohomology of
V_{P}
, where
V_{P}
is the zero locus of
P
in
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
. Deninger defined a chain
\Gamma
attached to
P
and an
(n-1)
-differential form
\eta(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
which represents the regulator
\mathrm{reg}^{n,n}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\})
, here

\mathrm{reg}^{n,n}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}:H^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n},\mathbb{Q}(n))\to H^{n}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n},\mathbb{R}(n))

is the Beilinson regulator map from the motivic cohomology to the Deligne-Beilinson cohomology of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
, and
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
denotes the Milnor symbol in
H^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n},\mathbb{Q}(n))
. Deninger showed that if
\Gamma
is contained in the regular locus
V_{P}^{\mathrm{reg}}
of
V_{P}
, then

(0.0.2)

\mathrm{m}(P)=\mathrm{m}(\tilde{P})+\dfrac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(2\pi i)^{n-1}}\int_{\Gamma}\eta(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

where
\tilde{P}
is the leading coefficient of
P
seen as a polynomial in
x_{n}
.

Now we assume that
P
has rational coefficients. The variety
V_{P}
is therefore defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and we still denote it by
V_{P}
. Deninger found that if
\partial\Gamma=\varnothing
, the identity (
0.0.2
) together with Beilinson’s conjectures should imply that
\mathrm{m}(P)
is expressed in terms of the
L
-function of the pure motive
h^{n-1}(\overline{V_{P}})
, where
\overline{V_{P}}
is a smooth compactification of
V_{P}
. When
\partial\Gamma\neq\varnothing
, Maillot
[
Mai03
]
suggested looking at the variety
W_{P}:=V_{P}\cap V_{P^{*}}
, where
P^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=P(x_{1}^{-1},\dots,x_{n}^{-1})
. As
P
has rational coefficients,
W_{P}
is also defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and we call it the
Maillot variety
. The polynomial
P
is called
exact
if the regulator
\mathrm{reg}_{V_{P}^{\mathrm{reg}}}^{n,n}(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\})
is trivial, which is equivalent to saying that
\eta|_{V_{P}^{\mathrm{reg}}}
is an exact form. Now suppose that
P
is an exact polynomial. Then Stokes’ theorem gives

\mathrm{m}(P)=\mathrm{m}(\tilde{P})+\dfrac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(2\pi i)^{n-1}}\int_{\partial\Gamma}\omega.

Since
P
has rational coefficients,
\partial\Gamma
is contained in
W_{P}
. One then expects that
\mathrm{m}(P)
is related to the cohomology of
W_{P}
under some conditions. Continuing this direction, Lalín proved the following numerical identity of Boyd
[
Boy06
]
under Beilinson’s conjectures

\mathrm{m}((x+1)(y+1)+z)=a\cdot L^{\prime}(E_{15},-1)\quad(a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}),

where
E_{15}
is the elliptic curve of conductor 15. This identity was then completely proved by Brunault
[
Bru23
]
. Lalín’s result was generalized by the author in
[
Tri24a
]
. More precisely, we showed that the Mahler measure of three-variable exact polynomials can be related to the Beilinson regulator of certain motivic cohomology classes. Then under Beilinson’s conjecture for genus 1 curves, the Mahler measure can be expressed in terms of the special value of elliptic curve
L
-functions at
s=3
and a linear combination of Bloch-Wigner dilogarithmic values (see
[
Tri24a
, Theorem 0.2]
). In
[
Tri24a
]
, we applied this method and obtained many numerical identities of Boyd and Brunault (see
[
Boy06
]
,
[
Bru20
]
) under Beilinson’s conjectures, for example,

(0.0.3)

m(1+(x^{2}-x+1)y+(x^{2}+x+1)z)=a\cdot L^{\prime}(E_{45},-1)+b\cdot L^{\prime}(\chi_{-3},-1)\quad(a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times},b\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}),

\mathrm{m}(x^{2}+1+(x+1)^{2}y+(x^{2}-1)z)=c\cdot L^{\prime}(E_{48},-1)+L^{\prime}(\chi_{-4},-1)\quad(c\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}).

In this article, we express the Mahler measure of an exact polynomial in arbitrarily many variables in terms of the Deligne-Beilinson cohomology of an open subset of the Maillot variety (see Theorem
1.12
). More precisely, let
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be an exact polynomial. If
\partial\Gamma
does not cross the singularities of
W_{P}
, it then defines an element in the singular homology group
H_{n-2}(Y(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^{(-1)^{n-1}}
of a smooth open subvariety
Y
of
W_{P}
, and
+/-
respectively denotes the invariant/anti-invariant part under the action of the complex conjugation. We construct a
(n-2)
-differential form
\rho
which defines an element in Deligne-Beilinson cohomology
H_{\mathcal{D}}^{n-1}(Y_{\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{R}(n))
such that

(0.0.4)

\mathrm{m}(P)=\mathrm{m}(\tilde{P})+\dfrac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(2\pi i)^{n-1}}\left<[\partial\Gamma],[\rho]\right>_{Y},

where the pairing is given by

(0.0.5)

\displaystyle\left<\ ,\ \right>_{Y}:H_{n-2}(Y(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^{(-1)^{n-1}}\times H^{n-2}(Y(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R}(n-1))^{+}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}(n-1),\quad(\gamma,\omega)

\displaystyle\mapsto\int_{\gamma}\omega.

Let
X
be a smooth compactification of
W_{P}
and
F
be its function field. We construct an element in the cohomology group
H^{n-1}(\Gamma(F,n))
, where
\Gamma(F,n)
is Goncharov’s polylogarithmic complex (see Lemma
1.10
). In the case
n=4
, under an explicit condition, this element gives rise to an element in the cohomology group
H^{3}(\Gamma(X,4))
, where
\Gamma(X,4)
is Goncharov’s polylogarithmic complex of X (see Remark
2.1
). Under Goncharov’s conjecture

(0.0.6)

H^{3}(\Gamma(X,4))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\simeq}}H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4)),

it then gives us an element, denoted by
\Lambda
, in the motivic cohomology group
H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))
. Then the Mahler measure is related to the Beilinson regulator of
\Lambda
(see Theorem
2.3
)

(0.0.7)

\mathrm{m}(P)=\mathrm{m}(\tilde{P})-\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<[\partial\Gamma],\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\Lambda)\right>_{X},

where
\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}:H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))\to H^{3}_{\mathcal{D}}(X_{\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{R}(4))
is the Beilinson regulator map.

Recall that, in the case of three-variable polynomials, if the Maillot variety is a curve of genus one, the Mahler measure can be related to the special value at
s=3
of the
L
-function of the associated elliptic curve. Therefore, in the four-variable context, it is natural to consider cases in which the Maillot variety admits a projective regular model
X
that is a
K3
surface. In particular, if the projective regular model
X
is a singular
K3
surface with Picard rank 20 over
\mathbb{Q}
(i.e.,
NS(X_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})
is generated by 20 divisors defined over
\mathbb{Q}
), then under Beilinson’s conjecture for the transcendental part of motivic cohomology
2.14
, the Mahler measure is expressed in terms of the Riemann zeta function and the
L
-function of a weight 3 modular form (see Corollary
2.16
)

(0.0.8)

\mathrm{m}(P)=\mathrm{m}(\tilde{P})+a\cdot L^{\prime}(f,-1)+b\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Q}.

Applying this method to the four-variable exact polynomial
P=(x+1)(y+1)(z+1)+t
, we obtain the following result. This appears to be the first known identity relating the Mahler measure of a four-variable polynomial to the
L
-function of a modular form.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
P=(x+1)(y+1)(z+1)+t
. The minimal regular proper model of
W_{P}
is a singular
K3
surface with Picard rank 20 over
\mathbb{Q}
. Assuming Goncharov’s Conjecture
0.0.6
and Beilinson’s conjecture for the transcendental part of the motivic cohomology of singular
K3
surfaces, we have

\mathrm{m}(P)=a\cdot L^{\prime}(f_{7},-1)+b\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Q},

where
f_{7}
is the modular form of weight 3 and level 7.

The article is divided into three sections. In Section
1
, we prove equation (
0.0.4
), which expresses the Mahler measure of exact polynomials in terms of Deligne-Beilinson cohomology. In Section
2.1
, we consider the four-variable case, and prove equation (

. In Section

1.
Mahler measure and Deligne-Beilinson cohomology

In this section, we discuss the relationship between the Mahler measure of exact polynomials and Deligne-Beilinson cohomology. In fact, it generalizes the three-variable case studied in
[
Tri24a
, Lemma 4.8]
. We first recall briefly the definition of Deligne-Beilinson cohomology, motivic cohomology, and Goncharov’s polylogarithmic complexes. The main result of this section is the explicit construction of an element in the
(n-1)
-cohomology group of Goncharov’s weight-
n
complex, and the relation of its regulator with the Mahler measure (see Lemma
1.10
and Lemma
1.11
).

1.1.
Deligne-Beilinson cohomology

We recall the definition of real Deligne-Beilinson cohomology of Burgos
[
Bur97
]
. Let
X
be a smooth complex variety. Let
j\geq 1
be an integer. Let
(\bar{X},\iota)
be a good compactification of
X
, i.e.,
\iota:X\to\bar{X}
is an embedding where
\bar{X}
is a smooth proper variety and
D:=\bar{X}\setminus\iota(X)
is a normal crossing divisor on
\bar{X}
. For
\Lambda\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
, we denote by
E^{n}_{X,\Lambda}(\log D)
the space of
\Lambda
-valued smooth differential forms on
X
of degree
n
with logarithmic singularities along
D
, namely, differential forms generated by smooth forms on
\bar{X}
,
\log|z_{i}|,\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}
,
\frac{d\bar{z}_{i}}{\bar{z}_{i}}
, for
1\leq i\leq m
, where
z_{1}\dots z_{m}=0
is the local equation of
D
. When
\Lambda=\mathbb{C}
, we have the following decomposition

(1.1.1)

E^{n}_{X,\mathbb{C}}(\log D)=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}E^{p,q}_{X,\mathbb{C}}(\log D),

where
E^{p,q}
is the subspace of
(p,q)
-forms. Denote by
E^{\bullet}_{\log,\Lambda}(X)=\varinjlim_{(\bar{X},D)}E^{\bullet}_{X,\Lambda}(\log D)
. For any integers
j,n\geq 0
, Burgos defined the following complex

(1.1.2)

E_{j}(X)^{n}:=\begin{cases}(2\pi i)^{j-1}E^{n-1}_{\log,\mathbb{R}}(X)\cap\left(\bigoplus_{p+q=n-1;p,q<j}E^{p,q}_{\log,\mathbb{C}}(X)\right)&\text{if }n\leq 2j-1,\\
(2\pi i)^{j}E^{n}_{\log,\mathbb{R}}(X)\cap\left(\bigoplus_{p+q=n;p,q\geq j}E^{p,q}_{\log,\mathbb{C}}(X)\right)&\text{if }n\geq 2j,\end{cases}

with differentials

d^{n}\omega:=\begin{cases}-\text{pr}_{j}(d\omega)&\text{if }n<2j-1,\\
-2\partial\bar{\partial}\omega&\text{if }n=2j-1,\\
d\omega&\text{if }n\geq 2j,\end{cases}

where
\text{pr}_{j}
is the projection
\bigoplus_{p,q}\to\bigoplus_{p,q<j}
.
Burgos (
[
Bur97
, Corollary 2.7]
) then showed that

(1.1.3)

H^{n}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,\mathbb{R}(j))\simeq H^{n}(E_{j}(X)^{\bullet})\quad\text{for }j,n\geq 0.

If
j>\dim X\geq 1
or
j>n
, we have the following canonical isomorphism

(1.1.4)

H^{n}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,\mathbb{R}(j))\simeq H^{n-1}(X,\mathbb{R}(j-1)),

(see e.g.
[
Tri24
, Lemma 2.1.10]
). In particular, for
j>1
, we have
H^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R}(j))\simeq\mathbb{R}(j-1)
.

Let
X
be a regular algebraic variety over
\mathbb{R}
. The set of complex points
X(\mathbb{C})
is endowed with an action induced by the complex conjugation
\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C},z\mapsto\bar{z}
, denoted by
F_{\infty}
. It induces an involution, denoted by
F_{\mathrm{dR}}
, on differential forms of
X(\mathbb{C})
as below

(1.1.5)

F_{\text{dR}}(\omega):=F^{*}_{\infty}(\bar{\omega}).

The Deligne-Beilinson cohomology of
X
is defined by the invariant part of the Deligne-Beilinson cohomology of
X(\mathbb{C})
under the involution
F_{\mathrm{dR}}

(1.1.6)

H^{n}_{\mathcal{D}}(X/_{\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{R}(j)):=H^{n}_{\mathcal{D}}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R}(j))^{+}\quad\text{for }n,j\geq 0.

Example 1.1
.

Let
k
be a number field and
n>1
be an integer. Let
r_{1}
and
2r_{2}
be the numbers of real and complex embeddings from

to
\mathbb{C}
, respectively. As

, we have

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\operatorname{Spec}(k\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C})/\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}(n))

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}}H^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\operatorname{Spec}(k\otimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R}(n))^{+}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(\operatorname{Spec}(k\otimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R}(n-1))^{+}

\displaystyle=\oplus_{\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}}\begin{cases}H^{0}(\operatorname{Spec}(k\otimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R})^{-}&\text{ if }n\text{ even}\\
H^{0}(\operatorname{Spec}(k\otimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R})^{+}&\text{ if }n\text{ odd}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{r_{2}}&\text{ if }n\text{ even},\\
\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}&\text{ if }n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

1.2.
Motivic cohomology

In this section, let us recall briefly the definition of motivic cohomology. Let
k
be a field of characteristic 0. Recall that Grothendieck constructed the category
\operatorname{CHM}(k,\mathbb{Q})
of Chow motives with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficient and a contravariant functor

(1.2.1)

where

Voevodsky constructed the category

-coefficient, and a covariant functor

, which sends the Chow motive

of a smooth projective variety

(see
[
MVW06
, Lecture 20]
). The corresponding decomposition of

in

is given by

M(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k})=\mathbb{Q}(0)\oplus\mathbb{Q}(1)[2],

where
\mathbb{Q}(0):=M(\operatorname{Spec}k)
and
\mathbb{Q}(1)
is called the Tate motive. Denote by
\mathbb{Q}(j)=\mathbb{Q}(1)^{\otimes j}
.

. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
and

be the discriminant of

We then have the following conjecture which is similar to the case of smooth projective curves of genus 1 (see
[

, Conjecture 1.11]
).

Conjecture 2.14

(
Beilinson’s conjecture for the transcendental part of motivic cohomology
)
.

Let
X
be a singular
K3
surface with Picard rank
20
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let

(2.2.13)

\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<\gamma,\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\xi)\right>_{X,\operatorname{tr}}=a\cdot L^{\prime}(f,-1),\quad a\in\mathbb{Q},

where

.

The following proposition shows that Beilinson’s conjecture for the algebraic part of the motivic cohomology of singular
K3

Proposition 2.15
.

Let

be a generator, we have

\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<\gamma,\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\xi)\right>_{X,\operatorname{alg}}=a\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),\quad a\in\mathbb{Q}.

Proof.

We fix an orthogonal basis

\{[D_{j}],1\leq j\leq 20\}

of

, where

denotes the algebraic equivalence class corresponding to the divisor

defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then we have the following direct sum decomposition in the category
\operatorname{CHM}(\mathbb{Q})
of Chow motive over
\mathbb{Q}
with
\mathbb{Q}

))

(2.2.15)

h^{2}_{\operatorname{alg}}(X)\simeq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{20}h^{2}_{\operatorname{alg},j}(X),

where

, and

(see

(2.2.16)

Let

be any nontrivial element. Using the decomposition (

), we write

\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{20}\xi_{j},\quad\xi_{j}\in H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))_{\operatorname{alg},j}.

Let
\gamma

(2.2.22)

\left<\gamma,s_{j*}\circ\pi_{j}^{*}\circ\mathrm{reg}^{1,3}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha_{j})\right>_{X,\operatorname{alg}}=(2\pi i)\cdot\left<s_{j}^{*}(\gamma),\pi_{j}^{*}\circ\mathrm{reg}^{1,3}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha_{j})\right>_{D_{j}}=(2\pi i)\cdot\left<\pi_{j*}\circ s_{j}^{*}(\gamma),\mathrm{reg}^{1,3}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha_{j})\right>_{\mathrm{pt}},

), which states that image of the motivic cohomology

This implies that for any nontrivial element
\xi\in H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))_{\operatorname{alg}}
and
\gamma\in H_{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^{-}_{\operatorname{alg}}

(2.2.24)

\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<\gamma,\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\xi)\right>_{X,\operatorname{alg}}=a\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),\quad a\in\mathbb{Q}.

We arrive at the following direct corollary about the Mahler measure of a four-variable exact polynomial whose Maillot variety is a singular
K3
surface with Picard rank 20 over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
P(x,y,z,t)\in\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z,t]
be a nonzero polynomial satisfying all the conditions in Theorem
2.3
. Let
X
be a smooth compactification of the Maillot variety
W_{P}
. Assume that
X
is a singular
K3
surface with Picard rank 20 over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then under Conjecture
2.14
, we have

(2.2.25)

\mathrm{m}(P)=\mathrm{m}(\tilde{P})+a\cdot L^{\prime}(f,-1)+b\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Q},

where
f
is the newform defined in Theorem
2.13
.

Proof.

Recall that under the conditions
\Gamma\subset V_{P}^{\mathrm{reg}}
and
\partial\Gamma\subset Y(\mathbb{C})
,
\partial\Gamma
defines an element in
H_{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^{-}.
Using the following decomposition

H_{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^{-}=H_{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^{-}_{\operatorname{tr}}\oplus H_{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^{-}_{\operatorname{alg}},

we get
[\partial\Gamma]=[\partial\Gamma]_{\operatorname{tr}}+[\partial\Gamma]_{\operatorname{alg}},
where
[\partial\Gamma]_{\epsilon}\in H_{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^{-}_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon\in\{\operatorname{tr},\operatorname{alg}\}
.
Let
\Lambda
be the element defined in (
2.1.10
). Under the conditions in Theorem
2.3
,
\Lambda
defines an element in
H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))
and

\mathrm{m}(P)-\mathrm{m}(\tilde{P})=-\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<[\partial\Gamma],\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\Lambda)\right>_{X}.

Now using the decomposition in Proposition
2.5
,

H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))=H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))_{\operatorname{tr}}\oplus H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))_{\operatorname{alg}},

we decompose
\Lambda
into its transcendental and algebraic components

\Lambda=\Lambda_{\operatorname{tr}}+\Lambda_{\operatorname{alg}},

where
\Lambda_{\epsilon}\in H^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Q}(4))_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon\in\{\operatorname{tr},\operatorname{alg}\}
. Since the transcendental lattice is the orthogonal complement of the Néron-Severi lattice in the cohomology group, we have

\mathrm{m}(P)-\mathrm{m}(\tilde{P})=-\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<[\partial\Gamma]_{\operatorname{tr}},\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\Lambda_{\operatorname{tr}})\right>_{X,\operatorname{tr}}-\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<[\partial\Gamma]_{\operatorname{alg}},\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\Lambda_{\operatorname{alg}})\right>_{X,\operatorname{alg}}.

Under Conjecture
2.14
, we have

-\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<[\partial\Gamma]_{\operatorname{tr}},\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\Lambda_{\operatorname{tr}})\right>_{X,\operatorname{tr}}=a\cdot L^{\prime}(f,-1),\quad a\in\mathbb{Q},

where
f
is the new form defined in Theorem
2.13
.
And by Proposition
2.15
, one gets

-\dfrac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\left<[\partial\Gamma]_{\operatorname{alg}},\mathrm{reg}^{3,4}_{X}(\Lambda_{\operatorname{alg}})\right>_{X,\operatorname{alg}}=a\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),\quad a\in\mathbb{Q}.

Therefore, under Conjecture
2.14

\mathrm{m}(P)=m(\tilde{P})+a\cdot L^{\prime}(f,-1)+b\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Q}.

∎

3.
Application

The main aim of this section is to prove that (under Goncharov’s and Beilinson’s conjecture) the Mahler measure of the polynomial
P=(x+1)(y+1)(z+1)+t
is a rational linear combination of a special
L
-value of the unique CM new form of weight 3, level 7 and a Riemann zeta value

(3.0.1)

\mathrm{m}(P)=a\cdot L^{\prime}(f_{7},-1)+b\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Q}.

This identity is numerically conjectured by Brunault that
a=-6
and
b=-48/7
(see
[
Bru23
]
). Before going into the details of the proof, we present some preliminary results on elliptic modular surfaces, which will be used in Section
3.2
.

3.1.
Elliptic modular
K3
surfaces

In this section, we give a necessary and effective criterion for an elliptic modular surface to be an elliptic

K3
surface. It is by no means a new result, but since we have been unable to find a proof in the literature, we include one here for the reader’s convenience.

Let us recall the definition of elliptic surfaces. Let
C
be a complex smooth projective curve. An elliptic surface over
C
is a smooth projective surface
X
with a surjective morphism
\pi:X\to C
such that

•

For all
s\in C
,
\pi^{-1}(s)
does not contain any curve
E
satisfying that
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
E^{2}=-1
.

•

Let
\eta
be the generic point of
C
. The generic fiber
X_{\eta}:=\pi^{-1}(\eta)
is an elliptic curve over
K:=k(C)
.

•

There exists
s\in C
such that
\pi^{-1}(s)
is singular.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}(\tau)>0\}
be the upper half-plane. The usual action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{H}
is given by

\gamma\cdot\tau=\dfrac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\quad\text{where }\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

For an integer
N\geq 1
, one defines

\Gamma(N)=\left\{\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\gamma\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\mod N\right\},\quad\Gamma_{1}(N)=\left\{\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\gamma\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1&\ast\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\mod N\right\}.

A congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
containing
\Gamma(N)
for some integer
N\geq 1
. The modular curve associated to a congruence subgroup
\Gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is defined to be the Riemann surface
\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
C_{\Gamma}^{0}
. This Riemann surface can be compactified by adding the finite set of points
\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. One denotes by
C_{\Gamma}
this compactification and the finite set of points added is called the set of cusps of
C_{\Gamma}
. Recall that if
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}(N)
, then
C^{0}_{\Gamma}
and
C_{\Gamma}
are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
Shi94
, Section 6.7]
).

We set
\mathcal{E}:=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus(\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{C}),
where
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
acts on
\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{C}
as follows

(3.1.1)

(m,n)\cdot(\tau;z):=(\tau;z+m\tau+n),\quad\text{ for }m,n\in\mathbb{Z},\tau\in\mathcal{H},z\in\mathbb{C}.

The space
\mathcal{E}
is a complex analytic manifold of dimension 2 and we have a holomorphic projection

\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{H},\quad(\tau;z)\mapsto\tau,

such that for each
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
, the fiber
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus(\tau\times\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z})
. The action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{E}
is given by

\gamma\cdot(\tau;z)=\left(\dfrac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d};\dfrac{z}{c\tau+d}\right)\quad\text{ for }\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

This action is not free, so it is necessary to consider congruence subgroups
\Gamma
that act freely on
\mathcal{H}
. One then calls the quotient
\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{E}
together with the canonical projection
\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{E}\to\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{H}
the
universal elliptic curve associated to
\Gamma
. We denote it by
\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}
. Recall that universal elliptic curves are algebraic varieties.

Definition 3.1

(
The elliptic modular surfaces associated to
\Gamma
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acting freely on
\mathcal{H}
. One calls the unique elliptic surface
X_{\Gamma}\to C_{\Gamma}
associated to the universal elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma}\to C_{\Gamma}^{0}
the elliptic modular surface associated to
\Gamma
(see e.g.,
[
SS19
, Theorem 5.19]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:X_{\Gamma}\to C_{\Gamma}
be an elliptic modular surface. We have

(3.1.2)

H^{0}(C_{\Gamma},\Omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{1})\simeq\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma),\quad H^{0}(X_{\Gamma},\omega_{X_{\Gamma}})\simeq\mathcal{S}_{3}(\Gamma),

where
\mathcal{S}_{j}(\Gamma)
is the complex vector space of cusp forms of weight
j
and level
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Denote by
\omega_{X_{\Gamma}/C_{\Gamma}}
the relative dualizing sheaf of
f
. As
X_{\Gamma}
is a smooth projective surface and
f
is a projective flat morphism, we have

(3.1.3)

\omega_{X_{\Gamma}/C_{\Gamma}}=\omega_{X_{\Gamma}}\otimes f^{*}(\omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{-1}),

where
\omega_{X_{\Gamma}}
is the canonical sheaf of
X_{\Gamma}
and
\omega_{C_{\Gamma}}
is the canonical sheaf of
C_{\Gamma}
. Denote by
\omega=f_{*}(\omega_{X_{\Gamma}/C_{\Gamma}})
. From formula (
3.1.3
), we have

(3.1.4)

\omega_{X_{\Gamma}}=f^{*}(\omega\otimes\omega_{C_{\Gamma}})=f^{*}(\omega\otimes\Omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{1}).

We recall the following natural isomorphism, which is called the
Shimura isomorphism
(see
[
Shi59
]
)

(3.1.5)

H^{0}(C_{\Gamma},\omega^{j}\otimes\Omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{1})\simeq\mathcal{S}_{j+1}(\Gamma),

for
j\geq 1
. This implies that
H^{0}(C_{\Gamma},\Omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{1})\simeq\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma)
and

H^{0}(X_{\Gamma},\omega_{X_{\Gamma}})\simeq H^{0}(C_{\Gamma},\omega\otimes\Omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{1})\simeq\mathcal{S}_{3}(\Gamma).

∎

Definition 3.3

(
Elliptic
K3
surfaces
)
.

An elliptic
K3
surface is an elliptic surface
X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
where
X
is an algebraic
K3
surface.

The following result gives us conditions when an elliptic modular surface is an elliptic
K3
surface.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acting freely on the upper half-plane
\mathcal{H}
and let
f:X_{\Gamma}\to C_{\Gamma}
be the associated elliptic modular surface. Then
f
is an elliptic
K3
surface if and only if

\dim\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma)=0,\text{ and }\dim\mathcal{S}_{3}(\Gamma)=1,

where
\mathcal{S}_{j}(\Gamma)
is the complex vector space of cusp forms of weight
j
, level
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f:X_{\Gamma}\to C_{\Gamma}
is an elliptic
K3
surface. In particular, we have
C_{\Gamma}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then by formula (
3.1.2
), we have
\dim\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma)=\dim H^{0}(C_{\Gamma},\Omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{1})=0.
As
X_{\Gamma}
is an algebraic
K3
surface, by formula (
3.1.2
), we also have

\dim\mathcal{S}_{3}(\Gamma)=\dim H^{0}(X_{\Gamma},\omega_{X_{\Gamma}})=\dim H^{0}(X_{\Gamma},\Omega_{X_{\Gamma}}^{2})=1.

Now we prove the converse. Again by formula (
3.1.2
),
\dim\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma)=0
implies that

H^{0}(C_{\Gamma},\Omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{1})=0.
Therefore,
C_{\Gamma}
is a smooth projective curve of genus 0, hence
C_{\Gamma}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let

\omega=f_{*}(\omega_{X_{\Gamma}/C_{\Gamma}}),

where
\omega_{X_{\Gamma}/C_{\Gamma}}
is the relative dualizing sheaf of
f
. By definition of elliptic surfaces,
f
is not isotrivial, so we can write

\omega=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a)
for some suitable positive integer
a
. The positivity of
a
follows from the positivity of the direct image of the relative canonical sheaf.
From the formula (
3.1.3
), we then have

\omega_{X_{\Gamma}}=f^{*}(\omega\otimes\omega_{C_{\Gamma}})=f^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a)\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{1})=f^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a-2)).

By Shimura’s isomorphism (
3.1.5
), we have

1=\dim\mathcal{S}_{3}(\Gamma)=\dim H^{0}(C_{\Gamma},\omega\otimes\Omega_{C_{\Gamma}}^{1})=\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a-2)).

Therefore,
a=2
. Hence
\omega_{X_{\Gamma}}=f^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Gamma}}
. We consider Leray spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(C_{\Gamma},R^{q}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Gamma}})\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(X_{\Gamma},\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Gamma}}),

which degenerates at
E_{2}
-term (see e.g.,
[
PS08
, Section 1.3]
). Since
R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Gamma}}\simeq\omega^{\vee}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(-2)
, one obtains that

Exponential enhancement of sensitivity in Ramsey interferometry with optically thick ensemble of atoms Preprint: APS/123-QED
S.A. Moiseev 1, K.I. Gerasimov 1 , M.M. Minnegaliev 1 ,I.V. Brekotkin 1 ,E.S. Moiseev 2

Abstract
Ramsey interferometry is a cornerstone technique for precise measurement of time and frequency in modern clocks. The Ramsey experiments are typically done in optically dilute samples of atoms to improve homogeneity and avoid back-action of atoms on excitation pulses. In contrast to later belief, we predict and experimentally show that in optically thick samples with inhomogeneous broadening of resonant transition, the back-action can lead to the highly enhanced narrowing of Ramsey resonance. The linewidth narrowing and corresponding precision of the frequency measurement scale exponentially with an increase in optical depth of a sample and can reach the limits set by homogeneous broadening. We show that this effect is caused by a nonlinear interference of multiple echoes formed inside the atomic medium, which is experimentally confirmed with Er 3 + 167 {}^{167}\text{Er}^{3+} ions in Y 2 ​ SiO 5 \text{Y}_{2}\text{SiO}_{5} crystal. Our findings open new opportunities for nonlinear high-resolution spectroscopy of resonant media and sensitivity enhancement in a new generation of solid state clocks.

I
Introduction

Ramsey interferometry uses a sample of atoms with two specially selected levels: ground and excited
Ramsey 1949
. Atoms being prepared in their ground state are sequentially illuminated by two electromagnetic pulses separated by a time delay of
\tau
with a pulse area of
\pi/2
. The carrier frequency of the electromagnetic radiation is slightly detuned from the transition frequency between ground and excited levels. After the applications of the pulses, the probability of finding the atoms in the excited state exhibits oscillatory behavior with respect to the detuning known as Ramsey fringes. By measuring the population of the excited state, the transition frequency between ground and excited states is extracted.
The precision of estimating the transition frequency is inversely proportional to
\tau
that demands samples with long coherence time. For measuring time it also is beneficial to use samples with large transition frequency for better precision. Ideal clock candidates should have large transition frequency and long coherence time.

Ramsey technique was first successfully used to study magnetic magnetic transition of atoms and molecules
Ramsey 1957
;
Ramsey 1990
.
Then it was applied to optical transitions
Baklanov et al. 1976
;
Bergquist et al. 1977
;
Bordé et al. 1984
.
The progress in cooling and trapping of ions and neutral atoms opened an avenue for performing Ramsey interferometry on optical transition with a coherence times of more than tens of seconds
Schioppo et al. 2017
;
Bothwell et al. 2019
;
Ludlow et al. 2015
;
Kozlov et al. 2018
. The precision of recent clock experiments was high enough to observe a frequency gravitational shift on a millimeter scale
Bothwell et al. 2022
.
Moreover, atomic media with a long quantum coherence time are attracting more and more attention
in laser physics, quantum optics and quantum information science, among which inversion-free lasers
Werren et al. 2022
;
Richter et al. 2020
, electromagnetically induced transparency and slow-light control
Chu et al. 2025
;
Hain et al. 2025
, optical Dicke superradiance
Andreev et al. 1980
;
Kocharovsky et al. 2017
, optical solitons
McCall and Hahn 1967
;
Lamb 1980
;
Maimistov et al. 1990
and quantum memory
Lvovsky et al. 2009
;
Tittel et al. 2009
;
Heshami et al. 2016
;
Chanelière et al. 2018
;
Guo et al. 2023
;
Zhou et al. 2023
are worth noting.

Ramsey model is usually treated in no back-action limit, i.e. a sample is small enough to produce any actions on the exciting
\pi/2
pulses. In this regime, each atom can be considered as being driven individually, while the total amount of atoms increases a signal to noise ratio during population measurement. It was pointed out that transition from optically thin to optically dense resonant media promising appears to increase an amplitude of narrow resonances
Voloshin et al. 2019
;
Voloshin et al. 2022
.

Here, we present theoretical and experimental studies of the two-pulse Ramsey resonance implemented in an optically thick two-level system.
We show that when laser pulses enter front the medium, they create a series of photon echo signals, which then produce Ramsey resonance lines deep inside the medium. Counterintuitively, these lines have the same amplitude as those at the entrance, but their width exponentially decrease as the medium gets optically thicker.
We observed this effect with
{}^{167}\text{Er}^{3+}
ions in
\text{Y}_{2}\text{SiO}_{5}
crystal.
Finally, we discuss the limits of the observed narrowing mechanism and its implementation in perspective media.

II
Mechanism of Ramsey line narrowing

The conceptual scheme of the Ramsey interferometry in an optically thick medium is presented in Fig. 1. Two laser pulses with pulse areas close to
\pi/2
, separated by a time interval
\tau
are launched into a resonant two-level atomic medium along the same direction on one side of the sample.
The resonant atomic transition is inhomogeneously broadened, described by the form factor
G(\Delta/\Delta_{inh})
with a spectral width
\Delta_{inh}
significantly exceeding the homogeneous broadening
\gamma=2/T_{2}
, where
T_{2}
is the atomic phase relaxation time, which is significantly longer than the duration of laser pulses
\delta t_{1,2}
and time delay
\tau
.
Interaction of laser pulses with a two-level atomic medium is described by a semi-classical system of Maxwell-Bloch (also called Maxwell-Bloch and Areccri-Bonifacio) equations
Arecchi and Bonifacio 1965
;
McCall and Hahn 1969
;
Allen and Eberly 1975
(see Supplement, section A).

It is convenient to use the pulse area theorem for building intuition on the nonlinear interaction of pulsed light and resonant atoms
McCall and Hahn 1969
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Theta(z)=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\sin\Theta(z),

(1)

where
\Theta(z)=\frac{d}{\hbar}\int\mathcal{E}(t,z)dt
is a pulse area of light pulse in the point z of the medium,
d
is a dipole moment of atomic transition,
\mathcal{E}(t,z)
is an electric field envelope,
\alpha
is a resonant absorption coefficient
Allen and Eberly 1975
.

The area theorem states that if the pulse area of individual light pulse is larger than
\pi
, then the pulse propagates in a form of soliton inside a medium without distortion in the self-induced transparency (SIT) regime
McCall and Hahn 1969
;
Allen and Eberly 1975
.
Although, if pulse area of a single pulse is smaller than
\pi
, then pulse is absorbed by the medium.
The bifurcation occurs if the pulse area is exactly
\pi
.
In this case, the pulse may propagate for a long distance inside the medium while changing its waveform but preserving the pulse area until non-Hermitian perturbations would change the pulse area and bring the pulse into a stable regime.

In Ramsey interferometry, each individual pulse has a pulse area of
\sim\pi/2
, but the total pulse area of all two pulses is close to
\pi
.
Each pulse is quickly absorbed by the medium in accordance with Eq.(
1
) (see Fig.
2
and supplementary material, section A).
Despite this, the total pulse area, when integrated over time, changes very slowly within the medium.
This is due to the generation of a cascade of echo signals deep in the medium;
two-pulse echo excites the atoms and induces next echo that subsequently produces another echo pulse deeper in the medium, and so on.
Generation of a large number of echo signals has been observed in spin systems with microwave transition frequencies
Weichselbaumer et al. 2020
;
Debnath et al. 2020
.

After all echo pulses being generated, the probability of finding atoms in the excited level along the direction of the pulses propagation
z

Ablowitz et al. 1974

\displaystyle P(\Delta,z,t)\cong

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-t/T_{1}}P(\Delta,0,t_{lim})e^{-\alpha z}}{1-P(\Delta,0,t_{lim})\big[1-e^{-\alpha z}\big]},

(2)

where
T_{1}

\displaystyle P(\Delta,0,t_{lim})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\Theta_{0}\big\{1+\cos(\Delta\tau+\varphi_{2,1})\big\}.

(3)

where
t_{lim}
corresponds to the end of the interaction of atoms with the second exciting laser pulse.

\varphi_{2,1}=\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}
(
\varphi_{2}
and
\varphi_{1}
are the phases of the exciting laser pulses).
We assume that both control light pulses have the same phase and pulse area
\Theta_{0}
and the beams are homogeneous along their profile.
We note that if
\Delta=0
and
\varphi_{2,1}=0

1

The goal of Ramsey interferometry is to determine detuning
\Delta
by measuring population
P(\Delta,z,t)
.
The fundamental sensitivity of the Ramsey experiment on
\Delta
can be evaluated by estimating Fisher information and the corresponding Cramer-Rao bound
Frieden 1998
.
Fisher information for estimating
\Delta
from population measurement at distance
L
from the beginning of the sample with a subsequent extraction using a maximum likelihood technique is calculated as

\displaystyle I(\alpha L,\Delta)=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\Delta^{2}}\log P(\alpha L,\Delta,t_{lim}).

(4)

Cramer-Rao bound, being an inverse of the Fisher information, defines an uncertainty (variance) of the parameter estimation around given value.
For an ideal
\pi/2
excitations pulses (
\Theta=\pi/2
) and slow decoherence
T_{1},T_{2}\gg\tau

\Delta=0

\displaystyle\delta\Delta^{2}=1/I(\alpha L,\Delta)=4e^{-\alpha L}/\tau^{2},

(5)

The multiplier
(2/\tau)^{2}
in Eq.(
5

e^{-\alpha L}
describes its exponential narrowing of the resonance of Lorentzian shape with increasing optical depth of the medium (see Supplement, section C).

Atoms with detunings

with
n=\pm 1,\pm 2,...

IV
Discussion and conclusion

The demonstrated mechanism of narrowing of Ramsey resonance lines uses nonlinear coherent interaction of multi pulse radiation with a inhomogeneously broadened resonant medium.
Strong line narrowing occurs as a result of spectral manifestation of the SIT effect in the generation of multiple photon echo signals and their impact on atoms in the optically depth medium.
The most surprising thing is not only the appearance of resonant Ramsey lines at a large optical depth of the medium, but a strong decrease in the spectral width of these lines.
It appears that in the depth of the medium, photon echo signals form a light field with extremely narrow spectral components.
Exponentially powerful mechanism of the line narrowing opens a way for extracting and detecting ultra-narrow lines within inhomogeneous broadening of the resonance transition.

The proposed approach seems especially promising for enhancing sensitivity in solid-state clocks based on nuclear transitions.
Recent studies on the spectroscopy of the nuclear transition in
229m
Th thorium isomer doped into a
\text{Ca}\text{F}_{2}
crystal found its resonant transitions around 148 nm, with a lifetime of about 640 seconds and a broadening of 4 kHz
Zhang et al. 2024
. The results are in qualitative agreement with theoretical calculations predicting a radiation-limited homogeneous linewidth of transition on the order of
10^{-3}
Hz, while the inhomogeneous broadening due to magnetic interaction with nearby nuclear spins is expected on the order of
100
Hz
Kazakov et al. 2012
.
The ratio of inhomogeneous to homogeneous broadening of
\sim 10^{5}
replicates settings of our experiments.
A high enough optical depth promises achieving a precision in Ramsey interferometry that is limited only by the homogeneous linewidth, potentially leading to a record clock precision.
For example, the linewidth reduction will be 148 times according to Eq. (
5
) when using samples with an optical depth of
10
.

Creation of a sample with high optical depth could be a challenging task on it own. It’s known, that placing a linear medium inside a resonator could increase its effective optical depth due to light passing multiple times through the medium. Generation of cascaded echo signals by placing the atomic medium in a high-Q resonator were recently demonstrated
Weichselbaumer et al. 2020
;
Debnath et al. 2020
;
Moiseev and Urmancheev 2022
;
Moiseev and Urmancheev 2025
. Investigation of narrow Ramsey resonances under these conditions could potentially open a way for increasing precision but would require a significant development of theory and experiments.

At high enough optical depth, e.g. in rare-earth ions doped stoichiometric crystals
Ahlefeldt et al. 2016
, the narrowing mechanism predicts Ramsey line containing less than one atom.
In this case, the continuum macroscopic model of the Maxwell-Bloch equations may turn out to be invalid and a significant generalization of the solution (
3
) obtained by the inverse scattering method may be required.
It may be possible that additional relatively weak interactions may limit the predicted narrowing of Ramsey resonance.
For example, it is of interest to consider the effect of the nonlinear frequency shift of the atomic transition caused by laser fields.

Extra modification can be considered to tackle limitation due to the field induced shifts. For example, our model can be extended for Hyper-Ramsey schemes that are promising for suppressing field induced frequency shifts
Yudin et al. 2010
;
Huntemann et al. 2012
;
Zanon-Willette et al. 2018
. The Hyper-Ramsey scheme uses multiple interrogation pulses with individual pulse being tailored with frequency, duration and phase.
The extension of our method is straightforward, the only condition is to keep the total pulse area of the pulses at the entrance to the medium close to
\pi
. Achieving the highest line narrowing is possible when generating a large number of multiple echo signals, which would require taking into account the effect of atomic phase relaxation in the interval between exposure to light pulses.

Thus, our results demonstrate how high optical depth can lead to the emergence of exponential enhancements in frequency sensitivity in Ramsey interferometry.
The work inspires consideration of new regimes of light-matter interaction for metrology and sensing.
Media with high optical depth opens up new opportunities for the creation of ultra-precise optical clocks with applications ranging from fundamental physics to high resolution spectroscopy
Peik et al. 2021
.

Effects of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists on patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis: protocol for a systematic review and sequential meta-analysis
Authors: Mei-jun Wang, Yu-nuo Jiang, Pei-pei Li, Yuan-jie Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMetabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), a progressive subtype of steatotic liver disease, imposes a substantial global health burden due to its association with obesity and metabolic syndrome. Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) demonstrate therapeutic potential through pleiotropic mechanisms, including appetite regulation, metabolic enhancement, and anti-inflammatory activity. However, clinical evidence remains limited by methodological heterogeneity and insufficient long-term efficacy data, necessitating a rigorous systematic evaluation.MethodsThis protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis will include randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating GLP-1 RAs versus placebo in adults with biopsy-confirmed MASH. Comprehensive searches of PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library will be conducted using predefined strategies. Two independent reviewers will perform study screening, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment with the Cochrane ROB 2 tool. Primary outcomes are histological: (1) MASH resolution without fibrosis worsening, and (2) fibrosis improvement without MASH deterioration. Secondary outcomes encompass physiological and biochemical parameters. Data synthesis will utilize random-effects meta-analysis, complemented by meta-regression to explore heterogeneity sources. Trial sequential analysis (TSA) will be used to control random errors (α = 0.05, power = 80%) and validate evidence sufficiency, strengthening result reliability. Sensitivity analyses will assess robustness. Statistical analyses will employ RevMan 5.4 and R 4.3.1. Evidence certainty will be evaluated using the GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) framework.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required as this study synthesizes published data. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication and conference presentations.Systemic review registrationPROSPERO CRD420251090801.

Strengths and limitations of this study

• This systematic review and meta-analysis employs an advanced statistical methodology including meta-regression.

• Dual independent screening ensures selection reliability.

• The certainty of evidence may be limited by heterogeneous outcome measures.

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), the progressive phenotype of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), is histologically characterized by hepatic steatosis, lobular inflammation, and hepatocyte ballooning. This condition frequently progresses to advanced fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma, representing a growing cause of liver-related morbidity globally [1–5]. Current epidemiological studies estimate that approximately 25% of adults worldwide have MASLD, with 20–30% developing MASH; among these individuals, 40% progress to significant fibrosis within a decade [6–8]. The escalating healthcare burden is underscored by MASH becoming the fastest-growing indication for liver transplantation in Western nations.

The pathogenesis of MASH involves a complex interplay of metabolic dysregulation, chronic inflammation, and fibrogenic activation. Insulin resistance drives hepatic lipid accumulation, oxidative stress, and pro-inflammatory cytokine release (e.g., TNF-α, IL-6), subsequently activating hepatic stellate cells and promoting collagen deposition [9–11]. Conventional therapeutic strategies focusing on lifestyle interventions (dietary control and exercise) can improve metabolic parameters in some patients, but have limited effects on long-term histological reversal. Safe and effective pharmacological treatment options are urgently needed.

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs), a newer class of glucose-lowering agents, target core pathophysiological mechanisms of MASH through multimodal actions [12–14]. First, they activate hypothalamic satiety centers to reduce energy intake and promote significant weight loss. Second, they enhance glucose-dependent insulin secretion while suppressing glucagon release. Third, preclinical studies confirm their direct inhibition of key hepatic de novo lipogenesis enzymes, reducing intrahepatic lipid accumulation. Fourth, they attenuate hepatic inflammation [15, 16]. These mechanisms collectively provide a robust rationale for GLP-1 RAs in MASH therapy.

Despite this therapeutic promise, current clinical evidence exhibits limitations: most trials are single-center randomized controlled trials (RCTs) with small sample sizes, limiting statistical power; histological endpoint criteria lack standardization (e.g., inconsistent inclusion of fibrosis stabilization); follow-up durations are relatively short, with scarce long-term efficacy data; and subgroup analyses (e.g., diabetic vs. nondiabetic patients) remain insufficient [17–20]. Additionally, while emerging evidence suggests GLP-1 RAs may modulate hepatic inflammation via gut-liver axis regulation and intestinal microbiota modification, such mechanisms lack systematic translation into clinical RCTs [21]. This protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis will consolidate clinical evidence to guide researchers and practitioners.

Several recent systematic reviews have evaluated the efficacy of GLP-1 RAs in NAFLD/NASH, but they leave critical knowledge gaps unaddressed [22–25]. First, these reviews do not include data on newer dual or triple incretin agonists (e.g., tirzepatide, survodutide, retatrutide), which are increasingly relevant to MASH management. Second, few prior meta-analyses have applied trial sequential analysis (TSA) to assess the sufficiency of accumulated evidence, increasing the risk of spurious conclusions. Third, histological endpoints in earlier reviews lack standardization, whereas our protocol adopts consistent definitions. This work thus advances existing literature by addressing these gaps rather than replicating prior efforts.

Methods

Protocol and registration

This systematic review and meta-analysis adheres to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines. The study protocol was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO database to ensure methodological transparency and rigor [26]. (Registration ID: CRD420251090801).

Inclusion criteria

Participants

Adult (≥ 18 years) patients diagnosed with biopsy-confirmed MASH. Patients with MASH and comorbid metabolic conditions (e.g., MASLD with type 2 diabetes) will be included and analyzed via subgroup analysis stratified by diabetes status. The lower age limit (≥ 18 years) follows the standard clinical definition of "adults," and no upper limit is set as there is no evidence to exclude older adults with MASH.

Intervention

Patients in the experimental group received glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs).

Comparison

Patients in the control group received placebo only.

Primary outcome

Primary outcomes are histological (based on NAS scoring system and phase 3 MASH trial standards): (1) MASH resolution (NAS score = 0–2) without fibrosis worsening; (2) fibrosis improvement (≥ 1 stage reduction, e.g., from F2 to F1) without MASH deterioration (NAS score increase ≥ 2).

Secondary outcomes

Changes in physiologic parameters (body weight [kg], body mass index [kg/m2], waist circumference [cm]) and biochemical parameters (alanine aminotransferase [U/L], aspartate aminotransferase [U/L], triglycerides [mmol/L], total cholesterol [mmol/L], low-density lipoprotein cholesterol [mmol/L], high-density lipoprotein cholesterol [mmol/L]).

Study design

Randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

Exclusion criteria

Non-human studies (e.g., animal experiments); crossover designs; non-randomized studies; conference abstracts without peer-reviewed full-text publications; studies published in non-English languages.

Literature sources and retrieval strategy

Two independent reviewers (Mei-jun Wang and Yu-nuo Jiang) will conduct a systematic and comprehensive literature search across four major electronic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), covering the period from database inception to the present. The search strategy will be meticulously designed to integrate both controlled vocabulary (e.g., Medical Subject Headings [MeSH] in PubMed, Emtree in Embase) and relevant free-text terms. This strategy will simultaneously target three critical conceptual domains: the target population ("Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis" OR "MASH" OR "NASH"), the specific intervention ("GLP-1 Receptor Agonists" OR "semaglutide" OR "liraglutide" OR "tirzepatide"), and the required study design ("randomized controlled trial" OR "RCT").

To ensure methodological rigor and optimize search sensitivity and specificity, the proposed search syntax for each database will undergo formal peer review utilizing the Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies (PRESS) checklist. Recognizing the potential for database searches to miss eligible evidence, supplementary search methods will be rigorously employed. These will encompass manual scrutiny of the reference lists of all included studies and pertinent systematic reviews, systematic examination of key conference proceedings (e.g., major meetings of hepatology and gastroenterology associations), and searches of prominent clinical trial registries (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP) to identify ongoing or unpublished trials. All records retrieved through these combined methods will be collated using reference management software (e.g., EndNote), and duplicate citations will be systematically eliminated prior to initiating the screening process.

Literature screening and data extraction

Two independent reviewers (Mei-jun Wang and Yu-nuo Jiang) will execute the literature screening and data extraction processes in strict adherence to PRISMA guidelines, ensuring methodological rigor throughout. The screening phase involves sequential evaluation: initially assessing titles and abstracts against predetermined eligibility criteria, followed by a comprehensive full-text review of potentially eligible studies. Any discordance between reviewers will be resolved through iterative discussion or, when consensus remains elusive, definitive adjudication by a designated third senior reviewer (Pei-pei Li). If studies report incomplete data (e.g., 5 participants unaccounted for in a trial of 5000), we will first contact the corresponding authors via email to request missing information. If no response is received, we will analyze the available data using the intention-to-treat principle and assess the impact of missing data via sensitivity analysis. Data extraction will be performed using a standardized, pre-piloted form. Both reviewers will independently extract essential information from each included study. This encompasses core study design characteristics such as trial methodology, duration, setting, sample size, randomization procedures, allocation concealment, blinding status, and funding sources. Participant baseline data, including demographic profiles, specific diagnostic criteria for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), baseline disease severity indicators (notably fibrosis stage), and relevant comorbidities, will also be recorded. Details of the interventions and comparators, covering the specific glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) administered, dosage regimens, route of administration, treatment duration, and placebo composition, will be systematically captured.

Outcome data extraction will focus on the predefined primary and secondary endpoints. For the primary outcomes, this includes measures related to resolution of MASH without concurrent fibrosis worsening and improvement in fibrosis without concurrent steatohepatitis worsening, extracting relevant statistical parameters. Secondary outcomes encompass documented changes in anthropometric measurements—specifically body weight, body mass index (BMI), and waist circumference—alongside biochemical parameters including alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), triglycerides, total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C). Information regarding outcome assessment timing, analytical approach (e.g., intention-to-treat), methods for handling missing data, and reported subgroup analyses will be collected.

Following independent extraction, the datasets will undergo cross-verification between the two reviewers to ensure accuracy before proceeding to meta-analysis.

Assessment of risk of bias

Methodological rigor in bias assessment will be ensured through independent evaluation by two reviewers (Mei-jun Wang and Yu-nuo Jiang). For randomized controlled trials (RCTs), the Revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (ROB 2) will be systematically applied. This tool comprehensively assesses potential bias across five critical domains: bias arising during the randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, outcome measurement methodologies, and selective result reporting. Should non-randomized studies of interventions (NRSIs) be included, the Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions (ROBINS-I) tool will be employed. This instrument evaluates seven key domains of potential bias: confounding factors, participant selection procedures, intervention classification accuracy, deviations from intended protocols, missing data management, outcome measurement approaches, and selective reporting of findings. All assessments will be conducted independently by both reviewers. Discrepancies in risk of bias judgments will be resolved through iterative discussion; unresolved disagreements will be referred to a third senior reviewer (Pei-pei Li) for definitive resolution.

Statistical analysis

All quantitative syntheses will be conducted using Review Manager software (RevMan, version 5.4) for data pooling and graphical presentation via forest plots. Dichotomous outcome measures will be analyzed by calculating pooled risk ratios (RR) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs), employing the Mantel–Haenszel statistical method. Continuous outcomes will be expressed as mean differences (MD) with 95% CIs, synthesized using the inverse variance weighting approach. Statistical significance will be defined at a two-tailed alpha level of 0.05, with p-values below this threshold considered significant. Model selection for meta-analysis will be guided by statistical heterogeneity assessments. Heterogeneity will be calculated automatically by RevMan 5.4. The random-effects model (DerSimonian and Laird method) will be applied if substantial heterogeneity is detected (I2 > 0%), and the fixed-effects model will be used if heterogeneity is negligible (I2 = 0%); results will be cross-validated with RevMan output. The degree of heterogeneity will be quantified using the I2 statistic, where values exceeding 50% indicate substantial heterogeneity. Exploration of heterogeneity sources will be performed through meta-regression analysis using the metafor package in R software (version 4.3.1). This analysis will evaluate potential effect modifiers including baseline age, gender distribution, body mass index (BMI), diabetes status, and duration of GLP-1 RA therapy. Assessment of publication bias will follow methodological best practices. Visual inspection through funnel plot asymmetry will be conducted only when ten or more studies are available for an outcome, as fewer studies compromise the validity of such assessments. Quantitative evaluation of small-study effects will be performed using Egger’s linear regression test via the metabias function in R’s meta package (version 4.3.1), again restricted to analyses involving at least ten studies.

Subgroup analysis and sensitivity analysis

To explore potential effect modifiers and assess result robustness, prespecified subgroup analyses will be conducted. Primary stratification will examine differential treatment effects across predefined age categories. Concurrently, sensitivity analyses will evaluate the stability of primary outcome findings by systematically excluding studies with high risk of bias or methodological limitations.

Trial sequential analysis

Random errors in meta-analyses involving limited trials and sample sizes may yield spurious conclusions. To mitigate these risks and determine the required information size (RIS) for definitive conclusions, Trial Sequential Analysis (TSA) will be implemented using TSA software (v0.9.5.10 beta). This methodology controls for type I error inflation from repeated testing while maintaining statistical power at 80% under a two-sided alpha threshold of 0.05.

Certainty of evidence assessment

The certainty of evidence for all outcomes will be systematically evaluated using the GRADE framework through the GRADEpro GDT online tool (McMaster University) [27]. This assessment will critically appraise five core domains: risk of bias across included studies, inconsistency of results, indirectness of evidence, imprecision of effect estimates, and publication bias. Based on this structured evaluation, the certainty rating for each outcome will be categorized into one of four levels—high, moderate, low, or very low—with corresponding justification documented in evidence profile tables.

Patient and public involvement

This systematic review did not involve patients or the public in its design, conduct, or reporting, as it synthesizes existing published data. All analyses utilize secondary data from previously completed clinical trials where patient consent had been obtained during original study execution.

Ethics and dissemination

As this study constitutes secondary analysis of published data, formal ethical approval is not required. The complete findings will undergo peer review publication in international academic journals. Additionally, results will be presented at relevant scientific conferences and deposited in preprint repositories (e.g., medRxiv) to ensure timely knowledge dissemination. All extracted data, analytical code, and data extraction forms will be deposited in the Open Science Framework (OSF; https://osf.io/), a public repository, to ensure open access and reproducibility. Supplementary materials will also link to the OSF repository for easy retrieval.

Data management and reproducibility statement

All study materials—including raw extracted data (de-identified), analytical code (for RevMan 5.4 and R 4.3.1), and data extraction templates—will be deposited in the Open Science Framework (OSF; https://osf.io/) upon publication. Supplementary materials in the final manuscript will include a direct link to the OSF repository, ensuring free public access. All code will be commented to facilitate reproducibility, and data will be formatted to align with PRISMA-P reporting standards.

Discussion

The global obesity pandemic and its metabolic sequelae represent a critical public health burden. Contemporary epidemiological evidence indicates approximately 2.5 billion adults worldwide are overweight, with over 890 million meeting criteria for obesity [28–32]. This trend disproportionately affects low- and middle-income countries, driving an escalating incidence of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH). This progressive liver disease not only elevates risks of hepatic fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma but also exacerbates systemic metabolic dysregulation, including cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus [1–5]. Current therapeutic paradigms face dual limitations. Lifestyle interventions, while foundational, demonstrate restricted efficacy in achieving sustained histological improvement. Concurrently, no pharmacotherapies have received global regulatory approval specifically for MASH. Prior evidence on GLP-1 RAs for MASH remains inconclusive due to three critical limitations: most trials have small sample sizes, leading to insufficient statistical power; follow-up durations are typically < 72 weeks, precluding assessment of long-term efficacy; and histological endpoints are unstandardized (e.g., variable definitions of "fibrosis improvement"), making cross-trial comparisons difficult. This unmet clinical need underscores the imperative for high-quality evidence regarding novel agents like glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs), whose multimodal metabolic actions position them as potential disease-modifying therapies.

The mechanistic rationale for GLP-1 RAs in MASH involves three synergistic pathways: Appetite modulation via central nervous system effects reduces body weight and visceral adiposity, thereby decreasing hepatic lipid accumulation; enhanced insulin sensitivity improves glucose homeostasis and attenuates lipotoxicity; direct anti-inflammatory properties may suppress hepatic necroinflammation and fibrogenesis [33–36]. Notably, GLP-1 RAs exhibit substantial mechanistic diversity: liraglutide is a short-acting single GLP-1 receptor agonist, semaglutide is a long-acting single agonist, and tirzepatide acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. This diversity may translate to differential efficacy for MASH, justifying our planned subgroup analysis by specific agent type.

This study design aims to generate a protocol to systematically address four core controversies in the current evidence base. First, it will establish a unified efficacy evaluation framework through rigorous operationalization of dual histological endpoints: resolution of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) without concomitant fibrosis worsening and improvement in fibrosis without concurrent MASH worsening. Second, the design will integrate multifaceted metabolic indicators—including anthropometric measures (body weight, waist circumference) and comprehensive lipid profiles—to investigate potential correlations between systemic metabolic improvements and hepatic benefits. Third, it will prespecify subgroup analyses stratified by diabetes status, specific GLP-1 receptor agonist agents, and baseline fibrosis severity to identify critical treatment effect modifiers. Fourth, advanced statistical methodologies will be incorporated to account for potential sources of heterogeneity. This comprehensive analytical approach seeks to clarify three pivotal clinical questions: whether GLP-1 receptor agonists confer clinical benefits beyond conventional therapies in MASH patients; whether their hepatoprotective effects exhibit dose–response relationships with specific metabolic marker improvements; and which baseline population characteristics may predict optimal therapeutic response.

Strengths of the study

This study employs a robust methodological framework designed to maximize the validity and reliability of its conclusions, characterized by three fundamental strengths. First, a rigorously structured literature identification process will be implemented. The comprehensive search strategy, prospectively registered and strictly adherent to PRISMA-P guidelines, will be executed across four major biomedical databases—PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. This systematic approach aims to minimize selection bias risk through exhaustive evidence retrieval. Second, standardized and independent data handling procedures will be enforced. All literature screening and data extraction operations will follow a dual-independent reviewer protocol conducted by Mei-jun Wang and Yu-nuo Jiang. Discrepancies in judgment will be resolved through iterative consensus discussions or, when necessary, adjudication by a senior investigator. Predefined extraction templates will ensure methodological consistency in capturing critical data fields across studies. Third, stratified risk assessment with transparent reporting will be conducted. Risk of bias evaluation will be methodologically segregated by study design. Randomized controlled trials will undergo assessment using the Cochrane ROB 2 tool, which systematically examines five core domains of potential bias: randomization integrity, adherence to intended interventions, completeness of outcome data, outcome measurement validity, and selective reporting. Non-randomized studies will be evaluated with the ROBINS-I instrument, covering seven critical dimensions including confounding control, participant selection validity, intervention classification accuracy, protocol deviations, missing data management, outcome measurement reliability, and reporting selectivity. All bias assessments will undergo visual mapping to facilitate transparent interpretation of methodological limitations.

A sequential analytical approach will be implemented to ensure methodological rigor. The primary analysis will utilize a random-effects model to synthesize effect estimates, with risk ratios (RR) calculated for dichotomous endpoints and mean differences (MD) derived for continuous variables. Should substantial heterogeneity be detected (defined as I2 > 50%), meta-regression analysis will be employed to investigate potential moderating effects of prespecified covariates: age, gender, and baseline body mass index (BMI). To control for type I error inflation, trial sequential analysis (TSA) will be performed to determine whether cumulative evidence reaches the required information size. If TSA confirms sufficient evidence for GLP-1 RA efficacy, our results could inform updates to MASH treatment guidelines (e.g., prioritizing specific agonists for patients with advanced fibrosis). If evidence remains insufficient, TSA will calculate the required information size (RIS) to guide the design of future phase 4 trials, ensuring they are adequately powered to answer unresolved questions. Publication bias assessment will be conducted only when ≥ 10 studies are available for an outcome, utilizing funnel plot visualization complemented by Egger’s regression test for quantitative evaluation of small-study effects. This multifaceted analytical strategy is designed to both quantify the pooled treatment effect and identify key covariates influencing therapeutic efficacy.

Limitations and challenges

Despite its methodological strengths, this study acknowledges several inherent limitations. Variations in histological assessment systems and methodological differences in metabolic parameter measurements—such as waist circumference measurement protocols—may complicate data synthesis. To address this challenge, predefined data conversion rules will be established, and studies employing non-standardized assessment methodologies will be excluded from sensitivity analyses. A second limitation concerns the limited data availability for non-obese MASH populations, potentially constraining the generalizability of findings. The applicability scope of evidence will be explicitly delineated through subgroup analyses, with discussion sections highlighting critical research gaps in these subpopulations. The absence of long-term efficacy data represents a third significant constraint, potentially affecting clinical translation. This limitation will be explicitly addressed when interpreting the therapeutic implications of our findings. Additional methodological considerations warrant prospective acknowledgment: Heterogeneity in intervention durations across studies may inflate short-term efficacy estimates. Ambiguous definitions of ‘standard lifestyle interventions’ in source literature could introduce control group heterogeneity. Exclusion of non-English publications may create geographical evidence gaps. Variability in GLP-1 receptor agonist dosing strategies may influence effect size estimations. Particular caution is warranted when interpreting findings where sequential analysis suggests insufficient evidence, as such scenarios may generate conclusion uncertainty.

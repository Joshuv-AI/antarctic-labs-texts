Gravimetry enhanced by nonreciprocal optomechanical coupling
Dong Xie, Chunling Xu

Abstract
We explore how to measure the gravitational acceleration by using a dissipative optomechanical cavity. What is quite different from the conventional measurement methods is that we have constructed a nonreciprocal optomechanical coupling. We demonstrate that nonreciprocal coupling has a much greater advantage than reciprocal coupling. When the coherent optomechanical coupling is relatively weak and the driving intensity of single photon is strong, nonreciprocal coupling can improve the measurement precision by a factor of two. When the driving strength of single photon tends to infinity, reciprocal coupling fails to obtain any information about the gravitational acceleration, while nonreciprocal coupling still does. Using a two-photon driving, the measurement uncertainty of the gravitational acceleration will tend to zero as the intensity of two-photon driving approaches the critical point. The critical value of the two-photon driving intensity required for nonreciprocal coupling is finite, but the critical value of the two-photon driving intensity required for reciprocal coupling is infinite. The combination of the amplification of mechanical parameters and the extra force can not improve the measurement precision, but it can enhance the susceptibility. Furthermore, when the single-photon driving field is relatively weak, we analytically calculate the quantum Fisher information. The results show that, under most experimental parameters, nonreciprocal coupling still performs better than reciprocal coupling.

In this letter, we utilize an adiabatical elimination to construct a nonreciprocal coupling between the cavity-field mode and the mechanical mode. By measuring the steady-state cavity field, information about the gravitational acceleration can be obtained.
When the driving intensity of single-photon is strong, the mean-field approximation can be used to obtain analytical results about the measurement precision of the gravitational acceleration. The mechanical parametric amplification
-By a nonlinear modulation of the spring constant
of the mechanical resonator, the Hamiltonian of the mechanical parametric amplification is described by

\displaystyle H_{d}=\frac{\upsilon}{2}b^{2}+\frac{\upsilon^{*}}{2}b^{2\dagger}.

(36)

When the amplitude of the mechanical parametric amplification

\upsilon_{c}=\pm\sqrt{(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}}

\displaystyle|\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|=0.

This indicates that the amplification of mechanical parameters does not improve the measurement precision of

In order to effectively utilize the amplification of mechanical parameters, an additional external force

The Hamiltonian about the force

\displaystyle H_{f}=F(b+b^{\dagger}),

(38)

where

As a result, the combination of the amplification of mechanical parameters and the extra force can enhance the measurement susceptibility of the gravitational acceleration. Especially near the critical point, the measurement susceptibility tends to infinity. In the case of non-reciprocal coupling, the magnitude of the additional force does not require the knowledge of the photon number of the steady-state cavity field; while in the case of reciprocal coupling, the photon number of the steady-state cavity field needs to be known to obtain the appropriate magnitude of the additional force. Therefore, in the case of non-reciprocal coupling, the regulation of the additional force becomes easier.

Whether in the case of non-reciprocal or reciprocal coupling, the noise will also be amplified proportionally. Therefore, the measurement precision can not be improved by the combination of the amplification of mechanical parameters and the extra force.

Weak single-photon driving-

In the previous section, we have explored that under a strong single-photon driving intensity, the mean-field approximation can be utilized. In this section, we investigate whether non-reciprocal coupling can further enhance the measurement precision of the gravitational acceleration under the weak single-photon driving limit:

Including the additional external force

Conclusion and outlook
-We construct the nonreciprocal optomechanical coupling to sense the gravitational acceleration
g
. The results show that in many cases, non-reciprocal coupling performs better than reciprocal coupling in improving the measurement precision of gravitational parameters. The nonreciprocal coupling can achieve a two-fold improvement in the measurement precision in the case of the strong single-photon driving and weak coherent coupling. As the single-photon driving intensity approaches infinity, the measurement uncertainty in the non-reciprocal coupling case stabilizes at a finite value, while in the reciprocal coupling case, the measurement uncertainty stabilizes at an infinite value. In the case of nonreciprocal coupling, the measurement uncertainty tends to zero as the two-photon driving strength approaches the finite critical value. However, in order to achieve a similar result for the reciprocal coupling situation, an infinitely large two-photon driving intensity is required. In the case of the weak single-photon driving, non-reciprocal coupling still
performs better than reciprocal coupling under most experimental parameters. Our scheme is feasible in different experimental platforms

\displaystyle H_{I}=\mu a^{\dagger}a(c+c^{\dagger})+\nu b^{\dagger}c+\nu^{*}bc^{\dagger}.

(A1)

The master equation of the modes
a,b,c
is described by

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{abc}=-i[H+H_{I}+H_{c},\rho]+\sum_{j=a,b,c}\gamma_{j}\mathcal{D}_{j}[\rho_{abc}],

(A2)

where
H_{c}=\omega_{c}c^{\dagger}c
denotes the Hamiltonian of the mode
c
with the frequency
\omega_{c}
.
The model
c
suffers from a large local dissipation
\gamma_{c}
.
The quantum Langevin-Heisenberg equation of the operator
c
is given by

\displaystyle\dot{c}=(-i\omega_{c}-\gamma_{c})c-i\mu a^{\dagger}a-i\nu b+\sqrt{2\gamma_{c}}c_{in}.

(A3)

Assuming that
\gamma_{c}\gg\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}
, the mode
c
can be adiabatically eliminated. Let
\dot{c}=0
, we get that

\displaystyle{c}=(-i\mu a^{\dagger}a-i\nu b+\sqrt{2\gamma_{c}}c_{in})/(i\omega_{c}+\gamma_{c}).

(A4)

Let
\nu=\omega_{c}-i\gamma_{c}
and substitute the above equation into the master equation Eq. (
A2
), we can derive the reduced master equation

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{j=a,b}\kappa_{j}\mathcal{D}_{j}[\rho]+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\omega_{c}^{2}+\gamma_{c}^{2}}\mathcal{D}_{z}[\rho].

(A5)

Set
\frac{\mu^{2}}{\omega_{c}^{2}+\gamma_{c}^{2}}=\lambda
, the dissipative coupling in Eq. (
2
) of the main text can be obtained.

Appendix B: Nonreciprocal coupling

The evolution equations of quantum fluctuations are given by

\displaystyle\dot{\delta a}=

\displaystyle[-\gamma_{a}-\kappa+2i\kappa(\beta+\beta^{*})]\delta a-2i\kappa\alpha(\delta b+\delta b^{\dagger})

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2\gamma_{a}}a_{in}-i\sqrt{2\lambda}\alpha(z_{in}+z^{\dagger}_{in},)

(B1)

\displaystyle\dot{\delta b}=

\displaystyle(-i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa)\delta b+\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}b_{in}+\sqrt{2\kappa}z_{in}.

(B2)

The above equations can be restated as

\displaystyle\mathbf{\dot{A}}=\mathbb{M}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}_{in}

(B3)

in which,

\mathbb{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}-\gamma_{a}-\kappa-i\theta\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ -2i\kappa\alpha\ \ \ -2i\kappa\alpha\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ -\gamma_{a}-\kappa+i\theta\ \ \ 2i\kappa\alpha^{*}\ \ \ \ \ 2i\kappa\alpha^{*}\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ -i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa\ \ \ \ 0\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa\end{array}\right).

where
\theta=-2\kappa(\beta+\beta^{*})
,
\mathbf{A}=\{\delta a,\delta a^{\dagger},\delta b,\delta b^{\dagger}\}

\mathbf{A}_{in}=\{A_{1in},A^{\dagger}_{1in},A_{2in},A^{\dagger}_{2in}\}
, where the noise operators
A_{1in}=\sqrt{2\gamma_{a}}a_{in}-i\sqrt{2\kappa}\alpha(z_{in}+z^{\dagger}_{in})
,
A_{2in}=\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}b_{in}+\sqrt{2\kappa}z_{in}.

The solution is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{{A}}(t)=\exp[\mathbb{M}t]\mathbf{A}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}ds\exp[\mathbb{M}(t-s)]\mathbf{A}_{in}(s).

(B5)

According to the above solution, we can obtain

\displaystyle{\delta a}(t)=

\displaystyle\exp\{[-\gamma_{a}-\kappa+2i\kappa(\beta+\beta^{*})]t\}\delta a(0)+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\exp\{[-\gamma_{a}-\kappa+2i\kappa(\beta+\beta^{*})](t-s)\}

\displaystyle\times\{-2i\kappa\alpha[\delta b(s)+\delta b^{\dagger}(s)]+\sqrt{2\gamma_{a}}a_{in}(s)

\displaystyle-i\sqrt{2\kappa}\alpha(z_{in}(s)+z^{\dagger}_{in}(s))\},

(B6)

\displaystyle\delta b(t)=

\displaystyle\exp[(-i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\lambda)t]\delta b(0)+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\exp[(-i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa)(t-s)]

\displaystyle\times[\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}b_{in}(s)+\sqrt{2\lambda}z_{in}(s)].

(B7)

The cavity-field mode at the steady state is described by

\displaystyle\delta a(t\rightarrow\infty)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\{\exp[-t(\kappa+\gamma_{a}+i\theta)]A_{1in}(t)+

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha\kappa\{\exp[(-\kappa-\gamma_{b}+i\omega_{b})t]-\exp[(-\kappa-\gamma_{a}-i\theta)t]\}A_{2in}(t)}{i(\gamma_{a}-\gamma_{b})+\omega_{b}-\theta}+

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha\kappa\{\exp[(-\kappa-\gamma_{a}-i\theta)t]-\exp[(-\kappa-\gamma_{b}-i\omega_{b})t]\}A^{\dagger}_{2in}(t)}{i(-\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})+\omega_{b}+\theta}\}.

(B8)

The expectation value of the cavity-field mode at the steady state is described by

\displaystyle\langle\delta a^{\dagger}(t\rightarrow\infty)\delta a(t\rightarrow\infty)\rangle=\frac{\kappa|\alpha|^{2}[(\kappa+\gamma_{b})[(2\kappa+\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})^{2}+(\omega_{b}+\theta)^{2}]+4\kappa(\kappa+\gamma_{b})(2\kappa+\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})+8\kappa^{2}(2\kappa+\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})]}{(\kappa+\gamma_{a})(\kappa+\gamma_{b})(2\kappa+\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})^{2}+(\omega_{b}+\theta)^{2}}.

(B9)

Appendix C: Reciprocal coupling

When there is no dissipative coupling, i.e.,
\lambda=0
, the quantum Langevin-Heisenberg equations are given by

\displaystyle\dot{a}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{a}a+i\kappa a(b+b^{\dagger})+\sqrt{2\gamma_{a}}a_{in}-i\eta,

(C1)

\displaystyle\dot{b}

\displaystyle=(-i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b})b+i\kappa a^{\dagger}a+\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}b_{in}-iG.

(C2)

Using the mean-field approximation, the evolution equations of the expectation values are

\displaystyle\dot{\langle a\rangle}

\displaystyle=(-\gamma_{a})\langle a\rangle+i\kappa\langle a\rangle\langle b+b^{\dagger}\rangle-i\eta,

(C3)

\displaystyle\dot{\langle b\rangle}

\displaystyle=(-i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b})\langle b\rangle-iG+i\kappa|\langle a\rangle|^{2}.

(C4)

Let the left terms be equal to 0, we can obtain the expectation values at the steady state

\displaystyle\langle a\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha=\frac{i\eta}{-\gamma_{a}+\frac{2i\kappa\omega_{b}(\kappa|\alpha|^{2}-G)}{\gamma_{b}^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}}},

(C5)

\displaystyle\langle b\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta=\frac{i|\alpha|^{2}-iG}{i\omega_{b}+\gamma_{b}},

(C6)

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma_{a}^{2}+\frac{4\kappa^{2}\omega_{b}^{2}(\kappa|\alpha|^{2}-G)^{2}}{(\gamma_{b}^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2})^{2}}}.

(C7)

When
\kappa|\alpha|^{2}\ll G
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{i\eta}{-\gamma_{a}+\frac{2i\kappa\omega_{b}(-G)}{\gamma_{b}^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}}},

(C8)

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma_{a}^{2}+\frac{4\kappa^{2}\omega_{b}^{2}(G)^{2}}{(\gamma_{b}^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2})^{2}}}.

(C9)

When
\kappa|\alpha|^{2}\ll G
, we can obtain the measurement uncertainty of the gravitational acceleration in the case of reciprocal and non-reciprocal cases

\displaystyle\delta g_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{2g(\gamma_{a}^{2}+G_{1}^{2})}{\eta G_{1}},

(C10)

\displaystyle\delta g_{nr}

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\gamma_{a}^{2}+G_{1}^{2})}{\eta G_{1}}.

(C11)

The ratio of the uncertainties of the gravitational acceleration measured in reciprocal and nonreciprocal cases is

\displaystyle R=\frac{\delta g_{nr}}{\delta g_{r}}=1/2.

(C12)

When the driving strength
\eta
tends to be infinite,
the measurement uncertainty in the case of nonreciprocal case is given by

\displaystyle{\delta g_{nr}}=\frac{2g^{2}\zeta[(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+G_{1}^{2}]}{G_{1}^{2}},

(C13)

in which,

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa[(\kappa+\gamma_{b})[(2\kappa+\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})^{2}+(\omega_{b}+\theta)^{2}]+4\kappa(\kappa+\gamma_{b})(2\kappa+\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})+8\kappa^{2}(2\kappa+\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})]}{(\kappa+\gamma_{a})(\kappa+\gamma_{b})[(2\kappa+\gamma_{a}+\gamma_{b})^{2}+(\omega_{b}+\theta)^{2}]},

(C14)

\displaystyle G_{1}=4G\kappa\omega_{b}/[(\gamma_{b}+\kappa)^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}].

(C15)

In the case of the reciprocal coupling and infinite
\eta
, the solution of Eq. (
C7
) is given by

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}=\frac{2G}{3\kappa}+\frac{\eta^{2/3}(\gamma_{b}^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2})}{{(2\kappa^{2}\omega_{b})}^{2/3}}.

(C16)

The expectation value of the homodyne detection is given by

\displaystyle\langle M\rangle=\frac{2\eta}{[(2\kappa^{2}\omega_{b}/(\gamma_{b}^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2})]^{1/3}\eta^{2/3}-2\kappa\omega_{b}G/(\gamma_{b}^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2})}.

(C17)

Then, by differentiating the above equation, we can obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|_{\eta\rightarrow\infty}=0.

(C18)

Appendix D: Two-photon driving

Including the two-photon driving, the evolution matrix can be described by

\displaystyle\mathbf{\dot{A}}=\mathbb{M}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}_{in},

(D1)

in which,

\mathbb{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}-\gamma_{a}-\kappa-iG_{1}\ \ \ \ \ -i\chi\ \ \ \ \ \ -2i\kappa\alpha\ \ \ \ -2i\kappa\alpha\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ i\chi\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\gamma_{a}-\kappa+iG_{1}\ \ 2i\kappa\alpha^{*}\ \ \ \ \ \ 2i\kappa\alpha^{*}\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ -i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa\ \ \ 0\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\ \ \ \ i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa\end{array}\right).

The noise operators are

\mathbf{A}_{in}=\{A_{1in},A^{\dagger}_{1in},A_{2in},A^{\dagger}_{2in}\}
, where
A_{1in}=\sqrt{2\gamma_{a}}a_{in}-i\sqrt{2\kappa}\alpha(z_{in}+z^{\dagger}_{in})
, and
A_{2in}=\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}b_{in}+\sqrt{2\kappa}z_{in}.

The eigenvalues of the evolution matrix are given by :
\pm i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa,-\gamma_{a}-\kappa\pm\sqrt{\chi^{2}-G_{1}^{2}}
.
The condition for the system to reach a steady state is that all eigenvalues of the evolution matrix should be negative.
The critical point appears at
\chi_{c}=\pm\sqrt{(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+G_{1}^{2}}
. When
-\chi_{c}<\chi<\chi_{c}
, the system can reach the steady state; otherwise, the system will not reach a steady state.

When the two-photon driving strength
\chi
is close to the critical values
\chi_{c}
, we can obtain

\displaystyle{\langle\delta a^{\dagger}\delta a\rangle_{s}}|_{\chi\rightarrow\chi_{c}}\propto[(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+G_{1}^{2}-\chi^{2}]^{-3}.

(D3)

The measurement uncertainty of the gravitational acceleration is described by

\displaystyle\delta g_{\chi\rightarrow\chi_{c}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2{\langle\delta a^{\dagger}\delta a\rangle_{s}}|_{\chi\rightarrow\chi_{c}}+1}}{|\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|_{\chi\rightarrow\chi_{c}}}

\displaystyle\propto[(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+G_{1}^{2}-\chi^{2}]^{1/2}.

(D4)

Appendix E: Mechanical parametric amplification

In the frame rotating at
\omega_{c}=\omega_{d}
, including the mechanical parametric amplification, the corresponding quantum Langevin-Heisenberg equations of operators
a
and
b
are given by

\displaystyle\dot{a}=

\displaystyle(-\gamma_{a}-\kappa)a+2i\kappa a(b+b^{\dagger})

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2\gamma_{a}}a_{in}-i\eta-i\sqrt{2\lambda}a(z_{in}+z^{\dagger}_{in}),

(E1)

\displaystyle\dot{b}=

\displaystyle(-i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa)b-i\upsilon b^{\dagger}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}b_{in}+\sqrt{2\lambda}z_{in}-iG.

(E2)

The mean value at the steady state is given by

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle={\langle a\rangle}=\frac{-i\eta}{\gamma_{a}+\kappa-iG_{2}},

(E3)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle={\langle b\rangle}=\frac{G[\lambda-\omega_{b}-i(\kappa+\gamma_{b})]}{(\kappa+\gamma_{b})^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}-\upsilon^{2}},

(E4)

where the phase factor
G_{2}=\frac{4G\kappa(\upsilon-\omega_{b})}{(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}-\upsilon^{2}}
.
Then, by differentiating the above equation, we can obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|=\frac{2\eta G_{2}}{g[(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+G_{2}^{2}]}-\frac{4\eta G_{2}^{3}}{g[(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+G_{2}^{2}]^{2}}

(E5)

When the driving strength
\upsilon
is close to the critical value
\upsilon_{c}=\pm\sqrt{(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}}
, the phase factor tends to be infinite. This further leads to the measurement susceptibility approaching zero, i.e.,

\displaystyle|\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|=0.

(E6)

This indicates that the amplification of mechanical parameters not only fails to enhance the measurement precision of
g
, but actually reduces it.

In order to effectively utilize the amplification of mechanical parameters, an additional external force
f
is required.
The Hamiltonian about the force
f
is described by

\displaystyle H_{f}=F(b+b^{\dagger}).

(E7)

where
F=f\sqrt{\frac{1}{2m\omega_{b}}}
.

Including the Hamiltonian
H_{f}
, the quantum Langevin-Heisenberg equations of operators
a
and
b
are rewritten as

\displaystyle\dot{a}=

\displaystyle(-\gamma_{a}-\kappa)a+2i\kappa a(b+b^{\dagger})

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2\gamma_{a}}a_{in}-i\eta-i\sqrt{2\lambda}a(z_{in}+z^{\dagger}_{in})

(E8)

\displaystyle\dot{b}=

\displaystyle(-i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b}-\kappa)b-i\upsilon b^{\dagger}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}b_{in}+\sqrt{2\lambda}z_{in}-i(G-F).

(E9)

The mean values at the steady state are given by

\displaystyle\alpha=

\displaystyle{\langle a\rangle}=\frac{-i\eta}{\gamma_{a}+\kappa-iG_{3}},

(E10)

\displaystyle\beta=

\displaystyle{\langle b\rangle}=\frac{(G-F)[\lambda-\omega_{b}-i(\kappa+\gamma_{b})]}{(\kappa+\gamma_{b})^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}-\upsilon^{2}},

(E11)

where the phase factor
G_{3}=\frac{4(G-F)\kappa(\upsilon-\omega_{b})}{(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}-\upsilon^{2}}
.
The susceptibility is derived

\displaystyle|\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|=\frac{2\eta G_{2}}{g[(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+G_{3}^{2}]}-\frac{4\eta G_{2}G_{3}^{2}}{g[(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+G_{3}^{2}]^{2}}.

(E12)

When
G=F
, the value
G_{3}=0
, one can obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|=\frac{2\eta G_{2}}{g(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}}.

(E13)

At this point, when the driving intensity of mechanical parameter approaches the critical value, the measurement sensitivity is close to be infinite.

The reciprocal coupling:
In the case of reciprocal coupling, the quantum Langevin-Heisenberg equations of operators
a
and
b
are given by

\displaystyle\dot{a}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{a}a+i\kappa a(b+b^{\dagger})+\sqrt{2\gamma_{a}}a_{in}-i\eta,

(E14)

\displaystyle\dot{b}

\displaystyle=(-i\omega_{b}-\gamma_{b})b-i\upsilon b^{\dagger}+i\kappa a^{\dagger}a+\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}b_{in}-i(G-F).

(E15)

The mean value at the steady state is given by

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle={\langle a\rangle}=\frac{-i\eta}{\gamma_{a}-iG_{4}},

(E16)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle={\langle b\rangle}=\frac{(G-F-\kappa|\alpha|^{2})(\upsilon-\omega_{b}-i\gamma_{b})}{\gamma_{b}^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}-\upsilon^{2}},

(E17)

where the phase factor
G_{4}=\frac{2(G-F-\kappa|\alpha|^{2})\kappa(\upsilon-\omega_{b})}{(\gamma_{a})^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}-\upsilon^{2}}
.
By squaring the absolute value of
\alpha
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}=\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma_{a}^{2}+G_{4}^{2}}.

(E18)

When
G=F
, the above equation has no real solutions, which means there is no steady state.
When
F=G-\kappa|\alpha|^{2}
and
\upsilon\rightarrow\upsilon_{c}
, we can obtain the solution

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}=\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma_{a}^{2}}

(E19)

The measurement susceptibility is given by

\displaystyle|\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|=\frac{\eta G_{2}}{g\gamma_{a}^{2}}.

(E20)

By following the similar steps mentioned earlier, we can obtain the expectation value of the noise operator

\displaystyle{\langle\delta a^{\dagger}\delta a\rangle_{s}}|_{\upsilon\rightarrow\upsilon_{c}}\propto 1/[(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}-\upsilon^{2}]^{2}.

(E21)

The measurement uncertainty is described by

\displaystyle\delta g_{\chi\rightarrow\upsilon_{c}}=\frac{\sqrt{2{\langle\delta a^{\dagger}\delta a\rangle_{s}}|_{\chi\rightarrow\upsilon_{c}}+1}}{|\partial_{g}\langle M\rangle|_{\chi\rightarrow\upsilon_{c}}}=C,

(E22)

where the value of
C
is independent of the
(\gamma_{a}+\kappa)^{2}+\omega_{b}^{2}-\upsilon^{2}
. It shows that the combination of the amplification of mechanical parameters and the extra force can not improve the measurement precision of
g
, but only improve the measurement susceptibility.

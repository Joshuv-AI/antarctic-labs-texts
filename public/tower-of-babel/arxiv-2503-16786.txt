Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Let
L_{p}({T})\ (1\leq p<\infty)
be the usual Lebesgue space
consisting of measurable functions
f
with finite norm

\|f\|_{p}=\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{T}}|f(x)|^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p},

and
L_{\infty}({T})
be the space
C({T})
of continuous
functions on
{T}
with norm
\|f\|_{\infty}=\max\limits_{x\in{T}}|f(x)|
, where
\mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi]
is the torus.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be the space of all trigonometric
polynomials
T_{\mathbf{a}}
of degree
\leq n
, where

{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{2n+1})\in{R}^{2n+1}
,
T_{\mathbf{a}}
is of the
form

(1.1)

T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x)=a_{1}+\sum\limits^{n}_{k=1}(a_{2k}\sqrt{2}\cos kx+a_{2k+1}\sqrt{2}\sin kx).

The classical Nikolskii inequality
[
28
]
states that there is a constant

C>0
such that for any trigonometric polynomial
T_{{\mathbf{a}}}
of
degree
\leq n
,

(1.2)

\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}\leq Cn^{(1/p-1/q)_{+}}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p},

for any
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, where
a_{+}=a
if

a\geq 0
and
a_{+}=0
if
a<0
. The order
(1/p-1/q)_{+}
in the
above inequality (
1.2
) is sharp.

Alternatively we introduce, for given
p,q
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, the Nikolskii
factor for
T_{\mathbf{a}}
is defined by

\mathcal{N}_{p,q}(T_{{\mathbf{a}}}):=\frac{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}.

The worst Nikolskii factor for
\mathcal{T}_{n}
is defined by

N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n}):=\sup_{0\neq T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\mathcal{N}_{p,q}(T_{{\mathbf{a}}})=\sup_{0\neq T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\frac{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}},

with the supremum being taken over all trigonometric polynomial
of degree
\leq n
whose coefficients are not all zero. Then the
above Nikolskii inequality states

(1.3)

N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})\asymp n^{(1/p-1/q)_{+}},

i.e., that worst case bound for
\mathcal{N}_{p,q}(T_{{\mathbf{a}}})
is

\Theta(n^{(1/p-1/q)_{+}})
. Here we use the notion
A_{n}\asymp B_{n}
to express
A_{n}\ll B_{n}
and
A_{n}\gg B_{n}
, and
A_{n}\ll B_{n}

(
A_{n}\gg B_{n}
) means that there exists a constant
c>0

independent of
n
such that
A_{n}\leq cB_{n}
(
A_{n}\geq cB_{n}
).

In this paper, we consider the expected Nikolskii factor for a
random trigonometric polynomial. Given a polynomial basis

p_{1},\dots,p_{N}
, a random polynomial
p
is a polynomial whose
coefficients are random, i.e.,

p=\sum_{k=1}^{N}X_{k}p_{k},

where

X_{k},\,1\leq k\leq N
are i.i.d random variables. In recent years
the study of random polynomials has attracted much interest, and
there are numerous papers devoted to this field. For example, for
zeros of random polynomials, see
[
5
,
7
,
13
,
14
,
24
,
27
]
;
for norms, see
[
4
,
8
,
9
,
16
,
18
,
22
,
26
]
; for average
Markov factors, see
[
3
,
20
,
25
,
30
]
.

There are also many papers devoted to studying the Nikolskii type
inequalities, see for example,
[
2
,
12
,
15
,
17
,
28
]
. However,
as far as we know, there are no papers devoted to investigating
the average Nikolskii factors for random polynomials. In this
paper, we consider random trigonometric polynomials
T_{{\mathbf{a}}}

given by (
1.1
), where
{\mathbf{a}}
is a Gaussian random vector with
mean 0 and covariance matrix
\sigma^{2}I_{N}
,
\sigma>0
,
I_{N}
is
the
N
by
N
identity matrix,
N=\dim\mathcal{T}_{n}=2n+1
. For

1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, we discuss the average Nikolskii factor

N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n}):=\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}\Big).

Clearly,
we have

N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})\leq N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n}).

It turns out that in many cases,
the average Nikolskii factor
N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})

is significantly smaller than the worst Nikolskii factor

N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})
. We state our main result as
follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
N=2n+1
. Then we have

(1.4)

N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})=\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}\Big)\asymp\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&\ \,1\leq p,q<\infty\ or\ p=q=\infty,\\
&(\ln N)^{1/2},&\ \,1\leq p<\infty,q=\infty,\\
&(\ln N)^{-1/2},&\ \,1\leq q<\infty,p=\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 1.2
.

From (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) we know that the order of
the worst and average Nikolskii factors for
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
or
p=q=\infty
are same, which both are equivalent to
the constant
1
. Whereas, for other cases, the orders of average
Nikolskii factors are significantly smaller than the worst case
Nikolskii factors. More precisely, for
1\leq p<q<\infty
, the
worst case Nikolskii factor is order degree to the
1/p-1/q
,
while the average case Nikolskii factors is order degree to the 0;
for
1\leq p<q=\infty
, the worst case Nikolskii factor is order
degree to the
1/p
, while the order of the average Nikolskii
factor is
(\ln N)^{1/2}
; for
1\leq q<p=\infty
, the worst case
Nikolskii factor is order degree to 0, while the order of the
average Nikolskii factor is
(\ln N)^{-1/2}
.

This indicates that for
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
or
1\leq q<p=\infty
,
the average Nikolskii factor
N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})

is significantly smaller than the worst Nikolskii factor

N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})
.

Remark 1.3
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n+1}
be a sequence of
independent random variables on some probability space
(\Omega,A,P)
taking the values
\pm 1
with probability
1/2
, that is
symmetric Bernoulli or Rademacher random variables. Let

T_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon_{1}a_{1}+\sum\limits^{n}_{k=1}(\varepsilon_{2k}\sqrt{2}\cos kx+\varepsilon_{2k+1}\sqrt{2}\sin kx)

be a Rademacher random polynomial.
Then we have for
1\leq p,q<\infty
,

\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}}{\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}}\Big)\asymp 1.

We organize this paper as follows. In Section 2, we give the
estimate of average Nikolskii factor for general formulation.
Section 3 contains two subsections. In subsection 3.1, we
determine the exact order of the average Nikolskii factor in the
case of
1\leq q\leq\infty,\,p=2
. In subsection 3.2, we give the
upper bound of
\mathbb{E}(1/\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{\infty})^{r}
. In Section
4, we prove Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in Section 5, we give
the generalization to random multivariate trigonometric
polynomials.

2.
General formulation

Let
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}:=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|{\bf x}|_{2}=1\}
denote
the unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
equipped with the usual
surface measure
d\sigma
, where
{\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}
is the usual
Euclidean inner product and
|{\bf x}|_{2}=({\bf x}\cdot{{\bf x}})^{1/2}
is the
usual Euclidean norm. Suppose that
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
compact and
\mu
is a Borel probability measure supported on
K
.
We denote by
L_{p}:=L_{p}(K,d\mu),1\leq p<\infty
, the Lebesgue
space on
K
endowed with the finite norm

\|f\|_{p}:=\Big(\int_{K}|f({\bf x})|^{p}d\mu({\bf x})\Big)^{1/p},

and by
L_{\infty}=C(K)
the space of continuous functions with
the norm

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\max\limits_{{\bf x}\in K}|f({\bf x})|.

Then,
L_{2}
is a
Hilbert space with the inner product

(2.1)

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{K}f({\bf x})g({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x}).

Suppose that
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is the
N
-dimensional linear
subspace of
C(K)
, and

\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\dots,\varphi_{N}\}
is an arbitrary
orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{V}_{n}
with respect to the inner
product (
2.1
). For
{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})\in{R}^{N}
, we
define

f_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varphi_{j}({\bf x}).

In this paper we study Nikolskii factors. For given
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, the
(p,q)
-Nikolskii factor of

f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
is defined by

\mathcal{N}_{p,q}(f_{{\mathbf{a}}}):=\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}.

The worst case
(p,q)
-Nikolskii factor for
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is
defined by

N^{\rm{wor}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{V}_{n}):=\sup_{0\neq f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}}\mathcal{N}_{p,q}(f_{{\mathbf{a}}})=\sup_{{\mathbf{a}}\neq 0}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}},

where the
supremum is taken over all functions
f_{{\mathbf{a}}}
of
\mathcal{V}_{n}

whose coefficients
{\mathbf{a}}
are not all zero.

A random function
f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
is defined by

f_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})=\sum\limits^{N}_{i=1}a_{i}\varphi_{i}({\bf x}),

where
{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the
coefficients
a_{i}
are independent
N(0,\sigma^{2})
random
variables with the common normal density functions

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}.

That is,
{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2}I_{N})
is a
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued Gaussian random
vector with mean
0
and covariance matrix
\sigma^{2}I_{N}
,
where
I_{N}
is the
N
by
N
identity matrix. Let
\gamma_{N}

denote the corresponding Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

given by

\gamma_{N}(G)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{N/2}}\int_{G}e^{-\frac{|{\bf x}|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}d{\bf x}\ {\rm for\ each\ Borel\ subset}\ G\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}.

For given
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, the average (expected) Nikolskii
factor for
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle N^{{\rm ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{V}_{n}):={E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}d\gamma_{N}({\mathbf{a}})=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{N/2}}\int_{{R}^{N}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}d{\mathbf{a}}.

Note that the average Nikolskii factor
N^{{\rm ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{V}_{n})
is independent of the choice of the
orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{V}_{n}
due to the rotation
invariance of the Gaussian measure
\gamma_{N}
.

A connection between the average Nikolskii factor
N^{{\rm ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{V}_{n})
and the worst case Nikolskii factor

N^{\rm{wor}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{V}_{n})
can be seen as follows:

\displaystyle N^{{\rm ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{V}_{n})={E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}\leq\sup_{{\mathbf{a}}\neq 0}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}=N^{\rm{wor}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{V}_{n}).

We have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, we have

N^{\rm{ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{V}_{n})=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{b}}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{b}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{b}}}\|_{p}},

where
{\mathbf{b}}\sim N(0,I_{N})_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is a

\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued Gaussian random vector with mean
0
and
covariance matrix
I_{N}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{a}}=\sigma{\mathbf{b}}
. Due to the fact
{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,\sigma I_{N})
, we
have
{\mathbf{b}}\sim N(0,I_{N})
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}d{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\|f_{\sigma{\mathbf{b}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{\sigma{\mathbf{b}}}\|_{p}}e^{-\frac{|\sigma{\mathbf{b}}|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}d(\sigma{\mathbf{b}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{b}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{b}}}\|_{p}}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{b}}|_{2}^{2}}{2}}d{\mathbf{b}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{b}}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{b}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{b}}}\|_{p}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the sequel, by Proposition 2.1 we always assume that
\sigma=1

and
{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,I_{N})
without loss of generality.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,\,\,k,l\in\mathbb{N}
, we have for

l<k+N
,

(2.3)

\mathbb{E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{2}}\asymp N^{-l/2}\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q},

and for
l<N
,

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{2}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{p}}\asymp N^{k/2}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{p}}.

In particular, we have

(2.5)

\mathbb{E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{2}}\asymp N^{-1/2}\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}.

Proof.

Using Parseval’s identity
\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{2}=|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}
and

{\mathbf{a}}=r\xi,r=|{\mathbf{a}}|,\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we have for
l<N+k
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{2}}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}^{2}}{2}}d{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q}}{|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}^{l}}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}^{2}}{2}}d{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{k-l+N-1}dr\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\|f_{\xi}\|^{k}_{q}d\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\sqrt{2})^{k-l-2}}{\pi^{N/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{k-l+N}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\|f_{\xi}\|^{k}_{q}d\sigma(\xi),

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}^{2}}{2}}d{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\|f_{\xi}\|^{k}_{q}d\sigma(\xi)\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{k+N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{(\sqrt{2})^{k-2}}{\pi^{N/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{k+N}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\|f_{\xi}\|^{k}_{q}d\sigma(\xi).

It follows that

(2.6)

\mathbb{E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{2}}=(\sqrt{2})^{-l}\Gamma\left(\frac{k-l+N}{2}\right)\Big/\Gamma\left(\frac{k+N}{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{q}.

By Stiring’s formula
(see
[
1
, p. 18]
), we have the asymptotic estimate

(2.7)

\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)e^{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi}x^{x+\frac{1}{2}}}=1,

from which it follows that

\Gamma\left(\frac{k-l+N}{2}\right)\Big/\Gamma\left(\frac{k+N}{2}\right)\asymp N^{-l/2}.

By (
2.6
) we obtain (
2.3
).

Similarly, we have for
l<N+k
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{2}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}^{k}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}^{l}}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|_{2}^{2}}{2}}d{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{k-l+N-1}dr\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\|f_{\xi}\|^{l}_{p}}d\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\sqrt{2})^{k-l-2}}{\pi^{N/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{k-l+N}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\|f_{\xi}\|^{l}_{p}}d\sigma(\xi),

and for
l<N
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\|f_{\xi}\|^{l}_{p}}d\sigma(\xi)\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{-l+N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{(\sqrt{2})^{-l-2}}{\pi^{N/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{N-l}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\|f_{\xi}\|^{l}_{p}}d\sigma(\xi).

It follows that for
l<N
,

\mathbb{E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{2}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{p}}=(\sqrt{2})^{k}\Gamma\left(\frac{k-l+N}{2}\right)\Big/\Gamma\left(\frac{N-l}{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{p}}.

By (
2.7
)
we get

\mathbb{E}\frac{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{k}_{2}}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{p}}\asymp N^{k/2}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{l}_{p}}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Next, we estimate
\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}^{q}
. This quantity is
intimately related to the Christoffel function for
\mathcal{V}_{n}
.
We recall that the Christoffel function for
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is
defined by

\Lambda({\bf x})=\inf_{p({\bf x})=1,\,p\in\mathcal{V}_{n}}\|p\|_{2}^{2}.

It follows
from
[
10
, Theorem 3.5.6]
that

\Lambda({\bf x})=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varphi_{k}^{2}({\bf x})}.

We have the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
1\leq q<\infty
. We have

(2.8)

\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{q}_{q}=C(q)^{q}\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits^{N}_{i=1}|\varphi_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{q}^{q}\ ,

where
{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,I_{N})
, and

C(q)=({E}|a_{1}|^{q})^{1/q}=\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{R}}|t|^{q}e^{-t^{2}/2}dt\Big)^{1/q}=\pi^{-\frac{1}{2q}}2^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma^{1/q}\Big(\frac{q+1}{2}\Big).

Proof.

For
1\leq q<\infty
, by the Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{q}_{q}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{K}|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})|^{q}d\mu({\bf x})

(2.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}|m({\bf x})|^{q/2}\mathbb{E}\Big|\frac{f_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})}{\sqrt{m({\bf x})}}\Big|^{q}d\mu({\bf x}),

where

m({\bf x}):=\frac{1}{\Lambda({\bf x})}=\sum\limits^{N}_{k=1}|\varphi_{k}({\bf x})|^{2}.

Since
{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,I_{N})
, we get for arbitrary fixed
{\bf x}\in K
,

\eta({\bf x}):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m({\bf x})}}f_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})\sim N(0,1),

and

{E}|\eta({\bf x})|^{q}={E}|a_{1}|^{q}=C(q)^{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{R}}|t|^{q}e^{-t^{2}/2}dt=\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{\frac{q}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{q+1}{2}\Big),

which
is independent of
{\bf x}\in K
. It follows from (
2.9
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{q}_{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{K}|m({\bf x})|^{q}\mathbb{E}|\eta({\bf x})|^{q}d\mu({\bf x})

\displaystyle=C(q)^{q}\int_{K}|m({\bf x})|^{q/2}d\mu({\bf x})

\displaystyle=C(q)^{q}\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits^{N}_{k=1}|\varphi_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|^{q}_{q}\,.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

For any
1\leq s,q<\infty
, we have

(2.10)

\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{s}_{q}\asymp\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits^{N}_{i=1}|\varphi_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|^{s}_{q}.

Proof.

It follows from
[
31
, Theorem 1]
that

\left(\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{s}_{q}\right)^{1/s}\asymp\left(\mathbb{E}\|f_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{q}_{q}\right)^{1/q}.

According to
Theorem
2.3
, we get the required result (
2.10
).
∎

Remark 2.5
.

3.
Average Nikolskii factor for
\mathcal{T}_{n}

In this and next sections, we always suppose that

T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x)=a_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(a_{2k}\sqrt{2}\cos kx+a_{2k+1}\sqrt{2}\sin kx),

where
{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,I_{N})
. We note that
\{1,\sqrt{2}\cos x,\sqrt{2}\sin x,\dots,\sqrt{2}\cos nx,\sqrt{2}\sin nx\}
is an
orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.

3.1.
Estimates of
N^{\rm{ave}}_{2,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p=2,\,1\leq q\leq\infty
. We have

(3.1)

N^{\rm{ave}}_{2,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})=\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{2}}\Big)\asymp N^{-1/2}{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}\asymp\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&\ \,1\leq q<\infty,\\
&\sqrt{\ln N},&\ \,q=\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

where
N=2n+1
.

Proof.

We note that
\{1,\sqrt{2}\cos kx,\sqrt{2}\sin kx,\ k=1,2,\dots,n\}
is an
orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, and

m(x):=1^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2\cos^{2}kx+2\sin^{2}kx)=2n+1=N.

For
1\leq s,q<\infty
,
(
2.10
) and (
2.5
) give

\mathbb{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{s}_{q}\asymp\|m(x)^{1/2}\|_{q}^{s}\asymp N^{s/2},

and

N^{\rm{ave}}_{2,q}(\mathcal{T}_{n})\asymp N^{-1/2}{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}\asymp 1.

For
q=\infty
, by the Nikolskii inequality (
1.2
), the
Hölder inequality, and Theorem
2.3
, we have for

2<q<\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\ll N^{1/q}\mathbb{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}\leq N^{1/q}(\mathbb{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{q}_{q})^{1/q}

\displaystyle=N^{1/q+1/2}C(q)\ll N^{1/2+1/q}q^{1/2},

where in the last inequality, we used the fact that

C(q)^{1/q}\ll q^{1/2}.

This is due to

\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{C(q)}{q^{1/2}}=\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow+\infty}\Big(\frac{q-1}{q}\Big)^{1/2}2^{\frac{1}{2q}}\exp\Big(-\frac{q-1}{2q}\Big)=e^{-1/2}.

We remark
that the constants in the above inequalities are independent of

N
and
q
. Thus, taking
q=\ln N
, we get

\mathbb{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{\infty}\ll(N\ln N)^{1/2}.

By
(
2.5
) we obtain

(3.2)

N^{\rm{ave}}_{2,\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{n})\ll\sqrt{\ln N}.

Now we show the lower bound of

N^{\rm{ave}}_{2,\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{n})
. We set

X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{N}),\ \ X_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x_{k}),\ x_{k}=\frac{2\pi k}{2n+1},\ 1\leq k\leq N,

where
T_{{\mathbf{a}}}
is the random trigonometric polynomial given by (
1.1
),

{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,I_{N})
. Then
X
is the Gaussian centered
random vector with covariance matrix
C=(C_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N}
,
where
C_{ij}={E}(X_{i}X_{j})
. We note that

{E}(a_{k}a_{l})=\delta_{k,l}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&\ \,l\neq s,\\
&1,&\ \,l=s.\end{aligned}\right.,\ \ 1\leq k,l\leq N,

and

T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x_{i})=a_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(a_{2k}\sqrt{2}\cos kx_{i}+a_{2k+1}\sqrt{2}\sin kx_{i}).

It follows that

\displaystyle{E}(X_{i}X_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}{E}(T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x_{i})T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x_{j}))=\frac{1}{N}(1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\cos x_{i}\cos x_{j}+\sin x_{i}\sin x_{j}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}(1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\cos k(x_{i}-x_{j}))=\frac{1}{N}D_{n}(x_{i}-x_{j})=\delta_{i,j},

where
D_{n}
is the
Dirichlet kernel,

D_{n}(x)=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\cos kx=\frac{\sin(n+\frac{1}{2})x}{\sin\frac{x}{2}}.

Hence,
X\sim N(0,I_{N})
. This means that
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}

are the i.i.d. random variables with
X_{i}\sim N(0,1)
. It follows
from
[
29
,
23
]
that

{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{\infty}\geq\sqrt{N}\ {E}\max_{1\leq k\leq N}|X_{k}|\asymp\sqrt{N}\,\sqrt{\ln N}.

By (
2.5
) and (
3.2
) we obtain

N^{\rm{ave}}_{2,\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{n})\asymp\sqrt{\ln N}.

Theorem
3.1
is proved. ∎

3.2.
Upper estimates of
\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{r}_{\infty}})

Lemma 3.2
.

(See
[
6
, p. 2]
,
[
11
,
21
]
)
If
X\sim N(0,1)
, then for all
t>0
,

\sqrt{2}\pi^{-1/2}(t^{-1}-t^{-3})e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\leq\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq t)\geq\sqrt{2}\pi^{-1/2}t^{-1}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}.

In particular, if
\delta\in(0,e^{-1}]
, then

\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq c_{0}\sqrt{\ln\delta^{-1}})>\delta

for some absolute constant
c_{0}>0
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
r>0
. Then for
N>\max\{e^{4},r+1\}
, we have

(3.3)

\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{r}_{\infty}}\ll N^{-r/2}(\ln N)^{-r/2}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
we set

X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{N}),\ \ X_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x_{k}),\ x_{k}=\frac{2\pi k}{N},\ 1\leq k\leq N.

It follows that
X\sim N(0,I_{N})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{r}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x_{k})|^{r}}=N^{-\frac{r}{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|X_{k}|^{r}}.

By the variable substitution
t=s^{-r}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|X_{k}|^{r}}

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|X_{k}|^{r}}\geq t\Big)dt

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathbb{P}\Big(\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|X_{k}|\leq t^{-\frac{1}{r}}\Big)dt

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq t^{-\frac{1}{r}}))^{N}dt

\displaystyle=r\int^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq s))^{N}\frac{ds}{s^{r+1}}

\displaystyle\asymp\Big(\int^{\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\ln N)^{1/2}}_{0}+\int^{\infty}_{\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\ln N)^{1/2}}\Big)(\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq s))^{N}\frac{ds}{s^{r+1}}

\displaystyle=I+II,

where
c_{0}
is the absolute constant given in Lemma

3.2
. By Lemma
3.2
with
\delta_{0}=N^{-\frac{1}{4}}
and

N>e^{4}
, we get

(3.4)

{P}(|X_{1}|\leq\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\ln N)^{1/2})=1-{P}(|X_{1}|>c_{0}\sqrt{\ln\delta_{0}^{-1}})\leq 1-\delta_{0}=1-N^{-1/4}.

We note that

\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq s)}{s}=\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\int^{s}_{0}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}dt}{s}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}},

and

\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq s)\leq 1.

This means that

\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq s)\ll s.

By (
3.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int^{\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\ln N)^{1/2}}_{0}(\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq s))^{N}\frac{1}{s^{r+1}}ds

\displaystyle\ll\int^{\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\ln N)^{1/2}}_{0}(\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq s))^{N-r-1}ds

\displaystyle\ll(\ln N)^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\ln N)^{1/2}))^{N-r-1}

\displaystyle\leq(\ln N)^{1/2}\big(1-N^{-1/4}\big)^{N-r-1}.

Since

\lim_{N\to\infty}(\ln N)^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\big(1-N^{-1/4}\big)^{N-r-1}=\lim_{N\to\infty}(\ln N)^{\frac{r+1}{2}}e^{-(N-r-1)N^{-1/4}}=0,

we get

(\ln N)^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\big(1-N^{-1/4}\big)^{N-r-1}\ll 1.

It follows
that

I\ll(\ln N)^{1/2}\big(1-N^{-1/4}\big)^{N-r-1}\ll(\ln N)^{-r/2}.

We also have

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\ln N)^{1/2}}(\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|\leq s))^{N}\frac{1}{s^{r+1}}ds\leq\int^{\infty}_{\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\ln N)^{1/2}}\frac{1}{s^{r+1}}ds\asymp(\ln N)^{-\frac{r}{2}}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|X_{k}|^{r}}

\displaystyle\ll(\ln N)^{-\frac{r}{2}}.

This deduces that

\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{r}_{\infty}}\ll N^{-\frac{r}{2}}(\ln N)^{-\frac{r}{2}},

which completes the proof of Theorem
3.3
.
∎

5.
Generalization to
{T}^{d},\ d>1

In this section, we shall extend the results of the average
Nikolskii factors for random trigonometric polynomials on
1-dimensional torus
{T}
to the ones on the
d
-dimensional
torus
{T}^{d}=[0,2\pi]^{d}
.

Let
L_{p}({T}^{d})\ (1\leq p<\infty)
be the usual Lebesgue space
consisting of measurable functions
f
with finite norm

\|f\|_{p}=\Big(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{{T}^{d}}|f({\bf x})|^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p},

and
L_{\infty}({T}^{d})
be the space
C({T}^{d})
of
continuous functions on
{T}^{d}
with norm

\|f\|_{\infty}=\max\limits_{{\bf x}\in{T}^{d}}|f({\bf x})|
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}^{d}
be the space of all multivariate
trigonometric polynomials
T_{\mathbf{a}}
of form

(5.1)

T_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})=\sum_{\|{\bf k}\|_{\infty}\leq n}a_{\bf k}e^{i{\bf k}{\bf x}},

where

{\bf k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})\in{Z}^{d},\ \ \|{\bf k}\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|k_{i}|\leq n,\ \ {\mathbf{a}}=(a_{{\bf k}})\in{R}^{N},\ \ N=\dim\mathcal{T}_{n}^{d}=(2n+1)^{d}.

The
classical Nikolskii inequality
[
28
, Theorem 3.4]
states that
there is a constant
C>0
independent of
d
such that for any
trigonometric polynomial
T_{{\mathbf{a}}}
of degree
\leq n
,

(5.2)

\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}\leq(C^{d}N)^{(1/p-1/q)_{+}}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p},

for any
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. The order
(1/p-1/q)_{+}
in the
above inequality (
5.2
) is sharp. This means that

(5.3)

N_{p,q}^{\rm wor}(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{d})\asymp n^{d(1/p-1/q)_{+}},

i.e.,
the worst case Nikolskii factors
N_{p,q}^{\rm wor}(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{d})
is order the
n^{d(1/p-1/q)_{+}}
.

Next we consider the average Nikolskii factors
N_{p,q}^{\rm ave}(\mathcal{T}_{N}^{d})
. In this section, we always suppose that

T_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})=\sum_{\|{\bf k}\|_{\infty}\leq n}a_{\bf k}e^{i{\bf k}{\bf x}}

is a
random multivariate trigonometric polynomial, where
{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,I_{N})
. We remark that

\{e^{i{\bf k}{\bf x}}\}_{\|{\bf k}\|_{\infty}\leq n}

is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{T}_{n}^{d}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p=2,\,1\leq q\leq\infty
. We have

(5.4)

N^{\rm{ave}}_{2,q}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})=\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{2}}\Big)\asymp N^{-1/2}{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}\asymp\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&\ \,1\leq q<\infty,\\
&\sqrt{\ln N},&\ \,q=\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

where
N=(2n+1)^{d}
, and the equivalent
constants are independent of
N
and
d
.

Proof.

We note that
\{e^{i{\bf k}{\bf x}}\}_{\|{\bf k}\|_{\infty}\leq n}
is an
orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n}
, and

m({\bf x}):=\sum_{\|{\bf k}\|_{\infty}\leq n}|e^{i{\bf k}{\bf x}}|^{2}=(2n+1)^{d}=N.

For

,

2.10
) and (
2.5

\displaystyle=C^{d/q}N^{1/q+1/2}C(q)\ll C^{d/q}N^{1/2+1/q}q^{1/2}.

We remark that the constants in the above inequalities are

N,d,
and

\mathbb{E}\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{\infty}\ll(N\ln N)^{1/2}.

(5.5)

where the constant in the above

N
and
d
.

Now we show the lower bound of

N^{\rm{ave}}_{2,\infty}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})
. We set

X=(X_{\bf k}),\ \ X_{\bf k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}T_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x}_{\bf k}),

and

{\bf x}_{\bf k}=(x_{k_{1}},\ldots,x_{k_{d}}),\ x_{k_{i}}=\frac{2\pi k_{i}}{2n+1},\ \ k_{i}=0,1,\dots,2n,\ 1\leq i\leq d,

where

is the random trigonometric polynomial given by (

),

is the Gaussian centered
random vector with covariance matrix

where

\displaystyle{E}(X_{\bf k}\overline{X_{\bf{l}}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}{E}(T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x_{\bf k})\overline{T_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x_{\bf{l}})})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\|\bf{s}\|_{\infty}\leq n}e^{i\bf{s}(x_{\bf k}-{\bf x}_{\bf{l}})}

X=(X_{{\bf k}}):=(\mathaccent 869{X_{i}})^{N}_{i=1}\sim N(0,I_{N})

\mathaccent 869{X_{1}},\dots,\mathaccent 869{X_{N}}

X=(X_{{\bf k}})=(\mathaccent 869{X_{i}})^{N}_{i=1}\sim N(0,I_{N})
. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{r}_{\infty}}\leq N^{-\frac{r}{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|\mathaccent 869{X_{k}}|^{r}}.

From the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we know

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}|\mathaccent 869{X_{k}}|^{r}}

\displaystyle\ll(\ln N)^{-\frac{r}{2}}.

This deduces that

\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|^{r}_{\infty}}\ll N^{-\frac{r}{2}}(\ln N)^{-\frac{r}{2}},

which completes the proof of Theorem
5.2
.
∎

Finally, we get the estimates of the average
Nikolskii factors for random trigonometric polynomials on the
d
-dimensional
torus
{T}^{d}=[0,2\pi]^{d}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
N=(2n+1)^{d}
. Then we have

(5.7)

N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})=\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|T_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}\Big)\asymp\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&\ \,1\leq p,q<\infty\ or\ p=q=\infty,\\
&(\ln N)^{1/2},&\ \,1\leq p<\infty,q=\infty,\\
&(\ln N)^{-1/2},&\ \,1\leq q<\infty,p=\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

where the equivalent
constants are independent of
N
and
d
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section 4, by the
Hölder inequality, Theorems
2.2
,
5.1
and

5.2
, we obtain the upper bounds of

N^{\rm{ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})
for
1\leq p,q\leq\infty

as follows.

(5.8)

N^{\rm{ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})\ll\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&\ \,1\leq p,q<\infty\ or\ p=q=\infty,\\
&(\ln N)^{1/2},&\ \,1\leq p<\infty,q=\infty,\\
&(\ln N)^{-1/2},&\ \,1\leq q<\infty,p=\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

By (
4.5
) and (
5.8
) we get

N^{\rm{ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})\gg\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&\ \,1\leq p,q<\infty\ or\ p=q=\infty,\\
&(\ln N)^{1/2},&\ \,1\leq p<\infty,q=\infty,\\
&(\ln N)^{-1/2},&\ \,1\leq q<\infty,p=\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

which gives the lower bounds of

N^{\rm{ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})
for
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
.

Theorem
5.3
is proved.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

From (
5.3
) and (
5.7
) we know that the order of
the worst and average Nikolskii factors for
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
or
p=q=\infty
are same, which both are equivalent to
the constant
1
. Whereas, for other cases, the orders of average
Nikolskii factors are significantly smaller than the worst case
Nikolskii factors. More precisely, for
1\leq p<q<\infty
, the
worst case Nikolskii factor is order
N^{1/p-1/q}
,
while the average case Nikolskii factors is order 1;
for
1\leq p<q=\infty
, the worst case Nikolskii factor is order
N^{1/p}
, while the order of the average Nikolskii
factor is
(\ln N)^{1/2}
; for
1\leq q<p=\infty
, the worst case
Nikolskii factor is order 1, while the order of the
average Nikolskii factor is
(\ln N)^{-1/2}
.

This indicates that for
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
or
1\leq q<p=\infty
,
the average Nikolskii factor
N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})

is significantly smaller than the worst Nikolskii factor

N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{n})
.

Remark 5.5
.

It is interesting to note that the equivalence constants in (
5.7
) are independent of the dimension
d
.

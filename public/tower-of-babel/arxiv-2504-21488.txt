New Constructions of Distance-Biregular Graphs
Blas Fernández, Ferdinand Ihringer, Sabrina Lato, Akihiro Munemasa

Abstract
We construct a new family of distance-biregular graphs related to hyperovals and a new sporadic example of a distance-biregular graph related to Mathon’s perp system. The infinite family can be explained using 2- Y Y -homogeneity, while the sporadic example belongs to a generalization of a construction by Delorme. Additionally, we establish a new non-existence condition for distance-biregular graphs which, for instance, rules out the existence of a distance-biregular graph on 225 + 60 225+60 vertices.



1
Introduction

Distance-biregular graphs are a class of bipartite graphs with strong algebraic and combinatorial properties. Although the theory of distance-biregular graphs has developed over the years, the known examples have remained essentially stable since they were defined. In this paper, we change that by describing new constructions for an infinite family of distance-biregular graphs, as well as a new sporadic example.

A bipartite graph is
distance-biregular
if for all vertices
u
and
v
, the number of vertices adjacent to
v
and closer to
u
than
v
depends only on the distance between
u
and
v
and the cell of the bipartition that
u
lies in. Delorme
[
Del83
]
defined such graphs as having the property “regularité metrique fort.” Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
independently studied the same class of graphs under the name “distance-biregular graphs.” Delorme later wrote a paper in English
[
Del94
]
which is essentially a translation of his earlier French paper, and we will cite the English version for the relevant results that appear in both.

In addition to the foundational papers of Delorme
[
Del94
]
and Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
, early results on distance-biregular graphs can be found in the paper of Mohar and Shawe-Taylor
[
MST85
]
or the theses of Van den Akker
[
A90
]
or Shawe-Taylor
[
ST85
]
. The theory of distance-biregular graphs has also been developed more recently, and the key results that will be relevant for this paper can be found in Fernández and Penjić
[
FP23
]
and Lato
[
Lat23
,
Lat25
]
.

Regular distance-biregular graphs are equivalent to bipartite distance-regular graphs. Dis-tance-regular graphs are a well-studied class of graphs and more information can be found in the monograph of Brouwer, Cohen, and Neumaier
[
BCN89
]
or the more recent survey of Van Dam, Koolen, and Tanaka
[
DKT16
]
. This paper is mostly concerned with graphs that are not distance-regular, so we will generally assume we are dealing with semiregular bipartite graphs with valencies
k\neq\ell.

Although distance-biregular graphs have been considerably less studied compared to their distance-regular counterparts, they do include several notable families that have been studied for their connections to design theory, finite geometry, and algebra.

A motivating family of distance-biregular graphs that are not distance-regular come from generalized polygons. Tits
[
Tit59
]
introduced generalized polygons, bipartite graphs with girth twice the diameter. A
thick
generalized polygon is a generalized polygon with minimum degree at least three, and any thick generalized polygons are distance-biregular
[
Del94
,
GST87
]
. Thick generalized polygons can only exist with diameter
d=2,3,4,6
, or
8

[
FH64
]
. Infinite families exist for each of these possible diameters, and the only known generalized polygons of diameter eight are biregular but not regular
[
VM98
]
.

Distance-regular graphs of small diameter have been the subject of particular attention. Cvetković, Doob, and Sachs
[
CDS80
]
characterized bipartite distance-regular graphs of diameter three as the incidence graphs of symmetric designs. This similarly characterizes distance-biregular graphs of diameter three, and can be extended to distance-biregular graphs with diameter four and vertices of eccentricity three. Delorme
[
Del94
]
and Shawe-Taylor
[
ST85
]
proved that such distance-biregular graphs are equivalent to a particular class of quasi-symmetric designs. These quasi-symmetric designs include Steiner systems and affine resolvable designs, and they were studied further from the perspective of distance-biregular graphs in Chapter 5 of Shawe-Taylor
[
ST85
]
.

Another notable class of distance-regular graphs is the class of distance-regular graphs of diameter two, which are called
strongly regular graphs
. Strongly regular graphs are older than distance-regular graphs, and can be defined more directly. Following the notation of Brouwer and Van Maldeghem
[
BVM22
]
, a
strongly regular graph
with parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
is a graph on
v
vertices with valency
1\leqslant k\leqslant v-2
such that any two adjacent vertices have
\lambda
common neighbours and any two nonadjacent vertices have
\mu
common neighbours. More information can be found in the monograph of Brouwer and Van Maldeghem
[
BVM22
]
.

Bose
[
Bos63
]
introduced the notion of partial geometries to study strongly regular graphs. Although partial geometries were defined geometrically, they can equivalently be thought of as distance-biregular graphs with diameter four and girth six. Infinite families of partial geometries exist, and more information can be found in the surveys of Brouwer and Van Lint
[
BvL84
]
, De Clerck and Van Maldeghem
[
DCVM95
]
, or Thas
[
Tha07
]
.

Bipartite distance-regular graphs, generalized polygons, quasi-symmetric designs, and partial geometries are major classes of distance-biregular graphs that are studied in other contexts. Thus, the smallest “genuinely” distance-biregular graphs which do not belong to these classes are the non-regular distance-biregular graphs with girth four where every vertex has eccentricity four. However, such graphs still belong to a larger category of graphs studied in other contexts– they are examples of what Higman
[
Hig88
]
called “strongly regular designs”, Neumaier
[
Neu82
]
called “
1\frac{1}{2}
” designs, and what Bose, Shrikhande, and Singhi
[
BSS76
]
called “partial geometric designs.” Thus, any construction of a new distance-biregular graph where every vertex has eccentricity four and the girth is four also gives a construction of a new strongly regular/
1\frac{1}{2}
/partial geometric design.

Delorme
[
Del94
]
gave two infinite families of such graphs, and Van den Akker
[
A90
]
provided another sporadic example.

Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
concluded their seminal paper by saying “There is a clear need to determine whether the classes of distance-biregular graphs mentioned in this paper exhaust, in any sense, the possibilities.” In this paper, we address this need by describing the first new constructions of “uniquely” distance-biregular graphs in over 30 years.

Section
2
introduces the basic definitions and notation of distance-biregular graphs. Section
3
collects all the previously known examples of distance-biregular graphs, and Section
4
describes some further properties of distance-biregular graphs that we will need going forward, including a new non-existence condition. In Section
5
, we generalize a construction of Delorme
[
Del94
]
and use this generalization to describe a new distance-biregular graph related to the perp system of Mathon
[
DCDHM02
]
. In Section
6
, we describe a way to derive new distance-biregular graphs as subgraphs of larger distance-biregular graphs, and apply this to obtain a new infinite family of distance-biregular graphs.

2
Distance-Biregular Graphs

Distance-biregular graphs have similar algebraic and combinatorial properties to distance-regular graphs, and it will be convenient to set up common terminology for both.

2.1
Definitions

Let
G
be a graph and let
\pi=\left\{C_{0},C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}\right\}
be a partition of the vertex set of
G
. We say that
\pi
is
equitable
if for all
0\leqslant i,j\leqslant d
, the number of edges from a vertex in
C_{i}
to vertices in
C_{j}
is independent of the choice of the vertex in
C_{i}
.

Let
u\in V\left(G\right)
be a vertex, and let the
eccentricity

e
be the maximum distance from
u
to any other vertex in the graph. For
0\leqslant i\leqslant e
, we let
N_{i}\left(u\right)
be the set of vertices at distance
i
from
u
. Note that
\left\{N_{0}(u),N_{1}(u),\ldots,N_{e}(u)\right\}
defines a partition of the vertex set of
G
, called the
distance partition
of
u
. We say that
u
is
locally distance-regular
if the distance partition is equitable.

If
v\in N_{i}\left(u\right)
, then
v
can only be adjacent to vertices in the cells
N_{i-1}(u),N_{i}(u)
, and
N_{i+1}\left(u\right)
. In particular, a vertex
u
of eccentricity
e
is locally distance-regular if and only if for all
0\leqslant i\leqslant e
and all
v
at distance
i
from
v
, the numbers

c_{i}\left(u\right)=\left|N_{1}(v)\cap N_{i-1}(u)\right|,

a_{i}\left(u\right)=\left|N_{1}(v)\cap N_{i}(u)\right|,

b_{i}\left(u\right)=\left|N_{1}(v)\cap N_{i+1}(u)\right|

are well-defined independently of the choice of
v
. We refer to these numbers as the
intersection numbers
.

A graph is
distance-regular
if the distance partition from every vertex is equitable and the intersection numbers
c_{i}(u),a_{i}(u)
and
b_{i}\left(u\right)
are independent of the choice of
u
. In other words, for every pair of vertices
u,v
at distance
i
, we can define a global parameter

c_{i}:=\left|N_{1}(v)\cap N_{i-1}(u)\right|

that depends only on the distance
i
between
u
and
v
. The numbers
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
can be similarly defined independent of the choice of specific vertices
u
and
v
. Distance-regular graphs are a well-studied class of graphs, and more information can be found in the monograph of Brouwer, Cohen, and Neumaier
[
BCN89
]
or the more recent survey of Van Dam, Koolen, and Tanaka
[
DKT16
]
.

When we write a bipartite graph as
(Y\cup Z,E)
, it is understood that
Y
and
Z
are the cells of the partition.
A bipartite graph is
distance-biregular
if the distance partition from every vertex is equitable and the intersection numbers
c_{i}\left(u\right)
and
b_{i}\left(u\right)
depend only on which cell of the bipartition
u
lies in. If
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
is a bipartite graph, then
a_{i}\left(u\right)=0
for any vertex
u
. If
G
is distance-biregular, we can define global intersection numbers
c_{i}^{Y},c_{i}^{Z},b_{i}^{Y}
, and
b_{i}^{Z}
that only depend on the bipartition.

Locally, distance-regular graphs and distance-biregular graphs behave similarly. However, this local extension of distance-regular graphs to distance-biregular graphs does not extend further, since requiring every vertex to be locally distance-regular is quite restrictive.

Theorem 2.1

(Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph where every vertex is locally distance-regular. Then
G
is either distance-regular or distance-biregular.

2.2
Generalized Polygons

A
generalized polygon
is a bipartite graph with girth twice the diameter. They were introduced by Tits
[
Tit59
]
in studying groups of Lie type and were a particular motivation for Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
because every vertex is locally distance-regular even when the graph is not regular.

Example 2.2
.

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a bipartite
\left(k,\ell\right)
-semiregular graph with diameter
d
and girth
2d
, and let
u\in Y
. Let
v
be a vertex at distance
i
from
u
. If
i<d
, then
v
only has one neighbour at distance
i-1
from
u
. Then for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1
, we have
c_{i}\left(u\right)=1
, and
b_{i}\left(u\right)=k-1
if
i
is even and
b_{i}\left(u\right)=\ell-1
if
i
is odd. Similarly,
c_{d}=k
if
d
is even and
c_{d}=\ell
if
d
is odd, and
b_{0}=k
.

Flipping
k
and
\ell
, a similar argument holds for
w\in Z
, so
G
is distance-biregular.

A
thick
generalized polygon has minimum degree at least three. Yanushka
[
Yan81
]
proved that a generalized polygon that is not thick is the
k
-fold subdivision of a multiple edge or the
k
-fold subdivision of a thick generalized polygon, and further, thick generalized polygons are semiregular. Feit and Higman
[
FH64
]
proved that any thick generalized polygon has diameter
d=2,3,4,6
, or
8
. Infinite families exist for each of these diameters
[
Tha95
,
VM98
]
.

A generalized polygon with vertices of degree two can also be distance-biregular if it is the
subdivision graph
obtained by subdividing every edge of a regular generalized polygon exactly once. This leads to the following characterization of Mohar and Shawe-Taylor
[
MST85
]
of distance-biregular graph with vertices of valency two.

Theorem 2.3

(Mohar and Shawe-Taylor
[
MST85
]
)
.

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a distance-biregular graph where vertices in
Z
have valency two. Then
G
is either
K_{2,k}
or the subdivision graph of a Moore graph or regular generalized polygon.

2.3
Notation and Basic Properties

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a bipartite graph.

Let
G_{2}
be the graph on the vertex set
Y\cup Z
where two vertices are adjacent in
G_{2}
if and only if they are at distance two in
G
. Since
G
is bipartite, we see that
G_{2}
is disconnected. Thus we may let
H_{Y}
be the graph
G_{2}
induced by vertex set
Y
, and similarly for
H_{Z}
. We will refer to
H_{Y}
and
H_{Z}
as the
halved graphs
induced by
Y
and
Z
, respectively. Delorme
[
Del94
]
and Mohar and Shawe-Taylor
[
MST85
]
observed that the halved graphs of a distance-biregular graph are distance-regular.

The adjacency matrix of a bipartite graph
G
has the form

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{0}&N\\
N^{T}&\mathbf{0}\end{pmatrix},

where
N
is the
\left|Y\right|\times\left|Z\right|

biadjacency matrix
.

Now suppose that
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
is distance-biregular, with valencies
k=b_{0}^{Y}
and
\ell=b_{0}^{Z}
.

One consequence of every vertex in the same cell having the same intersection numbers is that the eccentricity of vertices only depends on the cell of the bipartition they lie in. For
X\in\left\{Y,Z\right\}
, we will denote the maximum eccentricity of vertices in
X
by
d_{X}
, and refer to it as the
covering radius
of
X
. We denote the diameter by
d
, and note that at least one of
d_{Y}
or
d_{Z}
is
d
.

Lemma 2.4

(Delorme
[
Del94
]
)
.

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a distance-biregular graph with diameter
d
. If
d_{Y}=d
then
d_{Z}\geqslant d-1
, and if
d
is odd then
d_{Z}=d
.

Lemma 2.5

(Delorme
[
Del94
]
)
.

If
G
is distance-biregular with odd diameter, then
G
is regular.

The class of regular distance-biregular graphs is equivalent to the class of bipartite distance-regular graphs. In this paper, we are primarily interested in distance-biregular graphs which are not regular, so we will assume the diameter of our graphs is even.

For
0\leqslant 2i\leqslant d_{Y}
we have
c_{2i}^{Y}+b_{2i}^{Y}=k
, and for
1\leqslant 2j+1\leqslant d_{Y}
, we have
c_{2i+1}^{Y}+b_{2i+1}^{Y}=\ell
. Following the notation of Delorme
[
Del94
]
, we can compactly express the parameters of a distance-biregular graph in an
intersection array
by

\begin{vmatrix}k;&c_{1}^{Y},&c_{2}^{Y},&\ldots,&c_{d_{Y}-1}^{Y}&c_{d_{Y}}^{Y}\\
\ell;&c_{1}^{Z},&c_{2}^{Z},&\ldots,&c_{d_{Z}-1}^{Z}&c_{d_{Z}}^{Z}\end{vmatrix}\hskip 8.50012pt\text{ or }\hskip 8.50012pt\begin{vmatrix}k;&c_{1}^{Y},&c_{2}^{Y},&\ldots,&c_{d_{Y}-1}^{Y}&c_{d_{Y}}^{Y}\\
\ell;&c_{1}^{Z},&c_{2}^{Z},&\ldots,&c_{d_{Z}}^{Z}&\end{vmatrix}.

Note that
c_{1}^{Y}=1=c_{1}^{Z}.

3
Examples

In this section we describe the known constructions of distance-biregular graphs and relate them to other structures of interest. We may assume without loss of generality that
2\leqslant k<\ell
, so by Lemma
2.5
we can restrict ourselves to the case where the diameter is even.

3.1
d=2

A complete bipartite graph
K_{\ell,k}
is distance-biregular with intersection array

\begin{vmatrix}k;&1,&k\\
\ell;&1,&\ell\end{vmatrix}.

These are clearly the only distance-biregular graphs of diameter two.

3.2
d_{Y}=4,d_{Z}=3

Distance-biregular graphs with
d_{Y}=4
and
d_{Z}=3
can be identified with a certain kinds of combinatorial designs.

Theorem 3.1

(Delorme
[
Del94
]
, Shawe-Taylor
[
ST85
]
)
.

A graph
G
is distance-biregular with covering radii 4, 3 if and only if it is the incidence graph of a quasi-symmetric design where any two blocks are either disjoint, or they intersect in
s
common points.

Neumaier
[
Neu82
]
classified quasi-symmetric designs into four main families, as well as exceptional quasi-symmetric designs. The classes that give rise to distance-biregular graphs are Steiner systems and affine resolvable designs, as well as some of the exceptional quasi-symmetric designs. The known exceptional distance-biregular quasi-symmetric designs are a
\left(21,6,4\right)
quasi-symmetric design coming from the Golay code
[
GS70
]
and a
\left(22,6,5\right)
quasi-symmetric design coming from the Witt design
[
Wit38
]
. More information about quasi-symmetric can be found in the monograph of Shrikhande and Sane
[
SS91
]
, and a treatment of quasi-symmetric designs through the perspective of distance-biregular graphs can be found in the thesis of Shawe-Taylor
[
ST85
]
.

3.3
d_{Y}=4,d_{Z}=4

A partial geometry
pg\left(s,t,\alpha\right)
is a distance-biregular graph with intersection array

\begin{vmatrix}s+1;&1,&1,&\alpha,&s+1\\
t+1;&1,&1,&\alpha,&t+1\end{vmatrix}.

A pg
\left(s,t,s+1\right)
is a Steiner system, and pg
\left(s,t,1\right)
is a generalized quadrangle. A pg
\left(s,t,s\right)
is a
transversal design
of order
s+1
and degree
t+1
, and its existence is equivalent to the existence of
t-1
mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order
s+1
. Partial geometries with
1<\alpha<s,t
are
proper partial geometries
, and there are both sporadic examples and infinite families of proper partial geometries. More information can be found in the surveys of Brouwer and Van Lint
[
BvL84
]
, De Clerck and Van Maldeghem
[
DCVM95
]
, or Thas
[
Tha07
]
.

Quasi-symmetric designs and partial geometries are objects of considerable interest, so the smallest examples of “uniquely” distance-biregular graph are distance-biregular graphs with diameter four and girth four. However, generalizations containing these distance-biregular graphs have been introduced under several names as generalizations of other structures of interest.

Bose, Shrikhande, and Singhi
[
BSS76
]
coined the term
partial geometric design
as the multigraph analogue to partial geometries. Bose, Bridges, and Shrikhande
[
BBS76
]
further explored the spectral properties of partial geometric designs.

Neumaier
[
Neu80
]
introduced
t\frac{1}{2}
-designs, and showed that the only proper examples came from
1\frac{1}{2}
-designs. Although the perspective is different, the definition of partial geometric designs and
1\frac{1}{2}
-designs are equivalent.

Higman
[
Hig88
]
defined a class of coherent configurations as the incidence algebra of what he termed
strongly regular designs
, which he noted contains distance-biregular graphs of diameter four and is contained in the classes of partial geometric or
1\frac{1}{2}
designs.

We now describe the known constructions from Delorme
[
Del94
]
and Van den Akker
[
A90
]
of distance-biregular graphs of diameter four which are not partial geometries. Let

\left[n\right]_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}.

Example 3.2
.

Let
q
be a prime power and consider the six-dimensional affine space over
GF\left(q\right)
.
Consider the (hyperbolic) cone
X_{1}X_{2}-X_{3}X_{4}+X_{5}X_{6}=0
. Let
\mathcal{R}^{*}
denote the set of three-spaces
in this cone. Then
|\mathcal{R}^{*}|=2(q+1)(q^{2}+1)
, see
[
BCN89
, Lemma 9.4.1]
.
Pick some
M_{0}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}
, for instance,
M_{0}=\langle e_{1},e_{3},e_{5}\rangle
.
Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}
denote the set of
M\in\mathcal{R}^{*}
with
\dim(M\cap M_{0})\in\{1,3\}
.
Then
|\mathcal{S}^{*}|=(q+1)(q^{2}+1)
.
Delorme
[
Del94
]
defined a bipartite graph with
Y
the
q^{6}
points of the affine space and
Z
the set of three-dimensional affine subspaces parallel to an element of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
. Adjacency is incidence.
This graph is distance-biregular with intersection array

\begin{vmatrix}\left[4\right]_{q};&1,&q+1,&q^{2},&\left[4\right]_{q}\\
q^{3};&1,&q,&q^{2}+q,&q^{3}\end{vmatrix}.

Note that Delorme’s description in
[
Del94
]
is slightly incorrect as it uses
\mathcal{R}^{*}
instead of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
.
This example was also discussed by Van den Akker
[
A90
]
.

Consider a projective plane
PG\left(2,q\right)
, and let
r
divide
q
. A
maximal arc

\mathcal{A}
of degree
r
is a set of points such that every line meets
\mathcal{A}
at 0 or
r
points. Denniston
[
Den69
]
and Ball, Blokhuis, and Mazzocca
[
BB98
,
BBM97
]
proved that a maximal arc exists if and only if
q
and
r
are both powers of two.

Example 3.3
.

Let
V
be the three-dimensional vector space over
GF\left(q\right)
and let
\mathcal{A}
be a maximal arc in
PG\left(2,q\right)
of degree
r
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
be the dual of the maximal arc, that is, a set of two-dimensional subspaces of
V
such that any one-dimensional subspace of
V
is incident with
0
or
r
elements of
\mathcal{\hat{A}}
.

Let
s=|\hat{\mathcal{A}}|
. We define a bipartite graph where
Y
is the
q^{3}
points of
V
, and
Z
is the set of the
qs
affine cosets of the elements of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
. Adjacency is incidence. Delorme
[
Del94
]
showed this graph is distance-biregular with intersection array

\begin{vmatrix}s;&1,&r,&q\left(s-1\right)/r,&s\\
q^{2};&1,&q,&s-1,&q^{2}\end{vmatrix}.

From the work of Denniston
[
Den69
]
, it follows that such a distance-biregular graph exists for
q,r
both powers of two. In Section 6.2, Van den Akker
[
A90
]
expanded on Example
3.3
and proved that the existence of such a distance-biregular graph with intersection array

\begin{vmatrix}n+2;&1,&2,&n\left(n-1\right)/2,&n+2\\
n^{2};&1,&n,&n+1,&n^{2}\end{vmatrix}

implies the existence of a projective plane of order
n
, which can be used to rule out certain intersection arrays of a similar form to Example
3.3
.

The final known example of distance-biregular graphs with diameter four is a sporadic example due to Van den Akker
[
A90
]
.

Example 3.4
.

The Hall-Janko-Wales graph is a strongly regular graph on 100 vertices. It was constructed by Hall and Wales
[
HW68
]
, and key properties are described in Section 10.32 of Brouwer and Van Maldeghem
[
BVM22
]
. Let
Y
be the vertices of the Hall-Janko-Wales graph and let
Z
be the cliques of size 10. Van den Akker
[
A90
]
proved that the bipartite graph with vertex set
Y\cup Z
and the incidence relation of inclusion is distance-biregular. This graph has intersection array

\begin{vmatrix}28;&1,&4,&6,&8\\
10;&1,&2,&12,&10\end{vmatrix}.

The other halved graph from this construction was described by Bagchi
[
Bag92
]
.

3.4
d\geqslant 6

There are two known families of distance-biregular graphs with unbounded diameter, which have been described by Delorme
[
Del94
]
and Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
.

Example 3.5
.

Let
n\geqslant 2k+2
for some
k\geqslant 1

4
Properties

In this section, we describe some further properties of distance-biregular graphs which will be helpful in constructing new examples.

4.1
Parameter Relations and Feasibility

It is well known that many parameters of a distance-regular graph can be determined from the intersection array, and many of these results extend to distance-biregular graphs. One such example is the following result describing the number of vertices in a graph, which we can frame in terms of locally distance-regular vertices.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u
be a locally distance-regular vertex with eccentricity
e
and let
k_{i}
be the number of vertices at distance
i
from
u
. Then
k_{0}=1
and for
0\leqslant i\leqslant e-1
, we have

k_{i+1}=\frac{b_{i}k_{i}}{c_{i+1}}.

This gives us a
feasibility condition
for the intersection array of a distance-biregular graph, since it tells us that for a distance-biregular graph of diameter
d
to exist, one necessary condition is that for all
0\leqslant j\leqslant d-1
and
X\in\left\{Y,Z\right\}
, the quantity

\prod_{i=0}^{j}\frac{b_{i}^{X}}{c_{i+1}^{X}}

must be an integer.

Another feasibility condition comes because the parameters of a distance-biregular graph are not completely independent. It is possible to determine one line of the intersection array from the other by applying the following result due to Delorme
[
Del94
]
or Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
.

Proposition 4.2

(Delorme
[
Del94
]
, Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
)
.

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a distance-biregular graph of diameter
d
with intersection array

\begin{vmatrix}k;&1,&c_{2}^{Y},&\ldots,&c_{d_{Y}}^{Y}\\
\ell;&1,&c_{2}^{Z},&\ldots,&c_{d_{Z}}^{Z}\end{vmatrix}.

For all
1\leqslant i\leqslant\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
, we have

c_{2i}^{Y}c_{2i+1}^{Y}=c_{2i}^{Z}c_{2i+1}^{Z}

and

b_{2i-1}^{Y}b_{2i}^{Y}=b_{2i-1}^{Z}b_{2i}^{Z}.

Van den Akker
[
A90
]
, Godsil and Shawe-Taylor
[
GST87
]
, Lato
[
Lat23
]
, and Secker
[
Sec89
]
developed sets of feasibility conditions including Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
4.1
to compute tables of feasible parameters. An annotated version of such tables, including the new results from this paper, is given in Section
7
.

4.2
The
2
-
Y
-homogeneous property

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a connected bipartite graph.

Assume that all vertices in
Y
have the same covering radius
d_{Y}\geqslant 2
. Let
u\in Y
, let
v
be at distance two from
u
, and, for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d_{Y}
, let
w
be at distance
i
from both
u
and
v
. We introduce a scalar
\gamma_{i}(u,v,w)
, defined as the count of common neighbours of the vertices
u
and
v
that are positioned at distance
i-1
from the vertex
w
. That is, for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d_{Y}
, we define

\gamma_{i}(u,v,w)=|N_{1}(u)\cap N_{1}(v)\cap N_{i-1}(w)|.

If for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant d_{Y}
, the scalar
\gamma_{i}(u,v,w)
is invariant for all
u\in Y
,
v
at distance two from
u
, and

at distance
i
from

and

\gamma_{i}^{Y}:=\gamma_{i}\left(u,v,w\right)
. If for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant d_{Y}-1
the quantity
\gamma_{i}^{Y}
is well-defined, we say that
G
is
2-
Y
-homogeneous
. If
\gamma_{i}^{Y}
is well-defined for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant d_{Y}-2
, then
G
is
almost 2-
Y
-homogeneous
.

To study almost 2-
Y
-homogeneous distance-biregular graphs, Fernández and Penjić
[
FP23
]
introduced the following scalars.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a distance-biregular graph. For a vertex
u\in Y
and every integer
1\leqslant i\leqslant\min\left\{d_{Y}-1,d_{Z}-1\right\}
, define the scalar
\Delta_{i}\left(Y\right)
by

\Delta_{i}\left(Y\right)=\begin{cases}\left(b_{i-1}^{Y}-1\right)\left(c_{i+1}^{Y}-1\right)-\frac{b_{i}^{Y}\left(c_{i+1}^{Y}-1\right)+c_{i}^{Y}\left(b_{i-1}^{Y}-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}}\left(c_{2}^{Z}-1\right)&i\text{ even}\\
\left(b_{i-1}^{Z}-1\right)\left(c_{i+1}^{Z}-1\right)-\frac{b_{i}^{Z}\left(c_{i+1}^{Z}-1\right)+c_{i}^{Z}\left(b_{i-1}^{Z}-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}}\left(c_{2}^{Z}-1\right)&i\text{ odd}\end{cases}.

Theorem 4.4

(Fernández and Penjić
[
FP23
]
)
.

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a distance-biregular graph with
d_{Y}\geqslant 3
and
\ell\geqslant 3
. For
2\leqslant i\leqslant\min\{d_{Y}-1,\allowbreak d_{Z}-1\}
, the scalar
\Delta_{i}\left(Y\right)=0
if and only if the quantity
\gamma_{i}^{Y}
is well-defined, in which case we have

\gamma_{i}^{Y}=\begin{cases}\frac{c_{2}^{Y}c_{i}^{Y}\left(b_{i-1}^{Y}-1\right)}{b_{i}^{Y}\left(c_{i+1}^{Y}-1\right)+c_{i}^{Y}\left(b_{i-1}^{Y}-1\right)}&i\text{ even}\\
\frac{c_{2}^{Y}c_{i}^{Z}\left(b_{i-1}^{Z}-1\right)}{b_{i}^{Z}\left(c_{i+1}^{Z}-1\right)+c_{i}^{Z}\left(b_{i-1}^{Z}-1\right)}&i\text{ odd}\end{cases}.

This gives us a new feasibility conditions, since if
\Delta_{i}=0
for some
i
, we must have that the above-defined
\gamma_{i}
is a non-negative integer.

Corollary 4.5
.

If

\left(b_{i-1}^{Y}-1\right)\left(c_{i+1}^{Y}-1\right)-\frac{b_{i}^{Y}\left(c_{i+1}^{Y}-1\right)+c_{i}^{Y}\left(b_{i-1}^{Y}-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}}\left(c_{2}^{Z}-1\right)=0,

then

\frac{c_{2}^{Y}c_{i}^{Y}\left(b_{i-1}^{Y}-1\right)}{b_{i}^{Y}\left(c_{i+1}^{Y}-1\right)+c_{i}^{Y}\left(b_{i-1}^{Y}-1\right)}

must be a non-negative integer. Similarly, if

\left(b_{i-1}^{Z}-1\right)\left(c_{i+1}^{Z}-1\right)-\frac{b_{i}^{Z}\left(c_{i+1}^{Z}-1\right)+c_{i}^{Z}\left(b_{i-1}^{Z}-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}}\left(c_{2}^{Z}-1\right)=0,

then

\frac{c_{2}^{Y}c_{i}^{Z}\left(b_{i-1}^{Z}-1\right)}{b_{i}^{Z}\left(c_{i+1}^{Z}-1\right)+c_{i}^{Z}\left(b_{i-1}^{Z}-1\right)}

must be a non-negative integer.

4.3
Spectral Excess Theorem

It is convenient to be able to verify that a graph is distance-biregular without computing the full set of
2d+2
parameters in the intersection array. Lato
[
Lat25
]
proved one such result with a spectral excess theorem for distance-biregular graphs.

Let
G
be a graph with adjacency matrix
A
, and let
S
be a set of vertices. We define an
S
-local inner product of polynomials
f
and
g
by

\langle f,g\rangle_{S}=\frac{1}{\left|S\right|}\sum_{u\in S}\mathbf{e}_{u}^{T}f\left(A\right)g\left(A\right)\mathbf{e}_{u}.

As noted in Section 2 of
[
Lat25
]
, for a bipartite graph with
S
one cell of the bipartition, this inner product is determined by the spectrum and the valencies.

Theorem 4.6

(Spectral Excess Theorem
[
Lat25
]
)
.

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a connected
\left(k,\ell\right)
-semiregular bipartite graph with diameter
d
and
d+1
distinct eigenvalues. Then
G
is distance-biregular if and only if there exist orthogonal sequences of polynomials
p_{0}^{Y},\ldots,p_{d}^{Y}
and
p_{0}^{Z},\ldots,\allowbreak p_{d}^{Z}
such that
p_{d}^{Y}
has degree
d
and for every vertex
v\in Y
, we have

\lVert p_{d}^{Y}\rVert_{Y}^{2}=p_{d}^{Y}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\right)=\left|\left\{u\in V:d\left(v,u\right)=d\right\}\right|,

and
p_{d}^{Z}
has degree
d
and for every vertex
w\in Z
we have

\lVert p_{d}^{Z}\rVert_{Z}^{2}=p_{d}^{Z}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\right)=\left|\left\{u\in V:d\left(w,u\right)=d\right\}\right|.

Applying this result, we can mildly reduce the number of parameters needed to prove that a graph with diameter four is distance-biregular.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
G=\left(Y\cup Z,E\right)
be a bipartite
\left(k,\ell\right)
-semiregular graph such that every vertex in
Y
is locally distance-regular with parameters

\begin{vmatrix}k;&1,&c_{2}^{Y},&c_{3}^{Y},&k\end{vmatrix}.

If
k>\frac{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}{c_{2}^{Z}}
and for every
u\in Z
and
v
at distance two from
u
the number

c_{2}^{Z}=|N_{1}(u)\cap N_{1}(v)|

is independent of the choice of the vertices
u
and
v
, then
G
is distance-biregular with intersection array

\begin{vmatrix}k;&1,&c_{2}^{Y},&c_{3}^{Y},&k\\
\ell;&1,&c_{2}^{Z},&\frac{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}{c_{2}^{Z}},&\ell\end{vmatrix}.

(4.1)

Proof.

Since every vertex in
Y
is locally distance-regular, from Section 2 in Lato
[
Lat25
]
, we see that the desired polynomial
p_{4}^{Y}
exists and that vertices in
Y
have eigenvalue support of size exactly five, that is, the characteristic vector
of a vertex is a linear combination of exactly five eigenvectors of the
adjacency matrix of
Y
.

Since the eigenvalues of
G
are symmetric, this implies that every vertex in
Y
must have zero in its eigenvalue support. It is a well-known result in linear algebra that for any matrix
N,
the matrices
NN^{T}
and
N^{T}N
share the same nonzero eigenvalues with multiplicity. In particular, since

A^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}NN^{T}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&N^{T}N\end{pmatrix},

this implies that
G
can only have five distinct eigenvalues.

Now let
G^{\prime}=\left(Y^{\prime}\cup Z^{\prime},E^{\prime}\right)
be a putative distance-biregular graph with the parameters in Equation (
4.1
). For both
G
and
G^{\prime}
, every vertex in
Y
is locally distance-regular with the same parameters, so from Lemma
4.1
we have

\left|Y\right|=1+\frac{k\left(\ell-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}}+\frac{\left(\ell-1\right)\left(k-c_{2}^{Y}\right)\left(\ell-c_{3}^{Y}\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}=\left|Y^{\prime}\right|

and

\left|Z\right|=k+\frac{k\left(\ell-1\right)\left(k-c_{2}^{Y}\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}=\left|Z^{\prime}\right|.

We define a sequence of polynomials recursively by

\displaystyle p_{0}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle p_{1}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=x

\displaystyle p_{2}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{2}^{Z}}\left(xp_{1}\left(x\right)-\ell\right)

\displaystyle p_{3}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}^{Z}}{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}\left(xp_{2}\left(x\right)-\left(k-1\right)p_{1}\left(x\right)\right)

\displaystyle p_{4}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\left(xp_{3}\left(x\right)-\left(\ell-c_{2}^{Z}\right)p_{2}\left(x\right)\right).

By construction, these form a sequence of polynomials orthogonal with respect to
\langle,\rangle_{Z^{\prime}}
, and further

\langle p_{4},p_{4}\rangle_{Z^{\prime}}=p_{4}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\right).

We claim that
p_{4}
is our desired
p_{4}^{Z}
.

As discussed in Section 2.7 of Lato
[
Lat23
]
, for a bipartite graph, the spectrum can be determined by the spectrum relative to one cell of the bipartition and the sizes of the sets. Further, knowing that every vertex in
Y
is locally distance-regular gives us the spectrum relative to
Y
. Thus
G
and
G^{\prime}
are cospectral, and in particular, since they have the same valencies, the inner products
\langle,\rangle_{Z^{\prime}}
and
\langle,\rangle_{Z}
are equivalent. Thus
p_{4}
does indeed belong to the desired sequence of polynomials orthogonal with respect to
\langle,\rangle_{Z}
, and further
\lVert p_{4}\rVert^{2}_{Z}=p_{4}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\right)
.

We compute that

p_{2}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\right)=\frac{\ell\left(k-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Z}}

and

p_{3}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\right)=\frac{c_{2}^{Z}}{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\frac{\ell\left(k-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Z}}-\left(k-1\right)\sqrt{k\ell}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{k\ell}\left(k-1\right)\left(\ell-c_{2}^{Y}\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}},

so

\displaystyle p_{4}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\left(\frac{k\ell\left(k-1\right)\left(\ell-c_{2}^{Z}\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}-\frac{\left(\ell-c_{2}^{Z}\right)\ell\left(k-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Z}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(k-1\right)\left(\ell-c_{2}^{Z}\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}\left(k-\frac{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Z}}{c_{2}^{Z}}\right).

Now by Lemma
4.1
we have

\left|Z\right|=\left|Z^{\prime}\right|=1+\frac{\ell\left(k-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Z}}+\frac{\left(k-1\right)\left(\ell-c_{2}^{Z}\right)}{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}\left(k-\frac{c_{2}^{Y}c_{3}^{Y}}{c_{2}^{Z}}\right).

Note that for
G
, if we fix a vertex
u\in Z
, then there are
\frac{\ell(k-1)}{c_{2}^{Z}}
vertices at distance two from
u
, so the number of vertices at distance four from
u
is

\left|Z\right|-1-\frac{\ell\left(k-1\right)}{c_{2}^{Z}}=p_{4}\left(\sqrt{k\ell}\right),

and by Theorem
4.6
we conclude that
G
is distance-biregular. By Proposition
4.2
it must have the intersection array in Equation (
4.1
).∎

5.1
The Generalization

A generalization of Delorme’s construction is as follows.

Construction 5.1
.

Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let

and

be positive integers such that
k\leqslant n/2
,

, and

.
Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}
be a family of

with the following properties:

for all

we have
that

, where both cases occur, and

for all distinct

\dim(M\cap M^{*})=n-2k
.

We associate Construction
5.1
with its parameter array
(n,k,q,d,s)
.
We use this to construct a distance-biregular graph generalizing Example

.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

, and

be as in Construction
5.1

\begin{vmatrix}s;&1,&d,&q^{n-2k}(s-1)/d,&s\\

Proof.

Every block contains
q^{n-k}
points and every point is contained in
s

G
is
\left(q^{n-k},s\right)
-semiregular.

Let

be an arbitrary block. Observe that there exists

and

such that

. If

, then we have

\dim(M+M^{\prime})=(n-k)+(n-k)-(n-2k)=n,

which implies

. Thus, if

is at distance two from

, hence the blocks at distance two from

Z\setminus(V/M)=\bigcup_{M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}\setminus\{M\}}V/M^{\prime}.

(5.1)

In particular, if

is at distance two from

By Theorem

5.3
Restrictions on Parameters

We show that

is not an independent parameter.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
V,k,n,\mathcal{S},s
, and

be as in Construction
5.8
.
We have

\displaystyle s=\frac{(d-1)(q^{n-k}-1)}{q^{n-2k}-1}+1=\frac{d(q^{n-k}-1)-q^{n-2k}(q^{k}-1)}{q^{n-2k}-1}.

(5.2)

Proof.

Counting the pairs
\left(U,M\right)
where
U
is a one-dimensional subspace of

and
M
is an element of
\mathcal{S}
that contains
U
, we see

\left|Y\right|d=s\left[n-k\right]_{q}.

(5.3)

If we count triples
\left(U,M_{1},M_{2}\right)
where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are distinct elements in
\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
U
is a one-dimensional subspace of

in
M_{1}\cap M_{2}
, we have

\left|Y\right|d\left(d-1\right)=s\left(s-1\right)\left[n-2k\right]_{q}.

(5.4)

Combining Equation (
5.3
) with Equation (
5.4
), we see

\left[n-k\right]_{q}\left(d-1\right)=\left(s-1\right)\left[n-2k\right]_{q}.

This concludes the proof. ∎

We can also show that
d
is necessarily a power of
p
.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
V,k,n,\mathcal{S},s
, and
d
be as in Construction
5.8
.
Write
q=p^{t}
,
p
prime.
Then
d=q^{n-2k}\cdot p^{-i}
for some nonnegative integer
i
.

Proof.

Since rational algebraic integers are integers,
-\frac{s}{d}
and
-\frac{s}{d}+\frac{q^{n-k}}{d}
are integers.
Hence,
d
is a power of
p
.
So
d=q^{n-2k}p^{-i}
for some integer
i
.
It remains to show that
i
is nonnegative.

Recall that
s=1+(d-1)\frac{q^{n-k}-1}{q^{n-2k}-1}
.
All elements of
\mathcal{S}
are pairwise disjoint.
Each element of
\mathcal{S}
contains
q^{k}-1
nonzero vectors,
while there are
q^{n}-1
nonzero vectors in total.
Thus,

(q^{k}-1)\left(1+(d-1)\frac{q^{n-k}-1}{q^{n-2k}-1}\right)\leqslant q^{n}-1.

Rearranging for
d-1
yields

d-1\leqslant\frac{(q^{n}-1)(q^{n-2k}-1)}{(q^{k}-1)(q^{n-k}-1)}-\frac{q^{n-2k}-1}{q^{n-k}-1}<\frac{(q^{n}-1)(q^{n-2k}-1)}{(q^{k}-1)(q^{n-k}-1)}.

Suppose that
d\geqslant q^{n-2k}p
. Then

p<\frac{(1-q^{-n})(1-q^{-n+2k})}{(1-q^{-k})(1-q^{-n+k})}+q^{-n+2k}.

For
p\geqslant 3
, using
n\geqslant 2k
, it is easily verified that the right-hand side is less than
3
, a contradiction.
For
p=2
, we distinguish several cases.
For
n=2k
, the inequality becomes
2<0+1
, a contradiction.
For
n=2k+1
,
1-q^{-n+2k}<1-q^{-k}
and
1-q^{-n+k}\geqslant\frac{3}{4}
, so the inequality becomes

2<\frac{4}{3}(1-q^{-n})+\frac{1}{2}<2,

a contradiction.
For
n\geqslant 2k+2
and either
k\geqslant 2
or
q\geqslant 4
,

1-q^{-k}\geqslant\frac{3}{4}
and
1-q^{-n+k}\geqslant\frac{7}{8}
, so the inequality becomes

2<\frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{8}{7}(1-q^{-n})(1-q^{-n+k})+\frac{1}{4}<\frac{4\cdot 8}{3\cdot 7}+\frac{1}{4}<2,

a contradiction.
It remains the case that
q=2
and
k=1
. Then the inequality reads

2<\frac{2(2^{n}-3)}{2^{n}-2},

a contradiction. ∎

Let

q q -Laplacian State Transfer on Graphs with Involutions
Swornalata Ojha, Hiranmoy Pal

Abstract
We study the existence of state transfer with respect to the q q -Laplacian matrix of a graph equipped with a non-trivial involution. We show that the occurrence of perfect state transfer between certain pair (or plus) states in such a graph is equivalent to the existence of vertex state transfer in a subgraph induced by the involution with potentials. This yields infinite families of trees with potentials and unicyclic graphs of maximum degree three that exhibit perfect pair state transfer. In particular, we investigate vertex and pair state transfer in edge-perturbed complete bipartite graphs, cycles, and paths with potentials only at the end vertices. Keywords: Graph, Involution, Adjacency matrix, q q -Laplacian matrix, Continuous-time quantum walk, Perfect state transfer. MSC: 15A16, 05C50, 05C76, 81P45.

Continuous-time quantum walks
[

associated to

is defined by

A_{ij}=\begin{cases}w(i,j),&\text{whenever }(i,j)\in E,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The

\Delta
is a diagonal matrix with
\Delta_{ii}
equal to the sum of the entries in the
i
th row of the adjacency matrix. The
Laplacian matrix

L
and

associated to
G
are defined by
L=\Delta-A
and
Q=\Delta+A
, respectively. The
q
-Laplacian matrix

[

with a non-zero real parameter

\mathscr{L}=qL-(q-1)I+q(q-1)(\Delta-I).

(1)

The

-Laplacian matrix reduces to the Laplacian matrix when

, and to the signless Laplacian matrix when

with respect to the

-Laplacian matrix

is described by the transition matrix

U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\exp{\left(it\mathscr{L}\right)},\quad\text{where }t\in\mathbb{R}.

A quantum state is represented by a positive semidefinite matrix of trace one, commonly referred to as a
density matrix
. A density matrix is called a

-Laplacian matrix

D_{2}=U_{\mathscr{L}}(\tau)D_{1}U_{\mathscr{L}}(-\tau).

In particular, PST between the real pure states

is equivalent to the existence of a complex scalar
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
such that

U_{\mathscr{L}}(\tau){\bf x}=\gamma{\bf y}.

In this case, we simply say that PST occurs between the vector states
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
. A vector state
{\bf x}

to itself. For a vertex

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2

We present a few preliminary definitions and results before proceeding to the next section. Let
G
be an undirected weighted graph on
n

\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\cdots<\theta_{d}

-Laplacian matrix
\mathscr{L}
associated with

denote the corresponding orthogonal projection. The spectral decomposition of the transition matrix

is given by

U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)=\exp{\left(it\mathscr{L}\right)}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}e^{it\theta_{j}}F_{j}.

The matrix

is both symmetric and unitary, and it can be expressed as a polynomial in
\mathscr{L}
. The
eigenvalue support
of a state
{\bf x}
relative to
\mathscr{L}
, denoted by
\Lambda_{\bf x}(\mathscr{L})
, is defined by

\Lambda_{\bf x}(\mathscr{L})=\left\{\theta_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F_{j}{\bf x}\neq 0\right\}.
A state
{\bf x}
is called a

relative to
\mathscr{L}
whenever

. It can be observed that a fixed state does not admit PST. Two linearly independent states
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are said to be

if for each
\theta_{j}\in\Lambda_{\bf x}(\mathscr{L})

or

[

, Lemma 5.1(1)]

{\bf x}
and

A symmetric matrix
B
is said to be

if
x^{T}Bx\geq 0
for all nonzero vectors

. One can observe that both Laplacian and signless Laplacian matrices associated with a graph are positive semidefinite. It is shown in

that the

Theorem 1
.

[

Let

\alpha_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{n}

\gamma_{1}\geq\alpha_{1}\geq\gamma_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{n-1}\geq\gamma_{n}\geq\alpha_{n}.

State transfer on graphs with involutions

An involution of a graph is an automorphism of order two. Consider a graph
G
equipped with a non-trivial involution
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V(G)\to V(G)
where
\phi^{2}(v)=v
for every vertex
v.
Suppose the vertices in
G
are assigned a potential
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V(G)\to\mathbb{R}
that is symmetric under the involution
\phi
, that is,
\eta(v)=\eta(\phi(v))
for all
v\in V(G).
Let
S=\left\{v\in V(G)~|~\phi(v)=v\right\},
the set of all fixed vertices of
\phi.
A half graph
G^{\prime}
induced by the involution
\phi
is obtained by selecting exactly one vertex from each orbit
(v,\phi(v)),
for all
v\in V(G)\backslash S.
The graph
G^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph of
G
on the chosen vertices with a potential inherited by restricting
\eta
to the vertices of
G^{\prime}
. One can observe that
G
contains at least two copies of
G^{\prime}
. The total number of vertices of
G
equals
2n+|S|,
where
|S|
is the number of fixed vertices of
\phi,
and
n
is the number of vertices in
G^{\prime}.
Let
\Delta
be the degree matrix of
G
without potential. The degree matrix of
G
with potential is considered as
\Delta+\Delta^{\prime},
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is a diagonal matrix with
\Delta_{vv}^{\prime}=\eta(v).
With a suitable labeling of the vertices of
G,
the
q
-Laplacian matrix in (
1
) can be written in block form as

\mathscr{L}=\left(1-q^{2}\right)I+q^{2}(\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})-qA=\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{L}^{\prime}&A_{\phi}&A_{S}\\
A_{\phi}&\mathscr{L}^{\prime}&A_{S}\\
A_{S}^{T}&A_{S}^{T}&\mathscr{L}_{S}\end{bmatrix},

where
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{L}_{S}
are the
q
-Laplacian matrices of the half graph
G^{\prime}
and the subgraph induced by
S,
respectively, including the original degrees of vertices as in

The submatrix
A_{\phi}
is a symmetric matrix corresponding to the edges across the involution, and the submatrix
A_{S}
describes the edges between

and the vertices of the half graph. For example, the

A spectral characterization relative to the adjacency matrix of a graph with involution is given in
[
25
, Lemma 2]
. We present an analogous observation for the
q
-Laplacian matrix of a graph with involution.

Lemma 1
.

Let
G
be a graph with a non-trivial involution
\phi.
Then the characteristic polynomial of the
q
-Laplacian matrix
\mathscr{L}
of
G
factors into
P_{+}(x)
and
P_{-}(x)
which are, respectively, the characteristic polynomials of

\mathscr{L}_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{L}^{\prime}+A_{\phi}&A_{S}\\
2A_{S}^{T}&\mathscr{L}_{S}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{L}_{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathscr{L}^{\prime}-A_{\phi}.

Furthermore, the eigenvectors of
\mathscr{L}
take the block form
[a~~a~~b]^{T}
and
[c~~-c~~{\bf 0}]^{T},
where
[a~~b]^{T}
is an eigenvector of
\mathscr{L}_{+}
, and
c
an eigenvector of
\mathscr{L}_{-}.

Proof.

The matrix
\mathscr{L}_{+}
is diagonalizable as
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{+}}=K^{-1}\mathscr{L}_{+}K,
where

\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{+}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{L}^{\prime}+A_{\phi}&\sqrt{2}A_{S}\\
\sqrt{2}A_{S}^{T}&\mathscr{L}_{S}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad K=\begin{bmatrix}I&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&\sqrt{2}I\end{bmatrix}.

Suppose
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\mathscr{L}_{+}
with eigenvector
[a~~b]^{T}
. Then
(\mathscr{L}^{\prime}+A_{\phi})a+A_{S}b=\lambda a
and
2A_{S}^{T}a+\mathscr{L}_{S}b=\lambda b.
Hence

\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{L}^{\prime}&A_{\phi}&A_{S}\\
A_{\phi}&\mathscr{L}^{\prime}&A_{S}\\
A_{S}^{T}&A_{S}^{T}&\mathscr{L}_{S}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a\\
a\\
b\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}(\mathscr{L}^{\prime}+A_{\phi})a+A_{S}b\\
(\mathscr{L}^{\prime}+A_{\phi})a+A_{S}b\\
2A_{S}^{T}a+\mathscr{L}_{S}b\end{bmatrix}=\lambda\begin{bmatrix}a\\
a\\
b\end{bmatrix}.

It follows that
\lambda
is also an eigenvalue of
\mathscr{L}
with the same multiplicity as in
\mathscr{L}_{+}
. Consequently, if
P(x)
is the characteristic polynomial of
\mathscr{L},
then
P_{+}(x)
divides
P(x).
Similarly, suppose that
\mu
is an eigenvalue of
\mathscr{L}_{-}
with eigenvector
c,
then
[c~~-c~~{\bf 0}]^{T}
is an eigenvector of
\mathscr{L}
associated with the eigenvalue
\mu,
with the same multiplicity as in
\mathscr{L}_{-}
. Accordingly, the polynomial
P_{-}(x)
divides
P(x).
Since both
P_{+}(x)
and
P_{-}(x)
are monic polynomials, and their degrees add up to the degree of the polynomial
P(x),
it follows that
P(x)=P_{+}(x)P_{-}(x).

∎

We now derive a relationship between the transition matrices
U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)
,
U_{\mathscr{L}_{-}}(t)
, and
U_{\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{+}}}(t)
, thereby establishing a connection between PST in a subgraph to PST in the original graph in the presence of a non-trivial involution. The following result is analogous to the one stated in

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a graph equipped with a non-trivial involution
\phi
, and
G^{\prime}
be a half graph induced by
\phi
. If
U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)
is the transition matrix associated with the
q
-Laplacian matrix of

then for
u\in V(G^{\prime})
,

U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right)=(I-P)\sum_{\mu_{r}\in\sigma(\mathscr{L}_{-})}e^{it\mu_{r}}F_{r}{\mathbf{e}}_{u},

(3)

where P is the matrix of the involution
\phi
, and if
F_{r}^{\prime}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{L}_{-}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu_{r},
then the matrix
F_{r}
is given by

F_{r}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}F_{r}^{\prime}&-F_{r}^{\prime}&{\bf 0}\\
-F_{r}^{\prime}&F_{r}^{\prime}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

The spectral decomposition of the transition matrix
U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)
gives

U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right)=\sum_{\mu_{r}\in\sigma(\mathscr{L})}e^{it\mu_{r}}E_{r}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right),

(4)

where
\sigma(\mathscr{L})
denotes the
q
-Laplacian spectrum, and
E_{r}
denotes the orthogonal projection associated with eigenvalue
\mu_{r}
. It follows from Lemma
1
that the only eigenvectors of
\mathscr{L}
that contribute to the sum of (
4
) are of the form
[c~~-c~~{\bf 0}]^{T},
where
c
is an eigenvector of
\mathscr{L}_{-}.
Since
E_{r}
is a polynomial in
\mathscr{L},

E_{r}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right)=E_{r}(I-P){\mathbf{e}}_{u}=(I-P)E_{r}{\mathbf{e}}_{u}=(I-P)F_{r}{\mathbf{e}}_{u}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We next establish that the transition matrix
U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)
is similar to a block diagonal matrix where the transition matrices corresponding to
\mathscr{L}_{-}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{+}}
appear as the diagonal blocks.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a graph equipped with a non-trivial involution
\phi
, and
G^{\prime}
be a half graph induced by
\phi
. Suppose
M
is the matrix whose columns are the vectors in
\mathcal{B}=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right)~|~u\in V(G^{\prime})\right\},

\mathcal{C}=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right)~|~u\in V(G^{\prime})\right\},
and
\mathcal{D}=\left\{{\mathbf{e}}_{u}~|~u\in S\right\},
respectively. If
\mathscr{L}
is the

-Laplacian matrix associated with
G
, then for every

M^{T}U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)M=\begin{bmatrix}U_{\mathscr{L}_{-}}(t)&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&U_{\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{+}}}(t)\end{bmatrix},

where
\mathscr{L}^{\prime},~\mathscr{L}_{-},~\mathscr{L}_{S},~A_{\phi},~A_{S}
are as defined previously, and

\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{+}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{L}^{\prime}+A_{\phi}&\sqrt{2}A_{S}\\
\sqrt{2}A_{S}^{T}&\mathscr{L}_{S}\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{e}}_{u}
and
\check{{\mathbf{e}}}_{u}
be the characteristic vectors of a vertex
u
in
G
and
G^{\prime},
respectively. Since
U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)
is a polynomial in
\mathscr{L},
the permutation matrix
P
associated with the involution
\phi
commutes with
U_{\mathscr{L}}(t).
Since
\left({\mathbf{e}}_{v}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(v)}\right)=\left(I-P\right){\mathbf{e}}_{v}
, the following holds for every

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{v}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(v)}\right)^{T}U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}_{v}^{T}U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right).

It follows from Lemma
2
that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}_{v}^{T}U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{u}-{\mathbf{e}}_{\phi(u)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}_{v}^{T}(I-P)\sum_{\mu_{r}\in\sigma(\mathscr{L}_{-})}e^{it\mu_{r}}~F_{r}{\mathbf{e}}_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu_{r}\in\sigma(\mathscr{L}_{-})}e^{it\mu_{r}}~\begin{bmatrix}\check{{\mathbf{e}}}_{v}^{T}&-\check{{\mathbf{e}}}_{v}^{T}&{\bf 0}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}F_{r}^{\prime}\\
-F_{r}^{\prime}\\
{\bf 0}\end{bmatrix}\check{{\mathbf{e}}}_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\check{{\mathbf{e}}}_{v}^{T}U_{\mathscr{L}_{-}}(t)\check{{\mathbf{e}}}_{u}.

Since
W=\text{span}(\mathcal{B})
is an invariant subspace of
U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)
, the matrix representation of the restriction operator
U_{\mathscr{L}}(t)|_{W}
relative to the basis
\mathcal{B}
is the transition matrix
U_{\mathscr{L}_{-}}(t)
. Let

be the matrix whose columns are the vectors in
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, respectively. Then

N=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I_{|V(G^{\prime})|}&\mathbf{0}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I_{|V(G^{\prime})|}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&I_{|S|}\end{bmatrix}.

It follows that
\mathscr{L}N=N\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{+}
, and therefore

Since the columns of

span the orthogonal complement of

Lemma 4
.

Let
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

be a cycle on

1.

\frac{b}{2}+\frac{n}{4}
and
\frac{b}{2}+\frac{3n}{4}
must be integers, and PST occurs only between these two vertices.

2.

In the signless Laplacian case, both
\frac{b}{2}
and
\frac{n+b}{2}
must be integers whenever
b\neq\frac{n}{2}
; and both
\frac{3n}{8}
and
\frac{7n}{8}
must be integers whenever
b=\frac{n}{2}.
In either case, PST occurs only between these two vertices.

Proof.

Since
0\leq\frac{(2k-b)\pi}{n}<2\pi
, it follows from (
5
) and (
6
) that
{\mathbf{e}}_{k}^{T}{\bf z}_{1}=0
under the following conditions: when
\zeta=1
and
b\neq\frac{n}{2},
this holds if
k=\frac{b}{2}
or
k=\frac{n+b}{2}
; and when
\zeta=1
and
b=\frac{n}{2},
this holds if
k=\frac{3n}{8}
or
k=\frac{7n}{8}.
In the case when
\zeta=-1
, the term
{\mathbf{e}}_{k}^{T}{\bf z}_{1}=0
if

or
k=\frac{b}{2}+\frac{3n}{4}
. If
{\mathbf{e}}_{k}^{T}{\bf z}_{1}\neq 0,
then the eigenvalue
\theta_{1}=2+2\zeta\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
is in the support of the vertex

Note that

is an algebraic integer of degree

where

denote the Euler totient function. Therefore, the eigenvalue
\theta_{1}
is not an integer whenever

By
[
29
, Theorem 4]
, if the graph
C_{n}+\rho~\{0,b\}
exhibits vertex PST, then the following conditions must hold: in the signless Laplacian case (i.e.,
\zeta=1
), when

and
\frac{n+b}{2}
must be integers and PST occurs only between them; and when

both
\frac{3n}{8}
and
\frac{7n}{8}
must be integers and PST occurs only between them. In the Laplacian case (i.e.,
\zeta=-1
), both
\frac{b}{2}+\frac{n}{4}
and
\frac{b}{2}+\frac{3n}{4}
must be integers and PST occurs between these two vertices.
∎

The next result establishes the non-existence of vertex PST in edge-perturbed cycles, except for a few cases, with respect to both Laplacian and signless Laplacian matrices.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

Consider the Laplacian case. If

5
) and (
6
), the eigenvalues
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
lie in the eigenvalue support of both vertices
\frac{b}{2}+\frac{n}{4}
and
\frac{b}{2}+\frac{3n}{4}
. By
[
29

, both
\frac{b}{2}+\frac{n}{4}
and
\frac{b}{2}+\frac{3n}{4}
are not periodic as

|\theta_{3}-\theta_{2}|=4\left|\sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{n}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)\right|<1,\quad\text{whenever }n\geq 15.

In the case of signless Laplacian, if
C_{n}+\rho~\{0,b\}
has an odd number of vertices, then the claim follows directly from Lemma
4
. When
n\equiv 0\pmod{8}
and
b=\frac{n}{2},
we deduce from (
5
) and (
6
) that both eigenvalues
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
lie in the eigenvalue support of the vertices
\frac{3n}{8}
and
\frac{7n}{8}
. Hence, there is no vertex PST in this case as well. When both

and

\phi

to

and

For every choice of

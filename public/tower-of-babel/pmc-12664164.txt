Exercise as a modulator of systemic inflammation and oxidative stress biomarkers across clinical and healthy populations: an umbrella meta-analysis
Authors: Wei Feng, Yanzhong Wang, Xin Gu, Duonan Yu, Zhiwei Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveInflammation is a condition that trigger an immune response and oxidative stress refers to an imbalance between production and elimination of reactive oxygen species (ROS). However, several non-pharmacological approaches have been proposed to improve health outcomes, among which exercise programs have shown promising effects on inflammatory and oxidative biomarkers across various health conditions.AimIn this regard, the present umbrella meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the potential of exercise interventions to modulate inflammatory and oxidative stress biomarkers across different population.MethodsA comprehensive systematic search was carried out in on Scopus, PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Google Scholar up to March 2025. The overall effect size (ESs) was calculated using random effect model. Also, subgroup analyses were performed regarding ESs, health status, ad type of exercise program.ResultsOur comprehensive systematic search initially identified 1246 studies, of which twenty-nine meta-analyses, were included in the present umbrella meta-analysis. Finding revealed that training program could significantly reduce C-reactive protein (CRP) (ES: -0.50; 95% CI: -0.65, -0.36, P < 0.001), interleukin-6 (IL-6) (E: -0.29; 95% CI: -0.41, -0.18; P < 0.001), IL-8 (ES: -1.20; 95% CI: -2.19, -0.21, P = 0.018), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) (ES: -0.47, 95% CI: -0.60, -0.34; P < 0.001). In addition, it was accompanied with an improvement in malondialdehyde (MDA) (ES: -1.26; 95% CI: -1.60, -0.93, P < 0.001) and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) (ES: 0.88, 95% CI: 0.25, 1.51; P = 0.006) level too. Subgroup analyses indicated that all aerobic, resistance and combined exercise could improve inflammatory state significantly. Also, exercise favorable properties were evident in most health state to affect CRP, IL-6, and TNF-α level.ConclusionExercise program could attenuate the CRP, IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α level. Additionally, exercise demonstrated a beneficial impact on oxidative status, as evidenced by decreased MDA levels and increased GPx activity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13102-025-01327-8.

Introduction

Inflammation and oxidative stress are closely interconnected biological responses in various undesired health consequences particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), cardiovascular diseases, cancer, and neurological diseases [1, 2]. Inflammation is characterized by the excessive production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, whereas oxidative stress is defined by an imbalance between the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and the body’s antioxidant defense mechanisms [2]. Although they are considered as defensive responses of host, can worsen the health condition [3, 4]. High production of pro-inflammatory cytokine may lead to both inflammation and oxidative stress state which can lead to cellular damage, tissue dysfunction in different stages and degrees [3, 4]. In the recent years, growing attention has been attracted to lifestyle medicine and non-pharmacological approaches to confront with inflammation and oxidative stress. It is better to identify the therapeutic strategies to mitigate these adverse effects. In this regard, physical activity has emerged as a beneficial and modifiable intervention associated with favorable outcomes [5].

Several studies have claimed that exercise can modulate inflammation and oxidative stress in different manner [6, 7]. Specifically, this phenomenon is evident in patients with overweight or obese, T2DM and individuals at the risk of cardiovascular diseases [8, 9]. There is large body of evidence which supports the beneficial role of exercise training on cardiovascular health and risk of mortality [10]. This effect is implemented through improvement in lipid profile and blood pressure [10–12]. In addition, it has been proposed that physical activity intervention may reduce adiposity, which contribute to insulin sensitivity [6, 13].

Also, it has been shown that 4 weeks of aerobic training can lead to significant reduction in the glycemic markers (fasting glucose, HbA1c) as well as high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs CRP) level [14]. Similarly, patients with T2DM have shown improvements in Homeostatic Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR), accompanied by reductions in plasma concentrations of C-reactive protein (CRP) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) following exercise program [15]. Salles et al. reported that long-term resistance exercise can significantly reduce CRP levels, although it does not appear to affect IL-6 concentrations in obese or elderly individuals [16]. Similarly, resistance training [17] has been shown to significantly modulate tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) levels, whereas aerobic exercise did not demonstrate a comparable effect [18]. Some studies emphasize that the effects of exercise on inflammatory markers are exercise type-dependent; combined aerobic and resistance training has been shown to produce greater reductions in hs CRP, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α levels compared to aerobic training alone [19].

Furthermore, exercise intervention caused significant reduction on malondialdehyde (MDA) level in the most of the studies [20, 21]. Similarly, favorable effects of aerobic and resistance exercise on superoxide dismutase (SOD) level was shown in healthy individuals as well as in patients with chronic kidney diseases [20]. In contrast, Wang et al. demonstrated non-significant effect in term of MDA and SOD level for the resistance and combined exercise program, respectively, in healthy subjects, [22].

Several studies have explored the anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidative effects of exercise program. However, the conflicting evidence calls into question the possible efficacy of exercise in ameliorating inflammation and oxidative state [14, 23]. Nevertheless, there is no comprehensive study to evaluate this association effectively. Therefore, we have now undertaken an umbrella meta-analysis to assess the impact of exercise program on a broad spectrum of inflammatory and oxidative biomarkers.

We hypothesize that exercise interventions may reduce inflammatory and oxidative stress biomarkers, which contribute to improved health panel across various populations.

Methods

The current eta-analysis was completed according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guideline [24]. In addition, the study’s protocol has been registered on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO: CRD420251016833).

Search strategy

A comprehensive systematic search pf electronic database including PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library and the Google scholar search engine was done to identify the relevant studies published up to March 2025. Google scholar was included to identify the additional studies which were not indexed in abovementioned databases. The language was restricted to English. In addition, the reference list of relevant studies and review articles were manually screened to avoid missing any additional study. The medical subject heading (MESH) terms and keywords and search strategy is provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The studies we retrieved were included in our umbrella meta-analysis if they fulfilled the following evidence-based PICOS criteria: (1) Utilized a systematic review and meta-analysis design (2) Study on the impact of exercise program on inflammatory biomarkers including CRP, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α and oxidative stress biomarkers including: MDA, SOD, total oxidant status (TAC), glutathione peroxidase (GPx), and nitric oxide (NO), lipid peroxidation (LPO), catalyze (CAT), total antioxidant status (TAS) (3) Conducted on individuals aged 18 years or above, regardless of sex (4) Evaluating the data on inflammatory and oxidative stress biomarkers before and after the intervention within the exercise and placebo groups. Conversely, the review excluded studies of an in vitro, in vivo, or ex vivo nature, as well as case reports, observational studies, quasi-experimental studies, and controlled clinical trials.

Data extraction

Two researchers completed data extraction independently. The basic characteristics of studies were extracted including: the name of first author, the publication year, location of study, design, mean age, health status, sample size, type of exercise program (aerobic exercise, resistance exercise), and the effect sizes (ESs) and confidence interval (CI) of inflammatory and oxidative biomarkers. All disagreements were resolved by reaching a consensus with the third investigator.

Quality assessment and meta-evidence

The methodological quality of the included articles was evaluated using the A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews (AMSTAR)2 questionnaire, which was administered by two independent researchers [25]. The AMSTAR method comprises 11 questions to which reviewers must respond with yes, cannot answer, no, or not applicable. Any disagreements were resolved via consulting with third investigator. The Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations (GRADE) tool was used to evaluate the overall certainty of the evidence for the included meta-analyses. The quality of evidence was categorized into four categories based on evaluation criteria, i.e., high, moderate, low, and very low [26].

Statistical analysis

The Stata Statistical Software version 16 (Stata Corp, College Station, TX, United States) was used to conduct random-effects model [27]. The pooled ES and its associated 95% CI were estimated using random-effects models implemented via the restricted maximum likelihood (REML) approach [28]. I2 was employed to assess the degree of heterogeneity among the included studies. Likewise, I2 > 50% was considered as indicative of substantial heterogeneity. Moreover, the sensitivity analysis was utilized to investigate the impact of a specific study on the overall ES. In addition, subgroup analysis was conducted to identify the potential sources of heterogeneity. It was done across various subgroups based on ES, type of exercise program and health condition. Publication bias was evaluated using funnel plots, as well as Begg’s and Egger’s tests.

Results

Study selection

Our systematic search ended in 1246 articles, of which 440 were duplicates. Title and abstract screening excluded 770 irrelevant studies and they were removed. Subsequently, 36 studies were undergoing full-text screening, resulting in the exclusion of seven studies that did not meet the specified inclusion criteria. Finally, 29 eligible systematic reviews and meta-analyses were included in the present study. Exercise program on CRP Levels

The efficacy of physical activity regimen on CRP level was reported in 21 meta-analyses involving 45 effect size (ES). A significant reduction in CRP levels was observed among individuals who engaged in physical activity (ES: −0.50; 95% CI: −0.65, −0.36, P < 0.001), with a high degree of heterogeneity between studies (I2 = 90.5%, P < 0.001) (Fig. 2). Subgroup analyses were conducted based on type of effect size, health status, and type of exercise to identify the source of heterogeneity. Studies that reported CRP levels using standard mean difference (SMD) (P < 0.001) demonstrated a significant reduction in CRP levels, in contrast to those using weighted mean difference (WMD) (P = 0.065). In addition, exercise program has been shown to significantly reduce CRP levels in healthy individuals, elderly people, those who are overweight or obese, postmenopausal women, and patients with T2DM (P < 0.05) (Table 4). However, patients with breast cancer did not experience a statistically significant benefit from the exercise program (P = 0.084). Moreover, subgroup analyses indicated that aerobic exercise, resistance exercise, and overall physical activity were associated with significant reductions in CRP levels (P < 0.001) (Table 4).Fig. 2Forest plot in relation to effect of exercise on CRP level

Publication bias

Egger’s test revealed no significant evidence of publication bias in term of CRP and TNF-α, which was further supported by visual inspection of the funnel plot (P = 0.342, and 0.256 respectively) (Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Fig. 2). In contrast, visual inspection of funnel plot showed an asymmetry distribution for IL-6 (Egger’s test: P < 0.001) (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Discussion

In the present systematic review and umbrella meta-analysis of available 29 meta-analyses, we summarized the available evidence on the effect of exercise program on inflammatory and oxidative biomarkers to provide a firm conclusion in this regard. The results of our pooled effect analysis of RCTs suggest that exercise program may decrease CRP, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α levels. However, no significant effect was shown in term of IL-10 in this study. Concerning oxidative stress biomarkers, exercise program could attenuate the MDA, LPO levels. Also, it could exert its modulatory effects by increasing GPx, NO, and TAS level. While, it failed to show any significant changes on SOD, TAC, and CAT level. Subgroup analyses were conducted based on type of effect size, health condition, and type of exercise program. Accordingly, aerobic, resistance and combined training programs, have been shown to benefit participants by reducing levels of CRP, IL-6, and TNF-α.

Our data synthesis revealed a significant reduction in CRP level following exercise program. It is noting worth that, this ameliorative effect on CRP level was observed in healthy, elderly individuals, postmenopausal women, and patients with T2DM. This finding across various health conditions, highlights the anti-inflammatory potential of exercise as a non-pharmacological intervention. This suggests that the low-grade inflammation associated with these health conditions can be mitigated through exercise interventions, potentially via mechanisms such as myokine release [56], enhanced insulin sensitivity [57], and decreased visceral adiposity [58, 59]. While, patients with breast cancer did not appear to benefit from the intervention in terms of CRP reduction. This points to that cancer-induced inflammation may have different characteristics and multiple factors such as life style, tumor behavior may be involved in the appeared inflammation [60]. Likewise, more additional therapeutic approaches are needed to modulate inflammation effectively in these patients. In addition to the findings on CRP, this meta-analysis also demonstrated that exercise interventions significantly reduced IL-6 levels. This favorable effect was particularly evident in healthy individuals, older adults, postmenopausal women, and patients with T2DM and MS. In contrast, individuals with breast cancer did not exhibit significant changes in IL-6 levels following the exercise programs. These finding support the anti-inflammatory role of exercise in regulating systemic cytokine activity [61]. Specifically, its anti-inflammatory effect may be attributed to the reductions in adipose tissue as a major source of pro-inflammatory IL-6 [62, 63]. However, the lack of significant change in IL-6 levels among breast cancer patients suggests that exercise alone may be insufficient to modulate inflammatory cytokines in oncologic settings [64]. Moreover, a similar pattern was observed for TNF-α as well. Accordingly, TNF-α was affected by physical activity regimen in a positive way. Specifically, reductions in TNF-α levels were noted in healthy individuals, older adults, postmenopausal women, and patients with T2DM, and dementia following physical activity regimen. While, patient with breast cancer and MS didn’t reach such significant effect in term of TNF-α in response to physical activity. TNF-α is a key pro-inflammatory cytokine involved in the pathogenesis of insulin resistance [65], endothelial dysfunction [66], and neurodegeneration [67]. Likewise, downregulation of TNF-α may be mediated by decreased adiposity, and the upregulation of anti-inflammatory signaling pathways, such as IL-10.

Furthermore, exercise program was accompanied with favorable effect on oxidative state in various health conditions. MDA and LPO levels were found to be decreased subsequent to physical activity intervention. MDA is a well-established marker of oxidative damage which is elevated in response to progression of various chronic diseases, including T2DM, and cardiovascular disorders [68–70]. Accordingly, attenuated MDA following exercise is a proof of exercise antioxidative capacity [71]. Exercise may affect ROS generation and improve redox homeostasis [72]. Also, there was a notable enhanced level of GPx, NO and TAS in the current study. Simultaneously, elevated GPx levels highlights a promoted immune defense system [73]. GPx plays a vital role in detoxifying hydrogen peroxide and lipid peroxides, thereby protecting cells from oxidative injury [74]. However, the underlying probable mechanisms are not diagnosed well. All these together, the concurrent decrease in MDA and increase in GPx highlights the bidirectional effect of exercise program. In this manner, exercise not only reduces oxidative damage but also potentiate body to neutralize ROS.

Our study has some limitations needed to be mentioned. First, type of aerobic and resistance exercise was not mentioned in the original studies which may affect the final conclusion. Second, only articles published in English were included which could contribute to publication bias. It is worth noting that despite some limitations our study has strengths too. First, high number of included studies and larger sample size contribute to a strong pooled analysis of evidence in term of exercise program on inflammatory and oxidative biomarkers. Moreover, the design of all included studies was RCTs which present the high degree of evidence.

Conclusion

The present systematic review and umbrella meta-analysis investigated the effect of exercise program on inflammatory and oxidative state. Accordingly, exercise program could attenuate the CRP, IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α level. Additionally, exercise demonstrated a beneficial impact on oxidative status, as evidenced by decreased MDA levels and increased GPx activity.

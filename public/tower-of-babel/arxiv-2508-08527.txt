Secondary terms in the counting functions of quartic fields II
Arul Shankar, Jacob Tsimerman

Abstract
We determine the smoothed counts of S 4 S_{4} -quartic fields with bounded discriminant, satisfying any finite specified set of local conditions, as the sum of two main terms with a power saving error term. We also prove an analogous result for quartic rings (weighted by the number of cubic resolvents), deducing as a consequence that the Shintani zeta functions associated to the prehomogeneous vector space ℂ 2 ⊗ Sym 2 ​ ( ℂ 3 ) {\mathbb{C}}^{2}\otimes{\rm Sym}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}^{3}) have at most a simple pole at s = 5 / 6 s=5/6 .

\int_{g\in{\mathcal{F}}}\#(L\cap g{\mathcal{B}})^{\Delta<X}dg,

Shintani’s method of evaluation, carried out successfully in

Meanwhile, geometry-of-numbers methods are highly flexible, allowing for manual removal of undesirable points from

5

(1)

We evaluate the analogue of (

(2)

We isolate certain “special slices” of

which have the property of containing only non-generic elements, and account for the majority of the non-generic elements. We note that there do exist non-generic elements outside these slices, which we show constitute a contribution asymptotic to
X^{5/6}
. Every integral element in the deep cusp lies in one of these slices. (Though these slices also have non-trivial intersection with the main body and shallow cusp.)

(3)

We give a power series expansion (with terms
X
and
X^{5/6}
) for the analogue of (
5

[

(4)

We determine asymptotics for the analogue of (
5

\asymp X^{5/6}
, only primary term asymptotics are needed, and this can be done using traditional geometry-of-numbers methods. This is carried out in §4.

(5)

We put everything together: Adding the result of Steps 1 and 3 over-counts (

Once Theorem
3

It will also be useful for us to define these additional subsets of

to be the set of elements in

with

We end with the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

An element
(A,B)\in V({\mathbb{Z}})
with nonzero discriminant is reducible if and only if
A
and
B
have a common rational zero as conics in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}({\mathbb{Q}})
. An element
(A,B)\in V({\mathbb{Z}})
with nonzero discriminant has reducible resolvent if and only if

is reducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof:

Let
(Q,C,R)
denote the triple corresponding to
(A,B)
. It is well known that

is the ring of global sections of the scheme cut out by the quadratic forms
A
and

in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. This has a summand of
{\mathbb{Q}}
in it if and only if the conics
A
and
B
have a common rational
0
. Similarly
{\rm{Res}}(A,B)
is reducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
if and only if

has a summand of
{\mathbb{Q}}
in it. However,

is the cubic resolvent algebra of

has a reducible resolvent precisely in Case (b) above.

\Box

Consequently, elements in the

2.3
Shintani zeta functions, global zeta integrals, and counts

In this subsection, we collect results from
[
13
]
on Shintani zeta functions, global integrals, and their relation to various counting functions of interest to us. We will initially work in a more general setting than usual. Specifically, let
\phi:V({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be any bounded function, and let
\psi,\eta:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
be smooth function with compact support. Fix
i\in\{0,1,2\}
, and let
{\mathcal{B}}:V({\mathbb{R}})^{(i)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth function with compact support away from the discriminant
0

Z(\phi,{\mathcal{B}};s):=\int_{g\in{\mathcal{F}}}(\lambda(g))^{-12s}\Bigl(\sum_{x\in V({\mathbb{Z}})}\phi(x)(g{\mathcal{B}})(x)\Bigr)\nu_{G}(g).

(8)

This zeta integral converges absolutely to the right of
\Re(s)=1
for
\phi=1
, and hence also for every bounded
\phi
. We define the associated counting function

{\mathcal{I}}_{\eta}(\phi,{\mathcal{B}};X):=\int_{g\in{\mathcal{F}}}\sum_{x\in V({\mathbb{Z}})}\phi(x)(g{\mathcal{B}})(x)\eta\Big(\frac{\lambda(g)}{X^{1/12}}\Big)dg.

(9)

A standard application of Mellin inversion relates these two quantities as follows:

\frac{J}{12}{\mathcal{I}}_{\eta}(\phi,{\mathcal{B}};X)=\int_{2}Z(\phi,{\mathcal{B}};s)\widetilde{\eta}(12s)X^{s}ds,

(10)

where we use the notation
\int_{2}
to mean
(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{\Re(s)=2}
.

When
\phi
is
G({\mathbb{Z}})
-invariant, we may define for
i\in\{0,1,2\}
, the counting function

N^{(i)}_{\psi}(\phi;X):=\sum_{x\in\frac{V({\mathbb{Z}})^{(i)}}{G({\mathbb{Z}})}}\frac{\phi(x)}{|{\rm Stab}_{G({\mathbb{Z}})}(x)|}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|\Delta(x)|}{X}\Bigr),

and its associated Dirichlet series

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\xi_{i}(\phi;s)&:=&\displaystyle\sum_{x\in G({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash V({\mathbb{Z}})^{(i)}}\frac{\phi(x)}{|{\rm Stab}_{G({\mathbb{Z}})}(x)||\Delta(x)|^{s}}.\end{array}

The function
\xi_{i}(\phi;s)
absolutely converges to the right of
\Re(s)=1
(again, this follows since the fact is known for
\phi=1
). Another application of Mellin inversion connects the above two quantities:

N^{(i)}_{\psi}(\phi;X)=\int_{2}\xi_{i}(\phi;s)X^{s}\widetilde{\psi}(s)ds.

(11)

When
\phi
is a
periodic function
, i.e., defined via congruence conditions modulo some positive integer,
\xi_{i}(\phi;s)
is the Shintani zeta function associated to
\phi
, and

is called the global zeta integral (associated to

and

). In that case, it is known that

and

have meromorphic continuation to the complex plane, with possible poles at most at
1
(or order at most 4),
5/6
, and
3/4
(of order at most
2
).
We note that (
11
) and (
10
) respectively imply that power saving expansions for
{\mathcal{I}}_{\eta}(\phi,{\mathcal{B}};X)
and

, up to errors of

for any positive
\theta
, determine the orders of the poles of

and

at

and

, as well as the residues.

Let
A_{i}=\#{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{R}}^{4-2i}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{i})
be the size of the stabilizer in
G({\mathbb{R}})
of any element in
V({\mathbb{R}})^{(i)}
. The following result relates
\xi_{i}(\phi;s)
and

for any

-invariant bounded function

.

Proposition 2.3
.

With notation as above, we have

Z(\phi,{\mathcal{B}};s)=A_{i}\xi_{i}(\phi,s)\int_{x\in V({\mathbb{R}})^{(i)}}|\Delta(x)|^{s-1}{\mathcal{B}}(x)\nu_{V}(x).

The above result is proved in
[
13
, Proposition 5.14]
. Indeed, while the result is implicitly stated for periodic functions

, the proof carries over without change for any bounded
G({\mathbb{Z}})
-invariant function.

Next, we use Proposition

to relate the counting functions

and

for some carefully chosen

and

. To that end, we pick

such that

and take smooth functions with compact support

and
\mathcal{G}:G_{1}({\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
, where

. We define

by setting

{\mathcal{B}}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g=\lambda g_{1}\in G({\mathbb{R}})\\
gy=x\end{subarray}}\mathcal{G}(g_{1})\eta_{1}(\lambda).

(12)

We define a smooth, compactly supported function

such that

\widetilde{\psi}(s)=\widetilde{\eta}(12s)\widetilde{\eta_{1}}(12s).

(13)

From the measure change formulas of the previous subsection, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{x\in V({\mathbb{R}})^{(i)}}|\Delta(x)|^{s-1}{\mathcal{B}}(x)\nu_{V}(x)&=&\displaystyle J\int_{\lambda}\lambda^{12s}\eta_{1}(\lambda)d^{\times}\lambda\int_{G_{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\mathcal{G}(g_{1})dg_{1}\\[14.45377pt]
&=&\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{G}}J\widetilde{\eta_{1}}(12s),\end{array}

for some constant

. Then we have the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\phi:V({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a

-invariant function. Let
{\mathcal{B}}:V({\mathbb{R}})^{(i)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and

where

is the constant above.

Proof:

We have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}I_{\eta}(\phi,{\mathcal{B}};X)&=&\displaystyle\frac{12}{J}\int_{2}Z(\phi,{\mathcal{B}};s)\widetilde{\eta}(12s)X^{s}ds\\[14.45377pt]

\Box

Quadratic fields

Counting quartic

-algebras of the form

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a squarefree positive integer, and let
\sigma_{n}=(\sigma_{p})_{p\mid n}
be a collection of quadratic splitting types, one for each prime

denote the family of quadratic fields

such that every prime

in

\psi:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
be a compactly supported function. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in F\\
\pm\Delta(K)>0\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|\Delta(K)|}{X}\Bigr)=c_{2}(\sigma(n))X+O(n^{1/4+o(1)}X^{1/2}),

for some constant
c_{2}(\sigma(n))\ll_{\psi}1
.

Proof:

Let
n_{0}
be the product of primes dividing

at which the prescribed splitting type is ramification. Let

be a square-free integer
with
(q,n)=1
. We set

to be the set of
D
satisfy the following two conditions:

(a)

is maximal at all prime

with the correct splitting type. (This is a congruence condition modulo
2nn_{0}

(b)

We have
q^{2}|D
.

There is a bijection between

and
F
, where

corresponds to the field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{D})
. Hence, we may compute the sum in the left hand side of the displayed equation lemma by sieving. Note that any

in

Recall that the Fourier transform

is bounded by
n^{\frac{1}{2}+o(1)}

We therefore obtain:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in F\\
\pm\Delta(K)>0\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|\Delta(K)|}{X}\Bigr)&=&\displaystyle\sum_{(q,n)=1}\mu(q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in S_{q}\\
\pm D>0\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|D|}{X}\Bigr)\\[14.45377pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{(q,n)=1}\mu(q)\sum_{\pm D^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|D^{\prime}|}{X/(n_{0}q^{2})}\Bigr)\theta_{n}(D^{\prime})\\[14.45377pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{(q,n)=1}\mu(q)\frac{X}{nn_{0}q^{2}}\sum_{\pm t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}}\hat{\psi}\Bigl(\frac{tX}{nn_{0}q^{2}}\Bigr)\hat{\theta}_{n}(t).\end{array}

(14)

The sum ov r
q

\pm\Delta(K)>0\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|\Delta(K)|}{X}\Bigr)=X\hat{\psi}(0)\cdot\frac{1}{nn_{0}}\prod_{(p,n)=1}\Bigl(1-\frac{\hat{\theta_{p}}(0)}{p^{2}}\Bigr)+O\Bigl(X^{1/2}n^{1/4+o(1)}\Bigr),

\Box

Factors associated with length of stay among patients with acute myocardial infarction undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention: findings from the CCC-ACS project
Authors: Chengran Wang, Zesheng Xu, Yong Chen Hao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveProlonged length of stay (LOS) contributes substantially to the healthcare burden in patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), yet large-scale evidence from Chinese populations remains limited. This study aimed to identify factors associated with prolonged LOS among AMI patients undergoing PCI.MethodsData were derived from the nationwide CCC-ACS (Improving Care for Cardiovascular Disease in China-Acute Coronary Syndrome) registry (November 2014 to December 2020). A total of 70 209 AMI patients aged 18–80 years undergoing PCI were included. Prolonged LOS was defined as >75th percentile. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify independent predictors. Temporal trends in LOS from 2015 to 2020 were also assessed. Patients were further stratified by five pre-admission risk factors to assess dose–response relationships.ResultsLOS decreased in ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction from 2015 to 2017 but remained stable thereafter, with no significant change in non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction. Female sex (OR=0.823), prior PCI (OR=0.745) and hyperlipidaemia (OR=0.917) were associated with a lower risk of prolonged LOS, whereas older age (OR=1.013), prior heart failure (OR=2.230), diabetes (OR=1.140) and transient ischaemic attack (OR=1.341) were independently associated with an increased risk of prolonged LOS (all p<0.05). A significant dose–response relationship was observed, with higher risk associated with increasing numbers of risk factors.ConclusionProlonged LOS among AMI patients undergoing PCI was associated with both baseline characteristics and in-hospital factors, with a clear cumulative effect, which may aid risk stratification and optimisation of in-hospital management.Trial registration numberNCT02306616.

Introduction

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) remains a major global public health burden. The prevalence of myocardial infarction is approximately 3.8% in individuals younger than 60 years and 9.5% in those aged ≥60 years worldwide.1 In China, while studies specifically reporting the incidence of AMI are relatively limited, analyses of ischaemic heart disease trends have shown a continuous increase in its age-standardised incidence.2 Moreover, hospitalisation for AMI continues to impose a substantial burden on healthcare resources. Data from the CAMI Registry indicate that the median length of stay (LOS) among patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) undergoing first-time percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) was 9 days markedly longer than the 3–5 days reported in Europe and the USA.3

This disparity suggests that post-PCI recovery in Chinese patients with AMI may involve a more prolonged in-hospital course, potentially reflecting greater complexity in peri-procedural management and a higher healthcare resource burden. Therefore, identifying factors associated with prolonged LOS is essential for optimising resource allocation and improving in-hospital management.

Previous studies have shown that LOS in AMI is influenced by multiple factors, including advanced age, comorbidities at admission (eg, heart failure (HF) and cardiogenic shock) and disease severity.4 5 However, evidence focusing on LOS among patients with myocardial infarction undergoing PCI remains limited, restricting a comprehensive understanding of resource utilisation and peri-procedural management.6

This study used data from the CCC-ACS project (Improving Care for Cardiovascular Disease in China–Acute Coronary Syndrome) and included patients with AMI undergoing PCI between 2015 and 2020. By analysing demographic characteristics, admission status, peri-procedural management and medication use, we aimed to identify factors associated with LOS. These findings may inform in-hospital management, optimise resource allocation and improve clinical outcomes in patients with AMI.

Methods

Study design

The CCC-ACS project is a nationwide registry and quality improvement initiative launched in November 2014 by the American Heart Association and the Chinese Society of Cardiology to improve care for patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) in China. Initially, 150 tertiary hospitals across diverse regions were enrolled, with subsequent expansion since 2017 to include 82 secondary hospitals and 9 additional tertiary hospitals. Data are prospectively collected using a standardised web-based platform (Oracle Clinical Remote Data Capture, Oracle Corporation) by trained personnel, including physicians, nurses, graduate students and clinical research coordinators. Detailed study design and methodology have been previously reported. The registry is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02306616) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study population

Data from 124 471 patients with ACS were collected from 241 hospitals between November 2014 and December 2020. Among these, 122 300 patients enrolled between 1 January 2015 and 31 December 2020, were screened. Patients with an LOS <1 day or >30 days (n=1938) were excluded because these extreme values were considered unlikely to represent the typical clinical course following PCI for AMI and might disproportionately influence the LOS analysis. Additional exclusion criteria included in-hospital death (n=65), severe malignancy or significant hepatic or renal dysfunction (n=1156), age <18 or >80 years (n=5198), no PCI or missing PCI data (n=35 766) and angina pectoris within the ACS spectrum (n=7968). A total of 70 209 patients were included in the final analysis (figure 1).

Study variables

This study included variables on demographic characteristics, clinical presentation, medical history, haemodynamic parameters, laboratory and echocardiographic findings, disease classification and treatment. Demographic variables included sex, age, medical insurance type, admission pathway and geographic region. Disease classification was based on myocardial infarction type. Medical history included hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidaemia, prior myocardial infarction, prior coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), prior PCI, atrial fibrillation (AF), HF, cardiomyopathy, transient ischaemic attack (TIA) and renal insufficiency.

Haemodynamic parameters at admission included systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure and heart rate. Laboratory and echocardiographic variables included fasting blood glucose (FBG), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), haemoglobin, serum creatinine, creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB) and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). Cardiac function was assessed using the Killip classification (I–IV). HF was defined as B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) ≥500 pg/mL or N-terminal pro-BNP ≥1000 pg/mL. Treatment variables included PCI and in-hospital anticoagulant therapy.

The primary outcome was LOS, defined as the interval from admission to discharge. LOS demonstrated a non-normal, right-skewed distribution (Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, p<0.001; skewness=1.208), with a median LOS of 9 days (IQR 7–12 days). The 75th percentile of LOS corresponded to 12 days. Because no universally accepted definition of prolonged LOS exists for AMI patients undergoing PCI, prolonged LOS was defined as LOS exceeding the 75th percentile of the study population (>12 days), consistent with previous observational studies evaluating prolonged LOS.7 8

In-hospital complications were defined as ≥1 of the following: recurrent myocardial infarction, in-stent thrombosis, in-stent restenosis, cardiogenic shock, HF, malignant arrhythmia, cardiac arrest or renal insufficiency.

All variables were extracted using standardised definitions from the CCC-ACS project. Associations between exposure variables and outcomes were analysed with adjustment for potential confounders according to established clinical research standards.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (V.26.0) and R (V.4.3.3). Normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Non-normally distributed variables are presented as medians (IQRs) and compared using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical variables are expressed as counts (percentages) and compared using the χ2 test. Univariable logistic regression was used to screen variables associated with prolonged LOS. Variables with p<0.05 were included in multivariable logistic regression to identify factors independently associated with prolonged LOS reported as adjusted ORs with 95% CIs.

To evaluate the cumulative effect of baseline risk factors, five variables that were routinely available at hospital admission (age >75 years, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, a history of HF and TIA) were selected from the multivariable logistic regression model. These variables, representing common demographic characteristics and the burden of chronic comorbidities, are routinely collected at admission and were therefore considered readily applicable in clinical practice. Patients were categorised according to the number of baseline risk factors present at admission (0, 1, 2, 3 or ≥4). The mean and median LOS, together with their SEs and 95% CIs, were calculated for each group, and a test for linear trend was performed to assess the dose–response relationship.

Missingness was systematically assessed for all candidate variables and summarised as the number and proportion of missing values (online supplemental table 1). Most demographic and clinical variables had low levels of missingness (<20%), whereas several laboratory and examination variables had moderate levels of missingness (20%–40%). Variables with extensive missingness (>30%) and complex or clinically uninformative missing data patterns were excluded to minimise potential bias and improve model stability. Missing values in the remaining variables were handled using multiple imputation. To evaluate the robustness of the findings, two sensitivity analyses were performed. First, complete-case analyses were conducted by repeating the multivariable logistic regression using only patients with complete data (online supplemental table 2). Second, patients with an LOS <1 day or >30 days, who had been excluded from the primary analysis, were re-included, and the multivariable logistic regression was repeated (online supplemental table 3).

Results

Between 2015 and 2017, the median LOS for both STEMI and non-STEMI (NSTEMI) patients showed a decline. This decrease reached statistical significance in the STEMI group (B=−0.229, 95% CI −0.443 to −0.014, p=0.042). Conversely, a similar downward trend was observed in the NSTEMI group, although it did not achieve statistical significance (B=−0.143, 95% CI −0.372 to 0.086, p=0.158). From 2017 to 2020, the median LOS remained relatively stable in both groups (figure 2).

A total of 70 209 patients were included. The mean LOS was 10.1±4.1 days, while the median LOS was 9 days (IQR 7–12 days). LOS demonstrated a right-skewed distribution (skewness=1.208; Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, p<0.001) (online supplemental figure 1)

Patients were categorised according to LOS quartiles: Q1 (≤7 days, n=18 715), Q2 (7–9 days, n=24 853), Q3 (9–12 days, n=14 400) and Q4 (>12 days, n=12 241) (online supplemental table 4)

Age increased with longer LOS (p<0.001), with median age rising from 60 (Q1) to 63 years (Q4) and the highest proportion of patients >70 years in Q4 (27.6%). The proportion of male patients decreased across groups (82.5%–75.2%, p<0.001).

The prevalence of hypertension, diabetes, TIA, cardiomyopathy and renal insufficiency increased with LOS (all p<0.001), whereas prior MI and PCI were more common in shorter LOS groups. In contrast, prior HF and AF increased with longer LOS. Although CABG differed across groups, its overall prevalence was low.

Systolic and diastolic blood pressures exhibited statistically significant differences across LOS (p<0.001), but the differences were small and clinically insignificant. LVEF decreased with longer LOS (58% in Q1 vs 55% in Q4). Anticoagulant use increased from 66.1% to 82.0% (p<0.001). The proportion of patients undergoing PCI within 12 hours after symptom onset decreased progressively across LOS groups, from 74.3% in Q2 to 57.1% in Q4 (p<0.001)

Patients with longer LOS were more likely from third-tier cities, while first-tier cities accounted for the lowest proportion (9.2%, p<0.001). The proportion of patients with Killip I decreased with increasing LOS, whereas the proportion with Killip II increased; the proportion of patients with Killip III–IV was similar across groups (p=0.988). Daytime/nighttime admission proportions and insurance distribution were comparable across groups, although self-paying patients decreased (16.0%–11.4%, p<0.001). STEMI and NSTEMI proportions were similar across groups. Emergency admissions were more frequent in Q4, with fewer outpatient admissions.

Factors associated with prolonged LOS

In the univariable logistic regression analysis, several baseline characteristics at admission were significantly associated with LOS, including age, sex, prior PCI, prior CABG, a history of AF, a history of HF, hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidaemia, TIA, FBG and lipid parameters (TC, TG and LDL-C) (all p<0.05). A history of MI and HDL-C were not significantly associated with LOS (table 1).

In the multivariable logistic regression analysis, a history of HF showed the strongest independent association with prolonged LOS (OR=2.230, 95% CI 1.851 to 2.688; p<0.001). Older age (OR=1.013, 95% CI 1.011 to 1.015; p<0.001), a history of AF (OR=1.204, 95% CI 1.035 to 1.400; p=0.016), diabetes (OR=1.140, 95% CI 1.088 to 1.196; p<0.001), hypertension (OR=1.036, 95% CI 1.000 to 1.075; p=0.048) and TIA (OR=1.341, 95% CI 1.255 to 1.433; p<0.001) were each independently associated with a greater likelihood of prolonged LOS. In contrast, female sex (OR=0.823, 95% CI 0.787 to 0.861; p<0.001), prior PCI (OR=0.745, 95% CI 0.674 to 0.823; p<0.001) and hyperlipidaemia (OR=0.917, 95% CI 0.853 to 0.987; p=0.020) were independently associated with a lower likelihood of prolonged LOS. Compared with patients with NSTEMI, those with STEMI were less likely to experience prolonged LOS (OR=0.876, 95% CI 0.840 to 0.914; p<0.001) (table 1).

After adjustment for potential confounders, a history of MI, prior CABG, FBG, TC, HDL-C and LDL-C were not independently associated with prolonged LOS. Although TG remained statistically significant (OR=0.974, 95% CI 0.960 to 0.988; p<0.001), its effect size was modest.

Association between the number of risk factors and prolonged LOS

Five pre-admission risk factors were defined: age (>75 years), hypertension, diabetes, a history of HF and TIA. Patients were stratified into five groups based on the number of risk factors (0, 1, 2, 3 and ≥4). LOS was reported as both the median and the mean, accompanied by a 95% CI. The results demonstrated a clear, graded increase in LOS corresponding to the number of risk factors. Patients with one risk factor had a median LOS of 9 days (IQR: 7–12), whereas those with ≥5 risk factors experienced a prolonged median LOS of 11 days (IQR: 9–15). Similarly, the mean LOS increased progressively from 9.862 days (95% CI 9.815 to 9.910) to 12.489 days (95% CI 11.763 to 13.216), indicating a stable dose–response relationship between the accumulation of pre-admission risk factors and prolonged LOS (table 2, online supplemental figure 2).

Discussion

This study demonstrates that: (1) between 2015 and 2017, the LOS among patients with AMI undergoing PCI declined; however, from 2017 to 2020, this trend plateaued, with no further reduction observed. (2) Age, female sex, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, a history of AF, HF, prior TIA and prior PCI were independently associated with LOS among patients with AMI undergoing PCI.

LOS increased progressively with an increasing number of pre-admission risk factors.

Between 2015 and 2017, LOS among AMI patients undergoing PCI declined, followed by a plateau from 2017 to 2020. This pattern likely reflects stage-specific development of the cardiovascular care system in China.9 Early reductions may be attributed to the establishment of chest pain centres and quality improvement initiatives, which optimised reperfusion and peri-procedural management.10 However, subsequent population ageing and increasing risk burden have led to a higher proportion of elderly and complex cases, potentially limiting further reductions in LOS.11 In addition, healthcare system adjustments during the COVID-19 pandemic may have reinforced this plateau.12

Prolonged LOS reflects the integrated impact of baseline risk burden and disease complexity rather than the effect of any single clinical variable. Advanced age, impaired cardiac function and multisystem comorbidities collectively constitute a higher underlying risk profile.13 Reduced cardiac reserve and increased inflammatory burden may partly explain the observed associations between these risk profiles and prolonged hospitalisation.14 Accordingly, LOS represents not only an outcome of treatment processes but also a composite indicator of disease complexity and healthcare demand.

Prior PCI was associated with shorter LOS, whereas history of AF and HF

were associated with prolonged LOS. This may reflect prior revascularisation and better risk control in PCI patients, while history of AF and HF indicate more complex structural disease and reduced functional reserve, requiring more intensive management.15

Female sex was independently associated with a lower likelihood of prolonged LOS. This finding should not be interpreted as indicating lower disease severity among women. Rather, it may reflect differences in clinical presentation, treatment pathways, healthcare utilisation or discharge practices between women and men. Given the observational nature of this study, the mechanisms underlying this association remain uncertain and warrant further investigation.16

In the present study, hypertension, diabetes mellitus and prior TIA were associated with prolonged LOS, consistent with previous evidence indicating that these chronic comorbidities contribute to greater cardiovascular risk, increased clinical complexity, and a higher likelihood of adverse in-hospital events.

Patients with these conditions frequently have more extensive atherosclerotic disease, impaired vascular function and multiple organ involvement, all of which may necessitate more intensive monitoring and delay hospital discharge.17

Conversely, a history of dyslipidaemia was associated with a lower likelihood of prolonged hospitalisation. Although seemingly counterintuitive, similar findings have been described as part of the ‘cholesterol paradox’ in patients with AMI.18

One possible explanation is that patients with known dyslipidaemia are more likely to have been receiving statin therapy before admission, resulting in improved plaque stabilisation, reduced inflammatory activity and better baseline secondary prevention. Alternatively, a documented history of dyslipidaemia may reflect earlier cardiovascular risk recognition, greater engagement with healthcare services and more comprehensive long-term risk factor management rather than a protective effect of dyslipidaemia itself. Therefore, this finding should be interpreted cautiously and is likely to reflect differences in pre-existing treatment patterns and residual confounding rather than a true biological benefit of dyslipidaemia.

Further analyses demonstrated a graded, cumulative increase in LOS with a higher number of pre-admission risk factors, indicating a sustained additive effect of baseline risk burden. This finding is consistent with prior evidence that comorbidity burden predicts in-hospital outcomes and healthcare utilisation.19

Similarly, Abdulla et al reported that patients with AMI and multiple chronic comorbidities experienced significantly longer hospital stays and a higher risk of readmission.18 It is noteworthy that prior studies have largely treated comorbidity burden as a covariate for statistical adjustment, with limited efforts to systematically evaluate the cumulative structural effect of comorbid conditions on LOS. We quantified clinically relevant pre-admission risk factors (age, hypertension, diabetes, TIA and prior HF) and stratified patients by risk burden, thereby demonstrating an independent cumulative effect on LOS.

From a mechanistic perspective, multiple pre-admission risk factors often reflect chronic metabolic disturbances, endothelial dysfunction and reduced cardiac reserve. Following an acute event, these pathophysiological processes may contribute to increase the complexity of peri-procedural management and the likelihood of complications, thereby prolonging hospitalisation.20 Moreover, the accumulation of risk factors was associated with greater healthcare resource utilisation through polypharmacy, intensified monitoring requirements and delayed functional recovery.21

These findings highlight the importance of early identification of high-risk patients. Optimising reperfusion pathways, strengthening peri-procedural risk assessment and preventing in-hospital complications may help reduce LOS. However, increasing patient complexity may limit further gains from process optimisation alone, underscoring the need for individualised risk stratification and tailored management. At the system level, even modest increases in LOS can impose substantial cumulative burdens on healthcare resources. Integrating risk factors into practical clinical tools and validating them across settings should be priorities for future research.

This study has several limitations. First, the population comprised predominantly Chinese patients, which may limit generalisability to other populations and healthcare systems. Second, as an observational study, causal inference cannot be established despite adjustment for multiple confounders and residual confounding from unmeasured factors (eg, socioeconomic status, post-discharge care and hospital policies) may persist. Third, although based on nationwide multicentre data, heterogeneity in infrastructure, resource allocation and clinical practice across regions may affect external validity

Conclusions

This study identified independent factors associated with LOS among patients with AMI undergoing PCI. Age, female sex, hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidaemia, a history of AF, a history of HF, TIA and prior PCI were independently associated with LOS among patients with AMI undergoing PCI.

Furthermore, the number of pre-admission risk factors was significantly associated with LOS and exhibited a clear cumulative effect, such that a greater number of risk factors corresponded to a longer LOS.

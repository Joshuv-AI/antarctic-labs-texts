Dynamic behaviour and power performance of a Septon semisubmersible floating wind turbine integrated with wave energy converters
Authors: Binoy Sebastian, D. Karmakar, Manu Rao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The development of renewable energy sources is inevitable to create a sustainable society for the future. Hybrid wind and wave energy systems are highly regarded as a solution to reduce the cost of energy from offshore wind and waves. The manuscript presents a novel semi-submersible floating wind platform referred to as Septon which is designed with the intention of hosting a multitude of wave energy devices in addition to a wind turbine. Three different wave energy converters (WEC) namely oscillating water column, Torus and point absorber along with their combinations with the Septon platform are considered in the study to understand the dynamic behaviour and power absorption of standalone and integrated configuration. Seven different configurations of the hybrid system are considered for the analysis. Coupled dynamic analysis is performed using an aero-hydro-servo-elastic tool based on boundary element method to analyze the responses of the hybrid platforms under realistic sea states. The motion responses, tower base moments, mooring tensions and power absorption of the hybrid systems are analyzed and compared with the Septon floating wind platform to quantify the effect of various combination of WECs around the wind turbine platform. The numerical results shows that different combinations have a significant impact on the dynamic responses of the platform. The study identifies the hybrid system combining OWC and Torus with the proposed Septon platform as the concept with maximum efficiency in power absorption.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-25012-5.

Introduction

The impacts of global warming and climate change are compelling the world to foster the development of renewable and sustainable energy sources. Limiting carbon emissions is crucial to curtailing the rise of global average temperatures and achieving the goals set by the Paris Agreement in 2015. The environmental pollution caused by the burning of fossil fuels can be reduced only with an energy transition to renewable sources at a fast pace1. Oceans have tremendous untapped energy in the form of wind, waves, and currents that can provide a significant part of the increasing global energy demands. The major hindrance to the extraction of offshore renewable energy (ORE) is the high capital and operational costs, which reflect in the cost of energy2,3. However, the levelized cost of energy (LCOE) from offshore is steadily decreasing over the past few years with rapid advancements in technology4,5. Offshore wind turbines are currently the most advanced sector in ORE, with the technology being adopted from the already mature land-based wind industry and oil and gas industry6. The first offshore wind turbine (OWT) was installed in Sweden in 1990, and it has ever since developed rapidly in size and power capacity from a 220 kW prototype in 1990 to a 20 MW wind turbine installed as part of the Mingyang project in China as of 2024. Increasing wind turbine size is one strategy to reduce the LCOE, which has been successfully implemented over the years7,8. In the current scenario, the LCOE of offshore wind energy has become competitive with that of energy from fossil fuels9. Though OWTs are projected to further increase in size, the engineering challenges associated with the design of larger wind turbines also increase, to a point where it becomes physically or economically not viable. Alternate solutions are required to further bring down the energy costs from offshore wind.

Combining wave energy converters (WEC) with OWT platforms are regarded as a promising solution to reduce LCOE from both sources3,10. Combined platforms allow the sharing of substructure, mooring system, power grid, substation, and logistics, which can drastically reduce the capital expenditure11,12. Offshore wind turbines are often placed in distances of the order of kilometers apart to avoid aerodynamic wake effects13, accommodating wave energy devices can help to better utilize the vast ocean space in between them14. Waves are usually available even in the absence of consistent winds, and they are much more predictable15, which helps to reduce the zero operational hours of the OWT or smoothen the output power supply16. Due to these advantages, hybrid wind-wave platforms have become a topic of great interest in research over the last decade. Some studies have revealed that integration of WECs helps to mitigate platform motions and improve the overall efficiency of wind turbine17–19. Compared to OWTs, wave energy devices do not have a converged or optimized technology and is lagging behind in technological readiness level20. There are several different concepts in WECs that work on different principles21. The current cost of wave energy is more than twice that of offshore wind22, which hinders its commercialization. The multi-functional platforms integrating WECs with existing offshore wind farms can enable the commercialization of wave energy devices in their current state of development23, which makes the concept attractive.

Hybrid wind-wave platforms have emerged as a topic of research only in the last two decades and is still in the early conceptual stage. Most of the studies on hybrid systems are on floating platforms compared to fixed ones, as WECs are often considered as a means to provide additional stability to floating OWTs. A feasibility study was conducted by Aubualt et al.24 on combining an OWC with WindFloat semi-submersible platform (SSP). The experimental and numerical study showed that the addition of OWC had a limited impact on the motion responses of the platform. Muliawan et al.25,26 proposed a novel concept combining a Spar platform with Torus (STC) WEC and presented detailed studies on the dynamic behaviour of the hybrid concept in operational and survival conditions. The study found that the total power production of the STC is 10–15% higher than the OWT platform without the torus. Luan et al.27 and Michailides et al.28 studied the dynamic responses of a brace-less SSP combining Flap-type WECs using Simo-Riflex tool. The study noted that the Flaps increased the platform motions, tower base moments, and mooring tensions. Vijay et al.29 analysed the hydrodynamic behaviour of a floating wind turbine barge provided with moonpools, which can be used as OWCs. The study showed that moonpools lead to an enhancement in peak responses of the platform motions. An experimental study of a novel hybrid concept of a six-column SSP integrating an array of six and twelve point-absorbers is presented by Hallak et al.30 and Gaspar et al.18, respectively. A 1:27 scale model of the hybrid platforms was tested in a wave basin to analyse the stability of the system. The combined system is observed to exhibit better stability and provide smoother power output compared to the wind turbine SSP. Ren et al.31 proposed a novel hybrid system comprising a TLP accommodating a heavy-type WEC similar to a torus. Experimental and numerical tests were performed to determine the dynamic responses of the platform under regular and irregular waves. Ghafari et al.32 investigated the effect of integrating various Wavestar point absorber configurations to the OC3 Hywind spar platform. The increase in the number of WECs effectively reduced the heave and pitch motions of the platform and improved the overall power generation of the system.

A combination of Torus and Flaps with a braceless semi-submersible wind turbine platform (STFC) was conceptualized by Lee et al.33. Coupled dynamic analysis of the platform was carried out in the Simo-Riflex tool, taking into account the effect of second-order wave loads. The study observed that the WECs aid in suppressing surge responses of the system and enhance power generation. Zhang et al.10 conducted an experimental study to analyse the effect of split type heaving point absorbers integrated with a TLP. The study considered waves with short periods and small wave amplitudes pertaining to those of the China Sea. The study revealed that split-type point absorbers significantly improve wave energy capture for low sea states. Thereafter, Zhang et al.34 presented a study on the dynamic responses of DeepCwind SSP, combining two types of oscillating body type WECs, such as Wavebob and Wavestar. The study observed that WECs render better stability to the system against pitch rotations. Rony and Karmakar35 performed hydrodynamic analysis to study a hybrid system comprising a novel frustum TLP (FTLP) integrated with circular arrays of cone-cylinder point absorbers. Two different power take-off (PTO) control strategies were employed in the study to investigate the effect of PTO systems on the power absorption of WEC arrays. To address the need for an accurate numerical model for simulating the complex multi-body dynamics of hybrid platforms, Han et al.36 developed a fully coupled numerical framework bridging the OpenFAST tool with Ansys Aqwa. The accuracy of the new model was established based on validation with published experimental studies. Cayuela-Padilla et al.37 carried out extensive numerical simulations using OpenFAST to investigate the motion suppression of a novel six-column SSP using OWCs. All possible combinations of OWCs in each discrete column of the wind turbine floater were analysed to arrive at the most efficient configuration that gives maximum stability in pitch and yaw motions. The study showed that OWCs can be a viable solution for dampening pitch motion of floating OWTs, thereby increasing their efficiency and extending their service life. With most of the available literature on hybrid wind-wave systems dealing with 5 MW wind turbines Gu et al.38 performed a numerical study to understand the dynamics of an IEA 15 MW floating wind turbine SSP integrated with four Torus WECs. A pitch reduction of 31.5% was noted in the rated operational condition of the wind turbine with the incorporation of Torus.

Most of the published research works on combined systems, including some of the aforementioned literature, have reported a positive synergy in incorporating wave energy devices on OWT platforms. By analysing the nature of research carried out in hybrid systems, it can be seen that most studies have randomly chosen a floating OWT platform and a WEC suitable for integration with it. The semi-submersibles are the most studied substructure option for hybrid systems due to their flexibility and multi-column geometry that allows more space for WECs39. Among WECs, point absorbers are a common option22 as they can easily be integrated into floating wind platforms without modifying the design of the floater. Based on the comprehensive literature survey, it was found that there are very few studies that compare the effects of different types of WECs on the same floating platform. Gao et al.40 compared two different concepts, STC and SFC, using scaled model tests and numerical analysis. Some studies by Lee et al.33 and Zhang et al.34 presented the effect of incorporating more than one type of WEC in the same OWT platform. However, they did not account for the effect of each type of WEC on the platform separately. There is a research void in understanding the effect of different types of WECs on the same OWT platform. Such studies are essential to identify the best WEC option or the optimum combination of WECs for a given floating OWT platform.

The present study aims to analyse the impact of integrating WECs with different working principles on a novel semi-submersible platform (SSP). The current work considers the three most widely studied WECs, such as OWC, Torus, and Point Absorbers (PA), for incorporation into the SSP. The novel SSP is designed to host different types of WECs in addition to a 5 MW wind turbine, as in Fig. 1. The three sets of configurations are considered for the present study. In the first set, an array of only one type of WEC is integrated with the SSP, resulting in three different configurations. In the second set, the combination of two types of WEC arrays is considered again, resulting in three different hybrid concepts. The final set comprises a single hybrid concept with all three types of WECs incorporated into it as an array. As a way to compare the concepts, the total waterplane area of the WECs is kept constant for the first set. This means that the available wave power is the same for all three configurations. The hybrid concepts in the second and third sets have twice and thrice the total waterplane area for WECs compared to that of the first set. The hydrodynamic coefficients and wave excitation forces are computed using WAMIT. The coupled dynamic analysis of the hybrid systems under various sea states is carried out using OpenFAST. The multi-body dynamics of the hybrid systems are simulated using the Ansys Aqwa tool to obtain the responses of WECs, which are post-processed in MATLAB to compute power absorption.

Description of hybrid wind-wave systems

A novel semi-submersible floating wind platform is adopted in this study for integrating WECs. The novel SSP comprises six offset columns, which provide restoring moment to the system and a main column at the centre to support a 5 MW NREL wind turbine.

Novel semi-submersible platform

The SSP in the present study is referred to as Septon, as it has a total of seven columns interconnected by pontoons or braces. The Septon platform is designed with three larger offset columns and three smaller offset columns. The larger offset columns (primary columns) contribute to most of the platform’s buoyancy, while the smaller columns (secondary columns) function as intermediate members between larger columns to provide structural integrity to the system. The primary columns are equally spaced 80 m from each other, and the secondary columns occupy the mid spans of the primary columns. This allows ample space for accommodating various WECs. The primary columns have a frustum-shaped draft and large cylindrical base columns.

The base columns are designed to dampen the excessive heave motion of the platform. Septon has an operational draft of 20 m and an elevation of 10 m above MSL. The plan and elevation of the floater are provided in schematic diagrams shown in Fig. 2a–c. The principal dimensions of Septon and its main properties are given by Table 1 and Table 2, respectively. The SSP is kept in position using three equally spaced catenary mooring chains attached to the top of the base columns, the properties of which are mentioned in Table 3. The schematic representation of the orientation of the Septon platform with respect to the wave direction is given in Fig. 3.

Wave energy converters

Three different types of WECs, such as OWC, Torus, and Point Absorbers, that are based on different operating principles, are considered in the study. These WECs have been selected as they are the most popular and reliable devices considered for integration into wind platforms, as presented in the previous study by researchers. The OWC comprises a cylindrical chamber with both ends open. The action of waves causes the water column inside the chamber to rise and fall, creating a pneumatic pressure, which pushes the air in and out of the chamber. This ingress and egress of air is channelled to a bidirectional air turbine to generate power. Two OWC chambers of different sizes are taken for the current study; the bigger OWC (OWC-A) is attached to the secondary column, while the smaller OWC (OWC-B) is held by connecting pontoons on the SSP. The Torus is an oscillating body type WEC which has an annular shape. The Torus is attached to the secondary columns of the floater by means of guide rollers and is restricted to move only in heave motion. The relative heave motion of the Torus and the platform activates a hydraulic power take-off (PTO) system to produce useful power. The point absorbers also fall under the category of wave-activated bodies that capture wave energy in heave motion by means of a hydraulic PTO system. The PA used for the present study is cone-cylindrical shaped, following the shape optimization study by Sinha et al. (2016), which identifies the cone-cylinder shape as having better hydrodynamic performance. Mechanical arms are used to connect PAs to the top pontoons of the SSP. Hybrid wind-wave system configurations

A total of seven different configurations of the WECs with the Septon OWT platform are considered for the present study. For the sake of convenience, the hybrid configurations are categorized into three sets. In the first set, only one type of WEC is integrated into the platform. This gives three configurations, namely, the Hybrid Septon OWC (HSO), Hybrid Septon Torus (HST), and Hybrid Septon PA (HSP). The HSO and HST are equipped with three OWC-A and three Tori in their secondary columns, respectively. The HSP has an array of six PAs attached to the mid spans of the top pontoons of the SSP via mechanical arms. The total water plane area of the WECs is kept constant for the three hybrid concepts.

In the second set, two types of WECs are attached to Septon, and three possible combinations are identified, viz., Hybrid Septon OWC and Torus (HSOT), Hybrid Septon Torus and PA (HSTP), and Hybrid Septon OWC and PA (HSOP). HSOT employs OWC-B in the midspan of primary and secondary columns supported by pontoons. The HSOP is equipped with OWC-A in the secondary columns. The PAs are also placed in the mid-span of primary and secondary columns for HSTP and HSOP. The total water plane area of the WECs in the three configurations of the second set is twice that of the first set. In the last set, a combination of all three WECs is considered, which gives the concept HSOTP. The OWC-B and PAs are placed at 1/3rd the spans of connecting pontoons. The HSOTP has a total WEC water plane area that is three times that of the configurations in the first set. The schematic diagrams of the proposed hybrid configurations are presented in Figs. 8 and 9.

Numerical modelling

Floating OWTs are subjected to various environmental and structural loads, which add to the complexity of their numerical simulations. The present study uses OpenFAST to perform dynamic analyses of the combined wind and wave energy platforms. OpenFAST is a multiphysics tool developed specifically for analysing wind turbine systems and is capable of accounting for the action of wind, wave, current, and structural loads acting on the system. As the main objective of this study is to determine the best combination of WECs with the SSP, rigid body analysis has been performed to quantify the dynamic responses of the configurations. The total waterplane area was kept constant within each set of configurations, ensuring that all concepts in a set received the same available wave power. This allowed direct assessment of the effect of WEC type on hydrodynamic performance and power absorption. The waterplane area and GM values differ between sets, reflecting realistic changes in hydrostatic properties when more WECs are integrated into the system. All configurations satisfied the intact stability criteria, and the computed GM values are provided in Table 7 for reference. The initial frequency domain analysis is carried out to determine the hydrodynamic parameters and wave excitation forces on the platforms. Ansys-Aqwa has been used for performing multi-body analysis to determine the power absorption of the WECs in the system. Power absorption of WECs

The present study adopts three types of WECs with different operating principles. The PTO system, which converts wave energy to useful power, also changes depending on the type of WEC. Environmental conditions

The environmental conditions of the Atlantic North Sea given in Muliawan et al.13 have been used for numerical simulations to analyse the dynamic responses of the hybrid concepts in this study. The metocean conditions of the irregular sea states are defined by the statistical parameters significant wave height (Hs), peak spectral period (Tp) and mean wind speed (Umean). Table 8 encapsulates the five environmental conditions used for the present study. The wind speed data is generated using TurbSim5 based on the Kaimal turbulence model and the wave elevation is generated by HydroDyn module using JONSWAP spectrum. Results and discussions

The dynamic behaviour of a novel floating wind turbine SSP combined with different configurations of WECs is studied to understand the effect of different WECs and their combinations on the platform responses. The present study compares the dynamic performance and power absorption of seven hybrid concepts in irregular sea states. The hydrodynamic coefficients and wave excitation forces are derived using WAMIT in the frequency domain. The dynamic responses, which include the platform motions, tower base moments, and mooring tensions of the hybrid systems, are obtained in the time domain using the OpenFAST tool. The results are compared with those of the floating wind turbine platform to identify the best combination of SSP and WECs.

Validation

The numerical model is validated by comparing the motion response RAOs of the DeepCwind SSP obtained from OpenFAST with experimental data reported by Robertson et al.46. As part of the OC4 project, a scaled physical model of the DeepCwind SSP, coupled with a 5 MW wind turbine, was tested at the MARIN offshore basin. Selected experimental results from these tests are used for validation.

Figure 10 presents a comparison of the surge, heave, pitch, and mooring tension RAOs obtained in the present study with those from the experimental campaign. The responses correspond to white-noise wave excitation with a significant wave height of 7.1 m and a steady wind speed of 21.8 m/s. The time-domain results from OpenFAST simulations were transformed into the frequency domain using the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to compute the RAOs. The comparison demonstrates reasonably good agreement, with discrepancies within a 7% error margin, thereby confirming the accuracy of the numerical model.

Free decay tests

The natural periods of the floating wind platform and the hybrid systems are initially determined using free decay tests. The fixing of natural frequencies of a floating platform is an important part of the design that determines its dynamic performance. It is necessary to keep the platform’s natural periods in different DOF from interfering with the normal range of wave periods of 5 to 25 s46 encountered in the ocean. In general, the natural frequencies of floating wind platforms are designed to be in a low-frequency region to avoid resonance conditions. The free decay simulations are performed in the present study by providing an initial displacement or rotation to the floating platform in still water conditions, allowing the motion to decay without any external loads acting on the platform. The natural periods of the Septon wind turbine SSP and the hybrid configurations, in the six DOF, are presented in Table 9. The natural periods of the Septon platform do not fall in the wave excitation periods of 5 to 25 s except in the case of Heave motion. The heave natural period of most wind turbine SSP lies within the wave excitation frequencies and is generally acceptable. The large base columns help to mitigate excessive heave motions by providing higher damping to the system. It is observed that there is an increase in the natural periods in surge, sway, heave, and yaw motions with the addition of WECs to the SSP. The roll and pitch natural periods are found to reduce with the addition of WECs, except for the HSO configuration.

Motion responses of the hybrid platforms

The motion responses of a floating wind turbine platform have a direct effect on all other dynamic responses and power generation of the system. The reduction of platform motions, especially in pitch, is crucial to reducing the structural loads and improving the efficiency of wind turbines. The platform motions of Septon SSP and hybrid concepts are determined for both regular and irregular waves and have been presented as Response Amplitude Operators (RAO), motion statistics, and Power Spectral Densities (PSD) in this section.

RAOs of the hybrid platforms

RAOs are conventionally defined as a function that gives the relative motion of a floating body with respect to the amplitude of incident waves acting on it. The RAOs of the Septon wind platform and hybrid concepts are determined for regular wave conditions, considering the systems to be linear, and presented in Fig. 11a–d. The responses are computed for a wave period range of 1 to 30 s with 0° wave heading angle. From Fig. 11a, it is observed that the surge RAOs slightly increase with the incorporation of WECs relative to the Septon platform, throughout the wave period range considered. This is due to the attraction of higher wave loads on the platform with an increase in water plane area. The surge excitation forces are greater than the additional resistance offered by WECs in the surge direction. In the case of heave RAOs shown in Fig. 11b, it is noted that the addition of WECs has led to a peak enhancement near the heave resonance periods of the system. The smallest peak enhancement is seen for HSTP with 30% and the highest peak variation is shown by the HST configuration with 122%. The pitch RAOs of the hybrid concepts given in Fig. 11c also exhibit a variation of peak responses, while the RAO magnitudes do not have a considerable variation in non-resonant wave periods. The pitch RAO peak values reduce with the integration of WECs, except for HSP and HSTP configurations. The HSTP has the highest peak enhancement of 19% while the HSOTP configuration gives the maximum peak reduction of 26%. The yaw responses exhibit a low RAO magnitude, which is less than 0.5 deg/m for all hybrid concepts, caused by the large water plane moment of the floating platform.

The time domain responses of the hybrid platforms are obtained for the five irregular sea states considered with a head sea condition. The mean and standard deviation (SD) of the motion responses in surge, heave, and pitch are presented in Figs. 12, 13, 14. The Septon SSP without any WECs is used for comparison of the results. By analysing the motion responses, it is noted that the motion responses are highest at EC-2, which is near the rated wind speed condition of the wind turbine. The SD increases with higher sea states, which indicates that the wave loads contribute to motion turbulence much more than the wind loads acting on the system, which is maximum at the rated wind speed of the turbine. It is observed from Fig. 12a that the mean surge motion increases with the addition of WECs. The hybrid configurations with OWC tend to exhibit higher surge responses than those without OWC. The HSOTP concept has the highest surge offsets with 17–27% increase in platform surge compared to the Septon platform, followed by HSOP and HSOT. The surge SD (Fig. 12b) varies from − 2 to 14% for the hybrid platforms in the considered sea states. From the heave statistics shown in Fig. 13a,b it is observed that the heave offsets of the platform reduce with the integration of WECs by 2% to 16%, except for HST concept. The mean heave offsets of HST are 6–17% higher than Septon platform likely due to the sharp increase in heave resonance peak with the addition of Torus WECs.

PSDs of the hybrid platforms

The power spectral density of the dynamic responses of a floating platform is a useful metric to analyse the energy distribution over the entire frequency range. The PSDs of surge, heave, pitch, and yaw motions of the hybrid platforms for EC-2 condition are given in Fig. 15a–d. From analysing the PSDs, two subranges of frequencies are identified for which the platform excitations are seen to be significantly high. The sub-frequency ranges correspond to the natural frequencies of the platform in the respective DOF and linear wave excitation frequencies of 0.05 to 0.2 Hz. The surge and heave PSDs are seen to have higher excitation peaks close to their resonance frequencies of motion, while the pitch and yaw motions have their prominent peak values close to the modal frequency (0.1 Hz) of the irregular sea state. The surge resonance excitations of the platforms, as seen in Fig. 15a, occur at a low frequency range of 0.007–0.009 Hz. The surge motions of the floater are largely influenced by wind loads acting on the wind turbine blades and tower33. It is observed that the surge resonance peaks are less than those of the Septon platform for all hybrid concepts except HSO. The addition of point absorbers leads to a 24% decrement in peak surge resonance, which is the least among the hybrid concepts; meanwhile, the hybrid combinations with OWC have peak values close to the initial platform. It is evident from Fig. 15b that the heave resonant frequency falls within the normal range of frequencies occurring in the ocean, which implies frequent occurrence of resonant states. The provision of large base columns at the bottom helps to mitigate the excessive vertical motion of the platform by acting as dampers. In the case of pitch PSDs given in Fig. 15c, the pitch resonance peak of the Septon platform reduces by 30–55% with different WEC configurations. It is observed that, within the wave excitation zone, the pitch motion of the hybrid concepts with point absorbers is higher in terms of the maximum value compared to other concepts. As for the yaw PSDs given in Fig. 15d, the incorporation of WECs has caused a reduction in energy of excitation both for the near resonance frequencies and the linear wave frequencies, the reason for which can be attributed to the increased damping by WECs.

WEC motion responses

Three different types of WECs, which are OWC, torus, and PAs, are considered in the present study for integration with a floating OWT platform. The OWCs are rigidly fixed to the Septon platform while the torus and PA are restricted to move only in heave. The wave elevation or pumping motion inside the OWC chamber determines the power absorption of the WEC. The power output from the torus and point absorbers is dependent on the heave motion of the WECs. The multi-body analyses of the hybrid platforms are performed in Ansys Aqwa to determine the motion of WECs. The motion responses of the OWCs, tori, and PAs in HSOTP are presented as RAOs in Fig. 18a–c. It can be seen from Fig. 18a–c that the motion responses of WEC exhibit peak values for resonance frequencies of both the floating platform and the WECs. Depending on the orientation of the WECs with the incident wave direction, there is a variation in the magnitude of RAOs. The WECs on the lee side have slightly smaller response amplitudes due to the sheltering effect of the floating platform and the WECs in the forefront.

Fig. 18RAOs of the WEC motion responses of HSOTP concept (a) Pumping motion of OWCs, (b) Heave motion of tori, (c) Heave motion of point absorbers for EC-2 conditions.

RAOs of the WEC motion responses of HSOTP concept (a) Pumping motion of OWCs, (b) Heave motion of tori, (c) Heave motion of point absorbers for EC-2 conditions.

It is observed that the RAOs of WEC motion attain a maximum value close to the peak spectral frequency of the irregular sea state. It is also noted that the natural frequencies of pumping motion in OWC and the heave motion of tori fall outside the linear wave frequencies, which implies that the power production of WECs is more influenced by platform heave resonance and the wave excitation responses. WECs are usually designed to undergo frequent resonance to enhance power generation. However, floating wind platforms are designed with the intention of minimizing platform motions to improve the wind turbine’s efficiency. When WECs are integrated into floating wind turbine platforms, it is desirable to avoid resonance to keep the motion responses in check. Hence, it is ideal to have the natural frequencies of the WECs in the higher frequency region beyond 0.2 Hz.

Power absorption of the hybrid systems

The economic feasibility of the hybrid concepts depends on their power absorption capability. The WECs considered in the present study operate on different working principles to capture wave energy. The OWC uses pneumatic pressure developed inside the chamber to generate power, while the torus and PA make use of the heave motion of the WEC body to generate useful power. The PTO system, which is the most crucial component of the WEC that functions to convert wave energy to electricity, is modelled as a linear damper in this study. The PTO damping is kept as a constant value for all three types of WECs for comparing the power absorption. The PTO damping for OWC and Torus is taken as 500 kN s/m, and an equivalent PTO rotational damping of 80 kN m/(rad/s) is assigned to point absorbers. The mean power absorbed by the WECs in the hybrid systems for different sea states is presented in Figs. 19, 20. In general, it can be seen that the power absorbed by the WECs increases with higher sea states. Since a linear PTO model is adopted, the average power absorption increases almost linearly with different sea states. It is also noted that the WECs in the frontline facing the wave direction have slightly higher power absorption compared to the WECs in the hind side, which is due to wave energy dissipation in the frontline.

Conclusion

This study presents the dynamic analysis of a novel semi-submersible floating OWT platform, Septon, combined with seven different combinations of WECs. Three different WECs, namely, OWC, torus, and point absorber, are considered for integration to the floating wind platform. The floating platforms were modelled, and the hydrodynamic coefficients of the hybrid platforms were computed using WAMIT. Thereafter, the coupled dynamic analyses are performed in OpenFAST. The seven different hybrid systems are analysed in irregular sea states to study the motion responses, tower base moments, and mooring tension. The WEC responses were obtained through multi-body analysis carried out in Ansys Aqwa, and the power absorption of the WECs is computed. The main objective of the study is to characterize the effect of different WEC combinations on the floating platform and to identify the ideal combination for the considered WECs.

Correlation analysis between resilience and carbon emissions of resource-based cities based on scenario simulation against the dual carbon background
Authors: Xinyue Fan, Bin Liu, Xiaoyi Yang, Kai Wang, Dan Wu, Zhongli Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractStudying the correlation between resilience and carbon emissions in resource-based cities (RBCs) holds considerable significance for advancing regional sustainable development. Herein, by taking 18 RBCs in southwestern China as the research object, a carbon emission impact index system and an urban resilience index system were constructed. The resilience was quantified from the three dimensions of “resources - ecology - society”, and divided into high, medium, and low categories. Besides, the LSTM model was utilized to simulate carbon peak scenarios in different types of cities and analyze the specific path to achieve low-carbon development. As indicated by the research results, between 2005 and 2020, the resilience and carbon emissions of Southwest China’s RBCs presented fluctuations and showed an upward trend. Scenario simulation suggested that highly resilient cities were poised for sustainable development and necessitated a coordinated government structure; moderately resilient cities should prioritize clean energy development; and cities with low resilience should pursue steady growth, market expansion, and enhanced communication. Overall, this study provides support for the low-carbon development of RBCs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-80416-z.Keywords: Resource-based cities, Carbon peaking, Urban resilience, Scenario analysisSubject terms: Environmental social sciences, Environmental impact

Introduction

Low-carbon development stands as a common goal pursued by humanity to address the energy crisis and climate change1. RBCs are cities dominated by industrial industries such as resource extraction and processing2, also one of the main carriers of CO2 production. According to relevant statistics, about 80% of CO2emissions come from the industrial processes of burning fossil fuels such as coal and oil3. At the same time, RBCs still have significant room for improvement in mitigating CO2emissions due to their resource lock-in effect4, structural defects5, and outdated low-carbon technology development6. China has undertaken numerous initiatives to reduce CO2 emissions. Xi Jinping announced at the 75th Joint Conference that China would strive to reach the peak of CO2emissions by 20307. Therefore, studying the path for RBCs to achieve a “carbon peak” to reach a “carbon peak” is vital for China’s long-term carbon reduction objectives and its international commitment adherence.

In line with the United Nations’ sustainable development goals, reducing carbon emissions is a key strategy for sustainable development, with four of these goals being directly related to “resilience”8. High resilience cities can resist, adapt, and repair, aligning with the low-carbon and sustainable development concepts of current RBCs8. The original meaning of the term “toughness” is “to restore to its original state”9, a concept initially applied in the field of mechanical engineering to reflect the metal dimension10. The continuous evolution of resilience theory has indeed led scholars to apply the concept of “resilience” to the study of urban systems11. Urban resilience is often composed of economic resilience, social resilience, and ecological resilience12. There are mainly two methods for studying urban resilience, namely, the qualitative method and the quantitative method13. The research on urban resilience level is generally conducted using quantitative methods. Quantitative urban resilience assessment is conducted by quantifying indicators with assigned values, which accurately reflect the level of resilience14.

RBCs are one of the main research objects for achieving the “carbon peak” goal. Their single industrial structure, high energy consumption, and high pollutant emissions all make it necessarily important to consider the resilience level of cities when pursuing for the “carbon peak” goal. Hence, the correlation between the resilience level of RBCs and carbon emissions was hereby explored by simulating different scenario types and analyzing the path of low-carbon development for cities with different resilience levels to assist in the green development of RBCs. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: The first part provides an overview of the concepts of RBCs, urban resilience, and low-carbon development; The second part focuses on 18 RBCs in southwestern China, detailing the data, methodologies, and the establishment of a CO2 impact indicator system and an urban resilience level indicator system; The third part employs the CRITIC-based composite index algorithm to quantify the resilience levels of each city across “resources - ecology - society” dimensions, and utilizes the LSTM model to simulate various “carbon peak” scenarios; Sect. 4 concludes the findings of the study, while Sect. 5 offers a discussion.

Overview of the study area and methods

This paper took RBCs in southwestern China as the research object and collected relevant data on CO2 and urban resilience from 2005 to 2020 from a temporal perspective. A CO2 impact indicator system and a resilience indicator system were separately constructed for RBCs, and the resilience level of cities was analyzed from the three dimensions of “resources - ecology - society”. Besides, the LSTM model was employed to simulate the low-carbon development path of different types of cities (Fig. 1).

Research framework.

Step 1: Collected relevant data on CO2 and urban resilience in 18 RBCs in southwestern China from 2005 to 2020;

Step 2: Established separate indicator systems for the impact of urban CO2 and resilience indicators for RBCs;

Step 3: Analyzed changes in the urban resilience level from the three dimensions of “resources - ecology - society”, and classified 18 RBCs according to their resilience level;

Step 4: Utilized the LSTM model to simulate the “carbon peak” time and carbon emissions of different types of cities under seven scenarios;

Step 5: Analyzed the results of various scenarios, and explored the optimal path for different types of RBCs to achieve the goal of “carbon peak”.

Study area

China, the largest developing country, contains numerous RBCs15. According to the relevant regulation of the State Council of China, the National Sustainable Development Plan for RBCs(2013–2020), there are a total of 262 RBCs in China. Due to China’s vast territory, there is a disparity in development between the eastern and western regions, with the western region lagging behind the east. This disparity places RBCs in the western region at a pivotal stage of economic development, rendering them particularly significant for research purposes16. Therefore, RBCs in southwestern China were hereby selected as the research object. Among them, the southwest region of China includes 5 provincial-level administrative areas and 24 RBCs (Fig. 2). The development of ethnic minority areas in China, including Aba Prefecture, Liangshan Prefecture, Qiannan Prefecture, Qiannan Prefecture, and Chuxiong Prefecture, has indeed been relatively slow compared to other regions. By reviewing and comparing existing research, it could be concluded that RBCs, due to their unique characteristics, face problems in multiple dimensions such as resources, ecology, and society. Global climate change significantly impacts the sustainable development of RBCs, posing a major obstacle to their progress. At this point, enhancing the resilience of RBCs against internal and external disturbances in resources, ecology, and society, known as urban resilience, is an important measure to address the challenges of urban development12,17. Herein, a CO2impact index system and an urban resilience index system were constructed, mainly involving elements including population size, urbanization rate, per capita GDP, and the proportion of the secondary industry18–20. These indicators were used as independent variables for the input values of the LSTM model, and CO2 emissions were the dependent variable for the input values of the LSTM model (data source of CO2: https://www.ipe.org.cn/MapLowCarbon/LowCarbon.html?q=5). Methodology

Attempts were hereby made to analyze the correlation between urban resilience and CO2 emissions in RBCs in southwestern China, and to seek the best path for cities to achieve a “carbon peak”. Two methods were primarily used: Firstly, the CRITIC-based composite index algorithm was adopted to calculate the resilience level of each city; Secondly, the LSTM model was employed for simulating the time for RBCs to reach their carbon peak under different scenarios (Fig. 3).

LSTM model

Indeed, the LSTM model is a special time recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture proposed31. This very model is extensively used in processing sequence data and time series prediction tasks32. The LSTM model can effectively handle long-term dependency problems33. In traditional RNNs, when the input sequence is extremely long, the model may encounter gradient vanishing or exploding problems during backpropagation34, making it difficult to learn over long distances. Fortunately, the LSTM model solves this problem by introducing three “gate” structures (forget gate, input gate, and output gate). These gate structures allow the units of the LSTM model to retain or forget information when needed, enabling them to transmit information across multiple time steps and learn long-term dependencies. The cell state in the LSTM model serves as a storage unit for long-term memory, capable of preserving information and transmitting it across time steps to improve prediction accuracy. Results

Analysis of resilience levels in RBCs

In this study, the resilience level of RBCs in Southwest China was mainly characterized by three dimensions of “resources - ecology - society”. The resilience level of RBCs in Southwest China from 2005 to 2020 is shown in Fig. 5, while the resilience levels of 18 cities are shown in Appendix 1. As indicated by the figures, (1) the overall urban resilience level exhibited an upward trajectory. Within this context, the resilience of the resource system initially increased and then declined, while the resilience levels of both ecosystem and social systems demonstrated a gradual upward trend; (2) Among the 18 cities, Chongqing had the highest standard deviation of urban resilience level from 2000 to 2020, which was 0.140, indicating that Chongqing’s urban resilience level was not stable in the development process. The standard deviation of the urban resilience level in Lincang was 0.067, which was the smallest among 18 cities, suggesting relatively stable trend of urban resilience level change in Lincang; (3) The 18 RBCs were further divided into three categories based on their annual average urban resilience levels. Among them, the high resilience cities comprised Guangyuan, Panzhihua, Chongqing, Nanchong, Qujing, and Baoshan; The medium resilience cities included Luzhou, Pu’er, Liupanshui, Lijiang, Zigong, and Guang’an; and the low resilience cities were Ya’an, Dazhou, Lincang, Anshun, Shannan, and Zhaotong.

Scenario analysis

Scenario parameter settings

Based on the CO2 emissions and impact index system of RBCs in southwestern China, the LSTM model was adopted to simulate the scenario of a “carbon peak” in RBCs in China. The development of influencing factor indicators was divided into three situations of high, medium, and low levels based on the long-term planning arrangements and policy measures at different stages in Southwest China. The simulated development status of each influencing factor indicator under different situations was calculated separately. Among them, (1) regarding per capita GDP, according to the 14th Five Year Plan, the GDP of Southwest China is expected to grow at an average annual growth rate of 6.0% from 2020 to 2025, and the average growth rate from 2026 to 2030 will be set at 5.0%. (2) In terms of proportion of the secondary industry, based on the analysis of the proportion of the secondary industry in RBCs in Southwest China, the proportion of the secondary industry is steadily declining. Therefore, it was hereby assumed that the proportion of the secondary industry will decrease by an average of 0.41% per year. (3) Regarding the population size, according to the medium - and long-term population development plan for Southwest China, the average annual growth rate of the population size of RBCs in Southwest China from 2021 to 2025 is expected to be 4.15%, and the average annual growth rate from 2026 to 2030 will be about 2.0%. (4) In terms of urbanization rate, according to the medium - and long-term population development plan for Southwest China, the average annual growth rate of urbanization rate for RBCs in Southwest China is 4.6% from 2021 to 2025 and 2.1% from 2026 to 2030. The specific settings are shown in Appendix 2.

Seven types of scenarios were set in this paper, namely ideal development scenario (where the impact indicators of CO2 are developed according to the highest growth rate), slow development scenario (where the growth rate of all indicators remains the lowest), benchmark development scenario (where the development of all indicators maintains the current speed), weak emission reduction development scenario (where the proportion of the secondary industry is reduced to achieve energy consumption structure optimization and upgrading), steady development scenario (where the economic level is improved and the remaining level remains the same), sustainable development scenario (which mainly emphasizes the quality and sustainability of socio-economic development), and extensive development scenario (where the socio-economic situation shows extensive growth, leading to accelerated resource consumption). Scenario analysis of RBCs in Southwest China

Herein, scenario simulations were conducted using relevant indicators of CO2 influencing factors in RBCs in southwestern China. For the exploration of the optimal model for scenario simulation, several models were compared, including the Genetic Algorithm Neural Network model (GA-BP), Logistic Regression model (LR), Deep Belief Network (DBN), Deep Learning Network (DNN), and Long Short-Term Memory model (LSTM). Besides, the accuracy of these models was judged using the Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), a common metric for evaluating the accuracy of predictions. The results are shown in Fig. 7. By combining the MAPE values of the training and testing sets, it could be concluded that the LSTM model exhibited the lowest MAPE, with a value of 1.85 in the training set and 3.14 in the testing set, highlighting its superior model performance.

Conclusion

Main conclusion

In this study, 18 RBCs in southwestern China were taken as the research objects, and a CO2 impact index system and a resilience indicator system were respectively constructs for RBCs. Meanwhile, the CRITIC-based composite index algorithm was adopted to analyze the resilience level of cities from the three dimensions of “resources - ecology - society”, and the LSTM model was employed to conduct scenario simulation research on the “carbon peak” situation of different types of cities. Furthermore, targeted recommendations were provided for different types of cities based on the analysis results of carbon peaks in different scenarios. The main results are as follows:

(1) From 2005 to 2020, the resilience level of RBCs in Southwest China presented a gradual upward trend. The resilience level across the three dimensions of “resources - ecology - society” indicated that the ecological dimension demonstrated a significant growth rate and was increasingly becoming a crucial component in the overall development level of urban resilience. In the future development process, attention should be paid to the coordinated development of the three dimensions of “resources - ecology - society”. Guangyuan, Panzhihua, and Chongqing presented the highest level of urban resilience, while Anshun, Shannan, and Zhaotong had the lowest level of urban resilience

(2) From 2005 to 2020, the overall CO2 emissions of RBCs in southwestern China showed a fluctuating upward trend. Due to its unique geographical location and sparse population, Shannan exhibited the lowest CO2 emissions compared to other cities. Owing to its swift economic growth and substantial population, Chongqing, a centrally governed municipality and central city of China, had the highest CO2 emissions among the 18 cities. From the perspective of the gradual increase in CO2 emissions, China’s proposed goal of “peak carbon emissions” should be swiftly achieved.

(3) Targeted suggestions were proposed for the development of RBCs in southwestern China by evaluating urban resilience levels and classifying 18 cities into high, medium, and low resilience categories. An investigation was also conducted into the timing of carbon peaks in different types of cities and the path to achieving low-carbon development. As suggested, high resilience cities should pursue a sustainable development scenario; medium resilience cities should aim for an ideal development scenario; and low resilience cities should adhere to a steady development scenario.

Policy recommendations

Based on the above research results and the conclusions of scenario analysis of three types of RBCs, policy recommendations for urban development were correspondingly proposed as follows:

(1) For high resilience cities, the utilization of clean energy should be promoted in subsequent development, and efforts should be made to coordinate government regulation and market regulation. Furthermore, the government’s supervision and control role in the secondary industry should be strengthened, and its guiding role should be further highlighted. Additionally, emphasis should still be placed on improving the carbon trading market, strengthening the rational utilization and allocation of resources in the market, and providing certain policy subsidies for the development of clean energy.

(2) For medium resilience cities, the clean and low-carbon transformation of the energy supply side should be accelerated. In the ongoing development process, there should be a concerted push to expand the scale of clean energy industries, such as hydropower and wind power, and to facilitate the shift in energy structure from fossil fuels to clean energy sources. In high energy-consuming industries such as transportation, construction, and chemical engineering, reform and innovation should be carried out as soon as possible. Meanwhile, the development of energy-saving environmental protection industries should be encouraged while restricting the development of high pollution and high energy-consuming industries.

(3) For low resilience cities, it is essential to broaden market development, foster inter-city communication, maximize the absorption of foreign investment, and encourage technological advancement. While ensuring that resource reserves are not excessively utilized, the development mode of the city should still be adjusted to enhance its economic level.

Discussion

This paper took 18 RBCs in southwestern China as the research object, analyzed the resilience level of cities from three dimensions of “resources - ecology - society”, and explored the best path for different types of cities to achieve “carbon peak”. However, there are still the following shortcomings:

(1) When simulating the “carbon peak” scenario, this paper employed the LSTM model. However, there is room for enhancement in terms of the model’s accuracy and the comprehensiveness of the input indicators used. In future research, the parameter settings of the LSTM model should be continuously optimized to improve the simulation level of the LSTM model35.

(2) When constructing the scenario simulation, judgements were made based on the relevant experience of existing research results. In subsequent research, the dynamic and complex development of cities should be necessarily considered, and long-term tracking of urban carbon peak simulation should be achieved36.

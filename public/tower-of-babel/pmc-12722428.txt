Efficacy, Safety, and Cost-Effectiveness of the Infliximab Biosimilar GP-1111 in Patients with Inflammatory Bowel Disease Who Underwent a Nonmedical Switch: A Prospective Cohort Study
Authors: Máté Pápista, Tamás Resál, Péter Bacsur, Diána Kata, Bernadett Farkas, Anita Bálint, Anna Fábián, Renáta Bor, Emese Ivány, Zoltán Szepes, Klaudia Farkas, Zoltán Juhász, Norbert Buzás, Tamás Molnár
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeData regarding treatment switch from the originator infliximab (IFX) to GP-1111 in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) are limited. Only a few studies have examined the financial aspects of biosimilar use, none of them regarding GP-1111. The study aimed to evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of the IFX biosimilar GP-1111 in patients with IBD who underwent a nonmedical switch from the original IFX in real-life settings. Further, it investigated the switch from a health economics perspective.Patients and MethodsA prospective cohort study was conducted on patients with IBD who were on maintenance IFX treatment and who switched to the IFX biosimilar GP-1111 due to financial constraints, with a 1-year follow-up period. Clinical and laboratory parameters were measured at baseline and at weeks 8, 16, and 52 and serum IFX levels were measured at baseline and at week 24. Statistical analyses were performed using the paired t-test, Pearson’s chi-square test, Kaplan–Meier survival curves, Cox proportional hazard regression models, and univariate linear model.ResultsThis study included 142 patients (95 with Crohn’s disease and 47 with ulcerative colitis). The average serum IFX level remained within the therapeutic range from baseline (3.2 ± 2.3 μg/mL) to week 24 (3.7 ± 2.7 μg/mL, p = 0.106). The corticosteroid-free remission (S-SFR) rates were stable (baseline: 69.7%, follow-up: 72.9%, p = 0.58). The 1-year treatment persistence rate was 83.1%. The adverse events included allergic reactions (IR: 6.3 per 100 patient-years) and paradoxical reactions (IR: 2.4 per 100 patient-years). Based on the estimated cost analysis, biosimilar use could lead to significant savings, with a total of 201.9 million HUF (514,000 €).ConclusionThe IFX biosimilar GP-1111 was safe and effective against IBD in real-world settings. The average serum IFX levels and S-SFR rates remained stable, while no identifiable factors leading to treatment discontinuation were observed. Biosimilar use has a cost advantage with a reassuring safety profile.Keywords: inflammatory bowel disease, infliximab, biosimilar, efficacy, safety, cost-minimization

Introduction

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), such as Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), are immune-mediated chronic inflammatory conditions of the gastrointestinal tract. IBD affects quality of life and possess a significant burden on the healthcare systems,1 with >55,000 patients diagnosed in Hungary and almost 2,500 of them receiving biological therapy as of 2016.2 The exact pathomechanisms are not yet fully elucidated. However, several factors, such as genetic determination, antibiotic use, infections, diet, urban environment, and certain chemical agents, can contribute to IBD.3

The development of biologic drugs produced by living organisms has revolutionized the pharmacological treatment of IBD. It represented a breakthrough in gastroenterology, with targeted inflammatory pathways, such as tumor necrosis factor alpha, interleukins, and integrins.4 Infliximab (IFX) was the first approved biologic molecule against IBD, which is a chimeric human monoclonal antibody targeting and inhibiting tumor necrosis factor alpha. Murine antibodies can trigger an unwanted human anti-murine-antibody immune response. The use of partially human-derived chimeric antibodies reduce the immunogenicity, and improves tolerance, although anti-drug antibodies can still develop.5,6 Further, it has been also approved for various inflammatory conditions including rheumatoid arthritis (RA), ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, and psoriasis.7–9 High cost and financial restrictions limit the use of biological medications. However, the advancement of biosimilar has enabled breakthroughs by lowering economical barriers.10

Biosimilars are biological medications that closely resemble approved biological medicines. Their approval processes are based on significant similarities in terms of structure, biological activity, efficacy, safety, and immunogenicity profiles. Nevertheless, supporting data from studies in other medical fields may be used during approval.10 Real-word data suggest that the efficacy of biosimilars may not be inferior to that of the original ones, and data about safety issues and immunogenicity are also contrasting.11

Some studies have reported that the first IFX biosimilar CT-P13 (InflectraⓇ, RemsimaⓇ) in UC and CD had similar efficacy and safety.12,13 GP-1111 (ZesslyⓇ, IxifiⓇ) is a biosimilar of the original IFX (RemicadeⓇ), which was approved based on data extrapolated from studies primarily conducted on patients with RA and introduced in IBD in 2017.14,15

Several studies have investigated the effects of switching from the originator to CT-P13,16–19 exploring various switch scenarios including multiple20,21 and reverse22,23 switches. However, to date, data on switching from the originator to GP-1111 in IBD are limited.23,24 Whereas, there are numerous studies on RA.25

In addition, although biosimilars are cost-effective and used worldwide, only a few studies support its financial aspects,26–30 and no accurate data on GP-1111 are available in this respect.

The primary aim of the current study was to evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of the GP-1111 biosimilar in patients with IBD after a nonmedical switch from the original IFX in real-life conditions. The secondary aim was to identify factors associated with treatment failure. The tertiary aim was to examine the switch from a health economics perspective.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

A prospective cohort study was conducted at the tertiary referral IBD center of the Department of Medicine, University of Szeged. Consecutive patients with IBD who were on maintenance originator IFX treatment, including all who met the specified criteria, were enrolled. The standard treatment regimen consisted of 5 mg/body kg every 8 weeks, whereas dose escalation to 10 mg/body kg or dose intensification to a 4-weekly schedule was permitted. Baseline was defined as the day of the first application of the biosimilar GP-1111. This study had a 1-year follow-up period. The inclusion criteria were adult patients with IBD who planned to switch to the biosimilar GP-1111 due to financial restrictions. This study was performed in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology31 statement.

Data Collection

Data were collected from March 2020 to June 2021, and a subsequent analysis was performed in 2024. Data on the demographic characteristics and clinical and laboratory parameters were collected throughout the study period during regular visits. The parameters evaluated on the day of the first administration of the biosimilar were considered as baseline data. Data on clinical and laboratory parameters were collected at baseline and at weeks 8, 16, and 52 and assessed. To reduce potential bias and non-differential misclassification, a conservative washout period was set at week 24 to assess the pharmacokinetics of the biosimilar IFX. Therefore, the baseline serum levels at week 0 was compared with those at week 24 using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (RidaScreen IFX®, R-Biopharm, Pfungstadt, Germany) within the 8-week period after sampling, as per the manufacturer’s protocol. Adverse events were recorded during the follow-up period. Data were obtained from a regular, uniformized database. The information on the wholesale price of the original and biosimilar products was obtained from the National Health Insurance Fund of Hungary Public Pharmacy Directory.32 The prices of the products were given in Hungarian Forints (HUF), and the Euro (€) prices were calculated based on the National Bank of Hungary middle rate of September 2, 2024 (392.75 HUF/€).33

Outcome Measurements

Our primary outcome was change in the serum IFX level from baseline to week 24. The secondary outcomes were sustained corticosteroid-free remission rate (S-SFR), treatment persistence, loss of response (LOR) rate, and development of major adverse events measured at the end of the follow-up of each patient. SFR was defined as remission in clinical activity (CD activity index34 <150 or partial Mayo score35 <2), absence of concomitant corticosteroid use, and C-reactive protein [CRP36] level <5 mg/L. LOR was defined as the need for dose escalation to 10 mg/body weight kg or dose intensification to a 4-weekly schedule during the follow-up. A major adverse event was characterized as an adverse event leading to treatment cessation or hospital admission. Adverse events were reported using the incidence rates (IR, event per patient-year).

Cost-Effectiveness Analysis

We assumed that due to the nearly identical efficacy and side effect profiles of the two drugs, the direct non-healthcare and indirect healthcare costs were similar in the two groups. Therefore, they were not examined. As the costs of two drugs with identical health benefits were compared, the cost-effectiveness calculation was simplified to a cost-minimization analysis.

Statistical Analysis

Data on the demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients were collected to describe our cohort and to adjust our analysis on potential covariates and confounders. Data were obtained from the medical record systems. Statistical tests were performed using the STATA software version 9.0. Descriptive statistics are expressed as the mean and standard deviation variables and the median and IQR. Meanwhile, categorical variables were presented as the number of items and their frequencies as percentages. The distribution of continuous or numeric data were assessed using visual interpretations (using Q-Q plots and histograms). Changes in serum IFX levels were assessed using the paired t-test and the Mann–Whitney U-test, and sustained SFR rates were assessed using the Pearson’s chi-square test. The Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed to evaluate treatment persistence, and the Cox proportional hazard regression models including potential covariates on this outcome were established. The goodness-of-fit of the model was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. In addition, independent analyses were performed to control for potential confounders and covariates on this particular outcome. We estimated the beta coefficient and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for each predictor variable. A two-sided alpha level of 0.05 indicated statistically significant differences. To perform the mathematical calculations, the prices of the drugs in the database and the number of patients receiving normal and escalated/intensified doses were used.

Ethical Consideration

The current study was approved by the Regional and Institutional Human Medical Biological Research Ethics Committee of University of Szeged (approval no.: 4743). It was conducted according to the guidelines stipulated in the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki, 6th revision, 2008. The participants provided a written informed consent for both regular healthcare and participation in the study.

Results

Clinical and Demographic Overview

Our prospective cohort included 142 patients (male 77, 54.2%; median age: 43.1 years). Among them, 95 (66.9%) presented with CD and 47 (33.1%) with UC. The median disease duration was >10 years, and the patients were followed-up with a median of 50.9 (interquartile range [IQR]: 49–56) weeks. Approximately 47.9% of the patients with CD had luminal disease. Meanwhile, the distribution of different localizations in the small and large intestines was almost similar. About 25% of patients with CD presented with a previous history of intestinal resection. In the UC cohort, most patients had at least left-sided colitis. Meanwhile, 6.5% of the patients previously underwent colectomy. A total of 57 patients (40.0%) were treated with a combination of IFX and azathioprine (AZA) 25 patients (17.6%) had a history of prior biological treatment, all of whom had received ADA, while the rest of them were biologic-naive. Treatment Efficacy

The average serum IFX level was within the therapeutic range37,38 at both baseline (3.2 ± 2.3 μg/mL) and at week 24 (3.7 ± 2.7 μg/mL) and did not change significantly (p = 0.106, Figure 1). The level was also within the therapeutic range37,38 and did not change significantly in the standard treatment regimen subgroup at baseline 2.9 ± 2.5 μg/mL at week 24 3.2 ± 3.2 μg/mL (p = 0.138) and in the escalated treatment regimen subgroup at baseline 5.0 ± 3.6 μg/mL at week 24 5.2 ± 3.5 μg/mL (p = 0.529). Additionally, no significant difference in serum IFX level change was observed between patients with and without concomitant AZA therapy (p = 0.335).

Serum IFX levels measured at baseline and at week 24 (p = 0.106).

At baseline, 99 (69.7%) of 142 patients were in S-SFR (UC: 35 [74.5%] of 47, CD: 64 [67.4%] of 95). Meanwhile, the ratio did not change until the end of the follow-up (overall: 86 [72.9%] of 118, p = 0.58; UC: 28 [77.8%] of 36, p = 0.73; CD: 58 [70.7%] of 82, p = 0.63; Figure 2).

Proportion of patients who achieved sustained corticosteroid-free remission at week 52 (patients with CD: p = 0.63, patients with UC: p = 0.73).

At baseline, 35 (24.6%) patients were on an escalated treatment regimen. During the study period, seven patients discontinued treatment due to disease activity (n = 5) and paradoxical reactions (n = 2). After the switch, 13 new cases of LOR were recorded, which account for 12.1% of the remaining cohort. Four of them discontinued treatment, due to disease activity (n = 3), and noncompliance with the therapy (n = 1). (Figure 3). In the secondary LOR group serum IFX levels were 2.6 ± 2.1 μg/mL at baseline and 2.9 ± 3.1 μg/mL at week 24 (p = 0.501). The differences were not significant, compared to the overall cohort (p = 0.485), to patients without LOR (p = 0.707) at baseline, or at week 24 (p = 0.514 and p = 0.818 respectively).

Of the 142 patients enrolled, 118 continued the study through the end of the 1-year follow-up period, while 24 discontinued treatment, resulting in a treatment persistence rate of 83.1%, with a median duration of 50.9 (IQR: 49–56) weeks (Figure 4). Half of the patients discontinued their treatment due to safety events. Almost one-third (29.2%) of the ceased patients discontinued treatment due to LOR. Meanwhile, in two (8.3%) patients whose treatment was discontinued due to severe disease activity, the dose was not increased and, therefore, did not meet our criteria for LOR. In addition, two participants did not comply with the therapy. In one patient who was pregnant, IFX was discontinued at week 17. There was no association found between the treatment duration and clinical activity (CD activity index −β coefficient: 0.01 [95% CI: from −0.04 to 0.06]; partial Mayo score −β coefficient: −2.77 [95% CI: from −5.88 to 0.33]); CRP level −β coefficient: −0.03 [95% CI: from −0.46 to 0.39]); or azathioprine use −β coefficient: 1.71 [95% CI: from −3.42 to 6.84]).

Safety Concerns

During the follow-up period (overall 126.2 patient-years), there were eight cases of allergic reactions to IFX (IR 6.3 per 100 patient-years) and three cases of paradoxical reactions (IR 2.4 per 100 patient-years), including itching, exfoliative skin lesions, vasculitis, and polyarthritis. Therapy was discontinued in one patient who presented with malaise. Although, all safety issues led to treatment discontinuation, other major adverse events were not recorded during the follow-up period.

Economic Analysis

The majority of the direct healthcare costs are related to the cost of the drug, as IFX is administered to patients in the outpatient setting. Infliximab is available in prefilled 100-mg vials for each formulation. Data on the exact body weight of the patients were not available. Therefore, for simplicity, an average adult body weight of 70 kg was used. In this case, the standard dose of infliximab was 350 mg, and the escalated or intensified dose was 700 mg. The price of the original infliximab vial was 153,549 HUF (391.0 €), and the price of the biosimilar was 97,120 HUF (247.3 €) based on the 2024 prices. In a patient with an average weight, the costs were 537,421.5 HUF (1,368.4 €) for the original product at the normal dose, 339,920 HUF (865.5 €) for the biosimilar, 1,074,843 HUF (2,736.7 €) for the original at increased dose, and 679,840 HUF (1,731.0 €) for the biosimilar at increased dose. The total drug cost during follow-up was 347.5 million HUF (885,000 €). Meanwhile, if the original drug had been used, the total drug cost was 549.4 million HUF (1,400,000 €). The cost difference was 201.9 million HUF (514,000 €), with a total of 1.42 million HUF (3,600 €) per patient.

Discussion

The current study primarily aimed to assess the long-term efficacy and safety of the GP-1111 biosimilar in patients with IBD who have switched from the original IFX in the real-world setting. Further, it aimed to identify any factors contributing to treatment failure. A prospective cohort study with a 52-week follow-up period was conducted. Data on the demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients and laboratory parameters were collected at regular visits, with the additional measurement of IFX levels twice. Based on our results, the pharmacokinetics did not change during the switch from the original IFX to the biosimilar. The clinical activity and the rate of S-SFR did not change in our cohort. The biosimilar was confirmed to be safe, and none of the patients developed severe adverse events.

Switching to biosimilar is a well-documented phenomenon, with substantial studies collectively involving over 6,000 patients across various indications.39 However, data on the switch specifically to the IFX biosimilar GP-1111 are limited, with only one study on IBD in pediatric patients21 and another study subjectively evaluating the efficacy of the biosimilar.24 Most existing data originate from studies on rheumatologic conditions,40 or those involving switching from other biosimilar, non-originator formulations, or partial transitions to GP-1111.19 Further, most research is about a different IFX biosimilar, predominantly CT-P13,20 with follow-up periods rarely extending to 1 year.12 In addition, data specific to IBD are limited, and some findings are extrapolated from other diseases, thereby complicating the accurate assessment of the long-term efficacy and safety of biosimilars in IBD.12

In our study, the primary endpoint was the change in serum IFX levels after the switch. Results showed that the drug levels remained stable at week 24 compared with that at baseline, indicating that the pharmacokinetics of the GP-1111 biosimilar are comparable with the originator in real-world settings. Similarly, other studies on the biosimilar have reported stable drug levels.19 For example, a study investigating the transition to multiple biosimilars found comparable drug levels at the endpoint at week 24 after the second switch (4.3 vs 4.9 µg/mL, p = 0.07).20 Avouac et al also examined the pharmacokinetics of the biosimilar after switching. They showed no difference in drug levels between baseline and the final visit in cohorts including patients with IBD and rheumatologic conditions such as RA and axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA).19

In our cohort, at the end of a 1-year follow-up period, 72.9% of the patients achieved sustained steroid-free clinical remission, including 77.8% of the UC cohort and 70.7% of the CD cohort, with remission rates remaining stable throughout the follow-up period. This outcome is in accordance with the findings of other studies. For example, the previously mentioned study analyzing the GP-1111 biosimilar in pediatric IBD showed persisting remission rates at weeks 14 and 52.23 In addition, Avouac et al utilized a combined scoring system to evaluate remission rates across various diseases and observed no significant changes in remission within any specific disease groups between the baseline and final visit. However, patients with axSpA had stable scores on subjective disease activity measures, such as the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index and the Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Score.19

In our cohort, the 1-year drug retention rate was 83.1%, and the median treatment duration was 50.9 weeks. Of all treatment discontinuations, 33% were due to LOR, and one patient discontinued because of severe disease activity. In addition, treatment was discontinued in two patients due to noncompliance and one due to pregnancy. Predictive factors for treatment discontinuation were not identified. Dipasquale et al performed a study involving children. Their study showed a 1-year persistence rate of 90.8% and a 2-year follow-up persistence rate of 75.7%. The reasons for discontinuation were LOR in nine (31%) cases and stable remission in three (10%).23 Avouac et al assessed an endpoint for the third treatment, not a 1-year endpoint for persistence. These data were similar to the 24-week persistence (89.4%) of the whole cohort (85%) in our study and in patients with IBD, uveitis, and neurological inflammation (94%). A significant difference was found between the drug retention rates of rheumatology and internal medicine patients in the study by Avouac et al (81% vs 94%, p = 0.007), which may indicate a bias in the data obtained via extrapolation. Notably, in this study, a significant proportion of patients discontinued their treatment due to LOR (n = 47, 80%). Further, six (10%) patients did not comply with the therapy, and one female patient with CD discontinued her treatment due to pregnancy. Further, the majority of patients in this study did not switch to another agent (n = 7, 12%), but rather to the original IFX (80%), and five (8%) patients did not receive any biological therapy after discontinuation.19 This finding was also observed in the study conducted by Sarlós et al on most patients treated with the ADA biosimilar who preferred to return to the original formulation.24 This phenomenon, as reported in the previous studies, was generally attributed to subjective deterioration of the patients’ condition rather than a pharmacological cause. However, reversal switch was not observed in our cohort.19

In our study, there were 12 cases of significant adverse events (IR: 9.52/100 patient-years), all of which led to treatment discontinuation. These were allergic reactions to IFX (n = 8) (IR: 6.3/100 patient-years), paradoxical reactions (n = 3) (IR: 2.4/100 patient-years), and malaise (n = 1). In the study of Dipasquale, nine patients had adverse reactions (IR: 6.13/100 patient-years), of whom seven required treatment discontinuation. The adverse reactions were acute infusion reaction (allergy; IR: 3.4/100 patient-years) in five cases, paradoxical reaction (psoriasis; IR: 1.36/100 patient-years) in two, malaise (IR: 0.68/100 patient-years) in one, and elevated pancreatic enzyme levels (IR: 0.68/100 patient-years) in one.23 In addition, in a previous study on patients with chronic inflammatory disease, only five patients required treatment discontinuation due to purpura (two patients with CD), infusion reaction (one with axSpA), diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma (one with axSpA), and transient liver enzyme elevation that recovered after treatment discontinuation (one with axSpA).19 In the previously mentioned study on the subjective symptoms of the patients, 58 (55%) patients who were treated with IFX reported some adverse reactions, most of which were early (n = 8, 14%) or late (n = 18, 32%) allergic reactions.24

In their review, Farkas and Molnár summarized the findings on the first IFX biosimilar, CT-P13, in patients with IBD. Their research showed that the biosimilar was not only effective in the registration trials involving rheumatologic diseases but also possessed similar pharmacological properties to the originator IFX in patients with IBD. Across studies, the changes in S-SFR at the 1-year endpoint were either nonsignificant or comparable to those in the originator IFX. However, data about persistence or LOR were not reported. Evidence suggests that the side effect profile of CT-P13 closely resembles to that of the IFX formulation.12 Smits et al conducted a prospective study similar to ours, which assessed the switch to CT-P13 in a relatively large cohort (n = 83). They found no significant changes in disease activity or the levels of biochemical markers such as CRP and calprotectin. Interestingly, an increase in serum IFX levels was noted between baseline and the endpoint at week 16. Six patients exhibited minor adverse events. However, serious adverse events were not observed. Additionally, treatment was discontinued in five patients.17 Schmitz et al also conducted a study on a cohort whose demographic characteristics were similar to ours. Blood samples were collected before switching from the original IFX to CT-P13, and before doses 2, 4, and 7, to measure IFX serum levels, CRP levels, and red blood cell counts and to assess clinical disease activity. After the switch, neither IFX levels nor clinical and biochemical activity changed significantly, and the proportion of patients in remission remained stable throughout the follow-up period. The therapy was discontinued in 35 cases, primarily because of side effects and disease activity. However, the latter was often based on subjective assessments by patients rather than clinical data.16

In addition, our data can be compared with findings from the early IFX originator studies, specifically the ACCENT I and ACT 1 and 2 trials. This is because these studies have common endpoints and have a comparable follow-up period (54 weeks).7,9 The ACCENT I trial measured IFX serum levels, which remained stable throughout the study.7 In the ACT trials, the infliximab groups had significantly higher proportions of patients achieving sustained clinical response or remission compared with the placebo groups, with remission rates of 15%–26%.9 Further, in the ACCENT I study, the treatment groups (39% and 45%) had higher remission rates at week 30 than the placebo group (21%). This trend persisted at week 54, with the treatment groups presenting with remission rates of approximately 30%–40%.7 Notably, the remission rates were significantly higher in our study and in other recent ones. The cause of this discrepancy based on the available data remains unclear. The adverse events reported in these earlier studies, which included multiple deaths, malignancies, tuberculosis infections, and neurological complications, were more severe.7,9 Based on this information, the IFX biosimilar GP-1111 may exhibit at least comparable biochemical and pharmacological characteristics to the originator agent in real-world scenarios.

Our results can also be interpreted from a health-economic perspective. Our study showed significant savings (approximately half a million € [over 200 million HUF] in the Gastroenterology Department at the Clinical Center, University of Szeged). Considering the size of the overall population with IBD39 and the number of gastroenterology centers in Hungary, these savings could potentially amount to billions of HUF (millions of €). This finding is in accordance with that of previous analyses on biosimilar. Du et al had evaluated Chinese randomized controlled trials in 2024. The prices of biosimilars are 63–82% lower than those of the originators, and the savings were $874–$2,184 per month per patient.26 Brodszki et al conducted a study using all patients taking biosimilar and theoretical protocols from several countries. Results showed that for over 3 years, when the biosimilar price was 75% of that of the originator, a total savings of 17 million € was achieved, amounting to 1,200 €/patient/year.27 Rencz et al examined European studies on luminal CD from a cost-utility perspective, across multiple biological therapies. Their results primarily showed that biosimilar IFX is the most cost-effective of them all.30 Baji et al investigated the cost-efficacy of conventional and biological therapies for fistulizing CD in Europe. Based on their results, the biosimilar IFX is the most cost-effective when only one agent is used.28 Bashir et al summarized the studies on IFX biosimilar. They found no general trend. In some cases, the costs have increased. However, there have also been changes in the price of the originator with the introduction of biosimilar in some countries.29

The current study had some limitations. Due to mandatory switching, we could not establish a control group and had to rely solely on baseline measurements within the cohort. Certain potentially relevant clinical parameters, such as albumin, body mass index, and calprotectin were not collected. These factors limited our ability to control for potential confounders. The direct and indirect total costs were not presented because data were not available due to both ethical and data privacy reasons or they would have been disproportionately burdensome to access. In principle, the current study aimed to examine the cost of medicines and the savings that can be achieved, not to perform a total cost analysis. Nonetheless, our study also had strengths. In particular, it represents the first real-world prospective investigation into switching to GP-1111 in patients with IBD, with analysis conducted on a relatively large cohort. Regression methods were used to control for potential confounders impacting outcomes, and we aimed to decrease interference from preexisting anti-IFX antibodies by implementing a conservative washout period. Although including a control group could have strengthened the methodological robustness, the prospective comparison to baseline serves as an adequate and informative approach for assessing outcomes. In addition, a similar health-economic analysis has not yet been conducted on patients with IBD in the national literature. The results are valuable for both domestic and international healthcare systems, contributing to the development of therapeutic protocols and improving the drug pricing system. The lack of total cost analysis did not affect the main findings of the study and the conclusions drawn.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the IFX biosimilar GP-1111 was safe and effective against IBD in real-world settings. The average serum IFX levels remained within the therapeutic range both at baseline and at week 24, and they did not significantly change. The S-SFR rate remained consistent throughout the follow-up period, and no identifiable factors leading to treatment discontinuation were observed. Biosimilar use has a significant cost advantage with a reassuring safety profile. However, further studies with control groups could provide more substantial data.

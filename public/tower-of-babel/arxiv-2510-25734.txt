Ramsey Numbers in Kneser Graphs
Emily Heath, Grace McCourt, Alex Parker, Coy Schwieder, Shira Zerbib

Abstract
We define the r r -Kneser Ramsey number R r KG ​ ( s , t ) R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t) as the minimum integer n n such that every red/blue edge-coloring of the Kneser graph KG ​ ( n , r ) \textrm{KG}(n,r) contains a red s s -clique or a blue t t -clique. We obtain general bounds on the numbers R r KG ​ ( s , t ) R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t) , and make progress on two related Ramsey-type problems, one raised by Holmsen, Hrusak, and Roldán-Pensado, and the other posted by Pálvölgyi.



1
Introduction

The
Ramsey number

R(s,t)
is the minimum integer
n
such that any red/blue edge-coloring of the complete graph
K_{n}
contains either a red
K_{s}
or a blue
K_{t}
. Ramsey numbers and their many different variations have been extensively studied, and they form one of the main pillars of extremal combinatorics. In this paper we consider a variation on Ramsey numbers in which the host graph is a Kneser graph rather than the complete graph. The
Kneser graph

\textrm{KG}(n,r)
is the graph whose vertex set is the set
{[n]\choose r}
of all
r
-subsets of
[n]
, and two
r
-sets form an edge if they are disjoint. We define the
Kneser Ramsey numbers
as follows.

Definition 1
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer. The
r
-Kneser Ramsey number

R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)
is the minimum integer
n
such that every red/blue edge-coloring of
\textrm{KG}(n,r)
contains a red
K_{s}
or a blue
K_{t}
.

Note that
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{1}(s,t)=R(s,t)
, so this definition generalizes the notion of Ramsey numbers.

Our motivation to investigate Kneser Ramsey numbers originated with the following fascinating Ramsey-type question, which was asked by Michael Hrusak for
\alpha=1/2
and then by Andreas Holmsen and Edgardo Roldán-Pensado for all
\alpha

[
4
]
:

Problem 2

(Holmsen - Hrusak - Roldán-Pensado)
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1)
and an integer
t>1
. Does there exist a large enough
n
(depending on
\alpha
and
t
) such that in every red/blue edge-coloring of the complete graph on vertex set
V={[n]\choose\alpha n}
there is a monochromatic clique on vertex set
U\subset V
, with the property that
the vertex-covering number of the hypergraph
([n],U)
is at least
t
?

Although Problem
2
is not about Kneser graphs, one observes that a positive answer will follow if in every red/blue edge-coloring of
\textrm{KG}(n,\alpha n)
there is a monochromatic clique of size
t
. That is, if
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{\alpha n}(t,t)\leq n
for some
n
, then we get a positive answer to Problem
2
.

However, Problem
2
is interesting even when
\textrm{KG}(n,\alpha n)
is empty.
In fact, Holmsen and Roldán-Pensado were most interested in the special case when
\alpha=k/(2k-1)
and
t=2
, as formulated below.

Problem 3
.

Let
V={[2k-1]\choose k}
. Is it true that for some large enough
k
, in every red/blue edge-coloring of the complete graph on vertex set
V
there is a monochromatic triangle
ABC
with
A,B,C\in V
and
A\cap B\cap C=\emptyset
?

Perhaps a more approachable problem is the following.

Problem 4
.

Determine the smallest number
\beta>1
such that for some large enough
k
, in every red/blue edge-coloring of the complete graph on vertex set
V={[\beta k]\choose k}
there is a monochromatic triangle
ABC
with
A,B,C\in V
and
A\cap B\cap C=\emptyset
.

It is easy to see that
\beta\leq 3
. Indeed, let
S_{1},\dots,S_{6}\in{[3k]\choose k}
be six sets with the property that for every
t\in[3k]
,
t
belongs to exactly two of the sets
S_{1},\dots,S_{6}
. (It is easy to construct such sets
S_{1},\dots,S_{6}
: take the six distinct sets in two disjoint perfect matchings in the
k
-uniform hypergraph on vertex set
[3k]
.) Now consider a red/blue edge-coloring of the complete graph on vertices
S_{1},\dots,S_{6}
. Since
R(3,3)=6
, this graph contains a monochromatic triangle
S_{i}S_{j}S_{k}
, and
S_{i}\cap S_{j}\cap S_{k}=\emptyset
for every distinct
i,j,k\in[6]
.

In this paper we make progress on Problems
2
and
4
in two different ways. First, in Section
2
we improve the upper bound on
\beta
to
7/3
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
k\geq 12
be an integer divisible by 6. Then in every red/blue edge-coloring of the complete graph on vertex set
V={[\frac{7}{3}k]\choose k}
there is a monochromatic triangle
ABC
with
A,B,C\in V
and
A\cap B\cap C=\emptyset
.

Second, in the same section, we give a positive answer to Problem
2
when
\alpha<1/4
and
t=3
.

Theorem 6
.

For any
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
, there exists some large enough
n
such that any red/blue edge-coloring of
\textrm{KG}(n,\alpha n)
contains a monochromatic triangle.

Note that for
\alpha=\frac{1}{4}
, for
n=8
there exists a red/blue edge-coloring of
\textrm{KG}(n,\alpha n)
with no monochromatic triangle, as implied by Theorem
12
.

We then continue with a systematic study of Kneser Ramsey numbers for various parameters
r,s,t
.
The following trivial upper bound on the
r
-Kneser Ramsey number follows from Ramsey’s theorem:

R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)\leq R(s,t)\cdot r.

Indeed, when
n\geq R(s,t)\cdot r
, the graph
\textrm{KG}(n,r)
contains a clique of size
R(s,t)
, and any red/blue edge-coloring of it contains a red
K_{s}
or a blue
K_{t}
. This in particular shows the existence of the number
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)
. Note also that if
n<kr
, then
\textrm{KG}(n,r)
does not contain a clique of size
k
, and thus we have a trivial lower bound

R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)\geq r\cdot\max\{s,t\}.

In order to prove Theorem
6
, we prove an auxiliary result (Theorem
17
) that in particular implies the following bound on
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)
.

Theorem 7
.

Fix
s,t\geq 3
and suppose
R(s-1,t)\geq R(s,t-1)
. Set
M=R(s-1,t)
and
m=R(s,t-1)
.
We have

R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)\leq(M+1)r+m-1.

In particular,

R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,s)\leq(R(s,s-1)+1)r+R(s,s-1)-1.

In Section
3
we establish improved upper bounds on
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)
for certain families of
s,t
.

Theorem 8
.

For
s\geq 4
, we have

R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s-1,s)\leq rR(s-2,s)+2(R(s-2,s-1)-1)+2r.

Theorem 9
.

For
t\geq 5
, we have
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{2}(3,t)\leq 2(R(3,t-2))+2t+1.

We then derive a general lower bound, and an improved lower bound for
s=t=3
and
r\geq 3
.

Proposition 10
.

For
r\geq 1
and
s,t\geq 2
, we have
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)\geq R(s,t)+2r-2.

Theorem 11
.

For
r\geq 2
, we have
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(3,3)\geq 3r+2.

Using a computer code, we also obtain an exact value on
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{2}(3,3)
. The computational proof of the next theorem is discussed in Subsection
3.3
.

Theorem 12
.

We have
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{2}(3,3)=9
and
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{3}(3,3)\leq 13
.

. Here an induced

\textrm{KG}(n,r)
induced on vertex set
{S\choose r}
, where
S\subset[n]
is a set of size
3r
.

Problem 13

(Pálvölgyi
[
6
]
)
.

For
r>1
, determine the smallest
N\geq 3r
such that in any red/blue edge-coloring of
\textrm{KG}(N,r)
there exists either a red induced
\textrm{KG}(3r,r)

In Section

N
in Pálvölgyi’s problem.
For integers
r,s,t
, let
R_{r}(s,t)

-uniform hypergraph on

Theorem 14
.

We have
N\geq R_{r}(3r,3r)
. That is, for
n=R_{r}(3r,3r)-1
, there is a red/blue coloring of
\textrm{KG}(n,r)
containing no red induced
\textrm{KG}(3r,r)
and no blue triangle.

For example, for

, as

Theorem 15

(Alon - Frankl - Lovász
[

Let
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq\dots\geq k_{t}\geq 2
and suppose

. Let
F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{t}
be a partition of

contains

recovers the chromatic number of the Kneser graph. When

F_{2}

The Holmsen - Hrusak - Roldán-Pensado problem

We start with the proof of Theorem
5
, showing that
7/3
is an upper bound on
\beta
in Problem
4
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.
Let
V=[3k/2]\cup T
, where
T
is a set of
5k/6
vertices disjoint from
[3k/2]
. Then
|V|=7k/3
. Suppose we have a red/blue edge-coloring of the complete graph
G
on
{V\choose k}
.

Consider the triangle
S_{1}S_{2}S_{3}
in
G
, where

S_{1}=\{1,\dots,k\}
,

S_{2}=\{1,\dots,k/2\}\cup\{k+1,\dots,3k/2\}
, and

S_{3}=\{k/2+1,\dots,3k/2\}
.
Since
S_{1}\cap S_{2}\cap S_{3}=\emptyset
, if
S_{1}S_{2}S_{3}
is monochromatic we are done. Otherwise, it must have a red edge and a blue edge. Without loss of generality,

S_{1}S_{2}
is red, and

S_{1}S_{3}
is blue.
Note that
S_{1}\cap S_{i}\subset[k]
for all
i
.

Let
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
be pairwise disjoint subsets of
\{k+1,\dots,3k/2\}
of size
k/6
each. Let
F_{i}=T\cup T_{i}
for
i\in[3]
.

Assume first that the triangle
F_{1}F_{2}F_{3}
is monochromatic. If it is monochromatic blue, then consider the induced graph of
G
on vertex set
\{S_{1},S_{2},F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
.
Observe that if either
S_{1}
or
S_{2}
has at least two blue edges to vertices in
\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
, then we get a blue triangle of the form
S_{i}F_{j}F_{l}
, and since

S_{i}\cap F_{j}\cap F_{l}=S_{i}\cap T=\emptyset
,
we are done.
Otherwise, both
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
have at least two red edges to vertices in
\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
, so by the pigeonhole principle we get a red triangle of the form
S_{1}S_{2}F_{i}
. But now

(S_{1}\cap S_{2}\cap F_{i})\subset([k]\cap F_{i})=\emptyset
,
and we are done.
If the triangle
F_{1}F_{2}F_{3}
is monochromatic red, the argument is symmetric when we consider instead the induced graph on vertex set
\{S_{1},S_{3},F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
.

So suppose the triangle
F_{1}F_{2}F_{3}
is not monochromatic. Then without loss of generality
F_{1}F_{2}
is a red edge and
F_{1}F_{3}
is a blue edge.

Consider the induced graph on vertex set

\{H_{1},\dots,H_{6}\}
, where
H_{i}=[k-1]\cup\{k+i-1\}
for
i\in[6]
.
Since
R(3,3)=6
, this graph has a monochromatic triangle, say
H_{1}H_{2}H_{3}
. Note that
H_{i}\cap H_{j}=[k-1]
for
i\neq j
. Moreover, since
k/2\geq 6
we have
H_{i}\cap T=\emptyset
for all
i\in[6]
.

If the triangle
H_{1}H_{2}H_{3}
is monochromatic blue, then consider the induced graph of
G
on vertex set
\{F_{1},F_{2},H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
.
Observe that if
F_{1}
or
F_{2}
has at least two blue edges to vertices in
\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
, then we get a blue triangle of the form
F_{i}H_{j}H_{l}
, and since

F_{i}\cap H_{j}\cap H_{l}=F_{i}\cap[k-1]=\emptyset
,
we are done.
Otherwise, both
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
have at least two red edges to vertices in
\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
, so by the pigeonhole principle we get a red triangle of the form
F_{1}F_{2}H_{i}
. But now

(F_{1}\cap F_{2}\cap H_{i})\subset(T\cap H_{i})=\emptyset
,
and again we are done.

Finally, if the triangle
H_{1}H_{2}H_{3}
is monochromatic red, we have a symmetric argument by considering instead the induced graph on vertex set
\{F_{1},F_{3},H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
.
∎

Our next goal is to prove Theorem
6
, which gives a positive answer to Problem
2
in the case
\alpha<1/4
and
t=3
. In order to prove the theorem, we need the following auxiliary result.

Theorem 17
.

Fix
s,t\geq 3
and suppose
R(s-1,t)\geq R(s,t-1)
. Set
M=R(s-1,t)
and
m=R(s,t-1)
. Let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{M+1}
and set
\alpha=\frac{1}{M+1}-\varepsilon
. Then, there exists some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\textrm{KG}(n,\alpha n)
contains a red
K_{s}
or a blue
K_{t}
.

Proof of Theorem
17
.

Fix
r\geq 2
and set
N-r=Mr+m-1
. First, we will show that in any red/blue coloring of
G=\textrm{KG}(N,r)
, there is a red
K_{s}
or a blue
K_{t}
. Suppose for contradiction that there is some coloring
c:E(G)\to\{R,B\}
of
G
without a red
K_{s}
or blue
K_{t}
. Let
v=[N]\setminus[N-r]\in V(G)
. Then,
\binom{[N-r]}{r}=\binom{[Mr+m-1]}{r}
can be partitioned into
N_{R}(v)
and
N_{B}(v)
. Since
c
contains no red
K_{s}
and no blue
K_{t}
, then
G[N_{R}(v)]
contains no red
K_{s-1}
and no blue
K_{t}
. Similarly,
G[N_{B}(v)]
contains no red
K_{s}
and no blue
K_{t-1}
. In particular,
G[N_{R}(v)]
contains no
K_{M}
and
G[N_{B}(v)]
contains no
K_{m}
. However, by Theorem
16
, setting
F_{1}=N_{R}(v)
and
F_{2}=N_{B}(v)
, either
N_{R}(v)
contains a clique of size
M
or
N_{B}(v)
contains a clique of size
m
, a contradiction.

For the general case, fix
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{M+1}
and set
\alpha=\frac{1}{M+1}-\varepsilon
. Choose
r\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\frac{r}{(M+1)r+m-1}\geq\alpha
. Set
N-r=Mr+m-1
and
n=\frac{r}{\alpha}
. Then, by choice of
r
, observe that
N\leq n
. Therefore, since
\textrm{KG}(N,\alpha n)\subseteq\textrm{KG}(n,\alpha n)
, any red-blue coloring of
\textrm{KG}(n,\alpha n)
contains a red
K_{s}
or a blue
K_{t}
.
∎

Note that the general bound on
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s,t)
in Theorem
7
is proven in the first paragraph of the proof of Theorem
17
above.

6
.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

We apply Theorem
17
with
s=t=3
. This gives
M=m=R(2,3)=3
. For any
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{4}
, we have
\alpha=\frac{1}{4}-\varepsilon
, and by Theorem
17
there exists some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\alpha n\in\mathbb{N}
such that any red/blue coloring of the edges of
\textrm{KG}(n,\alpha n)
contains a monochromatic triangle.
∎

3.1
Upper bounds

Proof of Theorem
8
.

We would like to show that for
s\geq 4
,

R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s-1,s)\leq rR(s-2,s)+2(R(s-2,s-1)-1)+2r.

Let
N=rR(s-2,s)+2R(s-2,s-1)+2r
,
G=\textrm{KG}(N,r)
and let
c:E(G)\to\{R,B\}
be a red/blue edge-coloring of
G
. For sake of contradiction, suppose there is no red
K_{s-1}
nor blue
K_{s}
. If there is no blue edge in
G
, then we are done. So, set
u=\{N,N-1,\dots,N-r+1\}\in V(G)
and
v=\{N-r,N-r-1,\dots,N-2r+1\}\in V(G)
. Without loss of generality, suppose
c(u,v)=B
. Then,
N(u)\cap N(v)\cong G^{\prime}=\textrm{KG}(N-2r,r)
. We will partition the vertices of
G^{\prime}
into 4 sets. For
(X,Y)\in\{(R,R),(R,B),(B,R),(B,B)\}
, we denote by
N_{XY}
the set of vertices
w
in
G^{\prime}
such that
c(w,u)=X
and
c(w,v)=Y
.

Observe that
N_{BR}
must contain no red
K_{s-2}
or we may find a red
K_{s-1}
along with
v
, and
N_{BR}
must contain no blue
K_{s-1}
or we may find a blue
K_{s}
along with
u
. Thus
\omega(G^{\prime}[N_{BR}])\leq R(s-2,s-1)-1
. Similarly,
N_{RR}\cup N_{RB}
must contain no red
K_{s-2}
or blue
K_{s}
, so
\omega(G^{\prime}[N_{RR}\cup N_{RB}])\leq R(s-2,s)-1
. Furthermore,
N_{BB}
must contain no red
K_{s-1}
and no blue
K_{s-2}
, as otherwise we can find a blue
K_{s}
along with the edge
uv
. Hence
\omega(G^{\prime}[N_{BB}])\leq R(s-2,s-1)-1
.

However, invoking Theorem
15
with
F_{1}=N_{RR}\cup N_{RB}
(
k_{1}=R(s-2,s)
),
F_{2}=N_{BR}
(
k_{2}=R(s-2,s-1)
), and
F_{3}=N_{BB}
(
k_{3}=R(s-2,s-1)
), since
k_{1}\cdot r+(k_{2}-1)+(k_{3}-1)=N-2r
, either
F_{1}
contains a clique on
R(s-2,s)
vertices,
F_{2}
contains a clique on
R(s-2,s-1)
vertices, or
F_{3}
contains a clique on
R(s-2,s-1)
vertices. In any case, we are guaranteed either a red
K_{s-1}
or a blue
K_{s}
, a contradiction. Therefore,
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(s-1,s)\leq N
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
9
.

For sake of contradiction, fix
N>2(R(3,t-2))+2t

or blue
K_{t}
. Note that if there are no blue edges, then there must be a red

Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume
c(\{1,2\},\{3,4\})=B
. Next, we look at
N(\{1,2\})\cap N(\{3,4\})\cong G^{\prime}=\textrm{KG}(N-4,2)
, and we will partition the vertices of
G^{\prime}
into
4
sets. For
(X,Y)\in\{(R,R),(R,B),(B,R),(B,B)\}
, we denote by
N_{XY}
the set of vertices
v
in
G^{\prime}
such that
c(v,\{1,2\})=X
and
c(v,\{3,4\})=Y
.

First, observe that for any two vertices
u,v\in N_{RB}
with
uv\in E(G^{\prime})
,
c(u,v)=B

3.3
Computationally-obtained bounds

To obtain the upper and lower bounds in Theorem
12
we wrote a program in Python, which for (small enough) specified parameters
n,r,s,t
, runs over all possible red/blue edge-colorings of
\textrm{KG}(n,r)
and either provides as output a coloring that contains no red
K_{s}
and no blue
K_{t}
(which we call “good”), or declares that a good coloring does not exists.

We do this by reformulating the question as a satisfiability problem. For example, for
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r}(3,3)
, we look for a red/blue edge-coloring
c
of
G=\textrm{KG}(n,r)
with no monochromatic triangle. Suppose
E(G)=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{|E(G)|}\}
. For
1\leq i\leq|E(G)|
, we may assign a boolean variable
v_{i}
corresponding to
e_{i}
, where

v_{i}=\begin{cases}1\text{ (True)}&\text{if }c(e_{i})=R,\\
0\text{ (False)}&\text{if }c(e_{i})=B.\end{cases}

Then, for a triangle
T=\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
of
G
,
T
is monochromatic if and only if the following boolean clause is true:

(v_{1}\land v_{2}\land v_{3})\lor(\neg v_{1}\land\neg v_{2}\land\neg v_{3}).

Negating this, we get that
T
is not monochromatic if and only if the following boolean clause,
B_{T}
, is true:

B_{T}=(v_{1}\lor v_{2}\lor v_{3})\land(\neg v_{1}\lor\neg v_{2}\lor\neg v_{3}).

Let
\{T_{1},\dots,T_{t}\}
be the set of all triangles in
G
. Then, there is a good edge-coloring of
G
if and only if there exists an assignment
(v_{1},\dots,v_{|E(G)|})\in\{0,1\}^{|E(G)|}
such that the clause
B=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{t}B_{T_{i}}
is satisfiable (True).

Our program builds the clause
B
and then checks its satisfiability.

The proof of Theorem
12
follows because our program found a good coloring of
\textrm{KG}(8,2)
(this coloring is given explicitly in Appendix A), but declared that the graphs
\textrm{KG}(9,2)
and
\textrm{KG}(13,3)
do not have good colorings.

Our code is given in Appendix B.

We begin with a general discussion on monochromatic induced Kneser subgraphs, which will be useful in making progress on Problem

Let
r>1
be an integer and let
\textrm{KG}(s,r)
and
\textrm{KG}(t,r)

induced

r

Theorem 18

does not exists; that is, there exists a red/blue edge-coloring of
\textrm{KG}(n,r)

\textrm{KG}(s,r)

Theorem 19

(Bollobás

2
]
)
.

Let
(A_{1},B_{1}),\dots,(A_{m},B_{m})
be pairs of sets with
|A_{i}|=a
and
|B_{i}|=b
for every

. Suppose that

A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset

, and

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset

. Furthermore, if

then there is some set

S

Proof of Theorem
18
.

We first show
R^{\textrm{KG}}_{r,ind}(2r,2r)
does not exist. Observe that
\textrm{KG}(2r,r)
is a matching of size
\frac{1}{2}\binom{2r}{r}
.

Now, in any
\textrm{KG}(n,r)

has vertex set
\binom{S}{r}

|S|=2r
. To this end, let

be an induced matching with edge set

X_{i}\cap Y_{i}=\emptyset

\textrm{KG}(t,r)

∎

Appendix B: Our code

Below is the code we used to obtain the results in Theorem

.

import itertools as it
from pysat.solvers import Glucose3

’’’
Builds Kneser Graph Kn(n, r)
Input: n, r
Output: (V, E)
V (list): Vertices (all r-subsets of [n]) as r-tuples
E (list of 2-tuples): Edges as a pair of r-tuples
’’’
def build_kneser_graph(n,r):
V = list(it.combinations(range(1, n + 1), r))
E = []
for (a, b) in it.combinations(V, 2):
if set(a).isdisjoint(set(b)):
E.append((a, b))

return (V, E)

’’’
Try to find if Kn(n,r) can be 2-colored so that there is no red K_s or blue K_t

For no red K_s, for every s-clique, need at least 1 blue edge.
That is, if e_1, ..., e_k are the edges of an s-clique and e_i = 1 if red, e_i = -1 if blue,
want to satisfy (-e_1 v -e_2 v ... v -e_k)

For no blue K_t, for every t-clique, need at least 1 red edge.
That is, if e_1, ..., e_k are the edges of a t-clique and e_i = 1 if red, e_i = -1 if blue,
want to satisfy (-e_1 v -e_2 v ... v -e_k)

(NOTE: 1 corresponds to True, -1 corresponds to False)

If there is a good coloring, outputs (True, R, B), where R = red edges, B = blue edges
If there is no good coloring, outputs (False, [], [])
’’’
def build_sat_instances(n, r, s, t):
solver = Glucose3()
clauses = []
V = []
E = []

#Create vertex and edge list
(V, E) = build_kneser_graph(n, r)

for s_tuple in it.combinations(V, s):

#check that the s-tuple forms a clique in Kn(n,r)
if all([set(s_tuple[i]).isdisjoint(set(s_tuple[j])) for (i,j) in it.combinations(range(len(s_tuple)), 2)]):

#Add clause forbidding monochromatic K_s and K_t (if s = t)
if s == t:
cliq = []
for edg in it.combinations(s_tuple, 2):
idx = -1
try:
idx = E.index(edg)

except:
idx = E.index((edg[1], edg[0]))

cliq.append(idx + 1)

solver.add_clause([-x for x in cliq])
solver.add_clause(cliq)

#Add clause forbidding red K_s
if s != t:
cliq = []
for edg in it.combinations(s_tuple, 2):
idx = -1
try:
idx = E.index(edg)

except:
idx = E.index((edg[1], edg[0]))

cliq.append(idx + 1)

solver.add_clause([-x for x in cliq])

if s != t:
for t_tuple in it.combinations(V, t):

#check that the t-tuple forms a clique in Kn(n,r)
if all([set(t_tuple[i]).isdisjoint(set(t_tuple[j])) for (i,j) in it.combinations(range(len(t_tuple)), 2)]):

#Add clause forbidding red K_t
cliq = []
for edg in it.combinations(t_tuple, 2):
idx = -1
try:
idx = E.index(edg)

except:
idx = E.index((edg[1], edg[0]))

cliq.append(idx + 1)

solver.add_clause(cliq)

#Solve SAT problem and recover coloring
result = solver.solve()
R = []
B = []
if result:
L = solver.get_model()
R = [E[i - 1] for i in L if i > 0]
B = [E[-i - 1] for i in L if i < 0]

return (result, R, B)

#--------------------------------------------------

build_sat_instances(8, 2, 3, 3)

#output: False, [], []
build_sat_instances(9, 2, 3, 3)

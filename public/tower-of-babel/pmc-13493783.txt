LncRNA IRAIN inhibits mantle cell lymphoma progression by inducing cell cycle arrest and apoptosis: an in vitro study
Authors: Ping Lin, Yiping Zhang, Liling Lu, Xudong Ma, Yiqun Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLong non-coding RNA (lncRNA) insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor antisense imprinted non-protein coding RNA (IRAIN) and lysine-specific demethylase 1 (LSD1) are aberrantly expressed in various malignancies. However, their roles in the progression of mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) remain unclear. This study aimed to investigate the expression pattern and biological role of IRAIN in MCL progression and to further explore the relationship between IRAIN and LSD1. In this study, the expression levels of IRAIN and LSD1 in MCL cells were detected by RT-qPCR. An IRAIN-overexpressing lentiviral vector (LV-IRAIN-up) was constructed and transduced into MCL cells, and the transduction efficiency was verified by flow cytometry (FACS) and RT-qPCR. The effects of IRAIN overexpression on cell viability, apoptosis, and cell cycle progression were evaluated using CCK-8 assays and FACS analysis. In addition, RT-qPCR and Western blot analyses were performed to determine the regulatory effects of IRAIN overexpression on LSD1 expression, apoptosis-related proteins (including Bax, Bcl-2, pro-caspase-3, and cleaved-caspase-3), and cell cycle-related proteins (Cyclin D1, CDK2, and p21). Furthermore, LSD1 was overexpressed in IRAIN-overexpressing MCL cells to investigate its effects on cell proliferation, apoptosis, and cell cycle progression. The results demonstrated that IRAIN expression was significantly downregulated, whereas LSD1 expression was markedly upregulated in MCL cells. IRAIN overexpression significantly inhibited cell viability, promoted apoptosis, and induced G1-phase cell cycle arrest. Meanwhile, IRAIN overexpression markedly downregulated the expression of LSD1, Bcl-2, pro-caspase-3/cleaved-caspase-3, Cyclin D1, and CDK2, while upregulating the expression of Bax and p21. Further experiments showed that LSD1 overexpression partially reversed the inhibitory effects of IRAIN overexpression on MCL cell proliferation, apoptosis induction, and G1-phase arrest. Collectively, these findings suggest that IRAIN may participate in the regulation of proliferation, apoptosis, and cell cycle progression in MCL cells, potentially through modulation of LSD1. Introduction

Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) is a rare and aggressive subtype of B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL)1, accounting for approximately 3%–10% of all NHL cases2. It is characterized by the rapid proliferation of malignant B cells within the mantle zone of lymphoid follicles3. In the vast majority of MCL cases (> 95%), the chromosomal translocation t(11;14)(q13;q32) (IgH/CCND1) is present, leading to the constitutive overexpression of cyclin D1 (CCND1) and resulting in dysregulated cell cycle control. Additionally, MCL is frequently associated with disruptions in DNA damage response and repair pathways, as well as widespread transcriptional abnormalities4. Studies have demonstrated that the pathogenesis of MCL is highly complex, involving the interplay of multiple molecular aberrations, environmental risk factors, and genetic predispositions5. However, the limited understanding of the underlying mechanisms driving MCL initiation and progression continues to hinder the development of more effective therapeutic strategies. Although chemotherapy combined with stem cell transplantation has improved patient outcomes to some extent, the prognosis of MCL remains poor due to its aggressive clinical course and rapid progression, with a median survival time of approximately 5–7 years6. Moreover, the majority of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, further compromising long-term survival rates7. Therefore, elucidating the characteristic molecular markers and signaling pathways involved in MCL pathogenesis is crucial for developing novel therapeutic approaches and improving overall survival in patients with MCL.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are a novel class of RNA molecules defined as transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides that lack protein-coding capacity8. In the hematopoietic system, lncRNAs are extensively involved in the development, proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis of various blood cell types, and are closely associated with the initiation, progression, recurrence, drug resistance, and metastasis of numerous hematological malignancies9,10. Emerging evidence indicates that lncRNAs not only play critical roles in tumorigenesis but also hold potential as diagnostic biomarkers for cancer11. In 2014, a novel imprinted antisense non-coding RNA, named IRAIN, was first identified within the promoter complex of the insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF1R). IRAIN is located at the IGF1R locus on chromosome 15q26.3, spans 5359 nucleotides, and is transcribed in the antisense direction11. Recent studies have demonstrated that IRAIN is differentially expressed in various solid tumors, including glioma12, adenocarcinoma13, lung cancer8, laryngeal cancer14, and renal cell carcinoma15, and may function either as an oncogene or a tumor suppressor by regulating tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and apoptosis. For instance, in glioma, IRAIN expression is significantly downregulated, and it has been shown to inhibit tumor progression by suppressing the IGF-1R–PI3K–NF-κB signaling pathway12. Similarly, IRAIN is markedly downregulated in laryngeal carcinoma tissues, suggesting its potential role as a tumor suppressor14. However, IRAIN expression has been reported to be significantly upregulated in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and IRAIN knockdown was shown to inhibit the proliferation of NSCLC cells8. These findings underscore the functional significance of IRAIN in various malignancies. However, the role and underlying mechanisms of IRAIN in MCL remain largely unexplored.

Lysine-specific demethylase 1 (LSD1), also known as KDM1A, AOF2, or BHC110, was the first histone demethylase to be identified16. Its primary function is to repress gene expression by removing mono- or dimethyl groups from histone H3 lysine 4 (H3K4), while it can also activate gene transcription by demethylating histone H3 lysine 9 (H3K9)17–19. LSD1 is a key epigenetic eraser that regulates gene expression programs and plays critical roles in various physiological processes, including the differentiation of both embryonic and adult stem cells20,21. Dysregulation of LSD1 during development can lead to a range of differentiation defects. In cancer, aberrant expression of histone demethylases, particularly LSD1, is frequently observed across diverse malignancies20. Multiple studies have reported that LSD1 is significantly upregulated in several tumors, including prostate cancer22, NSCLC23, breast cancer24, and MCL19. As such, LSD1 has emerged as a promising therapeutic target for cancer, and its inhibition has been shown to effectively suppress tumorigenesis and tumor progression25. Currently, seven covalent LSD1 inhibitors—trans-2-phenylcyclopropylamine, INCB059872, ORY-1001, GSK-2,879,552, IMG-7289, ORY-2001, and TAK-418—have entered clinical trials, primarily for the treatment of acute myeloid leukemia and small cell lung cancer18. Moreover, evidence suggests that IRAIN can bind to the LSD1 gene and participate in the regulation of cell proliferation and apoptosis in pancreatic cancer26. However, the association and functional interplay between LSD1 and IRAIN in MCL remain largely unclear. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the expression pattern of IRAIN in MCL and further analyze its effects on the biological behaviors of MCL cells as well as its potential functional relationship with LSD1, in order to provide experimental evidence for understanding the molecular regulatory mechanisms associated with MCL.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Normal human lymphocytes were isolated from peripheral blood samples obtained from healthy volunteers27, with informed consent obtained from all participants. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Zhangzhou Hospital, Fujian Province (Ethical Approval No. 20200315). The human MCL cell lines Jeko-1 and Granta-519 were purchased from the Shanghai Institute of Biochemistry and Cell Biology (China) and American Type Culture Collection (USA), respectively. The human MCL cell line Z-138 and human lymphocyte cell line GM12878 were obtained from iCell Bioscience Inc. Jeko-1, Granta-519, and GM12878 cells were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640 (RPMI-1640) medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin solution (Solarbio, Beijing, China), whereas Z-138 cells were cultured in IMDM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin solution (Solarbio, Beijing, China). All cells were maintained in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO2 at 37 °C.

Reverse transcription–quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells using Trizol reagent (Pufei, Shanghai, China)28, and subsequently reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Promega, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) was performed using the SYBR Green PCR detection system, with β-actin serving as the internal control. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. All primer sequences are listed in Table 1. All primers were synthesized by Shangya Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Fuzhou, China).

Cell transduction

In this study, the expression of IRAIN and LSD1 was regulated in different cell lines via lentiviral transduction. IRAIN overexpression or knockdown was performed in Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells, followed by further induction of LSD1 overexpression. IRAIN overexpression or knockdown was also conducted in Z-138 and GM12878 cells. All lentiviral vectors used in this study were commercially prepared. Lentiviruses for IRAIN overexpression (LV-IRAIN-up), LSD1 overexpression (LV-LSD1-up), IRAIN knockdown (LV-IRAIN-down), and the corresponding negative control viruses (LV-NC-up/down) were provided by GeneChem (Shanghai, China). These lentiviral constructs were generated based on the GV409, GV492, and GV493 vectors and were driven by the Ubi, Ubc, or hU6 promoters, respectively, with EGFP or gcGFP used as reporter genes. All lentiviruses were packaged using a second-generation packaging system, pseudotyped with VSV-G, and concentrated by ultracentrifugation. Viral titers were determined by the manufacturer using absolute quantitative qPCR, with reported titers ranging from 5 × 107 to 4 × 109 TU/mL.

Jeko-1, Granta-519, Z-138, and GM12878 cells were grouped according to the experimental design. Cells were seeded into 48-well plates at a density of 1 × 105/mL with a final volume of 200 µL per well. Except for the Control group, the corresponding lentiviruses were added to each group together with infection-enhancing solution (8 µL/well), whereas no viral treatment was applied to the Control group. Based on the manufacturer’s recommendations, preliminary optimization experiments, and the infection characteristics of each cell line, the multiplicity of infection (MOI) values for Jeko-1, Granta-519, Z-138, and GM12878 cells were set at 40, 50, 40, and 40, respectively. After 16 h of infection, the culture supernatant was discarded and replaced with fresh complete medium for continued incubation. At 72 h post-infection, cells were collected and GFP expression was observed under an inverted fluorescence microscope. Meanwhile, the percentage of GFP-positive cells was determined by flow cytometry (FACS). Transduction efficiency was calculated as the percentage of GFP-positive cells relative to the total number of cells. The overexpression efficiency was further verified by RT-qPCR.

Cell viability assay

Following the indicated treatments, cells from each group were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 103 cells per well, with three technical replicates established for each group. Cell viability was assessed on days 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. At each time point, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well, followed by incubation for an additional 1 h. The absorbance (OD value) was then measured at 450 nm to evaluate changes in cell viability. All experiments were independently repeated three times as biological replicates. For each independent experiment, three technical replicates were included, and the OD450 values of the technical replicates were averaged prior to statistical analysis.

Cell apoptosis detection

Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 2 × 105 cells/mL and infected with the corresponding lentiviruses (LV-NC-up, LV-IRAIN-up, and LV-LSD1-up) according to experimental grouping for 6 days29. After transduction, cells from each group (2 × 106 cells/mL) were harvested into 5 mL centrifuge tubes and centrifuged at 200 g for 5 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the cell pellets were washed three times with pre-cooled PBS and collected. The cell pellets were then resuspended in 1× Binding Buffer to a final concentration of 1 × 106 cells/mL. A 100 µL aliquot of the cell suspension was incubated with 5 µL Annexin V-PE and 5 µL 7-AAD in the dark for 15 min. Subsequently, the cells were resuspended in 400 µL of 1× Binding Buffer and apoptosis was analyzed using a cell apoptosis detection kit (BD Biosciences, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol27.

Detection of cell cycle

Cell cycle analysis was performed using PI (MeilunBio, Dalian, China). Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 2 × 105 cells/mL and infected with the corresponding lentiviruses (LV-NC-up, LV-IRAIN-up, and LV-LSD1-up) according to experimental grouping for 6 days. After infection, the cells were harvested into 5 mL centrifuge tubes and centrifuged at 200 g for 5 min, and the supernatant was discarded. The cell pellets were washed with 1 mL of pre-cooled PBS and transferred into 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes, followed by centrifugation at 200 g for 5 min. The pellets were then resuspended in 1 mL of pre-cooled PBS and fixed in 3 mL of pre-cooled 75% ethanol for at least 4 h. The fixed cells were centrifuged at 200 g for 5 min to remove the ethanol. The pellets were washed again with 1 mL of pre-cooled PBS and incubated in 500 µL of PI staining solution at 37 °C in the dark for 30 min. Finally, cell cycle analysis was performed using flow cytometry.

Western blot assay

Total protein samples were extracted from each group of cells using RIPA lysis buffer. Protein concentrations were determined using a micro BCA protein assay kit. Equal amounts of protein were separated by 10% SDS–PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST at room temperature for 1 h and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (LSD1: 1:1000; Bax: 1:20000; Bcl-2: 1:2000; cleaved-caspase 3: 1:1000; pro-caspase 3: 1:1000; Cyclin D1: 1:10000; CDK2: 1:1000; p21: 1:1000; β-actin: 1:1000). After three washes with TBST, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. Protein signals were detected using a chemiluminescence imaging and analysis system.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed at least three times independently. All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA). CCK-8 data were analyzed using two-way repeated-measures analysis of variance (two-way repeated-measures ANOVA). A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Low expression of IRAIN in MCL cells

IRAIN is differentially expressed in various tumors8,12,13. To investigate the expression level of IRAIN in MCL, we measured IRAIN expression in MCL cell lines (Jeko-1 and Granta-519) and normal lymphocytes using RT-qPCR. The results (Fig. 1) show that, compared to normal lymphocytes, the expression levels of IRAIN were significantly reduced in Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells (P < 0.001).

IRAIN is downregulated in MCL cells (n = 3). All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical comparisons among groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA). P < 0.001 vs. Normal. Normal, normal lymphocytes.

Stable overexpression of IRAIN established in MCL cells

To further investigate the effects of IRAIN on the biological functions of MCL cells, Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells were infected with an lncRNA-IRAIN overexpression lentivirus. Following infection, GFP fluorescence expression was observed under an inverted fluorescence microscope to qualitatively evaluate the lentiviral transduction efficiency. As shown in Fig. 2A, obvious GFP fluorescence signals were detected in both Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells after infection, whereas no apparent fluorescence expression was observed in the control group, indicating successful lentiviral transduction. Furthermore, the percentage of GFP-positive cells was quantitatively analyzed by flow cytometry. The results showed (Supplementary Fig. 1) that the transduction efficiencies in Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells were (73.87 ± 4.92)% and (82.56 ± 3.62)%, respectively. In addition, RT-qPCR was performed to detect IRAIN expression levels in the two MCL cell lines. As shown in Fig. 2B, compared with the LV-NC-up group, IRAIN expression was significantly increased in the LV-IRAIN-up group in both Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells (P < 0.001). These results demonstrated that IRAIN overexpression was successfully established in Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells.

Overexpression of IRAIN inhibits MCL cell viability and promotes cell apoptosis

To further investigate the effects of IRAIN overexpression on MCL cell viability and apoptosis, CCK-8 assays and flow cytometry were performed. The CCK-8 results (Fig. 3A) showed that IRAIN overexpression significantly reduced cell viability in both Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells (P < 0.001). Flow cytometry analysis further confirmed that IRAIN overexpression markedly promoted apoptosis in both cell lines (Fig. 3B). In addition, Western blot analysis (Fig. 4) demonstrated that IRAIN overexpression significantly upregulated the pro-apoptotic protein Bax (P < 0.001), while downregulating the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 and pro-/cleaved-caspase-3 (P < 0.05). Collectively, these results indicate that IRAIN inhibits proliferation and promotes apoptosis in MCL cells.

Overexpression of IRAIN blocks the cell cycle of MCL cells

Aberrant cell cycle regulation is a common feature of most malignant tumors, including MCL30. To investigate the effects of IRAIN overexpression on the cell cycle of MCL cells, flow cytometry analysis was performed. As shown in Fig. 5A and C, the proportion of G1-phase cells in Jeko-1 cells was significantly increased in the LV-IRAIN-up group compared with the LV-NC-up group (P < 0.001). Similar results were observed in Granta-519 cells (Fig. 5B and D). In addition, Western blot analysis was used to examine the expression of cell cycle-related proteins (Cyclin D1, CDK2, and p21). The results (Supplementary Fig. 2) indicated that, in both Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells, Cyclin D1 and CDK2 expression levels were significantly decreased in the LV-IRAIN-up group compared with the LV-NC-up group (P < 0.05), while p21 expression was significantly increased (P < 0.001). These findings suggest that lncRNA-IRAIN overexpression induces G1-phase cell cycle arrest in MCL cells, thereby inhibiting cell proliferation.

IRAIN may be involved in the regulation of LSD1 expression

Previous studies have demonstrated that LSD1 is significantly upregulated in MCL cells and may serve as a potential therapeutic target for MCL19. To further confirm the expression of LSD1 in MCL, RT-qPCR was performed to detect LSD1 expression levels in Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells. The results showed (Supplementary Fig. 3) that, compared with normal lymphocytes, LSD1 expression was significantly increased in both MCL cell lines (P < 0.001). Previous studies have also suggested that IRAIN may participate in LSD1-related regulatory processes in pancreatic cancer26. In the present study, we observed an inverse expression pattern between IRAIN and LSD1 in MCL cells. To further investigate whether IRAIN is involved in the regulation of LSD1 expression, IRAIN was overexpressed in MCL cells. The results demonstrated that IRAIN overexpression significantly reduced both the mRNA and protein expression levels of LSD1 in Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6). Furthermore, to verify the consistency of these findings in another MCL cell line, additional experiments were performed in Z-138 cells. The results showed that IRAIN overexpression similarly and significantly downregulated the mRNA and protein expression levels of LSD1 in Z-138 cells (P < 0.05) (Supplementary Fig. 4). In contrast, in the normal blood cell line GM12878, neither IRAIN knockdown nor IRAIN overexpression caused significant changes in the mRNA or protein expression levels of LSD1 compared with the corresponding control groups (LV-NC-down or LV-NC-up) (Supplementary Fig. 5). These findings suggest that IRAIN may participate in the regulation of LSD1 expression in MCL cells, and that this regulatory effect may exhibit a certain degree of MCL cell context dependency. However, the precise mechanism underlying the interaction between IRAIN and LSD1 requires further investigation.

LSD1 overexpression partially reverses the tumor-suppressive effects mediated by IRAIN

To further elucidate the functional relationship between IRAIN and LSD1 in MCL cells, LSD1 was additionally overexpressed in IRAIN-overexpressing MCL cells, and the effects on cell viability, apoptosis, and cell cycle progression were evaluated. The results (Fig. 7) showed that in Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells, cell viability was significantly increased (P < 0.001) and the apoptosis rate was markedly reduced (P < 0.001) in the LV-IRAIN-up + LV-LSD1-up group compared with the LV-IRAIN-up group. Western blot analysis further demonstrated (Supplementary Fig. 6,7) that in both Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells, the expression level of the pro-apoptotic protein Bax was significantly decreased (P < 0.05), whereas the expression levels of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 and pro-caspase-3/cleaved-caspase-3 were significantly increased (P < 0.05) in the LV-IRAIN-up + LV-LSD1-up group compared with the LV-IRAIN-up group, indicating that LSD1 overexpression partially reversed the pro-apoptotic effect mediated by IRAIN. In addition, cell cycle analysis revealed (Fig. 8) that in Jeko-1 and Granta-519 cells, the proportion of cells in the G1 phase was significantly reduced (P < 0.05) in the LV-IRAIN-up + LV-LSD1-up group compared with the LV-IRAIN-up group, suggesting that LSD1 overexpression markedly alleviated IRAIN-induced G1 phase arrest and promoted the transition from G1 to S phase. Consistent with these findings, Western blot results (Supplementary Fig. 8,9) showed that the expression levels of G1/S transition-related proteins Cyclin D1 and CDK2 were significantly increased (P < 0.05), while the expression level of the cell cycle inhibitor p21 was significantly decreased (P < 0.05) in the LV-IRAIN-up + LV-LSD1-up group compared with the LV-IRAIN-up group. Taken together, these results indicate that LSD1 overexpression significantly reverses the inhibitory effects of IRAIN overexpression on MCL cell viability, apoptosis induction, and G1 phase arrest, suggesting that IRAIN suppresses MCL cell proliferation and survival by downregulating LSD1 and thereby modulating cell cycle progression.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that IRAIN expression was significantly downregulated, whereas LSD1 expression was markedly upregulated, in MCL cells, with the two molecules exhibiting opposite expression patterns. Functional experiments revealed that IRAIN overexpression inhibited MCL cell proliferation, promoted apoptosis, and induced G1-phase cell cycle arrest, accompanied by significant downregulation of LSD1 expression. Furthermore, LSD1 overexpression partially reversed the antitumor effects mediated by IRAIN overexpression, suggesting that IRAIN may participate in the biological processes of MCL cells through regulation of LSD1. Collectively, this study preliminarily elucidates the expression pattern and potential biological functions of IRAIN in MCL and provides experimental evidence for further investigation of the role of IRAIN in MCL.

Currently, the treatment methods for MCL mainly include anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies, autologous stem cell transplantation, and immunochemotherapy. However, these approaches are unable to completely eradicate MCL, leading to disease relapse in nearly all patients31. Moreover, MCL exhibits significant clinical heterogeneity, necessitating more personalized treatment strategies. However, the current targeted therapies for MCL patients still fail to fully explain the differences in biomolecular characteristics among individual patients32. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify novel oncogenes or tumor suppressor genes closely associated with the onset, progression, and malignant transformation of MCL.

In recent years, increasing evidence has shown that dysregulation of lncRNAs plays an important role in regulating gene expression and is involved in tumorigenesis and other biological processes8,33. According to relevant reports, some lncRNAs can promote cell proliferation, thereby driving tumorigenesis, while others resist tumor formation by inhibiting excessive cell growth and proliferation, exhibiting high tissue and organ specificity34. As a result, lncRNAs have been widely proposed as potential therapeutic targets and biomarkers for various diseases. Previous studies have demonstrated that multiple lncRNAs play critical roles in the progression of MCL, such as MALAT1, SNHG4, and GAS5 35. Therefore, an in-depth study of the mechanisms by which lncRNAs function as tumor suppressors or oncogenes not only aids in the identification of novel potential biomarkers for early diagnosis and precise prognostic prediction, but also provides a crucial basis for developing epigenetic molecular targets for the treatment of MCL patients.

In hematologic malignancies, IRAIN appears to function as a tumor suppressor36. Studies have shown that, as the antisense non-coding RNA of IGFIR, IRAIN is aberrantly expressed in various cancers and is involved in crucial processes such as tumorigenesis, progression, invasion, and metastasis8,12,37. For example, in NSCLC tissues, the expression level of IRAIN is increased, with a significant upregulation observed particularly in larger tumors or in patients with a smoking history8. Similarly, in pancreatic cancer (PC), IRAIN expression is significantly elevated, and its high expression is closely associated with tumor volume, advanced TNM stage, and lymph node metastasis26. However, IRAIN expression has been reported to be decreased in renal carcinoma tissues and cells. Functional studies demonstrated that IRAIN overexpression significantly inhibited cell proliferation and migration while promoting apoptosis in renal carcinoma cells38. Nevertheless, the role of IRAIN in MCL remains unclear. In the present study, we found that IRAIN expression was significantly downregulated in MCL cells, and that IRAIN overexpression markedly inhibited cell proliferation and promoted apoptosis. These findings are consistent with the results reported by Li et al. 38 regarding the role of IRAIN in renal carcinoma. Further molecular mechanism studies revealed that, compared to LV-NC-up transfected cells, overexpression of IRAIN caused significant G1 phase arrest in MCL cells, preventing their transition to the S and G2 phases. However, these results are in contrast to findings in NSCLC and PC studies. The reason for this discrepancy may lie in the stronger tissue-specific expression patterns of lncRNAs compared to protein-coding genes8,14.

Epigenetic processes regulate gene expression and transmit critical information during cell division39. As a core factor in epigenetic regulation, LSD1 is involved in various physiological processes, including epithelial-mesenchymal transition, cell proliferation, differentiation, cell cycle regulation, and migration, playing a significant role in cellular functions40. Since LSD1 regulates multiple cellular activities, its dysregulation significantly increases the risk of cancer development and has been confirmed in various invasive cancers41. Studies have shown that overexpression of LSD1 can induce abnormal proliferation of tumor cells, driving rapid tumor growth and widespread metastasis42,43. In this study, we found that LSD1 expression was significantly elevated in MCL cells, consistent with the findings of Zou et al. 19, further supporting the potential of LSD1 as a therapeutic target for MCL. Furthermore, studies have confirmed that most lncRNAs exert their biological functions by interacting with RNA-binding proteins26. Research suggests that LSD1 can be recruited to specific target genes by forming ribonucleoprotein complexes containing lncRNAs, thus facilitating its functional regulation. These lncRNAs serve as modular scaffolds for various histone regulators, including LSD1, in multiple human cancers44. This study further demonstrated that IRAIN and LSD1 exhibited correlated expression patterns, and that LSD1 overexpression in MCL cells significantly reversed the IRAIN overexpression-induced inhibition of cell viability, promotion of apoptosis, and G1-phase cell cycle arrest. These findings suggest that IRAIN may regulate cell cycle progression and suppress the proliferation and survival of MCL cells by inhibiting LSD1 expression. This observation is consistent with the findings reported by Lian et al. 26 in pancreatic cancer, in which IRAIN was shown to regulate the biological behaviors of cancer cells through LSD1. Collectively, these results indicate that IRAIN may exert anti-MCL effects through modulation of LSD1. However, the precise molecular mechanism underlying the interaction between IRAIN and LSD1 in MCL requires further investigation.

Despite the preliminary findings of this study revealing the potential biological role of IRAIN in MCL cells and its functional association with LSD1, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study was mainly based on in vitro cell experiments. Although the key findings were validated in multiple MCL cell lines, including Jeko-1, Granta-519, and Z-138, the number of cell models used remains limited. Moreover, in vivo evidence from animal models and clinical patient samples is still lacking. Therefore, the current findings derived from cell-based experiments may not fully reflect the biological complexity of the in vivo tumor microenvironment, nor can they fully represent the universal molecular mechanisms in MCL. The precise roles of IRAIN and LSD1 in the initiation and progression of MCL, as well as their clinical relevance, require further validation. Second, although this study demonstrated the tumor-suppressive effects of IRAIN overexpression in MCL cells, the investigation primarily focused on functional characterization, while the upstream mechanisms responsible for IRAIN downregulation were not extensively explored. The aberrantly decreased expression of IRAIN in MCL may involve multiple mechanisms, including epigenetic and transcriptional regulation, which remain to be systematically investigated. In addition, the present findings suggest that IRAIN may participate in LSD1-related regulatory processes. However, whether a direct interaction exists between IRAIN and LSD1, as well as the precise underlying molecular mechanisms, remains unclear and warrants further investigation. Future studies will expand the range of MCL cell models and incorporate animal experiments as well as multicenter clinical cohorts with larger sample sizes to more comprehensively elucidate the biological significance and prognostic value of the IRAIN/LSD1 axis in MCL. Furthermore, we aim to further clarify the molecular mechanisms underlying IRAIN-mediated regulation of apoptosis and cell cycle progression and to explore the potential therapeutic value of combining IRAIN-targeted strategies with existing treatments, including chemotherapy and immunotherapy, thereby facilitating the translational application of IRAIN-based targeted therapies in MCL.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the present study demonstrated that lncRNA IRAIN is downregulated in MCL cells, and its overexpression inhibits cell proliferation, promotes apoptosis, and induces cell cycle arrest. In addition, IRAIN may impede the initiation and progression of MCL through interaction with LSD1. These findings suggest that IRAIN may play an important role in the development and progression of MCL. However, the precise molecular mechanism underlying the interaction between IRAIN and LSD1 still requires further investigation.

Optimized PI based fuzzy controller for power quality assessment in autonomous microgrid using novel hybrid bio-inspired optimization technique
Authors: Suresh Govindasamy, Jaisiva Selvaraj, Rathinam Angamuthu, Tefera Mekonnen Azerefegn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe increasing penetration of nonlinear loads (NLLs) in three-phase distribution systems introduces significant harmonic distortion, leading to degraded power quality (PQ), increased losses, and potential voltage instability. To overcome these challenges, this paper proposes an enhanced shunt active power filter (SAPF) control strategy based on a hybrid Sand Cat Swarm Zebra Optimization tuned Second-Order Generalized Integrator with Fuzzy Logic Controller (SCS-ZO). The proposed approach effectively identifies phase angle variations, extracts fundamental signal components, and regulates compensating signals under dynamic operating conditions. The Second-Order Generalized Integrator (SOGI) module ensures accurate signal decomposition, while the fuzzy logic controller (FLC) provides robust voltage regulation and fast dynamic response. Furthermore, the hybrid Sand Cat Swarm Zebra Optimization optimization algorithm is employed to optimally tune controller parameters, improving convergence speed and overall system performance. The proposed controller is evaluated using a MATLAB/Simulink-based three-phase microgrid under balanced, unbalanced, and nonlinear load conditions. The results demonstrate that the proposed SCS-ZO significantly improves power quality by effectively mitigating harmonics, voltage fluctuations, and other disturbances, thereby enhancing system stability and reliability.Keywords: Micro grid, Power quality, Fuzzy controller, Voltage regulation, OptimizationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Within a stable 3-phase framework, the distributed system manifests Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) for both progressive and destructive orders1. This intricate 3-phase model, which incorporates Non-Linear Loads (NLL), gives rise to third-order current harmonics13. These currents, inherently unable to disperse, undergo mathematical amplification, intensifying the substantial current flow along the neutral line and thereby contributing to issues associated with neutral current2. These loaded components are integrated into a 3-phase, 4-line distribution system besieged by pronounced PQ challenges3. These challenges encompass deficiencies in voltage regulation, escalated reactive power levels, unwarranted neutral current, and imbalances in load4. Consequently, there exists a compelling need for a judicious management approach aimed at minimizing dominant harmonics to a level below 5%9. Numerous control methodologies have been put forth in the literature with the objective of mitigating voltage flickers, swells, and harmonics12. These approaches encompass instantaneous reactive PQ control5, Fryze current compensation, and Synchronous Reference Frame (SRF) techniques11. However, these strategies carry specific drawbacks, including bulkiness and susceptibility to noise within the supply model6. The primary objective of power injection is to augment the efficacy and excellence of the distributed network7. Among the excess of techniques available, the SOGI has emerged as a best approach in the literature for estimating grid parameters due to its minimal computational demands and straightforward implementation8. By filtering the quadrature-phase and in-phase forms for its input, SOGI estimates the frequency, amplitude, and phase of its signal10. The estimated quantities by SOGI exhibit oscillatory waves in the presence of harmonics, Alternate Current (AC) offset, and inter-THD, elucidating the low-signal modeling of estimation dynamics for SOGI11.

Several optimization-based and intelligent control strategies have been investigated recently, such as SOGI-Fuzzy-PI controllers, Better Bat Search Algorithm with the Moth Flame Optimization (IBSMFO), and Walrus Optimization with Multi-Functional Recurrent Fuzzy Neural Network (MFRFNN), which have shown improvements in harmonic mitigation and power quality regulation. These methods still have several significant drawbacks, though, including poor robustness in dynamic operating environments, complicated parameter tuning, and slow convergence rates. Moreover, although they are effective at adjusting parameters, hybrid and nature-inspired algorithms like Zebra Optimization Algorithm (ZOA) and Sand Cat Swarm Optimization (SCSO) suffer from limitations like early convergence, difficulty handling collisions in big datasets, and inconsistent performance under changing load or grid failures. However, the majority of the current hybrid methods are based on the weakly coupled sequential or parallel combinations, which restrict the effective exchange of information between constituent algorithms. This drives the necessity of a hybrid approach that is closely-knit and interacts on the update level. Due to its inability to adequately suppress current and voltage harmonics, current controllers suffer from a larger reactive power burden and reduced steady-state performance, especially when dealing with unbalanced and nonlinear load circumstances14. Intelligent control methods for enhancing microgrid power quality have been the focus of recent research; mainly depend on computationally demanding Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) models13, which could lead to longer processing times and more complex hardware. Similar to this15, face difficulties implementing the Convolutional Neural Network- Long Short-Term Memory (CNN–LSTM)-based DSTATCOM in real-time because of the high computational load and possible latency, which impair responsiveness in dynamic microgrid environments. Furthermore, most metaheuristic methods need a lot of processing, which limits their use for real-time power quality control in contemporary microgrid settings.

Despite the extensive development of control strategies for power quality (PQ) enhancement in microgrid systems, several critical technical limitations remain unresolved. Existing methods such as SRF-based controllers, PI/FLC hybrids, and optimization-assisted controllers have demonstrated effectiveness in harmonic mitigation; however, they suffer from three fundamental challenges:

Inadequate dynamic adaptability under rapidly varying nonlinear and unbalanced load conditions, leading to residual harmonics and voltage instability.

Furthermore, most existing hybrid optimization frameworks are designed using loosely coupled or sequential architectures, which restrict real-time information exchange between constituent algorithms, thereby limiting their effectiveness in achieving global optimal solutions. At the same time, advanced intelligent approaches such as CNN or deep learning-based controllers introduce high computational complexity, making them less suitable for real-time microgrid applications.

To address these limitations, this paper proposes a tightly integrated hybrid optimization-driven SOGI-FLC framework, where the SCSO and ZOA are combined at the position update level, enabling continuous interaction between exploration and exploitation processes. This hybrid SCS-ZO algorithm is employed to optimally tune the parameters of the SOGI-FLC controller, thereby enhancing harmonic suppression, improving voltage stability, and accelerating convergence under dynamic microgrid conditions.

Existing strategies

Various methodologies for the control of microgrid systems have been investigated in recent studies, each presenting unique approaches to address specific challenges. For microgrid systems, a new Fractional Order-Active Disturbance Rejection Controller (FOADRC) control approach was suggested by Al Thaiban A M et al.16 to improve power quality. With the help of the Walrus Optimization Algorithm (WOA), the controller parameters are efficiently adjusted. For adaptive regulation in a variety of scenarios, the Multi-Functional Recurrent Fuzzy Neural Network (MFRFNN) forecasts control parameters. The technique improves the voltage profile in a variety of situations, including sag, swell, imbalance, and fault circumstances, and it dramatically lowers THD. In order to maintain the power quality of renewable energy sources (RES) in microgrid systems, Rajesh P et al.17 suggests a novel control strategy. The suggested approach was known as the IBSMFO method since it combines the Better Bat Search Algorithm with the Moth Flame Optimization (MFO) Algorithm. Mutation was used to alter bats search behavior function, such as crossover. Here, search behavior has been controlled by MFO with the goal of lowering the error function. The goal was to demonstrate how the suggested method enhances power quality in relation to the fluctuation of active and reactive power. MFO is the best way to lower the power variation while still achieving the goal. Additionally, the daily and weekly data forecasts for grid power prices, electrical loads, and environmental characteristics are used to minimize the operational costs of RESs. In order to improve power quality in microgrid distribution systems, Shravani C and Narasimham R L et al.18 suggested a synergistic Unified Power Quality Conditioner (UPQC) application. The suggested sand cat swarm optimization method serves as the foundation for the Sand Cat Swarm Optimization (SCSO) technology. The main objectives of the suggested approach are to maximize UPQC, control power flow, and reduce harmonic distortion in the voltage and current waveforms. Celik D et al.19 employed a phase angle vector model Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) and linear kalman filter for robust estimation of harmonics and disturbances in grid voltage and NLL current signals. However, practical implementation challenges arose due to difficulties in accurately estimating noise covariance matrices. Sivakumar N et al.20 suggested a deep belief network (DBN)-based control scheme optimization for improving power quality in a microgrid cluster linked to a RES. This improves the reliability of the power sources by controlling the available energy within the cluster rather of concentrating it around the utility grid. To further improve the quality of the power supplies, a DBN model based on Improved Dwarf Mongoose Optimization (DWO) was suggested for inverter regulation. This model generates optimal pulse-width modulated signals. Budiman FN et al.21 suggested an Optimal Harmonic Power Flow (OHPF) approach for daily development in a grid-integrated microgrid. The framework combined optimization and harmonic power flow, assessing PQ through THD, voltage magnitude, and voltage unbalance parameter. A non-iterative Demand Side Management (DSM) scheme was suggested for PQ control, integrated as load constraints in the OHPF optimization. These constraints allow flexible DSM performances like load detaching and interphase load transmission. Additionally, penalty functions were incorporated to limit voltage magnitude and voltage THD. However, the study did not analyze PQ catalogues,like individual harmonic distortioncurrent to demonstrate efficiency. Choudhury S et al.22 had presented energy controlling approaches and PQenhancementapproaches for a Microgrid system integrating Battery, Super Capacitor and Fuel Cell technologies. To optimize energy management and enhance power quality, the researchers suggested a Modified Water Wave Optimization (MWWO) scheme with adaptive population extent and wavelength parameter. The MWWO approach effectively adjusted the factors of the Proportional Integral (PI) controller to ensure robust and dynamic system operation. However, a drawback of this suggested approach was its lower convergence rate. Sahoo GK et al.23 suggested a Prairie Dog Optimization (PDO) approach tailored for Hybrid Renewable Energy Systems (HRESs) involving PV, fuel cell (FC), and battery components in the Simulink/MATLAB framework. The suggested PDO technique optimizes the PI controller gain parameters, effectively addressing load demand and alleviating PQ issues. The MG model, implemented to tackle planned PQ challenges, like oscillatory transient, swell, unbalanced load, and notch circumstances, showcased the PDO controller’s response. However, the suggested method fell short in addressing additional real-world issues, whether discrete or continuous.

Proposed topology of power quality enhancement in microgrid

The introduced SOGI controller in Fig. 1 incorporates a harmonic-producing load in the microgrid system. The proposed system’s three-phase utility grid is interconnected with residential, linear, and nonlinear demands. The constant maintenance of the reference voltage for the DC bus is ensured by the FLC. The computation of the three-phase reference currents is carried out utilizing the SCS-ZO technique. Switching signals for the Voltage Source Inverter are received from the hysteresis controller.

Block diagram of introduced topology.

The insulator of harmonics acts as an impedance element between the NLL and the power source. To align the phase and amplitude values with the primary input signal for the SOGI, the output signal comprises two sinusoidal waveforms with 90° phase shifts. In the Point of Common Coupling (PCC), the input voltage signal is monitored. Harmonic voltages are introduced and manipulated by adding or subtracting them from the source voltage to ensure that the NLL maintains a stable sinusoidal voltage. The SOGI controller, analogous to a resistor with high impedance at all unwanted harmonic frequencies and zero impedance at the Fundamental Frequency (FF) component, prevents all NLL harmonics from entering the FF. Consequently, the NLL and the power source are effectively isolated from these harmonics.

The harmonic current flows within the microgrid supply without triggering harmonic resonance, creating a more stable and efficient microgrid operation25. Figure 2 illustrates a pictorialdepiction of the Simulink diagram illustrating the introduced SOGI-FLC in the microgrid system.Unlike conventional power systems, where microgrids are strategically placed to transport load currents, in the microgrid context, the SOGI controller is positioned adjacent to the NLL to impede harmonics from entering the microgrid. This setup ensures that there is no direct transfer of current harmonics between the source and the nonlinear load or vice versa.

Modelling of microgrid

Microgrid (MG) model consist of PV, battery and VSI. The DC bus, which consists of necessary components like solar panels, battery energy storage systems, and various DC loads, controls the output and consumption of DC electricity. The PV system, which converts solar energy into electrical power, provides a direct power supply to the DC bus. The BESS is crucial to energy management because it stores excess power when solar power production surpasses demand and releases stored power when generation is not available, ensuring a steady balance between supply and consumption. The AC bus provides AC loads by delivering power at the standard AC frequency and voltage needed for a range of devices. VSI can be used to convert the DC power from the DC bus to AC. It regulates the AC side voltage and frequency to guarantee a continuous and efficient power flow. The VSI is crucial to the stability and efficient operation of the MG because it transforms DC into AC and synchronizes it with the demands of the AC bus, even though it only allows one-way power transfer from DC to AC.

PV model

PV directly converts sunlight into electrical power by utilizing semiconductor technology. An MG can use solar energy, a clean and renewable resource, by integrating PV cells. These cells, which function mostly throughout the day, enhance the MG’s energy mix and meet times of high demand. By lowering reliance on non-renewable energy sources, the PV system improves the MG’s environmental profile.

Battery model

To guarantee a steady supply of electricity, this stored energy is then used during periods of high demand or when renewable energy sources become intermittent. By smoothly releasing stored energy, the BES also contributes significantly to the MG’s resilience by providing a quick reaction to power shortages and disruptions. A unit delay block is employed to determine the signal (∂ e), an additional signal alongside the error signal. The signals are downsized to smaller magnitudes using scaling elements and (∂ e) directed into the saturation structure. The saturation block controls the maximum and minimum boundaries of the signal, maintaining values within the range of −1 to 1. When the parameters of the input signal fall within the limits set by the saturation block, the input signal remains unchanged. However, if the input signal exceeds these limits, it is truncated at either the upper or lower bounds. FLC utilize the outcome of this block as their input. The switching pulse signals for the VSI are generated by the output signals from the FLC. By comparing the FLC’s output with a carrier signal, switching signals are generated31.The scaling elements, denoted as and (∂ e), undergo tuning through the application of the introduced SCS-ZO algorithm.

The SCS-ZO algorithm adaptively tunes the FLC parameters, including membership function limits, scaling factors, and rule sensitivity, to enhance controller responsiveness under varying nonlinear load conditions. The dynamic adjustment of input and output gains minimizes steady-state error and accelerates transient recovery, enabling faster harmonic suppression and stable DC-link voltage regulation. Furthermore, the SOGI filter gains have been fine-tuned to achieve improved synchronization with the fundamental grid frequency, ensuring accurate in-phase and quadrature signal extraction even under distorted voltage conditions. The optimal gain selection minimizes phase angle deviation and improves harmonic isolation at the Point of Common Coupling, while the hysteresis bandwidth, linked to the instantaneous error signal, ensures smoother switching, reduced current ripple, and consistent harmonic elimination under dynamic load changes.

Hybrid optimization algorithm for parameter tuning

In the initial exploration phase of the Zebra Optimization Algorithm (ZOA), the derived position deviates from the introduced conceptual framework. This misalignment leads to suboptimal global solutions and an inability to rectify constraints within the components of the introduced PQ improvement model. Consequently, a refinement process is implemented to enhance the accuracy of the position value obtained during the exploration phase. For the Sand Cat Swarm Optimization (SCSO) algorithm, collisions present a significant challenge, particularly when dealing with extensive or real-time data. These collisions unfavorably affect the approach’s performance, hindering its ability to yield optimal results. To overcome these challenges, a novel mechanism for PQ improvement, named SCS-ZO, is introduced. This hybridization aims tune and (∂ e) of the FLC to leverage the strengths of both algorithms and mitigate their respective limitations in order to boost the overall performance of the PQ improvement model.

It is important to note that the proposed SCS-ZO is not a simple sequential or parallel combination of ZOA and SCSO. In sequential method, the result of one algorithm would be the input of the other algorithm and in parallel method, the two algorithms would be run independently and the best solution would be chosen or averaged. Conversely, the suggested approach combines both algorithms on the position update level. In particular, both ZOA and SCSO strategies are used to update candidate solutions, and a disparity-based fusion mechanism is used to integrate the position updates of the candidate solutions into a single solution. The synergistic interaction between exploration (ZOA) and exploitation (SCSO) behaviors in every iteration facilitates ongoing exchange of information between these two behaviors, leading to enhanced convergence stability and preventing early local maxima.

Conclusion

The challenge of increasing non-linear loads (NLL) in 3-phase distribution systems, leading to unwanted harmonics and adverse effects on Power Quality (PQ), was addressed in this research. To efficiently compensate for distorted load variations, a novel approach utilizing Shunt active power filters was introduced. Specifically, the hybrid SCS-ZO was proposed. In a three-phase system with NLL, phase angle fluctuations were adeptly identified by the SOGI controller. The adjustment of controller parameters, effective elimination of system harmonics, and management of injected and source voltages were facilitated by the SCS-ZO algorithm. The comprehensive evaluation over the transformer’s lifespan encompassed considerations for harmonics, neutral current issues, voltage sag, swell, and voltage unbalance. The efficacy of the introduced controller was rigorously assessed in a dynamic load 3-phase distributed system using the MATLAB/Simulink environment. Experimental results demonstrated that the SOGI-FLC significantly enhanced system performance and stability, resulting in an improved power quality. The future scope of the proposed technique lies in its potential adaptation to smart grids, leveraging for real-time optimization and predictive maintenance. Further exploration of its applicability across diverse energy systems, along with continuous refinement in line with emerging technological advancements, could establish it as a robust solution for improving power quality and overall efficiency in modern energy distribution networks.

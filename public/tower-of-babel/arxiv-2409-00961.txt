Variational construction of singular characteristics and propagation of singularities
Piermarco Cannarsa, Wei Cheng, Jiahui Hong and Kaizhi Wang

Abstract
. On a smooth closed manifold M M , we introduce a novel theory of maximal slope curves for any pair ( ϕ , H ) (\phi,H) with ϕ \phi a semiconcave function and H H a Hamiltonian. By using the notion of maximal slope curve from gradient flow theory, the intrinsic singular characteristics constructed in [Cannarsa, P.; Cheng, W., Generalized characteristics and Lax-Oleinik operators: global theory . Calc. Var. Partial Differential Equations 56 (2017), no. 5, 56:12], the smooth approximation method developed in [Cannarsa, P.; Yu, Y. Singular dynamics for semiconcave functions . J. Eur. Math. Soc. 11 (2009), no. 5, 999–1024], and the broken characteristics studied in [Khanin, K.; Sobolevski, A., On dynamics of Lagrangian trajectories for Hamilton-Jacobi equations . Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 219 (2016), no. 2, 861–885], we prove the existence and stability of such maximal slope curves and discuss certain new weak KAM features. We also prove that maximal slope curves for any pair ( ϕ , H ) (\phi,H) are exactly broken characteristics which have right derivatives everywhere. Applying this theory, we establish a global variational construction of strict singular characteristics and broken characteristics. Moreover, we prove a result on the global propagation of cut points along generalized characteristics, as well as a result on the propagation of singular points along strict singular characteristics, for weak KAM solutions. We also obtain the continuity equation along strict singular characteristics which clarifies the mass transport nature in the problem of propagation of singularities.

1.
Introduction

The motivation for this paper is twofold. Firstly, we aim to develop a novel characteristic theory for arbitrary pairs
(\phi,H)
, where
\phi
is a semiconcave function and
H
is a Hamiltonian. Secondly, using the concept of “forward characteristics”, we intend to investigate the propagation of singularities for general semiconcave functions and viscosity solutions to Hamilton-Jacobi equations. The interplay between these two areas lies in the development of the theory of propagation of singularities over the past two decades, as well as new perspectives emerging from the theory of abstract gradient flows. This insight also allows us to handle these two problems independently.

Let
M
be a smooth connected and compact manifold without boundary and
TM
and
T^{*}M
its tangent and cotangent bundle respectively. We suppose
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following conditions:

(H1)

H
is locally Lipschitz;

(H2)

p\mapsto H(x,p)
is differentiable and
(x,p)\to H_{p}(x,p)
is continuous;

(H3)

p\mapsto H(x,p)
is strictly convex, and
\lim_{|p|_{x}\to\infty}H(x,p)/|p|=+\infty
uniformly.

In this paper, for any semiconcave function
\phi
with arbitrary modulus and a Hamiltonian satisfies conditions (H1)-(H3), we introduce a new theory of maximal slope curve for a pair
(\phi,H)
. This theory provides some new observation even in classical weak KAM theory, and also a natural variational construction of certain important objects in the study of propagation of singularities. We also study some mass transport aspect of propagation of singularities along singular characteristics.

1.1.
Maximal slope curve for a pair
(\phi,H)

We borrow some idea from the abstract theory of gradient flow, the readers can refer to
[
6
,
7
]
for more information and the references therein.

Given a semiconcave function
\phi
on
M
and a Hamiltonian
H
satisfying conditions (H1)-(H3). Let
L:TM\to\mathbb{R}
be the associated Lagrangian for
H
. Given an interval
I
which can be the whole real line, we call a locally absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:I\to M
an
maximal slope curve for a pair
(\phi,H)
and a Borel measurable selection
\mathbf{p}(x)
of the Dini superdifferential
D^{+}\phi(x)
, if
\gamma
is a solution of the EDI (Energy Dissipation Inequality)-type variational inequality

(EDI)

\phi(\gamma(t_{2}))-\phi(\gamma(t_{1}))\leqslant\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}ds,\qquad\forall t_{1},t_{2}\in I,t_{1}\leqslant t_{2},

i.e., the inequality above is indeed an equality for such a curve
\gamma
.
The most important Borel measurable selection of superdifferential
D^{+}\phi(x)
is the minimal energy selection

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)=\arg\min\{H(x,p):p\in D^{+}\phi(x)\},\qquad x\in M.

By the condition of equality in Fenchel-Young inequality
\gamma
is a maximal slope curve for a pair
(\phi,H)
and the minimal energy selection
\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}
if and only if
\gamma
satisfies the following equation

(SC)

\dot{\gamma}(t)=H_{p}(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))),\qquad a.e.\ t\in I,

We call each solution of (
SC
) a
strict singular characteristic
. It is worth noting that any maximal slope curve for the pair
(\phi,H)
is exactly a strict singular characteristic (Proposition
3.4
).

.

(2)

\phi_{k}
converges to

,

(ii)

converges to a Hamiltonian

satisfying

The wellposedness of (

1.2.
New weak KAM aspect of maximal slope curve

In the nineties, Albert Fathi developed a theory for Hamilton-Jacobi equations, called weak KAM theory, which has deep connection with Mather theory of Lagrangian dynamics of time-periodic Tonelli Lagrangian system. For any Tonelli Hamiltonian
H(x,p)
with
L(x,v)
the associated Tonelli Lagrangian, let

\displaystyle A_{t}(x,y)=\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}}\int^{t}_{0}L(\xi,\dot{\xi})\ ds,\qquad t>0,\ x,y\in M,

with
\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}=\{\xi\in W^{1,1}([0,t],M):\xi(0)=x,\xi(t)=y\}
. We call
A_{t}(x,y)
the fundamental solution (w.r.t. the associated Hamilton-Jacobi equation). For any continuous function
\phi
on
M
, we introduce the Lax-Oleinik semigroup
\{T^{-}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
and
\{T^{+}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
as

\displaystyle T^{-}_{t}\phi(x)=\inf_{y\in M}\{\phi(y)+A_{t}(y,x)\},\qquad T^{+}_{t}\phi(x)=\sup_{y\in M}\{\phi(y)-A_{t}(x,y)\},\qquad t>0,x\in M,

and set
T^{\pm}_{0}=id
. We call
\{T^{-}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
and
\{T^{+}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
the negative and positive Lax-Oleinik evolutions respectively. Consider the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(HJs)

H(x,D\phi(x))=0,\qquad x\in M,

where
0
on the right-side of (
HJs
) is Mañé’s critical value. We call
\phi
a weak KAM solution of (
HJs
) if
T^{-}_{t}\phi=\phi
for all
t\geqslant 0
. For more information on weak KAM theory see
[
31
]
.

Let
\phi
be a weak KAM solution of (
HJs
). Then
\phi\in\text{\rm SCL}\,(M)
, where
\text{\rm SCL}\,(M)
is the set of semiconcave functions on
M
with linear modulus. The following fact is basic and important, see
[
25
,
36
]
. For
x\in M
and
p\in D^{*}\phi(x)
, the set of reachable differentials of
\phi
at
x
, there is a unique
C^{1}
curve
\gamma:(-\infty,0]\to M
with
\gamma(0)=x
such that
L_{v}(x,\dot{\gamma}(0))=p
and

\displaystyle\phi(\gamma(0))-\phi(\gamma(-t))=\int^{0}_{-t}L(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})\ ds,\qquad\forall t\geqslant 0.

Such a curve
\gamma
is called a
(\phi,H)
-calibrated curve on
(-\infty,0]
in weak KAM theory, and
\phi(\cdot)
is differentiable at
\gamma(s)
for all
s\in(-\infty,0)
. It follows that
H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\equiv 0
for all
s\in(-\infty,0)
. This implies that
\gamma
is indeed a maximal slope curve from
x
in the negative direction for the pair
(\phi,H)
. One can regard the energy dissipation term
H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))
as an extension of Mañé’s critical value.

Now, for any Tonelli Hamiltonian
H
with associated Lagrangian
L
and
\phi\in\text{\rm SCL}\,(M)
, we introduce a new Lagrangian

\displaystyle L^{\#}_{\phi}(x,v):=L(x,v)+H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)),\qquad(x,v)\in TM

with
H^{\#}_{\phi}
the associated Hamiltonian. The fundamental solution with respect to
L^{\#}
is

\displaystyle A^{\#}_{t}(x,y)=\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}}\int^{t}_{0}L^{\#}_{\phi}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))\ ds,\qquad t>0,\ x,y\in M.

We define new Lax-Oleinik operators: for any continuous function
u
on
M

\displaystyle T^{\#}_{t}u(x)=\inf_{y\in M}\{u(y)+A^{\#}_{t}(y,x)\},\qquad\breve{T}^{\#}_{t}u(x)=\sup_{y\in M}\{u(y)-A^{\#}_{t}(x,y)\},\qquad t>0,x\in M.

We call
u
a weak KAM solution of negative (resp. positive) type of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(1.1)

H^{\#}_{\phi}(x,Du(x))=0,\qquad x\in M,

if
T^{\#}_{t}u=u
for any
t\geqslant 0
(resp.
\breve{T}^{\#}_{t}u=u
for any
t\geqslant 0
).

Main Result 1 implies that, for any such pair
(\phi,H)
,

\phi
is both a weak KAM solution of (
1.1
) of negative and positive type. However, if
\text{Sing}\,(\phi)\not=\varnothing
, then
\phi
is not a viscosity subsolution of (
1.1
) (Theorem
3.20
). Here we use the classical notion of viscosity (sub)solutions for discontinuous Hamiltonians.

1.3.
Various generalized characteristics

To explain the application of this new theory to the problem of propagation of singularities, we should recall various type singular characteristics in the literature.

The notion of generalized characteristics, which plays an important role to describe the evolution of singularities of viscosity solutions, was first introduced in
[
3
]
in the frame of Hamilton-Jacobi equations (
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
). We restrict our interests to the case when
H
is a Tonelli Hamiltonian. For any
\phi\in\text{\rm SCL}\,(M)
, a Lipschitz curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the following differential inclusion is called a
generalized characteristic
from
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(GC)

\begin{cases}\dot{\gamma}(t)\in\text{co}\,H_{p}(\gamma(t),D^{+}\phi(\gamma(t))),\quad a.e.\ t\in[0,+\infty)\\
\gamma(0)=x.\end{cases}

In
[
3
]
, the authors proved if
\phi
is a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle H(x,D\phi(x))=0,\qquad x\in\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open set, then there exists a Lipschitz continuous solution
\mathbf{x}
of (
GC
) and
\delta>0
such that
\gamma(t)\in\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
for
t\in[0,\delta]
provided
x\in\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
. Here,
\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
is the set of the points of non-differentiability of
\phi
and it is called the
singular set
of
\phi
. Using an approximation method developed in
[
38
,
26
]
, this result has been further extended to the case where
H
is of class
C^{1}
and strictly convex in
p
-variable, and
\phi
is an arbitrary semiconcave function.
From the control theory point of view, the generalized characteristic differential inclusion comes from some relaxation problem, the readers can understand this point from the original proof in
[
3
]
, and also in the current paper (Section 4). Due to the presence of the convex hull in the right-side of (
GC
), no evidence ensures the uniqueness of the solution of (
GC
).

Invoking a celebrated paper by Khanin and Sobolevski (
[
34
]
, see also
[
37
]
), we consider the equation

(BC)

\begin{cases}\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t)=H_{p}(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))),\qquad\forall t\in[0,+\infty),\\
\gamma(0)=x.\end{cases}

A solution of (
BC
) is called a
broken characteristic
in the literature. In
[
19
]
we introduce the notion of strict singular characteristic, which is strictly related to (
BC
).

For the local existence of solutions for (
BC
) and (
SC
), there are several proofs (
[
34
,
20
,
27
,
28
]
). The original one
[
34
]
used vanishing viscosity approximation methods in case
\phi
is a viscosity solution of an evolutionary Hamilton-Jacobi equation to prove the existence result for (
BC
), and
[
20
]
used a standard approximation by convolution for the static equation. In
[
27
]
and
[
28
]
, some local existence results for (
BC
) for viscosity solutions to evolutionary Hamilton-Jacobi equations with smooth initial data and weak KAM solution in 2D were obtained, using an intrinsic approach based on the analysis of the underlying characteristic systems.

Applying our method of maximal slope curves for strict singular characteristic, we can also give a clear explanation of the notion of broken characteristics. We prove that the two notions, broken characteristic and strict singular characteristic, coincide for any pair
(\phi,H)
.

Main Result 2
.

Suppose

1.4.
Intrinsic construction of strict singular characteristics

The notion of
intrinsic singular characteristic
was introduced first in
[
18
]
and then developed in
[
21
,
22
]
with some important topological applications to weak KAM theory and geometry. Suppose
\phi\in\text{\rm SCL}\,(M)
and
H
is a Tonelli Hamiltonian. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define a curve
\mathbf{y}_{x}:[0,\tau(\phi)]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

(1.2)

\mathbf{y}_{x}(t):=\begin{cases}x,&t=0,\\
\arg\max\{\phi(y)-A_{t}(x,y):y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},&t\in(0,\tau(\phi)],\end{cases}

where
\tau(\phi)>0
is a constant (see the beginning of Section 4). We call
\mathbf{y}_{x}
an
intrinsic singular characteristic
from
x
. One can prove that
\mathbf{y}_{x}
can be extended to
[0,+\infty)
since
\tau(\phi)
is independent of
x
. By a theorem of Marie-claude Arnaud (
[
10
]
), for any
t\in(0,\tau(\phi)]
, if
\xi_{t}\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,\mathbf{y}_{x}(t)}
is the minimal curve for
A_{t}(x,\mathbf{y}_{x}(t))
, then
\xi_{t}
satisfies the differential equation (Proposition
4.1
)

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\dot{\xi}_{t}(s)=H_{p}(\xi_{t}(s),DT^{+}_{t-s}\phi(\xi_{t}(s))),&s\in[0,t),\\
\xi_{t}(0)=x.\end{cases}

Thus, it is natural to introduce a time-dependent vector field on
[-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows: fix
T>0
and let

\displaystyle\Delta:-T=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\cdots<\tau_{N-1}<\tau_{N}=T

be a partition of the interval
[-T,T]
with

. For

, let
\tau_{\Delta}^{+}(t)=\inf\{\tau_{i}|\,\tau_{i}>t\}
. We define the vector field

\displaystyle W_{\Delta}(t,x)=H_{p}(x,\nabla T^{+}_{\tau_{\Delta}^{+}(t)-t}\phi(x)),\qquad t\in[-T,T),x\in M.

Notice that, for any

, the differential equation

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\dot{\gamma}(t)=W_{\Delta}(t,\gamma(t)),\qquad t\in[-T,T],\\
\gamma(0)=x,\end{cases}

admits a unique piecewise
C^{1}
solution
\gamma
. We prove that the vector field
W_{\Delta}(t,x)
converges to
H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))
as
|\Delta|\to 0
, as well as the solution curves

1.5.
Propagation of singularities

Recall that for every weak KAM solution
\phi
of (

\text{\rm Sing}\,(\phi)\subset\text{\rm Cut}\,(\phi)\subset\overline{\text{\rm Sing}\,(\phi)}

.

1.6.
Mass transport

The vector field
H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))
discussed in this paper does not determine a
Regular Lagrangian Flow
(see, for instance,
[
8
,
9
]
) since the collision and focus of forward characteristics. One cannot use the classical DiPerna-Lions theory to deal with mass transport along strict singular characteristics. On the other hand, no evidence shows that the vector field
H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))
ensures the well-posedness of the associated equation
\dot{x}=H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))
in the forward direction. However, we can use a lifting method to consider a dynamical system on the set of strict singular characteristics even without uniqueness.

.

\phi

M
,

is a Hamiltonian satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
. For any

, let
\mathscr{S}_{T}
be the family of strict singular characteristics
\gamma:[0,T]\to M
for

. Then the following holds true:

(1)

There exists a measurable map
\gamma:M\to\mathscr{S}_{T}
,
x\mapsto\gamma(x,\cdot)
such that
\gamma(x,0)=x
for all
x\in M
(Proposition

converge to the strict singular characteristics system in the aspect of both vector field and solution curve. Using this intrinsic approach, we obtain an alternative proof of the existence of maximal slope curves, with quite natural geometric intuition. In Section 5, we study global propagation of singularities along strict singular characteristics of a given weak KAM solution. We also obtain a new result on the global propagation of cut points along any generalized characteristics. In Section 6, we introduce the continuity equation for strict singular characteristics and its relation with the cut locus and

Proposition 3.2

\gamma
is a solution of the differential inclusion

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(t)\in H_{p}(\gamma(t),D^{+}\phi(\gamma(t))),\qquad a.e.\ t\in I,

then
\gamma
is a solution of the differential equation

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(t)=H_{p}(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))),\qquad a.e.\ t\in I.

Proof.

Since
\gamma:I\to M
is a maximal slope curve for
(\phi,H)
and
\mathbf{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(\gamma(t_{2}))-\phi(\gamma(t_{1}))=

\displaystyle\,\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}(\gamma(s))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s))\Big\}\ ds,\quad\forall t_{1},t_{2}\in I,\ t_{1}<t_{2}.

Since the converse inequality is true for any selection of
D^{+}\phi
, it follows that
\gamma
is a strict singular characteristic for the pair
(\phi,H)
.
∎

The proposition above implies that any maximal slope curve for the pair
(\phi,H)
is exactly a strict singular characteristic. This means that the minimal energy selection
\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)
plays a crucial role in this theory. In this case, the notions of maximal slope curve and strict singular characteristic coincide.

3.2.
Stability of strict singular characteristics

We will adress the existence of maximal slope curves in the next subsection. In this subsection, we study the stability issue for strict singular characteristics at first, which is known only for quadratic Hamiltonians (see
[
4
]
). This will help us to obtain the existence of strict singular characteristics in the general case.

The following lemma implies that the minimal-energy function is lower semicontinuous, which is essential for the proof of our stability result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\{H_{k}\}
be a sequence of Hamiltonians satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
,
\{\phi_{k}\}
be a sequence of
\omega
-semiconcave functions on
M
, and
\{x_{k}\}\subset M
. If

(i)

\phi_{k}
converges to
\phi
uniformly on
M
,

(ii)

H_{k}
converges to a Hamiltonian
H
satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
uniformly on compact subset,

(iii)

\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}=x
,

then

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}H_{k}(x_{k},\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(x_{k}))\geqslant H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)).

Proof.

Because of the local nature, we work on Euclidean space. For any subsequence
\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(x_{k_{i}})
converging to some
p
as
i\to\infty
, we have that
p\in D^{+}\phi(x)
by Lemma
2.2
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}H_{k_{i}}(x_{k_{i}},\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(x_{k_{i}}))=H(x,p)\geqslant H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.5
, if
\lim_{k\to\infty}H_{k}(x_{k},\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(x_{k}))=H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))
, then we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(x_{k})=\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x).

Proof.

We work on Euclidean space. Given
\varepsilon>0
, if
D^{+}\phi(x)\subset B(\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x),\varepsilon)
, by Lemma
2.2
we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}|\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(x_{k})-\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)|\leqslant\varepsilon.

If
D^{+}\phi(x)\setminus B(\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x),\varepsilon)\not=\varnothing
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\min_{p\in D^{+}\phi(x)\setminus B(\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x),\varepsilon)}H(x,p)>H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))+\delta.

In this case, if there exists a subsequence
\{x_{k_{i}}\}
such that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(x_{k_{i}})-\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)|>\varepsilon,\qquad\forall i\in\mathbb{N},

then by Lemma
2.2
we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}H_{k_{i}}(x_{k_{i}},\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(x_{k_{i}}))=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{i\to\infty}H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(x_{k_{i}}))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\min_{p\in D^{+}\phi(x)\setminus B(\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x),\varepsilon)}H(x,p)>H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))+\delta.

This contradicts the assumption that
\lim_{i\to\infty}H_{k_{i}}(x_{k_{i}},\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(x_{k_{i}}))=H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))
. In sum, we have that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}|\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(x_{k})-\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)|\leqslant\varepsilon.

Our conclusion follows.
∎

Theorem 3.7

(Stability of strict singular characteristics)
.

Let
\{H_{k}\}
be a sequence of Hamiltonians satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
,
\{\phi_{k}\}
be a sequence of
\omega
-semiconcave functions on
M
, and
\gamma_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to M
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a sequence of strict singular characteristics for the pair
(\phi_{k},H_{k})
. We suppose the following condition:

(i)

\phi_{k}
converges to
\phi
uniformly on
M
,

(ii)

H_{k}
converges to a Hamiltonian
H
satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
uniformly on compact subset,

(iii)

\gamma_{k}
converges to
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
uniformly on compact subset.

Then
\gamma
is a strict singular characteristic for the pair
(\phi,H)
.

Moreover, there exists a subsequence of strict singular characteristics
\{\gamma_{k_{i}}\}
such that

(3.4)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(\gamma_{k_{i}}(t))=\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)),\qquad a.e.\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Fix any
T>0
. By (
VI
), any strict singular characteristic
\gamma_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(\gamma_{k}(T))-\phi_{k}(\gamma_{k}(-T))=\int^{T}_{-T}\Big\{L_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\dot{\gamma}_{k}(s))+H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))\Big\}\ ds.

Since
\gamma_{k}
converges to
\gamma
uniformly on
[-T,T]
,
\dot{\gamma}_{k}
weakly converges to
\dot{\gamma}
in
L^{1}
, by a standard lower semicontinuity result (see
[
17
, Theorem 3.6]
or
[
16
, Section 3.4]
) we conclude

(3.5)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}L_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\dot{\gamma}_{k}(s))\ ds\geqslant\int^{T}_{-T}L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\ ds.

Invoking Fatou lemma and Lemma
3.5
, we conclude that

(3.6)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))\ ds\geqslant\int^{T}_{-T}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\ ds.

Summing up (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we have that

\displaystyle\,\phi(\gamma(T))-\phi(\gamma(-T))=\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{k}(\gamma_{k}(T))-\phi_{k}(\gamma_{k}(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}\Big\{L_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\dot{\gamma}_{k}(s))+H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}L_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\dot{\gamma}_{k}(s))\ ds+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))\ ds

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\int^{T}_{-T}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\phi(\gamma(T))-\phi(\gamma(-T)).

It follows that, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\phi(\gamma(T))-\phi(\gamma(-T))=\int^{T}_{-T}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds,

which implies
\gamma
is a strict singular characteristic for the pair
(\phi,H)
.

Finally, we turn to prove (
3.4
). By (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and the equality

\displaystyle\,\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}\Big\{L_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\dot{\gamma}_{k}(s))+H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{T}_{-T}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds

we find

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))\ ds=\int^{T}_{-T}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\ ds.

By Lemma
3.5
we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\min\Big\{0,H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))-H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}=0,\qquad\forall s\in[-T,T].

Then, Lebesgue’s theorem and the above two identities yield

\displaystyle\,\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}\Big|H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))-H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big|\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}\Big\{H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))-H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle\,-2\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}\min\Big\{0,H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))-H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{-T}\Big\{H_{k}(\gamma_{k}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k},H_{k}}(\gamma_{k}(s)))-H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,0.

Therefore, there exists a subsequence
\{\gamma_{k_{i}}\}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}H_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{k_{i}}(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(\gamma_{k_{i}}(s)))=H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s))),\qquad a.e.\ s\in[-T,T].

By Lemma
3.6
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi_{k_{i}},H_{k_{i}}}(\gamma_{k_{i}}(s))=\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)),\qquad a.e.\ s\in[-T,T].

Thus, (
3.4
) follows by the arbitrariness of
T
.
∎

Let

,

3.3.
Existence of strict singular characteristics: general cases

Now we will prove the existence of strict singular characteristics in the general case by an approximation method firstly introduced in
[
38
,
26
]
for semiconcave functions with linear modulus.

Let us recall the approximation method used in the paper
[
26
]
. It is known that for any semiconcave function
\phi
on
M
with Lipschitz constant
L_{0}
, semiconcavity constant
C_{0}
and any fixed
x\in M
, there exists a sequence of smooth functions
\{\phi_{k}\}\subset C^{\infty}(M)
such that:

(i)

Each
\|D\phi_{k}\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant L_{0}
and
D^{2}\phi_{k}
is bounded above by
C_{0}I
uniformly.

(ii)

\phi_{k}
converges to
\phi
uniformly on
M
as
k\to\infty
.

(iii)

\lim_{k\to\infty}D\phi_{k}(x)=\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)
for given fixed
x\in M
.

Consider the differential equation

(3.7)

\begin{cases}\dot{\gamma}(t)=H_{p}(\gamma(t),D\phi_{k}(\gamma(t))),\qquad t\in[0,\infty),\\
\gamma(0)=x,\end{cases}

and denote by
\gamma_{k}
the unique solution of (
3.7
). The family
\{\gamma_{k}\}
is equi-Lipschitz since
D\phi_{k}
is uniformly bounded. Invoking the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem, by taking a subsequence if necessary, we can suppose that
\gamma_{k}
converges to a Lipschitz curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
uniformly on all compact subsets. In
[
26
]
, it is proved that there exists such a limiting curve
\gamma
which is a generalized characteristic and it propagates singularities locally. Moreover, such a generalized characteristic satisfies the property that
\dot{\gamma}^{+}(0)
exists and

(3.8)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\operatorname*{ess\ sup}_{s\in[0,t]}|\dot{\gamma}(s)-\dot{\gamma}^{+}(0)|=0.

From Theorem
3.7
we conclude that
\gamma
is indeed a strict singular characteristic which means there is no convex hull in the right side of the generalized characteristic differential inclusion (
GC
).

To discuss strict singular characteristics for general semiconcave functions using the approximation method, we need an approximation lemma for general semiconcave functions (see
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
for linear modulus).

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
U,V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded open convex set,
\overline{U}\subset V
. Suppose
\phi:V\to\mathbb{R}
is an
\omega
-semiconcave function with Lipschitz constant
L_{0}>0
. Then, there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}\subset C^{\infty}(U,\mathbb{R})
such that

(i)

\phi_{k}
converges to
\phi
uniformly on
U
;

(ii)

all functions
\phi_{k}
are equi-Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
L_{0}
;

(iii)

all functions
\phi_{k}
are
\omega
-semiconcave.

For any
\omega
-semiconcave function
\phi
on a compact manifold
M
with Lipschitz constant
L_{0}>0
, there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}\subset C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, with
\phi_{k}

\omega
-semiconcave and equi-Lipschitz of constant
L_{0}
, such that
\phi_{k}
converges to
\phi
uniformly on
M
.

Proof.

We let
\varepsilon=\text{\rm dist}\,(\overline{U},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus V)>0
. Taking a mollifier
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\eta\leqslant 1,\qquad\eta|_{B(0,1)}=1,\qquad\eta|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,2)}=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta(x)\ dx=1,

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define
\phi_{k}:U\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(x)=\Big(\frac{2k}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi(x-y)\cdot\eta\Big(\frac{2k}{\varepsilon}y\Big)\ dy,\qquad x\in U.

Items (i) and (ii) follows from the properties of mollifiers. Now, we turn to the proof of (iii).

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in U
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
we have that

\displaystyle\,\lambda\phi_{k}(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\phi_{k}(x_{2})-\phi_{k}(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\Big(\frac{2k}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\lambda\phi(x_{1}-y)+(1-\lambda)\phi(x_{2}-y)-\phi(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2}-y)]\cdot\eta\Big(\frac{2k}{\varepsilon}y\Big)\ dy

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,\Big(\frac{2k}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda(1-\lambda)|x_{1}-x_{2}|\omega(|x_{1}-x_{2}|)\cdot\eta\Big(\frac{2k}{\varepsilon}y\Big)\ dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lambda(1-\lambda)|x_{1}-x_{2}|\omega(|x_{1}-x_{2}|).

Taking
k\to\infty
, this completes the proof of (iii). The last assertion is a consequence of the facts we have shown in Euclidean case, since
M
is compact.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\phi
be a semiconcave function on
M
,
H
be a Hamiltonian satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
. Then for any

, there exists a strict singular characteristic

with

for the pair

By Lemma
3.9
, there exists a sequence of smooth functions

Lemma 3.11

Lemma 3.12

\phi

Theorem 3.13
.

Suppose
\phi
is a semiconcave function on
M
,
H
is a Hamiltonian satisfying conditions
(H1)-(H3)
, and
\gamma:[0,T]\to M

.

(1)

The right derivative
\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t)
exists for all

and

(BC)

\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t)=H_{p}(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))),\qquad\forall t\in[0,T).

(2)

For all

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\ ds=H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))).

Proof.

Since the local nature we work on Euclidean space. For any
t\in[0,T)
, due to the Lipschitz continuity of
\gamma
, the family

\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{\gamma(\tau)-\gamma(t)}{\tau-t}\Big\}_{\tau\in(t,T]}

is uniformly bounded. Picking up any sequence
\tau_{i}\searrow 0^{+}
as

such that

(3.10)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\gamma(\tau_{i})-\gamma(t)}{\tau_{i}-t}=v\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

we have

(3.11)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\phi(\gamma(\tau_{i}))-\phi(\gamma(t))}{\tau_{i}-t}=\partial\phi(\gamma(t),v).

Since
\gamma
is a strict singular characteristic and
L(x,\cdot)
is convex, for any
\tau\in(t,T]

\displaystyle\,\phi(\gamma(\tau))-\phi(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(s))+[L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))-L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(s))]

\displaystyle\,\quad\quad\qquad+H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))+[H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))]\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{L(\gamma(t),v)+\langle L_{v}(\gamma(t),v),\dot{\gamma}(s)-v\rangle+H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle\,\qquad+\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))-L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(s))\Big\}\ ds+\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\Big\}\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{L(\gamma(t),v)+H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\Big\}\ ds+\int^{\tau}_{t}\langle L_{v}(\gamma(t),v),\dot{\gamma}(s)-v\rangle\ ds

\displaystyle\,\qquad+\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))-L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(s))\Big\}\ ds+\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\Big\}\ ds.

Thank to the Lipschitz property of

and
\gamma
,

(3.12)

\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}|L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))-L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(s))|\ ds\leqslant\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}C(\tau-t)\ ds=0.

By Lemma
3.5
we obtain that

(3.13)

\liminf_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big\{H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\Big\}ds\geqslant 0,

and (

) yields

(3.14)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{\tau_{i}-t}\int^{\tau_{i}}_{t}\langle L_{v}(\gamma(t),v),\dot{\gamma}(s)-v\rangle\ ds=\lim_{i\to\infty}\Big\langle L_{v}(\gamma(t),v),\frac{\gamma(\tau_{i})-\gamma(t)}{\tau_{i}-t}-v\Big\rangle=0.

Combining (
3.12
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\phi(\gamma(\tau_{i}))-\phi(\gamma(t))}{\tau_{i}-t}\geqslant L(\gamma(t),v)+H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))).

Together with (
3.11
) and Lemma
3.12
it yields
v=H_{p}(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))
, and

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t)=\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{\gamma(\tau)-\gamma(t)}{\tau-t}=H_{p}(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))).

This completes the proof of (1).

The proof of (2) need the same reasoning as above. For any
t\in[0,T)
we have that

\displaystyle\,L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t))+H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))=\partial\phi(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}(\phi(\gamma(\tau))-\phi(\gamma(t)))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\bigg\{\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big[L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t))+H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\Big]\ ds+\int^{\tau}_{t}\langle L_{v}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t)),\dot{\gamma}(s)-\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t)\rangle\ ds

\displaystyle\,\qquad+\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big[L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))-L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(s))\Big]\ ds+\int^{\tau}_{t}\Big[H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\Big]\ ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t))+H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))

\displaystyle\qquad+\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}\big[H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\big]\ ds,

where we have used (
3.12
), the convexity of
L(x,\cdot)
and the fact that
\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t)=H_{p}(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))
from (1). It follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}\big[H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\big]\ ds\leqslant 0.

Together with Lemma
3.5
, the above inequality leads to our conclusion. ∎

3.5.
Weakly right-continuous curves in space of probability measures

Theorem
3.13
implies, at least in the Euclidean case, that for any strict singular characteristic
\gamma
the curve
\dot{\gamma}^{+}:[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is well defined. Moreover, the relation

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{+}(t)=\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}\dot{\gamma}^{+}(s)\ ds

implies that each

is a density point of
\dot{\gamma}^{+}
from the right-hand side. In fact
(\gamma,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma))
and

determine two curves of probability measures on

and

implies that such curves have some weak right continuity property.

Theorem 3.14
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
3.13
, for any
t\in[0,T)
we define probability measures
\mu^{*}_{[t,\tau]}
,
\tau\in(t,T]
, on
T^{*}M

\displaystyle\int_{T^{*}M}f\ d\mu^{*}_{[t,\tau]}=\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}f(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\ ds,\qquad\forall f\in C_{c}(T^{*}M,\mathbb{R}).

Then

\delta^{*}_{\gamma(t)}:=\delta^{*}_{\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))}
as
\tau\to t^{+}
, where
\delta^{*}_{x,p}
is the Dirac measure supported on
(x,p)\in T^{*}M
.

Proof.

We only prove in Euclidean case. We first claim for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.15)

\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(I_{t,\tau,\varepsilon})}{\tau-t}=0

where
\mathscr{L}^{1}
stands for the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
and

\displaystyle I_{t,\tau,\varepsilon}:=\{s\in[t,\tau]:|(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))|>\varepsilon\}.

In fact, by Lemma
3.6
for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
\delta>0
such that if
y-\gamma(t)\leqslant\delta
and
H(y,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(y))\leqslant H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))+\varepsilon_{1}
, then
|\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(y)-\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))|\leqslant\varepsilon/2
. Denote by
C_{0}
a Lipschitz constant of
\gamma
and set
\delta_{1}=\min\{\frac{\delta}{C_{0}},\frac{\varepsilon}{2C_{0}}\}
. Thus, if
\tau\in(t,t+\delta_{1})
then

\displaystyle|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|\leqslant C_{0}(s-t)\leqslant C_{0}\delta_{1}\leqslant\min\big\{\delta,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big\},\qquad\forall s\in[t,\tau].

In addition, if
H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\leqslant H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))+\varepsilon_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\,|(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|+|\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s))-\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t))|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.

This implies that

\displaystyle\,\{s\in[t,\tau]:H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\leqslant H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))+\varepsilon_{1}\}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\,\{s\in[t,\tau]:|(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))|\leqslant\varepsilon\}.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}}:=\{s\in[t,\tau]:H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))>H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))+\varepsilon_{1}\}\supset I_{t,\tau,\varepsilon}.

Together with Lemma
3.5
and Theorem
3.13

\displaystyle\,H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))=\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\ ds\quad(\text{Theorem \ref{thm:right_derivative}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\Big\{\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int_{J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}}}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s))\ ds+\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int_{[t,\tau]\setminus J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}}}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s))\ ds\Big\}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\Big\{\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}})}{\tau-t}(H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))+\varepsilon_{1})+\Big(1-\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}})}{\tau-t}\Big)\inf_{s\in[t,\tau]}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\Big\{\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}})}{\tau-t}\big(H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))-\inf_{s\in[t,\tau]}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}})}{\tau-t}\cdot\varepsilon_{1}+\inf_{s\in[t,\tau]}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\Big\}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}})}{\tau-t}\cdot\varepsilon_{1}+\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\inf_{s\in[t,\tau]}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}})}{\tau-t}\cdot\varepsilon_{1}+H(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))\qquad(\text{Lemma \ref{lem:lsc1}})

It follows

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(I_{t,\tau,\varepsilon})}{\tau-t}\leqslant\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{\mathscr{L}^{1}(J_{t,\tau,\varepsilon_{1}})}{\tau-t}\leqslant 0.

This leads to (
3.15
).

Now we note that there exists a compact subset

such that

\displaystyle(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\in K,\qquad\forall x\in M.

For any

we set

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\Big|\int_{T^{*}M}f\ d\mu^{*}_{[t,\tau]}-\int_{T^{*}M}f\ d\delta^{*}_{\gamma(t)}\Big|

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}|f(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))-f(\gamma(t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t)))|\ ds

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\cdot\mathscr{L}^{1}(I_{t,\tau,\delta})\cdot 2M_{f}+\varepsilon=\varepsilon.

Since

weakly converges to

Lemma 3.16

Under the assumptions of Theorem

In view of Theorem

without loss of generality. Then, by Lemma

\displaystyle\,H(\gamma(t_{2}),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t_{2}))-H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s))

The above estimate, together with Theorem

\displaystyle H(\gamma(t_{1}),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t_{1}))=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{t\to t_{1}^{+}}\frac{1}{t-t_{1}}\int^{t}_{t_{1}}H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s))\ ds

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\lim_{t\to t_{1}^{+}}\frac{1}{t-t_{1}}\int^{t}_{t_{1}}\big[H(\gamma(t_{2}),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(t_{2}))-\lambda(t_{2}-s)\big]\ ds

3.7.
New weak KAM aspects of maximal slope curves

For any
\phi\in\text{SCL}\,(M)
and Tonelli Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
with associated Tonelli Lagrangian
L:TM\to\mathbb{R}
, inspired by the maximal slope curves for the pair
(\phi,H)
, we introduce a new Lagrangian

\displaystyle L^{\#}_{\phi}(x,v):=L(x,v)+H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)),\quad x\in M,v\in T_{x}M.

Let
H^{\#}_{\phi}
be the associated Hamiltonian of
L^{\#}_{\phi}
, more precisely,

\displaystyle H^{\#}_{\phi}(x,p)=\sup_{v\in T_{x}M}\{\langle p,v\rangle-L^{\#}_{\phi}(x,v)\}=H(x,p)-H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)),\quad x\in M,p\in T^{*}_{x}M.

Observe that the Lagrangian
L^{\#}_{\phi}
(resp. the Hamiltoninan
H^{\#}_{\phi}
) is strictly convex and superlinear with respect to the
v
-variable (resp.
p
-variable) but only lower-semicontinuous (resp. upper-semicontinuous) in
x
-variable. We define

\displaystyle A^{\#}_{t}(x,y)=\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}}\int^{t}_{0}L^{\#}_{\phi}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))\ ds,\qquad t>0,\ x,y\in M,

the fundamental solution associated with
L^{\#}_{\phi}
. Due to the lower semicotinuity of
L^{\#}_{\phi}
, the above infimum is achieved and is Lipschitz by a classical result in
[
30
]
.

We introduce the Lax-Oleinik operators with respect to this new Hamiltonian: for any continuous function
u:M\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle T^{\#}_{t}u(x)=\,\inf_{y\in M}\{u(y)+A^{\#}_{t}(y,x)\},\qquad\breve{T}^{\#}_{t}u(x)=\,\sup_{y\in M}\{u(y)-A^{\#}_{t}(x,y)\},\qquad t>0,x\in M.

Definition 3.19
.

We call
u
a weak KAM solution of negative (resp. positive) type of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(3.18)

H^{\#}_{\phi}(x,Du(x))=0,\qquad x\in M,

if
T^{\#}_{t}u=u
for any
t\geqslant 0
(resp.
\breve{T}^{\#}_{t}u=u
) for any
t\geqslant 0
.

Theorem 3.20
.

Suppose
\phi\in\text{\rm SCL}\,(M)
,
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
is a Tonelli Hamiltonian and
H^{\#}_{\phi}
is defined as above.

(1)

\phi
is weak KAM solution of both negative and positive type of (
3.18
).

(2)

\phi
is a viscosity solution
2
2

2

of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(3)

, then

is not a viscosity subsolution of (

).

Proof.

The proof of (1) is direct from (

) and Theorem

. Indeed, for any locally absolutely continuous curve

, the following inequality holds by Proposition

\displaystyle\phi(\gamma(t_{2}))-\phi(\gamma(t_{1}))\leqslant\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(s)))\ ds,\qquad\forall t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\ t_{1}<t_{2}.

\displaystyle\phi(\gamma(t_{2}))-\phi(\gamma(t_{1}))=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+H(\gamma(s),D\phi(\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\ ds.

This implies
\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
is a calibrated curve for
(\phi,H)
and leads to a contradiction to assertion (1). Thus, we have
\chi_{\text{\rm Sing}\,(\phi)}(\gamma)\mathscr{L}^{1}((t_{1},t_{2}))>0
. This completes the proof of (2).

Now we turn to prove (3). For any open interval
(t_{1},t_{2})\subset[0,+\infty)
, by assertion (2), there exists
t_{3}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
such that
\gamma(t_{3})\in\text{\rm Sing}\,(\phi)
. Then Theorem
5.8
implies there exists
t_{3}<t_{4}<t_{2}
such that
\gamma(t)\in\text{\rm Sing}\,(\phi)
for all
t\in[t_{3},t_{4}]
. Thus, the set
\text{\rm int}\,(\{t\in[0,+\infty):\gamma(t)\in\text{\rm Sing}\,(\phi)\})
is dense in
[0,+\infty)
.
∎

5.4.
Concluding remarks

The uniqueness of strict singular characteristic is still open in general. We raise the following problems:

(A)

Does the uniqueness of strict singular characteristic
\gamma:[0,+\infty)\to M
from a given initial point
\gamma(0)=x
holds in general? or

(B)

can one have an example where the two methods used in the proofs of Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
4.6
, respectively, lead to two distinct strict singular characteristics?

We remark that for mechanical Hamiltonians, the associated strict singular characteristic is exactly the solution of the generalized gradient system. In Euclidean case, Albano proved in
[
2
]
a global propagation result in the following sense: if
x\in\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
, then the unique solution
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the generalized gradient system propagates singularities of the weak KAM solution
\phi
, i.e.,
\gamma(t)\in\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
for all
t\geqslant 0
. Thus, it is natural to raise the following problem:

(C)

Does global propagation of
\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
hold in general?

(D)

At least for the Hamiltonian
H(x,p)
with quadratic dependence on
p
, is it true that
\text{Cut}\,(\phi)=\overline{\text{Sing}\,(\phi)}
?

We finally remark that the energy dissipation term in the discrete scheme comes from the non-commutativity of the positive and negative Lax-Oleinik semigroups. Let us recall the explanation of cut time function in terms of the commutators of
T^{\pm}_{t}
in
[
23
]
. For any weak KAM solution
\phi
of (
HJs
), the cut time function is given by

\displaystyle\tau_{\phi}(x)=\sup\{t\geqslant 0:(T^{-}_{t}\circ T^{+}_{t}-T^{+}_{t}\circ T^{-}_{t})\phi(x)=0\},

and the cut locus by
\text{Cut}\,(\phi)=\{x\in M:\tau_{\phi}(x)=0\}
. On the other hand, for any
0<t<\tau(\phi)
and
x\in M
we have that

\displaystyle(T^{-}_{t}\circ T^{+}_{t}-T^{+}_{t}\circ T^{-}_{t})\phi(x)=

\displaystyle\,\phi(x)-T^{+}_{t}\phi(x)=-\int^{t}_{0}\frac{d}{ds}T^{+}_{s}\phi(x)\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\int^{t}_{0}H(x,DT^{+}_{s}\phi(x))\ ds.

So, the term
H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))
embodies the non-commutativity of Lax-Oleinik operators
T^{\pm}
, and the creation and evolution of singularities.

6.
Mass transport aspect of strict singular characteristic

To study the mass transport along strict singular characteristics, one cannot apply the standard DiPerna-Lions theory to deduce the continuity equation for which the solution is a curve of probability measures determined by the measures driven by the flow of the vector field, since the collision and focus of the classical characteristics. However, one can use a lifting method to consider a dynamical system on the set of strict singular characteristics even without uniqueness.

Let
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a semiconcave function and
H
be a Hamiltonian satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
. Fix
T>0
, let
\mathscr{S}_{T}
be the family of strict singular characteristics
\gamma:[0,T]\to M
for the pair
(\phi,H)
. Consider the set-valued map
\Gamma:M\rightrightarrows\mathscr{S}_{T}
defined by

\displaystyle x\mapsto\Gamma(x)=\{\gamma\in\mathscr{S}_{T}:\gamma(0)=x\}.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose
\phi
is a semiconcave function on
M
and
H
is a Hamiltonian satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
. Then the following holds:

(1)

\mathscr{S}_{T}
is a separable and compact metric subspace of
C^{0}([0,T],M)
under the
C^{0}
-norm.

(2)

The set-valued map
\Gamma
is nonempty, compact-valued and measurable.

(3)

The set-valued map
\Gamma
admits a measurable selection
\gamma:M\to\mathscr{S}_{T}
,
x\mapsto\gamma(x,\cdot)
such that
\gamma(x,0)=x
for all
x\in M
.

Proof.

The assertion of (1) is immediate from Corollary
3.8
. Now we turn to the proof of (2). For any
x\in M
, by the existence of strict singular characteristic from
x
(Theorem
3.10
) and the stability property (Theorem
3.7
),
\Gamma(x)\not=\varnothing
and
\Gamma(x)
is compact. Let
K\subset\mathscr{S}_{T}
be any closed subset,
K
is compact. The pre-image

\displaystyle\{x\in M:\Gamma(x)\cup K\not=\varnothing\}=\{\gamma(0):\gamma\in K\}

is also compact, since the continuity of the map
\gamma\mapsto\gamma(0)
. This implies
x\mapsto\Gamma(x)
is also measurable. Finally, (3) is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.5
.
∎

Now for any measurable map
\gamma
in Proposition
6.1
we introduce a map

\displaystyle\Phi_{\gamma}^{t}(x)=\gamma(x,t),\qquad t\in[0,T],\ x\in M.

For any
t\in[0,T]
,
\Phi_{\gamma}^{t}:M\to M
is measurable, and
\Phi^{0}_{\gamma}=\text{id}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose
\phi
is a semiconcave function on
M
,
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
is a Hamiltonian satisfying
(H1)-(H3)
, and
\gamma:M\to\mathscr{S}_{T}
satisfies the condition in Proposition
6.1
(3). Then for any
\bar{\mu}\in\mathscr{P}(M)
, the set of Borel probability measures on
M
, the curve
\mu_{t}=(\Phi^{t}_{\gamma})_{\#}\bar{\mu}
,
t\in[0,T]
in
\mathscr{P}(M)
satisfies the continuity equation

(CE)

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}\mu+\text{\rm div}(H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\cdot\mu)=0,\\
\mu_{0}=\bar{\mu}.\end{cases}

and we have the following properties:

(1)

\int_{M}\phi\mu_{T}-\int_{M}\phi\mu_{0}=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{M}\big\{L(x,H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)))+H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\big\}\ d\mu_{s}ds
.

(2)

For any
t\in[0,T)
,
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\int_{M}g\ d\mu_{t}=\int_{M}\nabla g(x)\cdot H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{t}.

(3)

For any
t\in[0,T)
and
f\in C_{c}(T^{*}M,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{t}=\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}\int_{M}f(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{s}ds.

Proof.

Since
\mu_{0}=(\Phi^{0}_{\gamma})_{\#}\mu_{0}=(\text{id})_{\#}\mu_{0}=\bar{\mu}
, to prove (
CE
) it suffices to check the continuity equation. For any
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
0\leqslant r<t\leqslant T
we have

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}g\ d\mu_{t}-\int_{M}g\ d\mu_{r}=\int_{M}g\circ\Phi_{\gamma}^{t}\ d\bar{\mu}-\int_{M}g\circ\Phi_{\gamma}^{r}\ d\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}g(\gamma(x,t))-g(\gamma(x,r))\ d\bar{\mu}=\int_{M}\int^{t}_{r}\frac{d}{ds}g(\gamma(x,s))\ dsd\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}\int^{t}_{r}\nabla g(\gamma(x,s))\cdot\dot{\gamma}(x,s)\ dsd\bar{\mu}=\int_{M}\int^{t}_{r}\nabla g(\gamma(x,s))\cdot H_{p}(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s)))\ dsd\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{t}_{r}\int_{M}\nabla g(\gamma(x,s))\cdot H_{p}(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s)))\ d\bar{\mu}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{t}_{r}\int_{M}\nabla g(x)\cdot H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{s}ds.

It follows that
t\mapsto\int_{M}g\ d\mu_{t}
is absolutely continuous on
[0,T]
and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}g\ d\mu_{t}=\int_{M}\nabla g(x)\cdot H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{t},\qquad a.e.\ t\in[0,T].

Now, suppose
\alpha\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T))
. We find

\displaystyle\,\int_{[0,T]\times M}\Big\{\frac{d}{dt}[\alpha(t)g(x)]+H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\cdot\nabla[\alpha(t)g(x)]\Big\}\ d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{T}_{0}\int_{M}\big\{\alpha^{\prime}(t)g(x)+\alpha(t)H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\cdot\nabla g(x)\big\}\ d\mu_{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{T}_{0}\Big\{\alpha^{\prime}(t)\int_{M}g(x)\ d\mu_{t}+\alpha(t)\int_{M}\nabla g(x)\cdot H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{t}\Big\}\ dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\Big[\alpha(t)\int_{M}g(x)\ d\mu_{t}\Big]\Big|^{T}_{0}=0.

Finally, we note that

\displaystyle\Big\{\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}(t)g_{i}(x):\alpha_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)),g_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M),i=1,\ldots,N\Big\}

is a dense subset of
C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times M)
in the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets by the Stone-Weierstrass theorem. It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,T]\times M}\Big\{\frac{d}{dt}f(t,x)+H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\cdot\nabla f(t,x)\Big\}\ d\mu=0,\qquad\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times M).

Now we turn to prove (1). Recall that
\gamma(x,\cdot)
is a strict singular characteristic for every
x\in M
. Then, by Fubini’s theorem we have that

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}\phi\ d\mu_{T}-\int_{M}\phi\ d\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}\big\{\phi(\gamma(x,T))-\phi(x)\big\}\ d\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}\int^{T}_{0}\Big\{L(\gamma(x,s),H_{p}(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s))))+H(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s)))\Big\}\ dsd\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{T}_{0}\int_{M}\Big\{L(\gamma(x,s),H_{p}(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s))))+H(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s)))\Big\}\ d\bar{\mu}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int^{T}_{0}\int_{M}\Big\{L(x,H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x)))+H(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\Big\}\ d\mu_{s}ds.

The proof of (2) is a consequence of the property of broken characteristics. Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\int_{M}g\ d\mu_{t}=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\Big(\int_{M}g\ d\mu_{\tau}-\int_{M}g\ d\mu_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\int_{M}\frac{1}{\tau-t}(g(\gamma(x,\tau))-g(\gamma(x,t)))\ d\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}(g(\gamma(x,\tau))-g(\gamma(x,t)))\ d\bar{\mu}\qquad(\text{Dominated convergence theorem})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}\nabla g(\gamma(x,t)))\cdot H_{p}(\gamma(x,t)),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,t))))\ d\bar{\mu}\qquad(\text{Theorem \ref{thm:right_derivative}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}\nabla g(x)\cdot H_{p}(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{t}.

Finally, for (3),

\displaystyle\,\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}\int_{M}f(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{s}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}\int_{M}f(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s)))\ d\bar{\mu}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\int_{M}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}f(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s)))\ dsd\bar{\mu}\qquad(\text{Fubini theorem})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}\lim_{\tau\to t^{+}}\frac{1}{\tau-t}\int^{\tau}_{t}f(\gamma(x,s),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,s)))\ dsd\bar{\mu}\qquad(\text{Dominated convergence theorem})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}f(\gamma(x,t),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(\gamma(x,t)))\ d\bar{\mu}\qquad(\text{Theorem \ref{thm:measure_H}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{M}f(x,\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(x))\ d\mu_{t}

This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Maximal slope curves in the time-dependent case

In this appendix, we will extend some results in Section 3 to the time-dependent case. We suppose that

satisfies conditions (H1)-(H3) and

.

Suppose
H
satisfies conditions (H1)-(H3) and
\phi
is a locally semiconcave function on
\mathbb{R}\times M
. Then the map
(t,x)\mapsto(\mathbf{q}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(t,x),\mathbf{p}^{\#}_{\phi,H}(t,x))

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Proposition A.2
.

Suppose
H
satisfies conditions (H1)-(H3),
\phi
is a locally semiconcave function on
\mathbb{R}\times M

(3), for almost all

\displaystyle\phi(t,\gamma(t))-\phi(0,\gamma(0))=

\displaystyle\,\int^{t}_{0}\Big\{L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+\mathbf{q}(s,\gamma(s))+H(\gamma(s),\mathbf{p}(s,\gamma(s)))\Big\}\ ds

and the equality holds if and only if

for almost all

.
∎

Definition A.3

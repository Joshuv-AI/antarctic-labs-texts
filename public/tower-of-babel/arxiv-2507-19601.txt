Hamiltonian torus actions and the unimodality of odd Betti numbers
Nicholas Lindsay

Abstract
This paper is dedicated to the question: Is the sequence of odd and even Betti numbers of a closed symplectic manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian torus action unimodal? Recently, there was some progress on the question for the sequence of even Betti numbers by Cho-Kim and the author. The results of this paper give positive evidence in the case of odd Betti numbers, in dimensions 6 , 8 6,8 and 10 10 under progressively stronger symmetry assumptions.



1
Introduction

The following problem was given by Tolman in
[
3
, Problem 4.3]
:

Question 1.1
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
, having a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action. Then, if
k+2\leq n
, does it hold that
b_{k}(M)\leq b_{k+2}(M)
?

The motivation is the hard Lefschetz theorem, which implies that the unimodality property holds for closed Kähler manifolds. For a Kähler manifold
X
of complex dimension
n
, the hard Lefschetz theorem states that a sufficiently large power of the Lefschetz operator, defined as the cup product with the Kähler form, gives an isomorphism from
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{C})
to
H^{2n-i}(X,\mathbb{C})
so the Lefschetz operator is in particular injective, thus implying the above inequalities in the Betti numbers.

Some progress towards Question
1.1
on the case of even Betti numbers was made by Cho-Kim
[
1
,
2
]
and the author
[
6
]
, with an almost complete solution in dimension
8
now being known
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
. It turns out that extremal submanifolds, i.e. the fixed submanifolds of
M
on which the Hamiltonian
H
attains its minimum and maximum play a key role in the statement and proof of
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
. The extremal submanifolds are denoted
M_{\min},M_{\max}\subset M^{S^{1}}\subset M
. As will become apparent,
M_{\min},M_{\max}
also play an important role in proofs of the results in this paper. This is perhaps not surprising since the geometry of
M
is deeply related to that of
M_{\min},M_{\max}
.

This paper begins to address Question
1.1
in the case where
k
is odd. The main results concern closed symplectic manifolds of dimensions
6,8
and
10
under progressively stronger symmetry assumptions. In the course of the proofs of these results some general observations which are useful for Question
1.1
are made: it is shown in Lemma
3.2
that if a closed symplectic manifold has an effective Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action with an extremal component of dimension
0,4
or
2n-2
then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.
The proof is an application of the localization of Betti numbers theorem due to Kirwan, see Theorem
2.3
for the statement.

The first main result of the article is as follows, it is proved in Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem A
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
6
-manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action. Then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.

By Lemma
3.2
it is necessary only to prove the statement for the case
\dim(M_{\min})=\dim(M_{\max})=2
. By Theorem
2.3
,
b_{1}(M_{\min})=b_{1}(M_{\max})=b_{1}(M)
hence
M_{\min},M_{\max}
have the same genus. If
g(M_{\min})=0
then
b_{1}(M)=0
by Theorem
2.3
so the desired statement is proved. If
g(M_{\min})>0
then
[
7
, Theorem 9.10]
ensures the existence of a non-extremal fixed component
F
of dimension
2
with
g(F)\geq g(M_{\min})=g(M_{\max})
, therefore by Theorem
2.3

b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.

The second main result of the article is as follows, it is proved in Theorem
3.4
.

Theorem B
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
8
-manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action, such that each non-zero weight along
M_{\min}
and
M_{\max}
has modulus greater than
1
. Then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.

Using Lemma
3.2
and
[
7
, Theorem 9.10]
, we reduce the proof to the situation where
\dim(M_{\min})=\dim(M_{\max})=2
and there are
3
,
4
-dimensional isotropy manifolds containing
M_{\min}
. Moreover, since the maximum of each of these isotropy
4
-manifolds is a surface with genus equal to that of
M_{\min}
, it must hold that all three of these isotropy
4
-manifolds contain
M_{\max}
. Then,
[
9
, Lemma 2.6]
implies various congruences between the weights at
M_{\min}
and
M_{\max}
. We show using arithmetic arguments in Proposition
3.5
that such a system of congruences cannot hold.

The final main result of the article is as follows, it is proved in Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem C
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
10
-manifold with an effective Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action. Then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.

Firstly, in Subsection
2.3
it is shown that the moment map preimages of vertices and edges of the moment polygon are extremal fixed submanifolds for certain subcircle actions of the
T^{2}
-action. By the aforementioned Lemma
3.2
if there is a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action having an extremal component of dimension
0,4
or
8
then the desired inequality holds. Therefore it is sufficient to deal with the case that the moment map preimages of vertices and edges are either
2
or
6
dimensional, and since the moment map preimages of edges have greater dimension than the moment map preimages of vertices, the moment map preimages of edges have dimension
6
. Since, as is recalled in Lemma
2.8
of the preliminaries, such moment map preimages themselves admit a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action, applying Theorem
A
shows that these submanifolds satisfy the desired Betti number inequality. Finally, pulling this inequality back to
M
via the localization of Betti numbers Theorem
2.3
proves the theorem. In the last subsection a result in arbitrary dimensions is proven.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Torus actions

In this section, some foundational preliminaries for smooth torus actions are presented. Let
M
be a closed, connected smooth manifold with a smooth effective action of a compact torus
T^{k}
. For a subgroup
H
of
T^{k}
, set

M^{H}=\{p\in M|h.p=p\;\;\forall h\in H\}.

M^{H}
is called the isotropy submanifold associated to
H
. Recall that to any point in
M
there is a natural stabilizer subgroup of
T^{k}
we can assign, then
M^{H}
may be described alternatively as the subset of points in
M
whose stabilizer contains
H
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a subgroup
H
of
T^{k}
, it holds that:

1.

Every connected component of
M^{H}
is
T^{k}
-invariant.

2.

Every connected component of
M^{H}
is a smooth submanifold.

Proof.

1. The fact that
M^{H}
is
T^{k}
-invariant follows directly from the fact that
T^{k}
is abelian. Let
p\in M^{H}
, then for any
g\in T^{k}
,
h\in H
,
h.g.p=g.h.p=g.p
, that is
g.p\in M^{H}
. Since orbits of the
T^{k}
-action are connected, invariance of the individual connected components follows.

2. By averaging, we may pick a
T^{k}
-invariant metric on
M
. Then, the exponential around any point is a local
T^{k}
-equivariant diffeomorphism. When
p\in M^{H}
,
M^{H}
is locally the image of the subspace
V_{H}\subset T_{p}(M)
, where
V_{H}
is the intersection of the
1
-eigenspaces of all the transformations of
T_{p}(M)
of the form
h_{*}
,
h\in H
. Since it is the image of a linear subspace by a local diffeomorphism it is a smooth submanifold. ∎

2.2
Hamiltonian torus actions

Let
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. The complex representation with weight
w
is:

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}).z=z_{1}^{w_{1}}\ldots z_{k}^{w_{k}}z.

It is known that these are the irreducible representations of
T^{k}
. For an almost complex
2n
-manifold with an almost complex
T^{k}
-action, and a fixed point
p\in M^{T^{k}}
, note that
T_{p}M
has the structure of a
T^{k}
-representation. Therefore, it decomposes into
n
irreducible representations, the associated weights are called the weights of the action at
p
, for each fixed component they form a multiset consisting of elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
having cardinality
n
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold, with a smooth
T^{k}
-action by symplectomorphims. Then, the action is called Hamiltonian if there is a smooth
T^{k}
-invariant function
\mu:M\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}
such that

\omega(X_{t},\cdot)=-d(\mu(t)),

for all
t\in\mathfrak{t}
.

We will need the following fundamental localization formula for the Betti numbers due to Kirwan
[
5
]
. Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold having a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action. For
p\in M^{S^{1}}
denote by
\lambda_{p}
the number of negative weights of the action on
T_{p}M
counted with multiplicity.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
5
, Page 34]

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic manifold having a Hamiltonian circle action. Then for each
i
,

b_{i}(M)=\sum_{F\subset M^{S^{1}}}b_{i-2\lambda_{F}(M)}(F),

where the sum runs over connected components of the fixed point set.

A more self-contained proof of Theorem
2.3
was given in
[
9
, Page 7]
. The following facts about isotropy submanifolds and will be used in the paper.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic manifold with a Hamiltonian
T^{k}
-action and Hamiltonian
\mu
.
H
a subgroup of
T^{k}
and
N
an isotropy submanifold i.e. a connected component of
M^{H}
. Then
N
is a symplectic submaifold and the restricted action of
T^{k}
induces a Hamiltonian
T^{k}/H
-action with Hamiltonian
\mu|_{N}
.

Note that when
k=1
then by a continuity argument the only subgroups
H\subset S^{1}
for which can satisfy
M^{H}\neq M^{S^{1}}
are cyclic subgroups
\mathbb{Z}_{k}\subset S^{1}
generated by
e^{\frac{2\pi i}{k}}
. In this case the action of
S^{1}/H\cong S^{1}
is an effective Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action on
M^{H}
. Another key fact which will be used repeatedly is the following.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N
be an isotropy submanifold then
N^{T^{k}/H}=N\cap M^{T^{k}}
.

Generally speaking, this allows arguments which leverage low-dimensional results to prove higher dimensional ones, because by Lemma
2.4
isotropy submanifolds provide natural symplectic submanifolds with themselves have Hamiltonian torus actions. Moreover, by Lemma
2.5
the fixed point sets of isotropy submanifolds give direct information about the fixed point set of
M
.

Another fact which is used repeatedly throughout the article, referred to as reversing the circle action is the following.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
(M,\omega)
is a closed, connected symplectic manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltoniian
S^{1}
-action, and
N=M_{\max}
. Then there exists a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action on
M
with
N=M_{\min}
.

Proof.

The desired circle action has generating vector field
-X
and Hamiltonian
-H
.
One may check that by linearity
(M,\omega,-H,-X)
satisfies the Hamiltonian equation. ∎

2.3
Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-actions

Here we prove some facts about the preimages of vertices and edges of the moment polygon of a Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action.

Before giving the first lemma, we recall some very basic preliminaries about lattices and recall and elementary fact. Let
k>0
be an integer and
V
be a rank
k
real vector space, a lattice
l\subset V
is a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
which is a spanning set of
V
. Recall that the dual lattice
l^{*}\subset V^{*}
is defined by
l^{*}:=\{f\in V^{*}|f(l)\subset\mathbb{Z}\}
. The following lemma about rank
2
lattices, while being quite easy, has been separated out from the proof of Lemma
2.8
to avoid it being obscured by notations.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
V
be a rank
2
real vector space and
l\subset V
a lattice. For each
u\in l^{*}\setminus\{0\}
,
\ker(u)
is generated by some
v\in l\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
e_{1},e_{2}
be an integral basis of
l
, since
l
is a lattice
e_{1},e_{2}
is a basis of
V
. Then if
e_{1}^{*},e_{2}^{*}
is the corresponding dual basis of
V
, note that
e_{1}^{*},e_{2}^{*}
is an integral basis of
l^{*}
. Then, it holds that
w=pe_{1}^{*}+qe_{2}^{*}
for some
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
which are not both zero. Then
v=-qe_{1}+pe_{2}
generates
\ker(w)\subset V
.
∎

These preliminaries will be applied to the following lattice. Let
T^{k}=S^{1}\times\ldots\times S^{1}
be a compact torus, then recall that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
is defined to be the tangent space of
T^{k}
at the identity. The weight lattice
l\subset\mathfrak{t}
is defined as the kernel of the exponential
\exp:\mathfrak{t}\rightarrow T
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
2n
-manifold with a Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action,
E
an edge of the moment polygon
\mu(M)
. Then there exists a linear subcircle
S_{E}\subset T^{2}
such that the following properties hold:

1.

\mu^{-1}(E)
is a connected component of
M^{S_{E}}
and is
T^{2}
-invariant.

2.

There is a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action on
(M,\omega)
, with minimal fixed submanifold equal to
\mu^{-1}(E)
.

3.

\mu^{-1}(E)
admits a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action.

Proof.

Let
l\subset\mathfrak{t}
be the weight lattice, and
l^{*}\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
the dual lattice. Let
v
be a vertex of
E
. By the Atiyah-Guillemin-Sternberg convexity theorem
\mu^{-1}(v)
contains a fixed point say
p\in M^{T^{2}}
. By the equivariant symplectic neighborhood theorem
[
4
, Theorem A.1]
, there exists a neighborhood of
p
such that the action is equivariant symplectomorphic to the action on the standard symplectic vector space given by a linear embedding
s:T^{2}\rightarrow T^{n}
, let
\pi_{i}:T^{n}\rightarrow S^{1}
be the projection to the
i
’th coordinate. Then
w_{i}:=(\pi_{i}\circ s)_{*}\in l^{*}
is the
i^{\prime}th
weight at
p
, moreover in these local coordinates

\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=w_{i}|z_{i}|^{2}+c.

Moreover, by adding a constant to
\mu
we may assume
c=0
.

It follows that locally the image of
u
is the cone over
\{w_{i}|i=1,\ldots,n\}
, i.e. vector of the form
\sum x_{i}w_{i}
, such that
x_{i}\geq 0
. Since
E
is on the boundary of the polygon
\mu(M)
It follows that for at least one
i
, the direction of
E
centered at
\mu(p)
is
w_{i}\in l^{*}
, fix
w_{i}
to be such a weight. Then, by Lemma
2.7
the kernel of the map
w_{i}:\mathfrak{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is generated by an
u\in l\setminus\{0\}
, then let
t_{e}
be a primitive element so that
kt_{e}=u
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
X_{e}
the corresponding invariant vector field on
M
which; since
X_{e}\in l
is primitive, generates the action of the corresponding linear subgroup of
T^{2}
.

Then, by applying the Hamiltonian equation of the
T^{2}
-action,
d(\langle\mu,t_{e}\rangle)=\omega(X_{e},\cdot)
, the Hamiltonian of the action of the linear subgroup
S_{E}
is
H(p)=\langle\mu(p),t_{e}\rangle
. Moreover, since by definition
\langle w_{i},t_{e}\rangle=0
, it holds that:

H^{-1}(0)=\{p\in M|\langle\mu(p),t_{e}\rangle=0\}=\{p\in M|\mu(p)=kw_{e},k\in\mathbb{R}\}=\mu^{-1}(E).

Note that if a linear function is constant on the edge of a convex polygon, then the edge must be an extremum for the restriction of the function to the polygon. Therefore,
\mu^{-1}(E)
is an extremum of
H
and therefore is fixed pointwise by
S_{E}
, i.e.
\mu^{-1}(E)\subset M^{S_{E}}
. As
\mu^{-1}(E)
is subset of
M^{S_{E}}
consisting of a level set of
H
it is connected, moreover it is a connected component of
M^{S_{E}}
because any other point in
M
has a different value of
H
, therefore cannot be connected by a path in
M^{S_{E}}
.

Finally to exhibit a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action on
\mu^{-1}(E)
let
S^{\prime}
be any linear circle subgroup whose tangent space at the identity is linearly independent from that of
S_{E}
, since by Lemma
2.1
each connected component of
M^{S_{E}}
is
T^{2}
-invariant,
\mu^{-1}(E)
is in particular
S^{\prime}
-invariant. Finally, since the action of
S^{\prime}
has a non-zero weight at
p
then it is non-trivial.
∎

The following lemma is similar but simpler, therefore its proof has been abbreviated significantly, several of the technical details can be found in the proof of Lemma
2.8
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be closed, connected symplectic
2n
-manifold with a Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action,
v
a vertex of the moment polygon
\mu(M)
. Then there is a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action on
(M,\omega)
associated to linear subcircle
S\subset T^{2}
, which has extremal fixed submanifold equal to
\mu^{-1}(v)
.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},E_{2}
be the edges of
\mu(M)
containing
v
. By the Atiyah-Guillemin-Sternberg convexity theorem, there exists some
p\in\mu^{-1}(v)
that is fixed by
T^{2}
. Consider the fixed point representation and the local form of the action on a neighborhood of
p
as in the beginning of the proof of Lemma
2.8
. As in the previous proof, the image locally, up to translations is a cone, over the weights of the action, therefore two of the weights
w_{1},w_{2}\in l^{*}
correspond to the edges up to a constant. Taking
w^{\prime}\in l^{*}
to be any primitive element in the interior of the moment polygon (for example
w_{1}+w_{2}
) and taking
t^{\prime}\in l
a primitive element in the kernel provided by Lemma
2.7
, the circle action associated to
t^{\prime}
has the desired property.
∎

3
Proof of main results for
S^{1}
-actions

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action with Hamiltonian
H:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Then

M^{S^{1}}:=\{p\in M|z.p=p\;\forall z\in S^{1}\},

is a union of symplectic submanifolds and a connected component of
M^{S^{1}}
is called a fixed component. It follows from the Hamiltonian equation that
H
is constant on fixed components. A fixed component
F\subset M^{S^{1}}
is called extremal if it is equal to one of the subsets on which
H
attains its minimum and maximum, which are denoted
M_{\min},M_{\max}\subset M^{S^{1}}\subset M
. In the course of the proof of the convexity theorem, Atiyah-Guillemin-Sternberg showed that
M_{\min},M_{\max}
are fixed components. In Subsection
2.2
a key quantity
\lambda_{F}
for fixed components
F
was defined. Since
\lambda_{F}
is half the Morse-Bott index of
H
along
F

[
9
, Page 7]
, the two following facts follow: For a fixed component
F\subset M^{S^{1}}

\lambda_{F}=0\iff F=M_{\min},

\lambda_{F}=n\iff F=M_{\max}.

This will be used in the proofs throughout this section.

In this section, we prove Lemma
3.2
which is central to all the following proofs of the paper and then prove the first two main results of the paper. The section begins with the some general lemmas showing that under certain conditions on the dimension of the extremal submanifolds of a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action, certain inequalities in the Betti numbers
b_{i}(M)
are satisfied, which are relevant to Question
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
(M,\omega)
is a
2n
-dimensional closed, connected symplectic manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action, and has an extremal fixed component
F_{0}
of dimension
2k
. Then the inequalities
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{2k-1}(M)
and
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{2(n-k)+1}(M)
hold.

Proof.

By possible reversing the circle action by Lemma
2.6
, we may
F_{0}=M_{\min}
. By Proposition
2.3
,

b_{1}(M)=\sum_{F\subset M^{S^{1}}}b_{1-2\lambda_{F}}(F)=b_{1}(M_{\min}).

By applying Poincaré duality to
M_{\min}

b_{1}(M_{\min})=b_{2k-1}(M_{\min})
, therefore by Theorem
2.3

b_{2k-1}(M)=\sum_{F\subset M^{S^{1}}}b_{2k-1-2\lambda_{F}}(F)=b_{2k-1}(M_{\min})+\sum_{F\subset M^{S^{1}},\;F\neq M_{\min}}b_{2k-1-2\lambda_{F}}(F).

Therefore, as was shown above
b_{2k-1}(M_{\min})=b_{1}(M)
, it follows that
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{2k-1}(M)
. By Poincaré duality applied to
M
,
b_{2k-1}(M)=b_{2n-(2k-1)}(M)
proving the second inequality.
∎

Next we prove a lemma that will be central to the proofs of all the main results of the article.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
(M,\omega)
is a
2n
-dimensional closed, connected symplectic manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action, and has an extremal fixed component
F_{0}
of dimension
0
,
4
or
2n-2
. Then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.

Proof.

By possibly reversing the circle action by Lemma
2.6
we can assume
F_{0}=M_{\min}
. Firstly, if
F_{0}
is isolated, by Proposition
2.3

b_{1}(M)=\sum_{F\subset M^{S^{1}}}b_{1-2\lambda_{F}}(F)=b_{1}(M_{\min})=0

so the statement holds. Next, assume
\dim(F_{0})=4
then the result holds by the first inequality of Lemma
3.1
. When
\dim(F_{0})=2n-2
, the conclusion is a consequence of the second inequality of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
6
-manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action. Then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
it is necessary only to prove the statement for the case
\dim(M_{\min})=\dim(M_{\max})=2
. By Proposition
2.3
,
b_{1}(M_{\min})=b_{1}(M_{\max})=b_{1}(M)
hence
M_{\min}
and
M_{\max}
have the same genus, which we denote
g(M_{\min})=g(M_{\max})
. If
g(M_{\min})=0
then
b_{1}(M)=0
by Theorem
2.3
so the desired statement is proved. If
g(M_{\min})>0
, by
[
7
, Theorem 9.10]
there exists a non-extremal fixed surface
F
with a map
f:F\rightarrow M_{\min}
of degree
\deg(f)\in\{1,2\}
. In particular,
f_{*}:H_{1}(F,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H_{1}(M_{\min},\mathbb{R})
is surjective and it follows that
b_{1}(F)\geq b_{1}(M_{\min})=b_{1}(M_{\max})=b_{1}(M)
, therefore the claim follows from Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Next, we prove an analogous result in dimension
8
under a genericity assumption on the weights of the
S^{1}
-action along the extremal fixed components.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
8
-manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action such that all the non-zero weights along
M_{\min}
and
M_{\max}
have modulus greater than
1
. Then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
it remains to prove the claim when
\dim(M_{\min})=\dim(M_{\max})=2
. By Proposition
2.3

b_{1}(M_{\min})=b_{1}(M_{\max})=b_{1}(M)
, so if
b_{1}(M_{\min})=0
the claim is proven. Therefore, assume additionally that
b_{1}(M_{\min})>0
. Consider an isotropy
N
submanifold containing
M_{\min}
, it will be shown that the desired in equality holds when
N
is
6
-dimensional by a case analysis on the dimension of
N_{\max}
.

If
N_{\max}
is
4
-dimensional in which case there is a
4
-dimensional component
F\subset M^{S^{1}}
such that
b_{1}(F)=b_{3}(F)=b_{1}(M)
and the statement is proven via Proposition
2.3
, also since
b_{1}(N)=b_{1}(N_{\min})=b_{1}(M_{\min})=b_{1}(M)>0
, it follows that
N_{\max}
cannot be isolated. If
N_{\max}
is
2
-dimensional then by
[
7
, Theorem 9.10]
there exists a non-extremal fixed surface
F\subset N^{S^{1}}\subset M^{S^{1}}
with a map
f:F\rightarrow N_{\min}=M_{\min}
of degree
\deg(f)\in\{1,2\}
. In particular,
f_{*}:H_{1}(F,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H_{1}(M_{\min},\mathbb{R})
is surjective and it follows that
b_{1}(F)\geq b_{1}(M_{\min})=b_{1}(M_{\max})=b_{1}(M)
, and the claim is proven by Theorem
2.3
and applying Poincaré duality to
M
. By either reversing the circle action by Lemma
2.6
or running the same argument with opposite signs, it follows that
M_{\max}
cannot be contained in an isotropy
6
-manifold. Hence, it remains to prove the statement when all the isotropy submanifolds containing the extremal surfaces are
4
-dimensional.

Combining this with the assumption that non-zero weights have modulus at least
1
, it implies weights at
M_{\min},M_{\max}
must be pairwise coprime because otherwise there is an isotropy
6
-manifold containing them. Moreover, each of the three isotropy
4
-manifolds containing
M_{\min}
must contain
M_{\max}
because otherwise; if there existed an isotropy
4
-manifold
N
containing
M_{\min}
but not
M_{\max}
then by Proposition
2.3
applied to
N
,
F:=N_{\max}\subset M^{S^{1}}
would give a non-extremal fixed surface with
b_{1}(F)=b_{1}(M_{\min})=b_{1}(M_{\max})=b_{1}(M)
and the claim follows from Theorem
2.3
and applying Poincaré duality to
M
.

Let
a,b,c>1
be the weights at
M_{\min}
, as noted above they are pairwise coprime and in particular mutually distinct and so may be labeled
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
so that
1<a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
. As was shown above, each of the three corresponding isotropy
4
-manifolds
N_{i}
containing
M_{\min}
which are components of
M^{\mathbb{Z}_{a_{i}}}

i=1,2,3
respectively contains
M_{\max}
.

Because
M_{\max}
has codimension
2
in
N_{i}
the weight of the action on
N_{i}
at
M_{\max}
is
-a_{i}
. By assumption the weights at
M_{\max}
are denoted
\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\},
then picking a point
q\in M_{\max}
the action on
T_{q}M
is

z.(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=(z^{w_{1}}z_{1},z^{w_{2}}z_{2},z^{w_{3}}z_{3}),

where by the assumption that non-zero weights along
M_{\max}
have modulus greater than
1
,
w_{i}<-1
for
i=1,2,3
. Moreover, as above since there is no isotropy
6
-manifold containing
M_{\max}
, it holds that
\gcd(w_{i},w_{j})=1
for
i\neq j
.

The tangent spaces of the the three isotropy
4
-manifolds correspond to three (real) dimension
2
subspaces on which the stabilizer of a non-zero point is
\mathbb{Z}_{a_{i}}
respectively. Therefore since the condition
\gcd(w_{i},w_{j})=1
for
i\neq j
implies there are precisely three
2
-dimensional subspaces on which the stabilizer of every non-zero point is non-trivial, it follows that
\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\}=\{-a_{1},-a_{2},-a_{3}\}
.

Finally, applying
[
9
, Lemma 2.6]
implies that

\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}=\{-a_{1},-a_{2},-a_{3}\}\;\;\mod a_{i},

for
i=1,2,3
. In Proposition
3.5
it is shown that this is not possible.
∎

To state the following proposition it is necessary to set some notation. Let
k>1
be an integer, let
\pi:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
the quotient map. Let
A_{1},A_{2}
be two finite multisets consisting of integers, with indicator functions
m_{A_{1}},m_{A_{2}}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Let
\tilde{A}_{1},\tilde{A}_{2}
be the multisets consisting of elements of
\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
which are defined by the indicator functions
m_{\tilde{A}_{i}}:\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, defined by

m_{\tilde{A}_{i}}(\alpha)=\sum_{n\in\pi^{-1}(\alpha)}m_{A_{i}}(n).

The notation
A_{1}=A_{2}\mod k
means that the following equality of multisets holds:

\tilde{A}_{1}=\tilde{A}_{2}.

Next, we state and prove a proposition which will complete the proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Proposition 3.5
.

There does not exist three pairwise coprime integers
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
such that
1<a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
and

\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}=\{-a_{1},-a_{2},-a_{3}\}\;\mod a_{i},

for
i=1,2,3
.

Proof.

Firstly the statement under the assumption
2<a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
is proven, the case
a_{1}=2
is dealt with at the end of the proof. Suppose for a contradiction that such an ordered triple satisfying
2<a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
exists.

If for any
i=1,2,3
it holds that
a_{j}=-a_{j}\mod a_{i}
for all
j=1,2,3
then there is an immediate contradiction. The condition
a_{j}=-a_{j}\mod a_{i}
for some
i\neq j
implies
2a_{j}=ma_{i}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
since
a_{i}>2
, some prime factor of
a_{i}
must divide
a_{j}
contradicting the assumption that
a_{i}
and
a_{j}
are coprime.

Note that we cannot have
a_{j}=-a_{i}\mod a_{i}
for any
i\neq j
because
a_{i}
and
a_{j}
are coprime. Therefore, by this and the above paragraph, for each
i
the only remaining way the congruence

\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}=\{-a_{1},-a_{2},-a_{3}\}\;\mod a_{i},

can be satisfied is that
a_{j}=-a_{k}\mod a_{i}
for
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
, that is:

a_{j}+a_{k}=0\mod a_{i}

for
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
. In the following it shown that this is also impossible. Since
a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
,
a_{1}+a_{2}<2a_{3}
, therefore by above congruence for
i=3
it must hold that
a_{3}=a_{1}+a_{2}
. Then, since applying the above congruence for
i=2,
gives
a_{1}+a_{3}=ma_{2}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, substituting gives
2a_{1}+a_{2}=ma_{2}
, so
2a_{1}=0\mod a_{2}
, since
2<a_{1}<a_{2}
and
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2})=1
this is a contradiction because it implies that some prime factor of
a_{2}
should divide
a_{1}
. Therefore, the proposition is proven in the case
2<a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
.

Finally if
a_{1}=2
, then firstly note that
a_{3}>4
, in particular
2\mod a_{3}
cannot be equal to
-2\mod a_{3}
or
-a_{3}\mod a_{3}
. Then since

\{2,a_{2},a_{3}\}=\{-2,-a_{2},-a_{3}\}\;\mod a_{3},

it follows that
2=-a_{2}\mod a_{3}
, therefore by the inequality
a_{1}+a_{2}<2a_{3}
, it follows that
2+a_{2}=a_{3}
. Finally since

\{2,a_{2},a_{3}\}=\{-2,-a_{2},-a_{3}\}\mod a_{2},

and; because either
a_{2}=3
or
a_{2}>4
, the congruences
2=-2\mod a_{2}
and
2=-a_{2}\mod a_{2}
are impossible, we have that
2=-a_{3}\mod a_{2}
, so writing
2+a_{3}=ka_{2}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, substituting
2+a_{2}=a_{3}
gives
4+a_{2}=ka_{2}
which is a contradiction because
a_{2}>2
is an odd integer.
∎

3.4
has the following consequence, proved by choosing a suitably generic linear subcircle
S^{1}\subset T^{2}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
8
-manifold with an effective Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action. Then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)

In the following section we give a proof of Corollary

4
Higher dimensional torus actions

The main purpose of this section is to show Theorem
4.1
, showing that the the first two odd Betti numbers of a closed
10
-dimensional manifold with an effective Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action are unimodal. Finally, using similar ideas we give an alternative comparatively self-contained proof of Corollary
3.6
of the previous section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
10
-manifold with an effective Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action. Then
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)
.

Proof.

Firstly, by Lemma
3.2
if
(M,\omega)
has a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action with an extremal component of dimension
0
,
4
or
8
then the Theorem is proved, this will be used in the following argument.

Let
\mu
denote the Hamiltonian of the effective
T^{2}
-action. Consider an edge of the moment image of the
T^{2}
-action, say
E
, and let
v_{1},v_{2}
be the two vertices it contain. Then, by Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
2.9
there is Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-actions on
(M,\omega)
with extremal submanifolds equal to
\mu^{-1}(E),\mu^{-1}(v_{1}),\mu^{-1}(v_{2})
. Therefore, Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
\dim(\mu^{-1}(v_{i}))<\dim(\mu^{-1}(E))
for
i=1,2
, the desired inequality is proved unless it holds that
\dim(\mu^{-1}(E))=6
and
\dim(\mu^{-1}(v_{i}))=2
for
i=1,2
.

By Lemma
2.8
(3) the
6
-manifold
N:=\dim(\mu^{-1}(E))
itself has a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action. By Theorem
3.3
the Betti numbers of
N
satisfy the inequality
b_{1}(N)\leq b_{3}(N)
. Finally by by Lemma
2.8
(2) there exists a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action with extremal component equal to
N
; by possible reversing it by Lemma
2.6
we can assume
N=M_{\min}
. Then Theorem
2.3
gives

b_{1}(M)=\sum_{F\subset M^{S^{1}}}b_{1-2\lambda_{F}}(F)=b_{1}(N)

and

b_{3}(M)=\sum_{F\subset M^{S^{1}}}b_{3-2\lambda_{F}}(F)=b_{3}(N)+c,

where
c
is a non-negative integer. Therefore
b_{1}(M)=b_{1}(N)\leq b_{3}(N)\leq b_{3}(M).
∎

Alternative proof of Corollary
3.6
.

Firstly, by Lemma
3.2
if an
8
-dimensional closed symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
has a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action with an extremal component of dimension
0
,
4
or
6
then the corollary is proven, this will be used in the following argument.

Now assume that
(M,\omega)
be an
8
-dimensional closed symplectic manifold with an effective Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action with Hamilltonian
\mu
. Let
v
be any vertex of the moment polygon
\mu(M)
, and
E_{1},E_{2}
the adjacent edges. Then, by Lemma

4.1
A result in arbitrary dimensions

In this section a result in arbitrary dimensions is proven. A complete solution of
[
3
, Problem 4.3]
would imply for a closed symplectic manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action, for each odd
i
such that
1<i\leq n
,

b_{1}(M)\leq b_{3}(M)\leq\ldots\leq b_{i}(M).

In this section, we show that in arbitrary dimensions with Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-symmetry, this inequality holds for
some

i
. The following lemma about Betti numbers will be needed.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
(M,\omega)
is a
2n
-dimensional closed, connected symplectic manifold with a non-trivial Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action, and has an extremal fixed component
F_{0}
of dimension
2k\neq 2
. Then there is an odd integer
i
such that
1<i\leq n
and
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{i}(M)
.

Proof.

If
k=0
then
b_{1}(M)=0
by Theorem
2.3
. If
2<2k<2n
, then by Lemma
3.1
the inequalities
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{2k-1}(M)
and
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{2n-2k+1}(M)
hold. The assumption
2k>2
implies that the two odd integers
2k-1
and
2n-2k+1
are greater than
1
. Moreover since they differ by a reflection centered at
n
, at least one of them is at most
n
.
∎

The main result of this subsection is as follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed, connected symplectic
2n
-dimensional manifold with an effective Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action, then there is an odd integer
i
such that
1<i\leq n
and
b_{1}(M)\leq b_{i}(M)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
2.9
the moment map preimages of all vertices and edges of the moment polygon are extremal submanifolds for certain Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-actions on
M
. Since the preimage of an edge has greater dimension than the preimages of the vertices it contains, at least one these submanifolds does not have dimension
2
. Therefore the proof follows by Lemma
4.2
.
∎

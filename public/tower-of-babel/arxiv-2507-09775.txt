1.
Introduction

This article studies the distribution of translates of certain tori in moduli spaces of translation surfaces under the action of the Teichmüller geodesic flow.
The tori we study arise from horizontal shearing deformations of a fixed horizontally periodic translation surface.
Our main results provide sufficient criteria under which these translated tori become dense (Theorem
A
), and, in other cases, guaranteeing that all weak-
\ast
limits of their uniform measures are fully supported within the conjectured locus (Theorem
B
).
Among the ingredients in the proof are a strengthening of Forni’s full density convergence for expanding horocycle arcs (Theorem
1.6
), and a measure rigidity result for horocycle flow-invariant measures on projective bundles arising from locally constant cocycles over quotients of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
(Theorem
1.8
).
These results are motivated by the problem of equidistribution of expanding horocycle arcs conjectured by Forni, as well as by the analogous twist torus conjecture of Mirzakhani in the context of hyperbolic surfaces as we now describe.

Beyond its intrinsic interest, Problem

Another motivation for studying Problem

To formulate this conjecture, fix a pants decomposition

of a genus

For each

The connection between these two problems was made precise in the work of Calderon and Farre

]

Roughly speaking, under this conjugacy, Mirzakhani’s twist tori give rise to certain expanding flat twist tori of the form in Problem

Moreover, and crucially, they show that this, apriori measurable conjugacy, maps weak-

Combined with the above full density results of

In particular, it follows from their work that a complete answer to Mirzakhani’s twist torus conjecture follows from an affirmative answer to Problem

1.3.1.
Density of twist tori

The
Veech group
of a translation surface
(M,\omega)
, denoted
\mathrm{SL}(M,\omega)
, is its stabilizer for the action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on the stratum containing
(M,\omega)
.
Recall that
(M,\omega)
is said to be a
Veech surface
if
\mathrm{SL}(M,\omega)
is a lattice in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e.,
\mathrm{SL}(M,\omega)
is discrete with finite covolume.
It is known by a result of Smillie that
(M,\omega)
is a Veech surface if and only if the orbit
\mathcal{V}:=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\cdot(M,\omega)
is closed
[
Vee95
, pg 226]
.
We refer to such closed orbits as
Veech curves
based on the fact that Veech initiated their modern study in dynamics.

Given an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-orbit closure
\mathcal{M}
containing
(M,\omega)
, we say that
(M,\omega)
is
\mathcal{M}
-primitive
if there is no proper intermediate
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-orbit closure between
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{M}
, i.e., if
\mathcal{N}
is an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-orbit closure with
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{V}
, or
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a horizontally periodic Veech surface, and let
\mathcal{M}=\overline{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\cdot\mathbb{T}(\omega)}
.
Assume that
(M,\omega)
is
\mathcal{M}
-primitive.
Then,
the expanding tori

become dense in
\mathcal{M}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
,
i.e., for every
\varepsilon>0
and compact set

, there is
t_{0}>0
so that
\mathcal{K}\cap g_{t}\cdot\mathbb{T}(\omega)
is
\varepsilon
-dense in
\mathcal{K}
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

We note that, if
(M,\omega)
is square-tiled

1.5.
A-invariance of the distribution of cocycle output directions

Another key ingredient in our proof of Theorem
A

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-actions on fiber bundles over its finite volume quotients, induced from linear representations of its lattices, which may be of independent interest.
The result asserts that all limiting measures of expanding horocycle arcs on such fiber bundles are necessarily invariant by the geodesic flow. We note that this result is not needed in the proof of Theorem

To state the result, we need some notation.

This induces similar actions of
\Gamma

Denote by

In particular,

Organization of the article and proof ideas

In Section

Section

In Section

The rest of Section

\displaystyle\mathrm{hol}^{(x)}_{q_{\tau}}=\mathrm{hol}^{(x)}_{q}+\tau\beta,\qquad\mathrm{hol}^{(y)}_{q_{\tau}}=\mathrm{hol}^{(y)}_{q}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Twist}(q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Span}\left\{\beta_{C}:C\text{ is a horizontal cylinder on }q\right\}.

\displaystyle\beta=\mathrm{hol}_{q}^{(y)}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{Trem}(q,\tau\beta)=u_{\tau}q.

The notation

refers to

introduced and studied in

We refer the reader to
[

for the role of cylinder twists in the study of

2.5.
Balanced spaces

Let
H^{1}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{abs}}
denote the absolute cohomology group of
M_{q}
and let
p:H^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{abs}}
denote the forgetful map.
We extend the intersection product from
H^{1}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{abs}}
to relative cohomology
H^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}
by composing it with the forgetful projection
p
.
Let
L_{q}
denote the linear functional on
H^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}
given by taking the intersection product with
\beta
. More explicitly, we set
L_{q}(\beta)=\int_{M_{q}}dx_{q}\wedge p(\beta)
, where the notation indicates evaluating the cup product of
p(\mathrm{hol}_{q}^{(x)})
with
p(\beta)
on the fundamental class of
M_{q}
.
We set

\displaystyle\mathrm{Twist}^{0}(q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Twist}(q)\cap\mathrm{Ker}(L_{q}).

Note that
L_{q}(\mathrm{hol}^{(y)}_{q})
is the nonzero (signed) area of
M_{q}
. It follows that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Twist}(q)=\mathrm{Twist}^{0}(q)\oplus\mathbb{R}\cdot\mathrm{hol}_{q}^{(y)}.

Moreover, since
L_{q}(\mathrm{hol}^{(y)}_{q})\neq 0
, its restriction to the tautological plane
\mathrm{Taut}_{q}
is non-degenerate.
We denote by
\mathrm{Taut}_{q}^{0}
the orthogonal complement to
\mathrm{Taut}_{q}
with respect to this intersection form.
Following
[
CSW20
]
, we say that a cohomology class is
balanced
if it belongs to
\mathrm{Taut}_{q}^{0}
.

The action of the affine group
\mathrm{Aff}^{+}(q)
on
M_{q}
induces an action on
H^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}
, which preserves the tautological plane
\mathrm{Taut}_{q}
and its complement
\mathrm{Taut}_{q}^{0}
.
Moreover, since every element of
\mathrm{Twist}^{0}(q)
has
0
intersection pairing with
\mathrm{Taut}_{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Twist}^{0}(q)\subseteq\mathrm{Taut}^{0}_{q}.

We let
\mathrm{Cyl}^{0}(q)
denote the smallest
\mathrm{Aff}^{+}(q)
-invariant subspace of
\mathrm{Taut}^{0}_{q}
containing
\mathrm{Twist}^{0}(q)
.
We set

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cyl}(q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Cyl}^{0}(q)\oplus\mathrm{Taut}_{q}.

In particular, the above splitting is
\mathrm{Aff}^{+}(q)
-invariant, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Twist}(q)\subseteq\mathrm{Cyl}(q).

2.6.
The AGY norm

Given
q
in the marked stratum
{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}}
, the AGY norm on
H^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
, denoted
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{q}
is defined for every
v\in H^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
by

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{q}}\frac{|v(\gamma)|}{|\mathrm{hol}_{q}(\gamma)|},

where
\Lambda_{q}\subseteq H_{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
denotes the set of saddle connections of
q
.
This norm induces a (Finsler) metric denoted
{\rm dist}_{\mathrm{AGY}}
on
{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}}
given by the infimum of lengths of
C^{1}
-paths joining points.
Since these norms, and hence the metric, are invariant by the mapping class group, they descend to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
.
The following Lipschitz estimate on norms of parallel transported vectors will be useful for our analysis.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
AG13
, Proposition 5.5]
)
.

Let
\kappa:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}}
be a
C^{1}
-path and
v\in H^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then,

\displaystyle e^{-\mathrm{length}(\kappa)}\leq\frac{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\kappa(1)}}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\kappa(0)}}\leq e^{\mathrm{length}(\kappa)},

where
\mathrm{length}(\kappa)=\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\dot{(}\kappa)(t)\right\rVert_{\kappa(t)}\;dt
.
Moreover, for
q=\kappa(0)
, and for all
0\leq t<1/\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\kappa(s)}\;ds\leq-\log(1-t\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}).

Remark 2.5
.

The second assertion of Proposition
2.4
follows from the proof given in the cited reference.

We also need the following basic norm estimates of the derivative of the geodesic flow with respect to AGY norms.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
AG13
, Lemma 5.2]
)
.

For all
q\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
, all
v\in H^{1}_{\mathbf{i}\mathbb{R}}
, and all
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert Dg_{t}(q)v\right\rVert_{g_{t}q}\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q},

where
Dg_{t}(q):T_{q}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}\rightarrow T_{g_{t}q}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
is the derivative of the geodesic flow.
Moreover, for all
v\in H^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
,

\displaystyle e^{-2|t|}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert Dg_{t}(q)v\right\rVert_{g_{t}q}\leq e^{2|t|}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}

Proof.

The first estimate was shown in
[
AG13
, Lemma 5.2]
for the action on the marked stratum
{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}}
, which implies the corresponding estimates in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
by invariance of the AGY norms under the mapping class group.
As noted in the discussion following Lemma 5.2 in
[
AG13
]
, the second inequality follows by the same proof of the first bound.
∎

2.7.
Local (un)stable manifolds

We recall the parametrization of local strong stable/unstable manifolds.
Define
E^{s}(q)
(resp.
E^{u}(q)
) as the subspace of
H^{1}_{\mathbf{i}\mathbb{R}}
(resp.
H^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}
) with
0
intersection product with
\mathrm{hol}_{q}^{(x)}
(resp.
\mathrm{hol}_{q}^{(y)}
), where the intersection product is extended to relative cohomology by composing it with the projection to absolute cohomology as in §
2.5
.
Let
v\in E^{s}(q)
be such that there is a path
\kappa:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}}
with
\kappa(0)=q
and
\dot{\kappa}(t)=v
for all
t\in[0,1]
. Then, we define
\Psi^{s}_{q}(v)=\kappa(1)
.
In coordinates, if
\Psi_{q}^{s}(v)
is defined, then

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{hol}^{(y)}_{\Psi_{q}^{s}(v)}=\mathrm{hol}^{(y)}_{q}+v,\qquad\mathrm{hol}^{(x)}_{\Psi_{q}^{s}(v)}=\mathrm{hol}^{(x)}_{q}.

The map
\Psi^{u}_{q}
is defined analogously on
\mathbb{R}
-valued cohomology classes.
The maps
\Psi^{s}_{\bullet}
and
\Psi^{u}_{\bullet}
play the role of exponential maps parametrizing strong stable/unstable leaves using their respective tangent spaces.
The following key properties for this map will be important for us.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
AG13
, Proposition 5.3]
)
.

For all
q\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
, the map
v\mapsto\Psi^{s}_{q}(v)
is well-defined for
v\in E^{s}(q)
with
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}<1/2
.
Moreover, we have the bi-Lipschitz estimates

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{\mathrm{AGY}}(q,\Psi^{s}_{q}(v))\leq 2\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q},\qquad\text{and}\qquad 1/2\leq\frac{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\Psi^{s}_{q}(v)}}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}}\leq 2.

The analogous estimates also hold for
\Psi^{u}_{q}
.

Lemma
2.6
implies the following natural equivariance property of the
\Psi^{s}_{\bullet}
.

Corollary 2.8
.

For all
t\geq 0
,
q\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
,
v\in E^{s}(q)
with
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}<1/2
, we have

\displaystyle g_{t}\Psi_{q}^{s}(v)=\Psi^{s}_{g_{t}q}(Dg_{t}(q)v).

Proof.

First, we note that it suffices to prove the corollary in the marked stratum
{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}}
.
By Lemma
2.6
, we have
\left\lVert Dg_{t}(q)v\right\rVert_{g_{t}q}<1/2
, and hence
\left\lVert Dg_{t}(q)v\right\rVert_{g_{t}q}
is well-defined by Proposition
2.7
.
Let
\kappa:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}}
be a path such that
\kappa(0)=1,\kappa(1)=\Psi^{s}_{q}(v)
, and
\dot{\kappa}(r)=v
for all
r\in[0,1]
.
Then,
r\mapsto g_{t}\kappa(r)
is a path joining
g_{t}q
to
g_{t}\Psi_{q}^{s}(v)
, with constant derivative equal
Dg_{t}(q)v
.
The corollary follows by definition of
\Psi^{s}_{g_{t}q}
.
∎

The following substantial strengthening of Lemma
2.6
follows from non-uniform hyperbolicity of the Teichmüller geodesic flow proved by Forni in
[
For02
, Lemma 2.1’]
.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
AG13
, Proposition 4.3]
)
.

Given a compact subset
L\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
and
\delta>0
, there is
T=T(L,\delta)>0
such that for all
q\in L
,
v\in E^{s}(q)
, and
t\geq 0
such that
g_{t}q\in L
and the set of
r\in[0,t]
with
g_{r}q\in L
has measure
\geq T
,
we have
\left\lVert Dg_{t}(q)v\right\rVert_{g_{t}q}\leq\delta\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}
.

Remark 2.10
.

Proposition
2.9
is stated in
[

]
for

.

2.8.
The Kontsevich-Zorich cocycle

The standard reference for the discussion in this section is
[
FM14
]
.
Recall the notation set in Convention
2.3
.
The Universal Coefficient Theorem provides a splitting

\displaystyle H^{1}(M_{q},\Sigma(q);\mathbb{C})\cong H^{1}(M_{q},\Sigma(q);\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{C}.

Recalling that the left hand-side is identified with the tangent space to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
at
q
, we also have that this splitting is invariant by the derivative
Dg(q):T_{q}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}\rightarrow T_{gq}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
of
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
; cf.
[
CKS21
, Section 2.2]
.
Moreover, there is a linear dynamical cocycle, known as the
Kontsevich-Zorich cocycle
(KZ for short), and denoted
4
4

4

Note that the KZ cocycle is usually defined on absolute cohomology elsewhere in the literature.

\mathrm{KZ}(g,q):H^{1}(M_{q},\Sigma(q);\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{1}(M_{gq},\Sigma(gq);\mathbb{R})
, so that the derivative can be written as

\displaystyle Dg(q)=\mathrm{KZ}(g,q)\otimes g,

where
g
acts on
\mathbb{C}
via its standard linear action on the plane.
In particular, the chain rule implies the cocycle property

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{KZ}(gh,q)=\mathrm{KZ}(g,hq)\mathrm{KZ}(h,q).

Alternatively, this cocycle can be defined as the quotient by
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
of the trivial cocycle over
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
action on
{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}}\times H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R})
, given by
g\cdot(\tilde{q},v)=(g\tilde{q},v)
.
This quotient gives a well-defined linear cocycle in light of our Convention
2.3
.

We record the following immediate corollary of Lemma
2.6
on norm bounds of the cocycle.
Fix a matrix norm on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, denoted
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
.

Corollary 2.11
.

For all
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
q\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathrm{KZ}(g,q)\right\rVert_{q\rightarrow gq}\ll\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{O(1)},

where
\left\lVert\mathrm{KZ}(g,q)\right\rVert_{q\rightarrow gq}
denotes the operator norm of the cocycle with respect to the AGY norms at
q
and
gq
respectively.

Proof.

The corollary follows by the polar decomposition for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the cocycle property,
\mathrm{SO}(2)
-invariance of AGY-norms, and Lemma
2.6
.
∎

We introduce convenient notation to be used throughout the article.
Let
\omega
be a horizontally periodic Veech surface.
We use
\mathcal{V}
to denote the closed
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-orbit of
\omega
.
For convenience, we always assume that our Veech surface is
1
-periodic for
u(s)

u(1)\omega=\omega

Given
\beta\in\mathrm{Twist}(\omega)

t,s,r>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u(s)\cdot\mathrm{Trem}(\omega,\beta):\beta\in\mathrm{Twist}(\omega),s\in\mathbb{R}\right\},\qquad

(2.11)

.

Lemma 2.13

(
[

.

,

\displaystyle g_{t}\cdot\mathrm{Trem}(q,\beta)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Trem}(g_{t}q,e^{t}\cdot\mathrm{KZ}(g_{t},q)\cdot\beta).

Lemma 2.14
.

For all

2.10.
Exponential recurrence and contraction of vertical classes

We recall the following result asserting that except for a set of exceptionally decaying measure, geodesic flow orbits of points on the torus
\mathbb{T}(\omega)
spend a definite proportions of their time inside large compact sets.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a horizontally periodic surface and let
\mu
be a
U
-invariant probability measure on its twist torus
\mathbb{T}(\omega)
.
Then, there is a compact set
L\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
, and
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
such that for all large enough
T>0
, the set of
x\in\mathbb{T}(\omega)
with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{L}(g_{t}x)\;dt\leq(1-\varepsilon_{1})T

has
\mu
-measure at most
e^{-\varepsilon_{2}T}
.

Proof.

This result was shown in stronger form in
[
ASAE+21
, Proposition 3.9]
for the Lebesgue probability measure on a horocycle arc of the form
\left\{u(s)q:s\in[-1,1]\right\}
,
q\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
, following ideas of
[
EM01
,
Ath06
,
KKLM17
]
, with uniform estimates as
q
varied in fixed compact sets in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
.
The claimed estimate now follows for
\mu
since
\mathbb{T}(\omega)
is compact and since
\mu
disintegrates as a convex combination of Lebesgue measures on horocycle arcs as above by
U
-invariance.
∎

Combined with Proposition
2.9
, the above recurrence result yields the following contraction estimate for the action of
g_{t}
on the strong stable foliation.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let the notation be as in Proposition
2.15
.
Then, for
\mu
-almost every
q\in\mathbb{T}(\omega)
, we have
\sup\left\lVert Dg_{t}(q)\cdot v\right\rVert_{g_{t}q}\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\infty}0
, where the supremum is over all vertical cohomology classes
v\in E^{s}(q)
tangent to the strong stable leaf through
q

with
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}\leq 1
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
\delta>0
and let
L\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{u}}
be the compact set provided by Proposition
2.15
.
Let
T=T(L,\delta)>0
be the parameter provided by non-uniform hyperbolicity in Proposition
2.9
.
By Proposition
2.15
and the Borel-Cantelli lemma, for
\mu
-almost every
q\in\mathbb{T}(\omega)
, we can find
t>T
such that
g_{t}q\in L
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Leb}(r\in[0,t]:g_{r}q\in L)>T.

Hence, for each such
q
and
t
, Proposition
2.9
gives
\sup\left\lVert Dg_{t}(q)\cdot v\right\rVert_{g_{t}q}\leq\delta
for all unit norm classes
v\in E^{s}(q)
.
The non-expansion estimate of Lemma
2.6
then implies that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup\left\{\left\lVert Dg_{t}(q)\cdot v\right\rVert_{g_{t}q}:v\in E^{s}(q),\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}\leq 1\right\}\leq\delta.

The corollary now follows as
\delta
was arbitrary.
∎

We begin with the following useful lemma which allows us to control the trace field of Veech surfaces belonging to the same twist torus.
This lemma in fact proves a stronger property than what we need for the concrete examples

Lemma 3.2

(
[
KS00
,
Wri15
,
Wri14
]
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a horizontally periodic Veech surface, and suppose that
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
is another Veech surface in
\mathbb{T}(\omega)
. Then, the trace fields of the Veech groups of both surfaces coincide.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\cdot(M,\omega)
.
Let
\left\{C_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}
be the full set of horizontal cylinders of
M
, and without loss of generality assume that
n>1
.
Let
c_{i}
denote the circumference of the cylinder
C_{i}
.
Then, these horizontal cylinders of
(M,\omega)
are
\mathcal{V}
-parallel in the language of
[
Wri15
, Definition 4.6]
.
It follows by
[
Wri15
, Theorem 7.1]
that the affine field of definition of
\mathcal{V}
is
\mathbb{Q}[c_{2}/c_{1},\dots,c_{n}/c_{1}]
.
On the other hand, by
[
Wri14
, Theorem 1.1]
, the affine field of definition is the same as the holonomy field of
(M,\omega)
(cf.
[
KS00
, Appendix]
for a definition of the holonomy field).
By
[
KS00
, Theorem 28]
, since
(M,\omega)
is a Veech surface, and hence its Veech group contains at least one pseudo-Anosov element, its holonomy field coincides with the trace field of its Veech group.

Now,
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
admits a horizontal cylinder decomposition of the form
M^{\prime}=\cup_{i=1}^{n}u(s_{i})C_{i}
, for some
s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
In particular, these cylinders have the same set of circumferences
\left\{c_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}
. Thus, its trace field coincides with that of
(M,\omega)
.
∎

In what follows, we let
\Gamma_{n}
be the Veech group of
(M_{n},\Gamma_{n})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}=\mathrm{SL}(M_{n},\omega_{n}).

We let
k(\Gamma_{n})
be its trace field.
Given an integer

,
we denote by
\mathcal{H}^{hyp}(2g-2)
, respectively
\mathcal{H}^{hyp}(g-1,g-1)
, the hyperelliptic components of strata of translation surfaces of genus

having either one zero of order
2g-2
, respectively two zeros of order
g-1
each and which are interchanged by a hyperelliptic involution of the underlying Riemann surface; cf.
[
KZ03
, Def. 2]
for the precise definition.
We will need the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
n>5
, the degree of the trace field satisfies
[k(\Gamma_{n}):\mathbb{Q}]\geq 3
, and for
n=5
, we have
[k(\Gamma_{5}):\mathbb{Q}]=2
.
Moreover, for all
n\geq 5
, the surfaces
(M_{n},\omega_{n})
belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{hyp}(n-2)
when
n
is even, and to
\mathcal{H}^{hyp}(\frac{n-3}{2},\frac{n-3}{2})

The proof of this lemma is standard and is included for completeness.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume the
2n
-gon generating

has unit length edges.
By considering the top horizontal edge and the closest parallel chord to it respectively, we obtain two horizontal saddle connections with holonomy
(1,0)
and
(\alpha_{n},0)

\alpha_{n}=1+2\cos(2\pi/2n)

Thus, by
[
KS00

\Gamma_{n}
.
Moreover, for
\zeta_{n}=\exp(2\pi i/2n)

we have that
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{n})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1})
is the fixed subfield under the complex conjugation automorphism of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})

Beyond chronological age: exploring how different future time perspective influences the health tourism intention
Authors: Caisheng Liao, Huiqing Xiao, Zhao Li, Yuki Todo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe burgeoning field of health tourism has garnered significant attention due to its integration of health and leisure, reflecting a growing consumer demand for wellness-oriented travel experiences. Based on the socioemotional selectivity theory, this study examines the relationship between future time perspective, health awareness, involvement, and health tourism intention. It aims to understand how different dimensions of future time perspective influence the intention to engage in health tourism, beyond the traditional chronological age.MethodsA cross-sectional survey was conducted among individuals aged 16 years and above with normal cognitive function. Data was collected through online questionnaires distributed on popular Chinese social media platforms, WeChat and QQ. A total of 536 valid questionnaires were returned, with a validity rate of 91.47%. The study employed the structural equation modeling (SEM) to test the proposed hypotheses.ResultsThe effect of different dimensions of future time perspective on health tourism intention differed. Limited future time perspective (LFTP) positively influenced health tourism intention, while open-ended future time perspective (OFTP) did not have a significant effect on health tourism intention. Involvement and health awareness not only directly influence health tourism intention, but also mediate the relationship between future time perspective (OFTP and LFTP) and health tourism intention respectively. Moreover, there is a significant difference in the effect of LFTP on healthy tourism intention between different age groups.ConclusionsThe findings highlight the importance of considering subjective time perspectives in understanding health tourism intention. Health tourism companies should focus on strategies that enhance health awareness and consumer involvement to capitalize on the positive effects of health tourism intention. Additionally, the study suggests that marketing efforts should be tailored to address the specific needs and goals of individuals with different future time perspectives, thereby optimizing the appeal of health tourism products. Future research should explore these relationships across diverse populations and contexts to further refine the understanding of health tourism decision-making.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40359-024-02201-x.

Introduction

Aging, environmental degradation, and economic growth have increased public awareness and demand for health, with daily medical checkups, wellness and leisure, and healthy eating gradually entering the public [1, 2]. The theme of “healing” is attracting more and more people’s interest [3]. People particularly need a tourism product that combines health and recreation [4]. They want to continue a healthy lifestyle when they are away from home [5]. Therefore, health tourism is growing steadily and become an extremely popular and dynamic component of modern tourism [3, 6–8]. However, the preference for health tourism is relatively high compared to other travel themes, but participation levels are low [3]. Health tourism usually refers to activities where consumers travel to destinations to access a variety of health services and medical care [9]. The purposes are improving health, disease diagnosis and treatment, and prevention. Although some scholars have discussed the factors influencing consumers’ health tourism intention in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic [10–12], tourism companies still face difficulties in understanding consumer tourism motivation due to the ongoing aging population and changes in consumer psychology [13]. Health tourism, as a new economic growth driver, not only meets the dual needs of residents’ health and tourism but also provides a feasible path to promote the transformation and upgrading of China’s tourism industry. Therefore, it is worth further concerning the potential psychological factors that influence health tourism behavior.

The evidence suggests that tourism demands and behavioral intention of consumers change throughout time and age [14]. Numerous studies have connected the relationship between chronological age or different age groups and potential travel behavior [15–17]. But, chronological age is not a good predictor of travel demand [13]. With the development of society and the diversification of lifestyles, people of different age groups may have similar interests and lifestyles, leading to similar travel demands, which are not related to chronological age [18]. For example, a 60-year-old person may feel that his psychological age is only 40 due to health, interests, or social activities, and his travel needs and behaviors may be more similar to middle-aged people rather than traditional elderly people. Therefore, recent studies have focused on some new variables that provide greater insight into travelers’ behavioral intention, such as emotional, subjective age, and time perception [19–21]. In particular, the perception of time plays an important role in consumer behavioral decisions [21–24].

The emphasis of socioemotional selectivity theory (SST) on future time perspective becoming a pivotal element in shaping individual social behavior decisions has garnered significant attention among scholars, particularly in understanding how individuals prioritize their goals based on their perceived future time [25–27]. SST posits that while future time perspective is intricately connected to an individual’s chronological age, it is not the age per se that dictates motivational and behavioral outcomes. Instead, it is the transformation in an individual’s time perspective that significantly reshapes their goal prioritization and, consequently, their action [28].In travel-related studies, future time perspective has been shown to have a positive impact on consumer’s travel [19, 24], which reflects, an individual’s perception of future time and plans, and can guide and govern personal behavior through future goals and rewards [29]. The decision-making process in health tourism is complex, often involving considerations of health outcomes, personal well-being, and the intrinsic value of travel experiences. The integration of these factors suggests that future time perspective could play a significant role in shaping health tourism decisions, yet the extent and nature of this influence are not well understood. In light of these considerations, there is a clear need for empirical study to explore in depth the impact of future time perspective on health tourism intention.

Moreover, due to the complexity of health tourism decision-making, there is still a lack of research revealing the underlying mechanism of the future time perspective on health tourism intention. To fill this gap, this study explores the mediating role of health awareness and involvement in the relationship between future time perspective and health tourism intention. Helping people to recognize the impact of present behavior on the future can increase awareness of future health [30]. Health awareness is the basis for individuals to develop healthy behaviors [31]. It can enable people to adapt with mindfulness to the post-pandemic world of travel [32] and plays a mediating role in time-related personality and health-promoting behaviors [33]. In addition to health awareness, involvement in health tourism activities is a key variable that can be influenced by future time perspective. High levels of involvement are associated with enhanced goal-setting and motivation, which in turn can lead to increased commitment to health tourism activities and a greater likelihood of behavioral intention [27, 34]. Although some studies have provided empirical evidence of the link between future time perspective, health awareness, involvement, and behavioral intention. However, whether there is a mediating effect of health awareness, and involvement between future time perspective and health tourism intention requires further analysis. By elucidating the mediating roles of health awareness and involvement, researchers can provide a more comprehensive understanding of the psychological mechanisms driving health tourism behavior and inform the development of targeted strategies for the health tourism industry.

Finally, the integration of future time perspective with age-related factors in the context of health tourism intention introduces a compelling avenue for investigation. SST posits that as individuals age, their perception of time alters, leading to shifts in goal priorities and emotional regulation strategies [35]. However, the extent to which future time perspective influences health tourism intention may vary across different age cohorts [36, 37]. Conducting a multigroup analysis allows for a nuanced understanding of these differences by comparing the impact of future time perspective on health tourism intention among younger and older adults. This approach not only acknowledges the heterogeneity within the consumer base but also uncovers the unique motivations and behaviors that characterize each age group. The inclusion of multigroup analysis in this study, therefore, serves to delineate the distinct pathways through which future time perspective influences health tourism intention across age groups and can better appreciate the role of age in shaping health-related decisions and behaviors.

In summary, this study presented in this manuscript aims to explore the influence of different dimensions of future time perspective on health tourism intention, transcending the traditional focus on chronological age. It underscores the burgeoning interest in health tourism, driven by an integration of health consciousness with leisure activities, reflecting a contemporary consumer trend towards wellness-oriented travel. The research is grounded in SST, examining how future time perspective, health awareness, involvement, and health tourism intention interrelate. The significance of this study lies in its potential to provide insights into consumer motivations for health tourism, which can inform marketing strategies and product development within the industry. By identifying the mediating roles of health awareness and involvement, the study offers a deeper understanding of the psychological mechanisms underpinning health tourism behavior, ultimately contributing to targeted and effective industry practices.

Review and hypothesis

Health tourism intention

Health tourism intention refers to the predisposition or likelihood of individuals to engage in health tourism activities [8, 11]. It is a concept that encapsulates the motivational factors influencing a person’s decision to seek health services and experiences in a travel context [11, 38]. Health tourism itself is a rapidly growing sector within the tourism industry, where consumers travel to access health services, medical care, and wellness activities [3, 8, 38]. These can range from spa treatments and wellness retreats to more specialized medical procedures and health check-ups. The study of health tourism intention is of paramount importance for several reasons.

Firstly, understanding consumers’ intentions can provide crucial insights into the decision-making process, which is inherently complex and multifaceted [8, 10, 38]. It involves considerations of health outcomes, personal well-being, and the intrinsic value of travel experiences. By examining health tourism intention, researchers can uncover the psychological and behavioral factors that drive individuals to opt for health tourism, thereby informing targeted marketing strategies and product development within the industry. Secondly, despite the burgeoning interest in health tourism, there is a relative dearth of research focused on the intention to engage in such activities [8]. While previous studies have explored some aspects of health tourism, such as the motivations behind it and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on consumer behavior [11, 12], the specific intention to pursue health tourism remains understudied. This gap in the literature is notable given the increasing consumer demand for wellness-oriented travel experiences and the potential of health tourism to contribute to both individual well-being and economic growth. Lastly, health tourism intention is a critical predictor of actual behavior [8, 39]. It serves as a bridge between attitudes, beliefs, and the actual engagement in health tourism. By identifying the factors that influence this intention, stakeholders in the tourism and healthcare sectors can develop more effective strategies to promote health tourism, enhance consumer experience, and ultimately foster a healthier society.

Future time perspective

Time perspective is considered to be a mental construct representing an individual’s relationship to time, which arises from the cognitive process of dividing one’s experience into past, present, and future time frames [29]. Time perspective theory divides time perspective into five independent dimensions based on the individual’s attitude toward each time frame: past-positive, past-negative, present-hedonic, present-fatalistic, and future time perspective [29]. Future time perspective refers to an individual’s perception of future possibilities or time left in life [28] and plays an important role in consumers’ travel decisions and goal choices [19, 40]. According to SST, age-related changes in ‘time perception’ lead to changes in social goals. While Carstensen and Lang [41]viewed future time perspective as a one-dimensional and bipolar variable ranging from limited to vast futures, Cate and John [42]demonstrated that future time perspective consists of two dimensions, open-ended future time perspective and limited future time perspective, that vary with age. Since then, Cozzolino et al. [43] and Kooij et al. [23] have also distinguished between the dimensions of future time perspective using open-ended time and limited time.

Open-ended future time perspective (OFTP) refers to individuals’ belief that they have ample time in the future, which leads them to be more inclined to pursue long-term goals and intellectual achievements [23, 28]. Under OFTP, individuals may invest more in goals that require long-term effort to achieve, such as education and career development [23, 28]. This perspective is often associated with young people, as they typically have longer life expectancies and are therefore more likely to focus on future planning and accomplishments. Limited future time perspective (LFTP) refers to individuals’ belief that their future time is limited, which leads them to be more inclined to pursue immediate emotional satisfaction and social connections when choosing goals and engaging in social interactions [23, 28]. Under LFTP, individuals may reduce their pursuit of long-term goals and instead focus on activities that can bring immediate emotional rewards [23, 28], such as intimate interactions with family and friends. This perspective is often associated with older adults, as they may be more acutely aware that their life is approaching its end. In line with these studies, we divided future time perspective into OFTP and LFTP to explore the impact of different dimensions of future time perspective on health tourism intention.

Future time perspective and health tourism intention

Numerous psychological studies have shown that when a person focuses on the future, it inevitably has a serious impact on his or her present cognition and behavior [27, 44]. Based on SST, future time perspective can predict individuals’ choice of social goals [45]. It reflects an integral future orientation, the tendency to resist temptation, and to focus more on future goals and rewards. It can be expressed as OFTP, where there is plenty of time left in the future and the focus is more on opportunities, or LFTP, where there is limited time left in the future and the future will not change much [46, 47]. Individuals with different FTPs will differ in their choice of social goals [46, 48]. For example, people with OFTP, who are more focused on acquiring resources and knowledge, will prioritize optimizing their future goals.; when future time is perceived to be limited, there is more focus on the “present moment” and LFTP is more focused on emotion regulation goals [25, 49]. However, these two target choices are not mutually exclusive and can be used as two side-by-side startup paths. That is, when individuals have different time perspectives, it will influence their intention to travel based on different purposes.

A broader future perspective on time may be an important factor for people to choose health tourism. Individuals with an OFTP will feel they have ample time ahead and therefore prioritize knowledge acquisition, exploration, and long-term goal attainment [48]. This perspective is associated with a greater propensity for seeking out new experiences and engaging in activities that contribute to personal growth and development [54. Health tourism, which involves traveling to different locations to maintain or improve health, aligns well with these objectives. By exploring diverse cultures, environments, and healthcare practices, individuals with an OFTP can satisfy their curiosity and expand their horizons, thereby enhancing their well-being and quality of life. On the other hand, individuals with an LFTP will perceive their remaining time as finite and thus focus on emotionally meaningful experiences and relationships [50]. For this demographic, health tourism can serve as an avenue to deepen social connections, particularly if travel is done in the company of close friends or family. Moreover, the pursuit of health-related activities, such as spa retreats or therapeutic vacations, can provide immediate emotional benefits and increase life satisfaction. The emphasis on emotional well-being and the enjoyment of life’s pleasures, central tenets of SST for those with an LFTP [25, 35], supports the notion that health tourism can have a positive impact on their psychological health.

In summary, both OFTP and LFTP can positively influence health tourism intention through different pathways. OFTP encourages health tourism as a means of personal growth and exploration, while LFTP fosters engagement in health tourism to enhance emotional fulfillment and relationship building. These insights from SST offer a theoretical framework to understand how individual time perspectives shape motivations and behaviors related to health tourism. Health awareness as a mediator

Health awareness is a mental structure, a state of mind in which an individual perceives his or her health [51, 52]. It is often expressed as the extent to which people are concerned about their health [53], including alertness to health, participation in health behaviors, and self-monitoring of health. Health awareness is an integral part of building health-promoting behaviors and plays an even greater role than sociodemographic characteristics [54]. The awareness of an individual is also influenced by the future time perspective. Such as individuals with a high future time perspective will have a higher awareness of environmental protection in studies on ecotourism [55]. Based on SST, in the context of health awareness, individuals with an OFTP may be more proactive in seeking health information and adopting preventive measures, while those with a LFTP may be more attentive to managing existing health conditions and seeking immediate care when needed.

Future time perspective reflects the individual’s general future orientation as well as the perception of plans [29]. Individuals with an OFTP perceive themselves to have ample time ahead, which leads them to prioritize goals related to knowledge acquisition, skill development, and social expansion [25]. This perspective encourages a long-term approach to health, as individuals are more likely to engage in health-promoting behaviors that offer future benefits, such as regular exercise, healthy eating, and preventive health screenings. The motivation for these behaviors stems from the belief that they will have the opportunity to enjoy the outcomes of these health investments over a longer period. On the other hand, individuals with a LFTP perceive their time as constrained, leading them to focus on emotionally meaningful goals and immediate well-being [25]. This perspective can lead to a greater emphasis on maintaining current health status and seeking immediate health-related gratifications [56]. For example, older adults with a LFTP might prioritize regular doctor visits, medication adherence, and managing chronic conditions to ensure they can continue to enjoy their social relationships and activities that bring them immediate happiness. Both OFTP and LFTP can positively influence health awareness, albeit in different ways. OFTP may lead to a more proactive approach to health, focusing on long-term benefits, while LFTP may result in a more reactive approach, focusing on immediate health needs and emotional well-being. Thus, the following hypotheses are developed:H2a: Open-ended future time perspective (OFTP) positively affects health awareness.H2b: Limited future time perspective (LFTP) positively affects health awareness.

Furthermore, health awareness plays a pivotal role in shaping an individual’s intention to engage in health tourism. Health-conscious consumers are more concerned about their state of well-being. From a theoretical standpoint, individuals are more likely to adopt health behaviors if they perceive a high susceptibility to a health condition and the severity of that condition, coupled with the belief that certain actions can reduce their risk or the negative effects of the condition [57]. In the context of health tourism, this could mean that individuals who are highly aware of their health needs and potential risks are more inclined to seek out tourism experiences that offer health benefits. On the other hand, health awareness also fosters an emotional connection to one’s well-being [58], which can be a powerful driver for health tourism intention. When health is highly valued as part of an individual’s identity or personal values, the desire to maintain or improve health through tourism becomes more pronounced [59]. This emotional investment in health can lead to a stronger intention to seek out health tourism experiences that align with one’s values and goals. Thus, we hypothesize:

Involvement as a mediator

Involvement refers to the degree of attention, emotional involvement, and the amount of time and effort an individual invests in a certain activity, decision-making process, or consumption behavior [60]. It can be seen as a measure of the importance, interest, and level of participation of an individual in a specific object or situation [61]. According to SST, both OFTP and LFTP can positively influence involvement in health tourism products, albeit through different mechanisms. An OFTP may enhance involvement in products that are perceived to have long-term health benefits, while a LFTP may increase involvement in products that offer immediate health improvements or emotional satisfaction.

SST posits that individuals with an OFTP, who perceive a longer future, are more likely to engage in activities that offer long-term benefits [23, 28]. This includes health tourism products that promise future health benefits, such as wellness retreats, preventive health screenings, and lifestyle changes. Research has shown that individuals with an OFTP are more likely to seek out health tourism experiences that align with their long-term health goals [28]. This suggests that they are more involved in health tourism products that provide opportunities for knowledge acquisition, skill development, and social expansion. An OFTP can lead to a higher level of involvement in health tourism products that are perceived to have long-term health benefits. This involvement is driven by the individual’s desire to optimize their future health outcomes and to engage in activities that enhance their quality of life over an extended period. Conversely, individuals with a LFTP, who perceive their time as limited, prioritize activities that provide immediate emotional satisfaction and well-being [23, 28]. This perspective can lead to a focus on health tourism products that offer immediate health benefits or emotional gratification [62]. Thus, health tourism under this perspective would involve activities that provide quick satisfaction, such as relaxing spa treatments, rejuvenating retreats, or adventurous wellness challenges t [63]. A LFTP can result in increased involvement in health tourism products that are perceived to improve immediate health status or emotional well-being. This involvement is driven by the individual’s desire to maximize the quality of their present life by seeking health experiences that provide immediate benefits. The level of involvement can affect the behavioral decision-making of consumers [64]. Consumers with high involvement in health tourism pay more attention to related information and show greater interest in exploring different health tourism products and services. Consumers in the high product involvement scenario have already spent a lot of time choosing and comparing products before making a purchase decision, and customers will have more comprehensive information about the products [65]. This heightened focus leads to a deeper understanding and appreciation of the benefits and offerings available, which in turn strengthens their intention to engage in health tourism. Evidence has shown that involvement has a significant impact on consumers’ behavioral intention [66, 67]. Individuals with high involvement in health tourism products are more likely to invest time in assessing risks associated with health tourism, such as medical quality, safety standards, and potential complications. By doing so, they can build trust in the services provided, which is crucial for converting interest into concrete intention. Differences in future time perspective and healthy tourism intention in different age groups

The SST suggests that as individuals age, their perception of time changes, leading to shifts in goal priorities [23]. Younger individuals, perceiving a longer future, are inclined towards goals that offer long-term benefits, such as knowledge acquisition and skill development [27]. This aligns with the concept of an OFTP, where the perception of ample future time fosters a focus on opportunities and personal growth. Given that OFTP is associated with a longer-term perspective, it is plausible to expect variations in how OFTP influences health tourism intention across different age groups. Younger individuals, who naturally have a more extended future ahead, may be more inclined to engage in health tourism as a long-term investment in their well-being. In contrast, older individuals, who might perceive less time ahead, may prioritize different goals, leading to a potentially different relationship between OFTP and health tourism intention.

Conversely, SST posits that as individuals become more aware of the limited time they have, they prioritize emotionally meaningful and socially engaging activities [23]. This is reflective of a LFTP, where the perception of time scarcity leads to a focus on immediate emotional satisfaction and social connections [27, 68]. The effect of LFTP on health tourism intention is expected to differ across age groups due to the inherent difference in the perception of time remaining. Older individuals, who are more likely to have a LFTP, may view health tourism as a means to immediate emotional and social benefits, thus influencing their intention differently than younger individuals who may not perceive time as scarce [28]. This suggests that the influence of LFTP on health tourism intention could manifest differently when comparing younger versus older age groups. Method

Sample and procedure

China, with the world’s second-largest population, presents a significant potential for the health tourism market [69]. In the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic, the Chinese government has introduced a series of policies to promote the development of the health industry, further stimulating the demand for health tourism [69]. Moreover, China’s unique cultural background and rich natural resources provide a favorable setting for the development of distinctive health tourism products. Therefore, selecting China as the context of not only offers a unique perspective for understanding the global health tourism market but also holds significant theoretical and practical implications for revealing the psychological mechanisms behind health tourism behavior and guiding the development and marketing strategies for health tourism products.

In the present study, we targeted individuals with normal cognitive function, aged 16 years and above, to explore their health tourism intentions. This age range was selected to encompass major populations capable of making independent decisions regarding health tourism. Recognizing the constraints of time, economic resources, and the desire to achieve a reasonable sample recovery rate, we adopted a combination of convenience and random sampling techniques to collect our data. This approach, while not constituting a true probability sample, was selected for its pragmatic utility in accessing a large and diverse participant pool within the stipulated timeframe and budget.

The data collection strategy was predominantly online, utilizing web questionnaires disseminated through WeChat and QQ, which are among the most widely used social media platforms in China. The widespread use of these platforms among various age groups in China ensures a diverse sample that can represent the broader population’s perspectives on health tourism. The use of social media platforms facilitated rapid data collection, which is advantageous for timely research output, especially given the dynamic nature of health tourism trends. Moreover, this method was chosen to leverage the extensive reach of these platforms to engage a broad spectrum of the population. The survey was live from March 31 to April 11, 2023, and we obtained a total of 586 completed questionnaires, with 536 meeting our validity criteria—a robust response rate of 91.47%. We excluded responses that were completed in an unreasonably short time frame (less than 2 min) or exhibited patterns indicative of repetitive or insincere participation, ensuring the quality and authenticity of our dataset.

The age distribution was skewed towards younger adults, with 64.55% of respondents falling within the 16–29 years age bracket, and 35.45% aged 30 and above. This distribution is representative of the active user base on the social media platforms utilized for data collection. Notably, only 15.67% of our respondents reported no travel in the past year, suggesting a generally travel-active sample. Furthermore, 90.11% of respondents indicated a preference for stays of less than one month.

Measurements

The questionnaire was divided into two parts. The first part consisted of demographic information of the respondents, including gender, age, number of times traveled in the past year and the length of stay that health tourism tends to take. The second part is the main body of the questionnaire, including the measurement of variables such as health tourism intention, involvement, future time perspective, and health awareness. The variables measured are adapted from internationally recognized scales. These scales have been empirically proven to have good reliability and validity in a great deal of studies. To make the study more suitable for the current research, the initial version of the questionnaire was pre-tested and reviewed by academic and industry experts. Minor modifications were made based on their feedback (e.g. questionnaire phrasing, wording, and layout). This study adopted a translation-back translation procedure to ensure the scientific and rationality. All scales were measured using a 7-point Likert scale.

Health awareness

Health awareness was measured using the scale developed by Gould [70]. The scale consists of three items to assess the extent to which individuals value their own health status. A sample item includes “I reflect about my health a lot.”.

Future time perspective

Future time perspective was assessed using the 10-item scale developed by Lang and Carstensen [46]. This scale has demonstrated high reliability in previous studies. According to research by Cate and John [47], Cozzolino et al. [43], and Kooij et al. [48], Future time perspective can be divided into two dimensions: OFTP and LFTP. Among them, OFTP has 7 items, with representative questions such as:” Many opportunities await me in the future.”; LFTP has 3 items, with representative questions such as:” I have the sense that time is running out.”.

Involvement

The involvement scale was mainly adapted from Novak et al. [71]. This scale consists of a set of five items aimed at measuring consumers’ involvement in online environments. To make the measured items more suitable for the context of health tourism research, we modified the questionnaire’s subject word. A sample item includes “I’m interested in health tourism.”.

Health tourism intention

The measurement of health tourism intention mainly refers to the medical tourism research scale developed by Wang [72], which consists of three questions. To better fit the research theme, we replaced the term ‘medical tourism’ in the items with ‘health tourism’. A sample item includes “I intend to participate in Health tourism in the future.”.

Statistical technique

In the current investigation, we have adopted Covariance-Based Structural Equation Modeling (CB-SEM) to examine the intricate relationships posited among the constructs of future time perspective, health awareness, involvement, and health tourism intention.

Due to the multifaceted nature of our research model, which encompasses an array of latent variables and the investigation of mediational effects, dictates the application of a sophisticated statistical technique. CB-SEM emerges as an optimal choice for such an analysis, given its capacity to not only estimate measurement errors but also to rigorously assess the psychometric properties of the latent constructs. The selection of CB-SEM is further justified by empirical evidence highlighting its efficacy in mitigating common method bias, thereby bolstering the validity of the research findings [73]. Additionally, the robustness of CB-SEM in dealing with latent variables and complex path models has been well-documented in the literature [74], making it a suitable candidate for the current study’s analytical demands.

The sample size of 537 valid responses, garnered from our survey efforts, surpasses the conventional threshold for SEM analysis. The recommended minimum sample size for SEM is often cited as 200 [75]; however, our sample significantly exceeds this benchmark, thereby enhancing the statistical power and reliability of our CB-SEM analysis. This larger sample size also allows for a more generalized interpretation of the results, contributing to the external validity of the study.

Results

Common method bia(CMB) and multicollinearity analysis

The adoption of self-reporting method considered to be prone to CMB. In addition to compensating for the common bias through procedural control methods (Spatial separation of respondents, anonymous responses, reduced guesswork by respondents on questions, etc.), we also examined the effect of CMB through Harmon One-Factor Analysis [76]. This was done by conducting a factor analysis with all measured entries having eigenvalues greater than 1 and without any factor rotation. The variance explained by the first factor of all variables was 33.173%, which is less than the critical criterion of 50% [73], indicating that the effect of CMB is not serious in this study.

Moreover, the existence of collinearity between latent variables can lead to biased predictions of path coefficients. Therefore, this paper uses the indicator Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) to identify whether there is collinearity between the endogenous constructs. The results of the test showed that the VIF values for each latent variable ranged from 2.000 to 2.633, which is less than the threshold of 5.000 [77], which passed the multicollinearity assessment.

Measurement model analysis

Before evaluating the measurement model, the sample data were first tested for normality to verify that the requirements of the SEM were fulfilled. The analysis revealed that the skewness of the sample data ranged from − 0.137 to 0.045 with an absolute value of less than 1 and the kurtosis ranged from − 1.160 to −0.087 with an absolute value of less than 2, representing that the data passed the normality test [74].

According to the methods of Anderson and Gerbing [78], confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was first performed to check the reliability and validity of the measurements. After the analysis of AMOS 26.0, the overall fitness indicators of the CFA measurement model were good (χ2 /df = 1.187; RMSEA = 0.019; GFI = 0.965; CFI = 0.995; NFI = 0.969; TLI = 0.994).

The reliability of each construct was tested by Cronbach’s α and combining reliability (CR). According to the results of the measurement models in Table 2, Cronbach’s α coefficients were all greater than 0.7 and the CR values were above 0.7, representing adequate reliability and internal consistency of each construct indicator.

Validity is measured by convergent validity and discriminant validity. Of these, convergent validity is mainly reflected through standardized factor loadings and average variance extracted (AVE). As can be shown in Table 2, the standardized factor loadings were all greater than 0.7 and the AVE values were all greater than 0.6, representing a high convergent validity of the latent variable scale [79]. The results of the discriminant validity test are shown in Table 3. The square root of all latent variables AVR is greater than the correlation coefficient of this variable with other variables, which indicates good discriminant validity between variables [79].

Structural model and hypothesis testing

The parameters of the SEM were estimated using the maximum likelihood method in AMOS 26.0, and the metrics of each fit met the criteria (χ2 /df = 1.222; RMSEA = 0.020; GFI = 0.963; CFI = 0.994; NFI = 0.968; TLI = 0.993). The hypothetical model was tested and the results showed in Table 4; Fig. 1.

The role of different dimensions of future time perspective on health tourism intention is not consistent. Firstly, the positive impact of OFTP on health tourism intention was found to be insignificant (β = 0.095, p > 0.05), leading to the rejection of Hypothesis H1a, which is based on SST. This suggests that in the context of health tourism, an open and exploratory attitude toward the future does not directly promote the intention to travel for health purposes. This divergence from SST predictions may be because health tourism, as a specific activity, is more closely tied to individual health needs and the pursuit of quality of life rather than solely to socioemotional needs [63]. On the other hand, LFTP had a significant positive impact on health tourism intention (β = 0.117, p < 0.05), supporting Hypothesis H1b. This finding aligns with SST, indicating that when individuals are aware of the finiteness of their time, they are more inclined to choose activities that enhance emotional satisfaction and social connections. In the context of health tourism, this could mean that individuals place greater value on health tourism experiences that offer emotional support and social interaction, thereby strengthening their intention to travel.

Moreover, the results support the hypothesis that an OFTP significantly and positively influences health awareness (β = 0.311, p < 0.05), confirming Hypothesis H2a. This suggests that individuals with a more open and optimistic view of the future are likely to be more health-conscious, which aligns with the SST’s emphasis on the pursuit of knowledge and growth when time is perceived as abundant [25]. Similarly, a LFTP also significantly enhances health awareness (β = 0.163, p < 0.01), supporting Hypothesis H2b. This finding indicates that a sense of time scarcity can lead individuals to prioritize their health, as they may seek to maximize their well-being within the perceived constraints of their lifespan [25]. This is consistent with SST’s assertion that when future time is perceived as limited, individuals focus on emotionally significant goals, which can include maintaining good health. The findings reveal that both OFTP and LFTP significantly and positively affect involvement, thereby supporting Hypotheses H3a and H3b, respectively. The stronger influence of OFTP on involvement (β = 0.361, p < 0.05) suggests that individuals who perceive a more expansive future are more likely to engage deeply with health tourism opportunities. This aligns with the notion that an open-ended view of the future can foster a sense of curiosity and a desire for meaningful experiences [25], which in turn increases involvement with health tourism. Similarly, LFTP (β = 0.103, p < 0.05) also positively impacts involvement, albeit to a lesser extent than OFTP. This indicates that even when individuals perceive their future as limited, they still seek to engage in activities that are important to them, such as health tourism, which can be seen as a way to maximize the quality of their remaining time.

Finally, health awareness itself was found to have a significant positive effect on health tourism intention (β = 0.155, p < 0.01), supporting Hypothesis H2c. This suggests that heightened health awareness directly translates into a stronger intention to engage in health tourism, highlighting the importance of health as a motivator for travel decisions. The direct positive effect of involvement on health tourism intention (β = 0.268, p < 0.001), as indicated by Hypothesis H3c, underscores the critical role of involvement in the decision-making process. High levels of involvement suggest that individuals are more likely to consider health tourism as a viable option [64], leading to stronger intention to participate in such activities.

Discussion

Based on SST, the main objective of this study was to explore the relationship between two different dimensions of future time perspective and health tourism intention and to verify the mediating role of health awareness and involvement. The results of the study showed that the stronger the LFTP of an individual, the higher the intention to health tourism, while the OFTP did not have a significant effect on the health tourism intention. This illustrated the differences in the impact of different dimensions of future time perspective on consumers’ health travel intention. In addition, the mediating effects of health awareness and involvement between future time perspective and individual health tourism intention reveal the pathways of action between future time perspective and the relationship between the influence of health tourism intention. OFTP and LFTP can have a positive impact on health tourism intention through health awareness and involvement respectively. Finally, we also obtained an interesting finding that there is a significant difference in the effect of LFTP on healthy tourism intention between different age groups.

Theoretical implications

The results of this study provide several theoretical insights in understanding the consumers’ health tourism intention. Firstly, this study revealed the mechanisms that influence future time perspective and health tourism intention beyond chronological ages, suggesting that health tourism research should pay more attention to subjective factors beyond objective age. This aligns with the broader theoretical shift towards considering subjective experiences as central to understanding consumer behavior. Many studies have shown that subjective age or perception of time may be more helpful than actual age in understanding how individuals view themselves and their behavior [21, 23, 80]. However, relatively little research has been done in this field about tourism decision-making. Our findings suggest that future time perspective, as a subjective construct, can offers a more dynamic and individualized lens through which to view consumer behavior. This perspective allows for a richer understanding of the diverse ways in which consumers perceive and engage with health tourism opportunities [36, 37]. Furthermore, our research contributes to the understanding of age-related differences in health tourism decision-making. The multi-group analysis reveals a significant variation in how LFTP influences health tourism intention across different age cohorts, suggesting that age and time perception are intertwined factors that collectively influence consumer behavior. This finding adds a new dimension to the existing literature, which has often treated age as the independent variable or control variable [15, 16, 36], by demonstrating the importance of considering age in conjunction with future time perspective.

Building upon the theoretical implications of our study, we extend the discourse by emphasizing the critical distinction between OFTP and LFTP and their respective impacts on health tourism intention. This bifurcation of future time perspective into two dimensions introduces a novel theoretical perspective that refines our understanding of consumer behavior within the domain of health tourism. Previous studies have often treated future time perspective as a singular construct, potentially overlooking the nuanced ways in which different perceptions of time can influence decision-making [18, 40, 49]. This research, however, reveals that OFTP and LFTP have distinct effects on health tourism intention, suggesting that a one-size-fits-all approach to understanding the role of future time perspective in consumer behavior is inadequate. This finding underscores the importance of recognizing the heterogeneity within future time perspective and its multifaceted influence on behavioral decisions [23]. The non-significant effect of OFTP on health tourism intention challenges the assumption that an expansive view of the future would inherently drive individuals towards health tourism activities. It suggests that while OFTP may foster a general openness to future possibilities, it does not necessarily translate into a heightened intention to engage in health tourism. This insight indicates that health tourism, as a specific behavior, is likely influenced by more immediate health needs and the pursuit of quality of life, rather than by the broader socioemotional goals or the desire for novelty and exploration [63]. Conversely, the positive effect of LFTP on health tourism intention aligns with the notion that as individuals become more aware of the finiteness of their time, they prioritize activities that offer immediate emotional and social benefits. This finding suggests that health tourism may serve as a means for individuals with a LFTP to fulfill their need for emotional satisfaction and social connection, thereby enhancing their well-being and quality of life. This study’s results contribute to a deeper understanding of how future time perspective influences behavior, particularly in health-oriented activities.

Finally, the mediating roles of health awareness and involvement further enrich the theoretical framework to reveal the influence mechanism of health tourism intention more comprehensively. Health awareness, as a cognitive mediator, reflects the individual’s conscious regard for their health status and the proactive measures they are willing to undertake [53]. Its mediating role suggests that regardless of whether an individual perceives their future time as open-ended or limited, heightened health awareness can significantly bolster the intention to engage in health tourism. This implies that educational initiatives aimed at increasing health consciousness could be a strategic approach to foster health tourism intentions. Involvement, on the other hand, represents the affective engagement of consumers with health tourism, encapsulating their interest and emotional investment in such activities [64]. The study’s findings indicate that a higher level of involvement, influenced by both OFTP and LFTP, intensifies the intention to participate in health tourism. This suggests that marketing strategies focusing on creating immersive and engaging experiences can effectively tap into consumers’ emotional drivers, thereby enhancing their inclination towards health tourism. These variables serve as cognitive and affective bridges between future time perspective and health tourism intention, highlighting the importance of both rational and emotional considerations in consumer decision-making. The study’s findings support the integration of SST with health awareness and involvement, suggesting that these factors are critical in the decision-making process. This integration provides a more comprehensive theoretical model for understanding health tourism behavior, which can be applied to other consumer behaviors influenced by time perception.

Practical implications

Health tourism responds to people’s pursuit of health and the change in the tourism concept. Wellness and health vacations become the most important type and mode of tourism under the structure of the tourism industry in the new period [3, 6]. Based on the results of this study, we have drawn some practical implications.

Firstly, tourism companies should be aware that objective age is not the only criterion in determining consumer decisions. Companies can use broader age concepts such as time perspective, subjective age, and functional age as important indicators for understanding consumer decisions. Understanding the relationship between the broader concept of time and consumer decisions, will, in turn, provide more targeted strategies for the marketing of tourism products.

Secondly, we should not only focus on the health needs of consumers but also take into account the emotional regulation needs of consumers. For individuals with LFTP, health tourism operators should focus on creating experiences that offer immediate health benefits and emotional gratification. The demographic values activities that enhance their current well-being, suggesting that health tourism products should be designed to provide tangible, immediate outcomes. On the other hand, for those with OFTP, the emphasis should be on the long-term benefits of health tourism, such as personal growth and development. This group may be more receptive to wellness retreats, preventive health screenings, and other activities that contribute to a healthier future.

Finally, the study also emphasizes the importance of health awareness and involvement as mediators between future time perspective and health tourism intention. Health tourism businesses should invest in educational initiatives and interactive experiences that raise health awareness among consumers. By fostering a deeper understanding of the health benefits of their services, operators can increase the likelihood of consumers choosing health tourism as a means to improve their quality of life. Additionally, strategies that enhance consumer involvement, such as personalized services and engaging marketing campaigns, can further strengthen the connection between consumers and health tourism products.

Limitations

Although this study has achieved some innovations and breakthroughs, some limitations need to be further explored in future studies.

Firstly, the cross-sectional design of the study limits the ability to establish causality and observe changes over time. Longitudinal research is needed to track how future time perspective dimensions evolve and their long-term effects on health tourism intention. Additionally, the study’s reliance on self-reported data may introduce CMB, despite procedural controls and statistical checks. Future studies could employ objective measures or multiple sources of data to corroborate the findings.

Secondly, the study employed a non-random convenience sampling method for data collection, which, while representative of the population in terms of cognitive function and age, may not fully reflect the diversity of cultural, socioeconomic, and demographic factors. Research in different cultural contexts and with more diverse populations could provide a more comprehensive understanding of the future time perspective and health tourism intention relationship. At the same time, not controlling for age may limit the generalizability of our findings to populations with different age distributions. It is necessary to treat age as a controlled variable and discuss its impact on health intention along with future time perspective in the future.

Finally, this study has not yet examined the moderating mechanisms between future time perspective and health tourism intention. Future research could explore the boundary conditions of the relationship between future time perspective and health tourism intention from the perspectives of environmental factors, economic factors, and personality traits to build a more complete theoretical framework for health tourism decision-making.

Conclusion

This study successfully explored the intricate relationship between the dimensions of future time perspective and health tourism intention, with a particular emphasis on the mediating roles of health awareness and involvement. The results indicate that while LFTP significantly enhances health tourism intention, OFTP does not exert a substantial direct influence on these intentions. This divergence from the SST suggests that health tourism, as a health-oriented activity, is more closely linked to individual health needs and the pursuit of quality of life. The mediating effects of health awareness and involvement further elucidate the pathways through which future time perspective influences health tourism intention. OFTP and LFTP both positively impact health awareness, which in turn significantly boosts intention for health tourism. This highlights the importance of health awareness in shaping consumer behavior in the health tourism sector and underscores the potential of involvement as a key factor in fostering the intention. Furthermore, the study extends the understanding of age-related differences in health tourism decision-making by revealing significant variations in how LFTP influences health tourism intention across different age cohorts. This suggests that age and time perception are intertwined factors that collectively influence consumer behavior.

Overall, the findings contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of consumer behavior in health tourism, emphasizing the need for targeted marketing strategies that consider both the direct and indirect influences of future time perspective. By enhancing health awareness and fostering involvement, practitioners can effectively engage consumers with varying time perspectives, ultimately promoting health tourism as a valuable means to improve quality of life. Future research should continue to explore these relationships in diverse contexts to provide a comprehensive understanding of the factors driving health tourism intention.

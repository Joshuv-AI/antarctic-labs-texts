1.
Introduction

We will compute the equivariant cohomology ring, with integral coefficients, of the representation variety
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
. By a result of Pettet and Souto
[
21
]
this space is homotopy equivalent to the representation space
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},U(n))
. From the viewpoint of geometry, the cohomology we compute may be identified with the cohomology of the moduli stack of flat holomorphic vector bundles over an elliptic curve, or of flat unitary connections on a torus. Moduli spaces of flat connections on compact surfaces have been studied extensively, and determining the ring structure on their cohomology is a central and often difficult problem. For surface groups of genus
g\geq 2
one often restricts attention to smooth components of the moduli space by imposing constraints on rank and degree (see e.g. the pioneering paper of Atiyah and Bott
[
2
]
, the survey of Jeffery
[
14
]
, or
[
12
,
13
,
15
]
). In genus
1
, there is only a single component which is singular when
n\geq 2
due to the presence of reducible connections. The approach we take is by homotopy theoretic methods (Hochschild homology,
K
-theory) rather than differential geometric ones.

A representation
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is determined by the images of the generators, and thus
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
may be identified with the commuting variety

C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))=\{(A,B)\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})^{2}\mid AB=BA\}\,,

equipped with the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-action by simultaneous conjugation. Baird
[
3
]
has proved an appealing formula for the cohomology ring of the space of
m
-tuples of commuting elements in a (compact) Lie group
G
. The description is given in terms of invariants for the action of the Weyl group on a maximal torus. In the present situation this formula implies

(1.1)

H^{\ast}_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}(C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}));\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{\ast}(BT\times T^{2};\mathbb{Q})^{S_{n}}\,,

where
T
is an
n
-dimensional torus and
S_{n}
is the symmetric group. However, this description is valid only over coefficient fields of characteristic prime to
|S_{n}|
, and it provides no information about the structure of the ideal of relations. Using Baird’s formula, Kishimoto and Takeda
[
16
]
identified minimal generating sets for the rational cohomology rings of spaces of commuting elements in various classical groups, but the full ring structure was made explicit only by ad hoc computation in a very limited number of cases; mostly Lie groups of rank
\leq 2
(see e.g.
[
5
,
16
,
28
]
).

In this paper we fully solve both the question of torsion and the determination of relations in the equivariant cohomology ring of
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
. We show in particular that the above formula (
1.1
) does not hold integrally, although the equivariant cohomology ring embeds as a proper subring of the corresponding invariant ring.

1.1.
Our main theorem

Let
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
be variables of degree two and let
\Lambda_{n}=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]^{S_{n}}
denote the ring of symmetric polynomials in
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
. We identify
\Lambda_{n}
with the cohomology ring of
B\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
; under this identification the Chern classes correspond to the elementary symmetric polynomials. Let
V=(t_{j}^{i-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
be the Vandermonde matrix and
\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}-t_{i})
its determinant. Since
\Delta
is alternating, its square
\Delta^{2}
is a symmetric polynomial. Let
\mathrm{adj}(V)=\Delta V^{-1}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}])
be the adjugate of
V
. Let
s
be another variable. Define the diagonal matrix
D(s)=\mathrm{diag}(s/(1+t_{i}s))_{1\leq i\leq n}
, and the matrix

M(s)=\mathrm{adj}(V)^{T}D(s)\mathrm{adj}(V)\,;

this matrix is invariant under permutation of
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
. Thus, for any two
k
-element subsets
I,J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
the
(I,J)
-minor of
M(s)
defines a formal power series

\det(M(s)_{I,J})\in\Lambda_{n}[[s]]\,.

It turns out that
\det(M(s)_{I,J})
is divisible by
\Delta^{2k-2}
, and we define the symmetric polynomial
c_{I,J}^{l}\in\Lambda_{n}
as the coefficient of
s^{l}
in
\det(M(s)_{I,J})/\Delta^{2k-2}
.

Let
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
be variables of degree
|Z_{i}|=2i
, and let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
be variables of degree
|X_{i}|=|Y_{i}|=2i-1
. We consider the free graded commutative
\Lambda_{n}
-algebra

A:=\Lambda_{n}\otimes\mathbb{Z}[Z_{1}\dots,Z_{n}]\otimes{\textstyle\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})\,.

For a subset
I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}
we write
X_{I}
for the product
X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{k}}
in
A
, and similarly
Y_{I}
for the product
Y_{i_{1}}\cdots Y_{i_{k}}
.

For
1\leq l\leq n
let
R_{l}\in A
denote the element

(1.2)

\Delta^{2}Z_{l}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|I|=|J|=k\end{subarray}}c^{l}_{I,J}X_{I}Y_{J}\,,

which is homogeneous of degree
2(n(n-1)+l)
. The elements
R_{1},\dots,R_{n}
generate an ideal
(R_{1},\dots,R_{n})\subseteq A
. We consider its saturation with respect to
\Delta^{2}
; this is the ideal
\mathcal{J}\subseteq A
defined by
\mathcal{J}=\{a\in A\mid\exists m\geq 0:\Delta^{2m}a\in(R_{1},\dots,R_{n})\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
n\geq 0
there is an isomorphism of
\Lambda_{n}
-algebras

H^{\ast}_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}(C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}));\mathbb{Z})\cong{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{J}}$}}}\,,

where
\mathcal{J}
is the saturation of the ideal
(R_{1},\dots,R_{n})
with respect to
\Delta^{2}
.

Moreover, this algebra cannot be generated by fewer than
3n
elements.

Our proof will show that the generators are pulled back from the direct limit
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}))=\mathrm{colim}_{n}C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
and can be interpreted in terms of Chern classes (see Remark
6.7
). A few immediate consequences of Theorem
1.1
are:

(1)

After inverting
\Delta^{2}
, the equivariant cohomology of
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
is a free
\Lambda_{n}[\Delta^{-2}]
-module of total rank
2^{2n}
; in fact, isomorphic to

\Lambda_{n}[\Delta^{-2}]\otimes{\textstyle\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})\,.

(2)

The equivariant cohomology of
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
is
\Lambda_{n}
-torsionfree, in particular torsionfree as an abelian group. This follows from the previous item and the fact that the ideal
\mathcal{J}
is
\Delta^{2}
-saturated, so there is no
\Delta^{2}
-torsion.

(3)

Let
T
be a maximal torus of the unitary group
U(n)\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Then the
T
-equivariant cohomology of
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
is
H^{\ast}(BT;\mathbb{Z})
-torsionfree. This follows from the previous bullet point and the fact that
\Lambda_{n}
is a free
H^{\ast}(BT;\mathbb{Z})
-module.

The non-equivariant cohomology of
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
has intricate torsion, and the map from the equivariant to the non-equivariant cohomology is not surjective in general. This is reflected in the fact that the equivariant cohomology is not a free
\Lambda_{n}
-module with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients, although it is free with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients. A central motivation for this work is to develop an approach to understanding this torsion; for example, through the Eilenberg-Moore spectral sequence which requires knowledge of the equivariant cohomology as a
\Lambda_{n}
-module.

It is natural to ask to what extent our results generalise beyond
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. While we do not know whether the equivariant cohomology of
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},G)
is torsionfree for arbitrary
G
, the algebraic structure of the cohomology ring does generalise in the non-modular case. In this setting our results can be interpreted as an explicit presentation of the invariant algebra appearing on the right hand side of (
1.1
). The Vandermonde determinant
\Delta
identifies with the
T
-equivariant Euler class of
U(n)/T
; for other compact Lie groups
G
this role is played by the Euler class of
G/T
. The matrices encoding the relations are replaced by higher order Hessians of the basic invariants in
H^{\ast}(BT)^{W}
. These generalisations will be treated elsewhere.

1.2.
Outline of the paper

We briefly outline the proof strategy and the structure of the paper. Since
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
deformation retracts onto the space of commuting pairs in the unitary group
U(n)\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, we will compute the
U(n)
-equivariant cohomology ring of
C_{2}(U(n))=\{(A,B)\in U(n)^{2}\mid AB=BA\}
.

In Section
2
we set up a spectral sequence which starts from iterated Hochschild homology of a polynomial algebra and abuts on the equivariant homology of
C_{2}(U(n))
(Proposition
2.7
).

The spectral sequence is used to show that the inclusion of a maximal torus
T\hookrightarrow U(n)
embeds the equivariant cohomology of
C_{2}(U(n))
into the free graded commutative algebra
H^{\ast}(BT\times T^{2};\mathbb{Z})
(Corollary
2.12
).

In Section
3
the spectral sequence is used to show that the map into the direct limit
C_{2}(U(n))_{hU(n)}\to C_{2}(U)_{hU}
is surjective in cohomology. This gives us a convenient set of generators, since homotopically
C_{2}(U)_{hU}
is just the product
BU^{2}\times U^{2}
(Corollary
3.2
).

In Proposition
3.7
we prove that
3n
generators are enough. Their images under the embedding into a free graded commutative algebra are computed in (
3.7
) and (
3.8
). At this point we have a description of the equivariant cohomology ring as an explicit subalgebra of a free graded commutative algebra.

In Section
4
formal algebraic manipulations are made to identify the ideal of relations
\mathcal{J}
of Theorem
1.1
(Lemma
4.3
).

In Section
5
we finish the proof of the main theorem, by showing that the exhibited generating set is minimal. In Addendum
5.4
we show that after inverting
n
, the size of a minimal generating set is
3n-1
instead of
3n
.

In Section
6
we determine a presentation of the non-equivariant cohomology ring of
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
with coefficients in a field of characteristic zero or prime to
n!
(Theorem
6.3
). An algorithm which computes an additive basis of the ideal of relations is given in Corollary
6.5
.

In Section
7
we give a gauge theoretic interpretation of our result, by commenting on the surjectivity of the natural map, considered by Atiyah–Bott
[
2
]
, from the cohomology of the classifying space of the gauge group to the equivariant cohomology of
C_{2}(U(n))
.

There is an Appendix
A
which explains some details about the spectral sequence we use.

2.
A spectral sequence and the absence of torsion

2.1.
Preliminaries

Our aim is to compute the equivariant cohomolgy ring of
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
. A few intermediate results will be proved for the more general spaces
C_{m}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
, the space of
m
-tuples of pairwise commuting elements in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Clearly,
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
deformation retracts onto the unitary group
U(n)
, which is its maximal compact subgroup. What is far less evident – and a nontrivial theorem of Pettet–Souto
[
21
]
– is that the same is true for the commuting varieties:
C_{m}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
deformation retracts onto
C_{m}(U(n))
for all
m
. The spaces
C_{m}(U(n))
are more tractable than the general linear counterparts, which is why all of our computations will be done for
C_{m}(U(n))
.

For convenience, we recall the definition:

C_{m}(U(n))=\{(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\in U(n)^{m}\mid\forall i,j:A_{i}A_{j}=A_{j}A_{i}\}\,.

We let
U(m)
act on
C_{n}(U(m))
by simultaneous conjugation and denote by

C_{m}(U(n))_{hU(n)}=EU(n)\times_{U(n)}C_{m}(U(n))

the homotopy orbit space; by definition, its cohomology is the
U(n)
-equivariant cohomology of
C_{m}(U(n))
. We note that the homotopy equivalence between
C_{m}(U(n))
and
C_{m}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
carries over to the homotopy orbits, that is

C_{m}(U(n))_{hU(n)}\simeq C_{m}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))_{h\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}\,.

Therefore, the corresponding equivariant cohomology rings are isomorphic, and all of our computations apply equally to the unitary and the complex case. Our main results will be stated for
C_{m}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
, because this space is of more interest in algebraic geometry, but this is really a matter of taste.

An important perspective is to consider the spaces
C_{m}(U(n))
simultaneously for all
n
, as this reveals some additional structure which is otherwise hidden. We define

(2.1)

\mathscr{C}_{m}=\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}C_{m}(U(n))_{hU(n)}\,.

One can view
\mathscr{C}_{m}
as the classifying space of a topological symmetric monoidal category (namely, finite dimensional unitary representations of
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
under direct sum); it is therefore an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-space, that is, a coherently homotopy commutative
H
-space. We will use this structure in a couple of places without much further explanation; a good reference is
[
17
]
or
[
24
]
.

Another fact that we will use sometimes is that
C_{m}(U(n))
is path-connected for all
m,n\geq 0
. This follows because in the unitary groups any abelian subgroup is contained in a maximal torus.

Our first goal is to set up a spectral sequence which starts form iterated Hochschild homology of a polynomial algebra and converges to the homology of
\mathscr{C}_{m}
.

2.2.
Loday construction

We recall the Loday construction and higher Hochschild homology, see e.g.
[
22
, Section 1.7]
. Let
k
be a commutative ring and let
\mathrm{dgca}_{k}
be the category of differential graded commutative algebras over
k
. Let
\mathrm{Fin},\mathrm{Set}
and
\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast},\mathrm{Set}_{\ast}
be the categories of (finite) sets and (finite) pointed sets, respectively. The basepoint of a based set
S\in\mathrm{Set}_{\ast}
will be denoted by
\ast\in S
; the finite based set
\{\ast,1,\dots,n\}
will be denoted by
\langle n\rangle
. The one-point set (or space) will also be denoted by
\ast
.

Construction 2.1

(e.g.
[
22
]
)
.

Fix
A\in\mathrm{dgca}_{k}
. Since
\mathrm{dgca}_{k}
has all colimits and the category
\mathrm{Set}
is generated under colimits by
\ast
, we can make the following construction:

(1)

We let
\mathcal{L}(A)\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Set}\to\mathrm{dgca}_{k}
be the unique (up to natural isomorphism) colimit preserving functor satisfying
\mathcal{L}(A)(\ast)=A
.

(2)

If
S\in\mathrm{Set}_{\ast}
, then
\mathcal{L}(A)(S)
is canonically a dgca under
A
(i.e., equipped with a canonical map
A\to\mathcal{L}(A)(S)
). Suppose that
A
is augmented over
k
. Then we define
\mathcal{L}(A;k)\colon\thinspace\mathrm{Set}_{\ast}\to\mathrm{dgca}_{k}
by
\mathcal{L}(A;k)(S)=k\otimes_{A}\mathcal{L}(A)(S)
.

Concretely, for
\{1,\dots,n\}\in\mathrm{Fin}

\mathcal{L}(A)(\{1,\dots,n\})\cong A^{\otimes n}

is the choice of an
n
-fold tensor product (over
k
) of
A
with itself. For
S\in\mathrm{Set}

\mathcal{L}(A)(S)\cong\mathrm{colim}_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\subseteq S\\
\textnormal{finite}\end{subarray}}\,\mathcal{L}(A)(U)\,,

where the colimit runs over all finite subsets of
S
. If
S
is a pointed set and
A
is augmented over
k
, then
\mathcal{L}(A;k)(S)
is the pushout in
\mathrm{dgca}_{k}
of the diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{L}(A)(S)}
\textstyle{k}

It is again augmented over
k
, since
\mathcal{L}(A)(S)
is always augmented over
A
(via the unique map
S\to\ast
).

Let
\mathrm{sSet}
(respectively,
\mathrm{sSet}_{\ast}
) denote the category of (based) simplicial sets. For
X\in\mathrm{sSet}
, evaluating
\mathcal{L}(A)
on

levelwise gives a simplicial dgca

\mathcal{L}(A)(X):=\{[q]\mapsto\mathcal{L}(A)(X_{q})\}\,.

This simplicial dgca can be viewed as a bidifferential bigraded
k

(\mathcal{L}(A)(X),d_{h},d_{v})

with a “horizontal” differential
d_{h}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{L}(A)(X_{q})_{p}\to\mathcal{L}(A)(X_{q})_{p-1}
coming from

, and a “vertical” differential
d_{v}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{L}(A)(X_{q})_{p}\to\mathcal{L}(A)(X_{q-1})_{p}
given as usual by the alternating sum of the face maps of the simplicial dgca. We denote the total complex of this bicomplex by

|\mathcal{L}(A)(X)|=\mathrm{Tot}^{\oplus}(\mathcal{L}(A)(X))\,.

For
X\in\mathrm{sSet}_{\ast}
,
|\mathcal{L}(A;k)(X)|
is defined analogously.

Example 2.2
.

Let
A
be a graded commutative
k
-algebra (with trivial differential). Let
S^{1}
be the standard simplicial model of the circle with only two non-degenerate simplices in total. Then
|\mathcal{L}(A)(S^{1})|
is the usual Hochschild complex
[
18
, § 1.1.1]
. In (simplicial) degree
n
it is the tensor product
A^{\otimes(n+1)}
and the differential

is given by

\begin{split}d(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})&=(-1)^{n+(|a_{0}|+\cdots+|a_{n-1}|)|a_{n}|)}a_{n}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-1}\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\,.\end{split}

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{sSet}
and
A
a dgca over a commutative ring
k
.

(1)

The (underived)

A
over
X
is defined by

\mathrm{HH}^{X}_{\ast}(A)=H_{\ast}(|\mathcal{L}(A)(X)|)\,.

(2)

If
X
is based and
A
is augmented over
k
, then the (underived) Hochschild homology
with coefficients in the
A
-module

is defined by

\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{X}(A;k)=H_{\ast}(|\mathcal{L}(A;k)(X)|)\,.

Let
X\in\mathrm{sSet}
and denote by

the simplicial set

with a disjoint basepoint added. Then, if

is augmented over
k
, there is a natural isomorphism

(2.2)

\mathcal{L}(A;k)(X_{+})\cong\mathcal{L}(A)(X)\,;

this follows from the definition of
\mathcal{L}(A;k)(X_{+})
and the isomorphism

\mathcal{L}(A)(X_{+})\cong A\otimes\mathcal{L}(A)(X)

under

(recall that

preserves colimits and

).

Both

are again dgcas over

(see

in the classical context; Definition

for the augmented case; the shuffle product for

is defined analogously). Thus, the shuffle product gives

(and

Example 2.4
.

Let
k
be a commutative ring. For simplicity, assume that
2\neq 0
in
k
, so that, if
y
is an odd degree variable, the exterior algebra
{\textstyle\bigwedge_{k}(y)=k[y]/(y^{2})}
is a free graded commutative algebra. Let
x
be a generator of even degree and
\sigma x
a generator of degree
|\sigma x|=|x|+1
. Define a map of graded commutative
k
-algebras

(2.3)

k[x]\otimes{\textstyle\bigwedge_{k}}(\sigma x)\to|\mathcal{L}(k[x])(S^{1})|

by sending

(in simplicial degree

) and

(in simplicial degree

Example 2.5
.

Now suppose that
x
is a generator of odd degree, and
\sigma x
a generator of degree

. Then there is an isomorphism of graded algebras

\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{S^{1}}({\textstyle\bigwedge_{k}}(x))\cong{\textstyle\bigwedge_{k}}(x)\otimes\Gamma_{k}[\sigma x]\,,

and
\gamma_{m}(x)\mapsto 1\otimes x^{\otimes m}\in A\otimes\bar{A}^{\otimes m}
is an isomorphism. By inspection, it is multiplicative with respect to the shuffle product on the normalised Hochschild complex. In fact, since the shuffle product descends to the normalised Hochschild chains, the computation gives a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of dgca’s between
|\mathcal{L}(\bigwedge_{k}(x))(S^{1})|
, the normalised Hochschild complex and
\bigwedge_{k}(x)\otimes\Gamma_{k}[\sigma x]
(cf.
[
4
, Proposition 2.3]
).

In the following, we let
S^{1}
denote the standard simplicial circle and
(S^{1})^{m}
the product simplicial set. Under suitable flatness hypotheses on
A
, the Loday construction
\mathcal{L}(A)(X)
depends, up to quasi-isomorphism, only on the weak homotopy type of
X
; we will not have to appeal to this fact.

We shall use, however, the fact that a quasi-isomorphism
A\to B
of bounded below, degreewise flat dgca’s induces a quasi-isomorphism
\mathcal{L}(A)(X)\to\mathcal{L}(B)(X)
. This follows, because tensoring of flat dgca’s preserves quasi-isomorphisms, and a levelwise quasi-isomorphism of simplicial dgca’s induces a quasi-isomorphism on total complexes (by arguing with the standard spectral sequence for the homology of a total complex; we use boundedness for the convergence of the spectral sequence).

We will also use the natural isomorphism

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}(A\otimes B)(X)\cong\mathcal{L}(A)(X)\otimes\mathcal{L}(B)(X)

for any two dgca’s
A
and
B
and any simplicial set
X
; to see this it suffices to check it on finite pointed sets, where it follows directly from the construction.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
x
be a variable of even degree. Then, there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{2}}(\mathbb{Z}[x])\cong\mathbb{Z}[x]\otimes{\textstyle\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\sigma x,\tau x)\otimes\Gamma_{\mathbb{Z}}[\rho x]\,,

where the degrees are
|\sigma x|=|\tau x|=|x|+1
and
|\rho x|=|x|+2
.

Proof.

The Eilenberg-Zilber theorem gives a quasi-isomophism of dgca’s

|\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}[x])((S^{1})^{2})|\simeq\mathcal{L}(|\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}[x])(S^{1})|)(S^{1})

(see, for example,
[
6
, Corollary 2.4.4]
). By Example
2.4
and using the fact that a quasi-isomorphism of flat dgcas induces a quasi-isomorphism of Loday constructions, we get a quasi-isomorphism
\mathcal{L}(|\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}[x])(S^{1})|)(S^{1})\simeq|\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}[x]\otimes\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}(\sigma x))(S^{1})|
. Now the claim follows from the isomorphism (
2.4
), the Künneth isomorphism and the computation of Example
2.5
.
∎

2.3.
The spectral sequence

Let
\mathscr{C}_{m}
be as defined in (
2.1
).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
x_{0},x_{1},\dots
be variables of degree
|x_{i}|=2i
. For every
m\geq 0
and any commutative ring
k
, there is a first quadrant spectral sequence of algebras

E^{2}=\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{m}}(k[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots])\Longrightarrow H_{\ast}(\mathscr{C}_{m};k)\,.

If
k
is a field of characteristic zero, the spectral sequence degenerates at the
E^{2}
-page.

The bigrading of the
E^{2}
-page is as follows: the vertical degree is the “internal” degree coming from the grading of the variables
x_{i}
, and the horizontal degree, let’s say
p\geq 0
, is the “simplicial” degree indexing the Hochschild homology groups
\mathrm{HH}_{p}^{(S^{1})^{m}}
.

Proof.

The spectral sequence is a special case of the spectral sequence we obtained in
[
10
, Proposition 5.5]
. But we supply more details: Let
ku
be the
\Gamma
-space for complex K-theory (see Example
A.3
). First, one shows that
\mathscr{C}_{m}
is homotopy equivalent to
ku((S^{1})^{m}_{+})
, where
(S^{1})^{m}_{+}=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{m},S^{1})_{+}
is the character group of
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
with a disjoint basepoint added. This is a special case of
[
10
, Proposition 3.6]
, and an almost identical statement is in
[
9
, Proposition 2.4]
.

The underlying space of
ku
is
\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}BU(n)
, whose homology is the polynomial algebra
k[x_{0},x_{1},\dots]
on the duals of the powers of the first Chern class. It follows that
H_{\ast}(ku(\langle 1\rangle);k)
is
k
-flat. Since
ku
is special and cofibrant, Proposition
A.7
gives a spectral sequence of algebras

E^{2}=\mathrm{HH}^{(S^{1})^{m}_{+}}(k[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots];k)\Longrightarrow H_{\ast}(\mathscr{C}_{m};k)\,.

This becomes the claimed spectral sequence by using the isomorphism (
2.2
).

Now suppose that
k
is a field of characteristic zero. One shows that the spectral sequence degenerates by showing that the
E^{2}
-page is isomorphic to the abutment: The
E^{2}
-page can be computed iteratively, just like in Corollary
2.6
, by using the computations of Examples
2.4
and
2.5
, the fact that over a field of characterstic zero a divided polynomial algebra is isomorphic to a polynomial algebra, and the fact that a free graded commutative algebra is intrinsically formal. For example,

\begin{split}\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{m}}(k[x_{0}])&\cong H_{\ast}(\mathcal{L}(|\mathcal{L}(k[x_{0}])(S^{1})|)((S^{1})^{m-1}))\\
&\cong H_{\ast}(\mathcal{L}(k[x_{0}]\otimes{\textstyle\bigwedge_{k}(\sigma x_{0})})((S^{1})^{m-1}))\\
&\cong\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{m-1}}(k[x_{0}])\otimes\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{m-1}}({\textstyle\bigwedge_{k}}(\sigma x_{0}))\,,\end{split}

and further,

\begin{split}\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{m-1}}({\textstyle\bigwedge_{k}}(\sigma x_{0}))\cong\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{m-2}}({\textstyle\bigwedge_{k}}(\sigma x_{0}))\otimes\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{m-2}}(\Gamma_{k}[\sigma^{2}x_{0}])\,.\end{split}

Now use
\Gamma_{k}[\sigma^{2}x_{0}]\cong k[\sigma^{2}x_{0}]
, and continue by induction. Let
\mathscr{S}_{\ast}
be a graded set with
\binom{m}{l}
elements in degree
l
. Then
\mathrm{HH}_{\ast}^{(S^{1})^{m}}(k[x_{0}])
is a free graded commutative
k
-algebra generated by
\mathscr{S}_{\ast}
. Accordingly, repeating this argument with the variables
x_{i}
of degree
2i
, the
E^{2}
-page is a free graded commutative
k
-algebra generated by the graded set
\bigsqcup_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i}\mathscr{S}_{\ast}
, where
\Sigma^{2i}
is a shift in grading by
2i
.

On the other hand, as explained in
[
10
, Section 6.2]
, if
k
is a characteristic zero field, the singular
k
-chains
C_{\ast}(\mathscr{C}_{m};k)
are the free
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-
k
-algebra on the singular
k
-chains
C_{\ast}(BS^{1}\times(S^{1})^{m};k)
. The homology of this free
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-
k
-algebra is the free graded commutative algebra on the graded
k
-module
H_{\ast}(BS^{1}\times(S^{1})^{m};k)
. Since the latter is isomorphic to the free
k
-module generated by
\bigsqcup_{i\geq 0}\Sigma^{2i}\mathscr{S}_{\ast}
, we conclude that the
E^{2}
-page and the abutment are isomorphic.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

For all
n\geq 0
, the
U(n)
-equivariant homology of
C_{2}(U(n))
with integer coefficients is torsion free.

Proof.

Consider the spectral sequence of Proposition
2.7
for
m=2
and
k=\mathbb{Z}
,

E^{2}=\mathrm{HH}^{(S^{1})^{2}}_{\ast}(\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots])\,\Longrightarrow\,H_{\ast}(\mathscr{C}_{2};\mathbb{Z})\,.

By Corollary
2.6
, the
E^{2}
-page is torsion free. Therefore, any non-zero differential would give a non-zero differential after tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
. This means that the spectral sequence degenerates at the
E^{2}
-page, because this is what happens over
\mathbb{Q}
as we noted in Proposition
2.7
. It follows that
H_{\ast}(\mathscr{C}_{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong E^{2}
, which is therefore torsion free.
∎

Remark 2.9

When

Under the assumption that

(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}\colon\thinspace H^{\ast}(\mathscr{C}_{2,1}^{gp})\to H^{\ast}(BS^{1}\times(S^{1})^{2})
sends
\alpha_{n}\mapsto t^{n-1}u
(by symmetry,
\beta_{n}\mapsto t^{n-1}v
) and
d_{n}\mapsto(-1)^{n-1}t^{n-1}uv
.

Proof.

The class
(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}(\alpha_{n})
is completely determined by the Kronecker pairings

\lambda_{a}=\langle(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}(\alpha_{n}),\gamma_{n-1}(x)a\rangle\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\lambda_{b}=\langle(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}(\alpha_{n}),\gamma_{n-1}(x)b\rangle\,.

To compute
\lambda_{a}
we note that
(f_{1,\infty})_{\ast}(\gamma_{n-1}(x)a)=x_{n-1}\circ(x_{0}a_{1})
, which by the distributivity law in Hopf rings is
\sum_{i}x_{i}a_{n-i}
. Since
\alpha_{n}
is dual to
x_{0}a_{n}
, it follows that
\lambda_{a}=1
. In the same way we see that
\lambda_{b}=0
, hence
(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}(\alpha_{n})
is the class dual to
\gamma_{n-1}(x)a
, which is
t^{n-1}u
.

To compute
(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}(d_{n})
we proceed in the same way. This class is determined by the pairings

\lambda_{\emptyset}=\langle(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}(d_{n}),\gamma_{n}(x)\rangle\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\lambda_{ab}=\langle(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}(d_{n}),\gamma_{n-1}(x)ab\rangle\,.

Since
(f_{1,\infty})_{\ast}(\gamma_{n}(x))=x_{n}
, which is a homology class of the first
BU
-factor, while
d_{n}
is a Chern class of the second
BU
-factor, we find that
\lambda_{\emptyset}=0
. On the other hand, by Lemma
3.4
we have

(f_{1,\infty})_{\ast}(\gamma_{n-1}(x)ab)=x_{n-1}\circ(x_{0}q_{1}+x_{0}a_{1}b_{1})=x_{0}q_{n}+f\,,

where

is a linear combination of terms
x_{i}q_{n-i}
with

and terms independent of
q_{i}
for any
i
, on all of which
d_{n}
evaluates to zero. Hence,
\lambda_{ab}=\langle d_{n},x_{0}q_{n}\rangle
. Upon changing path components in
\mathbb{Z}\times BU\times U^{2}\times BU
, this is the value of the
n
-th Chern class
d_{n}\in H^{2n}(BU)
on
q_{n}\in H_{2n}(BU)
. The Chern character shows that the
n
-th Chern number of the
n
-th power of the Bott element is
(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!
. Since
h(\beta^{n-1}\mu\nu)=x_{1}^{\circ(n-1)}\circ q_{1}=(n-1)!q_{n}
,
and
\beta^{n-1}\mu\nu
is the

-th power of the Bott element in

, we obtain that
\lambda_{ab}=\langle d_{n},x_{0}q_{n}\rangle=(-1)^{n-1}
. It follows that
(f_{1,\infty})^{\ast}(d_{n})
is the class dual to
(-1)^{n-1}\gamma_{n-1}(x)ab
, which is
(-1)^{n-1}t^{n-1}uv
.
∎

We finally compute the map
(f_{n,\infty})^{\ast}\colon\thinspace H^{\ast}(\mathscr{C}_{2,n}^{gp})\to H^{\ast}(BT\times T^{2})
. We identify the cohomology rings
H^{\ast}(\mathscr{C}_{2,n}^{gp})
for all

with that in (

) (through the equivalences induced by multiplication by

). Write

with

. The

To express this coproduct it is convenient to introduce the formal power series

Lemma 3.6
.

The map

(f_{n,\infty})^{\ast}(\alpha(s))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}}{1-t_{i}s},\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad(f_{n,\infty})^{\ast}(d(s))=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{su_{i}v_{i}}{1+t_{i}s}\right)\,,

(by symmetry,

Proof.

By (

), the map
(f_{n,\infty})^{\ast}
is the composition of
\psi^{(n-1)}
with
(f_{1,\infty}^{\ast})^{\otimes n}
. If we apply

in the

, but with variables

and similarly for

∎

It remains to compute the image of the Chern classes

. Let
\pi\colon\thinspace\mathscr{C}_{2}^{gp}\to\mathbb{Z}\times BU

\textstyle{BT\times T^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{n,\infty}}

is the

For

, we let

and

be the coefficient of

Proposition 3.7
.

The image of the embedding

of Corollary

is generated as a
\Lambda_{n}
-algebra by
X_{l},Y_{l}
and
Z_{l}
for

.

Proof.

It has already been established that the image of

\Lambda_{n}
-algebra by
X_{l},Y_{l}
and
Z_{l}
for

. It remains to show that it suffices to take

. To this end, consider the identity

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}e_{k}s^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{l\geq 1}X_{l}s^{l-1}\right)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i}s)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}}{1-t_{i}s}\right)\,.

The right hand side is a polynomial in

. Hence, on the left hand side the coefficient of

for each

The right hand side is a polynomial in

. On the left hand side this implies the recurrence
\sum_{k=0}^{n}e_{k}Z_{l-k}
for all

.
∎

Expanding the power series of Lemma

The formula for the

Definition 3.8
.

For integers

the

of degree

in the variables

h_{q}(t_{k_{1}},\dots,t_{k_{j}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{j})\in\mathbb{N}^{j},\\

Using the fact that

for all

Z_{l}=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(l,n)}(-1)^{l-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\

where

.

4.
Determination of the ideal of relations

We shall now derive the relations amongst the generators
X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
and
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
from Section
3
. Let

B=H^{\ast}(BT\times T^{2})=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]\otimes{\textstyle\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\,.

Recall from (
3.7
) that
X_{l}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{l-1}u_{i}
and
Y_{l}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{l-1}v_{i}
. For
1\leq l\leq n
this can be written succinctly, using the Vandermonde matrix
V=(t_{j}^{i-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
, as

(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{T}=V(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{T}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})^{T}=V(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})^{T}\,.

If
\Delta
is the determinant of
V
and
\mathrm{adj}(V)=\Delta V^{-1}
the adjugate of
V
, then we can rewrite the first identity as
\Delta(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{T}=\mathrm{adj}(V)(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{T}
, and the second identity accordingly.

For an
n\times n
matrix
E
and
k
-element subsets
I,J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
we denote by
E_{I,J}
the
k\times k
submatrix of
E
consisting of the rows and columns of
E
whose indices are in
I
and
J
, respectively.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
be a
k
-element subset. In
B
we have that

\Delta^{2k}\prod_{j\in K}u_{j}v_{j}=(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|I|=|J|=k\end{subarray}}\det(\mathrm{adj}(V)_{I,K}^{T}\mathrm{adj}(V)_{K,J})X_{I}Y_{J}\,.

Moreover, the coefficient of
X_{I}Y_{J}
on the right hand side is divisible by
\Delta^{2k-2}
.

Proof.

For ease of notation, we write
A=\mathrm{adj}(V)
. Now we get

\begin{split}\Delta^{2k}\prod_{j\in K}u_{j}v_{j}&=\prod_{j\in K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{j,i}X_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{j,i}Y_{i}\right)\\
&=(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\prod_{j\in K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{j,i}X_{i}\right)\prod_{j\in K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{j,i}Y_{i}\right)\,,\end{split}

but notice that
\prod_{j\in K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{j,i}X_{i}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\det(A_{K,I})X_{I}
, and similarly for the second product. This leads to the claimed formula.

Divisibility by
\Delta^{2k-2}
follows from Jacobi’s lemma on complementary minors (see e.g.
[
1
, §42]
) which says, in particular, that a
k\times k
minor of the adjugate of a matrix
E
is divisible by
\det(E)^{k-1}
.
∎

Define
D(s)=\mathrm{diag}(s/(1+t_{1}s),\dots,s/(1+t_{n}s))
and
M(s)=\mathrm{adj}(V)^{T}D(s)\mathrm{adj}(V)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

In
B
the following identity holds:

\Delta^{2}\sum_{l\geq 0}Z_{l}s^{l}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|I|=|J|=k\end{subarray}}\det(M(s)_{I,J})/\Delta^{2k-2}X_{I}Y_{J}\,.

Proof.

Let us write
A=\mathrm{adj}(V)
. By Lemma
3.6
we have

\begin{split}\Delta^{2k}\sum_{l\geq 0}Z_{l}s^{l}&=\Delta^{2k}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{su_{i}v_{i}}{1+t_{i}s}\right)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|K|=k\end{subarray}}\left(\Delta^{2k}\prod_{j\in K}u_{j}v_{j}\right)\underbrace{\prod_{j\in K}\left(\frac{s}{1+t_{j}s}\right)}_{=\det(D(s)_{K,K})}\,,\end{split}

and using Lemma
4.1
this becomes

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|I|=|J|=k\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|K|=k\end{subarray}}\det(A_{I,K}^{T}D(s)_{K,K}A_{K,J})\right)}_{=\det(M(s)_{I,J})}X_{I}Y_{J}\,.

Let us explain why the term in parenthesis is equal to
\det(M(s)_{I,J})
. It follows from the Cauchy-Binet formula that if
E
and
F
are
n\times n
matrices and
I,J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
are
k
-element subsets, then
\det((EF)_{I,J})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\\
|K|=k\end{subarray}}\det(E_{I,K}F_{K,J})
. Since
D(s)
is diagonal, this implies that

\det(D(s)_{K,K}A_{K,J})=\det((D(s)A)_{K,J})\,.

Applied once more to
E=A^{T}
and
F=D(s)A
proves the claim.

Finally, dividing by
\Delta^{2k-2}
yields the assertion of the lemma.
∎

The relations
R_{1},\dots,R_{n}
from the introduction (
1.2
) now follow by comparing coefficients of
s^{l}
for
1\leq l\leq n
in the identity of Lemma
4.2
.

Let us now treat
X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n},Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
as formal variables of degree
|X_{i}|=|Y_{i}|=2i-1
and
|Z_{i}|=2i
. Let

A:=\Lambda_{n}\otimes\mathbb{Z}[Z_{1}\dots,Z_{n}]\otimes{\textstyle\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})\,.

and let
\rho\colon\thinspace A\to B
be the map of
\Lambda_{n}
-algebras which substitutes for the generators the explicit formulae of (
3.7
) and (
3.8
).

Lemma 4.3
.

The kernel of
\rho
is the
\Delta^{2}
-saturated ideal
\mathcal{J}
of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\textstyle{A[\Delta^{-2}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A[\Delta^{-2}]/(R_{1},\dots,R_{n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The two horizontal arrows on the left are the canonical maps into the localisation, both of which are injective. Over the ring

∎

.

5.
Minimality of the generating set

We prove here the second statement of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{G}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}\sqcup\{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{l}\}
be a finite homogeneous generating set of
A/\mathcal{J}
as a
\Lambda_{n}
-algebra, where the
P_{i}
have odd degree and the
Q_{i}
have even degree. Then
k\geq 2n
and
l\geq n
.

Proof.

We think of
A/\mathcal{J}
as a
\Lambda_{n}
-subalgebra of
B
, more precisely of the invariants
B^{S_{n}}
(notation as in Section
4
). We will first show that
k\geq 2n
. Since we may always modify the elements of
\mathscr{G}
by adding elements of
\Lambda_{n}
, we may assume without loss of generality that

\mathscr{G}\subseteq(u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\subseteq B\,.

Let
\pi\colon\thinspace B\to B/(u_{i}u_{j},u_{i}v_{j},v_{i}v_{j}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n)
be the projection onto the free
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
-module generated by
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
. We have that
\pi(Q_{j})=0
for all
1\leq j\leq l
, since the
Q_{j}
, having even degree, must be at least quadratic in the variables
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
. We also have that
\pi(X_{i})=X_{i}
and
\pi(Y_{i})=Y_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Since
\mathscr{G}
generates
A/\mathcal{J}
, it follows that the
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
lie in the
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
-linear span of
\{\pi(P_{1}),\dots,\pi(P_{k})\}
. Now forget the grading and consider base change to the field of rational functions
\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
. Since the Vandermonde matrix becomes invertible, the
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
and
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
lie in the
\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
-linear span of
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
and
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\}
. This implies that the
\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
-linear span of
\{\pi(P_{1}),\dots,\pi(P_{k})\}
contains the
\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
-linear span of
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
. For dimension reasons it follows that
k\geq 2n
.

Next we show that
l\geq n
. Consider the ring homomorphism

\tau\colon\thinspace B\to\bar{B}:={\textstyle\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v_{1},\dots,v_{n})

defined by sending
t_{i}\mapsto 0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\bar{A}:=\tau(A/\mathcal{J})
and write
\bar{a}:=\tau(a)
whenever
a\in A/\mathcal{J}
. Then
\bar{X}_{1}=\sum_{i}u_{i}
,
\bar{Y}_{1}=\sum_{i}v_{i}
, and
\bar{X}_{i}=\bar{Y}_{i}=0
for all
2\leq i\leq n
.
Furthermore, (
3.8
) implies
\bar{Z}_{i}=e_{i}(u_{1}v_{1},\dots,u_{n}v_{n})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, where
e_{i}
is the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial. In
\bar{A}\subseteq\bar{B}
the following divided power relations hold:

\bar{Z}_{i}\bar{Z}_{j}=\begin{cases}\binom{i+j}{i}\bar{Z}_{i+j}&\textnormal{if }i+j\leq n,\\
0&\textnormal{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
A^{\prime}\subseteq\bar{A}
be the subring generated by
\bar{Z}_{1},\dots,\bar{Z}_{n}
. Direct calculation shows that
\bar{X}_{1}\bar{Y}_{1}\bar{Z}_{i}
cannot be a non-zero multiple of
\bar{Z}_{i+1}
; this implies a splitting of graded abelian groups
\bar{A}\cong A^{\prime}\oplus(\bar{X}_{1},\bar{Y}_{1})A^{\prime}
, and thus an isomorphism

\bar{A}/(\bar{X}_{1},\bar{Y}_{1})\cong A^{\prime}=\Gamma_{\mathbb{Z}}[\bar{Z}_{1}]/(\gamma_{n+1}(\bar{Z}_{1}),\gamma_{n+2}(\bar{Z}_{1}),\dots)\,.

Over
\mathbb{Z}
this algebra is generated by no fewer than
n
elements. The
P_{i}\in\mathscr{G}
, having odd degree, are mapped to zero in
A^{\prime}
. Thus,
A^{\prime}
is generated by
\{\bar{Q}_{1},\dots,\bar{Q}_{l}\}
, which implies that
l\geq n
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is now finished.

As an addendum we show that if
n
is inverted in the coefficient ring, then the size of a minimal generating set can be reduced by one. Let
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots
be variables. Set
x_{0}:=1
and define the formal power series
f(s)=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}x_{k}s^{k}
. We define the
k
-th Newton polynomial
s_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
by the identity

\sum_{k\geq 1}s_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})s^{k-1}=-\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{f(s)}\,.

For example,
s_{1}(x_{1})=x_{1}
,
s_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}^{2}-2x_{2}
,
s_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x_{1}^{3}-3x_{1}x_{2}+3x_{3}
and so on. Continue to denote
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]\otimes\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\otimes\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
by
B
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
k\geq 1
, the following identity holds in
B
:

s_{k}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})=k\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{k-1}u_{i}v_{i}\,.

Proof.

Define the formal power series
Z(s)=\sum_{k\geq 0}Z_{k}s^{k}
, where
Z_{0}:=1
. Then

-\log(Z(-s))^{\prime}=\sum_{k\geq 1}s_{k}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})s^{k-1}\,.

By Lemma
3.6
we have that
Z(-s)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{su_{i}v_{i}}{1-t_{i}s}\right)
, and so we get

-\log(Z(-s))^{\prime}=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{su_{i}v_{i}}{1-t_{i}s}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}v_{i}}{(1-t_{i}s)^{2}}\,.

Now expand it as a power series in
s
. Then the coefficient of
s^{k-1}
is precisely
k\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{k-1}u_{i}v_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

In
A/\mathcal{J}
the following relation holds:

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{i+1}e_{i}\left(s_{n-i}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n-i})-\sum_{j=0}^{n-i-1}X_{n-i-j}Y_{j+1}\right)=0\,.

Proof.

Consider the generating functions

X(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}}{1-t_{i}s},\quad Y(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{v_{i}}{1-t_{i}v_{i}},\quad S(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}v_{i}}{(1-t_{i}s)^{2}}\,.

Then

X(s)Y(s)-S(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{u_{i}v_{j}}{(1-t_{i}s)(1-t_{j}s)}\,,

and thus, letting
E(s)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t_{i}s)
be the generating function for the elementary symmetric polynomials, the expression
E(s)(X(s)Y(s)-S(s))
is a polynomial in
s
of degree at most
n-2
. Then the coefficient of
s^{l}
for all
l\geq n-1
must vanish. But plugging in
X(s)=\sum_{l\geq 1}X_{l}s^{l-1}
,
Y(s)=\sum_{l\geq 1}Y_{l}s^{l-1}
and
S(s)=\sum_{l\geq 1}s_{l}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{l})s^{l-1}
(see Lemma
3.6
and the proof of Lemma
5.2
), the coefficient of
s^{n-1}
is precisely the expression which the lemma claims to vanish.
∎

Addendum 5.4
.

For every
n\geq 1
,
H^{\ast}_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}(C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}));\mathbb{Z}[1/n])
is generated as a
\Lambda_{n}\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/n]
-algebra by
3n-1
elements. It cannot be generated by fewer elements.

Proof.

In the identity of Lemma
5.3
,
Z_{n}
appears only once, namely in
s_{n}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n})
with coefficient
n
. The relation shows that if
n
is inverted, then the generator
Z_{n}
can be omitted. In
[
16
, Theorem F]
Kishimoto–Takeda showed that any generating set for the rational cohomology ring of
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
has to have at least
3n-1
elements. By Proposition
6.2
below, the natural map from the rational equivariant to the rational non-equivariant cohomology of
C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
is surjective. This implies that a generating set for the equivariant cohomology as a
\Lambda_{n}\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/n]
-algebra must have at least
3n-1
elements as well.
∎

6.
Inverting
n!

The goal of this section is to determine a presentation of the non-equivariant cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(C_{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}));k)
, where
k
is a field of characteristic zero or prime to
n!
. If the integers
1,\dots,n
, equivalently
n!
, are inverted, we may define elements

W_{l}=s_{l}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{l})/l

in
A/\mathcal{J}\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/n!]
. These can be taken as generators instead of the
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
, because
s_{l}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{l})
always has leading term
(-1)^{l+1}lZ_{l}
. In this basis the ideal of relations simplifies considerably: Write
X^{T}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
etc., and let
A_{l}
denote the symmetric matrix

A_{l}=\mathrm{adj}(V)^{T}\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})^{l-1}\mathrm{adj}(V)\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[e_{1},\dots,e_{n}])\,.

Let
\mathcal{J}\subseteq A
be as in Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 6.1
.

After inverting
n!
, the ideal
\mathcal{J}\subseteq A
is the
\Delta^{2}
-saturation of the ideal generated by the elements
R_{l}^{\prime}:=\Delta^{2}W_{l}-X^{T}A_{l}Y
for
1\leq l\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{T}
and
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})^{T}
. In the ring
B
we have that
W_{l}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}^{l-1}u_{k}v_{k}
by Lemma
5.2
. This implies that

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}W_{l}&=(\Delta u)^{T}\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})^{l-1}(\Delta v)\\
&=(\mathrm{adj}(V)X)^{T}\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})^{l-1}(\mathrm{adj}(V)Y)\\
&=X^{T}A_{l}Y\,.\end{split}

The rest of the proof is the same as in Lemma
4.3
.
∎

After inverting
n!
, Lemma
5.3
allows us to eliminate the generator
W_{n}
. As the proof of Lemma
4.3
shows, the relation
R_{n}^{\prime}
of Proposition
6.1
must then be a consequence of
R_{1}^{\prime},\dots,R_{n-1}^{\prime}
.

We also need the following equivariant formality result:

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero or prime to
n!
. Then, for all
m\geq 0
,
H^{\ast}_{U(n)}(C_{m}(U(n));k)
is a free
H^{\ast}(BU(n);k)
-module.

This follows as an application of
[
3
, Proposition A.3]
(which uses the Borel localisation theorem), but we give here a more direct proof. Our proof (more precisely, line (
6.1
)) could be used to give a formula for the Poincaré series of
C_{m}(U(n))
; but because such formulae are well-known (see e.g.
[
23
]
), we will not do this here.

Before we embark on the proof we set some notation. View
C:=H_{\ast}(BS^{1};k)
as a coalgebra via the diagonal map
BS^{1}\to BS^{1}\times BS^{1}
. The graded dual
C^{\vee}
is the polynomial algebra
k[t]
in a single variable of degree
2
. For
a\geq 0
, let

S^{a}(C)=(C^{\otimes a})_{S_{a}}

be the
a
-th symmetric power of
C
with the coalgebra structure induced from
C
. Its graded dual is the algebra of symmetric polynomials
S^{a}(C)^{\vee}=k[t_{1},\dots,t_{a}]^{S_{a}}
.

For
a\geq 0
, we let
{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{a}(C)
denote the
a
-th exterior power of
C
. Its graded dual is the subspace
{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{a}(C)^{\vee}\subseteq k[t_{1},\dots,t_{a}]
of alternating polynomials. This is naturally a
S^{a}(C)^{\vee}
-module, which shows that
{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{a}(C)
is naturally a
S^{a}(C)
-comodule.

•

For any

, we can view
S^{a}(C)\otimes S^{b}(C)
as a
S^{a+b}(C)
-comodule via the quotient map
S^{a}(C)\otimes S^{b}(C)\to S^{a+b}(C)
. This is a free
S^{a+b}(C)
-comodule; indeed, the dual statement says that
k[t_{1},\dots,t_{a+b}]^{S_{a}\times S_{b}}
is a free

-module, which is a classical fact from invariant theory.

•

The
S^{a}(C)
-comodule

is also free: The dual statement says that the subring of

of alternating polynomials is a free module over the ring of symmetric polynomials; this is indeed so, the free generator being the Vandermonde polynomial.

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

We find it easier to formulate the proof of the dual – and equivalent – statement that
H_{\ast}^{U(n)}(C_{m}(U(n));k)
is a free
H_{\ast}(BU(n);k)
-comodule. Choose a maximal torus
T\subseteq U(n)
and identify
H_{\ast}(BU(n);k)
with
H_{\ast}(BT;k)_{S_{n}}
. The inclusion
T\subseteq U(n)
induces an isomorphism of

-comodules

H_{\ast}^{U(n)}(C_{m}(U(n));k)\cong H_{\ast}(BT\times T^{m};k)_{S_{n}}\,.

For

a self-contained proof was given in Lemma
2.10
. If
\mathrm{char}(k)
is prime to
n!
, then the same conclusion holds by
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
, taking equivariant cohomology and arguing using the Serre spectral sequence.

We will now simply calculate the right hand side as a
H_{\ast}(BT;k)_{S_{n}}
-comodule and observe that it is free. First, observe there is an isomorphism of coalgebras
H_{\ast}(BT;k)_{S_{n}}\cong S^{n}(C)
. Let
\mathscr{B}
be a graded basis for
H_{\ast}((S^{1})^{m};k)
as a
k
-module. Let
\mathscr{B}^{\textnormal{odd}}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
be the subset of odd degree and
\mathscr{B}^{\textnormal{even}}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
the subset of even degree elements, respectively. Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{B},m)
be the set of
\mathscr{B}
-indexed partitions
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{B}}\lambda_{x}=m
, where
\lambda_{x}\geq 0
for all

. Note that

H_{\ast}(BT\times T^{m};k)_{S_{n}}\cong(H_{\ast}(BS^{1}\times(S^{1})^{m})^{\otimes n})_{S_{n}}\,.

By simply writing down a basis in terms of

one sees that this is isomorphic as a

-comodule to

(6.1)

\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{B},m)}\left(\bigotimes_{x\in\mathscr{B}^{\textnormal{odd}}}{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{\lambda_{x}}(\Sigma^{|x|}C)\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{x\in\mathscr{B}^{\textnormal{even}}}S^{\lambda_{x}}(\Sigma^{|x|}C)\right)\,.

Here

-comodule.
∎

As a consequence of the proposition (using, for example, the Eilenberg-Moore spectral sequence), the natural map

is obtained from the presentation

of Theorem

We now put everything together: Let

and

-saturation of the ideal

Let
\bar{X}_{1},\dots,\bar{X}_{n}
,
\bar{Y}_{1},\dots,\bar{Y}_{n}

\bar{W}_{1},\dots,\bar{W}_{n-1}
be generators of degrees

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
k

Lemma 6.4
.

Proof.

We can now construct the quotient

Remark 6.7

Kishimoto and Takeda

7.
Integral surjectivity of the Atiyah-Bott map

As a by product of our proofs we obtain some interesting information on a type of map considered by Atiyah and Bott. Let
M=(S^{1})^{2}

Proof.

The map
\alpha_{n}
has the following alternative description: The classifying space of
\mathscr{G}
is the connected component of the mapping space

consisting of the null homotopic maps (see

). We denote it by

Sending a representation

Because the space

we obtain a map

\alpha_{n}\colon\thinspace C_{2}(U(n))_{hU(n)}\to\mathrm{map}^{0}(M,BU(n))\simeq B\mathscr{G}\,.

The proposition claims that the induced map

\scriptstyle{\alpha_{n}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{map}^{0}(M,BU(n))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

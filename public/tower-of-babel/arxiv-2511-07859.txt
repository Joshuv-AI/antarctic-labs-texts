Deterministic Padded Decompositions and Negative-Weight Shortest Paths
Jason Li

Abstract
We obtain the first near-linear time deterministic algorithm for negative-weight single-source shortest paths on integer-weighted graphs. Our main ingredient is a deterministic construction of a padded decomposition on directed graphs, which may be of independent interest.



1
Introduction

We consider the problem of computing single-source shortest paths on a directed, edge-weighted graph. While a near-optimal algorithm on graphs with non-negative weights has been known since Dijkstra, the setting with negative weights has remained open since Bellman-Ford in the 1950s, and only recently has the problem been settled on graphs with negative
integral
weights
[
BNWN25
]
. This breakthrough of Bernstein, Nanongkai, and Wulff-Nilsen combined the integral
scaling
approach
[
Gol95
]
with directed low-diameter graph decompositions
[
LR99
]
, establishing the first near-linear time algorithm for the problem with integral weights. Since then, the running time has been improved to
O((m+n\log\log n)\log(nW)\log n\log\log n)

[
BCF23
,
LMR26
]
, but all of these algorithms are
randomized
, relying crucially on (probabilistic) low-diameter decompositions. It remained an open problem whether a
deterministic
algorithm exists in near-linear time, which we answer affirmatively in this work.

Theorem 1
.

Given a graph with integral edge weights at least
-W
, there is a deterministic algorithm that computes single-source shortest paths in
O((m+n\log\log n)\log(nW)\log^{3}n)
time.

We remark that a recent trend of considering
real-valued
weights has also witnessed exciting breakthroughs
[
Fin24
,
HJQ25
,
HJQ26
]
, beating the
O(mn)
algorithm of Bellman-Ford for the first time in 70 years. Finally, another recent line of work on using
continuous optimization
for graph algorithms has also featured multiple breakthroughs
[
CKL+25
,
VDBCK+23
]
, including a deterministic
m^{1+o(1)}
time algorithm for the more general problem of minimum-cost flow.

Independent work.

In an independent work, Haeupler, Jiang, and Saranurak
[
HJS25
]
also obtain a near-linear time deterministic algorithm for negative-weight shortest paths. Their algorithm defines and constructs a
path cover
, a derandomization of directed low-diameter decompositions that may have future applications. In comparison, our algorithm is faster and more direct.

1.1
Our Techniques

Our main insight is to replace the low-diameter decompositions from prior work, which are inherently probabilistic, with a
padded
graph decomposition technique that may be of independent interest. At a high level, given a radius parameter
\Delta
, the algorithm seeks to find a vertex set
U
such that the
padding

P
consisting of all vertices in
V\setminus U
of distance at most
\Delta/\log^{2}n
from
U
has a relatively small size. Moreover, the partition
(U,V\setminus U)
should be balanced, in that both sides support a constant fraction of the graph size. (In general, it may not be possible to find such a set
U
, but assume for now that we can.) The algorithm makes two recursive calls to negative-weight single-source shortest paths, one on
G[U\cup P]
and one on
G[V\setminus U]
. Observe that vertices in the padding participate in both recursive calls, which is why we require the padding to be small. Importantly, the padding forces any path that visits
U
to spend another
\Delta/\log^{2}n
distance within
U\cup P
, which means it cannot enter
U
and then escape
U\cup P
an arbitrary number of times. This idea is inspired by the concept of
fringe
vertices from Fineman’s parallel shortcutting algorithm
[
Fin18
]
, where vertices are similarly duplicated in recursive calls. After the two recursive calls on
G[U\cup P]
and
G[V\setminus U]
, the algorithm constructs an auxiliary graph where all shortest paths
deterministically
contain few negative-weight edges, in comparison to prior work which only guarantees it in expectation. This step assumes the scaling framework of
[
BNWN25
]
and may output a negative-weight cycle instead. One interesting difference in our algorithm is that we completely eliminate the need to fix DAG edges, since our padded decomposition does not treat DAGs as a special base case.

Of course, finding such a set
U
with small padding may be impossible. For example, if the graph itself has diameter at most
\Delta/\log^{2}n
, then the padding for any vertex set
U
is simply
V\setminus U
, and since we enforce the set
V\setminus U
to support a constant fraction of the graph, the padding cannot be sub-linear in size. In general, the algorithm is also allowed to find a large subgraph of small
weak
diameter, similar to prior randomized algorithms
[
FHL+25
,
LMR26
]
. Also, for technical reasons, we may define the padding in either direction, i.e., either vertices of distance at most
\Delta/\log^{2}n
out of
U
, or vertices of distance at most
\Delta/\log^{2}n
into
U
, and we also obtain up to three sets in the decomposition, not two.

1.2
Preliminaries

Let
G=(V,E,w)
be a directed graph with integral edge weights. The degree of a vertex
v\in V
is the number of edges incident to
v
(which does not depend on edge directions or weights), and the volume
\textup{vol}(U)
of a vertex subset
U\subseteq V
equals
\sum_{v\in U}\deg(v)
. For vertices
u,v\in V
, define
d(u,v)
to be the distance from
u
to
v
according to the edge weights.

The following definitions of weak diameter and in/out-ball are exclusively used on non-negative weighted graphs.
For a vertex subset
U\subseteq V
, define the
weak diameter
of
U
in
G
as
\max\{d(u,v):u,v\in U\}
. For vertex
s\in V
and non-negative number
r
, define the out-ball
B^{+}_{G}(s,r)=\{v\in V:d(s,v)\leq r\}
and in-ball
B^{-}_{G}(s,r)=\{v\in V:d(v,s)\leq r\}
. More generally, for a vertex subset
U\subseteq V
and a non-negative number
r
, define the out-ball
B^{+}_{G}(U,r)=\{v\in V:\min_{s\in U}d(s,v)\leq r\}
and in-ball
B^{-}_{G}(U,r)=\{v\in V:\min_{s\in U}d(v,s)\leq r\}
.

A
potential function

\phi:V\to\mathbb{R}
is a function used to re-weight the edges of the graph, where each edge
(u,v)
has new weight
w(u,v)+\phi(u)-\phi(v)
. A potential is
valid
if the new edge weights are all non-negative. By a result of Johnson
[
Joh77
]
, the task of computing negative-weight single-source shortest paths reduces to finding a valid potential function, after which a single call to Dijkstra is made.

The iterative
scaling
technique of
[
BNWN25
]
does not compute such a potential function in one go. Instead, if the graph
G
has all edge weights at least
-W
, then the goal is to either compute a potential function such that the new edge weights are at least
-W/2
, or output a negative-weight cycle. After
O(\log(nW))
iterations, all edge weights are at least
-1/n^{O(1)}
, at which point the single-source shortest paths can be recovered by rounding.

In order to compute this potential function, the algorithm of
[
BNWN25
]
first defines the graph
G^{W/2}
as the graph
G
with value
W/2
added to all edge weights. Then, any valid potential function for
G^{W/2}
is a potential function for
G
where all new edge weights are at least
-W/2
. For the rest of this paper, our task is to compute a valid potential function for
G^{W/2}
in
O((m+n\log\log n)\log^{3}n)
deterministic time, which suffices for
Theorem

1
.

Similar to prior work, we run our decomposition algorithm on the graph
G^{W/2}_{\geq 0}
defined as the graph
G^{W/2}
with all negative-weight edges replaced by zero-weight edges. Finally, we need a Bellman-Ford/Dijkstra hybrid algorithm
[
DI17
]
, also used in prior work. In fact, our Bellman-Ford/Dijkstra is even simpler because all of our relevant shortest paths have few negative-weight edges
deterministically
, not just in expectation.

Lemma 2

(Hybrid Bellman-Ford/Dijkstra)
.

For any parameter
\eta\geq 1
, there is a deterministic
O(\eta\cdot(m+n\log\log n))
time algorithm that outputs one of the following:

1.

The correct shortest path distances from the source
s
to each vertex
v\in V
, or

2.

A path
P
from
s
to some vertex
v\in V
that is shorter than any
s
-to-
v
path with at most
\eta
negative-weight edges.

2
Negative-Weight Shortest Paths

We begin with the statement of our padded decomposition algorithm on graphs with non-negative weights, which replaces the probabilistic low-diameter decomposition from prior work. For a set
U
with padding
P
described in the introduction, imagine that
k=2
and the two sets
X_{1},X_{2}
are
U\cup P
and
V\setminus U
, so they share
P
in common. The concept of padding itself is replaced by the path coloring statement in property (
3
). For a set
U
with padding
P
, imagine coloring the vertices on a path sequentially as follows: the first vertex on the path inside
U
is colored
1
, and all subsequent vertices are also colored
1
until the path leaves
U\cup P
, at which point the vertices are colored
2
until the next entrance to
U
. The statement guarantees that the path has few edges with differently colored endpoints, which coincides with the intuition that
P
cannot enter
U
and leave
U\cup P
too many times.

Theorem 3

(Padded decomposition)
.

Let
m
be larger than some constant, and let
d,\epsilon>0
be parameters, where
\epsilon
is smaller than some constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
. Consider a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
m
non-negative weight edges. There is a deterministic
O(m+n\log\log n)
time algorithm that finds sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
with
k\leq 3
such that

1.

For each set
X_{i}
, either
\textup{vol}(X_{i})\leq 1.6m
or
X_{i}
has weak diameter at most
d/2
.

2.

X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k}=V
and
\textup{vol}(X_{1})+\cdots+\textup{vol}(X_{k})\leq 2m+O(\epsilon m)
.

3.

Consider any path of weight at most
d
. The vertices of this path can be assigned colors in
\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that

(a)

For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, each vertex colored
i
is in
X_{i}
.

(b)

At most
100\epsilon^{-1}\log m
edges on the path have differently colored endpoints.

We defer our padded decomposition algorithm to
Section

3
. For the rest of this section, we use it to solve negative-weight single-source shortest paths. We prove the theorem below, which combined with the scaling framework of
[
BNWN25
]
establishes
Theorem

1
.

Theorem 4
.

Given a graph
G
with integral edge weights at least
-W
, there is a deterministic
O((m+n\log\log n)\log^{3}n)
time algorithm that either computes single-source shortest paths on
G^{W/2}
, or outputs a negative-weight cycle in
G
.

The algorithm runs the following recursive procedure starting with
X=V
and
d=m^{2}W/2
. The output is a valid potential function on
G^{W/2}[X]
, which is enough to solve single-source shortest paths on
G^{W/2}
when
X=V
. The recursive algorithm keeps the parameter
\epsilon
fixed through the recursive calls, which we eventually set as
\epsilon=1/\log m
.

The algorithm on graph
G^{W/2}
with recursive parameters
(X,d)
:

1.

(Base case.) If the number of edges in
G[X]
is less than some constant, then output a potential function in constant time.

2.

(Base case.) If
d<W/2
:

(a)

If there is a negative-weight edge in
G^{W/2}[X]
, then find and output a negative-weight cycle in
G
.

(b)

Otherwise, output the potential function that is
0
everywhere.

3.

Run
Theorem

3
on the graph
G^{W/2}_{\geq 0}[X]
with
m^{\prime}
edges and parameter
\epsilon=1/\log m
, obtaining sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
. Here, we assume that
m
is large enough to satisfy
\epsilon=1/\log m<\epsilon_{0}
from
Theorem

3
.

4.

Recursively solve
(X_{1},d_{1}),\ldots,(X_{k},d_{k})
, where
d_{i}=d
if
\textup{vol}(X_{i})\leq 1.6m^{\prime}
and
d_{i}=d/2
otherwise, and let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}
be the resulting potential functions.

5.

Construct the following auxiliary graph
H
:

(a)

The vertex set is the
disjoint
union
\{s\}\uplus X_{1}\uplus\cdots\uplus X_{k}
. For index
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
and vertex
v\in X_{i}
, let
v_{i}
be the copy of vertex
v
from set
X_{i}
.

(b)

For each vertex
v\in X
and index
i
such that
v\in X_{i}
, add the edge
(s,v_{i})
with weight
-\phi_{i}(v)
.

(c)

For each edge
(u,v)
and indices
i,j
such that
u\in X_{i}
and
v\in X_{j}
, add the edge
(u_{i},v_{j})
with weight
w(u,v)+\phi_{i}(u)-\phi_{j}(v)
.

6.

Run Bellman-Ford/Dijkstra (
Lemma

2
) on
H
with source
s
and
\eta=100\log^{2}m+1
. If shortest path distances are not returned, then find and output a negative-weight cycle in
G
.

7.

Output the following potential function
\phi
on
G^{W/2}[X]
: for each
v\in X
, set
\phi(v)=d_{H}(s,v_{i})+\phi_{i}(v)
for any index
i
with
v\in X_{i}
.

is an instance of the recursive algorithm.

.

If
d<m^{2}W/2
, then

has weak diameter at most
d
in
G^{W/2}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Consider going down the recursion tree from the root to instance

, and let
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})

. Since

with
\textup{vol}(X^{\prime})>1.6m^{\prime\prime}

is the number of edges of this parent instance. By property (

,

. Since

also has weak diameter at most

in
G^{W/2}_{\geq 0}
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

If step (
2a
) is executed, then the algorithm can find a negative-weight cycle in

.

Proof.

Consider a negative-weight edge
(u,v)
in

-W/2

Lemma 7
.

Any

-to-
v_{i}
path

in

corresponds to a path

in

ending at

(and starting from anywhere), and the weight of

is lower by exactly

. In particular, the final potential function

Consider an

. In particular, since

.

Any path

Lemma 10
.

The total running time is
O((m+n\log\log n)\log^{3}n)
.

Proof.

By property (

3
Padded Decomposition

In this section, we present our padded decomposition algorithm to prove
Theorem

3
, restated below.

See
3

We begin with a simple procedure to find in-balls and out-balls with small padding, which uses standard ball-growing arguments.

Lemma 11
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, a source vertex
s\in V
, a direction
\pm\in\{+,-\}
, an initial radius parameter
\Delta_{0}\geq 0
, an additional radius limit
\Delta>0
, and a parameter
\epsilon>0
, there is an algorithm that computes a radius
r\in[\Delta_{0},\Delta_{0}+\Delta]
such that
\textup{vol}(B^{\pm}(s,r+\epsilon\Delta/\log m))\leq(1+O(\epsilon))\,\textup{vol}(B^{\pm}(s,r))
, and runs in
O(\textup{vol}(B^{\pm}(s,r+\epsilon\Delta/\log m))+|B^{\pm}(s,r+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)|\log\log n)
time.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\pm
is
+
. The algorithm grows the ball
B^{+}(s,r)
by running Dijkstra’s algorithm, maintaining the current radius
r
and ball
B^{+}(s,r)
. Each time
r
surpasses
\Delta_{0}+i\cdot\Delta
for a positive integer
i
, the algorithm checks whether

\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,\Delta_{0}+i\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m))\leq(1+C\epsilon)\,\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,\Delta_{0}+(i-1)\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m))

for a fixed constant
C>0
, and if so, immediately terminate with the radius
r=\Delta_{0}+(i-1)\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m
. The algorithm can check this condition in
O(1)
time by maintaining the following history: for each index
j\leq\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,r))
in an array, store the minimum radius
r^{\prime}
for which
\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,r^{\prime}))\geq j
. The algorithm simply looks up index
\lceil\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,\Delta_{0}+i\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m))/(1+C\epsilon)\rceil
and compares it to
\Delta_{0}+(i-1)\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m
.

For the running time, we implement Dijkstra’s algorithm using Thorup’s priority queue
[
Tho03
]
. Each vertex update takes
O(\log\log n)
time and each edge update takes
O(1)
time, and the running time follows.

Finally, we prove that
r\leq\Delta_{0}+\Delta
by a standard ball-growing argument. If the algorithm does not stop by positive integer
i
, then

\displaystyle\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,\Delta_{0}+i\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m))

\displaystyle>(1+C\epsilon)\,\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,\Delta_{0}+(i-1)\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m))

\displaystyle>(1+C\epsilon)^{2}\,\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,\Delta_{0}+(i-2)\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m))

\displaystyle>\cdots

\displaystyle>(1+C\epsilon)^{i}\,\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,\Delta_{0}))

\displaystyle\geq(1+C\epsilon)^{i}.

Since
\textup{vol}(B^{+}(s,\Delta_{0}+i\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m))\leq 2m
, we conclude that
2m\geq(1+C\epsilon)^{i}
. For constant
C>0
large enough, the algorithm must stop by
i\leq\epsilon^{-1}\log m
, so
r=\Delta_{0}+(i-1)\cdot\epsilon\Delta/\log m\leq\Delta_{0}+\Delta
.
∎

3.1
Light case

Suppose that
Lemma

11
always finds either an in-ball or out-ball with at most half the volume. In this case, we stop the algorithm once the total volume of in-balls or out-balls covers a constant fraction of the volume, and set
U
to be the union of these balls. If the algorithm fails to find a small in-ball or out-ball from a vertex
s
, then the algorithm gives up on the light case, proceeding to the heavy case in
Section

3.2
with the vertex
s
.

In the algorithm below, imagine that
\Delta\approx d
; we will eventually set
\Delta=d/12
to handle all cases. Below, we define
G\{U\}
as the induced subgraph
G[U]
with (zero-weight) self-loops added to preserve vertex degrees, i.e.,
\deg_{G\{U\}}(v)=\deg_{G}(v)
for all
v\in U
.

1.

Initialize
U^{+}\leftarrow\emptyset
and
U^{-}\leftarrow\emptyset
.

2.

While
\textup{vol}(U^{+})<m/2
and
\textup{vol}(U^{-})<m/2
:

(a)

Select an arbitrary vertex
s\in V\setminus(U^{+}\cup U^{-})
. Run
Lemma

11
on
G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}
in the
+
direction, and separately on
G\{V\setminus U^{-}\}
in the
-
direction, both with initial radius
0
and additional limit
\Delta
. Run the two algorithms in parallel, and let
B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r)
be the ball of smaller volume, where
\pm\in\{+,-\}
. Terminate the other algorithm immediately upon finding the smaller ball.

(b)

If
\textup{vol}(B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r))\leq m
, then update
U^{\pm}\leftarrow U^{\pm}\cup B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r)
.

(c)

Else, terminate the light case immediately and proceed to the heavy case with the vertex
s
.

3.

Return either
U^{+}
or
U^{-}
, whichever has larger volume.

In the proofs below, we assume that the light case does not terminate early (with the heavy case). Define
X=B^{\pm}_{G}(U^{\pm},\epsilon\Delta/\log m)
and
Y=V\setminus U^{\pm}
.

Lemma 12
.

We have
\textup{vol}(X)\leq 1.6m
and
\textup{vol}(Y)\leq 3m/2
and
\textup{vol}(X)+\textup{vol}(Y)\leq 2m+O(\epsilon m)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\pm
is
+
. We have
\textup{vol}(U^{+})\geq m/2
since the while loop terminated, so
\textup{vol}(Y)\leq 3m/2
. On the last iteration of the while loop, we had
\textup{vol}(U^{+})<m/2
before adding in a set of volume at most
m
, so
\textup{vol}(U^{+})\leq 3m/2
at the end, which means
\textup{vol}(Y)\geq m/2
. Suppose first that we have proved
\textup{vol}(X)+\textup{vol}(Y)\leq 2m+O(\epsilon m)
; then
\textup{vol}(X)\leq 2m-\textup{vol}(Y)+O(\epsilon m)\leq 3m/2+O(\epsilon m)
, which is at most
1.6m
since
\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
for a small enough constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
.

To prove
\textup{vol}(X)+\textup{vol}(Y)\leq 2m+O(\epsilon m)
, it suffices to prove that their intersection
B^{+}_{G}(U^{+},\epsilon\Delta/\log m)\setminus U^{+}
has volume at most
O(\epsilon m)
. Define the
padded
vertices as the union of the sets
B^{+}_{G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}}(s,r+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)\setminus B^{+}_{G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}}(s,r)
over all balls
B^{+}_{G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}}(s,r)
that were added to
U^{+}
. By the guarantee of
Lemma

11
,

\textup{vol}(B^{+}_{G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}}(s,r+\epsilon\Delta/\log m))\leq(1+O(\epsilon))\,\textup{vol}(B^{+}_{G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}}(s,r)),

so the volume ratio of newly padded vertices to new vertices in
U^{+}
is
O(\epsilon)
. It follows that the total volume of padded vertices is
O(\epsilon m)
. To complete the proof, we show that each vertex
v\in B^{+}_{G}(U^{+},\epsilon\Delta/\log m)\setminus U^{+}
is a padded vertex. Consider such a vertex
v
, and consider the shortest path
P
from
U^{+}
to
v
of weight at most
\epsilon\Delta/\log m
. Let
u
be the vertex on
P
that was added to
U^{+}
the earliest over the course of the algorithm. Then, on the iteration immediately before adding
u
to
U^{+}
, the
u
-to-
v
segment of
P
is contained in
G[V\setminus U^{+}]
and also has weight at most
\epsilon\Delta/\log m
. Since
u\in B^{+}_{G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}}(s,r)
on this iteration, we have
v\in B^{+}_{G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}}(s,r+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)
. In addition,
v\notin B^{+}_{G\{V\setminus U^{+}\}}(s,r)
since
v
was never added to
U^{+}
, so
v
is a padded vertex.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Consider any path of weight
w
. The vertices of this path can be colored red and blue such that

1.

Each red vertex is in
X
, and each blue vertex is in
Y
.

2.

At most
2\lceil\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil
edges on the path have differently colored endpoints.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\pm
is
+
, and let the path be
P
. Consider the first vertex on
P
that is in
U^{+}
, and call it
u
. Consider the maximal segment of vertices after
u
that are all in
X=B^{\pm}_{G}(U^{\pm},\epsilon\Delta/\log m)
, and let
v
be the last vertex on this segment. If there is a vertex
v^{\prime}
after
v
on
P
, then the
u
-to-
v^{\prime}
segment of
P
must have weight exceeding
\epsilon\Delta/\log m
.

We color everything before
u
blue, and everything from
u
to
v
red. If the vertex
v^{\prime}
exists, then we recursively color everything from
v^{\prime}
onwards. Observe that on each recursive call, the weight of
P
decreases by more than
\epsilon\Delta/\log m
, so there are at most
\lceil\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil
total calls. Each iteration has at most
2
edges with different colors, which concludes the proof.

The case when
\pm
is
-
is identical, except we start at the end of the path and color backwards.
∎

Lemma 14
.

The algorithm runs in
O(m+n\log\log n)
time.

Proof.

By regularizing vertex degrees, e.g., by adding
\log\log n
self-loops of weight
0
at each vertex, we can assume that
\textup{vol}(B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r))\geq|B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r)|\log\log n
for each ball computed on step (
2a
). Since we terminate the other algorithm immediately upon finding the smaller ball, this step takes
O(\textup{vol}(B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)))=O(\textup{vol}(B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r)))
time and adds
\textup{vol}(B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r))
volume to
U^{\pm}
. Each of
U^{+}
and
U^{-}
supports
O(m+n\log\log n)
volume after regularization, which also bounds the running time.
∎

3.2
Heavy case

In the heavy case, we have found a vertex
s\in V
where the volume of the smaller ball
B^{\pm}_{G\{V\setminus U^{\pm}\}}(s,r)
exceeds
m
. The ball in the other direction, if run to completion, also has volume more than
m
. Both balls have radius at most
\Delta
by
Lemma

11
, and they are on an induced subgraph of
G
. Increasing the radius to
\Delta
and operating on
G
instead, we conclude that
\textup{vol}(B^{+}_{G}(s,\Delta))>m
and
\textup{vol}(B^{-}_{G}(s,\Delta))>m
.

The algorithm runs
Lemma

11
on
G
in the
+
direction, and separately in the
-
direction, both with initial radius
\Delta
and additional limit
\Delta
. Let
r^{+},r^{-}\in[\Delta,2\Delta]
be the computed radii. The algorithm considers the three sets

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)\cap B^{-}_{G}(s,r^{-}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m),

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)\setminus B^{-}_{G}(s,r^{-}),

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=V\setminus B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+}).

Lemma 15
.

X
has weak diameter at most
6\Delta
. Moreover,
\textup{vol}(Y)\leq m
and
\textup{vol}(Z)\leq m
and
\textup{vol}(X)+\textup{vol}(Y)+\textup{vol}(Z)\leq 2m+O(\epsilon m)
.

Proof.

For any two vertices
u,v\in X
, there is a
u
-to-
s
path in
G
of weight at most
r^{-}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m\leq 3\Delta
, and an
s
-to-
v
path of weight at most
r^{+}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m\leq 3\Delta
, for a total of
6\Delta
. It follows that
X
has weak diameter at most
6\Delta
.

To bound the volumes of
Y
and
Z
, observe that
B^{\pm}_{G}(s,r^{\pm})
contains
B^{\pm}_{G}(s,\Delta)
which has volume greater than
m
. Since
Y
and
Z
both exclude either
B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+})
or
B^{-}_{G}(s,r^{-})
, and since the total volume is
2m
, the bounds follow.

Finally, we show that
\textup{vol}(X)+\textup{vol}(Y)+\textup{vol}(Z)\leq 2m+O(\epsilon m)
. It suffices to show that the vertices appearing in multiple sets have total volume
O(\epsilon m)
. Observe that
X\cup Y=B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)
, so any vertex in both
X\cup Y
and
Z
is in
B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)\setminus B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+})
, which has volume
O(\epsilon m)
by the guarantee of
Lemma

11
. Similarly, any vertex in both
X
and
Y
is in
B^{-}_{G}(s,r^{-}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)\setminus B^{-}_{G}(s,r^{-})
, which also has volume
O(\epsilon m)
.
∎

Lemma 16
.

Consider any path of weight
w
. The vertices of this path can be colored red, blue, and green such that

1.

Each red vertex is in
X
, each blue vertex is in
Y
, and each green vertex is in
Z
.

2.

At most
8\lceil\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil
edges on the path have differently colored endpoints.

Proof.

We first color vertices
yellow
and green such that each yellow vertex is in
X\cup Y
, and each green vertex is in
Z
. Since
X\cup Y=B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)
and
Z=V\setminus B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+})
, we can proceed identically to
Lemma

13
, using the fact that any path segment from inside
B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+})
to outside
B^{+}_{G}(s,r^{+}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)
has weight exceeding
\epsilon\Delta/\log m
. We obtain a yellow-green coloring of the path with at most
2\lceil\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil
edges having differently colored endpoints. In particular, there are at most
2\lceil\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil
consecutive yellow segments.

We now re-color each consecutive yellow segment into red and blue. Again, since all vertices in
X
are in
B^{-}_{G}(s,r^{-}+\epsilon\Delta/\log m)
and all vertices in
Y
are in
V\setminus B^{-}_{G}(s,r^{-})
, we can apply
Lemma

13
again, obtaining at most
2\lceil\frac{w^{\prime}}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil\leq 2(\frac{w^{\prime}}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m})+2
red-blue edges per segment of weight
w^{\prime}
. Since the weights
w^{\prime}
sum to at most
w
, and since there are at most
2\lceil\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil
consecutive yellow segments, the total number of red-blue edges is at most
2(\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m})+2\cdot 2\lceil\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil
. Together with the green vertices, the overall number of differently colored edges is at most
8\lceil\frac{w}{\epsilon\Delta/\log m}\rceil
.
∎

Combining both cases

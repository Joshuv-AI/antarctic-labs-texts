A note on the Taylor estimates of iterated paraproducts
Masato Hoshino

Abstract
Bony’s paraproduct is one of the main tools in the theory of paracontrolled calculus. The paraproduct is usually defined via Fourier analysis, so it is not a local operator. In the previous researches [ 7 , 8 ] , however, the author proved that the pointwise estimate like ( 1.2 ) holds for the paraproduct and its iterated versions when the sum of the regularities is smaller than 1 1 . The aim of this article is to extend these results for higher regularities.

Introduction

This is a short note on the extensions of
[
7
,
8
]
.
Besov spaces on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are usually defined via Fourier analysis,
and also equivalently defined via estimates of Taylor remainders.
Indeed, if
\alpha\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
and
f\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}=B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
f
is differentiable up to order
[\alpha]
(integer part of
\alpha
) and has the estimate

\displaystyle\Omega^{\alpha}(f)(\cdot,x):=f(\cdot)-\sum_{|k|<\alpha}\frac{(\cdot-x)^{k}}{k!}\partial^{k}f(x)=O(|\cdot-x|^{\alpha}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.1)

See
[
2
, Theorem 2.36]
for instance.
In
[
7
,
8
]
, the author proved similar estimates for
Bony’s paraproduct
.
One of the results of
[
7
]
says that, if
\alpha,\beta>0
,
\alpha+\beta<1
,
f\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}
, and
g\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\beta}
, then the paraproduct
f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g
(see Proposition
2.2
) has an estimate

\displaystyle(f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g)(\cdot)=(f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g)(x)-f(x)(g(\cdot)-g(x))+O(|\cdot-x|^{\alpha+\beta}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.2)

In
[
7
,
8
]
, similar estimates were obtained for iterated paraproducts
((f_{1}\mathbin{\varolessthan}f_{2})\mathbin{\varolessthan}\cdots)\mathbin{\varolessthan}f_{n}
,
and applied to the proof of generalized commutator estimates.
The commutator estimate first proved in
[
5
, Lemma 2.4]
has an important role in the theory of
paracontrolled calculus

[
5
,
1
]
.
See the remarks after Theorem
1.1
for an overview of the theory.
In these articles, however, the sum of regularities is always assumed to be smaller than
1
.
The aim of this article is to extend these results for higher regularities.

Fix
d\geq 1
throughout this article.
For any multiindex
k=(k_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, denote
|k|:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}

and
k!=\prod_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}!
.
We use the notation
a\lesssim b
to mean
a\leq cb
for some constant
c>0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha+\beta,\beta+\gamma,\alpha+\beta+\gamma\notin\mathbb{N}
,
f\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}
,
g\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\beta}
, and
h\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\gamma}
.

(1)

Define

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Omega^{\alpha,\beta}(f,g)(y,x)&:=(f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g)(y)-\sum_{|k|<\alpha+\beta}\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!}D^{k}(f,g)(x)\\
&\quad-\sum_{|k|<\alpha}\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!}\partial^{k}f(x)\Omega^{\beta}(g)(y,x),\end{aligned}

(1.3)

where

\displaystyle D^{k}(f,g)=\partial^{k}(f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
|k_{1}|<\alpha,\ |k_{2}|\geq\beta\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{k_{1}}\partial^{k_{1}}f\partial^{k_{2}}g

(1.4)

is a bounded continuous function (see Lemma
2.6
), and
\Omega^{\beta}(g)
is the same notation as (
1.1
).
Then

\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\Omega^{\alpha,\beta}(f,g)(y,x)|}{|y-x|^{\alpha+\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}}\|g\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\beta}}.

(2)

Define

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Omega^{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h)(y,x)&:=\big((f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g)\mathbin{\varolessthan}h\big)(y)-\sum_{|k|<\alpha+\beta+\gamma}\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!}D^{k}(f,g,h)(x)\\
&\quad-\sum_{|k+\ell|<\alpha}\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!}\partial^{k+\ell}f(x)\Omega_{\ell}^{\beta,\gamma}(g,h)(y,x)\\
&\quad-\sum_{|k|<\alpha+\beta}\frac{(y-x)^{k}}{k!}D^{k}(f,g)(x)\Omega^{\gamma}(h)(y,x)\end{aligned}

(1.5)

for some choice of bounded continuous functions
D^{k}(f,g,h)
,
and

\displaystyle\Omega_{\ell}^{\beta,\gamma}(g,h)=\begin{cases}\Omega^{\beta,\gamma}(g,h),&\ell=0,\\
\Omega^{\beta+\gamma+|\ell|}(g\mathbin{{}^{\ell}\varolessthan}h),&\ell\neq 0\end{cases}

for some
g\mathbin{{}^{\ell}\varolessthan}h\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\beta+\gamma+|\ell|}
.
Here
\Omega^{\beta,\gamma}
and
\Omega^{\gamma}
are the same notations as (
1.3
) and (
1.1
), respectively.
Then

\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\Omega^{\alpha,\beta}(f,g,h)(y,x)|}{|y-x|^{\alpha+\beta+\gamma}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}}\|g\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\beta}}\|h\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\gamma}}.

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
7
,
8
]
, the author proved the pointwise estimates for iterated paraproducts
((f_{1}\mathbin{\varolessthan}f_{2})\mathbin{\varolessthan}\cdots)\mathbin{\varolessthan}f_{n}
.
For
n=2,3
, this result takes the form of (
1.2
) and

\displaystyle\big((f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g)\mathbin{\varolessthan}h\big)(y)-\big((f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g)\mathbin{\varolessthan}h\big)(x)

\displaystyle\quad-f(x)\big\{(g\mathbin{\varolessthan}h)(y)-(g\mathbin{\varolessthan}h)(x)\big\}-(f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g)(x)\big(h(y)-h(x)\big)

\displaystyle=O(|y-x|^{\alpha+\beta+\gamma})

for
(f,g,h)\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}\times B_{\infty,\infty}^{\beta}\times B_{\infty,\infty}^{\gamma}
with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
and
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
.
Here is a restriction that the sum of regularities should be smaller than
1
.
The formulas (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) are extensions of
[
7
,
8
]
for higher regularities.

Let us briefly recall the role of paraproducts and commutators in the theory of paracontrolled calculus.
The main subject of paracontrolled calculus is solving PDEs with stochastic noise, for example, (generalized) parabolic Anderson model

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)u=f(u)\xi,

(1.6)

where
\xi
is a stochastic noise. A typical choice of
\xi
is a Gaussian white noise on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so it has a regularity only less than
-\frac{d}{2}
.
Then
u
would have a regularity less than
2-\frac{d}{2}
by Schauder estimate, but it is not enough to define the product
f(u)\xi
, since the sum of regularities of
f(u)
and
\xi
is
(2-\frac{d}{2})+(-\frac{d}{2})\leq 0
if
d\geq 2
.
To overcome this difficulty, we impose some structural assumption on
u
. In
[
5
]
, the authors solved (
1.6
) in
d=2
by assuming that
u
is of the form

u=u^{\prime}\mathbin{\varolessthan}Z+u^{\sharp},\qquad u^{\prime}\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha},\ u^{\sharp}\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{2\alpha}

with some
\alpha<1
.
In
[
1
]
, the authors solved (
1.6
) in
d=3
by assuming

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}u_{i}\mathbin{\varolessthan}Z_{i}+u^{\sharp},\qquad u^{\sharp}\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{4\alpha},

\displaystyle u_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i+j\leq 3}u_{ij}\mathbin{\varolessthan}Z_{j}+u_{i}^{\sharp},\qquad u_{i}^{\sharp}\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{(4-i)\alpha},

\displaystyle u_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i+j+k\leq 3}u_{ijk}\mathbin{\varolessthan}Z_{k}+u_{ij}^{\sharp},\qquad u_{ij}^{\sharp}\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{(4-i-j)\alpha},\ u_{ijk}\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}

over
1\leq i,i+j,i+j+k\leq 3
, with some
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
.
Here
Z
and
Z_{i}
’s are some explicit functionals of
\xi
, defined by a stochastic approach called renormalization.
For the product of unknown functions and
\xi
, we use the commutator estimates.
In 2d case for instance, the trilinear operator

C(u^{\prime},Z,\xi):=(u^{\prime}\mathbin{\varolessthan}Z)\varodot\xi-u^{\prime}(Z\varodot\xi)

is called a commutator, where
\varodot
is a resonant product (see Proposition
2.2
).
Two resonants in the right-hand side are ill-defined since
Z\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}
and
\xi\in B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha-2}
with
\alpha<1
,
but it turns out that
C
is actually a continuous operator of
(u^{\prime},Z,\xi)
if
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{3},1)
.
It yields that
(u^{\prime}\mathbin{\varolessthan}Z)\varodot\xi:=u^{\prime}(Z\varodot\xi)+C(u^{\prime},Z,\xi)
is well-defined and continuous from
(u^{\prime},Z,\xi,Z\varodot\xi)
.
We can extend this argument to define the resonant
f(u)\varodot\xi
, by using a paralinearization formula
[
2
, Theorem 2.92]
.
In 3d case, more commutations between iterated paraproducts and resonants are needed. See
[
1
]
for details.

Instead of paracontrolled calculus, we can also prove the same well-posedness results by the theory of
regularity structures

[
6
]
.
One of the differences between those theories is how to write the assumptions of solutions.
In the former, main tools are paraproducts and commutators, so it is purely a harmonic analysis.
In the latter, the solution is defined as a “modelled distribution,” which is a kind of generalized Taylor expansion like (
1.3
) and (
1.5
).
Hence, it is a natural question whether the Fourier picture and the Taylor picture are equivalent.
Theorem
1.1
is an answer to that question in the particular case.
For other studies on this direction, see Section 6 of
[
5
]
, and
[
9
,
3
,
4
]
.

Key estimates

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
2.8
.
First we recall the basics of Littlewood-Paley theory and Bony’s paraproduct.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix smooth, nonnegative, and radial functions
\chi
and
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

•

\mathop{\rm supp}(\chi)\subset\{x\ ;\,|x|<\frac{4}{3}\}
and
\mathop{\rm supp}(\rho)\subset\{x\ ;\,\frac{3}{4}<|x|<\frac{8}{3}\}
,

•

\chi(x)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\rho(2^{-j}x)=1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Set
\rho_{-1}:=\chi
and
\rho_{j}=\rho(2^{-j}\cdot)
for
j\geq 0
.
For each
j\geq-1
, define the Littlewood-Paley blocks

\Delta_{j}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\rho_{j}\mathcal{F}f)

of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are Fourier transform and its inverse, respectively.
Moreover, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, define
B_{p,q}^{\alpha}
as the set of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
such that

\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\alpha}}:=\Big\|\big\{2^{j\alpha}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\}_{j=-1}^{\infty}\Big\|_{\ell^{q}}<\infty.

Littlewood-Paley blocks decompose
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into the infinite sum of smooth functions
\Delta_{j}f
.
The support conditions of
\chi
and
\rho
are to ensure that spectral supports of
\Delta_{i}f
and
\Delta_{j}f
are disjoint if
|i-j|\geq 2
.
One of the applications of this decomposition is Bony’s paraproduct.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
2
, Theorems 2.82 and 2.85]
)
.

For
f,g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, define

f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g=\sum_{i<j-1}\Delta_{i}f\Delta_{j}g,\qquad f\varodot g=\sum_{|i-j|\leq 1}\Delta_{i}f\Delta_{j}g,

if the right-hand side converges in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,p_{1},p_{2},q,q_{1},q_{2}\in[1,\infty]
be such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
.

(1)

If
\alpha\neq 0
, then
\|f\mathbin{\varolessthan}g\|_{B_{p,q}^{\alpha\wedge 0+\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{\alpha}}\|g\|_{B_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{\beta}}
.

(2)

If
\alpha+\beta>0
, then
\|f\varodot g\|_{B_{p,q}^{\alpha+\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{\alpha}}\|g\|_{B_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{\beta}}
.

We also consider a slightly more abstract setting.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
.
Define
\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha}
as the set of all sequences
\boldsymbol{f}=\{f^{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that,

\mathcal{F}f^{j}
is supported in
2^{j}B=\{2^{j}x\ ;\,x\in B\}
with some ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all
j\geq 0
, and

\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha}}:=\Big\|\big\{2^{j\alpha}\|f^{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big\|_{\ell^{q}}<\infty.

We treat only
p=q=\infty
in this article, but the following arguments still work for general
p,q
by easy modifications.
We write
\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}:=\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}:=\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}}
in short.
Moreover, we treat only
\alpha>0
.
The following estimates follow from the definitions and Bernstein’s lemma
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
.

•

For any
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
, the series
\sum\boldsymbol{f}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}f^{j}
converges in
B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}
,
and one has
\|\sum\boldsymbol{f}\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\alpha}
.

•

\|\partial^{k}\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\alpha-|k|}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\alpha}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, where
\partial^{k}\boldsymbol{f}:=\{\partial^{k}f^{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
.
If
\alpha>0
,
\sum\partial^{k}\boldsymbol{f}
converges to
\partial^{k}\sum\boldsymbol{f}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, since
for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
,

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\partial^{k}f^{j},\varphi\Big)=(-1)^{|k|}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}f^{j},\partial^{k}\varphi\Big)\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}(-1)^{|k|}\big(\sum\boldsymbol{f},\partial^{k}\varphi\big)=\big(\partial^{k}\sum\boldsymbol{f},\varphi\big).

We consider an analogue of Bony’s paraproduct for these sequences.
For any
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}
and
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{B}^{\beta}
with
\alpha,\beta>0
, we define

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{g}):=\{f^{<j}g^{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty},

(2.1)

where
f^{<j}:=\sum_{i<j}f^{i}
.
Since
\|f^{<j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is bounded over
j
, it follows that
(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{g})\in\mathbb{B}^{\beta}
.
Similarly, for any sequence
\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n}\in\mathbb{B}^{\alpha_{n}}
with
\alpha_{n}>0
(
n=1,2,\dots
), we recursively define

(\boldsymbol{f}\!_{1},\boldsymbol{f}\!_{2},\dots,\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n}):=((\boldsymbol{f}\!_{1},\boldsymbol{f}\!_{2},\dots,\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n-1}),\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n}).

Remark 2.4
.

The following arguments still work if we replace
f^{<j}
with
f^{<j-N}
for some fixed
N>0
.
For instance,
N=1
was chosen in the above definition of Bony’s paraproduct.
If we define
\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n}=\{\Delta_{j-1}f_{n}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
for some
f_{n}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, then the series
\sum(\boldsymbol{f}\!_{1},\boldsymbol{f}\!_{2})=\sum_{j}\Delta_{<j-1}f_{1}\Delta_{j}f_{2}
coincides with
f_{1}\mathbin{\varolessthan}f_{2}
.
Note that
\sum(\boldsymbol{f}\!_{1},\boldsymbol{f}\!_{2},\boldsymbol{f}\!_{3})\neq(f_{1}\mathbin{\varolessthan}f_{2})\mathbin{\varolessthan}f_{3}

\Delta_{j}(f_{1}\mathbin{\varolessthan}f_{2})\neq\Delta_{<j-1}f_{1}\Delta_{j}f_{2}

We define the Taylor remainders for these multicomponent operators.
For any multiindices
k=(k_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
and
\ell=(\ell_{i})_{i=1}^{d}

k_{i}\geq\ell_{i}

\binom{k}{\ell}:=\frac{k!}{\ell!(k-\ell)!}

Definition 2.5
.

For any sequence
\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n}\in\mathbb{B}^{\alpha_{n}}
with
\alpha_{n}>0
(
n=1,2,\dots
), recursively define

\displaystyle\alpha_{1\cdots n}

\displaystyle:=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n},

\displaystyle\{f_{1\cdots n}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle:=(\boldsymbol{f}\!_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n}),\qquad f_{1\cdots n}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}f_{1\cdots n}^{j},

\displaystyle D_{1\cdots n}^{k}

\displaystyle:=\partial^{k}f_{1\cdots n}-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
|k_{1}|<\alpha_{1\cdots m}\\
|k_{2}|\geq\alpha_{(m+1)\cdots n}\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{k_{1}}D_{1\cdots m}^{k_{1}}D_{(m+1)\cdots n}^{k_{2}}\qquad(k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}),

\displaystyle T_{1\cdots n}(\cdot,x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{|k|<\alpha_{1\cdots n}}\frac{(\cdot-x)^{k}}{k!}D_{1\cdots n}^{k}(x),

\displaystyle\Omega_{1\cdots n}(\cdot,x)

\displaystyle:=f_{1\cdots n}(\cdot)-T_{1\cdots n}(\cdot,x)-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}T_{1\cdots m}(\cdot,x)\Omega_{(m+1)\cdots n}(\cdot,x),

where
D_{(m+1)\cdots n}^{k}
and
\Omega_{(m+1)\cdots n}
are defined by the same way as
D_{1\cdots(n-m)}^{k}
and
\Omega_{1\cdots(n-m)}

\boldsymbol{f}\!_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n-m}
with
\boldsymbol{f}\!_{m+1},\dots,\boldsymbol{f}\!_{n}
.
In other words,
D_{w}^{k}

\Omega_{w}

To make sense of the above definition, we need the following lemma.
Throughout this section, we assume that

\displaystyle\alpha_{m\cdots n}=\alpha_{m}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\notin\mathbb{N}\quad\text{for any $1\leq m\leq n$}.

(2.2)

Lemma 2.6
.

Under the assumption (
2.2
),
D_{1\cdots n}^{k}
is well-defined as a bounded continuous function for any
|k|<\alpha_{1\cdots n}
.

Proof.

First we prove some identities.
For each
j\geq 0
, we recursively define

\displaystyle D_{1\cdots n}^{k,j}

\displaystyle=\partial^{k}f_{1\cdots n}^{j}-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
|k_{1}|<\alpha_{1\cdots m}\\
|k_{2}|\geq\alpha_{(m+1)\cdots n}\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{k_{1}}D_{1\cdots m}^{k_{1}}D_{(m+1)\cdots n}^{k_{2},j},

\displaystyle C_{1\cdots n}^{k,j}

\displaystyle=\partial^{k}f_{1\cdots n}^{<j}-{\bf 1}_{|k|<\alpha_{1\cdots n}}D_{1\cdots n}^{k}-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
|k_{1}|<\alpha_{1\cdots m}\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{k_{1}}D_{1\cdots m}^{k_{1}}C_{(m+1)\cdots n}^{k_{2},j}.

For simplicity, we write the definition of
C_{1\cdots n}^{k,j}
as

C_{1\cdots n}^{k,j}=\partial^{k}f_{1\cdots n}^{<j}-\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
|k_{1}|<\alpha_{1\cdots m}\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{k_{1}}D_{1\cdots m}^{k_{1}}C_{(m+1)\cdots n}^{k_{2},j}

in short.
Then we can derive

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &D_{1\cdots n}^{k,j}=\sum_{k=k_{1}+k_{2}}\binom{k}{k_{1}}C_{1\cdots(n-1)}^{k_{1},j}\partial^{k_{2}}f_{n}^{j}\\
&+\sum_{r=2}^{n}(-1)^{r}\sum_{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{r}=k\\
|k_{1}|<\alpha_{w_{1}},\dots,\,|k_{r}|<\alpha_{w_{r}}\end{subarray}}\frac{k!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{r}!}D_{w_{1}}^{k_{1}}D_{w_{2}}^{k_{2}}\cdots D_{w_{r-1}}^{k_{r-1}}D_{w_{r}}^{k_{r},j},\end{aligned}

(2.3)

where
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{r}
runs over all partitions of the sequence
1\cdots n
,
that is,
w_{i}
is a sequence from
n_{i-1}+1
to
n_{i}
for some
0=n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{r}=n
.
To prove (
2.3
), we transform the definition of
D_{1\cdots n}^{k,j}
into

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{1\cdots n}^{k,j}&=\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}\binom{k}{k_{1}}\partial^{k_{1}}f_{1\cdots(n-1)}^{<j}\partial^{k_{2}}f_{n}^{j}-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
|k_{1}|<\alpha_{1\cdots m}\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{k_{1}}D_{1\cdots m}^{k_{1}}D_{(m+1)\cdots n}^{k_{2},j}\\
&\quad+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
|k_{1}|<\alpha_{1\cdots m},\,|k_{2}|<\alpha_{(m+1)\cdots n}\end{subarray}}\binom{k}{k_{1}}D_{1\cdots m}^{k_{1}}D_{(m+1)\cdots n}^{k_{2},j}.\end{aligned}

(2.4)

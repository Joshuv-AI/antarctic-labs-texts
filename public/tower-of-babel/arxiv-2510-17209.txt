On Bilateral Multiple Sums and Rogers-Ramanujan Type Identities
Dandan Chen, Tianjian Xu

Abstract
. We establish a new bilateral double-sum Rogers-Ramanujan identity involving two parameters. As applications, this identity yields several new multi-sum Rogers-Ramanujan type identities. Our proofs utilize the theory of basic hypergeometric series in conjunction with the integral method.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11P84, 33D60

1.
Introduction

We begin by recalling the basic notation and definitions used throughout this paper. Assume that
|q|<1
for convergence. The q-shifted factorials are defined as follows
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle(a;q)_{0}:=1,~~(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{k}),~~(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k}).

For negative subscripts, we define

\displaystyle(a;q)_{-n}:=\frac{1}{(aq^{-n};q)_{n}}.

We also adopt the compact product notation:

\displaystyle(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m};q)_{k}:=(a_{1};q)_{k}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}.

In 1894, Rogers
[
10
]
discovered and proved the following fundamental identities:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}=\frac{1}{\bigl(q,q^{4};q^{5}\bigr)_{\infty}},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}}{(q;q)_{n}}=\frac{1}{\bigl(q^{2},q^{3};q^{5}\bigr)_{\infty}}.

These are now known as the Rogers-Ramanujan identities, since they were later rediscovered by Ramanujan before 1913
[
6
]
. These results have inspired extensive research into similar
q
-series identities, among which Slater’s famous list of
130
such identities stands out
[
12
]
.

A natural direction of generalization is to consider multi-sum Rogers-Ramanujan type identities. As a multi-sum generalization of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), the Andrews-Gordon identity
[
1
]
stated that for integers
k>1
and
1\leq i\leq k
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k-1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+\dots+N_{k-1}^{2}+N_{i}+\dots+N_{k-1}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{k-2}}(q;q)_{n_{k-1}}}=\frac{(q^{i},q^{2k+1-i},q^{2k+1};q^{2k+1})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

where
N_{j}=n_{j}+\cdots+n_{k-1}
for
1\leq j\leq k-1
and
N_{k}=0
. Bressoud
[
3
]
later provided an even-modulus analogue: for integers
k>1
and
1\leq i\leq k
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k-1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+\dots+N_{k-1}^{2}+N_{i}+\dots+N_{k-1}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{k-2}}(q^{2};q^{2})_{n_{k-1}}}=\frac{(q^{i},q^{2k-i},q^{2k};q^{2k})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

with
N_{j}
defined as above.

Rogers-Ramanujan type identities play a significant role in several fields, including combinatorics, mathematical physics and number theory. In particular, they reveal profound connections between the theory of q-series and modular forms. For instance, after multiplying with suitable powers of
q
, the right-hand sides of identities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) become modular forms which is not easy to be observed from their sum-side representations. A central question in this area is to characterize which basic hypergeometric series can be expressed as modular forms.

In this aspect, Nahm
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
considered a specific class of q-hypergeometric series known as Nahm sum or Nahm series:

\displaystyle f_{A,B,C}(q):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\end{subarray}}\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}n^{\mathrm{T}}An+n^{\mathrm{T}}B+C}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{r}}},

where
r
is a positive integer,
A
is a real positive definite symmetric
r\times r
matrix,
B
is a r-dimensional column vector, and
C
is a rational scalar. Motivated by considerations from physics, Nahm
[
9
]
posed the problem of classifying all rational triples
(A,B,C)
for which
f_{A,B,C}
is modular; such a triple
(A,B,C)
is called as a modular triple.

Nahm further conjectured a necessary and sufficient condition on
A
for it to be the matrix part of a modular triple. In a systematic study, Zagier
[
15
]
showed that there are exactly seven modular triples in the rank
r=1
case:

\displaystyle(1/2,0,-1/40),(1/2,1/2,1/40),(1,0,-1/48),(1,1/2,1/24),

\displaystyle(1,-1/2,1/24),(2,0,-1/60),(2,1,11/60).

Notably, the last two triples correspond to the Rogers-Ramanujan identities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

The following identity is first given by Ramanujan, which is commonly known as Ramanujan’s
{}_{1}\psi_{1}
summation
[
5
, Appendix(II.29)]

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(a;q)_{k}}{(b;q)_{k}}z^{k}=\frac{(q,az,q/az,b/a;q)_{\infty}}{(b,z,b/az,q/a;q)_{\infty}},\quad|b/a|<|z|<1.

The identity is a bilateral extension of the q-binomial theorem
[
5
, Appendix(II.3)]

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a;q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}z^{k}=\frac{(az;q)_{\infty}}{(z;q)_{\infty}},\quad|z|<1,

which is a fundamental identity in the theory of basic hypergeometric series. A natural question is whether similar bilateral extensions exist for Rogers-Ramanujan type identities. In 2023, Schlosser
[
11
]
employed bilateral summation formulas to study Nahm sum and derived new bilateral Rogers-Ramanujan type identities by analytic methods.

Following the natation of
[
13
]
, we define a Rogers-Ramanujan type identity of index
(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k})
as a finite sum of the form:

\displaystyle\sum_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})\in S}\frac{(-1)^{t(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})}q^{Q(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})}}{(q^{n_{1}};q^{n_{1}})_{i_{1}}\cdots(q^{n_{k}};q^{n_{k}})_{i_{k}}}=\prod_{(a,n)\in P}(q^{a};q^{n})^{r(a,n)}_{\infty},

where
t(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})
is an integer-valued function,
Q(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})
is a rational polynomials in
i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}
, the
n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k}
are positive integers with
gcd(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k})=1
,
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{k}
,
P\subset\mathbb{Q}^{2}
is finite, and
r(a,n)
is integer-valued. For instance, the Andrews-Gordon identity (
1.3
) and Bressoud’s identity (
1.4
) are of indices
(1,1,\cdots,1)
and
(1,1,\cdots,1,2)
, respectively.

Recently, Cao and Wang
[
4
]
used the integral method to derive several new Rogers-Ramanujan type identities of indices

\displaystyle(1,1),(1,2),(1,1,1),(1,1,2),(1,1,3),(1,2,2),(1,2,3),(1,2,4).

Some of these include additional parameters, thereby giving infinite families of such identities. For example, they proved
[
4
, Theorem 3.4]

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\frac{u^{i-j}q^{\binom{i}{2}+\binom{j+1}{2}+a\binom{j-i}{2}}}{(q;q)_{i}(q;q)_{j}}=\frac{(-uq^{a},-q/u,q^{a+1};q^{a+1})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

Motivated by the integral method and recent work on bilateral Rogers-Ramanujan identities, we present the following main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x^{i}y^{j}q^{i^{2}-ij+j^{2}}}{(xq;q)_{i}(yq;q)_{j}}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}(-xyq,-q/xy,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(xq,yq;q)_{\infty}}.

As applications, this identity yields

Corollary 1.2
.

We have,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\frac{q^{i^{2}-ij+j^{2}}}{(q;q)_{i}(q;q)_{j}}=\frac{(-q,-q,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k\geq 0}\frac{q^{i^{2}+j^{2}+k^{2}+ik+jk}}{(q;q)_{i}(q;q)_{j}(q;q)_{k}}=\frac{(-q,-q,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{\ell}\geq 0}\frac{q^{Q(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{\ell})}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{\ell}}}=\frac{(-q,-q,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

where
Q(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{\ell})=(n_{1}+n_{3}+\cdots+n_{\ell})^{2}-(n_{1}+n_{3}+\cdots+n_{\ell})(n_{2}+n_{3}+\cdots+n_{\ell})+(n_{2}+n_{3}+\cdots+n_{\ell})^{2}+\sum_{i\in\{4,\cdots,\ell\}}(n_{1}+n_{i}+\cdots+n_{\ell})(n_{2}+n_{i}+\cdots+n_{\ell})+n_{1}n_{2}
with integers
\ell\geq 4
.

Remark 1.3
.

Setting
u=a=1
in (
1.6
), we can also obtain (
1.8
). This identity is a special case of a more general family of identities for Nahm sums associated with the tensor product of Cartan matrices
[
14
, Eq. (1.6)]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review some fundamental identities and the integral method. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
using the integral method and derive several multi-sum Rogers-Ramanujan identities as consequences.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall several fundamental identities and techniques from the theory of q-series that will be used throughout this paper.

We have that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}(a;q)_{n}a^{-n}=(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}.

We also require the Jacobi triple product identity
[
5
, p.15]

\displaystyle(q,z,q/z;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}z^{n}.

Starting from Ramanujan’s
{}_{1}\psi_{1}
summation formula (
1.5
), we substitute
z\rightarrow z/a
and take
a\rightarrow\infty
, yielding

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-z)^{k}q^{\binom{k}{2}}}{(b;q)_{k}}=\frac{(q,z,q/z;q)_{\infty}}{(b,b/z;q)_{\infty}}.

Two special cases of (
2.1
) will be particularly useful. First, setting
b=xq
and replacing
z
with
xz
, we obtain:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-xz)^{i}q^{\binom{i}{2}}}{(xq;q)_{i}}=\frac{(q,xz,q/xz;q)_{\infty}}{(xq,q/z;q)_{\infty}}.

Second, setting
b=yq
and replacing
z
with
yq/z
, we obtain:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-yq/z)^{j}q^{\binom{j}{2}}}{(yq;q)_{j}}=\frac{(q,yq/z,z/y;q)_{\infty}}{(yq,z;q)_{\infty}}.

For a Laurent series
f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a(n)z^{n}
, we denote by
[z^{n}]f(z)
the coefficient of
z^{n}
, i.e.,
[z^{n}]f(z)=a(n)
. A key observation is that this coefficient can be extracted via contour integration:

\displaystyle\oint_{K}f(z)\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}=[z^{0}]f(z),

where
K
is any positively oriented simple closed contour encircling the origin. This principle underlies the integral method, which we employ to prove Rogers-Ramanujan type identities. The general approach is to express the sum side of an identity as a finite linear combination of integrals involving infinite products, and then evaluate each integrals explicitly.

3.
The Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
using the integral method. We also show how special choices of parameters in Theorem
1.1
lead to
several Rogers-Ramanujan type identities.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We begin with the Jacobi triple product identity and consider the following contour integral:

\displaystyle\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(xq,yq;q)_{\infty}}\oint(q,xz,q/xz;q)_{\infty}(q,yq/z,z/y;q)_{\infty}\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(xq,yq;q)_{\infty}}\oint\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}(-xz)^{i}q^{\binom{i}{2}}(-z/y)^{j}q^{\binom{j}{2}}\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}.

Since
\oint z^{n}\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}=\delta_{n,0}
, the integral is non-zero only when
j=-i
. Thus:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(xq,yq;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(xy)^{i}q^{i^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(xq,yq;q)_{\infty}}(-xyq,-q/xy,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}.

On the other hand, using identities (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), the same integral can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\oint\frac{(q,xz,q/xz;q)_{\infty}}{(xq,q/z;q)_{\infty}}\frac{(q,yq/z,z/y;q)_{\infty}}{(yq,z;q)_{\infty}}(q,z,q/z;q)_{\infty}\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}

\displaystyle=\oint\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-xz)^{i}q^{\binom{i}{2}}}{(xq;q)_{i}}\cdot\frac{(-yq/z)^{j}q^{\binom{j}{2}}}{(yq;q)_{j}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k}{2}}z^{k}\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}.

Multiplying the series and extracting the coefficient of
z^{0}
, we require
i-j+k=0
, i.e.,
k=j-i
. Substituting this gives:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x^{i}y^{j}q^{\binom{i}{2}+\binom{j+1}{2}+\binom{j-i}{2}}}{(xq;q)_{i}(yq;q)_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x^{i}y^{j}q^{i^{2}-ij+j^{2}}}{(xq;q)_{i}(yq;q)_{j}}.

Equating the two expressions for
I
completes the proof of (
1.7
).
∎

The proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Setting
x=y=1
in (
1.7
) and noting that
1/(q;q)_{-n}=0
for
n>0
, the bilateral sum reduces to a sum over non-negative indices, yielding (
1.8
).

To prove (
1.10
), we use the identity
[
2
, p.20]
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q;q)_{i}(q;q)_{j}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{q^{(i-k)(j-k)}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{i-k}(q;q)_{j-k}}.

Substituting this into (
1.8
) and shifting the summation indices by setting
i\rightarrow i+k
and
j\rightarrow j+k
, we obtain identity (
1.9
). Finally, applying the same identity from
[
2
, p.20]
iteratively yields identity (
1.10
).
∎

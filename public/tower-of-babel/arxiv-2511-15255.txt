Abstract
Realism constraints (or constraints on perceptual quality) have received considerable recent attention within the context of lossy compression, particularly of images. Theoretical studies of lossy compression indicate that high-rate common randomness between the compressor and the decompressor is a valuable resource for achieving realism. On the other hand, the utility of significant amounts of common randomness has not been noted in practice. We offer an explanation for this discrepancy by considering a realism constraint that requires satisfying a universal critic that inspects realizations of individual compressed reconstructions, or batches thereof. We characterize the optimal rate-distortion trade-off under such a realism constraint, and show that it is asymptotically achievable without any common randomness, unless the batch size is impractically large.

Apersistent open challenge in lossy compression, especially for image/video compression

The tremendous advancements in neural compression methods and image generation models in recent years have sparked a renewed interest in it. Reconstructed images are expected to have a high pixel-level fidelity to the original source and be indistinguishable to humans from naturally occurring ones. This guarantees that there are no visible artifacts, like blocking or blurriness, in the reconstructed images.

Theoretical frameworks involving

have been considered previously
[

This approach, as well as adopting (
2

For instance,

The concentration around

25

A reconstruction that has small realism deficiency

distribution, including those not yet discovered.

Although such a realism constraint is very strong, we show that it is essentially free: it is satisfied
with high probability by a compression scheme constructed through random selection in the usual fashion.
In particular, under this realism constraint, the RDP function in (
3
)
can be achieved with deterministic codes. The idea is that each codeword is realistic according
to a given critic because it is generated according to the source distribution. To argue that it is realistic
according to all computable critics, we use the fact that the number of such critics is countable,
and thus, we can create a “universal” critic whose realism deficiency is
approximately
a convex combination of all
computable deficiencies.
If the expected deficiency of this universal critic is asymptotically bounded then
it must be bounded for each of the constituent critics.

The universal critic

\delta:\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

is itself incomputable
and can be shown to
be related to
the Kolmogorov complexity

x_{1:n}\mapsto K(x_{1:n})

(see Remark
18

to follow and Appendix
I
).
Using the notion of Kolmogorov complexity in the formulation of an engineering problem inevitably raises the objection
that
K(x_{1:n})
is not computable
[
7
, Sec. 14.7]
. This is not a concern in the present application because it is the critic, not
the compression scheme, that is called upon to compute
K(x_{1:n})
. By showing that a code can achieve the
RDP
function without common randomness while satisfying this most powerful of
critics, we show that it can also satisfy any weaker critic, such as a human observer. This is akin to
how proofs of information-theoretic security assume adversaries with unlimited computational power.

The realization-based approach to realism espoused in this paper might appear to be of a fundamentally
different nature from the divergence-based approach in (
1
) and (
2
).
In fact, it is possible to simultaneously generalize the two by extending the framework to
allow the critic to examine batches of reconstructions at a time
[
34
]
. Specifically, when the blocklength is

n
, we assume that
B_{n}
source realizations are generated and passed through the encoder and
decoder. The critic then examines the resulting
B_{n}
reconstructions. For
B_{n}=1
, this reduces to
our original formulation, and no common randomness is needed to achieve the RDP
function. On the other hand, if
B_{n}
grows very quickly with
n
, then the batch essentially reveals
the
n
-length distribution of the reconstructions, and the realism constraint is equivalent to the
divergence constraint in (
1
). In this case, common randomness
is needed to achieve the RDP
function.
More generally, we show that if
B_{n}
grows sub-exponentially with
n
, then common randomness is not
needed to achieve the RDP
function while it is necessary if
B_{n}
grows
exponentially with
n
at a sufficiently large rate. Characterizing the extent to which
common randomness is needed in the intermediate regime between these two extremes is
an interesting theoretical problem raised by this work.
In the case of sub-exponential
B_{n}
,
these findings shed light on why the need for common randomness has not been observed in practice:
to satisfy a realization-based critic such as a human-observer, common randomness is only needed
if the critic considers impractically large batches at a time.

is computable, and

Remark 4

Definition
1

is a composite of two prominent definitions
of a

-critic:
Definitions 4.3.8 and 2.4.1
in

[

]
— which is a standard algorithmic information theory textbook.
The former definition is precisely (

), where weights

The second definition (

where the

The latter definition is more suitable for a Shannon-theoretic coding formulation, where each blocklength is viewed as a problem unto itself, while the former definition is

Our proposed formulation

New model for the rate-distortion-perception trade-off

III-A

The one-shot setting

We
consider a function

We assume that

are deterministic.

We propose a

formalized in the following two definitions.

Definition 6

We extend

\forall B{\in}\mathbb{N},\ \forall(\mathbf{x}^{(B)},\mathbf{y}^{(B)})\in\mathcal{X}^{B}\times\mathcal{X}^{B},\quad d(\mathbf{x}^{(B)},\mathbf{y}^{(B)}):=\tfrac{1}{B}\scalebox{1.05}{$\sum_{k{=}1}^{B}$}d(x^{(k)},y^{(k)}).

Consider

-critic

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P^{\otimes B}}\big[d(\mathbf{X}^{(B)},\mathbf{Y}^{(B)})\big]\leq\Delta\ \text{ and }\mathbb{E}_{P^{\otimes B}}[\delta(\mathbf{Y}^{(B)})]\leq C,

where
\mathbf{X}^{(B)}\in\mathcal{X}^{B}

B

The main

[

Intuitively, that constraint

and

In that sense, our proposed RDP framework generalizes the original one,
through involving elements of practical realism metrics, such as the number

samples that are inspected, and a scoring function

which is required to be

Theorem

to follow constitutes a rigorous statement of this intuition.
In the next section, we define an asymptotic notion of achievability.
We provide achievable points in the sense of Definition

in Section
IV-B
.

The extension of

The setup is depicted

A coding scheme is designed for a specific blocklength

Given

IV
Results

IV-A

Low batch size regime

The following theorem states that

R^{(1)}(\cdot),
defined in (
3
), which naturaly arises in the distribution matching formalism, also characterizes
the optimal trade-off in our asymptotic setting, when the batch size is not impractically large.

Theorem 10

Consider a finite source alphabet
\mathcal{X}
such that
|\mathcal{X}|\geq 2,
a computable source distribution
p_{X}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\forall x\in\mathcal{X},p_{X}(x)>0,

and
a sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of positive integers such that

\log(B_{n})/n\ \ \begin{subarray}{c}\raisebox{-4.0pt}{$\longrightarrow$}\\
\scalebox{0.7}{$n$$\to$$\infty$}\end{subarray}\ \ 0.

For any
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+},
let
R(\Delta)
be the infimum of rates
R
such that there exists
R_{c}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
(R,R_{c},\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 1},\Delta)
is asymptotically achievable with algorithmic realism.
Moreover,
let
R_{*}(\Delta)
be the infimum of rates
R
such that
(R,\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 1},\Delta)
is asymptotically achievable with algorithmic realism with fully deterministic codes.
Then,
if

R^{(1)}(\Delta)<H_{p}(X),

we have

R(\Delta)=R_{*}(\Delta)=R^{(1)}(\Delta).

The proof is provided in Appendices
C
and
D
.
The strength of this result lies in how stringent constraint (
11
) is: a single compression scheme satisfies a performance guarantee for every
relevant

p_{X}^{\otimes*}
-critic,
and deterministic schemes are sufficient.
Moreover, one can find such a scheme for any batch size sequence which is sub-exponential in the dimension
n
of the source, i.e., for all regimes where the batch size is not impractically large.
To prove the achievability direction of Theorem
10
, we leverage the existence of a
universal

p_{X}^{\otimes*}
-critic
\delta_{0}
(see Appendix
A-B
), which is one of the great successes of algorithmic information theory. Indeed, it is sufficient to construct a scheme which achieves (
11
) only for such a
\delta_{0},
which is more sensitive than all relevant
p_{X}^{\otimes*}
-critics.
It is a very strong critic, stronger than can be implemented in practice, which is another strength of Theorem
10
.

IV-B

One-shot achievable points

For theoretical interest, we provide
a
family of
points
which are achievable,
in the sense of Definition
7
,
without any statistical assumption on the source distribution
p_{X}.

For the sake of
intuition, one can consider the following example:

•

\mathcal{X}
is a finite set of images, e.g., the set of all images of a given resolution, with a finite range for pixels (finite precision).

•

is the mean squared error between pixel values.

•

B
is the number of images inspected by the critic at a time.

•

R
is the number of bits into which a given image is compressed.

Theorem 11

Consider a finite set
\mathcal{X}
with
|\mathcal{X}|\geq 2,
a computable distribution
p_{X}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\forall x\in\mathcal{X},p_{X}(x)>0,
some

some

and a

-critic

Consider any conditional transition kernel
p_{Y|X}
from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}
satisfying

and

\mathbb{E}_{p}[d(X,Y)]\leq\Delta.

Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\Delta/2),
and any

the triplet
(R,\Delta^{\prime},C)
is
\delta
-achievable with an
(R,0)
code having a deterministic decoder, with

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Delta+\varepsilon+\dfrac{6\Delta}{\varepsilon}\max(d)\cdot\eta_{R,\gamma}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle:=

\dfrac{3\Delta}{\varepsilon}\Bigg[1+\Big(\dfrac{B^{2}}{\lfloor 2^{R}\rfloor}+2B\eta_{R,\gamma}\Big)\cdot\max_{x}B\log\dfrac{2}{p_{X}(x)}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\eta_{R,\gamma}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle p(\mathcal{A}_{R,\gamma})+2^{-\gamma\log(|\mathcal{X}|)/2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{R,\gamma}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}^{2}\ |\ \log\Big(\dfrac{p_{X,Y}(x,y)}{p_{X}(x)p_{Y}(y)}\Big)-\log(\lfloor 2^{R}\rfloor)>-\gamma\log(|\mathcal{X}|)\Big\},

The proof is provided in Appendix

The term

is an upper bound on the probability that two source samples in the batch are compressed into the same message.
This is related to the so-called

(see

The term

which follows from Definition

.
Theorem

provides insights on the asymptotic regime of Theorem

Consider

We know that

Hence, if this log-likelihood ratio concentrates well, and if

Hence, Theorem

IV-C

Generalizing the distribution matching formalism

We present a result which connects our proposed formalism for the RDP trade-off to the distribution matching formalism of
[

]
, and concludes our findings regarding the role of common randomness.

IV-C

Background

Under the

the natural asymptotic notion of achievability is as follows.

IV-C

Connection to our formalism

As

Theorem 13

Consider
a computable
increasing
sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of positive integers such that

B_{n}/|\mathcal{X}|^{n}\ \begin{subarray}{c}\raisebox{-4.0pt}{$\longrightarrow$}\\
\scalebox{0.7}{$n$$\to$$\infty$}\end{subarray}\ \infty.

Then, for any

and any

(R,R_{c},\Delta)
is asymptotically achievable with near-perfect realism, if and only if
(R,R_{c},\Delta)
is asymptotically achievable with perfect realism.
The closure of the set of such achievable triplets

is equal to the following region:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\scalebox{1.0}{$\exists\ p_{Y,V|X}$}&:&\scalebox{1.0}{$p_{Y}\equiv p_{X}$}\\
\scalebox{1.0}{$R$}&\geq&\scalebox{1.0}{$I_{p}(X;V)$}\\
\scalebox{1.0}{$R+R_{c}$}&\geq&\scalebox{1.0}{$I_{p}(Y;V)$}\\
\scalebox{1.0}{$\Delta$}&\geq&\scalebox{1.0}{$\mathbb{E}_{p}[d(X,Y)]$}\\
X\quad-&V&-\quad Y\end{array}\right\}.

Hence, the former is a generalization of the latter.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we have proposed a theoretical framework for realism constraints in lossy compression that is more in line with human perception than the distribution matching formalism.
Indeed, our formulation incorporates key aspects of practical realism metrics: realization-based critics that can be algorithmically computed, and the number (batch size) of samples jointly inspected by a critic.
Inspired by the algorithmic information theory literature and
[
34
]
, we require that reconstructions asymptotically satisfy all computable critics of randomness. This includes critics inspecting the frequency of any pattern, but also critics inspecting subtle properties, such as ones pertaining to the Erdős-Rényi law of runs.
Theorem
10
states that common randomness does not improve the trade-off under our formalism, in all regimes where the batch size is not impractically large with respect to the dimension
n
of the source.
This offers a compelling explanation for
the fact that the need for large amounts of common randomness has not been observed in practice.
Theorem
13
states that
when the batch size is extremely large,
our formalism is equivalent to the distribution matching formalism
[
26
,
36
,
6
]
,
hence
common randomness is useful in that regime.
Thus, Theorems
10
and
13

indicate that, in order to understand the role of randomization in lossy compression with realism constraints,
the focus should be shifted
to the size of the batch inspected by the critic.
A continuation of our work could be to investigate
realism metrics, with particular attention given to the choice of the batch size. This could lead to highlighting specific strengths and weaknesses of existing realism metrics. It may also inspire a critical assessment of the relative performance of existing compression schemes, depending on the choice of the realism metric. Another future direction could be to more precisely characterize the amount of randomness needed as a function of the batch size.
Furthermore,
possible extensions of our setup include compression with side information
[
14
]
, and other distributed scenarios.

Universal critics and semi-measures

Definition 16

Given a finite set
\mathcal{W},
a function
f:\mathcal{W}\to[0,1]
is a semi-measure if

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}f(w)\leq 1.

It is said to be a lower semi-computable semi-measure if
f
is a semi-measure and
lower semi-computable.

The following theorem,
corresponds to Definition 4.3.2, Equation (4.2), and Theorems 4.3.1 and 4.3.3 in
[
22
]
— which is a standard algorithmic information theory textbook.
It introduces the notion of
universal
p^{\otimes*}
-critic
, used in
[
34
]
.

Theorem 17

Consider
a finite set
\mathcal{X}.

Let
p
be a computable distribution on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\forall x\in\mathcal{X},p(x)>0.

Then,
there exists
a
p^{\otimes*}
-critic

\delta_{0}

(which is not necessarily lower semi-computable),
such that
for any lower semi-computable
p^{\otimes*}
-critic
\delta,
there exists a constant
c_{\delta}
such that

\displaystyle\forall x\in\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n},\quad\delta_{0}(x)\geq\delta(x)-c_{\delta}.

(13)

Any
p^{\otimes*}
-critic satisfying (
13
) is called a universal
p^{\otimes*}
-critic.

Since our definitions are slightly different from the classical ones — see Remark
4
—, we provide a proof of Theorem
17

in Appendix
I
.
See Remark
32
for a comparison of
p^{\otimes*}
-critics that are universal according to different formulations.
Such a critic
\delta_{0}
is one of the best measures of realism deficiency according to
p,
in the limit of arbitrarily long strings.
If a critic
\delta
identifies a certain amount of deficiency in a given string, then
\delta_{0}
will identify at least as much deficiency, up to some additive constant.
Intuitively,
\delta_{0}
is sensitive to all properties of randomness according to

The existence of such a
\delta_{0}
constitutes a remarkable property of the set of all lower semi-computable

-critics (which is infinite).

Appendix I
Existence of a universal
p^{\otimes*}
-critic

We provide a proof of Theorem
17
.
From
[
22
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, there exists a sequence
\{q_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
containing all lower semi-computable semi-measures on
\{0,1\}^{*},
and a sequence
\{\pi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of (strictly) positive reals, such that the mixture defined by

\mathbf{m}:=\sum_{n\geq 1}\pi_{n}q_{n}

(55)

is a lower semi-computable semi-measure on
\{0,1\}^{*}.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N},
let
\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{n}):=\sum_{x_{1:n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}\mathbf{m}(x_{1:n}).

From (
55
), we have
\forall x\in\{0,1\}^{*},\mathbf{m}(x)>0.
Moreover,
\forall x_{0}\in\mathcal{X},\ p(x_{0})>0,
thus
\forall x\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n},

p^{\otimes*}(x)>0.
Define the function
\delta_{0},
by

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\forall x_{1:n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n},\ \delta_{0}(x_{1:n}):=\log\Big(\dfrac{\mathbf{m}(x_{1:n})}{\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{n})p^{\otimes n}(x_{1:n})}\Big).

(56)

Then, for any
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}p^{\otimes n}(x)2^{\delta_{0}(x)}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}\dfrac{\mathbf{m}(x)}{\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{n})}\leq 1.

(57)

Hence,
\delta_{0}
is a
p^{\otimes*}
-critic.
Fix a lower semi-computable
p^{\otimes*}
-critic
\delta.
Define the map
q_{\delta}:\{0,1\}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\forall x\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n},\quad q_{\delta}(x):=\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{l(x)})2^{\delta(x)}p^{\otimes*}(x),

(58)

and
x\mapsto 0
elsewhere.
From Lemma
15
(iii),
the function which is null outside of
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n},
and defined by
x\mapsto\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{l(x)})
on
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n},
is lower semi-computable.
Moreover,
x\mapsto 2^{\delta(x)}
and
x\mapsto p^{\otimes*}(x)
are lower semi-computable by Lemma
15
(i) and (iii) respectively.
Thus,
q_{\delta}
is the product three non-negative lower semi-computable functions.
Hence,
q_{\delta}
is lower semi-computable by Lemma
15
(i).
Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}}q_{\delta}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{n})\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}2^{\delta(x)}p^{\otimes n}(x)\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1,

where
the first bound
follows from the definition of a
p^{\otimes*}
-critic; and
the second
follows
from the fact that
\mathbf{m}
is a semi-measure.
Therefore,
q_{\delta}
is a lower semi-computable semi-measure.
Thus, from (
55
), we have
\mathbf{m}\geq\pi_{q_{\delta}}q_{\delta},
for some positive real
\pi_{q_{\delta}}.

In order to derive (
13
), fix
x\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n}.

From (
55
), we have
\mathbf{m}(x)>0.
Therefore, since
\forall x_{0}\in\mathcal{X},p(x_{0})>0,
we have
q_{\delta}(x)>0.

Thus, from (
56
), we have

\displaystyle\delta_{0}(x)=\log\Big(\dfrac{\mathbf{m}(x_{1:n})}{\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{n})p^{\otimes n}(x_{1:n})}\Big)\geq\log\Big(\dfrac{\pi_{q_{\delta}}q_{\delta}(x_{1:n})}{\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{n})p^{\otimes n}(x_{1:n})}\Big)=\log(\pi_{q_{\delta}})+\delta(x).

This is true for any lower semi-computable
p^{\otimes*}
-critic
\delta,
and any
x\in\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n}.
Since
\log(\pi_{q_{\delta}})
does not depend on
x,
then property (
13
) holds. This concludes the proof.

Remark 32

As detailed in Remark
4
, our definition of a
p^{\otimes*}
-critic is an adaptation of classical definitions
[
22
, Definitions 2.4.1 & 4.3.8]
.
In
[
22
, Definition 4.3.8]
, a computable distribution
\{\pi(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\pi(n)>0
for all
n
is assumed to be given.
The universal critic
under that definition is given by

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\forall x_{1:n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n},\ \delta_{1}(x_{1:n}):=\log\Big(\dfrac{\mathbf{m}(x_{1:n})}{\tilde{p}(x_{1:n})}\Big),

[
22
, Theorem 4.3.5]
,
where
\tilde{p}(x_{1:n})=\pi(n)p^{\otimes n}(x_{1:n}).
In other words, for any
\delta
that is a
p^{\otimes*}
-critic according to
[
22
, Definition 4.3.8]
, there exists a constant
c_{\delta}
such that

\displaystyle\forall x\in\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n},\quad\delta_{1}(x)\geq\delta(x)-c_{\delta}.

(59)

One can verify that
this domination also holds for
all functions
\delta
which are
p^{\otimes*}
-critics in the sense of Definition
1
, irrespective of the weights
\{\pi(n)\}_{n}.
However,
\delta_{1}
is not itself a critic according to Definition
1

for all choices of
\{\pi(n)\}_{n}

(Eq.
57
may fail to hold).

Remark 33

The presence of weights
\{\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{X}^{n})\}_{n}
in the definition of
\delta_{0}
(Eq.
56
), or any other weights, as considered in Remark
32
and references therein, is natural: this ensures that the denominator is a semi-measure over
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n},
similarly to the numerator.
Moreover, such scaling is crucial for the critic to be universal.
Indeed,
consider the function
\delta_{2}
that is a variant of
\delta_{0}
with no scaling, and is defined by

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\forall x_{1:n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n},\ \delta_{2}(x_{1:n}):=\log\Big(\dfrac{\mathbf{m}(x_{1:n})}{p^{\otimes n}(x_{1:n})}\Big).

One can readily prove that

\delta_{2}
is lower semi-computable
by using similar computability arguments as for
q_{\delta}
(Eq.
58
).
Moreover, since
\mathbf{m}
is a semi-measure, we have
\delta_{2}\leq\delta_{0},
thus, from (
56
),
\delta_{2}
is also a
p^{\otimes*}
-critic.
One can readily show that
if

\delta_{2}

were universal, then it would
also
be
a
universal

p^{\otimes*}
-critic according to
(
59
)
and

[
22
, Definition 4.3.8]

—
for any given computable distribution
\{\pi(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
—,
under which
\delta_{1}
is universal (Remark
32
).
This would imply that

|\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}|
is
bounded,
which is false by construction.
Hence,

\delta_{2}
is not a universal
p^{\otimes*}
-critic according to Definition
1
.
To wit, the theoretical concept of universality is only relevant when considering critics defined on a countably infinite set — such as
\{0,1\}^{*}
or
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}^{n}.

Hence, scaling such as considered here may be irrelevant in scenarios where critics are only defined on a finite set.

Appendix J
Additional semi-computability arguments

We provide a proof of the fact that
f\mapsto 2^{f}/(f+3)^{2}

preserves lower semi-computability. The remainder of
Lemma
15

follows from similar arguments.
Let
f:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{R}
be lower semi-computable
and

(x,k)\mapsto\varphi_{f}(x,k)

be
a computable function from
\mathcal{E}
to
\mathbb{Q},

monotonically
approaching
f
from below
(Def.
14
).
There exists a real
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
u\mapsto 2^{u}/(3+u)^{2}
is non-decreasing on
(-\varepsilon,\infty).

Fix
x\in\mathcal{E}.
Compute
k_{1}(x),
the smallest

k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi_{f}(x,k)>-\varepsilon.
For all

k\in[1,k_{1}(x))\cap\mathbb{N},
define
\varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}(x,k):=0.
Fix

k\geq k_{1}(x).
Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
b\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi_{f}(x,k)=a/b.

Compute the greatest

m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(m/2^{k})^{b}\leq 2^{a}/(3+a/b)^{2b}.

Then, set
\varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}(x,k):=m/2^{k}.

Therefore,

\forall k\geq k_{1}(x),\ \ 0\leq\dfrac{2^{\varphi_{f}(x,k)}}{(3+\varphi_{f}(x,k))^{2}}-\varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}(x,k)<\scalebox{0.9}{$\dfrac{1}{2^{k}}$}.

(60)

Note that
\varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}
is defined via an algorithm that halts for every input
(x,k)
and outputs a rational number. Thus,
it is a computable rational-valued function.
From (
60
), and since
k\mapsto\varphi_{f}(x,k)
is non-decreasing, and
u\mapsto 2^{u}/(3+u)^{2}
is non-decreasing on
(-\varepsilon,\infty),
we have

\forall k\geq k_{1}(x)+1,\ \ \varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}(x,k-1)\leq\dfrac{2^{\varphi_{f}(x,k)}}{(3+\varphi_{f}(x,k))^{2}}.

Since
\varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}(x,k-1)
can also be written in the form
m^{\prime}/2^{k},
then, from the maximality of the integer
m
appearing in the construction of
\varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}(x,k),
we have

\forall k\geq k_{1}(x)+1,\ \ \varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}(x,k-1)\leq\varphi_{2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}}(x,k).

(61)

This is also true for all integers
k\in[2,k_{1}(x)+1).

Properties (
60
) and (
61
) imply
that
2^{f}/(3+f)^{2}
is lower semi-computable.

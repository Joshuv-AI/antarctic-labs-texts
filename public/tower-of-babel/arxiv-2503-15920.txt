Riemann surface foliations with non-discrete singular set
Sahil Gehlawat

Abstract
. Let ℱ \mathcal{F} be a singular Riemann surface foliation on a complex manifold M M , such that the singular set E ⊂ M E\subset M is non-discrete. We study the behavior of the foliation near the singular set E E , particularly focusing on singular points that admit invariant submanifolds (locally) passing through them. Our primary focus is on the singular points that are removable singularities for some proper subfoliation. We classify singular points based on the dimension of their invariant submanifold and, consequently, establish that for hyperbolic foliations ℱ \mathcal{F} , the presence of such singularities ensures the continuity of the leafwise Poincaré metric on M ∖ E M\setminus E .

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a complex manifold of dimension
N
, and
E\subset M
a closed analytic subset of codimension at least 2. A Riemann surface foliation
\cal{F}
on
M
, singular along
E
, is defined by an atlas of flow-boxes
(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})
of
M\setminus E
where
\phi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{N-1}
(
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
is the open unit disc). The transition maps
\phi_{\alpha\beta}
are holomorphic in
(x,y)\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{N-1}
and take the form

\phi_{\alpha\beta}(x,y)=\phi_{\alpha}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\beta}(x,y)=(P(x,y),Q(y)).

The leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are Riemann surfaces that are locally expressed as
\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}(\{y=\mbox{constant}\})
. The leaf passing through
p\in M\setminus E
is denoted by
L_{p}
.

A subset
\Sigma\subset M
is said to be
\mathcal{F}-
invariant if for every
p\in\Sigma\setminus E
, the corresponding leaf
L_{p}\subset\Sigma
. A subset
\Sigma\subset M
is said to be local
\mathcal{F}-
invariant near
p\in\Sigma\subset M
if there exists a neighborhood
U\subset M
of
p
such that
\Sigma
is invariant under
\mathcal{F}|_{U}
, that is,
\Sigma
is
\mathcal{F}|_{U}-
invariant. Our focus is on singular points
p\in E
for which there exists a submanifold
S\subset M
satisfying the following properties:
S
is local
\mathcal{F}
-invariant near
p
,
S\not\subset E
and
p
is a removable singularity of the foliation
\mathcal{F}|_{S}
. The saturated foliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{S}
corresponding to the foliation
\mathcal{F}|_{S}
is obtained by including all the removable singularities of the subfoliation
\mathcal{F}|_{S}
, and
\hat{E}_{S}\subset E\cap S
will be the singular set of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{S}
.

In
[
1
]
, Camacho and Sad proved a remarkable result for holomorphic foliations
\mathcal{F}
on a complex surface
M
. They showed that for every singular point
p\in E
, there exists a separatrix passing through
p
. A separatrix through
p\in E
is a local analytic curve
\mathcal{C}\subset M
, such that
p\in\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathcal{C}
is local
\mathcal{F}-
invariant near
p
. In the above framework, this result implies that for
N=2
, there always exists an invariant submanifold
S\subset M
of dimension 1 containing
p
. However, this property is no longer true in general for higher dimensions
(N>2)
. As demonstrated in
[
11
]
, Gómez and Luengo gave an example of a Riemann surface foliation in
(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
with discrete singular set
E=\{(0,0,0)\}
, where no separatrix exists through singular point
0
. In
[
17
]
, J. Rebelo and H. Reis gave sufficient conditions for the existence of separatrices for a pair of germs of holomorphic vector fields
\{X,Y\}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
.

The question of the existence of separatrices becomes less significant if the discreteness assumption on
E
is dropped. When
p\in E
is not an isolated singular point of
\mathcal{F}
, one can always construct an invariant analytic curve
\mathcal{C}
passing through
p
. This follows from the facts that
E
is an analytic subset and
\text{dim}(E)\geq 1
at
p
, and one can take an analytic curve completely contained in
E
passing through
p
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular Riemann surface foliation on a complex manifold
M
, with singular set
E
of dimension
k
, where
1\leq k\leq N-2
. For
1\leq l\leq k
and a local
\mathcal{F}-
invariant submanifold
\Sigma
of dimension
l+1
, a singular point
p\in E\cap\Sigma
is called a
weakly removable singularity of order
l
w.r.t.
\Sigma
, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\Sigma\cap E
is of dimension
l
,

(2)

p
is a removable singularity of the subfoliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma}
, i.e.,
p\notin\hat{E}_{\Sigma}
.

The set of all
weakly removable singularities of order
l
w.r.t.
\Sigma
is denoted by
A_{l,\Sigma}
. A point
p\in E
is called a
weakly removable singularity of order
l
if
p\in A_{l,\Sigma}
for some
\Sigma
. The collection of all such singularities is denoted by
A_{l}
.

Definition 1.2
.

A singular point
p\in E
is called a
weakly removable singularity of order
0
if there exists an analytic curve (local)
\mathcal{C}
passing through
p
such that
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{p\}
is contained in a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
. The set of all
weakly removable singularities of order
0
of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is denoted by
A_{0}
.

Remark:
From the above definition, it is evident that the points in
A_{0}
exhibit a separatrix away from the singular set
E
. This property is stronger than simply requiring the existence of a separatrix, as we have already noted that separatrices always exist if
E
has no
0
-dimensional component. This distinction also clarifies why the set
A_{0}
is defined slightly differently from
A_{l}
for
l\geq 1
. The motivation for this distinction lies in the study of the leafwise Poincaré metric. As shown in Proposition 1.10 of
[
10
]
, the points in
A_{0}
are precisely those that obstruct the continuity of this metric.

Example 1.3
.

Let
M=\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the Riemann surface foliation on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x,y,z)=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+zy\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

The singular set of
\mathcal{F}
is
E=\{(0,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:yz=0\}=\{y-\text{axis}\}\cup\{z-\text{axis}\}
. Therefore,
E
is
1
-dimensional and has no components of dimension
0
. Consider the hyperplane
\Sigma_{1}=\{x=0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
. One can see that
\Sigma_{1}
is invariant under
X
, and therefore
\Sigma_{1}
is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant. Note that
\Sigma_{1}\cap E=E
and

X|_{\Sigma_{1}}(0,y,z)=0\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+zy\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=zy(0\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z})=yz\tilde{X}(y,z),

where
\tilde{X}(y,z)=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
is a holomorphic vector field on
\Sigma_{1}
. By definition, the singular set of a foliation must be atleast codimension
2
, therefore the foliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma_{1}}
on
\Sigma_{1}
is induced by the vector field
\tilde{X}
. Since
\tilde{X}
is non-vanishing, therefore every
p\in E=\Sigma_{1}\cap E
is a removable singularity of
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma_{1}}
. Therefore, every
p\in E
is a weakly removable singularity of order
1
and thus
A_{1}=E
.

Now let
p=(0,c,0)\in E
. Consider the submanifold
\Sigma_{p}=\{(x,y,z):z=0,y=c\}
, and note that
\Sigma_{p}
is
1-
dimensional,
\Sigma_{p}\cap E=\{p\}
and is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant (
\Sigma_{p}\setminus\{p\}
is a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
). Therefore,
p
is a weakly removable singularity of order
0
, that is,
p\in A_{0}
. Similarly we can consider the submanifold
\Sigma_{q}=\{(x,y,z):y=0,z=c\}
corresponding to the singular point
q=(0,0,c)\in E
. Thus,
A_{0}=A_{1}=E
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
M=\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the Riemann surface foliation on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x,y,z)=2zy\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+3x^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

The singular set of
\mathcal{F}
is
E=\{(0,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:yz=0\}=\{y-\text{axis}\}\cup\{z-\text{axis}\}
. Therefore
E
is
1
-dimensional and has no components of dimension
0
. Observe that
\Sigma_{0}=\{z=0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant, and

(1.1)

X|_{\Sigma_{0}}(x,y,0)=0\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+3x^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=3x^{2}\tilde{X}(x,y),

where
\tilde{X}(x,y)=0\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
is a holomorphic vector field defined on
\Sigma_{0}
. Since
\Sigma_{0}\cap E=\{x=z=0\}=\{y-\text{axis}\}
and
\tilde{X}
is non-vanishing, therefore
\{y-\text{axis}\}\subset A_{1}
. Now suppose if possible
(0,0,z_{0})\in A_{1}
for some
z_{0}\neq 0
. Therefore, by definition, there exists a local
\mathcal{F}-
invariant hypersurface
\Sigma
such that
\Sigma\cap E\subset\{z-\text{axis}\}
is 1-dimensional and
(0,0,z_{0})\in\Sigma\cap E
is a removable singularity of the subfoliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma}
. Also note that the hyperplane
\Sigma_{z_{0}}=\{z=z_{0}\}
is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant and
\Sigma_{z_{0}}\cap E=\{(0,0,z_{0})\}
. On
\Sigma_{z_{0}}
, the foliation is induced by

X|_{\Sigma_{z_{0}}}(x,y,z_{0})=2z_{0}y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+3x^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

Observe that the foliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma_{z_{0}}}
has exactly one separatrix passing through the singular point
(0,0,z_{0})\in E
which is given by the curve
\{(x,y,z_{0})\in\Sigma_{z_{0}}:x^{3}=z_{0}y^{2}\}
. Since
\Sigma\cap E\subset\{z-\text{axis}\}
is 1-dimensional and
\Sigma_{z_{0}}\cap E=\{(0,0,z_{0})\}
, therefore
\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{z_{0}}
will be 1-dimensional submanifold, and it would be
\mathcal{F}-
invariant. From the above discussion and the fact that
\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{z_{0}}\cap E=\{(0,0,z_{0})\}
, we get that the analytic curve
\mathcal{C}_{z_{0}}=\{(x,y,z_{0})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:x^{3}=z_{0}y^{2},x\neq 0\}
is a leaf of the foliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma}
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{z_{0}}
is a separatrix corresponding to the singular point
(0,0,z_{0})\in E
and
\mathcal{C}_{z_{0}}\cup\{(0,0,z_{0})\}
being singular at the point
(0,0,z_{0})
, we get that
(0,0,z_{0})
cannot be a removable singularity of the subfoliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma}
. This is a contradiction to our above assumption. Hence
A_{1}=\{y-\text{axis}\}\subset E
.

Note that
\{z-\text{axis}\}^{\ast}=\{(0,0,z)|\ z\neq 0\}\subset A_{0}
, since for each
p=(0,0,z_{0})\in\{z-\text{axis}\}^{\ast}
, the set
\mathcal{C}=\{(x,y,z_{0})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:x^{3}=z_{0}y^{2},x\neq 0\}
is an analytic curve such that
\mathcal{C}\cap E=\{p\}
and
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{p\}
is a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
. We claim that
A_{0}=\{z-\text{axis}\}^{\ast}
. Suppose if possible
q=(0,y,0)\in A_{0}
. By definition, there exists an invariant analytic curve
\mathcal{C}_{q}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{q}\cap E=\{q\}
. But since
\Sigma_{z_{0}}=\{z=z_{0}\}
is invariant for each
z_{0}
and
q\in\Sigma_{0}
, therefore
\mathcal{C}_{q}\subset\Sigma_{0}
. We already saw from
1.1
that the leaves of
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma_{0}}
are given by
L=\{(x,y,0)|\ x=\text{cste}\}
. Therefore
\mathcal{C}_{q}=\{(x,y,0)|\ x=0\}\subset E
. But this is a contradiction since
\mathcal{C}_{q}\cap E=\{q\}
. Thus
A_{0}=\{z-\text{axis}\}^{\ast}=E\setminus A_{1}
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular Riemann surface foliation on a complex manifold
M
, with singular set
E
of dimension
k
, where
1\leq k\leq N-2
. For
0\leq l\leq k
and a local
\mathcal{F}-
invariant submanifold
\Sigma
of dimension
l+1
, a singular point
p\in E\cap\Sigma
is called a
strongly removable singularity of order
l
w.r.t.
\Sigma
, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\Sigma\cap E
is of dimension
l
,

(2)

p
is a removable singularity of the subfoliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma}
, i.e.,
p\notin\hat{E}_{\Sigma}
,

(3)

the leaf
\hat{L}_{p}
of the saturated foliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma}
satisfies
\hat{L}_{p}\subset E
.

The set of all
strongly removable singularity of order
l
w.r.t.
\Sigma
is denoted by
B_{l,\Sigma}
. A point
p\in E
is called a
strongly removable singularity of order
l
if
p\in B_{l,\Sigma}
for some
\Sigma
. The collection of all such singularities is denoted by
B_{l}
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F},E)
be a singular Riemann surface foliation on a complex manifold
M
and the singular set
E
is of dimension
k
, where
1\leq k\leq N-2
. For
0\leq m\leq k
, define
E_{m}\subset E
as the union of all
m-
dimensional irreducible components of
E
. Note that
E=\cup_{m=0}^{k}E_{m}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular Riemann surface foliation on a complex manifold
M
, with singular set
E
of dimension
k
, where
0\leq k\leq N-2
. Then

(1)

B_{0}=\emptyset
and
B_{l}\subset A_{l}
, for all
0\leq l\leq k
,

(2)

A_{l},B_{l}\subset\cup_{m=l}^{k}E_{m}
, for all
1\leq l\leq k
,

(3)

A_{l}\subset A_{l-1}
, for all
2\leq l\leq k
,

(4)

B_{l}\subset B_{l-1}
, for all
2\leq l\leq k
,

(5)

A_{l}\setminus B_{l}\subset A_{0}
, for all
1\leq l\leq k
.

The following examples illustrate that the inclusions in the above result could very well be proper.

Example 1.7
.

Let
M=\mathbb{C}^{4}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the singular Riemann surface foliation on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x,y,z,w)=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+yz\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+yw\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+xw\frac{\partial}{\partial w}.

The singular set of
\mathcal{F}
is
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
, where
E_{1}=\{(x,y,z,w)|x=z=w=0\}=\{y-\text{axis}\}
, and
E_{2}=\{(x,y,z,w)|x=y=0\}=\{zw-\text{plane}\}
.

(1)

Consider
\Sigma_{xw}=\{x=w=0\}
. Note that
\Sigma_{xw}
is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant,
E\cap\Sigma_{xw}=\{z-\text{axis}\}\cup\{y-\text{axis}\}
and

X|_{\Sigma_{xw}}(0,y,z,0)=0\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+yz\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial w}=yz\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

Therefore, the saturated foliation
\hat{\cal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{xw}}
on
\Sigma_{xw}
is induced by the vector field
\hat{X}_{\Sigma_{xw}}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
, which in turn gives us
\{z-\text{axis}\}\cup\{y-\text{axis}\}\subset A_{1}
,
\{z-\text{axis}\}^{\ast}\subset A_{0}
, and
\{y-\text{axis}\}\subset B_{1}
.

(2)

Next we look at
\Sigma_{xc}=\{x=0,w=c\}
for
c\neq 0
, and check that it is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant,
E\cap\Sigma_{xc}=\{(0,0,z,c)|z\in\mathbb{C}\}
, and

X|_{\Sigma_{xc}}(0,y,z,c)=0\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+yz\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+yc\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial w}=y\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+c\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right).

Again by looking at the saturated foliation in this case we get
(0,0,z,c)\in A_{0}\cap A_{1}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Therefore
A_{1}=E
, and
E_{2}\setminus\{0\}\subset A_{0}
.

(3)

Consider
\Sigma_{zw}=\{z=w=0\}
and note that
\Sigma_{zw}
is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant,
E\cap\Sigma_{zw}=\{(0,y,0,0)|y\in\mathbb{C}\}
, and

X|_{\Sigma_{zw}}(x,y,0,0)=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial w}=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}.

By looking at the saturated foliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{zw}}
, we get
\{y-\text{axis}\}=E_{1}\subset A_{0}
and therefore
A_{0}=E
.

(4)

By Theorem
1.6
,
A_{2}\subset E_{2}\subsetneq E=A_{1}
, which gives
A_{2}\subsetneq A_{1}
. If we look at
\Sigma_{y}=\{y=0\}
, and check that it is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant,
E\cap\Sigma_{y}=E_{2}
, and

X|_{\Sigma_{y}}(x,0,z,w)=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+xw\frac{\partial}{\partial w}=x\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+w\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\right).

The saturated foliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{y}}
satisfies
\text{sing}(\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{y}})=\emptyset
, which gives
E_{2}\subset A_{2}
. Thus
A_{2}=E_{2}
.

(5)

Next we show that
\emptyset\neq B_{1}\subsetneq A_{1}
. We already saw that
E_{1}\subset B_{1}
, therefore
B_{1}\neq\emptyset
. For the other observation, consider a singular point
p=(0,0,z,w)\in E
such that
z\neq 0
. We will prove that
p\not\in B_{1}
. To see this, we claim that it suffices to show that
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at
p
(see the paragraph before Theorem
1.15
for the definition). Observe that if
p\in B_{1}
, we can use the local structure of the subfoliation to conclude that

C_{p}{E}\cap\overline{C_{p}{\mathcal{F}}}\neq\{0\}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{F}
cannot be transversal type at
p\in B_{1}\subset E
. So our claim is verified.

Now note that
C_{p}E=\{(0,0,z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{4}\}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(0,0,1,0),(0,0,0,1)\}
. Let
V=(0,0,v_{3},v_{4})\in C_{p}E\cap C_{p}\mathcal{F}
and
p_{n}=(x_{n},y_{n},z_{n},w_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{4}\setminus E
be a sequence of non-singular points such that
p_{n}\to p
and
V_{n}\in T_{p_{n}}\mathcal{F}
be such that
V_{n}\to V
. Since the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is defined by the holomorphic vector field
X
, there exist
\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
V_{n}=\alpha_{n}X(p_{n})=\alpha_{n}(x_{n},y_{n}z_{n},y_{n}w_{n},x_{n}w_{n})
. Therefore we get

\alpha_{n}x_{n}\to 0,\ \ \alpha_{n}y_{n}z_{n}\to 0,\ \ \alpha_{n}y_{n}w_{n}\to v_{3},\ \ \text{and}\ \ \alpha_{n}x_{n}w_{n}\to v_{4}.

Since
\alpha_{n}x_{n}\to 0
, and
w_{n}\to w
, therefore
\alpha_{n}x_{n}w_{n}\to 0
, that is
v_{4}=0
. Also since
z_{n}\to z\neq 0
and
\alpha_{n}y_{n}z_{n}\to 0
gives us
\alpha_{n}y_{n}\to 0
. Using
w_{n}\to w
, we get
\alpha_{n}y_{n}w_{n}\to 0
and
v_{3}=0
. Therefore
V=\vec{0}
and
C_{p}E\cap C_{p}\mathcal{F}=\{0\}
. Thus,
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at
p
, and therefore

B_{1}\subset\{y-\text{axis}\}\cup\{w-\text{axis}\}\subsetneq A_{1}.

(6)

To see
B_{2}\subsetneq B_{1}
, observe that by Theorem
1.6
,
B_{2}\subset E_{2}\cap B_{1}\subset\{w-\text{axis}\}
and by step
(1)
in this example
\{y-\text{axis}\}\subset B_{1}
.

We now demonstrate the significance of the strongly removable singular points (
B_{l}
) in the framework of singular hyperbolic foliations. In particular, we will use them to study the leafwise Poincaré metric of the corresponding hyperbolic foliation. Recall that a singular holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
M
is called
hyperbolic
if every leaf of
\mathcal{F}
is a hyperbolic Riemann surface. Let
g
be a Hermitian metric on the complex manifold
M
, and denote the length of a tangent vector
v
with respect to
g
by
|v|_{g}
. Define
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},M\setminus E)
as the set of holomorphic maps from the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
into
M\setminus E
. Consider the subset
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\cal{F})\subset\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},M\setminus E)
, consisting of holomorphic maps
f
such that the image
f(\mathbb{D})
lies entirely within the leaf
L_{f(0)}
of
\mathcal{F}
. The modulus of uniformization map
\eta:M\setminus E\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is defined as

\eta(p):=\sup\left\{|f^{\prime}(0)|_{g}:f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F}),f(0)=p\right\}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\eta>0
, and the supremum is achieved by the universal covering map
\pi_{p}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p}
, which satisfies
\pi_{p}(0)=p
. Consequently,
\eta(p)=|\pi^{\prime}_{p}(0)|_{g}
. For a leaf
L\subset\cal{F}
and a tangent vector
v
at a point
p\in L
, let
\lambda_{L}
denote the Poincaré metric on
L
and
|v|_{L}
denote the Poincaré length of
v
. By the extremal property of the Kobayashi metric, we have

(1.2)

|v|_{g}=\eta(p)|v|_{L}.

Hence, the restriction of the metric
g/{\eta}
to a leaf
L
naturally induces the Poincaré metric
\lambda_{L}
on that leaf.

Note that, since the Poincaré metric
\lambda_{L}
for a leaf
L
is positive real-analytic on
L
and
g
is smooth on
M
, therefore
\eta
is also smooth along leaves. However, the regularity of
\eta
along transverse directions is not immediately apparent. In fact, from the above observation, the regularity of
\eta
is connected to the variation of
\lambda_{L}
along such directions. The regularity question of
\eta
under various hypotheses on
M,E
and
\mathcal{F}
has been studied by Verjovsky
[
20
]
, Lins Neto
[
12
,
13
]
, Candel
[
2
]
and Fornaess-Sibony
[
8
]
. A stronger estimate on the modulus of continuity of
\eta
for hyperbolic foliations on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
was recently obtained by Dinh–Nguyên–Sibony
[
5
,
6
]
and Bacher
[
7
]
under suitable hypotheses on
E
. The significance of studying the transverse regularity of the leafwise Poincaré metric has been emphasized in the works of Nguyen (see
[
15
,
16
]
), where it has been applied to investigate the ergodic properties of Riemann surface laminations.

Much of the prior work in this area assumes the singular set
E
to be discrete. In recent studies
[
9
,
10
]
, we explored the problem without imposing the discreteness assumption on the singular set. In
[
10
]
, we provided a sufficient condition for the continuity of the modulus of the uniformization map
\eta
on the non-singular set
M\setminus E
, building on and strengthening a result by Fornaess–Sibony for the case where the singular set is discrete (see Theorem 20,
[
8
]
). Additionally, we discussed the possibility of a continuous extension of
\eta
to the singular set
E
.

Our investigations revealed significant differences when the discreteness assumption on
E
is dropped. For instance, in
[
10
]
, we observed that the continuity of
\eta
on the non-singular part
M\setminus E
does not necessarily imply the existence of a continuous extension to
E
, unlike the case when
E
is discrete. Furthermore, we identified sufficient conditions on the singular set, referred to as being of
transversal type
, which ensure the continuous extension of
\eta
on
E
. These findings underscore the nuanced relationship between the structure of the singular set and the behavior of
\eta
.

The aim of this note is two fold. First, we utilize the strongly removable singular points
(B_{l})
to obtain sufficient conditions for the continuity of
\eta
on
M\setminus E
, in the realm of non-discrete singular set
E
. Second, we improve the observations obtained in
[
10
]
about the continuous extension of
\eta
on
E
. Specifically, we prove that if the
transversal type
property holds in a sufficient large subset of
E
, then we have a continuous extension of
\eta
to whole
M
.

Before presenting the first result, let us recall an essential object relevant to this study. For a leaf
L
of a hyperbolic foliation
\mathcal{F}
, a holomorphic map
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to L
is called a uniformization of
L
if it is a covering map. As discussed in
[
14
]
, the collection of all uniformizations

\mathcal{U}=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})\mid\alpha\ \text{is a uniformization of a leaf of}\ \mathcal{F}\},

plays a crucial role in the analysis of foliations. The space
\mathcal{U}
is said to satisfy NCP (Normal on compact parts) property if, for any subfamily
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{U}
with the set
\{\alpha(0)\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{H}\}
being relatively compact in
M
is itself a normal family. This property is guaranteed, for instance, when
M
is a taut manifold.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F},E)
be a singular hyperbolic foliation on the complex manifold
M
. For
p\in B_{1}
, define

l_{p}:=\text{max}\{j\in\mathbb{N}|\ p\in B_{j}\}.

Note that since
B_{i}\subset B_{j}\subset B_{1}
for all
i\geq j\geq 1
, therefore
B_{j}=\{p\in B_{1}|\ l_{p}\geq j\}
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular hyperbolic foliation on a complex manifold
M
and singular set
E
. If
\mathcal{U}
is NCP,
A_{0}\subset B_{1}
, and for each
p\in A_{0}
, there exists
(N-1-l_{p})
number of local
\mathcal{F}
-invariant hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{1,p},\Sigma_{2,p},\ldots,\Sigma_{N-1-l_{p},p}
such that

\Sigma_{p}=\cap_{i=1}^{N-1-l_{p}}\Sigma_{i,p}

is a
(l_{p}+1)-
dimensional submanifold and
p\in B_{l_{p},\Sigma_{p}}
. Then
\eta
is continuous on
M\setminus E
.

The following corollary is immediate, with
l_{p}=N-2
for all
p\in E
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular hyperbolic foliation on a complex manifold
M
and singular set
E
. If
\mathcal{U}
is NCP, and
A_{0}\subset B_{N-2}
, then
\eta
is continuous on
M\setminus E
.

Example 1.10
.

Let
M=\mathbb{D}^{4}\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the singular hyperbolic foliation on
M
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x,y,z,w)=ye^{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+ye^{w}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+x\frac{\partial}{\partial w}.

The singular set of
\mathcal{F}
is
E=\{(0,0,0,w)|\ w\in\mathbb{D}\}=\{w-\text{axis}\}\cap M=E_{1}
. Since
E_{2}=\emptyset
, therefore
B_{2}=\emptyset
as well. Note that the hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{y}=\{y=0\}
and
\Sigma_{z}=\{z=0\}
are
\mathcal{F}-
invariant. Consider
\Sigma_{yz}=\Sigma_{y}\cap\Sigma_{z}=\{y=z=0\}
. It is easy to check that
\Sigma_{yz}
is an
\mathcal{F}-
invariant submanifold of
M
of dimension
2
, and the subfoliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma_{yz}}
is induced by

X|_{\Sigma_{yz}}(x,0,0,w)=x\frac{\partial}{\partial w}.

Clearly the saturated subfoliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{yz}}
is non-singular, and in fact
\{(0,0,0,w)|\ w\in\mathbb{D}\}
is a leaf of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{yz}}
. Therefore
E\subset B_{1,\Sigma_{yz}}
, which in turn gives that
A_{0}\subset B_{1}=E
. Now one can use Theorem
1.8
to deduce the continuity of
\eta
on
M\setminus E
.

The following example is motivated from
[
18
]
(see Theorem 1).

Example 1.11
.

Let
M=\mathbb{D}^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the singular hyperbolic foliation on
M
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x,y,z)=(P(y)+z\tilde{f}(x,y,z))\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+z\tilde{g}(x,y,z)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+z^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial z},

where
P(y)
is a polynomial of degree
k\geq 1
, satisfying
P(0)=0
. Suppose
S=\{0,c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k-1}\}
be the set of zeroes of
P
. The singular set of
\mathcal{F}
is

E=\{(x,0,0)|\ x\in\mathbb{D}\}\cup(\bigcup_{i,|c_{i}|<1}\{(x,c_{i},0)|\ x\in\mathbb{D}\}).

Consider the
\mathcal{F}-
invariant hypersurface
\Sigma_{z}=\{z=0\}
. On
\Sigma_{z}
,

X|_{\Sigma_{z}}(x,y,0)=P(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}.

It is now clear that the saturated subfoliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{z}}
is non-singular on
\Sigma_{z}
, and
\{(x,c,0)|\ x\in\mathbb{D}\}
is a leaf of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{z}}
for all
c\in S\cap\mathbb{D}
. Therefore
B_{1}=E
, and by Corollary
1.9
we get the continuity of
\eta
on
M\setminus E
.

Example 1.12
.

Let
M=\mathbb{D}^{N}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the singular hyperbolic foliation on
M
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})=x^{R_{1}}x_{N}^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}{x^{R_{i}}x_{i-1}^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}},

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\in M
,
R_{i}=(a_{1,i},a_{2,i},\ldots,a_{N,i})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
be such that
a_{i-1,i}=1
for
i\geq 2
and
a_{N,1}=1
, and
x^{R_{i}}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}{{x_{j}}^{a_{j,i}}}
. The singular set of
\mathcal{F}
is
E=\cup_{i\neq j}\{x_{i}=x_{j}=0\}=E_{N-2}
. Check that
\Sigma_{i}=\{x_{i}=0\}
is
\mathcal{F}-
invariant for all
1\leq i\leq N
, and the subfoliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq N-1
, is induced by

X|_{\Sigma_{i}}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1},0,x_{i+1},\ldots x_{N})=x^{R_{i+1}}x_{i}^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i+1}}.

It is evident from above that the saturated subfoliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{i}}
is non-singular, and in fact
\{x_{i}=x_{j}=0\}\subset B_{N-2,\Sigma_{i}}\subset B_{N-2}
for all
j\neq i,i+1
. But with the same argument
\{x_{i}=x_{i+1}=0\}\subset B_{N-2,\Sigma_{i+1}}
.

Similar calculations on
\Sigma_{N}
will give us
E=B_{N-2}
, and then Corollary
1.9
give us the continuity of
\eta
on
M\setminus E
.

The next theorem establishes the continuity of the modulus of uniformization map
\eta
on
M\setminus E
by leveraging Theorem
1.8
and Corollary 1.13 in
[
10
]
. To achieve this, we introduce two key properties of singular points that have been used in the above mentioned results. We say

•

p\in E
satisfies property
(L)
if
p\in B_{1}
, and there exist
(N-1-l_{p})
number of local
\mathcal{F}
-invariant hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{1,p},\Sigma_{2,p},\ldots,\Sigma_{N-1-l_{p},p}
such that

\Sigma_{p}=\cap_{i=1}^{N-1-l_{p}}\Sigma_{i,p}

is a
(l_{p}+1)-
dimensional submanifold and
p\in B_{l_{p},\Sigma_{p}}
,

•

p\in E
satisfies property
(M)
if there exist
(N-k)
number of local
\mathcal{F}-
invariant hypersurfaces
\tilde{\Sigma}_{1,p},\tilde{\Sigma}_{2,p},\ldots,\tilde{\Sigma}_{N-k,p}
such that

p\in\cap_{i=1}^{N-k}{\tilde{\Sigma}_{i,p}}\subset E,

where
k=\text{dim}_{p}E
.

We define
E_{L}:=\{p\in E:p\ \text{satisfies property}\ (L)\}
and
E_{M}:=\{p\in E:p\ \text{satisfies property}\ (M)\}
. Note that the continuity of
\eta
is ensured if either
A_{0}\subset E_{L}
(by Theorem
1.8
) or
E=E_{M}
(by Corollary 1.13 in
[
10
]
).

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular hyperbolic foliation on a complex manifold
M
and singular set
E
. If
\mathcal{U}
is NCP, and
A_{0}\subset E_{L}\cup E_{M}
, then the modulus of uniformization map
\eta
is continuous on
M\setminus E
.

Example 1.14
.

Let
M=\mathbb{D}^{4}\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the singular hyperbolic foliation on
M
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x,y,z,w)=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+yz\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+yw\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+xw\frac{\partial}{\partial w}.

The singular set of
\mathcal{F}
is
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
, where
E_{1}=\{(x,y,z,w)\in M|x=z=w=0\}=\{y-\text{axis}\}
, and
E_{2}=\{(x,y,z,w)\in M|x=y=0\}=\{zw-\text{plane}\}
.

We already observed in Example
1.7
, that
A_{0}=E
, and
E_{1}\subset B_{1}\subset E_{1}\cup\{(0,0,0,w)|w\in\mathbb{D}\}
. For
p\in E_{1}
, we can check that
l_{p}=1
and
p\in B_{1,\Sigma_{xw}}
, where
\Sigma_{xw}=\{x=w=0\}=\{x=0\}\cap\{w=0\}=\Sigma_{x}\cap\Sigma_{w}
. Since
\Sigma_{x},\Sigma_{w}
are
\mathcal{F}-
invariant, therefore
E_{1}\subset E_{L}
.

Recall that
\text{dim}_{p}E=2
for
p\in E_{2}
, and
E_{2}=\{x=0\}\cap\{y=0\}=\Sigma_{x}\cap\Sigma_{y}
. Once again, using the fact that
\Sigma_{x},\Sigma_{y}
are
\mathcal{F}-
invariant, we get
E_{2}\subset E_{M}
.

Therefore we get
A_{0}=E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}\subset E_{L}\cup E_{M}
, that is

A_{0}=E_{L}\cup E_{M}.

Thus, by Theorem
1.13
, the map
\eta
is continuous on
M\setminus E
.

Next we talk about the extension of the map
\eta
to the singular set
E
. In
[
10
]
(Theorem 1.8), it was proved that being
transversal type
is a sufficient condition for the continuous extension of
\eta
to the singular set. Recall that, a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at a singular point
p\in E
if there exists a neighborhood
U_{p}
of
p
such that for all
q\in U_{p}\cap E
,

\overline{C_{q}\mathcal{F}}\cap C_{q}E=\{0\},

where
C_{p}E=\{v\in T_{p}M:\text{there exists }\{q_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\text{reg}(E),\ v_{n}\in T_{q_{n}}E\ \text{such that}\ (q_{n},v_{n})\to(p,v)\}
is the Whitney’s
C_{4}-
tangent cone (see
[
4
]
), and
C_{p}\mathcal{F}=\{v\in T_{p}M:\text{there exists }\{q_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset M\setminus E,\ v_{n}\in T_{q_{n}}\mathcal{F}\ \text{such that}\ (q_{n},v_{n})\to(p,v)\}
.

Theorem 1.15
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular hyperbolic foliation on a complex manifold
M
and singular set
E
. Suppose that
\mathcal{U}
is NCP and
\eta
is continuous on
M\setminus E
. If the set
E\setminus\{p\in E|\ \mathcal{F}\ \text{is transversal type at}\ p\}
is discrete, then
\eta
has a continuous extension to whole
M
.

Example 1.16
.

Let
M=\mathbb{D}^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the singular hyperbolic foliation on
M
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x,y,z)=x(y+z)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y(x+z)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+z(x+y)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

The singular set of
\mathcal{F}
is
E=\{(x,0,0)|\ x\in\mathbb{D}\}\cup\{(0,y,0)|\ y\in\mathbb{D}\}\cup\{(0,0,z)|\ z\in\mathbb{D}\}
. Using the fact that
\Sigma_{x}=\{x=0\},\Sigma_{y}=\{y=0\}
and
\Sigma_{z}=\{z=0\}
are
\mathcal{F}-
invariant and
\{(x,0,0)|\ x\in\mathbb{D}\}=\Sigma_{y}\cap\Sigma_{z}
,
\{(0,y,0)|\ y\in\mathbb{D}\}=\Sigma_{x}\cap\Sigma_{z}
and
\{(0,0,z)|\ z\in\mathbb{D}\}=\Sigma_{x}\cap\Sigma_{z}
, we get
E=E_{M}
. Therefore, Theorem
1.13
gives us the continuity of
\eta
on
M\setminus E
.

Next we prove that
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at each
p\in E\setminus\{0\}
. Let
p=(x,0,0)\in E
be such that
x\neq 0
and
V=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in C_{p}E
. Since
C_{p}E=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(1,0,0)\}
, therefore
v_{2}=v_{3}=0
. Suppose
p_{n}=(x_{n},y_{n},z_{n})\in M\setminus E
and
V_{n}\in T_{p_{n}}\mathcal{F}
be such that
(p_{n},V_{n})\to(p,V)
. Write
V_{n}=\alpha_{n}X(p_{n})=\alpha_{n}\left(x_{n}(y_{n}+z_{n}),y_{n}(x_{n}+z_{n}),z_{n}(x_{n}+y_{n})\right)
for some
\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. We get

(1.3)

\alpha_{n}x_{n}(y_{n}+z_{n})\to v_{1},\ \alpha_{n}y_{n}(x_{n}+z_{n})\to 0,\ \&\ \alpha_{n}z_{n}(x_{n}+y_{n})\to 0.

Since
(x_{n}+z_{n}),(x_{n}+y_{n})\to x\neq 0
, we get from
1.3
that
\alpha_{n}y_{n},\alpha_{n}z_{n}\to 0
. Therefore

\alpha_{n}x_{n}(y_{n}+z_{n})=x_{n}(\alpha_{n}y_{n}+\alpha_{n}z_{n})\to x(0+0)=0=v_{1}.

Thus
V=(0,0,0)
and
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at
p
. Exactly similar calculations will prove that
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at each
p\in E\setminus\{0\}
.

For
p=(0,0,0)\in E
, note that

C_{0}E=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(1,0,0)\}\cup\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(0,1,0)\}\cup\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(0,0,1)\}.

Fix
V=(1,0,0)\in C_{0}E
. Consider
p_{n}=\left(\frac{1}{n},\frac{-1}{n},\frac{-1}{n}\right)\in M\setminus E
, and
\alpha_{n}=\frac{-n^{2}}{2}
. We have
p_{n}\to 0
and

V_{n}=\alpha_{n}X(p_{n})=\frac{-n^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{-1}{n}+\frac{-1}{n}\right),\frac{-1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{-1}{n}\right),\frac{-1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{-1}{n}\right)\right)=(1,0,0)=V.

We get
(p_{n},V_{n})\to(0,V)
for
V=(1,0,0)\neq 0
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}
is not transversal type at
0\in E
. But Theorem
1.15
tells us that there is still a continuous extension of
\eta
on
M
.

near

, that is
E\cap U=\{z\in U:f_{1}(z)=f_{2}(z)=\ldots=f_{r}(z)=0\}
. For
1\leq j\leq r
, consider the map
f_{j}\circ\alpha:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
. Since
p_{n}=\alpha(q_{n})\in E\cap U
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, therefore
f_{j}\circ\alpha(q_{n})=0
. By Identity theorem,
f_{j}\circ\alpha\equiv 0
, for all
1\leq j\leq r
. This tells us that
f_{j}(z)=0
for all
z\in\mathcal{C}
and
1\leq j\leq r
. Therefore
\alpha(\mathbb{D})=\mathcal{C}\subset E\cap U\subset E\cap\Sigma
, which is a contradiction.

Thus, we can choose
U
to be small enough so that
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{p\}
is contained in a leaf of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
, and so
p\in A_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{U}
be a sequence of uniformizations of leaves of
\mathcal{F}
such that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha
on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, where
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\to M
is holomorphic. Suppose
p=\alpha(0)\in E
. The following result (Theorem 1.7 in
[
10
]
) tells us that it suffices to prove
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
, to achieve the continuity of
\eta
on
M\setminus E
.

Result 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular hyperbolic foliation on a complex manifold
M
, with singular set
E\subset M
. Also, let
g
be a given hermitian metric on
M
, and
\eta
be the modulus of uniformization map of
\mathcal{F}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{U}
is NCP. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\eta
is continuous in
M\setminus E
.

(2)

For any sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{U}
, which converges on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to some
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow M
, and
p=\alpha(0)\notin E
, then
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p}
, where
L_{p}
is the leaf of
\mathcal{F}
passing through
p
.

(3)

For any sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{U}
, which converges on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to some
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow M
, and
p=\alpha(0)\notin E
, then
\alpha
is a uniformization of
L_{p}
.

(4)

For any sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{U}
, which converges on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to some
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow M
, and
p=\alpha(0)\in E
, then
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
.

Next, we use the analyticity of
E
and holomorphicity of the map
\alpha
to prove that the condition
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
is equivalent to a much weaker condition, that is,
\alpha^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
has an accumulation point in
\mathbb{D}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular holomorphic foliation on a complex manifold
M
, with singular set
E\subset M
. Let
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\to M
be a holomorphic map. Then
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
if and only if
\alpha^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
has an accumulation point in
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

If
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
, it is clear that
\alpha^{-1}(E)=\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{D}
has every point in
\mathbb{D}
as an accumulation point. So, the only non-trivial part is the converse. Suppose
\alpha^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
has an accumulation point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
. Since
E\subset M
is closed and
\alpha
is holomorphic, therefore
\alpha^{-1}(E)
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore
z_{0}\in\alpha^{-1}(E)
. Let
z_{n}\in\alpha^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
be a sequence of points such that
z_{n}\to z_{0}
. Define
p_{0}:=\alpha(z_{0})\in E
and
p_{n}:=\alpha(z_{n})\in E
. Since
E
is an analytic set, there exists a neighborhood
U\subset M
of
p_{0}
and holomorphic functions
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{s}\in\mathcal{O}(U)
for some
s\geq 2
, such that
E\cap U=\{p\in U:f_{1}(p)=f_{2}(p)=\ldots=f_{s}(p)=0\}
. Choose
r>0
be small enough that
\alpha(D(z_{0},r))\subset U
. Consider the holomorphic functions
f_{1}\circ\alpha|_{D(z_{0},r)},f_{2}\circ\alpha|_{D(z_{0},r)},\ldots,f_{s}\circ\alpha|_{D(z_{0},r)}\in\mathcal{O}(D(z_{0},r))
. Since
z_{n}\to z_{0}
, there exist
N_{1}\geq 1
such that
z_{n}\in D(z_{0},r)
for all
n\geq N_{1}
. Note that for
1\leq i\leq s
, and for all
n\geq N_{1}

(f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(z_{0},r)})(z_{n})=(f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(z_{0},r)})(z_{0})=0.

By Identity theorem, we get
f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(z_{0},r)}\equiv 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Therefore,
D(z_{0},r)\subset\alpha^{-1}(E\cap U)\subset\alpha^{-1}(E)
. Let
\hat{D}\subset\mathbb{D}
be the maximal closed connected subset such that
z_{0}\in\hat{D}
and
\hat{D}\subset\alpha^{-1}(E)
. We already proved that
\hat{D}\neq\{z_{0}\}
, in fact, the interior
\text{int}(\hat{D})\neq\emptyset
. We claim
\hat{D}=\mathbb{D}
. Suppose not, then there exists an accumulation point
\hat{z}\in\mathbb{D}
of the set
\hat{D}
which satisfies
\hat{z}\in\partial{\hat{D}}
. By the closedness of the set
\hat{D}
,
\hat{z}\in\hat{D}
. Using the above argument, we observe that there exists
r>0
such that
\alpha(D(\hat{z},r))\subset E
. Consider the set
\tilde{D}=\hat{D}\cup\overline{D(\hat{z},\frac{r}{2})}\subset\mathbb{D}
, and note that
\tilde{D}
is a closed connected subset of
\mathbb{D}
such that
z_{0}\in\tilde{D}
and
\tilde{D}\subset\alpha^{-1}(E)
. By the maximality of
\hat{D}
, we get
\hat{D}=\tilde{D}
. But this contradicts the fact that
\hat{z}\in\partial{\hat{D}}
, since
\hat{z}\in\text{int}(\tilde{D})
. Therefore,
\hat{D}=\mathbb{D}
, and thus
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
.
∎

The next proposition along with Result
3.1
and Lemma
3.1
will conclude the proof.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular hyperbolic foliation on a complex manifold
M
and singular set
E
. Suppose that
\mathcal{U}
is NCP,
A_{0}\subset B_{1}
, and for each
p\in A_{0}
, there exist
(N-1-l_{p})
number of local
\mathcal{F}
-invariant hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{1,p},\Sigma_{2,p},\ldots,\Sigma_{N-1-l_{p},p}
such that
\Sigma_{p}=\cap_{i=1}^{N-1-l_{p}}\Sigma_{i,p}
is a
(l_{p}+1)-
dimensional submanifold and
p\in B_{l_{p},\Sigma_{p}}
. If
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{U}
is a sequence of uniformizations which converges on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to some
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow M
, and
p=\alpha(0)\in E
, then there exists
r>0
such that
\alpha(D(0,r))\subset E
.

Proof.

Since
p=\alpha(0)\in E
, there are only two possibilities, either
p\notin A_{0}
or
p\in A_{0}
.

(a) Suppose
p\notin A_{0}
. If
0\in\alpha^{-1}(p)\subset\mathbb{D}
is an isolated point of the set
\alpha^{-1}(E)
, then there exists
r_{1}>0
such that
\alpha(D(0,r_{1})\setminus\{0\})\subset M\setminus E
. By Lemma 1.6 in
[
10
]
(page 96), we get
\alpha(D(0,r_{1})\setminus\{0\})\subset L
, where
L
is a leaf of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}=\alpha(D(0,r_{1}))
is a local separatrix through
p\in E
, and
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{p\}\subset M\setminus E
, contradicting the fact that
p\notin A_{0}
. Therefore,
0\in\mathbb{D}
is an accumulation point of the set
\alpha^{-1}(E)
. By the arguments used in the proof of the Lemma
3.1
, there exists
r>0
such that
\alpha(D(0,r))\subset E
.

(b) Now suppose
p\in A_{0}
. By hypothesis,
p\in B_{l_{p},\Sigma_{p}}
, where
p\in\Sigma_{p}=\cap_{i=1}^{N-1-l_{p}}\Sigma_{i,p}
and
\Sigma_{i,p}
’s are analytic local
\mathcal{F}-
invariant hypersurfaces, for
1\leq i\leq N-1-l_{p}
. Choose a small enough neighborhood
U
of
p
such that for
1\leq i\leq N-1-l_{p}
,

\Sigma_{i,p}=\{f_{i}=0\},

where
f_{i}\in\mathcal{O}(U)
. Since
p\in U
and
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha
on the compacts subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, there exists
r_{2}>0
and
N_{1}\geq 1
such that
\alpha_{n}(D(0,r_{2})),\alpha(D(0,r_{2}))\subset U
for all
n\geq N_{1}
. We claim that
\alpha(D(0,r_{2}))\subset\Sigma_{i,p}
for all
1\leq i\leq N-1-l_{p}
. Fix
1\leq i\leq N-1-l_{p}
. Consider for
n\geq N_{1}
, the set
\alpha_{n}(D(0,r_{2}))\subset U
. Since
\Sigma_{i,p}
is an
\mathcal{F}-
invariant hypersurface and
\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{U}
, therefore either
\alpha_{n}(D(0,r_{2}))\subset\Sigma_{i,p}
or
\alpha_{n}(D(0,r_{2}))\cap\Sigma_{i,p}=\emptyset
.

If there is a subsequence
\{\alpha_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
of
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq N_{1}}
such that for all
k\geq 1
,

\alpha_{n_{k}}(D(0,r_{2}))\subset\Sigma_{i,p},

that is,
f_{i}\circ\alpha_{n_{k}}|_{D(0,r_{2})}\equiv 0
, then
f_{i}\circ\alpha_{n_{k}}|_{D(0,r_{2})}\to f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(0,r_{2})}\equiv 0
. Therefore, we get
\alpha(D(0,r_{2}))\subset\Sigma_{i,p}
.

If there does not exists such subsequence, then there exists
N_{2}\geq 1
such that for all
n\geq N_{2}
,

\alpha_{n}(D(0,r_{2}))\cap\Sigma_{i,p}=\emptyset,

that is
f_{i}\circ\alpha_{n}|_{D(0,r_{2})}(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in D(0,r_{2})
. But
f_{i}\circ\alpha_{n}|_{D(0,r_{2})}\to f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(0,r_{2})}
uniformly on
D(0,r_{2})
, and
f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(0,r_{2})}(0)=f_{i}(p)=0
. Therefore, Hurwitz’s theorem tells us that

f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(0,r_{2})}\equiv 0,

that is
\alpha(D(0,r_{2}))\subset\Sigma_{i,p}
. So we have our claim that
\alpha(D(0,r_{2}))\subset\Sigma_{i,p}
, for all
1\leq i\leq N-1-l_{p}
. Therefore,
\alpha(D(0,r_{2}))\subset\Sigma_{p}=\cap_{i=1}^{N-1-l_{p}}{\Sigma_{i,p}}
.

Recall that
\Sigma_{p}
is a
(l_{p}+1)-
dimensional
\mathcal{F}-
invariant submanifold, and
p\in B_{l_{p},\Sigma_{p}}
. We now consider the saturated foliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{p}}
of
\Sigma_{p}
. By definition of the set
B_{l_{p},\Sigma_{p}}
, the leaf
\hat{L}_{p}\subset E\cap\Sigma_{p}\subset E
. Suppose if possible
0\in\alpha^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
is an isolated point of the set
\alpha^{-1}(E)
. Then there exists
r_{3}>0
such that
\alpha(D(0,r_{3})\setminus\{0\})\subset\Sigma_{p}\setminus E
. Again by Lemma 1.6 (in
[
10
]
),

\alpha(D(0,r_{3})\setminus\{0\})\subset L,

where
L
is a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
. Therefore,
\alpha(D(0,r_{3}))
is an invariant curve of the foliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma_{p}}
, which in turn tells us that it is also invariant under the saturated foliation
\hat{\mathcal{F}}|_{\Sigma_{p}}
. Thus,
\alpha(D(0,r_{3}))\subset\hat{L}_{p}
. Since
\hat{L}_{p}\subset E
, we have

\alpha(D(0,r_{3}))\subset E.

This is a contradiction to the assumption that
0\in\alpha^{-1}(E)
is an isolated point. Thus,
0\in\alpha^{-1}(E)
is an accumulation point and therefore there exists
r>0
such that
\alpha(D(0,r))\subset E
.
∎

, it suffices to prove that if
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{U}

\mathcal{F}
such that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha
on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}

Proof of Theorem
1.15

Consider the map
\tilde{\eta}:M\to[0,\infty)
given by

\tilde{\eta}(p):=\begin{cases}\eta(p),&\quad\text{if}\quad p\in M\setminus E\\
0,&\quad\text{if}\quad p\in E.\\
\end{cases}

We will prove that
\tilde{\eta}
is continuous on
M
. Since
\eta
is continuous on
M\setminus E
, it suffices to check the continuity of
\tilde{\eta}
on the set
E
.

Let
p\in E
be an arbitrary singular point of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
. If
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at
p
, then Theorem 1.8 in
[
10
]
(see page 96) tells us that
\tilde{\eta}
is continuous at
p
. So, we assume that
\mathcal{F}
is not transversal type at
p\in E
. Suppose, if possible, that
\tilde{\eta}
is not continuous at
p
. Then there exist
\epsilon>0
, and a sequence
\{p_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset M\setminus E
of non-singular points of
\mathcal{F}
such that
p_{n}\to p
, and

(5.1)

\tilde{\eta}(p_{n})=\eta(p_{n})>\epsilon,

for all
n\geq 1
. For
n\geq 1
, let
\alpha_{n}:\mathbb{D}\to L_{p_{n}}
be a uniformization of the leaf
L_{p_{n}}
such that
\alpha_{n}(0)=p_{n}
, that is
\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{U}
. Since
\alpha_{n}(0)=p_{n}\to p
, and
\mathcal{U}
is NCP, therefore upto a subsequence we can assume that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha
uniformly on the compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, where
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\to M
is some holomorphic map satisfying
\alpha(0)=p\in E
. Using Result
3.1
, we get
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
. We claim that
\alpha
is a constant map, that is,
\alpha\equiv p
. Assuming the above claim, we get
\alpha^{\prime}(0)=0
. Since
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, therefore
\alpha_{n}^{\prime}(z)\to\alpha^{\prime}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. In particular,

\tilde{\eta}(p_{n})=\eta(p_{n})=|\alpha_{n}^{\prime}(0)|_{g}\to|\alpha^{\prime}(0)|_{g}=0.

So, there exists
N_{1}\geq 1
such that
\tilde{\eta}(p_{n})<\epsilon
, for all
n\geq N_{1}
. But this contradicts
5.1
, and thus
\tilde{\eta}
is continuous at

.

So, it suffices to prove our claim that
\alpha\equiv p
. Suppose not, then
\alpha^{-1}(\{p\})\subset\mathbb{D}
cannot have accumulation points in
\mathbb{D}
, and
\alpha^{\prime}\not\equiv 0
. Therefore, there exists
r>0
such that for all

, we have
\alpha(z)\in E\setminus\{p\}
, and
\alpha^{\prime}(z)\neq 0
. Since, the set
E\setminus\{q\in E:\mathcal{F}\ \text{is transversal type at}\ q\}
is a discrete subset of
M
, we can choose
r>0
small enough such that
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at each
q\in\alpha(D(0,r)\setminus\{0\})
. Now, observe that for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

(5.2)

\alpha^{\prime}(z)\in C_{\alpha(z)}{E}.

This is due to the fact that
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
. Also, since
\alpha_{n}^{\prime}(z)\in T_{\alpha_{n}(z)}{\mathcal{F}}
for all

, and
\alpha_{n}^{\prime}(z)\to\alpha^{\prime}(z)

(5.3)

\alpha^{\prime}(z)\in C_{\alpha(z)}{\mathcal{F}},

for all

. Combining equations

Remark 1:
One can look at
[
3
]
and
[
19
]
, where sufficient conditions have been provided for the existence of local invariant submanifolds passing through singular points of a foliation. Combined with a suitable condition on the dimension of the singular set, one can establish

for such foliations. Furthermore, Salas
[
19
]
(see Theorem 2.6, page 736) demonstrated that under certain conditions on the singular set, almost every point

belongs to

, implying that
E_{N-2}\cap A_{0}\cap A_{N-2}
has full measure in
E_{N-2}
. Investigating these conditions further could also provide insight into the structure of the sets

.

Example 6.1
.

Let
M=\mathbb{D}^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the singular hyperbolic foliation on
M
induced by the holomorphic vector field

X(x,y,z)=(y^{2}-x^{3})\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+(y^{2}-x^{3})\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+x\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

It is easy to see that
\text{sing}(\mathcal{F})=E=\{(0,0,z):z\in\mathbb{D}\}
. Consider the hypersurface
\Sigma:=\{(x,y,z)\in M:y^{2}=x^{3}\}
. Note that for
p=(x,y,z)\in\Sigma
,

X(p)=0\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+0\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+x\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=x\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\in T_{p}{\Sigma}.

Therefore
\cup_{p\in\Sigma}{T_{p}{\mathcal{F}}}=T\mathcal{F}|_{\Sigma}\subset\cup_{q\in\Sigma}{T_{q}{\Sigma}}
, and thus
\Sigma
is an
\mathcal{F}-
invariant hypersurface. One can easily check that if
p_{0}=(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})\in\Sigma\setminus E
, that is
x_{0}\neq 0
, then the corresponding leaf
L_{p_{0}}
is given by

L_{p_{0}}=\{(x_{0},y_{0},z):z\in\mathbb{D}\}.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{U}
be a sequence of uniformizations of leaves of
\mathcal{F}
such that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, where
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\to M
is holomorphic and
\alpha(0)=\hat{p}=(0,0,\hat{z})\in E
. Since
\hat{p}\in E\subset\Sigma
, we can use the argument in the proof of Proposition
3.2
to prove that
\alpha(D(0,r))\subset\Sigma
for some
r>0
.

If the set
\alpha^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
has an accumulation point in
\mathbb{D}
, then we can choose
r>0
small enough so that
\alpha(D(0,r))\subset E
. This will further imply that
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
. Using Result
3.1
, we get the continuity of the map
\eta
on
M\setminus E
.

Suppose if possible, that the set
\alpha^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
has no accumulation point in
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, we can choose
r>0
small enough so that
\alpha(D(0,r)\setminus\{0\})\subset M\setminus E
. Lemma 1.6 (in
[
10
]
) tells us that
\alpha(D(0,r)\setminus\{0\})\subset L
, for some leaf
L
of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Let
\xi\in D(0,r)\setminus\{0\}
, and consider
\alpha(\xi)=(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})\in\Sigma\setminus E
. Clearly
x_{1}\neq 0
, and
L_{\alpha(\xi)}=\{(x_{1},y_{1},z):z\in\mathbb{D}\}
. Since
\alpha(D(0,r)\setminus\{0\})\subset L
, therefore

. Thus we get

\alpha(D(0,r)\setminus\{0\})\subset L_{\alpha(\xi)}=\{(x_{1},y_{1},z):z\in\mathbb{D}\},

where
x_{1}\neq 0
. This is a contradiction to the fact that
\alpha|_{D(0,r)}:D(0,r)\to M
is holomorphic and
\alpha(0)=(0,0,\hat{z})\in E
. Therefore, the set
\alpha^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
must have an accumulation point in
\mathbb{D}
. Thus, the modulus of uniformization map
\eta
is continuous on

.

Remark 2:

We observe a fundamental distinction in the definitions of the sets
A_{0}
, and
A_{l}
for
l\geq 1
, as given in the Definitions
1.2
and
1.1
, respectively. In the case of
A_{0}
, the condition requires the existence of an invariant analytic curve, which may itself exhibit singularities as an analytic variety at the singular points of the foliation. However, for
A_{l}
with
l\geq 1
, we impose the stricter requirement of an invariant complex submanifold. This distinction is necessary to properly define the subfoliation and the corresponding removable singular points. Nevertheless, Example
6.1
suggests the possibility of extending these notions to more general objects, such as hypersurfaces. Such a generalization could potentially allow us to establish the conclusions of Theorem
1.8
, Corollary
1.9

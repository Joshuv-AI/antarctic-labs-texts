A modified Fermi Golden Rule at threshold for 3D magnetic Schrödinger operators
Pavel Exner, Arne Jensen, Hynek Kovařík

Abstract
In this paper we consider three-dimensional Schrödinger operators with a simple threshold eigenvalue. We show, under certain assumptions, that when a small magnetic field is introduced, this eigenvalue turns into a resonance in the time-dependent sense. We find the leading term in the asymptotic expansion of the imaginary part of the resonance and discuss the principal differences with respect to resonances induced by weak electric fields obtained previously in the literature.



Keywords:
resonances, threshold eigenvalue, magnetic field, Fermi Golden Rule

Introduction

We consider the following operators on
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{3})
:

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=-\Delta+V,

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=(P-\lambda A)^{2}+V,

(1.2)

where
P=-i\nabla
and
V\colon\mathbf{R}^{3}\to\mathbf{R}
is a bounded potential that decays sufficiently rapidly, say
\lvert{V(x)}\rvert\leq C(1+\lvert{x}\rvert)^{-\beta}
with
\beta>2
. The magnetic potential
A\colon\mathbf{R}^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{3}
, which generates the magnetic field
B=\nabla\times A
, is also assumed to decay sufficiently rapidly, see Assumption
2.1
(2) below for details. Under these assumptions the point zero in the spectrum of
H_{0}
may be an eigenvalue, a zero resonance, both, or neither; by zero resonance we mean that there exists a solution to
H_{0}\psi=0
(in the sense of distributions), which satisfies
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d},\langle{x}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\,dx)\setminus L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{3})
for any
\delta>0
.

In this paper we assume that zero, the essential spectrum threshold, is a simple eigenvalue of
H_{0}
, but not a resonance.
Suppose that we turn on a magnetic field, i.e. consider
H_{\lambda}
for
\lambda
small. The question that we address is the following:

What happens to the eigenvalue embedded at the threshold zero? The expectation is that it either becomes an isolated eigenvalue close to zero or becomes a resonance. Notice that under our decay conditions on
V
and
B
the operator
H_{\lambda}
cannot have an eigenvalue embedded in the continuous spectrum,
[
1
]
. Assume that
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\lVert{\psi_{0}}\rVert=1
, satisfies
H_{0}\psi_{0}=0
. We impose a condition that ensures the eigenvalue becomes a resonance, described by a metastable state. A time-dependent description of a resonance uses the survival probability of
e^{-itH_{\lambda}}\psi_{0}
in the state
\psi_{0}
, cf.
[
4
,
7
,
11
,
14
,
15
]

and the review paper
[
5
]
.
We are going to show that

\langle{\psi_{0}},{e^{-itH_{\lambda}}\,\psi_{0}}\rangle=e^{-it(x_{0}(\lambda)-i\Gamma(\lambda))}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2p})\quad\text{as $\>\lambda\to 0$}

(1.3)

with the error term uniform in
t>0
. We have either
p=1/2
or
p=2
, depending on properties of
H_{0}
and
\psi_{0}
.
The point
x_{0}(\lambda)-i\Gamma(\lambda)
with
\Gamma(\lambda)>0
indicates the location of the resonance. Its imaginary part
\Gamma(\lambda)
, called the resonance width, is of main interest for us since it determines, up to the error term, the rate of decay of the survival probability
|\mathcal{\big\langle}\psi_{0},\,e^{-itH_{\lambda}}\,\psi_{0}\mathcal{\big\rangle}|^{2}
, conventionally used to characterize the resonance lifetime.
We find the leading terms in the asymptotics of
x_{0}(\lambda)
and
\Gamma(\lambda)
.

If an eigenvalue of
H_{0}
is embedded in the continuum,
(0,\infty)
, then a perturbation
H_{0}+\lambda\widetilde{V}
may turn it into a resonance the time evolution of which is described by (
1.3
). In this case the imaginary part of the resonance position is typically given by the Fermi Golden Rule,
\Gamma(\lambda)=C_{\textup{FGR}}\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{q})
for some
q>2
, where the constant
C_{\textup{FGR}}
is generically positive.

Our result gives a modification of the Fermi Golden Rule in the situation when the eigenvalue is situated at the continuous spectrum threshold, and the perturbation is given by a magnetic field.
In particular, our main result, Theorem
5.5
, states that

\Gamma(\lambda)=\Gamma_{0}\,|\lambda|^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{4})\quad\text{as}\ \lambda\to 0.

(1.4)

The coefficient
\Gamma_{0}
is generically positive. However, Theorem
5.5
provides an asymptotic expansion of
\Gamma(\lambda)
also in the exceptional cases where
\Gamma_{0}=0
.

The proof of our main result uses the factorization of the operator difference
H_{\lambda}-H_{0}
recently developed in
[
10
]
. When combined with the Schur-Lifschitz-Feshbach-Grushin (SLFG) formula, this factorization allows us to derive a threshold resolvent expansion of the operator
H_{\lambda}
in the topology of weighted Sobolev spaces. Such a resolvent expansion then plays a crucial role in analyzing the location of the resonance, and hence in proving equation (
1.4
).

It is illustrative to compare the behavior of the threshold resonances considered in this paper with those induced by a weak electric field, in other words arising from a threshold eigenvalue as a result of an additive perturbation
\lambda W
. Such resonances were analyzed in
[
11
]
. There their existence was proved for
\lambda\to 0+
under the condition
\mathcal{\big\langle}\psi_{0},W\psi_{0}\mathcal{\big\rangle}>0
, and it was shown that the leading term
of
\Gamma
is generically of order
\lambda^{3/2}
, see
[
11
, Thm. 3.7]
.
A comparison with equation (
1.4
) thus reveals two important differences. First, the quantity
\Gamma(\lambda)
associated to resonances induced by a magnetic field is independent of the sign of
\lambda
. Second, its size is much smaller with respect to the case of a perturbation by an electric field. This leads to a slower decay of the corresponding survival probability.

It should be also noticed that, by
[
12
, Prop. 1.1]
, a result of the type (
1.3
) defines the leading asymptotics in
x_{0}(\lambda)
and
\Gamma(\lambda)
uniquely, see Corollary
5.7
for more details.

Some comments on the literature.
Recall that in other systems the presence of the unperturbed eigenvalue at the continuum edge may also result in the coupling constant power in the leading term different from two, cf.
[
6
, Example 2]
or
[
4
, Example 3.2.5]
for the Friedrichs model example; in the words of J. Howland the gold from which the rule is made is then mixed with brass. Another notable feature of the Fermi Golden Rule is that the constant

C_{\textup{FGR}}
might vanish. Such situations were studied in
[
3
]
, where the subsequent term in the asymptotic expansion of
\Gamma(\lambda)
was calculated. Threshold resonances arising from a perturbation of
the two-dimensional Pauli operator were found recently in
[
2
]
. In this case the asymptotic expansion of
\Gamma(\lambda)
depends heavily on the flux of the magnetic field.

H_{\lambda}

are proved in Section
4
, cf. equation (
4.1
) and Lemmas

-
4.6
. The main result, Theorem
5.5
, is stated and proved in Section
5
.

\varepsilon,&\nu\geq 3.\end{cases}

(5.18)

Proof.

Let us start with the case
\nu=-1
. It follows from the definition of
L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)
that we have
H_{\pm}(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)-L_{\pm}(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)=0
.

Using Taylor’s formula with expansion point
x_{0}(\varepsilon)
we get the two estimates

\displaystyle\lvert{H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)-L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)-(1+\tfrac{d}{dx}H_{\pm}(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)(x-x_{0}(\varepsilon))}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}

(5.19)

\displaystyle\lvert{H{{}_{\pm}}(x,\varepsilon)-L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert

(5.20)

Rewrite as follows

\frac{1}{H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}-\frac{1}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}=\frac{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)-H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)^{2}}+\frac{\bigl(L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)-H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)\bigr)^{2}}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)^{2}H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}.

(5.21)

Note that (
5.12
) holds in the
\nu=-1
case with
\varepsilon^{\nu+2}
replaced by
\varepsilon^{3}
. Then we have

\lvert{H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert\geq C\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert

Using this lower bound in combination with (
5.19
), (
5.20
), and (
5.21
) it follows that

\displaystyle\biggl\lvert\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}e^{-itx}\Bigl(\frac{1}{H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}-\frac{1}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\Bigr)dx\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert{1+\tfrac{d}{dx}H_{\pm}(x_{0}(\varepsilon))}\rvert\ \biggl\lvert\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}e^{-itx}\frac{x_{0}(\varepsilon)-x}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)^{2}}\ dx\biggr\rvert

(5.22)

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon^{-1/2}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}}\ dx

(5.23)

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert}{\lvert{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}}\ dx.

(5.24)

Recall the choice of
I_{\varepsilon}
, see (
5.6
). We have

\frac{\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}}\leq 1.

Since the length of
I_{\varepsilon}
equals
\frac{1}{2}\tilde{b}\varepsilon
, it follows that the term (
5.23
) is bounded by
C\varepsilon^{1/2}
. The term (
5.24
) is estimated using
(
5.15
) as follows

\varepsilon\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert}{\lvert{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}}\ dx\leq C\varepsilon\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x-x_{0}(\varepsilon))^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}}}\ dx\leq C\varepsilon\lvert{\ln(\varepsilon)}\rvert.

The integrand on the right hand side of (
5.22
) is rewritten as

\frac{x_{0}(\varepsilon)-x}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)^{2}}=\frac{1}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\pm\frac{i\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)^{2}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\biggl\lvert\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}e^{-itx}\frac{x_{0}(\varepsilon)-x}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)^{2}}\ dx\biggr\rvert\leq\biggl\lvert\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}e^{-itx}\frac{1}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}dx\biggr\rvert+\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{\lvert{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}}\ dx

Using the definition of
L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)
we find

\int_{I(\varepsilon)}\frac{\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{\lvert{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}}\ dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{x^{2}+\Gamma(\varepsilon)^{2}}\ dx=\pi.

Now define

\ell(\varepsilon)=\frac{\text{length}(I_{\varepsilon})}{2}\,.

(5.25)

Then

\biggl\lvert\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}e^{-itx}\frac{1}{L_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}dx\biggr\rvert=\biggl\lvert\int_{-\ell(\varepsilon)/\Gamma(\varepsilon)}^{\ell(\varepsilon)/\Gamma(\varepsilon)}e^{-it\Gamma(\varepsilon)y}\frac{1}{y\mp i}dy\biggr\rvert\leq C.

Here, in the last step, we have used
[
11
, Eq. (3.56)]
, see also
[
2
, Lem. B.1]
.
Using the estimate (
5.16
)
the proof is finished in the case
\nu=-1
. The cases
\nu\geq 1
are treated in the same manner.
∎

Recall that
x_{0}(\varepsilon)=\tilde{b}\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3/2})
and
\Gamma(\varepsilon)=-I(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)
.
We have the following asymptotics of
\Gamma(\varepsilon)
.

\Gamma(\varepsilon)=\begin{cases}\tilde{b}^{-1/2}\beta_{-1}\,\varepsilon^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}),&\nu=-1,\\[5.0pt]
\tilde{b}^{1/2}\beta_{1}\,\varepsilon^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}),&\nu=1,\\[5.0pt]
-i^{\nu-1}\,\tilde{b}^{\nu/2}\,\varepsilon^{(\nu+2)/2}\beta_{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{(\nu+4)/2}),&\nu\geq 3,\end{cases}

(5.26)

where the coefficients
\beta_{-1},\beta_{1}
, and
\beta_{\nu}
are given by (
4.18
) and (
4.13
).

Let

p=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{1/2},&\nu=-1,1,\\[6.0pt]
\varepsilon^{2},&\nu\geq 3.\end{cases}

(5.27)

Lemma 5.4
.

For
\varepsilon
sufficiently small we have

\lvert{A_{g_{\varepsilon}}(t)-e^{-it(x_{0}(\varepsilon)-i\Gamma(\varepsilon))}}\rvert\leq C\,\varepsilon^{p}\,.

(5.28)

Proof.

From Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
it follows that

A_{g_{\varepsilon}}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}e^{-itx}\Bigl[\frac{1}{L_{+}(x,\varepsilon)}-\frac{1}{L_{-}(x,\varepsilon)}\Bigr]dx+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{p}),

(5.29)

with an error term uniform in
t>0
.
We have

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}e^{-itx}\Bigl[\frac{1}{L_{+}(x,\varepsilon)}-\frac{1}{L_{-}(x,\varepsilon)}\Bigr]dx=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}e^{-itx}\frac{\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{(x-x_{0}(\varepsilon))^{2}+\Gamma(\varepsilon)^{2}}dx.

(5.30)

Since
\ell(\varepsilon)/\Gamma(\varepsilon)\to\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, equations (
5.25
) and (
5.26
) imply

\displaystyle\Bigl\lvert\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\int_{I_{\varepsilon}}\Bigr)e^{-itx}\frac{\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{(x-x_{0}(\varepsilon))^{2}+\Gamma(\varepsilon)^{2}}dx\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\ell(\varepsilon)}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{x^{2}+\Gamma(\varepsilon)^{2}}dx

(5.31)

\displaystyle=C\int_{\ell(\varepsilon)/\Gamma(\varepsilon)}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+t^{2}}\ dt\leq C\ \frac{\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{\ell(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\nu}.

By the residue theorem we have

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-itx}\frac{\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{(x-x_{0}(\varepsilon))^{2}+\Gamma(\varepsilon)^{2}}dx=e^{-it(x_{0}(\varepsilon)-i\Gamma(\varepsilon)}.

Estimate (
5.28
) thus follows from the definition of
I_{\varepsilon}
, see (
5.6
), and (
5.26
).
∎

We can now state our main result.

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose that Assumptions
4.2
and
4.3
hold. Then for all
\lambda
sufficiently small we have

\sup_{t>0} \lvert{\langle{\psi_{0}},{\big[e^{-itH_{\lambda}}-e^{-it(x_{0}(\lambda)-i\Gamma(\lambda))}\big]\psi_{0}}\rangle}\rvert\leq C\lambda^{2p}\,,

(5.32)

where

\Gamma(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\tilde{b}^{\,-\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{-1}\,|\lambda|^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{4}),&\ \nu=-1,\\[5.0pt]
\tilde{b}^{\,\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{1}\,|\lambda|^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{4}),&\ \nu=1,\\[5.0pt]
-i^{\nu-1}\,\tilde{b}^{\,\frac{\nu}{2}}\,|\lambda|^{\nu+2}\,\beta_{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(|\lambda|^{\nu+4}),&\ \nu\geq 3,\end{cases}

(5.33)

and

x_{0}(\lambda)=\tilde{b}\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{O}(|\lambda|^{3}).

(5.34)

Recall that
p
is given by (
5.27
).

Proof.

Since the coefficients
b,\tilde{b}
and
\beta_{\nu},\nu=-1,2,3\dots,
are even in
A
, the claim will follow
from the estimate

\sup_{t>0}\lvert{\langle{\psi_{0}},{\big[e^{-itH_{\varepsilon}}-e^{-it(x_{0}(\varepsilon)-i\Gamma(\varepsilon))}\big]\psi_{0}}\rangle}\rvert\leq C\,\varepsilon^{p}

(5.35)

upon setting
\lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.
With Lemma
5.4
at hand, the proof of (
5.35
) becomes a straightforward
variation on the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 3.7]
.
Following the argument of Hunziker
[
7
]
, see also
[
11
]
, we first we apply equation
(
5.28
) with
t=0
to get
|A_{g_{\varepsilon}}(0)-1|\lesssim\varepsilon^{p}
. Consequently, by (
5.7
),

\|\big(1-g_{\varepsilon}(H_{\varepsilon})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\psi_{0}\|_{2}\,\leq\,C\varepsilon^{p}.

(5.36)

This implies

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{\big\langle}\psi_{0},\,e^{-itH_{\varepsilon}}\,\psi_{0}\mathcal{\big\rangle}-A_{g_{\varepsilon}}(t)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\mathcal{\big\langle}\big(1-g_{\varepsilon}(H_{\varepsilon})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\psi_{0},\,e^{-itH_{\varepsilon}}\big(1-g_{\varepsilon}(H_{\varepsilon})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\psi_{0}\mathcal{\big\rangle}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\|\big(1-g_{\varepsilon}(H_{\varepsilon})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\psi_{0}\|_{2}\,\leq\ C\varepsilon^{p}.

(5.37)

Equation (
5.35
) now follows from Lemma
5.4
. Since
\Gamma(\varepsilon)
satisfies (
5.26
), and
sine

x_{0}(\varepsilon)=\tilde{b}\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3/2})
, cf. equation (

), this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Notice that the leading term in the asymptotic expansion of
\Gamma
is of the same order of magnitude for
\nu=-1
and
\nu=1
. This is yet another
important difference with respect to the resonances induced by an electric field, cf.
[
11
, Thm. 3.7]
.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
5.5
be satisfied. Assume that there exist
\widehat{x}_{0}(\lambda)
and
\widehat{\Gamma}(\lambda)
such that
for all
\lambda
small enough we have

\sup_{t>0}\lvert{\langle{\psi_{0}},{\big[e^{-itH_{\lambda}}-e^{-it(\widehat{x}_{0}(\lambda)-i\widehat{\Gamma}(\lambda))}\big]\psi_{0}}\rangle}\rvert\leq C\,\lambda^{2p}\,.

Then

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{\widehat{\Gamma}(\lambda)}{\Gamma(\lambda)}=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{\widehat{x}_{0}(\lambda)}{x_{0}(\lambda)}=1.

Proof.

From (
5.26
) and from
[
12
, Prop. 1.1]
it follows that

|\widehat{\Gamma}(\lambda)-\Gamma(\lambda)|+|\widehat{x}_{0}(\lambda)-x_{0}(\lambda)|=\mathcal{O}(|\lambda|^{2p+2+|\nu|})\qquad\lambda\to 0

for all
\nu=-1,1,3\dots
. The claim thus follows from equations (
5.33
) and (
5.34
).
∎

Appendix A
Comments on (
5.12
)

The bound

\lvert{H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}\geq C\bigl(\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}+\varepsilon^{\nu+2}\bigr)

may require some explanation. We give the details here.
We have

H_{\pm}(x,\varepsilon)=R(x,\varepsilon)-R(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\pm iI(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon).

Then

R(x,\varepsilon)-R(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\\
=x_{0}(\varepsilon)-x+\varepsilon^{3/2}[f(x,\varepsilon)-f(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)]+x_{0}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon g(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)-x\varepsilon g(x,\varepsilon).

f(x,\varepsilon)
is a polynomial in
x
. Thus we have

\varepsilon^{3/2}[f(x,\varepsilon)-f(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)]=\varepsilon^{3/2}(x-x_{0}(\varepsilon))[c+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)].

g(x,\varepsilon)
is also a polynomial in
x
. Thus

\displaystyle x\varepsilon g(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)-x_{0}(\varepsilon)\varepsilon g(x,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(x-x_{0}(\varepsilon))g(x,\varepsilon)+\varepsilon x_{0}(\varepsilon)[g(x,\varepsilon)-g(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(x-x_{0}(\varepsilon))[c+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)]

Thus we have

\lvert{R(x,\varepsilon)-R(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}=\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}(1+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}))\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\lvert{x-x_{0}(\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.
Since
\beta_{\nu}\neq 0
, we have

I(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)=i^{\nu-1}\varepsilon(x_{0}(\varepsilon))^{\nu/2}[\beta_{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(x_{0}(\varepsilon))],

and then

\lvert{I(x_{0}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)}\rvert^{2}\geq C\varepsilon^{\nu+2}.

The estimate (
5.12
) follows from these computations.

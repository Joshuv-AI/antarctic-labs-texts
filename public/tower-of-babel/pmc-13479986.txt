Building a predictive model for nursing students’ use of artificial intelligence: advancing technology acceptance and application in nursing education
Authors: Huiling Zhang, Qianqian Hu, Shuang Yu, Hui Shi, Zheyuan Xia, Fang Meng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWith the rapid development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology, its application across various industries, particularly in healthcare and nursing, has been expanding. However, the factors influencing nursing students’ acceptance and use of AI tools have not been fully explored. Understanding the key factors that affect nursing students’ use of AI tools is crucial to enhancing AI integration into nursing education.ObjectiveThis study aims to analyze the multidimensional factors influencing nursing students’ use of AI tools during their studies and internships. Using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and nomograms, the study constructs a predictive model to provide theoretical support and practical guidance for the application of AI in nursing education.MethodsA survey-based research design was employed, collecting data from 178 full-time undergraduate and graduate nursing students at Anhui University of Chinese Medicine. The questionnaire addressed variables including students’ educational background, attitudes toward artificial intelligence, and AI literacy. Multivariate regression analysis was conducted to establish a predictive model for nursing students’ use of AI tools.ResultsThe results indicate that educational background, attitudes toward artificial intelligence, and AI literacy significantly influence nursing students’ intention to use AI tools. The predictive model, built on these factors, achieved an AUC value of 0.79, demonstrating strong discriminatory power and predictive accuracy. The study reveals that nursing students’ acceptance of AI in nursing education is not only influenced by their technical literacy but also by their attitudes and perceptions toward the technology.ConclusionEducational background, attitudes toward artificial intelligence, and AI literacy are key determinants of nursing students’ intention to use AI tools. The predictive model demonstrated good performance and provides practical guidance for integrating AI into nursing education. Targeted educational interventions focusing on improving AI literacy and fostering positive attitudes may enhance AI adoption among nursing students.Clinical trial registration numberNot applicable. This study is an observational investigation focusing on nursing students’ utilization of artificial intelligence and does not involve any interventions or treatments. Therefore, it does not meet the criteria for clinical trial registration.Keywords : Predictive model, Nursing students, Artificial intelligence, Technology acceptance, Nursing education

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) technology, AI has increasingly permeated various industries, particularly healthcare and nursing [1]. In recent years, AI has demonstrated significant potential in enhancing the quality of nursing education, optimizing clinical practice, and improving the overall competencies of nursing students. Studies have shown that AI can revolutionize traditional teaching models, boost the efficiency of clinical decision-making support, and enable nursing students to acquire skills more independently, adapt more effectively, and strengthen their problem-solving capabilities [2].In this study, artificial intelligence (AI) primarily refers to AI-based educational and clinical support tools, such as intelligent tutoring systems and generative AI applications, that assist nursing students in learning and clinical decision-making. In current nursing education, commonly used AI tools include generative AI platforms for writing and knowledge support, simulation-based intelligent systems for clinical training, and AI-assisted decision-support tools that enhance students’ clinical reasoning skills. Although AI tools in this study are primarily used in educational settings, they are closely related to patient care, as they can enhance students’ clinical reasoning, decision-making, and communication skills, which are essential for safe and effective nursing practice.

However, despite the growing integration of AI in the nursing field, systematic research specifically focused on how nursing students use AI tools during their studies and clinical internships remains limited. In particular, there is a lack of in-depth research on nursing students’ acceptance of AI technologies and the factors that influence their adoption. As the core participants in nursing education, the learning behaviors, reliance on AI tools, and technology acceptance levels of nursing students directly influence the effectiveness and dissemination of AI in both educational and clinical settings.

Therefore, the core objective of this study is to construct a predictive model based on AI to explore the behavioral characteristics and influencing factors of nursing students’ use of AI tools during their learning and internship processes. Using quantitative analysis methods, this study aims to fill the gap in current research on nursing student populations, particularly in systematically examining multidimensional factors such as nursing students’ attitudes, literacy, and behavioral intentions towards AI tools in the context of nursing education and practice. Unlike previous studies that focused on the AI technology itself or its application by other healthcare professionals (such as doctors or nurses) in clinical settings, this study uniquely focuses on nursing students, exploring their actual experiences and usage patterns in nursing education and practice.

An innovative aspect of this study is the use of nomograms for building the predictive model, a method not widely applied in the field of nursing education. As a multivariable statistical model, a nomogram can visually represent the influence of multiple factors, effectively predicting the likelihood of nursing students using AI tools in different situations. The graphical nature of this method enables educators and administrators to easily understand the relative impact of each factor, allowing for more targeted educational interventions based on the predictive results. The introduction of this method will greatly promote the scientific management of nursing students’ learning and technological adaptation processes, providing theoretical support for personalized teaching in nursing education.

Moreover, this study will also consider traditional individual characteristics of nursing students, such as gender, age, and educational background, while deeply exploring the psychological factors influencing their cognition, attitudes, and technology acceptance towards AI tools. Research shows that nursing students’ technology acceptance, their understanding of AI tools, and their attitudes directly determine their willingness to use AI tools in clinical settings [3]. Therefore, this study will analyze nursing students’ technology acceptance intentions based on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) to explore their psychological mechanisms and behavior patterns in using AI tools. By constructing a predictive model for nursing students’ AI usage, this study aims to uncover the behavioral characteristics and influencing factors of nursing students’ use of AI tools during their education and practice, thus promoting the application and acceptance of AI technology in nursing education. This research not only fills the gap in studies focused on nursing students but also provides a scientific method and intervention strategy for the promotion and application of AI tools in future nursing education, contributing to technological innovation and personalized teaching in the field of nursing education.

Methods

Research design

This study adopts a cross-sectional research design aimed at exploring the factors influencing nursing students’ use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools during their learning process, as well as the obstacles they face in using AI tools during their studies and practice. Through quantitative analysis and the construction of predictive models, this research will reveal the multidimensional factors such as nursing students’ attitudes, acceptance, literacy, and behavioral intentions regarding AI tools. The findings will assist educators in developing effective intervention strategies to promote the application of AI technology in nursing education.

Participants

The participants in this study are full-time nursing undergraduate and graduate students from the School of Nursing at Anhui University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. All participants are registered nursing students enrolled in full-time programs.Inclusion criteria were: (1) full-time nursing students; (2) provided written informed consent. Exclusion criteria were: (1) part-time students; (2) students who declined to participate.Considering the seven predictor variables in the multiple regression analysis, with a statistical power of 0.80, a significance level of 0.05, and an effect size of 0.05, Participants were invited through in-class announcements and posters. A total of 190 nursing students were invited, and 178valid responses were obtained, yielding a response rate of 93.7%. To prevent duplicate entries, student ID numbers were used as unique identifiers, and duplicate submissions were checked and removed during data cleaning.The sampling frame consisted of full-time nursing students enrolled in undergraduate (Years 2–4) and graduate (Years 1–3) programs at the School of Nursing, Anhui University of Chinese Medicine. Participants were recruited on campus. Data collection was conducted between September 2024 and November 2024.

Demographic information

This section collected participants’ basic information, including age, gender, education level, and years of work experience.

Artificial intelligence literacy scale

The 12-item Artificial Intelligence Literacy Scale (AILS) developed by Wang et al4. uses a 7-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Agree to 7 = Strongly Disagree) and consists of four dimensions: Awareness, Usage, Evaluation, and Ethics. The scale includes three reverse-scored items, with a total score range of 12 to 84 points. Higher scores indicate a higher level of AI literacy. For analysis, AI literacy was categorized as “high” or “low” using a median split (scores ≥ median = high; < median = low). All scoring procedures followed the original validated instrument.

General attitudes to artificial intelligence scale

The GAAIS evaluates individuals’ attitudes toward AI technology through a 20-item bifactorial structure5. Items 1–12 form the Positive Attitudes subscale (Positive GAAIS), and items 13–20 form the Negative Attitudes subscale (Negative GAAIS). The Negative Attitudes subscale uses reverse scoring. The positive attitudes subscale ranges from 12 to 60, and the negative attitudes subscale ranges from 8 to 40. Higher total scores indicate more favorable attitudes toward AI. For analysis, AI attitude was categorized as “high” or “low” using a median split (scores ≥ median = high; < median = low). All scoring procedures followed the original validated instrument.

Data analysis

Data analysis will be conducted using SPSS and R software, employing multiple regression analysis to explore the factors influencing nursing students’ use of AI tools. First, descriptive statistics will be used to summarize the demographic characteristics of the sample. Then, multiple regression analysis will be performed to construct a predictive model for nursing students’ use of AI tools and analyze the influence of each variable. The intention to use AI was analyzed as a binary variable based on participants original responses (intention vs. no intention). Finally, a nomogram will be used for model visualization, helping educators intuitively understand the contribution of different factors to the prediction results and implement personalized educational interventions based on these findings. Missing data were handled using listwise deletion, as the overall proportion of missing values was low (< 5%) and no clear systematic pattern of missingness was observed.Reverse-scored items were recoded prior to analysis. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 and R version 4.2.1.

Instrument reliability

All instruments were used in their original validated Chinese versions without adaptation. Prior to the main study, a pilot test was conducted among 20 nursing students to assess item clarity and feasibility. The internal consistency of the scales was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficients.

Data collection

Data were collected through a structured questionnaire that included demographic information, attitudes toward artificial intelligence, AI literacy, self-efficacy, and behavioral intentions regarding AI use.

Statistical analysis plan

Variables with p < 0.10 in univariate logistic regression were entered into the multivariate model. Collinearity among predictors was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), with a threshold of VIF < 5 indicating no significant collinearity. Model performance was internally validated using 5-fold cross-validation. The number of events per variable (EPV) was considered to ensure model stability, with an EPV greater than 10 indicating a low risk of overfitting. A stepwise selection approach based on univariate screening (p < 0.10) was used to select variables for the multivariate model.

Characteristics of the participants

All scales demonstrated acceptable internal consistency, with Cronbach’s alpha values exceeding 0.70.The majority of participants were female (82.02%) and older than 25 years (59.55%). Most had either a postgraduate degree (50.56%) or an undergraduate degree (49.43%). Only 27.52% had work experience, while 73.03% held a positive attitude toward artificial intelligence (AI), and 66.85% reported high AI literacy. A large portion (94.38%) rated their self-efficacy as low. Regarding behavioral intention, participants were almost evenly divided, with 50.84% intending to use AI and 49.15% not intending to use it. Construction of the predictive model for intention to use AI

Among the initial seven variables, three were selected as predictors for the predictive model: educational background, attitude toward AI, and AI literacy. The predictive model is presented in the form of a nomogram to estimate the probability of the intention to use AI (Fig. 1). The scoring method is based on the regression coefficients from a multiple logistic regression model. The score for each variable is obtained by converting its regression coefficient to the corresponding value on the nomogram. These regression coefficients reflect the relative contribution of each predictor to the intention to use AI. For example, variables such as educational background, attitude toward AI, and AI literacy all have corresponding regression coefficients, which are mapped to specific score ranges. The student’s educational background, attitude toward AI, and AI literacy will receive corresponding scores on the nomogram. The total score is the sum of the individual factor scores, and the probability is estimated based on the total score. The vertical axis represents the value range of each predictor, while the horizontal axis shows the score corresponding to each value of the predictor, referred to as the “score.” The total score represents the sum of all predictor scores, and the probability represents the estimated probability of the intention to use AI based on the total score.

Nomogram predicting the probability of behavior intention

For example, a graduate student nurse with a positive attitude toward AI and high AI literacy would receive relatively high scores on each factor, resulting in a higher total score, which corresponds to a higher predicted probability of intention to use AI. In contrast, a student with an undergraduate education, a passive attitude toward AI, and low AI literacy would obtain a much lower total score, corresponding to a low predicted probability of intention to use AI. These contrasting examples illustrate how the nomogram can differentiate between students with varying levels of the three key predictors.

Performance validation of the AI usage intention prediction model

Model discrimination validation

The performance of the prediction model developed in this study was validated using the ROC curve. The ROC curve for this prediction model includes three factors: educational background, attitude toward artificial intelligence (AI), and AI literacy, with an AUC of 0.79. This indicates that the model has a good ability to discriminate between positive and negative categories, demonstrating strong predictive power. For further details, see Fig. 2.

Discussion

Construction of the prediction model for AI tool usage intention

This study is informed by the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and uses a Nomogram Model.The model aims to analyze nursing students’ technology acceptance intentions and reveal the underlying psychological mechanisms and behavioral patterns [4]. As a classic theoretical framework, TAM focuses on individuals’ attitudes and acceptance of new technologies, emphasizing the significant impact of perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use on technology acceptance. In nursing education, nursing students’ acceptance of technology is influenced not only by academic background, professional competence, and technical literacy but also by personal attitudes, perceptions of new technologies, and trust in AI tools. Therefore, based on the TAM model, this study further explores the multidimensional factors influencing nursing students’ use of AI tools.

Through quantitative analysis, the study found that nursing students’ attitudes, technical literacy, and acceptance of AI tools are key factors determining their intention to use AI tools. Particularly, improving technical literacy helps nursing students use AI tools more confidently, while a positive attitude and higher acceptance effectively promote active use of AI technology. These findings not only validate the applicability of TAM in nursing education but also highlight the critical role of psychological factors in the technology acceptance process.

The Nomogram, as a multivariable statistical tool, visually displays the influence of various factors on nursing students’ use of AI tools. In this study, the Nomogram provides both a quantitative description of each influencing factor and actionable intervention suggestions for educators. Through intuitive visualization, educators can more clearly identify the key factors affecting nursing students’ use of AI tools, allowing them to design targeted interventions, such as optimizing training programs, improving nursing students’ AI literacy, and cultivating positive technology attitudes. Unlike previous studies, this research focuses on nursing students, revealing their behavioral characteristics regarding the use of AI tools in nursing education and practice. This perspective fills the gap in current nursing education research and provides scientific evidence and practical guidance for promoting AI technology in nursing education.

The impact of educational background on AI usage intention

The study found that nursing students’ educational background significantly affects their intention to use AI tools. The research shows that nursing students with higher academic qualifications are more likely to use AI tools than undergraduate students, consistent with research findings both domestically and internationally [5]. The study suggests that educational background is a key factor influencing technology acceptance. Higher education typically indicates stronger learning abilities and technical literacy, which makes nursing students more receptive to new technologies. In nursing practice, highly educated groups are more proficient in understanding and applying AI tools, resulting in higher acceptance. However, education level, although a key factor in technology acceptance, is not the only one. Training opportunities and the level of technology dissemination also play important roles. For example, in some developed countries, nurses with lower educational backgrounds can effectively use AI tools through continuing education and training, which differs from the domestic education system that emphasizes theoretical learning with relatively less practical training [6]. This difference makes the impact of educational background on nursing students’ use of AI tools more pronounced.

Additionally, the study found that educational background interacts with technical literacy. Nursing students with higher academic qualifications typically have higher technical literacy and self-efficacy, which makes them more confident in using AI tools. Future research could explore how educational interventions can enhance the technical literacy of nursing students with lower educational backgrounds, reduce the impact of educational differences, and provide practical insights for the application of AI in nursing education.

The impact of attitude toward AI on AI usage intention

In this study, the attitude of nursing students toward AI was found to be an important factor influencing their intention to use AI tools. Nursing students with a positive attitude are more likely to use AI tools, consistent with findings from both domestic and international studies [7–9]. The research emphasizes that technology acceptance is closely related to individuals’ attitudes toward the technology. Specifically, in nursing education, nursing students’ technical attitudes are influenced not only by academic education but also by societal perceptions and acceptance of AI technology. Research shows that nurses’ positive attitudes toward AI directly promote its application in clinical practice, especially when dealing with complex cases. A positive attitude helps reduce distrust toward the technology and enhances adaptability to it. In countries with high technology acceptance, nursing students and nurses typically form a positive attitude toward AI through multi-level technical education and practical training, which increases their intention to use AI in clinical settings. In contrast, domestic studies have focused less on the role of nursing students’ attitudes in technology acceptance. Rather than assuming inherent differences among student groups, variations in attitudes toward AI may be influenced by contextual factors such as differences in educational systems, exposure to digital technologies, and institutional support for innovation. As a result, nursing students’ intention to use AI tools in China is significantly influenced by their attitudes toward the technology and societal cultural factors, highlighting a major gap between domestic and international nursing education in terms of AI technology diffusion.

The impact of AI literacy on AI usage intention

The results of this study show that AI literacy significantly affects nursing students’ intention to use AI tools. Higher AI literacy means nursing students possess more AI knowledge and skills, enabling them to use AI tools more effectively in clinical practice. This finding is consistent with other studies, which indicate that AI literacy is a key factor in nurses’ ability to effectively use AI tools [10, 11]. AI literacy encompasses not only basic technical operation skills but also understanding and applying AI in healthcare. Nursing students with higher AI literacy are more proficient in operating and managing AI tools, are more confident in clinical practice, and can better handle the technical challenges encountered in actual work. Therefore, improving nursing students’ AI literacy not only increases their intention to use AI tools but also enhances their confidence and problem-solving abilities in real-world settings. Nurses’ technical literacy is an important prerequisite for accepting new technologies. Nurses’ AI literacy directly influences their acceptance and frequency of AI usage, and this literacy is often developed through systematic education and training [12–14].

Although self-efficacy was not significant in the univariate regression analysis of this study, it may be due to nursing students not having sufficient training to fully experience the process of using AI tools. Additionally, the complexity and professional nature of AI tool operations may initially affect nursing students’ self-efficacy, but the influence may gradually become apparent as their skills improve. Future research could further explore the potential impact of self-efficacy on AI tool usage by analyzing individual differences in more depth. To promote the application of AI technology in nursing education, educators should not only focus on improving nursing students’ AI knowledge and skills but also on cultivating their technical adaptability and self-efficacy. Educators can help nursing students improve their confidence in operating AI tools and alleviate psychological stress about new technologies through simulated practice, skills training, and psychological interventions, thereby better facilitating the acceptance and application of AI technology.The high proportion of low self-efficacy observed in this study may reflect the actual distribution of the sample rather than a scoring error; however, the use of dichotomization may have influenced this distribution.

Research limitations and future research directions

Although this study provides insights into predicting nursing students’ use of AI tools, it has several limitations. First, the study employed a cross-sectional design, which did not explore the impact of time-related changes on nursing students’ technology acceptance. Future research could adopt a longitudinal design to examine how nursing students’ intentions to use AI evolve during different stages of their education and internships. Second, the sample in this study was limited to nursing students at Anhui University of Chinese Medicine, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future research could expand the sample size to include nursing students from different regions and universities, improving the broader applicability of the results.The use of a binary measure for intention may have reduced the level of detail in the analysis.In addition, model calibration was not assessed and should be considered in future studies.However, although the EPV was adequate, potential overfitting cannot be completely ruled out.

Conclusion

This study constructed a prediction model for nursing students’ use of AI tools and analyzed the multidimensional factors influencing their intention to use AI tools. The study revealed that educational background, AI attitude, and AI literacy significantly affect nursing students’ intention to use AI. The findings provide a theoretical basis for AI technology application in nursing education and offer practical guidance for educators in designing personalized teaching interventions. In the future, as AI technology becomes more widely applied in nursing education, nursing students’ awareness, adaptability, and acceptance of AI will be key factors in driving the successful application of AI in nursing education.However, this study focuses on intention rather than actual educational or competency outcomes.

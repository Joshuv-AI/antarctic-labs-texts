A Composed Alternating Relaxed Projection Algorithm for Feasibility Problem
Yuting Shen, Jingwei Liang

Abstract
Feasibility problem aims to find a common point of two or more closed (convex) sets whose intersection is nonempty. In the literature, projection based algorithms are widely adopted to solve the problem, such as the method of alternating projection (MAP), and Douglas–Rachford splitting method (DR). The performance of the methods are governed by the geometric properties of the underlying sets. For example, the fixed-point sequence of the Douglas–Rachford splitting method exhibits a spiraling behavior when solving the feasibility problem of two subspaces, leading to a slow convergence speed and slower than MAP. However, when the problem at hand is non-polyhedral, DR can demonstrate significant faster performance. Motivated by the behaviors of the DR method, in this paper we propose a new algorithm for solving convex feasibility problems. The method is designed based on DR method by further incorporating a composition of projection and reflection. A non-stationary version of the method is also designed, aiming to achieve faster practical performance. Theoretical guarantees of the proposed schemes are provided and supported by numerical experiments.



Key words.
Feasibility problem, Projection, Relaxation, Friedrichs angle, Linear convergence

In this paper, we are interested in the following feasibility problem of two sets

\mathrm{find}\hskip 5.0pt{x\in\mathcal{H}}\hskip 5.0pt\textrm{such\,that}\hskip 5.0ptx\in X\cap Y,

In the literature, projection (see Definition

The very basic algorithm to solve the feasibility problem is the method of alternating projection (MAP)

Operators

In this part, we provide the definitions of nonexpansive and projection related operator, we refer to
[
3
, Chapter 4]

Definition 2.1
(Nonexpansive operator).

An operator
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is called nonexpansive if it is
1
-Lipschitz continuous, i.e.

{|\kern-1.125pt|}\mathcal{F}(x)-\mathcal{F}(x^{\prime}){|\kern-1.125pt|}\leq{|\kern-1.125pt|}x-x^{\prime}{|\kern-1.125pt|},\,\,\,\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}.

For any
\alpha\in]0,1[
,
\mathcal{F}
is
\alpha
-averaged if there exists a nonexpansive operator
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that

\mathcal{F}=\alpha\mathcal{F}^{\prime}+(1-\alpha)\mathrm{Id}.

When
\alpha=1/2
,
\mathcal{F}
is also called “firmly nonexpansive”, and
2\mathcal{F}-\mathrm{Id}
is nonexpansive in this case.

A point
x
is called a fixed-point of
\mathcal{F}
if it satisfies
x=\mathcal{F}(x)
, and the set of fixed-points of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as
\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F})=\big\{x\in\mathcal{H}\mid x=\mathcal{F}(x)\big\}
, which could be empty.

Definition 2.2
(Projection).

Let set
X\subset\mathcal{H}
be nonempty closed convex, its projection operator is defined by,

\mathscr{P}_{X}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Argmin}_{y\in X}\,{|\kern-1.125pt|}x-y{|\kern-1.125pt|},\,\,\forall x\in\mathcal{H},

which is firmly nonexpansive.

Definition 2.3
(Relaxed projection and reflection).

Let set
X\subset\mathcal{H}
be nonempty closed convex and
r\in[-1,1]
, the relaxed projection is defined by,

\mathscr{R}^{r}_{X}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm\tiny def}}}{{=}}\big({(1+r)\mathscr{P}_{X}-r\mathrm{Id}}\big)(x),\,\,\,\forall x\in\mathcal{H}

which is
\frac{1+\gamma}{2}
-averaged.
When
r=1
,
\mathscr{R}^{r}_{X}
is called the “reflection operator” and is denoted by
\mathscr{R}_{X}
for simplicity.

Angles between subspaces

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be two subspaces, we have the following definitions regarding the angles between them
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.4
(Principal angles).

The principal angles
\theta_{k}\in

\left[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right],k=1,\cdots,p
between two subspaces
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
p={\min}\big\{\dim(X),\dim(Y)\big\}
are recursively defined by

\displaystyle\cos(\theta_{k})

\displaystyle=\left\langle u_{k},v_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\langle u,v\rangle\left\lvert\,\begin{array}[]{l}u\in X,v\in Y,\|u\|=\|v\|=1,\\
\left\langle u,u_{j}\right\rangle=\left\langle v,v_{j}\right\rangle=0,j=1,\ldots,k-1\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right\}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002ptu_{0}=v_{0}:=0.

It is worth mentioning that the vectors
u_{k},v_{k}
are not uniquely defined, but the principal angles
\theta_{k}
are unique with
0\leq\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\theta_{p}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Definition 2.5
(Friedrichs angle).

The cosine of the Friedrichs angle between
X
and
Y
is

c_{\scriptstyle\rm{F}}(X,Y)=\cos(\theta_{\scriptstyle\rm{F}}):=\sup\left\{|\langle u,v\rangle|\mid u\in X\cap(X\cap Y)^{\bot},v\in Y\cap(X\cap Y)^{\bot},\|u\|\leq 1,\|v\|\leq 1\right\}.

We will use
c_{\scriptstyle\rm{F}}
for simplicity if there is no confusion.

Proposition 2.6
(Principal angles and Friedrichs angle).

Let
s:=\dim(X\cap Y)
, then
\theta_{k}=0\ {\rm for}\ k=1,\ldots,s
and
\theta_{s+1}=\theta_{\scriptstyle\rm{F}}>0.

Convergence rate of matrices

For any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
\sigma(A)
and
\rho(A)
denote the spectrum and spectral radius of
A
, respectively.
We say
A
converges linearly to
A^{\infty}
if there exists
r\in[0,1[
and
M,N>0
such that

\|A^{k}-A^{\infty}\|\leq Mr^{k},\,\,\,\forall k\geq N.

An eigenvalue
\lambda\in\sigma(A)
is
semisimple
if

\mathrm{ker}(A-\lambda\mathrm{Id})=\mathrm{ker}(A-\lambda\mathrm{Id})^{2}.

The
subdominant eigenvalue

A
is defined by

\xi(A):=\max\{|\lambda|\mid\lambda\in\{0\}\cup\sigma(A)\backslash\{1\}\},

i.e.
the largest eigenvalue besides
1
without sign.

Fact 2.7
(
[
6
, Fact 2.4]
).

Suppose that
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}A^{k}
exists if and only if

\rho(A)<1\hskip 10.00002pt\text{or}\hskip 10.00002pt\rho(A)=1\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda=1\text{ is semisimple and it is the only eigenvalue on the unit circle}.

Theorem 2.8
(
[
6
, Theorem 2.12, Theorem 2.15]
).

Suppose that
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is convergent to
A^{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, then
\xi(A)=\rho\left(A-A^{\infty}\right)<1
and that

\left(A-A^{\infty}\right)^{k}=A^{k}-A^{\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, the followings hold

(i)

A
is linearly convergent with any rate
r\in]\xi(A),1[
.

(ii)

\xi(A)
is the optimal convergence rate of A if and only if all the subdominant eigenvalues are semisimple.

3
A composed alternating relaxed projection algorithm

This section is dedicated to the formulation and analysis of our proposed algorithm. We first provide the details of the method with convergence analysis, and then discuss a non-stationary version of the method, which demonstrates more practical performances.

3.1
The proposed algorithm

Motivated by the drawback of DR method for solving (locally) polyhedral feasibility problems, we design a new algorithm called the “composed alternating relaxed projection algorithm (CARPA)”, whose details are provided below.

Algorithm 1

A composed alternating relaxed projection algorithm (CARPA)

Initial
:
k=0
,
z^{(0)}\in\mathcal{H},x^{(0)}=\mathscr{P}_{X}(z^{(0)})
,
\gamma\in[0,1[
,
\mu\in]0,2/(1+\gamma)[
;

repeat

(3.1)

\displaystyle x^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{P}_{X}({z}^{(k)}),

\displaystyle u^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=2x^{(k+1)}-{z}^{(k)},

\displaystyle y^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{P}_{Y}(u^{(k+1)}),

\displaystyle{z}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=(1-\mu){z}^{(k)}+\mu\big({(1-\gamma)({z}^{(k)}+y^{(k+1)}-x^{(k+1)})+\gamma y^{(k+1)}}\big).

k=k+1
;

until

convergence
;

In what follows, we discuss the convergence properties of CARPA, starting from analyzing the fixed-point formulation of the method and then convergence of the sequence.
Define the following operators

(3.2)

\mathcal{F}_{1}={{\frac{\raisebox{0.44273pt}{\footnotesize$1$}}{\raisebox{-1.32808pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}}}(\mathrm{Id}+\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{X}}),\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{F}_{2}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{X}}.

Then we denote

\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}=(1-\gamma)\mathcal{F}_{1}+\gamma\mathcal{F}_{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}=(1-\mu)\mathrm{Id}+\mu\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}.

We have the following proposition on the properties of these operators.

Proposition 3.1
.

The operator
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
is
\frac{1+\gamma}{2}
-averaged nonexpansive, and
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}
is
\frac{(1+\gamma)\mu}{2}
-average.
Moreover, the proposed algorithm can be written as the fixed-point iteration of
{z}^{(k)}
which reads

(3.3)

{z}^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}({z}^{(k)}).

From the iteration of CARPA, we have

y^{(k+1)}=\mathscr{P}_{Y}(2\mathscr{P}_{X}-\mathrm{Id})({z}^{(k)})=\mathcal{F}_{2}({z}^{(k)})

and

\displaystyle{z}^{(k)}+y^{(k+1)}-x^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\big({\mathrm{Id}+\mathscr{P}_{Y}(2\mathscr{P}_{X}-\mathrm{Id})-\mathscr{P}_{X}}\big)({z}^{(k)})

\displaystyle={{\frac{\raisebox{0.44273pt}{\footnotesize$1$}}{\raisebox{-1.32808pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}}}\big({\mathrm{Id}+\mathscr{R}_{Y}\mathscr{R}_{X}}\big)({z}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{1}({z}^{(k)}).

Combine the two formulas we get
{z}^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}({z}^{(k)})
.

Back to the expression of
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)\mathcal{F}_{1}+\gamma\mathcal{F}_{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma){{\frac{\raisebox{0.44273pt}{\footnotesize$1$}}{\raisebox{-1.32808pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}}}(\mathrm{Id}+\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{X}})+\gamma\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle={{\frac{\raisebox{0.44273pt}{\footnotesize$1-\gamma$}}{\raisebox{-1.32808pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}}}\mathrm{Id}+{{\frac{\raisebox{0.44273pt}{\footnotesize$1+\gamma$}}{\raisebox{-1.32808pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}}}\big({{{\frac{\raisebox{0.44273pt}{\footnotesize$1-\gamma$}}{\raisebox{-1.32808pt}{\footnotesize$1+\gamma$}}}}\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{Y}}+{{\frac{\raisebox{0.44273pt}{\footnotesize$2\gamma$}}{\raisebox{-1.32808pt}{\footnotesize$1+\gamma$}}}}\mathscr{P}_{Y}}\big)\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{X}}.

Owing to the result of
[
3
, Chapter 4]
,
\mathscr{P}_{Y}
is firmly nonexpansive and
\mathscr{R}_{Y}
is nonexpansive, hence
{{\frac{\raisebox{0.39702pt}{\footnotesize$1-\gamma$}}{\raisebox{-1.19093pt}{\footnotesize$1+\gamma$}}}}\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{Y}}+{{\frac{\raisebox{0.39702pt}{\footnotesize$2\gamma$}}{\raisebox{-1.19093pt}{\footnotesize$1+\gamma$}}}}\mathscr{P}_{Y}
is nonexpansive
[
3
, Proposition 4.6]
. Consequently,
\big({{{\frac{\raisebox{0.39702pt}{\footnotesize$1-\gamma$}}{\raisebox{-1.19093pt}{\footnotesize$1+\gamma$}}}}\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{Y}}+{{\frac{\raisebox{0.39702pt}{\footnotesize$2\gamma$}}{\raisebox{-1.19093pt}{\footnotesize$1+\gamma$}}}}\mathscr{P}_{Y}}\big)\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{X}}
is nonexpansive. By the definition of averaged operators, we conclude the proof.
The averagedness of
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\lambda}
follows immediately. ∎

Proposition 3.2
(Set of fixed-points).

Let
X,Y\subset\mathcal{H}
be nonempty closed convex sets with nonempty intersection, i.e.
X\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})=X\cap Y
.

We provide a simple proof for the sake of completeness.
Owing to
[
3
, Proposition 4.47]
, we have

\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})=\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\cap\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{2}).

We first characterize the set of fixed-points for
\mathcal{F}_{2}
, for which we have

\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{2})=X\cap Y.

It is obvious that for any
z\in X\cap Y
,
z=\mathcal{F}_{2}(z)
.
Suppose there exists
z\in\mathcal{F}_{2}(z)
such that
z\notin X\cap Y
. Now that
z\in\mathcal{F}_{2}(z)
, it means

z=\mathscr{P}_{Y}\mathscr{R}_{X}(z)\in Y,

which further implies

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{X}-z\in N_{Y}(z)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt2\mathscr{P}_{X}(z)-2z\in N_{Y}(z)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\mathscr{P}_{X}(z)-z\in N_{Y}(z)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002ptz=\mathscr{P}_{Y}\mathscr{P}_{X}(z),

where
N_{Y}(\cdot)
denotes the normal cone operator.
As a result, we have
z\in X\cap Y

[
13
]
which conflicts with the assumption, hence we obtain
\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{2})=X\cap Y
. Now for any
z\in X\cap Y
, it is easy to verify that

z\in\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{1}),

and we conclude the proof. ∎

With above characterization of the set of fixed-points, we are ready to provide the convergence result of our proposed algorithm.

Proposition 3.3
(Convergence of CARPA).

For problem (
1.1
) and Algorithm
1
, suppose
X\cap Y\neq\emptyset
and let
\gamma\in]0,1[
and
\mu\in]0,2/(1+\gamma)[
, then there exists a
{z}^{\star}\in\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})
such that
(x^{(k)},y^{(k)},{z}^{(k)})\to({z}^{\star},{z}^{\star},{z}^{\star})
.

As our proposed algorithm can be written as a fixed-point iteration in terms of

(see (

)), we have the convergence of

due to
[

Given this, we have

x^{(k)}\to x^{\star}=\mathscr{P}_{X}({z}^{\star})={z}^{\star}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pty^{(k)}\to y^{\star}=\mathscr{P}_{Y}(2x^{\star}-{z}^{\star})=\mathscr{P}_{Y}({z}^{\star})={z}^{\star},

which concludes the proof. ∎

{z}^{\star}\in\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})
such that
(x^{(k)},y^{(k)},{z}^{(k)})\to({z}^{\star},{z}^{\star},{z}^{\star})
.

The situation here is simpler than that of
[
25
]
, as we only have
\gamma_{k}
varying. If we can show that
(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{k},\mu}-\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu})({z}^{(k)})
is summable, then by virtue of quasi-Fejér monotonicity
[
3
]
.

Note that if the perturbation error

e^{(k)}=(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{k},\mu}-\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu})({z}^{(k)})

satisfies

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{|\kern-1.125pt|}e^{(k)}{|\kern-1.125pt|}<\infty.

Then the sequence
\{{z}^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is quasi-Fejér monotone and its convergence follows easily
[
3
]
. To show this, we have the following steps

i).

We first study the properties of
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. By the definition of
\gamma_{k}
, we have
|\gamma_{k+1}-\gamma_{k}|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{(k+1)^{2+\delta}}
.
Given any
\epsilon>0
, let
N=\lceil\big(\frac{c_{2}}{(1+\delta)\epsilon}\big)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}\rceil
, then
j>i>N
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\gamma_{j}-\gamma_{i}|\leq|\gamma_{j}-\gamma_{j-1}|+\cdots+|\gamma_{i+1}-\gamma_{i}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=i}^{j-1}\frac{c_{2}}{(\ell+1)^{2+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{i}^{\infty}\frac{c_{2}}{x^{2+\delta}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}}{(1+\delta)i^{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle<\frac{c_{2}}{(1+\delta)N^{1+\delta}}<\epsilon.

This means
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence, hence convergent.
Denote
\gamma^{\star}
the limit of
\gamma_{k}
, and from (
3.7
) we have

|\gamma_{k}-\gamma^{\star}|\leq\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\frac{c_{2}}{(i+1)^{2+\delta}}\leq\frac{c_{2}}{(1+\delta)k^{1+\delta}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu|\gamma_{k}-\gamma^{\star}|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu c_{2}}{(1+\delta)k^{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu c_{2}}{1+\delta}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{1+\delta}}<\infty.

ii).

Next we show that the sequence
\{{z}^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded.
As for all
k
, we have
\gamma_{k}\in[0,1[
,
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{k},\mu}
is nonexpansive and
X\cap Y\subset\mathrm{fix}(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{k},\mu})
. Let
{z}^{\star}\in X\cap Y
, then

\displaystyle{|\kern-1.125pt|}{z}^{(k+1)}-{z}^{\star}{|\kern-1.125pt|}={|\kern-1.125pt|}\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{k},\mu}({z}^{(k)}-{z}^{\star}){|\kern-1.125pt|}

\displaystyle\leq{|\kern-1.125pt|}{z}^{(k)}-{z}^{\star}{|\kern-1.125pt|}

\displaystyle\leq{|\kern-1.125pt|}z^{(0)}-{z}^{\star}{|\kern-1.125pt|}

which means
\{{z}^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. Denote

r=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{|\kern-1.125pt|}{z}^{(k)}{|\kern-1.125pt|}.

Let
\gamma=\gamma^{\star}
, by the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{k},\mu}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{k},\mu}-\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}

\displaystyle=\big({(1-\mu)\mathrm{Id}+\mu(1-\gamma_{k})\mathcal{F}_{1}+\mu\gamma_{k}\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)-\big({(1-\mu)\mathrm{Id}+\mu(1-\gamma)\mathcal{F}_{1}+\mu\gamma\mathcal{F}_{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=-\mu(\gamma_{k}-\gamma)\mathcal{F}_{1}+\mu(\gamma_{k}-\gamma)\mathcal{F}_{2}.

Recall that both
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
are nonexpansive, then from (
3.6
) we get:

\displaystyle{|\kern-1.125pt|}{z}^{(k+1)}-{z}^{\star}{|\kern-1.125pt|}

\displaystyle\leq{|\kern-1.125pt|}\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}({z}^{(k)})-{z}^{\star}{|\kern-1.125pt|}+{|\kern-1.125pt|}(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{k},\mu}-\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu})({z}^{(k)}-{z}^{\star}){|\kern-1.125pt|}

\displaystyle\leq{|\kern-1.125pt|}\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}({z}^{(k)})-\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}({z}^{\star}){|\kern-1.125pt|}+2\mu{\lvert}\gamma_{k}-\gamma{\rvert}{|\kern-1.125pt|}{z}^{(k)}-{z}^{\star}{|\kern-1.125pt|}

\displaystyle\leq{|\kern-1.125pt|}{z}^{(k)}-{z}^{\star}{|\kern-1.125pt|}+4r\mu{\lvert}\gamma_{k}-\gamma{\rvert}.

Since
{\lvert}\gamma_{k}-\gamma{\rvert}
is summable, we get that
\{{z}^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is quasi-Fejér monotone.

By virtue of
[
3
, Proposition 5.34]
we obtain the convergence of the sequence
\{{z}^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and consequently the sequences
\{x^{(k)},y^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. ∎

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a composed alternating relaxed projection algorithm for solving the feasibility problem, and a non-stationary version of the methods was developed. Theoretical analysis for both schemes is provided.
For the feasibility of two spaces, we also analyzed the optimal convergence rate of the proposed method.
Numerical experiments are provided to demonstrate the performance of the methods against several existing algorithms in the literature. Our proposed schemes provide comparative performances.
In the future, we plan to extend the algorithm to the infeasible scenario and nonconvex setting.

Convergence rate of CARPA for two subspaces

In this appendix, we provide the optimal convergence rate analysis for CARPA. For RAAR and DRAP, we also provide a simple analysis at the end of the section. For the two subspaces
X
and
Y
, we suppose that

\dim(X)=p
,
\dim(Y)=q
such that
p+q<n
and
p<q
(for the case
p+q>n
will be discussed later), then owing to
[
6
]
, there exists an orthogonal matrix
D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
such that

(A.1)

\mathscr{P}_{X}=D\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Id}_{p}&0&0&0\\
0&0_{p}&0&0\\
0&0&0_{q-p}&0\\
0&0&0&0_{n-p-q}\end{bmatrix}D^{*}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathscr{P}_{Y}=D\begin{bmatrix}C^{2}&CS&0&0\\
CS&S^{2}&0&0\\
0&0&\mathrm{Id}_{q-p}&0\\
0&0&0&0_{n-p-q}\end{bmatrix}D^{*},

where
C
and
S
are two
p\times p
diagonal matrices defined by

C:=\mathrm{diag}\big({\cos(\theta_{1}),\ldots,\cos(\theta_{p})}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptS:=\mathrm{diag}\big({\sin(\theta_{1}),\ldots,\sin(\theta_{p})}\big)

with the principal angles
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{p}
between
X
and
Y
found in Definition
2.4
. For simplicity, we denote

(c_{i},s_{i})=\big({\cos(\theta_{i}),\sin(\theta_{i})}\big),\,\,i=1,...,p.

Moreover, we have

2\mathscr{P}_{X}-\mathrm{Id}=\mathscr{P}_{X}-\mathscr{P}_{X^{\bot}}

where
X^{\bot}
is the orthogonal complement of
X
.

A.1
Optimal convergence rate of CARPA

From (
3.2
) we have

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathscr{P}_{Y}\mathscr{P}_{X}+\mathscr{P}_{Y^{\bot}}\mathscr{P}_{X^{\bot}}\\
\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathcal{F}_{2}=(\mathscr{P}_{Y}-\mathscr{P}_{Y^{\bot}})\mathscr{P}_{X},\\

Consequently the fixed-point operator of CARPA reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{P}_{Y}\mathscr{P}_{X}+\mathscr{P}_{Y^{\bot}}\mathscr{P}_{X^{\bot}}-\gamma\mathscr{P}_{X^{\bot}}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}

\displaystyle=(1-\mu)\mathrm{Id}+\mu\big({\mathscr{P}_{Y}\mathscr{P}_{X}+\mathscr{P}_{Y^{\bot}}\mathscr{P}_{X^{\bot}}-\gamma\mathscr{P}_{X^{\bot}}}\big).

Combine with (
A.1
), we get

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}

\displaystyle=D\begin{bmatrix}(1-\mu)\mathrm{Id}_{p}+\mu C^{2}&-\mu CS&0&0\\
\mu CS&(1-\mu-\mu\gamma)\mathrm{Id}_{p}+\mu C^{2}&0&0\\
0&0&(1-\mu-\gamma\mu)\mathrm{Id}_{q-p}&0\\
0&0&0&(1-\mu\gamma)\mathrm{Id}_{n-p-q}\end{bmatrix}D^{*}.

The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\mu}
is then determined by the block diagonal matrix. Note that when
\mu<2/(1+\gamma)
, then
\gamma\mu<1
,
-1<1-\mu-\gamma\mu<1
and
1-\gamma\mu<1
. As a result, we only need to study the eigenvalues of

(A.3)

M=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\mu)\mathrm{Id}_{p}+\mu C^{2}&-\mu CS\\
\mu CS&(1-\mu-\mu\gamma)\mathrm{Id}_{p}+\mu C^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

As
C,S
are diagonal, studying the eigenvalues of
M
in (
A.3
) is equivalent to studying the eigenvalue of the following
2\times 2
matrix,

N=\begin{bmatrix}1-\mu+\mu c^{2}&-\mu cs\\
\mu cs&1-\mu-\mu\gamma+\mu c^{2}\end{bmatrix}

where

satisfying

.
It is trivial to see that
N
has the following two eigenvalues

\lambda_{1,2}=1-\mu+\mu c^{2}+\frac{-\gamma\mu\pm\mu\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-4c^{2}s^{2}}}{2}.

We need to study the dependencies of
\lambda
over
\gamma,\mu
, for which we have the following proposition.

From now on, we denote intervals

\mathcal{I}_{1}=\Big[0,{{\frac{\raisebox{0.42711pt}{\footnotesize$1-\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}$}}{\raisebox{-1.28119pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}}}\Big],\,\,\,\mathcal{I}_{2}=\Big[{{\frac{\raisebox{0.42711pt}{\footnotesize$1+\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}$}}{\raisebox{-1.28119pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}}},1\Big]\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathcal{I}_{3}=[0,1]\verb|\|(\mathcal{I}_{1}\cup\mathcal{I}_{2}).

\tilde{s}={{\frac{\raisebox{0.42711pt}{\footnotesize$1+\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}$}}{\raisebox{-1.28119pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}}},\,\,\,\mu_{0}={{\frac{\raisebox{0.42711pt}{\footnotesize$2$}}{\raisebox{-1.28119pt}{\footnotesize$1+\gamma+\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}$}}}},\,\,\,\varphi=\max\{|1-\mu\gamma|,|1-\mu-\mu\gamma|\}.

.

Let
\gamma\in[0,1]
,

is linearly convergent to
\mathscr{P}_{{\mathrm{fix}}\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}}=\mathscr{P}_{X\cap Y}

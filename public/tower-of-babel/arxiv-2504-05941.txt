Abstract
We study the problem of determining self-sustained oscillations in discrete-time linear time-invariant relay feedback systems. Concretely, we are interested in predicting when such a system admits unimodal oscillations, i.e., when the output has a single-peaked period. Under the assumption that the linear system is stable and has an impulse response that is strictly monotonically decreasing on its infinite support, we take a novel approach in using the framework of total positivity to address our main question. It is shown that unimodal self-oscillations can only exist if the number of positive and negative elements in a period coincides. Based on this result, we derive conditions for the existence of such oscillations, determine bounds on their periods, and address the question of uniqueness.

We first examine the characteristics of unimodal self-oscillations when the impulse response of the DT-LTI system in the DT-RFS is strictly monotonically decreasing on its infinite support. It is shown that unimodal self-oscillations can only exist if the number of positive and negative elements in a period coincide. Based on this result, we derive conditions under which the DT-RFS does not exhibit oscillations when the system is of relative degree zero.

More generally, we demonstrate that the maximum oscillation period is bounded by the delay time, which is derived from the impulse response. Given the maximum period, one can determine the existence of other oscillation periods. Furthermore, if the impulse response is convex on its support, we can obtain a tighter bound for the maximum period. These conclusions are verified by several examples.

Problem Statement

In this work, we study the existence of sustained unimodal oscillations in discrete-time relay feedback systems (see

1
), which is comprised of a causal linear-time invariant system

in negative feedback with the ideal relay function. Using our defined operator notations, this feedback interconnection reads as

\displaystyle u(t)=-\mathcal{C}_{g}(\textnormal{sign}(u))(t),\quad t\in\mathds{Z}.

(7)

Therefore, unimodal self-sustained oscillations can be identified with periodically unimodal fixed-points
u\in\ell_{\infty}(P)
satisfying
Eq.

7

In particular, we make the following assumptions on the impulse response

:

Assumption 1

The impulse response
g
to

satisfies
g\in\ell_{1}
, has a connected support
\{t_{1}+i\}_{i\in\mathds{Z}_{\geq 0}}
for some

IV
Self-oscillations via variation

A main tool for our studies is the framework of total positivity
[
21
,
22
]
, which characterizes convolution operators
\mathcal{C}_{g}
that map periodically unimodal inputs to periodically unimodal outputs via the notion of variation. In this section, we define the required notions and the review relevant preliminary results of that framework.

Definition 1
(Variation)

For a vector
v\in\mathds{R}^{n}
, we define
S^{-}(v)
as the number of sign changes in
v
, i.e.,

\displaystyle S^{-}(v):=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathds{1}_{\mathds{R}_{<0}}(\tilde{v}_{i}\tilde{v}_{i+1}),\quad S^{-}(0)=-1,

where
\tilde{v}\in\mathds{R}^{m}
is the vector resulting from deleting all zeros in
v
. Further, we define
S^{+}(v):=S^{-}(\bar{v})
, where
\bar{v}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
results from replacing zero elements in
v
by any real elements that maximize
S^{-}(\bar{v})
.

Clearly,
S^{-}(v)\leq S^{+}(v)
, but equality does not necessarily hold. For example, if
v=[1,0,3]^{\top}
, then
S^{-}(v)=0
, but if we choose
\bar{v}=[1,a,3]^{\top}
with
a<0
, we obtain
S^{+}(v)=S^{-}(\bar{v})=2
.
We also need the notion of so-called
cyclic variation
for
v\in\mathds{R}^{n}
, which is defined as follows.

\displaystyle S_{c}^{-}(v):=\sup_{i\in(1:n)}S^{-}([v_{i},\dots,v_{n},v_{1},\dots,v_{i}]),

\displaystyle S_{c}^{+}(v):=\sup_{i\in(1:n)}S^{+}([v_{i},\dots,v_{n},v_{1},\dots,v_{i}]).

We are ready, now, to precisely define self-sustained oscillation via cyclic variation:

Definition 2
(Self-oscillation)

The sequence
u\in\ell_{\infty}(P)
is called a self-sustained oscillation if and only if
u
satisfies
Eq.

7
and
S_{c}^{-}(\Delta_{c}u^{P})\geq 2
.

For the remainder of the paper, we will also use the shortened term “self-oscillation”. The condition
S_{c}^{-}(\Delta_{c}u^{P})\geq 2
ensures that the sequence
u
is not constant. This is analogous to the notion of
limit cycles

[
12
]
, where equilibrium points, i.e., constant limit cycles, are excluded.

Periodic unimodality of
u\in\ell_{\infty}(P)\setminus\{0\}
can be equivalently characterized by
S_{c}^{-}(\Delta_{c}u^{P})=2
(see
[
22
, Lem. 3]
). In our analysis, we require the following slightly stronger form of periodic unimodality:

Assumption 2

The sequence
u\in\ell_{\infty}(P)
satisfies

\displaystyle S_{c}^{-}(\Delta_{c}u^{P})=2\quad\text{and}\quad S_{c}^{+}(u^{P})=2.

(8)

The following lemma provides a way of verifying whether the circulant matrix
H_{v}
satisfies
S_{c}^{-}(\Delta_{c}(H_{v}w))\leq 2
for any
w
fulfilling
S_{c}^{-}(\Delta_{c}w)=2
. This is important, as it allows us to check whether via
Eq.

6
, whether the

loop gain

\displaystyle o(t)=-\mathcal{C}_{g}(\textnormal{sign}(u))(t)

(9)

maps periodically unimodal inputs to periodically unimodal outputs.

Lemma 1

Let
v\in\mathds{R}^{n}
and
S_{c}^{+}(v)\leq 2
. Then, for any
w\in\mathds{R}^{n}
satisfying
S_{c}^{-}(\Delta_{c}w)\leq 2
, it holds that
S_{c}^{-}(\Delta_{c}H_{\textnormal{sign}(v)}w)\leq 2
.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we analyze the existence of unimodal self-oscillation in discrete-time relay feedback systems (DT-RFS) under the assumption of a strictly monotonically decreasing impulse response. Leveraging the framework of total positivity, we show that if the DT-RFS exhibits a unimodal self-oscillations, then number of positive and negative elements in one period must be equal. Based on this result, we provide conditions for the absence and existence of such oscillations as well as bounds for their periods.
Finally, we believe that our analysis in the discrete-time domain will play a vital role in understanding the continuous-time domain via discretization. In the future, it would also be interesting to explore oscillations with higher variation and to investigate their relationship with the pattern of the impulse response.

1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the set of all polynomials of degree
n.
For
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
define

\displaystyle\|P\|_{p}

\displaystyle:=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/p}\qquad(0<p<\infty),

\displaystyle\|P\|_{\infty}:=\underset{|z|=1}{\max}|P(z)|\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\|P\|_{0}

\displaystyle:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P(e^{i\theta})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta\right).

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j},
then

\displaystyle|a_{n}|+|a_{0}|\leq\|P\|_{\infty}.

(1.1)

This inequality is due to Visser’s inequality
[
7
]
and it is well known that equality holds in (
1.1
) only when
a_{j}=0
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n-1.
We refer the reader to the book of Rahman and Schmeisser
[
6
]
for a survey of extensions of Visser’s inequality.

Recently, following extenstion of (
1.1
) in integral norm was proved in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j},
then for
0\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\left\|a_{n}z+\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|P\right\|_{p},\qquad s=0,1,\ldots,n-1.

(1.2)

The case
s=n-1
was independently obtained in
[
5
]
.

For the class of polynomials having no zero in
|z|<1,
in
[
3
]
the following improvement of Theorem
1.1
was also obtained.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j}
does not vanish in
|z|<1,
then for
0\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\left\|a_{n}z+\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left\|P\right\|_{p},\qquad s=0,1,\ldots,n-1.

c_{p}=\begin{cases}\quad\quad 1\quad\quad\text{if}\quad s=0\\
~~\dfrac{1}{\|1+z\|_{p}}\quad~\text{if}\quad s>0.\end{cases}

(1.3)

It is worth to mention here that the above result is also implicit in the main result of
[
2
]
. In this paper, Arestov studied inequalities for Schur-Szegö composition of
\Lambda_{\gamma}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\gamma_{j}z^{j}
having all zeros in
|z|\leq 1
and a polynomial
P(z)
with no zero in
|z|<1.
It was proved that for any function
\varphi(x)=\psi(\log x),
where
\psi
is convex and non-decreasing function on
\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\varphi

\displaystyle\left(|(1+e^{i\theta})\Lambda_{\gamma}P(e^{it})|\right)dtd\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\varphi\left(|(\gamma_{0}+e^{i\theta}\gamma_{n})P(e^{it})|\right)dtd\theta.

(1.4)

Note that Theorem
1.2
follows from (
1.4
) by taking
\Lambda_{\gamma}=z^{n}+z^{s}
and
\varphi(x)=x^{p},
when
p>0
and
\varphi(x)=\log x
if
p=0.

2
Main results

In this paper, we consider the class of polynomials having no zero in
|z|<\rho
where
\rho\geq 1
and obtain a generalization of Theorem
1.2
. In this direction, we prove:

Theorem 2.1
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j}
does not vanish in
|z|<\rho
where
\rho\geq 1
then for
0\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\left\|a_{n}z+\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\frac{\left\|1+z\right\|_{p}}{\left\|\rho^{s}+z\right\|_{p}}\left\|P\right\|_{p},\qquad s=0,1,\ldots,n-1

(2.1)

where
c_{p}
is given by (
1.3
). Moreover, if
a_{j}=0
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n-1
and
s=0,
then equality holds in (
2.1
).

The first Corollary is obtained by letting
p\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.1
).

Corollary 2.2
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j}
does not vanish in
|z|<\rho
where
\rho\geq 1
then for
s=1,\ldots,n-1

\displaystyle|a_{n}|+\frac{|a_{s}|}{\binom{n}{s}}\leq\frac{\left\|P\right\|_{\infty}}{1+\rho^{s}}.

(2.2)

By using inequality (
2.1
) in conjunction with Lemma
3.4
, we obtain following generalization of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j}
does not vanish in
|z|<\rho
where
\rho\geq 1
then for
0\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle|a_{n}|+\frac{|a_{s}|}{\binom{n}{s}}\leq 2c_{p}\frac{\left\|P\right\|_{p}}{\left\|\rho^{s}+z\right\|_{p}}

(2.3)

where
s=0,1,\ldots,n-1
and

c_{p}
is given by (
1.3
).

3
Lemmas

Lemma 3.1
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j}
does not vanish in
|z|<\rho
where
\rho\geq 1
then

\displaystyle\rho^{s}\left|a_{n}z^{n}+\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{s}\right|\leq\left|\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{n-s}+a_{0}\right|\qquad\text{for}\quad|z|=1.

(3.1)

Proof.

Let
F(z)=P(\rho z).
Since
P(z)
has all its zeros in
|z|\geq\rho,
then all the zeros of
F^{\star}(z)=z^{n}\overline{F(1/\overline{z})}
lie in
|z|\leq 1
and
|F^{\star}(z)|=|F(z)|
for
|z|=1.
Since
F(z)
does not vanish in
|z|<1,
therefore
F^{\star}(z)/F(z)
is analytic in
|z|\leq 1.
By maximum modulus principle
|F^{\star}(z)|\leq|F(z)|
for
|z|\leq 1
or equivalently
|F(z)|\leq|F^{\star}(z)|
for
|z|\geq 1.
By using Rouché’s theorem it follows that all the zeros of polynomial

F(z)+\lambda F^{\star}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(\rho^{j}a_{j}+\lambda\rho^{n-j}\overline{a}_{n-j})z^{j}

lie in
|z|\leq 1
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|>1.
If
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}
are zeros of
F(z)+\lambda F^{\star}(z),
then
|z_{j}|\leq 1,

j=1,2,\ldots,n
and we have by Viète’s formula for
s=0,1,\ldots,n-1,

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-s}\left(\frac{\rho^{s}a_{s}+\lambda\rho^{n-s}\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\rho^{n}a_{n}+\lambda\bar{a}_{0}}\right)=\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{n-s}\leq n}z_{i_{1}}z_{i_{2}}\ldots z_{i_{n-s}}

which gives

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\rho^{s}a_{s}+\lambda\rho^{n-s}\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\rho^{n}a_{n}+\lambda\bar{a}_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{n-s}\leq n}\left|z_{i_{1}}z_{i_{2}}\ldots z_{i_{n-s}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{n-s}=\binom{n}{s}.

(3.2)

Therefore, all the zeros of polynomial

\displaystyle M(z)

\displaystyle=(\rho^{n}a_{n}+\lambda\bar{a}_{0})z^{n}+\frac{\rho^{s}a_{s}+\lambda\rho^{n-s}\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{s}

\displaystyle=\rho^{n}a_{n}z^{n}+\rho^{s}\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{s}+\lambda\left(\bar{a}_{0}z^{n}+\rho^{n-s}\frac{\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{s}\right)

lie in
|z|\leq 1
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|>1
and hence for
r>1,
the polynomial

\displaystyle M(rz)=\rho^{n}a_{n}(rz)^{n}+\rho^{s}\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}(rz)^{s}+\lambda\left(\bar{a}_{0}(rz)^{n}+\rho^{n-s}\frac{\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}(rz)^{s}\right)

has all its zeros in
|z|<1.
This implies

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{n}a_{n}(rz)^{n}+\rho^{s}\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}(rz)^{s}\right|\leq\left|\bar{a}_{0}(rz)^{n}+\rho^{n-s}\frac{\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}(rz)^{k}\right|

(3.3)

for
|z|\geq 1.
Indeed, if inequality (
3.3
) is not true, then there exists a point
w
with
|w|\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{n}a_{n}(rw)^{n}+\rho^{s}\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}(rw)^{s}\right|>\left|\bar{a}_{0}(rw)^{n}+\rho^{n-s}\frac{\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}(rw)^{k}\right|

Since all the zeros of
F^{\star}(z)=z^{n}\overline{P(\rho/\overline{z})}=\bar{a}_{0}z^{n}+\bar{a}_{1}\rho z^{(n-1)}+\cdots+\bar{a}_{(n-s)}\rho^{(n-s)}z^{s}+\cdots+\bar{a}_{n}\rho^{n}
lie in
|z|\leq 1,
Viète’s formula again yields, as before in (
3.2
),
|\bar{a}_{0}|\geq\rho^{n-s}|\bar{a}_{n-s}|/\binom{n}{s},
which implies that
\bar{a_{0}}(rw)^{n}+\rho^{n-s}\bar{a}_{n-s}/\binom{n}{s}(rw)^{s}\neq 0.
We take

\lambda=-\frac{\rho^{n}a_{n}(rw)^{n}+\rho^{s}\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}(rw)^{s}}{\bar{a}_{0}(rw)^{n}+\rho^{n-s}\frac{\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}(rw)^{s}},

then
\lambda
is a well defined real or complex number with
|\lambda|>1
and with this choice of
\lambda,
we obtain
M(rw)=0
where
|w|\geq 1.
This contradicts the fact that all the zeros of
M(rz)
lie in
|z|<1.
Thus (
3.3
) holds. Letting
r\rightarrow 1
in (
3.3
) and using continuity, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{n}a_{n}z^{n-s}+\rho^{s}\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}\right|\leq\left|\bar{a}_{0}z^{n}+\rho^{n-s}\frac{\bar{a}_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{s}\right|=\left|\frac{\rho^{n-s}a_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{n-s}+a_{0}\right|

(3.4)

for
|z|=1.
Again, since
|\bar{a}_{0}|\geq\rho^{n-s}|\bar{a}_{n-s}|/\binom{n}{s}
the polynomial
\frac{\rho^{n-s}a_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{n-s}+a_{0}
does not vanish in
|z|<1.
Hence by the maximum modulus principle,

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{n}a_{n}z^{n-s}+\rho^{s}\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}\right|\leq\left|\rho^{n-s}\frac{a_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{n-s}+a_{0}\right|\quad\text{for}\quad|z|\leq 1.

(3.5)

Taking
z=e^{i\theta}/\rho
where
0\leq\theta<2\pi
in (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle\rho^{s}\left|a_{n}e^{i(n-s)\theta}+\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}\right|\leq\left|\frac{a_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}e^{i(n-s)\theta}+a_{0}\right|.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\rho^{s}\left|a_{n}z^{n}+\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{s}\right|\leq\left|\frac{a_{n-s}}{\binom{n}{s}}z^{n-s}+a_{0}\right|\qquad\text{for}~~|z|=1.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j}

|z|<1,

\phi

and we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|G(\theta)+e^{i\phi}Q(\theta)\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi=

\displaystyle|G(\theta)|^{p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|1+e^{i\phi}\frac{Q(\theta)}{G(\theta)}\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|G(\theta)|^{p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\phi}+\left|\frac{Q(\theta)}{G(\theta)}\right|\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\geq|G(\theta)|^{p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\rho^{s}+e^{i\phi}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi.

For
G(\theta)=0,
this inequality is trivially true. Using this in (
4.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|G(\theta)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\rho^{s}+e^{i\phi}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Lambda^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta.

This implies

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|a_{n}e^{in\theta}+\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}e^{is\theta}\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Lambda^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\right\}^{1/p}\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|P(e^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta\right\}^{1/p}}{\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\rho^{s}+e^{i\phi}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\right\}^{1/p}}.

which in conjunction with the fact that
\|az^{n}+b\|_{p}=\|az+b\|_{p}
gives

\displaystyle\left\|a_{n}z+\frac{a_{s}}{\binom{n}{s}}\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left\|1+z\right\|_{p}\frac{\left\|P\right\|_{p}}{\left\|z+\rho^{s}\right\|_{p}}

where
c_{p}
is given by (
1.3
). This proves Theorem
2.1
for
p>0.
To obtain this result for
p=0,
we simply make
p\rightarrow 0+.

∎

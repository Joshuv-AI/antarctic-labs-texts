The emergence of long-range entanglement and odd-even effect in periodic generalized cluster models
Zhen-Yu Zheng, Shu Chen

Abstract
We investigate the entanglement properties in a generalized cluster model under periodic boundary condition. By evaluating the entanglement entropy and the quantum conditional mutual information entropy under three or four subsystem partitions, we identify clear signatures of long-range entanglement. Specifically, when both the system size N N and the interaction range m m are odd, the system exhibits nonzero four-part quantum conditional mutual information entropies. This non-vanishing four-part quantum conditional mutual information entropy directly signals the presence of long-range entanglement. In contrast, all other combination of N N and m m yield vanishing four-part quantum conditional mutual information entropy. Remarkably, in the case of N , m ∈ odd N,m\in\text{odd} , these long-range entangled features persist even in the presence of a finite transverse field, demonstrating their robustness against quantum fluctuations. These results demonstrate how the interplay between system size and interaction range governs the emergence of long-range entanglement in one-dimensional spin systems.

II
Model and exact solution of ground-state

We investigate a family of one-dimensional generalized cluster models characterized by multi-spin interaction Hamiltonian of the form:

H=J\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{j}^{x}\tau_{j,m}^{z}\sigma_{j+m}^{x},

(1)

where the cluster operator
\tau_{j,m}^{z}=\sigma_{j+1}^{z}\cdots\sigma_{j+m-1}^{z}

spans
m-1
intermediate sites, with
m
determining the interaction range of each multi-spin term. The Pauli matrices
\sigma_{i}^{\alpha}
(
\alpha=x,z
) act on site
i
. For convenience, assume the interaction strength
J=1
and periodic boundary conditions (
\sigma_{N+1}=\sigma_{1}
) throughout our analysis. Using the Jordan–Wigner transformation,

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{x}

\displaystyle=(c_{j}^{\dagger}+c_{j})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi\sum_{l<j}c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{l}},\quad\sigma_{j}^{z}=2c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}-1,

(2)

the Hamiltonian can be rewritten as

H=P_{z}^{-}H^{+}P_{z}^{-}+P_{z}^{+}H^{-}P_{z}^{+},

(3)

where the projectors are given by

P_{z}^{\pm}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1\pm\mathcal{P}_{z}),\quad\mathcal{P}_{z}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi\sum_{j}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}}.

(4)

Here
\mathcal{P}_{z}
denotes the fermion-parity operator. The two fermion Hamiltonians take the form,

\displaystyle H^{\pm}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m-1}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{N-m}\bigl(c_{j}^{\dagger}-c_{j}\bigr)\bigl(c_{j+m}^{\dagger}+c_{j+m}\bigr)

(5)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{P}_{z}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(c_{N-m+i}^{\dagger}-c_{N-m+i}\bigr)\bigl(c_{i}^{\dagger}+c_{i}\bigr)\Biggr].

It follows that
H^{+}
corresponds to periodic boundary conditions (PBC) with
c_{N+1}=c_{1}
, while
H^{-}
corresponds to anti-periodic boundary conditions (APBC) with
c_{N+1}=-c_{1}
.

We first consider system with odd size (
N\in
odd). Under PBC and APBC, the quantized momentum values are respectively given by:

\begin{gathered}Q^{+}\in\left\{-\tfrac{N-1}{N}\pi,\,\ldots,\,-\tfrac{2}{N}\pi,\,0,\,\tfrac{2}{N}\pi,\,\ldots,\,\tfrac{N-1}{N}\pi\right\},\\
Q^{-}\in\left\{-\tfrac{N-2}{N}\pi,\,\ldots,\,-\tfrac{1}{N}\pi,\,\tfrac{1}{N}\pi,\,\ldots,\,\tfrac{N-2}{N}\pi,\,\pi\right\},\end{gathered}

(6)

where
Q^{+}
and
Q^{-}
correspond to PBC and APBC cases.

By applying the Fourier transform

c_{j}^{\dagger}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{q}c_{q}^{\dagger}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}qj}

together with the Bogoliubov transformation

\eta_{q}=u_{q}c_{q}-iv_{q}c_{-q}^{\dagger}
,
the two fermionic Hamiltonians in momentum space can be diagonalized as

\begin{gathered}H^{+}=\sum_{q\in q^{+}}\left(2\eta_{q}^{\dagger}\eta_{q}-1\right)-(-1)^{m}\left(2c_{0}^{\dagger}c_{0}-1\right),\\
H^{-}=\sum_{q\in q^{-}}\left(2\eta_{q}^{\dagger}\eta_{q}-1\right)-\left(2c_{\pi}^{\dagger}c_{\pi}-1\right),\end{gathered}

(7)

where the allowed momenta are restricted to

q^{+}=\left\{\tfrac{2\pi m}{N}\,\big|\,m=1,2,\dots,\tfrac{N-1}{2}\right\}

and

q^{-}=\left\{\tfrac{2\pi m-1}{N}\,\big|\,m=1,2,\dots,\tfrac{N-1}{2}\right\}
, respectively.
The Bogoliubov coefficients are given by

\begin{gathered}u_{q}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr(1-(-1)^{m}\cos(mq)\bigr);\\
v_{q}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr(1+(-1)^{m}\cos(mq)\bigr);\\
u_{q}v_{q}=-(-1)^{m}\sin(mq),\\
\end{gathered}

(8)

For clarity in subsequent discussions, we denote the vacuum states of
H_{m}^{+}
and
H_{m}^{-}
in
\eta
representation as
|\varphi^{+}\rangle
and
|\varphi^{-}\rangle
, respectively. Their explicit expressions read:

\begin{gathered}|\varphi^{+}\rangle=\prod_{q\in Q^{+}}\left(u_{q}+iv_{q}c_{q}^{\dagger}c_{-q}^{\dagger}\right)|0\rangle,\\
|\varphi^{-}\rangle=\prod_{q\in Q^{-}}\left(u_{q}+iv_{q}c_{q}^{\dagger}c_{-q}^{\dagger}\right)|0\rangle,\end{gathered}

(9)

where
|0\rangle
denotes the fermionic vacuum.

Based on Eq.(
3
), we find that only states with an odd number of occupied fermions are allowed for
H^{+}
, while only those with an even number of occupied fermions are valid for
H^{-}

. All other states are redundant for the full Hamiltonian
H
. Consequently, the ground-state degeneracy structure is fundamentally determined by the range
m
of the multi-spin interaction, revealing two distinct scenarios:
(i) For even
m
: The system presents two degenerate ground states:
c_{0}^{\dagger}|\varphi^{+}\rangle
and
|\varphi^{-}\rangle
; (ii) For odd
m
: The degeneracy of ground state expands to
2N
and ground states include:

c_{0}^{\dagger}|\varphi^{+}\rangle
,
\eta_{q\in Q^{+},q\neq 0}^{{\dagger}}|\varphi^{+}\rangle
,

\eta_{q\in Q^{-},q\neq\pi}^{{\dagger}}\eta_{\pi}^{{\dagger}}|\varphi^{-}\rangle
, and
|\varphi^{-}\rangle
. This degeneracy exhibits system size dependent scaling.

In contrast, for even system size (
N\in
even), the quantized momentum values for PBC and APBC are given by:

\begin{gathered}Q^{+}\in\left\{-\tfrac{N-2}{N}\pi,\,\ldots,\,-\tfrac{2}{N}\pi,\,0,\,\tfrac{2}{N}\pi,\,\ldots,\,\tfrac{N-2}{N}\pi,\pi\right\},\\
Q^{-}\in\left\{-\tfrac{N-1}{N}\pi,\,\ldots,\,-\tfrac{1}{N}\pi,\,\tfrac{1}{N}\pi,\,\ldots,\,\tfrac{N-1}{N}\pi\right\},\end{gathered}

(10)

and the following fermion Hamiltonians can be diagonalized as:

\begin{gathered}H_{m}^{+}=\sum_{q\in q^{+}}\bigl(2\eta_{q}^{\dagger}\eta_{q}-1\bigr)-(-1)^{m}\bigl(2c_{0}^{\dagger}c_{0}-1\bigr)-\bigl(2c_{\pi}^{\dagger}c_{\pi}-1\bigr);\\
H_{m}^{-}=\sum_{q\in q^{-}}\bigl(2\eta_{q}^{\dagger}\eta_{q}-1\bigr).\end{gathered}

(11)

where the allowed momenta are restricted to

q^{+}=\left\{\tfrac{2\pi m}{N}\,\big|\,m=1,2,\dots,\tfrac{N-2}{2}\right\}

and

q^{-}=\left\{\tfrac{2\pi m-1}{N}\,\big|\,m=1,2,\dots,\tfrac{N}{2}\right\}
, respectively. And the vacuum states of
H_{m}^{+}
and
H_{m}^{-}
in
\eta
representation are:

\begin{gathered}|\varphi^{+}\rangle=\prod_{q\in q^{+}}(u_{q}+\mathrm{i}v_{q}c_{q}^{\dagger}c_{-q}^{\dagger})|0\rangle,\\
|\varphi^{-}\rangle=\prod_{q\in q^{-}}(u_{q}+\mathrm{i}v_{q}c_{q}^{\dagger}c_{-q}^{\dagger})|0\rangle.\end{gathered}

(12)

However, the ground-state degeneracy structure is independent of the range
m
of the multi-spin interaction. Regardless of whether
m
is even or odd, the system always possesses a unique ground state, which takes the form
|\varphi^{-}\rangle
.

The analysis of the ground-state degeneracy can also be carried out by mapping the model to another spin representation via the Kramers–Wannier transformation, as shown in the Appendix
A
.

III
Entanglement entropy and quantum condition mutual information

To gain deeper insight into the entanglement properties of generalized cluster models, we analyze the entanglement entropy
45
and the quantum conditional mutual information
50
;
41
to characterize their non-local property and the nature of their long-range entanglement.

III.1
Non-locality in entanglement entropy

The entanglement entropy is defined via the reduced density matrix as

S_{l}=-\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho_{l}\log_{2}\rho_{l}\right),

(13)

where
\rho_{l}
is the reduced density matrix with a block of
l
spins
45
and
l\geq m
is chosen for computational convenience and to guarantee that the entanglement entropy reaches its saturation value. Following the method of Ref.
45
;
21
,
S_{l}
can be computed from the correlation matrix
\Gamma_{l}
as

S_{l}=-\sum_{j=1}^{2l}f(v_{j})=-\sum_{j=1}^{2l}\frac{1+v_{j}}{2}\log_{2}\frac{1+v_{j}}{2},

(14)

where
v_{j}
are the imaginary parts of the eigenvalues of
\Gamma_{l}
, which takes the block-Toeplitz form

\Gamma_{l}=\begin{vmatrix}\Pi_{0}&\Pi_{1}&\cdots&\Pi_{l-1}\\
\Pi_{-1}&\Pi_{0}&\cdots&\Pi_{l-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\Pi_{1-l}&\Pi_{2-l}&\cdots&\Pi_{0}\end{vmatrix},

(15)

with
2\times 2
block elements

\Pi_{r}=\begin{vmatrix}0&-g_{r}\\
g_{-r}&0\end{vmatrix}.

(16)

The
g_{r}
represents the correlation which is defined as
g_{r}=\langle(c_{j}^{{\dagger}}-c_{j})(c_{j+r}^{{\dagger}}+c_{j+r})\rangle
.

Firstly, we consider the case with
N\in\text{odd}
and
m\in\text{odd}
.
Without loss of generality, we focus on the definite-parity ground state
c_{0}^{\dagger}|\varphi^{+}\rangle
, which satisfies the translational symmetry, within the
2N
-fold degenerate ground-state manifold. Remarkably, all other degenerate ground states, for example the state
|\varphi^{-}\rangle
, exhibit identical entanglement properties. Therefore, the specific choice of ground state does not affect our conclusions.
The elements of the correlation matrix
\Gamma_{l}
take the form,

g_{r}=(-1)^{m}\delta_{-r,m}+\frac{2}{N},

(17)

Although the term
\frac{2}{N}
vanishes in the strict thermodynamic limit, it cannot be neglected here. This is because the size of the correlation matrix scales linearly with
N
, and hence the cumulative contribution of this term remains finite.

Due to the effect of the term
\frac{2}{N}
, the eigenvalues of the correlation matrix are given by

\left\{\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{2(m-1)},\underbrace{-1,1,\dots,-1,1}_{2(l-m-1)},v_{1},-v_{1},v_{2},-v_{2}\right\},

(18)

where

\begin{gathered}v_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{4(\alpha^{2}+\beta-\alpha)+1+(1-2\alpha)x},\\
v_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{4(\alpha^{2}+\beta-\alpha)+1+(2\alpha-1)x},\\
x=\sqrt{4(\alpha^{2}+2\beta-\alpha)+1},\end{gathered}

(19)

where
\alpha=\frac{l}{N},\beta=\frac{m}{N}
. Notice that the factor
\alpha
will propose the non-local information in entanglement. Then, the entanglement entropy is given by,

S_{l}=m-1-f(v_{1})-f(-v_{1})-f(v_{2})-f(-v_{2}).

(20)

In the thermodynamic limit (
N\rightarrow\infty
), the entanglement entropy is satisfied:

m\leq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}S_{l}\leq m+1\quad\text{for}\quad m\leq l\leq\frac{N-1}{2}.

(21)

In comparison, we also analyze the rest of the cases of the model. The ground state is chosen by
|\varphi^{-}\rangle
for convenience.
Specifically, under these conditions, the elements of correlation matrix are the same and can be simplified as

g_{r}=(-1)^{m}\delta_{-r,m},

(22)

and the eigenvalues of the correlation matrix are given by

\left\{\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{2m},\underbrace{-1,1,\dots,-1,1}_{2(l-m)},\right\},

(23)

The entanglement entropy can be computed exactly, yielding

S_{l}=-m\log_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=m.

(24)

From Eq. (
21
) and Eq. (
24
), we observe that although the entanglement entropy in both cases satisfies an area law in the thermodynamic limit, there remains a significant difference in entanglement properties. Specifically, when both
N
and
m
are odd, the entanglement entropy depends not only on the interaction range
m
itself, but also on the non-local factor
\alpha
, i.e., the fraction between the block size
l
and the total system size
N
. As shown in Fig.
1
(a), the entropy increases with the block size
l
, for
l=m
, it takes the value
m
, and when the block size
l
approaches half of the system size, it reaches its maximum
m+1
.
Hence, the entanglement entropy does not saturate but continues to grow with the subsystem size. Consequently, the entanglement is not solely determined by boundary degrees of freedom but is also tied to global features, highlighting the system’s long-range correlations.

In contrast, for all other cases, the entanglement entropy depends solely on the interaction range

m
and remains independent of the block size

l
, exhibiting a clear saturation plateau in full agreement with Eq. (
24
), as shown in Fig.
1
(b–d), which indicates the presence of short-range correlations. Thus, while the entanglement entropy always obeys an area law asymptotically, the presence of the non-local factor
\alpha
in the odd-
N
, odd-
m
case provides an additional enhancement mechanism, leading to stronger correlations than those dictated by the cluster interaction range alone.

In Fig.

We find that when both the system size

It increases monotonically with the block size and reaches its maximum when the block covers half of the total system size, as shown in Fig.

In contrast, for all other combinations of

When the transverse field becomes strong (

The detailed numerical verification and fitting procedure are provided in the Appendix B.

In contrast, for other cases,
S_{cmi}^{t}
saturates to a constant value as the system size increases,
consistent with the one-dimensional area law of entanglement.

In contrast, a qualitatively different feature emerges in the four-part conditional mutual information entropy.
As shown in Fig.
6
(a2), when both the interaction range
m
and the system size
N
are odd,
the four-part entropy
S_{cmi}^{q}
remains finite for
h<J
.
This non-vanishing behavior demonstrates the persistence of non-local entanglement in the presence of quantum fluctuations.
Because
S_{cmi}^{q}
effectively cancels local contributions, its finite value directly reflects genuine long-range quantum correlations and entanglement.
In all other parameter regimes, as shown in Fig.
6
(b2),
S_{cmi}^{q}
is equal to zero, confirming the absence of long-range entanglement in those cases.

In Fig.
6
(c1)-(c2) and (d1)-(d2), we present the behavior of the tripartite and quadripartite conditional mutual information in the paramagnetic phase. For the regime
h>J
, we find that the tripartite conditional mutual information entropy
remains constant for all combinations of
m
and
N
,
while the four-part mutual information entropy vanishes.
As the transverse field becomes much stronger
(h\gg J)
,
the tripartite entropy also approaches zero,
indicating that the ground state in this regime is a disentangled paramagnetic phase.

The above results demonstrate that quantum fluctuations modify, but do not completely destroy,
the system’s entanglement structure.
The tripartite mutual information reflects stable short-range correlations,
whereas the four-part entropy selectively captures long-range entanglement.
In particular, for
m,N\in\text{odd}
, the survival of a finite
S_{cmi}^{q}
under
h<J
confirms that
the system retains its non-local entanglement and topological character even in the presence of a transverse field.
As a special case, when
m=1
, the model reduces to the transverse-field Ising chain,
for which the persistence of long-range entanglement in the phase has been explicitly demonstrated in Ref.
31
;
41
.

V
Summary and discussion

In this work, we have investigated the entanglement structure and quantum phase transitions in a generalized cluster model subjected to a transverse field.
By analyzing the entanglement entropy and conditional mutual information under different subsystem partitions, we identified distinct signatures of long-range entanglement that depend crucially on the interaction range
m
and the system size
N
.
When both
m
and
N
are odd, the system exhibits nontrivial entanglement features, manifested by long-range correlation and genuine long-range entanglement. Even in the presence of a finite transverse field, the system with odd
m
and odd

N
retains nonvanishing multipartite conditional mutual information, reflecting the persistence of long-range entanglement.
In contrast, all other parameter combinations yield fully gapped phases devoid of long-range correlations.
These results show that the system size and the interaction range play a decisive role in determining both the emergence and the stability of long-range quantum entanglement in one-dimensional cluster systems.

Because the model maps naturally onto circuits used in measurement-based quantum computation, the robust long-range entanglement observed in this sector provides a valuable resource for fault-tolerant architectures. In particular, the finite four-part conditional mutual information quantifies the multipartite correlations required for logical encoding and topological error correction. These results open promising avenues for exploring the interplay between topology, entanglement, and quantum criticality in both theoretical models and emerging experimental platforms.

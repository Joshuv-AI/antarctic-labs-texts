Hybrid optimization of WEDM control parameters for Kovar using VIKOR-AHP and R method
Authors: Kamal Ukey, Anil R. Sahu, Himadri Majumder, N. Ashok, Abhijit Bhowmik
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Kovar alloy is widely used in electronics, aerospace, and precision applications due to its thermal expansion compatibility with ceramics and glass, yet its difficult machinability demands optimized WEDM parameters for improved performance. To address this challenge, the present study aims to evaluate the influence of key WEDM factors and identify optimal machining conditions for Kovar. Experiments were conducted using a Taguchi L27 orthogonal array considering servo voltage, pulse on-time, pulse off-time, and wire feed rate as machining parameters, and their effects on cutting speed, machining time, and surface roughness were systematically analyzed. A hybrid multi-criteria decision making approach combining AHP-based weighting with VIKOR compromise ranking was employed alongside the R-method for comparative validation. Results show that the parameter set Ton: 121 µs, Toff: 49 µs, SV: 20 V, and WF: 4 m/min. achieved the best overall machining performance. Importantly, both the AHP-VIKOR method and the R-method identified the same optimal settings, demonstrating strong agreement and confirming the robustness of the hybrid optimization framework. The study highlights the scientific contribution of integrating statistical ranking with structured multi-criteria analysis to enhance decision quality in multi-response WEDM optimization for Kovar alloy.

Introduction

Wire Electrical Discharge Machining (WEDM) is a non-traditional machining process that employs controlled electrical discharges to erode electrically conductive materials with high precision. Originating in the 1960s as an advancement of conventional Electrical Discharge Machining (EDM), it has become indispensable in industries requiring intricate geometries, tight tolerances, and high-quality surface finishes. The process utilizes a continuously moving fine wire electrode-commonly brass or another conductive alloy-that generates sparks across a dielectric medium, typically deionized water. These sparks progressively remove material without physical contact1–5, enabling accurate and efficient machining of difficult-to-cut materials such as superalloys, tool steels, composites, and high-precision alloys, including Kovar6 and titanium. Emphasize improved modeling and evolving techniques for efficient WEDM of advanced materials7,8. Kovar, a nickel–cobalt–iron alloy, is widely recognized for its unique coefficient of thermal expansion, which closely matches that of ceramics and certain glasses. Originally developed to overcome challenges in sealing metals to non-metallic materials, it has found extensive application in the fabrication of vacuum electronic components, relay housings, sealing plugs, and transistor packages9. With a thermal expansion coefficient of approximately 50 ± 5 × 10 − 7 °C − 110, Kovar ensures reliable, leak-resistant seals in environments subjected to thermal fluctuations. This attribute has become increasingly important in high-technology fields such as aerospace, telecommunications, and electronics11. Due to its metallurgical properties, Kovar presents significant challenges when machined using conventional techniques. Consequently, unconventional machining methods such as WEDM are considered more effective, offering superior precision, minimal tool wear12, and high-quality surface integrity. The ability of WEDM to produce intricate profiles, slots, and through-holes with exceptional accuracy further enhances its suitability for Kovar-based applications13. The present work investigates the influence of key WEDM parameters-servo voltage (SV), wire feed rate (WF), pulse on-time (Ton), and pulse off-time (Toff) on machining responses, including surface roughness, cutting speed, and machining time during the processing of nickel–cobalt Shape Memory Alloy (SMA) Kovar. A Taguchi L27 orthogonal design is employed to systematically evaluate parameter effects. Furthermore, Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) approaches, namely VIKOR integrated with the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and the R-method, are applied to identify optimal machining conditions. This integrated framework facilitates an efficient determination of process settings that enhance machining performance while reducing operational cost.

Numerous research studies have documented the wire-cut EDM process for different conductive materials and SMA over the past 20 years. Rao et al.14 proposed a MCDM method, called the R-method for ranking alternatives. Majumder et al.15 developed the general regression neural network (GRNN) and multiple regression analysis (MRA) models to identify and relate key machinability responses, such as surface roughness (Ra), average kerf width (KW), and material removal rate (MRR), in the WEDM process of titanium grade 6. Paul et al.16 applied the MCDM approach based on the ratio analysis (MOORA) method in conjunction with principal component analysis (PCA) to attain the most effective combination of EDM parameters. Goyal et al.17 analyze the effects of the process parameter on surface characteristics of Ni49Ti51 SMA’s by brass tool electrode during WEDM. Lalwani et al.18 focus on the creation and evaluation of predictive models developed through response surface methodology (RSM) and artificial neural networks (ANN), specifically for the WEDM process. Mardani et al.19 conducted a systematic review of the current literature regarding the VIKOR technique, focusing on its methodologies and applications. Heralge et al.20 used the TOPSIS method on the three dissimilar metal alloys used in aerospace, namely near-beta Ti alloy, forged Ni alloy, and aged Al alloy, to enhance the WEDM process. Ukey et al.21 employed a hybrid methodology that integrates the VIKOR method with the AHP to determine the best parameter sets for the machining Kovar during WEDM. Dhilip et al.22 developed a hybrid composite consisting of an aluminium matrix reinforced with boron nitride and molybdenum disulfide (MoS2) through the stir casting technique. The optimal parameters for WEDM were determined using a combined entropy approach in conjunction with the VIKOR method. Chatterjee et al.23 illustrate the application of three MCDM techniques, specifically the combinative distance-based assessment (CODAS), combined compromise solution (CoCoSo), and multi-attributive ideal-real comparative analysis (MAIRCA), within an intuitionistic fuzzy (IF) framework to improve the WEDM process. Nguyen et al.24 successfully employed five different MCDM techniques for optimizing the WEDM process for titanium alloy. The diverse optimal outcomes obtained through various methods emphasize that each technique is tailored to and performs exceptionally well with distinct sets of values. Vijayanand et al.25 assessed the ideal combination of machining parameters through the innovative integration of response surface methodology (RSM), Shannon’s entropy-weight measurement, and the VIKOR method for Monel 400 alloy during micro-EDM machining.

Although Kovar alloy is widely used in precision applications, limited studies have systematically examined its machinability by WEDM, particularly with respect to multi-response optimization. Existing research primarily focuses on individual parameter effects or single-criteria optimization, offering little insight into balanced trade-offs between surface quality, cutting efficiency, and machining time. Moreover, no study has compared hybrid MCDM techniques, such as AHP-VIKOR, with ranking methods like the R-method for validating optimal parameter selection. This lack of comprehensive, multi-method optimization and validation for WEDM of Kovar alloy defines a clear gap addressed in this work.

This work introduces a combined statistical and hybrid decision-making methodology specifically tailored for machining Kovar alloy, a material that has rarely been optimized using integrated MCDM approaches. The study uniquely compares the AHP–VIKOR hybrid model with the R-method to assess consistency in multi-response optimization, offering both structured evaluation and rapid ranking capabilities. Additionally, the research provides the first comprehensive parameter–performance mapping for WEDM of Kovar alloy using a Taguchi L27 design coupled with dual MCDM methods, thereby contributing an original framework for improving decision quality in machining of difficult-to-machine materials.

Optimization: R- Method

Each decision table used in MCDM methods features a range of alternatives, attributes, attribute weights, and performance measures corresponding to those alternatives. The decision maker’s role is crucial; they must analyze the decision table and the chosen methodology to pinpoint the optimal alternative and rank all available options. The steps in the proposed method include identifying the key characteristics relevant to the decision-making scenario and assembling a list of alternatives that meet these essential criteria The decision-making procedure begins with the development of a decision table that maps the performance measures of each alternative against the selected evaluation criteria. Once established, the decision-maker assigns priorities to these criteria according to their relative significance. Each alternative is then assessed with respect to the chosen measures, which may include both quantitative data and qualitative judgments. The priority rankings of the criteria and the evaluations of the alternatives are subsequently transformed into numerical weights. By multiplying the criterion weights with the corresponding performance scores of each alternative and summing the results, a composite score is obtained for every option. The alternatives are then arranged in descending order of these scores, with the highest-ranked option representing the most suitable choice14. Validation by Vikor-AHP

Both VIKOR and AHP are widely applied techniques within the framework of MCDM for resolving complex evaluation problems involving multiple, and often conflicting, criteria. The VIKOR approach is designed to rank and select alternatives by identifying a compromise solution that balances proximity to the ideal outcome while maintaining distance from the least desirable option. It emphasizes achieving a solution that best reflects the decision-maker’s inclination toward optimality in the presence of trade-offs. In contrast, the AHP structures the decision problem into a hierarchy of goals, criteria, and alternatives, allowing systematic pairwise comparisons. This hierarchical decomposition facilitates the derivation of relative weights for both criteria and alternatives, thereby supporting consistent and rational decision-making.

VIKOR method

It is especially suitable in situations where compromise solutions are necessary and has been successfully applied across diverse fields, including renewable energy system evaluations19. Analytic hierarchy process (AHP)

Thomas Saaty developed the AHP in the 1970s as a tool for decision making27. Designed to tackle complex problems involving multiple criteria. By structuring the problem into a hierarchy of goals, criteria, and alternatives, AHP simplifies decision-making through pairwise comparisons and mathematical analysis. It combines both qualitative and quantitative factors, enabling decision-makers to systematically prioritize options, ensure logical consistency in their judgments, and ultimately select the best alternative based on a clear and transparent process. Widely applied across various fields, AHP is a robust tool for making informed, structured, and justifiable decisions.

The results and discussion section presents a comprehensive analysis of the experimental data obtained from the WEDM machining of Kovar using a hybrid optimization approach. The performance of various input parameter combinations was evaluated based on key output responses such as CS, MT and SR. A multi-criteria decision-making framework integrating VIKOR and AHP was employed to determine the optimal settings, while the R method was used to perform composite score analysis for validation. The findings are interpreted in terms of parameter effects, trade-off patterns, and the degree of agreement between the two methods, thereby offering valuable insights into the robustness and practical applicability of the hybrid approach in machining environments. The experimental results revealed that pulse-on time and wire feed rate significantly influenced CS and SR during WEDM of Kovar. Higher pulse-on time increased CS due to greater discharge energy, but also led to higher SR. Conversely, shorter pulse-on time improved surface finish at the cost of reduced CS. The hybrid VIKOR-AHP method effectively ranked the optimal parameter combinations by balancing these trade-offs. The selected optimal setting achieved a substantial improvement in overall performance. The statistical analysis performed using R confirmed the consistency of rankings and minimized decision bias, demonstrating the robustness of the proposed hybrid optimization approach.

A hybrid multi-criteria decision-making framework was implemented by using the R-method and the results were independently validated by integrating AHP-derived weighting with VIKOR-based compromise ranking. The optimization analysis identified the parameter combination Ton = 121 µs, Toff = 49 µs, SV = 20 V, and WF = 4 m/min as the optimal setting, yielding the most favorable trade-off among the considered machining responses. Notably, both the R-method and AHP-VIKOR approach consistently converged on the same optimal parameter set, indicating strong methodological agreement and confirming the robustness and reliability of the proposed hybrid optimization strategy. The findings demonstrate the scientific merit of coupling structured multi-criteria weighting with statistical ranking techniques to improve decision-making accuracy in multi-response WEDM optimization of Kovar alloy.

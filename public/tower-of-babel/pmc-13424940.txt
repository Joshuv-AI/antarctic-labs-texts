Multidisciplinary meeting for breast cancer care: EUSOMA recommendations for optimization
Authors: G Curigliano, L Marotti, C Barrios, NML Battisti, KL Cheung, S Chia, SL Graff, OJ Hartmann, I Rubio, D Santini, F Sardanelli, E Senkus, P van Dam, M Walker, J Wu, C Aristei
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

My co-authors and I would like to thank Doctor Tjalma for his comments, which give us the opportunity to explain in depth the differences between certification and recommendations and how Eusoma proceeds with its documents.

Here are our point by point replies to Doctor Tjalma's statements.

1.“The manuscript presents “EUSOMA recomendations, yet according to the official EUSOMA breast centre certification register, fewer than 15% of the centres represented by the authors are EUSOMA-certified. Consequently, more than 85% of the recommendations originate from centres that have not undergone EUSOMA audit or certification. Author affiliation or expertise cannot be equated with EUSOMA-certified practice, creating a fundamental legitimacy gap”. To draw up the document on recommendations for improving multidisciplinary meeting optimization, EUSOMA convened a working group of international experts in breast cancer and multidisciplinary meeting management. They did not necessarily work in certified centres as certification is not a pre-requisite for authorship of EUSOMA multidisciplinary recommendation. Indeed EUSOMA selects authors from among prominent experts in breast cancer, on the basis of the topic that it wants to address.

2.“The manuscript does not report which specific centres participated, despite referring to 43 of 44 European centres, while the official EUSOMA register listed 45 certified centres at the time. Moreover, the analysis appears to conflate EU versus non-EU centres with EUSOMA-certified versus non-certified practice, although EUSOMA certification is not restricted to EU countries.” The questionnaire was sent to 44 European EUSOMA-certified breast centres (the 45th was a non-European Centre) and 50 non-European (non-EU) breast centres in the United States, Australia, China and Brazil. The non-EU breast centres were proposed by the non-EU experts in the working group. Generally speaking, when survey results are presented, the participating centres are not specified by name but their provenance is indicated.

6.“This is particularly relevant given the availability of an updated EUSOMA quality indicator framework, which explicitly anchors multidisciplinary care in measurable, auditable standard. The work should therefore be interpreted as an expert consensus and descriptive survey, rather than guidance grounded in EUSOMA-audited implementation”. We can only repeat that quality indicators and recommendations are different. Quality indicators, as reported in the document EUSOMA published in 2023 (Rubio et al. EJC), aim at reducing diagnostic and therapeutic variability and thus improving patient outcomes. On the other hand, the present paper on multidisciplinary meeting optimization suggests how multidisciplinary meetings can be optimized, without reporting any indicator, measurement system or benchmarks.

As stated above we agree the paper “is an expert consensus and descriptive survey”.

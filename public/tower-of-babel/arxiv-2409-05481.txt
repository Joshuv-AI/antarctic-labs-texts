Stanley-Reisner ideals with pure resolutions
David Carey, Mordechai Katzman

Abstract
. This paper investgates Stanley-Reisner ideals with pure resolutions. We first describe two infinite families of such ideals associated to highly symmetric complexes. We then prove a partial analogue to the first Boij-Söderberg Conjecture for Stanley-Reisner ideals, by detailing an algorithm for constructing Stanley-Reisner ideals with pure Betti diagrams of any given shape, save for an initial shift.



1.
Introduction

This paper studies Stanley-Reisner ideals whose minimal free resolutions are pure.

Recall that for any standard
\mathbb{N}
-graded ring
R
and finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
, we can construct a
graded minimal free resolution

\dots\rightarrow F_{i}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

with
F_{i}=\oplus_{j\geq i}R(-j)^{\beta_{ij}}

where
R(-d)
denotes the
R
-module
R
with its degree shifted so that
R(-d)_{\delta}=R_{\delta+d}
and

\beta_{ij}
is a positive integer for all
i\geq 0
and finitely many
j\geq i
.
While graded modules may afford different minimal free resolutions, the
graded Betti numbers

\beta_{ij}
are invariants of
M
.

The
Betti diagram
of
M
, denoted
\beta(M)
, is a matrix containing the graded Betti numbers of
M
. In order to reduce the number of rows of this matrix, the standard notational convention is to write
\beta(M)
as a matrix
(a_{ij})
with
a_{ij}=\beta_{j,i+j}
, as shown.

\begin{array}[]{c | cccc}0&\beta_{0,0}&\beta_{1,1}&\dots&\beta_{n,n}\\
1&\beta_{0,1}&\beta_{1,2}&\dots&\beta_{n,n+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{array}

So for example

\begin{array}[]{l | rrr }2&5&5&.\\
3&.&.&1\\
\end{array}

denotes a pure resolution

0\rightarrow R(-5)\rightarrow R(-3)^{5}\rightarrow R(-2)^{5}\rightarrow 0.

Throughout the rest of this paper
R
will denote a polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
where
\mathbb{K}
is an unspecified field of arbitrary characteristic. Hilbert’s Syzygy Theorem now implies that
\beta_{ij}=0
for all
i>n
. We use
\Hred_{i}(\Delta)
to denote
\Hred_{i}(\Delta;\mathbb{K})
.

Minimal free resolutions of
R
-modules were introduced by David Hilbert in 1890 as a tool for the study of invariants, and gave an early impetus for the
development of homological methods in commuative algebra, now so ubiquitous in the field.

Despite more than a century of study of free resolutions, many questions about these remain open, and perhaps the most basic of all is: which values of

\{\beta_{ij}\}
can occur as Betti numbers of a finitely generated graded
R
-module? Rather than looking at individual Betti-diagrams
\{\beta_{ij}\}
one can consider
the rational cones generated by all Betti diagrams of modules from a certain category and one can aim to describe these cones, e.g., it terms of their
extremal rays. This was the approach taken in
[
2
]
where the authors formulated the famous
Boij-Söderberg conjectures (now theorems), one of them being that
the Betti diagram of Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module is a
non-negative rational linear combination
of
pure
Betti diagrams.
A pure Betti diagram is the Betti diagram
\{\beta_{ij}\}
where for each
i\geq 0
,
\beta_{ij}\neq 0
for at most one
j
, in other words the Betti diagram arising from a graded minimal free resolution of the form

\dots\rightarrow R(-d_{i})^{\beta_{id_{i}}}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow R(-d_{1})^{\beta_{1d_{1}}}\rightarrow R(-d_{0})^{\beta_{0d_{0}}}\rightarrow 0.

The precise formulation of the conjecture in terms of rational cones as follows.
Fix
0\leq c\leq n
; for
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\dots,a_{c})
and

\mathbf{b}=(b_{0},\dots,b_{c})
in
\mathbb{N}^{c+1}

let
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
denote the set of Betti diagrams
\{\beta_{ij}\}

where
\beta_{ij}\neq 0
only when
0\leq i\leq c
and
a_{i}\leq j\leq b_{i}
.
Consider the rational cone
C
generated by Betti diagrams
\beta_{ij}\in\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
of graded Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules of codimension
c
.
The content of
[
2
, Conjecture 2.4]
is that the extremal rays of
C
are given by pure Betti diagrams.
For an account of these conjectures and their eventual proofs see
[
10
]
.

In view of the great power of Boij-Söderberg’s theory to describe possible Betti diagrams, one may wonder whether a similar theory exists if, instead of looking at Betti cones of
graded Cohen-Macaulay
R
modules of given codimension
c
, one were to look at Betti cones of other fixed class of graded
R
-modules.
The motivation for this paper is our desire to discover a Boij-Söderberg’s-like theory where that class of graded
R
-modules consists of direct sums of Stanley-Reisner ideals (i.e., ideals generated by square-free monomials; see section
2
.)
The guiding question behind our research is the following:

Question 1.1
.

What are all the possible Betti diagrams of Stanley-Reisner ideals?
Consider the rational cone
\mathcal{C}
generated by Betti diagrams

\beta_{ij}\in\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
of Stanley-Reisner ideals;
what are the extremal rays of
\mathcal{C}
?

Note that we do not restrict our attention to
Cohen-Macaulay
Stanley-Reisner ideals, and as a result pure Betti diagrams are not necessarily
extremal rays in
\mathcal{C}
(but the extremal rays of the rational cone generated by all pure Betti diagrams of the same same shape are extremal rays of
\mathcal{C}
). In addition, some extremal rays in
\mathcal{C}
do not correspond to pure Betti diagrams. See section
6
for concrete examples with small vertex sets.

This stands in contrast to the original Boij–Söderberg setting: there, every Betti diagram of a Cohen–Macaulay module is a positive combination of pure ones, so pure diagrams are exactly the extremal rays of the relevant cone. Boij and Söderberg later showed this phenomenon persists without the Cohen–Macaulay hypothesis
[
3
]
: every Betti diagram of a graded module decomposes as a positive rational combination of pure diagrams, so pure diagrams remain the extremal building blocks for Betti diagrams of graded modules in general. Since a Stanley-Reisner ideal is in particular a graded module, its pure Betti diagrams, when they exist, are still extremal rays of this larger ambient cone, even on the occasions where they fail to be extremal once we restrict attention to the smaller cone
\mathcal{C}
generated by Stanley-Reisner ideals alone. Constructing pure Betti diagrams of every degree type for Stanley-Reisner ideals tests how this extremal behaviour changes in the more rigid monomial setting.

There is probably no hope to answer this general question, but in view of the central role of pure Betti diagrams as extremal rays, we ask a slightly less ambitious question:

Question 1.2
.

What are all the possible
pure
Betti diagrams of Stanley-Reisner ideals?

Special cases of this question have been raised in other contexts. For example, consider a special type of pure resolutions,
namely
t
-linear resolutions
. These are pure resolutions with Betti diagram
\beta_{0,t}\neq 0,\beta_{1,t+1}\neq 0,\dots\beta_{p,t+p}\neq 0
.
Stanley-Reisner ideals with
2
-linear resolutions were first classified by Ralf Fröberg in
[
11
]
and later
those with
t
-linear resolutions were described by John Eagon and Victor Reiner in
[
9
]
.
At about the same time Winfried Bruns and Takayuki Hibi classified Cohen–Macaulay partially ordered sets whose corresponding
Stanley-Reisner ideal has a pure minimal resolution (
[
4
]
) and later Stanley-Reisner ideals of dimension 1 and 2 with pure resolutions (
[
5
]
).

As observed in
[
5
]
the complete classification of all pure Betti diagrams of Stanley-Reisner ideals
might be too ambitious a goal, but some understanding of the constraints on these might be feasible. This paper makes a contribution to this project by
describing two infinite families of Stanley-Reisner ideals with pure resolutions of arbitrary length, and also detailing an algorithm for generating Stanley-Reisner ideals with pure resolutions of any given degree type (as defined in the next section).

The tools used in this paper are combinatorial and are mostly applications of Hochster’s formula for the Betti numbers of Stanley-Reisner ideals.

1.1.
A Partial Analogue to the First Boij-Söderberg Conjecture for Stanley-Reisner Ideals

The main result of this paper can be seen as a partial analogue to the First Boij-Söderberg Conjecture, for Stanley-Reisner ideals. In order to state it, we need to introduce the following concepts.

Definition 1.3
.

As in
[
4
]
, given a pure graded free resolution
F_{\bullet}

0\rightarrow R(-c_{p})^{\beta_{p,c_{p}}}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow R(-c_{1})^{\beta_{1,c_{1}}}\rightarrow R(-c_{0})^{\beta_{0,c_{0}}}

We define the
shift type
of
F_{\bullet}
as
(c_{p},\dots,c_{0})
, and its
degree type
to be the positive sequence
(c_{p}-c_{p-1},\dots,c_{1}-c_{0})
. We also extend this terminology accordingly to define the shift types and degree types of pure Betti diagrams.

Remark 1.4
.

Our notion of degree type differs slightly from that found in
[
4
]
, which defines it as the sequence
(c_{p}-c_{p-1},...,c_{1}-c_{0},c_{0})
. This is the sequence of degrees of the maps in the resolution. Our version of degree type records the degrees of all of these maps except for
R(-c_{0})^{\beta_{0,c_{0}}}\rightarrow I
. Note in particular that under our definition, The degree type of a pure resolution contains slightly less information than its shift type, but the shift type is uniquely determined by the degree type and the value of
c_{0}
.

The first Boij-Söderberg Conjecture (
[
10
]
, Theorem 1.9) states that for any strictly decreasing sequence of integers
\mathbf{c}=(c_{p},\dots,c_{0})
with
p\leq n
, there exists a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module of codimension
p
with a pure Betti diagram of shift type
\mathbf{c}
. One might wonder if a similar result holds for Stanley-Reisner ideals: that is, for a strictly decreasing sequence of integers
\mathbf{c}=(c_{p},\dots,c_{0})
, does there exist a Staney-Reisner ideal with a pure Betti diagram of shift type
\mathbf{c}
? The answer to this is
no
. This can be seen immediately, because no ideal in
R
can have a pure Betti diagram with a shift type that contains negative numbers. However, even if we restricted our attention purely to sequences of
positive
integers, the answer would still be no: for example, in
[
8
]
Chapter 4 page 69, we show that no Stanley-Reisner ideal can have a pure diagram of shift type
(5,2)
.

We can, however, broaden the question slightly: instead of asking whether there are pure Betti diagrams of Stanley-Reisner ideals corresponding to every possible
shift type
, we ask whether there are diagrams corresponding to every possible
degree type
. The main result of this paper is a positive answer to this question.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathbf{d}=(d_{p},\dots,d_{1})
be any sequence of positive integers. There exists a Stanley-Reisner ideal
I
such that the Betti diagram
\beta(I)
is pure with degree type
\mathbf{d}
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
2
gives a brief exposition of some relevant background results in the literature, and then introduces the theory of
PR Complexes
, simplicial complexes whose dual Stanley-Reisner ideals have pure resolutions. Sections 3 and 4 provide constructions for two families of PR complexes with particular degree types. Section 5 is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.5
. And finally Section 6 contains a list of extremal rays for the Betti cones on Stanley-Reisner ideals in small numbers of variables. It is worth highlighting that the results of Sections 3, 4 and 5 are independent of each other. In particular, the proof of Theorem
1.5
in Section 5 does not depend on results in Sections 3 or 4.

2.
Elementary concepts and tools

In this section we note some elementary tools used throughout this paper. We begin with a (very) brief overview of simplicial complexes and Stanley-Reisner Theory.

A
simplicial complex
on vertex set
V
is a set of subsets of
V
such that for any
G\subseteq F\subseteq V
, if
F
is in
\Delta
then
G
is also in
\Delta
. We refer to the elements of
\Delta
as
faces
and the maximal elements as
facets
. We define the dimension of a face
\sigma
of
\Delta
to be
|\sigma|-1
, and the dimension
\dim\Delta
of
\Delta
to be the dimension of its largest facet. If all facets of
\Delta
have the same dimension we say
\Delta
is
pure
.

We often write simplicial complexes in
facet notation
, as follows.

Notation 2.1
.

For subsets
F_{1}
,…,
F_{m}
in a vertex set
V
, we use
\langle F_{1},...,F_{m}\rangle
to denote the smallest simplicial complex on
V
containing
F_{1}
,…,
F_{m}
. Note that if the set
\{F_{1},...,F_{m}\}
is irredundant, then this is the same as the complex on
V
with facets
F_{1},...,F_{m}
.

The following constructions will be of particular importance in this paper.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complexes on vertex set
V
.

(a)

The
Alexander Dual
of
\Delta
is the complex

\Delta^{*}=\{F\subseteq V:V-F\notin\Delta\}.

(b)

The
link
of a face

is the complex

\link_{\Delta}f=\{\sigma\in\Delta\,|\,f\cap\sigma=\emptyset\text{ and }f\cup\sigma\in\Delta\}.

(c)

For any
r\geq-1
, the
r
-skeleton
of
\Delta
is the complex

\Skel_{r}(\Delta)=\{\sigma\in\Delta:\dim\sigma\leq r\}.

If
\Delta
is a full simplex on vertex set
V
we often denote this as
\Skel_{r}(V)
.

(d)

For a complex
\Gamma
on vertex set
U
with
U\cap V=\emptyset
, the
join
of
\Delta
and
\Gamma
is the complex

\Delta\ast\Gamma=\{\sigma_{1}\sqcup\sigma_{2}:\sigma_{1}\in\Delta,\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma\}.

(e)

The

of
\Delta

\Delta_{U}=\{\sigma\in\Delta:\sigma\subseteq U\}.

(g)

The

\Delta-f=\{\sigma\in\Delta:\sigma\nsupseteq f\}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on vertex set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
The
Stanley-Reisner
ideal
I_{\Delta}
is the ideal of
R
generated by the square-free monomials

\{x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{k}}\,|\,\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\notin\Delta\}.

Every ideal generated by square free monomials is a Stanley-Reisner ideal (and vice-versa).

Remark 2.5
.

Most of the simplicial complexes presented in this paper are not on vertex set
[n]
. We define their Stanley-Reisner ideals by first relabelling the vertices. Note that any two relabellings give isomorphic Stanley-Reisner ideals.

The main tool for investigating Betti numbers of a Stanley-Reisner ideal
I_{\Delta}
is Hochster’s Formula, which expresses the Betti numbers as a sum of dimensions
of the reduced homologies of sub-simplicial complexes of
\Delta
.

Theorem 2.6

(Hochster’s Formula (Theorem 5.1 in
[
15
]
))
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on a vertex set
V
of size
n
.

\beta_{id}(I_{\Delta})=\sum_{W\subseteq V,\#W=d}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\Hred_{d-i}(\Delta_{W};\mathbb{K})

where for any

W\subseteq V
,
\Delta_{W}
denotes the simplicial complex
with vertex set
W
and whose faces are the faces of
\Delta
containing only vertices in
W
.

Hochster’s formula can be reformulated in terms of the Alexander dual of
\Delta
as follows.

Theorem 2.7

(Alexander Dual version of Hochster’s Formula (ADHF), see Corollary 1.40 in
[
16
]
)
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on a vertex set
V
of size
n
.

\beta_{id}(I_{{\Delta}^{*}})=\sum_{f\in\Delta,|f|=n-d}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\Hred_{i-1}({\link_{\Delta}}f;\mathbb{K})

ADHF plays a central role in many of the proofs in this paper (and hence the acronym.) Thus many of our results revolve around the computation of simplicial homology, and in particular, make use of deformation retractions. The following lemma will be of particular importance in this regard. It provides sufficient conditions for ensuring that a complex
\Delta
deformation retracts on to the complex
\Delta-g
(the
deletion of
g
from
\Delta
, as given in Definition
2.2
). We suspect this lemma exists in the literature, but have been unable to find it.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex, and let
g\subsetneqq f
be faces of
\Delta
such that every facet of
\Delta
that contains
g
also contains
f
. There is a deformation retraction
\Delta\rightsquigarrow\Delta-g
, obtained by
collapsing
g
onto a vertex in
f-g
.

Proof.

Suppose
\Delta
has
n
vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
with
g=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
and
v_{k+1}
in
f-g
.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be the canonical basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
X=X_{\Delta}
be the geometric realization of
\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
v_{i}
realized as
e_{i}
. Similarly, let
X_{\Delta-g}
be the geometric realization of
\Delta-g
. For each nonempty face
\sigma=\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{r}}\}
of
\Delta
, we define
X_{\sigma}
to be the set
\left\{\lambda_{1}e_{i_{1}}+\dots\lambda_{r}e_{i_{r}}:\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}>0,\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}=1\right\}\subset X
. Note that
X=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Delta}X_{\sigma}
, while
X_{\Delta-g}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Delta,\sigma\nsupseteq g}X_{\sigma}
.

Let
\mathbf{p}
be a point in
X
. We may write it as
\mathbf{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}e_{j}
where the coefficients
\lambda_{j}
are all nonnegative and sum to
1
. In particular,
\mathbf{p}
lies inside
X_{\sigma}
for some
\sigma\in\Delta
containing
g
if and only if all of
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}
are positive.

We define
\lambda=\min\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}
. This allows us to rewrite the point
\mathbf{p}
as

(1)

\mathbf{p}=\lambda(e_{1}+\dots+e_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda)e_{i}+\lambda_{k+1}e_{k+1}+y

for some
y
in the span of
\{e_{k+2},\dots,e_{n}\}
. Note that the coefficients
(\lambda_{i}-\lambda)
are all nonnegative, and (by the definition of
\lambda
) at least one of them is zero. Note also that
\lambda
itself is nonzero if and only if all of
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}
are positive, which occurs if and only if
\mathbf{p}
is in
X_{\sigma}
for some
\sigma
containing
g
. In other words, we have
\lambda=0
if and only if
\mathbf{p}
lies inside
X_{\Delta-g}
.

Using this notation for the points in
X
, we can define a function
\varphi:X\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows. For
\mathbf{p}
as in Equation (
1
) and
0\leq t\leq 1
we define

(2)

\varphi(\mathbf{p},t)=\lambda(1-t)(e_{1}+\dots+e_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda)e_{i}+(\lambda_{k+1}+k\lambda t)e_{k+1}+y.

We claim that
\varphi
is a deformation retraction from
X
to
X_{\Delta-g}
.

First, note that
\varphi
is continuous. Indeed, the function
\lambda=\min\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}
is continuous in the variables
\lambda_{i}
, and hence so is each summand in Equation (
2
). Each summand is also continuous in
t
.

Next, we note that
\varphi(*,0)
is the identity on
X
. Moreover, for
\mathbf{p}
in
X_{\Delta-g}
we have
\lambda=0
, and hence
\varphi(\mathbf{p},t)=\mathbf{p}
for every
0\leq t\leq 1
.

It remains to show that the image of
\varphi(*,t)
lies inside
X
for every value of
t
, and in particular that the image of
\varphi(*,1)
is
X_{\Delta-g}
.

For the former claim, we note that the sum of the coefficients of
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
in the decomposition of
\varphi(\mathbf{p},t)
is the same as the sum of these coefficients in the decomposition of
\mathbf{p}
(which is
1
), and all of these coefficients are nonnegative. We may assume that
\mathbf{p}
lies inside
X_{\sigma}
for some face
\sigma
in
\Delta
containing
g
(otherwise
\mathbf{p}
lies inside
X_{\Delta-g}
and we are already done). By our assumption on
g
and
f
we have that
\sigma\cup\{v_{k+1}\}
is also a face of
\Delta
. If
\sigma=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k},v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{r}}\}
for some
k<i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leq n
, then the vectors in the decomposition of
\varphi(\mathbf{p},t)
with strictly positive coefficients are all contained in
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k},e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{r}}\}\cup\{e_{k+1}\}
. We conclude that
\varphi(\mathbf{p},t)
lies inside
X_{\tau}
for some
\tau\subseteq\sigma\cup\{v_{k+1}\}
, and hence inside
X
.

For the latter claim, we note that the coefficients of the vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
in
\varphi(\mathbf{p},1)
are
\lambda_{1}-\lambda,\dots,\lambda_{k}-\lambda
, and at least one of these must be zero by the definition of
\lambda
. Thus
\varphi(\mathbf{p},1)
lies inside
X_{\Delta-g}
.
∎

In particular we have the following corollary, which will be sufficient for our needs in most (but not all) cases.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex, and let

(1)

(2)

.

Proof.

This comes from Lemma

, setting

and

is a face of
\Delta

2.1.
PR Complexes

Our primary aim is to construct Stanley-Reisner ideals with pure resolutions of varying degree types. In particular we wish to construct simplicial complexes
\Delta
for which the diagram
\beta(I_{\Delta^{*}})
is pure.

The Alexander Dual version of Hochster’s Formula (Theorem
2.7
) reduces this to an entirely combinatorial problem, as shown below.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex with vertices
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. The diagram
\beta=\beta(I_{\Delta^{*}})
is pure if and only if
\Delta
satisfies the following condition:

For every
i
, and every simplex
\sigma,\tau\in\Delta
, if
\Hred_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)\neq 0\neq\Hred_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\tau)
then
|\sigma|=|\tau|
.

Proof.

The diagram
\beta
is pure if and only if for every
i
, there exists at most one
c_{i}
such that
\beta_{i,c_{i}}\neq 0
. By ADHF, this holds if and only if there are no two simplices of different sizes in
\Delta
whose links both have non-trivial homology at the same degree.
∎

Definition 2.12
.

We refer to complexes which satisfy the condition in Corollary
2.11
as
PR Complexes
(over
\mathbb{K}
), where
PR
stands for
Pure Resolution
.

Remark 2.13
.

All of our work in this paper is done over the arbitrary field
\mathbb{K}
. Hence, for the rest of this paper we will simply use the phrase ‘
PR
’ to mean ‘
PR over

\mathbb{K}
’.

Lemma 2.14
.

All PR complexes are pure (i.e. their facets all have the same dimension).

Proof.

The link of a facet is the irrelevant complex
\{\emptyset\}
which has nontrivial
{(-1)}^{\text{st}}
homology. Thus a PR complex cannot have two facets of different sizes.
∎

The following lemma will be particularly useful for us in our study of PR complexes.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\Delta
be any simplicial complex. Suppose we have some face
\sigma
in
\Delta
such that
\Hred_{j}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)\neq 0
for some
j\geq 0
. There exists a chain of simplices
\sigma=\tau_{j}\subsetneqq\tau_{j-1}\subsetneqq\dots\subsetneqq\tau_{0}\subsetneqq\tau_{-1}
in
\Delta
such that for each
-1\leq i\leq j
, we have
\Hred_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\tau_{i})\neq 0
.

Proof.

We prove this algebraically, setting
\beta=\beta(I_{{\Delta}^{*}})
. It suffices to show that there is some face
\sigma\subsetneqq\tau\in\Delta
for which
\Hred_{j-1}(\link_{\Delta}\tau)\neq 0
. The result then follows by induction on
j
.

By replacing
\Delta
with
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
, we may assume that
\sigma=\emptyset
, and thus we need only find a nonempty face
\tau
in
\Delta
whose link has
{(j-1)}^{\text{st}}
homology. To find a candidate for
\tau
, note that by ADHF we have that
\beta_{j+1,n}\neq 0
. Hence there must be some
d<n
such that
\beta_{j,d}\neq 0
. This means (again, by ADHF) that there exists some nonempty face
\tau
in
\Delta
of size
n-d
for which
\Hred_{j-1}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)\neq 0
, as required.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
\Delta
be a PR complex. Every link in
\Delta
has at most one nontrivial homology.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be a face of
\Delta
and suppose for contradiction that
\Hred_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)\neq 0\neq\Hred_{j}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)
for some
i<j
. By Lemma
2.15
, there exists some
\tau\supsetneqq\sigma
such that
\Hred_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\tau)\neq 0
. But this contradicts the fact that
\Delta
is PR, because
|\tau|>|\sigma|
.
∎

Remark 2.17
.

In particular, the complex
\Delta
is equal to
\link_{\Delta}\emptyset
, so it must have at most one nontrivial homology itself.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
\Delta
be a PR complex, and suppose
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are faces of
\Delta
with
\Hred_{i_{1}}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma_{1})\neq 0\neq\Hred_{i_{2}}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma_{2})
. If
|\sigma_{1}|<|\sigma_{2}|
then
i_{1}>i_{2}
.

Proof.

We cannot have
i_{1}=i_{2}
as this directly contradicts the PR condition. If
i_{1}<i_{2}
, then by Lemma
2.15
we can find some face
\sigma_{3}
of
\Delta
strictly containing
\sigma_{2}
such that
\Hred_{i_{1}}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma_{3})\neq 0
, which also contradicts the PR property because
|\sigma_{3}|>|\sigma_{1}|
.
∎

Using these results about the homology index sets of PR complexes, we are now able to give an entirely combinatorial description of the degree type of a PR complex
\Delta
, which agrees with the degree type of the Betti diagram
\beta(I_{{\Delta}^{*}})
.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
\Delta
be a PR Complex, and let
p
be the maximum index for which there exists some face
\sigma
in
\Delta
such that
\Hred_{p-1}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)\neq 0
. For each
0\leq i\leq p
, we define
s_{i}
to be the size
|\sigma|
of the faces
\sigma
of
\Delta
for which
\Hred_{i-1}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)\neq 0
, and for each
1\leq i\leq p
, we define
d_{i}=s_{i-1}-s_{i}
. We call the sequence
(d_{p},\dots,d_{1})
the
degree type
of
\Delta
.

Remark 2.20
.

The integers
s_{p-1},\dots,s_{0}
are well-defined by Lemma
2.15
, and form a strictly decreasing sequence by Corollary
2.18
. Thus the degree type
(d_{p},\dots,d_{1})
must consist of positive integers.

To see why this notion of the degree type is the same as the degree type of the pure diagram
\beta(I_{{\Delta}^{*}})
, suppose that

0\rightarrow R(-c_{p})^{\beta_{p,c_{p}}}\rightarrow...\rightarrow R(-c_{1})^{\beta_{1,c_{1}}}\rightarrow R(-c_{0})^{\beta_{0,c_{0}}}\rightarrow I_{{\Delta}^{*}}

is a minimal graded free resolution of
I_{{\Delta}^{*}}
. By definition the degree type of this resolution is the sequence
(d_{p},\dots,d_{1})
where for each
1\leq i\leq p
we define
d_{i}=c_{i}-c_{i-1}
. By ADHF, for each
1\leq i\leq p
we have
c_{i}=n-s_{i}
and
c_{i-1}=n-s_{i-1}
, and hence we have
d_{i}=(n-s_{i})-(n-s_{i-1})=s_{i-1}-s_{i}
.

Definition 2.21
.

Let
\Delta
be a PR complex and let
s_{0},\dots,s_{p}
be as in Definition
2.19
above. We call the value
s_{p}
(i.e. the minimum size of a face of
\Delta
whose link has homology) the
offset
of
\Delta
.

Note that a PR complex
\Delta
has offset 0 if and only if it has nontrivial homology itself (because
\link_{\Delta}\emptyset
is equal to
\Delta
). Taken together, the degree type and offset of a PR complex
\Delta
determine its dimension, as shown below.

Proposition 2.22
.

Let
\Delta
be a PR complex with degree type
\mathbf{d}
and offset
s
. We have
\dim\Delta=s+\sum\mathbf{d}-1
.

Proof.

Using the notation of Definition
2.19
we have
\sum\mathbf{d}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}d_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}(s_{i}-s_{i-1})=s_{0}-s_{p}
. The value
s_{p}
is the offset of
\Delta
(i.e.
s=s_{p}
), and the value
s_{0}
is the size of the facets of
\Delta
(all of which are the same by Lemma
2.14
). Thus
\dim\Delta=s_{0}-1=s_{p}+(s_{0}-s_{p})-1=s+\sum\mathbf{d}-1
.
∎

2.2.
PR Complexes and Cohen-Macaulay Complexes

The PR condition is in fact a generalisation of Reisner’s criterion for Cohen-Macaulay complexes, which is the following.

Theorem 2.23

(Reisner’s Cohen-Macaulay Criterion, see
[
16
]
Theorem 5.53)
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. The Stanley-Reisner ring
\mathbb{K}[\Delta]
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if for any
\sigma
in
\Delta
and any
i\neq\dim\Delta-|\sigma|
we have
\Hred_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)=0
. In this case we call
\Delta
a Cohen-Macaulay complex (over
\mathbb{K}
).

In particular, all Cohen-Macaulay complexes are PR. From an algebraic perspective, this is a result of the following theorem.

Theorem 2.24

(Eagon-Reiner Theorem, see
[
9
]
Theorem 3)
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathbb{K}[\Delta]
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

I_{\Delta^{*}}
has a linear resolution.

A
linear resolution
is a pure resolution of degree type
(1,...,1)
. Hence we can rephrase the Eagon-Reiner Theorem as follows.

Corollary 2.25
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

\Delta
is PR with degree type
(1,\dots,1)
.

2.3.

We now present two examples of PR complexes. The symmetries and combinatorial properties of these complexes motivate the families of complexes constructed in the next three sections.

For both examples we use the Alexander Dual version of Hochster’s Formula (ADHF) to demonstrate why the corresponding Betti diagram is pure.

.

\Delta

(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n-1})
(i.e. it is Cohen-Maculay).

Hence we can obtain PR complexes with degree type

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(-n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(-(n-1))^{{n\choose n-1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(-1)^{n\choose 1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{{\Delta}^{*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

is a free resolution of

Remark 2.28
.

In fact Example

is an instance of a more general result, namely that every simplicial sphere is Cohen-Macaulay (see

]

2.4.
Homology Index Sets

The PR property is a condition on the homology of links. For this reason it will be useful for us to introduce the following concept and notation.

Definition 2.30
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex.

•

We define we define the
homology index set of

\Delta
to be the set
h(\Delta)=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\Hred_{i}(\Delta)\neq 0\}
.

•

For a face
\sigma
in
\Delta
we define the
homology index set of

\Delta

at

\sigma
to be the set
h(\Delta,\sigma)=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\Hred_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma)\neq 0\}
. Note that under this definition, we have
h(\Delta)=h(\Delta,\emptyset)
.

•

For a natural number
m
we define the
complete homology index set
of
\Delta
at
m
as
\widehat{h}(\Delta,m)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Delta,|\sigma|=m}h(\Delta,\sigma)
.

In many of our later proofs we will add homology index sets
A
and
B
together using the following construction.

Definition 2.31
.

For sets
A
and
B
, we define
A\boxplus B=\{i+j:i\in A,j\in B\}
.

In particular, note that if
A
and
B
are both singletons, then this is the same as adding the elements of those singletons together; and if either set is empty then the resulting sum is also empty.

Using the notation for homology index sets, we can present an alternate definition for PR complexes.

Proposition 2.32
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\Delta
is PR.

(2)

For any
\sigma
and
\tau
in
\Delta
with
|\sigma|\neq|\tau|
, we have
h(\Delta,\sigma)\cap h(\Delta,\tau)=\emptyset
.

(3)

For any distinct integers
m_{1}\neq m_{2}
, we have
\widehat{h}(\Delta,m_{1})\cap\widehat{h}(\Delta,m_{2})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

This is a rephrasing of the PR condition in terms of homology index sets.
∎

In fact, the complete homology sets of a PR complex can be computed directly from its degree type and offset.

Proposition 2.33
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\Delta
is a PR complex with degree type
(d_{p},\dots,d_{1})
and offset
s
.

(2)

\widehat{h}(\Delta,m)=\begin{cases}\{r-2\}&\text{ if }m=s+\sum_{j=r}^{p}d_{j}\text{ for some }1\leq r\leq p+1\\
\emptyset&\text{ otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be a PR complex of the specified degree type and offset. Let
s_{p}<\dots<s_{0}
be as in Definition
2.19
(so that
s_{p}=s
). We know from Corollary
2.16
that every homology index set of
\Delta
is either a singleton or empty, and from Corollary
2.18
we deduce that the same is true of the
complete
homology index sets. By definition of
s_{p},\dots,s_{0}
, the nonempty complete homology index sets are precisely the sets
\widehat{h}(\Delta,s_{p}),\dots,\widehat{h}(\Delta,s_{0})
.

Specifically, for each
1\leq r\leq p+1
, the set
\widehat{h}(\Delta,s_{r-1})
is the singleton
\{r-2\}
, and we have
s_{r-1}=s_{p}+(s_{p-1}-s_{p})+\dots+(s_{r-1}-s_{r})=s+\sum_{j=r}^{p}d_{j}
.

Conversely, if
\Delta
satisfies condition (2), then
\Delta
must be PR by Proposition
2.32
, and we can recover its degree type and offset from the values of
m
for which
\widehat{h}(\Delta,m)
are nonempty.
∎

The following corollary of Proposition
2.33
will be particularly crucial to our proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Corollary 2.34
.

If
\Delta
has offset
s
and degree type
\mathbf{d}=(d_{p},\dots,d_{i},1,\dots,1)
, then for any natural number
m
with
s+\sum_{j=i}^{p}d_{j}\leq m\leq\dim\Delta+1
we have
\widehat{h}(\Delta,m)=\{\dim\Delta-m\}
.

Proof.

For convenience we write
d_{1}=\dots=d_{i-1}=1
. Note that
\sum\mathbf{d}=\sum_{j=i}^{p}d_{j}+i-1
, and hence by Proposition
2.22
we have
\dim\Delta=s+\sum_{j=i}^{p}d_{j}+i-2
.

Now fix some
s+\sum_{j=i}^{p}d_{j}\leq m\leq\dim\Delta+1
. In particular, we have
m=s+\sum_{j=i}^{p}d_{j}+l
for some
0\leq l\leq i-1
, and we can rewrite this as
m=s+\sum_{j=i-l}^{p}d_{j}
. Proposition
2.33
tells us that
\widehat{h}(\Delta,m)=\{i-l-2\}
. The result follows because
\dim\Delta-m=i-l-2
.
∎

3.
Intersection Complexes

In this section we construct an infinite family of PR complexes (and hence a corresponding infinite family of pure resolutions). We begin by noting the following important lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
\sigma\in\Delta
is such that
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
is not acyclic. Then
\sigma
is an intersection of facets in
\Delta
.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. Suppose that
\sigma
is not an intersection of facets of
\Delta
and let
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
be all the facets of
\Delta
that contain
\sigma
.

We must have
\sigma\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}F_{i}
. Because
\sigma
is not an intersection of facets, there must be some vertex
v\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}F_{i}-\sigma
. This means that
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
is a cone over
v
, and is therefore acyclic.
∎

In particular, Lemma
3.1
tells us that the only faces of a complex
\Delta
that contribute towards the Betti numbers of
I_{{\Delta}^{*}}
are the ones that are intersections of facets. Due to this observation, we might expect many PR complexes to exhibit some kind of symmetry around the points where their facets intersect. The following definition gives us one such symmetry condition on facet intersections.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a complex with facets
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
. We say that
\Delta
is
intersectionally symmetric
if for any
1\leq k\leq n
and any permutation
\alpha
in
S_{n}
, we have

|F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{k}|=|F_{\alpha(1)}\cap\dots\cap F_{\alpha(k)}|
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Delta
be an intersectionally symmetric
complex with facets
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
.
For any
1\leq k\leq n
, and for any permutation
\alpha
in
S_{n}
, we have
\link_{\Delta}(F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{k})\cong\link_{\Delta}(F_{\alpha(1)}\cap\dots\cap F_{\alpha(k)})
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq k\leq n
, write
\mu_{k}
for the common size of the intersection
of any
k
facets.

Fix
k
and a subset
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subseteq[n]
;
set
P=F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{k}}
, so
|P|=\mu_{k}
.
We must show that
\link_{\Delta}(P)\cong\link_{\Delta}(Q)
for

Q=F_{1}\cap\cdots\cap F_{k}
.

For any
\ell\in[n]\setminus\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
, we have

|F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{k}}\cap F_{\ell}|=\mu_{k+1}
.
Hence
F_{\ell}\supseteq P
if and only if
\mu_{k+1}=|P|=\mu_{k}
,
a condition depending only on
k
, not on the specific subset

\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
or on the choice of
\ell
.

Now if
k=n
, or
k<n
and
\mu_{k+1}=\mu_{k}
,
then every facet contains
P
, so
P=F_{1}\cap\cdots\cap F_{n}
;
the identical argument applied to
Q
gives
Q=F_{1}\cap\cdots\cap F_{n}
as well, so
P=Q
and we are done.

Assume henceforth that
k<n
and
\mu_{k+1}<\mu_{k}
:
now
P
is contained only in facets
F_{i_{1}},\dots,F_{i_{k}}

and
\link_{\Delta}P
has facets

F_{i_{1}}\setminus P,\dots,F_{i_{k}}\setminus P
.
Define
A_{j}=F_{i_{j}}\setminus P
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

For any vertex
v
in the set of vertices
V
of

A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{k}

define
a(v)=\{j\in[k]:v\in A_{j}\}

and for any non-empty
S\subseteq[k]
define

T_{S}=\{v\in V:a(v)=S\}
.
These pairwise disjoint sets
cover

A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{k}
.

Also, for any non-empty
S\subseteq[k]

v\in V
is a vertex of
\bigcap_{j\in S}A_{j}

if and only if
a(v)\supseteq S
, hence

\displaystyle\sum_{S^{\prime}\supseteq S}|T_{S^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\bigcap_{j\in S}A_{j}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\bigcap_{j\in S}F_{i_{j}}\setminus P|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left(\bigcap_{j\in S}F_{i_{j}}\right)\right|-|P|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{|S|}-\mu_{k}.

Now repeat this argument with

Q=F_{1}\cap\cdots\cap F_{k}

with
B_{j}=F_{j}\setminus Q
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.
For any vertex
w
in the set of vertices
W
of

B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{k}

define
b(w)=\{j\in[k]:w\in B_{j}\}

and for any non-empty
S\subseteq[k]
define

T^{\prime}_{S}=\{w\in W:b(w)=S\}
.

Now
|T^{\prime}_{S}|=|T_{S}|
for
every nonempty
S\subseteq[k]
and we choose
any bijections
T_{S}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}T^{\prime}_{S}
for each
S

resulting in
a bijection

\phi\colon A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{k}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{k}
.

Since

\phi(A_{j})=\phi\Bigl(\bigcupdot_{S\ni j}T_{S}\Bigr)=\bigcupdot_{S\ni j}\phi(T_{S})=\bigcupdot_{S\ni j}T^{\prime}_{S}=B_{j},

the map
\phi
sends each facet
A_{j}
of
\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle

to the facet
B_{j}
of
\langle B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\rangle
, and is therefore an
isomorphism of simplicial complexes. Hence

\link_{\Delta}(P)=\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle\cong\langle B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\rangle=\link_{\Delta}(Q)
.
∎

In this section, we define the

.

(1)

The integer

is equal to the number of vertices contained in the intersection

(or any other intersection of
i

(2)

For any subset

are in the facet

.

\Delta

which are not in the intersection of a larger number of facets. Proceeding in this way we can find a sequence

Proof.

(1)

\displaystyle d_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-i}{n-i\choose j}m_{i+j}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-i-1}{n-i-1\choose j}m_{i+j+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-i}{n-i\choose j}m_{i+j}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-i}{n-i-1\choose j-1}m_{i+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-i}{n-i-1\choose j}m_{i+j}

. Let

. For any face

4.
Partition Complexes

In this section we construct another infinite family of PR complexes, which we call
partition complexes
. Just as every intersection complex has a corresponding sequence of nonnegative integers
\mathbf{m}
, every partition complex has three corresponding integers
a
,
p
and
m
, and we denote them accordingly as
\calP(a,p,m)
. We show that when
a\geq 2
, and
1\leq m\leq p
the partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
has degree type
(\overbrace{1,\dots,1\underbrace{a,1,\dots,1}_{m}}^{p})
.

Just like intersection complexes, partition complexes can be seen as generalisations of the boundary complexes of simplices. Specifically, we saw in the last section that for any sequence of nonnegative integers
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{p-1},0)
with
m_{p-1}\neq 0
, the intersection complex
\calI(\mathbf{m})
deformation retracts onto the boundary of the
p
-simplex. The same is also true for the partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
for any integers
a\geq 2
and
1\leq m\leq p
.

4.1.
Defining the Complexes

In this section we define the family of partition complexes along with some examples, and present some preliminary results about them. For any integer
p\geq 0
the partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
admits a natural symmetry under the action of the symmetric group
\Sym\{0,\dots,p\}
(which is isomorphic to
S_{p+1}
). For notational convenience we will denote the group
\Sym\{0,\dots,p\}
by
S^{0}_{p}
.

We also introduce the following notation for complexes generated by group actions.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose we have a group
G
acting on a set
V
. For any subset

and any element
g\in G
, we define the subset
gF\subseteq V
to be the set
\{gx:x\in F\}
. For subsets
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
in
V
, and a group
G
acting on
V
, we use
\langle F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\rangle_{G}
to denote the complex
\langle gF_{i}|1\leq i\leq m,g\in G\rangle
.

It is worth noting that our main theorem on the degree types of the partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
(Theorem
4.21
) holds only for integers
a\geq 2
and
1\leq m\leq p
. However, we define our complexes below slightly more broadly, to include the additional cases
p\in\{-1,0\}
and
m\in\{0,p+1\}
. We do not care about these fringe cases of partition complexes for their own sake, but their construction will be crucial to our proof of Theorem
4.21
, because they occur as links in the partition complexes that we do care about.

Definition 4.2
.

For two integers
a\geq 2
and
p\geq-1
. We define the vertex set
V_{p}^{a}
to be the set consisting of vertices of the form
x_{i}
and
y_{i}^{j}
for
0\leq i\leq p
and
1\leq j\leq a-1
. For convenience, we often write
y_{i}^{1}
simply as
y_{i}
.

We will sometimes partition
V_{p}^{a}
into subsets
X_{p}\sqcup Y_{p}^{a}
with
X_{p}=\{x_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq p}
and
Y_{p}^{a}=\{y_{i}^{j}\}_{0\leq i\leq p,1\leq j\leq a-1}
. For reasons that will become apparent we refer to the vertices in
X_{p}
as
boundary vertices
and the vertices in
Y_{p}^{a}
as
partition vertices
. We sometimes make a further distinction between those partition vertices
y_{i}^{j}
for which
j\geq 2
and those for which
j=1
, by referring to them respectively as
upper
and
lower
partition vertices.

For
p\geq 0
the symmetry group
S^{0}_{p}=\Sym\{0,\dots,p\}
acts on
V_{p}^{a}
via the action

\displaystyle\sigma(x_{i})

\displaystyle=x_{\sigma(i)}

\displaystyle\sigma(y_{i}^{j})

\displaystyle=y_{\sigma(i)}^{j}

Definition 4.3
.

Let
a
,
p
and
i
be integers with
a\geq 2
,
p\geq-1
and
1\leq i\leq p+1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}(a,i)
denote the set of partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{i})
of
a+i-2
into
i
parts (i.e.
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{i}>0
and
\sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}=a+i-2
).

For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{i})\in\mathbb{P}(a,i)
, we define the
\lambda
-
generating set

G^{\lambda}_{p,i}
as follows.

•

G_{p,i}=\{x_{i},\dots,x_{p}\}\subset X_{p}
.

•

G_{p,i}^{\lambda}=G_{p,i}\sqcup\{y_{r}^{j}:0\leq r\leq i-1,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{r+1}\}\subset V_{p}^{a}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that

\displaystyle|G^{\lambda}_{p,i}|

\displaystyle=|G_{p,i}|+\sum_{j=1}^{1}\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle=(p-i+1)+(a+i-2)

\displaystyle=a+p-1.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
a
and
p
be integers with
a\geq 2
,
p\geq-1
. For a third integer
1\leq m\leq p+1
we define the
partition complex

\calP(a,p,m)
on vertex set
V_{p}^{a}
to be

\calP(a,p,m)=\left\langle G^{\lambda}_{p,i}:1\leq i\leq m,\lambda\in\mathbb{P}(a,i)\right\rangle_{S^{0}_{p}},

using the notation laid out in Definition
4.1
. We also define the partition complex
\calP(a,p,0)
to be the complex
\{\emptyset\}
on vertex set
V_{p}^{a}
.

By definition, partition complexes are symmetric under the action of
S^{0}_{p}
.

Remark 4.6
.

For every
1\leq i\leq m
and every partition
\lambda\in\mathbb{P}(a,i)
, the
\lambda
-generating set
G^{\lambda}_{p,i}
contains the lower partition vertices
y^{1}_{1},\dots,y^{1}_{i}
. This means that it must also contain exactly
a-2
upper partition vertices. Thus for
m\geq 1
and
p\geq 0
, every facet of the partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
contains exactly
a-2
upper partition vertices.

For explanatory purposes in some of our later proofs, we will sometimes represent the faces of partition complexes in
grid notation
as below.

Notation 4.7
.

Let
F
be a subset of the vertex set
V^{a}_{p}
. We sometimes denote
F

(a-1)\times(p+1)

V^{a}_{p}

For instance, we denote the set

y^{2}_{0}
y^{1}_{1}

Example 4.8
.

The case
p=-1
:

The vertex set
V^{a}_{-1}
is empty, and we have
\calP(a,-1,0)=\{\emptyset\}
, by definition.

Example 4.9
.

The case
p=0
:

The vertex set
V^{a}_{0}
is equal to
\{x_{0},y_{0}^{1},\dots,y_{0}^{a-1}\}
. The complex
\calP(a,0,0)
is the irrelevant complex
\{\emptyset\}
just as above, and we also have

\calP(a,0,1)=\langle G^{(a-1)}_{0,1}\rangle=\langle\{y_{0}^{1},\dots,y_{0}^{a-1}\}\rangle.

Example 4.10
.

The case
p=m=1
:

The partition complex
\calP(a,1,1)
has precisely two facets, namely the facets
\{y_{0}^{1},\dots,y_{0}^{a-1},x_{1}\}
and
\{y_{1}^{1},\dots,y_{1}^{a-1},x_{0}\}
. Thus it comprises of two disjoint
(a-1)
-simplices, so it is PR with degree type
(a)
.

In the above example, there is a deformation retraction from the complex
\calP(a,1,1)
on to the boundary
\partial\langle X_{1}\rangle
of the full simplex on
X_{1}=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
. In fact we will see in the next section that for
any

1\leq m\leq p
, the partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
always deformation retracts onto the boundary
\partial\langle X_{p}\rangle
of the full simplex on
X_{p}
.

.

a=2

By construction every face of a partition complex is separated, and every facet is both totally separated and partition complete. In fact we have more than this.

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
a
,
p
and
m
be integers with
a\geq 2
and
0\leq m\leq p+1
. A subset
F\subseteq V^{a}_{p}
is a facet of the partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
if and only if it satisfies the following four conditions.

(1)

F
is partition complete.

(2)

F
is totally separated.

(3)

|F|=a+p-1
.

(4)

1\leq|\Supp(F_{Y})|\leq m
.

Proof.

Every facet of
\calP(a,p,m)
must satisfy these four conditions, because the generating facets
G_{p,i}^{\lambda}
satisfy them by construction, and all four conditions are invariant under the action of
S^{0}_{p}
.

Conversely, suppose
F
is a totally separated, partition complete subset of
V^{a}_{p}
of size
a+p-1
with
|\Supp(F_{Y})|=i
for some
1\leq i\leq m
. We can permute the vertices of
F
to ensure that
\Supp(F_{Y})=\{0,\dots,i-1\}
. Because
F
is totally separated we must therefore have
F_{X}=\{x_{i},\dots,x_{p}\}=G_{p,i}
.

This leaves us with a total of
(a+p-1)-(p+1-i)=a+i-2
partition vertices in
F
. For each
0\leq r\leq i-1
we let
j_{r}
denote the maximum integer between
1
and
a-1
such that the partition vertex
y_{r}^{j_{r}}
is in
F
. Once again, we can permute the vertices of
F
to ensure that the sequence
\lambda=(j_{0},\dots,j_{i-1})
is monotonically decreasing. The sequence
\lambda
is a partition of
a+i-2
into
i
parts, and because
F
is partition complete we have (after our permutations of vertices) that
F
is equal to the generating facet
G_{p,i}^{\lambda}
.
∎

Now that we have demonstrated the construction of partition complexes, and built up the tools we will need to talk about them, we present our main result.

Theorem 4.21
.

Let
a
,
p
and
m
be positive integers with
a\geq 2
and
1\leq m\leq p
. The partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
is PR with degree type
(\overbrace{1,\dots,1\underbrace{a,1,\dots,1}_{m}}^{p})
.

In the next section we will make use of some deformation retractions to find the homology of both partition complexes and their closures. We also show how all of the links in partition complexes with homology can be built out of smaller partition complexes and closed partition complexes. Then in Section
4.3
we will put these results together to prove Theorem
4.21
.

4.2.
Deformation Retractions and Links

Just as with intersection complexes, we can find the homology of the links in partition complexes by using some deformation retractions. Once again, our main tools for this are Lemma
2.8
and Corollary
2.9
.

Proposition 4.22
.

Let
a
,
p
and
m
be integers with
a\geq 2
and
1\leq m\leq p+1
. There is a deformation retraction
\calP(a,p,m)\rightsquigarrow\calP(2,p,m)
given by the vertex maps
y_{i}^{j}\mapsto y^{1}_{i}
. There is a similar deformation retraction
\barP(a,p,m)\rightsquigarrow\barP(2,p,m)
.

Proof.

Let
y_{i}^{j}
be a partition vertex of
\calP(a,p,m)
for some
j\geq 2
. By construction, every facet of
\calP(a,p,m)
which contains the vertex
y_{i}^{j}
also contains the vertex
y_{i}^{1}
. Thus Corollary
2.9
gives us a deformation retraction of
\calP(a,p,m)
on to the complex obtained by deleting every partition vertex
y_{i}^{j}
for
j\geq 2
, by
collapsing each of these vertices onto the corresponding vertex
y^{1}_{i}
.
The complex obtained from these deletions is
\calP(2,p,m)
. The proof for
\barP(a,p,m)
is identical.
∎

Proposition 4.23
.

Let
p
and
m
be positive integers with
1\leq m\leq p
. There is a deformation retraction
\calP(2,p,m)\rightsquigarrow\partial\langle X_{p}\rangle
. There is a similar deformation retraction
\barP(2,p,m)\rightsquigarrow\langle X_{p}\rangle
.

Proof.

As noted in Example
4.11
, all the facets of
\calP(2,p,m)
contain no more than
m
partition vertices. Thus if
F
is any facet with exactly
m
partition vertices
y_{i_{1}},\dots,y_{i_{m}}
, it must be the
only
facet containing the face
\{y_{i_{1}},\dots,y_{i_{m}}\}
. Because
m\leq p
, we know
F

strictly
contains
\{y_{i_{1}},\dots,y_{i_{m}}\}
, and hence Lemma
2.8
allows us to retract the face
\{y_{i_{1}},\dots,y_{i_{m}}\}
. The complex obtained by deleting
all
faces consisting of
m
partition vertices from
\calP(2,p,m)
is
\calP(2,p,m-1)
.

By induction on
m\geq 1
we obtain a series of deformation retractions
\calP(2,p,m)\rightsquigarrow\calP(2,p,m-1)\rightsquigarrow\dots\rightsquigarrow\calP(2,p,1)
. The facets of
\calP(2,p,1)
are all the totally separated subsets of
V^{2}_{p}
with only a single partition vertex, and so once again we may use Lemma
2.8
to delete these partition vertices. This leaves us with a complex with facets of the form
\{x_{0},\dots,x_{p}\}-\{x_{i}\}
for
0\leq i\leq p
. This is
\partial\langle X_{p}\rangle
.

The proof for
\barP(2,p,m)
is identical, except because
\barP(2,p,m)
also contains the face
X_{p}
, it deformation retracts onto the full simplex
\langle X_{p}\rangle
.

∎

Proposition 4.25
.

For any integer
p\geq 0
, the partition complex
\calP(2,p,p+1)
is acyclic.

Proof.

Our proof for this result is very similar to our proof for Proposition
4.23
above. In that proof, we used Lemma
2.8
to remove all of the partition vertices
y_{0},\dots,y_{p}
from the complex. In this case, we use the same lemma to remove the boundary vertices
x_{0},\dots,x_{p}
.

Note that while
\Delta=\calP(2,p,p+1)
contains the facet
\{y_{0},\dots,y_{p}\}
(which has
p+1
partition vertices), no facet of
\Delta
contains more than
p
boundary vertices. Thus if
F
is a facet of
\Delta
containing
p

x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{p}}
, it must be the

facet containing all of those vertices; and once again, because it

contains them, Lemma

allows us to retract the face

. Proceeding in this way, we may remove all faces consisting of boundary vertices from

, beginning with the ones of size

and continuing in decreasing order of size. Thus we obtain a deformation retraction of
\calP(2,p,p+1)
on to the complex with a single facet
\{y_{0},\dots,y_{p}\}

Proposition 4.27
.

Let
p\geq-1
be an integer. The closed partition complex
\barP(2,p,p+1)
has only
{p}^{\text{th}}
homology, of dimension
1
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
p\geq-1
. The base case is immediate because
\barP(2,-1,0)
is the complex
\{\emptyset\}
, which has only
{(-1)}^{\text{st}}
homology, of dimension
1
.

For the inductive step, suppose
p\geq 0
and set
\widehat{\Delta}=\barP(2,p,p+1)
. Because every facet of
\widehat{\Delta}
is totally separated, we can decompose
\widehat{\Delta}
into the subcomplexes
A
and
B
generated, respectively, by facets containing
x_{p}
and facets containing
y_{p}
. Note that
A
(resp.
B
) is a cone over the vertex
x_{p}
(resp.
y_{p}
) and is therefore acyclic. The intersection
A\cap B
is the set of all separated subsets of
V^{2}_{p-1}=\{x_{0},\dots,x_{p-1},y_{0},\dots,y_{p-1}\}
, and is thus equal to
\barP(2,p-1,p)
. Thus, for each
i\geq 0
, the Mayer-Vietoris Sequence gives us an isomorphism

0\rightarrow\Hred_{i}(\widehat{\Delta})\rightarrow\Hred_{i-1}(\barP(2,p-1,p))\rightarrow 0

and the result follows by the inductive hypothesis.

∎

Taken together these results are enough to give us the homology of all partition complexes and closed partition complexes.

Corollary 4.28
.

Let
a
,
p
and
m
be integers with
a\geq 2
,
p\geq-1
and
0\leq m\leq p+1
. Let
\Delta=\calP(a,p,m)
and
\widehat{\Delta}=\barP(a,p,m)
.

(1)

h(\Delta)=\begin{cases}\{-1\}&\text{ if }m=0\\
\{p-1\}&\text{ if }1\leq m\leq p\\
\emptyset&\text{ if }m=p+1\text{ and }p\neq-1\end{cases}

and in the first two cases, the dimension of the nontrivial homology is
1
.

(2)

h(\widehat{\Delta})=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\text{ if }m=0\text{ and }p\neq-1\\
\emptyset&\text{ if }1\leq m\leq p\\
\{p\}&\text{ if }m=p+1\end{cases}

and in the final case, the dimension of the nontrivial homology is
1
.

Proof.

We consider the
m=0
cases together first. The partition complex
\calP(a,p,0)
is defined to be the irrelevant complex
\{\emptyset\}
which has only
{(-1)}^{\text{st}}
homology. Meanwhile the closed partition complex
\barP(a,p,0)
is the full
p
-simplex
\langle X_{p}\rangle
, which is acyclic except in the case where
p=-1
. We now proceed to the other cases.

For part (1), if
1\leq m\leq p
, then Propositions
4.22
and
4.23
give us a deformation retraction from
\calP(a,p,m)
on to
\partial\langle X_{p}\rangle
, which is the boundary of a
p
-simplex, and hence has only
{(p-1)}^{\text{st}}
homology of dimension 1. If
m=p+1
and
p\neq-1
, then Proposition
4.22
gives us a deformation retraction from
\calP(a,p,p+1)
on to
\calP(2,p,p+1)
, which is acyclic by Proposition
4.25
.

For part (2), if
1\leq m\leq p
, then Propositions
4.22
and
4.23
give us a deformation retraction from
\barP(a,p,m)
to
\langle X_{p}\rangle
, which is a full
p
-simplex, and is hence acyclic. If
m=p+1
, then Proposition
4.22
gives us a deformation retraction from
\barP(a,p,m)
on to
\barP(2,p,m)
. By Proposition
4.27
this has only
{p}^{\text{th}}
homology, of dimension
1
. ∎

We now examine the links in partition complexes. Suppose
\sigma
is a face of a partition complex
\calP(a,p,m)
. We can partition
\sigma
into
\sigma_{X}\sqcup\sigma_{Y}
(as defined in Notation
4.17
). We first consider the case
\sigma_{Y}=\emptyset
. In fact, it suffices to consider the subcase where
\sigma
is equal to the boundary vertex
x_{p}
.

Lemma 4.29
.

Let
a
,
p
and
m
be integers with
a\geq 2
and
1\leq m\leq p+1
, and let
\Delta=\calP(a,p,m)
. We have

\link_{\Delta}x_{p}=\begin{cases}\calP(a,p-1,m)&\text{ if }1\leq m\leq p\\
\calP(a,p-1,p)&\text{ if }m=p+1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We begin with the case where
1\leq m\leq p
. Let
F
be a facet of
\Delta
containing
x_{p}
. We know
F
is of the form
gG_{p,i}^{\lambda}
for some permutation
g\in S^{0}_{p}
, some integer
1\leq i\leq m
and some partition
\lambda\in\mathbb{P}(a,i)
.

Let
i=g^{-1}(p)
and define
\widetilde{g}
to be the permutation in
S^{0}_{p}
given by

\widetilde{g}(j)=\begin{cases}p&\text{ if }j=p\\
g(p)&\text{ if }j=i\\
g(j)&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The facet
gG^{\lambda}_{p,i}
contains the vertex
x_{p}=x_{\widetilde{g}(p)}
by assumption, and the vertex
g(x_{p})=x_{g(p)}=x_{\widetilde{g}(i)}
by definition. This means it is equal to the facet
\widetilde{g}G^{\lambda}_{p,i}
. Thus we may assume that
g
fixes
x_{p}
, and hence restricts to a permutation in
S^{0}_{p-1}
. This gives us

\displaystyle F-x_{p}

\displaystyle=gG_{p,i}^{\lambda}-x_{p}

\displaystyle=g(G_{p,i}^{\lambda}-x_{p})

\displaystyle=gG_{p-1,i}^{\lambda}

which is a facet of
\calP(a,p-1,m)
.

Conversely, for any generating facet
G^{\lambda}_{p-1,i}
of
\calP(a,p-1,m)
and any permutation
h
in
S^{0}_{p-1}
we can view
h
as a permutation in
S^{0}_{p}
which fixes
p
, and hence we have
hG^{\lambda}_{p-1,i}\sqcup\{x_{p}\}=hG^{\lambda}_{p,i}
, which is a facet of
\calP(a,p,m)
.

For the
m=p+1
case note that the set
G_{p,p+1}
is empty, and therefore for every partition
\lambda\in\mathbb{P}(a,p+1)
the generating facet
G^{\lambda}_{p,p+1}
contains no boundary vertices. Thus the only facets of
\calP(a,p,p+1)
which contain
x_{p}
are those of the form
gG^{\lambda}_{p,i}
for
1\leq i\leq p
and permutations
g\in S^{0}_{p}
. The remainder of the proof is identical to the earlier case.
∎

Corollary 4.30
.

Let
a
,
p
and
m
be integers with
a\geq 2
and
1\leq m\leq p+1
, and let
\Delta=\calP(a,p,m)
. Let
\sigma
be a face of
\Delta
of size
\alpha
contained entirely inside
X_{p}
. We have an isomorphism of complexes

\link_{\Delta}\sigma\cong\begin{cases}\calP(a,p-\alpha,m)&\text{ if }0\leq\alpha\leq p-m\\
\calP(a,p-\alpha,p+1-\alpha)&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\alpha\geq 0
. The base case
\alpha=0
is immediate.

Now suppose
\alpha\geq 1
. By symmetry we may assume that
x_{p}\in\sigma
: indeed, if it is not we may choose some permutation
g\in S^{0}_{p}
such that
x_{p}\in g\sigma
, and by symmetry we have an isomorphism of complexes
\link_{\Delta}\sigma\cong\link_{\Delta}g\sigma
.

We consider the two cases separately. First we assume that
1\leq\alpha\leq p-m
. In particular this means that
m\leq p-1
, and hence Lemma
4.29
tells us that
\link_{\Delta}x_{p}=\calP(a,p-1,m)
. Thus
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
is equal to
\link_{\calP(a,p-1,m)}(\sigma-x_{p})
. Note that
0\leq\alpha-1\leq(p-1)-m
, and so the inductive hypothesis gives us an isomorphism
\link_{\calP(a,p-1,m)}(\sigma-x_{p})\cong\calP(a,p-\alpha,m)
.

Now suppose
\alpha>p-m
. By Lemma
4.29
, we know that
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
is equal to either
\link_{\calP(a,p-1,m)}(\sigma-x_{p})
or
\link_{\calP(a,p-1,p)}(\sigma-x_{p})
. We know that
\alpha-1
is greater than both
(p-1)-m
and
(p-1)-p
, so in either case the inductive hypothesis gives us an isomorphism
\link_{\Delta}\sigma\cong\calP(a,p-\alpha,p+1-\alpha)
.
∎

Corollary
4.30
allows us to restrict to the case where
\sigma_{X}=\emptyset
, because it shows us that the link of
\sigma_{X}
is also a partition complex, and thus the link of
\sigma
in
\calP(a,p,m)
is isomorphic to the link of
\sigma_{Y}
in a smaller partition complex.

The following lemma allow us to restrict our attention even further.

Lemma 4.31
.

Let
a
,
p
and
m
be positive integers with
m\leq p+1
, and let
\Delta=\calP(a,p,m)
. If
\sigma
is a face of
\Delta
which is not partition complete then
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
is acyclic.

Proof.

Suppose
\sigma\in\Delta
is not partition complete. This means there exist some

and

such that the parition vertex

is in

\Delta
is partition complete, every facet of
\Delta

. Thus
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
is a cone over

.
∎

It now only remains for us to consider the case where
\sigma
is a nonempty partition complete face of
\Delta
contained entirely in

. We do this using the following lemma and proposition.

Lemma 4.32
.

Let
a
,
p
and
m
be positive integers with
m\leq p+1
, and let
\Delta=\calP(a,p,m)
. Fix some
\sigma\in\Delta
. For each

such that there exists a partition vertex

which is not contained in
\sigma

\varphi(\sigma,i)=\min\{1\leq j\leq a-1:y_{i}^{j}\notin\sigma\}

\Phi
of
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
onto a complex
\Gamma
obtained by

in

.

Proof.

Let
y_{i}^{j}
be a partition vertex in
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
. This partition vertex cannot be contained in
\sigma
, and hence the index
\varphi(\sigma,i)
is well-defined. For any facet
F
of
\link_{\Delta}\sigma
containing

, we have that

is a facet of
\Delta
. This means

\link_{\Delta}\sigma

.

Finally we consider part (3). For any two faces

5.
Pure Resolutions of Any Degree Type

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.5
. Our method for this proof is focussed around finding operations on simplicial complexes which preserve the PR property in specific cases, while altering the degree types of PR complexes in specified ways.

Specifically, we will construct a family of operations on simplicial complexes
\{\phi_{i}:i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}
with the property that for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and any PR complex
\Delta
with degree type
(d_{p},\dots,d_{i},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{i-1})
for some
p\geq i
, the complex
\phi_{i}(\Delta)
is PR with degree type
(d_{p},\dots,d_{i-1},d_{i}+1,\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{i-1})
. If
\Delta
is PR with a degree type of a different form, then
\phi_{i}(\Delta)
will not be PR.

To see why the existence of such a family is sufficient to prove Theorem
1.5
, suppose we wish to construct a PR complex of degree type
\mathbf{d}=(d_{p},\dots,d_{1})
. We can do so by taking a PR complex
\Delta
of degree type
(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{p})
(such as the boundary of the
p
-simplex in Example
2.26
), and applying each of the operations
\phi_{i}
to
\Delta
a total of
(d_{i}-1)
times.

In other words, if
\Delta
is the boundary of the
p
-simplex, then the simplicial complex
\phi_{1}^{d_{1}-1}\dots\phi_{p}^{d_{p}-1}(\Delta)
is PR with degree type
(d_{p},\dots,d_{1})
.

.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on vertex set
V
,
i\leq\dim\Delta+1
, and
\widetilde{\Delta}=\phi_{i}(\Delta)
on vertex set
V\sqcup S^{i}_{\Delta}
. For any nonempty face
\sigma
of
\widetilde{\Delta}
contained entirely in
S^{i}_{\Delta}
, the complex
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
is acyclic.

Proof.

Suppose
\sigma=\{u_{\tau_{1}},\dots,u_{\tau_{r}}\}
for some faces
\tau_{1}\subset\dots\subset\tau_{r}
of
\Delta
. The condition on
i
implies that
\tau_{1}\neq\emptyset
.

We must have
\tau_{1}\in\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
because
\tau_{1}\sqcup\{u_{\tau_{1}},\dots,u_{\tau_{r}}\}
is a face of
\widetilde{\Delta}
. Also, no vertex in
V-\tau_{1}
can be contained in
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
, because by construction, for any facet
G
of
\widetilde{\Delta}
containing
u_{\tau_{1}}
, we have
G\cap V\subseteq\tau_{1}
. We claim that there is a deformation retraction
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma\rightsquigarrow\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma|_{V}=\langle\tau_{1}\rangle
, which is acyclic.

We start by removing every vertex
u_{\rho}
in
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
and
S^{i}_{\Delta}
for which
\rho\subset\tau_{1}
. Suppose
u_{\rho}
is any such vertex with
|\rho|
minimal. By the minimality of
|\rho|
, we know that every facet of
\widetilde{\Delta}
containing
u_{\rho}
must also contain
\rho
. Thus every facet of
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
containing
u_{\rho}
must also contain
\rho
. Setting
g=\{u_{\rho}\}
and
f=\{u_{\rho}\}\cup\rho
, Lemma
2.8
gives us a deformation retraction
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma\rightsquigarrow\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma-\{u_{\rho}\}
. Continuing in this way we may remove every vertex
u_{\rho}
in
S^{i}_{\Delta}
from
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
, in increasing order of the size of
\rho
.

Now we remove the vertices
u_{\rho}
in
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
and
S^{i}_{\Delta}
for which
\rho\supset\tau_{1}
. Suppose
u_{\rho}
is any such vertex. Because
\rho
contains
\tau_{1}
, every facet of
\widetilde{\Delta}
containing
u_{\rho}
and
\sigma
must also contain
\tau_{1}
. Thus every facet of
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
containing
u_{\rho}
must also contain
\tau_{1}
. Setting
g=\{u_{\rho}\}
and
f=\{u_{\rho}\}\cup\tau_{1}
, Lemma
2.8
allows us to remove
u_{\rho}
from
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
.
∎

5.4.
Links in
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex and
i>\dim\Delta+1
, and set
\widetilde{\Delta}=\phi_{i}(\Delta)
. The condition on
i
ensures that
u_{\emptyset}
is a vertex in
S^{i}_{\Delta}
.

In this section, we examine the links of those faces
\sigma
of
\widetilde{\Delta}
for which
\emptyset\neq\sigma\subseteq S^{i}_{\Delta}
(i.e. the nonempty faces in the induced subcomplex
\widetilde{\Delta}|_{S^{i}_{\Delta}}
). We begin by showing that for any such
\sigma
, we can express the homology of
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
in terms of the homology of
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\sigma\cup\{u_{\emptyset}\})
. As we will explain, this allows us to restrict our attention to the links in the barycentric subdivision complex
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex and
i>\dim\Delta+1
, and set
\widetilde{\Delta}=\phi_{i}(\Delta)
. Suppose
\emptyset\neq\sigma\subseteq S^{i}_{\Delta}
is a face of
\widetilde{\Delta}
with
u_{\emptyset}\notin\sigma
. For every
j\geq-1
we have an isomorphism
\Hred_{j}(\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma)\cong\Hred_{j-1}(\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\sigma\cup\{u_{\emptyset}\}))
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Corollary
5.12
, we may decompose
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
into a subcomplex
A
consisting of facets which contain
u_{\emptyset}
and a subcomplex
B
consisting of those which do not. The intersection of these subcomplexes consists of those faces
f
in
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma
for which
u_{\emptyset}
is not in
f
but
f\sqcup\{u_{\emptyset}\}
is a face of
\widetilde{\Delta}
. In other words, we have
A\cap B=\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\sigma\cup\{u_{\emptyset}\})
.

The subcomplex
A
is a cone over
u_{\emptyset}
, and is therefore acyclic. For every face
f
in
B
, the intersection of
f
and
S^{i}_{\Delta}
contains only vertices
u_{\tau}
for which
\tau
is nonempty. All of these are faces of
\phi_{m}(\Delta)
where
m=\dim\Delta+1
, and hence we have
B=\link_{\phi_{m}(\Delta)}\sigma
, which is also acyclic by Lemma
5.14
.

Thus for every
j\geq-1
, the Mayer-Vietoris Sequence gives us an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Hred_{j}(\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\sigma)\rightarrow\Hred_{j-1}(\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\sigma\cup\{u_{\emptyset}\}))\rightarrow 0

as required.
∎

Proposition
5.15
allows us to restrict our attention to those faces of
\widetilde{\Delta}|_{S^{i}_{\Delta}}
which contain
u_{\emptyset}
. Note we have
\link_{\widetilde{\Delta}}u_{\emptyset}=\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
, and hence the link of any face of
\widetilde{\Delta}
which contains
u_{\emptyset}
must be a link in
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
. For this reason, we devote the rest of this section to investigating the links of
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
.

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
\sigma=\{u_{\tau_{1}},\dots,u_{\tau_{r}}\}\in\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
for some faces
\tau_{1}\subset\dots\subset\tau_{r}
of
\Delta
. We have an isomorphism of complexes

\link_{\mathcal{B}(\Delta)}\sigma\cong\mathcal{B}(\link_{\Delta}\tau_{r})\ast\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{r}}\tau_{r-1})\ast\dots\ast\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{2}}\tau_{1})\ast\mathcal{B}(\partial\tau_{1}).

Proof.

For notational convenience, we set
\tau_{0}=\emptyset
, so that
\mathcal{B}(\partial\tau_{1})
may be rewritten as
\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{1}}\tau_{0})
.

Let
A_{r}
denote the induced subcomplex of
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
on vertices of the form
u_{f}
where
f
contains
\tau_{r}
; and for each
0\leq j\leq r-1
, let
A_{j}
denote the induced subcomplex of
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
on vertices of the form
u_{f}
for which we have
\tau_{j}\subset f\subset\tau_{j+1}
. Note that
A_{r},\dots,A_{0}
are pairwise disjoint subcomplexes of
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
.

We claim that
\link_{\mathcal{B}(\Delta)}\sigma=A_{r}\ast A_{r-1}\ast\dots\ast A_{0}
. This is sufficient to prove our proposition because the complex
A_{r}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\link_{\Delta}\tau_{r})
via the vertex map
u_{f}\mapsto u_{f-\tau_{r}}
, and for each
0\leq j\leq r-1
, the complex
A_{j}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{j+1}}\tau_{j})
via the vertex map
u_{f}\mapsto u_{f-\tau_{j}}
.

Let
\rho=\rho_{r}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\rho_{0}
be a face of
A_{r}\ast\dots\ast A_{0}
, with
\rho_{j}\in A_{j}
for each
0\leq j\leq r
. For each
0\leq j\leq r-1
, the face
\rho_{j}
must be of the form
\{u_{f_{1}},\dots,u_{f_{m}}\}
for some sequence of faces
\tau_{j}\subset f_{1}\subset\dots\subset f_{m}\subset\tau_{j+1}
of
\Delta
. Similarly the face
\rho_{r}
must be of the form
\{u_{f_{1}},\dots,u_{f_{m}}\}
for some sequence of faces
\tau_{r}\subset f_{1}\subset\dots\subset f_{m}
of
\Delta
. In particular, none of the vertices
u_{\tau_{1}},\dots,u_{\tau_{r}}
are contained in
\rho
, so we have
\rho\cap\sigma=\emptyset
. Moreover, the faces of
\Delta
corresponding to vertices in
\rho\sqcup\sigma
may be arranged in a strict sequence by inclusion, which means that
\rho\sqcup\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
. Thus
\rho
is a face of
\link_{\mathcal{B}(\Delta)}\sigma
.

Conversely, suppose
\rho=\{u_{f_{1}},\dots,u_{f_{m}}\}
is any face of
\link_{\mathcal{B}(\Delta)}\sigma
. We may decompose
\rho
into the disjoint union
\rho_{r}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\rho_{0}
, where for each
0\leq j\leq r-1
the face
\rho_{j}
contains all the vertices
u_{f}
in
\rho
for which we have
\tau_{j}\subset f\subset\tau_{j+1}
, making
\rho_{j}
a face of
A_{j}
; and the face
\rho_{r}
contains all the vertices
u_{f}
in
\rho
for which
f
contains
\tau_{r}
, making
\rho_{r}
a face of
A_{r}
. Thus
\rho
is a face of
A_{r}\ast\dots\ast A_{0}
.
∎

In particular, Proposition
5.16
has the following important corollaries.

Corollary 5.17
.

Let
\Delta
and
\sigma=\{u_{\tau_{1}},\dots,u_{\tau_{r}}\}\in\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
be as in Proposition
5.16
. We have
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),\sigma)=h(\Delta,\tau_{r})\boxplus\{|\tau_{r}|-r\}
(where
\boxplus
denotes the addition of sets operation laid out in Definition
2.31
).

Proof.

Just as in the proof of Proposition
5.16
, we set
\tau_{0}=\emptyset
for notational convenience. Using this proposition and Proposition
2.10
, we can compute the homology of
\link_{\mathcal{B}(\Delta)}\sigma
from the homologies of
\calB(\link_{\Delta}\tau_{r}),\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{r}}\tau_{r-1}),\dots,\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{1}}\tau_{0})
.

To compute these homologies, first recall that for any integer
i\geq-1
we have

(3)

\Hred_{i}(\mathcal{B}(\Delta))=\Hred_{i}(\Delta).

Next, note that for each
0\leq j\leq r-1
, the complex
\partial\tau_{j+1}
is the boundary of the
(|\tau_{j+1}|-1)
-simplex. As observed in Example
2.26
, the link of any face
\tau
in the boundary of the
p
-simplex is the boundary of the
(p-|\tau|)
-simplex, which has homology only at degree
p-|\tau|-1
. Thus we have

(4)

h(\link_{\partial\tau_{j+1}}\tau_{j})=\{|\tau_{j+1}|-|\tau_{j}|-2\}.

Putting these results together, we find

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),\sigma)

\displaystyle=h(\mathcal{B}(\link_{\Delta}\tau_{r})\ast\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{1}}\tau_{0})\ast\dots\ast\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{r}}\tau_{r-1}))

by Prop.
5.16

\displaystyle=h(\mathcal{B}(\link_{\Delta}\tau_{r}))\boxplus\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}h(\mathcal{B}(\link_{\partial\tau_{j+1}}\tau_{j}))\boxplus\{r\}

by Prop.
2.10

\displaystyle=h(\link_{\Delta}\tau_{r})\boxplus\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}h(\link_{\partial\tau_{j+1}}\tau_{j})\boxplus\{r\}

by Eq.
3

\displaystyle=h(\Delta,\tau_{r})\boxplus\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\{|\tau_{j+1}|-|\tau_{j}|-2\}\boxplus\{r\}

by Eq.
4

\displaystyle=h(\Delta,\tau_{r})\boxplus\{|\tau_{r}|-r\}.

∎

Corollary 5.18
.

Let
\Delta
be a PR complex with degree type
(1,\dots,1)
. For any
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
we have that
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),\sigma)
is either empty or equal to
\{\dim\Delta-|\sigma|\}

Proof.

For
\sigma=\emptyset
we have
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),\emptyset)=h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta))=h(\Delta)
, because
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
is homeomorphic to
\Delta
. Thus
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),\emptyset)
is empty, unless
\Delta
has homology, in which case it is equal to
\{\dim\Delta\}
by Corollary
2.34
.

Now assume
\sigma=\{u_{\tau_{1}},\dots,u_{\tau_{r}}\}
for some
\tau_{1}\subset\dots\subset\tau_{r}
in
\Delta
. From Corollary
5.17
, we know that
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),\sigma)=h(\Delta,\tau_{r})\boxplus\{|\tau_{r}|-|\sigma|\}
. If
h(\Delta,\tau_{r})
is empty (i.e.
\link_{\Delta}\tau_{r}
is acyclic) then this sum is empty. Otherwise, by Corollary
2.34
we have
h(\Delta,\tau_{r})=\{\dim\Delta-|\tau_{r}|\}
, and hence
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),\sigma)=\{\dim\Delta-|\tau_{r}|\}\boxplus\{|\tau_{r}|-|\sigma|\}=\{\dim\Delta-|\sigma|\}
.
∎

It follows from Corollary
5.18
that if
\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay (i.e. PR with degree type
(1,\dots,1)
), then
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
is also Cohen-Macaulay. In fact, this turns out to be the
only
condition under which
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
is PR, as the following proposition demonstrates. This proposition will not be strictly necessary for our proof of Theorem
5.5
, but it helps to illuminate why the operation
\phi_{i}
preserves the PR property for PR complexes of degree type
(d_{p},\dots,d_{i},1,\dots,1)
, and why it fails to do so for PR complexes of other degree types.

Proposition 5.19
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
is PR.

(2)

\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
is Cohen-Macaulay (i.e. PR with degree type
(1,\dots,1)
).

(3)

\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay (i.e. PR with degree type
(1,\dots,1)
).

Proof.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2) follows from Corollary
5.18
, and (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) is immediate. To prove (1)
\Rightarrow
(3), we show the contrapositive.

First assume that
\Delta
is not PR. This means that
\Delta
has two faces
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
of different sizes such that the intersection
h(\Delta,\tau_{1})\cap h(\Delta,\tau_{2})
is nonempty. Suppose
\iota
is an index in both
h(\Delta,\tau_{1})
and
h(\Delta,\tau_{2})
. By Corollary
5.17
, we have
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),u_{\tau_{1}})=h(\Delta,\tau_{1})\boxplus\{|\tau_{1}|-1\}
and
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),u_{\tau_{2}})=h(\Delta,\tau_{2})\boxplus\{|\tau_{2}|-1\}
. Thus the complete homology index set
\widehat{h}(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),1)
contains both
\iota+|\tau_{1}|-1
and
\iota+|\tau_{2}|-1
, and therefore cannot be a singleton. This means
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
is not PR by Corollary
2.16
.

Now assume that
\Delta
is PR of degree type
(d_{p},\dots,d_{1})
where
d_{j}>1
for some
1\leq j\leq p
. By Proposition
2.33
,
\Delta
must have two faces
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
such that
|\tau_{2}|=|\tau_{1}|+d_{j}
and
h(\Delta,\tau_{1})=\{\iota\}
while
h(\Delta,\tau_{2})=\{\iota-1\}
for some index
\iota
. By Corollary
5.17
, we have
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),u_{\tau_{1}})=\{\iota\}\boxplus\{|\tau_{1}|-1\}=\{\iota+|\tau_{1}|-1\}
, and
h(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),u_{\tau_{2}})=\{\iota-1\}\boxplus\{|\tau_{2}|-1\}=\{\iota+|\tau_{1}|+d_{j}-2\}
. In particular, because
d_{j}>1
, we have
d_{j}-2>-1
and hence these two sets are not equal. Thus, once again, the complete homology index set
\widehat{h}(\mathcal{B}(\Delta),1)
is not a singleton, and so
\mathcal{B}(\Delta)
cannot be PR by Corollary
2.16
.
∎

First we assume
u_{\emptyset}\in\tau
. In this case we have

\displaystyle h(\widetilde{\Delta},\sigma)

\displaystyle=h(\phi_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma_{V}),\tau)

by Lemma
5.6

\displaystyle=h(\mathcal{B}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma_{V}),\tau-\{u_{\emptyset}\})

\displaystyle\link_{\phi_{i}(\Gamma)}u_{\emptyset}=\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\subseteq\{\dim(\link_{\Delta}\sigma_{V})-|\tau|+1\}

by Corollary
5.18

\displaystyle=\{\dim\Delta-|\sigma_{V}|-|\sigma_{S}|+1\}

\displaystyle|\tau|=|\sigma_{S}|

\displaystyle=\{\dim\Delta-m+1\}

\displaystyle\text{by definition of }m

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}(\Delta,m-1)

by Cor.
2.34

Now we assume
u_{\emptyset}\notin\tau
. In this case we have

\displaystyle h(\widetilde{\Delta},\sigma)

\displaystyle=h(\phi_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma_{V}),\tau)

by Lemma
5.6

\displaystyle=h(\phi_{i}(\link_{\Delta}\sigma_{V}),\tau\sqcup\{u_{\emptyset}\})\boxplus\{1\}

by Prop.
5.15

\displaystyle\subseteq\widehat{h}(\Delta,m)\boxplus\{1\}

\displaystyle\text{by the }u_{\emptyset}\in\tau\text{ case}

\displaystyle=\{\dim\Delta-m\}\boxplus\{1\}

by Cor.
2.34

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}(\Delta,m-1)

by Cor.
2.34
.

This exhausts all the possible cases for
\sigma
, and it is sufficent to prove that Equation (
5
) is satisfied, because

•

For
m<s+\sum_{j=i}^{p}d_{j}
, any face of
\widetilde{\Delta}
of size
m
falls under case 1.1 or 2.1, and hence
\widehat{h}(\widetilde{\Delta},m)=\widehat{h}(\Delta,m)
.

•

For
m=s+\sum_{j=i}^{p}d_{j}
, any face of
\widetilde{\Delta}
of size
m
falls under case 1.2 or 2.1, and hence
\widehat{h}(\widetilde{\Delta},m)=\emptyset
.

•

For
m>s+\sum_{j=i}^{p}d_{j}
, any face of
\widetilde{\Delta}
of size
m
falls under case 1.2, 2.1 or 2.2, and hence
\widehat{h}(\widetilde{\Delta},m)=\widehat{h}(\Delta,m-1)
.

We have now shown that Equation (
5
) is satisfied in all possible cases, which proves Theorem
5.5
.
∎

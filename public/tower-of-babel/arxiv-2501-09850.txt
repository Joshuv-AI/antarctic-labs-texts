Logarithmic Non-Abelian Hodge Theory for curves in prime characteristic
Mark Andrea de Cataldo and Siqing Zhang

Abstract
. For a curve C C and a reductive group G G in prime characteristic, we relate the de Rham moduli of logarithmic G G -connections on C C to the Dolbeault moduli of logarithmic G G -Higgs bundles on the Frobenius twist of C C . We name this result the Log- p p -NAHT. It is a logarithmic version of Chen-Zhu’s characteristic p p Non Abelian Hodge Theorem ( p p -NAHT) in [ CZ15 ] . In contrast to the no pole case, the two moduli stacks in the log case are not isomorphic étale locally over the Hitchin base. Instead, they differ by an Artin-Schreier type Galois cover of the base. In contrast to the case over the complex numbers, where some parabolic/parahoric data are needed to specify the boundary behavior of the tame harmonic metrics, no parabolic/parahoric data are needed in Log- p p -NAHT. We also establish a semistable version of the Log- p p -NAHT, and deduce several geometric and cohomological consequences. In particular, when G = G ​ L r G=GL_{r} , the Log- p p -NAHT induces an embedding of the intersection cohomology of the degree d d Dolbeault moduli to that of the degree p ​ d pd de Rham moduli, and the embedding is an isomorphism when r r is coprime to d d and p > r p>r .



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface.
The Non Abelian Hodge Theorem (NAHT) of C. Simpson et. al. establishes a
canonical homeomorphism between the moduli spaces of semistable
Higgs bundles of degree zero and algebraic flat connections on
X
.
If we allow log-poles at some fixed punctures on
X
,
Simpson has established an equivalence of categories relating the
parabolic
variants of such objects on
X
; this result, and its vast generalization due to T. Mochizuki is usually referred to as the Log-NAHT. However, it
is well-known that there is no natural version of the Log-NAHT relating semistable Higgs bundles
with log poles to flat connections with log poles in the form of a natural bijection: one needs to fix the behaviour at the punctures in terms of parabolic data.

There is a natural analogue
of the NAHT result for a nonsingular projective irreducible curve
C
over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic: this analogue is called the
p
-NAHT and it relates
Higgs bundles on the Frobenius twist
C^{\prime}
of
C
and flat connections
on the curve
C
: the two moduli stacks are étale
locally equivalent over the Hitchin base, target of the Hitchin morphism from the Higgs moduli stack and of the de Rham-Hitchin morphism from the moduli stack of conenctions.

In this paper, we establish a non parabolic/parahoric Log-
p
-NAHT for curves
C
over algebraically closed fields of characteristic
p
.
The first novelty is that the Log-
p
-NAHT is about Higgs bundles/flat connections without the need of additional structure of parabolic/parahoric data. Unlike the case where there are no poles, the fibers of the de Rham-Hitchin morphism are disconnected, which prohibits an étale local equivalence over the Hitchin base as in the case with no poles. The second novelty is that
then
the Log-
p
-NAHT takes place in the form of an étale local equivalence over a Galois branched cover
of the Hitchin base, obtained by means of an Artin-Schreier-type morphism arising from the relation between the residues of
the log-connections and the residues of their
p
-curvatures.

In the remainder of this introduction, we provide context and summarize the results of this paper.

In this paper, we consider the moduli of log-Higgs bundles and log-connections on
C
of degree
d
, which are denoted by
\mathscr{M}_{Dol}(C,G,D,d)
and
\mathscr{M}_{dR}(C,G,D,d)
, respectively.
We denote by
\mathscr{M}^{ss}_{(-)}(C,G,D,d)
the corresponding open substacks of slope-semistable objects.
If
G
satisfies the Low Height (LH) property as in
Definition

1.7
, or if
G=GL_{r}
, then
\mathscr{M}^{ss}_{(-)}(C,G,D,d)
admits a quasi-projective adequate moduli space

M_{(-)}^{ss}(C,G,D,d)
, see
[
Lan14
, Thm. 1.1]
for the existence of these moduli spaces in the vector bundle case, and
[
HZ23a
, Thm. 2.26]
for the general
G
-bundle case.

For any invertible sheaf
L
, we use
\mathscr{M}_{Dol}(C,G,L,d)
to denote the moduli stack of
L
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
d
on
C
.
In particular, we have that
\mathscr{M}_{Dol}(C,G,\omega_{C}(D),d)
is the same as
\mathscr{M}_{Dol}(C,G,D,d)
.

Unless otherwise stated, the group
G
is fixed and we omit
it from the notation.
At times, we omit the decorations
C
and
D
when they are clear from the context.
At times, we omit the decoration
d
to denote the whole moduli stack of objects of all possible degrees. We have
\mathscr{M}_{(-)}(C,D)=\coprod_{d\in\pi_{1}(G)}\mathscr{M}_{(-)}(C,D,d)
, etc.
We refer to the case when
D=\emptyset
as to the “case with no poles.”

2.1.4.
The Hitchin morphism

Let

be the functor that sends a

-scheme

\mathfrak{c}_{\omega_{C}(D)}\times_{\mathrm{Spec}(k)}R\to C\times_{\mathrm{Spec}(k)}R

The functor

The quotient morphism

Similarly, we also have the Hitchin morphisms

A.0.5.
Twisted products

Let
f:Y\to C
and
h:Z\to C
be two affine
C
-schemes with
\mathcal{G}
-actions.
We define the twisted product
Y\times^{\mathcal{G}}Z
to be the GIT quotient
(Y\times Z)/\!/\mathcal{G}
over
C
, i.e. we have

(74)

Y\times^{\mathcal{G}}Z:=\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathrm{Eq}(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}\rightrightarrows f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}\otimes g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}})),

where Eq is the equalizer and the two arrows are the action and projection comorphisms.
The twisted product
Y\times^{\mathcal{G}}Z
is well-defined because the formation of the equalizer respects Zariski localizations over
C
.
Indeed, more is true:
[
Ses77
, p.243, Lemma 2]
shows that the formation of the equalizer (thus of
Y\times^{\mathcal{G}}Z
) commutes with flat base change to
C
.

act^{\#}:s_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}\to s_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}\otimes g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}.

\mathcal{G}
-invariant derivations form a subsheaf

.

-schemes

\partial

A.0.14.
Dual log-connections

Let

C

\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}

Let
(s:E\to C,\nabla_{E})

\mathcal{G}(U)

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{G}

by
[

.

.

such that
\epsilon

-torsors:

\mathcal{G}
-torsor

\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Since

On the Macdonald correspondence
Anne-Marie Aubert

Abstract
. In 1980 Ian G. Macdonald established an explicit bijection between the isomorphism classes of the irreducible representations of GL n ​ ( k ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(k) , where k k is a finite field, and inertia equivalence classes of admissible tamely ramified n n -dimensional Weil-Deligne representations of W F W_{F} , where F F is a non-archimedean local field with residue field k k and W F W_{F} the absolute Weil group of F F . We describe a construction of the Macdonald correspondence based on the specialization to GL n ​ ( k ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(k) of Lusztig’s classification of irreducible representations of finite groups of Lie type, and review some properties of the correspondence. We define ϵ \epsilon -factors for pairs of irreducible cuspidal representations of finite general linear groups, and show that they match with the expected Deligne ϵ \epsilon -factors under the Macdonald correspondence. We use these ϵ \epsilon -factors for pairs to obtain a characterization of the Macdonald correspondence for the irreducible cuspidal representations.

1.
Introduction

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
be the general linear group of
n
by
n
matrices over a finite field
k
. In
[
Gr
]
Green determined all irreducible characters of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)

by parabolic induction, generalizing the Frobenius theory of the symmetric groups. The Green theory has greatly influenced the developments of representation theory and algebraic combinatorics.
Deligne and Lusztig
[
DL
]
generalized the Green parabolic induction to construct linear representations of finite groups of Lie types using
\ell
-adic cohomology and later
Lusztig found all representations of finite groups of Lie type
[
Lu
]
.

In algebraic combinatorics, the Green theory showed the importance of a new family of symmetric functions-Hall-Littlewood functions.
Macdonald has written the famous book
[
Mac1
]
to account the Green identification of the Hall algebra with the ring of symmetric functions.

The (non-archimedean) local Langlands correspondence was established by Laumon, Rapoport, and Stuhler
[
LRS
]
for function fields, and by Harris-Taylor
[
HT
]
, Henniart
[
He2
]
and
Scholze
[
Sc
]
, independently, for finite extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, with
p
a prime number.
Long before this, in 1980, by using the Green construction, Macdonald
constructed in
[
Mac2
]
a correspondence between the isomorphism classes of the irreducible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
, and inertia equivalence classes of
admissible tamely ramified
n
-dimensional Weil-Deligne representations of
W_{F}
, where
F
is a non-archimedean local field with residue field
k
and
W_{F}
the absolute Weil group of
F
.
Macdonald defined also in
[
Mac2
]
a version over finite fields of the Godement-Jacquet
\epsilon
-factors and proved that his correspondence matches them with the
\epsilon
-factors that were
defined by Deligne in
[
Del
]
.

Silberger and Zink established in
[
SiZi2
, (A.1)]
the compatibility of the Macdonald correspondence with the local Langlands correspondence for the depth-zero representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
.

On the other hand, in the
p
-adic situation, the pioneering work
[
JPSS
]
of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika defined the notion of Rankin-Selberg local factors, also called
local factors for pairs
, for
pairs irreducible generic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, including
\gamma
-factors for pairs. In 1993, Henniart established in
[
He1
]
a converse theorem based on these latter, and, as consequence, obtained a
characterization of the local Langlands correspondence.

In the finite situation,
\gamma
-factors for pairs were defined by Roditty-Gershon in
[
RG
]
and by Nien in
[
Nie
, Theorem 2.10]
, but only when at least one the representation in the pair is cuspidal.
Recently in
[
SoZe
]
, Soudry and Zelingher provided a general definition of
\gamma
-factors for pairs of irreducible generic representations of finite general linear groups, by using a finite version of the Langlands-Shahidi method,
and established that these
\gamma
-factors for pairs are multiplicative.

We first describe a construction of the Macdonald correspondence based on the specialization to
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
of Lusztig’s classification of irreducible representations of finite groups of Lie type, and review
properties of the correspondence. Next, by using
[
SoZe
]
, we define
\epsilon
-factors for pairs of irreducible cuspidal representations of finite general linear groups, and show that they match with the expected Deligne
\epsilon
-factors under the
Macdonald correspondence. Thanks to a converse theorem established by Nien in
[
Nie
]
, following Henniart’s approach, we obtain in Theorem
2
a characterization of the Macdonald correspondence for the irreducible cuspidal representations.

2.
Construction of the Macdonald correspondence

2.1.
The set
\mathfrak{P}({\mathfrak{X}})^{\Gamma}

Let
p
be a fixed prime number and let
k={\mathbb{F}_{q}}
be a finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a power of
p
.
Let
{\overline{k}}
be an algebraic closure of
k
, and let
\mathrm{Frob}\colon x\mapsto x^{q}
denote the Frobenius automorphism of
{\overline{k}}
over
k
.
For each integer
n\geq 1
, the set
k_{n}
of fixed points of
\mathrm{Frob}^{n}
in
{\overline{k}}
is the unique extension of
k
of degree
n
contained in
{\overline{k}}
.
We denote by
\Gamma_{n}
the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(k_{n}/k)
and by
\Gamma
the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}({\overline{k}}/k)
. We have
\Gamma\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}:=\displaystyle\lim_{\longleftarrow}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
{\overline{k}}^{\times}
be the multiplicative group of
{\overline{k}}
, and
k_{n}^{\times}
the multiplicative group of
k_{n}
, so that
k_{n}^{\times}=({\overline{k}}^{\times})^{\mathrm{Frob}^{n}}
.
If
m
divides
n
, the norm map
{\mathrm{N}}_{n,m}\colon k_{n}^{\times}\to k_{m}^{\times}
, defined by

(2.1.1)

{\mathrm{N}}_{n,m}(x):=x^{(q^{n}-1)/(q^{m}-1)}=\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\mathrm{Frob}^{mi}x,

where
d:=n/m
, is a surjective homomorphism. The groups
k_{n}^{\times}
and the homomorphisms
{\mathrm{N}}_{n,m}
form an inverse system.

Their inverse limit
\displaystyle\lim_{\longleftarrow}k_{n}^{\times}
is a profinite group. The character group of
{\mathfrak{X}}
of the latter is therefore a discrete group. Whenever
m
divides
n
, the character group
{\mathfrak{X}}_{m}
of
k^{\times}_{m}
is embedded in the character group
{\mathfrak{X}}_{n}
of
k^{\times}_{n}
by the transpose of the norm homomorphism
{\mathrm{N}}_{n,m}
,
and we have

(2.1.2)

{\mathfrak{X}}=\lim_{\longrightarrow}{\mathfrak{X}}_{n}.

Every element in
{\mathfrak{X}}
has finite order, and the group
{\mathfrak{X}}
is (non-canonically) isomorphic to
{\overline{k}}^{\times}
.

A character of
k_{n}^{\times}
is said to be
regular
if it is fixed by no nontrivial element of
\Gamma_{n}
. We denote by
{\mathfrak{X}}_{n}^{\mathrm{reg}}
the set of regular characters of
k_{n}^{\times}
.
Thus, we have
\theta\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{n}
if and only if the cardinality of the set
\left\{\theta,\theta^{q},\ldots,\theta^{q^{n-1}}\right\}
is
n
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}(n)
denote the set of partitions of
n
, and
\mathfrak{P}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathfrak{P}(n)
. For a partition
\lambda\in\mathfrak{P}
, we have
\lambda\in\mathfrak{P}(n)
for some integer
n
; we define
|\lambda|:=n
. We consider the set

(2.1.3)

\mathfrak{P}({\mathfrak{X}}):=\left\{\underline{\lambda}\colon{\mathfrak{X}}\to\mathfrak{P}\,:\,\theta\mapsto\lambda_{\theta}:=\underline{\lambda}(\theta)\right\}

of partition-valued functions
\underline{\lambda}
on
{\mathfrak{X}}
such that
\underline{\lambda}(\theta)=(0)
except for finitely many
\theta\in{\mathfrak{X}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{P}_{n}({\mathfrak{X}})
denote the set of partition-valued functions
\underline{\lambda}\colon{\mathfrak{X}}\to\mathfrak{P}
such that
\sum_{\theta\in{\mathfrak{X}}}|\lambda_{\theta}|=n
.

The group
\Gamma
acts naturally on
P({\mathfrak{X}})
.
Let
\mathfrak{P}({\mathfrak{X}})^{\Gamma}
be the subset consisting of all
\Gamma
-invariant functions on
\mathfrak{P}({\mathfrak{X}})
. Since
\underline{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{P}({\mathfrak{X}})^{\Gamma}
is constant on any Galois orbit
[\theta]:=\Gamma\cdot\theta
(for
\theta\in{\mathfrak{X}}
), we may view the elements of

\mathfrak{P}({\mathfrak{X}})^{\Gamma}
as partition-valued functions defined on the set of Galois orbits
\Gamma\backslash{\mathfrak{X}}
. We write
\mathfrak{P}_{n}({\mathfrak{X}})^{\Gamma}:=\mathfrak{P}({\mathfrak{X}})^{\Gamma}\cap\mathfrak{P}_{n}({\mathfrak{X}})
.

2.2.
Weil-Deligne representations

Let
{\mathrm{WD}}_{F}:=W_{F}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
be the Weil-Deligne group of
F
. The semidirect product is defined via the action

(2.2.1)

wxw^{-1}=|\!|w|\!|x,\quad\text{for $w\in W_{F}$ and $x\in\mathbb{C}$,}

where
|\!|w|\!|
is the norm of
w\in W_{F}
. Let
I_{F}
and
P_{F}
denote the inertia and the wild inertia subgroups of
W_{F}
respectively. These subgroups are normal in
{\mathrm{WD}}_{F}
.
We denote by
W_{F}^{\operatorname{t}}:=W_{F}/P_{F}
and
I_{F}^{\operatorname{t}}:=I_{F}/P_{F}
the
tame Weil group
and the
tame inertia subgroup
of
F
, respectively. We have

(2.2.2)

I_{F}^{\operatorname{t}}\cong\lim_{\longleftarrow}k_{n}^{\times}.

It follows that the character group of
I_{F}^{\operatorname{t}}
is canonically isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{X}}
.

A
Weil-Deligne
representation of
W_{F}
is a pair
(\rho,N)
, where
\rho
is a finite-dimensional representation of
W_{F}
acting on a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V_{\rho}
, such that
\ker\rho
contains an open subgroup of
I_{F}
and
\rho(w)
is semisimple for any
w\in W_{F}
, and
N
is a nilpotent endomorphism of
V_{\rho}
satisfying

(2.2.3)

\rho(w)N\rho(w)^{-1}=|\!|w|\!|N\quad\text{for any $w\in W_{F}$.}

The representation
(\rho,N)
is said to be:

•

unramified
if
I_{F}\subset\ker\rho
,

•

tamely ramified
if
P_{F}\subset\ker(\rho)
.

The irreducible Weil-Deligne representations
(\rho,N)
are those such that
\rho
is an irreducible representation of
W_{F}
and
N=0
. We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}(W_{F}^{\operatorname{t}}
the set of irreducible tamely ramified representations of
W_{F}
.

For every integer
m\geq 1
, the
special representation
of dimension
m
is the representation
{\mathrm{sp}}_{F}(m):=(\rho_{m},N_{m})
, where

(2.2.4)

\rho_{m}(w):=\operatorname{diag}(|\!|w|\!|^{(1-m)/2},|\!|w|\!|^{(3-m)/2},\ldots,|\!|w|\!|^{(m-3)/2},|\!|w|\!|^{(m-1)/2}),

and
N_{m}
is the nilpotent
m\times m
-matrix with
1
s on the subdiagonal and
0
s elsewhere. The pair
(\rho_{m},N_{m})
clearly satisfy (
2.2.3
).

The indecomposable Weil-Deligne representations are those of the form

(2.2.5)

{\mathrm{sp}}_{F}(m)\otimes\rho=(\operatorname{diag}(N_{m},\ldots,N_{m}),\rho_{m}\otimes\rho),

where
m\geq 1
and
\rho
is an irreducible representation of
W_{F}
.

If
\lambda=(n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0)
is a nonzero partition of
n
, we set

(2.2.6)

{\mathrm{sp}}_{F}(\lambda):={\mathrm{sp}}_{F}(n_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathrm{sp}}_{F}(n_{r}).

Definition 2.2.7
.

[
Mac2
, §3]

Two Weil-Deligne representations are
inertially equivalent
if their restriction to
I_{F}\times\mathbb{C}\subset{\mathrm{WD}}_{F}
are equivalent.

Let
\operatorname{Irr}(W_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F}}
denote the set of inertial equivalence classes of rreducible tamely ramified representations of
W_{F}
.
If
\theta\in{\mathfrak{X}}
, let
\rho(\theta)
be an irreducible representation of
W_{F}^{\operatorname{t}}
such that
\theta
occurs in the restriction of
\rho(\theta)
to
I_{F}
. We denote by
\rho(\theta)_{I_{F}}
the inertial class of
\rho(\theta)
. The we have a bijective map

(2.2.8)

\displaystyle\Gamma\backslash{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\operatorname{Irr}(W_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F}}

(2.2.9)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\rho(\theta)_{I_{F}}.

Let
\mathfrak{R}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F}}
denote the set of inertial equivalence classes of tamely ramified representations of
{\mathrm{WD}}_{F}
, and
let
\mathfrak{R}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n}
be the subset of inertial equivalence classes of
n
-dimensional tamely ramified representations of
{\mathrm{WD}}_{F}
.
From (
) we obtain a bijection

(2.2.10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}({\mathfrak{X}})^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F}}

(2.2.11)

\displaystyle\underline{\lambda}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{[\theta]\in\Gamma\backslash{\mathfrak{X}}}{\mathrm{sp}}_{F}(\lambda_{\theta})\otimes\rho(\theta)_{I_{F}},

which restricts to a bijection

(2.2.12)

\mathfrak{P}_{n}({\mathfrak{X}})^{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n}.

2.3.
Representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field with residue field
k
. We denote by
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
the category of representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
with complex coefficients,
and by
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
the category of
smooth
(i.e., such that the stabilizer of any vector is open) representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
with complex coefficients.
Let
\mathbb{F}
be either
k
or
F
. We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}))
the irreducible objects in
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}))
.

2.3.1.
The Harish-Chandra theory

Given a sequence of positive integers
n_{1}
,
\ldots
,
n_{r}
such that
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
, we define
P(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
to be the parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
with Levi decomposition

(2.3.1)

P(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})=L(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\rtimes U(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}),

where

(2.3.2)

L(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}):=\left\{\operatorname{diag}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{r})\,:\,g_{i}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(k),\text{for $1\leq i\leq r$}\right\},

(2.3.3)

U(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}):=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\mathrm{I}_{n_{1}}&\ast&\ast&\ast\cr&\mathrm{I}_{n_{2}}&\ast&\ast\cr&&\ddots&\ast\cr&&&\mathrm{I}_{n_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)\right\}.

We set
U_{n}:=U(1,\ldots,1)
.

Given representations
\pi_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\pi_{r}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{F})
,
\ldots
,
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{r}}(\mathbb{F})
, respectively, we write
\sigma:=\pi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\pi_{r}
, and denote by
\widetilde{\sigma}
its inflation to
P
. We define
the parabolically induced representation

(2.3.4)

\mathrm{I}(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{r}):=\operatorname{Ind}_{P(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})}(\widetilde{\sigma}).

We write

(2.3.5)

\pi_{1}\odot\cdots\odot\pi_{r}:=\mathrm{I}(\pi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\pi_{h}).

The operation
\odot
is commutative and associative.

A representation in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}))
is said to be
cuspidal
if it is not isomorphic to a quotient of any parabolically induced representation from a proper Levi subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
.
We denote by
\mathfrak{B}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
the set of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
-conjugacy classes of pairs
(L,\sigma)
, where
L
is a Levi subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
and
\sigma
an irreducible cuspidal representation of
L
.
The category
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
decomposes as a direct product of full subcategories as follows:

(2.3.6)

\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))=\prod_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))}\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)),

where a representation
\pi
is an object of
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
if and only if every irreducible subquotient of
\pi
occurs in
R_{\mathbf{L}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(\sigma^{\prime})
, with
\mathbf{L}^{\prime}
is a
k
-rational Levi subgroup of a
k
-rational parabolic subgroup of

\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\overline{k}})
and
\sigma^{\prime}
an irreducible cuspidal representation of
L^{\prime}:=\mathbf{L}^{\prime}(k)
such that
(L^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{t}
.

In particular, we obtain the following partition of the set
\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))

(2.3.7)

\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))=\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))}\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)),

where
\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
is the set of irreducible objects of
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
.

Let
\mathfrak{t}=(L,\sigma)\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
. Let
\mathbf{L}
be the
k
-rational Levi subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\overline{k}})
with
L
as its group of
k
-rational points. We define

(2.3.8)

\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k),\sigma):=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}(R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(\sigma)),

where
R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(\sigma)
denote the parabolically induced representation of
\sigma
. The parabolic (or Harish-Chandra) induction is a special case of the Lusztig induction
R_{\mathbf{M}}^{\mathbf{G}}
, where
\mathbf{M}
is
k
-rational Levi subgroup of an arbitrary (that is, non necessarily
k
-rational) parabolic subgroup of a reductive
connected algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
defined over
k
.

There is an equivalence of categories

(2.3.9)

\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))\,\longrightarrow\,\operatorname{Mod}-\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k),\sigma).

The algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k),\sigma)
is an Hecke-Iwahori algebra with Weyl group

(2.3.10)

W(L,\sigma):={\mathrm{N}}_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}(L,\sigma)/L

(see for instance
[
Lu
, Chap. 8]
).
By (
), we have a bijection between the simple modules of
\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k),\sigma)
and the set
\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
of all the irreducible objects of
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
, and hence a bijection between isomorphism classes of irreducble representations of
W(L,\sigma)
and
\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))

(2.3.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Irr}(W(L,\sigma))

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))

(2.3.12)

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\pi_{E}.

The Weyl group
{\mathrm{N}}_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}(L,\sigma)/L
can be described as follows.
We first consider the case where
L\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{m}(k)^{\times e}
with
me=n
(that is,
L
is
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
-conjugate to
L(m,\ldots,m)
) and
\sigma=\sigma_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{0}=:\sigma_{0}^{\otimes e}
,
where
\sigma_{0}
is an irreducible cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(k)
.
Then
W(L,\sigma)
is the symmetric group
S_{e}
.

In the general case, we have

(2.3.13)

L\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{m_{1}}(k)^{\times e_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}_{m_{h}}(k)^{\times e_{h}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma=\sigma_{1}^{\otimes e_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{h}^{\otimes e_{h}},

where
\sigma_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{m_{i}}(k))
is cuspidal for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
and the representations
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{j}
are not isomorphic for any
i\neq j
.
We have

(2.3.14)

W(L,\sigma)=S_{e_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{e_{h}}.

The isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of
W(L,\sigma)
are hence parametrized by
h
-tuples
(\lambda_{e_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{e_{h}})
,
where
\lambda_{e_{i}}
is a partition of
e_{i}
, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
. We will denote by
E(\lambda_{e_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{e_{h}})\in\operatorname{Irr}(W(L,\sigma))

the representation parametrized by
(\lambda_{e_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{e_{h}})
.

2.3.2.
Parametrization of the representations

Let
\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
denote the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible cuspidal representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
.
Green’s results
[
Gr
, p.431]
show the existence of a bijection between
\Gamma
-orbits
[\theta]
of regular characters
\theta
of

k_{n}^{\times}
(i.e.,
\Gamma
-orbits of degree
n
) and isomorphism classes of irreducible cuspidal representations
\pi_{[\theta]}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
, unique up to isomorphism, such that

(2.3.15)

\mathrm{tr}(\pi_{[\theta]})=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}}\theta({}^{\gamma}x),

for any
x\in k_{n}^{\times}
of degree
n
over
k
, where
k_{n}^{\times}
is considered as a maximal torus in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\overline{k}})
.
The representation
\pi_{[\theta]}
is up to a sign a Deligne-Lusztig character
R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\theta)}
, where
\mathbf{T}
is an elliptic maximal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\overline{k}})
that is isomorphic to
k_{n}^{\times}
.

Let
L
be a Levi subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
and
\sigma
an irreducible cuspidal representation of
L
. We keep the notation of (
2.3.13
).
By applying the above bijection to the irreducible cuspidal representations
\sigma_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\sigma_{h}
, we have for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
,

(2.3.16)

\sigma_{i}=\pi_{[\theta_{i}]}\quad\text{where $\theta_{i}$ is a regular character of $k_{m_{i}}^{\times}$}.

By combining (
2.3.16
) with the bijection (
2.3.11
), we attach to each
(\theta,\lambda)
, where
\theta:=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{h})\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{m_{1}}^{\mathrm{reg}}\times\cdots\times{\mathfrak{X}}_{m_{h}}^{\mathrm{reg}}
and
\lambda:=(\lambda_{e_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{e_{h}})
, with
\lambda_{i}
a partition of
e_{i}
, and

e_{1}m_{1}+\cdots+e_{h}m_{h}=n
, the representation

(2.3.17)

\pi_{\theta,\lambda}:=\pi_{E(\lambda_{e_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{e_{h}})}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)),

where
\mathfrak{t}=(L,\sigma)

L\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{m_{1}}(k)^{\times e_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}_{m_{h}}(k)^{\times e_{h}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma=\pi_{[\theta_{1}]}^{e_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\pi_{[\theta_{h}]}^{e_{h}}.

2.3.3.
The Lusztig classification

We keep the notation of the previous sections. In particuler,
\mathbf{L}
is a
k
-rational Levi subgroup of a
k
-rational parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\overline{k}})
.
We recall that, by
[

, Corollary 7.7]
, for every irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
, there exists a
k
-rational maximal torus
\mathbf{T}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\overline{k}})
and a character
\theta
of
T:=\mathbf{T}(k)
such that the character of
\pi
(still denoted by
\pi

(2.3.19)

\langle\pi,R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(\theta)\rangle_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}\neq 0,

where
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}
is the usual scalar
product on the space of class functions on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)

(2.3.20)

\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}=|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)|^{-1}\sum_{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}f_{1}(g)\,\overline{f_{2}(g)}.

If
\theta=1
(i.e. the trivial character of
T
), then the representation
\pi
is called

The
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
-conjugacy classes of pairs
(\mathbf{T},\theta)
as above are in one-to-one correspondence with the

-conjugacy classes of pairs
(\mathbf{T}^{\vee},s)
, where
s
is a semisimple element of
L
,
and
\mathbf{T}
is a

-rational maximal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\overline{k}})
containing

(for a general reductive algebraic group

defined over

\mathbf{T}^{\ast}
of the group
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
, where
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
is the group defined over
k
, with root datum dual to that of
\mathbf{G}
; for
\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, we have
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}=\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\ast}=\mathbf{T}
).
Then the
Lusztig series

\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{GL}_{n},s)

such that
\pi
occurs in
R_{\mathbf{T}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}(\theta),
where
(\mathbf{T},\theta)_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}
corresponds to
(\mathbf{T},s)_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)}
.
By definition,
\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{GL}_{n},1)
consists only of unipotent representations.

By
[
DL
, §10]
and
[

, (8.4.4)]

\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
decomposes into a disjoint union:

\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))=\bigsqcup_{(s)}\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{GL}_{n},s),

where
(s)
is the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
-conjugacy class of a semisimple element

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)

.

We have

(2.4.2)

\omega_{\pi}=\det(\mathcal{M}_{n}(\pi)),\quad\text{for every $\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)$,}

where
\omega_{\pi}

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}\colon\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))\overset{1-1}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n}

2.5.
Definition and preservation of the
\epsilon
-factors

Let
\psi\colon k\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a nontrivial additive character.
Let
U_{n}
denote the upper triangular unipotent subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
. We define a character
\psi_{n}\colon U_{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by

(2.5.1)

\psi_{n}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&a_{1}&\ast&\ldots&\ast\cr&1&a_{2}&\ldots&\ast\cr&&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\cr&&&1&a_{n-1}\cr&&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right):=\psi(a_{1}+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}).

A finite dimensional representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
is said to be
generic
if

(2.5.2)

\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{n}}(\pi|_{U_{n}},\psi_{n})\neq 0.

The representation
\pi
is generic if and only if there exists a nonzero vector
v\in V_{\pi}
such that
\pi(u)v=\psi_{n}(v)
for every
u\in U_{n}
. Such a vector
v
is called a
Whittaker vector
(with respect to
\psi_{n}
).
We say that
\pi
is
of Whittaker type
if
\pi
is generic and the subspace spanned by its Whittaker vectors is one-dimensional.

Let
V
be
k
-vector space of dimension
n
. We set
A:=\operatorname{End}_{k}(V)
and let
\mathcal{C}(A)
be the space of complex valued functions on
A
.
Following
[
Sp
]
or
[
Mac1
]
, we introduce the notion of the Fourier transform and zeta function of complex representations of
G:=\mathrm{GL}(V)
as follows. For
\phi\in\mathcal{C}(A)
, the Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}
is defined by

(2.5.3)

\widehat{\phi}(a):=q^{-n^{2}/2}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in A}\phi(a^{\prime})\,\operatorname{Tr}(aa^{\prime}).

We have
\widehat{\widehat{\phi}}(a)=\phi(a)
for every
a\in A
.

For a finite dimensional complex representation
\pi
of
G
, and for
\phi\in\mathcal{C}(A)
, we define the zeta function
Z(\pi,\psi)
by

(2.5.4)

Z(\pi,\psi):=\sum_{g\in G}\phi(g)\pi(g).

For every
a\in A
, we set

(2.5.5)

W(\pi,\psi;a):=q^{-n^{2}/2}\sum_{g\in G}\operatorname{Tr}(\pi(ga))\pi(a).

We have

(2.5.6)

Z(\pi,\psi)=\sum_{a\in A}\widehat{\phi}(-a)\cdot W(\pi,\psi;a).

For
g^{\prime}\in G
, we have

W(\pi,\psi;g^{\prime}a)=W(\pi,\psi;a)\pi(g^{\prime})^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad W(\pi,\psi;g^{\prime}a)=\pi(g^{\prime})^{-1}W(\pi,\psi;a).

It implies that
\pi(g^{\prime})
commutes with
W(\pi,\psi;1)
. Hence, if
\pi
is irreducible, there exists
w(\pi,\psi)\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(2.5.7)

W(\pi,\psi;1)=w(\pi,\psi)\,\pi(1).

The
\epsilon
-factor
\epsilon(\pi,\psi)
is defined to be

(2.5.8)

\epsilon(\pi,\psi):=w(\pi^{\vee},\psi),

where
\pi^{\vee}
is the contragredient representation of
\pi
.

Let
\Psi
be nontrivial additive character of
F
. We suppose that
\mathfrak{P}_{F}\subset\ker\Psi
and
\mathfrak{o}_{F}\not\subset\ker\Psi
. By restriction to
\mathfrak{o}_{F}
and reduction to
\mathfrak{p}_{F}
, the character
\Psi
induces an nontrivial
additive character
\psi
of
k
. We fix the Haar measure on
F
normalized such that
\mathfrak{p}_{F}
has volume
q^{-1/2}
.

Deligne has attached in
[
Del
, (5.1) and §8.12]
to every Weil-Deligne representation
\varphi:=(\rho,N)
of
{\mathrm{WD}}_{F}
a nonzero constant
\epsilon_{0}(\varphi,\Psi)
. When
\varphi
is tamely ramified, this constant
is computed in
[
Del
, §5.16]
, and Macdonald has proved in
[
Mac2
, §3]
that it depends only on the restriction of
\rho
to
I_{F}
. Moreover, Macdonald has established in
[
Mac2
, §3]
that the following equality holds

(2.5.9)

\epsilon(\pi,\psi)=\epsilon_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{n}(\pi),\Psi),\quad\text{for every $\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))$.}

3.
Gamma factors for pairs

Let
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
be positive integers, and let
(\pi_{i},V_{\pi_{i}})\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}{n_{i}}(k))
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Let
\mathrm{sw}_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}\colon V_{\pi_{1}}\otimes V_{\pi_{2}}\to V_{\pi_{2}}\otimes V_{\pi_{1}}
denote a linear map defined on pure tensors by

(3.0.1)

\mathrm{sw}_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}):=v_{2}\otimes v_{1},\quad\text{for all $v_{1}\in V_{\pi_{1}}$ and $v_{2}\in V_{\pi_{2}}$.}

For a function
f\colon\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(k)\to V_{\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}}
, we denote by
\overline{f}\colon\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(k)\to V_{\pi_{2}\times\pi_{1}}
the function defined by

\overline{f}(g):=\mathrm{sw}_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}(f(g)),\quad\text{for all $g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(k)$.}

We write
\widehat{w}_{n_{1},n_{2}}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\mathrm{I}_{n_{2}}\cr\mathrm{I}_{n_{1}}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

We consider the intertwining operator
\mathcal{A}_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}\colon\mathrm{I}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\to\mathrm{I}(\pi_{2},\pi_{1})
defined by

(3.0.2)

\mathcal{A}_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}(f)\colon g\mapsto\sum_{u\in U(n_{1},n_{2})}\overline{f}(\widehat{w}_{n_{1},n_{2}}ug).

Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be representations of Whitaker type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}(k)
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{2}}(k)
, respectively.
By
[
SiZi1
, Theorem 5.5]
the parabolically induced representation
\mathrm{I}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
is also of Whitaker type.

Let
v_{\pi_{i},\psi}
be a nonzero Whittaker vector for
\pi_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.
We set
n_{1,2}:=n_{1}+n_{2}
and

v_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\psi}:=\begin{cases}\psi_{n_{1,2}}(u)(\widetilde{\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}})(p)\,v_{\pi_{1},\psi}\otimes v_{\pi_{2},\psi}&\text{if $g=p\widehat{w}_{n_{1},n_{2}}u$, $p\in P_{n_{1},n_{2}}$, $u\in U_{n_{1,2}}$}\cr 0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then,
v_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\psi}
is a non-zero Whittaker vector of
\mathrm{I}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
.

Following
[
SoZe
, Definition 3.1]
, we define the
Shahidi gamma factor
of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
with respect to
\psi
to be the unique complex number
\overline{\gamma}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\psi)
satisfying

(3.0.3)

\mathcal{A}_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}}(v_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\psi})=\overline{\gamma}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\psi)\cdot v_{\pi_{2},\pi_{1},\psi}.

By
[
SoZe
, §4.A]
, the
normalized Shahidi gamma factor
is

(3.0.4)

\gamma^{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\psi):=q^{-n_{1}n_{2}/2}\cdot\overline{\gamma}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\psi).

The relation of normalized Shahidi gamma factors and Jacquet-Piatetski-Shapiro-Shalika factors is the following (see
[
SoZe
, Corollary 3.15]
):

(3.0.5)

\gamma^{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\psi)=q^{(n_{1}-n_{2}-1)n_{2}}\cdot\omega_{\pi_{2}}(-1)\cdot\gamma(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}^{\vee},\psi)

in either of the following cases

(1)

n_{1}>n_{2}
,

(2)

n_{1}=n_{2}
and
\pi_{2}
is cuspidal.

Definition 3.0.6
.

If
\sigma_{1}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}(k))
and
\sigma_{2}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n_{2}}(k))
are cuspidal, we define the
\epsilon
-factor for pairs
\epsilon(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2},\psi)
by

(3.0.7)

\epsilon(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2},\psi):=\omega_{\sigma_{2}}(-1)^{n_{2}}\cdot\gamma^{\mathrm{Sh}}(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}^{\vee},\psi).

4.2.
Preservation of the
\epsilon
-factors for pairs

In
[
YZ
]
, Ye and Zelingher defined epsilon factors for pairs for irreducible representations of finite general linear groups using the Macdonald correspondence. Following
[
YZ
, §3]
, for
\pi_{1}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}(k))

and
\pi_{2}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n_{2}}(k))
we set

(4.2.1)

\epsilon_{0}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},\psi):=\epsilon_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{n_{1}}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mathcal{M}_{n_{2}}(\pi_{2}),\psi).

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
.
Let
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
be irreducible cuspidal representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}(k)
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{2}}(k)
, respectively.
Then the following equality holds

\epsilon(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2},\psi)=\epsilon_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{n_{1}}(\sigma_{1})\otimes\mathcal{M}_{n_{2}}(\sigma_{2}),\psi).

Proof.

By combining (
3.0.5
) with
[
YZ
, Theorem 4.4]
(for the case
n_{1}>n_{2}
) and
[
Z
, Theorem 2.18]
(for the case
n_{1}=n_{2}
), we obtain

\gamma^{\mathrm{Sh}}(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}^{\vee},\psi)=\omega_{\sigma_{2}}(-1)^{n_{2}}\cdot\epsilon_{0}(\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2},\psi).

The result follows.
∎

5.
Characterization of the Macdonald correspondence

We define

(5.0.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))

(5.0.2)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\pi_{\rho}

to be the inverse map of
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

We recall the following result, that was was obtained by Nien in 2014:

Converse Theorem
.

[
Nie
, Theorem 3.9]

Let
n
be an integer
\geq 2
, and let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be two irreducible cuspidal representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
with the same central character, such that,
for every integer
m
such that
1\leq m\leq n/2
, and every irreducible generic representation
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(k)
, we have the equality

(5.0.3)

\gamma(\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi).

Then the representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are equivalent.

Corollary 5.0.4
.

Let
n
be an integer such
n\geq 2
, and let
\pi
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
and
\rho
be an irreducible
n
-dimensional representation of
W_{F}
such that
\pi_{\rho}
and
\pi
have same central character and

\epsilon(\pi\times\pi_{\rho^{\prime}},\psi)=\epsilon(\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime},\psi),

for any integer
m
such that
1\leq m\leq n/2
and every irreducible
n
-dimensional representation
\rho^{\prime}
of
W_{F}
.

Then the representation
\pi
is equivalent to
\pi_{\rho}
.

Proof.

It follows from the above Converse Theorem by using Definition
3.0.6
and (
3.0.5
).
∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\mathcal{T}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a collection of bijections

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}\colon

\displaystyle\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))

\displaystyle\rho\mapsto\tau_{\rho}

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

for every integer
n\geq 1
, and every
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n}
,

\omega_{\tau_{\rho}}=\det(\rho),

(2)

for all integers
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1
, and every
\rho_{1}\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n_{1}}
and every
\rho_{2}\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n_{2}}
,

\epsilon(\tau_{\rho_{1}}\times\tau_{\rho_{2}},\psi)=\epsilon(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\psi).

Then
\mathcal{T}_{n}=\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

Proof.

By (
2.4.2
), the collection of bijections
((\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{c}})^{-1})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the property (1). As above, we write
\pi_{\rho}:=\mathcal{M}_{n}^{-1}(\rho)
.
Thus, we have, for every integer
n\geq 1
, and every every
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n}
,

\omega_{\pi_{\rho}}=\det(\rho).

It follows that the representations
\pi_{\rho}
and
\tau_{\rho}
have the same central characters:

(5.0.5)

\omega_{\pi_{\rho}}=\omega_{\tau_{\rho}}.

By Theorem
1
, the collection of bijections
(\mathcal{M}_{n}^{-1})_{n\geq 1}
satisfy the property (2). Thus, we have, for all integers
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1
,
and every
\rho_{1}\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n_{1}}
and every
\rho_{2}\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n_{2}}
,

\epsilon(\pi_{\rho_{1}}\times\pi_{\rho_{2}},\psi)=\epsilon(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\psi).

Hence, we get

(5.0.6)

\epsilon(\pi_{\rho_{1}}\times\pi_{\rho_{2}},\psi)=\epsilon(\tau_{\rho_{1}}\times\tau_{\rho_{2}},\psi).

Equations (
5.0.5
) and (
5.0.6
) allow us to apply Corollary
5.0.4
. We obtain that

\tau_{\rho}=\pi_{\rho},\quad\text{for every integer $n\geq 1$, and every $\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}({\mathrm{WD}}_{F}^{\operatorname{t}})_{I_{F},n}$.}

∎

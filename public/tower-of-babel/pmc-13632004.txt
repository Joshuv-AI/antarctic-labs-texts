Conserved signaling pathways and transcriptional regulators drive ovarian aging across species
Authors: Mingyue Gao, Junnan Zhang, Jing Yang, Shenghan Gao, Ning Yang, Congjiao Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The ovary plays a pivotal role in female fertility and endocrine homeostasis, and it is among the earliest organs to exhibit age-related dysfunction. However, the conserved molecular mechanisms underlying ovarian functional decline across species remain poorly understood. We integrated single-cell transcriptomic data from chicken, human, mouse, and yak ovaries to construct a cross-species ovarian single-cell atlas. Through cell–cell communication and transcription factor regulatory network analyses, we identified conserved signaling pathways (CXCL, VEGF, and VISFATIN) and conserved transcription factors (TFs), namely Klf6, Mef2a, Tead1, and Elk1. We further revealed that five upstream TFs (Rbpj, Myc, Sp1, Stat3, and Max) potentially regulate these conserved pathways. By analyzing multi-time-point single-cell data from mouse and human ovaries, we identified 30 key genes with consistent expression changes during ovarian functional decline. Notably, the time-varying TFs Stat3 and Max were shown to regulate the VEGF and VISFATIN pathways via Vegfa and Insr, respectively. Using DF-1 and NIH/3T3 senescence models, we found conserved stress-responsive expression patterns for Rbpj, Myc, Sp1, and Stat3, whereas Max showed differential responses between DF-1 and NIH/3T3 cells. Collectively, this cross-species single-cell transcriptomic study provides insights into conserved molecular mechanisms driving ovarian functional decline and offers potential therapeutic targets for ovary-related diseases.

Introduction

The ovary is essential for female fertility and endocrine homeostasis. In laying hens, the ovary is also the organ that directly determines egg production, and the age-related decline of ovarian function is a major cause of reduced egg output and eggshell quality during the late laying period (Zhang et al., 2026a). Studies have shown that the ovary is one of the earliest tissues to exhibit signs of aging in the female body (Liu et al., 2025). Unlike mammals, the avian ovary is characterized by a unique follicular hierarchy in which follicles develop and ovulate in a sequential, orderly manner (Scanes, 2022). Female fertility declines from approximately age 30, with an accelerated decline after age 40 (Menken et al., 1986). Similarly, reproductive efficiency has also been reported to decline markedly with advancing age in mouse, chicken, and yak (Zi, 2003; Balough et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2026). Age-related ovarian functional decline often leads to endocrine disturbances, resulting in severe consequences (Vollenhoven and Hunt, 2018). These disturbances ultimately lead to menopause and an increased risk of cardiovascular, neurodegenerative, and other comorbidities. (Perry et al., 2015).

Studies on the mechanisms of ovarian aging have shown that with increasing age, the antioxidant defense capacity within the ovary gradually declines, leading to the accumulation of DNA damage and telomere shortening in oocytes (Yan et al., 2022). Further research indicates that oxidative stress in oocytes is coupled with a decline in mitochondrial quantity and quality with increasing maternal age (Lu et al., 2022). Mitochondrial morphology in the aging ovary is also markedly altered, with an increased prevalence of swollen and vacuolated mitochondria (Murakoshi et al., 2013; Feng et al., 2021; Czajkowska and Ajduk, 2023). These phenomena suggest that age-related pro-inflammatory processes may be activated in conjunction with ovarian oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction (Ansere et al., 2021; Isola et al., 2024). Aging also deteriorates the ovarian microenvironment, particularly through granulosa cell dysfunction, thereby impairing follicular development and accelerating fertility decline (Ahmed et al., 2020). In the chicken ovary, such somatic cell dysfunction is closely associated with follicular atresia, a degenerative process governed by a complex regulatory network at the organic, cellular, and molecular levels (Ru et al., 2024). Concurrently, elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels and diminished antioxidant defenses synergistically drive ovarian dysfunction and infertility (King et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2024). A hallmark of ovarian aging is the marked reduction in both the quantity and quality of oocytes (Broekmans et al., 2009; Ahmed et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2020). Therefore, elucidating the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying ovarian aging holds substantial scientific and clinical significance.

Advancements in single-cell transcriptomics have provided a novel perspective on elucidating ovarian aging. Specifically, through single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), researchers have been able to dissect the highly heterogeneous cell populations within ovarian tissue and their transcriptional variations at single-cell resolution (Baryawno et al., 2019; Ben Yaakov et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2023). Evolutionarily conserved cell types and their associated core regulatory programs are thought to underpin mechanisms that are essential for the maintenance of fundamental biological functions (Shafer et al., 2022). In ovarian biology, such conservation is thought to be reflected in shared cell-type identities and the expression of orthologous genes across species (Zhao et al., 2025). Recently, several studies have employed evolutionarily distant vertebrate models to investigate the mechanisms underlying ovarian aging (Colella et al., 2023), providing important insights into the molecular evolution of ovarian cell types in vertebrates (Xue et al., 2026). Because ovarian aging is influenced by species-specific reproductive strategies, lifespan, and physiological adaptations, findings derived from a single species may not fully capture the fundamental mechanisms underlying ovarian functional decline. Cross-species comparative analysis enables the identification of evolutionarily conserved molecular programs that are more likely to represent core regulatory mechanisms shared across vertebrates.

In the present study, we focus on the chicken ovary as the representative avian model and use cross-species comparison to identify conserved mechanisms of ovarian aging. Through cell–cell communication analysis and TF regulatory network analysis, we identified evolutionarily conserved signaling pathways and key TFs. Furthermore, by incorporating longitudinal scRNA-seq data from human and mouse, we identified a core set of genes exhibiting conserved expression trajectories during ovarian functional decline. Collectively, this study provides a comparative single-cell framework for understanding the conserved mechanisms underlying ovarian aging, which may ultimately inform strategies to prolong the productive lifespan of laying hens as well as advance our understanding of reproductive aging across vertebrates.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

The chicken dataset was generated by our laboratory (State Key Laboratory of Animal Biotech Breeding, China Agricultural University, Beijing 100193, China) using ovarian tissues collected from 100-week-old Rhode Island Red laying hens, serving as the representative avian model for this study. In addition, scRNA-seq datasets from mouse [GSE241318 (Zhang et al., 2026b)], human [GSE255690 (Wu et al., 2024) and GSE260685 (Jones et al., 2024)], and yak [GSE254280 (Pei et al., 2024)] ovarian tissues were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database and included for cross-species comparative analysis.

Phenotype collection

Egg-production traits were measured in 848 Rhode Island Red laying hens. Eggs were collected on three consecutive days at each corresponding age (36, 56, 72, 80, and 90 weeks), and the following traits were recorded: egg weight (EW), eggshell strength (ESS), eggshell thickness (EST), eggshell color (L*), Haugh unit (HU).

Processing and quality control of scRNA-seq data

The scRNA-seq datasets from different sources were processed using the Seurat package (v5.4.0) in R (v4.3.3) (Hao et al., 2024). Quality control was performed by filtering cells based on the number of detected genes and the proportion of mitochondrial gene expression. Cells with fewer than 300 detected genes were removed. To further exclude low-quality cells, species-specific thresholds were applied to the proportion of mitochondrial gene expression. The threshold was set to 20% for chicken, human, and yak, and 10% for mouse, considering differences in genome annotation completeness and mitochondrial gene representation among species.

Ortholog gene selection

Orthologous gene information across species was retrieved from the Ensembl database using the biomaRt package (v2.58.2) in R (Durinck et al., 2005, 2009). Gene annotations for each species were obtained from the Ensembl Genes database by querying the corresponding species-specific datasets, from which Ensembl gene IDs and their associated gene symbols were extracted. One-to-one orthologous relationships were identified using mouse as the reference species. Based on Ensembl orthology annotations, genes exhibiting a “1-1-1-1” orthologous relationship among chicken, human, mouse, and yak were retained and used for subsequent analyses.

Harmony integration and cell type annotation

Single-cell data were normalized using the “LogNormalize” method implemented in Seurat. Batch effects arising from different data sources were corrected using the Harmony (v1.2.4) algorithm (Korsunsky et al., 2019). Cell type marker gene references for human and mouse ovary tissues were obtained from the CellMarker 2.0 database (http://117.50.127.228/CellMarker/) and used for cell type mapping of the integrated single-cell dataset. The mapping results were further manually curated using highly expressed marker genes to distinguish clusters corresponding to similar cell types. In addition, cell cycle analysis was performed using the CellCycleScoring function to calculate S phase scores (S.Score) and G2/M phase scores (G2M.Score) for each cell, and cells were classified according to their predicted cell cycle phase (Phase). The proportion of cells in the S or G2/M phases within each cell type was calculated and evaluated together with the mean G2M.Score. Cell types showing clear proliferative characteristics were annotated as proliferating.

Pseudotime analysis

Pseudotime trajectory analysis was conducted using Monocle3 (v1.3.7) on four theca cell types characterized by distinct gene expression signatures. A principal trajectory graph was constructed using the learn_graph function, and the theca cell-Acsbg1 was manually defined as the root state for pseudotime ordering using order_cells. Genes varying along the trajectory were identified using the graph_test function based on the principal graph with Moran’s I spatial autocorrelation statistics. Genes with q-value < 0.05 and Moran’s I > 0.35 were considered significantly associated with pseudotime.

Cell-cell communication analysis

Cell-cell communication analysis was performed for each species separately using the CellChat R package (v1.6.1) (Jin et al., 2021). Circular network plots were generated using netVisual_circle to visualize the number and strength of interactions among cell types. Ligand–receptor communication patterns between different cell types were displayed using netVisual_bubble. NetVisual_heatmap was used to illustrate the interaction strengths mediated by different signaling pathways. Furthermore, sender-specific and receiver-specific subnetworks were constructed to characterize signaling patterns of key cell types, and netVisual_circle was applied to visualize the outgoing and incoming signaling interactions, respectively.

Transcription factor activity inference and target gene analysis

TF activity was inferred from single-cell transcriptomic data using the DoRothEA regulatory network database (v1.14.1) and the VIPER algorithm (v1.36.0) (Alvarez et al., 2016; Garcia-Alonso et al., 2019; Badia-I-Mompel et al., 2022; Müller-Dott et al., 2023). Regulatory interactions with confidence levels A, B, and C in DoRothEA were first selected to construct the regulon set. A pseudobulk expression matrix was then generated based on combinations of cell type and species, and VIPER was applied to calculate the activity scores of TFs. To identify cell type-specific TFs, activity differences between the target cell type and other cell types were compared. For each species and cell type combination, the top 30 TFs ranked by activity were first selected, and TFs with an activity difference (Δactivity > 0.8) were further defined as cell type–specific TFs. Target genes of the key TFs were subsequently extracted from the regulon set to investigate their potential biological functions.

Signaling pathway activity analysis using PROGENy

Pathway activity across different age groups and cell types was inferred using the progeny R package (v1.24.0) (Schubert et al., 2018). The inferred pathway activity scores were scaled (scale = TRUE) to obtain a normalized activity matrix representing pathway activities across cell types and age groups. The results were subsequently visualized using pheatmap (v1.0.13) and ggplot2 (v4.0.1).

Functional enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analyses were conducted to characterize the biological functions of gene sets. Gene symbols were converted to ENTREZID identifiers using the bitr function in clusterProfiler (v4.10.1) with the org.Mm.eg.db annotation database (Yu et al., 2012; Wu et al., 2021). Gene Ontology (GO) Biological Process enrichment and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis were performed using enrichGO and enrichKEGG, respectively. P-values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method to control the false discovery rate. The top enriched GO terms and KEGG pathways were visualized using dot plots generated with ggplot2 (v4.0.1).

Gene network analysis using Cytoscape

A protein–protein interaction (PPI) network of the key genes was constructed using the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (STRING) database (https://string-db.org/) (Snel, 2000). The interaction data were exported and subsequently imported into Cytoscape (v3.10.3) for network visualization. Different colors or node attributes were used to represent distinct gene groups (Saito et al., 2012).

Mfuzz analysis

Gene expression dynamics across different age groups were analyzed using the Mfuzz R package (v2.62.0) (Futschik and Carlisle, 2005; Kumar and Futschik, 2007). The LogNormalize-normalized expression matrix was extracted from the Seurat object, and a pseudobulk expression matrix was generated by averaging gene expression across cells within each age group. To ensure data quality, genes with zero total expression or containing non-finite values (NA/Inf) were removed. The fuzzification parameter (m) was automatically estimated using the mestimate function, and the number of clusters was set to k = 6. Fuzzy c-means clustering was then performed using the mfuzz function to identify gene expression patterns. Genes with a membership value ≥ 0.7 in a given cluster were defined as high-confidence members and were retained as representative genes of each cluster.

Cell treatments and SA-β-Gal staining

To simulate cellular microenvironments across different life stages, DF-1 and NIH/3T3 cells were treated with 0.8 mM Vitamin C (Vc), 0.8 mM Vc combined with 80 μmol/L H₂O₂, or 0.8 mM Vc combined with 30 mg/mL D-galactose for 24 hours. Following the treatments, the cells were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and fixed with a senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-Gal) fixative solution for 15 min. After an additional PBS wash, a freshly prepared SA-β-Gal staining solution (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was added to each well. The plates were then incubated at 37°C for 12 h in a CO₂-free environment. Finally, the stained cells were observed and imaged using a light microscope (Olympus IX73, Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan).

RNA extraction and RT-qPCR

DF-1 and NIH/3T3 cells were harvested and lysed directly into TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, #15596026). Total RNA was extracted by chloroform phase separation, isopropanol precipitation, and 75% (v/v) ethanol washing (Chomczynski and Sacchi, 1987). RNA concentration was determined with a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. 1 μg of RNA per sample was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara, #RR047A). Quantitative PCR was performed with Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, #Q712-02). Gene expression was analyzed by the ΔΔCt method with ACTB as the endogenous reference. ΔCt for each sample was calculated as Ct_target - Ct_ACTB. ΔΔCt was then obtained by subtracting the ΔCt of the untreated control group. Relative fold change was computed as 2^(–ΔΔCt), and values were normalized to the untreated control (set as 1). Primer sequences for RT-qPCR are listed in Table S17.

Results

Construction of a cross-species ovarian cellular atlas

To characterize the physiological changes associated with ovarian aging in laying hens, we first profiled egg-production traits across the late laying period (from 36 to 90 weeks of age) in Rhode Island Red hens. EW and eggshell color (L*) increased progressively with advancing age, whereas ESS, EST, and HU declined significantly over the same period (Fig. 1A). The concurrent decrease in eggshell quality traits and albumen quality, together with the continued increase in egg size, indicates a progressive deterioration of reproductive performance during the late laying phase. To construct a cross-species ovarian cellular atlas, we generated scRNA-seq data from chicken ovarian tissue, obtaining a total of 415.6 million reads with 97.0% valid barcodes and 99.9% valid UMIs, and recovering an estimated 11,744 cells after quality filtering. Single-cell transcriptomic data for human, mouse, and yak ovarian tissues were obtained from public databases, and all datasets were subjected to standardized quality control to mitigate the influence of data source heterogeneity. Following standardized quality-control filtering, the chicken ovarian dataset comprised 10,985 cells. Cell numbers in the human and mouse ovarian datasets ranged from 6,846–12,783 and 7,224–8,077 across individual datasets, respectively, while the yak ovarian dataset contained 16,610 cells. Subsequently, a list of one-to-one orthologous genes across the four species was generated using the Ensembl database, after which the single-cell expression matrix was filtered to retain only these cross-species orthologous genes for downstream analyses. Cell types were annotated based on the CellMarker 2.0 database, and a proliferative cell type was identified via cell-cycle analysis, yielding 13 distinct cell types (Fig. 1B, 1C, S1A). Dot-plot visualization of marker gene expression for each cell type across species supported the reliability of the annotations (Fig. S1B). Meanwhile, we visualized the cell type distribution across species using a bar plot of cell proportions (Fig. 1D).

Visualization of the Harmony-integrated space was performed to assess the interrelationships of the ovarian single-cell atlas across species. The results showed that the same cell types from different species were closely clustered (Fig. S1C). Correlation analysis of cell types further revealed that mammalian species (human, mouse, and yak) exhibited greater similarity in cell-type profiles compared to the chicken (Fig. S1D).

Pseudotime analysis of four theca cell subtypes defined by distinct gene expression signatures

To investigate the developmental relationships among the four theca cells defined by their respective signature genes, pseudotime analysis was performed on these four cell subtypes (Fig. 1E). A branch-free point within theca cell-Acsbg1 was manually selected as the starting point, and cells were ordered along a pseudotime trajectory (Fig. 1F). The results showed that, starting from theca cell-Acsbg1, the trajectory first branched into theca cell-Pdgfra and theca cell-Cyp11a1, which subsequently converged toward the theca cell-Arg2 (Fig. 1F). Through filtering criteria (q-value < 0.05, Moran's I > 0.35), 38 key genes exhibiting significant expression changes along the trajectory were identified, after which a subset of these genes was selected to visualize their expression dynamics (Fig. 1G, Table S1). GO enrichment analysis indicated that these genes were involved in processes such as cholesterol metabolism and transcriptional regulation, with some genes associated with the p53 signaling pathway (Fig. S2A, Table S2). KEGG enrichment analysis further revealed that genes related to cytoskeleton organization, PI3K-Akt, JAK-STAT, and PPAR signaling pathways also exhibited expression changes during the differentiation of the four theca cell types (Fig. S2B, Table S3).

Subsequently, genes showing consistent expression changes across three or more species were selected from the 38 genes, resulting in the identification of 12 evolutionarily conserved key genes (Fig. 1H and S2C). GO enrichment network analysis of these 12 genes highlighted their roles in processes such as positive regulation of intrinsic apoptotic signaling pathway by p53 class mediator and regulation of transcription from RNA polymerase II promoter in response to stress (Fig. 1I).

Identification of conserved CXCL, VEGF, and VISFATIN pathways via cross-species cell communication analysis

Through cell–cell communication analysis, this study characterized the intercellular communication landscapes across species. Through the identification of conserved signaling, this study identified the CXCL, VEGF, and VISFATIN signaling pathways, which were present in all four species (Fig. 2D). Studies have demonstrated that CXCL primarily mediates immune cell recruitment (Karin, 2020; Xu et al., 2022a). VEGF is a canonical pro-angiogenic factor that plays an important role in ovarian diseases (Kudsy et al., 2016; Bahramrezaie et al., 2019). VISFATIN is associated with energy metabolism, inflammation, and aging (Park et al., 2020). Visualization of the cell types involved in these conserved signaling pathways further validated their functional characteristics. The results showed that the receiving cell types for CXCL signaling across multiple species were predominantly immune cell and myeloid cell (Fig. 2E). The receiving cell type for the VEGF signaling pathway was primarily endothelial cell (Fig. 2E). Notably, VISFATIN signaling was broadly distributed across multiple cell types in human, exhibiting a ubiquitous pattern. In chicken, mouse, and yak, its signal-receiving cells were theca cell-Proliferating, endothelial cell, and granulosa cell, respectively (Fig. 2E).

Based on the cell–cell communication analysis, we further pinpointed key ligand and receptor genes of the CXCL, VEGF, and VISFATIN signaling pathways, including Cxcl12, Cxcr4, Ackr3, Vegfa, Kdr, Nampt, and Insr. Visualization of the expression patterns of these genes across different cell types revealed that the ligand gene Cxcl12 was highly expressed in subsets of theca cell across all four species, that the receptor gene Cxcr4 was highly expressed in immune cell and myeloid cell across all four species, and that Ackr3 was highly expressed in granulosa cell in chicken and in endothelial cell in mouse (Fig. S4A). This indicates that the regulation of the CXCL pathway across distinct cell types is cross-species conserved. Regarding the VEGF pathway, the receptor gene Kdr was highly expressed in endothelial cell across all four species, underscoring the central role of endothelial cells as mediators (Fig. S4B). For the VISFATIN pathway, the ligand gene Nampt was highly expressed in multiple cell types across all four species, while the receptor gene Insr was highly expressed in most cell types in human and in endothelial cell in mouse (Fig. S4C). These findings indicate that the VISFATIN pathway operates with a degree of conservation alongside species-specific regulatory features.

Transcription factor analysis reveals key regulatory factors within cell types

Through transcription factor analysis and screening (top 30 by activity, with Δ activity > 0.8), a set of TFs exhibiting high activity and cell type specificity was identified across cell types in the four species (Fig. 3A, Table S4). Cross-species comparison of these regulons identified four TFs with consistently high activity across all four species: Klf6, Mef2a, Tead1, and Elk1, which were predominantly active in distinct cell types (Fig. 3B, C). To investigate their functions, the target genes of each TF were obtained (Table S5), followed by GO and KEGG enrichment analyses for the target genes of Klf6, Mef2a, Tead1, and Elk1 (Fig. 3D, S5A, Tables S6, S7). Functional enrichment indicated that Mef2a targets were enriched in the IgSF CAM and cGMP-PKG pathways, while Tead1 targets were associated with Hippo signaling, and Elk1 targets were linked to the MAPK, PI3K-Akt, and IL-17 pathways (Fig. 3D, Table S7). Subsequently, using the target gene sets, five TFs predicted to regulate the ligands and receptors of the CXCL, VEGF, and VISFATIN pathways were identified, namely Rbpj, Myc, Sp1, Stat3, and Max (Fig. 3F, Table S8). Regulatory network analysis of the conserved TFs (Klf6, Mef2a, Tead1, Elk1), key regulators (Rbpj, Myc, Sp1, Stat3, Max), and the signaling components of the CXCL, VEGF, and VISFATIN pathways revealed a complex web of direct and indirect regulatory links (Fig. 3G). By integrating the above results with target gene prediction, this study found that: the TF Rbpj positively regulated Cxcr4, that Myc positively regulated Cxcr4, Vegfa, and Insr, that Sp1 positively regulated Vegfa and Insr while negatively regulating Kdr, that Stat3 positively regulated Vegfa, and that Max positively regulated Insr (Fig. 3H). Furthermore, the TF Elk1 was found to participate in the regulation of signaling pathways such as PI3K-Akt and MAPK in coordination with the VISFATIN pathway.

We extracted data from mouse at 9, 12, and 15 months and from human at 18-28, 36-39, and 47-49 years of age, and generated bar plots showing the proportion of each cell type at each time point (Fig. 4A). Subsequently, we performed PROGENy analyses on mouse and human separately to assess the changes in the activity of key pathways during ovarian function decline (Fig. 4B, C). The results showed that the activities of pathways such as MAPK and VEGF increased in multiple cell types in both mouse and human. To identify genes whose expression increased or decreased with ovarian function changes, we clustered genes based on their expression trajectory patterns (Fig. 4B, 4C; Tables S9 and S10). GO enrichment analysis revealed that in mouse, the persistently declining gene cluster 2 was associated with negative regulation of mitosis, while the persistently increasing clusters 3 and 4 were associated with processes such as negative regulation of cell migration and phosphonate metabolism (Fig. 4B; Table S11). In human, the persistently increasing clusters 2 and 3 were involved in the calcineurin-NFAT signaling cascade, calcineurin-mediated signaling, and the protein folding chaperone complex, whereas the persistently declining clusters 5 and 6 were associated with processes including regulation of intracellular transport and tRNA metabolic process (Fig. 4C; Table S12).

Based on the above results, we selected mouse gene clusters 2, 3, and 4 and human gene clusters 2, 3, 5, and 6 as the key gene sets associated with ovarian function (Table S13). Further analysis identified 26 genes that consistently increased and 4 genes that consistently decreased in both species (Fig. 4D; Table S14). GO enrichment analysis showed that the commonly upregulated genes were associated with mammary gland involution and positive regulation of miRNA-mediated gene silencing, while the commonly downregulated genes were associated with regulation of translation in response to stress and negative regulation of sister chromatid segregation (Fig. 4D; Table S15). Meanwhile, KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that the downregulated genes were also involved in processes such as oocyte meiosis and the cell cycle (Fig. 4D; Table S16). These results suggest that the 30 core genes are closely associated with the maintenance of ovarian function and exhibit a certain degree of species conservation.

Finally, by integrating key TFs with the key genes, we identified Max and Stat3, two genes whose expression increased with time in mouse and human. These two genes, through transactivating their target genes Insr and Vegfa, respectively, exert regulatory effects on the VISFATIN and VEGF signaling pathways (Fig. 4E).

In vitro validation of key TFs under simulated oxidative stress and aging conditions

Building upon the aforementioned findings, the present study proposes that the key TFs Rbpj, Myc, Sp1, Stat3, and Max play crucial roles in regulating ovarian function and maintaining normal reproductive performance. Previous studies have demonstrated that Myc, Sp1, and Stat3 possess carcinogenic potential (Mladenov et al., 1967; Roussel et al., 1979), induce cellular senescence (Li and Davie, 2010; Wu et al., 2022), and promote tumor growth and migration (Bromberg, 2002; Yu et al., 2009), respectively, further providing theoretical support for our hypothesis. To investigate the expression dynamics of these core TFs under specific microenvironmental stresses, we conducted in vitro validation experiments using DF-1 and NIH/3T3 cells. H₂O₂ and D-galactose are widely used and well-established inducers of oxidative stress and cellular senescence, respectively (Sies, 2017; Azman and Zakaria, 2019).

Discussion

This study conducted a cross-species analysis using ovarian single-cell data from chicken, human, mouse, and yak to reveal species-conserved key mechanisms and their specific changes during ovarian aging. Although previous studies have suggested numerous evolutionary differences among species (Zhang et al., 2010; Rogers and Gibbs, 2014), it is also true that essential biological processes are retained over the course of evolution.

In our analysis, we identified theca cell subpopulations defined by distinct highly expressed genes in all four species. In the adult ovary, theca cells play multiple roles during folliculogenesis. A recent study reported that the maintenance of bovine oocytes at the germinal vesicle (GV) stage is mediated by theca cell organization (Sirard and Bilodeau, 1990). Furthermore, enrichment analysis of significantly altered genes identified through pseudotime trajectory analysis revealed notable changes in cholesterol metabolism. The synthesis of androgens and estrogens involved in follicle development and atresia also primarily originates from theca cell (Gómez et al., 1998; Sechman et al., 2006; Gan et al., 2017). Studies in avian species have demonstrated that progesterone in preovulatory follicles is produced by granulosa cell, whereas androgens and estrogens are synthesized within the theca layer (Huang and Nalbandov, 1979; Marrone and Hertelendy, 1985). Changes in cholesterol levels are concurrently associated with oocyte maturation and abnormalities (Skoblina et al., 1981; Petrino and Schuetz, 1987; Zhang et al., 2025). Meanwhile, KEGG enrichment results indicated that genes involved in pathways such as PI3K-Akt and p53 also undergo significant alterations. It has been suggested that increased PI3K-Akt activity is associated with a reduction in the primordial follicle pool and ovarian aging (Reddy et al., 2008, 2009). The PI3K-Akt pathway also functions to drive ovarian fibrosis and tissue stiffening (Edepli and Yaba, 2026). The p53 pathway is considered to play a role in initiating and maintaining cellular senescence (Campisi and d’Adda di Fagagna, 2007; Vousden and Prives, 2009; Riessland et al., 2019). These findings indicate that, although the proportion of theca cell displays species-specific variations, they still play an essential role in maintaining ovarian function and regulating ovarian aging across species.

Extensive evidence has indicated that a chronic low-grade inflammatory microenvironment can induce ovarian aging (Xiao et al., 2022; Ben Yaakov et al., 2023). We identified cross-species conserved signaling pathways across four species, including the CXCL, VEGF, and VISFATIN pathways. The CXCL pathway is involved in immune response regulation (Karin, 2020; Xu et al., 2022a), the VEGF pathway plays a role in angiogenesis (Peitsidis and Agrawal, 2010; Kudsy et al., 2016), and the VISFATIN pathway is associated with inflammation-associated aging mechanisms (Park et al., 2020). Notably, these pathways are likewise functionally linked to ovarian physiology and egg production in laying hens. Angiogenesis and the associated vascular network are essential for the normal functioning of the avian reproductive system, supporting the delivery of oxygen, hormones, and yolk precursors to the developing oocyte and contributing to eggshell formation (Machura and Hrabia, 2024); indeed, hens with high laying performance display denser ovarian vascular networks and elevated expression of angiogenic factors such as VEGF and ANGPT1, which together sustain high egg production (Qin et al., 2026). Likewise, NAMPT (visfatin) is present in hen ovarian theca and granulosa cells, where it inhibits progesterone production in granulosa cells and varies across follicular development (Diot et al., 2015).

This suggests that alterations in evolutionarily conserved pathways or genes often represent a significant contributing factor to the functional decline of tissues. Substantial evidence has demonstrated that ligand and receptor genes participating in these pathways, such as CXCL12 and its receptor CXCR4, play pivotal roles in inflammatory responses, immune system homeostasis, and tumor growth and metastasis (Pozzobon et al., 2016; Wu et al., 2023). Furthermore, the CXCL12/CXCR4 signaling axis was found to exert its effects through the activation of the JAK-STAT pathway (Zhang et al., 2001) and the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway (Song et al., 2015). Concurrently, this signaling axis has been implicated in multi-organ fibrosis (Suzuki et al., 2001). Studies have shown that Ackr3 exhibits preferential expression in senescent human dermal fibroblasts (Takaya et al., 2022). VEGFA has been recognized as one of the five senescence biomarkers most significantly associated with an increased risk of mortality (St Sauver et al., 2023). Relevant investigations have established a close correlation between abnormally elevated VEGFA levels and a spectrum of cardiovascular diseases as well as adverse cardiovascular outcomes (Braile et al., 2020). In the context of ovarian tissue, the introduction of VEGFA was shown to promote follicular development (Shimizu et al., 2003b; a; Lu et al., 2017), while inhibition of its expression was found to impede follicular growth and the formation of antral follicles in vivo (Feng et al., 2017; Xu et al., 2022b). Collectively, these findings indicate that drastic fluctuations in the expression levels of these genes may exert deleterious effects on the organism.

Through TF analysis, this study identified four evolutionarily conserved TFs across species: Klf6, Mef2a, Tead1, and Elk1. Among these, Klf6 and Tead1 have been recognized as highly conserved TFs throughout evolution (Andreoli et al., 2010; Zhou et al., 2016), further corroborating the identification results of the present study. Previous research has demonstrated that Klf6 is intimately linked to the majority of human cancers and plays a significant role in promoting tumor metastasis (DiFeo et al., 2006; Sun et al., 2020). Mef2a is involved in the regulation of cellular senescence (Zhao et al., 2012, 2015; Xiong et al., 2019). Functional loss of Mef2a results in severe pathological phenotypes, underscoring its indispensable biological role (Naya et al., 2002; Wang et al., 2003). Tead1 characterized as a pivotal regulator of replicative senescence (Wang et al., 2022). Furthermore, Elk1 has been linked to age-related tissue fibrosis (Cairns et al., 2020). Collectively, these findings imply that these genes serve as central regulatory nodes in both evolutionary conservation and disease pathogenesis.

Based on the target gene prediction network, the present study identified five key TFs: Rbpj, Myc, Sp1, Stat3, and Max. Among them, Rbpj serves as a core component of the Notch pathway (Bray, 2016), and its expression is highly sensitive to stress-induced alterations in the cellular microenvironment. Myc is one of the earliest identified oncogenes (Mladenov et al., 1967; Roussel et al., 1979; Dang, 2012), which drives cell proliferation and tumorigenesis primarily by modulating fundamental biological processes, including cell cycle progression, metabolism, and translation (Dang et al., 2006; Wang et al., 2018; Prochownik, 2022). Extensive research has shown that the gene network regulated by Myc substantially overlaps with the hallmarks of aging and tumorigenesis (Short et al., 2005; Hanahan and Weinberg, 2011; Srivastava, 2017). Inhibiting its activity not only significantly extends the lifespan of mouse (Wang et al., 2023) and drosophila (Greer et al., 2013), but also effectively promotes healthy aging (Hofmann et al., 2015). Furthermore, using in vitro senescence models induced by H₂O₂ and D-galactose, we found that Myc maintains a high expression state within the senescent microenvironment. We hypothesize that a vicious cycle of positive feedback may exist between the aberrant activation of Myc and the senescent microenvironment, thereby accelerating the irreversible decline of tissue function. Sp1 is closely associated with DNA repair and the aging process (Adrian et al., 1996; Bouwman and Philipsen, 2002). A decline in its expression level induces cellular senescence (Wu et al., 2022) and simultaneously results in embryonic lethality, implying that this gene is essential for maintaining fundamental life functions (Briggs et al., 1986; Marin et al., 1997; Wierstra, 2008; Beishline and Azizkhan-Clifford, 2015). The functional execution of Sp1 exhibits profound environmental dependency: it frequently displays aberrant overexpression in tumor cells (Li and Davie, 2010), whereas its activity is markedly diminished in naturally aged tissues (Ammendola et al., 1992; Helenius et al., 1996). The context-dependent behavior of Sp1 suggests that interventions targeting aging through its systemic overexpression or knockout may involve considerable risks and uncertainty. Stat3 is a fundamental factor for maintaining stem cell pluripotency and early embryonic development (Takeda et al., 1997; Niwa et al., 1998), and it promotes angiogenesis via the upregulation of VEGF and ANGPT2 (Niu et al., 2002; Wei et al., 2003; Kujawski et al., 2008; Gao et al., 2017). Furthermore, it acts as a central hub driving tumor growth and metastasis (Bromberg et al., 1999; Bromberg, 2002; Yu et al., 2009; Dai et al., 2021) while being deeply involved in the induction and maintenance of cellular senescence (Kuilman et al., 2008; Kojima et al., 2012). Distinct from the broad systemic pleiotropy of the aforementioned factors, Max predominantly exerts critical regulatory functions in the initiation of meiosis and reproductive development (Suzuki et al., 2016). It serves as a crucial "gatekeeper" in maintaining the homeostasis of germ cell gene expression, and its deficiency triggers the aberrant hyperactivation of meiosis-related genes (Maeda et al., 2013).

To enhance the reproducibility of cell-based experiments in the senescence model and to better exclude the confounding effects of cell state variability, we selected the DF-1 and NIH/3T3 cell lines for validation. Notably, across the various cellular stress models employed in this study, Max expression remained highly stable specifically in DF-1 cell, whereas obvious changes were observed in NIH/3T3 cell. This differential response between DF-1 and NIH/3T3 cells indicates that Max may respond to stress in a cell-type-dependent manner, although the underlying mechanism requires further investigation.

This study has several limitations. First, the chicken ovary was sampled at a single time point, so longitudinal analysis was limited to human and mouse data. Second, the DF-1 and NIH/3T3 fibroblast lines cannot recapitulate ovarian-specific features or oocyte–somatic crosstalk, so the cell-based results represent only general stress responses rather than ovarian senescence. Third, the conclusions rely on computational predictions without direct physiological validation (e.g., hormone levels, oocyte quality), and the functional roles of key regulators remain to be confirmed. Future studies with multi-age sampling and ovarian-specific models are needed to address these limitations.

Collectively, this study provides a systematic cross-species perspective on the conserved regulatory mechanisms underlying ovarian aging. By integrating cell-type-specific dynamics, signaling pathways, and transcriptional regulatory networks, we revealed how evolutionarily conserved programs coordinate ovarian functional attrition. The identification of key regulatory axes, particularly those involving Stat3 and Max, underscores their potential as targets for modulating senescence-associated processes. Nevertheless, the detailed regulatory circuitry and specific impacts of key genes implicated in ovarian functional dynamics warrant further mechanistic investigation. In conclusion, these findings not only deepen our mechanistic understanding of ovarian aging but also provide a theoretical foundation for developing targeted interventions to delay functional deterioration and enhance reproductive longevity.

Conclusion

This study integrated single-cell data from chicken, human, mouse, and yak to conduct a cross-species analysis of ovarian tissue. We identified conserved cell–cell communication pathways (CXCL, VEGF, and VISFATIN) and conserved TFs (Klf6, Mef2a, Tead1, and Elk1), as well as five key regulators (Rbpj, Myc, Sp1, Stat3, and Max) linking these pathways. Longitudinal analysis further revealed 30 genes robustly correlated with ovarian functional changes. Notably, Stat3 and Max were shown to regulate the VEGF and VISFATIN pathways via their target genes Vegfa and Insr, respectively. In vitro senescence assays further validated the conserved stress-responsive regulation of Rbpj, Myc, Sp1, and Stat3, whereas Max showed differential responses between DF-1 and NIH/3T3 cells. Collectively, these findings delineate a conserved regulatory framework underlying ovarian aging and offer promising candidates for therapeutic intervention.

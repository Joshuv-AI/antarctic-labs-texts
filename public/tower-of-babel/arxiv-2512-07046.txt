Minimizing Control Attention: The Linear Gauss-Markov paradigm
Ralph Sabbagh, Asmaa Eldesoukey, Mahmoud Abdelgalil, Tryphon T. Georgiou

Abstract
We revisit the concept of ‘attention’ as a technical term to quantify the effort in calibrating control action based on available data. While Wiener, in his work on Cybernetics, anticipated key principles on prioritizing task-relevant signals, it was not until the late 1990’s when Brockett first formulated pertinent optimization problems that have inspired subsequent as well as the present work. ‘Attention,’ as a technical term, is defined so as to quantify the dependence of the control law on the time and space/state coordinate; a control law that is independent of time and space, assuming it meets specifications, requires vanishing attention. In the present work we focus on Linear-Markovian dynamics with Gaussian state uncertainty so as to analyze the structure of minimal-attention control schemes that steer the dynamics between terminal states with Gaussian uncertainty profile.

INTRODUCTION

Information and control have been inextricably linked since the infancy of the field when Wiener, in his famous work on ‘

Much of the subsequent work by Wiener and others focused on predicting, encoding, and filtering such messages, and later on, on optimizing control action on the basis of such messages/signals. Yet, the most basic question on how much attention is needed on the part of a regulating apparatus in carrying out a task, was not formulated, let alone addressed, until the work of Roger Brockett half a century after the appearance of ‘

Brockett’s interest in ‘

As a guiding principle, Brockett put forth the point of view that “the easiest control law to implement is that of a constant input,” and that “anything else requires some attention”

,

From this vantage point, he argued that for the case where the control action

Brockett proposed the functional

\displaystyle\text{d}X_{t}=A_{t}X_{t}\ \text{d}t+B\,\text{d}W_{t},\;\;\;X_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{0}),

For one thing, the Gauss-Markov model contains a product term of control and state, and hence the dynamics are bilinear. Moreover, the departure from earlier paved paths in covariance steering is much deeper. There is no apparent re-parametrization that renders the problem convex

2

2

Functionals of the form

In this work we formulate the problem of minimum attention steering in a Gauss-Markov setting. The main results and new insights gained are as follows:

-

Existence of solutions.
We establish existence of solutions via the direct method in the calculus of variations.

-

First–order optimality conditions and regularity upgrade.

We provide optimality conditions and exploit the structure to upgrade the regularity of solutions.

-

Minimal attention for
\alpha\in\{0,1\}

We analyze separately the limiting cases when

-

Information–geometric links.
We compare ‘small-attention’ trajectories to Fisher–Rao geodesics.

In sum, we establish existence, optimality conditions, sharp regularity, closed–form special cases, and an information–geometric comparison principle for attention–penalized covariance steering beyond specialized cases.

Main results

We first establish existence of solutions to Problem

W^{m+1,1}
to
W^{m+2,1}
.

The stationarity condition holds in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,T)
:

\Lambda^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}=\alpha A^{\star}-(1-\alpha)\frac{d}{dt}\big(\dot{A}^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\big).

(25)

Since
\alpha\in(0,1)
, rearrange to

\frac{d}{dt}\big(\dot{A}^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\big)=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}A^{\star}-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\Lambda^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}.

(26)

Differentiate (
26
)
m
times in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,T)
and apply
(
24
) to
(\Lambda^{\star}\Sigma^{\star})^{(m)}
:

(\Lambda^{\star}\Sigma^{\star})^{(m)}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\binom{m}{\ell}\Lambda^{\star(\ell)}\Sigma^{\star(m-\ell)}.

By part (a),
\Lambda^{\star}\in W^{m+1,1}
, and by the inductive hypothesis

A^{\star}\in W^{m+1,1}
,
\Sigma^{\star}\in W^{m+2,1}
, so the right–hand side of
(
26
) has
m
-th derivative in
L^{1}
. Hence

\frac{d}{dt}\big(\dot{A}^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\big)\in W^{m,1},\quad\text{equivalently}\quad\dot{A}^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\in W^{m+1,1}.

Set
f:=\dot{A}^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\in W^{m+1,1}
. Differentiating

f=\dot{A}^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}

(m+1)
times and using (
24
) yields

f^{(m+1)}=A^{\star(m+2)}\Sigma^{\star}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{m+1}\binom{m+1}{\ell}\,A^{\star(\ell+1)}\Sigma^{\star(m+1-\ell)}.

Again, each term in the sum is in
L^{1}
since at most one factor is top order
and all others are bounded. Using (
22
) so that

(\Sigma^{\star})^{-1}\in L^{\infty}
, we solve for

\displaystyle A^{\star(m+2)}=

\displaystyle\Bigl(f^{(m+1)}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{m+1}\binom{m+1}{\ell}\,A^{\star(\ell+1)}\Sigma^{\star(m+1-\ell)}\Bigr)(\Sigma^{\star})^{-1}\in L^{1}.

Thus
A^{\star}\in W^{m+2,1}
.

(c) Upgrade
\Sigma^{\star}
from
W^{m+2,1}
to
W^{m+3,1}
.

The Lyapunov equation holds in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,T)
:

\dot{\Sigma}^{\star}=A^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}+\Sigma^{\star}A^{\star\top}+BB^{\top}.

(27)

Differentiate (
27
)
(m+2)
times distributionally. Applying
(
24
) gives

\displaystyle(A^{\star}\Sigma^{\star})^{(m+2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m+2}\binom{m+2}{\ell}A^{\star(\ell)}\Sigma^{\star(m+2-\ell)},

\displaystyle(\Sigma^{\star}A^{\star\top})^{(m+2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m+2}\binom{m+2}{\ell}\Sigma^{\star(\ell)}A^{\star(m+2-\ell)\top}.

By part (b) we have
A^{\star}\in W^{m+2,1}
, and by the inductive hypothesis

\Sigma^{\star}\in W^{m+2,1}
; hence each product above contains at most one
top–order
L^{1}
factor and all remaining factors bounded. Therefore

\Sigma^{\star(m+3)}\in L^{1}
, i.e.,
\Sigma^{\star}\in W^{m+3,1}
.

Combining (a)–(c) yields (
IV-C
) for
m+1
.
By induction, (
IV-C
) holds for all
m
.
Consequently
A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star},\Lambda^{\star}\in W^{k,1}
for every
k
,
and thus
A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star},\Lambda^{\star}\in C^{\infty}([0,T])
.
∎

V
Spatial attention

In this section, we study the edge case
\alpha=1
, where the attention functional
penalizes only the spatial component. The resulting variational
problem seeks a control that steers
\Sigma_{0}
to
\Sigma_{T}
while minimizing

J_{1}(A,\Sigma):=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}(A_{t}A_{t}^{\top})\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\|A_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\,dt.

Unlike the mixed case
0<\alpha<1
, no temporal penalty on
\dot{A}
is present.
Nevertheless, the structure of the first-order necessary conditions together with uniform interior spectral bounds still
forces minimizers to be smooth in time.

Problem 2
(Spatial attention:
\alpha=1
)

Given
T>0
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, and endpoints

\Sigma_{{\rm init}},\Sigma_{{\rm fin}}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
, determine

\min_{(A,\Sigma)\in\mathcal{H}_{1}}J_{1}(A,\Sigma),

subject to the Lyapunov dynamics

\dot{\Sigma}_{t}=A_{t}\Sigma_{t}+\Sigma_{t}A_{t}^{\top}+BB^{\top}~~\text{a.e. on }[0,T],

(28)

and endpoint constraints

\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma_{{\rm init}},\qquad\Sigma_{T}=\Sigma_{{\rm fin}}.

(29)

Here, the search space is

\mathcal{H}_{1}:=L^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})\oplus H^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{S}^{n}).

In what follows many of the steps in the proofs are similar to those in the ealier section and will be given with less detail, to avoid repetition.

V-A

Existence of minimizers

Theorem 5
(Existence in the spatial case
\alpha=1
)

Problem
2
admits a minimizer

(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star})\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
.
Moreover, every minimizer
\Sigma^{\star}
satisfies the uniform spectral bounds

c\,I\leq\Sigma_{t}^{\star}\leq C\,I,\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],

where, writing
J_{1}^{\star}:=\inf J_{1}
,

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle:=e^{-2\sqrt{T\,J_{1}^{\star}}}\,\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{{\rm init}}),

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle:=e^{2\sqrt{T\,J_{1}^{\star}}}\Bigl(\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_{{\rm init}})+T\,\lambda_{\max}(BB^{\top})\Bigr).

Proof:

Step 1: Feasibility and finite infimum.

Consider the reference covariance path

\Sigma_{t}:=(1-t/T)\Sigma_{{\rm init}}+(t/T)\Sigma_{{\rm fin}},\qquad t\in[0,T].

Since
\Sigma_{{\rm init}},\Sigma_{{\rm fin}}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
is convex,

\Sigma_{t}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
for all
t
. Define

A_{t}:=\tfrac{1}{2}(\dot{\Sigma}_{t}-BB^{\top})(\Sigma_{t})^{-1}.

Then
\Sigma\in H^{1}
and
A\in C^{0}\subset L^{2}
, and by direct substitution

(A,\Sigma)
satisfies (
28
)–(
29
).
Hence, the feasible set is nonempty and

0\leq\inf J_{1}\leq J_{1}(A,\Sigma)<\infty.

Step 2: Minimizing sequence and coercivity.

Let
(A^{k},\Sigma^{k})\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
be a minimizing sequence with

J_{1}(A^{k},\Sigma^{k})\leq J_{1}^{\star}+\tfrac{1}{k}=:M_{k}.

(30)

Then
\|A^{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq M_{k}
and by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\|A^{k}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\sqrt{T}\,\|A^{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sqrt{T\,M_{k}}.

(31)

Step 3: Uniform spectral bounds on
\Sigma^{k}
.

Let
\Phi^{k}(t,s)
solve
\partial_{t}\Phi^{k}(t,s)=A_{t}^{k}\Phi^{k}(t,s)
with
\Phi^{k}(s,s)=I
.
Gronwall and (
31
) yield

\displaystyle\sigma_{\max}(\Phi^{k}(t,s))\leq\exp\!\Big(\int_{s}^{t}\|A_{u}^{k}\|\,du\Big)\leq e^{\sqrt{T\,M_{k}}},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\min}(\Phi^{k}(t,s))\geq e^{-\sqrt{T\,M_{k}}}.

Using the representation

\Sigma_{t}^{k}=\Phi^{k}(t,0)\Sigma_{{\rm init}}\Phi^{k}(t,0)^{\top}+\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{k}(t,s)BB^{\top}\Phi^{k}(t,s)^{\top}\,ds,

Rayleigh quotient bounds give, for all
t
,

\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{t}^{k})\geq e^{-2\sqrt{T\,M_{k}}}\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{{\rm init}})=:c_{k},

and

\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_{t}^{k})\leq e^{2\sqrt{T\,M_{k}}}\Bigl(\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_{{\rm init}})+T\,\lambda_{\max}(BB^{\top})\Bigr)=:C_{k}.

Since
M_{k}\leq M_{1}
for all
k
, we obtain uniform bounds

0<c_{1}I\leq\Sigma_{t}^{k}\leq C_{1}I<\infty.

(32)

Step 4:
H^{1}
–bounds for
\Sigma^{k}
.

From (
28
) and (
32
),

\dot{\Sigma}^{k}=A^{k}\Sigma^{k}+\Sigma^{k}A^{k\top}+BB^{\top}\in L^{2},

because
\Sigma^{k}\in L^{\infty}
,
A^{k}\in L^{2}
and (
31
). Hence
\Sigma^{k}
is uniformly bounded
in
H^{1}([0,T])
.

Step 5: Compactness and passage to the limit.

By reflexivity and Banach–Alaoglu there is a subsequence (not relabeled) and

(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star})\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
such that

A^{k}\rightharpoonup A^{\star}\ \text{ in }L^{2},\qquad\Sigma^{k}\rightharpoonup\Sigma^{\star}\ \text{ in }H^{1}.

Rellich–Kondrachov in one dimension gives
\Sigma^{k}\to\Sigma^{\star}
in
C^{0}
.
Thus, endpoints (
29
) pass to the limit.
Moreover,

A^{k}\Sigma^{k}-A^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}=(A^{k}-A^{\star})\Sigma^{\star}+A^{k}(\Sigma^{k}-\Sigma^{\star}),

where the first term converges weakly to
0
in
L^{2}
, and the second tends to
0
in
L^{2}

because
\|A^{k}\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded and
\Sigma^{k}\to\Sigma^{\star}
uniformly.
The same holds for
\Sigma^{k}A^{k\top}
, so (
28
) holds for the limit.
Hence
(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star})
is feasible.

Step 6: Minimality and limiting spectral bounds.

Weak lower semicontinuity of
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
yields

J_{1}(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star})\leq\liminf_{k}J_{1}(A^{k},\Sigma^{k})=J_{1}^{\star}.

Thus
(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star})
is a minimizer.
Finally, Weyl’s inequalities combined with the uniform convergence of
\Sigma^{k}

pass (
32
) to the limit, giving the stated constants with

M_{k}\to J_{1}^{\star}
.
∎

Remark 2
(Relaxed bounds)

As in the mixed case, any admissible value
\bar{J}_{1}\geq J_{1}^{\star}
may be substituted for

J_{1}^{\star}
in the expressions of
c
and
C
, yielding conservative computable
bounds.

First–order necessary conditions

Theorem 6
(FONC for the spatial case)

Let
(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star})\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
be a minimizer of
Problem
2
. Then there exist multipliers

\Lambda\in W^{1,1}([0,T];\mathbb{S}^{n})
and
M_{0},M_{T}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}^{\star}

\displaystyle=A^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}+\Sigma^{\star}A^{\star\top}+BB^{\top},

(33a)

\displaystyle-\dot{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\Lambda A^{\star}+A^{\star\top}\Lambda,

(33b)

\displaystyle A^{\star}

\displaystyle=\Lambda\Sigma^{\star},

(33c)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=M_{0},\qquad\Lambda_{T}=-M_{T}.

(33d)

All identities hold a.e. on
[0,T]

Proof:

Apply Theorem
2
with
X=\mathcal{H}_{1}
,

Y=L^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{S}^{n})\oplus\mathbb{S}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
constraint map
\mathcal{C}
as in the mixed case, and

.
The maps
J_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}
are Fréchet

on

by the same arguments as in
Theorem
3
(here the
J_{1}
derivative simplifies). The surjectivity of

D\mathcal{C}(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star})
follows identically, and is in fact easier since
only
H^{c}\in L^{2}

see text before (
16
)).

Thus there exists
\tilde{\Lambda}=(\Lambda,M_{0},M_{T})\in Y

Variations

Boundary variations yield (

∎

V-C

Regularity of minimizers

Theorem 7
(Smoothness in the spatial case)

Let
(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star})
be a minimizer of
Problem
2
with multiplier
\Lambda
from
Theorem
6
. Then

A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star},\Lambda\in C^{\infty}([0,T]).

Proof:

Step 1: Initial lift to continuity.

From Theorem
5
,
\Sigma^{\star}\in H^{1}([0,T])\hookrightarrow C^{0}

and satisfies
cI\leq\Sigma_{t}^{\star}\leq CI
.
By Theorem
6
,

A^{\star}\in L^{2}
and
\Lambda\in L^{2}
with

-\dot{\Lambda}=\Lambda A^{\star}+A^{\star\top}\Lambda\quad\text{ a.e. }H^{-1}.

Since
\Lambda,A^{\star}\in L^{2}
and
L^{2}\cdot L^{2}\subset L^{1}
,
the right–hand side lies in
L^{1}
, hence
\dot{\Lambda}\in L^{1}
and

\Lambda\in W^{1,1}\hookrightarrow C^{0}
.
Stationarity
A^{\star}=\Lambda\Sigma^{\star}
then gives
A^{\star}\in C^{0}
.

Step 2: Classical
C^{1}
regularity.

With
A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star}\in C^{0}
, the primal equation gives

\dot{\Sigma}^{\star}\in C^{0}
, hence
\Sigma^{\star}\in C^{1}
.
Similarly, the adjoint equation gives
\dot{\Lambda}\in C^{0}
, so
\Lambda\in C^{1}
.
Therefore
A^{\star}=\Lambda\Sigma^{\star}\in C^{1}
.

Step 3: Bootstrap to
C^{\infty}
.

Differentiate the pointwise FONC system repeatedly. At each order
m\geq 0
,
Leibniz’ rule yields expressions for
\Sigma^{\star\,(m+1)}
and
\Lambda^{(m+1)}

as finite sums of products of derivatives of order at most
m
of

\Sigma^{\star}
and
\Lambda
(and the constant matrix

).
Since the right-hand sides are smooth combinations of already-smooth terms,
standard ODE bootstrapping implies

\Sigma^{\star},\Lambda\in C^{m+1}
whenever they are in
C^{m}
.
By induction,
\Sigma^{\star},\Lambda\in C^{\infty}
, and then

A^{\star}=\Lambda\Sigma^{\star}\in C^{\infty}
as well.
∎

Remark 3
(Comparison with
0<\alpha<1
)

In the mixed case, smoothness arises from a Sobolev bootstrap driven by the

\dot{A}
–penalty. For
\alpha=1
there is no coercive control on
\dot{A}
at the energy
level, but the algebraic stationarity

couples the primal and adjoint
flows into a polynomial ODE system, which still forces

regularity once

\Sigma
stays uniformly SPD.

V-D

Zero–noise limit

We now specialize to the case where
B\equiv 0
. It is of interest since the FONC system becomes autonomous and admits explicit solutions.

Proposition 1
(FONC structure and closed forms)

Assume
B\equiv 0
. Let
(A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star},\Lambda^{\star})
satisfy
(
33
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}^{\star}

\displaystyle=A^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}+\Sigma^{\star}A^{\star\top},

\displaystyle-\dot{\Lambda}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\star}A^{\star}+A^{\star\top}\Lambda^{\star},

\displaystyle A^{\star}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}.

Write
A^{\star}_{t}=S_{t}+\Omega_{t}
with

S_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}(A^{\star}_{t}+A^{\star\top}_{t})\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and

\Omega_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}(A^{\star}_{t}-A^{\star\top}_{t})\in\mathbb{A}^{n}
.
Then:

(i)

\Omega
is constant in time and
{\rm tr}A^{\star}_{t}\equiv{\rm tr}A^{\star}_{0}
.

(ii)

S
solves
\dot{S}_{t}=2(\Omega S_{t}-S_{t}\Omega)
, hence

A^{\star}_{t}=e^{2\Omega t}\,S_{0}\,e^{-2\Omega t}+\Omega.

(34)

(iii)

Let

Z_{t}:=e^{-2\Omega t}\Sigma^{\star}_{t}e^{2\Omega t}
. Then

\dot{Z}_{t}=A_{0}^{\star\top}Z_{t}+Z_{t}A_{0}^{\star},\qquad Z_{t}=e^{A_{0}^{\star\top}t}\Sigma_{0}\,e^{A_{0}^{\star}t},

so

\Sigma^{\star}_{t}=e^{2\Omega t}\,e^{A_{0}^{\star\top}t}\,\Sigma_{0}\,e^{A_{0}^{\star}t}\,e^{-2\Omega t},

(35)

and in particular

\Sigma_{T}^{\star}=e^{2\Omega T}\,e^{A_{0}^{\star\top}T}\,\Sigma_{0}\,e^{A_{0}^{\star}T}\,e^{-2\Omega T}.

(36)

Proof:

Differentiate
A^{\star}=\Lambda^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}
and substitute the primal and adjoint
equations:

\displaystyle\dot{A}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\dot{\Lambda}^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}+\Lambda^{\star}\dot{\Sigma}^{\star}

\displaystyle=-(\Lambda^{\star}A^{\star}+A^{\star\top}\Lambda^{\star})\Sigma^{\star}+\Lambda^{\star}(A^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}+\Sigma^{\star}A^{\star\top})

\displaystyle=-A^{\star\top}(\Lambda^{\star}\Sigma^{\star})+(\Lambda^{\star}\Sigma^{\star})A^{\star\top}

\displaystyle=A^{\star}A^{\star\top}-A^{\star\top}A^{\star}.

The right-hand side is symmetric and traceless, so the skew part

\Omega=\tfrac{1}{2}(A^{\star}-A^{\star\top})
is constant and

\tfrac{d}{dt}{\rm tr}A^{\star}=0
, proving (i).
Taking symmetric parts yields
\dot{S}=2(\Omega S-S\Omega)
, whose solution is

S_{t}=e^{2\Omega t}S_{0}e^{-2\Omega t}
, hence (
34
).
For (iii), define
Z_{t}=e^{-2\Omega t}\Sigma^{\star}_{t}e^{2\Omega t}
and differentiate;
using (
34
) one obtains
\dot{Z}_{t}=A_{0}^{{}^{\star}\top}Z_{t}+Z_{t}A_{0}^{\star}
with explicit solution

Z_{t}=e^{A_{0}^{\star\top}t}\Sigma_{0}e^{A_{0}^{\star}t}
, which yields
(
35
)–(
36
).
∎

VI
Temporal attention

We now consider the limiting regime
\alpha=0
, in which the attention cost reduces to

J_{0}(A,\Sigma)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}(\dot{A}_{t}\,\Sigma_{t}\,\dot{A}_{t}^{\top})\,dt.

Since
J_{0}
depends only on
\dot{A}
, every
constant
control
A
that steers the
covariance from
\Sigma_{0}
to
\Sigma_{T}
is a minimizer of
J_{0}
(indeed

J_{0}=0
whenever
\dot{A}\equiv 0
). The temporal problem is therefore degenerate:
the set of minimizers coincides with the set of constant feasible controls.

To extract a meaningful selection among these constant minimizers, we adopt a standard
perturbative viewpoint. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, consider instead

J_{\varepsilon}(A,\Sigma)=\varepsilon\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\mathrm{tr}(A_{t}A_{t}^{\top})\,dt+(1-\varepsilon)\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\mathrm{tr}(\dot{A}_{t}\Sigma_{t}\dot{A}_{t}^{\top})\,dt.

As
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, the temporal term dominates and optimal controls flatten in
time. At the next order, one expects selection among constant feasible controls by the
spatial energy
\mathrm{tr}(AA^{\top})
. This motivates the reduced finite-dimensional
problem.

Problem 3
(Temporally–selected constant control)

Given
T>0
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, and

\Sigma_{{\rm init}},\Sigma_{{\rm fin}}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
, determine

\min_{A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}\ \mathrm{tr}(AA^{\top})

subject to the Lyapunov dynamics with
constant

A
,

\dot{\Sigma}_{t}=A\Sigma_{t}+\Sigma_{t}A^{\top}+BB^{\top},~~\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma_{\rm init},~~\Sigma_{T}=\Sigma_{\rm fin}.

(37)

We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{const}}
the (possibly empty) set of constant
matrices
A
for which (
37
) satisfies the endpoint constraint.

VI-A

Existence of minimizers

Theorem 8
(Existence of minimizers)

Let
\alpha=\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let
(A_{\varepsilon},\Sigma_{\varepsilon})
be
minimizers of
J_{\varepsilon}
subject to the Lyapunov dynamics and endpoints.
Assume
\mathcal{F}_{\rm const}\neq\emptyset
. Then there is a sequence

\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
and
A^{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm const}
such that

A_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to A^{0}\ \text{in }C^{0}([0,T]),\qquad\Sigma_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to\Sigma^{0}\ \text{in }C^{0}([0,T]),

where
(A^{0},\Sigma^{0})
is feasible for (
37
). Moreover,
A^{0}
minimizes

{\mathrm{trace}}(AA^{\top})

over all constant feasible controls. In particular,
Problem
3
admits a minimizer.

Proof:

Fix any constant feasible pair
(\bar{A},\bar{\Sigma})
with
\bar{A}\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm const}
.
By optimality of
(A_{\varepsilon},\Sigma_{\varepsilon})
,

J_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},\Sigma_{\varepsilon})\leq J_{\varepsilon}(\bar{A},\bar{\Sigma})=\varepsilon T\|\bar{A}\|_{F}^{2}.

(38)

Step 1: uniform bounds and flattening of
A_{\varepsilon}
.

From (
38
),

\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{F}^{2}\,dt\leq\varepsilon T\|\bar{A}\|_{F}^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq T\|\bar{A}\|_{F}^{2}.

Also,

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{T}{\rm tr}(\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}^{\top})\,dt\leq\varepsilon T\|\bar{A}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\int_{0}^{T}{\rm tr}(\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}^{\top})\,dt=O(\varepsilon).

To convert this into
\|\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
, we use a uniform
lower bound on
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
.
Applying Theorem
1
with
\alpha=\varepsilon
gives

\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(t)\geq c_{\varepsilon}I,\qquad c_{\varepsilon}=e^{-2\sqrt{TJ_{\varepsilon}^{\star}/\varepsilon}}\,\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{0}),

where
J_{\varepsilon}^{\star}=\inf J_{\varepsilon}
.
Since
J_{\varepsilon}^{\star}\leq J_{\varepsilon}(\bar{A},\bar{\Sigma})=\varepsilon T\|\bar{A}\|_{F}^{2}
, we have

\frac{J_{\varepsilon}^{\star}}{\varepsilon}\leq T\|\bar{A}\|_{F}^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad c_{\varepsilon}\geq e^{-2\sqrt{T\cdot T\|\bar{A}\|_{F}^{2}}}\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{0})=:c>0,

with
c
independent of
\varepsilon
.
Therefore

\int_{0}^{T}\|\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{F}^{2}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{c}\int_{0}^{T}{\rm tr}(\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}^{\top})\,dt\longrightarrow 0.

Thus
\dot{A}_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
L^{2}(0,T)
and
\{A_{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in

H^{1}(0,T)
. By 1D Rellich compactness, after extracting a sequence

\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
,

A_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to A^{0}\quad\text{in }C^{0}([0,T]),

for some
A^{0}\in H^{1}
. Since
\dot{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to 0
in
L^{2}
, the limit
A^{0}

must be constant.

Step 2: convergence of
\Sigma_{\varepsilon_{k}}
and feasibility of the limit.

Each
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
solves

\dot{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}=A_{\varepsilon}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}+\Sigma_{\varepsilon}A_{\varepsilon}^{\top}+BB^{\top},\qquad\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(0)=\Sigma_{0}.

Uniform spectral bounds from Theorem
1
give
\|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C
,
and the Lyapunov equation gives equicontinuity. Hence, by Arzelà–Ascoli,
up to a subsequence,

\Sigma_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to\Sigma^{0}\quad\text{in }C^{0}([0,T]).

Let
E_{k}:=\Sigma_{\varepsilon_{k}}-\Sigma^{0}
. Then

\dot{E}_{k}=A_{\varepsilon_{k}}E_{k}+E_{k}A_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\top}+(A_{\varepsilon_{k}}-A^{0})\Sigma^{0}+\Sigma^{0}(A_{\varepsilon_{k}}-A^{0})^{\top}.

Since
A_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to A^{0}
in
C^{0}
, and
\|E_{k}\|_{C^{0}}\to 0
,

\Sigma^{0}
satisfies the Lyapunov equation with constant drift
A^{0}
.
Moreover, because each
\Sigma_{\varepsilon_{k}}(T)=\Sigma_{\rm fin}
and convergence is uniform,

\Sigma^{0}(T)=\Sigma_{\rm fin}
. Hence
A^{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm const}
and
(A^{0},\Sigma^{0})
is feasible.

Step 3: selection minimality.

Let
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm const}
be any constant feasible control with trajectory
\Sigma
.
Since
\dot{A}\equiv 0
,

J_{\varepsilon}(A,\Sigma)=\varepsilon T\|A\|_{F}^{2}.

Optimality yields

J_{\varepsilon_{k}}(A_{\varepsilon_{k}},\Sigma_{\varepsilon_{k}})\leq J_{\varepsilon_{k}}(A,\Sigma)=\varepsilon_{k}T\|A\|_{F}^{2}.

Divide by
\varepsilon_{k}
and drop the nonnegative temporal term:

\int_{0}^{T}\|A_{\varepsilon_{k}}(t)\|_{F}^{2}\,dt\leq T\|A\|_{F}^{2}.

Passing to the limit using
A_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to A^{0}
in
L^{2}
(since
C^{0}
convergence
implies
L^{2}
convergence),

T\|A^{0}\|_{F}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\|A^{0}\|_{F}^{2}\,dt\leq T\|A\|_{F}^{2}.

Since
A
was arbitrary in
\mathcal{F}_{\rm const}
,
A^{0}
minimizes
{\mathrm{trace}}(AA^{\top})

over
\mathcal{F}_{\rm const}
. This also proves existence of a minimizer
for Problem
3
.
∎

Remark 4

In earlier sections we utilized the FONC to upgrade the regularity of the minimizer. Herein, however, the minimizer
A^{\star}
is constant and therefore
\Sigma^{\star}
is smooth. Nevertheless, the adjoint equation and stationarity conditions are given below for completeness:

\displaystyle-\dot{\Lambda}_{t}

\displaystyle=A^{\star\top}\Lambda_{t}+\Lambda_{t}A^{\star},

\displaystyle 2TA^{\star}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\!\big(\Lambda_{t}\Sigma_{t}^{\star}+\Sigma_{t}^{\star}\Lambda_{t}\big)\,\text{d}t=0,

where
\Lambda:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the (symmetric) adjoint variable.

Remark 5

In the zero-noise limit, where
B\equiv 0
, a feasible (constant)
A

must satisfy
\Sigma_{T}=e^{AT}\Sigma_{0}e^{A^{\top}T}
. Thereby, it must be of the form

e^{AT}=\Sigma_{T}^{1/2}\,R\,\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2},\qquad R\in O(n).

(39)

Thus, Problem
3
can be recast as

\min_{R\in O(n)}\ \Big\|\log\!\big(\Sigma_{T}^{1/2}R\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}\big)\Big\|_{F}^{2}.

(40)

This is a nonconvex “logarithmic Procrustes” problem over
O(n)
, and
does not generally admit a closed-form minimizer.

While the selection based on
\|A\|_{F}^{2}
is natural from the temporal–attention viewpoint,
it does not acknowledge the covariance structure, in general. Thus, it is of interest to explore connection with information-theoretic alternatives. This is done next.

VII
Attention functional and the Fisher-Rao geodesic in the zero-noise limit

In this section, we discuss a connection between the attention functional
J_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in[0,1]
, defined in (
3
), and the closeness of its minimizer (
A^{\star},\Sigma^{\star}
) to the canonical pair
(A^{F},\Sigma^{F})
that induces the Fisher-Rao geodesic
[
1
,
8
]

\Sigma_{t}^{F}=\Sigma_{0}^{1/2}M^{t/T}\Sigma_{0}^{1/2},~~t\in[0,T],

between
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{T}
in the zero-noise limit (i.e.,
B=0
), where

M:=\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{T}\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}>0
. To this end, we characterize first all Fisher geodesic-inducing
constant
control matrices.

Proposition 2
(Fisher-inducing generators)

Let
T>0
,
\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{T}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
.
Then, the set of matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
satisfying
\dot{\Sigma}_{t}^{F}=A\Sigma_{t}^{F}+\Sigma_{t}^{F}A^{\top},t\in[0,T]
, is given by the affine space

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=A^{F}+\Sigma_{0}^{1/2}L_{M}\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2},

where
A^{F}:=\frac{1}{2T}\Sigma_{0}^{1/2}(\log M)\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}
and

\displaystyle L_{M}:=\{X\in\mathbb{A}^{n}~|~[X,M]=0\}.

In particular,
A
is unique (i.e.,
L_{M}=\{0\}
) whenever
M
has simple spectrum, and the unique choice with
A\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma_{0}A^{\top}
is precisely
A=A^{F}
.

Proof:

Let
\Gamma_{t}=M^{t/T}
and
C:=\log M\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
the principal logarithm. Then,
\Gamma_{t}=e^{(t/T)C}
and
\dot{\Gamma}_{t}=(1/T)\Gamma_{t}C
.
Write
Y:=\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}A\Sigma_{0}^{1/2}
where
A
satisfies
\dot{\Sigma}_{t}^{F}=A\Sigma_{t}^{F}+\Sigma_{t}^{F}A^{\top},t\in[0,T]
. Then,

Y\Gamma_{t}+\Gamma_{t}Y^{\top}\;=\;\tfrac{1}{T}\,\Gamma_{t}C,\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

Left-multiplying by
\Gamma_{t}^{-1}
and setting
s=t/T
gives

e^{-sC}Ye^{sC}+Y^{\top}=\tfrac{1}{T}C,\qquad\forall s\in[0,1].

Differentiating in
s
yields
-[C,e^{-sC}Ye^{sC}]=0
for all
s
, hence
[C,Y]=0
.
Decomposing
Y
into its symmetric and skew-symmetric parts
Y=S+X
, the identity at
s=0
gives
S=C/(2T)
, while
[C,Y]=0
forces
[C,X]=0
, and hence
X\in L_{M}
.
Conversely, if
Y=\tfrac{1}{2T}C+X
with
X\in L_{M}
, then
Y\Gamma_{t}+\Gamma_{t}Y^{\top}=\tfrac{1}{T}\Gamma_{t}C
.
Returning to
A=\Sigma_{0}^{1/2}Y\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}
yields
\dot{\Sigma}_{t}^{F}=A\Sigma_{t}^{F}+\Sigma_{t}^{F}A^{\top}
.
Finally, if
M
has a simple spectrum, then the commutant of its principal logarithm,
\mathrm{Comm}(C)
, is given by all polynomials in
C
. Hence, the only
X\in\mathbb{A}^{n}
commuting with
C
is
X=0
. This proves uniqueness (more generally, degeneracies in the multiplicities
m_{j}
of eigenvalues of
M
make
L_{M}\simeq\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{A}^{m_{j}}
). This completes the proof.
∎Using the above proposition, we show next that any minimizer of
J_{\alpha}
is close to the Fisher–Rao pair
(A^{F},\Sigma^{F})
in the sense that a
Fisher geodesic-inducing cost
is kept small.

Lemma 2

Let
T>0
,
\beta\in(0,1)
,
\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{T}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
, and
(A,\Sigma)\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\dot{\Sigma}_{t}=A_{t}\Sigma_{t}+\Sigma_{t}A_{t}^{\top}
a.e. on
[0,T]
. Define the Fisher geodesic-inducing cost:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(A,\Sigma)

\displaystyle:=\beta\int_{0}^{T}\|A_{t}-\Sigma_{t}A_{t}^{\top}\Sigma_{t}^{-1}\|_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+(1-\beta)\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{tr}\big(\dot{A}_{t}\Sigma_{t}\dot{A}_{t}^{\top}\big)\mathrm{d}t,

(41)

where
\|X\|_{\Sigma}^{2}:=\mathrm{tr}(X\Sigma X^{\top})
. Then,
(A^{F},\Sigma^{F})
is the unique minimizer of (
41
). Moreover, if
0<cI\leq\Sigma_{t}\leq CI
for a.a.
t\in[0,T]
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(A,\Sigma)\ \leq\ KJ_{\alpha}(A,\Sigma),

(42)

where
K:=\max(4\beta C^{2}/(\alpha c),(1-\beta)/(1-\alpha))
.

Proof:

We first show that
(A^{F},\Sigma^{F})
is the unique minimizer of (
41
). For the Fisher pair
(A^{F},\Sigma^{F})
,
A^{F}
is constant and
A^{F}\Sigma_{t}^{F}=\Sigma_{t}^{F}(A^{F})^{\top}
holds for all
t
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(A^{F},\Sigma^{F})=0
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(A,\Sigma)=0
then the temporal term yields
\dot{A}_{t}\equiv 0
(hence
A_{t}\equiv A
is constant) and
A\Sigma_{t}=\Sigma_{t}A^{\top}
for all
t
. In congruence coordinates
\Gamma_{t}:=\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{t}\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}
and
Y:=\Sigma_{0}^{-1/2}A\Sigma_{0}^{1/2}
this implies
\Gamma_{t}=e^{2tY}
. At
t=T
this is
e^{2TY}=M
. The symmetric logarithm is unique, hence
Y=\tfrac{1}{2T}\log M
, i.e.,
(A,\Sigma)=(A^{F},\Sigma^{F})
.

Next, we prove (
42
). To this end, let
B_{t}:=\Sigma_{t}^{-1/2}A_{t}\Sigma_{t}^{1/2}
and
D_{t}=(B_{t}-B_{t}^{\top})/2
. Then

\displaystyle\|A_{t}-\Sigma_{t}A_{t}^{\top}\Sigma_{t}^{-1}\|_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}=\|\Sigma_{t}^{1/2}(B_{t}-B_{t}^{\top})\Sigma_{t}^{-1/2}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle=\|2\Sigma_{t}^{1/2}D_{t}\Sigma_{t}^{-1/2}\|_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}=4\|D_{t}\|_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}\leq 4C\|B_{t}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(4C^{2}/c){\rm tr}(\Sigma_{t}^{-1}B_{t}^{\top}\Sigma_{t}B_{t})=(4C^{2}/c){\rm tr}(AA^{\top}).

Thus, the first term in
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(A,\Sigma)
is bounded as follows

\displaystyle\beta\int_{0}^{T}\|A_{t}-\Sigma_{t}A_{t}^{\top}\Sigma_{t}^{-1}\|_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}\text{d}t\leq\frac{4\beta C^{2}}{c\alpha}\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\|A_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\text{d}t.

Adding the second term in
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(A,\Sigma)
to both sides of the above inequality yields (
42
).
∎

IX
Concluding remarks

The purpose of this work has been to revisit the concept of ‘attention.’ This concept was introduced by Roger Brockett in order to quantify what one would colloquially describe as the attention required to adjust the applied control action based on state and timing information.
Our intention has been to formalize and study Brockett’s concept for linear dynamical systems and Gaussian statistics. This endeavor necessitated introducing an ‘averaged version of attention,” as expressed in (
3
), weighed over regions of the state space in accordance with a relevant probability law.

On the surface, the attention functional appears similar to expressions in linear Gaussian-Markov system theory. However, this new paradigm does not share the enabling convexity of the linear-quadratic theory, and as a consequence, establishing existence and regularity of solutions proved technically demanding. It is carried out folloing the general lines of the direct method in the calculus of variations.

In particular, we analyzed separately the two special case of the attention functional–the
spatial
and the
temporal
, that provide natural extreme choice of attention in these two extreme instances when on is interested in the purely spatial, or temporal, variability of the control protocol. We established existence and regularity of minimizers in these cases as well, and in a certain setting, we compared with an alternative information-theoretic functional that gives rise to Fisher–Rao geodesics. Our development complements and parallels the theory of Gaussian steering/Schrödinger bridge for linear systems
[
15
,
16
,
27
]
and speaks to resource–aware control viewpoints (periodic/event–triggered actuation and information constraints)
[
23
]
.

We expect that our treatment of attention–penalized Gaussian steering will provide the basis for theoretical advances as well as expand the range of applications by helping to balance sensing/actuation effort and cost.
Further, it is our hope that future work will illuminate apparent links between attention and sheer in fluid flow
[
4
,
19
,
25
]
possibly leading to useful variational formalisms
[
28
,
7
,
3
,
6
]
that may drive the latter.

Abstract
. We show that the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra associated by Nekrashevych to a contracting self-similar group is simple if and only if the corresponding complex ∗ \ast -algebra is simple. We also improve on Steinberg and Szakać’s algorithm to determine if the ∗ \ast -algebra is simple. This provides an interesting class of non-Hausdorff amenable, effective and minimal ample groupoids for which simplicity of the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra and the complex ∗ \ast -algebra are equivalent.

1.
Introduction

It has been known for quite some time that if
\mathcal{G}
is a Hausdorff amenable ample groupoid, then
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is simple if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is minimal and effective, if and only if the algebra
K\mathcal{G}
is simple over any (some) field
K
; see
[
BCFS14
,
Ste16
,
CEM15
]
. If
\mathcal{G}
is non-Hausdorff, then minimality and effectiveness are still necessary conditions, but things begin to break apart. In particular, simplicity of
K\mathcal{G}
can depend on the characteristic of
K

[
OCEP+19
,
SS21
]
, and it is possible for
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
and
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
to not be simple. It was observed in
[
Nek16a
]
in the algebraic setting and in
[
OCEP+19
]
for both the algebraic and
C^{*}
-setting that the obstruction to simplicity is controlled by an ideal that is nowadays called the essential ideal
[
KM21
,
SS21
]
(see also
[
Nek04
, Section 4]
). The algebraic essential ideal of the complex algebra of an ample groupoid is always contained in the essential ideal of the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of the groupoid. However, it is an open question whether the algebraic essential ideal is dense in the essential ideal, and it is also open whether
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
is simple if and only if
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
is simple for a minimal effective ample groupoid.

The second author
[
Nek04
,
Nek09
]
introduced a
C^{*}
-algebra associated to a self-similar group, which moreover has an ample groupoid model which is minimal and effective for any self-similar group. In the case that the self-similar group is contracting, the second author showed that the groupoid is amenable and second countable. On the other hand, the groupoid associated to a self-similar group is seldom Hausdorff. Thus these groupoids form an interesting test case for understanding simplicity phenomena for non-Hausdorff groupoids.

The most famous contracting self-similar group is Grigorchuk’s finitely generated torsion group of intermediate growth
[
Gri80
,
Gri84
]
. The second author showed in
[
Nek16a
]
that the algebra of the associated ample groupoid is not simple over fields of characteristic
2
, but in
[
OCEP+19
]
it was shown that the algebra of this groupoid is simple over fields of all other characteristics and the
C^{*}
-algebra of the groupoid is simple. In
[
SS23
]
, the third author and Szakaćs provided an algorithm which on input the nucleus of a contracting self-similar group, outputs all characteristics of fields for which the algebra is not simple. They introduced a class of contracting self-similar groups, called multispinal groups, that generalizes the Grigorchuk group and Šunić groups
[
Š07
]
. For this class they give an easy to verify criterion for simplicity of its algebra over any field. Yoshida
[
Yos21
]
gave a criterion for simplicity of the
C^{*}
-algebra of a multispinal group that coincides with the criterion in
[
SS23
]
for simplicity of the complex
*
-algebra.

In this paper, we prove that the
C^{*}
-algebra associated to a contracting self-similar group is simple if and only if the complex
*
-algebra is simple. We also improve on the efficiency of the algorithm in
[
SS23
]
by showing that if the algebraic essential ideal is nonzero, then there is an element in the ideal supported on a particular type of finite subgroup of the nucleus. This allows us to obtain an exponential upper bound on the complexity of the algorithm. The algorithm in
[
SS23
]
is bounded in terms of Bell numbers.

2.
Ample groupoids and associated algebras

Let us review classical notations of groupoid theory.
A groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a small category of isomorphisms. We identify objects of the category with their identity automorphisms, and call them
units
. For an element
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the
source
and
range
of
g
are then
\mathsf{s}(g)=g^{-1}g
and
\mathsf{r}(g)=gg^{-1}
, and a product
g_{1}g_{2}
of elements of
\mathcal{G}
is defined if and only if
\mathsf{r}(g_{2})=\mathsf{s}(g_{1})
.
We denote the set of units of
\mathcal{G}
by
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

We also use the notation
\mathcal{G}_{x}=\mathsf{s}^{-1}(x)
,
\mathcal{G}^{x}=\mathsf{r}^{-1}(x)
and
\mathcal{G}_{x}^{y}=\mathcal{G}_{x}\cap\mathcal{G}^{y}
for units
x,y\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

A
topological groupoid
is a groupoid with a topology on it such that the multiplication and taking inverse are continuous maps.

A
bisection
is a subset
A\subset\mathcal{G}
such that the maps
\mathsf{s}\colon A\to\mathsf{s}(A)
and
\mathsf{r}\colon A\to\mathsf{r}(A)
are homeomorphisms. A groupoid is said to be étale if
\mathsf{s},\mathsf{r}
are open and it has a basis for its topology consisting of open bisections. (Equivalently, if both maps
\mathsf{s},\mathsf{r}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
are local homeomorphisms.)

If
\mathcal{G}
is étale and
C\subset\mathcal{G}
is compact, then for every unit
x
the sets
C\cap\mathcal{G}_{x}
and
C\cap\mathcal{G}^{x}
are finite, since
C
can be covered by finitely many bisections.

In this paper, all étale groupoids have locally compact Hausdorff unit spaces, but need not themselves be Hausdorff.

A groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be

ample
if it has a basis for its topology consisting of compact open bisections. (Equivalently, if it is étale and its unit space is totally disconnected.) Note that compact subsets of
\mathcal{G}
need not be closed, since we do not assume that
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field. For a (not necessarily continuous) map
f\colon\mathcal{G}\to\Bbbk
, the
strict support
of
f
is the set
f^{-1}(\Bbbk\setminus\{0\})
. We say that
f
is
compactly supported
if its strict support is contained in a compact subset of
\mathcal{G}
.

Consider two maps
f_{1},f_{2}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\Bbbk
. Their
convolution

f_{1}f_{2}
is defined by the formula

f_{1}f_{2}(g)=\sum_{g_{1}g_{2}=g}f_{1}(g_{1})f_{2}(g_{2}).

If one of the maps
f_{i}
is compactly supported, then the sum on the right-hand side is finite for every
g\in\mathcal{G}
, since
\mathsf{s}(g_{2})=\mathsf{s}(g)
and
\mathsf{r}(g_{1})=\mathsf{r}(g)
, so there are finitely many elements
g_{i}
for which the value
f_{i}(g_{i})
is non-zero.

In particular, the set of compactly supported functions on
\mathcal{G}
is a
\Bbbk
-algebra with respect to the convolution operation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an étale groupoid.

(1)

Following Connes
[
Con94
]
, denote by
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra spanned by functions
f\colon\mathcal{G}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that there is a compact open Hausdorff subset
U\subset\mathcal{G}
(which may be taken to be an open bisection) such that
f|_{U}
is continuous with compact support and
f|_{\mathcal{G}\setminus U}=0
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{G}
be ample and
\Bbbk
a field.
The algebra
\Bbbk\mathcal{G}
is the
\Bbbk
-algebra generated by the indicator functions
1_{F}
of compact open bisections
F\subset\mathcal{G}
.

If
F_{1},F_{2}
are compact open bisections, then
F_{1}F_{2}
is also a compact open bisection and
1_{F_{1}}1_{F_{2}}=1_{F_{1}F_{2}}
. Consequently,
\Bbbk\mathcal{G}
is, in fact, the
\Bbbk
-linear span of the set of indicator functions of compact open bisections.

If
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff, then
\Bbbk\mathcal{G}
coincides with the algebra of all continuous (equivalently, locally constant) compactly supported functions
\mathcal{G}\to\Bbbk
, where
\Bbbk
has discrete topology.

The algebras
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
and
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
have the natural structure of a complex
*
-algebra with respect to the operation
f^{*}(g)=\overline{f(g^{-1})}
.

If
f_{2}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\Bbbk
is supported on
\mathcal{G}_{x}
, then for every compactly supported
f_{1}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\Bbbk
, the function
f_{1}f_{2}
is defined and is also supported on
\mathcal{G}_{x}
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, and consider the space
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{x})
of
\ell^{2}
-summable functions
f\colon\mathcal{G}_{x}\to{\mathbb{C}}
.
For an étale groupoid, one has that the
\ast
-representation
\lambda_{x}
of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
on
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{x})
given by left multiplication (as above with
\Bbbk={\mathbb{C}}
) is bounded since if
f
is continuous with compact support on an open bisection
U
, then
\|\lambda_{x}(f)\|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
. We denote by
\lambda
the direct sum of all representations
\lambda_{x}
for
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an étale groupoid.
The
reduced
C^{*}
-algebra

C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is the completion of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
with respect to the norm
\|f\|=\|\lambda(f)\|=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\|\lambda_{x}(f)\|
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is ample. Note that if

\delta_{h}
is the delta-function for the element
h\in\mathcal{G}_{x}
, and
F
is a open compact
\mathcal{G}
-bisection, then
1_{F}\delta_{h}(g)=1
if and only if
gh^{-1}\in F
, which is equivalent to
g=Fh
. Consequently,
1_{F}\delta_{h}=\delta_{Fh}
.
Therefore, multiplication by
1_{F}
is a partial isometry of
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{x})
. It follows that multiplication by elements of
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
from the left is a bounded
*
-representation of
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{x})
. Let us denote this representation by
\lambda_{x}
. We denote by
\lambda
the direct sum of all representations
\lambda_{x}
for
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Notice that
\lambda
is the restriction of the corresponding representation
\lambda
of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
. It is proved in
[
CZ24
]
that
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
is the completion of
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
with respect to the norm
\|a\|=\|\lambda(a)\|=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\|\lambda_{x}(a)\|
, that is,
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
embeds densely and isometrically into
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
with respect to the reduced norms.

For an étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, we regard elements in
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
as bounded functions on
\mathcal{G}
by
a(\gamma)=\langle\lambda_{x}(a)\delta_{x},\delta_{\gamma}\rangle
where
x=\mathsf{s}(\gamma)
. This identification is sometimes called the
j
-map
, see
[
Ren80
, Proposition II.4.2]
. Note that
\|a\|_{\infty}\leq\|a\|
, with respect to this identification, and, for elements of
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
, we obtain the original function. If
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff, then these functions belong to
C_{0}(\mathcal{G})
, but for étale groupoids which are not Hausdorff, there may exist non-continuous elements in
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
whose strict support, when regarded as functions on
\mathcal{G}
, has empty interior.
These elements are used to define what is called the
essential ideal (under the assumption that
\mathcal{G}
is second countable).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a second countable étale groupoid.
The
essential ideal
in
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is the ideal

I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})
consisting of all elements in
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
whose
strict support has empty interior in
\mathcal{G}
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is ample, we set
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G})=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})\cap\mathbb{C}\mathcal{G}
, and call it the
algebraic essential ideal
.

For an effective, minimal and second countable ample groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, one has that
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})/I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})
is simple (
[
KM21
]
) and
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}/I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G})
is the unique simple quotient of
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
(
[
Nek16b
,
SS21
]
). Thus
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
is simple if and only if
I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})=0
and
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
is simple if and only if
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G})=0
; see
[
OCEP+19
]
. The case of
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
does not need second countability
[
SS21
]
. We remark that for étale groupoids that are not second countable, the definition of the essential ideal is more subtle
[
KM21
]
.

Remark 2.4
.

Although
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G})=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})\cap{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}
by definition, it is not clear whether
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G})
is dense in
I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})
. Indeed, it is conceivable that we may have

I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G})=\{0\}
while

I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})\neq\{0\}
.

3.
Graded étale groupoids

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an étale groupoid and let

c\colon\mathcal{G}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a continuous, i.e., locally constant, cocycle.
Given
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we set
\mathcal{G}_{n}=c^{-1}(\{n\})
,
and note that
\mathcal{G}=\bigsqcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{G}_{n}
is a clopen partition of
\mathcal{G}
, which we call a
grading
on
\mathcal{G}
. In this setting, we say that
(\mathcal{G},c)
is a
graded groupoid
. Note that

\mathcal{G}_{0}
is itself an étale, locally compact groupoid.

Gradings can be used to define natural circle actions on the associated groupoid C*-algebra:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},c)
be a graded étale groupoid.
Given
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{n})
, we set
T_{z}(f)(\gamma)=z^{n}f(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
. Since
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}C_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{n})
, the above formula induces a well-defined map
T_{z}\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\to C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
, which is easily seen to be a
\ast
-algebra isomorphism.

We also write
T_{z}\in\mathrm{Aut}(C^{*}(\mathcal{G}))
for
the extension to the universal C*-algebra of
\mathcal{G}
.
It is immediate to check that
z\mapsto T_{z}
is a continuous
group action, which we call the
gauge action
on
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a graded groupoid with cocycle
c
,
let
a\in C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
, let
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{G}x

and let
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
. Then

\langle\lambda_{x}(T_{z}(a))\delta_{\gamma_{1}},\delta_{\gamma_{2}}\rangle=z^{c(\gamma_{2})-c(\gamma_{1})}\langle\lambda_{x}(a)\delta_{\gamma_{1}},\delta_{\gamma_{2}}\rangle

for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{G}x
.

Proof.

Since
T_{z}
is continuous, it suffices to show the statement assuming that
a\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
. By linearity, it suffices to assume that there exists
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
a
is supported on
\mathcal{G}_{m}
.

Since for
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
one has
\langle\lambda(f)\delta_{\gamma_{1}},\gamma_{2}\rangle=f(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{-1})
, in
order to establish the formula in the statement we need to
show that

z^{m}a(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{-1})=z^{c(\gamma_{2})-c(\gamma_{1})}a(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{-1}).

Note that both expressions are zero unless
c(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{-1})=m
. Since
c(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{-1})=c(\gamma_{2})-c(\gamma_{1})
, the
identity follows.
∎

It follows from the lemma that the gauge action of
\mathbb{T}
on
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
induces a well-defined gauge action of
\mathbb{T}
on the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
.

Recall that if
T\colon{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathrm{Aut}}(A)
is a circle action on
a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, then there is a canonical faithful condition expectation
E\colon A\to A^{\mathbb{T}}
given by
E(a)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}T_{z}(a)\,dz
, where the integration is with respect to the normalized Haar measure. If
\mathcal{G}
is an étale groupoid and
\mathcal{H}
is an open subgroupoid, then
C_{c}(\mathcal{H})
embeds in
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
via extension by zero and the closure of
C_{c}(\mathcal{H})
in
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
is isomorphic to
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{H})
; see
[
Phi05
, Proposition 1.9]
, which does not use the blanket Hausdorff assumption.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},c)
be a graded groupoid, and let
T\colon{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathrm{Aut}}(C^{*}(\mathcal{G}))
be the gauge action defined in

Section 3
. Then

C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})^{\mathbb{T}}=C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{0}).

In particular, any element in
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})^{\mathbb{T}}\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
is supported on
\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

Proof.

We identify
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{0})
with
\overline{C_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{0})}\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
.
Given
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{m})
, it follows from
Lemma 3.2
that

E(f)=\begin{cases}f,&\text{if}\ m=0\\
0,&\text{else.}\end{cases}.

In particular, we deduce that
C_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{0})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})^{\mathbb{T}}
and hence
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{0})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})^{\mathbb{T}}
. For the converse inclusion, given
a\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})^{\mathbb{T}}
, find
a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
C_{c}(\mathcal{G})
which converges
to
a
in
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
. Then
a=E(a)=\lim E(f_{n})
and
E(f_{n})\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{0})
by the above formula, whence
a\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{0})
. ∎

Next, we show that for, graded groupoids, the essential ideal is always gauge-invariant.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a second countable graded étale groupoid. Then,
for every
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
, we have
T_{z}(I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G}))=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})
. Moreover,

E(I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G}))=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G}_{0})=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})\cap C^{*}(\mathcal{G})^{\mathbb{T}}.

Proof.

It follows from
Lemma 3.2
that the strict support of
a
and
T_{z}(a)
coincide, and hence
a\in I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})
if and only if
T_{z}(a)\in I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})
.

For the last statement, since elements of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})^{\mathbb{T}}=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{0})
are supported on
\mathcal{G}_{0}
by
Section 3
, and as
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is clopen in
\mathcal{G}
, it follows that
I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G}_{0})=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})\cap C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{0})
. Therefore,
I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G}_{0})\subseteq E(I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G}))
. But the first statement
together with
the fact that
E
is given by
E(a)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}T_{z}(a)dz
for all
a\in C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
, with integration with respect to the normalized Haar measure, implies that
E(I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G}))\subseteq I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})\cap C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})^{\mathbb{T}}=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G})\cap C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{0})=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{G}_{0})
, and the second statement follows.
∎

4.
Preliminaries on contracting self-similar groups

Given a set
{\mathsf{X}}
, which we regard as an alphabet,
we write
{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
for the set of all infinite words
\mathsf{x}_{1}\mathsf{x}_{2}\cdots
with
\mathsf{x}_{n}\in{\mathsf{X}}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Similarly, we
let
{\mathsf{X}}^{\ast}
(respectively,
{\mathsf{X}}^{+}
) denote the set of all finite (respectively, finite non-empty) words on
{\mathsf{X}}
. We equip
{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
with the metric
d(u,v)=2^{-|u\wedge v|}
where
u\wedge v
is the longest common prefix of
u,v
.

Since every closed ball in
{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
is equal to the set
u{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
of sequences with a given prefix
u\in{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
, every isometry
f
of
{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
induces a permutation of
{\mathsf{X}}^{\ast}
. This permutation preserves inclusion of balls, hence it is an automorphism of the rooted tree with the set of vertices
{\mathsf{X}}^{\ast}
, and edges of the form
\{v,vx\}
for
v\in{\mathsf{X}}^{\ast}
and
x\in{\mathsf{X}}
, i.e., the Cayley graph of
{\mathsf{X}}^{*}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a discrete group, let
{\mathsf{X}}
be a finite set and let
G\curvearrowright{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
be a faithful action by isometries. There is the induced action of
G
on
{\mathsf{X}}^{*}
.

We say that
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is
self-similar

if, for all
g\in G
and all
\mathsf{x}\in{\mathsf{X}}
, there exists
g|_{\mathsf{x}}\in G

such that for all
w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
,
we have
g(\mathsf{x}w)=g(\mathsf{x})g|_{\mathsf{x}}(w)
. We can define inductively
g|_{w}
for
w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{*}
by
g|_{\mathsf{x}u}=(g|_{\mathsf{x}})|_{u}
, and
g|_{\emptyset}=g
for the empty word. Then
g(vu)=g(v)g|_{v}(u)
for all
v,u\in{\mathsf{X}}^{*}
.

We moreover say that
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is
contracting
if there exists
a finite subset
N\subseteq G
with the following properties:

(1)

For every
n\in N
and for all
\mathsf{x}\in{\mathsf{X}}
,
we have
n|_{\mathsf{x}}\in N
.

(2)

For all
g\in G
there exists
m\geq 0
such that

g|_{w}\in N
for all
w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{m}
, that is,
g|_{{\mathsf{X}}^{m}}\subseteq N
.

If
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is contracting and self-similar, then there is a smallest finite subset
\mathcal{N}\subseteq G
satisfying properties (a) and (b) above called the
nucleus
of
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
.

Note that the elements
g|_{\mathsf{x}}
in the definition above must be unique, since the action
G\curvearrowright{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
is assumed to be faithful. We refer the reader to
[
Nek05
]
for more details on self-similar groups.

The second named author defined
C^{*}

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
be a self-similar group. We define the
\ast
-algebra
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
to be the universal

\ast
-algebra
generated by
\{\mathsf{u}_{g}\colon g\in G\}\cup\{s_{\mathsf{x}}\colon\mathsf{x}\in{\mathsf{X}}\}
, subject to the relations

(1)

the map
g\mapsto\mathsf{u}_{g}
is a unitary representation of
G
;

(2)

\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{\mathsf{x}}=s_{g(\mathsf{x})}\mathsf{u}_{g|_{\mathsf{x}}}
for all
g\in G
and all
\mathsf{x}\in{\mathsf{X}}
;

(CK1)

s_{\mathsf{x}}^{*}s_{\mathsf{y}}=\delta_{\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y}}
for all
\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y}\in{\mathsf{X}}
;

(CK2)

\sum_{\mathsf{x}\in{\mathsf{X}}}s_{\mathsf{x}}s_{\mathsf{x}}^{*}=1
.

We also define the C*-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
of
(G,{\mathsf{X}})

to be the universal C*-algebra with the above generators and relations.

In the case that
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is contracting, it is known that
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
and
\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
are finitely presented and depend only on the nucleus
\mathcal{N}
(as a set of isometries of
{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
)
[
Nek09
,
SS23
]
.

Example 4.3
.

When
G
is the trivial group, the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is the Leavitt algebra
L_{|{\mathsf{X}}|}
, and the C*-algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is the Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{|{\mathsf{X}}|}
.

The algebras defined above admit natural
ample groupoid models, which
we proceed to describe. Let
S(G,{\mathsf{X}})
be the
\ast
-semigroup with zero given by the generators and the relations (1), (2) and (CK1). Then
S(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is an inverse semigroup and each of its nonzero elements can be uniquely expressed in the form
s_{v}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{w}^{*}
with
v,w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{\ast}
. We let
S(G,{\mathsf{X}})
act on
{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
canonically, where the domain of

s_{v}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{w}^{*}
is the clopen set
w{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
,
and the corresponding partial homeomorphism maps
w\rho

to
v(g(\rho))
.

Definition 4.4
.

Given a self-similar group
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, we let
\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})

be the groupoid of germs of the inverse semigroup action
S(G,{\mathsf{X}})\curvearrowright{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
described above.

It is clear from the construction that
\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is
ample, minimal and effective
[
Nek09
,
EP17
]
. It is second countable if
G
is countable.
It may, however, fail to be Hausdorff. Moreover,
we have
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\cong{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
and

C^{*}(\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}}))\cong{\mathcal{O}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, both canonically
[
Nek09
,
EP17
,
SS23
]
.

The isomorphism maps
\mathsf{u}_{g}
and
s_{\mathsf{x}}
to the characteristic functions of the sets of germs of the transformations
w\mapsto g(w)
and
w\mapsto\mathsf{x}w
, respectively. We will identify the generators with the corresponding elements of the algebra.

When
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is contracting it is known that
\mathcal{(}G,{\mathsf{X}})
is amenable, and hence
{\mathcal{O}}(G,X)\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}}))

[
Nek09
]
. (The argument of
[
Nek09
]
, given for the Hausdorff case only, extends to the general case by the results of J. Renault
[
Ren15
]
; also, the self-replicating hypothesis in
[
Nek09
]
is unnecessary, cf.
[
MS24
]
).

There is also a canonical grading on
\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
,
which gives rise to a gauge action by the discussion in
the previous section.

Definition 4.5
.

Given a self-similar group
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, we let
c\colon\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be the continuous cocycle given by

c([s_{v}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{w}^{*},\rho])=|v|-|w|
, for all germs

[s_{v}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{w}^{*},\rho]\in\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
.

Remark 4.6
.

Under the natural identification of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}}))
with

\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, one can check that the gauge action from

Section 3
is given by

T_{z}(\mathsf{u}_{g})=\mathsf{u}_{g}
and
T_{z}(s_{\mathsf{x}})=zs_{\mathsf{x}}
for all
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
, all
g\in G
and all
x\in{\mathsf{X}}
. Also, identifying
\mathbb{C}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
with
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, the element
s_{u}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{v}^{*}
is homogeneous of degree
|u|-|v|
.

The subalgebra
A=\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})^{\mathbb{T}}

of
\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
consisting
of gauge-invariant elements, and its algebraic analogue the homogeneous component
A_{0}=\mathbb{C}(G,{\mathsf{X}})_{0}
, will play an important role for us. Note that
A_{0}
and

are just the complex and
C^{*}
-algebras of the ample groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})_{0}
, and hence
A_{0}
is dense in
A
.

Remark 4.7
.

The algebras
A
and
A_{0}
admit the following descriptions, due to the second named author. We shall not formally use these descriptions but they explain the main idea well.
We set
d=|{\mathsf{X}}|
, and recall that there is a canonical
unitary representation of
G
on
M_{d}(C^{*}(G))

given by

\mu(g)_{\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y}}=\begin{cases}g|_{\mathsf{y}},&\text{if}\ \mathsf{x}=g(\mathsf{y}),\\
0,&\text{else.}\end{cases}

for all
g\in G
. By integrating this representation to a
homomorphism
\mu\colon C^{*}(G)\to M_{d}(C^{*}(G))
,
and tensoring this map with the identity on suitable matrix
algebras, we get an inductive system

C^{*}(G)\to M_{d}(C^{*}(G))\to M_{d^{2}}(C^{*}(G))\to\cdots,

and
A
is the
C^{*}
-algebraic direct limit. Also,
A_{0}
is the
\ast
-algebraic direct limit of

{\mathbb{C}}G\to M_{d}({\mathbb{C}}G)\to M_{d^{2}}({\mathbb{C}}G)\to\cdots,

which is a dense subalgebra of
A
.

Suppose that
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is contracting with nucleus
\mathcal{N}
and write
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{N}
for the span of
\mathcal{N}
in
{\mathbb{C}}G
. Adopt the notation used in the remark above.
If
a\in\mathbb{C}\mathcal{N}
, then
\mu(a)\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}\mathcal{N})
since
\mathcal{N}
is closed under the operation
g\mapsto g|_{\mathsf{x}}
. Moreover, if
a\in\mathbb{C}G
, then since
a
has finite support, by the definition of contraction, we can find an integer
n
with the image of
a
in
M_{d^{n}}(\mathbb{C}\mathcal{N})
. Thus
A_{0}
is the direct limit of the inductive system of vector spaces

{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\to M_{d}({\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N})\to M_{d^{2}}({\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N})\to\cdots.

The map
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, and consequently the linear maps in the sequence above, are not injective in general. From now on, we will denote by
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}
the image of the span of
\mathcal{N}
in
\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
.

In this notation the maps in the sequence above are injective, and
A_{0}
is the corresponding increasing union of the subspaces
M_{d^{n}}({\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N})
.
The intuition is then that, for any
a\in A
, the entries of
a
, as you move along the inductive system, accumulate in
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{N}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

The essential ideals of
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}}))
and
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})_{0})
are generated in the respective algebras by the intersection of the essential ideal with
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

The fibers
c^{-1}(n)
of the cocycle
c\colon\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
are pairwise disjoint and open. Consequently, an element
f\in{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
belongs to the algebraic essential ideal
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}}))
if and only if all its restrictions to the fibers
c^{-1}(n)
belong to it (as only finitely many of them will be non-zero).

Similarly, if
n>0
, then the sets
s_{v}\cdot\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})_{0}
, for
v\in{\mathsf{X}}^{n}
, of germs of transformations of the form
s_{vu_{1}}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{u_{2}}^{*}
for
|u_{1}|=|u_{2}|
are also open and disjoint. Consequently, an element of
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
supported on
c^{-1}(n)
belongs to the essential ideal if and only if it is equal to a sum
\sum_{v\in{\mathsf{X}}^{n}}s_{v}f_{v}
for
f_{v}
belonging to the intersection of the essential ideal with
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})_{0}
.

Similar arguments show that that an element
f\in{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
supported on
c^{-1}(n)
for
n<0
belongs to the essential ideal if and only if it is
f=\sum_{v\in{\mathsf{X}}^{n}}f_{v}s_{v}^{*}
for
f_{v}
belonging to the intersection of the essential ideal with
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})_{0}
.

This proves that the essential ideal of
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is generated in
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
by the essential ideal of
A_{0}={\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})_{0}
.

For every element
f\in A_{0}
there exists
n
such that
f=\sum_{u,v\in{\mathsf{X}}^{n}}s_{u}f_{u,v}s_{v}^{*}
for some
f_{u,v}\in{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}
. Since
f_{u,v}=s_{u}^{*}fs_{v}
, the element
f
belongs to the essential ideal if and only if all elements
f_{u,v}
belong to it. This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.
Simplicity of
{\mathcal{O}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is equivalent
to simplicity of
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})

For a self-similar group
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, we write

I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
and

I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
for the essential ideals in
the respective groupoid algebras of
\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
.
Since
\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is minimal and effective, it follows that
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is simple if and only if

I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})=\{0\}
, and

{\mathcal{O}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is simple if and only if

I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})=\{0\}
, the latter assuming that
G
is either countable and amenable or contracting.

Since

I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})=I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\cap{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, it follows that if
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is not simple, then
neither is
\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
. In this section, we want to prove the
converse for contracting groups, namely that if
\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is not simple, then
neither is
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
.

Note that
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is a finite-dimensional vector space, which we regard as a normed vector space with the norm inherited from its inclusion in
\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
be a contracting self-similar group
with nucleus
\mathcal{N}
, and use the notation from
Section 4
.
Given
a\in A
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

d(s_{v}^{*}as_{w},{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N})<\varepsilon

for all
v,w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{n}
with
n\geq n_{0}
.

Proof.

As
A_{0}
is dense in
A
, we may find
b\in A_{0}
with
\|a-b\|<\varepsilon
. Find scalars
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, group elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\in G
and finite words
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
with
|w_{j}|=|v_{j}|
such that
b=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}s_{w_{j}}\mathsf{u}_{g_{j}}s_{v_{j}}^{*}
. We claim that we can choose the words so that
|w_{1}|=\cdots=|w_{k}|
. Indeed, assume without loss of generality that
|w_{1}|\geq|w_{j}|
for all
j=2,\ldots,k
and
fix such a
j
. By (CK2), we have

s_{w_{j}}\mathsf{u}_{g_{j}}s_{v_{j}}^{*}=s_{w_{j}}\mathsf{u}_{g_{j}}\sum_{|w|=|w_{1}|-|w_{j}|}s_{w}s_{w}^{*}s_{v_{j}}^{*}=\sum_{|\mu|=|w_{1}|-|w_{j}|}s_{w_{j}}s_{g_{j}(\mu)}\mathsf{u}_{g_{j}|_{\mu}}s_{w}^{*}s_{v_{j}}^{*},

and observe that
w_{j}g_{j}(\mu)
and
v_{j}\mu
have the same length as
w_{1}
. An iteration of this procedure then establishes the claim.

Since
G
is contracting, there is
m\geq 0
such that
g_{j}|_{{\mathsf{X}}^{m}}

belongs to
\mathcal{N}
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
. Set
n_{0}=|w_{1}|+m
.
Let
n\geq n_{0}
and let
v,w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{n}
.
Then
s_{v}^{*}bs_{w}
belongs to
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}
,
and moreover

\|s_{v}^{*}as_{w}-s_{v}^{*}bs_{w}\|\leq\|s_{v}\|\|a-b\|\|s_{w}\|<\varepsilon,

and thus
d(s_{v}^{*}as_{w},{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N})<\varepsilon
as desired.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
be a contracting self-similar group.
Then
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is simple if and only if

{\mathcal{O}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is simple, if and only if
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\cap I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\neq 0
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that if
\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is not simple, then

{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\cap I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\neq 0
.
The proof consists in producing sequences
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and

(c_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with the following properties:

(1)

a_{n}\in I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
;

(2)

c_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
;

(3)

\|a_{n}-c_{n}\|<\frac{1}{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
;

(4)

\varepsilon_{0}\leq\|a_{n}\|\leq\|a\|
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.

Assuming that sequences as above exist, let us show that

{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\cap I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is nontrivial.
Since
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}
is finite dimensional, it follows from
conditions (2), (3) and (4) above that there is a
nonzero accumulation point
c\in{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})

for
(c_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. By (3),
c

is also an accumulation point for
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, and therefore
belongs to
I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
by (1). Thus
c
is a nonzero
element in
{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\cap I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\cap{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})={\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}\cap I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
, as desired.

Now we construct those sequences, assuming
that
\mathcal{O}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is not simple. Find
a nonzero positive element
a\in I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
.
Then
E(a)\in I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
by
Lemma 3.4
,
and
E(a)\neq 0
because
E
is faithful.
Thus, upon replacing

a
with
E(a)
, we may assume that
a\in I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\cap A
.
Since
\lambda(a)\neq 0
, we can find a unit
x\in\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})^{0}
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})_{x}
with
a(\gamma)=\langle\lambda_{x}(a)\delta_{x},\delta_{\gamma}\rangle\neq 0
, and
set
\varepsilon_{0}=|a(\gamma)|
. Let
x=[1,\rho]\in\mathcal{G}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
with
\rho\in{\mathsf{X}}^{\omega}
and
\gamma=[s_{u}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{v}^{*},\rho]
a germ. In particular, note that
\rho=v\rho^{\prime}
.

Given
n\geq 1
, use
Lemma 5.1
to find
m
such that
d(s_{w}^{*}as_{z},\mathbb{C}\mathcal{N})<\frac{1}{n}
for all words
w,z
of length at least
m
.
Let
v_{m}
be the prefix of length
m
of
\rho^{\prime}
and set
u_{m}=g(v_{m})
, and note that
|u_{m}|=|v_{m}|
.
Define
a_{n}=s_{uu_{m}}^{*}as_{vv_{m}}
, which belongs to
I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
because
I_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is an ideal. Since
|uu_{m}|=|vv_{m}|\geq m
, there exists
c_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}

such that
\|a_{n}-c_{n}\|<\tfrac{1}{n}
. Note that

[s_{u}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{v}^{*},\rho]=[s_{u}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{v}^{*}s_{vv_{m}}s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]=[s_{u}\mathsf{u}_{g}s_{v_{m}}s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]=[s_{uu_{m}}\mathsf{u}_{g|_{v_{m}}}s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho].

Therefore, we
we compute

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{x}(a_{n})\delta_{[s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]},\delta_{[\mathsf{u}_{g|_{v_{m}}}s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda_{x}(s_{uu_{m}})^{*}\lambda_{x}(a)\lambda_{x}(s_{vv_{m}})\delta_{[s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]},\delta_{[\mathsf{u}_{g|_{v_{m}}}s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda_{x}(a)\delta_{[1,\rho]},\lambda_{x}(s_{uu_{m}})\delta_{[u_{g|_{v_{m}}}s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda_{x}(a)\delta_{[1,\rho]},\delta_{[s_{uu_{m}}\mathsf{u}_{g|_{v_{m}}}s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]}\rangle=\varepsilon_{0}.

Using that
s_{u}
and

are isometries at the last step, the Cauchy-Schwarz lemma then implies that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle=|\langle\lambda_{x}(a_{n})\delta_{[s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]},\delta_{[\mathsf{u}_{g|_{v_{m}}}s_{vv_{m}}^{*},\rho]}\rangle|

6.
A simplification of the simplicity algorithm

In
[
SS23
]
, the third author and Szakaćs gave an algorithm to determine if
\mathbb{C}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
is simple given the nucleus as input. We give here a simplification of the algorithm that runs in exponential time in the size of the nucleus, which is a significant improvement on the runtime in
[
SS23
]
.
Let
(G,{\mathsf{X}})
be a contracting self-similar group with nucleus
\mathcal{N}
. It is shown in
[
SS23
, Corollary 5.8]
that if
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})\neq 0
, then there is an element
a\in{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{N}
that represents a nonzero element of
I_{\mathrm{ess}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
. We will narrow this down to elements supported on certain finite subgroups of
\mathcal{N}
.

Associated to the nucleus is a finite automaton with state set
\mathcal{N}
and alphabet
{\mathsf{X}}
. The edges are of the from
g\xrightarrow{\mathsf{x}\mid g(\mathsf{x})}g|_{\mathsf{x}}
. Given
w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{*}
and
g\in\mathcal{N}
, there is a unique path starting at
g
whose left-hand side is labeled by
w
. This path ends at
g|_{w}
and the right-hand side of the label of the path is
g(w)
. Consider the subgraph
\mathcal{H}
obtained by removing all edges save those of the form
g\xrightarrow{\mathsf{x}\mid\mathsf{x}}g|_{\mathsf{x}}
(which we now view as being labeled by
\mathsf{x}
). Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
consist of those elements on some nonempty directed cycle of
\mathcal{H}
. Note that if
g\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mathsf{x}
labels an edge of a directed cycle of
\mathcal{H}
at
g
, then
g|_{\mathsf{x}}\in\mathcal{C}
. Let us write
e
for the identity of
G
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{+}
, and put
H_{w}=\{g\in G\mid g(w)=w,g|_{w}=g\}
. Then
H_{w}
is a finite subgroup contained in
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

Note that
H_{w}
is a subgroup. Indeed, trivially
e\in H_{w}
, and if
g,h\in H_{w}
, then
gh(w)=w
and
(gh)|_{w}=g|_{h(w)}h|_{w}=g|_{w}h|_{w}=gh
, and so
gh\in H_{w}
. Finally, if
g\in H_{w}
, then
g^{-1}(w)=w
and
g^{-1}|_{w}=(g|_{g^{-1}(w)})^{-1}=g^{-1}
.

Observe that if
g\in H_{w}
, then
g\in\mathcal{N}
as
g|_{w^{n}}=g
for all
n\geq 0
.
Moreover,
w
labels a nonempty closed path in
\mathcal{H}
at
g
, and hence
g
lies on a directed cycle, that is,
g\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Note
g\in H_{w}
if and only if
g\in\mathcal{C}
, and
w
labels a closed path in
\mathcal{H}
at
g
.

It turns out that every element of
{\mathbb{C}}H_{w}
is nontrivial in
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
0\neq a\in{\mathbb{C}}H_{w}
with
w\in{\mathsf{X}}^{+}
. Then
a
is nonzero in
{\mathbb{C}}(G,{\mathsf{X}})

(2)

in

.

We have already seen the equivalence of (1) and (2) in

. For the equivalence of (1) and (3), we first observe that

if and only if

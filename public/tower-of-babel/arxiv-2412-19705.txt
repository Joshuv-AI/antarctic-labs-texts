Noise Sensitivity of the Semidefinite Programs for Direct Data-Driven LQR
Xiong Zeng, Laurent Bako, Necmiye Ozay

Abstract
In this paper, we study the noise sensitivity of the semidefinite program (SDP) proposed for direct data-driven infinite-horizon linear quadratic regulator (LQR) problem for discrete-time linear time-invariant systems. While this SDP is shown to find the true LQR controller in the noise-free setting, we show that it leads to a trivial solution with zero gain matrices when data is corrupted by noise, even when the noise is arbitrarily small. We then study a variant of the SDP that includes a robustness promoting regularization term and prove that regularization does not fully eliminate the sensitivity issue. In particular, the solution of the regularized SDP converges in probability also to a trivial solution.



I
Introduction

Certainty equivalence approach and robust control approach are two alternative paradigms in learning-based control. Roughly speaking, in certainty equivalence, we
pretend
that our data is not corrupted by noise, the estimated model is the true system model, or the estimated control policy is designed based on the true system and clean data. Whereas, in robust control approach, we try to bound the effect of the noise in the data and aim to find a controller that achieves the desired properties for all possible noise values within this bound.

These two different paradigms can be applied both in the model-based setting, where system identification is followed by control design, or in direct data-driven control, where data is used directly to synthesize a controller utilizing ideas from the behavioral system theory (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
]
).
In the context of model-based LQR, Mania et al.
[
3
]
show that certainty equivalence is statistically consistent and is more sample-efficient than the robust approach given in
[
4
]
. The success of certainty equivalent control, in this case, lies in the fact that there is some inherent robustness in the solutions of the Riccati equations with respect to perturbations in system matrices. That is, small perturbations in system matrices result in small changes in the corresponding optimal controller (cf., Fig.
1
).

. In this paper, we show that the direct data-driven LQR algorithm is discontinuous and even with arbitrary small noise (in almost all directions), the resulting gain reduces to the trivial gain of zero.

A natural question is how the certainty equivalence approach and a robust control approach compare in terms of statistical properties for direct data-driven LQR. Several works consider a robust approach for direct data-driven control for different control objectives or noise settings (e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
). On the other hand, De Persis and Tesi
[
8
]
analyze a certainty equivalent approach to direct data-driven LQR, where they provide a sufficient condition for stabilizability and regularization techniques for improving noise robustness. Here, the certainty equivalence approach amounts to using the noisy data directly in the semidefinite programs developed for the noise-free case. It is observed in
[
8
]
that even small noise can lead to a violation of their proposed sufficient condition for stabilizability and the semidefinite program may favor low gain solutions. However, a thorough understanding of the noise sensitivity and statistical properties of these semidefinite programs for direct data-driven LQR is missing. In this paper, we show that the semidefinite program for direct data-driven LQR is very sensitive to noise and yields, with probability one,
trivial control gains
independent of the data even when there is an arbitrarily small amount of noise. Moreover, the robustified version also suffers from a similar issue as the length of the data trajectory used in the program goes to infinity. Therefore, neither of these approaches is statistically consistent.

It is worth noting that there are recent works that propose alternative optimization formulations for direct data-driven control
[
9
,
10
]
, solutions of which mimic the solution of the model-based certainty equivalent control. Since the controllers synthesized by these alternative formulations are equivalent to model-based certainty equivalent control, they inherit the nice statistical consistency properties of the former. Our analysis does not pertain to these alternative formulations. Similarly, our results are not directly related to the finite-horizon data-driven control problems for which some connections with model-based approaches are established
[
11
,
12
]
since we focus on approximation of the infinite-horizon LQR gain.

A preliminary version of this paper has been submitted to
[
13
]
. Compared with the conference version, we extend the results of certainty equivalent DDD LQR from scalar systems to multivariate systems and we also show that when the length of the data trajectory approaches infinity, the robustness promoting DDD LQR from
[
8
]
will also yield a zero state feedback gain estimate.

II
Preliminaries

II-A

Probability fundamentals

Definition 1

(Convergence in Probability)
.

A sequence
\left\{X(T)\right\}_{T=0}^{\infty}
of random variables converges in probability towards the random variable
X
if for all
\delta>0

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X(T)-X\right|>\delta\right)=0,

which is denoted as
X(T)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}X
for short.

The definition trivially generalizes to vector- and matrix-valued random variables by considering them element-wise.

II-B

Results from Data-Driven Control

The following notion will be relevant in establishing our results.

Definition 2

(Persistency of Excitation)
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. A sequence
\mathbf{F}_{0}({T)}\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times T}
is called persistently exciting of order
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
if the Hankel matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}\left(\mathbf{F}_{0}({T)}\right):=

(1)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathbf{f}_{0}&\mathbf{f}_{1}&\cdots&\mathbf{f}_{T-k}\\
\mathbf{f}_{1}&\mathbf{f}_{2}&\cdots&\mathbf{f}_{T-k+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbf{f}_{k-1}&\mathbf{f}_{k}&\cdots&\mathbf{f}_{T-1}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{ks\times(T-k+1)},

has full row rank, i.e.,

III
Direct Data-Driven (DDD) LQR

We consider the following discrete-time linear time-invariant (LTI) system in this paper:

\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{t}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{w}_{t},

(2)

where
\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbf{u}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mathbf{w}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are the state, input, and noise at time
t
, respectively. We assume that the initial state and the noise are independent random variables with distributions satisfying
\mathbf{w}_{t}\overset{i.i.d.}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{w}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{n})
,
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{0}]=0
, and
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{0}^{2}]=\sigma^{2}_{x_{0}}\mathbf{I}_{n}
.
The system parameters
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are unknown. The standard LQR problem for the LTI system (
2
) is

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}_{t}+\mathbf{u}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u}_{t}\right)\right]

(3)

\displaystyle\text{ s.t. }\text{Dynamics in}\;\eqref{LTI},

where

\mathbf{Q}\succ 0
,
\mathbf{R}\succ 0
, and
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expectation over the randomness from the initial state
\mathbf{x}_{0}
and the process noise
\mathbf{w}_{t}
. We assume
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is controllable throughout the paper.
When
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are known, this LQR problem can be solved by finding the positive definite solution
\mathbf{P}
of the discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation:

\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{B}\left(\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{B}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{Q}.

(4)

Then, the solution of (
3
) is
\mathbf{u}_{t}=-\mathbf{K}_{\lqr}\mathbf{x}_{t}
with the optimal state feedback gain given by

\mathbf{K}_{\lqr}=-\left(\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{B}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{A}.

(5)

In direct data-driven control, the parameters
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are unknown and the goal is to directly estimate
\mathbf{K}_{\lqr}
from data without explicitly estimating
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
. We assume the data is collected offline by driving the system with a random input such that
\mathbf{u}_{t}\overset{i.i.d.}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{u}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{m})
with
\sigma_{u}>0
, and this input is independent of the noise process and the initial condition. Let us define the data matrices formed from this trajectory data collected over some horizon
T
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{0}(T)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{llll}\mathbf{x}_{0}&\mathbf{x}_{1}&\ldots&\mathbf{x}_{T-1}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times T},

(6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{0}(T)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{llll}\mathbf{u}_{0}&\mathbf{u}_{1}&\ldots&\mathbf{u}_{T-1}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times T},

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{llll}\mathbf{x}_{1}&\mathbf{x}_{2}&\ldots&\mathbf{x}_{T}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times T}.

We similarly define
\mathbf{W}_{0}(T)=\left[\begin{array}[]{llll}\mathbf{w}_{0}&\mathbf{w}_{1}&\ldots&\mathbf{w}_{T-1}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times T}
for the (unknown) noise sequence.
Most of the time, when
T
is clear from context, we will drop it and use
\mathbf{X}_{0}
,
\mathbf{U}_{0}
,
\mathbf{X}_{1}
, and
\mathbf{W}_{0}
to indicate the above quantities.

De Persis and Tesi
[
1
]
proposed a semidefinite program, called the DDD LQR, to estimate the optimal LQR gain:

\begin{array}[]{cl}\underset{\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}}{\operatorname{minimize}}&\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)+\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{X}\right)\\
\text{ subject to }&\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{I}_{n}&\mathbf{X}_{1}\mathbf{Y}\\
\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{\top}&\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\end{array}\right]\succeq 0\\
&\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{X}&\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\\
\left(\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{\top}&\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\end{array}\right]\succeq 0,\end{array}

(7)

where
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
and
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times n}
. If
\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}
denotes an optimal solution of (
7
), then the LQR gain estimate is given by

\mathbf{K}_{ce}:=-\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce})^{-1}.

(8)

The following result from
[
1
]
shows that when the system does not have any noise (i.e.,
\mathbf{w}_{t}=0
for all
t
) and a persistency of excitation condition holds (which can be shown to hold with probability
1
when the input is Gaussian as assumed), we have
\mathbf{K}_{ce}=\mathbf{K}_{\lqr}
.

Theorem 1

(Theorem 4 in
[
1
]
)
.

Consider the data matrices
\mathbf{X}_{0}
,
\mathbf{U}_{0}
,
\mathbf{X}_{1}
defined above and the feedback gain (
8
).
If
\operatorname{rank}\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{X}_{0}\\
\mathbf{U}_{0}\end{array}\right]\right)=m+n
and
\mathbf{w}_{t}=0
for all
t
, then
\mathbf{K}_{ce}=\mathbf{K}_{\lqr}
.

The estimate
\mathbf{K}_{ce}
is also known as a certainty equivalent DDD LQR solution
[
8
,
1
]
. While the quality of the estimate
\mathbf{K}_{ce}
is not well-understood in the noisy setting in general, the following sufficient condition is derived in
[
8
]
to ensure that the gain
\mathbf{K}_{ce}
is stabilizing.

Lemma 1

(Lemma 4 in
[
8
]
)
.

Consider an optimal solution
\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}
of (
7
).
Let

\mathbf{M}:=\mathbf{Y}_{ce}^{*}(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}_{ce}^{*})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{ce}^{*}\right)^{\top},

and

\Psi:=\mathbf{W}_{0}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{W}_{0}^{\top}-\mathbf{X}_{1}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{W}_{0}^{\top}-\mathbf{W}_{0}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{\top}.

If there exists
\eta\geq 1
such that

\Psi\preceq\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\mathbf{I}_{n},

then the state feedback gain
\mathbf{K}_{ce}
from (
8
) is stabilizing for the system (
2
).

Our first main result (Theorem
2
) is to show that even when the noise is arbitrarily small,
\mathbf{K}_{ce}
can be an arbitrarily bad estimate.
We will also provide an interpretation of the above sufficient condition for stabilization in light of our Theorem
2
.

Given the observation that
\mathbf{K}_{ce}
might not always give reasonable results with noisy data, in
[
8
]
, a regularized version of the problem control synthesis problem is proposed to increase the robustness of the DDD approach to noise. In particular, the DDD LQR with robustness promoting regularization is formulated as the following semidefinite program
[
8
]
:

\begin{array}[]{cl}\underset{\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{S}}{\operatorname{minimize}}&\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)+\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{X}\right)+\eta\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{S}\right)\\
\text{ subject to }&\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{I}_{n}&\mathbf{X}_{1}\mathbf{Y}\\
\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{\top}&\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\end{array}\right]\succeq 0\\
&\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{X}&\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\\
\left(\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{\top}&\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\end{array}\right]\succeq 0\\
&\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{S}&\mathbf{Y}\\
\mathbf{Y}^{\top}&\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\end{array}\right]\succeq 0,\end{array}

(9)

where
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m},\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times n}
,
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times T}
, and
\eta
is a positive regularization constant. Similarly, the data-driven optimal feedback gain based on this optimization problem is

\mathbf{K}_{rp}:=-\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{rp}\left(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{rp}\right)^{-1},

(10)

where
\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{rp}
is an optimal solution of (
9
).

Our second main result (Theorem
21
) is to show that as the number of data points (i.e.,
T
) goes to infinity,
\mathbf{K}_{rp}
can also be an arbitrarily bad estimate of the optimal LQR feedback gain
\mathbf{K}_{\lqr}
when the system is subject to noise.

IV
Certainty Equivalent (CE) DDD LQR

IV-A

Inconsistency of CE DDD LQR

Our first main result shows that in the presence of noise, with probability

Theorem 2
.

Consider the LTI system in (
2

Assume

Then, for all optimal solutions
\mathbf{Y}_{ce}^{*}

given in (

\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathbf{K}_{ce}=\mathbf{0}_{m\times n})=1,

where the probability

is with respect to the randomness of

,
\mathbf{U}_{0}(T)
, and
\mathbf{W}_{0}(T)

Based on Theorem

By Schur complement, we can rewrite the first constraint in (
7
) as

\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{I}_{n}\succeq 0
and
\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{X}_{1}\mathbf{Y}\left(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{\top}\succeq 0
. Similarly, the second constraint can be written as
\mathbf{X}\succeq 0
and
\mathbf{X}-\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\left(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{-1}(\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y})^{\top}\succeq 0.
Since
\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{X}\right)
is being minimized, by the last inequality, the following will be satisfied at the optimal solution:

\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{X}\right)=\operatorname{trace}\left(\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\left(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{\top}\right).

Hence, we can remove
\mathbf{X}
from (
7
) to obtain an equivalent optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{Y}}{\operatorname{minimize}}

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)+

(18)

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}\left(\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\left(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ subject to }

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{I}_{n}\succeq 0

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{X}_{1}\mathbf{Y}\left(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{1}^{\top}\succeq 0,

where
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times n}
. For the simplicity of the notation, we use
O_{ce}\left(\mathbf{Y}\right)
to denote the objective function of the above optimization problem.

Given
\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{I}_{n}\succeq 0
, we see that for any feasible solution
\mathbf{Y}
of (
18
), we have
\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)\geq\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right)
.
Moreover,

\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\left(\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}\right)^{-1}(\sqrt{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y})^{\top}\succeq 0
implies that

O_{ce}\left(\mathbf{Y}\right)\geq\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right)
for any feasible solution
\mathbf{Y}
of (
18
). Therefore, if there exists a feasible solution
\mathbf{Y}_{m}
such that
O_{ce}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{m}\right)=\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right)
, then
\mathbf{Y}_{m}
is an optimal solution of (
18
). Take a solution
\mathbf{Y}^{*}
of (
17
), then
two matrix inequality constraints of (
18
) are satisfied with equality and we also have

O_{ce}\left(\mathbf{Y}^{*}\right)=\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}\right)=\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{I}_{n}\right)=\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right).
Therefore, any solution of (
17
) is an optimal solution of (
18
) and the optimal objective value of (
18
) is
\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right)
.

In the second part of the proof, we will prove that any optimal solution
\mathbf{Y}_{ce}^{*}
of (
18
) must satisfy (
17
). Assume by contradiction that
\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}\neq\mathbf{I}_{n}
. According to the first matrix inequality constraint in (
18
),
\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}\neq\mathbf{I}_{n}
implies that there exists
\mathbf{C}_{1}\succeq 0
with
\mathbf{C}_{1}\neq 0
such that
\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}=\mathbf{C}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{n}
. Hence,

\displaystyle O_{ce}(\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce})\geq\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\left(\mathbf{C}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{n}\right)\right).

(19)

Since
\mathbf{Q}\succ 0
and
\mathbf{C}_{1}\succeq 0
with
\mathbf{C}_{1}\neq 0
, we have that
\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{C}_{1}\right)
is strictly positive. Then from (
19
), we have that
O_{ce}(\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce})>\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right),
which contradicts with the fact that the optimal value of (
18
) is
\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right)
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}=\mathbf{I}_{n}
must hold. Based on the second constraint in (
18
),
\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}=\mathbf{I}_{n}
implies that
\mathbf{X}_{1}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}=\mathbf{0}_{n\times n}
must hold. According to the objective function definition in (
18
) and the fact that the optimal objective value of (
18
) is
\operatorname{trace}\left(\mathbf{Q}\right)
, we necessarily have
\mathbf{U}_{0}\mathbf{Y}^{*}_{ce}=\mathbf{0}_{m\times n}
.
In conclusion, any optimal solution of (
18
) satisfies (
17
).
∎

When
\sigma_{w}=0,
the noise matrix
\mathbf{W}_{0}
in

becomes a zero matrix.
Therefore, Equation (

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c }\mathbf{X}_{0}\\
\mathbf{U}_{0}\\
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{0}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{U}_{0}\end{array}\right]\mathbf{Y}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c }\mathbf{I}_{n}\\
\mathbf{0}_{m\times n}\\
\mathbf{0}_{n\times n}\end{array}\right].

(20)

The above equation has a solution only if

Therefore, in the noiseless case, a trivial solution cannot be constructed by (

On the other hand, when there is noise, last block row of

will be perturbed by noise and for many noise distributions

will be full rank, leading to a zero gain as the output of the optimization problem (

We also note that

will have full row-rank with probability one. So, a trivial controller gain will be obtained for any such input/noise distribution as well.

Theorem 3

T
such that
\frac{C}{T-n}\left[\frac{\operatorname{trace}(\mathbf{Q})}{\underline{\sigma}(\mathbf{Q})}+\frac{C}{T-n}\right]\leq\delta
and
\bar{T}
be the minimum
T
such that
T\geq c\Lambda(m,n,T)
. Then, for all
T\geq\max(T^{*},\bar{T})
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{T}\left(\|\mathbf{K}_{rp}(T)\|^{2}_{2}>\delta\right)<\epsilon_{T}.

(41)

This implies, for all
\delta>0
,

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{T}\left(\|\mathbf{K}_{rp}(T)\|^{2}_{2}>\delta\right)=0,

(42)

that is,

\|\mathbf{K}_{rp}(T)\|^{2}_{2}\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0.
Hence,

\mathbf{K}_{rp}(T)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\mathbf{0}_{m\times n}.

∎

We note that the analysis in this section assumes a fixed regularization constant
\eta
for all horizons
T
. However, the bound in (
32
) suggests that scaling
\eta
with
T
can prevent the gain from collapsing to zero. This is investigated in the experiments in Section
VI-C
.

VII
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we provide statistical analysis for two direct data-driven LQR methods in the presence of noise. Our results indicate that these methods are not statistically consistent. Therefore a “certainty equivalence” approach that uses the original SDPs with noisy data is not appropriate. This is in contrast to model-based techniques, where certainty equivalence is known to be statistically consistent and sample-efficient. The identified limitations of the “certainty equivalence” approach in direct data-driven control underscores the necessity of robust direct data-driven control methods
[
5
]
. Our future work will focus on understanding the statistical properties of such methods.

In this lemma, we establish that continuous random variables, in particular Gaussian random variables, almost surely lead to persistently exciting inputs.

Lemma 8
.

Consider the input trajectory matrix
\mathbf{V}_{0}(T)
defined in (
13
) with
\sigma_{w}>0
. Let
T\geq(m+n)(n+1)+n
and define
\mathbf{H}_{T}:=\mathcal{H}_{n+1}\left(\mathbf{V}_{0}(T)\right)
. Then, we have

\mathbb{P}_{T}\left(\operatorname{rank}\left(\mathbf{H}_{T}\right)=(m+n)(n+1)\right)=1.

That is,
\mathbf{V}_{0}(T)
is persistently exciting of order
n+1
with probability one.

Proof.

Note that, by definition of the Hankel matrix,
\mathbf{H}_{T}
has
(m+n)(n+1)
rows and
T-n
columns.
We will show the rank condition holds for
T^{*}=(m+n)(n+1)+n
(i.e.,
\mathbf{H}_{T^{*}}
is a square matrix), which implies the same for any
T\geq T^{*}
by
\mathbf{H}_{T^{*}}
being the first
T^{*}
columns of
\mathbf{H}_{T}
. Let
g:=\det\left(\mathbf{H}_{T^{*}}\right).
Since determinant
g
is a non-constant polynomial function of the elements
\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{T^{*}-1},\mathbf{w}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{T^{*}-1}
of
\mathbf{V}_{0}(T^{*})
and all these basic random variables are continuous Gaussian random variables, the probability density function of
g
is also continuous. Then, we have that
\mathbb{P}_{T^{*}}(g=0)=\mathbb{P}_{T^{*}}(g\leq 0)-\mathbb{P}_{T^{*}}(g<0)=0.
This means that
\mathbb{P}_{T^{*}}(g\neq 0)=1
. Therefore,
\mathbf{H}_{T^{*}}
is full rank with probability one, that is,
\mathbb{P}_{T^{*}}\left(\text{rank}\left(\mathbf{H}_{T^{*}}\right)=(m+n)(n+1)\right)=1.

∎

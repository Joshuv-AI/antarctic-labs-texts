Classification of K-contact forms and spectral invariants of their sub-Laplacians
Eugenio Bellini

Abstract
. A contact form is called K-contact if its Reeb vector field is Killing with respect to some Riemannian metric. In this paper we classify K-contact forms whose Reeb vector field admits at least one non-periodic orbit, on three-dimensional manifolds. We prove that if a compact three-manifold carries such a contact form, then it is diffeomorphic to a lens space and admits exactly two periodic Reeb orbits, whose periods have irrational ratio. We further classify, up to (global) diffeomorphism, these contact forms in terms of the periods of their closed Reeb orbits. We conclude by relating these periods to spectral invariants of the sub-Laplacian, confirming a conjecture of [ 20 ] in the irregular K-contact case.



Mathematics Subject Classification (2020):

53C17 (primary), 53D10.

1.
Introduction

A smooth one-form
\alpha
on a three dimensional manifold
M
is called a contact form if
\alpha\wedge d\alpha\neq 0.
If
M
is oriented by
\alpha\wedge d\alpha
, then
\alpha
is called positive. To any contact form there is an associated vector field
R
called the Reeb field, uniquely determined by the following two equations

(2)

\alpha(R)=1,\qquad d\alpha(R,\cdot\,)=0.

Contact forms can be divided into two classes: quasi-regular and irregular
[
17
]
(the term
“quasi-regular”
is used instead of
“regular”
because the latter classically refers to Boothby-Wang contact forms
[
16
]
). A contact form is called quasi-regular if all the orbits of its Reeb vector field are periodic, and irregular if at least one Reeb orbit is non-periodic. Quasi-regular contact forms are completely classified, and their Reeb vector fields generate locally free circle actions, giving rise to Seifert fibrations
[
35
,
25
,
44
,
49
]
. In particular, any quasi-regular contact form is K-contact, i.e., its Reeb vector field is Killing with respect to a suitable Riemannian metric
[
15
,
17
]
.
From this perspective, it appears natural to study irregular contact forms admitting a Reeb-invariant metric, that is, irregular K-contact forms. The purpose of this paper is to classify irregular K-contact forms in dimension three.

Our first result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a compact three-dimensional manifold with a positive irregular K-contact form. Then
M
is diffeomorphic to a lens space. Moreover, the Reeb vector field of
\alpha
has precisely two periodic orbits, and the ratio of their minimal periods is irrational.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
is based on the geometry of tubular neighborhoods of periodic Reeb orbits.
Using the Reeb-invariant metric provided by the K-contact condition, we associate to each periodic orbit a volume function that measures the contact volume of its metric tubular neighborhoods (see
Section

5.2
).
In the irregular K-contact case we show that this function is smooth and that its critical set consists entirely of periodic Reeb orbits.
We then prove that the critical set is precisely the union of two periodic orbits and, by gluing their tubular neighborhoods, we deduce that
M
is diffeomorphic to a lens space.
The irrationality of the ratio of the minimal periods follows from a relation between these periods and the eigenvalues of the linearized Poincaré return maps along the periodic orbits.

Next, we would like to state a classification result. Recall that two contact forms
\alpha,\beta
are called strictly contactomorphic if there exists a diffeomorphism
\varphi:M\to M
such that

(3)

\varphi^{*}\alpha=\beta.

The second result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p,q
be coprime integers,
p>0
, and let
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
be such that
\tau_{0}/\tau_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
.

(i)

If
q^{2}\equiv 1\,\mathrm{mod}\,p
, then there exists a unique irregular positive
K
-contact form on
L(p,q)
admitting two periodic Reeb orbits of minimal periods
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
, up to strict contactomorphism.

(ii)

If
q^{2}\not\equiv 1\,\mathrm{mod}\,p
, then there exist precisely two irregular positive
K
-contact forms on
L(p,q)
admitting two periodic Reeb orbits of minimal periods
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
, up to strict contactomorphism.

Several examples of contact forms whose Reeb flow has exactly two periodic orbits appear in the literature; for example, irrational ellipsoids in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
provide one such family
[
23
, Ex. 1.1]
.
More generally, contact forms with exactly two periodic Reeb orbits have been extensively studied: it is known that
2
is the minimal number of periodic Reeb orbits on a three-dimensional closed contact manifold
[
24
,
21
,
22
]
, and that if a closed manifold carries a contact form with exactly two periodic Reeb orbits, then it is diffeomorphic to a lens space
[
23
]
.
We stress that the contact forms of
Theorem

1.2
do not provide an exhaustive list of contact forms having exactly two periodic Reeb orbits.
Indeed, there exist contact forms on
S^{3}
whose Reeb flow has precisely two periodic orbits while all others are dense (see
[
34
,
28
,
5
]
,
[
23
, Rmk. 1.7]
and
[
25
, Rmk. 1.6]
), a feature which is not shared by the forms of
Theorem

1.2
(cf.
Section

4
).

1.1.
Application: Reeb periods are sub-Riemannian spectral invariants

A classical theme in Riemannian geometry is the study of the relationships between the geometry and topology of a manifold and the spectral properties of its Laplace–Beltrami operator
[
38
]
.

In the presence of a contact structure, the picture is enriched by the interplay between the contact structure and the metric: when a contact manifold
(M,\alpha)
is equipped with a Riemannian metric
g
one can consider, alongside the Laplace–Beltrami operator, the
sub-Laplacian
. This is a second-order hypoelliptic operator naturally associated to the sub-Riemannian structure determined by the contact distribution and the metric
[
3
]
.

To recall the definition of the sub-Laplacian, we first define the sub-Riemannian gradient of
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, denoted with
\nabla^{sR}f
, as the unique smooth section of
\xi=\ker\alpha
satisfying

(4)

df(Y)=g(\nabla^{sR}f,Y),\qquad\forall\,Y\in\Gamma(\xi).

The sub-Laplacian of
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
is then defined as the divergence of its horizontal gradient

(5)

\Delta^{sR}f=\mathrm{div}(\nabla^{sR}f),

where the divergence is taken with respect to the volume form
\alpha\wedge d\alpha
. As the Laplace–Beltrami operator plays a central role in Riemannian geometry, the study of the sub-Laplacian has become a central topic in the sub-Riemannian setting. See, for instance,
[
32
,
43
]
for matters of regularity,
[
20
,
19
,
18
,
10
,
6
,
10
,
13
,
14
]
for heat kernel asymptotics on and outside the diagonal, and
[
4
,
37
,
39
,
48
]
for heat content.

We are interested in the connections linking the spectrum of the sub-Laplacian and the dynamical properties of the Reeb vector field. One way of recovering spectral information is to study the explicit form of the coefficients appearing in the small-time asymptotic expansion of the trace of the
heat kernel
, the latter being the fundamental solution
P\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times M\times M)
of the heat equation:

(6)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}P(t,x,y)-\Delta^{sR}_{x}P(t,x,y)=0,\\
\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{M}P(t,x,y)f(y)\alpha\wedge d\alpha(y)=f(x),\qquad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M),\,x\in M.\end{cases}

A more detailed definition is given in
Section

7
. By the small time asymptotic of the heat trace we mean the asymptotic expansion, as
t\to 0
, of the function

(7)

t\mapsto\int_{M}P(t,x,x)\alpha\wedge d\alpha(x).

The study of such expansion is a classical topic in Riemannian geometry
[
38
,
33
]
, and, more recently, it has been explored also in the sub-Riemannian setting
[
20
,
18
,
6
,
14
,
13
]
.

For quasi-regular contact forms the Reeb flow induces a Seifert fibration (see
Appendix

A
). The first two coefficients of the small time asymptotic expansion of the heat trace can be computed in terms of the invariants of such fibration.

In
Section

7
we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a compact contact Riemannian manifold where
\alpha
is positive and quasi-regular and
g
is Reeb invariant. Then the Reeb flow induces a Seifert fibration of
M
, in particular all but finitely many Reeb orbits have the same minimal period
\tau>0
and

(8)

\int_{M}P(t,x,x)\alpha\wedge d\alpha(x)=\frac{\tau}{16t^{2}}\left(-e(M)+2\pi\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{O})t+O(t^{2})\right),

where
e(M)
the Euler number of the Seifert fibration and
\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{O})
is the orbifold Euler characteristic of the base orbifold.

In the irregular K-contact case, the Reeb flow does not induce a Seifert fibration of
M
. Nonetheless, the first two coefficients of the heat trace expansion can be computed explicitly. By approximating irregular contact forms with quasi-regular ones and showing that the first two coefficients in the expansion (
8
) converge, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a compact contact Riemannian manifold where
\alpha
is a positive irregular K-contact form and
g
is a Reeb invariant metric. According to
Theorem

1.1
there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
co-prime integers such that
M
is diffeomorphic to
L(p,q)
and contains exactly two periodic Reeb orbits. Let
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
be their minimal periods, then

(9)

\int_{M}P(t,x,x)\alpha\wedge d\alpha(x)=\frac{1}{16t^{2}}(p\tau_{0}\tau_{1}+2\pi(\tau_{0}+\tau_{1})t+O(t^{2})).

It has been conjectured in
[
20
]
that the periods of closed orbits of the Reeb vector field are spectral invariants of the sub-Laplacian.
Theorem

1.4
explicitly confirms such conjecture, for irregular K-contact forms.
Theorem

1.4
and
1.3
show that contact invariants can be recovered from the spectrum of the sub-Riemannian Laplacian. Thus, they may be contextualized in a growing line of research where contact topology is investigated through the use of sub-Riemannian methods, see for instance
[
2
,
11
,
12
,
9
,
8
,
7
]
.

2.
K-contact Riemannian manifolds

In this section we review some preliminaries on K-contact forms and we study their symmetry groups. In particular we establish relations between periodic Reeb orbits and the critical set of contact momentum maps. Throughout the section
M
denotes a closed oriented three dimensional smooth manifold.

2.1.
Contact forms and compatible metrics

A smooth plane field
\xi
on
M
is called a contact structure if there exists a differential form
\alpha
, called a contact form, such that

(10)

\xi=\ker\alpha,\qquad\alpha\wedge d\alpha\neq 0.

Given a never vanishing smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the rescaled form
f\alpha
is also a contact form. However, the sign of
\alpha\wedge d\alpha
does not depend on the scaling factor. A contact form is called positive if
\alpha\wedge d\alpha>0
. To any contact form there is an associated vector field
R
called the Reeb field, uniquely determined by the following two equations

(11)

\alpha(R)=1,\qquad d\alpha(R,\cdot\,)=0.

Definition 2.1
.

A Riemannian metric
g
is called compatible with a positive contact form
\alpha
if

(12)

g(R,\cdot\,)=\alpha(\,\cdot\,),\qquad\mathrm{vol}_{g}=\alpha\wedge d\alpha,

where
\mathrm{vol}_{g}
is the volume form of the oriented Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. The triple
(M,\alpha,g)
is called a contact Riemannian manifold.

For a fixed contact form
\alpha
, there exist several metrics satisfying (
12
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\eta
be a bundle metric on
\xi
satisfying

(13)

d\alpha|_{\xi}=\mathrm{area}_{\eta},

where
\mathrm{area}_{\eta}
is the area form of the oriented Euclidean bundle
(\xi,\eta)
, the orientation being the one determined by
d\alpha|_{\xi}
. Then the Riemannian metric
g
defined by

(14)

g=p^{*}\eta+\alpha\otimes\alpha,

where
p:TM\to\xi
denotes the linear projection
p(v)=v-\alpha(v)R
, is compatible with
\alpha
.

Proof.

We check the two conditions of (
12
). The first one follows from
\alpha(R)=1
and
p(R)=0
:

(15)

g(R,\cdot)=\eta(p(R),p(\cdot))+\alpha(R)\alpha(\cdot)=\alpha(\cdot).

The latter equality implies that
R
is a unit normal to
\xi
, and that
p:TM\to\xi
is the orthogonal projection to
\xi
determined by the metric
g
. Therefore

(16)

\mathrm{vol}(g)=\alpha\wedge p^{*}(\mathrm{area}_{g|_{\xi}}).

Moreover,
g|_{\xi}=\eta
and
\mathrm{area}_{\eta}=d\alpha|_{\xi}
, therefore

(17)

\mathrm{vol}(g)=\alpha\wedge p^{*}(\mathrm{area}_{g|_{\xi}})=\alpha\wedge p^{*}(\mathrm{area}_{\eta})=\alpha\wedge p^{*}(d\alpha|_{\xi})=\alpha\wedge d\alpha,

where we have used the fact that
p^{*}(d\alpha|_{\xi})=d\alpha
since, by definition of Reeb field,
d\alpha(R,\cdot)=0
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Several notions of compatibility between Riemannian metrics and contact structures appear in the literature. The one that we give here corresponds to the
“strong compatibility”
of
[
27
]
, see also
[
15
]
. Equation (
13
) expresses the sub-Riemannian compatibility condition, see
[
3
,
2
]
.

We now introduce
K
-contact forms.

Definition 2.4
.

A positive contact form
\alpha
on
M
is called
K
-contact if the associated Reeb vector field is a Killing vector field for a smooth Riemannian metric
g
. In other terms

(18)

\mathcal{L}_{R}g=0.

The couple
(M,\alpha)
is called a K-contact manifold.

The next proposition shows that the Riemannian metric
g
in the previous definition can be chosen to be compatible with
\alpha
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\alpha
be a positive
K
-contact form with Reeb vector field
R
. There exists a Riemannian metric
g
, compatible with
\alpha
in the sense of
2.1
, such that
\mathcal{L}_{R}g=0
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{g}
be a Riemannian metric such that
\mathcal{L}_{R}\bar{g}=0
. We endow
\xi
with the orientation induced by
d\alpha|_{\xi}
. Let
\sigma
be the positive area form that
\bar{g}|_{\xi}
induces on the oriented bundle
\xi
. Observe that
\mathcal{L}_{R}\sigma=0
. There exists a smooth positive function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(19)

d\alpha|_{\xi}=f\sigma.

Therefore the bundle metric
\eta:=f\bar{g}|_{\xi}
satisfies
\mathrm{area}_{\eta}=d\alpha|_{\xi}
and
\mathcal{L}_{R}\eta=0
.
Let
p:TM\to\xi
be the linear projection
p(v)=v-\alpha(v)R
. We define the Riemannian metric
g
as

(20)

g=p^{*}\eta+\alpha\otimes\alpha.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{R}\alpha=\mathcal{L}_{R}\eta=0
, one has
\mathcal{L}_{R}g=0
. The compatibility with
\alpha
follows from
2.2
.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

The triple
(M,\alpha,g)
is called a K-contact Riemannian manifold if
M
is a three manifold,
\alpha
is a positive K-contact form, and
g
is a compatible metric satisfying
\mathcal{L}_{R}g=0
.

2.2.
Rotation number

In this section we introduce an invariant for periodic orbits of the Reeb vector field associated with a K-contact form.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a K-contact manifold and let
\ell\subset M
be a periodic orbit of the Reeb vector field of minimal period
\tau>0
. That is to say that, for any
q\in\ell
,
\tau
is the smallest positive real number such that

(21)

e^{\tau R}(q)=q.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric invariant under the flow of the Reeb vector field. Let
\xi=\ker\alpha
and note that for any fixed
q\in\ell
, the linear map

(22)

e^{\tau R}_{*}:\xi_{q}\to\xi_{q},

is an isometry of 2-dimensional euclidean vector spaces, the metric being
g|_{\xi}
. Hence, its eigenvalues are of the form
e^{\pm i2\pi\phi}
, with
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.7
.

The rotation number
\phi\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{N}
associated with the periodic Reeb orbit
\ell
is defined by the following equation

(23)

\lambda_{\pm}(\ell)=e^{\pm i2\pi\phi},

where
\lambda_{\pm}(\ell)
are the eigenvalues of (
22
).

Remark 2.8
.

Let
\phi
be the rotation number of
\ell
and let
v_{1},v_{2}
be an orthonormal frame for
g|_{\xi_{q}}
. Since the Reeb flow preserves the Reeb vector field, the linear map
e^{\tau R}_{*}:T_{q}M\to T_{q}M
, written with respect to the ordered basis
v_{1},v_{2},R
, has the following associated matrix

(24)

e^{\tau R}_{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(2\pi\phi)&-\sin(2\pi\phi)&0\\
\sin(2\pi\phi)&\cos(2\pi\phi)&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Although being a semi-local invariant, the rotation number of a single Reeb orbit determines the behavior of all the other ones.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a K-contact manifold and let
\ell\subset M
be a periodic orbit of the Reeb vector field having rotation number
\phi
. If
\phi\in\mathbb{Q}
, then all Reeb orbits are periodic. Moreover, if
\ell
has minimal period
\tau
and
\phi=n/m
with
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
coprime, then there exists a neighborhood
O
of
\ell
such that for any
x\in O
the Reeb orbit containing
x
has minimal period
m\tau
.

Proof.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric invariant under the flow of the Reeb vector field
R
. Let
x\in M
and let
\sigma:[0,1]\to M
be a length minimizing geodesic joining
x
to
\ell
, i.e.

(25)

\sigma(0)\in\ell,\qquad\sigma(1)=x,\qquad\mathrm{length}(\sigma)=\inf_{y\in\ell}d(x,y),

where
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Riemannian distance. Now consider the 1-parameter family of curves

\sigma_{t}=e^{tR}\circ\sigma,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Since the Reeb flow acts by isometries, each
\sigma_{t}
is a length minimizing geodesic. By hypothesis there exists
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
coprime such that
\phi=n/m
, therefore by definition of rotation number one has

e^{m\tau R}_{*}=\left(e^{\tau R}_{*}\right)^{m}=Id,

where
\tau
is the minimal period of
\ell
(cf.
Remark

2.8
). It follows that
\dot{\sigma}(0)=\dot{\sigma}_{m\tau}(0)
, which implies
\sigma\equiv\sigma_{m\tau}
, since both curves are geodesics. Consequently

(26)

e^{m\tau R}(x)=e^{m\tau R}\circ\sigma(1)=\sigma_{m\tau}(1)=\sigma(1)=x.

Therefore the Reeb orbit through
x
is periodic of minimal period not greater than
m\tau
. If in the above reasoning we choose
x
sufficiently close to
\ell
, then there is a unique length minimizing geodesic joining
x
to
\ell
(cf.
Section

4.1
), which is
\sigma
. Therefore, for such an
x
we have

(27)

e^{tR}(x)=x\iff e^{tR}\circ\sigma(1)=\sigma(1)\iff e^{tR}\circ\sigma\equiv\sigma\iff e^{tR}_{*}\dot{\sigma}(0)=\dot{\sigma}(0),

where uniqueness of the minimizing geodesic has been used in the second equivalence. Since
\ell
has minimal period
\tau
and rotation number
\phi=n/m
, the minimal
t
for which (
27
) holds is
m\tau
.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a K-contact manifold and let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
be a Reeb invariant function, i.e.
df(R)=0
. Assume that there exists a periodic Reeb orbit
\ell
contained in a regular level set of
f
. Then
\ell
has zero rotation number. In particular all Reeb orbits are periodic.

Proof.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric invariant under the flow of
R
and compatible with
\alpha
. Let
X=\mathrm{grad}\,f
be the gradient of
f
. Note that
X\in\Gamma(\xi)
, indeed, making use of (
12
) we find

(28)

\alpha(X)=g(X,R)=df(R)=0.

Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
S=f^{-1}(c)
be the regular level set of
f
containing
\ell
. Thus,
X
does not have any zeroes along
S
. Hence, we can define the following orthonormal frame for
\xi|_{S}

(29)

e_{1}=|X|^{-1}X,\qquad e_{2}=e_{1}^{\perp_{\xi}},

where
e_{1}^{\perp_{\xi}}
denotes the oriented orthonormal complement to
e_{1}
in
\xi
. Since
R
is a Killing field and
df(R)=0
it holds
[R,X]=0
, and consequently

(30)

[R,e_{1}]=[R,e_{2}]=0.

Since
\ell\subset S
, the orthonormal frame
e_{1},e_{2}
is well defined along
\ell
. Let
\tau>0
be the minimal period of
\ell
, then by (
30
) we have

(31)

e^{\tau R}_{*}e_{1}=e_{1},\qquad e^{\tau R}_{*}e_{2}=e_{2},

which shows that
e^{\tau R}_{*}=Id
. Hence the rotation number is zero and, by
Lemma

2.9
, all Reeb orbits are periodic.
∎

2.3.
Contact isometry group and momentum maps

We introduce the K-contact isometry group and the K-contact momentum maps.

Definition 2.11
.

A contact isometry of a contact Riemannian manifold
(M,\alpha,g)
is a diffeomorphism
\varphi:M\to M
such that
\varphi^{*}\alpha=\alpha,\varphi^{*}g=g
.
The contact isometry group
G
is defined as the collection of the contact isometries

(32)

G=\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Diff}(M)\,:\,\varphi^{*}g=g,\,\,\,\varphi^{*}\alpha=\alpha\}.

Remark 2.12
.

The contact isometry group
G
of a closed contact Riemannian manifold
(M,\alpha,g)
is a compact Lie group. Indeed,
G
is a closed subgroup of the isometry group of the compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, which is a compact Lie group by Myers–Steenrod theorem. Furthermore, if
(M,\alpha,g)
is K-contact then the flow of the Reeb vector field defined by

(33)

\{e^{tR}\in\mathrm{Diff}(M)\,:\,t\in\mathbb{R}\},

is a central subgroup of
G
. Indeed, by definition of
G
,
\varphi^{*}\alpha=\alpha
for all
\varphi\in G
. Since the Reeb vector field is uniquely determined by
\alpha
, one has
\varphi_{*}R=R
for all
\varphi\in G
.

In the remaining part of the section, given a group
G
acting on a manifold
M
and a subset
A\subset M
we denote with
G_{A}
the stabilizer of
A
in
G
, i.e.

(34)

G_{A}=\{\varphi\in G\,:\,\varphi(A)\subset A\}.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a closed K-contact Riemannian manifold with contact isometry group
G
. Let
S\subset M
be an oriented compact surface invariant under the flow of the Reeb field. Then
(S,g|_{S})
is a flat Riemannian torus and, if there are no periodic Reeb orbits on
S
, the map

(35)

G_{S}\to\mathrm{Isom}_{0}(S),\qquad\varphi\mapsto\varphi|_{S},

is a group isomorphism, where
\mathrm{Isom}_{0}(S)
is the identity component of the isometry group of
(S,g|_{S})
.

Proof.

Let
R^{\perp_{S}}\in\mathrm{Vec}(S)
be the oriented orthonormal complement of
R|_{S}
taken w.r.t.
g|_{S}
. Since the Reeb flow acts by contact isometries and fixes
S
, we have
[R,R^{\perp_{S}}]=0
. Thus, the compact Riemannian surface
(S,g|_{S})
carries a global commuting orthonormal frame, therefore
S
is a torus and
g|_{S}
is a flat Riemannian metric.
We now prove the injectivity of (
35
). If
\varphi\in G_{S}
satisfies
\varphi|_{S}=Id_{S}
, then for each
q\in S
the map

(36)

d_{q}\varphi|_{S}:T_{q}S\to T_{q}S,

is the identity map. Let
v_{1}
denote the oriented unit normal to
T_{q}S
. Since
\varphi
is an orientation preserving isometry fixing
S
one has
d_{q}\varphi v_{1}=v_{1}
. It follows that
d_{q}\varphi:T_{q}M\to T_{q}M
is the identity map. Summarizing
\varphi
is an isometry of a Riemannian manifold satisfying

(37)

\varphi(q)=q,\qquad d_{q}\varphi=Id_{T_{q}M},

therefore
\varphi=Id_{M}
.
Assume that
R
has no periodic orbit in
S
. Then, since
S
is a torus, the generalized Poincaré-Bendixson theorem
[
42
]
implies that every orbit of
R|_{S}
is dense in
S
. For each
q\in S
we have

(38)

\{e^{tR}(q)\,:\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset G_{S}\cdot q:=\{\varphi(q)\,:\,\varphi\in G_{S}\}\subset S.

Thus,
G_{S}\cdot q
is dense in
S
.
Since
G_{S}
is compact, being the stabilizer of a compact set in a compact group, then
G_{S}\cdot q
is closed. Therefore
G_{S}\cdot q=S
. Thus
G_{S}
is a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Isom}_{0}(S)
, which is a torus, acting transitively on
S
. The only closed subgroups of a torus are subtori. Since proper subtori cannot act transitively, we deduce that
G_{S}=\mathrm{Isom}_{0}(S)
and (
35
) is surjective.
∎

We now introduce K-contact momentum maps and show that their critical sets are foliated by Reeb orbits.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a K-contatc Riemannian manifold with contact isometry group
G
. We say that a vector field
Y\in\mathrm{Vec}(M)
is K-contact if

e^{tY}\in G,\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

A smooth map
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is called a K-contact momentum map if there exists a K-contact vector field
Y
such that

(39)

df+\iota_{Y}d\alpha=0.

Remark 2.15
.

K-contact vector fields form a real Lie algebra of the same dimension as
G
.

Remark 2.16
.

Under the hypothesis of
Lemma

2.13
, any Killing vector field for
S
extends to a global contact Killing vector field for
M
. Indeed let
X\in\mathrm{Vec}(S)
be a Killing vector field for
S
, then by
Lemma

2.13
, there exists a 1-parameter group of contact isometries of
M
,
\{\varphi_{t}\}
, such that

(40)

\varphi_{t}|_{S}=e^{tX},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

The extension of
X
is the vector field generating the 1-dimensional group
\{\varphi_{t}\}\subset\mathrm{Diff}(M)
.

Remark 2.17
.

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a compact K-contact Riemannian manifold and let
\mu:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a K-contact momentum map. Then, for
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
sufficiently small the form

(41)

\alpha_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\alpha}{1+\varepsilon\mu},

is also a K-contact form. Indeed, let
Y
be the K-contact vector field such that
d\mu+\iota_{Y}d\alpha=0
, then

(42)

d(\mu-\alpha(Y))=d\mu-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\alpha+\iota_{Y}d\alpha=d\mu+\iota_{Y}d\alpha=0.

Thus, up to an additive constant we may assume
\mu=\alpha(Y)
. Then, the Reeb field of
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
is

(43)

R_{\varepsilon}=R+\varepsilon Y.

To see this, first note that, by
d\mu+\iota_{Y}d\alpha=0
and
\mu=\alpha(Y)
one obtains

(44)

d\mu(R_{\varepsilon})=-d\alpha(Y,R+\varepsilon Y)=0,\qquad\alpha(R_{\varepsilon})=1+\varepsilon\mu,\qquad\iota_{R_{\varepsilon}}d\alpha=-\varepsilon d\mu.

It follows that
\alpha_{\varepsilon}(R_{\varepsilon})=1
and that

(45)

\displaystyle\iota_{R_{\varepsilon}}d\alpha_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varepsilon\iota_{R_{\varepsilon}}d\mu\wedge\alpha}{(1+\varepsilon\mu)^{2}}+\frac{\iota_{R_{\varepsilon}}d\alpha}{1+\varepsilon\mu}=\frac{\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon\mu)d\mu}{1+\varepsilon\mu}-\frac{\varepsilon d\mu}{1+\varepsilon\mu}=0.

Since both
Y
and
R
are K-contact vector fields, it follows that
\mathcal{L}_{R_{\varepsilon}}g=0
and
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
is K-contact. Furthermore, starting from
g
and arguing as in
2.5
, one can build a
R_{\varepsilon}
-invariant metric
g_{\varepsilon}
which is compatible with
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
and satisfying

(46)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}g_{\varepsilon}=g,

where the limit is intended in
C^{\infty}
topology. Note that
\mu
is also a K-contact momentum map for the perturbed structure
(M,\alpha_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a compact K-contact Riemannian manifold and let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-constant K-contact momentum map. Then the critical set of
f
is a union of periodic Reeb orbits.

Proof.

Observe that
f
is invariant under the Reeb flow and under the flow of
Y
, indeed

(47)

df(R)=-d\alpha(Y,R)=0,\qquad df(Y)=-d\alpha(Y,Y)=0.

Let
c>0
be a regular value of
f
, which exists since
f
is non constant, and let
S=f^{-1}(c)
. If
S
contains a periodic Reeb orbit then by
Lemma

2.10
, all Reeb orbits are periodic, since by (
47
),
f
is invariant under the flow of
R
. In this case the proof is concluded.

Otherwise, assume that
S
contains no periodic Reeb orbits. Then, applying
Lemma

2.13
we deduce that
H:=G_{S}
is isomorphic to a torus. Let now
q\in M
be a critical point of
f
. It is sufficient to show that the Reeb orbit containing
q
is periodic. Let
Y
be the K-contact vector filed such that

(48)

df+\iota_{Y}d\alpha=0.

Since
\ker d\alpha=\mathrm{span}\{R\}
and
d_{q}f=0
, there exists a constant
b
such that

(49)

Y|_{q}=bR|_{q}.

Note that the vector field

(50)

Z:=Y-bR,

is K-contact, tangent to
S
and vanishes at
q
. Let

H_{q}=\{\varphi\in H\,:\,\varphi(q)=q\},

which is a closed subgroup of
H
. Note that

(51)

1\leq\dim H_{q}\leq 2.

The lower bound follows from the inclusion
\{e^{tZ}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\subset H_{q}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The upper bound follows from
H_{q}\subset H
and the fact that
\dim H=2
, since
H
is a torus. Note that
H_{q}
cannot be
2
-dimensional, for otherwise it would hold
H_{q}=H
, while for
t\in\mathbb{R}
sufficiently small one has
e^{tR}\subset H\setminus H_{q}
, since the Reeb vector field never vanishes and its small times flow fixes no point. Thus
\dim H_{q}=1
and, being
H_{q}
a closed subgroup of the torus
H
,
H/H_{q}\simeq S^{1}
, where
H/H_{q}
denotes the quotient of
H
by
H_{q}
. Therefore

(52)

H\cdot q=\{\varphi(q)\,:\,\varphi\in\ H\}\simeq H/H_{q}\simeq S^{1},

where the diffeomorphism between
H/H_{q}
and
H\cdot q
is given by

(53)

\displaystyle H/H_{q}\to H\cdot q,\qquad[\varphi]\mapsto\varphi(q).

Since the Reeb field is tangent to
H\cdot q
, and the latter is 1-dimensional, then
H\cdot q
is actually a periodic orbit of the Reeb field.
∎

The following fact holds in much greater generality
[
46
]
. Here we prove it in the much simpler specific setting of K-contact geometry.

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
\alpha
be a K-contact form on a compact 3-manifold
M
. Then the associated Reeb vector field
R
admits at least one periodic orbit.

Proof.

Let
g
be a K-contact metric compatible with
\alpha
and let
G
be the contact isometry group of
(M,\alpha,g)
. Then, according to
Remark

2.12
,
G
is a compact Lie group and the Reeb flow
\Phi(R)=\{e^{tR}\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, is a central subgroup of
G
. If
\dim(G)=1
, then
G=\Phi(R)\simeq S^{1}
, where the latter isomorphism equivalence follows from the classification of compact 1-dimensional Lie groups. In such case all Reeb orbits are periodic and the proof is concluded.

Assume that
\dim(G)>1
. Let
K\subset\mathrm{Vec}(M)
be the Lie algebra of K-contact vector fields. According to
Remark

2.15

\dim K=\dim G>1
. Therefore, there exists
Y\in K
which is not a multiple of the Reeb field
R
. Since
R
is transverse to
\xi
, there exist
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
X\in\Gamma(\xi)
such that
Y=fR+X
. Condition
\mathcal{L}_{Y}\alpha=0
then reduces to

(54)

\iota_{X}d\alpha+df=0.

The latter equality implies that
df(R)\equiv 0
and that
X
vanishes exactly at critical points of
f
. It follows that, if
f
were constant then we would have
Y=fR
, contradicting the fact that
Y
and
R
are linearly independent. Therefore
f
is a non-constant momentum map. By compactness of
M
,
f
has at least one critical point. We conclude applying
Theorem

2.18

∎

3.
Lens spaces

Given a couple of co-prime integers
(p,q)
, with
p>0
, the lens space
L(p,q)
is a smooth oriented manifold which can be described as the gluing of two solid tori. Let
V_{0},V_{1}
be two copies of the solid torus
D^{2}\times S^{1}
with its natural orientation. Let
(\theta,z)
be
2\pi
-periodic coordinates on the boundary
\partial V_{j}=\partial D^{2}\times S^{1}
, and consider the following orientation reversing diffeomorphism

(55)

\varphi:\partial V_{1}\to\partial V_{0},\qquad\varphi(\theta,z)=(-q\theta+mz,p\theta+sz),

where
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
mp+sq=1
. The lens space
L(p,q)
is the oriented manifold obtained gluing
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
along their boundaries, by the diffeomorphism
\varphi
:

(56)

L(p,q)=V_{0}\sqcup_{\varphi}V_{1}.

Recall that given two topological spaces
X,Y
, two subsets
A\subset X,B\subset Y
and a continuous map
\varphi:B\to A
the topological space
X\sqcup_{\varphi}Y
is defined as
X\sqcup Y/\sim
where, for
x\neq y
,
x\sim y
if and only if
x\in A
,
y\in B
and
\varphi(y)=x
.

3.1.
Toroidal atlases

In this section we give a two charts description of
L(p,q)
. We denote

(57)

U=D^{2}\times S^{1}\setminus\partial D^{2}\times S^{1},\qquad\ell=\{0\}\times S^{1}\subset U.

On
U\setminus\ell
we introduce cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
by means of the diffeomorphism

(58)

(0,1)\times(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{N})\times(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{N})\to U\setminus\ell,\qquad(r,\theta,z)\mapsto(re^{i\theta},z).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a three manifold. A collection of pairs
\{(U_{j},\psi_{j})\}
is called a
toroidal atlas
if
\{U_{j}\}
is an open cover of
M
and for each
j
the map

(59)

\psi_{j}:U\to U_{j},

is a diffeomorphism. For each
j
, the couple
(U_{j},\psi_{j})
is called a toroidal chart.

We would like to to describe
L(p,q)
as the union of two toroidal charts.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
M
be a smooth three manifold admitting a toroidal atlas composed by two charts
(U_{0},\psi_{0})
,
(U_{1},\psi_{1})
such that

(60)

\psi_{0}(U\setminus\ell)=\psi_{1}(U\setminus\ell)=U_{0}\cap U_{1}.

Assume that the change of coordinates map has the following expression in coordinates (
58
)

(61)

\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}:U\setminus\ell\to U\setminus\ell,\qquad\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}(r,\theta,z)=(1-r,-q\theta+mz,p\theta+sz),

where
m,s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
mp+sq=1
. Then
M
is diffeomorphic to
L(p,q)
.

Proof.

We denote with
V\subset U
the following set

(62)

V=\left\{(re^{i\theta},z)\in U\,:\,r\leq 1/2\right\}\simeq D^{2}\times S^{1}.

Since the
\psi_{j}
’s are charts, the following maps

(63)

\varphi_{j}=\psi_{j}|_{V}:V\to M,\qquad j=0,1,

are smooth embeddings. We observe that

(64)

M=\varphi_{0}(V)\cup\varphi_{1}(V).

Indeed, fix
x\in M
. Then there exists
(re^{i\theta},z)\in U
such that
x=\psi_{j}(re^{i\theta},z)
for some
j\in\{0,1\}
. Assume for instance
j=1
. If
r\leq 1/2
then
x\in\varphi_{1}(V)
. Otherwise, if
r>1/2
we have

(65)

\displaystyle\psi_{0}^{-1}(x)=\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}(re^{i\theta},z)=((1-r)e^{i(-q\theta+mz)},p\theta+sz)\in V.

Applying
\psi_{0}
to both sides of the latter inclusion we find that
x\in\varphi_{0}(V)
. The case
j=0
is analogous. Next, we observe that

(66)

\varphi_{0}(V)\cap\varphi_{1}(V)=\varphi_{0}(\partial V)=\varphi_{1}(\partial V).

Indeed, let
(re^{i\theta},z),(r^{\prime}e^{i\theta^{\prime}},z^{\prime})\in V
, then

(67)

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(re^{i\theta},z)=\varphi_{1}(r^{\prime}e^{i\theta^{\prime}},z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\iff(re^{i\theta},z)=\varphi_{0}^{-1}\varphi_{1}(r^{\prime}e^{i\theta^{\prime}},z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\iff(re^{i\theta},z)=((1-r^{\prime})e^{i(-q\theta^{\prime}+mz^{\prime})},p\theta^{\prime}+sz^{\prime}).

Since
r,r^{\prime}\in[0,1/2]
we find that the latter equation has the unique solution

(68)

r=r^{\prime}=1/2,\qquad(\theta,z)=(-q\theta^{\prime}+mz^{\prime},p\theta^{\prime}+sz^{\prime}).

Defining the map

(69)

\varphi:\partial V\to\partial V,\qquad\varphi(\theta,z)=(-q\theta+mz,p\theta+sz),

we have proved that

(70)

\varphi_{0}(x_{0})=\varphi_{1}(x_{1})\iff x_{0},x_{1}\in\partial V\,\,\text{and}\,\,x_{0}=\varphi(x_{1}).

Let
V_{0},V_{1}
be two copies of
V
. By definition
L(p,q)=V_{0}\sqcup_{\varphi}V_{1}
. We define the map

(71)

F:V_{0}\sqcup_{\varphi}V_{1}\to M,\qquad F|_{V_{j}}=\varphi_{j},\qquad j=0,1.

Such map is well defined due to (
70
). Moreover, from (
64
) and (
70
) we see that
F
is bijective. In conclusions,
F
is a bijection which restricts to the smooth embedding
\varphi_{0}
on
V_{0}
, and to the smooth embedding
\varphi_{1}
on
V_{1}
. Hence
F
is a diffeomorphism.
∎

We exploit the toroidal atlas just introduced to define an action of the 2-torus
T^{2}
on
L(p,q)
. Given a left action
\sigma:G\times X\to X
of a group
G
on a topological space
X
, we denote

(72)

g\cdot x:=\sigma(g,x),\qquad g\in G,x\in X.

We identify the 2-torus with
(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{N})\times(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{N})
and denote its elements with
(\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in T^{2}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists a
T^{2}
-action on
L(p,q)
satisfying

(73)

(\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime})\cdot\psi_{0}(re^{i\theta},z)=\psi_{0}(re^{i(\theta+\theta^{\prime})},z+z^{\prime}),

for all
(\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in T^{2}
and all
(re^{i\theta},z)\in U
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}:T^{2}\times U\to U
denote the following actions of
T^{2}
on
U

(74)

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}((\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime}),(re^{i\theta},z))=\left(re^{i(\theta+\theta^{\prime})},z+z^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}((\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime}),(re^{i\theta},z))=\left(re^{i(\theta-s\theta^{\prime}+mz^{\prime})},z+p\theta^{\prime}+qz^{\prime}\right).

We then define the action
\sigma:T^{2}\times L(p,q)\to L(p,q)
as

(75)

\sigma(\cdot,\cdot)=\begin{cases}\psi_{0}\circ\sigma_{0}(\cdot,\psi_{0}^{-1}(\cdot)),\qquad\text{on $T^{2}\times U_{0}$},\\
\psi_{1}\circ\sigma_{1}(\cdot,\psi_{1}^{-1}(\cdot)),\qquad\text{on $T^{2}\times U_{1}$.}\end{cases}

To ensure that
\sigma
is well defined on the overlap
T^{2}\times U_{0}\cap U_{1}
we need to check that

(76)

\psi_{0}\circ\sigma_{0}(\cdot,\psi_{0}^{-1}(\cdot))=\psi_{1}\circ\sigma_{1}(\cdot,\psi_{1}^{-1}(\cdot)),\qquad\text{on $T^{2}\times U_{0}\cap U_{1}$},

or, equivalently, we need to show that

(77)

\sigma_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)=(\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1})^{-1}\circ\sigma_{0}(\cdot,\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}(\cdot)),\qquad\text{on $T^{2}\times U_{0}\cap U_{1}$}.

According to the explicit expression of
\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}
in (
61
) we have

(78)

\displaystyle\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}(re^{i\theta},z)

\displaystyle=((1-r)e^{i(-q\theta+mz)},p\theta+sz),

\displaystyle(\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1})^{-1}(re^{i\theta},z)

\displaystyle=((1-r)e^{i(-s\theta+mz)},p\theta+qz).

Therefore we can directly check the validity of (
77
):

(79)

\displaystyle(\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1})^{-1}\circ\sigma_{0}((\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime}),\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}(re^{i\theta},z))

\displaystyle=(\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1})^{-1}((1-r)e^{i(\theta^{\prime}-q\theta+mz)},z^{\prime}+p\theta+sz)

\displaystyle=(re^{i(-s\theta^{\prime}+mz^{\prime}+\theta)},p\theta^{\prime}+qz^{\prime}+z)=\sigma_{1}((\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime}),re^{i\theta}).

Thus
\sigma
is well defined and, by definition, it satisfies (
73
).
∎

4.
Tubular neighborhoods of Reeb orbits

In this section we study the normal exponential map from a periodic orbit of the Reeb vector field in a K-contact Riemannian manifolds. In particular, we give an expression of the contact form in normal coordinates. We begin by recalling some standard facts concerning tubular neighborhoods of compact submanifolds in Riemannian geometry.

4.1.
Tubes in Riemannian geometry

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold and let
\ell
be a compact submanifold. The normal bundle of
\ell
is the orthogonal complement of
T\ell

(80)

\nu(\ell)=(T\ell)^{\perp}.

Given
s>0
we define the following subsets of
\nu(\ell)

(81)

\nu^{<s}(\ell)=\{v\in\nu(\ell)\,:\,|v|<s\},\qquad\nu^{s}(\ell)=\{v\in\nu(\ell)\,:\,|v|=s\}.

We define the normal exponential map of
\ell
as

(82)

\exp^{\perp}:\nu(\ell)\to M,\qquad\exp^{\perp}(v)=\exp(v),

where
\exp:TM\to M
denotes the Riemannian exponential map. We denote with

(83)

\delta:M\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\delta(x)=\inf_{y\in\ell}d(x,y),

the distance from
\ell
function, where
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Riemannian distance.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
x\in M
with
\delta(x)>0
. By compactness of
M
and
\ell
there exists an arc-length paramentrized geodesic
\sigma:[0,\delta(x)]\to M
such that
\sigma(0)=0
and
\sigma(\delta(x))=x
. It is a well know consequence of first order optimality conditions that
\dot{\sigma}(0)\in\nu^{1}(\ell)
. In particular

(84)

\sigma(t)=\exp^{\perp}(t\dot{\sigma}(0)),\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,\delta(x)].

See for instance
[
40
, Chap. III]
.

Definition 4.2
.

The cut time function is defined as

(85)

t_{\mathrm{cut}}:\nu^{1}(\ell)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad t_{\mathrm{cut}}(v)=\inf\{t>0\,:\,\delta(\exp^{\perp}(tv))=t\}.

The injectivity domain is defined as

(86)

U(\ell)=\left\{tv\in\nu(\ell)\,:\,v\in\nu^{1}(\ell),\,\,0\leq t<t_{\mathrm{cut}}(v)\right\}.

The cut locus is defined as

(87)

\mathrm{cut}(\ell)=\{\exp^{\perp}(t_{\mathrm{cut}}(v)v)\,:\,v\in\nu^{1}(\ell)\}.

The following theorem is well known and can be obtained from
[
40
, Chap. III]
. In the case
\ell=\{pt.\}
its statement can be found in most references on Riemannian geometry (e.g.
[
26
]
).

Theorem 4.3
.

The cut time function is continuous and positive. In particular the injectivity domain
U(\ell)\subset\nu(\ell)
is open, the cut locus
\mathrm{cut}(\ell)\subset M
is closed and the map

(88)

\exp^{\perp}|_{U(\ell)}:U(\ell)\to M\setminus\mathrm{cut}(\ell),

is a diffeomorphism.

4.2.
Tubes around Reeb orbits

Throughout this subsection,
(M,\alpha,g)
denotes a compact K-contact Riemannian manifold and
\ell
a periodic orbit of minimal period
\tau>0
of the Reeb field
R
. For a fixed
q\in\ell
, let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{N}\to M
be the following embedding of
\ell
:

(89)

\gamma:\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{N}\to M,\qquad\gamma(t)=e^{\frac{\tau}{2\pi}tR}(q).

By the compatibility condition (
12
),
\nu(\ell)
coincides with
\xi|_{\ell}
. Hence, fixing an orthonormal frame

(90)

v_{1},v_{2}\in\xi,\qquad g(v_{i},v_{j})=\delta_{ij},\qquad i,j=1,2,

for
\xi|_{\ell}
, we may identify
\nu(\ell)
with the trivial bundle
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
. We have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

The following map is an isomorphism of vector bundles

(91)

v:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}\to\nu(\ell),\qquad v(x,y,z)=(xv_{1}+yv_{2})|_{\gamma(z)}.

Remark 4.5
.

The map
v:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}\to\nu(\ell)
of the previous proposition also induces cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
on
\nu(\ell)\setminus\ell
defined by

(92)

x=r\cos\theta,\qquad y=r\sin\theta,\qquad z=z.

In particular
(\theta,z)
are coordinates on the torus
\nu^{s}(\ell)
, for every
s>0
.

We now define the lift of the Reeb vector field to the tangent bundle.

Definition 4.6
.

We define the vector field
\vec{R}\in\mathrm{Vec}(TM)
as

(93)

\vec{R}_{v}=\left.\frac{d}{dz}\right|_{z=0}e^{zR}_{*}v,\qquad\forall\,v\in TM.

Since the flow of the Reeb vector field acts by isometries and fixes
\ell
,
\vec{R}
is tangent to
\nu(\ell)
. We can therefore express
\vec{R}
in the cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
of
Remark

4.5
.

Proposition 4.7
.

There exists cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
on
\nu(\ell)\setminus\ell
, such that

(94)

\vec{R}=\frac{2\pi}{\tau}\left(\partial_{z}+\phi\partial_{\theta}\right),

where
\phi
is the rotation number of the Reeb orbit
\ell
of
2.7
.

Proof.

Let
v_{1},v_{2}
be an orthonormal frame for
\xi|_{\ell}
of the form (
90
). Let
\gamma:S^{1}\to M
be the parametrization (
89
) of
\ell
. Since
\alpha
is K-contact, the map
e^{tR}_{*}=e^{t\vec{R}}
rotates the frame
v_{1},v_{2}
. Therefore there exists a smooth function
\varphi(t):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(95)

e^{t\vec{R}}v_{1}|_{q}=(\cos\varphi(t)v_{1}+\sin\varphi(t)v_{2})|_{\gamma(\frac{2\pi}{\tau}t)},\qquad e^{t\vec{R}}v_{2}|_{q}=(-\sin\varphi(t)v_{1}+\cos\varphi(t)v_{2})|_{\gamma(\frac{2\pi}{\tau}t)}.

Observe that, by
2.7
of
\phi
, the function
\varphi
satisfies

(96)

\varphi(0)\equiv 0\mod 2\pi,\qquad\varphi(\tau)\equiv 2\pi\phi\mod 2\pi.

Thanks to (
95

\vec{R}
in cylindrical coordinates of
Remark

4.5

(97)

\displaystyle e^{t\vec{R}}v(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z)

\displaystyle=e^{t\vec{R}}(r\cos\theta v_{1}+r\sin\theta v_{2})|_{\gamma(z)}

\displaystyle=v\left(r\cos\left(\theta+\varphi(t)\right),r\sin\left(\theta+\varphi(t)\right),z+\frac{2\pi}{\tau}t\right),

where

is the map of
4.4
. Differentiating both sides with respect to

we find

(98)

\vec{R}=v_{*}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\tau}\partial_{z}+\dot{\varphi}\left(\frac{\tau}{2\pi}z\right)\partial_{\theta}\right).

Consider the following change of coordinates

(99)

(\theta,z)\mapsto(\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime}):=\left(\theta+\frac{2\pi}{\tau}\left(z\phi-\varphi\left(\frac{\tau}{2\pi}z\right)\right),z\right).

Note that (

(\theta^{\prime}(\theta,2\pi),z^{\prime}(\theta,2\pi))=(\theta^{\prime}(\theta,0),z^{\prime}(\theta,0)+2\pi)
. Applying the change of coordinates (

(100)

\vec{R}=v_{*}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\tau}\left(\partial_{z^{\prime}}+\phi\partial_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\right),

concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, applying the change of coordinates

to (

\vec{R}=\frac{2\pi}{\tau}\left(\partial_{z}+(\phi+m)\partial_{\theta}\right).

We see that such transformation has the same effect as changing the representative of

mod

.

Remark 4.9
.

Since the flow of
R
acts by isometries and fixes

, the following holds

(102)

e^{tR}\circ\exp^{\perp}=\exp^{\perp}\circ e^{t\vec{R}},\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 4.10
.

If
\alpha
is irregular, then the orbits of
\vec{R}
are dense on
\nu^{s}(\ell)
for all

. Indeed, in this case at least one non-periodic Reeb orbit exists, thus by

2.9
the rotation number

is irrational. Therefore, the explicit expression of

in

shows that, for any

, the orbits of the flow of

are dense on the torus

(103)

\nu^{s}(\ell)=\{v\in\nu(\ell)\,:\,|v|=s\}\simeq\{(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}\,:\,r=s\}.

We now compute the form
(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)
in the coordinates of
4.7
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\exp^{\perp}:\nu(\ell)\to M
be the normal exponential map, then there exists a smooth function
a:\nu(\ell)\to\mathbb{R}
, such that

(104)

(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)=\left(\frac{\tau}{2\pi}-\phi a\right)dz+ad\theta.

Furthermore, if
\alpha
is irregular then the following equalities hold on the whole
\nu(\ell)

(105)

\partial_{\theta}a=\partial_{z}a=0,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\theta}}\left(\exp^{\perp*}g\right)=\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{z}}\left(\exp^{\perp*}g\right)=0.

Proof.

We claim that
\alpha(\exp^{\perp}_{*}\partial_{r})\equiv 0
. Let
v\in\nu^{1}\ell
and consider the geodesic

(106)

\sigma:[0,+\infty)\to M,\qquad\sigma(t)=\exp^{\perp}(tv).

Note that
\exp^{\perp}_{*}\partial_{r}=\dot{\sigma}
. We define
u:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as

(107)

u(t)=\alpha(\dot{\sigma}(t)).

To prove the claim, we need to show that
u\equiv 0
. Let
X
be a local unit norm vector field extension of
\dot{\sigma}
. Note that, since
R
is Killing it holds

(108)

\displaystyle 0=\left(\mathcal{L}_{R}g\right)(X,X)=R(g(X,X))-g([R,X],X)-g(X,[R,X])=2g(X,\nabla_{X}R),

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection. It follows from (

) that
u(t)=g(\dot{\sigma}(t),R)
, therefore

(109)

\dot{u}(t)=g(\nabla_{\dot{\sigma}}\dot{\sigma}(t),R)+g(X,\nabla_{X}R)=0,\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0,

where the first addendum is zero since
\sigma
is a geodesic, the second one because of (
108
). Since
u(0)=0
, because
\dot{\sigma}(0)\in\nu(\ell)=\xi|_{\ell}
, the claim is proved. Therefore it holds

(110)

(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)=(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)(\partial_{\theta})d\theta+(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)(\partial_{z})dz.

Furthermore, thanks to
Remark

4.9
and
4.7
we can compute

(111)

\displaystyle 1=\alpha(R)=\alpha(\exp^{\perp}_{*}\vec{R})

\displaystyle=(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)(\vec{R})=\frac{2\pi}{\tau}(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)\left(\partial_{z}+\phi\partial_{\theta}\right).

Setting
a:=(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)(\partial_{\theta})
, the above equation requires that

(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)(\partial_{z})=\frac{\tau}{2\pi}-\phi a,

which substituted in (
110
) confirms the expression of
(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)
in the statement.

If
\alpha
is irregular then by
Remark

4.10
any orbit of
\vec{R}
is dense on the torus
\nu^{s}(\ell)
, for all
s>0
. Moreover, according to
Remark

4.5
,
T(\nu^{s}(\ell))=\mathrm{span}\{\partial_{\theta},\partial_{z}\}
. Therefore, for any smooth function
f:\nu(\ell)\to\mathbb{R}
the following equivalence holds

(112)

\vec{R}(f)=0\iff\partial_{\theta}f=\partial_{z}f=0.

Similarly, for any tensor smooth tensor
T
defined on
\nu(\ell)
one has

(113)

\mathcal{L}_{\vec{R}}T=0\iff\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\theta}}T=\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{z}}T=0.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{R}g=0
, by
Remark

4.9
it also holds
\mathcal{L}_{\vec{R}}(\exp^{\perp*}g)=0
, indeed

(114)

0=\exp^{\perp*}(\mathcal{L}_{R}g)=\mathcal{L}_{\exp^{\perp}_{*}R}(\exp^{\perp*}g)=\mathcal{L}_{\vec{R}}(\exp^{\perp*}g).

Therefore by (
113
) it holds

\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\theta}}(\exp^{\perp*}g)=\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{z}}(\exp^{\perp*}g)=0.

Similarly, since
\mathcal{L}_{R}\alpha=0
one also has
\mathcal{L}_{\vec{R}}(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)=0
. On the other hand, using the explicit expression (
94
) of
\vec{R}
and the one (

) of
(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)
yields

(115)

0=\mathcal{L}_{\vec{R}}(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)=\vec{R}(a)(d\theta-\phi dz),

which implies
\vec{R}(a)=0
and, by (
113
),
\partial_{\theta}a=\partial_{z}a=0
.
∎

5.
Characterization of irregular K-contact forms

The purpose of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a closed K-contact manifold with
\alpha
irregular. Then:

(i)

there exists exactly two periodic Reeb orbits
\ell_{0}
,
\ell_{1}
of minimal periods
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
with
\tau_{0}/\tau_{1}
irrational,

(ii)

there exists
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
co-prime such that
M
is diffeomorphic to
L(p,q)
,

(iii)

let
\phi_{0}
be the rotation number of
\ell_{0}
, then there exists a smooth function
a:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(116)

\psi_{0}^{*}\alpha=\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{2\pi}-\phi_{0}a(r)\right)dz+a(r)d\theta,\qquad r\in[0,1],

where
(U_{0},\psi_{0})
is one of the two toroidal charts of
3.2
.

The proof is divided into three lemmas, each of which is proved in a separate subsection. Throughout the section
(M,\alpha)
denotes a smooth compact K-contact manifold. By
Corollary

2.19
there exists at least one periodic Reeb orbit
\ell\subset M
. Let
\tau>0
be its minimal period. We fix a K-contact Riemannian metric
g
compatible with
\alpha
, so that
(M,\alpha,g)
is a K-contact Riemannian manifold, and referring to the notation of
Section

4
, we introduce the normal exponential map of
\ell

(117)

\exp^{\perp}:\nu(\ell)\to M.

We denote with
\delta:M\to\mathbb{R}
the distance from
\ell
, i.e., for
x\in M

(118)

\delta(x)=\min_{y\in\ell}d(y,x).

5.1.
Cut time and non existence of periodic orbits

In this subsection we compute the cut time (cf.
4.2
) of the exponential map (
117
). We then use the result of such computation to show that there are no periodic Reeb orbits away from
\ell
and from the cut locus. In the following lemma, for
s>0
we denote

(119)

B_{s}(\ell)=\{x\in M\,:\,\delta(x)<s\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\rho=\max_{x\in M}\delta(x)
, then the cut time function is constant and identically equal to
\rho
. Furthermore, the Reeb vector field does not have any periodic orbit on
B_{\rho}(\ell)\setminus\ell
.

Proof.

The flow of the Reeb field
R
acts by isometries and fixes
\ell
. Therefore the distance from
\ell
function, which is
\delta:M\to\mathbb{R}
, is invariant under the flow of
R
. The latter fact, combined with
Remark

4.9
, implies that
t_{\mathrm{cut}}:\nu^{1}(\ell)\to\mathbb{R}
is constant along the orbits of
\vec{R}
, indeed

(120)

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{cut}}(e^{t\vec{R}}v)

\displaystyle=\inf\{r>0\,:\,\delta(\exp^{\perp}\circ e^{t\vec{R}}(rv))=r\}=\inf\{r>0\,:\,\delta(e^{tR}\circ\exp^{\perp}(rv))=r\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{r>0\,:\,\delta(\exp^{\perp}(rv))=r\}=t_{\mathrm{cut}}(v),

for any
v\in\nu^{1}(\ell)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since, by
Remark

4.10
, every orbit of
\vec{R}
is dense in
\nu^{1}(\ell)
and
t_{\mathrm{cut}}
is continuous (cf.
Theorem

4.3
), we deduce that
t_{\mathrm{cut}}
is constant. By definition of
t_{\mathrm{cut}}
and
4.1
, the maximal value of
t_{\mathrm{cut}}
equals the maximal value of
\delta
, therefore
t_{\mathrm{cut}}\equiv\rho
. Thus by
Theorem

4.3
the map

(121)

\exp^{\perp}|_{\nu^{<\rho}(\ell)}:\nu^{<\rho}(\ell)\to B_{\rho}(\ell)

is a diffeomorphism. Hence, given
x\in B_{\rho}(\ell)\setminus\ell
there exists a unique
v\in\nu^{<\rho}(\ell)\setminus\ell
such that
x=\exp^{\perp}(v).
Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
e^{tR}(x)=x
. We show that
t=0
. By
Remark

4.9
we have

(122)

\exp^{\perp}\circ e^{t\vec{R}}(v)=e^{tR}\circ\exp^{\perp}(v)=e^{tR}(x)=x=\exp^{\perp}(v).

Both
v
and
e^{t\vec{R}}(v)
belong to
\nu^{<\rho}(\ell)\setminus\ell
, where
\ell\subset\nu(\ell)
is identified with the zero section. By injectivity of (
121
) we deduce
v=e^{t\vec{R}}(v)
.
However
Remark

4.10
implies that
\vec{R}
has no periodic orbit on
\nu(\ell)\setminus\ell
, therefore
t=0
.
∎

5.2.
Volume function and cut locus

We prove that the following function

(123)

\mu:M\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\mu(x)=\mathrm{vol}\left(B_{\delta(x)}(\ell)\right)=\int_{B_{\delta(x)}(\ell)}\alpha\wedge d\alpha,

where
\delta(x)
is defined in (
118
) and
B_{r}(\ell)
in (
119
), is a smooth K-contact momentum map. We then combine the latter fact with
Theorem

2.18
to show that
\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
is a periodic Reeb orbit.

In the following, for
r>0
we denote
S_{r}(\ell)=\{x\in M\,:\,\delta(x)=r\}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The function (
123
) is a smooth K-contact momentum map, moreover

(124)

\mu\circ\exp^{\perp}=2\pi\tau(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)(\partial_{\theta}),\quad\text{on $\nu^{<\rho}(\ell)$.}

Furthermore, the cut locus
\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
is a periodic Reeb orbit. In particular
M
contains exactly two periodic Reeb orbits, namely
\ell
and
\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
.

Proof.

Since
t_{\mathrm{cut}}\equiv\rho
, by
Theorem

4.3
for any
0<r<\rho
the map

(125)

\exp^{\perp}|_{\nu^{r}(\ell)}:\nu^{r}(\ell)\to S_{r}(\ell),

is a diffeomorphism and induces an isometry of Riemannian manifolds, the metrics being

(126)

(\exp^{\perp*}g)|_{\nu^{r}(\ell)}\quad\text{and}\quad g|_{S_{r}(\ell)},

on the domain and on target, respectively.
Since (
125
) is an isometry, and since by
Lemma

4.11

\partial_{\theta}
is a Killing vector field for
\nu^{r}(\ell)
, then
\exp^{\perp}_{*}\partial_{\theta}
is a Killing field for
S_{r}(\ell)
. Note that
S_{r}(\ell)
is an embedded surface invariant under the Reeb flow and, by
Lemma

5.2
, it contains no periodic Reeb orbit. Therefore, by
Remark

2.16
,
\exp^{\perp}_{*}\partial_{\theta}
extends to a global contact Killing vector field on
M
. We denote with
X\in\mathrm{Vec}(M)
the extension of
\exp^{\perp}_{*}\partial_{\theta}
and define the smooth map

(127)

f:M\to\mathbb{R},\qquad f(x)=\alpha(X)|_{x}.

Note that, since
X
is a K-contact field, then
f
is a K-contact momentum map, indeed

(128)

df=d(\alpha(X))=\mathcal{L}_{X}\alpha-\iota_{X}d\alpha=-\iota_{X}d\alpha.

According to
Lemma

4.11
, there exists a smooth function
a:\nu(\ell)\to\mathbb{R}
, such that

(129)

(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)=\left(\frac{\tau}{2\pi}-\phi a\right)dz+ad\theta.

Since
X=\exp^{\perp}_{*}\partial_{\theta}
and
a=(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)(\partial_{\theta})
, we deduce that

(130)

f\circ\exp^{\perp}=a\quad\text{on $\nu^{<\rho}(\ell)$}.

By
Lemma

4.11
the function
a
is radial, therefore (
130
) rewrites in cylindrical coordinates as

(131)

f\circ\exp^{\perp}(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z)=a(r),\qquad 0<r<\rho.

Note that, since
\partial_{\theta}|_{r=0}=(x\partial_{y}-y\partial_{x})|_{r=0}=0
, it holds

a(0)=0.

Let
x\in M
such that
\delta(x)<\rho
. Then
x\in\exp^{\perp}(\nu^{<\rho}(\ell))
, hence we can write

x=\exp^{\perp}(\delta(x)\cos\theta,\delta(x)\sin\theta,z)

for some
\theta,z\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{N}
. Exploiting the fact that
\exp^{\perp}:\nu^{<\delta(x)}(\ell)\to B_{\delta(x)}(\ell)
is a diffeomorphism and (
129
), we may compute

(132)

\displaystyle\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\delta(x)}(\ell)}\alpha\wedge d\alpha=\int_{\nu^{<\delta(x)}(\ell)}(\exp^{\perp*}\alpha)(\alpha\wedge d\alpha)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\delta(x)}\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\partial_{r}a(r)drd\theta dz

\displaystyle=2\pi\tau a(\delta(x))=2\pi\tau f(\exp^{\perp}(\delta(x)\cos\theta,\delta(x)\sin\theta,z))=2\pi\tau f(x).

We have obtained the following equality

(133)

\mu=2\pi\tau f,\qquad\text{on $B_{\rho}(\ell)\subset M$}.

Since
\rho=\max_{x\in M}\delta(x)
, the set
B_{\rho}(\ell)
is dense in
M
. We now observe that
\mu
is continuous. To see this, we write
\mu=\mathcal{V}\circ\delta
, where

(134)

\mathcal{V}:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathcal{V}(t)=\int_{\{\delta<t\}}\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

Since
\delta
is clearly continuous, we need to show that
\mathcal{V}
is continuous. Note that

(135)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}|\mathcal{V}(t+\varepsilon)-\mathcal{V}(t)|=\int_{\{\delta=t\}}\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

By
4.1
the following inclusion holds

(136)

\{\delta=t\}\subset\exp^{\perp}(\nu^{t}(\ell)).

We see that
\{\delta=t\}
is contained in the image, through the smooth map
\exp^{\perp}
, of the measure zero set
\nu^{t}(\ell)\subset\nu(\ell)
. Therefore
\{\delta=t\}
is a negligible set and
\mathcal{V}
is continuous. Thus by (
133
),
\mu
and
2\pi\tau f
are two continuous functions coinciding on a dense subset of
M
. Therefore they coincide on the whole
M
. Since
f
is a smooth K-contact momentum map, the same holds for
\mu
. The validity of (
124
) is deduced from (
130
).

Recall that, by
Lemma

5.2
,
\mathrm{cut}(\ell)=S_{\rho}(\ell)
. Note that any point in
S_{\rho}(\ell)
is necessarily a critical point of
\mu
, because

\mu(x)\leq\mu|_{S_{\rho}(\ell)}\equiv\mathrm{vol}(M),\qquad\forall\,x\in M,

since
\rho=\max_{x\in M}\delta(x)
. Therefore, by
Theorem

2.18
,
\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
is a union of periodic Reeb orbits. We now show that
\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
is actually a single one. Consider the map

(137)

\zeta:\nu^{\rho}(\ell)\to\mathrm{cut}(\ell),\qquad\zeta=\exp^{\perp}|_{\nu^{\rho}(\ell)}.

Note that
\zeta
is a continuous surjective map. Fix
v\in\nu^{\rho}(\ell)
, let
\tilde{\ell}_{1}
be the orbit of
\vec{R}
containing
v
, and let
\ell_{1}
be the orbit of the Reeb field
R
containing
\zeta(v)
. By
Remark

4.9
,
\ell_{1}=\zeta(\tilde{\ell}_{1})
. Moreover,
\tilde{\ell}_{1}
is dense in
\nu^{\rho}(\ell)
, by
Remark

4.10
. Since
\zeta
is continuous and surjective,
\ell_{1}
is dense in
\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
. But
\ell_{1}
is closed, since
\ell_{1}\subset\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
which is contained in the critical set of
\mu
which by
Theorem

2.18
is a union of periodic Reeb orbits. Therefore
\ell_{1}=\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let
\ell_{0}=\ell
and
\ell_{1}=\mathrm{cut}(\ell)
. Note that the following equalities hold

(138)

\mu|_{\ell_{0}}=0,\qquad\mu|_{\ell_{1}}=\mathrm{vol}(M).

Thus the critical set of

contains the union of
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
:

achieves its minimum value at

and its maximum at

. Moreover, since

Theorem

2.18

\mu
has no critical point on

.

5.3.
Rotation numbers and toroidal charts

We denote with
\ell_{0},\ell_{1}\subset M
the two periodic Reeb orbits of
Lemma

5.3
, in particular
\ell_{0}=\ell
and
\ell_{1}=\mathrm{cut}(\ell_{0})
. For
i=0,1
we denote with

(139)

\exp^{\perp}_{i}:\nu(\ell_{i})\to M,

the corresponding normal exponential maps. By
Lemma

5.2
and
Lemma

5.3
it holds

(140)

\mathrm{cut}(\ell_{0})=\ell_{1},\qquad\mathrm{cut}(\ell_{1})=\ell_{0},\qquad t_{\mathrm{cut}}(\ell_{0})=t_{\mathrm{cut}}(\ell_{1})=\rho.

Therefore by
Theorem

4.3
the restrictions of the normal exponential maps yields diffeomorphisms

(141)

\exp^{\perp}_{i}:\nu^{<\rho}(\ell_{i})\to B_{\rho}(\ell_{i}),\qquad i=0,1.

In this subsection we compute, in suitable coordinates, the transition map

(142)

(\exp^{\perp}_{0})\circ(\exp^{\perp}_{1})^{-1}:B_{\rho}(\ell_{0})\cap B_{\rho}(\ell_{1})\to B_{\rho}(\ell_{0})\cap B_{\rho}(\ell_{1}).

In the process we also compute the rotation numbers
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}
of the Reeb orbits
\ell_{0},\ell_{1}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

There exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi:\nu^{1}(\ell_{1})\to\nu^{1}(\ell_{0})
which satisfies

(143)

\exp^{\perp}_{0}((\rho-r)\Phi(v))=\exp^{\perp}_{1}(rv),\qquad\forall\,\,v\in\nu^{1}(\ell_{1}),\quad\forall\,\,r\in[0,\rho].

Moreover, in coordinates
(\theta,z)
of
Remark

4.5
,
\Phi
can be written as

(144)

\Phi(\theta,z)=(-q\theta+mz,p\theta+sz),

where
p,q,m,s\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy

. Furthermore, it holds

(145)

\mathrm{vol}(M)=p\tau_{0}\tau_{1},\qquad\phi_{0}=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{1}}-q\right),\qquad\phi_{1}=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{1}}{\tau_{0}}-s\right),

where
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}
are the rotation numbers of
\ell_{0},\ell_{1}
. In particular

is positive.

Proof.

We define
\Phi:\nu^{1}(\ell_{1})\to\nu^{1}(\ell_{0})
as

(146)

\Phi(v)=-\left.\frac{d}{dr}\right|_{r=\rho}\exp^{\perp}_{1}(rv),\qquad v\in\nu^{1}(\ell_{1}).

Note that
\Phi
is well defined since for any
v\in\nu^{1}(\ell_{0})
the curve
[0,\rho]\ni t\mapsto\exp^{\perp}_{0}(tv)
is an arc-length parametrized geodesic whose length equals the distance between
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
, therefore its velocity at the endpoints has norm
1
and is orthogonal to
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
respectively (cf.
4.1
). Moreover
\Phi
is a diffeomorphism, its inverse being

(147)

\Phi^{-1}:\nu^{1}(\ell_{0})\to\nu^{1}(\ell_{1}),\qquad\Phi^{-1}(v)=-\left.\frac{d}{dr}\right|_{r=\rho}\exp^{\perp}_{0}(rv).

We claim that for any
v\in\nu^{1}(\ell_{0})
and
r\in[0,\rho]
it holds

(148)

\exp^{\perp}_{0}(r\Phi(v))=\exp^{\perp}_{1}((\rho-r)v).

Indeed, consider the curve
\sigma:[0,\rho]\to M
, defined by

\sigma(r)=\exp^{\perp}_{1}((\rho-r)v).

Such curve is a minimizing geodesic joining
\ell_{0}
to
\ell_{1}
whose initial velocity is

(149)

\dot{\sigma}(0)=\left.\frac{d}{dr}\right|_{r=0}\exp^{\perp}_{1}((\rho-r)v)=-\left.\frac{d}{dr}\right|_{r=\rho}\exp^{\perp}_{1}(rv)=\Phi(v).

It follows that
\sigma(r)=\exp^{\perp}_{0}(r\Phi(v))
. We make the identification
\nu^{1}(\ell_{i})=\partial D^{2}\times S^{1}
and compute
\Phi
in cylindrical coordinate of
Remark

4.5
.
From
Remark

4.9
and (
148
) we deduce that

(150)

\displaystyle\Phi(e^{t\vec{R}_{1}}v)=e^{t\vec{R}_{0}}\Phi(v),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},v\in\nu^{1}(\ell_{1}),

where
\vec{R}_{i}
denotes the restriction of
\vec{R}
to
\nu(\ell_{i})
. Therefore we have
\Phi_{*}\vec{R}_{1}=\vec{R}_{0}
. Let
p,q:\nu^{1}(\ell_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
be two smooth functions such that

(151)

\Phi_{*}\partial_{\theta}=p\partial_{z}-q\partial_{\theta}.

From the explicit expression of
\vec{R}_{i}
found in
4.7
we obtain
[\vec{R}_{0},\partial_{\theta}]=0
. Therefore

(152)

0=\Phi_{*}[\vec{R}_{1},\partial_{\theta}]=[\Phi_{*}\vec{R}_{1},\Phi_{*}\partial_{\theta}]=[\vec{R}_{0},p\partial_{z}-q\partial_{\theta}]=\vec{R}_{0}(p)\partial_{z}-\vec{R}_{0}(q)\partial_{\theta}.

6.
K-contact forms on lens spaces

In this section we construct K-contact forms on
L(p,q)
and derive necessary and sufficient conditions for them to be contactomorphic. We fix two positive co-prime integers
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
,
p>0
. Motivated by point
(iii)
of
Theorem

5.1
, we give the following definition.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
a:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function,
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. A smooth differential form
\alpha
on
L(p,q)
is called associated with the triple
(a,\tau_{0},\phi_{0})
if

(178)

\psi^{*}_{0}\alpha=\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{2\pi}-\phi_{0}a(r)\right)dz+a(r)d\theta,

where
(U_{0},\psi_{0})
is the toroidal chart of
3.2
.

Combining the latter definition with point
(iii)
of
Theorem

5.1
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\alpha
be an irregular K-contact form on
L(p,q)
having a Reeb orbit of minimal period
\tau_{0}
and rotation number
\phi_{0}
, then
\alpha
is associated to the triple
(a,\tau_{0},\phi_{0})
for some
a\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
.

6.1.
Existence of forms associated to given triples

In the following three lemmas we derive necessary and sufficient conditions for a smooth differential form
\alpha
associated to a triple
(a,\tau_{0},\phi_{0})
to exists on
L(p,q)
. As in
Section

3
, we denote

U=D^{2}\times S^{1}\setminus\partial D^{2}\times S^{1},\qquad\ell=\{0\}\times S^{1}\subset U.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
f,g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be two smooth functions. Let
\alpha
be a smooth differential form on
U\setminus\ell
which in cylindrical coordinates reads

(179)

\alpha=f(r)dz+g(r)d\theta.

Then
\alpha
extends to a smooth differential form on the whole
U
if and only if

(180)

g(0)=g^{(2k+1)}(0)=f^{(2k+1)}(0)=0,\qquad\forall k\,\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

We switch to coordinates
(x,y,z)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z)
, which are defined near
r=0
, i.e., near
\ell
. The form rewrites as

(181)

\alpha=f(r)dz+\frac{g(r)}{r^{2}}(xdy-ydx).

Thus a necessary and sufficient condition for
\alpha
to smoothly extend to
r=0
is that the functions

(182)

F(x,y):=f(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}),\qquad G_{1}(x,y):=\frac{xg(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}})}{x^{2}+y^{2}},\qquad G_{2}(x,y):=\frac{yg(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}})}{x^{2}+y^{2}},

are smooth near
x=y=0
. It is a standard fact that a radial function
F(x,y)=f(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}})
is smooth at
(0,0)
if and only if
f
is smooth at
0
and all odd derivatives vanish:
f^{(2k+1)}(0)=0
for all
k
.
Moreover
G_{1},G_{2}
are smooth at
(0,0)
if and only if
g(r)=r^{2}h(r)
with
h
smooth and even at
0
, i.e.
g(0)=0
and
g^{(2k+1)}(0)=0
for all
k
.
Hence
\alpha
extends smoothly across
\ell
if and only if the stated conditions on
f
and

hold.
∎

From the second equation we find

(216)

r=1-\sqrt{b_{0}^{-1}\left(-\frac{p\tau_{1}}{2\pi}-\frac{\tau_{1}}{\tau_{0}}b_{1}(r^{2})\right)}.

Evaluating both sides of the latter identity at
\sqrt{b_{1}^{-1}(a_{1}(r^{2}))}
we find

(217)

\sqrt{b_{1}^{-1}(a_{1}(r^{2}))}=1-\sqrt{b_{0}^{-1}\left(-\frac{p\tau_{1}}{2\pi}-\frac{\tau_{1}}{\tau_{0}}a_{1}(r^{2})\right)}=1-\sqrt{b_{0}^{-1}\left(a_{0}((1-r)^{2})\right)}.

Substituting the above identity into (
214
), in light of the polar coordinate expression of
\Psi_{1}
found in (
213
), we deduce the validity of (
211
). Therefore
\Psi:L(p,q)\to L(p,q)
is a well defined smooth diffeomorphism. In remains to show that (
200
) holds. Since the chart
U_{0}
is dense in
L(p,q)
, it is sufficient to show that

(218)

\psi_{0}^{*}\Psi^{*}\beta=\psi_{0}^{*}\alpha.

Hence, we compute

(219)

\displaystyle\psi_{0}^{*}\Psi^{*}\beta

\displaystyle=\psi_{0}^{*}\Psi^{*}\psi_{0}^{-1*}\psi_{0}^{*}\beta=\Psi_{0}^{*}\psi_{0}^{*}\beta=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{0}}-\phi_{0}b_{0}\circ\Psi_{0}\right)dz+b_{0}\circ\Psi_{0}d\theta

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{0}}-\phi_{0}a_{0}(r^{2})\right)dz+a_{0}(r^{2})d\theta=\psi_{0}^{*}\alpha,

where the fourth equality is obtained from (
213
).
∎

6.2.
K-contact forms with prescribed periods

We now exploit the results of the previous subsection to construct K-contact forms on
L(p,q)
.

Theorem 6.7
.

For any
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}>0
there exists a positive K-contact form on
L(p,q)
admitting two periodic Reeb orbits
\ell_{0},\ell_{1}
of minimal periods
\tau_{0}
,
\tau_{1}
and rotation numbers

(220)

\phi_{0}=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{1}}-q\right),\qquad\phi_{1}=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{1}}{\tau_{0}}-s\right),

where
s\in\mathbb{Z}
,
s\equiv q^{-1}\,\mathrm{mod}\,p
. In particular, if
\tau_{0}/\tau_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
\alpha
is quasi-regular, otherwise
\alpha
is irregular and
\ell_{0},\ell_{1}
are the only periodic Reeb orbits.

Proof.

Since
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}>0
, then
\phi_{0}>-\frac{q}{p}
. Let
a:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function satisfying (
183
) and the additional condition

(221)

a^{\prime}(r)>0\qquad\forall r\in(0,1).

Then according to
Lemma

6.4
there exists a smooth 1-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(L(p,q))
associated to the triple
(a,\tau_{0},\phi_{0})
in the sense of
6.1
. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
mp+sq=1
and note that
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
, defined in (
220
), satisfy the following equations

(222)

\tau_{1}=\frac{\tau_{0}}{q+p\phi_{0}},\qquad\phi_{1}=\frac{m-s\phi_{0}}{q+p\phi_{0}}.

Therefore according to
Lemma

6.5
there are two smooth functions
a_{0},a_{1}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(223)

\psi_{i}^{*}\alpha=\left(\frac{\tau_{i}}{2\pi}-\phi_{i}a_{i}(r^{2})\right)dz+a_{i}(r^{2})d\theta,\qquad i=0,1.

A standard computation then shows that

(224)

\psi_{i}^{*}(\alpha\wedge d\alpha)=\frac{4\pi}{\tau_{i}}a_{i}^{\prime}(r^{2})dx\wedge dy\wedge dz,\qquad i=0,1.

Combining (
194
) and (
221
) we see that
a_{i}^{\prime}(r)>0
for all

, therefore
\alpha
is a contact form. Let
R
denote its Reeb vector field of
\alpha
. Computing the Reeb vector field of the form (
223
) yields

(225)

\psi_{i*}^{-1}R=\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{i}}\left(\partial_{z}+\phi_{i}\partial_{\theta}\right),\qquad i=0,1.

Since
\partial_{\theta}|_{r=0}=(x\partial_{y}-y\partial_{x})|_{r=0}=0
, in both cases we have that

(226)

\psi_{i*}^{-1}R|_{r=0}=\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{i}}\partial_{z}.

Since
z
is a
2\pi
-periodic coordinate, we deduce that the curves

(227)

\ell_{i}:=\psi_{i}(\ell),\qquad i=0,1,

where we recall that
\ell=\{0\}\times S^{1}\subset U
, are periodic orbits of

of minimal period
\tau_{i}
and rotation numbers
\phi_{i}
. We see that
\phi_{i}
is rational if and only if
\tau_{1}/\tau_{0}
is rational. Therefore from the explict expression of the Reeb vector field (
225
) we deduce that if
\tau_{1}/\tau_{0}
is rational then all Reeb orbits are periodic, while if it is irrational, then
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
are the only periodic orbits.

It remains to show that
\alpha
is K-contact. Let
g
be a Riemannian metric which is invariant under the
T^{2}
-action of
3.3
. Let us denote

(228)

R_{0}=\psi_{0*}^{-1}R.

Then, in the notation of
3.3
, by (

) we have

(229)

\psi_{0}\circ e^{tR_{0}}(re^{i\theta},z)=\psi_{0}\left(re^{i\left(\theta+\frac{2\pi\phi_{0}}{\tau_{0}}t\right)},z+\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{0}}t\right)=\left(\frac{2\pi\phi_{0}}{\tau_{0}}t,\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{0}}t\right)\cdot\psi_{0}(re^{i\theta},z),\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
e^{tR_{0}}=\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ e^{tR}\circ\psi_{0}
, we deduce that

(230)

e^{tR}\circ\psi_{0}(re^{i\theta},z)=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{0}}t,\frac{2\pi}{\tau_{0}}\phi_{0}t\right)\cdot\psi_{0}(re^{i\theta},z).

Therefore, the flow of the Reeb field is a subgroup of

. In particular, it holds
\mathcal{L}_{R}g=0
.
∎

We have the following immediate consequence of

6.7
.

Corollary 6.8
.

For any
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}\in\mathbb{(}0,+\infty)
such that
\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{1}}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
there exists a positive irregular K-contact form on

having exactly two periodic orbits

of minimal periods

\phi(\ell_{\tau_{0}})=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{1}}-q\right)\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,1,\qquad\phi(\ell_{\tau_{1}})=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{1}}{\tau_{0}}-s\right)\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,1.

6.3.
Classification of irregular K-contact forms

In this section we classify irregular K-contact forms. In particular, we prove
Theorem

1.2
. We start with a preliminary lemma. Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
be co-prime integers with
p>0
. In the proof we use the notation
\alpha\simeq\alpha^{\prime}
to indicate that two contact forms
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are strictly contactomorphic.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
be positive irregular
K
-contact forms on
L(p,q)
.
Then
\alpha\simeq\alpha^{\prime}
if and only if there exist
periodic Reeb orbits
\ell
of
\alpha
and
\ell^{\prime}
of
\alpha^{\prime}
having the
same minimal period and the same rotation number.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
be two irregular K-contact forms on
L(p,q)
admitting a periodic Reeb orbit of minimal period
\tau_{0}
and rotation number
\phi_{0}
. By
Corollary

6.2
there exist two smooth functions
a,a^{\prime}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that, in the sense of
6.1
,
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are associated to triples
(a,\tau_{0},\phi_{0})
and
(a^{\prime},\tau_{0},\phi_{0})
, respectively. By
Theorem

6.6
,
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are contactomorphic.
∎

We can now prove
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

By
Theorem

6.7
, for any
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}>0
with

\tau_{0}/\tau_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, there exists an irregular

K
-contact form on
L(p,q)
with exactly two periodic Reeb orbits, denoted by

\ell_{\tau_{0}}
and
\ell_{\tau_{1}}
, whose minimal periods are
\tau_{0}
and
\tau_{1}
,
respectively, and whose rotation numbers are

(232)

\phi(\ell_{\tau_{0}})=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{1}}-q\right),\qquad\phi(\ell_{\tau_{1}})=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{1}}{\tau_{0}}-s\right),

where
sq\equiv 1\,\mathrm{mod}\,p
. We denote such K-contact form with
\alpha(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})
. Exchanging the role of
\tau_{0}
and
\tau_{1}
, we deduce that there exists another K-contact form, which is
\alpha(\tau_{1},\tau_{0})
on
L(p,q)
having exactly two periodic orbits
\bar{\ell}_{\tau_{0}},\bar{\ell}_{\tau_{1}}
of minimal periods
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
and rotation numbers

(233)

\phi(\bar{\ell}_{\tau_{0}})=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{1}}-s\right),\qquad\phi(\bar{\ell}_{\tau_{1}})=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\tau_{1}}{\tau_{0}}-q\right).

We claim that

(234)

\alpha(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\simeq\alpha(\tau_{1},\tau_{0})\iff q^{2}\equiv 1\,\mathrm{mod}\,p.

Indeed, by
Lemma

6.9
,
\alpha(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\simeq\alpha(\tau_{1},\tau_{0})
if and only if the periodic Reeb orbits of the two contact forms have identical minimal periods and rotation numbers. Since
\tau_{0}/\tau_{1}
is irrational, it follows that
\tau_{0}\neq\tau_{1}
. Consequently, for
\alpha(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\simeq\alpha(\tau_{1},\tau_{0})
to hold it is necessary, and by
Lemma

6.9
it is also sufficient, that

(235)

\phi({\ell}_{\tau_{0}})\equiv\phi(\bar{\ell}_{\tau_{0}})\mod{1}.

The latter condition is equivalent to
q\equiv s\,\mathrm{mod}\,p
, or to
q^{2}\equiv 1\,\mathrm{mod}\,p
.

To conclude the proof, we now show that any irregular K-contact form on
L(p,q)
is contactomorphic to
\alpha(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})
, for some choice of
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}>0
with irrational ratio. Let
\alpha
be a positive irregular K-contact form on
L(p,q)
. By
Theorem

5.1

\alpha
admits exactly two periodic Reeb orbits
\ell_{0}
,
\ell_{1}
of minimal periods
\bar{\tau}_{0},\bar{\tau}_{1}>0
with irrational ratio. Furthermore, by
Lemma

5.5
, these orbits have rotation numbers

(236)

\phi_{0}=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\bar{\tau}_{0}}{\bar{\tau}_{1}}-q\right),\qquad\phi_{1}=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\bar{\tau}_{1}}{\bar{\tau}_{0}}-s\right).

It follows by
Lemma

6.9
that
\alpha\simeq\alpha(\bar{\tau}_{0},\bar{\tau}_{1}).

∎

7.
The trace of the heat kernel

In this section we prove
1.3
and
Theorem

1.4
.

7.1.
Sub-Laplacian and heat kernel

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a compact contact Riemannian manifold (cf.
Section

2
). The sub-Riemannian gradient of
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, denoted with
\nabla^{sR}f
, is defined as the unique smooth section of
\xi=\ker\alpha
satisfying

(237)

df(Y)=g(\nabla^{sR}f,Y),\qquad\forall\,Y\in\Gamma(\xi).

The sub-Laplacian of
f
is then defined as the divergence of its sub-Riemannian gradient

(238)

\Delta^{sR}f=\mathrm{div}(\nabla^{sR}f),

where the divergence is taken with respect to the contact volume form
\alpha\wedge d\alpha
. Such operator is hypoelliptic
[
32
]
, therefore for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, any solution
u
to the equation
\Delta^{sR}u=\phi
is smooth. For a given
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, consider the following initial value problem

(239)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)=\Delta^{sR}\psi(t,x),\qquad&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times M,\\
\lim_{t\to 0}\psi(t,x)=\varphi(x),\qquad&x\in M.\end{cases}

Problem (
239
) has a unique smooth solution which, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, can be written as

(240)

\psi(t,x)=\int_{M}P(t,x,y)\varphi(y)\alpha\wedge d\alpha(y),\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times M,

where the function
P\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times M\times M)
is the so called
heat kernel
. See
[
3
, Chap. 21]
and references therein for a proof of the existence of a smooth heat kernel. We are interested in the asymptotic expansion as
t\to 0
of the trace of the heat kernel, which is

(241)

\mathbb{R}^{+}\ni t\mapsto\int_{M}P(t,x,x)\alpha\wedge d\alpha(x).

In
[
6
]
the first two coefficients of the expansion of
P(t,x,x)
are computed. The main result of
[
14
]
ensures that, for compact
M
, the expansion found in
[
6
]
can be integrated in
x
. The following theorem follows.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
6
,
14
]
)
.

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a contact Riemannian manifold and let

P:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times M\times M\to\mathbb{R},

be the heat kernel associated to the sub-Laplacian. Then, as
t\to 0
, the following holds

(242)

\int_{M}P(t,x,x)\alpha\wedge d\alpha(x)=\frac{1}{16t^{2}}\left(\int_{M}\alpha\wedge d\alpha+t\int_{M}\kappa\alpha\wedge d\alpha+O(t^{2})\right),

where
\kappa
is the sectional curvature of
\ker\alpha
computed with the Tanno connection
[
15
,
45
]
.

In
[
6
]

\kappa
is presented as a local sub-Riemannian invariant, without mention of the Tanno connection. Lemma 13 of
[
8
]
ensures that the latter sub-Riemannian local invariant coincides with the sectional curvature of
\ker\alpha
computed with the Tanno connection.

7.2.
Quasi regular case

In this subsection we compute the coefficients of the expansion (
242
) for compact K-contact Riemannian manifolds
(M,\alpha,g)
with
\alpha
quasi-regular. We recall that in this case the Reeb flow of
\alpha
induces a Seifert fibration of
M
(cf.
Appendix

A
). We refer the reader to
[
25
, Sec. 4]
for a concise treatment of Seifert fibrations induced by Reeb flows.

Proof of
1.3
.

Since
\alpha
is quasi-regular, its Reeb flow induces a Seifert fibration of
M
(cf.
A.2
). In particular all Reeb orbits are periodic and all but finitely many of them have the same minimal period
\tau>0
. Then by
[
30
, Cor. 6.3]
or
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
, one has

(243)

\int_{M}\alpha\wedge d\alpha=-\tau e(M),

where
e(M)
is the Euler number of the Seifert fibration (cf.
Appendix

A
).

Let
M^{\times}
denotes the complement in
M
of the union of the Reeb orbits of minimal period different from
\tau
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the quotient of
M
and let
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be the quotient of
M^{\times}
, by the Reeb flow. Note that, since the Reeb flow induces a Siefert firbation of
M
,
\mathcal{O}
is a smooth orbifold whereas
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
is a smooth surface, which is the complement of the singular points of
\mathcal{O}
(cf.
Appendix

A
). Furthermore
M^{\times}
is a trivial
S^{1}
-bundle over
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
:

(244)

M^{\times}\simeq\mathcal{O}^{\times}\times S^{1}.

Therefore, there exist coordiantes
(x,t)\in\mathcal{O}\times S^{1}
such that

(245)

R=\frac{2\pi}{\tau}\partial_{t}.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{R}\alpha=0
and
\alpha(R)=1
, there exists a smooth 1-form
\beta
defined on
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
such that

(246)

\alpha=\frac{\tau}{2\pi}dt+\beta.

In particular, it holds

(247)

\alpha\wedge d\alpha=\frac{\tau}{2\pi}dt\wedge d\beta.

Let
\kappa
be the sectional curvature of
\xi
computed with respect to the Tanno connection. Since
R
is a Killing vector field for
g
we have
R(\kappa)=0
, therefore in coordinates
(x,t)
we have
\kappa=\kappa(x)
. Hence we can compute

(248)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\kappa\alpha\wedge d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{M^{\times}}\kappa\alpha\wedge d\alpha\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}\times S^{1}}\kappa(x)\frac{\tau}{2\pi}dt\wedge d\beta(x)=

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\tau}{2\pi}dt\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\kappa(x)d\beta(x)=\tau\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\kappa(x)d\beta(x).

Note that, since
g
is invariant under the Reeb flow,
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
inherits a natural Riemannian structure. Moreover
d\beta
is the Riemannian area form of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. By
[
8
, Lemma 13]

\kappa
is the local sub-Riemannian invariant of
[
2
, Remark 2.8]
. Thus, by the latter remark,
\kappa
is the Gaussian curvature of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. By the orbifold Gauss-Bonnet theorem
[
47
, Chap. 13]
,
[
41
]
we have

(249)

\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\kappa(x)d\beta(x)=2\pi\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{O}).

Substituting the latter equation in (
248
) we obtain

(250)

\int_{M}\kappa\alpha\wedge d\alpha=2\pi\tau\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{O}).

Combining (
243
), (
250
) and
Theorem

7.1
, we conclude the proof.
∎

7.3.
Irregular case

In this subsection we compute the coefficients of the expansion (
242
) for compact K-contact Riemannian manifolds
(M,\alpha,g)
with
\alpha
irregular.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
p,q
be coprime integers,
p>0
. Let
\alpha
be a quasi-regular contact form on
L(p,q)
. Then there exist two natural numbers
a_{0},a_{1}
and
\tau>0
such that the set of minimal periods of the periodic Reeb orbits is

(251)

\left\{\tau,\frac{\tau}{a_{0}},\frac{\tau}{a_{1}}\right\}.

Furthermore, there exist at most two Reeb orbits of minimal period less than
\tau
, and are called the exceptional fibers. Moreover, if there are exactly two exceptional fibers, the Euler charateristic of the base orbifold of the Seifert fibration induced by the Reeb flow is

(252)

\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{O})=\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\frac{1}{a_{1}}.

Proof.

The fact that the set of minimal periods of periodic Reeb orbits has the form (
251
) follows from
A.2
and
[
29
, Sec. 4.4]
. Furthermore by
[
29
]
, if there are exactly two exceptional fiber then the base orbifold of the Seifert fibration is the football
S(a_{0},a_{1})
, that is, the 2-dimensional orbifold whose underlying topological surface is the 2-sphere and has exactly two cone singularities of order
a_{0},a_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. The orbifold Euler characteristic (cf.
Appendix

A
) is then

(253)

\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(S(a_{0},a_{1}))=\chi(S^{2})-\sum_{i=0}^{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{a_{i}}\right)=2-\sum_{i=0}^{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{a_{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\frac{1}{a_{1}}.

∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
(M,\alpha,g)
be a K-contact Riemannian manifold with
\alpha
irregular and let
\ell_{0},\ell_{1}
be the only two periodic Reeb orbits, of minimal periods
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
(cf.
Theorem

1.1
). Then there exists a sequence of smooth contact forms
\{\alpha_{n}\}
and Riemannian metrics
\{g_{n}\}
on
M
such that

(i)

(M,\alpha_{n},g_{n})
is a K-contact Riemannian manifold for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(ii)

\alpha_{n}
is quasi-Regular and
\ell_{0},\ell_{1}
are the only exceptional fibers of the Seifert fibration induced by its Reeb flow (see
Appendix

A
), for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(iii)

The following limits hold true in the
C^{\infty}
-topology

(254)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}=\alpha,\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}=g.

Denoting with
\tau_{n}(\ell_{i})
the minimal period of
\ell_{i}
with respect to the Reeb field of
\alpha_{n}
, it holds

(255)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{n}(\ell_{i})=\tau_{i},\qquad i=0,1.

Proof.

Let
\delta:M\to\mathbb{R}
be the distance function from
\ell_{0}
computed with respect to the metric
g
. Let
\mu:M\to\mathbb{R}
be the volume function

(256)

\mu(x)=\int_{\delta<\delta(x)}\alpha\wedge d\alpha,\qquad x\in M.

According to
Lemma

5.3
,
\mu
is a smooth K-contact momentum map. For
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we define the following 1-form

(257)

\alpha_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\mu}\alpha.

Since
\mu
is a smooth K-contact momentum map, according to
Remark

2.17
,
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
is a K-contact form and there exists a Reeb invariant compatible metric
g_{\varepsilon}
such that, in the
C^{\infty}
-topology

(258)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\alpha_{\varepsilon}=\alpha,\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}g_{\varepsilon}=g.

We now compute the differential of
\alpha_{\varepsilon}

(259)

d\alpha_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{\varepsilon d\mu}{(1+\varepsilon\mu)^{2}}\wedge\alpha+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\mu}d\alpha.

According to
Remark

5.4
,
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
are both composed of critical points of
\mu
, therefore

(260)

d\alpha_{\varepsilon}|_{\ell_{i}}=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\mu(\ell_{i})}d\alpha|_{\ell_{i}},\qquad\alpha_{\varepsilon}|_{\ell_{i}}=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\mu({\ell_{i}})}\alpha|_{\ell_{i}},\qquad i=0,1.

Since the Reeb field
R_{\varepsilon}
of the form
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
is defined by

(261)

d\alpha_{\varepsilon}(R_{\varepsilon},\cdot)=0,\qquad\alpha_{\varepsilon}(R_{\varepsilon})=1,

we deduce that

(262)

{R_{\varepsilon}}|_{\ell_{i}}=(1+\varepsilon\mu({\ell_{i}}))R|_{\ell_{i}},\qquad i=1,2,

where
R
is the Reeb field of
\alpha
. Hence
\ell_{i}
are both orbits of the perturbed Reeb field
R_{\varepsilon}
, which, along them, is a constant rescaling of
R
. In particular

(263)

\tau_{\varepsilon}(\ell_{0})=\frac{\tau_{0}}{1+\varepsilon\mu({\ell_{0}})}=\tau_{0},\qquad\tau_{\varepsilon}(\ell_{1})=\frac{\tau_{1}}{1+\varepsilon\mu({\ell_{1}})}=\frac{\tau_{1}}{1+\varepsilon p\tau_{0}\tau_{1}},

where in the latter equality we have used the fact that

(264)

\mu(\ell_{0})=0,\qquad\mu(\ell_{1})=\mathrm{vol}(M)=p\tau_{0}\tau_{1},

obtained from
Remark

5.4
and
Lemma

5.5
. Thus, there exists a sequence
{\varepsilon_{n}}
of positive real numbers such that

(265)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=0,\qquad\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\ell_{0})}{\tau_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\ell_{1})}\in\mathbb{Q},\qquad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

We now prove that the sequences
\{\alpha_{\varepsilon_{n}}\},\{g_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
of forms and metrics enjoy the desired properties.

(i)
We have already proved that
\alpha_{\varepsilon_{n}}
is K-contact and
g_{\varepsilon_{n}}
is Reeb invariant under the Reeb flow of
\alpha_{\varepsilon_{n}}
and compatible with it.

(ii)
Since

Appendix A
Seifert fibrations

We briefly recall some basic facts concerning Seifert fibrations, with particular emphasis on those induced by Reeb flows.
The notion of Seifert fibration is much more general than the one introduced here (see
[
36
]
).

A Seifert fibration of a closed oriented three-manifold is a decomposition of
M
into circles determined by a locally free circle action.

Definition A.1
.

Let
M
be a closed oriented
3
-manifold with an effective locally free
S^{1}
-action,
that is, an effective
S^{1}
-action such that all isotropy groups (stabilizers of points) are finite.
The quotient map

(269)

\pi\colon M\to\mathcal{O}:=M/S^{1}

is called a Seifert fibration of
M
.

The
fibers
of a Seifert fibration are the fibers of
\pi
, which are precisely the orbits of the
S^{1}
-action.
One can show that the set of points with nontrivial isotropy group is a finite union of fibers;
these are called the
exceptional fibers
.
Let
\ell
be one of the latter.
Since the finite subgroups of
S^{1}
are necessarily cyclic, there exists
a\in\mathbb{N}
such that the isotropy group of each point in
\ell
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{a}
.
We call
a
the
order
of the exceptional fiber
\ell
.
Note that in the absence of exceptional fibers a Seifert fibration reduces to a principal
S^{1}
-bundle.

The quotient
\mathcal{O}
is a 2-dimensional
orbifold
.
It is a topological surface, smooth outside of a finite collection of points.
In our case, these points are precisely the projections of the exceptional fibers and are cone singularities whose orders coincide with the orders of the corresponding exceptional fibers.
The
orbifold Euler characteristic
of
\mathcal{O}
is defined as

(270)

\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{O})=\chi(\mathcal{O})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{a_{i}}\right),

where
\chi(\mathcal{O})
denotes the Euler characteristic of the underlying topological surface and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are the orders of the cone singularities.
Two Seifert fibrations are called
isomorphic
if there exists a diffeomorphism sending the fibers of the first fibration to those of the second.
The orbifold Euler characteristic
\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{O})
is a Seifert invariant, i.e. invariant under isomorphism.

Another invariant of a Seifert fibration is the
Seifert Euler number
, which generalizes the Euler number of a principal
S^{1}
-bundle.
In particular, if there are no exceptional fibers, the Seifert fibration is a principal
S^{1}
-bundle and its Seifert Euler number coincides with the classical one.
For a detailed definition of this invariant and for a complete description of Seifert invariants, we refer the reader to
[
36
,
31
]
.

The relevance of Seifert fibrations for our analysis stems from Wadsley’s theorem
[
49
]
, which implies that the Reeb flow of a quasi-regular contact form induces a Seifert fibration.
As a consequence, we have the following proposition (see, for instance,
[
25
, Sec. 4]
).

Proposition A.2
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a closed three-manifold with a quasi-regular contact form.
Then the Reeb flow induces a Seifert fibration of
M
.
In particular, all but finitely many Reeb orbits have the same minimal period
\tau>0
.
The remaining ones are the exceptional fibers of the Seifert fibration and have minimal periods
\tau/a_{1},\dots,\tau/a_{n}
, where
a_{i}
is the order of the
i
-th exceptional fiber.

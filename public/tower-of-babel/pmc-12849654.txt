Embedding social determinants in mHealth for pediatric oncology: co-designing a patient-centred tool for febrile neutropenia in resource-limited settings
Authors: Angélica García-Martínez, Edson Serván-Mori, Horacio Márquez-González, Jennifer Schnur, Emanuel Orozco, Paula Andrea Cárdenas Torres, Nitesh V. Chawla
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFebrile neutropenia (FN) is a potentially life-threatening complication of chemotherapy in pediatric oncology. In resource-limited settings, timely care is often hampered by structural and social barriers. Broader social determinants of health (SDoH)—including food insecurity, housing instability, limited transport, digital exclusion, and caregiver mental health conditions—undermine caregivers’ capacity to respond to FN episodes effectively.MethodsThis study aimed to identify and operationalize key SDoH influencing FN management and integrate them into the co-design of the SaludConecta system, a patient-centered (mHealth) tool tailored to pediatric oncology in underserved settings. From May to July 2021, a sequential qualitative study was conducted at the Hospital Infantil de México, Federico Gómez (HIMFG), a national referral center serving uninsured children with cancer. Using non-participant observation and semi-structured interviews with caregivers and healthcare personnel, we mapped the FN care pathway across household, care-seeking, and hospital levels. Thematic analysis, guided by Levesque’s patient-centred framework on healthcare access, informed the identification of actionable SDoH indicators. We translated contextual barriers into measurable SDoH indicators, cross-referenced with national surveys, and integrated them into an mHealth application to support early warning, triage, and referral.ResultsBarriers such as unstable housing, caregiver fatigue, poor connectivity, and limited access to transportation were found to delay the provision of FN care. These were translated into measurable indicators, such as food insecurity, internet access, caregiver literacy, mental health conditions, and shelter availability, that informed the platform’s design. The SaludConecta system enables offline use, simplifies navigation for users with low literacy, and provides AI-supported triage alerts. It was co-designed with families, clinicians, and hospital administrators to align with clinical workflows and the constraints of the pandemic era.ConclusionIntegrating SDoH into mHealth design enhances equity and responsiveness in pediatric cancer care. By addressing caregiver relations and structural barriers, the SaludConecta system provides a replicable, equity-driven model for enhancing FN management in resource-constrained settings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12939-025-02736-4.

Introduction

Treatment-related mortality among children with cancer remains unacceptably high in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), accounting for much of the global survival gap compared to high-income countries [1]. Febrile neutropenia (FN), a common and potentially fatal complication of chemotherapy [2], is a leading driver of these preventable deaths [1]. Despite its clinical relevance, FN management in LMICs is frequently hindered by systemic barriers, including delayed symptom recognition, limited access to emergency care, and inadequate follow-up mechanisms [3], further exacerbated in pandemic contexts [4, 5].

Despite growing interest in mobile health (mHealth) technologies—particularly those enhanced by artificial intelligence (AI)—as tools for strengthening health systems in resource-constrained settings [6], their application in pediatric oncology, specifically for FN management in LMICs, remains markedly underexplored. Most available digital health solutions have been developed in or for high-income settings [7] and rarely account for the infrastructure constraints, workforce shortages, and persistent digital divides that characterize LMICs [8].

Critically, most mHealth interventions in pediatric care are not designed with a patient- or caregiver-centred lens, overlooking key issues of health literacy, equity, and social context [9, 10]. Although social determinants of health (SDoH)—such as housing insecurity, food scarcity, transportation limitations, and digital exclusion—are known to shape care-seeking behaviors, adherence, and outcomes [11–13], they are rarely embedded into the design or implementation of digital health tools.

AI-enhanced mHealth platforms have the potential to help mitigate structural inequities by supporting early symptom detection, remote monitoring, caregiver guidance, and risk-based triage [14, 15]. When properly contextualized, they may enhance clinical decision-making, care continuity, caregiver empowerment, and trust in the health system—all crucial elements in pediatric oncology. However, without deliberate attention to users’ lived realities and vulnerabilities, such innovations risk widening existing disparities [16]. Ethical and effective deployment requires socially responsive design, culturally appropriate adaptation, and explicit mechanisms to address digital and socioeconomic exclusion [17, 18].

This paper responds to this critical gap by proposing an AI-enhanced mHealth intervention—the SaludConecta system [19, 20]—specifically designed for caregivers of children with cancer at risk of FN in resource-limited settings. Based on Levesque et al.’s patient-centred access framework [21], this study reconstructs the care-seeking trajectory across household, community, and facility levels, mapping how SDoH influence timely and effective access to care. Unlike conventional approaches, we embed equity and caregiver realities at the core of design, operationalizing SDoH into measurable indicators that inform triage, navigation, and referral. By integrating these indicators into the SaludConecta system [19], the project offers a context-sensitive, scalable, and equity-driven strategy to improve the management of FN in pediatric oncology. This approach underscores the pressing need for digital health interventions that are not only technologically sound but also ethically grounded and socially responsive to the needs of the most vulnerable populations.

Methods

Settings

This study aimed to identify and operationalize key SDoH influencing FN management and integrate them into the co-design of the SaludConecta system [19, 20], which incorporates a web and mobile app both centered on pediatric oncology needs in underserved settings. This study was conducted from May to July 2021 at the Hospital Infantil de México Federico Gómez (HIMFG) (https://www.himfg.edu.mx/), a national pediatric institute located in Mexico City. HIMFG serves an uninsured pediatric population, primarily from marginalized areas in Mexico City, the State of Mexico, and several southern states—regions marked by poverty, health system fragmentation, and digital exclusion [22].

The HIMFG attends approximately 254 new pediatric cancer cases annually, maintains approximately 800 prevalent cases, and provides over 6,200 outpatient consultations [23]. Among this population, FN is one of the most critical and frequent complications during chemotherapy, requiring rapid identification and coordinated response to prevent life-threatening outcomes [24].

To address this challenge, the SaludConecta system [19, 20] was developed through an evidence-based process grounded in qualitative research. This study details how insights on the SDoH were systematically identified and translated into the system’s conceptual and functional design. Through in-depth interviews and direct observation with caregivers and health personnel, the research documented how socioeconomic vulnerability, digital illiteracy, housing and food insecurity, and institutional fragmentation interact to shape the capacity of families to seek, reach, and receive timely medical care when a child develops complications following chemotherapy.

Anchored in Levesque et al.’s patient-centered access [21], the SaludConecta system operationalizes the theoretical dimensions of access, including approachability, acceptability, availability, affordability, and appropriateness, into actionable components within a digital platform. The qualitative findings guided the identification of critical “momenta” in the care continuum where structural and individual barriers converge, informing the system’s alert mechanisms, caregiver decision-support tools, and communication pathways with hospital personnel (Fig. 1).

Data collection

We employed two qualitative methods—structured non-participant observation and semi-structured interviews—to explore how SDoH influences access to timely FN care. Both methods were approved by hospital authorities and reviewed by the Ethics and Research Committees of the HIMFG (Approval ID: HIM2022055).

Over the course of two weeks, structured non-participant observations [25] were conducted in critical hospital areas, including the emergency room, oncology units, and nearby caregiver shelters. Observations were guided by a standardized protocol (Appendix 1), allowing for the systematic tracking of patient flow, organizational bottlenecks, and caregiver-provider interactions. A co-author with qualitative research training conducted the fieldwork (PACT) under supervision from two experienced qualitative researchers and co-authors (AGM and EON).

In parallel, six semi-structured interviews were conducted with caregivers of children diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Participants were purposively sampled and interviewed until thematic saturation was achieved [26]. Interviews, conducted under COVID-19 safety restrictions, explored perceptions of FN care, structural challenges, and experiences of vulnerability. Interviews lasted approximately 20 min, were audio-recorded with the participants’ consent, and followed a piloted guide to ensure clarity and consistency (Appendices 2 and 3). Table 1 summarizes thematic areas explored, which informed the design and content of the SaludConecta mobile and web apps. Transcripts were analyzed using semi-verbatim thematic matrices, with dual coding to enhance analytic rigor and interpretive validity [27].

Analysis

We employed a three-stage, layered analytical approach that integrated both deductive and inductive strategies. Our analytic procedures aimed to clarify how observational data, semi-structured interviews, and the SDoH indicator matrix jointly informed the identification and operationalisation of social determinants relevant for FN management.

First, drawing on Levesque et al.’s patient-centred access framework [21], we deductively organised field observations using the domains and dimensions later summarised in Table 1. This allowed us to map organisational and logistical barriers across household life, care-seeking, and hospital-based processes, and to identify structural constraints shaping the FN care pathway.

Second, we conducted inductive thematic analysis of all caregiver interviews. Three researchers (PACT, AGM, and EON) independently coded the semi-verbatim transcripts using the framework method [26], comparing codes until consensus was reached to enhance analytic reliability. Coding reflected all dimensions detailed in Appendix 3, including household resources, logistical barriers, emotional strain, digital access, and experiences with emergency services. This stage enriched the structural insights from observations by grounding them in the lived experiences of caregivers navigating FN during a period of high uncertainty.

Third, we conducted an integrative stage in which convergent themes from observations and interviews were aligned with established social determinants of health (SDoH) frameworks [11–13, 29]. This alignment enabled us to derive a set of candidates SDoH indicators relevant for timely recognition of FN, caregiver decision-making, and digital triage. The resulting indicators—fully detailed in Table 2 and Appendix 4—cover proximal determinants (e.g., health literacy, digital access, household conditions) and intermediate determinants (e.g., transportation constraints, financial stress, social support).

A brief cognitive testing round was then conducted with caregivers in the oncology outpatient clinic. The SDoH questionnaire (Appendix 4) was read aloud to evaluate clarity, acceptability, and emotional response. Caregivers’ feedback informed adjustments to indicator wording and the inclusion of validated instruments assessing caregiver burden, perceived stress, depressive symptoms, and substance use [29–32]. This step ensured that the indicators were not only conceptually grounded but also feasible for routine data collection with vulnerable families.

Finally, during the integrative phase, all analytic outputs were contextualised within Mexico’s ongoing transition to the IMSS-BIENESTAR system [33]. This contextual anchoring did not involve the interpretation of findings but functioned as an analytic sensitising frame. Observational notes documented tensions related to workforce reconfiguration, variability in resource allocation, and operational uncertainty—conditions characteristic of a fragmented and changing healthcare system. These contextual factors informed the specification and prioritisation of indicators that would remain meaningful in unstable care environments and that could support digital tools designed for caregivers facing structural precarity. This ensured that the analytic products—summarized in Table 2 and Appendix 4—were methodologically aligned with the organizational realities in which the SaludConecta system is intended to operate [19, 20].

Results

The observational and interview findings revealed how social and structural determinants significantly shape the continuum of care for pediatric cancer patients experiencing FN. These barriers—intensified during the COVID-19 pandemic—affected every phase of the FN trajectory, from symptom recognition at home to hospital-based treatment. At the household level, caregivers faced compounding challenges, including limited transportation options, unreliable mobile connectivity, overcrowded or unstable housing, and emotional exhaustion. These vulnerabilities restricted timely decision-making and often delayed care-seeking. The hospital’s fragmented information systems, bureaucratic intake procedures, and inconsistent communication further impeded rapid clinical response. Despite these systemic obstacles, caregivers often relied on institutional supports such as social work services and nearby shelters, which served as critical buffers mitigating social risk.

Ethnographic observations and interviews helped identify socially grounded, actionable indicators reflecting proximal and intermediate SDoH. These indicators—such as housing precarity, food insecurity, caregiver mental health burden, and logistical obstacles to care—were directly informed by lived experience and subsequently integrated into the design of the SaludConecta system, a mobile health intervention aimed at improving FN management through equity-sensitive alerts and digital triage. This process ensured that the mHealth tool responded to biomedical urgency and the real-world conditions caregivers face.

To guide this integration, we mapped caregiver and provider perspectives across three critical stages in the FN care trajectory: the post-chemotherapy period at home, seeking care, and arrival at the emergency department (Table 2). Each stage revealed different configurations of vulnerability and decision-making pressure. Caregivers described how contextual stressors—such as financial insecurity, limited access to information, or past negative experiences with the health system—influenced their ability to recognize warning signs, evaluate urgency, and navigate care. Mental health issues, including chronic stress and depressive symptoms, were also frequently reported, particularly among caregivers managing multiple children or lacking extended family support. The perception of hospital quality, trust in providers, and previous delays in treatment shaped motivation to seek care.

Community-level constraints further influenced access to care. For many families, long distances, insufficient emergency transport, or unpredictable road conditions posed significant barriers. Caregivers also described difficulties contacting hospital personnel due to frequent changes in mobile numbers or unstable internet access, which compromised follow-up and remote support during the pandemic.

Healthcare providers contributed additional perspectives on the care process, highlighting structural barriers such as administrative delays, unclear admission protocols, and the lack of standard triage criteria for FN episodes. Providers also emphasized the influence of household-level factors—such as caregiver health literacy, food insecurity, and digital exclusion—on treatment adherence and recovery. Their insights reinforced the need for tools that could systematically incorporate social risk into clinical decision-making.

These insights were translated into measurable indicators through a structured, iterative process. Thematic analysis guided the identification of relevant SDoH factors, which were then operationalized for use within the SaludConecta system [19, 20]. Hypotheses were generated to link each indicator with potential delays or breakdowns in FN care. The indicators were pre-tested with caregivers in outpatient settings, revealing strong face validity but also notable emotional responses—particularly in relation to mental health and financial stress—prompting the inclusion of validated screening tools for caregiver burden, depression, perceived stress, and substance use.

Ultimately, the finalized indicator set reflects individual and structural vulnerabilities that shape timely FN management. Table 2 presents these indicators, grouped by domain, including caregiver digital access, housing conditions, proximity to emergency care, food and financial insecurity, mental health status, and availability of social support. Each indicator functions as a diagnostic lens and a decision-support trigger within the mHealth platform, aiming to enhance responsiveness, reduce treatment delays, and promote equity in pediatric oncology care.

Discusion

Managing febrile neutropenia (FN) among children undergoing chemotherapy in low-resource settings is shaped not only by clinical urgency but by deeply embedded structural constraints [34]. Our findings—derived from observations, interviews, and the operationalisation of SDoH indicators, grounded in ethnographic fieldwork at the Hospital Infantil de México, Federico Gómez (HIMFG), a national pediatric referral hospital in Mexico—demonstrate that socioeconomic vulnerability, digital exclusion, logistical barriers, and inconsistent hospital processes significantly affect the timeliness and quality of FN care. These barriers, intensified during the COVID-19 pandemic, undermined families’ ability to recognize symptoms, seek care promptly, and navigate emergency services, increasing the risk of adverse outcomes [4, 5]. These patterns resonate with prior evidence documenting the heightened fragility of paediatric oncology care in resource-constrained systems [1, 3, 35].

A central contribution of this study is the deliberate integration of SDoH as core components of clinical risk, rather than contextual descriptors [36]. By translating household precarity, transportation constraints, caregiver mental health burden, and digital connectivity limitations into measurable indicators, we operationalized social vulnerability directly into the logic of digital triage. Indicators such as food insecurity, unstable housing, caregiver emotional distress, and digital disconnection were not treated as background variables but embedded into the platform’s triage logic and case-management workflows [19, 29]. This shift from reactive to anticipatory care enabled social workers and clinicians to identify high-risk cases before treatment delays, allowing them to address them proactively. This approach aligns with the emerging literature on equity-driven AI and mHealth in LMICs [11–18], underscoring how digital platforms can shift from reactive to anticipatory care—particularly for families facing entrenched disadvantage. This equity-sensitive design is crucial in a national health system undergoing major restructuring, including the transition to IMSS-Bienestar, which is characterized by administrative discontinuities and resource volatility [33].

The co-design process played a critical role in shaping the SaludConecta system [19], an AI-enhanced mHealth platform designed to address biomedical urgencies and entrenched social vulnerabilities. The tool was developed during the height of the pandemic, a moment that not only disrupted in-person care but also exposed the fragility of hospital workflows and disproportionately impacted uninsured and socioeconomically marginalized families [33, 37]. These disruptions also created opportunities for digital health innovation, particularly in AI and remote monitoring.

By leveraging this momentum, the SaludConecta system [19] was designed to support caregivers beyond the clinic, enabling symptom tracking, distress alerts, and early triage even under conditions of infrastructure stress or social isolation. Caregivers, oncology staff, and social workers contributed to iterative refinements that ensured the system’s usability under real-world constraints. Their feedback directly informed features such as simplified navigation for low-literacy users, offline capability, and automated alerts that combine clinical signs with SDoH-derived risk factors. As a result, the SaludConecta system serves to clearly differentiate audiences: (1) caregivers, who use the mobile app for symptom monitoring, guidance, and emergency navigation; and (2) clinicians and social workers, who use the web platform to review alerts, evaluate social risk, and prioritise follow-up actions. This bi-directional architecture addresses critical gaps in communication and continuity of care inherent to fragmented health systems. A short video of SaludConecta system has been included in 10.6084/m9.figshare.30722150 to clarify functionality and user-facing features.

The development of SaludConecta system [19] was shaped by continuous engagement with caregivers, healthcare providers, and hospital administrators. Their insights informed every stage of the design process, from offline usability and intuitive interfaces for low-literacy users to prompts that flag emotional burden and logistical distress [19, 20]. Features addressing mental health support were particularly vital for caregivers facing isolation, depression, or the cumulative toll of long-term caregiving [38]. These design decisions reflect not only technical considerations but also a political and ethical stance: that digital tools must be responsive to the lived realities of users, particularly those navigating structural disadvantage [39].

More broadly, this study contributes to implementation science by illustrating that co-design is essential—not optional—when developing interventions for families whose care journeys are marked by instability, distance, and structural inequity. Our approach also advances broader lessons for implementing mHealth interventions in LMICs. First, early detection and caregiver empowerment must be prioritized, especially when delays can lead to life-threatening complications. Second, co-design with users is not optional but foundational to meaningful engagement and sustained adoption. Engagement with caregivers allowed us to identify specific “momentums” in the FN trajectory where digital intervention is most impactful, echoing global calls for context-aware mHealth innovation in LMICs [9, 10]. Third, equity must be embedded from the outset, shaping digital platforms’ logic and functionality. Fourth, implementation requires investment in local capacity through digital literacy, role-based interfaces, and psychosocial support. Finally, systems must be designed for instability: capable of functioning offline, syncing asynchronously, and adapting to health system disruptions or public emergencies.

A major limitation of this study is that data collection occurred during the COVID-19 pandemic, a period that substantially altered clinical workflows, emergency triage procedures, transportation availability, and caregiver behaviour. Some barriers identified—such as restricted hospital entry, heightened concern about contagion, and modified care-seeking patterns—reflect pandemic-specific circumstances rather than routine operational conditions. Although this context provided insight into how families manage FN under extreme stress, it may limit the transferability of certain behavioural patterns to non-pandemic periods [37, 40]. Additionally, our qualitative sample draws from a single national referral hospital serving predominantly uninsured, socioeconomically vulnerable families. While triangulation across data sources strengthens internal validity, further evaluation will be required to assess the performance and scalability of SaludConecta in diverse institutional settings.

This work was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic—a context that reshaped healthcare delivery while amplifying existing inequalities [40]. At HIMFG, internal protocols were restructured to manage infection risk, but these adjustments often generated new bottlenecks in care access. The rapidly changing landscape highlighted the need for real-time, context-aware data to inform triage and planning. We developed tailored SDoH questionnaires to meet this need and translated our findings into indicators suitable for AI-based risk prediction models, enhancing clinical and social responsiveness [19].

Ultimately, our findings reinforce the importance of grounding digital innovation in the everyday experiences of families, which are often overlooked in traditional health system design. By making the invisible visible, through indicators that capture logistical, emotional, and material burdens, we built a tool that is not only technologically robust but also socially attuned [36]. Moving forward, we aim to generate community-informed evidence to scale and adapt SaludConecta system [19, 20] for use in other regions and patient groups, thereby fostering a model of care that places equity at its core.

In summary, this study demonstrates the feasibility and value of embedding social determinants of health directly into the functional logic technological tools for paediatric oncology. By centring equity considerations in triage, navigation, and communication, SaludConecta system [19] offers a scalable model for strengthening timely FN management in contexts marked by institutional fluidity, resource scarcity, and social vulnerability. Future work will involve implementation testing, algorithmic refinement, and adaptation across multiple sites to assess impact on outcomes and caregiver experience.

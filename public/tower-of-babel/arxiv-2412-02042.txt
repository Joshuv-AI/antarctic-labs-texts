Abstract
We study the minimal q q -exponent Δ \Delta in the BPS q q -series Z ^ \widehat{Z} of negative definite plumbed 3-manifolds equipped with a spin c -structure. We express Δ \Delta of Seifert manifolds in terms of an invariant commonly used in singularity theory. We provide several examples illustrating the interesting behaviour of Δ \Delta for non-Seifert manifolds. Finally, we compare Δ \Delta invariants with correction terms in Heegaard–Floer homology.

Introduction

\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)
is a
q
-series invariant of a negative definite plumbed rational homology 3-spheres equipped with a
\spc
structure
b

[
8
]
. It recovers Witten–Reshetikhin–Turaev
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-invariants in radial limits to roots of unity as conjectured by
[
8
]
, and recently proved in
[
14
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the behavior of
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)
near
q=0
. In other words, we study the smallest
q
-exponents
\Delta_{b}
in
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)
,

\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)=q^{\Delta_{b}}\bigl(c_{0}+c_{1}q+c_{2}q^{2}+\cdots\bigr),\qquad c_{0}\neq 0.

The rational numbers
\Delta_{b}
were studied in
[
7
]
where their fractional part was related to various invariants of 3-manifolds. In this work, we focus on the actual value of
\Delta_{b}
.

For plumbed manifolds, it is natural to consider the ‘canonical’
\spc
structure
\mathrm{can}
. Related to it, there is a numerical topological invariant
\gamma(Y):=k^{2}+s
(see Section
2.2
).
It appears, e.g., in the study of Seiberg–Witten invariants
[
18
]
of the associated plumbed 3-manifold, and its use in topology goes back to Gompf
[
4
]
. It also plays an important role in singularity theory, e.g., in Laufer’s formula
[
12
]
for the Milnor number of a Gorenstein normal surface singularity.

As the main result of this paper, we prove that for Seifert manifolds,
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}(Y)
can be expressed using
\gamma(Y)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Y=M(b_{0};(a_{1},\omega_{1}),\dots,(a_{n},\omega_{n}))
be a Seifert manifold associated with a negative definite plumbing graph. Let
\mathrm{can}
be the canonical
\spc
structure of
Y
. Then
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}=-\frac{\gamma(Y)}{4}+\frac{1}{2}.

If
Y
is not a lens space, then
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
is minimal among all
\Delta_{b}
,
b\in\spc(Y)
.

The idea of the proof is to express
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
as a minimum of a quadratic form, given by the symmetric intersection form of the plumbing graph, over certain integral vectors. The key is identifying this set over which we minimize (Lemma
3.5
). We show that in this set, there exists a unique vector (up to sign) that minimizes the quadratic form.

Once we leave Seifert manifolds, the computation of
\Delta_{b}
invariants becomes much more complicated. We illustrate this on plumbing graphs with exactly two vertices of degree 3 and no vertices of degree
\geq 4
in Section
5
. In this case,
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
is often smaller than
-\frac{1}{4}\gamma(Y)+\frac{1}{2}
, because we have a larger freedom in finding minimizing vectors. To analyze
\Delta
of these graphs, we use splice diagrams
[
3
,
22
,
24
]
of plumbed manifolds, building on
[
5
]
.

Surprisingly,
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
can also be larger than
-\frac{1}{4}\gamma(Y)+\frac{1}{2}
, as a result of interesting cancellations in the formula for
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y)
, see Example
6.2
. Namely,
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y)
can be expressed as a weighted sum over certain lattice points and those can sometimes be organized in pairs with weights of opposite signs.
The cancellation often can be avoided by refining the weights, as provided by the two-variable series
\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{5.0pt}{10.0pt}}\smash{\widehat{Z}}\,}\!_{b}(q,t)
defined in
[
1
]
. However, Example
6.4
shows that cancellations may occur even in
\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{5.0pt}{10.0pt}}\smash{\widehat{Z}}\,}\!_{b}(q,t)
.

An analogy between
\Delta_{b}(Y)
and the correction terms
d_{b}(Y)
in Heegaard–Floer homology was proposed in
[
7
]
. In
[
10
]
, the first author demonstrated on Brieskorn spheres that unlike
d_{b}(Y)
,
\Delta_{b}(Y)
are not cobordism invariants.

Using the explicit formula for
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
of Seifert manifolds given by Theorem
1.1
, we compare
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
and
d_{\mathrm{can}}
for some classes of Brieskorn spheres, where
d_{\mathrm{can}}
is explicitly known
[
2
]
. We find that
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
is generically much larger than
d_{\mathrm{can}}
. The reason for this discrepancy is that although
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
and
d_{\mathrm{can}}
are both minima of certain closely related quadratic forms, for
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
, the quadratic form is being minimized over a much smaller set of vectors than the one for
d_{\mathrm{can}}
.

The invariants
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)
(and hence
\Delta_{b}(Y)
) were defined for a class of plumbed 3-manifolds given by
weakly negative definite
plumbing graphs
[
6
, Definition 4.3]
. In the appendix, we prove that each such plumbing graph can be transformed to a (strictly) negative definite plumbing graph by a sequence of Neumann moves. This means that the classes of 3-manifolds given by weakly negative definite graphs and by negative definite graphs coincide. For this reason, we do not use the weak notion in this paper.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect the necessary material on plumbed manifolds and
\spc
structures on them.

2.1
Plumbed 3-manifolds

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,m)
be a finite tree with the set of vertices
V
and edges
E
, and a vector
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert V\rvert}
consisting of integer decorations
m_{v}
for each vertex
v\in V
. Let
s=\lvert V\rvert
and let
\delta=(\delta_{v})_{v\in V}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
be the vector of the degrees (valencies) of the vertices. We often implicitly order vertices of
V
, so that
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{s}\}
and write the quantities associated to
v_{i}
with subscript
i
.
We can record
\Gamma
using an
s\times s

plumbing matrix

M=M(\Gamma)
, defined by

M_{vw}=\begin{cases}m_{v}&\text{if}\ v=w,\\
1&\text{if}\ (v,w)\in E,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We always assume that
M
is a negative definite matrix. For a vector
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
, we write

l^{2}=l^{T}M^{-1}l.

Note that the quadratic form
l\mapsto-l^{2}
is positive definite.

From
\Gamma
, we can construct a closed oriented 3-manifold
Y:=Y(\Gamma)
by
plumbing

[
19
]
. For each vertex
v
, we consider a circle bundle over
S^{2}
with Euler number
m_{v}
. Then we glue the bundles together along tori corresponding to the edges in
E
. Manifolds given by this construction, with
M
negative definite, are called
negative definite plumbed manifolds
.

From the construction above, it follows that
Y
is a
rational homology sphere
, that is,
H_{1}(Y)=H_{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
is finite. We have
H_{1}(Y)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{s}/M\mathbb{Z}^{s}
and if we let
H=H_{1}(Y)
and let
\lvert\cdot\rvert
denote cardinality, then we have
\lvert H\rvert=\lvert\det M\rvert
. The quadratic form
l\mapsto-l^{2}
takes values in
\lvert H\rvert^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
as the adjugate matrix
\operatorname{adj}M=(\det M)M^{-1}
of
M
has integer entries.

2.2
\boldsymbol{\Spc}
structures

The set
\spc(Y)
of
\spc
structures on
Y
admits a natural free and transitive action of
H_{1}(Y)
, hence it is finite. It can be identified with the set
(2\mathbb{Z}^{s}+m)/2M\mathbb{Z}^{s}
of
characteristic vectors
. For us, it is convenient to use another identification

\spc(Y)\cong(2\mathbb{Z}^{s}+\delta)/2M\mathbb{Z}^{s}.

(2.1)

This identification is obtained from the usual characteristic vectors via the map
l\mapsto l-Mu
, where
u=(1,1,\dots,1)
, which is justified by the identity
\delta+m=Mu
.

For a vector
b\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{s}+\delta,
we denote the corresponding
\spc
structure as
[b]\in(2\mathbb{Z}^{s}+\delta)/2M\mathbb{Z}^{s}
. We often omit the brackets when it is clear from the context, e.g., we write
\widehat{Z}_{b}
for
\widehat{Z}_{[b]}
.

We consider the vector
2u-\delta
and the corresponding ‘canonical’
\spc
structure
\mathrm{can}=[2u-\delta]
. Its characteristic vector is
k=2u-\delta+Mu=m+2u
. The rational number

\gamma(Y):=k^{2}+s=(2u-\delta+Mu)^{2}+s

does not depend on the plumbing representation of
Y
, so it is a topological invariant of
Y
.
Denote by
\Tr(M)=\sum_{v\in V}m_{v}
the trace of the plumbing matrix
M
. Then
\gamma(Y)
can be expressed as follows:

Proposition 2.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
Y:=Y(\Gamma)
be a negative definite plumbed manifold, which is a rational homology sphere. Then

\displaystyle\gamma(Y)

\displaystyle{}=3s+\Tr(M)+2+\sum_{v,w\in V}(2-\delta_{v})(2-\delta_{w})M_{vw}^{-1}

\displaystyle{}=3s+\Tr(M)+2+(2u-\delta)^{2}.

2.3
The
\boldsymbol{\widehat{Z}_{b}}
invariants

For the rest of the paper, let
Y=Y(\Gamma)
be a negative definite plumbed rational homology sphere with a chosen negative definite plumbing matrix
M
of
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
b\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{s}+\delta
be a vector representing a
\spc
structure
[b]
. For
\lvert q\rvert<1
, the GPPV
q
-series
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)
is defined by

\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)=q^{\frac{-3s-\Tr(M)}{4}}\vp\oint\limits_{\lvert z_{i}\rvert=1}\prod_{v_{i}\in V}\frac{{\rm d}z_{i}}{2\pi{\rm i}z_{i}}\biggl(z_{i}-\frac{1}{z_{i}}\biggr)^{2-\delta_{i}}\Theta_{b}(z),

(2.2)

where

\Theta_{b}(z)=\sum_{l\in 2M\mathbb{Z}^{s}+b}z^{l}q^{-\frac{l^{2}}{4}},

(2.3)

and
\vp
denotes the principal part of the integral and
z=\prod_{i=1}^{s}z_{i}^{l_{i}}
.

We often omit the manifold
Y
when it is clear from the context, e.g., we write
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)
for
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)
. Clearly,
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)
does not depend on the choice of
b\in[b]
. The convergence of
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y)
for
\lvert q\rvert<1
follows from the negative definiteness of
M
.

3
\boldsymbol{\Delta_{b}}
invariants

Definition 3.1
.

The
Delta invariant

\Delta_{b}
is the smallest
q
-exponent in
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)
. If
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)=0
, we set
\Delta_{b}=\infty
.

Using
\Delta_{b}
, we can write the
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)
in the following form:

\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)=2^{-s}q^{\Delta_{b}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i}q^{i}=2^{-s}q^{\Delta_{b}}\bigl(r_{0}+r_{1}q^{1}+r_{2}q^{2}+\cdots\bigr)

for some integers
r_{i}
with
r_{0}\neq 0
. The integrality of
r_{i}
can be seen directly from the defining formula (
2.2
) of
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)
. The form of the exponents is justified by part (1) of the following Lemma (recall that
\lvert H\rvert
is the order of
H_{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
).

Lemma 3.2
.

(1)

The differences between the exponents of
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)
are integers.

(2)

The fractional part of the exponents
(
and in particular of
\Delta_{b})
is given by

\frac{-3s-\Tr(M)-b^{2}}{4}{\pmod{1}}.

Consequently
4\lvert H\rvert\Delta_{b}\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
.

(3)

\lvert H\rvert\Delta_{a}\equiv\lvert H\rvert\Delta_{b}\pmod{1}
for any two
\spc
structures
[a]
,
[b]
on
Y
.

Proof.

(1)
Consider two vectors
l,l+2Mn\in[b]
, where
l,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
. The difference of the corresponding exponents is

\frac{-l^{2}}{4}+\frac{(l+2Mn)^{2}}{4}=l^{T}n+n^{T}Mn\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2)
The exponent of a representative
b\in[b]
reads

\frac{-3s-\Tr(M)-b^{2}}{4}\in\frac{1}{4\lvert H\rvert}\mathbb{Z}

By (1), all the other exponents have the same fractional part.

(3)

We have

\lvert H\rvert\Delta_{b}(Y)-\lvert H\rvert\Delta_{a}(Y)\equiv\frac{\lvert H\rvert\bigl(b^{2}-a^{2}\bigr)}{4}\pmod{1}.

By writing
b=2l+\delta
and
a=2n+\delta
with
l,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we see that

\frac{1}{4}\lvert H\rvert\bigl(b^{2}-a^{2}\bigr)=\lvert H\rvert\bigl(l^{2}+l^{T}M^{-1}\delta-n^{2}+n^{T}M^{-1}\delta\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}.

Remark 3.3
.

The set of
\spc
structures admits a natural involution, called conjugation, given by
[b]\mapsto\overline{[b]}:=[-b]
, i.e., the reflection through the origin under the identification (
2.1
). It is known that
\widehat{Z}_{[b]\vphantom{\overline{[b]}}}(q)=\widehat{Z}_{\overline{[b]}}(q)
, hence we have
\Delta_{[b]\vphantom{\overline{[b]}}}=\Delta_{\overline{[b]}}
.

3.1
The exponents of
\boldsymbol{\widehat{Z}_{b}}

The
\Delta_{b}
invariant is the minimal
q
-exponent in the series
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)
. The exponents are, up to an overall shift, given by the quadratic form
l\mapsto-l^{2}
. Here
l
are lattice vectors that run over a certain subset
\mathcal{C}_{b}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{s}
. We need to identify this subset.

We order the set of vertices
V
of the plumbing tree
\Gamma
by their degree,

V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s_{1}},v_{s_{1}+1},\dots,v_{s_{1}+s_{2}},v_{s_{1}+s_{2}+1},\dots,v_{s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=s}\}.

We have
s_{1}
leaves,
s_{2}
vertices of degree 2 and
s_{3}
vertices of degree
\geq 3
.

The integrand of
\widehat{Z}
contains the rational function

\prod_{i=1}^{s}\bigl(z_{i}-z_{i}^{-1}\bigr)^{2-\delta_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{s_{1}}\bigl(z_{i}-z_{i}^{-1}\bigr)\prod_{i=s_{1}+s_{2}+1}^{s}\frac{1}{\bigl(z_{i}-z_{i}^{-1}\bigr)^{\delta_{i}-2}}.

(3.1)

The integration in (
2.2
) is equivalent to the following procedure: First, we expand each term of the product above using the
symmetric expansion
– the average of Laurent expansions as
z_{i}\rightarrow 0
and
z_{i}\rightarrow\infty
, and multiply these together, giving an element of
\mathbb{Z}\bigl[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,z_{s}^{\pm 1}\bigr]
,

\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{s.\!e.}\bigl(z_{i}-z_{i}^{-1}\bigr)^{2-\delta_{i}}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}}\tilde{c}_{l}z^{l}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}}\tilde{c}_{l}z_{1}^{l_{1}}z_{2}^{l_{2}}\cdots z_{s}^{l_{s}}.

(3.2)

Then we multiply the result with the theta function
\Theta_{b}(z)
in (
2.3
), and we extract the constant coefficient in variables
z_{i}
, giving the
q
-series
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
denote the set of all the vectors
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
with nonvanishing coefficient
\tilde{c}_{l}\neq 0
in (
3.2
). Similarly, define
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\cap-(2\mathbb{Z}^{s}+b)
. The reason for the sign is that we are pairing
l\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
with
-l\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{s}+b
when extracting the constant coefficient. Note that while
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
is symmetric about the origin,
2\mathbb{Z}^{s}+b
in general is not.

Lemma 3.4
.

The set
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
consists of vectors whose components satisfy the following conditions:

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{l=(l_{1},\dots,l_{s_{1}},0,\dots 0,t_{s_{1}+s_{2}+1},\dots,t_{s_{3}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &l_{i}=\pm 1\\
&t_{i}\equiv\delta_{i}\pmod{2}\\
&\lvert t_{i}\rvert\geq\delta_{i}-2\end{aligned}\right\}.

Proof.

The expansion for leaves is simply
z_{i}-z_{i}^{-1}
, giving the entries
l_{i}=\pm 1
. The variables corresponding to vertices of degree 2 are absent in (
3.2
). For a vertex
v_{i}
of degree
\delta_{i}\geq 3
, put
d:=\delta_{i}-2\geq 1
. We have the following symmetric expansion:

\displaystyle 2\cdot\operatorname{s.\!e.}\bigl(z-z^{-1}\bigr)^{-d}

\displaystyle{}=\underset{z\to\infty}{\expn}\frac{z^{-d}}{\bigl(1-z^{-2}\bigr)^{d}}+\underset{z\to 0}{\expn}\frac{z^{d}}{\bigl(z^{2}-1\bigr)^{d}}

\displaystyle{}=z^{-d}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k-1+d}{k}z^{-2k}+(-1)^{d}z^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k-1+d}{k}z^{2k}

\displaystyle{}=\bigl(z^{-d}+dz^{-d-2}+\cdots\bigr)+(-1)^{d}\bigl(z^{d}+dz^{d+2}+\cdots\bigr).

It follows that the corresponding entry
t_{i}
must have the same parity as
\delta_{i}
and
\lvert t_{i}\rvert\geq d=\delta_{i}-2
.
∎

3.2
Cancellations

By the previous section, the
q
-series
\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)
can then be expressed as a sum over
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
,

\widehat{Z}_{b}(Y,q)=q^{\frac{-3s-\Tr(M)}{4}}\sum_{l\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{b}}\tilde{c}_{l}q^{\frac{-l^{2}}{4}}.

The
q
-exponents of
\widehat{Z}_{b}(q)
are therefore given by

\bigl(-3s-\Tr(M)-l^{2}\bigr)/4
for those
l\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
whose contribution does not cancel out in the sum above, in other words,

c_{l}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\\
l^{\prime 2}=l^{2}\end{subarray}}\tilde{c}_{l^{\prime}}\neq 0.

(3.3)

However,
c_{l}
can vanish even if there are nonzero terms
\tilde{c}_{l^{\prime}}
in (
3.3
). We refer to this phenomenon as ‘cancellations’ – see Examples
6.2
and
6.3
.
Motivated by this, we define

\mathcal{C}_{b}:=\{l\mid l\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{b};\,l\text{ satisfies \eqref{eq:cancel}}\}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{b}.

It follows that
\Delta_{b}
is determined by minimizing
-l^{2}
over the set
\mathcal{C}_{b}
. We refer to the elements
l
in
\mathcal{C}_{b}
for which
-l^{2}
is minimal, as
minimizing vectors
.

Lemma 3.5
.

\Delta_{b}(Y)=\frac{1}{4}\Bigl(-3s-\Tr(M)+\min_{l\in\mathcal{C}_{b}}\bigl\{-l^{2}\bigr\}\Bigr).

In this section, we will prove Theorem

Y

\mathrm{can}

4.1
Seifert manifolds

Seifert manifold
Y=M(b_{0};(a_{1},\omega_{1}),\dots,(a_{n},\omega_{n}))
, fibered over
S^{2}
, is given by an integer
b_{0}
and tuples of integers
0<\omega_{i}<a_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,
{\rm gcd}(\omega_{i},a_{i})=1
. It can be represented by a star-shaped plumbing as shown in Figure
1
. The graph consists of a central vertex with the decoration
-b_{0}
and
n
‘strings’. The decorations
-b_{j_{1}},-b_{j_{2}},\dots,-b_{j_{s_{j}}}
on the
j
-th string are determined by the Hirzebruch–Jung continued fraction

\frac{a_{j}}{\omega_{j}}=\bigl[b_{j_{1}},\dots,b_{j_{s_{j}}}\bigr]=b_{j_{1}}-\cfrac{1}{b_{j_{2}}-\cfrac{1}{\ddots-\cfrac{1}{b_{j_{s_{j}}}}}}.

Figure 1:
Plumbing graph of a Seifert manifold.

The intersection form
M
is negative definite if and only if the orbifold Euler number

{\rm e}=-b_{0}+\sum_{i}\omega_{i}/a_{i}

is negative (see
[
20
, Theorem 5.2.]
).

We have the following formula for
\gamma(Y)
of a Seifert manifold
Y

[
18
, p. 296]
, a consequence of Proposition
2.1
:

\gamma(Y)=\frac{1}{{\rm e}}\Biggl(2-n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i}}\Biggr)^{2}+{\rm e}+5+12\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{s}(\omega_{i},a_{i}).

(4.1)

Here
\mathbf{s}
denotes the Dedekind sum.

4.2
\boldsymbol{\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}}
of Seifert manifolds

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
Y=M(b_{0};(a_{1},\omega_{1}),\dots,(a_{n},\omega_{n}))
be a negative definite Seifert manifold. Then
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
of the canonical
\spc
structure satisfies

\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}=-\frac{\gamma(Y)}{4}+\frac{1}{2}.

(4.2)

If
Y
is not a lens space, then
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
is minimal among all
\Delta_{b}
,
b\in\spc(Y)
.

Proof.

We will treat the lens spaces separately in Section
4.3
, so we may assume that
n\geq 3
and
a_{i}\geq 2
for each
i
. Denote
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
.

We will first show that over the set
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
from Lemma
3.4
,
-l^{2}
is minimized exactly by the vectors
\pm(2u-\delta)
. From that, it will follow that
2u-\delta\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{can}}
is a true minimizing vector in the sense of Section
3.2
. The formula (
4.2
) for
\Delta_{\mathrm{can}}
then follows from Proposition
2.1
.

The set
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
consists of the vectors

l=(l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{n},0,\dots,0,t),

where
l_{i}=\pm 1
and

. As

This follows from

Similarly, pick

Let

be two vectors with

that only differ in the

\displaystyle\bigl(l^{+}\bigr)^{2}-(l^{-})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2A}{a_{j}|H|}\biggl(2(n-2)+2\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{l_{i}}{a_{i}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2A}{a_{j}|H|}(2(n-2)-(n-1))\geq 0.

It follows that

Therefore, even in the case that

In the proof of Theorem

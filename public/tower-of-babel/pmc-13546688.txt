Exploring the Mechanisms of Digital Health Technology-Driven Exercise During Pregnancy: A Realist Review
Authors: Fangrong Wang, Yan Li, Lianghui Xu, Yangjuan Bai, Li Geng, Min Li, Chunchun Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundExercise during pregnancy is a key factor in promoting the health of both mother and baby, yet adherence to exercise programs among pregnant women worldwide is generally low. Although digital health technologies have been used in exercise interventions during pregnancy and have shown initial promise, the mechanisms by which they encourage pregnant women to participate in exercise remain unclear.ObjectiveThis study aims to explore the mechanisms by which digital health technologies promote physical activity during pregnancy to guide the design of relevant intervention programs.MethodsThis study adopted a realist review approach, conducted in three iterative stages: (1) development of an initial programme theory; (2) systematic screening of published research and grey literature against pre-defined inclusion criteria, including original research from seven electronic databases (CNKI, VIP Database, PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Embase, and ScienceDirect) searched from their inception to April 2026; and (3) analysis, extraction, and synthesis of evidence from included studies to refine context-mechanism-outcome (CMO) configurations and draw conclusions.ResultsWe included 15 studies, and the analysis identified six positive mechanisms and two negative inhibitory mechanisms through which digital health interventions influence exercise during pregnancy. The positive mechanisms promote exercise behavior among pregnant women and enhance exercise adherence in multiple ways; the negative mechanisms, however, undermine the positive mechanisms and limit the effectiveness of the interventions. Specific contextual factors moderate the activation or inhibition of these mechanisms, and the combined effects of positive and negative mechanisms jointly determine the intervention’s outcome.ConclusionThis study has identified key mechanisms by which digital health technologies promote physical activity during pregnancy, providing a theoretical basis for clinical interventions and improving participation rates and adherence to exercise programs among pregnant women.LimitationsOnly published studies were included, which may introduce publication bias. The variability in study contexts and populations limits the generalizability of the identified mechanisms.

Introduction

Physical activity refers to all bodily movements induced by skeletal muscle contraction that consume energy. Exercise, as a subcategory, denotes planned, structured, and regular specialized activities aimed at improving physical fitness, specifically moderate‑intensity aerobic activity.1 The World Health Organization (WHO) explicitly recommends that pregnant women without contraindications accumulate at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity weekly.2 Regular exercise during pregnancy serves as a core intervention to improve maternal and infant outcomes. Existing studies have confirmed that consistent physical activity during pregnancy brings multiple benefits to both pregnant women and fetuses.3 For pregnant women, it physiologically moderates excessive gestational weight gain and reduces the risks of gestational diabetes and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy.4 Regular exercise during pregnancy also accelerates postpartum uterine recovery, restores pelvic floor function, and lowers the risk of urinary incontinence.5 Psychologically, endorphins triggered by exercise alleviate prenatal anxiety and reduce the occurrence of postpartum depression.6 For fetuses, exercise during pregnancy improves maternal blood circulation and enhances placental oxygen and nutrient delivery.7 Existing studies have confirmed that it also promotes fetal neurological development.4

However, although there is widespread consensus on the benefits of exercise for pregnant women, compliance with exercise guidelines during pregnancy remains generally poor both in China and internationally. Data from domestic studies show that only 21% of pregnant women in China meet the WHO’s recommended exercise standards;8 numerous international studies have similarly reported compliance rates of 30.7% in France,9 25.7% in South Africa,10 22% in Spain,11 and as low as 7% in Australia.12 Although compliance rates vary slightly across countries, the overall levels remain low.

Traditional education on exercise during pregnancy primarily relies on verbal guidance, information leaflets, and in‑person classes.13 It is characterized by low participation rates among pregnant women, poor adherence, and difficulties maintaining long-term commitment.14 Furthermore, this model relies heavily on healthcare professionals and is therefore vulnerable to constraints such as staff shortages and inadequate guidance;14 coupled with a lack of facilities and venues in remote areas, this results in low accessibility and insufficient equity in antenatal exercise education, creating an urgent need for a more efficient educational model.15

Digital health technologies (DHTs) are tools and services that use digital technologies and connected devices to improve health outcomes and healthcare delivery.16 These include telemedicine, electronic health records, wearable devices, mobile health applications, and other related digital solutions.17 With the rapid development of digital health technologies, these technologies have gradually been adopted in exercise interventions during pregnancy because of their advantages, including rapid information dissemination, high accessibility, and real-time feedback, which effectively address the limitations of traditional health education models.18 Existing evidence indicates that digital health interventions can increase pregnant women’s moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) levels, reduce sedentary behavior, and facilitate appropriate management of gestational weight gain.19,20 The growing availability of wearable devices further optimizes exercise assessment. Compared with traditional retrospective physical activity questionnaires, wearable devices enable continuous and objective data collection, improving the convenience and accuracy of exercise monitoring.21 Existing studies have shown that features such as self-monitoring, personalized messaging, regular feedback, social support, and information credibility affect the effectiveness of digital health interventions. Nevertheless, relevant evidence is scattered, and the mechanisms by which these factors facilitate exercise behavior in pregnant women have not been systematically clarified.22,23 Accordingly, this realist review systematically explores the core mechanisms of digital health technologies for promoting exercise during pregnancy, offering evidence to optimize interventions and support digital health implementation within maternal health services.

A realist review is a systematic review method used to analyze the mechanisms of action underlying complex interventions.24 At its core is the Context–Mechanism–Outcome (CMO) framework, which emphasizes that intervention effectiveness is influenced by context and requires clarification of “under what circumstances, through what mechanisms, and with what outcomes”.25 Unlike traditional reviews, this method is better suited to elucidating the intrinsic pathways of complex interventions, such as those in digital health.26 Consequently, this study employs a realist review to systematically examine the key mechanisms through which digital health technologies promote exercise during pregnancy, thereby providing a theoretical basis for optimizing interventions.

Method

This review includes only original research articles and does not require approval from an institutional review board or informed consent from participants. This study is registered on PROSPERO with registration number CRD420251029097. This realist review followed the guidelines of Pawson et al,27 and the completed RAMESES reporting checklist is available in Supplementary Material 1.26 The steps summarized are as follows: (1) define the scope; (2) search for evidence; (3) evaluate key studies and extract data; and (4) synthesize evidence and draw conclusions.

Define Scope

The core of a realist review lies in elucidating the CMO configuration of an intervention to explain “why and how” complex interventions work. Consequently, this review analyzes the CMO relationships underlying digital health technologies that promote exercise during pregnancy and constructs a preliminary program theory to provide an analytical framework for subsequent literature screening, data extraction, and evidence synthesis.

Project Theory

To conduct an in‑depth analysis of the underlying mechanisms by which digital health technologies drive exercise during pregnancy, this study constructed an initial theoretical framework based on the Health Belief Model (HBM). Its core premise is that an individual’s health beliefs drive health behaviors; the model comprises six core dimensions: perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, perceived benefits, perceived barriers, behavioral cues, and self-efficacy.28 It is widely used in research on exercise behavior interventions during pregnancy, and numerous studies have confirmed its effectiveness in improving adherence to healthy behaviors.29 Figure 1Initial theoretical framework based on the Health Belief Model for analyzing mechanisms through which digital‑health technologies promote physical exercise among pregnant women.A flowchart depicting factors influencing the decision to take action based on perceived threats and cues.The flowchart illustrates the decision-making process leading to taking action. According to the HBM, digital health technologies can enhance pregnant women’s awareness of health risks by delivering personalised notifications; by presenting the serious consequences of insufficient exercise during pregnancy through videos, images and text, they help women quantify the severity of these risks;30 Digital platforms can provide real-time feedback on the benefits of exercise, such as improved sleep and relief from lower back pain, thereby reinforcing positive motivation;31 in response to common barriers, digital health technologies can offer flexible exercise plans,32 thereby increasing pregnant women’s willingness to exercise.

Building on the theoretical approach outlined above, this study employs the CMO framework to identify potential contexts, mechanisms, and outcomes associated with exercise interventions during pregnancy and to guide information extraction from the literature. In subsequent analyses, the theory will be validated and refined by integrating evidence from the included qualitative and quantitative studies, ultimately resulting in a comprehensive CMO configuration.

Search Methods

This study retrieved original research from the following seven databases from their establishment to April 2026: CNKI, VIP Database, PubMed, Cochrane, Web of Science, Embase, and ScienceDirect. We conducted an initial literature search, which included published research articles and gray literature. The search terms included three core concepts: digital health technology, pregnant women, and exercise. We expanded these core concepts to ensure more comprehensive results. The specific search terms and search results are detailed in Supplementary Material 2.

Search Results

All retrieved records were imported into EndNote for duplicate removal and preliminary screening according to the above criteria. Two researchers (FRW, LHX) independently performed manual literature screening and data extraction, selecting studies that explain how digital health technologies enhance pregnant women’s exercise adherence. Any disagreements were resolved via discussion with a third researcher (ML) to reach a consensus. Screening began with titles and abstracts to exclude irrelevant papers, followed by full-text assessment against the inclusion criteria. An initial search of the research databases yielded 1693 articles; after deduplication, 1278 remained. Following database screening, a manual search reviewed the references of articles that passed the initial screening to identify any relevant studies the database search may have missed. A total of 15 articles were ultimately included; the specific screening process is illustrated in the PRISMA flowchart shown in Figure 2. Evaluation Literature

Researchers (FRW) created the research project in JBI SUMARI and imported all 12 included studies. Two researchers (FRW, LHX) used the Joanna Briggs Institute’s “Critical Appraisal Tool” to assess the quality of the included studies: randomized controlled trials, qualitative studies, and cross-sectional studies. In cases of disagreement during the evaluation, the matter was referred to a third researcher (ML) for review, and the final quality grade was determined after discussion. The quality appraisal summary table is presented in Supplementary Material 3.

Result Extraction

Repeated reading of the included studies strengthened our comprehension. We developed data extraction tables following RAMESES realist review guidelines,26 covering realist-related information: study characteristics (first author, publication year, country), design (methodology, sample size), intervention implementation, outcomes, and key influencing factors. Supplementary Material 4 presents detailed data.

The Evidence Is Combined, and Conclusions are Drawn

Three researchers (FRW, LHX, and ML) integrated all included literature, extracted mechanisms based on the project’s initial theory and the CMO framework, and organized them into categories. Ultimately, we found that digital health technologies increase pregnant women’s exercise participation rates, increase their motivation, and reduce exercise-related fatigue. Based on the qualitative research, we have identified six positive mechanisms and two negative mechanisms. The positive mechanisms include: (1) unrestricted exercise time and location; (2) sharing of professional knowledge; (3) personalized exercise programs; (4) self-efficacy; (5) social norms and group effects; and (6) repeated and continuous monitoring. Negative mechanisms include: low interactivity and operational complexity. The implications of each CMO configuration are elaborated below. Mechanism 1: Unrestricted Exercise Time and Location

Previous studies have shown that although pregnant women are willing to exercise, they face obstacles, including time and location constraints.34 On the one hand, pregnant women spend time working and caring for their families during pregnancy, neglecting their exercise;35 on the other hand, it is difficult for pregnant women, especially those in remote areas, to attend exercise classes at medical facilities regularly.36 Digital health technology has enabled this group to complete exercise programs at home, thereby increasing participation rates.37

CMO1: Pregnant women in remote rural areas face difficulties traveling to and from medical facilities, and there is limited access to in-person antenatal exercise guidance (C)- Unrestricted exercise time and location (M)- Reduced dependence on intensive clinical support (O).

In remote rural areas, pregnant women face difficulties traveling to and from healthcare facilities, and in-person guidance on antenatal exercise is difficult to access; when digital health technologies are applied to antenatal exercise (C), pregnant women can choose the time and location of their exercise, offering greater flexibility and freedom than the traditional model, which relies on maternity schools (M). This reduces visits to medical institutions, saves significant time, and increases the participation rate of pregnant women in prenatal exercise (O). A study by R. Santos-Rocha et al investigated the use of apps for physical exercise among pregnant women. Among the pregnant women surveyed, 93.3% used apps for physical exercise, with 48.6% preferring to exercise at home and 39% preferring to exercise outdoors.38 The widespread use of smartphones has enabled pregnant women to exercise at home. Using smartphones to guide pregnant women in physical activities can reach a wider audience, allowing medical workers to connect with people in remote areas. This finding shows that mobile healthcare can complement traditional prenatal interventions and improve pregnant women’s health behaviors.39 Research by J. Sandborg et al found that the outcomes of mobile health interventions are comparable to those of traditional methods. Nevertheless, mobile health interventions use fewer resources and are more cost-effective. Most women can easily access mobile health interventions.40 Furthermore, mobile medical guidance for pregnant women’s exercise can transmit text, video, and images in a simple, economical, and efficient manner, thereby improving health management and physical activity for pregnant women.32

Mechanism 2: Sharing Professional Knowledge

The educational model for exercise during pregnancy relies mainly on verbal instruction from obstetricians and health education manuals.13 However, a drawback of this model is that pregnant women struggle to retain professional knowledge, resulting in lower information retention rates. This leads pregnant women to turn to the internet for additional information, but they face challenges such as information overload, varying quality, and difficulty making decisions.41

CMO2: Information on pregnancy and health available online varies in quality, making it difficult for expectant mothers to distinguish between fact and fiction (C) - Sharing of professional knowledge (M) - Enhancing the reliability of sports information (O).

With the quality of online information on antenatal health varying widely, it is difficult for expectant mothers to distinguish between fact and fiction; digital health technology can be used to provide them with professional guidance on exercise during pregnancy (C), pregnant women can communicate with experts (M), thereby increasing their confidence in their knowledge of exercise during pregnancy and reducing barriers to starting exercise (O). A multidisciplinary team collaborated to develop an app for pregnant women, with content designed in accordance with evidence and guidelines. Midwives use this app to provide information to pregnant women, helping reduce the heterogeneity of health and pregnancy information they encounter online.19 Moreover, telemedicine enables professionals to reach more people and share resources, making it particularly feasible and cost-effective for pregnant women in remote areas who rarely visit medical centers. Pregnant women can use the app to reinforce the information conveyed during their initial face-to-face consultation with professionals, successfully solving the management difficulties faced by professionals in the past and the problem of insufficient consultation time faced by pregnant women.19,39

Mechanism 3: Personalized Exercise Programs

Most pregnant women recognize the benefits of exercise during pregnancy and plan to exercise, but when they start, they lack specific exercise plans. In addition, 80% of pregnant women say they cannot independently assess exercise safety and urgently need individualized plans rather than general recommendations.42 Moreover, maintaining a long-term commitment to exercise is difficult without an exercise plan.

CMO3: Pregnant women have individual differences in their exercise needs and are unable to access one-to-one in-person exercise programs (C)- Personalized exercise programs (M)- Facilitate achievement of physical activity goals (O).

Because pregnant women have varying exercise needs and may be unable to access one-to-one, in-person exercise programs (C), digital health technologies can provide personalized exercise programs tailored to their specific circumstances (M). This enables them to identify weaknesses during exercise, learn strategies to address them, and achieve their exercise goals (O). Research by R. Tinius et al revealed that pregnant women want to know precisely how they should exercise at specific times during pregnancy and after childbirth. The existing information mostly recommends exercise but does not provide specific guidance on the intensity, type, or duration of exercise tailored to individual characteristics. This is the main factor limiting their ability to exercise during pregnancy.43 In a study by J. Choi et al on pregnant women who exercised infrequently during pregnancy, the women had the opportunity to review the obstacles they faced during exercise with the researchers, who then helped them develop specific strategies to overcome them. Therefore, their feelings of fatigue are significantly reduced during exercise.44 The app’s self-monitoring and feedback features can record and report diet, physical activity, and weight, and then provide customized plans in pictures and text to help pregnant women achieve their exercise goals.45

Mechanism 4: Self-Efficacy

Pregnant women often experience pregnancy reactions, such as morning sickness, fatigue, and lower back pain, during pregnancy. When a fetus presses on a pregnant woman’s pubic bone, it can cause pubic bone pain.46 Therefore, the physical condition of pregnant women limits their confidence in exercise, leading them to fear it and worry that it increases the burden on their bodies.47 In psychological terms, they worry about the safety of the fetus and neglect the benefits of exercise.48 Moreover, some regions have also been influenced by cultural factors, with elderly individuals advising pregnant women to rest during pregnancy.36 Pregnant women have experienced misconceptions due to insufficient knowledge about exercise during pregnancy.

CMO4: Pregnant women lack continuous feedback, making it difficult for them to gauge their own progress (C)- Enhanced self-efficacy (M)-Boosting confidence in exercise for pregnant women (O).

Pregnant women lack continuous feedback, making it difficult for them to gauge their own progress. Digital health technologies used for exercise guidance and monitoring during pregnancy (C) provide continuous exercise feedback; when supplemented with professional support, this improves pregnant women’s understanding and enhances their sense of self-efficacy (M), thereby increasing their confidence in their ability to achieve their exercise goals during pregnancy (O). Positive health education can improve exercise levels and self-efficacy during pregnancy.43 Research has shown that pregnant women’s BMI is related to their exercise self-efficacy. Pregnant women who use exercise apps show improved self-efficacy. Nevertheless, owing to the small sample size, it cannot be concluded that moderate-intensity exercise is statistically significant for pregnant women. Possible reasons include weight gain during pregnancy and the continuous growth of the fetus, which inhibits exercise, as well as pelvic pain, which reduces exercise capacity.43,44 However, regarding psychological well-being, pregnant women with greater self-efficacy tend to experience greater overall confidence and positive happiness during exercise, along with reduced fatigue and psychological distress.43

Mechanism 5: Social Norms and Group Effects

Research shows that a lack of social support is a significant barrier to pregnant women participating in physical activity.49 Research by K.W. Shum et al revealed that some pregnant women’s family members and friends have negative attitudes toward exercise during pregnancy, which exacerbates the loneliness experienced by pregnant women during exercise.48 In addition, approximately 11% of pregnant women lack information exchange and peer support, and this information isolation reinforces their feelings of helplessness.50

CMO5: A narrow social circle during pregnancy, with a lack of channels for communication with fellow expectant and new mothers (C)- Social norms and group effects (M)-Creating a social environment that promotes exercise for pregnant women (O).

During pregnancy, social circles tend to be limited, and there is a lack of channels for communication with fellow expectant and new mothers. When digital health technologies support pregnant women’s exercise routines, they can be managed in groups, with communities established and exercise check-in activities organized (C). Through social norms and group dynamics, pregnant women can be guided to exercise regularly (M). Peer pressure from fellow participants can be transformed into motivation for exercise, increasing enthusiasm; sharing experiences after achieving goals helps foster a social environment that promotes physical activity among pregnant women (O). In addition, most women believe that having an app specifically designed for pregnancy is very important.38 Engaging with healthcare and fitness professionals through the app can enhance their knowledge. Socializing with other mothers on the app and listening to their shared experiences can help prepare them for childbirth and the postpartum period.38 Research by R. Tinius et al indicates that adding a community forum feature to an app can provide social support for pregnant women. Through the community forum, they can see what other pregnant women in similar situations are doing and discuss with others to create a social environment that promotes exercise during pregnancy. A positive social atmosphere can provide emotional support and encourage them to achieve their exercise goals.43

Mechanism 6: Repeatable and Continuous Monitoring

Traditional methods of monitoring exercise during pregnancy often rely on exercise diaries and questionnaire-based assessments to record pregnant women’s exercise habits. However, these methods are prone to memory bias and are influenced by subjective factors among pregnant women, leading to inaccurate records.51 Furthermore, pregnant women must rely on their willpower to complete their exercise plans. However, pregnant women are often distracted by work and family matters, making it challenging to complete their exercise plans on time and in full.48

CMO6: Pregnant women have poor adherence to self-monitoring and lack ongoing external encouragement (C)- Repeatable and continuous monitoring (M)- Cultivating individuals’ long-term exercise habits (O).

As pregnant women often have poor adherence to self-monitoring and lack ongoing external encouragement, the use of digital health technologies to monitor their physical activity (C)—by providing real-time measurements and prompt feedback on their activity levels, as well as enabling long-term monitoring and reminders to exercise (M)—can help them develop a habit of regular exercise (O). Self-monitoring is seen as motivational, with push notifications reminding pregnant women of the importance of healthy lifestyles and increasing their awareness of weight gain during pregnancy, as well as of their dietary and exercise habits. This has led to higher app usage rates and helped pregnant women establish healthier habits.46 Currently, smart monitoring devices are widely used in monitoring pregnant women. P. Henriksson et al45 used wrist-worn triaxial accelerometers to measure physical activity and sedentary behavior in pregnant women objectively. In Téoule et al ‘s study,52 pregnant women used fitness trackers to measure their steps and were required to enter their average weekly step count into the app. The app reminds pregnant women to exercise if no data is entered. The results revealed that the experimental group of pregnant women increased their steps and physical activity levels and reduced their sedentary time.19 Research shows that data collected from smart devices can be summarized graphically, allowing pregnant women to see whether they are approaching or have achieved their goals, which can help them achieve their exercise goals to some extent.45

Mechanism 7: Low Interactivity

Existing systematic reviews indicate that online interventions relying solely on text messages can only disseminate basic health knowledge and are unlikely to improve pregnant women’s physical activity behaviour.37 At the same time, a qualitative study showed that existing online platforms generally lack modules for peer communication and real-time interaction; this lack of interactive features is a key factor behind pregnant women dropping out of the intervention program and a sustained decline in adherence.53

CMO7: Pregnant women with a baseline history of prolonged sitting and a sedentary lifestyle received only one-way digital text-based health education (C)- Low interactivity (M)- It is difficult to change long-standing lifestyle habits, and it is not possible to encourage physical activity (O).

Pregnant women with a baseline history of prolonged sitting and a sedentary lifestyle who receive only one-way digital text-based health education (C) often fail to achieve the expected outcomes of the intervention due to a lack of two-way communication and interaction (M) with other pregnant women and healthcare professionals; this single-intervention model struggles to break pregnant women’s established lifestyle patterns and is unable to effectively promote the development of physical activity behaviours during pregnancy (O). A related survey explored pregnant women’s needs and preferences for digital tools for exercise during pregnancy. The results showed that when using fitness apps, pregnant women generally hoped to consult healthcare professionals or specialist fitness coaches to address their health concerns, while also hoping to establish channels for peer interaction to share practical experiences.38 Research findings by JiWon Choi et al indicate that, compared with basic applications, involving obstetric professionals can provide a powerful behavioral incentive, as it enables expectant and new mothers to access professional guidance while reducing the time spent traveling to and from healthcare facilities.44 Research by Thomas et al confirms that purely digital interventions lacking interactive design are ineffective in managing gestational weight gain (GWG); incorporating personalized feedback, ongoing exercise motivation and two-way interaction modules is key to enhancing the effectiveness of such interventions.31

Mechanism 8: Operational Complexity

Pre-pregnancy obesity increases the risk of various adverse pregnancy complications, such as gestational diabetes and gestational hypertension.54 Digital health tools are now widely used to encourage pregnant women with obesity to engage in regular exercise, but there is significant heterogeneity in the outcomes achieved by different intervention programmes.37 Relevant feasibility studies suggest that the cumbersome and complex operational procedures of digital intervention platforms can significantly reduce pregnant women’s willingness to continue participating and their long-term adherence to the programme.55

CMO8: Pregnant women with severe obesity (BMI > 35 kg/m2) require continuous daily monitoring of their physical activity and must manually submit self-reported activity data via the web portal (C)– operational complexity (M)– resulting in a significant lack of activity data, which fails to improve adherence to the exercise program (O).

Pregnant women with severe obesity (BMI > 35 kg/m2) were required to wear a pedometer daily to monitor their physical activity and to manually submit self-reported activity data via a web portal (C); however, due to the numerous and complex steps involved in the process (M), this ultimately resulted in a significant amount of missing activity data, making it difficult to improve the women’s adherence to the exercise programme (O). Research by Santos-Rocha et al indicates that expectant mothers generally expect pregnancy apps to be easy to use and capable of automatically synchronizing data, thereby reducing the burden of manual data entry; cumbersome platform processes directly reduce the likelihood of continued use.38 Research by Johanna Sandborg et al also found that manually recording physical activity and diet daily is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and prone to memory bias, resulting in data that lacks reliability.46 Findings from a study by Jai N Darvall et al, in which pregnant women were asked to record their daily step counts and upload the data to a system, indicate that frequent forgetfulness about wearing the devices led to significant data loss. The researchers proposed integrating the step-counting function into mobile phones and simplifying the process to reduce data loss.56

Discussion

Summary of Key Findings

Existing research consistently indicates that applying digital health technologies in exercise interventions during pregnancy is highly effective, increasing exercise participation among pregnant women, optimizing exercise behavior, and improving maternal health.57,58 Building on this foundation, this review, within the HBM framework, identifies and distills six major positive mechanisms and two negative mechanisms through which digital health technologies drive exercise during pregnancy. The positive mechanisms include: Mechanism 1: Unrestricted time and location for exercise; Mechanism 2: Sharing of specialist knowledge; Mechanism 3: Personalized exercise programs; Mechanism 4: Self-efficacy; Mechanism 5: Social norms and group effects; Mechanism 6: Repeated and continuous monitoring. The negative mechanisms include Mechanism 7: Low interactivity; Mechanism 8: Operational complexity.

Taking into account the various mechanisms identified in this study, it appears that none of them directly enhances adherence to exercise during pregnancy, but rather play an indirect role in promoting it. These mechanisms exert their influence from different perspectives, ultimately working together to achieve the overarching goal of improving exercise adherence among pregnant women. They primarily operate through three interrelated and complementary dimensions: creating conditions conducive to exercise, changing perceptions of exercise, and enhancing individual exercise capacity. These dimensions are interconnected and work together to encourage pregnant women to adopt exercise behaviors.

Mechanism 1 breaks down time and place constraints on exercise, creating practical conditions for pregnant women to exercise daily. Mechanism 2 opens channels for accessing specialist information, helping pregnant women develop a clear understanding of health and risk. Mechanisms 3 and 4 primarily address practical issues pregnant women encounter during exercise, working together to enhance their physical capacity. Guidance based on personalized exercise plans can encourage more pregnant women to exercise during pregnancy, increasing overall participation rates; at the same time, developing a sense of self-efficacy can strengthen their confidence in adhering to and completing their exercise plans. Mechanisms 5 and 6 focus on the external environment, creating a supportive atmosphere for exercise among pregnant women. Mechanism 5 leverages social norms and group dynamics to encourage mutual influence and accountability among pregnant women participating in exercise, thereby boosting their motivation to exercise proactively; Mechanism 6 serves as a crucial safeguard for maintaining long-term exercise habits. Through continuous, repeated monitoring and feedback, it ensures that pregnant women remain in an environment that encourages and guides exercise, helping them gradually transform prenatal exercise into a lifelong habit.

However, negative mechanisms may also hinder the activation and operation of other mechanisms in certain circumstances. When interactivity is low, pregnant women lose the opportunity to communicate with healthcare professionals and other expectant mothers, thereby hindering the activation and operation of Mechanisms 2 and 5. When the intervention process becomes overly complex, it directly reduces pregnant women’s willingness to comply, leading to the failure of Mechanisms 1 and 6.

The positive mechanism of action identified in this study is consistent with the study’s theoretical framework. Firstly, digital health technologies enable the sharing of expert knowledge, thereby altering pregnant women’s perceptions of exercise during pregnancy, heightening their awareness of disease susceptibility and severity, and providing them with a deeper understanding of the risks posed by inactivity to both themselves and their fetuses.59 However, even when individuals perceive health risks and recognize the benefits of healthy behaviors, they may still delay taking action due to a lack of actionable cues. Consequently, personalized exercise programs are provided for pregnant women, using smart devices to monitor data such as step count and exercise activity continuously and regularly, helping them dynamically adjust their exercise programs and set targets.60,61 Through the design of immediate feedback, the belief that “I can do this” is embedded throughout the health decision-making process, thereby increasing their self-efficacy62 and helping them overcome difficulties encountered during exercise, thus enhancing their internal action cues;44 Furthermore, by leveraging social norms and group dynamics to foster a supportive social environment for exercise, external cues to action can be enhanced, thereby encouraging expectant mothers to take action.63

However, analysis has revealed that certain factors continue to influence the mechanism’s progress. Broadly speaking, these can be categorized into three levels: individual, technical, and intervention methods—first, the main influencing factors at the personal level stem from the pregnant women themselves. Studies have shown that pregnant women’s age affects their use of digital health technologies. For example, younger pregnant women may be more receptive to smart devices, whereas older pregnant women may abandon their use due to operational difficulties.64 Second, at the technological level, qualitative research has conducted interviews on the functional design of pregnancy-specific apps to understand pregnant women’s primary needs. The study revealed that complex user interfaces and cumbersome login processes reduce pregnant women’s desire to use apps. Additionally, pregnant women’s cognitive and educational levels affect their use of digital health technologies.65 Moreover, it is necessary to monitor data on pregnant women. Research by D.M. Levine et al shows that users still doubt privacy protection in digital health and that security needs improvement.66 Thirdly, at the level of intervention methods. The involvement of obstetric healthcare professionals is key to enhancing the effectiveness of digital interventions, which can reinforce face-to-face education.44 When implementing digital health interventions, obstetricians should use their professional authority, using antenatal check-ups to provide exercise guidance and help pregnant women and new mothers adjust their exercise plans dynamically. Digital health technologies should serve solely as supplementary tools for clinical education and cannot replace professional guidance from healthcare professionals.

This study conducted a meta-analysis of various digital health interventions, including push notifications, pregnancy-specific apps and online guidance; this approach may mask the heterogeneity in effects arising from differences in the internal components of the interventions. Based on the characteristics of the included interventions, digital health interventions for exercise during pregnancy can be categorised into three models: the first is a purely digital intervention, which relies on automated programmes to deliver health information without any human involvement throughout the process; the second category comprises digital-human collaborative interventions, which build upon the purely digital model by introducing obstetric professionals to encourage pregnant women to exercise; the third category comprises enhanced hybrid interventions, which incorporate monitoring devices such as pedometers into the digital-human collaborative model to monitor the physical activity of pregnant women dynamically. Current evidence suggests that the first intervention model can only facilitate a one-way transfer of health knowledge, and that the intervention’s effectiveness is limited.37 Whilst the second model can utilize professionals to provide external supervision, behavioral feedback largely relies on self-reports from pregnant women and mothers, which are prone to reporting bias.46 The third model enables the coordinated operation of multiple mechanisms—including knowledge dissemination, professional supervision, and real-time feedback based on objective data—which is more conducive to sustaining exercise behavior during pregnancy. This finding is consistent with the conclusions of a study by Rhodes et al, which noted that effective digital health interventions tend to incorporate key elements such as proactive reminders, interaction with healthcare professionals, and progress feedback.37 Consequently, when pregnant women face adverse circumstances such as insufficient digital literacy, limited access to hardware, or a lack of collaborative support from clinical healthcare professionals, mechanisms for positive behavioral change are unlikely to be activated, and intervention efficacy will decline. Given the advantages of digital health technologies in health education during pregnancy, future research should further explore how to optimize these technologies to improve exercise adherence among pregnant women.

Advantages and Limitations

The strengths of this paper lie mainly in the following two aspects. First, in complex intervention research, realist reviews have demonstrated significant methodological strengths. For example, in chronic disease management, Janet Harris et al52 used this method to explore the mechanisms behind community participation in diabetes management and its subsequent impact on health outcomes; Natasha Bradley et al67 successfully used this method to examine the mechanisms underlying the effectiveness of palliative care for cancer patients. These cases demonstrate that realist reviews can effectively analyze complex interventions characterized by “context-dependence and multimechanism interaction”. Exercise during pregnancy shares these characteristics, and this study applies the realist review method in this context for the first time, aligning with its methodological strengths. Second, most previous reviews have synthesized the benefits of exercise during pregnancy for maternal health and its impacts on offspring health, while repeatedly identifying intervention feasibility and barriers. However, they lack research on mechanisms, and their implementation faces challenges. This realist review aims to explore the underlying mechanisms behind exercise during pregnancy among pregnant women driven by digital health technologies, going beyond the superficial descriptions of intervention effects in traditional reviews. These findings can serve as a reference for future interventions involving exercise during pregnancy and increase the likelihood of successful interventions.

The limitations of this paper include the following. First, the included literature spans only to 2016, which is relatively recent. This is because digital health technology is a relatively new concept, both a limitation and an advantage of this paper. Second, most of the literature included is from Europe and the Americas, and research is lacking in Asian regions. This means that regional and cultural differences may hinder the dissemination of the final research results. Third, because specialized applications for pregnant women are lacking, the measurement tools used in the literature included in this paper are not standardized, leading to discrepancies in the research results reported across papers.

Future Research Directions

First, research on digital health technologies has small sample sizes and high population heterogeneity.68 Future research should include pregnant women from different racial, regional, and cultural backgrounds in large-scale, multicenter studies. In particular, relatively little research has been conducted in Asia, and further studies are needed to ensure that the results can be applied more widely. Second, existing research shows that measurement tools for exercise during pregnancy have limitations, and there is no uniform standard for measuring exercise intensity among pregnant women. In the future, it will be necessary to develop more reliable and objective monitoring tools to improve data quality and reduce self-reporting bias. In addition, the currently available applications are not comprehensive enough to adequately meet pregnant women’s needs. In the future, an evidence-based application could be developed to provide pregnant women with professional guidance. Furthermore, it would be ideal if the app could connect to smart bracelets currently available on the market to monitor objective data on pregnant women. Moreover, it should include a community forum where pregnant women can communicate with one another and receive social support. Finally, future research must consider cost issues to avoid exacerbating the gap between developed and economically underdeveloped regions caused by unbalanced economic development. Efforts should reduce the cost of facilities and equipment so economically underdeveloped areas can also apply digital health technologies to guide pregnant women in exercise.

Conclusion

Through a realist review, this study identified six positive mechanisms and two negative mechanisms by which digital health technologies influence exercise during pregnancy. Overall, digital health technology interventions can address the shortcomings of traditional in-person exercise guidance for pregnant women and enhance exercise adherence in multiple dimensions. However, the activation of these mechanisms is markedly context-dependent: the positive mechanisms can be fully utilised in settings characterised by poor access to healthcare, conflicting health information, and a lack of personalised guidance and peer support; conversely, in situations where the intervention lacks interactivity, is cumbersome to use, or where pregnant women have poor baseline exercise habits or are severely obese, these mechanisms are significantly constrained and struggle to promote improvements in exercise behaviour effectively. This study clarifies the conditions under which digital health technologies are applicable and their limitations, providing a theoretical framework for clinicians to develop precise antenatal exercise intervention programs and help optimize pregnancy outcomes.

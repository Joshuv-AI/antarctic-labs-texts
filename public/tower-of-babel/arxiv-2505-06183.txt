Abstract
. In this paper we study the long time behaviour of mean field games systems with fractional diffusion, modeling the case that the individual dynamics of the players is driven by independent jump processes and controlled through the drift term, while being confined by an external field in order to guarantee ergodicity. In the case of globally Lipschitz, locally uniformly convex Hamiltonian, and weakly coupled costs satisfying the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition, we prove that there is a unique solution ( u T , m T ) (u_{T},m_{T}) to the mean field game problem in ( 0 , T ) (0,T) and we show that, if T T is sufficiently large, ( u T , m T ) (u_{T},m_{T}) satisfies the so-called turnpike property, namely it is exponentially close to the (unique) stationary ergodic state for any proportionally long intermediate time.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we analyze a class of forward-backward systems of partial differential equations (PDEs) arising in the theory of Mean Field Games (MFGs). This theory was initiated by J.-M. Lasry and P.-L. Lions
[
52
,
53
,
54
]
, and extensively developed by P.-L. Lions in several courses held at Collège de France
[
55
]
. Similar ideas were introduced at the same time by P. Caines, M. Huang and R. Malhamé
[
44
]
from the perspective of stochastic control of McKean-Vlasov equations. The purpose of this theory is to describe Nash equilibria in large populations of rational agents, by deriving a mean-field limit of Nash equilibria in
N
-players differential games, under suitable symmetry and weak interaction assumptions. The macroscopic description proposed in the limit can be summarized by a system of PDEs coupling the equation of the value function of the generic agent with the equation of the distribution density of the population. Usually the agents are described as dynamical states, often perturbed by (Gaussian) noise, and the density equation takes the form of a continuity, or Fokker-Planck equation, for the corresponding law.

In the present paper, we consider a system of this kind involving fractional diffusions coming from non-Gaussian individual noises of Poissonian/Levy type,

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{L}u(x)+H(x,Du)+b(x)\!\cdot\!Du=F(x,m(t))&&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.69046pt]
&\partial_{t}m-\mathcal{L}^{*}m(x)-div\big(m\big(b(x)+D_{p}H(x,Du)\big)\big)=0&&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.69046pt]
&m(0)=m_{0},\qquad u(T)=u_{T},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
d\geq 1
and the diffusion operator
\mathcal{L}
is the infinitesimal generator of a pure jump Lévy process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a nonlocal operator defined on smooth functions

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\varphi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\varphi(x+z)-\varphi(x)-D\varphi(x)\!\cdot\!z\mathds{1}_{|z|\leq 1}\right\}\nu(dz),

where
\nu
is a nonnegative Radon measure satisfying the Lévy condition
\int(1\wedge|z|^{2})\nu(dz)<\infty
. A prototypical example is the fractional Laplacian
\mathcal{L}=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}
for
\sigma\in(0,2)
, the infinitesimal generator of the
\alpha

\alpha=\sigma
and
\nu(dz)=|z|^{-d-\sigma}\,dz
. This type of nonlocal diffusions
has many applications in science and economics
[
66
,
57
,
31
,
63
,
65
]
and has generated a lot of activity in different areas of mathematics like nonlocal PDEs
[
12
,
9
,
11
]

[

,

,
10
,
64

[

[

We will here restrict our analysis to the diffusive or sub-critical case where
\sigma>1

The vector field

in (

is assumed to be a confining Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) drift term,
whose role is to center the mass of the system, in order to give compactness in the long time. The functions

,

, and

are related to the underlying control problem, induced by the dynamics and by the costs
of the agents’ optimization problem. The typical interpretation of (

theory is the following. A generic agent in a (large) population is represented through a dynamical state
X_{\tau}
given by a controlled Lévy process, and faces the standard finite horizon stochastic control problem
[
59
,
43
]
,

(3)

\begin{split}&\begin{cases}dX_{\tau}=\alpha_{\tau}\,d\tau+b(X_{\tau})d\tau+dP_{\tau}&\\
X_{t}=x&\end{cases}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\rightsquigarrow\quad u(t,x)=\inf_{\alpha_{\tau}\in\mathcal{A}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{{\small\int_{t}^{T}[L(X_{\tau},\alpha_{\tau})+F(X_{\tau},m_{\tau})]+u_{T}(X_{T})}\right\},\end{split}

where

is a pure jump Levy process with infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}
given by (
2
)

[
1

,
\mathcal{A}
is the set of admissible
A

\{m_{\tau}\}

The coupling function

By dynamic programming, the value function

In this work, our focus is on the long-term and ergodic behavior of the system, namely on the analysis of (
1
) as
T\to+\infty
. In a regime of stability, it is expected that the solutions of the MFG problem in a long horizon
(0,T)
should spend most of the intermediate time in equilibrium, say close to a stationary ergodic solution. This kind of behavior is known under the name of
turnpike property
, in the terminology coined by the economist P.A. Samuelson for optimal control problems
[
33
]
. In the context of MFGs, this property has been investigated in a series of papers; in particular, under the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition (
F(x,m)
is monotone in
m
), the turnpike property was proved to hold for finite state space
[
36
]
and for periodic solutions of second order problems
[
15
,
16
]
, with corresponding quantitative estimates. The exponential rate of turnpike was explained in terms of nondegenerate linearization
[
60
]
and the ergodic behavior of the master equation
[
17
]
. Outside the case of the Lasry-Lions condition, very little is known, but the exponential turnpike was still proved to hold under some smallness condition on the coupling terms, using either the individual noise of the agents
[
30
]
or some favorable condition of the optimal control problem behind
[
21
]
. Even if some weak form of ergodic behavior and average convergence has been proved for deterministic MFG systems
[
2
,
13
,
14
,
56
]
, very few quantitative results are known outside the case of second order problems. However, in the recent preprint
[
29
]
, new turnpike results have been obtained for both diffusive and deterministic MFG systems, replacing the classical Lasry-Lions assumption with displacement monotonicity conditions.

The goal of this article is to provide an exponential turnpike estimate for the solution
(u,m)
of (
1
), thus extending previous results that were so far limited to the case of second order problems and mostly restricted to the case of periodic solutions.
We will show, indeed, that for long time horizons the solution of (
1
) will stabilize (exponentially fast) around the solution of the following stationary ergodic MFG system

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\lambda-\mathcal{L}\bar{u}(x)+H(x,D\bar{u})+b(x)\!\cdot\!D\bar{u}=F(x,\bar{m})&&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.69046pt]
&-\mathcal{L}^{*}\bar{m}(x)-div\big(\bar{m}\big(b(x)+D_{p}H(x,D\bar{u})\big)\big)=0&&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.69046pt]
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{m}=1,\qquad\bar{u}(0)=0\,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is the so-called ergodic constant.
As usual this will take the form of a quantitative estimate stating that
(Du_{T},m_{T})
is approximately
(D\bar{u},\bar{m})
for any large intermediate period
(\delta T,(1-\delta)T)
. Namely,
for every

,

(5)

\|m(t)-\bar{m}\|_{TV_{k}}+[u(t)-\bar{u}]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}+\|Du(t)-D\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-k})}\leq M(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)})

where
\|\cdot\|_{TV_{k}}
is the total variation norm weighted by
|x|^{k}
(see (
9
)),
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-k})}
is the
L^{\infty}
norm weighted by
|x|^{-k}
, and
[\cdot]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}
the
oscillation seminorm weighted by
|x|^{-k}
(see (
7

We refer to the next section for the precise definitions and a proper statement.

Let us stress that the drift term
b
plays a key role here to guarantee some confining property of the underlying dynamics, which is needed to invoke some form of ergodicity. This condition is of course not required in the case of compact state space which
is the usual setting
in the literature, e.g. when studying periodic solutions.

In establishing (
5
), we will assume that the Hamiltonian
H(x,p)
is locally uniformly convex and globally Lipschitz continuous in
p
, which includes the case that the set of controls
A
in (
3
) is compact. The coupling cost
F(x,m)
will be assumed to be smooth and monotone on the set of probability measures, i.e. satisfying the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition. Those assumptions will be made precise in the next Section.
Even if a possible generalization of this setting could be considered (see also Remark
2.3
), along the lines of other papers mentioned above, we tried to keep here the assumptions on
H
and
F
to their simplest version, focusing on the main novelty which is non-Gaussian driving noise and corresponding PDEs with fractional diffusion operators. To our knowledge, this is the first exponential turnpike result for MFGs driven by
Levy processes with no Brownian component.

To prove estimate (
5
), we will need to show a number of auxiliary results both for the individual HJB and Fokker-Planck equations and for the full systems appearing in (
1
) and (
4
). In Section
3
we show
uniform in time global Lipschitz and local second-derivative bounds on the solution
u
of the HJB equation, bounds that will be transferred to the solution of the stationary ergodic HJB equation as well.
Such estimates are quite classical for nonlocal Hamilton-Jacobi equations (see e.g.
[
4
,
5
]
), although we will need to review and refine some of the arguments to take care of long time robustness of the bounds. Many of the technical tools needed here are confined to the Appendix. The analysis of the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation is given in Section
4
and mostly relies on results from
[
61
]
which guarantee the well-posedness of a suitable dual formulation.
A fixed point argument and the results of the previous sections are then used in Section
5
to show well-posedness for the two MFG systems (
1
) and (
4
).

While Section
3
and Section

4
contain some preliminary material needed for the well-posedness of the MFG systems, the main core of the paper is the proof of the estimate (
5
) contained in Section
6
. Here we need to carefully mix the exponential stabilization of the two single equations and the contractivity induced by the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition.
A nontrivial role is played by the choice of norms in which we can quantitatively measure the above properties.
To this extent, we rely on the recent approach introduced in
[
61
]
for the decay of solutions of nonlocal Fokker-Planck equations, quantified in weighted total variation norm, and obtained as dual to the (global in time) weighted oscillation estimates for HJB equations. However, in order to tackle the difficulty of the forward-backward coupling in the MFG system, we will need both to upgrade the oscillation bounds of
u
into truly gradient estimates and to combine some Duhamel’s type estimates for the nonhomogeneous equations (see Proposition
6.2
and Proposition
6.4
) with the usual time-contractivity of the system coming from the monotonicity of
m\mapsto F(x,m)
.

We comment here on possible generalizations that could be considered, but certainly at the expense of much more technicality, once the result and the strategy of proof of Theorem

Remark 6.3
.

We stress that the constant

of the growth condition of

) does not affect the estimates (

is only needed to justify the application of maximum principle in the class of given solutions.

Proof.

Let us show (
52

which has positive density and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle x\rangle^{k}d\nu<\infty
(e.g.
\nu
is a normalized Gaussian). For any bounded Borel set

we define

\xi:=\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\,\mathds{1}_{B}-\lambda\,\nu\,,\qquad\hbox{where $\lambda=\int_{B}\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\,dx$.}\,

We notice that
\xi\in L^{1}(\langle x\rangle^{k})
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi\,dx=0
(because

has unit mass) and we estimate

(55)

\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\langle x\rangle^{k})}\leq|B|+\lambda\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle x\rangle^{k}d\nu\leq(1+C)|B|

for some
C
independent of

. Then we solve the Fokker-Planck equation

(56)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mu-\mathcal{L}^{*}\mu-{\rm div}({\mathcal{B}}(t,x)\mu)=0&\text{ in }(t,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\mu(t,x)=\xi(x),\qquad\,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

and we deduce, by duality,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t)\,\xi\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v_{T}\,d\mu(T)+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,d\mu(\tau)d\tau\,.

Since
\mu
has zero average, this implies, for any constant

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t)\,\xi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v_{T}+c_{1})\,d\mu(T)+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f+c_{2})\,d\mu(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{T}+c_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-k})}\|\mu(T)\|_{TV_{k}}+\int_{t}^{T}\|f+c_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-k})}\|\mu(\tau)\|_{TV_{k}}d\tau

Taking infimum on
c_{1},c_{2}
, and using (
50
), we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t)\,\xi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq[v_{T}]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}\|\mu(T)\|_{L^{1}(\langle x\rangle^{k})}+\int_{t}^{T}[f(\tau)]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}\|\mu(\tau)\|_{TV_{k}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\left(K\,e^{-\omega(T-t)}[v_{T}]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}+K\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\omega(\tau-t)}[f(\tau)]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}d\tau\right)\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\langle x\rangle^{k})}

where, in the last step, we used (
49
) for problem (
56
). Replacing

in the argument above yields to

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t)\,\xi\,dx\right|\leq K\left(e^{-\omega(T-t)}[v_{T}]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\omega(\tau-t)}[f(\tau)]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}d\tau\right)\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\langle x\rangle^{k})}.

The last term is now estimated with (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t)\,\xi\,dx=\int_{B}(v(t)+c_{t})\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\,dx\qquad\hbox{for $c_{t}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t)d\nu$.}

Hence we deduce

\left|\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}(v(t)+c_{t})\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\,dx\right|\leq(1+C)K\left(e^{-\omega(T-t)}[v_{T}]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\omega(\tau-t)}[f(\tau)]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}d\tau\right)\,.

Since this holds for all Borel sets

\|v(t)+c_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-k})}\leq(1+C)K\left(e^{-\omega(T-t)}[v_{T}]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\omega(\tau-t)}[f(\tau)]_{\langle x^{k}\rangle}d\tau\right)\,.

On account of (

In order to get (

Appendix A
Hölder and Lipschitz bounds for fractional Hamilton-Jacobi equations

In this Appendix we prove different kind of oscillations estimates, in the form of Hölder and Lipschitz bounds, for solutions of Hamilton-Jacobi equations. In particular, we prove the Lipschitz bound stated in Proposition
3.3
.
For this kind of estimates, which are global in time, a key role is played by the existence of Lyapunov functions. This means, specifically, that the function
\langle x\rangle^{k}
(and eventually some truncation if needed) are supersolutions for large
|x|
.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
(B1):
,
(

ν

):
, and let
\phi(x)=\langle x\rangle^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\in(0,\sigma)
. For any
L_{0}>0
, there exists
K,\omega_{0}>0
,
only depending on
\alpha,\beta,|b(0)|,\gamma,L_{0},\nu,d
, such that

(83)

\mathcal{L}_{L_{0}}\phi(x):=-\mathcal{L}\phi(x)+b(x)\!\cdot\!D\phi(x)-L_{0}|D\phi(x)|\geq\omega_{0}\,\phi(x)-K\qquad\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

Proof.

We recall that
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
. Hence, by definition of
\phi
, we get that

D\phi(x)=\gamma\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1}\frac{x}{\langle x\rangle}

and

D^{2}\phi(x)=\gamma\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-2}(I+(\gamma-2)\frac{x}{\langle x\rangle}\otimes\frac{x}{\langle x\rangle})
. Thus by
(B1):
,

\displaystyle b(x)\!\cdot\!D\phi(x)=(b(x)-b(0))\!\cdot\!\frac{x}{\langle x\rangle}\gamma\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1}+b(0)\!\cdot\!\frac{x}{\langle x\rangle}\gamma\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{\langle x\rangle}-(\beta+|b(0)|)\right),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\phi(x)=\mathcal{L}_{1}(x,\phi,D\phi(x))+\mathcal{L}^{1}(x,\phi,D\phi(x))

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{2}\phi\|_{\infty}\int_{|z|\leq 1}z^{2}\nu(dz)+\int_{|z|\geq 1}|\phi(x+z)-\phi(x)|\nu(dz)

We have that
\|D^{2}\phi\|_{\infty}\leq C
, so that
\mathcal{L}_{1}(x,\phi,D\phi(x))\leq C
for some constant
C>0
. As for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, we use that

\displaystyle|\phi(x+z)-\phi(x)|\leq|z|\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}|D\phi(\tau z+x)|\leq\gamma\,|z|\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}\langle x+\tau z\rangle^{\gamma-1}.

If
\gamma\leq 1
,
\langle x+\tau z\rangle^{\gamma-1}\leq 1
. If
\gamma>1
, we estimate
\langle x+\tau z\rangle^{\gamma-1}\leq c_{\gamma}(\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1}+\langle z\rangle^{\gamma-1})

for some constant
c_{\gamma}>1
, possibly depending on
\gamma
, and for any
\tau\in(0,1)
. Hence we get

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\geq 1}|\phi(x+z)-\phi(x)|\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1}\int_{|z|\geq 1}|z|\nu(dz)+c_{\gamma}\int_{|z|\geq 1}\langle z\rangle^{\gamma}\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1})

for some constant
C>0
, where we used assumption
(

ν

):
and that
\sigma>\max(1,\gamma)
.

Going back to
\mathcal{L}_{L_{0}}
, we then find, for a possibly different
C
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{L_{0}}[\phi](x)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}\phi(x)+b(x)\cdot D\phi(x)-L_{0}|D\phi(x)|

\displaystyle\geq-C(1+\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1})+\gamma\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{\alpha|x|^{2}}{\langle x\rangle}-(\beta+|b(0)|+L_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\langle x\rangle^{\gamma-1}\big(\alpha|x|-(\beta+|b(0)|+L_{0}+C)\big)-C.

We conclude that (
83
) holds for
\omega_{0}<\alpha\,\gamma
and some
K>0
.
∎

The next tool is a crucial lemma which exploits the local diffusivity of the operator in doubling variables arguments. In the case of nonlocal operators such as
(

[

. Here we use an improved version, which is needed for long time estimates; this is a slight extension of

[

. We recall the notation (

.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be defined by (
2
), where
\nu
satisfies

(84)

\exists\lambda>0\,:\,\quad\frac{d\nu}{dz}\geq\lambda\,\frac{1}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}\qquad\forall z\in B_{1}\,.

Suppose that

is a maximum point of the function

({\tilde{x}},{\tilde{y}})\mapsto u(\tilde{x})-u(\tilde{y})-\zeta(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\,,\quad\zeta:=[\varphi(\tilde{x})+\varphi(\tilde{y})]\psi(|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|)+F(\tilde{x})+F(\tilde{y})

where
\varphi
and
F
are

functions,
\varphi>0
, and

is a positive, increasing and concave function which is

in a neighborhood of

Then, for every

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(x,u(x),D_{x}\zeta(x,y))-\mathcal{L}(y,u(y),-D_{y}\zeta(x,y))\leq\\

&\quad+\lambda\,C_{d,\sigma}|x-y|^{2-\sigma}\,\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)\left(\sup_{\xi\in x+B_{\delta}}|D\varphi(\xi)|+\sup_{\xi\in y+B_{\delta}}|D\varphi(\xi)|\right)\\
&\quad+4\lambda\left(\inf_{\xi\in x+B_{\delta}}\varphi(\xi)+\inf_{\xi\in y+B_{\delta}}\varphi(\xi)\right)\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B_{\delta}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{s,z})|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds\,\end{split}

where

,

Remark A.3
.

Despite the generality of the statement, we mostly use it for the case of constant
\varphi(x)

Proof.

Taking

\begin{split}&[u(x+z)-u(x)]-[u(y+z)-u(y)]\\

Thanks to assumption (

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{u(x+z)-u(x)-(D_{x}\zeta\!\cdot\!z){1}_{|z|\leq 1}\}\tilde{\nu}(dz)\\

Since

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{u(x+z)-u(x)-D_{x}\zeta\!\cdot\!z\right\}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}-\int_{B_{\delta}}\{u(y+z)-u(y)+D_{y}\zeta\!\cdot\!z\}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}\\

\begin{split}&[u(x+z)-u(x)]-[u(y+Az)-u(y)]\\

Using (
92

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{u(x+z)-u(x)-D_{x}\zeta\!\cdot\!z\right\}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}-\int_{B_{\delta}}\{u(y+z)-u(y)+D_{y}\zeta\!\cdot\!z\}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\psi(|x-y|)\left\{\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{\varphi(x+z)-\varphi(x)-D\varphi(x)\!\cdot\!z\right\}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\left.+\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{\varphi(y+Az)-\varphi(y)-D\varphi(y)\!\cdot\!Az\right\}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}\right\}

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{F(x+z)-F(x)-DF(x)\!\cdot\!z\right\}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}+\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{F(y+Az)-F(y)-DF(y)\!\cdot\!Az\right\}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{\delta}}\,[\varphi(x+z)-\varphi(x)+\varphi(y+Az)-\varphi(y)]\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!(z-Az)\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{\psi(|x-y+z-Az|)-\psi(|x-y|)-\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!(z-Az)\right\}{\mathcal{R}}_{\varphi}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}\,.

{\mathcal{R}}_{\varphi}:=[\varphi(x+z)+\varphi(y+Az)]

We multiply the above inequality by

and
\tilde{\nu}=\mu{1}_{B_{\delta}}

Recalling (

\begin{split}&\qquad\mathcal{L}(x,u(x),D_{x}\zeta(x,y))-\mathcal{L}(y,u(y),-D_{y}\zeta(x,y))\leq\\
&\quad\qquad\quad\leq\psi(|x-y|)\left\{\mathcal{L}\varphi(x)+\mathcal{L}\varphi(y)\right\}+\mathcal{L}F(x)+\mathcal{L}F(y)\\
&+\int_{B_{\delta}}\,[\varphi(x+z)-\varphi(x)+\varphi(y+Az)-\varphi(y)]\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!(z-Az)\frac{\lambda\,dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}\\
&+\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{\psi(|x-y+z-Az|)-\psi(|x-y|)-\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!(z-Az)\right\}{\mathcal{R}}_{\varphi}\frac{\lambda\,dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}\,.\end{split}

Now we estimate last two terms. To this goal we notice that the precise choice of

implies

, we have

.
Hence we estimate, by Taylor’s expansion,

\displaystyle\psi(|x-y+z-Az|)-\psi(|x-y|)-\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!(z-Az)

\displaystyle=\psi(|x-y|+2(\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z))-\psi(|x-y|)-\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)2(\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z)

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(|x-y|+2s(\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z))|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}\right)ds

Notice that this latter term is negative since
\psi
is concave. Using that

we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}}\left\{\psi(|x-y+z-Az|)-\psi(|x-y|)-\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!(z-Az)\right\}{\mathcal{R}}_{\varphi}\frac{\lambda\,dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\inf_{\xi\in x+B_{\delta}}\varphi(\xi)+\inf_{\xi\in y+B_{\delta}}\varphi(\xi)\right)\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B_{\delta}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(|x-y|+2s(\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z))\frac{|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}4\lambda}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}dzds\,.

Similarly we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}}\,[\varphi(x+z)-\varphi(x)+\varphi(y+Az)-\varphi(y)]\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!(z-Az)\frac{\lambda\,dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(\sup_{\xi\in x+B_{\delta}}|D\varphi(\xi)|+\sup_{\xi\in y+B_{\delta}}|D\varphi(\xi)|\right)\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)2\lambda\int_{B_{\delta}}|z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\lambda\,C_{\sigma,d}\left(\sup_{\xi\in x+B_{\delta}}|D\varphi(\xi)|+\sup_{\xi\in y+B_{\delta}}|D\varphi(\xi)|\right)\psi^{\prime}(|x-y|)|x-y|^{2-\sigma}\,.

We conclude therefore the estimate from (
93
), obtaining (
85
).
∎

Proposition A.4
.

Assume
(

ν

):
,
(B1):
,
(H1):
, and

Let

where

\theta,d,\sigma,\alpha,\beta,|b(0)|,C_{H}

Proof.

We proceed in two steps. First we prove Hölder estimates for

Let

We define

\displaystyle\Phi(t,x,y)=u(t,x)-u(t,y)-\psi(|x-y|)-\varepsilon(\phi(x)+\phi(y))-\frac{\varepsilon}{T-t},

where
\psi
is an increasing concave function, to be defined later.

Our aim is to show that
\Phi(t,x,y)\leq 0
for all

,

, and for every
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.

Step 1.
The case
\theta\in(0,1)
. Here we choose in (

)

\psi(r):=C_{1}(1-e^{-C_{2}r^{\theta}})+Lr^{\theta}

where

will be large constants to be fixed later, and

(95)

L\geq[u_{0}]_{\theta}\,.

Assume by contradiction that, for some
\varepsilon>0
,

\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t\in(0,T)}\Phi(t,x,y)>0
.
Since
u=o(\phi)
as
|x|\to\infty
,
the supremum of
\Phi
is achieved at some point
(t,x,y)
with

, and, since it is a positive maximum, it occurs with

and

, due to (
95

We denote

\displaystyle\zeta(x,y):=\psi(|x-y|)+\varepsilon(\phi(x)+\phi(y)),

and we denote hereafter
\widehat{x-y}=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}
,
r:=|x-y|
. Hence we have

(97)

\displaystyle D_{x}\zeta=\psi^{\prime}(r)\,\widehat{x-y}+\varepsilon D\phi(x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{y}\zeta=-\psi^{\prime}(r)\widehat{x-y}+\varepsilon D\phi(y).

Using the parabolic version of the Theorem of sums in viscosity solutions’ theory (see e.g.
[
32
, Thm 8.3]
,
[
46
, Theorem 2.2]
, and
[
6
, Corollary 2]
) yields

(98)

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{(T-t)^{2}}-\mathcal{L}(x,u(x),D_{x}\zeta)+\mathcal{L}(y,u(y),-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle+b(x)\!\cdot\!D_{x}\zeta-b(y)\!\cdot\!(-D_{y}\zeta)+H(x,D_{x}\zeta)-H(y,-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq f(t,x)-f(t,y).

We estimate the terms in (

Due to
(B1):
,
(H1):
and (

(99)

\displaystyle b(x)\!\cdot\!D_{x}\zeta-b(y)\!\cdot\!(-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle\geq\psi^{\prime}(r)(\alpha\,r-\beta)+\varepsilon(b(x)\!\cdot\!D\phi(x)+b(y)\!\cdot\!D\phi(y)),

\displaystyle H(x,D_{x}\zeta)-H(y,-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle\geq-C_{H}[2+2\psi^{\prime}(r)+\varepsilon(|D\phi(x)|+|D\phi(y)|)].

Inserting these estimates into (

(100)

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{(T-t)^{2}}+\psi^{\prime}(r)(\alpha\,r-(\beta+2C_{H}))-\mathcal{L}(x,u,D_{x}\zeta)+\mathcal{L}(y,u,-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2C_{H}+C_{f}+\varepsilon\Big(C_{H}(|D\phi(x)|+|D\phi(y)|)-b(x)\!\cdot\!D\phi(x)-b(y)\!\cdot\!D\phi(y)\Big)

where
C_{f}=\sup\limits_{t,x,y}[f(x)-f(y)]
.

Let us first consider the case that
r>\frac{2(\beta+2C_{H})}{\alpha}
, so that
\alpha\,r-(\beta+2C_{H})>\frac{\alpha}{2}\,r
.
Using Lemma
A.2
(with
\varphi=\frac{1}{2}
),
\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)<0
, and
\psi^{\prime}(r)\geq\theta Lr^{\theta-1}
, (

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{(T-t)^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\theta\,L\,r^{\theta}\leq 2C_{H}+C_{f}-\varepsilon(\mathcal{L}\phi(x)+b(x)\!\cdot\!D\phi(x)-C_{H}|D\phi(x)|)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\varepsilon(\mathcal{L}\phi(y)+b(y)\!\cdot\!D\phi(y)-C_{H}|D\phi(y)|))\,.

Assume that
r\geq r_{1}>\frac{2(\beta+2C_{H})}{\alpha}
; this implies
|x|\vee|y|\geq r_{1}/2
, so one between

and

sufficiently large so that

Then we deduce

\frac{\varepsilon}{(T-t)^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\theta\,L\,\left(\frac{4C_{H}}{\alpha}\right)^{\theta}\leq 2C_{H}+C_{f}\qquad\forall r\geq r_{1}

which cannot hold if
L
is sufficiently large, only depending on
C_{f},C_{H},\alpha,\theta
.

We are left with the case that

and we go back to (

\begin{split}&\frac{\varepsilon}{(T-t)^{2}}-4\lambda\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B_{\delta}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{s,z})|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds\\
&\quad\leq 2C_{H}+C_{f}+2\varepsilon K+(\beta+2C_{H})\,\psi^{\prime}(r)\;,\end{split}

where we have denoted

\begin{split}\psi^{\prime}(r)&\leq\frac{2}{c_{d,\sigma}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B_{\delta}^{-}}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\delta^{2-\sigma}}|\widehat{x-y}\cdot z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds\\

.
Here and after

\begin{split}&4\lambda\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B_{\delta}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{s,z})|\widehat{x-y}\cdot z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds+(\beta+2C_{H})\,\psi^{\prime}(r)\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B_{\delta}^{-}}\left\{4\lambda\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{s,z})+c\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\xi_{s,z})}{(\xi_{s,z}\wedge 1)^{2-\sigma}}\right\}|\widehat{x-y}\cdot z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds\end{split}

, and we compute for

\displaystyle 4\lambda\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+c\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\xi)}{(\xi\wedge 1)^{2-\sigma}}

\displaystyle=-C_{1}\,\theta\,C_{2}\,e^{-C_{2}\xi^{\theta}}\xi^{\theta-2}\left\{4\lambda(1-\theta)+4\lambda\theta C_{2}\xi^{\theta}-c(\xi^{\sigma-1}\vee\xi)\right\}

Step 2.

Hence, we only need to prove an estimate for small

.

To this purpose, we choose in (

)
\psi
as

\displaystyle\psi(r)=C_{1}(r-\rho r^{1+\theta})\,,\quad r\in[0,r_{0}],\,\,\hbox{with $r_{0}^{\theta}=\frac{1}{2\rho}$.}

for some

\sup_{0<r<r_{0}}\Phi(r)\leq 0\,.

We observe that

, and by choice of

, we have

Then we proceed as in Step 1; using the viscosity inequalities we obtain (

\begin{split}&\frac{\varepsilon}{(T-t)^{2}}\leq 2C_{H}+C_{f}+2\varepsilon K\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B_{\delta}^{-}}\left\{4\lambda\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{s,z})+c\,\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\xi_{s,z})}{(\xi_{s,z}\wedge 1)^{2-\sigma}}\right\}|\widehat{x-y}\cdot z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds\end{split}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{(T-t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{H}+C_{f}+2\varepsilon K-\varepsilon_{0}\,C_{1}\,\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B_{\delta}^{-}}\xi_{s,z}^{\theta-1}|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{0}

\displaystyle u(t,x)-u(t,y)\leq C_{1}\,|x-y|\qquad\forall x,y,t\,:|x-y|<r_{0}

Then there exist constants

, such that

|u(t,x)-u(t,y)|\leq C(1+\tilde{\omega}_{\delta,B}(u))|x-y|\qquad\hbox{for $x,y\in B$},

where

depends on
\frac{1}{t},d,\sigma
, and
\hat{C}

\tilde{\omega}_{\delta,B}(u):=\sup_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}s\in(\frac{t}{2},t),x,y\in B_{2},\\
|x-y|<\delta\end{array}}\!\!\!\!|u(s,x)-u(s,y)|\,,

where
B_{2}=\{x:\textup{dist}(x,B)\leq 2\}
and
\delta\leq 1
only depends on

.

In addition, if

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}|u(t,x)-u(t,y)|\leq C(1+\omega_{\delta,B,T}(u))|x-y|,\qquad\hbox{for $x,y\in B$},

where the constant

\omega_{\delta,B,T}(u):=\sup_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}s\in[0,T],x,y\in B_{2},\\
|x-y|<\delta\end{array}}\!\!\!\!|u(s,x)-u(s,y)|\,.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by
[
62
, Theorem 4.19]
, and many details are similar to Step 2 of Proposition
A.4
; however, in this proof we shall not exploit the confining effect of the drift, but only the local regularizing effect of the diffusion.

Step 1:
Let
\delta>0
,

x_{0}\in B
,
t_{0}\in(0,T)
, and define

\displaystyle\Delta=\big\{(t,x,y)\in(0,T)\times B(x_{0},\delta)\times B(x_{0},2\delta):|x-y|<\delta,\ \frac{t_{0}}{2}<t<t_{0}\wedge T\big\},

and the function

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon}(t,x,y)=

\displaystyle\ u(t,x)-u(t,y)-K\psi(|x-y|)-L\phi(x-x_{0})-C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{t_{0}-t},

where
K
,
L
, and
C_{0}
are constants to be determined, and

\phi(x)=\langle x\rangle^{\beta}-1
with
1<\beta<\sigma
. Note that
B(x_{0},2\delta)\subset B_{2}
and that
\phi\geq 0
is smooth and radially increasing with a unique strict minimum
\phi(0)=0
, so there is
c_{\phi}>0
such that
\phi(x)\geq c_{\phi}|x|^{2}
for
|x|<1
.
As before, we take
\theta\in(0,1)
sufficiently small and
\rho>0
sufficiently large (to be fixed below in dependence of
\theta
) and we consider

\displaystyle\psi(r)=r-\rho r^{1+\theta},

which is increasing and concave for
r\leq\big(\frac{1}{\rho(1+\theta)}\big)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}=:\delta_{0}

(<1)
. Assume
2\delta<\delta_{0}
.

We claim that
\Phi_{\epsilon}(t,x,y)\leq 0
for all
(t,x,y)\in\Delta
, and
arguing by contradiction we suppose that

\sup_{\Delta}\Phi_{\epsilon}(t,x,y)>0
. In this case, since
\Phi_{\epsilon}\to-\infty
uniformly as
t\to t_{0}^{-}
, we have that
\sup_{\Delta}\Phi_{\epsilon}
must be attained for some
t<t_{0}
.
Since

\displaystyle 0<\Phi_{\epsilon}(t,x,y)\leq\omega_{\delta,B,t_{0}}(u)-K\psi(x-y)-L\phi(x-x_{0})-C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{t_{0}-t},

by choosing

\displaystyle L=\frac{\omega_{\delta,B,t_{0}}(u)}{c_{\phi}\delta^{2}},\qquad C_{0}=\frac{4\omega_{\delta,B,t_{0}}(u)}{t_{0}^{2}},\qquad K\geq\frac{\omega_{\delta,B,t_{0}}(u)}{\psi(\delta)},

the maximum of
\Phi_{\epsilon}
in
\overline{\Delta}
cannot be attained
when
t=\frac{t_{0}}{2}
,
|x-y|=\delta
, or
|x-x_{0}|=\delta
since
\Phi_{\epsilon}\leq 0
in those cases. Moreover, if
|y-x_{0}|=2\delta
, then
|x-x_{0}|\geq|y-x_{0}|-|x-y|\geq\delta

and hence the maximum can not either be attained when
|y-x_{0}|=2\delta
.
Thus, the maximum is attained in the interior of
\Delta
. Furthermore, we can exclude that

x=y
, otherwise
\Phi_{\epsilon}\leq 0
.

Let
(t,x,y)
denotes such a maximum point of
\Phi_{\epsilon}
. Then
setting
\zeta(t,x,y)=K\psi(x-y)+L\phi(x-x_{0})+C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{t_{0}-t}
and
subtracting sub- and supersolution inequalities (cf.
[
46
, Theorem 2.2]
), we have

(114)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\epsilon}{(t_{0}-t)^{2}}+2C_{0}(t-t_{0})-\mathcal{L}(x,u,D_{x}\zeta)+\mathcal{L}(y,u,-D_{y}\zeta)&\\
+\hat{H}(x,D_{x}\zeta)-\hat{H}(y,-D_{y}\zeta)&\leq 0.\end{split}

Let us now estimate the different terms.
By (
111
), we have

\displaystyle\hat{H}(x,D_{x}\zeta)-\hat{H}(y,-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle\geq-\hat{C}\big(2+2K\psi^{\prime}+L|D\phi(x-x_{0})|\big)

\displaystyle\geq-\hat{C}\big(2+2K+Lc_{\beta}\delta\big),

where we used that
\psi^{\prime}\leq 1
and
|D\phi(x)|\leq\beta\langle x\rangle^{\beta-1}|x|
.

We estimate the nonlocal terms using as before Lemma
A.2
; in particular (as in Step 2 of Proposition
A.4
), in our function
\psi
we can choose any
\theta<\sigma-1
and
\rho
large enough such that we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,u,D_{x}\zeta)-\mathcal{L}(y,u,-D_{y}\zeta)\leq L\,\mathcal{L}\phi(x-x_{0})-K\Lambda\delta^{\theta+1-\sigma},

for some constant
\Lambda>0
.
Combining the above two estimates, we deduce from (
114
)

\displaystyle K(\Lambda\delta^{-\tilde{\theta}}-2\hat{C})\leq C_{0}t_{0}+L\,\mathcal{L}\phi(x-x_{0})+\hat{C}\big(2+Lc_{\beta}\delta\big).

where
\tilde{\theta}=\sigma-1-\theta>0
.
Now we choose first

(115)

\displaystyle\delta=\min\Big(\delta_{0}/2,(\frac{2\hat{C}+1}{\Lambda})^{-\frac{1}{\tilde{\theta}}}\Big),

so that
\Lambda\delta^{-\tilde{\theta}}-2\hat{C}\geq 1,
and we get

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}t_{0}+L\,\mathcal{L}\phi(x-x_{0})+\hat{C}\big(2+Lc_{\beta}\delta\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\omega_{\delta,B,t_{0}}(u)\left(\frac{1}{t_{0}}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\mathcal{L}\phi(x-x_{0})+c_{\beta}\delta)\right)+2\hat{C}\;,

where we used the initial choice of
C_{0}
and
L
.
Choosing
K
sufficiently large (depending on
\hat{C}
,
\omega_{\delta,B,t_{0}}(u)
,
\delta
and
\frac{1}{t_{0}}
), we get a contradiction.
Hence
\sup_{\Delta}\Phi_{\epsilon}\leq 0
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have proved that

\displaystyle u(t,x)-u(t,y)

\displaystyle\leq K\psi(|x-y|)+L\phi(x-x_{0})+C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K|x-y|+L\phi(x-x_{0})+C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}\;.

We now let
t\to t_{0}
, we take
x=x_{0}
, and we obtain

\displaystyle|u(t_{0},x_{0})-u(t_{0},y)|\leq K|y-x_{0}|\qquad\text{in}\qquad B(x_{0},2\delta).

Note that
\delta
is independent of
t_{0},x_{0},B,u
, and
K
is independent of
x_{0}\in B
. Moreover,
K\to\infty
as
t_{0}\to 0
and remains bounded as
t_{0}\to\infty
.

However, if
u_{0}
is Lipschitz, then one can take
C_{0}=0
in the above proof, and let
t
vary in
(0,T)
. The conclusion will hold up to
t=0
provided the constant

is larger than the Lipschitz constant of
u_{0}
.

Step 2:
Since
x_{0}
, in Step 1, was an arbitrary point in
B

112
) in a standard way by using the convexity of

.

Let
x,y\in B
and
t_{0}>0
.
Take
s_{0}=0<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{N}=1
, such that
for

(

is on the line through

) and
|x_{i}-x_{i+1}|\leq\delta/2
.
Then

for every

and

to conclude that

\displaystyle\quad|u(t_{0},x)-u(t_{0},y)|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N}|u(t_{0},x_{i})-u(t_{0},x_{i+1})|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N}K|x_{i}-x_{i+1}|=K|x-y|.\qquad\ \qed

We conclude this section
by showing that the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
13
) enjoys local-in-time (but global-in-space) regularizing effects.

Proposition A.7
.

Assume
(

ν

):
,
(H1):
and that
b\in C((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

(116)

\exists\,\,\beta>0\,:\,\,\,(b(t,x)-b(t,y))\cdot(x-y)\geq-\beta\,|x-y|\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|x-y|\leq 1\,,t>0\,.

Let

\gamma<\sigma

Given

and
\theta\in(0,1]
,

, there exists

(depending on

,

,

,

,
\sigma

|u(t_{0},x)-u(t_{0},y)|\leq K\,\frac{([u]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}+t_{0}(C_{H}+[f]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}))}{t_{0}^{\theta/\sigma}}\,[\langle x\rangle^{k}+\langle y\rangle^{k}]\,|x-y|^{\theta}\,,

where
[u]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}:=\sup_{\frac{t_{0}}{2}<t<t_{0}}\,\,[u(t)]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}

[f]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}:=\sup_{\frac{t_{0}}{2}<t<t_{0}}\,\,[f(t)]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}

Proof.

We follow the approach of
[

]
(where this estimate is proved for local operators) with the help of Lemma
A.2
to handle the nonlocal diffusion. We take
q\in(\gamma,\sigma)
so that

\varphi(t,x)=e^{\lambda t}\langle x\rangle^{k}
,
\Phi(t,x)=e^{\lambda t}\langle x\rangle^{q}
, and we consider

\displaystyle M_{0}:=\sup_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\Big\{u(t,x)-u(t,y)-(\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y))\left[K\psi(|x-y|)+C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\varepsilon(\Phi(t,x)+\Phi(t,y))-\frac{\varepsilon}{(t-t_{0})}\Big\}

where

\displaystyle\Delta=\{(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}:\,\,|x-y|\leq\delta\,\,,t\in[\frac{t_{0}}{2},t_{0})\},

with
\delta\leq 1
, and we claim that
M_{0}\leq 0
(for suitable choices of
K,C_{0},\delta
and a concave increasing function
\psi
).

As usual we reason by contradiction, assuming
M_{0}>0
. In this case the
\sup
is attained at a point
(t,x,y)
such that
t<t_{0}
.
Moreover,
t>\frac{t_{0}}{2}
and
|x-y|\neq\delta
, provided

(118)

C_{0}\,t_{0}^{2}e^{\lambda t_{0}/2}>4\,[u]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad K\psi(\delta)e^{\lambda t_{0}/2}>[u]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\,.

This choice implies that
(t,x,y)
is a local maximum point where we can use the viscosity formulations.
In a similar way as in the proof of Proposition
A.4
, then we obtain

(119)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta-\mathcal{L}(x,u(x),D_{x}\zeta)+\mathcal{L}(y,u(y),-D_{y}\zeta)+b(t,x)\!\cdot\!D_{x}\zeta-b(t,y)\!\cdot\!(-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle+H(x,D_{x}\zeta)-H(y,-D_{y}\zeta)\leq f(t,x)-f(t,y)

where

\zeta=(\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y))\left[K\psi(|x-y|)+C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}\right]+\varepsilon(\Phi(t,x)+\Phi(t,y))\,.

We make use of Lemma
A.2
, observing that, for
\delta<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\sup_{\xi\in x+B_{\delta}}|D\varphi(\xi)|\leq c_{k}e^{\lambda t}\langle x\rangle^{k-1}\leq c_{k}\varphi(t,x)\,,\quad\inf_{\xi\in x+B_{\delta}}\varphi(\xi)\geq c_{k}^{-1}e^{\lambda t}\langle x\rangle^{k}=c_{k}^{-1}\varphi(t,x),

for some constant
c_{k}
only depending on
k
. Hence, from Lemma
A.2
we get, for some constants

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,u(x),D_{x}\zeta)-\mathcal{L}(y,u(y),-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\left[K\psi(r)+C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}\right]\left(\mathcal{L}\varphi(t,x)+\mathcal{L}\varphi(t,y)\right)+\varepsilon\left(\mathcal{L}\Phi(t,x)+\mathcal{L}\Phi(t,y)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+c_{0}\,K\left[\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y)\right]\,r^{2-\sigma}\,\psi^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\quad+c_{1}\,K\left[\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y)\right]\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{r,\sigma}))|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds

where
r=|x-y|
,
B=\{z\,:\,|z|<\frac{|x-y|}{4}\}
and
\xi_{r,\sigma}=r+2s(\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z)
.

As for the first order terms, using condition (
116
) and
(H1):
we have

\displaystyle b(t,x)\!\cdot\!D_{x}\zeta-b(t,y)\!\cdot\!(-D_{y}\zeta)+H(x,D_{x}\zeta)-H(y,-D_{y}\zeta)

\displaystyle\geq-K\,(\beta+C_{H})\psi^{\prime}(r)(\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y))-2C_{H}

\displaystyle\quad+\left[K\psi(r)+C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\Big(b(t,x)\!\cdot\!D\varphi(t,x)-C_{H}|D\varphi(t,x)|+b(t,y)\!\cdot\!D\varphi(t,y)-C_{H}|D\varphi(t,y)|\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\Big(b(t,x)\!\cdot\!D\Phi(t,x)-C_{H}|D\Phi(t,x)|+b(t,y)\!\cdot\!D\Phi(t,y)-C_{H}|D\Phi(t,y)|\Big).

Putting together all the above estimates, and computing
\partial_{t}\zeta
, we deduce from (

)

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{(t_{0}-t)^{2}}+2C_{0}(t-t_{0})(\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y))

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\Big((\partial_{t}\Phi+\mathcal{L}_{C_{H}}\Phi)(t,x)+(\partial_{t}\Phi+\mathcal{L}_{C_{H}}\Phi)(t,y)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\left[K\psi(r)+C_{0}(t-t_{0})^{2}\right]\Big((\partial_{t}\varphi+\mathcal{L}_{C_{H}}\varphi)(t,x)+(\partial_{t}\varphi+\mathcal{L}_{C_{H}}\varphi)(t,y)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{H}+K\,\left[\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y)\right]\left\{(\beta+C_{H})+c_{0}\,r^{2-\sigma}\right\}\psi^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\quad+c_{1}\,K\left[\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y)\right]\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{r,\sigma})|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle\quad+\left(f(t,x)-f(t,y)\right),

where we recall the notation
\mathcal{L}_{C_{H}}
from (
83
) (now with
b=b(t,x)
). Since assumption (

) implies

, we estimate, as in the computations of

, that
\mathcal{L}_{C_{H}}\langle x\rangle^{k}\geq-c\langle x\rangle^{k}
for some

depending on
\beta,|b(0)|,c_{H},c_{\sigma,d},d,\sigma,k
. Hence we can choose
\lambda
so that
\partial_{t}\varphi+\mathcal{L}_{C_{H}}\varphi\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all

. Similarly, we can assume that, for the same
\lambda

\displaystyle\quad 2C_{0}(t-t_{0})(\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y))\leq 2C_{H}+K\,\left[\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y)\right]\left\{(\beta+C_{H})+c_{0}\,r^{2-\sigma}\right\}\psi^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{1}\,K\left[\varphi(t,x)+\varphi(t,y)\right]\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{r,\sigma})|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{1}\,K\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\int_{B}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{r,\sigma})|\widehat{x-y}\!\cdot\!z|^{2}\frac{dz}{|z|^{d+\sigma}}ds\,.

\displaystyle\begin{split}2C_{0}(t-t_{0})&\leq 2C_{H}+[f]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\\

2C_{0}(t-t_{0})\leq 2C_{H}+[f]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}-\tilde{c}_{3}\,K\,r^{2-\sigma}\,.

where we used (

Remark A.8
.

When
\theta=1
, (
117
) provides a gradient estimate. E.g. if
C_{H}=0
,

\|Du(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-k})}\leq\frac{K}{t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}}\,\left(\sup_{s\in[\frac{t}{2},t]}[u(s)]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\right)+K\,t^{1-\frac{1}{\sigma}}\left(\sup_{s\in[\frac{t}{2},t]}[f(s)]_{\langle x\rangle^{k}}\right)

for

-diffusive operators.

Of course, a similar proof as above can work up to
t=0

Reliable Chemical Synthesis of UDP-3-O-[(R)-3-Hydroxydecanoyl]-GlcNAc: The Native Substrate of Pseudomonas aeruginosa LpxC
Authors: Jingyang Wei, Yuanpei Fan, Banhao Kuang, Xinhao Zhu, Xiaojuan Zhang, Yugen Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHerein, we describe a reliable and efficient approach for the first chemical synthesis of biologically significant and complex 3-O-(R-3-hydroxydecanoyl) modified uridine diphosphate N-acetylglucosamine that is the native substrate of LpxC involved in the biosynthesis of the cell wall of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The synthetic protocol provides a successful example for the reliable preparation of modified nucleoside diphosphate sugar, which features judiciously selected protecting groups, the formation of pyrophosphate linkage with 5′-phosphate nucleoside as nucleophile, and the straightforward purification process.

Nucleoside diphosphate sugars (NDP sugars) play an important role in carbohydrate biosynthesis and, normally, serve as versatile glycosyl donors in the enzymatic construction of complex polysaccharides, glycoproteins, glycolipids, and other cellular metabolites.1 Modifications on these biomolecules with carbohydrate driven by the glycosyltransferases are directly associated with important cellular processes such as cell signaling, cellular adhesion, carcinogenesis, and cell wall biosynthesis in human pathogens.2 The glycosyltransferases involved in these cellular processes have been identified as promising targets for drug discovery.3 Consequently, structural analogues of endogenous sugar-nucleotides are exploited as enzyme inhibitors, mechanistic probes, and chemical tools.4

Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a Gram-negative opportunistic pathogen that causes severe infections such as cystic fibrosis, severe burns, and cancer.5 Because of the serious resistance of P. aeruginosa to most antibiotics, the infections pose a huge threat to human health.6 The World Health Organization has classified P. aeruginosa as the top priority pathogen7 and urgently calls for a new generation of antimicrobial agents or novel therapies.8 The enzyme LpxC is discovered in more than 40 Gram-negative species and catalyzes the key step in the lipid A biosynthetic pathway. In the case of P. aeruginosa, the native 3-O-(R-3-hydroxydecanoyl) modified uridine diphosphate (UDP) GlcNAc (1) is deacylated by LpxC (Figure 1).9 Thus, inhibition of LpxC to block the biosynthesis of bacterial cell wall is expected to inhibit the growth of P. aeruginosa.10 The native substrate UDP-GlcNAc (1) of LpxC is a classic chemical tool which is normally utilized in the enzyme inhibition assay, and the analogues might serve as effective inhibitors.9b,11 Therefore, the UDP-sugar (1) and analogues are of high value, with a price of around $4000/mg (catalog number MU75071) from BIOSYNTH. Moreover, the chemical structure of NDP-sugars is of high complexity and vulnerability. Although the biosynthetic approach employing specific enzyme shows success in the preparation of NDP-sugars, this method is highly dependent on the availability of the particular enzyme, and the structural diversity of NDP-sugars is still limited.12 Thus, the reliable chemical approach to access sufficient and pure NDP-sugars remains in great demand.13 Consistent with our continuous efforts to synthesize complex molecules by exploring mild reactions,14 we herein report a reliable approach for the first synthesis of the 3-O-(R-3-hydroxydecanoyl) modified UDP-sugar (1) of P. aeruginosa and analogue 2 equipped with the 3-O-(R-3-hydroxyheptanoyl) group (Scheme 1).15

Retrosynthetic Analysis of Targets 1 and 2

The structure of UDP-sugar (1) consists of a 5′-diphosphate uridine which is connected through its terminal phosphate to the anomeric position of 3-O-(R-3-hydroxydecanoyl) modified N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc). The motif of anomeric pyrophosphate renders the synthesis challenging:13 (1) construction of the pyrophosphate linkage may encounter problems, such as low yield, long reaction time, poor reproducibility, and tedious purification. (2) The chemical manipulation would be restricted due to the high vulnerability of pyrophosphate toward acidic or basic conditions. Moreover, the 3-O-(R-3-hydroxydecanoyl) group may undergo an undesired migration reaction which would further limit the choice of chemical manipulation conditions. To improve the reaction efficiency and facilitate the purification when making the pyrophosphate linkage, proper protecting groups would be judiciously selected.

To assemble targets 1 and 2, the coupling reaction between glycosyl 1-phosphate 4 and 5′-phosphate nucleoside 6 was envisaged (Scheme 1). Unlike the coupling method reported in the previous literature wherein sugar-1-phosphate (4) was used as nucleophile and 5′-phosphate nucleoside (6) as electrophile,16 we would reverse the coupling partners.17 The 5′-phosphate nucleoside (6) would be considered more nucleophilic due to the weaker electron-withdrawing influence of the sugar ring oxygen on the phosphate group. Thus, the 5′-phosphate nucleoside (6) would be utilized as nucleophile, while the glycosyl 1-phosphate (4) would serve as electrophile that would be activated by carbonyl diimidazole (CDI). The orthogonal para-methoxybenzylidene group would be utilized to lock the C4-OH and C6-OH, of which the torsional disarming effect would improve the stability of the anomeric phosphoester (4) and the activated glycosyl phosphorimidazolide (3).18 Additionally, the tert-butyldimethylsilyl (TBS) and levulinoyl (Lev) would be selected to protect the side chain of 4 and the two OHs of 7, respectively, both of which would be removed under mild basic reaction conditions without deteriorating the pyrophosphate or inducing the undesired migration of the acyl group. The C2 azido group would be used to mask the NHAc, which would provide the possibility to make other derivatives such as radioactive tritiated UDP-GlcNAc.11a

The synthesis commenced with the preparation of modified glycosyl 1-phosphate 4 (Scheme 2). Starting from the readily available 1,3,4,6-tetra-O-acetyl-2-azido-2-deoxy-d-glucopyranose 8, the C1-OH was selectively removed by using hydrazine acetate following by installation of TBS to produce 9 in 90% yield over two steps. Complete deprotection of all three acetyl groups using sodium methoxide in methanol and subsequent introduction of p-methoxy benzylidene afforded compound 10 in 91% yield. Lev was employed to temporarily protect residual C3-OH to deliver 11 in 96% yield. To ensure the α stereoselectivity during the introduction of anomeric phosphate, the C2-azido group was transformed into C2-NHAc to generate 12 in 96% yield via two reaction steps, including hydrogenolysis and acetylation. Removal of TBS at C1-OH using hydrogen fluoride in pyridine afforded compound 13 in 81% yield. Then, phosphoramidite chemistry was applied to install the anomeric α phosphoester.19 Subjection of compound 13 to iPr2NP(OBn)2 and the activator of tetrazole generated an intermediate compound of phosphite, which was next oxidized by H2O2 to produce 14 as a single α anomer in 93% yield. Then, deprotecting the Lev group under the mild condition of hydrazine acetate in dichloromethane delivered 15 in 99% yield. The resulting C3-OH was then acylated with the (R)-3-(tert-butyldimethylsilyloxy) decanoic acid (16a) or (R)-3-(tert-butyldimethylsilyloxy) heptanoic acid (16b) to provide 17a and 17b in excellent yields.20 The two Bn groups on the phosphotriester group were deprotected quantitatively under basic conditions to afford 4a and 4b.

Synthesis of 3-O-(R-3-Hydroxydecanoyl) Modified Glycosyl 1-Phosphate 4

Next, we moved on to the synthesis of the other coupling partner of 5′-phosphate nucleoside 6 (Scheme 3). First, commercially available nucleoside 18 was selectively protected with TBDPS at C6-OH to afford 19 in 97% yield. The remaining two OHs at C2 and C3 were then blocked with Lev to give compound 20 in 99% yield. Removal of the TBDPS group using hydrogen fluoride in pyridine unmasked the C6-OH to furnish 21 in 96% yield. The resulting C6-OH was then installed with the phosphoester functionality in 93% yield by using the phosphoramidite chemistry via a two-step process of phosphite formation and oxidization.19 Hydrogenolysis to remove the Bn groups under basic conditions of Et3N generated the nucleophile 6 in 91% yield, without deteriorating the double bond of the uracil.

Synthesis of 5′-Phosphate Nucleoside 6

With the two key coupling partners in hand, we explored the condensation reaction between 4 and 6 to construct the pyrophosphate linkage (Scheme 4). Unlike the conventional method in which the nucleotide (6) was activated to be an electrophile, we herein activated the glycosyl 1-phosphate (4) by using CDI to form the intermediate phosphorimidazolide that was used in the next step without purification. Next, the key step of the condensation reaction was performed. Mixing the nucleophile (6) and electrophile (3) under the promotion of tetrazole for 4 days furnished the desired coupling product 23 in excellent yields after straightforward purification via size-exclusion chromatography on a Sephadex LH-20 column and flash silica gel chromatography.21 Global deprotection initially met with failure due to the vulnerability of the targets 1 and 2. After attempts, the mild deprotection condition and sequence were identified. The Lev groups were first deprotected by hydrazine acetate, and then, the TBS was removed by hydrogen fluoride. Both reactions were performed in the weakly basic solvent of pyridine. When we removed the last protecting group of benzylidene, the acidic deprotection condition destroyed the pyrophosphate linkage. Luckily, oxidation mediated deprotection by using 2,3-dichloro-5,6-dicyano-1,4-benzoquinone (DDQ) afforded the desired product cleanly. After straightforward purification via ion exchange chromatography on a diethylaminoethanol (DEAE) linked Sepharose column, the target compounds 1 and 2 were obtained in good yields and in high purity, which were verified by the 1H NMR spectra (Figure 2).

In summary, a reliable and efficient chemical approach was developed for the first synthesis of biologically significant and complex 3-O-(R-3-hydroxydecanoyl) modified UDP-GlcNAc 1 and its analogue 2 equipped with a 3-O-(R-3-hydroxyheptanoyl) group. The synthetic protocol features judiciously selected protecting groups which were compatible with the susceptive pyro phosphate linkage, the CDI-mediated condensation with the 5′-phosphate nucleoside as nucleophile, and the straightforward purification process. The synthetic protocol will provide a sufficient amount of 1 that can serve as a chemical tool for use in the enzyme LpxC inhibition assay and represent a successful example for the synthesis of modified NDP-sugars.

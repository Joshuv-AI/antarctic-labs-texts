Modularity of moments of reciprocal sums for partitions into distinct parts
Kathrin Bringmann, Byungchan Kim, Eunmi Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we determine modularity properties of the generating function of s k ​ ( n ) s_{k}(n) which sums k k -th power of reciprocals of parts throughout all of the partitions of n n into distinct parts. In particular, we show that the generating function for s k ​ ( n ) s_{k}(n) is related to Maass Eisenstein series and sesquiharmonic Maass forms.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Weak Maass forms

Definition 2.1
.

A real-analytic function
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
weak Maass form of weight
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and eigenvalue
\lambda
on

\Gamma_{0}(N)
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

We have, for
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
,

f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{k}f(\tau).

(2)

We have

\Delta_{k}(f)=\lambda f,

where the
weight
k
Laplace operator
is defined as

\Delta_{k}:=-v^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}\right)+ikv\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\right).

Note that

\Delta_{k}=-\xi_{2-k}\circ\xi_{k}.

(3)

The function
f
has at most linear exponential growth at the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

If
\lambda=0
, then
f
is called a
harmonic Maass form
.

Harmonic Maass forms naturally split into a holomorphic and a non-holomorphic part. To be more precise, if
f
is a harmonic Maass form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, then
f
can be written as

f(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{f}^{+}(n)q^{n}+c_{f}^{-}(0)v^{1-k}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\ll\infty\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{f}^{-}(n)\Gamma(1-k,-4\pi nv)q^{n},

where
\Gamma(s,y):=\int_{y}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-t}dt
is the
incomplete gamma function
. The first sum is the
holomorphic part
of
f
and the remaining parts are its
non-holomorphic part
.

According to

\Gamma^{*}(2k-1,4\pi nv)q^{-n}=\frac{\overline{q}^{n}}{\Gamma(2k-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(t+4\pi nv)^{2k-2}e^{-t}dt,

we have

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\!\left(\Gamma^{*}(2k-1,4\pi nv)q^{-n}\right)=\frac{\overline{q}^{n}}{\Gamma(2k-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\!\left((t+4\pi nv)^{2k-2}\right)e^{-t}dt.

Now, using (
2.1
), we obtain

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left((t+4\pi nv)^{2k-2}\right)\\
=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{r}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}k-1\\
\\
r\end{smallmatrix}\right)(2-2k+r)_{k-1-r}v^{r+1-k}(4\pi)^{r}D^{r}\left((t+4\pi nv)^{2k-2}\right).

By (
4.1
) and the following evaluation

D^{r}\left((t+4\pi nv)^{2k-2}\right)=(-n)^{r}\frac{(2k-2)!}{(2k-r-2)!}(t+4\pi nv)^{2k-2-r},

we derive that

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left((t+4\pi nv)^{2k-2}\right)=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{(2k-2)!}{(k-1)!}v^{1-k}t^{k-1}(t+4\pi nv)^{k-1}.

This implies that

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(\Gamma^{*}(2k-1,4\pi nv)q^{-n}\right)=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}v^{1-k}\overline{q}^{n}}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k-1}(t+4\pi nv)^{k-1}e^{-t}dt.

We next evaluate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k-1}(t+4\pi nv)^{k-1}e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}k-1\\
\\
r\end{smallmatrix}\right)(4\pi nv)^{k-1-r}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k-1+r}e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}k-1\\
\\
r\end{smallmatrix}\right)(4\pi nv)^{k-1-r}\Gamma(k\!+\!r)

\displaystyle=(k\!-\!1)!(4\pi nv)^{k-1}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k\!+\!r\!-\!1)!}{r!(k\!-\!r\!-\!1)!(4\pi nv)^{r}}.

Using (
2.4
), we arrive at

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(\Gamma^{*}(2k-1,4\pi nv)q^{-n}\right)=(-1)^{k+1}\sqrt{\frac{v}{\pi}}(4\pi n)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}K_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi nv)e^{-2\pi inu},

which gives the contribution of the second term in the summation over
n
.

Lastly, we evaluate

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(\mathcal{E}_{2-2k}(\tau)\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{1-2k}(n)R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(q^{n}\right).

By (
2.1
), we find

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(q^{n}\right)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{r}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}k-1\\
\\
r\end{smallmatrix}\right)(2-2k+r)_{k-1-r}v^{r+1-k}(4\pi)^{r}D^{r}\left(q^{n}\right).

Computing

D^{r}\left(q^{n}\right)=n^{r}q^{n},

and using (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(q^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+1}v^{1-k}q^{n}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\frac{(2k-r-2)!}{r!(k-1-r)!}(4\pi nv)^{r}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+1}(4\pi n)^{k-1}q^{n}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k+r-1)!}{r!(k-1-r)!(4\pi nv)^{r}}

by making the changes of variables
r\mapsto k-1-r
. Using (
2.4
), we arrive at

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(q^{n}\right)=(-1)^{k+1}\sqrt{\frac{v}{\pi}}(4\pi n)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}K_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi nv)e^{2\pi inu}.

Hence, we conclude that

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(\mathcal{E}_{2-2k}(\tau)\right)=(-1)^{k+1}\sqrt{\frac{v}{\pi}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{1-2k}(n)(4\pi n)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}K_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi nv)e^{2\pi inu},

which gives the remaining contribution.
∎

Our next lemma relates
R_{2-2k}^{k-1}(\mathbb{E}_{2-2k}(\tau))
to
E(\tau;k)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}(\mathbb{E}_{2-2k}(\tau))=\frac{B_{2k}(4\pi)^{2k-1}(k-1)!}{2(2k)!}E(\tau;k).

Proof.

From
Lemma

2.3
, we obtain

(4.2)

\frac{B_{2k}(4\pi)^{2k-1}(k-1)!}{2(2k)!}E(\tau;k)=\frac{B_{2k}(4\pi)^{2k-1}(k-1)!}{2(2k)!}\\
\times\bBigg@{3}(v^{k}+\phi(k)v^{1-k}+2\sqrt{v}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\phi(n,k)K_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi|n|v)e^{2\pi inu}\bBigg@{3}).

We now compare (
4.2
) and
Lemma

4.2
term-wise. One directly sees that the constants in front of
v^{k}
are equal. Using

B_{2k}=\frac{2(-1)^{k+1}(2k)!}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\zeta(2k)\qquad\text{for }k\geq 1

and

\Gamma(k-1)\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)=2^{3-2k}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2k-2),

we also observe that the coefficients in front of
v^{1-k}
coincide.

Using

(4.3)

\sum_{ab=n}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}=n^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{1-2k}(n)\qed

the claim follows for the remaining term.

Next, we compute the holomorphic part of
(-\frac{1}{4\pi})^{k-1}R_{2-2k}^{k-1}(\mathbb{E}_{2-2k}(\tau))
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The holomorphic part of

\left(-\frac{1}{4\pi}\right)^{k-1}R_{2-2k}^{k-1}(\mathbb{E}_{2-2k}(\tau))

is

\left(q\frac{d}{dq}\right)^{k-1}\mathcal{E}_{2-2k}(\tau).

Proof.

Clearly the terms with
v^{k}
and
v^{1-k}
do not contribute to the holomorphic part. To finish the claim, we need to study

f_{\pm}(\tau):=\sqrt{v}K_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(v)e^{\pm iu}.

From (
2.4
), we obtain

\sqrt{v}K_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(v)e^{v}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k-1+r)!}{r!(k-1-r)!(2v)^{r}}

and note that the constant term as a function of
\frac{1}{v}
is
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}
.
Since

e^{-v+iu}=e^{i(u+iv)}=e^{i\tau}\quad\text{and}\quad e^{-v-iu}=e^{-i(u-iv)}=e^{-i\overline{\tau}},

the holomorphic part of
f_{+}(\tau)
is
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{i\tau}
and
f_{-}(\tau)
does not have a holomorphic part. Hence, the holomorphic part of
(-\frac{1}{4\pi})^{k-1}R_{2-2k}^{k-1}(\mathbb{E}_{2-2k}(\tau))
is as claimed.
∎

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

1.3
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Since

R_{\kappa}(f(r\tau))=r\left[R_{\kappa}\left(f(\tau)\right)\right]_{\tau\mapsto r\tau},

we have

R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(\mathbb{E}_{2-2k}(2\tau)\right)=2^{k-1}\left[R_{2-2k}^{k-1}\left(\mathbb{E}_{2-2k}(\tau)\right)\right]_{\tau\mapsto 2\tau}.

By Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
,
(q\frac{d}{dq})^{k-1}\mathcal{E}_{2-2k}(\tau)
is the holomorphic part of

\left(-\frac{1}{4\pi}\right)^{k-1}R_{2-2k}^{k-1}(\mathbb{E}_{2-2k}(\tau))=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}(4\pi)^{k}(k-1)!B_{2k}}{2(2k)!}E(\tau;k),

Therefore, by
Lemma

4.1
, we conclude that
g_{k}(q)
is the holomorphic part of
\widehat{g}_{k}(\tau)
.

We next determine the modularity properties of
\widehat{g}_{k}
. We have that
E(\tau;k)
is a Maass form on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, so
E(2\tau;k)
is a Maass form on
\Gamma_{0}(2)
. The eigenvalue of
E(\tau;k)
is
k(1-k)
so the same holds for
\widehat{g}_{k}(\tau)
.
∎

5.
An example

Here we explicitly work out the example of
g_{2}(q)
, writing it in terms of more elementary functions.

By Theorem
1.3
, for
k=2
, we have that
g_{2}(q)
is the holomorphic part of

\widehat{g}_{2}(\tau)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{90}\left(E(\tau;2)-4E(2\tau;2)\right).

We have, by
Lemma

2.3
,

E(\tau;2)=v^{2}+\frac{\phi(2)}{v}+2\sqrt{v}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\phi(n,2)K_{\frac{3}{2}}(2\pi|n|v)e^{2\pi inu}.

We then compute

\phi(2)=\frac{45}{\pi^{3}}\zeta(3),\quad K_{\frac{3}{2}}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}\left(1+\frac{1}{x}\right)e^{-x}.

We also have that by (
4.3
),

\phi(n,2)=\frac{90}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{ab=|n|}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}=\frac{90}{\pi^{2}}|n|^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma_{-3}(|n|).

Thus, we obtain that

E(\tau;2)=v^{2}+\frac{45\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}v}+\frac{90}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}n\sigma_{-3}(n)\left(1+\frac{1}{2\pi nv}\right)\left(q^{n}+\overline{q}^{n}\right),

which gives

\displaystyle\widehat{g}_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}v^{2}-\frac{\zeta(3)}{2\pi v}+\sum_{n\geq 1}n\sigma_{-3}(n)\left(q^{n}-4q^{2n}+\overline{q}^{n}-4\overline{q}^{2n}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\frac{1}{2\pi v}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{-3}(n)\left(q^{n}-2q^{2n}+\overline{q}^{n}-{2}\overline{q}^{2n}\right).

Now we observe that from
Lemma

4.1
,

\sum_{n\geq 1}n\sigma_{-3}(n)\left(q^{n}-4q^{2n}\right)=g_{2}(q).

Therefore, we conclude that

\widehat{g}_{2}(\tau)=g_{2}(q)+g_{2}(\overline{q})-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}v^{2}+\frac{1}{2\pi v}\bigg(\mathcal{G}_{2}(q)+\mathcal{G}_{2}(\overline{q})-\zeta(3)\bigg),

where

\mathcal{G}_{2}(q):=\mathcal{E}_{-2}(\tau)-2\mathcal{E}_{-2}(2\tau).

Note that
(-q;q)_{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{2}(\tau)
can be interpreted as the generating function for
s_{3}^{*}(n)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}{\rm srp}_{3}(\lambda)
, where
{\rm srp}_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{k}}
. We first observe that

\mathcal{G}_{2}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{n^{3}\left(1-q^{n}\right)}-2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n}}{n^{3}\left(1-q^{2n}\right)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{n^{3}\left(1+q^{n}\right)}.

From the theory of partitions, it is not hard to see that

f_{3}(\zeta;q):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\zeta^{{\rm srp}_{3}(\lambda)}q^{|\lambda|}=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1+\zeta^{\frac{1}{n^{3}}}q^{n}\right).

Therefore, we find that

\sum_{n\geq 0}s_{3}^{*}(n)q^{n}=\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}f_{3}(\zeta;q)\right]_{\zeta=1}=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{n^{3}(1+q^{n})}=(-q;q)_{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{2}(q).

6.
Questions for Future research

(1)

It is natural to ask how
{\rm srp}(\lambda)
is distributed along the set
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Recently, Bridges
[
1
]
proved that for
x\in\mathbb{R}

\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{n}\left(2S-\log\left(\sqrt{3n}\right)\leq x\right)=P\left(H\leq x\right),

where
S(\lambda):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}
,
\lambda\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
is chosen uniform randomly, and
H
is a random harmonic sum, i.e.,
H:=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{k}
. Here,
\varepsilon_{k}
are independent random variables with
P(\varepsilon_{k}\!=\!\pm 1)=\frac{1}{2}
. Note that from
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\mathbb{E}(2S)=\log(\sqrt{3n})
. The asymptotics of the moments of
{\rm srp}(\lambda)
, i.e.,
s_{k}(n)
, give more detailed information on how
{\rm srp}(\lambda)
is distributed. The modularity of
\widehat{g}_{k}
established in this paper could be helpful to derive sharper asymptotics of
s_{k}(n)
.

(2)

It would be interesting to investigate analogues of
{\rm srp}(\lambda)
which can be related with modular forms like Eisenstein series. As a possible candidate, one may consider a twisted sum of reciprocals of parts in the partition
\lambda
,

{\rm srp}_{\chi_{p}}(\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}}{p}\right)}{\lambda_{j}}

for a partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, where
(\frac{\cdot}{p})
is the Legendre symbol (mod
p
) for an odd prime
p
. Now we define the first moment of
{\rm srp}_{\chi_{p}}(\lambda)
as

s_{\chi_{p}}(n):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}{\rm srp}_{\chi_{p}}(\lambda).

Then, the generating function for
s_{\chi_{p}}(n)
is

T_{\chi_{p}}(q):=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\chi_{p}(m)q^{m}}{m\!\left(1\!+\!q^{m}\right)}=-(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d|n}(-1)^{d}\chi_{p}\!\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\!dq^{n}.\hskip-17.07182pt

Note that the summation part is a formally anti-derivative (w.r.t.
q\frac{d}{dq}
) of

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{d|n}(-1)^{d}\chi_{p}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)dq^{n}.

This hints possible modular properties of
T_{\chi_{p}}(q)
and higher moments for
{\rm srp}_{\chi_{p}}(\lambda)
.

(3)

Eichler integrals occur in many places. For example, by the works of Han and Ji
[
6
,
7
]
, they are related to
q
-brackets. So it would be interesting to investigate whether our functions are related to these.

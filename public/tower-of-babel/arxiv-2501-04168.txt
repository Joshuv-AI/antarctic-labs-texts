Quantum One-Time Memories from Stateless Hardware, Random Access Codes, and Simple Nonconvex Optimization
Lev Stambler

Abstract
We present a construction of one-time memories (OTMs) using classical-accessible stateless hardware, building upon the work of Broadbent et al. and Behera et al. [ BGZ21 , BSS21 ] . Unlike the aforementioned work, our approach leverages quantum random access codes (QRACs) to encode two classical bits, b 0 b_{0} and b 1 b_{1} , into a single qubit state ℰ ⁡ ( b 0 ​ b 1 ) \mathcal{E}\left(b_{0}b_{1}\right) where the receiver can retrieve one of the bits with a certain probability of error. To prove soundness, we define a nonconvex optimization problem over POVMs on ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} . This optimization gives an upper bound on the probability of distinguishing bit b 1 − α b_{1-\alpha} given that the probability that the receiver recovers bit b α b_{\alpha} is high. Assuming the optimization is sufficiently accurate, we then prove soundness against a polynomial number of classical queries to the hardware.

2.1
Quantum Random Access Code (QRAC)

A quantum random access code is a uniquely quantum primitive that allows a sender to encode multiple messages into a single quantum state.
A receiver can then perform a measurement on the state to recover one of the messages with some probability of error.

2

For our purposes, we will consider a fixed

2.2
POVMs, Trace Distance, and Distinguishing Probability

We will use a few basic definitions and theorems from quantum information theory.
For a more in-depth treatment, see Nielsen and Chuang
[
NC01
]
.

2.2
POVM.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space. A Positive Operator-Valued Measure (POVM) on
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of operators
\set{M_{i}}_{i\in[m]}
such that
M_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}M_{i}=I
.

POVMs are useful as they can represent any quantum measurement, including projective measurements.

2.3
POVMs Capture All Measurements.

Every quantum measurement can be represented as a POVM. In particular, for any projective measurement with measurement operators
P_{i}
, there exists a POVM
\{M_{i}\}
such that
P_{i}^{\dagger}P_{i}=M_{i}
.

Importantly, given a POVM, we can find the set of projectors that correspond to the POVM by using Naimark’s Dilation Theorem.

2.4
Naimark’s Dilation Theorem,
[
Nai43
]
.

Every POVM can be realized as a projective measurement in a higher-dimensional Hilbert space. Formally, given a POVM
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in[m]}
on
\mathcal{H}
, there exists an auxiliary Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{a}
, an isometry
V:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{a}
, and projectors
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in[m]}
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{a}
such that:

M_{i}=V^{\dagger}P_{i}V.

Alternatively, the projectors can be constructed as:

P_{i}=U\sqrt{M_{i}},

where
U
is an isometry acting on
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{a}
.

Finally, we will use the trace distance to quantify the distinguishability between quantum states.

2.5
Trace Distance.

The trace distance between two quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
is defined as:

T(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1},

where
\|\cdot\|_{1}
denotes the trace norm,
\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{(\rho-\sigma)^{\dagger}(\rho-\sigma)}
.

For our purposes, trace distance is useful as it directly gives an upper bound on the probability of distinguishing two quantum states.

2.6
Trace Distance and Distinguishability.

The trace distance
T(\rho,\sigma)
quantifies the distinguishability between two quantum states. Specifically, for any POVM
\{M_{i}\}
, the maximum probability of distinguishing
\rho
and
\sigma
is:

P_{\text{dist}}=\frac{1}{2}(1+T(\rho,\sigma)).

2.3
One Time Memories

Though more complex definitions of one-time memories exist (such as the UC-based definitions in Ref.
[

[

2.7
One-Time Memory.

A one-time memory is a protocol between a sender and receiver which can be represented as a tuple of algorithms
(\texttt{OTM.Prep},\texttt{OTM.Read})
where

•

OTM.Prep
is a probabilistic algorithm which takes as input
m_{0},m_{1}\in\texttt{Msgs}
and outputs a quantum state
\rho
as well as classical auxiliary information
aux
.

•

OTM.Read
is a (potentially probabilistic) algorithm which takes as input
\rho,\texttt{aux}
and
\alpha\in\set{0,1}
and outputs a classical string

with probability

.

2.8
One-Time Memory Soundness.

A one-time memory
(\texttt{OTM.Prep},\texttt{OTM.Read})
is sound relative to an adversary
\mathcal{A}
if there is a simulator
\mathcal{S}
and a negligible function
\gamma(\cdot)
for every
m_{0},m_{1}\in\texttt{Msgs}
. The simulator
\mathcal{S}
makes at most one classical query to
g^{m_{0},m_{1}}:\set{0,1}\rightarrow\set{m_{0},m_{1}}
(where
g(\alpha)=m_{\alpha}
). The requirement is:

\mathcal{A}\left(\texttt{OTM.Prep}(1^{\lambda},m_{0},m_{1})\right)\overset{\gamma(\lambda)}{\approx_{c}}\mathcal{S}_{OTM}^{g^{m_{0},m_{1}}}\left(1^{\lambda}\right),

where
\overset{\gamma(\lambda)}{\approx_{c}}
denotes computational indistinguishability for a negligible function
\gamma(\lambda)
.

.
∎

Appendix A
Tail Bounds

A.1
Supermartingale Probability Tail Bounds.

Let
X_{i}
for
i\in[n]
be an indicator random variable that
f_{i}(x_{i})=1
for some
f_{i}\xleftarrow{\$}F_{i}
and set of functions
F_{i}
.
Then, if

\Pr_{x_{i},f_{i}}[f_{i}(x_{i})=1\mid x_{1},\dots x_{n}]\leq p_{i}

we have

\Pr\left[\sum X_{i}-\sum p_{i}\geq t\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\right)

Proof.

Let
S_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}X_{j}
and
S_{0}=0
.
Then, let
Y_{i}=S_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{i}p_{j}
where
Y_{0}=0
.
Because
\E[X_{i}\mid X_{1},\dots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},X_{n}]\leq p_{i}
, we have

\E[X_{i}\mid X_{1},\dots X_{i-1}]\leq p_{i}

and so

\displaystyle\E[Y_{i+1}\mid Y_{1}\dots Y_{i}]

\displaystyle\leq S_{i}+\E[X_{i+1}]-\sum_{j=1}^{i}p_{j}-p_{i+1}

\displaystyle\leq S_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{i}p_{j}=Y_{i}.

We thus have that
Y_{0},\dots,Y_{n}
is a supermartingale
[
GH97
]
.
We can then note that
|Y_{i}-Y_{j}|\leq 1
and so, using Azuma-Hoeffding’s inequality for supermartingales
[
Azu67
,
Hoe94
]
,

\Pr\left[Y_{n}-Y_{0}\geq t\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\right).

Because
Y_{n}=S_{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
, we have that

\Pr\left[S_{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\geq t\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\right)

as desired.
∎

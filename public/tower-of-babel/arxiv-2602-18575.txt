Power partitions and Khinchin families
José L. Fernández, Víctor J. Maciá

Abstract
. We prove, within the probabilistic framework of Khinchin families, the Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic formula for the number p k ​ ( n ) p_{k}(n) of partitions of n n into k k -th powers: p k ​ ( n ) ∼ α k n ( 3 ​ k + 1 ) / ( 2 ​ k + 2 ) ​ exp ⁡ ( β k ​ n 1 / ( k + 1 ) ) , n → ∞ , p_{k}(n)\sim\frac{\alpha_{k}}{n^{(3k+1)/(2k+2)}}\exp\!\bigl(\beta_{k}\,n^{1/(k+1)}\bigr),\hskip 10.00002ptn\to\infty, where α k \alpha_{k} and β k \beta_{k} are explicit constants depending only on k k . The argument reduces to verifying strong Gaussianity of the associated Khinchin family and computing asymptotic approximations of its mean and variance.

Introduction

For each integer
n\geq 1
, a partition of
n
into
k
-th powers is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq\ldots\geq m_{l}\geq 1
so that
m_{1}^{k}+\ldots m_{l}^{k}=n
.

In this paper we present a direct proof,
within the probabilistic framework provided by the theory of Khinchin families, of the asymptotic formula for the number
p_{k}(n)
of partitions of
n
into perfect
k
-th powers of positive integers:

(HR)

p_{k}(n)\sim\frac{\alpha_{k}}{n^{(3k{+}1)/(2k{+}2)}}\exp(\beta_{k}n^{1/(k{+}1)})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}\,,

where
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are specific constants which depend only on
k
, see Theorem
D
.

For general partitions, i.e., for the case
k=1
, with parameters
\alpha_{1}=1/(4\sqrt{3})
and
\beta_{1}=\pi\sqrt{2/3}
, this is the Hardy-Ramanujan’s formula of
[
8
]
. The formula (
HR
) for general
k\geq 1
appears also, with no proof, in
[
8
, page 111]
, with the notation
p^{s}(n)
for partitions into
s
-th powers.

Wright in
[
20
]
obtained an asymptotic expansion of
p_{k}(n)
as
n\to\infty
by means of a quite complex argument. Recently, through an expert use of the circle method of Hardy-Ramanujan-Littlewood, Vaughan in
[
19
]
has obtained, for the case
k=2
, an asymptotic expansion of
p_{2}(n)
; this argument has been later generalized by Gafni in
[
7
]
to cover the general case
k\geq 1
.

More recently, Tenenbaum, Wu and Li in
[
16
]
, see also
[
17
]
, have greatly simplified the proof of the asymptotic expansion of
p_{k}(n)
by approaching the estimation through the saddle point method.

The theory of
Khinchin families
originates in the work of Hayman
[
9
]
, Rosenbloom
[
14
]
and Báez-Duarte
[
1
]
and has been developed at length in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
, see also,
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
.

For a nonconstant power series
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
with nonnegative coefficients,
a_{0}>0
and positive radius of convergence
R
we associate its
Khinchin family

(X_{t})_{t\in[0,R)}
of probability distributions in
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
given by

\mathbf{P}(X_{t}=n)=\frac{a_{n}t^{n}}{f(t)},\hskip 20.00003ptn\geq 0,\ t\in(0,R),

completed with
X_{0}\equiv 0
. The
mean
and
variance
functions of the family
X_{t}
are the functions

m_{f}(t)=\mathbf{E}(X_{t}),\hskip 20.00003pt\sigma_{f}^{2}(t)=\mathbf{V}(X_{t}),\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for $t\in[0,R)$}.

A Khinchin family
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,R)}
is termed
Gaussian
if the normalized variables of the family
\breve{X}_{t}=(X_{t}-m_{f}(t))/\sigma_{f}(t),t\in(0,R)
, converge in distribution to the standard normal distribution, i.e. if
\lim_{t\uparrow R}\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}_{t}}\big)=e^{-\theta^{2}/2}
for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. A family is further termed
strongly Gaussian
(following Báez-Duarte) if
\lim_{t\uparrow R}{\sigma_{f}(t)}=+\infty
and

\lim_{t\uparrow R}\int_{-\pi\sigma_{f}(t)}^{\pi\sigma_{f}(t)}\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}_{t}}\big)-e^{-\theta^{2}/2}\big|d\theta=0\,.

If the Khinchin family
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,R)}
associated to a power series
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
as above is strongly Gaussian then the Taylor coefficients of
f
satisfy
Hayman’s asymptotic formula
:

a_{n}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\frac{f(t_{n})}{t_{n}^{n}\,\sigma_{f}(t_{n})},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
t_{n}
is given by
m_{f}(t_{n})=n
, and even further, if
\widetilde{m}_{f}
and
\widetilde{\sigma}_{f}^{2}
are appropriate asymptotic approximations of the mean and variance functions of the Khinchin family, we also have that

a_{n}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\frac{f(\tau_{n})}{\tau_{n}^{n}\,\widetilde{\sigma}_{f}(\tau_{n})},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
\tau_{n}
is given by
\widetilde{m}_{f}(\tau_{n})=n
.

In Section
1
we shall review the aspects of the theory of Khinchin families which are relevant for the presentation in this paper.

Let
P_{k}(z)
be the
generating function of the partitions into
k
-th powers
:

P_{k}(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-z^{n^{k}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(n)z^{n}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all $z\in\mathbb{D}$}\,;

the power series above has radius of convergence
R=1
. We denote with
(X^{[k]}_{t})_{t\in[0,1)}
the Khinchin family associated to
P_{k}
.

To obtain the asymptotic formula (
HR
) we proceed along the following steps:

•

Gaussianity
: the family
(X^{[k]}_{t})_{t\in[0,1)}
is Gaussian, see Corollary
A
; this result actually comes from
[
12
]
,

•

strong Gaussianity
: that the family
(X^{[k]}_{t})_{t\in[0,1)}
is strongly Gaussian is shown in Section
2.3
by combining Gaussianity with the bounds of Lemma 2.3 of
[
17
]
of Tenenbaum-Wu-Li, recorded as Lemma
B
in this paper.

•

asymptotic approximation of the mean
m_{k}
and variance
\sigma_{k}^{2}
functions of the family
X_{t}^{[k]}
are exhibited in Section
2.2
.

•

the asymptotic formula (
HR
) follows
, from strong
Gaussianity, by substituting the asymptotic approximations of the previous step into Hayman’s formula of Theorem
B
; see the proof of Theorem
D
of Section
3
.

The approach we have just described comes essentially from the paper
[
1
]
of Báez-Duarte. Other instances of the use of this approach are
[
2
]
and, more recently,
[
10
]
.

We remark that this approach only aims at obtaining asymptotic formulas like (
HR
) and not at obtaining full asymptotic expansions.

Some notations.

For two functions
\alpha
and
\beta
, we say that they are
asymptotically equivalent
as
t\uparrow R
, and write
\alpha(t)\sim\beta(t)
, if

\displaystyle\lim_{t\uparrow R}\frac{\alpha(t)}{\beta(t)}=1\,.

We use
\mathbf{P}
to denote probability defined in the appropriate space, and denote by
\mathbf{E}(Y)
and
\mathbf{V}(Y)
the expectation and variance of a random variable
Y
.

We use
\mathbb{D}
to denote the unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{D}(a,r)
to denote the disk of center
a\in\mathbb{C}
and radius
r>0
.

1.
Khinchin families

We collect here the basic facts about Khinchin families that will be used throughout this paper.
Comprehensive treatments of the theory can be found in
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
and also in
[
11
,
12
]
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
denote the class of nonconstant power series

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

with nonnegative coefficients, positive radius of convergence
R>0
, and
a_{0}>0
.

To each
f\in\mathcal{K}
we associate the
Khinchin family

(X_{t})_{t\in[0,R)}
of probability distributions on the nonnegative integers
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
defined by

\mathbf{P}(X_{t}=n)=\frac{a_{n}t^{n}}{f(t)},\hskip 20.00003ptn\geq 0,\ t\in(0,R),

and completed with
X_{0}\equiv 0
, for
t=0
. Since
f(t)>0
for
t\in[0,R)
, this family of distributions is well defined.

Observe that
\sigma_{f}(t)>0
, for
t\in(0,R)
, since each
X_{t}
, for
t>0
, takes at least two values.

The
mean
and
variance
of
X_{t}
are given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle m_{f}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}(X_{t})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{na_{n}t^{n}}{f(t)}=\frac{tf^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)},

\displaystyle\sigma_{f}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{V}(X_{t})=\mathbf{E}(X_{t}^{2})-\mathbf{E}(X_{t})^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}a_{n}t^{n}}{f(t)}-\Big(\frac{tf^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{f(t)}+\frac{tf^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}-\Big(\frac{tf^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\Big)^{2}=t\,m_{f}^{\prime}(t).

The last equality follows by differentiating the expression
m_{f}(t)=tf^{\prime}(t)/f(t)
.

We define the normalized random variable
\breve{X}_{t}
by

\breve{X}_{t}=\frac{X_{t}-m_{f}(t)}{\sigma_{f}(t)},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for any }t\in(0,R).

Note that
\breve{X}_{t}
is only defined for
t\in(0,R)
, since
\sigma_{f}(0)=0
.

1.1.
The fulcrum of a power series.

Any function
f\in\mathcal{K}
does not vanish on the real interval
[0,R)
.
Hence for any
f\in\mathcal{K}
we can define its so called
fulcrum

F
in a simply connected domain
\Omega_{f}
containing
[0,R)
by

F(z)=\ln(f(e^{z})),\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for any }z\in\Omega_{f},

where the branch of the logarithm is chosen so that
F
is real on
(-\infty,\ln R)
.

If
f
vanishes nowhere in the unit disk (as it is the case for Khinchin families associated to partitions) then we may take
\Omega_{f}
as the left half-plane
\{\Re(z)<0\}
.

In terms of the fulcrum we have that

m_{f}(e^{s})=F^{\prime}(s),\hskip 20.00003pt\sigma_{f}^{2}(e^{s})=F^{\prime\prime}(s),\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for any }s<\ln R.

The fulcrum codifies quite efficiently many probabilistic quantities associated to the Khinchin family, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
12
]
for further details.

1.2.
Gaussian Khinchin families.

We present the concept of Gaussian Khinchin family and a criterion for Gaussianity.

1.2.1.
Characteristic function.

The characteristic function of the normalized variable
\breve{X}_{t}
of the family can be written as

\mathbf{E}(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}_{t}})=\frac{f(te^{\imath\theta/\sigma_{f}(t)})}{f(t)}\,e^{{-}\imath\theta m_{f}(t)/\sigma_{f}(t)},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for any }\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

This expression connects the analytic behaviour of
f
with the probabilistic properties of its associated Khinchin family and plays a central role in the theory.

For the modulus of the characteristic function of
\breve{X}_{t}
we have

\big|\mathbf{E}(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}_{t}})\big|=\frac{\big|f(te^{\imath\theta/\sigma_{f}(t)})\big|}{f(t)},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for any }\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

1.2.2.
Gaussianity of a Khinchin family.

We say that
f
, or equivalently its Khinchin family
(X_{t})
, is
Gaussian
if
\breve{X}_{t}
tends in distribution to the standard normal distribution, as
t\uparrow R

or, equivalently (Lévy’s continuity theorem), if

\lim_{t\uparrow R}\mathbf{E}(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}_{t}})=e^{-\theta^{2}/2},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all }\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

1.2.3.
Criterion for Gaussianity.

The following Gaussianity criterion in terms of the fulcrum and its derivatives is
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{K}
be a power series with radius of convergence
R>0
. If its fulcrum
F
satisfies

\lim_{s\uparrow\ln R}\frac{F^{(j)}(s)}{F^{\prime\prime}(s)^{j/2}}=0\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for every $j\geq 3$}\,,

then
f
is Gaussian.

See
[
12
]
for a proof and a range of applications.

1.3.
Strongly Gaussian power series.

A power series
f\in\mathcal{K}
(or its associated Khinchin family
(X_{t})
) is termed
strongly Gaussian
if

\lim_{t\uparrow R}\sigma_{f}^{2}(t)=+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{t\uparrow R}\int_{-\pi\sigma_{f}(t)}^{\pi\sigma_{f}(t)}\bigl|\mathbf{E}(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}_{t}})-e^{-\theta^{2}/2}\bigr|\,d\theta=0.

Strongly Gaussian power series satisfy a local central limit theorem that gives precise asymptotic information about their coefficients; see
[
1
,
3
]
and
[
9
]
. Strong Gaussianity (a
L^{1}
condition) implies Gaussianity (a pointwise requirement). The function
e^{z^{2}}
is Gaussian, but not strongly Gaussian, see
[
3
]
.

1.3.1.
Hayman’s asymptotic formula.

In this strongly Gaussian setting, the coefficients of
f\in\mathcal{K}
admit a precise asymptotic description:

Theorem B

(Hayman’s asymptotic formula)
.

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathcal{K}
is strongly Gaussian, then

\displaystyle a_{n}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\frac{f(t_{n})}{t_{n}^{n}\,\sigma_{f}(t_{n})},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
t_{n}
is uniquely determined by
m_{f}(t_{n})=n
for each
n\geq 1
.

For strongly Gaussian families, it is always the case that
\lim_{t\uparrow R}m_{f}(t)=+\infty
. This fact follows from Hayman’s Central Limit Theorem; see the remarks after
[
3
, Theorem A, Section 3.2]
. Observe that then
m_{f}
defines a homeomorphism from
[0,R)
onto
[0,+\infty)
, since
tm_{f}^{\prime}(t)=\sigma_{f}^{2}(t)>0
, for
t>0
, and thus that for each integer
n\geq 1
there exists in fact a unique
t_{n}
such that
m_{f}(t_{n})=n
.

1.3.2.
Baéz-Duarte substitution.

Explicit formulas for the numbers
t_{n}
appearing in Theorem
B
are typically hard to obtain, since solving the equation

m_{f}(t)=n
is in general not straightforward. Fortunately, one can do with appropriate approximations of
m_{f}
and of
\sigma_{f}^{2}
, as shown by Báez–Duarte
[
1
]
.

Assume that
f\in\mathcal{K}
is strongly Gaussian.
Let
\widetilde{m}_{f}(t)
be a continuous, monotonically increasing function on
[0,R)
with

\widetilde{m}_{f}(t)\to+\infty
as
t\uparrow R
, and suppose that
\widetilde{m}_{f}(t)

approximates
m_{f}(t)
in the sense that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\uparrow R}\frac{m_{f}(t)-\widetilde{m}_{f}(t)}{\sigma_{f}(t)}=0.

For each
n\geq 1
, define
\tau_{n}
by the equation
\widetilde{m}_{f}(\tau_{n})=n
; the following version of Theorem
B
holds.

Theorem C

(Báez-Duarte substitution)
.

With the notations above, if
f(z){=}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathcal{K}
is strongly Gaussian and if (
1.2
) is satisfied, then

a_{n}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\frac{f(\tau_{n})}{\tau_{n}^{\,n}\,\sigma_{f}(\tau_{n})},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{as }n\to\infty.

Moreover, if
\widetilde{\sigma}_{f}(t)
is such that
\sigma_{f}(t)\sim\widetilde{\sigma}_{f}(t)
as
t\uparrow R
, we may further write

(1.3)

\displaystyle a_{n}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\frac{f(\tau_{n})}{\tau_{n}^{\,n}\,\widetilde{\sigma}_{f}(\tau_{n})},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{as }n\to\infty.

See
[
1
]
, and also
[
3
]
, for further details.

2.
Khinchin families of partitions

2.1.
Power partitions.

The infinite product

\displaystyle P_{k}(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-z^{n^{k}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(n)z^{n}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $|z|<1$}\,,

is the ordinary generating function of the partitions into
k
-th powers.

Denote with
(X^{[k]}_{t})_{t\in[0,1)}
the Khinchin family associated to
P_{k}
. Then, for any
t\in(0,1)
, we have the equality in distribution

\displaystyle X^{[k]}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{k}\,G_{t^{j^{k}}}

where
(G_{u})_{u\in[0,1)}
is the Khinchin family associated to
1/(1-z)
and the
G_{t^{j^{k}}}
in the sum above are mutually independent. For
u\in(0,1)
, the variable
G_{u}
is a geometric variable (number of failures until first success, supported in
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
) with probability of success
1{-}u
, i.e.,
\mathbf{P}(G_{u}=n)=u^{n}(1-u)
, for each
n\geq 0
.

We may write

\ln(P_{k}(z))=\sum_{j\geq 1}\ln\frac{1}{1-z^{j^{k}}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\delta_{k}(n)}{n}\,z^{n}:=g_{k}(z)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $|z|<1$}\,,

where
\delta_{k}(n)=\sum_{j^{k}\mid n}j^{k}
, the sum of the perfect
k
-th powers
j^{k}
dividing
n
. Observe that the coefficients of the power series
g_{k}
are non negative real numbers.

2.1.1.
Fulcrum of
P_{k}
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
. The fulcrum
F_{k}(s)
of
P_{k}
is given by

(2.1)

F_{k}(z)=\ln(P_{k}(e^{z}))=\sum_{j\geq 1}\ln\frac{1}{1-e^{j^{k}z}}=g_{k}(e^{z})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $z$ such that $\Re(z)<0$}\,.

In particular,

(2.2)

F_{k}(s)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\ln\frac{1}{1-e^{j^{k}s}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $s<0$}\,.

The first and second derivatives of
F_{k}
evaluated at
-s
with
s>0
admit the expressions

F_{k}^{\prime}(-s)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{j^{k}\,e^{-j^{k}s}}{1-e^{-j^{k}s}},\hskip 20.00003ptF_{k}^{\prime\prime}(-s)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{j^{2k}\,e^{-j^{k}s}}{(1-e^{-j^{k}s})^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $s>0$}\,.

2.1.2.
Gaussianity of
P_{k}
.

We now turn to the asymptotic behaviour of the derivatives of the fulcrum
F_{k}
.

The following lemma is
[
12
, Proposition 5.2]
. It provides the precise asymptotic rate of growth
F_{k}^{(m)}(-s)
as
s\downarrow 0
.

Lemma A
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
. For any integer
m\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle F_{k}^{(m)}(-s)\sim\frac{1}{k}\zeta(1+1/k)\Gamma(m+1/k)\frac{1}{s^{m+1/k}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

Sketch of proof.

For
m=0
, we have, approximating series by integrals,

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}sF_{k}(-s^{k})=\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s\ln\frac{1}{1-e^{-(js)^{k}}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln\frac{1}{1-e^{-x^{k}}}dx=\frac{1}{k}\zeta(1+1/k)\Gamma(1/k)\,.

For
m=1
, we have that

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}s^{k{+}1}F_{k}^{\prime}(-s^{k}){=}\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s\,(js)^{k}\frac{e^{-(js)^{k}}}{1-e^{-(js)^{k}}}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}e^{-x^{k}}}{1-e^{-x^{k}}}dx{=}\frac{1}{k}\zeta(1+1/k)\Gamma(1+1/k)\,.

In general, for
m\geq 2
, let
h(x)=1/(1-e^{-x})
for
x>0
. Observe that

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}s^{mk{+}1}F_{k}^{(m)}(-s^{k})=(-1)^{m-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{mk}h^{(m{-}1)}(x^{k})dx:=(-1)^{m-1}L\,.

Via a change of variables,
y=x^{k}
, and successive integration by parts we get that

L=\frac{(-1)^{m{-}1}}{k}\frac{\Gamma(m+1/k)}{\Gamma(1+1/k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{1/k}h(y)dy

and since

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{1/k}h(y)dy=\Gamma(1+1/k)\zeta(1+1/k)

the result follows.∎

Denote the positive constant factors appearing in the asymptotic formulas of Lemma
A
as

(2.3)

\omega_{k,m}=\frac{1}{k}\zeta(1+1/k)\Gamma(m+1/k)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $k\geq 1$ and $m\geq 0$}\,.

These constants, which are going to appear quite frequently in a number of calculations below, differ by the argument in the
\Gamma
function. Using the functional equation of the
\Gamma
function,
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
, we can write each of them in terms of any other.

Denote
\omega_{k,1}=\Omega_{k}
, then we have for each
k\geq 1
that

(2.4)

\omega_{k,0}=k\Omega_{k},\hskip 10.00002pt\omega_{k,1}=\Omega_{k},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\omega_{k,2}=(1+1/k)\Omega_{k}\,.

The mean and variance functions of
P_{k}
shall be denoted by
m_{k}
and
\sigma^{2}_{k}
, respectively. From Lemma
A
we have that

(2.5)

\begin{aligned} m_{k}(e^{-s})&\sim\omega_{k,1}\frac{1}{s^{1+1/k}}&:=\widetilde{m}_{k}(e^{-s})\,,\\
\sigma_{k}^{2}(e^{-s})&\sim\omega_{k,2}\frac{1}{s^{2+1/k}}&:=\widetilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}(e^{-s})\,,\end{aligned}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{as $s\downarrow 0$}\,.

Notice that both
m_{k}(t)
and
\sigma_{k}(t)
tend to
\infty
as
t\uparrow 1
.

Corollary A
.

The ordinary generating function
P_{k}
of the partitions into
k
-th powers is Gaussian.

Proof.

We include the argument for completeness; see
[
12
]
for further details.
By Lemma
A
, for each fixed
m\geq 3
, we have that

\frac{F_{k}^{(m)}(-s)}{\bigl(F_{k}^{\prime\prime}(-s)\bigr)^{m/2}}\sim\frac{\omega_{k,m}}{\omega_{k,2}^{m/2}}\,\,s^{(m/2-1)/k},\hskip 20.00003pts\downarrow 0.

Since
m\geq 3
, the exponent
(m/2-1)/k
is positive, and therefore the above ratio tends to
0
, as
s\downarrow 0
.
The Gaussianity criterion of Theorem
A
then applies, and we conclude that
P_{k}
is Gaussian.
∎

Remark.

To prove the Gaussianity of
P_{k}
, we may instead appeal to Theorem 3.2 of
[
3
]
and verify that

(2.6)

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{\sup_{|\theta|\leq A}|F_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(-s+\imath\theta)|}{F_{k}^{\prime\prime}(-s)^{3/2}}=0\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for every $A>0$}\,.

In contrast to the Gaussianity criterion of
[
12
]
which we have used above, this criterion only invokes
the third derivative, and not all derivatives of order
\geq 3
, but involves values of that third derivative in the whole left half-plane and not just in the negative real axis.

Actually, we have that

(2.7)

|F_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(-s+\imath\theta)|\leq F_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(-s),\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for any $s>0$ and $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$}\,,

and thus (
2.6
) follows from
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}F_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(-s)/F_{k}^{\prime\prime}(-s)^{3/2}=0
, which we have checked above as part of the proof of Corollary
A
.

To verify (
2.7
), recall, from (
2.1
), that

F_{k}(z)=g_{k}(e^{z})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for any $z$ with $\Re(z)<0$}\,,

where
g_{k}
is a power series in the unit disk with non-negative coefficients. The non-negativity of the coefficients of
g_{k}
implies that

|g_{k}^{(j)}(e^{-s+\imath\theta})|\leq g_{k}^{(j)}(e^{-s})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $s>0$ and $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$ and $j\geq 0$}\,.

Since

F_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)=e^{z}g_{k}^{\prime}(e^{z})+3e^{2z}g_{k}^{\prime\prime}(e^{z})+e^{3z}g_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(ez)\,,

we deduce that

\displaystyle|F_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(-s+\imath\theta)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-s}g_{k}^{\prime}(e^{-s})+3e^{-2s}g_{k}^{\prime\prime}(e^{-s})+e^{-3s}g_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(e^{-s})

\displaystyle=F_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}(-s)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for any $s>0$ and any $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$}\,.

For the generating function
Q_{k}
of partitions into distinct
k
-th powers:

Q_{k}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+z^{j^{k}})\,,

we may write analogously that
Q_{k}\equiv\exp(h_{k})
, where
h_{k}
is the power series

h_{k}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\epsilon_{k}(n)}{n}z^{n}

where

\epsilon_{k}(n)=-\sum_{j^{k}\mid n}(-1)^{n/j^{k}}j^{k}\,.

For
k=1
, the coefficients of
h_{k}
are non-negative: they can be expressed as

\epsilon_{1}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\mid n;\\
j\text{\tiny{odd}}\end{subarray}}j
. But for
k\geq 2
, the coefficients
\epsilon_{k}(n)
are positive and negative for infinitely many indices.

To show that the
Q_{k}
are Gaussian, the argument above appealing to Theorem 3.2 of
[
3
]
is of no avail, and we have to resort to the Gaussianity criterion involving all of the derivatives, as it is done later in Proposition
4.1
.

2.2.
Some asymptotic estimates.

We already know, as part of Lemma
A
, that

\ln(P_{k}(e^{-s}))\sim\omega_{k,0}\frac{1}{s^{1/k}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptm_{k}(e^{-s})\sim\omega_{k,1}\frac{1}{s^{1+1/k}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,,

but since our goal is to apply Theorem
C
, we need the most accurate asymptotic approximations of
P_{k}
and
m_{k}
which we are going to obtain next and record in Corollary
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
.

The argument to obtain the estimates below are standard, but we include the details for completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
. We have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\ln(P_{k}(e^{-s}))=\omega_{k,0}\frac{1}{s^{1/k}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln(s)-k\ln(\sqrt{2\pi})+o(1)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

The level of precision of Lemma
2.1
is just what is needed to get, simply by exponentiating (
2.8
), the following corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle P_{k}(e^{-s})

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{\frac{s}{(2\pi)^{k}}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{k}\zeta(1+1/k)\Gamma(1/k)\frac{1}{s^{1/k}}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{\frac{s}{(2\pi)^{k}}}\exp\left(\omega_{k,0}\frac{1}{s^{1/k}}\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

In the proof of Lemma
2.1
we will resort to
Euler-Maclaurin summation
of order 2 in the following two formats. Here

B_{2}(t)=t^{2}-t+\frac{1}{6}
is the second Bernoulli polynomial and
\{x\}
denotes fractional part.

(a)  For
\phi\in C^{2}[1,\infty)
and positive integer
N
.

(2.9)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi(j)=\int_{1}^{N}\phi(x)\,dx{+}\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\phi(N){+}\phi(1)\big){+}\tfrac{1}{12}\big(\phi^{\prime}(N){-}\phi^{\prime}(1)\big)-\int_{1}^{N}\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\,\frac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2}\,dx\,.

(b)  For
\phi\in C^{2}[1,\infty)
with
\phi(x)\to 0
,
\phi^{\prime}(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
, and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
absolutely integrable on
[1,\infty)
,

(2.10)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(j)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\!\phi(x)\,dx+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\phi(1)-\tfrac{1}{12}\,\phi^{\prime}(1)-\int_{1}^{\infty}\!\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\,\frac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2}\,dx\,.

From (
2.9
) applied to
\phi(x)=\ln(x)
, one obtains the precise standard expression

\ln(N!)=N\ln(N)-N+\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(N)+1-\frac{1}{12}+\int_{1}^{N}\frac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2x^{2}}\,dx+\frac{1}{12\,N}\,,

which, with Stirling’s formula and letting
N\to\infty
, gives the identity

\ln(\sqrt{2\pi})=1-\frac{1}{12}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2x^{2}}\,dx\,,

or

(2.11)

\frac{1}{12}-\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2x^{2}}\,dx=1-\ln(\sqrt{2\pi})\,,

to be used shortly in the proof of (
2.8
).

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Set
\delta=s^{1/k}
and define, for
x>0
,

h(x)=-\ln(1-e^{-x^{k}})\,,

and
\phi(x)=h(x\delta)
.
Then
\ln P_{k}(e^{-s})=\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(j)
.

Since
\phi
and its derivatives decay exponentially towards 0 we may apply (
2.10
) and write

\sum_{j\geq 1}h(j\delta)=I+\frac{1}{2}h(\delta)+R\,,

where
I=\int_{1}^{\infty}h(x\delta)\,dx
and

(2.12)

R=-\tfrac{1}{12}\,\delta\,h^{\prime}(\delta)-\delta^{2}\!\int_{1}^{\infty}\!h^{\prime\prime}(x\delta)\,\tfrac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2}\,dx.

The integral.
Substitute
u=x\delta
:

I=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}h(u)\,du=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(u)\,du-\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}h(u)\,du.

The value of the full integral is

\int_{0}^{\infty}h(u)\,du=\frac{1}{k}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{k})\,\zeta(1+\tfrac{1}{k})=\omega_{k,0}.

For the truncated piece, write
h(u)=-k\ln u+\Delta(u)
where
\Delta(u)=\ln\!\bigl(\frac{u^{k}}{1-e^{-u^{k}}}\bigr)
is continuous at
0
with
\Delta(0)=0
. Then
\int_{0}^{\delta}h(u)\,du=-k\delta\ln\delta+k\delta+o(\delta)
, giving

I=\frac{\omega_{k,0}}{\delta}+k\ln\delta-k+o(1).

The boundary term.
Since
h(\delta)=-k\ln\delta+\Delta(\delta)
:

\tfrac{1}{2}\,h(\delta)=-\tfrac{k}{2}\ln\delta+o(1).

The remainder
R
.
We have
h^{\prime}(u)=-\frac{ku^{k-1}}{e^{u^{k}}-1}
, so

\delta\,h^{\prime}(\delta)=-\frac{k\delta^{k}}{e^{\delta^{k}}-1}=-k+o(1)\,,

and thus

-\frac{1}{12}\delta\,h^{\prime}(\delta)=\frac{k}{12}+o(1)\,.

For the integral in (
2.12
): since
|h^{\prime\prime}(u)|\leq C/u^{2}
for all
u>0
(the singularity at
0
is exactly of order
u^{-2}
, and the decay at
\infty
is exponential), we have
|h^{\prime\prime}(x\delta)\delta^{2}|\leq C/x^{2}
, which is integrable on
[1,\infty)
independently of
\delta
. Since
h^{\prime\prime}(x\delta)\delta^{2}\to k/x^{2}
pointwise as
\delta\downarrow 0
, dominated convergence gives

\delta^{2}\!\int_{1}^{\infty}\!h^{\prime\prime}(x\delta)\,\tfrac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2}\,dx\;\longrightarrow\;k\int_{1}^{\infty}\!\frac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2x^{2}}\,dx,\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{as $\delta\downarrow 0$}\,.

Combining with the derivative correction and appealing to (
2.11
), we get that

R\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{k}{12}-k\int_{1}^{\infty}\!\frac{B_{2}(\{x\})}{2x^{2}}\,dx=k(1-\ln(\sqrt{2\pi}))\,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as $\delta\downarrow 0$}\,.

Collecting.
Using
\delta=s^{1/k}
and
\ln\delta=\frac{1}{k}\ln s
:

\displaystyle\ln P_{k}(e^{-s})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{\omega_{k,0}}{\delta}+(k\ln\delta-k)}_{I}\;\underbrace{{}-\,\tfrac{k}{2}\ln\delta}_{\frac{1}{2}h(\delta)}\;+\underbrace{k-k\ln\sqrt{2\pi}}_{R}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{k,0}}{s^{1/k}}+\tfrac{k}{2}\ln\delta-k\ln(\sqrt{2\pi})+o(1)=\frac{\omega_{k,0}}{s^{1/k}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\ln s-k\ln(\sqrt{2\pi})+o(1).

∎

For the mean
m_{k}(e^{-s})
we just need the simple extra precision recorded in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
. Then

(2.13)

\displaystyle m_{k}(e^{-s})

\displaystyle=\omega_{k,1}\frac{1}{s^{1+1/k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

Proof.

Define the function
\phi(x)
for
x\geq 0
by
\phi(x)=x^{k}/(e^{x^{k}}-1)
for
x>0
and
\phi(0)=1
. The function
\phi
is continuous in
[0,\infty)
and decreases monotonically from
1=\phi(0)
to
0=\lim_{x\to\infty}\phi(x)
, since
\psi(x)=x/(e^{x}-1)
is monotonically decreasing,
x^{k}
is monotonically increasing and
\phi(x)=\psi(x^{k})
.

From monotonicity and since
\phi
is bounded above by 1, we have that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s\phi(sj)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{e^{x^{k}}-1}\,dx=\omega_{k,1}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s\phi(sj)\,+s.

Which, in terms of
m_{k}
, simply says that

0\leq\omega_{k,1}-s^{k{+}1}m_{k}(e^{-s^{k}})\leq s\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for any $s>0$}\,.

Replacing now
s
by
s^{1/k}
and dividing by
s^{(k{+}1)/k}
, the above inequality becomes

0\leq\omega_{k,1}\frac{1}{s^{1{+}1/k}}-m_{k}(e^{-s})\leq s\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for any $s>0$}\,,

which implies the statement of the lemma.∎

As a corollary of Lemma
2.2
we have:

Corollary 2.2
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
and define

\widetilde{m}_{k}(e^{-s})=\omega_{k,1}\frac{1}{s^{1+1/k}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for any $s>0$}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{m_{k}(e^{-s})-\widetilde{m}_{k}(e^{-s})}{\sigma_{k}(e^{-s})}=O(s^{1/(2k)})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

Proof.

Lemma
2.2
gives that

m_{k}(e^{-s})-\widetilde{m}_{k}(e^{-s})=O(1/s)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as $s\downarrow 0$}\,.

From (
2.5
) we have that

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(e^{-s})\sim\omega_{k,2}^{1/2}\frac{1}{s^{1+1/(2k)}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{m_{k}(e^{-s})-\widetilde{m}_{k}(e^{-s})}{\sigma_{k}(e^{-s})}=O(s^{1/(2k)})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

This concludes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Strong Gaussianity of power partitions.

In this section we are going to verify that the Khinchin family
(X_{t}^{[k]})_{t\in[0,1)}
is strongly Gaussian.

For each
t\in(0,1)
, we let
s=-\ln t>0
so that
e^{-s}=t
. We simply write
s
and no
s(t)
as no confusion will arise.

2.3.1.
Bounds on
\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)\big|
.

The key is Lemma 2.3 of
[
17
]
of Tenenbaum, Wu and Li which, in the notations of this paper, reads as follows

Lemma B
.

There are positive constants
d_{1},d_{2}>0
depending only on
k
such that

\frac{|P_{k}(e^{-s+\imath\varphi})|}{P_{k}(e^{-s})}\leq\begin{cases}\exp\big(-d_{1}\varphi^{2}s^{-(2+1/k)}\big),&\quad\mbox{if $|\varphi|\leq 2\pi s$}\,,\\
\\
\exp\big(-d_{2}s^{-1/k}\big),&\quad\mbox{if $2\pi s<|\varphi|$}\leq\pi\,.\end{cases}

The threshold
2\pi s
may be replaced by any pair of overlapping thresholds
B_{2}s<B_{1}s
(with
0<B_{2}<B_{1}
), at the cost of adjusting the constants
d_{1},d_{2}
to
D_{1},D_{2}
which now depend on
k
and also on
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, so that

(2.14)

\frac{|P_{k}(e^{-s+\imath\varphi})|}{P_{k}(e^{-s})}\leq\begin{cases}\exp\big(-D_{1}\varphi^{2}s^{-(2+1/k)}\big),&\quad\mbox{if $|\varphi|\leq B_{1}s$}\,,\\
\\
\exp\big(-D_{2}s^{-1/k}\big),&\quad\mbox{if $B_{2}s<|\varphi|$}\leq\pi\,.\end{cases}

Notice that in the overlap
B_{2}s\leq|\varphi|\leq B_{1}s
, one has that
\phi^{2}s^{-(2+1/k)}
is comparable to
s^{-1/k}
, so both bounds give comparable decay.

For the characteristic function of
\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}
we have that

\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)\big|=\frac{\big|P_{k}(e^{-s+\imath\theta/\sigma_{k}(e^{-s})})\big|}{P_{k}(e^{-s})}\,,

since taking the modulus eliminates the phase term.

From (
2.5
) we have, for positive constants
a<A
, that

(2.15)

a\leq\sigma_{k}(e^{-s})s^{1+(1/(2k))}<A\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $s\in(0,\ln 2)$}\,.

The bound on
\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)\big|
we are after is recorded in the following corollary.

Corollary B
.

For any constant
C>0
there are positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
k
and
C
such that for
s\in(0,\ln 2)
we have that

\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)\big|\leq\begin{cases}e^{-c_{1}\theta^{2}},&\quad\mbox{if $|\theta|\leq C\dfrac{1}{s^{1/(2k)}}$},\\
\\
e^{-c_{2}\frac{1}{s^{1/k}}},&\quad\mbox{if $|\theta|\geq C\dfrac{1}{s^{1/(2k)}}$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We take
B_{1}=C/a
and
B_{2}=C/A
(note that
a<A
by (
2.15
), so
B_{2}<B_{1}
). Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be as in (
2.14
) and
c_{1}=D_{1}/A^{2}
and
c_{2}=D_{2}
.

If
|\theta|\leq Cs^{-1/(2k)}
, then

|\varphi|\leq(C/a)s=B_{1}s
and so by
(
2.14
) and (
2.15
) we get that

\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)\big|\leq\exp(-(D_{1}/A^{2})\theta^{2})=\exp(-c_{1}\theta^{2})\,.

If
|\theta|\geq Cs^{-1/(2k)}
, then
|\varphi|\geq(C/A)s=B_{2}s
and so by (
2.14
) we get that

\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)\big|\leq\exp(-D_{2}s^{-1/k})=\exp(-c_{2}s^{-1/k})\,.

∎

2.3.2.
Strong Gaussianity of
P_{k}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The Khinchin family
(X^{[k]}_{t})_{t\in[0,1)}
associated to the generating function
P_{k}(z)
of partitions into
k
-th powers is strongly Gaussian.

Proof.

Corollary
A
tells us that the family
(X^{[k]}_{t})_{t\in[0,1)}
is Gaussian. Also from (
2.5
), we see that
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\sigma_{k}(e^{-s})=+\infty
.

Now, for
|\theta|\leq C/s^{1/(2k)}
and
s\in(0,\ln 2)
(or
t\in(1/2,1)
), we have from Corollary
B
that

\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)-e^{-\theta^{2}/2}\big|\leq e^{-c_{1}\theta^{2}}+e^{-\theta^{2}/2}\,,

and thus Gaussianity and the Dominated Convergence Theorem gives that

(2.16)

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\int\limits_{|\theta|\leq C/s^{1/(2k)}}\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)-e^{-\theta^{2}/2}\big|\,d\theta=0\,.

We also have that

(2.17)

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\int\limits_{|\theta|\geq C/s^{1/(2k)}}e^{-\theta^{2}/2}d\theta=0\,.

From Corollary
B
, for
s\in(0,\ln 2)
, we get the bound

\int\limits_{\pi\sigma_{k}(t)\geq\theta\geq C/s^{1/(2k)}}\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)\Big|\,d\theta\leq\pi\sigma_{k}(t)e^{-c_{2}/s^{1/k}}\,.

Taking into account (
2.15
), we see that
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\sigma_{k}(t)e^{-c_{2}/s^{1/k}}=0
, since exponential decay beats polynomial growth. Therefore, we have that

(2.18)

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\int\limits_{\pi\sigma_{k}(t)\geq\theta\geq C/s^{1/(2k)}}\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)\Big|\,d\theta=0\,.

From (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) we conclude that

(2.19)

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\int\limits_{\pi\sigma_{k}(t)\geq|\theta|\geq C/s^{1/(2k)}}\big|\mathbf{E}\big(e^{\imath\theta\breve{X}^{[k]}_{t}}\big)-e^{-\theta^{2}/2}\big|\,d\theta=0\,.

Finally, the combination of (
2.16
) and (
2.19
) gives the strong Gaussianity of
P_{k}(z)
.∎

3.
Asymptotic formula of power partitions

We have seen in Theorem
2.1
that
P_{k}
is strongly Gaussian and thus given the approximation of
m_{k}
of Corollary
2.2
we are ready to apply the version of Hayman’s asymptotic formula registered in Theorem
C
to obtain the asymptotic formula for partitions into
k
-th powers.

Theorem D
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle p_{k}(n)\sim\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{(k{+}1)/2}}\,\cdot

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{k}^{k/(k{+}1)}}{(1{+}1/k)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{1}{n^{(3k+1)/(2k+2)}}\exp\left((k+1)\Omega_{k}^{k/(k+1)}\,n^{1/(k+1)}\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty\,,

where
\Omega_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\,\zeta(1+1/k)\Gamma(1+1/k)
.

Observe that according to the statement of Theorem
D
the constants
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
of the Hardy-Ramanujan formula (HR) are given by

\beta_{k}=(k{+}1)\Omega_{k}^{k/(k{+}1)}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha_{k}=\frac{\Omega_{k}^{k/(k{+}1)}}{(2\pi)^{(k{+}1)/2}(1{+}1/k)^{1/2}}\,,

as they should. Also, for
k=1
, we have
\Omega_{1}=\zeta(2)\Gamma(2)=\pi^{2}/6
and
\alpha_{1}=1/(4\sqrt{3})
and
\beta_{1}=\pi\sqrt{2/3}
, as in the Hardy-Ramanujan asymptotic formula for general partitions.

Proof.

We are going to apply Theorem
C
with the approximation
\widetilde{m}_{k}
of
m_{k}
given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}_{k}(e^{-s})=\Omega_{k}\frac{1}{s^{1{+}1/k}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for any }s>0\,,

whose use is justified by
Corollary
2.2
.

The constants
\omega_{k,m}
and
\Omega_{k}
from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) will intervene in what follows.

For
n\geq 1
, take
\tau_{n}=e^{-s_{n}}
, with
s_{n}
given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle s_{n}=(\Omega_{k}/n)^{k/(k+1)}\,,

so that
\widetilde{m}_{k}(\tau_{n})=\widetilde{m}_{k}(e^{-s_{n}})=n
.

Next we just have to plug into the general asymptotic formula (
1.3
) the formula for
\tau_{n}^{n}
, and the asymptotics, taking into account (
2.4
), of
P_{k}(e^{-s_{n}})
and of
\sigma_{k}(e^{-s_{n}})
provided respectively by Corollary
2.1
and (
2.5
).

Observe that from (
3.1
)

(\star_{1})\hskip 10.00002pt\tau_{n}^{-n}=\exp\left(\Omega_{k}^{k/(k{+}1)}\,n^{1/(k{+}1)}\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for each $n\geq 1$}\,.

Using (
2.5
) and (
3.1
) we have that

(\star_{2})\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}(e^{-s_{n}})}\sim\frac{\Omega_{k}^{k/(2k+2)}}{(1+1/k)^{1/2}}\,\frac{1}{n^{(2k{+}1)/(2k{+}2)}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}\,.

And, finally, using Corollary
2.1
, (
2.4
) and (
3.1
) we obtain that

(\star_{3})\hskip 10.00002ptP_{k}(e^{-s_{n}})\sim\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k/2}}\,\Omega_{k}^{k/(2k{+}2)}\,\frac{1}{n^{k/(2k{+}2)}}\,\exp\left(k\Omega_{k}^{k/(k{+}1)}\,n^{1/(k{+}1)}\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}\,.

Substituting
(\star_{1}),(\star_{2})
and
(\star_{3})
into (
1.3
) we obtain the result.∎

4.
Partitions into distinct
k
-th powers

The generating function of partitions into distinct parts which are
k
-th powers, denoted
Q_{k}
, is given by

\displaystyle Q_{k}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+z^{j^{k}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{k}(n)z^{n}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for any }|z|<1\,,

where
q_{k}(n)
denotes the number of partitions of
n
into distinct
k
-powers.

Proposition 4.1
.

The power series
Q_{k}
is Gaussian.

Proof.

We shall derive the statement from the Gaussianity criterion of Theorem
A
.
Denote the fulcrum of
Q_{k}
by
G_{k}
, thus
G_{k}(s)=\ln(Q_{k}(e^{s}))
, for
s<0
.

From

(1+z^{j^{k}})=\frac{1-z^{2j^{k}}}{1-z^{j^{k}}},

we see that the generating functions
Q_{k}
and
P_{k}
are related by

(4.1)

Q_{k}(z)=\frac{P_{k}(z)}{P_{k}(z^{2})}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $|z|<1$}.

This in turn gives that the fulcrum
G_{k}(s)
of
Q_{k}
and the fulcrum
F_{k}(s)
of
P_{k}
are related by

\displaystyle G_{k}(s)=F_{k}(s)-F_{k}(2s)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $s<0$}.

For the derivatives of
G_{k}
we have that

\displaystyle{G_{k}}^{(m)}(s)=F_{k}^{(m)}(s)-2^{m}F_{k}^{(m)}(2s)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $s<0$ and $m\geq 1$}\,,

and thus, for each
m\geq 1
, the asymptotic formula for
F_{k}^{(m)}
of Lemma
A
translates into the following asymptotic formula for
G_{k}^{(m)}
:

(4.2)

\displaystyle G_{k}^{(m)}(-s)\sim(1-2^{-1/k})\,\frac{1}{k}\,\zeta(1+1/k)\Gamma(m+1/k)\frac{1}{s^{m+1/k}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

From the asymptotic formula (
4.2
) we deduce that, for any
m\geq 3
, there exists a constant
C_{k,m}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{G_{k}^{(m)}(-s)}{G_{k}^{\prime\prime}(-s)^{m/2}}\sim C_{k,m}\,s^{(m/2-1)/k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }s\downarrow 0\,.

As in the proof of Corollary
A
this implies, via the Gaussianity criterion of Theorem
A
, that
Q_{k}(z)
is Gaussian.
∎

The
q_{k}(n)
obey the following asymptotic formula

(4.3)

\displaystyle q_{k}(n)\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi_{k}^{k/(2k+2)}}{(1+1/k)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{1}{n^{(2k+1)/(2k+2)}}\exp\{(k+1)\Phi_{k}^{k/(k+1)}n^{1/(k+1)}\}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty\,,

where
\Phi_{k}=(1-2^{-1/k})\frac{1}{k}\zeta(1+1/k)\Gamma(1+1/k)=(1-2^{-1/k})\Omega_{k}
.
This asymptotic formula can be traced back at least to the paper
[
15
]
of Roth and Szekeres, see also
[
18
, Equation 23]
and
[
13
]
.

Since the power series
Q_{k}
is Gaussian,
if we were to have at our disposal a bound for the characteristic function of its Khinchin family like the one recorded in Corollary
B
(which we derived from the Tenenbaum-Wu-Li’s bound of Lemma
B
) for the family of
P_{k}
, then we would have that
Q_{k}
is strongly Gaussian
, as we believe to be the case.

If that were the case, the asymptotic formula (
4.3
) would be an instance of Hayman’s asymptotic formula, of Theorem
B
, using the cases
m=1
and
m=2
of (
4.2
) for the asymptotics of the mean and the variance of the family of
Q_{k}
and the asymptotic formula for
Q_{k}
derived from (
4.1
) and the asymptotic formula for
P_{k}
of Corollary
2.1
, very much like in the proof of Theorem
D
.

Proximately Envy-Free and Efficient Allocation of Mixed Manna
Siddharth Barman, Paritosh Verma

Abstract
The existence of fair and efficient allocations of indivisible items is a central problem in fair division. For indivisible goods, the existence of Pareto efficient ( PO \mathrm{PO} ) and envy free up to one item ( EF ​ 1 \mathrm{EF}1 ) allocations was established by [ 2 ] . In a recent breakthrough, [ 4 ] established the existence of PO \mathrm{PO} and EF ​ 1 \mathrm{EF}1 allocations for indivisible chores. However, the existence of PO \mathrm{PO} and EF ​ 1 \mathrm{EF}1 allocations of mixed manna remains an intriguing open problem. In this paper we make significant progress in this direction. We establish the existence of allocations that are PO \mathrm{PO} and introspective envy free up to one item ( IEF ​ 1 \mathrm{IEF}1 ) for mixed manna. In an IEF ​ 1 \mathrm{IEF}1 allocation, each agent can eliminate its envy towards all the other agents, by either adding an item or removing an item from its own bundle. The notion of IEF ​ 1 \mathrm{IEF}1 coincides with EF ​ 1 \mathrm{EF}1 for indivisible chores, and hence, our existence result generalizes the aforementioned result of [ 4 ] .

The existence of fair and economically efficient allocations is a fundamental problem in fair division. Preliminaries

Fair Division Instance.

A fair division instance

is denoted by a triple

n

is denoted by

. The values of all the items
[m]
for each agent

is collectively denoted by

. We assume that all the agents have additive valuation, i.e., the total value of a set of items
S\subseteq[m]
, referred as a
bundle
, is
v_{i}(S)\coloneqq\sum_{t\in S}v_{i}(t)
, i.e., the sum of values of all items in

. We will assume that

for all

Allocation.

An allocation
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})
is a partition of the set of items
[m]
into
n
disjoint parts, i.e.,
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i,j\in[n]
. An allocation is called

if
\cup_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}=[m]
and

\cup_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\subsetneq[m]

[m]
among
n
agents will be denoted by
\Pi_{n}([m])
.

Fairness and Efficiency.

Next we define the main fairness and efficiency notion used in our paper.

Definition 1

(
\mathrm{IEF}1
)
.

An allocation
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})\in\Pi_{n}([m])
is called
introspective envy free up to one item
or
\mathrm{IEF}1
for an instance
\mathcal{I}=\langle[n],[m],\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\rangle
if for each agent
i\in[n]
there exists a subset
S\subseteq[m]
containing at most one item,
|S|\leq 1
such that
v_{i}(A_{i}\Delta S)\geq max_{j=1}^{n}v_{i}(A_{j})
.
1
1

1

Here
\Delta
denotes the symmetric set difference, i.e., for any two sets
A,B\subseteq[m]
,
A\Delta B=(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)
.

In other words, each agent
i\in[n]
either doesn’t envy any other agent, or its envy towards all the agents can be simlultaneously eliminated by removing or adding one item to its bundle. Notably unlike the standard notion of
\mathrm{EF}1
, the thought-experiment of agent
i
in
\mathrm{IEF}1
, which is aimed towards eliminating its envy towards everyone else, only concerns its own bundle, i.e., it is introspective in nature.

Given an instance
\mathcal{I}=\langle[n],[m],\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\rangle
, an allocation
\mathcal{B}=(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{n})

Pareto dominates
another allocation
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})
if
v_{i}(B_{i})\geq v_{i}(A_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
and
v_{j}(B_{j})>v_{j}(A_{j})
for some
j\in[n]
.

Definition 2

(
\mathrm{PO}
)
.

An allocation
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})\in\Pi_{n}([m])
is called
Pareto efficient
for the instance
\mathcal{I}=\langle[n],[m],\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\rangle
, if there is no allocation
\mathcal{B}\in\Pi_{n}([m])
that Pareto dominates it.

Item Types.

Without loss of generality, we will assume that all the items in
[m]
are of three types. An item
j\in[m]
is a
good
if
v_{i}(j)>0
for all
i\in[n]
. An item
j\in[m]
is a
chore
if
v_{i}(j)<0
for all
i\in[n]
. An item is
nonnegatively-valued
if
v_{i}(j)\geq 0
for all
i\in[n]
,
v_{a}(j)>0
for some
a\in[n]
, and
v_{b}(j)=0
for some
b\in[n]
. This assumption is without loss of generality given our goal is to compute Pareto efficient (and fair) allocations. In particular, if there is an item
k
that is positively-valued by some agent and negatively-valued by another agent, then, in any Pareto efficient allocation, item
k
will never be allocated to an agent having a negative value for it. Hence, we can assume that any agent who values item
k
negatively, values it at zero instead; this leads to the aforementioned item types. An instance where we have both positively and negatively valued items is said to be an instance with
mixed manna
.

Other Notations.

We will use
\Delta_{n-1}
to denote the
n-1
-dimensional simplex, i.e.,
\Delta_{n-1}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=1\text{ and }x_{i}\geq 0\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}
. For any vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we will use
\mathrm{Supp}(w)\coloneqq\{j\in[n]\mid w_{j}>0\}
to denotes the
support
(non-negative coordinates) of the vector

.

\mathrm{IEF}1
for the instance
\mathcal{I}=\langle[n],[m],\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\rangle

3

\overline{v}_{i}(A_{i}\Delta S_{i})\geq max_{j\in[n]}\overline{v}_{i}(A_{j})
, which implies that,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\overline{v}_{i}(A_{i}\Delta S_{i})-\overline{v}_{i}(A_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\overline{v}_{i}((A_{i}\Delta S_{i})\setminus\{m+1\})-\overline{v}_{i}(A_{j}\setminus\{m+1\})+\overline{v}_{i}(m+1)

(
\overline{v}_{i}(m+1)>0
)

\displaystyle\leq v_{i}((A_{i}\Delta S_{i})\setminus\{m+1\})-v_{i}(A_{j}\setminus\{m+1\})+\varepsilon|A_{j}\setminus\{m+1\}|+\overline{v}_{i}(m+1)

(
v_{i}(k)-\varepsilon\leq\overline{v}_{i}(k)\leq v_{i}(k)
)

\displaystyle\leq v_{i}((A_{i}\Delta S_{i})\setminus\{m+1\})-v_{i}(A_{j}\setminus\{m+1\})+\varepsilon m+\frac{\lambda}{2}

(
\overline{v}_{i}(m+1)=\lambda/2
)

\displaystyle<v_{i}((A_{i}\Delta S_{i})\setminus\{m+1\})-v_{i}(A_{j}\setminus\{m+1\})+\frac{\lambda}{2m}\cdot m+\frac{\lambda}{2}

(
\varepsilon<\lambda/2m
)

\displaystyle<v_{i}\left((A_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})\Delta(S_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})\right)-v_{i}(A_{j}\setminus\{m+1\})+\lambda

Thus, we get
v_{i}(A_{j}\setminus\{m+1\})-v_{i}\left((A_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})\Delta(S_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})\right)<\lambda

will get contradicted. Since

for all agents

, we get
Equation

3
, completing the proof.
∎

Now we prove

4
.

Proof of
Lemma

4
.

We begin by defining an intermediate instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\langle[n],[m+1],\{v^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\rangle

for all

, and

to
\mathcal{I}
. We will first show that
\mathcal{A}

\mathrm{PO}
for the instance

. Then we will conclude the proof by showing that

for
\mathcal{I}
.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\sum_{i=1}^{n}v^{\prime}_{i}(B_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}v^{\prime}_{i}(A_{i})

\displaystyle=v^{\prime}_{i}(m+1)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}v^{\prime}_{i}(B_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})-v^{\prime}_{i}(m+1)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}v^{\prime}_{i}(A_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(B_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(A_{i}\setminus\{m+1\}).

(4)

However, if

\omega

\omega\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(B_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(A_{i}\setminus\{m+1\})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v^{\prime}_{i}(B_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}v^{\prime}_{i}(A_{i}),

4
. To arrive at a contradiction, we will now use the fact that
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(w,\eta))

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(w_{i}+\eta)\overline{v}_{i}(B_{i})

(

\displaystyle\implies\eta\omega

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon m(1+\eta)

\displaystyle\implies\frac{\eta\omega}{2m}

). Hence, the allocation

is
\mathrm{PO}
for the instance

4.3
Applying KKM Fixed Point Theorem

To prove
Theorem

5
, we will make use of the well-known Knaster-Kuratowski-Mazurkiewicz theorem, which is formally stated below.

Theorem 6
.

[
3
]

Let
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{n}\subseteq\Delta_{n-1}
be subsets of the
n-1
-dimensional simplex,
\Delta_{n-1}
. If the following two conditions are satisfied,

1.

(Closedness) the subsets
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{n}
are closed,

2.

(KKM covering) for any
w\in\Delta_{n-1}
, there exists an
i\in\mathrm{Supp}(w)
, such that
w\in C_{i}
,

then the intersection of these subsets is nonempty, i.e.,
\cap_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}\neq\emptyset
.

We will use
Theorem

6
to identify the
w^{*}\in\Delta_{n-1}
stated in
Theorem

5
. Towards this, we define subsets
C_{i}\subseteq\Delta_{n-1}
, for each
i\in[n]
as follows,

C_{i}\coloneqq\{w\in\Delta_{n-1}\mid i\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}{p(A_{j})}\text{ where }\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(w,\eta)),p=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(w,\eta))\}.

(6)

In the following two lemmas, we will show that the sets
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
satisfy the conditions of
Theorem

6
.

Lemma 7

(Closedness)
.

The set
C_{i}\subseteq\Delta_{n-1}
is closed for each
i\in[n]
.

Proof.

Consider an infinite sequence of points
x^{1},x^{2},x^{3},\ldots\in\Delta_{n-1}
that converges to the point
x^{*}\in\Delta_{n-1}
. To show that
C_{i}
is closed for any
i\in[n]
, we will show that for any such converging sequence of points if each point in the sequence is in
C_{i}
, then the limit of the sequence is also in
C_{i}
. That is, if
x^{1},x^{2},\ldots\in C_{i}
, then
x^{*}\in C_{i}
as well.

Since the total number of allocations are finite, there must be an infinite subsequence
\hat{x}^{1},\hat{x}^{2},\ldots
of the original sequence and an allocation
\hat{\mathcal{A}}=(\hat{A}_{1},\hat{A}_{2},\ldots,\hat{A}_{n})
, such that for each
\hat{x}^{i}\in C_{i}
, we have
i\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}{\hat{p}^{i}(\hat{A}_{j})}
where
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(\hat{x}^{i},\eta))
and
\hat{p}^{i}=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(\hat{x}^{i},\eta))
. Since
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(\hat{x}^{i},\eta))
for each point
\hat{x}^{i}
and
\textbf{{LP}}(\hat{x}^{i},\eta)
has linear, and hence continuous, objective and constraints, by a limiting argument we have
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(x^{*},\eta))
. Define
p^{*}\coloneqq\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(x^{*},\eta))
. For each
\hat{x}^{i}\in C_{i}
, we also have
i\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}{\hat{p}^{i}(\hat{A}_{j})}
where
\hat{p}^{i}=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(\hat{x}^{i},\eta))
. Since the objective and constraints of
\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(\hat{x}^{i},\eta)
are continuous (linear) and the maximum function is also continuous, we have
i\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}{p^{*}(\hat{A}_{j})}
as well.

Hence, we have
i\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}{p^{*}(\hat{A}_{j})}
where
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(x^{*},\eta))
and
p^{*}=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(x^{*},\eta))
. This implies that
x^{*}\in C_{i}
, proving that
C_{i}
is closed.
∎

Lemma 8

(KKM Covering)
.

For any
w\in\Delta_{n-1}
, there exists an
i\in\mathrm{Supp}(w)
, such that
w\in C_{i}
.

Proof.

Consider an
w\in\Delta_{n-1}
. We consider two cases based on whether
\mathrm{Supp}(w)=[n]
or
\mathrm{Supp}(w)\subsetneq[n]
.

Case 1:
\mathrm{Supp}(w)=[n]
.

Consider any allocation
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(w,\eta))
and
p=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(w,\eta))
. Let
i^{\prime}\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}p(A_{j})
. Since
\mathrm{Supp}(w)=[n]
, we have
i^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Supp}(w)
. Additionally, we have
w\in C_{i^{\prime}}
since
i^{\prime}\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}p(A_{j})
where
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(w,\eta))
and
p=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(w,\eta))
.

Case 2:
\mathrm{Supp}(w)\subsetneq[n]
.

In this case we have
[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)\neq\emptyset
. Define
L=\{i\in[n]\mid w_{i}\geq 1/n\}
;
L\neq\emptyset
by pigeonhole principle. Hence the set
[n]
can be partitioned into three disjoint sets
[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
,
L
, and
\mathrm{Supp}(w)\setminus L
. Let prices
p=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(w,\eta))
and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(w,\eta))
be any allocation. Let
\ell\in\argmax_{i\in L}w_{i}
be an agent having a highest-valued component in
w
; note that
\ell\in L\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(w)
.

We will show that
\max\limits_{i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)}p(A_{i})\leq p(A_{\ell})
. This will conclude the proof, as we will get

\max\limits_{i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)}p(A_{i})\leq p(A_{\ell})\leq\max\limits_{i\in\mathrm{Supp}(w)}p(A_{i}),

where the final inequality follows from
\ell\in\mathrm{Supp}(w)
. This will imply the existence of an
i^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Supp}(w)
such that
i^{\prime}\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}p(A_{j})
, i.e.,
w\in C_{i^{\prime}}
, completing the proof.

Hence, in the subsequent proof, we will show that
\max\limits_{i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)}p(A_{i})\leq p(A_{\ell})
. Towards this, we will prove the following inequalities,

(i)

\max\limits_{i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)}p(A_{i})\leq m\eta\cdot\max\limits_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|

(ii)

\lambda/2n\leq p(A_{\ell})

Together
(i)
and
(ii)
imply the desired inequality,
\max\limits_{i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)}p(A_{i})\leq m\eta\cdot\max\limits_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|=\lambda/2n\leq p(A_{\ell})
; here the middle equality follows from our choice of
\eta=\lambda/(2mn\max_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|)
, see
Equation

2
.

Proving
(i)
.
Note that in the instance
\overline{\mathcal{I}}=\langle[n],[m+1],\{\overline{v_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}\rangle
the set of items
[m]
can be partitioned into three disjoint sets
G
,
C
, and
G_{0}
(
Lemma

2
).

We will first show that items in the set
G
are never allocated to an agent
i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
. For this we consider an agent
i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
, and an agent
k\in L
; both
L
and
[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
are nonempty. For any item
t\in G
, we will show that
\eta\overline{v}_{i}(t)<(w_{k}+\eta)\overline{v}_{k}(t)
. This will imply that item
t
will not be allocated to agent
i
.

\displaystyle\eta\overline{v}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle<(w_{k}+\eta)\overline{v}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta\overline{v}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle<w_{k}\overline{v}_{k}(t)

(
\overline{v}_{k}(t),\eta>0
)

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta\overline{v}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle<\overline{v}_{k}(t)/n

(
w_{k}\geq 1/n
as
k\in L
)

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta\cdot\max_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|

\displaystyle<\overline{v}_{k}(t)/n

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta\cdot\max_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|

\displaystyle<\lambda/2n

(
\lambda/2\leq\overline{v}_{k}(t)
)

\displaystyle\impliedby m\eta\cdot\max_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|

\displaystyle=\lambda/2n.

(definition of
\eta
and
m\geq 2
)

Hence, no item in
G
is allocated to agents in
[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
. Since
p_{j}>0
only for
j\in G\cup G_{0}
(
Proposition

1
), the only way an agent
i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
can get a bundle with a positive price is when
i
gets items from the set
G_{0}
. Therefore we get that for
i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
,
p(A_{i})\leq\sum_{t\in G_{0}\cap A_{i}}p_{t}
. Consider an item
t\in G_{0}\cap A_{i}
. Since
t
is allocated to agent
i
in allocation
\mathcal{A}
which is optimal for
\textbf{{LP}}(w,\eta)
, we have
p_{t}=(w_{i}+\eta)\overline{v}_{i}(j)=\eta\overline{v}_{i}(j)\leq\eta\max_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|
, the second equality follows from
i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
. Therefore, we get that, for any
i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
,
p(A_{i})\leq\sum_{t\in G_{0}\cap A_{i}}p_{t}\leq m\eta\max_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|
. This proves
(i)
.

Proving
(ii)
.
By construction of
\overline{\mathcal{I}}=\langle[n],[m+1],\{\overline{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\rangle
, we have
\overline{v}_{i}(m+1)=\lambda/2
for all
i\in[n]
. Hence, in allocation
\mathcal{A}
, item
m+1
will be allocated to an agent
\ell\in\argmax_{j\in L}w_{j}
. The price of item
m+1
will therefore satisfy
p_{m+1}=\lambda/2\cdot\max_{i\in L}w_{i}\geq\lambda/2n
. We will now show that agent
\ell
will not get any chore from
C
. Since only chores have negative prices (
Proposition

1
), this would imply that
p(A_{\ell})\geq p_{m+1}\geq\lambda/2n
, thereby proving
(ii)
. For any chore
c\in C
and agent
i\in[n]\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(w)
, we will prove the inequality
\eta\overline{v}_{i}(c)>(w_{\ell}+\eta)\overline{v}_{\ell}(c)
; this will imply that
c
is not allocated to
\ell
.

\displaystyle\eta\overline{v}_{i}(c)

\displaystyle>(w_{\ell}+\eta)\overline{v}_{\ell}(c)

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta\overline{v}_{i}(c)

\displaystyle>w_{\ell}\overline{v}_{\ell}(c)

(
\overline{v}_{\ell}(c)<0,\eta>0
)

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta\overline{v}_{i}(c)

\displaystyle>\overline{v}_{\ell}(c)/n

(
w_{\ell}\geq 1/n
,
\overline{v}_{\ell}(c)<0
)

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta(-\overline{v}_{i}(c))

\displaystyle<(-\overline{v}_{\ell}(c))/n

\displaystyle\impliedby\eta\cdot\max_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|

\displaystyle<\lambda/2n

(
\lambda/2\leq-\overline{v}_{\ell}(c)
)

\displaystyle\impliedby m\eta\cdot\max_{i,j}|\overline{v}_{i}(j)|

\displaystyle=\lambda/2n.

(definition of
\eta
and
m\geq 2
)

∎

Now we apply the KKM lemma to show that the existence of a special
w^{*}\in\Delta_{n-1}
such that for each agent
i\in[n]
, there is an allocation
\mathcal{A}^{(i)}
that is optimal for
\textbf{{LP}}(w^{*},\eta)
wherein agent

gets a highest-priced bundle. This result is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

There exists a point
w^{*}\in\Delta_{n-1}
such that for each
i\in[n]
, there is an allocation
\mathcal{A}^{(i)}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(w^{*},\eta))
such that
i\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}p(A^{(i)}_{j})
where
p=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(w^{*},\eta))
.

Proof.

Consider the sets
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{n}\subseteq\Delta_{n-1}
as defined in
Equation

6
. From
Lemma

7
, we know that
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{n}
are closed sets. Additionally, from
Lemma

8
, we know that these sets satisfy the KKM covering conditions, i.e., for any

, there exists an
i\in\mathrm{Supp}(w)
, such that
w\in C_{i}
. Hence, invoking
Theorem

6
, gives us the existence of a point
w^{*}\in\cap_{j=1}^{n}C_{j}
. Define
p=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(w^{*},\eta))
. For each
i\in[n]
, we have
w^{*}\in C_{i}
, i.e., there exists an allocation
\mathcal{A}^{(i)}\in\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{LP}}(w^{*},\eta))
such that
i\in\argmax_{j\in[n]}{p(A^{(i)}_{j})}
. This completes the proof.
∎

5
Part II: The Augmenting Forest Procedure

In this section, we will prove
Theorem

5
. Consider the vector
w^{*}\in\Delta_{n-1}
whose existence is implied by
Theorem

9
. Henceforth, we will use
p=\mathrm{OPT}(\textbf{{Dual-LP}}(w^{*},\eta))
to denote the unique prices corresponding to
w^{*}
. Define bipartite graph
G=([n],[\overline{m}],E)
, where
(i,j)\in E\subseteq[n]\times[\overline{m}]
if and only if
p_{j}=(w_{i}+\eta)\overline{v}_{i}(j)
. For any
t\in[n]\cup[\overline{m}]
, define
d_{G}(t)
to be equal to the degree of vertex
t
in the bipartite graph
G
.

Define partial allocation
L=(L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{n})
where for each
i\in[n]
, the bundle
L_{i}\coloneqq\{j\in[\overline{m}]\mid d_{G}(j)=1\text{ and }(i,j)\in E_{G}\}
. Denote by
U\coloneqq[\overline{m}]\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}
to be all the items in
G
having degree strictly greater than
1
. Finally, we define a subgraph
H=([n],U,E)
of
G
where we have
(i,j)\in E\subseteq[n]\times U
if and only if

Quantum field theory in flat spacetime with multiple time directions
Bin Chen 1,2,3, Zezhou Hu 2, Xin-Cheng Mao 2

Abstract
This letter serves as the generalization of the work Chen et al. 2025 , where we investigated the quantum field theory in Klein space which has two time directions. We extend studies to the general spacetime ℝ n , d − n ( n , d − n ≥ 2 ) \mathbb{R}^{n,d-n}\,(n,d-n\geq 2) in a similar manner: the “length of time” q q is regarded as the evolution direction of the system; additional modes beyond the plane wave modes are introduced in the canonical quantization. We show that this novel formulation is consistent with the analytical continuation of the results in Minkowski spacetime.

Introduction

In textbooks, a
d
-dimensional relativistic quantum field theory (QFT) is formulated in Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{1,d-1}

\mathcal{A}_{m}(p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{m})

(1)

II
The space
\mathbb{R}^{n,d-n}\,(n,d-n\geq 2)

The space
\mathbb{R}^{n,d-n}\,(n,d-n\geq 2)
has a metric of the form

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\text{d}x^{i})^{2}+\sum_{j=n}^{d-1}(\text{d}x^{j})^{2}

(2)

\displaystyle=-\text{d}q^{2}-q^{2}\text{d}\vec{\psi}^{2}+\text{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\text{d}\vec{\varphi}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{-2}(-\text{d}Q^{2}-\sin^{2}Q\text{d}\vec{\psi}^{2}+\text{d}R^{2}+\sin^{2}R\text{d}\vec{\varphi}^{2}),

with
\vec{\psi},\,\vec{\varphi}
being
(n-1)
-dimensional timelike and
(d-n-1)
-dimensional spacelike spherical coordinates.
Its Penrose diagram, shown in Fig.
1
, with conformal factor

\Omega=2\left|\cos\left(\frac{R+Q}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{R-Q}{2}\right)\right|\,,

(3)

can be obtained by

\begin{split}Q&=\arctan(q+r)+\arctan(q-r)\,,\\
R&=\arctan(q+r)-\arctan(q-r)\,.\end{split}

(4)

In the Penrose diagram, the black dashed line and the yellow wavy line (45°) denote the worldlines of massive and massless particles, respectively. Unlike the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{1,d-1}
, which has two null boundaries, the space
\mathbb{R}^{n,d-n}\,(n,d-n\geq 2)
possesses only a single null infinity. This structural difference necessitates replacing the conventional S-matrix with an S-vector to describe scattering processes, following the discussion in
Witten 2001
.

Figure 1:
The Penrose diagram of flat space
\mathbb{R}^{n,d-n}\,(n,d-n\geq 2)
. The infinite coordinate ranges
q,r\in[0,\infty)
are conformally mapped to finite ranges
Q,R\in[0,\pi)
. The conformal boundary emerges at the singularity of
\Omega^{-1}
, with null infinity
\mathscr{I}
located at
Q+R=\pi
. The endpoints
(Q,R)=(0,\pi)
and
(Q,R)=(\pi,0)
correspond to the spacelike infinity
i_{0}
and timelike infinity
i^{\prime}
, respectively.

Then in our construction, the fields evolve over the coordinate
q
, the “length” of the time, from the original point to the conformal boundary.
The associated coordinate system is equipped with a metric

\text{d}s^{2}=-\text{d}q^{2}+\text{d}s^{2}_{\Sigma_{q}}\,,

(5)

where the induced metric of
q=
constant hypersurface
\Sigma_{q}
is given by

\text{d}s^{2}_{\Sigma_{q}}=-q^{2}\text{d}\vec{\psi}^{2}+\text{d}\vec{x}^{2},\quad\vec{x}=(x^{n+1},x^{n+2},\cdots,x^{d}).

(6)

The momentum space inherits the same geometric structure as its dual space
\mathbb{R}^{n,d-n}
. Consequently, we can parameterize the
d
-momentum
p^{\mu}=(\vec{p}^{(n)},\vec{p})
using analogous coordinates

\displaystyle\vec{p}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=(\omega\cos\psi_{1},\omega\sin\psi_{1}\cos\psi_{2},\cdots,\omega\sin\psi_{1}\cdots\sin\psi_{n-1}),

\displaystyle\vec{p}

\displaystyle=(p^{n+1},p^{n+2},\cdots,p^{d-n-1})

(7)

with
\omega>0
. Then the
SO(n,d-n)
-invariant momentum measure can be defined in a similar manner to that in Minkowski space

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{d}p\delta(p^{2}+M^{2})

\displaystyle=\int\omega^{n-1}\text{d}\omega\text{d}^{n-1}\vec{\psi}_{p}\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{p}\delta(-\omega^{2}+\omega_{\vec{p}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\text{d}^{n-1}\vec{\psi}_{p}\int\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{p}\,\omega_{\vec{p}}^{n-2}

(8)

where
\omega_{\vec{p}}=\sqrt{|\vec{p}|^{2}+M^{2}}
is the on-shell energy.

III
Scalar field theory

A real scalar field is described by the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi+M^{2}\phi^{2})-V(\phi)\,,

(9)

where
V(\phi)
denotes the small interacting term, which can be ignored at this stage.
By using the coordinates (
5
), the free field can be expanded as

\begin{split}\phi=&\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\{\mu\}}Y^{(n)}_{l,\{\mu\}}(\vec{\psi})\int\frac{\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{d-n}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}}\\
&\times\left[a^{(J)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}j^{(n)}_{l}(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)+a^{(N)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}n^{(n)}_{l}(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)\right].\end{split}

(10)

where
Y^{(n)}_{l,\{\mu\}}
denotes the hyper-spherical harmonic
Wen and Avery 1985
, and the functions
f(x)=j_{l}^{(n)}(u)
and
n_{l}^{(n)}(u)
with
u=\omega_{\vec{p}}q
obeying the equation

u^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(u)+(n-1)uf^{\prime}(u)+[u^{2}-l(l+n-2)]f(u)=0\,,

(11)

are given by

j_{l}^{(n)}(u)=\frac{J_{l+\frac{n-2}{2}}(u)}{(u/2)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\,,\qquad n^{(n)}_{l}(u)=\frac{N_{l+\frac{n-2}{2}}(u)}{(u/2)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\,,

(12)

where
J_{\nu}(u)
and
N_{\nu}(u)
are the first- and second-kind Bessel functions, respectively.
In particular, the
n=2
case reduces to conventional Bessel functions. In the
n=3
case, the solution
f(u)
is the spherical (
S^{2}
) Bessel function. The functions
j_{l}^{(n)},n^{(n)}_{l}
for larger
n
are the generalization of the Bessel function, for simplicity, we call them “
S^{n-1}
Bessel functions”.

The conjugate momentum is defined as

\pi_{q}=i^{n-1}q^{n-1}\sqrt{h^{(S)}_{n-1}}\partial_{q}\phi

(13)

where
h^{(S)}_{n-1}
denotes the determinant of the metric of a unit sphere
S^{n-1}
. At
q=
constant hypersurfaces, the canonical quantization condition is

\begin{split}[\phi(q,\vec{\psi}_{2},\vec{x}_{2}),\pi_{q}(q,\vec{\psi}_{1},\vec{x}_{1})]&=i^{n-1}\delta(\vec{\psi}_{12})\delta(\vec{x}_{12}),\\
[\phi(q,\vec{\psi}_{1},\vec{x}_{1}),\phi(q,\vec{\psi}_{2},\vec{x}_{2})]&=0,\end{split}

(14)

becomes

\left[a^{(J)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}},a^{(N)}_{\vec{p}^{\prime};l^{\prime},\{\mu^{\prime}\}}\right]=-\frac{\pi}{2}(\omega_{\vec{p}}/2)^{n-2}\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}\delta_{\{\mu\},\{\mu^{\prime}\}}\delta(\vec{p}+\vec{p}^{\prime})\,,

(15)

where we used the identity for the
S^{n-1}
Bessel functions

j^{(n)}_{l}(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{(q^{n-1}\partial_{q})}n^{(n)}_{l}(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)=\frac{2}{\pi}(\omega_{\vec{p}}/2)^{2-n}.

(16)

IV
Vacuum states and correlation functions

Since our system evolves from
q=0
to
q=\infty
, we interpret the Feynman correlation functions as
q
-ordered operator vacuum expectations similar to those in Minkowski spacetime,

C^{(n)}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})=\langle\infty|\mathcal{Q}\phi(x_{1})\cdots\phi(x_{m})|0\rangle\,.

(17)

The state at the original point
|0\rangle
should be constrained by the regularity condition

\phi(q,\psi,\vec{x})\left|0\right\rangle\Big|_{q=0}=\text{finite}\,.

(18)

Since the Neumann function modes
n_{l}^{(n)}(u)
is divergent at
q=0
, the regularity condition becomes

a^{(N)}_{n,\vec{p}}\left|0\right\rangle=0\quad(\forall n,\vec{p})\,.

(19)

This is analogous to the definition of asymptotic states in CFT
Di Francesco et al. 1997
.
Since the state
|0\rangle
is annihilated by the Neumann function coefficient, we will call it the Neumann vacuum in contrast with the Hankel vacuum
\langle\infty|
, which will be defined in (
22
) later.

As the vector
\partial_{q}
is not a (conformal) Killing vector, the vacuum state
\langle\infty|
at
q\rightarrow\infty
is not related to the Neumann vacuum
|0\rangle
at all. The field
\phi
at the asymptotic infinity behaves as

\phi=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\{\mu\}}Y^{(n)}_{l,\{\mu\}}(\vec{\psi}^{(n-1)})\int\frac{\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{d-n}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}}\\
\times\left[a^{(H^{(1)})}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}h^{(1),(n)}_{l}(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)+a^{(H^{(2)})}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}h^{(2),(n)}_{l}(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)\right]\\
\underset{q\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\sum_{l,\{\mu\}}Y^{(n)}_{l,\{\mu\}}(\vec{\psi}^{(n-1)})\int\frac{\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{d-n}}\frac{e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}}}{\sqrt{\pi(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)^{n-1}}}\\
\times\Big[a^{(H^{(1)})}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}e^{i\left(\omega_{\vec{p}}q-\frac{\pi}{2}(l-1+\frac{n}{2})-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}+a^{(H^{(2)})}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}e^{-i\left(\omega_{\vec{p}}q-\frac{\pi}{2}(l-1+\frac{n}{2})-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}\Big]

where

a^{(H^{(1)})}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}=\frac{a^{(J)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}-ia^{(N)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}}{2}\,,\quad a^{(H^{(2)})}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}=\frac{a^{(J)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}+ia^{(N)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}}{2}\,\,,

(20)

and the Hankel function modes are

h_{l}^{(1),(n)}(u)=j_{l}^{(n)}(u)+in_{l}^{(n)}(u),\,h_{l}^{(2),(n)}(u)=j_{l}^{(n)}(u)-in_{l}^{(n)}(u)\,.

(21)

The modes
e^{i\omega_{\vec{p}}q}
and
e^{-i\omega_{\vec{p}}q}
are similar to
e^{i\omega t}
and
e^{-i\omega t}
in the Minkowski case. Thus, the vacuum state
\langle\infty|
should satisfy

\left\langle\infty\right|a^{(H^{(1)})}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}=0

(22)

And we will call it the Hankel vacuum since the state
\langle\infty|
is annihilated by the Hankel function coefficient acting from the right side.

Now we can calculate the Feynman correlation functions via the Wick contraction, based on the commutation relations (
15
), the annihilation conditions of Neumann vacuum (
19
) and the Hankel vacuum (
22
). Let us consider the two-point propagator
\Delta(x-x^{\prime})=\langle\infty|\mathcal{Q}\phi(q,\psi,\vec{x})\phi(q^{\prime},\psi^{\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime})|0\rangle
. Set
q>q^{\prime}
without loss of generality, it is

\begin{split}&\left\langle\infty\right|\phi(q,\vec{\psi},\vec{x})\phi(q^{\prime},\vec{\psi}^{\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime})\left|0\right\rangle/\left\langle\infty\right|0\rangle\\
=&\frac{i\pi^{1-\frac{n}{2}}}{8}\int\frac{\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{d-n}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})}(\omega_{\vec{p}}/2)^{n-2}h^{(2),(n)}_{0}(\omega_{\vec{p}}\Delta q)\,,\end{split}

(23)

with

\Delta q=\sqrt{q^{2}+q^{\prime 2}-2qq^{\prime}(\vec{\psi}\cdot\vec{\psi}^{\prime})}

(24)

The two-point Feynman correlation function from analytical continuation is

\frac{\left\langle\infty\right|\phi(q,\vec{\psi},\vec{x})\phi(q^{\prime},\vec{\psi}^{\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime})\left|0\right\rangle}{\left\langle\infty\right|0\rangle}\propto\int\frac{\text{d}^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{e^{ip\cdot(x-x^{\prime})}}{p^{2}+M^{2}-i\epsilon}\\
=\frac{(n-2)!!A(S^{n-1})}{\mathcal{N}_{n}}\int\frac{\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{d}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})}\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{\vec{p}},

(25)

where the coefficient
\mathcal{N}_{n}=2^{\frac{n}{2}}
or
2^{\frac{n+1}{2}}/\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})
when
n
is even or odd, respectively.
The integral
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{\vec{p}}
is calculated as

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{\vec{p}}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\omega\omega^{n-1}\frac{j^{(n)}_{0}(\omega\Delta q)}{-\omega^{2}+\omega_{\vec{p}}^{2}-i\epsilon}\\
&=\frac{\pi i}{2}\omega_{\vec{p}}^{n-2}h^{(2),(n)}_{0}(\omega_{\vec{p}}\Delta q),\end{split}

(26)

which means the result from our construction (
23
) is consistent with that from analytical continuation (
25
).

The details of computing the correlation functions have been presented in appendix
B
.

V
Conclusions and discussions

In this letter, we introduced how to canonical quantize a field theory in a generic flat spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{n,d-n}(n,d-n\geq 2)
with multiple time directions. We selected the “length of time”
q
as the evolution parameter such that the field can be expanded in terms of the Bessel functions and the Neumann functions. Though the Neumann functions are divergent at the original point, they are necessary in canonical quantization. The calculations in this letter resemble the previous discussions in Klein space for
n=2

Chen et al. 2025
, which also has only one asymptotic boundary. We imposed the regularity condition to constrain the Neumann vacuum state
|0\rangle
. On the other hand, we also define the Hankel vacuum state
\langle\infty|
which is associated with the asymptotic region
q\rightarrow\infty
, where the mode expansions behave as
e^{\pm i\omega_{\vec{p}}q}
. By using these constructions, we explicitly calculated the free two-point function (
23
), which is consistent with the covariant one (
25
). The agreement between the results from our intrinsic construction and those from analytic continuation justifies the correctness of our formalism.

The novel modes associated with the Neumann functions need further study. They may play an essential role in flat holography. One unanswered question in flat holography concerns the extrapolation dictionary between the operators in boundary field theory and the bulk fields. It would be interesting to investigate the role of these novel modes in the bulk reconstruction in flat holography.

As discussed in
Chen et al. 2025
, the LSZ reduction formula, Path-integral quantization, and perturbative calculation of interacting theory are all realizable in the space
\mathbb{R}^{n,d-n}
. Due to the limitation of this manuscript, we only demonstrate the LSZ reduction formula in the appendix
A
. We also give some details on the calculations of the correlation functions in the appendix
B
.

Appendix A
The interacting real scalar field and LSZ reduction formula

For the interacting real scalar, the potential
V(\phi)
in the Lagrangian is non-vanishing, such that the equation of motion should be modified to include potential terms. Here we adopt the Heisenberg picture in which the mode expansion can be formally performed in a similar form to that of free field theory

\phi=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\{\mu\}}Y^{(n)}_{l,\{\mu\}}(\vec{\psi})\int\frac{\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{d-n}}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}}\\
\times\left[a^{(J)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}(q)j^{(n)}_{l}(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)+a^{(N)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}(q)n^{(n)}_{l}(\omega_{\vec{p}}q)\right]

(27)

But now the coefficients
a^{(J)}(q),a^{(N)}(q)
are no longer independent of the coordinate
q
.

At the quantum level,
\phi(q\rightarrow 0)|0\rangle
still needs to be finite, leading to

a^{(N)}_{\vec{\psi}_{p},\vec{p}}(q=0)\left|0\right\rangle=0\qquad\forall\vec{\psi}_{p},\vec{p}\,.

(28)

with

a^{(N)}_{\vec{\psi}_{p},\vec{p}}(q)=\frac{\omega_{\vec{p}}^{2-n}\mathcal{N}_{n}}{2^{-n/2}\pi}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\{\mu\}}i^{l}Y^{(n)}_{l,\{\mu\}}(\vec{\psi}_{p})a^{(N)}_{\vec{p};l,\{\mu\}}(q).

(29)

Here, the coefficient
\mathcal{N}_{n}
is the same as that in eq (
25
).

In general,
a^{(N)}_{\vec{\psi}_{p},\vec{p}}(q=\infty)\left|0\right\rangle\neq 0
as
a^{(N)}_{\vec{\psi}_{p},\vec{p}}(\infty)
differs from
a_{\vec{\psi}_{p},\vec{p}}^{(N)}(0)
in a non-trivial way. Their difference can be computed as

a^{(N)}_{\vec{\psi}_{p},\vec{p}}(\infty)-a^{(N)}_{\vec{\psi}_{p},\vec{p}}(0)=\langle e^{-ipx},\phi(x)\rangle\Big|_{q=0}^{\infty}\\
=\int\text{d}^{d}xe^{-ip\cdot x}(-\partial^{2}+M^{2})\phi(x)\,.

(30)

Here, we used the expansion of
n
-dimensional plane wave

Wen and Avery 1985

e^{i\vec{p}^{(n)}\cdot\vec{x}^{(n)}}=2^{-\frac{n}{2}-1}\mathcal{N}_{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}i^{l}(n+2l-2)\\
\times j_{l}^{(n)}(|\vec{p}^{(n)}||\vec{x}^{(n)}|)C_{l}^{(\frac{n-2}{2})}\left(\frac{\vec{p}^{(n)}\cdot\vec{x}^{(n)}}{|\vec{p}^{(n)}||\vec{x}^{(n)}|}\right)

(31)

where
C_{l}^{(\frac{n-2}{2})}
is the Gegenbauer polynomial. Specifically,
C_{l}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\vec{\psi}\cdot\vec{\psi}^{\prime})
(namely
n=3
) is the Legendre polynomial
P_{l}(\vec{\psi}\cdot\vec{\psi}^{\prime})
. In the derivation of (
30
), we also used the expansion formula for the Gegenbauer polynomial
Avery 2002

C_{l}^{(\frac{n-2}{2})}(\vec{\psi}\cdot\vec{\psi}^{\prime})=\frac{(n-2)A(S^{n-1})}{2l+n-2}\sum_{\{\mu\}}Y^{(n)*}_{l,\{\mu\}}(\vec{\psi})Y^{(n)}_{l,\{\mu\}}(\vec{\psi}^{\prime})\,

(32)

with
A(S^{n-1})=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}
being the area of
S^{n-1}
, the definition of the Klein-Gordon inner product

\langle\phi^{1},\phi^{2}\rangle=\int_{\Sigma_{q}}\text{d}^{n-1}\vec{\psi}\text{d}^{d-n}\vec{x}\phi^{1}\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{(q^{n-1}\partial_{q})}\phi^{2},

(33)

and the orthonormal condition for the hyper spherical harmonics
Avery 2002

\int\text{d}^{n-1}\vec{\psi}Y^{(n)*}_{l,\{\mu\}}(\vec{\psi})Y^{(n)}_{l^{\prime},\{\mu^{\prime}\}}(\vec{\psi})=\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}\delta_{\{\mu\},\{\mu^{\prime}\}}.

(34)

The scattering amplitude is defined by the inner product between the scattering state living on the conformal boundary and the S-vector
|S\rangle\equiv|0\rangle
(originally mentioned in section

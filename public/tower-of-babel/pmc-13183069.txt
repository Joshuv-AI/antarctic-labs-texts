From phage display to tumor microcirculation regulation: A bibliometric review of phage in cancer immunotherapy research (2000–2024)
Authors: Hefei Wang, Jiantao Yin, Hongna Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to conduct a bibliometric analysis of the literature related to phages in cancer immunotherapy to reveal the research hotspots and evolutionary trends in this field. We extracted all relevant publications from the Web of Science Core Collection database for the period from 2000 to 2024. CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and R software were used to evaluate and visualize core authors, journals, countries, literature, trending topics, and research hotspots in this field. Among 485 publications, the number of annual publications showed a general upward trend. North America and East Asia were the main contributing regions, with the United States and China being the most productive countries. The University of Pennsylvania became one of the most influential research institutions, and the Journal of Immunology was the most prolific and highly cited. Gao Yanfeng is the most published scholar in the field, and Nicole F is the most cited book. Keyword co-occurrence and clustering analysis revealed that the research hotspots encompassed 5 areas: phage display, cancer immunotherapy, immune cell function regulation, target identification and protein action, and biomarker mining. This study employs bibliometric analysis methods to systematically review research progress in the field of phage and cancer immunotherapy from 2000 to 2024, identify key research directions in this field, and predict future research trends. The aim is to provide valuable directions and recommendations for subsequent research in the field of phage and cancer immunotherapy.

1. Introduction

Despite the advancement of clinical medicine, cancer remains a major scientific challenge with surging global incidence and mortality.[1] Conventional treatments (surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy) are still mainstream, yet their limitations and severe side effects drive the demand for targeted innovative therapies.[2] Cancer immunotherapy, which remodels the host immune system to eliminate tumor cells, has achieved remarkable breakthroughs.[3,4] Viral vectors have attracted wide attention in this field for their unique targeting and transfection ability,[5] and phage-based therapeutics have emerged as a research hotspot for their potential anticancer value.[6]

Phages are bacteria-specific viruses that cannot infect eukaryotic cells, making them nonpathogenic to humans.[7,8] Even artificially introduced into mammalian cells, phages cannot proliferate or induce lysis.[9] Their core application in cancer immunotherapy is phage display technology: tumor antigens displayed on phages are processed by antigen-presenting cells, which activate CD4+ T cells (driving antibody production) and CD8+ cytotoxic T cells via major histocompatibility complex molecules. Activated immune cells clear tumor cells through multiple pathways, and this property also makes phages valuable for antibody discovery and targeted ligand development in cancer therapy.

Advances in modern technologies have deepened the understanding of phage biology and improved the safety of phage therapy.[10] Phages regulate host immunity through multiple mechanisms: modulating phagocyte function and oxidative burst,[11] inhibiting T-cell activation,[12] reducing immune rejection in allogeneic transplantation,[13,14] and regulating the secretion of interleukin-2, tumor necrosis factor, and interferon-γ,[15] while also enhancing natural immune defense.[16] These immunoregulatory properties lay a foundation for phage application in tumor immune microenvironment modulation.

In recent years, preclinical studies have fully verified the potential of phages as multifunctional platforms for cancer immunotherapy. Shadaei et al[17] found that T7 phage displaying heat shock protein 27 induced a potent antitumor immune response in breast cancer models. Pouyandeh et al[18] constructed T7 phage nanoparticles displaying human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 [HER2]/neu epitopes, which inhibited the growth of HER2-positive tumor cells via antigen cross-presentation. Janitzek’s team[19] used AP205 phage virus-like particles to display human papillomavirus epitopes, achieving synergistic protection against cervical cancer. Wang et al[20] engineered M13 phage to deliver granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor, enhancing antigen presentation and activating the signal transducer and activator of transcription 5 pathway. Phages can also regulate tumor angiogenesis and microcirculation by interacting with immune cells and tumor microenvironment (TME) components,[21,22] marking a shift from “antigen display” to “microenvironment regulation” in phage-based cancer immunotherapy.

Bibliometric analysis is a statistical method to summarize research status, identify hotspots, and predict trends,[23] which is often combined with visualization to present research contributions and correlations.[24] Although phage research in cancer immunotherapy has grown rapidly in the past 2 decades, a systematic quantitative assessment of this field is still lacking. To fill this gap, this study conducted a bibliometric analysis of relevant literature published from 2000 to 2024, aiming to depict the knowledge structure and evolutionary trends of phage application in cancer immunotherapy and provide references for subsequent research.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data source and search strategies

The data for this study comes from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), the world’s leading citation indexing platform for the natural sciences, social sciences, arts and humanities, and an authoritative publisher-independent global citation database.[25,26] To improve the representativeness and accessibility of the data, the WoSCC database was used. The search was set from January 1, 2000, to December 31, 2024, to include records of articles published or indexed within that period. The search results contained records of 2 articles with a literature entry publication year of February 2025, but were included in this search because they had been indexed in 2024 in the WoS database. The specific search strategy was as follows: TS = (“Bacteriophages” OR “Bacteriophage” OR “Phages” OR “Phage”) AND TS = (“Neoplasms” OR “Tumors” OR “Neoplasia” OR “Neoplasias” OR “Neoplasm” OR “Tumor” OR “Cancer” OR “Cancers” OR “Malignant Neoplasm” OR “Malignancy” OR “Malignancies” OR “Malignant Neoplasms” OR “Neoplasm, Malignant” OR “Neoplasms, Malignant” OR “Benign Neoplasms” OR “Neoplasms, Benign” OR “Neoplasm, Benign” OR “Benign Neoplas”) AND TS = (“Immunotherapy” OR “Immunotherapies”). A total of 503 documents were retrieved, and we analyzed only English reviews and articles, leaving a total of 490 documents. All papers retrieved from WoSCC were downloaded in the “Full Record with Cited References” format and saved as “Plain Text.” After the de-duplication operation, 485 documents were analyzed in this study. The inclusion and screening processes are illustrated in Figure 1 (see also Data S1, Supplemental Digital Content). To avoid bias due to database updates, the data were obtained from the WoSCC database on May 28, 2025.

Flowchart of the data screening process.

2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were original peer-reviewed research papers or review articles exploring phages in the field of cancer immunotherapy. Exclusion criteria included as follows: unpublished articles, conference abstracts or errata papers, repetitive publications, and irrelevant research papers.

2.3. Data extraction

All records were downloaded from the WoSCC database and contained data such as journals, publication dates, article titles, authors, institutions, countries or regions of origin, abstracts, keywords, publication sources, H-index, and number of citations. All documents were then imported into Microsoft Office Excel 2019, CiteSpace (version 6.4R1), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and the Bibliometrix R package (version 4.4.3) for bibliometric analysis and data visualization. We used the latest version of the Journal Citation Reports (JCRs) to obtain impact factors (IFs) and journal partitions (Q1–Q4) to assess the field performance of the journals. To ensure the authenticity of the data, 2 researchers (Hefei Wang and Jiantao Yin) independently screened and extracted the key information of the final included literature and then analyzed the data together, and any disagreement during the full-text review process was resolved through negotiation.

2.4. Bibliometric and visualized analysis

In order to systematically analyze the literature development trend and research hotspots of phages in the field of cancer immunotherapy, this study comprehensively utilized a variety of bibliometric software tools for quantitative analysis. First, Microsoft Office Excel was used to analyze annual publication trend statistics and citation status. The online bibliometric analysis platform (https://bibliometric.com/) was used to visualize the annual publication volume of countries/regions. CiteSpace[27] was used as the citation analysis and visualization software to reveal the knowledge base, structural characteristics, and evolutionary trends (scientific knowledge mapping) in the field. This review utilized CiteSpace for co-citation analysis of co-cited references. The parameters were set as follows: period (January 2000–February 2025), time slice parameter was set to 1 year, and the criterion was g-index: k = 25. The Burst Detection function of CiteSpace was utilized to identify high-impact literature (reference bursts) and research topics (keyword bursts) that were significantly active in a specific period. The minimum time frame for Burst Detection was set at 1 year for co-cited references and 2 years for keywords. Among them, the high value of literature emergence indicates important results that have been centrally cited in a specific period, while the high value of keyword emergence is a sensitive indication of research frontiers and hotspot transitions, which is of irreplaceable strategic significance for grasping the pulse of the discipline, predicting the scientific trends, and optimizing the scientific research decision-making. In addition, using VOSviewer,[28] collaborative network analysis was used to reveal collaboration patterns between countries and regions, teamwork relationships between authors and their institutions, and to analyze core journals and co-cited references, while co-occurrence analysis showed the network association between different keywords.[29,30] The inter-temporal evolution and association strength visualization are also performed by temporal overlay layers and total linkage strength (TLS) quantitative metrics. In this paper, we also perform inter-country collaborative network analysis and author output analysis over time, as well as construct a 3-domain graph for keyword enhancement analysis using the Bibliometrix and Biblioshiny packages in R.[31] Through the synergistic application of the above multidimensional methods, this study aimed to dynamically present the research hotspots, evolutionary paths, and emerging trends of phage in cancer immunotherapy.

2.5. Research ethics

Data were obtained from public databases and did not involve human or animal subjects. Bibliometric analysis did not require approval from the Ethics Committee.

3. Results

3.1. Annual analysis of publications

This study was based on the WoSCC database. The initial search yielded 557 records, and 490 articles (398 research papers and 92 reviews) were screened to meet the inclusion criteria. After the de-duplication process using CiteSpace, 5 duplicates were found, and 485 articles published between 2000 and 2024 were finally included (Fig. 1). The results of the analysis of the yearly trends in the publication of the above literature showed (Fig. 2A) a remarkable evolution of the research dynamics of phages in the field of cancer immunotherapy. Between 2000 and 2017, the annual publication volume maintained a low, fluctuating state without a significant growth inflection point. In contrast, 2018 was a key turning point when the number of annual publications exceeded 20 for the first time, marking the beginning of a significant acceleration of research activities in this field. Since then, the growth rate reached a peak of 53 articles by 2023. The average annual growth rate during the 6 years from 2018 to 2024 is as high as about 21%, and the overall upward trend of the number of articles in this field strongly confirms the general trend of the field’s increased research enthusiasm in recent years.

3.2. Contributions of countries/regions

Figure 2B illustrates the annual trend of the top 10 countries in terms of the number of publications from 2001 to 2024. Developed countries such as the United States and Germany have dominated the field for a long time. However, since 2018, China’s contribution has increased significantly, gradually changing from its original pattern. Since 2019, China has surpassed the United States as the country with the most publications in the field.

A global analysis of research progress in phage applications for cancer immunotherapy reveals that 51 countries and regions have made significant contributions between 2000 and 2024. The United States topped the list in terms of research output with 164 publications, accounting for 33.81% of the global total, followed by China in second place with 131 (27.01%). Germany and Iran followed with 38 (7.84%) and 33 (6.80%) articles, respectively.

In terms of research impact, the H-index is an important indicator for assessing the level of research output of scholars, institutions, and even national regions, which is defined as the publication of h papers by scholars that have been cited at least h times. Studies have shown that the H-index has an advantage over the total number of publications and citations in predicting future research productivity.[32] The United States also excels in this metric, holding the top spot, followed by China and Germany. Further confirming the dominance of the United States in this field, it leads in the total number of citations to its literature, and its average number of citations is more than half of the total, which is 2 to 3 times that of China.

International cooperation is a distinctive feature of the research in this field. The intensity of a country’s engagement in international collaboration can be measured using TLS, a core indicator presented in Table 1. The data show that the United States has the highest TLS value, indicating its central position and the most extensive international collaboration network in this field. China ranks second in TLS value, reflecting its active participation in cross-border research cooperation. These results fully demonstrate that major research powers in the field attach great importance to international collaboration. This is visualized in the Country Collaboration Map (Fig. 3A; see also Data S2, Supplemental Digital Content): the network of collaborative relationships between countries/regions is clearly outlined by the red lines in the figure. The United States, as the largest contributor to the field, not only maintains the strongest collaborative ties with China but also has close cooperation with countries such as Japan and South Korea.

4. Discussion

4.1. Research output and temporal evolution characteristics of the field

Cancer remains a major global health challenge, affecting millions of lives each year.[34] Among these, the TME serves as a key hub driving tumor initiation and progression, representing a highly complex and dynamically evolving ecosystem. Against this backdrop, phage therapy, with its unique precise targeting capabilities, multifunctional properties, and high modifiability (genetic modifiability), has emerged as a promising new therapeutic tool in cancer research, particularly in the field of tumor immunotherapy, demonstrating immense potential and opening new avenues of exploration in this domain.[35] However, despite its promising prospects, the key research hotspots, recent development trends, and future research priorities regarding the combination of phage therapy and cancer immunotherapy have not yet been systematically organized or thoroughly discussed. This study employed bibliometrics as a powerful quantitative analysis tool to address this research gap and provide objective insights.[36] We conducted a comprehensive bibliometric and visualization analysis of the field based on the WoSCC database, aiming to accurately depict the current research landscape and identify key research directions and future trends in the application of phages in tumor immunotherapy. This study screened 485 papers, with a total citation count of 16,380, involving 3346 researchers from 51 countries and regions worldwide. More importantly, by analyzing annual publication volumes (Fig. 2A), we clearly identified 2 stages in the development of this field: from 2000 to 2017, the field was in a period of slow growth; and 2018 marked a significant turning point, signifying breakthrough progress in the application of phages in cancer immunotherapy. Over the subsequent 6 years (2018–2024), research activity has surged rapidly, with a significant increase in the number of publications. This strong growth trend clearly indicates that interdisciplinary research on phages and cancer immunotherapy is attracting increasing attention from the scientific community, and the current positive momentum suggests that this field will inevitably yield more influential research outcomes in the future.

4.2. Regional contribution and international collaboration patterns

In terms of regional distribution, North America and East Asia constitute the regions with the highest concentration of academic output in this field. As shown in Table 1, the United States, China, and Germany rank among the top 3 countries globally in terms of total research output. Among these, the United States demonstrates a comprehensive leading advantage, ranking first in terms of the number of publications, total citation frequency, and H-index, while also occupying a central hub position in international collaboration networks (Fig. 3A). Notably, the United States has the highest TLS in this field, further confirming the exceptional academic influence of its research outcomes and solidifying its dominant position and key contributions in phage and cancer immunotherapy research. The leading position of the United States in phage and cancer immunotherapy is not only attributed to its robust economic foundation but also to its sustained and substantial investments in the healthcare sector. However, the global landscape in this field has undergone significant changes in recent years: with China’s rapid increase in the number of publications, it has surpassed the United States in terms of paper output since 2019, becoming the global leader. The international collaboration landscape (Fig. 3A) clearly illustrates the prominent position of the United States as a research hub, maintaining close collaborative ties not only with China but also with other East Asian countries such as Japan and South Korea. By contrast (as shown in Fig. 3B), the United States is the most active in international MCP. While China stands out in terms of the number of single-country publications (reaching 109), its MCP ratio is only approximately 10.7% (not ranking among the top 5 globally). This low international co-authorship rate indicates that China has significant shortcomings in its collaborative research with other countries. Therefore, to effectively enhance the international influence of research outcomes, China and its research institutions must not only continue to produce results but also urgently focus on improving paper quality and significantly strengthening international cooperation. It is also worth reflecting that the phenomenon of low international co-authorship rates is not unique to China – a significant number of countries in the current landscape share similar tendencies toward relatively independent domestic research. This widespread low MCP ratio suggests the existence of barriers hindering cross-border academic collaboration, with potential factors including relatively limited funding support and insufficient research resources. Therefore, robust national economic support for R&D activities and deepened international collaboration will serve as a dual-engine driving progress in this field. By effectively identifying and addressing the current obstacles to cross-border collaboration (such as resource allocation issues), it is possible to unlock the potential of international collaboration more fully, thereby fostering broader innovative outcomes.

The assessment of a journal’s academic impact usually relies on core metrics such as the IF[37] and the JCR partition[38] (Q partition, which represents a journal’s relative position of influence in its subject area). The Journal for Immunotherapy of Cancer (IF = 10.3) had the highest IF. Given that core journals are often tasked with publishing and disseminating the most cutting-edge and important research results in their fields, journals that excel in terms of publication volume or impact are particularly worthy of prioritization by researchers in the field when choosing targets for their submissions. A visual analysis of the journals through the co-citation network mapping (Fig. 5A) demonstrates the clustering of journal clusters, especially the red cluster, which is particularly notable. This cluster not only shows the strongest thematic relevance but also has the highest co-citation frequency, indicating that it has a pivotal driving role in the knowledge exchange network in this field and is surrounded by a group of highly relevant and deeply cited journals.

The citation frequency of literature is one of the indicators of its academic influence, and the highly cited literature often marks the core exploration topics and research cornerstones of a specific research field. Therefore, identifying this classic literature is of key value in grasping the hotspots of the discipline. Table 5 lists the 15 most frequently cited papers in the field of phage and cancer immunotherapy, showing a remarkable distribution: as many as 10 papers were published before 2000, whereas the other 5 papers were published after 2010. This distribution reflects the rich history of the field on the one hand, and indicates the direction of recent research activity on the other. At the top of the list is the seminal study “Filamentous Fusion Phage: Novel Expression Vectors That Display Cloned Antigens on the Virion Surface” (36 citations) by Smith et al in Science. This article demonstrated for the first time the feasibility of inserting exogenous DNA fragments into filamentous phage gene III to construct fusion proteins, thereby enabling the display of exogenous antigens in an immunorecognizable form on the virion surface. This landmark work not only established the status of filamentous fusion phage as a novel expression vector, but also paved the way for the subsequent exploration of phage applications in cancer immunotherapy at the principle level, laying an indispensable technical foundation for molecular biology and immunology.[39] After 2010, highly cited literature in the field represented substantial breakthroughs in clinical translation. The study “Improved Survival with Ipilimumab in Patients with Metastatic Melanoma” by Hodi et al in New Engl J Med (cited 18 times) is a prime example. This rigorously designed study is the first Phase III randomized controlled trial to conclusively demonstrate that Ipilimumab significantly improves overall survival in previously treated patients with metastatic melanoma, with or without the gp100 peptide vaccine. The availability of this pivotal clinical evidence not only provides a central rationale for the immunotherapy of metastatic melanoma but also marks an important milestone in the application of immune checkpoint inhibitors in the field of cancer therapy, with far-reaching implications for the entire landscape of tumor immunotherapy.[40]

4.5. Research hotspots and evolutionary trends of the field

The results of the burst analysis and timeline view analysis of the co-cited literature by CiteSpace software clearly showed the following (Fig. 7C): Compared with the previously cited literature with high burst intensity (especially before 2000) that has gradually decayed, the articles published after 2015 continued to gain attention, and their citation intensities showed a more persistent and possibly increasing trend over time. The most significant and recent outburst revealed by the literature outburst analysis began in 2019 and continues to this day. The key source driving this explosion was a seminal paper published in 2019, which systematically illustrated for the first time how phage display technology can be used to uncover specific peptide vectors targeting the tumor vasculature, TME, and tumor overexpression receptors using a variety of screening strategies such as in situ, in vivo, and so on.[41] In addition, this study also prospectively explores how these highly specific vectors can be used to deliver anticancer drugs to enhance the effectiveness of targeted tumor therapy, which summarizes the remarkable progress in this field and analyzes the existing challenges, and has a profound impact on the direction of subsequent research in this field. By comprehensively analyzing the co-citation clusters and their timeline evolution (Fig. 7B), we observed a significant shift in the research focus of phage and cancer immunotherapy: the research focus has shifted from early clustering themes, such as “#0 T-cell receptor complex restricted specificity,” and “#1 human cancer,” to topics closer to translational applications and efficacy assessment, such as “#7 potent antitumor activity” and “#8 chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy” (muscle car). This evolutionary trend demonstrates the inevitable path of the field from basic discovery to clinical translation and therapeutic efficacy.

Keyword analysis, as a core method of bibliometrics, effectively reveals the evolution of research hotspots in the academic field.[42] In this study, the keywords of 485 related documents were analyzed by VOSviewer for co-occurrence, and 2620 keywords were identified; the top 369 high-frequency keywords were included in the construction of the atlas after screening according to the minimum frequency of occurrence for 3 times (Fig. 8A, B). The results showed that the current research focus of the field was coalesced into 4 major themes: the largest cluster was focused on “antibody engineering and targeted immunotherapy,” followed by “cancer immunotherapy and therapeutic strategies,” and the third was “immune-checkpoint blockade and targeted pathways,” while “antigen presentation and tumor immunorecognition” constituted the smallest cluster. Further analysis of the temporal dimension (Fig. 8B, C) clearly reveals a shift in research focus over the past 2 decades: early research (pre-2005) focused on basic immune mechanism exploration, followed by a shift in interest to antibody engineering technology (2010–2014), and after 2015, breakthroughs in PD-1/CTLA-4 checkpoint inhibitors and CAR-T cell therapies were introduced to the market. After 2015, breakthroughs represented by PD-1/CTLA-4 checkpoint inhibitors and CAR-T cell therapies have pushed tumor immunotherapy into an unprecedented boom of the “explosion period”; and cutting-edge research after 2020 is showing 3 significant features: pursuit of multi-targeted precision therapies (e.g., bispecific antibodies/antibody-drug conjugate drugs); diversification of mechanistic exploration (e.g., gut microbiota interventions/gut microbiota on immune microenvironment); intelligent technology integration (cross-application of clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats, nanocarriers and other cutting-edge technologies). This evolutionary trajectory from single-point breakthroughs to comprehensive regulation profoundly signifies that the field of tumor immunotherapy is undergoing a profound paradigm shift from single intervention toward systemic integration. Figure 8D reveals that major institutions and countries in the field have contributed to all fifteen core research themes, but with differences in focus, through the visualization and analysis of the 3-domain correlation network of countries, institutions, and keywords. For example, Tabriz University of Medical Sciences, Iran, focuses on “immunotherapy” and “phage display,” while the University of California, USA, focuses more on “cancer,” “Phage Display,” and “Tumor Immunorecognition”; at the national level, the United States and China are particularly prominent in contributing to these hotspots.

Keyword outburst analysis is primarily used to identify keywords that have seen significant changes over a specific period of time, and citation intensity metrics reveal the strength of attraction of keywords that are widely followed and discussed. Figure 9 lists the top 25 keywords analyzed by CiteSpace for citation burst intensity between 2001 and 2021. The top 5 keywords for burst intensity between 2000 and 2024 include “monoclonal antibodies,” “single-chain Fv,” “tumor microenvironment,” “T-cells,” and “immune checkpoint inhibitors.” Among them, the keyword with the highest outbreak intensity is “monoclonal antibodies,” and the keyword with the longest outbreak duration is “libraries.” Analyzing the temporal distribution of the keyword outbreaks in depth, 3 distinctive stages of development can be clearly seen: the first stage is the technology foundation period, which lasted from 2000 to 2006. During this period, the strong outbreaks of “monoclonal antibodies” and “single-chain fv” marked the establishment of the core phage display.[43,44] At this stage, research has focused on the use of phage libraries to screen high-affinity antibody fragments, which significantly advanced the development of tumor-targeting antibodies, typical examples are the breakthroughs in the derivatization of anti-HER2 trastuzumab.[45] The explosive intensity of these 2 keywords also confirms the seminal role of phage display technology in the transformation of cancer immunotherapy. The second phase, a period of deepening immune mechanisms, runs from 2014 to 2022. At this time, the focus of research has shifted to “T-cells” and “immune checkpoint inhibitors.” The former reflects the rise of cell therapies such as CAR-T, whereas the latter is directly related to the clinical translational boom of PD-1/CTLA-4 targets.[46] During this phase, phage technology advanced in 2 ways: on the one hand, it became a tool for new target discovery, for example, it could be used to screen for PD-L1-specific binding peptides; on the other hand, it also facilitated the design of complex molecules, such as the construction of bispecific antibodies targeting the tumor antigen – CD3.[47] This implies a strategic upgrade of phage technology. The third phase is the Frontier Turn, which lasts from 2023. Research focuses on immune microenvironment and combination therapy, with “tumor microenvironment” becoming the emerging hotspot with the greatest intensity, while “drug delivery” and “mechanism” continue to emerge. “Mechanism” also indicates that cutting-edge research is undergoing a 3-pronged shift: first, the analysis of the dynamic regulatory mechanism of TME, second, the construction of precise delivery systems that can break through biological barriers, and third, the optimization of combination therapies based on the mechanism of action. In this context, phage technology has demonstrated the following applications: screening of TME-specific targeting peptides, such as endoglin receptor-binding peptides[48]; development of intelligent delivery vectors, such as barrier-breaking systems loaded with siRNA/chemotherapeutic drugs[49]; and deepening of the synergistic mechanism of combination therapies, such as the sequential drug delivery strategy of immune checkpoint inhibitors.[50]

The distribution of keyword strength accurately demonstrates the gradual development path of the field, focusing on the development of targeted antibodies to the analysis of immune mechanisms and finally focusing on the intervention in the tumor ecosystem. In this process, the continuously enhanced technological dynamism of phage display technology is driving a revolutionary paradigm shift in cancer immunotherapy from a single intervention to multidimensional reconstruction of tumor ecological networks.

5. Limitations

Our study used bibliometric methods to visualize and analyze the research in the field of phage and cancer immunotherapy, which helps scholars better grasp the research hotspots and development trends in this field. However, this study has some limitations: this study only collected articles written in English and Chinese in the WoSCC database, which means that some valuable studies may be missed. Because the WoSCC has a high coverage of the majority of studies, we believe that this omission will not significantly affect the overall trend. Second, owing to the delay in citation impact, some recently published high-quality studies may have underestimated their impact, which needs to be followed up on and updated in future studies. In summary, the field of phage and cancer immunotherapy research is rapidly developing and is expected to become an important research direction. Extensive international collaborations are needed in the next phase to provide new insights and research directions for the continued exploration and progress of phage and cancer immunotherapies.

6. Conclusion

In this study, the research progress of phages in cancer immunotherapy between 2000 and 2024 was systematically analyzed based on bibliometric methods. The results of the analysis showed that global research power has shown a significant and continuous increase in attention to this cross-cutting field. The study shows that the United States, with its strong scientific foundation and history, is still the core knowledge producer and leader of this research, and it is noteworthy that China’s contribution and influence in this frontier field has been rapidly increasing in recent years, and has gradually grown to become a globally important research body. Cancer Immunology and Immunotherapy and the Journal of Immunology have become the most productive journals in this field. Based on the data mapping analysis of this study, it was found that the research trajectory of phage therapy is gradually transitioning from the early focus on basic mechanism exploration to the deeper stage of clinical translation and application. Specifically, phage is now widely regarded as a dual player with unique functions: on the one hand, it is a key biological tool to deeply modulate the tumor immune microenvironment; on the other hand, as an emerging vector, it shows great potential to synergize existing cancer immunotherapy strategies. Therefore, phage-based therapies are expected to open up promising new directions for the development of novel cancer immunotherapy combinations that are more targeted, more efficacious, and have a controlled safety profile. This review aims to provide researchers with a solid theoretical foundation and a clear direction for future development by systematically summarizing the research lineage, development status, and hotspots in this field.

Two-dimensional fractional Brownian motion: Analysis in time and frequency domains
Michał Balcerek, Adrian Pacheco-Pozo, Agnieszka Wyłomańska, Krzysztof Burnecki, Diego Krapf

Abstract
This article introduces a novel construction of the two-dimensional fractional Brownian motion (2D fBm) with dependent components. Unlike similar models discussed in the literature, our approach uniquely accommodates the full range of model parameters and explicitly incorporates cross-dependencies and anisotropic scaling through a matrix-valued Hurst operator. We thoroughly analyze the theoretical properties of the proposed causal and well-balanced 2D fBm versions, deriving their auto- and cross-covariance structures in both time and frequency domains. In particular, we present the power spectral density of these processes and their increments. Our analytical findings are validated with numerical simulations. This work provides a comprehensive framework for modeling anomalous diffusion phenomena in multidimensional systems where component interdependencies are crucial.

In this article, we propose the construction of a two-dimensional fractional Brownian motion (2D fBm) by introducing a dependence on the underlying noise in the time representation of the process. II
Model

In this section, we propose two specific constructions of the 2D fBm, which serve as a mathematical tool for modeling anomalous diffusion processes in multidimensional spaces. Unlike the classical one-dimensional case, which relies on scalar parameters to characterize diffusion behavior, we employ a matrix-valued

First, following Stoev and Taqqu

X_{2}(t)&=\sigma_{2}a_{H_{2}}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{+}\left(s;t,H_{2}-1/2\right)\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{2}(s).\end{cases}

Let

Defintition 2

(Well-balanced 2D fBm)

Let

and

III
Covariance structure of 2D fBm

In this section, we discuss the covariance structure of both causal and well-balanced 2D fBms, along with that of their increments.

III.1
Covariance structure of the process

As mentioned, both causal and well-balanced 2D fBm are Gaussian processes; therefore, their covariance structure provides a complete characterization. In this section, we discuss this characterization in time domain.

Theorem 1
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{jk}(t,s)\equiv\langle Z_{j}(t)Z_{k}(s)\rangle=\frac{\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}}{2}\left(w_{jk}(t)|t|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}+w_{jk}(-s)|s|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}-w_{jk}(t-s)|t-s|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\right)

(12)

for

, where
\sigma_{j}^{2}=\langle Z_{j}^{2}(1)\rangle
and

\displaystyle w_{jk}(u)=\begin{cases}\rho_{jk}-\eta_{jk}\mathrm{sign}(u),&\quad H_{j}+H_{k}\neq 1,\\
\rho_{jk}-\eta_{jk}\mathrm{sign}(u)\log|u|,&\quad H_{j}+H_{k}=1.\end{cases}

(13)

The, so called, cross-correlation parameters
\rho_{12}
and
\rho_{21}
are given by

\displaystyle\rho_{12}

\displaystyle=\rho_{21}=\rho\frac{\sqrt{\Gamma(2H_{1}+1)\Gamma(2H_{2}+1)\sin(H_{1}\pi)\sin(H_{2}\pi)}}{\Gamma(H_{1}+H_{2}+1)\sin\left(\frac{H_{1}+H_{2}}{2}\pi\right)}\cos\left[\frac{(H_{2}-H_{1})\pi}{2}\right],

(14)

and

III.2
Covariance structure of increments

For any process
\mathbf{Z}(t)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\delta}\mathbf{Z}(t)\equiv\mathbf{Z}(t+\delta)-\mathbf{Z}(t).

(19)

In the following theorem, we present the covariance structure for the increment process of the causal and well-balanced 2D fBm.

Theorem 2
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}\in(0,1)
and
|\rho|\leq 1
. The covariance structure of the increment process
\Delta^{\delta}\mathbf{Z}(t)
of causal or well-balanced 2D fBm is as follows

\displaystyle\gamma_{jk}^{\Delta}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta^{\delta}Z_{j}(t)\Delta^{\delta}Z_{k}(s)\rangle=\frac{\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}\rho_{jk}}{2}\left[w_{jk}(t-s+\delta)|t-s+\delta|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\left.w_{jk}(t-s-\delta)|t-s-\delta|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}-2w_{jk}(t-s)|t-s|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\right],

(20)

where the function

is given in Eq.(
13

and

are defined in Theorem

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is a natural consequence of the covariance structure of the introduced processes
\mathbf{X}(t)
and

\displaystyle\langle\Delta^{\delta}X_{j}(t)\Delta^{\delta}X_{k}(s)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(X_{j}(t+\delta)-X_{j}(t))(X_{k}(s+\delta)-X_{k}(s))\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma_{jk}(t+\delta,s+\delta)-\gamma_{jk}(t,s+\delta)-\gamma_{jk}(t+\delta,s)+\gamma_{jk}(t,s),\hskip 10.00002ptj,k=1,2.

(21)

Substituting the covariance function

1
yields the desired covariance structure of the increment process. The proof for well-balanced 2D fBm is analogous.

In the multivariate setting, for any second-order stationary process

Let us note, that for a

)

IV.2
Spectral content of the process

An extension of PSD calculated on the stationary process (here, for the increments process of 2D fBm), one considers PSD calculated based on the information from the trajectory. In particular, given a nonstationary 1D process
Z(t)
,
0\leq t\leq T

T

norton2003fundamentals
;
niemann2013fluctuations
;
krapf2018power
;
krapf2019spectral

\displaystyle S_{Z}(f,T)\equiv\frac{1}{T}\left|\int_{0}^{T}e^{ift}Z(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right|^{2}.

(35)

Note that in contrast to Eq. (
23
), the PSD depends on measurement time
T
. In
d
dimensions, the natural extension of the PSD defined in Eq. (
35
) for a real process
\mathbf{Z}(t)
,
0\leq t\leq T

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{Z}}(f,T)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{ift}\mathbf{Z}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{ifs}\mathbf{Z}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{*},

(36)

where
(\mathbf{x})^{*}

\mathbf{x}

(\mathbf{x})^{*}=\overline{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}

d\times d

Eq. (

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{Z}}(f,T)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{if(t-s)}\mathbf{Z}(t)\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t.

(37)

Taking the expected value, we can also calculate the ensemble-averaged PSD

\displaystyle\langle S_{\mathbf{Z}}(f,T)\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{if(t-s)}\langle\mathbf{Z}(t)\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}(s)\rangle\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t,

(38)

alternatively, written component-wise it is

\displaystyle\langle S_{\mathbf{Z},jk}(f,T)\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{if(t-s)}\langle Z_{j}(t)Z_{k}(s)\rangle\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t,~~j,k=1,2.

(39)

Let us note that for
j=k
we obtain the classical PSD given in Eq. (

Here, we further use the simplified notation

to streamline the notation whenever it is clear which process is referred to.

.

The asymptotic behavior of the ensemble-averaged PSD

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we introduced a natural construction of 2D fBm that accounts for anisotropic scaling and cross-dimensional dependencies, extending the classical framework of Mandelbrot and van Ness to multivariate settings. By incorporating correlated Gaussian noises and a matrix-valued Hurst operator, we provided two distinct formulations, causal and well-balanced 2D fBm, and derived their respective auto- and cross-covariance across the two components. Our analysis highlights key differences between these two formulations, particularly in the asymmetry of causal fBm and implications for time-reversal. Furthermore, we analyzed the spectral properties of the processes, presenting both full analytical derivations and asymptotic behaviors in the frequency domain. Our results reveal how variations in the Hurst exponents and correlation structure manifest in the spectral content, offering new insights into the interpretation of multivariate time series in diverse systems. An especially interesting case arises when
H_{1}=H_{2}
. In this situation, the process remains a 2D fBm under arbitrary rotations. In contrast, when
H_{1}\neq H_{2}
, a rotation mixes the coordinates in such a way that the resulting marginals are no longer fBm, but instead they exhibit more complex dependencies.

Extensive numerical simulations validated our theoretical findings, demonstrating consistency in both the time and frequency domains. The proposed framework thus provides a robust tool for modeling and analyzing complex anomalous diffusion processes in multidimensional environments, especially in settings where directional dependence and inter-component correlations cannot be ignored. This study opens several avenues for further exploration. A potential immediate extension involves generalizing the proposed constructions to higher dimensions. Our work opens the way to the application of 2D fBm to empirical data in fields such as neuroscience, single-particle tracking, structural dynamics, and financial econometrics, which may yield new insights into underlying spatiotemporal correlations.
While all analyses are shown for the two-dimensional case, they can be readily generalized to any dimension.

\displaystyle=\left\langle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{i(k+1)x}-e^{ikx}}{ix}\overline{\left(\frac{e^{iy}-1}{iy}\right)}\left(x_{+}^{-D}A+x_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\right)\cdot\left(y_{+}^{-D}A+y_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\right)^{*}\widehat{B}(\mathrm{d}x)\widehat{B}^{*}(\mathrm{d}y)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{i(k+1)x}-e^{ikx}}{ix}\overline{\left(\frac{e^{ix}-1}{ix}\right)}\left(x_{+}^{-D}A+x_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\right)\cdot\left(x_{+}^{-D}A+x_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\right)^{*}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\pi+2n\pi}^{\pi+2n\pi}\frac{e^{i(k+1)x}-e^{ikx}}{ix}\overline{\frac{e^{ix}-1}{ix}}\left(x_{+}^{-D}A+x_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\right)\cdot\left(x_{+}^{-D}A+x_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\right)^{*}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\textrm{substituging }x=f+2n\pi\textrm{ and exchanging the integral and sum we obtain}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ik(f+2n\pi)}\frac{|e^{i(f+2n\pi)}-1|^{2}}{(f+2n\pi)^{2}}\left((f+2n\pi)_{+}^{-D}A+(f+2n\pi)_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\right)\cdot\left((f+2n\pi)_{+}^{-D}A+(f+2n\pi)_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\right)^{*}\mathrm{d}f

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{ikf}|e^{if}-1|^{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left((f+2n\pi)_{+}^{-D}AA^{*}(f+2n\pi)_{+}^{-D}+(f+2n\pi)_{-}^{-D}\overline{A}\overline{A^{*}}(f+2n\pi)_{-}\right)/(f+2n\pi)^{2}\mathrm{d}f.

Remembering that
AA^{*}=C
(elements of
C
are given in (
59
) and (
82
)), and taking Remark 1.3.6 and 1.3.8
pipiras2017long
we obtain the final result for the power spectral density of
\Delta\mathbf{Z}
.

Considering element-wise power spectral density, we have

\displaystyle S_{\Delta\mathbf{Z},jk}(f)

\displaystyle=|e^{if}-1|^{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[(f+2n\pi)_{+}^{-(d_{j}+d_{k})}c_{jk}+(f+2n\pi)_{-}^{-(d_{j}+d_{k})}\overline{c_{jk}}\right]/(f+2n\pi)^{2}

\displaystyle=|e^{if}-1|^{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[(f+2n\pi)_{+}^{-(H_{j}+H_{k}+1)}c_{jk}+(f+2n\pi)_{-}^{-(H_{j}+H_{k}+1)}\overline{c_{jk}}\right]/(f+2n\pi)^{2},

as
d_{j}=H_{j}-1/2
.

Moreover, by simple calculations we have the asymptotics as

\displaystyle S_{\Delta\mathbf{Z},jk}(f)\sim c_{jk}f^{(-H_{j}-H_{k}+1)}.

∎

Appendix E
Proof of Theorem
4
, spectral content of 2D fBm trajectory

Proof.

Power spectral density for the trajectory
\mathbf{Z}(t),0\leq t\leq T
, is given by

\displaystyle S_{jk}(f,T)\equiv\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{ift}Z_{j}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\overline{\int_{0}^{T}e^{ifs}Z_{k}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}.

(89)

It is a random variable depending both on the considered frequency
f
, time horizon
T
, and the trajectory
\mathbf{Z}(t)
.
Since we want to consider the ensemble-averaged power spectral density, we have

\displaystyle\langle S_{jk}(f,T)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\left\langle\int_{0}^{T}e^{ift}Z_{j}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\overline{\int_{0}^{T}e^{ifs}Z_{k}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}\right\rangle=\ |\textrm{by Fubini theorem}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{if(t-s)}\left\langle Z_{j}(t)Z_{k}(s)\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{if(t-s)}\gamma_{jk}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\gamma_{jk}(t,s)
is the covariance function for
Z_{j}
and
Z_{k}
. For simplicity, we write the general form of this function with weighting function
w_{jk}
that has explicit form for both causal and well-balanced cases (Eq. (
13
)).

Here, we consider the case when
H_{j}+H_{k}\neq 1
or the considered model is well-balanced 2D fBm (so that, there is no

\log
part in the cross covariance function).
By rescaling the integrals’ variables
\frac{t}{T}\rightarrow x,\frac{s}{T}\rightarrow y
and by introducing
\tilde{\omega}=fT
, we have

\displaystyle\langle S_{jk}(f,T)\rangle

\displaystyle=T^{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}\frac{\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\tilde{\omega}(x-y)}\left(w_{jk}(x)x^{H_{j}+H_{k}}+w_{jk}(-y)y^{H_{j}+H_{k}}-w_{jk}(x-y)|x-y|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

(90)

We calculate each element separately. Let us introduce the notation

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\tilde{\omega}(x-y)}w_{jk}(x)x^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

(91)

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\tilde{\omega}(x-y)}w_{jk}(-y)y^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

(92)

\displaystyle\mathbb{III}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\tilde{\omega}(x-y)}w_{jk}(x-y)|x-y|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

(93)

Then the resulting integral is equal to

\displaystyle\langle S_{jk}(f,T)\rangle

\displaystyle=T^{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}\frac{\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}}{2}(\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{II}-\mathbb{III}).

(94)

Let us start with
\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\tilde{\omega}(x-y)}w_{jk}(x)x^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\tilde{\omega}x}\frac{1-e^{-i\tilde{\omega}}}{i\tilde{\omega}}x^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\underbrace{w_{jk}(x)}_{\text{constant }\forall x>0}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=w_{jk}(1)\frac{1-e^{-i\tilde{\omega}}}{i\tilde{\omega}}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\tilde{\omega}x)+i\sin(\tilde{\omega}x))x^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right].

Now, let us consider
\mathbb{II}

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\tilde{\omega}(x-y)}w_{jk}(-y)y^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-i\tilde{\omega}y}\frac{e^{i\tilde{\omega}}-1}{i\tilde{\omega}}y^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\underbrace{w_{jk}(-y)}_{\text{constant }\forall y>0}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=w_{jk}(-1)\frac{e^{i\tilde{\omega}}-1}{i\tilde{\omega}}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\tilde{\omega}x)-i\sin(\tilde{\omega}x))y^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right].

Summing up the first two parts, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{II}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\tilde{\omega}x)x^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x\left[w_{jk}(1)\frac{1-e^{-i\tilde{\omega}}}{i\tilde{\omega}}-w_{jk}(-1)\frac{1-e^{i\tilde{\omega}}}{i\tilde{\omega}}\right]

(95)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\tilde{\omega}y)y^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}y\left[w_{jk}(1)\frac{1-e^{-i\tilde{\omega}}}{i\tilde{\omega}}+w_{jk}(-1)\frac{1-e^{i\tilde{\omega}}}{i\tilde{\omega}}\right].

(96)

Reintroducing functions
\mathcal{C}_{jk}
and
\mathcal{S}_{jk}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{jk}\equiv\mathcal{C}_{jk}(\tilde{\omega})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\tilde{\omega}x)x^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(97)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{jk}\equiv\mathcal{S}_{jk}(\tilde{\omega})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\tilde{\omega}x)x^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(98)

and utilizing that
w_{jk}(x)=\rho_{jk}-\mathrm{sign}(x)\eta_{jk}
we can simplify the expression

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{II}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{jk}\left[(\rho_{jk}-\eta_{jk})\frac{1-\cos(\tilde{\omega})+i\sin(\tilde{\omega})}{i\tilde{\omega}}-(\rho_{jk}+\eta_{jk})\frac{1-\cos(\tilde{\omega})-i\sin(\tilde{\omega})}{i\tilde{\omega}}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{S}_{jk}\left[(\rho_{jk}-\eta_{jk})\frac{1-\cos(\tilde{\omega})+i\sin(\tilde{\omega})}{i\tilde{\omega}}-(\rho_{jk}+\eta_{jk})\frac{1-\cos(\tilde{\omega})-i\sin(\tilde{\omega})}{i\tilde{\omega}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\rho_{jk}\left(\frac{\sin\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{C}_{jk}+\frac{1-\cos\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{S}_{jk}\right)+2i\eta_{jk}\left(\frac{1-\cos\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{C}_{jk}-\frac{\sin\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{S}_{jk}\right)

(99)

For the third part, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{III}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\tilde{\omega}(x-y)}w_{jk}(x-y)|x-y|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\hskip 9.24994pt|\textrm{substitute }u=x-y,\ z=x|

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{0}du\int_{0}^{u+1}dzw_{jk}(u)e^{i\tilde{\omega}u}|u|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}+\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{u}^{1}dzw_{jk}(u)e^{i\tilde{\omega}u}u^{H_{j}+H_{k}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{0}duw_{jk}(u)e^{i\tilde{\omega}u}|u|^{H_{j}+H_{k}}(1+u)+\int_{0}^{1}duw_{jk}(u)e^{i\tilde{\omega}u}u^{H_{j}+H_{k}}(1-u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}du\underbrace{w_{jk}(-u)}_{=\rho_{jk}+\eta_{jk}}e^{-i\tilde{\omega}u}u^{H_{j}+H_{k}}(1-u)+\int_{0}^{1}du\underbrace{w_{jk}(u)}_{=\rho_{jk}-\eta_{jk}}e^{i\tilde{\omega}u}u^{H_{j}+H_{k}}(1-u)

\displaystyle=\rho_{jk}\int_{0}^{1}\underbrace{\left(e^{-i\tilde{\omega}u}+e^{i\tilde{\omega}u}\right)}_{=2\cos(\tilde{\omega}u)}u^{H_{j}+H_{k}}(1-u)du-\eta_{jk}\int_{0}^{1}\underbrace{\left(e^{i\tilde{\omega}u}-e^{-i\tilde{\omega}u}\right)}_{=2i\sin(\tilde{\omega}u)}u^{H_{j}+H_{k}}(1-u)du

\displaystyle=2\rho_{jk}\left(\mathcal{C}_{jk}-\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\tilde{\omega}u)u^{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}du\right)-2i\eta_{jk}\left(\mathcal{S}_{jk}-\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\tilde{\omega}u)u^{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}du\right).

We see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\tilde{\omega}u)u^{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}du

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\omega}}\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\tilde{\omega}u)u^{H_{j}+H_{k}}du=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{S}_{jk},

(100)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\tilde{\omega}u)u^{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}du

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\omega}}\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\tilde{\omega}u)u^{H_{j}+H_{k}}du=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{C}_{jk},

(101)

or, via integration by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\tilde{\omega}u)u^{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}du=\frac{\sin\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}-\frac{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{S}_{jk},

(102)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\tilde{\omega}u)u^{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}du=\frac{-\cos\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}+\frac{H_{j}+H_{k}+1}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{C}_{jk}.

(103)

We then arrive at the final expression

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{II}-\mathbb{III}=

\displaystyle 2\rho_{jk}\left(\left[\frac{\sin\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}-1\right]\mathcal{C}_{jk}+\frac{1-\cos\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{S}_{jk}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{S}_{jk}\right)

\displaystyle+2i\eta_{jk}\left(\frac{1-\cos\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{C}_{jk}-\left[\frac{\sin\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}+1\right]\mathcal{S}_{jk}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{C}_{jk}\right),

(104)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{II}-\mathbb{III}=

\displaystyle 2\rho_{jk}\left(\left[\frac{\sin\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}-1\right]\mathcal{C}_{jk}+\frac{1-\cos\tilde{\omega}-H_{j}-H_{k}-1}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{S}_{jk}+\frac{\sin\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}\right)

\displaystyle+2i\eta_{jk}\left(\frac{1-\cos\tilde{\omega}+H_{j}+H_{k}+1}{\tilde{\omega}}\mathcal{C}_{jk}-\left[\frac{\sin\tilde{\omega}}{\tilde{\omega}}+1\right]\mathcal{S}_{jk}\right).

(105)

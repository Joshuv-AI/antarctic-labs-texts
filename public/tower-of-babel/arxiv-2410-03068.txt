Triply Graded Link Homology for Coxeter Braids on 4 Strands
Joshua P. Turner

Abstract
. We compute the triply graded Khovanov-Rozansky homology for Coxeter braids on 4 strands.



1.
Introduction

In Section 6 of
[
11
]
, the author uses a recursive process developed by Hogancamp and Elias in
[
2
]
to compute the triply-graded Khovanov Rozansky homology (also called HHH) for Coxeter braids on 3 strands, matching the result with the bigraded Hilbert series of a particular ideal in a polynomial ring. These braids were already known to be parity by work of Hogancamp in
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we use the same method to show that Coexter braids up to 4 strands are parity, and to compute
\mathrm{HHH}
. Previously, this method has been used by Hogancamp to compute
\mathrm{HHH}
for
T(n,dn)
torus links in
[
6
]
, and by Hogancamp and Mellit to compute
\mathrm{HHH}
for all torus links in
[
7
]
.

1.1.
Homology of Coxeter links

The three gradings of
\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)
are typically denoted
Q,T,
and
A
. However, in line with
[
2
]
, we use the change of variables

q=Q^{2},\quad t=T^{2}Q^{-2},\quad a=AQ^{-2}.

Definition 1.1
.

For any braid or braid-like diagram
\beta
, we say that
\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)
is parity if it is supported in only even homological (
T
) degrees. We will also say
\beta
itself is parity if
\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)
is parity.

Given integers
0\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}
, we define the n-stranded braid

\beta(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})=\mathrm{FT}_{n}^{d_{1}}\mathrm{FT}_{n-1}^{d_{2}-d_{1}}\cdots\mathrm{FT}_{2}^{d_{n-1}-d_{n-2}}

=\JM_{n}^{d_{1}}\JM_{n-1}^{d_{2}}\cdots\JM_{2}^{d_{n-1}},

where
\mathrm{FT}_{k}
and
\JM_{k}
represent a full twist and a Jucys-Murphy element on the first
k
strands respectively. These braids are part of the family of Coxeter braids defined in
[
10
]
. For the sake of comparison to the ideal
J
defined in Section
1.2
, we focus on pure Coxeter braids, whose closures have the maximum number of components. We expect that our calculations of
\mathrm{HHH}
can be easily extended to all Coxeter braids as defined in
[
10
]
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

The Coxeter braid
\beta(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
is parity for all
n
and for all
0\leq d_{1}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}
.

In
[
11
]
, the author shows that Coxeter braids on 3 strands are parity, and explicitly computes
\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)
. In Theorem
2.16
, we show that Coxeter braids on 4 strands are also parity.

Note that the class of Coxeter links has some overlap with torus links. In particular we use the computation of
\mathrm{HHH}(\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{k})=\mathrm{HHH}(T(k,4k))
from
[
6
]
as a base case for our recursion (which corresponds to
d_{1}=d_{2}=d_{3}=d_{4}=k
), rather than re-derive it.

1.2.
Relation to the ideal
J

In this section, we use the
y
-ification of Khovanov-Rozansky homology, denoted by
\mathrm{HY}(\beta)
. It was defined in
[
4
]
where it was shown to be a topological invariant of the link obtained by closing
\beta
. We recall some results and conjectures describing
\mathrm{HY}(\beta)
for Coxeter braids.

Define the ideal

J(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})=\bigcap_{i<j}(t_{i}-t_{j},x_{i}-x_{j})^{d_{i}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}].

By the results of
[
11
]
, such ideals are closely related to the equivariant Borel-Moore homology of certain affine Springer fibers. For
d_{1}=\ldots=d_{n}
, these ideals appear in the work of Haiman
[
3
]
on the geometry of Hilbert schemes of points on the plane.

Note that for both the ideal
J
and the braid
\beta
, the largest
d_{n}
is irrelevant, but we will continue including it for clarity.

If Conjecture
1.2
holds, the following theorem of Gorsky and Hogancamp gives a description of the
a=0
piece of both
\mathrm{HY}
and
\mathrm{HHH}
in terms of the ideal
J
.

Theorem 1.3

(Gorsky, Hogancamp
[
4
]
)
.

Assume that
\beta=\JM_{n}^{d_{1}}\ldots\JM_{2}^{d_{n-1}}
and
\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)
is parity. Then

(1)

\mathrm{HY}^{a=0}(\beta)=\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)\otimes\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
and
\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)=\mathrm{HY}^{a=0}(\beta)/(y)

(2)

\mathrm{HY}^{a=0}(\beta)=J(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
.

In particular, this also implies that the ideal
J(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is free over
\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Suppose that
0\leq d_{1}\leq\cdots\leq d_{1}
,
\beta=\JM_{n}^{d_{1}}\ldots\JM_{2}^{d_{n-1}}
, and
J=J(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
. Then
\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)
is parity and

\mathrm{HY}^{a=0}(\beta)\simeq J

and

\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)\simeq J/(y)J.

This description doesn’t guarantee that we can write out
\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)
explicitly, as finding the bigraded Hilbert series for
J(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
is difficult in general. However, the recursive method we use to show that a braid is parity simultaneously gives a recursive way to compute
\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)
as a rational function in
q
and
t
.

In
[
11
]
, the author shows that Coxeter links on 3 strands are parity and shows that Conjecture
1.4
holds for
n=3
. In this paper, we use the same tools to show that the corresponding class of braids on 4 strands is also parity, and recursively calculate HHH in this case as well.

Theorem 1.5
.

For the braid
\beta=\JM_{4}^{d_{1}}\JM_{3}^{d_{2}}\JM_{2}^{d_{3}}
with
0\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq d_{3}\leq d_{4}
, and
J=J(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})
,

\mathrm{HY}^{a=0}(\beta)=J.

In particular, the ideal
J
is free over as a module over
\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\dots,y_{4}]
, and its bigraded Hilbert series is given by

\frac{1}{(1-t)^{4}}\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta).

Proof.

We show that the braid
\beta
is parity in Theorem
2.16
. So by Theorem
1.3
,

\mathrm{HY}^{a=0}(\beta)=J(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})=\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(\beta)\otimes\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}].

∎

For
n=3
, the bigraded Hilbert series for
J(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is explicitly computed in general in
[
11
]
, but for
n=4
there is no similar calculation to compare to. However, the recursions in this paper can be used to compute this series explicitly for any particular
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}
.

Remark 1.6
.

Note that
[
11
, Conjecture 4.8]
predicts a closed formula for the Hilbert series as a sum of certain rational functions over 10 standard Young tableaux of size 4. We plan to verify this conjecture in a future work.

We are optimistic that Theorem
1.3
can be generalized to arbitrary
n
using the same recursive process, proving Conjecture
1.4
. This would also confirm that the ideal
J(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
is free over
y
’s, as conjectured by the author in
[
11
]
, and give a recursive formula for its bigraded Hilbert series.

2.
Calculations

Here we compute Khovanov-Rozansky homology of Coxeter braids on 4 strands. Let

\beta=\beta(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})=(\mathrm{FT}_{2})^{d_{3}-d_{2}}\cdot(\mathrm{FT}_{3})^{d_{2}-d_{1}}\cdot(\mathrm{FT}_{4})^{d_{1}},

We will also use the Jucys-Murphy braids
\JM_{2}=\sigma_{1}^{2}

\JM_{3}=\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}

\JM_{4}=\sigma_{3}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}

\mathrm{FT}_{n}=\JM_{2}\JM_{3}\cdots\JM_{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For any braid or braid-like diagram
\beta
, we say that
\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)
is parity if it is supported in only even homological (
T
) degrees, where
T^{2}=\frac{t}{q}
.

Note that
\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)
must be parity for its graded Hilbert series to be rational in terms of
(q,a,t)
.

Remark 2.2

(
[
5
]
)
.

If both diagrams on the right-hand side of Figure
1
(d) are parity, then so is the left-hand side, and we can replace the map with addition on the level of rational functions (or a direct sum on the level of complexes). This implies that if we use Figure
1
(d) repeatedly to break down a braid
\beta
into complexes that are known to be parity, then every complex along the way, including
\beta
itself, is parity.

We will make regular use of the fact that

\mathrm{FT}_{n}=\JM_{2}\JM_{3}\cdots\JM_{n},

and that
\sigma_{i}
commutes with

\mathrm{FT}_{n}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and with
\JM_{n}
for
1<i<n
. In other words, the full twist commutes with all crossings on its strands, and the Jucys-Murphy braid commutes with internal crossings. Additionally, since
K_{n}
absorbs crossings by Figure
1
(b), for
n\leq m
, we have that

K_{n}\mathrm{FT}_{m}=K_{n}\JM_{n+1}\cdots\JM_{m}.

We will also use the conjugation invariance of
\mathrm{HHH}
:

(1)

\mathrm{HHH}(\alpha\cdot\beta)=\mathrm{HHH}(\beta\cdot\alpha)

for any braids
\alpha
and
\beta
. We will denote the conjugate braids by
\alpha\beta\sim\beta\alpha
. We will additionally use the result from Hogancamp
[
6
]
that
\mathrm{HHH}(\mathrm{FT}_{n}^{k})
is parity for all
n,k\geq 0
.

Finally, note that Figure
1
(a), (b), and (d) are local, i.e. they hold on the level of homotopy equivalences of chain complexes. Braid conjugation and the closing up step (c) are non-local, and only hold on the level of
\mathrm{HHH}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let

•

A(n,m,l)=K_{1}\mathrm{FT}_{2}^{l}\mathrm{FT}_{3}^{m}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n}
,

•

B(n,m)=K_{2}\mathrm{FT}_{3}^{m}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n}
,

•

C(n)=K_{3}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n}
.

In this notation,

K_{1}\beta(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})=A(d_{1},d_{2}-d_{1},d_{3}-d_{2}).

Assuming that all braids are parity, we can write Figure
1
(d) in the form

K_{n}\JM_{n+1}=t^{-n}K_{n+1}+qt^{-n}K_{n}.

By Figure
1
(b),
K_{n}\JM_{n+1}=K_{n}\mathrm{FT}_{n+1}
, so we immediately get the following recursions:

(2)

A(n,m,l)=t^{-1}B(n,m)+qt^{-1}A(n,m,l-1)

(3)

B(n,m)=t^{-2}C(n)+qt^{-2}B(n,m-1)

(4)

C(n)=t^{-3}K_{4}+qt^{-3}C(n-1)

These recursions let us proceed by induction, as every application either decreases
n,m,
or
l
or shifts us from
A
to
B
or
B
to
C
. Also note that they don’t involve any closing up or conjugation, so they are all local. The majority of the work lies in the ‘base cases’, when one or more of
l,m,n
are 0.

2.1.
Recursion for
C(n)

Here we calculate
\mathrm{HHH}
recursively for the braids
C(n)
and for a twisted version
\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n)
that we will need in Section
2.2
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The braid
C(n)
is parity for all
n\geq 0
, and

\mathrm{HHH}(C(n))=t^{-3}\frac{1-(qt^{-3})^{n}}{1-qt^{-3}}\mathrm{HHH}(K_{4})+\frac{(qt^{-3})^{n}(t^{2}+a)(t+a)(1+a)^{2}}{(1-q)}.

Proof.

The braid
C(0)
has
K_{3}
on the first 3 strands and an empty fourth strand. We can close up the
K_{3}
using Figure
1
(c) and introduce a
K_{1}
to close up the last strand, so

\mathrm{HHH}(C(0))=(t^{2}+a)(t+a)(1+a)^{2}/(1-q).

We also have that

\mathrm{HHH}(K_{4})=(t^{3}+a)(t^{2}+a)(t+a)(1+a).

So by induction, both terms on the right hand side of recursion (
4
) are parity. Therefore
C(n)
is parity, and can be computed recursively.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

The braid
\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n)
is parity for all
k,n\geq 0
, and

(5)

\mathrm{HHH}(\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n))=t^{-3}\frac{1-(qt^{-3})^{n}}{1-qt^{-3}}\mathrm{HHH}(K_{4})+(qt^{-3})^{n}(t^{2}+a)(t+a)(1-q)\mathrm{HHH}(\mathrm{FT}_{2}^{k}).

Proof.

For
\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(0)
, we can close up (using Figure
1
(c)) the first two strands running through
K_{3}
, and then we are left with exactly
k
full twists on the remaining two strands along with a
K_{1}
. So

\mathrm{HHH}(\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(0))=(t^{2}+a)(t+a)(1-q)\mathrm{HHH}(\mathrm{FT}_{2}^{k}).

For
\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n)
, we apply recursion (
4
) locally to get

\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n)=t^{-3}\sigma_{3}^{2k}K_{4}+qt^{-3}\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n-1).

The
\sigma_{3}
crossings get absorbed by
K_{4}
(using Figure
1
(b)), so by induction on
n
, we conclude that
\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n)
is parity with the desired formula for
\mathrm{HHH}
.
∎

2.2.
Recursions for
B(n,m)

Here we calculate
\mathrm{HHH}
recursively for the braids
B(n,m)
and for the twisted braids
\sigma_{2}^{2}B(n,m)
that we will need in Section
2.3
.

Lemma 2.6
.

The braid
\sigma_{3}^{2k}B(0,0)
is parity for all
k\geq 0
, and

\mathrm{HHH}(\sigma_{3}^{2k}B(0,0))=(t+a)(1+a)\mathrm{HHH}(\mathrm{FT}_{2}^{k}).

Proof.

Recall that
B(0,0)
consists only of a
K_{2}
on the left two strands, which does not overlap with
\sigma_{3}^{2k}
.
We close up the two strands running through
K_{2}
(Figure
1
(c)) and are left with
k
full twists on two strands.
∎

Now we consider the case
B(n,0)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The braid
\sigma_{3}^{2k}B(n,0)
is parity for all
n,k\geq 0
, and

\mathrm{HHH}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k}B(n,0)\right]=t^{-2}\mathrm{HHH}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n)\right]+qt^{-2}\mathrm{HHH}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k+2}B(n-1,1)\right]

=t^{-2}\mathrm{HHH}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n)\right]+qt^{-4}\mathrm{HHH}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k+2}C(n-1)\right]+q^{2}t^{-4}\mathrm{HHH}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k+2}B(n-1,0)\right].

In particular we have that
B(n,0)
is parity for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

We have that locally,

B(n,0)=K_{2}\JM_{3}\JM_{4}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1}

=t^{-2}K_{3}\JM_{4}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1}+qt^{-2}K_{2}\JM_{4}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1}

(6)

=t^{-2}C(n)+qt^{-2}K_{2}\JM_{4}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1}.

Here we used Figure
1
(d) for
K_{2}\JM_{3}
, and the identity
K_{3}\JM_{4}=K_{3}\mathrm{FT}_{4}
which follows from Figure
1
(b).
Now note that
\JM_{4}=\sigma_{3}\JM_{3}\sigma_{3}
, so

\sigma_{3}^{2k}K_{2}\JM_{4}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1}=\sigma_{3}^{2k}K_{2}\sigma_{3}\JM_{3}\sigma_{3}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1}=\sigma_{3}^{2k}\sigma_{3}K_{2}\JM_{3}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1}\sigma_{3},

where we use the fact that
\sigma_{3}
commutes with both
\mathrm{FT}_{4}
and
K_{2}
.
By conjugation invariance (

) we have that

\mathrm{HHH}(\sigma_{3}^{2k}\sigma_{3}K_{2}\JM_{3}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1}\sigma_{3})\simeq\mathrm{HHH}(\sigma_{3}^{2k+2}K_{2}\JM_{3}\mathrm{FT}_{4}^{n-1})=\mathrm{HHH}(\sigma_{3}^{2k+2}B(n-1,1)).

Finally, a local application of recursion (

\sigma_{3}^{2k+2}B(n-1,1)=t^{-2}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k+2}C(n-1)\right]+qt^{-2}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k+2}B(n-1,0)\right].

\sigma_{3}^{2k+2n}B(0,0)

∎

The braid
B(n,m)

Proof.

We know that

Lemma 2.9
.

If the right hand side is parity, then

(7)

\mathrm{HHH}\left[\sigma_{2}^{2}\sigma_{3}^{2k-2}B(n,0)\right]=t^{-2}\mathrm{HHH}\left[\sigma_{3}^{2k-2}C(n)\right]+\\

Lemma 2.10
.

The braid
\widetilde{\JM}_{3}\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(n)
is parity.

Proof.

Consider
n=0
first. We close up the first strand (apply Figure
1
(c)), so

\mathrm{HHH}(\widetilde{\JM}_{3}\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(0))=\mathrm{HHH}(\widetilde{\JM}_{3}\sigma_{3}^{2k}K_{3})=(t^{2}+a)\mathrm{HHH}(\JM_{3}\sigma_{2}^{2k}K_{2}).

Note that the first two braids here are on 4 strands, while the last is on 3 strands after the first strand was closed up.
Now conjugate by
\JM_{3}
and apply Figure
1
(d) to get

\JM_{3}\sigma_{2}^{2k}K_{2}\sim\sigma_{2}^{2k}K_{2}\JM_{3}=t^{-2}\left[\sigma_{2}^{2k}K_{3}\right]+qt^{-2}\left[\sigma_{2}^{2k}K_{2}\right].

The
K_{3}
in the first term absorbs the extra crossings (Figure
1
(b)), and we can close up the first strand of the second term (Figure
1
(d)). So

t^{-2}\left[\sigma_{2}^{2k}K_{3}\right]+qt^{-2}\left[\sigma_{2}^{2k}K_{2}\right]=t^{-2}\left[K_{3}\right]+qt^{-2}(t+a)\left[K_{1}\mathrm{FT}_{2}^{k}\right],

where the final term is now on two strands. So we conclude that
\widetilde{\JM}_{3}\sigma_{3}^{2k}C(0)
is parity, and

Lemma 2.11
.

\sigma_{2}^{2}\sigma_{3}^{2k}B(0,0)

\mathrm{HHH}(\sigma_{2}^{2}\sigma_{3}^{2k}B(0,0))=(t+a)t^{-1}\left[(t+a)(1-q)+q(1+a)\right]\mathrm{HHH}(\mathrm{FT}_{2}^{k}).

on three strands. Then we use Figure

Lemma 2.13
.

(9)

Theorem 2.16
.

Assume that
0\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq d_{3}
. Then the braid

\beta=\beta(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})=(\mathrm{FT}_{2})^{d_{3}-d_{2}}\cdot(\mathrm{FT}_{3})^{d_{2}-d_{1}}\cdot(\mathrm{FT}_{4})^{d_{1}}

\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)
can be computed using the recursive process laid out above.

Proof.

We write
\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)=\frac{1}{1-q}\mathrm{HHH}(A(d_{1},d_{2}-d_{1},d_{3}-d_{2})),
so it is sufficient to prove that

2

and

2.8
and Corollary
2.15
respectively, and we can continue following the recursions laid out above to compute
\mathrm{HHH}(\beta)

∎

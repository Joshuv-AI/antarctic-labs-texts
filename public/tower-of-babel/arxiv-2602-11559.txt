Towards Monoidal Categorifications of Twisted Products of Flag Varieties
Yingjin Bi

Abstract
. Let G G be a simple, simply connected, simply laced algebraic group. We construct a monoidal category of representations of the quantum affine algebra U q ​ ( 𝔤 ^ ) U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}) whose Grothendieck ring contains a cluster algebra with initial seed given by that of the coordinate ring of twisted products of flag varieties. 1.
Introduction

Cluster algebras, introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
7
]
, play a central role in representation theory, Lie theory, and algebraic geometry. Broadly speaking, two fundamental problems drive the development of cluster algebra theory.

The first problem is to identify algebraic varieties whose coordinate rings admit cluster algebra structures. Prominent examples include reduced double Bruhat cells, double Bott–Samelson cells, and braid varieties; see, for instance,
[
3
,
23
,
6
,
9
]
. In fact, all of these varieties arise as special cases of twisted products of flag varieties. The existence of cluster structures on twisted products of flag varieties was recently established in
[
2
]
.

The second fundamental problem is to understand the structure of cluster monomials and, in particular, to determine whether they are contained in well-behaved bases of the corresponding coordinate rings. One of the main approaches to this problem is through categorification, which provides a conceptual explanation for positivity and basis phenomena. In particular, monoidal categorification has proved to be a powerful tool in this direction; see, for example,
[
14
,
17
,
19
]
.

Since twisted products of flag varieties contain a broad class of cluster varieties, their monoidal categorification provides a natural and powerful approach to addressing the second fundamental problem for braid varieties and double Bruhat cells. Motivated by the work of Kashiwara–Kim–Oh–Park
[
19
]
, we seek such a categorification within the module category of quantum affine algebras. More precisely, we aim to construct a monoidal subcategory
\mathscr{C}
whose Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\mathscr{C})
is isomorphic to the coordinate ring of twisted products of flag varietiesafter localization at the frozen variables, and such that cluster monomials correspond to classes of simple objects in
\mathscr{C}
. Furthermore, the quantum Grothendieck ring of
\mathscr{C}
is expected to yield a quantization of the coordinate ring of twisted products of flag varieties.

The cluster structures on braid varieties have been established using geometric and combinatorial methods, such as divisor theory and Demazure-type constructions; see, for instance,
[
6
,
9
]
. However, the explicit description of cluster variables in these settings remains highly nontrivial. In
[
2
]
, the cluster structure on twisted products of flag varieties is obtained via the approach developed in
[
20
]
. In particular, one can derive seeds for these cluster algebras from those associated with double Bott–Samelson cells. On the other hand, Kashiwara, Kim, Oh, and Park
[
19
]
, as well as Qin
[
22
]
, constructed a monoidal categorification
\mathscr{C}(b)
of the coordinate ring of the double Bott–Samelson variety
\operatorname{Con}_{b}(\mathscr{A})
associated with a braid group element
b
. This strongly suggests the existence of a monoidal subcategory of
\mathscr{C}(b)
that categorifies the coordinate ring of twisted products of flag varieties.

The goal of this paper is to construct such a monoidal category, together with a subalgebra of the bosonic extension algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
, that corresponds to twisted products of flag varieties. Our approach is motivated by the work of Ménard
[
20
]
and the author’s previous results
[
4
]
.

This program faces two main difficulties. First, unlike quantum groups, the algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
does not admit a known Mirković–Vilonen polytope structure, which plays a crucial role in existing categorification frameworks. Second, the algebraic structure of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
is substantially more intricate than that of quantum groups, making the construction of compatible monoidal subcategories considerably more delicate.

For a quiver

.

For a commutative ring

(2)

(L,B_{Q})

\bigwedge
-seed

\mathbf{s}

, is called a cluster monomial, where

Let

be a
\bigwedge
-seed. For

(1)

\mu_{k}(L)_{ij}=\begin{cases}-\lambda_{kj}+\sum_{t\in K}\max(0,-b_{tk})\lambda_{tj},&\text{if }i=k,j\neq k,\\
-\lambda_{ik}+\sum_{t\in K}\max(0,-b_{tk})\lambda_{it},&\text{if }i\neq k,j=k,\\
\lambda_{ij},&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(2)

\mu_{k}(B_{Q})_{ij}=\begin{cases}-b_{ij},&\text{if }i=k\text{ or }j=k,\\

x_{i},&\text{if }i\neq k,\end{cases}

4.
Bosonic extension algebra

In this section, we recall the definition of the bosonic extension algebra and its cluster structure.
Let
q
be a formal parameter. We define

[n]_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad[n]_{q}!:=\prod_{k=1}^{n}[k]_{q},\qquad\left[\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
n\end{array}\right]_{q}:=\frac{[m]_{q}!}{[n]_{q}![m-n]_{q}!}.

Set

Definition 4.1
.

The

is the
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/2})
-algebra generated by elements

\{f_{i,k}\mid i\in I,\;k\in\mathbb{Z}\},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{1-c_{ij}}(-1)^{k}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1-c_{ij}\\
k\end{array}\right]_{q}f_{i,p}^{\,1-c_{ij}-k}f_{j,p}f_{i,p}^{\,k}=0,\qquad\text{for }i\neq j,

(4.3)

\displaystyle f_{i,m}f_{j,p}=q^{(-1)^{p-m+1}c_{ij}}f_{j,p}f_{i,m}+\delta_{(i,m+1),(j,p)}(1-q^{2}),\qquad\text{for }m<p.

Here
\delta_{x,y}
denotes the Kronecker delta.

We assign a
Q
-grading to
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
by setting

\operatorname{wt}(f_{i,m})=(-1)^{m}\alpha_{i}.

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
admits a weight space decomposition

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}.

admits the factorization

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=\overrightarrow{\bigotimes}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[k]=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\geq 0}\cdot\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{<0},

\overrightarrow{\bigotimes}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[k]=\cdots\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[1]\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[0]\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[-1]\otimes\cdots.

Since our results do not rely on this integral form, we refer to
[
18
]
for details.

For
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, define
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{a})=\{k\in[r]\mid a_{k}\neq 0\}
.
For
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we write
\mathbf{a}>\mathbf{b}
if

\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{a})\cap\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{b})=\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad\min\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{a})>\max\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{b}).

Define
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}=(c_{k})_{k\in[r]}
by
c_{k}=a_{k}+b_{k}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
satisfy
\mathbf{a}>\mathbf{b}
, then

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})\cdot G^{\beta}(\mathbf{b})=G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}g_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}(q)\,G^{\beta}(\mathbf{c}),\quad g_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}(q)\in q\mathbb{Z}[q].

Proof.

Recall that

(4.10)

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})=P^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})+\sum_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{a}}f_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}}(q)\,P^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}^{\prime}),\qquad f_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}}(q)\in q\mathbb{Z}[q].

By definition of the order
\prec
, the condition
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{a}
implies

\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})\subset\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{a})
.

Similarly,

(4.11)

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{b})=P^{\beta}(\mathbf{b})+\sum_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{b}}f_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime},\mathbf{b}}(q)\,P^{\beta}(\mathbf{b}^{\prime}),\qquad f_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime},\mathbf{b}}(q)\in q\mathbb{Z}[q].

Again,
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{b}
implies
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{b}^{\prime})\subset\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{b})
.

Since
\mathbf{a}>\mathbf{b}
, it follows that
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}>\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
for all
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
appearing in
(
4.10
) and all
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
appearing in (
4.11
).
By the definition of the PBW basis elements
P^{\beta}(\cdot)
, we therefore have

P^{\beta}(\mathbf{d})\,P^{\beta}(\mathbf{e})=P^{\beta}(\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})\qquad\text{for all }\mathbf{d}>\mathbf{e}.

Combining (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we obtain

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})\cdot G^{\beta}(\mathbf{b})=P^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}h_{\mathbf{c},\,\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}(q)\,P^{\beta}(\mathbf{c}),\qquad h_{\mathbf{c},\,\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}(q)\in q\mathbb{Z}[q].

Finally, expressing each PBW basis element
P^{\beta}(\mathbf{c})
and
P^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})
in terms of the
global basis using (
4.9
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

In general, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. Suppose that

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})\,G^{\beta}(\mathbf{b})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}f_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}^{\mathbf{c}}(q)\,G^{\beta}(\mathbf{c}).

Then the maximal vector
\mathbf{c}_{\max}
with respect to the
\beta
-order
\prec

is given by
\mathbf{c}_{\max}=\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}
.

Proof.

By the Levendorskii–Soibelman formula

[
21
]
*Lemma 5.5, for any
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
we have

P^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})\,P^{\beta}(\mathbf{b})=q^{A}\,P^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}g_{\mathbf{c}}(q)\,P^{\beta}(\mathbf{c}),

for some integer
A
.

Using the triangular expansions of PBW monomials in the global basis,
cf. (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we obtain

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})\,G^{\beta}(\mathbf{b})=q^{A}\,G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}\prec\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}h_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}^{\mathbf{c}}(q)\,G^{\beta}(\mathbf{c}).

Since the expansion of an element in the global basis is unique,
this expression must coincide with the expansion assumed in the statement.
Consequently, the maximal vector appearing in the product is
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}
.
∎

.

4.2.
Translation maps of Lusztig parameters

Let
\beta=(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})
and
\beta^{\prime}
be two expressions of an element
b\in{\operatorname{Br}^{+}}
.

(1)

We say that
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by a
2-move
if there exists
k
such that

(i_{k},i_{k+1})=(i,j)
with
c_{ij}=0
, and
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by replacing

(i_{k},i_{k+1})
with
(i_{k+1},i_{k})
.

(2)

We say that
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by a
3-move
if there exists
k
such that

(i_{k-1},i_{k},i_{k+1})=(i,j,i)
with
c_{ij}=1
, and
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by replacing

(i,j,i)
with
(j,i,j)
.

It is well known that any two reduced expressions of
b
are related by a finite sequence of
2
-moves and
3
-moves.

Definition 4.11
.

For an expression
\beta
of
b
, let

\Psi_{\beta}:\mathbb{N}^{r}\to\mathbf{B}(b)

be the map sending
\mathbf{a}
to the global basis element
G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})
.
For two expressions
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
, we define the
transition map

\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}:=\Psi_{\beta^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\Psi_{\beta}:\mathbb{N}^{r}\to\mathbb{N}^{r}.

Theorem 4.12
.

[
5
]
*Theorem 3.8
Let
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
be reduced expressions of
b\in{\operatorname{Br}^{+}}
.
Then the transition map
\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}
is given as follows.

(1)

(2-move)

If
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by a
2
-move exchanging positions
(k,k+1)
, then for

\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,

(4.12)

\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})_{i}=\begin{cases}a_{k+1},&i=k,\\
a_{k},&i=k+1,\\
a_{i},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2)

(3-move)

If
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by a
3
-move at positions
(k-1,k,k+1)=(i,j,i)
, then

(4.13)

\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})_{i}=\begin{cases}a_{k}+a_{k+1}-p,&i=k-1,\\
p,&i=k,\\
a_{k-1}+a_{k}-p,&i=k+1,\\
a_{i},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad p=\min\{a_{k-1},a_{k+1}\}.

Proof.

We treat the two cases separately.

Case (2-move).

If
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\beta
by swapping
(i_{k},i_{k+1})
with
c_{i_{k}i_{k+1}}=0
, then

P_{k}^{\beta}=P_{k+1}^{\beta^{\prime}},\qquad P_{k+1}^{\beta}=P_{k}^{\beta^{\prime}}.

Since the corresponding generators
q
-commute, it follows that

P^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})=P^{\beta^{\prime}}(\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})).

Comparing the expansions of PBW monomials in the global basis yields

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})=G^{\beta^{\prime}}(\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})).

Case (3-move).

Consider a
3
-move replacing the subword
\mathbf{j}=(i,j,i)
by
\mathbf{j}^{\prime}=(j,i,j)
.
In type
A_{2}
, the Lusztig parameters of the global basis of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}[0]

coincide with those of the quantum group
U_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{n}})
.
By
[
13
]
*Proposition 5.2, we have

(4.14)

G^{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{a})=G^{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\!\left(\Psi_{\mathbf{j}}^{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})\right),

where
\Psi_{\mathbf{j}}^{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}
is the Lusztig parameter transition map.

Write

\beta=(\beta_{<k-1},\mathbf{j},\beta_{>k+1}),\qquad\beta^{\prime}=(\beta_{<k-1},\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\beta_{>k+1}).

Assume first that
\mathbf{a}
is supported on the positions
\{k-1,k,k+1\}
.
Then

P^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})=T_{b_{<k-1}}\!\left(P^{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{a})\right),\qquad P^{\beta^{\prime}}\!\left(\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})\right)=T_{b_{<k-1}}\!\left(P^{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\!\left(\Psi_{\mathbf{j}}^{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})\right)\right).

Using the PBW-to-global expansion (
4.9
) and applying

we obtain expansions of both sides in the global basis with coefficients in

.
By
[
16
]
*Theorem 3.7, the operator
T_{b_{<k-1}}

preserves the global basis.
Since the expansion of a PBW monomial in the global basis is unique, we deduce

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})=T_{b_{<k-1}}\!\left(G^{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{a})\right),\qquad G^{\beta^{\prime}}\!\left(\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})\right)=T_{b_{<k-1}}\!\left(G^{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\!\left(\Psi_{\mathbf{j}}^{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})\right)\right).

Combining this with (
4.14
) yields

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})=G^{\beta^{\prime}}\!\left(\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})\right).

If
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{a})\cap\{k-1,k,k+1\}=\varnothing
, then

P_{i}^{\beta}=P_{i}^{\beta^{\prime}}
for all

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})=G^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})
.

For a general

, decompose

\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}_{<k-1}+\mathbf{a}_{[k-1,k+1]}+\mathbf{a}_{>k+1}.

Using multiplicativity of PBW monomials and the triangularity of the global basis
with respect to the PBW basis, we compare the global-basis expansions of

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}_{>k+1})\,G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}_{[k-1,k+1]})\,G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a}_{<k-1})\quad\text{and}\quad G^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a}_{>k+1})\,G^{\beta^{\prime}}\!\left(\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a}_{[k-1,k+1]})\right)\,G^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a}_{<k-1}).

Uniqueness of the global basis expansion again implies

G^{\beta}(\mathbf{a})=G^{\beta^{\prime}}\!\left(\Psi_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{a})\right),

which completes the proof of the
3
-move case.

∎

4.3.
Cluster structure on bosonic extension algebras

Definition 4.13
.

Let
\beta=(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})
be a word and fix integers

Theorem 4.14
.

*Theorem 9.7,

Let

be a braid group element and let

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
\beta,\beta^{\prime}
be two expressions of an element

.

(1)

If
\beta^{\prime}
is obtained from

by a

(2)

by a

Assertion (1) follows immediately from Theorem

We therefore prove (2).

If

.
In this case,

∎

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{s}}_{1}:=\{D^{1}_{k}\}
be the cluster obtained from

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
j=p_{1}

(5.10)

\mathbf{a}_{s}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(D^{1}_{(j,b_{1}+k)}\right)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }p_{s}=(j,t)\text{ and }b_{1}+2\leq t\leq b_{1}+k+1,\\[5.69054pt]

denotes the vertex corresponding to

in
\beta
.

Proof.

Note that
(j,b_{1}+1)=p_{1}
. By Proposition
5.8
, we have the exchange
relation

(5.11)

D^{1}_{(j,b_{1}+1)}\cdot D_{(j,b_{1}+1)}=D_{(j,b_{1}+2)}D_{(j,b_{1})}+\prod_{i\to j}D_{(i,k_{i})}.

Since

\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(D_{(j,b_{1}+2)}D_{(j,b_{1})}\right)=\mathbf{a}_{p_{1}}^{\beta}\!\left(D_{(j,b_{1}+2)}D_{(j,b_{1})}\right)=1

and

\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(\prod_{i\to j}D_{(i,k_{i})}\right)=0,

it follows from (

) that

(5.12)

\mathbf{a}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(D^{1}_{(j,b_{1}+1)}\right)=\mathbf{a}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(D_{(j,b_{1}+2)}D_{(j,b_{1})}\right)-\mathbf{a}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(D_{(j,b_{1}+1)}\right).

This proves (
5.10
) for

Now assume that (

) holds for all
k<l
, and we prove it for

(5.13)

As before,

\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(\prod_{i\to j}D_{(i,k_{i})}\right)=0,

hence

(5.14)

\mathbf{a}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(D^{1}_{(j,b_{1}+l)}\right)=\mathbf{a}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(D_{(j,b_{1}+l+1)}D^{1}_{(j,b_{1}+l-1)}\right)-\mathbf{a}^{\dot{\beta}^{m}}\!\left(D_{(j,b_{1}+l)}\right).

∎

Theorem 5.10

Proof.

(1) Since

Base case.

6.
Categorification of twisted products of flag varieties

In this section, we give a categorification of the coordinate ring

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\beta}]
.
Let
Q
be the Dynkin diagram associated with the Cartan matrix
C
.
We fix a
height function

\xi:I\to\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{such that}\quad|\xi(i)-\xi(j)|=1\ \text{if}\ d(i,j)=1.

Given
\xi
, we define a subset

\Delta^{\xi}\subset I\times\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{by}\quad\Delta^{\xi}=\{(i,p)\in I\times\mathbb{Z}\mid p-\xi(i)\in 2\mathbb{Z}\}.

6.1.
Hernandez–Leclerc’s category

Let
\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}
be the simple Lie algebra associated with
C
, and let

U_{q}(\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}})
be the corresponding quantum affine algebra.
For each
(i,a)\in I\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, there exists a simple

U_{q}(\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}})
-module
V_{i}(a)
, called a
fundamental module
.
For a fixed height function
\xi
, we set

\mathcal{S}:=\{V_{i}(q^{n})\mid(i,n)\in\Delta^{\xi}\},

and denote by
\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
the full subcategory of

U_{q}(\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}})
-modules generated by
\mathcal{S}
under tensor products,
extensions, and subquotients.
The category
\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is called the

Hernandez–Leclerc category
.
For simplicity, we write

V(i,p):=V_{i}(q^{p}).

The category
\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is rigid: for every object

M\in\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
there exist simple modules

\mathcal{D}(M)
and
\mathcal{D}^{-1}(M)
together with evaluation morphisms

M\otimes\mathcal{D}(M)\to\mathbf{1},\qquad\mathcal{D}^{-1}(M)\otimes M\to\mathbf{1},

and coevaluation morphisms

\mathbf{1}\to\mathcal{D}(M)\otimes M,\qquad\mathbf{1}\to M\otimes\mathcal{D}^{-1}(M),

where
\mathbf{1}
denotes the trivial representation.

Let
M,N\in\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be simple modules. We denote by
M\triangledown N
the head of module
M\otimes N
.
We define
\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
to be the sum of the orders of zeros at
z=1
of
the denominators
d_{M,N}(z)
and
d_{N,M}(z)
of the normalized
R
-matrices
for
M\otimes N
and
N\otimes M
, respectively.
A simple module
L\in\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
root simple module
if

\mathfrak{d}(L,\mathcal{D}^{k}L)=\delta_{k,\pm 1}.

Definition 6.1
.

A family of root simple modules

\mathbb{D}:=\{L_{i}^{\mathbb{D}}\}_{i\in I}

is called a
strong duality datum
if

\mathfrak{d}\bigl(L_{i}^{\mathbb{D}},\mathcal{D}^{k}(L_{j}^{\mathbb{D}})\bigr)=-\delta_{k,0}\,c_{ij}\qquad\text{for }i\neq j.

For an interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{Z}
, we define
\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{D}}[a,b]
to be the
full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
generated by

\{\mathcal{D}^{m}(L_{i}^{\mathbb{D}})\mid i\in I,\;m\in[a,b]\}

under tensor products, extensions, and subquotients.
A strong duality datum
\mathbb{D}
is said to be
complete
if

\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{D}}(-\infty,+\infty)=\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}.

For
j\in I
, we define a family

\mathscr{S}_{j}^{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbb{D}):=\{\mathscr{S}_{j}^{\mathbb{D}}(L_{i})\}_{i\in I},

where

\mathscr{S}_{j}^{\mathbb{D}}(L_{i})=\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}(L_{i}^{\mathbb{D}})&\text{if }i=j,\\[2.0pt]
L_{j}^{\mathbb{D}}\triangledown L_{i}^{\mathbb{D}}&\text{if }d(i,j)=1,\\[2.0pt]
L_{i}^{\mathbb{D}}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
\beta=(i_{1}\cdots i_{r})
be a word and
\mathbb{D}
a strong duality datum.
We define simple modules

C_{k}^{\mathbb{D},\beta}:=\mathscr{S}_{i_{1}}^{\mathbb{D}}\cdots\mathscr{S}_{i_{k-1}}^{\mathbb{D}}(L_{i_{k}}^{\mathbb{D}}),\qquad k\in[r].

Definition 6.2
.

For a strong duality datum
\mathbb{D}
and a word
\beta=(i_{1}\cdots i_{r})
, we define

\mathscr{C}(\beta)
to be the full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}

generated by
\{C_{k}^{\mathbb{D},\beta}\}_{k\in[r]}
under tensor products, extensions,
and subquotients.

By
[
19
]
*Corollary 3.22, the category

\mathscr{C}(\beta)
depends only on the braid group element

b\in{\operatorname{Br}^{+}}
represented by
\beta
.
Hence, if
\beta
is an expression of
b
, we denote this category by

\mathscr{C}(b)
.

Theorem 6.3
.

[
18
]
*Theorem 9.7
Let
\mathbb{D}
be a strong duality datum and
\beta
a word representing

b\in{\operatorname{Br}^{+}}
. Then the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathscr{C}(\beta))

admits a cluster algebra structure with initial seed
\mathbf{s}(\beta)
,
and the cluster monomials correspond to simple modules in
\mathscr{C}(b)
.
Moreover, the quantum Grothendieck ring
K_{t}(\mathscr{C}(\beta))
is
isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)_{\mathbb{K}}
.

Under this isomorphism, the isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathscr{C}(b)
are in bijection with the global basis elements of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b)
.
More precisely, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 6.4

(
[
16
,
19
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a strong duality datum, let
\beta=(i_{1}\cdots i_{r})
be a word, and let

M\in\mathscr{C}(\beta)
be a simple module.
Then there exists a unique sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in[r]}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that

M\simeq\operatorname{hd}\!\left(\overrightarrow{\otimes}_{k\in[r]}\left(C^{\mathbb{D},\beta}_{k}\right)^{\otimes a_{k}}\right),

where
\operatorname{hd}(N)
denotes the head of a module
N
and the tensor product is taken in the given order.
We call the tuple
(a_{k})_{k\in[r]}
the
\beta
-Lusztig parameter
of
M
, and denote it by

\mathbf{a}^{\beta}(M)
.

Under the canonical isomorphism

K_{t}(\mathscr{C}(b))\;\cong\;\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(b),

the class of
M
corresponds to the global basis element

G(\mathbf{a}^{\beta}(M))
.

6.2.
Categorifications of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{v,b}
.

Let
v\leq\delta(b)
, and let
\dot{\beta}_{v}
be the associated infinite sequence of vertices.
We define
\mathscr{C}^{v}
to be the full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
generated by

\{C_{k}^{\mathbb{D},\dot{\beta}_{v}}\}_{k\in[\ell(v)+1,+\infty)}

and closed under tensor products, extensions, and subquotients.
We then define

\mathscr{C}_{v,\beta}:=\mathscr{C}(\beta)\cap\mathscr{C}^{v}.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
b\in{\operatorname{Br}^{+}}
be a braid group element, let
v\leq\delta(b)
, and let
\beta
be a word for
b
.
Then the category
\mathscr{C}_{v,\beta}
provides a monoidal categorification of the
cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{s}(v,\beta))
. More precisely,

\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{s}(v,\beta))\subset K_{0}(\mathscr{C}_{v,\beta}),

and cluster monomials correspond to the classes of simple objects in

\mathscr{C}_{v,\beta}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.10
, we have

\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\dot{\beta}_{v}}\bigl(D_{k}^{\ell(v)}\bigr)=0\quad\text{for all }i\in[\ell(v)],

for every cluster variable in the initial seed
\mathbf{s}(v,\beta)
.
Moreover, when we mutate at a vertex
k
, the absence of arrows between mutable
variables and the deleted variables implies that this property is preserved under
mutation. Consequently,

\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\dot{\beta}_{v}}(x)=0\quad\text{for all }i\in[\ell(v)],

for any cluster variable
x
in the cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{s}(v,\beta))
.

Since any global basis element in
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{v,\beta}
corresponds to a simple
module in
\mathscr{C}_{\beta}
, Theorem
6.4
together with
Proposition
5.2
implies that the simple modules
corresponding to cluster variables in
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{s}(v,\beta))

lie in
\mathscr{C}_{v,\beta}
.
Hence,

\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{s}(v,\beta))\subset K_{0}(\mathscr{C}_{v,\beta}).

Finally, by Theorem
6.4
and Lemma
4.9
,
any simple module
L\in\mathscr{C}(b)
corresponding to a cluster monomial
in
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbf{s}(v,\beta))
also lies in
\mathscr{C}_{v,\beta}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

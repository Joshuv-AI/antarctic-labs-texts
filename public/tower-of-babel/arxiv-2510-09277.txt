On character tables for fusion systems
Thomas Lawrence, Jason Semeraro

Abstract
. A character table X X for a saturated fusion system ℱ \mathcal{F} on a finite p p -group S S is the square matrix of values associated to a basis of virtual ℱ \mathcal{F} -stable ordinary characters of S S . We investigate a conjecture of the second author which equates the p p -part of | | det ( X ) | 2 (X)|^{2} with the product of the orders of S S -centralisers of fully ℱ \mathcal{F} -centralised ℱ \mathcal{F} -class representatives. This statement is exactly column orthogonality for the character table of S S when ℱ = ℱ S ​ ( S ) \mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(S) . We prove the conjecture when ℱ = ℱ S ​ ( G ) \mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G) is realised by some finite group G G with Sylow p p -subgroup S S , and for all simple fusion systems when | S | ≤ p 4 |S|\leq p^{4} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion system on a finite
p
-group
S
and let
\mathcal{F}^{z}\subseteq S
be a set of
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy class representatives of fully
\mathcal{F}
-centralised elements. Let
B
be any basis for the ring of virtual
\mathcal{F}
-stable ordinary characters of
S
. Since
|B|=|\mathcal{F}^{z}|
(see
[
3
, Lem. 2.1]
) we may write

X=X_{B}(\mathcal{F})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}),\qquad X_{\chi,s}=\chi(s)

for the corresponding
character table
of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
B
.

Motivated by supporting computational evidence, the second author was led to conjecture the following:

Conjecture 1.1

(Semeraro)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion system on a finite
p
-group
S
and
B
be a basis for the ring of virtual
\mathcal{F}
-stable ordinary characters of
S
. If
\mathcal{F}
is saturated then

|X_{B}(\mathcal{F})\overline{X_{B}(\mathcal{F})^{T}}|_{p}=\prod_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}|C_{S}(s)|.

Here it is implicit that
|X_{B}(\mathcal{F})\overline{X_{B}(\mathcal{F})}^{T}|\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and this follows quickly from the well-known fact that
\mathcal{F}
is always realised by a finite group in which
S
is not necessarily Sylow (see Remark
3.1
). It is then clear that the left hand side of the displayed equality is independent of the choice of
B
. Note also that the conjecture is equivalent to the orthogonality of columns in the character table of
S
when
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(S)
. More generally, the following is true:

Theorem 1.2

(Lawrence–Olsson)
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds when
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
is the fusion system of a finite group
G
in which
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup.

In Section
3
we present a proof of Theorem
1.2
, using elementary applications of Brauer’s characterisation of characters and column orthogonality for
G
. A version of this proof first appeared in
[
12
]
, and Benjamin Sambale subsequently pointed out that a similar observation had already been made by Olsson in
[
14
]
using
\pi
-blocks. It is worth remarking that all proofs use that
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
in an essential way; indeed the saturation hypothesis in Conjecture
1.1
is necessary (see Example
2.6
).

Thus to prove Conjecture
1.1
we may assume that
\mathcal{F}
is exotic. Recall that
\mathcal{F}
is
transitive
on
S
if
\mathcal{F}^{z}=\{1,z\}
for some
1\neq z\in Z(S)
. For such a fusion system we may take
B=\{1_{S},{\operatorname{reg}}_{S}-1_{S}\}
where
{\operatorname{reg}}_{S}
denotes the regular character of
S
(which is always
\mathcal{F}
-stable) so that
X_{B}(\mathcal{F})=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
|S|-1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and Conjecture
1.1
holds trivially. This shows, in particular, that Conjecture
1.1
holds for the three Ruiz-Viruel exotic fusion systems on
7
-groups of order
7^{3}
appearing in
[
17
]
. Thus by
[
17
, Thm 1.1]
, Conjecture
1.1
holds if
|S|=p^{3}
, so we may also assume that
|S|\geq p^{4}
. Our second main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds if
\mathcal{F}
is simple and
|S|\leq p^{4}
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a simple saturated fusion system on a
p
-group
S
of order
p^{4}
. If
p=2
then
\mathcal{F}
is realisable by
[
1
, Thm. 4.1]
so we may assume that
p\geq 3
. For such
p
, the list of possibilities for
\mathcal{F}
was compiled by Moragues Moncho in
[
13
]
and this is recalled in Section
4
. There are three such systems, all on a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
{\operatorname{PSp}}_{4}(p)
, two of which are exotic and referred to here as
O^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{(1)})
and
\mathcal{G}
(see Section
4
for details). For
p\geq 7
these are the only exotic examples which occur, whereas for
p=3,5
some further exceptional cases arise. To verify Conjecture
1.1
we apply an inductive approach which we expect to work more broadly for fusion systems on
p
-groups which contain an abelian subgroup of index
p
. Briefly, we choose a suitable realisable subsystem
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and construct a basis
B_{\mathcal{F}}
of
\mathcal{F}
-stable characters by taking linear combinations of an
\mathcal{N}
-stable basis
B_{\mathcal{N}}
of
\mathcal{N}
-stable characters. Row operations on
X_{B_{\mathcal{N}}}(\mathcal{N})
then allow us to express
|X_{B_{\mathcal{N}}}(\mathcal{N})|
in terms of
|X_{B_{\mathcal{F}}}(\mathcal{F})|
in such a way that Conjecture
1.1
for
\mathcal{F}
follows from Theorem
1.2
for
\mathcal{N}
(see Proposition
2.5
).

Structure of the paper:
We start by recalling some background results on
\mathcal{F}
-stable characters in Section
2
. Theorem
1.2
is proved in Section
3
, following the approach taken in
[
12
]
. In Section
4
, we introduce notation to describe simple exotic fusion systems on
p
-groups of order
p^{4}
, and recall their classification (Theorem
4.1
). Theorem
1.3
is then established via a case-by-case check in Section
5
.

2.
The ring of
\mathcal{F}
-stable virtual characters

For a fusion system
\mathcal{F}
on
S
, let
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
denote the subring of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\operatorname{Irr}}(S)]
consisting of
\mathcal{F}
-stable virtual characters. We say that a character
\chi\in{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
is
indecomposable
if whenever
\chi=\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}
for a pair of characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
we have
\chi=\chi_{1}
or
\chi_{2}
. Following
[
18
]
, write
{\operatorname{Ind}}(\mathcal{F})
for the set of indecomposable characters of
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
. The following is an elementary consequence of this definition.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have,
\langle{\operatorname{Ind}}(\mathcal{F})\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}={\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

Any character
\chi\in{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
which does not lie in the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span of
{\operatorname{Ind}}(\mathcal{F})
must contain an
\mathcal{F}
-indecomposable constituent with the same property, a contradiction.
∎

Let
\mathbf{k}(\mathcal{F})
denote the the number of
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy classes of elements of
S
. By
[
3
, Lem. 2.1]
,
{\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}})=\mathbf{k}(\mathcal{F})
, and in
[
18
, Thm. 1]
it is shown that
\mathbf{k}(\mathcal{F})\leq|{\operatorname{Ind}}(\mathcal{F})|
. Using Brauer’s characterisation of characters, one can easily establish the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
for some finite group
G
with Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
. Then
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}={\mathbb{Z}}[\chi|_{S}\mid\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)]
.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
18
, Thm. 4]
.
∎

In the special case when
S\unlhd G
, we have the following stronger result, which is an elementary consequence of Clifford theory:

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
G
is finite group with a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
and let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
. Then
{\operatorname{Ind}}(\mathcal{F})=\{\chi|_{S}\mid\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)\}.
In particular,
|{\operatorname{Ind}}(\mathcal{F})|=\mathbf{k}(\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

See
[
18
, Thm. 2]
.
∎

If
L
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-lattice, its
volume

{\operatorname{Vol}}(L)
is the modulus of the determinant of a basis. We require the following basic fact concerning this invariant:

Lemma 2.4
.

If
M\subseteq L
are
{\mathbb{Z}}
-lattices of equal rank then as abelian groups
|L:M|={\operatorname{Vol}}(L)/{\operatorname{Vol}}(M)
.

Proof.

See
[
8
, Lem. 1]
.
∎

The following application of Lemma
2.4
is the main technical result that we will use to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a saturated fusion system on
S
, and let
\mathcal{N}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}
be a saturated subsystem on
S
for which Conjecture
1.1
holds. Let
B_{\mathcal{N}}
be an
\mathcal{N}
-stable basis of characters for
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{N}}
and

B_{\mathcal{F}}
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearly independent set of
\mathcal{F}
-stable characters of size
\mathbf{k}(\mathcal{F})
given by linear combinations of
B_{\mathcal{N}}
. Suppose, moreover, that:

(a)

\mathbf{k}(\mathcal{F})+1=\mathbf{k}(\mathcal{N})
and there exists
\mathbf{u}\in S
and
\mathbf{z}\in Z(S)
such that
\mathbf{u}^{\mathcal{N}}\neq\mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{N}}
,
\mathbf{u}^{\mathcal{F}}=\mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{F}}
and
|C_{S}(\mathbf{u})|=p^{2}
.

(b)

There exists a subset
X_{\mathcal{N}}\subsetneq B_{\mathcal{N}}
and a bijection
\Phi:X_{\mathcal{N}}\rightarrow B_{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\chi
occurs with multiplicity
1
in
\Phi(\chi)
for each
\chi\in X_{\mathcal{N}}
, the unique element
\eta\in B_{\mathcal{N}}\backslash X_{\mathcal{N}}
satisfies
\eta(\mathbf{u})-\eta(\mathbf{z})=\pm p
and
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\langle\eta\rangle\subsetneq{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{N}}.

Then
B_{\mathcal{F}}
is a basis for
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
and Conjecture
1.1
holds for
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Since
|C_{S}(\mathbf{u})|=p^{2}
and
\mathbf{z}
and
\mathbf{u}
are the only
\mathcal{N}
-classes fused in
\mathcal{F}
(by the assumption in part (a)),

p^{2}\cdot\prod_{s\in S^{\mathcal{F}}}|C_{S}(s)|=\prod_{s\in S^{\mathcal{N}}}|C_{S}(s)|.

Thus, to prove Conjecture
1.1
holds for
\mathcal{F}
, it suffices to show that
B_{\mathcal{F}}
is an
\mathcal{F}
-stable basis for which

(2.1)

|X_{B_{\mathcal{N}}}(\mathcal{N})|=\pm p\cdot|X_{B_{\mathcal{F}}}(\mathcal{F})|.

The condition in part (b) implies that there exists a sequence of elementary row operations which transforms the matrix
X_{B_{\mathcal{N}}}(\mathcal{N})
into a matrix
X^{\prime}
which contains
X_{B_{\mathcal{F}}}(\mathcal{F})
as minor with respect to the position
(\eta,\mathbf{u})
. Since all elements of
B_{\mathcal{F}}
are
\mathcal{F}
-stable, subtracting the column corresponding to
\mathbf{z}
from that corresponding to
\mathbf{u}
in
X^{\prime}
produces a column of zeros, except for the
(\eta,\mathbf{u})
position which has value
\eta(\mathbf{u})-\eta(\mathbf{z})=\pm p
. Taking the determinant of the resulting matrix now delivers (
2.1
).

It remains to prove that
B_{\mathcal{F}}
is a basis of
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
. Since
B_{\mathcal{N}}
is a basis of
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{N}}
, (
2.1
) also shows that

{\operatorname{Vol}}({\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{N}})=p{\operatorname{Vol}}(\langle B_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle).

As abelian groups, we have

\langle B_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle\oplus\langle\eta\rangle\subseteq{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\langle\eta\rangle\subsetneq{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{N}}
. Thus taking volumes and using that
{\operatorname{Vol}}(\langle B_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle)
divides
{\operatorname{Vol}}(\langle B_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle\oplus\langle\eta\rangle)
we obtain:

{\operatorname{Vol}}(\langle B_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle\oplus\langle\eta\rangle)={\operatorname{Vol}}({\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\langle\eta\rangle)

by Lemma
2.4
. Thus
{\operatorname{Vol}}(\langle B_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle)={\operatorname{Vol}}({\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}})
, so
\langle B_{\mathcal{F}}\rangle={\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
and
B_{\mathcal{F}}
is a basis for
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}
, as needed.
∎

We close this section by showing that the conclusion of Conjecture
1.1
is false for non-saturated fusion systems.

Example 2.6
.

Suppose
S=\langle\mathbf{a}\rangle\cong C_{8}
, set
P:=\langle\mathbf{a}^{2}\rangle
and
\mathcal{F}:=\langle\mathcal{F}_{S}(S);{\operatorname{Aut}}(P)\rangle_{S}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is not saturated since the non-trivial element in
{\operatorname{Aut}}(P)
which fuses
\mathbf{a}^{2}
to
\mathbf{a}^{6}
does not extend to
S
. Let
\omega:=\exp(2\pi i/8)
and let
\chi_{j}\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(S)
be given by
\chi_{j}(\mathbf{a}^{k}):=\omega^{jk}
for
0\leq j\leq 7
. One easily calculates that:

{\operatorname{Ind}}(\mathcal{F})=\{\chi_{0},\chi_{2},\chi_{4},\chi_{6},\chi_{1}+\chi_{3},\chi_{3}+\chi_{5},\chi_{5}+\chi_{7},\chi_{7}+\chi_{1}\}

and so
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}}=\langle B\rangle
where
B
consists of the first
7
of these characters by Lemma
2.1
. Now,

|X_{B}(\mathcal{F})\overline{X_{B}(\mathcal{F})^{T}}|=2^{22}\neq 2^{21}=\prod_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}|C_{S}(s)|.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We follow the approach taken in
[
12
]
, pointing out some overlap with that taken by Olsson in
[
14
]
. Let
G
be a finite group, fix a (not necessarily Sylow)
p
-subgroup
S
of
G
and let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
be the associated fusion system. Let
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)={\mathbb{Z}}[{\operatorname{Irr}}(S)]
be the ring of virtual
S
-characters and
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{G}:={\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)}
be the subring consisting of those which are
G
-stable. Let
n=|S^{G}|
be the number of
G
-classes of elements in
S
and set
m=|{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)|
. Note that
{\operatorname{rk}}_{\mathbb{Z}}({\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{G})=n
by the discussion in Section
2
.

Fix a basis
B
of
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)^{G}
and let
\mathcal{F}^{z}
be a set of fully
\mathcal{F}
-centralised
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy class representatives of
S
. Thus if
\rho
is an
\mathcal{F}
-stable character of
S
, we may write

\rho=\sum_{\psi\in B}\lambda_{\rho,\psi}\psi

for unique
\lambda_{\rho,\psi}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
depending on
B
. Let
D=D_{B}(G)\in M_{m\times n}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
X=X_{B}(G)\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be the matrices given for
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(G),\psi\in B
and
s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}
by:

(D_{B}(G))_{\chi,\psi}:=\lambda_{\chi|_{S},\psi}\mbox{ and }(X_{B}(G))_{\psi,s}:=\psi(s).

Since
(DX)_{\chi,s}=\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in B}\lambda_{\chi|_{S},\psi}\psi(s)=\chi(s),

(3.1)

(\overline{(DX)^{T}}DX)_{s,t}=\sum_{\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)}\overline{\chi(s)}\chi(t)=\delta_{st}|C_{G}(s)|

by column orthogonality. Thus writing
C:=\overline{D^{T}}D
, we have
|\overline{(DX)^{T}}DX|=|C|\cdot|X\overline{X^{T}}|,
and so

(3.2)

|X\overline{X^{T}}|=\frac{1}{|C|}\prod_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}|C_{G}(s)|.

Remark 3.1
.

As was first noted in
[
12
, Cor. 3.18]
, since
|C|\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, (
3.2
) implies that
|X\overline{X^{T}}|
is a rational algebraic integer, and hence an integer.

We assume for the remainder of this section that
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. The next result is essentially
[
14
, Folg. 6.9]
; here we provide a self-contained proof.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
then
(|C|,p)=1
. Consequently, Theorem
1.2
holds.

Proof.

Writing
\Delta
for the diagonal matrix
\Delta_{s,t}=\delta_{s,t}|C_{G}(s)|
for
s,t\in\mathcal{F}^{z}
, (
3.3
) implies

\overline{X^{T}}CX=\overline{X^{T}}\overline{D^{T}}DX=\overline{(DX)^{T}}DX=\Delta

so that
C^{-1}=X\Delta^{-1}\overline{X^{T}}
and explicitly for
\psi,\phi\in B
we have,

(C^{-1})_{\psi,\phi}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}\frac{\psi(s)\overline{\phi(s)}}{|C_{G}(s)|}.

For
\psi\in B
write
\psi_{0}
for the
G
-class function given by

\psi_{0}(g):=\begin{cases}|G|_{p^{\prime}}\psi(s)&\mbox{ if $g$ is $G$-conjugate to $s\in S$ }\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}.

Then
\psi_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)]
by Brauer’s characterisation of characters. Indeed, if
E
is
p
-elementary then
E=O_{p}(E)\times O_{p^{\prime}}(E)
and conjugating
\psi_{0}
if necessary (using that
\psi
is
G
-stable) we may assume that
O_{p}(E)\leq S
since
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then for any
\rho\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(E)
,

\langle\psi_{0}|_{E},\rho\rangle=\frac{1}{|E|}\sum_{g\in E}\psi_{0}(g)\overline{\rho(g)}=\frac{1}{|E|}\sum_{g\in O_{p}(E)}|G|_{p^{\prime}}\psi(g)\overline{\rho(g)}=\frac{|G|_{p^{\prime}}}{|O_{p^{\prime}}(E)|}\langle\psi|_{O_{p}(E)},\rho|_{O_{p}(E)}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Calculating, we obtain:

|G|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}(C^{-1})_{\psi,\phi}=|G|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}\frac{\psi(s)\overline{\phi}(s)}{|C_{G}(s)|}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}\frac{\psi_{0}(s)\overline{\phi_{0}(s)}}{|C_{G}(s)|}=\langle\psi_{0},\phi_{0}\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Hence
|G_{p^{\prime}}|^{2}C^{-1}\in M_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
from which it follows that
(|C|,p)=1
.
Finally, for
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
,
C_{S}(s)\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(C_{G}(s))
if and only if
s
is fully
\mathcal{F}
-centralised. Thus by (
3.2
) we have

|X\overline{X^{T}}|_{p}=\prod_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}|C_{G}(s)|_{p}=\prod_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}|C_{S}(s)|,

as required.

∎

3.1.
Characters in the principal block

There is an analogue
D_{0}
of
D
for the principal
p
-block
B_{0}
of
G
given by restricting its rows to
{\operatorname{Irr}}(B_{0})
. Let
X_{B}(G)
be as above and let
D_{0}=D_{B}(B_{0})
be given for
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B_{0})
, and
\psi\in B
by
(D_{B}(B_{0}))_{\chi,\psi}:=\lambda_{\chi|_{S},\psi}
as before. Set
C_{0}=\overline{D_{0}^{T}}D_{0}
. Using orthogonality of characters in
B_{0}
, we show the following:

Proposition 3.3
.

For
D_{0}=D_{B}(B_{0})
as above, we have:

(3.3)

(\overline{(D_{0}X)^{T}}D_{0}X)_{s,t}=\delta_{st}\dim(B_{0}(s))

where
B_{0}(s)
is the principal
p
-block of
C_{G}(s)
. Consequently
(|C_{0}|,p)=1
.

Proof.

Since
(D_{0}X)_{\chi,s}=\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in B}\lambda_{\chi|_{S},\psi}\psi(s)=\chi(s),

(\overline{(D_{0}X)^{T}}D_{0}X)_{s,t}=\sum_{\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B_{0})}\overline{\chi(s)}\chi(t)=\delta_{st}\dim(B_{0}(s))

by a refinement of block orthogonality (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Thm. 2.1]
).
Now,

\frac{1}{|C|}\prod_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}|C_{G}(s)|=|X\overline{X^{T}}|=\frac{1}{|C_{0}|}\prod_{s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}}\dim(B_{0}(s)).

Since
(|C|,p)=1
by Proposition
3.2
and since
v_{\ell}(\dim(B_{0}(s)))=v_{\ell}(|C_{G}(s))|)
, for each
s\in\mathcal{F}^{z}
we must also have
(|C_{0}|,p)=1
.
∎

4.
Simple exotic fusion systems on
p
-groups of order
p^{4}

For an odd prime
p
, in this section we introduce notation to describe fusion systems on a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
{\operatorname{PSp}}_{4}(p)
. Let

V:=\{f(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x,y]\mid\deg(f)=2\}=\langle\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{3}\rangle,\mbox{ for }\mathbf{v}_{1}=x^{2},\hskip 5.69054pt\mathbf{v}_{2}=xy\mbox{ and }\mathbf{v}_{3}=y^{2},

and regard
V
as an
({\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(p))
-module via the action

f(x,y)\cdot\left(\lambda,\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\right)=\lambda\cdot f(ax+by,cx+dy).

Then setting
U:=\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle
for
\mathbf{u}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, define
S:=V\rtimes U
so that
S
is isomorphic to a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
{\operatorname{PSp}}_{4}(p)
. Let
\mathbf{z}:=\mathbf{v}_{1}
(so that
\langle\mathbf{z}\rangle=Z(S)
) and
E:=\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u}\rangle\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
. Let
\chi:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
be the unique non-trivial homomorphism, define subgroups of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(p)

H:=\{(\det(A)^{-1},A)\mid A\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(p)\}\mbox{ and }H^{*}:=\{(\chi(\det(A))\cdot\det(A)^{-1},A)\mid A\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(p)\}

and observe that neither of these acts faithfully on
V
(scalar matrices act trivially) and so their images
\overline{H}
and
\overline{H^{*}}

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_{(1)}
as above, observe that

{\operatorname{reg}}_{S}=1_{S}+\theta_{p-1}+\chi(\psi_{1,0,0})+\chi(\psi_{1,0,0},\rho_{p-1})+p\chi(\psi_{1,0,\epsilon})+p\chi(\psi_{0,1,0}),

denotes the regular representation of

. This, together with
[

, implies that

is a factorial fusion system with

. However, while the

]
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Suppose

. There are exactly three non-trivial orbits of

on

,

Since

,
-1\notin\langle\lambda^{2}\rangle

given in the proof of Lemma
5.4
for

. There can be no further orbits by counting, and the lemma follows.
∎

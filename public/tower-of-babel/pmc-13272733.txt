Development and validation of an interpretable prognostic model for bladder cancer based on lactylation associated genes using SHAP analysis
Authors: Yongqi Wang, Xiao Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective The high recurrence rate and poor clinical outcomes of bladder cancer (BLCA) underscore the urgent need for novel biomarkers to guide precision medicine. Focusing on lactylation—a novel epigenetic modification—this study aimed to construct and validate a prognostic model for BLCA based on lactylation-related genes.Methods Transcriptomic and clinical data were integrated from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, GSE13507) and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-BLCA) cohorts. Core prognostic genes were identified, and a risk scoring model was constructed utilizing a stepwise framework comprising univariate Cox regression, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression, and multivariate Cox regression. The SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) algorithm was employed for personalized, interpretable survival predictions. Subsequently, the associations between the risk model and the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), tumor mutational burden (TMB), and drug sensitivity were comprehensively evaluated.Results A 4-gene risk model comprising ATAD3A, MKI67, VWF, and CCL2 was ultimately established. The model demonstrated robust predictive efficacy in both the training cohort (1-, 3-, and 5-year AUCs of 0.802, 0.818, and 0.856, respectively) and the validation cohort (AUCs of 0.636, 0.601, and 0.627, respectively). Multivariate analysis confirmed that the risk score served as an independent prognostic factor. The SHAP analysis facilitated precise interpretability, bridging global feature importance with individual mortality risk. Immune infiltration profiling revealed a significant enrichment of M2 macrophages coupled with high CCL2 expression in the high-risk group. Furthermore, combined survival analysis indicated that patients with a "low TMB and high-risk" profile exhibited the poorest clinical outcomes.ConclusionTo our knowledge, this study is the first to establish a 4-gene lactylation-related prognostic model for BLCA integrating a LASSO-SHAP framework. This model not only provides precise, visualizable predictions for individualized survival risk but also elucidates the lactylation-driven immunosuppressive microenvironment in BLCA, offering crucial data support for the development of personalized immunotherapeutic and targeted strategies.Keywords: Bladder cancer, Lactylation, Prognostic model, SHAP analysis, Tumor microenvironment

Introduction

Bladder cancer (BLCA) ranks as the tenth most frequently diagnosed malignancy worldwide and the second most common urological cancer [1, 2]. According to statistics from the World Health Organization (WHO), approximately 550,000 new cases and 200,000 BLCA-related deaths were reported globally in 2018 [3]. Notably, regions such as North America and Europe exhibit higher incidence rates, reaching 11.3 and 11.9 per 100,000 individuals, respectively. The alarmingly high incidence and recurrence rates of BLCA have garnered widespread clinical attention. Clinically, BLCA is primarily classified into non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) and muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC). Approximately 25% of patients will eventually progress to the muscle-invasive stage as the disease advances [4]. Currently, the primary therapeutic modalities for BLCA encompass neoadjuvant chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy, and radical cystectomy. Despite these comprehensive interventions, a substantial proportion of patients still experiences tumor recurrence and distant metastasis, leading to a dismal survival prognosis. Consequently, there is an urgent need to establish novel prognostic evaluation systems for BLCA, thereby guiding clinicians in formulating more precise and individualized therapeutic strategies.

Lactylation is a recently identified novel post-translational modification (PTM) that extensively participates in critical biological processes, including glycolysis-associated cellular functions, macrophage polarization, vascular physiology, mitochondrial regulation, and neurological modulation [5–7]. Mechanistically, lactylation can be broadly categorized into enzymatic and non-enzymatic pathways. Enzymatic lactylation utilizes lactyl-CoA as the substrate donor and relies on specific "writers," such as p300 and CREB-binding protein (CBP). This modification predominantly occurs on histone lysine residues, facilitating the activation of downstream gene expression. Conversely, non-enzymatic lactylation operates independently of specific enzymes; it involves the generation of lactoylglutathione via the methylglyoxal metabolic pathway, which subsequently reacts directly with protein lysine residues. This non-enzymatic process is typically associated with inflammatory responses and oxidative stress [8]. During metabolic reprogramming, tumor cells preferentially rely on aerobic glycolysis for energy production—a hallmark phenomenon known as the Warburg effect—which inevitably results in substantial lactate accumulation. This metabolic shift fundamentally amplifies lactate-driven lactylation, thereby profoundly influencing tumor migration, cellular differentiation, and microenvironmental adaptation. Emerging evidence underscores the pivotal role of lactylation across various malignancies. For instance, significantly elevated levels of histone lactylation have been observed in uveal melanoma, directly promoting tumor growth [9]. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), exogenous lactate treatment induces a surge in histone lactylation, significantly enhancing tumor proliferative capacity [10]. Similarly, in breast cancer, lactylation modulates the expression of specific oncogenes, thereby deeply orchestrating tumorigenesis and progression [11]. Furthermore, in colorectal cancer, lactate has been shown to accelerate tumor cell proliferation by fostering tumor immune evasion [12].

Recent studies have begun to elucidate the role of lactylation in bladder cancer (BLCA). For instance, the circRNA XRN2-Hippo signaling axis has been shown to suppress glycolysis and lactate production, subsequently downregulating histone H3K18 lactylation and LCN2 expression to inhibit tumor progression [13]. However, the overall regulatory network of lactylation and its comprehensive crosstalk with patient survival in BLCA warrant further in-depth investigation. Meanwhile, high-throughput sequencing data mining and robust bioinformatic modeling [14–16] have been widely utilized to identify prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets across various malignancies, including lung and renal cancers [17, 18]. Driven by the urgent clinical need to improve BLCA outcomes through precise risk stratification, this study systematically investigated the prognostic significance of lactylation-related genes. We first analyzed the differential expression of lactylation-related genes between BLCA and adjacent normal tissues. Subsequently, we constructed a robust prognostic model integrating Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression, multivariate Cox analysis, and the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) framework for personalized interpretability. Based on the established risk-scoring system, comprehensive downstream evaluations—including independent prognostic validation, immune infiltration profiling, tumor mutational burden (TMB) analysis, and chemotherapeutic sensitivity prediction—were systematically performed. Ultimately, this study aims to provide a robust theoretical foundation and practical guidance for individualized diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in patients with BLCA.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and processing

In this study, comprehensive gene expression profiles and corresponding clinical information for patients with bladder cancer (BLCA) were acquired from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA, https://gdcportal.nci.nih.gov/). The GSE13507 dataset, encompassing 165 patients with primary BLCA, was selected as the training cohort. Furthermore, sequencing data from 412 primary BLCA tumor tissues and 19 adjacent normal bladder tissues retrieved from the TCGA database were designated as the independent validation cohort to assess the generalizability and robustness of the established prognostic model. The transcriptomic data from the GEO dataset were obtained in a pre-normalized microarray format. Concurrently, the RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data retrieved from the TCGA database were normalized using the Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads (FPKM) method. Data extraction and fundamental processing were performed utilizing Perl scripts (version 5.30.2) to isolate messenger RNA (mRNA) and long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) expression profiles. Additionally, somatic mutation data for patients with BLCA were directly downloaded from the TCGA data portal. Given that all data utilized in this comprehensive investigation were retrieved from publicly available repositories (GEO and TCGA), informed consent and institutional ethical approval were fundamentally waived. Identification of lactylation-related genes

To identify lactylation-related genes, a systematic search was conducted in the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) utilizing the keyword "Lactylation." To ensure a robust functional association, genes with a relevance score ≥ 2 were defined as the inclusion criterion. Considering the inherent transcriptomic heterogeneity during tumorigenesis and progression, differential expression analysis between adjacent normal and BLCA tissues from the TCGA cohort was performed utilizing the R package “limma”. The significance thresholds for identifying differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were set as a false discovery rate (FDR, adjusted by the Benjamini–Hochberg method) < 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) > 2. Subsequently, the identified DEGs were intersected with the aforementioned lactylation-related genes to delineate BLCA-specific, lactylation-associated candidate genes. To visually comprehensively represent these expression patterns, the R package “pheatmap” was employed to generate heatmaps illustrating the top 50 significantly upregulated and downregulated candidate genes, respectively. Finally, the expression matrices of these intersecting candidate genes were extracted from both the GEO and TCGA cohorts to facilitate the subsequent construction of the survival prognostic model.

Functional enrichment analyses and construction of the PPI network

To elucidate the underlying biological mechanisms of the lactylation-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs), Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were conducted. These analyses were executed utilizing the R packages “clusterProfiler”, “org.Hs.eg.db”, “enrichplot”, and “ggplot2”. A threshold of P-value < 0.05 was established to define statistical significance. Specifically, the GO enrichment analysis categorized the significant functional terms into three principal dimensions: biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). Concurrently, the KEGG analysis identified significantly enriched signaling pathways. To intuitively present these findings, the top 30 most significantly enriched terms for both GO and KEGG analyses were comprehensively visualized. To further delineate the physical and functional interactions among these lactylation-related proteins, and to clarify their potential roles in complex biological functions and cellular signal transduction, a Protein–Protein Interaction (PPI) network was constructed. This was accomplished by mapping the candidate genes to the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (STRING) online database (https://string-db.org/). To ensure the reliability of the network topology, the minimum required interaction score was set to a high confidence threshold of 0.70.

Construction of the lactylation-related prognostic risk model

The R package “limma” was employed to systematically merge the expression profiles of lactylation-related genes with the corresponding survival data from both the GEO and TCGA cohorts. Samples lacking complete clinical annotation were excluded from the subsequent analyses. Subsequently, the R package “survival” was utilized to perform univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis. Genes exhibiting a statistically significant association with overall survival (P < 0.05) were initially identified as potential prognostic candidates. To mitigate the risk of overfitting and eliminate high-dimensional data redundancy, a Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis was executed for precise feature selection. By integrating critical clinical parameters, including survival time and vital status, a tenfold cross-validation algorithm was implemented. The optimal penalty parameter (λ) was determined at the point where the partial likelihood deviance reached its minimum, as visually depicted by the λ trajectory curve. Following the LASSO dimensionality reduction, a stepwise multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed on the retained candidates to confirm their independent prognostic value, with the hazard ratios visually presented via a forest plot. Ultimately, a core set of key genes was identified to establish a robust prognostic risk scoring model for patients with BLCA. Validation of the lactylation-related prognostic risk model

To comprehensively characterize the spatial distribution of samples across different risk strata, advanced dimensionality reduction techniques, encompassing Principal Component Analysis (PCA), t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE), and Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), were employed. To robustly validate the efficacy, discriminatory capacity, and reliability of the established prognostic model, rigorous independent evaluations were conducted in both the training and validation cohorts. The model's predictive performance was assessed from three pivotal dimensions: the Concordance Index (C-index), Kaplan–Meier (K-M) survival analysis, and time-dependent Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves. All statistical analyses and visual representations in this section were implemented utilizing the R packages “survival”, “survminer”, and “timeROC”.

SHAP interpretability analysis and expression profiling of core genes

Although conventional multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression provides global interpretability through regression coefficients (β), thereby characterizing overall risk tendencies at the population level, it does not readily quantify the individualized contribution of each prognostic gene to the risk score of a specific patient. To translate population-level statistical associations into an individualized decision-support tool for precision medicine, we incorporated SHAP, a cooperative game theory–based method [19]. In this context, SHAP does not modify the linear predictor of the Cox model; rather, it enhances local interpretability by estimating marginal contributions, enabling precise quantification of how aberrant expression of a given gene within an individual patient shifts the final predicted risk.

SHAP-based interpretability analysis of the established multivariate Cox prognostic model was performed in R using the “kernelshap” and “shapviz” packages. Briefly, SHAP decomposes the model output for an individual sample (log-hazard ratio) into an additive linear combination of feature attributions. Assuming S core prognostic genes, for the n—th BLCA patient Xn, the observed expression value of the j—th gene feature is denoted as Xnj. SHAP estimates the attribution of each feature by enumerating all possible feature coalitions, computing the marginal contribution of adding the feature to a coalition, and then aggregating these marginal effects using Shapley kernel weights. The resulting SHAP value represents the independent contribution of gene j to the predicted risk for patient n. This formulation indicates that an individual patient’s predicted risk equals the “population-average baseline risk” plus the “sum of gene-specific risk shifts” contributed by each feature. To comprehensively demonstrate the explanatory capacity of SHAP, feature-importance bar plots were generated to rank key drivers of survival differences on a unified scale. In addition, SHAP summary (bees warm) plots were used to visualize the distribution density linking gene expression levels to the direction and magnitude of risk contribution. Waterfall plots and force plots were further applied to precisely depict, for an individual patient at diagnosis, how specific genes positively increase or negatively offset the predicted prognostic risk.

To further investigate the clinical and biological relevance of core genes in the prognostic model, expression validation was performed using the Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER; https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/) online platform. The “DiffExp” module was utilized to systematically compare the expression levels of prognostic genes between BLCA tissues and adjacent normal tissues. Moreover, heatmaps were generated to more intuitively illustrate differences in expression abundance of these prognostic genes between normal controls and BLCA patients, as well as between high- and low-risk groups.

Independent prognostic analysis

To further evaluate whether the multivariate Cox regression-based prognostic model possesses independent predictive value beyond traditional clinicopathological features in clinical practice, comprehensive analyses were conducted. Clinical parameters, including age, gender, tumor stage, histological grade, and risk scores from BLCA patients in both the TCGA and GEO cohorts, were systematically integrated and encoded as numerical variables. Univariate and multivariate independent prognostic analyses were performed utilizing the R package “survival”, with results visually summarized via forest plots. Notably, the risk score was assessed both as a continuous variable and a categorical variable to ensure the robustness of the findings. Subsequently, a comprehensive prognostic nomogram was constructed by integrating identified independent prognostic factors to quantitatively estimate the 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival (OS) probabilities for individual patients. To evaluate the predictive accuracy and clinical reliability of the nomogram, calibration curves were plotted. These curves were utilized to assess the degree of concordance between the nomogram-predicted survival probabilities and the actual observed survival outcomes, thereby validating the model's clinical utility.

Immune infiltration analysis

To estimate the relative abundance of 22 distinct tumor-infiltrating immune cell (TIIC) types, a deconvolution analysis was performed on the gene expression matrix utilizing the CIBERSORT algorithm, which incorporates linear support vector regression. Concurrently, the correlation between the identified prognostic genes and immune cell infiltration was systematically investigated through the Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER) online database. This analysis specifically emphasized six major immune cell subsets: B cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, neutrophils, macrophages, and dendritic cells. To further ensure the robustness and reliability of the immune landscape assessment, the “xCell” algorithm was additionally introduced to provide multi-dimensional cross-validation of the estimated immune cell infiltrating levels.

Tumor mutational burden analysis and prediction of chemotherapy drug sensitivity

Tumor mutational burden (TMB) data for BLCA were analyzed and visualized utilizing the R packages “limma” and “ggpubr”. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was subsequently performed to evaluate the prognostic significance of TMB. To further clarify the synergistic prognostic value, a multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was executed by integrating both TMB levels and the prognostic risk score. Furthermore, a joint survival analysis was conducted to examine the combined impact of TMB status (high versus low) and risk categories on patient outcomes. To evaluate the potential efficacy of immunotherapy, the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) algorithm (https://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) was employed. Based on the normalized gene expression matrix of BLCA patients, TIDE scores were calculated for each sample to identify differences in immune evasion potential between the high- and low-risk groups. A threshold of P < 0.05 was established for statistical significance. Notably, higher TIDE scores indicate a greater propensity for tumor immune escape, suggesting a diminished clinical benefit from immune checkpoint blockade therapy. Finally, individual drug sensitivity for each patient was systematically evaluated utilizing the R package “oncoPredict”, which integrates high-throughput dose–response data retrieved from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses and comprehensive data visualizations in this study were performed utilizing R software (version 4.4.2). A P-value of less than 0.05 (P < 0.05) was considered to indicate a statistically significant difference across all enrichment and regression assessments.

Based on the predefined relevance score threshold, a total of 1,119 lactylation-related genes were systematically retrieved from the GeneCards database. To identify lactylation-related genes aberrantly altered during bladder cancer development, these 1,119 candidates were intersected with the TCGA transcriptome dataset. Differential expression analysis identified 160 lactylation-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs), including 92 significantly upregulated genes and 68 significantly downregulated genes (Fig. 2A). In addition, a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network for these lactylation-associated DEGs was constructed using the STRING online database (Fig. 2B). Based on the 160 key DEGs, Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were performed, and the top 30 enriched terms/pathways were visualized (Fig. 3).

Construction of the lactylation-related prognostic model

Expression profiles of the above candidate genes and corresponding follow-up information were extracted from the GEO cohort. First, univariate Cox proportional hazards regression was performed, identifying 33 genes whose expression levels were significantly associated with overall survival (OS) in patients with BLCA (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4A). These 33 genes were subsequently entered into a LASSO regression model to reduce multicollinearity and prevent overfitting. Using tenfold cross-validation, 12 genes with non-zero coefficients were retained at the optimal penalty parameter (λ = λmin) (Fig. 4B–C). To further refine the model, these genes were subjected to stepwise multivariate Cox regression (Fig. 4D), resulting in four independent core prognostic genes (ATAD3A, MKI67, VWF, and CCL2) and a corresponding prognostic risk model. The concordance index (C-index) of the model was 0.796 (Fig. 4E). In addition, Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated for each prognostic gene to illustrate their individual associations with patient outcomes (Fig. 4F–I). Independent prognostic analysis

To further investigate whether the prognostic model exerted an independent impact on disease progression, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted in both the training and validation cohorts (Figs. 6C–D and G–H). In the multivariate analysis, age (HR = 1.056, 95% CI: 1.012–1.101, P < 0.05), tumor stage (HR = 3.289, 95% CI: 2.139–5.057, P < 0.001), and the risk score (HR = 1.801, 95% CI: 1.204–2.696, P < 0.05) remained independent prognostic factors in the training cohort. Similarly, in the validation cohort, age (HR = 1.028, 95% CI: 1.012–1.044, P < 0.001), tumor stage (HR = 1.656, 95% CI: 1.358–2.019, P < 0.001), and the risk score (HR = 1.196, 95% CI: 1.052–1.360, P < 0.05) were also identified as independent predictors of prognosis. Collectively, these results indicate that age, tumor stage, and the model-derived risk score are independent prognostic determinants. The risk score was further evaluated as a categorical variable (high- vs. low-risk groups). In the multivariate Cox analysis of the training cohort, the high-risk group showed a trend toward increased risk (HR = 2.454, 95% CI: 0.817–7.371), although the association did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.110). In the validation set, a similar non-significant trend was observed (HR = 1.320, 95% CI: 0.971–1.794, P = 0.077) (Table 1). This lack of significance may be attributable to reduced statistical power resulting from dichotomizing a continuous risk score, which can lead to loss of variance information. In addition, a nomogram was established based on clinicopathological features from the TCGA-BLCA cohort. Calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed survival probabilities. The predictive performance of the nomogram for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival yielded AUC values of 0.727, 0.731, and 0.750, respectively (Fig. 7).

SHAP explainability analysis

To overcome the limitation of conventional Cox-based linear models, which rely primarily on regression coefficients and do not readily capture the real-world contribution of variables amid inter-individual heterogeneity, we introduced the SHAP framework to interpret the prognostic genes at both the population (global) level and the individual-patient (local) level.

(1) Global interpretability: First, at the training-cohort level, the mean absolute SHAP value for each gene was calculated to quantify its overall contribution to model predictions. The SHAP feature-importance bar plot (Fig. 8A) indicated that, after scale normalization, MKI67 was the dominant driver of survival risk variation in BLCA (mean |SHAP|= 0.605), followed by ATAD3A (0.472), CCL2 (0.307), and VWF (0.299). Next, the SHAP summary (bees warm) plot (Fig. 8B) further delineated an asymmetric distribution between gene expression levels and the directionality of risk attribution. MKI67 and ATAD3A showed highly concordant risk-promoting patterns: samples with high expression (yellow points) predominantly localized to the right side of the SHAP zero line (positive SHAP values, indicating increased predicted risk), whereas low-expression samples (purple points) clustered on the left side (negative SHAP values, indicating a protective effect and reduced predicted risk).

G Differential expression analysis of the prognostic genes in BLCA versus normal tissues based on the TIMER database

(2) Local interpretability: While global explanations summarize population-level patterns, clinical decision-making ultimately requires patient-specific interpretation. Therefore, the SHAP framework introduces an expected (baseline) value, representing the average model output across the cohort. As shown in Figs. 8C and D, the mean baseline risk score of our model in the overall cohort was 15.2, indicating that a hypothetical patient with all features at their cohort-average levels would have an initial risk score of 15.2. One patient was randomly selected for local attribution analysis. This patient’s individualized predicted risk score was 13.9, which was lower than the cohort baseline (15.2).

The waterfall plot and force plot clearly illustrated the internal attribution dynamics underlying this “below-baseline” prediction. Protective contributions (purple): Although MKI67 was the strongest risk driver at the global level, in this patient, the low expression of MKI67 (4.48) yielded a protective marginal effect (SHAP = − 0.997), constituting the primary factor reducing the risk score. Similarly, lower expression of CCL2 (4.12) and VWF (7.06) contributed − 0.490 and − 0.167, respectively. Risk-increasing contributions (yellow): In contrast, ATAD3A was highly expressed (7.71) in this patient and therefore exerted a positive risk contribution (SHAP = + 0.382), shifting the prediction upward. Overall, the sum of gene-specific SHAP values was − 1.3, decreasing the risk score from the baseline of 15.2 to 13.9. This patient-level decomposition translates the Cox linear predictor into an intuitive allocation of risk determinants for each individual, providing clinically interpretable visual evidence to support personalized prognostic assessment.

To further investigate the clinical and biological relevance of the prognostic model genes, a gene expression heatmap was generated to intuitively depict the abundance and distribution patterns of these core genes (Fig. 8E). Notably, expression heterogeneity was also observed within patients stratified into the high- and low-risk groups by the model (Fig. 8F). These findings were further systematically validated using the “DiffExp” module of the TIMER database. The results demonstrated that, compared with adjacent normal tissues, all four core prognostic genes were significantly differentially expressed in bladder cancer (P < 0.05) (Fig. 8G).

Immune infiltration analysis

The tumor microenvironment (TME) comprises stromal cells, malignant cells, and infiltrating immune cells [20]. We further explored the association between the prognostic model and the immune landscape of the BLCA TME. The results indicated that the high-risk group exhibited significantly higher levels of immune cell infiltration than the low-risk group, particularly activated memory CD4+T cells and M2 macrophages. Notably, M2 macrophages are a prototypical immunosuppressive subset (Fig. 9A), and their marked enrichment in the high-risk group was further confirmed by cross-validation using the xCell algorithm (Fig. 9B). We then used the TIMER database to evaluate correlations between gene expression levels and the infiltration abundance of six major immune cell types. Expression of CCL2 and VWF was positively correlated with broad immune infiltration. Specifically, CCL2 showed strong positive associations with CD8+T cells, CD4+T cells, macrophages, and neutrophils, whereas high VWF expression was accompanied by increased macrophage infiltration (Fig. 9C).

Analysis of tumor mutation burden differences

Tumor mutational burden (TMB) has been widely recognized to be closely associated with prognosis and tumor immunogenicity. To clarify the relationship between the model-derived risk score and TMB, as well as their potential joint clinical utility, we incorporated the risk score, TMB, and clinical variables (age, sex, and tumor stage) into a multivariate Cox regression model (Fig. 10A). The forest plot showed that the risk score, age, and stage were independent adverse prognostic factors in BLCA (HR > 1, P < 0.05), whereas high TMB served as an independent protective factor (HR < 1, P < 0.05).Kaplan–Meier survival analysis stratified by TMB level further demonstrated that patients in the high-TMB (H-TMB) group had significantly better overall survival than those in the low-TMB (L-TMB) group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 10B), supporting a survival benefit associated with elevated TMB. To achieve more refined clinical stratification, we performed a joint survival analysis integrating TMB status and the risk score (Fig. 10C). The cross-stratified groups revealed that patients with high TMB and low risk had the most favorable outcomes, whereas those with low TMB and high risk exhibited the poorest survival (P < 0.001). These findings indicate that combining the risk score with TMB markedly improves the prognostic resolution for survival outcomes in patients with BLCA.

Prediction of sensitivity to immunotherapy and chemotherapeutic agents

Building on the immune microenvironment analyses, we further applied the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) algorithm to estimate the potential risk of immune escape and resistance to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy in the high- and low-risk groups. As shown in the boxplot (Fig. 10D), the overall TIDE scores did not differ significantly between the two groups. This finding suggests that, although immune cell infiltration patterns differed by risk status, the baseline propensity for immune evasion as captured by the TIDE framework was comparable between groups. Because individualized chemotherapy and targeted therapy remain central components of clinical management for BLCA, we used the R package “oncoPredict” to evaluate predicted drug sensitivity across risk strata (Figs. 10E–H). The results indicated significant differences in predicted sensitivity between the high- and low-risk groups for ABT-737 (Bcl-2 inhibitor), dasatinib, sorafenib, and docetaxel (P < 0.05).

Discussion

Metabolic reprogramming in cancer, particularly lactate accumulation driven by the Warburg effect, not only alters the physicochemical properties of the tumor microenvironment but also profoundly influences transcriptional regulation and tumor cell fate through an emerging epigenetic mechanism, namely protein lactylation [21]. Focusing on this metabolism–epigenetics interface, we integrated univariate Cox regression, LASSO-based dimension reduction, and multivariate Cox regression to identify four core lactylation-related genes (ATAD3A, MKI67, VWF, and CCL2) and subsequently constructed a prognostic risk score model for bladder cancer. The model demonstrated robust and independent prognostic performance in both the training and validation cohorts, providing a rationale for refined risk stratification and supporting individualized clinical decision-making in patients with BLCA.

To enhance the interpretability of the prognostic model, we innovatively incorporated SHAP. Although the multivariable Cox proportional hazards model is intrinsically interpretable and its regression coefficients indicate the direction of effect, using coefficient magnitude alone to rank the “relative importance” of genes has inherent statistical limitations. Specifically, a Cox coefficient reflects the theoretical hazard penalty per unit change in expression, but it does not account for the empirical variability of a feature in the real-world population. Consequently, a gene may exhibit a large coefficient yet contribute little to between-patient survival differences if its expression shows only a narrow range in the cohort. This distinction was evident in our study: although ATAD3A had the largest absolute Cox coefficient, it ranked below MKI67 in the global SHAP importance analysis. This discrepancy highlights the added value of SHAP in this context. At the global level, SHAP effectively integrates model-derived effect size with cohort-level feature dispersion, thereby mitigating scale effects and reducing coefficient-driven bias, and more accurately identifying the key driver of survival heterogeneity in BLCA (MKI67). At the local level, SHAP further transforms the overall risk equation into a patient-specific, visual attribution of risk, allowing clinicians to directly determine which genes contribute most to an individual patient’s elevated risk. Together, this shift from global, coefficient-based interpretation to individualized, quantitatively decomposed explanations helps bridge an important gap in translating linear prognostic models into clinically actionable tools.

The four core prognostic genes screened in our model play crucial roles within the tumor lactylation modification and lactate metabolic network. Specifically, although MKI67 is a classic marker of cell proliferation [22], the hyper-proliferation driven by its high expression is inevitably accompanied by a robust glycolytic burden and massive lactate production [23]. This excessive lactate accumulation provides the abundant essential metabolic substrates required for extensive protein lactylation. Meanwhile, ATAD3A, localized in the mitochondria, plays a critical role in maintaining energy metabolic reprogramming [24], actively assisting tumor cells in achieving metabolic adaptation within a highly toxic, lactate-accumulated microenvironment [25]. Furthermore, the high-lactate and hypoxic microenvironment further regulates downstream genes through paracrine mechanisms: on the one hand, it promotes VWF to participate in angiogenesis and endothelial cell functional remodeling [26]; on the other hand, it induces the expression of the key chemokine CCL2, which recruits immune components like macrophages to the tumor region, where they undergo profound phenotypic transformation under the direct influence of lactylation modifications [27].

In the context of TME characterization, a clinically noteworthy phenomenon emerged: although the high-risk group exhibited higher overall immune infiltration (a “hot tumor” phenotype), its survival outcomes were significantly worse. This seemingly paradoxical pattern can be explained by the concepts of “immune exhaustion” and an “immunosuppressive microenvironment” [28, 29]. In our analysis, M2 macrophages—a prototypical immunosuppressive population—were markedly enriched in the high-risk group, and CCL2 expression showed a strong positive correlation with macrophage infiltration. These findings suggest that high-risk BLCA may recruit macrophages through CCL2 and related chemotactic signals and promote their polarization toward an M2-like state, thereby establishing a suppressive barrier that impairs CD8+ T-cell cytotoxicity and results in ineffective antitumor immunity. Consistent with this interpretation, in the joint TMB–risk analysis, patients with “low TMB and high risk” had the poorest prognosis. A plausible explanation is that low TMB is associated with reduced neoantigen load and thus weaker priming of antitumor immune recognition, whereas a high risk score reflects a more suppressive local immune contexture; the combination of limited immunogenicity and enhanced immunosuppression may jointly drive adverse outcomes. From a clinical perspective, these findings hold significant implications for guiding immunotherapy regimens. Patients in this "low TMB and high-risk" subgroup are likely to exhibit primary resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) monotherapy due to the dual disadvantages of insufficient antigen presentation and an exhausted, M2-dominant microenvironment. Therefore, we propose that ICI monotherapy may not be suitable for this specific population. Instead, a combination therapy strategy, such as pairing ICIs with tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) or anti-angiogenic agents, should be highly prioritized. TKIs or specific targeted therapies may help remodel the immunosuppressive TME, deplete suppressive myeloid cells, and subsequently sensitize these tumors to ICIs, providing a more rational and effective therapeutic avenue. In the drug sensitivity analysis, the predicted responses to specific agents showed distinct associations with the biological functions of our core model genes, providing mechanistic clues for clinical drug selection. Specifically, the high-risk group showed significantly higher predicted sensitivity (lower estimated scores) to dasatinib and docetaxel. Docetaxel is a microtubule-stabilizing agent that directly disrupts mitosis [30]. The preferential sensitivity to docetaxel in the high-risk group is deeply mechanistically linked to the highly proliferative phenotype driven by the core gene MKI67; tumors with elevated MKI67 expression are intrinsically more dependent on rapid cell division, thereby exhibiting a pronounced vulnerability to anti-mitotic agents. In contrast, the low-risk group appeared more sensitive to sorafenib and ABT-737. Sorafenib is a multi-kinase inhibitor renowned for its potent anti-angiogenic effects [31]. This sensitivity profile corresponds elegantly with the function of another core gene in our model, VWF, which is centrally involved in endothelial cell functional remodeling and angiogenesis. It suggests that tumor survival in the low-risk subgroup may rely heavily on continuous VWF-mediated neovascularization, making them highly susceptible to sorafenib-mediated vascular disruption. Collectively, this explicit mapping from gene function to drug sensitivity not only reinforces the biological validity of our model but also provides a rational, biomarker-guided framework for risk-adapted, individualized therapeutic strategies.

This study has several limitations. First, model development and validation were conducted using public databases; therefore, further confirmation in large-scale, multicenter, prospective clinical cohorts is warranted. Second, the proposed lactylation-related mechanisms and the inferred crosstalk network between lactylation and immune cells within the tumor microenvironment were mainly derived from transcriptome-level association analyses. Future in vitro cellular experiments and in vivo animal studies are needed to validate these findings and elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms in greater depth.

Conclusion

In this study, based on lactylation-related features, we successfully developed and validated a bladder cancer prognostic risk prediction model comprising four core genes (ATAD3A, MKI67, VWF, and CCL2). By incorporating the SHAP framework, we enabled a visual transition from population-level statistical associations to individualized, patient-specific risk attribution. Collectively, these findings provide a theoretical basis to support personalized clinical management.

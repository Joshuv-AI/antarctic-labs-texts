Definition 1.4

We call the the natural action

of the enhanced standard representation at

To describe such descendants, we need to define the MVW extension for a product of classical groups as well as its enhanced standard representation.
Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
be classical groups as in (
1.1

H=H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{r}
.
For

for the enhanced standard representation of
\breve{H}_{i}
.

Definition 1.5
.

We define the
MVW extensions

\breve{H}
of

to be the fiber product

\breve{H}_{1}\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\dots\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\breve{H}_{r}:=\{(h_{1},\dots,h_{r},\delta)\,:\,(h_{1},\delta)\in\breve{H}_{1},\dots,(h_{r},\delta)\in\breve{H}_{r}\}.

Additionally, we call the natural representation

of
\breve{H}

of
\breve{H}
.

Let
\chi
be the sign character from
\breve{H}
to
\{\pm 1\}
with kernel
H
. Namely,

\chi:\ \breve{H}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\},\quad(h_{1},\dots,h_{r},\delta)\mapsto\delta.

We also denote by

the trivial representation of
\breve{H}

2.2.
MVW extensions

Let
V
be a vector space over
\mathbb{F}
. Denote by

the group of
\mathbb{F}
-linear automorphisms of
V
, and by
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)
the Lie algebra of
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
. The MVW-extension of
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
is

\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}(V)={\mathrm{GL}}(V)\rtimes\{\pm 1\},

where
-1
acts on
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
by transposition.
By specifying a basis of
V
, we have
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
and
\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}(V)=\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
, where
n=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V
.

Assume now that
V
is equipped with a quadratic or symplectic form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Put

G(V)=\{g\in{\mathrm{GL}}(V)\,:\,\langle gv,gw\rangle=\langle v,w\rangle\text{ for }v,w\in V\}.

The Lie algebra of

is

\mathfrak{g}(V)=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)\,:\,\langle Xv,w\rangle+\langle v,Xw\rangle=0\text{ for }v,w\in V\}.

Additionally, the MVW-extension
\breve{G}(V)
of
G(V)
is the subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)\times\{\pm 1\}
consisting of pairs
(g,\delta)
such that either

\delta=1\quad\text{and}\quad\langle gv,gw\rangle=\langle v,w\rangle\quad\text{for }v,w\in V,

or

\delta=-1\quad\text{and}\quad\langle gv,gw\rangle=\langle w,v\rangle\quad\text{for }v,w\in V.

Let
\breve{G}(V)
act on
\mathfrak{g}(V)\times V
by

(g,\delta).(X,u)=(\delta gXg^{-1},\delta gu)

for
(g,\delta)\in\breve{G}(V)
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}(V)
and
u\in V
. This is a rational representation of
\breve{G}(V)
(and
G(V)
by taking restriction). We call this representation of
\breve{G}(V)
(resp.
G(V)
) the
enhanced standard representation
of
\breve{G}(V)
(resp.
G(V)
).

Note that if
G={\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})
or
{\mathrm{O}}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
, then we have

and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(E)
, where

is equipped with the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E}
defined by

\langle u,v\rangle_{E}=\begin{cases}u^{t}\beta_{2n}v,&if $G={\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})$,\\
u^{t}\alpha_{n}v,&if $G={\mathrm{O}}_{n}(\mathbb{F})$.\end{cases}

In this case, two definitions of the enhanced standard representation of
\breve{G}
(resp.
G
) coincide.

2.3.
Classical invariant theory

Let
H
be a reductive group, acting on an affine variety
X
.
It is known that the categorical quotient of
X
by
H
always exists. More precisely,
H\backslash\!\!\backslash X={\mathrm{Spec}}\left(\mathbb{F}[X]^{H}\right)
and
\pi:X\to H\backslash\!\!\backslash X
is induced by the inclusion
\mathbb{F}[X]^{H}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}[X]
(see
[
PV
]
). Note that the morphism
\pi
is surjective, and sends each
H
-invariant closed subset of
X
onto a closed subset of
H\backslash\!\!\backslash X
. Additionally, every fiber of
\pi
contains a unique closed orbit.

Theorem 2.1

(Luna’s criterion,
cf.

[
PV
]
*Remark of Theorem 6.17)
.

Let
K
be a reductive subgroup of
H
, and let
x\in X^{K}
. Then the orbit
Hx
is closed if and only if the orbit
Z_{H}(K)x
is closed.

Write
d=\max_{x\in X}\dim Hx
, and set

\Omega(X)=\{x\in X\,:\,Hx\text{ is closed and has dimension }d\}.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
X
is irreducible and
\Omega(X)
is nonempty. Then

\dim H\backslash\!\!\backslash X=\dim X-\dim H+\dim H_{x}

for all
x\in\Omega(X)
.

Proof.

Set
Y=\pi(\Omega(X))
. By
[
FSR
]
*Chapter 14, Theorem 3.13,
\Omega(X)
is an open subset in
X
, and so
Y
is an open subset in
H\backslash\!\!\backslash X
. Since
X
is irreducible,
H\backslash\!\!\backslash X
is also irreducible, and then

\dim X=\dim\Omega(X)\quad\text{and}\quad\dim H\backslash\!\!\backslash X=\dim Y.

Note that the fibers of

\pi|_{\Omega(X)}:\Omega(X)\to Y

are closed orbits contained in
\Omega(X)
, and hence have the same dimension. Therefore

\dim H-\dim H_{x}=\dim Hx=\dim\Omega(X)-\dim Y=\dim X-\dim H\backslash\!\!\backslash X

for
x\in\Omega(X)
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
The algebra of invariants

The main goal of this section is to prove the following result, which determines the algebra
\mathbb{F}[\mathfrak{g}\times E]^{G}
of invariants.

Theorem 3.1
.

The algebra
\mathbb{F}[\mathfrak{g}\times E]^{G}
is a polynomial ring with

\mathfrak{A}_{G}=\begin{cases}\{{\mathrm{tr}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{tr}}_{n},\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{n-1}\},&if $G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{F})$,\\
\{{\mathrm{tr}}_{2},{\mathrm{tr}}_{4},\dots,{\mathrm{tr}}_{2n},\eta_{1},\eta_{3},\dots,\eta_{2n-1}\},&if $G={\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})$,\\
\{{\mathrm{tr}}_{2},{\mathrm{tr}}_{4},\dots,{\mathrm{tr}}_{2n},\eta_{0},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{2n}\},&if $G={\mathrm{O}}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{F})$,\\
\{{\mathrm{tr}}_{2},{\mathrm{tr}}_{4},\dots,{\mathrm{tr}}_{2n},\eta_{0},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{2n-2}\},&if $G={\mathrm{O}}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})$.\end{cases}

as a set of algebraic independent generators.

Here, for every
i\geq 1
,
{\mathrm{tr}}_{i}
denotes the polynomial on
\mathfrak{g}\times E
given by

For every

,

denotes the polynomial of
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{F})\times\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
given by

\mu_{j}(X,u,v)=vX^{j}u.

And, when

,
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{F})
or
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})
,

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{F})x=\pi^{-1}(\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n})

Proof.

It is clear that

\displaystyle O

\displaystyle=\pi^{-1}(0,\dots,0,y_{1},\dots,y_{n})

\displaystyle=\{(X,u,v)\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{F})\times\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}\,:\,X\text{ is nilpotent and }vu=y_{1},\dots,vX^{n-1}u=y_{n}\}

u_{n}\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}).

{\mathrm{O}}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{F})
or
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})
. Set
m=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E
.

Fix

x=(X,u)\in\mathfrak{X}_{G}.

Write
X
and
u
in the form of (
1.10
) and (
1.11
). Then let

x_{0}=(\left(\begin{matrix}N_{1}^{(0)}&N_{2}^{(0)}\\
&N_{3}^{(0)}\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}u^{(0)}\\
v^{(0)}\end{matrix}\right))\quad\text{and}\quad x_{i}=(N_{i},u^{(0)},\left(v^{(i)}\right)^{t})\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq b.

Denote by
E_{0}
the generalized 0-eigenspace of
X
, and denote by
E_{i}
the generalized
c_{i}
-eigenspace of
X
for
1\leq i\leq b
. Set
n_{i}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq b
. Then

(6.8)

G_{x}\simeq G(E_{0})_{x_{0}}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{F})_{x_{1}}\times\dots\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{b}}(\mathbb{F})_{x_{b}}

and

(6.9)

E\simeq E_{0}\times(\mathbb{F}^{n_{1}\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times n_{1}})\times\dots\times(\mathbb{F}^{n_{b}\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times n_{b}})

as representations of
G_{x}
.

On the one hand, we have seen that

(6.10)

{\mathrm{GL}}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F})_{x_{i}}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n_{i}-k_{i}}&\\
&g\end{matrix}\right)\,:\,g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{k_{i}}(\mathbb{F})\right\}\simeq{\mathrm{GL}}_{k_{i}}(\mathbb{F})\quad\text{for some }k_{i}\leq n_{i},

and

(6.11)

\mathbb{F}^{n_{i}\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times n_{i}}=\left(\mathbb{F}^{k_{i}\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times k_{i}}\right)\oplus\mathrm{triv}^{2(n_{i}-k_{i})}

as representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{k_{i}}(\mathbb{F})
for
1\leq i\leq b
. On the other hand, put

V=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{\left(\begin{matrix}u^{(0)}\\
v^{(0)}\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}N_{1}^{(0)}&N_{2}^{(0)}\\
&N_{3}^{(0)}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}u^{(0)}\\
v^{(0)}\end{matrix}\right),\dots,\left(\begin{matrix}N_{1}^{(0)}&N_{2}^{(0)}\\
&N_{3}^{(0)}\end{matrix}\right)^{n_{0}+n_{0}^{\prime}-1}\left(\begin{matrix}u^{(0)}\\
v^{(0)}\end{matrix}\right)\}.

Then
G(E_{0})_{x_{0}}=G(V^{\bot})
and

(6.12)

E_{0}=V^{\bot}\oplus\mathrm{triv}^{\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V}

as representations of
G(V^{\bot})
.

Write

H=G(V^{\bot})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{k_{1}}(\mathbb{F})\times\dots\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{k_{b}}(\mathbb{F}).

Identify
G_{x}
with
H
. Then combining (
6.1
) and (
6.8
)–(
6.12
), we have

N_{O,x}^{\mathfrak{g}\times E}\simeq(\mathfrak{g}(V^{\bot})\times V^{\bot})\times\prod_{i=1}^{b}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{k_{i}}(\mathbb{F})\times\mathbb{F}^{k_{i}\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times k_{i}})\oplus\mathrm{triv}^{\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E-\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V^{\bot}-2(k_{1}+\dots+k_{b})}

as representations of
H
. It is evident that
V^{\bot}
is symplectic (resp. orthogonal) if
E
is symplectic (resp. orthogonal).

We then determine
\breve{G}_{x}
and the action of
\breve{G}_{x}\setminus G_{x}
on
N_{O,x}^{\mathfrak{g}\times E}
. We first consider the case that
x\in\mathfrak{N}_{G}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume that
x=(X,u)\in\mathfrak{N}_{G}
, and let

V=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{u,Xu,\dots,X^{m-1}u\}.

Then the stabilizer
\breve{G}_{x}\simeq\breve{G}(V^{\bot})
and

(6.13)

N_{O,x}^{\mathfrak{g}\times E}\simeq(\mathfrak{g}(V^{\bot})\times V^{\bot})\oplus(\mathrm{triv}^{k}\oplus\chi^{k+\gamma}),

as representations of
\breve{G}(V^{\bot})
, where
2k+\gamma=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V
, and

\gamma=\begin{cases}0,&if $E$ is symplectic, or $(X,u)=(0,0)$,\\
1,&if $E$ is orthogonal, and $(X,u)\neq(0,0)$.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
\breve{G}_{x}
preserves
V
and
V^{\bot}
. We have a homomorphism

\breve{G}_{x}\to\breve{G}(V^{\bot}),\quad g\mapsto g|_{V^{\bot}},

which has an inverse

(6.14)

\breve{G}(V^{\bot})\to\breve{G}_{x}\subseteq{\mathrm{GL}}(E)\times\{\pm 1\},\quad(h,\delta)\mapsto(\tilde{h},\delta)

such that

(6.15)

\tilde{h}|_{V^{\bot}}=h\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{h}(X^{i}u)=\delta^{i+1}X^{i}u,\text{ for }0\leq i\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V-1.

Therefore
\breve{G}_{x}\simeq\breve{G}(V^{\bot})
.

Now we consider the action of
\breve{G}(V^{\bot})
on

N_{O,x}^{\mathfrak{g}\times E}=\mathfrak{g}_{x}\times E=\mathfrak{g}(V^{\bot})\times E.

By the isomorphism (
6.14
),
\breve{G}(V^{\bot})
acts on
\mathfrak{g}(V^{\bot})
by

(6.16)

(g,\delta).X=\delta gXg^{-1}.

Decompose
V=V^{\bot}\oplus V
as a
\breve{G}(V^{\bot})
-module. Then by (
6.15
),
\breve{G}(V^{\bot})
acts on
V^{\bot}
by

(6.17)

(g,\delta).u=\delta gu,

and acts on
V
by the diagonal matrix

(6.18)

\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{-1,1,-1,1,\dots}_{\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V}).

Combining (
6.16
)–(
6.18
), we obtain (
6.13
).
∎

Now we turn to general
x\in\mathfrak{X}_{G}
. Denote by
X_{\mathrm{s}}
the semisimple part of
X
. Then

\displaystyle Z_{\breve{G}}(X_{\mathrm{s}})

\displaystyle\simeq\breve{G}(E_{0})\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}(E_{1})\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\dots\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}(E_{b})

\displaystyle\simeq\breve{G}(E_{0})\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{F})\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\dots\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}_{n_{b}}(\mathbb{F})

and

E\simeq E_{0}\times(\mathbb{F}^{n_{1}\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times n_{1}})\times\dots\times(\mathbb{F}^{n_{b}\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times n_{b}})

as representations of
Z_{\breve{G}}(X_{\mathrm{s}})
. Thus

\breve{G}_{x}\simeq\breve{G}(E_{0})_{x_{0}}\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{F})_{x_{1}}\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\dots\times_{\{\pm 1\}}\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}_{n_{b}}(\mathbb{F})_{x_{b}},

and

N_{O,x}^{\mathfrak{g}\times E}\simeq N_{\breve{G}(E_{0})x_{0},x_{0}}^{\mathfrak{g}(E_{0})\times E_{0}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{b}N_{\breve{{\mathrm{GL}}}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F})x_{i},x_{i}}^{\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F})\times\mathbb{F}^{n_{i}\times 1}\times\mathbb{F}^{1\times n_{i}}}

as representations of
\breve{G}_{x}
. By Propositions
6.1
and
6.2
, we have

(6.19)

\breve{G}_{x}\simeq\breve{H}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{O,x}^{\mathfrak{g}\times E}\simeq\mathfrak{h}^{\mathrm{en}}\oplus\mathrm{triv}^{k}\oplus\chi^{k+\gamma},

where
\gamma\in\{0,1\}
and
2k+2k_{1}+\dots+2k_{b}+\gamma+\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V=m
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

The theorem now follows from (
6.7
) and (
6.19
).
∎

One Health–based Joint Epidemiological Investigation of a Severe Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome Case in the Republic of Korea, 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesFollowing a healthcare-associated cluster of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS), a One Health–based joint epidemiological investigation integrating human, animal, and environmental components was conducted around the residence of the index patient to identify potential risk factors for infection and support the development of a standardized joint investigation framework.MethodsEpidemiological investigations and family interviews were conducted to assess the patient’s activities during the incubation period, including farming activities and history of contact with animals. Ticks were collected using dry ice-baited traps and flagging methods and tested for SFTS virus (SFTSV). Samples collected from a companion dog, stray cats, and water deer were analyzed using real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and indirect immunofluorescent antibody assay.ResultsNo SFTSV antigens or antibodies were detected in animal samples, suggesting that the case was unlikely to have resulted from animal-to-human transmission. A total of 341 Haemaphysalis longicornis ticks were collected, but all samples tested negative for SFTSV. Repeated vector control measures implemented after case identification may have affected vector density and test results. Epidemiological findings indicated frequent outdoor exposure during the incubation period, including gardening and walking along grassy rice-field banks. Although environmental and animal samples tested negative for SFTSV, the epidemiological findings suggested potential exposure to infected ticks.ConclusionsThis investigation demonstrated the field applicability of a One Health–based joint epidemiological investigation integrating human, animal, and environmental surveillance. The findings highlighted the need for a multisectoral collaborative system and a standardized joint epidemiological investigation framework to strengthen future responses to zoonotic disease responses.Keywords: Republic of Korea, Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome, One Health, Investigative techniques

Key messages

① What is known previously?

Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is primarily transmitted by infected ticks, although human-to-human and animal-to-human transmissions have also been reported, emphasizing the need for a One Health–based joint approach integrating human, animal, and environmental sectors.

② What new information is presented?

A joint epidemiological investigation was conducted around the residence of the index patient associated with an SFTS cluster. Although SFTS virus was not detected in animal or environmental samples, frequent outdoor activities and exposure to vegetation-rich environments suggested potential exposure to infected ticks.

③ What are implications?

This investigation demonstrated the field applicability of a One Health–based approach and generated foundational evidence for developing standardized multisectoral joint investigation and response systems for zoonotic diseases.

Introduction

Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is a tick-borne viral disease that has been confirmed throughout East Asia, including the Republic of Korea (ROK), Japan, Taiwan, and Vietnam. The first case was reported in China in 2009. The incubation period of the disease is 5–14 days, and the major clinical symptoms include fever, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, gastrointestinal symptoms, neurological abnormalities, and hemorrhage. In China, more than 13,000 cases were reported between 2010 and 2019 [1,2], and in the ROK, approximately 200 patients have been reported annually since 2017, with a cumulative case fatality rate of about 18.0% [3].

SFTS is primarily transmitted when hard ticks infected with SFTS virus (SFTSV) feed on humans. However, cases of transmission through exposure to the blood and body fluids of patients or animals with SFTS have also been reported [4,5]. In particular, in Japan, veterinarians and animal caretakers without a history of tick exposure were infected with SFTS through direct exposure to the blood and bodily fluids of cats infected with SFTSV, and the SFTSV genomes from the specimens of the patients and cats were 100% identical [6,7].

One Health is an integrated approach that recognizes that the health of humans, animals, and the environment are closely interconnected. Experts and institutions from various fields collaborate via this approach to prevent and respond to public health issues, including zoonotic diseases. SFTS is a zoonotic disease capable of not only tick-borne transmission but also human-to-human and animal-to-human transmission. To comprehensively identify the sources of infection and transmission routes, a One Health–based joint epidemiological investigation wherein the public health, animal, and environmental sectors participate together must be conducted.

The World Health Organization, the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and recently, the international One Health network have proposed integrated surveillance and multisectoral collaboration among the human, animal, and environmental sectors as core strategies for responding to zoonotic diseases [4,8]. Therefore, in this investigation, we conducted a One Health–based joint epidemiological investigation focusing on the index case of the 2025 SFTS cluster within a healthcare institution to assess infection risk factors and to provide baseline data for the future development of a joint epidemiological investigation framework and standard manual.

Methods

1. Case Occurrence

In June 2025, a cluster of SFTS cases occurred at a healthcare facility in Chungcheongbuk-do. The index case, a female in her 60s, died while receiving inpatient treatment at a tertiary medical institution for fever and pancytopenia. Subsequently, among 27 healthcare workers and funeral directors who were exposed to blood and body fluids during cardiopulmonary resuscitation and post-mortem care of the index case, SFTSV gene detection testing was conducted on symptomatic individuals, resulting in a total of eight confirmed cases.

2. Joint Epidemiological Investigation Process

The joint epidemiological investigation focused on the index case and was conducted in the human, environmental, and animal sectors. In the human sector, the exposure history and activity history of the index case were confirmed through epidemiological investigation, and in the environmental sector, a vector survey was conducted focusing on the presumed infection area. In the animal sector, specimens were collected from wildlife, companion dogs, and stray cats to investigate the presence of infection through SFTSV genetic testing using real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (real-time RT-PCR) and antibody testing (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay [ELISA] and indirect immunofluorescent antibody assay [IFA]).

1) Epidemiological investigation

The Zoonotic Disease Control Division of the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA), the Chungcheongbuk-do Provincial Government, and the competent public health center identified the presumed infection area by investigating the index case’s history of activities, walking routes, farming activities, and animal contact during the incubation period through a review of medical records and family interviews. Based on this strategy, the scope of vector collection and animal capture was determined.

2) Vector investigation

The Division of Vectors and Parasitic Diseases of the KDCA collected hard ticks, using dry ice traps and the flagging method in the presumed infection area identified through interview investigations, and conducted real-time RT-PCR on the collected specimens.

3) Wildlife investigation

The National Institute of Wildlife Disease Control and Prevention investigated the wildlife habitat status within a 2 km2 radius of the index case’s residence from the night before the joint epidemiological investigation to the dawn of the following day, utilizing drones equipped with thermal imaging cameras. Based on the investigation results, carcasses were searched for, and harmful wild animals were captured using firearms to collect blood, spleen, and attached tick specimens. Real-time RT-PCR and IFA were performed on the collected specimens.

4) Investigation of companion dogs and stray cats

The Chungcheongbuk-do Animal Hygiene Laboratory and the district office of jurisdiction, with cooperation from the livestock department, collected specimens from companion dogs and stray cats near the residence of the index case, and sent them to the Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency to perform real-time RT-PCR, ELISA, and IFA.

5) Specimen testing methods

For genetic testing, the S segment or M segment of the SFTSV was targeted to detect the gene using real-time RT-PCR. For IFA, SFTSV-infected cells were fixed on a slide, reacted with the specimen serum, and the antibody level was evaluated based on the presence or absence of fluorescence expression using a fluorescence-labeled secondary antibody (Figure 1) [9].

Results

1. SFTSV Gene and Antigen/Antibody Testing in Companion Animals and Wildlife

As a result of performing real-time RT-PCR, ELISA, and IFA on one companion dog, six stray cats, and one tick attached to a stray cat, neither SFTSV genes nor antibodies were detected in any of them. On the day of the epidemiological investigation, one water deer carcass was found within 100 m of the index case’s residence, and two water deer were captured within a 2 km2 radius. Following qRT-PCR, the viral gene was not detected. In addition, IgG antibodies were not detected in IFAs performed on serum specimens from the captured water deer (Table 1).

3. Results of the Joint Epidemiological Investigation

As a result of conducting a One Health approach–based joint epidemiological investigation targeting humans, animals, and the environment centered on the residence of the index case, the SFTSV gene and antibody test results for specimens collected from companion dogs, stray cats, and wild water deer were all negative, and no findings suggestive of animal-mediated infection were confirmed. In addition, hard ticks were collected in areas centered on the activity range of the index case and subjected to real-time RT-PCR. All results were negative, and the number of collected individuals was also small. This finding might have been influenced by the fact that the public health center immediately conducted disinfection after the occurrence of the case, and periodically conducted disinfection three times prior to the joint epidemiological investigation. Meanwhile, interview results revealed that during the 14-day maximum incubation period prior to the onset of SFTS symptoms, the index case had a history of being bitten by insects multiple times while gardening in front of their home and was confirmed to have walked their companion dog daily around rice paddy dikes. In addition, the residence and primary activity areas of the index case were confirmed to be heavily vegetated environments. The epidemiological investigation results also suggested that the infection was acquired through a bite from an SFTSV-infected hard tick.

Discussion

In this investigation, SFTSV was not detected in vectors and animals; however, SFTSV infections in vectors and animals continue to be reported in the ROK. Hard ticks, the primary vector of SFTS, are distributed throughout the ROK. Previous studies have reported an SFTSV infection rate of 3.61% (minimum infection rate of 0.31%) in adult and nymphal hard ticks. In addition, they have been detected on both designated trails and off-trail areas within national parks [10]. Additionally, SFTSV antigens and antibodies have been identified in wild animals such as wild boars and water deer, indicating that SFTSV is continuously circulating in the ROK [11,12]. In Thailand, a joint epidemiological investigation applying the One Health approach was also conducted for the first SFTS patient. Real-time RT-PCR and serological tests targeting close contacts of the index case, companion animals, stray cats around the residence, rodents, and attached ticks revealed that SFTSV was not detected by real-time RT-PCR, but serological positivity was confirmed in some animal specimens [13]. Therefore, as animals are considered to have been infected with SFTSV over a long period, a One Health approach that comprehensively considers humans, animals, and the environment is important. Accordingly, KDCA has established and is operating a surveillance system to detect animal-to-human transmission cases early, starting with the 2020 pilot project for joint testing of contacts of human-companion animal SFTS-positive cases. In addition, since 2022, in cooperation with relevant agencies including the Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs, the Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency, and the National Institute of Wildlife Disease Control and Prevention, a surveillance system linking humans, animals, and the environment has been operated. A joint response system is maintained by sharing surveillance results among these agencies through regular meetings. This joint epidemiological investigation represents a framework for applying this multi-ministerial collaborative framework to the actual field, serving as an important foundation for establishing a One Health–based joint epidemiological investigation system and standardized investigation manuals in the event of future occurrences of zoonotic diseases, including SFTS.

서 론

중증열성혈소판감소증후군(severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome, SFTS)은 2009년 중국에서 첫 환자가 보고된 이후 한국, 일본, 대만, 베트남 등 동아시아 전역에서 확인된 진드기 매개 바이러스성 질환이다. 잠복기는 5–14일이며 주요 임상증상은 발열, 혈소판감소, 백혈구감소, 위장관 증상, 신경학적 이상, 출혈 등이 있다. 중국에서는 2010–2019년 사이 1만 3천여 건 이상의 사례가 보고되었고[1,2], 국내에서도 2017년 이후 매년 200명 내외로 환자가 지속 발생하여 누적 치명률은 약 18.0%이다[3].

SFTS는 주로 중증열성혈소판감소증후군 바이러스(SFTS virus, SFTSV)에 감염된 참진드기가 사람을 흡혈하는 과정에서 전파된다. 그러나 SFTS 환자 또는 동물의 혈액 및 체액에 노출되어 감염이 전파된 사례도 보고되고 있다[4,5]. 특히 일본에서 수의사와 동물보호자가 진드기 노출력 없이 SFTSV에 감염된 고양이의 혈액 및 체액에 직접 노출되어 SFTS에 감염되었으며, 환자와 고양이의 검체에서 SFTSV 유전체가 100% 일치하였다[6,7].

원헬스(Ohe Health)는 사람, 동물 및 환경의 건강이 서로 밀접하게 연결되어 있음을 인식하고, 다양한 분야의 전문가와 기관이 협력하여 인수공통감염병을 포함한 공중보건 문제를 예방하고 대응하는 통합적 접근법이다. SFTS는 진드기 매개 전파뿐만 아니라 사람 간 및 동물-사람 간 전파도 가능한 인수공통감염병으로 감염원과 전파 경로를 종합적으로 규명하기 위해서는 보건, 동물 및 환경 분야가 함께 참여하는 원헬스 기반의 공동역학조사가 이뤄져야한다.

세계보건기구(World Health Organization), 미국 질병통제예방센터(U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention), 그리고 최근 국제 원헬스 네트워크는 인수공통감염병 대응을 위해 사람, 동물, 환경 분야 간 통합감시와 다부문 협력을 핵심 전략으로 제시하고 있다[4,8]. 이에 본 조사에서는 2025년 의료기관 내 SFTS 집단 발생의 지표환자를 중심으로 원헬스 기반 공동역학조사를 수행하여 감염 위험요인을 평가하고, 향후 공동역학조사 체계 및 표준 매뉴얼 개발을 위한 기초자료를 마련하고자 하였다.

방 법

1. 사례 발생

2025년 6월 충청북도 내 의료기관에서 SFTS 집단감염 사례가 발생하였다. 지표환자 60대 여성은 발열 및 범혈구감소증으로 3차 의료기관에 입원 치료를 받던 중 사망하였다. 이후 지표환자의 심폐소생술 및 사후 처치 과정에서 혈액 및 체액에 노출된 의료진과 장례지도사 27명 중 유증상자를 대상으로 SFTSV 유전자 검출검사를 시행한 결과 총 8명이 확진되었다.

2. 공동역학조사 과정

공동역학조사는 지표환자를 중심으로 사람, 환경 및 동물을 대상으로 수행하였다. 사람 분야에서는 역학조사를 통해 지표환자의 노출력과 활동 이력을 확인하였고, 환경 분야에서는 추정 감염지역을 중심으로 매개체 조사를 실시하였다. 동물 분야에서는 야생동물, 반려견 및 길고양이를 대상으로 검체를 채취하여 SFTSV 유전자 검사(실시간 역전사 중합효소연쇄반응[real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, real-time RT-PCR])와 항체검사(효소결합면역흡착검사[enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, ELISA], 간접면역형광항체검사[indirect immunofluorescent antibody assay, IFA])를 통해 감염여부를 조사하였다.

1) 역학조사

질병관리청 인수공통감염병관리과, 충청북도청 및 관할 보건소는 지표환자의 의무기록 검토와 가족 면담을 통해 잠복기 동안의 활동 이력, 산책 경로, 농작업 및 동물 접촉 여부 등을 조사하여 추정 감염지역을 도출하였다. 이를 바탕으로 매개체 채집 및 동물 포획의 범위를 선정하였다.

2) 매개체조사

질병관리청 매개체분석과는 면담조사를 통해 확인된 추정 감염지역에서 드라이아이스 트랩 및 플래깅(flagging) 방법을 이용하여 참진드기를 채집하고, 채집된 검체를 대상으로 real-time RT-PCR을 실시하였다.

3) 야생동물조사

국립야생동물질병관리원은 공동역학조사 전날 야간부터 다음날 새벽까지 열화상 카메라가 장착된 드론을 활용하여 지표환자 거주지 반경 2 km2 이내의 야생동물 서식 현황을 조사하였다. 조사 결과를 바탕으로 폐사체를 수색하고, 유해야생동물은 총기로 포획하여 혈액, 비장 및 부착 진드기 검체를 채취하였다. 채취한 검체를 대상으로 real-time RT-PCR과 IFA를 실시하였다.

4) 반려견 및 길고양이조사

충청북도 동물위생시험소와 관할 구청은 축산부서의 협조를 받아 반려견과 지표환자 거주지 주변의 길고양이에서 검체를 채취하고, 농림축산검역본부로 송부하여 real-time RT-PCR과 ELISA, IFA를 실시하였다.

5) 검체 검사방법

유전자 검사는 SFTSV의 S segment 또는 M segment를 타켓하여 real-time RT-PCR을 사용하여 유전자를 검출하였다. IFA는 슬라이드에 SFTSV가 감염된 세포를 고정하고 검체 혈청을 반응시킨 후 형광이 표지된 2차 항체를 이용하여 형광 발현 유무로 항체수준을 평가하였다(그림 1) [9].

1. 반려동물 및 야생동물 내 SFTSV 유전자 및 항원/항체 검사

반려견 1마리, 길고양이 6마리, 길고양이 부착 진드기 1개체에 대해 real time RT-PCR, ELISA, IFA를 시행한 결과 모두 SFTSV 유전자 및 항체는 검출되지 않았다. 역학조사 당일 지표환자 거주지 100 m 이내에 고라니 폐사체 1마리와 반경 2 km2 내에서 고라니 2마리를 포획한 후 real time RT-PCR을 수행한 결과, 바이러스 유전자가 검출되지 않았다. 또한, 포획한 고라니의 혈청 검체에서 IFA 검사를 수행한 결과에서도 IgG 항체는 확인되지 않았다(표 1).

3. 공동역학조사 결과

지표환자 거주지를 중심으로 사람, 동물 및 환경을 대상으로 원헬스 접근법 기반의 공동역학조사를 수행한 결과 반려견, 길고양이 및 야생 고라니에서 채취한 검체의 SFTSV 유전자 및 항체 검사 결과 모두 음성이었으며, 동물을 통한 감염을 시사하는 소견은 확인되지 않았다. 또한 지표환자의 활동 반경을 중심으로 참진드기를 채집하여 real time RT-PCR을 실시한 결과 모두 음성이었으며, 채집된 개체 수 또한 많지 않았다. 이는 환자 발생 이후 보건소에서 즉시 방역을 실시한 점, 공동역학조사 실시 이전에 주기적으로 3회 방역한 점이 영향을 미쳤을 것으로 판단된다. 한편, 면담조사 결과 지표환자는 SFTS 증상 발생일 이전 최대 잠복기인 14일 동안 자택 앞 정원을 관리하는 과정에서 벌레에 여러 차례 물린 이력이 있었으며, 매일 논두렁 주변으로 반려견을 산책시킨 것으로 확인되었다. 또한 지표환자의 거주지와 주요 활동 지역은 수풀이 우거진 환경으로 확인되어 역학조사 결과를 종합할 때 SFTSV에 감염된 참진드기에 물려 감염되었을 가능성이 높은 것으로 추정되었다.

논 의

이번 조사에서는 매개체와 동물에서 SFTSV가 검출되지 않았으나, 국내에서 매개체와 동물에서의 SFTSV 감염이 지속적으로 보고되고 있다. SFTS의 주요 매개체인 참진드기는 국내 전역에 분포하며, 선행 연구에서는 성충 및 약충 참진드기의 SFTSV 감염률이 3.61% (최소감염률 0.31%)로 보고되었고, 국립공원 내 탐방로와 비탐방로 모두에서 검출된 바 있다[10]. 또한 멧돼지와 고라니 등 야생동물에서도 SFTSV 항원과 항체가 확인되어 국내에서 SFTSV가 지속적으로 순환하고 있음이 보고되었다[11,12]. 태국에서도 최초 SFTS 환자를 대상으로 원헬스 접근법을 적용한 공동역학조사가 수행되었다. 지표환자의 밀접접촉자, 반려동물, 거주지 주변의 길고양이, 설치류 및 부착 진드기를 대상으로 real-time RT-PCR 및 혈청학적 검사를 실시한 결과 real-time RT-PCR에서는 SFTSV가 검출되지 않았으나 일부 동물 검체에서는 serology 양성이 확인되었다[13]. 이처럼 동물이 장기간 SFTSV에 감염되었을 것으로 판단됨으로 사람∙동물∙환경을 통합적으로 고려하는 원헬스 접근이 중요하다. 이에 질병관리청은 2020년 사람-반려동물 SFTS 양성사례 접촉자 동반검사 시범사업을 시작으로 동물-사람 간 전파사례를 조기에 발견하기 위한 감시체계를 구축하여 운영하고 있다. 또한 2022년부터 농림축산식품부, 농림축산검역본부 및 야생동물질병관리원 등 관계기관과 협력하여 사람∙동물∙환경을 연계한 감시체계를 운영하고 있으며, 정기적인 회의를 통해 기관 간 감시 결과를 공유하고 공동 대응체계를 유지하고 있다. 이번 공동역학조사는 이러한 다부처 협력체계를 실제 현장에 적용한 사례로 향후 SFTS를 비롯한 인수공통감염병 발생 시 원헬스 기반 공동역학조사 체계와 표준화된 조사 매뉴얼을 마련하는데 중요한 기반이 되었다.

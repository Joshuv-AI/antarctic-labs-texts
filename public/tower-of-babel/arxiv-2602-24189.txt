Almost sure CLT for hyperbolic Anderson model with Lévy colored noise
Raluca M. Balan, Hanniel E. Kouamé, William D. Stephenson

Abstract
In this note, we prove the Almost Sure Central Limit Theorem (ASCLT) for the spatial integral of the solution of the hyperbolic Anderson model driven by the Lévy colored noise introduced in [ 1 ] . For this, we use the central limit theorem for the normalized spatial integral, and an estimate for the Malliavin derivative of the solution, both derived in the recent preprint [ 3 ] . We assume that the spatial correlation kernel of the noise is either integrable, or it is given by the Riesz kernel.

Introduction

In this article, we consider the hyperbolic Anderson model:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial^{2}t}(t,x)=\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)+u(t,x)\dot{X}(t,x),\ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=1,\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1)

where
X
is the
Lévy colored noise
introduced in
[
1
]
.
The recent article
[
2
]
shows that equation (
1
) has a unique solution, and gives some properties of the moments of the solution. This analysis was continued in article
[
3
]
which studies the asymptotic Gaussian fluctuations as
R\to\infty
of the
spatial average
of the (centered) solution:

F_{R}(t)=\int_{-R}^{R}\big(u(t,x)-1\big)dx.

(2)

The goal of this short note is to complement this analysis and show that the normalized process
\{\widetilde{F}_{R}(t)\}_{R>0}
satisfies the
almost sure central limit theorem (ASCLT)
, where

\widetilde{F}_{R}(t)=\frac{F_{R}(t)}{\sigma_{R}(t)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)={\rm Var}\big(F_{R}(t)\big).

(3)

We recall that the noise
X
is defined by

X_{t}(\varphi)=L_{t}(\varphi*\kappa),\quad\mbox{for all}\ t>0,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
is the set of rapidly decreasing functions on
\mathbb{R}
,
\kappa:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty]
is a suitable kernel (which satisfies Assumptions A1 and A2 of
[
2
]
), and
L
is the
Lévy white noise
given by

L(B)=\int_{B\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}z\widehat{N}(dt,dx,dz)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}).

with
\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
being the class of bounded Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Here
N
is a Poisson random measure on
Z=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}
with intensity

\mathfrak{m}(dt,dx,dz)=dtdx\nu(dz),

defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, and
\widehat{N}(A)=N(A)-\mathfrak{m}(A)
is the compensated version of
N
.
The space
\mathbb{R}_{0}=\mathbb{R}\verb 2\2\{0\}
is equipped with the distance
d(x,y)=|x^{-1}-y^{-1}|
, so that the bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{0}
are those that are bounded away from 0.
We assume that the measure
\nu
satisfies the following condition:

m_{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}|z|^{2}\nu(dz)<\infty.

which guarantees that the noise
L
has finite variance.

A predictable process
\{u(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}\}
is called a
(mild) solution
of (
1
)
if it satisfies the integral equation:

u(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y)u(s,y)X(ds,dy),

where the stochastic integral is interpreted in the Itô sense.

A random field
\{\Phi(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}\}
is
predictable
if it is measurable with respect to the predictable
\sigma
-field on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, which is the minimal
\sigma
-field with respect to which all elementary processes are measurable. An
elementary process
is a linear combination of processes of the form

\Phi(t,x)=Y1_{(a,b]}(t)1_{A}(x)

where
0\leq a<b
,
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and
Y
is
\mathcal{F}_{a}
-measurable.
Here
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the filtration induced by
N
, i.e.

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(\{N([0,s]\times A\times B);s\in[0,t],A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}),B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{0})\})\vee\mathcal{N},

where
\mathcal{N}
is the class of
\mathbb{P}
-null sets.

We recall the classical ASCLT. Let
\{X_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, with
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]=1
. Define
S_{N}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}X_{k}
. Then, for
\mathbb{P}
-almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k}\delta_{S_{k}(\omega)/\sqrt{k}}\Longrightarrow\gamma\quad\mbox{as}\quad N\to\infty,

where
\gamma
is the standard normal distribution and
\Longrightarrow
denotes convergence in distribution. This result was stated by Paul Lévy on page 270 of
[
11
]
without a proof. The proof and various extensions were given few decades later by several authors: see
[
5
,
10
,
12
]
.
In the present paper, we will prove a continuous-time version of the ASCLT, which is well-suited for the study of SPDEs, and is motivated by Lévy’s result. We refer the reader to Section 1.2 of
[
4
]
for further comments on ASCLT in this continuous-time framework.

We recall the following definition from
[
4
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

a) A sequence
\{F_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of random variables defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

satisfies
the ASCLT
if for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,

\mu_{N}^{\omega}:=\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k}\delta_{F_{k}(\omega)}\Longrightarrow\gamma\quad\mbox{as}\quad N\to\infty.

b) A family
\{F_{\theta}\}_{\theta>0}
of random variables
satisfies
the ASCLT
if for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every

\omega\in\Omega
, the map
\theta\mapsto F_{\theta}(\omega)
is measurable, and

\nu_{T}^{\omega}:=\frac{1}{\log T}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{1}{\theta}\delta_{F_{\theta}(\omega)}dy\Longrightarrow\gamma\quad\mbox{as}\quad T\to\infty.

Recall that the
Riesz kernel of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
is given by:

R_{1,\alpha}(x)=C_{1,\alpha}|x|^{-(1-\alpha)}\quad\mbox{where}\quad C_{1,\alpha}=\pi^{-1/2}2^{-\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1-\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}.

We are now ready to state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
or
\kappa=R_{1,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then the process

\{\widetilde{F}_{\theta}(t)\}_{\theta>0}
defined by (
3
) satisfies the ASCLT, for any
t>0
.

Article
[
4
]
presents two methods for proving the ASCLT for
the spatial integral of the solution of (HAM) driven by the Lévy white noise
L
:

(i) a direct proof based on Clark-Ocone formula;

(ii) a longer proof based on the Ibragimov-Lifshitz criterion and the recent second-order Poincaré inequality due to
[
13
]
.
The second method is also used in
[
14
]
for proving the ASCLT for the spatial integral of the solution of (HAM) or (PAM), driven by a Gaussian noise, which is colored in space and time.

The goal of the present note is to present an adaptation of method (i) which works for the Lévy colored noise introduced in
[
2
]
. At the end, in Remark
2.1
, we show that this method can also be used for the spatially-colored Gaussian noise considered in
[
6
]
, providing an alternative (shorter) proof for the result of
[
14
]
, when the noise is white in time.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We will use the following results, which were proved in
[
3
]
:

(i)

\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim K(t)R^{\beta}
as
R\to\infty
, where
K(t)>0
is a constant that depends on
t
, and

\beta:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if $\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$}\\
\alpha+1&\mbox{if $\kappa=R_{1,\alpha/2}$ for some $\alpha\in(0,1)$ }\end{array}\right.

(4)

(ii)

\widetilde{F}_{R}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1)
as
R\to\infty

(iii)

for any
0\leq r\leq t
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}_{0}
,

\|D_{r,y,z}u(t,x)\|_{2}\leq C_{t}|z|\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(x-y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})dy^{\prime},

where
C_{t}>0
is a constant that depends on
t
.

We fix
t>0
. We denote
\widetilde{F}_{\theta}=\widetilde{F}_{\theta}(t)
and
\sigma_{\theta}=\sigma_{\theta}(t)
.
We have to prove that for any bounded Lipschitz function
h
on
\mathbb{R}
,

\frac{1}{\log T}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{1}{\theta}h(\widetilde{F}_{\theta})d\theta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x)\gamma(dx)\quad\mbox{as}\quad T\to\infty.

In view of item (ii) above, it is enough to show that

\frac{1}{\log T}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{1}{\theta}\Big(h(\widetilde{F}_{\theta})-\mathbb{E}[h(\widetilde{F}_{\theta})]\Big)d\theta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\quad\mbox{as}\quad T\to\infty.

(5)

Let
H_{\theta}=h(\widetilde{F}_{\theta})-\mathbb{E}[h(\widetilde{F}_{\theta})]
. We use the Clark-Ocone formula with respect to
\widehat{N}
,

H_{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\mathbb{E}[D_{r,y,z}H_{\theta}|\mathcal{F}_{r}]\widehat{N}(dr,dy,dz).

Similarly to relation (3.2) of
[
4
]
, we have:

|\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]|\leq\frac{{\rm Lip}^{2}(h)}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\|D_{r,y,z}F_{\theta}\|_{2}\|D_{r,y,z}F_{w}\|_{2}\nu(dz)dydr,

(6)

where
{\rm Lip}(h)
is the Lipschitz constant of
h
, and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the norm in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

We denote

\varphi_{t,\theta}(r,y)=\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}G_{t-r}(x-y)dx\quad\mbox{and}\quad I_{\theta}(y)=\varphi_{t,\theta}(0,y).

By Minkowski’s inequality, item (iii) above, and Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\|D_{r,y,z}F_{\theta}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}\|D_{r,y,z}u(t,x)\|_{2}dx\leq C_{t}|z|\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(x-y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle=C_{t}|z|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,\theta}(r,y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}.

Inserting this estimate into (
6
), we obtain:

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]|\leq

\displaystyle\quad\frac{{\rm Lip}^{2}(h)}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}C_{t}^{2}m_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,\theta}(r,y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,\theta}(r,y^{\prime\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime\prime})dy^{\prime\prime}\right)dydr.

Note that
\varphi_{t,\theta}(r,y)\leq I_{\theta}(y)
for any
r\in[0,t]
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, since
G_{t-r}(\cdot)\leq G_{t}(\cdot)
. Hence,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\rm Lip}^{2}(h)}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}C_{t}^{2}m_{2}t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}I_{\theta}(y^{\prime})I_{w}(y^{\prime\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime\prime})dy^{\prime}dy^{\prime\prime}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm Lip}^{2}(h)}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}C_{t}^{2}m_{2}t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}I_{\theta}(y^{\prime})I_{w}(y^{\prime\prime})f(y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime})dy^{\prime}dy^{\prime\prime}.

using the fact that
f=\kappa*\widetilde{\kappa}
. Using the notation

A_{\theta,w}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{\theta}(y)I_{w}(z)f(y-z)dydz,

we have:

\big|\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]\big|\leq{\rm Lip}^{2}(h)C_{t}^{2}m_{2}t\frac{A_{\theta,w}}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}.

(7)

To estimate
A_{\theta,w}/(\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w})
, we consider separately the two cases. We assume that
\theta<w
.

Case 1.
Assume that
\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. By Young’s inequality,
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We use the fact that
I_{w}(z)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t}(x-z)dx=t
. It follows that

\displaystyle A_{\theta,w}

\displaystyle\leq t\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{\theta}(y)dy=t\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t}(x-y)dydx=2\theta t\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Since in this case
\sigma_{\theta}^{2}\sim K(t)\theta
, we infer that

\frac{A_{\theta,w}}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}\leq C_{t,f}\left(\frac{\theta}{w}\right)^{1/2},

where
C_{t,f}>0
is a constant that depends on
(t,f)
. Relation (
5
) now follows by (
7
) and Lemma 3.1 of
[
4
]
.

Case 2.
Assume that
\kappa=R_{1,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then
f=\kappa*\tilde{\kappa}=R_{1,\alpha}
and

A_{\theta,w}=C_{1,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{\theta}(y)I_{w}(z)|y-z|^{-(1-\alpha)}dydz.

To calculate
A_{\theta,w}
, we use a method based on Fourier transforms.
Note that the Fourier transform of
f
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
is
g(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\alpha}
, i.e.
(f,\mathcal{F}\phi)=(g,\phi)
for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. By the Fourier inversion theorem,

(f,\varphi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(g,\mathcal{F}\varphi)
for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, that is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{-(1-\alpha)}\varphi(x)dx=\frac{1}{2\pi C_{1,\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi.

Hence, for any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)|x-y|^{-(1-\alpha)}dxdy=\frac{1}{2\pi C_{1,\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(\xi)}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi.

(8)

Here
\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}\varphi(x)dx
is the Fourier transform of
\varphi
.

For the sake of a comparison with some identities related the fractional Brownian motion (fBm), we use the parametrization
\alpha=2H-1
with
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi C_{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\pi^{-1/2}2^{2H-2}\frac{\Gamma(H-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(1-H)}=\pi^{-1/2}2^{2H-2}\frac{\Gamma(H-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(H)\sin(\pi H)}{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(\pi H)}{\pi}\Gamma(2H-1)=\frac{\sin(\pi H)}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(2H+1)}{2H(2H-1)}=\frac{c_{H}}{\alpha_{H}},

using the Gamma identities

\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)}
,
\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+\frac{1}{2})=2^{1-2z}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2z)
and

\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
(in this order), and the notation:

\alpha_{H}:=H(2H-1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad c_{H}:=\frac{\Gamma(2H+1)\sin(\pi H)}{2\pi}.

In this parametrization, relation (
8
) becomes:

\alpha_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)|x-y|^{2H-2}dxdy=c_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(\xi)}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi,

(9)

for any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, and

A_{\theta,w}=\frac{\alpha_{H}}{2\pi c_{H}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{\theta}(y)I_{w}(z)|y-z|^{2H-2}dydz.

Identity (
9
) holds also for non-negative functions
\varphi,\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for which
\mathcal{E}(\varphi)<\infty
and
\mathcal{E}(\psi)<\infty
, where

\mathcal{E}(\varphi):=\alpha_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)\varphi(y)|x-y|^{2H-2}dxdy=c_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi.

(Note that if
\varphi=1_{[0,t]}
and
\psi=1_{[0,s]}
for
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
, then (
9
) coincides with the covariance
\mathbb{E}[B_{t}B_{s}]
of a fractional Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of index
H
, where
1_{[0,t]}:=1_{[t,0]}
if
t<0
.)

In our case, we apply (
9
) to
I_{\theta},I_{w}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
:

A_{\theta,w}=\frac{\alpha_{H}}{2\pi c_{H}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{\theta}(y)I_{w}(z)|y-z|^{2H-2}dydz=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}I_{\theta}(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}I_{w}(\xi)}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi.

We calculate the Fourier transform of
I_{\theta}
. By Fubini’s theorem, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}I_{\theta}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi y}I_{\theta}(y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi y}\left(\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}G_{t}(x-y)dx\right)dy=\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}\mathcal{F}G_{t}(x-\cdot)(\xi)dx

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)}\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}e^{-i\xi x}dx=2\overline{\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)}\,\frac{\sin(|\xi|\theta)}{|\xi|}.

It follows that

\displaystyle A_{\theta.w}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\frac{\sin(|\xi|\theta)\sin(|\xi|w)}{|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi\leq\frac{2t^{2}}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin(|\xi|\theta)\sin(|\xi|w)}{|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi,

where for the last line we used the fact that
|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t}(x)dx=t
.

By formula 2.6 (3) on p. 78 of
[
9
]
,

c_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin(|\xi|\theta)\sin(|\xi|w)}{|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi=\frac{|\theta+w|^{2H}-|\theta-w|^{2H}}{4}.

(This expression coincides with the covariance
\mathbb{E}[B_{w}^{o}B_{\theta}^{o}]
, where
B_{t}^{o}=\frac{B_{t}-B_{-t}}{2}
is “odd part” of the
(B_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
; see relation (10) of
[
8
]
.) Therefore,

\displaystyle A_{\theta,w}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2\pi c_{H}}\big[(w+\theta)^{2H}-(w-\theta)^{2H}\big]=\frac{t^{2}H}{\pi c_{H}}\int_{w-\theta}^{w+\theta}x^{2H-1}dx=\frac{t^{2}H}{\pi c_{H}}\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}(w+z)^{2H-1}dz.

For any
z\in(-\theta,\theta)
,
w+z\geq w-\theta\geq 0
and hence

(w+z)^{2H-1}=|w+z|^{2H-1}\leq w^{2H-1}+|z|^{2H-1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle A_{\theta,w}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2}H}{\pi c_{H}}\left(2\theta w^{2H-1}+\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}|z|^{2H-1}dz\right)=\frac{t^{2}H}{\pi c_{H}}\left(2\theta w^{2H-1}+\frac{1}{H}\theta^{2H}\right).

By item (i) mentioned at the beginning of the proof,

\sigma_{\theta}\sim K(t)\theta^{H}
and
\sigma_{w}\sim K(t)w^{H}
. Hence,

\frac{A_{\theta,w}}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}\leq C_{t,H}\left[\left(\frac{\theta}{w}\right)^{1-H}+\left(\frac{\theta}{w}\right)^{H}\right],

where
C_{t,H}>0
is a constant that depends on
(t,H)
. Relation (
5
) now follows by (
7
) and Lemma
A.1
below.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
F_{R}(t)
be the spatial average given by (
2
), where
u(t,x)
is the solution of (HAM) driven by the Gaussian noise
F
:

F_{t}(\varphi)=W_{t}(\varphi*\kappa),\quad\mbox{for}\ t>0,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}),

and
W
is the space-time Gaussian white noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. Note that
F
is the spatially-homogeneous Gaussian noise introduced in
[
6
]
, which is white in time and has covariance:

\mathbb{E}[F_{t}(\varphi)F_{s}(\psi)]=(t\wedge s)\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi*\kappa)(x)(\psi*\kappa)(x)dx=(t\wedge s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)f(x-y)dxdy.

If
\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
or
\kappa=R_{1,\alpha/2}
, properties
(i)
and
(ii)
still hold, as shown in
[
7
]
.
Regarding property
(iii)
, we notice that the first chaos term in the series expansion of
u(t,x)
is:

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(x-y)F(dr,dy)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(G_{t-r}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)W(dr,dy).

Therefore,
\big(G_{t-r}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)
is the first term (i.e. the mean) which appears in the series expansion of the Malliavin derivative
D_{r,y}^{W}u(t,x)
of
u(t,x)
,
with respect to
W
. (Note that
D_{r,y}^{W}u(t,x)
is different than the usual Malliavin derivative
D_{r,y}u(t,x)
of
u(t,x)
, with respect to
F
.) Using a well-established procedure (see e.g.
[
7
]
), it can be shown that this first term dominates the other terms, in the sense that the following property holds:

(iii)’
for any
0\leq r\leq t
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p\geq 2
,

\|D_{r,y}^{W}u(t,x)\|_{p}\leq C_{t}^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(x-y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})dy^{\prime},

where
C_{t}^{\prime}>0
is a constant depending on
t
.

To prove the ASCLT, we use the same procedure as above. Let
H_{\theta}
be the same as above. By the Clark-Ocone formula with respect to
W
,

H_{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[D_{r,y}^{W}H_{\theta}|\mathcal{F}_{r}]W(dr,dy),

where
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the filtration induced by
W
. From this, we infer that:

|\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]|\leq\frac{{\rm Lip}^{2}(h)}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|D_{r,y}^{W}F_{\theta}\|_{2}\|D_{r,y}^{W}F_{w}\|_{2}dydr.

(10)

By Minkowski’s inequality, property
(iii)’
, and Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|D_{r,y}^{W}F_{\theta}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}\|D_{r,y}^{W}u(t,x)\|_{2}dx\leq C_{t}\int_{-\theta}^{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(x-y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle=C_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,\theta}(r,y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}.

Inserting this estimate into (
10
), we obtain:

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]|\leq

\displaystyle\quad\frac{{\rm Lip}^{2}(h)}{\sigma_{\theta}\sigma_{w}}C_{t}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,\theta}(r,y^{\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,\theta}(r,y^{\prime\prime})\kappa(y-y^{\prime\prime})dy^{\prime\prime}\right)dydr.

Then, the same argument as above shows that if
\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
or
\kappa=R_{1,\alpha/2}
, then the process
\{\widetilde{F}_{\theta}\}_{\theta>0}
defined by (

) satisfies the ASCLT.

An auxiliary result

The following result is a version of Lemma 3.1 of

4
]
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\{H_{\theta}\}_{\theta>0}
be a family uniformly bounded random variables such that

\big|\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]\big|\leq C\left[\left(\frac{\theta}{w}\right)^{\beta_{1}}+\left(\frac{\theta}{w}\right)^{\beta_{2}}\right]\quad\mbox{for any $\theta<w$},

for some
C,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}>0
. Assume that
\theta\mapsto H_{\theta}
is a measurable
function almost surely. Then,

\displaystyle L_{T}:=\frac{1}{\log T}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{1}{\theta}H_{\theta}d\theta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\quad\mbox{as $T\to\infty$}.

Proof.

The second moment of
L_{T}
is estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[L_{T}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\log T)^{2}}\int_{1}^{T}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{1}{\theta w}\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]\,d\theta dw=\frac{2}{(\log T)^{2}}\int_{1<\theta<w<T}\frac{1}{\theta w}\mathbb{E}[H_{\theta}H_{w}]\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{(\log T)^{2}}\int_{1<\theta<w<T}\frac{1}{\theta w}\left[\left(\frac{\theta}{w}\right)^{\beta_{1}}+\left(\frac{\theta}{w}\right)^{\beta_{2}}\right]\,d\theta dw=\frac{2C}{\log T}\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{2}}\right)

using the fact that for any
\beta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{1<\theta<w<T}\frac{1}{\theta w}\left(\frac{\theta}{w}\right)^{\beta}d\theta dw=\int_{1}^{T}\left(\int_{1}^{w}\theta^{\beta-1}d\theta\right)w^{-\beta-1}dw=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{1}^{T}w^{\beta}w^{\beta-1}dw=\frac{\log T}{\beta}.

The rest of the proof is the same as in Lemma 3.1 of
[
4
]
.

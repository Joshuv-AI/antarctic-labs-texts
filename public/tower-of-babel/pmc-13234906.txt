MolFoundry: A Structure-Aware Generative Framework for De Novo Design of PRRSV Nucleocapsid Candidate Binders
Authors: Jindong Hao, Weibo Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Motivation: despite being one of the most economically devastating swine pathogens, porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (PRRSV) currently lacks approved antiviral therapeutics. The viral nucleocapsid (N) protein, being structurally conserved and indispensable for genome packaging, presents an attractive yet underexplored target for small-molecule inhibitor design. However, contemporary generative frameworks are predominantly structure-agnostic, frequently failing to reconcile geometric complementarity with drug-likeness and synthetic accessibility. Results: here, we present MolFoundry, a structure-aware de novo molecular design framework. MolFoundry integrates an SE(3)-equivariant pocket–ligand affinity scorer, a pocket-conditioned cross-attention generator, and a deterministic multiobjective optimization module guided by Pareto ranking and medicinal chemistry constraints. Trained on a comprehensive data set of 18,412 PDBbind complexes and specifically deployed against the PRRSV N-protein pocket, the framework generates highly diverse, synthetically tractable molecules with superior geometric consistency. Crucially, MolFoundry outperforms state-of-the-art diffusion, transformer, and reinforcement learning baselines across key metrics, achieving exceptional validity (0.938), perfect novelty (1.000), robust internal diversity (0.918), and highly favorable predicted binding affinity (−9.201 kcal/mol). Comprehensive ablation studies further substantiate that explicit pocket conditioning and multiobjective optimization are paramount for securing balanced pharmacological and affinity profiles. Data and software availability: MolFoundry is a Python-based molecular generation framework. The project code and documentation are available in the GitHub repository at https://github.com/hjd20030114-blip/MolFoundry.git under the MIT License.

Introduction

Porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (PRRSV) continues to cause substantial economic losses to the swine industry worldwide. Despite the development of multiple vaccine platforms, rapid viral evolution, immune evasion, and persistent infection severely limit their effectiveness. Currently, no antiviral small-molecule therapy has been approved. Recent structural studies have identified the nucleocapsid (N) proteina highly conserved, RNA-binding structural element essential for genome encapsidation and virion assemblyas a promising therapeutic target. Similar nucleocapsid-directed strategies have been successfully explored in SARS-CoV-2, influenza A, and Nipah virus, suggesting a generalizable antiviral mechanism. Specifically, the RNA-binding groove of the PRRSV N protein (PDB: 1P65) presents a druggable cavity with a moderate volume (∼680 Å3) and favorable physicochemical properties (hydrophobicity index = 0.42, enclosure = 0.68). However, compared to classical enzyme active sites, the relatively shallow and solvent-exposed nature of this pocket poses significant challenges for achieving high-affinity binding.

Concurrently, deep generative models have transformed the landscape of molecular design. Transformer-based methods, variational autoencoders, and denoising diffusion models can efficiently explore chemical space to generate syntactically valid and highly novel molecules. However, most existing frameworks remain structure-agnostic, failing to incorporate protein pocket geometry directly into the generative process. This limitation frequently yields molecules with poor geometric complementarity, limited potency, and suboptimal physicochemical profiles. Furthermore, existing structure-aware models face specific technical hurdles: (1) models such as DiffDock or Pocket2Mol often struggle to ensure chemical synthesizability; (2) multiobjective optimization strategies typically rely on unstable scalarized reward functions that fail to balance conflicting pharmacological properties; and (3) physics-based evaluations (e.g., molecular docking) remain computationally prohibitive within tight propose-and-evaluate iterative loops.

These limitations highlight the urgent need for a structure-aware, geometrically consistent, and pharmacologically informed generative model. To address these structural and computational challenges simultaneously, we introduce MolFoundry, a unified pipeline for pocket-conditioned de novo molecular generation. By explicitly conditioning molecular generation on the 3D geometry of the binding pocket, MolFoundry optimally explores the chemical space tailored to the specific topological and electrostatic features of the PRRSV N-protein cavity.

As illustrated in Figure , the MolFoundry pipeline integrates geometric deep learning, attention-guided generation, and multiobjective optimization to fundamentally improve the potency and synthesizability of generated inhibitors. Specifically, the framework is architected around three core technical modules: (i) module 1 introduces an SE(3)-equivariant affinity scorer that directly encodes 3D atom geometry, serving as a rapid, structure-aware surrogate for docking while providing essential pocket embeddings (h pocket); (ii) module 2 features a pocket-conditioned cross-attention generator that dynamically embeds this pocket geometry into an autoregressive graph decoder for context-aware chemical assembly; and (iii) module 3 deploys a deterministic multiobjective optimization engine utilizing Pareto-front rankingaugmented by a targeted reinforcement learning (RL) refinement loopto rigorously balance predicted affinity, QED, synthetic accessibility (SA), and core physicochemical properties. Through extensive benchmarking and ablation studies, we demonstrate that MolFoundry significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in validity, novelty, internal diversity, and predicted binding affinity, establishing it as a robust framework for structure-aware antiviral design.

Schematic overview of the MolFoundry pipeline. The framework comprises three highly integrated modules for structure-aware de novo molecular design. Module 1 employs an SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network (EGNN) to process joint pocket–ligand graphs, providing rapid affinity predictions and crucial structural embeddings (h pocket). Materials and Methods

Target Preparation and Data Set Construction

The crystal structure of the PRRSV nucleocapsid (N) protein was retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB ID: 1P65, 2.60 Å resolution). − Chain A was utilized for all subsequent calculations. To prepare the target, missing side chains were reconstructed utilizing PDBFixer, followed by protonation at a physiological pH of 7.4. , Crystallographic water molecules residing beyond a 5 Å radius of the putative RNA-binding groove were stripped. The refined structure subsequently underwent energy minimization (1000 steps of steepest descent) employing the AMBER14 force field. The explicit ligand-binding pocket was defined by residues within 6.5 Å , of the modeled RNA-binding region documented in the original structural study.

For generative model development, we curated data from the PDBbind-Plus “general set,” which encompasses 19,037 diverse protein–ligand complexes. Pockets were standardized to include residues within 6–10 Å , of the cognate ligand. Complexes exhibiting incomplete atomic coordinates were systematically discarded, yielding a high-quality corpus of 18,412 complexes. To mimic stringent virtual screening scenarios, negative decoys were generated by randomly cross-pairing ligands with noncognate pockets at a 1:1 ratio utilizing a fixed random seed. This binary data set was partitioned based on scaffolds into training (80%), validation (10%), and test (10%) sets. The training and validation cohorts were further subjected to 5-fold stratified cross-validation.

Molecular and pocket graph representations protein pockets were abstracted as 3D graphs G p=(V p, E p), where each node i ∈ V p denotes an atom annotated with 12 structural features (e.g., element type, formal charge, hybridization, solvent-accessible surface area, and distance to the pocket centroid). , Ligands were analogously represented as graphs G l=(V l, E l) encompassing both node features (element, aromaticity, partial charge) and edge properties (bond order, conjugation). Interatomic distance geometry was embedded utilizing 16 radial basis function (RBF) kernels. Additionally, 2048 bit ECFP4 fingerprints were computed via RDKit to assess structural novelty and internal diversity.

SE­(3)-Equivariant Pocket–Ligand Affinity Scorer

To establish a computationally efficient, structure-aware surrogate for docking evaluations, we implemented an SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network (EGNN) , functioning over the joint pocket–ligand topology. Node features (h i ) and spatial coordinates (x i ) are iteratively updated according to the following operations hi(l+1)=ϕh(hi(l),∑j∈N(i)ϕm(hi(l),hj(l),dij)) xi(l+1)=xi(l)+1Ci∑j∈N(i)(xi(l)−xj(l))ϕx(hi(l),hj(l),dij) where d ij are RBF-encoded distances and ϕ h, ϕ m , ϕ x are MLPs shared across layers. The network consists of 6 EGNN layers with hidden size 128 and dropout 0.1. After message passing, node embeddings are aggregated by global mean pooling and fed into a two-layer MLP regression head to predict a scalar binding affinity (approximating the negative docking energy).The scorer was trained with mean squared error loss against experimentally measured binding affinities (K i/K d/IC50) from the PDBbind database. AutoDock Vina docking scores were used as an independent validation metric on the PDBbind complexes. We used AdamW with learning rate 2 × 10–4, batch size 32, weight decay 1 × 10–5, and cosine learning-rate decay for 80 epochs. The final model was selected by validation RMSE and used both as (i) an offline evaluator for baselines and (ii) an online filter during generation. The pocket embedding z p obtained by pooling only pocket nodes was cached and reused as a conditioning vector for the generator and the multiobjective optimizer, ensuring representational consistency across stages.

Pocket-Conditioned Molecular Generator

MolFoundry’s generative component is a conditional autoregressive graph generator p(G l |z p )­that constructs molecules atom-by-atom in the presence of the pocket embedding. − We first map z p to a sequence of pocket tokens using transformer encoder (12 layers, 8 attention heads, , hidden size 512). Ligand nodes are represented by contextual embeddings h l updated by a stack of 4 cross-attention layers Attn(Hl,Hp)=softmax(HlWQ(HpWK)Tdk)HpWV where H p are pocket token embeddings and H l are ligand tokens. The resulting pocket-aware ligand embeddings parametrize a graph decoder that iteratively samples the next atom type, bond type and attachment position under valency and ring-closure constraints. For each pocket, we sample 2000 candidate molecules. Immediately after generation, each molecule is evaluated by the EGNN scorer; candidates with predicted affinity worse than −6.0 kcal/mol or violating basic valency rules are discarded. The remaining molecules constitute the screened library used for multiobjective optimization.

Multi-Objective Optimization and Reinforcement Learning Refinement

Each screened molecule is annotated with Predicted affinity Â from the EGNN scorer; drug-likeness (QED); Synthetic accessibility (SA score); Physicochemical descriptors: molecular weight (M W), log P, H-bond donors/acceptors (HBD/HBA), and topological polar surface area (TPSA) We first enforce soft filters corresponding to typical lead-like space (250 ≤ M W ≤ 500, 1 ≤ log P ≤ 3, 0 ≤ HBD ≤ 5, 0 ≤ HBA ≤ 10). Remaining molecules are subjected to nondominated sorting over three maximization objectives: transformed affinity Ã, QED, and 1 – SA∼ where tildes denote min–max normalization within the library. Molecules on the first Pareto front are further ranked by a composite score: S = W 1Ã + W 2QED + W 3 (1−SA∼) ), with default weights W 1 = 0.5, W 2 = 0.3, W 3 = 0.2. We refer to this deterministic procedure as Multi-Obj. To explore under-sampled regions of chemical space, we optionally fine-tune the generator with a lightweight reinforcement learning (RL) phase using policy-gradient updates. ,, The reward is defined as the composite score S clipped to [0,1]. For RL, we freeze the pocket encoder and train only the generator parameters with REINFORCE and a moving-average baseline for 10 additional epochs, using a smaller learning rate (5 × 10–5) to avoid mode collapse. The ablation “w/o RL” disables this refinement and uses the pretrained generator with Multi-Obj only.

Molecular Docking and Baseline Evaluation Protocol

Orthogonal molecular docking was executed utilizing AutoDock Vina v1.2.5 against the PRRSV N-protein pocket (PDB ID: 1P65). A bounding grid (dimensions: 55.85 × 49.77 × 70.43 Å) was centered precisely over the RNA-binding groove (x = −0.24, y = 26.06, z = 65.48). To ensure a highly rigorous and equitable comparison across generative architectures, all evaluated models (including baselines) were constrained to produce an identical volume of candidate molecules (N = 10,000 per model). The aggregate libraries were processed through a unified postprocessing pipelineencompassing RDKit-based SMILES validation, canonical deduplication, and Lipinski filteringguaranteeing that benchmark deviations reflect intrinsic algorithmic capabilities rather than statistical sampling artifacts.

To clarify, “validation accuracy” reported in Figure a refers to pair-discrimination accuracy computed on a balanced validation benchmark derived from the held-out folds during 5-fold stratified cross-validation. Positive samples correspond to experimentally confirmed cognate protein–ligand pairs from PDBbind, whereas negative samples are randomly cross-paired noncognate pocket–ligand combinations at a 1:1 ratio. Continuous model outputs were converted to binary decisions using the same thresholding protocol determined on the training folds for all methods, and accuracy was computed as accuracy = (TP + TN)/(TP + TN + FP + FN). The box plots in Figure a summarize the distribution of this metric across the five validation folds.

To rigorously support our comparative claims, we performed pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (exact method, appropriate for the small sample size of 5-fold CV) comparing MolFoundry against each baseline model. The statistical results are as follows: MolFoundry vs SMILES-Transformer (p = 0.125, Cohen’s d = 1.23), MolFoundry vs BIMODAL (p = 0.063, Cohen’s d = 5.31), MolFoundry vs QADD (p = 0.063, Cohen’s d = 2.57), and MolFoundry vs Diffusion (p = 0.063, Cohen’s d = 2.28). It is important to note that with a sample size of 5, the minimum achievable exact two-sided p-value for the Wilcoxon signed-rank test is 0.0625, mathematically precluding conventional significance at alpha = 0.05. However, the exceptionally large effect sizes (Cohen’s d > 0.8 across all comparisons) robustly demonstrate that the performance advantages of MolFoundry are practically and highly meaningful. Furthermore, 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples) for MolFoundry’s validation accuracy are narrow [0.868, 0.885], strongly confirming the reliability and stability of our generative framework.

To assess the quality of generated molecules in terms of predicted binding affinity, we docked the molecules from each method into the PRRSV N-protein pocket , and summarized the score distributions in Figure b. MolFoundry produces ligands with the most favorable docking scores, as reflected by the left-shifted violin and lower median binding energy compared with BIMODAL, QADD, SMILES-Transformer and diffusion baselines. The BIMODAL and QADD distributions are moderately strong, but still shifted toward weaker binding, while the diffusion and SMILES-Transformer models generate a substantial fraction of molecules with relatively poor predicted affinities. These results demonstrate that the combination of an SE(3)-equivariant scorer and pocket-conditioned generator allows MolFoundry to discover ligands that are not only valid and novel, but also consistently predicted to bind more strongly to the PRRSV N-protein pocket.

Ablation Performance of MolFoundry Under Different configurations

Removing pocket conditioning leads to the most pronounced degradation in performance. Compared with the full model, the “w/o Pocket” variant shows reduced validity, novelty, internal diversity, and QED. Importantly, the optimal binding energy becomes noticeably weaker, indicating that pocket information plays a crucial role in steering generation toward high-affinity ligands. Disabling the multiobjective optimizer (“w/o Multi-Obj”) produces molecules with slightly improved internal diversity but clearly worse QED and binding affinity, suggesting that the optimizer is essential for balancing potency and drug-likeness. Overall, the ablation study confirms that all three components contribute to the final performance, with pocket conditioning and multiobjective optimization being particularly important.

Discussion

The results above highlight several key advantages of the MolFoundry framework. First, by tightly coupling an SE(3)-equivariant pocket–ligand scorer with a pocket-conditioned generator, MolFoundry consistently outperforms strong sequence-based and ligand-only baselines in both validation accuracy and docking-predicted binding affinity (Figure a,b). This suggests that explicitly modeling 3D pocket geometry and using it as a conditioning signal during generation is more effective than treating the protein indirectly or ignoring it altogether. In the context of PRRSV N-protein inhibitor design, where the target pocket is relatively shallow and structurally constrained, such precise geometric awareness appears to be particularly beneficial.

Our baseline selection was designed to cover the major architectural paradigms in molecular generation: BIMODAL represents RNN-based autoregressive generation; QADD represents reinforcement learning-guided property optimization; SMILES-Transformer represents sequence-to-sequence attention-based generation; and Diffusion represents score-based generative modeling. These four baselines collectively span the dominant methodological families in the field.

Limitations and future experimental validation. We acknowledge several limitations of the current study. First, all binding affinity evaluations are based on computational methods (AutoDock Vina docking scores and EGNN surrogate predictions), which, while widely used in virtual screening, represent approximations of true binding behavior. Orthogonal experimental validation remains essential to confirm the predicted activities. Second, the negative sampling strategy employs random pocket–ligand cross-pairing, which may produce relatively easy negatives and thus provide optimiztic classification accuracy estimates. Third, direct comparison with the most recent pocket-conditioned diffusion models (TargetDiff, Pocket2Mol, DiffSBDD) requires retraining on our PRRSV-specific data, which is planned for future work. To address these limitations, we outline the following experimental validation roadmap: (1) surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) to measure binding affinities of top candidates; (2) cell-based antiviral assays in PRRSV-infected MARC-145 cells to assess antiviral activity (EC50) and cytotoxicity (CC50); (3) co-crystallization or cryo-EM to verify predicted binding modes; and (4) preliminary in vivo studies in a porcine model. These experiments are currently being planned in collaboration with our virology colleagues at Zhejiang Sci-tech University.

We clarify that the EGNN affinity scorer was trained on the PDBbind database, where binding labels are derived from experimentally measured binding affinities (K i, K d, or IC50 values) reported in the original literature, not from Vina-computed scores. Therefore, the scorer learns to predict experimental binding affinities from 3D structural features, providing an independent assessment that is complementary torather than redundant withVina docking scores. However, we acknowledge that (1) the negative samples were generated through random cross-pairing, which may not fully represent the difficulty of real-world inactive compounds, and (2) the model’s generalization to entirely novel protein families beyond PDBbind warrants further investigation.

We acknowledge that the negative sampling strategy employed in training the affinity scorerrandom cross-pairing of pocket and ligand structures from different PDBbind complexesmay generate relatively easy negatives that do not fully capture the difficulty of distinguishing true binders from structurally similar but inactive decoys. This limitation could lead to optimiztic classification accuracy estimates. More sophisticated negative sampling strategies, such as using DUD-E decoys or property-matched decoys, could provide harder negatives and a more stringent evaluation. Nevertheless, the primary role of the affinity scorer within MolFoundry is to provide relative ranking among generated candidates rather than absolute binding affinity prediction, and our PDBbind test set evaluation (Table ) demonstrates that the scorer generalizes well to unseen complexes with Pearson r = 0.911.

To evaluate the robustness of MolFoundry to the multiobjective weight configuration, we performed a systematic weight sensitivity analysis using Dirichlet sampling. We generated 200 random weight vectors w = (w_aff, w_qed, w_sa) from a symmetric Dirichlet distribution (α = 1.0) and evaluated the resulting multiobjective scores. The analysis reveals that MolFoundry maintains stable performance across a wide range of weight configurations: the coefficient of variation (CV) of the composite score is below 15% for all sampled weight vectors. Pareto front analysis identifies clear trade-offs between binding affinity and synthetic accessibility, with the default weights (w_aff = 0.5, w_qed = 0.3, w_sa = 0.2) located near the Pareto optimal region. These results confirm that the framework is not overly sensitive to the specific weight configuration and that practitioners can adjust weights based on their priorities without catastrophic performance degradation.

We acknowledge the importance of comparing MolFoundry with recent pocket-conditioned 3D diffusion models, including TargetDiff, Pocket2Mol, and DiffSBDD. These methods generate molecular 3D coordinates directly within the binding pocket using equivariant diffusion processes, representing the state-of-the-art in structure-based drug design. However, direct comparison is complicated by several factors: (1) these models were designed and evaluated on the CrossDocked2020 benchmark rather than PDBbind; (2) they generate 3D atom clouds that require posthoc bond perception, whereas MolFoundry generates SMILES strings with guaranteed chemical validity; and (3) retraining these models on our PRRSV-specific data would require substantial computational resources and careful hyperparameter tuning. We plan to include these comparisons in future work once standardized evaluation protocols across generation paradigms are established. Nonetheless, MolFoundry offers complementary advantages including interpretable SMILES output, integrated multiobjective optimization, and end-to-end pocket-to-molecule generation without separate docking steps.

The reported novelty score of 1.000 indicates that none of the generated SMILES strings appeared in the training set, representing fingerprint-level (string-level) novelty. To provide a more informative assessment of structural diversity, we computed scaffold-level novelty using Murcko decomposition. The analysis reveals that MolFoundry achieves a scaffold novelty of 0.947 (i.e., 94.7% of generated molecules contain Murcko scaffolds not present in the training set), with 156 unique scaffolds among 200 valid generated molecules (scaffold diversity = 0.780). In comparison, SMILES-Transformer achieves scaffold novelty of 0.912, BIMODAL 0.876, QADD 0.834, and Diffusion 0.891. The high scaffold novelty of MolFoundry demonstrates that the framework achieves supports nontrivial scaffold exploration beyond exact-string novelty, exploring diverse chemical scaffolds rather than trivial modifications of training set molecules.

We agree that molecular dynamics (MD) simulations would substantially strengthen the validation of our docking results by assessing pose stability. While full MD simulations are beyond the scope of the current computational framework paper, we note that the AutoDock Vina docking protocol employed generates multiple binding poses (num_modes = 20) and selects the lowest-energy conformation. The top-ranked poses for our best candidates show consistent binding modes across independent docking runs (pose RMSD < 2.0 Å between the top 3 modes), suggesting convergent and reproducible binding geometries. Second, MolFoundry’s deterministic multiobjective optimization strategy effectively constrains generated molecules to occupy a desirable region of medicinal-chemistry space. The distributions of logP, HBA and TPSA (Figures c-e) show that MolFoundry avoids the extreme values produced by some baselines, which are often associated with poor pharmacokinetic properties or low developability. Instead, the method yields candidates that simultaneously satisfy binding, drug-likeness and synthetic accessibility criteria. This balance is further reflected in the representative top candidates (Figure a), which combine favorable docking scores with chemically reasonable scaffolds and substituent patterns.

Third, the docking poses of MolFoundry-generated molecules (Figure b) provide structural support for the predicted activity. The ligands occupy the RNA-binding groove of the PRRSV nucleocapsid and form coherent interaction networks with residues that have been implicated in RNA binding and oligomerization. The recurring involvement of residues such as Ser44, Gln29 and Glu52 across multiple top candidates suggests that MolFoundry has learned to exploit key interaction hotspots in the pocket. This structural consistency increases confidence that at least a subset of the designed molecules may exhibit real binding in experimental assays.

Finally, the ablation results (Table ) offer mechanistic insight into how different components of the framework contribute to these outcomes. Pocket conditioning clearly provides the largest performance gain, reinforcing the central hypothesis that protein geometry should be treated as a first-class input to the generative model. Multiobjective optimization is equally critical, as it prevents the model from sacrificing drug-likeness in pursuit of extreme affinity scores. Reinforcement learning refinement offers an additional, but more incremental, benefit by modestly enhancing diversity and affinity. Taken together, these findings indicate that the full MolFoundry architecture is required to achieve the best trade-off between potency, drug-likeness and chemical diversity.

Conclusion

In summary, MolFoundry is a structure-aware generative framework that couples an SE(3)-equivariant pocket–ligand scorer, a pocket-conditioned molecular generator and deterministic multiobjective optimization to design candidate binders for the PRRSV nucleocapsid protein. By propagating pocket geometry throughout scoring, generation and ranking, it consistently yields molecules with stronger predicted binding affinity, better drug-likeness and competitive diversity compared with state-of-the-art baselines, while maintaining high validity and novelty. Docking analyses and ablation studies further confirm that explicit pocket conditioning and multiobjective reasoning are key to these gains. Given its modular design, MolFoundry can be readily adapted to other viral or human targets, providing a practical route to accelerate structure-based antiviral drug discovery.

Coexistence of Rashba and Ising Spin-Singlet Pairings in Two-Dimensional IrTe 2
Kunal Dutta 1, Rajesh O. Sharma 2,3, Shreya Das 4,5, Indra Dasgupta 1, Tanmoy Das 2, Tanusri Saha-Dasgupta 4

Abstract
Symmetry offers a useful approach to unfold the intertwined degrees of freedom. Thus it paves the way to resolve coexisting quantum orders into distinct symmetry sectors. Motivated by the recent observation of superconductivity in nano-flaked IrTe 2 , we investigate the superconductivity in strain-stabilized two-dimensional (2D) limit of IrTe 2 by combining density-functional theory with mean-field solution of spin-fluctuation mediated pairing interaction on a symmetry-constrained 𝐤 ⋅ 𝐩 {\bf k}\cdot{\bf p} model. The spin-orbit coupled band structure shows Γ \Gamma -centred Fermi sheets with coexistence of band-selective Rashba-like (in-plane) and Ising-like (out-of-plane) superconductivity. Remarkably, the superconducting gaps are odd in spin, orbital, and momentum channels despite the presence of global inversion symmetry. Fermi surface topologies and little-group symmetry enforce distinct irreducible representations to the Rashba and Ising channels, forbidding their mixing. Our findings open up a symmetry-based route to multichannel superconductivity in 2D transition-metal dichalcogenides with unique functionalities.

Recently, the extraordinary properties driven by the interplay between emergent spin-orbit coupling (SOC) and crystalline symmetry in 2D materials have attracted considerable attention

Wang et al. 2019

Based on the guiding principle of SOC-induced band splittings around high-symmetry points,

;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Jureczko et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2020

In this letter, we focus on the unexplored case of a monolayer of IrTe

(b) These Rashba and Ising pairings coexist but do not mix, allowing for the clean disentanglement of in-plane versus out-of-plane spin pairings into distinct bands. This further opens up a broader landscape of possible realization of intriguing symmetry-driven non-trivial superconductivity in the general class of 2D TMDs in the parameter space of filling, SOC, and crystal symmetry.

Symmetry resolved Cooper pair:

Enforcing symmetries with fermion exchange is subtle. Following Ref.
Sharma and Das 2025
, we apply symmetries in the product basis for the Cooper pair wavefunction, defined as

\Psi({\bf k})=\mathsf{P}(\mathbb{Z}_{\bf k}\otimes\mathbb{W}\otimes\mathbb{X}),

(4)

\mathbb{X}_{ss^{\prime}}=\mathsf{P}_{X}\left[{X}_{s}\otimes{X}_{s^{\prime}}\right]

\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{P}_{Z}\mathsf{P}_{W}\mathsf{P}_{X}

(Fig. 3(c,f)), while that in inner FS sheet follows the Rashba spin texture
\langle\sigma_{x,y}\rangle
(Figs. 3(a-e)).
Note, the Rashba pairing is helical due to the strong in-plane SOC.
This suggests both Ising SOC (
h_{zz}
) and
Rashba SOC (
h_{xz},h_{yz})
drive pairing between opposite spins and orbitals at
\pm{\bf k}
in the corresponding electronic structure of 2D IrTe
2
, albeit in two different FSs.
2D IrTe
2
thus presents a rare case of band-selective co-existence of Ising and Rashba superconductivity.

Conclusion:
We uncover in strain-stabilized monolayer IrTe
2
a band-selective coexistence of Rashba (in-plane) and Ising (out-of-plane) spin texture induced Cooper pairings. Both pairings are odd under fermion exchange in spin, orbital, and momentum channels, and protected from mixing by inversion symmetry and distinct irreducible representations. This unique situation overcomes the parity-mixing and singlet-triplet mixing limitations of inversion-broken Rashba systems. This in turn enables effective spin filtering, large and anisotropic
H_{c2}
, and spin-sensitive Josephson responses that selectively probe in-plane versus out-of-plane Cooper pairs. Our investigation on 2D IrTe
2
thus open up the way for symmetry-and-spin-texture engineering as a general strategy to realize multichannel odd pairing in 2D TMDs. This may be tuned via strain, carrier density, and electric field.

S8
Details of the pairing symmetry calculation

We consider a multi-orbital Hubbard model given by

H_{I}=\sum_{{\bf q},m_{l},m^{\prime}_{l},m_{s},m_{s}^{\prime}}V_{m_{l},m^{\prime}_{l}}n_{m_{l},m_{s}}({\bf q})n_{m_{l}^{\prime},m_{s}^{\prime}}(-{\bf q}).

(22)

Here,
V_{m_{l},m^{\prime}_{l}}
incorporates both intra-orbital (
m_{l}=m^{\prime}_{l}
,
m_{s}\neq m^{\prime}_{s}
) contribution, which we denote by
U_{m_{l}}
, and the inter-orbital contribution
m_{l}\neq m^{\prime}_{l}
, where
m_{s}
and
m_{s}^{\prime}
can be the same spin or different.
n_{m_{l},m_{s}}({\bf q})=\sum_{\bf k}c^{\dagger}_{m_{l},m_{s}}({\bf k}+{\bf q})c_{m_{l},m_{s}}({\bf k})
is the density matrix between different orbitals
(m_{l},m_{l}^{\prime})
, spins
(m_{s},m_{s}^{\prime})
, and a momentum transfer of
{\bf q}
. The operators
c^{\dagger}_{m_{l},m_{s}}({\bf k})
correspond to the creation of electrons in the
m_{l},m_{s}
basis with momentum
{\bf k}
. All momentum summations are assumed to be weighted with the Brillouin zone volume. We ignore the spin exchange and pair exchange interactions as they possess less contribution.

Now, performing the standard RPA-level perturbation expansion of
H_{I}
,
Scalapino et al. 1986
;
Schrieffer et al. 1989
;
Sigrist and Ueda 1991b
;
Takimoto et al. 2004
;
Mazin et al. 2008
;
Graser et al. 2009
;
Scalapino 2012
;
Das et al. 2014
, and collecting the terms which give an effective pairing interaction
V_{\nu_{l}}^{\nu_{s}}({\bf q})
, where
\nu_{l}
corresponds to the three irreps of the orbitals
(A_{1g}
,
A_{2g}
, and
E_{g}
) that are defined in Eq. 5 in the main text, and
\nu_{s}=\pm
corresponds to the spin-triplet and spin-singlet irreps. Here, we are not using the irreps of the Bloch phase
Z({\bf q})
, although it can be easily done. Then the effective interaction term with the RPA correction can be written in terms of the pairing fields
\Psi({\bf k})
, in (Eq. 4), in the
\nu_{l},\nu_{s}
channels as

H_{I,{\rm RPA}}=\sum_{\nu_{l},\nu_{s}}\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf q}}V_{\nu_{l},\nu^{\prime}_{l}}^{\nu_{s},\nu^{\prime}_{s}}(\textbf{q})\Psi^{\dagger}_{\nu_{l},\nu_{s}}(\textbf{k})\Psi_{\nu^{\prime}_{l},\nu^{\prime}_{s}}(\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}).

(23)

In the second quantization, the pair fields are written as

\Psi_{\nu_{l},\nu_{s}}(\textbf{k})=\mathsf{P}_{-}\left[\sum_{m_{l},m_{l}^{\prime}}\sum_{m_{s},m_{s}^{\prime}}U_{\nu_{l}}^{m_{l},m_{l}^{\prime}}U_{\nu_{s}}^{m_{s},m_{s}^{\prime}}\left(c_{m_{l},m_{s}}(-\textbf{k})c_{m_{l}^{\prime},m_{s}^{\prime}}(\textbf{k})\right)\right]

. Here
U_{\nu_{l}}^{m_{l},m^{\prime}_{l}}
gives the transformation from the two-orbital product state to the irreps state, and
U_{\nu_{s}}^{m_{s},m_{s}^{\prime}}
gives the same for the spins.
\mathsf{P}_{-}
ensures the pair fields remain antisymmetric under the change of orbitals, spins, and also momentum.

The pair interaction
V_{\nu_{l},\nu^{\prime}_{l}}^{\nu_{s},\nu^{\prime}_{s}}
in this form is assumed to mix different irreps in both spin and orbital channels, in general. Such mixing can occur when the interaction term allows spin or pair exchange terms. Also, because we have not used the irreps of the Bloch phase above, at a given momentum, different orbital irreps can, in principle, mix. In the actual calculation, we, however, find that the different irreps do not mix. The pair interaction term can be transformed to the orbital basis in a similar way as
V_{\nu_{l},\nu^{\prime}_{l}}^{\nu_{s},\nu^{\prime}_{s}}(\textbf{q})=\sum_{\{m_{l}\}}U_{\nu_{l}}^{m_{l},m^{\prime}_{l}}U_{\nu^{\prime}_{l}}^{m^{\prime\prime}_{l},m^{\prime\prime\prime}_{l}}\left(V_{\{m_{l}\}}^{\nu_{s},\nu^{\prime}_{s}}(\textbf{q})\right)
.
\{m_{l}\}=(m_{l},m^{\prime}_{l},m^{\prime\prime}_{l},m^{\prime\prime\prime}_{l})
. We only have
\nu_{s}=\nu_{s}^{\prime}=\pm
terms for the spin singlet and spin triplets, and no singlet-triplet mixing is found here. Therefore, we have the pairing potential in the orbital basis is then written as

\displaystyle V^{\nu_{s}}_{\{m_{l}\}}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\nu_{s}}{2}\Big[\eta_{\nu_{s}}U_{s}\chi_{s}(\textbf{q})U_{s}+\nu_{s}U_{c}\chi_{c}(\textbf{q})U_{c}-\nu_{s}(U_{s}+U_{c})\Big]_{\{m_{l}\}}.

(24)

where
\eta_{\pm}=3
for
\nu_{s}=-1
and 1 for
\nu_{s}=+1
. The subscripts
s
and
c
refer to the spin and charge density fluctuation channels, respectively, and the corresponding on-site interaction matrices are represented by
U_{s/c}
. In the present case
U_{s}=U_{c}
and this matrix has intra-orbital interaction
U_{m_{l}}
in the diagonal entries and inter-orbital terms
V_{m_{l},m^{\prime}_{l}}
in the off-diagonal entries.

\chi_{s/c}
are the RPA density-density correlators for the spin and charge density channels. The RPA susceptibilites are related to the Lindhard susceptibility
\chi_{0}
as:

\chi_{s/c}(\textbf{q})=\chi_{0}(\textbf{q})\left({\mathbb{I}}\mp{U}_{s/c}{\chi}_{0}(\textbf{q})\right)^{-1}

(25)

where

is the unit matrix.

Association between pericoronary CT fat attenuation index and coronary artery spasm
Authors: Chen Yu, Zhen-Qiang Sheng, Lei Li, Shao-Ming Zhou, Ye-Yu Wu, Ye-Fei Li, Dong-Xue Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fat attenuation index (FAI) is currently used in research on coronary atherosclerotic heart disease, but there are no reports on FAI applications in coronary artery spasm (CAS). In this study, we explored the association between pericoronary FAI and CAS. Patients who had chest pain and stenosis < 50% in major coronary branches, as indicated by coronary CT angiography (CCTA) at our hospital from January 2022 to June 2023 were included. According to the diagnostic criteria for vasospastic angina published by the Japanese Circulation Society in 2013, patients confirmed to have CAS were assigned to a CAS group, while those who were not consistent with CAS and had a negative treadmill test were assigned to a control group. Pericoronary FAI and circulating inflammatory factor levels were measured at enrollment. Count data were analyzed using the χ2 test, measurement data using the t-test or nonparametric test, and CAS-related risk factors using multivariate logistic regression. There were 230 patients in the CAS group and 112 patients in the control group. The pericoronary FAI and smoking rate were significantly lower in the control group than in the CAS group (P < .05), but there were no significant differences in other data, including general clinical data, medication use, and circulating inflammatory factor levels (all P > .05). Univariate analysis exhibited that the CAS-related risk factors included smoking, diabetes, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and pericoronary FAI. Multivariate logistic regression indicated that smoking and FAI around the left anterior descending branch were significantly associated with CAS. Elevated pericoronary FAI is an independent risk factor for CAS. Local coronary inflammation plays a dominant role in CAS, while circulating inflammatory factor levels seem not to be associated with CAS.

1. Introduction

With the increasing popularity of coronary angiography, it has been found that a considerable proportion of patients with chest pain and myocardial ischemia have no obstructive lesions in the epicardial coronary arteries. Accordingly, recent studies have proposed the concepts of angina with nonobstructive coronary arteries (ANOCA), ischemia with nonobstructive coronary arteries (INOCA), and myocardial infarction with nonobstructive coronary arteries (MINOCA).[1] ANOCA and INOCA share common pathophysiological mechanisms, diagnostic workflows, and therapeutic strategies. The 2024 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Guidelines for the management of chronic coronary syndromes state that epicardial coronary artery spasm (CAS) and coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) represent the major mechanisms underlying ANOCA/INOCA.[2] CAS constitutes the primary pathological basis for vasospastic angina (VSA),[3–7] so findings from the present study provide direct implications for the clinical diagnosis and management of VSA. Some studies have suggested that circulating inflammatory factors are closely associated with the onset of CAS.[8–10] Conversely, other investigations argue that local coronary inflammation, rather than systemic inflammation, plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of CAS.[11,12]

The detection of coronary artery inflammation is a key tool for conducting clinical and basic research on coronary artery inflammation. Coronary CT angiography (CCTA) can detect attenuation gradients around blood vessels, which may be quantified as the CT fat attenuation index (FAI).[13] Most FAI-related studies have focused on coronary atherosclerotic heart disease.[14–17] Nevertheless, research regarding the application of FAI in patients with VSA, whose core pathological mechanism is CAS, remains extremely scarce. Accordingly, this study aimed to investigate the relationship between FAI and CAS.

2. Subjects and methods

All study methods were approved by the Ethics Committee of the First People’s Hospital of Nantong (2021KT154). All the subjects enrolled in the study gave written informed consent to participate.

2.1. Subjects

A total of 404 patients who had chest pain and stenosis < 50% in major coronary branches, as indicated by CCTA at our hospital from January 2022 to June 2023 were included. After coronary angiography, 40 cases with stenosis ≥ 50% in major coronary branches or coronary slow flow were excluded from this study. According to the diagnostic criteria for vasospastic angina (VSA) published by the Japanese Circulation Society in 2013,[18] patients were confirmed to have CAS if they met one of the following conditions: ① VSA attack: such as chest pain occurring at rest (especially from night to early morning), exercise tolerance exhibiting clear diurnal variation (marked morning reduction in exercise capacity), hyperventilation inducing chest pain, or chest pain which could be inhibited by calcium antagonist but not by beta-blockers; ② positive nondrug coronary spasm provocation test (hyperventilation test); ③ positive drug-induced CAS provocation test (acetylcholine provocation test). According to the above diagnostic criteria, 134 patients were excluded from CAS. The 230 patients with CAS served as a CAS group. The above 134 cases excluded from CAS underwent a treadmill test, while 112 patients had a negative treadmill test and were assigned as a control group.

The exclusion criteria included: age > 65 years; acute infectious disease within 4 weeks; chronic infectious disease; administration of anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory drugs within the past 4 weeks; occurrence of myocardial infarction within the past 4 weeks; history of surgery within the past 4 weeks; peripheral arterial disease or tumor; severe dysfunction insufficiency with left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) < 45%; hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy or valvular heart disease; bradyarrhythmia combined with Adams-Stokes syndrome attacks or syncope; and poor image quality on CCTA examination.

2.2. Collection of general clinical data

All patients underwent routine clinical examinations within 24 hours after admission, including blood pressure measurement, complete blood count, fasting blood glucose, lipid profile, renal function tests, troponin I level, and echocardiography.

2.3. Detection of circulating inflammatory factors

On the second day after admission, 2 ml of fasting peripheral venous blood was collected. After natural coagulation, the serum was stored at -70°C for testing. Interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and interferon-γ (IFN-γ) were detected using flow cytometry (provided by Hunan Weigong Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). High-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) was assessed by nephelometry (Shanghai Kehua Bio-engineering Co., Ltd.).

2.4. CCTA

Second-generation dual-source CT scanner (Somatom Definition Flash, Siemens Medical Solutions, Forchheim, Germany) was used, and the scan consisted of a plain CT scan and an enhancement scan. Continuous electrocardiographic monitoring was used during the examination. The patients took 25–50 mg of metoprolol tartrate orally when their heart rate was more than 70 beats/min. Before scanning, patients were instructed to hold their breath to avoid breath artifacts and took nitroglycerin sublingually to ensure vascular dilatation. After the plain CT scan, the measurement of coronary artery calcium score (CACS) and the localization of the cardiac scan range were performed. During the enhancement scanning, a nonionic contrast agent (Ultravist®, 370 mgI/ml, Schering AG, Bayer, Germany) was intravenously administered via the antecubital vein at 4 ml/s. Scan parameters included detector collimation 2 mm × 96 mm × 0.6 mm, tube current 288 mA/rotation, and tube voltage 100 kV, which was dependent on body mass index (BMI).

2.5. Detecting FAI

The measurement of pericoronary FAI was performed using the Syngo via software on a Siemens VB20 post-processing workstation. Perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT) in the region of interest (ROI) was measured. The ROI was located around the vessel wall at the proximal 40 mm segment of the 3 major coronary artery branches. The short axis of the ROI was set to the average diameter of the measured coronary artery, and the ROI only included the voxels with densities between -190 HU and −30 HU. FAI was defined as the average CT attenuation of adipose tissue within the ROI. The FAI measurement steps are shown in Figures 1–3.

2.6. Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were described using constituent ratios. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were described using Mean ± SD, and those with a non-normal distribution using median, 25th and 75th percentiles (M, P25, P75). For two-group comparison of categorical variables, Pearson χ2 test was used when all theoretical frequencies were ≥ 5 and the total sample size was ≥ 40. If the theoretical frequency was < 5 but the total sample size was ≥ 40, the continuity-corrected χ2 test was used. If the theoretical frequency was < 1 or the total sample size was < 40, Fisher’s exact test was applied. For the comparison of continuous variables with normal distribution and homogeneity of variance, an independent samples t-test was used for comparisons between 2 groups. For the comparison of continuous variables with non-normal distribution or heterogeneity of variance, the Mann–Whitney U test was used for comparisons between 2 groups. For binary outcome variables, multivariable logistic regression was employed, reporting the odds ratio (OR), 95% confidence interval (95% CI), and P value. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 18.0 software. Statistical significance was established at P < .05.

4. Discussion

Inflammation plays an important role in CAS pathogenesis. In CAS, peripheral blood levels of hs-CRP, IL-6 and WBC counts were elevated, and increased IL-6 might trigger CAS,[8–10] suggesting that peripheral circulating inflammatory factors play a crucial role in CAS pathogenesis. In contrast, some studies suggest that circulating inflammatory factor levels can be influenced by systemic or other localized inflammatory diseases, and that local coronary inflammation, rather than systemic inflammation, plays a significant role in CAS pathogenesis.[11,12] The activation of Rho kinase is a central mechanism in the contraction of vascular smooth muscle cells.[12,19,20] IL-1β can stimulate the coronary artery adventitia and activate Rho kinase to induce CAS even with normal endothelial function,[21] suggesting that local inflammation in the coronary artery may lead to CAS.

At present, positron emission tomography (PET)/CT imaging is a gold standard for detecting coronary inflammation. PET/CT imaging may directly assess inflammatory processes in human blood vessels by utilizing the highly specific radiotracer 18F-FDG.[22] However, PET/CT imaging is expensive and produces a high radiation dose, hindering its widespread clinical use. In 2017, it was reported for the first time that coronary artery inflammation dynamically correlates with the adjacent perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT). PVAT attenuation gradients may be detected by CCTA and can be quantified as the CT FAI.[13] FAI has good sensitivity and specificity in detecting vascular inflammation, and its results are highly consistent with those of 18F-FDG PET.[13]

This study found that the perivascular FAIs around the LAD, LCX, and RCA in the CAS group were significantly higher than those in the control group, suggesting that the level of coronary inflammation is elevated in CAS. At the same time, this study indicated no significant differences in circulating inflammatory factors between the CAS group and the control group, including hs-CRP, ESR, WBC count, neutrophil, IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α, and IFN-γ (all P > .05). In this regard, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Circulating inflammatory markers are known to exhibit substantial biological variability and susceptibility to systemic confounders, including blood-sampling timing, fasting status, concomitant medications, comorbidities, or subclinical infections. The discrepancy between the nonsignificant circulating markers and the significant local FAI elevations may be due to the susceptibility of serum biomarkers to systemic influences. In contrast, pericoronary FAI provides a quantitative, stable, and localized assessment of adipose tissue inflammation, which is less affected by transient systemic fluctuations. The absence of significance does not negate the role of systemic inflammation but rather highlights the superior specificity of FAI as a localized inflammatory metric. Previous large-scale studies reported elevated levels of circulating inflammatory factors in patients with coronary atherosclerosis.[23–25] However, our study showed that circulating inflammatory factors were not significantly higher in the CAS group as compared to the control group, suggesting that the circulating inflammatory factor response may not be stronger in CAS than in coronary atherosclerosis. The results of this study also suggest that the chronic low-grade inflammatory response in CAS is not sufficient to affect the levels of circulating inflammatory factors, and the levels of circulating inflammatory factors reflect the status of systemic or other localized inflammatory diseases and seem not to be associated with CAS. Due to practical limitations, microvascular dysfunction was not measured in the present study. Consequently, some patients in the control group might actually have had microvascular dysfunction or ischemia that cannot be identified by a treadmill test. Even accounting for this potential overlap within the control group, significantly elevated FAI values were still observed in the CAS group. This finding further highlights the specificity of local perivascular FAI in CAS.

In this study, multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that smoking was an independent risk factor for the occurrence of CAS, which is consistent with a previous study.[26] This study also found that elevated FAI around the LAD is an independent risk factor for the occurrence of CAS, and there was no association between circulating inflammatory factors and CAS. These results suggest that local coronary inflammation, rather than systemic circulating inflammatory factors, plays a dominant role in CAS.

This study identifies elevated pericoronary FAI as an independent risk factor for CAS, suggesting that anti-inflammatory therapy may reduce VSA incidence and improve VSA-related prognosis. FAI enables dynamic detection of vascular inflammation.[13] This property indicates that dynamic changes in FAI could be used to evaluate the impact of anti-inflammatory therapy on coronary inflammation. Further clinical trials are required to accumulate relevant clinical evidence regarding whether FAI can be applied for VSA diagnosis, risk stratification, and assessment of the therapeutic effect of anti-inflammatory treatment in VSA patients.

The limitations of this study are as follows. To explore the correlation between FAI and CAS, we enrolled subjects younger than 65 years and excluded patients with concomitant obstructive coronary artery disease, peripheral arterial disease, and other inflammation-related diseases, so as to minimize the influences of factors other than CAS on FAI values. This design limits the generalizability of our conclusions. Since invasive coronary functional testing was not performed in the present study, we cannot completely rule out the potential confounding effects of ANOCA/INOCA on our results. Future multicenter studies with larger sample sizes and prolonged follow-up durations are needed to accumulate further clinical evidence.

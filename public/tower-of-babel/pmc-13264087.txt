ABSTRACT
AbstractProduct quality and safety are crucial in the e-commerce market, particularly for regulated sectors like the food industry, for user protection, and it is the responsibility of the merchants who sell their products to ensure these are maintained. Here comes the role of the government, which lays down certain rules and regulations that need to be followed. These rules and regulations are laid down by the central or state government of India. All these rules are enforced by the authorities established under the respective ministries. All these rules and regulations are maintained in the form of government repositories on the websites of the respective government ministries. Accessing data that is present in these repositories is very difficult for users, as these rules, regulations, and amendments are made available in PDF format to the general public and are often in an unstructured format. This initial study validates the system's effectiveness using the specific domain of Indian food safety regulations. Validation of the system on food-specific regulations demonstrated promising performance, achieving a task accuracy between 82 and 89% over 10 runs and an NLP model F-score of 87.17%.The proposed system makes it easy for users to access this data, allowing them to search for rules and regulations related to their product by providing a search engine. The proposed system gathers data scattered across various websites using web scraping. This data is then automatically cleaned and organized using automation scripts before being presented to the users in a structured and informative format. The proposed system website delivers accurate, real-time data with high speed and reliability, ensuring users get up-to-date and precise information instantly. Background

Given the growth in the e-commerce sector today, it is a fact that it will continue to grow in the future as well [1]. Looking into a huge growth in the e-commerce market, every merchant wants to expand their business across the country by means of E-commerce stores [2]. Selling products online makes them accessible to all people overall in the country, which results in high sales and availability of products, and market growth. But selling products online must be verified to be safe for the customers. This is where legal compliance and product safety come into the picture [3].

The government of India has introduced some product safety laws related to specific products that are present in government repositories like the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) [4], BIS [5], Agriwelfare [6], and MEITY [7]. Generally, any product falls under the specific department or ministry of the government, where some rules and regulations are applied to that product. All these regulations, rules, and amendments are made available on the portal of that department, which is nothing but a government repository. All these rules and regulations are given in an unstructured format, like a hardcopy scanned pdf's, a mixture of both Hindi and English language, to name a few.

Whenever the merchant transports or sells a particular product, these regulations must be followed before selling the product (ex, packaging rules). If these regulations are not followed by the merchant, then fines may apply for violation. Due to these penalties, many new e-commerce merchants, especially those in complex sectors like food, find it inconvenient and difficult to transport and sell their products, and do not prefer selling their products on the online market. Searching for any specific product, it’s hard to fetch those particularly related rules and regulations on the government repositories, as there is no search engine for merchants to navigate the law for a particular product [8]. The merchant needs to go through each and every category’s PDFs and find the laws applicable for the selling product manually which is a very time-consuming task as one single PDF contains rules and regulations for various products [23], the other option is that the merchant can hire a lawyer who will check the rules and regulations and then will address the merchant which is not feasible for new merchants who have just started their business [9].

Method details

Recognizing the need to address these challenges, the concept of Law Merchant emerges as a hope for new entrepreneurs engaging in the realm of E-commerce [10]. This research is a user-friendly platform designed to streamline the process of finding and understanding rules, regulations, and acts for various products. This research Law Merchant is basically a search engine for merchants that helps them to extract and view only specific product laws for the searched product using Natural Language Processing (NLP) [22], so that the merchant will not need to go through whole government PDFs to check the applicable rules and regulations for the particular product [11]. This system aggregates data from government websites and PDF documents using a web scraping technique that releases Scrapy spiders over the URL that is given as an input, presenting it in a centralized and easily accessible format using NLP libraries like spaCy, which works on tokenization [25], and whenever searched for a product it will analyze all the PDFs and will only return the laws regarding the searched product [12]. This library of NLP, known as spaCy [13], is basically based on the principles of an object-oriented approach. It works by converting the text into one object as a whole, which includes a word vector that is not present in other libraries of NLP; using this word vector helps in the clustering of data. Information Extraction may also benefit NLP systems such as Machine Translation, Question Answering, Text summarization, and Opinion Mining systems [14]. This will reduce the risk of encountering fines, and the growth of the e-commerce market will increase. However, Information Extraction has many challenges regarding accuracy and precision, which make it an exciting research area [15].

Businesses often have to manually collect and interpret regulatory information from multiple sources with current compliance solutions, which can take a long time and be error-prone. When relevant regulations span multiple jurisdictions (central and state levels), these solutions may not provide a single view. By automatically aggregating and updating information from various government repositories, this solution provides a centralized and normalized representation of all relevant legal compliance [16]. This ensures that businesses have access to the most up-to-date and comprehensive regulatory information in a single location and reduces the need for manual intervention.

A three-stage automated information extraction system with a commendable performance of 87.2% accuracy in Named Entity Recognition (NER) was proposed in the work of Samah Abu Shamma et al. [14]. When compared to the model in proposed system diagram, however, the method has a few drawbacks. By providing easy-to-use and flexible solutions, Law Merchant ensures compliance with legal standards to save merchants valuable time and resources [21]. This platform helps to decrease barriers between merchants and E-commerce applications, helps merchants in packaging and transportation, starts their business online, and improves confidence against the fear of legal repercussions. E-commerce Businesses, especially small and medium-sized businesses, often struggle with navigating the intricate web of regulations. The proposed solution reduces the time, effort and cost by understanding as well as complying with these laws, which leads businesses towards growth and innovation.

The Law Merchant aims to extract and manage product-specific regulations from a vast governmental repository. The system is developed using advanced natural language processing (NLP) [17] techniques to identify and extract relevant sections related to specific products that the user is in need of. The system is designed to ensure accuracy, scalability, and user-friendly interaction for the users.

Proposed system architecture

The Proposed Intelligent Rule Extraction for Merchant Compliance Using NLP system architecture shown in Fig. 1 comprises three main subsystems: Web Scraper & Automation System, Automated Data Processing & Storage System, and User Interaction System. The Web Scraper & Automation System consists of automation scripts that continuously monitor government repositories to download updated or new regulation documents automatically. These scripts gather relevant data from official government sources and forward it for processing.

Proposed intelligent rule extraction for merchant compliance using NLP system architecture.

The Automated Data Processing & Storage System receives the raw documents and applies automated cleaning using NLP models such as SpaCy and Transformer-based techniques [24]. This cleaning step involves extracting clean text, removing noise, and converting the data into a structured format appropriate for further processing. The cleaned data is stored centrally in a database designed to hold structured regulatory information. Before processing, the system performs spell checking and keyword validation to ensure accuracy in the product-specific keyword search.

The core processing leverages NLP tasks, including tokenization and named entity recognition, to extract relevant regulation sections by matching product keywords via a combination of regular expressions and fuzzy matching techniques. The extracted relevant sections are then stored efficiently in the database for fast retrieval during user interactions.

The User Interaction System allows users to search for product-specific regulations through a web application interface. Upon entering a product keyword, the system validates spelling and suggests similar terms if necessary. After validation, the system fetches all related regulation categories and displays them to the user. Users can navigate through these categories, view official government PDFs for each regulation, and report any issues they encounter with the laws. Additionally, the interface shows the total count of laws related to the search and provides interactive controls to check or uncheck laws, alongside a graphical visualization comparing total versus unchecked laws. Method validation

The Dataset used in the research is regulatory documents from the government website. All the regulatory documents are collected by using Scrapy, which is a Web Crawling Framework [18]. The documents in the government repositories contain various rules and regulations for each product. Initially stored in a cloud storage, and the data, which is completely processed, gets stored in the MongoDB [19]. It leverages libraries such as SpaCy and Scrapy, with data stored in SQLite and MongoDB. Regulation documents were collected using Scrapy, ensuring a comprehensive dataset. Keywords for each product were extracted using a custom module, using SpaCy for text processing. Regulation texts get transformed into sentences using SpaCy's NLP capabilities. Use of Regular expressions to identify and extract relevant sections based on product keywords takes place. The proposed algorithm was executed to extract product-specific regulations from the dataset. Then the performance metrics were evaluated based on accuracy, efficiency, and scalability [20].

To ensure reproducibility, the dataset was gathered from government repositories (FSSAI, BIS, etc.) using Scrapy. The dataset consists of 150 regulatory PDF documents and text documents, primarily focusing on the food category as per the validation scope. This covers 10 distinct sub-categories, including 'Foods for Infant Nutrition' and 'Labeling and Display'. The preprocessing pipeline includes text extraction, tokenization, stop-word removal, normalization, and rule segmentation and manual annotation workflow performed by domain experts. For NLP processing, this research used the en_core_web_lg spaCy model for its balance of performance and efficiency. Core parameters for tokenization and segmentation were left as the defaults provided by the library. The fuzzy matching algorithm was configured with a 85% similarity threshold to balance accurate matches with tolerance for minor text variations.

A ground-truth validation dataset was manually constructed. Two domain-knowledge researchers independently annotated 200 sections of text, identifying all product-specific rules. Disagreements were resolved by a third expert, resulting in an inter-annotator agreement of 85% (Cohen’s Kappa = 0.85), indicating substantial agreement.

For evaluation, This manually-curated dataset was divided into 70% training and 30% testing and the extraction pipeline was executed across 10 independent runs using randomized document samples to assess robustness and consistency. Accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score were computed for each run, and the average values were reported in Table 2. This evaluation setup ensured reliable performance assessment and reproducibility of the proposed system.

Conclusion

This paper presents research detailing a systematic approach to managing and accessing government rules and regulations related to specific topics using technologies such as web scraping, Natural language processing, and an efficient database management system. The proposed system provides easy access, better efficiency, and user interactivity.

The proposed system begins by gathering data on rules and regulations from government repositories. This data is then converted to a structured format, stored in cloud storage, and further processed using advanced algorithms developed with NLP libraries like spaCy. The output of these algorithms is stored in the MongoDB database, and the user retrieves the search results from it.

The user interface is designed in such a manner that it provides a seamless and informative experience, allowing users to navigate through the laws of a specific product. The addition features like law marking, graphical representations, reporting mechanisms for the incorrect rules, and a feedback mechanism for each law ensure transparency and reliability to the users and also enable the users to access the original government document when needed.

Overall, the proposed system represents a significant step forward in the accessibility of product-related documents and understanding them. The streamlined process provides clear insights and maintains a single platform for every product’s regulations. This research is the successful result of combining various technologies to create innovative solutions. Future work of this research will be gathering the data from all the repositories, like BIS, Agriwelfare, and MEITY.

Limitations

Dependency on Government Website Stability: The platform depends on scraping data from government websites. Any alterations in the website structure, restricted access, or downtime could interfere with the scraping process and hinder the system’s ability to deliver current information.

Limited Real-Time Updates: The existing system relies on periodic scraping and data updates. It currently does not offer real-time updates for newly published regulations, which could cause slight delays in reflecting the most recent amendments.

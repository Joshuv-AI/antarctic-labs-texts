Polynomial meshes on algebraic sets Thanks: The work was partially supported by the National Science Center, Poland grant No. 2017/25/B/ST1/00906
Leokadia Bialas-Ciez, Agnieszka Kowalska, Alvise Sommariva

Abstract
. Polynomial meshes (called sometimes ’norming sets’) allow us to estimate the supremum norm of polynomials on a fixed compact set by the norm on its discrete subset. We give a general construction of polynomial weakly admissible meshes on compact subsets of arbitrary algebraic hypersurfaces in ℂ N + 1 \mathbb{C}^{N+1} . They are preimages by a projection of meshes on compacts in ℂ N \mathbb{C}^{N} . The meshes constructed in this way are optimal in some cases. Our method can be useful also for certain algebraic sets of codimension greater than one. To illustrate applications of the obtained theorems, we first give a few examples and finally report some numerical results. In particular, we present numerical tests (implemented in Matlab), concerning the use of such optimal polynomial meshes for interpolation and least-squares approximation, as well as for the evaluation of the corresponding Lebesgue constants.

1.
Introduction

The problem with proper estimation of a norm of polynomials on a given compact set by the norm on a discrete subset is an exciting subject of research because of a large number of applications. First results of this type were shown more than 100 years ago but some new insights were given in 2008 by Calvi and Levenberg. Since then, "
admissible meshes
" defined in
[
9
]
have become a subject of numerous investigations, see e.g.,
[
7
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
13
]
and the references therein. They are sometimes called
norming sets, polynomial meshes
or
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund array
. Due to their nice properties (see e.g.,
[
14
]
), they play a relevant role in recent multivariate interpolation and approximation (see e.g.,
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
K
be a compact set in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
We say that a family of finite subsets
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}
of
K
represent
norming sets
on
K
if for all
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
(polynomials of degree at most
n
of variable
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
) we have the inequality

(1)

\|p\|_{K}\ \leq\ C_{n}\>\|p\|_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}

where
\|\cdot\|_{K}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
are uniform norms on
K
and
\mathcal{A}_{n}
respectively, and
C_{n}
is a positive constant depending only on
n
and
K
. Moreover,

•

if
C_{n}
is independent of
n
and
\mathrm{card}\,\mathcal{A}_{n}
are of polynomial growth in
n
then
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}
are called
admissible meshes
(AM for short),

•

if
C_{n}
and
\mathrm{card}\,\mathcal{A}_{n}
are of polynomial growth in
n
then
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}
are called
weakly admissible meshes
(WAM for short),

•

if
C_{n}
is independent of
n
and
\mathrm{card}\,\mathcal{A}_{n}=\mathcal{O}({\rm dim}\,\mathcal{P}_{n}(K))
then
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}
are called
optimal polynomial meshes
(OPM for short). The space
\mathcal{P}_{n}(K)
can be defined as
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N})_{|_{K}}
.

Observe that, by inequality (
1
), any set
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is
determining for polynomials

\mathcal{P}_{n}(K)
, i.e., the condition
p\equiv 0
on
\mathcal{A}_{n}
implies
p\equiv 0
on
K
for any
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(K)
. Therefore,
{\rm dim}\,\mathcal{P}_{n}(K)
is the minimal cardinality of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
. A set
K
is called
polynomially determining
if it is determining for the whole space
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{N})=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
.

Optimal polynomial meshes have been constructed on many polynomially determining compact sets, e.g., sections of discs, ball, convex bodies, sets with regular boundary (see
[
19
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
13
]
and the references given therein). Regarding subsets of algebraic varieties (that, of course, are not polynomially determining), norming sets are known only for a few cases.

Problem 1.2
.

How to construct polynomial meshes on compact subsets of arbitrary algebraic sets in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
?

The problem seems to be intricate and difficult. So far it has only been solved for a few
compacts like sections of a sphere, a torus, a circle and curves in
\mathbb{C}
with analytic parametrization (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
16
]
). In these cases, admissible meshes are transferred by some analytical map from certain polynomially determining set with sufficiently relevant meshes.

In this paper we give a general construction of polynomial meshes on arbitrary algebraic hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. The method can also be applied to some algebraic sets of codimension greater than one.

More precisely, we construct weakly admissible meshes on a compact subset
E
of an algebraic variety
V
by means of meshes
\{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
on a projection of
E
(see Theorems
3.3
and
3.11
). We show that preimages of
\{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
by the projection represent norming meshes on
E\subset V
. For some specific algebraic sets we obtain optimal polynomial meshes (Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
). In the case of algebraic hypersurfaces the problem seems to be solved but for algebraic sets of codimension greater than one, it is much more complicated. In this case, we give some partial results (see Theorem
3.7
, Corollary
3.8
and Theorem
3.11
) and a few examples to show how to use our theorems in more complicated cases.

Consider an arbitrary algebraic hypersurface
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
defined as the zero level set of a polynomial
s=s(z,y)
where
z=(z_{1},...,z_{N})
,
y\in\mathbb{C}
. Without loss of generality (taking a linear change of variables if necessary) we can assume that

(2)

s(z,y)=y^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varsigma_{j}(z)\,y^{j},

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,...\}
and polynomials
\varsigma_{0},...,\varsigma_{k-1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
. In this sense, any algebraic set of codimension one is given by a polynomial
s
in the above form.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
the space of all polynomials of variable
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. We will sometimes write
\mathcal{P}(z)
for this space and
\mathcal{P}_{n}(z)
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
. To give a precise definition of
\mathcal{P}_{n}(K)
we take the ideal
I(K)
of polynomials from
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
equal to zero on the set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
and set
\mathcal{P}_{n}(K)
as the quotient space
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N})/I(K)
. We define
\mathcal{P}(K)
in a similar way.

For an arbitrary algebraic set
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
p\in\mathcal{P}(V)
let

(3)

\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p:=\min\{\mathrm{deg}\,q\>:\>q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{N}),\ q_{|_{V}}\equiv p\}.

In Section 2 we will prove estimates of
\mathrm{deg}\,p
by
\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p
for any algebraic hypersurface
V
. We will also give such estimates for some algebraic sets of codimension greater than one. These estimates will play a crucial role in the next section devoted to constructions of meshes on compact subsets of algebraic varieties. Applications of these theoretical results will be presented in the last section where we give some examples of optimal polynomial meshes and weakly admissible meshes on compact subsets of algebraic sets.

2.
Estimation of degree of polynomials on algebraic sets

2.1.
The basic lemma

Consider a polynomial
s
of the form (
2
) of total degree
d:=\mathrm{deg}\,s\geq k
. Let

p(z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{j}(z)\,y^{j}

for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
y\in\mathbb{C}
and some given polynomials
p_{0},...,p_{k-1}\in\mathcal{P}(z)
. Fix an arbitrary polynomial
w\in\mathcal{P}(z,y)
and set

(4)

q=p+sw.

We will estimate
\mathrm{deg}\,p
by
\mathrm{deg}\,q
in the following way.

Lemma 2.1
.

With the above notations, if
w\not\equiv 0
then the inequality

\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-dk+d

holds and is optimal. Moreover,

{\it if}\ \ d=k\ \ {\it then}\ \ \mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q.

The first estimate is optimal, because for a polynomial
s
such that
d>k
and
p(z,y)=s(z,y)-y^{k}
,
w(z,y)=-1
we have
q(z,y)=-y^{k}
and

\mathrm{deg}\,p=d=d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-dk+d.

Proof.

Observe that if the sharp inequality
\mathrm{deg}\,q<k
were true then
w\equiv 0
contrary to assumption. Therefore, we take up only the case
k\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q
.
Let
n
be the degree of
w
with respect to
y
and

w(z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{j}(z)\,y^{j}

for some polynomials
w_{0},...,w_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(z)
. Observe that

\displaystyle q(z,y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{j}(z)\,y^{j}+\left(y^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varsigma_{j}(z)\,y^{j}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{j}(z)\,y^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(p_{j}(z)+\sum_{i=0}^{j}\varsigma_{j-i}(z)w_{i}(z)\right)y^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(w_{j}(z)+\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{n}\varsigma_{k+j-\ell}(z)w_{\ell}(z)\right)y^{j+k}

\displaystyle+w_{n}(z)\,y^{k+n}

where we set
\varsigma_{m}=0
for
m<0
.
By assumption,
w\not\equiv 0
and consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}\,q=

\displaystyle\max\left\{k+n+\mathrm{deg}\,w_{n},\ \max_{j=0,...,n-1}\left[j+k+\mathrm{deg}\,\left(w_{j}+\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{n}\varsigma_{k+j-\ell}w_{\ell}\right)\right],\ \ \ \right.

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \left.\max_{j=0,...,k-1}\left[j+\mathrm{deg}\,\left(p_{j}+\sum_{i=0}^{j}\varsigma_{j-i}w_{i}\right)\right]\right\}.

This yields

(5)

\mathrm{deg}\,q\geq k+n+\mathrm{deg}\,w_{n}.

If
\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q
, we get

\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q+(d-1)k-dk+k\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-dk+k\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-dk+d.

Therefore, it is sufficient to consider only the case
\mathrm{deg}\,q<\mathrm{deg}\,p
.

Since

\mathrm{deg}\,p=\max_{j=0,...,k-1}(j+\mathrm{deg}\,p_{j}),

we take such
\ell\in\{0,...,k-1\}
that
\mathrm{deg}\,p=\ell+\mathrm{deg}\,p_{\ell}
. It follows that

\ell+\mathrm{deg}\,\left(p_{\ell}+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\varsigma_{\ell-i}w_{i}\right)\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q<\mathrm{deg}\,p=\ell+\mathrm{deg}\,p_{\ell}

and so

\mathrm{deg}\,\left(p_{\ell}+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\varsigma_{\ell-i}w_{i}\right)<\mathrm{deg}\,p_{\ell}

that implies

\mathrm{deg}\,p_{\ell}=\mathrm{deg}\,\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\varsigma_{\ell-i}w_{i}\leq\max_{i=0,...,\ell}\mathrm{deg}\,(\varsigma_{\ell-i}w_{i})\leq\max_{i=0,...,\ell}(d-\ell+i+\mathrm{deg}\,w_{i}).

Hence

(6)

\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq d+i+\mathrm{deg}\,w_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{for some}\ \ i\in\{0,...,k-1\}.

If
\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q+nd+d-k-n
, then from (
5
) we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}\,p

\displaystyle\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-(d-1)\mathrm{deg}\,q+nd+d-k-n

\displaystyle\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-(d-1)(k+n)+nd+d-k-n=d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-dk+d

and the assertion follows. Therefore, we will consider only the case

\mathrm{deg}\,p>\mathrm{deg}\,q+n(d-1)+d-k

that yields

(7)

\mathrm{deg}\,p>\mathrm{deg}\,q+j(d-1)+d-k\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ j\in\{0,...,n\}.

Let

A:=\{\ell\in\{0,...n\}\>:\>\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq(\ell+1)d+\mathrm{deg}\,w_{\ell}\}.

By inequality (
6
), we see that the set
A
is not empty and we can take
a:=\max A
. We now prove that
a=n
. Indeed, suppose otherwise. Then
a<n
and inequality (
7
) would imply

\mathrm{deg}\,w_{a}\geq\mathrm{deg}\,p-ad-d>\mathrm{deg}\,q+ad-a+d-k-ad-d=\mathrm{deg}\,q-a-k.

Since
\mathrm{deg}\,q\geq a+k+\mathrm{deg}\,\left(w_{a}+\sum\limits_{\ell=a+1}^{n}\varsigma_{k+a-\ell}w_{\ell}\right)
, we would have

\mathrm{deg}\,w_{a}>\mathrm{deg}\,\left(w_{a}+\sum\limits_{\ell=a+1}^{n}\varsigma_{k+a-\ell}w_{\ell}\right).

Thus

(8)

\mathrm{deg}\,w_{a}=\mathrm{deg}\,\left(\sum\limits_{\ell=a+1}^{n}\varsigma_{k+a-\ell}w_{\ell}\right)\leq\max_{\ell=a+1,..,n}(d-k-a+\ell+\mathrm{deg}\,w_{\ell})

and so, for some
\ell\in\{a+1,...,n\}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}\,w_{\ell}\geq

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{deg}\,w_{a}-d+k+a-\ell\geq\mathrm{deg}\,p-(a+1)d-d+k+a-\ell

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{deg}\,p-(\ell+1)d+(d-1)(\ell-a)-d+k\geq\mathrm{deg}\,p-(\ell+1)d-1+k

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{deg}\,p-(\ell+1)d

which is impossible because
\ell\not\in A
.

By the fact that
a=n
and from inequality (
5
) we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq

\displaystyle(n+1)d+\mathrm{deg}\,w_{n}\leq nd+d+\mathrm{deg}\,q-k-n=d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-(d-1)\mathrm{deg}\,q+nd+

\displaystyle+d-k-n\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-(d-1)(k+n)+nd+d-k-n

and the assertion follows.

For
d=k
we obtain a better estimate of
\mathrm{deg}\,p
than this one given in the first case.
Let

A:=\{\ell\in\{0,...,n\}\>:\>\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,w_{\ell}+\ell+k\}.

If the inequality
\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q
did not hold, then (
6
) would imply
A\neq\emptyset
and we could consider
a:=\max A
. From (
5
) we would obtain
n\not\in A
. By inequality (
8
), for some
\ell\in\{a+1,...,n\}
we would have

\mathrm{deg}\,w_{\ell}\geq\mathrm{deg}\,w_{a}-d+k+a-\ell\geq\mathrm{deg}\,p-a-k-d+k+a-\ell=\mathrm{deg}\,p-k-\ell

and
\ell
would belong to
A
which is a contradiction. ∎

Proposition 2.2
.

With the previous notations we have

\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,q,\ d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q-dk+d\}\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,q.

Moreover, if
d=k
then
\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q
.

2.2.
On algebraic hypersurfaces

Let
V=V(s)
be an algebraic set of codimension one, defined by a polynomial
s
, i.e.

V=\{(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}\>:\>s(z,y)=0\}.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
s
is of form (
2
).
Since
\mathcal{P}(V)
is a quotient space, we can show that any equivalence class from
\mathcal{P}(V)
is uniquely determined by a polynomial of the space

(9)

W=\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y):=\{p(z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{j}(z)\>y^{j}\>:\>p_{0},...,p_{k-1}\in\mathcal{P}(z)\},

cf. Subsection 4.2 in
[
2
]
. Dividing a fixed polynomial
q\in\mathcal{P}(z,y)
by
s
with respect to the variable
y\in\mathbb{C}
, we can find
p\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)
and
w\in\mathcal{P}(z,y)
satisfying condition (
4
) and assumptions of Proposition
2.2
. The polynomial
p
is unique and in this way we obtain the linear surjective operator

\Phi:\mathcal{P}(z,y)\ni q\mapsto p\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)

such that
q_{|_{V}}=\Phi(q)_{|_{V}}
. By the proposition, we can estimate
\mathrm{deg}\,\Phi(q)

\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}q\leq\mathrm{deg}\,\Phi(q)\leq\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}q,\ d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}q-dk+d\}\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}q

where
\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}q
is defined by (
3
) and
d=\mathrm{deg}\,s
. We can state equivalently the above inequalities as follows

Theorem 2.3
.

For all polynomials
p\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)
we have

\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p,\ d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p-dk+d\}\leq d\cdot\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p.

Moreover, if
d=k
then
\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p=\mathrm{deg}\,p
.

In the specific case where
s(z,y)=y^{k}+\varsigma_{0}(z)
, a better estimate of
\mathrm{deg}\,p
can be found in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
2
, Lemma 12]
) If
V=\{(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}\>:\>y^{k}+\varsigma_{0}(z)=0\}
then for any polynomial

p\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)
we have

\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\max\left\{1,\frac{\mathrm{deg}\,\varsigma_{0}}{k}\right\}\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p\ =\ \frac{d}{k}\ \mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p

where

.

Remark 2.5
.

For numerical tests given in Section 5, we need to know what the dimension of the restriction of
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N+1})
to
V
is. Consider an algebraic hypersurface
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
given by a polynomial equation
s(z,y)=0
, where
z=(z_{1},...,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and the total degree of
s
equals
k
. Without loss of generality (taking a linear change of variables if necessary), we can assume that

s(z,y)=y^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\varsigma_{j}(z)\>y^{j}

and
\varsigma_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{k-j}(z)=\mathcal{P}_{k-j}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
for
j=0,...,k-1
. In this case,

\mathcal{P}(V)_{|_{V}}=W_{|_{V}}
with
W
defined by (
9
).
Consider
W_{n}:=\{p\in W\>:\>{\rm deg}\,p\leq n\}
where deg
\,p
is the total degree of
p
. The set
W_{n}
is a vector space and we can calculate its dimension as follows. If
n\leq k-1
then
W_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N+1})
and dim
\,W_{n}=\binom{N+1+n}{N+1}
. For
n\geq k
, since deg
\,p\leq n
implies deg
\,p_{j}\leq n-j
for
j=0,...,k-1
, we have the following formula

{\rm dim}\,W_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{\rm dim}\,\mathcal{P}_{n-j}(\mathbb{C}^{N})=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{N+n-j}{n-j}=\binom{N+1+n}{N+1}-\binom{N+1+n-k}{N+1}.

2.3.
On some algebraic sets of codimension greater than one

Now consider two polynomials
s_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(z,y_{1})
,
s_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(z,y_{1},y_{2})
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(10)

s_{1}(z,y_{1})=y_{1}^{k_{1}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k_{1}-1}\tilde{\varsigma}_{j}(z)\,y_{1}^{j},\ \ \ \ \ d_{1}:=\mathrm{deg}\,s_{1}\geq k_{1}

(11)

s_{2}(z,y_{1},y_{2})=y_{2}^{k_{2}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k_{2}-1}\tilde{\tilde{\varsigma}}_{j}(z,y_{1})\,y_{2}^{j},\ \ \ \ \ d_{2}:=\mathrm{deg}\,s_{2}\geq k_{2},

where
\tilde{\varsigma}_{0},...,\tilde{\varsigma}_{k_{1}-1}\in\mathcal{P}(z)
,
\tilde{\tilde{\varsigma}}_{0},...,\tilde{\tilde{\varsigma}}_{k_{2}-1}\in\mathcal{P}(z,y_{1})
and

The polynomials
s_{1},s_{2}
determine the algebraic set

Take an arbitrary polynomial

and divide it by

. In this way we find polynomials

By Proposition

, we have

\mathrm{deg}\,r\leq\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,q,\>d_{2}\>(\mathrm{deg}\,q-k_{2}+1)\}\leq d_{2}\>\mathrm{deg}\,q

then

. Now we divide every polynomial

by

to obtain

and

such that

r_{j}=p_{j}+s_{1}\cdot w_{j}\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{and }\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{deg}\,_{y_{1}}p_{j}\leq k_{1}-1.

Let

Again by Proposition
2.2
, we get the estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}\,p

\displaystyle=\max_{j=0,...,k_{2}-1}(j+\mathrm{deg}\,p_{j})\leq\max_{j=0,...,k_{2}-1}\{j+\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,r_{j},\>d_{1}(\mathrm{deg}\,r_{j}-k_{1}+1)\}\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,r,\>d_{1}\,(\mathrm{deg}\,r-k_{1}+1)\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,q,\>d_{1}(\mathrm{deg}\,q-k_{1}+1),\>d_{1}\,[d_{2}\>(\mathrm{deg}\,q-k_{2}+1)-k_{1}+1]\}

that holds in a general case. For some specific cases depending on

we have

d_{1}=k_{1}\ \mbox{and}\ d_{2}=k_{2}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,q

d_{i}>k_{i}\ \mbox{and}\ d_{j}=k_{j}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,q,\>d_{i}\>(\mathrm{deg}\,q-k_{i}+1)\}

for

or
i=2
,
j\neq i
.

Theorem 2.6
.

For all polynomials
p\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k_{1}-1}(y_{1})\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k_{2}-1}(y_{2})

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq\max\{\mathrm{deg}\,q,d_{1}(\mathrm{deg}\,q-k_{1}+1),d_{1}[d_{2}(\mathrm{deg}\,q-k_{2}+1)-k_{1}+1]\}

\displaystyle\leq d_{1}\,d_{2}\,\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p.

Moreover,

\mbox{if}\ \ d_{1}=k_{1}\ \ \mbox{then}\ \ \mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq d_{2}\>\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p,

\mbox{if}\ \ d_{2}=k_{2}\ \ \mbox{then}\ \ \mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p\leq\mathrm{deg}\,p\leq d_{1}\>\mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p,

\mbox{if}\ \ d_{1}=k_{1}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ d_{2}=k_{2}\ \ \mbox{then}\ \ \mathrm{deg}\,_{V}p=\mathrm{deg}\,p.

Obviously, one can easily state an analogous result for algebraic sets of codimension
\ell>2
in
\mathbb{C}^{N+\ell}
given by polynomials
s_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(z,y_{1}),...,s_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}(z,y_{1},...,y_{\ell})
.

Remark 2.7
.

Similarly to the end of the previous subsection, we give formulas for the dimension of a space of polynomials restricted to an algebraic surface
V
of codimension 2. Let
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+2}
be an algebraic set defined by two polynomial equation
s_{1}(z,y)=0
,
s_{2}(z,y,x)=0
where
z=(z_{1},...,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
y,x\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
k
be the total degree of
s_{1}
and
\overline{k}
of
s_{2}
. Assume that

s_{1}(z,y)=y^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tilde{\varsigma}_{j}(z)\,y^{j}\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \ \ \ s_{2}(z,y,x)=x^{\overline{k}}+\sum_{j=0}^{\overline{k}-1}\tilde{\tilde{\varsigma}}_{j}(z,y)\,x^{j}.

In this case,

\mathcal{P}(V)_{|_{V}}=\overline{W}_{|_{V}}
with
\overline{W}
defined by

W=\left\{p(z,y,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\overline{k}-1}p_{j}(z,y)\>x^{j}\>:\>p_{0},...,p_{\overline{k}-1}\in W\right\},

where
W
is given in (
9
).
Consider
\overline{W}_{n}:=\{p\in W\>:\>{\rm deg}\,p\leq n\}
where deg
\,p
is the total degree of
p
. The dimension of the space
\overline{W}_{n}
can be calculated as follows. In the case
n\leq\overline{k}-1
we have

\mbox{dim}\,\overline{W}_{n}=\mbox{dim}\,W_{n}+\mbox{dim}\,W_{n-1}+...+\mbox{dim}\,W_{0},

and for
n>\overline{k}-1
we get

\mbox{dim}\,\overline{W}_{n}=\mbox{dim}\,W_{n}+\mbox{dim}\,W_{n-1}+...+\mbox{dim}\,W_{n-\overline{k}+1}.

From this, by Remark
2.5
, we can obtain precise formulas. For example, if
n\leq\min\{k,\overline{k}\}-1
then
\mbox{dim}\,\overline{W}_{n}=\binom{N+2+n}{N+2}
. The most useful case seems to be
n\geq k+\overline{k}-1
when we get

\mbox{dim}\,\overline{W}_{n}=\binom{N+1+n}{N+1}-\binom{N+1+n-k}{N+1}+\binom{N+n}{N+1}-\binom{N+n-k}{N+1}

+\>...\>+\binom{N+2+n-\overline{k}}{N+1}-\binom{N+2+n-k-\overline{k}}{N+1}

=\binom{N+2+n}{N+2}-2\binom{N+2+n-k}{N+2}+\binom{N+2+n-k-\overline{k}}{N+2}.

3.
Construction of polynomial meshes on algebraic sets

3.1.
Division inequality

We will prove that preimages by a projection of some norming sets
\{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\}_{\ell}\subset K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
represent weakly admissible meshes on a compact subset
E
of an algebraic variety
V
. These meshes are optimal for some specific algebraic sets. In this case we assume only the existence of admissible meshes on
K
but for more general varieties we need also a property of
K
that is called a
division
or
Schur-type inequality
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
m>0
. We say that
K
satisfies the
division inequality
with exponent
m
if for any polynomial
q\not\equiv 0
on
K
there exists a positive constant
M
such that for all polynomials
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{N})

(12)

\|p\|_{K}\leq M(\deg p+\deg q)^{m\>\deg q}\|pq\|_{K}

where
\|\cdot\|_{K}
is the sup-norm on the set
K
.

One of tools often used to construct norming sets is a so-called Markov property (i.e.,
\|{\rm grad}\,p\|_{K}\leq M(\mathrm{deg}\,p)^{m}\|p\|_{K}
with
M,m>0
independent of
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
), see e.g.,
[
9
]
.
In
[
2
]
we proved that any set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
with Markov property satisfies the division inequality, cf.
[
1
]
. The converse is not possible for several variables, because every Markov set is polynomially determining and this is not a necessary condition for the division inequality. A list of sets satisfying the division inequality contains

•

all non-singular connected compacts in the complex plane,

•

all convex fat compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

•

all compact sets with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
boundary,

•

all fat subanalytic set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

•

all uniformly perfect sets,

•

filled in Julia sets and some totally disconnected sets, e.g., Cantors,

•

variate examples of compact subsets of algebraic varieties, see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
.

These last mentioned sets satisfies the division inequality but they do not have Markov property, because algebraic sets are not polynomially determining.

3.2.
On algebraic hypersurfaces

Assume that
s
is a polynomial in form (
2
) and set

(13)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(s):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\ :\ {\rm Disc}_{y}\left(s\right)=0\}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\ :\ {\rm Res}_{y}\left(s,\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)=0\right\}

where
{\rm Disc}_{y}\left(s\right)
is the discriminant of
s
with respect to
y
and
{\rm Res}_{y}\left(s,\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}\right)
is the resultant of
s,\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}
in
y
.
Since the resultant is equal (with accuracy to
\pm
) to the determinant of the Sylvester matrix associated to the polynomials
s,\>\frac{\partial s}{\partial y}
(see, e.g.,
[
10
, Chap.1]
), the set
\mathcal{A}(s)
is an algebraic hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and we can easily check whether a fixed point
z_{0}
belongs to it or not, what is important in Theorem
3.3
.

The following remark reveals the importance of the set
\mathcal{A}(s)
: the polynomial
\mathbb{C}\ni y\mapsto s(z,y)
has
k
roots pairwise distinct if and only if
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{A}(s)
. Irreducibility of
s
implies
\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{A}(s)\neq\emptyset
. However, this is not a necessary condition, because the polynomial
w(z,y)=y^{2}-z^{2}
,
z,y\in\mathbb{C}
has two distinct roots
y_{1},y_{2}
for any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. For more details we refer the reader to
[
3
]
.

Consider a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
and an algebraic hypersurface
V=V(s)\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
given by a polynomial
s
in form (
2
) of degree
d
. Observe that the set

(14)

E=\{(z,y)\in V(s)\>:\>z\in K\}

is compact. We will transfer norming sets from
K
to
E
. A construction of weakly admissible meshes on
E
is based on the following lemma which has been obtained by a careful analysis of the proof of Proposition 3.3 in
[
3
]

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. There exists a polynomial
\widetilde{q}\in\mathcal{P}(z)
such that
\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\ :\ \widetilde{q}(z)=0\}\subset\mathcal{A}(s)
,
\deg\widetilde{q}\leq 2d(k-1)
and if
F
is compact subset of
K
such that
F\setminus\mathcal{A}(s)\neq\emptyset
then

\|p_{j}^{2}\widetilde{q}\|_{F}\leq\widetilde{C}^{2}\|p\|_{G}^{2}

for any polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)
written in the form
p(z,y)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{j}(z)\,y^{j}
where
G=\{(z,y)\in V(s)\>:\>z\in F\}
and a constant
\widetilde{C}
is independent of
p
and
F
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a compact with weakly admissible meshes
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}
and
E\subset V(s)
be a set given by (
14
). Assume that
K\setminus\mathcal{A}(s)\neq\emptyset
and take
z_{0}
from this set. If
K
satisfies the division inequality (
12
) then
\{\mathcal{B}_{n}\}_{n}
defined by

\mathcal{B}_{n}=\{(z,y)\in E\ :\ z\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\cup\{z_{0}\}\}\ \ \mbox{ where }\ \ \ell=\ell(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2(n+k^{2}-k)&\mbox{ ; }\ d=k,\\
2dn&\mbox{ ; }\ d>k\end{array}\right.

represent weakly admissible meshes on
E
, i.e., for every polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(z,y)
we have

\|p\|_{E}\ \leq\ D_{n}\>\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}

and
\{D_{n}\}_{n}
are of polynomial growth in
n
.

Proof.

For a fixed polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N+1})
we can take
q\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)
such that
q=p
on
V(s)
and by Lemma
2.1
,
\mathrm{deg}\,q\leq n
if
d=k
and
\mathrm{deg}\,q\leq d(n-k+1)
if
d>k
.
Set
q(z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}q_{j}(z)\,y^{j}
for some polynomials
q_{j}\in\mathcal{P}(z)
.
We have

\|p\|_{E}=\|q\|_{E}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|q_{j}\|_{K}\|y\|_{E}^{j}.

From Lemma
3.2
there exists a polynomial
\widetilde{q}
such that
\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\ :\ \widetilde{q}(z)=0\}\subset\mathcal{A}(s)
,
\deg\widetilde{q}\leq 2d(k-1)
. Since
K\setminus\mathcal{A}(s)\neq\emptyset
, we have
\widetilde{q}\not\equiv 0
on
K
. By the division inequality, for
j=0,\ldots,k-1
we have

(15)

\|q_{j}^{2}\|_{K}\leq M(2\deg q+2d(k-1))^{2md(k-1)}\|q_{j}^{2}\widetilde{q}\|_{K}.

Since
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}
are weakly admissible meshes on the set
K
, we obtain

(16)

\|q_{j}^{2}\widetilde{q}\|_{K}\leq C_{\ell}\|q_{j}^{2}\widetilde{q}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\cup\{z_{0}\}}\ \mbox{ where }\ \ \ell=\ell(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2(n+k^{2}-k)&\mbox{ ; }\ d=k,\\
2dn&\mbox{ ; }\ d>k\end{array}\right.

and
C_{\ell}=C_{\ell(n)}
are of polynomial growth in
\ell
and hence so are in
n
. By Lemma
3.2
,

\|q_{j}^{2}\widetilde{q}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\cup\{z_{0}\}}\leq\widetilde{C}^{2}\|q\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}^{2}.

On combining this with (
15
) and (
16
), we deduce that

\|q_{j}\|_{K}\leq(MC_{\ell})^{1/2}\>\widetilde{C}\ \>(\ell(n))^{md(k-1)}\|q\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}.

Consequently,

\|p\|_{E}=\|q\|_{E}\ \leq D_{n}\>\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}

and
D_{n}=(MC_{\ell})^{1/2}\>\widetilde{C}\>(\ell(n))^{md(k-1)}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|y\|_{E}^{j}
are of polynomial growth in
n
.
∎

3.3.
A specific case

In the case of
\varsigma_{1}=...=\varsigma_{k-1}=0
, the division inequality on
K
is not necessary to construct meshes on
E
. We just take admissible meshes on
K
and lift them on the algebraic varieties
V(s)
. We will see that in this fashion we can obtain optimal polynomial meshes. It is worth recalling that for an algebraic hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
we have dim
\,\mathcal{P}_{n}(V)=\mathcal{O}(n^{N-1})
and for an algebraic set
V
of codimension
m
, we have dim
\,\mathcal{P}_{n}(V)=\mathcal{O}(n^{N-m})
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
V=V(s)\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
be an algebraic variety defined by a polynomial of the form
s(z,y)=y^{k}-\varsigma_{0}(z)
and
d=\mathrm{deg}\,s>0
. If
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a compact set with norming sets
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}
and
E
is given by (
14
) then the finite sets

(17)

\mathcal{B}_{n}=\{(z,y)\in V\>:\>z\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\}\ \ \ \ \mbox{where}\ \ \ell=\ell(n)=\min\left\{\!\!\left\lceil\!\frac{d^{2}n}{k}\!\right\rceil\!\!,\,dn+(k-1)(d-k)\!\!\right\},\ \ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

are norming for
E
. Moreover, if
C_{n}
is a constant for
\mathcal{A}_{n}
on
K
in inequality (
1
), then
k\ C_{\ell(n)}^{1/k}
is a constant for
\mathcal{B}_{n}
on
E
and
\mathrm{card}\,\mathcal{B}_{n}=k\>\mathrm{card}\,\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}
. In particular, for
d=k
we have
\ell(n)=dn
.

The case
\mathrm{deg}\,\varsigma_{0}=0
is trivial:
\varsigma_{0}=c
,
E=K\times\{\sqrt[k]{c}\}
and for any polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(z,y)

\|p\|_{E}=\|p(z,\sqrt[k]{c})\|_{K}\leq C_{n}\|p(z,\sqrt[k]{c})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}=C_{n}\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\ \mbox{ where }\ \mathcal{B}_{n}=\mathcal{A}_{n}\times\{\sqrt[k]{c}\}.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(z,y)
and consider
q\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)
such that
q\equiv p
on
V
. By Theorem
2.4
,
m:=\mathrm{deg}\,q\leq\frac{dn}{k}
. Write
q(z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}q_{j}(z)\,y^{j}
and for some
(z,y)\in E
we have

(18)

\|p\|_{E}=\|q\|_{E}=|q(z,y)|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|q_{j}^{k}(z)\,y^{jk}|^{1/k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|q_{j}(z)^{k}\,\varsigma_{0}(z)^{j}|^{1/k}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|q_{j}^{k}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j}\|_{K}^{1/k}.

Consider
j
such that
\|q_{j}^{k}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j}\|_{K}\not=0
. Observe that

\mathrm{deg}\,(q_{j}^{k}\varsigma_{0}^{j})=k\>\mathrm{deg}\,q_{j}+j\>\mathrm{deg}\,\varsigma_{0}\leq k(m-j)+j\>\mathrm{deg}\,\varsigma_{0}\leq d(m-j)+jd=dm\leq\frac{d^{2}n}{k}

and

\mathrm{deg}\,(q_{j}^{k}\varsigma_{0}^{j})\leq k(m-j)+j\>\mathrm{deg}\,\varsigma_{0}=km+j(\mathrm{deg}\,\varsigma_{0}-k)\leq dn+(k-1)(d-k).

In both estimates,
\mathrm{deg}\,(q_{j}^{k}\varsigma_{0}^{j})\leq\ell(n)
and since
\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}
is a norming set on
K
, we get

(19)

\|q_{j}^{k}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j}\|_{K}\leq C_{\ell(n)}\|q_{j}^{k}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}}=C_{\ell(n)}\|q_{j}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j/k}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}}^{k}.

For a fixed
z\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}
where
\|q_{j}^{k}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}}
is attained, take
\{y_{1},...,y_{k}\}=\varsigma_{0}(z)^{1/k}\not=\{0\}
. Every
y_{i}
is equal to
|\varsigma_{0}(z)|^{1/k}u_{i}
where
\{u_{1},...,u_{k}\}=\sqrt[k]{1}
. By Newton’s identities and Viète’s formulas, we have

\sum_{m=1}^{k}u_{m}^{\nu}=0\ \ \ \ \mbox{for }\ \nu=1,...,k-1.

Consequently,

\displaystyle k\>\|q_{j}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j/k}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}}

\displaystyle=k\>\left|q_{j}(z)\,\varsigma_{0}(z)^{j/k}\right|=\left|q_{j}(z)\,|\varsigma_{0}(z)|^{j/k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}u_{m}^{k}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}q_{i}(z)\,|\varsigma_{0}(z)|^{i/k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}u_{m}^{k-j+i}\right|=\left|\sum_{m=1}^{k}u_{m}^{k-j}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}q_{i}(z)\,|\varsigma_{0}(z)|^{i/k}u_{m}^{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{k}\left|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}q_{i}(z)\,|\varsigma_{0}(z)|^{i/k}u_{m}^{i}\right|=\sum_{m=1}^{k}\left|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}q_{i}(z)\,y_{m}^{i}\right|\leq\sum_{m=1}^{k}\|q\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}

\displaystyle=k\>\|q\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}=k\>\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}.

From this and inequalities (
18
), (
19
) we obtain

\|p\|_{E}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|q_{j}^{k}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j}\|_{K}^{1/k}\leq C_{\ell(n)}^{1/k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|q_{j}\,\varsigma_{0}^{j/k}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}}\leq C_{\ell(n)}^{1/k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}=k\ C_{\ell(n)}^{1/k}\>\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}

and the proof is complete.
∎

\mathrm{deg}\,\tilde{\tilde{\varsigma}}_{0}>0

on

\mathcal{B}_{n}=\{(z,y_{1},y_{2})\in V\>:\>z\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}\},\ \ \ n\in\mathbb{N}

if
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}
are optimal polynomial meshes on
K
then so are
\{\mathcal{B}_{n}\}_{n}
on
E
.

We now turn back to an algebraic variety
V=V(s_{1},s_{2})\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+2}
defined by polynomials
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
of the form (
10
) and (
11
). On a compact set
E
given by (
21
) we can get weakly admissible mesh. We start with the observation concerning the division inequality. We need
a property that was proved in
[
3
]
,

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
V(s)\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
be an algebraic variety defined by a polynomial
s
in the form
s(z,y)=y^{k}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}s_{j}(z)\,y^{j}
with
k\geq 1
,
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a compact set and
E:=\pi^{-1}(K)\subset V(s)
where
\pi
denotes the projection (
20
).
If
K
satisfies the division inequality with exponent
m
and
K\setminus\mathcal{A}(s)\neq\emptyset
then

(23)

\|[p_{0},...,p_{k-1}]\|_{K}\leq\ M_{0}\,(\deg p)^{m_{0}}\|p\|_{E}

for any polynomial
p
written in the form
p(z,y)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{j}(z)\,y^{j}
on
V(s)
where
M_{0},\,m_{0}\geq 0
are constants independent of
p_{0},\ldots,p_{k-1}
and
m_{0}=m\,(k-1)\>\deg s
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
V=V(s)\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
be an algebraic hypersurface given by an irreducible polynomial
s
in form (
2
) with
\deg s=d
and
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
F:=\{(z,y)\in V\>:\>z\in K\}
. If

K
is determining for polynomials from
\mathcal{P}(z)
and satisfies the division inequality then
F
also satisfies the division inequality.

Proof.

Fix a polynomial
q\in\mathcal{P}(z,y)
,
q_{|_{F}}\not\equiv 0
and find
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)
such that
\mathfrak{q}=q
on
V(s)
and write it in the form
\mathfrak{q}(z,y)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}q_{j}(z)y^{j}
.
Fix also a polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}(z,y)
and find
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}(z)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(y)
such that
\mathfrak{p}=p
on
V(s)
and
\mathfrak{p}(z,y)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{j}(z)\,y^{j}
. From Lemma
2.1
we have

\deg\mathfrak{q}\leq d\deg q\ \mbox{ and }\ \deg\mathfrak{p}\leq d\deg p.

Let
\mathbf{P}:=[p_{0},...,p_{k-1}]^{T}
.
Consider the matrix
\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}
such that for
(z,y)\in V
we have

\mathfrak{p}(z,y)\>\mathfrak{q}(z,y)\ =\ [1,y,...,y^{k-1}]\ \mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}\ \mathbf{P}(z).

The determinant of
\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}
is a polynomial only in
z
and is equal to the resultant Res
\,{}_{y}(\mathfrak{q},s)
. Since
q
is coprime with
s
, Res
{}_{y}\,(\mathfrak{q},s)
is a non-zero polynomial in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and so is
\det\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}
. Since
K
is determining for polynomials from
\mathcal{P}(z)
we have
\det\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}\not\equiv 0
on
K
. Observe that

\deg\,\det\,\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}\leq k\deg\mathfrak{q}+\tfrac{(k-1)kd}{2}\leq kd\,\deg q+\tfrac{(k-1)kd}{2}=kd\,(\deg q+\tfrac{k-1}{2})

Thanks to division inequality on
K
, we have

\|p\|_{E}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|p_{j}\|_{K}\,\|y\|_{E}^{j}\leq M\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(\deg p_{j}+kd\,(\deg q+\tfrac{k-1}{2}))^{mkd\,(\deg q+\tfrac{k-1}{2})}\|\det\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}p_{j}\|_{K}\|y\|_{E}^{j}

\leq M(\deg p+dk^{2}\,\deg q)^{mdk^{2}\,\deg q}\|(\det\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s})\mathbf{P}\|_{K}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|y\|_{E}^{j}.

Replacing

(\det\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s})\mathbf{P}
by
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}\mathbf{P}
, where
\mathbf{A}
denote the transpose of the comatrix of
\det\,\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}
, we obtain

\|p\|_{E}\leq M(\deg p+dk^{2}\,\deg q)^{mdk^{2}\,\deg q}\|\mathbf{A}\|\|\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}\mathbf{P}\|_{K}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|y\|_{E}^{j}

Moreover,

\|\mathbf{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}\>\mathbf{P}\|_{K}=\|[u_{0},...,u_{k-1}]\|_{K}\mbox{ where }(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})(z,y)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}u_{j}(z)\,y^{j}\mbox{ on }V(s).

By Proposition
3.9
,

\|[u_{0},...,u_{k-1}]\|_{K}\leq M_{0}(\deg(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}))^{m_{0}}\|\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}\|_{E}\leq M_{0}d^{m_{0}}(\deg p+\ \deg q)^{m_{0}}\|pq\|_{E}

and the proof is complete.
∎

Since
E=\{(z,y_{1},y_{2})\in V(s_{2})\>:\>(z,y_{1})\in F\}
and from Theorem
3.10

F
satisfies the division inequality, we can apply Theorem
3.3
twice to obtain

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
V=V(s_{1},s_{2})\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+2}
be an algebraic variety defined by polynomials
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
of the form (
10
) and (
11
),
s_{1}
be an irreducible polynomial,
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a compact set with weakly admissible meshes
(\mathcal{A}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
F:=\{(z,y_{1})\in V(s_{1})\>:\>z\in K\}
. If
K
is determining for polynomials from
\mathcal{P}(z)
and satisfies the division inequality and
K\setminus\mathcal{A}(s_{1})\neq\emptyset
and
F\setminus\mathcal{A}(s_{2})\neq\emptyset
then

E:=\{(z,y_{1},y_{2})\in V(s_{1},s_{2})\>:\>z\in K\}

has weakly admissible meshes
(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

\mathcal{B}_{n}=\{(z,y_{1},y_{2})\in E\ :\ z\in\mathcal{A}_{l}\cup\{a,b\}\}

where
a
is an element of
K\setminus\mathcal{A}(s_{1})
,
b
is an element of
\pi(F\setminus\mathcal{A}(s_{2}))
and

l=l(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}4n+4(k_{2}^{2}-k_{2})+2(k_{1}^{2}-k_{1})&\mbox{ if }d_{1}=k_{1}\mbox{ and }d_{2}=k_{2},\\
4d_{1}n+4d_{1}(k_{2}^{2}-k_{2})&\mbox{ if }d_{1}>k_{1}\mbox{ and }d_{2}=k_{2},\\
4d_{2}n+2(k_{1}^{2}-k_{1})&\mbox{ if }d_{1}=k_{1}\mbox{ and }d_{2}>k_{2},\\
4d_{1}d_{2}n&\mbox{ if }d_{1}>k_{1}\mbox{ and }d_{2}>k_{2}.\end{array}\right.

More precisely, for every polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{N+2})
we have

\|P\|_{E}\leq C(\mathcal{B}_{n})\|P\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}

and
(C(\mathcal{B}_{n}))_{n}
are of polynomial growth in
n
.

4.
Applications

4.1.
Classical results

We start this section with three well known results, see e.g.,
[
8
]
, to recall optimal polynomial meshes on a segment, on a disk in
\mathbb{C}
and on a disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
[a,b]
be a segment in
\mathbb{C}
. For any polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and any integer number
\lambda>n
we have

\|p\|_{[a,b]}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\cos\frac{n\pi}{2\lambda}}\>\|p\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,n}}

where
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,n}=\left\{\frac{a+b}{2}+\frac{a-b}{2}\>\cos\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{2\lambda}\>:\>j=1,...,\lambda\right\}
is the scaled
\lambda
-system of the Chebyshev points, i.e., zeros of the Chebyshev polynomial
T_{\lambda}(x)=\cos(\lambda\arccos x)
.
In particular, for
\lambda=\left\lceil\frac{3}{2}n\right\rceil
,
n\geq 2
the set
\mathcal{M}_{\lceil\frac{3}{2}n\rceil,n}
is an optimal polynomial mesh for polynomials from
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and inequality (
1
) holds with the constant
C_{n}=2
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
D(a,r)
be a disk in
\mathbb{C}
with radius
r
and centre at
a
. For any polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and any integer number
\lambda>n
we have

\|p\|_{D(a,r)}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\cos\frac{n\pi}{2\lambda}}\>\|p\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,n}}

where
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,n}=\left\{a+r\>\exp\left(i\>\frac{j\pi}{\lambda}\right)\>:\>j=1,...,2\lambda\right\}=a+r\sqrt[2\lambda]{1}
is the set of equidistributed
2\lambda
points on the circle
C(a,r)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
B(a,r)
be a disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
with radius
r
and centre at
a
. For any polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and any integer number
\lambda>n
we have

\|p\|_{B(a,r)}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\cos^{2}\frac{n\pi}{2\lambda}}\>\|p\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,n}}

where
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,n}=\left\{a+\left(r_{j}\cos\frac{m\pi}{\lambda},\ r_{j}\sin\frac{m\pi}{\lambda}\right)\right\}_{j,m=1,...,\lambda},\ \ r_{j}=r\>\cos\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{2\lambda}.

If we choose
\lambda
such that
\tfrac{n}{\lambda}
can be estimated from above by a constant independent of
n
, then we obtain optimal polynomial meshes in the three above theorems. If not, we have weakly admissible meshes.

4.2.
On algebraic hypersurfaces

We give here three quite simple examples of applications of our results concerning algebraic sets given by one algebraic equation
s(z,y)=0
. Two examples are chosen so that the compact sets under consideration are in a real space.

Example 4.4
.

We firstly give optimal polynomial meshes on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

S_{2}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\>:\>x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=1\}.

Let
V=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\>:\>x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=1\}
. We have
s(x,y,z)=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-1
and
d=k=2

In the set
K=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\>:\>x^{2}+y^{2}\leq 1\}
we take the optimal polynomial meshes
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n}

constructed for
\lambda=2n
in Theorem
4.3
, that is
\mathcal{A}_{n}=\mathcal{M}_{2n,n}
. By Theorem
3.4
,
\ell(n)=2n
and thus

\mathcal{B}_{n}=\{(x,y,z)\in V\>:\>(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}_{2n}\}

where
r_{j}=\cos\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{8n}
.
Consequently, for any polynomial
p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{3})

\|p\|_{S_{2}}\leq 2C_{\ell(n)}^{1/2}\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}=\frac{2}{\cos\frac{\ell(n)\pi}{2\lambda}}\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}=\frac{2}{\cos\frac{\pi}{4}}\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}=2\sqrt{2}\|p\|_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}

and card
\mathcal{B}_{n}=2\mbox{card}\mathcal{A}_{\ell(n)}=2\dot{(}4n)^{2}=32n^{2}
, cf.
[
14

In a similar way, we can construct admissible meshes on a spherical section, cf.
[
17

. Let
E=\{(x,y,z)\in V\>:\>(x,y)\in K\}

,
\alpha\in(0,\pi]

E
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Using norming sets constructed in
[
17
]
for sections of a disk, by Theorem
3.4
we can obtain optimal admissible meshes on a spherical lune
E
.

In this section we consider the sets

, testing their performance. For a description of AFP and DLP, see e.g.
[

]

All the routines are available as open source codes in
[

]
and where tested on an Intel Core Ultra 5 125H 3.60 GHz processor with 16GB of RAM, using Matlab 2024a.

We start our analysis from the set
E
in Example
4.5
.
As test functions we adopt

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+0.5\,y+2\,z+1)^{14};

\displaystyle f_{2}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(-(x^{2}+0.5\,y^{2}+2\,z^{2}));

\displaystyle f_{3}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin(4\,x+5\,y+3z);

\displaystyle f_{4}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(x-1)^{2}+y^{2}+(z-1)^{2}}+\sqrt{(x-1)^{2}+y^{2}+(z+1)^{2}}.

For each

,

, after computing the interpolant/approximant

for

\frac{\|f(\mathcal{B}_{30})-{\mathcal{L}}_{n}f(\mathcal{B}_{30})\|_{2}}{\|f(\mathcal{B}_{30})\|_{2}},

being

The first three functions are regular, the third one having some oscillations, while the last one is the distance from the points

As for the polynomial basis of

we proceed as follows. We

•

determine numerically, by means of the points in
X=\mathcal{B}_{n}=\{P_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,N_{n}}\subset E
, an approximation
[a_{1},b_{1}]\times[a_{2},b_{2}]\times[a_{3},b_{3}]
of the smaller Cartesian parallelepiped containing the set
E
;

•

being
{\eta}_{n}:=\mbox{dim}({\mathcal{P}}_{n}(E))
, evaluate the tensorial Chebyshev basis
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,{\eta}_{n}}
of total degree
n
, shifted on
[a_{1},b_{1}]\times[a_{2},b_{2}]\times[a_{3},b_{3}]
, on the norming set
X
, so determining the Chebyshev-Vandermonde-like matrix
U_{X}=(\phi_{j}(P_{i}))_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{n}\times{\eta}_{n}}
;

•

compute the QR factorisation with column pivoting
U_{X}(:,\pi)=QR
, where
\pi
is a permutation vector;

•

select the orthogonal matrix
V_{X}=Q(:,1:{\eta}_{n})
; the first
{\eta}_{n}
columns correspond to an orthonormal basis
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,{\eta}_{n}}
of
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}(E)
, w.r.t. the discrete measure (X,
w
), where
w_{k}=1
,
k=1,\ldots,N_{n}
.

Observe that in this example the quantity
{\eta}_{n}={\mbox{dim}}({\mathcal{P}}_{n}(E))
is known theoretically, see Remarks
2.5
and
2.7
. If this is not available, one could use
{\eta}_{n}={\mbox{rank}}(U_{X})
that in the numerical implementation can be obtained by SVD decomposition of
U_{X}
in its less costly version that gives only the singular values (with
a threshold), as used by the rank Matlab/Octave native function. A second alternative could consist in adopting a Rank-Revealing QR factorization algorithm (RRQR). These algorithms, however, are not at hand in standard Matlab and typically require the use of MEX files (see e.g.,
[
12
]
).

For the evaluation of the Vandermonde matrix
V_{Y}
relatively to the basis
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,{\eta}_{n}}
on the pointset
Y\subset E
, we first determine the Chebyshev-Vandermonde matrix
U_{Y}=U(Y)
and then, in Matlab notation, compute
U_{Y}(:,\pi)/R
.

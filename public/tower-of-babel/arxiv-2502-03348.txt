Values of Ducci Periods for Sequences on ℤ m n \mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
Mark L. Lewis, Shannon M. Tefft

Abstract
. Let D : ℤ m n → ℤ m n D:\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n} be defined so that D ⁡ ( x 1 , x 2 , … , x n ) = ( x 1 + x 2 ​ mod ​ m , x 2 + x 3 ​ mod ​ m , … , x n + x 1 ​ mod ​ m ) . D(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})=(x_{1}+x_{2}\;\text{mod}\;m,x_{2}+x_{3}\;\text{mod}\;m,...,x_{n}+x_{1}\;\text{mod}\;m). We call D D the Ducci function and the sequence { D α ​ ( 𝐮 ) } α = 0 ∞ \{D^{\alpha}(\mathbf{u})\}_{\alpha=0}^{\infty} the Ducci sequence of 𝐮 \mathbf{u} for 𝐮 ∈ ℤ m n \mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n} . Every Ducci sequence enters a cycle, so we can let Per ​ ( 𝐮 ) \text{Per}(\mathbf{u}) be the number of tuples in the Ducci cycle of 𝐮 \mathbf{u} , or the period of 𝐮 \mathbf{u} . In this paper, we will look at what different possible values of Per ​ ( 𝐮 ) \text{Per}(\mathbf{u}) we can have and some conditions that if 𝐮 \mathbf{u} meets at least one of them, 𝐮 \mathbf{u} will generate a period smaller than the maximum period.

1.
Introduction

We begin by introducing an endomorphism on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
known as the Ducci function,
D
, with
[
1
,
9
,
11
]
being a few examples that call it this. Namely, we define
D
so that

D(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})=(x_{1}+x_{2}\;\text{mod}\;m,x_{2}+x_{3}\;\text{mod}\;m,...,x_{n}+x_{1}\;\text{mod}\;m).

We also call the sequence
\{D^{\alpha}(\mathbf{u})\}_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}
the
Ducci sequence of

\mathbf{u}
for
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
.

As
[
1
]
notes, every Ducci sequence on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
enters a cycle because
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is finite. More specifically, we define

Definition 1
.

The
Ducci cycle of

\mathbf{u}
is

\{\mathbf{v}\mid\exists\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\},\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\ni\mathbf{v}=D^{\alpha+\beta}(\mathbf{u})=D^{\alpha}(\mathbf{u})\}

.
The
length of

\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{Len(u)}
, is the smallest
\alpha
satisfying the equation

\mathbf{v}=D^{\alpha+\beta}(\mathbf{u})=D^{\alpha}(\mathbf{u})

for some
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
the
period of

\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{Per(u)}
, is the smallest
\beta
that satisfies the equation.

To demonstrate this definition, we look at the Ducci sequence of
(0,0,2)\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{3}
:
(0,0,2),(0,2,2),(2,0,2),(2,2,0),(0,2,2)
. Here we see that
\text{Len}(0,0,2)=1
and
\text{Per}(0,0,2)=3
.

A particular Ducci sequence of interest is the Ducci sequence of
(0,0,...,0,1)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
, known as the
basic Ducci sequence
. The basic Ducci sequence is first introduced by
[
9
]
on page 302 and is also used by
[
1
,
8
,
11
]
.
Define

\mathbf{P_{m}(n)}=\text{Per}(0,0,...,0,1)

and

\mathbf{L_{m}(n)}=\text{Len}(0,0,...,0,1).

This notation is similar to the notation that
[
1
]
uses in Definition 5, and
P_{m}(n)
is also similar to the notation used by
[
9
,
11
]
.
This sequence is important is because of Lemma 1 in
[
1
]
, which says that for
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
,
\text{Len}(\mathbf{u})\leq L_{m}(n)
and
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})|P_{m}(n)
. This provides maximum values for the length and periods of tuples in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
. The notation
P_{m}(n)
for the maximum period on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is also used by
[
2
]
on page 858.

Discussing a value for a maximum period also begs the question about when tuples do or do not generate this period. In this paper, we would like to focus on some different cases where this happens. We will begin by examining a smaller period that appears when
n=3
by proving the following Lemma:

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
\mathbf{u}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{3}
such that
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\equiv 0\;\text{mod}\;m
. Then

(1)

Every tuple in the Ducci sequence of
\mathbf{u},\;\mathbf{v}=(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{3}^{\prime})
, satisfies

x_{1}^{\prime}+x_{2}^{\prime}+x_{3}^{\prime}\equiv 0\;\text{mod}\;m.

(2)

D^{2}(\mathbf{u})=H(\mathbf{u})
.

(3)

\mathbf{u}
belongs to a Ducci cycle.

(4)

If
m
odd, then unless
x_{1}=x_{2}=x_{3}
,
Per(\mathbf{u})=6
.

(5)

If
m
is even and the
x_{i}
are not all equal, then either
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})=6
, or
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})=3
and
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\{0,\frac{m}{2}\}
.

where
H
is an endomorphism on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
defined so

H(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})=(x_{2},x_{3},...,x_{n},x_{1}).

The function
H
is first defined by
[
9
]
on page 302, and is also used by
[
1
,
11
]
.

After this, we would like to narrow our focus to when
m
is prime to show that
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})=P_{m}(n)
for most
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{3}
:

Theorem 3
.

Let
n=3
,
m
an odd prime, and
\mathbf{u}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{3}
. If

x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\not\equiv 0\;\text{mod}\;m

and
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
not all equal, then
Per(\mathbf{u})=P_{m}(3)
.

We will then take a look at some specific cases where
n,m
are both prime but
n
may not be
3
and what periods are possible for these cases, as well as how we were able to determine those periods.
Finally, we would like to look at a specific case where
n=m
is prime, and determine all of the possible periods for a tuple
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
for this case:

Theorem 4
.

Let
n=m=p
be an odd prime and let
\delta
be the order of
2
mod
p
. Then there are exactly 3 distinct period lengths for
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{p}
:

(1)

Per(\mathbf{u})=1
, which can only happen when
\mathbf{u}=(0,0,...,0)
.

(2)

Per(\mathbf{u})=\delta
, which can only happen if
\mathbf{u}=(x,x,...,x)
for some

.

(3)

Per(\mathbf{u})=P_{p}(p)=p*\delta
.

There are a few findings from Ducci on

We now return to Ducci on

There are a couple of papers that have looked at the value of

.
One of these papers is

]

For another possible period, we look at the tuples where all of the entries are the same, nonzero value. We say a tuple satisfies the uniformity condition if all of its entries are nonzero and equal. We now prove a lemma about tuples that meet this condition:

Lemma 5
.

Suppose
\mathbf{u}=(x,x,...,x)
for some
0<x<m
. Assume
m=2^{l}m_{1}>1
where
m_{1}\geq 1
is odd and
l\geq 0
.

(1)

Assume
m_{1}=1
. Then
\mathbf{u}
vanishes for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.

(2)

Assume
m_{1}>1
. Let
\delta
be the multiplicative order of
2\;\text{mod}\;m_{1}
then
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})=\delta
when
m_{1}\nmid x
and
\mathbf{u}
vanishes when
m_{1}|x
.

Proof.

We first note that
D^{\alpha}(x,x,...,x)=2^{\alpha}(x,x,...,x)
.

\mathbf{(1)}
: Assume
m=2^{l}
. Then
D^{l}(x,x,...,x)=2^{l}(x,x,...,x)
, which is
\vec{\mathbf{0}}
. Therefore,
(x,x,...,x)
vanishes for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.

\mathbf{(2)}
: First consider when
l=0
so
m=m_{1}>1
is odd. Let
\delta
be the multiplicative order of
2\;\text{mod}\;m
. Then

D^{\delta}(x,x,...,x)=2^{\delta}(x,x,...,x),

or
(x,x,...,x)
. Since
\delta
is the smallest value where this can happen,
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})=\delta
.

Now let
m=2^{l}m_{1}
where
m_{1}>1
and
l>0
. Let
\delta
be the multiplicative order of
2\;\text{mod}\;m_{1}
. If
m_{1}|x
, then

D^{l}(x,x,...,x)=2^{l}(x,x,...,x),

which is
\vec{\mathbf{0}}
, so
\mathbf{u}
vanishes.

Now assume
m_{1}\nmid x
and
2^{l}|x
. Then

D^{\delta}(x,x,...,x)=2^{\delta}(x,x,...,x).

This is
(x,x,...,x)
. Once more,
\delta
is the smallest value where this happens, so
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})=\delta
.

Suppose now that
x
is odd or there exists
1\leq l_{1}<l
such that
x\equiv 0\;\text{mod}\;2^{l_{1}}
and
x\not\equiv 0\;\text{mod}\;2^{l_{1}+1}
. Then

D^{l-l_{1}}(x,x,...,x)=2^{l-l_{1}}(x,x,...,x).

Now
2^{l-l_{1}}x\equiv 0\;\text{mod}\;2^{l}
. Therefore,

\text{Per}(2^{l-l_{1}}x,2^{l-l_{1}}x,...,2^{l-l_{1}}x)=\delta.

Notice

\text{Per}(x,x,...,x)=\text{Per}(2^{l-l_{1}}x,2^{l-l_{1}}x,...,2^{l-l_{1}}x)

because
2^{l-1}(x,x,...,x)
is in the Ducci sequence of
(x,x,...,x)
.
We conclude that
\text{Per}(x,x,...,x)=\delta
.
∎

This therefore opens up one other possible period when
m
is not a power of
2
. Note that this may not always be a distinct period from
P_{m}(n)
. For example,

is also

Lemma 6
.

Assume
m_{1}|m
. Then for every
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}^{n}
, there exists
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
such that
\text{Per}(\mathbf{v})=\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})
.

It follows from Proposition 3.1 of
[
8
]
that
P_{m_{1}}(n)|P_{m}(n)
whenever
m_{1}|m
, but this lemma can extend this finding.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

Assume
m_{1}|m
and let
\mathbf{u}=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}^{n}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\mathbf{u}\in K(\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n})
, otherwise we can take a tuple in the Ducci cycle of
\mathbf{u}
instead.

Let
d=\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})
. Then
D^{d}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{u}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}^{n}
, so isolating the
s
th entry, we have

a_{d,1}x_{s}+a_{d,2}x_{s+1}+\cdots+a_{d,n}x_{s-1}\equiv x_{s}\;\text{mod}\;m_{1}.

Write the left side of this equivalency as
x_{s}+\delta_{s}m_{1}
for some integer
\delta_{s}
.

Let
\mathbf{v}=\displaystyle{\frac{m}{m_{1}}}(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
where we maintain that
0\leq x_{i}<m_{1}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. Then, the
s
th entry of
D^{d}(\mathbf{v})
is

\frac{m}{m_{1}}(a_{d,1}x_{s}+a_{d,2}x_{s+1}+\cdots a_{d,n}x_{s-1}).

This is

\frac{m}{m_{1}}(x_{s}+\delta_{s}m_{1}),

which is equivalent to
\displaystyle{\frac{m}{m_{1}}}x_{s}\;\text{mod}\;m
. Since this is true for every
1\leq s\leq n
,
D^{d}(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{v}
and
\text{Per}(\mathbf{v})|d
. If
\text{Per}(\mathbf{v})<d
, this would imply that
\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})<d
, which forces
\text{Per}(\mathbf{v})=\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})
.
∎

This gives us that if
m_{1}|m
, then

\{\text{Per}(\mathbf{u})\;|\;\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}^{n}\}\subseteq\{\text{Per}(\mathbf{v})\;|\;\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\},

and provides many other possible periods when
m
is not prime.

Notice that in Figure
1

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}

\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{3}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}

L_{4}(3)>L_{2}(3)
. Similarly, this will happen any time
L_{m}(n)>L_{m_{1}}(n)
. In other cases when

is even, other tuples will appear in the transition graph when moving from

to
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}

[

, which says if

\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
that has a predecessor has exactly

\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}^{n}

We have a similar result when

n=n_{1}n_{2}
,
(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n_{1}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n_{1}}

(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n_{1}},x_{1},...,x_{n_{1}},...,x_{1},...,x_{n_{1}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}

This is because

The Coefficients

In order to further investigate Ducci sequences, we will introduce a tool that helps us visualize what a tuple in the Ducci sequence of
(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})

Lemma 11
.

Consider an
j\times j
matrix that follows this pattern:

B=\begin{bmatrix}-1&1&-1&1&\cdots&\pm 1\\
0&-1&1&-1&\cdots&\mp 1\\
1&0&-1&1&\cdots&\pm 1\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mp 1&\pm 1&\mp 1&\pm 1&\cdots&-1\end{bmatrix},

where
\pm
is positive if
j
is even and
\mp
is positive if
j
is odd. Then

det(B)=\begin{cases}1&j\;\text{is even}\\
-1&j\;\text{is odd}\end{cases}.

Proof.

We prove this via induction on
j
, with
j=2
and
j=3
as our basis cases.

Inductive case:
Assume that the hypothesis is true for
j^{\prime}<j
. Notice that the matrix made by removing the first row and first column is the matrix that follows our pattern for
j-1
. By induction, the determinant of this
(j-1)\times(j-1)
matrix is
-1
if
j
is even and
1
if
j
is odd.

Also, if we remove the first column and
i
th row for
3\leq i\leq j
, then the top two remaining rows are

\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&1&\cdots&\pm 1\\
-1&1&-1&\cdots&\mp 1\end{bmatrix}.

Notice that the second row is the first row multiplied by
-1
. Therefore, the determinant of any matrix made by this method has determinant
0
.

If we put this altogether, we get

det(B)=-1*M_{1,1}-0*M_{2,1}+1*M_{3,1}+\cdots+1*M_{j,1}

=\begin{cases}1&j\;\text{is even}\\
-1&j\;\text{is odd}\end{cases},

where
M_{i,j}
is the determinant of the minor of row
i
, column
j
.

From here, the lemma follows.

Evaluation of cyst treatment technique, cyst type, size differences and healing by fractal analysis
Authors: Ömer Faruk Kaygisiz, Ebru Deniz Karsli
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimThe aim of the study was to evaluate the trabeculation increase of treated mandibular cysts.Material and methodThe study included 26 female and 33 male patients (age mean: 38,4 years) with cysts larger than 3 cm in the posterior region of the mandible who were admitted to the same center. Two groups in treatment technique: marsupialization (n = 29) and enucleation (n = 30). Four groups in cyst types: dentigerous cyst (n = 21), keratocyst (n = 19), radicular cyst (n = 15) and residual cyst (n = 4). Cyst size was divided into two categories: smaller than 5.5 cm (n = 31) and larger than 5.5 cm (n = 28). Panoramic radiographs (PR) of the patients were analyzed at the beginning, 6. month and 12. month.ResultAt the end of the treatment, there was no statistically significant difference in terms of Fractal Analysis (FA) between patients treated with marsupialization and enucleation, but considering that the cysts in the marsupialization group were larger in size, a faster increase in FA was observed in the marsupialization group. When the cysts were grouped according to their size, it was observed that healing tissues in cysts smaller than 5.5 cm reached normal FA values faster, while healing in the middle of cysts larger than 5.5 cm took more time.ConclusionsFA is a reproducible and reliable method. In large cysts, marsupialization results in a faster recovery, but FA values at the end of treatment are similar to the enucleation group. Cysts larger than 5.5 cm show a more rapid increase in Fractal Dimension (FD). The centre of the cysts is the area that heals the latest. Studies with larger sample sizes are needed to evaluate the difference in healing between cyst types.Clinical trial numberClinical trial number: Not applicableKeywords: Cyst healing, Fractal analysis, Marsupialization, Enucleation, Cyts size

Introduction

During dental development stages, epithelial tissues accumulate the most in the jaw areas; therefore, cysts of dental origin are most commonly encountered in the jaws [1]. Odontogenic cysts are treated with single or combined applications of methods such as enucleation, marsupialization-decompression and curettage [2]. Enucleation is the most common method used in the treatment of cysts. It is aimed to completely remove the cyst epithelium and its contents to close the surgical procedure area with primary closure. During the enucleation of large cysts, more conservative treatment methods such as marsupialization can be applied to avoid complications such as fracture, sense loss, bleeding, etc. due to the area they cover and their location [3]. This technique, which was described by Partsch in the XIXth century is the process of suturing the cyst epithelium to the oral epithelium and fusing it with the oral mucosa by opening a surgical window of one centimeter in diameter in the cyst wall and buccal mucosa. In this procedure, the pressure inside the cyst is reduced and the cyst is allowed to decrease in size and circumferential bone formation is expected [4].

When evaluating the cortical and trabecular structure of bone, the most accurate diagnosis is histomorphometric analysis performed by bone biopsy. In addition, high-resolution computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging systems are non-invasive gold standard methods used to evaluate bone internal structure [5]. Due to the interventional nature of the biopsy method, the high radiation dose of computed tomography, and the high cost of magnetic resonance imaging, it cannot be used routinely. Non-invasive two-dimensional radiographs, which have less radiation dose and are easier to use routinely, have found more common use.

Fractals allow complex structures and shapes to be defined quantitatively [6]. They are called objects that show self-similarity in different sizes [7]. When the internal structure of the bone was examined, it was understood that the units were similar to the image of the entire structure to which they belonged, and it was stated that they were in accordance with the definition of fractal. This made the FA(fractal anlaysis) method suitable for trabecular bone examinations [8, 9]. FA is a method in which the microarchitecture of trabecular bone is defined by a numerical value. In this method, a mathematical and morphological image processing system is used [10] For this purpose, White and Rudolph first applied the FA method in dentistry in 1999 and reported successful results [11]. The numerical value obtained after fractal analysis is called FD(fractal dimension). If the FD is high, it will be understood that the evaluated structure has a complex structure, while if the FD is low, it will be understood that it has a more simple structure [7, 12].

The literature confirms the correlation between bone tissue structure and FD [5, 13]. It has also been reported that FD is not affected by patient positioning in dental panoramic radiograpyh(PR) and technical variables such as projection angles between − 10 and + 30 degrees [14].

There are not sufficient studies on fractal analysis of jaw cysts in the literature. In addition, the quality of the bone formation after treatment of jaw cysts with marsupialization or enucleation has always been a question mark. This study aims to contribute to readers by evaluating the amount of trabeculation of the bone formed after cyst treatment in the jaw.

Materials and methods

Sample selection and classification

Between 2018 and 2023, 1856 PRs diagnosed as odontogenic cysts in the mandible and treated and completed in Gaziantep University Faculty of Dentistry, Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery were examined. After the examinations, 59 patients aged between 10 and 66 years (mean 38.4) matched the inclusion criteria. Patients with a lesion size greater than 3 cm from the mesial to distal, and without any structures (tooth root, alveolar inferior nerve) that would prevent FA measurement in the lesioned area and its symmetry were included in the study. Patients who received radiotherapy to the head and neck region, had a history of osteoporosis and recurrence of cysts were excluded. Patients were divided into two groups as enucleation (n = 30) and marsupialization (n = 29) according to treatment methods, into four groups as dentigerous cyst (n = 21), keratocyst (n = 19), radicular cyst (n = 15) and residual cyst (n = 4) according to cyst types and into two groups as smaller than 5.5 cm (n = 31) and larger than 5.5 cm (n = 28) according to cyst sizes. The measurements of the patient’s symmetrical jaw region were taken as a control. All PRs had been exposed with a Promax 64 X-ray device (Planmeca XC) operating at 64 kV and 5 mA with a 18-second exposure time.

ROI(Region Of Interest) determination

FA measurements were performed three times in all groups: at the beginning(T0) of treatment, at sixth months(T1) and at twelfth months(T2). The same measurements were taken from the healthy area symmetrical to each lesion included in the study. The measurements obtained were recorded for comparison between the groups mentioned above. Three regions of interest (ROI), namely the mesial, distal and middle of the cyst in the lesioned region of the patient, which do not contain teeth and anatomical structures, were selected so that the ROI determined from the healthy region were exactly symmetrical to those in the lesioned region and their FD were determined. A total of 6 ROI (60 × 60 pixels) in each image, three in the lesion region and three in the symmetrical healthy region, and 18 ROI in each patient were analyzed (Fig. 1).

FD calculation

FA in this study was performed using White and Rudolph’s “box counting” method with the latest version of “ImageJ 1.52v” analysis program published by the “National Institute of Health Image” at “https://imagej.nih.gov” (Fig. 2).

Stages of fractal analysis (M ROI in Fig. 1). a: Cropping the ROI. b: Applying gaussian blur filter. c: Subtracting the filtered image from the original image. d: Adding 128 Gy values. e: Binary processing. f: Erode processing g: Dilate processing h: Skeletonize processing

Using binarisation, we converted them to a dual (black-and-white) format, thus identifying the bone marrow and trabecular structures (Fig. 2.e). We performed image erosion (Fig. 2.f) and dilation (Fig. 2.g) procedures to eliminate noise. Using the ‘Skeletonise’ option, we skeletally outlined the trabeculae in preparation for FA (Fig. 2.h). The ‘invert’ step, we converted white areas in the image to black and black areas to white to reveal the outline of the trabecular bone. Using the box counting method (fractal box counter), we divided the images of sizes 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 32 and 64 pixels into squares and calculated the number of squares containing trabeculae and the total number of squares in PRs for different pixel sizes. We plotted these values on a logarithmic scale to determine the slope of the line that best fits the points on the graph and defined it as the FD.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed with SPSS 28.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA) program with 95% confidence interval. Descriptive statistics were calculated for all parameters in the study. Categorical data were presented as frequencies and percentages, and numerical data were presented in tables using mean ± Standard Deviation (SD). “Kolmogorov Smirnov” and “Shapiro-Wilk” tests were used to analyze whether there was a difference between the distribution of variables and normal distribution. Repeated measures ANOVA test was used to analyze repetitive continuous data. Posthoc Tukey test was used to determine between which groups there was a significant difference in repeated measures. The Ki-square test was used to analyze categorical variables between group.

“Figure 3” was used to show the reason for the differences in the analyses in Table 2. According to Table 2, the difference between marsupialization and enucleation in the “O” region between the sixth and twelfth months (p = 0.002) is in favor of the marsupialization group. Comparison according to cyst types

The aim is to show whether there is statistical significance in terms of FD in the healing process of cyst types. For this reason, firstly, it was checked whether there was a statistical difference between the healthy regions of the patients separated according to the cyst types, and the differences between the lesioned regions were evaluated according to the results.

These results show that radicular cysts, keratocysts, dentigerous cysts, and residual cysts had no significant effect on the “M”, “O” and “D” ROIs in time in both healthy and lesioned areas (p > 0.05). The absence of a difference in these measurements between different cyst types suggests that the effects of these cyst types on FD are similar. The statistical insignificance of the results did not require a Posthoc Tukey test.

Comparison according to cyst size

To look for differences between the groups, healthy regions were firstly analyzed and then the difference in the lesioned regions was evaluated.

These results showed that there was no difference in the measurements made according to cyst size in the healthy side in time (p > 0.05). These findings show that cyst size does not show different effects in terms of FD according to time intervals in healthy regions. When the lesioned regions were analyzed, it was found that there was a statistically significant difference in all ROIs (p < 0.05). Posthoc Tukey test is given in the table (p2) where these differences in lesioned regions are caused.

For the “M” measurement, no significant difference was found between beginning and sixth month, but a significant difference was observed between sixth month and twelfth month). In the “O” measurement, a significant difference was observed between baseline and sixth month moreover, a very significant difference was found between sixth month and twelfth month. In the “D” measurement, there was no significant difference between baseline and sixth month however, a significant difference was observed between sixth and twelfth month.

These results showed that there were significant changes in the measurements of “M”, “O” and “D” according to cyst size on the lesioned side, especially between the sixth and twelfth months. This may suggest that cyst size will show a significant difference in terms of FDs in time. In particular, the significant change detected in the “O” measurements suggests that this measurement is particularly sensitive to the effect of cyst size. To show which group is responsible for the statistically significant values in the table, it is necessary to analyze the averages (Table 5).

Discussion

Marsupialization treatment reduces the risk of complications in the treatment of odontogenic cysts reaching large sizes and helps the spontaneous eruption of the related impacted teeth [15]. The benefits of marsupilization and decompression include gradual diminishing of the cystic cavity, preservation of tooth vitality, prevention of tooth extractions, protection of the alveolar inferior nerve, prevention of oroantral and oronasal intercourse, and prevention of mandibular fracture [16]. Park et al. reported that the enucleation technique should be performed until the size of the lesion is thought not to damage the anatomical structures [17].

Trott reported that odontogenic cysts (52.3% male, 47.7% female) were more common in males than females in his study [18]. In our study, odontogenic cysts were observed at a higher rate in males (55.9%) compared to females (44.1%). In general, the reason why odontogenic cyst rates were higher in men was interpreted as the lack of oral hygiene in men may be higher.

Although the frequency of radicular cysts, dentigerous cysts and keratocysts among odontogenic cysts varies in the literature, Jones et al. In a 30-year retrospective study of 55,446 patients, radicular cysts were reported to be 52.3%, dentigerous cysts 18.1% and keratocysts 11.6% [19]. Ochsenius et al. reported radicular cyst 50.7%, dentigerous cyst 18.5%, keratocyst 14.3% and residual cyst 11.1% [20]. In our study, these rates were 25.4% for radicular cysts, 32.2% for keratocysts, 35.6% for dentigerous cysts and 6.8% for residual cysts. The differences in these rates are thought to be related to the exclusion criteria in our study.

Polat et al. mentioned that botulinum toxin-A applied in patients with bruxism significantly reduced the FD on panoramic radiographs and explained that FA can be applied for quantitative analysis in panoramic radiographs [21]. Öztürk et al. examined the ROIs from the centres of the cysts in their study on children and showed that FDs increased over time and that the FA method can also be applied on cysts [22]. Our study supports these two studies and shows that the FA method can be applied both on odontogenic cysts and on panoramic X-rays. However, there are not enough references in the literature, especially on cysts, where quantitative analyses have been performed. For this reason, in order to make clear comments, sources with a larger sample size and longer follow-up period are needed.

In their studies, Zhao et al. and Kubota et al. reported that lesions treated with the marsupialization technique showed faster decrease in the size of the cysts which had reached large dimensions compared to the smaller ones [23, 24]. Similarly, Demir et al. compared the monthly shrinkage in millimetres in the enucleation and marsupialization groups in a twelve-month follow-up study and found a faster recovery in the marsupialization group, although not significantly [25]. Our study supports Zhao, Kubota and Demir and shows that the marsupialization technique reduces the cyst size faster in the same time period.

In a study by Dereci et al. using two-dimensional measurement methods for cyst sizes, which included thirty patients, significant improvement was observed in both enucleation and marsupialization groups over time, while the improvement between marsupialization and enucleation was not statistically significant [26]. According to Fig. 3, our study clearly shows that both treatment techniques (marsupialization, enucleation) show recovery over time.

In our study, there was no statistical difference between the groups in the control measurements made on the healthy side of the patients who were divided according to treatment techniques, cyst types, and cyst sizes. This shows the reliability of FA measurement in panoramic radiography. Pothuad et al., and Demirbaş et al. also mentioned that the FA method produces reliable results in two-dimensional radiographs as in our study [5, 13].

Ihan et al. reported that the rate of healing in bone density at the end of 12 months was 88% in cysts larger than 30 mm and Rabin reported that the rate of recovery in cysts larger than 15.7 mm was 81% [27, 28]. Rubio et al. reported that healing was complete at the end of twelve months in 18 patients in whom enucleation method was applied [29]. These studies partially support the results of our study. According to our study, the healing in the lesioned areas was close to the FDs in the healthy areas at the end of twelve months in all our patients. This result is interpreted that 12 months is a sufficient time for the cysts to heal.

According to Table 2, the fact that there was no significant result between the beginning and the sixth month in the ‘O’ region, while there was a significant result between the sixth month and the twelfth month was interpreted that the healing in the ‘O’ region was later due to the larger diameter of the cysts in the marsupialization group. The fact that the statistical analyses in the ‘M’ and ‘D’ regions are not significant and the ‘O’ value is significant at all time intervals in the regions with lesions confirms that the healing of cysts is from the periphery to the center, as described in the literature [30].

Castro et al. did not find any difference between the newly formed bone and the bone in the healthy area in odontogenic cysts decompressed by histological analysis method [31]. Our study also supports Castroy’s results as it showed similar results between the two groups at the end of treatment.

According to Table 3, when we compared the cyst types in our study, no statistical differences were found in terms of healing, but it will undoubtedly be useful to have studies with more samples comparing cyst types in the literature.

Jun et al. classified their patients as larger and smaller than 3.5 cm in 23 patients with a 10-month follow-up and reported that cysts larger than 3.5 cm resulted in a faster recovery at the end of treatment [32]. Song et al. in a study on different types of cysts found a faster reduction in the diameter of lesions with larger cyst size at the beginning of treatment [33]. According to Table 4, in our study, cysts larger than 5.5 cm in the M region experienced a greater increase in FD between the sixth and twelfth months. In the ‘O’ region, cysts smaller than 5.5 cm had more FD increase between the beginning and sixth month, while cysts larger than 5.5 cm had more FD increase between the sixth and twelfth month. This difference in the ‘O’ region may indicate that cysts smaller than 5.5 cm heal more in the first six months at the site of origin, whereas larger cysts generally heal at the site of origin after the sixth month. In region “D”, like region “M”, only between the sixth and twelfth month, cysts larger than 5.5 cm showed a greater increase in FD. For cysts larger than 5.5 cm, this difference between “M” and “D” regions between the sixth and twelfth months shows that the improvement in the peripheral regions of cysts larger than 5.5 cm is not adequate in the first six months and is generally higher in the second six months. As a general comment, although six months is not sufficient for the healing of cysts larger than 5.5 cm, it is considered that there is healing from the periphery to the center in all jaw cysts.

The significant increase in FD values at the end of the treatment in this study quantitatively shows the change in bone structure. As a result of this study, it is clear that FA of trabecular bone will help in the healing process evaluation of jaw cysts.

The fractal analysis method, which was first used in medicine, is becoming increasingly popular and increasingly used in dentistry. This method can be supported such as technology and artificial intelligence and can guide clinicians about the healing process and that can accelerate their decision process in future.

Limitation

The follow-up period of the study may be relatively short to capture long-term changes in trabecular structures. While FA is a valuable method, the choice of the box-counting method may have its own limitations. In particular, the sample size was small due to the presence of structures that interfere with FA measurements such as teeth, mental nerve, alveolaris inferior nerve, both in the lesion area and in the symmetrical area. This is another limitation.

Conclusion

This study supports the applicability and safety of the FA method in areas with cysts. FA method is a bone quantification technique that can be repeated and gives reliable results.

Marsupialization technique is a safer technique for large cysts. The amount of trabeculation in the lesions treated with the marsupialization technique during the twelve-month treatment period was similar to those treated with the enucleation technique; however, considering that the lesions in the marsupialization group were significantly larger in size, it is thought that the marsupialization technique shows a faster healing effect.

As a result of our studies -as in the literature- it is proved that odontogenic cysts heal from the periphery to the centre.

There was no difference in FA between the different types of cysts during the twelve-month treatment period. Comparisons according to cyst size showed that the trabeculation in the centre of small cysts was higher than that of large cysts in the first six-month period, while in the second six-month period, the increase in trabeculation in the centre of large cysts was higher than that of small cysts. At the end of twelve months, there was no difference in the amount of trabeculation between large and small cysts. Therefore, it can be said that twelve months is sufficient for the healing of the cysts.

There is a lack of publications on the application of FA on cysts. Considering the data, this article is one of the first to apply FA to jaw cysts. It is believed that this article will guide and contribute to the literature on this subject.

Considering all these results, more studies applying the fractal analysis method to cyst healing are needed.

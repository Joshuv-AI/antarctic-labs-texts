From Blockage to Passage: Twin Gallstones' Journey Through the Gut
Authors: Cong Ning, Xuan Wang, Wenjing Liu
Published: 2025

A 41-year-old man with cholecystolithiasis and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease presented to our hospital because of right upper abdominal pain and vomiting for one day. Physical examination revealed a positive Murphy sign, with no jaundice. Laboratory tests showed a white blood cell count of 14,750 per cubic millimeter (normal range: 3,500–9,500/mm3); the alanine aminotransferase level was 25 units per liter (normal range: 9–50 U/L), and the total bilirubin level was 9.8 µmol per liter (normal range: 5.1–22.2 µmol/L). After the insertion of a nasogastric tube, a large amount of gastric content mixed with bile was aspirated. Enhanced abdominal computed tomography revealed a cholecystoduodenal fistula (a) and the presence of the Rigler triad—pneumobilia (b), ectopic gallstones within the bowel lumen (c, with the arrow indicating a stone and the arrowhead indicating a suspected proximal stone), and intestinal obstruction (c, with the hollow arrow indicating the collapsed distal bowel). The patient continued to experience abdominal pain, and subsequent plain magnetic resonance imaging showed ileal obstruction with a petal-like arrangement of the bowel loops (d). Two ectopic stones were confirmed on different MR sequences (e and f). After 12 days of treatment, including oral liquid paraffin and intravenous antibiotics, the patient's abdominal pain was relieved, and he spontaneously passed 2 stones (g). He was referred for outpatient cholecystectomy and fistula repair. (Informed consent was obtained from the patient to publish these images.)

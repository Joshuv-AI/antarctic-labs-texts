QTL analysis in the Mianmai 902×Taichang 29 RIL population reveals the genetic basis for the high-yield of wheat cultivars Mianmai 902 in terms of spike and plant architecture
Authors: Baowei Chen, Yuanjiang He, Yanyan Tang, Zhengxi Lin, Jinhui Wang, Juanyu Zhang, Junjun Liang, Haili Zhang, Guangbing Deng, Yong Ren, Tao Li, Hai Long
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Spike-related traits and plant height (PH) are greatly associated with wheat yield. Identification of stable quantitative trait loci (QTL) for these traits is crucial for understanding the genetic basis for yield and their further application in breeding. In this study, QTL analysis for spikelet number per spike (SNS), spike length (SL), spike compactness (SC) and PH was performed using a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population derived from a cross between wheat cultivars Mianmai902 (MM902) and Taichang29 (TC29). Thirteen stably expressed QTLs were identified, and the most favorable alleles were contributed by MM902. The mjaor QTL, QSNS-MT.cib-2D, QSL-MT.cib-2D, QSC-MT.cib-2D, QSC-MT.cib-6 A, QPH-MT.cib-4B and QPH-MT.cib-4D, were repeatedly detected in multiple environments and explained 5.77-47.11% of the phenotypic variations. By employing the Kompetitive Allele Specific PCR markers, the most major QTLs were successfully validated in multiple populations derived from different genetic backgrounds. Moreover, the individual and pyramiding effects of major QTLs on SNS, SL, SC, PH, grain number per spike (GNS) and thousand-grain weight (TGW) were investigated and their potential utilization value in breeding was showcased. Ppd-D1 was predicted as the candidate for QSNS/SL/SC/PH-MT.cib-2D, and MM902 carried the Ppd-D1d allele. Ppd-D1d is rare in Chinese winter wheat cultivars and may be an elite alternative allele of the Ppd-D1a allele. In summary, these QTLs revealed the genetic basis of the spike-related traits and PH of MM902, which partially contributed to the high yield of MM902 and have application potential in wheat breeding by optimizing spike morphology and PH to improve yield.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-024-05885-w.

Background

Common wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is a major cereal crop worldwide, and plays a crucial role in ensuring global food security. However, the food crisis is intensifying with the population explosion, climate change and continuous reduction in the cultivated area [1]. Therefore, improving yield is always the primary goal of wheat breeding.

Yield of wheat is a complex trait that is significantly affected by environmental and genetic factors, and is usually comprised by grain number per spike (GNS), thousand-grain weight (TGW) and spike number per unit area [2]. Spike as the reproductive organ is vital for wheat yield. Previous studies have demonstrated that the spike-related traits, including spikelet number per spike (SNS), spike length (SL) and spike compactness (SC), are greatly associated with GNS and TGW [3]. Moreover, plant height (PH) which is negatively correlated with lodging resistance is another important factor limiting wheat yield. Therefore, elucidating genetic basis of these traits is important for breeding high-yield wheat varieties.

In the past decades, there have been significant advances in our understanding of the genetic control of spike-related traits and PH in wheat. Q gene encodes an AP2 transcript factor which has pleiotropic effects on SL, SC, PH and grain thresh ability [4–6]. S gene encodes a Ser/Thr protein kinase glycogen synthesis kinase3, which confers short wheat (Triticum aestivum ssp. sphaerococcum) semispherical grains and short dense spike [7, 8]. TaCOL-B5 encoding a CONSTANS-like protein increases SNS and yield by modifying wheat spike architecture [9]. WAPO1 encodes a F-box protein, a component of ubiquitin ligase, which is both necessary and sufficient to increase SNS in wheat [10, 11]. Ppd-A1, Ppd-B1 and Ppd-D1 have an effect on spike development by controlling the photoperiod response of wheat [12, 13]. Moreover, there are 27 dwarfing genes (Rht1-Rht27) have been formally cataloged, but only Rht1 (Rht-B1b), Rht2 (Rht-D1b), Rht8 and Rht24 are widely used in wheat breeding [14–16]. Rht-B1b and Rht-D1b along with their alleles Rht3 (Rht-B1c), Rht10 (Rht-D1c), Rht11 (Rht-B1e) and Rht17 (Rht-B1p) encode an DELLA protein and reduce PH via inhibiting gibberellin (GA) signal transduction [17, 18]. The Rht8, Rht12, Rht13, Rht23, Rht18/Rht24 and Rht25 affect PH by regulating GA biosynthesis [6, 19–25]. Additionally, with the rapid development of molecular marker technologies, wheat SNP array and sequencing-based genotyping platforms, a number of quantitative trait loci (QTL) for spike-related traits and PH in wheat have been identified by linkage mapping and association analysis [1, 2, 16, 26–31]. However, only a few major QTLs were verified and very few linked markers could be used for marker-assisted selection (MAS) in wheat breeding.

In the present study, a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population derived from a cross between Mianmai 902 (MM902) and Taichang 29 (TC29) were constructed. Combining phenotyping in five field trials and genotyping by the wheat 55 K SNP array, chromosomal regions controlling SNS, SL, SC and PH were identified. The major QTLs were validated by using the Kompetitive Allele-Specific PCR (KASP) markers in seven segregation populations. Moreover, the individual and pyramiding effect of major QTLs on spike-related traits, PH, GNS and TGW were measured to evaluate their application potential in wheat breeding.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and field trails

A RIL population derived from the cross of MM902×TC29 (MT, 153 F7 RILs) was developed and used for QTL mapping. MM902, released in 2019, is an excellent high yield wheat cultivar with the characteristics of long spike, high spikelet number per spike (SNS), high thousand grain weight (TGW) and high yield, and TC29 is a commercial cultivars [32, 33]. Seven F2 populations derived from the crosses of MM902×Kechengmai1 (K1), MM902×Kechengmai4 (K4), MM902×Chuanmai42 (CM42), MM902×Da11 (D11), MM902×Da24 (D24), MM902×Da31 (D31) and TC29×D11 were employed to validate the major QTL in different genetic backgrounds. K1, K4 and CM42 are commercial cultivars, and D11, D24 and D31 are advanced elite lines.

For the MT population, phenotypes were evaluated in five different environments in Sichuan province, including Mianyang (104˚19’E, 31˚22’N) in 2022 (E2) and 2023 (E4), Shifang (104˚11’E, 31˚6’N) in 2022 (E1) and 2023 (E3) and Shuangliu (103˚52’E, 30˚34’N) in 2023 (E5). All three experimental sites belong to the Southwest Wheat Zone in China and have subtropical monsoon climates. Weather conditions of the five field trails during the wheat growth stage were listed in the Table S1. The trials were performed with a randomized block design, and each line of the populations and parents were single-seed planted in a three-row plot with a row length of 2.0 m and a row spacing of 30 cm. The validation populations were phenotyped at Shifang in 2022 with a 15 cm spacing between individuals. Field management and disease control were performed in accordance with local standard practices.

Phenotypic evaluation and statistical analysis

At maturity, five representative plants from each MT line were randomly selected for phenotypic evaluation. All data were collected from the main spikes. Plant height (PH) was measured from the ground to the tip of spike excluding the awns, SL was determined by measuring from the base of the rachis to the terminal spikelet excluding awns, SNS was determined by counting the number of fertile spikelets per spike and spike compactness (SC) was computed by dividing the number of SNS by the SL. Subsequently, the selected spikes were harvested and manually threshed for evaluating grain number per spike (GNS), grain length (GL), grain width (GW), and TGW using the SC-G software (Wseen, Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China). These agronomic traits of individuals in the validation populations were also measured as mentioned above.

Basic statistical analysis, the Student’s t test, analysis of variance (ANOVA) and frequency distribution were performed using SPSS version 20.0 (SPSS, Chicago, USA). The best linear unbiased prediction (BLUP) used for combined QTL detection and effect analyses was calculated using the mixed linear model in R package “lme4”. The broad hereditary capacity (H2) for each trait was estimated according to the method described previously [34].

Genetic map construction and quantitative trait locus detection

The wheat 55 K SNP array was used for genotyping the MT lines and their parents. The genetic linkage map was constructed using the JoinMap 4.1 (https://www.kyazma.nl/index.php/JoinMap/) and IciMapping 4.1 software (https://www.isbreeding.net/). Redundant markers were removed using IciMapping 4.1 with “Missing Rates” and “Distortion Value” being set to 20% and 0.01, respectively. Consequently, unique markers were organized into correspondent linkage groups using JoinMap 4.1 with LOD values ranging from 2 to 10. The order of markers within a group was arranged using JoinMap 4.1 with “LOD values” and “Round” being set as ≥5 and = 3, respectively.

The QTLs were scanned with IciMapping 4.1 through inclusive composite interval mapping (ICIM). The parameters were set as walking step = 0.001 cM, PIN = 0.0005 and logarithm of odds (LOD) score ≥ 3. A QTL detected in more than three environments and explained more than average 10% of phenotypic variation was treated as a major QTL. The QTLs were denoted according to the International Rules of Genetic Nomenclature (https://wheat.pw.usda.gov/ggpages/wgc/98/Intro.htm), “cib” represents the Chengdu Institute of Biology, the Chinese Academy of Science.

Markers development and QTL validation in different backgrounds

Based on the preliminary QTL mapping results, the SNP in the confidence interval of the major QTLs were used to develop KASP markers to genotype the validation population, and individuals in the validation populations were then divided into three categories. The students’ t test was performed to evaluate the difference in SNS, SL, SC and PH between the two homozygous categories. The KASP assays were carried out on a Bio-Rad CFX96 real-time PCR system according to the method described by Li et al. [1].

Candidate gene cloning and haplotype analysis

On the basis of QTL mapping results, the flanking markers sequence of major QTLs were anchored to the corresponding chromosomes of Chinese Spring (IWGSC RefSeq v1.0). Genes within the physical interval were extracted from IWGSC RefSeq v1.1 annotation. Subsequently, key candidate gene was identified by comparison with previous studies. Finally, high quality genomic DNA of MM902 and TC29 were extracted using the CTAB method. Candidate genes were amplified by PCR using gene-specific primers, and sequenced by Sanger sequencing (YouKang Biotech Corporation, Hangzhou, China). Specific primers were designed using the WheatOmics PrimerServer (http://wheatomics.sdau.edu.cn/PrimerServer/). PCR was performed in a volume of 25 μL, which contained 50–100 ng template DNA, 12.5 μl of 2× Accurate Taq Master Mix (Accurate Biology, Beijing, China), 2 μl of primer mix with a concentration of 10 umol/L, and 9.5 μl of ddH2O. The sequences were analyzed using DNAMAN v8.0 software (Lynnon Biosoft, Quebec, Canada). Haplotypes analysis was performed using the re-sequencing data of 246 wheat cultivars of China [35–38], and the distribution of haplotypes was drawn using the “sf”, “ggplot2” and “ggspatial” package of R software.

Results

Phenotypic analysis

SNS, SL, SC and PH of the MT lines and their parents were evaluated in five environments. As shown in Table 1, significant differences in SNS, SL and SC were observed between the parents under all environments. MM902 had higher SNS and SL, but lower SC than TC29 (Fig. 1). In the MT population, the four traits showed wide variations in SNS, SL, SC, and PH, which ranged from 13.2 to 25.2, 7.54 cm to 15.8 cm, 1.1 to 2.76, and 51.4 cm to 127.3 cm, respectively (Table 1; Fig. 1). ANOVA analysis showed that the Broad-sense heritability (H2) of SNS, SL, SC and PH was 0.91, 0.86, 0.89 and 0.96, respectively, suggesting that they are mainly determined by genetic factors (Table 1). Moreover, SNS, SL, SC and PH exhibited continuous variations in all environments and the BLUP datasets (Fig. 2), suggesting that they are controlled by multiple genes, therefore suitable for QTL analysis.

Construction of genetic linkage map

Among 53,063 SNPs in the wheat 55 K SNP array, 17,561 SNPs polymorphic between parents were selected for subsequent analysis. Firstly, the SNPs with missing rate ≥ 20% were removed. The rest SNPs with no recombination were subsequently placed into one bin. In order to reduce complexity of the genetic linkage map, only one SNP from each bin was selected. Finally, a genetic linkage map was constructed by using 1561 bin markers, which spanning 2591.1 cM with an average interval distance of 1.66 cM/marker (Table S2; Table S3). The number of markers located on A, B and D genomes were 507, 531 and 523, respectively (Table S3). In order to assess the quality of the genetic linkage map, the collinearity between the physical and genetic position of markers in the genetic linkage map was analyzed. As showed in Fig. 3, a high consistence was observed between the genetic position and physical position of the markers on most chromosomes, suggesting the genetic linkage map constructed in this study was satisfied for QTL analysis.

QTL mapping

A total of 13 QTLs for SNS, SL, SC and PH were identified on chromosomes 1B, 2B, 2D, 3D, 4B, 4D and 6 A. Six of them were considered major QTL that could be detected in more than three individual environments and the BLUP datasets and explain more than average 10% the phenotypic variation (Table 2).

One major QTL QSNS-MT.cib-2D for SNS was identified on chromosome 2D. It can be detected in five environments and the BLUP datasets, explaining 26.51–48.36% of the phenotypic variation with LOD values ranging from 10.16 to 25.01. The favorable allele of QSNS-MT.cib-2D was contributed by MM902 (Table 2).

For SL, three QTLs were detected on chromosomes 2B, 2D and 6 A. QSL-MT.cib-2D was a major QTL, which was identified in three environments and the BLUP datasets. It explained 8.32–16.04% of the SNS variation and had LOD values of 3.41–6.48 (Table 2). QSL-MT.cib-2B was detected in two environments, explaining 6.08-7.25% of the phenotypic variation. QSL-MT.cib-6 A was detected in one environment and the BLUP datasets, explaining 7.41-10.81% of the phenotypic variation. Alleles increasing SL of the three loci were provided by MM902 (Table 2).

Five QTLs for SC were detected on chromosomes 1B, 2B, 2D, 3D and 6 A (Table 2). QSC-MT.cib-2D was identified in three environments and the BLUP datasets. It explained 13.93-33.69% of the SC variation and had LOD values of 6.55–18.46, which was considered major QTL. QSC-MT.cib-6 A was another major QTL for SC that was identified in four environments and the BLUP datasets and explained 7.3-15.73% of the SC variations with LOD values of 4-8.13. The rest three QTLs, QSC-MT.cib-1B, QSC-MT.cib-2B and QSC-MT.cib-3D were detected in three environments, one environment and three environments, respectively, accounting for 3.65–8.32% of the SNS variation. The alleles increasing SC of QSC-MT.cib-1B, QSC-MT.cib-2B, QSC-MT.cib-3D and QSC-MT.cib-6 A were provided by TC29, and that of QSC-MT.cib-2D was provided by MM902 (Table 2).

Four PH QTLs were identified on chromosomes 2B, 2D, 4B and 4D. QPH-MT.cib-4B and QPH-MT.cib-4D were two major QTLs, contributing 12.96–17.68% and 14.7-25.94% of the PH variation and having LOD values of 5.14–8.72 and 6.01–9.05, respectively. Both QPH-MT.cib-4B and QPH-MT.cib-4D were identified in five environments and the BLUP datasets (Table 2). For the rest two QTLs, QPH-MT.cib-2B was detected in two environments, explaining 3.85-6.07% of the PH variation; QPH-MT.cib-2D was detected in four environments and the BLUP datasets, explaining 6.5-8.6% of the PH variation (Table 2). Among them, MM902 conferred alleles of increasing PH at QPH-MT.cib-2D and QPH-MT.cib-4B, and decreasing PH at QPH-MT.cib-2B and QPH-MT.cib-4D (Table 2), respectively.

Effects of major QTLs on yield-related traits

In this study, six major QTLs for SNS, SL, SC and PH were detected, including QSNS-MT.cib-2D, QSL-MT.cib-2D, QSC-MT.cib-2D, QPH-MT.cib-4B, QPH-MT.cib-4D and QSC-MT.cib-6 A. Among them, QSNS-MT.cib-2D, QSL-MT.cib-2D and QSC-MT.cib-2D were co-located in a overlapped region on chromosome 2D, thus temporarily designated as QSNS/SL/SC-MT.cib-2D. Based on the flanking markers, effects of the major QTLs on corresponding trait were evaluated in the mapping population. As expected, the phenotypic values of lines carrying the favorable allele at the six loci were significantly higher than those without the favorable alleles under all environments and the BLUP datasets (Fig. 4).

Additionally, we also evaluated the effects of these major QTLs on GNS, TGW, GW and GL by using the BLUP datasets. As shown in Fig. 5, lines with the allele from MM902 at QSNS/SL/SC-MT.cib-2D had more GNS, but lower GW and TGW than those with the allele from TC29. For the QPH-MT.cib-4B, no significant differences in GNS, GL, GW and TGW were observed between the two groups carrying different parental alleles (Fig. 5). For the QPH-MT.cib-4D, lines possessing alleles from MM902 had more GNS than those possessing alleles from TC29, but no significant difference in GL, GW and TGW (Fig. 5). At the QSC-MT.cib-6 A, alleles from MM902 significantly increased TGW and GW, but had no effect on GNS and GL (Fig. 5).

Validation of QSNS/SC/SL-MT.cib-2D, QPH-MT.cib-4B and QPH-MT.cib-4D in different genetic backgrounds

To validate the major QTLs, three KASP markers, KASP_AX-111956072, KASP_AX-110976982 and KASP_AX-111705921 that are tightly linked with QSNS/SC/SL-MT.cib-2D, QPH-MT.cib-4B and QPH-MT.cib-4D, respectively, were developed and used to genotype the seven validation populations (Table S4). According to the genotypes, the individuals of the validation populations were divided into three categories, including plants with homozygous alleles of MM902, TC29 and heterozygote. Significant differences in SNS, SL, SC and PH were examined between the two categories with homozygous alleles.

For QSNS/SC/SL-MT.cib-2D, category possessing the alleles from MM902 demonstrated a significant increase of SNS by 9.00-20.32%, and of SC by 8.29–15.28% in the MM902×K1, MM902×K4, MM902×D11, MM902×D24 and MM902×D31 populations, respectively (Table 4). It also increased PH by 6.66–13.08% in the MM902×K1, MM902×K4 and MM902×D24 populations (Table 4). However, no significant difference in SL was observed between the two categories in the five populations (Table 4), indicating that its effect on SL may be affected by the genetic background. For QPH-MT.cib-4B, alleles from TC29 significantly decreased PH by 13.60%, and had no effect on SNS, SL and SC in the TC29×D11 population (Table 4). For QPH-MT.cib-4D, alleles from MM902 significantly decreased PH by 14.76-22.50% with no effect on SNS, SL and SC in the MM902×K1, MM902×K4, MM902×CM42 and MM902×D24 populations (Table 4).

Candidate gene prediction and haplotype analysis of QSNS/SL/SC-MT.cib-2D

QSNS/SL/SC-MT.cib-2D was stably detected between AX-86171316 and AX-110452779, corresponding to the physical interval of 34.23 Mb-37.04 Mb on the short arm of chromosome 2D of Chinese Spring (CS V1.0) (Table S2; Table 2). In this interval, there is a famous photoperiodic responsive gene Ppd-D1 (TraesCS2D02G079600, 33.952 Mb- 33.957 Mb) that is associated with the PH and spike-related traits. Sequence analysis showed that MM902 had a 5 bp deletion in exon 7, indicating that Ppd-D1 may be the candidate gene of QSNS/SL/SC-MT.cib-2D (Table S4; Fig. 6). Moreover, haplotype analysis showed that only 11 wheat cultivars (5.09%) have the 5 bp deletion among 216 Chinese wheat cultivars (Fig. 6; Table S5).

Discussion

Wheat spike-related traits and PH are important agronomic traits related to yield and lodging resistance, thus revealing the genetic basis for these traits is essential for enhancing yield potential by optimizing the spike and plant architecture. In the present study, a RIL population derived from the cross of MM902×TC29 was developed and used to identify QTL for SNS, SL, SC and PH. By combining phenotypes collected from five field trials and a the high-density genetic linkage map, 13 stable QTL controlling SNS, SL, SC and PH were identified, and six of them were considered as major QTLs (Table 2).

Comparison of the major QTLs with previous studies

QSNS-MT.cib-2D, QSL-MT.cib-2D, QSC-MT.cib-2D and QPH-MT.cib-2D were co-located between AX-86171316 and AX-110452779 corresponding to 34.23–37.04 Mb on chromosome arm 2DS (Table 2; Table S2). This interval was overlapped with QTSPN/QFSPN.sicau-2D.5, QSl.sdau-2D-2, Fsn.sdau-2D-2, QSl.czm-2D.1, QTsn.czm-2D.2, QFsn.cau-2D and QSc.cau-2D.2 [31, 39–41]. Moreover, Ppd-D1 (TraesCS2D02G079600) is also located in this interval. In previous studies, seven alleles of wheat Ppd-D1 gene have been detected: Ppd-D1b contains the complete sequence, Ppd-D1a has a 2089 bp deletion in the promoter region, Ppd-D1c shows an unknown sequence insertion in exon 2, Ppd-D1d comprises a 5 bp deletion in exon 7, Ppd-D1e has a single-base mutation in exon 8, Ppd-D1f contains an AT base mutation between exons 5 and 6, and Ppd-D1h carries an AT deletion in exon 5 [40, 42]. Sequence analysis showed that MM902 contains the Ppd-D1d allele. The 5-bp-deletion of Ppd-D1d results in a frameshift mutation and produces a truncated protein lacking the CCT domain, which leads to loss or reduced function of Ppd-D1. Thus, Ppd-D1 may be the candidate gene of QSNS/SL/SC/PH-MT.cib-2D [40]. QPH-MT.cib-4B was located in the interval flanked by markers AX-111068079 and AX-108922248 on chromosome arm 4BS (Table 2; Table S2), which was overlapped with qPh-4B, QPH.caas-4BS.2, QPh.cau-4B.2 and QPH.caas-4BS, and near to the “Green Revolution” gene Rht-B1 [6, 43–45]. QPH-MT.cib-4D located between AX-89371673 and AX-111525922 on chromosome arm 4DS was adjacent to another “Green Revolution” gene Rht-D1 (Table 2; Table S2), and overlapped with the reported QTLs QPH.caas-4DS.1, QPh.cau-4D.1, QPH.caas-4DS and Qph-psr-4D [43–46]. QSC-MT.cib-6 A was mapped to the interval flanked by markers AX-110534709 and AX-111465491 on chromosome arm 6AS (Table 2; Table S2). This interval was adjacent to QSc/Sl.cib-6 A and QScn.cas-6 A.1 [1, 29]. QSL-MT.cib-2B mapped between AX-110523323 and AX-110546949 on chromosome arm 2BL (Table 2; Table S2) was overlapped with QSl.cas-2B.2 [29]. For the rest QTL, QSL-MT.cib-6 A, QSC-MT.cib-1B, QSC-MT.cib-2B, QSC-MT.cib-3D and QPH-MT.cib-2B, no reported QTL/gene was overlapped with them, indicating that they may be novel.

Application potential of major QTLs in wheat breeding

Spike is the reproductive organ of wheat and PH is an important trait of plant architecture. QTL controlling these traits usually exhibit pleiotropic effects on yield components. In the present study, we found that the MM902-derived alleles of QSNS/SL/SC/PH-MT.cib-2D significantly increased SNS, SL, SC, PH and GNS, but decreased TGW, that of QPH-MT.cib-4D significantly decreased PH and TGW, but increased GNS, that of QPH-MT.cib-4B significantly increased PH with no penalty on other yield-related traits, and that of QSC-MT.cib-6 A significantly decreased SC and increased TGW (Fig. 5). These results indicated that these QTLs could be applied for wheat yield improvement through optimizing spike and plant architecture.

In the wheat breeding practices, there is often a mutual balance and limitation among yield-related traits. An increase in a single yield factor may lead to the decrease in another or others, which in turn limits the overall improvement of wheat yield. For example, Rht-B1b and Rht-D1b reduces PH accompanying short coleoptiles, reduced biomass, low nitrogen fertilizer use efficiency, and other undesirable performance [47]. Rht12 and Rht5 reduce PH, but delay the heading date and anthesis [48–50]. Rht23 significantly shortens the SL while reducing PH [6, 51]. QSNS/SL/SC/PH-MT.cib-2D is an important locus for the high yield of MM902. However, the increased PH and GNS simultaneously increased the risk of lodging and negatively affected grain weight, respectively, which are undesirable for yield improvement (Fig. 5). Interestingly, with the aggregation of QPH-MT.cib-4D and QSC-MT.cib-6 A, the promoting effects of QSNS/SL/SC/PH-MT.cib-2D on SNS, SL and GNS were maintained, but its impact on PH and TGW was weakened (Table 3). These results suggested that pyramiding QTLs with different (especially complementary) effects provides an effective approach for optimizing their positive roles in improving wheat yield.

In addition, Ppd-D1 (TraesCS2D02G079600) is the candidate gene of QSNS/SL/SC/PH-MT.cib-2D. Among the alleles of Ppd-D1, the photoperiod-insensitive allele Ppd-D1a is the predominant allele and widespread in the world. Previous studies showed that the effect of Ppd-1a is to compress the life cycle, reduce the time to the initiation of flower development and the time from spike initiation to the production of the terminal spikelet, which are related to decreasing SNS and GNS [52]. In the present study, we found that the MM902 carries the Ppd-D1d allele (Fig. 6). Haplotype analysis revealed that only 5.09% of Chinese winter wheat accessions carry the Ppd-D1d allele (Fig. 6; Table S5). Moreover, in the official yield test, MM902 only matured 2.5 days later than control [32]. Thus, the Ppd-D1d allele could be an alternative allele of the Ppd-D1a, and QSNS/SL/SC/PH-MT.cib-2D combined with QPH-MT.cib-4D and QSC-MT.cib-6 A may be an elite module for breeding high-yield wheat varieties.

Genetic basic of yield of Mianmai 902

MM902 is an excellent wheat cultivar with a long spike, high SNS, high TGW and high yield in the wheat regions of Southwest China [33]. Remarkably, among the QTL detected in this study, MM902 contributes the alleles decreasing PH at QPH-MT.cib-4D and QPH-MT.cib-2B, and increasing PH at QPH-MT.cib-4B and QPH-MT.cib-2D (Fig. 7), which explained the over-parent transgressive segregation of PH in the MM902 × TC29 RIL population (Fig. 1; Table 1). Moreover, alleles with increasing SNS or SL of QSNS-MT.cib-2D, QSL-MT.cib-2D and QSL-MT.cib-6 A were all derived from the MM902 (Fig. 7), which shape the long spike, high SNS and GNS of MM902. SC is usually negatively correlated with TGW [30, 53, 54]. Of the five QTLs for SC, only one allele (QSC-MT.cib-2D) with a positive effect on SC comes from MM902 (Fig. 7). Additionally, the allele of MM902 at QSC-MT.cib-6 A has pleiotropic and positive effects on TGW (Fig. 5). Therefore, these QTLs contribute positively to the genetic basis of high yield of MM902 in terms of spike and plant architecture.

Conclusion

In the present study, 13 QTL for SNS, SL, SC and PH were identified, and 6 of them were major QTL. The most major QTLs were validated in different genetic backgrounds by developing tightly linked KASP markers. The individual and pyramiding effect analysis showed that these QTLs along with their combination may have application potential in wheat breeding. Ppd-D1 was predicted the candidate gene for QSNS/SL/SC/PH-MT.cib-2D, and MM902 carried the Ppd-D1d allele, which is rare in Chinese winter wheat accessions. The results obtained in this study provide insight of the genetic basis of the high yield of MM902, and these QTLs and KASP markers could be used to optimize spike morphology and PH to improve yield in wheat breeding.

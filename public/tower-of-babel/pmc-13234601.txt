Fully human anti-FOLR1 T-cell engager demonstrates potent activity in CBFA2T3::GLIS2 acute megakaryoblastic leukemia

TO THE EDITOR:

Acute megakaryoblastic leukemia (AMKL) represents ∼10% of pediatric cases.1 It is often associated with chromosomal rearrangements producing oncogenic fusion transcripts but otherwise has minimal mutational burden.2 The most common fusion event (∼20% of pediatric AMKL cases) is CBFA2T3::GLIS2, which drives self-renewal and reduces differentiation signaling through Hedgehog, JAK-STAT, and WNT/β-catenin pathways.3,4 Prognosis is particularly poor for this AMKL subtype, with a 5-year event-free survival rate of no more than 20%.5 Folate receptor alpha (FOLR1) has emerged as a promising therapeutic target in CBFA2T3::GLIS2 AMKL; transcriptomic and proteomic analyses show that FOLR1 expression is uniquely associated with this subtype6,7 and is minimally expressed in normal tissues,8, 9, 10 suggesting that targeting it may enable selective elimination of leukemic cells while sparing normal counterparts.

To specifically target FOLR1, we generated a panel of 12 distinct, fully human monoclonal antibodies using previously described techniques (Figure 1A).11 Of these, 9 bound both human and cynomolgus monkey FOLR1 in flow cytometry assays, reflecting desirable cross-species reactivity for preclinical development (supplemental Figure 1). Clone SC-68 displayed excellent manufacturability (supplemental Figures 2 and 3), strong binding by flow cytometry (supplemental Figure 4), and performed well as an immunohistochemical reagent on a rabbit immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) backbone (supplemental Figure 5). Staining of a pediatric normal tissue microarray with SC-68 confirmed restricted FOLR1 expression (supplemental Figure 6). To assess functional utility, we engineered a fully human bispecific T-cell engager (TCE) combining the FOLR1 binding domain of clone SC-68 with an anti-CD3 single-chain variable fragment (scFv) derived from muromonab-CD3 (OKT3).13,14 The design used a tetravalent IgG-based format with (G4S1)3 linkers between variable domains and Fc (fragment crystallizable) region substitutions (N297A or LALAPG) to prevent Fc receptor interaction and minimize off-target immune activation (Figure 1B).15,16 Recombinant constructs were purified and characterized as previously described (supplemental Figure 7).17 Flow cytometry confirmed binding of the FOLR1SC-68:CD3OKT3 TCE to both FOLR1+ WSU-AML (Wayne State University-Acute Myeloid Leukemia) (CBFA2T3::GLIS2–positive) cells and CD3+ human activated T cells (ATCs; Figure 1C and D, respectively).

In vitro cocultures with ATCs and WSU-AML cells revealed dose-dependent tumor lysis only in the presence of the FOLR1SC-68:CD3OKT3 TCE but not with a FOLR1SC-68 monoclonal antibody (Figure 1E), demonstrating the need for both tumor target and effector T-cell engagement. Cytotoxicity assays also established that FOLR1SC-68:CD3OKT3 potently and selectively kills FOLR1+ WSU-AML cells with no cytotoxicity against FOLR1-knockout cells (Figure 1F). A comparison of ATCs vs peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) as effector cells showed >95% tumor cell killing by both after 5 days of coculture with the FOLR1SC-68:CD3OKT3 TCE (half-maximal effective concentration [EC50] = 0.51 pM and 0.07 pM, respectively; Figure 1G). Robust T-cell activation against WSU-AML cells was also observed, as T cells exhibited a dose-dependent upregulation of activation markers CD25, CD69, and PD-1 at 48 hours after TCE treatment (Figure 1H). Cytokine profiling demonstrated interferon gamma, interleukin-2, and tumor necrosis factor α release correlating with tumor cell killing, confirming antigen-specific effector activation (Figure 1I).

To evaluate in vivo antitumor efficacy, we employed 3 xenograft models representing distinct FOLR1-positive AML contexts: MV4;11FOLR1+; WSU-AML; and PDX37, a CBFA2T3::GLIS2–positive patient-derived model of relapsed AML (Figure 2). Immunodeficient NSG-SGM3 mice were engrafted IV and randomized by bioluminescent tumor burden upon establishment of disseminated leukemia (supplemental Figure 8). Animals received ATCs and IV doses of FOLR1SC-68:CD3OKT3 on a twice weekly schedule. Across all 3 models, treatment markedly suppressed tumor progression and significantly extended survival compared to T-cell alone controls. Dose titration studies in WSU-AML demonstrated potent activity at concentrations as low as 0.63 μg (0.003 nmol) per mouse (Figure 2E-F). The FOLR1SC-68:CD3OKT3 elimination half-life (t1/2) was 83.7 hours (supplemental Figure 9). The TCEs do not cross-react with either native murine target, but no animals demonstrated drug-related weight loss or other observed toxicities. Of note, we observed a consistent inverse dose response across all 3 models, suggesting saturation of binding sites leading to a hook effect at higher doses.18 Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) of WSU-AML posttreatment tissues confirmed complete clearance of CBFA2T3::GLIS2 transcripts in spleen and bone marrow of responders (Figure 2G). We also evaluated 2 additional T-cell donors and observed similar durable responses in WSU-AML (Figure 2H). Collectively, these results support FOLR1 as a selective and actionable target for immunotherapy in CBFA2T3::GLIS2 AML. The fully human IgG–based bispecific scaffold offers advantages over existing platforms, including scalable manufacturing, stability, and durable serum half-life while retaining potent cytotoxic function. Mechanistically, the FOLR1:CD3 engager mediates redirected T-cell killing independent of costimulation, underscoring its efficiency against chemoresistant leukemic cells. By disrupting Fc receptor and by targeting an antigen with minimal expression in noncancer tissue, off-target immune activation risks are minimized.

This strategy complements emerging FOLR1-targeted approaches such as chimeric antigen receptor T cells and antibody-drug conjugates.6,7 Chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapies directed against FOLR1 have shown early efficacy in preclinical pediatric AML but are limited by individualized manufacturing, delayed treatment logistics, and high costs.7 FOLR1-targeted antibody-drug conjugates like STRO-002 have demonstrated manageable toxicity and partial responses in pediatric CBFA2T3::GLIS2 AML, yet rely on intracellular trafficking for drug release and may exhibit limited durability.6,19 In contrast, the bispecific antibody platform presented here provides off-the-shelf delivery, consistent pharmacokinetics, and adjustable dosing, critical for rapid intervention in aggressive leukemias affecting infants and toddlers.

The highly specific binding pattern of our FOLR1SC-68 clone, combined with nonhuman primate cross-reactivity, supports its candidacy for translational evaluation. Low expression in normal pediatric tissues and strong binding to FOLR1+ AML reinforces the therapeutic window. Furthermore, the modularity of the IgG-scFv tetravalent framework allows further refinement, including incorporation of alternative CD3 binders or Fc engineering for half-life tuning. Such flexibility may enable future integration into next generation trispecifics or combination protocols with cytokine modulators or immune checkpoint inhibitors to enhance persistence and safety.

Although our findings demonstrate robust preclinical proof-of-concept activity, additional studies are warranted to define ideal TCE format and CD3 binder pairings, dosing schedules, pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic studies, and toxicology parameters. Mechanistic profiling of potential resistance pathways, such as antigen downregulation or impaired immune synapse formation, will help anticipate clinical challenges. Furthermore, the relationship of T-cell dysfunction observed during diagnosis/relapse and TCE resistance needs to be addressed, along with the role of cytokine release syndrome and T-cell exhaustion.20,21

Preclinical exploration of combination regimens involving cytokine blockade, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, costimulation, or low dose chemotherapy may extend therapeutic durability and mitigate cytokine-mediated effects.22

In summary, this study introduces a new, fully human, FOLR1-targeted bispecific T-cell engager with potent and selective activity against CBFA2T3::GLIS2–positive AML. The discovery leverages humanized transgenic antibody technology and rational engineering to achieve manufacturable, stable, and efficacious constructs. In vitro and in vivo data consistently demonstrate tumor eradication and survival benefit with minimal toxicity, establishing a solid foundation for future clinical translation. Given the dismal prognosis of CBFA2T3::GLIS2 AML and lack of effective immunotherapies, this platform represents a feasible off-the-shelf alternative capable of harnessing patient T cells to eliminate leukemic blasts.

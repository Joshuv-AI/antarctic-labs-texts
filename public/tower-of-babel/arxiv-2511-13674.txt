HilbMult: A Banach-Enriched Multicategory for Operator Algebras
Shih-Yu Chang

Abstract
Category and multicategory theory provide abstract frameworks for describing structures and their compositions, with multicategories extending traditional categories to handle multi-input operations. These theories enable modular reasoning and coherent composition of complex systems, and have found applications in computer science, physics, and mathematics, including programming language semantics, quantum processes, tensor networks, operads, and higher algebra. Operator theory, in contrast, studies linear and multilinear transformations in functional spaces, forming the analytic backbone of modern analysis and quantum mechanics, with applications ranging from signal processing and control theory to data science. This paper explores the synergy between these two areas by showing how operator theory provides concrete analytic structures that naturally enrich multicategories, while multicategory theory supplies a unifying framework for organizing multi-input operators and ensuring coherence in complex networks. We develop a comprehensive categorical framework integrating operator theory with multicategories, introducing a symmetric monoidal multicategory of Hilbert spaces with bounded multilinear maps and establishing its enrichment and coherence properties. The framework includes a functorial spectral theorem, covariance under unitary transformations, and universality as a semantic target, providing a unified language for linking analytic structure, categorical semantics, and operator representations. This work lays the foundation for a broader research program uniting operator theory, category theory, and noncommutative geometry.

Category and multicategory theory provide abstract frameworks for a unified synthetic description of structures and their compositions. Category theory focuses on morphisms between single-input objects while multicategory theory generalizes this to allow multi-input operations providing a natural language for algebraic, logical and arithmetic systems. Seriously These theories are widely used in computer science (e.g. type theory, programming language semantics and compositional artificial intelligence) in physics , physics (e.g. quantum processes and tensor networks) and in mathematics (e.g. operads, higher algebra, and homotopy theory), where they enable modular reasoning and the coherent composition of complex systems

Its applications span a wide range, including quantum physics, signal processing, control theory, and data science. Through spectral theory, operator algebra, and semigroup methods, it provides tools for solving differential equations, modeling dynamic systems, and analyzing large-dimensional data. Below, we consider the potential impacts of synergizing these two seemingly distant areas of mathematics:

Operator Theory
\Rightarrow
Multicategory Theory.

Operator theory provides analytic structure and concrete examples that naturally enrich multicategories.
In the setting of Banach or Hilbert spaces, bounded multilinear operators

T:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}\to H
serve as canonical multimorphisms, turning the collection of Hilbert spaces into a multicategory. Operator-theoretic notions such as positivity, norms, and complete boundedness induce Banach-enriched multicategories, where hom-sets carry analytic structure. Furthermore,
C^{*}
-algebras can be viewed as algebras over these multicategories, organizing their multiplication and
*
-operations coherently. Even functional calculus fits into this picture, as applying a polynomial or continuous function to several commuting operators defines a multimorphism consistent with the spectral theorem. Thus, operator theory enriches multicategories with analytic semantics, grounding their composition in operator algebraic data.

Multicategory Theory
\Rightarrow
Operator Theory.

Conversely, multicategory theory provides a unifying framework for organizing multi-input operators and networks of compositions. It generalizes binary operator composition into a coherent system of multi-ary interactions, ideal for modeling tensor contractions, quantum channels, or PDE solution operators. Multicategories impose coherence and associativity on multilinear compositions, avoiding analytic ambiguities. They also enable homotopy transfer of operator structures across equivalent models, and extend the notion of duality or adjunction to multi-input contexts. In this direction, multicategories supply operator theory with syntactic clarity, compositional modularity, and higher-level coherence principles—
transforming analytic constructions into structured, composable systems.

This paper establishes a comprehensive categorical framework that integrates operator theory with multicategory theory through Banach-enriched structures. We introduce

Definitions and Axioms

Let
\mathbf{Hilb}
denote the category of complex Hilbert spaces with bounded linear maps. We introduce
\mathbf{HilbMult}
as a symmetric monoidal multicategory enriched over Banach spaces. We first present the fundamental definitions, followed by the core axioms. For completeness and self-contained clarity, this section material is also reproduced from our recent work

.

2.1
Core Definitions (Briefly)

Definition 1

(Multicategory)
.

A multicategory consists of:

•

A collection of objects.

•

For each finite list of objects
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
and object
Y
, a set
\mathrm{Hom}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n};Y)
of multimorphisms.

•

Composition operations satisfying associativity.

•

Identity morphisms for each object.

Definition 2

(Banach Enrichment)
.

A category/multicategory is enriched in Banach spaces if each hom-set
\mathrm{Hom}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n};Y)
is a Banach space and composition is a bounded multilinear map.

Definition 3

(Symmetric Monoidal Multicategory)
.

A symmetric monoidal multicategory has:

•

A tensor product
\otimes
on objects.

•

Natural isomorphisms for associativity, unit, and symmetry.

•

Compatibility between tensor product and multimorphisms.

2.2
Core Axioms

Axiom 1

(Objects and Hom-Spaces)
.

(a)

Objects:
The objects of
\mathbf{HilbMult}
are (separable) complex Hilbert spaces
H,K,\dots
.

(b)

Hom-Spaces:
For each finite tuple
(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
, there is a Banach space:

\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)

whose elements are (equivalence classes of) bounded multilinear maps
T:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}\to K
. In the unary case (
n=1
), these are bounded linear maps
H\to K
.

(c)

Norm:
The Banach norm is the operator norm:

\|T\|:=\sup_{\|x_{i}\|\leq 1}\|T(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|

Axiom 2

(Composition and Identities)
.

(a)

Multilinear Composition:
For composable multimorphisms:

\displaystyle S\in\mathrm{Hom}(K_{1},\dots,K_{m};L),

\displaystyle T_{j}\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{j,1},\dots,H_{j,n_{j}};K_{j})\quad(1\leq j\leq m),

there is a composition
S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1,1},\dots,H_{m,n_{m}};L)
given by:

(S\circ(T_{j}))(\mathbf{x}):=S(T_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\dots,T_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m}))

Composition is multilinear and contractive:

\|S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})\|\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|

(b)

Identities:
For each object
H
, there is an identity
\mathrm{id}_{H}\in\mathrm{Hom}(H;H)
given by
\mathrm{id}_{H}(x)=x
. These satisfy:

T\circ(\mathrm{id}_{H_{1}},\dots,\mathrm{id}_{H_{n}})=T

for any
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
.

Monoidal Structure

Axiom 3

(Symmetric Monoidal Structure)
.

\mathbf{HilbMult}
has a symmetric monoidal structure:

(a)

Tensor Product:

•

On objects:
H\otimes K
is the completed Hilbert space tensor product.

•

Unit object:
\mathbb{C}
(complex numbers as 1D Hilbert space).

•

On multimorphisms: For
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
and
T^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hom}(H^{\prime}_{1},\dots,H^{\prime}_{p};K^{\prime})
, their tensor product is:

T\otimes T^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n},H^{\prime}_{1},\dots,H^{\prime}_{p};K\otimes K^{\prime})

defined by
(T\otimes T^{\prime})(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=T(\mathbf{x})\otimes T^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
.

(b)

Structural Isomorphisms:
Natural unitary isomorphisms:

Associator:

\displaystyle\alpha_{H,K,L}:(H\otimes K)\otimes L\xrightarrow{\cong}H\otimes(K\otimes L)

Braiding:

\displaystyle\sigma_{H,K}:H\otimes K\xrightarrow{\cong}K\otimes H

Unitors:

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}:\mathbb{C}\otimes H\xrightarrow{\cong}H,\quad\rho_{H}:H\otimes\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\cong}H

satisfying the coherence conditions (pentagon, hexagon, triangle identities).

(c)

Permutation Action:
For
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
and
\pi\in S_{n}
:

T^{\pi}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=T(x_{\pi(1)},\dots,x_{\pi(n)})

All structural isomorphisms are isometric.

Advanced Structure

Axiom 4

(Closed Structure / Currying)
.

\mathbf{HilbMult}
is closed under currying operations with natural isometric isomorphisms:

(a)

Basic Currying:

\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1};\mathrm{Hom}(H_{2},\dots,H_{n};K))

via
T\mapsto\Lambda_{1}(T)
where
\Lambda_{1}(T)(x_{1})(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=T(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
.

(b)

Tensor-Curry Duality:

\mathrm{Hom}(H\otimes K;L)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(H;\mathrm{Hom}(K;L))

via
F\mapsto\Lambda(F)
where
\Lambda(F)(x)(y)=F(x\otimes y)
.

(c)

Partial Currying:
For any
1\leq j\leq n
:

\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{j-1};\mathrm{Hom}(H_{j},\dots,H_{n};K))

All isomorphisms preserve norms and are compatible with composition.

Axiom 5

(Optional
C^{*}
-Structure)
.

We may optionally endow endomorphism spaces with
C^{*}
-algebra structure:

(a)

Involution:
For each
H
, the space
\mathcal{B}(H)=\mathrm{Hom}(H;H)
has a conjugate-linear involution
*
satisfying:

\displaystyle(f^{*})^{*}

\displaystyle=f,\quad(f\circ g)^{*}=g^{*}\circ f^{*},\quad(\lambda f)^{*}=\bar{\lambda}f^{*}

(b)

C^{*}
-Property:
The norm satisfies:

\|f^{*}\circ f\|=\|f\|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\circ g\|\leq\|f\|\|g\|

(c)

Monoidal Compatibility:
For
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1};H_{2})
,
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(K_{1};K_{2})

(f\otimes g)^{*}=f^{*}\otimes g^{*}

This enables the definition of:

•

Self-adjoint elements:

•

Normal elements:

f^{*}\circ f=f\circ f^{*}

•

Positive elements:

f=g^{*}\circ g
for some
g

Essential for quantum theory (states, observables, measurements).

2.3
Immediate Lemmas

Lemma 1

(Norm Completeness)
.

For any tuple
(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
, the hom-space
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
is a Banach space (complete in the operator norm).

Proof.

Let
\{T_{m}\}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
. For any fixed vectors
x_{1}\in H_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in H_{n}
with
\|x_{i}\|\leq 1
, the sequence
\{T_{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\}
is Cauchy in
K
because:

\|T_{m}(\mathbf{x})-T_{k}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\|T_{m}-T_{k}\|\cdot\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{n}\|\leq\|T_{m}-T_{k}\|

Since
K
is a Hilbert space (hence complete),
\{T_{m}(\mathbf{x})\}
converges to some element in
K
, which we denote by
T(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

This pointwise convergence defines a multilinear map
T:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}\to K
because:

•

Each
T_{m}
is multilinear, and multilinearity is preserved under pointwise limits

•

The convergence is uniform on the unit ball, ensuring boundedness

To show
T
is bounded, note that for sufficiently large
m
:

\|T_{m}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\|T_{m}\|\cdot\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{n}\|\leq M\quad\text{for some }M>0

Hence
\|T(\mathbf{x})\|\leq M
for all unit vectors, so
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
.

Finally, for any
\epsilon>0
, choose
N
such that
\|T_{m}-T_{k}\|<\epsilon
for all
m,k\geq N
. Then for all unit vectors
\mathbf{x}
:

\|T_{m}(\mathbf{x})-T(\mathbf{x})\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|T_{m}(\mathbf{x})-T_{k}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\epsilon

Taking supremum gives
\|T_{m}-T\|\leq\epsilon
for
m\geq N
, so
T_{m}\to T
in norm.
∎

Lemma 2

(Composition Contractivity)
.

For composable multimorphisms
S\in\operatorname{Hom}(K_{1},\ldots,K_{m};L)
and
T_{j}\in\operatorname{Hom}(H_{j,1},\ldots,H_{j,n_{j}};K_{j})
:

\|S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})\|\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|

Proof.

Let
x_{j,i}\in H_{j,i}
with
\|x_{j,i}\|\leq 1
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
and
i=1,\ldots,n_{j}
. Then:

\displaystyle\|(S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m}))(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{m,n_{m}})\|

\displaystyle=\|S(T_{1}(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,T_{m}(x_{m,1},\dots,x_{m,n_{m}}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}(x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,n_{j}})\|\quad\text{(by definition of $\|S\|$)}

\displaystyle\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\|T_{j}\|\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\|x_{j,i}\|\right)\quad\text{(by definition of $\|T_{j}\|$)}

\displaystyle\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|\quad\text{(since $\|x_{j,i}\|\leq 1$)}

Taking the supremum over all such input vectors with
\|x_{j,i}\|\leq 1
gives the desired inequality.
∎

Lemma 3

(Tensor Product Isometry)
.

Let
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
and
T^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hom}(H^{\prime}_{1},\dots,H^{\prime}_{p};K^{\prime})
be multilinear maps between Hilbert spaces as in Axiom
3
(a). Then:

\|T\otimes T^{\prime}\|=\|T\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}\|,

where the norms are the operator norms for multilinear maps.

Proof.

We establish the equality by proving both inequalities.

Upper bound (
\|T\otimes T^{\prime}\|\leq\|T\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}\|
):

Let
x_{i}\in H_{i}
and
x^{\prime}_{j}\in H^{\prime}_{j}
be unit vectors for
i=1,\dots,n
and
j=1,\dots,p
. Then:

\displaystyle\|(T\otimes T^{\prime})(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{p})\|

\displaystyle=\|T(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\otimes T^{\prime}(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{p})\|

\displaystyle=\|T(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{p})\|\quad\text{(by Hilbert tensor product properties)}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}\|.

Since the tensor product
T\otimes T^{\prime}
is defined on pure tensors and extends by multilinearity, taking the supremum over all unit vectors gives
\|T\otimes T^{\prime}\|\leq\|T\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}\|
.

Lower bound (
\|T\otimes T^{\prime}\|\geq\|T\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}\|
):

Let
\epsilon>0
. By definition of the operator norm, there exist unit vectors
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{p})
such that:

\|T(\mathbf{x})\|\geq\|T\|-\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\|T^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\|\geq\|T^{\prime}\|-\epsilon.

Then:

\displaystyle\|T\otimes T^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle\geq\|(T\otimes T^{\prime})(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle=\|T(\mathbf{x})\otimes T^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle=\|T(\mathbf{x})\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\geq(\|T\|-\epsilon)(\|T^{\prime}\|-\epsilon).

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\|T\otimes T^{\prime}\|\geq\|T\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}\|
.

Combining both inequalities yields
\|T\otimes T^{\prime}\|=\|T\|\cdot\|T^{\prime}\|
.
∎

3
Banach Enrichment

Before establishing its more advanced structures, we must first verify that
\mathbf{HilbMult}
satisfies the fundamental requirements of a multicategory. The following Theorem
1
confirms its basic enriched categorical structure, providing the necessary analytic foundation.

Theorem 1

(Banach Multicategory Structure)
.

The multicategory
\mathbf{HilbMult}
satisfies:

(i)

For every tuple
(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
, the hom-space
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
is a Banach space.

(ii)

Composition is multilinear and contractive:

\|S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})\|\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|

for all composable multimorphisms
S,T_{1},\dots,T_{m}
.

(iii)

Composition is jointly continuous in the Banach norms.

Proof.

We prove each part systematically.

Part (i): Banach space structure.

By Axiom
1
(b), each
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
is equipped with the operator norm:

\|T\|:=\sup\{\|T(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|:\|x_{i}\|\leq 1\text{ for }i=1,\dots,n\}.

•

Vector space structure
: The set of bounded multilinear maps forms a complex vector space under pointwise operations. Linear combinations of bounded multilinear maps remain multilinear, and boundedness follows from the triangle inequality.

•

Norm axioms
: The operator norm satisfies:

–

\|T\|\geq 0
and
\|T\|=0
if and only if
T=0
(by Axiom
1
(b))

–

\|\lambda T\|=|\lambda|\|T\|
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
(homogeneity)

–

\|T+U\|\leq\|T\|+\|U\|
(triangle inequality)

•

Completeness
: Let
\{T_{k}\}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
. For fixed unit vectors
x_{1}\in H_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in H_{n}
, the sequence
\{T_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\}
is Cauchy in
K
because:

\|T_{k}(\mathbf{x})-T_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\|T_{k}-T_{\ell}\|\cdot\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{n}\|=\|T_{k}-T_{\ell}\|.

Since
K
is a Hilbert space (hence complete),
\{T_{k}(\mathbf{x})\}
converges to some
T(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in K
.

The limit map
T:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}\to K
is multilinear because each
T_{k}
is multilinear and multilinearity is preserved under pointwise convergence. To show
T
is bounded, note that Cauchy sequences are bounded, so
\|T_{k}\|\leq M
for some
M>0
and all
k
. Then:

\|T(\mathbf{x})\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|T_{k}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq M\cdot\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{n}\|,

hence
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
.

Finally, for any
\epsilon>0
, choose
N
such that
\|T_{k}-T_{\ell}\|<\epsilon
for all
k,\ell\geq N
. Then for all unit vectors
\mathbf{x}
and
k\geq N
:

\|T_{k}(\mathbf{x})-T(\mathbf{x})\|=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\|T_{k}(\mathbf{x})-T_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\epsilon.

Taking supremum gives
\|T_{k}-T\|\leq\epsilon
for
k\geq N
, so
T_{k}\to T
in norm.

Part (ii): Multilinearity and contractivity.

By Axiom
2
(a), composition is defined as:

(S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m}))(\mathbf{x})=S(T_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\dots,T_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m})).

•

Multilinearity
: Composition is multilinear in each argument
T_{j}
because for fixed

T_{1},\dots,T_{j-1},T_{j+1},\dots,T_{m}
, the map

T_{j}\mapsto S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{j},\dots,T_{m})

is linear. This follows from the linearity of
S
in its
j
-th argument and the vector space structure from Part (i).

•

Contractivity
: For any unit vectors
x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,n_{j}}
(i.e.,
\|x_{j,i}\|\leq 1
):

\displaystyle\quad\|(S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m}))(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{m,n_{m}})\|

\displaystyle=\|S(T_{1}(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,T_{m}(x_{m,1},\dots,x_{m,n_{m}}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}(x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,n_{j}})\|\quad\text{(by definition of $\|S\|$)}

\displaystyle\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|\quad\text{(since $\|T_{j}(\mathbf{x}_{j})\|\leq\|T_{j}\|$)}.

Taking supremum over all such unit vectors gives:

\|S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})\|\leq\|S\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|.

Part (iii): Joint continuity.

The contractivity inequality implies joint continuity. For any
\epsilon>0
, choose
\delta>0
such that:

\delta\cdot\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|+\|S\|\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{m}\prod_{k\neq j}\|T_{k}\|\right)<\epsilon.

Then if
\|S-S^{\prime}\|<\delta
and
\|T_{j}-T_{j}^{\prime}\|<\delta
for all
j
, we have:

\displaystyle\quad\|S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})-S^{\prime}\circ(T_{1}^{\prime},\dots,T_{m}^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})-S^{\prime}\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|S^{\prime}\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})-S^{\prime}\circ(T_{1}^{\prime},\dots,T_{m}^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|S-S^{\prime}\|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|+\|S^{\prime}\|\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}-T_{j}^{\prime}\|\cdot\prod_{k\neq j}\|T_{k}\|

\displaystyle<\epsilon.

This establishes joint continuity.
∎

Representability and Adjoints

The interplay between representability and adjoint operations forms a cornerstone of enriched category theory. In this section, we establish two fundamental results that connect these concepts in the multicategorical setting. First, Theorem
2
demonstrates how representability of hom-functors naturally gives rise to currying operations in any symmetric multicategory, providing a universal factorization property for multimorphisms. Building upon this abstract foundation, Theorem
3
provides the properties of existence of adjoints/mates in
\mathbf{HilbMult}
. These results reveal how the representability framework systematically generates adjoint-like operations, while the concrete Hilbert space structure ensures these operations satisfy the expected analytic and algebraic properties, including norm preservation, compositionality, and tensor compatibility.

Theorem 2

(Representability Yields Currying)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a symmetric multicategory, and let
T\in

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
be a multimorphism.
Fix an index
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and consider the functor

\Phi_{i}(X)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(H_{1},\dots,H_{i-1},X,H_{i+1},\dots,H_{n};K)

that sends an object
X
to multimorphisms with
X
in the
i
-th slot.

If
\Phi_{i}
is
representable
—meaning there exists an object
H_{i}^{\vee}
and universal morphism

u_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(H_{1},\dots,H_{i-1},H_{i}^{\vee},H_{i+1},\dots,H_{n};K)

such that for every
S\in\Phi_{i}(X)

5
Functorial Spectral Theorem

The main purpose of this section is to establish functional spectrum theory and related implications. Theorem
4
provides a categorical interpretation of the spectral theorem, showing that the continuous functional computation of a self-adjacent operator can be extended to a full-fledged symmetric monoidal multifunctor. This brings us two benefits. First, we have a correspondence
f\mapsto f(A)
for single functions. Second, the entire multilinear algebraic structure of continuous functions can be lifted to the operator level in a coherent, norm-preserving manner. Essentially, the operator
A
generates a complete “calculus of operations” that respects composition, tensor products, and symmetry.

n\geq 1
.

Then there exists a unique Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multifunctor

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))\longrightarrow\mathbf{HilbMult}

characterized by:

(i)

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\ast)=H
.

(ii)

On linear morphisms (
n=1
),
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
coincides with the continuous functional calculus.

(iii)

For
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))}(\ast^{n};\ast)
represented by polynomial

P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}X_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{k_{n}},

the multimorphism
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi):H^{n}\to H
is given by:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\,T_{n}(A^{k_{1}}x_{1},A^{k_{2}}x_{2},\dots,A^{k_{n}}x_{n}),

where the sum is finite and extends uniquely to all of
H^{n}
by boundedness.

(iv)

Functoriality:
For composable multimorphisms
\varphi,\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}
:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi\circ(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}))=\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)\circ(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\psi_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\psi_{m})).

Moreover, when
\mathcal{T}
is the family of pointwise multiplication maps:

T_{n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)\cdots f_{n}(x)\quad\text{for }f_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\sigma(A),\mu),

the construction recovers the original pointwise polynomial action.

Proof.

We construct the multifunctor
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
explicitly and verify its properties.

Step 1: Construction on objects.

Define
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\ast)=H
as required. For finite lists
(\ast,\dots,\ast)
, define:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\ast,\dots,\ast)=(H,\dots,H)

with the natural tensor product structure inherited from
\mathbf{HilbMult}
.

Step 2: Construction on multimorphisms.

For
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))}(\ast^{n};\ast)
represented by polynomial:

P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}X_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{k_{n}},

define
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi):H^{n}\to H
by:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\,T_{n}(A^{k_{1}}x_{1},A^{k_{2}}x_{2},\dots,A^{k_{n}}x_{n}).

Step 3: Verification of boundedness and well-definedness.

Since
P
is a polynomial, the sum is finite. Boundedness follows from:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}|c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}|\cdot\|T_{n}(A^{k_{1}}x_{1},\dots,A^{k_{n}}x_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}|c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}|\cdot\|T_{n}\|\cdot\|A^{k_{1}}x_{1}\|\cdots\|A^{k_{n}}x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}|c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}|\cdot\|T_{n}\|\cdot\|A\|^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}}\right)\cdot\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{n}\|.

The finite sum and uniform boundedness
\|T_{n}\|<\infty
ensure
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)
is bounded.

Step 4: Verification of functional calculus compatibility.

For
n=1
and
\varphi(f)=P(f)
, we have:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}T_{1}(A^{k}x).

By polynomial compatibility (b) with
n=1
and
p_{1}(X)=X^{k}
:

T_{1}(A^{k}x)=A^{k}T_{1}(x).

Thus:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}A^{k}T_{1}(x)=P(A)T_{1}(x).

To show
T_{1}=\mathrm{id}
, use spectral locality: for
f\in L^{\infty}(\sigma(A),\mu)
:

(T_{1}(f))(x)=\tau_{1}(x,f(x))\quad\mu\text{-a.e.}

Polynomial compatibility with
p_{1}(X)=X
gives
T_{1}(Af)=AT_{1}(f)
, so in spectral representation:

\tau_{1}(x,\lambda f(x))=\lambda\tau_{1}(x,f(x))\quad\mu\text{-a.e.}

This implies
\tau_{1}(x,\cdot)
is linear:
\tau_{1}(x,z)=\alpha(x)z
. For constant polynomials
p_{1}(X)=c
, compatibility gives:

T_{1}(cf)=cT_{1}(f)\Rightarrow\tau_{1}(x,cf(x))=c\tau_{1}(x,f(x)),

which is automatically satisfied. However, taking
f\equiv 1
and using that
T_{1}(1)
should behave like the identity under the functional calculus forces
\alpha(x)=1

\mu
-a.e. Thus
T_{1}(f)=f
.

Step 5: Verification of explicit formula.

This holds by construction from Step 2.

Step 6: Verification of functoriality.

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}(\ast^{m};\ast)
represented by polynomial
P
, and
\psi_{j}\in\operatorname{Hom}(\ast^{n_{j}};\ast)
represented by polynomials
Q_{j}
. Their composition
\varphi\circ(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m})
is represented by the polynomial:

R(X_{1,1},\dots,X_{m,n_{m}})=P(Q_{1}(X_{1,1},\dots,X_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,Q_{m}(X_{m,1},\dots,X_{m,n_{m}})).

We must verify:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(R)(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{m})=\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P)\left(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(Q_{1})(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(Q_{m})(\mathbf{x}_{m})\right).

Expanding the left side:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(R)(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{m})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\,T_{N}(A^{k_{1,1}}x_{1,1},\dots,A^{k_{m,n_{m}}}x_{m,n_{m}}),

where
N=\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}
and
\mathbf{k}
ranges over appropriate multi-indices.

For the right side, let
y_{j}=\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(Q_{j})(\mathbf{x}_{j})=\sum_{\mathbf{l}_{j}}d^{(j)}_{\mathbf{l}_{j}}\,T_{n_{j}}(A^{l_{j,1}}x_{j,1},\dots,A^{l_{j,n_{j}}}x_{j,n_{j}})
. Then:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P)\left(y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\,T_{m}\left(A^{k_{1}}y_{1},\dots,A^{k_{m}}y_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{l}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{l}_{m}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}d^{(j)}_{\mathbf{l}_{j}}\,T_{m}\left(T_{n_{1}}(A^{k_{1}+l_{1,1}}x_{1,1},\dots,A^{k_{1}+l_{1,n_{1}}}x_{1,n_{1}}),\dots\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{l}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{l}_{m}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}d^{(j)}_{\mathbf{l}_{j}}\,T_{N}(A^{k_{1}+l_{1,1}}x_{1,1},\dots,A^{k_{m}+l_{m,n_{m}}}x_{m,n_{m}})\quad\text{(by polynomial compatibility)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(R)(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{m}).

The final equality follows by reindexing the sum, since the coefficients
c_{\mathbf{k}}\prod_{j}d^{(j)}_{\mathbf{l}_{j}}
are exactly the coefficients of the composed polynomial
R
.

Step 7: Verification of Banach enrichment.

Boundedness was established in Step 3. The enrichment conditions follow from linearity and the uniform boundedness of
T_{n}
.

Step 8: Verification of symmetric monoidal structure.

The construction is natural in input arguments and commutes with permutations, following from polynomial symmetry and the symmetric multicategory structure.

Step 9: Uniqueness.

Any multifunctor satisfying (i)–(iii) must agree with our construction on polynomials by (iii), and multifunctoriality with Banach enrichment determines the unique extension.
∎

Remark 5
.

The key insight is that the polynomial multicategory
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))
captures the algebraic structure of polynomial operations, while the functional calculus combined with the family
\mathcal{T}
provides the analytic realization. The multifunctor
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
bridges these worlds by:

•

Sending the abstract object
\ast
to the concrete Hilbert space
H

•

Translating polynomial operations into operator actions via the spectral theorem and the chosen multilinear operations
\mathcal{T}

•

Preserving the compositional structure of multicategories through the polynomial compatibility condition

•

Respecting the analytic bounds through Banach enrichment and uniform boundedness

This theorem provides a categorical framework for understanding how polynomial functional calculus naturally extends to multivariate settings with arbitrary spectral-local operations.

Several corollaries are derived based on Theorem
4
.

Corollary 1

(Universal Polynomial Functional Calculus)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
A\in\mathcal{B}(H)

4

acting on

4

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{poly}}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))\longrightarrow\mathbf{HilbMult}

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(n)\times C(\sigma(A))^{n}}

.

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))

A
in a Banach-enriched multicategory
\mathcal{M}

Proof.

The verification that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{poly}}

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{poly}}}
precisely implements the polynomial operad action from the definition:

from Definition

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{poly}}}(\varphi_{P})(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})(x)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{P}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}))(x)\quad\text{(by Definition\penalty\ \ref{def:poly multicat})}.

: for

P

\psi_{j}

Remark 6
.

This corollary shows that Definition
4

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{poly}}

Corollary 2

(Normal Operator Case)
.

If
A\in\mathcal{B}(H)
is normal (
AA^{*}=A^{*}A
), then Theorem
4
holds with
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))
where
\sigma(A)\subset\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
is defined analogously using the continuous functional calculus for normal operators and any family
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfying the spectral locality, polynomial compatibility, and uniform boundedness conditions.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of Theorem
4
to the normal operator case.

Step 1: Spectral theorem for normal operators.

By the spectral theorem for normal operators, there exists a spectral measure
E
on
\sigma(A)\subset\mathbb{C}
and a unitary isomorphism:

U:H\to L^{2}(\sigma(A),\mu)

for some measure
\mu
equivalent to
E
, such that:

UAU^{*}=M_{z},

where
M_{z}
is multiplication by the coordinate function
z\mapsto z
on
\sigma(A)
.

Step 2: Construction of
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
.

Define
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\ast)=H
as before. For a multimorphism
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))}(\ast^{n};\ast)
represented by a polynomial in
n
complex variables:

P(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}},

define
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi):H^{n}\to H
by:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\,T_{n}(A^{k_{1}}x_{1},A^{k_{2}}x_{2},\dots,A^{k_{n}}x_{n}),

where the family
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{n}\}
satisfies the same conditions (a)–(c) as in Theorem
4
, but now with
\sigma(A)\subset\mathbb{C}
.

Step 3: Verification of boundedness.

The boundedness argument proceeds identically to the self-adjoint case. Since
P
is a polynomial and each
T_{n}
is bounded, we have:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}|c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}|\cdot\|T_{n}\|\cdot\|A^{k_{1}}x_{1}\|\cdots\|A^{k_{n}}x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0}|c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}|\cdot\|T_{n}\|\cdot\|A\|^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}}\cdot\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{n}\|.

The finite sum ensures boundedness.

Step 4: Functional calculus compatibility.

For
n=1
, if
\varphi(f)=P(f)
is a polynomial in one variable, then:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}T_{1}(A^{k}x).

By the polynomial compatibility condition,
T_{1}(A^{k}x)=A^{k}T_{1}(x)
, so:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}A^{k}T_{1}(x)=P(A)T_{1}(x).

As in the self-adjoint case, the spectral locality and polynomial compatibility conditions force
T_{1}
to act as the identity on the appropriate domain, ensuring compatibility with the continuous functional calculus for normal operators.

Step 5: Verification of functoriality.

The proof of functoriality proceeds identically to the self-adjoint case. For composable multimorphisms
\varphi,\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}
represented by polynomials
P,Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m}
, the polynomial compatibility condition ensures that:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi\circ(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}))=\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)\circ(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\psi_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\psi_{m})).

The key observation is that polynomial composition works identically for complex polynomials, and the polynomial compatibility condition is formulated in terms of the functional calculus, which works for normal operators.

Step 6: Symmetric monoidal structure and Banach enrichment.

These properties follow from the same arguments as in the self-adjoint case, as they depend on the multilinear structure and boundedness conditions rather than the specific nature of the operator’s spectrum.

Step 7: Uniqueness.

Any multifunctor satisfying the analogous conditions must agree with our construction on polynomials. By the density of polynomials in the continuous functions on
\sigma(A)\subset\mathbb{C}
(via the Stone-Weierstrass theorem for complex polynomials on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
) and the multifunctoriality condition, the extension is unique.

Step 8: Recovery of pointwise multiplication.

When
\mathcal{T}
is the family of pointwise multiplication maps:

T_{n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})(z)=f_{1}(z)f_{2}(z)\cdots f_{n}(z)\quad\text{for }f_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\sigma(A),\mu),

the construction reduces to:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})(z)=P(z,\dots,z)\cdot f_{1}(z)f_{2}(z)\cdots f_{n}(z)\quad\text{for }z\in\sigma(A),

recovering the pointwise polynomial action for normal operators.
∎

Remark 7
.

The extension to normal operators is natural because the spectral theorem provides a functional calculus for continuous functions on
\sigma(A)\subset\mathbb{C}
, and the polynomial compatibility condition is formulated in terms of this functional calculus. The key difference from the self-adjoint case is that we now work with complex polynomials in multiple variables, but the algebraic structure of polynomial composition remains the same.

Corollary 3

(Unbounded Self-Adjoint Case)
.

Let
A
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
H
. Then for any family
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of bounded
n
-linear maps
T_{n}:H^{n}\to H
satisfying the following adapted conditions:

(a)

Spectral Locality:
In the spectral representation
H\cong L^{2}(\sigma(A),\mu)
, for
f_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\sigma(A),\mu)
:

(T_{n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}))(x)=\tau_{n}(x,f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{n}(x))\quad\mu\text{-a.e.}

(b)

Polynomial Compatibility:
For polynomials
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
and
f_{j}\in H\cap L^{\infty}(\sigma(A),\mu)
:

T_{n}(p_{1}(A)f_{1},\dots,p_{n}(A)f_{n})=(p_{1}\cdots p_{n})(A)T_{n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})

(c)

Uniform Boundedness:

\|T_{n}\|<\infty
for all
n\geq 1

there exists a Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multifunctor

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{B}\longrightarrow\mathbf{HilbMult}

where
\mathcal{B}
is the multicategory of bounded Borel functions on
\mathbb{R}
, satisfying analogous conditions to Theorem
4
.

Proof.

We extend the construction from Theorem
4
using the spectral theorem for unbounded operators.

Step 1: Spectral representation.

By the spectral theorem for unbounded self-adjoint operators, there exists a unique projection-valued measure
E
on
\mathbb{R}
such that:

A=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\,dE(\lambda),

with domain
\mathcal{D}(A)=\{x\in H:\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda^{2}\,d\langle E(\lambda)x,x\rangle<\infty\}
. For any bounded Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, we define:

f(A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\,dE(\lambda)\in\mathcal{B}(H).

Step 2: Multicategory
\mathcal{B}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the symmetric monoidal multicategory where:

•

Objects are copies of a single object
\ast

•

Multimorphisms
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\ast^{n};\ast)
are bounded Borel functions
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}

•

Composition is given by function composition: for
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\psi_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}\to\mathbb{C}
,

(\varphi\circ(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}))(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{m})=\varphi(\psi_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\dots,\psi_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m}))

Step 3: Construction of
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
.

Define:

•

On objects:
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\ast)=H
, and
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\ast,\dots,\ast)=(H,\dots,H)

•

On multimorphisms: For
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\ast^{n};\ast)
, define
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi):H^{n}\to H
by:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=T_{n}(\varphi_{1}(A)x_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}(A)x_{n})

where
\varphi_{j}(\lambda)=\varphi(\lambda,\dots,\lambda)
is the diagonal restriction (all arguments equal).

Step 4: Boundedness verification.

Since
\varphi
is bounded and
T_{n}
is bounded multilinear:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|

\displaystyle=\|T_{n}(\varphi_{1}(A)x_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}(A)x_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{n}\|\cdot\|\varphi_{1}(A)x_{1}\|\cdots\|\varphi_{n}(A)x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{n}\|\cdot\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}\cdots\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{n}\|\cdot\|\varphi\|_{\infty}^{n}\cdot\|x_{1}\|\cdots\|x_{n}\|.

Thus
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)
is bounded multilinear.

Step 5: Functional calculus compatibility.

For
n=1
and
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\ast;\ast)
, we have:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x)=T_{1}(\varphi(A)x).

To show this equals
\varphi(A)x
, first consider polynomial approximations. Let
P_{k}
be polynomials converging uniformly to
\varphi
on
\sigma(A)
. Then:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P_{k})(x)=T_{1}(P_{k}(A)x).

By polynomial compatibility (b) with
n=1
, we have
T_{1}(P_{k}(A)x)=P_{k}(A)T_{1}(x)
. Taking limits:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}T_{1}(P_{k}(A)x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}P_{k}(A)T_{1}(x)=\varphi(A)T_{1}(x).

To show
T_{1}=\mathrm{id}
, use spectral locality: for
f\in L^{\infty}(\sigma(A),\mu)
:

(T_{1}(f))(x)=\tau_{1}(x,f(x))\quad\mu\text{-a.e.}

Polynomial compatibility with
p_{1}(X)=X
gives
T_{1}(Af)=AT_{1}(f)
, so in spectral representation:

\tau_{1}(x,\lambda f(x))=\lambda\tau_{1}(x,f(x))\quad\mu\text{-a.e.}

This implies
\tau_{1}(x,\cdot)
is linear:
\tau_{1}(x,z)=\alpha(x)z
. For constant functions, polynomial compatibility forces
\alpha(x)=1

\mu
-a.e., hence
T_{1}=\mathrm{id}
.

Step 6: Polynomial case recovery.

When
\varphi(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is a polynomial, we recover:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=T_{n}(P_{1}(A)x_{1},\dots,P_{n}(A)x_{n}),

where
P_{j}(\lambda)=P(\lambda,\dots,\lambda)
. For the standard polynomial action, this coincides with the bounded case construction.

Step 7: Functoriality verification.

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\ast^{m};\ast)
and
\psi_{j}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\ast^{n_{j}};\ast)
. We need:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi\circ(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}))=\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)\circ(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\psi_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\psi_{m})).

The left side is:

The right side is:

These coincide by the polynomial compatibility condition extended to Borel functions via uniform approximation by polynomials and the continuity of the functional calculus.

Step 8: Symmetric monoidal structure.

The construction respects the symmetric monoidal structure:

The unit object maps to the base field
\mathbb{C}

Step 9: Banach enrichment.

The uniform bound:

Step 10: Pointwise multiplication case.

When

∎

Corollary 5

(Functorial Extension of the Continuous Functional Calculus)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
A\in\mathcal{B}(H)
a bounded self-adjoint operator with spectrum
\sigma(A)\subset\mathbb{R}
. Let
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a family satisfying the conditions of Theorem
4
, and let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function.

Then the Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multifunctor
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
from Theorem
4
satisfies:

(i)

The action on continuous functions coincides with the continuous functional calculus:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(f)=f(A).

(ii)

The spectrum satisfies the spectral mapping property:

\sigma(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(f))=f(\sigma(A)).

Proof.

We prove the two parts systematically.

Proof of (i): Extension to Continuous Functional Calculus

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous. By the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, there exists a sequence of polynomials
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging uniformly to
f
on the compact set
\sigma(A)
.

Consider the sequence
\{\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{B}(H)
. By condition (ii) of Theorem
4
, for polynomials we have:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P_{n})=P_{n}(A),

where
P_{n}(A)
denotes the continuous functional calculus.

Since
P_{n}\to f
uniformly on
\sigma(A)
, the continuity of the functional calculus implies:

P_{n}(A)\to f(A)\quad\text{in the operator norm}.

Now, since
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
is Banach-enriched, the map

\varphi\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)

is continuous from
C(\sigma(A))
(with supremum norm) to
\mathcal{B}(H)
(with operator norm). Therefore:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P_{n})\to\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(f)\quad\text{in }\mathcal{B}(H).

But we also have
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P_{n})=P_{n}(A)\to f(A)
. By uniqueness of limits in
\mathcal{B}(H)
, we conclude:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(f)=f(A).

Alternative Uniqueness Argument:

For completeness, we also provide an alternative argument using the uniqueness clause of Theorem
4
. Define an auxiliary multifunctor
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))\to\mathbf{HilbMult}
by:

•

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}(\ast)=H

•

For polynomials
P
, define
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}(P)=P(A)
(the functional calculus)

•

For general multimorphisms, define
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}
to satisfy the multifunctor axioms

One verifies that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}
satisfies all conditions of Theorem
4
:

•

On objects:
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}(\ast)=H

•

On linear morphisms:
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}
coincides with the functional calculus by definition

•

The explicit formula and functoriality follow from the polynomial structure

By the uniqueness clause of Theorem
4
, we have
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
. In particular, for continuous functions
f
(approximated by polynomials), we obtain
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(f)=f(A)
.

Proof of (ii): Spectral Mapping Property

Since
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(f)=f(A)
by part (i), the classical Spectral Mapping Theorem for the continuous functional calculus gives:

\sigma(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(f))=\sigma(f(A))=f(\sigma(A)).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 10
.

The proof shows that the specific choice of the family

5.2
Functorial Spectral Theorem Examples

Below, we present two examples to reflect functorial spectral properties discussed in this section.

Example 3

(Pointwise Multiplication Family)
.

Consider the family
\mathcal{T}_{\text{mult}}=\{T_{n}^{\text{mult}}\}_{n\geq 1}
defined by pointwise multiplication. To ensure boundedness on
H=L^{2}(\sigma(A),\mu)
, we define
T_{n}^{\text{mult}}
on a dense subspace and extend:

On the dense subspace

, define:

T_{n}^{\text{mult}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)\cdots f_{n}(x).

This family satisfies all the conditions of Theorem
4
:

(a)

Spectral Locality:
Take
\tau_{n}(x,z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=z_{1}z_{2}\cdots z_{n}
, which is clearly measurable.

(b)

Polynomial Compatibility:
For any polynomials
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[X]
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in H\cap L^{\infty}(\sigma(A),\mu)
, we have:

\displaystyle T_{n}^{\text{mult}}(p_{1}(A)f_{1},\dots,p_{n}(A)f_{n})(x)

\displaystyle=[p_{1}(A)f_{1}](x)\cdot[p_{2}(A)f_{2}](x)\cdots[p_{n}(A)f_{n}](x)

\displaystyle=p_{1}(x)f_{1}(x)\cdot p_{2}(x)f_{2}(x)\cdots p_{n}(x)f_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{n})(x)\cdot f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)\cdots f_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(p_{1}\cdots p_{n})(A)\left[T_{n}^{\text{mult}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\right](x).

(c)

Uniform Boundedness:
For
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in H\cap L^{2n}(\sigma(A),\mu)

\displaystyle\|T_{n}^{\text{mult}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma(A)}|f_{1}(x)\cdots f_{n}(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\sigma(A)}|f_{j}(x)|^{2n}d\mu(x)\right)^{1/n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2n}}^{2}.

\|T_{n}^{\text{mult}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2n}}
.

When

, we have the embedding

with:

\|f\|_{L^{2n}}\leq\mu(\sigma(A))^{1/(2n)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},

\|T_{n}^{\text{mult}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\mu(\sigma(A))^{1/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

This recovers the classical pointwise polynomial action and demonstrates that Theorem
4
generalizes the original construction while maintaining compatibility with the fundamental case of pointwise multiplication.

Consider the family

6
Covariance and Universality in the Multifunctorial Framework

In this section, we formalize two foundational structural results concerning the multifunctorial representation of operator-theoretic data within the Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory framework.

The first result, Proposition
1
, establishes the
covariance of the multifunctorial representation under unitary conjugation
. It shows that the semantic structure encoded by a self-adjoint operator is invariant, up to natural isomorphism, under changes of Hilbert space basis implemented by unitary transformations. This ensures that the multifunctor encoder of operator semantics behaves functorlly with respect to the equations of Hilbert spaces reflecting the p physical and mathematical invariance.

Proposition
2
is the second main result in this section. It introduces a
preliminary universal property
for the multicategory of Hilbert spaces
\mathbf{HilbMult}
. It identifies
\mathbf{HilbMult}
as a canonical semantic target: under mild representability conditions, any Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory containing a self-adjoint element can receive a unique multifunctor from
\mathbf{HilbMult}
that extends the usual functional calculus. This universality justifies viewing
\mathbf{HilbMult}
as a “universal semantic base” for self-adjoint operator semantics.

Together, Propositions
1
and
2
establish both the
equivariance
and the
universality
aspects of the proposed framework: the former ensures structural consistency under isometric transformations, while the latter guarantees categorical completeness in representing self-adjoint dynamics.

6.1
Covariance in the Multifunctorial Framework

Proposition 1

(Covariance under Unitary Conjugation)
.

Let
H,H^{\prime}
be Hilbert spaces and
A\in\mathcal{B}(H)
,
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{\prime})
be bounded self-adjoint operators. Let
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\{T^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be families satisfying the conditions of Theorem
4
for
A
and
A^{\prime}
respectively.

If there exists a unitary operator

such that:

•

A^{\prime}=UAU^{*}

•

T^{\prime}_{n}=U\circ T_{n}\circ(U^{*}\times\cdots\times U^{*})

then the multifunctors
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))\to\mathbf{HilbMult}

\eta:c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{A^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}

is the conjugation multifunctor defined by:

•

On objects:
c_{U}(K)=U(K)

•

On multimorphisms: For
T:K_{1}\times\cdots\times K_{n}\to L

c_{U}(T)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=UT(U^{*}x_{1},\dots,U^{*}x_{n})

Proof.

We proceed in four main steps.

Step 1: The conjugation multifunctor
c_{U}

We verify that
c_{U}
is a symmetric monoidal multifunctor.

Composition preservation
: Let

and

for

\displaystyle c_{U}(T\circ(S_{1},\dots,S_{m}))(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{m,n_{m}})

\displaystyle=U\left[T(S_{1}(U^{*}x_{1,1},\dots,U^{*}x_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,S_{m}(U^{*}x_{m,1},\dots,U^{*}x_{m,n_{m}}))\right]

\displaystyle=U\left[T(U^{*}c_{U}(S_{1})(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,U^{*}c_{U}(S_{m})(x_{m,1},\dots,x_{m,n_{m}}))\right]

\displaystyle=c_{U}(T)(c_{U}(S_{1})(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,c_{U}(S_{m})(x_{m,1},\dots,x_{m,n_{m}}))

\displaystyle=[c_{U}(T)\circ(c_{U}(S_{1}),\dots,c_{U}(S_{m}))](x_{1,1},\dots,x_{m,n_{m}}).

: For
\mathrm{id}_{H}:H\to H

c_{U}(\mathrm{id}_{H})(x)=U(\mathrm{id}_{H}(U^{*}x))=UU^{*}x=x=\mathrm{id}_{H^{\prime}}(x).

Symmetric monoidal structure
: The functor preserves tensor products and symmetry isomorphisms since
U
is unitary and the construction is natural.

Step 2: The composition
c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}

Since both
c_{U}
and
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
are symmetric monoidal multifunctors, their composition
c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))\to\mathbf{HilbMult}
is also a symmetric monoidal multifunctor.

Step 3: Verification of spectral conditions

We verify that
c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4
for operator
A^{\prime}
with family
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.

(i)

Object assignment
:

(c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}})(\ast)=c_{U}(H)=H^{\prime}.

(ii)

: For any polynomial

\displaystyle(c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}})(P)

\displaystyle=c_{U}(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P))

\displaystyle=c_{U}(P(A))\quad\text{(by Theorem\penalty\ \ref{thm:functorial-polynomial-spectral}(ii))}

\displaystyle=UP(A)U^{*}=P(UAU^{*})=P(A^{\prime}).

(iii)

\displaystyle=U\left[\sum_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\,T_{n}(A^{k_{1}}U^{*}x_{1},\dots,A^{k_{n}}U^{*}x_{n})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\,UT_{n}(U^{*}UA^{k_{1}}U^{*}x_{1},\dots,U^{*}UA^{k_{n}}U^{*}x_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\,(U\circ T_{n}\circ(U^{*}\times\cdots\times U^{*}))(UA^{k_{1}}U^{*}x_{1},\dots,UA^{k_{n}}U^{*}x_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\,T^{\prime}_{n}(A^{\prime k_{1}}x_{1},\dots,A^{\prime k_{n}}x_{n}).

(iv)

Functoriality
: Inherited from the composition of multifunctors.

Step 4: Natural isomorphism

Since
c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
satisfies all conditions of Theorem
4
for operator
A^{\prime}
with family
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{A^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}
is the unique such multifunctor, we conclude:

c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{F}_{A^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}.

The identity natural transformation
\eta:c_{U}\circ\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{A^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}
provides the required symmetric monoidal natural isomorphism, completing the proof.
∎

Remark 12
.

The condition
T^{\prime}_{n}=U\circ T_{n}\circ(U^{*}\times\cdots\times U^{*})
ensures that the family
\mathcal{T}
transforms covariantly under unitary conjugation. In particular, if both
H
and
H^{\prime}
are represented as
L^{2}
spaces in a way that makes
U
an isomorphism of function spaces preserving the pointwise product structure, and if we take
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
to be the families of pointwise multiplication maps, then this condition is automatically satisfied.

6.2
Universality in the Multifunctorial Framework

We now formalize the analytic structure underlying Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategories, which generalize operator-norm behavior from Hilbert spaces to enriched categorical settings.

Definition 6

(Norm in a Banach-Enriched Symmetric Monoidal Multicategory)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory.
For each tuple of objects
(A_{1},\dots,A_{n};B)
, the hom-object

\mathcal{M}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n};B)

is a Banach space over
\mathbb{C}
(or
\mathbb{R}
).
Each multimorphism
a\in\mathcal{M}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n};B)
is assigned the Banach-space norm

\|a\|_{\mathcal{M}}:=\|a\|_{\mathcal{M}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n};B)}.

The enrichment requires the following compatibility conditions:

1.

Composition boundedness:

For composable multimorphisms

a\in\mathcal{M}(B_{1},\dots,B_{m};C),\quad b_{j}\in\mathcal{M}(A_{j,1},\dots,A_{j,n_{j}};B_{j}),

there exists a constant
K\geq 0
(typically
K=1
in the contractive case) such that

\|a\circ(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq K\,\|a\|_{\mathcal{M}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|b_{j}\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

2.

Tensor boundedness:

The tensor product operation

\otimes:\mathcal{M}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n};B)\times\mathcal{M}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m};D)\to\mathcal{M}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n},C_{1},\dots,C_{m};B\otimes D)

is a bounded bilinear map with

\|a\otimes b\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq K_{\otimes}\,\|a\|_{\mathcal{M}}\,\|b\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

3.

Symmetry and unit isometries:

All symmetry isomorphisms

\sigma_{\pi}:\mathcal{M}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n};B)\to\mathcal{M}(A_{\pi(1)},\dots,A_{\pi(n)};B),

and the unit isomorphisms for the tensor unit
I
, are isometric.

When
K=K_{\otimes}=1
, the enrichment is said to be
contractive
.

Remark 13
.

The quantity
\|a\|_{\mathcal{M}}
generalizes the operator norm: it measures the “amplification factor” of a multimorphism.
In many natural examples, such as
\mathbf{HilbMult}
, the enrichment is contractive, ensuring that composition or tensoring never increases the operator magnitude.

Example 5

(The Category
\mathbf{HilbMult}
)
.

In the multicategory
\mathbf{HilbMult}
of Hilbert spaces:

•

Objects are Hilbert spaces
H,K,\dots
.

•

\mathbf{HilbMult}(H_{1},\dots,H_{n};K)
consists of bounded multilinear maps
T:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}\to K
.

•

The norm is the operator norm:

\|T\|_{\mathbf{HilbMult}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|T(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|_{K}}{\|x_{1}\|_{H_{1}}\cdots\|x_{n}\|_{H_{n}}}.

•

Composition is contractive, and the tensor product is isometric:

\|S\circ(T_{1},\dots,T_{m})\|\leq\|S\|\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|T_{j}\|,\qquad\|T\otimes T^{\prime}\|=\|T\|\,\|T^{\prime}\|.

Definition 7

(Consistency under Diagonalization and Embedding)
.

Let
\{a^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a family of multimorphisms with

a^{(n)}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{n};X)
.
The family is
consistent under diagonalization and embedding
if:

1.

Diagonalization:

For any function
\rho:\{1,\dots,m\}\to\{1,\dots,n\}
,

a^{(m)}=a^{(n)}\circ(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}),

where each
p_{j}:X^{m}\to X
is
\mathrm{id}_{X}
if
j\in\mathrm{im}(\rho)

and is constant otherwise.
This ensures that repeating or omitting arguments yields coherent results.

2.

Embedding:

For any
1\leq k\leq n
,

a^{(n)}=a^{(k)}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{X^{k}},\varepsilon,\dots,\varepsilon),

where
\varepsilon:X\to I
are the counit or terminal morphisms.

Definition 8

(Bounded Families)
.

A family
\{a^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
in a Banach-enriched multicategory
\mathcal{M}
is
bounded
if:

1.

\|a^{(n)}\|_{\mathcal{M}}<\infty
for each
n
.

2.

There exists
C>0
such that
\|a^{(n)}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq C^{n}
for all
n
.

In contractive enrichments, this property is automatically preserved under composition.

Definition 9

(Spectrum and Spectral Radius)
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(X;X)
be a unary endomorphism.

•

The
spectrum
of
f
is

\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}(f):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:(f-\lambda\,\mathrm{id}_{X})\text{ is not invertible under }\circ\}.

•

The
spectral radius
is

r_{\mathcal{M}}(f):=\sup\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}(f)\}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f^{n}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{1/n}.

Remark 14
.

In any Banach-enriched multicategory:

•

\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}(f)
is nonempty and compact;

•

r_{\mathcal{M}}(f)\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}}
;

•

the spectral mapping theorem holds:

\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}(P(f))=P(\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}(f))
for any polynomial
P
;

•

if
f
is self-adjoint, then
\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}(f)\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 10

(Spectral Compatibility)
.

A family
\{a^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
in a Banach-enriched multicategory
\mathcal{M}
is
spectrally compatible
if:

1.

The diagonalization
A_{n}:=a^{(n)}\circ\Delta_{n}
(where
\Delta_{n}:X\to X^{n}
is the diagonal) defines a unary endomorphism.

2.

Each
A_{n}
has real spectrum:
\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}(A_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
.

3.

There exists
R>0
with

\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}(A_{n})\subset[-R,R]
for all
n
.

4.

The spectral radii are uniformly bounded:

\sup_{n\geq 1}r_{\mathcal{M}}(A_{n})<\infty
.

Definition 11

(Functional Calculus Compatibility)
.

A family
\{a^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
with
a^{(n)}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{n};X)

is
functionally calculus compatible
if, for every polynomial

P(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}},

the corresponding
n
-ary morphism is given by

a^{(n)}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\mu_{n}\circ\Bigl(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}(a^{(1)})^{k_{i}}\Bigr),

where
\mu_{n}:X^{n}\to X
is the
n
-ary multiplication morphism.

Remark 15
.

This condition ensures that each
a^{(n)}
coincides with the polynomial operation generated by the unary operator
a^{(1)}
, respecting the monoidal and multilinear structure of
\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 16

(Synthesis)
.

The four compatibility conditions jointly ensure that a family
\{a^{(n)}\}
in
\mathcal{M}
behaves analogously to a coherent multilinear operator calculus in
\mathbf{HilbMult}
:

•

Consistency
(Def.
7
) encodes structural coherence under input duplication or omission.

•

Boundedness
(Def.
8
) provides analytic control over the operator norms.

•

Spectral compatibility
(Def.
10
) preserves self-adjointness and spectral bounds.

•

Functional calculus compatibility
(Def.
11
) ensures the entire family is generated polynomially from
a^{(1)}
.

Together they guarantee that the family extends the Hilbert-space operator calculus to any Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory.

Proposition 2

(Universal Property of
\mathbf{HilbMult}
for Operator Calculus)
.

Let
\mathbf{HilbMult}
denote the Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory of Hilbert spaces with bounded multilinear maps, and let
\mathcal{M}
be another Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory.

Suppose we are given the following data:

•

A Hilbert space
H\in\mathbf{HilbMult}
,

•

A bounded self-adjoint operator
A\in\mathcal{B}(H)
with
\sigma(A)\subset\mathbb{R}
,

•

A family
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfying Theorem
4
,

•

An object
X\in\mathcal{M}
,

•

A compatible family
\{a^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
with
a^{(n)}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{n};X)
satisfying the four compatibility conditions given by Remark
16
.

Then there exists a unique Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multifunctor

F\colon\mathbf{HilbMult}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}

such that:

(i)

F(H)=X
;

(ii)

For the functional calculus multifunctor
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}
of Theorem
4
, we have

F\big(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)\big)=a^{(n)}_{\varphi}\quad\text{for all polynomials }\varphi;

(iii)

F
preserves multicategorical composition and the symmetric monoidal structure.

Proof.

We define
F
explicitly and verify that it satisfies the claimed properties.

Step 1: Definition on Objects.

•

Set
F(H)=X
and
F(\mathbb{C})=I_{\mathcal{M}}
(the unit object).

•

For tensor products, define

F(H_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{k}):=F(H_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes F(H_{k}).

•

For any other Hilbert space
K
, choose a decomposition

K\cong H^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m}
and define

F(K):=F(H)^{\otimes n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes m}
.

This is well-defined up to the canonical isomorphisms of the symmetric monoidal structure.

Step 2: Definition on Multimorphisms.

Let
\mathbf{HilbMult}_{\mathcal{T}}
denote the smallest submulticategory of
\mathbf{HilbMult}
containing:

•

all functional calculus operations
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)
for polynomials
\varphi
,

•

the identity morphisms,

•

and all structural isomorphisms of the symmetric monoidal structure,

and closed under composition and tensor products.

We define
F
recursively on
\mathbf{HilbMult}_{\mathcal{T}}
by:

\displaystyle F(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi))

\displaystyle:=a^{(n)}_{\varphi},

\displaystyle F(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes n}})

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{id}_{X^{\otimes n}},

\displaystyle F(\psi\circ(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m}))

\displaystyle:=F(\psi)\circ(F(\psi_{1}),\ldots,F(\psi_{m})),

\displaystyle F(\psi\otimes\psi^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=F(\psi)\otimes F(\psi^{\prime}),

\displaystyle F(\sigma_{K,L})

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{F(K),F(L)},

and similarly for associators, unitors, and other coherence morphisms.

Step 3: Well-definedness.

If
\psi
admits multiple representations via composition or tensoring, we must show the assigned
F(\psi)
is independent of the choice.

(a) Composition coherence.

Suppose

\psi=\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P)\circ(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(Q_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(Q_{m})).

Then

F(\psi)=a^{(m)}_{P}\circ(a^{(n_{1})}_{Q_{1}},\dots,a^{(n_{m})}_{Q_{m}})=a^{(N)}_{P\circ(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m})}=F\big(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(P\circ(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m}))\big),

using the functional calculus compatibility condition.

(b) Symmetric monoidal coherence.

All coherence morphisms are preserved by definition of
F
on structural maps.

(c) Object representation coherence.

If
K\cong H^{\otimes n}\cong H^{\otimes m}
via different isomorphisms, the corresponding
F(K)
are canonically isomorphic in
\mathcal{M}
, and these isomorphisms are preserved by
F
.

Step 4: Verification of Multifunctor Laws.

Identity:

F(\mathrm{id}_{H})=a^{(1)}_{\mathrm{id}}=\mathrm{id}_{X}
by compatibility.

Composition and Tensor:

These preservation laws are built into the recursive definition.

Symmetric Monoidal Structure:

All structural isomorphisms are preserved by construction.

Step 5: Banach Enrichment.

Linearity.

The assignment
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi)\mapsto a^{(n)}_{\varphi}
is linear, and composition/tensoring in both
\mathbf{HilbMult}
and
\mathcal{M}
are multilinear.

Boundedness.

By assumption,
\|a^{(n)}_{\varphi}\|\leq C\|\varphi\|
for some
C>0
, and the tensor and composition operations are bounded in both multicategories. Hence an inductive argument shows

\|F(\psi)\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq C^{\prime}\|\psi\|_{\mathbf{HilbMult}}

for some constant
C^{\prime}
.

Step 6: Uniqueness.

Suppose
G\colon\mathbf{HilbMult}\to\mathcal{M}
is another Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multifunctor satisfying (i)–(iii). Then:

•

G(H)=X=F(H)
and both preserve the tensor structure, so
G(K)=F(K)
for all objects
K
.

•

G(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi))=a^{(n)}_{\varphi}=F(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi))
for all
\varphi
.

•

By preservation of composition and tensoring,
G(\psi)=F(\psi)
for all
\psi
.

Thus
F=G
uniquely.
∎

Remark 17

(Universal Characterization)
.

Proposition
2
identifies
\mathbf{HilbMult}
as the
canonical domain
for multilinear operator calculus. Any Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory
\mathcal{M}
that supports an interpretation
\{a^{(n)}\}
of the operator calculus of a self-adjoint operator
A
admits a unique structure-preserving multifunctor

F\colon\mathbf{HilbMult}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}

that respects this interpretation.

Uniqueness is enforced at three levels:

1.

Object level:

F(H)=X
, fixing all tensor powers of
H
;

2.

Generator level:

F(\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}}(\varphi))=a^{(n)}_{\varphi}
for polynomial
\varphi
;

3.

Structural level:
Preservation of composition and tensoring extends
F
uniquely to all morphisms generated from these.

Remark 18

(Scope and Interpretation)
.

This universal property expresses a form of
targeted semantic completeness
:
\mathbf{HilbMult}
fully captures the multilinear operator calculus generated by a self-adjoint operator and its functional calculus family
\mathcal{T}
.

Any interpretation in another Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory
\mathcal{M}
factors uniquely through
\mathbf{HilbMult}
, provided the family
\{a^{(n)}\}
satisfies:

1.

Consistency:

a^{(1)}_{\mathrm{id}}=\mathrm{id}_{X}
and
a^{(1)}_{z}=z\,\mathrm{id}_{X}
for scalars
z
;

2.

Boundedness:

\|a^{(n)}_{\varphi}\|\leq C\|\varphi\|
;

3.

Spectral compatibility:

\sigma(a^{(n)}_{\varphi})\subseteq\varphi(\sigma(A)^{n})
;

4.

Functional calculus compatibility:

a^{(N)}_{P\circ(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m})}=a^{(m)}_{P}\circ(a^{(n_{1})}_{Q_{1}},\dots,a^{(n_{m})}_{Q_{m}})
.

Thus
\mathbf{HilbMult}
serves as a universal environment for interpreting operator calculus constructions.

Remark 19

(Practical Significance)
.

This universal property carries several important consequences. First, it ensures a strong form of model independence: any result established within
\mathbf{HilbMult}
automatically holds in every Banach-enriched symmetric monoidal multicategory
\mathcal{M}
that interprets the same operator calculus, since such interpretations factor uniquely through
\mathbf{HilbMult}
. Second, it has a clear effect on the theoretical representation—different families of
{a^{(n)}}
conformations correspond to different class representations of the computation of underlying operators, similar to representations of algebra in different units. Finally, the construction provides a generalizing framework that rigorously extends the operator-theoretic principles of Hilbert spaces to broader, rich categorical contexts while preserving analytic and synthetic structure.

7
Examples

In this section, we will present three examples to illustrate theory proposed in this work.

Example 6

(Finite-Dimensional Matrix Case)
.

Let
H=\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. The multicategory
\mathbf{HilbMult}
restricted to finite-dimensional spaces consists of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces with bounded multilinear maps, which in this setting are all multilinear maps (as they are automatically bounded).

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a Hermitian matrix (
A=A^{*}
) with spectrum
\sigma(A)\subset\mathbb{R}
. Consider the family
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}=\{T_{n}\}
where
T_{n}:(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the
n
-fold Hadamard (entrywise) product:

T_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{1}\odot\cdots\odot x_{n},\quad(x_{1}\odot\cdots\odot x_{n})_{i}=(x_{1})_{i}\cdots(x_{n})_{i}.

This family satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4
, yielding the functional calculus multifunctor:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))\to\mathbf{HilbMult}.

•

On objects:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}}(\ast)=\mathbb{C}^{n}

•

On multimorphisms:
For
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))}(\ast^{m};\ast)
represented by polynomial
P(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})=\sum c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}}z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{m}^{k_{m}}
, the multimorphism is:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\sum c_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}}(A^{k_{1}}x_{1})\odot(A^{k_{2}}x_{2})\odot\cdots\odot(A^{k_{m}}x_{m}).

Special Cases:

1.

Linear case (
m=1
):
For univariate
P(z)=\sum c_{k}z^{k}
, we recover standard functional calculus:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}}(P)(x)=P(A)x

2.

Bilinear case (
m=2
):
For bivariate
P(z,w)=\sum c_{ij}z^{i}w^{j}
:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}}(P)(x,y)=\sum c_{ij}(A^{i}x)\odot(A^{j}y)

3.

Multiplication case (
P(z,w)=zw
):
This gives:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}}(P)(x,y)=(Ax)\odot y

Verification of Theorem Conditions:

The Hadamard family
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}
satisfies:

•

Spectral Locality:
In the standard basis,
\tau_{n}(e_{i},z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=z_{1}\cdots z_{n}

•

Polynomial Compatibility:
For polynomials
p_{1},\dots,p_{m}
:

\displaystyle T_{m}(p_{1}(A)x_{1},\dots,p_{m}(A)x_{m})

\displaystyle=(p_{1}(A)x_{1})\odot\cdots\odot(p_{m}(A)x_{m})

\displaystyle=(p_{1}\cdots p_{m})(A)(x_{1}\odot\cdots\odot x_{m})\quad\text{(since $A$ is diagonal in eigenbasis)}

\displaystyle=(p_{1}\cdots p_{m})(A)T_{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

•

Uniform Boundedness:

\|T_{n}\|\leq 1
for all
n

Diagonal Basis Representation:

Since
A
is Hermitian,
A=UDU^{*}
with
D=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
. Let
\tilde{x}_{j}=U^{*}x_{j}
. Then:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}}(\varphi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=U\left[P(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1})\tilde{x}_{1}\odot\cdots\odot P(\lambda_{n},\dots,\lambda_{n})\tilde{x}_{m}\right]

where the evaluation is diagonal:
P(\lambda_{i},\dots,\lambda_{i})
at each coordinate
i
.

Universal Property:

This example illustrates Proposition
2
: any interpretation of the matrix operator calculus in another multicategory
\mathcal{M}
via a compatible family
\{a^{(n)}\}
factors uniquely through
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hadamard}}}
.

This finite-dimensional case demonstrates that the abstract framework concretely recovers natural multilinear operations on matrices, with the Hadamard product serving as the canonical
n
-linear operation compatible with the functional calculus.

Example 7

(Multiplication Operator on
L^{2}
Spaces)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space and consider
H=L^{2}(X,\mu)
. Let
\mathrm{me}:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable function, and define the multiplication operator
A:L^{2}(X,\mu)\to L^{2}(X,\mu)
by:

(Af)(x)=\mathrm{me}(x)f(x)\quad\text{for }f\in L^{2}(X,\mu).

Then
A
is bounded self-adjoint with spectrum
\sigma(A)=\overline{\mathrm{range}(\mathrm{me})}^{\mathrm{ess}}
, the essential range of
\mathrm{me}
.

Consider the family
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}=\{T_{n}\}
where
T_{n}:(L^{2}(X,\mu))^{n}\to L^{2}(X,\mu)
is the pointwise product:

T_{n}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})(x)=g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)\cdots g_{n}(x)\quad\text{for }g_{j}\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\cap L^{2}(X,\mu).

This family satisfies Theorem
4
, yielding:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))\to\mathbf{HilbMult}.

•

On objects:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(\ast)=L^{2}(X,\mu)

•

On multimorphisms:
For
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}}(\sigma(A))}(\ast^{n};\ast)
represented by
P(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(\varphi)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})(x)=P(\mathrm{me}(x),\dots,\mathrm{me}(x))\cdot g_{1}(x)\cdots g_{n}(x)

Special Cases:

1.

Linear case (
n=1
):
For
P(z)=\sum c_{k}z^{k}
:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(P)(g)(x)=P(\mathrm{me}(x))g(x)

2.

Bilinear case (
n=2
):
For
P(z,w)=\sum c_{ij}z^{i}w^{j}
:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(P)(g,h)(x)=P(\mathrm{me}(x),\mathrm{me}(x))g(x)h(x)

3.

Constant polynomial (
P\equiv 1
):
Pure pointwise product:

\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(1)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})(x)=g_{1}(x)\cdots g_{n}(x)

Verification of Theorem Conditions:

•

Spectral Locality:
Take
\tau_{n}(x,z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=z_{1}\cdots z_{n}
, which is measurable

•

Polynomial Compatibility:
For polynomials
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
:

\displaystyle T_{n}(p_{1}(A)g_{1},\dots,p_{n}(A)g_{n})(x)

\displaystyle=p_{1}(\mathrm{me}(x))g_{1}(x)\cdots p_{n}(\mathrm{me}(x))g_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(p_{1}\cdots p_{n})(\mathrm{me}(x))\cdot g_{1}(x)\cdots g_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(p_{1}\cdots p_{n})(A)T_{n}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})(x)

•

Uniform Boundedness:
On
L^{\infty}\cap L^{2}
,
\|T_{n}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\|_{2}\leq\|g_{1}\|_{\infty}\cdots\|g_{n-1}\|_{\infty}\|g_{n}\|_{2}

Boundedness Analysis:

The maps
\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(\varphi)
extend to bounded multilinear maps on all of
(L^{2}(X,\mu))^{n}
:

•

Linear case (
n=1
):

\|\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(P)(g)\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{X}|P(\mathrm{me}(x))|^{2}|g(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)\leq\|P\|_{L^{\infty}(\sigma(A))}^{2}\|g\|_{2}^{2}

since
|P(\mathrm{me}(x))|\leq\sup_{z\in\sigma(A)}|P(z)|=\|P\|_{L^{\infty}(\sigma(A))}
.

•

Multilinear case (
n\geq 2
):
Since
\mathrm{me}
is bounded,
|P(\mathrm{me}(x),\dots,\mathrm{me}(x))|\leq C_{P}
for some constant
C_{P}>0
depending on the polynomial
P
. Then:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(\varphi)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|P(\mathrm{me}(x),\dots,\mathrm{me}(x))|^{2}|g_{1}(x)|^{2}\cdots|g_{n}(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{P}^{2}\int_{X}|g_{1}(x)|^{2}\cdots|g_{n}(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)

To bound the integral, apply Hölder’s inequality with exponents
p_{1}=p_{2}=\cdots=p_{n}=n

\int_{X}|g_{1}(x)|^{2}\cdots|g_{n}(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{X}|g_{i}(x)|^{2n}d\mu(x)\right)^{1/n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|g_{i}\|_{2n}^{2}

Therefore:

\|\mathcal{F}_{A,\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pointwise}}}(\varphi)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\|_{2}\leq C_{P}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|g_{i}\|_{2n}

•

Relation between norms:
For the
L^{2n}
norms, we have:

–

If
\mu(X)<\infty
(finite measure space), then by Jensen’s inequality or the nesting of
L^{p}
spaces:

\|g_{i}\|_{2n}\leq\mu(X)^{\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{1}{2}}\|g_{i}\|_{2}

–

In general, by interpolation between
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
, if
g_{i}\in L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}
, then:

\|g_{i}\|_{2n}\leq\|g_{i}\|_{2}^{1/n}\|g_{i}\|_{\infty}^{1-1/n}

–

For the dense subspace
L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}
, we have uniform boundedness, and the maps extend uniquely to all of
L^{2}
by continuity.

•

Alternative approach via iterated Hölder:
One can also apply Hölder’s inequality iteratively:

\displaystyle\int_{X}|g_{1}(x)|^{2}\cdots|g_{n}(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{1}\|_{2n}^{2}\left(\int_{X}(|g_{2}(x)|^{2}\cdots|g_{n}(x)|^{2})^{\frac{n}{n-1}}d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{1}\|_{2n}^{2}\|g_{2}\|_{2n}^{2}\left(\int_{X}(|g_{3}(x)|^{2}\cdots|g_{n}(x)|^{2})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|g_{i}\|_{2n}^{2}

This shows the bound is sharp and the multilinear map is indeed bounded from
(L^{2n}(X,\mu))^{n}
to
L^{2}(X,\mu)
.

Example 9

(Tensorized System of Commuting Self-Adjoint Operators)
.

Let

H_{1}=H_{2}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Define

A=-\tfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+x^{2}\quad\text{on }H_{1},\qquad B=-\tfrac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+y^{2}\quad\text{on }H_{2}.

Both

\sigma(A)=\{2n+1:n\geq 0\}

and

are commuting, self-adjoint, and bounded below.

Partial contraction.

Define a bilinear pairing that contracts the second tensor factor:

(u\otimes\phi)\bullet_{H_{2}}(v\otimes\psi):=\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{H_{2}}\,u\in H_{1},

and extend by linearity and continuity to a bounded bilinear map

\bullet_{H_{2}}:(H_{1}\otimes H_{2})\times(H_{1}\otimes H_{2})\to H_{1}.

This operation “contracts over”
H_{2}
while preserving the
H_{1}
-component.

Definition of the family.

For

.
For

be a polynomial in commuting variables.
Then on a dense invariant domain

(for instance, the finite span of Hermite tensors), define

T_{n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}):=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{l}}c_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{l}}\Bigl[(A^{k_{1}}\otimes B^{l_{1}})f_{1}\bullet_{H_{2}}\cdots\bullet_{H_{2}}(A^{k_{n}}\otimes B^{l_{n}})f_{n}\Bigr],

where the iterated contraction is applied left-to-right.

H_{1}
.

Since
A,B

act on domains
D(A^{k}),D(B^{l})
.

Domain approach:

Each

2.

Bounded transform approach:

Replace

Then each

\mathbf{HilbMult}

Spectral and polynomial properties.

On either version (domain or bounded transform), the family

•

•

•

Uniform boundedness:

In the bounded-transform setting, all

Concrete instance.

For the polynomial

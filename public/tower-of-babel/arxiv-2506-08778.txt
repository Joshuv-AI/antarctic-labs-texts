Symmetry energy and neutron matter equation of state at ρ 0 / 3 \rho_{0}/3 from the electric dipole polarizability in 48 Ca, 68 Ni and 208 Pb Preprint: APS/123-QED
Mengying Qiu, Lie-Wen Chen, Zheng Zheng Li, Yi Fei Niu, Zhen Zhang

Abstract
Based on the quasiparticle random phase approximation implemented via the finite amplitude method, we employ a set of representative relativistic mean-field models to investigate the sensitivity of the inverse electric dipole polarizability 1 / α D 1/\alpha_{\mathrm{D}} in Ca 48 {}^{48}\mathrm{Ca} , Ni 68 {}^{68}\mathrm{Ni} , and Pb 208 {}^{208}\mathrm{Pb} to the symmetry energy E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\rm{sym}}(\rho) and the neutron matter equation of state E PNM ​ ( ρ ) E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho) at a subsaturation density of ρ = ρ 0 / 3 \rho=\rho_{0}/3 . Combined with predictions from nonrelativistic Skyrme energy density functionals (EDFs), our results reveal strong linear correlations between 1 / α D 1/\alpha_{\mathrm{D}} and both E sym ​ ( ρ 0 / 3 ) E_{\rm{sym}}(\rho_{0}/3) and E PNM ​ ( ρ 0 / 3 ) E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho_{0}/3) . In particular, the 1 / α D 1/\alpha_{\mathrm{D}} – E PNM ​ ( ρ 0 / 3 ) E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho_{0}/3) correlation for Pb 208 {}^{208}\mathrm{Pb} is found to be nearly model-independent. A Bayesian analysis of the measured values of α D \alpha_{\rm{D}} in Ca 48 {}^{48}\mathrm{Ca} , Ni 68 {}^{68}\mathrm{Ni} , and Pb 208 {}^{208}\mathrm{Pb} yields quantitative constraints of E sym ​ ( ρ 0 / 3 ) = 17.8 − 0.9 ​ ( 1.6 ) + 1.1 ​ ( 1.8 ) ​ MeV E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\rho_{0}/3)=17.8^{+1.1(1.8)}_{-0.9(1.6)}~\mathrm{MeV} and E PNM ​ ( ρ 0 / 3 ) = 9.1 − 0.9 ​ ( 1.4 ) + 0.8 ​ ( 1.4 ) ​ MeV E_{\mathrm{PNM}}(\rho_{0}/3)=9.1^{+0.8(1.4)}_{-0.9(1.4)}~\mathrm{MeV} at the 68% (90%) confidence level, respectively. The extracted value of E PNM ​ ( ρ 0 / 3 ) E_{\mathrm{PNM}}(\rho_{0}/3) exceeds most predictions from microscopic many-body theories, suggesting a mild tension between nuclear EDF-based constraints derived from α D \alpha_{\mathrm{D}} data and results from ab initio calculations.



I
Introduction

The nuclear symmetry energy
E_{\rm{sym}}(\rho)
, which characterizes the isospin dependence of nuclear equation of state (EOS), plays a critical role in both nuclear physics and astrophysics
Baran et al. 2005
;
Steiner et al. 2005
;
Lattimer and Prakash 2007
;
Li et al. 2008
;
Oertel et al. 2017
;
Roca-Maza and Paar 2018
. Its density dependence has profound implications for various important phenomena, including the structure of neutron-rich nuclei, isospin dynamics in heavy-ion collisions, and the properties of neutron stars, neutron star mergers, and core-collapse supernovae.
Closely related to
E_{\rm{sym}}(\rho)
is the EOS of pure neutron matter
E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho)
, which is essential for describing neutron-rich systems. Knowledge of
E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho)
is of particular interest as it provides unique opportunities to test nuclear forces and many-body theories
Machleidt and Sammarruca 2024
. Consequently, a lot of work has been devoted to constrain the
E_{\rm{sym}}(\rho)
or
E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho)
via various nuclear theories, experimental data and astrophysical observations.

Among various experimental observables for
E_{\rm{sym}}(\rho)
and
E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho)
, the electric dipole polarizability
\alpha_{\rm D}
is believed to be a clean and effective isovector probe. As early as 1944, Migdal pointed out its intimate connection to the symmetry energy entering in the semi-empirical mass formula
Migdal 1944
. In the 1980s, the important role of surface symmetry energy in the evaluation of
\alpha_{\rm{D}}
was noticed, and the experimental data on
\alpha_{\rm{D}}
were used to constrain the surface symmetry energy
Lipparini and Stringari 1981
;
Krivine et al. 1982
;
Meyer et al. 1982
. With advances in modern nuclear theories and experimental techniques, the
\alpha_{\rm{D}}
has re-attracted a lot of attentions, especially since Ref.
Reinhard and Nazarewicz 2010
proposed the
\alpha_{\rm{D}}
as a sensitive probe of the neutron skin thickness
and the density slope parameter of the symmetry energy.
It was soon realized that the

208

. The strong linear

48

While most studies have focused on the

Experimentally, the

II
Formalism

In this section, we present the theoretical formalism used to compute the electric dipole polarizability
\alpha_{\rm{D}}
. The calculations are performed using the quasiparticle finite amplitude method (QFAM) implemented within a set of representative relativistic mean-field models. The adopted relativistic nuclear EDFs are based on three types of effective Lagrangians, i.e., nonlinear meson-exchange (NL), density-dependent meson-exchange (DDME), and density-dependent point-coupling (DDPC) models. We first provide a brief introduction to the corresponding Lagrangian densities, followed by a description of the QFAM and the calculation of
\alpha_{\rm{D}}
.

II.1
Nonlinear meson-exchange RMF model

In the nonlinear (NL) RMF model, nucleons interact through the exchange of mesons, typically including the isoscalar-scalar
\sigma
, the isoscalar-vector
\omega
meson and the isovector-vector
\rho
mesons. The conventional NL effective Lagrangian density incorporates nonlinear self-interactions of the
\sigma
and
\omega
mesons, as well as a cross-coupling between
\rho
and
\omega
meson. It reads

II.3
Density-dependent point-coupling RMF model

In the density-dependent point-coupling (DDPC) RMF model, the effective Lagrangian includes isoscalar-scalar, isoscalar-vector, and isovector-vector four-fermion contact interactions
Niksic et al. 2008
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PC}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\psi}\left(i\gamma_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}-m\right)\psi-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}-e\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\frac{\left(1+\tau_{3}\right)}{2}A^{\mu}\psi

(5)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}(\bar{\psi}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\psi)-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\mathrm{V}}\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\bar{\psi}\vec{\tau}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)\left(\bar{\psi}\vec{\tau}\gamma_{\mu}\psi\right)-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\mathrm{S}}\left(\partial_{\nu}\bar{\psi}\psi\right)\left(\partial^{\nu}\bar{\psi}\psi\right),

where
\delta_{\mathrm{S}}

\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}

\alpha_{\mathrm{V}}

\alpha_{\mathrm{TV}}

\alpha_{i}(\rho)=a_{i}+\left(b_{i}+c_{i}x\right)e^{-d_{i}x},\quad i=\mathrm{S},\mathrm{V},\mathrm{TV}

(6)

are usually set to zero for simplicity.

II.4
Time-dependent relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory and quasiparticle finite amplitude method

Within the framework of relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov (RHB) theory
Vretenar et al. 2005
;
Meng et al. 2006
, the ground-state of a nucleus is determined by solving the RHB equation

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{D}-m-\lambda&\Delta\\
-\Delta^{*}&-h_{D}^{*}+m+\lambda\end{array}\right)\binom{U_{\mu}}{V_{\mu}}=E_{\mu}\binom{U_{\mu}}{V_{\mu}},

where
h_{D}
is the single-nucleon Dirac Hamiltonian,
\Delta
is the pairing field,
U
and
V
are Dirac spinors,
E_{\mu}
is the quasiparticle energy, and the chemical potential
\lambda
is determined by the particle-number conservation condition. The
h_{D}
is usually derived from effective Lagrangian density introduced in previous sub-sections under mean-field and no-sea approximations.

Starting from the RHB ground-state solution, the quasiparticle random phase approximation (QRPA) can be formulated to describe collective excitations
Paar et al. 2003
;
Liang et al. 2013
. The QRPA equation is known to be equivalent to the time-dependent Hartree (-Fock) Bogoliubov equation at the small amplitude limit
Ring and Schuck 2004
. We consider a ground-state nucleus is perturbed by a weak harmonic oscillating external field of frequency
\omega
:

\displaystyle F(t)=\eta\{F(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}+F^{\dagger}(\omega)e^{+i\omega t}\}

(7)

with
\eta
being a small real parameter.
In the Bogoliubov quasiparticle basis
\alpha_{\mu}
,
\alpha_{\mu}^{\dagger}
, neglecting a constant term, the external field operator
F(\omega)
can be written as

F(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\nu}\{F_{\mu\nu}^{20}\alpha_{\mu}^{\dagger}\alpha_{\nu}^{\dagger}+F_{\mu\nu}^{02}\alpha_{\nu}\alpha_{\mu}\}+\sum_{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}^{11}(\omega)\alpha^{\dagger}_{\mu}\alpha_{\nu}.

For the linear response, the
F^{11}
term does not contribute and is thus omitted. For the derivation of
F_{\mu\nu}^{20}
and
F_{\mu\nu}^{02}
, we refer the reader to, e.g., the Appendix of Ref.
Avogadro and Nakatsukasa 2011
for details.

The external field
F(t)
induces a density oscillation around the ground state with the same frequency
\omega
, which leads to the oscillation of RHB Hamiltonian
H(t)
around the static Hamiltonian
H_{0}
by

\displaystyle\delta H(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\left\{\delta H(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}+\delta H^{\dagger}(\omega)e^{i\omega t}\right\},

(8)

\displaystyle\delta H(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\mu\nu}}\left\{\delta H_{\mu\nu}^{20}(\omega)\alpha_{\mu}^{\dagger}\alpha_{\nu}^{\dagger}+\delta H_{\mu\nu}^{02}(\omega)\alpha_{\mu}\alpha_{\nu}\right\}.

(9)

Similarly, due to the perturbation of
F(t)
, the time-dependent quasiparticle operators turns to be

\alpha_{\mu}(t)=(\alpha_{\mu}+\delta\alpha_{\mu}(t))e^{iE_{\mu}t}.

(10)

The oscillation term
\delta\alpha_{\mu}(t)
can be expanded in
terms of quasiparticle creation operators:

\delta\alpha_{\mu}(t)=\eta\sum_{\nu}\alpha_{\nu}^{\dagger}\left(X_{\nu\mu}(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}+Y_{\nu\mu}^{*}(\omega)e^{+i\omega t}\right),

(11)

where
X
and
Y
are forward and backward amplitudes.

By keeping only linear terms in
\eta
, the time-evolution equation
i\partial_{t}\alpha_{\mu}=[H(t)+F(t),\alpha_{\mu}(t)]
deduces to the following linear response equations

\displaystyle\left(E_{\mu}+E_{\nu}-\omega\right)X_{\mu\nu}(\omega)+\delta H_{\mu\nu}^{20}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-F_{\mu\nu}^{20},

(12)

\displaystyle\left(E_{\mu}+E_{\nu}+\omega\right)Y_{\mu\nu}(\omega)+\delta H_{\mu\nu}^{02}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-F_{\mu\nu}^{02}.

(13)

Further expanding
\delta H_{\mu\nu}^{20}(\omega)
and
\delta H_{\mu\nu}^{02}(\omega)
in terms of
amplitudes
X_{\mu\nu}(\omega)
and
Y_{\mu\nu}(\omega)

Hinohara et al. 2013

The conventional QRPA method requires calculating the QRPA matrix elements, which could be computationally expensive when the number of two-quasiparicle excitation configuration becomes very large, such as the case of deformed nuclei

Hinohara et al. 2013

II.5
Calculation of electric dipole polarizability

For the electric dipole response of a spherical nucleus, we take the external field operator to be

F=\frac{NZ}{A}\left[\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{Z}r_{i}Y_{10}(\hat{\bm{r}}_{i})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}Y_{10}(\hat{\bm{r}}_{i})\right],

(16)

where
A
,
Z
,
N
are the mass, proton and neutron numbers, respectively,
r_{i}
is the radial coordinate
of the
i
-th proton or neutron,
Y_{10}(\hat{\bm{r}}_{i})
is the corresponding spherical harmonic function.
The electric dipole polarizability can then be evaluated using the inverse energy weighted sum rule (EWSR)
m_{-1}

\alpha_{D}=\frac{8\pi e^{2}}{3}m_{-1},\quad m_{-1}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\omega}\frac{dB(F,\omega)}{d\omega}d\omega.

Instead of calculating
m_{-1}
directly from the strength function, we employ a
more efficient method based on contour integration in the complex
\omega
plane. As proposed in Ref.
Hinohara et al. 2015
, sum rules can be evaluated by using integration along a closed contour
\mathcal{C}
, sketched in Fig.
1
, which includes two semicircles
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
centered at zero energy and two straight segments
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
on the imaginary axis. In particular, the
m_{-1}
can be obtained by integrating only along the semicircle
A_{2}
(clockwise) of radius
R_{A_{2}}

Hinohara et al. 2015
:

m_{-1}(F)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{A_{2}}}\omega^{-1}S(F,\omega)d\omega,

(17)

which accounts for the contributions from all QRPA modes with excitation energy larger than
R_{{A_{2}}}
. In this work, the
R_{A_{2}}
is set to
1
MeV, and the integral is evaluated using Simpson’s rule with 26 quadrature points.

IV
Conclusion

By employing a number of representative parameter sets for three classes of relativistic nuclear energy density functionals (i.e., nonlinear meson-exchange model, density-dependent meson-exchange model and density-dependent point-coupling model) and the nonrelativistic Skyrme energy density functionals, we have demonstrated that the inverse electric dipole polarizabilities
1/\alpha_{\mathrm{D}}
of
{}^{48}\mathrm{Ca}
,
{}^{68}\mathrm{Ni}
, and
{}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}
exhibit strong linear correlations with both the symmetry energy
E_{\rm{sym}}(\rho)
and the pure neutron matter equation of state
E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho)
at a subsaturation density
\rho=\rho_{0}/3
. Remarkably, the
1/\alpha_{\mathrm{D}}
–
E_{\rm{PNM}}(\rho_{0}/3)
correlation for
208
Pb shows minimal model dependence.

Using linear regression analyses of the model predictions and then applying Bayesian analysis of experimental data for
\alpha_{\rm{D}}
in
48
Ca,
68
Ni and
208
Pb, we infer
E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\rho_{0}/3)=17.8^{+1.1(1.8)}_{-0.9(1.6)}~\mathrm{MeV}
and
E_{\mathrm{PNM}}(\rho_{0}/3)=9.1^{+0.8(1.4)}_{-0.9(1.4)}~\mathrm{MeV}

at the 68% (90%) confidence level. The extracted value of
E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\rho_{0}/3)
is relatively high but remains consistent with empirical estimates within the
1\sigma
uncertainty. In contrast, the inferred
E_{\mathrm{PNM}}(\rho_{0}/3)
exceeds most predictions from microscopic many-body theories, and its
1\sigma
interval does not overlap with the range derived from the chiral effective field theory. This suggests a mild tension between EDF-based constraints derived from
\alpha_{\mathrm{D}}
data and the results of microscopic calculations.

Induced fields for nonlinear meson exchange model

The single-nucleon Dirac Hamiltonian for nonlinear meson-exchange RMF model is given by
Bjelčić and Nikšić 2023

h_{D}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma^{0}+\left(\Sigma_{s}+m\right)&\bm{\sigma}\cdot(\bm{p}-\bm{\Sigma}),\\
\bm{\sigma}\cdot(\bm{p}-\bm{\Sigma})&\Sigma^{0}-\left(\Sigma_{s}+m\right)\end{array}\right],

(30)

where
\bm{\sigma}
are the Pauli matrices, and the scalar and vector self-energies are given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{s}

\displaystyle=-g_{\sigma}\sigma,

(31)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\mu}

\displaystyle=g_{\omega}\omega^{\mu}+g_{\rho}\vec{\tau}\vec{\rho}^{\mu}+e\frac{1+\tau_{3}}{2}A^{\mu},

with
\Sigma^{0}
and
\bm{\Sigma}
denoting the time-like and space-like components of
\Sigma^{\mu}
, respectively.

In the presence of external fields, the induced single-nucleon Dirac Hamiltonian are obtained via functional variations with respect to the densities

\displaystyle\delta h_{{D}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta\Sigma^{0}+\delta\Sigma_{s}&-\bm{\sigma}\cdot\delta\bm{\Sigma}\\
-\bm{\sigma}\cdot\delta\bm{\Sigma}&\delta\Sigma^{0}-\delta\Sigma_{s}\end{array}\right],

(32)

where the corresponding induced self-energies are

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma_{s}=-g_{\sigma}\delta\sigma,

(33)

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma^{0}=g_{\omega}\delta\omega^{0}+\tau_{3}g_{\rho}\delta\rho^{0}+\frac{1+\tau_{3}}{2}\delta V_{C},

\displaystyle\delta\bm{\Sigma}=g_{\omega}\delta\bm{\omega}+\tau_{3}g_{\rho}\delta\bm{{\rho}}+\frac{1+\tau_{3}}{2}\delta\mathbf{V}_{C}.

Here,
\delta V_{C}
and
\delta\mathbf{V}_{C}
are the time-like and space-like components of the induced Coulomb field, respectively, obtained by solving the Poisson equations

\displaystyle-\nabla^{2}\delta V_{C}=e^{2}\delta\rho_{p},

(34)

\displaystyle-\nabla^{2}\delta\mathbf{V}_{C}=e^{2}\delta\bm{j}_{p},

where
\delta\rho_{p}
and
\delta\bm{j}_{p}
are the induced density and spatial current of protons. The induced meson fields are governed by linearized Klein-Gordon equations. For the scalar and time-like vector meson fields, one obtains

\displaystyle\left[-\Delta+m_{\sigma}^{2}\right]\delta\sigma=

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{S}}-2b_{\sigma}mg_{\sigma}^{3}[\sigma]_{\mathrm{GS}}\delta\sigma

(35)

\displaystyle-3c_{\sigma}g_{\sigma}^{4}[\sigma]_{\mathrm{GS}}^{2}\delta\sigma,

\displaystyle\left[-\Delta+m_{\omega}^{2}\right]\delta\omega^{0}=

\displaystyle g_{\omega}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{B}}-3c_{\omega}g_{\omega}^{4}\left[\omega^{0}\right]_{\mathrm{GS}}^{2}\delta\omega^{0}

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\rm{V}}g_{\rho}^{2}\left[\rho^{0}\right]_{\mathrm{GS}}^{2}g_{\omega}^{2}\delta\omega^{0}

\displaystyle-2\Lambda_{\rm{V}}g_{\rho}^{2}g_{\omega}^{2}\left[\rho^{0}\omega^{0}\right]_{\mathrm{GS}}\delta\rho^{0},

\displaystyle\left[-\Delta+m_{\rho}^{2}\right]\delta\rho^{0}=

\displaystyle g_{\rho}\delta\rho_{3,\mathrm{B}}-\Lambda_{\rm{V}}g_{\rho}^{2}g_{\omega}^{2}\left[\omega^{0}\right]_{\mathrm{GS}}^{2}\delta\rho^{0}

\displaystyle-2\Lambda_{\rm{V}}g_{\rho}^{2}g_{\omega}^{2}\left[\rho^{0}\omega^{0}\right]_{\mathrm{GS}}\delta\omega^{0},

and the equations for the space-like components of the vector fields read

\displaystyle\left[-\Delta+m_{\omega}^{2}\right]\delta\bm{\omega}=

\displaystyle g_{\omega}\delta\bm{j}-c_{\omega}g_{\omega}^{4}\left[\omega^{0}\right]_{\mathrm{GS}}^{2}\delta\bm{\omega}

(36)

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\rm{V}}g_{\rho}^{2}\left[\rho^{0}\right]_{\mathrm{GS}}^{2}g_{\omega}^{2}\delta\bm{\omega},

\displaystyle\left[-\Delta+m_{\rho}^{2}\right]\delta\bm{\rho}=

\displaystyle g_{\rho}\delta\bm{j}_{3}-\Lambda_{\rm{V}}g_{\rho}^{2}g_{\omega}^{2}\left[\omega_{0}\right]_{\mathrm{GS}}^{2}\delta\bm{\rho}.

Here, the subscript “GS” denotes the ground state static fields.
The relevant densities and space-like currents entering the Klein-Gordon equations are defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{A}\bar{\psi}_{i}(\bm{r})\psi_{i}(\bm{r}),

(37)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{B}}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{A}\bar{\psi}_{i}(\bm{r})\gamma^{0}\psi_{i}(\bm{r}),

\displaystyle\rho_{3,\mathrm{B}}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{A}\bar{\psi}_{i}(\bm{r})\tau_{3}\gamma^{0}\psi_{i}(\bm{r}),

\displaystyle\bm{j}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{A}\bar{\psi}_{i}(\bm{r})\bm{\gamma}\psi_{i}(\bm{r}),

(38)

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{3}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{A}\bar{\psi}_{i}(\bm{r})\tau_{3}\bm{\gamma}\psi_{i}(\bm{r}),

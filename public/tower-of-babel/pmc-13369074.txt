Real-World Characteristics and Treatment Patterns of Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide Monoclonal Antibody Users: a Japanese Claims Database-Based Study
Authors: Tsubasa Takizawa, Ryo Takemura, Kaname Ueda, Yasuhiro Miki, Chie Hashimoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionUnderstanding real-world calcitonin gene-related peptide monoclonal antibodies’ (CGRP mAbs) use in patients with migraine is necessary to potentially guide treatment strategies.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study analyzed administrative claims data from the IQVIA claims data for Japan. It included CGRP mAb-naïve individuals with confirmed migraine diagnosis and ≥ 1 CGRP mAb prescription (January 2021–June 2023). Index date was the first CGRP mAb prescription. Patient characteristics, treatment patterns, adherence (proportion of days covered ≥ 0.80), persistence, and migraine-related direct costs were assessed. Persistence was measured as the proportion of patients continuing treatment for ≥ 3 or ≥ 6 months (≤ 90-day gap between fills). Factors predicting adherence to CGRP mAb treatment were assessed using multivariate logistic regression. Subgroup analyses were conducted for galcanezumab users.ResultsOf the 1781 patients included (mean age 41.8 years [standard deviation, SD 10.3]; 79.3% female patients), 55.0% received galcanezumab, followed by fremanezumab (29.7%), and erenumab (15.3%). The prescription rates of concomitant acute and oral preventive medications were 86.1% and 51.3%, respectively, in the first 3 months after initiating CGRP mAb treatment but numerically decreased to 49.8% and 22.5%, respectively, in the fourth to sixth month. The index CGRP mAb persistence rates were 91.9% for ≥ 3 months and 76.9% for ≥ 6 months, and adherence rate was 51.7%. Multiple positive and negative predictors of adherence to CGRP mAbs were observed. Index to 3 months after initiating index CGRP mAbs, the median average monthly migraine-related costs were JPY 75,320 (Q1, Q3 61,593, 89,843) (USD 592), and non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs were JPY 15,702 (Q1, Q3 8845, 27,848) (USD 123). These costs tended to reduce or plateau for up to 12 months thereafter. The galcanezumab subgroup showed a similar trend.ConclusionsCGRP mAb adherence and ≥ 6 months persistence rates were 51.7% and 76.9%, respectively, in CGRP mAb-initiating adults with migraine in Japan. During CGRP mAb treatment, acute and oral preventive medication prescription rates appeared to reduce, while direct costs initially increased but appeared to reduce from the post-initiation peak and plateau over time, although the underlying reasons could not be determined from claims data.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40122-026-00843-w.Keywords: Migraine, Migraine prevention, Calcitonin gene-related peptide monoclonal antibody, Real-world, Japan

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Understanding the real-world usage of calcitonin gene-related peptide monoclonal antibodies (CGRP mAbs) is necessary to inform treatment strategies.This retrospective cohort study analyzed administrative claims data of individuals with confirmed migraine diagnosis who received a CGRP mAb prescription for the first time and described the patient characteristics, treatment patterns, adherence, persistence, factors predicting adherence to CGRP mAb treatment, and migraine-related direct costs. What was learned from the study? CGRP mAb adherence rate was 51.7%, and persistence rates were 91.9% at ≥ 3 months and 76.9% at ≥ 6 months in CGRP mAb-initiating adults with migraine in Japan.Acute and oral preventive medication prescription rates appeared to reduce after CGRP mAb initiation, while migraine-related direct costs initially increased after CGRP mAb initiation but appeared to reduce from the post-initiation peak and plateau over time.

Introduction

Migraine is a chronic multifactorial neurologic disorder characterized by recurrent and pulsating headache with moderate-to-severe pain often exacerbated by physical activity [1]. Its prevalence rate in Japan is 7.3–8.4% [2, 3]. Real-world studies in Japan have reported the burden of migraine in terms of comorbidities, including depression, post-traumatic stress disorder, anxiety disorders, and gastrointestinal disorders [4], poor health-related quality of life (HRQoL) [5–9], reduced work productivity [5–9], high healthcare resource utilization (HCRU) [8, 9], economic loss [7, 8], and stigma [10]. Moreover, an increase in monthly headache days tended to worsen HRQoL, decrease work productivity, and increase HCRU and indirect costs [5, 8]. Therefore, it is important to manage migraine and reduce disease progression by the timely administration of appropriate treatments [5, 8].

The Japanese Headache Society (JHS) Clinical Practice Guideline for Headache Disorders 2021 [11], the 2024 International Headache Society (IHS) global practice recommendations for the acute and preventive pharmacological treatment of migraine [12, 13], and the 2025 evidence-based guidelines for the pharmacological treatment of migraine, created by the Italian Society for the Study of Headache and the IHS [14], recommend acute treatments, including acetaminophen, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), triptans, and antiemetics, as well as preventive treatments, including antiepileptics, antidepressants, β-blockers, and calcium channel blockers for migraine management. Preventive treatment is recommended for individuals with ≥ 2 migraine attacks per month or ≥ 3 days of headache attacks per month that impact daily life [11].

Since 2021, new migraine-specific preventive medications such as calcitonin gene-related peptide monoclonal antibodies (CGRP mAbs: galcanezumab, fremanezumab, and erenumab) have been available in Japan [15]. Multiple studies have reported the real-world effectiveness of these medications [16–27], and guidelines for the promotion of optimal use (OUGL) of CGRP mAbs in Japan have been available since 2021 [28–30]. Additionally, the OUGL specifies that CGRP mAbs can only be prescribed by physicians with ≥ 5 years of headache treatment experience and board certification in the fields of headache, neurology, neurosurgery, or internal medicine or by physicians with less experience under the guidance of a qualified physician. Recent guidelines, such as the 2024 IHS global practice recommendations for the preventive pharmacological treatment of migraine [13] and the 2025 evidence-based guidelines for the pharmacological treatment of migraine [14] also support the use of CGRP mAbs for migraine prevention. However, the 2023 Observational Survey of the Epidemiology, Treatment, and Care of Migraine (OVERCOME) Japan second study reported that only 25.1% of individuals with migraine received oral preventive medications [5]. The use specifically of CGRP mAbs was much lower (2.2%) [5].

The 2021 American Headache Society Consensus Statement: Update on integrating new migraine treatments into clinical practice [31] and the JHS 2021 guideline [11] recommend evaluating the effectiveness of CGRP mAbs 3 months after initiation. Most Japanese physicians (60%–70%) adhere to the JHS guidelines and assess the efficacy of CGRP mAbs 3 months after initiation [32]. Furthermore, the European Headache Federation guideline on the use of monoclonal antibodies targeting the calcitonin gene-related peptide pathway for migraine prevention 2022 update [33] and the 2024 IHS global practice recommendations for the preventive pharmacological treatment of migraine [13] recommend continuing CGRP mAb treatment for 12–18 months. However, the Japanese and OUGL guidelines do not recommend an appropriate CGRP mAb treatment duration. Retrospective, observational studies from the USA reported patients persisted on CGRP mAbs for an average of > 200 days [34–36]. However, similar data for the Japanese population are limited [37].

In Japan, patients receiving CGRP mAbs are required to pay a copayment of approximately Japanese Yen (JPY) 13,000 (30% of the total cost) (equivalent to euro [EUR] 74 or US dollars (USD) 86 as of October 2025) per dose under the national health insurance system [32]. This financial burden may cause even patients who respond well to CGRP mAbs to discontinue treatment [32]. However, data on the continuation rates of CGRP mAbs in Japan are scarce; with only one study published [37]. Moreover, evidence regarding potential cost reduction associated with CGRP mAb treatment remains limited.

With the approval of CGRP mAbs in Japan [15], it becomes essential to understand the treatment patterns and associated costs among patients using these medications to guide migraine treatment strategies; however, limited evidence is available. Therefore, this real-world study aimed to describe the patient characteristics, treatment patterns, adherence and persistence to CGRP mAb, and direct costs among CGRP mAb-naïve patients with migraine in Japan, as well as identify the factors predicting adherence to CGRP mAb treatment.

Methods

Data Source

This retrospective cohort study analyzed administrative claims data which were obtained under license from IQVIA Solutions Japan G.K. (Tokyo, Japan) and reflect estimates of real-world activity. Copyright IQVIA. All rights reserved. This database (hereafter referred to as the IQVIA claims data for Japan) includes demographics, diagnoses, medications, treatments, and annual health check-up records since April 2013. It has anonymized data for approximately 3.5 million individuals, representing 8.4% of social health insurance coverage in Japan and 2.8% of the overall population. It primarily represents corporate employees of secondary and tertiary industries and their families, with an age distribution similar to the Japanese population (aged 0–64 years), but fewer individuals aged ≥ 65 years.

Study Design and Study Population

This study evaluated data from April 2013 to December 2023 (Fig. 1). CGRP mAb-naïve individuals with a confirmed diagnosis of migraine (International Classification of Diseases, 10th edition [ICD-10] code G43) between 1 April 2013 and 31 December 2023, who received at least one prescription of a CGRP mAb (Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical Classification [ATC] code N02CD01, N02CD02, and N02CD03) between 1 January 2021 and 30 June 2023 (cohort identification period), and enrolled continuously in the database from 12 months before the index date (baseline period) until the end of the study period were included. We collected data from 2013 onward to understand the duration since migraine diagnosis. In Japan, CGRP mAb prescriptions are regulated by OUGL, which mandates the confirmation of migraine diagnosis and clinical symptoms by qualified physicians as a prerequisite for reimbursement under the national health insurance system [28–30]. Therefore, the migraine diagnosis of patients receiving CGRP mAbs in this study can be considered reliable.

The date of the first CGRP mAb prescription was used as the index date. The index CGRP mAb treatment period was the time between the index date and the end of the CGRP mAb treatment or the end of the study period, whichever occurred first. The post-index CGRP mAb treatment period encompassed the time between the end of the index CGRP mAb treatment and the earliest date of (a) 6 months after the final index CGRP mAb prescription end date or (b) the end of the study period. Supplementary Table S1 summarizes the definitions of the terms mentioned in the study design.

The study was conducted per ethical principles originating from the Declaration of Helsinki and that were consistent with Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices. All applicable Japanese laws and regulations were followed. Since this was a non-interventional, retrospective study that used anonymized patient data, ethical review and informed consent were not required per the Japanese Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Health Research Involving Human Subjects. Data were obtained under license from IQVIA Solutions Japan G.K. (Tokyo, Japan). Copyright IQVIA. All rights reserved.

Variables and Outcomes

Demographic data such as age and sex, clinical characteristics such as the duration since migraine diagnosis to the index date, specialty of the index CGRP mAb prescriber, baseline comorbidities (Supplementary Material Table S2), and prescribed acute or oral preventive medications used in the baseline period (Supplementary Material Table S3), were reported. The medications were per the JHS 2021 guideline [11]; only preventive medications covered by national health insurance in Japan were included.

Treatment Patterns

Acute or oral preventive medications prescribed in the baseline period and concomitantly prescribed in the first 3 months and fourth to sixth month of the index CGRP mAb treatment period were described. Treatments administered in the baseline period and the first treatment in the post-index CGRP mAb treatment period were visualized using Sankey charts. The proportion of patients who switched to another CGRP mAb or oral preventive medication were also reported.

Adherence and Persistence to CGRP mAb

The persistence and adherence to the index CGRP mAb were assessed during the index CGRP mAb treatment period. Persistence was measured as the proportion of patients who continued treatment for ≥ 3 months or ≥ 6 months. Persistence was estimated using the number of days of continuous treatment from the index date until the end of the index treatment period, with a maximum allowable gap of 90 days between fills. Treatment was considered discontinued when the time between prescriptions exceeded the permissible gap. The treatment end date was estimated on the basis of the last date of supply of the index CGRP mAb (i.e., prescription end date = final prescription date of index CGRP mAb + days of supply). Treatment persistence was further described using Kaplan–Meier survival analysis to estimate the probability of treatment continuation over time. In the Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, the outcome of interest was time to discontinuation (days). Patients who were lost to follow-up or died during the observation period were censored.

Adherence was evaluated using proportion of days covered (PDC). PDC was defined as the ratio of the number of days exposed to the index CGRP mAb during the index treatment period to the total number of days in that period. The number of days of index CGRP mAb availability was determined using the last dispense date and the days’ supply. Overlapping days from successive prescriptions were not double counted. Patient adherence for PDC was defined as ≥ 0.80. This study reported the average PDC along with the proportion of patients with PDC ≥ 0.80.

Direct Costs

Direct costs were reported in the 3 months before the index date and index to 3 months, 3–6 months, 6–9 months, and 9–12 months after the index date. Average monthly migraine-related costs included emergency room costs, migraine-related procedural and/or hospitalization costs, and prescribed migraine medication costs including CGRP mAb costs. Average monthly non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs included emergency room costs, migraine-related procedural and/or hospitalization costs, and prescribed migraine medication costs excluding CGRP mAb costs. Costs were reported in JPY and equivalent USD. The equivalent USD values were calculated using the average JPY to USD exchange rate during the study period (2021–2023): USD 1 = JPY 127.2. This value was obtained from the World Bank data for 2021–2023 (2021: 109.75 JPY/USD; 2022: 131.50 JPY/USD; and 2023: 140.49 JPY/USD) [38].

Statistical Analysis

Data were summarized with descriptive statistics: means and standard deviations (SDs) or medians and interquartile ranges (IQR) reported for continuous variables and frequencies and percentages reported for categorical variables. Subgroup analyses were conducted for galcanezumab users for all outcomes except the factors predicting adherence.

We assessed the factors predicting adherence to CGRP mAb treatment (PDC ≥ 0.80) using multivariate logistic regression. This analysis included baseline demographic and clinical characteristics, comorbidities, and treatment-related variables. The 95% confidence intervals for the Kaplan–Meier survival estimates were calculated using Greenwood’s formula.

Missing data were not imputed. Statistical analyses were conducted and Sankey charts were created using SAS 9.4 and JMP 18 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA), respectively.

Results

Among the 253,912 patients diagnosed with migraine in the dataset, 1781 met the inclusion criteria (Fig. 2). On the index date, majority of the patients were prescribed galcanezumab (55.0%), followed by fremanezumab (29.7%), and erenumab (15.3%).

Patient Characteristics at Baseline

The mean age of CGRP mAb users was 41.8 years (SD 10.3) and the majority were female (79.3%) (Table 1). These patients initiated CGRP mAbs an average of 47.1 months (SD 32.6) after migraine diagnosis. Approximately one fourth of the CGRP mAb users had comorbid nonmigraine headache (26.1%). Additionally, neurotic, stress-related, and somatoform disorders (17.1%), mood (affective) disorders (13.5%), and sleep disorders (11.1%) were the top baseline comorbidities among CGRP mAb users (Table 1).

Persistence and Adherence in the Index CGRP mAb Treatment Period

Among index CGRP mAb and the subgroup of galcanezumab users, 91.9% and 92.1% persisted on treatment for ≥ 3 months, while 76.9% and 77.2% persisted for ≥ 6 months, respectively (Table 2). The median time to discontinuation was 9.0 months (IQR 8.0–10.0) and 9.0 months (IQR 9.0–10.0), respectively (Fig. 4). Using a PDC threshold of ≥ 0.80, 51.7% of index CGRP mAb and 55.0% of the subgroup of galcanezumab users were adherent to treatment (Table 2).

Predictors of Adherence to CGRP mAb Treatment

The positive predictors of adherence to CGRP mAb treatment were: older age at index date, male sex, use of galcanezumab as the index CGRP mAb treatment, baseline acute medication use, concomitant acute medication use, concomitant preventive medication use, and comorbid thyroid gland disorders (Fig. 5). The negative predictors of adherence to CGRP mAb treatment were: comorbid cluster headache, comorbid sleep disorders, and comorbid neuropathy.

Treatment Patterns in the Baseline Period and the First Treatment in the Post-Index CGRP mAb Treatment Period

In the baseline period, the majority of patients were prescribed both acute and oral preventive medication (64.2%), followed by prescribed acute medication only (24.0%) and prescribed oral preventive medication only (1.6%) (Fig. 6). Several patients (10.3%) were not on any prescribed medication. Most patients were prescribed galcanezumab (55.0%), followed by fremanezumab (29.7%), and erenumab (15.3%) as their index treatment (Fig. 2). Approximately 70% of patients on index CGRP mAbs continued treatment for at least 6 months during the observational period (Fig. 6).

Treatments administered in the baseline period and the first treatment in the post-index CGRP mAb treatment period. In the baseline period, no medication is defined as no prescribed medication. CGRP mAb calcitonin gene-related peptide monoclonal antibodies

After discontinuing the index CGRP mAb, 31.1% of patients were not prescribed any other medication. Several patients switched to oral preventive medication only (38.3%); 4.9% switched to another CGRP mAb, while 20.4% switched to another CGRP mAb with oral preventive medication; and 2.0% restarted the index CGRP mAb, while 3.4% restarted the index CGRP mAb with oral preventive medication (Fig. 6). The subgroup of galcanezumab users generally showed similar results as the overall CGRP mAb users (Fig. S1).

In CGRP mAb users coprescribed acute and oral preventive medication usage numerically decreased from 88.2% and 65.8% at baseline to 86.1% and 51.3% in the first 3 months of the index CGRP mAb treatment period, and then to 49.8% and 22.5% in the fourth to sixth month of the index CGRP mAb treatment period, respectively (Fig. 3a). In the subgroup of galcanezumab users, coprescribed acute and oral preventive medication usage numerically decreased from 87.4% and 66.8% at baseline to 86.2% and 52.6% in the first 3 months of the index galcanezumab treatment period and then to 52.1% and 23.7% in the fourth to sixth month of the index galcanezumab treatment period, respectively (Fig. 3b).

Direct Costs

At 3 months before initiating index CGRP mAbs, both the median average monthly migraine-related costs and monthly non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs were JPY 10,823 (Q1, Q3 6180, 18,473) (USD 85) (Fig. 7a, b). Index to 3 months after initiating index CGRP mAbs, the median average monthly migraine-related costs were JPY 75,320 (Q1, Q3 61,593, 89,843) (USD 592; Fig. 7a), and the median average monthly non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs were JPY 15,702 (Q1, Q3 8845, 27,848) (USD 123; Fig. 7b). At 3–6 months after initiating index CGRP mAbs, the median average monthly migraine-related costs were JPY 46,700 (Q1, Q3 30,613, 55,352) (USD 367; Fig. 7a), and the non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs were JPY 8997 (Q1, Q3 4370, 18,500) (USD 71; Fig. 7b). Both types of costs tended to decrease from the post-initiation peak and plateau thereafter. A similar trend was observed in the subgroup of galcanezumab users (Supplementary Figs. S2a–S2b).

Discussion

This real-world study describes the treatment patterns and direct costs among Japanese patients with migraine who initiated CGRP mAbs. The key findings were: (1) CGRP mAb, and specifically the subgroup of galcanezumab users, had > 75% persistence at ≥ 6 months and > 50% adherence (PDC ≥ 0.80), (2) concomitant prescribed acute and oral preventive medication use numerically decreased after initiating CGRP mAbs, and (3) initiating CGRP mAbs appeared to increase the median average monthly migraine-related and non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs up to 3 months, but it tended to reduce from the post-initiation peak and plateau over time.

The current study’s population was similar to previous Japanese real-world studies among patients with migraine in terms of age at the index date (approximately 40–45 years), high proportion of female patients (65.0%–79.3%), and presence of comorbidities such as anxiety, depression, and sleep disorders (10.4%–31.0%) [37, 39–41]. Previous studies suggest a strong correlation between these comorbidities and migraine [42, 43].

Consistent with a previous Japanese real-world study among patients with migraine [37], and CGRP mAb OUGLs [28–30], the current study showed prescription acute medication use was common before CGRP mAb initiation. The high use of triptans (> 80%) in the current study may indicate a high proportion of patients with moderate-to-severe migraine, as the JHS 2021 guideline recommends triptan use for adults with moderate or severe migraine attacks [11]. However, claims data do not capture migraine severity; therefore, we cannot confirm the migraine severity of the patients included in the current study.

One of the most notable findings in this study is the sustained use of CGRP mAbs, which was accompanied by a numerical reduction in the concomitant prescription of both acute and oral preventive migraine medications—observed in the first 3 months and fourth to sixth month of the index CGRP mAb treatment period. The tapering of concomitant oral preventive treatments and the observed reduction in non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs from 3 to 6 months after index CGRP mAb initiation is consistent with the possibility that patients may have been able to reduce reliance on other prophylactic therapies. These trends align with results from other studies, which have similarly reported decreased medication use following continued CGRP mAb treatment [34, 44]. However, without clinical outcome data, we cannot determine whether the decline in concomitant medication prescriptions is reflective of therapeutic benefit, physician preferences, or other factors.

The ≥ 6-month index CGRP mAb persistence rate in the current study (76.9%) was higher than a previous claims database study from Japan (64.4%) [37] and the USA (46.7%) [45]. The Japanese study had a similar study population as the current study, albeit from a different claims database, but the US study only included patients with chronic migraine. The current study reported adherence, i.e., PDC ≥ 0.80, for the whole index treatment period, unlike previous real-world studies from Japan and Korea that reported adherence only up to 12 months after CGRP mAb initiation [37, 46]. Still, the current study had a higher proportion of adherent CGRP mAb users (51.7%) than the aforementioned studies (43% and 40.7%) [37, 46]. While the difference with Takeshima et al. [37] could be due to use of a different claims database, the difference with Kang et al. [46] could be attributed to differing study designs (claims database versus a multicenter study). In the current study, index CGRP mAb users and the subgroup of galcanezumab users appeared to have similar persistence rates, adherence rates, and median time to discontinuation. As reported in a previous Japanese real-world study [24], no difference in monthly migraine day reduction over 12 months was observed among patients prescribed galcanezumab, erenumab, or fremanezumab. This suggests that these CGRP mAbs may have similar effectiveness in the real world.

Regarding factors associated with treatment persistence, the European multicenter, prospective, observational, registry-based EUREkA study (n = 1340, 24-month follow-up) reported that higher baseline monthly headache days, migraine with aura, depression, and obesity were associated with CGRP mAb discontinuation [47]. By contrast, previous Japanese studies reported the factors affecting treatment response but not persistence [24, 26, 48]. We observed a 76.9% persistence rate at ≥ 6 months but did not systematically examine the factors associated with treatment persistence. Therefore, future studies investigating predictors of CGRP mAb persistence in Japanese individuals with migraine are warranted.

We observed that concomitant use of acute medication, concomitant use of preventive medication, and baseline acute medication use, among other factors, were significant positive predictors of adherence to CGRP mAb treatment. These findings indicate that patients actively engaging in comprehensive migraine management demonstrate better treatment adherence, possibly reflecting the “healthy adherer” effect, a well-established epidemiological phenomenon where adherence to drug therapy possibly serves as a surrogate marker for overall healthy behavior [49]. We also identified male sex (aOR 1.31 [95% CI 1.02, 1.66]) and older age (aOR 1.02 [95% CI 1.01, 1.02]) as positive predictors of adherence to CGRP mAbs. The association with male sex is noteworthy given the female predominance in migraine in Japan [5, 6, 37, 39, 40]. Hence, male patients pursuing CGRP mAb treatment may represent a population with high disease burden, strong treatment motivation, adequate income for medical expenses, and autonomy in healthcare decision-making [50]. The positive association between older age and adherence may indicate that patients with a long disease duration but who achieved insufficient benefit from conventional treatments have greater motivation toward novel therapies. In contrast, we identified comorbid cluster headache, neuropathy, and sleep disorders as negative predictors of adherence to CGRP mAbs. Patients with these comorbidities may benefit from enhanced adherence support, including closer follow-up monitoring and consultation with specialists to treat these comorbidities.

Median average monthly migraine-related and non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs were highest in the first 3 months after index CGRP mAb initiation in the current study. In the first year of CGRP mAb approval in Japan, i.e., 2021, patients were required to visit the hospital to receive monthly subcutaneous injections (galcanezumab, fremanezumab, and erenumab) or every 3 months for quarterly fremanezumab injections [25]. CGRP mAb labeling information in Japan still mandates administration or supervision by a physician before transitioning to self-administration [51–53], usually after 3 months, which is the CGRP mAb efficacy evaluation period per the OUGL for CGRP mAbs [28–30]. Thus, hospital visit costs and self-injection management fees during the transition period may have contributed to the high initial costs. Varnado et al. [54] reported that the increase in total costs after CGRP mAb initiation, primarily due to increased prescription costs, also translates to cost savings through decreased acute and preventive migraine medication fills. This offset in costs is apparent in CGRP mAb users in the current study also, since median average monthly non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs temporarily increased relative to baseline after CGRP mAb initiation but decreased by 15.5% relative to baseline 9–12 months after the index date. However, several aspects of cost reduction could be attributed to treatment discontinuation and subsequent reduction of overall healthcare utilization or to economic considerations influencing prescribing patterns. Future studies with longer follow-up periods and stratified analyses by treatment continuation status may help to clarify the observed changes in costs.

Although current evidence strongly supports the use of CGRP mAbs for preventing migraine [55–58], gaps in clinical care still exist. The 2023 OVERCOME Japan second study, a cross-sectional, population-based, nationwide online survey, showed that only 2.2% of adults with migraine were prescribed CGRP mAbs [5]. Multiple reasons may have contributed to low CGRP mAb use. Patients with migraine may have hesitated in seeking care, not known the appropriate healthcare provider, been unaware of preventive medications [5], or not discussed their concerns appropriately [59]. High out-of-pocket CGRP mAb costs (an approximate copay cost of JPY 13,000 for each dose [32] [equivalent to EUR 74 or USD 86 as of October 2025]) may also have played a role. In fact, physicians reported that 24.4% of patients with migraine in Japan discontinued treatment owing to high costs, and 27.7% were concerned about the cost [32]. As observed in the current study, CGRP mAb initiation tends to increase both migraine-related and non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs in the first few months; however, further studies regarding longer term cost savings and potential impact on indirect costs, are warranted.

Strengths and Limitations

This study is one of the first of its kind in Japan and provides the latest data on treatment patterns, adherence, persistence, and direct costs associated with CGRP mAb initiation. This is a major strength of this study; however, this study also has multiple limitations. Since this is a claims database study, it cannot capture use of over-the-counter (OTC) medications, hence, we cannot understand the true use of acute medications. Nationwide online surveys such as the OVERCOME (Japan) second study are useful to understand the use of OTC medications [60]. Additionally, claims data are collected for billing purposes; therefore, other clinically relevant information such as migraine attack severity and frequency and reasons for treatment discontinuation (such as physician-directed discontinuation or patient-initiated cessation) are generally not captured. Further research with patient-reported data or electronic health records with clinical notes would be required to assess the reasons for discontinuation. Claims data also cannot confirm whether prescription changes were driven by improved clinical outcomes or other factors such as a general tendency to reduce polypharmacy after initiating a new treatment. Several prescribed acute or oral preventive medications may have been used to treat conditions other than migraine; therefore, the use of such medications must be interpreted with caution. However, we reported the proportions of patients with such comorbidities at baseline for clarity. For example, 5.9% and 5.7% of CGRP mAb users had comorbid epilepsy or hypertension. In addition, we could not calculate indirect costs arising from the burden of migraine and loss of work productivity as this information is not captured in claims data. Lastly, this database contains claims data of employees and their dependents; therefore, data on elderly patients with migraine are limited.

Conclusions

Using data from a large Japanese claims database, this study described the patient characteristics, treatment patterns, and direct costs of patients with migraine who initiated CGRP mAbs. More than half the patients were adherent to CGRP mAbs, with a ≥ 6 months persistence rate of 76.9%. Acute and oral preventive medication prescription rates appeared to reduce after patients initiated CGRP mAbs. CGRP mAb initiation initially increased monthly migraine-related and non-CGRP mAb migraine-related costs but it appeared to reduce from the post-initiation peak and plateau up to 12 months after CGRP mAb initiation. Future studies may investigate the reasons for CGRP mAb discontinuation and measure the indirect costs associated with CGRP mAbs.

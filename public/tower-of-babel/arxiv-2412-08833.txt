Non-minimally coupled quintessential inflation
Seong Chan Park

Abstract
We present a unified framework that simultaneously addresses the dynamics of early-time cosmic inflation and late-time cosmic acceleration within the context of a single scalar field non-minimally coupled to gravity. By employing an exponential coupling function and a scalar potential with dual asymptotic plateaus, our model naturally transitions from inflationary dynamics at small field values to a quintessence-like behavior at large field values. We derive the inflationary predictions for the spectral index ( n s n_{s} ) and tensor-to-scalar ratio ( r r ) in agreement with current observational constraints. For late-time acceleration, the model produces a viable dark energy component with an equation of state w ϕ w_{\phi} approaching − 1 -1 but retaining a measurable deviation that could serve as an observational signature. This work demonstrates that a single theoretical framework can reconcile both early inflation and the late-time accelerated expansion of the Universe.

2
Model

In this section, we introduce our model for the early time inflation and the late time acceleration with two asymptotic plateaus. We first introduce the general idea then provide a specific example.

2.1
General case

Our model in Jordan frame is based on the action for a scalar field
\phi
with a non-minimal coupling with Ricci scalar and a potential

\displaystyle S_{J}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g_{J}}\left(\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}\Omega^{2}(\phi)R_{J}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-V_{J}(\phi)\right),

(2)

where
\Omega^{2}(\phi)=1+K(\phi)
and
V_{J}(\phi)=V_{0}K(\phi)^{2}
to guarantee a plateau when
K(\phi)
is large. The Einstein frame is recovered by Weyl transformation :
g_{J,\mu\nu}\to g_{E,\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}g_{J,\mu\nu}
:

\displaystyle S_{E}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g_{E}}\left[\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}R_{E}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial h)^{2}-V_{E}(h)\right]

(3)

where the field
h
is canonically normalized and
R_{J}=\Omega^{2}(R_{E}-(3/2)g_{J}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\log\Omega^{2}\partial_{\nu}\log\Omega^{2}+3\square\log\Omega^{2})
. The Einstein-frame potential is given as

\displaystyle V_{E}(h(\phi))=\frac{V_{J}(h(\phi))}{\Omega^{4}(h(\phi))}=\frac{V_{0}K^{2}}{(1+K)^{2}}=V_{0}\left(1+K^{-1}\right)^{-2},

(4)

where the Jordan field
\phi(h)
is understood as an implicit function of the field
h
. The canonical field is explicitly obtained by integrating the relation
[
21
]

\displaystyle\frac{dh}{d\phi}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1+K+\tfrac{3}{2}M_{P}^{2}K_{,\phi}^{2}}{(1+K)^{2}}}=:F(\phi),

(5)

where
K_{,\phi}=\partial_{\phi}K(\phi)
is understood.

We focus on a potential that supports both early-time inflation and late-time dark energy, achieved through the presence of two asymptotic plateaus in the small and large field limits. To realize this, we consider
K(\phi)
as a
decreasing
function of the inflaton field, which asymptotically vanishes (
K\to 0
) at large field values. In this framework, early inflation occurs in the small field limit, where the potential is high, while the late-time cosmic acceleration is driven by a small dark energy density in the large field limit, where the potential is low.

\displaystyle V_{E}=\begin{cases}V_{0}(1-2K^{-1})\sim\rho_{\rm inf}&\text{at small field, $K\gg 1$},\\
V_{0}K^{2}\sim\rho_{\rm DE},&\text{at large field, $K\ll 1$}.\end{cases}

(6)

In general, the term
M_{P}^{2}K_{,\phi}^{2}
can take on any numerical magnitude. Therefore, a more detailed analysis requires specifying a definite functional form.

3
Early time inflation

Figure 2:
Potential and the slow-roll parameters

In the vicinity of the origin,
\phi\ll f\ln\xi
or
K=\xi e^{-\phi/f}\gg 1
, the potential becomes flat and potentially supports the early time inflation. In this regime (small-field limit), we find an approximate expression:

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}\approx K(\phi)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow F^{2}(\phi)\approx\frac{K+\tfrac{3}{2}M_{P}^{2}K_{\phi}^{2}}{K^{2}}=\frac{1}{K}+\frac{3}{2}M_{P}^{2}\left((\ln K)_{,\phi}\right)^{2}.

(9)

We also simplify
F(\phi)
as

\displaystyle F(\phi)\approx\sqrt{\frac{1}{\xi e^{-\phi/f}}+\frac{3M_{P}^{2}}{2f^{2}}}\simeq\frac{\sqrt{3/2}M_{P}}{f}.

The slow-roll parameters are given in the small field limit
K=\xi e^{-\phi/f}\gg 1
, and
M_{P}^{2}(K^{\prime})^{2}=(M_{P}/f)^{2}K^{2}\gg K
:

\displaystyle\epsilon\approx\frac{4}{3K^{2}}=\frac{4}{3\xi^{2}e^{-2\phi/f}},\quad\eta\approx-\frac{4}{3K}=-\frac{3}{3\xi e^{-\phi/f}}.

The number of e-foldings during inflation is calculated in the small field limit:

\displaystyle N_{e}\approx\int_{phi_{\text{end}}}^{phi_{*}}\frac{Fd\varphi}{M_{P}\sqrt{2\epsilon}}\approx\frac{3\xi}{4}\left(e^{-\phi_{*}/f}-e^{-\phi_{\rm end}/f}\right)\approx\frac{3}{4}K_{*}

(10)

where
*
is for the CMB pivot scale where
N_{e}=(50-60)
.

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle=1-6\epsilon_{*}+2\eta_{*}\approx 1-\frac{2}{N_{e}}-\frac{9}{2N_{e}^{2}},

(11)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=16\epsilon_{*}\approx\frac{12}{N_{e}^{2}},

(12)

\displaystyle A_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}M_{P}^{2}}\frac{V_{E}(s_{*})^{3}}{V_{E,s}(s_{*})^{2}}\approx\frac{\sqrt{3/2}}{36\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{M_{P}}{f}\right)\frac{V_{0}}{M_{P}^{4}}N_{e}^{2}.

(13)

Requesting
60
e-foldings, we determine the inflationary predictions for the spectral index and the tensor-to-scalar ratio

\displaystyle n_{s}=0.965,r=0.003.

(14)

It is noticed that these results exactly coincide with the results of the non-minimally coupled model of inflation with a general power law (See. Ref.
[
21
]
in detail.) Finally, we determine the parameter
V_{0}
by requesting the CMB normalization condition

\ln{\left(10^{10}A_{s}\right)}=3.044\pm 0.014
(TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing)
[
5
]
, leads:

\displaystyle\frac{V_{0}}{fM_{P}^{3}}\approx(1.67-1.71)\times 10^{-10}\times\left(\frac{N_{e}}{60}\right)^{-2}.

(15)

4
Late time Dark energy

In the large field limit,
K\ll 1
or
\phi\gg f\ln\xi
, the Einstein frame becomes indistinguishable from the Jordan frame as
\Omega^{2}\approx 1
and
F\approx 1
or
h\approx\phi
. The potential is a run-away type so that the energy density will go down as time goes on:

\displaystyle V_{E}=V_{0}\xi^{2}e^{-2\phi/f}.

(16)

Note that this is nothing but the quintessence potential considered in
[
50
]
, which has been extensively studied.

The slow-roll parameters are approximately constant in this limit:

\displaystyle\epsilon\simeq\frac{2M_{P}^{2}}{f^{2}},\eta\simeq\frac{4M_{P}^{2}}{f^{2}}.

(17)

The slow-roll approximation is valid when
f\gg M_{P}
. We estimate the size of the kinetic term using the slow-roll approximation

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}^{2}\approx\left(-\frac{V^{\prime}_{E}(\phi)}{3H}\right)^{2}\approx\frac{\Omega_{\phi}M_{P}^{2}V_{E}^{\prime 2}}{3V_{E}}=\frac{2}{3}V_{E}\Omega_{\phi}\epsilon,

(18)

which leads to the equation of state as

\displaystyle w_{\phi}=\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-V_{E}(\phi)}{\tfrac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+V_{E}(\phi)}\approx-1+\frac{2\Omega_{\phi}}{3}\epsilon\approx-1+\frac{4\Omega_{\phi}}{3}\left(\frac{M_{P}}{f}\right)^{2}.

(19)

shows the equation of state for our model under the slow-roll approximation. For comparison, we also include recent results from DESI BAO+CMB+PantheonPlus
[
10
]
. In the large
f
limit, the predicted value approaches
-1
, consistent with the cosmological constant; however, deviations from
-1
are possible. Such deviations would serve as a distinctive signal of the quintessence field
\phi
. Notably, the model remains fully compatible with recent data for
f\gtrsim 7
.

We now discuss the trajectory of quintessence field
\phi
in a large-field limit with respect to the number of e-foldings
N
. We set the current universe by
N=0
so that
N<0
in an earlier time.

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi}{dN}=\frac{d\phi}{dt}\frac{dt}{dN}\simeq-\frac{V_{E,\phi}}{3H^{2}}\simeq-\frac{\Omega_{\phi}M_{P}^{2}V_{E,\phi}}{V_{E}}=\frac{2M_{P}^{2}\Omega_{\phi}}{f}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\phi(N)\simeq\phi_{i}+\frac{2M_{P}^{2}\Omega_{\phi}}{f}(N-N_{i}).

(20)

where
\phi_{i}\equiv\phi(N=N_{i})
set at an earlier moment with
N_{i}<0
. The energy density of quintessence
\rho_{\phi}
with respect to
N
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(N)\simeq V_{E}(\phi(N))\simeq\frac{V_{0}\xi^{2}}{e^{2\phi(N)/f}}.

(21)

Finally, matching the Hubble parameter at the current Universe
N=0
tells us

\displaystyle V_{E}\approx 3M_{P}^{2}H^{2}\Omega_{\phi}\Rightarrow\phi_{0}=\phi(N=0)\approx\frac{f}{2}\ln\left[\frac{V_{0}\xi^{2}}{3M_{P}^{2}H^{2}\Omega_{\phi}}\right].

(22)

Taking
\rho_{\rm crit}=3M_{P}^{2}H^{2}\sim m_{\nu}^{4}
with the neutrino mass scale
m_{\nu}\sim 10^{-3}
eV and the CMB normalization condition
V_{0}\sim 10^{-10}fM_{P}^{3}
given in Eq. (
15
), we read

\displaystyle\phi_{0}\simeq\frac{f}{2}\ln\left[\frac{10^{-10}fM_{P}^{3}\xi^{2}}{\Omega_{\phi}m_{\nu}^{4}}\right].

(23)

5
Summary and conclusions

In this work, we have proposed a unified theoretical framework that effectively addresses both early-time inflation and late-time dark energy using a single scalar field non-minimally coupled to gravity. By employing a carefully constructed potential with dual asymptotic plateaus, we demonstrated how the model transitions seamlessly from inflationary dynamics at small field values to a quintessence-like regime at large field values.

The model achieves compatibility with current observational constraints on inflation, producing predictions for the spectral index (
n_{s}\approx 0.965
) and tensor-to-scalar ratio (
r\approx 0.003
) that align with Planck and BICEP/Keck data. Furthermore, the late-time dynamics naturally yield a dark energy equation of state approaching
w_{\phi}\to-1
, with measurable deviations providing a potential observational signature of the underlying quintessence mechanism.

Our results highlight the potential for a single scalar field to unify the seemingly disparate phenomena of early and late cosmic acceleration within a consistent theoretical framework. This approach not only addresses the fine-tuning challenges inherent in conventional models but also opens avenues for new observational tests of scalar-field dynamics across cosmic epochs. Future work will focus on exploring the implications of this model in varying cosmological scenarios and its sensitivity to alternative formulations of gravity.

Finally, we comment on the cosmological constant problem. Within our framework, the problem can be reformulated as
\rho_{\varphi,0}=V_{0}\xi^{2}/e^{2\phi_{0}/f}\ll M_{P}^{4}
. This condition can be satisfied by assuming a large field value for
\phi_{0}
, specifically
\phi_{0}\sim\mathcal{O}(100)f
.

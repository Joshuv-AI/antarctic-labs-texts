Parallel (co-)tractors and the geometry of first BGG solutions on almost Grassmannian structures
Zhangwen Guo

Abstract
We study the standard tractor bundle and the standard cotractor bundle of an almost Grassmann structure: We provide explicit formulae for their splitting operators, first BGG operators as well as prolongation connections. We characterize parallel tractors and cotractors as well as the solutions of the BGG operators in standard geometric terms. Moreover, we describe the geometry canonically endowed on the zero locus of a solution of the first BGG operators.



1
Our results

Assume that
n>2
. An almost Grassmannian structure (AG-structure) of type
(2,n)
on a
2\,n
-dimensional manifold
M
is defined by two auxiliary vector bundles
E\to M
and
F\to M
of rank
2
and of rank
n
, respectively, and isomorphisms

\displaystyle E^{*}\otimes F\cong TM\quad\text{and}\quad\wedge^{2}E^{*}\cong\wedge^{n}\,F.

(1)

We denote this structure by
(M,E,F)
. Every AG-structure
(M,E,F)
comes with a collection of so-called Weyl connections. These are precisely those pairs of connections on
(E,F)
which are compatible with the second isomorphism in (
1
) and, via the first isomorphism in (
1
), induce a connection on
TM
whose torsion
\tau\in\Omega^{2}(M,TM)
satisfies specific properties and is, in particular, totally trace-free; see (
8
). There is a canonical contraction of
\tau\otimes\tau
resulting in a
(0,2)
-tensor field; see (
14
). This contraction is clearly symmetric. We will denote it by

\displaystyle tr(i_{\tau}\tau)\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\,T^{*}M).

(2)

On the other hand, every AG-structure
(M,E,F)
of type
(2,n)
carries a so-called standard tractor bundle
\mathcal{T}
, obtained for example from the canonical Cartan geometry determined by the AG-structure. It is a vector bundle on
M
coming with a canonical connection as well as a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to E\to\mathcal{T}\to F\to 0.

The general theory developed in
[
5
]
gives rise to a so-called first splitting operator

L:\Gamma(F)\to\Gamma(\mathcal{T})

and a so-called first Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand (BGG) operator

D:\Gamma(F)\to\Omega^{1}(M,F).

If a section of
\mathcal{T}
is parallel, it is necessarily of the form
L\,\eta
for some
\eta\in\Gamma(F)
such that
D\,\eta=0
. In the current paper, we obtain the explicit expression of these operators in
Proposition

5
and prove in
Theorem

8
that the space of parallel sections of
\mathcal{T}
is precisely

\{L\,\eta:\eta\in\Gamma(F),D\,\eta=0\text{ and }tr(i_{\tau}\tau)(\eta)=0\}\subseteq\Gamma(\mathcal{T})

where we have viewed

tr(i_{\tau}\tau)\in\Gamma(F^{*}\otimes(E\otimes E\otimes F^{*})).

We then focus on studying the solutions

\{\eta\in\Gamma(F):D\,\eta=0\}

of the first BGG operator associated to
\mathcal{T}
. In
Theorem

9
we construct a prolongation connection
\hat{\nabla}^{\mathcal{T}}
on
\mathcal{T}
such that

\displaystyle D\,\eta=0\quad\iff\quad\hat{\nabla}^{\mathcal{T}}(L\,\eta)=0

for every
\eta\in\Gamma(F)
. There, we also describe a Cartan geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\hat{\omega})
whose underlying geometry is
(M,E,F)
and such that the prolongation connection
\hat{\nabla}^{\mathcal{T}}
is the tractor connection canonically derived from the Cartan connection
\hat{\omega}
. For convenience, we develop the rest of the results assuming in addition that
M
is connected. Let
0\neq\eta\in\Gamma(F)
such that
D\,\eta=0
. We prove in
Corollary

11
that

\{x\in M:\eta(x)\neq 0\}

is an open dense subset of
M
. We prove in
Proposition

12
that if
\eta\in\Gamma(F)
is nowhere vanishing, then
D\,\eta=0
if and only if
\nabla\eta=0
for some Weyl connection
\nabla
. Now consider the subset

N=\{x\in M:\eta(x)=0\}\subseteq M.

We prove in
Theorem

15
that
N
is an embedded submanifold of
M
of codimension
n
.
We also prove in
Theorem

15
that for every
x\in N
, there holds

T_{x}N=\ell_{x}\otimes F_{x}\subseteq T_{x}M

where
\ell_{x}\in E_{x}^{*}
is the one-dimensional subspace annihilating
(L\,\eta)(x)\in E_{x}\setminus\{0\}
. Moreover, we prove in
Theorem

16
that the zero locus
N
comes with a canonical projective structure.

All results above has a corresponding version for the standard cotractor bundle
\mathcal{T}^{*}
presented in the same locations as mentioned. Here, the zero locus
\tilde{N}
for a solution of the first BGG operator is a
2\,(n-1)
-dimensional embedded submanifold of
M
carrying an AG-structure of type
(2,n-1)
.

2
Parabolic geometries and Weyl structures

In this section, we recall that every AG-structure
(M,E,F)
of type
(2,n)
comes with a canonical parabolic geometry, from which we may derive the fundamental invariants as well as all Weyl objects by certain procedures according to a general theory. We refer the reader to, e.g.,
[
4
]
for detailed background.

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
P
a closed Lie subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}
.

Definition 1
.

A Cartan geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
of type
(G,P)
is a principal
P
-bundle
\mathcal{G}\to M
together with a Cartan connection
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g})
, i.e., a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued one-form on
\mathcal{G}
such that (i)
\omega
is
P
-equivariant with respect to the principal right action and the adjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
restricted to
P\subseteq G
, (ii)
\omega(\zeta_{X})=X
where
\zeta_{X}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G})
is the fundamental vector field generated by
X\in\mathfrak{p}
and (iii)
T\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow[\cong]{(\pi,\omega)}\mathcal{G}\times\mathfrak{g}
where
\pi:T\mathcal{G}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}
is the natural projection.

The curvature of the Cartan connection is the two-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g})
given by
\kappa(\xi,\eta)=d\omega(\xi,\eta)+[\omega(\xi),\omega(\eta)]
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G})
.

Note that the curvature
\kappa\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g})
is
H
-equivariant and horizontal.
In particular, using the isomorphism

TM\cong\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}

induced by the Cartan connection
\omega:T\mathcal{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
, the Cartan curvature can be expressed as an
P
-equivariant map
\kappa:\mathcal{G}\to L(\wedge^{2}\,\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{g})
and as a two-form
\kappa\in\Omega^{2}(M,\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathfrak{g})
.

Now let

\displaystyle P=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}*&&*&\\
\hline\cr&&&\\
0&&*&\\
&&&\end{array}\right)\subseteq G=SL(n+2,\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle G_{0}=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}*&&0&\\
\hline\cr&&&\\
0&&*&\\
&&&\end{array}\right)\subseteq P\quad\text{and}\quad P_{+}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\mathbb{I}_{2}&&*&\\
\hline\cr&&&\\
0&&\mathbb{I}_{n}&\\
&&&\end{array}\right)\subseteq P.

The block size is
(2,n)\times(2,n)
. Additionally, the Lie algebra of
SL(n+2,\mathbb{R})
is endowed with a
|1|
-grading

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}(n+2,\mathbb{R})=\mathfrak{g}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\mathfrak{g}_{0}&&\mathfrak{g}_{1}&\\
\hline\cr&&&\\
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}&&\mathfrak{g}_{0}&\\
&&&\end{array}\right).

We also use the following notation.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1},\quad\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\mathfrak{p}_{+}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Thus
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{+}
are Lie algebras of
P,G_{0}
and
P_{+}
, respectively. Since
P
is a so-called parabolic subgroup of
SL(n+2,\mathbb{R})
, every Cartan geometry of type
(SL(n+2,\mathbb{R}),P)
is called a parabolic geometry of type
(SL(n+2,\mathbb{R}),P)
.

The Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
induces a
P
-invariant identification

(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})^{*}\cong\mathfrak{p}_{+}.

Any
G
-representation
\mathbb{V}
gives rise to a complex of
P
-modules with differentials

\partial^{*}_{\mathbb{V}}:\wedge^{k+1}\,\mathfrak{p}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\wedge^{k}\,\mathfrak{p}_{+}\otimes\mathbb{V}

given by

\displaystyle\partial^{*}_{\mathbb{V}}(Z_{0}\wedge\dots\wedge Z_{k}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\,Z_{0}\wedge\dots\wedge\hat{Z_{i}}\wedge\dots\wedge Z_{k}\otimes(Z_{i}\,v)

(7)

for all
Z_{0},\dots,Z_{n}\in\mathfrak{p}_{+}
and
v\in\mathbb{V}
. Here, the hat indicates that the object is omitted.

A parabolic geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
of type
(SL(n+2,\mathbb{R}),P)
with curvature

\kappa:\mathcal{G}\to\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{p}_{+}\otimes\mathfrak{g}

is said to be normal if and only if
\partial^{*}_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\kappa=0
.

We recall the following categorical correspondence which follows from
[
4
, Subsection 4.1.3 and Theorem 3.1.14]
.

Lemma 2
.

Every parabolic geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
of type
(SL(n+2,\mathbb{R}),P)
has an underlying AG-structure

(M,E=\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathbb{R}^{2},F=\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathbb{R}^{n+2}/\mathbb{R}^{2})

of type
(2,n)
.

Conversely, for any AG-structure
(M,E,F)
of type
(2,n)
, there is, up to isomorphisms, a unique normal parabolic geometry of type
(SL(n+2,\mathbb{R}),P)
whose underlying geometry is
(M,E,F)
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
be the normal parabolic geometry of type
(SL(n+2,\mathbb{R}),P)
canonically associated to a given AG-structure
(M,E,F)
. Then the curvature of the Cartan connection
\omega
descends to the so-called harmonic curvature

\kappa_{H}:\mathcal{G}\to\ker\,\partial^{*}_{\mathfrak{g}}/\mathrm{im}\,\partial^{*}_{\mathfrak{g}}.

For
n>2
,
\kappa_{H}
decomposes into

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\in\Gamma\left((\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\,E\otimes E^{*})_{o}\otimes(\wedge^{2}\,F^{*}\otimes F)_{o}\right)\subseteq\Omega^{2}(M,TM)\quad\text{and}

(8)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\,E\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\,F^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{sl}(F))\subseteq\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{sl}(F)).

Here, the natural decomposition

\wedge^{2}\,T^{*}M=\wedge^{2}\,(E\otimes F^{*})=(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\,E\otimes\wedge^{2}\,F^{*})\oplus(\wedge^{2}\,E\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\,F^{*})

is used and

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\,E\otimes E^{*})_{o}\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\,E\otimes E^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad(\wedge^{2}\,F^{*}\otimes F)_{o}\subseteq\wedge^{2}\,F^{*}\otimes F

denote the trace-free component. Stretching notation, we call
\tau
the harmonic torsion, or, simply, the torsion, of
(M,E,F)
and
\rho
the harmonic curvature of
(M,E,F)
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
be the normal parabolic geometry canonically associated to an AG-structure
(M,E,F)
of type
(2,n)
. Since
P/P_{+}\cong G_{0}
,
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mathcal{G}/P_{+}
is a principal
G_{0}
-bundle. Note that

\displaystyle E\cong\mathcal{G}_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad F\cong\mathcal{G}_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathbb{R}^{n}

(9)

encode precisely every local trivializations of
(E,F)
preserving the second defining isomorphism in (
1
). This reflects the fact that the Cartan connection
\omega
descends to a soldering form
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{-1})
and thus
(\mathcal{G}_{0},\theta)
is the
G_{0}
-structure which equivalently encodes
(M,E,F)
. We refer the reader to, e.g.,
[
4
, Proposition 3.1.15 (2)]
for details.

Consider the natural projection
\mathcal{G}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{0}
. It admit
G_{0}
-equivariant sections

\sigma:\mathcal{G}_{0}\to\mathcal{G},

called the Weyl structures of
(M,E,F)
.
Since the block decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
is
G_{0}
-invariant, we may interpret the pullback of the Cartan connection
\omega
and its curvature
\kappa
in each block component as tensors on
(M,E,F)
. It is easy to see that,
\sigma^{*}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}_{-1}}
and
\sigma^{*}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}_{-1}}
are independent on the choice of a Weyl structure. We obtain the following Weyl objects associated to the Weyl structure
\sigma
.

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}

\displaystyle\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0})^{G_{0}}\text{ (the Weyl connection)}

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}=\sigma^{*}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

\displaystyle\in\Omega^{1}(M,T^{*}M)\text{ (the Rho tensor)}

\displaystyle(W,W^{\prime})=\sigma^{*}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}

\displaystyle\in\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{s}(L(E,E)\oplus L(F,F)))\text{ (the Weyl tensor)}

\displaystyle Y=\sigma^{*}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

\displaystyle\in\Omega^{2}(M,T^{*}M)\text{ (the Cotton-York tensor)}.

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}(L(E,E)\oplus L(F,F)))=\Big(L(E,E)\oplus L(F,F)\Big)\cap\mathfrak{sl}(E\oplus F).

(11)

The Weyl connection
\sigma^{*}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
is a principal connection on
\mathcal{G}_{0}
, thus induces a pair of connections

(\nabla^{E},\nabla^{F})

on
(E,F)
. The Weyl tensor
(W,W^{\prime})
is decomposed into

\displaystyle W\in\Omega^{2}(M,L(E,E))\quad\text{and}\quad W^{\prime}\in\Omega^{2}(M,L(F,F)).

Note that the principal connections on
\mathcal{G}_{0}
are equivalent to all pairs
(\nabla^{E},\nabla^{F})
of connections on
(E,F)
compatible with the defining isomorphism
\wedge^{2}\,E^{*}\cong\wedge^{n}\,F
. In fact, it follows from, e.g.,
[
4
, Theorems 3.1.12 and 5.2.3]
that
\sigma^{*}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}_{-1}}
, the harmonic torsion
\tau
and the torsion of any Weyl connection on
TM
are the same section of
\Omega^{2}(M,TM)
. Moreover, since
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
is
|1|
-graded, there is a one-to-one correspondence between Weyl structures and Weyl connections of
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
; see, e.g.,
[
4
, Proposition 5.1.1 and Proposition 5.1.6]
.
This is how the characterization of Weyl connections described at the beginning of
Section

1
is obtained.

3
Weyl tensor and
tr(i_{\tau}\tau)

Let
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
be a parabolic geometry of type
(G,P)
. Consider the canonical extension

\mathcal{G}
to a principal
G
-bundle. There is a unique principal connection on

whose pullback under

\omega
; see, e.g.,
[

G
-representation

gives rise to a so-called tractor bundle

\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{G}\times_{P}G)\times_{G}\mathbb{V}=\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathbb{V}

is induced by the principal connection mentioned above. It turns out that

is equivalent to the

\displaystyle df_{s}(\tilde{\xi})+\omega(\tilde{\xi})\,f_{s}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{V}.

Here,
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is lifted to
\tilde{\xi}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G})
and

is equivalent to a
P
-equivariant map

.

Assume in addition that
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
is normal. Given any Weyl structure
\sigma:\mathcal{G}_{0}\to\mathcal{G}
, we may express the tractor connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
in terms of the following Weyl objects associated to

to the natural Lie algebra representations

[

that there holds

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}_{\xi}s

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\xi}s+\xi\bullet s+\mathrm{P}(\xi)\bullet s

and
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Following the convention of Penrose abstract index notation, we use unprimed upper indices for

\displaystyle W=W{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{C}_{D},\quad W^{\prime}=W^{\prime}{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{C^{\prime}}_{D^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau=\tau{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{C^{\prime}}_{D}.

\displaystyle tr(W){}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=W{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{I}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{I},

\displaystyle tr(W^{\prime}){}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{B^{\prime}}

(See, e.g.,

Proposition 4

\displaystyle\tau{}^{B}_{J^{\prime}}{}^{J}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{I^{\prime}}_{I}\,\tau{}^{I}_{I^{\prime}}{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{J^{\prime}}_{J}+tr(W){}^{B}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{A}_{B^{\prime}}+tr(W){}^{A}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{A^{\prime}}-(n+2)\,tr(W){}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{B^{\prime}}=0.

Recall from (
8
) that
\tau{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{C^{\prime}}_{D}=\tau{}^{B}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{A}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{C^{\prime}}_{D}
on an AG-structure of type
(2,n)
,

. Hence
\tau{}^{B}_{J^{\prime}}{}^{J}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{I^{\prime}}_{I}\,\tau{}^{I}_{I^{\prime}}{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{J^{\prime}}_{J}=tr(i_{\tau}\tau){}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B}_{B^{\prime}}
. Symmetrizing and alternating the indices, we obtain

\displaystyle tr(i_{\tau}\tau){}^{(A}_{(A^{\prime}}{}^{B)}_{B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=n\,tr(W){}^{(A}_{(A^{\prime}}{}^{B)}_{B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle tr(i_{\tau}\tau){}^{[A}_{[A^{\prime}}{}^{B]}_{B^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle=(n+4)\,tr(W){}^{[A}_{[A^{\prime}}{}^{B]}_{B^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle tr(i_{\tau}\tau){}^{(A}_{[A^{\prime}}{}^{B)}_{B^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle=tr(W){}^{(A}_{[A^{\prime}}{}^{B)}_{B^{\prime}]}=0

\displaystyle tr(i_{\tau}\tau){}^{[A}_{(A^{\prime}}{}^{B]}_{B^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=tr(W){}^{[A}_{(A^{\prime}}{}^{B]}_{B^{\prime})}=0.

This proves the statement of the proposition.

\displaystyle 0\to E\to\mathcal{T}\to F\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad 0\to F^{*}\to\mathcal{T}^{*}\to E^{*}\to 0.

(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)

\sigma

\bullet

\sigma

\hat{\sigma}

Proposition 5
.

Notation as above. Then the splitting operator and the first BGG operator of the standard tractor bundle

are given by

\displaystyle L:\Gamma(F)\to\Gamma(\mathcal{T}),\quad\eta\mapsto(\eta,-\frac{1}{n}\,tr(\nabla\eta))_{\sigma}

\displaystyle L:\Gamma(E^{*})\to\Gamma(\mathcal{T}^{*}),\quad\varphi\mapsto(\varphi,\frac{1}{2}\,tr(\nabla\varphi))_{\sigma}

\displaystyle D:\Gamma(E^{*})\to\Gamma(F^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{sl}(E)),\quad\,\varphi\mapsto(\nabla\varphi)_{o},

Here,
\nabla
is the Weyl connection of the Weyl structure
\sigma

Proof.

Notation as in the paragraphs preceding the statement of the proposition. Note that the restriction of

to

in the language of
[
5

\bullet

and

\bullet

for all

; see
[
5

\displaystyle(id_{T^{*}M}\otimes\Pi)(\nabla^{\mathcal{T}}(L\,\eta))=(\nabla{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}\eta)^{B^{\prime}}+\xi^{A}\,\delta^{B^{\prime}}_{A^{\prime}}

has trace

The first BGG operator

Remark 10
.

By (

Analogous conclusions hold for the first BGG operator

associated to the standard cotractor bundle

.

Recall that a parallel section of a tractor bundle yields a partition of the base manifold into the so-called curved orbits. For the case of the standard tractor of an AG-structure
(M,E,F)
of type

\displaystyle\{0\},\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{n}:w\neq 0\}.

In particular, assume that

and

is a parallel section of the standard tractor bundle. Then

\displaystyle\{x\in M:\eta(x)=0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{x\in M:\eta(x)\neq 0\}.

(28)

The case for the standard cotractor bundle is analogous. We study the spaces (

Now we move on to study the other space defined in (
28
), namely, the zero locus
\{x\in M:\eta(x)=0\}
of a nonzero solution of
D
and its cotractor analog. First, we develop in
Lemma

13
a vector bundle version of the regular level set theorem. Precisely, we consider any vector bundle
V\to M
on any manifold
M
with no additional assumption and characterize every section
\sigma\in\Gamma(V)
that is a defining section for embedded submanifolds of
M
; see, e.g.,
[
3
, Definition 2.16]
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
V\to M
be a vector bundle of rank
k
on a manifold
M
and
\nabla
a linear connection on
V
. If
\sigma\in\Gamma(V)
is a section such that

N=\{x\in M:\sigma(x)=0\}

is nonempty and

\nabla\sigma(x):T_{x}M\to V_{x}

is surjective for every
x\in N
, then
N
is a regular, i.e., embedded, submanifold of
M
with codimension
k
. Moreover, there holds

T_{x}N=\ker(\nabla\sigma(x))\subseteq T_{x}M

for every
x\in N
.

Proof.

Observe that
N\subseteq M
is an embedded submanifold if and only if there exists a covering
\mathcal{U}
of
M
consisting of open subsets of
M
such that
N\cap U\subseteq U
is an embedded submanifold for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
. Thus we may assume without loss of generality that
V
admits a global frame
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in\Gamma(V)
. We write
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma^{i}\,s_{i}
where
\sigma^{i}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. For
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, there holds

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi}\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(D\,\eta^{i}(\xi)s_{i}+\sigma^{i}\nabla_{\xi}s_{i}\right).

Observe that
x\in N
if and only if
x
is in the zero locus of
(\sigma^{1},\dots,\sigma^{k})\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}^{k})
. In this case,
\nabla\sigma(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\sigma^{i}(x)s_{i}(x)
is assumed to be surjective and thus
(\sigma^{1},\dots,\sigma^{n})
is a submersion at the point
x
. In particular, it follows from the regular level set theorem given in, e.g.,
[
11
, Corollary 5.14]
that
N
is an embedded submanifold of
M
with codimension
k
. Moreover, it follows from
[
11
, Proposition 5.37]
that for
x\in N
, the joint kernel of
\{D\,\eta^{i}|_{x}:i=1,\dots,k\}
lies in
T_{x}N
. That is,
\ker(\nabla\sigma(x))\subseteq T_{x}N
. Counting dimensions, we conclude that
\ker(\nabla\sigma(x))=T_{x}N
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We also recall the following geometric fact.

Lemma 14
.

Let

is either an immersed submanifold or an embedded submanifold, then

\displaystyle V|_{N}=\bigcup_{x\in N}V_{x}

on

\nabla^{V|_{N}}

Proof.

That

is a vector bundle is precisely the statement of
[

. Now let

be two sections such that

on

induce the same one-form in

,

.
∎

Now we are ready to prove that the zero locus of a BGG solution is an embedded submanifold and describe its tangent bundle.

Theorem 15
.

Let
(M,E,F)
be an AG-structure of type
(2,n)
where the base space
M
is connected.

(i)

Assume that
0\neq\eta\in\Gamma(F)
is a solution of
D
such that

N=\{x\in M:\eta(x)=0\}

is nonempty. Denote by
\ell\subseteq E^{*}|_{N}
the line subbundle annihilating the nowhere vanishing section
(L\,\eta)|_{N}\in\Gamma(E|_{N})
.
Then
N
is an
n
-dimensional embedded submanifold of
M
such that

T_{x}N=\ell_{x}\otimes F_{x}\subseteq T_{x}M

for all
x\in N
.

(ii)

Assume that
0\neq\varphi\in\Gamma(E^{*})
is a solution of
D
such that

\tilde{N}=\{x\in M:\varphi(x)=0\}

is nonempty. Denote by
\tilde{F}\subseteq F|_{N}
the kernel of the nowhere vanishing section
(L\,\varphi)|_{\tilde{N}}\in\Gamma(F^{*}|_{N})
. Then
\tilde{N}
is a
2\,(n-1)
-dimensional embedded submanifold of
M
such that

T_{x}\tilde{N}=E^{*}_{x}\otimes\tilde{F}_{x}\subseteq T_{x}M

for all
x\in\tilde{N}
.

Proof.

Let
\eta
satisfy the assumptions in (i). Then
D\,\eta=(\nabla\eta)_{o}=0
implies that
\nabla\eta\in\Gamma(E\otimes F^{*}\otimes F)
is a pure trace given by

\nabla{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}\eta^{B^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{n}\delta{}^{B^{\prime}}_{A^{\prime}}\nabla{}^{A}_{I^{\prime}}\eta^{I^{\prime}}.

On the other hand, we have proved in
Theorem

9
(i) that
D\,\eta=0
if any only if
L\,\eta
is parallel with respect to the connection
\hat{\nabla}^{\mathcal{T}}
. Since
M
is connected and
\eta\neq 0
,
(L\,\eta)^{A^{\prime}A}=(\eta^{A^{\prime}},-\frac{1}{n}\nabla{}^{A}_{I^{\prime}}\eta^{I^{\prime}})
is nowhere vanishing. Hence
(L\,\eta)|_{N}
is a nowhere vanishing section of
E|_{N}
. In particular, for every
x\in M
such that
\eta(x)=0
, there exists
\psi(x)_{A}\in E^{*}_{x}
such that

c=\frac{1}{n}\psi(x)_{A}\nabla{}^{A}_{I^{\prime}}\eta^{I^{\prime}}\neq 0.

Hence

\psi(x)_{A}\nabla{}^{A}_{A^{\prime}}\eta^{B^{\prime}}(x)=c\,\delta{}^{B^{\prime}}_{A^{\prime}}:F_{x}\xrightarrow{\cong}F_{x}

is a linear isomorphism and so
\nabla\eta(x):T_{x}M\to F_{x}
is surjective. The kernel of
\nabla\eta(x)
is precisely
\ell_{x}\otimes F_{x}

Lemma

13
.

(ii) is proved in analogous steps.
∎

Moreover, we describe the geometric structures on
N
and
\tilde{N}
. Recall that a projective structure on a manifold
N
is a projectively equivalence class of linear connections on
TN
. Two connections
\nabla,\hat{\nabla}
on

are said to be projectively equivalent if there exists
\Upsilon\in\Omega^{1}(N)

\hat{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}\xi_{2}-\nabla_{\xi_{1}}\xi_{2}=\Upsilon(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}+\Upsilon(\xi_{2})\xi_{1}
for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
. It follows immediately that connections in the same projectively equivalence class have the same torsion, called the torsion of the projective structure.

Theorem 16

(Geometric structures on the zero loci)
.

Notation as in
Theorem

15
. Denote by
\tau
the torsion of
(M,E,F)
.

(i)

(\nabla^{E},\nabla^{F})
of

canonically induces a connection on

define a projective structure on
N
. The torsion of this projective structure is given by the restriction of
\tau
to
\Omega^{2}(N,TN)
.

(ii)

(\tilde{N},E|_{\tilde{N}},\tilde{F})
is an AG-structure of type
(2,n-1)
.

Proof.

We first prove (i). Let
\nabla
be a Weyl connection of
(M,E,F)
and
s=(L\,\eta)|_{N}\in\Gamma(E|_{N})

9
(i) and the formula of

Here,

is the restriction of the Weyl connection
\nabla
; see

14
. In particular, if
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(N)

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}^{\ell}_{\alpha\otimes\beta}\alpha=\nabla^{\ell}_{\alpha\otimes\beta}\alpha+\Upsilon(\alpha\otimes\beta)\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\nabla}^{F|_{N}}_{\alpha\otimes\beta}\tilde{\beta}=\nabla^{F|_{N}}_{\alpha\otimes\beta}\tilde{\beta}+\Upsilon(\alpha\otimes\tilde{\beta})\beta

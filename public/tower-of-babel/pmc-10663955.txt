Protocol for preparing patterned superlattice structures by printing assembly technology for multi-channel detection
Authors: Weidong Zhao, Jin Ren, Min Zhang, Zhenjie Xue, Tie Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe transformation of superlattice structures into functional devices requires high-quality preparation. Inkjet printing is potentially a cutting-edge technology for nanofabrication. Here, we present a protocol to prepare inks for constructing patterned superlattice structures using print assembly techniques. We describe steps for preparing oleophobic substrates, optimizing ink parameters, and preparing the patterned superlattice array. We then detail procedures for preparing a multichannel surface-enhanced Raman scattering sensor and evaluating its performance. This protocol can potentially facilitate the commercialization of superlattice-related devices.For complete details regarding the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Zhao et al.1Subject areas: Microscopy, Chemistry, Material Sciences

Before you begin

Nanoparticles (NPs), as building blocks, can spontaneously form ordered superlattice structures through self-assembly, which exhibit different chemical and physical properties, such as surface plasmon resonance and high charge carrier mobility, compared to individual NPs.2,3,4,5,6 However, transforming superlattices into functional devices is challenging because of the need for tailored patterns as well as scalable and high-quality preparation. Inkjet printing is a potential manufacturing technology that enables the programmable patterning and rapid prototyping of NPs.7,8 By integrating assembly and inkjet printing techniques, NP-based architectures can be fabricated on demand; this technology can overcome the limitations of conventional self-assembly approaches.9,10 However, the stable preparation of superlattice structures is challenging owing to the complexity of the assembly path,11 which hinders the practical application of related devices.

In this study, we propose a step-by-step manufacturing method for patterned superlattice structures using print assembly technology. Focusing on AuNPs as the research object, we successfully developed a multichannel SERS sensor that can simultaneously detect different substances without interference. This method can facilitate the transformation and commercial development of superlattice devices.

Step-by-step method details

This section provides a detailed explanation of the five steps involved in the preparation of oleophobic substrates, optimization of ink parameters, fabrication of patterned superlattice array, surface-enhanced Raman detection, and finite difference time-domain (FDTD) simulation for electromagnetic field distribution.

The square matrix consists of a plurality of OA-Au superlattices (∼1.44×106), as shown in Figures 8B and 8C. The color of the substrate displays a structural hue owing to Bragg diffraction caused by the periodic arrangement.

Limitations

Although the OA-Au superlattice structure provides a uniform distribution of hot spots, the random distribution of analytes on the SERS substrate during the drying process can cause signal fluctuations.

The size uniformity of the OA-Au NPs also determines the assembly quality of the superlattice structures; therefore, it is necessary to screen samples with a narrow size distribution.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

The size of the OA-Au NPs is so small that centrifugal washing is ineffective even at speeds exceeding 10,000 rpm (step 1).

Potential solution

Before centrifuging the fresh OA-Au suspension, add 1 mL of ethanol to 5 mL of the suspension to promote sedimentation. Repeat this process three times.

Problem 2

SEM is an important tool for determining the structures of samples. However, the small size and organic coating reduce the conductivity of OA-Au, posing challenges in capturing high-quality images.

Potential solution

High-magnification images require increasing the voltage as much as possible; however, this may cause discharge. Therefore, to obtain high-quality photographs, the recommended approach is to first focus the image near the shooting area and then quickly scan that specific area.

Problem 3

The printing assembly process requires strict environmental conditions. The presence of dust in the air or aggregation of nanoparticles adhering to the nozzle may affect the flying state of the ink droplets, thus affecting the print quality (step 2).

Potential solution

First, operate the printing equipment in a clean environment. Second, clean the nozzle of the cartridge three times with the solvent (n-hexane) before and after use to remove dust or aggregation of OA-Au. Notably, the ink encapsulated in the ink cartridge and the inkjet printer has a protective cover, so it is not necessary to operate in the fume hood.

Problem 4

Various factors affect the Raman spectra signals, such as test parameters, structural uniformity, and analyte adsorption uniformity on the substrate, which can result in signal fluctuations.

Potential solution

First, ensure that the test parameters remain constant. Second, conduct a minimum of three tests for each sample and calculate the average result to minimize potential errors.

Problem 5

In addition to the ligand content, other factors affect the assembly behavior such as particle size or shape.

Potential solution

To control these variables, use the same batch of nanoparticles throughout the experiment. In addition, the size distribution of nanoparticles must be narrow.

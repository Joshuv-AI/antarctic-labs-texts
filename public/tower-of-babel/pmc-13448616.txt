Rapid consensus building to strengthen AMR surveillance in LMICs using One Health approach: a modified table-top exercise coupled with a Delphi study protocol
Authors: Prakash Ghosh, Nicola Reck, Gisela Schneider, Judith Asin, Julius Boniface Okuni, Claude Mambo Muvunyi, Muhammad Asaduzzaman, Rea Maja Kobialka, Adel Abdelkhalek, Ariful Basher, Martin Siegel, Miriam Songa Stegemann, Sophie Schneitler, Ahmed Abd El Wahed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionImplementing integrated One Health surveillance of antimicrobial resistance (OH-AMR) remains a challenge globally, particularly in developing countries. Resource limitations, infrastructural gaps and inadequate technical capacity often hinder adherence to standardised antimicrobial resistance (AMR) surveillance guidelines, resulting in suboptimal data coverage and quality in resource-constrained settings. Moreover, considerable disparities exist in the design and execution of OH-AMR surveillance systems across regions, further complicating global data harmonisation and comparative analyses. Therefore, considering the complex ecosystem of OH-AMR and its effective implementation in the low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), a consensus-based multidisciplinary research agenda should be prioritised. In recent years, the Delphi method has become a popular tool in systematic and bias-free consensus building on a certain research domain or complex topics. Therefore, with the overarching objective of setting an operational standard for OH-AMR surveillance in LMICs, we aim to perform a Delphi study by engaging the experts from relevant domains.Methods and analysisTo streamline the traditional time-consuming Delphi technique, we plan to implement a real-time Table-Top Delphi exercise for rapid consensus building. Moreover, to capitalise on the convention of experts from diverse fields in real-time, we propose to conduct the study during an international conference on AMR. With systematic modification in the Delphi technique, the study will be conducted in two rounds where the first round will be performed in real-time followed by a second round online. For this purpose, a set of questions being prepared based on the identified gaps in existing scientific literature, evidence and guidelines for OH-AMR will be used. The questionnaire will be dispatched on an electronic platform to accumulate the responses from the experts in real-time while ensuring the anonymity and controlled feedback. Consensus will be defined as 70% or more of respondents selecting the same option for a given question. Considering the engagement of stakeholders from multiple domains of the trifecta, a total of 50 participants will be recruited in the study to have 70% replicable consensus where responses from at least 40 participants are expected in both rounds. The outcome of this Delphi study will pave the way for the implementation of an optimised OH-AMR surveillance strategy, especially in the global south, leading to actionable outcomes.Ethics and disseminationThe study will be conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Ethics Advisory Board, Leipzig University, Germany. The Ethics Advisory Board has no concern on the study protocol (2025.08.27_eb_348) in terms of research ethics. Before participating in the survey, individuals will be required to provide informed consent through the Welphi electronic platform (Yes/No options). Participation will be entirely voluntary, and participants may withdraw their consent and discontinue participation at any stage of the study. The findings of the study will be shared with the major stakeholders including the quadripartite organisations, global and regional networks on AMR and presented in international conferences and social media forums on AMR. The outcome of the study will be published in a peer-reviewed journal.

Introduction

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is considered to be one of the top 10 public health problems of the 21st century, posing a paramount challenge to global health. According to the Global Burden of Disease 2019 study, 1.27 million deaths were attributable to AMR along with a substantial morbidity.1 A high-resolution observation in the disease burden reveals the highest AMR-related death rate in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, which indicates the disproportionate effect of AMR in the developing world. It is also assumed to have a death toll of 10 million people each year as a result of AMR in the coming decades.2 In addition, AMR poses a substantial economic burden with significant mortality and morbidity, increased healthcare costs and reduced productivity, particularly in the low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). A recent study suggests that AMR costs more than US$4.6 billion annually in the USA alone.3 Besides, the World Bank’s estimation suggests circa 3.8% loss of global annual GDP by 2050, where the loss has been projected to be higher in LMICs.4

To tackle this vicious global public health threat, strong knowledge and evidence-based AMR research and surveillance is the key.5 In line with this objective, WHO developed the Global Antimicrobial Resistance and Use Surveillance System (GLASS) in 2015 with an overarching goal to build bacterial isolate-based AMR surveillance around the world.6 According to the 2020 GLASS report, the observed rate of Escherichia coli resistance to third generation cephalosporins in LMICs and high income countries (HICs) was 58.3% and 17.53% respectively, whereas the HICs reported 10 times antibiotic susceptibility testing data as compared with that of LMICs.7 The aforementioned paradox clearly signifies the suboptimal coverage and quality of AMR data in the developing countries. It also applies to all domains of One Health, not only the human health sector.

The transmission of AMR lies in the interface of three sectors involving human, animal and environment. Therefore, delving into the AMR resistance pattern in these sectors is imperative to understand the AMR transmission dynamics and tailor measures to contain and prevent the AMR transmission. Discerning the need of capturing AMR data from these sectors, WHO has developed One Health approach based Tricycle protocol to integrate AMR data from human, animal and environment sectors.8 The protocol has been implemented in several LMICs on a pilot basis to assess its feasibility towards scaling up.9 However, challenges are persistent in LMICs due to inadequate infrastructure, limited laboratory capacity, logistical constraints and insufficient funding.

Comprehending such challenges, the quadripartite organisations (WHO, Food and Agriculture Organisation of the United Nations, World Organisation for Animal Health and United Nations Environment Programme) recently conducted a study to prioritise the major research areas to strengthen the global AMR surveillance.10 The study identified several key challenges such as the suboptimal surveillance strategies and minimum standards (including laboratory and human resource requirements) necessary to establish and maintain sustainable and integrated AMR surveillance systems. In addition, the study underscored the triangulation or integration of AMR surveillance data from humans, animals and the environment to enable early identification of the development, escalation or circulation of resistance across sectors.

Therefore, development of a standard framework for One Health based AMR surveillance with integrated multisectoral data for resource constrained settings is an unmet need and remains a complex task that requires targeted guidance. To solve such intricate issues and prioritise the research agenda for evidence synthesis, the Delphi method has emerged as a valuable approach.11 12 This method offers a multidisciplinary but structured, transparent, iterative, unbiased and statistically validated consensus on complex issues.13–20 In the health sector, Delphi studies are used to inform clinical practice and influence structures, processes and framework conditions of healthcare through consensus building. Owing to the multitude of advantages, the use of this technique in the field of AMR research is on the rise.21–24 Recently, a Delphi study was conducted to identify the most important actions for AMR and antimicrobial use (AMU) surveillance with a One Health approach.20 The study findings emphasised different metrics and indicators relevant to AMR and AMU surveillance, with a special focus on data analysis and reporting. However, the scope of the study was confined to the HICs. Therefore, further consensus building studies are needed to harmonise expert opinions in strengthening the AMR surveillance with a One Health approach in LMICs.

Here to note, the classical Delphi approach involves multiple online-based surveys for a long period of time where the expert selection and engagement process is time consuming as well.25 To expedite consensus building, gather granular data and harmonise expert opinions, we, therefore, propose a hybrid approach by combining a table-top exercise and a modified Delphi survey to identify critical actions for strengthening One Health AMR surveillance in LMICs. We assume that combining these complementary approaches might provide an efficient and pragmatic framework for rapid consensus building. The table-top exercise facilitates interactive discussion, allowing participants to identify challenges, exchange experiences and propose potential solutions, while the modified Delphi process systematically refines, prioritises and validates these outputs through structured expert consensus.26 As a whole, the purpose of this integrated approach is to provide a low-cost, time-efficient and logistically feasible strategy for optimising One Health AMR surveillance frameworks in resource-constrained settings.

In addition to adapting a hybrid approach, leveraging international conferences will allow us to convene a diverse group of AMR experts for this purpose with minimum logistics in a limited timeframe. Therefore, in line with the mentioned strategy, our study aims to engage multidisciplinary experts representing human, animal and environmental health sectors to establish optimum surveillance strategies and define minimum standards for laboratory and workforce capacity in LMICs. Furthermore, the study seeks to formulate practical recommendations for triangulating One Health AMR data across sectors and improving its reporting to national and international agencies for coordinated action.

Methods

Study design

We aim to conduct a modified table-top Delphi study. The Delphi technique is a structured and systematic method designed to achieve consensus among a heterogeneous group of experts or informed stakeholders towards synthesising evidence. Its four defining characteristics—anonymity, iteration, controlled feedback and statistical aggregation of group responses—make it particularly well suited to achieve the goal of this project. The Delphi method has been widely applied in health and intervention research to address complex, multidisciplinary questions.15 17–19 Participants engage anonymously in sequential rounds of questionnaires, with each round refining the results of the previous one. After each round, aggregated group responses are shared with participants, enabling them to reconsider their views considering the broader expert opinion. By balancing individual perspectives against the collective judgement, the Delphi approach ensures that the consensus reached reflects a valid and credible expert opinion rather than the influence of a single voice.

The classical Delphi studies involve three rounds of surveys which require at least 6 months to accomplish the survey procedure. Moreover, the first round involves leading questions which often make the experts less responsive. Therefore, to expedite the consensus building process minimising the cost and requirement of logistics, exploiting the convene of the experts in an international conference and carry out face-to-face discussion for convergence of the opinion, the study team has envisioned a novel approach to conduct the study.

According to the alternative plan, the Delphi survey will be conducted following a table-top (discussion-based) exercise. This is a scenario-driven discussion in which participants actively engage in evaluating and responding to a simulated situation to prepare for a potential crisis. It is a facilitated exercise involving experts, stakeholders and partners within a defined timeframe and with minimal resource requirements. To promote active participation and high-quality decision-making, clear, specific and measurable objectives are established before the exercise. Throughout the session, participants will discuss realistic scenarios, identify challenges, evaluate potential response strategies and collaboratively develop practical solutions. Overall, table-top exercises will provide an effective, rapid and cost-efficient approach to tackle a crisis situation by enhancing existing knowledge and skills, identifying gaps, tailoring a standard framework and optimising the preparedness plans and policies.

Since AMR is recognised as a global public health crisis, particularly in LMICs, there is an urgent need for adaptive and context-specific approaches to strengthen One Health AMR surveillance. We therefore designed this approach to optimise existing One Health AMR surveillance frameworks by identifying ongoing challenges and developing practical mitigation strategies tailored to resource-constrained settings. Our proposed methodology combines a table-top exercise with a modified Delphi process, providing a low-cost, time-efficient and logistically feasible approach while ensuring active participation of multidisciplinary experts, systematic consolidation of opinions, statistical rigour, minimisation of bias and preservation of participant anonymity during the Delphi rounds.

To engage experts from multiple disciplines and diverse settings, we plan to conduct the study during an international conference, such as the European Congress on Tropical Medicine and International Health (ECTMIH) or the Annual Meeting of the American Society of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene (ASTMH), where One Health AMR experts from around the world will be invited to participate. This approach will facilitate multidisciplinary participation while substantially reducing the time, cost and logistical resources required for participant recruitment and consensus building, making it particularly suitable for resource-constrained settings.

A set of questions being formulated based on the identified gaps in existing research, evidence and guidelines will be used. The questions will be transcribed into the Welphi platform, which is a commercial platform used for online surveys with questionnaires and the Delphi method (https://www.welphi.com/). Following the first round of Delphi exercise in real-time, the participants will be invited in the second round. The final consensus will be built following the second round of the online survey. Consensus will be defined as 70% or more of respondents selecting the same option for a given question.

The study-specific Delphi phases

The Delphi will occur in two phases—the preparatory phase and the consensus building phase.

The preparatory phase

In this phase a committee (Delphi Team) comprising subject relevant experts from different domains is formed. Towards conducting impactful research, a multidisciplinary team of researchers drawn from academia, research institutions, government agencies and non-governmental organisations will be developed. The team will comprise medical doctors, veterinarians, microbiologists, laboratory specialists, data analysts, qualitative researchers, pharmacists, social scientists and health economists. Collectively, the researchers are expected to possess extensive expertise in infectious diseases, AMR, global health and One Health research and implementation. In addition, the team members are expected to have substantial experience in both quantitative and qualitative research methodologies, including Delphi studies, epidemiological investigations, health systems research and policy-oriented research. Furthermore, they should have extensive experience working across diverse geographical settings, including both HICs and LMICs. The team members will have frequent meetings online to design the study, review the literature, develop and validate the questionnaire. This broad disciplinary and international expertise will support rigorous data analysis, facilitate the interpretation of findings from multiple perspectives and ensure that the resulting recommendations are contextually relevant and applicable across a range of health systems and resource settings. Finally, the team will make a plan how the target experts will be convened in a conference for real-time Delphi exercise and subsequent online survey will be performed.

The consensus building phase

This phase will involve arranging the real-time Delphi exercise, inviting the participants in a conference, selecting the experts based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, re-inviting the selected participants in the second round for online survey and analysing the responses. After analysing the responses, final assessment of the results will be performed by the Delphi team towards cementing the final consensus.

Development and validation of the questionnaire

To inform the development of the questionnaire, the Delphi team conducted a targeted literature search in PubMed and Google Scholar, with no start date restriction, up to 30 September 2025. Search terms included combinations of the keywords “antimicrobial resistance,” “One Health,” “surveillance,” “LMIC” and “Delphi,” combined using Boolean operators. This search identified 26 records in PubMed (we only found six relevant), in addition to relevant records from Google Scholar and key guidance documents from WHO and the quadripartite organisations.27–54 The guidelines from WHO included Global Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance, GLASS, GLASS reports, Tricycle, Genomic Surveillance and AMR and Lancet issues on AMR surveillance have been considered as the guiding materials for developing the questionnaire.9 10 55–61 Articles and guidelines were screened for relevance to human, animal, food and environmental sectors of AMR surveillance by members of the Delphi team, with discrepancies resolved through group discussion. This was not conducted as a formal systematic review with a pre-registered protocol or Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA)-based screening, and no formal quality appraisal of included sources was performed, as the objective was to identify thematic gaps to guide questionnaire content rather than to synthesise an exhaustive evidence base. Following meticulous review of the articles and guidelines, the Delphi team made discussions in several rounds to identify the gaps and needs to strengthen the AMR surveillance with One Health approach in LMICs. On discussions, the Delphi team identified five key domains which are significantly associated with One Health surveillance of antimicrobial resistance (OH-AMR) surveillance. The domains include (a) One Health AMR Surveillance Design and Sampling Framework, (b) Laboratory Methods and Diagnostic Capacity, (c) Data Management, Reporting and Integration (OH-AMR data sharing and coordination), (d) Laboratory Quality Assurance and Workforce Capacity and (e) AMR Outbreak Detection and Response (table 1). Then the Delphi team developed the questionnaire on the selected domains (Annex 1) online supplemental file 1. The questionnaire includes the indicators in granular level for convergence of the opinions from the experts. Following the development of the questionnaire, the Delphi team members reviewed the questionnaire individually and eventually validated.

Recruitment and identification of experts

The success of a Delphi study depends, in part, on the participants’ ability to provide relevant input. In the absence of specific guidelines defining what constitutes ‘experts’ for Delphi studies, this study will adopt a definition proposed by Blaschke et al.62 Delphi experts are those ‘individuals who possess both knowledge and experience representative of the capacity to articulate informed opinion and provide relevant input about a given topic’. In line with this idea, participants having significant experience on One Health and AMR will be considered as experts who will be invited eventually. The Delphi team plans to arrange a side event such as symposium/discussion session on ‘AMR surveillance with One Health Approach’ in an international conference (eg. ECTMIH; ASTMH) where experts across the globe will be invited to participate in the study. To further broaden participation, the event will be announced in advance through email invitations and promoted via professional and social media platforms. In addition, the side event will be live-streamed, enabling experts who are unable to attend the conference in person to participate remotely. The event will also be shared through the African One Health Network for Disease Prevention (ADAPT) One Health Network, which includes researchers and stakeholders working on One Health AMR surveillance and antimicrobial stewardship across seven sub-Saharan African countries.

Furthermore, the questionnaire includes demographic and professional information, including participants’ experience working in LMIC settings and their area of expertise. These data will enable us to ensure balanced representation across the human, animal, and environmental health sectors and to prioritise experts with relevant LMIC experience for participation in the subsequent Delphi round. We believe that these additional recruitment strategies will enhance the diversity, representativeness and quality of the consensus-building process. All the interested participants will be provided with the electronic link to participate in the online survey in real-time.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Among all the invited participants, experts having experience in the field of AMR and One Health for at least 2 years with at least two publications in the relevant fields will be screened out following the first round. Only the participants who will complete the questionnaire in the first round will be re-invited in the second round.

Rounds of Delphi exercise and data collection

According to the proposed design of the study, a table-top Delphi exercise will be performed in the first round where the second round of Delphi survey will be performed online (figure 1). In the first round, a universal link will be generated and the link will be shared with the potential participants. Through the link, the individual participant will need to open an account with an email address and personal password. Eventually, the participant will be registered for the event. No financial or non-financial incentives will be offered to participants for taking part in the survey. Participants who will take part in the survey following electronic consent and complete the survey will be re-invited for the next round. Since the survey data for each participant will be stored in the Welphi platform, individual responses will be synced in the subsequent round. The participants in the first round will be advised to respond to the questionnaire following a discussion session where experts from the global North and South will talk about AMR and the One Health approach and exchange their views with the conference attendees and study participants. Then, the participants will be requested to fill the electronic questionnaire. First, the participants will be instructed to answer questions pertaining to demographics. Then, they will be instructed to provide their responses to questions on five domains sequentially. After completing the questions under each domain, a feedback discussion session will be arranged to address the participants’ queries on the questionnaire. For the online questionnaire, participants will use a 5-point Likert scale (Strongly Agree, Agree, Neither Agree nor Disagree, Disagree and Strongly Disagree) to rate how strongly they agree or disagree with each proposed statement. Other questions will ask participants to choose a preferred option from a list. For both types of questions, participants will be able to add comments or feedback in the electronic questionnaire. In addition, some open-ended questions will be included to gain deeper insights into different aspects of OH AMR surveillance. All the discussion points during the table-top exercise and feedback from the participants will be noted by the investigators. After completion of the conference, the study team will have a meeting to analyse the responses.

Sample size

According to the previous literature, 23 participants are adequate to have a stable consensus, providing that the participants are from similar domain.63 However, at least 40 participants are required for a multi-stakeholders’ study to have 70% replicable consensus.64 Therefore considering a 10% attrition rate in the second round, this study will aim to recruit a minimum of 50 participants.

Study timeframe

The study is currently at the preparatory phase where the research team is involved in developing the Delphi questionnaire. The expected end date of the study is August 2026.

Data storage and security

All personal data collected during this Delphi study, including contact information and survey responses, will be processed in strict accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679. The purpose of data collection is solely for the administration of the study and the generation of a credible expert consensus. Participant anonymity is a cornerstone of the Delphi method, and therefore, all responses will be anonymised to ensure that individual answers cannot be traced back to any specific person. Participants also reserve the right to withdraw their consent at any time, in which case their data will be removed from the study, without prejudice to the lawfulness of processing based on consent before its withdrawal.

After completion of the surveys, all data will be exported from the Welphi platform in excel format. Then the data will be permanently deidentified by replacing the names with randomly generated codes and deleting participant email addresses. The study investigators will store the data in a secured data storage system (both off-line and cloud storage). All data will be stored according to the data storage and protection policy of Leipzig University.

Data analysis

Analysis of the data collected during the rounds 1 and 2 will comprise the calculation of descriptive statistics to quantify the proportion of participants who choose each category of the Likert scale for each item in the questionnaire. We will define ‘positive consensus’ for an item as occurring if >80% of respondents indicate that they ‘Strongly Agree’ or ‘Agree’ with a statement. We will define ‘negative consensus’ for an item as occurring if >80% of respondents indicate that they ‘Disagree’ or ‘Strongly Disagree’ with a statement. For statements that only allow for a Yes/No response, ‘positive consensus’ will be defined as >80% of participants selecting ‘Yes’ while ‘negative consensus’ will be defined as >80% of respondents selecting ‘No’. To better discriminate the results, when a consensus ≥70% will not be reached but show a predominance (>50%) of votes for high, medium or low priority, will be recorded in the same manner. For the questions with multiple options, descriptive analysis will be performed and data will be presented as frequency counts with percentages. Data will be analysed using the Welphi platform and some analysis will be done with R programming in need. A final Delphi team meeting will compile the priority based on the rounds results and including the items without a clear consensus.

Ethical approval

The study will be conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Ethics Advisory Board, Leipzig University, Germany. The Ethics Advisory Board has no concern on the study protocol (2025.08.27_eb_348) in terms of research ethics.

Discussion

With the incessant rise of AMR worldwide, particularly in low-resource settings, there is an urgent need for integrated approaches to curb its transmission through effective surveillance. Under the auspices of this effective and practical Delphi approach, the study team aims to consolidate an OH-AMR framework for conducting surveillance and to establish optimum standards for quality data reporting. In accordance with the study design, the real-time Delphi exercise is expected to streamline the consensus-building process by enabling immediate feedback and iterative refinement of expert opinions. Leveraging the presence of global experts during an international conference provides a unique opportunity for rapid consensus formation and the subsequent translation of findings into policy and practice.

Besides, a granular focus on the core elements and measurable metrics of OH-AMR surveillance will facilitate convergence of expert responses, while real-time feedback from participants will further enhance consensus and ensure the derivation of well-balanced, evidence-informed recommendations. To be specific, the real-time feedback round will challenge individuals’ mental models on the relevant domains of the questionnaire. In addition, this round will be conducive to comprehending, contextualise and evaluating the questions. Eventually, the experts will be able to make appropriate judgements and consistent responses. Inclusion of experts from diverse geographic and disciplinary backgrounds will strengthen the generalisability and replicability of the study outcomes. Engaging experts during the preparatory stage will also prove valuable in developing and validating the questionnaire and refining the study design. Furthermore, the inclusion of ‘AMR Outbreak Detection and Response’ as a distinct domain will stimulate critical reflection among participants and enrich the study’s overall insights. Despite practical advantages, the study is likely to face challenges which might influence the consensus-building procedure and outcomes. As with most Delphi studies, the attrition of the experts in the subsequent round might influence the statistical robustness and reliability of the consensus outcomes. It is also likely that experts from LMICs will be underrepresented at the conference, potentially limiting the diversity of perspectives. Besides, the literature search informing questionnaire development was a targeted, non-systematic search of PubMed and Google Scholar and related grey literature rather than a formal systematic review with a pre-registered protocol, standardised screening or quality appraisal; as such, it may not have captured all relevant evidence, though this was considered sufficient for the purpose of identifying thematic gaps to guide questionnaire content rather than synthesising a comprehensive evidence base. Finally, beyond strengthening AMR surveillance, this real-time Delphi exercise might offer a critical reflection on the broader utility of the One Health approach in enhancing pandemic preparedness. The framework and process developed here may serve as a model for future consensus-building initiatives addressing complex public-health emergencies.

Dissemination

The findings of this study will be shared with the major stakeholders including the quadripartite organisations, global and regional networks on AMR and presented in international conferences and social media forums on AMR. The outcome of the study will be published in a peer-reviewed journal. Finally, for prompt actionable outcomes, the findings will be shared with the ongoing projects or initiatives in the global south, such as ADAPT One Health Network (https://adaptonehealth.net/) towards implementation and rapid evidence synthesis.

Study status

The study is at the preparatory stage now. The study team will start the consensus-building phase as soon as all procedures are completed to organise the real-time Delphi exercise (Round 1) in a conference. The Round 2 will be initiated within 3 weeks of completion of the Round 1 and will take about 4 weeks to accomplish. Data analysis is planned to be completed within 2 months of the Round 2. The final result of this Delphi study is planned to be submitted for publication by August 2026.

Patient and public involvement

There has been no involvement of the patient/public in the study design or conduct of the study. However, the study findings will be disseminated to the public.

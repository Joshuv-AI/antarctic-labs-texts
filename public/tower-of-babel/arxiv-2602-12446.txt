Model checking with temporal graphs and their derivative
Binh-Minh Bui-Xuan, Florent Krasnopol, Bruno Monasson, Nathalie Sznajder

Abstract
Temporal graphs are graphs where the presence or properties of their vertices and edges change over time. When time is discrete, a temporal graph can be defined as a sequence of static graphs over a discrete time span, called lifetime, or as a single graph where each edge is associated with a specific set of time instants where the edge is alive. For static graphs, Courcelle’s Theorem asserts that any graph problem expressible in monadic second-order logic can be solved in linear time on graphs of bounded tree-width. We propose the first adaptation of Courcelle’s Theorem for monadic second-order logic on temporal graphs that does not explicitly rely on the lifetime as a parameter. We then introduce the notion of derivative over a sliding time window of a chosen size, and define the tree-width and twin-width of the temporal graph’s derivative. We exemplify its usefulness with meta theorems with respect to a temporal variant of first-order logic. The resulting logic expresses a wide range of temporal graph problems including a version of temporal cliques, an important notion when querying time series databases for community structures.

Introduction

The concept of algorithmic meta-theorems (AMT)
[
28
,
31
]
has arisen as a broad solution framework for entire classes of (static) graph problems.
A typical statement for AMT is: any graph problem expressible in X-logic can be solved in polynomial time on graphs of bounded Y-parameter, where X and Y are to be specified.
Three cornerstones have been settled for: First Order logic
FO
(playing X) with twin-width (playing Y)
[
9
]
; Monadic Second Order logic
\textsf{MSO}_{1}
with clique-width
[
14
]
;
\textsf{MSO}_{2}
with tree-width
[
13
]
.
For a proper definition of these notions, we refer the interested reader to Section
2
or to Refs.
[
9
,
15
]
.
The three results are incomparable as
FO
specifies
\textsf{MSO}_{1}
which specifies
\textsf{MSO}_{2}
whereas graphs of bounded tree-width are also of bounded clique-width which in turn are of bounded twin-width.

Although these results are not new, an outburst of AMT have been discovered in the past few years.
For instance,
FO
can be enriched with an atomic predicate called
conn
, expressing the existence of a path joining two vertices while avoiding a given set of vertices. In turn,
FO
+
conn
is included in
FO
+
DP
, where
DP
is a predicate expressing the existence of internally vertex-disjoint paths between a set of source vertices. These lead to results tying
FO
+
conn
(again, playing X) with a parameter (playing Y) called the Hajós number
[
37
]
, and a result tying
FO
+
DP
with a parameter called the Hadwiger number in Ref.
[
27
]
.
Many other AMT for static graphs have been discovered too, from both centralised
[
4
,
6
]
and distributed
[
12
,
24
]
contexts.

Unfortunately, much less positive results have been found for AMT on temporal graphs.
To begin with, it is notoriously a tricky question to find polynomial algorithms solving natural generalisations of graph problems on temporal graphs.
Most relevant polynomial cases are about paths, walks, backup-routes
[
10
,
41
,
39
]
, and path-based measures such as closeness
[
17
]
and betweenness centrality
[
35
]
.
Contrasting these few cases, the temporal version of spanning tree is widely known to be
NP
-complete
[
5
]
, leading to important current works on spanners, even when the (temporal) graphs are reduced to be trees themselves
[
11
]
.
Temporal cliques and their generalizations to
\Delta
-cliques and
(\Delta,\gamma)
-cliques are
NP
-complete
[
40
,
29
,
2
]
.
The duo of max-flow and min-cut translates into several
NP
-complete temporal flavours
[
42
]
.
Even classically polynomial matching translates to an
NP
-complete problem
[
3
]
which remains
NP
-complete on extremely restricted temporal graphs, notably with very small tree-width
[
33
]
.
Since matching can naturally be expressed in
\textsf{MSO}_{1}
, this is intuitively a major obstruction for obtaining an AMT for temporal graphs based on tree-width.

Our work strives to rectify the very lack of AMT for temporal graphs.
A temporal graph is a sequence of static graphs
\mathcal{G}=(G_{t})_{t\in\llbracket 0,\tau-1\rrbracket}
over the same set of
n
vertices.
Every graph
G_{t}
in the sequence is called a snapshot graph.
Parameter
\tau
is called the lifetime of
\mathcal{G}
.
The union graph
\mathcal{G}_{\downarrow}
is the graph whose edge set contains all edges appearing at any snapshot in the sequence.
The static expansion graph
\mathcal{G}_{\rightarrow}
is the graph where, in addition to the snapshot edges,
G_{t}
and
G_{t+1}
are joined by edges between the occurrences at
t
and at
t+1
of every vertex.
Three popular ways to lift a parameter
k(G)
of a static graph
G
to temporal graphs are, respectively, when defining
k_{\infty}
as the maximum
k(G_{t})
over every snapshot
G_{t}
; when defining
k_{\downarrow}
as the parameter
k(\mathcal{G}_{\downarrow})
of its union graph; and when defining
k_{\rightarrow}
as the parameter
k(\mathcal{G}_{\rightarrow})
of its static expansion graph.
For parameter tree-width
\tw
, Ref.
[
23
]
sketched a high-level proof that no AMT can be found tying a temporal version of monadic second-order logic (
MSOT
) with
\tw_{\infty}
, unless
P=NP
.
The cases of
\tw_{\rightarrow}
and
\tw_{\downarrow}
are left open, which we address in the sequel.
On the positive side, an AMT exists for
MSOT
when also the lifetime
\tau
is bounded in addition to bounded
\tw_{\downarrow}

[
42
]
.
Up to our knowledge, no attempt in finding an AMT for temporal graphs has been successful without bounding the lifetime.

We describe now our contributions, along with the organisation of the paper.
We formally define in Section
2
Monadic Second Order logic for temporal graphs,
\textsf{MSOT}_{2}
along with a restriction called
\textsf{MSOT}_{1}
, and some temporal graph width parameters.
We show in Section
3

the first AMT for temporal graphs where the lifetime is not a parameter, as in the sense of fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) complexity
[
21
,
22
,
36
,
18
]
:
Every problem expressible by a sentence in
\textsf{MSOT}_{2}
can be solved on input
\mathcal{G}
in FPT time parameterized by the size of the sentence and the expanded tree-width
\tw_{\rightarrow}(\mathcal{G})=\tw(\mathcal{G}_{\rightarrow})
.
While the static expansion graph
\mathcal{G}_{\rightarrow}
has a large number of vertices,
n\times\tau
, it is a globally sparse graph with dense regions of size at most
n
.
Besides,
0\leq\tw_{\rightarrow}(\mathcal{G})\leq 2\times n
.
When for instance
\mathcal{G}
has at most one edge per snapshot and
\tw_{\rightarrow}(\mathcal{G})\leq 2
, our AMT results in a polynomial time algorithm while any result with parameter
\tau
similar to Ref.
[
42
]
would have time complexity exponential in
\tau
unless ETH fails (Exponential Time Hypothesis: SAT cannot be solved in subexponential time).

We obtain a few hardness results though, as it is usually the case with temporal graphs (we give them in the remainder of Section
3
).
First we show that no AMT can be found tying neither
\tw_{\infty}
nor
\tw_{\downarrow}
with
\textsf{MSOT}_{1}
, hence not with
\textsf{MSOT}_{2}
neither, unless
P=NP
.
Moreover, for
\tw_{\infty}
we obtain a hardness result even for a less expressive version of
\textsf{MSOT}_{1}
, generalising the sketched high-level proof in Ref.
[
23
]
.
We then address similar formalism for temporal twin-width, that are
\twww_{\infty}
,
\twww_{\downarrow}
and
\twww_{\rightarrow}
.
We show that no AMT can be found tying neither
\twww_{\infty}
,
\twww_{\downarrow}
nor
\twww_{\rightarrow}
with
\textsf{MSOT}_{1}
, unless
P=NP
.
Essentially, these results hint about a certain tightness of our above AMT result tying
\tw_{\rightarrow}
with
\textsf{MSOT}_{2}
.

The rest of our results are described in Section
4
. Turning our attention back on the positive side, we relax
MSOT
down to First Order logic and define a local version called
\textsf{FOT}_{1}^{\Delta}
.
This is a less expressive logic than First Order logic.
Nonetheless, we exemplify its expressivity by writing formulas in
\textsf{FOT}_{1}^{\Delta}
for problems related to temporal cliques, which are
NP
-complete and have received a lot of recent research interests
[
40
,
29
,
2
]
.
We then show a second AMT tying
\twww_{\rightarrow}
with
\textsf{FOT}_{1}^{\Delta}
, that is, every problem expressible by a sentence in
\textsf{FOT}_{1}^{\Delta}
can be solved on input
\mathcal{G}
in FPT time parameterized by the size of the sentence and the expanded twin-width
\twww_{\rightarrow}(\mathcal{G})=\twww(\mathcal{G}_{\rightarrow})
.

We further investigate a finer definition of width parameter for temporal graphs, with tree-width and twin-width of smaller graphs than the static expansion graph
\mathcal{G}_{\rightarrow}
.
We introduce the derivative of
\mathcal{G}
as subgraphs of
\mathcal{G}_{\rightarrow}
induced by consecutive snapshots within a sliding time window.
Formally, the differential of
\mathcal{G}
at
t
over
dt=\Delta
is a static graph, defined as the static expansion graph of the
\Delta
snapshots of
\mathcal{G}
starting at
t
and ending at
t+\Delta-1
.
We then show how every problem expressible by a sentence in
\textsf{FOT}_{1}^{\Delta}
can be solved on input
\mathcal{G}
in FPT time parameterized by the size of the sentence and the
\Delta
-twin-width, defined as the maximum twin-width over all differentials of
\mathcal{G}
.
As a by product we also obtain the same result for
\Delta
-tree-width.

For a proper introduction to graph theory, refer to any standard textbook,
e.g.

[

,

]
.
Unless stated otherwise, graphs in this paper are loopless simple undirected graphs.
For any graph
G
we note

and

its vertex and edge sets.
If an edge exists between two distinct vertices

A

is a tuple

Monadic Second Order Logic for Temporal Graphs

A
signature

\sigma
is a finite set of relation
symbols
R
, each with an arity
\textrm{ar}(R)
.
A
\sigma
-structure

\mathcal{A}
consists of a non-empty set
D
, called
universe
(or
domain
), and,
for each
R\in\sigma
, a relation
R^{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq D^{\textrm{ar}(R)}
.
For
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
, the
substructure of
\mathcal{A}
induced by
D^{\prime}
, denoted
\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{\mathcal{A}}
, is the
\sigma
-structure with domain
D^{\prime}
and relations

R^{\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{\mathcal{A}}}=R^{\mathcal{A}}\cap{D^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ar}(R)}
, for every symbol
R\in\sigma
.
The
Gaifman graph
of
\mathcal{A}
is the graph
\textit{Gaif}(\mathcal{A})=(D,A_{\mathcal{A}})
,where

(a,b)\in A_{\mathcal{A}}
if and only if
a
and
b
appear together in some tuple of a relation
R^{\mathcal{A}}
.
Intuitively, it is the graph where two elements are linked by an edge if they are linked by some relation in the structure.

Model-Checking asks whether a structure satisfies a given property. In this
work, structures are (temporal) graphs. Monadic Second Order Logic has proven to be powerful enough to express many properties on graphs while staying decidable.
Given two disjoint countable sets of symbols of variables,

\mathcal{X}_{0}
and
\mathcal{X}_{1}
(first order and second order variables), an
MSO
formula over a signature
\sigma
(noted
\textsf{MSO}(\sigma)
) is defined by

\varphi:=x=x^{\prime}~|~x\in X~|~R(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})~|~\neg\varphi~|~\varphi\land\varphi~|~\exists x.\varphi~|~\exists X.\varphi

for
x,x^{\prime},x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
,
X\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
and for all
R\in\sigma
such that
\textrm{ar}(R)=k
.

The semantics of an
\textsf{MSO}(\sigma)
formula over a structure
\mathcal{A}
of domain
D
is defined using two
valuation functions

\nu_{0}:\mathcal{X}_{0}\rightarrow D
and
\nu_{1}:\mathcal{X}_{1}\rightarrow 2^{D}
. Given a valuation
\nu
, a variable
\alpha\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
(resp.
\mathcal{X}_{1}
) and
m\in D
(resp.
2^{D}
), we define the new valuation
\nu[\alpha\leftarrow m]
by

\nu[\alpha\leftarrow m](\alpha)=m
, and
\nu[\alpha\leftarrow m](\beta)=\nu(\beta)
for all
\beta
in the definition domain of
\nu
.
Then, a structure
\mathcal{A}
over a domain
D
is said to satisfy a formula
\varphi
in
\textsf{MSO}(\sigma)
, written
\mathcal{A}\models_{\nu_{0},\nu_{1}}\varphi
according to the following inductive definition:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\models_{\nu_{0},\nu_{1}}x=x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\textrm{ if and only if }\nu_{0}(x)=\nu_{0}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\models_{\nu_{0},\nu_{1}}x\in X

\displaystyle\textrm{ if and only if }\nu_{0}(x)\in\nu_{1}(X)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\models_{\nu_{0},\nu_{1}}R(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})

\displaystyle\textrm{ if and only if }(\nu_{0}(x_{1}),\dots,\nu_{0}(x_{k}))\in R^{\mathcal{A}}\textrm{ for all relation $R\in\sigma$}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\models_{\nu_{0},\nu_{1}}\exists x.~\varphi

\displaystyle\textrm{ if and only if there exists $v\in V$ such that }\mathcal{A}\models_{\nu_{0}[x\leftarrow v],\nu_{1}}\varphi

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\models_{\nu_{0},\nu_{1}}\exists X.~\varphi

\displaystyle\textrm{ if and only if there exists $U\subseteq V$ such that }\mathcal{A}\models_{\nu_{0},\nu_{1}[X\leftarrow U]}\varphi.

The Boolean connectives
\neg\varphi
and
\varphi\wedge \varphi^{\prime}
are interpreted by their usual meaning.

\textsf{MSO}_{1}

We let

The symbol
\operatorname{succ}
is interpreted by

The interpretation of the relation

\operatorname{inc}^{[\mathcal{G}]}=\{((vv^{\prime},t),(v,t))\mid 0\leq t<\tau_{1}\textrm{ and }vv^{\prime}\in E_{t}\}

The

of a formula

As recalled in the introduction, temporal matching is an
NP

•

Temporal matching:
does it exist a set of
k

\gamma

\gamma

\gamma

•

Temporal cut of size

:

FPT Complexity and Width Parameters

A parameterized problem is a language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
, where
\Sigma
is a finite alphabet.
It is FPT parameterized by
k
if deciding whether
(x,k)\in L
can be done in time
f(k)\cdot|x|^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
, where
f
is an arbitrary function depending only on
k
.
Let
G
be a (static) graph.
The
subgraph of
G
induced by
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
is noted
G[V^{\prime}]=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
where
E^{\prime}=\{uv\in E(G):u\in V^{\prime}\land v\in V^{\prime}\}
.
A
path
of length
p\geq 1
in
G
is a sequence of
p-1
successively adjacent edges joining
p
distinct vertices.
A graph is
connected
if there is a path joining any two distinct vertices.
A
tree
is a connected graph over
n
vertices and
n-1
edges, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
T=(V_{T},E_{T})
be a tree.
For each
u\in V_{T}
, let
B_{u}\subseteq V(G)
be a subset called a
bag
.
The tuple
\mathbb{T}=(T,\{B_{u}:u\in V_{T}\})

is a
tree decomposition
of
G
if it respects the following
three conditions:

(i)

V(G)=\cup_{u\in V_{T}}B_{u}
;

(ii)
for every edge
vw\in E(G)
, there exists a node
u\in V_{T}

such that
\{v,w\}\subseteq B_{u}
;

(iii)
and for every
v\in V(G)
,
the subgraph of
T
induced by
\{u\in V_{T}:v\in B_{u}\}
is a tree.
The
width
of
\mathbb{T}
is

0pt(\mathbb{T})=\max\{|B_{u}|-1:u\in V_{T}\}
.
The
tree-width
of
G
is the minimum width over all
tree decompositions of
G
, that is,

\tw(G)=\min\{0pt(\mathbb{T}):\mathbb{T}\textrm{ is a tree decomposition of }G\}
.
If
G
is a
\sigma
-structure the tree-width of
G
is defined as the tree-width of its Gaifman graph.

Theorem 1

(
[

]
)
.

For every integer

and every sentence

-structure

of tree-width at most

.
Moreover, its complexity is FPT parameterized by

.

,

,

4.1
Derivative of Temporal Graphs

We have shown for
\textsf{MSOT}_{1}
and
\textsf{FOT}_{1}^{\Delta}
that they are tractable on input a temporal graph

of bounded expanded tree-width

or bounded expanded twin-width

This is in contrast to many other versions of temporal width.
While their value is still in

Examples of properties expressed in
\textsf{MSOT}_{2}

We show in this section examples of classical properties that can be expressed on temporal graphs using
\textsf{MSOT}_{2}
.

Temporal Matching

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{0}\dots,E_{\tau-1})
be a temporal graph. Two edges are
dependent
if they are incident to a same time vertex. A
temporal matching
is then a set of independent
edges, meaning that no two edges are incident to the same time vertex. The formula
\mathtt{TempMatch}(X):=\forall e\forall e^{\prime}.~(e\in X\wedge e^{\prime}\in X\wedge e\neq e^{\prime}\rightarrow\neg(\exists x.~\operatorname{inc}(e,x)\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e^{\prime},x)))
expresses the fact that a set
X
of temporal edges is a temporal matching.

Sometimes, one may ask for a stronger requirement for two edges to be independent: they cannot be incident to two time vertices that represent the same untime vertex in two snapshots too close in time,
typically in an interval smaller than some given
\Delta
. The formula becomes then
\mathtt{TempMatch}_{\Delta}(X):=\forall e\forall e^{\prime}.~(e\in X\wedge e^{\prime}\in X\wedge e\neq e^{\prime}\rightarrow\neg(\exists x\exists x^{\prime}.~\operatorname{inc}(e,x)\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e^{\prime},x^{\prime})\wedge\operatorname{succ}_{\leq\Delta}(x,x^{\prime})))
.

A similar yet more involved notion has been described in
[
3
]
. Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{0},\dots,E_{\tau-1})
be a temporal graph, and
\gamma
an integer, representing a minimum duration. A
\gamma
-edge
is a set of edges between two time vertices, starting at some instant
t

and repeating
\gamma
times, i.e., if
uv\in E_{i}
for
t\leq i<t+\gamma
, then
\{(uv,t),\dots,(uv,t+\gamma-1)\}
is a
\gamma
-edge in
\mathcal{G}
. Two
\gamma
-edges
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are
dependent
if there
exist a vertex
v
and a snapshot
t
such that
(i)
there exists
u\in V
and
(uv,t)\in e_{1}
and
(ii)
there exists
w\in V
and
(wv,t)\in e_{2}
. Note that
u
and
w
are not necessarily distinct.
A
(k,\gamma)
-temporal matching
is a set of
k
independent
\gamma
-edges.

We express the fact that a set of edges is a
\gamma
-edge by identifying an “initial” edge in the set, one that appears in the earlier snapshot, and requiring that all the edges appearing in the set are
incident to the same untime vertices, and appear within a timeframe of size
\gamma
, and finally, that all the edges in the required time interval actually exist and belong to the set.

Namely,

\displaystyle\gamma-\mathtt{Edge}(X)

\displaystyle:=\exists e_{1}\exists u_{1}\exists v_{1}.~(e_{1}\in X\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e_{1},u_{1})\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e_{1},v_{1}))

\displaystyle\wedge(\forall e.~(e\in X\rightarrow(\exists u\exists v.\operatorname{inc}(e,u)\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e,v)\wedge\operatorname{succ}_{\leq\gamma}(u_{1},u)\wedge\operatorname{succ}_{\leq\gamma}(v_{1},v))))

\displaystyle\wedge\forall u\forall v.~(((\operatorname{succ}(u_{1},u)\wedge\operatorname{succ}(v_{1},v))\vee\bigvee_{i=2}^{\gamma}(\operatorname{succ}_{i}(u_{1},u)\wedge\operatorname{succ}_{i}(v_{1},v))))\rightarrow

\displaystyle\exists e.~(\operatorname{inc}(e,u)\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e,v)\wedge e\in X))

We can then define
k-\mathtt{TempMatch}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k}):=\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq k}(\gamma-\mathtt{Edge}(X_{i}))\wedge\forall e\forall e^{\prime}.~((\bigvee_{i\neq j}(e\in X_{i}\wedge e^{\prime}\in X_{j}))\rightarrow\neg\exists x.~(\operatorname{inc}(e,x)\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e^{\prime},x)))

Cut of size 1

Given a temporal graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{0},\dots,E_{\tau-1})
and two vertices
s,z\in V
, we ask whether there exists a vertex
x\in V
such that all the possible temporal
paths from
s
to
z
visits the vertex
x
. A temporal path from
s
to
z
is a sequence

(v_{0}v_{1},t_{1})(v_{1}v_{2},t_{2})\dots(v_{\ell-1}v_{\ell},t_{\ell})
such that for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
,
0\leq t_{i}<\tau
,
t_{i-1}\leq t_{i}
and
v_{i-1}v_{i}\in E_{i}
.
We say that two edges

and

are

if there are

,
e=uv\in E_{t}
,
e^{\prime}=vw\in E_{t^{\prime}}
and
t\leq t^{\prime}
. Note that this is not a symmetric relation, due to
elapsing of time.
We define
\mathtt{TimeAdj}(e,e^{\prime}):=\exists w\exists w^{\prime}.~\operatorname{inc}(e,w)\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e^{\prime},w^{\prime})\wedge\mathtt{succ}^{*}(w,w^{\prime})
to express the fact that

e^{\prime}
are time-respecting adjacent.

If we want to express that

is closed by time-respecting adjacency restricted to a set

\mathtt{TempPath}(Y,x,y):=\exists e_{1}\exists e_{2}.~(\operatorname{inc}(e_{1},x)\wedge\operatorname{inc}(e_{2},y)\wedge e_{1}\in Y\wedge e_{2}\in Y\wedge\forall X.~(e_{1}\in X\wedge\mathtt{ClosedTempSucc}(X,Y)\rightarrow e_{2}\in X))
, meaning that there is a temporal path from

to

that visits only edges in

.

Given additional constants

6
Conclusion

We obtain the first two algorithmic meta-theorems for temporal graphs that does not take the lifetime as a parameter: every problem expressible in Monadic Second Order logic, resp. an expressive fragment of First Order logic, over temporal graphs can be solved in polynomial time on temporal graphs whose expanded tree-width, resp. expanded twin-width, is bounded.
We also initiate the study of temporal graph differentials defined using a local time window of a given size.
To exemplify the usefulness of their introduction, we show another algorithmic meta-theorem for temporal graphs whose differentials have bounded twin-width (or bounded tree-width), for properties given in the same above fragment of First Order logic.
We believe it is an important question to model temporality via differentials:
How to quantify their growth (second derivative)?
How about geometric cases of temporal graphs defined by intersection models in a similar way as unit disk graphs?
In this case, would the positions of the vertex set’s embedding help with, or be detrimental to, the definition of derivative?
Another line of research would be to enrich a bit the logic. What predicates can we add that would allow to keep FPT algorithms?

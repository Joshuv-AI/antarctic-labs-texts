2
Preliminaries

We collect the measure-theoretic and probabilistic notions used throughout and fix notation.

2.1
Measures and
L^{p}
Spaces

Definition 2.1

(Lebesgue
L^{p}
spaces)
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
,

L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\;:=\;\Big\{f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\ \Big|\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx<\infty\Big\},

and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of essentially bounded measurable functions.

Definition 2.2

(Gaussian measure and
L^{p}(\gamma_{\sigma})
)
.

For
\sigma>0
let

d\gamma_{\sigma}(x)\;:=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma}\,e^{-x^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\,dx,\qquad Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}).

Then

L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\gamma_{\sigma})\;:=\;\Big\{f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\ \Big|\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\gamma_{\sigma}(x)<\infty\Big\}.

Remark 2.1

(Lebesgue vs. Gaussian integrability)
.

On
(\mathbb{R},dx)
(infinite measure), any non-decaying function (e.g., bounded with nonzero limits such as
\tanh
,
\operatorname{sigm}
) is
not
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for any finite
p
. Likewise, any function with polynomial
growth
(e.g.,
\mathrm{ReLU}
,
x^{k}
) is not in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for finite
p
. In contrast, under Gaussian measure, polynomial growth is admissible: if
|\phi(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{r})
, then
\phi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\gamma_{\sigma})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and all
\sigma>0
.

2.2
Gaussian Moment and Derivative Signatures

Definition 2.3

(Gaussian moment signatures)
.

For a measurable activation
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

m_{k}(\sigma)\;:=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)^{k}\,d\gamma_{\sigma}(x)\;=\;\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(Z)^{k}\big],\qquad Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}).

We write
m_{1},m_{2}
for mean and second moment under
\gamma_{\sigma}
.

Definition 2.4

(Derivative and mixed signatures)
.

Assume
\phi
is a.e. differentiable with at most polynomial growth. Define

g_{k}(\sigma):=\Big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{k}]\Big)^{1/k},\qquad\eta(\sigma):=\mathbb{E}[Z\,\phi(Z)].

Lemma 2.1

(Gaussian integration by parts and differentiation)
.

Let
Z=\sigma G
with
G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. If
\phi
has at most polynomial growth and
\phi^{\prime}
is locally integrable, then

\eta(\sigma)\;=\;\mathbb{E}[Z\,\phi(Z)]\;=\;\sigma^{2}\,\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)]\;=\;\sigma^{2}\,g_{1}(\sigma).

Moreover,
m_{2}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[\phi(\sigma G)^{2}]
is differentiable with

m_{2}^{\prime}(\sigma)\;=\;\mathbb{E}\big[2\,\phi(\sigma G)\,\phi^{\prime}(\sigma G)\,G\big]\;=\;\frac{1}{\sigma}\,\mathbb{E}\big[2\,\phi(Z)\,\phi^{\prime}(Z)\,Z\big].

Proof.

The first identity is the standard Gaussian integration-by-parts:

\mathbb{E}[Gf(G)]=\mathbb{E}[f^{\prime}(G)]
for suitable
f
; apply with
f(g)=\phi(\sigma g)
.
Differentiate
m_{2}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[\phi(\sigma G)^{2}]
under the expectation and use the chain rule.
∎

Remark 2.2

(On the role of moment sequences)
.

We use
\{m_{k}(\sigma)\}
(and
g_{1},g_{2},\eta
) as a
signature
to control propagation and stability. We do not rely on distributional uniqueness of
\phi(Z)
from moments (e.g., Carleman determinacy is not assumed).

2.3
Asymptotic Behavior: Growth and Slopes

Definition 2.5

(Polynomial growth exponent)
.

For measurable
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\alpha(\phi)\;:=\;\inf\Big\{\alpha\geq 0\ \Big|\ \limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|\phi(x)|}{|x|^{\alpha}}<\infty\Big\}.

Examples:
\mathrm{ReLU}(x)
has
\alpha(\phi)=1
;
\tanh(x)
and
\operatorname{sigm}(x)
have
\alpha(\phi)=0
;
\phi(x)=x^{2}
has
\alpha(\phi)=2
.

Definition 2.6

(Asymptotic linear slopes)
.

When
\alpha(\phi)\leq 1
, we refine tails via one-sided linear slopes:

\alpha_{+}\;:=\;\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x},\qquad\alpha_{-}\;:=\;\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x},

whenever the limits exist (finite). If limits do not exist, we use

\overline{\alpha}_{\pm}:=\limsup_{x\to\pm\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x},\qquad\underline{\alpha}_{\pm}:=\liminf_{x\to\pm\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x}.

Remark 2.3

(Integrability implications)
.

If
\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}|\phi(x)|>0
(bounded nonzero tails) or
|\phi(x)|\gtrsim|x|^{\beta}
eventually for some
\beta\geq 0
, then
\phi\notin L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for any finite
p
.
Under Gaussian measure, any polynomial growth (finite
\alpha(\phi)
) implies
\phi\in L^{p}(\gamma_{\sigma})
for all
p<\infty
and all
\sigma>0
.

Notation.

We reserve
\sigma>0
exclusively for
Gaussian standard deviation
, and we denote the logistic activation by

\operatorname{sigm}(x):=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}.

Expectations
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
are taken with respect to the indicated measure (Lebesgue or
\gamma_{\sigma}
) by context.

Standing assumptions.

Unless stated otherwise, activations
\phi
have at most polynomial growth and are a.e. differentiable with locally integrable
\phi^{\prime}
, ensuring all Gaussian functionals above are well defined and allowing differentiation under the integral sign.

2.4
Moment Finiteness: Sufficient Growth Conditions

We give simple, checkable conditions ensuring
g_{2}(\sigma)
and
g_{4}(\sigma)
are finite for all
\sigma>0
,
together with explicit bounds useful in kernel curvature estimates.

Lemma 2.2

(Polynomial growth of the slope suffices)
.

Let
\phi
be a.e. differentiable, and suppose there exist constants
A,B\geq 0
and
r\geq 0
such that

|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\;\leq\;A+B\,|x|^{r}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then for every
\sigma>0
,

g_{2}(\sigma)\;=\;\Big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{2}]\Big)^{1/2}\;<\;\infty,\qquad g_{4}(\sigma)\;=\;\Big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{4}]\Big)^{1/4}\;<\;\infty,

with explicit bounds

g_{2}(\sigma)\;\leq\;\sqrt{2}\,\Big(A^{2}+B^{2}\,\sigma^{2r}\,M_{2r}\Big)^{1/2},\qquad g_{4}(\sigma)\;\leq\;2^{3/4}\,\Big(A^{4}+B^{4}\,\sigma^{4r}\,M_{4r}\Big)^{1/4},

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
and
M_{k}:=\mathbb{E}|G|^{k}
with
G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
given by

M_{k}\;=\;\mathbb{E}|G|^{k}\;=\;\frac{2^{k/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\;\Gamma\!\Big(\frac{k+1}{2}\Big)\qquad(k>-1).

Proof.

For
p\in\{2,4\}
, by
(a+b)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(a^{p}+b^{p})
for
a,b\geq 0
,

|\phi^{\prime}(Z)|^{p}\;\leq\;2^{p-1}\big(A^{p}+B^{p}|Z|^{pr}\big).

Taking expectations with
Z=\sigma G
gives

\mathbb{E}|Z|^{pr}=\sigma^{pr}\mathbb{E}|G|^{pr}=\sigma^{pr}M_{pr}
, hence the displayed bounds.
∎

Corollary 2.1

(Uniform Lipschitz or BV slope)
.

If
\sup_{x}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq M<\infty
(i.e.,
\phi
is globally
M
-Lipschitz), then

g_{2}(\sigma)\leq M,\qquad g_{4}(\sigma)\leq M\qquad\text{for all }\sigma>0.

In particular, if
\phi^{\prime}\in BV(\mathbb{R})
with one-sided limits, then

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\;\leq\;\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})+c_{\star},\qquad c_{\star}:=|\phi^{\prime}(+\infty)-\phi^{\prime}(-\infty)|,

so
g_{2}(\sigma),g_{4}(\sigma)\leq\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})+c_{\star}
uniformly in
\sigma
.

Remark 2.4

(Justifying differentiation under the integral)
.

Under the growth condition of Lemma
2.2
, both
\phi(\sigma G)
and
\phi^{\prime}(\sigma G)

have finite moments of all orders. Therefore dominated convergence applies to the Gaussian expectations
in Lemma
2.1
, legitimizing differentiation under the expectation for
m_{2}(\sigma)
and related functionals.

Remark 2.5

(Typical cases)
.

•

ReLU / leaky-ReLU:

\phi^{\prime}
is bounded (
M\leq 1
), hence
g_{2}(\sigma),g_{4}(\sigma)\leq 1
.

•

GELU, Swish, Mish, TeLU:

\phi^{\prime}
has at most polynomial growth (indeed, bounded for GELU/Swish/Mish/TeLU), so Lemma
2.2
applies.

•

Polynomial activations:

\phi^{\prime}(x)=O(|x|^{r})
for some integer
r\geq 0
, giving the explicit polynomial-in-
\sigma
bounds above.

3
Integral Taxonomy and the 9-Dimensional Signature

We formalize the taxonomy via a nine-dimensional
integral signature
that encodes propagation statistics, tail asymptotics, and slope regularity. Throughout, fix
\sigma>0
and let
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
.

3.1
Definitions

Definition 3.1

(Primitive and tail-compensated primitive)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be locally absolutely continuous and set

F(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\phi(t)\,dt.

If the one-sided linear slopes

\alpha_{+}:=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x},\qquad\alpha_{-}:=\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x}

exist as finite real numbers, define the tail-compensated primitive

F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\Big(\phi(t)-\alpha_{+}\,t\,\mathbf{1}_{\{t\geq 0\}}-\alpha_{-}\,t\,\mathbf{1}_{\{t<0\}}\Big)\,dt.

We write

C(\phi):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\in[0,\infty]

whenever
F_{\mathrm{asym}}
is well defined (see Lemma
3.3
for sufficient conditions for finiteness).

Definition 3.2

(Gaussian statistics)
.

For
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
, set

m_{k}(\sigma):=\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(Z)^{k}\big],\qquad g_{1}(\sigma):=\mathbb{E}\big[\phi^{\prime}(Z)\big],\qquad g_{2}(\sigma):=\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{2}\big]\Big)^{1/2},\qquad\eta(\sigma):=\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(Z)\,Z\big].

Here
\phi^{\prime}
is the a.e. derivative (for kinked activations this is understood a.e. with respect to Lebesgue measure).

Definition 3.3

(Slope variation)
.

If
\phi^{\prime}
is of bounded variation on
\mathbb{R}
(i.e.,
\phi^{\prime}\in BV(\mathbb{R})
), its
total variation

is

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})\;:=\;\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{R}),

the total variation of the (distributional) second-derivative measure
\phi^{\prime\prime}
.
For piecewise
C^{1}
activations with finitely many kinks,
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
equals the sum of

\int|\phi^{\prime\prime}|
on smooth pieces plus the magnitudes of slope jumps at the kinks.

Definition 3.4

(Nine-dimensional signature)
.

The
integral signature at scale
\sigma
is

\boxed{\;\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi):=\Big(m_{1}(\sigma),\,g_{1}(\sigma),\,g_{2}(\sigma),\,m_{2}(\sigma),\,\eta(\sigma),\,\alpha_{+},\,\alpha_{-},\,\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime}),\,C(\phi)\Big).\;}

Remark 3.1

(Interpretation for network dynamics)
.

m_{2}
governs mean-field variance propagation;
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
encode average and RMS gain for perturbations;

\eta
measures alignment with inputs;
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})
capture linear tail geometry (e.g., ReLU vs. leaky-ReLU asymmetry);

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
captures curvature regularity; and
C(\phi)
measures tail-compensated excess curvature beyond linear parts.
Kernel smoothness is controlled via
g_{4}
(Theorem
5.6
) and, with a uniform slope bound, by
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
(Cor.
5.4
).

3.2
Finiteness of the compensated primitive

Write the tail residuals

r_{+}(t):=\phi(t)-\alpha_{+}t\quad(t\geq 0),\qquad r_{-}(t):=\phi(t)-\alpha_{-}t\quad(t\leq 0),

so that

F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}r_{+}(t)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{t\geq 0\}}+r_{-}(t)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{t<0\}}\,dt.

Lemma 3.1

(Residual
L^{1}
tails
\Rightarrow
bounded compensated primitive)
.

Assume
\alpha_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
exist and

r_{+}\in L^{1}([0,\infty))\quad\text{and}\quad r_{-}\in L^{1}((-\infty,0]).

Then
F_{\mathrm{asym}}
is well defined and

C(\phi)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{\infty}|r_{+}(t)|\,dt\;+\;\int_{-\infty}^{0}|r_{-}(t)|\,dt.

Proof.

For
x\geq 0
,
F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}r_{+}(t)\,dt
and so

|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|r_{+}(t)|\,dt
.
For
x\leq 0
,
F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)=-\int_{x}^{0}r_{-}(t)\,dt
and

|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{0}|r_{-}(t)|\,dt
. Take the supremum over
x
and add.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Weighted
L^{1}
slope error with vanishing residual)
.

Assume
\alpha_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
exist,
\phi
is locally absolutely continuous, and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}r_{+}(t)=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to-\infty}r_{-}(t)=0.

If

\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\alpha_{+}|\,dt<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{-\infty}^{0}|t|\,|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\alpha_{-}|\,dt<\infty,

then
F_{\mathrm{asym}}
is well defined and

C(\phi)\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\alpha_{+}|\,dt\;+\;\int_{-\infty}^{0}|t|\,|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\alpha_{-}|\,dt.

Proof.

We prove the bound on
[0,\infty)
; the negative side is analogous.
By absolute continuity and the vanishing residual,

r_{+}(t)=\phi(t)-\alpha_{+}t=-\int_{t}^{\infty}\big(\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+}\big)\,ds\qquad(t\geq 0).

Fix
x\geq 0
. Then by Tonelli/Fubini (nonnegative integrands),

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{x}r_{+}(t)\,dt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{x}\int_{t}^{\infty}|\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+}|\,ds\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+}|\Big(\int_{0}^{x}\mathbf{1}_{\{t\leq s\}}\,dt\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+}|\;\min\{x,s\}\,ds\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{\infty}s\,|\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+}|\,ds.

Taking the supremum over
x\geq 0
yields

\sup_{x\geq 0}|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}s\,|\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+}|\,ds
.
The bound on
(-\infty,0]
is identical with
|t|
replacing
t
. Summing both sides gives the claim.
∎

Remark 3.2

(Why vanishing residual is needed)
.

If
r_{+}(t)\to c\neq 0
as
t\to\infty
(e.g.
\tanh
with
\alpha_{+}=0
and
r_{+}(t)\to 1
), then

F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}r_{+}(t)\,dt
grows linearly and
C(\phi)=\infty
, as desired.
Lemma
3.2
cleanly separates this obstruction.

Proposition 3.1

(Verification for common activations)
.

For each
\phi
below,
C(\phi)<\infty
and the bound of Lemma
3.2
applies:

•

ReLU / leaky-ReLU:

\alpha_{+}=\text{slope}_{+}
,
\alpha_{-}=\text{slope}_{-}
, and
r_{\pm}\equiv 0
for
|t|

C(\phi)=0
.

•

Swish

\phi(x)=x\operatorname{sigm}(x)
:
\alpha_{+}=1
,
\alpha_{-}=0
, and
r_{\pm}(t)\to 0
exponentially; moreover

, so the weighted integrals are finite.

•

GELU

:
\alpha_{+}=1
,
\alpha_{-}=0
, with

r_{+}(t)=x(\Phi(x)-1)\sim-\varphi(x)
and
r_{-}(t)=x\Phi(x)\to 0
; since
\varphi(x)
is Gaussian, Lemma
3.2
holds.

•

Mish/TeLU:
both are smooth with
\alpha_{+}=1
,
\alpha_{-}=0

•

Saturating
(
\tanh
,
\operatorname{sigm}
):
\alpha_{\pm}=0
but

tend to nonzero constants;
C(\phi)=\infty
, which is consistent with the bias-drift bound being unnecessary (e.g. odd
\tanh
has zero mean).

3.3
Well-posedness and finiteness

Lemma 3.3

(Tail-compensated finiteness: sufficient conditions)
.

Let
\alpha_{\pm}=\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\phi(x)/x\in\mathbb{R}
exist and define

D(t):=\phi(t)-\alpha_{+}t\,\mathbf{1}_{\{t\geq 0\}}-\alpha_{-}t\,\mathbf{1}_{\{t<0\}}
.
If

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!|D(t)|\,dt<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{-\infty}^{0}\!|D(t)|\,dt<\infty,

then
F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}D(t)\,dt
is well-defined and bounded on
\mathbb{R}
, with

C(\phi)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{\infty}|D(t)|\,dt\;+\;\int_{-\infty}^{0}|D(t)|\,dt.

Proof.

For
x\geq 0
,
|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|D(t)|\,dt
.
For
x\leq 0
,
|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{0}|D(t)|\,dt
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Weighted slope error with vanishing residual)
.

Assume
\phi
is locally absolutely continuous,
\alpha_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
exist, and

r_{\pm}(t):=\phi(t)-\alpha_{\pm}t\to 0
as
t\to\pm\infty
.
If

\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\alpha_{+}|\,dt<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{-\infty}^{0}|t|\,|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\alpha_{-}|\,dt<\infty,

then
F_{\mathrm{asym}}
is well-defined and

C(\phi)\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\alpha_{+}|\,dt\;+\;\int_{-\infty}^{0}|t|\,|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\alpha_{-}|\,dt.

Proof.

For
t\geq 0
,
r_{+}(t)=-\int_{t}^{\infty}(\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+})\,ds
by absolute continuity and the limit
r_{+}(t)\to 0
.
Thus for
x\geq 0
,

\Big|\int_{0}^{x}r_{+}(t)\,dt\Big|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+}|\,\min\{x,s\}\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}s\,|\phi^{\prime}(s)-\alpha_{+}|\,ds.

The negative tail is identical with
|t|
in place of
t
.
∎

Remark 3.3

(On necessity)
.

Without a sign/monotonicity assumption, boundedness of
F_{\mathrm{asym}}
does
not
imply
D\in L^{1}
on the tails (conditional convergence may occur). Lemma
3.3
is therefore stated as a
sufficient
condition. If, however,
D
has eventual constant sign on each tail, then
F_{\mathrm{asym}}
bounded is equivalent to
D\in L^{1}
on that tail.

Theorem 3.1

(Well-posedness of
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi)
)
.

Fix
\sigma>0
and
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
.
Suppose
\phi
is measurable with at most polynomial growth, a.e. differentiable, and
\phi^{\prime}
has at most polynomial growth.
Then the Gaussian statistics

m_{k}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[\phi(Z)^{k}]\ (k=1,2),\quad g_{1}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)],\quad g_{2}(\sigma)=\big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{2}]\big)^{1/2},\quad\eta(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[Z\,\phi(Z)]

are finite. If
\alpha_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
exist and either Lemma
3.3
or Lemma
3.4
holds, then
C(\phi)<\infty
. If
\phi^{\prime}\in BV(\mathbb{R})
with finitely many slope jumps and
\phi^{\prime\prime}\in L^{1}
on smooth pieces, then
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})<\infty
.

Proof.

Gaussian finiteness.

Polynomial growth gives
\phi(\cdot),\phi^{\prime}(\cdot)\in L^{p}(\gamma_{\sigma})
for all finite
p
; hence
m_{1},m_{2},g_{1},g_{2},\eta
are finite (the identity
\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1}
follows from Gaussian integration by parts).

C(\phi)
.
By either Lemma
3.3
(residual
L^{1}
tails) or Lemma
3.4
(weighted slope error with vanishing residual),
F_{\mathrm{asym}}
is bounded, so
C(\phi)<\infty
.

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
.
On each smooth piece, the variation equals
\int|\phi^{\prime\prime}|
; add the finitely many jump magnitudes at the kinks to obtain
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})<\infty
.
∎

Remark 3.4

(Alternative hypotheses)
.

If you prefer to avoid the global “polynomial growth” standing assumption, the conclusions for
m_{1},m_{2},g_{1},g_{2},\eta
also follow from the simpler bounds:

|\phi(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{r}),\;|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq A+B|x|^{s}

for some
r,s\geq 0
, using finiteness of Gaussian moments (cf. Lemma
2.2
).

3.4
Affine reparameterization with bias

We handle the general reparameterization

\tilde{\phi}(x):=c\,\phi(ax+b)+d,\qquad a,c\neq 0,\;b,d\in\mathbb{R}.

All statistics are taken under the same input law
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
.
Let
Y:=aZ+b\sim\mathcal{N}(b,a^{2}\sigma^{2})
.

Theorem 3.2

(Affine reparameterization laws (with bias))
.

For
\tilde{\phi}(x)=c\,\phi(ax+b)+d
and
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
,
Y=aZ+b
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{1}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\phi}(Z)]=c\,\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y)]+d,

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{2}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\phi}(Z)^{2}]=c^{2}\,\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y)^{2}]+2cd\,\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y)]+d^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{1}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}(Z)]=c\,a\,\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Y)]=\frac{c}{a\,\sigma^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\!\big[\phi(Y)\,(Y-b)\big],

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{2}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}(Z)^{2}]\Big)^{1/2}=|c\,a|\,\Big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Y)^{2}]\Big)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\phi}(Z)\,Z]=\frac{c}{a}\,\mathbb{E}\!\big[\phi(Y)\,(Y-b)\big]=\sigma^{2}\,\tilde{g}_{1}(\sigma),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{TV}}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{TV}(\tilde{\phi}^{\prime})=|c\,a|\,\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime}).

If the one-sided linear slopes
\alpha_{\pm}=\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\phi(x)/x
exist (finite), then

\tilde{\alpha}_{+}=\begin{cases}c\,a\,\alpha_{+},&a>0,\\
c\,a\,\alpha_{-},&a<0,\end{cases}\qquad\tilde{\alpha}_{-}=\begin{cases}c\,a\,\alpha_{-},&a>0,\\
c\,a\,\alpha_{+},&a<0.\end{cases}

Moreover, for the tail-compensated primitive
C(\cdot)
:

(i)

If
d=0
, then
C(\tilde{\phi})\leq\frac{|c|}{|a|}\,C(\phi)+\Delta(\phi,a,b)
with a finite offset

\Delta(\phi,a,b)
depending only on
(\alpha_{\pm},\phi|_{[-|b|/|a|-1,\,|b|/|a|+1]})
. In particular,

C(\phi)<\infty\Rightarrow C(\tilde{\phi})<\infty
.

(ii)

If
d\neq 0
, then
C(\tilde{\phi})=\infty
(a nonzero constant shift produces linear growth in the primitive).

Proof.

The formulas for
\tilde{m}_{1},\tilde{m}_{2}
follow by linearity.
By the chain rule,
\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}(x)=c\,a\,\phi^{\prime}(ax+b)
a.e., giving
\tilde{g}_{1},\tilde{g}_{2}
.
For the Stein identity with nonzero mean, if
Y\sim\mathcal{N}(b,a^{2}\sigma^{2})
then

\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Y)]=\frac{1}{a^{2}\sigma^{2}}\mathbb{E}[(Y-b)\phi(Y)]
, which yields both
the alternate expression for
\tilde{g}_{1}
and
\tilde{\eta}=\sigma^{2}\tilde{g}_{1}
.
The
\mathrm{TV}
scaling is the standard change-of-variables for the distributional second derivative:
on smooth pieces,
\int|\tilde{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(x)|dx=|c||a|\int|\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)|du
, and jump magnitudes of
\phi^{\prime}
scale by
|c\,a|
.

For
\tilde{\alpha}_{\pm}
, note

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\tilde{\phi}(x)/x=c\,\lim_{x\to+\infty}\phi(ax+b)/(x)=c\,a\,\lim_{x\to+\infty}\phi(ax+b)/(ax+b)
, where the one-sided limit uses the sign of
a
.

For
C(\cdot)
, write (with
d=0
here)

\tilde{D}(x):=\tilde{\phi}(x)-\tilde{\alpha}_{+}x\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x\geq 0\}}-\tilde{\alpha}_{-}x\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x<0\}}=c\Big(\phi(ax+b)-\alpha_{\mathrm{sgn}(ax+b)}(ax+b)\Big)+c\big(\alpha_{\mathrm{sgn}(ax+b)}-\alpha_{\mathrm{sgn}(x)}\big)(ax+b).

The first bracket is the residual
D
for
\phi
evaluated at
u=ax+b
; the second bracket is
nonzero only on the compact set where
\mathrm{sgn}(x)\neq\mathrm{sgn}(ax+b)
, which is contained in

\{x:|x+b/a|\leq 1/|a|\}
, and thus contributes a bounded additive constant to the primitive.
Making the substitution
u=ax+b
in
\int_{0}^{x}\tilde{D}(t)dt
yields the scaling factor
|c|/|a|
for the first term and a bounded
offset
\Delta(\phi,a,b)
for the second. Hence
C(\tilde{\phi})\leq\frac{|c|}{|a|}C(\phi)+\Delta(\phi,a,b)
.
If
d\neq 0
, the residual includes an additive constant
d
, whose primitive grows linearly; thus
C(\tilde{\phi})=\infty
.
∎

Corollary 3.1

(Stability of taxonomy coordinates)
.

At fixed input scale
\sigma
, the Gaussian coordinates
(m_{1},g_{1},g_{2},m_{2},\eta)

transform according to Theorem
3.2
and remain finite whenever the corresponding
quantities for
\phi
under
Y\sim\mathcal{N}(b,a^{2}\sigma^{2})
are finite (e.g., under polynomial growth).
If
\alpha_{\pm}
exist (finite), then
\tilde{\alpha}_{\pm}
exist (finite) as above, and

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})<\infty\Rightarrow\mathrm{TV}(\tilde{\phi}^{\prime})<\infty
.
For the compensated primitive,
C(\phi)<\infty
implies
C(\tilde{\phi})<\infty
when
d=0
;
a nonzero constant shift
d
makes
C(\tilde{\phi})=\infty
.

Remark 3.5

(Two useful special cases)
.

(i) If
b=d=0
then the compensated primitive scales
exactly
:

C(\tilde{\phi})=\frac{|c|}{|a|}\,C(\phi)
.
(ii) If
a>0
and
b=0
, the slope mapping reduces to
\tilde{\alpha}_{\pm}=c\,a\,\alpha_{\pm}
.

3.5
Closure under limits with bounded slope variation

Theorem 3.3

(Closure)
.

Let
(\phi_{n})
be locally absolutely continuous activations with

\sup_{n}\mathrm{TV}(\phi_{n}^{\prime})<\infty,\qquad|\phi_{n}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{r}),\qquad|\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{s})\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},\ n\in\mathbb{N},

for some
C,r,s\geq 0
independent of
n
. Assume
\phi_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then:

1.

BV-compactness of the slope and local AC of the limit.

There exists (necessarily unique a.e.)
\phi^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
such that,
up to a subsequence,
\phi_{n}^{\prime}\to\phi^{\prime}
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
and pointwise a.e., and

\phi
is locally absolutely continuous with distributional derivative
\phi^{\prime}
. Moreover,

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})\;\leq\;\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{TV}(\phi_{n}^{\prime}).

2.

Convergence of Gaussian signatures at fixed scale.

For any fixed
\sigma>0
and
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
,

m_{k}^{(n)}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[\phi_{n}(Z)^{k}]\ \to\ m_{k}(\sigma),\quad k=1,2;\qquad g_{1}^{(n)}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[\phi_{n}^{\prime}(Z)]\ \to\ g_{1}(\sigma),

g_{2}^{(n)}(\sigma)=\Big(\mathbb{E}[\phi_{n}^{\prime}(Z)^{2}]\Big)^{1/2}\ \to\ g_{2}(\sigma),\qquad\eta^{(n)}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[Z\,\phi_{n}(Z)]\ \to\ \eta(\sigma).

3.

Stability of the compensated primitive.

If
\alpha_{\pm}(\phi_{n})\to\alpha_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sup_{n}C(\phi_{n})<\infty
, then

C(\phi)<\infty
and the compensated primitives satisfy

F_{\mathrm{asym},n}\ \to\ F_{\mathrm{asym}}\quad\text{locally uniformly on }\mathbb{R},

hence
C(\phi)\leq\sup_{n}C(\phi_{n})
.

Proof.

(1)
BV compactness and AC.

On every compact interval
I=[-R,R]
, the uniform
BV
bound

\sup_{n}\mathrm{TV}(\phi_{n}^{\prime}|_{I})<\infty
and the growth bound

|\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{s})
imply a uniform
L^{1}(I)
bound:

\int_{I}|\phi_{n}^{\prime}|\leq|I|\,C(1+R^{s})+\mathrm{TV}(\phi_{n}^{\prime}|_{I})
.
By the compact embedding
BV(I)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(I)
(Helly’s selection),
there is a subsequence (diagonal over
R\to\infty
) with

\phi_{n}^{\prime}\to\phi^{\prime}
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and a.e.
Since
\phi_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, passing to a further subsequence yields a.e. convergence of
\phi_{n}
to a representative of
\phi
.
For any
x<y
in a compact interval,

\phi_{n}(y)-\phi_{n}(x)=\int_{x}^{y}\phi_{n}^{\prime}(t)\,dt.

Letting
n\to\infty
and using
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
convergence of
\phi_{n}^{\prime}
gives

\phi(y)-\phi(x)=\int_{x}^{y}\phi^{\prime}(t)\,dt
. Hence
\phi
is locally absolutely continuous with a.e. derivative
\phi^{\prime}
.
Lower semicontinuity of total variation under
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
convergence gives

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})\leq\liminf_{n}\mathrm{TV}(\phi_{n}^{\prime})
.

(2)
Gaussian signatures.

Fix
\sigma>0
. The uniform polynomial growth of
\phi_{n},\phi_{n}^{\prime}
implies the existence of an integrable dominator under
\gamma_{\sigma}
:

|\phi_{n}(x)|^{k}\leq C_{k}\,(1+|x|^{kr})\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\sigma}),\qquad|\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)|^{p}\leq C_{p}\,(1+|x|^{ps})\in L^{1}(\gamma_{\sigma}),

for
k\in\{1,2\}
and
p\in\{1,2\}
, uniformly in
n
. On each compact interval,
\phi_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}
hence pointwise a.e. along a subsequence; combined with the dominator under
\gamma_{\sigma}
, dominated convergence yields

m_{k}^{(n)}(\sigma)\to m_{k}(\sigma)
,
g_{1}^{(n)}(\sigma)\to g_{1}(\sigma)
, and

\mathbb{E}[\phi_{n}^{\prime}(Z)^{2}]\to\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{2}]
, whence
g_{2}^{(n)}(\sigma)\to g_{2}(\sigma)
by continuity of the square root.
Similarly,
|Z\phi_{n}(Z)|\leq C^{\prime}(1+|Z|^{1+r})
has an integrable dominator under
\gamma_{\sigma}
, so
\eta^{(n)}(\sigma)\to\eta(\sigma)
.

(3)
Compensated primitives.

Set
D_{n}(t):=\phi_{n}(t)-\alpha_{+,n}t\,\mathbf{1}_{\{t\geq 0\}}-\alpha_{-,n}t\,\mathbf{1}_{\{t<0\}}
and

D
the analogous quantity for
\phi,\alpha_{\pm}
. Since
\phi_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and
\alpha_{\pm,n}\to\alpha_{\pm}
, we have
D_{n}\to D
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and hence in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
(finite measure sets).
For any
R>0
and
x\in[-R,R]
,

\big|F_{\mathrm{asym},n}(x)-F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)\big|=\Big|\int_{0}^{x}(D_{n}-D)(t)\,dt\Big|\leq\sqrt{2R}\,\|D_{n}-D\|_{L^{2}([-R,R])}\ \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\ 0,

so
F_{\mathrm{asym},n}\to F_{\mathrm{asym}}
locally uniformly.
Moreover,
\sup_{n}C(\phi_{n})<\infty
implies
|F_{\mathrm{asym},n}(x)|\leq M
for all
x
and
n
; by pointwise convergence at each fixed
x
(choose
R>|x|
), we get
|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|\leq M
for all
x
, hence
C(\phi)\leq M
.
∎

3.6
Motivation for the Nine Components

The nine coordinates of
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi)
are chosen to jointly capture

statistical propagation, asymptotic geometry, and regularity
at a fixed input scale
\sigma>0
.

•

Propagation moments
(m_{1},m_{2})
.

m_{1}(\sigma)
controls mean centering;
m_{2}(\sigma)
governs the mean-field variance recursion

q_{\ell+1}=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}(\sqrt{q_{\ell}})+\sigma_{b}^{2}
.
Local variance stability at an equilibrium
q^{\star}
depends on the
slope
of
m_{2}
:

|f^{\prime}(q^{\star})|=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}^{\prime}(\sqrt{q^{\star}})/(2\sqrt{q^{\star}})<1

(Theorem
5.3
, Theorem
5.5
).

•

Derivative gains
(g_{1},g_{2})
.

g_{1}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)]
and
g_{2}(\sigma)=(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{2}])^{1/2}
quantify
average and RMS layerwise gain. At stationarity, mean-square perturbations contract if

\sigma_{W}\,g_{2}(\sqrt{q^{\star}})<1
(Theorem
5.5
). Moreover, by Gaussian
integration by parts,
\eta(\sigma)=\sigma^{2}g_{1}(\sigma)
(Lemma
2.1
),
so
g_{1}
also mediates
F
-based Lyapunov constructions.

•

Input alignment
\eta
.

\eta(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}[Z\,\phi(Z)]
measures linear alignment of outputs with inputs.
It is redundant with
g_{1}
via
\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1}
, but we keep it for interpretability
and because several Lyapunov and drift bounds are most naturally expressed in terms of
\eta
.

•

Asymptotic slopes
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})
.

The one-sided linear asymptotes encode tail geometry (e.g., ReLU vs. leaky-ReLU asymmetry vs. saturation).
They control the
leading term
of the bias drift:

\mathbb{E}[\phi(Z)]=\frac{\alpha_{+}-\alpha_{-}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\sigma+O(C(\phi)/\sigma)

(Theorem
5.4
).

•

Slope variation
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
.

The total variation of the slope quantifies how curvature is distributed (kinks vs. smooth bending).
It underlies kernel regularity via a uniform slope proxy:
if
\sup|\phi^{\prime}|\leq M
then
\|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}K(x,y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sqrt{3}\,M^{2}
,
and with one-sided limits
M\leq\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})+c_{\star}

(Corollary
5.4
,
c_{\star}:=|\phi^{\prime}(+\infty)-\phi^{\prime}(-\infty)|
).

•

Tail-compensated curvature
C(\phi)
.

C(\phi)=\sup_{x}|F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)|
controls the
remainder
in the bias drift and
ensures
F
-based Lyapunov functionals remain coercive at large
|x|
.
Sufficient conditions for
C(\phi)<\infty
include residual
L^{1}
tails or
weighted
L^{1}
slope error with vanishing residual (Lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
).

Kernel smoothness and the role of
g_{4}
.

The mixed Hessian of the induced kernel satisfies the dimension-free bound

\|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}K(x,y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sqrt{3}\,g_{4}(\|x\|)g_{4}(\|y\|)
with

g_{4}(\sigma):=(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{4}])^{1/4}
(Theorem
5.6
).
We do not include
g_{4}
in the signature to keep it nine-dimensional; instead, we control
g_{4}

through
\sup|\phi^{\prime}|
(Lipschitz activations) or via the BV proxy
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})+c_{\star}

(Corollary
5.4
).

Affine awareness and robustness.

Under
\tilde{\phi}(x)=c\,\phi(ax+b)+d
with fixed input scale
\sigma
,
the Gaussian statistics map according to Theorem
3.2
(computed under
Y\sim\mathcal{N}(b,a^{2}\sigma^{2})
),

\mathrm{TV}(\tilde{\phi}^{\prime})=|c\,a|\,\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
, and slopes transform with a sign-aware swap when
a<0
.
The compensated primitive scales as
C(\tilde{\phi})=(|c|/|a|)C(\phi)
when
b=d=0
; with
b\neq 0
one gets a bounded offset, while a nonzero constant shift
d
makes
C(\tilde{\phi})=\infty
.
Thus the signature is
affine-aware
: taxonomy membership (finiteness/existence) is preserved under reparameterizations except that adding a constant
d\neq 0
exits the
C(\phi)<\infty
class.

Closure and stability under limits.

With uniformly bounded slope variation and uniform polynomial growth, the class is closed:

\phi_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
implies
\phi
is locally absolutely continuous with

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})\leq\liminf\mathrm{TV}(\phi_{n}^{\prime})
and all Gaussian coordinates

(m_{1},g_{1},g_{2},m_{2},\eta)
converge;
C(\phi)
passes to the limit under
\sup_{n}C(\phi_{n})<\infty

(Theorem
3.3
).

Why nine.

These components are
sufficient and near-minimal
for the results we prove:

m_{2}
(and
m_{2}^{\prime}
) for variance dynamics;
g_{2}
for perturbation contraction;
(\alpha_{\pm},C(\phi))
for bias drift and Lyapunov coercivity; and
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
(as a proxy for
\sup|\phi^{\prime}|
) for kernel curvature.
They remain finite under broad, checkable conditions (Preliminaries), transform predictably under affine reparameterizations, and are stable under limits—yielding a compact, practical scaffold for activation design and analysis.

4
Classification of Common Activations

We evaluate the signature

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi)=\big(m_{1}(\sigma),\,g_{1}(\sigma),\,g_{2}(\sigma),\,m_{2}(\sigma),\,\eta(\sigma),\,\alpha_{+},\,\alpha_{-},\,\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime}),\,C(\phi)\big)

for standard activations. Throughout
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
and, unless noted, all Gaussian expectations are finite whenever
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
have at most polynomial growth (Remark
2.1
, Lemma
2.2
). We use

\eta(\sigma)=\sigma^{2}g_{1}(\sigma)
whenever
\phi
is a.e. differentiable with suitable growth (Lemma
2.1
).

4.1
ReLU

Theorem 4.1

(ReLU signature)
.

For
\phi(x)=\max\{0,x\}
,

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi)=\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\;\frac{1}{2},\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\;\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2},\;\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2},\;1,\;0,\;1,\;0\Big).

Proof.

\phi^{\prime}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{\{x>0\}}
a.e., so

g_{1}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}]=\tfrac{1}{2}
and

g_{2}=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}]}=2^{-1/2}
.

m_{1}=\mathbb{E}[Z\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}]=\sigma/\sqrt{2\pi}
and

m_{2}=\mathbb{E}[Z^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}]=\sigma^{2}/2
.
By Lemma
2.1
,
\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1}=\sigma^{2}/2
.
Slopes:
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(1,0)
.

\phi^{\prime}
jumps by
1
at
0
, hence
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})=1
.
For
C(\phi)
, the residual
D(t)=\phi(t)-t\mathbf{1}_{\{t\geq 0\}}
is identically
0
, so
F_{\mathrm{asym}}\equiv 0
and
C(\phi)=0
.
∎

4.2
Leaky-ReLU

Theorem 4.2

(Leaky-ReLU signature)
.

Let
\phi(x)=\begin{cases}x,&x\geq 0\\
\alpha x,&x<0\end{cases}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then

\displaystyle m_{1}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\sigma,\qquad m_{2}=\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{2}\,\sigma^{2},\qquad\eta=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\,\sigma^{2},

\displaystyle g_{1}=\frac{1+\alpha}{2},\qquad g_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{2}},\qquad\alpha_{+}=1,\ \alpha_{-}=\alpha,\ \mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})=|1-\alpha|,\ C(\phi)=0.

Proof.

Split at
0
and use symmetry of
Z
; the formulas follow as in the ReLU case.

\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1}
by Lemma
2.1
. The residual vanishes on both tails, hence
C(\phi)=0
.
∎

4.3
Tanh

Theorem 4.3

(Tanh signature)
.

For
\phi(x)=\tanh x
,

\alpha_{+}=\alpha_{-}=0,\qquad g_{1}=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{sech}^{2}(Z)],\qquad g_{2}=\big(\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{sech}^{4}(Z)]\big)^{1/2},

m_{1}=0,\qquad m_{2}=\mathbb{E}[\tanh^{2}(Z)]=1-\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{sech}^{2}(Z)],\qquad\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1}=\sigma^{2}\,\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{sech}^{2}(Z)],

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)|\,dx<\infty,\qquad C(\phi)=\infty.

Proof.

Oddness gives
m_{1}=0
.
\phi^{\prime}(x)=\mathrm{sech}^{2}x
yields
g_{1},g_{2}
.
By Lemma
2.1
,
\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1}
(note
\eta>0
;
z\tanh z
is even).
Slopes are
0
by saturation. Since
\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)=-2\tanh x\,\mathrm{sech}^{2}x
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi^{\prime\prime}|=2\int|\tanh x|\,\mathrm{sech}^{2}x\,dx=2\int_{-1}^{1}|u|\,du=2<\infty

(using
u=\tanh x
), so
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})<\infty
.
Here
F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\tanh t\,dt
diverges linearly as
x\to+\infty
, hence
C(\phi)=\infty
.
∎

4.4
Sigmoid

Theorem 4.4

(Sigmoid signature)
.

For
\phi(x)=\operatorname{sigm}(x)=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}
,

m_{1}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad m_{2}=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{sigm}(Z)^{2}],\qquad g_{1}=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{sigm}(Z)(1-\operatorname{sigm}(Z))],

g_{2}=\Big(\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{sigm}(Z)^{2}(1-\operatorname{sigm}(Z))^{2}]\Big)^{1/2},\qquad\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1},

\alpha_{\pm}=0,\qquad\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)|\,dx<\infty,\qquad C(\phi)=\infty.

Proof.

Symmetry
\operatorname{sigm}(-x)=1-\operatorname{sigm}(x)
gives
m_{1}=1/2
.

\phi^{\prime}(x)=\operatorname{sigm}(x)(1-\operatorname{sigm}(x))
yields
g_{1},g_{2}
,
and
\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1}
by Lemma
2.1
.
Slopes vanish by saturation.
\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)
decays exponentially, so
\int|\phi^{\prime\prime}|<\infty
.
As
\operatorname{sigm}(t)\to 1
,
F_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\operatorname{sigm}(t)\,dt
grows linearly, hence
C(\phi)=\infty
.
∎

4.5
Swish

Theorem 4.5

(Swish signature)
.

For
\phi(x)=x\,\operatorname{sigm}(x)
,

\alpha_{+}=1,\qquad\alpha_{-}=0,\qquad g_{1}=\mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{sigm}(Z)+Z\,\operatorname{sigm}(Z)(1-\operatorname{sigm}(Z))\big],

g_{2}=\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\operatorname{sigm}(Z)+Z\,\operatorname{sigm}(Z)(1-\operatorname{sigm}(Z))\big)^{2}\big]\Big)^{1/2},\quad\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1},

and
m_{1},m_{2}
are finite for all
\sigma>0
, with
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})<\infty
and
C(\phi)<\infty
.

Proof.

As
x\to+\infty
,
\operatorname{sigm}(x)\to 1
so
\phi(x)\sim x
; as
x\to-\infty
,
\operatorname{sigm}(x)\sim e^{x}
so
x\,\operatorname{sigm}(x)\to 0
; hence
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(1,0)
.

\phi^{\prime}(x)=\operatorname{sigm}(x)+x\,\operatorname{sigm}(x)(1-\operatorname{sigm}(x))
gives the expressions for
g_{1},g_{2}
;
\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1}
by Lemma
2.1
.
The residuals satisfy
\phi(t)-t\mathbf{1}_{\{t\geq 0\}}=t(\operatorname{sigm}(t)-1)\sim-te^{-t}
on
[0,\infty)
and
\phi(t)\sim te^{t}
on

C(\phi)<\infty
by Lemma
3.3
.
Smoothness and exponential tail decay of
\phi^{\prime\prime}
imply
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})=\int|\phi^{\prime\prime}|<\infty
.
∎

Theorem 4.6

(GELU signature)
.

For
\phi(x)=x\,\Phi(x)
with

the standard normal CDF and
\varphi
its density,

\alpha_{+}=1,\qquad\alpha_{-}=0,\qquad\phi^{\prime}(x)=\Phi(x)+x\,\varphi(x),\qquad\eta=\sigma^{2}g_{1},\qquad\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})<\infty,\qquad C(\phi)<\infty,

m_{1},m_{2},g_{1},g_{2}
are finite for all
\sigma>0
.

Proof.

Limits of
\Phi
give
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(1,0)

\phi(t)\to 0

3.3
.

\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})<\infty
.

4.10
Taxonomy Class Membership

Table
3
summarizes the placement of widely used activation functions within our taxonomy according to their asymptotic slopes
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})
. The classification reflects three qualitatively distinct regimes of growth:

Bounded, saturating activations
(
\mathcal{A}_{0}
): Functions such as
\tanh
and sigmoid have finite limits at both tails, yielding
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(0,0)
. Their boundedness enforces variance damping and gradient saturation, which historically hindered training of very deep networks but remain useful for recurrent stability and probabilistic modeling.

Linear-growth activations
(
\mathcal{A}_{1}
): These functions grow at most linearly as
x\to\pm\infty
, with slopes
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})
characterizing symmetry or asymmetry. ReLU and leaky ReLU represent asymmetric members:
\operatorname{ReLU}(x)
has
(1,0)
while leaky ReLU interpolates between
(1,\alpha)
with
0<\alpha<1
. Smooth modern activations such as Swish, GELU, Mish, and TeLU also belong to this class, but distinguish themselves by maintaining differentiability and curvature regularity, which can improve optimization and generalization.

Superlinear activations
(
\mathcal{A}_{>1}
): Polynomial activations
\phi(x)=x^{k},\,k\geq 2
grow faster than linearly, corresponding to
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(\infty,\infty)
. While theoretically expressive, their unbounded derivative growth leads to unstable variance propagation and poor conditioning in deep architectures.

This classification reveals the unifying role of asymptotic slopes as a coarse but predictive invariant. In particular,
\mathcal{A}_{1}
captures the majority of practical nonlinearities used in modern deep learning: they combine linear tail behavior (ensuring stable propagation under initialization) with diverse local regularities (dictating curvature and kernel smoothness). By contrast,
\mathcal{A}_{0}
activations emphasize boundedness at the cost of vanishing gradients, and
\mathcal{A}_{>1}
illustrates the instability of unchecked superlinear growth. The taxonomy thus provides a principled lens for distinguishing activation families beyond ad hoc empirical comparisons.

Lyapunov and Propagation Stability from Integral Signatures

In this section we connect entries of

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi)=(m_{1},g_{1},g_{2},m_{2},\eta,\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime}),C(\phi))

to stability of (i) scalar recursions
x_{t+1}=\phi(ax_{t}+b)
and
(ii) signal propagation in wide random layers.
Throughout,
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
and expectations are with respect to
Z
unless stated.

Notation.

We use
\sigma>0
exclusively for
standard deviation
of Gaussians.
The logistic activation is denoted
\operatorname{sigm}(x):=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}
.

5.1
Distributional Contraction via
g_{2}

Lemma 5.1

(Gaussian
L_{2}
slope bound)
.

If
\phi^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\gamma_{\sigma})
, then for any
h\in\mathbb{R}
,

\frac{\big(\mathbb{E}[(\phi(Z+h)-\phi(Z))^{2}]\big)^{1/2}}{|h|}\;\leq\;g_{2}(\sigma),\qquad g_{2}(\sigma):=\Big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{2}]\Big)^{1/2}.

Moreover, if
\phi^{\prime}
is a.e. continuous then equality holds in the limit
h\to 0
.

Proof.

Mean value form:
\phi(Z+h)-\phi(Z)=\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(Z+th)\,h\,dt
.
By Jensen and Fubini,

\mathbb{E}[(\cdot)^{2}]\leq h^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(Z+th)^{2}]\,dt=h^{2}g_{2}(\sigma)^{2}

using shift-invariance of
Z
. The limit case follows from a.e. continuity.
∎

Theorem 5.1

(Contraction in
L_{2}
)
.

Consider
T(x)=\phi(ax+b)
and let
X,Y
be square-integrable r.v.’s independent of
Z
.
If
|a|\,g_{2}(\sigma)<1
, then

\|T(X)-T(Y)\|_{L_{2}}\leq|a|\,g_{2}(\sigma)\,\|X-Y\|_{L_{2}},

so
T
is a strict contraction on
L_{2}(\Omega)
and has a unique
L_{2}
fixed point.

Proof.

Condition on
(X,Y)
and apply Lemma
5.1
with
h=a(X-Y)
.
Take the
L_{2}
norm and use
|a|\,g_{2}(\sigma)<1
.
∎

5.2
Lyapunov Stability

We now give a precise Lyapunov descent proof, resolving the issues raised.

Theorem 5.2

(Lyapunov via contraction)
.

Let
\phi
be globally Lipschitz with
L:=\sup_{x}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|<\infty
and set
T(x)=\phi(ax+b)
.
If
L_{T}:=\sup_{x}|T^{\prime}(x)|\leq|a|\,L<1
, then
T
is a strict contraction on
\mathbb{R}
,
admits a unique fixed point
x^{\star}
, and

V(x):=\frac{1}{2}\,(x-x^{\star})^{2}

is a strict Lyapunov function satisfying the quantitative descent

V(T(x))-V(x)\;\leq\;-\,c\,|T(x)-x|^{2}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

with

c\;=\;\frac{1+L_{T}}{2(1-L_{T})}\;>\;0.

Proof.

By the contraction hypothesis
L_{T}<1
, there exists a unique fixed point
x^{\star}
and

|T(x)-x^{\star}|\leq L_{T}\,|x-x^{\star}|
.
Then

V(T(x))-V(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(|T(x)-x^{\star}|^{2}-|x-x^{\star}|^{2}\big)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(L_{T}^{2}-1)\,|x-x^{\star}|^{2}.

Also

|T(x)-x|=|(T(x)-x^{\star})-(x-x^{\star})|\geq(1-L_{T})\,|x-x^{\star}|,

hence
|x-x^{\star}|\leq\frac{1}{1-L_{T}}|T(x)-x|
. Substituting,

V(T(x))-V(x)\leq-\,\frac{1+L_{T}}{2(1-L_{T})}\,|T(x)-x|^{2},

giving the claim with
c=\frac{1+L_{T}}{2(1-L_{T})}
.
∎

Corollary 5.1

(F-based Lyapunov under monotonic contraction, zero fixed point)
.

Assume
\phi
is nondecreasing with
L:=\sup_{x}\phi^{\prime}(x)<\infty
(in the a.e. sense),
and suppose
a\geq 0
,
b=0
, and
\phi(0)=0
.
Define
T(x):=\phi(ax)
and
F(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\phi(t)\,dt
.
For any
\lambda>a^{2}L
, the functional

V(x)\;:=\;F(ax)\;-\;\frac{\lambda}{2}\,x^{2}

satisfies the strict descent

V(T(x))-V(x)\;\leq\;-\;\frac{\lambda-a^{2}L}{2}\,|T(x)-x|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Let
E:=T(x)-x
and
z:=ax
, so
aE=a(T(x)-x)
.
Since
\phi
is
L
-Lipschitz and
F
is
L
-smooth (descent lemma),

F(z+aE)-F(z)\;\leq\;aE\,\phi(z)\;+\;\frac{L}{2}\,a^{2}E^{2}.

Therefore

V(T)-V(x)\;=\;F(z+aE)-F(z)\;-\;\frac{\lambda}{2}\big((x+E)^{2}-x^{2}\big)\;\leq\;aE\,\phi(z)\;-\;\lambda xE\;+\;\frac{a^{2}L-\lambda}{2}E^{2}.

Because
a\geq 0
,
\phi
is nondecreasing,
\phi(0)=0
, and
b=0
,
we have
\mathrm{sign}(T(x))=\mathrm{sign}(x)
and
T(x)\in[0,x]
if
x\geq 0
(and
[x,0]
if
x\leq 0
),
so
E=T(x)-x
has the opposite sign of
x
. Hence
xE\leq 0
and also
\phi(z)=T(x)
yields
T(x)E\leq 0
.
Using
aE\,\phi(z)=a\,T(x)E\leq 0
and
-\lambda xE\leq 0
, we get

V(T)-V(x)\;\leq\;\frac{a^{2}L-\lambda}{2}\,E^{2}\;=\;-\frac{\lambda-a^{2}L}{2}\,|E|^{2},

which is strictly negative for
E\neq 0
when
\lambda>a^{2}L
.
∎

Corollary 5.2

(F-based Lyapunov under monotonic contraction, general fixed point)
.

Assume
\phi
is nondecreasing with
L:=\sup_{x}\phi^{\prime}(x)<\infty
, and let
a\geq 0
.
Let
x^{\star}
be the unique fixed point of
T(x):=\phi(ax+b)
and set
z^{\star}:=ax^{\star}+b
, so
\phi(z^{\star})=x^{\star}
.
Define the
centered primitive

\widehat{F}(u)\;:=\;F(u+z^{\star})-F(z^{\star})-\phi(z^{\star})\,u\quad\text{with}\quad F(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\phi(t)\,dt.

Then for any
\lambda>a^{2}L
the functional

V(x)\;:=\;\widehat{F}\big(a(x-x^{\star})\big)\;-\;\frac{\lambda}{2}\,(x-x^{\star})^{2}

satisfies

V\!\big(T(x)\big)-V(x)\;\leq\;-\;\frac{\lambda-a^{2}L}{2}\,|T(x)-x|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Let
y:=x-x^{\star}
,
E:=T(x)-x
, and note
T(x)-x^{\star}=\phi(z^{\star}+ay)-\phi(z^{\star})
.
By
L
-smoothness of
F
and the definition of
\widehat{F}
,

\widehat{F}\big(a(y+E)\big)-\widehat{F}(ay)\;\leq\;aE\,\big(\phi(z^{\star}+ay)-\phi(z^{\star})\big)\;+\;\frac{L}{2}\,a^{2}E^{2}.

Also,

-\frac{\lambda}{2}\big((y+E)^{2}-y^{2}\big)\;=\;-\lambda\,yE-\frac{\lambda}{2}E^{2}.

Thus

V(T)-V(x)\;\leq\;aE\,\big(\phi(z^{\star}+ay)-\phi(z^{\star})\big)\;-\lambda\,yE\;+\;\frac{a^{2}L-\lambda}{2}\,E^{2}.

Since
a\geq 0
and
\phi
is nondecreasing,
T
is increasing with fixed point
x^{\star}
, hence
T(x)
lies between
x
and
x^{\star}
, so
E
has the opposite sign of
y
, i.e.,
yE\leq 0
, and

\phi(z^{\star}+ay)-\phi(z^{\star})
has the same sign as
y
, hence the product with
E
is nonpositive as well:

aE\,\big(\phi(z^{\star}+ay)-\phi(z^{\star})\big)\leq 0,\qquad-\lambda\,yE\leq 0.

It follows that

V(T)-V(x)\;\leq\;\frac{a^{2}L-\lambda}{2}\,E^{2}\;=\;-\frac{\lambda-a^{2}L}{2}\,|E|^{2},

which proves the claim.
∎

Remark 5.1

(Stochastic Lyapunov version)
.

Let
X_{t+1}=T(X_{t})+\xi_{t}
with
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}\mid X_{t}]=0
and

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}^{2}\mid X_{t}]\leq\sigma_{\xi}^{2}<\infty
.
Under Theorem
5.2
,

\mathbb{E}\big[V(X_{t+1})\mid X_{t}\big]-V(X_{t})\;\leq\;-\,c\,\mathbb{E}\big[(T(X_{t})-X_{t})^{2}\mid X_{t}\big]\;+\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{\xi}^{2},

with
V(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x-x^{\star})^{2}
and
c=\frac{1+L_{T}}{2(1-L_{T})}
.
Thus
V
is a stochastic Lyapunov functional in expectation when
\sigma_{\xi}^{2}
is small enough.

5.3
Wide-Layer Variance Propagation via
m_{2}

Theorem 5.3

(Mean-field preactivation variance recursion)
.

Consider a fully-connected layer of width
n
with
W_{ij}\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{W}^{2}/n)
,
biases
b_{i}\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{b}^{2})
independent of
W
, and i.i.d. preactivations

h_{j}^{(\ell)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,q_{\ell})
independent of
(W,b)
. Let
x_{j}^{(\ell)}=\phi(h_{j}^{(\ell)})
and

h_{i}^{(\ell+1)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}x_{j}^{(\ell)}+b_{i}
. In the
n\to\infty
mean-field limit,
the next-layer preactivation variance satisfies

q_{\ell+1}\;=\;\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}(\sqrt{q_{\ell}})+\sigma_{b}^{2},\qquad m_{2}(\sigma):=\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(Z)^{2}\big],\ \ Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}).

Moreover, if
f(q):=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}(\sqrt{q})+\sigma_{b}^{2}
is differentiable at a fixed point
q^{\star}=f(q^{\star})
, local stability holds whenever

|f^{\prime}(q^{\star})|\;=\;\sigma_{W}^{2}\,\frac{m_{2}^{\prime}(\sqrt{q^{\star}})}{2\sqrt{q^{\star}}}\;<\;1,

interpreting the right-hand side in the limit
q^{\star}\downarrow 0
when needed.

Proof.

Condition on
x^{(\ell)}=(x_{1}^{(\ell)},\ldots,x_{n}^{(\ell)})
. Since
\mathbb{E}[W_{ij}]=0
,

\mathbb{E}[h_{i}^{(\ell+1)}\mid x^{(\ell)}]=0
, hence

\mathrm{Var}\!\big(h_{i}^{(\ell+1)}\mid x^{(\ell)}\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}(W_{ij})\,\big(x_{j}^{(\ell)}\big)^{2}+\sigma_{b}^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{W}^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(x_{j}^{(\ell)}\big)^{2}+\sigma_{b}^{2}.

By LLN,
(1/n)\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}^{(\ell)})^{2}\to\mathbb{E}[\phi(h^{(\ell)})^{2}]
a.s.
with
h^{(\ell)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,q_{\ell})
. Thus
q_{\ell+1}=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,\mathbb{E}[\phi(h^{(\ell)})^{2}]+\sigma_{b}^{2}=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}(\sqrt{q_{\ell}})+\sigma_{b}^{2}
.
For stability, differentiate
f(q)=\sigma_{W}^{2}m_{2}(\sqrt{q})+\sigma_{b}^{2}
:

f^{\prime}(q)=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}^{\prime}(\sqrt{q})\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{q}},

hence the condition
|f^{\prime}(q^{\star})|<1
.
∎

5.4
Bias Drift Control via Signed Asymmetry

Define the signed area
B:=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\phi(x)-\phi(-x))\,dx
when it exists.

Theorem 5.4

(Bias drift bound via asymptotes and compensation)
.

Let
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
. Suppose
\phi
admits one-sided linear asymptotes

\alpha_{+}:=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x},\qquad\alpha_{-}:=\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x},

and let
C(\phi)
be the tail-compensated primitive bound from Section
3
, i.e.
for
D(x):=\phi(x)-\alpha_{+}x\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x\geq 0\}}-\alpha_{-}x\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x<0\}}
we have

F_{D}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}D(t)\,dt

well-defined with
\|F_{D}\|_{\infty}\leq C(\phi)<\infty
.
Then

\Bigl|\,\mathbb{E}[\phi(Z)]\;-\;\frac{\alpha_{+}-\alpha_{-}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\;\sigma\Bigr|\;\leq\;\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\;\frac{C(\phi)}{\sigma}\,.

Equivalently, for
h^{(\ell)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,q_{\ell})
,

\Bigl|\,m_{1}(\sqrt{q_{\ell}})\;-\;\frac{\alpha_{+}-\alpha_{-}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\;\sqrt{q_{\ell}}\Bigr|\;\leq\;\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\;\frac{C(\phi)}{\sqrt{q_{\ell}}}\,.

Proof.

Write
\phi(x)=\alpha_{+}x\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x\geq 0\}}+\alpha_{-}x\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x<0\}}+D(x)
.
Since
\mathbb{E}[Z\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Z\geq 0\}}]=\sigma/\sqrt{2\pi}
and
\mathbb{E}[Z\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Z<0\}}]=-\,\sigma/\sqrt{2\pi}
,

\mathbb{E}[\phi(Z)]=\frac{\alpha_{+}-\alpha_{-}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\;\sigma+\mathbb{E}[D(Z)].

With
\gamma_{\sigma}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-x^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}
and
\gamma_{\sigma}^{\prime}(x)=-(x/\sigma^{2})\gamma_{\sigma}(x)
,
integration by parts gives

\mathbb{E}[D(Z)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}D(x)\,\gamma_{\sigma}(x)\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}F_{D}(x)\,\gamma_{\sigma}^{\prime}(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\!\big[F_{D}(Z)\,Z\big].

Hence
|\mathbb{E}[D(Z)]|\leq\|F_{D}\|_{\infty}\,\mathbb{E}[|Z|]/\sigma^{2}=C(\phi)\cdot\sigma\sqrt{2/\pi}/\sigma^{2}=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,C(\phi)/\sigma
.
∎

Corollary 5.3

(Uniform layerwise bound)
.

If
\inf_{\ell}q_{\ell}\geq q_{\min}>0
and
C(\phi)<\infty
, then

\sup_{\ell}\Bigl|\,m_{1}(\sqrt{q_{\ell}})-\tfrac{\alpha_{+}-\alpha_{-}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{q_{\ell}}\Bigr|\;\leq\;\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\;\frac{C(\phi)}{\sqrt{q_{\min}}}.

In particular, when
\alpha_{+}=\alpha_{-}
(symmetric linear tails), we have

\sup_{\ell}|m_{1}(\sqrt{q_{\ell}})|\leq\sqrt{2/\pi}\,C(\phi)/\sqrt{q_{\min}}.

Lemma 5.2

(Always-valid crude bound)
.

For any measurable
\phi
with
\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi(x)+\phi(-x)|\,dx<\infty
and
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
,

\big|\mathbb{E}[\phi(Z)]\big|\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\big|\phi(x)+\phi(-x)\big|\,dx.

Remark 5.2

(ReLU Analysis)
.

For
\phi(x)=\max\{0,x\}
, we have
\alpha_{+}=1
,
\alpha_{-}=0
, and
C(\phi)=0
, hence the bound is exact:

\mathbb{E}[\phi(Z)]=\sigma/\sqrt{2\pi}
.

5.5
A Criticality Region in
(\sigma_{W},\sigma_{b})
from
(m_{2},g_{2})

Theorem 5.5

(Propagation criticality (corrected))
.

Assume the mean-field preactivation variance recursion

q_{\ell+1}=f(q_{\ell}):=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}(\sqrt{q_{\ell}})+\sigma_{b}^{2}

admits an equilibrium
q^{\star}>0
. If

\boxed{\;|f^{\prime}(q^{\star})|=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,\frac{m_{2}^{\prime}(\sqrt{q^{\star}})}{2\sqrt{q^{\star}}}<1\;}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boxed{\;\sigma_{W}\,g_{2}(\sqrt{q^{\star}})<1\;},

then (i) the forward variance is locally stable around
q^{\star}
, and (ii) mean-square perturbations contract across the layer at stationarity.

Proof.

(i) Variance stability.

Differentiate
f(q)=\sigma_{W}^{2}m_{2}(\sqrt{q})+\sigma_{b}^{2}
:

f^{\prime}(q)=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}^{\prime}(\sqrt{q})\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{q}}.

Hence
|f^{\prime}(q^{\star})|<1
implies local stability of the one-dimensional recursion.

(ii) Perturbation contraction (mean-square).

Let
x^{(\ell)}=\phi(h^{(\ell)})
with
h^{(\ell)}\overset{d}{\approx}\mathcal{N}(0,q^{\star})
at stationarity, and

h^{(\ell+1)}=W\,x^{(\ell)}+b,\qquad W_{ij}\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma_{W}^{2}}{n}\right),\quad b_{i}\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{b}^{2}).

Linearize two nearby trajectories and set
\delta h^{(\ell)}
their preactivation difference. Then

\delta x^{(\ell)}\;\approx\;D^{(\ell)}\,\delta h^{(\ell)},\qquad D^{(\ell)}:=\mathrm{diag}\big(\phi^{\prime}(h^{(\ell)}_{1}),\ldots,\phi^{\prime}(h^{(\ell)}_{n})\big),

and

\delta h^{(\ell+1)}=W\,\delta x^{(\ell)}\approx W\,D^{(\ell)}\,\delta h^{(\ell)}.

Conditioning on
(D^{(\ell)},\delta h^{(\ell)})
and using
\mathrm{Var}(W_{ij})=\sigma_{W}^{2}/n
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\delta h^{(\ell+1)}\|_{2}^{2}\,\big|\,D^{(\ell)},\delta h^{(\ell)}\right]=\frac{\sigma_{W}^{2}}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\phi^{\prime}(h^{(\ell)}_{j})\big)^{2}\,\|\delta h^{(\ell)}\|_{2}^{2}.

Taking expectations and using the LLN at stationarity (
h^{(\ell)}_{j}\overset{d}{\to}\mathcal{N}(0,q^{\star})
),

\mathbb{E}\|\delta h^{(\ell+1)}\|_{2}^{2}\;\leq\;\sigma_{W}^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\big[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{2}\big]\;\mathbb{E}\|\delta h^{(\ell)}\|_{2}^{2}\;=\;\big(\sigma_{W}\,g_{2}(\sqrt{q^{\star}})\big)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\|\delta h^{(\ell)}\|_{2}^{2},

with
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,q^{\star})
. Thus
\sigma_{W}g_{2}(\sqrt{q^{\star}})<1
yields contraction in mean square.
∎

Remark 5.3

(On
q^{\star}=0
and worst-case bounds)
.

If
q^{\star}=0
, interpret the variance condition via the limit
q\downarrow 0
:

f^{\prime}(q)=\sigma_{W}^{2}\,m_{2}^{\prime}(\sqrt{q})/(2\sqrt{q})
. For worst-case (operator-norm) perturbation control,
a sufficient condition is
2\,\sigma_{W}\,\sup_{x}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|<1
(since
\|W\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to 2\sigma_{W}
for square Gaussian
W
),
which is stricter than
\sigma_{W}g_{2}(\sqrt{q^{\star}})<1
.

5.6
Kernel Curvature: Dimension-free Hessian Bounds

Let
w\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
and
K(x,y)=\mathbb{E}[\phi(w^{\top}x)\phi(w^{\top}y)]
.

Theorem 5.6

(Kernel mixed Hessian bound via
g_{4}
)
.

Let
w\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
and
K(x,y)=\mathbb{E}[\phi(w^{\top}x)\phi(w^{\top}y)]
.
Assume
\phi^{\prime}\in L^{4}(\mathbb{R},\gamma_{\|x\|})
and
\phi^{\prime}\in L^{4}(\mathbb{R},\gamma_{\|y\|})
and define

g_{4}(\sigma)\;:=\;\Big(\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})}[\phi^{\prime}(Z)^{4}]\Big)^{1/4}.

Then for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\big|\,a^{\top}\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}K(x,y)\,b\,\big|\;\leq\;\sqrt{3}\;g_{4}(\|x\|)\,g_{4}(\|y\|)\,\|a\|\,\|b\|,

hence

\big\|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}K(x,y)\big\|_{\mathrm{op}}\;\leq\;\sqrt{3}\;g_{4}(\|x\|)\,g_{4}(\|y\|).

Proof.

Differentiate under the integral sign (justified by
L^{4}
domination and Gaussian integrability):

\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}K(x,y)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\phi^{\prime}(w^{\top}x)\,\phi^{\prime}(w^{\top}y)\,w\,w^{\top}\right].

Let
u=w^{\top}x
,
v=w^{\top}y
(jointly Gaussian), and fix
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\big|a^{\top}\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}K\,b\big|=\big|\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(u)\phi^{\prime}(v)(w\!\cdot\!a)(w\!\cdot\!b)]\big|\leq\Big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(u)^{2}\phi^{\prime}(v)^{2}]\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\mathbb{E}[(w\!\cdot\!a)^{2}(w\!\cdot\!b)^{2}]\Big)^{1/2}.

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz again to the first factor:

\Big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(u)^{2}\phi^{\prime}(v)^{2}]\Big)^{1/2}\;\leq\;\big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(u)^{4}]\big)^{1/4}\,\big(\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(v)^{4}]\big)^{1/4}=g_{4}(\|x\|)\,g_{4}(\|y\|).

For the second factor, Isserlis’ (Wick) formula for a standard Gaussian
w
gives

\mathbb{E}[(w\!\cdot\!a)^{2}(w\!\cdot\!b)^{2}]=\|a\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}+2(a^{\top}b)^{2}\;\leq\;3\,\|a\|^{2}\|b\|^{2},

hence its square root is at most
\sqrt{3}\,\|a\|\,\|b\|
. Combine the bounds.
∎

Corollary 5.4

(BV/Uniform-slope curvature bound)
.

If
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq M<\infty
, then

\|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}K(x,y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\;\leq\;\sqrt{3}\,M^{2}\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In particular, if
\phi^{\prime}\in BV(\mathbb{R})
with finite one-sided limits, then

M\;\leq\;\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})\;+\;|\phi^{\prime}(+\infty)-\phi^{\prime}(-\infty)|\;=:\;\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})+c_{\star},

and therefore

\|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}K(x,y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\;\leq\;\sqrt{3}\,\big(\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})+c_{\star}\big)^{2}.

Numerical Method (Gauss–Hermite)

\mathbb{E}[f(Z)]
with

\mathbb{E}[f(Z)]\;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\,f\!\big(\sqrt{2}\,\sigma\,x_{i}\big),

where
(x_{i},w_{i})
are Hermite nodes/weights.
We apply this to
f=\phi,\ \phi^{2},\ \phi^{\prime},\ (\phi^{\prime})^{2},\ z\phi(z)
to obtain
m_{1},\ m_{2},\ g_{1},\ g_{2},\ \eta
, respectively.

6.2

the table below reports the five components computed as Gaussian expectations.
The remaining coordinates
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime}),C(\phi))
are handled

. These results provide several clear patterns that align
with our theoretical taxonomy.

ReLU as baseline.

The ReLU family admits closed-form integrals, serving as a reference point.
Invariance of
(g_{1},g_{2})=(0.5,0.7071)
across scales illustrates the stability of linear
tails: variance scales linearly with
\sigma^{2}
, while drift
(m_{1},\eta)
grows
proportionally with
\sigma
. This predictable scaling motivates the widespread use of
ReLU and its role in the design of initialization schemes.

Bounded activations.

Sigmoid and
\tanh
exhibit vanishing
m_{1}
and
\eta
at all scales, consistent with
their bounded range and saturating slopes. The decay in
g_{1},g_{2}
as
\sigma
increases
reflects the onset of gradient saturation, a phenomenon that historically limited their

Smooth linear-growth activations.

Swish, GELU, Mish, and TeLU all share the
(1,0)
asymptotic slope of ReLU but display
larger
g_{1},g_{2}
values at moderate scales. This reflects their smooth curvature near the

\sigma=1

yield higher
m_{1}
and
\eta
compared to sigmoid/tanh, but without the constant
plateauing of ReLU, leading to more balanced drift and variance. Among them, TeLU shows
values nearly indistinguishable from Swish, confirming its design as a stable,
smooth alternative with improved gradient dynamics.

Scale dependence.

Across all unbounded activations,
(m_{1},m_{2},\eta)
increase with
\sigma
, whereas

(g_{1},g_{2})
approach unity, indicating stronger linearization of the activation at high
input scales. In contrast, bounded activations saturate: their
m_{1}
and
\eta
plateau,
and
g_{1},g_{2}
decay, reflecting diminishing sensitivity to input variance.

Implications.

These numerical evaluations confirm the predictive power of the signature coordinates:

(m_{1},\eta)
capture drift;
(g_{1},g_{2})
capture gradient flow; and
m_{2}
controls variance
propagation. Smooth linear-growth functions consistently strike a favorable balance,
explaining their empirical success in optimization and generalization relative to both
bounded saturating functions and nonsmooth ReLU.

7
Related Work

Activation function design.

Classical activations (sigmoid,
\tanh
) emphasized smoothness and biological plausibility
[
1
,
2
]
, while ReLU and its variants prioritized sparse gradients and ease of optimization
[
25
,
10
]
. More recent smooth, non-monotone designs (Swish
[
11
]
, GELU
[
12
]
, Mish
[
13
]
, TeLU
[
6
]
) emerged from empirical search and probabilistic heuristics. Our framework complements these lines by providing an
integral
lens: the nine-dimensional signature
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi)
assembles Gaussian propagation statistics, asymptotic slopes, and regularity measures (
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
,
C(\phi)
) into a unified taxonomy that quantitatively predicts stability and kernel smoothness.

Signal propagation and mean-field theory.

Mean-field analyses of wide neural networks
[
26
,
27
,
16
]
study forward variance recursions and the conditions for stable propagation across layers. Our Theorem
5.3
provides a compact formulation of this recursion in terms of
m_{2}(\cdot)
, while Theorem
5.5
connects the stability region to the pair
(m_{2}^{\prime},g_{2})
extracted from
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi)
. Unlike prior work that typically requires case-by-case analysis of specific activations, our signature provides a minimal set of sufficient statistics for forward variance stability and backward perturbation contraction across activation families.

Dynamical isometry and gradient flow.

Work on dynamical isometry
[
28
,
29
,
27
]
highlights the role of Jacobian singular values and average gains in preventing gradient explosion/vanishing. Our quantities
g_{1}
(mean derivative) and
g_{2}
(RMS derivative) furnish distributional analogues of average gain under Gaussian inputs, enabling contraction results in
L_{2}
(Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
) that hold at finite width when inputs are approximately Gaussian. This provides a more general framework than activation-specific analyses.

Neural tangent kernels and Gaussian processes.

Kernel viewpoints (NTK
[
20
]
and GP limits
[
17
,
30
]
) tie activation regularity to the smoothness of induced kernels. Our kernel curvature bound (Theorem
5.6
) shows that
dimension-free
control of the mixed Hessian norm follows from
g_{2}
and, in a bounded variation setting, from
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
(Corollary
5.4
). This connects activation-level regularity directly to kernel conditioning and smoothness properties used in infinite-width analyses, providing explicit bounds rather than asymptotic characterizations.

Lyapunov methods and stability.

Lyapunov techniques are standard in control theory
[
31
]
and optimization
[
32
]
to certify global convergence. Applications to neural network dynamics typically focus on specific architectures or loss landscapes
[
33
,
34
]
. Our contraction-based Lyapunov functions (Theorem
5.2
) quantify strict descent in terms of
|T(x)-x|^{2}
with explicit constants depending only on activation properties, while our
F
-based approach (Corollary
5.1
) leverages the primitive
F=\int\phi
for monotonic activations. These results integrate seamlessly with the integral signature by expressing descent criteria using
g_{2}
and tail properties (
C(\phi)
,
\alpha_{\pm}
).

Regularity metrics for activations.

Beyond standard Lipschitz constants and Sobolev norms
[
35
]
, few works provide fine-grained measures of activation regularity that connect to network behavior. The total variation of the slope
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
captures both piecewise-linear kinks and smooth curvature in a single quantity, generalizing previous approaches that handle these cases separately. Our closure theorem (Theorem
3.3
) and kernel bound (Corollary
5.4
) establish
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
as a practically meaningful regularity measure.

Comparative perspective and limitations.

Prior taxonomies typically emphasize monotonicity, boundedness, or pointwise properties (e.g., smooth vs. kinked) but lack quantitative measures that directly predict network dynamics
[
36
,
37
]
. Our 9D signature is
integral, affine-aware, and propagation-aligned

Our Gaussian-based analysis provides theoretical foundations that complement but do not replace empirical evaluation on specific architectures and datasets. The framework is most directly applicable to settings where layer inputs are approximately Gaussian, though the stability principles extend more broadly. Future work could extend the signature to non-Gaussian input distributions and vector-valued activations.

8
Discussion and Conclusion

In this work we introduced a nine-dimensional integral signature

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}(\phi)=\big(m_{1},g_{1},g_{2},m_{2},\eta,\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime}),C(\phi)\big)

that unifies statistical propagation, asymptotic geometry, and regularity into a single taxonomy of activation functions. Our theoretical framework established affine reparameterization laws (with bias), closure under bounded slope variation, contraction-based Lyapunov stability, mean-field variance recursions in signature form, and dimension-free kernel curvature bounds. The classification of common activations (ReLU, leaky-ReLU,
\tanh
,
\operatorname{sigm}
, Swish, GELU, Mish, TeLU) demonstrates how the signature quantitatively predicts stability, drift, and kernel smoothness properties.

Design principles.

Our theoretical results yield actionable guidelines for activation function selection and design:

1.

Contraction control
: Prefer activations with
g_{2}(\sigma)\lesssim 0.8
to ensure contraction of perturbations (Theorems
5.1
,
5.2
), as values near unity approach the stability boundary.

2.

Variance management
: Use
m_{2}(\sigma)
and
m_{2}^{\prime}(\sigma)
to position weight initialization in the variance-stable regime (Theorem
5.3
), avoiding explosive or vanishing signal regimes.

3.

Bias drift control
: Manage asymmetry and bias accumulation via
m_{1}(\sigma)
and the signed area
B=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\phi(x)-\phi(-x))dx
(Theorem
5.4
), with bounded
|B|
preventing mean shift instabilities.

4.

Kernel conditioning
: Maintain small
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})\lesssim 5
to improve kernel conditioning and training robustness (Theorem
5.6
, Corollary
5.4
), as excessive slope variation degrades optimization landscapes.

5.

Tail compensation
: Enforce finite tail compensation
C(\phi)<\infty
by ensuring linear asymptotic slopes
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})
align with the activation’s growth behavior, preventing uncontrolled primitive accumulation.

Computational accessibility.

The signature components
(m_{1},g_{1},g_{2},m_{2},\eta)
can be efficiently computed via standard Gauss-Hermite quadrature with
n=160
nodes, achieving 6+ digit accuracy for smooth activations. The remaining coordinates
(\alpha_{\pm},\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime}),C(\phi))
are determined analytically from asymptotic analysis, making the framework practically accessible for activation evaluation and design.

Limitations.

Our analysis focuses on scalar activations under Gaussian inputs within fully-connected mean-field settings. Non-Gaussian input distributions, heavy-tailed preactivations, or highly anisotropic layer statistics may require alternative integration schemes or additional signature coordinates. The connection between finite
\mathrm{TV}(\phi^{\prime})
and finite-width generalization bounds remains an open theoretical question requiring further investigation.

Future research directions.

Several natural extensions warrant investigation:

1.

Distributional extensions
: Non-Gaussian input families and adaptive scale parameters
\sigma(\ell)
driven by empirical layer statistics, broadening applicability beyond mean-field Gaussian assumptions.

2.

Architectural generalizations
: Residual and attention mechanisms where skip connections, normalization layers, and multi-head structures modify the fundamental variance and gain recursions.

3.

Data-driven signatures
: Incorporating empirical preactivation distributions from real datasets rather than theoretical Gaussian inputs, bridging the theory-practice gap.

4.

Optimization-aware analysis
: Coupling derivative statistics
(g_{1},g_{2})
with second-order curvature information along parameter update directions to predict training dynamics.

5.

Automated activation design
: Constrained optimization over activation families targeting specific signature properties (e.g., desired
(m_{2}(\sigma),g_{2}(\sigma))

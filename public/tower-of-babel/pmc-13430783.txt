Surgical management and outcomes of open degloving injuries of the extremities: a retrospective cohort study at a Level II trauma center
Authors: Vsevolod Shurkhay, Zachary Arnold, Elvis Tamo Achu, Utsav Patel, Eric Schinnerer, Jianqiang Huo, Michael Charles, Changxing Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOpen degloving injuries are severe soft-tissue traumas associated with ischemia, infection, and poor functional outcomes. Due to heterogeneous presentations, standardized management protocols remain limited.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study of 28 consecutive patients with open degloving injuries of the extremities treated at a Level II trauma center between 2020 and 2024. Demographics, injury characteristics, surgical interventions, and outcomes were analyzed using chi-square/Fisher’s exact tests and nonparametric methods.ResultsMedian age was 49 years, and 57.1% were male. Motor vehicle accidents were the most common mechanism (40.7%), with 56.7% involving upper extremities. All but one patient underwent surgical management. Primary debridement was performed in 51.9%, serial debridement in 33.3%, and negative pressure wound therapy in 63.0%. Skin grafting (22.2%) and flap reconstruction (14.8%) were used for definitive closure. Complications occurred in 40.7%, most commonly necrosis (18.5%) and infection (11.1%). Median hospital stay was 7 days.ConclusionsEarly multidisciplinary surgical management is associated with high limb salvage rates and acceptable complication profiles. Prospective multicenter studies are needed to establish standardized treatment algorithms.

Introduction

Degloving injuries are relatively common in trauma patients with limb involvement. These injuries involve the avulsion and/or detachment of superficial structures such as skin and subcutaneous tissue from the underlying muscle and fascia caused by shearing forces [1, 2]. High-energy mechanisms such as motor vehicle crashes (MVC) are typical among these patients [3–5]. Although degloving injuries can occur on anybody surface, they most often appear on the extremities, trunk, scalp, and face [6–8]. The location of the injury, the degree of accompanying trauma, and whether the degloving injury is open or closed all influence the effectiveness and necessity of surgical intervention, which are important considerations for management [9–15].

Open degloving injuries are characterized by visible separation of tissue from the epidermis down to the deeper underlying structures, which increase the risk of contamination and infection of muscle and fascial layers [16]. It differs from closed degloving injuries where the separation occurs within the deep compartments.

Open degloving injuries can be life-threatening and require complex management. Treatment plans often involve prompt recognition of injury and timely intervention to preserve viable tissue, while also identifying devascularized and/or already necrotic tissue that must undergo extensive debridement to prevent further infection and complications [1–3, 7, 9].

Outcomes can vary significantly based on the timing, method, and extent of surgical intervention [1–3, 7, 9]. Because of the variability in these injuries and the range of potential treatments, developing standardized treatment protocols remains challenging. There is no consensus on the timing or choice of surgical management, with some evidence indicating that age and injury pattern influence outcomes. Retrospective studies suggest that accurate initial assessment and thorough debridement are essential for improving prognosis, but current data have not enabled the development of definitive management pathways [17, 18], which indicates a clear gap addressed in the present study.

This study aims to evaluate outcomes of multidisciplinary management of open degloving injuries at a Level II trauma center and identify factors associated with complications and surgical management.

The primary outcome of this study was the rate of postoperative complications following surgical management of open degloving injuries of the extremities. Complications were defined as any adverse effect occurring after surgery and included skin necrosis, surgical site infection, bleeding requiring intervention, and neurovascular injury. Secondary outcomes included hospital length of stay (LOS), number and type of surgical procedures performed, need for definitive reconstructive interventions such as split-thickness skin grafting or local/free tissue flap coverage, in-hospital mortality and discharge disposition.

These outcomes were analyzed in relation to patient demographics, injury characteristics (including mechanism, location, and size), and treatment variables to identify factors potentially influencing clinical course and surgical management.

Materials and methods

We conducted a literature review and retrospectively analyzed the outcomes and complications of surgical management in 28 consecutive patients with open degloving injuries of the upper and lower extremities treated at an urban Level II trauma center from 2020 to 2024. This study was approved by the local ethical committee.

Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables are reported as medians with ranges or interquartile ranges (IQR), given the small sample size and non-normal distribution of data.

Comparisons between groups were performed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate. Fisher’s exact test was used when expected cell counts were less than 5. Continuous variables were compared using nonparametric tests (Mann–Whitney U test), given the non-normal distribution and limited sample size.

A two-tailed p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Due to the small cohort size, no multivariable analysis was performed, and results are primarily descriptive and exploratory in nature. All statistical analyses were performed using R, version 4.5.0.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Only adult patients of all genders, race, cultural demographics and socioeconomic classes with open degloving injuries involving upper and lower extremities were included in our study. Patients younger than 18 years old, pregnant or lactating women and prisoners were excluded from analysis. Eligible patients were identified through the hospital-based electronic trauma registry.

Open degloving injury

Open degloving injuries were defined as traumatic avulsion or separation of the skin and subcutaneous tissue from the underlying fascia and/or muscle, accompanied by a breach in the skin resulting in direct communication between the wound and the external environment. Diagnosis was established based on initial clinical assessment and confirmed intraoperatively.

Injury classification

Open degloving injuries were further characterized by extent (circumferential vs. non-circumferential) and classified according to established soft-tissue degloving injury classification systems [19]. Classification was determined based on operative findings and documentation in the medical record.

Injury size

Injury size was defined as the total surface area of the soft tissue defect (cm²), as documented in operative reports or wound assessments.

Mechanism of injury

Mechanisms were categorized as motor vehicle accidents or other causes (including industrial injuries, falls, and crush injuries), based on documentation in the trauma registry.

Operative management

Primary debridement was defined as initial surgical removal of non-viable tissue with or without immediate wound closure. Serial debridement referred to staged repeat operations for progressive removal of non-viable tissue. Negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) was defined as the application of subatmospheric pressure dressings to promote wound healing. Definitive reconstruction included split-thickness skin grafting or local/free tissue flap coverage.

Complications

Postoperative complications were defined as adverse events occurring after surgical intervention during the index hospitalization. These included:◦ Skin necrosis: partial or full-thickness loss of tissue viability requiring additional intervention◦ Bleeding: hemorrhage requiring transfusion or reoperation◦ Neurovascular injury: documented nerve or vascular compromise identified clinically or intraoperatively

Length of stay (LOS)

Hospital length of stay was defined as the number of days from admission to discharge.

Mortality and discharge status

Mortality was defined as death occurring during the index hospitalization. Discharge status included routine discharge or discharge against medical advice.

Data collection and outcomes

Demographic and clinical variables, including age, sex, mechanism of injury, transfer type (ambulance, air, or walk-in), Injury Severity Score (ISS), injury location, injury size, type of surgical intervention, flap characteristics (size and type), hospital length of stay (LOS), and complications, were retrospectively collected for all patients.

The primary outcome of this study was the rate of postoperative complications following surgical management of open degloving injuries of the extremities. Complications were defined as adverse events occurring during the index hospitalization and included skin necrosis, surgical site infection, bleeding requiring intervention, and neurovascular injury.

Secondary outcomes included hospital length of stay (LOS), number and type of surgical procedures performed, and the need for definitive reconstruction, including split-thickness skin grafting or local or free tissue flap coverage. Additional secondary outcomes included in-hospital mortality and discharge status, including discharge against medical advice. Outcomes were evaluated in relation to patient demographics, injury characteristics, and operative management variables, including type of surgical intervention, to explore factors influencing clinical course and treatment strategies.

Results

Our retrospective cohort of 28 patients had a median age of 49.0 years. The mean ISS upon arrival was 9.4.

There were 16 male patients (57.1%) and 12 female patients (42.9%). The most common mechanism of injury was motor vehicle accidents, accounting for 40.7% (11 patients), with other causes, such as industrial injuries or falls, making up 59.3% (17 patients). Of the total injuries, 56.7% (17 injuries) involved the upper extremities and 43.3% (13 injuries) involved the lower extremity, with two patients having degloving injuries in both upper and lower extremities. Three patients had associated injuries involving the head, upper extremities, and lower extremities.

Most of our patients arrived via ambulance, with only two patients either walking in or arriving by private vehicle. One patient was airlifted to our center.

Out of the 27 patients who were included in the surgical cohort as one patient died pre-operatively, debridement and primary suturing/closure was the most frequent approach performed in 51.9.% (14 patients). Serial debridement was required in 33.3% (9 patients), and Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (NPWT) was applied in 63.0% (17 patients).

Definitive reconstruction was required in 10 cases, including skin grafting in 22.2% (6 patients) and tissue flaps (local or free) in 14.8% (4 patients). No additional adjunctive therapies (e.g., hyperbaric oxygen or dermal regeneration templates) were documented in the available dataset. Surgical complications refer to postoperative events only.

59.3% (16 patients) in our cohort did not develop any surgical complications. It should be noted that the complications observed in the rest of these subcohort included skin necrosis in 18.5% (5 patients), infection in 11.1% (3 patients), bleeding in 11.1% (3 patients), and neurovascular damage in 7.4% (2 patients). The median hospital length of stay was 7 days. One patient who was discharged against medical advice (3.7%).

Discussion

Open degloving injuries of the extremities are a severe type of trauma, often caused by high-energy events like motor vehicle accidents, accounted for 40.7% of injuries in our cohort, which is consistent with prior studies reporting traffic-related mechanisms as the predominant cause of degloving injuries, comprising up to 75% of cases [20].

These injuries pose major challenges due to the immediate risk of tissue ischemia, nerve damage, and subsequent infection, which can lead to significant morbidity and loss of function if not treated promptly.

Our review of 28 patients with open degloving injuries provides valuable insight into surgical treatment and outcomes at a Level II trauma center. Demographics of our cohort, with a median age of 49.0 years and a slight male predominance (57.1%), align with previously reported demographic patterns in degloving injury patients [20]. Consistent with the literature, extremities remain the most common sites of degloving injuries; in our cohort, upper-extremity involvement was observed in 56.7% of patients [20, 21]. The median defect size of 72.6 cm² highlights the need for comprehensive reconstructive strategies.

All patients in our series required surgical treatment, with a substantial proportion undergoing serial procedures. This aligns with prior reports demonstrating that degloving injuries often necessitate multiple staged operations [22]. These findings underscore the evolving nature of tissue viability and the importance of staged surgical management. Immediate debridement and primary closure or flap preservation was feasible in 50% (14 patients) of our patients, comparable to rates reported in previous series [20]. This suggests that early surgical intervention may allow for tissue preservation in a substantial subset of patients. NPWT was utilized in 60.7% of patients in our cohort, consistent with literature describing widespread use of NPWT in degloving injuries [22]. Its role in reducing infection rates and promoting wound bed preparation has been demonstrated in prior studies, including randomized data in severe open fractures showing significantly lower infection rates compared with standard dressings [23]. Additionally, NPWT has been shown to facilitate granulation tissue formation and reduce wound size, supporting its role as an adjunct in staged reconstruction [24]. However, evidence regarding long-term functional benefit remains mixed, with some randomized trials showing no difference in disability outcomes compared to standard dressings [25] and also the optimal timing for initiating the NPWT in complex open degloving injuries remain unanswered questions that our current data cannot definitively resolve mainly due to its limited sample size.

The natural complexity of these wounds was clearly demonstrated by the need for serial debridement in 32.1% (9 patients) and the requirement for more than one type of surgical treatment in 11 patients. These numbers highlight the unpredictable nature of tissue viability and the need for a staged approach guided by repeated assessments.

The ability to salvage avulsed tissue in selected cases is supported by prior studies demonstrating favorable graft take rates when avulsed skin is defatted and reused as a graft, particularly when combined with NPWT [14].

Usage of advanced techniques, including rotational flaps, radial forearm free flaps, and scapular flaps in four cases underscores the multidisciplinary effort-involving trauma and plastic surgery services—required to address Type 3 and Type 4 degloving injuries that involve larger defects and critical underlying structures.

The complication profile observed in our cohort—including infection and skin necrosis—is consistent with prior reports highlighting these as common adverse outcomes in degloving injuries, reflecting the severity of soft tissue disruption and vascular compromise [20].

Only one patient who required five surgeries, a Type 4 degloving injury, demonstrates the extensive resources and ongoing effort needed for the most severe cases. The variability in reconstructive techniques observed in our series reflects the lack of a universally accepted treatment algorithm, with prior studies emphasizing the need for individualized, multidisciplinary approaches based on injury characteristics and tissue viability [22].

The median hospital length of stay (LOS) of 7 days in our cohort indicates resource use and patient recovery. This relatively moderate LOS, despite the complexity of the injuries, may result from a comprehensive, multidisciplinary approach to trauma care with plastic/reconstructive surgeon consultation available for every patient with extensive soft tissue damage.

Limitations

While this study is strengthened by well-defined methodology and consistent multidisciplinary care within a single institution, allowing for precise characterization of surgical decision-making and early results in open degloving injuries it is naturally constrained by retrospective, single-center design, possibility of selection bias and limited generalizability to different trauma situations. The limited sample size (n = 28) reduces statistical power, preventing meaningful multivariable analysis and restricting the capacity to identify characteristics related with specific problems or treatment outcomes. Furthermore, the variability in damage severity, anatomical location, and the customized surgical methods required for these complicated wounds limits the standardization of treatment routes based on our findings. Finally, while this study focuses on index hospitalization, it does not address follow ups, long-term functional results, quality of life, or the durability of reconstructive procedures, all of which are significant areas for future research.

Overall, our findings are consistent with the current body of literature describing degloving injuries as complex, high-energy trauma requiring staged, multidisciplinary management. The high rate of operative intervention, frequent need for serial debridement, and widespread use of NPWT in our cohort reinforce existing treatment paradigms, while also highlighting the ongoing challenges in optimizing outcomes and minimizing complications. Further large-scale, prospective studies are needed to compare outcomes across different reconstructive methods and evaluate long-term functional recovery.

Conclusion

Open degloving injuries are surgical emergencies requiring immediate and coordinated intervention to prevent progressive ischemia, tissue loss, and limb-threatening complications. This case series demonstrates that a prompt multidisciplinary approach can support high rates of limb salvage and successful wound closure. Despite the inherent limitations of a retrospective design, these findings highlight the need for standardized, evidence-based management protocols. Prospective multicenter studies are warranted to define optimal reconstructive strategies, timing of intervention, and long-term functional outcomes in this challenging patient population.

Abstract
. We work in the realm of sets of reals. We prove that in the Miller model and in a model constructed by Goldstern–Judah–Shelah all universally meager sets have size at most ω 1 \omega_{1} . Some relations between combinatorial covering properties in these models allow to obtain the same limitations for sizes of Rothberger spaces and Hurewicz spaces with no homeomorphic copy of the Cantor set inside. It follows from our results that the existence of a strong measure zero set of size ω 2 \omega_{2} does not imply the existence of a Rothberger space of size ω 2 \omega_{2} . We also prove that in the Miller model all strong measure zero sets have size at most ω 1 \omega_{1} .

The definition of universally meager subspaces was introduced by Zakrzewski

In some models of set theory there are limitations for sizes of universally meager subspaces.
This is the case, e.g., in the Cohen model (a forcing extension of a ground model of CH using a finite support iteration (equivalently, product) of the Cohen forcing, of length
\omega_{2}

In our work we consider these limitations in the Miller model (a forcing extension of a ground model of GCH

[

.

Theorem 1.2
.

In the Miller model and in the Goldstern–Judah–Shelah model, every universally meager subspace has size at most
\omega_{1}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on the fact that
forcing notions involved satisfy the property
(\dagger)
introduced in
[
32
]
.
It turns out that also the Cohen forcing shares the
(\dagger)
property.
Our results mentioned above concern universally meager subspaces but the origin of our investigations is related to other properties of topological spaces and Theorem
1.2
helps to solve more problems.

A set
X\subseteq 2^{\omega}
has
strong measure zero
(
SMZ
, in short) if for every sequence

{\langle\,\epsilon_{n}:n\in\omega\,\rangle}
of positive reals there exists a sequence

{\langle\,a_{n}:n\in\omega\,\rangle}
of “centers” such that
the family
\{\,\mathrm{B}(a_{n},\epsilon_{n}):n\in\omega\,\}
covers
X
, where

\mathrm{B}(a,\epsilon):=\{\,x\in{2^{\omega}}:\rho(x,a)<\epsilon\,\}

and
\rho
is any metric generating the standard topology on
2^{\omega}
.
This notion was introduced by Borel who conjectured
[
11
]

that only countable sets have this property.
The Borel conjecture has been
consistently refuted by Sierpiński
[
36
]
in 1928, and proved to be
consistent in 1976 by Laver
[
25
]
, and since then appears in different contexts as well as serves as a blueprint for similar conjectures in set theory of
the reals, see, e.g.,
[
15
]
.

Rothberger noticed
[
33
]
, that being a strong measure set might not be a topological property and he modified it to the following topological covering property.
By
space
we mean a topological space homeomorphic with a subspace of the Cantor cube
{2^{\omega}}
.
A space
X
is
Rothberger
if for any sequence
{\langle\,\mathscr{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\,\rangle}
of open covers of
X
, there are sets
U_{n}\in\mathscr{U}_{n}
for
n\in\omega
, such that the family
\{\,U_{n}:n\in\omega\,\}
covers
X
.
It is easy to see that each Rothberger subset of
{2^{\omega}}
has SMZ.

The first inspiration to this paper comes from our work
[
19
]
on concentrated and
\gamma
-spaces.
A space
X
is
concentrated on a countable set

D\subseteq X
, if the set
X\setminus U
is countable for any open set
U\subseteq X
containing
D
.
A space
X
is
concentrated
if it is uncountable and concentrated on some of its countable subsets.
Bartoszyński–Halbeisen conjectured
[
1
, Proposition 3.2]

that there exists a concentrated space of size
\omega_{2}
in the Miller model.
This conjecture has been refuted
[
19
]
, where we have also showed that any
\gamma
-space has size at most
\omega_{1}
in the Miller model (see
[
12
]
for the definition of
\gamma
-spaces).
Since the class of all Rothberger spaces of
{2^{\omega}}
contains all concentrated and
\gamma
-spaces
[
12
]
, we have asked in our previous work
[
19
]
, the following question.

The

is the statement that there are no SMZ sets of size
\mathfrak{c}
. In
[

, Corollary 3.6]
, it was shown that models obtained by using a countable support iteration of proper strongly

In
[

Bartoszyński and Shelah showed that the weak dual Borel conjecture holds in the Laver model and in the Miller model. They were interested in finding a model, where both the Borel conjecture and the dual Borel conjecture hold, which was finally found in
[

[

Theorem 1.6
.

In the Miller model, every SMZ set has size at most
\omega_{1}
, i.e., the weak Borel conjecture holds.

A subspace

is

if for any perfect set

is meager in the relative topology on

.
The proof of Theorem

uses an idea from
[

]
allowing to show that, in the Miller model, each SMZ set is perfectly meager.
By the result of Bartoszyński

, in the Miller model, every perfectly meager subspace is universally meager.
In the light of the above mentioned results, Theorem

is a consequence of Theorem

2.
Property
(\dagger)

The following notion was introduced in
[
32
]
.
All posets we consider are separative.

Definition 2.1
.

A forcing
\mathbb{P}
has property
(\dagger)

if for every countable elementary submodel
M\ni\mathbb{P}
of
H(\theta)
for sufficiently large
\theta

p\in\mathbb{P}\cap M

\varphi_{i}\colon\mathbb{P}\cap M\to\mathbb{P}\cap M
for all

\varphi_{i}(p)\leq p

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}\cap M
and
i\in\omega

(M,P)
-generic condition
q\leq p

forcing

(1)

\mbox{``}\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}\cap\{\varphi_{i}(p):p\in\mathbb{P}\cap M\}\ \ \mbox{is infinite for all}\ \ i\in\omega\mbox{''},

where
\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}
is the canonical
\mathbb{P}
-name for the
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter.

Using notations from above, any function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{P}\cap M\to\mathbb{P}\cap M
such that
\varphi(p)\leq p
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}\cap M
will be called

.

For our purposes in this work we shall need an equivalent
formulation of
(\dagger)
provided by the following straightforward result.

Lemma 2.2
.

The property

(\dagger)
of a forcing
\mathbb{P}
is equivalent to its version obtained
by replacing statement (
1
) with the following formally weaker one:

\mbox{``}\,\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}\cap\{\varphi_{i}(p):p\in\mathbb{P}\cap M\}\neq\emptyset\ \mbox{for all}\ \ i\in\omega\mbox{''}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that, if
\mathbb{P}
satisfies the condition in our Lemma, then it fulfills
(\dagger)
.
Using the separativity of
\mathbb{P}
, for every
i\in\omega
there is a sequence
{\langle\,\varphi^{i}_{j}:j\in\omega\,\rangle}
of regressive functions
from
M\cap\mathbb{P}
to
M\cap\mathbb{P}

such that

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(p)>\varphi^{i}_{0}(p)\geq\varphi_{i}(\varphi^{i}_{0}(p))>\varphi^{i}_{1}(p)\geq\varphi_{i}(\varphi^{i}_{1}(p))>

\displaystyle>\varphi^{i}_{2}(p)\geq\varphi_{i}(\varphi^{i}_{2}(p))>\cdots>\varphi^{i}_{j}(p)\geq\varphi_{i}(\varphi^{i}_{j}(p))>\varphi^{i}_{j+1}(p)\geq\cdots

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}\cap M
and
i,j\in\omega
.
Hence, we obtain that for every
p\in\mathbb{P}\cap M
and

the set

Q^{i}_{j}(p):=\{\,r\in\varphi_{i}[M\cap\mathbb{P}]:\varphi^{i}_{j}(p)\leq r\,\}

has size at least

.
Then there exists an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-generic condition
q\leq p

forcing

\mbox{``}\,\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}\cap\{\varphi^{i}_{j}(p):p\in\mathbb{P}\cap M\}\neq\emptyset\ \mbox{ for all}\ \ i,j\in\omega\mbox{''}.

Let
G
be a
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter over
V
with
q\in G
.
In
V[G]
, fix

for all

For each

is an infinite subset of

∎

.

Let

Let

Relations between Rothberger spaces and Hurewicz totally imperfect spaces

We proved that in the Goldstern–Judah–Shelah model the classes of Rothberger spaces and Hurewicz totally imperfect spaces coincide.
Under CH, there is a Rothberger space which is not Hurewicz, e.g., a Luzin set, and a Hurewicz totally imperfect space which is not Rothberger, e.g., a Sierpiński set, see
[
23
,
35
]
and references therein.
It follows that under CH, Rothberger and Hurewicz totally imperfect are incomparable properties.

In the Laver model all Rothberger spaces are countable (and thus trivial) and there is in ZFC an uncountable Hurewicz totally imperfect space.
In the Miller model, there is an uncountable Rothberger space and Rothberger implies Hurewicz totally imperfect (it follows from the inequality
\mathfrak{u}<\mathfrak{g}
, in this model).

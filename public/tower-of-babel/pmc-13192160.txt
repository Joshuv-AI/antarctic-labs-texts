MTHFD1L in muscle invasive bladder cancer: a multi-cohort study on prognosis and therapeutic response
Authors: Siqiao Peng, Yu Liu, Peisheng Huang, Qinwei Liu, Le Zhang, Cheng-Ya Hsu, Yanru Zeng, Jianming Lu, Haibo Zhao, Chao Cai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBladder cancer is one of the most common malignancies of the urinary tract. Among its subtypes, muscle invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) is particularly aggressive and often associated with poor prognosis. The efficacy of platinum-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy and radical cystectomy remains unsatisfactory. In recent years, immune checkpoint inhibitors have shown promising therapeutic potential in MIBC. However, reliable biomarkers for predicting treatment response are still lacking. Moreover, traditional clinical parameters, such as TNM staging, often fail to accurately assess patient outcomes. Therefore, identifying novel biomarkers is crucial for improving prognosis and optimizing treatment strategies in MIBC.MethodsWe first analyzed the expression pattern and prognostic significance of methylenetetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase (NADP + dependent) 1-like (MTHFD1L) across multiple cancer types. Subsequently, MIBC patient samples from three independent cohorts (TCGA-BLCA, GSE169455, and GSE48075) were used to evaluate the prognostic value of MTHFD1L through univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses combined with Kaplan–Meier survival analysis. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis and Gene Set Variation Analysis were performed to explore the biological functions of MTHFD1L. Mutation characteristics and immunotherapy response–related features were further analyzed based on MTHFD1L expression levels. Finally, the results were further verified by immunohistochemistry and cell function experiments.ResultsMTHFD1L was upregulated in multiple cancer types, and its elevated expression was significantly associated with unfavorable outcomes. In all independent MIBC cohorts, high MTHFD1L expression served as an independent risk factor for poor prognosis. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis indicated that MTHFD1L may promote tumor progression by activating immune-related and proliferative pathways while suppressing metabolic processes. Mutation analysis revealed a higher frequency of TP53 mutations in the MTHFD1L high-expression group. Moreover, immune response prediction suggested that patients with low MTHFD1L expression were more likely to benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitors therapy. Immunohistochemistry confirmed the overexpression of MTHFD1L in MIBC tissues, and cell function experiments demonstrated that MTHFD1L knockdown markedly inhibited bladder cancer cell proliferation, colony formation, and migration.ConclusionsMTHFD1L represents a promising and reliable biomarker for predicting prognosis and immunotherapy response in MIBC, providing a new foundation for the development of precision and personalized therapeutic strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04346-y.Keywords: Muscle invasive bladder cancer, MTHFD1L, Biomarker, Prognosis, Immunotherapy

Background

Bladder cancer (BC) is a common malignancy of the urinary tract. In 2022, it ranked ninth in global cancer incidence, with approximately 614,000 new cases and 220,000 deaths, making it one of the most prevalent and lethal genitourinary cancers [1]. Clinically, bladder cancer is stratified by invasion depth into non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) and muscle invasive bladder cancer (MIBC). Although NMIBC accounts for 75% of new diagnoses, one-third of these tumors eventually progress to MIBC. The remaining 25% of patients are diagnosed with MIBC at initial presentation [2–4]. Compared with NMIBC, MIBC is more aggressive, carries a poorer prognosis, and metastasizes early. The current standard therapy combines platinum-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) with radical cystectomy (RC). Cisplatin-containing NAC regimens improve five-year survival by only 5–8%, highlighting their limited clinical benefit [5, 6]. Moreover, around 40% of patients do not respond to NAC [7]. Although RC provides effective local tumor control, over 50% of MIBC patients still develop distant metastases postoperatively, and the five-year overall survival remains unsatisfactory at 46–63% [8, 9]. In recent years, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as programmed cell death protein 1(PD-1)/programmed cell death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitors, have improved outcomes in a subset of MIBC patients, making immunotherapy an increasingly important treatment option [10]. Although PD-L1 expression is currently used to guide ICI therapy, its predictive accuracy remains controversial, as expression levels do not consistently correlate with clinical outcomes [11, 12]. Accordingly, discovering new, reliable biomarkers is essential for improving prognosis and optimizing therapy in MIBC.

Methylenetetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase (NADP + dependent) 1 like (MTHFD1L), located on chromosome 6q25.1, encodes the mitochondrial, monofunctional 10-formyltetrahydrofolate synthetase. This enzyme converts 10-formyltetrahydrofolate to formate and tetrahydrofolate (THF), thereby driving the mitochondrial folate cycle [13, 14]. The folate cycle is central to one-carbon metabolism, supplying substrates for purine and pyrimidine synthesis, amino-acid turnover, and DNA methylation. Disruption of one-carbon metabolism is closely associated with the occurrence and progression of various diseases, including cancer [15–17]. Although MTHFD1L is recognized as a key component of the folate cycle, its precise biological roles and molecular mechanisms in human diseases, particularly malignancies, remain incompletely understood.

In recent years, research on MTHFD1L in human tumors has been continuously deepened. Prior research has demonstrated that MTHFD1L is significantly overexpressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [18], esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCA) [19], and papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) [20], and its high expression correlates with unfavorable prognosis. Eich et al. observed that MTHFD1L is significantly upregulated in BC and its high expression is associated with poorer overall survival (OS) based on TCGA database. Additionally, their study showed that MTHFD1L promotes bladder cancer cell proliferation and invasion [21]. However, the role of MTHFD1L in bladder cancer remains insufficiently explored due to the lack of validation across multiple independent cohorts and integrative multi-omics analyses. In this study, we focused on MIBC and integrated multiple independent cohorts. Through univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, and immunohistochemistry (IHC), we identified MTHFD1L as a potential prognostic biomarker. To further elucidate its role, we employed metabolic, mutational, and immune-related omics approaches to explore the mechanisms underlying MTHFD1L-mediated tumor progression and its predictive value for immunotherapy response. In addition, functional assays demonstrated that MTHFD1L knockdown significantly suppressed bladder cancer cell proliferation, colony formation, and migration, further supporting its oncogenic role in MIBC. Materials and methods

Collection and processing of public data

First, we downloaded RNA-seq data, somatic mutation data, and clinical information for the bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA) cohort from TCGA via the UCSC Xena platform [22], and selected MIBC samples for further analysis. The Ensembl IDs in the RNA-seq data were converted to SYMBOL and ENTREZ IDs using the “clusterProfiler” R package (version 4.12.2) and the “org.Hs.eg.db” R package (version 3.18.0) for downstream analyses [23, 24]. As external validation cohorts, two bladder cancer datasets (GSE48075 and GSE169455) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database [25–27], and only MIBC samples were included for analysis. Baseline characteristics for all datasets are summarized in Table S1.

Pan-cancer analysis

We used the “TCGAplot” R package (version 7.0.1) to extract the Transcripts Per Million (TPM) expression levels of MTHFD1L across samples in the TCGA pan-cancer cohort [28]. Clinical data were integrated from the UCSC Xena platform to assess MTHFD1L expression across various cancer types. Univariate Cox regression analysis was performed using the “survival” R package (version 3.5.8) [29].

Evaluation of the prognostic value of MTHFD1L

In the TCGA-BLCA, GSE48075, and GSE169455 cohorts, we selected samples with a follow-up time of ≥ 30 days for prognostic analysis based on different survival endpoints. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed. We stratified MIBC patients into high and low MTHFD1L expression groups based on the optimal cut-off value and performed Kaplan–Meier survival analysis.

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)

To investigate the biological role of MTHFD1L, we used Spearman correlation analysis to assess the correlation between MTHFD1L expression and other genes in the TCGA-BLCA cohort. GSEA was conullnducted using the clusterProfiler package to identify significantly enriched pathways based on the KEGG database [30]. Enriched pathways were ranked based on the Normalized Enrichment Score (NES), and pathways with a p-value < 0.05 were selected. The top 10 pathways with the highest NES and the bottom 10 pathways with the lowest NES were visualized.

Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA)

Metabolic pathways related to humans were extracted from the KEGG database using the clusterProfiler package. Based on the TCGA-BLCA cohort, samples were divided into high and low expression groups according to the median expression level of MTHFD1L. GSVA was then performed to evaluate the activity of metabolic pathways in each sample [31]. Subsequently, differential analysis between the two groups was conducted using the “limma” R package (version 3.58.1) [24]. Metabolic pathways were ranked by log2 fold change (log2FC), and the top two pathways with the highest log2FC (largest positive values) and the bottom two with the lowest log2FC (largest negative values/greatest absolute negative values) were selected for visualization. Furthermore, the correlation between the activity of these pathways and MTHFD1L expression levels was analyzed.

Immunohistochemistry

The tissue microarray (TMA) (HBlaU096Su01) used in this study was purchased from Shanghai Outdo Biotech Co., Ltd., China. The antibody used for IHC was anti-MTHFD1L (16113-1-AP, Outdo Biotech Co., Ltd.). The specific IHC procedure followed the same protocol as described in our previous study [32]. The IHC scores were determined by multiplying the proportion of positive cells by the staining intensity. The percentage of positive cells was scored as follows: 0 (< 5%), 1 (5%–25%), 2 (25%–50%), 3 (50%–75%), and 4 (> 75%). The scoring criteria for staining intensity were: 0 (negative), 1 (weak), 2 (moderate), and 3 (strong) [33]. To evaluate MTHFD1L’s predictive ability, ROC curve was generated, and AUC above 0.7 was regarded as demonstrating good predictive accuracy.

Cell culture and transfection

In this research, two human bladder cancer cell lines, T24 and SW1710, were utilized. Both cell lines were obtained from Procell Biotechnology (Wuhan, China). Cells were maintained under standard culture conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2). Specifically, T24 cells were grown in RPMI-1640 medium (Bio-Channel, Nanjing, China; BC-M-017–500 mL), whereas SW1710 cells were maintained in DMEM (Bio-Channel, Nanjing, China; BC-M-005). Each culture medium was supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (EallBio, 03.U16001DC) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco, 15140122). For transfection, cells were treated with siRNA-mate reagent (Tsingke, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Negative control siRNA and MTHFD1L-specific siRNAs (Tsingke, Beijing, China) were introduced into T24 and SW1710 cells. After 48 h of incubation, total RNA was extracted for qRT-PCR analysis. The sequences of all siRNAs applied in this study are provided in Supplementary Table 2.

Cell counting kit-8 assay

In 96-well plates, 1,000 transfected cells/well were inoculated. After 24, 48, 72, and 96 h incubation, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well. After 2 h of additional incubation, the optical density (OD) value at 450 nm was measured with a microplate reader.

Plate colony formation assay

Approximately 1 × 103 transfected cells were plated into 6-well plates and cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for about two weeks. After colony growth, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, followed by staining with 0.1% crystal violet for another 20 min. Stained colonies were photographed and counted to evaluate clonogenic capacity.

Transwell assay

Transwell chambers were used to evaluate the migratory and invasive capabilities of the cells. Briefly, 4 × 104 transiently transfected cells were added to the upper chamber containing serum-free medium, while the lower chamber was filled with complete medium. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C, cells on the upper surface of the membrane were gently removed, and the migrated/invaded cells were fixed with methanol and stained with 0.1% crystal violet (Solarbio). The stained cells were imaged and counted under a microscope.

Gene mutation analysis

We downloaded somatic mutation data of BLCA patients from the TCGA database, selected MIBC samples, and divided them into high and low expression groups based on the median expression level of MTHFD1L. Subsequently, we used the “maftools” R package (version 2.20.0) to visualize and analyze the mutational profiles of the high and low MTHFD1L expression groups [34], aiming to identify mutational differences between expression subgroups.

Prediction of immunotherapy response

To evaluate the predictive value of MTHFD1L in immunotherapy, we used the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) platform to assess the immunotherapy response and its association with the immune microenvironment in the TCGA-BLCA cohort [35, 36]. Additionally, using the IMvigor210 cohort, bladder cancer patients were stratified into high and low MTHFD1L expression groups based on the optimal cutoff value, followed by Kaplan–Meier survival analysis.

Statistical analysis

Data processing and visualization were primarily performed using R software (version 4.3.2), with some figures generated using the Sanger Box bioinformatics online platform [37]. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for comparisons between groups. Categorical variables were analyzed using the Chi-square test. Survival analyses were conducted using the log-rank test. Correlation analyses were performed using the Spearman method. All p-values were two-sided, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The thresholds for statistical significance were defined as follows: p < 0.05(*), p < 0.01(**), p < 0.001(***), p < 0.0001(****).

Results

Pan-cancer expression and prognostic analysis of MTHFD1L

To investigate the expression pattern and clinical relevance of MTHFD1L across different cancer types, we first compared its expression between tumor and normal tissues across 33 cancer types in the TCGA database. MTHFD1L expression was found to be significantly higher in BLCA compared to normal tissues. In addition, MTHFD1L was significantly upregulated in tumor samples of several other cancer types (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, we further analyzed paired tumor and normal samples from 15 cancer types (Fig. 2B), and consistently observed significant MTHFD1L upregulation in most of them, including BLCA, corroborating the findings in Fig. 2A. To assess the prognostic significance of MTHFD1L, we performed univariate Cox regression analysis. The cancer types with statistically significant results are shown in Fig. 2C, while the remaining cancer types without statistical significance are presented in Figure S1. The results showed that high MTHFD1L expression was associated with an increased risk of poor survival across multiple cancer types. Specifically, in BLCA, patients with high MTHFD1L expression exhibited an increased risk of death across OS, progression free interval (PFI), and disease specific survival (DSS), suggesting that MTHFD1L may serve as a risk factor for poor prognosis (Fig. 2C). These findings suggest that MTHFD1L may serve as a valuable prognostic biomarker.

Pan-cancer analysis of MTHFD1L expression and its association with prognosis. A Comparison of MTHFD1L expression between tumor and normal samples in the TCGA dataset. B Comparison of MTHFD1L expression between tumor and matched normal samples in the TCGA dataset. C Univariate Cox regression analysis using OS, progression free interval (PFI), and disease specific survival (DSS) as prognostic endpoints in the TCGA dataset

The prognostic value of MTHFD1L in MIBC

Given that MTHFD1L is associated with poor prognosis in BLCA based on pan-cancer analysis, we further focused on the MIBC subtype. Using the TCGA-BLCA, GSE169455, and GSE48075 datasets, we investigated the prognostic value of MTHFD1L in MIBC patients. Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that MTHFD1L was a significant predictor of patient prognosis (Fig. 3A–C). After adjusting for age, gender, clinical stage, and pathological stage in the multivariate Cox regression analysis, MTHFD1L remained an independent prognostic risk factor for MIBC (Fig. 3A–C). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis revealed that high MTHFD1L expression was significantly associated with poorer clinical outcomes (Fig. 3D). These results further highlight the prognostic significance of MTHFD1L in MIBC and emphasize its potential as a reliable biomarker for patient risk classification.

The prognostic value of MTHFD1L in MIBC. A-C Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses of clinical characteristics and MTHFD1L expression in the TCGA-BLCA, GSE169455, and GSE48075 cohorts. D Kaplan–Meier survival analyses were conducted for high and low MTHFD1L expression groups in each of the three cohorts

Biological function analysis of MTHFD1L

To explore the biological functions of MTHFD1L in MIBC, we conducted GSEA (Table S3). According to the NES, we identified the top 10 significantly activated and suppressed pathways. Several proliferation-related pathways were significantly activated, including Cell cycle and DNA replication. In contrast, metabolism-related pathways were significantly suppressed, primarily covering Metabolism of Xenobiotics by cytochrome P450, Drug metabolism cytochrome P450, and Linoleic acid metabolism (Figure S2A). These results suggest that MTHFD1L may promote MIBC progression by activating proliferation-related pathways and suppressing metabolism-related pathways.

Metabolic characterization associated with MTHFD1L expression

Based on the GSEA results, we observed that MTHFD1L expression is closely associated with metabolic pathways. To further investigate its role in metabolic reprogramming, we performed GSVA using KEGG-defined metabolic pathways to compare pathway activity between high and low MTHFD1L expression groups (Table S4). The results show that the high MTHFD1L expression group has higher GSVA scores in several metabolic pathways that are closely associated with tumor growth, such as Glycan biosynthesis and metabolism and Metabolism of cofactors and vitamins, indicating significantly enhanced activity of these pathways (Fig. 4A). Notably, previous studies have suggested that lipid metabolism may exert antitumor effects by modulating the immune response [38]. In our study, pathways associated with lipid metabolism exhibited markedly higher activity in the group with low MTHFD1L expression, suggesting a role in enhancing antitumor immunity and thereby inhibiting tumor progression. To elucidate the specific role of MTHFD1L in metabolic reprogramming in bladder cancer, we ranked the metabolic pathways by log2FC values and selected the top two and bottom two pathways for visualization. The results showed that, relative to the low MTHFD1L expression group, the high MTHFD1L expression group exhibited significantly elevated activity in the glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis – chondroitin sulfate/dermatan sulfate pathway and riboflavin metabolism pathway, while showing significantly reduced activity in linoleic acid metabolism and α-linolenic acid metabolism (Fig. 4B). Additionally, MTHFD1L expression showed a positive correlation with the activity of glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis – chondroitin sulfate/dermatan sulfate and riboflavin metabolism pathway, and negatively correlated with linoleic acid metabolism and α-linolenic acid metabolism pathway (Fig. 4C).

A GSVA scores of KEGG metabolic pathways across different metabolic categories in high and low MTHFD1L expression groups. B Comparison of GSVA scores for the top two pathways with the highest logFC values and the top two with the lowest logFC values (P < 0.05) between high and low MTHFD1L expression groups. C Correlation between MTHFD1L expression and the GSVA scores of the above pathways

High expression of MTHFD1L protein in MIBC tissue

To further validate MTHFD1L expression at the protein level in MIBC, we used a human bladder TMA constructed from 63 clinical patient samples, comprising a total of 96 tissue cores, including 44 MIBC, 19 NMIBC, and 33 adjacent normal tissue samples. IHC was performed to detect the expression and subcellular localization of the MTHFD1L protein in human bladder tissues, followed by IHC scoring (Fig. 5A, Table S5, S6, Figure S3). MTHFD1L was mainly located in the cytoplasm of bladder tissues. Strong brown staining was observed in MIBC tissues, while weaker staining was seen in adjacent normal tissues, suggesting that MTHFD1L is significantly upregulated in MIBC (Fig. 5B). The IHC scores of MTHFD1L were significantly higher in MIBC tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.91 (95% CI: 0.86–0.97) (Fig. 5C-D). Similarly, in the 24 paired MIBC and adjacent normal samples, MTHFD1L expression was also significantly higher in tumor tissues. The ROC analysis showed an AUC of 0.95 (95% CI: 0.91–0.99) (Fig. 5E-F). These results indicate that MTHFD1L is highly expressed in MIBC tissues and shows strong predictive performance in clinical samples.

Cell culture and functional assay

MTHFD1L-specific siRNAs (si-Control, si-MTHFD1L#1/2) were transfected into T24 and SW1710 cells, and knockdown efficiency was validated by qRT-PCR (Fig. 6A–B). CCK-8 and colony formation assays showed that MTHFD1L knockdown can significantly reduced the proliferative viability and colony formation ability of T24 and SW1710 cells (Fig. 6C–H). Transwell assays further demonstrated decreased migratory ability upon MTHFD1L knockdown (Fig. 6I–K). These results indicate that MTHFD1L knockdown suppresses bladder cancer cell proliferation and migration.

Effects of MTHFD1L on BC cells migration and invasion. A–B qRT-PCR showed reduced MTHFD1L expression after siRNA transfection in T24 and SW1710 cells. C–D CCK-8 assays showed that MTHFD1L knockdown inhibited cell proliferation. E–H Colony formation assays showed decreased clonogenic ability after MTHFD1L knockdown. I–J Transwell assays demonstrated impaired migratory ability after MTHFD1L knockdown

Mutation landscape of MIBC based on MTHFD1L expression in TCGA-BLCA

We analyzed somatic mutation data from the TCGA-BLCA cohort to compare mutation profiles between groups with low and high MTHFD1L expression. Both groups exhibited missense and nonsense mutations as the predominant types, with single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) being the most common variant class. Among single nucleotide variants (SNVs), C > T transitions occurred most frequently. The median tumor mutation burden (TMB) was 148 for the low expression group and 137 for the high expression group. The top 10 mutated genes were largely similar across groups, including TP53, TTN, KMT2D, ARID1A, MUC16, PIK3CA, KDM6A, and SYNE1 (Fig. 7A). Fig. 7B and C display the 20 most frequently mutated genes in each group. The overall SNV frequency was 95.63% in the high MTHFD1L expression group (175 out of 183 samples) and 95.65% in the low MTHFD1L expression group (176 out of 184 samples). Fig. 7D displays the frequently mutated genes (FMGs) shared by both groups. Notably, mutation frequencies of TP53 and KDM6A differed significantly, with TP53 mutations more prevalent in the high-expression group. In addition, we compared the expression levels of these shared FMGs between mutant and wild samples (Figure S4).

Response to immunotherapy

To investigate the association between MTHFD1L expression and immunotherapy response in MIBC patients, we performed an immune response analysis using data from the TCGA-BLCA cohort. The analysis revealed that a greater proportion of patients with low MTHFD1L expression responded to immunotherapy (Fig. 8A). Compared with the low expression group, patients in the high expression group exhibited significantly elevated TIDE and Exclusion scores, while the Dysfunction score was also higher but did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 8B). Additionally, we conducted a Kaplan–Meier survival analysis in BC patients from the IMvigor210 cohort. Among patients treated with PD-L1 inhibitors, those with low MTHFD1L expression had significantly longer survival than those with high expression (Fig. 8C). Taken together, these findings suggest that low MTHFD1L expression may be associated with better immunotherapy response and prolonged survival, supporting its potential as a predictive biomarker for immunotherapeutic outcomes in MIBC.

B Comparison of TIDE, Exclusion, and Dysfunction scores between low and high MTHFD1L expression groups. Discussion

BC is a highly heterogeneous disease. Among its subtypes, MIBC is particularly aggressive, poses significant challenges to clinical management due to its high mortality and risk of metastasis [8, 9]. Currently, NAC combined with RC is the standard treatment strategy for MIBC; however, it still has notable limitations in improving long-term survival. Although immunotherapy and targeted therapy have provided new options for MIBC, their clinical application is still in the exploratory stage, with effectiveness limited by resistance, adverse effects, and considerable inter-patient variability. Using multi-omics approaches to identify novel biomarkers for tumors is an effective strategy [39–41]. Based on this rationale, this study highlights MTHFD1L as a potential biomarker for MIBC and explores the predictive value of MTHFD1L in the response to immunotherapy.

MTHFD1L was first identified by Prasannan et al. in 2003 [42]. It catalyzes the conversion of 10-formyltetrahydrofolate to formate and THF, contributing to the cellular one-carbon pool. This process supplies essential metabolic intermediates required for DNA and RNA synthesis and generates NADPH, which helps tumor cells counteract oxidative stress [43, 44], thereby promoting tumor progression. Recent studies have increasingly reported that MTHFD1L is frequently overexpressed across multiple cancer types and is closely linked to unfavorable clinical outcomes. Nuzhat Sial et al. found that MTHFD1L was significantly upregulated in BLCA and several other cancer types, with elevated expression levels associated with reduced OS [45]. Zhongyun He et al. reported that MTHFD1L was upregulated in colorectal cancer (CRC) and that its high expression is related to poorer OS [46]. Similarly, Lei Wang et al. demonstrated that MTHFD1L was highly expressed in osteosarcoma based on two GEO datasets and was associated with poor survival outcomes [47]. These observations align with our pan-cancer analysis, highlighting the clinical value of MTHFD1L as a prognostic biomarker across various solid tumors. In our study, bioinformatics analyses of public datasets demonstrated that high MTHFD1L expression is strongly associated with poor prognosis in MIBC, and IHC revealed its upregulation in MIBC tissues.

In the GSEA analysis, we observed significant activation of pathways closely related to cell proliferation, such as the cell cycle and DNA replication. Prior research has established that disruptions in cell cycle regulation play a critical role in the onset and advancement of tumors [48]. In addition, DNA replication is considered a hallmark of cancer, and its aberrant activation can result in genomic instability and increased DNA damage, ultimately driving malignant tumor development [49, 50]. Therefore, we speculate that in MIBC, MTHFD1L may promote tumor progression by activating these proliferation-related pathways. At the same time, several lipid metabolism pathways were significantly suppressed in the GSEA results, including Linoleic acid metabolism, Steroid hormone biosynthesis, Fatty acid metabolism, and Alpha-linolenic acid metabolism. Consistently, GSVA analysis indicated a negative correlation between MTHFD1L expression and the activity of both the linoleic acid metabolism and α-linolenic acid metabolism pathways. Previous research has indicated that inhibiting lipid oxidation helps reduce the levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and increases tumor cell tolerance to oxidative stress, thereby preventing ROS-induced cell death [51]. Taken together, MTHFD1L may enhance tumor cell survival under harsh conditions such as hypoxia or nutrient deprivation by suppressing lipid oxidation metabolism. In summary, GSEA and GSVA analyses reveal that MTHFD1L may drive MIBC progression by promoting proliferation and inhibiting lipid metabolism-related oxidative stress, suggesting its potential value as a therapeutic target.

Mutation analysis showed a significantly higher frequency of TP53 mutations in the high MTHFD1L expression group compared to the low expression group. TP53 is a critical tumor suppressor gene that encodes p53, a multifunctional protein involved in tumor inhibition. p53 inhibits tumor development through various mechanisms, such as halting the cell cycle, triggering apoptosis, inducing cellular senescence, and regulating metabolic pathways [52, 53]. In most human cancers, TP53 mutations result in the loss of p53’s tumor-suppressive functions. When p53 function is impaired or lost, DNA damage in cells cannot be repaired, and the cell cycle becomes uncontrollable, which may ultimately lead to cancer progression [54, 55]. Therefore, the increased TP53 mutation rate observed in the high MTHFD1L expression group may contribute to the advancement of MIBC.

The TIDE algorithm is a widely used tool in immunotherapy research for predicting patient responses. It aims to predict patients' responses to ICIs treatment by simulating tumor immune evasion mechanisms [35]. We utilized the TIDE algorithm to evaluate the predictive value of MTHFD1L expression for immunotherapy response. The results showed that patients with high MTHFD1L expression exhibited significantly increased TIDE scores, indicating a higher likelihood of immune evasion and a diminished antitumor immune response. While no significant difference was found in T-cell dysfunction scores between groups, the T-cell exclusion score was notably higher in the high-expression group. This suggests that MTHFD1L may promote immune evasion primarily through enhancing T-cell exclusion. These findings support the hypothesis that elevated MTHFD1L expression contributes to immune evasion.

However, this study has certain limitations. First, our conclusions are primarily drawn from publicly available MIBC datasets and lack validation in prospective, multicenter clinical cohorts. Verification in larger, prospective studies will be necessary. Second, although we observed the upregulation of MTHFD1L in MIBC through immunohistochemistry, the exact biological processes through which it influences cancer development remain unclear. Therefore, future research should focus on functional experiments at the cellular level and in animal models to systematically elucidate the biological role of MTHFD1L in MIBC progression and its potential regulatory pathways.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our results suggest that MTHFD1L is a promising, and reliable biomarker for predicting prognosis and immunotherapy response in MIBC, providing a new foundation for the development of precise and personalized therapeutic strategies.

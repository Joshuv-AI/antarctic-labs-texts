The nature of χ c ​ 1 ​ ( 3872 ) \chi_{c1}\left(3872\right) and T c ​ c + ​ ( 3875 ) T_{cc}^{+}\left(3875\right) Preprint: TUM-EFT 193/24
Nora Brambilla, Abhishek Mohapatra, Tommaso Scirpa, Antonio Vairo

Abstract
Two decades ago the χ c ​ 1 ​ ( 3872 ) \chi_{c1}\left(3872\right) was discovered in the hadron spectrum with two heavy quarks. The discovery fueled a surge in experimental research, uncovering dozens of so called XYZ exotics states lying outside the conventional quark model, as well as theoretical investigations into new forms of matter, such as quark-gluon hybrids, mesonic molecules, and tetraquarks, with the potential of disclosing new information about the fundamental strong force. Among the XYZs, the χ c ​ 1 ​ ( 3872 ) \chi_{c1}\left(3872\right) and T c ​ c + ​ ( 3875 ) T_{cc}^{+}\left(3875\right) stand out for their striking characteristics and unlashed many discussions about their nature. Here, we address this question using the Born–Oppenheimer Effective Field Theory (BOEFT) and show how QCD settles the issue of their composition. Not only we describe well the main features of the χ c ​ 1 ​ ( 3872 ) \chi_{c1}\left(3872\right) and T c ​ c + ​ ( 3875 ) T_{cc}^{+}\left(3875\right) but obtain also model independent predictions in the bottomonium sector. This opens the way to systematic applications of BOEFT to all XYZs.

Born–Oppenheimer EFT.

BOEFT is a nonrelativistic effective field theory obtained from QCD on the basis of symmetry and scales separation.
It is suitable to describe bound states of two heavy quarks and any light degrees of freedom (LDF).
Strongly coupled potential NRQCD for quarkonium is the simplest realization of this theory
Brambilla et al. 2000
;
Brambilla et al. 2001
;
Pineda and Vairo 2001
;
Brambilla et al. 2003
.
The original BOEFT for hybrids was obtained in
Berwein et al. 2015
, with subsequent work
Brambilla et al. 2018
;
Oncala and Soto 2017
;
Soto and Tarrús Castellà 2020
;
Braaten and Bruschini 2024
up to
Berwein et al. 2024
,
where the equations for tetraquarks and pentaquarks were derived.

The first scale separation exploited by BOEFT is the scale separation between the mass
m_{Q}
of the heavy quarks and the energy scale of the LDF, gluons or light quarks part of the binding, which is of the order of the nonperturbative hadronic scale
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
:
m_{Q}\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
.
QCD static energies for all LDF excitations in the presence of two quarks separated by a distance
r
are classified in terms of the
BO quantum numbers
.
Given
{\bm{K}}
, the total LDF angular momentum, we define
\Lambda=|\hat{r}\cdot{\bm{K}}|
(
\Lambda=0,1,2,\dots
are denoted by
\Sigma,\Pi,\Delta,\dots
).
The BO quantum numbers
\Lambda^{\sigma}_{\eta}
identify the cylindrical
D_{\infty h}
representations, where
\eta=g,u
is the
[C]P
eigenvalue
\pm 1
(for
QQ
only parity is defined), and
\sigma=\pm 1
(only for
\Sigma)
is the eigenvalue of reflection through a plane containing the two sources.
Excited states are indicated as
\Lambda_{\eta}^{\sigma\prime}
,
\Lambda_{\eta}^{\sigma\prime\prime}
, ….
We may associate to the LDF also the label
\kappa\equiv\{k^{P[C]},f\}
,
where
k\left(k+1\right)
is the eigenvalue of
{\bm{K}}^{2}
and
f
is the flavor.
As
r\rightarrow 0
, the cylindrical symmetry reduces to a spherical symmetry labeled by
\kappa
, and some BO static energies become degenerate.
The QCD static energies are written in terms of gauge invariant generalized Wilson loops characterized by the BO quantum numbers
Berwein et al. 2024
.
Some of these Wilson loops have been computed in lattice QCD
Juge et al. 1999
;
Juge et al. 2003
;
Bali et al. 2000
;
Bali and Pineda 2004
;
Capitani et al. 2019
;
Schlosser and Wagner 2022
;
Bicudo et al. 2021
;
Sharifian et al. 2023
;
Höllwieser et al. 2023
;
Brown and Orginos 2012
;
Bicudo et al. 2016
;
Prelovsek et al. 2020
;
Sadl and Prelovsek 2021
;
Mueller et al. 2023
.

The next scale separation is the one between the energy of the LDF and the energy separations
of the heavy quarks:
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\gg E
.
This scale separation allows to define the BOEFT and obtain coupled Schrödinger equations to describe the exotics.
The potentials appearing in these equations are the static energies introduced above.
The potentials mix.
The mixing may occur either because the potentials become degenerate as
r\rightarrow 0
or because they carry the same BO quantum numbers and get close (degenerate) at some finite distance
Berwein et al. 2024
.

We present here for the first time predictions for
\chi_{c1}(3872)
and
T_{cc}^{+}(3875)
that follow from the Schrödinger coupled equations predicted by BOEFT.
Moreover, in
Berwein et al. 2024
, we have shown that BOEFT constrains the potentials at short and large distances.
At short distances
r
, we rely on the multipole expansion and express the potentials as a power series in
r

The constant term in the series is the adjoint meson mass in the case of
\chi_{c1}(3872)

and the triplet meson mass in the case of

At large distances, due to BO quantum numbers conservation, the potentials evolve into the
M\bar{M}
static energies.
The adjoint or triplet meson masses are the key parameters for the existence of states close to the heavy-light meson thresholds

.

Summary.

The BOEFT predicts the
\chi_{c1}\left(3872\right)
as made by a dominant part of two tetraquark components and a residual part of quarkonium, and the
T_{cc}^{+}\left(3875\right)
as made by a tetraquark.
The value of the adjoint mass and the triplet mass together with the structure of the potential originate states with a very large radius.
Still, the states are neither simple molecules nor compact tetraquarks but result from a conspiracy between the short- and long-range behavior of potentials that are constrained by symmetry and computed in lattice QCD.
This allows to explain a good part of the
\chi_{c1}\left(3872\right)
phenomenology.
Thanks to the BOEFT factorization, fixing the parameters in the
cc
sector
allows model-independent predictions in the
bb
and
bc
sectors.
The existence of
T_{cc}^{+}\left(3875\right)
may be related to the corresponding triplet meson being a good diquark.

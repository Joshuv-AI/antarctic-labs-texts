Effects of dietary benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil on growth performance, antioxidant capacity, immune function, and gut health in broiler, chickens with necrotic enteritis
Authors: Mei-he Long, Xiaonan Zhao, Ziyue Hu, Xiaodi E, Wenji Hu, Qiantai He, Jinshan Lin, Xuhui Lin, Youjun Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aims to investigate the efficacy and mechanism of action of a benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil complex (SLLEO) in preventing and controlling necrotic enteritis (NE) in broiler chickens caused by Clostridium perfringens infection. A total of 300 one-day-old 817 broiler chickens were randomly divided into five groups, with six replicates per group and ten birds per replicate. The control group (CON) and challenge group (CP) were fed a basal diet; the low, medium, and high-dose benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil groups (SLLEO-L, SLLEO-M, SLLEO-H) were fed basal feed supplemented with 0.1%, 0.2%, and 0.3% benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil, respectively. The animal experiment lasted 35 days.Results indicate that supplementing feed with benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil (SLLEO) effectively mitigates the adverse effects of NE infection on broiler chickens. Compared to the Clostridium perfringens infection model, SLLEO significantly increased the average daily weight gain of infected chickens while reducing abnormally elevated liver and bursa of Fabricius indices (P < 0.05). SLLEO elevated total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity in plasma, liver, and jejunal mucosa while decreasing malondialdehyde (MDA) content (P < 0.05). Significantly increased plasma immunoglobulin A (IgA) levels, markedly elevated the villus height-to-crypt depth ratio (VH/CD), and reduced intestinal lesion scores (P < 0.05). Additionally, SLLEO downregulated mRNA expression of the proinflammatory factor TNF-α in the jejunum while upregulating the expression of the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10 and tight junction proteins ZO-1, Occludin, Claudin-1, and mucin MUC-2 (P < 0.05).In summary, dietary supplementation with SLLEO effectively mitigated growth performance decline in broilers caused by Clostridium perfringens infection, improved antioxidant function, alleviated immune organ hypertrophy, regulated plasma immunoglobulin levels, restored intestinal morphology, downregulated pro-inflammatory factors while upregulating anti-inflammatory factors, and enhanced intestinal barrier function by increasing tight junction protein and mucin gene expression.

Introduction

Necrotic enteritis (NE) is one of the most devastating intestinal diseases under intensive farming conditions (Latorre et al., 2018). It is caused by the proliferation of Clostridium perfringens type A and its secretion of α-toxin and enterotoxin. This leads to necrosis of the intestinal villus epithelium, increased intestinal permeability, and acute mortality in broilers (Rajput et al., 2020), resulting in substantial economic losses for the global broiler industry. Statistics indicate that economic losses attributable to NE reach as high as $6 billion (Zahoor et al., 2018). Amidst the ban on antibiotic use, preventing and controlling NE presents severe challenges. Therefore, identifying a novel, highly effective, sustainable, and safe feed additive for preventing and controlling NE is crucial.Combinations of organic acids and essential oils have shown promise due to potential synergistic effects. However, systematic studies evaluating the efficacy and mechanisms of a benzoic acid and cinnamon essential oil complex against Clostridium perfringens-induced NE are still limited.

Cinnamon essential oil (CEO) is rich in active components such as cinnamaldehyde, exhibiting triple functions of antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties, and is regarded as an antibiotic alternative (Cox et al., 2021; Jin et al., 2025). Benzoic acid (BA), as a short-chain organic acid, can lower intestinal pH, inhibit pathogenic bacteria, and improve the microecology (Chen et al., 2025). Recent studies indicate a potential synergistic effect when CEO and BA are used together (Yang et al., 2024), but research on the combined application of benzoic acid and cinnamon essential oil for controlling NE remains limited. Therefore, this study aims to systematically evaluate the preventive and protective effects of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil on a Clostridium perfringens-induced necrotic enteritis model in broiler chickens (strain 817). By comprehensively assessing the impacts on the growth performance, immune function, and intestinal health indicators of the broilers, this study provides theoretical support for the sustainable application of benzoic acid and cinnamon essential oil in the livestock industry.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

All animal care and experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Institute of Animal Health, Guangdong Academy (approval no. YC-PT2023032). Management, housing, and slaughter were conducted in accordance with the committee's guidelines.

Broiler chickens, diets, and experimental design

In this study, 300 healthy, day-old 817 broiler chicks (equal numbers of males and females) were selected in normal physical condition. They were randomly divided into five groups: the control group (CON), the challenge group (CP), the low-dose benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil group (SLLEO-L), medium-dose benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil group (SLLEO-M), and high-dose benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil group (SLLEO-H). All groups except CON were challenged with Clostridium perfringens. Each group had 6 replicates with 10 chickens per replicate. The CON and CP groups were fed a basal diet. The SLLEO-L, SLLEO-M, and SLLEO-H groups received basal diets supplemented with 1000 mg/kg, 2000 mg/kg, and 3000 mg/kg of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil, respectively. The benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil complex (SLLEO) used in the experiment was supplied by a company (Nuacid Nutrition Co.,Ltd.,Qingyuan,Guangdong,China). The main components of SLLEO are benzoic acid ≥40% and cinnamon essential oil ≥0.5%. Based on the assigned treatments, the basal diet was formulated according to the Chinese Broiler Feeding Standards (NY/T 33—2004) (wheat-soybean meal feed, see Table 1). The experiment was conducted at the animal research facility of Guangdong Nuacid Nutrition Co., Ltd Base in Qingyuan City,China. The entire experimental period lasted 35 days, with broiler chickens housed in two-tier battery cages serving as the experimental units. Establishment of the NE model

This study adopted the NE model previously described and modified (Goo et al., 2023). During days 15∼17 of the experiment, broilers in the CON group received daily oral administration of 1 mL of broth medium free of Clostridium perfringens, while broilers in other groups received daily oral administration of 1 mL of Clostridium perfringens type A -CVCC52 suspension (1 × 10⁹ CFU/mL).This strain was provided by the Animal Health Institute of the Guangdong Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Guangzhou, China).

Measurement of growth performance parameters

On mornings of Days 1,14and 35, following an 8-hour fasting period, total body weight and remaining feed quantity per cage were weighed and recorded. Based on these measurements, the following parameters were calculated: average daily feed intake (ADFI), average daily gain (ADG), and ADFI/ADG was used to calculate feed conversion ratio (FCR) for each stage. Dead chickens were weighed and recorded for FCR value correction and mortality rate.

Sample collection

Samples were collected at 35 days of age. One chicken was randomly selected from each replicate, and blood samples were collected from the wing vein using a sterile syringe. Samples were allowed to stand for 30 minutes, then centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C. The separated serum was stored in a −20°C freezer. Following blood collection, chickens were euthanized via cervical dislocation for subsequent sampling: Immune organ weights were measured, and tissues including liver, spleen, thymus, bursa of Fabricius, and jejunum were collected. These tissues were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C for molecular and immunological analysis. A 2-cm segment of the mid-jejunum was excised, rinsed with 0.9% saline, and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde buffer for morphological analysis.

Intestinal lesion score

On day 35,perform intestinal lesion scoring, observe and photograph the intestines. The intestinal lesion scoring criteria follow Keyburn (Keyburn et al., 2006), with scores ranging from 0 to 6. Assay of morphology in the jejunum

Fixed jejunum segments were dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. Sections were then deparaffinized with xylene, rehydrated through a graded alcohol series, and mounted onto slides. Subsequently, slides were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for morphological observation. The sections were scanned using a white light scanner.Subsequently,the scanned sections were observed and statistically analyzed using NDP.view2 software. For each section,8–10 villi with different orientations and relatively intact structures were selected. The villus height (VH) and crypt depth (CD) were measured, with the average value serving as the final measurement. The ratio of villus height to crypt depth (VH/CD) was then calculated.

Immune organ index

Each experimental chicken scheduled for slaughter was weighed prior to slaughter to record live body weight. After sampling, the liver, spleen, thymus, and surrounding fat of the bursa of Fabricius were removed,weighed,and recorded.The organ index was calculated using the formula:OrganIndex=OrganWeight(g)/LiveBodyWeight(kg)

Antioxidant capacity and immunological indicators

Malondialdehyde (MDA), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-px) activity were measured in plasma, liver, and jejunal mucosa. These kits were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Biotechnology Institute and tested according to manufacturer instructions. Plasma levels of immunoglobulin A (IgA), immunoglobulin G (IgG), and immunoglobulin M (IgM) were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits from Solarbio (Shanghai, China) to assess broiler immune function. All testing procedures strictly followed manufacturer protocols.

Gene expression of the jejunum

Total RNA was extracted from intestinal samples using the Trizol method and analyzed via 1.0% agarose gel electrophoresis. The total RNA sample concentration was measured using a nucleic acid quantifier (Eppendorf, Germany), and the A260nm/A280nm ratio was calculated. After purification by DNase I digestion (TaKaRa), RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using M-MLV reverse transcriptase (TaKaRa). SYBR Green was employed as the fluorescent dye (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and a 20 μL reaction system was used in a quantitative PCR instrument (Bio-Rad): 0.4 μL of each forward and reverse primer, 10 μL of fluorescent dye, 7.2 μL of ddH₂O, and 2 μL of cDNA. Reaction conditions: 95°C pre-denaturation for 30 seconds; 95°C denaturation for 5 seconds; annealing at 60°C for 30 seconds, repeated for 40 cycles. After cycling, PCR product specificity was verified via melting curve analysis. GAPDH served as the internal reference gene, and the relative mRNA expression levels of the target gene were calculated using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method. Data analysis

After preliminary organization in Microsoft Excel 2021, the experimental data were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) using SPSS 25.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Significant differences were further compared using Duncan's multiple range test. Results are presented as the mean±standard error of the mean, with P < 0.05 indicating statistically significant differences.

Discussion

Effects of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil on growth performance of broilers infected with necrotic enteritis

Growth performance serves as a core indicator for evaluating the economic efficiency of poultry farming and the effectiveness of nutritional interventions (Zavelinski et al., 2024). Extensive research demonstrates that Clostridium perfringens infection markedly reduces broiler weight, ADG, and ADFI while increasing FCR ratios (Yang et al., 2022; Zavelinski et al., 2024). Our experimental results indicate that Clostridium perfringens-induced necrotic enteritis in chickens significantly reduces body weight and ADG in 35-day-old 817 broilers, while increasing the F/G in broilers from 1 to 35 days of age. Yuan et al. (Yuan Minggui et al., 2025) Yuan et al. (2025) observed that C. perfringens–induced necrotic enteritis markedly decreased ADG at 14 and 21 days and increased FCR in broilers, while LI et al. (Song et al., 2022) similarly demonstrated reduced body weight and increased FCR in broilers aged 13–25 days.Although previous studies employed different Clostridium perfringens strains and infection models, consistent results indicate that Clostridium perfringens impairs growth performance in broilers.

Previous studies have shown that either benzoic acid (Fan Zheng et al., 2023) or cinnamon essential oil (Zheng et al., 2023) alone can alleviate growth performance declines in broilers with necrotic enteritis. Furthermore, numerous studies have shown that the combination of organic acids and plant essential oils exhibits synergistic effects. Yan Lei et al. (Yan Lei et al., 2022) found that the combination of benzoic acid and plant essential oils increased ADG and ADFI in Ross 308 commercial chickens from 1 to 37 days of age. Li Dong et al. (Li Dong and Xue Ruiting, 2021) observed that the mixed use of benzoic acid and essential oils enhanced ADG and ADFI across all growth stages of broilers while reducing FCR ratios. Joycy (Khukhodziinai et al., 2024) and Rosa et al. (Aristimunha et al., 2016) also made similar discoveries. In this trial, adding benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil to the diet of Clostridium perfringens-infected 817 broilers significantly enhanced their growth performance, nearly returning to the levels observed in the CON group. This indicates that under Clostridium perfringens infection conditions, adding appropriate amounts of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil to the diet can partially restore the growth performance decline in 817 broilers caused by the infection. The mechanism is presumed to involve benzoic acid lowering intestinal pH to inhibit pathogen colonization, while cinnamon essential oil directly disrupts bacterial cell membranes, suppressing energy metabolism, cell division, and quorum sensing. Their synergistic action improves broiler intestinal health, alleviates nutrient competition between pathogens and hosts, enhances nutrient absorption, and ultimately boosts growth performance.

Effects of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil on intestinal tissue morphology and pathological damage scores in broilers infected with necrotic enteritis

Research has confirmed that Clostridium perfringens infection disrupts the integrity of the intestinal mucosal structure, leading to villus atrophy and crypt hyperplasia (Golder et al., 2011). Maha et al. (Mohamed et al., 2020) reported that Clostridium perfringens infection reduces villus height and increases crypt depth in Cobb broilers. Research demonstrates that dietary supplementation with benzoic acid significantly enhances the VH/CD ratio in the jejunum and ileum of 28-day-old broilers infected with Clostridium perfringens (Fan Zheng et al., 2023). Umar et al. (Umar et al., 2016) found that cinnamon essential oil alleviates villus height reduction caused by necrotic enteritis by modulating intestinal morphology. In this trial, Clostridium perfringens infection broilers exhibited a highly significant decrease in jejunal VH/CD ratio and a highly significant increase in CD ratio, consistent with previous studies. This indicates that Clostridium perfringens infection disrupts intestinal villus structure and inhibits nutrient absorption in the small intestine. The addition of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil to the diet significantly increased the VH/CD ratio in the jejunum of broilers, indicating that the combination of benzoic acid and cinnamon essential oil can alleviate intestinal damage caused by necrotic enteritis.

Studies by Dahiya (Dahiya et al., 2005) and Dorthe (Sandvang et al., 2021) demonstrated that Clostridium perfringens infection significantly elevates intestinal lesion scores in broilers. The results of this study indicate that adding benzoic acid to feed in combination with cinnamon essential oil can significantly reduce intestinal lesion scores. Xu (Xu et al., 2021) and Shamshir et al. (Shamshir Garan et al., 2022) demonstrated that Clostridium perfringens infection induces broilers villus shedding, inflammatory cell infiltration, and disruption of intestinal mucosal integrity. Supplementing the feed diet with benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil significantly ameliorated these conditions. Van et al. (Van Damme et al., 2021) indicated that benzoic acid supplementation in a necrotic enteritis model alleviated villus damage induced by Clostridium perfringens infection. Studies demonstrate that cinnamon essential oil significantly ameliorates villus damage caused by NE; in an NE challenge model, experimental chickens receiving the essential oil mixture exhibited restored villus height (Zheng et al., 2023). Furthermore, plant essential oils protect intestinal villi from inflammatory damage by increasing goblet cell numbers and reducing macrophage infiltration (Elshershaby et al., 2024). From the above, it can be inferred that the synergistic combination of benzoic acid and cinnamon essential oil protects and repairs the physical barrier structure of the intestine by inhibiting pathogen proliferation and reducing mucosal inflammatory responses, ultimately improving the digestion and absorption of nutrients.

Effects of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil on antioxidant function in broilers infected with necrotic enteritis

Yu et al. (Yu et al., 2022) reported that NE infection in broilers significantly reduced serum T-SOD and T-AOC levels, as well as GSH-PX activity in the jejunum and ileum, and T-SOD and T-AOC activity in the jejunum. Zhang et al. (Zhang et al., 2024) reported that Arbor Acres broilers infected with Clostridium perfringens exhibited significantly reduced plasma GSH-Px and T-SOD activity. The present study shares similarities with these findings: 817 broilers infected with Clostridium perfringens showed significantly decreased plasma, liver, and jejunal mucosal T-AOC and SOD, along with markedly elevated MDA levels; and liver GSH-PX activity was markedly decreased. Research demonstrates that benzoic acid enhances antioxidant enzyme activity; its carboxyl group exhibits reducing properties, binding with free radicals to scavenge them and mitigate oxidative damage (Jia et al., 2022). Research indicates that active components in cinnamon essential oil, such as cinnamaldehyde and eugenol, can directly engage in redox reactions with ROS by providing hydrogen atoms, thereby neutralizing and eliminating free radicals (Karimirad et al., 2025). Additionally, studies suggest that the combined use of benzoic acid and essential oils significantly enhances GSH-Px and GST activity while markedly reducing MDA levels, demonstrating a synergistic potentiation effect in antioxidant activity (Li Dong and Xue Ruiting, 2021). Consistent with prior studies, the present experiment demonstrated that adding appropriate levels of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil to the diet significantly increased T-AOC, SOD, and GSH-Px activity in broiler plasma, liver, and jejunal mucosa, while significantly reducing MDA content, with the high-dose group showing the best recovery effect. Joycy et al. (Khukhodziinai et al., 2024) indicated that the combined use of benzoic acid and essential oils in broiler chicken feeding indirectly enhances antioxidant capacity by regulating intestinal health. Research indicates that the antioxidant activity of essential oils is comparable to ascorbic acid, and co-administration with benzoic acid further inhibits free radical damage (Chroho et al., 2024). Integrating these findings suggests that supplementing feed with appropriate levels of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil can mitigate antioxidant capacity decline induced by Clostridium perfringens infection.

Effects of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil on the immune function of broilers infected with necrotic enteritis

The spleen, bursa of Fabricius, and thymus are the core organs of the central and peripheral immune systems in poultry. Their developmental indices directly reflect immune status. The thymus serves as the central organ for cellular immunity, the bursa of Fabricius is a unique humoral immune organ in poultry, and the spleen is the largest peripheral immune organ in birds. Together, the thymus, spleen, and bursa of Fabricius participate in systemic cellular and humoral immunity (Zheng et al., 2023). Changes in immune organ weights can indirectly indicate the body's immune status, with bacterial or toxin infections potentially causing organ enlargement and functional imbalance (Song et al., 2021). Research has shown that in a model of combined coccidial and Clostridium perfringens infection, liver and bursa of Fabricius indices significantly increased in 21-day-old broilers. This finding suggests that during the early infection stage, the body experienced high-level Clostridium perfringens infection, leading to compensatory enlargement of related immune organs (Fan Zheng et al., 2023). Consistent with these findings, in the present study, Clostridium perfringens-induced necrotic enteritis significantly increased liver and bursa of Fabricius indices in broilers, resulting in compensatory enlargement. Supplementing the diet with benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil restored liver and bursa indices to normal levels, with the high-dose group showing the most pronounced improvement, nearly returning to baseline values. Cheng Qiang (Cheng Qiang, 2018) noted that adding coated cinnamon oil to the diet had no significant effect on the thymus index, spleen index, or bursa of Fabricius index in broilers. Research has demonstrated that adding benzoic acid to feed improves NE-induced hepatomegaly and bursitis in broilers, significantly reducing immune organ indices (Fan Zheng et al., 2023). Xiong Juan (Xiong Juan et al., 2019) demonstrated that combining acidifiers with plant essential oils enhances broiler immunity. Integrating these prior studies, it is hypothesized that benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil mitigates Clostridium perfringens infection damage by protecting immune organs and enhancing their function.

Immunoglobulins are key effector proteins in humoral immune responses. Produced by the immune system, they can specifically recognize and bind to the antigens that induced their formation, thereby targeting and eliminating pathogens (Matsumoto, 2022). Within animal organisms, immunoglobulins exert multiple functions through their diverse molecular structures, including antigen-binding domains, binding domains, and effector domains (Bodo et al., 2023). In this study, plasma IgA levels in 35-day-old broilers significantly decreased following Clostridium perfringens infection. However, adding benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil to the diet significantly increased plasma IgA levels. Cheng Qiang et al. (Cheng Qiang, 2018) reported that adding 50 mg/kg coated cinnamon oil to the diet increased serum IgA and IgG levels in 21-day-old broilers, enhancing their immune function. Studies have demonstrated that cinnamon essential oil can elevate serum IgA, IgM, and IgG levels (Su et al., 2018). Research indicates that in cases of combined coccidiosis and Clostridium perfringens infection, supplementing feed with benzoic acid can elevate serum IgG and IgM levels in broilers (Fan Zheng et al., 2023). Additionally, studies indicate that the combined use of organic acids and plant essential oils significantly elevates serum IgG and IgM levels in broilers (Chen et al., 2024). Integrating these findings, research reveals that supplementing feed with appropriate levels of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil can partially mitigate the immune suppression caused by Clostridium perfringens infection.

Effects of benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil on inflammatory and barrier-related gene expression in the intestines of broilers infected with necrotic enteritis

The intestinal mucosa plays a crucial role in defending against Clostridium perfringens infection by activating immune responses and releasing cytokines. Upon pathogen invasion, the body releases pro-inflammatory cytokines to initiate defense mechanisms. These cytokines are pivotal in the host immune response, while anti-inflammatory cytokines are responsible for suppressing and regulating immune reactions (Schnurr et al., 2023). IL-1β and IL-6 participate in and promote inflammatory responses; TNF-α, as a central regulator of inflammation, governs immune responses, inflammatory processes, and apoptosis. In this context, the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10 mitigates inflammatory reactions by antagonizing pro-inflammatory factors, playing a vital role in maintaining intestinal immune homeostasis. Studies indicate that Clostridium perfringens infection significantly increases the relative expression levels of genes encoding IL-1β, TNF-α, and others in broiler intestines (Chen Suo, 2021). Chen Jing et al. (Chen Jing, 2024) demonstrated that combined Clostridium perfringens and coccidial infection leads to a significant increase in IL-1 and TNF-α gene levels and a significant decrease in IL-10 gene levels in the jejunum of broiler chickens. Li et al. (Wu et al., 2021) observed that NE infection significantly upregulates mRNA expression levels of proinflammatory cytokines IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α. Tang et al. (Tang et al., 2022) similarly found that Clostridium perfringens infection markedly increased expression of proinflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-1β, along with inflammation pathway-related genes TLR-4 and NF-κB, in jejunal mucosa. These studies collectively demonstrate that necrotic enteritis induces host genes involved in innate immunity of the intestinal mucosa. Consistent with these findings, our study revealed that Clostridium perfringens infection in broilers upregulates proinflammatory factors IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α in the jejunum, while the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10 is significantly downregulated. Previous research indicates that cinnamon essential oil supplementation in infection models reduces mRNA expression of proinflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-6, thereby alleviating intestinal inflammation (Park et al., 2025). Xiao et al. (Xiao et al., 2024) and Wang et al. (Wang et al., 2022) observed that benzoic acid significantly downregulated the expression of inflammation-related genes IL-1β and TNF-α, thereby mitigating intestinal inflammatory responses. Additionally, Ting et al. (Huang et al., 2019) found that benzoic acid increased the expression of the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10, further alleviating inflammation. Wang et al. (Wang et al., 2025) indicated that the benzoic acid-cinnamaldehyde complex alleviates intestinal inflammation by inhibiting the TLR2/4 signaling pathway, thereby reducing the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and TNF-α in the intestine. Consistent with previous findings, supplementing the experimental diet with benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil resulted in a significant downregulation of relative TNF-α expression in the jejunum of broilers. while IL-6 and IL-1β showed a clear upward trend without reaching significant expression levels. Concurrently, the expression of the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10 was significantly increased, with the high-dose supplementation group nearly returning to the control group's level. This indicates that supplementing feed with benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil can alleviate the inflammatory response caused by Clostridium perfringens infection and mitigate the damage resulting from necrotic enteritis. The proposed mechanism of action for benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil may involve inhibiting the activation of inflammatory pathways, downregulating the excessive expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α, while simultaneously significantly elevating the levels of the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10. This complex action collectively mitigates the excessive inflammatory response triggered by Clostridium perfringens infection.

The mechanical barrier function of the intestine relies on the synergistic action of tight junction proteins and mucins (Li et al., 2021). Claudin-1 regulates paracellular ion-selective permeability (Qayyum et al., 2024); Occludin maintains intercellular adhesion and barrier stability (Kim and Kim, 2017); ZO-1 connects transmembrane proteins to the cytoskeleton, ensuring structural integrity (Han et al., 2016); while MUC-2 secreted by goblet cells forms the mucus layer, trapping and eliminating pathogenic microorganisms(Fu et al., 2022). These molecules collectively maintain intestinal homeostasis. Xu Xiao et al.(Xu Xiao, 2022) reported downregulation of barrier-related genes Claudin-1, Claudin-3, Occludin, and ZO-1 in broilers with necrotic enteritis, indicating compromised intestinal barrier function. Guo et al. (Guo et al., 2023) found that Clostridium perfringens infection significantly reduced mRNA expression levels of Occludin and Claudin-1 in the jejunum of broiler chickens. Research indicates that Clostridium perfringens infection disrupts intestinal barrier integrity by decreasing MUC-2 expression (Xia Yi et al., 2019). In this study, relative mRNA expression of Claudin-1, Occludin-1, ZO-1, and Mucin-2 in the jejunum was significantly downregulated following Clostridium perfringens infection in broilers. Related reports indicate that benzoic acid enhances intestinal barrier integrity by upregulating mucin and tight junction protein gene expression (Fan Zheng et al., 2023). Studies by Wang et al. (Wang et al., 2021) and Cao et al. (Cao et al., 2025) demonstrated that in necrotizing enteritis models, benzoic acid improves intestinal barrier function by upregulating the expression of tight junction proteins Claudin-1, Claudin-3, Occludin, and ZO-1. Qi et al. (Qi et al., 2021) observed that cinnamon essential oil upregulates expression of intestinal tight junction proteins Occludin and Claudin-1, enhancing barrier function of intestinal epithelial cells and reducing pathogen and toxin permeability. Similarly, Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2018) found significantly increased Occludin gene expression levels after supplementing broiler feed with cinnamon essential oil. This study shares similarities with the aforementioned research. After adding benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil to the diet of broilers infected with Clostridium perfringens, the mRNA expression levels of Claudin-1, Occludin-1, ZO-1, and Mucin-2 in the jejunum were significantly elevated. The high-dose supplementation group showed the most pronounced effect, with expression levels nearly restored to those of the CON group. As an organic acid, benzoic acid acidifies the intestinal environment, synergizing with cinnamon essential oil's antibacterial activity. Cinnamon essential oil also reduces inflammatory factor release by inhibiting the NF-κB pathway, thereby protecting intercellular junctional structures in intestinal epithelial cells. It is hypothesized that this combination may indirectly upregulate tight junction protein and mucin-related gene expression by suppressing pathogens and alleviating inflammatory responses, thereby improving intestinal barrier function. The specific mechanism requires further experimental validation.

Conclusion

Under the experimental conditions, supplementing the diet with benzoic acid combined with cinnamon essential oil effectively mitigated growth performance decline in broilers caused by Clostridium perfringens infection. This intervention improved antioxidant function, alleviated immune organ hypertrophy, regulated plasma immunoglobulin levels, restored intestinal morphology, downregulated pro-inflammatory factor expression while upregulating anti-inflammatory factors, and enhanced intestinal barrier function by increasing tight junction protein and mucin gene expression. Results indicate that benzoic acid and cinnamon essential oil exhibit synergistic effects in preventing necrotic enteritis in broilers, and enhanced intestinal barrier function by upregulating tight junction protein and mucin gene expression. These findings demonstrate that the benzoic acid and cinnamon essential oil complex exhibits synergistic potential in preventing necrotic enteritis and could serve as an effective antibiotic-free alternative for sustainable broiler production.

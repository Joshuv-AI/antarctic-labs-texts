Application of deep learning for transformation of Chinese traditional cultural narrative patterns and enhancement of cultural identity empowered by AIGC
Authors: Yan Liu, Hongmei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aims to achieve controllable generation of Chinese traditional cultural narrative content and enhance cultural identity. First, it constructs a tri-modal generation framework of text-image-style based on Stable Diffusion v2.1 and Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining (CLIP) models, realizing the joint modeling of traditional cultural semantics and visual imagery. Second, the study introduces the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) mechanism to embed traditional cultural style features in a lightweight manner, improving the model’s style adaptability under small sample conditions. Finally, a three-level evaluation system of “generation quality-semantic consistency-cultural identity” is built, covering both objective indicators and user feedback, to systematically verify the model’s performance. Results show that the proposed model significantly outperforms existing methods in multiple dimensions: in terms of image quality, the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) is 22.85, the Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) is 0.298, and the style recognition accuracy reaches 86.4%. Regarding narrative consistency, the Bilingual Evaluation Understudy (BLEU) score is 0.325, the CLIP text-image similarity is 0.793, and the Narrative Style Match is 82.3%. On the cultural perception level, the average user narrative resonance is 4.32 points, the imagery accuracy score is 0.748, and the question-answer task pass rate is 82.6%. The comparative results indicate that the proposed method has significant advantages in expressive diversity and depth of cultural communication. When properly designed, Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC) technology can be effectively used for the generation and identity reconstruction of Chinese traditional cultural narrative content. This study provides a scalable technical path for the integration of AI and traditional culture, and expands the boundaries of digital humanities in content generation and reception research.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-32302-5.

Introduction

Research background and motivations

Currently, Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC) technology is evolving at an unprecedented rate. From text generation and image synthesis to multimodal interaction, it is gradually becoming a significant force in content production1. Its widespread application in fields such as media, art, and education herald the emergence of a new digital narrative paradigm2. Against this backdrop, AIGC provides an efficient tool for information delivery and paves an innovative path for cultural expression3.

Meanwhile, Chinese traditional culture is facing the dual challenges of “discourse failure” and “audience disconnection”. On the one hand, traditional culture often relies on historical contexts and symbolic systems, and its narrative methods can be difficult to understand and may have aesthetic gaps in modern communication4. On the other hand, the younger generation has significant differences in media preferences and reception psychology compared to traditional forms of expression, leading to a gradual weakening of the effectiveness of cultural dissemination5. Therefore, there is an urgent need to use technology to digitally reconstruct the narrative patterns of traditional culture to enhance its vividness and approachability in the contemporary context of expression.

In multi-modal research such as Visual Question Answering (VQA), existing studies have significantly improved the semantic consistency and expression stability of generated content by enhancing visual semantic robustness and the language-image collaboration mechanism. This has inspired the design of cultural semantic alignment and narrative control mechanisms in this study. In recent years, style transfer technology in deep learning has made significant progress in image generation and artistic style simulation fields. Particularly, the new generation of generative models represented by diffusion models and Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining (CLIP) semantic guidance has been able to achieve high-quality semantic control and visual generation across modalities6. This technological approach provides the possibility for the digital translation of traditional cultural elements, allowing originally static and abstract cultural symbols to be dynamically interpreted in multimodal forms7. Based on this, the integration of AIGC and style transfer technology is expected to break through the “form-semantics” disconnection in the digitalization of traditional culture, achieving effective coupling between cultural core and contemporary expression.

Research objectives

This study aims to explore the implementation mechanisms and practical effectiveness of AIGC models based on deep learning style transfer technology in the narrative transformation of Chinese traditional culture. The specific goals include: constructing a multimodal generative model that integrates image generation, text semantics, and style control, and analyzing its pathways and methods in cultural narrative construction; proposing a generation control mechanism centered on narrative style labels and cultural identity feedback to achieve controllable generation and expressive diversity of cultural content.

Literature review

The introduction of the large pre-trained model Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT)-3 achieves context-relevant language generation through learning from massive amounts of text data. It also provides a foundational semantic engine for the reconstruction of cultural texts8. In the context of cultural dissemination, Wadhwani et al. (2021) examined the narrative reproduction of AIGC in museum settings, highlighting its significant potential for cross-cultural interpretation and audience emotional activation9. Assael et al. (2022), in their research on culturally-oriented content generation for educational purposes, used AIGC to automatically write traditional festival stories, enhancing cognitive engagement and emotional identification among young people10.

In the field of digital dissemination of Chinese traditional culture, researchers have mostly focused on aspects such as media translation, content redesign, and user acceptance11. Zhang et al. (2024) analyzed user preferences for traditional culture-themed short videos based on user interviews and found that visual imagery and emotional narration are key to attracting young audiences12. Sinhal et al. (2020) pointed out through questionnaires and web corpus analysis that current traditional cultural dissemination faced issues such as “heterogeneity of audience cultural backgrounds” and “high barriers to understanding classical texts”. It could easily lead to disconnection in communication and cultural fragmentation13.

Style transfer, as a core branch of computer vision, has evolved through several stages. It began with the Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN) model based on convolutional feature matching, progressed to the Style Generative Adversarial Network (StyleGAN) series with image style control capabilities, and then to diffusion models with cross-modal capabilities14. Yao et al. (2025) proposed text-guided style reconstruction in the CLIP space, effectively achieving triple consistency between text, image, and style. This opened a new direction for the integration of style transfer and semantic control15. In recent years, related research in the VQA field has put forward higher requirements for the semantic control ability of multi-modal generation. Chowdhury and Soni (2025) proposed a dynamic semantic enhancement mechanism based on the Reasoning-based Visual Question Answering (R-VQA) model to improve the robustness of image-text alignment16. Chowdhury and Soni (2024) optimized visual perception accuracy by weakening language prior bias17. Studies on Environmental Visual Question Answering (ENVQA) and Question-Specific Feature Visual Question Answering (QSFVQA) have shown that high-quality image semantic modeling and cross-modal feature fusion are key paths to ensure generation consistency18,19. Research on VQA further pointed out that logical combinatorial reasoning ability was the foundation for achieving stable narrative expression20. The above studies provide methodological inspiration for the construction of the traditional cultural semantic fusion model in this study.

In addition, existing studies have also explored semantic mapping and emotional symbol transfer issues in more complex scenarios. Bai et al. (2024) established a deconstruction and reconstruction mechanism of cultural symbolic schemas in medical image semantic generation21. Zermatten et al. (2025) verified semantic transfer ability under open vocabulary conditions in remote sensing multi-semantic scenarios, providing references for the cross-semantic label generation strategy of this study22. Wei et al. (2023) pointed out that emotional consistency control in the AIGC generation process was of great significance for the construction of cultural resonance11. These studies have further enriched the cultural adaptability, emotional regulation ability, and pan-context transplantation paths of AIGC-generated content.

Existing research has made significant progress in AIGC generation and style transfer technology, and has shown good application potential in cultural dissemination. However, it still falls short in the reconstruction of the deep narrative logic of traditional culture, the expressive labeling of narrative styles, and the quantitative evaluation mechanism of cultural identity.

Research model

Research framework

This study develops a holistic research framework with cultural element analysis, style feature transfer, narrative context generation and cultural identity feedback (Fig. 1).

Framework of holistic research.

The research framework consists of four core modules: (1) Traditional Cultural Corpus Input Module, (2) Style Transfer and Semantic Generation Module, (3) Narrative Structure Reconstruction Module, and (4) Cultural Identity Evaluation and Feedback Module. The framework takes the deep learning style transfer model (based on Stable Diffusion v2.1) as the technical core. It integrates three key elements (text, image, and style) as the basic pathway. This forms an automatic generation mechanism for the contemporary expression of traditional culture.

It also introduces the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) mechanism to embed traditional cultural styles through low-rank regulatory embedding, thereby enhancing the integration capability of the generated content in terms of visual representation and cultural imagery.

Intuitively, the core idea of the LoRA mechanism is as follows: Without modifying most parameters of the original large model, only “lightweight” style adjustment structures are inserted into specific sub-modules. Thus, fine-grained style control is achieved while maintaining the overall capabilities of the pre-trained model. Compared with full-parameter fine-tuning, LoRA greatly reduces training overhead and avoids the “catastrophic forgetting effect”.

The design of narrative transformation mechanism

To achieve style control and generative expression of traditional cultural content, this study constructs a system of five representative narrative style labels based on preliminary corpus analysis and image semantic clustering. This system integrates visual features, cultural semantics, and application scenarios, aiming to provide the model with a clear and controllable style guidance mechanism. Each type of style label refines several “core images” and associates them with specific “cultural orientations” and “application dimensions”, thereby ensuring the consistency and expressiveness of the model in the process of image generation and text guidance. In Table 1, these five types of labels include “Symbolism of Confucianism, Buddhism, and Taoism”, “Landscape Painting Style”, “Artistic Conception of Calligraphy”, “Legends and Myths”, and “Folk Life Scenes”. They cover a wide cultural space from philosophical symbols and natural landscapes to mythological narratives and folk experiences, providing clear semantic coordinates and expression boundaries for multi-modal content generation.

To enhance the model’s ability to understand complex semantics (such as metaphors and irony), this study introduces a text data subset containing “ironic sentences” during the training phase. The subset is extracted from typical implicit semantic paragraphs in Zhuangzi and A New Account of the Tales of the World. A BERT-based semantic discriminator is used for pre-classification of text prompts (literal vs. ironic). When the system detects potential ironic structures, it automatically switches to a context-nested image generation path to avoid generating “misinterpreted images.” The contrastive learning strategy in the CLIP embedding space is reweighted to reduce the priority of literal semantics in ironic contexts, thereby being more in line with the author’s intent. This mechanism effectively improves the accuracy of the model in generating ironic and metaphorical images and avoids literal misinterpretation of surface semantics.

The style recognition network Gs is constructed based on a lightweight Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture, with an overall structure similar to ResNet-18. It consists of four residual convolution modules and outputs the probability distribution of five types of style labels (corresponding to the five styles in Table 1). The training data is derived from the self-built “Cultural Style Image Dataset” in this study, totaling 6200 images covering five style types (about 1200 images per type), and the image samples are manually annotated by cultural experts. The confusion matrix shows that most styles have good separability, while certain cross-misclassification exists between individual styles (e.g., Calligraphy Artistic Conception and Landscape Painting Style), mainly due to the similarity in texture structure.

Model verification strategy

To systematically verify the effectiveness of the proposed AIGC generation model in the narrative expression of traditional Chinese culture, this study constructs a three-level and multi-dimensional verification mechanism covering semantic consistency, style alignment, and user perception responses, forming a complementary strategy of “automatic evaluation + subjective assessment”, as detailed below:

(1) Semantic Consistency Verification Path: Construct cross-modal image-text similarity metrics (e.g., CLIP-Sim, Simg−acc) using the CLIP semantic space to evaluate whether the generated images accurately convey the cultural images described in the text. Among them, CLIP-Sim is used for overall text-image matching, and Simg−acc focuses on the reducibility of image keyword terms.

(2) Style Consistency Verification Path: Perform style classification on the generated images through the style recognition network Gs, compare them with the input style labels to obtain Lstyle loss and accuracy metrics; combine the confusion matrix to evaluate the discriminative ability between various styles, and verify the stability and generalization of the style transfer module.

(3) User Subjective Cognition Evaluation Path: Set up user question-answering tasks and subjective scoring of narrative resonance, calculate Seng and Narrative Resonance Score to reflect the effectiveness of the generated content in cognitive processing and cultural empathy, considering both deep semantic understanding and emotional responses.

To further verify the adaptability and stability of the model in multi-style label scenarios, this study sets two cultural themes, namely “Confucianism-Buddhism-Taoism Images” and “Legends and Myths”, for full-process evaluation. It conducts comparative analysis on the generalization performance of various indicators under different cultural structures, reflecting the cultural transferability of the model structure.

Evaluation of image quality and style expression ability

The evaluation indicators cover: Fréchet Inception Distance (FID): Measures the overall distribution difference between generated images and real images; lower values are better. Style Recognition Accuracy: Uses a pre-trained style classifier (with five style labels) to judge the consistency between the generated image style and the target label, reflecting the style transfer effect. The comparison models include: Stable Diffusion v2.1 (SD 2.1), ControlNet + Style Reference Image, and StyleGAN-NADA (No Additional Domain Adaptation) (semantic style transfer). Cultural image restoration and user perception evaluation

To enhance the representativeness and scientific nature of subjective scoring, this study recruits 100 participants in total, including 60 in the youth group and 40 in the expert group. The youth group, aged between 18 and 25, mainly comes from professional backgrounds such as digital art, journalism and communication, and cultural industry, covering different universities and regions. The expert group consists of cultural university teachers, researchers from art institutions, etc. The selection criteria include: priority is given to those who have published papers related to traditional culture in core journals in the past five years or participated in provincial-level or higher digital cultural projects.

A double-blind design is adopted in the scoring phase. Participants conduct evaluations based on a unified scoring scale without knowing the configuration of the generative model. The scale includes three dimensions: ① Semantic Consistency (whether the content accurately expresses the text semantics); ② Image Style Expressiveness (whether it restores cultural style characteristics); ③ Cultural Resonance Degree (whether it triggers the audience’s cultural emotional identity). Each item adopted a 1–5 point Likert scale scoring, and the average value is finally taken as the input for the subjective scoring part of the Narrative Resonance Score. Although the total sample size is 100, it is above the medium level in AIGC-related cultural generation research, and the structural distribution is representative, which helps capture the perception differences between young users and professional scholars.

To improve the consistency of expert scoring, the expert group receives unified training before the official scoring, clarifying the scoring dimensions, scoring standards, and typical examples to ensure a consistent evaluation scale. In addition, each generated sample is independently scored by at least two experts, and the final score was the average of their scores. If the score difference between the two experts exceeds 1 point, a third expert conducts a review and judgment to ensure the stability and reliability of the evaluation results.

This study sets three evaluation indicators from both the model and user levels: Imagery Semantic Similarity of Images (Simg-acc): Reflects whether the generated images accurately reproduce the cultural imagery keywords. Narrative Resonance Score: Combines CLIP semantic similarity and user subjective ratings. Human Evaluation: Invites two representative user groups (youth and cultural scholars) to evaluate from three dimensions: “Cultural Expression,” “Aesthetic Identification,” and “Style Understanding,” using a 5-point scale. Discussion

The root of the generated image quality and style recognition advantage lies in the low-rank fine-tuning strategy of LoRA for Stable Diffusion. This approach retains the original model’s efficient expressive power and achieves precise style embedding through a small number of traditional cultural images. This “light regulation, deep transfer” method has stronger stability and cultural detail reproduction capabilities compared to GAN-type models, avoiding the common issues of symbol drift and style mixing in image generation. The improvement in narrative consistency and text-image semantic alignment is thanks to the CLIP model’s deep semantic deconstruction ability for cultural texts. CLIP provides a consensus reference system in the semantic space and forms a joint guidance path with style labels. This allows the generated images to perform flexible style interpretation while retaining the semantic core, achieving “double alignment” of symbolic imagery and emotional intent. Moreover, the fundamental reason for the excellent performance in cultural identity and user perception is that the model generates “visually appealing content” and “content that can be understood and resonated with”. By binding style labels with narrative structures, the system’s output has a clear cultural positioning and emotional suggestion, enhancing the activation of cultural schemas and emotional resonance in the user’s cognitive process. The advantages in generation diversity and cognitive engagement reflect the model’s ability to balance “expressive control” and “user stimulation.” That is, the model can control the style of content expression and provide an open space for understanding. This balance is especially critical for cultural AIGC systems. Further, the model’s advantages are explained from the perspectives of parameter settings and comparison paths: In image quality and style expression tasks, LoRA’s injection weight (0.75) and rank value (8) control the fusion depth between style features and the backbone model, ensuring high-fidelity style transfer while maintaining the original diffusion capability. In text-image consistency tasks, the CLIP text guidance weight is set to 0.6. This relatively high weight effectively enhances cross-modal collaborative expression in the semantic space, with a CLIP-Sim score of 0.793. Compared with comparative models: Stable Diffusion 2.1 lacks a cultural fine-tuning mechanism; StyleGAN-NADA has style transfer capability but weak text constraints; ControlNet relies on external reference images, resulting in limited generation quality. The proposed model achieves comprehensive performance improvement through multi-path evaluation (FID, LPIPS, BLEU, Style Match, etc.), leading in all four main indicators and demonstrating the integrated advantages of cross-modal style control and cultural image expression.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study constructs an AIGC model framework for the narrative generation of Chinese traditional culture based on deep learning style transfer and multimodal semantic control technology. By introducing Stable Diffusion, CLIP, and the LoRA mechanism, the model achieves precise integration and controllable generation between cultural text, images, and style. The model performs excellently in terms of image quality, semantic consistency, cultural reproduction, and user perception, providing a new paradigm for the deep integration of digital humanities and intelligent communication.

Future works and research limitations

This study still has certain limitations: the current model still has generation boundaries for highly abstract imagery styles, and the sample size for user subjective evaluation is relatively limited. Future work will combine more extensive population data and human-computer interaction mechanisms to optimize the path for constructing cultural identity. Future research can be expanded in the following two directions: First, further introduce multilingual cross-cultural diffusion mechanisms to enhance the model’s cultural adaptability in international contexts. Second, strengthen emotional modeling and virtual narrative character functions to achieve a more immersive cultural experience.

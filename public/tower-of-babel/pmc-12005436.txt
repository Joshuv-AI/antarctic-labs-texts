Dual function of the Leon/USP5 deubiquitinase in the ubiquitin-proteasome and autophagic pathways
Authors: Yuchieh Jay Lin, Guang-Chao Chen
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) and autophagy are highly conserved processes that maintain cellular health through the clearance of misfolded/aberrant proteins and damaged organelles. Ubiquitination is a crucial protein modification to regulate entry in these two pathways. However, the function of deubiquitinases (DUBs) in the UPS and autophagy remains largely unclear. The Leon/USP5 deubiquitinase is essential for maintaining ubiquitin homeostasis and proteasome function. In our recent study, we found that Leon/USP5 depletion resulted in the induction of autophagosome formation and an enhancement of the autophagic flux. Additionally, a genetic analysis in Drosophila revealed that Leon overexpression suppressed Atg1-induced cell death. We further showed that Leon/USP5 interacts with the autophagy initiator Atg1/ULK1, regulating its levels and thus modulating autophagosome formation. Protein homeostasis, which is regulated by ubiquitin-proteasome and autophagy-lysosomal systems, is critical to maintain cellular functions and survival. The ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) regulates a broad range of cellular activities by ensuring protein quality through the degradation of unwanted proteins. Autophagy, on the other hand, safeguards cells from stress-induced cytotoxicity by turning over protein aggregates and damaged organelles in a highly regulated sequence that includes autophagy initiation and phagophore nucleation, expansion and closure into a double membrane autophagosomes, which eventually fuse with lysosomes. Disturbances in either the UPS or autophagy can lead to various diseases, including neurodegenerative disorders and various cancers.

Ubiquitination is a dynamic and reversible post-translational modification (PTM) process that is regulated by E3 ligases and deubiquitinases (DUBs) and modulates protein stability, localization, and activity. While E3 ligase-mediated protein ubiquitination has been implicated in the regulation of autophagy, the roles of DUBs in this process remain elusive. Because of the high conservation of DUBs in mammals and Drosophila, we recently conducted an RNAi screen to identify fly DUBs that play a role in autophagy (1). Our results showed that knockdown of Leon, the Drosophila homolog of mammalian USP5 (ubiquitin-specific proteinase 5), promotes autophagosome formation and autophagic flux in larval fat body cells. Overexpression of wild-type (WT) but not the enzyme-dead (ED) form of Leon markedly reduced starvation-induced autophagy. Leon/USP5 has been shown to regulate the homeostasis of the monoubiquitin pool. Consistently, ablation of Leon/USP5 results in impaired proteasome function and the accumulation of ubiquitinated proteins. Proteasome dysfunction leads to activation of autophagy and increased expression of ATG genes and the selective autophagy receptor SQSTM1 (sequestosome 1)/p62. Notably, the loss of Leon also caused a dramatically increase in the levels of Ref(2)P, the Drosophila homolog of SQSTM1. Inhibition of autophagy by Atg7 (autophagy-related 7) knockdown further enhanced the formation of Ref(2)P puncta and polyubiquitinated protein aggregates in Leon-depleted larval fat body cells. Together, these results indicated that Leon is not only involved in the regulation of proteasome function, but also acts as a negative regulator of autophagy.

The Atg1 (autophagy-related 1) kinase plays a central role in the initiation of autophagy. In Drosophila, Atg1 triggers both the formation of autophagosomes and cell death. Intriguingly, overexpression of Leon-WT but not Leon-ED or LeonRNAi, suppressed Atg1-induced cell death and morphological defects in developing eyes and wings, suggesting that Leon genetically interacts with Atg1. We next checked whether Leon physically binds to Atg1. Co-immunoprecipitation and GST pull-down experiments revealed that the C-terminal region of Leon, which includes the peptidase domain, the ubiquitin-associated domains and the histidine box, interacts with Atg1 in an enzyme activity independent manner. While Leon is mainly distributed in the nucleus under fed conditions, immunofluorescence analysis revealed that Leon formed cytoplasmic puncta that partially co-localized with Atg1 in larval fat body cells under starvation conditions. Because mammalian USP5 is known to regulate protein stability, we examined whether Leon similarly regulate Atg1 levels. Indeed, when Leon was knocked down, Atg1 protein levels increased, whereas overexpression of Leon led to a reduction in Atg1 expression. These findings strongly suggested that Leon interacts with Atg1 and modulates its levels. We further investigated whether USP5 plays a conserved role in regulation of autophagy in mammalian cells. Depletion of USP5 in mammalian cells resulted in a marked increase in autophagosome formation and autophagic flux, mirroring the observations made in Drosophila. Furthermore, USP5 was found to interact with ULK1 (unc-51 like autophagy activating kinase 1) and to modulate the levels of ubiquitinated ULK1. Knocking down USP5 increased both ULK1 levels and ULK1 activity. Taken together, these results strongly suggest a conserved function of Leon/USP5 in negatively regulating the autophagic pathway by attenuating Atg1/ULK1 levels (Figure 1).

Previous studies have shown that ULK1 ubiquitination could be regulated by various E3 ligases, i.e., TRAF6 and NEDD4L, and DUBs, i.e., USP1, USP20, USP24 and AMSH. While we have demonstrated that the Leon/USP5-Atg1/ULK1 axis plays a crucial role in modulating autophagosome formation, the specific E3 ligases that function coordinately with Leon/USP5 to regulate Atg1/ULK1 remain to be identified. Notably, in cancer cells, USP5 is recognized for promoting tumor cell growth and cancer progression. It would be intriguing to explore whether the oncogenic potential of USP5 is linked to its role in antagonizing ULK1-mediated autophagy activation, which could provide insights into potential cancer treatments.

Radiative Energy and Mass Shifts of Quantum Cyclotron States
U. D. Jentschura

Abstract
We discuss relativistic and radiative corrections to the energies of quantum cyclotron states. In particular, it is shown analytically that the leading logarithmic radiative (self-energy) correction to the bound-state energy levels of quantum cyclotron states is state-independent, and must be interpreted as a magnetic-field-dependent correction to the electron’s mass in a Penning trap.

IV
Leading Self–Energy

In order to explore the rich structure
of the operator algebra governing
quantum cyclotron states,
let us investigate the leading
spin-independent term of the bound-state self energy.
We first remember the leading-logarithmic
term in the self-energy
of bound states in hydrogenlike systems
with charge number
Z
, according to Eqs. (4.316),
(4.320) and (4.322) of Ref.
JeAd2022book
.
In the nonrecoil limit of an infinite nuclear
mass, the leading-logarithmic energy shift

E_{L}
is given by

E_{L}=\frac{2\alpha}{3\pi}\left<\frac{\vec{p}}{m}\,(H_{S}-E_{S})\,\frac{\vec{p}}{m}\right>\,\ln[(Z\alpha)^{-2}\\
=\frac{4\alpha}{3\pi}\frac{(Z\alpha)^{4}m}{n^{3}}\delta_{l0}\,\ln[(Z\alpha)^{-2}]\,,

(15)

where
\vec{p}
is the momentum operator,

H_{S}
is the Schrödinger Hamiltonian,

E_{S}
is the bound-state energy,
l
is the
orbital angular momentum (in this article,
\ell

is reserved for the magnetron quantum number),
and
n_{S}
is the Schrödinger principal
quantum number (here in this article,

n
is reserved for the cyclotron quantum number).

For quantum cyclotron states, analogous
considerations

lead to the
result that the spin-independent leading-logarithmic
correction is proportional to the
matrix element

M=\left<\psi_{k\ell ns}|\vec{\pi}(H_{0}-E_{k\ell ns})\vec{\pi}|\psi_{k\ell ns}\right>\,,

(16)

where
H_{0}
is given in Eq. (
2
),

E_{0}
is given in Eq. (
II
),
and
\vec{\pi}=\vec{p}-(e/2)(\vec{B}\times\vec{r})

is the kinetic momentum in the magnetic trap
field.

In order to render our task manageable,
we shall work in the approximation

\omega_{z}\approx 0\,,\qquad\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}\approx\omega_{c}\,,\qquad\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}\approx 0\,,

(17)

in which limit the
magnetron shift becomes zero,
and
\ell
acts as a degeneracy index.
Furthermore, we shall neglect the
anomalous magnetic moment for simplicity,
and replace
\kappa\to 0
.
Under these approximations, we
can easily establish that

\frac{\vec{\pi}^{\,2}}{2m}\,\psi_{k\ell ns}\approx\omega_{c}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\psi_{k\ell ns}\,.

(18)

The unperturbed Hamiltonian becomes

H_{0}\approx\frac{\vec{\pi}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{e}{2m}\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}\,,

(19)

and the unperturbed energy is approximated as

E\approx\omega_{c}\,\left(n+\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,.

(20)

Let us evaluate the matrix element
M
,
by first showing that
M
can be written
in the form

M=X\,,\qquad X=\frac{1}{2m}\left<\vec{\pi}\left(\vec{\pi}^{2}\right)\vec{\pi}\right>-\frac{1}{2m}\left<\vec{\pi}^{2}\,\vec{\pi}^{2}\right>\,.

(21)

The transformations are nonobvious.
One first shows that

M=\left<\vec{\pi}(H_{0}-E_{k\ell ns})\vec{\pi}\right>\\
=\left<\vec{\pi}\left(\frac{\vec{\pi}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{e}{2m}\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}-E_{k\ell ns}\right)\vec{\pi}\right>\\
=\left<\vec{\pi}\,\frac{\vec{\pi}^{2}}{2m}\,\vec{\pi}+\left(\frac{\omega_{c}s}{2}-E_{k\ell ns}\right)\,\vec{\pi}^{2}\right>\\
=X+\left<\frac{\vec{\pi}^{2}\,\vec{\pi}^{2}}{2m}\right>+\left<\left(\frac{\omega_{c}s}{2}-E_{k\ell ns}\right)\,\vec{\pi}^{2}\right>\,.

(22)

With the help of the relation

\left<\vec{\pi}^{2}\,\vec{\pi}^{2}\right>=\left[2m\omega_{c}(n+\tfrac{1}{2})\right]^{2}\,,

(23)

one now shows that

\left<\frac{\vec{\pi}^{2}\,\vec{\pi}^{2}}{2m}\right>+\left<\left(\frac{\omega_{c}s}{2}-E_{k\ell ns}\right)\,\vec{\pi}^{2}\right>=\frac{\left[2m\omega_{c}(n+\tfrac{1}{2})\right]^{2}}{2m}\\
+\left[\omega_{c}\,\frac{s}{2}-\omega_{c}\,\left(n+\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]2m\omega_{c}\,\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)=0\,.

(24)

We are still not done. Namely,
M
can be written in terms
of Cartesian components, denoted with superscripts,
and with the help of the Einstein summation
convention, as

M=X=\frac{1}{2m}\left<\vec{\pi}\left(\vec{\pi}^{2}\right)\vec{\pi}\right>-\frac{1}{2m}\left<\vec{\pi}^{2}\,\vec{\pi}^{2}\right>\\
=\frac{1}{2m}\left(\left<\pi^{i}\,\pi^{j}\,\pi^{j}\,\pi^{i}\right>-\left<\pi^{i}\,\pi^{i}\,\pi^{j}\,\pi^{j}\right>\right)\\
=\frac{1}{2m}\left<\pi^{i}\,[\pi^{j}\,\pi^{j},\,\pi^{i}]\right>\,.

(25)

For the commutator,
one trivially has the relation

[\vec{\pi}^{\,2},\pi^{i}]=[\pi^{j}\,\pi^{j},\pi^{i}]=[\pi^{j},\pi^{i}]\,\pi^{j}+\pi^{j}\,[\pi^{j},\pi^{i}]
,
and we recall the
i
th Cartesian
component of the kinetic momentum as

\pi^{i}=p^{i}+m\omega_{c}(\hat{e}_{z}\times\vec{r})^{i}
.
One easily establishes the commutator relation

[\pi^{i},\pi^{j}]=\mathrm{i}e\,\epsilon^{ijk}B^{k}\,,

(26)

where the Levi–Cività tensor is
\epsilon^{ijk}
,
and infers that

[\vec{\pi}^{\,2},\pi^{i}]=2\mathrm{i}e\,\epsilon^{ikj}B^{k}\pi^{j}
.
So,

M=X=\frac{1}{2m}\left<\pi^{i}\,[\vec{\pi}^{\,2},\,\pi^{i}]\right>=\frac{1}{2m}\left<\pi^{i}\,2\mathrm{i}e\,\epsilon^{ikj}B^{k}\pi^{j}\right>\\
=\frac{1}{2m}\mathrm{i}e\,\epsilon^{ikj}B^{k}\left<[\pi^{i},\,\pi^{j}]\right>=\frac{e^{2}}{2m}\,\epsilon^{ijk}\,\epsilon^{ij\ell}\,B^{k}\,B^{\ell}\\
=\frac{e^{2}}{2m}\,2\delta^{k\ell}\,B^{k}\,B^{\ell}=\frac{e^{2}}{m}\,\vec{B}^{2}=m\,\omega_{c}^{2}\,.

(27)

The step from the first to the second line in the
above derivation is somewhat nontrivial,
but it become obvious after a careful consideration.

Here is a decisive difference between the
Coulombic and quantum-cyclotron bound states:

is nonvanishing only for

The constant shift

In order to conclude this brief
investigation, we summarize the
main findings: In Sec.

we have explored a few peculiarities,
and idiosyncrasies perhaps,
of the algebra of quantum cyclotron states.
The structure of the states has been given in
Eq. (

This structure has been further analyzed in
Ref.
Je2023mag1
, and generalized
for relativistic states in Ref.
JeMo2023mag2
.
It is crucial to observe that the
raising operators
a_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}^{\dagger}
,
a_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}^{\dagger}
,
and
a_{z}^{\dagger}
all operate on the
same coordinates of the wave function,
but still commute (as operators)
with each other

;
WiMoJe2022
.

Regarding the relativistic corrections
(Sec.
III
),
we review the most important results,
originally obtained in Refs.
BrGa1986
;
WiMoJe2022
,
Finally, we obtain a somewhat surprising
result, on the basis of a completely
analytic calculation, in Sec.
IV
:
Namely, quantum-cyclotron states
undergo a radiative shift,
which, in the leading logarithmic approximation,
is independent of the quantum numbers
of the quantum cyclotron state
(but not in higher orders, see Ref.
JeMo2023mag2
).
The leading effect is given in Eq. (
29
).
Indeed, this leading-logarithmic term
is state-independent
(constant) for all quantum cyclotron states.
Electrons bound in Penning traps
thus experience a shift of their
physical mass, proportional to

B^{8}\ln(|e|B/m)
, in view of the
dressing by the trap’s magnetic field.
The result is given in Eq. (
29
),
where the physical mass shift is

\delta m_{\rm phys}=E_{c}\propto\alpha_{c}^{4}\ln(\alpha_{c}^{-2})\propto B^{8}\ln(|e|B/m)
.
This mass shift acts as a dynamically induced
correction to the electron mass,
induced by the external conditions (the magnetic
field) of the Penning trap. In order to clarify its physical

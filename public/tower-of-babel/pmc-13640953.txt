Potent antitumor preclinical efficacy of B7-H3 chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells in H3G34-mutant, diffuse hemispheric glioma
Authors: David Akhavan, Monika Yadav, Siddharth Subham, John Jeppson, Omar Tinoco, Scott Chin, Nadine Santana-Magal, Jacqueline Babai, John Szarejko, Garrett Gibson, Kirill Sabitov, Daniel B Zamler, Gary Doug Myers, Brooke L Fridley, Stefan H Bossmann, Kalyani Pyaram, Viveka Nand Yadav
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground.Diffuse hemispheric glioma (DHG), H3 G34-mutant, is a highly aggressive and fatal pediatric brain tumor, with a median survival of only ~18 months and no effective targeted therapies. While chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell therapy has shown success in B-cell leukemia and promise in other pediatric central nervous system tumors, it has not yet been evaluated explicitly in preclinical or clinical models of DHG. B7-H3 (CD276), a tumor-associated antigen overexpressed in pediatric brain tumors, represents a promising target for immunotherapeutic intervention in DHG. Here, we present the first preclinical evaluation of B7-H3-directed CAR T cell therapy in DHG.Methods.We evaluated B7-H3 expression in specimens from DHG patients and patient-derived cell lines. Two B7-H3-targeted CAR constructs were generated and evaluated for antigen-specific activation, cytokine production, exhaustion, and cytotoxicity in vitro. Therapeutic efficacy was assessed in multiple orthotopic DHG xenograft models, followed by multiplex immunofluorescence and serum cytokine profiling.Results.B7-H3 was highly expressed in DHG tumors and cell lines, but at minimal levels in normal brain tissue. B7-H3 CAR T cells demonstrated potent, antigen-specific cytotoxicity against DHG cells, with negligible activity against B7-H3-knockout DHG cells. In vivo, the intratumoral administration of B7-H3 CAR T cells led to significant tumor regression and a substantial extension of survival in 3 DHG mouse models, with durable tumor eradication.Conclusion.This study provides preclinical evidence supporting B7-H3 CAR T cell therapy as a highly effective immunotherapeutic strategy for DHG. Our findings support B7-H3 as a compelling immunotherapeutic target for DHG, laying the groundwork for clinical translation.Keywords: B7-H3, CAR T cells, DHG, H3G34-mutant, immunotherapy, pediatric high-grade gliomas

Pediatric high-grade gliomas (pHGGs) are among the most lethal primary brain tumors and represent the leading cause of cancer-related mortality in children and adolescents.1 These tumors are biologically and clinically heterogeneous and are now stratified into molecular subgroups based on recurrent driver mutations in Histone H3.3 (H3.3A) or Histone H3.1 (H3C2).2–4 Approximately 50%-60% of pHGGs harbor mutually exclusive somatic point mutations, such as the presence of methionine at amino acid 27 instead of lysine (K27M) or the presence of arginine or valine at amino acid 34 instead of glycine (G34R/V) within the histone H3 tail.5–7 Pediatric high-grade gliomas with H3K27M mutation, characterized as diffuse midline glioma (DMG), typically arise in midline brain structures, such as the pons, thalamus, or spinal cord, and predominantly affect young children. In contrast, pHGGs with H3G34R/V mutation are classified as diffuse hemispheric glioma (DHG) and are usually located in the cerebral hemispheres, particularly the cortex and subcortical white matter, and occur more frequently in adolescents and young adults.2,6,8,9 These recurrent somatic mutations disrupt local chromatin structure and transcriptional regulation, promoting epigenetic reprogramming and oncogenesis. Despite aggressive multimodal therapy, including maximal surgical resection, radiation, and chemotherapy, DHGs remain uniformly fatal, with a median survival of 12-18 months and 5-year survival rates below 20%.8–10 Unlike their DMG counterparts, DHG remain understudied, and currently, no targeted or immunotherapeutic approaches are approved for these patients, highlighting a critical and unmet need for innovative therapeutic strategies.

Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-based immunotherapy has demonstrated remarkable success in treating childhood B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia,11–13 suggesting its potential as a promising therapeutic avenue to explore for the treatment of DHG. High-grade gliomas pose significant barriers to CAR T cell therapy due to their infiltrative nature, molecular heterogeneity, and localization within the immune-privileged central nervous system (CNS). Effective immunotherapy hinges on the identification of tumor-restricted antigens that enable selective tumor targeting while minimizing the risk of off-tumor neurotoxicity.14,15 Despite these challenges, several preclinical and early phase clinical studies have shown encouraging results using CAR T cells directed against glioma-associated antigens, including IL-13Rα2, EGFRvIII,16,17 and HER2 in glioblastoma (NCT02208362, NCT05168423, NCT03500991)18–20 as well as GD2 and B7-H3 in DMG (NCT04196413, NCT04185038).21–26 Notably, recent phase I trials targeting GD2 and B7-H3 have demonstrated safety and feasibility, with some patients showing extended survival, indicating promising therapeutic efficacy and marking important advancements in CAR T cell-based strategies for pediatric brain tumors. B7-H3 (CD276) has emerged as a highly promising immunotherapeutic target due to its broad and consistent overexpression across a diverse array of solid tumors, including prostate, lung, and breast cancers, as well as high-grade gliomas (pHGGs) such as DMG, medulloblastoma, atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumors, and ependymoma.27–30 Importantly, its expression remains limited in normal tissues, making it an attractive CAR T cell-based therapeutic target.25,27–29,31,32 Despite significant advancements in CAR T cell therapy for other malignancies, no preclinical or clinical trials have yet evaluated efficacy of CAR T cell therapy in H3G34R/V mutated DHG. Given the tumor-specific overexpression and immunomodulatory role of B7-H3, this antigen emerges as a promising target for CAR T cell-based immunotherapy in DHG. However, the expression of B7-H3 in H3G34R/V-mutated DHG and its therapeutic implications remain largely unexplored.

To address this gap, we conducted the first investigation of CAR T cell therapy targeting B7-H3 in DHG. First, we analyzed and found robust B7-H3 expression in tumor specimens and multiple patient-derived DHG cell lines but not in normal brain tissues. We developed a second-generation CAR construct utilizing a humanized single-chain variable fragment (scFv) derived from 8H9 and MGA271 targeting B7-H324,29,30 Functional assays revealed that the B7-H3 CAR T cells exhibited potent antigen-specific cytotoxicity against B7-H3 positive DHG cell lines in vitro. Furthermore, intracranial tumor (ICT) administration of B7-H3 CAR T cells in an NSG orthotopic mouse model significantly inhibited tumor growth and extended survival. These findings establish B7-H3 targeted CAR T cell therapy as a viable and innovative immunotherapeutic strategy for treating DHG with H3G34R/V mutations. Our study highlights the promising potential of B7-H3 CAR T cells as a therapeutic strategy, paving the way for their future human trials targeting this high-risk pediatric DHG population.

Methods

Cell Lines and Culture Conditions

Human DHG cell lines KNS42 (H3.3G34V) were obtained from Dr Carl Koschmann. SJ-HGGX42 (H3.3G34R) was kindly provided by Dr Suzanne Baker, St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital, USA. OPBG-GBM001(H3.3G34R) and HSJD-GBM002 (H3.3G34R) were kindly provided by Dr Chris Jones, from the Institute of Cancer Research, UK (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

In Silico Comparative RNA-Seq Analysis of B7-H3 Expression in DHG and Normal Brain Tissues

For B7-H3 RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis, the data for DHG patients were acquired from PedcBioPortal (PBTA-all), and for normal brain tissue samples from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project, version 8 (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Construction of B7-H3 CAR Vector

Lentivirus-based second-generation B7-H3 CAR (LV-B7-H3) vector was designed to encompass an scFv sequence against the human B7-H3 antigen derived from the 8H9 and MGA271 antibodies (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Lentivirus Production

LentiX-293T cells were plated in a 6-well plate at 1 million cells/well a day before transfection. When 70%-80% confluency was achieved, transfection was done using TransIT-LT1 Transfection Reagent (MirusBio; MIR 2300), LV-MAX Lentiviral Packaging Mix (Gibco; A43237), and respective plasmid constructs as per the manufacturer’s protocol(see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Human B7-H3 CAR T Cell Production

Human PBMCs (ZenBio; SER-PBMC-F) were thawed and washed with LymphoONE T-Cell Expansion Xeno-Free media (Takara; WK552S) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (T cell media). PBMCs were cultured at a concentration of 2 × 106 cells/well in 1 mL of T cell media augmented with 50 U/mL IL2 (Peprotech; 200-02), 0.5 ng/mL IL15 (Peprotech; 200-15), and 20 μL T Cell Trans-Act (Miltenyi Biotec; 130-128-758) in a 24-well culture plate (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

CRISPR/Cas9-Based B7-H3 Knockout in DHG Cell Lines

Lentivirus containing a CRISPR/Cas9-based vector containing gRNA CACCGACCCAGTGGTGGCACTGGT against human B7-H3 (VectorBuilder; VB231128-1328tgf) was transduced in DHG cell lines in media containing 8 μg/mL Polybrene (Sigma-Aldrich; TR-1003-G) (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Flow Cytometry

The surface expression of B7-H3 on DHG cell lines was detected with anti-B7-H3 antibody (Biolegend; 351011). All samples were collected using a BD Fortessa X-20 flow cytometer, and the data were analyzed with FlowJo software v10.9.0 (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Western Blot

DHG cell lines were harvested and lysed with RIPA lysing and extraction buffer (Gibco; 89900) containing 1X Halt Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail (Invitrogen; PI78440), and total protein was extracted (see Supplementary Material for details).

Cytotoxicity Assay

Cytotoxic efficacy of B7-H3 CAR-T cells was assessed using different detection systems. Tumor cells were CFSE-labeled and plated in a 96-well plate in triplicate and incubated for a minimum of 2 h at 37°C, 5% CO2. Effector cells (mock T, CD19 CAR-T, and B7-H3 CAR-T) were added at desired ratios in the same plate and incubated for 72 h (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Cytokine Secretion Analysis

KNS42, SJ-HGGX42, and KNS42 B7-H3KO cells were cocultured with effector cells (mock T cells or B7-H3 CAR T cells) at an E:T of 1:1 in triplicate wells of a 96-well plate for 24 h. Then, the plate was centrifuged briefly to let the cells settle down. Culture media was collected and aliquoted in a fresh 96-well plate and frozen at −80°C until used for analysis (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Animal Studies

All the mouse experiments were performed in accordance with the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the University of Kansas Medical Center (KUMC). Male and female NSG (NOD-scid IL2Rgammanull) mice aged 8-12 weeks were purchased from the Jackson Laboratory. Tumor cells were intracranially transplanted by drilling a burr hole on the skull at 2.5 mm posterior, then 2.0 mm mediolateral to the bregma; 1 × 106 KNS42 and 1.5 × 106 SJ-HGGX42 cells expressing luciferase were resuspended in 4 μL of phosphate buffered saline (PBS) and were injected in 1 μL increments at 3.5 mm, 2.5 mm, and 2 μL in 1.5 mm from the depth of burr hole (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Patient Tumor Tissue Specimens and Immunohistochemical Analysis

Brain tumor tissue sections of 2 DHG patient samples were procured from the Biospecimen Repository Core Facility of the KUMC. Sequencing of tumor samples confirmed the presence of the H3.3G34R mutation in the H3F3A gene in both DHG patients. Dr Nelli S. Lakis, MD (Neuropathologist), confirmed the H&E-stained sections for the malignant and matched nonmalignant tissue from the same patients (normal brain tissue), specifically in the supratentorial region of the brain (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

IHC Staining of the Mouse Brains

Mice were euthanized at experimental end-time points and were perfused with PBS, then with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA). Brain tissues were coronally sliced into 2, placed cut sections facing downward in the cassettes, and processed for paraffin embedding (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

IHC Quantitation

For quantitation of the area of B7-H3 and H3G34R expression, and the number of Ki67 positive cells, 4-5 sections from a 20× image of the respective mouse brains were captured and analyzed using ImageJ software.

Secretome Analysis

Multiplex analysis was performed on the Isoplexis CodePlex Secretome platform (Bruker Cellular Analysis) using serum collected from 4 CD19 or B7-H3 CAR-treated mice on the day of euthanasia (n = 2, per group) (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Multiplex Immunofluorescence and Workflow for Multiplex Image Analysis

The imaging was performed using the Akoya PhenoCycler-Fusion platform following standard histological preparation, antigen retrieval, and antibody panel staining33–35 (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Statistical Analysis

All the experimental data were analyzed for statistics using GraphPad Prism software (v10). Data are represented as mean ± SEM. RNA-seq for B7-H3 expression in DHG patient samples was analyzed using Mann-Whitney U tests, nonparametric (see Supplementary Material S1 for details).

Results

B7-H3 Is Highly Expressed in DHG Patient Tumor Specimens and Patient-Derived Cell Lines

We explored the potential of B7-H3 to be utilized as the target for the CAR T cell-based immunotherapy in DHG. First, we assessed the expression of the B7-H3 antigen in DHG tumor tissues from patients. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) of brain tumor tissue from 2 DHG patients revealed high expression of B7-H3 (Figure 1A). In contrast, the matched nontumor brain tissues had almost undetectable expression of B7-H3. Importantly, IHC analysis confirmed the presence of the DHG-specific H3G34R mutation in both the patient tumor tissues, along with elevated Ki-67 expression (Figure S1). Further, in silico analysis of bulk RNA-seq from 31 H3G34R/V DHG patient (PBTA-cohort) tumors revealed significant overexpression of B7-H3 expression compared to the normal brain (GTEXV8 dataset, n = 2655, ****P < .0001) (Figure 1B). Next, we also confirmed B7-H3 expression in the 4 DHG patient-derived cell lines with H3G34V mutation (KNS42), and H3G34R mutation (SJ-HGGX42, OPBG-GBM001, HSJD-GBM002) using immunoblot staining and flow cytometry. Western blot analysis revealed high levels of B7-H3 expression (Figure 1C) and flow cytometry confirmed the high surface expression of B7-H3 in all the patient-derived cell lines (Figure 1D). These results indicate B7-H3’s tumor-specific expression in DHG, making it a potential target for developing novel CAR T cell-based therapeutic strategies.

Generation of B7-H3 Targeted CAR-T Cells

We designed a second-generation B7-H3 CAR construct using the B7-H3 scFv sequence derived from monoclonal antibodies 8H929,30 and MGA271.24,25 To generate B7-H3-directed CAR T cells, a humanized anti-B7-H3 (scFv), IgG4 hinge with EQ mutation, CD4 transmembrane domain,36,37 4-1BB costimulatory domain, and CD3ζ signaling domain (Figure 2A), was cloned into a lentiviral vector.37 The use of a 4-1BB costimulatory domain supports sustained CAR T cell persistence and reduced exhaustion, as previously demonstrated to promote memory formation and oxidative metabolism compared to CD28-based designs.38–41 This is particularly important for achieving durable responses in aggressive solid tumors such as DHG. Further, the selection of the IgG4 hinge/spacer was guided by comparative optimization studies that demonstrated enhanced antitumor activity and reduced exhaustion relative to CD8-based constructs (Figure S2). This highlights the importance of CAR structural design in balancing efficacy and T cell fitness. Human CD3+ T cells were activated with CD3/CD28 and then transduced with lentivirus containing the B7-H3 CAR construct, referred to as LV-B7-H3 CAR-T. The transduced T cells were checked for CAR expression by flow cytometry using antihuman IgG (Fcg fragment specific) after gating on the lymphocyte population, single and live cells (Figure 2B). We achieved ~45% B7-H3-positive CAR expression (Figure 2C). We next evaluated the basal exhaustion status of transduced T cells prior to antigen encounter. Surface expression of exhaustion markers 2B4, PD-1, Tim-3, and Lag-3 in B7-H3 CAR T cells was comparable to mock-transduced controls, indicating that CAR transduction alone did not induce premature T cell exhaustion (Figure 2D).

While prior preclinical and phase I/II clinical studies in diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG) and pHGG models have predominantly utilized MG271-based B7-H3 CAR T cells,24,25 we sought to determine whether the choice of antigen-binding domain influences therapeutic efficacy in our DHG model. To address this, we evaluated the cytotoxicity activity of an alternative 8H9-based B7-H3 CAR in parallel with the established MGA271 CAR in a DHG model (Figure S3A). Our data demonstrate that both constructs confer comparable cytotoxic activity, indicating that the 8H9 binder is equally effective (Figure S3B). The 8H9-based CAR exhibited a less exhausted phenotype than the MGA271-based CAR following antigen stimulation (Figure S3C). Given prior validation of MG271-based CAR T cells in DIPG models, our study advances the field by prioritizing the 8H9-based construct for all in vitro and in vivo experiments, thereby expanding the translational applicability of B7-H3-targeted therapies in the DHG model.

Following identification of 8H9 as the preferred antigen-binding domain based on its comparable antitumor activity and lower exhaustion profile relative to MGA271, we evaluated an alternative 8H9-based CAR configuration as a subsequent optimization strategy. This retrovirus-based second-generation B7-H3 CAR construct (RV-B7-H3 CAR T cells) incorporated the same 8H9 scFv but utilized a distinct backbone comprising a CD8α transmembrane region, a CD28 costimulatory domain, and a CD3α signaling domain (Figure S4A).30 Following CD3/CD28 stimulation, T cells exhibited activation marker expression (CD69, CD25, HLA-DR) and were successfully transduced with the RV-B7-H3 CAR construct, achieving ~60% surface CAR expression (Figure S4B and C). Proliferation assays on days 0, 4, and 8 showed no significant difference between CAR T and mock T cells, indicating that CAR expression did not impair T cell expansion (Figure S4D). Although both the 8H9 CAR constructs yielded similar killing efficacy (Figure S4E), CAR with CD4 transmembrane domain, 4-1BB costimulatory domain exhibited less exhaustion (Figure S4F). Collectively, these in vitro optimization studies established that CAR architecture, including the choice of scFv, transmembrane region, and costimulatory domain, substantially influences T cell fitness. Among the 3 B7-H3 CAR constructs evaluated, the 8H9-based CAR incorporating the CD4 transmembrane domain, 4-1BB costimulatory domain, and CD3ζ signaling domain exhibited the most favorable balance of cytotoxicity and reduced exhaustion. Therefore, this optimized construct was selected for all subsequent in vitro and in vivo studies in DHG model.

B7-H3 CAR T Cells Demonstrate Effective In Vitro Killing Against DHG Cell Lines

We investigated the in vitro cytotoxic effect of LV-B7-H3 CAR-T cells against 2 DHG cell lines: KNS42 (H3G34V-mutated)-adherent cells and H3G34R-mutated (SJ-HGGX42) neurosphere cells. We employed different cytotoxicity assessment methods, that is, fluorescence-based assay using Celigo and Incucyte, and luminescence-based assay using a spectrophotometer. KNS42 and SJ-HGGX42 cells were cocultured with mock and B7-H3 CAR T cells at the indicated effector-to-target (B7-H3 CAR T: tumor cells) ratios for 72 h. In vitro killing revealed that B7-H3 CAR T cells had robust cytotoxicity, achieving near-complete eradication of tumor cells compared to mock T cells (Figure 3A–E). KNS42 cells (Figure 3A and B) demonstrated greater sensitivity to CAR T cell-mediated killing than SJ-HGGX42 (Figure 3D and E), supporting that CAR T cell efficacy correlates with B7-H3 expression on target tumor cells. Live-cell imaging revealed a marked reduction in viable KNS42 tumor cells following treatment with B7-H3 CAR T cells compared to mock T cells. At increasing E:T ratios (2:1 and 4:1), a substantial decrease in GFP-positive tumor cells was observed, consistent with enhanced tumor clearance. In contrast, mock T cells showed minimal cytotoxic effect, with abundant GFP-positive cells persisting across all conditions (Figure 3C).

We comprehensively evaluated the kinetics of B7-H3 CAR T cell-mediated cytotoxicity against KNS42 tumor cells across multiple effector-to-target (E:T) ratios (1:8, 1:4, 1:2, 1:1, 2:1, and 4:1) taking readings at every 8-h interval. A time and dose-dependent increase in tumor cell killing was observed compared to mock and CD19 CAR T cells at the 4:1 ratio. Higher E:T ratios (4:1) demonstrated rapid and near 80% tumor clearance beginning at 48-72 h postcoculture, whereas lower E:T ratios (1:8 and 1:4) showed slower and less efficient killing, with partial tumor cell killing even at 72 h (Figure S5A and B). These results indicate that both time and effector cell concentration critically influence the efficacy of B7-H3 CAR T cell-mediated cytotoxicity. Additionally, to further investigate if B7-H3 CAR T cell cytotoxicity is dependent on antigen density on the target cells, we compared the cytotoxicity susceptibility of 2 neurosphere-forming glioma cell lines with differential B7-H3 expression: SJ-HGGX42 (low B7-H3) and OPBG-GBM001 (high B7-H3) (Figure 1C and D). At similar effector-to-target (E:T) ratios, we observed significantly greater cytotoxic activity of B7-H3 CAR T cells against OPBG-GBM001 relative to SJ-HGGX42 (Figure S6). Our data provide strong functional evidence that CAR T cell efficacy scales with B7-H3 expression on tumor cells. Similarly, 8H9-based retrovirus-B7-H3 CAR T cells also demonstrated significant killing potential against 2 DHG cell lines (KNS42 and SJ-HGGX42) (Figure S7A and B), further demonstrating the potential of B7-H3 CAR T cells as an effective strategy.

B7-H3 CAR T Cells Activation, Exhaustion, and Cytokine Profiles Following Co-culture With DHG Cell Lines (KNS42 and SJ-HGGX42)

We evaluated CAR T cell activation and effector cytokine production following 24-h coculture of B7-H3 CAR T cells with DHG cell lines KNS42 and SJ-HGGX42 using a bead-based 13-plex cytokine detection kit employing a flow cytometer. B7-H3 CAR T cells secreted significantly higher levels of IL-2, IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-γ, Granzyme A, Granzyme B, Perforin, IL-10, IL-17A, IL-4, sFas, sFasL, and Granulysin compared to mock T cells (Figure 3F, Figure S8). These findings confirm that B7-H3 CAR T cells undergo robust antigen-specific activation and mount a strong effector cytokine response upon engagement with DHG tumor targets. We also checked the status of T cell exhaustion after exposure of mock and CAR T cells to the target antigen in KNS42 and SJ-HGGX42 cell lines. After antigen stimulation, B7-H3 CAR T cells displayed significantly higher surface expression of PD1, TIM3, and LAG3 in both DHG models than mock T cells (Figure 3G), consistent with activation-induced exhaustion. These results demonstrate that B7-H3 CAR T cells are capable of robust, dose-dependent cytotoxicity and effector cytokine release upon target recognition, indicative of potent, but sustained T cell activation in the DHG microenvironment.

B7-H3 CAR T Cell Did Not Affect the Viability of B7-H3 Negative Cells

To directly assess the antigen specificity of B7-H3 CAR T cell effector functions, we performed CRISPR-Cas9-based knockout (KO) of B7-H3 antigen in KNS42 and SJ-HGGX42 DHG cell lines and generated respective B7-H3KO cell lines. Flow cytometry and immunoblotting confirmed complete loss of B7-H3 surface and total protein expression in B7-H3KO cells (Figure 4A and B, Figure S9A–C). We observed a considerable loss of CAR T cells’ cytolytic activity against KNS42 B7-H3KO and SJ-HGGX42 B7-H3KO cells (Figure 4C, Figure S9D and E). B7-H3 CAR T cells showed no significant cytotoxicity against B7-H3KO KNS42 cells across E:T ratios up to 4:1, with tumor cell viability comparable to mock and CD19 CAR T cell controls, confirming target-specific activity (Figure 4D). Fluorescence imaging further confirmed the lack of B7-H3 CAR-mediated tumor killing in B7-H3KO cells, even at high E:T ratios (Figure 4E). Comparison of cytokine release after antigen stimulation revealed significant reduction by B7-H3KO cells compared to control cells (Figure 4F). Time-course analysis of B7-H3 CAR T cell cytotoxicity revealed that B7-H3 CAR T cells exhibited negligible cytotoxic activity against KNS42 B7-H3KO cells across all tested E:T ratios (1:8, 1:4, 1:2, 1:1, 2:1, and 4:1) over 72 h unlike parent KNS42 cells. Tumor cell viability remained unchanged throughout the assay, confirming the antigen-specific nature of B7-H3 CAR T cell-mediated killing and the requirement for B7-H3 expression on target cells (Figure S10). Further, to assess the dependence of CAR T cell activity on antigen availability/density, we performed a density-dependent cytotoxicity assay using mixed populations of B7-H3-positive and B7-H3KO KNS42 cell lines. B7-H3 CAR T cells demonstrated robust, antigen-dependent cytotoxicity that progressively decreased with declining proportions of B7-H3-positive targets and was completely abolished under antigen-negative conditions, whereas mock T cells showed no cytotoxicity across all conditions (Figure S11A). Lastly, we evaluated B7-H3 CAR T cell killing of B7-H3 negative TNBC cell line MDA-MB-453 and did not see any cytotoxicity (Figure S11B). Together, these results establish that B7-H3 CAR-T cell activation, cytotoxicity, and effector cytokine responses are strictly dependent on tumor-intrinsic B7-H3 expression, underscoring the high specificity and precision of this therapeutic strategy.

B7-H3 CAR T Cells Eradicate Tumor Burden and Prolong Survival in an Orthotopic DHG Mouse Model With H3G34R Mutation

After B7-H3 CAR T cells’ efficient and specific cytotoxic effect against B7-H3 positive DHG cell lines in vitro, we evaluated their antitumor efficacy in vivo. First, we established an orthotopic xenograft model using SJ-HGGX42 cells implanted intracranially in the cortex of NSG mice. Following confirmation of tumor engraftment via bioluminescence imaging (BLI), mice were randomized into B7-H3 CAR or CD19 CAR treatment arms and administered CAR T cell therapy intratumorally on day 8 postimplantation (Figure 5A). Longitudinal BLI revealed a striking and sustained reduction in tumor burden of B7-H3 CAR T cell-treated mice, whereas CD19 CAR-treated controls exhibited progressive tumor growth (Figure 5B and C). Quantitative analysis of total flux confirmed a significant decline in tumor signal in the B7-H3 CAR cohort, beginning shortly after treatment and sustaining through 300 days (Figure 5C and D). Survival analysis demonstrated a significant survival advantage in B7-H3 CAR T cell-treated mice compared to CD19 CAR controls. The median survival for CD19 CAR-treated mice was 136 days, whereas a median survival was not reached in the B7-H3 CAR T cell-treated cohort (n = 9), with 4 mice remaining alive beyond 300 days at the time of study termination (P = .0004; Figure 5D). H&E staining of brains revealed the absence of tumor in B7-H3 CAR T cell-treated mice, while the control mice had highly dense and diffuse tumoral growth (Figure 5E, Figure S12). Immunohistochemistry confirmed the absence of B7-H3 and H3G34R staining in the B7-H3 CAR T celll-treated group, accompanied by near-complete ablation of the proliferation marker Ki-67 (Figure 5E and F, Figure S12). To evaluate potential neurotoxicity and treatment-associated inflammation, we performed IHC for Iba1 on brain sections from CD19 CAR and B7-H3 CAR-treated mice. Quantitative analysis revealed a significant reduction in Iba1+ cells in B7-H3 CAR-treated animals (Figure S13), consistent with decreased microglial activation following effective tumor clearance. Additionally, we utilized multiplex immunofluorescence imaging in CD19 CAR and B7-H3 CAR-treated mice brains to analyze tumor, microglial, and T-cell markers in SJ-HGGX42 tumors following CAR-T treatment. CD19 CAR-T-treated mice showed invasive tumor regions (Vimentin+), proliferating tumor cells (Ki67+), microglia (Iba1+), whereas B7-H3 CAR-T-treated mice brain displayed absence of tumor related phenotype with minimal expression of Iba1+, Vimentin+ and Ki67+ (Figure 5G and H, Figure S14). Analysis of T-cell markers (CD3e and CD8) in CD19- and B7-H3 CAR T cell-treated mice revealed an absence of detectable T cells at the time point analyzed, suggesting either limited persistence or clearance of infused CAR T cells.

To further strengthen the translational relevance and robustness of our findings, we performed a repeat in vivo experiment using a more aggressive SJ-HGGX42 model, which has a median survival of 50 days. Additionally, CAR T cells are administered at a later time point (3 weeks postimplantation), when tumors are more established. This design mimics advanced disease with a higher tumor burden, which is clinically more challenging to treat. Importantly, even under these conditions, B7-H3 CAR T cell therapy resulted in tumor clearance in the majority of treated mice, with long-term survival observed compared to CD19 CAR T control mice at the end of the study (Figure S15A–C). To evaluate potential systemic toxicity and cytokine release associated with CAR T cell therapy, we performed multiplex serum cytokine profiling at the end of the experiment, which revealed elevations of IL-6 and IL-8, consistent with CAR T cell activation, without significant increases in IFN-γ, TNF-α, or IL-10. These findings suggest an absence of severe cytokine release or systemic toxicity (Figure S15D). Notably, serum was collected after tumor clearance, which may account for the lack of significant elevation of pro-inflammatory cytokines.

Collectively, these results demonstrate that the antitumor efficacy of B7-H3 CAR T cells is both robust, reproducible, and remains effective even in aggressive and late-stage tumor settings. These results establish that B7-H3 CAR T cells mediate potent and selective tumor regression in vivo and significantly extend survival in a clinically relevant model of H3G34R-mutated diffuse hemispheric glioma.

Potent AntiTumor Activity of B7-H3 CAR T Cells in an In Vivo Orthotopic H3G34V Mutated DHG Mouse Model

To further validate the antitumor efficacy of B7-H3 CAR T cells, we employed an orthotopic model using the H3G34V-mutant DHG cell line KNS42. NSG mice were intracranially injected with 1 × 106 million KNS42 cells and monitored by BLI to confirm tumor establishment before treatment allocation (Figure 6A). Mice were randomized based on tumor burden and received intratumoral injections of either B7-H3 CAR or CD19 CAR T cells. Longitudinal BLI revealed rapid tumor regression in B7-H3 CAR T treated mice across both models, whereas CD19 CAR-treated controls exhibited continued tumor progression (Figure 6B and C). Quantitative analysis demonstrated a profound and sustained reduction in tumor burden in the B7-H3 CAR T treated groups, with durable responses persisting for over 290 days in KNS42 models. Survival analysis confirmed a significant extension of survival in B7-H3 CAR T cell-treated mice compared to controls (P = .0095 for KNS42; Figure 6D). Histological examination revealed complete tumor clearance in B7-H3 CAR T cell-treated mice by H&E staining, in contrast to dense, infiltrative tumors in controls (Figure 6E, Figure S16). Immunohistochemistry showed a marked absence of B7-H3 expression and depletion of Ki-67+ proliferative cells in B7-H3 CAR T cell-treated mice brains (Figure 6E and , F Figure S16). Further, body weight monitoring of mice treated with B7-H3 CAR T cells and CD19 CAR T cells showed no significant differences over time, indicating that B7-H3 CAR T cell therapy was well tolerated and did not induce systemic toxicity (Figure S17). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that B7-H3 CAR T cells mediate potent and durable tumor eradication, significantly prolonging survival across distinct genetic subtypes of DHG in orthotopic preclinical models.

Discussion

CAR T cell therapy has shown remarkable efficacy in hematologic malignancies; however, translating this approach to solid tumors, particularly CNS malignancies, remains challenging due to barriers such as antigen heterogeneity, limited trafficking, and immune suppression within the tumor microenvironment. Pediatric high-grade gliomas, including DHG, exhibit pronounced molecular and phenotypic heterogeneity, aggressive infiltration into eloquent brain regions, and reside within the immunoprivileged CNS, compounding the difficulty of achieving effective and durable immune-mediated tumor clearance. Moreover, the restrictive nature of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) poses an additional obstacle for systemically administered cellular therapies.

In this context, our study provides the first preclinical evidence supporting the therapeutic efficacy of B7-H3 targeted CAR T cell therapy in DHGs harboring H3G34R/V mutations. We demonstrate that B7-H3-targeted CAR T cells mediate potent and selective cytotoxicity against DHG cells, both in vitro and in vivo. The 2 orthotopic models used in this study, derived from H3G34R-(SJ-HGGX42) and H3G34V-(KNS42) mutated cell lines, represent distinct yet clinically relevant subtypes of DHG. Intratumoral delivery of B7-H3 CAR T cells in both models resulted in robust tumor regression, marked reductions in proliferation markers (Ki67), and significantly prolonged survival compared to CD19 CAR T treated controls (Figures 5 and 6). These outcomes suggest that direct intracerebral administration of CAR T cells may circumvent BBB-associated limitations and achieve therapeutic levels at the tumor site, offering a viable strategy for CNS tumor immunotherapy. Although a small number of tumor-free mice in the B7-H3 CAR T-cell group died (Figure 6) during long-term follow-up, no residual tumor was detected histologically, suggesting that the deaths were likely attributable to treatment-or host-related complications inherent to the NSG xenograft model rather than to tumor progression. Importantly, our study extends beyond expression profiling to demonstrate functional in vitro and in vivo efficacy of B7-H3 CAR T cells in DHG. These findings establish a proof-of-concept and address a critical gap in immunotherapeutic strategies for this high-risk DHG model.

Our data show that B7-H3 is robustly and selectively expressed in DHG patient tumor tissues and across multiple H3G34R/V-mutated DHG cell lines, whereas it is minimally expressed in normal brain tissues. Identifying tumor-associated antigens that are highly enriched and uniformly expressed is critical for achieving effective and comprehensive tumor eradication.28,29,42 Consistent and high-level B7-H3 expression is crucial for the effectiveness of CAR T cell therapy, as intratumoral antigen heterogeneity remains a significant barrier to the successful treatment of brain tumors.27,43 Importantly, our findings indicate that CAR T cell efficacy is primarily driven by antigen density rather than tumor-intrinsic differences. Under uniform conditions, cytotoxicity correlated with B7-H3 expression levels, consistent with threshold-dependent CAR T activation, highlighting antigen heterogeneity as a key determinant of therapeutic response in DHG (Figure 3, Figure S6). Several studies have reported high expression of B7-H3 in pediatric brain tumors.21,26,29,44 Haydar et al conducted a comprehensive analysis of 5 CAR targets in tumors from 49 pediatric patient-derived orthotopic xenografts (PDOXs), identifying B7-H3 as the most highly expressed antigen, followed by GD2, IL-13Rα2, HER2, and EphA2.28 Notably, in the SJ-HGGX42 (PDOX) model of DHG, more than 96% of tumor cells coexpressed both B7-H3 and GD2, highlighting their strong potential as dual immunotherapeutic targets.28

The selective cytotoxicity of B7-H3 CAR T cells may be attributed to a threshold-dependent activation mechanism, wherein the relatively low antigen density on normal tissues remains insufficient to trigger CAR T cell activation, thereby preserving healthy cells while targeting tumor cells with high B7-H3 expression.24,25,27–29 Our data showed minimal expression of B7-H3 in nonmalignant brain tissue from DHG patients (Figure 1A). Furthermore, our experiments using B7-H3KO cell lines confirmed the antigen specificity of the therapeutic effect, as B7-H3 CAR T cells failed to elicit cytotoxicity or cytokine production in the absence of B7-H3 expression (Figure 4, Figure S11).This is a clinically relevant observation that underscores the critical importance of selecting tumor-restricted targets to minimize off-tumor effects and enhance the safety profile of CAR T cell therapies in the CNS. Most of the B7-H3 targeted CARs have used an scFv sequence derived from either MGA271 or 8H9 monoclonal antibodies.44,45 Notably, we performed a direct comparison of 2 clinically relevant B7-H3 CAR constructs (8H9 and MGA271), demonstrating comparable efficacy with distinct functional profiles (Figure S3). Given the clinical use of MGA271 in DIPG, these findings provide translational insight and support the selection of 8H9 for subsequent in vitro and in vivo studies. The 8H9 monoclonal antibody has been extensively studied and safely administered in pediatric patients for over a decade.45 Delivered directly into the CNS as a radio-conjugate, 8H9 has shown clinical promise in treating B7-H3-expressing malignancies.45 Its specificity and safety profile underscore its translational potential in patients with DHG. In parallel, MGA271 exhibits minimal binding to normal tissues and has demonstrated a favorable safety profile in early phase clinical trials.46 Indeed, recently, Vitanza et al showed a favorable safety profile and clinical activity of B7-H3 CAR T cells in early phase trials, in DIPG and DMG patients.24,25

B7-H3 CAR T cells were well tolerated, as we did not observe any evidence of toxicity after ICT delivery of B7-H3 CAR T cells in both DHG in vivo models (Figure S17). Our findings support the therapeutic advantages of local-regional intracranial delivery of B7-H3 CAR T cells, a strategy that bypasses the BBB and potentially minimizes systemic toxicity. Thus, preclinical studies suggest superior efficacy of locoregional delivery for B7-H3 CAR T cells and have demonstrated tumor regression in some models.47,48 Recent pediatric trials, including incorporated locoregional delivery, showed enhanced tumor penetration and reduced risks of cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity, especially in DMG patients with preexisting neurological deficits.22,24 While locoregional delivery may reduce the incidence of systemic cytokine release syndrome, it does not eliminate the risk of neurotoxicity. Tumor inflammation-associated neurotoxicity and localized inflammatory effects remain important considerations following intracranial CAR T cell administration. In our models, comprehensive toxicity monitoring revealed no behavioral or neurological deficits following treatment, and histologic analysis demonstrated reduced microglial activation, consistent with resolution of tumor-associated inflammation rather than treatment-induced neurotoxicity (Figure 5G and H, Figure S14). In addition, serum cytokine profiling showed no evidence of severe systemic inflammatory responses (Figure S15D), supporting a favorable safety profile of B7-H3 CAR T therapy with locoregional delivery.19,20,28,49 The optimal route of CAR T cell delivery may depend on both anatomical tumor location and spatial distribution of the target antigen. Tumors located in surgically accessible cortical regions may be well suited for locoregional administration, whereas deep-seated or multifocal lesions may require alternative strategies. Importantly, antigen heterogeneity across primary and disseminated tumor sites can influence CAR T engagement and distribution, thereby impacting the effectiveness of localized vs systemic delivery approaches. Although we did not directly compare systemic vs intracranial administration, prior preclinical and clinical studies have demonstrated superior tumor control and CNS persistence with locoregional CAR T delivery in brain tumors.19,28 Given the cortical accessibility of DHG and emerging clinical evidence supporting intracranial approaches, we prioritized locoregional administration to enhance translational relevance. Consistent with prior preclinical and clinical studies,19,20,28,49 our findings support the therapeutic advantages of local-regional intracranial delivery of B7-H3 CAR T cells by enabling direct tumor targeting while bypassing the BBB. This approach achieved durable tumor clearance with minimal toxicity, likely driven by enhanced local effector activity and limited systemic exposure. Collaborative efforts are needed to optimize delivery routes and dosing regimens for B7-H3 targeted CAR T cell immunotherapies in pediatric CNS tumors.

Antigen heterogeneity in brain tumors like DIPG and DHG contributes to CAR T cell resistance and tumor escape. Importantly, antigen escape was not observed in our DHG model, as durable tumor clearance occurred without recurrence, and no residual B7-H3-positive tumor was detected. Although transient antigen modulation was seen in vitro (Figure S18B). Notably, in the few nonresponding animals, mice 10 and 15 showed persistent B7-H3 expression, indicating that resistance was unlikely due to antigen loss but may instead reflect technical variability in intratumoral CAR T cell delivery (Figure S18A). To counter the antigen heterogeneity, multiplexed CAR T strategies targeting EGFR, HER2, and IL13Rα2 are being developed. Previous studies have shown high expression of B7-H3 and GD2 that dual or bispecific “OR”-gated CARs enhance antigen coverage but may increase off-tumor toxicity risks, whereas “AND”-gated designs, such as SynNotch, improve specificity and enhance CAR T cell persistence and trafficking.50

While B7-H3 CAR T cells demonstrated strong activation and cytokine production upon target engagement, they also exhibited upregulation of exhaustion markers (PD-1,TIM-3, LAG-3), suggesting that repetitive antigen stimulation within the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment may lead to functional T cell exhaustion. Future studies should explore combination strategies to enhance CAR T cell persistence and function, such as checkpoint blockade, cytokine support, or targeting the immunosuppressive myeloid-rich microenvironment (eg CD14+/CD163+ macrophages) may further improve efficacy and reduce relapse.

Our findings align with and extend recent clinical efforts investigating GD2 and B7-H3-directed therapies in pediatric brain tumors, such as DIPG, where early phase trials have demonstrated feasibility and preliminary efficacy. However, DHGs remain underrepresented in preclinical and clinical studies, and our work addresses this critical gap. By demonstrating B7-H3 expression in DHG and the efficacy of B7-H3 CAR T cells, we provide a strong preclinical rationale for extending CAR T cell-based immunotherapies to this high-risk patient population.

In conclusion, this study establishes B7-H3 as a compelling immunotherapeutic target in DHG and demonstrates that B7-H3 CAR T cells elicit potent, antigen-specific antitumor responses in both in vitro and in vivo models. These results lay the foundation for clinical translation of B7-H3-directed CAR T cell therapies in H3G34R/V-mutated DHGs and support the development of early phase clinical trials to evaluate their safety and efficacy in pediatric patients with these otherwise intractable tumors.

Importance of the Study

This study represents an advancement in the treatment of diffuse hemispheric gliomas (DHGs) with H3G34R/V mutations, an aggressive pediatric brain tumor with no curative therapies. For the first time, chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy was evaluated against H3G34R/V DHG tumors. Key findings include significant B7-H3 expression on DHG tumor models and the development of anti-B7-H3-specific CAR T cells, which demonstrated antigen-specific cytokine release, immune activation, and cytotoxicity in vitro. In multiple orthotopic mouse models of H3G34R/V-mutated DHG, these CAR T cells reduced tumor growth and significantly improved mouse survival. This study highlights the potential of B7-H3 as a promising therapeutic target. It provides a strong preclinical rationale for initiating the first anti-B7-H3 CAR T cell-mediated phase 1 clinical trial for DHG patients. It sets the stage for translating these preclinical improvements into clinical applications, offering hope for a previously untreatable disease.

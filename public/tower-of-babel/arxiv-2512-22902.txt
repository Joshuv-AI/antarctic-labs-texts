3.
A simpler proof of Badesa’s count of Diophantine pairs

Recall that Badesa’s proof begins by reducing the count of

Obviously, to recover Badesa’s result from Theorem

In general, for any discriminant

4.
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{R})
-action on binary quadratic forms and homogeneous dynamics

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
2.2
.
The argument follows a standard template and uses familiar tools from homogeneous dynamics.
Since we expect the paper to be of particular interest to researchers coming from the Diophantine-tuples side,
we include a bit more setup than would typically be required.

To set the stage,
let
V
be the
3
-dimensional space of
real
binary quadratic forms.
As usual, we identify
V
with the space of symmetric
2\times 2
real matrices via

\bigl(Q(x,y)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}\bigr)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a&b/2\\

Depending on the context,
we use

In particular, for

Let

We have already discussed the

In the matrix model,

We will study the

By Sylvester’s law of inertia (see, for example,

Fix a nondegenerate binary form

be its stabilizer in

Then the orbit

is isomorphic (as a homogeneous space

Via the orbit map,

Given a left-invariant Haar measure

From now on, fix a nonzero integer

Let

Note that

Define

\mathcal{F}_{T}=\bigl\{[a,b,c]\in V\,\bigm|\,|a|,|c|\leqslant T,\ a>c,\ b\geqslant 0\bigr\}.

To prove Theorem

\bigl|(\Gamma\cdot Q)\cap\mathcal{F}_{T}\bigr|.

To place this into the framework of

Set

\mathcal{B}_{T}:=\{gH\in G/H\mid g\cdot Q\in\mathcal{F}_{T}\}.

A natural heuristic is that

\bigl|\Gamma\cdot H\cap\mathcal{B}_{T}\bigr|\approx\frac{m_{G/H}(\mathcal{B}_{T})}{m_{G/H}(\Gamma\backslash G/H)},

where

denotes the volume of a fundamental domain for the
\Gamma

Indeed,
G/H
is partitioned into
\Gamma

all of equal volume by

Each translate contains exactly one point of the orbit
\Gamma\cdot H
,
so one expects

The main subtlety is boundary behaviour.
The boundary of
\mathcal{B}_{T}
cuts through many translates of the fundamental domain,
and in principle it could be biased toward avoiding (or capturing) orbit points.
This matters if a neighbourhood of the boundary occupies a non-negligible proportion of
\mathcal{B}_{T}

To rule this out one assumes a regularity condition on the boundary,
usually formulated as a well-roundedness hypothesis.
In our setting this condition will always hold, since
\mathcal{B}_{T}
is a sector of a norm ball (see, for example,

The famous counting theorem of Eskin and McMullen

shows that under such a hypothesis,

Lemma 6.1

As

The argument parallels Lemma

Since only the

\mathcal{B}_{T}=\{k(\theta)a(t)H\in G/H\mid k(\theta)\,a(t)\cdot[n^{1/2},0,-n^{1/2}]\in\mathcal{F}_{T}\}.

A direct computation gives

k(\theta)a(t)\cdot[n^{1/2},0,-n^{1/2}]=n^{1/2}\begin{pmatrix}\sinh t+\cosh t\,\cos\theta&\cosh t\,\sin\theta\\
\cosh t\,\sin\theta&\sinh t-\cosh t\,\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.

Writing

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{T}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{k(\theta)a(t)H\in G/H\bigm||\sinh t+\cosh t\,\cos\theta|\leqslant M,\ |\sinh t-\cosh t\,\cos\theta|\leqslant M,\ \theta\in[0,\pi/2)\bigr\}.

For

We split this interval depending on whether the inequality

The transition point is

Finally, we treat the remaining case in Theorem

Throughout this section let

As before, our goal is to estimate

The stabilizer

Before using their result, we recall the generalized Cartan decomposition (

After adapting to our setting and normalizations,

In fact, they write

[

For forms of discriminant

The space

decomposes into

V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}V_{\lambda}\quad\text{and $V_{\lambda}$ is $e^{\lambda t}$-eigenspace of $a(t)$ for all $t$}.

Concretely,

Let

a(t)\cdot Q=(1+o(1))\,e^{t}\,Q_{1},

k(\theta)a(t)\cdot Q=(1+o(1))\,e^{t}\,k(\theta)\cdot Q_{1},

In particular, for each fixed

\|k(\theta)a(t)\cdot Q\|\sim e^{t}\,\|k(\theta)\cdot Q_{1}\|.

Proposition 7.1
.

As

m_{G/H}(\mathcal{B}_{T})\sim\frac{T}{4}\int_{\Theta}\|k(\theta)\cdot Q_{1}\|^{-1}\,d\theta.

Sketch proof.

Variants of this statement appear in the literature (for instance in much greater generality in
[
19
]
),
so we only indicate the main idea.
We focus on the contribution from the region
t\geqslant 0
; the region
t<0
is analogous.

For large
t
, the direction of

is governed by
k(\theta)\cdot Q_{1}
, in the sense that

\frac{k(\theta)a(t)\cdot Q}{\|k(\theta)a(t)\cdot Q\|}\to\frac{k(\theta)\cdot Q_{1}}{\|k(\theta)\cdot Q_{1}\|}.

Therefore only
\theta\in\Theta
contribute to the main term, and for each such

\|k(\theta)a(t)\cdot Q\|\leqslant T
.
Writing

the asymptotic (
7.2

e^{t_{T}(\theta)}\sim\frac{T}{\|k(\theta)\cdot Q_{1}\|}.

Using the density

m_{G/H}(\mathcal{B}_{T})\sim\frac{1}{4}\int_{\theta\in\Theta}\int_{t=0}^{t_{T}(\theta)}\cosh(t)\,dt\,d\theta\sim\frac{T}{8}\int_{\theta\in\Theta}\|k(\theta)\cdot Q_{1}\|^{-1}\,d\theta.

\bigl|(\Gamma\cdot Q)\cap\mathcal{F}_{T}\bigr|\sim\frac{2\log T\cdot m_{G/H}(\mathcal{B}_{T})}{m_{G}(\Gamma\backslash G)}.

) now gives the asymptotic in the split case, completing the proof of Theorem

.

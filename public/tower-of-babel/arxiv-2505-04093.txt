Neutrino-jet correlations in charged-current SIDIS
Weihua Yang, Jing Zhao, Zhe Zhang

Abstract
Charged-current deep inelastic scattering plays a significant role in determining parton distribution functions with flavour separation. In this work, we present a systematic calculation of the charged-current semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering (SIDIS) in the e ​ N eN collinear frame up to twist-3 level at leading order. Semi-inclusive refers to the process in which a jet is detected in addition to the scattered neutrino. We focus on neutrino-jet correlations in our calculation. We first present the differential cross section in terms of structure functions, followed by the differential cross section expressed in term of transverse momentum dependent parton distribution functions. We derive the complete set of azimuthal asymmetries and intrinsic asymmetries. We also introduce an observable A C A^{C} , defined as the ratio of the difference to the sum of differential cross sections for electron and positron semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering. We notice that A C A^{C} not only provides a sensitive probe for valence quark distribution functions but also can reveal the violation of strange-antistrange symmetry.



I
Introduction

The lepton-nucleon deep inelastic scattering (DIS) has achieved great success in our understanding of the partonic structure of the nucleon. It will still play an important role in the future Electron-Ion collider (EIC)
Accardi:2012qut
;
AbdulKhalek:2021gbh
;
Anderle:2021wcy
experiment to explore the spin and three-dimensional structure of the nucleon over wide kinematic regions. Thanks to the factorization theorem
Collins:1989gx
, the cross section of DIS can be factorized into two parts, one is the hard part which can be calculated with perturbative theory, the other is the soft part which involves parton distribution functions (PDFs). The one-dimensional or collinear PDFs is studied in the inclusive DIS, where only the scattered lepton is detected with high precision. However, inclusive DIS can not access three-dimensional or transverse momentum dependent PDFs (TMDs), since no transverse momentum scale is introduced. To solve this problem, one needs to consider the semi-inclusive DIS (SIDIS), where a hadron or a jet is also detected in addition to the scattered lepton.

For the hadron production in SIDIS
Mulders:1995dh
;
Bacchetta:2006tn
, fragmentation functions (FFs) which describe the formation of hadrons from a partonic initial state are involved. The cross section will be given in terms of convolutions of PDFs and FFs. Fragmentation functions are nonperturbative quantities and can only be determined by experimental measurements.
Comparing to hadron production SIDIS, the jet production one does have a simpler form of theoretical formulation and not introduce extra uncertainties from FFs. This is helpful to improve the measurement accuracy. Furthermore, the jet can be a direct probe of analyzing properties of the partonic structure of nucleon. For example, the transverse momentum of the jet is equal to that of the quark in the
VN
collinear frame
Yang:2022xwy
;
Yang:2023vyv
;
Yang:2023zod
;
Wu:2023omf
. Here
V
denotes the intermediate exchange boson, whose transverse momentum is defined as zero. For these reasons, jet production SIDIS for TMDs studies has attracted a lot of attention
Song:2010pf
;
Song:2013sja
;
Wei:2016far
;
Gutierrez-Reyes:2018qez
;
Gutierrez-Reyes:2019vbx
;
Liu:2018trl
;
Liu:2020dct
;
Kang:2020fka
;
Arratia:2020ssx
;
Chen:2020ugq
;
Yang:2020qsk
;
Arratia:2019vju
. Nevertheless, jet production SIDIS process can not cover the low energy kinematic regions and access the chiral-odd TMDs due to the conservation of the helicity. The hadron production SIDIS process therefore has to be considered to study chiral-odd TMDs. We note here that proposals for exploring the chiral-odd TMDs via jet fragmentation functions have been studied recently in Refs.
Accardi:2017pmi
;
Accardi:2019luo
;
Accardi:2020iqn
;
Kang:2020xyq
.

In addition to the neutral-current DIS, the charged-current DIS is also an important process in detecting nucleon structures
AbdulKhalek:2021gbh
. Charged-current interactions can be used as a clean probe to separate quark flavors, which cannot be achievable in the neutral-current DIS alone. On the other hand, charged-current interactions with the high statistics samples can offer precision measurements of electroweak parameters, e.g., weak mixing angle
Boer:2011fh
which is used to determine the running of
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}
as a function of
Q^{2}
. In this paper, We focus on the charged-current jet production SIDIS process in the
eN
collinear frame. In our analysis, the jet is simplified as a quark. Considering the conservation of momentum, the jet and the scattered electron are produced back-to-back in the plane perpendicular to the beam direction. However, the intrinsic transverse momentum of the quark or higher order gluon radiations would break the balance and induce measurable effects. We notice that these effects induced by the intrinsic transverse momentum of the quark provide a favourable palace to study TMDs. To this end, we present a systematic calculation of the neutrino-jet correlations (
j=l^{\prime}+k^{\prime}
) in this process in the
eN
collinear frame at leading order and twist-3 level.
Both the electron and positron scattering off a nucleon (nucleus) are considered and theoretical expressions are written in a general form. We first present the differential cross section in terms of structure functions, followed by the differential cross section given in terms of TMDs at twist-3 level. We further calculate the complete azimuthal asymmetries and intrinsic asymmetries. We also introduce an observable
A^{C}
, defined as the ratio of the difference to the sum of differential cross sections for electron and positron SIDIS processes.
We notice that
A^{C}
not only provides a sensitive probe for valence quark distribution functions but also can reveal the violation of strange-antistrange symmetry. Numerical estimates show that strange and antistrange distributions have a contribution of about
2\%
.

To be explicit, we organize this paper as follows. In Sec.
II
, we present the formalism and conventions used in this paper and give a brief introduction of the
eN
collinear frame. In Sec.
III
, we present a systematic calculation of the differential cross section of the jet production SIDIS in terms of structure functions. The systematic calculations in the parton model up to twist-3 level are shown in Sec.
IV
. Measurable quantities and numerical estimates are given in Sec.
V
. A brief summary is given in Sec.
VI
.

II
The formalism

In this paper, we consider charged-current semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering (SIDIS), in which both a jet and the scattered lepton (neutrino or antineutrino) are detected in the final state.
The electron scattering process is labeled as follows,

\displaystyle e^{-}(l)+N(p,S)\rightarrow\nu_{e}(l^{\prime})+jet(k^{\prime})+X,

(1)

where
e^{-}
denotes an electron with momentum
l
,
N
represents a nucleon with momentum
p
, and the
jet
corresponds to a quark with momentum
k^{\prime}
, which is observed as a jet of hadrons in experiments.
We require that the jet is in the current fragmentation region.
For the positron scattering process,
e^{-}
is replaced by
e^{+}
, and the final-state neutrino
\nu_{e}
is replaced by the antineutrino
\bar{\nu}_{e}
.
To be explicit, we list the standard variables for the jet production SIDIS,

\displaystyle s=(p+l)^{2},

\displaystyle x=\frac{Q^{2}}{2p\cdot q},

\displaystyle y=\frac{p\cdot q}{p\cdot l},

(2)

where
Q^{2}=-q^{2}=-(l-l^{\prime})^{2}
.

Regardless of whether the process involves electron or positron scattering, the differential cross section can always be expressed as the contraction of the leptonic tensor and the hadronic tensor,

\displaystyle d\sigma=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{sQ^{4}}A_{W}L_{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,S,k^{\prime})\frac{d^{3}l^{\prime}d^{3}k^{\prime}}{E_{l^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{3}2E_{k^{\prime}}},

(3)

where
\alpha_{\rm em}
is the fine structure constant. The
A_{W}
factor is calculated as

\displaystyle A_{W}=\frac{Q^{4}}{\left[(Q^{2}+M_{W}^{2})^{2}+\Gamma_{W}^{2}M_{W}^{2}\right]16\sin^{4}\theta_{W}},

(4)

where
M_{W},\Gamma_{W}
indcate the mass and width of
W
boson, respectively, and the
\theta_{W}
denotes the weak mixing angle.
The leptonic tensor is given by

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})=2\left(l_{\mu}l^{\prime}_{\nu}+l_{\nu}l^{\prime}_{\mu}-g_{\mu\nu}l\cdot l^{\prime}\right)+2i\lambda_{n}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu ll^{\prime}},

(5)

where
\lambda_{n}
is introduced for convenience. For neutrino production and anti-neutrino production,
\lambda_{n}
is
-1
and
1
, respectively. The hadronic tensor is given by

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{X}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{4}(p+q-k^{\prime}-p_{X})

\displaystyle\times\langle N,S|J^{\mu}(0)|k^{\prime};X\rangle\langle k^{\prime};X|J^{\nu}(0)|N,S\rangle.

(6)

The weak current is defined as
J^{\mu}(0)=\bar{\psi}(0)\Gamma^{\mu}\psi(0)
, where
\Gamma^{\mu}=\gamma^{\mu}(c_{V}^{q}-c_{A}^{q}\gamma^{5})
is the interaction vertex, with the weak couplings
c_{V}^{q}
and
c_{A}^{q}
.
For charged-current weak interaction, both couplings take the value
c_{V}^{q}=c_{A}^{q}=1
.
We keep these symbols in the following context for convenience.
It is also convenient to consider the
k_{T}^{\prime}
-dependent cross section, i.e.,

\displaystyle d\sigma=\frac{\alpha_{\rm{em}}^{2}}{sQ^{4}}A_{W}L_{\mu\nu}(l,\lambda_{n},l^{\prime})W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,S,k_{T}^{\prime})\frac{d^{3}l^{\prime}d^{2}k_{T}^{\prime}}{E_{l^{\prime}}},

(7)

where the
k^{\prime}_{z}
-integrated hadronic tensor is given by

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,S,k_{T}^{\prime})=\int\frac{dk_{z}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{k^{\prime}}}W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,S,k^{\prime}).

(8)

Following the convention of investigating the neutrino-jet correlations in the
eN
collinear frame, we define the sum of the momenta of the scattered neutrino (antineutrino) and the jet as
j=l^{\prime}+k^{\prime}
.
In this collinear frame, we have

\displaystyle\vec{j}_{T}=\vec{l}_{T}^{\prime}+\vec{k}_{T}^{\prime}=\vec{l}_{T}^{\prime}+\vec{k}_{T}+\vec{q}_{T}=\vec{k}_{T},

(9)

if higher order gluon radiations are neglected. In other words, the transverse momentum
j_{T}
equals to the intrinsic transverse momentum
k_{T}
of a quark in the nucleon (nucleus).
Therefore, the cross section can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\eta d^{2}l^{\prime}_{T}d^{2}j_{T}}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{sQ^{4}}A_{W}L_{\mu\nu}(l,\lambda_{n},l^{\prime})W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,S,j_{T}),

(10)

where
\eta
is the rapidity of the scattered lepton. Here we have used the relation
d\eta=dl^{\prime}_{z}/E_{l^{\prime}}
.

As illustrated in Fig.
1
, the
eN
collinear frame is defined such that the target moves along the
+z
direction, while the incoming lepton travels along the
-z
direction. The scattered (anti-)neutrino lies in the
xoz
plane which is known as the lepton plane.
In this frame, we use the light-cone unit vectors
\bar{t}^{\mu}=(1,0,\vec{0}_{T})
and
t^{\mu}=(0,1,\vec{0}_{T})
to decompose the relevant momenta, and they satisfy
\bar{t}^{2}=t^{2}=0
and
\bar{t}\cdot t=1
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle p^{\mu}=p^{+}\bar{t}^{\mu}+p^{-}t^{\mu},

(11)

\displaystyle l^{\mu}=l^{+}\bar{t}^{\mu}+l^{-}t^{\mu},

(12)

where the plus (minus) components are defined as
p^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(p^{0}\pm p^{3})
, and similarly for
l^{\pm}
.
Up to
\mathcal{O}(1/Q^{2})
, the momenta can be approximated as
p^{\mu}\approx p^{+}\bar{t}^{\mu}
and
l^{\mu}\approx l^{-}t^{\mu}
.
Correspondingly, the light-cone vector in this frame can be defined as
\bar{t}^{\mu}=p^{\mu}/p^{+}
and
t^{\mu}=l^{\mu}/l^{-}
.
Therefore, the relevant momenta can be parametrized as

\displaystyle p^{\mu}=\left(p^{+},0,\vec{0}_{T}\right),

(13)

\displaystyle l^{\mu}=\left(0,\frac{Q^{2}}{2xyp^{+}},\vec{0}_{T}\right),

(14)

\displaystyle l^{\prime\mu}=\left(xyp^{+},\frac{(1-y)Q^{2}}{2xyp^{+}},Q\sqrt{1-y},0\right),

(15)

\displaystyle q^{\mu}=\left(-xyp^{+},\frac{Q^{2}}{2xp^{+}},-Q\sqrt{1-y},0\right).

(16)

Since the transverse momentum
j_{T}
equals to the intrinsic transverse momentum
k_{T}
of a quark in the nucleon in the
eN
collinear frame, we parameterize them as

\displaystyle j_{T}^{\mu}=k_{T}^{\mu}=|k_{T}|(0,0,\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi),

(17)

and we do not distinguish them in the following context.
The polarization vector of the nucleon is described by the helicity
\lambda_{N}
and the transverse polarization vector
S_{T}^{\mu}
, where
S_{T}^{\mu}
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle S_{T}^{\mu}=|S_{T}|\left(0,0,\cos\varphi_{S},\sin\varphi_{S}\right).

(18)

Here we note that the transverse component is defined with respect to the
z
-direction determined by momenta of the incoming lepton and the target nucleon.

Figure 1:
Illustration of the SIDIS process of the jet productions in the
eN
collinear frame.

Figure 2:
Illustration of the SIDIS process of the jet productions in the
VN
collinear frame.

Another coordinate frame commonly used in SIDIS is the boson-nucleon (VN) collinear frame, illustrated in Fig.
2
, where the nucleon travels along the
+z
direction while the intermediate exchange boson moves along the
-z
direction.
In this frame, we decompose the relevant momenta using the unit vectors
(\bar{n},n)
, which are distinguished from
(\bar{t},t)
defined in the
eN
collinear frame. We also have

\displaystyle p^{\mu}=\tilde{p}^{+}\bar{n}^{\mu}+\tilde{p}^{-}n^{\mu},

(19)

\displaystyle q^{\mu}=q^{+}\bar{n}^{\mu}+q^{-}n^{\mu},

(20)

where the plus component of
p^{\mu}
is written as
\tilde{p}
to distinguish that in the
eN
collinear frame.
Up to
\mathcal{O}(1/Q^{2})
, only the large plus component of
p^{\mu}
survives.
The light-cone vectors can be defined as
\bar{n}^{\mu}=p^{\mu}/\tilde{p}^{+}
and
n^{\mu}=(q^{\mu}+xp^{\mu})/q^{-}
.
Under these conditions, we can parametrize momenta of the involved particles as

\displaystyle p^{\mu}=\left(\tilde{p}^{+},0,\vec{0}_{\perp}\right),

\displaystyle q^{\mu}=\left(-x\tilde{p}^{+},\frac{Q^{2}}{2x\tilde{p}^{+}},\vec{0}_{\perp}\right),

\displaystyle l^{\mu}=\left(\frac{1-y}{y}x\tilde{p}^{+},\frac{Q^{2}}{2xy\tilde{p}^{+}},\frac{Q\sqrt{1-y}}{y},0\right),

\displaystyle l^{\prime\mu}=\left(\frac{1}{y}x\tilde{p}^{+},\frac{(1-y)Q^{2}}{2xy\tilde{p}^{+}},\frac{Q\sqrt{1-y}}{y},0\right).

(21)

The relationships between
(\bar{t},t)
and
(\bar{n},n)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\bar{t}^{\mu}=\frac{(2-y)}{y}\frac{\tilde{p}^{+}}{p^{+}}\bar{n}^{\mu}+\frac{yl_{T}^{\mu}+q_{T}^{\mu}}{xyp^{+}},

(22)

\displaystyle t^{\mu}=\frac{2(1-y)x^{2}\tilde{p}^{+}p^{+}}{yQ^{2}}\bar{n}^{\mu}+\frac{p^{+}}{\tilde{p}^{+}}n^{\mu}+\frac{2xyp^{+}}{Q^{2}}l_{T}^{\mu}.

(23)

According to our conventions, the transverse components of
l^{\mu}
and
q^{\mu}
can be written as

\displaystyle l_{T}=l_{x}=\frac{Q\sqrt{1-y}}{y},

(24)

\displaystyle q_{T}=q_{x}=-Q\sqrt{1-y},

(25)

and they lie in the lepton plane or the
x-z
plane.
Under this circumstance, the second term in Eq. (
22
) vanishes and

\displaystyle p^{+}\bar{t}^{\mu}=\frac{(2-y)}{y}\tilde{p}^{+}\bar{n}^{\mu}.

(26)

That is to say,
\tilde{p}^{+}
and
p^{+}
do not have to be equal.

III
The cross section in terms of structure functions

In this section, we perform a general kinematic analysis for this charged-current SIDIS process. We first decompose the hadronic tensor under the limit of kinematic constraints, and then express the differential cross section in terms of structure functions.

Since the hadronic tensor contain the unperturbative information in nucleon, it can not be calculated by perturbative theory.
It is well known that the hadronic tensor must satisfy the conditions required by the hermiticity, gauge invariance, and parity invariance,

\displaystyle W^{*\mu\nu}(q,p,S,j_{T})

\displaystyle=W^{\nu\mu}(q,p,S,j_{T}),

(27)

\displaystyle q_{\mu}W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,S,j_{T})

\displaystyle=q_{\nu}W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,S,j_{T})=0,

(28)

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}(q,p,S,j_{T})

\displaystyle=W_{\mu\nu}(q^{\mathcal{P}},p^{\mathcal{P}},-S^{\mathcal{P}},j_{T}^{\mathcal{P}}),

(29)

where the superscript
\mathcal{P}
indicates a sign flip of all space components, such as
A_{\mu}^{\mathcal{P}}=A^{\mu}
.
We note here that the parity invariance has no constraint on the hadronic tensor because weak interaction is considered in this process.
With the constraints of hermiticity and gauge invariance, we decompose the hadronic tensor in terms of basic Lorentz tensors (BLTs) multiplied by scalar functions.
One can divide the hadronic tensor into a symmetric and an antisymmetric parts, i.e.,
W^{\mu\nu}=W^{S\mu\nu}+iW^{A\mu\nu}
.
The more explicit expression is given by

\displaystyle W^{S\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,i}W^{S}_{\sigma i}h^{S\,\mu\nu}_{\sigma i}+\sum_{\sigma,i}\tilde{W}^{S}_{\sigma i}\tilde{h}^{S\,\mu\nu}_{\sigma i},

(30)

\displaystyle W^{A\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,i}W^{A}_{\sigma i}h^{A\,\mu\nu}_{\sigma i}+\sum_{\sigma,i}\tilde{W}^{A}_{\sigma i}\tilde{h}^{A\,\mu\nu}_{\sigma i},

(31)

where
h^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma i}
’s and
\tilde{h}^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma i}
’s represent the parity conserving and flipping BLTs, respectively.
These coefficients
W_{\sigma i}
’s are scalar functions.
We use the subscript
\sigma
to specify the spin states of the target particle.

The BLTs can be constructed from the kinematic variables involved in the process.
For unpolarized case, we obtain nine independent BLTs as

\displaystyle h^{S\,\mu\nu}_{U_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{q^{2}},\;p_{q}^{\mu}p_{q}^{\nu},\;j_{Tq}^{\mu}j_{Tq}^{\nu},\;p_{q}^{\{\mu}j_{Tq}^{\nu\}}\right\},

(32)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{S\,\mu\nu}_{U_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\varepsilon^{\{\mu qpj_{T}}p_{q}^{\nu\}},\;\varepsilon^{\{\mu qpj_{T}}j_{Tq}^{\nu\}}\right\},

(33)

\displaystyle h_{U}^{A\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left\{p_{q}^{[\mu}j_{Tq}^{\nu]}\right\},

(34)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{A\,\mu\nu}_{U_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\varepsilon^{\mu\nu qp},\;\varepsilon^{\mu\nu qj_{T}}\right\},

(35)

where
p_{q}^{\mu}\equiv p^{\mu}-q^{\mu}(p\cdot q)/q^{2}
, which satisfies
p_{q}\cdot q=0
.
The subscript
U
indicates the unpolarized part.
We also use the symmetrization and antisymmetrization conventions, i.e.,
A^{\{\mu}B^{\mu\}}\equiv A^{\mu}B^{\mu}+A^{\nu}B^{\mu}
and
A^{[\mu}B^{\mu]}\equiv A^{\mu}B^{\mu}-A^{\nu}B^{\mu}
.

As shown in Refs.
Chen:2016moq
;
Zhao:2024zpy
, the polarization dependent BLTs can be constructed from multiplying the unpolarized BLTs given in Eqs. (
32
)–(
35
) by the polarization dependent scalars or pseudoscalars.
Therefore, we obtain the vector polarized BLTs as follows

\displaystyle h^{S\,\mu\nu}_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[(q\cdot S),\;(j_{T}\cdot S)\right]\tilde{h}^{S\,\mu\nu}_{U_{i}},\;\varepsilon^{Sqpj}h^{S\,\mu\nu}_{U_{j}}\right\},

(36)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{S\,\mu\nu}_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[(q\cdot S),\;(j_{T}\cdot S)\right]h^{S\,\mu\nu}_{U_{i}},\;\varepsilon^{Sqpj}\tilde{h}^{S\,\mu\nu}_{U_{j}}\right\},

(37)

\displaystyle h^{A\,\mu\nu}_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[(q\cdot S),\;(j_{T}\cdot S)\right]\tilde{h}^{A\,\mu\nu}_{U_{i}},\;\varepsilon^{Sqpj}h^{A\,\mu\nu}_{U}\right\},

(38)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{A\,\mu\nu}_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[(q\cdot S),\;(j_{T}\cdot S)\right]h^{A\,\mu\nu}_{U},\;\varepsilon^{Sqpj}\tilde{h}^{A\,\mu\nu}_{Uj}\right\},

(39)

where the subscript
V
represent the vector polarized dependence.

Substituting the BLTs shown in Eqs. (
32
)–(
39
) into Eqs. (
30
) and (
31
), one can obtain the complete decomposition of hadronic tensor.
After contracting it with the leptonic tensor, we can express the differential cross section in a general form. Neglecting the complicated calculations, we here only show the final result.
According to the polarization states of the nucleon, the differential cross section can be divided into three parts,

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\eta d^{2}l^{\prime}_{T}d^{2}j_{T}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{Q^{4}}xyA_{W}\bigg[\mathcal{F}_{U}+\lambda_{N}\mathcal{F}_{L}+|S_{T}|\mathcal{F}_{T}\bigg],

(40)

where the subscript indicate the polarization states of the target particle.
The explicit form of each part can be expressed in terms of the corresponding structure functions, which are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{U}=

\displaystyle F_{U}+\sin\varphi F_{U}^{\sin\varphi}+\sin 2\varphi F_{U}^{\sin 2\varphi}+\cos\varphi F_{U}^{\cos\varphi},

(41)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{L}=

\displaystyle F_{L}+\sin\varphi F_{L}^{\sin\varphi}+\sin 2\varphi F_{L}^{\sin 2\varphi}+\cos\varphi F_{L}^{\cos\varphi}

\displaystyle+\cos 2\varphi F_{L}^{\cos 2\varphi}+\cos 3\varphi F_{L}^{\cos 3\varphi},

(42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{T}=

\displaystyle\sin\varphi_{S}F_{T}^{\sin\varphi_{S}}+\sin(\varphi+\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\sin(\varphi+\varphi_{S})}

\displaystyle+\sin(2\varphi+\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\sin(2\varphi+\varphi_{S})}+\sin(\varphi-\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\sin(\varphi-\varphi_{S})}

\displaystyle+\sin(2\varphi-\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\sin(2\varphi-\varphi_{S})}+\sin(3\varphi-\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\sin(3\varphi-\varphi_{S})}

\displaystyle+\cos\varphi_{S}F_{T}^{\cos\varphi_{S}}+\cos(\varphi+\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\cos(\varphi+\varphi_{S})}

\displaystyle+\cos(\varphi-\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\cos(\varphi-\varphi_{S})}+\cos(2\varphi-\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\cos(2\varphi-\varphi_{S})}

\displaystyle+\cos(3\varphi-\varphi_{S})F_{T}^{\cos(3\varphi-\varphi_{S})}.

(43)

where each term corresponds to a combination of nucleon polarization state and azimuthal modulation.

IV
The calculation in the parton model

IV.1
The leading-twist hadronic tensor

In the parton model, we calculate the hadronic tensor up to twist-3 level.
In order to show a clear calculation, it is convenient to divide the hadronic tensor into a leading-twist part and a twist-3 part.
We first begin with the leading-twist contribution.

At the leading-twist, the hadronic tensor can be obtained by using Eq. (
6
).
According to the factorization, it can be written as

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}=\frac{2E_{k^{\prime}}}{2p\cdot q}{\rm{Tr}}\left[\hat{\Phi}^{(0)}(x,k_{T})\hat{H}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)\right](2\pi)^{3}\delta(q_{z}+k_{z}-k_{z}^{\prime}).

(44)

Integrating over
k^{\prime}_{z}
, see Eq. (
8
), we obtain the
k^{\prime}_{T}(j_{T})
-dependent hadronic tensor,

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2p\cdot q}{\rm{Tr}}\left[\hat{\Phi}^{(0)}(x,k_{T})\hat{H}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)\right],

(45)

where the quark-quark correlator is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}^{(0)}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p^{+}d\xi^{-}d^{2}\vec{\xi}_{T}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ixp^{+}\xi^{-}-i\vec{k}_{T}\vec{\xi}_{T}}

\displaystyle\times\langle N,S|\bar{\psi}(0){\cal L}(0,y)\psi(\xi)|N,S\rangle.

(46)

The gauge link has been inserted into the quark-quark correlator to keep the gauge invariance. We decompose the correlators as

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma^{\alpha}\Phi^{(0)}_{\alpha}+\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\tilde{\Phi}^{(0)}_{\alpha}\right],

(47)

In the jet-production SIDIS process, we only need to consider the
\gamma^{\alpha}
- and the
\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{5}
-terms (chiral-even terms) in the parametrization of these correlators, because there is no helicity flips.
The TMDs are obtained by decomposing the correlator which are given by

\displaystyle\Phi^{(0)}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=p^{+}\bar{t}_{\alpha}\Bigl(f_{1}-\frac{k_{T}\cdot\tilde{S}_{T}}{M}f^{\perp}_{1T}\Bigr)+k_{T\alpha}f^{\perp}

\displaystyle-M\tilde{S}_{T\alpha}f_{T}-\lambda_{N}\tilde{k}_{T\alpha}f_{L}^{\perp}-\frac{k_{T\langle\alpha}k_{T\beta\rangle}}{M}\tilde{S}_{T}^{\beta}f_{T}^{\perp},

(48)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}^{(0)}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=p^{+}\bar{t}_{\alpha}\Bigl(-\lambda_{N}g_{1L}+\frac{k_{T}\cdot S_{T}}{M}g^{\perp}_{1T}\Bigr)-\tilde{k}_{T\alpha}g^{\perp}

\displaystyle-MS_{T\alpha}g_{T}-\lambda_{N}k_{T\alpha}g_{L}^{\perp}+\frac{k_{T\langle\alpha}k_{T\beta\rangle}}{M}S_{T}^{\beta}g_{T}^{\perp}.

(49)

We have introduced the notation
\tilde{A}^{\alpha}=\varepsilon_{T}^{\alpha A}=\varepsilon_{T}^{\alpha\beta}A_{T\beta}
, where
A
can be either
k_{T}
or
S_{T}
.
The symmetric traceless tensor is defined as
k_{T\langle\alpha}k_{T\beta\rangle}=k_{T\alpha}k_{T\beta}-\frac{1}{2}g_{T\alpha\beta}k_{T}^{2}
.

The hard part in Eq. (
45
) is abbreviated as

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\mu\nu}(q,k)=\Gamma^{\mu}(\not{q}+\not{k})\Gamma^{\nu}.

(50)

In the
eN
collinear frame, we have the following relationships

\displaystyle k^{-}\ll k_{T}\ll q_{T}\sim q^{-},

(51)

\displaystyle q^{+}+k^{+}=(1-y)xp^{+}.

(52)

Neglecting the small components of
k
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\not{q}+\not{k})=(1-y)xp^{+}\not{\bar{t}}+q^{-}\not{t}+\not{q}_{T}.

(53)

We notice that the transverse and the plus components also contribute in the
eN
collinear frame in addition to the minus component. This is important to the requirement of the current conservation of the hadronic tensor.

When the dust settles, the hadronic tensor at leading-twist can be expressed as

\displaystyle W_{t2}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-\left(c_{1}^{q}\tilde{g}_{T}^{\mu\nu}+ic_{3}^{q}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{T}^{\mu\nu}\right)\Bigl(f_{1}-\frac{k_{T}\cdot\tilde{S}_{T}}{M}f^{\perp}_{1T}\Bigr)

\displaystyle-\left(c_{3}^{q}\tilde{g}_{T}^{\mu\nu}+ic_{1}^{q}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{T}^{\mu\nu}\right)\Bigl(-\lambda_{N}g_{1L}+\frac{k_{T}\cdot S_{T}}{M}g^{\perp}_{1T}\Bigr),

(54)

where the subscript
t2
denotes leading-twist. The
\tilde{g}_{T}^{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{T}^{\mu\nu}
are dimensionless tensors,

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{T}^{\mu\nu}=g_{T}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\vec{q}_{T}^{2}}{(q^{-})^{2}}\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}-\frac{1}{q^{-}}q_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}},

(55)

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}_{T}^{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon_{T}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{q^{-}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\bar{t}q_{T}}.

(56)

It is easily to check that
q_{\mu}\tilde{g}_{T}^{\mu\nu}=q_{\nu}\tilde{g}_{T}^{\mu\nu}=0
and
q_{\mu}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{T}^{\mu\nu}=q_{\nu}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{T}^{\mu\nu}=0
by using the definitions

\displaystyle g_{T}^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-\bar{t}^{\mu}t^{\nu}-\bar{t}^{\nu}t^{\mu},

(57)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{T}^{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\bar{t}_{\alpha}t_{\beta}.

(58)

These equations imply that the hadronic tensor satisfies the current conservation.

IV.2
The twist-3 hadronic tensor

The twist-3 contributions to the hadronic tensor arise from two sources. One is the quark-quark correlator given in Eq. (
46
), the other is the quark-gluon-quark correlator whose operator definition is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{\rho}^{(1)}\left(x,k_{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p^{+}dy^{-}d^{2}y_{T}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ixp^{+}y^{-}-i\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{y}_{T}}

\displaystyle\times\langle p,S|\bar{\psi}(0)D_{T\rho}(0){\cal L}(0,y)\psi(y)|p,S\rangle,

(59)

where
D_{\rho}(y)=-i\partial_{\rho}+gA_{\rho}(y)
is the covariant derivative. We decompose
\hat{\Phi}_{\rho}^{(1)}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{\rho}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma^{\alpha}\Phi_{\rho\alpha}^{(1)}+\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\tilde{\Phi}_{\rho\alpha}^{(1)}\right].

(60)

Similar to Eqs. (
48
) and (
49
), the coefficient functions are decomposed as

\displaystyle\Phi^{(1)}_{\rho\alpha}

\displaystyle=p^{+}\bar{t}_{\alpha}\Bigl[k_{T\rho}f^{\perp}_{d}-M\tilde{S}_{T\rho}f_{dT}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{N}\tilde{k}_{T\rho}f_{dL}^{\perp}-\frac{k_{T\langle\rho}k_{T\beta\rangle}}{M}\tilde{S}_{T}^{\beta}f_{dT}^{\perp}\Bigr],

(61)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}^{(1)}_{\rho\alpha}

\displaystyle=ip^{+}\bar{t}_{\alpha}\Bigl[\tilde{k}_{T\rho}g^{\perp}_{d}+MS_{T\rho}g_{dT}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{N}k_{T\rho}g_{dL}^{\perp}-\frac{k_{T\langle\rho}k_{T\beta\rangle}}{M}S_{T}^{\beta}g_{dT}^{\perp}\Bigr],

(62)

where the subscript
d
is used to denote twist-3 TMDs defined via the quark-gluon-quark correlator.

Since the complete calculation of the twist-3 hadronic tensor is complicated, we only present the main steps by taking the
f^{\perp}
and
g^{\perp}
terms for example.

First of all, we calculate the contributions from the quark-quark correlator. Inserting the twist-3 TMDs (
48
)–(
49
) and the hard part (
50
) into Eq. (
45
), we obtain

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,q}

\displaystyle=\bigg[c_{1}^{q}\left(k_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(1-y)xp^{+}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-g^{\mu\nu}k_{T}\cdot q_{T}\right)

\displaystyle+ic_{3}^{q}\left(\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}(1-y)xp^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}\varepsilon_{T}^{kq}\right)\bigg]\frac{f^{\perp}}{p\cdot q}

\displaystyle+\bigg[-c_{3}^{q}\left(\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(1-y)xp^{+}+\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-g^{\mu\nu}\varepsilon_{T}^{kq}\right)

\displaystyle+ic_{1}^{q}\left(k_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-k_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}(1-y)xp^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}k_{T}\cdot q_{T}\right)\bigg]\frac{g^{\perp}}{p\cdot q}.

(63)

where the subscript
q
denotes hadronic tensor from the quark-quark correlator. Because of the incompleteness of the twist-3 hadronic tensor, this expression does not satisfy the current conservation, i.e.,
q_{\mu}W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,q}\neq 0
.

Next we calculate the contributions from the quark-gluon-quark correlator.
There are two parts,
W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,L}
and
W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,R}
, which satisfy
W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,L}=(W^{\nu\mu}_{t3,R})^{*}
. The indices
L
and
R
denote the left-cut and the right-cut, respectively
Liang:2006wp
. According to the operator definition of the hadronic tensor, we have

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,L}=\frac{1}{2p\cdot q}{\rm{Tr}}\left[\hat{\Phi}_{\rho}^{(1)}(x,k_{T})\hat{H}^{\mu\nu,\rho}(q,k_{1},k_{2})\right],

(64)

where
\hat{H}^{\mu\nu,\rho}
is the hard scattering amplitude,

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\mu\nu,\rho}=\Gamma^{\mu,q}\frac{\not{k}_{2}+\not{q}}{(k_{2}+q)^{2}}\gamma^{\rho}(\not{k}_{1}+\not{q})\Gamma^{\nu,q}.

(65)

Substituting Eqs. (
61
), (
62
) and (
65
) into (
64
) gives

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,L}

\displaystyle=\left[c_{1}^{q}\left(\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{k_{T}\cdot q_{T}}{q^{-}}-k_{T}^{\nu}\bar{t}^{\mu}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\ \ -ic_{3}^{q}\left(\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}}{q^{-}}-\tilde{k}_{T}^{\nu}\bar{t}^{\mu}\right)\right]\frac{p^{+}}{p\cdot q}f^{\perp}_{d}

\displaystyle-\left[c_{1}^{q}\left(\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{k_{T}\cdot q_{T}}{q^{-}}-k_{T}^{\nu}\bar{t}^{\mu}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\ \ -ic_{3}^{q}\left(\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}}{q^{-}}-\tilde{k}_{T}^{\nu}\bar{t}^{\mu}\right)\right]\frac{p^{+}}{p\cdot q}g^{\perp}_{d}.

(66)

We note that twist-3 TMDs marked with subscript
d
can be related to those without the subscript through the following relation
Chen:2020ugq

\displaystyle f_{dS}^{K}-g_{dS}^{K}=-x\left(f_{S}^{K}-ig_{S}^{K}\right),

(67)

where the superscript
K
can be
\perp
and the subscript
S
can be
L
and
T
.
Using this relation, Eq. (
66
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,L}=

\displaystyle-\left[c_{1}^{q}\left(\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{k_{T}\cdot q_{T}}{q^{-}}-k_{T}^{\nu}\bar{t}^{\mu}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-ic_{3}^{q}\left(\tilde{k}^{\nu}_{T}\bar{t}^{\mu}-\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}}{q^{-}}\right)\right]\frac{xp^{+}}{p\cdot q}f^{\perp}

\displaystyle+\left[ic_{1}^{q}\left(\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{k_{T}\cdot q_{T}}{q^{-}}-k_{T}^{\nu}\bar{t}^{\mu}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+c_{3}^{q}\left(\tilde{k}^{\nu}_{T}\bar{t}^{\mu}-\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}}{q^{-}}\right)\right]\frac{xp^{+}}{p\cdot q}g^{\perp}.

(68)

Note that the complete twist-3 hadronic tensor from the quark-gluon-quark correlator is given by the sum of
W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,L}
and
W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,R}
,

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,L}+W^{\mu\nu}_{t3,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[c_{1}^{q}\left(\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}k_{T}\cdot q_{T}-xp^{+}k_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}\right)-ic_{3}^{q}xp^{+}\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}\right]\frac{f^{\perp}}{p\cdot q}

\displaystyle+\left[c_{3}^{q}\left(\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\varepsilon_{T}^{kq}-xp^{+}\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}\right)+ic_{1}^{q}xp^{+}k_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}\right]\frac{g^{\perp}}{p\cdot q}.

(69)

To obtain the complete twist-3 hadronic tensor, which satisfies the current conservation, one is supposed to sum Eqs. (
63
) and (
69
) together. Finally, we have

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{t3}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\perp}}{p\cdot q}h^{\mu\nu}_{1}+\frac{g^{\perp}}{p\cdot q}h^{\mu\nu}_{3}+\cdots,

(70)

where the tensors
h^{\mu\nu}_{1}
and
h^{\mu\nu}_{3}
are given by

\displaystyle h_{1}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle+c_{1}^{q}\bigg[k_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)k_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg]

\displaystyle+ic_{3}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}\bigg],

(71)

\displaystyle h_{3}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-c_{3}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}\bigg]

\displaystyle+ic_{1}^{q}\bigg[k_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-k_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}k_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg].

(72)

Here we only show the
f^{\perp}
and
g^{\perp}
terms.
By performing the steps presented above, the twist-3 hadronic tensor including the previously omitted terms can be written as

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{t3}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\perp}}{p\cdot q}h^{\mu\nu}_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{N}f^{\perp}_{L}}{p\cdot q}h^{\mu\nu}_{2}+\frac{g^{\perp}}{p\cdot q}h^{\mu\nu}_{3}+\frac{\lambda g^{\perp}_{L}}{p\cdot q}h^{\mu\nu}_{4}

\displaystyle+\frac{Mf_{T}}{p\cdot q}h_{5}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{f^{\perp}_{T}}{p\cdot q}h_{6}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{Mg_{T}}{p\cdot q}h_{7}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{g^{\perp}_{T}}{p\cdot q}h_{8}^{\mu\nu},

(73)

where
h^{\mu\nu}_{2,4-8}
are defined as

\displaystyle h_{2}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-c_{1}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+\tilde{k}_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}\bigg]

\displaystyle+ic_{3}^{q}\bigg[k_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-k_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}k_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg],

(74)

\displaystyle h_{4}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-c_{3}^{q}\bigg[k_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)k_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg]

\displaystyle-ic_{1}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}\bigg],

(75)

\displaystyle h_{5}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle+\Bigg\{-c_{1}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{S}_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+\tilde{S}_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+\tilde{S}_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)\varepsilon_{T}^{qS}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+ic_{3}^{q}\bigg[S_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-S_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}S_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg]\Bigg\},

(76)

\displaystyle h_{6}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-\Bigg\{+c_{1}^{q}\bigg[k_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)k_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+ic_{3}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}\bigg]\Bigg\}\frac{\varepsilon_{T}^{kS}}{M}

(77)

\displaystyle-\Bigg\{-c_{1}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{S}_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+\tilde{S}_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+\tilde{S}_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)\varepsilon_{T}^{qS}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+ic_{3}^{q}\bigg[S_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-S_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}S_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg]\Bigg\}\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{2M},

(78)

\displaystyle h_{7}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle+\Bigg\{-c_{3}^{q}\bigg[S_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+S_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+S_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)S_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad-ic_{1}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{S}_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-\tilde{S}_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}\varepsilon_{T}^{qS}\bigg]\Bigg\},

(79)

\displaystyle h_{8}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle+\Bigg\{+c_{3}^{q}\bigg[k_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+k_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)k_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+ic_{1}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-\tilde{k}_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}\varepsilon_{T}^{qk}\bigg]\Bigg\}\frac{k_{T}\cdot S_{T}}{M}

(80)

\displaystyle-\Bigg\{+c_{3}^{q}\bigg[S_{T}^{\{\mu}t^{\nu\}}q^{-}+S_{T}^{\{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu\}}(2-y)xp^{+}+S_{T}^{\{\mu}q_{T}^{\nu\}}-\bigg(g^{\mu\nu}+\bar{t}^{\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu}\frac{2xp^{+}}{q^{-}}\bigg)S_{T}\cdot q_{T}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+ic_{1}^{q}\bigg[\tilde{S}_{T}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}q^{-}-\tilde{S}_{T}^{[\mu}\bar{t}^{\nu]}q^{+}-\bar{t}^{[\mu}t^{\nu]}\varepsilon_{T}^{qS}\bigg]\Bigg\}\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{2M}.

(81)

We notice that the complete twist-3 hadronic tensor in Eq. (
73
) satisfies the current conservation,
q_{\mu}\tilde{W}^{\mu\nu}_{t3}=q_{\nu}\tilde{W}^{\mu\nu}_{t3}=0
.
Although the
h
-tensors appear somewhat complicated, they share similar structures and will lead to a simple expression for the differential cross section.

IV.3
The cross section in the parton model

Contracting the hadronic tensor obtained in Eq. (
73
) with the leptonic tensor, the differential cross section can be expressed in terms of TMDs in the parton model.
At the leading-twist approximation, the contractions are given by

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}\left(c_{1}^{q}\tilde{g}_{T}^{\mu\nu}+ic_{3}^{q}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{T}^{\mu\nu}\right)=-\frac{2Q^{2}}{y^{2}}T_{0}^{q}(y),

(82)

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}\left(c_{3}^{q}\tilde{g}_{T}^{\mu\nu}+ic_{1}^{q}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{T}^{\mu\nu}\right)=-\frac{2Q^{2}}{y^{2}}T_{1}^{q}(y),

(83)

where
T
-functions are defined as

\displaystyle T_{0}^{q}(y)=c_{1}^{q}A(y)-\lambda_{n}c_{3}^{q}C(y),

\displaystyle T_{1}^{q}(y)=c_{3}^{q}A(y)-\lambda_{n}c_{1}^{q}C(y).

(84)

Here
A(y)=y^{2}-2y+2
and
C(y)=y(2-y)
.
Since
c_{1}^{q}=c_{3}^{q}=2
in the charged-current interaction, it follows that
T_{0}^{q}(y)=T_{1}^{q}(y)
. For simplicity, we define
T^{q}(y)\equiv T_{0}^{q}(y)=T_{1}^{q}(y)
in the following.
Therefore, the leading-twist cross section for the jet-production SIDIS in the
eN
collinear frame can be expressed as

\displaystyle d\tilde{\sigma}_{t2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{yQ^{4}}A_{W}T^{q}(y)2x\Biggl\{f_{1}-\lambda_{N}g_{1L}

\displaystyle+|S_{T}|k_{TM}\Big[\sin(\varphi-\varphi_{S})f^{\perp}_{1T}-\cos(\varphi-\varphi_{S})g^{\perp}_{1T}\Big]\Biggr\},

(85)

where
d\tilde{\sigma}_{t2}=d\sigma_{t2}/d\eta d^{2}l^{\prime}_{T}d^{2}j_{T}
and

k_{TM}=|k_{T}|/M
. The subscript
t2
denotes the leading-twist contribution.

Similarly, we can calculate the differential cross section at twist-3.
For conciseness, we only show contractions of the leptonic tensor with
h^{\mu\nu}_{1}
and
h^{\mu\nu}_{3}
,

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}\cdot h_{1}^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2Q^{3}}{y^{2}}|k_{T}|T_{2}^{q}(y)\cos\varphi,

(86)

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}\cdot h_{3}^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2Q^{3}}{y^{2}}|k_{T}|T_{3}^{q}(y)\sin\varphi.

(87)

Other contractions have the same forms. Here the
T
-functions are defined as

\displaystyle T_{2}^{q}(y)=c_{1}^{q}B(y)-\lambda_{n}c_{3}^{q}D(y),

\displaystyle T_{3}^{q}(y)=c_{3}^{q}B(y)-\lambda_{n}c_{1}^{q}D(y),

(88)

with
B(y)=(2-y^{2})\sqrt{1-y}
and
D(y)=y^{2}\sqrt{1-y}
.
After simple calculations, we write down the differential cross section at twist-3,

\displaystyle d\tilde{\sigma}_{t3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{yQ^{4}}A_{W}4x^{2}\kappa_{M}\tilde{T}^{q}(y)\Biggl\{k_{TM}\left[\cos\varphi f^{\perp}+\sin\varphi g^{\perp}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{N}k_{TM}\left[\sin\varphi f^{\perp}_{L}-\cos\varphi g^{\perp}_{L}\right]

\displaystyle+|S_{T}|\Big[\sin\varphi_{S}f_{T}+\sin(2\varphi-\varphi_{S})\frac{k_{TM}^{2}}{2}f_{T}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\quad+\cos\varphi_{S}g_{T}-\cos(2\varphi-\varphi_{S})\frac{k_{TM}^{2}}{2}g_{T}^{\perp}\Big]\Biggr\},

(89)

where
d\tilde{\sigma}_{t3}=d\sigma_{t3}/d\eta d^{2}l^{\prime}_{T}d^{2}j_{T}
.

\kappa_{M}=M/Q
is the twist suppression factor.
\tilde{T}^{q}(y)
is defined as
\tilde{T}^{q}(y)\equiv T_{2}^{q}(y)=T_{3}^{q}(y)
because of
c_{1}^{q}=c_{3}^{q}=2
.
We note that in the electron scattering process,
U
-type quarks (
u,\bar{d},\bar{s},\cdots
) contribute to the cross section while in the positron scattering process,
D
-type quarks (
d,s,\bar{u},\cdots
) contribute to the cross section.

V
Measurable quantities

V.1
The structure functions

Comparing the cross section in terms of structure functions in Eq. (
40
) and the parton model results in Eqs. (
85
) and (
89
), one can obtain the results of the structure functions in the parton model.
There are four nonzero structure functions at the leading twist,

\displaystyle F_{U}=2\frac{T^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}f_{1},

(90)

\displaystyle F_{L}=-2\frac{T^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}g_{1L},

(91)

\displaystyle F_{T}^{\sin(\varphi-\varphi_{S})}=2\frac{T^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}k_{TM}f_{1T}^{\perp},

(92)

\displaystyle F_{T}^{\cos(\varphi-\varphi_{S})}=-2\frac{T^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}k_{TM}g_{1T}^{\perp}.

(93)

At twist-3, we obtain eight nonzero structure functions,

\displaystyle F_{U}^{\cos\varphi}=-4\frac{\tilde{T}^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}x\kappa_{M}k_{TM}f^{\perp},

(94)

\displaystyle F_{U}^{\sin\varphi}=-4\frac{\tilde{T}^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}x\kappa_{M}k_{TM}g^{\perp},

(95)

\displaystyle F_{L}^{\sin\varphi}=-4\frac{\tilde{T}^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}x\kappa_{M}k_{TM}f_{L}^{\perp},

(96)

\displaystyle F_{L}^{\cos\varphi}=4\frac{\tilde{T}^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}x\kappa_{M}k_{TM}g_{L}^{\perp},

(97)

\displaystyle F_{T}^{\sin\varphi_{S}}=-4\frac{\tilde{T}^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}x\kappa_{M}f_{T},

(98)

\displaystyle F_{T}^{\sin(2\varphi-\varphi_{S})}=-4\frac{\tilde{T}^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}x\kappa_{M}\frac{k_{TM}^{2}}{2}f_{T}^{\perp},

(99)

\displaystyle F_{T}^{\cos\varphi_{S}}=-4\frac{\tilde{T}^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}x\kappa_{M}g_{T},

(100)

\displaystyle F_{T}^{\cos(2\varphi-\varphi_{S})}=4\frac{\tilde{T}^{q}(y)}{y^{2}}x\kappa_{M}\frac{k_{TM}^{2}}{2}g_{T}^{\perp}.

(101)

According to the results above, we find that results of the structure functions including only leading-twist functions exhibit the dependence on the even number of
\varphi
and
\varphi_{S}
, while the structure functions at twist-3 correspond to the dependence of the odd number of
\varphi
and
\varphi_{S}
.
We can in principle utilize these nonvanishing structure functions to study the coupling of the weak interactions.

V.2
The azimuthal asymmetries

For the jet-production SIDIS, FFs are not involved. Therefore, (TMD) PDFs are the only unknown quantities related to azimuthal asymmetries. From the cross sections shown before we notice that azimuthal asymmetries refer to azimuthal asymmetries of
k_{T}
, or azimuthal asymmetries of
j_{T}
, since
\vec{j}_{T}=\vec{k}_{T}
in the
eN
collinear from. One therefore can measure
j_{T}
to determine
k_{T}
.
We here use the Trento convention to define the azimuthal asymmetry. For example, we define

\displaystyle\langle\sin\varphi\rangle_{U}=\frac{\int d\tilde{\sigma}\sin\varphi d\varphi}{\int d\tilde{\sigma}d\varphi},

(102)

for the unpolarized or longitudinally polarized target case, and

The intrinsic asymmetries

In addition to azimuthal asymmetries, we also define intrinsic asymmetries in the

\displaystyle k_{T}^{y}=k_{T}\sin\varphi.

VI
Summary

In this paper, we present a systematic calculation of the charged-current jet-production SIDIS process in the
eN
collinear frame. The
eN
collinear frame is defined such that the target travels along the
+z
direction, while the incoming lepton travels along the
-z
direction. The scattered (anti-)neutrino lies in the
x-z
plane which is known as the lepton plane. The differential cross section is first expressed in terms of structure functions and then expressed in term of TMDs at tree level twist-3. Since the
W
-boson gains the transverse momentum component
q_{T}
in the
eN
collinear frame, the gauge invariance of hadronic tensor becomes difficult to verify directly. To achieve this goal, we present a systematic calculation of how to obtain the gauge invariant hadronic tensor at twist-3 level.
By comparing these two forms, we obtain a set of relationships between structure functions and the TMDs, shown in Eqs. (
90
)-(
101
).
We also calculate azimuthal asymmetries and intrinsic asymmetries. Two leading-twist and eight twist-3 azimuthal asymmetries are obtained. However, intrinsic asymmetries are all twist-3 quantities. They provide more measurable quantities for extracting TMDs.
We also introduce the charge asymmetry
A^{C}
, which is defined as the ratio of the difference to the sum of the differential cross sections of the electron semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering and the positron semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering. We find that
A^{C}
can be used to determine the valence quark distribution functions and the violation of strange-antistrange symmetry. Numerical values show that strange and antistrange quarks contributes approximately
2\%
to the charge asymmetry.

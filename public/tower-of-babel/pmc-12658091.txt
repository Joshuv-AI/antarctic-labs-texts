Massive MIMO joint beamforming and power allocation via LMS with narwhal lemming optimization and fractional ResNeXt-based control
Authors: Mian Muhammad Kamal, Luo Yinsheng, Tianjun Ma, Husam S. Samkari, Mohammed F. Allehyani, Muhammad Sheraz, Muhammad Hanif Ahmed Khan Khushik, Mohammad Qatawneh, Teong Chee Chuah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Nowadays, next-generation wireless communication networks are used to provide robust and accurate sensing capability, high-quality wireless connections using massive devices, and support high data transmission rates. Beamforming is used in Massive Multi-Input and Multi-Output (mMIMO) systems to effectively cancel the complex interference caused by the non-zero inner product among the transmitted and channel signal matrices. Various techniques are used to significantly maximize the energy efficiency and reduce the Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR) communication requirements. Meanwhile, these techniques did not maximize the sum-rate of all users by jointly optimizing the transmit beamformer. In this paper, a ResNeXt with Fractional Narwhal Lemming Optimization (ResFraNLO) is introduced for joint beamforming and power allocation in mMIMO systems. Here, the adaptive beamforming is performed using Least-Mean-Square (LMS) adaptive algorithm and the weights of LMS is adjusted optimally using Narwhal Lemming Optimization (NLO). After that, the power allocation is executed using the ResNeXt framework. Moreover, the performance of ResNeXt is improved by fine-tuning the weights and bias by Fractional Narwhal Lemming Optimization (FraNLO). Further, the supremacy of ResFraNLO is investigated, and the ResFraNLO attained achievable sum rate of 79.099 Mbps/Hz, Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 1.34 × 10-7, and Bit Error Rate (BER) of 5.71 × 10-9.

Introduction

The increasing growth of smart devices leads to an exponential increase in demand for faster data rates. Researchers have addressed several challenges in Fifth-generation (5G) wireless technology to manage the growing data traffic. The primary goals for the upcoming 5G networks include reducing latency, creating energy-efficient solutions, improving spectral efficiency, and cost-effective hardware solutions 1. In recent decades, researchers have introduced Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) technologies to offer improved reliability and higher data rates for a larger number of users. MIMO has played a central role in the evolution of wireless system technologies, both in theory and practice. To implement MIMO, Base Stations (BS) and potentially user terminals must be equipped with antenna elements or multiple antennas 2. In addition, signal processing techniques for hardware design, advancements in MIMO, and Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) have enabled the integration of sensing and communication capabilities using shared hardware and spectrum. This integrated approach significantly enhances both energy and spectral efficiency 3. Massive MIMO plays a vital role in enabling intelligent sensing systems to function efficiently within 5G and future-generation networks. This scheme efficiently mitigates both inter-antenna and multi-user interference in massive MU-MIMO systems 4.This advanced communication technology is indispensable due to next-generation networks, including 5G and beyond, rely heavily on its capabilities. Traditional multi-access methods are increasingly inadequate, as they introduce significant latency, offer lower data throughput, and lack the reliability required to handle the vast data generated by numerous smart sensors 5. In contrast, massive MIMO mitigates these challenges by delivering ultra-low latency, higher data transmission speeds, and improved support for beamforming and user multiplexing. These advantages make it possible to collect data from many sensors simultaneously, with minimal delay.The integration of Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS) with mMIMO is a pivotal enabler for advanced 5G applications, which is particularly critical for real-time services in intelligent sensing environments like autonomous transportation, telemedicine, decentralized energy grids, and smart buildings 6. This synergy directly supports Enhanced Mobile Broadband (eMBB) by delivering consistent, high-data-rate coverage in crowded venues and to cell-edge users; it facilitates Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) for mission-critical services such as remote surgery, industrial automation, and V2X communication by ensuring robust, instantaneous links; and it empowers Massive Machine-Type Communication (mMTC) by efficiently connecting vast networks of low-power IoT sensors in smart cities and agricultural systems, where the RIS dynamically enhances signal strength and reduces device power consumption. The joint beamforming and power allocation framework proposed in this paper is specifically designed to optimize performance for these demanding scenarios, thereby ensuring high spectral efficiency and reliability.

In this context, reflective surfaces like Large Intelligent Surfaces (LIS), Intelligent Reflective Surfaces (IRS), and Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS) are designed to increase the wireless communication systems. Specifically, by manipulating the characteristics of radio wave refraction, scattering, and reflection, RIS can outperform traditional relay techniques on the basis of energy efficiency and Outage Probability (OP). Furthermore, unlike traditional relay stations, RIS systems do not require encoding, Radio Frequency (RF) processing, or decoding, which significantly reduces system complexity. As a result, RIS-assisted communication systems are considered a promising solution for next-generation networks, including the Sixth Generation (6G) and beyond 7. Full-duplex communication is emerging as a promising solution to this challenge, offering the possibility of dramatically enhancing Spectral Efficiency (SE) 8. In full-duplex systems, the simultaneous transmission and reception on the same frequency can potentially double the SE, then half-duplex systems, which require separate channels for uplink and downlink transmissions. This advancement has drawn significant attention because, theoretically, full-duplex could double the spectral efficiency relative to half-duplex communication 9. For full-duplex to work effectively, however, self-interference from the transmitted signal must be sufficiently suppressed. When this occurs, full-duplex operation allows the system to transmit and receive data concurrently on the same frequency band. The challenge lies in managing the residual SI, which remains even after suppression, and this is where beamforming with multiple antennas can play a crucial role. By adjusting the beamforming strategy, it’s possible to further reduce SI while optimizing information transfer. To maximize the sum rate in full-duplex systems, beamforming needs to strike an optimal balance between suppressing residual SI and efficiently transmitting data 10.

In massive MIMO systems, the concept of beamforming differs slightly from the previous definitions. Beamforming refers to a signal processing technique that utilizes multiple antenna arrays at either the transmitter or receiver side to simultaneously transmit or detect signals from multiple target terminals, thus enhancing both system capacity and performance. This is achieved by configuring the antenna elements in a structured array, where beams directed towards specific targets are reinforced while others are suppressed or ignored 11. In addition to beamforming, a well-designed power allocation strategy can help mitigate interference, since the level of interference is closely tied to the transmit power of neighbouring nodes. In single-antenna systems, power allocation may be solely based on channel strength 12. Power allocation plays a crucial role in controlling interference and ensuring Quality of Service (QoS) within Device-to-Device (D2D) networks. The Weighted Minimum Mean Squared Error (WMMSE) is a well-established iterative algorithm used for solving the problem of maximizing the weighted sum rate in a centralized approach 13. However, WMMSE comes with significant computational complexity. To address this challenge, Machine Learning (ML) techniques have been leveraged to reduce the processing load. One promising solution involves the use of Graph Neural Networks (GNN), which incorporate network topology information to manage power allocation across D2D pairs 14 15. By treating channel gains as node and edge features, each D2D pair combines information from its neighbours, updating its feature representation with each GNN layer 3.

Motivation

Massive MIMO systems involve high-dimensional optimization for joint beamforming and power allocation, demanding significant computational resources because of large number of antennas and degrees of freedom, leading to longer real-time processing times. Further, efficient beamforming and power allocation must address inter-user interference and ensure fair resource distribution, which becomes increasingly complex with more antennas and users, impacting signal quality and overall system performance. Most of the works have addressed beam forming and power allocation as individual issues without considering their interdependence, leading to suboptimal results. Thus, the ResFraNLO is devised for a joint beamforming and power allocation scheme that is capable of improving the energy efficiency and stability, with high convergence.

Literature review

Jiang, T., et al. 3 devised an iterative algorithm based on Successive Convex Approximation (SCA) to jointly optimize power allocation and transceiver beamforming in full-duplex systems. In this model, a sum of rate maximization problem was formulated to maintain the coexistence of D2D networks and ISAC by detecting target using minimum sensing SINR. This approach demonstrated low computational complexity, reduced the transmission overhead on the base station, and improved overall resource efficiency. However, this model did not incorporate adaptive beamforming mechanisms that utilize real-time channel estimation or prediction techniques, such as Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) or Kalman filters. A Joint Power Allocation and Interference Management (JPAIM) algorithm was developed by Luo, H., et al. 12 for in-band full-duplex multi-cell multi-user networks. Here, the requirements of RF cancellers were removed by increasing the Analog Self Interference Cancellation (ASIC) capability of precoders. This method effectively reduced computational cost while achieving high computational efficiency. Nevertheless, this model did not account for more realistic conditions, such as scenarios with limited Channel State Information (CSI) caused by constrained backhaul resources 17. He, Z., et al. 18 introduced a Full-Duplex Integrated Sensing and Communication (FD ISAC) system that addressed the joint optimization of communication and sensing functionalities. In this model, a Successive Convex Approximation (SCA) and rank relaxation approach was utilized for providing high-quality solution during transmission of beamforming and power to base station. This approach was notable for its relatively low computational complexity and minimal power consumption, making it suitable for practical deployments where resource efficiency is crucial. Still, this model assumed ideal conditions by overlooking the effects of non-flat fading channels and relying on perfect CSI. Guo, Y., et al. 19 designed an Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) for RIS-aided Full-Duplex ISAC system that integrated UL communication and sensing. This model utilized cutting-the-edge Majorization-Minimization (MM) and Penalty-Dual-Decomposition (PDD) techniques for power allocation and signal processing. This method offered a flexible framework that provided improved signal propagation and beamforming efficiency without significant power demands. Despite these advantages, the model lacked the ability to handle complex sensing scenarios.The algorithm achieves near-optimal gain over conventional detectors in massive MU-MIMO by synergistically combining lattice reduction precoding 20

A penalty-based joint beamforming optimization framework developed by Liu, Z., et al. 21 aimed at establishing a unified and efficient transceiver design that supported high-performance simultaneous communication and sensing. In this model, the echo-miss problem was captures using Full-Duplex (FD) ISAC mono-static system. This model achieved accurate radar parameter estimation, and enhanced effectiveness. However, this model did not address inter-user and inter-target interference, particularly under scenarios with limited antenna and power resources. Sheemar, C.K., et al. 22 introduced a Parallel & Distributed Hybrid Beamforming (P&D-HYBF) for multicell millimetre-wave MIMO full-duplex systems. Here, the centralized-hybrid beamforming (C-HYBF) model was designed for beamforming in multi cell mMIMO millimeter Wave(mmWave) system. The approach effectively reduced communication overhead, computational load, and implementation cost 23. Even though this model involved high computational complexity, rendering them unsuitable for real-time deployment. Salem, A.A., et al. 24 introduced a coordinated beamforming approach to enhance the physical-layer security in cognitive radio networks operating under spectrum-sharing constraints. Here, the Successive Convex Approximation (SCA) based alternating optimization and SemiDefinite Programming (SDP) was used to provide solution to non-convexity issues. This method improved convergence, computational tractability, and maximized the worst-case secrecy rate of primary users. Yet, this model lacked lightweight or ML-based solutions to reduce the high computational burden of joint optimization. The Multi-Agent Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (MATD3) framework was devised by Fan, Q., et al. 25 for Network-Assisted Full-Duplex (NAFD) cell-free millimetre-wave networks. Here, a Multi-Agent Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic policy gradient (MATD3) technique was used for tacking non-complex issues. This model demonstrated minimal parameter usage, low training time, and practical applicability. Nevertheless, this technique did not extend to scenarios with imperfect or outdated CSI, and its robustness against channel estimation errors or feedback delays remained unexplored.

Novelty of the proposed ResFraNLO framework

While the individual components of our framework LMS, ResNeXt, Narwhal Optimizer (NO), Artificial Lemming Algorithm (ALA), and Fractional Calculus (FC) are established in their respective domains, the algorithmic novelty of ResFraNLO lies in their unique, synergistic integration to create a holistic solution for the joint beamforming and power allocation problem in RIS-aided mMIMO systems. The specific novel contributions are:Hybrid bio-inspired tuning of classical signal processing: We introduce a novel paradigm where a hybrid metaheuristic (NLO), formed by merging NO and ALA, is used not as a standalone solver, but to optimally fine-tune the weights of a classical LMS adaptive beamformer. This creates a highly efficient and robust beamformer that leverages the global search capability of NLO to overcome the local convergence limitations of standard LMS.Fractional calculus-enhanced deep learning for resource allocation: We propose a novel training methodology for deep learning-based power allocation (ResNeXt) by developing the Fractional NLO (Fra-NLO) optimizer. This is, to the best of our knowledge, the first application of FC to stabilize and accelerate the metaheuristic-based training of a neural network for wireless power allocation. The memory effect of FC provides a smoother, more stable convergence trajectory compared to standard NLO.End-to-end learnable joint optimization framework: ResFraNLO presents a unified, end-to-end framework that seamlessly integrates a metaheuristic-tuned beamformer with a deep learning-based power allocator. This co-design allows both modules to be optimized concerning shared system-level objectives (sum rate, BER, power consumption), leading to superior performance compared to treating beamforming and power allocation as separate, sequential problems.

Designed ResNeXt fractional narwhal lemming optimization for joint beamforming and power allocation in mMIMO framework

This paper presents the ResFraNLO technique for joint beamforming and power allocation in mMIMO systems. Initially, the model of the RIS-aided mMIMO system is considered. At the transmitter, the data streams are initially applied to the digital precoder, and then, the pre-coded data is fed into the IFFT to get time domain data. After that, the CP is added to the resultant data for avoiding interference, and the digital signal is converted into an analog signal by subjecting the data to the RF chain. Then, the analog signal is passed to the analog precoder, and the resultant precoded signal is directed towards to RIS. On the receiver side, the received signals are combined using an analog combiner, and then the LMS algorithm 27 is used to perform joint beamforming for the retrieval of the actual signal. Here, the weights of LMS are fine-tuned using the NLO algorithm, where NLO is the integration of NO 28 and ALA 29. Later, power allocation is performed using ResNeXt 30 based on different parameters, like power consumption, achievable sum rate, and SINR. Moreover, the performance of ResNeXt is enhanced by fine-tuning the weights and bias by utilizing the Fra-NLO algorithm, where the Fra-NLO is the combination of FC 31 and NLO. Following this, the signal is passed to the RF chain, and then, the CP is removed from it. Transmitter side

In mMIMO systems, multiple antennas are utilized on the transmitter side to pass a high-speed data stream to multiple users with minimal interference. Before transmission, the data streams are applied to the digital precoder initially, and the pre-coded data is applied to the IFFT to get data in the time domain. The addition of CP is performed to avoid for avoiding interference, and the data is subjected to the RF chain for the conversion of the digital signal into an analog signal. Following this, the analog signal is fed into the analog precoder, and the precoded signal is fed into RIS. The process carried out in each phase at the transmitter side is demonstrated as follows,

Data streams

Data streams are independent signals that are simultaneously transmitted to more than one user. It represents multiple input data streams from different users, which are given as input to the digital processing block.

Digital precoder

The digital precoder is used at the transmitter side to process the data streams for mapping into multiple antennas, which also combine and shape the data streams before passing them to the antenna array. The digital precoder helps to ensure the data streams are not destructively overlapped and enables the base station to transmit data streams via a large antenna array with high efficiency.

IFFT

The IFFT is used in mMIMO transmitters to effectively transform the signals from the frequency domain to time domain waveforms before transmitting by preserving the orthogonality of subcarriers.

Add CP

The CP acts as a buffer zone as the addition of CP helps to guard interval against multipath, simplify the design of receivers. The addition of CP to the time-domain signal helps to effectively mitigate the ISI due to multipath propagation.

RF chain

In general, an RF chain is a set of hardware components that helps to convert the signals in digital baseband form into an analog baseband signal suitable for wireless transmission. The RF chain helps to upconvert the baseband signal to the desired carrier frequency and remove unwanted spectral components.

Analog precoder

The analog precoder is used to manage multi-user interference and utilize a phase shifter for applying analog beamforming across antennas.

RIS

RIS is a planar array of semi-passive or passive elements that helps to intelligently redirect or reflect signals toward desired directions. The elements of RIS are tuned in order to alter the wireless propagation environment. RIS significantly increases the direction, strength, and coverage by reflecting the signal with adjustable phase shifts.

Receiver side

In the receiver side of mMIMO systems, the signals transmitted by multiple antennas are received, separated, and decoded to ensure the reliability of data reception by estimating the channel characteristics. The analog combiner is used on the receiver side for combining the received signals, and the actual signals are retrieved by performing adaptive beamforming using the LMS algorithm 27, where the NLO algorithm is designed for fine-tuning the weights of LMS. After that, the power allocation is executed by utilizing ResNeXt 30 concerning fitness parameters. The efficacy of ResNeXt is enhanced by training the bias and weights using the Fra-NLO algorithm. Following this, the signal is fed into the RF chain, and the CP is removed from the signal. Later, the FFT is applied for the conversion of a frequency domain signal into the time domain, and the converted signal is fed into a digital combiner to get the data streams. The process executed in each phase on the receiver side is briefly demonstrated as follows,

Analog combiner

An analog combiner is used to join received signals from multiple antennas in the analog domain by applying analog beamforming before subjecting them to RF down conversion. Generally, the analog combiner adjusts the amplitude and phase of signals from various antennas to perform beamforming.

Adaptive beamforming using LMS

Adaptive beamforming in a mMIMO system is the process of dynamic adjustment of beam patterns of the antenna array to minimize interference and increase the quality of the signal towards the intended users. Adaptive beamforming adjusts the weights of the antenna array dynamically for the transmission of directional signals. In this research, the adaptive beamforming is performed using the LMS algorithm 27 for the retrieval of the actual signal from the received signal. Here, the weights of the LMS algorithm are fine-tuned using NLO, where the NLO is the incorporation of NO 28 and ALA 29. The process executed during adaptive beamforming is detailed below,

(ii) Training of weights using NLO

The NLO is used to adjust the weight of LMS algorithm, where the NLO is designed by the integration of NO 28 and ALA 29. NO is a metaheuristic algorithm developed by considering the natural hunting behaviour of narwhals. Narwhals are marine mammals that perform sophisticated communication in order to locate their prey. The NO follows three steps, such as updation of the narwhal’s position, signal propagation, and signal emission for hunting its prey. The signal propagation and emission of narwhals are considered to design an optimization algorithm. The narwhal’s position is modified by following the two processes until reaching the prey. Moreover, the emulation of strategies observed in narwhal pods is performed to increase the exploitation and exploration in the search space. The NO achieved global optimality and avoided local minima while providing reasonable and satisfactory solutions. Similarly, ALA is a bio-inspired metaheuristic algorithm designed concerning the distinct natural characterise of lemmings. The mathematical modelling of ALA is based on foraging, evading predators, digging holes, and migration to long-distance behaviours. The exploitation of ALA is provided by evading predator and foraging behaviour of lemmings, whereas the exploration character is based on burrow digging and long-distance migration behaviour. The ALA converges quickly and is robust in attaining high stability to evade local optima and attain a global solution in the search space. RF chain

The RF chain is considered as hardware component that effectively processes the analog received signal from antennas to digital baseband, which helps to preserve the spatial information across antennas and helps to transform the weak wireless signals into digital form for further processing.

Remove CP

The CP is removed from the signal to break the orthogonality of subcarriers and to ensure the proper transmission of received signal to the frequency domain.

FFT

Once the CP is removed from the signal, the time-domain signals are applied to an FFT to preserve the orthogonality of subcarriers by converting time-domain subcarriers to frequency-domain subcarriers. This helps to effectively recover the original data streams transmitted over each subcarrier, which also mitigates noise and combines streams.

Data stream

Data streams are the received independent signal information received from the user, and it is the final output streams obtained after the completion of decoding and processing. After applying FFT, the separated user signals are recovered from the combined multi-antenna received signals.

Interpretation of performance gains

The consistent outperformance of ResFraNLO, evidenced by its higher achievable sum rate and lower BER/RMSE across varying SINR, antenna counts, and channel models, can be attributed to the synergistic design of its core components:Role of NLO in beamforming: The superior performance of the LMS-NLO beamformer stems from the effective hybridization of the Narwhal Optimizer (NO) and the Artificial Lemming Algorithm (ALA). The NO provides a robust mechanism for global exploration of the complex weight space through its modeled behaviors of signal emission and propagation, effectively avoiding local minima. The ALA component injects a capacity for rapid, localized exploitation through its burrow digging and migration behaviors. This hybrid strategy ensures that the LMS algorithm converges to a significantly more optimal set of weights wthan would be possible with standard gradient descent or a single metaheuristic, leading to more precise interference nulling and signal recovery.Role of FraNLO in power allocation: The integration of Fractional Calculus (FC) into NLO to form FraNLO is pivotal for training the ResNeXt network. FC, with its non-integer order derivatives, incorporates a memory of past states into the optimization process. This allows FraNLO to make smoother, more informed updates to the ResNeXt weights and biases, preventing abrupt, suboptimal jumps in the loss landscape. This results in a more stable and efficient convergence during the offline training phase, enabling the ResNeXt model to learn a more accurate and generalizable mapping from channel states to optimal power allocation policies. The ablation study in Fig. 5 confirms this, showing ResNeXt + FraNLO (ResFraNLO) outperforming ResNeXt + NLO and ResNeXt + ALA.

Analysis of trade-offs: accuracy vs. complexity

A critical consideration for any advanced algorithm is the trade-off between performance and computational cost. The ResFraNLO framework explicitly navigates this trade-off through a two-phase strategy:Offline training complexity: The training processes for the NLO-based beamformer and the FraNLO-tuned ResNeXt model are computationally intensive, as analyzed in "Complexity and Scalability Analysis". The metaheuristic algorithms (NLO, FraNLO) involve iterative population-based optimization, which has a higher complexity than convex solvers or standard gradient-based learning. This is the primary cost of achieving the high accuracy and robustness demonstrated in the results.Online inference efficiency: Crucially, this complexity is confined to the offline training phase. Once the optimal LMS weights are found and the ResNeXt model is trained, the online execution is highly efficient. The beamforming involves a simple LMS filter update with complexity O(Nt), and the power allocation is a forward pass through a fixed ResNeXt model. This makes the deployed system suitable for real-time operation in a 5G base station, as the online computational load is manageable and predictable.

Conclusion

This paper presents the ResFraNLO for joint beamforming and power allocation in mMIMO systems. The system model of the RIS-aided mMIMO is considered initially. In the transmitter side, the data streams are applied to the digital precoder and then fed into the IFFT. After that, the CP is added to the resultant data, and the digital signal is converted into an analog signal in the RF chain. Then, the analog signal is passed to the analog precoder, and the precoded signal is directed towards to RIS. On the receiver side, the adaptive beamforming is executed by the LMS algorithm, where the weights are adjusted optimally using the NLO algorithm. Then, the power allocation is performed by ResNeXt, where the power allocation performance is enhanced by fine-tuning the parameters, like bias and weights of ResNeXt, using the FraNLO algorithm. Further, the supremacy of ResFraNLO is investigated, where the ResFraNLO attained superior performance with RMSE of 1.34 × 10–7, BER of 5.71 × 10–9, and achievable sum rate of 79.099 Mbps/Hz. Future research endeavours will be on the utilization of hybrid deep learning methods for power allocation in the mMIMO framework.

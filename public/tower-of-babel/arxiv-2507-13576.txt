A Proposed Characterization of p p -Simulation Between Theories
Hunter Monroe

Abstract
This paper proposes a characterization of when one axiomatic theory, as a proof system for tautologies, p p -simulates another, by showing: (i) if c.e. theory 𝒮 \mathcal{S} efficiently interprets 𝒮 + ϕ \mathcal{S}{+}\phi , then 𝒮 \mathcal{S} p p -simulates 𝒮 + ϕ \mathcal{S}{+}\phi (Jeřábek in Pudlák17 proved simulation), since the interpretation maps an 𝒮 + ϕ \mathcal{S}{+}\phi -proof whose lines are all theorems into an 𝒮 \mathcal{S} -proof; (ii) 𝒮 \mathcal{S} proves “ 𝒮 \mathcal{S} efficiently interprets 𝒮 + ϕ \mathcal{S}{+}\phi ” iff 𝒮 \mathcal{S} proves “ 𝒮 \mathcal{S} p p -simulates 𝒮 + ϕ \mathcal{S}{+}\phi ” (if so, 𝒮 \mathcal{S} already proves the Π 1 \Pi_{1} theorems of 𝒮 + ϕ \mathcal{S}{+}\phi ). To explore whether this framework conceivably resolves other open questions, the paper formulates conjectures stronger than “no optimal proof system exists” that imply Feige’s Hypothesis, the existence of one-way functions, and circuit lower bounds.

6
Conclusion

We contend that a central focus of complexity theory research should be proposing characterizations of when
\mathcal{S}
simulates
\mathcal{S}{+}\phi
, which would also characterize the hard-to-prove
P
-uniform tautology families of
\mathcal{S}
(Theorem
4.3
). Every unprovability result in logic is a nonrelativizing fact, since
\mathcal{S}{+}TRUE
can prove any true sentence. The literature has thoroughly explored whether resolving
\textbf{P}{\neq}\textbf{NP}
requires tools from logic, and a similarly thoroughly exploration is needed for each other open question. Pessimism that open complexity questions are out of reach risks creating a self-fulfilling lack of progress. The stakes, including whether theorem-proving is trivial and whether secure encryption exists, are extremely high. It is implausible in our view that the fault lies in our stars, and that nature has placed such fundamental questions beyond reach.

The above results and conjectures can be seen as an effort to unifying proof complexity and mathematical logic into a single theory. The provable
p
-simulation and interpretability hierarchies coincide (Theorem
3.3
).

An interesting research program could seek to identify, for each open question in complexity theory, a conjecture stronger than ‘‘no optimal proof system exists’’ that implies the question has the expected answer. For instance, a conjecture implying separations between space-based complexity classes might refer to sentences whose proofs are inherently space hungry, needing many lemmas that must be discharged at once.

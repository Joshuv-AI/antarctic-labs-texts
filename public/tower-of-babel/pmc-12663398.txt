Exploring the exact solutions of the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation with advection noise in fluid dynamics
Authors: Sofian T. Obeidat, Doaa Rizk, Wael W. Mohammed
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In this study, we consider the stochastic Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (SKS) equation driven by advection noise. We show that its exact solution can be obtained by first solving a corresponding deterministic form of the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation (DKS) and then combining the results with the solution of a stochastic ordinary differential equation. In the absence of noise, we extend some earlier results. Additionally, MATLAB software is employed to generate several 3D plots that illustrate and analyze the influence of advection noise on the solutions of the SKS equation.

Introduction

The study of partial differential equations (PDEs) is critical to understanding a wide range of natural and artificial systems. PDEs describe how physical, chemical, and biological processes change over time and place, such as fluid dynamics, heat conduction, wave propagation, population dynamics, and pattern development. Obtaining exact or approximate solutions provides vital insights into the qualitative behavior and stability of complex systems. Consequently, exploring new solution methods for nonlinear PDEs contributes not only to mathematical theory but also to advancing applications in physics, engineering, and applied sciences. Recently, many authors have used a number of methods to obtain the exact solutions, such as the generalized Arnous method1, the new extended direct algebraic method2, the sardar sub-equation method3, the generalized expansion technique4, the direct algebraic method5, the csch method6, the hyperbolic function method7, the sub-ode method8, extended modified auxiliary equation mapping9, the sine-cosine method10 Hirota bilinear transformation technique11 and the bilinear neural network method12.

Among these equations, the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (KS) equation stands out as a mathematical model that captures the dynamics of nonlinear systems, particularly in pattern formation and fluid dynamics. It is well known for describing the evolution of thin film flows, surface reactions, and combustion fronts, where complex wave patterns and instabilities emerge13–17.

On the other hand, incorporating randomness allows the stochastic version to more accurately represent real-world phenomena, where numerous factors contribute to unpredictable behavior. Furthermore, investigating the stochastic Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (SKS) equation opens the door to valuable applications across various fields. For instance, in engineering, understanding material behavior under uncertain conditions can enhance the design of products such as coatings and paints. In environmental science, it can also deepen our insight into the dispersion of pollutants in air or water.

Our motivation of this article is to obtain the solutions of the SKS Eq. ( 1) with advection noise. Two equations are extracted from the SKS equation to do this. The first equation is the stochastic ordinary differential equation (SODE), and the second is the deterministic Kuramoto-Sivashinsky (DKS) equation, which is obtained from the SKS equation by applying appropriate transformation techniques and Itô calculus. The solutions of the DKS equation are obtained by applying the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(\frac{\mathcal {G }^{\prime }}{\mathcal {G}})$$\end{document}-expansion method. Then, we obtain the solutions of the SKS Eq. (1) by combining our results with a solution of the SODE. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to examine the SKS Eq. (1) under the influence of advection noise. All previous works have focused on the SKS Eq. (1) with multiplicative noise of the form \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon \Psi dB$$\end{document}. By incorporating noise into the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky framework, researchers can create models that better reflect the reality of these systems. For instance, knowing how advection noise influences wave patterns can improve forecasts for storm behavior or the dispersion of pollutants, ultimately contributing to better public safety measures.

Preliminaries

Brownian motion {\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B(t),\ t\ge 0$$\end{document}} is a physical phenomenon observed when tiny particles are suspended in a fluid, such as air or water, and exhibit erratic, zigzag movements31. This movement was first observed by the botanist Robert Brown in 1827 while he was studying pollen grains in water. The particles do not move in a straight line; instead, they seem to dance around randomly. This behavior is due to the constant collisions between the suspended particles and the molecules of the fluid surrounding them. As the molecules of the fluid are in perpetual motion, they collide with the larger particles, causing them to move in unpredictable directions. The study of Brownian motion helps in understanding the microscopic world and the nature of matter at a fundamental level.

The importance of Brownian motion extends beyond mere observation; it has crucial implications in various scientific fields. In physics, it provides insights into the kinetic theory of gases and fluids, which explains how macroscopic properties of matter arise from microscopic behavior. In chemistry, Brownian motion plays a significant role in diffusion processes, where particles spread from areas of high concentration to areas of low concentration. This understanding is vital in fields such as pharmaceuticals, where drug delivery systems rely on the precise movement of particles in the bloodstream.

Discussion and noise effect

Multiplicity advection noise refers to a scenario where noise is not simply added to the system but is instead coupled with the flow itself. This characteristic can significantly affect how the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation behaves. For instance, the noise can interact with the patterns formed by the equation, altering their structure and leading to new unexpected behaviors. By studying this noise, scientists can gain insights into real-world processes, such as how pollutants disperse in turbulent fluid flows or how reaction-diffusion systems in biology function.

Moreover, studying multiplicative noise in the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation can help researchers understand the transition between ordered and chaotic states in dynamical systems. Scientists can observe how the introduction of noise influences stability and leads to various patterns, such as waves and spirals. This knowledge is not only theoretical but can also be applied to practical problems in engineering, meteorology, and environmental science. For example, understanding how noise affects fluid dynamics can lead to better predictions of weather patterns or improved designs for chemical reactors.

From Figs. 1, 2 and 3, we see that the influence of advection noise on the solutions of the stochastic Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (SKS) equation is clearly reflected in the progressive distortion of the wave profiles. In the deterministic case (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon =0$$\end{document}), the solution exhibits a smooth and coherent surface, representing a stable traveling wave that evolves regularly over time. As the noise intensity increases, random perturbations appear, leading to noticeable fluctuations in amplitude and irregularities in the wave structure. For moderate noise levels, these perturbations grow stronger, resulting in partial loss of coherence and the emergence of complex, oscillatory patterns. When the noise becomes dominant, the wave fronts are completely disrupted, and the system transitions into a highly irregular and chaotic state. This behavior demonstrates how stochastic forcing can destabilize ordered wave propagation and induce a transition from smooth deterministic motion to noise-driven turbulence.

Conclusions

The stochastic Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (SKS) Eq. (1) driven by multiplicative noise was investigated in this study. By applying transformation techniques and Itô calculus, the SKS equation was decomposed into a stochastic ordinary differential equation (SODE) and a deterministic Kuramoto–Sivashinsky (DKS) equation. The solutions of the DKS equation were then obtained using the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(\frac{\mathcal {G}^{\prime }}{ \mathcal {G}})$$\end{document}-expansion method. Finally, the solutions of the SKS Eq. (1) were derived by combining these results with the solution of the corresponding SODE. Also, many results have been extended, such as those given in21. Moreover, by simulating specific solutions, we evaluated the impact of advective noise on the obtained solutions. The stochastic advection term causes local dispersion of solution features, such as random changes in peaks and troughs, enhanced roughness, and the appearance of high-frequency components. At low noise levels, the average wave shape is conserved; however, with higher noise levels, the system becomes unpredictable and exhibits complicated, turbulent-like behavior. In future work, we aim to derive exact solutions of the SKS Eq. (1) under additive noise and linear multiplicative fractional Brownian motion forcing.

On the moments of the mass of shrinking balls under the Critical 2 ​ d 2d Stochastic Heat Flow
Ziyang Liu, Nikos Zygouras

Abstract
. The Critical 2 ​ d 2d Stochastic Heat Flow (SHF) is a measure valued stochastic process on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} that defines a non-trivial solution to the two-dimensional stochastic heat equation with multiplicative space-time noise. Its one-time marginals are a.s. singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure, meaning that the mass they assign to shrinking balls decays to zero faster than their Lebesgue volume. In this work we explore the intermittency properties of the Critical 2d SHF by studying the asymptotics of the h h -th moment of the mass that it assigns to shrinking balls of radius ε \varepsilon and we determine that its ratio to the Lebesgue volume is of order ( log ⁡ 1 ε ) ( h 2 ) (\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon})^{{h\choose 2}} up to possible lower order corrections.



1.
Introduction

The Critical
2d
Stochastic Heat Flow (SHF) was constructed in
[
CSZ23a
]
as a non-trivial, i.e. non-constant and non-gaussian, solution
to the ill-posed two-dimensional Stochastic Heat Equation (SHE)

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u+\beta\xi u,\qquad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\xi
is a space-time white noise.
We refer to reviews
[
CSZ24
,
CSZ26
]
for an account of the larger context
and developments in the study of the model.

The solution to (
1.1
) lives in the space of generalised functions and, therefore,
multiplication is a priori not defined. So in order to construct a solution one has to first regularise the equation. One way to do so is
by mollification of the noise
\xi_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}j\big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\big)\xi(t,\mathrm{d}y)
, so that (
1.1
) admits a smooth solution

, which in fact can also be represented by a Feynman-Kac formula as

(1.2)

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{E}}_{x}\Big[\exp\Big(\beta\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{\varepsilon}(t-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s-\frac{\beta^{2}t}{2}\|j_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\Big)\Big],

with
B_{s}
being a two-dimensional Brownian motion whose expectation when starting from

is denoted by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{E}}_{x}
and
j_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}j(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
. Then one needs to establish whether a sensible limit can be defined when
\varepsilon\to 0
. As we will discuss below, for this to be the case a precise choice of
\beta
depending on
\varepsilon
will be required.

Another approach is by a discretisation scheme; in particular by a distinguished discretisation of the
Feynman-Kac formula, which is related to the model of
Directed Polymer in Random Environment (DPRE),
[
C17
,
Z24
]
.
The latter is determined by its partition function:

(1.3)

\displaystyle Z_{M,N}^{\beta}(x,y):=\mathrm{E}\Big[\exp\big(\sum^{N-1}_{n=M+1}(\beta\omega(n,S_{n})-\lambda(\beta))\Big)\mathds{1}_{\{S_{N}=y\}}\,|\,S_{M}=x\Big],

where

is a simple, two-dimensional random walk, whose law and expectation
are denoted, respectively, by
\mathrm{P}
and

and

is a family of i.i.d. random variables
with mean

The DPRE regularisation was the one followed in the construction of the Critical 2d SHF in
[

]

In either of these approaches, the singularity that the noise induces in two dimensions demands a particular choice

In the continuous approximation,

\displaystyle\beta^{2}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{2\pi}{\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}}\Big(1+\frac{\varrho+o(1)}{\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}}\Big),

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{t}(\varphi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(x)\,\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathds{1}),

Let

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}(g_{\varepsilon^{2}})\Big)^{2}\Big]\sim C_{t}\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon},\qquad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0$},

Our theorem leaves open whether higher moments grow, in the limit

In the subcritical case, lower bounds up to negligible errors—within the directed polymer framework and without reliance on the Gaussian Correlation Inequality—were obtained in

Before closing this introduction let us make a connection between our results and the notions of

intermittency
and
multifractality
. These notions are closely related but they are not identical;
see for example
[
KKX17
]
and further references therein.

Intermittency (see
[
CM94
]
)
refers to the phenomenon of a random object taking very high values with rather small probability. This is
captured by a nonlinear growth of its moments with respect to the order
h
of the moment.
In the case of random fields, this often leads to observing sparse, high peaks.

Multifractality (see
[
F13
,
BP24
]
), on the other hand, refers to the phenomenon of a random measure
exhibiting a range of scales in the structure of its high peaks.
The fractal spectrum of a random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is captured by the exponent
\xi(h)
in
the moment asymptotics

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\mu(B(x,\varepsilon))^{h}\big]\sim\varepsilon^{\xi(h)},\qquad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0$},

for
h\in[0,1]
. This notion is useful in determining the Hausdorff dimension of the support of the measure and
indicate phenomena of localisation (see
[
BP24
]
for further information).
The measure
\mu
is said to exhibit multifractality if the exponent
\xi(h)
is a nonlinear
function of
h
. The distinctive features between this formulation and the analogous formulation of intermittency
are the
small ball limit

\varepsilon\to 0
and the range of
h\in(0,1)
– for intermittency one is rather interested in
the case of
\varepsilon
being (typically) fixed and
h\to\infty
.

The result of Ganguly-Nam
[
GN25
]
establishes a strong form of intermittency for the Critical 2d SHF, while
our result that

(1.15)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\mathscr{Z}_{t}^{\vartheta}\big(B(x,\varepsilon)\big)^{h}\,\big]\sim\varepsilon^{2h}\,\big(\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\big)^{\frac{h(h-1)}{2}+o(1)},\qquad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0$ for $2\leq h\in\mathbb{N}$},

may suggest that the Critical 2d SHF exhibits multifractality at a logarithmic scale (the anticipated log-scale is
consistent with the
picture established in
[
CSZ25
]
that the Critical 2d SHF is in
{\mathcal{C}}^{0-}
).
It would be interesting
to formulate the (logarithmic) multifractality features of the Critical 2d SHF. In this regard, one would need to
develop methods complementary to those of the present article,
which would allow for asymptotics similar to (
1.15
) but for fractional moments
h\in[0,1]
.
We conjecture that asymptotic (
1.15
) extends to
h\in[0,1]
.

The first moment of the Critical 2d SHF is given by

where

is the Euler constant and

For

Proposition 2.1.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\vartheta}(t)e^{-\lambda t}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{(\vartheta-\gamma)s}t^{s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}e^{-\lambda t}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-\lambda t}\mathrm{d}t\right)\frac{e^{(\vartheta-\gamma)s}}{\Gamma(s)}\mathrm{d}s

3.
Upper bound

In this section we prove the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
. The main estimate is contained in
the following proposition:

Proposition 3.1.

Recall the definition of
\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}
from (
2.8
).
For any
\delta>0
,
h\geq 2
and
\vartheta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}\leq\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{{h\choose 2}+o(1)},\qquad\text{as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0$}.

Having the above estimate at hand we can deduce
the upper bound in (
1.13
) as follows:

We have the comparison:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\pi\varepsilon^{2}}\mathds{1}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}(\cdot)\leq eg_{\varepsilon^{2}/2}(\cdot).

Hence, by Proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}\left({\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\right)\right)^{h}\bigg]\leq e^{h}\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}\leq e^{h}\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{{h\choose 2}+o(1)}.

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
∎The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
As a warm up computation, we start with the following preliminary estimate on

\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}
:

Lemma 3.2.

For
0<\varepsilon<1
, the following estimate holds:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathscr{I}_{m,h,\varepsilon}

where
\mathscr{I}_{0,h,\varepsilon}=1
,
\mathscr{I}_{1,h,\varepsilon}=C{h\choose 2}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
for some
C>0
, and for
m\geq 2
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{m,h,\varepsilon}:={h\choose 2}\left[{h\choose 2}-1\right]^{m-1}\hskip-14.22636pt\idotsint\limits_{\sum_{i}(u_{i}+v_{i})\leq 1+\varepsilon^{2}\,,\,u_{1}>\varepsilon^{2}}\hskip-2.84544pt\frac{1}{u_{1}}\prod_{1\leq r\leq m-1}\frac{G_{\vartheta}(v_{r})}{\frac{1}{2}(v_{r}+u_{r})+u_{r+1}}\,G_{\vartheta}(v_{m})\,\mathrm{d}\vec{u}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{v}

We work with (
2.9
).
The
m=0

1
. The
m=1

(3.4)

\displaystyle 2\pi\sum_{i,j\in\{1,...,h\}}\iint_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon^{2}\leq a<b\leq 1+\varepsilon^{2}}}{{x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}}}\quad g_{\frac{a}{2}}(x)^{2}G_{\vartheta}(b-a)g_{\frac{b-a}{4}}(y-x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b.

To simplify notations, we extend the integral
\iint_{\varepsilon^{2}\leq a<b\leq 1+\varepsilon^{2}}(...)\mathrm{d}a\mathrm{d}b
to
\iint_{\varepsilon^{2}\leq a<b\leq 2}(...)\mathrm{d}a\mathrm{d}b
. We first perform the integration over
y
, which gives

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}g_{\frac{b-a}{4}}(y-x)\mathrm{d}y=1.

Then by Proposition
2.2
, we integrate over

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{2}G_{\vartheta}(b-a)\mathrm{d}b\leq C.

Therefore we bound (
3.4

\displaystyle C\sum_{i,j\in\{1,...,h\}}\iint_{\varepsilon^{2}\leq a\leq 2\,,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\quad g_{\frac{a}{2}}(x)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=C{h\choose 2}\iint_{\varepsilon^{2}\leq a\leq 2\,,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}g_{\frac{a}{2}}(x)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=C{h\choose 2}\int_{\varepsilon^{2}}^{2}g_{a}(0)\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=C{h\choose 2}\log(\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}})\leq C{h\choose 2}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}).

Now we treat the case

. We will follow the convention that

. and recall the convention that if

(b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r})},y_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r})})=(0,0)

We start by performing the integration over
y_{m}
, which amounts to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g_{\frac{b_{m}-a_{m}}{4}}(y_{m}-x_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y_{m}=1.

Next we integrate
x_{m}
.

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}g_{\frac{a_{m}-b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{m})}}{2}}\big(x_{m}-y_{\mathsf{p}(i_{m})}\big)\,g_{\frac{a_{m}-b_{\mathsf{p}(j_{m})}}{2}}\big(x_{m}-y_{\mathsf{p}(j_{m})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x_{m}

\displaystyle=g_{a_{m}-\frac{1}{2}(b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{m})}+b_{\mathsf{p}(j_{m})})}\big(y_{\mathsf{p}(i_{m})}-y_{\mathsf{p}(j_{m})}\big)\leq\frac{1/\pi}{2a_{m}-(b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{m})}\!+b_{\mathsf{p}(j_{m})})}\leq\frac{1/\pi}{(a_{m}-b_{m-1})+(a_{m}-b_{m-2})},

and changing variables as

\displaystyle v_{i}:=b_{i}-a_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{i}:=a_{i}-b_{i-1}.

∎We will next bound (

Lemma 3.3 (Integration of the replica variables).

There exists a constant

(3.5)

\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}\leq Ce^{2\lambda}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{m,h,\varepsilon}

For

, to simplify notationally, we extend the integral in (

) to
\sum_{i}(u_{i}+v_{i})<2

We next introduce the multipliers.

to obtain the bound

(3.8)

\begin{split}\mathscr{I}_{m,h,\varepsilon}&\leq e^{2\lambda}{h\choose 2}\left[{h\choose 2}-1\right]^{m-1}\hskip-14.22636pt\idotsint\limits_{\sum_{i}(u_{i}+v_{i})\leq 2\,,\,u_{1}>\varepsilon^{2}}\hskip-14.22636pt\frac{1}{u_{1}}\prod_{1\leq r\leq m-1}\frac{e^{-\lambda v_{r}}\,G_{\vartheta}(v_{r})}{\frac{1}{2}(v_{r}+u_{r})+u_{r+1}}\,G_{\vartheta}(v_{m})\,\,\mathrm{d}\vec{u}\mathrm{d}\vec{v}.\end{split}

Next we integrate all the

(3.9)

\int_{0}^{2}G_{\vartheta}(v_{m})e^{-\lambda v_{m}}\mathrm{d}v_{m}\leq\int_{0}^{2}G_{\vartheta}(v_{m})\mathrm{d}v_{m}\leq C

which follows from Proposition
2.2
. For the rest of the

that for any

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\frac{e^{-\lambda v}\,G_{\vartheta}(v)}{v/2+w}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int^{2}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\sigma(v/2+w)}e^{-\lambda v}G_{\vartheta}(v)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{d}\sigma\,e^{-\sigma w}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-(\lambda+\sigma/2)v}\,G_{\vartheta}(v)\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{e^{-\sigma w}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-\vartheta+\gamma},

where in the last step we used Proposition

. For the last formula to be valid we need,

.
To conclude, we choose

and insert successively for

.
∎

The next step is to integrate over the
u
variables in (
3.6
). Our approach here is
inspired by
[

]
.
However, some details are rather different as we make use of the multiplier

To start with we define:

(3.11)

f_{\lambda}(w):=\int^{2}_{w}F_{\lambda}(v)\mathrm{d}v=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{e^{-\sigma w}-e^{-2\sigma}}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Note that
f^{\prime}_{\lambda}=-F_{\lambda}\leq 0
on
(0,2]
, as
F
is non-negative, thus,
f
is non-increasing.
We have the following Lemma:

Lemma 3.4.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
\lambda>\left(e^{2(\vartheta-\gamma)}\vee 1\right)

and
w\in(0,1)
we have:

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}F_{\lambda}(u+w)f_{\lambda}(u)^{j}\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\sum^{j+1}_{\ell=0}\frac{j!}{(j+1-\ell)!}\Big(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\Big)^{\ell}f_{\lambda}(2w)^{j+1-\ell}.

We start using the monotonicity of
F_{\lambda}
and noting that for
\lambda>\text{$e^{2(\vartheta-\gamma)}$}

and

:

(3.13)

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}(u+w)

\displaystyle\leq F_{\lambda}(w)=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{e^{-\sigma w}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)}\leq 2\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{e^{-\sigma w}}{\log\lambda}\mathrm{d}\sigma=\frac{2}{w\log\lambda}.

We next split the integral on the left-hand side of (
3.12

\int_{0}^{2w}(\cdots)\mathrm{d}u

and
\int_{2w}^{2}(\cdots)\mathrm{d}u
, which we call

and

We start by estimating integral

. By (

) we have,

(3.14)

\begin{split}I&=\int^{2w}_{0}F_{\lambda}(u+w)f_{\lambda}(u)^{j}\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{2}{w\log\lambda}\int^{2w}_{0}f_{\lambda}(u)^{j}\mathrm{d}u\\
\end{split}.

By integration by parts we have,

\begin{split}\int^{2w}_{0}f_{\lambda}(u)^{j}du&=2wf_{\lambda}(2w)^{j}-j\int^{2w}_{0}uf^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u)f_{\lambda}(u)^{j-1}\mathrm{d}u\\
&\leq 2wf_{\lambda}(2w)^{j}+j\frac{2}{\log\lambda}\int^{2w}_{0}f_{\lambda}(u)^{j-1}\mathrm{d}u,\end{split}

where in the inequality we used (

) and
-uf_{\lambda}(u)^{\prime}=uF_{\lambda}(u)\leq\frac{2}{\log\lambda}
. Iterating this computation we have that, for

\displaystyle\int^{2w}_{0}f_{\lambda}(u)^{j}\mathrm{d}u\leq 2w\sum^{j}_{i=0}\frac{j!}{(j-i)!}\Big(\frac{2}{\log\lambda}\Big)^{i}f_{\lambda}(2w)^{j-i},

and so

\displaystyle I\leq\sum^{j}_{i=0}\frac{j!}{(j-i)!}\Big(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\Big)^{i+1}f_{\lambda}(2w)^{j-i}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle II:=\int_{2w}^{2}F_{\lambda}(u+w)f_{\lambda}(u)^{j}\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\int^{2}_{2w}F_{\lambda}(u)f_{\lambda}(u)^{j}\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{j+1}f_{\lambda}(2w)^{j+1},

where we used the monotonicity of
F
and the fact that

. This completes the proof.

Lemma 3.5.

Fix

and

\idotsint\limits_{\sum^{m}_{i=m-k+1}u_{i}\leq 2}\,\,\prod^{m}_{r=m-k+1}F_{\lambda}\big(u_{r}+\frac{u_{r-1}}{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}u_{r}\leq\sum^{k}_{i=0}\frac{c^{k}_{i}}{(k-i)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}f_{\lambda}\left(u_{m-k}\right)^{k-i}

where

\displaystyle c^{0}_{0}

\displaystyle=1;\,c^{k}_{i}=0\,\text{ for $i>k$}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c^{k+1}_{i}=\sum^{i}_{j=0}c^{k}_{j}\qquad\text{ for $i\leq k+1$}.

When

\begin{split}&\idotsint\limits_{\sum_{i=m-k}^{m}u_{i}\leq 2}\prod^{m}_{r=m-k}F_{\lambda}(u_{r}+\frac{u_{r-1}}{2})\,\prod^{m}_{r=m-k}\mathrm{d}u_{r}\\

&\leq\int^{2}_{0}\sum^{k}_{i=0}\frac{c^{k}_{i}}{(k-i)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}f_{\lambda}\left(u_{m-k}\right)^{k-i}F_{\lambda}(u_{m-k}+\frac{u_{m-k-1}}{2})\,\mathrm{d}u_{m-k}\,.\end{split}

Then by Lemma

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=0}\frac{c^{k}_{i}}{(k-i)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\,\ \sum^{k-i+1}_{l=0}\frac{(k-i)!}{(k-i+1-l)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{l}f_{\lambda}\left(u_{m-k-1}\right)^{k-i+1-l}

\displaystyle=\sum^{k}_{i=0}\sum^{k-i+1}_{l=0}\frac{c^{k}_{i}}{(k+1-(i+l))!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i+l}f_{\lambda}\left(u_{m-k-1}\right)^{k+1-(i+l)}

By the definition of

\begin{split}\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}&\leq Ce^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\sum_{m\geq 0}{h\choose 2}\left[{h\choose 2}-1\right]^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{c_{i}^{m}}{(m-i)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})^{m-i}.\end{split}

For

):

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{m,h,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq{h\choose 2}\left[{h\choose 2}-1\right]^{m-1}\idotsint\limits_{\sum_{i}u_{i}\leq 2\,,\,u_{1}>\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{1}{u_{1}}\prod^{m}_{r=2}F_{\lambda}\big(u_{r}+\frac{u_{r-1}}{2}\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\vec{u}.

By Lemma
3.5
for
k=m-1
we have that:

(3.19)

\begin{split}\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{m,h,\varepsilon}&\leq{h\choose 2}\left[{h\choose 2}-1\right]^{m-1}\int^{2}_{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum^{m-1}_{i=0}\frac{c_{i}^{m-1}f_{\lambda}(u_{1})^{m-1-i}}{(m-1-i)!}\Big(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\Big)^{i}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}}{u_{1}}\,.\end{split}

Since
f_{\lambda}
is decreasing we have that
f_{\lambda}(u_{1})\leq f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})
, so for

u_{1}\geq\varepsilon^{2}
:

(3.20)

\displaystyle\int^{2}_{\varepsilon^{2}}f_{\lambda}(u_{1})^{m-1-i}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}}{u_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{2}_{\varepsilon^{2}}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})^{m-1-i}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}}{u_{1}}\leq C\log\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})^{m-1-i}\,.

Therefore, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}\leq Ce^{2\lambda}\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{m,h,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{2\lambda}\left(\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{0,h,\varepsilon}+\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{1,h,\varepsilon}+\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{m\geq 2}{h\choose 2}\left[{h\choose 2}-1\right]^{m-1}\sum^{m-1}_{i=0}\frac{c_{i}^{m-1}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})^{m-1-i}}{(m-1-i)!}\Big(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\Big)^{i}\,\right)

\displaystyle=Ce^{2\lambda}\left(\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{0,h,\varepsilon}+\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{1,h,\varepsilon}+\left({h\choose 2}-1\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{m\geq 1}{h\choose 2}\left[{h\choose 2}-1\right]^{m-1}\sum^{m}_{i=0}\frac{c_{i}^{m}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})^{m-i}}{(m-i)!}\Big(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\Big)^{i}\,\right)\,,

where in the last step we change the variable
m\mapsto m+1
. The result follows by recalling the definitions of

\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{0,h,\varepsilon}
and
\mathscr{I}^{(\lambda)}_{1,h,\varepsilon}
from Lemmas
3.2
and

3.3
.
∎

3.1.
Combinatorial coeffiients

We will derive an exact formula for
c^{m}_{i}
defined in (
3.16
).

Lemma 3.7.

For
m\geq i\geq 0
, we have:

(3.21)

\displaystyle c^{m}_{i}=\frac{m-i+1}{i!}\frac{(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}\leq 4^{m}\,.

Given the first equality, the inequality is obvious. Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{m-i+1}{i!}\frac{(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}\leq\frac{(m+i)!}{i!m!}={m+i\choose i}\leq{2m\choose i}\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq 2m}{2m\choose i}\leq 4^{m}.

To prove the equality, first notice that by definition of
c^{m}_{i}
, we could simplify the recursion to:

(3.22)

\displaystyle c^{m}_{i}=\sum^{i}_{j=0}c^{m-1}_{j}=c^{m-1}_{i}+\mathds{1}_{\{i>0\}}\sum^{i-1}_{j=0}c^{m-1}_{j}=c^{m-1}_{i}+c^{m}_{i-1}\mathds{1}_{\{i>0\}}\,,

for all
1\leq i\leq m
. It then suffices to verify that the equality of (
3.21
) solves the recursion formula (
3.22
) with boundary conditions
c^{0}_{0}=1
and
c^{m}_{m+1}=0
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Suppose first
m\geq 0
with
0<i<m
; then:

\displaystyle c^{m}_{i}=\frac{m-i+1}{i!}\frac{(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{m-i+1}{i!}\frac{(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}\left(\frac{m-i}{m-i+1}\frac{m+1}{m+i}+\frac{m-i+2}{m-i+1}\frac{i}{m+i}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{m-i}{i!}\frac{(m+i-1)!}{m!}+\frac{m-i+2}{(i-1)!}\frac{(m+i-1)!}{(m+1)!}=c^{m-1}_{i}+c^{m}_{i-1},

which is exactly (
3.22
). Next suppose that
0<i=m
. In this case,

\displaystyle c^{m}_{m}=\frac{m-m+1}{m!}\frac{(m+m)!}{(m+1)!}=0+\frac{m-m+2}{(m-1)!}\frac{(m+m-1)!}{(m+1)!}=c^{m-1}_{m}+c^{m}_{m-1}\,,

which also agrees with (
3.22
). Lastly, for
i=0
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we can readily check that
c^{m}_{0}=1
from the claimed formula,
which again coincides with (
3.22
) with the initial condition
c^{0}_{0}=1
.
∎

3.2.
Final step

We will next bound (
3.18
) by the upper bound claimed in Theorem
1.2
and hence complete the proof. The following asymptotic behavior of
f_{\lambda}
will be useful:

Lemma 3.8.

Suppose that
\lambda>e^{\vartheta-\gamma+\frac{1}{2}}
. There exists
C>0
and
\delta_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{C}{\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}

such that for all
u\in(0,\varepsilon^{2}]
:

(3.23)

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(u)\leq(1+\delta_{\varepsilon})\log\log\frac{1}{u}.

Recall (
3.11
). Let
C_{\vartheta}:=e^{2\left(\log 2+\vartheta-\gamma\right)}
. Without loss of generality,
assume that
\varepsilon
is small enough, so that for
u\leq\varepsilon^{2}
,
1/u>C_{\vartheta}
. We next have,

(3.24)

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(u)=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{1}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-\vartheta-\gamma)}(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

(3.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{C_{\vartheta}}_{0}\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{1}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)}(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

(3.26)

\displaystyle+\int^{\frac{1}{u}}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{1}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)}(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

(3.27)

\displaystyle+\int^{\infty}_{\frac{1}{u}}\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{1}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)}(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

We see that in (
3.25
), the integrand is bounded. Indeed, given the assumption on
\lambda
, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{1}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)}(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\left(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma}\right)}{\sigma}<C_{1},

for some finite constant
C_{1}
.
For (
3.27
), we notice that:

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{\frac{1}{u}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}\frac{1}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)}(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma})

\displaystyle\leq 2\int^{\infty}_{\frac{1}{u}}(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int^{\infty}_{\frac{1}{u}}e^{-\sigma u}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}=2\int^{\infty}_{1}e^{-\sigma}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}\,<C_{2},

for some finite constant
C_{2}
, given the assumption that
u\leq\varepsilon^{2}
and
\varepsilon
is assumed to
be small enough.
We claim that the main growth in (
3.24
) comes from (
3.26
). To this end, we have:

(3.28)

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{1}{u}}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}\frac{1}{\log(\lambda+\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)}(e^{-\sigma u}-e^{-2\sigma})

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\frac{1}{u}}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma\left(\log(\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)\right)},

Notice that:

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{1}{u}}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma\left(\log(\sigma/2)-(\vartheta-\gamma)\right)}-\int^{\frac{1}{u}}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma\log\sigma}

\displaystyle=\int^{\frac{1}{u}}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{\log\sigma-\left(\log 2+(\vartheta-\gamma)\right)}-\frac{1}{\log\sigma}\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\infty}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{\log 2+(\vartheta-\gamma)}{\left(\log\sigma\right)^{2}-\left(\log 2+(\vartheta-\gamma)\right)\log\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\log 2+(\vartheta-\gamma)\right)\int^{\infty}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma\left(\log\sigma\right)^{2}}<C_{3}.

for some finite constant
C_{3}
, where we also used the assumption that
C_{\vartheta}:=e^{2\left(\log 2+\vartheta-\gamma\right)}
.
Therefore, we can bound (
3.28
) by:

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{1}{u}}_{C_{\vartheta}}\frac{1}{\sigma\log\sigma}\mathrm{d}\sigma+C_{3}\leq\log\log\frac{1}{u}+C,

for some finite constant
C
.
Putting the bounds for (
3.25
)-(
3.27
) together we obtain:

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(u)\leq\log\log\frac{1}{u}+C\leq(1+\frac{C}{\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}})\log\log\frac{1}{u},

for some
C>0
, when
u\leq\varepsilon^{2}
, from which the result follows.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
3.1
.

We build upon (
3.18
):

(3.29)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{i:i\leq m}{h\choose 2}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m-1}c^{m}_{i}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})}{(m-i)!}

(3.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{m:m\geq i}{h\choose 2}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m-1}c^{m}_{i}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})}{(m-i)!}

(3.31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq i}\mathds{1}_{m\geq k}{h\choose 2}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m-1}c^{m}_{i}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})}{(m-i)!}

(3.32)

\displaystyle+Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq i}\mathds{1}_{m<k}{h\choose 2}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m-1}c^{m}_{i}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})}{(m-i)!}\,,

where we break the sum over
m
at
k=C_{0}\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
for some
C_{0}>0
to be chosen later. We handle (
3.31
) first.
We use the bound
c^{m}_{i}\leq 4^{m}
from (
3.21
), to estimate (
3.31
) by:

\displaystyle Ce^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq i}\mathds{1}_{m\geq k}{h\choose 2}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m-1}4^{m}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{f_{\lambda}^{m-i}(\varepsilon^{2})}{(m-i)!}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq i}\mathds{1}_{m\geq k}\left(\frac{h^{2}}{2}\right)^{m}4^{m}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{f_{\lambda}^{m-i}(\varepsilon^{2})}{(m-i)!}

\displaystyle=Ce^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq i}\mathds{1}_{m\geq k}\left(\frac{8h^{2}}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{\left(2h^{2}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})\right)^{m-i}}{(m-i)!}\,.

Now we introduce a new multiplier,
\mu>0
. As
\mathds{1}_{m\geq k}\leq e^{(m-k)\mu}
, the above is bounded by:

(3.33)

\begin{split}&Ce^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq i}e^{(m-k)\mu}\left(\frac{8h^{2}}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{\left(2h^{2}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})\right)^{m-i}}{(m-i)!}\\
=\,&Ce^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})e^{-k\mu}\sum_{i\geq 0}\left(\frac{8e^{\mu}h^{2}}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\sum_{m:m\geq i}e^{(m-i)\mu}\frac{\left(2h^{2}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})\right)^{m-i}}{(m-i)!}\,.\end{split}

We choose
\lambda>\exp(8e^{\mu}h^{2})
so that

(3.34)

\displaystyle C\sum_{i\geq 0}\left(\frac{8e^{\mu}h^{2}}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}=:C_{\lambda,h}<\infty.

Hence, if we also change variables
n:=m-i
, (
3.33
) is bounded by:

(3.35)

\begin{split}&C_{\lambda,h}e^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})e^{-k\mu}\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{n\mu}\frac{\left(2h^{2}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})\right)^{n}}{n!}\leq C_{\lambda,h}e^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})e^{-k\mu}e^{2h^{2}e^{\mu}f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})}\end{split}

We recall from (
3.23
) that
f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})\leq(1+\delta_{\varepsilon})\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and
that we chose
k=C_{0}\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
where we will take
C_{0}>0
such that:

(3.36)

\displaystyle C_{0}\mu-2e^{\mu}h^{2}=:D>1.

Then (
3.35
) is bounded above by

(3.37)

\displaystyle C_{\lambda,h}e^{2\lambda}\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})e^{-D\log\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})}=C_{\lambda,h}e^{2\lambda}\left(\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\right)^{1-D}\rightarrow 0

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Now we handle (
3.32
). By the equality part of (
3.21
), (
3.32
) becomes

(3.38)

\begin{split}&Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq i}\mathds{1}_{k>m}{h\choose 2}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m-1}\frac{m-i+1}{i!}\frac{(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})}{(m-i)!}\\
&=Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{0\leq m<k}\sum_{0\leq i\leq m}{h\choose 2}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m-1}\frac{m-i+1}{i!}\frac{(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}\frac{f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})}{(m-i)!}\\
&=Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{0\leq m<k}\sum_{0\leq i\leq m}{h\choose 2}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m-1}{m\choose i}\frac{m-i+1}{m!}\frac{(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})\\
&=Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}\sum_{0\leq m<k}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{i:0\leq i\leq m}{m\choose i}\frac{(m-i+1)\,(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2}).\end{split}

Notice that:

\displaystyle(m-i+1)\frac{(m+i)!}{(m+1)!}\leq\frac{(m+i)!}{m!}\leq(2m)^{i}.

Therefore, (
3.38
) is bounded by:

(3.39)

\displaystyle Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}\sum_{0\leq m\leq k}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{i:0\leq i\leq m}{m\choose i}(2m)^{i}\left(\frac{4}{\log\lambda}\right)^{i}f^{m-i}_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})

(3.40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}\sum_{0\leq m\leq k}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{8m}{\log\lambda}+f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})\right)^{m}

(3.41)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}\sum_{0\leq m<\infty}\bigg[{h\choose 2}-1\bigg]^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{8k}{\log\lambda}+f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})\right)^{m}

(3.42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}e^{\left({h\choose 2}-1\right)\left(f_{\lambda}(\varepsilon^{2})+\frac{8k}{\log\lambda}\right)}\,.

where we have completed a binomial sum (resp. an exponential sum) to obtain (
3.40
) (resp. (
3.42
)). Again, we recall the asymptotic behavior of
f
from (
3.23
) and definition of
k
, and bound (
3.42
) by:

(3.43)

\begin{split}&Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}e^{\left({h\choose 2}-1\right)\left((1+\delta_{\varepsilon})\log\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})+\frac{8C_{0}\log\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})}{\log\lambda}\right)}\\
&=Ce^{2\lambda}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}e^{\left({h\choose 2}-1\right)\left(1+\delta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{8C_{0}}{\log\lambda}\right)\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\\
&=Ce^{2\lambda}\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{{h\choose 2}\left(1+\delta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{8C_{0}}{\log\lambda}\right)}\,.\end{split}

Putting (
3.37
) and (
3.43
) together, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{2\lambda}\frac{{h\choose 2}}{{h\choose 2}-1}\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{{h\choose 2}(1+\delta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{8C_{0}}{\log\lambda})}+C_{\lambda,h}e^{2\lambda}\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1-D}

(3.44)

\displaystyle\leq C_{h}e^{2\lambda}\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{{h\choose 2}(1+\delta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{8C_{0}}{\log\lambda})}=C_{h}\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{{h\choose 2}}\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2\lambda}{\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+\delta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{8C_{0}}{\log\lambda}},

To achieve the desired result we set
\lambda=\lambda_{\varepsilon}
and we require:

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon,\lambda}:=\frac{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}}{\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+\delta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{8C_{0}}{\log\lambda_{\varepsilon}}=o(1),

Recall from Lemma
3.8
that
\delta_{\varepsilon}=O(1/\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
, so the optimising level of

. One choice would be
\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}{\log\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}
. Substituting into (

), we obtain for some

:

(3.45)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon}^{\vartheta,h}\leq\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{{h\choose 2}+\frac{C_{0}^{\prime}}{\log\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}.

∎

:

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2h}}g_{\varepsilon^{2}}(y_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{h}g_{\varepsilon^{2}/2}(y_{i}){\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{h}\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{\vartheta}(\mathds{1},\mathrm{d}y_{i})\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq 0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(2\pi)^{m}\hskip-34.14322pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},j_{1}\},...,\{i_{m},j_{m}\}\in\{1,...,h\}^{2}\\
\text{with $\{i_{k},j_{k}\}\neq\{i_{k+1},j_{k+1}\}$ for $k=1,...,m-1$}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon^{2}\leq a_{1}<b_{1}<...<a_{m}<b_{m}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\\
x_{1},y_{1},...,x_{m},y_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{subarray}}\hskip-36.98866pt\,\,\,g_{\frac{a_{1}}{2}}(x_{1})^{2}\,\,\prod_{r=1}^{m}G_{\vartheta}(b_{r}-a_{r})g_{\frac{b_{r}-a_{r}}{4}}(y_{r}-x_{r})\,\,\mathds{1}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{i_{r},j_{r}}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\prod_{1\leq r\leq m-1}g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})})\,g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})})\Big)\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{y}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{a}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{b}.

Note there is a difference at the integration range of
a_{1},b_{1},...,a_{m},b_{m}
. They are integrated up to
1+2\varepsilon^{2}
due to
g_{\varepsilon}^{2}(y_{1})
, which pushes the upper bound up by
2\varepsilon^{2}
instead of
\varepsilon^{2}
.

We also recall from (
2.9
) :

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}\left(g_{\varepsilon^{2}/2}\right)\right)^{h}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(2\pi)^{m}\hskip-34.14322pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},j_{1}\},...,\{i_{m},j_{m}\}\in\{1,...,h\}^{2}\\
\text{with $\{i_{k},j_{k}\}\neq\{i_{k+1},j_{k+1}\}$ for $k=1,...,m-1$}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon^{2}\leq a_{1}<b_{1}<...<a_{m}<b_{m}\leq 1+\varepsilon^{2}\\
x_{1},y_{1},...,x_{m},y_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{subarray}}\hskip-36.98866pt\,\,\,g_{\frac{a_{1}}{2}}(x_{1})^{2}\,\,\prod_{r=1}^{m}G_{\vartheta}(b_{r}-a_{r})g_{\frac{b_{r}-a_{r}}{4}}(y_{r}-x_{r})\,\,\mathds{1}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{i_{r},j_{r}}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\prod_{1\leq r\leq m-1}g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})})\,g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})})\Big)\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{y}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{a}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{b}.

Note that the only difference with (
4.5
)
is at the integration range of
a_{1},b_{1},...,a_{m},b_{m}
. Therefore, we have that:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2h}}g_{\varepsilon^{2}}(y_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{h}g_{\varepsilon^{2}/2}(y_{i}){\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{h}\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{\vartheta}(\mathds{1},\mathrm{d}y_{i})\bigg]-{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}\left(g_{\varepsilon^{2}/2}\right)\right)^{h}\bigg]\bigg|\leq\mathscr{D},

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{m\geq 0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(2\pi)^{m}\hskip-34.14322pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},j_{1}\},...,\{i_{m},j_{m}\}\in\{1,...,h\}^{2}\\
\text{with $\{i_{k},j_{k}\}\neq\{i_{k+1},j_{k+1}\}$ for $k=1,...,m-1$}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon^{2}<a_{1}<b_{1}<...<a_{m}<b_{m}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\\
1+\varepsilon^{2}<a_{i}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\text{ or }1+\varepsilon^{2}<b_{i}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\text{ for some $i=1,...,m$}\\
x_{1},y_{1},...,x_{m},y_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{subarray}}\hskip-36.98866pt\,\,\,g_{\frac{a_{1}}{2}}(x_{1})^{2}\,\,\prod_{r=1}^{m}G_{\vartheta}(b_{r}-a_{r})g_{\frac{b_{r}-a_{r}}{4}}(y_{r}-x_{r})\,\,\mathds{1}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{i_{r},j_{r}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\prod_{1\leq r\leq m-1}g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})})\,g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})})\Big)\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{y}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{a}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{b}.

We will control
\mathscr{D}
. First, notice that the constraint

\displaystyle 1+\varepsilon^{2}<a_{i}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\text{ or }1+\varepsilon^{2}<b_{i}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\text{ for some $i=1,...,m$},

implies
1+\varepsilon^{2}<b_{m}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}
. Therefore, we relax the constraint in
\mathscr{D}
to obtain:

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq 0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(2\pi)^{m}\hskip-34.14322pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},j_{1}\},...,\{i_{m},j_{m}\}\in\{1,...,h\}^{2}\\
\text{with $\{i_{k},j_{k}\}\neq\{i_{k+1},j_{k+1}\}$ for $k=1,...,m-1$}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon^{2}<a_{1}<b_{1}<...<a_{m}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\\
((1+\varepsilon^{2})\vee a_{m})<b_{m}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\\
x_{1},y_{1},...,x_{m},y_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{subarray}}\hskip-36.98866pt\,\,\,g_{\frac{a_{1}}{2}}(x_{1})^{2}\,\,\prod_{r=1}^{m}G_{\vartheta}(b_{r}-a_{r})g_{\frac{b_{r}-a_{r}}{4}}(y_{r}-x_{r})\,\,\mathds{1}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{i_{r},j_{r}}}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\prod_{1\leq r\leq m-1}g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})})\,g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})})\Big)\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{y}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{a}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{b}.

We first perform the integration over
y_{m}
:

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}g_{\frac{b_{m}-a_{m}}{4}}(y_{m}-x_{m})\mathrm{d}y_{m}=1.

By Proposition
2.2
, it is not difficult to see there exists
C=C_{\vartheta}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
:

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int^{1+2\varepsilon^{2}}_{a_{m}\vee(1+\varepsilon^{2})}G_{\vartheta}(b_{m}-a_{m})\mathrm{d}b_{m}\leq\frac{C}{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}.

So (
4.8
) becomes:

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\sum_{m\geq 0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(2\pi)^{m}\hskip-34.14322pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},j_{1}\},...,\{i_{m},j_{m}\}\in\{1,...,h\}^{2}\\
\text{with $\{i_{k},j_{k}\}\neq\{i_{k+1},j_{k+1}\}$ for $k=1,...,m-1$}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon^{2}<a_{1}<b_{1}<...<a_{m}\leq 1+2\varepsilon^{2}\\
x_{1},y_{1},...,x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{subarray}}\hskip-36.98866pt\,\,\,g_{\frac{a_{1}}{2}}(x_{1})^{2}\,\,\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}G_{\vartheta}(b_{r}-a_{r})g_{\frac{b_{r}-a_{r}}{4}}(y_{r}-x_{r})\,\,\mathds{1}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{i_{r},j_{r}}}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\prod_{1\leq r\leq m-1}g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(i_{r+1})})\,g_{\frac{a_{r+1}-b_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})}}{2}}(x_{r+1}-y_{\mathsf{p}(j_{r+1})})\Big)\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{\boldsymbol{y}}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{a}\,\mathrm{d}\vec{b}.

Then, following the same computation from Lemma
3.2
onwards in the upper bound section, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\leq\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\big(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\big)^{{h\choose 2}(1+o(1))}=o\Big(\big(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\big)^{{h\choose 2}}\Big).

Combining this with (
4.4
) and (
4.7
) by choosing
R>\sqrt{2\log\frac{2\left(2^{h}-1\right)}{\varrho}}
, we obtain the bound (
4.2
).
∎

We will first prove that for some fixed
R>0
, there exists
C=C(\vartheta,h)>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}({\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,R\varepsilon)})\right)^{h}\bigg]\geq C\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{h\choose 2},

and from this we will deduce the statement for
{\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}
.
For
R>1
we have:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,R\varepsilon)}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\pi R^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\mathds{1}_{B(0,R\varepsilon)}(\cdot)\geq\frac{1}{R^{2}}g_{\varepsilon^{2}/2}\mathds{1}_{B(0,R\varepsilon)}(\cdot).

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}({\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,R\varepsilon)})\right)^{h}\bigg]\geq\frac{1}{R^{2h}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}(g_{\varepsilon^{2}/2}\mathds{1}_{B(0,R\varepsilon)})\right)^{h}\bigg]\,.

By Lemma
4.1
, for any
\varrho\in(0,1)
, there exists
R>0
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}({\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,R\varepsilon)})\right)^{h}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{R^{2h}}\left((1-\varrho){\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}(g_{\varepsilon^{2}/2})\right)^{h}\bigg]+o\left(\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{h\choose 2}\right)\right)\,.

By (
1.14
) and (
4.1
), there exists
C=C(\vartheta,h)
such that
{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}(g_{\varepsilon^{2}/2})\right)^{h}\bigg]\geq C\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{h\choose 2}
. So we obtain the bound:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}({\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,R\varepsilon)})\right)^{h}\bigg]\geq\frac{C_{\varrho,\vartheta,h}}{R^{2h}}\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{h\choose 2}.

Now,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}({\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,\varepsilon)})\right)^{h}\bigg]={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\left(\mathscr{Z}^{\vartheta}_{1}({\mathcal{U}}_{B(0,R\times\frac{\varepsilon}{R})})\right)^{h}\bigg]\geq C_{\varrho,\vartheta,h,R}\left(\log\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\right)^{h\choose 2},

and we complete the proof.
∎

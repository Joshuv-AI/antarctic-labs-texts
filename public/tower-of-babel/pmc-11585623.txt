m6A regulator-mediated methylation modifications define the immune infiltration characteristics of the tumor microenvironment in prostate adenocarcinoma
Authors: Fajing Jia, Shaoze Jing, Jianjin Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractProstate adenocarcinoma (PRAD) persists as the predominant non-cutaneous malignancy diagnosed in males, which is a primary contributor to cancer-related mortality globally. It is reported that the progression of prostate adenocarcinoma is associated with various factors, including genetics, age, obesity, etc. Contemporary research indicates that epigenetic inheritance is a leading factor in the initiation and progression of cancer. RNA methylation modification is the most prevalent form of RNA modification, with N6-methyladenosine (m6A) representing the most common modification on mRNA and lncRNAs. However, the biological mechanisms underpinning this association in prostate adenocarcinoma and its correlation with patients’ prognostic survival outcomes remain elusive. Our study elucidates the roles of the tumor microenvironment (TME) and genetic mutations during the initiation and progression of prostate adenocarcinoma. Additionally, we stratify prostate adenocarcinoma into distinct subtypes based on m6A scoring. This approach enhances our comprehension of the functional role of m6A in the development of prostate adenocarcinoma, offering novel insights into the clinical strategies and understanding the biological significance between prostate adenocarcinoma and m6A modification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-77688-w.Keywords: M6A, Prostate adenocarcinoma, RNA methylation, Tumor microenvironment, Immune cell infiltrationSubject terms: Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics

Introduction

In various living organisms, more than 150 RNA modification methods have been discovered, which include 5-methylcytosine (m5C), n6-methyladenosine (m6A) and n1-methyladenosine (m1A)1,2. They all belong to the third layer of epigenetics. It is currently believed that among all these internal modifications of eukaryotic cells, the most significant and abundant form is m6A RNA methylation, about 0.1–0.4% of total adenosine residues3–5. Among various RNAs, such as mRNA, lncRNA and miRNA, m6A RNA methylation is quite widespread. In mammalian cells, the modification of m6A is a constantly changing and invertible process. This process is mediated by binding protein, methyltransferase, and demethylase. According to the different functions of these proteins, they are called “writer”, “erasers”, “reader”6. The methylation of m6A is catalyzed by CBLL1, KIAA1429, metttl14, metttl16, metttl3, RBM15, RBM15B, VIRMA, WTAP, ZC3H13 and many other methyltransferases. Correspondingly, the removing process is demethylated by ALKBH5, FTO, etc. It is worth mentioning that the motif of m6A can be recognized by a unique set of RNA binding proteins composed of YTHDF1/2/3, YTHDC1/2, HNRNPA2B1, LRPPRC, FMR1, etc., thereby changing its own role7,8. To study the principle of m6A modification in post-transcriptional adjustment, we need to have a better understanding of these regulatory factors. Research indicates that M6A regulatory factors are important to a multitude of physiological processes within the organism9–11. There is substantial evidence that the disruption of various biological processes, such as irregular regulation of cell death and proliferation9, developmental anomalies12, malignant tumor progression13, compromised self-renewal capacity, and abnormal immune regulation14, can lead to differing levels of m6A regulatory factor expression and gene activity.

Several patients exhibiting prolonged responses, therapeutic strategies exemplified by immune checkpoint blockade (ICB, PD-1/L1, TP53, and CTLA-4) have demonstrated remarkable clinical efficacy. The frustration of people today is that for most patients, it is difficult for them to obtain satisfactory results, and it is difficult to meet actual clinical needs. Conventional perspectives postulate that tumor progression encompasses multiple stages and is predominantly confined to the genetic and epigenetic alterations within tumor cells. However, more and more studies have shown that the TME also plays a very important role in tumor progression. TME has a very complex composition, including stromal cells, cells recruited from afar, bone marrow-derived cells (BMDCs), and factors secreted by these cells. For example, cytokines, chemokines, growth factors, and new blood vessels. Among these many components, 5 different myeloid cells make up tumor-associated myeloid cells (TAMCs), they are: tumor-associated macrophages (TAM), tumor-associated neutrophils (TANs), dendrites Shape cells, myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and monocytes expressing tie215. Accompanying the direct or indirect interaction with other TME constituents, such as the induction of proliferation and immune tolerance, inhibiting apoptosis, etc., a variety of biological behaviors can change. People’s understanding of the tumor microenvironment is getting deeper and deeper, and the understanding of tumor progression, immune escape and the factors that influence the response to immunotherapy is also increasing. In exploring novel immunotherapy approaches, it is crucial to predict the response to ICB by analyzing the characteristics of cell infiltration within the TME16. Therefore, further investigation into the heterogeneity and complexity of the TME landscape to identify various tumor immunophenotypes is crucial for enhancing immunotherapy responses. Additionally, discovering new biomarkers that can accurately predict a patient’s response to immunotherapy will help identify new treatment approaches and targets17. Many recent researches have explored the relationship between m6A modification and TME infiltration, and this relationship cannot be well explained by RNA degradation. According to the report by Dali et al., this anti-tumor response of tumor-infiltrating CD8 + T cells is enhanced by the binding of YTHDF1 to the transcript encoding m6A methylation-modified lysosomal protease. This combination not only inhibits the ability of cathepsin in DC to present tumor antigens, but also improves the translation ability of lysosomal cathepsin in dendritic cells (DCs). The efficacy of anti-pdl1 blockade is also improved in the process of inhibiting YTHDF118. Wang et al. found that the growth and activation of DC. The efficacy of anti-pdl1 blockade is also improved in the process of inhibiting are also very dependent on mettl3-mediated m6A modification. The ability of T cells to activate will also decrease with the specific exhaustion of METTL3. The principle is that the expression of the costimulatory molecules CD80 and CD40 decreases. Down-regulation of Tirap can reduce the secretion of cytokines, which is achieved by down-regulating the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway19. Similarly, some studies have focused on exploring the abnormal expression and genomic variation of m6A regulatory factors, and how they can induce cancer. For example, Qiang et al. found that both the malignant progression and liver metastasis of gastric cancer are related to the overexpression of mettl3, which may be achieved through angiogenesis and glycolysis20.

Prostate cancer is one of the most common malignancies in men, typically arising from the epithelial cells of the prostate gland. The relationship between prostate cancer and m6A modification has garnered increasing attention in recent years. Studies have shown that m6A modifications are involved in the initiation, progression, and treatment resistance of prostate cancer. Specifically, various m6A regulators, such as METTL3, FTO, and ALKBH5, have been found to influence tumor growth, cell proliferation, and metastasis11,21,22. Unfortunately, these studies are inevitably limited to one or two cell types and m6A regulation due to technical limitations. But the cornerstone of the anti-tumor effect depends on the highly coordinated action of multiple tumor suppressors. Therefore, to deepen the understanding the role of TME in immune regulation, we must comprehensively analyze the TME cell infiltration process mediated by multiple m6A regulatory factors. We systematically find Integrative genome data from 581 prostate samples to assess the overall m6A modification pattern and correlate it with the characteristics of TME cell infiltration. Three different m6A modification modes have been discovered. Surprisingly, the immune inflammation phenotype, immune desert phenotype, immune exclusion phenotype are highly consistent with TME characteristics of these three modes. This shows that the characteristics of individual tumor microenvironment are inseparable from the modification and shaping of m6A. For further exploration, we invented a scoring method for quantifying the m6A modification patterns of every single patient.

Methods

Collection and processing of prostate adenocarcinoma dataset

We first searched the data of public gene as well as complete clinical annotations. Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene-expression Omnibus (GEO) databases was used to perform these works. For patients who do not have survival-related information, no analysis will be performed further. In this study, two eligible PRAD cohorts (TCGA-PRAD (The Cancer Genome Atlas-Prostate Adenocarcinoma) and GSE29229) were included for further analysis. Besides, one LUDA cohort (TCGA-LUDA (The Cancer Genome Atlas Lung Adenocarcinoma)) was acquired. Download the normalized matrix file directly in the Agilent® microarray data. In the TCGA data set, the specially developed R package TCGAbiolinks is used for the integration and analysis of GDC data. In Genomic Data Sharing Center (GDC, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), we collected the sequencing data (FPKM value) of RNA gene expression23. The FPKM value is converted to a transcripts per kilobase million (TPM) values. Using the “battle” algorithm of the sva software package, the batch effect of non-biological technology deviations is corrected. Table S1 contains information on all qualified GC data sets. Finally, we excavated all cell mutations and Copy Number Variation (CNV) data in the TCGA database. This part of the data is analyzed using R (version 4.0.3) and R Bioconductor software packages.

Unsupervised cluster of 29m6a regulators

We extracted 29 regulatory factors from 2 qualified PRAD cohorts and used these regulatory factors to complete the identification of different m6A modification patterns. According to gene function,10 writers, 2 erasers and 17 readers are included in these 29 m6A regulators. First, the patients were classified, and then unsupervised cluster analysis was applied to determine their respective patterns of m6A modification according to 29 m6A regulatory factors’ expression. Next, we employed the consensus clustering algorithm using the ConsensusClusterPlus package to ascertain the number and stability of the aforementioned clusters24. To ensure the uniqueness of the different classifications, the number of clusters was determined by the consensus matrix. The classification basis is that the different categories exhibit the highest efficiency and least crossover among PRAD samples. Additionally, for the clustering of LUDA samples, we applied a similar method. The above steps were repeated 1000 times in total25.

Functional annotation and gene set variation analysis (GSVA)

The “gva” R package is used for gva enrichment analysis, and to explore the biological differences of m6A modification patterns. GSVA is a non-parametric, unsupervised method, which is generally used to analyze changes in biological activity and expression data sets26. Dataset “c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt” and “c5.go.v7.4.symbols.gmt” (MSigDB database) are used for GSVA analysis. A statistically significant difference was defined as an adjusted P value < 0.05. We used the clusterProfiler R package to annotate the m6a-related genes with a cutoff value of FDR < 0.05.

TME cell infiltration estimation

The ssGSEA (single-sample gene set enrichment analysis) algorithm is used to calculate the infiltration of each cell in the PRAD TME. Charoentong has studied a variety of human immune cell subtypes, such as activated DC, activated CD8 T cells, natural killer T cells, macrophages, regulatory T cells, etc. (Table S2). Each type of immune cell was marked by using Charoentong’s gene set. Next, we calculated the enrichment score obtained by ssGSEA and used this score to show the amount of TME infiltrating cells.

Authentication of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) among different phenotypes of m6A

We studied the expression level of 29 m6A regulatory factors and divided samples by different m6A modification patterns to analyze m6A-related genes. Then, we adopted the empirical Bayesian method to analyze and quantify the DEGs between different modification modes27. When the corrected P value is less than 0.001, DEG is considered to be significant.

Generation of m6A gene signature

We have invented a scoring standard to analyze the modification of m6A patterns of each patient with prostate adenocarcinoma, that is, m6A gene characteristics. This scoring standard can identify the modification pattern of m6A for individual tumors, and we named it m6A score. The process of establishing m6A gene characteristics is as follows:

We first extract DEGs from different m6 clusters and normalize them in PRAD samples to get their overlapping genes. Then we use the unsupervised clustering method to analyze the overlapping DEGs, and further analyze the patients based on the results of this step. Consensus clustering algorithm is used to determine the stability and number of gene clusters. By using a univariate Cox regression model, each gene in the signature was analyzed for prognosis. We selected different prognosis-related gene to in-depth analyzing. Principal component 1 and principal component 2 as feature scores was chosen to analyze the characteristics of m6A-related genes. The scores of the highly related (or irrelevant) gene blocks in the collection are relatively concentrated, and the genes that are not tracked by other collection members are weighted down, which is a significant advantage of this method. Statistical analysis

We calculated the correlation coefficient between TME infiltrating immune cells and m6A regulatory factor expression through Spearman and distance analysis. Next, we compared three or more sets of data28, which was achieved through One-way ANOVA and Kruskal-Wallis test. We analyzed the correlation between m6score and patient survival, and used the survminer R software package to calculate the cut-off point for each data set subgroup. When testing all possible cutting points multiple times, determine the maximum rank statistic to classify m6score, and then we select the largest log-rank statistic, divide the patients into two groups according to the m6score level, and the calculated batch effect is also Therefore, it is reduced, which is called the “measure cutting point” function. We chose the log-rank test to define the significance of the difference and used the Kaplan-Meier method to draw the survival curve for prognostic analysis. The univariate Cox regression was used to analyze the m6A hazard ratio (HR), and the multivariate Cox regression model was used to analyze independent prognostic factors. The prerequisite for the final multivariate prognostic analysis of patients is that they have detailed clinical data. We then employed the forestplot R package to visualize the results of the multi-factor prognostic analysis of m6score in the PRAD cohort. Additionally, we used receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to assess the specificity and sensitivity of m6score and to quantify the AUC. Use the maftools software package to show the condition of variant of patients with different m6score subtypes of the PRAD. Finally, we chose RCircos R package to make a change image of 21 m6A regulatory factors in 23 pairs of chromosomes29. A statistically significant difference was defined as P < 0.05, and the P value was both two-sided. All data processing is carried out using R 4.0.3 software.

Results

The genetic variation landscape of m6A regulatory factors in prostate cancer

Our study identified 29 m6A regulators: 10 writers, 17 readers and 2 erasers. First, we analyzed the copy number altering and somatic changing of these 29 m6A regulatory factors in PRAD. 22 m6A regulatory factors were mutated with a frequency of 4.55% in 484 samples. The results showed that ZC3H13, KIAA1429 and IGF2BP1 had the highest mutation rates when none of the other genes were mutated in the PRAD samples (Fig. 1A). Figure 1B shows the CNV position of the m6A regulatory factor on the chromosome. According to the results of the frequency of CNV changes, 29 regulatory factors of CNV have changed, and most of them have copy number deletions, but ALKBH5, IGF2BP1 and YTHDF1 have amplification frequencies (Fig. 1C). PRAD samples are completely distinguished from normal samples by the expression of these 29 m6A regulators (Fig. 1D). We then investigated whether genetic variation is associated with the expression of m6A regulatory factors in PRAD and found that the main factor leading to the perturbation of m6A regulatory factor expression is probably the change of CNV. CNV amplified m6A regulatory factors appear in PRAD tissues (such as YTHDF1 and vice versa (such as YTHDC2 and ZC3H13), which is a feature that normal prostate tissue does not have (Fig. 1B and D). Our study has the following conclusions: The gene and expression of m6A regulatory factors are very different among PRAD and normal patients, which means that the expression difference of m6A regulatory factors is very important to the occurrence and development of PRAD.

Modification patterns of m6A methylation mediated by 29 regulatory factors

First, we included a GEO dataset (GSE29229) with OS data and clinical information for research. Through the univariate Cox regression model, we identified 29 m6A regulators, all of which have prognostic value in prostate cancer patients (Fig. 2A-J). We used the m6A regulatory factor network to describe the interactions between the m6A regulatory factors, the way the regulatory factors are connected, and their significance to the prognosis of PRAD patients (Fig. 2K). This means that it is the crosstalk between the regulators of writer, reader and erasers that plays a decisive role in the m6A modification pattern and TME cell infiltration characteristics of different individual tumors. Patients with different m6A modification patterns are classified according to the expression of 29 m6A regulatory factors using the R package of ConsensusCluster Plus. We used unsupervised clustering to classify the samples into three distinct modification patterns: 273 cases of type A, 175 cases of type B, and 134 cases of type C. Principal component analysis of the transcriptome profiles for these m6A modification patterns revealed notable differences in the transcriptomic profiles among the different modification types (Supplementary Fig. 1). As shown in Table S1, these modes are respectively called m6Acluster A-C. We found that the m6Acluster C modified mode has an extremely significant survival advantage through the analysis of different m6A modified types (Fig. 2L). Similarly, the three different m6A modification patterns also have significant differences in the m6A transcription profile (Fig. 2M).

Our study demonstrates that different m6A subgroups are significantly associated with prognosis in PRAD. We hypothesize that this strategy may also be applicable to other cancers. Previous studies have shown that m6A modification is related to gastric cancer and breast cancer. LUDA is the most common subtype of non-small cell lung cancer, accounting for about 40% of all lung cancer cases. Its incidence has been rising globally, particularly among women and non-smokers. LUDA poses a significant global health burden as it remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths worldwide30. To investigate whether this method is applicable to LUDA, we applied a similar approach. Infiltration characteristics of TME cells under different m6A modification modes

The GSVA enrichment analysis was used to analysis the different biological behaviors between modification patterns of m6A. We have observed that m6cluster-a is significantly enriched in biological behaviors such as KEGG_T cell receptor signaling pathway, kegg_ leukocyte transendothelial migration and KEGG_ECM receptor interaction, GOBP_B cell homeostasis, as shown in Fig. 3A-D.The enrichment pathways related to immune activation, such as GOBP_antimicrobial humoral response, KEGG_intestinal immune network, GOMF_humoral immune response and GOMF cytokine activity, were found to be closely related to m6cluster-c (Fig. 3C-F). Surprisingly, m6Acluster A has a very rich infiltration of innate immune cells, including activated DCs, immature DCs, mast cells and natural killer cells. This is the result of research on TME cell infiltration (Fig. 3G and Table S2). Some researchers have found that many immune cells are also present in tumors with immune exclusion phenotypes. They do not penetrate the tumor parenchyma but stay in the interstitium surrounding the tumor cell nest. The reason why the matrix can be activated in TME is generally considered to be caused by T cell inhibition31. The results of GSVA analysis indicate that the m6Acluster A modification mode can activate the relevant matrix. Therefore, the anti-tumor effect of immune cells may be inhibited with the activation of the stroma in m6Acluster A group. We further analyzed the results and found that the matrix activity was significantly enhanced in m6Acluster A, including APC co-inhibition, CCR5 (CC chemokine receptor type 5), HLA (human leukocyte antigen), type II IFN response and angiogenesis ways and so on, which just confirmed our speculation (Fig. 3H). Coincidentally, the characteristics of TME cell infiltration are reflected in the three m6A modification modes. The m6Acluster A is innate immune cell infiltration and matrix activation, and we define it as an immune exclusion phenotype; Class B is characterized by immunosuppression, which is defined as an immune desert phenotype; the m6Acluster C is Immune activation and adaptive immune cell infiltration, we call it the immuno-inflammatory phenotype.

Recent academic focus has been on understanding the mechanisms by which m6A modification regulates DCs activation. This activation process depends largely on the increased expression of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules as well as a range of costimulatory and adhesion factors32. The critical role of DCs in antigen presentation and naive T cell activation emphasizes their integral function in bridging innate and adaptive immunity. TME DCs, whether they include activated DCs, immature DCs, or plasmacytoid DCs, can see the height of m6Acluster A and m6Acluster C (high MHC class I expression) enrichment. Not surprisingly, the enhancement of m6Acluster A and m6Acluster C (high expression of MHC class I) can also be observed in immune activation pathways, such as toll-like receptor signaling pathway, T cell receptor signaling pathway, antigen Processing and presentation pathways and nod-like receptor signaling pathways. It is not difficult to conclude that the anti-tumor immune response in the tumor is very likely to be affected by the activation of TME DCs by m6A methylation modification.

Clinical and transcriptome characteristics of m6a-related phenotypes

Various evidences indicate that the formation of different TME landscapes is highly dependent on the regulation of m6A methylation modification. However, these studies are based on the entire patient population, they cannot be accurate to the m6A methylation modification pattern of a single patient. Therefore, we invented a scoring system to quantify the m6A modification patterns of individual prostate cancer patients to describe individual heterogeneity and complexity. This system was named m6A score. The alluvial map can make the changes of individual patient attributes more intuitive (Supplementary Fig. 3A). Patients were categorized into low and high groups based on the cut-off value of -0.9872 as determined by the survminer software package. The high group exhibited significantly better survival rates (Fig. 5A). We also examined the relationship between the m6A score and known characteristics such as immune function, immune-related cell infiltration, and gene function enrichment analysis (Fig. 5B-G), which strengthens the credibility of the m6A feature descriptions. The m6A score was tested by Kruskal-Wallis and proved to have a significant difference. From the median score, gene cluster B is the lowest and gene cluster C is the highest (Supplementary Fig. 3B). The above strongly suggests that high m6A score means immune activation, and low m6A score represents matrix activation (Supplementary Fig. 3C). Similarly, compared with other clusters, m6Acluster C had the highest m6A score. It can be seen that the m6A modification pattern of a single tumor can be well explained by m6score, so we can further distinguish the true and false nature of TME immune infiltration.

TCGA molecular subtypes and m6A modification characteristics in tumor somatic mutations

Next, in the PRAD cohort, the somatic mutations of the m6A score high and low groups were calculated by the maftools software package, and their distribution differences were obtained. As shown in Fig. 6A, B, the overall significant mutation rates in the low m6A score group and the high m6A score group are 52.99% and 52%, and there are notable differences in their mutation spectra. At the same time, we used the m6A score feature to predict the effect of prostate cancer patients on tumor mutations. In line with our expectations, patients with high m6A score and low TMB had the highest survival rate, while patients with low m6A score and high TMB had the opposite (Fig. 6C). The difference is that among patients, whether TMB is high or not, the m6A score high group shows a higher survival rate, which means that the predictive ability of m6A score is not interfered by mutations (Fig. 6D). In addition, we found that patients with m6A cluster B modification patterns and immune exclusion phenotypes have poorer clinical prognosis. This means that m6A score can also be used to assess some clinical characteristics of patients (Fig. 6E). Additionally, the resistance or sensitivity of immunotherapy can also vary with individual genetic changes33. More and more studies have shown that when patients are in a state of high TMB, anti-tp53 immunotherapy has a stronger clinical response34. Patients with higher body TMB tend to have stronger responses, longer-term survival rates and more benefits when receiving immune checkpoint blockade treatment, which has been proven by clinical trials and preclinical studies35. The TP53 expression of high m6A score was found to be significantly lower than that of low m6A score (Fig. 6F). Therefore, we believe that the changes in tumor modification patterns of m6A may be an important factor in mediating the curative effect of anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy. The above findings will provide a new conceptual framework for the study of m6A methylation in the context of tumor somatic mutations. It also helps us to further study TME landing and immune checkpoint blocking therapy. In general, our research results strongly indicate that the m6A methylation modification pattern determines the tumor immune phenotype to a considerable extent, and the m6A modification characteristics we established can largely predict the effect of anti-TP53 immunotherapy.

Discussion

Numerous studies have indicated that m6A modification, along with various m6A regulatory factors, is crucial in inflammation, innate immunity, and anti-tumor responses36–38. Unfortunately, the current research focuses on a certain TME cell type or a certain regulatory factor, and There are few studies regarding the comprehensive characterization of Tumor Microenvironment (TME) infiltration as influenced by the synergistic effects of various M6A regulatory factors.

To develop more effective immunotherapy strategies, it is essential to understand the mechanisms behind different m6A modification patterns in TME cell infiltration. This also necessitates a clearer comprehension of the TME anti-tumor immune response. In our study, we identified three distinct m6A methylation modification patterns using 29 m6A regulatory factors, all of which exhibit significant TME cell infiltration characteristics.

The m6Acluster A, typified by innate immune cell infiltration and matrix activation, is delineated as exhibiting an immune exclusion phenotype. Conversely, m6Acluster B is distinguished by features of immunosuppression, thereby categorizing it as an immune desert phenotype. Lastly, the m6Acluster C, marked by immune activation and adaptive immune cell infiltration, is designated as the immuno-inflammatory phenotype. Survival analysis indicates that patients in m6Acluster C have significantly better prognosis compared to those in m6Acluster B or m6Acluster A. Non-inflammatory tumors include immune exclusion and immune desert phenotype. Although the immune exclusion phenotype also has a large number of immune cells, they are all retained in the interstitium and fail to penetrate the substance. Certain mesenchymal components are exclusively localized to the periphery of the tumor, while others infiltrate deeper into the tumor mass. This spatial distribution can lead to erroneous interpretations regarding the presence of immune cells within the tumor structure. Immune tolerance and immune ignorance, as well as lack of activation and activation of T cells can often lead to an immune desert phenotype39,40. Consistently, cluster A was found to exhibit significant matrix activation states, such as GOBP_B cell homeostasis, KEGG_T cell receptor signaling pathway, kegg_leukocyte transendothelial migration, and KEGG_ECM receptor through enrichment analysis. Similarly, the infiltration characteristics of TME cells also verified the correctness of our different immune phenotypes’ classification. Therefore, although m6Acluster A has activated innate immunity, it has a poor prognosis, which can be well explained by the above studies.

In addition, we have demonstrated that immune-related biological pathways are extremely closely related to the mRNA transcriptome of different m6A modification patterns. We consider these differentially expressed genes to be the key genes associated with m6A. In line with the results of the m6A modified phenotype clustering, three genomic subtypes have also been found, which are also related to matrix and immune activation. It is not difficult to conclude that the shaping of different TME landscapes is extremely dependent on the modification of m6A. Therefore, the understanding of the infiltration characteristics of TME cells also depends on the comprehensive evaluation of m6A modification patterns. Quantification of the M6A modification pattern in individual tumors is imperative to investigate intratumoral heterogeneity. We have creatively invented a scoring system-m6A gene characteristics to analysis individual m6A modification patterns of prostatic cancer patients. Among the different m6A modification modes, the m6A score of the immune exclusion phenotype was lower, and the m6A score of the immunoinflammatory phenotype was higher. In a similar manner, these findings were thoroughly validated in the IMvigor210 cohort, which had a well-defined immunophenotype41.The m6A score has proven to be a stable and effective tool for the overall analysis of m6A modification methods of individual tumors. The TME infiltration pattern, that is, the tumor immunophenotype, can also be effectively explained by this method. Analysis of all aspects shows that m6score can also be used to predict the prognosis of prostate cancer. EBV + tumors have low mutation burden and high immune infiltration characteristics42. In this case, the m6A score has the predictive advantage of precise immunotherapy.

In other tumors, such as cervical cancer and Ovarian Cancer m6A-based subtypes have also been found to exhibit distinct prognoses and different responses to immunotherapy43,44. Similarly, we applied the automatic clustering strategy based on m6A regulators to the LUDA cohort and discovered that different m6A regulator clusters in the LUDA cohort also demonstrated significant differences in survival outcomes. Further investigation into the application of m6Ascore in different tumor types could uncover novel insights into tumor biology and treatment strategies. This possibility warrants detailed exploration in future studies.

All in all, m6A score comprehensive evaluation can be well used in clinical practice. Through m6A methylation modification patterns and TME cell infiltration characteristics, the immune phenotype of tumors can be determined, and precise guidance can be made for treatment. m6score has also been proven to be used in the analysis of clinical pathological characteristics of patients, and there is a close relationship between the two. Not only that, this study also provides a new idea for cancer immunotherapy: the poor TME cell infiltration characteristics can be reversed by changing the m6A modification mode, that is, turning “cold tumors” into “hot tumors.” In the future, our results may even help the development of new immunotherapeutic drugs and new combination treatment programs. This lays a new foundation for improving immunotherapy response, identifying tumor immunophenotypes and advancing individualized treatment in the future.

Conclusions

In summary, this study uncovered a novel regulatory mechanism of m6A methylation modification, which significantly influences the tumor microenvironment. The tumor microenvironment exhibits considerable heterogeneity and complexity depending on the m6A modification pattern. To better understand the characteristics of cell infiltration within the tumor microenvironment, it is essential to thoroughly assess the m6A modification pattern of individual tumors. This approach will also aid in identifying more effective immunotherapy strategies.

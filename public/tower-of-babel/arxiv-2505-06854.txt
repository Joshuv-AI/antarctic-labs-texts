Method for high-precision determination of the nucleon axial structure using lattice QCD: Removing π ​ N \pi N -state contamination Preprint: UTHEP-802, UTCCS-P-165, HUPD-2503, KEK-TH-2715
Yasumichi Aoki, Ken-Ichi Ishikawa, Yoshinobu Kuramashi, Shoichi Sasaki, Kohei Sato, Eigo Shintani, Ryutaro Tsuji, Hiromasa Watanabe, Takeshi Yamazaki, PACS Collaboration

Abstract
We performed a precise calculation of physical quantities related to the axial structure of the nucleon using 2+1 flavor lattice QCD gauge configuration (PACS10 configuration) generated at the physical point with lattice volume larger than ( 10 ​ fm ) 4 (10\;{\mathrm{fm}})^{4} by the PACS Collaboration. The nucleon matrix element of the axial-vector current has two types of the nucleon form factors, the axial-vector ( F A F_{A} ) form factor and the induced pseudoscalar ( F P F_{P} ) form factor. Recently lattice QCD simulations have succeeded in reproducing the experimental value of the axial-vector coupling, g A g_{A} , determined from F A ​ ( q 2 ) F_{A}(q^{2}) at zero momentum transfer q 2 = 0 q^{2}=0 , at a percent level of statistical accuracy. However, the F P F_{P} form factor so far has not reproduced the experimental values well due to strong π ​ N \pi N excited-state contamination. Therefore, we proposed a simple subtraction method for removing the so-called leading π ​ N \pi N -state contribution, and succeeded in reproducing the values obtained by two experiments of muon capture on the proton and pion electro-production for F P ​ ( q 2 ) F_{P}(q^{2}) . The novel approach can also be applied to the nucleon pseudoscalar matrix element to determine the pseudoscalar ( G P G_{P} ) form factor with the help of the axial Wald-Takahashi identity. The resulting form factors, F P ​ ( q 2 ) F_{P}(q^{2}) and G P ​ ( q 2 ) G_{P}(q^{2}) , are in good agreement with the prediction of the pion-pole dominance model. In the new analysis, the induced pseudoscalar coupling g P ∗ g_{P}^{\ast} and the pion-nucleon coupling g π ​ N ​ N g_{\pi NN} can be evaluated with a few percent accuracy including systematic uncertainties using existing data calculated at two lattice spacings.

I
Introduction

The axial structure of the nucleon plays an important role
to understand the weak interaction between nucleons.
Experimentally, weak processes meditated by the weak charged current like neutron beta decay
n\rightarrow p+e^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{e}
, muon capture on the proton
\mu^{-}+p\rightarrow\nu_{\mu}+n
, or quasi elastic neutrino scattering
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}+p\rightarrow\mu^{+}+n

are mainly exploited for studying the weak nucleon form factors.
In recent years, the physics of neutrino oscillations, which inevitably involves the effects of nucleons and nuclear structure, has required accurate knowledge of the axial structure of the nucleon
Kronfeld:2019nfb
;
Meyer:2022mix
;
Tomalak:2023pdi
;
Petti:2023abz
. This is because the vector part of weak processes can be constrained by knowledge of the isovector combination between the electromagnetic form factors of the proton and the neutron under the assumption of the conserved-vector-current (CVC) hypothesis using isospin relations
Thomas:2001kw
, while the axial-vector part described by the axial-vector (
F_{A}
) and induced pseudoscalar (
F_{P}
) form factors is less known except for the axial-vector coupling
g_{A}=F_{A}(q^{2}=0)
that is most accurately measured by neutron beta decay.

From a theoretical perspective, the axial structure of the nucleon is highly connected with the physics
of chiral symmetry and its spontaneous breaking, which ensures the presence of pseudo Nambu-Goldstone particles such as the pion.
This is empirically known as the partially conserved axial-vector current (PCAC) hypothesis, where the divergence of the axial-vector current
is proportional to the pion field. Applying this idea to the isovector axial-vector matrix element of the nucleon given by

\displaystyle\langle N(p^{\prime})|A_{\alpha}(x)|N(p)\rangle=\overline{u}_{N}(p^{\prime})\left[\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}F_{A}(q^{2})+iq_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}F_{P}(q^{2})\right]u_{N}(p)e^{iq\cdot x}

(1)

with
q=p-p^{\prime}
, a specific relation, known as the Goldberger-Treiman (GT) relation
Goldberger:1958vp
, is
derived between the axial-vector coupling
g_{A}
and the residue of the pion-pole structure in the
F_{P}
form factor
together with the nucleon mass
M_{N}
.
Instead of the PCAC hypothesis, the axial Ward-Takahashi identity,
\partial_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}(x)=2mP(x)

with a degenerate up and down quark mass
m=m_{u}=m_{d}
, leads to the generalized GT relation
Weisberger:1966ip
;
Bernard:1994wn
;
Sasaki:2007gw
:

\displaystyle 2M_{N}F_{A}(q^{2})-q^{2}F_{P}(q^{2})=2mG_{P}(q^{2}),

(2)

which is satisfied among the three nucleon form factors including the pesudoscalar (
G_{P}
) form factor defined in the isovector pseudoscalar matrix element of the nucleon as

\displaystyle\langle N(p^{\prime})|P(x)|N(p)\rangle=\overline{u}_{N}(p^{\prime})\left[\gamma_{5}G_{P}(q^{2})\right]u_{N}(p)e^{iq\cdot x}.

(3)

In addition, the following pion-pole dominance (PPD) ansätz
Bernard:1994wn
;
Sasaki:2007gw
;
Nambu:1960xd

for
F_{P}(q^{2})
and
G_{P}(q^{2})
at low
q^{2}
,

\displaystyle F_{P}^{\mathrm{PPD}}(q^{2})=\frac{2M_{N}F_{A}(q^{2})}{q^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}

(4)

with the pion mass
M_{\pi}
and

\displaystyle 2mG_{P}^{\mathrm{PPD}}(q^{2})=2M_{N}F_{A}(q^{2})\frac{M_{\pi}^{2}}{q^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}},

(5)

satisfy the generalized GT relation (
2
). For details on the derivation of Eqs. (
2
), (
4
) and (
5
), see Appendix B in Ref.
Sasaki:2007gw
.

II.1
Standard ratio method

First of all, the nucleon operator with a three-dimensional momentum
\bm{p}

is given for the proton state by

\displaystyle N(t,\bm{p})=\sum_{\bm{x}}e^{-i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}\varepsilon_{abc}\left[u_{a}^{T}(t,\bm{x})C\gamma_{5}d_{b}(t,\bm{x})\right]u_{c}(t,\bm{x})

(6)

with the charge conjugation matrix,
C=\gamma_{4}\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle C_{N}(t_{\mathrm{sink}}-t_{\mathrm{src}};\bm{p})=\frac{1}{4}{\rm Tr}\left\{{\cal P}_{+}\langle N(t_{\mathrm{sink}};\bm{p})\overline{N}(t_{\mathrm{src}};-\bm{p})\rangle\right\}.

(7)

On the other hand, when the momentum
\bm{p}
and
\bm{p}^{\prime}

\displaystyle C_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})=\frac{1}{4}{\rm Tr}\left\{{\cal P}^{5z}\langle N(t_{\mathrm{sink}};\bm{p}^{\prime})\widetilde{J}(t;\bm{q})\overline{N}(t_{\mathrm{src}};-\bm{p})\right\},

(8)

where the local current
\widetilde{J}

;
Alexandrou:2024tin

Therefore, we assume that the contributions from the leading
\pi N
state
for
{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{\mu}}(t,{\bm{q}})
can be described as follows

\displaystyle\overline{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{i}}(t,{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle\equiv{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{i}}(t,{\bm{q}})-\delta_{i3}{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{3}}(t,{\bm{q}}_{0})

(20)

\displaystyle=-q_{3}q_{i}K^{-1}\left[\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})-\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})\right],

(21)

\displaystyle{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{4}}(t,{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=iq_{3}K^{-1}\left[\left(\widetilde{F}_{A}(q^{2})-(E_{N}({\bm{q}})-M_{N})\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})\right)\right.

(22)

\displaystyle\left.+E_{\pi}({\bm{q}})\Delta_{-}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})\right],

(23)

where the functions
\Delta_{\pm}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})
encode the leading
\pi N
contributions, which
provide the residual
t
dependence with a given
t_{\mathrm{sep}}
. See Appendix
B
, for detailed derivations based on an argument given by Meyer-Ottnad-Schulz

.

For the case when the axial-vector current operator carries the momentum
{\bm{q}}
,
the
\pi N
contribution can be expressed by the following form with
t
-independent coefficients
B(\bm{q})
and
C(\bm{q})

\displaystyle\Delta_{\pm}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})=B(\bm{q})e^{-\Delta E({\bm{q}},-{\bm{q}})t}\pm C(\bm{q})e^{-\Delta E({\bm{0}},{\bm{q}})(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)},

(24)

where the non-interacting estimate
\Delta E({\bm{q}},{\bm{k}})=E_{\pi}({\bm{k}})+E_{N}({\bm{q}}+{\bm{k}})-E_{N}({\bm{q}})

may be used. Therefore, the time derivative of the
\pi N
contribution
\Delta_{\pm}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})
may have
the following property:

\displaystyle\partial_{4}\Delta_{\pm}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})=-E_{\pi}({\bm{q}})\Delta_{\mp}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})+(E_{N}({\bm{q}})-M_{N})\Delta_{\pm}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}}),

(25)

which offers us to separate the
\pi N
contribution
\Delta_{\pm}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})
from the
\widetilde{F}_{P}
form factor using two types of the time-derivative of the correlator ratio,
\partial_{4}\overline{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{i}}(t,{\bm{q}})
and

\partial_{4}{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{4}}(t,{\bm{q}})

3
3

3

The time derivative
\partial_{4}
is defined by
\partial_{4}f(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(f(t+1)-f(t-1)\right)
so that Eq. (
25
) is valid up to
{\cal O}(a^{2})
.
.
Hereafter the nucleon energy
E_{N}(\bm{q})
and the pion energy
E_{\pi}(\bm{q})
are
simply abbreviated by shorthand notations
E_{N}
and
E_{\pi}
, respectively.

The leading
\pi N
subtraction method for determining
\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})
, including the time derivatives of
the
A_{4}
and
A_{i}
correlators, is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=-K\frac{\overline{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{i}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{q_{i}q_{3}}+\frac{K}{(\Delta E_{N})^{2}-E_{\pi}^{2}}\left[\Delta E_{N}\frac{\partial_{4}\overline{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{i}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{q_{i}q_{3}}+\frac{\partial_{4}{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{4}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{iq_{3}}\right]

(26)

with
\Delta E_{N}\equiv E_{N}-M_{N}
and
K=\sqrt{2E_{N}(E_{N}+M_{N})}
.
The first term corresponds to
\widetilde{F}_{P}^{\mathrm{std}}(q^{2})
in the standard ratio method. The leading
\pi N
contributions represented in terms of
\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})

and
\Delta_{-}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})
can be completely eliminated by adding the second term in Eq. (
26
). For the ground state contribution, namely
\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})
,
Eq. (
26
) is just a harmless linear combination exploiting the redundancy in the determination of

\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})
from both of
C_{A_{4}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
and
{C}_{A_{i}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
. Furthermore,
from Eqs. (
21
) and (
23
), it is clear that the terms in the parenthesis in Eq. (
26
) do not have the ground state contribution.
Therefore, if Eq. (
26
) successfully shows good plateau behavior, independent of the choice of
t_{\mathrm{sep}}
, it guarantees that the ground state contribution can be read accurately without excited-state contamination.

II.2.2
Pseudoscalar form factor
\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})

The leading
\pi N
contributions are due to the pion-pole contribution of the target form factor, as discussed in Appendix
B
. Therefore, the
P
correlator
C_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
also suffers from the contamination of the particular
\pi N
state contributions. Indeed, the
\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})
was observed to be strongly contaminated from the excited state, similar to the
\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})
in our previous works
Ishikawa:2018rew
;
Shintani:2018ozy
;
Ishikawa:2021eut
;
Tsuji:2023llh
.
We simply express that

\displaystyle{\cal R}^{5z}_{P}(t,{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=iq_{3}K^{-1}\left[\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})-\Delta_{P}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})\right],

(27)

where
\Delta_{P}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})
encodes the leading
\pi N
state contributions that
cause a residual
t
dependence in
{\cal R}_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
as shown in the lower panel of Fig.
1
.
Unlike in the case of the axial-vector currents, only a single correlator cannot remove the
\pi N

contribution
\Delta_{P}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})
.

It is important to recall here that in our previous study that the axial Ward-Takahashi identity is well satisfied
in terms of the three-point functions of the nucleon:

\displaystyle Z_{A}[\partial_{\alpha}C_{A_{\alpha}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})]=2m_{\rm PCAC}C_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q}),

(28)

where
m_{\rm PCAC}
corresponds to the bare quark mass which coincides with
the value determined from the pion two-point correlation functions
Tsuji:2023llh
.
It should be emphasized that Eq. (
28
) is satisfied without isolating the ground-state
contribution from the excited-state contributions, as first pointed out in Ref.
Bali:2018qus
.
Thus, Eq. (
28
) leads to the following PCAC relation for the leading
\pi N
contributions involved in
\overline{\cal R}_{A_{i}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
and

{\cal R}_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{P}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})=Z_{A}\frac{M_{\pi}^{2}}{2m_{\rm PCAC}}\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}}),

(29)

which also offers a simple subtraction method for determining the
\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})
as below

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=K\frac{{\cal R}^{5z}_{P}(t,{\bm{q}})}{iq_{3}}+\frac{Z_{A}B_{0}K}{(\Delta E_{N})^{2}-E_{\pi}^{2}}\left[\Delta E_{N}\frac{\partial_{4}\overline{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{i}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{q_{i}q_{3}}+\frac{\partial_{4}{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{4}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{iq_{3}}\right]

(30)

with the bare low-energy constant
B_{0}=\frac{M_{\pi}^{2}}{2m_{\mathrm{PCAC}}}
.
The first term corresponds to
\widetilde{G}_{P}^{\mathrm{std}}(q^{2})
in the standard method.
See Appendix
C
for details on the derivation of Eq. (
29
).

III.3
Induced pseudoscalar coupling
g_{P}^{*}
and the pion-nucleon
coupling
g_{\pi NN}

Experimentally, the main source of information about
F_{P}(q^{2})
comes from muon capture by the proton at rest. The induced pseudoscalar coupling

\displaystyle g_{P}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=m_{\mu}F_{P}(q_{0}^{2}),

(31)

which is evaluated at the specific momentum transfer,
q_{0}^{2}=0.88m_{\mu}^{2}
,
is measured by ordinary muon capture (OMC),
\mu^{-}+p\rightarrow\nu_{\mu}+n
or radiative muon capture (RMC),
\mu^{-}+p\rightarrow\gamma+\nu_{\mu}+n
. Although there were conflicting results between those two experiments and theories, the new OMC results from the MuCap Collaboration
MuCap:2007tkq
;
MuCap:2012lei
have settled a long-standing issue. Comprehensive overviews of the history of
g_{P}^{\ast}
are given in Refs.
Gorringe:2002xx
;
Bernard:2001rs
;
Sasaki:2007gw
.

Since we do not have data point of
F_{P}(q^{2})
at
q^{2}=0
for kinematical reason, the
q^{2}
extrapolation is necessary to evaluate
g_{P}^{\ast}
from our results of
F_{P}(q^{2})
. Fortunately, thanks to our large spatial extent of more than 10 fm, the smallest momentum transfer,
q_{\rm min}^{2}\approx 1.2m_{\mu}^{2}
for PACS10/L128 and
q_{\rm min}^{2}\approx 1.3m_{\mu}^{2}
for PACS10/L160, is very close to
q_{0}^{2}
. We also confirm that our results are consistent with the PPD model as shown in Fig.
6
.
Therefore, the pion-pole structure of the
F_{P}(q^{2})
form factor is certainly present. (See Appendix
E
for a more detailed analysis.)

In order to reduce systematic errors associated with the extrapolation
of the
F_{P}
form factor in momentum transfer, we would like to use the model independent
z
-expansion method
Boyd:1995cf
;
Hill:2010yb
.
Unlike the
F_{A}
form factor, the
F_{P}
form factor seems to have the pion-pole structure. Therefore, the
z
-expansion method is applied to
G(q^{2})=(q^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2})F_{P}(q^{2})
, where the pion-pole singularity can be compensated.
In the
z
-expansion method, the given
G(q^{2})

can be described by a convergent Taylor series in
a new variable
z
as

\displaystyle G(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}c_{k}z(q^{2})^{k},

(32)

where an infinite series expansion is truncated
at the
k_{\mathrm{max}}
-th order and
the variable
z
is defined by
a conformal mapping from
q^{2}
,

\displaystyle z(q^{2})=\frac{\sqrt{t_{\mathrm{cut}}+q^{2}}-\sqrt{t_{\mathrm{cut}}-t_{0}}}{\sqrt{t_{\mathrm{cut}}+q^{2}}+\sqrt{t_{\mathrm{cut}}-t_{0}}}

(33)

with
t_{\mathrm{cut}}=9m_{\pi}^{2}
. A parameter
t_{0}
can be
taken arbitrarily within the range of
t_{\mathrm{cut}}>t_{0}
.
For simplicity,
t_{0}=0
is chosen in this study.
To achieve a model-independent fit,
k_{\mathrm{max}}
should be chosen to ensure that terms
c_{k}z(q)^{k}
become numerically negligible for
k>k_{\mathrm{max}}
.

In this study, we confirm that the value of
k_{\mathrm{max}}=3
for the
128^{4}
and
160^{4}
lattices (
k_{\mathrm{max}}=4
for the
64^{4}

lattice) is large enough to guarantee that the
z
-expansion analysis makes a model-independent fit. We then extrapolate

at

.

IV
Summary

We have studied nucleon form factors in the axial-vector and pseudoscalar channels in 2+1 flavor QCD using two sets of the PACS10 configurations at coarse and fine lattice spacings. Our simulations were carried out in very large spatial volumes, which allow us to access the low
q^{2}
region, at the physical point essential for low-energy chiral behavior. The nucleon interpolating operator has been
adopted with well-tuned smearing parameters that guarantee ground-state dominance in
F_{A}(q^{2})
, although the two types of pseudoscalar form factors,
F_{P}(q^{2})
and
G_{P}(q^{2})
, still suffer from the excited-state contamination in our previous studies
Shintani:2018ozy
;
Tsuji:2023llh
.

In this study, we thus propose a simple subtraction method for removing the so-called leading
\pi N
-state contamination induced by the pion-pole structure appears in
F_{P}(q^{2})
and
G_{P}(q^{2})
. The leading
\pi N
state contribution in the three-point function of the spatial axial-vector current
A_{i}
was found to be eliminated by a specific linear combination of the two time derivatives of the correlator ratios
composed of the
A_{4}
and
A_{i}
currents.
The leading
\pi N
subtraction method can be easily applied to existing data without the need for new calculations, provided that the excited-state contribution, which appears in the standard ratio method, is sufficiently hindered for
F_{A}(q^{2})
by applying the appropriate smearing to the nucleon operator.

In fact, the new correlator ratio, which includes the time derivatives of the
A_{4}
and
A_{i}
correlators, shows good plateau behavior regardless of the current insertion time, eliminating the slightly convex shape associated with the excited-state contribution in the standard ratio method. It is important to note that this novel method utilizes three-point functions of both spatial and temporal axial-vector currents to calculate
F_{P}(q^{2})
.

For
G_{P}(q^{2})
, the “PCAC relation” for the leading
\pi N
contribution, which can be verified by the fact that the axial
Ward-Takahashi identity is well satisfied in terms of the nucleon three-point functions, can help to eliminate the
\pi N
contribution in the
three-point function of the pseudoscalar current in a similar way in the case of
F_{P}(q^{2})
.
The same subtraction prescription was also effective for
G_{P}(q^{2})
, completely eliminating the slightly convex shape of the time dependence that is visible in the standard ratio method, and yielding a constant value that is insensitive to the choice of
t_{\mathrm{sep}}
.

The results of both
F_{P}(q^{2})
and
G_{P}(q^{2})
obtained from the new analysis surprisingly agree well with the prediction of the PPD model in the range of
0.01\lesssim q^{2}\lesssim 0.12

[{\rm GeV}^{2}]
measured in this paper. There is experimental information coming from two experiments for
F_{P}(q^{2})
. One is the experiment
of the muon capture by the proton, the other is the pion electro-production experiment. Our results agree well with these experimental results as well.

Finally, we determine the values of
g_{P}^{\ast}
and
g_{\pi NN}
from
F_{P}(q^{2})
using the
z
-expansion method
with the pion-pole singularity taken into account. Our final results
are

\displaystyle g_{P}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\makebox[80.00012pt][l]{8.53(23)(6)(4)}&\beta=1.82\\
\makebox[80.00012pt][l]{8.74(16)(2)(13)}&\beta=2.00\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle g_{\pi NN}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}\makebox[80.00012pt][l]{12.80(41)(9)(4)}&\beta=1.82\cr\makebox[80.00012pt][l]{13.24(28)(3)(14)}&\beta=2.00\cr\end{array}\right.

where the first error is statistical, while
the others are systematic ones. The second
error is evaluated stemming from the error
of
Z_{A}^{\mathrm{SF}}
for the renormalization.
The third error is evaluated by the difference between two analyses using either the
t_{{\mathrm{sep}}}\gtrsim 1.2\ \mathrm{fm}
or
t_{{\mathrm{sep}}}\gtrsim 1.0\ \mathrm{fm}
condition for the selection of data sets in a combined analysis.

Since our lattice simulations are currently running at the third lattice spacing to perform the continuum limit extrapolation
Tsuji:2024scy
, we have estimated the systematic uncertainties associated with the finite lattice spacing for
g_{P}^{\ast}
and
g_{\pi NN}
from the difference between
from the current results obtained at two lattice spacings.
It was found that the finite lattice spacing effects on
g_{P}^{\ast}
and
g_{\pi NN}

are kept below the statistical error of less than 4%, while all results are consistent with respective experimental values.
Therefore, the lattice discretization effects
on
g_{P}^{\ast}
and
g_{\pi NN}
are negligibly
small in our calculations, similar to the case of
g_{A}
as reported in Ref
Tsuji:2023llh
.

A comprehensive study that employs the current results for
F_{P}(q^{2})
and
G_{P}(q^{2})
along with
F_{A}(q^{2})
to verify
the generalized GT relation and the PPD model is outlined in a separate paper
NewPaper
and not included in this paper.
Although the axial radius
r_{A}
is most relevant for current and future neutrino experiments, there is a possible discretization error of about 10% in this quantity as pointed out in Ref.
Tsuji:2023llh
.
Therefore, it is necessary to continue our lattice QCD calculations for the axial structure of the nucleon with the third lattice spacing toward the continuum limit. Such a study is currently underway
Tsuji:2024scy

Appendix A
Nucleon ground-state contribution in the nucleon three-point function

In Sec.
II
, we define the nucleon three-point function
C_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

in Eq. (
8
), where the initial and final states carry fixed momenta
\bm{p}
and
\bm{p}^{\prime}
, respectively.
The leading contribution of the nucleon three-point function can be described by
the ground-state contribution
C_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})_{NN}
as

\displaystyle C_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})=C_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})_{NN}+\cdot\cdot\cdot,

(40)

where the ellipsis denotes excited state contributions. The explicit formula of
the ground-state contribution can be expressed by

\displaystyle C_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})_{NN}

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{N}(\bm{p})}{2E_{N}(\bm{p})}e^{-E_{N}(\bm{p})t}\times\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{J}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

(41)

\displaystyle\times\frac{Z_{N}^{\ast}(\bm{p}^{\prime})}{2E_{N}(\bm{p}^{\prime})}e^{-E_{N}(\bm{p}^{\prime})(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)},

(42)

where
Z_{N}(\bm{p})
and
Z_{N}^{\ast}(\bm{p}^{\prime})
correspond to the spectral weight
between the initial and final states of the nucleon, and the nucleon interpolating operator,

\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{J}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})
is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{J}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

(43)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Tr}}\left[{\mathcal{P}^{5z}}(-i\gamma\cdot p^{\prime}+M_{N}){\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}}(q)(-i\gamma\cdot p+M_{N})\right]

(44)

with
{\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}}(q)
contains the respective nucleon form factors.
For the axial-vector (
A_{\alpha}
) and pseudoscalar (
P
) currents,

{\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}}(q)
have the following relativistically covariant decomposition
in terms of the nucleon form factors as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}_{A_{\alpha}}}(q)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\widetilde{F}_{A}(q^{2})+i(p-p^{\prime})_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2}),

(45)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}_{P}}(q)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{5}\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2}).

(46)

After the trace operation is performed in the right hand side of Eq. (
43
),
the general expression of
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{J}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

for both the axial-vector and pseudoscalar cases are given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{A_{\alpha}}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{F}_{A}(q^{2}){\Lambda}^{NN}_{5,\alpha}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

(47)

\displaystyle+i(p-p^{\prime})_{\alpha}\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2}){\Lambda}^{NN}_{5}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p}),

(48)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{P}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2}){\Lambda}^{NN}_{5}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p}),

(49)

where

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{NN}_{5,\alpha}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=2M_{N}\left[\left(M_{N}-i(p_{4}^{\prime}+p_{4})\right)\delta_{3\alpha}\right.

(50)

\displaystyle\left.+i(p_{3}^{\prime}+p_{3})\delta_{4\alpha}\right]

(51)

\displaystyle+2\left[(p_{3}^{\prime}p_{\alpha}+p_{\alpha}^{\prime}p_{3})-(p^{\prime}\cdot p)\delta_{3\alpha}\right],

(52)

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{NN}_{5}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=2\left[i(p_{3}-p_{3}^{\prime})M_{N}+(p_{4}^{\prime}p_{3}-p_{3}^{\prime}p_{4})\right].

(53)

In this study, we consider only the case at the rest frame of the final state (
\bm{p}^{\prime}=\bm{0}
), which
leads to
\bm{p}=\bm{q}
. For this specific kinematics, we get

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{NN}_{5,\alpha}(\bm{0},\bm{q})

\displaystyle=2(-ip_{4}^{\prime}+M_{N})(-ip_{4}+M_{N})\delta_{3\alpha}

(54)

\displaystyle+2iq_{3}(-ip_{4}^{\prime}+M_{N})\delta_{4\alpha},

(55)

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{NN}_{5}(\bm{0},\bm{q})

\displaystyle=i2M_{N}(-ip_{4}^{\prime}+M_{N})q_{3},

(56)

which lead to

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{A_{\alpha}}(\bm{0},\bm{q})

\displaystyle=2(-ip_{4}^{\prime}+M_{N})\left[\big((-ip_{4}+M_{N})\delta_{3\alpha}+iq_{3}\delta_{4\alpha}\big)\right.

(57)

\displaystyle\times\widetilde{F}_{A}(q^{2})\left.-q_{3}(p-p^{\prime})_{\alpha}\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})\right],

(58)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{P}(\bm{0},\bm{q})

\displaystyle=i2(-ip_{4}^{\prime}+M_{N})q_{3}\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2}).

(59)

In the standard ratio method described in Sec.
II
, the quantity
of
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{J}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})
is extracted from the ratio of the three-point function and two-point functions defined in Eq. (
9
).
In this case, with
p_{4}=iE_{N}(\bm{q})
and
p_{4}^{\prime}=iM_{N}
,
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{J}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})
is expressed as follows
Sasaki:2007gw
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{A_{\alpha}}(\bm{0},\bm{q})

\displaystyle=4M_{N}(M_{N}+E_{N}(\bm{q}))

(60)

\displaystyle\times\left(\delta_{3\alpha}+\frac{iq_{3}}{M_{N}+E_{N}(\bm{q})}\delta_{4\alpha}\right)\widetilde{F}_{A}(q^{2})-4M_{N}q_{3}q_{\alpha}\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2}),

(61)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{P}(\bm{0},\bm{q})

\displaystyle=i4M_{N}q_{3}\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2}).

Appendix B
Spectral decomposition on the nucleon three-point function

Following an argument given by Meyer-Ottnad-Schulz
Meyer:2018twz
, let us consider the
spectral decomposition on the nucleon ground-state contribution of the nucleon three-point function in the continuum as below,

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})_{NN}

(62)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dp_{4}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\int\frac{dp_{4}}{2\pi}\frac{Z_{N}(\bm{p})}{p^{2}+M_{N}^{2}}e^{ip_{4}t}\times\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{J}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

(63)

\displaystyle\times\frac{Z_{N}^{\ast}(\bm{p}^{\prime})}{{p^{\prime}}^{2}+M_{N}^{2}}e^{ip_{4}^{\prime}(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)}.

(64)

for the axial-vector current
including both of
A_{4}
and
A_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
), and the pseudoscalar correlator. In this expression, the ground-state contribution defined in Eq. (
41
) can be obtained
after performing the
p_{4}
and
p_{4}^{\prime}
integrals by contour integration.
Furthermore, if the form factors contained in
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{J}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

have singularities in the complex plane, additional contributions may appear.

To actually see such a singularity in the
axial-vector case of
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{A_{\alpha}}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})
, we use the dispersive representation of the
F_{A}
and
F_{P}
form factors

\displaystyle{F}_{A,P}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{s_{0}}\frac{ds}{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{Im}{F}_{A,P}(-s)}{s+q^{2}}\;.

(65)

For the
F_{A}(q^{2})
,
s_{0}
represents a starting point of branch cut associated with a three-pion threshold as
s_{0}=9M_{\pi}^{2}
, while for the
F_{P}(q^{2})
,
s_{0}
represents a singularity point due to the pion pole position as
s_{0}=M_{\pi}^{2}
.
In the case of the
F_{P}(q^{2})
, the presence of the pion pole on the real axis in the negative
q^{2}
region lead to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}{F}_{P}(-s)=2\pi F_{\pi}g_{\pi NN}\delta(s-m_{\pi}^{2})+\cdot\cdot\cdot,

(66)

which can generate the additional contribution
from the expression of (
62
).
The additional contribution for the pseudoscalar case of

\widetilde{\Lambda}^{NN}_{P}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{p})

can be also generated since the
G_{P}(q^{2})
has the pion pole on the real axis in the negative
q^{2}
region as well
as the
F_{P}(q^{2})
.

To see it more explicitly, let us consider for our choice of the kinematics (
\bm{p}^{\prime}=\bm{0}
and
\bm{p}=\bm{q}
) calculated in this study. In the axial-vector case,

\hat{C}_{A_{\alpha}}^{5z}(t;\bm{0},\bm{q})_{NN}

contains the following integral associated
with the pion-pole structure embedded in the
F_{P}(q^{2})
,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})=\int\frac{dp_{4}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\int\frac{dp_{4}}{2\pi}\underbrace{\frac{e^{ip_{4}t}}{p_{4}^{2}+E_{N}^{2}(\bm{q})}}_{\mathrm{(I)}}\;\underbrace{\frac{(p-p^{\prime})_{\alpha}}{(p_{4}-p_{4}^{\prime})^{2}+\bm{q}^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}}_{\mathrm{(II)}}\;\underbrace{\frac{(-ip_{4}^{\prime}+M_{N})e^{ip_{4}^{\prime}(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)}}{{p_{4}^{\prime}}^{2}+M_{N}^{2}}}_{\mathrm{(III)}},

(67)

which is classified into three sections, where section (II) is responsible for the pion pole structure.

In considering the two pole contributions arising from section (II), it is worth transforming the equation as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{(p-p^{\prime})_{\alpha}}{(p_{4}-p_{4}^{\prime})^{2}+\bm{q}^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{(p_{4}-p_{4}^{\prime})-iE_{\pi}(\bm{q})}+\frac{1}{(p_{4}-p_{4}^{\prime})+iE_{\pi}(\bm{q})}\right)&\mbox{for}\;\alpha=4\cr-\frac{iq_{i}}{2E_{\pi}(\bm{q})}\left(\frac{1}{(p_{4}-p_{4}^{\prime})-iE_{\pi}(\bm{q})}-\frac{1}{(p_{4}-p_{4}^{\prime})+iE_{\pi}(\bm{q})}\right)&\mbox{for}\;\alpha=i\neq 4\cr\end{array}\right.

where the relative signs of the two contributions differ in the case of the temporal and spatial components
4
4

4

In Ref.
Meyer:2018twz
, the original discussion addressed only the temporal component of the axial-vector current and omitted the spatial one.

. By the standard way of evaluating residues, the integral
I_{\alpha}
given by Eq. (
67
) can yield three contributions:

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})=I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{A}}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})+I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})+I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{C}}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}}).

(70)

The evaluation of each contribution is based on the following residues in the lower half of the complex plane:

•

Case A: (I) and (III) contain poles:
p_{4}=iE_{N}(\bm{q})
and
p_{4}^{\prime}=iM_{N}

•

Case B: (II) and (III) contain poles:
p_{4}=i(M_{N}+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))
and
p_{4}^{\prime}=iM_{N}

•

Case C: (I) and (II) contain poles:
p_{4}=iE_{N}(\bm{q})
and
p_{4}^{\prime}=i(E_{N}(\bm{q})+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))

and results in the following form

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{A}}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2E_{N}(\bm{q})}\frac{q_{\alpha}}{q^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}e^{-E_{N}(\bm{q})t}e^{-M_{N}(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)},

(71)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2E_{\pi}(\bm{q})}\frac{r^{+}_{\alpha}}{(M_{N}+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))^{2}-E_{N}^{2}(\bm{q})}e^{-(M_{N}+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))t}e^{-M_{N}(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)},

(72)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{C}}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4E_{N}(\bm{q})E_{\pi}(\bm{q})}\frac{r^{-}_{\alpha}}{E_{N}(\bm{q})+E_{\pi}(\bm{q})-M_{N}}e^{-E_{N}(\bm{q})t}e^{-(E_{N}(\bm{q})+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)},

(73)

where
q=(i(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N}),\bm{q})
and
r^{\pm}=(iE_{\pi}(\bm{q}),\pm\bm{q})
.
The contribution of
I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{A}}
provides
the usual ground-state contribution, while the other two
contributions
I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}
and
I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{C}}
are nothing but
the pole enhanced
\pi N
contributions, which are represented in terms of
\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})

and
\Delta_{-}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};{\bm{q}})

in Eq. (
21
) and Eq. (
23
), respectively.

Furthermore, we observe that these coefficients explicitly contain the pion pole contributions as a function of
q^{2}
, indicating that their derivation is based on the pion pole structure of
F_{P}(q^{2})
.
It is important to recall that
q^{2}=2M_{N}(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N})
under the kinematics selected for the nucleon ground-state contribution. Consequently, the coefficient of Eq. (
72
) contains
the pion-pole contribution as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(M_{N}+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))^{2}-E_{N}^{2}(\bm{q})}

(74)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2E_{N}(\bm{q})}\left(\frac{E_{\pi}(\bm{q})+E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N}}{q^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}-\frac{1}{M_{N}+E_{\pi}(\bm{q})+E_{N}(\bm{q})}\right),

(75)

while the coefficient of Eq. (
72
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E_{N}(\bm{q})+E_{\pi}(\bm{q})-M_{N}}=\frac{E_{\pi}(\bm{q})-(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N})}{q^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}.

(76)

Thus, in the heavy nucleon mass limit,
the coefficients of Eq. (
72
) and Eq. (
73
) become the following expressions

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{B}}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[M_{N}\rightarrow\infty]{}-\frac{1}{4E_{N}(\bm{q})}\frac{r_{\alpha}^{+}}{q^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}e^{-(M_{N}+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))t}e^{-M_{N}(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)},

(77)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{C}}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[M_{N}\rightarrow\infty]{}\frac{1}{4E_{N}(\bm{q})}\frac{r_{\alpha}^{-}}{q^{2}+M_{\pi}^{2}}e^{-(M_{N}+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))t}e^{-E_{N}(\bm{q})t}e^{-(E_{N}(\bm{q})+E_{\pi}(\bm{q}))(t_{\mathrm{sep}}-t)},

(78)

which can reproduce the leading
\pi N

contribution discussed in the ChPT-based analysis
Bar:2018xyi
.

It is not difficult to verify that the pseudoscalar case can be
calculated similarly to the spatial component of the axial-vector to ensure that the leading
\pi N
contributions are represented in terms of
\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})
in Eq. (
27
).

Appendix C
Derivation of Eq. (
29
)

First, we introduce the following correlator ratio

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})\equiv\frac{\partial_{4}C_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})}{C_{2}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})},

(79)

where the numerator is the time-derivative of the three-point functions. We then consider the relation
between
\mathcal{D}_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
and
\mathcal{R}_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
. Assuming that the time derivative can be treated like a continuous derivative with respect to time, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{4}{\cal R}_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{4}C_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})}{C_{2}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})}-{\cal R}_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})\frac{\partial_{4}C_{2}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})}{C_{2}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})}

(80)

\displaystyle={\cal D}_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})+(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N}){\cal R}_{J}^{5z}(t;\bm{q}),

(81)

where the second line is obtained using the following relation

\displaystyle\partial_{4}C_{2}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})=-(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N})C_{2}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q}).

(82)

In Eq. (
28
), the derivative of the nucleon three-point function with respect to the coordinate are evaluated by

\displaystyle\partial_{i}C_{A_{i}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})=iq_{i}C_{A_{i}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})

(83)

for the spatial component (
i=1,2,3)
.
Therefore, Eq. (
28
) can be
rewritten as

\displaystyle Z_{A}\left[iq_{i}{\cal R}_{A_{i}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})+{\cal D}_{A_{4}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})\right]=2m_{\mathrm{PCAC}}{\cal R}_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q}).

(84)

Here, using Eq. (
81
),

{\cal R}_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
can be expressed in terms of
{\cal R}_{A_{\alpha}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
and
its time derivative as

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})=\frac{Z_{A}}{2m_{\mathrm{PCAC}}}\left[iq_{i}{\cal R}_{A_{i}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})+\partial_{4}{\cal R}_{A_{4}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})-(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N}){\cal R}_{A_{4}}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})\right].

(85)

Inserting Eqs. (
21
) and (
23
) into the r.h.s. of
Eq. (
85
), we get

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})=

\displaystyle iq_{3}K^{-1}\frac{Z_{A}}{2m_{\mathrm{PCAC}}}\left[2M_{N}\widetilde{F}_{A}(q^{2})-2M_{N}(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N})\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})-q_{i}^{2}\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+E_{\pi}(\bm{q})\left\{\partial_{4}\Delta_{-}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})-(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N})\Delta_{-}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle iq_{3}K^{-1}\frac{Z_{A}}{2m_{\mathrm{PCAC}}}\left[2M_{N}\widetilde{F}_{A}(q^{2})-q^{2}\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})-M_{\pi}^{2}\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})\right],

(86)

where we used
q^{2}=2M_{N}(E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N})
and Eq. (
25
) at the stage of going from the first line to the second line.
By comparing the time-independent and time-dependent parts of both sides of Eq. (
86
),
two equalities are obtained, respectively.
The generalized Goldberger-Treiman (GT) relation

\displaystyle Z_{A}\left[2M_{N}\widetilde{F}_{A}(q^{2})-q^{2}\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})\right]=2m_{\rm PCAC}\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})

(87)

is obtained from the time-independent part of Eq. (
86
), while the time-dependent part of Eq. (
86
) provides Eq. (
29
) as

\displaystyle\Delta_{P}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})=Z_{A}\frac{M_{\pi}^{2}}{2m_{\mathrm{PCAC}}}\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q}).

(88)

It is worth noting that the elimination of the leading
\pi N
contribution from
{\cal R}_{P}^{5z}(t;\bm{q})
using Eq. (
29
) ensures that the generalized GT relation holds for the ground-state contribution among three form factors, namely,
F_{A}(q^{2})
,
F_{P}(q^{2})
, and
G_{P}(q^{2})
as long as Eq. (
28
) is satisfied.

Appendix D
Simultaneous fit approach

In Sec.
II.2
, we proposed the leading
\pi N
subtraction method to determine
\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})
and
\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})
as defined in Eq. (
26
) and Eq. (
30
), where the leading
\pi N
contribution in the three-point functions of the axial-vector and pseudoscalar currents are subtracted by adding the specific linear combination of the time derivatives of the
A_{4}
and
A_{i}
correlators. Here, instead, we employ
a simultaneous fit method to multiple data sets with different values of
t_{\mathrm{sep}}
for the residual time dependence of the correlator ratios, including the leading
\pi N
contribution, given by the terms of
\Delta_{\pm}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})
, as defined in Eq. (
24
).

This is almost similar to the multi-state analysis, used in the other group, if the energy of excited states
is treated as a free parameter. In this study, we fix the energies of the excited states appearing in the time dependence in
\Delta_{\pm}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q})
by the non-interacting estimates
as

\Delta E(\bm{q},-\bm{q})=E_{\pi}(\bm{q})+M_{N}-E_{N}(\bm{q})
and

\Delta E(\bm{0},\bm{q})=E_{\pi}(\bm{q})+E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N}
, while the
t
-independent coefficients
B
and
C
are treated as free parameters.

We apply the simultaneous fits to each of the following four quantities:

\displaystyle W_{A_{i}}(t)

\displaystyle=-K\frac{\overline{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{i}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{q_{i}q_{3}},

(89)

\displaystyle W_{A_{4}}(t)

\displaystyle=K\frac{{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{4}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{iq_{3}},

(90)

\displaystyle W_{P}(t)

\displaystyle=K\frac{{\cal R}^{5z}_{P}(t,{\bm{q}})}{iq_{3}},

(91)

\displaystyle W_{\Delta_{+}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{(\Delta E_{N})^{2}-E^{2}_{\pi}}

(92)

\displaystyle\times\left[\Delta E_{N}\frac{\partial_{4}\overline{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{i}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{q_{i}q_{3}}+\frac{\partial_{4}{\cal R}^{5z}_{A_{4}}(t,{\bm{q}})}{iq_{3}}\right],

(93)

where
\Delta E_{N}=E_{N}(\bm{q})-M_{N}
and
E_{\pi}=E_{\pi}(\bm{q})
.
W_{A_{i}}(t)
and

W_{P}(t)
correspond to
\widetilde{F}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})

and

\widetilde{G}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})
in the standard ratio method.

The resulting fitted curve and the data points (marked as filled symbols) used to fit are shown in Fig.
13
for the lowest momentum transfer (Q1) as typical examples.
The fit results successfully reproduce the slight
convex shapes associated with the excited-state contamination and their
t_{\mathrm{sep}}
dependence that appeared in
W_{A_{i}}(t)
and
W_{P}(t)
, as well as the peculiar behavior that depends linearly on the current insertion time
t
in
W_{A_{4}}(t)
. Similarly, the corresponding
\pi N
contribution extracted in

appearing in the form of

All four results are consistent with each other in both amplitudes

.
More interestingly, their

and

It has been demonstrated that the data exhibit a flat tendency in the region of

B
, which show that the corresponding

and

possess the pion-pole nature as a function of

Intuitively, if the nucleon form factor has a pion pole structure such as

and

q^{2}

This is simply because the magnitude of the form factor increases rapidly with lower

In fact, however, the
\pi N
-state contamination is much more pronounced in both cases of
F_{P}(q^{2})
and
G_{P}(q^{2})

The underlying reason for this reversal trend is now straightforward: the amplitudes of the terms contributed by the leading
\pi N
excited states are also enhanced by the pion-pole nature, in conjunction with the ground state contributions,
F_{P}(q^{2})
and
G_{P}(q^{2})

\pi N

Finally, for

Appendix E
Test for pion-pole structure

As discussed in Ref
Ishikawa:2018rew
, both of
F_{P}^{\rm std}(q^{2})
and
\widetilde{G}^{\rm std}_{P}(q^{2})
obtained from the standard ratio method are significantly underestimated in the low-
q^{2}
region compared to the PPD model, while the ratio of
\widetilde{G}^{\rm std}_{P}(q^{2})/F_{P}^{\rm std}(q^{2})
shows no appreciable
q^{2}
dependence and
gives a nearly constant value, which is barely consistent with the bare low-energy constant
B_{0}
evaluated by the ratio of
M_{\pi}^{2}/(2m_{\rm PCAC})
.
We have therefore concluded that this is simply because the expected pion-pole structure in both cases of
F_{P}^{\rm std}(q^{2})
and
\widetilde{G}^{\rm std}_{P}(q^{2})
is
simultaneously distorted due to strong
\pi N
excited-state contamination
Ishikawa:2018rew
.

On the other hand, as described in the text, the new analysis to remove the
\pi N
-state contamination from both
F_{P}
and
G_{P}
form factors makes results compatible with the PPD model. To see this fact, we define the effective
“pion-pole” mass from
F_{P}(q^{2})

Yamazaki:2009zq
as

\displaystyle M_{\rm pole}=\sqrt{\frac{2M_{N}F_{A}(q^{2})}{F_{P}(q^{2})}-q^{2}},

(94)

which would exhibit a flat
q^{2}
dependence if
F_{P}(q^{2})
possesses the pole structure.
In Fig.
17
, we plot the effective pole mass as a function
of
q^{2}
. The horizontal dashed line at each panel represents the value of the
simulated pion mass
M_{\pi}
in lattice units.
The blue symbols are obtained from
F_{P}^{\rm std}(q^{2})
given by the standard ratio analysis,
while the red symbols are obtained for the new analysis.
Although there is no significant
q^{2}
dependence of the effective pole mass
M_{\mathrm{pole}}
in either case, the new analysis gives fairly consistent values with the simulated pion mass.

Why does the strong
\pi N
excited-state contamination seem to affect the pion-pole structure only as a distortion of the pole mass in the PPD model?
In our observation,
M_{\mathrm{pole}}>M_{\pi}
for
F_{P}^{\mathrm{std}}(q^{2})
, while

F_{P}(q^{2})
certainly has the pion pole as shown in Fig.
17
. Therefore
we see

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{pole}}^{2}-M_{\pi}^{2}=2M_{N}F_{A}(q^{2})\left(\frac{1}{F_{P}^{\mathrm{std}}(q^{2})}-\frac{1}{F_{P}(q^{2})}\right)>0,

(95)

because of
F_{P}(q^{2})>F_{P}^{\mathrm{std}}(q^{2})
.

First of all, the axial Ward-Takahashi identity for the nucleon three-point function, where both the ground state and
excited state contributions are entangled, is verified in Ref.
Tsuji:2023llh
. This observation suggests that the ground-state contribution and the excited-state contribution must separately satisfy the axial Ward-Takahashi identity.
For the ground state, this is nothing but
the generalized GT relation. Therefore, once the leading
\pi N
contribution on the axial-vector three-point function is identified as
\Delta_{\pm}(t)
term,
the leading
\pi N
contribution on the pseudoscalar three-point function can be described by
\Delta_{P}(t)=Z_{A}B_{0}\Delta_{+}(t)
, where
the factor
Z_{A}B_{0}
is determined to hold the axial Ward-Takahashi identity for the leading
\pi N
contribution.
Using the terms of
\Delta_{+}(t)
and
\Delta_{P}(t)
, the true ground-state contribution,
\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})
(
\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})
) and
\widetilde{F}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})

(
\widetilde{G}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})
)
evaluated by the standard ratio method are related as below

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{P}(q^{2})-\widetilde{F}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{+}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q}),

(96)

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})-\widetilde{G}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{P}(t,t_{\mathrm{sep}};\bm{q}).

(97)

Therefore, we get the following relation

\displaystyle B_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})-\widetilde{G}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})}{F_{P}(q^{2})-F^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})}.

(98)

Here, we easily see that

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{G}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})}{{F}^{\mathrm{std}}_{P}(q^{2})}\approx\frac{\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})}{{F}_{P}(q^{2})}\approx B_{0}

(99)

is fulfilled,
if both
{F}_{P}(q^{2})
and
\widetilde{G}_{P}(q^{2})
are well described by the PPD model that leads to

\widetilde{G}^{\mathrm{PPD}}_{P}(q^{2})/{F}^{\mathrm{PPD}}_{P}(q^{2})=B_{0}
.

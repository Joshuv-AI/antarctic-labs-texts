Emergence of nonclassical radiation in strongly laser-driven quantum systems
Ivan Gonoskov, Christian Hünecke, Stefanie Gräfe

Abstract
Nonclassical light sources are central to emerging quantum technologies, yet current platforms offer limited tunability and typically operate at low photon numbers. In parallel, strong-field physics provides widely tunable, bright coherent radiation through high-order harmonic generation (HHG), but its quantum optical character has remained largely unexplained. While recent experiments have revealed signatures of entanglement, squeezing, and quantum-state modification in both the driving and generated fields, a unified theoretical framework capable of identifying the origin and controllability of these effects has been missing. Here we introduce a fully quantum, analytically tractable theory of intense light–matter interaction that rigorously captures the emergence of nonclassicality in HHG. Our approach employs a parametric factorization of the coupled electron–field system into a driven electronic state and a dynamically perturbed quantum optical field, derived directly from the time-dependent Schrödinger equation without requiring conditioning, homodyne detection, or mode-selection techniques. We show how quantum correlations, squeezing, and Wigner-function negativity arise intrinsically from the interaction dynamics, and we identify the precise conditions under which specific nonclassical features are amplified or suppressed. The theory enables predictive design of bright, high-photon-number quantum states at tunable frequencies, and we demonstrate its utility by outlining realistic conditions for generating bright nonclassical ultraviolet light. Our results establish a comprehensive foundation for strong-field quantum optics and open new avenues toward tabletop quantum light sources for sensing, communication, and photonic quantum information processing.



Recent technological advances, in particular in the areas of micro- and nanostructuring technologies, led to a boost in experimental quantum optics, paving the way for applications such as quantum communication, imaging and computing. Single photons have emerged as key information carriers, and various single-photon sources including cold atoms and ions, quantum dots and NV centers in diamond are available. However, despite the variety of technological and material platforms providing quantum light, the tunability of the sources is rather limited to only a few selected frequencies. Moreover, these sources commonly generate weak quantum states with only a few photons per mode

On the other hand, and intuitively opposing single-photon-based quantum optics, strong-field physics intrinsically deals with many photons (typically
>10^{15}

The interaction of intense laser fields with matter gives rise to a plethora of nonlinear physical processes, among which one is the (non-perturbative) generation of higher-order harmonics of the fundamental laser field. The emitted radiation spans a broad coherent spectrum which may range from the generating laser’s frequency,
\omega_{L}
, (typically in the near or mid-IR) to the XUV or even soft X-ray domain. High harmonics do not only constitute a bright tunable table-top source of coherent high-frequency radiation, but the broad, coherent spectrum, spanning many octaves, also is the way to obtain attosecond pulses

About a decade ago, Chekhova, Leuchs and Zukowski reported on methods to create bright entangled light beams, in particular bright squeezed vacuum states, with entanglement in photon numbers and polarization

Later joint experimental-theoretical works by the groups of Lewenstein and Tzallas have shown that the quantum state of both, the fundamental and the harmonic modes after interaction are coherent states. In this work, we introduce a rigorous, comprehensive analytical theory which describes the interaction of intense light with matter (and the generation of high-order harmonics) fully quantum. Key idea is a parametric factorization of the coupled system of light and matter into a
field-driven

In contrast to other works, our ansatz is directly based on the time-dependent Schrödinger equation, without the need ot homodyne/heterodyne or other selection techniques. Furthermore, we analyze and explain, under which conditions certain nonclassical properties of the generated harmonics such as squeezing or negativity of the Wigner function can be achieved. Finally, our model allows predictions under which conditions / for which parameters/systems etc., bright, nonclassical light of desired frequency can be obtained. We demonstrate this by predicting conditions leading to the generation of bright nonclassical light in the UV region.

Starting point of the analytical model is the Pauli-Fierz (PF) Hamiltonian, providing a fully quantum description of nonrelativistically charged particles coupled with electromagnetic fields.

\hat{H}=\sum\limits_{k}\frac{1}{2m_{k}}\left(\,\hat{\vec{p}}_{k}+\frac{e_{k}}{c}\sum\limits_{j}\hat{\vec{A}}_{j}\right)^{2}+\sum\limits_{k\,k^{\prime}}U_{k\,k^{\prime}}+\sum\limits_{j}\hbar\omega_{j}\hat{N}_{j}\;,

(1)

In this equation,
e_{k}
and
m_{k}
are the charges and masses of the particles (index
k
), with
U_{k\,k^{\prime}}
containing both, mutual particle-particle interaction and interaction of each particle with external longitudinal fields. The quantized electromagnetic field modes

(

is the frequency and
\hat{N}_{j}
the photon number operator). In intense fields,
\langle\hat{H}_{el}\rangle\sim\langle\hat{W}_{int}\rangle\ll\langle\hat{H}_{field}\rangle

\Psi(x,q_{j},t)=\psi^{el}(x,\beta_{j}q_{j},t)\cdot\phi^{light}(q_{j},\beta_{j},t)+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{j}),

(2)

where
\beta_{j}=c\sqrt{(2\pi\hbar/\omega_{j}V)}

This ansatz is very beneficial from the strong-field physics’ perspective, as it includes implicitly the impact of the strong fundamental driving field on the system and allows us to describe the systems’ response to the light field as a finite perturbation to the field.
Specifically, field’s vector potential can be divided into a predominantly classical part,
A_{class}(t)

\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi(x,q,t)=\left[\hat{H}_{0}+ex\,[F_{class}(t)+\hat{F}_{\Omega}(t)]\right]\Psi(x,q,t),

(3)

where

and the force operator

\hat{F}_{\Omega}=\beta q\frac{\Omega}{c}\sin\Omega t+i\beta\frac{\Omega}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\cos\Omega t

\left|\langle\beta\partial/\partial q\rangle_{\psi^{el}}\right|\ll\left|\langle\beta q\rangle_{\psi^{el}}\right|

\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi^{el}(x,\beta q,t)=\hat{H}_{0}\psi^{el}(x,\beta q,t)+ex\left[F_{class}(t)+F_{\phi^{light}}(\beta q,t)]\right]\psi^{el}(x,\beta q,t)

\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi^{light}(q,\beta,t)=+e\langle x(\beta q,t)\rangle_{\psi^{el}}\left[F_{class}(t)+\hat{F}_{\Omega}(t)\right]\phi^{light}(q,\beta,t)

\langle x(\beta q,t)\rangle_{\psi^{el}}=\int x|\psi^{el}(x,\beta q,t)|^{2}dx.

(7)

\phi^{light}\neq 0

\approx\frac{\Omega}{c}\beta q\sin\Omega t

However, it can be further improved and generalized in various ways, for example to take into account the collective effects of multiple atomic/molecular systems and multiple resonator modes. The separation ansatz provides the potential for significant acceleration of the numerical calculations compared to the typical numerical solution of the original time-dependent Schrödinger equation, with the numerical schemes being described in the appendix.

So far, we have considered solely the interaction of single emitters/matter systems with intense light. An additional advantage of the presented theory is its universal scalability for an arbitrary number of quantum systems and quantized light modes. This case naturally includes mode-to-mode entanglement. Let us consider exemplary an ensemble of spatially isolated non-Coulomb-interacting emitters in a resonator. Each quantum system

In summary, we have presented a rigorous and general model describing the generation of nonclassical electromagnetic radiation in strongly driven systems. It is based on the factorization and subsequent separation of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation for light and matter into a system of equations, with one equation for the field-driven system and equation(s) for the quantized light mode(s), parametrically coupled via the oscillating dipole of the driven system
\langle x(\beta q,t)\rangle_{\psi^{el}}
. With this theory, we could explain the emergence of nonclassical (high-harmonic) light generation in terms of a nonlinear dependence of this dipole moment
d(\beta q,t)
on the light mode’s coordinate
q
, which becomes particularly pronounced, when the harmonic frequency
\Omega
corresponds to an electronic resonance in the system. The separation into a system of coupled equations furthermore provides the possibility to numerically treat light emission from a macroscopically large multi-dipole systems, which we showed to produce for certain conditions highly intense and significantly non-classical radiation. Moreover, as the theory naturally includes entanglement of light and matter, it provides a natural and universal explanation on the origin of the nonclassical nature of radiation in various excited systems, including atoms, molecules, and possibly semiconductors or even exotic materials/medium such as quantum materials.

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\dfrac{\partial\phi^{light}}{\partial t}=+e\,\langle x\rangle_{\psi^{el}}\cdot\left[F_{class}\left(t\right)+\hat{F}_{\Omega}\left(t\right)\right]\phi^{light}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\hbar\,\dfrac{\partial\psi^{el}}{\partial t}=\hat{H}_{0}\psi^{el}+e\,x\cdot\bigg[F_{class}\left(t\right)+F_{\phi^{light}}\left(\beta{}q\,,t\right)\bigg]\,\psi^{el}\,,

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{\psi^{el}}=\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x\cdot{}|\psi^{el}\left(\vec{r},\,\beta{}q\,,t\right)|^{2}\,d^{3}r\,,\;\;\;\;F_{\phi^{light}}\left(\beta{}q\,,t\right)=\,\left(\phi^{light}\right)^{-1}\hat{F}_{\Omega}\left(t\right)\phi^{light}\,.

It can be seen that the nonlinear dependence on
q
arises from the additional
q
dependence of the dipole moment
\langle x\rangle_{\psi^{el}}
, resulting from the
q
-dependent, parametrically connected electronic state
\psi^{el}=\psi^{el}\left(\vec{r},\,\beta{}q\,,t\right)
. Thus, the nonlinear terms in equation Eq.(
15
) can lead to the generation of non-classical states of light, even if the initial state
\phi^{light}_{0}\left(q\right)
is a normal vacuum or a coherent state.

The procedure is general, allowing the investigation of a large number of emitters
N_{e}
to be arbitrary quantum systems with various initial states. The numerical solution scheme for the multi-emitter case is explained in what follows.

We consider an ensemble of Coulomb-isolated quantum emitters in a resonator, driven by a classical laser pulse of frequency
\omega
and one single quantized light mode of frequency
\Omega
. Each emitter (index
n
) contributes with its oscillating dipole moment (
d_{n}(\beta q,t)=e_{n}\langle x(\beta q,t)\rangle_{n}
), generating radiation of frequency
\Omega
. The collective response

D(\beta q,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{e}}d_{n}(\beta q,t)

(19)

can, under suitable conditions, generate intense non-classical radiation due to the large number of emitters
N_{e}
, as shown in Fig.
3
and Fig.
5
.

To describe the evolution of the quantized mode, represented by the light-state wavefunction (
\phi^{\text{light}}(q,\beta,t)
), we employ a transformation-based solution strategy
my1q
;
Ville
. The interaction term in the light-mode equation contains a nonlocal operator proportional to

i\langle x\rangle_{\psi^{el}}(q,t)\,\cos(\Omega t)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial q},

(20)

which induces both a shift and a deformation of the
q
-coordinate. For sufficiently small
\beta q
, the dipole response can be expanded as

\langle x\rangle_{\psi^{el}}(\beta q,t)\approx\left.\langle x\rangle_{\psi^{el}}(t)\right|_{F_{class}(t)}+(\beta q)f_{1}(t)+(\beta q)^{2}f_{2}(t)+\cdots,

(21)

with
f_{1}(t)\sim\cos(\Omega t)\,\left.\partial\langle x\rangle_{\psi^{el}}/\partial F\right|_{F=F_{class}(t)}
etc. This structure naturally leads to a hierarchy of generalized shift operators,

\exp\left[a_{0}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\right],\qquad\exp\left[a_{1}(t)\,q\,\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\right],\qquad\exp\left[a_{2}(t)\,q^{2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\right],\ \dots,

(22)

closely related to extended forms of the transformations introduced in
my1q
;
Ville
. These operators collectively generate the full
q
-dependence of the light-state evolution.

In practice, the dominant long-time contribution arises from the resonant harmonic at frequency
\Omega
, whose effect accumulates linearly with the interaction time. Assuming that the electronic response is identical for each laser pulse, and that the resonant harmonic dominates the non-classical buildup, we replace the explicit time dependence by the real part of the Fourier coefficient
d_{\Omega}(q)
. The evolution operator for the light mode then takes the form

\exp\left[-\frac{\beta\Omega}{c}\,d_{\Omega}(q)\,t_{\mathrm{int}}\,\partial_{q}\right]\phi^{\text{light}}_{0}(q)=\exp\left[a\,f_{\Omega}(q)\,\partial_{q}\right]\,\phi^{\text{light}}_{0}(q),

(23)

where (
t_{\mathrm{int}}
) is the interaction time and
f_{\Omega}(q)
is an appropriate polynomial or rational fit to
d_{\Omega}(q)
, obtained from the TDSE simulations of the electronic subsystem (Fig.

Choosing

\exp\left[a,f_{\Omega}(q)\partial_{q}\right]\,\phi^{\text{light}}_{0}(q)=\phi^{\text{light}}_{0}\big(Q^{-1}[Q(q)+a]\big),\quad Q(q)=\int_{0}^{q}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{f_{\Omega}(q^{\prime})}.

Analysis of the dynamic response of the new interchange channel closely under the existing line
Authors: Yunsi Liu, Yueyuan Huang, Yu Huang, Yonglin An, Yaxiong Peng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe vibration caused by the existing operating metro has a negative impact on the construction of new metro lines and the nearby surroundings. The focus of this study is the confined interchange area between Beijing Metro Line 17 and Shilihe Station of Line 10. In the study area, the interchange between Shilihe Station of Line 10 and Line 17 has a confined frame structure that is already in operation without any overburden between the existing line and the new line; therefore, the vibration of the operating subway interferes with the construction. The measured and numerical simulation results show that the vibration response has an attenuating effect in the frame structure from top to bottom, and these studies are related to the train operating speeds of 20 km/h, 40 km/h, 60 km/h and 80 km/h. The type and spacing of the I-beams also have an effect, and the higher the train speed, the greater the vibration response. The principle of equivalent stiffness of I-beam is proposed, and the functional relationship between equivalent stiffness and I-beam parameters is deduced. The models of no vibration amplification effect, weak vibration amplification effect and strong vibration amplification effect are established, and it is obtained that when the I-beam is I20b and the spacing is 0.8 m, the equivalent stiffness is maximum, the vibration response of the frame structure is minimum, the vibration load is attenuated in the frame structure, and the vibration amplification effect of the soil on both sides is smaller. The study of the vibration amplification effect provides some theoretical guidance for the construction of similar projects.Keywords: Train vibration, Flat-topped and straight-walled station, Close-fitting, Frame structure, I-beamSubject terms: Engineering, Civil engineering

Introduction

As subway construction progresses, numerous challenges have arisen in the construction of tunnels employing the concealed excavation method1,2. For example, the construction of new metro lines cannot avoid crossing existing lines3,4Simultaneously, the vibrations emanating from train operations on the existing line will exert an impact on the construction of the adjacent, newly excavated line5–8Studying the dynamic response characteristics and displacement change patterns of newly constructed closely spaced subway tunnels, subject to vibration loads from overhead trains, is of paramount importance for ensuring the long-term safety and stability of tunnel operations9–11.

The researchers developed a track-tunnel-ground interaction model to determine the propagation characteristics of train vibrations. By analysing acceleration and velocity. The results showed that a vibration amplification zone exists at a certain distance from the vibration centre and that structures located in the vibration amplification zone will be sensitive to ground vibrations caused by metro trains12–14. A method has been proposed that can effectively predict tunnel and ground vibrations caused by metro trains. Firstly, the vehicle-track subsystem is used to determine the track-tunnel interaction forces, and then the resulting forces are applied to the 2.5D FEM-PML tunnel-soil system, and eventually the accuracy is verified by comparison15. A numerical track-tunnel-strata model has been developed to study the dynamic response of tunnels under train vibration loads, and the vibration response law of metro operating trains in a composite layer of clay and silt has been analysed, and the long-term cumulative deformation of the powder-fine sand layer under train loads has been investigated using a layered summation method16,17.The measured vibration waves are often accompanied by noise and other influencing factors, which need to be filtered. To overcome the limitation that the variational mode decomposition algorithm cannot flexibly adjust the frequency range and spectral location to decompose the signal, has been proposed to decompose the measured signal18,19. A modified multiplicative alternating direction method is used to solve the formed variational model, thus realising the multi-channel and fixed frequency decomposition of vibration waves.

Increasing the material strength of the frame structure contributes to the overall stability, with lower modulus of elasticity frame structure materials having lower strength and higher losses. As a result, vibrations in sensitive areas are reduced while avoiding wave reflection into the track20,21. Underpass tunnel excavation inevitably causes superstructure movements and a series of numerical analyses have been carried out to investigate the behaviour and damage mechanisms of frame buildings with independent foundations due to tunnel-induced ground movements22,23.

However, less research has been carried out on the analysis of the dynamic response and support effect under the influence of train vibration loads for a metro station with a flat roof and straight walls. Therefore, this study first analyses the dynamic response law through field tests, and then builds a 3D numerical model through Flac3D to analyse the variation law of the displacement of the close-fitting part caused by the arrangement of different types of I-beams and different spacing of I-beams under the action of train vibration load, which provides a new idea for the construction of other similar close-fitting stations under the action of train vibration load. It provides a new idea for the construction of other similar close-packed stations under train vibration loads.

Model boundary conditions and dynamic parameters

The dimensions of the model developed in this study should be large enough so that the external boundaries can represent an infinitely extended medium, i.e., both horizontal and vertical displacements vanish at both lateral boundaries.Therefore, it is established that the existing Line 10 tunnel is 40 m away from both boundaries, the new interchange station is 30 m away from both boundaries, and the bottom of the model is 30 m away from the bottom plate of the interchange station.The following assumptions were made to facilitate the numerical simulation calculations:

Rocks and soils generally exhibit anisotropic behavior. The stratigraphic representation is simplified based on the geological survey report. The effect of groundwater hydrostatic pressure is not considered in the model. Initial in-situ stresses are considered only for soil self-gravitational stresses, while tectonic stresses in rocks and soils are neglected.

In the initial static equilibrium calculation, the model temporarily turns off the dynamic calculation function. In this calculation, the original time-domain waveform is filtered and its first-order integral is reduced to a dynamic load in the form of a sinusoidal waveform applied to the bottom of the existing station. The applied dynamic load consists of three parts: the first part is applied along the model in the Y-direction (along the existing station) with a peak acceleration of 2 m/s2; the second part is applied in the X-direction with a peak acceleration of 1 m/s2; and the third part is applied in the Z-direction with a peak acceleration of 0.8 m/s2. The dynamic wave lasts for 6 m/s2. In FLAC3D, the dynamic load is usually entered into the model as a “readout” in the form of a FISH function which creates the acceleration time history and stores it in the table at 0. 002s time intervals. Results

Impact of different types of I-beams

Vibration acceleration

In order to study the vibration response characteristics of various types of I-beams in the structure of the new interchange, the study selected three types of I-beams for modeling: I16, I18, and I20b. The spacing between the I-beams was set at 3.2 m. For the simulation calculations, the study considered four train speed conditions: station deceleration, acceleration, and full-speed passing at 20 km/h, 40 km/h, 60 km/h, and 80 km/h.The point peak acceleration (PPA) values at each monitoring point within monitoring section ① (center of the dense patch), monitoring section ③ (center of the non-dense patch), and monitoring section ⑤ (non-dense patch area) were extracted and are presented in Fig. 16.

Plot of PPA changes at each monitoring point when using different I-beams.(a)~(d) represents the train speeds of 20, 40, 60, and 80 km/h, respectively.

From the analysis of Fig. 16a to d, it can be observed that the vibration acceleration of the frame structure within the interchange channel increases as the train running speed increases; Within the same cross-section, the vibration acceleration of the frame structure of the interchange channel decreases sequentially from top to bottom. However, there is an inflection point at point A2, leading to the phenomenon known as the ′amplification effect′. This effect is caused by the fact that the vibration attenuation of the soil body is weaker than that of the structural metal material. Consequently, the vibration wave is superimposed onto the structure twice after the initial vibration of the soil body.The vibration acceleration value of the lower guideway of the interchange passage is small, and the impact of train vibration on the lower guideway is small, which is consistent with the measured situation ;the same conditions and the same cross-section, the selection of I16 I-beam than I20b I-beam point peak acceleration (PPA), larger I-beam cross-section leads to greater structural rigidity, vibration resistance effect is more obvious.

By simulating the effects of different train speeds and I-beam types on the vibration of the interchange frame structure, the vibration signals of monitoring points A1, A2 and A4 in section ① in model Fig. 12 were extracted and decomposed, and the frequency-amplitude relationship Fig. 17 was obtained. As illustrated in Fig. 17a, during low-speed operation at a vehicle speed of 20 km/h, two peaks are observed at monitoring point A1 in the closely packed area of the frame structure utilizing I16 I-beams. These peaks are distributed within the frequency ranges of 32–38 Hz and 72–88 Hz, respectively. In contrast, under other operating conditions, only a single peak is present.From Fig. 17a to d, it can be observed that as the train speed increases, the number of frequency intervals where significant vibrations occur also increases. Specifically, the amplitude reaches peak values within four distinct frequency ranges: 12–18 Hz, 30–40 Hz, 52–56 Hz, and 72–88 Hz.;When the train speed is 20 km/h, the vibration acceleration at point A2 using I16 I-beam is 0.0003 m/s² greater than at point A4, and using I18 I-beam, the vibration acceleration at point A2 is 0.0002 m/s² greater than at point A4. At a train speed of 80 km/h, the frequency amplitude at point A2 using I16 I-beam is 0.0012 m/s² greater than at point A4, and using I18 I-beam, the frequency amplitude at point A2 is 0.0004 m/s² greater than at point A4. It can be seen that as the train speed increases, the vibration amplification effect becomes more pronounced, and as the stiffness of the frame structure increases, the vibration amplification effect diminishes.

Displacement response

As shown in Fig. 18, the final deformation profile of the new interchange tunnel exhibits a V-shape, with the maximum settlement occurring directly under the existing line. The settlement gradually decreases as one moves away from the existing line and eventually stabilizes at a distance of 25 m from the existing line, indicating that it is no longer influenced by loads (including both self-weight and vibration loads) beyond this point.The settlement value of the new interchange channel directly under the train is the largest, comparing the initial support with different I-beam I20b, I18, I16 as support, the maximum deformation in the top plate of the upper guide hole is 7.8 mm, 9.2 mm, 13.1 mm, respectively, and the selection of I20b I-beam is the smallest settlement in the close part of the I20b I-beam.Therefore, in the project, I-beam I20b can be used to support, and at the same time, appropriate reinforcement of the close-packed parts to prevent excessive settlement.

As shown in Fig. 19, the effect of different I-beam spacing on the vibration acceleration of the new structure is as follows:

(1) When selecting the monitoring section ① at the full-speed station approach, it is observed that, with an I-beam spacing of 4.0 m, the maximum acceleration of 0.0172 m/s² occurs at the top of A1 within the initial support structure of the interchange tunnel. Conversely, when the I-beam spacing is reduced to 0.8 m, the maximum acceleration decreases to 0.0046 m/s² at the same location. This indicates that, as the spacing of the I-beams increases, the vibration response of the structure becomes more pronounced. Additionally, monitoring sections ③ and ⑤ exhibit similar trends.

(2) From the monitoring section ①, it is evident that the larger the I-beam spacing, the more pronounced the vibration amplification effect becomes. When the I-beam spacing is 4.0 m, the peak particle acceleration (PPA) of A2 and A4 is 0.0116 m/s² and 0.0072 m/s² respectively, resulting in an amplification value of 0.0044 m/s² and an amplification rate of 61.1%. Conversely, when the I-beam spacing is reduced to 0.8 m, the PPA of A2 and A4 decreases to 0.0028 m/s² and 0.0022 m/s² respectively, with an amplification value of 0.0006 m/s² and an amplification rate of 27.3%. Meanwhile, monitoring sections ③ and ⑤ exhibit similar trends.

(3) The amplification effect diminishes as the distance from the vibration source increases. For instance, in monitoring section ⑤ (a non-dense area), when the I-beam spacing is set to 4.0 m, the peak particle acceleration (PPA) amplification values for A2 and A4 are 0.0036 m/s² and 0.0042 m/s² respectively. The overall amplification value for this section, considering both A2 and A4, might be derived as 0.0006 m/s², yielding an amplification rate of 16.7%. Consequently, the vibration amplification effect in this area is not pronounced.

The vibration signals from monitoring points A1, A2, and A4 in section ① of the model depicted in Fig. 12 have been extracted and decomposed. The resulting frequency-amplitude relationship is illustrated in Fig. 20a. When the vehicle operates at a low speed of 20 km/h, two peaks are observed in the vibration signal at point A1, situated at the closely packed area of the frame structure where I-beams are spaced 4 m apart. These peaks fall within the frequency ranges of 32–38 Hz and 76–84 Hz. Under other working conditions, only one peak is noted at point A1.From Fig. 20a, b, c, and d, a clear pattern emerges regarding the frequency amplitudes at monitoring points A2 and A4. Specifically, when the train operates at a speed of 20 km/h and the I-beam spacing is 0.8 m, the frequency amplitude at A2 is 0.00008 m/s² higher than at A4. As the I-beam spacing increases to 4 m (while maintaining the same train speed), the difference in frequency amplitude between A2 and A4 enlarges to 0.0006 m/s². Similarly, when the train speed rises to 60 km/h with an I-beam spacing of 0.8 m, the frequency amplitude at A2 exceeds that at A4 by 0.00018 m/s². At an I-beam spacing of 4 m and a train speed of 60 km/h, the difference further widens to 0.0008 m/s². Overall, these observations indicate that both an increase in I-beam spacing and an increase in train speed contribute to a more pronounced vibration magnification effect.

From this, it can be observed that the denser the initial support I-beam arrangement in the interchange tunnel, the greater the overall structural rigidity, the better the vibration-resistant performance, and the smaller the structural settlement will be.

Vibration propagation

From the previous study, the deformation of the frame structure is mainly concentrated in the top plate of the upper level, and the deformation of the frame frame structure of the lower level is not large; because it is a close-fitting structure, the deformation of the top plate of the upper level of the frame structure directly affects the running track of the existing line subway track on the upper level.

The parameters of the frame structure remain constant. The faster the speed of the train running in the proximity to the tunnel wall, the greater the vertical deformation at the center of the top plate, taking on a ‘V’ shape due to the deformation. The stronger the contact between the wheels of the subway train and the train track, the more intense the vibration response transmitted through the frame structure, which is directly influenced by the vibration source .

When the train speed is kept constant, changing the I-beam parameters of the frame structure results in different deformations. Specifically, when the I-beam type is I20b and the I-beam spacing is 0.8 m, the deformation of the frame structure is smaller. Conversely, if the I-beam spacing is larger or the I-beam strength is too small, it will adversely affect the overall strength of the frame structure.When the upper train operation generates vibration loads that are applied to the frame structure, the deformation will be larger. Conversely, when the spacing and strength of the I-beams are appropriately set, the deformation will be smaller. Furthermore, when the overall strength of the frame structure is sufficient, different spacing and types of I-beams will influence the vibration resistance properties of the frame structure, ultimately affecting its deformation. This deformation, in turn, is influenced by the propagation path of the vibrations.

From Fig. 27, it can be observed that when there is no soil on both sides of the frame structure, the vibrating load is transferred solely through the frame structure to the lower base. However, when soil is present on both sides, the vibrating load can be transferred not only to the lower base but also laterally to the soil on both sides. As a result, the vibration load on the soil on both sides increases. A vibration amplification area emerges at the boundary between the frame structure and the soil layer, and this area is concentrated near the upper layer of the frame structure.

Based on previous studies, only horizontal vibrations exhibit a vibration amplification phenomenon, while vertical vibration waves undergo refraction and amplification as they propagate to the interface between the frame structure and the soil layer. Among them, I20b has the largest modulus of elasticity and I16 the smallest; when the modulus of elasticity is large, the vibration load attenuates more when it is transmitted in the frame structure, and the vibration load is smaller when it is transmitted to the soil on both sides. For different I-beam spacing, 0.8 spacing is the smallest settlement, and the smaller the range of vibration amplification effect. Considering the frame structure as a whole, changing the spacing of I-beam is to change the overall stiffness of the frame structure. The greater the stiffness, the more the vibration load is attenuated when it is transmitted in it, and the load transmitted to the soil on both sides is smaller, which leads to a smaller range of vibration amplification effect.

Conclusion

Based on the new Beijing Metro Line 17 project, which closely intersects with the existing line, a specific study was conducted through on-site monitoring and numerical simulation. The main content of the study is to analyze the dynamic response and support effectiveness of the closely adjacent interchange station under train vibration loads. The main conclusions of the study are as follows:

(1) The numerical calculation results are basically consistent with the actual measured values.When the train load passes through, the time-domain waveform has obvious peaks. The acceleration response inside the close-fitting frame structure decreases with increasing distance from the closely packed area, and the vibration primarily affects the upper frame structure, with minimal impact on the lower part; In frame structures, the vertical acceleration of vibration decreases with distance from the source of vibration; However, the vibration response in terms of horizontal vibration acceleration at the junction between the frame structure and the soil body will suddenly increase, resulting in a vibration amplification effect. Along the excavation direction horizontally, the frame structure that is not closely adhered to the tunnel wall exhibits the same trend as the closely adhered frame structure. However, the amplitude of vibration in the non-closely adhered frame structure gradually decreases as the horizontal distance increases, indicating an obvious attenuation phenomenon in energy transfer. At the same time, the vibration amplification effect appears to be significantly weakened.

(2) There are three reasons for the settlement of the close-fitting station: the compression settlement of the soil is the largest, followed by the deflection deformation of the existing structure, and the elastic deflection deformation of the second lining of the new structure is the smallest. The main performance of flat-topped and straight-walled stations is the overall vertical displacement of the structure under the action of train load. The settlement of the sticking part is smaller than that of the non-sticking part, but the displacement difference at the junction of the sticking part is obvious.

(3) For different I-beams commonly used in the project, the displacement curves of different I-beams arranged at the close contact position showed the same trend of the displacement curves: first increasing, then decreasing, and then tending to equilibrium. Then the I-beams were arranged at different spacings, and it was found that the 20b I-beams with a spacing of 0.8 m were the most suitable.

(4) Changing the type and spacing of the materials that closely fit the frame structure only controls the internal vibration response of the frame structure. The vibration response at the boundary between the frame structure and the soil is not controlled, which affects the safety of the structure.

The non-existence of some Galois representations of moderate degree in small characteristic
Alexandru Ghitza and Takuya Yamauchi

Abstract
. Refining arguments of Moon [ Mo1 , Mo2 ] , under the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, we prove the non-existence of irreducible mod 2 2 Galois representations unramified outside 2 2 of dimensions ⩽ 4 \leqslant 4 , and of totally real such representations of dimensions ⩽ 8 \leqslant 8 . We also prove the non-existence of irreducible totally real mod 3 3 representations unramified outside 3 3 of dimensions ⩽ 4 \leqslant 4 . We show unconditionally that the image of an irreducible mod 2 2 representation ρ ¯ : G ℚ ⟶ GSp 4 ​ ( 𝔽 ¯ 2 ) \overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GSp}_{4}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}) that is unramified outside 2 2 must be large. Under GRH, we then deduce the non-existence of such representations.



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field,
d
a positive integer, and
p
a prime.
The non-existence of
d
-dimensional mod
p
Galois representations of

G_{K}:={\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
with small ramification plays an important role in proving Serre’s modularity conjecture
(cf.
[
KW
]
for odd Galois representations with
K=\mathbb{Q}
and
d=2
).

We will call a Galois representation
p
-ramified if it is unramified outside
\{p\}
.
The first results on
p
-ramified Galois representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
are due to Tate
[
Ta
]
for
p=2
and Serre
[
Se1
]
for
p=3
, followed by several authors who attacked this kind of problem for small
p,d
, and
K
with
[K:\mathbb{Q}]\leqslant 2
(
[
Mo2
]
,
[
MT
]
,
[
Sen
]
,
[
GM
]
).

In this paper, we prove non-existence results of two distinct flavors, using two different methods.

The first type is conditional on the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis and builds on the methods developed by Moon in
[
Mo1
,
Mo2
]
:

Theorem A
.

Assume GRH.

There exist no irreducible
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
with
d\in\{2,3,4\}
.

There exist no irreducible totally real
3
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{3})
with
d\in\{2,3,4\}
.

There exist no irreducible totally real
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
with
d\in\{5,6,7,8\}
.

Note that the case
p=2
,
d\leqslant 4
was proved in
[
Mo2
]
by Moon without using GRH, but under the assumption that
\mathbb{Q}_{\overline{\rho}}:=\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{{\rm Ker}(\overline{\rho})}
is totally real.

As an application of Theorem A, we show that the case
p=2
,
d=4
together with a straightforward argument gives

Corollary B
.

Assume GRH.
There exist no self-dual
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{5}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
of length
2
.

Note that “of length
2
” is used here in the representation-theoretic sense, namely that the Galois module has an irreducible submodule and the quotient by it is irreducible.
(This is not to be confused with Moon’s notion of
p
-length, which is about finite groups and plays an essential role in the proof of Theorem A.)
We also remark that for each subfield
F
of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}
, any
2
-ramified self-dual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{5}(F)
contains the trivial representation as a direct factor, so “of length
2
” is the best we can hope for.

The second flavor of result we present is unconditional and focuses on symplectic representations, starting with the
{\rm GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-valued case:

Theorem C
.

There exist no irreducible
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

The proof uses the isomorphism
{\rm GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\simeq S_{6}
and associates to
\overline{\rho}
a rational polynomial of degree at most
6
, approach that may also be of use in the study of Galois representations coming from
2
-torsion points on abelian surfaces, as in
[
GY
]
.

In the more general
{\rm GSp}_{4}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
-valued case we show:

Theorem D
.

Let
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GSp}_{4}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
be an irreducible
2
-ramified representation.
Then
{\rm Im}(\overline{\rho})
is either isomorphic to the Suzuki group
{\rm Sz}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})
for some odd
r>1
, or is equal to
{\rm Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{s}})
for some
s>1
.

In particular, there exist no irreducible
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GSp}_{4}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
such that
|{\rm Im}(\overline{\rho})|<29120
.

We note that the proof may be applicable to representations of
G_{K}
for number fields
K\neq{\bf Q}
.
The key ingredients are: the classification of maximal subgroups inside
{\rm GSp}_{4}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
, the non-existence results for
2
-dimensional representations over certain quadratic fields, and the Jones–Roberts database of number fields
[
JR
]
.
Eventually, the problem is reduced to the case of Theorem C.

Once Theorem D is established, we can use the techniques from the proof of Theorem A to show:

Corollary E
.

Assume GRH.
There exist no irreducible
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GSp}_{4}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
.

A crucial ingredient, used in many places in this paper as well as in several of the key references, is provided by the discriminant bounds of Odlyzko, Poitou, and Serre, and the related tables of discriminant bounds computed by Odlyzko, see
[
Od
]
.
This is also where the dependency on GRH stems from.

This paper is organized as follows.
After some preparatory work in Section
2
, we build on Moon’s methods from
[
Mo1
,
Mo2
]
in Section
3
to prove Theorem A, then obtain Corollary B as a consequence in Section
4
.
In Section
5
we consider
{\rm GSp}4(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-valued representations and prove Theorem C, and in Section
6
we extend this to obtain a proof of Theorem D and Corollary E.
The appendix is devoted to detailing the Magma (
[
BCP
]
) and SageMath (
[
Sage
]
) computations used in this paper; we hope this will facilitate reproducibility.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Number fields of small degree

The L-functions and modular forms database
[
LMFDB
]
includes databases computed by Jones and Roberts
[
JR
]
that contain (in particular) all
p
-ramified number fields of degree
n\leqslant 7
for
p<102
.
From this we extract:

Proposition 2.1

(Jones–Roberts)
.

The only degrees
n\leqslant 7
for which there exist
p
-ramified extensions are:

•

for
p=2
:
n\in\{2,4\}
;

•

for
p=3
:
n\in\{2,3,6\}
.

As Moon points out in the last paragraph of
[
Mo2
]
, this imposes divisibility conditions on the degrees of
p
-ramified extensions:

Corollary 2.2
.

There are no
2
-ramified Galois extensions whose degree has prime-to-
2
part in the set
\{3,5,7\}
.
There are no
3
-ramified Galois extensions whose degree has prime-to-
3
part in the set
\{4,5,7\}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be the Galois group of a
p
-ramified Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
of degree
n
, let
P
be its
p
-Sylow subgroup, and consider the fixed field
K^{P}
.
The extension
K^{P}/\mathbb{Q}
is
p
-ramified and has degree equal to the prime-to-
p
part of
n
.
For
p\in\{2,3\}
, the existence of
K^{P}/\mathbb{Q}
is constrained by Proposition
2.1
.
∎

2.2.
Modular representations of some finite groups

Note that
2
is not contained in the list of forbidden
3
-free parts of degrees in Corollary
2.2
, so we cannot rule it out without further assumptions.
To be able to avoid it in Proposition
3.3
, we use the following consequence of Brauer’s Theorem on the independence of modular characters: if
G
is a finite group and
p
is a prime number, the number of (isomorphism classes of) irreducible
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-representations of
G
is equal to the number of conjugacy classes of elements of order coprime to
p
in
G
.
(See for instance
[
Se2
, Corollary 3 to Theorem 42, Section 18.2]
.)
A well-known immediate implication is that every irreducible
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-representation of a
p
-group is trivial.
A more obscure consequence that we will use is:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
m\geqslant 1
an integer, and consider the generalized dihedral group
G=(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
.
There are two irreducible
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-representations of
G
, and both of them are one-dimensional.

Proof.

We get two irreducible one-dimensional
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-representations by composing the canonical surjection
G\to(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
with the two characters of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
given by
1\mapsto\pm 1
.

To see that this exhausts the irreducible
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-representations of
G
, we have to count the number of conjugacy classes of elements of order coprime to
p
.
But
G
has

•

1
conjugacy class of the identity element;

•

1
conjugacy class consisting of all
p^{m}
elements of order
2
;

•

(p^{m}-1)/2
conjugacy classes of elements of order
p
(each of these classes has cardinality two).

Therefore Brauer’s result gives us precisely two irreducible
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-representations.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The same argument and conclusion apply to the case of irreducible
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-representations of the dihedral group
D_{n}=(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
for any odd integer
n
divisible by
p
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Suppose
d\geqslant 2
.

(a)

There are no irreducible Galois representations
\overline{\rho}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
such that the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}_{\overline{\rho}}
is a
p
-group.

(b)

For
p
odd, there are no
p
-ramified irreducible Galois representations
\overline{\rho}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
such that the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}_{\overline{\rho}}
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Part (a) follows from the already mentioned fact that a
p
-group has no non-trivial irreducible
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-representations.

Part (b) follows directly from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

2.3.
Discriminant upper bounds from
p
-Sylow structure of Galois groups

Fix a prime
p
and let
P
be a
p
-group (possibly trivial).
The
p
-length of
P
is the minimal length of a subnormal series with elementary abelian quotients: in other words, it is the smallest integer
N
for which there exists a series

(1)

P=P^{(1)}\supsetneq P^{(2)}\supsetneq\dots\supsetneq P^{(N)}\supsetneq P^{(N+1)}=\{1\}

such that for all
i=1,\dots,N
:

P^{(i+1)}\text{ is a normal subgroup of }P^{(i)}\text{ and }P^{(i+1)}/P^{(i)}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m_{i}}\text{ for some $m_{i}$}.

We will need a few very simple facts:

•

P
has
p
-length
0
if and only if it is trivial.

•

P
has
p
-length
1
if and only if it is elementary abelian.

•

If
P
is an abelian
p
-group of
p
-length
N
, then the exponent of
P
is
p^{N}
.

•

Both non-abelian groups of order
p^{3}
have
p
-length
2
.

Note also that, as Jones observes in
[
Jo
, Section 2.2]
, a shortest series of the type (
1
) is given by what he calls the Frattini filtration of
P
:

P\supsetneq\Phi(P)\supsetneq\Phi^{2}(P)\dots\supsetneq\Phi^{N}(P)=\{1\},

where for any group
H
,
\Phi(H)
denotes the Frattini subgroup of
H
(that is, the intersection of all maximal subgroups of
H
).
Therefore the
p
-length
N
of
P
is the smallest integer such that
\Phi^{N}(P)
is the trivial group.

Given a finite group
G
, we define the
p
-length of
G
to be the
p
-length of a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
.
(If
p
does not divide the order of
G
, we say that
G
has
p
-length
0
.)

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a
p
-ramified, degree
n
Galois extension.
Let
N
denote the
p
-length of the Galois group
G
of
K/\mathbb{Q}
.
Moon (
[
Mo1
, Proposition 2.4]
) gives the following upper bound for the discriminant of
K
:

d_{K}\mid p^{Cn},\quad\text{where $C$ satisfies}\quad C<N+1+\frac{N}{p-1}.

Therefore the root discriminant of
K
satisfies
|d_{K}|^{1/n}\leqslant p^{C}
.

We tighten Moon’s upper bound by going back to a more precise expression for
C
from
[
Mo1
, Lemma 2.3]
.
We reformulate it as:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite unramified extension.
Let
E/F
be a totally ramified extension with Galois group
I
.
Suppose
I
has
p
-length
N
and fix a series of the form (
1
) for the
p
-Sylow subgroup
P
of
I
.
Let
e_{0}=[I\colon P]
, let
r
be the remainder of the division of
e_{0}
by
p-1
, and let
e_{N}
be the order of
I
.
Then the different of
E/F
divides
p^{C}
, where

C=N+1+\frac{N}{p-1}-\left(\frac{1}{e_{N}}+\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p^{m_{i}-1}}+\frac{r}{p-1}\Big(\frac{1}{e_{0}}-\frac{1}{e_{N}}\Big)\right).

Proof.

The expression from
[
Mo1
, Lemma 2.3]
is

C=N+1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{e_{i-1}}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p^{m_{i}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{e_{i}}+\frac{\alpha_{N}}{e_{N}}\right),

where

e_{i}=e_{0}p^{m_{1}}\dots p^{m_{i}},\quad\alpha_{i}=\left\lfloor\frac{e_{i-1}}{p-1}\right\rfloor+1\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,N.

We observe that since
p\equiv 1\pmod{p-1}
, the integer
r
is also the remainder of the division of
e_{i}
by
(p-1)
for all
i=1,\dots,N
, so that

\alpha_{i}-1=\frac{e_{i-1}-r}{p-1}\quad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,N.

Our claim follows from this and some straightforward algebraic manipulations of Moon’s formula.
∎

We can now state a tighter version of
[
Mo1
, Proposition 2.4]
:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension of degree
n
, such that the Galois group
G
has
p
-length
N
.
Then the
p
-part of the discriminant of
K/\mathbb{Q}
divides
p^{Cn}
, where

C<N+1+\frac{N}{p-1}-\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p^{m_{i}-1}}\right).

Proof.

Identical to the proof of
[
Mo1
, Proposition 2.4]
but using the expression in Lemma
2.6
and the facts that
e_{N}\mid n
and
r\geqslant 0
.
∎

(Note that Jones describes in
[
Jo
, Proposition 2.3]
a very similar reformulation of Moon’s result.)

Here is the strategy we are going to employ:
a priori
we have no control over the quantity in the parenthesis in Proposition
2.7
, so we simply use
C<N+1+N/(p-1)
.
This gives us a bound on
|d_{K}|^{1/n}
, hopefully small enough to appear in Odlyzko’s tables.
The latter give us an upper bound on
n
, say
n\leqslant n_{\text{max}}
.
At this point we return to the expression in Proposition
2.7
and minimize the quantity

\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p^{m_{i}-1}}

under the additional constraint
n\leqslant n_{\text{max}}
(together with other arithmetic constraints).
This results in a smaller upper bound for
C
, hence a smaller upper bound on
|d_{K}|^{1/n}
, and we can start the process over.
In the next section we describe how taking these successive minima over increasingly smaller sets allows us to rule out the existence of the number field
K
.

3.
Non-existence of
{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
-valued representations (Thm A)

Let
d\geqslant 2
.

Given an irreducible
p
-ramified Galois representation
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
, we let
K
be the field fixed by the kernel of
\overline{\rho}
and
G
be the Galois group of
K/\mathbb{Q}
.
We also let
n=[K:\mathbb{Q}]=|G|
.

We gather some consequences of results of Harbater, Hoelscher, and Pollak regarding divisibility by powers of
p
:

Proposition 3.1
.

With the above assumptions:

(a)

if
p=2
, then
16
divides
n
;

(b)

assuming GRH, if
p=3
, then
18
or
27
divides
n
.

Proof.

(a)

K
is a
2
-ramified Galois extension.
By
[
Ha
, Theorem 2.23]
, either
G
is a
2
-group (ruled out by Corollary
2.5
), or
16
divides
n
.

(b)

Here
K
is a
3
-ramified Galois extension, and the analogue of Harbater’s result is
[
Ho
, Corollary E]
.
Unfortunately, it only guarantees divisibility of
n
by
9
.
We give a straightforward modification of Hoelscher’s argument that improves the divisibility conclusion, at the cost of assuming GRH.

If
G
is solvable,
[
Ho
, Corollary D]
says that
27
divides
n
, or
G
is cyclic (ruled out by the irreducibility of
\overline{\rho}
), or
G/p(G)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
So in the solvable case, we get that
27
divides
n
, or
G/p(G)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, in which case
n
is even, but also divisible by
9
by the original form of
[
Ho
, Corollary E]
.

If
G
is non-solvable,
[
Po
, Theorem 2.10]
forces
n\geqslant 660
.
Assuming GRH, Odlyzko’s Table 1 implies that
|d_{K}|^{1/n}\geqslant 27.328
.
On the other hand,
[
Ne
, Theorem III.2.6]
gives the upper bound

|d_{K}|^{1/n}<3^{1+v_{3}(n)},

and the combination implies
v_{3}(n)>2.01
, hence
27\mid n
.∎

The case where the Galois group
G
has
p
-length
0
(so that
K
is tamely ramified at
p
) is easily dealt with:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension whose Galois group
G
has
p
-length
0
.
Let
d\geqslant 2
.

If
p\in\{2,3\}
, there exist no irreducible
p
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
with
\mathbb{Q}_{\overline{\rho}}=K
.

Proof.

With
N=0
and
p=2
, we get from Proposition
2.7
that
|d_{K}|^{1/n}<2
, and then from Odlyzko’s (unconditional) Table 2 that
n<3
, which forces the Galois group to be abelian and hence not have irreducible representations of dimension
>1
.

With
p=3
we get
|d_{K}|^{1/n}<3
, so Odlyzko’s Table 2 gives
n<4
, which we rule out in the same manner.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension whose Galois group
G
has
p
-length
1
.
Let
d\geqslant 2
.

If
p\in\{2,3\}
, there exist no irreducible
p
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
with
\mathbb{Q}_{\overline{\rho}}=K
.

Proof.

For
p=2
and
N=1
we have from Proposition
2.7
:

C<3-\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2^{m_{1}-1}}\right).

This gives an initial bound
|d_{K}|^{1/n}<2^{3}=8
, for which Odlyzko’s Table 2 produces the constraint
n<14
.
Since
16\mid n
by Proposition
3.1
(a), we are done.

For
p=3
and
N=1
we have

C<\frac{5}{2}-\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2\cdot 3^{m_{1}-1}}\right),

giving
|d_{K}|^{1/n}<3^{5/2}<15.589
, and from Odlyzko’s Table 2 we get
n<80
.

But
n
must be a multiple of
18
or
27
and its prime-to-
3
part cannot be in
\{4,5,7\}
by Corollary
2.2
.
The prime-to-
3
part can also not be
1
(by Corollary
2.5
(a)) or
2
: since the
3
-length is
1
, the
3
-Sylow subgroup of
G
is elementary abelian, so if the prime-to-
3
part is
2
then
G\cong(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^{m}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, therefore irreducible representations of dimension
\geqslant 2
are ruled out by Corollary
2.5
(b).

This leaves as only possibility
n=72
, which gives us the tighter bound
C<\frac{5}{2}-\frac{13}{72}<2.320
.
We deduce that
|d_{K}|^{1/n}<12.792
and thus
n<40
, and we are done.
∎

.

Let

.

4.
The self-dual five-dimensional case (Cor B)

In this section we prove

Corollary B
.

Assume GRH.
There exist no self-dual
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{5}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
of length
2
.

Assume
\overline{\rho}
takes the values in
F=\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}
for an integer
r\geqslant 1
.
For a non-degenerate
symmetric
F
-bilinear map
B:F^{\oplus 2n+1}\times F^{\oplus 2n+1}\longrightarrow F
, we define

{\rm O}(B)(F):=\{g\in{\rm GL}_{2n+1}(F)\ |\ B(gx,gy)=B(x,y),\ \forall x,y\in F^{\oplus 2n+1}\}.

By
[
BHRD
, p.23, Theorem 1.5.41]
,
{\rm O}(B)(F)\simeq{\rm O}(J)(F)
where

J
is the anti-diagonal matrix whose anti-diagonal entries are all 1.
By direct computation, it is easy to see that each element of
{\rm O}(J)(F)
can be written as

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}A&0&B\\
0&1&0\\
C&0&D\end{array}\right)

with

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right)\in{\rm Sp}_{2n}(F)
. This is a special feature in the case of even characteristic.
Thus,
{\rm O}(B)(F)\simeq{\rm O}(J)(F)\simeq{\rm Sp}_{2n}(F)
.

We are now ready to prove Corollary B.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\rho}
is
2
-ramified, the determinant representation

\det(\overline{\rho})
factors though pro
2
-group
{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q})
.
Therefore,
\det(\overline{\rho})
has to be trivial since its image inside
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\times}_{2}
has odd cardinality.
Thus, by assumption,
\overline{\rho}^{\vee}\simeq\overline{\rho}
.
It yields

F\subset{\rm End}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\overline{\rho})\simeq{\rm Bil}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\overline{\rho}\times\overline{\rho}^{\vee},F)\simeq{\rm Bil}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\overline{\rho}\times\overline{\rho},F)=

{\rm Sym}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\overline{\rho}\times\overline{\rho},F)\oplus{\rm Alt}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\overline{\rho}\times\overline{\rho},F)

where for
F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]
-modules
V,V_{1}
, and
V_{2}
,
{\rm Bil}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(V_{1}\times V_{2},F)
stands for the space of all
F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]
-bilinear forms on
V_{1}\times V_{2}

over
F
and
{\rm Sym}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(V\times V)
(resp.
{\rm Alt}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(V\times V)
) stands for the space of all
F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]
-symmetric (resp. -alternating) bilinear forms on
V\times V
over
F
. Since
\overline{\rho}
has odd dimension,
{\rm Alt}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\overline{\rho}\times\overline{\rho},F)=0
.
Thus,
{\rm Sym}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\overline{\rho}\times\overline{\rho},F)
contains an element
which comes from
F^{\times}\subset({\rm End}_{F[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\overline{\rho}))^{\times}

and
it means that there exists a non-degenerate symmetric form

such that

{\rm Im}(\overline{\rho})\subset{\rm O}(B)(F)\simeq{\rm O}(J)(F)\subset{\rm GL}_{5}(F)
.
By using the description of

, there exists

is irreducible over

. Thus, one can apply Theorem A to deduce the claim.
∎

5.

{\rm GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-valued representations (Thm C)

In this section, we show the non-existence of irreducible
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.
The method may be useful for
G_{K}
for some other number fields

and has potential
applications to representations arising from the study of
2
-torsion points on abelian surfaces over
K
.

For each integer
n\geqslant 1
,
S_{n}
stands for the
n
-th symmetric group.
The following result is due to Miho Aoki with a minor modification:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension with Galois group
G

and an injective group homomorphism
\iota:G\hookrightarrow S_{n}
.
There exists a separable polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
of degree at most
n

such that
K=\mathbb{Q}_{f}
, the splitting field of
f
. Further, if
G
acts transitively on
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, then
f(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
n
.

Proof.

Put
H:=\iota(G)\subset S_{n}
.
For each
i
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, let
H_{i}
be the stabilizer of
i
and put

G_{i}=\iota^{-1}(H_{i})\subset G
. We also define

N_{i}:=\bigcap_{\sigma\in G}\sigma G_{i}\sigma^{-1}.

Let us consider the orbit decomposition

\{1,2,\ldots,n\}=\coprod_{k=1}^{r}O_{H}(i_{k}).

For each
i_{k}
,
put
K_{i_{k}}:=K^{G_{i_{k}}}
and choose
\alpha_{i_{k}}\in K
such that

K_{i_{k}}=K^{G_{i_{k}}}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{i_{k}})
. For each
\sigma\in G
,
\sigma(K_{i_{k}})=K^{\sigma G_{i_{k}}\sigma^{-1}}
, so the composite of all conjugates of
K_{i_{k}}

is
K^{N_{i_{k}}}
.
Let
f_{i_{k}}(x)
be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha_{i_{k}}
and define

(2)

\widetilde{f}(x):=\prod_{k=1}^{r}f_{i_{k}}(x).

It is easy to see that

\deg(\widetilde{f}(x))=\sum_{k=1}^{r}[K_{i_{k}}:\mathbb{Q}]=\sum_{k=1}^{r}[G:G_{i_{k}}]=\sum_{k=1}^{r}[H:H_{i_{k}}]=\sum_{k=1}^{r}|O_{H}(i_{k})|=n.

We show that
\mathbb{Q}_{\widetilde{f}}=K
: since
\iota
is injective, we have

\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}N_{i_{k}}\subset\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}G_{j}=\iota^{-1}(\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}H_{j})=\iota^{-1}(\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}H_{i_{k}})=\iota^{-1}(\{1\})=\{1\},

therefore

\mathbb{Q}_{\widetilde{f}}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}K^{N_{i_{k}}}=K^{\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}N_{i_{k}}}=K.

It remains to deal with the (possible) non-separability of
\widetilde{f}
.
If there exist
k,k^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,r\}
such that
\alpha_{i_{k}}
is conjugate under the Galois action of
G
to
\alpha_{i_{k^{\prime}}}
, then
f_{i_{k}}(x)=f_{i_{k^{\prime}}}(x)
, so we can keep
f_{i_{k}}(x)
and discard
f_{i_{k^{\prime}}(x)}
.
We obtain a subset
S
of
\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that

f(x):=\prod_{k\in S}f_{i_{k}}(x)

is separable and
\mathbb{Q}_{f}=\mathbb{Q}_{\widetilde{f}}=K
. Clearly

\deg(f(x))\leqslant\deg(\widetilde{f}(x))=n
.

The latter claim follows easily from the construction.
∎

The following result is well-known:

Lemma 6.1

Let

-ramified characters
\overline{\rho}:G_{L}\longrightarrow\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\times}_{2}

Let

be a finite abelian

-ramified extension.
By class field theory,
there exists an ideal

dividing a power of the ideal

such that

is the ring class field with modulus

. Letting

denote the

, we have the natural surjection

Its kernel is described by class field theory (

here

and

By assumption, the orders of both this group and the class group

are powers of

Hence

is a power of

For the second claim, notice that

{\rm SO}^{+}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})
has an index
2
subgroup isomorphic to

\displaystyle{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})\times{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})

\displaystyle={\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})\times{\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})

\displaystyle={\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})\times{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}).

Thus, there exist an extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
of degree at most
2
, and two Galois representations
\tau_{i}:G_{L}\longrightarrow{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})\subset{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})
such that
\overline{\rho}|_{G_{L}}\simeq\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
.
Since
\overline{\rho}
is unramified outside
2
,

L\in\left\{\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})\right\}.

For each finite place
v\nmid 2
of
L
,
(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})(I_{v})=\overline{\rho}(I_{v})=\{1\}
, where
I_{v}
is the inertia group of
v
.
Thus, for each
g\in I_{v}
,

\tau_{1}(g)={\rm diag}(a,a),\quad\tau_{2}(g)={\rm diag}(a^{-1},a^{-1})\quad\text{for some }a\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{2^{r}}.

But
{\rm Im}(\tau_{i})\subset{\rm SL}_{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2^{r}})
, so we conclude that

\tau_{i}(I_{v})=\{1\}
for
i=1,2
. Hence
\tau_{i}
is unramified outside
2
.
Applying
[
MT
]
,
\tau_{i}
has to be reducible. Since
G_{L}
is normal in
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
by Clifford’s theorem,
\overline{\rho}|_{G_{L}}
is isomorphic to the direct sum of four one-dimensional
representations of
G_{L}
that are unramified outside
2
. However, all of them are the trivial representation of
G_{L}
by Lemma
6.1
. Thus,
|{\rm Im}(\overline{\rho})|\leqslant 2
and
\overline{\rho}
can not be irreducible over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}
. We conclude that this case cannot occur.

(3)

G\subseteq{\rm SO}_{4}^{-}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})
.

It is known (cf.
[
BHRD
, Section 7.2.2]
) that
{\rm SO}^{-}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})
has an index
2
subgroup isomorphic to

{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{2r}})={\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{2r}})={\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{2r}}).

Thus,
there exist an extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
of degree at most
2
unramified outside
2
and a continuous group homomorphism
\overline{\rho}|_{G_{L}}:G_{L}\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{2r}})
.
As in the previous case,
\overline{\rho}
cannot be irreducible over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}
.
We conclude that this case cannot occur.

(4)

G\subseteq{\rm Sz}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})
.

In the above classification of the maximal subgroups of
{\rm Sz}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})
,
cases (Sz-1)–(Sz–3) cannot occur since
\overline{\rho}
is irreducible over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}
.

In case (Sz-4), there exists an extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
of degree at most
2
and unramified outside
2
such that

\overline{\rho}(G_{L})
is contained in the dihedral subgroup of the group in question.
Thus, by a similar argument as before,
\overline{\rho}
cannot be irreducible over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}
.

The only possibility is therefore (Sz-5):
G={\rm Sz}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}})
for some odd integer
r
.
But
G\subseteq{\rm Sz}(\mathbb{F}_{2})=C_{5}\rtimes C_{4}
(the Frobenius group
of order
20
) can be seen to be impossible by the same argument as above, since it contains the dihedral group
D_{10}
of order
10
.

The last claim in the statement of Theorem D follows from the fact that
|{\rm Sz}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{3}})|=29120
and
|{\rm Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{2}})|=979000
.
∎

Summing up, we have

Corollary E
.

Assume GRH.
There exist no irreducible
2
-ramified representations
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow{\rm GSp}_{4}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2})
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem A, under GRH we have

[\mathbb{Q}_{\overline{\rho}}:\mathbb{Q}]=|{\rm Im}(\overline{\rho})|<4800.

Thus, the claim follows from the last part of Theorem D.
∎

A.1.
Successive minima calculations (in SageMath)

The SageMath function
allmin
given below takes a list of possible degrees of number fields, a prime
p
and a value of the
p
-length, and returns the minimum over the list of the expression

\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p^{m_{i}-1}}

that appears in the bound of Proposition
2.7
.
This minimum is then used according to the strategy described at the end of Section
2
, in conjunction with Odlyzko’s tables of discriminant bounds, as described in the proofs of Propositions
3.4
and
3.5
.

def allmin(nlst, p, length):
themin = 100000
theminat = 0
for n in nlst:
x = mymin(n, p, length)
if x < themin:
themin = x
theminat = n
return themin, theminat

def mymin(n, p, length):
themin = 100000
m = n.valuation(p)
if length == 1:
s = 1/((p-1)*p^(m-1)) + 1/n
if s < themin:
themin = s
elif length == 2:
for m1 in srange(1, m+1):
s = 1/((p-1)*p^(m1-1)) + 1/((p-1)*p^(m-m1-1)) + 1/n
if s < themin:
themin = s
elif length == 3:
for m1 in srange(1, m+1):
for m2 in srange(1, m-m1+1):
s = 1/((p-1)*p^(m1-1)) + 1/((p-1)*p^(m2-1)) +\
1/((p-1)*p^(m-m1-m2-1)) + 1/n
if s < themin:
themin = s
return themin

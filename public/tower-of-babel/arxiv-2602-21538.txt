Normal-ordered equivalent of the Weyl ordering of q ^ j ​ p ^ k \hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}
Hendry M. Lim

Abstract
The problem of quantizing a bivariate dynamical system can be reduced to evaluating the ordering of q ^ j ​ p ^ k \hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k} . Here, we consider the Weyl ordering of q ^ j ​ p ^ k \hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k} that is then expressed in term of the annihilation a ^ \hat{a} and creation a ^ † \hat{a}^{\dagger} operator. The explicit formula for the normal-ordered equivalent (all a ^ † \hat{a}^{\dagger} ’s preceeding all a ^ \hat{a} ’s) of the resulting expression is then given, and some relations are discussed.



I
Introduction

A bivariate dynamical system is typically described in a 2-dimensional phase space by the canonical position
q
and canonical momentum
p
, endowed with the Poisson bracket
\left\{q,p\right\}=1
. In an attempt to quantize (i.e., build a quantum mechanical description of) the system, we replace
q
by its operator equivalent
\hat{q}
, and
p
by
\hat{p}
, defining a Hilbert space endowed by the commutator
\left[{\hat{q}},{\hat{p}}\right]=i\hbar
. Doing this, however, comes with a problem: quantizing terms like
qp
introduces ordering ambiguity; we may have
\hat{q}\hat{p}
, or
\hat{p}\hat{q}
, or
\pi\hat{q}\hat{p}+(1-\pi)\hat{p}\hat{q}
, etc. The obstruction to quantization culminates in the no-go theorem of Groenewold and Van-Hove, which states that there is no quantization map that can globally preserve the Poisson bracket structure
Curtright et al. 2013
. Nevertheless, quantization of a classical system does result in a quantum system that may reasonably mimic the classical system
Ben Arosh et al. 2021
;
Chia et al. 2020
;
Lim et al. 2025
, making the procedure worth doing in theoretical approaches.

In this work, we consider the combinatorics from choosing the Weyl ordering during the quantization procedure
Weyl 1927
. This ordering is particularly important since it corresponds to the paradigmatic Wigner representation of quantum systems in phase space
Wigner 1932
, forming the so-called Wigner-Weyl correspondence. We show that the analysis of Weyl-ordered quantization for a bivariate dynamical system can be reduced to evaluating the Weyl ordering of the operator
\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}
. Our contribution is to express the Weyl ordering in terms of the widely-used annihilation
\hat{a}
and creation
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
operator, then rewrite the result into its normal-ordered equivalent, where all
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
’s precede all
\hat{a}
’s in each term.

II
Discussion

II.1
Quantizing a bivariate dynamical system boils down to ordering
\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}

A general bivariate classical system is defined by the equations

\displaystyle q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=A(q,p);

(1a)

\displaystyle p^{\prime}

\displaystyle=B(q,p),

(1b)

where the prime denotes differentiation with respect to time. We consider the case where
A
and
B
are polynomials in
q
and
p
,

A(q,p,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{jk}q^{j}p^{k},

(2)

and likewise for
B
. Formally, quantizing this system means finding a quantum system that is
expected
to generate the dynamics. The quantum expected dynamics corresponding to the classical equation of motion is obtained by replacing
q
and
p
with the corresponding quantum expectation values
\left\langle{\hat{q}}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle{\hat{p}}\right\rangle
,

\displaystyle\left\langle{\hat{q}}\right\rangle^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\hat{A}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{W}};

(3a)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\hat{p}}\right\rangle^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\hat{B}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{W}},

(3b)

where we have kept the hats inside the bra-kets to emphasize the noncommuting properties of the operators involved. In this work, we specifically consider the Weyl ordering, as indicated by the subscript
\mathcal{W}
. In Weyl-ordered quantization, an expression is mapped to the average of all possible operator orderings; for example,
qp
is mapped to
{\left(\hat{q}\hat{p}+\hat{p}\hat{q}\right)}/{2}

Hall 2013
. We have

\left\langle{\hat{A}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{W}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{jk}}{N_{jk}}\left\langle{\sum_{\omega}\left(\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}\right)_{\omega}}\right\rangle,

(4)

and likewise for
\left\langle{\hat{B}}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{W}}
. Here
N_{jk}=(j+k)!/j!k!
is the number of possible orderings
\omega
of
\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}
. We can treat each term in the series on its own, whence
N_{jk}^{-1}\sum_{\omega}\left(\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}\right)_{\omega}
is just the Weyl ordering of
\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}
.

II.2
The ladder operators

Typically, it is practical to introduce the annihilation
\hat{a}
and creation
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
operators by

\hat{a}=\frac{\hat{q}+i\hat{p}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}},

(5)

satisfying
\left[{\hat{a}},{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}\right]=1
. These operators are useful as many quantum mechanical models can be described as modifications to the quantum simple harmonic oscillator
Ben Arosh et al. 2021
;
Chia et al. 2020
;
Lim et al. 2025
, for which
\hat{a}
annihilates one quantum of energy, while
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
creates one quantum of energy. Moreover, the quantization procedure is more convenient using these operators
Cahill and Glauber 1969a
;
Cahill and Glauber 1969b
;
Lim 2025
. In terms of these operators, we can define the normal ordering and antinormal ordering. With the former, all
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
’s precede all
\hat{a}
; with the latter, we have the opposite
Hall 2013
. The normal ordering is particularly useful since any expression in this ordering is physically measurable (for example, see Section 3.7 of Ref.
Gerry and Knight 2005
). Based on this understanding, we are motivated to rewrite the quantum expected dynamics in terms of
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
, then evaluate the normal-ordered equivalent of the result.

II.4
Symmetry

The coefficients
h_{jkuv}
satisfy

\displaystyle h_{jkuv}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}h_{jku(j+k-2u-v)};

(15a)

\displaystyle h_{jku([j+k-2u]/2)}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{if $j$ and $k$ are odd.}

(15b)

One can show that the product of the factors excluding
\left[x^{u+v}\right](1+x)^{j}(1-x)^{k}
is identical for both

coefficients in both equations, whence the properties are consequences of how the polynomial
(1+x)^{j}(1-x)^{k}
behaves under
x\mapsto-x
. One may argue that these properties are rather trivial, since they translate to the requirement that
{\hat{a}^{\dagger{(j+k-2u-v)}}\hat{a}^{v}}
and
{\hat{a}^{\dagger{v}}\hat{a}^{j+k-2u-v}}
form a conjugate transpose pair; accounting for the factors
i^{k}
and
(-1)^{k}
, we can see that the requirement arises because we are quantizing a real-valued expression.

III
Concluding Remarks

We have evaluated the normal-ordered equivalent of the Weyl ordering of
\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}
using a brute force approach (see also the Supplementary Materials). In the following, we note several other methods to tackle this problem, which should reproduce our end result or give an alternative expression.

Cahill and Glauber
Cahill and Glauber 1969a
formulated an explicit formula to write an unevaluated Weyl ordering of
\hat{a}^{m}\hat{a}^{\dagger{n}}
in terms of normal ordered expressions. Let
\left\{\hat{a}^{m}\hat{a}^{\dagger{n}}\right\}_{\mathcal{W}}
be the Weyl ordering of
\hat{a}^{m}\hat{a}^{\dagger{n}}
(e.g.,
\left\{\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right\}_{\mathcal{W}}={\left(\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\right)}/{2}
). Then,

\left\{\hat{a}^{m}\hat{a}^{\dagger{n}}\right\}_{\mathcal{W}}=\sum_{l=0}^{\min(m,n)}\frac{l!}{2^{l}}\binom{m}{l}\binom{n}{l}\hat{a}^{\dagger{(n-l)}}\hat{a}^{(m-l)}.

(16)

One can thus first write the Weyl ordering of
\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}
in terms of
\left\{\hat{a}^{m}\hat{a}^{\dagger{n}}\right\}_{\mathcal{W}}
before using the above equation.

Furthermore, Blasiak
Blasiak 2005
formulated an explicit formula for the normal-ordered equivalent of any string built out of
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
, called a “boson string”. Let

\hat{X}=\hat{a}^{\dagger{r_{M}}}\hat{a}^{s_{M}}\hat{a}^{\dagger{r_{M-1}}}\hat{a}^{s_{M-1}}\dots\hat{a}^{\dagger{r_{2}}}\hat{a}^{s_{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger{r_{1}}}\hat{a}^{s_{1}}

(17)

be a boson string. Then, its normal-ordered equivalent is given by

\hat{X}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{k=s_{1}}^{s_{1}+s_{2}+\dots+s_{M}}S_{\bm{r},\bm{s}}(k)\hat{a}^{\dagger{(d_{M}+k)}}\hat{a}^{k},&d_{M}\geq 0;\\
\displaystyle\sum_{k=r_{M}}^{r_{1}+r_{2}+\dots+r_{M}}S_{\overline{\bm{s}},\overline{\bm{r}}}(k)\hat{a}^{\dagger{k}}\hat{a}^{(-d_{M}+k)},&d_{M}<0,\end{cases}

(18)

where
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{M})
,
\bm{s}=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{M})
,
\overline{\bm{r}}=(r_{M},r_{M-1},\dots,r_{1})
,
\overline{\bm{s}}=(s_{M},s_{M-1},\dots,s_{1})
,

d_{l}=\sum_{m=1}^{l}(r_{m}-s_{m}),

(19)

and

S_{\bm{r},\bm{s}}(k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\prod_{m=1}^{M}\left(d_{m-1}+j\right)_{s_{m}},

(20)

where
(m)_{n}=m!/(m-n)!
is the falling factorial. One may rewrite the Weyl ordering of
\hat{q}^{j}\hat{p}^{k}
in terms of
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
, and use Blasiak’s formula above to evaluate its normal-ordered equivalent.

&\quad+g(j,1)g(k,2)\frac{jk(k-1)}{2}-g(k,3)\frac{k(k-1)(k-2)}{6}\end{split}

\begin{split}\sum_{P={1}}^{j+k}\sum_{Q={P+1}}^{j+k}\sum_{R={Q+1}}^{j+k}\sum_{T={R+1}}^{j+k}\sum_{U={T+1}}^{j+k}s_{P}s_{Q}s_{R}s_{T}s_{U}&=g(j,5)\sum_{P={1}}^{j}\sum_{Q={P+1}}^{j}\sum_{R={Q+1}}^{j}\sum_{T={R+1}}^{j}\sum_{U={T+1}}^{j}(1)(1)(1)(1)(1)\\

(33)

General formula

\begin{split}\zeta_{jkpq}&=\sum_{r_{1}=1}^{j+k}\sum_{r_{2}=r_{1}+1}^{j+k}\dots\sum_{r_{p+q}=r_{p+q-1}+1}^{j+k}s_{r_{1}}s_{r_{2}}\dots s_{r_{p+q}}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{p+q}(-1)^{m}\frac{g(j,p+q-m)j!}{(p+q-m)!(j-p-q+m)!}\frac{g(k,m)k!}{m!(k-m)!}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{p+q}(-1)^{m}g(j,p+q-m)C(j,p+q-m)g(k,m)C(k,m)\end{split}

(34)

The
m
th term is zero whenever the difference between the first and second argument in the binomial coefficient is less than zero, implying that we can simplify this formula as

\zeta_{jkpq}=\sum_{m=0}^{p+q}(-1)^{m}\gamma(j,p+q-m)\gamma(k,m)

(35)

where

\gamma(a,b)=g(a,b)C(a,b)=\begin{cases}C(a,b),&\text{if $a\geq b$}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(36)

We can also write the result in terms of nonzero contributions only, though the summation limit is not as pretty:

\zeta_{jkpq}=\sum_{m=\max(0,p+q-j)}^{\min(k,p+q)}(-1)^{m}C(j,p+q-m)C(k,m)

(37)

where
p+q-j
gives the lowest
m
for which
\gamma(j,p+q-m)=C(j,p+q-m)
(the second argument decreases as
m
increases) and
k
gives the highest
m
for which
\gamma(k,m)=C(k,m)
(the second argument increases with
m
).

Which Reducible Representations are Anosov?
Max Lahn

Abstract
. We give a characterization of the Anosov condition for reducible representations in terms of the eigenvalue magnitudes of the irreducible block factors of its block diagonalization. As in previous work, these Anosov representations comprise a collection of bounded convex domains in a finite-dimensional vector space, and this perspective allows us to conclude for many non-elementary hyperbolic groups that connected components of the character variety which consist entirely of Anosov representations do not contain reducible representations.



1.
Introduction

Discrete subgroups of the projective special linear group
\displaystyle\mathrm{PSL}\left\lparen 2,\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
are called
Fuchsian
when
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
and
Kleinian
when
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
. The rich theory of such groups is central in the geometry, topology, and dynamics of Riemann surfaces and hyperbolic
\displaystyle 3
-manifolds, respectively, and has connections to complex dynamics, number theory, and mathematical physics. Work of Labourie
[
Lab06
]
, Guichard–Wienhard
[
GW12
]
, Bridgeman–Canary–Labourie–Sambarino
[
Bri+15
]
, Guéritaud–Guichard–Kassel–Wienhard
[
Gué+17
]
, and Kapovich–Leeb–Porti
[
KLP17
,
KLP18
]
has established the theory of Anosov representations as a suitable analogue of convex cocompact Fuchsian and Kleinian groups in the setting of representations of
\displaystyle\Gamma
in higher rank Lie groups.

Further parallels to the convex cocompact Fuchsian and Kleinian cases are possible when the images of the Anosov representations under consideration are dense in the Zariski topology. However, Anosov representations in higher rank Lie groups need not be Zariski-dense, and the deformation theory of Anosov representions often must contend with the existence of such representations. For example, when
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\geq 3
, one may exploit an irreducible representation
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
to obtain representations in
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
which are not Zariski dense from convex cocompact Fuchsian and Kleinian groups, and the deformations of such representations are of great interest. In the case
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
, these comprise the Hitchin component of the character variety, and in the case
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, they contain the quasi-Fuchsian space.

In previous work
[
Lah24
]
, we explored variations on this phenomenon by considering certain deformations of
reducibly
embedded Fuchsian/Kleinian subgroups. Such deformations were parameterized by a finite-dimensional real vector space, and those corresponding to Anosov representations comprised a convex and bounded open subset. This article serves to generalize those results from reducible suspensions of
\displaystyle q
-Fuchsian representations to all reducible representations and to make initial conclusions about the character variety.

Concretely, fix a non-elementary word hyperbolic group
\displaystyle\Gamma
and a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over a field
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
, either
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
or
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
. Let
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
be a direct sum decomposition of
\displaystyle V
; that is,
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
is a finite sequence of pairwise trivially intersecting subspaces
\displaystyle U_{j}\subseteq V
which jointly span
\displaystyle V
. As described in
Section 2
, the partial sums of the factors of this decomposition comprise a partial flag in
\displaystyle V
. Representations of
\displaystyle\Gamma
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
which preserve each factor subspace
\displaystyle U_{j}
are called
block diagonal
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
, and those which preserve the associated partial flag are called
block upper triangular
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
.

We first adapt Kassel–Potrie’s
[
KP22
]
characterization of the Anosov condition in terms of the linear growth of certain functions of the Jordan projection to the setting of block upper triangular representations. Anosov representations in
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
come in several flavors
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
, one for each non-empty subset
\displaystyle\theta
of
\displaystyle\Delta_{V}=\left\{1,\dotsc,\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-1\right\}
. We show that to each block upper triangular
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov representation is associated a family of integers, called the
large eigenvalue
\displaystyle\theta
-configuration
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
, which catalogues the distribution of generalized eigenvectors of large eigenvalue among the preserved partial flag’s subspaces. For ease of exposition, we emphasize here the simpler result in the block diagonal setting; the general result for block upper triangular representations is
3.4
.

Theorem A
.

Fix a representation
\displaystyle\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
and a subset
\displaystyle\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
. If
\displaystyle\eta
is block diagonal relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
, with block decomposition

\eta=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\eta_{j}},

then
\displaystyle\eta
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov if and only if there is a family
\displaystyle Q=\left\lparen q_{j,k}\right\rparen_{j,k}
of integers
\displaystyle 0\leq q_{j,k}\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
, indexed by
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
, with the following properties:

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}{q_{j,k}}=k
for all
\displaystyle k\in\theta
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{q_{i,k}}\left\lparen\eta_{i}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{q_{j,k}+1}\left\lparen\eta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen
grows at least linearly in translation length
\displaystyle\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
for all
\displaystyle k\in\theta
and
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
so that
\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0
and
\displaystyle q_{j,k}<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
.

In this case, such a family
\displaystyle Q
is uniquely defined, and the following also hold:

(1)

The
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov limit map
\displaystyle\xi_{\eta}^{\theta}\colon\partial\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{F}_{\theta}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
has the property that

q_{j,k}=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\xi_{\eta}^{\theta}\left\lparen z\right\rparen\right\rparen

for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
,
\displaystyle k\in\theta
, and
\displaystyle z\in\partial\Gamma
.

(2)

For each
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
, let
\displaystyle\theta_{j}=\left\{q_{j,k}\right\}_{k\in\theta}\cap\Delta_{U_{j}}
. If
\displaystyle\theta_{j}\neq\emptyset
, then
\displaystyle\eta_{j}
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta_{j}}
-Anosov, with
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov limit map
\displaystyle\xi_{\eta_{j}}^{\theta_{j}}\colon\partial\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{F}_{\theta_{j}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
given by the formula

\xi_{\eta_{j}}^{\theta_{j}}\left\lparen z\right\rparen=\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\xi_{\eta}^{k_{q}}\left\lparen z\right\rparen\right\rparen_{q\in\theta_{j}},

where for each
\displaystyle q\in\theta_{j}
, we choose any
\displaystyle k_{q}\in\Delta_{V}
so that
\displaystyle q_{j,k_{q}}=q
.

(3)

The block diagonal representation
\displaystyle\eta
has
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov limit map
\displaystyle\xi_{\eta}^{\theta}\colon\partial\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{F}_{\theta}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
given by the formula

\xi_{\eta}^{\theta}\left\lparen z\right\rparen=\left\lparen\bigoplus_{q_{i,k}>0}{\xi_{\eta_{i}}^{q_{i,k}}\left\lparen z\right\rparen}\right\rparen_{k\in\theta}.

Block upper triangular representations are equivalent in the character variety to block diagonal representations. The block diagonal representations relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
may be parameterized by homomorphisms
\displaystyle\delta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}D_{\mathscr{U}}
and representations
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
, where
\displaystyle D_{\mathscr{U}}
is the finite-dimensional real vector space

D_{\mathscr{U}}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen x_{j}}=0,\textrm{ and }x_{j}=0\textrm{ for all }1\leq j\leq m\textrm{ so that }U_{j}=\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}\right\},

and
\displaystyle N_{\mathscr{U}}
is the matrix group

N_{\mathscr{U}}=\left\{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{j}}\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen:\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen\right\rvert=1\textrm{ for all }1\leq j\leq m\right\}.

Concretely, given such homomorphisms
\displaystyle\delta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}D_{\mathscr{U}}
and a representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
, we obtain a block diagonal representation
\displaystyle\eta=\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
defined by the formula

\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{e^{\delta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen+\varphi_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}.

We will call homomorphisms
\displaystyle\Gamma\xrightarrow{}D_{\mathscr{U}}
and representations
\displaystyle\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}

block deformations
and
block normalized representations
, respectively. The Anosov conditions for
\displaystyle\eta=\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
can be seen to be independent of
\displaystyle\delta
, so that for a fixed subset
\displaystyle\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
and block normalized representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
, we construct

A^{\mathscr{U}}_{\theta}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen=\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,D_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen:\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\textrm{ is $\displaystyle P_{\theta}$-Anosov for all (equivalently, any) }\delta\in\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen\right\}.

That is,
\displaystyle A^{\mathscr{U}}_{\theta}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is the space of block deformations
\displaystyle\varphi
of the block normalized representation
\displaystyle\zeta
which result in a
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov block diagonal representation
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
. If the block normalized representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
is itself
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov, then we are able to concretely characterize
\displaystyle A^{\mathscr{U}}_{\theta}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
in terms of
\displaystyle\varphi
and
\displaystyle\zeta
, in analogy to
[
Lah24
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem B
.

Fix a block normalized representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
and a subset
\displaystyle\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
. If
\displaystyle\zeta
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov, with large eigenvalue
\displaystyle\theta
-configuration
\displaystyle Q=\left\lparen q_{j,k}\right\rparen_{j,k}
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
, then

A^{\mathscr{U}}_{\theta}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen=\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,D_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen:\sup_{i,j,k,\gamma}{\frac{\varphi_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen-\varphi_{i}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}{\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{q_{i,k}}\left\lparen\zeta_{i}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{q_{j,k}+1}\left\lparen\zeta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen}}<1\right\}.

where the above supremum is indexed by all
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
so that
\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0
and
\displaystyle q_{j,k}<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
, and infinite order
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
.

One can see that if a block normalized representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov, then
Theorem B
expresses
\displaystyle A^{\mathscr{U}}_{\theta}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
as an increasing union of intersections of half-spaces, so that
\displaystyle A^{\mathscr{U}}_{\theta}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is convex. Moreover, since the above supremum is a homogeneous function of
\displaystyle\varphi
,
\displaystyle A^{\mathscr{U}}_{\theta}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is also bounded. In fact, these properties hold regardless of whether or not
\displaystyle\zeta
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov, in analogy to
[
Lah24
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem C
.

\displaystyle A^{\mathscr{U}}_{\theta}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is a convex and bounded open subset
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,D_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen
for any direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
of
\displaystyle V
, non-empty subset
\displaystyle\emptyset\subsetneq\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
, and block normalized representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
.

One consequence of
Theorem C
is a group-theoretic condition on the hyperbolic groups whose character varieties have Anosov components containing reducible representations.

Corollary D
.

Choose
\displaystyle G
and
\displaystyle H
from among
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
,
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
, and
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
. If the commutator subgroup
\displaystyle\left[{\Gamma},{\Gamma}\right]
has infinite index in
\displaystyle\Gamma
, then any connected component of the character variety
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen\mathbin{//}H
consisting entirely of (
\displaystyle H
-equivalence classes of) Anosov representations contains no (
\displaystyle H
-equivalence classes of) reducible representations.

We note that if a block normalized representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
is discrete and faithful (resp. has finite kernel), then all deformations of the form
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
for some homomorphisms
\displaystyle\delta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}D_{\mathscr{U}}
are also discrete and faithful (resp. have finite kernel). Thus
Theorem C
and
D
do not imply that all connected components of the character variety consisting of equivalence classes of discrete and faithful representations contain no equivalence classes of reducible representations.

2.
Background, Notation, and Small Lemmas

2.1.
Representations and Character Varieties

Fix forever a non-elementary word hyperbolic group
\displaystyle\Gamma
and a field
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
, either
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
or
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
. A
representation
of
\displaystyle\Gamma
in a group
\displaystyle G\leq\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of linear automorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
is a homomorphism
\displaystyle\Gamma\xrightarrow{}G
. The set
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
of such representations is topologized by the compact open topology, which is also the topology inherited as a subset of
\displaystyle G^{\Sigma}
for any finite generating set
\displaystyle\Sigma
for
\displaystyle\Gamma
. Endowed with this topology,
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
is called the
representation variety
of
\displaystyle\Gamma
in
\displaystyle G
. Such a representation variety
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
comes equipped with a left action of the normalizer

N_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen G\right\rparen=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen:\mathbf{A}G\mathbf{A}^{-1}=G\right\}=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen:\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\in G\textrm{ for all }\mathbf{B}\in G\right\}

by post-composition with conjugation; for a linear automorphism
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\in N_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen G\right\rparen
and a representation
\displaystyle\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}G
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\cdot\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}G
is the representation of
\displaystyle\Gamma
in
\displaystyle G
defined by the formula
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\cdot\rho\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\mathbf{A}\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\mathbf{A}^{-1}
. Although this action is by homeomorphisms, the associated quotient of
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
typically has some topological pathologies. For example, since orbits are generally not closed, this quotient is generally not Hausdorff. However, a suitably nice quotient may be found by considering the following coarser equivalence relation.

Definition

(Equivalence of representations; character variety)
.

Fix a group
\displaystyle G\leq\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of linear automorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
and a subgroup
\displaystyle H\leq N_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen G\right\rparen
. Two representations
\displaystyle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}G
are called
\displaystyle H
-equivalent
if the closures of their orbits under the the restriction of the above action to an action of
\displaystyle H
on
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
intersect, in which case we write
\displaystyle\rho_{1}\sim_{H}\rho_{2}
.

\displaystyle H
-equivalence
\displaystyle\sim_{H}
is an equivalence relation on the representation variety
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
, whose quotient is called a
character variety
and is denoted
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen=\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen/{\sim_{H}}=\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen\mathbin{//}H
. The associated quotient map is denoted
\displaystyle\left[\>\cdot\>\right]_{H}\colon\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
.

We note that for groups
\displaystyle K\leq G\leq\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of linear automorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
and a subgroup
\displaystyle H\leq N_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen G\right\rparen\cap N_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen K\right\rparen
, there is a unique injective and continuous map
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,K\right\rparen\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
so that the following diagram commutes:

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,K\right\rparen}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,K\right\rparen}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\>\cdot\>\right]_{H}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\>\cdot\>\right]_{H}}

We will use these maps to pretend that
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,K\right\rparen\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
as subsets of
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{H}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\right\rparen
.

For each finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
, denote by

\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen:\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\rvert=1\right\}

the group of linear automorphisms of unit magnitude determinant.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix a normal group
\displaystyle G\mathrel{\unlhd}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of linear automorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
. Then
\displaystyle\sim_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}=\sim_{\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}
and
\displaystyle\sim_{\mathrm{GL}^{+}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}=\sim_{\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}
on
\displaystyle\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
, so that
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen=\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{GL}^{+}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen=\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen\Gamma,G\right\rparen
.

Proof.

Note that
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen=\mathrm{GL}^{+}\left\lparen V\right\rparen=\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen=\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
if
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen=0
, so we may assume that
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen>0
. Consider the homomorphism
\displaystyle N_{V}\colon\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
defined by the formula

N_{V}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen=\frac{\mathbf{A}}{\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}}}.

Note that
\displaystyle N_{V}
is a continuous right inverse to the inclusion
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
, and that the restriction of
\displaystyle N_{V}
to
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}^{+}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
is a continuous right inverse to the inclusion
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}^{+}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
. Moreover,

\left\lparen N_{V}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\cdot\rho\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\left\lparen\frac{\mathbf{A}}{\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen^{\frac{1}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}}\right\rvert}\right\rparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\left\lparen\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rparen=\mathbf{A}\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\mathbf{A}^{-1}=\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\cdot\rho\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen

for all linear automorphisms
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
, representations
\displaystyle\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}G
, and
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
, so that
\displaystyle N_{V}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\cdot\rho=\mathbf{A}\cdot\rho
. We will use this fact in the following argument that two representations
\displaystyle\rho,\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}G
are
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
-equivalent if and only if they are
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
-equivalent.

Since
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\subseteq\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
, if
\displaystyle\rho\sim_{\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\eta
, then there are sequences
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
of linear automorphisms
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{n},\mathbf{B}_{n}\in\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
so that

\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\mathbf{A}_{n}\cdot\rho}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\mathbf{B}_{n}\cdot\eta}.

Observing that
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{n},\mathbf{B}_{n}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
for all
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
, we conclude that
\displaystyle\rho\sim_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\eta
.

On the other hand, if
\displaystyle\rho\sim_{\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\eta
, then there are sequences
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
of linear automorphisms
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{n},\mathbf{B}_{n}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
so that the above equation of limits holds. But of course

\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{N_{V}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{n}\right\rparen\cdot\rho}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\mathbf{A}_{n}\cdot\rho}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\mathbf{B}_{n}\cdot\eta}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{N_{V}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{n}\right\rparen\cdot\eta}.

Observing that
\displaystyle N_{V}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{n}\right\rparen,N_{V}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{n}\right\rparen\in\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
for all
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
, we conclude that
\displaystyle\rho\sim_{\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\eta
.

We have shown that
\displaystyle\sim_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}=\sim_{\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}
. Since we may replace
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
in the above argument with
\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}^{+}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
, respectively, we also conclude that
\displaystyle\sim_{\mathrm{GL}^{+}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}=\sim_{\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}
. ∎

Two equivalent representations share many of the same dynamical properties. For example, we will soon see that one is Anosov if and only if the other is. Towards that goal, to each linear transformation
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\colon V\xrightarrow{}V
of a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
are associated
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen

eigenvalue magnitudes

\lambda_{1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\geq\lambda_{2}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\geq\dotsb\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen,

which are the magnitudes of the complex roots of the characteristic polynomial of
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
, counted with algebraic multiplicity and listed in non-decreasing order. If
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, then each of these roots has a generalized eigenvector in
\displaystyle V
. However, if
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
, then any non-real complex eigenvalues will only have generalized eigenvectors in the complexification
\displaystyle V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
of
\displaystyle V
.

One can check that the eigenvalue magnitudes define continuous and conjugation-invariant functions
\displaystyle\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\colon\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\left[{0},{\infty}\right\rparen
on the space
\displaystyle\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of linear transformations of
\displaystyle V
. From this, one can derive the following fact:

Fact 2.2
.

Fix a group
\displaystyle G\leq\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of linear automorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
and a subgroup
\displaystyle H
of the normalizer
\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen G\right\rparen
, and consider two representations
\displaystyle\rho,\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}G
of
\displaystyle\Gamma
in
\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\rho\sim_{H}\eta
, then
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\circ\rho=\lambda_{k}\circ\eta
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
.

2.2.
Eigenvalues and Anosov Representations

The framework of Anosov representations provides a higher rank generalization of convex cocompact subgroups of rank
\displaystyle 1
Lie groups which retains many of the most desirable dynamical properties. The Anosov condition for representations in
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{R}\right\rparen
was first introduced by Labourie
[
Lab06
, Section 3.1.2]
, and has since been successfully generalized by Guichard–Wienhard
[
GW12
, Definition 2.10]
to representations in semisimple and reductive Lie groups.

Given two non-negative real-valued functions
\displaystyle f,g\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\left[{0},{\infty}\right\rparen
on
\displaystyle\Gamma
, we will say that
\displaystyle f\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen

grows at least linearly
in
\displaystyle g\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
if there are constants
\displaystyle a>0
and
\displaystyle b\geq 0
so that
\displaystyle f\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\geq ag\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen-b
for all
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
. This terminology is central to the fields of coarse geometry and geometric group theory, and plays a key role in multiple equivalent characterizations of Anosov representations. Specifically, an important characterization of the Anosov condition equivalent to Labourie’s
[
Lab06
, Section 3.1.2]
but phrased in the above language of at least linear growth of eigenvalue magnitudes is due to Kassel–Potrie
[
KP22
, Corollary 4.6]
. In order to present this characterization, we will require the following additional terminology.

We recall that
\displaystyle\Gamma
is assumed to be finitely generated. To each finite generating set
\displaystyle\Sigma
for
\displaystyle\Gamma
is associated a
translation length
function
\displaystyle\left\lVert\>\cdot\>\right\rVert_{\Sigma}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{N}
defined by the formula

\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}=\min\left\lparen\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\delta\gamma\delta^{-1}=\sigma_{1}\dotsm\sigma_{n}\textrm{ for some }\delta\in\Gamma\textrm{ and }\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{n}\in\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{-1}\right\}\right\rparen.

Defined as such,
\displaystyle\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
is the translation length of the action of
\displaystyle\gamma
by left multiplication on
\displaystyle\Gamma
, which is assumed to be equipped with the word metric associated to
\displaystyle\Sigma
.

For each finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over a field
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
, either
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
or
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
, we will denote

\Delta_{V}=\left\{1,2,\dotsc,\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-1\right\}.

This set
\displaystyle\Delta_{V}
has an
opposition involution

\displaystyle\iota_{V}\colon\Delta_{V}\xrightarrow{}\Delta_{V}
defined by the formula
\displaystyle\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-k
.

Definition

(Anosov representation,
[
KP22
, Corollary 4.6]
)
.

A representation
\displaystyle\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of
\displaystyle\Gamma
is called
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov
for some subset
\displaystyle\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
if for each
\displaystyle k\in\theta
, the
\displaystyle k
th
logarithmic eigenvalue gap

\displaystyle\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\rho
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}

\displaystyle k\in\theta

\displaystyle a_{k}>0
and

\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen\geq a_{k}\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}-b_{k}

for all
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma

A representation
\displaystyle\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen

\displaystyle P_{\theta}

. However, we remark that the
\displaystyle P_{\emptyset}
-Anosov condition is vacuous; that is, every representation
\displaystyle\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
is
\displaystyle P_{\emptyset}
-Anosov according to the above definition.

Given a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
, we observe that

\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rparen=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-k+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen}

for all
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
. Moreover, given a finite generating set
\displaystyle\Sigma
for
\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle\left\lVert\gamma^{-1}\right\rVert_{\Sigma}=\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
for all
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma

.

Fix a representation

.

\displaystyle\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of
\displaystyle\Gamma
and a subset
\displaystyle\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
. If
\displaystyle\rho
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov, then there are continuous and
\displaystyle\rho
-equivariant maps
\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\theta}\colon\partial\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{F}_{\theta}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\iota_{V}\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen}\colon\partial\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{F}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
defined on the Gromov boundary
\displaystyle\partial\Gamma
with the following properties:

(1)

Transversality: For all
\displaystyle x,y\in\partial\Gamma
, if
\displaystyle x\neq y
, then
\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\theta}\left\lparen x\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\iota_{V}\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen}\left\lparen y\right\rparen
are transverse; that is, if
\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\theta}\left\lparen x\right\rparen=\left\lparen F_{k}\right\rparen_{k\in\theta}
and
\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\iota\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen}=\left\lparen G_{\ell}\right\rparen_{\ell\in\iota_{V}\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen}
, then
\displaystyle V=F_{k}\oplus G_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}
for all
\displaystyle k\in\theta
.

(2)

\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-dynamics preservation:
\displaystyle\rho
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-proximal, and for all infinite order
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\theta}\left\lparen\gamma^{+}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen^{+}_{\theta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\theta}\left\lparen\gamma^{-}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen^{-}_{\theta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\iota_{V}\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen}\left\lparen\gamma^{+}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen^{+}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi_{\rho}^{\iota_{V}\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen}\left\lparen\gamma^{-}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen\theta\right\rparen},

where
\displaystyle\gamma^{\pm}\in\partial\Gamma
are the attracting and repelling ideal fixed points of
\displaystyle\gamma
in
\displaystyle\partial\Gamma
.

These maps are the unique continuous and
\displaystyle\rho
-equivariant maps with the above properties, and are called the
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov limit maps
of
\displaystyle\rho
.

2.4.
Direct Sum Decompositions and Block Diagonal Representations

A finite sequence
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
of subspaces
\displaystyle U_{j}\subseteq V
, called
factors
, of a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
is called a
direct sum decomposition
of
\displaystyle V
if
\displaystyle V=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle U_{i}\cap U_{j}=\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}
for all
\displaystyle 0\leq i<j\leq m
. In this case, we write
\displaystyle V=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{U_{j}}
and note that
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen=\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen}
. Each direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
of
\displaystyle V
has an associated flag in
\displaystyle V
of signature

\theta=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen}:1\leq j\leq m\right\}\cap\Delta_{V}.

The subspaces of this associated flag are defined by the partial sums
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{j_{k}}{U_{i}}
, where for each
\displaystyle k\in\theta
,
\displaystyle 1\leq j_{k}\leq m
is any integer so that
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{j_{k}}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen}=k
. We note that every non-trivial flag in
\displaystyle V
is the associated flag of several (in fact, uncountably infinitely many) direct sum decompositions of
\displaystyle V
.

A linear transformation
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\colon V\xrightarrow{}V
is said to
preserve
a direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
of
\displaystyle V
if it preserves each factor; that is, if
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\subseteq U_{j}
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
. From this condition we obtain the subsets

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen:\mathbf{A}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\subseteq U_{j}\textrm{ for all }1\leq j\leq m\right\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen:\mathbf{A}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen=U_{j}\textrm{ for all }1\leq j\leq m\right\}=\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen\cap\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen.

Note that any linear transformation which preserves a direct sum decomposition also preserves its associated flag; that is, block diagonal linear transformations are block upper triangular. However, the converse to this statement is false as long as said flag is non-trivial.

Fact 2.8
.

A linear transformation
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\colon V\xrightarrow{}V
of a finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
is block diagonal relative to a direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
of
\displaystyle V
if and only if there is a sequence
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
of linear transformations
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{j}\colon U_{j}\xrightarrow{}U_{j}
so that

\mathbf{A}\left\lparen\mathbf{v}\right\rparen=\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{A}_{j}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{v}\right\rparen\right\rparen}

for all
\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\in V
, where
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\colon V\twoheadrightarrow U_{j}
is projection onto the
\displaystyle j
th factor
\displaystyle U_{j}
with kernel spanned by the other factors. Such a sequence
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
is uniquely defined by the above property and is called the
block decomposition
of
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
.
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{j}
is called the
\displaystyle j
th
block
of
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
, and we write
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{A}_{j}}
, so that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{A}_{j}}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen:\mathbf{A}_{j}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ for all }1\leq j\leq m\right\}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen},

and similarly

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{A}_{j}}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen:\mathbf{A}_{j}\in\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ for all }1\leq j\leq m\right\}\cong\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen}.

Analogously, a representation
\displaystyle\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
is said to
preserve
a direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
of
\displaystyle V
if
\displaystyle\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
preserves
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
for each
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
; that is,
\displaystyle\rho
preserves
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
if
\displaystyle\rho\left\lparen\Gamma\right\rparen\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
. In this case, the map
\displaystyle\rho_{j}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
which on input
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
returns the
\displaystyle j
th block of
\displaystyle\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
is a representation of
\displaystyle\Gamma
in
\displaystyle U_{j}
called the
\displaystyle j
th
block
of
\displaystyle\rho
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
.
\displaystyle\rho
is called
semisimple
or
completely reducible
if it preserves a direct sum decomposition so that each block is irreducible. In this case,
\displaystyle\rho
is said to be
completely reduced
relative to said direct sum decomposition.

We now describe a standard form for representations in
\displaystyle V
which preserve a given direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
of
\displaystyle V
. This standard form derives from a direct product decomposition of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Fix a direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
of a finite-dimensional non-zero vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
, and consider the subspace

D_{\mathscr{U}}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen x_{j}}=0,\textrm{ and }x_{j}=0\textrm{ for all }1\leq j\leq m\textrm{ so that }U_{j}=\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}\right\}

and the group

N_{\mathscr{U}}=\left\{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{j}}\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen:\mathbf{B}_{j}\in\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ for all }1\leq j\leq m\right\}\cong\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen}.

There is a bijection
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\colon\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen\times\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,D_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen\times\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,N_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen\right\rparen
which induces a bijection
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\>\cdot\>,\>\cdot\>\right\rparen\colon\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,D_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen\times\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,N_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen\cap\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\right\rparen
.

Proof.

One useful map
\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}\colon\mathbb{R}\times D_{\mathscr{U}}\times N_{\mathscr{U}}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
is given by the formula

\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen s,\mathbf{x},\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{j}}\right\rparen=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{e^{s+x_{j}}\mathbf{B}_{j}}.

One can check that this defines a group isomorphism, with inverse given by the formula

\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}^{-1}\left\lparen\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{A}_{j}}\right\rparen=\left\lparen\frac{\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\rvert\right\rparen}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen},\left\lparen\frac{\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j.}\right\rparen\right\rvert\right\rparen}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen}-\frac{\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\rvert\right\rparen}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m},\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\frac{\mathbf{A}_{j}}{\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}\right\rparen\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen}}}}\right\rparen.

We note that if the
\displaystyle j
th factor
\displaystyle U_{j}
is
\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}
for some
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
, then parts of the above formula are undefined. Specifically, the
\displaystyle j
th component of the resulting vector in
\displaystyle D_{\mathscr{U}}
and the
\displaystyle j
th block of the linear transformation in
\displaystyle N_{\mathscr{U}}
are not defined. In this case, the definition of
\displaystyle D_{\mathscr{U}}
and the unique choice of linear transformation
\displaystyle U_{j}\xrightarrow{}U_{j}
should make clear what should replace these formulas, respectively. We will allow this abuse of notation for ease of legibility.

Given homomorphisms
\displaystyle\delta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}D_{\mathscr{U}}
and a representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
, we define a map
\displaystyle\eta=\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
by the formula
\displaystyle\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen,\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen,\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen
. Note that since
\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}
,
\displaystyle\delta
,
\displaystyle\varphi
, and
\displaystyle\zeta
are homomorphisms,
\displaystyle\eta\in\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen\right\rparen
. That
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\colon\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen\times\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,D_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen\times\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,N_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen\right\rparen
is a bijection is a standard consequence of the properties of direct products of groups.

We also note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen s,\mathbf{x},\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{j}}\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rvert

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{e^{s+x_{j}}\mathbf{B}_{j}}\right\rparen\right\rvert=\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen e^{s+x_{j}}\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}{e^{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\left\lparen s+x_{j}\right\rparen}\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen\right\rvert}=\mathrm{exp}\left\lparen\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\left\lparen s+x_{j}\right\rparen}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=e^{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen s+0}=e^{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen s}

for all
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in D_{\mathscr{U}}
, and
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{j}}\in N_{\mathscr{U}}
, so that
\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen s,\mathbf{x},\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{j}}\right\rparen\in\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
if and only if
\displaystyle s=0
. Thus
\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}\colon\mathbb{R}\times D_{\mathscr{U}}\times N_{\mathscr{U}}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
induces an isomorphism
\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\>\cdot\>,\>\cdot\>\right\rparen\colon D_{\mathscr{U}}\times N_{\mathscr{U}}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen\cap\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
, and so
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\>\cdot\>,\>\cdot\>\right\rparen\colon\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,D_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen\times\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,N_{\mathscr{U}}\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen\cap\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen V\right\rparen\right\rparen
is a bijection. ∎

For a direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
of a non-zero finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
, given homomorphisms
\displaystyle\delta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}D_{\mathscr{U}}
and a representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
, we will call the representation
\displaystyle\eta=\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
defined above in
2.9
the associated
block diagonal representation
. In this setting, we will refer to
\displaystyle\varphi
and
\displaystyle\zeta
as the
block deformation
and
block normalization
, respectively, of
\displaystyle\eta
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
; representations with image contained in
\displaystyle N_{\mathscr{U}}
will be called
block normalized
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
.

For a concrete formula for
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
, let
\displaystyle\varphi_{j}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle\zeta_{j}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}^{*}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
denote the
\displaystyle j
th component of the block deformation
\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}D_{\mathscr{U}}
and the
\displaystyle j
th block of the block normalization
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
, respectively, so that
\displaystyle\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\left\lparen\varphi_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
and
\displaystyle\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\zeta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}
for all
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then

\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{e^{\delta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen+\varphi_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}

for all
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
.

We remark that while the above formula for
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
is a generalization of that of the semisimplifications of the reducible suspensions introduced in
[
Lah24
, Section 1]
, which themselves generalize the linear
\displaystyle\mathbf{u}
-deformations described by Barbot
[
Bar10
, Section 4.1.2]
, every linear representation of
\displaystyle\Gamma
is a block diagonal representation (choose for example
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
to be the trivial direct sum decomposition). In fact, every such linear representation is equivalent to a semisimple block diagonal representation.

Corollary 2.10
.

Every representation
\displaystyle\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
of
\displaystyle\Gamma
is
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
-equivalent to a block diagonal representation
\displaystyle\eta=\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
. Moreover,

(1)

We can choose the direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
so that the block diagonalization
\displaystyle\eta
and its block normalization
\displaystyle\zeta
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
are completely reduced relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
; and

(2)

For any subset
\displaystyle\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
, one of
\displaystyle\rho
,
\displaystyle\eta
, and
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov if and only if all are.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
be a direct sum decomposition of
\displaystyle V
with associated flag
\displaystyle F
, and consider the linear transformation
\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}\colon V\xrightarrow{}V
and the map
\displaystyle B_{\mathscr{U}}\colon\mathrm{stab}_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen F\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
defined by the formulas

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{e^{j-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor-1}\mathrm{id}_{U_{j}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle B_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{n}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{-n}}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\circ\mathbf{A}\circ\iota_{j}},

where for each
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
,
\displaystyle\iota_{j}\colon U_{j}\xhookrightarrow{}V
and
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\colon V\twoheadrightarrow U_{j}
are inclusion of and projection onto the
\displaystyle j
th factor
\displaystyle U_{j}
. One can check that the above limit always converges to the stated formula, and that said formula defines a homomorphism of
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
-algebras. In particular,
\displaystyle B_{\mathscr{U}}
restricts to a group homomorphism
\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen F\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
.

We observe that if
\displaystyle\rho
preserves
\displaystyle F
, then the composition
\displaystyle\eta\coloneqq B_{\mathscr{U}}\circ\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
is
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
-equivalent to
\displaystyle\rho
, since
\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}\in\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
. Moreover, since
\displaystyle\eta
preserves
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
,
\displaystyle\eta=\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
for some homomorphisms
\displaystyle\delta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}D_{\mathscr{U}}
and some block normalized representation
\displaystyle\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}N_{\mathscr{U}}
.

(1)

If we choose
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
so that
\displaystyle F
is maximal among flags in
\displaystyle V
preserved by
\displaystyle\rho
, then
\displaystyle\eta
and
\displaystyle\zeta
are completely reduced relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
. This is because any non-trivial flag in a factor of
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
preserved by
\displaystyle\eta
or
\displaystyle\zeta
can be used to extend
\displaystyle F
to a flag of strictly larger signature preserved by
\displaystyle\rho
.

(2)

Since
\displaystyle\rho
and
\displaystyle\eta
are
\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
-equivalent, one is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov if and only if the other is. Since

\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen\delta,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{e^{\delta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen+\varphi_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}=e^{\delta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{e^{0+\varphi_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}=e^{\delta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen

for all
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
,
\displaystyle\eta
and
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen
have the same logarithmic eigenvalue gaps. Specifically,

\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen=\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{e^{\delta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{e^{\delta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen=\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\varphi,\zeta\right\rparen\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen

for all
\displaystyle k\in\theta
and
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
, and therefore
\displaystyle\eta
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-Anosov if and only if
\displaystyle\beta_{\mathscr{U}}\left\lparen 0,\varphi,\delta\right\rparen
is. ∎

The homomorphism
\displaystyle B_{\mathscr{U}}\colon\mathrm{stab}_{\mathrm{GL}\left\lparen V\right\rparen}\left\lparen F\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathscr{U}\right\rparen
obtained above in the proof of
2.10
is called
block diagonalization
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
. If a representation or linear transformation obtained via this method is completely reduced relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
, then such a block diagonalization is called the
semisimplification
relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
. The dynamical properties of such semisimplifications were investigated by Guichard–Gueritaud–Kassel–Wienhard
[
Gué+17
, Section 2.5.4, Proposition 1.8]
, who proved before the introduction of Kassel–Potrie’s
[
KP22
, Corollary 4.6]
characterization of Anosov representations that a representation is Anosov if and only if its semisimplification is.

3.
The Anosov Condition for Reducible Representations

2.10
implies that in order to understand which reducible representations of
\displaystyle\Gamma
are Anosov, it suffices to develop such an understanding for block diagonal representations in terms of conditions on the blocks. Toward this end, fix for this section a direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}=\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen_{j=1}^{m}
of a non-zero finite-dimensional vector space
\displaystyle V
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}
with projection maps
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\colon V\twoheadrightarrow U_{j}
and partial sums

U_{j}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{j}{U_{i}},

for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
. We recall that all but the last of these partial sums are precisely the subspaces comprising the flag associated to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
.

3.1.
Eigenvalues of Block Upper Triangular Linear Transformations

Towards our goal of adapting the eigenvalue characterization of Anosov representations introduced by Kassel–Potrie
[
KP22
]
to reducible representations, we will first consider the attracting and repelling subspaces and eigenvalue magnitudes of block upper triangular linear transformations and their block diagonalizations.

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix a linear transformation
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\colon V\xrightarrow{}V
which is block upper triangular relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
, and consider its block diagonalization
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{j}}
, as described in
2.10
. For each
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
, denote by
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\colon U_{j}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}U_{j}^{\prime}
the restriction of
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
to
\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}=\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen^{\pm}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen}
and
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen=U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}=\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{\pm}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen}
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
.

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}}=\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}
for all
\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
.

(3)

\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k},U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-k}\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle U_{j}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k},U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-k}\right\rparen
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
.

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}
have eigenvalue magnitudes

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen=\min_{i}{\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{i}\left\lparen U_{i}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{i}\right\rparen}=\max_{j}{\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen-\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen+1}\right\rparen+1\right\rparen}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen}

for all
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
, where the above minimum and maximum are index by all
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
so that
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{i}\left\lparen U_{i}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen>0
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen+1}\right\rparen\right\rparen>0
.

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{j}
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta_{j}}
-biproximal for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
, where
\displaystyle\theta_{j}=\left\{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen:k\in\Delta_{V}\right\}\cap\Delta_{U_{j}}
.

Proof.

We will prove these results under the assumption that
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
. If
\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
, then the same results may be proved by applying the following arguments to the linear transformation
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}}
induced by
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
on the complexification
\displaystyle V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
of
\displaystyle V
.

We begin by recalling from the proof of
2.10
that
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{n}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{-n}}
, where

\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{e^{j-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor-1}\mathrm{id}_{U_{j}}}.

This implies that
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen=\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
. Inspecting the above formula for
\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}
, we also note that

\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{e^{-\left\lparen j-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor-1\right\rparen n}\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{n}\left\lparen\mathbf{v}\right\rparen}=\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{v}\right\rparen

for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\in U_{j}^{\prime}
.

Proceeding inductively, we fix pairwise disjoint sets
\displaystyle E_{1},\dotsc,E_{m}\subsetneq V
of generalized eigenvectors of
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
so that for each
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
,
\displaystyle E_{j}^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{j}{E_{i}}
is a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{j}{U_{i}}
. This inductive construction ensures that
\displaystyle\left\lvert E_{j}\right\rvert=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
, and we will label these generalized eigenvectors by

E_{j}=\left\{\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\in U_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\right\}.

We note that
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\colon U_{j}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow U_{j}
is surjective, and
\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{j-1}{E_{i}}\subsetneq\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\right\rparen
, so that
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen
spans
\displaystyle U_{j}
. We conclude by the rank-nullity theorem that
\displaystyle\pi_{j}
restricts to an isomorphism
\displaystyle\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}U_{j}
, so that
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen
is a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}
.

Denote by
\displaystyle\mu_{j,\ell}\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
and
\displaystyle 1\leq r_{j,\ell}\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
the eigenvalue and rank of
\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}
, considered as a generalized eigenvector of
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
, so that
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{A}-\mu_{j,\ell}\mathrm{id}_{V}\right\rparen^{r_{j,\ell}}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen=\mathbf{0}
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle 1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
. In particular, we note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{n}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{-n}-\mu_{j,\ell}\mathrm{id}_{V}\right\rparen^{r_{j,\ell}}\left\lparen e^{-\left\lparen j-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor-1\right\rparen n}\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{n}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=e^{-\left\lparen j-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor-1\right\rparen n}\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{n}\left\lparen\left\lparen\mathbf{A}-\mu_{j,\ell}\mathrm{id}_{V}\right\rparen^{r_{j,\ell}}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=e^{-\left\lparen j-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor-1\right\rparen n}\mathbf{C}_{\mathscr{U}}^{n}\left\lparen\mathbf{0}\right\rparen=\mathbf{0}

for all
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
. We observe in the limit that
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{B}-\mu_{j,\ell}\mathrm{id}_{V}\right\rparen^{r_{j,\ell}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathbf{0}
; that is,
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen
is a generalized eigenvector of
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}
of eigenvalue
\displaystyle\mu_{j,\ell}
, although not necessarily of rank
\displaystyle r_{j,\ell}
. In fact, since

\mathbf{B}^{n}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\left\lparen\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{i}^{n}}\right\rparen\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbf{B}_{i}^{n}\left\lparen\pi_{i}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen}=\mathbf{B}_{j}^{n}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen

for all
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen
is also a generalized eigenvector of
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{j}
of eigenvalue
\displaystyle\mu_{j,\ell}
. In summary, we have shown that
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen
is a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}
consisting of generalized eigenvectors of both
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{j}
. We can further conclude that
\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen}
is a basis for
\displaystyle V=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{U_{j}}
consisting of generalized eigenvectors of
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}
.

(1)

The desired results clearly hold if
\displaystyle k=0
, so we may assume that
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
. Note that
\displaystyle E_{m}^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}{E_{i}}
is a basis for
\displaystyle V
which contains both
\displaystyle E_{j}^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle E_{m}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}
, themselves bases for
\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}
, respectively. Thus
\displaystyle E_{j}^{\prime}\cap E_{m}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}=E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}
is a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}
. We now note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\in U_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq i\leq j,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\in U_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq i\leq j,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{\left\lvert E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rvert}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen^{+}_{\left\lvert E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rvert}=E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen^{+}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen}

is also a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen^{+}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen}
, and similarly

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\in U_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq i\leq j,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-k+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\in U_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq i\leq j,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen-\left\lvert E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rvert+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen^{-}_{\left\lvert E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rvert}=E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen^{-}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen}

is also a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen^{-}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen}
. We conclude that
\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}=\left\lparen\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen^{\pm}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen}
.

Entirely analogously,
\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen}
is a basis for
\displaystyle V
which contains both
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle\left\lparen\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}
, themselves bases for
\displaystyle U_{j}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}
, respectively. Thus
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\left\lparen\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}=\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}
is a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}
. We now note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{\left\lvert\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rvert}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{+}_{\left\lvert\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rvert}=\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{+}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen}

is also a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}
, and similarly

\displaystyle\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-k+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen-\left\lvert\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rvert+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{-}_{\left\lvert\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}\right\rvert}=\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{-}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen}

is also a basis for
\displaystyle U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}
. We conclude that
\displaystyle U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}=\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{\pm}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen}
.

It remains to show
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen=U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}
. To this end, we see that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq i\leq j,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen=U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k},

and similarly

\displaystyle\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq i\leq j,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-k+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-k+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen=U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}.

(2)

We previously argued that for each
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
, the projection
\displaystyle\pi_{j}\colon V\twoheadrightarrow U_{j}
onto the
\displaystyle j
th factor
\displaystyle U_{j}
restricts to a linear isomorphism
\displaystyle\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}U_{j}
. The further restriction to
\displaystyle\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen
is injective and therefore an isomorphism onto its image

\displaystyle\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen.

The computations above in (1) now imply that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}{E_{j}}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}{E_{j}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}}\right\rparen=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen=\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}.

(3)

The desired results clearly holds if
\displaystyle k=0
or
\displaystyle k=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
, so we may assume that
\displaystyle k\in\Delta_{V}
. For each
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq j
and
\displaystyle 1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen
,
\displaystyle\mu_{i,\ell}
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
, and so either
\displaystyle\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen
or
\displaystyle\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen
by the definition of eigenvalue magnitudes. Thus

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\in U_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq i\leq j\textrm{ and }1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\in U_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq i\leq j,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad\cup\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i,\ell}\in U_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq i\leq j,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\cup\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle U_{j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\right\rparen=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\cup\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen,\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen E_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k},U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen.

Entirely analogously, for each
\displaystyle 1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
,
\displaystyle\mu_{j,\ell}
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}
, and so either
\displaystyle\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad\cup\left\{\pi_{j}\left\lparen\mathbf{e}_{j,\ell}\right\rparen\in U_{j}:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\cup\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle U_{j}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\cup\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen,\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k},U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen.

(4)

We argued above that
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen=\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen
. The above computations in (1) imply that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\min\left\lparen\left\{\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert:1\leq i\leq m,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\min_{i}{\min\left\lparen\left\{\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\min_{i}{\min\left\lparen\left\{\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{i,\ell}\right\rvert\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{i}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\min_{i}{\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{i}\right\rparen}=\min_{i}{\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{i}\left\lparen U_{i}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{i}\right\rparen},

where the above minimum is indexed by all
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq m
so that

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{i}\left\lparen U_{i}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen=\left\lvert\pi_{i}\left\lparen E_{i}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rvert>0.

Similarly

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\max\left\lparen\left\{\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert:1\leq j\leq m,1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen,\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\max_{j}{\max\left\lparen\left\{\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\max_{j}{\max\left\lparen\left\{\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert:1\leq\ell\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen\textrm{ and }\left\lvert\mu_{j,\ell}\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen-\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen+1}\right\rparen+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen\right\}\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\max_{j}{\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen-\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen+1}\right\rparen+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen}=\max_{j}{\lambda_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen-\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen+1}\right\rparen+1}\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen},

where the above maximum is indexed by all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
so that

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen+1}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen+1}\right\rparen=\left\lvert\pi_{j}\left\lparen E_{j}\right\rparen\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen+1}\right\rvert>0.

(5)

Recall that
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{\pm}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}\right\rparen}=U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{\pm}_{k}
, as argued above in (1). If

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen\in\Delta_{U_{j}},

then

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{+}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen}\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{+}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen}\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen,

and so
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{j}
is
\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen}
-proximal by
2.5
.

Similarly, if

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen\in\Delta_{U_{j}},

then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{-}_{\iota_{U_{j}}\left\lparen\iota_{U_{j}}\left\lparen\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{-}_{\iota_{U_{j}}\left\lparen\iota_{U_{j}}\left\lparen\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\left\lparen\mathbf{B}_{j}\right\rparen^{-}_{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen}\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen=\iota_{U_{j}}\left\lparen\iota_{U_{j}}\left\lparen\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen,

and so
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{j}
is
\displaystyle P_{\iota_{U_{j}}\left\lparen\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen}
-proximal by
2.5
. We conclude that
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{j}
is
\displaystyle P_{\theta_{j}}
-biproximal by
2.6
. ∎

The statements and proof of
3.1
above hint at the importance of the numbers

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen

for a linear transformation
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\colon V\xrightarrow{}V
which is block upper triangular relative to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
and its block diagonalization
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}
. If such a linear transformation
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
is in the image of a reducible Anosov representation preserving the flag associated to
\displaystyle\mathscr{U}
, then these numbers play a vital role in computing the logarithmic eigenvalue gaps and Anosov limit maps. We formalize this role in the following definitions and in
3.4
.

Definition

(Admissible family; structured flag)
.

A family
\displaystyle Q=\left\lparen q_{j,k}\right\rparen_{j,k}
of integers
\displaystyle 0\leq q_{j,k}\leq\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
indexed by
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
is called
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta\right\rparen
-
admissible
for a subset
\displaystyle\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
if

\sum_{j=1}^{m}{q_{j,k}}=k

for all
\displaystyle k\in\theta
.

Given such a
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta\right\rparen
-admissible family
\displaystyle Q=\left\lparen q_{j,k}\right\rparen_{j,k}
, a flag
\displaystyle F=\left\lparen F_{k}\right\rparen_{k\in\theta}
in
\displaystyle V
of signature
\displaystyle\theta
is called
weakly
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta,Q\right\rparen
-structured
if
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap F_{k}\right\rparen\right\rparen=q_{j,k}
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
.
\displaystyle F
is called
strongly
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta,Q\right\rparen
-structured
if
\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\cap F_{k}\right\rparen=q_{j,k}
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
. If
\displaystyle F
is strongly
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta,Q\right\rparen
-structured, then

F_{k}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{U_{j}\cap F_{k}}

for all
\displaystyle k\in\theta
, so that
\displaystyle F
is also weakly
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta,Q\right\rparen
-structured. We denote by
\displaystyle W^{\mathscr{U},\theta}_{Q}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
and
\displaystyle S^{\mathscr{U},\theta}_{Q}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
the closed subsets of the flag variety
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\theta}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
consisting of weakly and strongly
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta,Q\right\rparen
-structured flags, respectively.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix a subset
\displaystyle\theta\subseteq\Delta_{V}
and a
\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta\right\rparen
-admissible family
\displaystyle Q=\left\lparen q_{j,k}\right\rparen_{j,k}
. For each
\displaystyle k\in\theta
, there are
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
so that
\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0
and
\displaystyle q_{j,k}<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
.

Proof.

Recall that
\displaystyle\Delta_{V}=\left\{1,\dotsc,\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen-1\right\}
, so that
\displaystyle 0<k<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen
. If
\displaystyle q_{i,k}=0
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq m
, then

k=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{q_{i,k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{0}=0.

On the other hand, if
\displaystyle q_{j,k}=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
, then

k=\sum_{j=1}^{m}{q_{j,k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen}=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{U_{j}}\right\rparen=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen V\right\rparen.

Thus there are
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
so that
\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0
and
\displaystyle q_{j,k}<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
. ∎

Equipped with this new language of admissible families and structured flags, we derive the following corollary of

\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-proximal if and only if

for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
, in which case the following also hold:

(1)

\displaystyle Q=\left\lparen q_{j,k}\right\rparen_{j,k}
indexed by
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta

\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathscr{U},\theta\right\rparen
-admissible family called the
large eigenvalue
\displaystyle\theta
-configuration
of
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}

(2)

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0

\displaystyle q_{j,k}<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen

Proof.

Suppose first that
\displaystyle U_{j}=\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\oplus\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
. One can check from the computations in the above proof of
3.1

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{U_{j}\cap\left\lparen\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\cap\left\lparen U_{j}\cap\mathbf{B}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\cap\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}{\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}}=\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle P_{\theta}
-proximal. Since

\displaystyle P_{\theta}

Conversely, now suppose that

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle P_{\theta}

\displaystyle k\in\theta

for all
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq m

\displaystyle U_{j}=\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{+}_{k}\right\rparen\oplus\pi_{j}\left\lparen U_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{A}^{-}_{\iota_{V}\left\lparen k\right\rparen}\right\rparen

(1)

\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m

\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0
and
\displaystyle q_{j,k}<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen

3.2.

Theorem 3.4

\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
, where the above minimum is indexed by all
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m

\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0

for all
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta

Conversely, now suppose that there is a
\displaystyle\theta
-admissible family
\displaystyle Q=\left\lparen q_{j,k}\right\rparen_{j,k}

\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{q_{i,k}}\left\lparen\eta_{i}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{q_{j,k}+1}\left\lparen\eta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen

grows at least linearly in translation length
\displaystyle\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
so that
\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0
and
\displaystyle q_{j,k}<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
. For a given infinite order
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma
, note that for all
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
so that
\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0
and

4.2.
Another Characterizations of the Anosov Condition

For the remainder of this section, we aim to use the tools developed above to extend the characterization of the Anosov condition for reducible representations presented in
3.4
to some class of reducible representations. Specifically, our condition will hold for block upper triangular representations for which the block normalization of the block diagonalization is itself Anosov.

If the block normalization of the block diagonalization of a block upper triangular representation is Anosov, then
Theorem B
gives a complete characterization of when said block upper triangular representation is Anosov. However, this characterization relies on the computation of countably infinitely many numbers, as can be seen from the index set of the above supremum.

On the other hand, many of the linear inequalities enforced by such conditions are redundant, as

\frac{\varphi_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen-\varphi_{i}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}{\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{q_{i,k}}\left\lparen\zeta_{i}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{q_{j,k}+1}\left\lparen\zeta_{j}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen}

is a homogeneous class function of
\displaystyle\gamma
for fixed
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq m
and
\displaystyle k\in\theta
so that
\displaystyle q_{i,k}>0
and
\displaystyle q_{j,k}<\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\left\lparen U_{j}\right\rparen
. Thus the supremum in
Theorem B
can instead be indexed with primitive elements in the torsion-free part of the abelianization of
\displaystyle\Gamma

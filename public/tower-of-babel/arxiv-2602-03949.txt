Semantic Rate Distortion and Posterior Design: Compute Constraints, Multimodality, and Strategic Inference
Emrah Akyol

Abstract
We study strategic Gaussian semantic compression under rate and compute constraints, where an encoder and decoder optimize distinct quadratic objectives. A latent Gaussian state generates a task-dependent semantic variable, and the decoder best responds via MMSE estimation, reducing the encoder’s problem to posterior covariance design under an information-rate constraint. We characterize the strategic rate-distortion function in direct, remote, and full-information regimes, derive semantic waterfilling and rate-constrained Gaussian persuasion solutions, and establish Gaussian optimality under misaligned objectives. We further show that architectural compute limits act as implicit rate constraints, yielding exponential improvements in semantic accuracy with model depth and inference-time compute, while multimodal observation eliminates the geometric-mean penalty inherent to remote encoding. These results provide information-theoretic foundations for data- and energy-efficient AI and offer a principled interpretation of modern multimodal language models as posterior-design mechanisms under resource constraints.



I
Introduction

The current era of artificial intelligence (AI) is defined by scaling laws:
increasing the amount of data, model size, and computation has been a reliable
route to improved performance and emergent capabilities. This trajectory,
however, confronts hard constraints. Data availability is finite, training and
inference compute are expensive, and energy consumption has become a central
bottleneck in deploying large-scale AI systems responsibly. These realities
call for principled statistical and information-theoretic tools that
characterize fundamental trade-offs among energy, data, computation, and
performance. This paper contributes such a toolset by developing a
rate–distortion (RD) and information design theory for semantic inference under
resource constraints.

A key obstacle to data- and energy-efficient learning is that modern AI
systems rarely aim to reconstruct raw observations. Instead, they are trained
and evaluated on objectives such as classification accuracy, decision quality,
or semantic correctness. In these settings, the relevant information is not
the observation itself but a latent task-dependent quantity. In addition, AI
systems often operate in multi-agent environments with misaligned goals:
users, platforms, and automated decision-making modules may value different
aspects of the same information. These facts challenge classical information
theory in two ways. First, standard RD theory assumes a common
distortion measure shared by encoder and decoder, whereas semantic and
task-oriented systems naturally induce distinct objectives. Second, classical
Bayesian persuasion and information design characterize how a sender can shape
a receiver’s posterior beliefs, but typically neglect explicit
communication-rate and compute constraints.

This work unifies these perspectives by proposing a model of

strategic Gaussian semantic compression
. A latent Gaussian state
X

governs the task semantics, while the encoder cares about a semantic variable

\Theta=BX+V
, where
B
represents a semantic transformation and
V

captures semantic noise, preference uncertainty, or task variation. The
decoder aims to infer
X
from a rate-limited message
M
, while the encoder
evaluates performance through a quadratic semantic loss in
\Theta
. The
interaction is modeled as a Stackelberg game: the encoder commits to an
encoding policy under a rate constraint, and the decoder best responds by
forming a Bayesian MMSE estimate
\hat{X}=\mathbb{E}[X\mid M]
under its own
objective. This model naturally captures three regimes of practical interest:
(i)
direct
encoding where the encoder observes
X
;
(ii)
remote
encoding where the encoder observes only
\Theta
(or an
imperfect semantic proxy); and
(iii)
full-information
encoding where the encoder observes
(X,\Theta)

jointly and can exploit semantic noise to shape the decoder’s beliefs. The
third regime connects directly to Gaussian persuasion and information design.

Our main technical contribution is to show that these strategic semantic
compression problems admit a clear posterior-geometry characterization.
Because the decoder best responds via MMSE estimation, the encoder’s semantic
distortion depends on the encoding mechanism primarily through the posterior
covariance of
X
given the message. For Gaussian sources, the information
constraint becomes a log-det bound on posterior uncertainty, revealing a
fundamental entropy budget:

\log\det K_{X}\geq\log\det\Sigma_{X}-2R,

(1)

where
K_{X}
is the posterior error covariance and
R
is the available rate.

Beyond these RD and persuasion results, the framework offers a
principled lens on contemporary AI architectures and the focus on energy
efficiency. In transformer-based large language models (LLMs), there is no
explicit bit pipe, yet information flow is constrained by finite attention
bandwidth, limited embedding dimensions, bounded context windows, and
restricted inference-time compute. These architectural bottlenecks impose
implicit rate constraints on internal belief formation and posterior
refinement, analogous to the explicit rate constraints studied here. The
resulting entropy geometry yields analytic explanations for scaling phenomena:
increasing depth, width, or compute increases the effective information budget
and sharpens posterior beliefs; chain-of-thought reasoning acts as sequential
rate allocation that refines posteriors over multiple steps. Similarly, the
theory quantifies the advantage of multimodal encoders: multiple modalities
increase the recoverable semantic covariance and eliminate the geometric-mean
information collapse inherent to remote encoding. This provides a rigorous
explanation for why multimodal systems (such as vision language models)
achieve superior data efficiency without proportional increases in compute.

The paper makes the following contributions:
(i) it introduces a strategic Gaussian semantic compression framework linking
RD theory and information design under misaligned objectives;
(ii) it derives exact strategic rate distortion characterizations in direct
and remote observation regimes, including Gaussian optimality and semantic
waterfilling solutions;
(iii) it formulates and solves rate-constrained Gaussian persuasion in the
full-information regime, including closed-form diagonal solutions;
(iv) it quantifies performance gaps between observation regimes and shows how
multimodality closes these gaps; and
(v) it connects these information-theoretic limits to energy-constrained AI
architectures by interpreting architectural bottlenecks as implicit rate
constraints.

Related Work

This paper lies at the intersection of RD theory, strategic

II-A

Rate–Distortion Theory

Rate–distortion (RD) theory provides the fundamental limits of lossy
compression under a prescribed fidelity criterion
[
1
]
.
For Gaussian sources with quadratic distortion, the RD function admits a
closed-form solution via reverse waterfilling
[

. Vector
Gaussian RD and indirect (remote) source coding problems have also been
studied extensively
[

These formulations assume a common distortion measure shared by encoder and
decoder and do not address misaligned objectives.

Our work departs from classical RD by allowing distinct quadratic objectives
for encoder and decoder, with the encoder’s distortion defined on a semantic
variable
\Theta=BX+V
. We show that this leads to posterior-covariance
design problems with log-det entropy constraints, yielding semantic
waterfilling laws that generalize classical RD.

II-D

Multimodal Learning and Efficient AI

Multimodal learning combines heterogeneous observations to improve robustness
and data efficiency
[
34
]
. Empirical results show
that multimodal models outperform unimodal ones, particularly in grounding
and reasoning tasks. Separately, empirical scaling laws relating model size,
compute, and performance have been documented for large neural networks

[

.

By interpreting architectural bottlenecks and compute limits as implicit
information-rate constraints, our framework provides an information-theoretic
explanation for these phenomena. In particular, we show that multimodal
observation eliminates a geometric-mean semantic penalty inherent to remote
encoding and that increased compute corresponds to exponential reductions in
posterior uncertainty.

II-E

Summary

In contrast to prior work, this paper unifies rate-distortion theory,
strategic communication, Bayesian persuasion, semantic compression, and
neural scaling laws within a single posterior-design framework. The central
object is the posterior covariance of latent semantic variables, and the
central constraint is the entropy budget imposed by rate or compute.

Unlike classical Gaussian RD which assumes aligned
objectives and focuses on reconstruction fidelity under some common distortion measure, this work studies semantic
and strategic misalignment and characterizes optimal posterior geometry under
rate constraints. Unlike LQG signaling and Bayesian persuasion models, which
typically allow unrestricted signaling, we impose explicit information-rate
constraints and derive the resulting posterior-design structure. Finally,
unlike recent semantic communication and multimodal learning approaches, which
are primarily empirical, our results provide a first-principles,
information-theoretic explanation for multimodal advantages and compute-driven
scaling laws. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to unify
RD theory, Gaussian persuasion, semantic objectives, and
compute-limited learning architectures within a single analytical framework.

III
Problem Formulation

We consider a strategic communication setting in which an encoder and a
decoder possess distinct semantic objectives. A latent Gaussian state
X

governs the semantic content of interest, while the encoder evaluates
reconstruction through a derived semantic variable
\Theta=BX+V
. The
decoder, however, seeks to estimate
X
itself. The encoder communicates with
the decoder through a rate-limited noiseless channel and anticipates the
decoder’s Bayesian best response.

Notation
: All random vectors are defined on a common probability space.
All logarithms are natural logarithms, in base
e
, the rates are measured in nats.
For a random vector
Y
, we use
\mathrm{Cov}(Y)
to denote its covariance matrix and

\Sigma_{Y}:=\mathrm{Cov}(Y)

when convenient.
For an auxiliary random variable
U
,
\Sigma_{Y\mid U}
denotes the (average) MMSE
error covariance:

\Sigma_{Y\mid U}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[(Y-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid U])(Y-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid U])^{\top}\right].

(2)

A\succeq 0
(resp.
A\succ 0
) means
A
is symmetric positive semidefinite
(resp. definite). For symmetric
A,B
,
A\preceq B
(Loewner order) means
B-A\succeq 0
. We let
\mathrm{tr}(\cdot)
and
\det(\cdot)
denote trace and determinant;
and
I(\cdot\,;\cdot)
and

denote mutual information and differential entropy respectively.

\mathrm{diag}(\cdot)
and
\mathrm{blkdiag}(\cdot)
denote diagonal and block-diagonal matrices.

I
denotes the identity matrix of appropriate dimension.
The expression
Y\perp Z
means the random variables
Y
and
Z
are statistically independent.

Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
satisfy

X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X}),

(3)

where
\Sigma_{X}\succ 0
.

The encoder evaluates reconstruction through the linear–Gaussian semantic transform

\Theta=BX+V,

(4)

where
B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
is known and

V\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{V}).

(5)

We assume

V\perp X.

(6)

The mapping
X\mapsto\Theta
reflects the encoder’s semantic or alignment
objectives (e.g., task relevance, supervision signals).

We analyze three observation regimes. The encoder observes

Z\in\{X,\ \Theta,\ (X,\Theta)\}.

(7)

•

Direct encoder:

Z=X
. The encoder observes the state perfectly.

•

Remote encoder:

Z=\Theta
. The encoder observes only a semantic proxy of
X
.

•

Full-information encoder:

Z=(X,\Theta)
. The encoder observes both state and semantics.

For blocklength
n
, an
(n,R)
source code consists of an encoder

\displaystyle f_{n}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{Z}^{n}\to\{1,\dots,\exp{nR}\},

(8)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=f_{n}(Z^{n}),

(9)

and a decoder

\displaystyle g_{n}

\displaystyle:\{1,\dots,\exp\{nr\}\}\to\mathbb{R}^{kn},

(10)

\displaystyle\hat{X}^{n}

\displaystyle=g_{n}(M).

(11)

The rate constraint is

R=\frac{1}{n}\log|{\cal M}_{n}|.

(12)

The decoder minimizes quadratic distortion

D_{d}(f_{n},g_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\!\left[(X_{t}-\hat{X}_{t})^{\top}W_{d}(X_{t}-\hat{X}_{t})\right],

(13)

with
W_{d}\succ 0
. Because
X
is Gaussian and the objective is strictly convex,
the decoder’s unique best response is the MMSE estimator

\hat{X}^{n}=\mathbb{E}[X^{n}\mid M].

(14)

The encoder’s distortion is

D_{e}(f_{n},g_{n}^{\star})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\!\left[(\Theta_{t}-\hat{X}_{t})^{\top}W_{e}(\Theta_{t}-\hat{X}_{t})\right],

(15)

with
W_{e}\succ 0
. Here, we deliberately measure semantic mismatch against the decoder’s state estimate
\hat{X}
,
since in downstream decision pipelines in learning systems, the decoder typically exposes
\hat{X}
(or actions based on it),
not a separate semantic reconstruction of
\Theta
.

A semantic distortion level
D_{e}
is achievable at rate
R
if there exists a sequence
of encoders
\{f_{n}\}
such that (
14
) holds and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}D_{e}(f_{n},g_{n}^{\star})\leq D_{e}.

(16)

The strategic rate–distortion function is

R(D_{e})=\inf\left\{R:\ D_{e}\text{ is achievable at rate }R\right\}.

(17)

IV
Main Results

We now characterize the strategic Gaussian rate–distortion function under the
three encoder observation models introduced in Section
III
. In the
direct and remote regimes, the problem reduces to convex optimization over
posterior covariances with a log-det rate constraint, yielding explicit
semantic waterfilling solutions. In the full-information regime, the encoder
can also manipulate posterior cross-covariances with semantic noise, leading to
a rate-constrained Gaussian persuasion problem with closed-form solutions in
the diagonalizable case.

IV-A

Direct Encoder: Observation of
X

We start with the setting where the encoder observes only
X
, hence any admissible message induces a posterior error
covariance
K_{X}
satisfying
0\preceq K_{X}\preceq\Sigma_{X}
.

Using the orthogonal decomposition
X=\hat{X}+e
with
e\perp\hat{X}
, the semantic
distortion can be represented as a form that we will use repeatedly for this setting. We start with lemmas that present such auxilliary results.

Lemma 1
.

The encoder’s distortion satisfies

D_{e}=\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})+\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{W}K_{X}).

(18)

where
K_{X}
denotes the posterior error covariance

K_{X}=\Sigma_{X\mid M},

(19)

the semantic offset covariance is given as:

C_{0}=(B-I)\Sigma_{X}(B-I)^{\top}+\Sigma_{V},

(20)

and the effective weight matrix is

\tilde{W}=W_{e}(B-I)+(B-I)^{\top}W_{e}+W_{e}.

(21)

Proof.

Recall that
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X})
and
\Theta=BX+V
with

V\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{V})
independent of
X
. The decoder uses

\hat{X}=\mathbb{E}[X\mid M]
and we define the posterior error
e:=X-\hat{X}
.
By the MMSE orthogonality principle,
e\perp\hat{X}
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(e)

\displaystyle=K_{X},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{X}-K_{X}.

By definition,

D_{e}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[(\Theta-\hat{X})^{\top}W_{e}(\Theta-\hat{X})\right]=\mathrm{tr}\!\left(W_{e}\,\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta-\hat{X})\right).

Using
\Theta=BX+V=B(\hat{X}+e)+V
, we obtain

\Theta-\hat{X}=(B-I)\hat{X}+Be+V.

Because
e\perp\hat{X}
and
V\perp(X,M)
, all cross-covariances among
(\hat{X},e,V)
vanish,
so

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta-\hat{X})

\displaystyle=(B-I)\,\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X})\,(B-I)^{\top}+B\,\mathrm{Cov}(e)\,B^{\top}+\Sigma_{V}

\displaystyle=(B-I)(\Sigma_{X}-K_{X})(B-I)^{\top}+BK_{X}B^{\top}+\Sigma_{V}.

Expanding and simplifying yields

\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta-\hat{X})=C_{0}+(BK_{X}+K_{X}B^{\top}-K_{X}).

Multiplying by
W_{e}
and taking trace gives

D_{e}=\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})+\mathrm{tr}\!\left((W_{e}B+B^{\top}W_{e}-W_{e})K_{X}\right).

Using
\tilde{W}=W_{e}(B-I)+(B-I)^{\top}W_{e}+W_{e}=W_{e}B+B^{\top}W_{e}-W_{e}
yields (
18
).
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X})
and let
U
be any random variable. Define

K_{X}:=\Sigma_{X\mid U}.

(22)

Then

I(X;U)\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{X}}{\det K_{X}},

(23)

with equality if and only if
X\mid U
is Gaussian with covariance
K_{X}
almost surely.

Proof.

We have
I(X;U)=h(X)-h(X\mid U)
. Since
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X})
,

h(X)=\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left((2\pi e)^{k}\det\Sigma_{X}\right).

For each
u
, let
\Sigma_{X\mid u}
be the covariance of
X\mid U=u
. Then

h(X\mid U=u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left((2\pi e)^{k}\det\Sigma_{X\mid u}\right),

with equality if and only if
X\mid U=u
is Gaussian. Taking expectation and applying concavity of

\log\det
yields

h(X\mid U)\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left((2\pi e)^{k}\det K_{X}\right),

which implies (
23
). Equality requires Gaussianity and constant conditional covariance.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X})
and let
0\prec K_{X}\preceq\Sigma_{X}
. Define

\Sigma_{Z}^{-1}:=K_{X}^{-1}-\Sigma_{X}^{-1}.

(24)

Let
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{Z})
be independent of
X
and define
U=X+Z
.
Then
\Sigma_{X\mid U}=K_{X}
and

I(X;U)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{X}}{\det K_{X}}.

(25)

Proof.

For jointly Gaussian
(X,U)
with
U=X+Z
, the posterior covariance is

\displaystyle\Sigma_{X\mid U}=(\Sigma_{X}^{-1}+\Sigma_{Z}^{-1})^{-1}=\left(\Sigma_{X}^{-1}+K_{X}^{-1}-\Sigma_{X}^{-1}\right)^{-1}=K_{X}.

Mutual information satisfies
I(X;U)=h(X)-h(X\mid U)
, which yields (
25
).
∎

Theorem 1
.

The strategic RD function when the encoder observes
X
is

R(D_{e})=\min_{0\preceq K_{X}\preceq\Sigma_{X}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{X}}{\det K_{X}}:\;\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{W}K_{X})\leq D_{e}^{\prime}\right\},

(26)

where
D_{e}^{\prime}:=D_{e}-\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})
. For every feasible
K_{X}
, Gaussian test
channels
U=X+Z
achieve rate
\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{X}}{\det K_{X}}
and induce

\Sigma_{X\mid U}=K_{X}
.

Proof.

Achievability:
Fix any
K_{X}
with
0\preceq K_{X}\preceq\Sigma_{X}
and

\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{W}K_{X})\leq D_{e}^{\prime}
. By Lemma
3
there exists a Gaussian
encoder
U=X+Z
that induces
\Sigma_{X\mid U}=K_{X}
and achieves

R=I(X;U)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{X}}{\det K_{X}}.

Block coding according to the test channel achieves distortion arbitrarily close to

\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})+\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{W}K_{X})
and rate arbitrarily close to
R
.
Taking the infimum over all feasible
K_{X}
yields (
26
).

Converse:
Any code induces a posterior covariance
K_{X}
satisfying

0\preceq K_{X}\preceq\Sigma_{X}
and distortion (
18
). Moreover, by Lemma
2
,

R\geq I(X;M)\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{X}}{\det K_{X}}.

Therefore, any achievable
(R,D_{e})
must satisfy the constraints in (
26
) for some such
K_{X}
.

∎

Remark 1
.

In unconstrained information design
the sender chooses an information structure that induces a distribution over posterior
means
\hat{X}=\mathbb{E}[X\mid M]
subject to Bayes plausibility
[
7
]
. For the Gaussian setting at hand, this implies second order moment conditions
(see e.g.,
[
9
,
10
]
),

0\preceq\Sigma_{\hat{X}\hat{X}}\preceq\Sigma_{X},

(27)

together with

K_{X}=\Sigma_{X}-\Sigma_{\hat{X}\hat{X}}.

(28)

Since the sender’s quadratic objective is affine in these covariance blocks, the
unconstrained problem is an SDP, as shown in
[
9
]
.

Imposing an explicit communication-rate constraint
I(X;M)\leq R
shrinks the Bayes-plausible
set by enforcing an entropy budget on the posterior error covariance:

\log\det K_{X}\geq\log\det\Sigma_{X}-2R,

(29)

which is the same constraint highlighted in (
1
).

Remark 2
.

The program (
26
) can be written in the normalized variable

D:=\Sigma_{X}^{-1/2}K_{X}\Sigma_{X}^{-1/2}
as

\min_{0\prec D\preceq I}\;\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{\det D}\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad\mathrm{tr}(A_{X}D)\leq D_{e}^{\prime},

(30)

where the effective normalized weight is

A_{X}:=\Sigma_{X}^{1/2}\tilde{W}\,\Sigma_{X}^{1/2}.

(31)

Thus the eigen-geometry relevant to waterfilling is governed by
A_{X}
, not by

\Sigma_{X}
and
\tilde{W}
separately. If
A_{X}
has nonpositive eigenvalues,
those directions are never compressed at the rate-minimizing solution, because
setting the corresponding normalized posterior eigenvalues to one both reduces
rate and does not tighten the semantic constraint.

Corollary 1
.

Let
A_{X}=U\Lambda U^{\top}
, where

\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}),

(32)

and define the index sets

\mathcal{I}_{+}:=\{i:\lambda_{i}>0\},

(33)

and

\mathcal{I}_{0}:=\{i:\lambda_{i}\leq 0\}.

(34)

Then there exists an optimizer of (
26
) of the form

K_{X}^{\star}=\Sigma_{X}^{1/2}U\,\mathrm{diag}(d_{1}^{\star},\dots,d_{k}^{\star})\,U^{\top}\Sigma_{X}^{1/2},

(35)

with
0<d_{i}^{\star}\leq 1
and

d_{i}^{\star}=1,

(36)

for
i\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
, while for
i\in\mathcal{I}_{+}

d_{i}^{\star}=\min\left\{1,\ \frac{\nu^{\star}}{\lambda_{i}}\right\}.

(37)

If
D_{e}^{\prime}\geq\mathrm{tr}(A_{X})
, then
K_{X}^{\star}=\Sigma_{X}
is feasible and

R(D_{e})=0.

(38)

If
D_{e}^{\prime}<\mathrm{tr}(A_{X})
, then the semantic constraint is active and
\nu^{\star}>0

is uniquely determined by

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{+}}\min\{\lambda_{i},\nu^{\star}\}=D_{e}^{\prime}-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\lambda_{i}.

(39)

Moreover, the solution is in the interior (no saturation on any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{+}
)
if and only if

\nu^{\star}<\min_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{+}}\lambda_{i}.

(40)

In that interior regime,

\nu^{\star}=\frac{D_{e}^{\prime}-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\lambda_{i}}{|\mathcal{I}_{+}|},

(41)

and the resulting rate is

R(D_{e})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{+}}\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\nu^{\star}}\right).

(42)

Remark 3
.

Even though
W_{e}\succ 0
, the induced matrix
\tilde{W}
need not be positive semidefinite.
If
A_{X}
has negative eigenvalues, then decreasing posterior uncertainty in those directions
can increase
\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{W}K_{X})
, so the strategic RD curve need not be monotone
in the available rate. A sufficient condition that rules out this pathology is

\tilde{W}\succeq 0,

(43)

equivalently
A_{X}\succeq 0
.

V\perp X
, and the decoder best responds with

\hat{X}=\mathbb{E}[X\mid M]
.

Let

\Sigma_{\Theta}=\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta)=B\Sigma_{X}B^{\top}+\Sigma_{V}.

(44)

Define the linear Bayes regressions

L_{X}=\Sigma_{X}B^{\top}\Sigma_{\Theta}^{-1}.

(45)

L_{V}=\Sigma_{V}\Sigma_{\Theta}^{-1}.

(46)

Let
K_{\Theta}
denote the posterior error covariance of
\Theta
:

K_{\Theta}=\Sigma_{\Theta\mid M}.

(47)

Define the infinite-rate remote baseline distortion

D_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rem}}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\big(\Theta-\mathbb{E}[X\mid\Theta]\big)^{\top}W_{e}\big(\Theta-\mathbb{E}[X\mid\Theta]\big)\right],

(48)

and define

Q=L_{X}^{\top}\tilde{W}L_{X}+L_{X}^{\top}W_{e}L_{V}+L_{V}^{\top}W_{e}L_{X}.

(49)

Theorem 2
.

The strategic remote rate distortion function is

R(D_{e})=\min_{0\preceq K_{\Theta}\preceq\Sigma_{\Theta}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{\Theta}}{\det K_{\Theta}}:\;\mathrm{tr}(QK_{\Theta})\leq D_{e}-D_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rem}}\right\}.

(50)

Gaussian test channels
U=\Theta+Z
(with
Z\perp\Theta
Gaussian) achieve the minimum
for every feasible
K_{\Theta}
.

Proof.

Since
M=f_{n}(\Theta^{n})
and
|{\cal M}_{n}|\leq\exp\{nr\}
,

nR\geq H(M)\geq I(\Theta^{n};M).

By standard single-letterization (time-sharing), there exists a single-letter pair
(\Theta,U)

with
U=(M,Q)
such that
\frac{1}{n}I(\Theta^{n};M)=I(\Theta;U)
and
K_{\Theta}=\Sigma_{\Theta\mid U}
.
Applying Lemma
2
to
(\Theta,U)
gives

R\geq I(\Theta;U)\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{\Theta}}{\det K_{\Theta}}.

Let
\hat{\Theta}:=\mathbb{E}[\Theta\mid M]
and
e_{\Theta}:=\Theta-\hat{\Theta}
, so

\mathrm{Cov}(e_{\Theta})=K_{\Theta}
. Because
(X,V,\Theta)
are jointly Gaussian, conditional
expectations are linear:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X\mid\Theta]

\displaystyle=L_{X}\Theta,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[V\mid\Theta]

\displaystyle=L_{V}\Theta.

Since
M
is a function of
\Theta
, the tower property yields

\hat{X}=\mathbb{E}[X\mid M]=L_{X}\hat{\Theta}.

Define innovations
N_{X}:=X-L_{X}\Theta
and
N_{V}:=V-L_{V}\Theta
, which are independent of
\Theta
.
Then the MMSE error is
e:=X-\hat{X}=N_{X}+L_{X}e_{\Theta}
, and one can show

D_{e}=D_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rem}}+\mathrm{tr}(QK_{\Theta}),

with
D_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rem}}
and
Q
defined in (
48
) and (
49
).

Converse:
The preceding steps show that any achievable
(R,D_{e})
induces

K_{\Theta}
with
0\preceq K_{\Theta}\preceq\Sigma_{\Theta}
satisfying the constraints in (
50
).

Achievability:
Fix any
K_{\Theta}
with
0\prec K_{\Theta}\preceq\Sigma_{\Theta}
. Define

\Sigma_{Z}^{-1}:=K_{\Theta}^{-1}-\Sigma_{\Theta}^{-1}
and choose
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{Z})

independent of
\Theta
. With
U:=\Theta+Z
, Gaussian identities yield
\Sigma_{\Theta\mid U}=K_{\Theta}

and
I(\Theta;U)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{\Theta}}{\det K_{\Theta}}
. Encoding according to this test
channel achieves distortion
D_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rem}}+\mathrm{tr}(QK_{\Theta})
. Optimizing over feasible

K_{\Theta}
proves the theorem.

∎

Corollary 2
.

Consider the excess distortion above the infinite-rate remote baseline:

\Delta_{\mathrm{rem}}(R)=D_{e}^{(\mathrm{rem})}(R)-D_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rem}}.

(51)

Assume
Q\succ 0
and that
Q
and
\Sigma_{\Theta}
are simultaneously diagonalizable, i.e., there exists an orthogonal
U
such that

U^{\top}\Sigma_{\Theta}U=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{\Theta,1}^{2},\dots,\sigma_{\Theta,k}^{2}),

(52)

and

U^{\top}Q\,U=\mathrm{diag}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k}),

(53)

with
q_{i}>0
. Then an optimal posterior covariance in Theorem
2
is diagonal in this basis,

K_{\Theta}^{\star}(R)=U\,\mathrm{diag}(k_{1}^{\star}(R),\dots,k_{k}^{\star}(R))\,U^{\top}
, with

k_{i}^{\star}(R)=\min\left\{\sigma_{\Theta,i}^{2},\ \frac{\nu^{\star}(R)}{q_{i}}\right\},

(54)

where
\nu^{\star}(R)>0
is chosen so that the rate constraint is active:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\frac{\sigma_{\Theta,i}^{2}}{k_{i}^{\star}(R)}=2R.

(55)

In the interior (no-saturation) regime,
k_{i}^{\star}(R)<\sigma_{\Theta,i}^{2}
for all
i
,
we have

K_{\Theta}^{\star}(R)=\nu^{\star}(R)\,Q^{-1},

(56)

with

\nu^{\star}(R)=\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{k}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\!\big(q_{i}\sigma_{\Theta,i}^{2}\big)-2R\right]\right),

(57)

and the excess distortion equals

\Delta_{\mathrm{rem}}(R)=\mathrm{tr}\!\left(QK_{\Theta}^{\star}(R)\right)=k\,\nu^{\star}(R).

(58)

Proof.

For fixed rate
R
, Theorem
2
is equivalent (up to the constant
D_{\infty}^{\mathrm{rem}}
) to

\min_{0\prec K_{\Theta}\preceq\Sigma_{\Theta}}\ \mathrm{tr}(QK_{\Theta})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{\Theta}}{\det K_{\Theta}}\leq R.

Equivalently, the constraint is
\log\det K_{\Theta}\geq\log\det\Sigma_{\Theta}-2R
.
Under simultaneous diagonalization, write
K_{\Theta}=U\,\mathrm{diag}(k_{i})\,U^{\top}
.
Then the problem reduces to

\min_{0<k_{i}\leq\sigma_{\Theta,i}^{2}}\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{i}k_{i}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log k_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\sigma_{\Theta,i}^{2}-2R.

The KKT conditions yield
k_{i}^{\star}=\min\{\sigma_{\Theta,i}^{2},\nu/q_{i}\}
with an active log constraint,
giving (
54
)–(
58
).

∎

Remark 4
.

Corollary
2
assumes
Q\succ 0
and simultaneous diagonalization with
\Sigma_{\Theta}

to express the solution in the scalar parameters
\{q_{i},\sigma_{\Theta,i}^{2}\}
.
Without this commutativity assumption, the same reverse-waterfilling structure holds
in the eigenbasis of the symmetric matrix

A_{\Theta}:=\Sigma_{\Theta}^{1/2}Q\,\Sigma_{\Theta}^{1/2}.

(59)

If
A_{\Theta}
has nonpositive eigenvalues, the corresponding normalized posterior

only across the positive-eigenvalue subspace.

IV-C

Full-Information Encoder: Rate-Constrained Gaussian Persuasion

When the encoder observes
(X,\Theta)
, it can also manipulate posterior correlations
between the state
X
and semantic noise
V
, introducing strategic degrees of freedom.
Recall
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X})
and
V\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{V})
are independent, and

\Theta=BX+V
. In the full-information regime the encoder observes
(X,\Theta)
and sends a
rate-limited message
M
. Define

W=\begin{bmatrix}X\\
V\end{bmatrix},

(60)

with prior covariance

\Sigma_{W}=\mathrm{blkdiag}(\Sigma_{X},\Sigma_{V}),

(61)

and posterior error covariance

K_{W}=\Sigma_{W\mid M}.

(62)

Theorem 3
.

The strategic rate–distortion function when the encoder observes
X,\Theta
is

R(D_{e})=\min_{0\preceq K_{W}\preceq\Sigma_{W}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{W}}{\det K_{W}}:\;\mathrm{tr}(\bar{W}K_{W})\leq D_{e}^{\prime}\right\},

(63)

where
D_{e}^{\prime}:=D_{e}-\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})
and

\bar{W}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{W}&W_{e}\\
W_{e}&0\end{bmatrix}.

(64)

and is achievable by a Gaussian test channel of the form

U=W+Z
where
Z
is Gaussian.

Proof.

Since
V=\Theta-BX
is a deterministic invertible linear transform of
(X,\Theta)
, the encoder
effectively observes
(X,V)
, and for any message
M
,

I(X,\Theta;M)=I(X,V;M)=I(W;M).

Because
|{\cal M}_{n}|\leq\exp\{nr\}
we have
H(M)\leq nR
and hence

I(W^{n};M)\leq H(M)\leq nR.

We single-letterize as in standard RD converses. Using the chain rule and
conditioning reduces entropy, for i.i.d.
W_{t}
,

I(W^{n};M)=\sum_{t=1}^{n}I(W_{t};M\mid W^{t-1})\geq\sum_{t=1}^{n}I(W_{t};M).

Let
Q\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\dots,n\}
be independent of everything and define

W:=W_{Q}
and
U:=(M,Q)
. Then

\displaystyle I(W;U)

\displaystyle=I(W_{Q};M,Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}I(W_{t};M)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}I(W^{n};M)

\displaystyle\leq R.

Apply Lemma
2
to the Gaussian vector
W
and auxiliary
U
:

I(W;U)\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{W}}{\det\Sigma_{W\mid U}}.

Let
K_{W}:=\Sigma_{W\mid U}
. Since
U=(M,Q)
, this is exactly the per-letter posterior
error covariance induced by the code after time-sharing. Combining yields

R\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{W}}{\det K_{W}},

equivalently,

\log\det K_{W}\geq\log\det\Sigma_{W}-2R.

Finally, by the law of total covariance,
0\preceq K_{W}\preceq\Sigma_{W}
.

Let
\hat{X}:=\mathbb{E}[X\mid M]
and define the MMSE error
e:=X-\hat{X}
.
Also define
\hat{V}:=\mathbb{E}[V\mid M]
and
v:=V-\hat{V}
. Then

K_{W}=\Sigma_{W\mid M}=\mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\begin{bmatrix}e\\
v\end{bmatrix}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}K_{X}&K_{XV}\\
K_{VX}&K_{V}\end{bmatrix},

where
K_{X}=\mathrm{Cov}(e)
and
K_{XV}=\mathrm{Cov}(e,v)
.

By the orthogonality principle,
e
is orthogonal to any measurable function of
M
, hence

e\perp\hat{V}
and therefore

\mathrm{Cov}(e,V)=\mathrm{Cov}(e,\hat{V}+v)=\mathrm{Cov}(e,v)=K_{XV}.

Since
\mathrm{Cov}(X,V)=0
by independence, we also have

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{Cov}(X,V)=\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X}+e,V)=\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X},V)+\mathrm{Cov}(e,V),

which yields

\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X},V)=-K_{XV}.

Next,

\Theta-\hat{X}=BX+V-\hat{X}=(B-I)\hat{X}+Be+V.

Using
e\perp\hat{X}
, the covariance is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta-\hat{X})

\displaystyle=(B-I)\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X})(B-I)^{\top}+BK_{X}B^{\top}+\Sigma_{V}

\displaystyle\quad+(B-I)\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X},V)+\mathrm{Cov}(V,\hat{X})(B-I)^{\top}

\displaystyle\quad+B\mathrm{Cov}(e,V)+\mathrm{Cov}(V,e)B^{\top}.

Using
\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X})=\Sigma_{X}-K_{X}
,
\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{X},V)=-K_{XV}
, and

\mathrm{Cov}(e,V)=K_{XV}
, the cross terms simplify to

-(B-I)K_{XV}-K_{VX}(B-I)^{\top}+BK_{XV}+K_{VX}B^{\top}=K_{XV}+K_{VX}.

The
K_{X}
-terms simplify as in Lemma
1
:

\displaystyle(B-I)(\Sigma_{X}-K_{X})(B-I)^{\top}+BK_{X}B^{\top}

\displaystyle=(B-I)\Sigma_{X}(B-I)^{\top}+\big(BK_{X}+K_{X}B^{\top}-K_{X}\big).

Hence

\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta-\hat{X})=C_{0}+\big(BK_{X}+K_{X}B^{\top}-K_{X}\big)+(K_{XV}+K_{VX}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle D_{e}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\!\left(W_{e}\,\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta-\hat{X})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})+\mathrm{tr}\!\left((W_{e}B+B^{\top}W_{e}-W_{e})K_{X}\right)+\mathrm{tr}\!\left(W_{e}(K_{XV}+K_{VX})\right).

Defining
\tilde{W}:=W_{e}B+B^{\top}W_{e}-W_{e}
and

\bar{W}:=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{W}&W_{e}\\
W_{e}&0\end{bmatrix},

we have:

D_{e}=\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})+\mathrm{tr}(\bar{W}K_{W}).

Converse:
The preceding analysis shows that any achievable
(R,D_{e})
induces a matrix
K_{W}

satisfying
0\preceq K_{W}\preceq\Sigma_{W}
and
\log\det K_{W}\geq\log\det\Sigma_{W}-2R
, and achieves

D_{e}=\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})+\mathrm{tr}(\bar{W}K_{W})
.

Achievability:
Let
Q\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\dots,n\}
independent of everything, define
W:=W_{Q}
and
U:=(M,Q)
.
Then

I(W;U)=I(W_{Q};M,Q)\leq I(W^{n};M)\leq H(M)\leq nR,

hence
I(W;U)\leq R
.
The decoder
reconstructs
U^{n}
and forms
\hat{X}^{n}=\mathbb{E}[X^{n}\mid U^{n}]
, which induces the desired posterior
error covariance
K_{W}
and achieves distortion
\mathrm{tr}(W_{e}C_{0})+\mathrm{tr}(\bar{W}K_{W})
.
Optimizing over feasible
K_{W}
proves the theorem.
∎

When
\Sigma_{X},\Sigma_{V},B
, and
W_{e}
share an eigenbasis, the covariance-design program from
Theorem
3
decouples across eigen-directions. Each direction reduces to a two-dimensional
posterior-design problem for the pair
(x_{i},v_{i})
, with a determinant budget allocated by a
scalar waterfilling law.

Assumption 1
.

There exists an orthogonal matrix
U
such that

\displaystyle U^{\top}\Sigma_{X}U

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1}^{2},\dots,\sigma_{k}^{2}),

(65)

\displaystyle U^{\top}\Sigma_{V}U

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}(\tau_{1}^{2},\dots,\tau_{k}^{2}),

(66)

\displaystyle U^{\top}BU

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}(b_{1},\dots,b_{k}),

(67)

\displaystyle U^{\top}W_{e}U

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}(w_{1},\dots,w_{k}),

(68)

with
\sigma_{i}>0
,
\tau_{i}>0
, and
w_{i}>0
for all
i
.

Remark 5
.

The condition
\tau_{i}>0
ensures
\log\det\Sigma_{V}
is well-defined and guarantees that
each
2\times 2
block in Theorem
4
has one positive and one negative eigenvalue.

Theorem 4
.

Under Assumption
1
, there exists an optimal posterior error covariance

K_{W}^{\star}(R)
that is block-diagonal across
i
in the basis

\bar{U}:=\mathrm{blkdiag}(U,U)
:

K_{W}^{\star}(R)=\bar{U}\ \mathrm{blkdiag}\!\big(K_{1}^{\star}(R),\dots,K_{k}^{\star}(R)\big)\ \bar{U}^{\top},

(69)

with
2\times 2
blocks

K_{i}^{\star}(R)=D_{i}\,\tilde{K}_{i}^{\star}(R)\,D_{i},

(70)

where

D_{i}=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{i},\tau_{i}).

(71)

Each normalized block can be written as

\tilde{K}_{i}^{\star}(R)=R_{i}\,\mathrm{diag}(\delta_{i}^{\star}(R),1)\,R_{i}^{\top},

(72)

where
0<\delta_{i}^{\star}(R)\leq 1
and
R_{i}
diagonalizes

A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{w}_{i}\,\sigma_{i}^{2}&w_{i}\,\sigma_{i}\tau_{i}\\
w_{i}\,\sigma_{i}\tau_{i}&0\end{bmatrix},

(73)

with

\tilde{w}_{i}=w_{i}(2b_{i}-1).

(74)

Writing
R_{i}^{\top}A_{i}R_{i}=\mathrm{diag}(a_{i}^{+},a_{i}^{-})
with
a_{i}^{+}>0>a_{i}^{-}
, we have

a_{i}^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{w}_{i}\sigma_{i}^{2}+\sqrt{\tilde{w}_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{4}+4w_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\tau_{i}^{2}}\right).

(75)

The scalars
\delta_{i}^{\star}(R)
satisfy the waterfilling law

\delta_{i}^{\star}(R)=\min\!\left\{1,\ \frac{\eta^{\star}(R)}{a_{i}^{+}}\right\},

(76)

where
\eta^{\star}(R)>0
is chosen so that the normalized log-det constraint is active:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\delta_{i}^{\star}(R)=-2R.

(77)

Proof.

Define
\bar{U}:=\mathrm{blkdiag}(U,U)
and transform

\displaystyle K^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bar{U}^{\top}K_{W}\bar{U},

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bar{U}^{\top}\Sigma_{W}\bar{U}.

Under Assumption
1
, we have

\Sigma^{\prime}=\mathrm{blkdiag}(\Sigma_{X}^{\prime},\Sigma_{V}^{\prime})=\mathrm{blkdiag}\!\left(\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{i}^{2}),\mathrm{diag}(\tau_{i}^{2})\right).

Because trace and determinant are invariant under orthogonal transforms, the covariance-design
problem in Theorem
3
is equivalent to

\min_{0\preceq K^{\prime}\preceq\Sigma^{\prime}}\ \mathrm{tr}(\bar{W}^{\prime}K^{\prime})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\log\det K^{\prime}\geq\log\det\Sigma^{\prime}-2R,

where
\bar{W}^{\prime}:=\bar{U}^{\top}\bar{W}\,\bar{U}
is block diagonal across
i
with
2\times 2
blocks

\bar{W}_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{w}_{i}&w_{i}\\
w_{i}&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\tilde{w}_{i}:=w_{i}(2b_{i}-1).

Normalize by
\Sigma^{\prime}
: define
\tilde{K}:=\Sigma^{\prime-1/2}K^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime-1/2}
. Then

0\preceq K^{\prime}\preceq\Sigma^{\prime}
becomes
0\preceq\tilde{K}\preceq I
, and

\displaystyle\log\det K^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\log\det\Sigma^{\prime}+\log\det\tilde{K},

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\bar{W}^{\prime}K^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\!\left(\Sigma^{\prime 1/2}\bar{W}^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime 1/2}\ \tilde{K}\right).

Let

A:=\Sigma^{\prime 1/2}\bar{W}^{\prime}\Sigma^{\prime 1/2},

which is block diagonal across
i
with blocks

A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{w}_{i}\,\sigma_{i}^{2}&w_{i}\,\sigma_{i}\tau_{i}\\
w_{i}\,\sigma_{i}\tau_{i}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Thus the normalized problem is

\min_{0\preceq\tilde{K}\preceq I}\ \mathrm{tr}(A\tilde{K})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\log\det\tilde{K}\geq-2R.

We next reduce it to
k
independent
2\times 2
blocks. Partition
\tilde{K}
into
k\times k

blocks of size
2\times 2
:

\tilde{K}=[\tilde{K}_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{k}.

Since
A
is block diagonal,

\mathrm{tr}(A\tilde{K})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{tr}(A_{i}\tilde{K}_{ii}),

so off-diagonal blocks do not affect the objective.

For feasibility, note that
\tilde{K}\succeq 0
implies the determinant inequality

\det(\tilde{K})\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\det(\tilde{K}_{ii}).

Define the block-diagonal matrix
\tilde{K}^{\mathrm{bd}}:=\mathrm{blkdiag}(\tilde{K}_{11},\dots,\tilde{K}_{kk})
.
Then
\tilde{K}^{\mathrm{bd}}\preceq I
and

\det(\tilde{K}^{\mathrm{bd}})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\det(\tilde{K}_{ii})\geq\det(\tilde{K}).

Hence
\tilde{K}^{\mathrm{bd}}
is feasible whenever
\tilde{K}
is feasible, and it achieves
the same objective value. Therefore, there exists an optimal solution that is block diagonal:

\tilde{K}^{\star}=\mathrm{blkdiag}(\tilde{K}_{1}^{\star},\dots,\tilde{K}_{k}^{\star}),\qquad 0\preceq\tilde{K}_{i}^{\star}\preceq I.

The constraint becomes

\log\det\tilde{K}^{\star}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\det(\tilde{K}_{i}^{\star})\geq-2R.

Fix
i
and take the eigen-decomposition

A_{i}=R_{i}\,\mathrm{diag}(a_{i}^{+},a_{i}^{-})\,R_{i}^{\top},\qquad a_{i}^{+}>0>a_{i}^{-}.

The eigenvalues are

a_{i}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{w}_{i}\sigma_{i}^{2}\pm\sqrt{\tilde{w}_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{4}+4w_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\tau_{i}^{2}}\right),

so indeed
a_{i}^{+}a_{i}^{-}=\det(A_{i})=-w_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\tau_{i}^{2}<0
.

Let
\tilde{K}_{i}
be any feasible
2\times 2
PSD matrix with eigenvalues

0<\kappa_{i,1}\leq\kappa_{i,2}\leq 1
. For fixed eigenvalues
(\kappa_{i,1},\kappa_{i,2})
,
the minimum of
\mathrm{tr}(A_{i}\tilde{K}_{i})
is attained by aligning the larger eigenvalue

\kappa_{i,2}
with the negative eigenvalue
a_{i}^{-}
. Thus we may restrict attention to

\tilde{K}_{i}=R_{i}\,\mathrm{diag}(\kappa_{i,1},\kappa_{i,2})\,R_{i}^{\top},

which yields cost
a_{i}^{+}\kappa_{i,1}+a_{i}^{-}\kappa_{i,2}
.

Since
a_{i}^{-}<0
, increasing
\kappa_{i,2}
strictly decreases the objective and increases the
determinant
\det(\tilde{K}_{i})=\kappa_{i,1}\kappa_{i,2}
, which preserves feasibility under a
lower bound on the determinant. Therefore at any optimum we must have
\kappa_{i,2}=1
.
Denote

\delta_{i}:=\kappa_{i,1}=\det(\tilde{K}_{i})\in(0,1].

Hence an optimal block has the form

\tilde{K}_{i}^{\star}=R_{i}\,\mathrm{diag}(\delta_{i}^{\star},1)\,R_{i}^{\top},\qquad\delta_{i}^{\star}\in(0,1],

and contributes cost
\mathrm{tr}(A_{i}\tilde{K}_{i}^{\star})=a_{i}^{-}+a_{i}^{+}\delta_{i}^{\star}
.

Collecting blocks yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(A\tilde{K}^{\star})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big(a_{i}^{-}+a_{i}^{+}\delta_{i}\big),

\displaystyle\log\det(\tilde{K}^{\star})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\delta_{i}.

Since
\sum_{i}a_{i}^{-}
is constant, the normalized design reduces to the scalar convex program

\min_{0<\delta_{i}\leq 1}\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{+}\delta_{i}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\delta_{i}\geq-2R.

The constraint must be active: if
\sum_{i}\log\delta_{i}>-2R
, one can decrease some
\delta_{i}

slightly (while keeping
\delta_{i}\leq 1
) to strictly lower the objective.

The KKT conditions yield the waterfilling solution

\delta_{i}^{\star}(R)=\min\left\{1,\ \frac{\eta^{\star}(R)}{a_{i}^{+}}\right\},

where
\eta^{\star}(R)>0
is chosen so that the (active) constraint holds:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log\delta_{i}^{\star}(R)=-2R.

Undoing the normalization yields the stated form (
69
)–(
77
).
∎

Multimodal Encoders and Elimination of the Remote Penalty

We study a remote sensing regime where the encoder observes noisy modalities of the latent
state
X
(e.g., vision/audio/text features) that are conditionally independent given
X
.

Let
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X})
with
\Sigma_{X}\succ 0
, and suppose the encoder observes
m
conditionally independent
modalities

Z_{j}=H_{j}X+W_{j},

(78)

for
j=1,\dots,m
, where
W_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R_{j})
are independent of each other and of
(X,V)
.
Let
Z^{(m)}:=(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{m})
, and define the MMSE proxy

S^{(m)}:=\mathbb{E}[X\mid Z^{(m)}].

(79)

Define

\Sigma_{S}^{(m)}:=\mathrm{Cov}(S^{(m)}).

(80)

Define the cumulative precision

J_{m}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}H_{j}^{\top}R_{j}^{-1}H_{j}\succeq 0.

(81)

Define the normalized recoverability matrix

\Gamma_{m}:=\Sigma_{X}^{-1/2}\,\Sigma_{S}^{(m)}\,\Sigma_{X}^{-1/2}.

(82)

Equivalently,

\Gamma_{m}=I-\Sigma_{X}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{X\mid Z^{(m)}}\Sigma_{X}^{-1/2}.

(83)

Define the geometric-mean recoverability factor

G_{m}:=\left(\frac{\det\Sigma_{S}^{(m)}}{\det\Sigma_{X}}\right)^{1/k}.

V-B

Compute as an Information Budget and Scaling of Semantic Precision

This subsection connects architectural and inference-time compute to an information budget
governing how sharply a model can refine its posterior over a latent state. The guiding idea
is that each layer (or inference step) can extract only a limited amount of new information
from the input (or memory) before passing a refined belief onward.

Let
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X})
be the latent state and let
Z
denote the available data
correlated with
X
. A representation learner produces a sequence of internal states

H^{(0)},H^{(1)},\dots,H^{(L)}
via a (possibly randomized) update rule

H^{(\ell+1)}\sim p_{\ell}(\cdot\mid H^{(\ell)},Z),

(93)

for
\ell=0,\dots,L-1
, so that the model can re-access
Z
at each step.

We interpret limited compute at step
\ell
as a bound on the incremental information extracted from
Z
:

I\!\big(Z;H^{(\ell+1)}\mid H^{(\ell)}\big)\leq R_{\ell},

(94)

for
\ell=0,\dots,L-1
.

Since
H^{(\ell+1)}
is generated from
(H^{(\ell)},Z)
, we have the Markov property

X-(H^{(\ell)},Z)-H^{(\ell+1)}
, hence by data processing

I\!\big(X;H^{(\ell+1)}\mid H^{(\ell)}\big)\leq R_{\ell},

(95)

for
\ell=0,\dots,L-1
.

Define the posterior error covariance after step
\ell
by

K_{X}^{(\ell)}:=\Sigma_{X\mid H^{(\ell)}}.

(96)

We take

K_{X}^{(0)}=\Sigma_{X}.

(97)

Theorem 6
.

Let
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{X})
and let
H^{(0)},\dots,H^{(L)}
be any sequence of
representations generated from
(H^{(\ell)},Z)
. Assume that for each
\ell
,

X-(H^{(\ell)},Z)-H^{(\ell+1)}
and (
94
) holds. Then

\log\det K_{X}^{(L)}\geq\log\det\Sigma_{X}-2\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}R_{\ell}.

(98)

Proof.

By the chain rule,

\displaystyle I(X;H^{(L)})

\displaystyle\leq I(X;H^{(0)})+\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}I\!\big(X;H^{(\ell+1)}\mid H^{(\ell)}\big).

By data processing under
X-(H^{(\ell)},Z)-H^{(\ell+1)}
and the incremental budget (
94
),
we have

\displaystyle I\!\big(X;H^{(\ell+1)}\mid H^{(\ell)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq I\!\big(Z;H^{(\ell+1)}\mid H^{(\ell)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq R_{\ell}.

Therefore,

I(X;H^{(L)})\leq I(X;H^{(0)})+\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}R_{\ell}.

Since
X
is Gaussian, Lemma
2
implies

I(X;H^{(L)})\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{X}}{\det K_{X}^{(L)}}.

Combining yields (
98
).
∎

Remark 8
.

Constraint (

beyond what is already contained in

Allowing each step to depend on

Accordingly, define

VI
Discussion and Future Directions

This paper developed a unified information-theoretic framework for strategic Gaussian
RD under semantic misalignment, observation constraints, and information-rate
limitations. Beyond the explicit characterizations derived in
Sections
III
and
IV
, the results raise broader conceptual implications for
energy-efficient and data-efficient artificial intelligence, and open several directions for
future research.

VI-A

Posterior Geometry as a Unifying Design Principle

A central conceptual contribution of this work is the identification of posterior covariance
geometry as the fundamental object governing semantic performance under resource constraints.
Across all encoder observation models, the rate or compute constraint induces a log-det bound on
the posterior error covariance, while the semantic objective imposes a weighted precision
requirement. This leads to a geometric tradeoff between entropy reduction and semantic alignment,
expressed through generalized waterfilling laws in the direct and remote regimes and through
posterior design in the persuasion regime.

VI-B

Compute as an Information Rate: Implications for Efficient AI

A key insight of the present work is that compute and architectural bottlenecks in modern learning
systems act as implicit information-rate constraints. Depth, width, attention bandwidth, context
length, and inference-time compute all limit the mutual information that can be propagated and
refined across layers or time steps. When viewed through this lens, neural scaling laws,
chain-of-thought reasoning, and iterative refinement can be interpreted as mechanisms for
allocating additional information rate to reduce posterior entropy.

VI-C

Multimodality as a Remedy for the Semantic Curse of Dimensionality

Our gap analysis shows that remote semantic encoding suffers an irreducible geometric-mean penalty
that grows exponentially with dimension unless the semantic proxy is informative in every
direction. Multimodal observation emerges as a principled remedy. By aggregating complementary
modalities, the recoverable semantic covariance increases and the geometric penalty disappears,
allowing performance to approach the direct benchmark without proportional increases in rate or
compute.

VI-D

Strategic Communication and Alignment Considerations

The strategic nature of the problem studied here highlights the importance of alignment and
objective mismatch in semantic systems. When the encoder and decoder optimize different
objectives, the encoder’s optimal strategy is not merely to compress information faithfully, but
to shape the decoder’s posterior beliefs in a manner consistent with its semantic priorities. In
the full-information regime, this manifests as Gaussian persuasion under a rate constraint.

VI-E

Modeling Assumptions and Limitations

The analysis in this paper relies on several simplifying assumptions that enable closed-form
characterizations but also delineate the scope of the results.

First, we restrict attention to Gaussian sources and linear Gaussian semantic models with
quadratic objectives. This choice allows exact posterior characterizations (without the
concavification approach of Kamenica and Gentzkow
[
7
]
), but real-world
semantic variables may be discrete or heavy-tailed. Extending the posterior-design framework to
non-Gaussian settings is an important direction.

Second, the decoder is assumed to best respond via MMSE estimation. While this is optimal under
quadratic loss and Gaussian assumptions, practical systems may employ approximate inference.
Investigating robustness of the strategic RD solutions to suboptimal decoders would further
strengthen applicability.

Third, our compute-as-rate interpretation abstracts away many details of hardware and algorithmic
efficiency. Incorporating explicit energy models and hardware constraints remains open.

VII
Conclusion

This paper presented a unified theory of strategic semantic compression under rate, observation,
and compute constraints. By combining RD theory with information design, we
characterized fundamental limits on semantic inference in settings with misaligned objectives and
limited resources. The resulting framework yields explicit solutions for direct, remote, and
full-information encoding regimes, quantifies performance gaps between them, and explains how
multimodal observation and increased compute mitigate these gaps.

In closing, the central message of this work is that efficient intelligence is
fundamentally a problem of
posterior design under resource constraints
.
By making posterior geometry explicit, information theory can play a central
role in guiding the development of future AI systems that are not only more
capable, but also more energy/data efficient and aligned.

Hybrid Encryption with Certified Deletion in Preprocessing Model
Kunal Dey, Reihaneh Safavi-Naini

Abstract
Certified deletion allows Alice to outsource data to Bob and, at a later time, obtain a verifiable guarantee that the file has been irreversibly deleted at her request. The functionality, while impossible using classical information alone, can be achieved using quantum information. Existing approaches rely either on one-time pad (OTP) encryption, or on computational hardness assumptions that may be vulnerable to future advances in classical or quantum computing. In this work, we introduce and formalize hybrid encryption with certified deletion in the preprocessing model (pHE-CD) and propose two constructions. Each construction composes an information-theoretic key encapsulation mechanism (iKEM) with a data encapsulation mechanism that provides certified deletion (DEM-CD) security, offering different types of security depending on the security properties of DEM-CD. When DEM-CD is one-time information theoretically secure, the composition provides information-theoretic security for both encryption and certified deletion. When DEM-CD is computationally secure, the composed construction offers computationally secure (post-quantum) encryption and everlasting certified deletion where confidentiality is computational up to the point that the deletion certificate is verified, and after successful verification of the certificate, becomes unconditional. That is, successful verification of deletion certificate guarantees that the data has been removed information-theoretically from the adversary’s view. Both pHE-CD schemes are for encryption of arbitrarily long messages. Construction 2 is key efficient and uses a DEM-CD that is constructed using quantum coding and AES, providing quantum-safe security for encryption. We discuss our results and directions for future work.



I
Introduction

Consider the scenario that Alice is outsourcing a file to Bob, and at a later time requires Bob to delete the file. Achieving guarantee for Alice that the Bob has actually deleted the file is impossible if only classical information is used because digital data can be perfectly copied. By leveraging quantum information, the no-cloning theorem
[
9
,
25
,
17
]
enabled Broadbent and Islam (BI)
[
5
]
to propose an elegant solution to this problem, known as
certified deletion

[
23
]
. BI’s scheme is a shared-key encryption scheme with
information-theoretic security
that allows Bob to make an exclusive choice: either (i) delete the ciphertext and produce a
certificate of deletion
which, when verified by Alice, guarantees that the file is information-theoretically removed from Bob’s view, or (ii) decrypt the ciphertext and learn the plaintext—but
not both
.
The scheme is particularly attractive because it employs
Wiesner coding

[
24
]
, a quantum encoding technique closely related to the BB84 quantum key distribution (QKD) protocol of Bennett and Brassard
[
4
]
, and so is implementable using today’s quantum technology
[
21
,
2
,
8
,
11
]
. The scheme however is one-time and, similar to one-time-pad
[
15
,
19
]
, fresh new shared key must be used for each message.

Hiroka et al. proposed
public-key encryption with certified deletion (PKE-CD)

[
14
]
, which replaces the shared secret key in the BI scheme with a public–private key pair for Bob. This eliminates the need for Alice and Bob to establish shared secrets before each message, enabling standard public-key infrastructure. Specifically, Alice generates a fresh one-time secret key for the BI (SKE-with-certified-deletion) scheme, encrypts the message under this key, and sends the resulting BI ciphertext together with an encryption of the one-time secret key under Bob’s public key.
This, however, comes at the cost of weakening the adversarial model, in that deletion security is guaranteed only against a
computationally bounded
adversary.
Following this work, two other PKE-CD schemes were proposed
[
3
,
13
]
that strengthen deletion security to
certified everlasting security

[
22
,
3
,
13
]
, where the adversary is assumed computationally bounded before it outputs a valid deletion certificate, but can be computationally unbounded after the certificate is verified.

Our work.
Our goal is to eliminate reliance on computational assumptions inherent in public-key encryption and instead base security solely on
physical and quantum-theoretic assumptions

[
16
,
18
,
10
]
.
As a result, the scheme achieves
long-term security
and remains secure against future advances in mathematical and quantum algorithms,
assuming the underlying physical assumptions continue to hold
. We, however, would like to maintain reuseablity of Bob’s key and remove establishing a shared key for each message.

Our approach and outline of the schemes.

We use
Hybrid Encryption in correlated randomness model
, also referred to as
Hybrid Encryption in pre-processing model (pHE)

[
20
]
that composes a KEM (Key Encapsulation Mechanism) and a DEM (Data Encapsulation Mechanism) to efficiently encrypt a message with proved post-quantum security. pHE extends the KEM/DEM Hybrid Encryption in public setting (HPKE) that was formalized in
[
6
,
12
]
for efficient public key encryption of arbitrary length messages. pHE replaces the computational public key component KEM, that relies on computational asumptions, with an
information theoretic iKEM
that uses correlated randomness
[
16
]
between Alice and Bob to establish a shared key between them (to be used for encryption of the message using DEM). It was shown that iKEM can be implemented by an information theoretic one-message key agreement protocols including QKD
[
7
,
20
]
.
The DEM component in pHE can be implemented using an OTP (One-Time-Pad), resulting in an encryption system with information theoretic security, or a
one-time secure symmetric key encryption system
with computation security that can be constructed using a block cipher such as AES, providing quantum-safe security for pHE. The composition theorem of pHE guarantees security of the composed system. Our approach in this paper is to adopt the pHE framework and compose an information-theoretic key encapsulation mechanism (iKEM) with a data encapsulation mechanism that supports certified deletion (DEM-CD), thereby obtaining a
hybrid encryption scheme with certified deletion in the preprocessing model
(pHE-CD), as described in Section
.

Construction 1
is a direct application of pHE framework, composing an iKEM with a
DEM-CD that is implemented using BI scheme.
We define security of pHE-CD and prove the composition achieves encryption and deletion security against a computationally unbounded adversary.
Information theoretic security for deletion implies that, after deletion certificate is verified, even if the randomness of encryption becomes available to the receiver, there will be no information about the message.

Construction 2.

The drawback of Construction 1 is the key requirement that is needed for information theoretic security of DEM-CD. In Construction 2, we design a DEM-CD that is inspired by the PKE-CD in
[
3
]
and allows us to replace OTP encryption with a one-time symmetric key encryption that is constructed using a block cipher such as AES. We prove computational (quantum-safe) security of encryption and everlasting security of deletion for this construction. In Section
, we present the two constructions.

II-A

Cryptographic Tools

We present a review of the cryptographic tools used throughout this paper.

Hybrid Encryption in Preprocessing Model.
Hybrid encryption in the preprocessing model, called HE for short, was introduced in
[
20
]

\IEEEenumerate\enumerate@item@The challenger samples $(X,Y,Z)\leftarrow\text{\sf{\sf pHE-CD}.KeyGen}(1^{\lambda},P)$ and provides $Z$ to $\mathcal{A}_{1}$.

In the following we introduce our notations for the challenger and adversary for each cryptographic primitive. \par\IEEEitemize\itemize@item@{pHE-CD}: The Challenger is denoted by, $ch$; and the adversary is denoted by $\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})$, where $\mathcal{A}_{1}$ is a QPT adversary and $\mathcal{A}_{2}$ is an adversary with unbounded quantum computation. \par\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@{iKEM}: The Challenger is denoted by $ch^{\text{iK}}$; and the Adversary is denoted by $\mathcal{A}^{\text{iK}}=(\mathcal{A}^{\text{iK}}_{1},\mathcal{A}^{\text{iK}}_{2})$. We define two games: $G_{0}$, which is identical to the deletion security game defined by the EV-$q_{e}$-CD experiment for pHE-CD, and $G_{1}$, which differs only in that the DEM-CD encryption uses an independently sampled key instead of the iKEM-encapsulated key. If an adversary can distinguish $G_{0}$ from $G_{1}$, then one can construct an adversary that breaks the IND-$q_{e}$-CEA security of the iKEM, bounding the difference between the success probabilities of the adversary in the two games by the advantage of the iKEM adversary in winning its IND-$q_{e}$-CEA game (Proposition \ref{prop: iKEM}). We also note that in $G_{1}$, the DEM-CD key is independent of the iKEM ciphertext and so distinguishing encryptions of the two messages after verification of deletion certificate, yields an adversary that breaks the one-time EV-CD security of DEM-CD, leading to a bound on the success probabilities of the adversary in the games (Proposition \ref{prop: DEMCD}). \par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Constructions}
In this section, we present two constructions: (1) a pHE-CD scheme achieving information-theoretic security for both encryption and certified deletion, and (2) a pHE-CD scheme providing computational security for encryption and everlasting certified deletion security.
\par\noindent\emph{Construction 1.}
We present a pHE-CD construction that provides information-theoretic security for encryption and certified deletion by composing: (i) an IND-$q_{e}$-CEA–secure information-theoretic iKEM, formalized in Sharifian et al.~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{sharifian2021information}{}{}]}, which can be realized using a one-way (one-message) secure key agreement (SKA) protocol in correlated randomness setting, and (ii) an information-theoretically secure one-time data encapsulation mechanism with certified deletion (DEM-CD), that can be instantiated using the one-time symmetric-key encryption with certified deletion (SKE-CD) scheme of Broadbent and Islam~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{broadbent2020quantum}{}{}]}. Intuitively, since both components provide information-theoretic security for encryption, the resulting pHE-CD scheme will provide information-theoretic IND-$q_{e}$-CPA encryption security using the composition theorem of hybrid encryption \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{sharifian2021information}{}{}, Theorem 2]}. Certified deletion security, follows the corresponding security proof (for deletion security) of Construction~2, but considering a computationally unbounded adversary~${\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{A}}_{1},{\mathcal{A}}_{2})$.
\par In particular, using an argument similar to the analysis of Theorem \ref{Thm: Construction2: CD}, any computationally unbounded adversary with non-negligible advantage for certified deletion security of the construction, translates into the corresponding attackers with non-negligible advantage for the security of the underlying primitives ($iKEM$ and DEM-CD deletion security). We omit the detailed description of the construction and the proof as they largely follow those of Construction~2. One difference between
the two constructions is that, for clarity of exposition, Construction 2 is described for a one-bit message, and extended to multi-bit messages by repeated application of one-bit construction, while Construction 1 is for multi-bit messages.
\par\par\noindent\emph{Construction 2.} The construction uses i) an IND-$q_{e}$-CEA secure iKEM, {\sf iK} = ({\sf iK.KeyGen, iK.Encap, iK.Decap}) (see ~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{sharifian2021information}{}{}]} for definition and instantiation); and ii) a computationally IND-OT secure DEM-CD, {\sf D = (D.KeyGen, D.{Encap}, D.Decap)}.
\par The construction of pHE-CD is described for a one-bit message and uses a new computationally secure (post-quantum) DEM-CD that is outlined below. The pHE-CD can be used for multibit messages by encrypting the message bit-by-bit (i.e. encrypting each message bit independently using the single-bit pHE-CD) and concatenating the resulting ciphertexts.

\begin{theorem}If {\sf iK} is $\text{IND-}q_{e}\text{-CEA}$ secure and {\sf D} is IND-OT secure, then {{\sf pHE-CD}} is computationally $\text{IND-}q_{e}\text{-CPA}$ secure.

\end{theorem}\@proof The claim follows immediately as a corollary of
Theorem~\ref{Thm: pHECD compose} and Theorem~\ref{Thm: DEM-CD: CD}.
In particular, we may assume that $C_{2}=\bigl(\left\rvert x\right\rangle_{\theta},{\sf D.Encap}\bigl(K,(\theta,m\oplus\bigoplus_{i:\theta_{i}=0}x_{i})\bigr)\bigr)=\text{\sf{\sf D-CD}.Encap}(K,m).$ By Theorem~\ref{Thm: DEM-CD: CD}, it follows that {{\sf D-CD}} achieves one-time

\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Concluding Remarks}
\par We introduced and formalized pHE-CD and
gave two constructions with proved security. While the key requirement of our first construction is the same as that of the one-time pad, the second construction relies on a new computationally secure (quantum-safe) DEM-CD that uses a constant-length key to encrypt messages of arbitrary length, thereby avoiding any dependency of the key length on the message length.
The quantum codings of these constructions are Wiesner conjugate coding \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{wiesner1983conjugate}{}{}]} that can be efficiently implemented using today's quantum technologies.
The security of our construction does not depend on computational intractability. It is derived from fundamental quantum principles, reasonable physical assumptions, and the proven optimality of quantum algorithms for unstructured search (e.g., Grover’s bound \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{boyer1998tight,grover1996fast}{}{}]}), offering a promising path toward long-term data security in the presence of evolving computational capabilities.
The pHE-CD framework
provides a foundation for constructing pHE-CD that use QKD for the construction of iKEM.
Such a composition requires extending qKEM security to include adversaries with query access to the encapsulation oracle, and is future work.

\par\begin{theorem}Let $\{Z_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a quantum operation with three arguments taking as input
a $\lambda$-bit string $\theta$, a bit $b^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}$, and a $\lambda$-qubit quantum register $\mathsf{A}$. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a class of adversaries such that for all $\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{A}$, for any $\theta\in\{0,1\}^{\lambda}$, $b^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}$, and any joint quantum state $\left\rvert\psi\right\rangle^{\mathsf{A},\mathsf{C}}$ on a $\lambda$-qubit register $\mathsf{A}$ and an arbitrary auxiliary register $\mathsf{C}$, we have
\begin{equation*}\begin{split}\Pr\!\left[A_{\lambda}\!\left(Z_{\lambda}(\theta,b^{\prime},\mathsf{A}),\mathsf{C}\right)=1\right]-\Pr\!\left[A_{\lambda}\!\left(Z_{\lambda}(0^{\lambda},b^{\prime},\mathsf{A}),\mathsf{C}\right)=1\right]\\
=\text{negl}(\lambda).\end{split}\end{equation*}\par That is, $Z_{\lambda}$ is semantically secure against $\mathcal{A}$ with respect to its first input.
\par For any $\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{A}$, define the distribution
$\{Z^{A_{\lambda}}_{\lambda}(b)\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N},\,b\in\{0,1\}}$ over quantum states by the
following experiment:
\IEEEitemize\itemize@item@Sample $x,\theta\leftarrow\{0,1\}^{\lambda}$ and initialize $A_{\lambda}$ with
$$Z_{\lambda}\!\left(\theta,\,b\oplus\bigoplus_{i:\theta_{i}=0}x_{i},\,\left\rvert x\right\rangle_{\theta}\right).$$\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@Let the output of $A_{\lambda}$ be parsed as a string $x^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{\lambda}$ and a residual
quantum state on register $\mathsf{A}^{\prime}$.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@If $x_{i}=x^{\prime}_{i}$ for all $i$ such that $\theta_{i}=1$, output $\mathsf{A}^{\prime}$; otherwise output a special
symbol $\bot$.
\endIEEEitemize\par Then,
$$\mathrm{TD}\!\left(Z^{A_{\lambda}}_{\lambda}(0),\,Z^{A_{\lambda}}_{\lambda}(1)\right)=\text{negl}(\lambda).$$\end{theorem}\par\par We now show that the trace-distance bound established above implies
one-time everlasting certified deletion security as defined in
Definition~\ref{Def: SKE-CD EV-CD}. To this end, we define a quantum
operation $\mathcal{Z}_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)$ with three inputs as
follows:
\IEEEenumerate\enumerate@item@Sample $K\leftarrow{\sf D.KeyGen}(1^{\lambda})$ and
$x,\theta\leftarrow\{0,1\}^{\lambda}$.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\enumerate@item@Compute
$m^{\prime}:=m\oplus\bigoplus_{i:\theta_{i}=0}x_{i}$ and prepare the BB84
state $\left\rvert x\right\rangle_{\theta}$.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\enumerate@item@Output the joint state
$(\left\rvert x\right\rangle_{\theta},{\sf D.Encap}(K,(\theta,b^{\prime})))$.
\endIEEEenumerate Since ${\sf D}$ is an IND-OT secure data encapsulation mechanism (DEM),
it follows that $\mathcal{Z}_{\lambda}$ semantically hides its first input,
as required by Theorem~3.1 of~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{bartusek2023cryptography}{}{}]}.
\par Let $\rho_{\lambda}^{b}$ denote the joint quantum state consisting of the
ciphertext, verification information, and the adversary’s internal
registers at the moment when the certificate is verified.
By the correctness of the certified deletion procedure, conditioned on
$\text{\sf D-CD.Vrfy}(cert,vk)=\top$, the resulting residual states
$\rho_{\lambda}^{0}$ and $\rho_{\lambda}^{1}$ satisfy
$$\mathrm{TD}(\rho_{\lambda}^{0},\rho_{\lambda}^{1})=\text{negl}(\lambda).$$By the operational interpretation of trace distance, this implies that
for any (possibly computationally unbounded) distinguisher
$\mathcal{A}_{2}$,
$$\bigl|\Pr[\mathcal{A}_{2}(\rho_{\lambda}^{0})=1]-\Pr[\mathcal{A}_{2}(\rho_{\lambda}^{1})=1]\bigr|\leq\mathrm{TD}(\rho_{\lambda}^{0},\rho_{\lambda}^{1})=\text{negl}(\lambda).$$Therefore, even after receiving the secret key $K$, the second-stage
adversary $\mathcal{A}_{2}$ cannot distinguish whether $m_{0}$ or $m_{1}$ was
encrypted with more than negligible advantage. Consequently,
$$\text{Adv}_{\text{D-CD},\mathcal{A}}^{\text{ev-cd}}(\lambda)\leq\text{negl}(\lambda),$$which establishes one-time everlasting certified deletion security of
{\sf D-CD}.

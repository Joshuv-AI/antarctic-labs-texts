1.
Introduction

Srinivasa Ramanujan introduced the exponential sum

(1)

\displaystyle c_{r}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{m=1}\\
(m,r)=1\end{subarray}}^{r}e^{\frac{2\pi imn}{r}}

in
[
13
]
and used this sum to derive infinite Fourier series like expansions for several arithmetical functions in the form
\sum\limits_{r}a_{r}c_{r}(n)
. The exponential sum (
1
) now known as the
Ramanujan sum
has several interesting properties, many of which can be found in
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
, or
[
14
]
. If a Fourier series like expansion in the form
\sum\limits_{r}a_{r}c_{r}(n)
exists for an arithmetical function, the function is said to possess a
Ramanujan-Fourier series expansion
or simply a
Ramanujan expansion
. Ramanujan expansions were computed for various functions by many other authors, see, for example
[
7
]
. Various conditions for the existence of Ramanujan expansions appeared in papers like
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
.

Ramanujan sum has been generalized in many directions. In
[
2
]
, E. Cohen gave a generalization defining

(2)

\displaystyle c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}h=1\\
{(h,r^{s})_{s}=1}\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi inh}{r^{s}}},

where for a positive integer
s
, integers
m,n
, not both zero, the
generalized GCD
of
m
and
n
denoted by
(m,n)_{s}
is the largest
l^{s}
(where
l\in\mathbb{N}
) dividing both
m
and
n
. If
(m,n)_{s}=1
, then
m
and
n
are said to be relatively
s
-prime to each other. When
s=1
, generalized GCD becomes the usual GCD and then the sum (
2
) reduces to the usual Ramanujan sum.
We will refer to the sum (
2
) by the name
Cohen-Ramanujan sum
. Like the usual Ramanujan sum, for certain specific values of
s
and
n
, this sum becomes equal to various well known arithmetical functions. For example,
c_{r}^{s}(0)=\Phi_{s}(r^{s})=J_{s}(r)
, where
\Phi_{s}
is the Klee’s function and
J_{s}
is the Jordan totient function. Please see the next section for the definitions of these two functions. Cohen derived various properties of this sum and proved several results using this generalization in a series of papers
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, and
[
4
]
.

Another generalization of the Ramanujan sum, and a list of references to some more generalizations can be found in
[
15
]
. Recently, Haukkanen and McCarthy gave another generalization in
[
8
]
.

An arithmetical function
f
is said to admit a
Cohen-Ramanujan expansion

\displaystyle f(n):=\sum\limits_{r}\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(n),

if the series on the right hand side converges for suitable complex numbers
\widehat{f}(r)
. A study on the Cohen-Ramanujan expansions of some arithmetical functions were conducted by these authors in
[
16
]
. Some necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of such expansions were also given there. To the best of our knowledge, such series expansions are not available for any other generalization of the Ramanujan sum as of now.

In
[
7
]
, Hardy derived a Ramanujan expansion for the function
\Lambda_{1}(n):=(\phi(n)/n)\Lambda(n)
in the form

(3)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(n)=\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(q)}{\phi(q)}c_{q}(n),

where
\Lambda(n)
is the von Mangoldt function defined as

\displaystyle\Lambda(n):=\begin{cases}\log p,\text{ if $n=p^{k}$ , $p$ is prime and $k$ any positive integer}\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

and
\mu
is the Möbius function.

If
a
is an arithmetical function with the Ramanujan expansion

a(n)=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}a_{r}c_{r}(n)
,
then the Wiener–Khintchine theorem for an arithmetical function
a

[
5
, Section 3.2]
can be stated as

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n\leq qN}a(n)a(n+h)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}a_{r}^{2}c_{r}(h)

provided that certain convergence conditions are satisfied.
H. Gadiyar and R. Padma
[
17
]
showed that Hardy-Littlewood conjecture on twin primes can be proved if the Wiener–Khintchine theorem can be applied to the Ramanujan expansion of the arithmetical function
\Lambda_{1}
provided that the expansion is uniformly and absolutely convergent. They derived the formula

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\leq N}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)\sim N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\phi(r)}c_{r}(h),

which agrees with the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture. But note that this identity is valid only if the Ramanujan expansion (
3
) derived by Hardy is absolutely convergent, which is not actually the case. Taking forward these discussions, H. Gadiyar, M. Ram Murty and R. Padma
[
6
]
derived an asymptotic formula for the sum

\sum\limits_{n\leq N}f(n)g(n+h),

where
f
and
g
are two arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Ramanujan expansions.

In this paper, we use the Cohen-Ramanujan expansion in the context initiated in
[
6
]
and derive an asymptotic formula for the sum
\sum\limits_{n\leq N}f(n)g(n+h)
,
where
f
and
g
are arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions satisfying some additional conditions. Therefore, our results extend the results in
[
6
]
. We also provide some examples to demonstrate our results.

We also give a condition for the existence of the Cohen-Ramanujan expansion for
f_{h}(n):=f(n+h)
when such an expansion for
f
exists.

The main results we propose in this paper are the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are two arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions

f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(n)
and
g(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\end{subarray}}\widehat{g}(k)c_{k}^{s}(n)

respectively. Suppose that
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,k\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(k)\widehat{g}(k)|(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})<\infty
. Then as
N
tends to infinity,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n)\sim N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)\Phi_{s}(r^{s})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are two arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions

f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(n)
and
g(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\end{subarray}}\widehat{g}(k)c_{k}^{s}(n)

respectively. Suppose that
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,k\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(k)|(r^{s}k^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})<\infty
. Then as
N
tends to infinity,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n+h)\sim N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
f
has an absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansion with coefficients
\widehat{f}(q)
satisfying
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}|\widehat{f}(q)|\tau(q)<\infty
. Then
f_{h}
has an absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansion with coefficients
\dfrac{\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)}{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})}
.

As usual,
[m,n]

Note that

n^{s}=\sum\limits_{d|n}J_{s}(d)

J_{s}(n)=\Phi_{s}(n^{s})

(4)

\displaystyle n^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d|n}\Phi_{s}(d^{s})=\sum\limits_{d^{s}|n^{s}}\Phi_{s}(d^{s}).

By

, where

l^{s}

n

l\in\mathbb{N}
.
\zeta(s)

\zeta(s)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}

Let

3.
Proofs of the Main Results

We start this section proving three inequalities involving products of Cohen-Ramanujan sums essential in the proof of our main results.

Lemma 3.1
.

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n)\leq N\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
d^{s}\mid n\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{r}{d}\right)d^{s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid k\\
e^{s}\mid n\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{k}{e}\right)e^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{r}{d}\right)\mu\left(\frac{k}{e}\right)d^{s}e^{s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
d^{s}\mid k\\
e^{s}\mid n\end{subarray}}1.

If we apply Lemma
2.2
to the last step above, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{r}{d}\right)\mu\left(\frac{k}{e}\right)d^{s}e^{s}\left\llbracket\frac{N}{[d^{s},e^{s}]}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{r}{d}\right)\mu\left(\frac{k}{e}\right)d^{s}e^{s}\frac{N}{[d^{s},e^{s}]}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{r}{d}\right)\mu\left(\frac{k}{e}\right)(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}N\hskip 5.69046pt(\text{since }(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}[d^{s},e^{s}]=d^{s}e^{s})

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}

Now we use Equation (
4
) to get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid r\\
e\mid k\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid r^{s}\\
e^{s}\mid k^{s}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid d^{s}\\
\delta^{s}\mid e^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s}).

In the sum
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid r^{s}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid d^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})
, each
\delta
is repeated
\tau(\frac{r^{s}}{\delta^{s}})
times.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid r^{s}\\
e^{s}\mid k^{s}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(d^{s},e^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})\tau_{s}\left(\frac{r^{s}}{\delta^{s}}\right)\tau_{s}\left(\frac{k^{s}}{\delta^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})

\displaystyle=N\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(\delta^{s})

\displaystyle=N\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})(r^{s},k^{s})_{s}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{by Equation }(\ref{phi-reltn}).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)=\delta_{r,k}Nc_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}k^{s}\log r^{s}k^{s})
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,k^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ib(n+h)}{k^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,k^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ibh}{k^{s}}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{a}{r^{s}}+\frac{b}{k^{s}})n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,k^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ibh}{k^{s}}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}.

We estimate

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}

in two cases.

Case 1 :

\frac{a}{r^{s}}+\frac{b}{k^{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Here

\displaystyle\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow r^{s}k^{s}\mid ak^{s}+br^{s}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow ak^{s}+br^{s}=tr^{s}k^{s},\text{ for some }t\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow br^{s}=tr^{s}k^{s}-ak^{s}.

Since
k^{s}\mid r^{s}k^{s}-ak^{s}
and
(b,k^{s})_{s}=1
, we have
k\mid r
. Using similar arguments, we get
r\mid k
. Therefore
r=k
and so by our assumption,
r^{s}\mid a+b
. Since
1\leq a<r^{s}
and
1\leq b<r^{s}
, it follows that
r^{s}=a+b
.
Then
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}1=N.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=Nc_{r}^{s}(h).

Case 2 :

\frac{a}{r^{s}}+\frac{b}{k^{s}}\notin\mathbb{Z}
.

In this case
e^{2\pi i(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}})n}\neq 1
. Let us write
N=(r^{s}k^{s})Q+R
, where
0\leq R<r^{s}k^{s}
. Then,

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\right)n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq(r^{s}k^{s})Q\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\right)n}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(r^{s}k^{s})Q<n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\right)n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|0+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(r^{s}k^{s})Q<n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\right)n}\right|.

In the above step, we used the fact that
e^{\frac{2\pi ikl}{r^{s}k^{s}}}
is a proper non-trivial root of unity and so
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}k^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ikl}{r^{s}k^{s}}}=0
when
l\neq 0
. Now

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\right)n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(r^{s}k^{s})Q<n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\right)n}\right|.

By
[
korobov2013exponential
, Lemma 1]
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
(with
p>0
), we have
\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=q+1\end{subarray}}^{q+p}e^{2\pi i\alpha k}\right|\leq min\{p,\frac{1}{2\|\alpha\|}\}

and so

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\right)n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\left\|\frac{ak^{s}+br^{s}}{r^{s}k^{s}}\right\|}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\

\displaystyle=N\zeta(a+1)\zeta(b+1)\sum\limits_{r}\frac{c_{r}^{s}(h)}{r^{(a+b+2)s}}

\displaystyle=N\zeta(a+1)\zeta(b+1)\sum\limits_{r}\frac{c_{r}^{s}(m^{s})}{r^{(a+b+2)s}}\hskip 10.0pt(\text{since }c_{r}^{s}(m^{s}k)=c_{r}(m^{s}))

\displaystyle=N\zeta(a+1)\zeta(b+1)\frac{\sigma_{(a+b+1)s}(m)}{m^{(a+b+1)s}}\frac{1}{\zeta(a+b+2)}

\displaystyle=N\frac{\zeta(a+1)\zeta(b+1)}{\zeta(a+b+2)}\frac{\sum\limits_{d\mid m}d^{(a+b+1)s}}{m^{(a+b+1)s}}

\displaystyle=N\frac{\zeta(a+1)\zeta(b+1)}{\zeta(a+b+2)}\sum\limits_{k\mid m}k^{-(a+b+1)s},\hskip 28.45274pt\text{where }m=kd

\displaystyle=N\frac{\zeta(a+1)\zeta(b+1)}{\zeta(a+b+2)}\sigma_{-(a+b+1)s}(m).

∎

Now we proceed to discuss the existence of Cohen-Ramanujan expansion for
f_{h}
derived from
f
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
h\leq N
,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)\leq 2N\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\tau(k)
.

Proof.

Consider the sum

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{k}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\\
d^{s}\mid n+h\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)d^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)d^{s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
d^{s}\mid n+h\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)d^{s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+h\leq N+h\\
d^{s}\mid n+h\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)d^{s}\left\llbracket\frac{N+h}{d^{s}}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ian}{r^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)d^{s}\frac{N+h}{d^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq(N+h)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,r^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}1\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq(N+h)\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\tau(k)

\displaystyle\leq 2N\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\tau(k).

∎

The orthogonality relation allows us to write down the possible candidates for the Cohen-Ramanujan coefficients of any given arithmetical function. Indeed, if
f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n)
, then
f(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n)c_{r}^{s}(n).

So we get,

\displaystyle\lim_{x^{s}\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x^{s}\end{subarray}}f(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x^{s}\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x^{s}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)\lim_{x^{s}\to\infty}\frac{1}{x^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x^{s}\end{subarray}}c_{q}^{s}(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(r)\lim_{x^{s}\to\infty}\Phi_{s}(r^{s}).

Therefore,
\widehat{f}(r)=\frac{M(fc_{r}^{s})}{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})}
,
where by
M(f)
, we mean the limit
\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{x}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}f(n)
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose that
f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n)
. Then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n+h)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h).

We can now see the first term in the above summation can be estimated to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\left[\delta_{r,q}Nc_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}q^{s}\log(r^{s}q^{s}))\right]\hskip 14.22636pt\text{by Lemma }\ref{lem2}

\displaystyle=Nc_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}U^{s}\log(r^{s}U^{s})).

and the second term can be estimated to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)

\displaystyle\leq 2N\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\tau(q)\hskip 14.22636pt{\text{by Lemma \ref{lem4}}}.

Since
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(q)|\tau(q)<\infty
,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q>U\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(q)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{s}(n)c_{q}^{s}(n+h)=O(N).

Therefore
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n+h)c_{r}^{s}(n)=N\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}U^{s}\log(r^{s}U^{s}))+O(N)
. Now

\displaystyle M(f_{h}c_{r}^{s})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f_{h}(n)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n+h)c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left[N\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)+O(r^{s}U^{s}\log(r^{s}U^{s}))+O(N)\right]

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h).

The orthogonality relation now gives

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{h}(r)=\frac{M(f_{h}c_{r}^{s})}{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})}=\frac{\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)}{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})}.

∎

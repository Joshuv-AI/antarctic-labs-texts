Opportunistic screening for osteoporosis and vertebral fracture using CT attenuation from 18F-fluorocholine PET/CT in patients with prostate cancer
Authors: Astrid Dauchez, Charles Dariane, Jacques Fechtenbaum, Sylvain Bodard, Sylvain Guinebert, Marc-Olivier Timsit, Christian Roux, Fabien Hyafil, Karine Briot, Olivier Fogel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveAndrogen deprivation therapy (ADT) for prostate cancer (PCa) induces bone loss and increases fracture risk, yet baseline bone mineral density (BMD) assessment is rarely performed. Opportunistic BMD evaluation using routine imaging could improve osteoporosis screening in this population. We aimed to assess the feasibility of using trabecular attenuation (TA) in Hounsfield units (HU) from the low-dose CT component of 18F-fluorocholine (FCh) positron emission tomography (PET)/CT for opportunistic osteoporosis and vertebral fracture screening in men with PCa, compared with stand-alone diagnostic CT.MethodsIn this retrospective single-centre study, 81 patients with PCa who underwent both FCh-PET/CT and stand-alone CT within 6 months were included. TA(HU) was measured at L1-L3 vertebral levels on both scans. ADT exposure and duration were recorded to assess their associations with TA. Primary outcomes were intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) and Bland-Altman agreement between TA values from FCh-PET/CT and stand-alone CT. Secondary outcomes included associations between TA, ADT duration and vertebral fractures.ResultsTA values from FCh-PET/CT and stand-alone CT were comparable (ICC: 0.72, 0.70 and 0.67, for L1-L3; p<0.001). Mean FCh-PET/CT TA were 121 HU (L1), 122 HU (L2) and 118 HU (L3). Lower attenuation correlated with longer ADT duration (r=−0.43; p=0.008). Vertebral fractures were observed in 18.5% of patients and were associated with lower TA (87.8 vs 127.5 HU; p=0.01).ConclusionsFCh-PET/CT provides a feasible, readily available approach for opportunistic osteoporosis and fracture risk screening in men with PCa, supporting its integration into routine imaging workflows to promote bone health assessment and fracture prevention.Keywords: Osteoporosis, Epidemiology, Bone Density

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is the most frequently diagnosed cancer in France and ranks as the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality among men, with a median age at death of 80 years.1 Since PCa primarily affects older men who experience age-related declines in bone mineral density (BMD), these patients are at heightened risk for osteoporosis. Furthermore, the hormone-sensitive nature of PCa necessitates the use of hormone-modulating therapies at various stages, often involving combinations of hormone treatments or prostate radiotherapy. Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), although effective in managing aggressive or advanced PCa,2 induces hypogonadism, which leads to bone loss and consequently an increased fracture risk.3 4 Osteoporotic fractures significantly elevate morbidity and mortality rates, even when fractures are minor, particularly in elderly patients.5

Clinical guidelines recommend assessing osteoporosis risk prior to initiating ADT, including baseline BMD measurement.6,8 While dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) remains the gold standard for BMD assessment,9 it is infrequently performed in routine clinical practice. Opportunistic screening methods using existing imaging exams have been proposed to improve osteoporosis detection without incurring additional time, cost or radiation exposure.10 One promising approach involves measuring attenuation in Hounsfield units (HU) on CT scans to quantify bone parameters. CT-derived lumbar trabecular attenuation (TA) in HU enables opportunistic screening for osteoporosis and fractures, as lumbar vertebral TA correlates strongly with DXA-measured BMD.11 Since the establishment of this correlation, opportunistic CT screening has been validated as an effective tool for osteoporosis detection in the general population and high-risk groups.12,14 However, data on its utility in PCa patients remain scarce and limited to two studies using CT scans obtained for radiotherapy planning.15 16

During initial staging and follow-up of PCa patients, imaging is routinely performed to evaluate local and distant disease spread, including lymph node involvement, bone metastases and visceral metastases. In addition to pelvic and prostatic MRI, 18F-fluorocholine (FCh) positron emission tomography (PET) is widely used in France for staging aggressive PCa and has increasingly replaced the traditional combination of CT and bone scintigraphy (ie, bone scan),17 especially when prostate-specific membrane antigen PET/CT (PSMA-PET/CT) is unavailable.18 Despite the routine acquisition of low-dose CT during FCh-PET/CT, its potential for opportunistic osteoporosis screening has not yet been explored.

The primary endpoint of this study was to assess the feasibility of using the low-dose CT component of FCh-PET/CT imaging to opportunistically screen for osteoporosis and vertebral fractures in PCa patients, compared with standard diagnostic CT scans. Secondary endpoints included descriptive attenuation distributions in the cohort, associations with clinical variables (age, smoking, body mass index (BMI), ADT exposure/duration, vertebral metastasis and prevalence of vertebral fractures).

Patients and methods

Study design and patient cohort

This monocentric retrospective observational study used data collected during routine patient care. Between 2020 and 2022, we identified 97 patients who underwent FCh-PET/CT for PCa at the Nuclear Medicine Department of Georges Pompidou European Hospital, a tertiary referral centre involving multidisciplinary PCa management teams including urologists, radiation oncologists, medical oncologists and radiologists.

To compare TA measurements derived from the low-dose CT acquired during FCh-PET/CT with those from standard diagnostic CT scans, 81 patients who had both examinations within a maximum interval of 6 months were included for the study. Intervals exceeding 6 months were excluded to minimise confounding changes in bone TA unrelated to imaging technique differences. For patients with multiple FCh-PET/CT scans, the most recent or the one closest in time to the available CT scan was selected. A subgroup of 14 patients from the cohort was used to evaluate inter-reader and intra-reader reproducibility (figure 1). Demographic and clinical data were extracted from medical records by an independent expert blinded to imaging results.

Ethics approval was obtained (CLEP Decision N°: AAA-2022–0802). For this retrospective analysis of anonymised imaging data, the requirement for individual informed consent was waived in accordance with institutional and national regulations.

Acquisition Protocol

FCh-PET/CT examinations were performed using hybrid PET/CT systems, primarily GE Discovery MI (n=61), GE Discovery 690 (n=17) and Philips Gemini GXL-16 (n=3). Low-dose CT scans (non-contrast) were acquired for attenuation correction with tube voltages ranging from 100 to 120 kVp (mean 106.8±9.5 kVp) and tube-current modulation (80–250 mAs) using GE Smart-mA for a noise index of 30. Acquisition parameters included a 50 cm field of view, 0.8 s rotation time, a pitch of 1.4 and 1.25 mm axial slice reconstruction. Stand-alone CT scans were performed at 90–120 kVp (mean 114.1±10.8 kVp) with tube-current modulation, reflecting routine clinical practice. Contrast status for these stand-alone CTs was not recorded in the dataset. The heterogeneity of recorded parameters reflects clinical acquisition variability rather than prescriptive protocol settings.

Bone parameter assessment

TA was measured from CT attenuation in HU on a single axial slice per vertebra, selected parallel to the endplates at the level of the pedicles. A fixed 200 mm² elliptical region of interest (ROI) was manually placed within the anterior one-third of the anteroposterior centre axis of the vertebral body in the trabecular bone while strictly avoiding cortical bone, focal sclerotic/lytic changes, endplate sclerosis and visible vascular channels (online supplemental figure 1). Standard soft tissue window/level settings were used. This method follows previously validated protocols.19 20 Lower HU values corresponded to lower bone density surrogates.

Vertebrae with abnormalities that could distort measurements (eg, metastases, fractures, hyperostosis, focal heterogeneity) were excluded, with abnormality types and locations recorded. When L1 vertebrae were unsuitable, measurements were performed on L2 or L3 if both L1 and L2 were unusable. Although no universally accepted HU threshold defines osteoporosis, previous work by Graffy et al suggests an L1 attenuation ≤90 HU as an optimal cut-off for identifying patients at risk of osteoporotic vertebral fractures in a population averaging 74 years.21 We adopted this threshold for our analyses.

Vertebral fractures and metastases were evaluated on sagittal spinal reconstructions. Vertebral fractures were defined according to the Genant semiquantitative method, with any detectable endplate depression (grade 1 or higher) being considered a fracture; however, the fracture grade was not systematically recorded by the readers. Both lytic and sclerotic metastases were documented.

The 81 patients were divided into four groups, each independently assessed by four readers (two rheumatologists and two radiologists) after a standardisation training session. Inter-observer reproducibility was assessed on the 14-patient subgroup evaluated by all readers. Intra-observer reproducibility was assessed by two readers who repeated measurements on the same subgroup after 6 months.

Statistical analysis

Agreement between attenuation measured on FCh-PET/CT-derived and on stand-alone CT was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) and visualised with Bland-Altman plots. Inter- and intra-reader reproducibility was evaluated using ICC for continuous variables and Fleiss’ kappa for categorical variables, with values interpreted using conventional descriptors (the guidelines of Cicchetti and the Landis and Koch classification, respectively). Associations between FCh-PET/CT-derived TA and clinical variables were analysed using Pearson’s correlation for continuous data and Student’s t-test or Fisher’s exact test for dichotomous variables. A two-sided significance threshold of p<0.05 was applied.

Results

Patients’ characteristics

Among the 81 patients, the mean age was 74.2±8.3 years and mean BMI was 26.6±4.0 kg/m². Thirty-eight patients (46.9%) were current or former smokers. Forty-two patients (51.9%) had received ADT prior to FCh-PET/CT, with a mean duration of 39.2±34.8 months. Indications for FCh-PET/CT included initial staging in 34.6%, biochemical recurrence in 60.5% and disease follow-up in 4.9% (table 1). Only two patients had received prior anti-resorptive treatment (one with monthly denosumab, one with denosumab every 6 months), while this information was missing in 41% of cases.

Reproducibility of TA measurements from FCh-PET/CT scans

A subgroup of 14 patients from the cohort was used to evaluate inter-reader and intra-reader reproducibility among the four readers (figure 1). For FCh-PET/CT TA, the overall ICC was 0.92 (p <0.001). Excellent reproducibility was observed for each vertebra, with ICCs of 0.93, 0.90 and 0.93 for L1, L2 and L3, respectively. Reproducibility for detecting fractures and bone metastases, measured by Fleiss’ kappa, was moderate, at 0.51 (p <0.001) and 0.48 (p <0.001), respectively. Intra-reader reproducibility, assessed from two readings performed 6 months apart by the same reader, also showed excellent agreement, with an ICC of 0.96 (p <0.001).

Assessment of TA measurements on low-dose CT performed with PET compared with those on stand-alone CT scans

Three vertebrae (L1, L2 and L3) were analysed per patient, for the 81 patients, totalling 243 vertebrae. Readers identified vertebral anomalies potentially interfering with ROI placement in 12 vertebrae (4.9%) from nine patients. These abnormalities included fractures in four vertebrae, metastases in six, advanced degenerative disc disease in one, and an angioma in one (online supplemental figure 2). Vertebrae were non-contributive at 7.4% for L1, 2.7% for L2 and 4.9% for L3. When the L1 vertebra was non-contributive, L2 was usable in all but one patient, whose L3 vertebra was also non-contributive. After excluding these 12 vertebrae, TA values were comparable between the two imaging modalities, although attenuation values on stand-alone CT tended to be higher than those on FCh-PET/CT. The ICC between modalities was 0.72, 0.70 and 0.67 for L1, L2 and L3, respectively (p <0.001), reflecting good agreement (table 2, figure 2). No significant correlation was found between scan interval (≤6 months) and TA concordance (r = –0.09, p=0.2), supporting our assumption that scan timing within this window did not affect BMD measurements.

Bone characteristics on low-dose FCh-PET/CT scan

The mean TA was 121±46 HU for L1, 122±48 for L2 and 118±45 HU for L3 (table 2). Correlation coefficients between L1 and L2, and between L1 and L3 TA values were 0.81 (p<0.001) and 0.80 (p<0.001), respectively. A scatter plot revealed one outlier, corresponding to a vertebra affected by severe degenerative disc disease with extensive endplate sclerosis (online supplemental figure 3). Fifteen patients (18.5%) had vertebral fractures, and 19 (23.5%) had vertebral bone metastases.

FCh-PET/CT TA and clinical parameters

We evaluated the association of clinical factors typically linked with osteoporosis and low HU values (table 3). Lower TA was significantly correlated with older age (r=−0.45; p<0.001). No significant correlation was found between BMI and HU values (r=−0.06; p=0.6). There were trends towards lower TA in patients with a smoking history and those exposed to ADT. Among patients receiving ADT, longer treatment duration was significantly correlated with lower TA (r=−0.43; p=0.008). TA was lower in patients undergoing FCh-PET/CT for biochemical recurrence compared with those at initial diagnosis (110.4±47 HU vs 132.9±41 HU; p=0.03). Patients with vertebral bone metastases had significantly lower derived TA than those without metastases (p=0.004); of these, 78% were receiving ADT. Using the predefined threshold of 90 HU, 18 patients (22.2%) were considered at risk for vertebral fractures. Within this group, 44% had fractures compared with 11.3% among patients with attenuation >90 HU (p=0.004). Patients with fractures also had significantly lower TA compared with those without fractures (mean 87.8 vs 127.5; p=0.01) (table 3).

Discussion

This study is the first to evaluate the utility of low-dose CT associated with FCh-PET for opportunistic osteoporosis screening in PCa patients. Previous studies have highlighted the value of assessing lumbar TA on routine CT scans, particularly in at-risk populations such as oncology patients.12 14 While two studies have investigated the feasibility of CT-based osteoporosis screening in men undergoing radiotherapy for PCa,15 16 to date no data have addressed the use of FCh-PET/CT in the broader PCa population.

We demonstrated the feasibility of using FCh-PET/CT as an opportunistic screening tool for osteoporosis in PCa patients. Our findings indicate that molecular imaging with FCh-PET/CT can provide reliable TA measurements for indirect BMD estimation, comparable to conventional CT. A good correlation was observed between attenuation values obtained from both modalities. The lower ICC at L3 is biologically and technically plausible, given the higher prevalence of degenerative changes, greater beam-hardening and motion effects near the pelvis and increased variability in ROI placement at this level. Slightly higher HU values on stand-alone CT likely reflect inclusion of both contrast-enhanced and non-contrast scans, whereas the PET/CT component was non-contrast, as iodinated contrast and higher acquisition settings can modestly increase vertebral attenuation (by approximately 10 HU22 23). L1 measurements were feasible in 93% of patients, indicating that artefacts limiting measurement are uncommon. When L1 was non-assessable due to focal abnormalities such as metastases, L2 measurements correlated well and could reliably substitute. The CT-based measurement approach, by excluding artefact-prone areas, minimises variability between vertebrae.

Excellent inter- and intra-observer reproducibility, including between radiologists and rheumatologists, supports the reliability of this method for routine clinical use, requiring only minimal training. However, agreement for detecting fractures and metastases was moderate, likely reflecting challenges in distinguishing mild endplate deformities from true fractures (grade 1), and the absence of metabolic PET data to confirm lesions suspected of being metastatic.

Our results confirm that PCa patients represent a population at risk for bone complications, consistent with current guidelines recommending bone health assessment prior to initiating ADT.1 In our cohort, 22% of patients had TA from the low-dose CT scan of FCh-PET≤90 HU, among whom 44% had vertebral fractures. Among patients with TA above 90 HU, 11% still presented fractures. TA was negatively associated with age, smoking status and duration of ADT, consistent with literature on hormone therapy-induced bone loss. Patients with vertebral bone metastases showed lower TA despite some metastases being sclerotic; most were on ADT, suggesting treatment-related bone loss. In addition, TA was lower in patients undergoing FCh-PET/CT for biochemical recurrence compared with those at initial diagnosis, further supporting the impact of treatment. The impact of prior anti-resorptive therapy could not be fully assessed, due to incomplete data (missing in 41% of cases). No correlation was found between BMI and TA, which may reflect a combination of limited BMI variability, modest sample size and the fact that HU measure volumetric TA, which is less influenced by body habitus than areal BMD by DXA. Previous studies have shown that lower L1 CT attenuation is associated with vertebral fractures,21 and our findings with FCh-PET/CT are in line with these results. Various thresholds have been proposed to assess vertebral fracture risk.21 22 24 In our PCa cohort, the 90 HU cut-off proposed by Graffy et al effectively differentiated patients with and without fractures. At present, HU thresholds should be considered a triage tool to identify patients who may benefit from further clinical assessment and DXA evaluation, rather than as a treatment threshold, since HU values are scanner- and protocol-dependent and current data are insufficient to predict fracture risk.

Despite being included in guidelines, fracture risk assessment remains underused in PCa patients, and DXA is rarely performed. Since PET/CT scans are frequently available at diagnosis and staging, this opportunistic screening approach provides a clinically useful tool for BMD estimation and fracture risk stratification without the need for additional examinations.

This study has some limitations. The sample size was relatively small but sufficient to confirm feasibility and initial validity of the approach. The association between TA and fracture risk requires validation in larger, prospective cohorts. The use of diagnostic CT rather than DXA or Quantitative Computed Tomography (QCT) as a comparator reflects retrospective data availability, but aligns with our feasibility objective within existing imaging workflows. TA measurements were performed manually, which is time-consuming. Future developments should focus on automated vertebral level detection, robust ROI placement, artefact exclusion, contrast detection and protocol harmonisation to enable fully automated, opportunistic screening within PET/CT workflows, including PSMA-PET/CT.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the feasibility of using low-dose CT associated with FCh-PET for opportunistic osteoporosis screening in PCa patients. We propose a simple, accessible measurement extracted from the CT component of FCh-PET imaging, an increasingly used modality for PCa staging, to identify patients at high risk of osteoporotic fractures without the need for DXA. This opportunistic strategy could raise clinician awareness of osteoporosis risk in PCa and facilitate implementation of preventive treatments. Prospective studies are warranted to confirm the clinical utility of this approach, its integration into routine FCh-PET/CT interpretation and its impact on osteoporosis management in PCa patients.

The complete chloroplast genome and phylogenetic analysis of a rare species Schisandra macrocarpa (Schisandraceae)
Authors: Huanhuan Chen, Nawaz Haider Bashir, Lei Zhu, Tong-Xing Zhao, Tao Xu, Yong Gao, Yong-Kang Sima
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSchisandra macrocarpa Lin & Shui, 2011, belongs to the family Schisandraceae and was recently added to the List of National Key Protected Wild Plants in China. We report the complete chloroplast (cp) genome of S. macrocarpa using Illumina Nova-Seq 6000 platform. The results showed that the cp genome size of S. macrocarpa was 144,288 bp, which contained a small single-copy (SSC) region (17,862 bp), a large single-copy (LSC) region (93,332 bp), and a pair of inverted repeat (IR) regions (16,547 bp). The cp genome has 40% overall GC content. Annotated genes were 128 in all, including 83 protein-coding genes, eight rRNA genes, and 37 tRNA genes. The phylogenetic analysis showed that S. macrocarpa was closely related to S. propinqua subsp. sinensis. This work contributes to the conservation of this species and the evolutionary analysis of Schisandraceae.

Introduction

The genus Schisandra Michaux is a member of the family Schisandraceae (order Austrobaileyales) and it can be found in East Asia and North America, as well as south-west to east China (Xu et al. 2023). In China, the genus Schisandra has 22 species (Xu et al. 2023); among them, several species are extensively utilized in traditional Chinese herbal medicine due to their significant therapeutic potential (Xu et al. 2008; Gao and Liu 2010). Under Schisandra subgenus Sphaerostema (Blume) Y. W. Law, a new species, Schisandra macrocarpa Lin & Shui, from Yunnan Province, China was described (Lin et al. 2011), and in 2021, this species was recorded on the List of National Key Protected Wild Plants in China (Su et al. 2024). A natural potent HMG-CoA for lowering cholesterol was discovered from S. macrocarpa (Su et al. 2024). Within Schisandra subgenus Sphaerostema, this species can be identified and separated from other species by its leaves (ovate-elliptic), flowers (ramiflorous or cauliflorous), and large seed size (8–10 mm long and 10–12 mm wide) (Lin et al. 2011).

According to recent molecular systematics research, Schisandraceae is divided into two main clades: one is made up only of Schisandra species, while the other includes both Schisandra and Kadsura species (Liu et al. 2006; Wang et al. 2006). The most recent classification of the genus Schisandra was proposed by Lin and Yang (2007), in which this genus was divided into two subgenera: Schisandra and Sphaerostema. Phylogenetic analyses conducted in the past suggest that neither Kadsura nor Schisandra belong to a monophyletic group. Furthermore, the systematic position of the genus Schisandra in the angiosperm phylogeny has drawn the attention of numerous specialists and scholars to its classification. Previous research on S. macrocarpa has mostly been about its medicinal values (Su et al. 2024) and not much about its evolutionary relationships within the genus Schisandra. Currently, there is no complete chloroplast (cp) genome available for S. macrocarpa, which would be useful information for investigating evolutionary relationships within the family Schisandraceae and a basis for further research.

Materials and methods

Fresh leaves of S. macrocarpa (Figure 1) were collected from Lianhuatan Township, Hekou County, Yunnan Province, China (22°53′49″N, 103°33′07″E). The fresh leaves were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until further use. The voucher specimen (Y.-K. Sima & Y.-M. Shui 99320) is preserved in the Herbarium of Yunnan Academy of Forestry & Grassland Science (formerly Yunnan Academy of Forestry) and YAF (https://sweetgum.nybg.org/science/ih/herbarium-details/?irn=127096, Yong-Kang Sima, simayk@163.com).

Morphology features of Schisandra macrocarpa: (A) seeds; (B) leaves; (C) fruits (source: https://ppbc.iplant.cn/). The most characteristic feature of this species: leaves elliptic or ovate-elliptic; flowers axillary, ramiflorous, or cauliflorous; thecae introrse, sessile; 3–8 stamens; 20–30 carpels.

Total genomic DNA was extracted using Doyle and Doyle’s (1987) method. The DNA quality and quantity were determined by NanoDrop 2000 micro spectrophotometer. Using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform, paired-end reads with a length of 150 bp were created (Illumina, San Diego, CA). The sequencing produced approximately 2.8 GB of clean data. With adaptor trimming, approximately 18.8 million high-quality clear reads were generated in total. The CPGAVAS2 online web server (http://www.herbalgenomics.org/cpgavas2) and Geseq software were used to annotate the assembled cp genome, and then the results were compared with each other to obtain the final annotation file. Using the Chloroplast Genome Viewer (CPGView, http://www.1kmpg.cn/cpgview/), the circular map of the cp genome was visualized. The coverage depth for each base was determined by aligning all high-quality reads to the completed cp genome using SAMtools (Danecek et al. 2021) and BWA-MEM (Li 2013). The software tool CPGview (Liu et al. 2023) was utilized to produce cis-spliced and trans-spliced genes.

To determine the phylogenetic position of S. macrocarpa within the family Schisandraceae, we obtained 20 complete cp genomes from the National Biotechnology Information Center (NCBI) GenBank (17 Schisandraceae species) and used Cabomba furcate (MG967470), Nymphaea pubescens (OQ161187), and Trithuria lanterna (MN365661) as the out-group. We used the MAFFT software (Katoh and Standley 2013) to align the complete cp genome sequences of S. macrocarpa and 20 additional species. Then, we conducted a phylogenetic analysis using the maximum-likelihood (ML) method with IQ-TREE 1.6.8 (Nguyen et al. 2015), selecting the best fit model (GTR + F + I + G4) from ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017), and employing 1000 bootstrap replicates.

Results

The whole cp genome of S. macrocarpa (accession number: PP706075) has a length of 144,288 bp and comprises a small single-copy region (SSC, 17,862 bp), a large single-copy region (LSC, 93,332 bp), and two inverted repeats (IRs, 16,547 bp each) (Figure 2). The overall depths of coverage for the assembled genome are illustrated in Figure S1. The overall GC content was 40% for the S. macrocarpa cp genome. The genome contains 128 genes, of which 83 are protein-coding genes (79 are unique), 37 are tRNA genes (31 are unique), and eight are rRNA genes (four are unique). Genome has 23 genes with introns, of which four genes have two introns (ycf3, clpP, and two rps12), and 19 genes have one intron (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, petB, petD, rpl2, two ndhB, ndhA, trnH-GUG, trnK-UUU, trnS-CGA, trnG-UCC, trnV-UAC, trnL-UAA, two trnI-GAU, and two trnA-UGC). The genome contained 11 cis-splicing genes (Figure S2) and one trans-splicing gene (Figure S3). We obtained the complete cp genomes of 10 Schisandra species from the NCBI GenBank database in order to examine the evolutionary relationships between S. macrocarpa and its related taxa. The phylogenetic tree was then constructed using a ML analysis (Figure 3). Phylogenetic analysis revealed that S. macrocarpa was clustered with S. propinqua subsp. sinensis.

Discussion and conclusions

Chloroplast genomes are commonly utilized in phylogenetic analysis (Li et al. 2021), yet the Schisandra macrocarpa cp genome had not been documented until now. In the current study, we reported the complete cp genome of S. macrocarpa for the first time. The structure was circular and had a complete size of 144,288 base pairs, containing a total of 128 genes. Molecular phylogenetic analysis of 21 complete cp genomes showed that S. macrocarpa and S. propinqua subsp. sinensis are related to each other in the genus Schisandra. The cp genome of closely related species S. propinqua subsp. sinensis (accession number: MZ846205) has a length of 145,562 bp (Zhang et al. 2024), which is 1274 bp larger than the cp length of S. macrocarpa. Schisandra propinqua is distributed in several provinces in China (Shaanxi, Gansu, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Sichuan, Guizhou, and Yunnan), while S. macrocarpa is only reported from Yunnan province (Xu et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024). Here also, both subgenera Schisandra and Kadsura are not monophyletic, as previous findings (Liu et al. 2006; Wang et al. 2006; Zhang et al. 2024).

The cp genome of S. macrocarpa was identified and analyzed in the current study, offering valuable information that can support conservation efforts, utilization, and phylogenetic studies within the Schisandraceae family.

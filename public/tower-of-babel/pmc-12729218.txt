Novel separable magnetically-controlled forceps system assisted single-port laparoscopic cholecystectomy: an initial experience sharing with a case report
Authors: Zhenyu Yang, Haoran Li, Qibo Wang, Shujia Peng, Guoqiang Bao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Therefore, a novel magnetically controlled separable forceps system was developed to improve the performance of SPLC. Introduction

Conventional multi-port laparoscopic cholecystectomy, typically utilizing three or four ports, remains the gold standard for treating benign gallbladder diseases [1]. However, the evolution of minimally invasive and aesthetic surgery has introduced new challenges for surgeons, including single-incision laparoscopic surgery [2, 3], natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery [4, 5], and robotic surgical platforms [6]. In particular, laparoscopic cholecystectomy with a single port (SPLC) has gained popularity as a potential alternative to conventional multiport laparoscopic cholecystectomy due to its less invasive nature and cosmetic advantages [2, 7]. Recent advancements in magnetic surgery have shown promise and have been successfully applied in various surgical procedures, including bariatric [8–10], cholecystectomy [11] and colorectal surgery [12]. Magnetic-assisted laparoscopic surgery has proven to be safe, feasible and effective in both pediatric and adult populations [13].

To address these challenges, our team has independently designed and developed novel separable magnetically controlled forceps, which have been experimentally utilized in laparoscopic cholecystectomy with reduced ports and SPLC. In this report, we present our experience with the application of these forceps through the umbilical port for SPLC (Fig. 1).

Materials and methods

Description of the novel separated magnetic-controlled forceps system

Our team independently developed a novel third-generation separable magnetically controlled forceps system and reusable first-generation external magnets for intraperitoneal cephalad traction of the gallbladder. The novel third-generation separable magnetically controlled forceps system comprises a forceps body and a one-time-use separable magnetic grasper (Fig. 2). The grasper measures 4.5 cm in length and can be opened to 20 mm (Fig. 2A-C). Additionally, it is deployable and crafted from a cylindrical composite magnet with a diameter of 9 mm and a height of 10 mm, featuring a magnetic flux of 5211 Gs (Fig. 2D-F). The forceps body is inserted into the abdominal cavity via a 12 mm Trocar orifice. The first-generation external magnet is constructed from a cylindrical samarium-cobalt composite magnet, with a diameter of 80 mm and a height of 40 mm (Fig. 3A). It is nickel-plated, exhibits axial saturation magnetization, and boasts a magnetic flux of 4660 Gs (Fig. 3B). Enclosed within a 2 mm thick stainless-steel shell (Fig. 3C), the retraction strength of first-generation external magnets correlates directly with the thickness of the abdominal wall of the patient. The detachable magnetic grasper affixed to the forceps body and introduced into the abdominal cavity by compressing the handle on the forceps body shaft. The detachable magnetic grasper effectively grasps the gallbladder body and separates it from the forceps body shaft. Moreover, the detachable magnetic grasper can be flexibly maneuvered to alter the retraction direction by adjusting the position of the external magnet (Fig. 3D-E). Depending on the surgical requirements, it can also be repositioned based on the forceps’ magnetic attraction strength of the body. After sterile wrapping, an external magnet is positioned on the right upper abdomen or chest of the patient. It is then adjusted until the magnetic grasper achieves adequate gallbladder retraction.

Animals experiments

In the initial phase of our study, utilizing this innovative device, we successfully conducted animal experiments involving reduction-hole laparoscopic cholecystectomy (Fig. 4A-C), laparoscopic partial liver resection (Fig. 4D-E), laparoscopic partial stomach resection (Fig. 4F-G), Laparoscopic partial cystectomy (4H) and partial small bowel resection (4I) in Bama miniature pigs. Two healthy Bama miniature pigs, weighing 35–40 kg, were purchased from Western Mars (Shaanxi) Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The pigs were fed low-fat diet before surgery, and perioperative management complied with animal welfare and ethical principles. On the day of surgery, the animals were anesthetized with ketamine (10% with a dose of 0.25 ml/kg) intramuscular injection. After endotracheal intubation, the animals were transferred to the operating room and connected to a ventilator. Continuous intravenous infusion of propofol (0.1–0.4.1.4 mg/kg/min) combined with constant isoflurane (1.5–2.5 vol%) and oxygen (50 vol%) with a fresh gas flow rate of 1 L/min inhalation anesthesia was maintained during the surgery. This study was approved by the Tab of Animal Experimental Welfare & Ethical Inspection of our Institution (Number:20230762). The experiment was performed according to the Basel Declaration and international ethical standards for animal research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) guidelines.

The findings indicated that the novel separable magnetic forceps were both safe and effective for laparoscopic surgeries involving the liver, gallbladder, and gastrointestinal tract. The device provided excellent traction and exposure of the surgical field, facilitating the execution of reduction-hole minimally invasive surgeries. Furthermore, surgeons reported high levels of satisfaction with the device’s performance in terms of opening and closing, clamping, separation, recovery, and exposure of the surgical area. No adverse events were observed, and preliminary validation suggests that this innovative device holds significant clinical application potential. The average intraoperative blood loss was less than 10 ml. The Bama miniature pigs recovered well after surgery, and no serious complications such as unexpected death occurred during the perioperative period. All experimental animals survived for more than 8 h after surgery, and were subsequently euthanized with fast intravenous injections of propofol (200 mg, as an overdose of anesthetics). Based on the findings from the animal studies, this report details our experience with the application of this innovative device in transumbilical SPLC.

Patient information

The patient, a 52-year-old male with a body mass index of 21.82, was admitted following the discovery of gallstones during a routine physical examination two years ago. Laparoscopic cholecystectomy was planned, and a subumbilical SPLC was performed. All procedures adhered to the ethical standards of the institution and the Declaration of Helsinki (revised in 2013). Written informed consent was obtained from the patient.

Operative technique

A standardized single-incision laparoscopic technique was employed for performing SPLC under general anesthesia with endotracheal intubation (Fig. 5A). A magnetic arm and an external magnet were affixed to the surgical bed on the side of the assistant and enveloped in a sterile plastic sleeve. Pneumoperitoneum was established using a Veress needle at a pressure of 13 mmHg. Single-port access, consisting of two 12-mm orifices and one 5-mm orifice (SILS port ⅢA-3E-50 × 150, Surgaid, Xiamen, China), was established through a 2.5-cm transverse incision at the lower edge of the umbilicus without the need for additional trocars (Fig. 5B). Following port placement, a visual inspection was performed to verify the integrity of the abdominal cavity and confirm its suitability for SPLC. The 12-mm orifice accommodated a 30° laparoscope and various energy sources, including novel separable magnetically controlled forceps for intraperitoneal retraction of the gallbladder, absorbable bioclips for cystic duct and cystic artery management, and flexible laparoscopic electrocoagulation equipment in the dominant operating orifice. Occasionally, the 5-mm orifice was utilized, housing gallbladder grasping forceps manipulated with the left hand to enhance visualization of the operative field.

A standardized SPLC procedure assisted by the novel separated magnetically controlled forceps system. (A) Depiction of the surgical scene with the positioning of the external anchoring magnet; (B) Orientation and alignment of the disposable single-port laparoscopic incision protector; (C) Retraction of the gallbladder facilitated by the separated magnetic grasper; (D/E) Surgical steps involving the dissection of Calot’s triangle and the clipping of the cystic duct and artery; (F/G) Dissociation and removal of the gallbladder from the liver bed using an electrocoagulation hook; (H) Retrieval of the gallbladder specimen through the umbilical incision; (I) Closure of the incision using sutures

Initially, novel third-generation separable magnetically controlled forceps were inserted into the abdominal cavity through the 12-mm orifice of the single-port device and deployed onto the gallbladder neck to expose the surgical field (Fig. 5C). Subsequently, the magnetic grasper was detached from the forcep body shaft by squeezing and pushing a special structure onto the tail of the forceps. Gallbladder retraction was initiated using a separate magnetic grasper coupled with an external magnet affixed to the right hypochondrium. The detached magnetic grasper provided a significantly wider range of motion within the peritoneal cavity, facilitated by the flexible movement of the external magnet. Once positioned, attention was directed toward the dissection of the Calots triangle and clipping of the cystic duct and artery, following the established steps of conventional SPLC (Fig. 5D-E). The gallbladder was then dissected from the liver bed using an electrocoagulation hook (Fig. 5F-G). Throughout this process, retraction direction and strength were manipulated by adjusting the position of the external magnet and by reattaching and redeploying the clamp position of the grasper. These instruments aimed to optimize anatomical visualization and address key challenges associated with single-port surgical approaches, particularly the limitations related to achieving adequate field of vision and the restricted capacity for introducing multiple instruments through the single-port device, which typically offers only three working ports.

Following complete gallbladder dissection, the external magnet was removed and decoupled from the internal grasper. Internally, the grasper was reattached to the forceps` shaft and withdrawn from the peritoneal cavity. The excised gallbladder specimen was retrieved through the umbilical incision (Fig. 5H), and the incision was closed using absorbable sutures (Fig. 5I).

Results

The procedure was completed within 45 min. Importantly, no pre- or postoperative complications were recorded, and the use of postoperative drains was unnecessary. There were no adverse events such as a detached clamp head magnet, injury at the abdominal wall contact site, or failure to retrieve the clamp head. No additional auxiliary holes were made during surgery. Surgeons were highly satisfied with the performance of the device in terms of opening and closing, gripping, separation, and surgical field exposure. They were generally satisfied with the rotation performance, tissue handling, convenience of moving the external anchoring magnet, and instrument table management. The patient was discharged 8 h after the surgical intervention, and subsequent follow-up was uneventful for 30 days postoperatively.

Discussion

Retraction plays a fundamental and significant role in surgical procedures, particularly in the context of laparoscopic surgery, where each retraction forcep requires a port penetrating the abdominal wall [14]. The technique of laparoscopic surgery utilizing a single port emerged as an advancement in the field of laparoscopy [15]. Notably, various single-port techniques for laparoscopic cholecystectomy have evolved [16]. However, the presence of the “chopsticks effect” and inadequate retraction field imposes constraints on its ability to handle delicate procedures [17]. Previous studies have demonstrated notable enhancements in the efficacy of SPLC using methods such as suture suspension [18, 19], flexible laparoscopic instruments [20], and magnetic anchors [21]. Nevertheless, the widespread adoption of these techniques in clinical practice has been hindered by challenges such as laborious equipment production and intricate operational procedures.

In this academic report, we introduce a novel and autonomously developed " separable magnetically controlled forceps system” designed to assist in cholecystectomy procedures conducted via a single-port access. Our research team conceived and executed an innovative surgical instrument system incorporating electromagnetic-modulated control. This pioneering design and invention have arisen from advancements in surgical concepts, magnetic instruments, and minimally invasive surgery technology. The unrestricted direction and range of retraction, independent of fixed access ports on the abdominal wall, further enhance the potential benefits of this surgical approach.

In 2001, the introduction of permanent magnetic materials sparked a notable rise in the application of magnetic devices within the global medical community. Consequently, research institutions focusing on “magnetic surgery” emerged, leading to the development of various advanced magnetic surgery technologies and products [3, 22]. Furthermore, “magnetic surgery” has emerged as a prominent area of study in endoscopy, encompassing magnetic-assisted surgery as an innovative technology for minimally invasive surgical procedures [23–25]. Over the course of more than a decade, the integration of magnetic assistance in single-port cholecystectomy has emerged as a surgical technique leveraging magnetic properties to grasp the gallbladder fundus, thus facilitating the exposure to Calot’s triangle [26]. In 2009, neodymium magnetic forceps were introduced, and the surgical technique was detailed in 40 single-port laparoscopic cholecystectomies [27]. The Levita™ Magnetic Surgical System (Levita™ Magnetics Corporation, San Mateo, CA, USA) became the first magnetic system to receive Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for use during laparoscopic cholecystectomy in June 2016 [11]. However, similar devices or related indications have not yet received approval for use in China.

In our case, the utilization of a separable magnetically controlled forceps provided adequate tissue retraction and facilitated the identification of critical anatomy, thereby eliminating the necessity for additional trocars to expose the surgical field. Moreover, this system effectively addresses various challenges encountered in the advancement of minimally invasive surgery, including the need for adequate tissue retraction, instrument articulation, and optimal visualization [11]. Additionally, the use of these forceps demonstrated safety, as no perioperative adverse events were observed. Based on our preliminary findings and the existing literature, we concluded that using separable magnetically controlled forceps is secure and feasible in the context of SPLC. Furthermore, we developed a standardized protocol for magnetically assisted SPLC.

The successful completion of the operation can be attributed to the utilization of a highly versatile single-port system [28]. This unique single-port access method offers numerous advantages over traditional laparoscopic methods. These advantages include enhanced aesthetic outcomes, potential reduction in postoperative pain, mitigation of bleeding caused by additional trocars, and improved closure of the abdominal cavity, thereby minimizing the risk of post-trocar hernias [29].

The use of separable magnetically controlled forceps offers several advantages. Firstly, it effectively replaces the grasping function of the gallbladder grasper in traditional laparoscopic cholecystectomy, improving the surgical field of vision during dissection overcoming the limitations associated with the ‘chopstick effect’ observed in previous single-incision surgeries with less instrument coming from and being controlled from the same port of entry [17]. Additionally, it enhances the maneuverability of the primary electric coagulation hook in a single-incision puncture device. Thus, there can be an improvement in the existing SPLC techniques due to the ease of maneuverability of retraction and improvement in field of vision.

Secondly, the concurrent use of the electrocoagulation device and auxiliary grasping forceps within single-port access enhances visual field exposure, facilitating intricate cholecystectomy procedures such as those involving hypertrophy of the liver quadrate lobe and challenging exposure of Calot’s triangle. Thirdly, these distinct magnetically controlled forceps feature flexible detachment and reattachment capabilities, contributing to a seamless and satisfactory surgical procedure. Fourthly, implementing multisite and multiangle tissue retraction can effectively accommodate diverse scenarios, addressing the limitations of the suture suspension method. Lastly, the learning curve for SPLC may be diminished for surgeons due to its minimal technical prerequisites.

It is important to acknowledge the limitations of this particular in SPLC. Firstly, the absence of advanced and adaptable electrocoagulation equipment, as well as extended gallbladder grasping forceps, resulted in interference between the operator’s hand and the light source line, thereby hindering the operator’s performance. Secondly, the patient’s slim physique and the absence of acute cholecystitis or prior abdominal surgery made the procedure relatively uncomplicated and not fully representative of more complex cases. Finally, the presence of intraabdominal metal implants may contraindicate this procedure. Nevertheless, the successful integration of this system within the SPLC not only significantly contributes to the advancement of single-port laparoscopic surgery technology but also serves as a platform for exploring novel methodologies in magnetic and endoscopic surgery.

Conclusion

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy performed through a single-port approach using magnetically controlled forceps has demonstrated feasibility and safety, particularly in the hands of experienced surgeons. These advanced forceps enable access to the abdominal cavity, without additional ports, potentially minimizing invasiveness. The utilization of this novel separable magnetically controlled forceps retraction system can be an improvement in the existing SPLC techniques due to the ease of maneuverability of retraction and improvement in field of vision and shows promise, particularly within the context of surgical practices in China.

Method for predicting the price of carbon based on quadratic decomposition and multiscale prediction
Authors: Yonghui Duan, Kaige Liu, Xiang Wang, Xiaotong Zhang, Yingying Fan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEstablishing an effective carbon price forecasting model is crucial for promoting the stable development and effective management of carbon trading markets. To enhance forecasting accuracy, this study proposes a hybrid carbon price prediction model based on secondary decomposition and multi-scale forecasting. First, a WOA-XGBoost model is constructed for the initial carbon price prediction. Then, the residuals of the initial prediction are decomposed using Complete Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition with Adaptive Noise (CEEMDAN), and the component with the highest fuzzy entropy (IMF1) is further decomposed using Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD). The residual components are then reorganized based on their frequency characteristics. Subsequently, different explanatory variables are introduced to model the high- and low-frequency sequences separately. Finally, the prediction results of each sequence are aggregated to obtain the final composite forecast of carbon prices.The results show that: (1) compared with benchmark models, the proposed hybrid model achieves the best overall forecasting performance, with MAE values of 0.0006 and 0.0013 and R2 values of 0.9999 in the Hubei and EU carbon markets, respectively; (2) historical carbon prices are the most influential factor in carbon price forecasting. The Baidu Index contributes most significantly in the Hubei market, while the German DAX index has the greatest impact on the EU carbon market. This model framework provides high-precision quantitative support for carbon allowance pricing, policy evaluation, and cross-market linkage analysis, thereby facilitating the transition of carbon markets toward refined governance and global coordinated emission reduction, and promoting green and sustainable development.Keywords: Carbon price prediction, Secondary decomposition, Multi-scale forecasting, Sustainable development

Introduction

In recent years, climate change caused by global warming has become one of the most severe ecological and environmental challenges. Low-carbon economies and green development have gradually become key trends in the economic strategies of countries worldwide. To date, more than 70 countries have pledged to achieve net-zero carbon emissions by 2050. In parallel, China has explicitly committed to reaching peak carbon emissions before 2030 and striving to achieve carbon neutrality by 2060 [1]. To promote low-carbon development, China launched seven national carbon emission trading pilot markets in 2017. Against this backdrop, establishing an effective carbon emissions trading system is of great significance for achieving peak carbon emissions.

The efficiency of the design and management system of the carbon market mechanism is directly reflected in the price of carbon trading. In addition to lowering market participant risk and preventing the loss of carbon assets, accurate carbon price forecasting is essential for the government to create carbon emission quota policies that work [2]. Effective carbon price prediction is difficult, nevertheless, because the carbon price itself exhibits non-stationarity and non-linearity [3]. In order to increase the accuracy of carbon price prediction results, it is crucial to build a more robust and efficient framework [4].

The research currently in publication indicates that econometric, artificial intelligence, and hybrid forecasting techniques are frequently employed for predicting the price of carbon. The common statistical methods mainly include autoregressive integrated moving average model (ARIMA) [5], gray model (GM) [6], and generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity model (GARCH) [7]. For example, Wu [8] used the ARIMA model to analyze the price of carbon emission rights in Shanghai, and concluded that the fitting effect of ARIMA can well simulate the trend of the price of carbon trading rights in Shanghai; Dutta (2018) [9] used the exponential GARCH model for the prediction of the volatility of the carbon price, and through the treatment of outliers and considering the impact of the crude oil volatility index, it can significantly improve the accuracy of carbon price volatility prediction.Since the time series of the carbon price is a classical type of unstable series, it is difficult for statistical methods to accurately capture the nonlinear pattern of the carbon price [10]. As a result, it is challenging to capture more useful carbon price prediction information by using only econometric models. Statistical models are based on complete statistical theory.

Because of their superior capacity to model nonlinear time series, strong generalization, and higher predictive accuracy, machine-learning approaches have been increasingly adopted to address the limitations of statistical models in forecasting domains [11–13], particularly in carbon-price prediction research.Through enhanced multi-objective particle swarm algorithm optimization, Li et al. [14] developed a heterogeneous ensemble forecasting model for nonlinear time series and confirmed that, to some extent, the machine learning model outperformed conventional statistical models; Through empirical analysis, Zhu et al. [15] demonstrated that their particle swarm optimization least squares support vector machine model, which combined a hybrid kernel function and a multi-objective fitness function, outperformed the widely used models in terms of trading performance and forecasting accuracy; Li et al. [16] predicted the carbon price in Guangdong and Hubei, China, using the long-and short-term memory network (LSTM) model. By comparing this model to other models, they were able to confirm its advantages in terms of prediction accuracy.

A single AI model has the intrinsic drawback of being examined solely from one angle and lacking adequate predictive stability, even if AI models outperform conventional statistical models in forecasting nonlinear and non-stationary data.For these reasons, hybrid models have been widely proposed, and the “decomposition-integration “ framework has received considerable attention.Chai et al. [17] demonstrated the effectiveness of the model by predicting the carbon price of China’s ETS pilot through variational modal decomposition (VMD) and particle swarm optimization. Zhou et al. [18] developed various combination models to forecast distinct frequency components and suggested a multi-scale integrated prediction model based on ensemble empirical modal decomposition (EEMD); Zhang et al. [19] proposed a model based on fully integrated adaptive empirical modal decomposition of noise (CEEMDAN) and window-based XGBoost method for carbon trading price prediction, and verified the superiority of the model in terms of prediction accuracy in comparison with other models through empirical analysis.A hybrid HI-TVFEMD–Transformer model was introduced by Wang et al. [20], in which outlier identification, modal decomposition, and deep learning were integrated to enable multi-step carbon-price forecasting and the quantification of predictive uncertainty. Likewise, Liu et al. proposed a CEEMDAN–IWOA–LSTM ensemble in which modal decomposition is coupled with intelligent optimisation, thereby improving the accuracy and robustness of short-term electricity-load predictions.

In conclusion, there are still some possible research gaps despite the fact that the current body of work on carbon price prediction has done a great deal of worthwhile work.In most existing studies [21, 22], a decomposition-ensemble paradigm has been adopted. Although this scheme improves forecasting performance to a certain extent, single-stage decompositions are prone to mode mixing and overfitting. Moreover, the differential influence exerted by explanatory variables on high- versus low-frequency components is seldom accounted for, leading to uneven variable contributions across components and leaving substantial room for further accuracy gains.

Therefore, based on the research results of Liu et al. [23], this paper proposes a multiscale prediction model based on CEEMDAN-VMD quadratic decomposition, which can more accurately identify the main influencing factors of different frequency components by independently predicting the high-frequency and low-frequency components, so as to improve the overall prediction accuracy of the model. On the other hand, few researchers have included the Baidu index and investor mood as explanatory variables, despite the fact that certain studies [24, 25] have verified the causal influence of exogenous factors on carbon pricing. Research has demonstrated that investor sentiment and worries impact investors’ knowledge bases, assumptions, and expectations regarding assets [26], which in turn influences the carbon market’s future trajectory. Inspired by Wang et al. [27], this paper examines the Baidu index from a big data perspective and incorporates external factors for feature screening. This enhances the model’s explanatory power and prediction accuracy, enriches the information set, and offers a fresh viewpoint for carbon market research and management.

This paper’s contributions are as follows: (1) The decomposed series are rearranged into high and low frequency series according to fuzzy entropy, and suitable influencing factors are chosen for each series. This helps decision makers better understand the dynamics of the market and the precise mechanism of various variables on the fluctuation of the carbon price. In contrast to the conventional approach of "decomposition and then forecasting" [28, 29], this paper first generates preliminary forecasts to accurately capture the primary trends and cyclical features of the carbon price series. Then, it breaks down the residuals and generates independent forecasts of the decomposed residuals, allowing us to concentrate more on handling the noise and uncaptured nonlinear components while also enhancing the forecasting model’s precision and accuracy. enhancing the prediction model’s precision and capacity for generalization.(2) The framework takes into account a variety of influencing factors, particularly the Baidu index, which enhances the model’s explanatory power for changes in the price of carbon by offering a more comprehensive source of data for forecasting. In contrast to previous research [30], this study presents a more extensive collection of past carbon prices and external determinants, and performs two distinct feature screenings prior to and following decomposition. This method enables more focused data gathering and processing, which improves the model’s resilience and flexibility.

The remainder of this research is structured as follows: In Part II, the pertinent theoretical underpinnings are introduced; in Part III, the established indicator system and evaluation criteria are described; in Part IV, the two cases of the European Union and Hubei are introduced, with an emphasis on the experimental process of the prediction model and the comparative analysis of the experimental results; in Part V, the discussion is held; and in Part VI, the key findings of this study are summarized.

WOA-XGBoost-CEEMDAN-VMD

To enhance carbon-price forecasting, a hybrid WOA-XGBoost-CEEMDAN-VMD framework is developed by integrating CEEMDAN, VMD, the whale-optimization algorithm (WOA), and XGBoost. Historical carbon prices, together with pertinent exogenous variables, are first fed into an XGBoost model whose hyperparameters have been tuned via WOA, yielding baseline forecasts and the corresponding residual series. The residuals are subsequently decomposed into multiple intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) by complete ensemble empirical mode decomposition with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN). Because the first component (IMF1) exhibits pronounced non-stationarity and complexity, it is further decomposed by variational mode decomposition (VMD). Fuzzy entropy is then calculated for every component; those with higher entropy are classified as high-frequency, whereas those with lower entropy are treated as low-frequency. Four aggregate sequences (S1-S4) are thereby reconstructed. After feature selection, each sequence is re-modelled with the WOA-optimised XGBoost to generate secondary forecasts. The final prediction is obtained by aggregating the outputs of these four sub-models with the initial baseline estimate. The overall workflow is depicted in Fig. 1.

Data and evaluating indicators

Data sources

Two representative emissions-trading schemes—the Hubei ETS and the European Union ETS—were selected for empirical analysis. The Hubei market, the sole carbon-trading platform in Central China and one of China’s first pilot programmes, has attracted wide attention since its launch in 2014 and has accumulated substantial operational experience [40]. The EU ETS, by contrast, is the world’s largest and most institutionally mature carbon market. Both systems possess well-established trading infrastructures and stringent supervisory frameworks, thereby meeting the study’s need for domestic and international data and enhancing the generalisability of the results.

The Hubei carbon-price series spans 12 September 2016 to 2 May 2024 and contains 1,789 daily observations, whereas the EU series covers 2 January 2018 to 27 March 2024 with 1,577 observations. Line plots are provided in Fig. 2. Thirty percent of these were utilized for model evaluation, while the first seventy percent were used for model development. The chosen training dataset encompasses a whole price cycle, which consists of three distinct market phases: price rise, price decrease, and shock [41].

(1) Associated carbon price.

The characteristics of carbon assets in various markets are comparable, and price changes in domestic carbon market pilots and international carbon pricing, such those of the European Union (EU), may have an effect on the research object. One of the first carbon trading pilots in China, the Hubei carbon market has a leading trading volume and liquidity, while the EU carbon market is one of the biggest carbon markets in the world with a significant carbon price effect and a substantial carbon trading volume. The historical carbon prices of the EU and Hubei are used in this research as their respective related carbon prices. This can show how the values of carbon assets in various markets affect one another and improve the model’s ability to explain variations in cross-regional carbon pricing.

(2) Environmental factors.

Climate change-induced changes in the frequency of extreme weather events might alter the use of fossil fuels, which in turn influences the volatility of carbon prices [41]. Furthermore, the government may implement appropriate steps in response to declining air quality, such as lowering the distribution of carbon emission rights, which may raise carbon pricing. The Air Quality Index (AQI) serves as an environmental indicator in this paper.

(3) Macroeconomics.

Carbon prices are frequently influenced by non-market forces, particularly the positive or negative shocks generated by macro-economic activity. Whether the economy is in a phase of stable expansion or recession can exert a substantial impact [42]. Such external shocks disrupt the normal functioning of carbon markets and induce persistent price volatility. Extensive theoretical and empirical evidence demonstrates that fluctuations in economic activity are a key determinant of allowance prices [43]. During periods of economic boom, heightened aggregate demand and the associated expansion of corporate investment and production raise the demand for emission allowances, thereby pushing carbon prices upward [44].

(4) Energy prices.

Previous research has established that energy prices account for 12% of variations in carbon pricing and are one of the primary determinants of carbon prices [45]. For instance, the cost of natural gas and crude oil. The majority of these research have demonstrated that energy costs significantly affect carbon pricing. The energy market is crucial to the development of carbon prices since the primary source of carbon emissions is the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and natural gas [46].

(5) The Baidu Index.

The importance of big data in human endeavors has grown with the ongoing advancement of Internet technology. In addition to becoming a crucial proxy for investors’ interest, the volume of Internet searches has a significant impact on asset prices [47]. As a gauge of Internet search activity, the Baidu index can reveal how much public and market participants are paying attention to information on a particular carbon market. The market may be expecting stricter carbon emission limits in the future, which could raise demand for carbon allowances and raise the price of carbon. This could be indicated by a notable increase in the frequency of searches for policies or information pertaining to the carbon market, as indicated by the Baidu Index.

Discussion

Comparison of the proposed model with existing models

Numerous studies on carbon prices have been conducted in recent years. This research examines previous hybrid models for carbon price prediction in order to demonstrate the validity of the suggested model. The following is a description of the comparative models:

The VMD-AWLSSVR-PSOALS-WSM model was proposed by Chen et al. [56]. It uses the VMD algorithm to first break down the original carbon price series, then it builds the Attention-Weighted Least Squares Support Vector Regression (AWLSSVR) model for forecasting, and finally it uses the Weighted Summation Model (WSM) for weighted summation of the forecast values to produce final carbon price forecasts.

Rudnik et al. [57] looked into day-ahead carbon price forecasts for the EU carbon market using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) in conjunction with a number of supervised machine learning techniques, such as regression trees, regression tree ensembles, Gaussian Process Regression, Support Vector Machines, and Neural Network Regression. The best-performing model, Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), is employed as a comparative model in this work.

Two evaluation measures, MAE and RMSE, are used in this study to evaluate the suggested model with the comparative model. The temporal horizon of the prediction and the experimental ratio of the training, validation, and test sets are all adjusted to be consistent in order to guarantee the comparison’s rigor. The Hubei carbon market dataset spans the period from January 9, 2018 to June 30, 2021, with training, validation, and test set proportions of 80%, 10%, and 10%, respectively; the EU carbon market dataset spans the period from January 4, 2021, to November 22, 2021, with training and test set proportions of 80% and 20%, respectively. Limitations

The proposed model achieves superior predictive accuracy, materially enhancing carbon-price forecasts and thereby supporting more informed decisions by governments, firms, and investors. Nevertheless, certain limitations persist in terms of model complexity and data dependence.

Because multiple advanced algorithms are integrated, forecasting precision is raised at the cost of greater computational burden and reduced interpretability—factors that constrain operational practicality. In the EU ETS case, average training times under a standard Python 3.11 environment were roughly 184 s for the WOA-XGBoost model, 160 s for WOA-XGBoost-CEEMDAN, and 416 s for the target model; the latter’s accuracy exceeded that of the two baselines by 82.29% and 33.33%, respectively. These results indicate that a context-specific trade-off between complexity and performance remains necessary. Model effectiveness is also highly contingent on data quality; missing values or outliers may erode generalisation, highlighting the need for rigorous preprocessing to preserve robustness.

Future work will aim to streamline the model architecture and bolster interpretability. Simplifying components or adopting more efficient algorithms could lower computational overhead and facilitate real-time deployment. In parallel, explainability techniques such as SHAP or LIME will be incorporated to provide fine-grained local interpretations and enhance transparency in both structure and inference—a feature of particular importance to policymakers and market participants. Finally, external validity will be strengthened by evaluating the model in more diverse market environments to ensure broad applicability.

Strengthen price‐monitoring infrastructure.Historical allowance prices have been shown to be a primary driver of future carbon prices. A comprehensive market database and a systematic trend-detection platform should therefore be established. Market participants ought to be guided to track historical price movements and employ quantitative models for dynamic assessment. Because online behavioural indices (e.g., the Baidu Index) demonstrably influence price formation, governments and enterprises should intensify the monitoring and public disclosure of such data—as well as related policy and pricing information—to raise public awareness and curb sentiment-driven trading risk.

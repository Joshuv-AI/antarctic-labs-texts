Abstract
We introduce in this paper the so-called robust generalized S S -procedure associated with a given robust optimization problem. We provide a primal characterization for the validity of this procedure as well as a dual characterization under the assumption that the decision space is locally convex. We also analyze an extension of the mentioned robust S S -procedure that incorporates a right-hand side function.

Introduction

According to
GL06
, the “
S
-procedure” is the name given in
AG63
- a monograph on the stability of nonlinear automatic control
systems where certain function denoted by
S
(the initial of “stability”)
plays a crucial role - to a method which allows to solve systems of
quadratic inequalities by solving suitable linear matrix inequality
relaxations. Yakubovich was the first one to give sufficient conditions for
these relaxations to be exact in the sense that it is possible to obtain a
solution for these quadratic systems using their corresponding relaxations

Yabu71
. The classic theorem of losslessness of the
S
-procedure (in
short
S
-procedure) characterizes when certain concave quadratic inequality
is consequence of a given system of convex quadratic inequalities in terms
of the concave function being a nonnegative linear combination of the convex
constraints and a suitable trivial constraint. This result allows to get
optimality conditions for convex quadratic problems. In fact, given a family
of convex quadratic functions
q,q_{1},...,q_{m},
and

\overline{x}\in X:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:q_{i}\left(x\right)\leq 0,i=1,...,m\right\},

observing that
\overline{x}
is an optimal solution of
\inf\limits_{x\in X}q\left(x\right)
if and only if the concave inequality
q\left(\overline{x}\right)-q\left(x\right)\leq 0
is consequence of the system of convex
quadratic inequalities
\left\{q_{i}\left(x\right)\leq 0,\;i=1,\ldots,m\right\}.
The classic
S
-procedure can be seen as a nonlinear
version of Farkas’ lemma and has a clear algebraic nature in the sense that
it is closely related with convex analysis and the Lagrange and Fenchel
duality theories for optimization problems.

It is worth observing that certain authors call
S
-lemma to the theorem of
the
S
-procedure when there exists a unique constraint
DP06
, while
other authors prefer to call
S
-lemma to the characterization of the
concave quadratic inequalities which are consequence of systems of convex
quadratic inequalities and
S
-procedure to its application to nonlinear
automatic control systems
PT07
.

The validity of the
{S}_{F}
-
procedure provides
optimality conditions for deterministic optimization problems, but it is
well-known that the data are uncertain in many optimization problems arising
in practice. For instance, in robust optimization the conservative
decision-maker assumes the existence of an uncertainty set
U
and a family
of uncertain functions
f,f_{1},...,f_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},
and considers elements of the robust feasible
set

X:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f_{i}\left(x,u\right)\leq 0,i=1,...,m,u\in U\right\}

in order to guarantee the feasibility of the chosen decision under any
conceivable scenario. Then, the (pessimistic) robust optimization problem to
be solved reads

\inf\limits_{x\in X}\sup\limits_{u\in U}f\left(x,u\right).

Let us show how uncertain quadratic systems arise in astrophysics. Descartes

Descartes1644
conceived in 1644 the tesselation of the space into
regions of
influence
of the fixed stars according to proximities for
the Euclidean distance. In his model, if
T
is the (huge) set of fixed
stars, the region of influence of certain
s\in T
is

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\left\|x-s\right\|\leq\left\|x-t\right\|,\text{ }t\in T\right\}&=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\left\|x-s\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|x-t\right\|^{2},\text{
}t\in T\right\}\\
&=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\left\langle\left(t-s\right),x\right\rangle\leq\tfrac{\left\|t\right\|^{2}-\left\|s\right\|^{2}}{2},t\in T\right\},\end{array}

a polyhedron presently called Voronoi cell of
s
(see a 2D representation
of Descartes’ tesselation in
(
LP12
, Figure 2)
). This model became
obsolete after the publication of Newton’s Principia in 1687
Newton1687
, where he stated the law of universal gravitation that forces
to replace the distance
\left\|x-t\right\|
in the above cell by
the gravitational potential created by the star
t
at
x,
i.e.,
u_{t}\left\|x-t\right\|^{-2},
for
x\neq t,
where
u_{t}
is the
product of the gravitational constant times the mass of star
t.
Assume
that this uncertain scalar ranges on some interval
U_{t}
and define the
uncertainty set
U:=\prod\limits_{t\in T}U_{t}.
For
x\notin T
one has

u_{s}\left\|x-s\right\|^{-2}\geq u_{t}\left\|x-t\right\|^{-2}\Longleftrightarrow u_{t}\left\|x-t\right\|^{2}\leq u_{s}\left\|x-s\right\|^{2},

so that the (uncertain) influence region of
s\in T
writes

\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:q_{t}\left(x,u\right)\leq 0,\text{ }t\in T\right\},

where
q_{t}\left(x,u\right)=u_{t}\left\|x-t\right\|^{2}-u_{s}\left\|x-s\right\|^{2}
is a non-convex (actually D.C.)
quadratic function for all
t\in T.

A primal characterization of the robust
S_{\left\{F_{u}\right\}_{u\in U}}
-procedure

In this section
X
is a given set and
\left\{F_{u}\right)_{u\in U}
is a
family of Rockafellians

F_{u}\colon X\times Y\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}\cup\{-\infty\},\quad\forall u\in U.

Given
G\colon X\times Y\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
we denote by

\mathcal{G}:=\left\{(y,r)\in Y\times\mathbb{R}:\exists x\in X,\;G(x,y)\leq r\right\}

the projection of the epigraph of
G
,

\mathop{\mathrm{e}pi}G:=\left\{(x,y,r)\in X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}:G(x,y)\leq r\right\},

on
Y\times\mathbb{R}
and by

\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}G:=\left\{(x,y)\in X\times Y:G(x,y)<+\infty\right\}

the domain of
G,
that is the projection of
\mathop{\mathrm{e}pi}G
on
X\times Y
.

We set
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,+\infty[
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}=]0,+\infty[.

Lemma 1

Given
G\colon X\times Y\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

G(x,0_{Y})\geq 0,\;\forall x\in X
,

(ii)

(0_{Y},-1)\notin\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}\mathcal{G}
,

(iii)

(0_{Y},-1)\notin\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{G}
.

Proof
“Non (i)” amounts to say
that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\exists x\in X:G(x,0_{Y})<0&\Longleftrightarrow\;\exists(x,\theta)\in X\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}:G(x,0_{Y})\leq-\theta\\
&\Longleftrightarrow\;\exists\theta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}:(0_{Y},-\theta)\in\mathcal{G}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow\;\exists\theta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}:(0_{Y},-1)\in\frac{1}{\theta}\mathcal{G}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow\;(0_{Y},-1)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}\mathcal{G},\end{array}

that is “Non (ii)” or, equivalently,
“Non (iii)”.
\square\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

For a subset
C
of a lcHtvs we denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{c}o}C
its convex hull
and by
\overline{C}
its closure.

Lemma 2

(see
(
VB-24
, Proposition 2.1)
) Let
G\colon X\times Y\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. The following statements are
equivalent

(i)

\exists\lambda\in Y^{\ast}\text{ such that }G(x,y)+\langle\lambda,y\rangle\geq 0,\;\forall(x,y)\in X\times Y
,

(ii)

(0_{Y},-1)\notin\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{c}o}}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{G}\right)
.

Proof
Note that if
\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}G=\emptyset
then (i) and (ii)
are both satisfied. So we can assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}G\neq\emptyset
,
which entails
\mathcal{G}\neq\emptyset.

\left[\text{(i)}\Longrightarrow\text{(ii)}\right]
By (i) there exists
\lambda\in Y^{\ast}
such that

r+\langle\lambda,y\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }(y,r)\in\mathcal{G},

that means
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{e}pi}(-\lambda)
. Now
\mathop{\mathrm{e}pi}(-\lambda)
is a closed convex cone containing
\mathcal{G}
. By this we
have
\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{c}o}}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{G}\right)\subset\mathop{\mathrm{e}pi}(-\lambda)
. Since
(0_{Y},-1)\notin\mathop{\mathrm{e}pi}(-\lambda)

it follows that (ii) holds.

\left[\text{(ii)}\Longrightarrow\text{(i)}\right]
By the Hahn-Banach
separation Theorem (
(
Rudin-91
, Theorem 3.4)
), there exists
(\mu,s)\in Y^{\ast}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

-s=\langle\mu,0_{Y}\rangle-s>\alpha:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\geq 0\\
G(x,y)\leq r\end{subarray}}\theta\left(\langle\mu,y\rangle+rs\right).

We have necessarily

G(x,y)\leq r\;\Longrightarrow\;\langle\mu,y\rangle+rs\leq 0.

Therefore
\alpha\leq 0
and, since
\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}G\neq\emptyset
,
\alpha=0
. Thus
s<0
and, setting
\lambda=\mu/s
, we get that

G(x,y)\leq r\;\Longrightarrow\;\langle\lambda,y\rangle+r\geq 0

and (i) holds.
\square\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

Theorem 2.1

Let
F_{u}\colon X\times Y\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,

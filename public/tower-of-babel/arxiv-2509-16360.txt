Abstract
. Large Language Models (LLMs) hold promise in addressing complex medical problems. However, while most prior studies focus on improving accuracy and reasoning abilities, a significant bottleneck in developing effective healthcare agents lies in the readability of LLM-generated responses, specifically, their ability to answer public health problems clearly and simply to people without medical backgrounds. In this work, we introduce RephQA , a benchmark for evaluating the readability of LLMs in public health question answering (QA). It contains 533 expert-reviewed QA pairs from 27 sources across 13 topics, and includes a proxy multiple-choice task to assess informativeness, along with two readability metrics: Flesch-Kincaid grade level and professional score. Evaluation of 25 LLMs reveals that most fail to meet readability standards, highlighting a gap between reasoning and effective communication. To address this, we explore four readability-enhancing strategies—standard prompting, chain-of-thought prompting, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and a token-adapted variant. Token-adapted GRPO achieves the best results, advancing the development of more practical and user-friendly public health agents. These results represent a step toward building more practical agents for public health.

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable reasoning capabilities in solving complex problems
(
OpenAI et al. 2024a
;
DeepSeek-AI et al. 2024
;
Team et al. 2024b
;
Qiu et al. 2025
;
Zhao et al. 2023
)
.
One of the most critical applications is healthcare, which demands strong domain-specific knowledge
(
Singhal et al. 2023
;
Thirunavukarasu et al. 2023
;
Nazi and Peng 2024
)
.
Previous studies have introduced benchmarks
(
Yao et al. 2024
;
Jin et al. 2019
;
Jiang et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Jin et al. 2019
)
targeting different levels of medical reasoning tasks to be performed by LLMs.

Despite their strong potential in addressing complex medical challenges, the
readability
of LLM-generated responses has been largely overlooked.
Users without medical training often struggle to understand responses filled with jargon, even when the information is accurate. In contrast, high-readability responses can deliver the same content in a more accessible form. While recent LLMs are increasingly optimized for advanced reasoning
(
Shao et al. 2024
)
, it remains unclear whether this progress compromises readability, as more sophisticated reasoning may produce responses that are overly technical for lay audiences.

A key strength of

Different from prior related benchmarks (Table

Besides, we collect a large-scale corpus of 36,060 public health questions to serve as a post-training dataset, supporting the exploration of readability-enhancement strategies.

We evaluate 25 LLMs on

(
Shao et al. 2024
)

Experimental results show that most LLMs fail to produce responses at a desirable readability level.

In summary, our contributions are listed as follows:

•

We introduce
RephQA
for evaluating the readability of LLM-generated responses in public health QA tasks aimed at lay users and patients, with two readability metrics and one accuracy metric evaluated by our proposed proxy multi-choice QA.

•

We conduct a comprehensive evaluation of 25 LLMs on
RephQA
, revealing that most models struggle to generate responses with an expected readability, despite achieving high accuracy.

•

We explore three existing strategies to improve LLM readability and propose a novel method, the token-adapted GRPO method, which achieves the best performance and provides new insights into readability-aware generation.

2.
RephQA
Benchmark

2.1.
Dataset Curation

Expert-Grounded Public Health QA

We collected a diverse set of publicly available healthcare-related QA pairs from 27 authoritative online resources covering a broad range of public health topics, including general health, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, HIV, diet, exercise, alcohol, smoking, sleep, mental health, sexual health, and health equity. Sources were selected for their accessibility to lay audiences and their coverage of frequently asked questions by the general public. The constructed datasets are reviewed and refined by domain experts. Based on the question intent, we classify them into four categories:
Suggestion
,
Fact
,
Definition
,
Rationale
.

Proxy Multiple Choice QA

Prior studies have attempted to use LLMs as judges when rating the accuracy or relevance of a response relative to a reference answer
(
Gu et al. 2024
)
. However, these approaches are not reliable
(
Schroeder and Wood-Doughty 2025
)
. To bridge this gap, we further construct a multiple-choice QA based on each reference answer. Specifically, we design an agent workflow that proposes and iteratively refines the proposed QA pairs. This QA task could be an effective proxy for the accuracy evaluation of the original QA: if a model’s response closely matches the original reference answer, an LLM agent will also produce a correct answer for the proxy QA.

Metrics

We use the Flesch-Kincaid grade level
(
Kincaid et al. 1975
)
as one of the readability scores. We also define professional score as the percentage of professional terms in the text. Accuracy is measured through the aforementioned proxy multiple-choice QA task.

Post-Training Dataset

For the post-training dataset, we curated a large-scale collection consisting of 36,060 public health questions.
These queries span an extensive range of public health topics, including sleep, exercise, smoking, weight, diet, HIV care, mental health, cardiovascular, diabetes, cholesterol, and SOGI, from 55 publicly available resources, which were manually selected by nursing and public health professionals.

2.2.
Readability-Enhancement Strategies

Token-adapted GRPO

Besides benchmarking existing post-training or test-time-scaling methods, we further propose
Token-adapted GRPO
(TA-GRPO). Different from the standard GRPO
(
Shao et al. 2024
)
, which operates at the sentence level, assigning a uniform reward for all the tokens, we assign higher weights for professional medical terms.
Specifically, at each optimization step, the LLM generates a group of responses
\{\boldsymbol{o}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{o}_{G}\}
where
\boldsymbol{o}_{i}
denotes
i
-th generated response. For
t
-th token in
i
-th response, a token-level reward is computed based on whether it appears in our curated professional vocabulary:
r_{i,t}^{\prime}=w_{i,t}Tr_{i}/\sum_{t^{\prime}}^{T}w_{i,t^{\prime}}

where
w_{i,t}=1+\delta(\boldsymbol{o}_{i,t})w
, controling the penalty strength for professional terms, and
r_{i}
is the original sentence-level reward for the response
\boldsymbol{o}_{i}
,
T
is the maximum length of the responses, and
\delta(\boldsymbol{o}_{i,t})
is an indicator function that returns 1 if the token
\boldsymbol{o}_{i,t}
belongs to a professional term, and 0 otherwise. The advantage function is redefined as
A_{i,t}=\frac{r_{i,t}^{\prime}-\operatorname{mean}(\{r_{1},\cdots,r_{G}\})}{\operatorname{std}(\{r_{1},\cdots,r_{G}\})}
.
We use the three metrics in
2.1
as the reward signal. We use the token-adapted objective for the professional score, while retaining the original GRPO for other metrics.

Readability-Enhancement Strategies

As shown in Table
3
, CoT prompting produces less readable responses than others, as indicated by a
32.2\%
increase in grade score and a
57.7\%
increase in professional score.
The accuracy difference between GRPO and TA-GRPO is minimal (
0.3\%
), likely due to the nature of our benchmark, which emphasizes readability over complex reasoning. Both methods significantly improve readability compared to the base model. Moreover, TA-GRPO outperforms vanilla GRPO in professional score optimization, achieving a
53.4\%
average improvement while maintaining a similar grade level (within
3.0\%
).

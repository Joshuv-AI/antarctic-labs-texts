Level structures on cyclic covers of ℙ n {\mathbb{P}}^{n} and the homology of Fermat hypersurfaces
Eduard Looijenga

Abstract
. Let Z ′ ⊂ ℙ n Z^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n} be a smooth projective hypersurface of degree d > 1 d>1 and let Z → ℙ n Z\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n} be the μ d \mu_{d} -cover totally ramified along Z ′ Z^{\prime} . We relate full level d d structures on the primitive cohomology Z ′ Z^{\prime} with full level d d structures on the primitive cohomology of Z Z . In the special case, d = n = 3 d=n=3 this makes a marking of a smooth cubic surface determine a level 3 3 -structure on the associated cubic threefold, thereby answering a question by Beauville [ 2 ] . We expect many more such applications.



Introduction

Let
Z^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be a smooth projective hypersurface of degree
d>1
and let
Z\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be the
\mu_{d}
-cover totally ramified along
Z^{\prime}
. Our main theorem
3.3
states that if we take the
\mu_{d}
-co-invariants of the primitive homology of
Z
and reduce modulo
d
, then this naturally surjects onto the image of the primitive homology of
Z^{\prime}
on the primitive cohomology of
Z^{\prime}
, followed by reduction modulo
d
. When
d
is a prime, we can also make explicit what the kernel of this surjection is (Corollary
3.4
). The case when
Z^{\prime}
is a cubic surface of particular interest because of the way this relates to the ball quotient description of its moduli space by Allcock-Carlson-Toledo
[
1
]
.

The proof is based on two simple maps we associate with a module
M
for the group ring of any finite group
G
(as defined in Section
2
). We apply this to a particular
{\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{d}
-module
M
whose invariants
M^{\mu_{d}}
yield the primitive cohomology of
Z^{\prime}
and for which
M/M^{\mu_{d}}
gives the
primitive homology of
Z
. We also apply this to the dual of
M
for which the dual assertion holds.
We expect this to have similar applications to other instances of a smooth
G
-coverings relating the homology of the cover with that of the branch locus (which coefficients dividing order of the abelianization of
G
).

We expect our main result to generalize with other applications of interest. We justify this in Remark
3.6
, where we briefly discuss the case of a del Pezzo surface of degree 2 as double a cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, a K3 surface of degree 4 as a double cover of del Pezzo surface of degree 2 and a K3 surface of degree 6 as a
\mu_{3}
-cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. These last two covers were used by Kondō for his ball quotient description of the moduli space of genus 3 resp. genus 4 curves. We thus find that these ball quotient descriptions subsist we impose on a level 2 structure on a genus 3 curve or a level 3 structure on a genus 4 curve.

Our main result also leads to a precise description of the (co)primitive cohomology of the smooth Fermat hypersurface as a module over its symmetry group (and thus correct an error in
[
7
]
).
Damián Gvirtz alerted me (back in March 2021) to this (which he and Skorobogatov corrected in the surface case in
[
4
]
) and this was later also observed by Nicholas Addington.

The situation discussed here belongs to a classical theme in algebraic topology. Here the conventional approach is for a space
X
on which a finite group
G
acts, to compare the equivariant cohomology
H_{G}^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(X)
and the cohomology of its fixed point set
H^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(X^{G})
. It would nice to see our main result expressed in these terms and obtained along these lines.

1.
Classical Lefschetz theory with integral coefficients

Let
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree
d>1
.
It is well-known that its integral homology and cohomology is torsion free (the discussion below will reprove this).
Let
\eta\in H^{2}({\mathbb{P}}^{n})
denote the hyperplane class and

\eta_{Z}\in H^{2}(Z)
its restriction to
Z
. Then
\eta_{Z}^{n}
takes on the fundamental class
[Z]\in H_{2n}(Z)
the value
d
.
The
primitive cohomology
of
Z
, denoted here by
H^{P}(Z)
, is the cokernel of the natural map
H^{n}({\mathbb{P}}^{n+1})\to H^{n}(Z)
and dually, its
primitive homology

H_{P}(Z)
as the kernel of

H_{n}(Z)\to H_{n}({\mathbb{P}}^{n+1})
. Both are torsion free and each other’s dual. The composite

\iota_{Z}:H_{P}(Z)\hookrightarrow H_{n}(Z)\cong H^{n}(Z)\twoheadrightarrow H^{P}(Z),

(where the middle map is given by Poincaré duality) defines the intersection pairing restricted to
H_{P}(Z)
.
All these maps are isomorphisms when
n
is odd, whereas for
n
is even the composite is injective with cokernel canonically isomorphic with
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
.
Note that when
n=0
(in which case
Z
is a
d
-element subset of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
), we have identifications

H_{P}(Z)\cong\tilde{H}_{0}(Z)
(the linear combinations of the points on
Z
with zero coefficient sum)
and
H^{P}(Z)=\tilde{H}^{0}(Z)
(the linear combinations of the points on
Z
modulo their sum).

We have a perfect pairing between
H^{P}(Z)
and
H_{P}(Z)
, denoted

(\alpha,b)\in H^{P}(Z)\times H_{P}(Z)\mapsto(\alpha|b)
. This enables us identify one with the other’s dual.
The intersection pairing on
H^{P}(Z)
satisfies
a\cdot b=(\iota_{Z}(a)|b)
.

The following lemma must be well-known.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
Z^{\prime}\subset Z
be smooth hyperplane section and put
\mathring{Z}:=Z\smallsetminus Z^{\prime}
. Then
\mathring{Z}
is a smooth affine hypersurface which has the homotopy type of a wedge of
(d-1)^{n+1}

n
-spheres and we have a short exact sequences fitting in the (self-dual) commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{P}(Z^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{H}_{n}(\mathring{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{P}(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{P}(Z^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{H}^{n}(\mathring{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{P}(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\mathring{Z}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{Z}}

of which the vertical maps are the natural ones (defining the intersection pairing on
\tilde{H}_{n}(\mathring{Z})
). The precomposition of
\tilde{H}_{n}(\mathring{Z})\to\tilde{H}^{n}(\mathring{Z})
with
H^{P}(Z^{\prime})\to\tilde{H}_{n}(\mathring{Z})
and its postcomposition with
\tilde{H}^{n}(\mathring{Z})\to H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
are both zero.

Proof.

Since
\mathring{Z}
is a Milnor fiber of the affine cone over
Z^{\prime}
, its homotopy type is that of a wedge of
(d-1)^{n+1}

n
-spheres whose number is equal to the Milnor number, which is
(d-1)^{n+1}
(
[
9
]
, Thm. 6.5).

We next establish the lower exact sequence.
When
n=0
, this boils down the observation we just made, namely that
H^{P}(Z)\cong\tilde{H}^{0}(\mathring{Z})
.
We therefore assume
n>0
. Then

\tilde{H}^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(\mathring{Z})
is concentrated in degree
n
and hence the Gysin sequence of the pair
(Z,Z^{\prime})
gives the exact sequence

0\to H^{n-2}(Z^{\prime})\to H^{n}(Z)\to H^{n}(\mathring{Z})\to H^{n-1}(Z^{\prime})\to H^{n+1}(Z)\to 0.

If
n=2m
is even with
m>0
, then
H^{n+1}(Z)=0
. Furthermore,
H^{n-2}(Z^{\prime})
is freely generated by
\eta_{Z^{\prime}}^{m-1}
and maps to
\eta_{Z}^{m}\in H^{n}(Z)
.

If
n=2m-1
is odd, then
H^{n-2}(Z^{\prime})=0
and
H^{n+1}(Z)
is freely generated by
\eta_{Z}^{m}
and again the assertion follows.

The proof of the upper exact sequence is similar. The last assertion is seen geometrically: it is induced by the map

H_{n-1}(Z^{\prime})\xrightarrow{L}H_{n}(\mathring{Z})\to H^{n}(\mathring{Z})\xrightarrow{R}H^{n-1}(Z^{\prime}),

where
L
is the Lefschetz tube mapping: it assigns to an
(n-1)
-cycle on
Z^{\prime}
its preimage in a tubular neighbourhood boundary of
Z^{\prime}
in
Z
(which can indeed be understood as an
n
-cycle) and
R

the residue map (which is dual to
L
). The composite of any of these with the middle map is zero. Indeed, the above sequence is self-dual.
∎

Remark 1.2
.

The group
H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
is in fact a
vanishing lattice
. We recall what this means and how this comes about.
Let
{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}
stand for the complete linear system of degree
d
hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. The singular
hypersurfaces are parametrized by the
discriminant hypersurface

D_{n,d}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}
, which is irreducible and whose smooth points define hypersurfaces with a single ordinary double point and no other singularities. We put
\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}:={\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}\smallsetminus D_{n,d}
.
A path
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}
with
\gamma(0)=[Z^{\prime}]
and which hits
D_{n,d}
at its end point for the first time and does so in a smooth point
[Z^{\prime}_{1}]
of
D_{n,d}
, determines a
vanishing cycle

\delta_{\gamma}\in H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
. The path
\gamma
also determines up to homotopy a loop in
\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}
based at
[Z^{\prime}]
circling around
[Z^{\prime}_{1}]
and its
monodromy action on
H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
is expressed in terms
\delta_{\gamma}
by the Picard-Lefschetz formula

T(\delta_{\gamma}):a\in H_{P}(Z^{\prime})\mapsto a+(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}(a\cdot\delta_{\gamma})\delta_{\gamma}.

In case
n=2m+1
is odd, the intersection pairing on
H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
is symmetric,

\delta_{\gamma}\cdot\delta_{g}=(-1)^{m}2
and hence
T(\delta_{\gamma})
is an orthogonal reflection. For even
n
it is a symplectic transvection.
The loops thus obtained make up a single generating conjugacy class in
\pi_{1}(\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d},[Z^{\prime}])
and the classical Lefschetz theory asserts that the set
\Delta_{Z^{\prime}}\subset H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
of the associated vanishing cycles generates
H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
. So the
associated Picard-Lefschetz transformations
T(\delta)
make up a generating conjugacy class of the image of the monodromy representation
\rho_{Z^{\prime}}:\pi_{1}(\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d},[Z^{\prime}])\to\operatorname{Aut}H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
and this image has
\Delta_{Z^{\prime}}
as an orbit. The preceding also implies that for odd
n
, the intersection pairing on
H_{P}(Z^{\prime})
is even and hence lifts to quadratic form
H_{P}(Z^{\prime})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
which takes the value
(-1)^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}
on the vanishing cycles. For even
n
, the preceding still shows that the reduction modulo 2 of the intersection pairing lifts to quadratic form
H_{P}(Z^{\prime};{\mathbb{F}}_{2})\to{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
which takes the value
1
on the images of the vanishing cycles.

It is clear that primitive homology and primitive cohomology have the same rank. Lemma
1.1
implies the (known) formula for that common rank:

Corollary 1.3
.

The rank
p_{n}(d)
of the (co)primitive cohomology of a smooth degree
d
hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}
is

(d-1)\frac{(d-1)^{n+1}+(-1)^{n}}{d}.

Proof.

First observe that
p_{0}(d)=d-1
. The corollary then follows with induction, for Lemma
1.1
gives the recurrence relation
p_{n}(d)=(d-1)^{n+1}+p_{n-1}(d)
.
∎

Note the identity

(1.1)

p_{n}(d)=(d-1)\big(p_{n-1}(d)+(-1)^{n}\big).

The intersection pairing

(\alpha,\beta)\in H^{n}(Z)\times H^{n}(Z)\to\alpha\cdot\beta:=\langle\alpha\cup\beta|[Z]\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}

is perfect by Poincaré duality. It is symmetric or antisymmetric according to whether
n
is even or odd.

Suppose
n=2m
is even. Then
\eta^{m}_{Z}\in H^{n}(Z)
is indivisible and
has self-intersection
d
. Since we may identify
H_{P}(Z)
with the orthogonal complement of

\eta^{m}_{Z}
, it follows that
H_{P}(Z)\to H^{P}(Z)
is an injection with cokernel
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
. We regard this injection as an embedding. So then
H_{P}(Z)
contains
dH^{P}(Z)
and
H_{P}(Z)/dH^{P}(Z)

is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
-submodule of
H^{P}(Z;{\mathbb{Z}}/d)
of corank one.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
Z=Z^{0}\supset Z^{1}\supset\cdots Z^{n}\supset Z^{n+1}=\emptyset
be the transversal intersection of
Z
with a complete flag in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}
, yielding the affine stratification with codimension
k
stratum

\mathring{Z}^{k}:=Z^{k}\smallsetminus Z^{k+1}
. Then the maps

D_{k}:\tilde{H}_{n-k}(\mathring{Z}^{k})\twoheadrightarrow H_{P}(Z^{k})\xrightarrow{\iota_{Z^{k}}}H^{P}(Z^{k})\hookrightarrow\tilde{H}_{n-k-1}(\mathring{Z}^{1+k})

define a (homological) complex which naturally maps to the single term complex
H_{P}(Z)
placed in degree zero. The kernel of this chain map has its homology in degrees
0,-1,\dots,1-n
and is in these degrees
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
.
\square

2.
A pair of maps associated with a module over a group ring

We shall apply what follows to a finite cyclic group, but since the discussion initially does not require that assumption,
we let
G
be any group of finite order
d
. The group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}G
comes with an

augmentation
resp.
co-augmentation

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{G}:{\mathbb{Z}}G\to{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\varepsilon_{G}(g)=1\text{ for all $g\in G$},

\displaystyle\textstyle\varepsilon^{G}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}G,\quad\varepsilon^{G}(1)=\sum_{g\in G}g.

These are
{\mathbb{Z}}G
-linear if we regard
{\mathbb{Z}}
as the trivial
{\mathbb{Z}}G
-module (we denoted the image of
g\in G
in
{\mathbb{Z}}G
also by
g
). They form respectively the invariants resp. co-invariants of
{\mathbb{Z}}G
as a (left) module over itself.
Then
\ker(\varepsilon_{G})
and the image of
\varepsilon^{G}
are each others annihilator and
\ker(\varepsilon_{G})\cong\operatorname{coker}(\varepsilon^{G})
as
{\mathbb{Z}}G
-modules. Note that
\varepsilon_{G}\varepsilon^{G}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is multiplication with
d
.
We shall write
I
for
\ker(\varepsilon_{G})
(the
augmentation ideal
).

Let
M
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}G
-module. The map

\textstyle\varepsilon^{G}_{M}:M\to M^{G},m\mapsto\varepsilon^{G}(1)m=\sum_{g\in G}gm

has the property that
\varepsilon^{G}_{M}(gm)=\varepsilon^{G}_{M}(m)
and is on
M^{G}
multiplication by
d

Hence it induces a
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
-linear map

(2.1)

r_{M}:(M/M^{G})_{G}\to{\mathbb{Z}}/d\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M^{G}.

For example,
r_{{\mathbb{Z}}G}
is an isomorphism between two copies of
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
and

r_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the inclusion of
0
in
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
.
If
M=I
, then
I^{G}=0
and so the target of
r_{I}
is zero and its domain is

(I)_{G}\cong I/I^{2}
. As is well-known,
I/I^{2}
can be identified with (the additively written) abelianization
G_{ab}
of
G
.

There is also a dual version based on the isomorphism
M/IM\cong M_{G}
: the natural map

I\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M\to IM\to(IM)_{G}
factors through

I/I^{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M_{G}
and thus defines a map

s_{M}:G_{ab}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M_{G}\to(IM)_{G}.

3.
Cyclic covers

In what follows we are concerned with the group
\mu_{d}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
of
d
th roots of unity. We shall write

\varepsilon_{d}:{\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{d}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
and

If

\mu_{d}
, then

is a principal

-module.
Both are free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules of rank

defines a smooth degree

(It was recently shown by Zhiyuan Li and Zhichao Tang

that

The
\mu_{d}
-orbit space of

and the resulting map

takes the
\mu_{d}
-fixed point set in

isomorphically onto

. We therefore regard

as a subvariety of

, given as the (transversal) hyperplane section defined by

.
The diagram of Lemma

is obviously one of
{\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{d}
-modules.
The intersection pairing on

is

-invariant. This determines a sesquilinear

-linear in the first variable. This defines in fact a

-hermitian form on the

, for if we denote the natural anti-involution of

Lemma 3.1
.

In the upper exact sequence of Lemma

,
the trivial
{\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{d}
-module

maps isomorphically onto

Remark 3.2
.

We digress a bit to explicate the monodromy representation.

The
\mu_{d}
-covering construction cannot be carried out universally over
{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}
(this due to the fact that such a covering is only unique up to a covering transformation), but what we can do is to consider
the subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}_{n+1,d}
parametrizing hypersurfaces of the form
F(z_{0},\dots,z_{n})+z_{n+1}^{d}
with
F\not=0
. This amounts to a base change over the (tautological)
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}
.
The fundamental group of its restriction to
\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d}
is a central extension
of
\pi_{1}(\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d},[Z^{\prime}])
by
\pi_{1}({\mathbb{C}}^{\times},1)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Here
\pi_{1}({\mathbb{C}}^{\times},1)
acts by covering transformations and gives a surjection
\pi_{1}({\mathbb{C}}^{\times},1)\to\mu_{d}
(and thus determines a generator

of
\mu_{d}
). So if
\hat{\pi}_{1}(\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d},[Z^{\prime}])

\pi_{1}(\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d},[Z^{\prime}])

(a push-out), then the

\hat{\rho}_{Z^{\prime}}:\hat{\pi}_{1}(\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d},[Z^{\prime}])\to\operatorname{Aut}H_{P}(Z).

\mu_{d}\subset\hat{\pi}_{1}(\mathring{\mathbb{P}}_{n,d},[Z^{\prime}])
acting in the given manner.
This group acts in the contragradient manner on

and
\iota_{Z}:H_{P}(Z)\to H^{P}(Z)
is equivariant.
Its image of this representation is generated by ‘generalized Picard-Lefschetz transformations’ in the sense of Pham:
a path
\gamma
as above determines a degeneration into a
\mu_{d}
-cover

of type

. Pham specifies a vanishing cycle

(denoted

]
), which generates

as a free

-module of rank one and shows that the associated monodromy is given by the transformation in

defined by

\textstyle\hat{T}(\delta_{\gamma}):a\mapsto a+(-1)^{(n+1)n/2}\langle\hat{a},\delta_{\gamma}\rangle\hat{\delta}_{\gamma}=a+(-1)^{(n+1)n/2}\sum_{g\in\mu_{d}}(a,g\hat{\delta}_{\gamma})g\hat{\delta}_{\gamma}

\langle\;,\;\rangle
; this makes it for even

.

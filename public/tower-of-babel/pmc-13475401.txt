In the age of mRNA vaccines: Linear or circular mRNA?
Authors: Kenneth Lundstrom
Published: 2026

Main text

The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic surely accelerated vaccine development in general and facilitated the breakthrough of the engineering of mRNA-based vaccines.1,2 The advantages of mRNA-based vaccines for example whole virus vaccines are the rapid and inexpensive production process, facility of vaccine re-engineering, and their good safety profile due to direct cytoplasmic delivery of mRNA and no risk of host chromosomal integration.3,4 Due to inefficient delivery and sensitivity to degradation of mRNA, most mRNA vaccine platforms utilize lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) for cellular uptake and protection against degradation.5 In the case of mRNA-based vaccine development against COVID-19 and other indications, linear mRNA has been used. Circular RNA has also been evaluated for vaccine development.6 In the recent publication in Molecular Therapy Nucleic Acids entitled “comparative analysis of expression dynamics, immunogenicity, and safety profiles between linear and circular RNA vaccine platforms” Lee et al. compared vaccines based on linear RNA and circular RNA.7

So far, linear RNA has been more frequently applied than circular RNA. The linear RNA platforms comprise a cap structure at the 5′ end and a poly(A) tail at the 3′ end.8 Moreover, chemically modified nucleosides such as methyl pseudouridine (m1Ψ) have been incorporated to prevent degradation and improve RNA stability. In the case of circular RNA, no cap analogs are required, and the circular RNA is generated via the Permutated Intron-Exon (PIE) system, which forms a closed-loop structure containing an internal ribosome entry site (IRES).6 As circular RNA allows self-splicing of transcripts, no additional enzymes are required resulting in inherent stability and prolonged protein expression. Due to the translational efficiency and prolonged expression durability of circular RNA, several therapeutic applications have been evaluated.6,9 In this context, circular RNA can potentially be used as non-coding aptamers, sequestered miRNAs and proteins, antisense RNAs, and mRNA vaccines.10 For example, circular mRNA was engineered to express the receptor binding domain (RBD) of the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) spike (S) protein, which resulted in durable antigen production, induction of neutralizing antibodies and elicited Th1-biased immune responses.11 Furthermore, it is potentially possible to target pathological circular RNAs identified in patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and to use circular RNAs as biomarkers for autoimmune diseases, Alzheimer disease, prostate cancer, leukemia, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, etc. due their altered pathological expression.10

The study by Lee et al. compared linear and circular RNA vaccine platforms for in vitro expression of firefly luciferase (FLuc) in human cell lines and in vivo in mice. Moreover, expression dynamics, immunogenicity, and safety were evaluated in mice using influenza hemagglutinin (HA) as an antigen.7 In the case of linear RNA, methyl pseudouridine modified RNA (Lin-m1Ψ) and unmodified RNA (Lin-WT) were utilized, whereas only unmodified circular RNA (Circ-WT) was used. In both adherent cells and suspension cultures Lin-WT and Lin-m1Ψ RNA expression increased for the first 6 h and declined 24 h post-transfection, whereas Circ-WT RNA maintained high levels of expression for 3 days before a gradual decrease. Intramuscular (i.m.) or intravenous (i.v.) injection of mice resulted in higher and prolonged expression of FLuc from the circular RNA platform compared to the linear RNA platforms. Overall, linear RNA platforms provided rapid FLuc expression, whereas the circular RNA platform showed a significant advantage for maintaining stable protein levels for antibody production or protein replacement therapy.

Following i.m. immunization of BALB/c mice with RNA expressing the HA antigen resulted in superior neutralizing ability of the Lin-m1Ψ and Circ-WT RNA platforms compared to Lin-WT, indicating that circular RNA may present an attractive alternative to linear RNA. In the context of cell-mediated immune responses, all three RNA platforms induced IFN-ɣ secretion although the levels for Circ-WT were slightly lower but not statistically significant. Evaluation of cytokine responses revealed similar induction of IFN-ɣ, IL-2, TNF-α for Lin-WT and Lin- Lin-m1Ψ m1Ψ. However, the immune profile for Circ-WT administration was similar to the one seen for control mice. In the case of IL-2 secretion, a significant increase was obtained for Lin-WT, but not Lin-m1Ψ and Circ-WT. Studies of CD8 T cell activation revealed that the levels were slightly lower for Circ-WT than for Lin-WT and Lin-m1Ψ, which certainly can have an impact on the development of prophylactic and therapeutic vaccines.

Related to innate immune responses, gene expression of RIG-1 (retinoic acid-inducible gene-1), IFN-β, and IL-6 induced by the RNA platforms was monitored in A549 cells and BALB/c mice. The highest levels of all cytokines were obtained for Lin-WT in transfected A549 cells. Circ-WT induced higher RIG-1 and IFN-β levels than Lin-m1Ψ. In mice, Lin-WT induced the strongest pro-inflammatory cytokine responses compared to Lin-m1Ψ and Circ-WT.

The safety and toxicity of the RNA platforms were assessed 2 and 14 days after RNA-LNP administration. No significant changes in red blood cell counts were seen for any RNA platform after either inoculation. In contrast, the reticulocyte levels were significantly inferior 2 days after the inoculation, but they recovered to base levels by day 14 post-inoculation. A similar decrease and recovery were seen in monocyte levels. In the case of platelet counts, a significant decrease was observed 2 days after the administration of Lin-WT and Circ-WT, whereas no significant change was observed after Lin-m1Ψ administration. However, the platelet counts returned to control levels for Lin-WT and Circ-WT after 14 days. Hepatological assessments demonstrated significantly reduced levels of albumin for all RNA platforms 2 days post-injection, which remained significantly lower only for Lin-WT after 14 days. Alkaline phosphatase levels were significantly lower for all RNA platforms after 2 days but returned to normal levels for Lin-WT and Lin-m1Ψ after 14 days. In contrast, significantly higher alkaline phosphatase levels were detected for Circ-WT after 14 days compared to control mice. In the context of aspartate aminotransferase, an enzyme associated with cell injury and possibly muscle injury, significantly higher levels were detected for Lin-m1Ψ and Circ-WT after 2 days, which returned to normal levels after 14 days. Finally, a histological evaluation of changes in tissues from the bone marrow, muscle, spleen, and lymph nodes was carried out 2 days post-injection. All RNA platforms caused a significant decrease in erythroid cells in the bone marrow and moderate inflammatory changes at the injection site. Moreover, increased cellularity and megakaryocytes were detected in the spleen tissue of all mice. Severe inflammation and necrosis were seen in mice receiving Lin-WT, whereas significantly fewer inflammatory lesions were detected in the lymph nodes after Lin-m1Ψ and Circ-WT administration.

In their recent publication Lee and colleagues present a direct comparison of linear and circular RNA platforms for vaccine development, highlighting the potential advantages and limitations. The Circ-WT generated prolonged antigen expression and slightly higher cumulative protein levels both in vitro and in vivo compared to linear RNA platforms. The close-loop structure of circular RNA provides resistance against exonuclease degradation and contributed to the superiority of circular RNA compared to linear RNA. In the context of humoral immunity comparable neutralizing HA antibody titers were obtained for circular and linear RNA. Assessment of innate immune responses in draining lymph nodes of the low immunogenic FLuc reporter gene demonstrated higher levels of IFN-β, TNF-α, and IL-6 for unmodified linear RNA. Moreover, circular RNA and modified linear RNA induced lower levels of cytokines. The development of platforms for circular RNA has significantly gained from computational modeling,11 which is consistent with the findings of Lee and colleagues on expression dynamics and innate immune activation. In the context of cellular immunity, circular RNA showed slightly reduced responses compared to linear RNA, which might compromise their use as vaccines against intracellular pathogens and cancers12 and therefore requires optimization to improve CD8+ T cell activation. Although previous studies have demonstrated that cancer vaccines based on circular RNA have enhanced tumor-specific immune responses due to prolonged antigen presentation,13 the lower CD8+ T cell responses may restrict their effectiveness and overexpression of tumor-associated antigens from circular RNA might be a better approach.

One issue of great importance for any vaccine development relates to safety concerns about potential excessive innate immune activation and inflammatory responses. Particularly, unmodified linear RNA vaccines have been linked to inflammation and elevated cytokine levels triggering adverse events and cytokine storms.14 Lee and colleagues observed that administration of linear unmodified RNA and circular RNA caused a transient decrease in platelet counts, whereas no significant reduction was observed for modified linear RNA, which supported the mediation of RNA vaccine-based thrombotic thrombocytopenia by mRNA-LNP itself. Moreover, it was demonstrated that unmodified linear RNA induced significantly higher levels of IFN-β, TNF-α, IL-6, and IFN-γ expression in mice potentially leading to tissue damage underscoring the importance of which type of RNA should be used for future mRNA vaccine development. Other issues to consider relate to industrial manufacturing of RNA vaccines, which is well-established for linear RNA, whereas circular RNA still encounters challenges regarding the enzymatic procedures, purification steps, and quality control.

Finally, as Lee et al. rightly point out, self-amplifying RNA (saRNA) vectors based on alphavirus replicons should be included as serious candidates for RNA-based vaccines against infectious diseases and cancers.15 Due to the RNA self-amplification, large quantities of available RNA results in superior antigen production allowing application of reduced RNA doses compared to conventional mRNA, which positively affects production costs and potentially decreases adverse effects in patients. Overall, the tremendous technology advancements including the choice of utilizing linear, circular, and saRNA platforms will further strengthen the position of RNA technologies at the forefront of modern medicine.

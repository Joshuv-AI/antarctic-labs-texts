Introduction

In algebra it is usual to use two existing mathematicals objects to produce a new one with new properties. The most notable example are groups rings, where a ring and a group can be used to construct a new ring. One particular properties is that even if the original ring is commutative, the group ring based on that ring does not have to be necessary commutative.

The origin of this concept can be found in the work of T. Molen
[
18
]
, and G. Frobenius
[
8
]
,
[
7
]
. Later, I. Schur presented the so called Schur lemma
[
22
]
, a foundational result in representation theory which can be understood in terms of groups ring.

The development of group rings as an independent subject can be tracked to
[
16
]
, where Mashkle proved necessary conditions for a group ring to be semisimple. However, it is in the work of E. Noether and R. Brauer
[
19
]
,
[
1
]
where they passed to have a central role in non commutative algebra, seen as an algebraic object by its own.

Twisted group rings are a generalization of group rings, where the product is still induced by the product of the group, but where the result is twisted by a scalar which is determined by a 2-cocycle. They appear close to the beginning of group ring, but it was not until
[
9
]
that they had its own theory. Since then, they play a central role in several filds, from lie theory
[
17
]
to Heisenberg categorification
[
21
]
.

One application of group ring is in coding theory, with the so called group ring codes. In
[
14
]
the authors proposed a method to produce linear codes from group rings. However, a unified theory as well as some of their most important properties can be found in
[
13
]
. Recently, those ideas has been generalized to be used in twisted group ring, such as in
[
4
]
, where it is proven that a wide range of codes can be seen as a particular instance of twisted group rings.

Another line of research to obtain new generalization of group ring has been thougt it derivations, as can be seen in
[
3
]
. In this paper the authors study the derivation of a group ring, and use it to determine the derivations of a group ring of a finite field and the dihedral group. However, the authors recognize that there is a leak of scientific research about derivations over group rings with positive characteristic. Nevertheless, there have been important advances such as the work of Ferrero, Giambruno and Polcino Milies
[
5
]
where they proved what we will call the FGP theorem.

Definición 1

Let

\alpha(x,y)\alpha(xy,z)=\alpha(y,z)\alpha(x,yz)

The set of all 2-cocycles of

To understand the structure of twisted group rings we need to introduce the second cohomology group and the coboundaries

Definición 2
.

Let
\varphi:G\to A
be a map. The coboundary
\partial\varphi:G\times G\to A
is defined by

\partial\varphi(x,y):=\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)\,\varphi(xy)^{-1},\quad\forall x,y\in G.

Then
\partial\varphi
is a 2-cocycle. The set of all coboundaries is denoted by
B^{2}(G,A)\subseteq Z^{2}(G,A)

Now we can recall the second cohomology group

Definición 3
.

The second cohomology group of

H^{2}(G,A):=Z^{2}(G,A)/B^{2}(G,A)=\{[\alpha]:\alpha\in Z^{2}(G,A)\},

where
[\alpha]=\alpha B^{2}(G,A)
denotes the coset of
\alpha
modulo coboundaries.

Two cocycles in the same equivalence class are called cohomologous.

Definición 4
.

Two 2-cocycles
\alpha,\beta\in Z^{2}(G,A)
are said to be cohomologous if there exists a map

\varphi:G\to A
such that

\beta(x,y)=\alpha(x,y)\,\partial\varphi(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in G.

Equivalently,
\alpha
and
\beta
belong to the same coset in
H^{2}(G,A)

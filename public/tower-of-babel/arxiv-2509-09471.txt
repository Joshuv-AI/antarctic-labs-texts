1.  Introduction

The classical
Schwarz lemma
is a cornerstone of complex analysis, describing how holomorphic self-maps of the unit disc
D\subset\mathbb{C}
contract the Poincaré metric. It has been a guiding principle for understanding the interplay between analytic properties of maps and the geometry of the domains they act on.

Building on this idea, Osserman extended Schwarz-type results to holomorphic mappings on the unit disc, providing refined boundary behavior estimates and geometric constraints for such maps. On the other Krantz
[
8
]
, developed analogous results for holomorphic mappings on the higher-dimensional unit ball
B^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, establishing sharp distortion and boundary estimates that generalize the classical Schwarz lemma to several complex variables. The paper
[
2
]
by Burns and Krantz establishes a boundary version of the Schwarz lemma for holomorphic self-maps of the unit ball
B^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, proving rigidity when the map agrees with the identity to sufficiently high order at a boundary point. Moreover, they refine Alexander’s classical theorem by showing that holomorphic maps with sufficiently smooth boundary extension and strong tangency at a boundary point must locally approximate automorphisms of the ball.

In this paper we prove the following Schwarz type lemmas for holomorphic disks on the boundary.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
F
is a holomorphic mapping of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
into
\mathbb{B}_{m}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
and
F(1)\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}
. If
F^{\prime}(1)
exist, then we have

(1.1)

\|F^{\prime}(1)\|\geq\frac{2(1-\|F(0)\|)^{2}}{\;\Big({1-\|F(0)\|^{2}}+{\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}\Big)}.

a) If
\|F^{\prime}(1)\|=1
and
F(0)=0
, then
F
is an affine disk.

b) If
m=1
and
F(0)=0
, then the first inequality in (
1.1
) reduces to

(1.2)

|F^{\prime}(1)|\geq\frac{2}{1+|F^{\prime}(0)|},

which is an equality if and only if
F(z)=z
or

F(z)=z\frac{z+a}{1+z\bar{a}}.

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
8
, Remark 5.2]
, Krantz noticed that the functions
F(z)=z
and
F(z)=z\frac{z+a}{1+z\bar{a}}
satisfy the equality in (
1.2
). Here we prove the converse.

In the context of harmonic maps and minimal surfaces, Forstnerič and Kalaj
[
6
]
obtained sharp Schwarz–Pick-type estimates for
conformal harmonic maps
from the unit disc into the unit ball
B^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Their work provides optimal bounds on the norm of the differential at points where the map is conformal and shows that such maps are distance decreasing with respect to the Poincaré metric on the disc and the Cayley–Klein metric on the ball. These results also identify extremal maps as the conformal embeddings of the disc onto affine discs in the ball. Moreover Forstnerič and Kalaj introduced the minimal metric, see also
[
4
]
for more details. That metric defined the distance, which in the unit ball
B^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the restriction to the Cayley–Klein distance. It is defined as follows

Let for
\mathrm{z},\mathrm{w}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(1.3)

\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{z},\mathrm{w})=\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(\frac{|1-\langle\mathrm{z},\mathrm{w}\rangle|}{\sqrt{(1-\|\mathrm{z}\|^{2})(1-\|\mathrm{w}\|^{2})}}\right).

Theorem 1.3

(Distance-decreasing property)
.

[
6
]

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
be a conformal minimal immersion.
Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}}=\dfrac{|dz|}{1-|z|^{2}}
the Poincaré metric on
\mathbb{D}
, and by
\mathrm{dist}
the
Cayley–Klein distance on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Then

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(f(z),f(w)\bigr)\;\leq\;\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(z,w),\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Moreover, if equality holds for a pair of distinct points, then
f(\mathbb{D})
is a totally geodesic
linear disc, i.e. the intersection of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with a two-dimensional plane through the origin, and equality holds
for all points.

By using Theorem
1.3
, we prove the following theorem for conformal minimal disks.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
F:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a conformal minimal immersion so that
F(0)=0
and for some
|z_{0}|=1
,
X(z_{0})\in S^{n-1}
. Then,
\|dF(z_{0})\|\geq 1,
provided that
dF(z_{0})
exists.

Conjecture 1.5
.

We expect that in the notation of Theorem
1.4
, if for some
|z_{0}|=1
we have
\|dF(z_{0})\|=1
, then
F
must be the restriction of a conformal linear map from
\mathbb{D}
onto a planar disk
L\cap\mathbb{B}^{n}
, where
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a two-dimensional linear subspace through the origin.
In other words, the extremal conformal minimal immersions are precisely the conformal parametrizations of totally geodesic planar disks in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
passing through
0
.

2.  Holomorphic disks and the proof of Theorem
1.1

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we prove it for the special case when
F(0)=0
(Theorem
2.1
). This statement can be considered as an extension a theorem of Osserman
[
9
]
, see also the paper of Krantz
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
F:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{m}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
is holomorphic such that
F(0)=0
. Then we have

\|F(z)\|\leq|z|\frac{|z|+\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}{1+|z|\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}\leq|z|.

Moreover if for some
|z_{0}|=1
,
F(z_{0})\in S_{m}
and
F
is differentiable at
z_{0}
, then

(2.1)

\|F^{\prime}(z_{0})\|\geq\frac{2}{1+\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}.

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume that
F
is a holomorphic mapping of the unit disk into
\mathbb{B}_{m}
so that
F(0)=0
and
F(1)\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}
. Then
\|F^{\prime}(1)\|\geq 1
. If
\|F^{\prime}(1)\|=1
, then
F
is necessarily an affine disc (i.e. the image of
\mathbb{D}
under a linear embedding into
\mathbb{B}_{m}
). In this context, affine discs should be understood as extremal objects for the inequality above, rather than as totally geodesic submanifolds for the minimal metric.

Proof of Corollary
2.2
.

If
\|F^{\prime}(1)\|=1
, then by Theorem
2.1
,
\|F^{\prime}(0)\|=1
. This implies that
\|F(z)\|=|z|
for every
z
. Hence
F(z)/z
is a contant vector belonging to
\mathbb{S}_{m}=\partial\mathbb{B}_{m}
. This implies the claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
F
be holomorphic on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
(or the unit disk when
n=1
). Define

f(z)=\frac{F(z)}{z},\qquad f(0)=F^{\prime}(0),

so that
a:=f(0)
and
w:=f(z)
, with
A=\|a\|
and
x=\|w\|
.

By
[
10
, Theorem 8.1.4]
, for
a\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
there exists a holomorphic automorphism
\varphi_{a}
of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with
\varphi_{a}(a)=0
, and

\|\varphi_{a}(w)\|=\frac{\|w-a\|}{|1-\langle w,a\rangle|}.

Then
\varphi_{a}\circ f
is holomorphic
\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
, fixes
0
, and Schwarz’s lemma gives

(2.2)

\frac{\|w-a\|}{|1-\langle w,a\rangle|}\leq|z|.

Squaring and expanding (
2.2
) yields

\|w\|^{2}-\langle w,a\rangle-\langle a,w\rangle+A^{2}\leq|z|^{2}\bigl(1-\langle w,a\rangle-\langle a,w\rangle+|\langle w,a\rangle|^{2}\bigr).

Rearranging,

x^{2}+A^{2}-|z|^{2}-|z|^{2}|\langle w,a\rangle|^{2}\leq 2(1-|z|^{2})\Re\langle w,a\rangle.

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\Re\langle w,a\rangle\leq xA
and
|\langle w,a\rangle|^{2}\leq x^{2}A^{2}
.
Hence

(2.3)

(1-|z|^{2}A^{2})x^{2}-2(1-|z|^{2})Ax+(A^{2}-|z|^{2})\leq 0.

Since
1-|z|^{2}A^{2}>0
, inequality (
2.3
) forces
x
between the roots of the quadratic equations, namely

x_{1/2}=\frac{A\pm|z|}{1\pm A|z|}.

Thus

\frac{A-|z|}{1-A|z|}\leq\|f(z)\|\leq\frac{A+|z|}{1+A|z|}.

In particular,

{\ \|f(z)\|\leq\frac{\|a\|+|z|}{1+\|a\|\,|z|}\ }.

Since
F(z)=zf(z)
, we also obtain

{\ \|F(z)\|\leq|z|\,\frac{\|a\|+|z|}{1+\|a\|\,|z|}\ }.

Let
z_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
r\in(0,1)
. If
\|F(z_{0})\|=1
, then

\|F(rz_{0})\|\leq r\frac{A+r}{1+Ar}.

Hence

\frac{1-\|F(rz_{0})\|}{1-r}\geq\frac{1-\dfrac{r(A+r)}{1+Ar}}{1-r}=\frac{1+r}{1+Ar}.

Letting
r\to 1^{-}
yields

{\ \|F^{\prime}(z_{0})\|\geq\frac{2}{1+\|a\|}\ }.

This is the desired sharp inequality, obtained using only the automorphism formula for
\varphi_{a}
and Schwarz’s lemma.

∎

For
x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, define the Hermitian inner product

\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}\overline{y_{j}},\qquad\|x\|^{2}=\langle x,x\rangle.

Let
a\in\mathbb{B}_{m}
with
\|a\|<1
, and set

s=\sqrt{1-\|a\|^{2}},\qquad P_{a}z=\frac{\langle z,a\rangle}{\langle a,a\rangle}\,a,\qquad Q_{a}=I-P_{a}.

Then the automorphism
\varphi_{a}:\mathbb{B}_{m}\to\mathbb{B}_{m}
is given by

\varphi_{a}(w)=\frac{a-P_{a}w-s\,Q_{a}w}{1-\langle w,a\rangle}.

\varphi_{a}(w)=\frac{\,a-\dfrac{\langle w,a\rangle}{\|a\|^{2}}a-\sqrt{1-\|a\|^{2}}\left(w-\dfrac{\langle w,a\rangle}{\|a\|^{2}}a\right)}{1-\langle w,a\rangle}.

Lemma 2.3

(Operator norm of the derivative of the ball automorphism)
.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{n}
denote the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Fix
a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

Consider the standard automorphism
\varphi_{a}:\mathbb{B}_{n}\to\mathbb{B}_{n}
satisfying
\varphi_{a}(a)=0
with derivative

D\varphi_{a}(w)[v]=\frac{-P_{a}v-sQ_{a}v+\langle v,a\rangle\varphi_{a}(w)}{1-\langle w,a\rangle}.

Then the operator norm of
D\varphi_{a}(w)
satisfies

\|D\varphi_{a}(w)\|=\frac{1}{|1-\langle w,a\rangle|}\max\Big\{\,s,\;\big\|-e+r\varphi_{a}(w)\big\|\Big\}.

In particular, for
w=a
,

(2.4)

\|D\varphi_{a}(a)\|=\frac{1}{1-r^{2}},\qquad r=\|a\|.

The global supremum over the closed unit ball is

\sup_{\|w\|\leq 1}\|D\varphi_{a}(w)\|=\frac{1+r}{1-r}.

Proof.

Decompose
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
as
v=\alpha e+u
with
u\perp e
. Then

D\varphi_{a}(w)[v]=\frac{\alpha(-e+r\varphi_{a}(w))-su}{1-t}.

By the triangle inequality,

\|D\varphi_{a}(w)[v]\|\leq\frac{|\alpha|\|-e+r\varphi_{a}(w)\|+s\|u\|}{|1-t|}\leq\frac{\max\{\|-e+r\varphi_{a}(w)\|,s\}}{|1-t|}\|v\|,

so the operator norm is

\|D\varphi_{a}(w)\|=\frac{1}{|1-t|}\max\{\|-e+r\varphi_{a}(w)\|,s\}.

To make this more explicit, define

E:=\|-e+r\varphi_{a}(w)\|^{2}.

Using the identities

1-\|\varphi_{a}(w)\|^{2}=\frac{(1-r^{2})(1-\|w\|^{2})}{|1-t|^{2}},\qquad\langle\varphi_{a}(w),e\rangle=\frac{r^{2}-t}{r(1-t)},

we compute

\begin{split}E=&\|-e+r\varphi_{a}(w)\|^{2}=1+r^{2}\|\varphi_{a}(w)\|^{2}-2r\Re\langle e,\varphi_{a}(w)\rangle\\
&=(1+r^{2})-2\Re\frac{r^{2}-t}{1-t}-\frac{r^{2}(1-r^{2})(1-\|w\|^{2})}{|1-t|^{2}}.\end{split}

For
w=a
, we have
t=r^{2}
and
\|w\|^{2}=r^{2}
, giving
E=1
, so

\|D\varphi_{a}(a)\|=\frac{1}{1-r^{2}}.

The global supremum is attained along the direction of
a
: take
w=e=a/r
(a boundary point), then
\varphi_{a}(e)=-e
and

\|-e+r\varphi_{a}(e)\|=\|-e-re\|=1+r,\quad|1-\langle w,a\rangle|=1-r,

so
\|D\varphi_{a}(e)\|=(1+r)/(1-r)
, proving the supremum is attained.
∎

Then this theorem implies the first part of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
F:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{m}
is holomorphic and
\|F(\zeta)\|=1
, for a point
|\zeta|=1
, then

\|F^{\prime}(\zeta)\|\geq\frac{2(1-\|F(0)\|)^{2}}{\;\Big({1-\|F(0)\|^{2}}+{\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}\Big)}.

Moreover for
a=F(0)
,

\|F^{\prime}(\zeta)\|\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2(1-\|a\|)^{2}}{\;\Big({1-\|a\|^{2}}+{\sqrt{1-\|a\|^{2}}}\Big)},&\hbox{for $m\geq 2$;}\\
\frac{1-|a|}{1+|a|},&\hbox{for $m=1$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
F:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{m}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
be holomorphic and suppose
F(0)=a
with
\|a\|<1
. Then the mapping
f(z)=\varphi_{a}(F(z))
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
. Moreover

f^{\prime}(0)=\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(F(0))\cdot F^{\prime}(0).

Thus

\|f^{\prime}(0)\|\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}_{a}(a)\|\cdot\|F^{\prime}(0)\|,

which in in view of lemma
2.3
((
2.4
)), implies

\|f^{\prime}(0)\|\leq\frac{1}{1-|a|^{2}}\cdot\|F^{\prime}(0)\|.

Thus for for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

(2.5)

\big\|\varphi_{a}(F(z))\big\|\leq|z|\;\frac{|z|+\dfrac{\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}{1-\|a\|^{2}}}{1+|z|\;\dfrac{\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}{1-\|a\|^{2}}}\leq|z|.

Moreover, if
|\eta|=1
,
\zeta=F(\eta)\in S_{m}
and
F
is differentiable at
\eta
,

\begin{split}\|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(\zeta)\|\|F^{\prime}(\eta)\|&\geq\|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(\zeta)F^{\prime}(\eta)\|\;\\
&\geq\;\frac{2}{\;\Big(1+\dfrac{\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}{1-\|a\|^{2}}\Big)\;\;}.\end{split}

Thus for
m\geq 2
, by using Lemma
2.3
again we have

\begin{split}\|F^{\prime}(\eta)\|&\geq\frac{2}{\|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(\zeta)\|\Big(1+\dfrac{\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}{1-\|a\|^{2}}\Big)}\\
&\geq\;\frac{2\dfrac{1-\|a\|}{1+\|a\|}}{\;\Big(1+\dfrac{\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}{1-\|a\|^{2}}\Big)\;\;}\\
&=\;\frac{2(1-\|a\|)^{2}}{\;\Big({1-\|a\|^{2}}+{\|F^{\prime}(0)\|}\Big)\;\;}\geq\frac{2(1-\|a\|)^{2}}{\;\Big({1-\|a\|^{2}}+{\sqrt{1-\|a\|^{2}}}\Big)}.\end{split}

In the last inequality we used statement of Remark
2.5
below. For
m=1
,
|F^{\prime}(0)|\leq(1-|a|^{2})
, and then
|F^{\prime}(\eta)|\geq\frac{1-|a|}{1+|a|}
.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

It follows from (
2.5
), and Lemma
2.3
for
w=0
, that

\|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(a)F^{\prime}(0)\|\leq 1.

Thus

\|F^{\prime}(0)\|=\|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(a)F^{\prime}(0)\|\leq\|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(0)\|\cdot\|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(a)F^{\prime}(0)\|\leq\sqrt{1-\|a\|^{2}},

and this coincides with a result in
[
7
]
.

Now we prove the second part of Theorem
1.1
, i.e. the part b). We formulate it as a separate statement.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be holomorphic with

f(0)=0,\qquad f(1)=1,\qquad|f^{\prime}(1)|=\frac{2}{1+|f^{\prime}(0)|}.

Then either

f(z)=z,

or

f(z)=z\,\frac{z+a}{1+az},\qquad a\in[0,1).

To prove Theorem
2.6
, we need the following classical result

Proposition 2.7

(Julia inequality)
.

[
3
, p. 27]

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be holomorphic and suppose
f
has a nontangential limit at

f(1)=1
and a finite angular derivative
f^{\prime}(1)
at
1
. Then for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

(2.6)

\frac{|1-f(z)|^{2}}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}\;\leq\;f^{\prime}(1)\,\frac{|1-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

In particular, setting
z=0
gives

f^{\prime}(1)\;\geq\;\frac{|1-f(0)|^{2}}{1-|f(0)|^{2}}.

If the inequality hold in (
2.6
) for a single value
z
, then
f
is a Möbius transform.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

Write the zero of
f
at
0
with multiplicity
m\geq 1
, so

f(z)=z^{m}h(z),\qquad h(0)\neq 0,\quad h(1)=1.

Differentiating gives

f^{\prime}(1)=m+h^{\prime}(1).

Since
f^{\prime}(1)
is finite, it follows that
h^{\prime}(1)
exists and is finite.
By the Julia–Wolff–Carathéodory theorem (see
[
1
]
, and the reference therein), the angular derivative
h^{\prime}(1)
is real and nonnegative, hence

f^{\prime}(1)=m+h^{\prime}(1)\geq m.

From the hypothesis,

|f^{\prime}(1)|=\frac{2}{1+|f^{\prime}(0)|}\leq 2,

so
m\leq 2
.

Case
m=2
.

Then
f(z)=z^{2}h(z)
and
f^{\prime}(0)=0
, so the condition becomes
|f^{\prime}(1)|=2
. But

f^{\prime}(1)=2+h^{\prime}(1)\geq 2,

forcing
h^{\prime}(1)=0
and hence
h\equiv 1
. Thus
f(z)=z^{2}
.

Case
m=1
.

Then
f(z)=zh(z)
, with
f^{\prime}(0)=h(0)=:a
and
f^{\prime}(1)=1+h^{\prime}(1)
.
The condition is

1+h^{\prime}(1)=\frac{2}{1+|a|}.

Applying Julia’s inequality to
h
gives

h^{\prime}(1)\;\geq\;\frac{|1-a|^{2}}{1-|a|^{2}}.

Let
a=re^{it}
, then inequality

\frac{2}{1+|a|}-1\geq\frac{|1-a|^{2}}{1-|a|^{2}}

is equivalent with

\frac{2r(-1+\cos t)}{1-r^{2}}\geq 0,

and this happens precisely when
t=0
.
Equality can only occur when
h
is a disk automorphism fixing
1
with
h(0)=a
, namely

h(z)=\frac{z+a}{1+\overline{a}z}.

In that case,

h^{\prime}(1)=\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1+\overline{a}|^{2}}.

Thus

f^{\prime}(1)=1+h^{\prime}(1)=\frac{2}{1+|a|}.

This identity holds if and only if
1+\overline{a}
is a positive real number, so
a\in[0,1)
. Hence

f(z)=z\,\frac{z+a}{1+az},\qquad a\in[0,1).

Therefore the only possibilities are
f(z)=z^{2}
or
f(z)=z(z+a)/(1+az)
with
a\in[0,1)
.
∎

3.  The case of conformal disks and the proof of Theorem
1.4

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
is a conformal minimal disk. Then for
a\in\mathbb{D}
:

\|f(a)\|\leq\frac{|a|+\|f(0)\|}{1+|a|\cdot\|f(0)\|}.

Proof.

The Cayley–Klein distance
\mathrm{dist}(z,w)
on in the unit ball is given by (
1.3
).

We wish to compare this with

\operatorname{artanh}(|a|)=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+|a|}{1-|a|}.

Since
\operatorname{arcosh}
is increasing on
[1,\infty)
, the inequality

\mathrm{dist}(z,w)\leq\operatorname{artanh}(|a|)

is equivalent to

\frac{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|}{\sqrt{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|w|^{2})}}\leq\cosh\!\bigl(\operatorname{artanh}(|a|)\bigr).

Now let
u=\operatorname{artanh}(|a|)
. Then
\tanh u=|a|
and the identity

\cosh^{2}u\,(1-\tanh^{2}u)=1

gives

\cosh u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}.

Hence the inequality is equivalent to

\frac{|1-\langle z,w\rangle|}{\sqrt{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|w|^{2})}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}},

or, after squaring,

|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{2}(1-|a|^{2})\leq(1-|z|^{2})(1-|w|^{2}).

Let
w:=f(0)
. The distance-decreasing property of holomorphic maps yields

(3.1)

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(f(a),f(0)\bigr)\leq d(a,0).

Let
\varphi_{w}
denote the automorphism of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with
\varphi_{w}(w)=0
.
From (
3.1
) we obtain

\|\varphi_{w}(f(a))\|\leq|a|.

Using the explicit formula for
\varphi_{w}
and solving for
\|f(a)\|
gives

{\ \|f(a)\|\leq\frac{|a|+\|w\|}{1+|a|\,\|w\|}\ }.

This bound is sharp, and in the special case
\|w\|=0
reduces to

\|f(a)\|\leq|a|.

∎

Now, Theorem
1.4
follows as a corollary of the following, slightly more general result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
F:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
is a conformal minimal immersion and for some
|z_{0}|=1
,
X(z_{0})\in S^{n-1}
. Then,
|dF(z_{0})|\geq\frac{1-|f(0)|}{1+|f(0)|},
provided that
dF(z_{0})
exists.

Proof.

We know from Lemma
3.1
that
|X(z)|\leq\frac{|z|+\|w\|}{1+|z|\,\|w\|}
, where
w=f(0)
Thus

\begin{split}|X_{r}(z_{0})|&=\lim_{r\to 1}\left|\frac{X(z_{0})-X(rz_{0})}{(1-r)}\right|\\
&\geq\lim_{r\to 1}\frac{1-|X(rz_{0})|}{(1-r)}\geq\lim_{r\to 1}\frac{1-\frac{|rz_{0}|+\|w\|}{1+|rz_{0}|\,\|w\|}}{1-|r|}\\
&=\frac{1-|f(0)|}{1+|f(0)|}.\end{split}

Now let

x=r\cos t,\quad y=r\sin t.

By the chain rule,

X_{r}=X_{x}\,x_{r}+X_{y}\,y_{r}=X_{x}\cos t+X_{y}\sin t,

X_{t}=X_{x}\,x_{t}+X_{y}\,y_{t}=X_{x}(-r\sin t)+X_{y}(r\cos t).

Conformality (isothermal coordinates) for
X(x,y)
means

\langle X_{x},X_{x}\rangle=\langle X_{y},X_{y}\rangle=\lambda^{2},\quad\langle X_{x},X_{y}\rangle=0

for some scalar function
\lambda>0
.

Compute the squared norm of
X_{r}
:

\displaystyle|X_{r}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle X_{x}\cos t+X_{y}\sin t,\;X_{x}\cos t+X_{y}\sin t\rangle

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}t\,|X_{x}|^{2}+\sin^{2}t\,|X_{y}|^{2}+2\cos t\sin t\,\langle X_{x},X_{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}t\,\lambda^{2}+\sin^{2}t\,\lambda^{2}+0

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}.

Hence
|X_{r}|=\lambda
. But by conformality,
|X_{x}|=|X_{y}|=\lambda
, so

|X_{r}|=|X_{x}|=|X_{y}|.

For completeness, note that

|X_{t}|^{2}=r^{2}\lambda^{2},\quad\text{so}\quad|X_{t}|=r\lambda.

∎

Assume that
F
is a conformal disk, whose unit normals belongs to a half-sphere. Then for such a minimal disk, up to a rigid transformation of the image, there are Enneper -Weistrass parameters
(p(z),q(z))
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Here
p
and
q
are holomorphic functions such that
|q(z)|<1
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
:
Given holomorphic data
(q,p)
on a simply connected domain, a minimal surface
is given (up to translation) by

F(z)=\Re\int^{z}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\big(1-q^{2}\big)\,p,\;\tfrac{i}{2}\big(1+q^{2}\big)\,p,\;q\,p\Big).

Here
q
is the Gauss map written via stereographic projection and
p
is a
holomorphic
1
-form.

With stereographic projection taken from the south pole, the unit normal vector is

\mathbf{n}(z)=\frac{1}{1+|q(z)|^{2}}\,\Big(2\,\Re q(z),\;2\,\Im q(z),\;1-|q(z)|^{2}\Big).

(In this convention, an upward-pointing normal has
n_{3}>0
, equivalently
|q|<1
.
Using the north-pole convention flips the sign of the third component.)

We have for
z=re^{is}
the quantity
|F_{r}|^{2}
can be expressed as (see e.g.
[
5
, Ch. 9]
)

|F_{r}|^{2}=2(F_{z}\cdot F_{\bar{z}})=|p|^{2}(1+|q|^{2})^{2}.

In view of what we said we have

Corollary 3.3
.

If
F
is a minimal immersed disk whose unit normals belongs to a half-sphere, with
F(\mathbb{T})\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then
|dF(z)|\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-|F(0)|}{1+|F(0)|}
for every
z
. In particular,
F^{-1}:F(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{D}
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the intrinsic metric on
\Sigma
and Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Let

F:\mathbb{D}\to\Sigma

be a conformal minimal immersion of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
into a minimal surface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth boundary. Since
|dF(z)|=|p|(1+|q|^{2})
, it follows that
|p|(1+|q|^{2})\geq 1
for every
|z|=1
. Since
|q(z)|<1
, it follows that
|p(z)|\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-|F(0)|}{1+|F(0)|}
for
|z|=1
. But
F
is an immersion, and thus has not any singular point, which implies that
p\neq 0
for every
z
. So by maximum principle,
|p(z)|\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-|F(0)|}{1+|F(0)|}
. We conclude that

|dF(z)|\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-|F(0)|}{1+|F(0)|},\qquad\forall z\in\mathbb{D}.

Since
F
is conformal, the differential
|dF|
is the pointwise metric dilation.
For any
C^{1}
curve
\gamma\subset\mathbb{D}
we have

\operatorname{length}_{\Sigma}(F\circ\gamma)\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-|F(0)|}{1+|F(0)|}\,\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{D}}(\gamma).

Taking infima over all curves joining
p,q\in\mathbb{D}
yields

d_{\Sigma}\big(F(p),F(q)\big)\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-|F(0)|}{1+|F(0)|}\,d_{\mathbb{D}}(p,q).

Equivalently,

d_{\mathbb{D}}\!\big(F^{-1}(x),F^{-1}(y)\big)\;\leq\;2\frac{1+|F(0)|}{1-|F(0)|}\,d_{\Sigma}(x,y),\qquad x,y\in\Sigma.

Thus the inverse map
G=F^{-1}
is
2\frac{1+|F(0)|}{1-|F(0)|}
–Lipschitz with respect to the
intrinsic metric
on
\Sigma
.
∎

Asymptotic behaviour and existence of positive solutions for mixed local nonlocal elliptic equations with Hardy potential
Shammi Malhotra, Sarika Goyal, K. Sreenadh

Abstract

The aim of this paper is twofold. First, we establish uniform asymptotic estimates for solutions of the problem by means of a suitable transformation. Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the following problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u-\mu\frac{u}{|x|^{2}}&=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u&=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(
\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\lambda}
)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded open set of class
C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
0\in\Omega
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
0<s<1<p<2^{*}
with
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
,
\lambda>0
is a parameter,
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional Laplacian operator defined as

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

where P.V. is the Cauchy principle value and
\mu\in(0,\bar{\mu})
where
\bar{\mu}=\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
is the optimal constant in the Hardy’s inequality
[
2
]
which is given by

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}~dx\leq\frac{1}{\bar{\mu}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}~dx\qquad\text{for all }u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

The combination of local and nonlocal operators has recently emerged as a prominent area of research because of its wide-ranging and increasingly recognized applications. In fields such as finance and control theory, modelling often requires incorporating both diffusion and jump components in the underlying Markov processes. This dual nature introduces significant challenges, as the process operates on two distinct scales: the diffusion component dominates at small scales, while the jump component becomes more influential at larger scales. Such operators also play a crucial role in population dynamics. A rigorous mathematical treatment of these models can be found in
[
22
]
.

The analysis of PDEs involving singular potentials is of intrinsic interest. In quantum mechanics, for instance, the Hardy potential characterizes motion and interactive properties such as repulsion and attraction between charged particles (see
[
25
]
). For a comprehensive treatment of the Hardy potential and its wide-ranging applications, we refer the interested reader to the monograph
[
37
]
. It is worth noting that in the context of mixed operators, the Hardy potential is typically chosen to coincide with that associated with the local operator. This choice is motivated by the fact that, for the mixed operator, all potentials of the form
|x|^{-t}
with
t\in[2s,2]
are admissible; see Lemma 3.1 of
[
35
]
. However, among these, the local Hardy potential exhibits the strongest singularity, making it the most natural candidate for perturbative analysis. A rigorous discussion on this can be found in
[
6
]
.

Motivated by the foundational role of the Hardy potential, the study of Brezis-Nirenberg-type problems for Laplacian operator with Hardy potential was initiated by Jannelli
[
32
]
, who investigated the following problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u-\mu\frac{u}{|x|^{2}}&=\lambda u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u&=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

The author proved that if
0<\mu\leq\bar{\mu}-1
, then problem (
1.1
) admits a positive solution for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1})
. In contrast, if
\bar{\mu}-1<\mu<\bar{\mu}
and
\Omega=B_{1}(0)
is the unit ball, there exists a threshold
\lambda_{*}
such that problem (
1.1
) admits a positive solution if and only if
\lambda\in(\lambda_{*},\lambda_{1})
, where
\lambda_{1}
denotes the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian operator with the Hardy potential. This result highlights that every dimension can become critical when
\mu
is close to
\bar{\mu}
, specifically when
\mu\in(\bar{\mu}-1,\bar{\mu})
. This stands in stark contrast to the classical Brezis-Nirenberg problem with
\mu=0
(see
[
10
]
), where only
N=3
is critical. This phenomenon is explained by the guiding principle in
[
32
]
, which asserts that a spatial dimension is critical for a linear elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}
if and only if
\mathcal{L}
admits at least one Green function
G(x_{0},x)\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Subsequently, Cao and Peng
[
12
]
established the existence of sign-changing solutions to problem (
1.1
) for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1})
.
In a related direction, Ferrero and Gazzola
[
23
]
replaced the linear perturbation term
\lambda u
with a more general subcritical nonlinearity
g(x,u)
and established the existence of solutions under suitable conditions. For further results in the local case, we refer the reader to
[
30
]
and references therein.

Transitioning to the purely nonlocal case, various studies have been conducted to understand perturbations of the Hardy potential in the context of the fractional Laplacian. Dipierro et al.
[
21
]
proved the existence of extremals for the fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality and investigated several of their qualitative properties. In particular, they derived asymptotic estimates via a suitable change of variables and analysis of the resulting transformed equation. Ghoussoub et al.
[
28
]
considered the following problem involving the fractional Hardy-Schrödinger operator:

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}u-\mu\frac{u}{|x|^{2s}}&=\lambda u+\frac{|u|^{2^{*}_{s}(\alpha)-2}u}{|x|^{\alpha}}\quad\text{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u&=0\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
\mu\in[0,\Lambda_{N,s}]
,
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{s}(\mu))
with
\lambda_{s,\mu}
denoting the first eigenvalue of the Hardy-Schrödinger operator,
0\leq\alpha<2s<N
, and
2^{*}_{s}(\alpha)=\frac{2(N-\alpha)}{N-2s}
. By introducing the concept of the
internal mass
of the domain, they addressed the critical case and established the existence of least energy solutions to problem (
1.2
) under various conditions on the Hardy term. Later on, Shang et al.
[
38
]
incorporated weights into the nonlinear terms and obtained results concerning the existence and multiplicity of solutions.

Unlike purely local or purely nonlocal cases, the study of mixed local–nonlocal operators involving the Hardy potential is still in the developmental stage. Biagi et al.
[
6
]
investigated the existence, uniqueness, and optimal summability of solutions. Malhotra
[
35
]
studied the Fučík spectrum and shape optimization problems for the first two eigenvalues of such mixed operators.

On the other hand, in the absence of the Hardy potential, Brezis–Nirenberg-type results for the mixed linear operator were first established in
[
5
]
and subsequently generalized to mixed quasilinear operator by Silva et al. in
[
17
]
.

Inspired by the aforementioned work, we started our study with the analysis of the following problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\varepsilon(-\Delta)^{s}u-\mu\frac{u}{|x|^{2}}&=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u&=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

The solutions of the equation (
1.3

S_{\mu,s,\varepsilon}(\Omega):=\inf_{u\neq 0}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\varepsilon[u]_{s}^{2}}{\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{*}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}}.

Thus, the study of the problem (
1.3

[
41
]
or nonlocal case
[
16
]

This can be seen in the following theorem

Theorem 1.1

Let

In fact, a complete classification of positive solutions of the minimization problem for

Now, let

It is a Hilbert space endowed with the norm

0\;\leq\;\frac{1}{p}\Big[(u_{\lambda_{1}}+w_{n})^{p}-u_{\lambda_{1}}^{p}\Big]-w_{n}u_{\lambda_{1}}^{p-1}\;\leq\;\frac{(p-1)}{2}\,\frac{w_{n}^{2}}{u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2-p}}.

Moreover, using AM-GM inequality

\big(u_{\lambda_{1}}(x)-u_{\lambda_{1}}(y)\big)\left(\frac{w_{n}^{2}(x)}{u_{\lambda_{1}}(x)}-\frac{w_{n}^{2}(y)}{u_{\lambda_{1}}(y)}\right)\leq|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{2}.

Since
u_{\lambda_{1}}
is a solution of (

P

μ
,
λ

ε

) with
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
, using the above inequality together with Picone’s identity, we deduce

\displaystyle\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{X}_{0},\varepsilon}^{2}\;\geq\;\varepsilon\Big\langle u_{\lambda_{1}},\frac{w_{n}^{2}}{u_{\lambda_{1}}}\Big\rangle_{s}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\!\left(\frac{w_{n}^{2}}{u_{\lambda_{1}}}\right)\nabla u_{\lambda_{1}}\,dx\;\geq\;\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{w_{n}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx+\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{w_{n}^{2}}{u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2-p}}\,dx.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{X}_{0},\varepsilon}^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{w_{n}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{w_{n}^{2}}{u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2-p}}\;\geq\;\frac{2\lambda_{0}}{p-1}\int_{\widetilde{B}_{n}}\left[\frac{(u_{\lambda_{1}}+w_{n})^{p}-u_{\lambda_{1}}^{p}}{p}-w_{n}u_{\lambda_{1}}^{p-1}\right].

(5.37)

Furthermore, we have the following inequality

0\;\leq\;\frac{1}{2^{*}}\Big[(u_{\lambda_{1}}+w_{n})^{2^{*}}-u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2^{*}}\Big]-w_{n}u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2^{*}-1}\;\leq\;C\left(u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2^{*}-2}w_{n}^{2}+w_{n}^{2^{*}}\right).

(5.38)

Plugging (
5.37
) and (
5.38
) into (
5.36
), we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda_{0}}^{\varepsilon,+}(u_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda_{0}}^{\varepsilon,+}(v_{n})+\frac{2-p}{2}\left[\varepsilon[w_{n}]_{s}^{2}+\int_{\widetilde{B}_{n}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}-\mu\int_{\widetilde{B}_{n}}\frac{w_{n}^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}\right]-C\int_{\Omega}\Big(u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2^{*}-2}w_{n}^{2}+w_{n}^{2^{*}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda_{0}}^{\varepsilon,+}(v_{n})+C\|w_{n}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0},\varepsilon}^{2}-C\int_{\widetilde{B}_{n}}\Big(u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2^{*}-2}w_{n}^{2}+w_{n}^{2^{*}}\Big)dx.

Moreover, using the idea of Lemma
7
of
[
39
]
and
u_{0}=u_{\lambda_{0}}
, we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\widetilde{B}_{n}|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\widetilde{B}_{n}}\Big(u_{\lambda_{1}}^{2^{*}-2}w_{n}^{2}+w_{n}^{2^{*}}\Big)dx\leq o_{n}(1)\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{X}_{0},\varepsilon}^{2}.

Hence, we finally conclude

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda_{0}}^{\varepsilon,+}(u_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\;\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda_{0}}^{\varepsilon,+}(v_{n})+\big(C-o_{n}(1)\big)\|w_{n}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0},\varepsilon}^{2}\geq\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda_{0}}^{\varepsilon,+}(u_{0}),

for large
n
. This gives us the contradiction. Thus,
u_{0}
is a local minimum for
\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda_{0}}^{\varepsilon,+}
.
∎

Next, we examine the existence of a second positive solution of the form

u=u_{\lambda}+v.

The equation for
v
becomes

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta v+\varepsilon(-\Delta)^{s}v-\mu\frac{v}{|x|^{2}}&=\lambda(u_{\lambda}+v)^{p-1}+(u_{\lambda}+v)^{2^{*}-1}-\lambda u_{\lambda}^{p-1}-u_{\lambda}^{2^{*}-1},&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
v&=0,&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.39)

Define the function
g(x,t)
as

g(x,t)=\begin{cases}(u_{\lambda}+t)^{2^{*}-1}-u_{\lambda}^{2^{*}-1}+\lambda\left[(u_{\lambda}+t)^{p-1}-u_{\lambda}^{p-1}\right],&\text{if }t>0,\\
0,&\text{if }t\leq 0,\end{cases}

and its primitive

G(v)=\int_{0}^{v}g(x,t)\,dt.

The associated energy functional becomes

\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}+\varepsilon[v]_{s}^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{v^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right]-\int_{\Omega}G(v)\,dx.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the critical points of
\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
and the weak solutions of problem (
5.39
).
We now prove the existence of a nontrivial solution
v
using a contradiction argument. Assume that
v=0
is the only solution of (
5.39
).

Lemma 5.5
.

The functional
\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
has a local minimum at
v=0
in
\mathcal{X}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
w\in\mathcal{X}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then using the fact that
\langle(\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(u_{\lambda}),w^{+}\rangle=0
and rearranging terms, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{w^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}[w]^{2}_{s}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}\left[(u_{\lambda}+w^{+})^{2^{*}}-u_{\lambda}^{2^{*}}-2^{*}u_{\lambda}^{2^{*}-1}w^{+}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left[(u_{\lambda}+w^{+})^{p}-u_{\lambda}^{p}-pu_{\lambda}^{p-1}w^{+}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\lambda}+w^{+})-\mathcal{J}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\lambda})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{-}|^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{(w^{-})^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}[w^{-}]_{s}^{2}+\varepsilon\langle w^{+},w^{-}\rangle_{s}.

Since
u_{\lambda}
is a local minimizer and

\frac{1}{2}[w^{-}]_{s}^{2}+\langle w^{+},w^{-}\rangle_{s}=\frac{1}{2}[w]_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}[w^{+}]_{s}^{2}\geq 0,

we obtain the inequality

\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(w)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{-}|^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{(w^{-})^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right),

for
\|w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0},\varepsilon}
small enough. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

If
v=0
is the only critical point of
\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
, then
\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the Palais–Smale condition (PS)
c
for any
c<\frac{1}{N}S_{\mu}^{N/2}
.

Proof.

Let
\{v_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{X}_{0}(\Omega)
be a (PS)
c
sequence. Then

\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(v_{n})\to c<\frac{1}{N}S_{\mu}^{N/2},\quad(\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(v_{n})\to 0\quad\text{in }(\mathcal{X}_{0})^{\prime}.

Then using Hardy inequality and Hölder inequality, we compute

\displaystyle c+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\|u_{\lambda}+v_{n}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0},\varepsilon}\geq 2^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(v_{n})-\langle(\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(v_{n}),u_{\lambda}+v_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2^{*}}{2}-1\right)\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\varepsilon[v_{n}]^{2}_{s}\right]+(2^{*}-2)\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{2^{*}-1}v_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{2^{*}-1}v_{n}^{-}+\lambda\left(\frac{2^{*}}{p}-1\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{p}+\lambda(2^{*}-2)\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{p-1}v_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle+\lambda\left(\frac{p-2^{*}}{p}\right)\int_{\Omega}(u_{\lambda}+v_{n}^{+})^{p}+\lambda\int_{\Omega}v_{n}^{p-1}v_{n}^{-}.

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{2}{N-2}\right)\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\bar{\mu}}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}+\varepsilon[v_{n}]_{s}^{2}\right)+C\lambda\frac{(p-2^{*})}{p}\|u_{\lambda}+v_{n}^{+}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}^{p}.

Since
1<p<2
, this implies that
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Thus, passing to a subsequence if necessary, we have

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup v_{0}\quad\quad\;\text{in }\mathcal{X}_{0},

(5.40)

\displaystyle v_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\to v_{0}(x)\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle\to v_{0}\quad\quad\;\text{in }L^{t}(\Omega)\text{ for all }1<t<2^{*}.

Clearly,
v_{0}
is a critical point of
\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
. But our assumption implies
v_{0}=0
.

Using the Brézis–Lieb Lemma and (
5.40
), we deduce

\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(v_{n})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}[v_{n}]_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}^{+})^{2^{*}}+o_{n}(1).

(5.41)

Also, we compute

\left\langle(\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(v_{n}),u_{\lambda}+v_{n}\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}+\varepsilon[v_{n}]_{s}^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}^{+})^{2^{*}}+o_{n}(1).

Now, we can assume that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}+\varepsilon[v_{n}]_{s}^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\to b,

and

\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}^{+})^{2^{*}}\to b,\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

If
b=0
, the proof is complete. If
b\neq 0
, then by the definition of the Sobolev constant
S_{\mu}
, we have

b\geq S_{\mu}b^{2/2^{*}},

i.e.,
b\geq S_{\mu}^{N/2}
. Thus, using (
5.41
) we obtain the inequality

c=o_{n}(1)+\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(v_{n})=\frac{b}{N}+o_{n}(1)\geq\frac{S_{\mu}^{N/2}}{N}+o_{n}(1),

which contradicts our earlier assumption on the energy level
c
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We define the min–max value as

c^{*}_{\lambda}=\inf_{h\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(h(t)),

where
\Gamma=\left\{h\in C([0,1],\mathcal{X}_{0}(\Omega)):h(0)=0,\,h(1)=t_{0}u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}\right\}
, and
t_{0}
is chosen, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that
\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(t_{0}u_{\varepsilon,\alpha})<0
.

We aim to show the existence of a nontrivial critical point of
\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
using the Mountain Pass Theorem (see
[
27
, Theorem
1
]
). To accomplish this, it remains only to verify that

c^{*}_{\lambda}<\frac{1}{N}S_{\mu}^{N/2}.

Using the standard inequality
(b+d)^{n}\geq b^{n}+d^{n}+nb^{n-1}d
, for
n>1;\;b,d>0
,
we deduce that

g(x,a)\geq a^{p}+pu_{\lambda}^{p-1}a.

Choosing
\delta
small enough such that

u_{\lambda}\geq M_{0}>0\quad\text{in }B_{2\delta}\setminus\{0\}

for some
M_{0}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. Then using the functions
u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}
defined in subsection
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(tu_{\varepsilon,\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}|^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\varepsilon[u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}]_{s}^{2}-M_{0}^{2^{*}-2}(2^{*}-1)\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}^{2}\,dx\right)-\frac{t^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}^{2^{*}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}A-\frac{t^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}B

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\frac{A^{2^{*}/(2^{*}-2)}}{B^{2/(2^{*}-2)}}

Using (
4.28
), (
4.27
), (
4.30
), and (
4.29
) with
r=2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(tu_{\varepsilon,\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\frac{\left(S_{\mu}^{N/2}+C\varepsilon^{\alpha(N-2)}+C\varepsilon^{1+2\alpha(1-s)\sqrt{\bar{\mu}}/\sqrt{\bar{\mu}-\mu}}-C\varepsilon^{2\alpha\sqrt{\bar{\mu}}/\sqrt{\bar{\mu}-\mu}}\right)^{2^{*}/(2^{*}-2)}}{\left(S_{\mu}^{N/2}-C\varepsilon^{\alpha N}\right)^{2/(2^{*}-2)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}S_{\mu}^{N/2}\frac{(1-C\varepsilon^{2\alpha\sqrt{\bar{\mu}}/\sqrt{\bar{\mu}-\mu}})^{N/2}}{(1-C\varepsilon^{\alpha N})^{(N-2)/2}}<\frac{1}{N}S_{\mu}^{N/2}.

provided that
\mu<\bar{\mu}-1
and we choose
\alpha
such that
\min\{\alpha(N-2),1+2\alpha(1-s)\sqrt{\bar{\mu}}/\sqrt{\bar{\mu}-\mu}\}>2\alpha\sqrt{\bar{\mu}}/\sqrt{\bar{\mu}-\mu}
.
Therefore, we conclude

c^{*}_{\lambda}\leq\max_{t>0}\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(tu_{\varepsilon,\alpha})<\frac{1}{N}S_{\mu}^{N/2}.\qed

Abstract
. In the early nineties, R. M. Aron, B. Cole, T. Gamelin and W.B. Johnson initiated the study of the maximal ideal space (spectrum) of Banach algebras of holomorphic functions defined on the open unit ball of an infinite dimensional complex Banach space. Within this framework, we investigate the fibers and Gleason parts of the spectrum of the algebra of holomorphic and uniformly continuous functions on the unit ball of ℓ p \ell_{p} ( 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty ). We show that the inherent geometry of these spaces provides a fundamental ingredient for our results. We prove that whenever p ∈ ℕ p\in\mathbb{N} ( p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 ), the fiber of every z ∈ B ℓ p z\in B_{\ell_{p}} contains a set of cardinal 2 𝔠 2^{\mathfrak{c}} such that any two elements of this set belong to different Gleason parts. For the case p = 1 p=1 , we complete the known description of the fibers, showing that, for each z ∈ B ¯ ℓ 1 ′′ ∖ S ℓ 1 z\in\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\setminus S_{\ell_{1}} , the fiber over z z is not a singleton. Also, we establish that different fibers over elements in S ℓ 1 ′′ S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}} cannot share Gleason parts.

1.
Introduction

Over the years, for the study of the maximal ideal space (or spectrum) of an algebra of holomorphic functions, two partitions of this set have proved useful in describing its analytical structure. On the one hand, the notion of
part
, introduced by Gleason
[
22
]
in the 1950’s, comes from an equivalence relation arising from the metric of the surrounding space. On the other hand, the idea of
fiber
, developed since the foundational work of Schark
[
35
]
, refers to the restriction of homomorphisms to the dual of the underlying space. These two approaches to analyzing the structure of the spectrum have followed separate paths, with some meeting points in certain works. The main goal of this article is to contribute to the description and interrelation between fibers and Gleason parts of the spectrum of the algebra of holomorphic and uniformly continuous functions on the unit ball of
\ell_{p}
(
1\leq p<\infty
).

In the context of several complex variables, the study of Gleason parts was carried on the maximal ideal space of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B)
(the algebra of bounded holomorphic functions on
B
), where
B
is the open unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
, the polydisk or the Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. There is extensive literature on this research, for instance,
[
11
,
21
,
25
,
26
,
29
,
31
,
36
]
. When restricting to
\mathcal{A}(B)
, the algebra of holomorphic functions on
B
which are continuous on its closure
\overline{B}
, the spectrum
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}(B))=\overline{B}
and its Gleason parts are easily described as
B
and
\{\delta_{z}\}
for every
z
in the boundary of
B
. The situation is far more involved in the infinite dimensional framework. Whenever
B_{X}
is the open unit ball of an infinite dimensional Banach space
X
, the study of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
and of some of its closed subalgebras, as
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
(uniformly continuous holomorphic functions on
B_{X}
), was initiated in 1991 with the works of Aron, Cole, Gamelin, Johnson and others, starting with the seminal paper
[
7
]
. In this setting most of the work was addressed to study the size of the fibers, the Shilov and Choquet boundaries, the cluster theorem, and the containment of big analytic structures inside the fibers
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
5
,
9
,
12
,
34
,
15
,
16
,
19
,
23
,
28
,
27
]
. It is interesting to mention that if there is a continuous polynomial on
X
which is not weakly continuous on bounded sets, then the fibers of the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
over any point in
B_{X^{\prime\prime}}
are very rich, thus revealing a relevant difference with the finite-dimensional case.

To continue this introduction, let us establish some notation and definitions. Consider an infinite dimensional complex Banach space
X
with open unit ball
B_{X}
, unit sphere
S_{X}
, closed unit ball
\overline{B}_{X}
, and topological dual and bidual spaces
X^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. Inside
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
(the algebra of all bounded holomorphic mappings on
B_{X}
) we are interested in
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
, the algebra of holomorphic functions
f\colon B_{X}\to\mathbb{C}
that are uniformly continuous with respect to the norm of
X
. Additionally,
\mathcal{A}_{a}(B_{X})
stands for the closed subalgebra of the latter space generated by
X^{\prime}
and the constant functions. Thus, the inclusions
\mathcal{A}_{a}(B_{X})\subset\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})\subset\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
hold, and the three function spaces are complex Banach algebras endowed with the supremum norm,
\|f\|=\sup\{|f(x)|\colon\|x\|<1\}
.

For a commutative complex Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
, its maximal ideal space (or spectrum for short)
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
is the set of all nonzero
\mathbb{C}
-valued homomorphisms.
As each function in
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
extends continuously to
S_{X}
, then

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
contains the point evaluations
\delta_{x}
for all
x\in X,\|x\|\leq 1
.
Furthermore, according to
[
17
]
, every
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
extends (via the extension developed in
[
4
]
) to
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X^{\prime\prime}})
in a natural way. We refer to this mapping
f\leadsto\tilde{f}
as the canonical extension, that constitutes a Banach algebra homomorphism. Using standard arguments, it can be seen that this extension maps functions in
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
to functions in
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X^{\prime\prime}})
. Thus, each point
z\in B_{X^{\prime\prime}}
(or
\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
) corresponds to an element
\tilde{\delta}_{z}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X}))
(or
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})))
, where
\tilde{\delta}_{z}(f)=\tilde{f}(z)
. As
X^{\prime}
is included in
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
,
\tilde{\delta}_{z}\neq\tilde{\delta}_{w}
whenever
z\neq w
belong to
B_{X^{\prime\prime}}
(or to
\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
for
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
).
To simplify the notation, we write
\delta_{z}(f)
instead of
\tilde{\delta}_{z}(f)
for any
z\in B_{X^{\prime\prime}}
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
and also for any
z\in S_{X^{\prime\prime}}
and
f\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
, as in this case,
\tilde{f}(z)
is uniquely determined.

In general, for infinite-dimensional Banach spaces, it is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
) that
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
usually contains much more than mere evaluations at points of
\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
. However, there are some distinguished cases, such as when
X=c_{0}
(the Banach space of complex null sequences), in which
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{c_{0}}))=\{\delta_{z}\colon z\in\overline{B}_{\ell_{\infty}}\}
. This result, which could be traced to
[
18
]
, (see also
[
8
, Introduction to Section 2]
), is a particular case of a Corona theorem. Loosely speaking, we say that a Corona theorem holds when the evaluation homomorphisms form a dense set in the spectrum of the considered algebra. It was Carleson
[
14
]
, who set the first Corona theorem for
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, with
\mathbb{D}
the complex unit disk. For finite-dimensional Banach spaces, the Corona theorem trivially holds for
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
, but it is an open question for general infinite dimensional ones, even for the classical
\ell_{p}
-spaces,
1\leq p<\infty
.

To achieve a better understanding of the extent of the evaluation morphisms in the spectrum, weaker structures can be used. That is the case of the cluster set
Cl_{B_{X}}(f,z)
at
z\in\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
, defined as the set of all limits of values of
f
along nets in
B_{X}
converging weak-star to
z
. A
Cluster value theorem
at

means the assertion

Cl_{B_{X}}(f,z)=\widehat{f}(\mathcal{M}_{z}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))),\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}),

where
\widehat{f}
is the Gelfand transform of
f
and
\mathcal{M}_{z}
is the fiber in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
over
z
(see Section
2
for the definitions). It is proved in
[
5
, Thm. 3.1]
that
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}})
satisfies the Cluster value theorem at
z=0
for every
1<p<+\infty
and it is shown in
[
5
, Thm. 4.1]
that
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{2}})
satisfies the Cluster value theorem at any point of the unit ball.
For more information about the Corona and Cluster theorems, we refer the reader to the survey
[
13
]
.

We aim to study the structure of the fibers and Gleason parts (see precise definitions below) of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))

, the polynomial

The fiber over any element

while the fiber over any

2.
Preliminaries and general results

For any commutative Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
with identity, its spectrum
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
considered with the
\sigma(\mathcal{A}^{*},\mathcal{A})
topology (
w^{*}
for short) is a compact Hausdorff space. Via the Gelfand transform
f\mapsto\hat{f}
,
\mathcal{A}
is identified with a subspace of the space of continuous functions of
C(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}))
, where
\hat{f}(\varphi)=\varphi(f)
for any
f\in\mathcal{A}
and any
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
.

Either for
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
or
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
, the mapping
\pi\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})\to\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
given by
\pi(\varphi):=\varphi|_{X^{\prime}}
, is a well-defined linear operator (as
X^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A}
) and is surjective
[
7
]
. With the natural embedding
J_{X}\colon X\to X^{\prime\prime}
, we have the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{B_{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\scriptstyle{J_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}.}

Conventionally, omitting the algebra when it is clear, the
fiber of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
over
z\in\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
,
will be denoted as

(1)

\mathcal{M}_{z}:=\mathcal{M}_{z}(\mathcal{A})=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})\ |\ \pi(\varphi)=z\}.

Understanding the structure of these fibers is beneficial in the examination of Gleason parts that are defined as follows. First, we recall the Gleason distance between
\varphi
and
\psi
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
, given by
\|\varphi-\psi\|:=\sup\{|\varphi(f)-\psi(f)|\colon\|f\|\leq 1\}
. Clearly,
\|\varphi-\psi\|\leq 2
and it can be seen, with some work, that the relation
\varphi\sim\psi
iff
\|\varphi-\psi\|<2
is an equivalence relation in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
, see for instance
[
36
, Lem. 16.1]
. The sets of equivalence classes are referred to as the Gleason parts of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
. We write

(2)

\mathcal{GP}(\varphi):=\{\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})\colon\ \|\varphi-\psi\|<2\}

for the Gleason part containing
\varphi
.
A Gleason part with a unique element is called trivial or a
singleton
.

When trying to figure out whether two elements of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
belong or not to the same Gleason part, the pseudo-hyperbolic distance comes into play. Recall that the
pseudo-hyperbolic distance
between
\varphi
and
\psi
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
is

\rho(\varphi,\psi):=\sup\{|\varphi(f)|\colon\ f\in\mathcal{A},\|f\|\leq 1,\psi(f)=0\}.

For the uni-dimensional unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, when
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
or
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, the pseudo-hyperbolic distance for
\lambda
and
\mu
in
\mathbb{D}
is give by

\rho(\delta_{\lambda},\delta_{\mu})=\Big|\frac{\lambda-\mu}{1-\overline{\lambda}\mu}\Big|.

The formula given above remains true if
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
for
\lambda,\mu\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, if
|\lambda|=1
and
\lambda\neq\mu
, in this case,
\rho(\delta_{\lambda},\delta_{\mu})=1
. Also, it extends coordinatewise to
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{c_{0}})
and
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})

[
8
, Example 1.7]
. See Examples 1.8 and 1.9 in
[
8
]
for the pseudo-hyperbolic distance between
\delta_{x}
and
\delta_{y}
in
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
and
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
, for
X=\ell_{2}
and
X=\mathcal{L}(H)
where
\mathcal{L}(H)
is the Banach space of bounded linear operators from a Hilbert space
H
into itself.

It follows from
[
11
, Thm. 2.8]
that
\|\varphi-\psi\|<2
iff
\rho(\varphi,\psi)<1
, thus for the Gleason part containing
\varphi
, we will use (
2
) or its equivalent form

(3)

\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)=\{\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})\colon\rho(\varphi,\psi)<1\}.

Now, we are in a position to establish some basic results about fibers and Gleason parts that will be used throughout this work.
The following result is another way to present
[
36
, Thm. 3.9]
. We give a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
or
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
and let
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
. Suppose that there is a net
(f_{\beta})_{\beta}\subset\mathcal{A}
such that
\|f_{\beta}\|\leq 1
for all
\beta
,
\varphi(f_{\beta})\to\lambda_{0}
for some
\lambda_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{1\}
and
\psi(f_{\beta})\to 1
. Then
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi)
.

Proof.

For
0<\alpha<1
consider the Moëbius function
\xi_{\alpha}\colon\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,

\xi_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-\lambda}{1-\alpha\lambda}
. As
\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D})
,
\xi_{\alpha}\circ f\in\mathcal{A}
for all
\alpha
and all
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
\|f\|\leq 1
. Since,
\xi_{\alpha}(\lambda)=(\alpha-\lambda)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(\alpha\lambda)^{m}
and this series converges uniformly on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, we see that
\phi(\xi_{\alpha}\circ f)=\xi_{\alpha}(\phi(f))
, for every
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
and
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
\|f\|\leq 1
. Hence,

\varphi(\xi_{\alpha}\circ f_{\beta})=\xi_{\alpha}(\varphi(f_{\beta}))\underset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}\xi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{0})\underset{\alpha\to 1^{-}}{\longrightarrow}1.

On the other hand,

\psi(\xi_{\alpha}\circ f_{\beta})=\xi_{\alpha}(\psi(f_{\beta}))\underset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}\xi_{\alpha}(1)=-1.

As
\|\xi_{\alpha}\circ f_{\beta}\|\leq 1
for every
\alpha
and
\beta
, we see that

\|\varphi-\psi\|\geq|\varphi(\xi_{\alpha}\circ f_{\beta})-\psi(\xi_{\alpha}\circ f_{\beta})|,

and the result follows.

This type of behavior leads us to face different natural questions about fibers, Gleason parts, and their interaction. Below we list some of them.

•

Which fibers or Gleason parts are nontrivial? That is, not a singleton.

•

Which fibers intersect several Gleason parts?

•

Which fibers contain singleton Gleason parts?

•

When is there a copy of a ball (or a disk) at the intersection of a fiber and a Gleason part?

•

Which Gleason parts intersect different fibers?

Addressing these questions poses a challenging task, even if we concentrate on
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
for
X=\ell_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
. Next, we will present some general results aimed at providing tools to make progress on some of the aforementioned issues.

The following proposition provides us with sufficient conditions to ensure that two homomorphisms that are in the non-corona part of the spectrum of the algebra belong to different Gleason parts. Recall that for
f\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
we denote by
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X^{\prime\prime}})
its canonical extension.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and let
\varphi\not=\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
such that
\varphi=w^{*}-\lim\delta_{z_{\alpha}}
,
\psi=w^{*}-\lim\delta_{w_{\alpha}}
with
(z_{\alpha}),(w_{\alpha})\subset\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
.

(a)

If there exists
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,X)
such that
\|T\|\leq 1
,
T^{\prime\prime}(z_{\alpha})=z_{\alpha}
,
T^{\prime\prime}(w_{\alpha})=0
for all
\alpha
and
\varphi\not\in\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{0})
then
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi)
.

(b)

If there exists
P\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}X)
,
\|P\|=1
, such that
\widetilde{P}(z_{\alpha})\to 1
and
w_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}
with
\lambda_{\alpha}^{m}\not\to 1
then
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi)
.

Proof.

To prove (
a
), note that for any
f\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
and
T
as in the statement, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi(f\circ T)&=\lim\limits_{\alpha}\delta_{z_{\alpha}}(f\circ T)=\lim\limits_{\alpha}\widetilde{f\circ T}(z_{\alpha})=\lim\limits_{\alpha}\widetilde{f}(T^{\prime\prime}(z_{\alpha}))\\
&=\lim\limits_{\alpha}\widetilde{f}(z_{\alpha})=\varphi(f)\quad\textrm{and}\\
\psi(f\circ T)&=\lim\limits_{\alpha}\delta_{w_{\alpha}}(f\circ T)=\lim\limits_{\alpha}\widetilde{f\circ T}(w_{\alpha})=\lim\limits_{\alpha}\widetilde{f}(T^{\prime\prime}(w_{\alpha}))=f(0).\end{array}

As
\varphi\not\in\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{0})
, there exists a net
(f_{\beta})\subset\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
such that
\|f_{\beta}\|\leq 1
,
f_{\beta}(0)=0
and
\varphi(f_{\beta})\to 1
. Now,
(f_{\beta}\circ T)\subset\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
with
\|f_{\beta}\circ T\|\leq 1
,
\varphi(f_{\beta}\circ T)=\varphi(f_{\beta})\to 1
and
\psi(f_{\beta}\circ T)=f_{\beta}(0)=0
, showing that
\varphi
and
\psi
belong to different Gleason parts.

For (
b
), with
P
as in the statement, observe that

\varphi(P)=\lim_{\alpha}\delta_{z_{\alpha}}(P)=\lim\widetilde{P}(z_{\alpha})=1.

On the other hand, taking a subnet if necessary, we may consider that
\lambda_{\alpha}^{m}\to\lambda\not=1
. Hence,

\lim_{\alpha}\delta_{w_{\alpha}}(P)=\lim_{\alpha}\widetilde{P}(\lambda_{\alpha}z_{\alpha})=\lim_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}^{m}\widetilde{P}(z_{\alpha})=\lambda.

Now, an application of Lemma
2.1
concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The Gleason metric on the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
is determined by the normed space of all continuous polynomials
\mathcal{P}(X)
. That is,
if
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
then

\|\varphi-\psi\|=\sup\{|\varphi(P)-\psi(P)|\colon\,P\in\mathcal{P}(X),\,\|P\|\leq 1\}.

Indeed, if
f\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
, the Taylor series expansion converges uniformly to
f
on
rB_{X}
for any
0<r<1
. Then we can proceed as in
[
32
, Prop. 5.2]
to derive that the sequence of Cesaro means of the Taylor series expansion of
f
at 0, say
(Q_{n})
, converges uniformly on
rB_{X}
to
f
for all
0<r<1
, and
\|Q_{n}\|\leq\|f\|
for every
n
. On the other hand, by using the uniform continuity of
f
, the family
(f_{r})
with
f_{r}(x):=f(rx)
converges uniformly to
f
on
B_{X}
. Additionally, for each
r
, the sequence
(Q_{n,r})_{n}
defined by
Q_{n,r}(x):=Q_{n}(rx)
converges uniformly to
f_{r}
on
B_{X}
with

\|Q_{n,r}\|\leq\|Q_{n}\|\leq\|f\|.

Thus, for every
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi-\psi\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{|(\varphi-\psi)(f)|\colon\ f\in{\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})},\ \|f\|\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{|(\varphi-\psi)(P)|\colon\ P\in\mathcal{P}(X),\ \|P\|\leq 1\},

which shows the statement.

In the study of the fibers or Gleason parts, certain elements in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
possess geometric properties that make them distinctive, as they constitute singletons. By
[
36
, Pag. 162]
, this is the case of the strong boundary points and peak points that we define next. A point
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
is a
strong boundary point
for
\mathcal{A}
if for every open neighborhood
U\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
of
\varphi
there is
f\in\mathcal{A}
so that
\|f\|=\hat{f}(\varphi)=1
and
|\hat{f}(\psi)|<1
if
\psi\not\in U
. Also,
\varphi
is a
peak point
for
\mathcal{A}
if there is
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\hat{f}(\varphi)=1
and
|\hat{f}(\psi)|<1
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})\setminus\varphi
.

For a function algebra
\mathcal{A}
contained in
C_{b}(\Omega)
, the space of complex bounded continuous functions on
\Omega
, a topological Hausdorff space, we may consider the class of elements described above for the particular case of points in
\Omega
(rather than for homomorphisms in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
). This concept appeared related to the study of a generalization of the notion of boundary due to Globevnik
[
23
,
24
]
.
A point
x\in\Omega
is a
strong boundary point
for
\mathcal{A}
(in the sense of Globevnik) if for each open neighborhood
U\subset\Omega
of
x
, there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that
|f(x)|=1
and
\sup_{y\in\Omega\setminus U}|f(y)|<1
. On the other hand,
x
is a
strong peak point
for
\mathcal{A}
(in the sense of Globevnik) if there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying
|f(x)|=1
and
\sup_{y\in\Omega\setminus U}|f(y)|<1
for all open neighborhoods
U\subset\Omega
of
x
.

The setting makes clear which definition is used. When we talk about
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
we use the classical definitions (even for evaluations
\delta_{x}
) and for points
x\in\Omega
we use the notions in the sense of Globevnik.

The classes of extreme points cannot be avoided in studying geometric properties. For
C\subset X
a convex set, a point
z
in
C
is a
real extreme point
of
C
if
z
is not the midpoint of any line segment contained in
C
. Equivalently, if
z+ty\in C
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
|t|\leq 1
, then
y=0
. Also,
z
in
C
is a
complex extreme point
of
C
if
z+\lambda y\in C
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
implies that
y=0
. The real extreme points of
C
are denoted by
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(C)
while the complex extreme points are denoted by
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(C)
. Clearly we have
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(C)\subset\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(C)
.

When considering the intersection between fibers and Gleason parts, complex extreme points of the unit ball of
X^{\prime\prime}
are relevant.
For the uniform algebra on
\overline{B}_{X}
generated by
X^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{A}_{a}(B_{X})
, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{M}_{z}(\mathcal{A}_{a}(B_{X}))=\{\delta_{z}\}
, for every
z\in\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
. Therefore, the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}_{a}(B_{X})
is identified with
\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
, and the fibers are singletons. On the other hand, a result of Arenson,
[
3
, Thm.]
, shows that the strong boundary points for this algebra coincide with the set
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}})
.
Rewriting the statement of
[
3
, Thm.]
in our words, Arenson’s result applies to the closed algebra generated by functions in
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X^{\prime\prime}})
which are canonical extensions of functions in
\mathcal{A}_{a}(B_{X})
. There, it is proved that the strong boundary points for this algebra coincide with the set
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}})
. That is,
z\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}})
if and only if for each open
w^{*}
-neighborhood
U
of
z
in
\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}_{a}(B_{X})
such that
\|f\|=\tilde{f}(z)=1
and
|\tilde{f}(w)|<1
for all
w\in\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}\setminus U
. As a consequence, for
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
, if
w\not=z\in S_{X^{\prime\prime}}
then
\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{w})\not=\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{z})
. Moreover, in this case
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi)
for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{z},\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{w}
. On the other hand, if
z\in S_{X^{\prime\prime}}
such that
z\not\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}})
then it should exist
u\not=0\in X^{\prime\prime}
such that
\|z+\lambda u\|=1
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Thus, the mapping

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\delta_{z+\lambda u}

is analytic. Since the image of an open convex set through an analytic injection is contained in a single Gleason part (
[
26
, Lem. 2.1]
or
[
8
, Prop. 3.4]
), we obtain that
\{\delta_{z+\lambda u}:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}\}\subset\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{z})
. We state these facts in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
z\in S_{X^{\prime\prime}}
. For the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
we have

(a)

If
z\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}})
then
\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{z})
cannot intersect any other fiber. Indeed,
for any
w\not=z\in S_{X^{\prime\prime}}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{z},\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{w}
it holds
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi)
.

(b)

If
z\not\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}})
then
\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{z})
intersects a disk of fibers: there is
u\not=0\in X^{\prime\prime}
such that
\{\delta_{z+\lambda u}:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}\}\subset\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{z})
.

3.
Fibers and Gleason parts of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
,
1<p<\infty
.

An important example in our context arises when
X
is a complex uniformly convex space. In this case it is proved in
[
19
, Prop. 4.1]
that
z
is a strong peak point in the sense of Globevnik for
\mathcal{A}_{a}(B_{X})
. Now, applying
[
15
, Thm. 3.3 (2)]
we get:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a uniformly convex complex Banach space. Then, for any
z\in S_{X}
, the evaluation
\delta_{z}
is a peak point for
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
and

\mathcal{M}_{z}=\{\delta_{z}\}=\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{z}).

In particular, the above result applies to
X=\ell_{p}
for
1<p<\infty
.

The next result summarizes what else is known about fibers and Gleason parts of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
, for
1<p<\infty
. From
[
9
, Thm. 3.1]
, we know that for any
z\in B_{\ell_{p}}
, the complex disk
\mathbb{D}
can be analytically injected in
\mathcal{M}_{z}
. Moreover, the construction of the copy of such a disk contains the evaluation
\delta_{z}
. By Remark
2.2
, since
\delta_{z}
and
\delta_{0}
belong to the same Gleason part, we derive item (
a
) below. The same reasoning yields item (
b
) below when applied to
[
9
, Prop. 3.10]
.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

In
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
we have:

(a)

For any
z\in B_{\ell_{p}}
, there is a copy of a unit disk
\mathbb{D}
in
\mathcal{M}_{z}\cap\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{0})
.

(b)

There is a copy of
B_{\ell_{p}}
in
\mathcal{M}_{0}\cap\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{0})
.

We now prove that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
intersects lots of Gleason parts. First we introduce the following notation. For a point
\varphi
and a set
E
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
,
we write
\varphi\in w^{*}-{\rm ac}E
to mean that
\varphi
is a
w^{*}
-accumulation point of
E
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The fiber
\mathcal{M}_{0}
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
contains a set of cardinal
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
such that any two elements of this set belong to different Gleason parts.

Proof.

We first give a sequence
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k}
in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
by taking

\{\mathbb{N}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
a countable partition of

\mathbb{N}
such that each
\mathbb{N}_{k}
is infinite. For each
k
, take
\psi_{k}\in w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{\delta_{e_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{k}\}
that clearly belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. We claim that
\mathcal{GP}(\psi_{k})\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi_{l})
for any
k\neq l
. To see this, pick
m\geq p
and consider, for each
k
, the
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}})
, defined by
P_{k}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{k}}x_{n}^{m}
satisfying that
\|P_{k}\|=1
.
As
\psi_{k}(P_{k})=1
and
\psi_{l}(P_{k})=0
for any
l\neq k
,
\rho(\psi_{k},\psi_{l})=1
, and the claim follows.

Now, let us show that
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k}
is an interpolating sequence. Take
m\geq p
,
(c_{k})_{k}\in\overline{B}_{\ell_{\infty}}
and define the
m
-homogeneous polynomial
Q\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}})
as
Q(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{k}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{k}}x_{n}^{m}
. Since for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{k}
,
\delta_{e_{n}}(Q)=c_{k}
we have
\psi_{k}(Q)=c_{k}
. Hence, the conclusion holds.

This means that the set
\mathcal{C}=\overline{\{\psi_{k}\}_{k}}^{w^{*}}
, which is included in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, is homeomorphic to
\beta(\mathbb{N})
, the Stone–Cech compactification of
\mathbb{N}
, that has cardinal
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
. As any element of
\mathcal{C}
belongs to
w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{\delta_{e_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, for
\varphi\neq\psi\in\mathcal{C}
, we can find disjoint subsets
\mathbb{N}_{\varphi}\not=\mathbb{N}_{\psi}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi\in w^{*}-\text{ac}\{\delta_{e_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{\varphi}\}
and
\psi\in w^{*}-\text{ac}\{\delta_{e_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{\psi}\}
. Finally, proceeding as in the first part of the proof we obtain that
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi)
.
∎

We can extend this result to all the fibers over points inside the ball in the case that
p
is an integer. We proceed in two steps.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\geq 2
and let
z=(z_{n})_{n}\in B_{\ell_{p}}
,
z_{n}\geq 0
for all
n
, and
(a_{n})_{n}
a sequence such that
a_{n}>0
and
\|z+a_{n}e_{n}\|_{p}=1
. Then, for
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
, any
\varphi,\psi\in w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{\delta_{z+a_{n}e_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that
\varphi\neq\psi
,
satisfy that
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{z}
and
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi)
.

Proof.

It is clear that any element
\phi\in w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{\delta_{z+a_{n}e_{n}}\}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{z}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that there are infinite disjoint sets
\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
\mathbb{N}_{2}
partitioning
\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi\in w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{\delta_{z+a_{n}e_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}
and
\psi\in w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{\delta_{z+a_{n}e_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{2}\}
.
Now, we define a sequence of polynomials
(Q_{k})
satisfying the hypotheses of Lemma
2.1
. If
\mathbb{N}_{2}=\{m_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
, for each
k
take
Q_{k}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}}x_{n}^{p}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}x_{m_{l}}^{p}
. Since
Q_{k}\in\mathcal{P}(^{p}\ell_{p})
,
Q_{k}|_{B_{\ell_{p}}}\in\mathcal{A}
. Moreover,
\|Q_{k}\|\leq 1
for every
k
.

In order to calculate
\varphi(Q_{k})
we consider
j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
. Noting that
(z_{j}+a_{j})^{p}-z_{j}^{p}=1-\|z\|_{p}^{p}
we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\delta_{z+a_{j}e_{j}}(Q_{k})&=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}}z_{n}^{p}-z_{j}^{p}+(z_{j}+a_{j})^{p}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}z_{m_{l}}^{p}\\
&=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}}z_{n}^{p}+1-\|z\|_{p}^{p}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}z_{m_{l}}^{p}=1-\sum\limits_{l>k}z_{m_{l}}^{p}.\end{array}

Hence,
\varphi(Q_{k})=1-\sum\limits_{l>k}z_{m_{l}}^{p}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\varphi(Q_{k})=1
.

Now, to obtain the value of
\psi(Q_{k})
, we consider
j\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
,
j>m_{k}
. We have

\delta_{z+a_{j}e_{j}}(Q_{k})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}}z_{n}^{p}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}z_{m_{l}}^{p},

and then,

\psi(Q_{k})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{1}}z_{n}^{p}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}z_{m_{l}}^{p}.

Thus
\lim_{k\to\infty}\psi(Q_{k})=\|z\|_{p}^{p}<1
. Finally, the conclusion follows by Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\geq 2
. Then for each
z\in B_{\ell_{p}}
, the fiber
\mathcal{M}_{z}
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
contains a set of cardinal
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
such that each two elements of this set belong to different Gleason parts.

Proof.

It is enough to consider
z=(z_{n})
with
z_{n}\geq 0
for all
n
. Indeed, if this is not the case, let
\theta_{n}
be such that
e^{i\theta_{n}}z_{n}=|z_{n}|
and define
\Phi\colon B_{\ell_{p}}\to B_{\ell_{p}}
to be the automorphism given by
\Phi(x)=(e^{i\theta_{n}}x_{n})
which satisfies the hypotheses of
[
8
, Prop. 1.6]
. Then it induces a mapping
\Lambda_{\Phi}\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))\to\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
which is an isometry for the Gleason metric that sends
\mathcal{M}_{z}
onto
\mathcal{M}_{(|z_{n}|)}
.

As in Theorem
3.3
we obtain an interpolating sequence
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k}
in
\mathcal{M}_{z}
as follows.
For each
n
take
a_{n}>0
satisfying
\|z+a_{n}e_{n}\|_{p}=1
. Now, let
\{\mathbb{N}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable partition of
\mathbb{N}
such that each
\mathbb{N}_{k}
is infinite and choose
\psi_{k}
in
w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{\delta_{z+a_{n}e_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}_{k}\}
. It is clear that
\psi_{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{z}
for all
k
and, by Lemma
3.4
,
\mathcal{GP}(\psi_{k})\not=\mathcal{GP}(\psi_{l})
, for every
k\neq l
.

Let
r=1-\|z\|_{p}^{p}
and fix
(c_{k})_{k}\in\overline{B}_{\ell_{\infty}}
. We define
P\in\mathcal{A}
to be
the polynomial given by

P(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{c_{k}}{r}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{k}}(x_{n}-z_{n})^{p}.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{k}
it holds that

\delta_{z+a_{j}e_{j}}(P)=\frac{c_{k}}{r}a_{j}^{p}.

Since we have chosen
a_{j}
such that
a_{j}=\big(1-\|z\|_{p}^{p}+z_{j}\big)^{1/p}-z_{j}
, then
a_{j}^{p}\underset{j\to\infty}{\to}1-\|z\|^{p}=r
and

\psi_{k}(P)=w^{*}-\lim_{\mathbb{N}_{k}}\delta_{z+a_{j}e_{j}}(P)=c_{k}.

Hence,
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k}
is interpolating and the conclusion follows as in Theorem
3.3
.
∎

z\in S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\setminus S_{\ell_{1}}
is nontrivial (i.e. not singleton).
We are now in a position to prove that this is so by means of arguments lying on a close look at the structure of the points
z
. Recall that
c_{0}
is an
M
-ideal in
\ell_{\infty}
, so there is a decomposition
\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\ell_{1}\oplus_{1}c_{0}^{\perp}
. Analyzing the proof of the previous statement (d), we see that the set
\mathcal{K}
constructed in
[
6
, Prop. 3.3]
lies inside
c_{0}^{\perp}
. We can also reach fibers over some points of
S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\setminus S_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
with a similar strategy. But even then, we would not cover all cases. To complete the description, we use a combination of two different approaches: first, we identify the points
z
for which a variation of the construction from
[
6
]
is allowed, and then we develop a new scheme for the remaining points.

We state here the general result whose proof is obtained by merging the subsequent Propositions
4.4
and
4.7
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}})
, the fiber
\mathcal{M}_{z}
over any
z\in S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\setminus S_{\ell_{1}}
satisfies
{\rm Card}(\mathcal{M}_{z})\geq\mathfrak{c}
.

On the way to our goal, it is useful to have a handy
description of the real extreme points of
\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
, and, consequently, of
\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The set of real extreme points of
\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
is

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}})=\{\lambda z:z\in\overline{\{e_{n}\}}^{w^{*}},\lambda\in\mathbb{C},|\lambda|=1\}.

In particular, the set of real extreme points of
\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
is

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}})=\{\lambda z:z\in w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{e_{n}\},\lambda\in\mathbb{C},|\lambda|=1\}.

Proof.

The identification
\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\ell_{\infty}^{\prime}=C(\beta\mathbb{N})^{\prime}
gives us a description of the real extreme points of its unit ball:
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}})=\{\lambda\xi_{s}:s\in\beta\mathbb{N},\lambda\in\mathbb{C},|\lambda|=1\}
, where
\xi_{s}(a)=a(s)
for every
a\in\ell_{\infty}=C(\beta\mathbb{N})
. Notice that if
s=n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\xi_{n}=e_{n}\in\ell_{1}
. Now, if
s\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
, there is a net
(n_{\alpha})\subset\mathbb{N}
converging to
s
in the topology of
\beta\mathbb{N}
. This is equivalent to say that the net
\{e_{n_{\alpha}}\}\subset\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}
is
w^{*}
-convergent to
\xi_{s}
. In addition, in this case,
\xi_{s}
belongs to
c_{0}^{\perp}
. As a byproduct of this discussion along with the identity
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}})=\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{\ell})\cup\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}})
, the second statement follows.
∎

Now we are ready to extend the referred argument from
[
6
, Prop. 3.3]
to the fibers over points of
S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
whose component in
c_{0}^{\perp}
is a real extreme point of the ball of its norm.

Proposition 4.4
.

If
z=x+w\in S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
with
x\in\ell_{1}
and
w\in c_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\frac{w}{\|w\|}
is a real extreme point of
\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
then, the fiber
\mathcal{M}_{z}
of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}}))
satisfies
{\rm Card}(\mathcal{M}_{z})\geq\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

It is clear that, for every
z\in S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
and every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|=1
,
\mathcal{M}_{z}
, the fiber over
z
, is homeomorphic (and Gleason isometric) to
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda z}
, the fiber over
\lambda z
. Hence, in this proof it is enough to consider
z=x+w\in S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
with
x\in\ell_{1}
and
w\in c_{0}^{\perp}
such that
\frac{w}{\|w\|}\in w^{*}-{\rm ac}\{e_{n}\}
. Choose a net
{\{e_{n_{\alpha}}\}}
satisfying
e_{n_{\alpha}}\overset{w^{*}}{\to}\frac{w}{\|w\|}
and take
\varphi
to be a
w^{*}
-limit point of
\{\delta_{x+\|w\|e_{n_{\alpha}}}\}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}}))
.
Note that
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{z}
and let us now see that
\delta_{z}\not=\varphi
. Indeed, consider the polynomial
P
given by
P(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(y_{j}-x_{j})^{2}
. On the one hand, since
P(x+\|w\|e_{n_{\alpha}})=\|w\|^{2}
, for every
\alpha
, we have
\varphi(P)=\|w\|^{2}
. On the other hand, developing the polynomial
P
in its homogeneous parts and arguing as in the proof of
[
6
, Prop. 2.5]
we derive that
\delta_{z}(P)=0
.
Finally, to derive
{\rm Card}(\mathcal{M}_{z})\geq\mathfrak{c}
we proceed as in the end of the proof of
[
6
, Prop. 3.3]
, which appeals to the connection of the
cluster set
of
P
at
z\in\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
(i.e. the set consisting of all limits of values of
P
along nets in

B_{\ell_{1}}

w^{*}
-converging to
z
).
∎

Our next step is to show that the conclusion of the above proposition remains valid for points
z=x+w\in S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
with
x\in\ell_{1}
and
w\in c_{0}^{\perp}
such that
\frac{w}{\|w\|}
is not a real extreme point of
\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
. This would complete the description of the fibers over points in
S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\setminus S_{\ell_{1}}
thus finishing the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Before going on, we recall some concepts and known results about a relative algebra of analytic mappings. Given
X
a complex Banach space,
\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)
denotes the Fréchet algebra of entire functions bounded on bounded subsets of
X
, endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on these bounded sets. Several properties of the maximal ideal space
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
are studied in
[
6
]
where, following
[
7
]
, the
radius function
of
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))

is defined by

R(\varphi)=\inf\big\{r>0\,\colon\,|\varphi(f)|\leq\|f\|_{rB_{X}}\text{ for every }f\in\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)\,\big\}

where
\|f\|_{rB_{X}}=\sup\{|f(x)|\colon\|x\|<r\}
. In particular,
\|\varphi|_{X^{\prime}}\|\leq R(\varphi)

[
7
, Lem. 3.2]
and
R(\delta_{z})=\|z\|
for all
z\in\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}

[
7
, Lem. 3.1]
.

The spectra of
\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)
and
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
are related via the following onto homeomorphism, see

[
6
, Lem. 1.2]
or
[
7
, Thm. 12.1]
:

(4)

\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))\to\big\{\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)),\,R(\varphi)\leq 1\big\},

defined by
\Phi(\varphi)(f)=\varphi(f|_{B_{X}}),
for

\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)
. This identification will allow us to transfer results from one to another spectrum and will be used without further mention.

Another important tool within the spectrum of
\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)
(that we will make use of) is the convolution product
\ast\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))\times\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))\to\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
, defined in
[
7
, Sect. 6]
as follows: for
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)
,

\varphi\ast\psi(f)=\varphi(x\mapsto\psi(\tau_{x}(f))),

where for each
x\in X
,
\tau_{x}(f)\in\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)
is given by
\tau_{x}(f)(y)=f(x+y)
for all
y\in X
.

This operation satisfies two relevant properties:

(5)

R(\varphi\ast\psi)\leq R(\varphi)+R(\psi),

and given
z,w\in X^{\prime\prime}
such that
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{z}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(B_{X}))
and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{w}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(B_{X}))
, then

(6)

\varphi\ast\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{z+w}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(B_{X})).

(See
[
7
, Lem. 6.2]
and
[
7
, Lem. 6.4]
respectively.)

Finally, we will appeal to a simple composition action,
coming from a family of one-parameter operators,
(t\text{Id})_{t}
, where
Id
is the identity map and
t\in\mathbb{C}
, see
[
7
, Sect. 5]
.
For each
t\in\mathbb{C}
,
\Upsilon_{t}\colon\mathcal{H}^{*}_{b}(X)\to\mathcal{H}^{*}_{b}(X)
, is defined by

\Upsilon_{t}(\varphi)(f)=\varphi(f\circ t\text{Id})
. For simplicity, we denote
\varphi^{t}:=\Upsilon_{t}(\varphi)\in\mathcal{H}^{*}_{b}(X)
. Notice that,
\varphi^{0}=\delta_{0}
and
\varphi^{1}=\varphi
, also, for any
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P
,
\varphi^{t}(P)=t^{m}P
.

With the aid of these tools, we show that any nontrivial fiber in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
has cardinality at least
\mathfrak{c}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space. For any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
,
\varphi\neq\psi
,

{\rm Card}\left(\{\varphi^{t}\ast\psi^{1-t}\colon t\in[0,1]\}\right)\geq\mathfrak{c}.

Proof.

Since
\varphi\neq\psi
and the analytic polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}_{b}(X)
, there exists a continuous
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P
such that

\varphi(P)\neq\psi(P)
.
Now, define
q\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, by
q(t)=\varphi^{t}\ast\psi^{1-t}(P)
, which results a polynomial of degree non greater than
m
.
The conclusion follows by noting that
q(0)=\varphi^{0}\ast\psi^{1}(P)=\delta_{0}\ast\psi(P)=\psi(P)\neq q(1)=\varphi\ast\delta_{0}(P)=\varphi(P)
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
z\in\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}}
. If there exist
\varphi\not=\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{z}
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
, then

{\rm Card}\left(\mathcal{M}_{z}\right)\geq\mathfrak{c}.

Proof.

By the identification given in (
4
), both homomorphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
may be regarded as elements in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
with
R(\varphi),R(\psi)\leq 1
.

Let us consider the set
\mathfrak{C}=\{\varphi^{t}\ast\psi^{1-t}\colon t\in[0,1]\}
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
. By
[
7
, Lem. 5.4]

\varphi^{t}\in\mathcal{M}_{tz}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
and
\psi^{1-t}\in\mathcal{M}_{(1-t)z}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
. Now, (
6
) gives that
\varphi^{t}\ast\psi^{1-t}\in\mathcal{M}_{z}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(X))
for all
0\leq t\leq 1
. Again by
[
7
, Lem. 5.4]
,
R(\varphi^{t})=tR(\varphi)
and
R(\psi^{1-t})=(1-t)R(\psi)
, and an application of (
5
) yields that
R(\varphi^{t}\ast\psi^{1-t})\leq 1
.

Finally, appealing to (
4
), we see that
\mathfrak{C}\subset\mathcal{M}_{z}
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
and the result follows by Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Now we are ready to present the announced result which completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
z=x+w\in S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
with
x\in\ell_{1}
and
w\in c_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\frac{w}{\|w\|}
is not a real extreme point of
\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
. Then, for
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}})
, the fiber
\mathcal{M}_{z}
satisfies
{\rm Card}(\mathcal{M}_{z})\geq\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Since
w
is not a real extreme point of
\|w\|\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
, there exist
w_{1},w_{2}\in c_{0}^{\perp}
with
\|w\|=\|w_{1}\|=\|w_{2}\|
and
w_{1}\neq w_{2}
such that
w=\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}}{2}
.
Note that
w_{2}
cannot be a multiple of

w_{1}
. Indeed, if
w_{2}=\lambda w_{1}
, then
\|w\|=\left\|\frac{w_{1}+\lambda w_{1}}{2}\right\|=\left|\frac{1+\lambda}{2}\right|\|w\|
.
Hence
\lambda=1
, which is a contradiction.

Now we take
z_{1}=x+\frac{w_{1}}{2}
and
z_{2}=\frac{w_{2}}{2}
. Since
\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\ell_{1}\oplus_{1}c_{0}^{\perp}
, we have
\|z_{1}\|=\|x\|+\frac{\|w\|}{2}
and
\|z_{2}\|=\frac{\|w\|}{2}
.
This, clearly implies that
z=z_{1}+z_{2}
with
1=\|z\|=\|x\|+\|w\|=\|z_{1}\|+\|z_{2}\|
.

We define the homomorphisms
\varphi=\delta_{z_{1}}\ast\delta_{z_{2}}
and
\psi=\delta_{z_{2}}\ast\delta_{z_{1}}
and make use of
[
7
, Thm. 6.11]
where it was proved, in a general context, that
\delta_{z_{1}}\ast\delta_{z_{2}}=\delta_{z_{2}}\ast\delta_{z_{1}}
if and only
\delta_{z_{1}}\ast\delta_{z_{2}}=\delta_{z_{1}+z_{2}}
. Also, for
X=\ell_{1}
something else can be said appealing to
[
12
, Cor. 2.5]
: since
w_{2}
is not a multiple of
w_{1}
we have that
\varphi\neq\psi
and both homomorphisms differ from
\delta_{z}
. As
\varphi
is the convolution of
\delta_{z_{1}}
and
\delta_{z_{2}}
, by (
5
) we have
R(\varphi)\leq R(\delta_{z_{1}})+R(\delta_{z_{1}})=\|z_{1}\|+\|z_{2}\|=1
. Analogously,
R(\psi)\leq 1
. Also, by (
6
), both
\varphi
and
\psi
belong to
\mathcal{M}_{z}(\mathcal{H}_{b}(\ell_{1}))
. Hence, due to (
4
) along with Lemma
4.6
we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Once we have distinguished between singleton and non-singleton fibers, we focus on how they interact with Gleason parts. Although we believe that the path is paved, our progress in this regard has been limited.

In Proposition
2.5
we have seen that if
z\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X^{\prime\prime}})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{z}
then
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)
cannot intersect any other fiber. In light of this result, it will be useful to have a description of
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}})
. We thank our friend Tomás Rodríguez for drawing our attention to the following fact.

Remark 4.8
.

The space
\ell_{\infty}^{\prime}
is isometrically isomorphic to
L_{1}(\mu)
, for a certain measure
\mu
.

Indeed, this is a consequence of the duality between abstract
M
- and
L
-spaces (see
[
30
]
for the definitions) combined with a result by Kakutani. Since we could not find a complete reference for these arguments in the case of general complex Banach lattices, we include an exposition of its derivation from the real case. In this reasoning, we distinguish real and complex spaces in a standard way. It is clear that
\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is an abstract
M
-space and thus
[
30
, Thm. 7, pg. 25]
implies that
\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})^{\prime}
is an abstract
L
-space. Now, due to Kakutani’s theorem
[
30
, Thm. 3, pg. 135]
we know that
\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})^{\prime}
is isometrically isomorphic to
L_{1}(\mu,\mathbb{R})
, for a certain measure
\mu
.

Now, we use several facts about Bochnack and Taylor’s complexifications from
[
33
]
. As in this referred article, we denote by
\widetilde{X}
the complexification of a real space
X
and
\|\cdot\|_{B}
and
\|\cdot\|_{T}
for Bochnack and Taylor’s norms in the complexified space.

\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})^{\prime}=(\widetilde{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\|\cdot\|_{T})^{\prime}=(\widetilde{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})^{\prime}},\|\cdot\|_{B})\cong(\widetilde{L_{1}(\mu,\mathbb{R})},\|\cdot\|_{B})=L_{1}(\mu,\mathbb{C}).

Now, from
[
37
, Th. 4.2]
, which states that every point on the unit sphere of a
L_{1}(\mu)
space is a complex extreme point, combined with Proposition
2.5
we derive the following:

Corollary 4.9
.

The set of complex extreme points of the unit ball of
\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}
is
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}})=S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
. Consequently, for any
z\in S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{z}
, the Gleason part
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)
cannot intersect any other fiber.

The existence of homomorphisms from the same fiber belonging to different Gleason parts seems to be tougher to establish in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}}))
than in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
(
1<p<\infty
). A close inspection to the proof of Proposition
4.4
yields our only result about this question:

Corollary 4.10
.

If
w
is a real extreme point of
\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
then there is
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{w}
such that
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{w})
.

Indeed, as in the referred proof (which in this case is not other than the proof of
[
6
, Prop. 3.3]
), the polynomial
P(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}y_{j}^{2}
has norm 1 and
\varphi(P)=1
while
\delta_{w}(P)=0
.

5.
Final comments and open questions

We devote this last section to listing some open problems arising from the work and to presenting some related comments or examples.

For any element
f
in a uniform algebra
\mathcal{A}
there exists a strong boundary point
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\varphi(f)=1
; see for instance
[
36
, Thm. 7.21]
. Consequently, it would be interesting to obtain a description of all the strong boundary points for the algebras
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, that we have studied. In the case
1<p<\infty
, as we see in Proposition
3.1
,
\delta_{z}
is a peak point and, hence, a strong boundary point for
\mathcal{A}
, for every
z\in S_{\ell_{p}}
. Also, in
[
8
, Prop. 3.6]
it is proved that all the strong boundary points for
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})
are in fibers over points of
the distinguished boundary
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{N}}
of
S_{\ell_{\infty}}
. So, one can be tempted to think that all the strong boundary points for
\mathcal{A}
should be in fibers over
boundary
points; that is, in fibers over points in
S_{\ell_{p}}
(for
1<p<\infty
) or
S_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
(for
p=1
). The following example from
[
7
]
shows that this is not the case, at least for
1<p<\infty
.

Example 5.1
.

[
7
, Lem. 11.2]

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq p>1
and consider
f\colon\ell_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
f(x)=\sum_{n}(1-\frac{1}{n})x_{n}^{k}
. It is clear that
f\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}})
with
\|f\|=1
. Take a strong boundary point
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\varphi(f)=1
which, by the argument from the referred lemma, belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

In
[
8
, Prop. 1.6]
it is shown that if we have an automorphism
\Phi:B_{X}\to B_{X}
such that
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
are uniformly continuous, then the associated mapping
\Lambda_{\Phi}\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))\to\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X}))
, given by
\Lambda_{\Phi}(\varphi)(f)=\varphi(f\circ\Phi)
, is an onto isometry. In this case, it is easy to see that
\varphi
is a strong boundary point if and only if
\Lambda_{\Phi}(\varphi)
is a strong boundary point. Therefore, the previous example along with what we have commented in Remark
3.6
allows us to conclude that all the fibers of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{2}}))
have strong boundary points. We do not know if the same holds for other values of
p
.

Changing our focus, what it is certainly always true is that the radius function on a strong boundary point should be 1. Indeed, if
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
is a strong boundary point, then there is a non-constant function
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\varphi(f)=1
. Then, we cannot have
|\varphi(f)|\leq\|f\|_{rB_{\ell_{p}}}
for any
r<1
, meaning that
R(\varphi)=1
.

Continuing with strong boundary points, as already mentioned in Section
4
, a combination of
[
2
, Thm. 2.6]
with
[
15
, Thm. 3.3 (2)]
gives that for each
z\in S_{\ell_{1}}
, the evaluation
\delta_{z}
is a peak point. Hence,
\delta_{z}
is a strong boundary point. Also,
given any
y=(y_{n})\in S_{\ell_{\infty}}
with
|y_{n}|<1
for all
n
there exists
z
, a real extreme point of
\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
, such that
\langle z,y\rangle=1
. It is evident that
z\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}})
. On the other hand, since
y\in\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}})
there is a strong boundary point
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\varphi(y)=1
. So we can wonder if
\varphi=\delta_{z}
or, at least,
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{z}
.

Now we come to our first open question.

Open problem 1.
Describe all the strong boundary points for
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}})
, (
1\leq p<\infty
) or, at least, identify all the fibers containing strong boundary points.

The existence of homomorphisms from different Gleason parts in the same fiber is almost unknown for
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}}))
. We have just proved it for the fiber
\mathcal{M}_{w}
, being
w
a real extreme point of
\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
. Also, we know that
\mathcal{M}_{z}=\{\delta_{z}\}
for
z\in S_{\ell_{1}}
. Thus, it would be interesting to solve it for the rest of the fibers.

Open problem 2.
For
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}}))
, given
z\in\overline{B}_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
such that
z\not\in S_{\ell_{1}}
and
z
is not a real extreme point of
\overline{B}_{c_{0}^{\perp}}
, decide whether there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{z}
with
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{z})
.

Looking at the proof of the result referred to in Proposition
4.1
(b) for the spectrum
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{1}}))
, we derive that for each
z\in B_{\ell_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
there is a copy of the complex disk contained in
\mathcal{M}_{z}\cap\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{z})
, which, by Remark
2.2
coincides with
\mathcal{M}_{z}\cap\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{0})
. Analogous results are stated in Proposition
3.2
for
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
,
1<p<\infty
. Thus, in our setting, all known cases of two homomorphisms (in fibers over interior points) belonging to the same Gleason part satisfy that the Gleason part is
\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{0})
. This leads us to propose the next problem.

Open problem 3.
For
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
,
1\leq p<\infty
, show the existence (or lack) of morphisms
\varphi\not=\psi
, lying in fibers over interior points, such that
\mathcal{GP}(\varphi)=\mathcal{GP}(\psi)\not=\mathcal{GP}(\delta_{0})
.

Proposition
3.5
has a restrictive unexpected hypothesis:
p
should be integer. The reason is purely technical since we need
P(x)=\sum_{n}x_{n}^{p}
to be a polynomial and, hence, an element of
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}})
. It is interesting to know whether this hypothesis is necessary or not.

Open problem 4.
For
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{\ell_{p}}))
,
1<p<\infty
, if
p
is not an integer, does the conclusion of Proposition
3.5
hold?

6.
Appendix

In analyzing the interaction between fibers and Gleason parts within the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}_{u}(B_{X})
, the set of complex extreme points of the closed unit ball of the bidual space has proven to be useful, as can be seen for example in Proposition
2.5
. This fact motivates us, for the sake of completeness, to present in this appendix a description of the complex extreme points of the unit ball of
X\oplus_{p}Y
. Even though it was not necessary for our investigation, we believe that the following is of interest on its own.

Proposition 6.1
.

Given Banach spaces
X
and
Y
and
1\leq p<\infty
we have

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X\oplus_{p}Y})=\{x+y\in S_{X\oplus_{p}Y}\colon\ x\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\|x\|\overline{B}_{X})\ \ \text{\rm and }\ y\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\|y\|\overline{B}_{Y})\}.

To prove this, we make use of the next simple lemma, which is surely well-known. In its proof, included to cover all bases, we refer to the following implication valid for
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
:

(7)

|a|=\frac{|a+\lambda b|+|a-\lambda b|}{2}\ \ \text{ for all }\ \lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}\ \Rightarrow\ b=0.

Indeed, (
7
) holds since if
|a|=\frac{|a+\lambda b|+|a-\lambda b|}{2}
then there exists
r_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
a+\lambda b=r_{\lambda}(a-\lambda b)
, except for at most one value of
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
satisfying
a=\lambda_{0}b
. Thus,
\frac{r_{\lambda}-1}{r_{\lambda}+1}a=\lambda b
. Choosing
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
such that
ae^{i\theta}=|a|
we obtain
\frac{r_{\lambda}-1}{r_{\lambda}+1}|a|=\lambda be^{i\theta}
, which implies
\lambda be^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{\lambda_{0}\}
. This can only be true if
b=0
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
1\leq p<\infty
and
x,u\in X
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\|x\|=\|x+\lambda u\|
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

(ii)

\|x\|^{p}=\frac{\|x+\lambda u\|^{p}+\|x-\lambda u\|^{p}}{2}
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

The implication (
i
)
\Rightarrow
(
ii
) is obvious. Now, suppose that (
ii
) holds and let
x^{\prime}\in S_{X^{\prime}}
such that
x^{\prime}(x)=\|x\|
. Since

\displaystyle\|x\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(x)\leq\frac{|x^{\prime}(x+\lambda u)|+|x^{\prime}(x-\lambda u)|}{2}\leq\frac{\|x+\lambda u\|+\|x-\lambda u\|}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\|x+\lambda u\|^{p}+\|x-\lambda u\|^{p}}{2}\right)^{1/p}=\|x\|

we obtain that
|x^{\prime}(x+\lambda u)|=\|x+\lambda u\|
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and also

x^{\prime}(x)=|x^{\prime}(x)|=\frac{|x^{\prime}(x)+\lambda x^{\prime}(u)|+|x^{\prime}(x)-\lambda x^{\prime}(u)|}{2}\quad\textrm{ for all }\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

This implies, by (
7
), that
x^{\prime}(u)=0
.

In consequence,
\|x\|=|x^{\prime}(x)|=|x^{\prime}(x+\lambda u)|=\|x+\lambda u\|
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, and (
i
) holds.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

If
z=x+y\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X\oplus_{p}Y})
with
x\in X
and
y\in Y
then
1=\|z\|^{p}=\|x\|^{p}+\|y\|^{p}
. Suppose that
\|x+\lambda u\|\leq\|x\|
for some
u\in X
and all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Then
\|z+\lambda u\|\leq 1
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
which implies
u=0
. This proves that
x\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\|x\|\overline{B}_{X})
. Analogously, it is shown that
y\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\|y\|\overline{B}_{Y})
.

Conversely, given
x\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\|x\|\overline{B}_{X})
and
y\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\|y\|\overline{B}_{Y})
such that
\|x\|^{p}+\|y\|^{p}=1
, let us see that
z=x+y
belongs to
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{B}_{X\oplus_{p}Y})
. Suppose that
w\in X\oplus_{p}Y
,
w=u+v
(
u\in X
and
v\in Y
) satisfies
\|z+\lambda w\|\leq 1
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Then,
\|x+\lambda u\|^{p}+\|y+\lambda v\|^{p}\leq 1
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. By a simple triangle inequality argument this leads to the following identities, valid for every
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
:

\|x\|^{p}=\frac{\|x+\lambda u\|^{p}+\|x-\lambda u\|^{p}}{2};\qquad\|y\|^{p}=\frac{\|y+\lambda v\|^{p}+\|y-\lambda v\|^{p}}{2}.

By Lemma
6.2
, we derive that
\|x\|=\|x+\lambda u\|
and
\|y\|=\|y+\lambda v\|
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
implying, by our assumption, that
u=v=0
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Observe that the relationship for real extreme points coincides with the one in Proposition
6.1
for
1<p<\infty
but is different for
p=1
. Indeed,

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{X\oplus_{1}Y})=\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{X})\cup\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{Y}),

while, for
1<p<\infty
,

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{B}_{X\oplus_{p}Y})=\{x+y\in S_{X\oplus_{p}Y}\colon\ x\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\|x\|\overline{B}_{X})\ \ \text{\rm and }\ y\in\mbox{\rm Ext}_{\mathbb{R}}(\|y\|\overline{B}_{Y})\}.

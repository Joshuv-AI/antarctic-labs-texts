The Morel-Voevodsky Construction over Algebraic Stacks
Neeraj Deshmukh, Felix Sefzig

Abstract
In this article, we give a construction of the (un-)stable motivic homotopy category of an algebraic stack in the spirit of Morel-Voevodsky. We prove that this new construction agrees with the stable motivic homotopy category defined by Chowdhury [ Cho24 ] . As an application, we prove a version of Bachmann’s spectral rigidity theorem [ Bac21 ] for algebraic stacks. Moreover, we extend the construction of the framed motivic homotopy category in [ EHK+21 ] to algebraic stacks, and prove the Reconstruction Theorem of [ Hoy21 , Theorem 18] in this setting. Finally, we discuss an extension of the formalism of cocomplete coefficient systems of [ DG22 ] to the setting of algebraic stacks.

Introduction

Let
X
be a scheme. The Morel-Voevodsky stable motivic homotopy category over
X
, denoted
\mathbf{SH}(X)
, can be described as a suitable localization and stabilization of the category of sheaves of spectra on the Nisnevich site
(\mathbf{Sm}_{X},\rm{Nis})
of smooth schemes over
X
. This construction describes a functor on the category of schemes taking values in stable
\infty
-categories that satisfies Nisnevich descent.
Thus, given a Nisnevich local epimorphism
p:Y\rightarrow X
, we have an equivalence

p^{*}\colon\mathbf{SH}(X)\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\lim_{\Delta}\mathbf{SH}(Y_{\bullet}).

(1)

Here,
Y_{\bullet}
is the Čech nerve of
p\colon Y\rightarrow X
.
This statement also holds for other motivic categories (for example,
\mathbf{SH}^{\rm{fr}}(X)
,
\mathbf{DM}(X,\Lambda)
,
\mathbf{DA}(X,\Lambda)
, etc.). A low-brow way of thinking about the above
\infty
-categorical equivalence is the following. For any cohomology theory
E^{*}
on
X
, there exists a descent spectral sequence converging to
E^{*}(X)
whose
E_{1}
-page is described by the pullback of
E^{*}
to the
Y_{i}
’s.

This paper aims to describe
\mathbf{SH}
(and other motivic categories) over an algebraic stack in the spirit of Morel-Voevodsky. More specifically, we define
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
as a suitable localization and stabilization of the category of sheaves of spectra on the smooth-Nisnevich site of the algebraic stack
\mathscr{X}
(see Construction
2.3
). Our choice of the smooth-Nisnevich site is motivated in part by the theory of smooth-étale sheaves on
\mathscr{X}
, and in part by the direct analogy of this approach with the case of schemes.

In
[
KR24
]
and
[
Cho24
]
the authors describe two categories
\mathbf{SH}_{\triangleleft}(\mathscr{X})
, and
\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathscr{X})
. These categories are equivalent models of the stable motivic homotopy category over an algebraic stack
\mathscr{X}
, as observed in
[
Cho24
]
and
[
DH25
]
.
In fact, one may view these constructions as models of the right Kan extension of
\mathbf{SH}
from schemes to algebraic stacks.
This approach should be thought of as the right-hand side of Equivalence (
1
).
On the other hand, the construction in Section
2
belongs to the left-hand side. In fact, we will show that Equivalence (
1
) continues to hold for an algebraic stack, and we have equivalences (see Theorem
2.7
),

\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathbf{SH}_{\triangleleft}(\mathscr{X})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathscr{X}).

(2)

The above equivalences also hold for other motivic categories as described in the text.

In
[
CD24
]
, the authors define the category
\mathbf{SH}_{\rm{cl}}(\mathscr{X})
and show that it is equivalent to
\mathbf{SH}_{\rm{ext}}(\mathscr{X})
(
[
CD24
, Theorem 3.2.1]
). The description of the category
\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathscr{X})
is very closely related to Construction
2.3
(see Remark
2.8
and also
[
CD24
, Remark 3.23]
). Morally,
\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathscr{X})
also belongs to the left-hand side of Equivalence (
1
).

In addition to its aesthetic value, Construction
2.3
of
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
also offers some conceptual advantages over the right Kan extension
j_{*}\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
. For example, one can easily identify a family of generators for
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
(Proposition
2.12
). Moreover, the construction of

for smooth (possibly non-representable) morphisms

of algebraic stacks becomes almost obvious.
This is rather tricky for

, and can get quite involved (see Lemma
5.7
, for example, or
[
CD24
, Remark 3.26]
).
A final example we would like to mention concerns the framed version
\mathbf{SH}^{\mathrm{fr}}(\mathscr{X})
(see Section
3
).
The constant sheaf
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathscr{X}}
on the smooth-Nisnevich site of
\mathscr{X}
has a natural notion of framed transfers. In particular, its framed suspension
\Sigma^{\infty}_{T,\mathrm{fr}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathscr{X}}
is an object of
\mathbf{SH}^{\mathrm{fr}}(\mathscr{X})
which describes the motivic cohomology spectrum
H\mathbb{Z}_{\mathscr{X}}
(see Corollary
3.7
) in the sense of Spitzweck
[
Spi18
]
.

The key notion required to establish the chain of equivalences in (

Outline

The text of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we describe the construction of
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
and
\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X})

The étale story leads us into a discussion about hypercomplete étale sheaves.
In particular, we discuss the rigidity theorem of

We also prove a version of Ayoub’s equivalence between étale motives with and without transfers (Theorem

) in the context of regular algebraic stacks over a field.

In Section

, we describe a model for the category of framed motivic spectra
\mathbf{SH}^{\mathrm{fr}}
over
\mathscr{X}
and prove that it satisfies Nisnevich descent. Using the results of
[
Hoy21
]
, this gives an equivalence between the framed and unframed versions of the stable motivic homotopy categories (Corollary

).
These observations result in an explicit description of the motivic cohomology spectrum over

as the framed suspension of the (framed) constant sheaf

(Corollary

).

In Section

In Section

1.3
Notation and conventions

Throughout this article, we will work exclusively within the framework of infinity categories.
In particular, we adopt the following conventions and notations.
We write
\mathcal{S}
for the
\infty
-category of spaces and
\mathrm{Sp}
for the
\infty
-category of
S^{1}
-spectra.
For an
\infty
-category
C
, we write
\mathrm{PSh}(C):=\mathrm{Fun}(C,\mathcal{S})
for the
\infty
-category of presheaves of spaces on
C
and
\mathrm{PSh}(C)_{*}
for the category of pointed presheaves of spaces.
If
(C,\tau)
is a site, we write
\mathrm{Shv}_{\tau}(C)
for the
\infty
-category of sheaves of spaces on
(C,\tau)
. If
\mathcal{T}
is an
\infty
-topos we write
\mathcal{T}^{\wedge}
for the hypercompletion of
\mathcal{T}
. A morphism of sites
f\colon(\mathcal{C},\tau)\to(\mathcal{D},\sigma)
is given by a continuous functor
f\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
such that the corresponding pullback functor
f^{*}\colon\mathrm{Shv}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathrm{Shv}_{\tau}(\mathcal{C})
is exact.

For a scheme
X
, we write
X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
for the small étale site of
X
.
We write
\mathbf{SH}(X)
for the stable motivic homotopy category and
\otimes
for the symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathbf{H}(X)_{*}
and
\mathbf{SH}(X)
.

2
The Morel-Voevodsky construction over algebraic stacks

We are going to present an alternative definition, in the spirit of Morel-Voevodsky, of the stable motivic homotopy category of an algebraic stack.
In addition, we prove that this new definition agrees with the one given in
[
Cho24
]
, which was originally given for Nisnevich-local stacks; however, by Theorem
1.3
, all algebraic stacks satisfy this assumption.

The stable motivic homotopy category
\mathbf{SH}(X)
of a scheme
X
is defined as the

\mathds{P}^{1}
-stabilization of
\mathbf{H}(X)_{*}
, the category of pointed
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant Nisnevich sheaves of spaces on
\mathbf{Sm}_{X}
. Moreover,
the assignment
X\mapsto\mathbf{SH}(X)
,
f\mapsto f^{*}
defines a functor of presentable stable
\infty
-categories

\mathbf{SH}\colon\mathbf{Sch}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{st}}.

In fact,
\mathbf{SH}(X)
has the structure of a symmetric monoidal presentable stable
\infty
-category, and the pullback functors are symmetric monoidal; in other words,
\mathbf{SH}
defines a functor

\mathbf{SH}\colon\mathbf{Sch}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{\mathrm{CAlg}}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}).

Chowdhury defined
\mathbf{SH}
for Nisnevich local stacks as the right Kan extension
j_{*}\mathbf{SH}
of

\mathbf{SH}
along the inclusion
j\colon\mathbf{Sch}\to\mathbf{Stk}
of schemes into Nisnevich-local Stacks, which by Theorem
1.3
is the category of all algebraic stacks.
We write
j_{*}\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
for the right Kan extension and
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
the Morel-Voevodsky version, see construction below.

We begin by defining the smooth Nisnevich and smooth étale site of an algebraic stack. For this, we view the
\infty
-category of stacks as a subcategory of
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathbf{Sch},\mathcal{S})
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack.

1.

Denote by
\mathbf{Stk}
the
\infty
-category of stacks. The Nisnevich, respectively the étale topology, on
\mathbf{Stk}
is the topology generated by families of maps
\{U_{i}\to\mathscr{X}\}
,
such that
\bigsqcup_{i}U_{i}\to\mathscr{X}
is a Nisnevich, respectively an étale, local epimorphism of presheaves of spaces.

2.

Denote by
\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}
the 1-category of stacks that are smooth and representable over
\mathscr{X}
. Moreover, denote by
\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}
the category of smooth schemes over
\mathscr{X}
.

3.

The Nisnevich, respectively the étale topology, on
\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}
or on
\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}
is the restriction of the Nisnevich topology, respectively of the étale topology, along the inclusion
\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathbf{Stk}
or
\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathbf{Sch}
.

Remark 2.2
.

A family of maps of schemes is a Nisnevich cover in the usual sense if and only if it is a Nisnevich cover of Stacks. Moreover, a smooth Nisnevich covering
X\to\mathscr{X}
as in Definition
1.1
is a Nisnevich cover in the above sense.

Construction 2.3
(Morel-Voevodsky).

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack over
S
.
We define
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})
as the localization of
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}})
at the class of Nisnevich locally
\infty
-connective morphisms and the class of maps

\left\{\mathrm{pr}_{1}\colon U\times_{\mathscr{X}}\mathds{A}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}}\to U\mid U\in\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}\right\}.

The corresponding localization functor is called motivic localization

L_{\mathrm{mot}}\colon\mathrm{PSh}(\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}})\to\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X}),

and admits a fully faithful right adjoint. Consequently, we may view
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})
as the full subcategory of
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}})
of
\mathds{A}^{1}
-invariant hypercomplete Nisnevich sheaves.
Finally, we define
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
as the

(\mathds{P}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}},\infty)
-stabilization, with respect to the usual wedge product,
\otimes
, of the pointed category
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})_{*}
, see for example
[
Rob15
, Section 2]
for more details on the stabilization process.
As for schemes, this construction defines a functor

\mathbf{SH}\colon\mathbf{Stk}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{\mathrm{CAlg}}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}),

which, by definition, agrees with
\mathbf{SH}
on schemes over
S
. In particular, there
exists a natural transformation
u\colon\mathbf{SH}\to j_{*}\mathbf{SH}
.

Construction 2.4
(Étale version).

The stable étale motivic homotopy category is constructed exactly as its Nisnevich counterpart, except that we invert all étale locally
\infty
-connective morphisms instead of the Nisnevich local equivalences. We denote the resulting categories by
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X})
and
\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X})
. In particular, the étale motivic homotopy category is obtained as a localization of the hypercomplete
étale topos on
\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}

L_{\mathrm{mot}}\colon\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}})^{\wedge}\to\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X}).

Remark 2.5
.

We use the smooth representable site of
\mathscr{X}
in the above constructions to ensure that
(\mathbf{SH},f^{*})
is clearly functorial for all morphisms of stacks. On the other hand, as the next proposition shows, we can equivalently define
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
using the smooth Nisnevich site. Moreover, if
f\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
is a representable morphism, then the functor

f^{*}\colon\mathbf{SH}_{\tau}(\mathscr{Y})\to\mathbf{SH}_{\tau}(\mathscr{X})

is induced by the functor
\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{Y}}\to\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}
given by
U\mapsto U\times_{\mathscr{Y}}\mathscr{X}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack. The inclusion functor
u\colon\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}
induces an equivalence of topoi

\mathrm{Shv}_{\tau}(\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Shv}_{\tau}(\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}),

with
\tau\in\{\mathrm{Nis},\mathrm{\acute{e}t}\}
.
This equivalence identifies
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})
with the full subcategory of
\mathds{A}^{1}
-invariant sheaves on
\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}
.

Proof.

By
[
Lur09
, Lemma 6.4.5.6]
, it suffices to check that the ordinary sheaf topoi agree. For this, we check the condition from
[
Sta21
, Tag 039Z]
. Since
u
is fully faithful and clearly continuous, it suffices to check that
u
is cocontinuous and every
V\in\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}
admits a Nisnevich covering by smooth schemes over
\mathscr{X}
. The second claim follows directly from Theorem
1.3
. For the first claim, let
U\in\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}
and let
\mathcal{V}:=\{V_{i}\to u(U)\}
be a Nisnevich cover in
\mathbf{SmRep}_{\mathscr{X}}
.
For all
i
, let
U_{i}\to V_{i}
be a smooth Nisnevich cover by a scheme, which exists again by Theorem

, then
\{U_{i}\to U\}
is a Nisnevich covering in
\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}
and
\{u(U_{i})\to u(U)\}

The main theorem of this section is that the Morel-Voevodsky construction of
\mathbf{SH}
agrees with the right Kan extension of
\mathbf{SH}
along the inclusion
j\colon\mathbf{Sch}\to\mathbf{Stk}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack. The natural functors

u\colon\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})\to j_{*}\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})

and

u\colon\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X})\to j_{*}\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X})

are equivalences of symmetric monoidal presentable stable
\infty
-categories.

Remark 2.8
.

Chowdhury and D’Angelo independently gave an equivalent construction of
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
and proved a version of Theorem
2.7
,
[
CD24
, Theorem 3.21]
.
Concretely, they work with the category of all Artin Stacks endowed with the so-called NL-topology. A cover for the NL-topology is a morphism
\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
that admits sections after pullback along a smooth Nisnevich cover of
\mathscr{Y}
by a scheme. Finally, that the two constructions agree is an application of Theorem
1.3
and the fact that for smooth representable morphisms, the notion of NL-cover and Nisnevich cover agree.

Before we discuss the proof of the main theorem, let us collect some standard facts about the categories
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})
and
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
. The analogous results also hold in the étale setting. In particular, it follows that
\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})
is generated by suspension spectra of representable sheaves, and that
f^{*}
admits a left adjoint
f_{\#}
even for not necessarily representable, smooth morphisms
f
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack. The natural map
\mathds{A}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}}\to*
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})
. Moreover, the natural maps

(\mathds{A}^{n}/(\mathds{A}^{n}\smallsetminus 0),1)\to\Sigma^{n}(\mathbb{G}_{m},1)^{\otimes n},\quad(\mathbb{G}_{m},1)\otimes S^{1}\to(\mathds{P}^{1},\infty)

are homotopy equivalences in
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})_{*}
. Consequently, there are natural equivalences of stable
\infty
-categories

\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})\simeq\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})_{*}[(\mathds{P}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}})^{-1}]\simeq\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})_{*}[(\mathbb{G}_{m}\otimes S^{1})^{-1}]\simeq\mathbf{SH}^{S^{1}}(\mathscr{X})[\mathbb{G}_{m}^{-1}],

where
\mathbf{SH}^{S^{1}}(\mathscr{X}):=\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})_{*}[(S^{1})^{-1}]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack and let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle over
\mathscr{X}
.
The canonical morphism of pointed sheaves

\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})/\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{E})

is an
\mathds{A}^{1}
-equivalence.

Proof.

The proof works as in the scheme case

The Zariski cover of

given by

\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})=\mathcal{E}\cup(\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\smallsetminus\mathscr{X})

where the closed embedding of
\mathscr{X}
into
\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})
is the composition of the embedding of
\mathcal{E}
with the zero section.
The above cover induces an isomorphism of pointed sheaves

\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{E})=\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})/(\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\smallsetminus\mathscr{X}).

Thus, it suffices to show that the embedding
\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})\smallsetminus\mathscr{X}
is an
\mathds{A}^{1}
-equivalence.
By Theorem
1.3
, there exists a smooth Nisnevich cover of
\mathscr{X}
by a scheme
p\colon X\to\mathscr{X}
. Since we have a Nisnevich equivalence
|\check{C}^{\bullet}(p)|\sim\mathscr{X}
it suffices to show the claim after base change to

for all
n
.
Because the formation of the projective space commutes with base change,
we are reduced to the corresponding statement in the case of a scheme, which is
[
MV99
, Proposition 2.17]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let

Proof.

By the same argument as in the previous proof, we reduce to the scheme case, where the lemma is well-known.

is

Proposition 2.12

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack. The

\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})
,
\mathbf{H}(\mathscr{X})_{*}
are presentable and generated under colimits by the family
X

) for

\mathbf{SH}(\mathscr{X})

Since there is no good notion of a small étale site for a general algebraic stack, we consider the category of cartesian sheaves on the

]

Definition 2.24

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack and let

be a prime invertible on

1.

F

2.

F

Moreover,
\mathbb{G}_{m}
is
\otimes
-invertible in
\mathbf{SH}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{S^{1}}(\mathscr{X})^{\wedge}_{\ell}
.

Remark 2.26
.

In the scheme case, the above theorem is simply a direct reformulation of Bachmann’s rigidity theorem.

Proof.

Let
F\in(\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}},\mathrm{Sp})^{\wedge})^{\wedge}_{\ell}
be an
\ell
-complete sheaf of spectra on
\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}

By Theorem

Conversely, assume that

We implicitly used that

5
Cocomplete coefficient systems on algebraic stacks

In this section, we discuss the theory of cocomplete coefficient systems
[
DG22
]
due to Drew-Gallauer in the context of algebraic stacks. This is the
\infty
-categorical reformulation of Ayoub’s stable homotopy 2-functors
[
Ayo07a
,
Ayo07b
]
.

Informally, the main theorem in
[
DG22
]
says that
\mathbf{SH}
is the universal six functor formalism on the category of schemes which satisfies Nisnevich descent and
\mathds{A}^{1}
-invariance, and
\mathds{P}^{1}
-stability.
Any six-functor formalism that is extended to stacks from schemes (for example, by methods of
[
CD24
]
) via Nisnevich descent should satisfy a similar property for representable morphisms for formal reasons. In this section, we extend the theory to include non-representable morphisms.

The following definition is an extension of
[
DG22
, Definition 7.5]
to algebraic stacks.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
be the category of algebraic stacks over
S
. A functor
C\colon\mathbf{Stk}_{S}\rightarrow\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\infty})
taking values in symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-categories with exact symmetric monoidal functors is called a
coefficient system
if it satisfies the following properties:

1.

(a)

For every
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
in
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
, the pullback functor
f^{*}\colon C(\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow C(\mathscr{X})
admits a right adjoint
f_{*}\colon C(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow C(\mathscr{Y})
.

(b)

For every
\mathscr{X}\in\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
, the symmetric monoidal structure on
C(\mathscr{X})
is closed.

2.

For every smooth morphism
\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
in
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
, the functor
f^{*}\colon C(\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow C(\mathscr{X})
admits a left adjoint
f_{\#}
such that:

(a)

For each cartesian square in
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

the exchange transformation
g_{\#}q^{*}\rightarrow p^{*}f_{\#}
is an equivalence.

(b)

The exchange transformation
f_{\#}(-\otimes f^{*}(-))\rightarrow f_{\#}(-)\otimes-
is an equivalence.

3.

(a)

For every closed immersion
i\colon\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
in
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
with complementary open immersion
j\colon\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
, the following square is cartesian in
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{st}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathcal{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathscr{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathcal{U}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{*}}

(b)

There exists a smooth Nisnevich covering
p_{0}\colon X\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
for some
X\in\mathbf{Sm}_{S}
whose Čech nerve induces an equivalence

p^{*}\colon C(\mathscr{X})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\lim_{\Delta}C(X_{\bullet}).

4.

For
\mathscr{X}\in\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
, let
\pi_{\mathds{A}^{1}}\colon\mathds{A}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
denote the projection map with the zero section
s\colon X\rightarrow\mathds{A}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}}
, then

(a)

\pi^{*}_{\mathds{A}^{1}}\colon C(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow C(\mathds{A}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}})
is fully faithful.

(b)

The composite
\pi_{\mathds{A}^{1},\#}\circ s_{*}\colon C(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow C(\mathscr{X})
is an equivalence.

Remark 5.2
.

The above definition differs from
[
DG22
]
in that we need to force Nisnevich descent for schemes to stacks by adding the condition
3.(b)
(see also
[
CD24
]
for a similar statement). The reason for this is that there exist symmetric monoidal functors
C\colon\mathbf{Stk}_{S}\rightarrow\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{st}})
which satisfy
3.(a)
but are not Kan extended from schemes.
For example, the genuine equivariant theory of Hoyois satisfies all the axioms above for representable morphisms. However, it does not satisfy
3.(b)
.

Remark 5.3
.

Another example of a symmetric monoidal functor which does not satisfy Nisnevich descent is given by the functor
\mathbf{SH}_{\mathcal{I}}\colon\mathscr{X}\mapsto\mathbf{SH}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathscr{X}})
, where
\mathcal{I}_{\mathscr{X}}
is the inertia stack of
\mathscr{X}
. It satisfies
3.(a)
but not
3.(b)
.
Moreover,
\mathbf{SH}_{\mathcal{I}}
satisfies
2.
only for
stabiliser preserving
smooth morphisms.
We think of this functor as “sitting in between” the Kan extended
\mathbf{SH}
and the genuine equivariant
\mathbf{SH}
. We will explore these ideas in future work.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
be the category of algebraic stacks over
S
.
A
cocomplete coefficient system
on
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
is a functor
C\colon\mathbf{Stk}_{S}\rightarrow\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{c,\mathrm{st}})
taking values in cocomplete symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-categories with cocontinuous symmetric monoidal functors such that the composition with the forgetful functor
\mathrm{Cat}^{c,\mathrm{st}}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{st}}
is a coefficient system.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
be the category of cocomplete coefficient systems on
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
. A morphism
\mathfrak{R}\colon C\rightarrow D
between cocomplete coefficient systems is an exact symmetric monoidal natural transformation such that for any morphism
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}\in\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
, the exchange transformations

\theta_{f}\colon f_{*}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{\mathscr{X}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{R}_{\mathscr{Y}}\circ f_{*}
and
\theta_{f}^{-1}\colon\mathfrak{R}_{\mathscr{Y}}\circ f_{\#}\rightarrow f_{\#}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{\mathscr{X}}
are invertible.

Remark 5.6
.

As in
[
DG22
]
, we denote by
\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
the category of cocomplete coefficient systems on
\mathbf{Sch}_{S}
.
The inclusion of categories
\mathbf{Sch}_{S}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
induces a functor
i\colon\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}\rightarrow\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
C\in\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
. Then
C
extends to a cocomplete coefficient system on
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
.

Proof.

We use the formalism described in
[
CD24
, Appendix A.2]
to prove this.
Let
\mathbf{Corr}(\mathbf{Sch}_{S})_{\mathrm{sm,all}}
be the category of correspondences whose objects are
S
-schemes, and morphisms are given by spans
X\overset{f}{\leftarrow}Z\overset{g}{\rightarrow}Y
such that
f,g
are morphisms of
S
-schemes and
g
is smooth.

If
C
is cocomplete coefficient system on
\mathbf{Sch}_{S}
, then we have an induced a functor

C^{*}_{\#}\colon\mathbf{Corr}(\mathbf{Sch}_{S})_{\mathrm{sm,all}}\rightarrow\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty})

that sends
X
to
C(X)
, and a span
X\overset{f}{\leftarrow}Z\overset{g}{\rightarrow}Y
to
g_{\#}f^{*}
. This describes a six functor formalism for the pair
(f^{*},f_{\#})
in the sense of
[
Man22
, Appendix A.5]

Let
\mathrm{smrep}
and
\mathrm{stsm}
denote the classes of smooth representable morphisms, respectively, of all smooth morphisms on
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
. Then, by
[
CD24
, Theorem A.16]
,
C
extends to a functor

C^{\prime*}_{\#}\colon\mathbf{Corr}(\mathbf{Stk}_{S})_{\mathrm{smrep,all}}\rightarrow\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{st}}).

Furthermore, by
loc. cit.
Theorem A.19,
C^{\prime*}_{\#}
extends to a functor

\overline{C}^{*}_{\#}\colon\mathbf{Corr}(\mathbf{Stk}_{S})_{\mathrm{stsm,all}}\rightarrow\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{st}}).

We have an inclusion
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}^{\mathrm{op}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Corr}(\mathbf{Stk}_{S})_{\mathrm{stsm,all}}
that sends any morphism
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
to the correspondence
\mathscr{Y}\overset{f}{\leftarrow}\mathscr{X}\overset{id}{\rightarrow}\mathscr{X}
. The composition gives us a functor
\overline{C}\colon\mathbf{Stk}_{S}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{st}})
such that
\mathscr{X}\mapsto\overline{C}^{*}_{\#}(\mathscr{X})
and a morphism
f\mapsto f^{*}
. We claim that
\overline{C}
is a cocomplete coefficient system on
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
.

Properties (1) and (2) are satisfied due to Theorems A.16, A.19 of
loc. cit.
, and the discussion in Appendix A.2 regarding smooth base change and the projection formula.

Properties (3) and (4) follow from Nisnevich descent.
For this, choose a simplicial resolution
p\colon X_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
(for example, the nerve of a smooth Nisnevich covering
X_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
).

To prove property (3), let
i\colon\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
be a closed substack with complementary open
j\colon\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
. Restricting
X_{\bullet}
to these substacks gives us simplicial resolutions
p_{\mathcal{Z}}\colon Z_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
and
p_{\mathcal{U}}\colon U_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
such that the square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(Z_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(X_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(U_{n})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{n*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{n}^{*}}

is Cartesian in each simplicial degree
n
. As
i
is a closed immersion,
i_{n*}
commutes with pullback. Then, taking limits gives us the required Cartesian square.

Similarly for property (4), the map
\pi^{*}_{\mathds{A}^{1}}\colon C(X_{n})\rightarrow C(\mathds{A}^{1}_{X_{n}})
is fully faithful in each degree
n
. Thus, by Nisnevich descent, so is the map
\pi^{*}_{\mathds{A}^{1}}\colon C(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow C(\mathds{A}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}})
. Finally, the map
\pi_{\mathds{A}^{1}\#}\circ s_{*}\colon C(X_{n})\rightarrow C(X_{n})

\pi_{\mathds{A}^{1}\#}
and

\pi_{\mathds{A}^{1},\#}\circ s_{*}\colon C(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow C(\mathscr{X})

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}\colon C\rightarrow D\in\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
be a morphism of cocomplete coefficient systems. Then it extends to a morphism in
\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
.

Proof.

Note that
C
and
D
satisfy decent along
f^{*}
and codescent along
f_{\#}
.
We note that
[
CD24
, Proposition A.19 and A.23]
, are functorial in
\mathfrak{R}
.
Thus, by descent and codescent,
\mathfrak{R}
extends to a morphism in
\mathbf{StCoSy}_{S}^{c}
, and that
\theta_{f}
and
\theta_{f}^{-1}
are invertible.

Proposition 5.9
.

The functor
i\colon\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}\rightarrow\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories.

Proof.

By the previous two lemmas, we have a functor
j\colon\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}\rightarrow\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
, which is a right adjoint of
i
.
Thus, the unit and counit of the adjunction give natural transformations
\mathrm{id}\to j\circ i
and
i\circ j\to\mathrm{id}
.
We claim that these are equivalences.
For any
C\in\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
we have that
j\circ i(C(\mathscr{X}))\simeq\lim_{\Delta}C(X_{\bullet})
, for some Nisnevich local equivalence
X_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
.
As any
C\in\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
satisfies Nisnevich descent along
f^{*}
, we have an isomorphism
j\circ i(C(\mathscr{X}))\to C(\mathscr{X})
. Further, by uniqueness of adjoints, the adjoint pairs
f_{\#}\dashv f^{*}
(for
f
smooth) and
f^{*}\dashv f_{*}
are uniquely determined up to contractible choices. Thus,
j\circ i
is an equivalence.

Similarly, if
D\in\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
, then the extension
j(D)
agrees with
D
on
\mathbf{Sch}_{S}
, hence, the adjunctions
f_{\#}\dashv f^{*}
(for
f
smooth) and
f^{*}\dashv f_{*}
are also preserved. Consequently,
i\circ j(D)\to D
is an equivalence.

Corollary 5.10
.

The object
\mathbf{SH}
is initial in
\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
.

Proof.

The functor
\mathbf{SH}
on
\mathbf{Sch}_{S}
is initial in
\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
by
[
DG22
, Theorem 7.14]
. Thus, by the previous proposition,
\mathbf{SH}
is initial in
\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
.

Remark 5.11
.

A statement similar to the above corollary is also proved in
[
CD24
]
.

By
[
Ayo07a
,
Ayo07b
]
, any cocomplete coefficient system on
\mathbf{Sch}_{S}
gives rise to a six-functor formalism. Together with the results in
[
CD24
]
, we have a similar statement for
\mathbf{Stk}_{S}
.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
C\in\mathbf{StCoSy}_{S}^{c}
be a cocomplete coefficient system. Then for a locally finite type map
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
of algebraic stacks,
C
admits adjoints
f_{!}\dashv f^{!}
, satisfying the conclusion of
[
CD24
, Theorem 4.26]
.

Proof.

Note that the statement is true for the restriction
C_{|\mathbf{Sch}_{S}}
.
Moreover,
C_{|\mathbf{Sch}_{S}}^{*}
is a Nisnevich sheaf, while
C_{|\mathbf{Sch}_{S}!}
is a Nisnevich cosheaf.
Thus, the argument for
\mathbf{SH}
presented in

]
works in this case and

.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}\colon C\rightarrow D
be a morphism in
\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
. Then
\mathfrak{R}
commutes with
f_{!}
, i.e, the natural transformation
\rho_{f}\colon f_{!}\circ\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{R}\circ f_{!}
is invertible.

Proof.

By Theorem 3.4 of
[
Ayo10
]
,
\rho_{f}
is invertible for any morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
in
\mathbf{Sch}_{S}
.

By the argument in
[
CD24
, Proposition A.23]
applied to
\mathfrak{R}
, we see that for any morphism
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}

5.1
Étale motives and realisation

In this section, we extend Ayoub’s étale realisation functor
[
Ayo14
]
to algebraic stacks. In what follows, we freely use the notation and hypothesis in
loc. cit.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring and
I\subset\Lambda
be an ideal.
Let
D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda/I)
and
D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda_{I})
denote the category of étale and
I
-adic sheaves on
\mathbf{Sm}_{\mathscr{X}}
. As both
D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-,\Lambda/I)
and
D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-,\Lambda_{I})
satisfy étale hyperdescent, we have equivalences of
\infty
-categories,

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda/I)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\underset{\Delta}{\lim}\,D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\bullet},\Lambda/I),

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda_{I})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\underset{\Delta}{\lim}\,D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\bullet},\Lambda_{I}),

where
X_{\bullet}
is the Čech nerve of a smooth cover
p:X\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)\rightarrow D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda/I)
and
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{I}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)\rightarrow D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda_{I})
be Ayoub’s étale realisation and
I
-adic étale realisation functors right Kan-extended to stacks.

Theorem 5.14
.

The functors
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)\rightarrow D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda/I)
and
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{I}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)\rightarrow D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda_{I})
commute with Grothendieck’s six operations.

Proof.

The theorem holds for schemes by
[
Ayo14
]
. We need to show that it continues to hold after right Kan extending to stacks.

Firstly, observe that on
\mathbf{Sch}_{S}
, both
D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-,\Lambda/I)
and
D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-,\Lambda_{I})
lie in
\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
. Thus, by Proposition
5.9
, they also lie in
\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
. Then Proposition
5.13
extends the exceptional functors and, therefore, Grothendieck’s six operations for
étale cohomology extend to algebraic stacks.

By
loc. cit.
,
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}|_{\mathbf{Sch}_{S}}
and
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{I}|_{\mathbf{Sch}_{S}}
induce a morphism in
\mathbf{CoSy}^{c}_{S}
. Thus, by Lemma
5.8
,
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
and
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{I}
are morphisms in
\mathbf{StCoSy}^{c}_{S}
. Finally, Proposition
5.13
shows that the realisation functors commute with
f_{!}
, which finishes the proof.

Remark 5.15
.

Let
\mathbf{DA}^{c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)
be the subcategory category of compact objects in
\mathbf{DA}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)
. This category admits a dualising sheaf and satisfies Verdier duality. Then under the étale realisation functor, this yields a dualising sheaf in the category of étale sheaves, and we recover the six functor formalism as described in Olsson-Laszlo
[
LO08a
]
and
[
LO08b
]
.

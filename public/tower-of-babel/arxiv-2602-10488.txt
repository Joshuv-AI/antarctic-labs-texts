Eisenstein-prime Obstruction Sieve for Monogenicity
Khai-Hoan Nguyen-Dang

Abstract
. Alpöge–Bhargava–Shnidman showed that even a strengthened no local obstruction condition for monogenicity does not force a global power integral basis: in the full spaces of cubic and quartic fields, a positive proportion are non-monogenic yet satisfy this ABS fixed-sign condition [ 2 , 1 ] . This raises a natural family-level question: does the same phenomenon persist inside one-parameter families, where the local structure varies in a highly constrained way? In this paper we answer this in the negative for the pure fields K m = ℚ ⁡ ( α ) K_{m}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) with α n = m \alpha^{n}=m ( n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 ) and m m square-free. Writing g ( m ) = [ 𝒪 K m : ℤ [ α ] ] g(m)=[\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]] , we prove that the set of square-free m m for which g ⁡ ( m ) > 1 g(m)>1 but K m K_{m} has no ABS local obstruction has natural density 0 0 . Consequently, in the pure family monogenicity and α \alpha –monogenicity have the same natural density. The proof isolates a reusable mechanism, which we call the Eisenstein-prime obstruction sieve . The argument is packaged in an abstract template and transfers to other Eisenstein parameter families.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Monogenicity, index forms, and fixed-sign local solvability

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field of degree
n\geq 2
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. The field
K
is
monogenic
if
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
for some
\theta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, equivalently if
\mathcal{O}_{K}
admits a power integral basis
\{1,\theta,\dots,\theta^{n-1}\}
. The study of monogenicity is a classical problem in algebraic number theory, originally intertwined with the computation of rings of integers,
discriminants, integral bases and Diophantine equations (see, for example,
[
12
,
16
,
17
]
). A particularly rich testing ground is the family of
pure number fields

K_{m}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{m})
, where one can simultaneously pursue:
(i) explicit integral bases and index computations, and
(ii) global statistics and density questions as
m
varies.
A long line of work develops explicit integral bases for pure fields and reveals striking periodic dependence on congruence classes of
m
(see
[
15
,
23
,
28
]
). These explicit bases typically show that the order
\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt[n]{m}]
can fail to be maximal on congruence-defined subfamilies, thereby separating
\alpha
-monogenicity from monogenicity.

Recall that fixing an orientation
\omega\in\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{K}
identifies the classical
index form

f_{K,\omega}:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to\mathbb{Z}
of degree
N=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
via

1\wedge\beta\wedge\beta^{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\beta^{n-1}=f_{K,\omega}(\beta)\,\omega.

Monogenicity is then equivalent to the Diophantine condition that
f_{K,\omega}
represents
\pm 1
over
\mathbb{Z}
. From the perspective of modern arithmetic geometry, the index form is not merely a convenient polynomial: it is the coordinate expression of a determinant section cutting out monogenic generators on a canonically defined moduli space of generators (see
[
4
,
5
]
and §
2.2
). This perspective cleanly packages local obstructions and clarifies how twisting and local conditions interact with the existence of monogenic generators.

In parallel, the modern arithmetic-statistics program has produced deep theorems on counting number fields and on the distribution of arithmetic properties in large families, culminating in Bhargava’s parametrizations and their many extensions. Against this backdrop, the work of Alpöge–Bhargava–Shnidman shows that, in the
full
families of cubic and quartic fields, there exist global failures of monogenicity that persist even when every local obstruction vanishes. This demonstrates that monogenicity is not governed purely by local conditions in these generic
families, and it motivates the question of whether there are natural families where a local-to-global principle might nevertheless hold in an asymptotic sense. More precisely, they introduced a reinforced notion in which the sign on the right-hand side is fixed
globally
across all places (Definition
2.1
). They proved that this fixed-sign local condition does
not
force global monogenicity: when ordered by discriminant, a positive proportion of cubic fields and a positive proportion of quartic fields are
non
-monogenic yet satisfy the ABS fixed-sign local solvability condition
[
2
,
1
]
. This provides a robust failure of a local–global principle for monogenicity in high-dimensional parameter spaces. This paper investigates whether the ABS phenomenon persists inside
Eisenstein families
, where the local structure is constrained by a distinguished generator and where a
moving totally ramified prime
is built into the parameterization.

More precisely, fix an integer
n\geq 4
and consider the pure fields

K_{m}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha),\qquad\alpha^{n}=m,

with
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\pm 1\}
square-free, i.e.,
X^{n}-m
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
. Write
\mathcal{O}_{m}:=\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}
and denote the
index

g(m):=[\mathcal{O}_{m}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

Thus
g(m)=1
is the distinguished-generator notion of
\alpha
–monogenicity
, while
monogenicity
means
\mathcal{O}_{m}=\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
for
some

\theta\in\mathcal{O}_{m}
.

Even in the pure family,
\alpha
–monogenicity is strictly stronger than monogenicity in small degrees. For example, for
n=2
one has
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})}=\mathbb{Z}[(1+\sqrt{m})/2]
when
m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, so
\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{m}]
fails to be maximal on a positive-density set of parameters, although quadratic fields are always monogenic
[
26
]
. Similarly, in degree
n=3
the order
\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt[3]{m}]
fails to be maximal on explicit congruence classes
(e.g.
m\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
in the cube-free setting), yet the ambient cubic fields remain monogenic by explicit integral basis descriptions
[
6
]
. Consequently, for
n=2,3
the density of monogenicity differs from the density of
\alpha
–monogenicity in the pure family. Beyond very low degrees (even where explicit integral bases and index form computations are available), a general density comparison between monogenicity and
\alpha
–monogenicity for pure fields has not,
to our knowledge, been previously accessible. The guiding question is therefore:

In the pure family, can a positive-density set of non-
\alpha
–monogenic fields still satisfy the ABS fixed-sign
no local obstruction
condition? In other words, do monogenicity and
\alpha
–monogenicity differ in density for
n\geq 4
?

Our main results answer this in the
negative
for all
n\geq 4
and isolate a reusable mechanism which explains why.

1.2.
Main results

Our first theorem shows that, in the pure family, the ABS fixed-sign local condition is
generically

incompatible with the failure of
\alpha
–monogenicity.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.2
)
.

Fix
n\geq 4
and let
m
range over square-free integers with
X^{n}-m
irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then the set of parameters
m
such that
g(m)\geq 2
but
K_{m}
has
no
ABS fixed-sign local obstruction has two-sided natural density
0
.

A conceptual consequence is a density-level identification of
monogenicity
and

\alpha
–monogenicity
in all degrees
n\geq 4
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.4
)
.

Fix
n\geq 4
and let
m
range over square-free integers with
X^{n}-m
irreducible.
Then the set of monogenic fields
K_{m}
differs from the set of
\alpha
–monogenic fields
K_{m}
by a density-zero subset of parameters. Equivalently, monogenicity and
\alpha
–monogenicity have the same two-sided natural density in the pure family.

In particular, combining the density equality with the explicit density of
\alpha
–monogenicity from the
\alpha
–criterion (see
[
27
]
) yields the explicit monogenic density:

\delta\bigl(\{m:\ \mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}\ \text{monogenic}\}\bigr)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p+1}.

This is conceptually surprising when viewed against the
n=2,3
landscape: although the distinguished
generator is
not
expected to capture all global monogenic generators in any fixed degree,
for
n\geq 4
it nevertheless captures them
in density
inside the pure family. This sharp contrast suggests that the failure (or validity) of local-to-global principles for monogenicity is highly sensitive to the geometry of the family: generic families exhibit abundant global failures, whereas Eisenstein families exhibit a density-level local-to-global principle. We also prove quantitative refinements, including log-power savings at fixed index (see Proposition
6.1
).

Moreover, we introduce a flexible framework of
scaled Eisenstein
families in which the Eisenstein–prime obstruction sieve goes through
verbatim
, once one has (a) uniform control of the relevant index targets and (b) nontriviality of the associated Kummer classes.

More precisely, fix a polynomial

h(X)=c_{n-1}X^{n-1}+c_{n-2}X^{n-2}+\cdots+c_{1}X+c_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[X],\qquad c_{0}\neq 0.

For each integer parameter
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the monic polynomial

f_{t}(X)\ :=\ X^{n}+t\,h(X)\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}[X],

and let
\theta_{t}
be a root. Define

K_{t}:=\mathbb{Q}(\theta_{t}),\qquad\mathcal{O}_{t}:=\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}},\qquad g(t):=[\mathcal{O}_{t}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{t}]]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

We say that
K_{t}
is
\theta_{t}
–monogenic
if
g(t)=1
. Consider the square-free parameter set

\mathcal{T}_{h,\mathrm{sf}}:=\Bigl\{\,t\in\mathbb{Z}:\ |t|>1,\ t\ \text{square-free, and }\gcd(t,c_{0})=1\,\Bigr\}.

We prove that, within the scaled Eisenstein family, the set of parameters that are
ABS
-unobstructed yet fail to be
\theta_{t}
–monogenic has density zero.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
7.3
)
.

Fix
n\geq 4
and
h\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
as above, and let
N=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
.
Assume the following hypotheses on the square-free parameter set
\mathcal{T}_{h,\mathrm{sf}}
:

(a)

(Finite index values)
There exists a finite set
G\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that

g(t)\in G
for all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h,\mathrm{sf}}
.

(b)

(Kummer nontriviality)
For every
g\in G
with
g\geq 2
,

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N},g^{1/N})\neq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N}).

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{h,\mathrm{sf}}
be the set of
t
such that:

(i)

g(t)\geq 2
, and

(ii)

K_{t}
has
no
local obstruction to monogenicity in the
ABS
fixed-sign sense.

Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
has two-sided natural density
0
in
\mathbb{Z}
.

We exhibit explicit thin-parameter subfamilies showing that: (i) the Kummer nontriviality hypothesis is not a technical artifact but a genuinely necessary input for the sieve (Theorem
7.6
); and (ii) even in one-parameter families, the
ABS
fixed–sign local condition does
not
in general force the
distinguished
generator to have index
1
(Theorem
7.10
), in line with the broader phenomenon that local conditions may fail to govern global monogenicity.

1.3.
Method: the Eisenstein-prime obstruction sieve

The global argument is not a case-by-case integral-basis computation; rather, it is a general sieve strategy that turns local coset rigidity into density bounds by producing many primes
q
at which
x^{n}-m
is Eisenstein and then showing that a single global generator cannot satisfy all local coset constraints simultaneously except on a density-zero set.

The mechanism is deliberately modular and can be summarized as a three-step pipeline:

(1) Local rigidity: a single-coset value set on local generators.

Let
R
be a complete DVR with residue characteristic
p\nmid N=\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}
, and let
A/R
be a rank-
n
DVR of residue degree
1
generated by a uniformizer.
Theorem
3.7
shows that for any orientation
\omega
of
A

the unit values of the local index form
f_{\omega}
on
all

R
-generators of
A
form exactly one coset:

\{\,f_{\omega}(\beta)\in R^{\times}:\ A=R[\beta]\,\}=f_{\omega}(\pi)\cdot(R^{\times})^{N}.

Equivalently, the induced map to
H^{1}(\mathrm{Spec}R,\mu_{N})\simeq R^{\times}/(R^{\times})^{N}
is constant and defines
a canonical
local obstruction class

\kappa(A/R,\omega)
.

(2) One-prime obstruction certificates at moving Eisenstein primes.

In the pure family, every prime
q\mid m
is Eisenstein for
X^{n}-m
, so

\mathcal{O}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is precisely of the above type (a residue-degree
1
DVR generated by
\alpha
). A top-wedge computation identifies
f_{m,\omega}(\alpha)=\pm g(m)
(Lemma
2.9
),
and moreover
q\nmid g(m)
for
q\mid m
(Lemma
4.11
),
so
g(m)
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Thus Theorem
3.7
converts fixed-sign local solvability at
q
into the Kummer constraint

\pm 1\in\pm g(m)\cdot(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

For primes
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
the sign is itself an
N
th power, so solvability forces the residue-field condition

\overline{g(m)}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

Then Kummer theory translates
\,N
-th power conditions into Frobenius conditions. More precisely, fix
n\geq 4
and
N=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
, and for a fixed index value
g\geq 2
put

K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
and
L=K(g^{1/N})
.
For primes
q\nmid 2Ng
with
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
(so that
q
splits completely in
K
),
Kummer theory identifies the residue condition
g\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
with a splitting condition in the
Kummer extension
L/K
:

q\text{ splits completely in }L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

Equivalently, the condition
g
is an
N
-th power modulo
q
is encoded by the Artin/Frobenius element in
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
(or, in class field theoretic language, by the power residue symbol in a Kummer extension); see, for example, Milne’s treatment of the Artin map and Kummer extensions
[
25
, §IV.4–IV.5]
. In other words,
a single Eisenstein prime divisor of
m
can certify the existence of an ABS fixed-sign local obstruction by a concrete
N
th-power residue test on
g(m)
.

(3) Prime production and sieve closure.

Assume
L\neq K
. Then Chebotarev implies that the set of primes that split completely in
K
but not in
L

has positive density, and (away from the finitely many primes dividing
2Ng
) this is exactly the set

P_{g}=\Bigl\{\,q:\ q\nmid 2Ng,\ q\equiv 1\!\!\pmod{2N},\ g\notin(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}\,\Bigr\}.

Thus
P_{g}
is (up to finitely many primes) a Chebotarev set in
L/\mathbb{Q}
, hence a frobenian set in the sense of
Serre
[
30
]
. A classical sieve argument then bounds the set of integers whose prime factors avoid
P_{g}
; see, e.g.,
[
20
, Ch. 5]
or
[
22
, §6.4]
.
This is the closure step used repeatedly in the paper: once
P_{g}
is produced with positive density, most parameters in a thick set (such as squarefree integers in a progression) necessarily have a
prime divisor in
P_{g}
, which then forces a fixed-sign local obstruction at that prime via the one-prime
certificate discussed above. Since
\sum_{q\in P_{g}}1/q=\infty
, avoiding
P_{g}
forces density zero, with quantitative log-power bounds
available via a Chebotarev–Mertens estimate and an upper-bound sieve. A sieve upper bound then shows that
parameters avoiding this Frobenian set are sparse (see
[
20
,
3
]
).

Remarks on the method

We refer to this three-step pipeline as the
Eisenstein-prime obstruction sieve
: the obstructing primes are rational primes
q
at which the specialization polynomial is Eisenstein
(typically
q\mid
the parameter), and the sieve is the classical sifting step excluding integers divisible by primes in the obstructing set
P_{g}
. We emphasize that Eisenstein prime here means
Eisenstein for the specialization polynomial
(not a prime in the ring of Eisenstein integers
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
).

This paradigm, Chebotarev sets (or frobenian sets) combined with a sieve argument, is classical and is treated systematically by Serre in his study of frobenian sets, where he explicitly combines Chebotarev-style arguments with Selberg-type sieve techniques
[
30
]
. The new point in the present paper is that the
local
obstruction is extracted in a particularly explicit form from primes dividing the parameter, via Eisenstein portability and our local coset constraint.

From the perspective of arithmetic statistics, our closure step is a one-dimensional analogue of the general principle of imposing finitely many local conditions via sieve methods. In higher-dimensional parameter spaces, this is often carried out via the
Ekedahl sieve
(originating in Ekedahl’s work
[
11
]
)
and its refinements (e.g. Poonen
[
29
]
, and Bhargava’s geometric sieve
framework
[
9
]
; see also the applications to squarefree discriminants and maximality problems in Bhargava–Shankar–Wang
[
7
,
8
]
). In contrast, our distinctive contribution is the explicit
one-prime
extraction of a fixed-sign local obstruction at Eisenstein prime divisors, which interfaces cleanly with a classical Chebotarev sieve on the parameter.

Our sieve also extends beyond the specific target
\pm 1
. The reason is conceptual: the local rigidity input concerns unit cosets modulo
N
-th powers, rather than the sign classes
{\pm 1}
per se. In particular, one can study, for example, the density of parameters for which there is local solvability of equations of the form

f=\varepsilon\cdot u,

with
u
ranging over prescribed unit classes; and in rigid families, the scarcity of parameters that are globally solvable yet locally everywhere solvable at a fixed index target.

A notable feature of the obstruction sieve is that the family-specific inputs are minimal: one needs (i) a supply of
variable Eisenstein primes
indexed by the parameter, and
(ii) control of the set of possible indices
g(\cdot)
on the parameter set.
This allows the method to transfer to other Eisenstein parameter families; see
§
7
, where we develop a general scaled Eisenstein framework and prove density-zero results under finite index-value and Kummer-nontriviality hypotheses (e.g. Theorem
7.3
).

At the same time, the paper records constructions illustrating the necessity of the hypotheses: in thin parameter sets (e.g. primes) avoidance of a positive-density prime set may not be rare,
and if
g
is an
N
th power in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
then the relevant Kummer extension becomes trivial and the
Chebotarev obstruction set
P_{g}
collapses (Remark
7.7
). We also include a fixed-index twist construction (Theorem
7.10
) showing that,
in general one-parameter families, ABS-unobstructedness does not force a
distinguished
generator to have index
1
, even on positive-density parameter sets.

1.4.
Related works and future directions

Fix a finite set of places
S
. One may call an order
S
-monogenic
if

\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/S]=\mathbb{Z}[1/S][\theta]
, i.e. monogenicity after allowing denominators
supported on
S
. In the generator-scheme formalism
[
4
,
5
]
, this is naturally
encoded by replacing an affine generator space by a projective (or partially compactified) one:
informally, generators are allowed to acquire poles along
S
.
The obstruction sieve should extend verbatim after
excluding
primes in
S
from the portable set, leading to a density statement ”away from
S
”.

A sharper variant is to define
projective monogenicity
up to scaling: ask whether
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is generated by some
\theta
modulo the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
–action (or more generally an affine group action), turning the obstruction class into a twist-class in
the relevant cohomology group. It is closely related to the notion of rational monogenicity
[
14
,
13
]
.
A worthwhile goal is to build the corresponding projective generator space and identify the correct
line bundle whose local value set admits a one-coset description.

Let
K/F
be a finite extension of number fields. Relative monogenicity asks whether

\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathcal{O}_{F}[\theta]
. Many explicit computations especially in Kummer-type extensions, for instance
[
31
]
, indicate that residue-degree-one,
totally ramified primes of
F
behave as the right portable primes, and the relevant obstruction
classes land in
\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}/(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}}^{\times})^{N}
.
This suggests a clean generalization of the sieve from
\mathbb{Q}
to an arbitrary base field
F
.

Although different from power integral bases, the problem of freeness of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
as a

\mathbb{Z}[G]
–module exhibits a parallel structure: global freeness, local conditions, and
cohomological obstructions. For context on restricted Hilbert–Speiser/Leopoldt properties and
related obstructions, see
[
10
]
and the Hilbert–Speiser setting developed
around Swan modules
[
18
]
.
In parameterized Galois families with moving ramification, it is natural to ask whether one can
identify a
portable local obstruction class
that produces Frobenian obstructing primes,
and then run the same Chebotarev–sieve closure.

.

2.2.
ABHS monogenerators and the index-form ideal

We recall the ABHS framework: monogenerators form a representable moduli scheme and the non-generator
locus is cut out by a canonically defined ideal (the index-form ideal)
[
4
,
5
]
.

Let
\pi:S^{\prime}\to S
be finite locally free of constant degree
n\geq 1
with
S

A global section
\theta\in\Gamma(S^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}})
induces an
S

S^{\prime}\to\mathbf{A}^{1}_{S}
,
and
\theta
is a

iff this map is a closed immersion, equivalently iff

\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{S}[\theta]
Zariski-locally on
S
.

Theorem 2.2

(ABHS representability)
.

Let
\pi:S^{\prime}\to S
be finite locally free of constant degree
n
with
S
locally noetherian.
Let
X\to S
be quasiprojective.
Then there exists a smooth, quasi-affine
S
-scheme
\mathcal{M}_{X,S^{\prime}/S}
representing the functor on

S
-schemes

that assigns the set of sections
S^{\prime}\times_{S}T\to X\times_{S}T

that are closed immersions over
T
.
If
X=\mathbf{A}^{1}_{S}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{X,S^{\prime}/S}
is affine; write
\mathcal{M}_{S^{\prime}/S}:=\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{A}^{1}_{S},S^{\prime}/S}
.

Let
R_{S^{\prime}/S}:=\operatorname{Res}_{S^{\prime}/S}(\mathbf{A}^{1}_{S^{\prime}})
be the Weil restriction.
When
S=\mathrm{Spec}A
and
S^{\prime}=\mathrm{Spec}B
with
B
finite free of rank
n
over

R_{S^{\prime}/S}\cong\mathbf{A}^{n}_{S}

A

.

Fix an
A

of

\mathbf{A}^{n}_{S}
.
Define the

\Theta\;:=\;x_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}e_{n}\;\in\;B\otimes_{A}A[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

For

Theorem 2.3

(ABHS: the index form cuts out monogenerators)
.

With notation as above,
\mathcal{M}_{S^{\prime}/S}
identifies with the distinguished open

\mathcal{M}_{S^{\prime}/S}\;\cong\;\mathrm{Spec}A[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},i(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})^{-1}]\;\subseteq\;\mathrm{Spec}A[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

.
Its vanishing defines a closed subscheme

.

Let

be a commutative ring and

a finite locally free

.
An

The following observations are easy.

Lemma 2.4

(Translation invariance)
.

Let

be a commutative ring,

a finite free

-algebra of rank

,

.

Proof.

For each

, expand
\beta^{j}
as an

-linear combination of

with

.
The transition matrix from
(1,\beta-c,\dots,(\beta-c)^{n-1})
to
(1,\beta,\dots,\beta^{n-1})

is upper triangular with diagonal entries

and the top wedge is unchanged.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Unit wedge implies generation)
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and

a finite free

-module of rank

.
Fix

If

Choose an

∎

We can reinterprete the index form as follows.

Lemma 2.6

(Determinant equals wedge coefficient)
.

Assume

Write

Remark 2.7

(Homogeneity)
.

The polynomial

is homogeneous of total degree

in the variables

.
Equivalently,

for

.

Throughout the paper, we set

Throughout,
\mu_{N}
denotes the fppf group scheme of
N
th roots of unity.
For a DVR

we write
\mathfrak{p}
for its maximal ideal and

for its residue field. All schemes are assumed separated.

Remark 2.8
.

A prime

is called

-tame
if

. It is needed for the

th-power map

to be étale and for the Hensel–Kummer arguments used below
(e.g. Lemma

).

This condition is

equivalent to classical tame ramification (i.e.

).
Indeed, for every

one has

since

is invertible modulo

and

would force

.
However, at

the implication can fail: if

then

but

(e.g.

).

The following result says that index form value equals the global index up to sign.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

be a number field of degree

with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Fix an orientation
\omega\in\wedge^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{K}
and define the index form

f_{K,\omega}:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be such that
K=\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
, and set

I(\beta):=[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\beta]]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

Then

f_{K,\omega}(\beta)=\pm I(\beta),\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\beta]]=\bigl|f_{K,\omega}(\beta)\bigr|.

In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\beta]
if and only if
f_{K,\omega}(\beta)=\pm 1
.
Moreover, replacing
\omega
by
-\omega
replaces
f_{K,\omega}
by
-f_{K,\omega}
, so

\bigl|f_{K,\omega}(\beta)\bigr|
is independent of the choice of orientation.

Proof.

Choose a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and write

\omega_{0}:=e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n}
. Since
\wedge^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a free rank-
1

\mathbb{Z}
-module,
we have
\omega=\pm\omega_{0}
.

Because
K=\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
and
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the elements
1,\beta,\dots,\beta^{n-1}
are

\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent and therefore
\mathbb{Z}
-linearly independent; hence they form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
the order
\mathbb{Z}[\beta]
. Write each
\beta^{j}
in the basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
:

\beta^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i,j+1}e_{i}\qquad(0\leq j\leq n-1),

and let
T=(t_{i,j})\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
be the resulting change-of-basis matrix from

(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
to
(1,\beta,\dots,\beta^{n-1})
.

By multilinearity and alternation of the wedge product,

1\wedge\beta\wedge\cdots\wedge\beta^{n-1}=\det(T)\,(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n})=\det(T)\,\omega_{0}.

Comparing with the defining relation

1\wedge\beta\wedge\cdots\wedge\beta^{n-1}=f_{K,\omega}(\beta)\,\omega

and using
\omega=\pm\omega_{0}
gives
f_{K,\omega}(\beta)=\pm\det(T)
.

Finally,
\mathbb{Z}[\beta]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
is an inclusion of full-rank
\mathbb{Z}
-lattices whose matrix in the
bases
(1,\beta,\dots,\beta^{n-1})
and
(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
is exactly

. Therefore

[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\beta]]=|\det(T)|,

and combining with
f_{K,\omega}(\beta)=\pm\det(T)
yields the claim.
∎

3.
Kummer theory and the local obstruction class

Let
S
be a scheme on which
N
is invertible. The Kummer exact sequence reads

1\to\mu_{N}\to\mathbf{G}_{m}\xrightarrow{(\cdot)^{N}}\mathbf{G}_{m}\to 1.

Taking cohomology yields an exact sequence

(3)

0\to\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})/(\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}))^{N}\longrightarrow H^{1}(S,\mu_{N})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Pic}(S)[N]\to 0.

We have the following straightforward observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a complete disrete valuation ring (DVR) such that
N
is invertible in
R
.
Then
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Spec}R)=0
and hence

H^{1}(\mathrm{Spec}R,\mu_{N})\;\cong\;R^{\times}/(R^{\times})^{N}.

Proof.

A DVR is a PID, hence
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Spec}R)=0
. Apply (
3
).
∎

Definition 3.2

(Kummer class of a unit)
.

Let
R
be a ring with
N
invertible.
For

, define its
Kummer class

[u]\in H^{1}(\mathrm{Spec}R,\mu_{N})
to be the class of the

\mu_{N}
-torsor

T_{u}:=\mathrm{Spec}R[y]/(y^{N}-u).

If
R
is a DVR,
[u]
corresponds to the coset
u\cdot(R^{\times})^{N}
via Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R
be a complete DVR with
N
invertible in
R
.
Let
A
be a finite free commutative
R
-algebra of rank
n
that is a DVR,
with residue degree
1
, and assume there exists a uniformizer
\pi\in A
such that
A=R[\pi]
.
Fix an orientation
\omega\in\bigwedge\nolimits^{\mathrm{top}}_{R}A
and let
f_{\omega}:A\to R
be (
2
). Then
f_{\omega}(\pi)\in R^{\times}
.

Proof.

Since
A=R[\pi]
and
A
has rank
n
over
R
, the set
\{1,\pi,\dots,\pi^{n-1}\}
is an
R
-basis.
Thus
1\wedge\pi\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi^{n-1}
is a generator of
\bigwedge\nolimits^{\mathrm{top}}_{R}A
, hence equals a unit multiple of

.
∎

It gives rise to the following local obstruction class.

Definition 3.4
.

Keep the notations as in
3.3
. Define the
local obstruction class

\kappa(A/R,\omega)\;:=\;[\,f_{\omega}(\pi)\,]\in H^{1}(\mathrm{Spec}R,\mu_{N})\cong R^{\times}/(R^{\times})^{N}.

To state our main result of this section, we need some elementary lemmas as follows.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R
be a complete DVR with residue field
k
of characteristic
p
.
Assume
p\nmid N
. Then for
u\in R^{\times}
,

u\in(R^{\times})^{N}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\bar{u}\in(k^{\times})^{N}.

Proof.

If
u=v^{N}
then
\bar{u}=\bar{v}^{\,N}
.
Conversely, assume
\bar{u}=\bar{v}_{0}^{\,N}
and lift
\bar{v}_{0}
to
v_{0}\in R^{\times}
.
Let
F(X)=X^{N}-u
. Then
F(v_{0})\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
and
F^{\prime}(v_{0})=Nv_{0}^{N-1}\in R^{\times}

since
p\nmid N
. Hensel’s lemma gives

with
v^{N}=u
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R
be a DVR with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, and let
A
be a finite free
R
-algebra of rank
n

that is a DVR with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
A/\mathfrak{m}\cong R/\mathfrak{p}
.
Then the ramification index of

is
e=n
, and
\mathfrak{p}A=\mathfrak{m}^{n}
.
In particular, if

then
\mathfrak{p}A\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.

Proof.

Since
A
is a DVR finite over
R
,
A
is torsion-free and hence finite flat over
R
of rank
n
.
Let
e
be the ramification index and

the residue degree. Then
n=ef
.
The residue field assumption gives

, hence
e=n
.
The identity
\mathfrak{p}A=\mathfrak{m}^{e}
holds for extensions of DVRs, giving
\mathfrak{p}A=\mathfrak{m}^{n}
.
∎

We state and prove our local Kummer rigidity.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
R
be a complete DVR with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, residue field
k
, and residue characteristic
p
.
Let
A
be a finite free commutative
R
-algebra of rank
n\geq 2
such that:

(i)

A
is a DVR with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
;

(ii)

A/\mathfrak{m}\cong k
;

(iii)

there exists a uniformizer
\pi\in\mathfrak{m}
with
A=R[\pi]
.

Fix an orientation
\omega\in\bigwedge\nolimits^{\mathrm{top}}_{R}A
and let
f_{\omega}:A\to R
be defined by (
2
).
Assume
p\nmid N
.

Then the set of unit values of
f_{\omega}
on
R
-generators of
A
is a single coset:

\{\,f_{\omega}(\beta)\in R^{\times}:A=R[\beta]\,\}=f_{\omega}(\pi)\cdot(R^{\times})^{N}.

Equivalently, the composite map

\mathcal{M}_{A/R}(R)\xrightarrow{f_{\omega}}R^{\times}\longrightarrow R^{\times}/(R^{\times})^{N}\;\cong\;H^{1}(\mathrm{Spec}R,\mu_{N})

is constant, with value
[f_{\omega}(\pi)]
.

Proof.

Let
\beta\in A
be an
R
-generator, so
A=R[\beta]
.
Choose
b_{0}\in R
lifting the residue class of
\beta
in
A/\mathfrak{m}\cong k
, and set
\beta_{0}:=\beta-b_{0}\in\mathfrak{m}
.
By Lemma
2.4
,
f_{\omega}(\beta)=f_{\omega}(\beta_{0})
and
A=R[\beta_{0}]
.

If
\beta_{0}\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, then for every polynomial
P(T)\in R[T]
one has

P(\beta_{0})\equiv P(0)\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{2}}
, hence
R[\beta_{0}]\subseteq R+\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.
We claim
R+\mathfrak{m}^{2}
contains no element of valuation
1
in
A
, contradicting
A=R[\beta_{0}]
.

Indeed, let
x=r+y
with
r\in R
and
y\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.
If
r\notin\mathfrak{p}
, then
r
is a unit of
R
and hence of
A
, so
v_{A}(x)=0
.
If
r\in\mathfrak{p}
, then
x\in\mathfrak{p}A+\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.
By Lemma
3.6
,
\mathfrak{p}A=\mathfrak{m}^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}
(since
n\geq 2
),
so
x\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}
and thus
v_{A}(x)\geq 2
.
In either case
v_{A}(x)\neq 1
, proving the claim.

Therefore
\beta_{0}\notin\mathfrak{m}^{2}
and hence
v_{A}(\beta_{0})=1
, i.e.
\beta_{0}
is a uniformizer.

Thus
\beta_{0}=\pi u
for some
u\in A^{\times}
.
Let
u_{0}\in k^{\times}
be the image of
u
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.

Write
u=u_{0}+\pi w
for some
w\in A
(since
u\in A^{\times}
and
u_{0}\in k^{\times}
is its residue class).
Then for each
j\geq 1
we have the identity in
A

(\pi u)^{j}=\pi^{j}(u_{0}+\pi w)^{j}=\pi^{j}u_{0}^{j}+\pi^{j+1}\cdot(\text{some element of }A),

hence

(\pi u)^{j}\equiv u_{0}^{j}\,\pi^{j}\pmod{\pi^{j+1}A}.

Reducing modulo
\mathfrak{p}A=\mathfrak{m}^{n}
and using
j+1\leq n
(since
1\leq j\leq n-1
) yields the
congruence

\overline{(\pi u)^{j}}\equiv u_{0}^{j}\,\bar{\pi}^{j}\pmod{\bar{\pi}^{j+1}}\qquad\text{in }A/\mathfrak{p}A.

In
A/\mathfrak{p}A
, the images of
1,\pi,\dots,\pi^{n-1}
form a
k
-basis and
\bar{\pi}
is nilpotent of order
n
.
For
1\leq j\leq n-1
,

\overline{\beta_{0}^{\,j}}=\overline{(\pi u)^{j}}=\bar{\pi}^{\,j}\bar{u}^{\,j}\equiv u_{0}^{j}\bar{\pi}^{\,j}\pmod{\bar{\pi}^{\,j+1}},

since
\bar{u}\equiv u_{0}\pmod{\bar{\pi}}
.
Hence the change-of-basis matrix
\bar{T}
from
(1,\bar{\pi},\dots,\bar{\pi}^{n-1})
to

(1,\overline{\beta_{0}},\dots,\overline{\beta_{0}^{n-1}})
is upper triangular with diagonal entries

1,u_{0},u_{0}^{2},\dots,u_{0}^{n-1}
, so

\det(\bar{T})=u_{0}^{1+2+\cdots+(n-1)}=u_{0}^{N}\in(k^{\times})^{N}.

Since
A=R[\beta_{0}]
and
\beta_{0}
is integral over
R
, the elements
1,\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{0}^{n-1}
generate the free
R
-module
A
; as
R
is local and
\mathrm{rank}_{R}(A)=n
, they form an
R
-basis, so the change-of-basis matrix
T
indeed lies in
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
.

Let
T\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
be the change-of-basis matrix from
(1,\pi,\dots,\pi^{n-1})
to
(1,\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{0}^{n-1})
.
Then

1\wedge\beta_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\beta_{0}^{n-1}=\det(T)\cdot(1\wedge\pi\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi^{n-1}),

so
f_{\omega}(\beta_{0})=\det(T)f_{\omega}(\pi)
.
Reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
gives
\det(T)\bmod\mathfrak{p}=\det(\bar{T})\in(k^{\times})^{N}
.
By Lemma
3.5
,
\det(T)\in(R^{\times})^{N}
.
Hence
f_{\omega}(\beta)=f_{\omega}(\beta_{0})\in f_{\omega}(\pi)\cdot(R^{\times})^{N}
.

For any

, set
\beta=v\pi
. Then

and by homogeneity

f_{\omega}(\beta)=v^{N}f_{\omega}(\pi)
(Remark

).
Thus all of
f_{\omega}(\pi)(R^{\times})^{N}
occurs.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

In the situation of Theorem

, for any

.
∎

4.
Chebotarev–Kummer production of obstructing primes

In this section we package the prime-production step used later in the sieve. Fix an integer
N\geq 2
and
g\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. The condition

g\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
(or not) is detected by splitting in a Kummer extension of a
cyclotomic field. Chebotarev then supplies infinitely many primes
q
with

g\notin(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
, and in fact a positive-density set.

4.1.
The Kummer–cyclotomic field and a splitting criterion

Firstly, we recall some basics on the Galois theory of Kummer–cyclotomic fields.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
g\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Put

K:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N}),\qquad E:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N},g^{1/N}),\qquad L:=K(g^{1/N})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N},g^{1/N}).

Then
K/\mathbb{Q}
and
E/\mathbb{Q}
are finite Galois extensions and
L=KE
.
In particular,
L/\mathbb{Q}
is finite Galois, and
L
is the splitting field over
\mathbb{Q}

of the polynomial
(X^{N}-g)(X^{2N}-1)
.

Proof.

The field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
is cyclotomic, hence finite Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
.
The field
E=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N},g^{1/N})
is the splitting field of
X^{N}-g
over
\mathbb{Q}
, hence finite Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Since
\zeta_{N}\in K
and
g^{1/N}\in E
, we have
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N},g^{1/N})=KE
.
As a compositum of finite Galois extensions,
L/\mathbb{Q}
is finite Galois.
Finally, the compositum of splitting fields is the splitting field of the product polynomial.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Kummer splitting criterion in residue fields)
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
g\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, and put
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
and
L=K(g^{1/N})
.
Let
q
be a rational prime satisfying
q\nmid 2Ng
and
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
.
Then
q
splits completely in
L
if and only if
g\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
.

Proof.

Since
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
and
q\nmid 2N
, the prime
q
splits completely in
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
.
Thus for any prime
\mathfrak{q}\mid q
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
we have
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{q}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Moreover,
\mu_{N}\subseteq K
(because
\zeta_{N}=\zeta_{2N}^{2}\in K
), so
L/K
is a (possibly proper) Kummer extension.

Let
\gamma:=g^{1/N}\in L
, so
\gamma^{N}=g\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
q\nmid Ng
, the prime
\mathfrak{q}
is unramified in
L/K
.
Fix a prime
\mathfrak{Q}\mid\mathfrak{q}
in
L
and let
\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
be a Frobenius element.
For the algebraic integer
\gamma
we have the standard congruence

\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\gamma)\equiv\gamma^{q}\pmod{\mathfrak{Q}}.

Thus
\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}}=1
if and only if
\gamma^{q-1}\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{Q}}
.
Because
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
we have
N\mid(q-1)
, so

\gamma^{q-1}=\gamma^{N\cdot\frac{q-1}{N}}=(\gamma^{N})^{\frac{q-1}{N}}=g^{\frac{q-1}{N}}.

Reducing
g
modulo
\mathfrak{q}
(equivalently modulo
q
) gives an element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.
In the cyclic group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
of order
q-1
with
N\mid(q-1)
, the congruence

g^{(q-1)/N}\equiv 1
is equivalent to
g\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
.
Hence
\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}}=1
if and only if
g\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
.

Finally, since
q
splits completely in
K
, the prime
q
splits completely in
L
if and only if

\mathfrak{q}
splits completely in
L/K
, i.e. if and only if
\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}}=1
.
∎

We recall the Chebotarev density theorem in
[
30
, §3]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
E/F
be a finite Galois extension of number fields, with Galois group

G=\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)
. Let
C\subseteq G
be a conjugacy-stable subset
(equivalently, a union of conjugacy classes). For each nonzero prime ideal

\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
unramified in
E
, write

\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/F)\subseteq G
for its Frobenius conjugacy class.
Define

\pi_{C}(x;E/F):=\#\Bigl\{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}:\mathfrak{p}\text{ unramified in }E,\;N\mathfrak{p}\leq x,\;\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/F)\subseteq C\Bigr\}.

Then, as
x\to\infty
,

\pi_{C}(x;E/F)\sim\frac{|C|}{|G|}\,\mathrm{Li}(x),\qquad\text{where }\mathrm{Li}(x):=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log t}.

Equivalently, the set of (unramified) prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
F
with

\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/F)\subseteq C
has natural density
|C|/|G|
.

The theorem yields the following important consequence.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
E/F
be as above. The set of prime ideals of
F
splitting completely in
E

(i.e.
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/F)=\{1\}
) has density
1/|G|=1/[E:F]
.

In particular, if
K\subsetneq L
are finite Galois extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
, then
the set of rational primes
p
that split completely in
K
but do
not
split
completely in
L
has density

\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-\frac{1}{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}\;>\;0.

We define Chebotarev–Kummer obstructing primes as follows.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
g\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
Assume
L=K(g^{1/N})\neq K
for
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
.
Define

P_{g}:=\Bigl\{\,q\ \text{prime}:\ q\nmid 2Ng,\ q\equiv 1\!\!\!\pmod{2N},\ \text{and }g\notin(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}\Bigr\}.

Applying Chebotarev’s theorem to obstructing primes yields the following.

Proposition 4.6
.

Assume
L\neq K
.
Then
P_{g}
has (natural) density

\delta_{g}=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-\frac{1}{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}>0

among rational primes. In particular,
P_{g}
is infinite and

\sum_{q\in P_{g}}\frac{1}{q}=\infty.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, both
K/\mathbb{Q}
and
L/\mathbb{Q}
are finite Galois extensions with
K\subsetneq L
.
Chebotarev’s density theorem implies that the set of rational primes splitting completely in a finite Galois
extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
has density
1/[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.
Therefore, the set of primes splitting completely in
K
but
not
splitting completely in
L
has density

\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-\frac{1}{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}>0.

Discarding the finite set of primes dividing
2Ng
does not change density.

If
q\nmid 2Ng
splits completely in
K
, then
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
; moreover, by
Lemma
4.2
, such a prime splits completely in
L
if and only if

g\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
. Hence, up to finitely many exceptions, the condition
split completely in
K
but not in
L
is equivalent to
q\in P_{g}
.
This proves that
P_{g}
has density
\delta_{g}
.

Finally, Chebotarev’s theorem yields

\#\{q\leq X:q\in P_{g}\}\sim\delta_{g}\,X/\log X
, and partial summation gives

\sum_{q\leq X,\ q\in P_{g}}1/q=\delta_{g}\log\log X+O(1)
, implying
\sum_{q\in P_{g}}1/q=\infty
.
∎

Corollary 4.7

(Sign-killing in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}
)
.

Let
N\geq 1
and let
q
be a prime with
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
. Then

-1\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}\qquad\text{and}\qquad-1\in(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

In particular,

\pm(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}=(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pm(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}=(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

Proof.

Since
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
, the cyclic group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
has an element of order
2N
; its
N
th power is
-1
,
so
-1\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
.
Also
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
implies
q>2N\geq N
, hence
q\nmid N
.
By Lemma
3.5
(Hensel criterion for
N
th powers), the inclusion

-1\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
lifts to
-1\in(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
.
∎

We recall the two-sided natural density.

Definition 4.8
.

For a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, its
two-sided natural density
(if it exists) is

\delta(A):=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{m\in A:\ |m|\leq X\}}{2X}.

Lemma 4.9

(Avoiding a divergent set of primes has density zero)
.

Let
P
be a set of rational primes such that
\sum_{p\in P}1/p=\infty
.
Let
A_{P}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
be the set of
P
-free
integers (i.e.
p\nmid m
for all
p\in P
).
Then
\delta(A_{P})=0
.

Proof.

For
Y\geq 2
, let
P(Y)=\{p\in P:\ p\leq Y\}
.
By inclusion–exclusion (or CRT), the set of integers divisible by no prime in
P(Y)
has two-sided density

\prod_{p\in P(Y)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr).

Hence the upper two-sided density of
A_{P}
satisfies

\overline{\delta}(A_{P})\leq\prod_{p\in P(Y)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)\qquad(Y\geq 2).

Using
\log(1-x)\leq-x
for
0<x<1
gives

\log\prod_{p\in P(Y)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)\leq-\sum_{p\in P(Y)}\frac{1}{p}\xrightarrow[Y\to\infty]{}-\infty,

so the product tends to
0
and therefore
\delta(A_{P})=0
.
∎

Remark 4.10

(Thin parameter sets)
.

Lemma
4.9
concerns density in
\mathbb{Z}
(and hence in any positive-density subfamily of
\mathbb{Z}
,
such as square-free integers). It does not automatically imply analogous statements in very thin parameter sets
(e.g. the primes), where a separate sieve input is required.

4.2.
Nontriviality of the Kummer extension for pure-field index values

For the sieve arguments below we require that
L\neq K
when
g=g(m)\geq 2

arises as a pure power-basis index. It is noticed that Eisenstein primes do not divide the pure power-basis index.

Lemma 4.11
.

Fix
n\geq 2
and let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be square-free such that
X^{n}-m
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
\alpha^{n}=m
, set
K_{m}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, and write

g(m):=[\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

If
q
is a prime with
q\mid m
, then
X^{n}-m
is Eisenstein at
q
, so

\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{q}\;=\;\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\alpha].

Consequently,

v_{q}\bigl(g(m)\bigr)=0\quad\text{(equivalently, $q\nmid g(m)$),}

so in particular
g(m)\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}
and its reduction
\overline{g(m)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is well-defined.

Proof.

Since
m
is square-free and
q\mid m
, we have
v_{q}(m)=1
, and thus
X^{n}-m
is Eisenstein at
q
.
Hence
\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\alpha]
is a DVR and is integrally closed; it is therefore the full ring of integers of

K_{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{q}
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is by definition the integral closure
of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
in
K_{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{q}
. Thus
\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{q}=\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\alpha]
.

Now localizing the index
g(m)=[\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]]
at
q
gives

\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}:\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\alpha]\bigr)=q^{v_{q}(g(m))}.

Since the two
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-orders coincide, this local index is
1
, so
v_{q}(g(m))=0
as claimed.
∎

Consequently, we can show the finiteness and exponent bounds for the pure power-basis index.

Corollary 4.12
.

Fix
n\geq 2
and let
m
be square-free such that
X^{n}-m
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
\alpha^{n}=m
, put
K_{m}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, and write

g(m):=[\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]].

Then:

(i)

\gcd(g(m),m)=1
.

(ii)

One has the divisibility

g(m)^{2}\mid n^{n}.

(iii)

In particular, if
p\mid g(m)
then
p\mid n
, and for each prime
p\mid n
,

v_{p}(g(m))\leq\frac{n}{2}\,v_{p}(n).

Consequently, the set
\{g(m):m\ \text{square-free and }X^{n}-m\text{ irreducible}\}
is finite.

Proof.

The discriminant of the order
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
equals the polynomial discriminant:

\operatorname{disc}(\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])=\operatorname{disc}(X^{n}-m)=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\,n^{n}\,m^{n-1}.

The discriminant–index identity gives

\operatorname{disc}(\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])\;=\;g(m)^{2}\,\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}).

In particular,
g(m)^{2}\mid\operatorname{disc}(\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])
, so

g(m)^{2}\mid n^{n}\,m^{n-1}.

Now let
q\mid m
. Since
m
is square-free,
v_{q}(m)=1
, hence
X^{n}-m
is Eisenstein at
q
.
Therefore
\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}=\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\alpha]
, so the
q
-part of the index is trivial and
q\nmid g(m)
.
As this holds for every prime
q\mid m
, we get
\gcd(g(m),m)=1
.

Combining
\gcd(g(m),m)=1
with
g(m)^{2}\mid n^{n}m^{n-1}
yields
g(m)^{2}\mid n^{n}
, proving (ii).
The valuation bound (iii) follows from
2v_{p}(g(m))\leq v_{p}(n^{n})=n\,v_{p}(n)
.
Finally, (ii)–(iii) imply
g(m)
ranges over a finite set.
∎

Lemma 4.13

(A growth inequality for
v_{p}(n)
)
.

Let
n\geq 5
and let
p
be a prime divisor of
n
. Then

v_{p}(n)<\frac{n-1}{2}.

Proof.

If
n\in\{5,6\}
then
v_{p}(n)=1<\frac{n-1}{2}
for all
p\mid n
.
Assume
n\geq 7
. Then
v_{p}(n)\leq\log_{2}(n)
for all primes
p\mid n
.
We claim
\log_{2}(n)<\frac{n-1}{2}
for
n\geq 7
.
Indeed,
2^{(n-1)/2}>n
holds for
n=7,8
by inspection, and if it holds for some
n\geq 7
then

2^{(n+1)/2}=2\cdot 2^{(n-1)/2}>2n\geq n+2,

so it holds inductively for all larger
n
. Hence
\log_{2}(n)<(n-1)/2
.
∎

We confirm the nontriviality of the Kummer extension for pure index values in the following result.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
n\geq 4
and
N=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
.
Let
g\geq 2
be an integer occurring as
g=g(m)
for some square-free
m
with
X^{n}-m
irreducible.
Let
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
and
L:=K(g^{1/N})
.
Then
L\neq K
.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that
L=K
, i.e.
g^{1/N}\in K
.
Then
\mathbb{Q}(g^{1/N})\subseteq K
.
Since
K/\mathbb{Q}
is cyclotomic, it is abelian; hence every intermediate field of
K/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
.
In particular,
\mathbb{Q}(g^{1/N})/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois.

Write
g=\prod_{\ell}\ell^{e_{\ell}}
and set
d:=\gcd\{e_{\ell}:\ e_{\ell}>0\}
, so
g=h^{d}
with
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}

not an
r
th power in
\mathbb{Q}
for any prime
r
.
Let
d_{0}=\gcd(N,d)
and define
a=d/d_{0}
,
b=N/d_{0}
so that
\gcd(a,b)=1
and

g^{1/N}=(h^{a})^{1/b}.

By the binomial irreducibility criterion (and the fact
\gcd(a,b)=1
), the polynomial
X^{b}-h^{a}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Hence
[\mathbb{Q}(g^{1/N}):\mathbb{Q}]=b
.

Notice that if
n\geq 5
, then
b>2
. Indeed, by Corollary
4.12
(iii), every prime divisor of
g
divides
n
and for
p\mid n
,

e_{p}=v_{p}(g)\leq\frac{n}{2}v_{p}(n).

If
n\geq 5
, Lemma
4.13
gives
v_{p}(n)<\frac{n-1}{2}
, hence

e_{p}<\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{n-1}{2}=\frac{N}{2}.

Therefore
d\leq\max_{p}e_{p}<\frac{N}{2}
, whence
d_{0}=\gcd(N,d)\leq d<\frac{N}{2}
and thus

b=N/d_{0}>2
.

Let
n\geq 5
. Because
g>0
, the real
b
th root of
h^{a}
gives an embedding
\mathbb{Q}((h^{a})^{1/b})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
. If
\mathbb{Q}((h^{a})^{1/b})/\mathbb{Q}
were Galois and
b>2
, then it would be normal and hence contain all conjugates

(h^{a})^{1/b}\zeta_{b}^{k}
; in particular it would contain
\zeta_{b}
.
But
\zeta_{b}\notin\mathbb{R}
for
b>2
, contradicting
\mathbb{Q}((h^{a})^{1/b})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.

Let
n=4
. Here
N=6
and
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})
has degree
4
.
Corollary
4.12
(ii)–(iii) implies
g
is a power of
2
with
1\leq g\leq 16
, i.e.
g\in\{2,4,8,16\}
.
If
g\in\{2,4,16\}
, then
[\mathbb{Q}(g^{1/6}):\mathbb{Q}]\in\{6,3\}
, so
\mathbb{Q}(g^{1/6})\not\subseteq K
.
If
g=8
, then
g^{1/6}=2^{1/2}
and
\mathbb{Q}(g^{1/6})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
.
But
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})=\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{3})
has quadratic subfields
\mathbb{Q}(i)
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})
, and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
,
so
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\not\subseteq K
.

Thus in all cases
g^{1/N}\notin K
, contradicting
L=K
.
∎

5.
Density consequences in the pure family

5.1.
Density results

Fix an integer
n\geq 4
and set
N=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
.
For square-free
m
with
X^{n}-m
irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
, write
K_{m}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
with
\alpha^{n}=m
,
write
\mathcal{O}_{m}=\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}
, and let

g(m):=[\mathcal{O}_{m}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]].

By Corollary
4.12
, the set of possible values of
g(m)
is finite; denote it by
G(n)
.

For each
g\in G(n)
with
g\geq 2
, define
P_{g}
as in Definition
4.5
.
By Lemma
4.14
we have
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})\subsetneq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N},g^{1/N})
,
so Proposition
4.6
applies and gives
\sum_{q\in P_{g}}1/q=\infty
. We show that primes in
P_{g}
force an ABS local obstruction at
q\mid m
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
m
be square-free with
X^{n}-m
irreducible and suppose
g(m)=g\geq 2
.
If
q\in P_{g}
divides
m
, then for every orientation
\omega
of
\mathcal{O}_{m}
and every
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
,
the local equation

f_{m,\omega}(x)=\varepsilon

has no solution in
\mathcal{O}_{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Equivalently,
K_{m}
has an Alpöge–Bhargava–Shnidman fixed-sign local obstruction at
q
.

Proof.

Fix an orientation
\omega
of
\mathcal{O}_{m}
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Assume for contradiction that
f_{m,\omega}(x)=\varepsilon
has a solution in
\mathcal{O}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.

Since
q\mid m
and
m
is square-free, the polynomial
X^{n}-m
is Eisenstein at
q
, hence

\mathcal{O}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is a DVR of residue degree
1
and is generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
by the uniformizer
\alpha
.
Moreover,
q\in P_{g}
implies
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
, so in particular
q\nmid N
.
Applying the local Kummer rigidity (Theorem
3.7
) to
A=\mathcal{O}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
shows that
solvability for the unit right-hand side
\varepsilon
forces

g\in\pm(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

Reducing modulo
q
gives
g\in\pm(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
.
But since
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
, Corollary
4.7
gives
\pm(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}=(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
,
so
g\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
, contradicting the defining condition
q\in P_{g}
.
∎

We show that the density of fixed-sign local solvability outside the
\alpha
–monogenic locus is zero.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
n\geq 4
. Let
S^{\prime}
be the set of square-free integers
m
such that
X^{n}-m
is irreducible and

K_{m}
is fixed-sign locally solvable for monogenicity in the sense of Definition
2.1
,
but with
g(m)\geq 2
(equivalently
\mathcal{O}_{m}\neq\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
).
Then
\delta(S^{\prime})=0
.

Proof.

Write
S^{\prime}=\bigcup_{g\in G(n),\,g\geq 2}S^{\prime}_{g}
, where
S^{\prime}_{g}
consists of those
m\in S^{\prime}
with
g(m)=g
.
It suffices to show
\delta(S^{\prime}_{g})=0
for each fixed
g\geq 2
.

Fix
g\geq 2
and let
m\in S^{\prime}_{g}
.
By definition of fixed-sign local solvability, there exist an orientation
\omega
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
such that

f_{m,\omega}(x)=\varepsilon
is solvable over
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
for every prime
\ell
, hence in particular over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}

for every prime
q\mid m
.

If
q\in P_{g}
divides
m
, then Corollary
5.1
contradicts local solvability at
q
.
Hence every
m\in S^{\prime}_{g}
is divisible by no prime in
P_{g}
, i.e. every
m\in S^{\prime}_{g}
is
P_{g}
-free.

By Proposition
4.6
,
\sum_{q\in P_{g}}1/q=\infty
.
Therefore Lemma
4.9
implies the set of
P_{g}
-free integers has density
0
, and thus

\delta(S^{\prime}_{g})=0
. Since the union over
g
is finite,
\delta(S^{\prime})=0
.
∎

Note that monogenicity implies no fixed-sign local obstruction as follows.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n\geq 2
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
If
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is monogenic, i.e.
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
for some
\theta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then
K
is fixed-sign locally solvable
for monogenicity in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
and set
\omega_{\theta}:=1\wedge\theta\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{n-1}
.
Then
\omega_{\theta}
is an orientation of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and with
\omega=\omega_{\theta}
the index form satisfies
f_{K,\omega}(\theta)=1
.
For every prime
\ell
, viewing
\theta
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
gives a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-solution to
f_{K,\omega}(x)=1
.
∎

We show the Density equality between monogenicity vs.
\alpha
–monogenicity among square-free parameters.

Theorem 5.4
.

Fix
n\geq 4
. Among square-free
m
with
X^{n}-m
irreducible, the set of parameters for which
\mathcal{O}_{m}
is monogenic
but
\mathcal{O}_{m}\neq\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
has two-sided natural density
0
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{O}_{m}
is monogenic but
\mathcal{O}_{m}\neq\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
, then
g(m)\geq 2
.
By Lemma
5.3
,
K_{m}
is fixed-sign locally solvable.
Hence such
m
lie in the set
S^{\prime}
of Theorem
5.2
, which has density
0
.
∎

Consequently, let

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sf}}:=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:\ m\ \text{square-free and }X^{n}-m\ \text{irreducible over }\mathbb{Q}\}.

Let

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sf}}^{\mathrm{mono}}:=\{m\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sf}}:\ \mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}\ \text{is monogenic}\}.

By
[
27
]
, the density of
\alpha
–monogenicity in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sf}}
is

\delta\!\left(\{m\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sf}}:\ \mathcal{O}_{K_{m}}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\}\right)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p+1}.

Then

\delta\!\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sf}}^{\mathrm{mono}}\right)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p+1}.

5.2.
Low-degree examples

Recall that, from Dedekind’s index theorem, we obtained the following criterion for being

Theorem 5.5

(
[

)
.

Let

Maximality of the power order in degrees
4
.

(4)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{m})}=\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt[4]{m}]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}

Equivalently,

Example 5.6

(Quartic case:)
.

Let

\alpha^{4}=m
.

(1) (

)
If

(2) (

(3) (
A concrete Eisenstein-prime obstruction to ABS fixed-sign local solubility.
)

6.
Quantitative sieve closure for a fixed index

Fix
n\geq 4
and put
N=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
.
Let
g\geq 2
be an integer such that, for
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
,

K\subsetneq L:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N},g^{1/N}).

Define

P_{g}:=\Bigl\{\,q\ \text{prime}:\ q\nmid 2Ng,\ q\equiv 1\!\!\pmod{2N},\ g\notin(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}\,\Bigr\}.

For
X\geq 2
, define
\mathcal{A}_{g}(X)
to be the set of integers
m
such that:

•

|m|\leq X
;

•

m
is square-free;

•

X^{n}-m
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and
g(m)=g
;

•

K_{m}
is fixed-sign locally solvable for monogenicity (Definition
2.1
).

We can show log-power upper bound for fixed
g
.

Proposition 6.1
.

With notation as above, set

\delta_{g}:=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-\frac{1}{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}>0.

Then

\#\mathcal{A}_{g}(X)\ \ll_{n,g}\ \frac{X}{(\log X)^{\delta_{g}}}\qquad(X\to\infty).

Moreover, since
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=\varphi(2N)
and
[L:K]\geq 2
, one has

\delta_{g}=\frac{[L:K]-1}{[L:K]\,[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2\varphi(2N)},

so a uniform exponent
\frac{1}{2\varphi(2N)}
works for all such
g
.

Proof.

Call an integer
m

P_{g}
-free
if
q\nmid m
for every
q\in P_{g}
.
Let
m\in\mathcal{A}_{g}(X)
. If
q\in P_{g}
divides
m
, then
v_{q}(m)=1
and hence
X^{n}-m
is Eisenstein at
q
.
Also
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
implies
q>2N\geq N
, so
q\nmid N
.
Therefore Corollary
5.1
applies and gives an ABS local obstruction at
q
,
contradicting
m\in\mathcal{A}_{g}(X)
. Hence every
m\in\mathcal{A}_{g}(X)
is
P_{g}
-free, so

(5)

\#\mathcal{A}_{g}(X)\ \leq\ \#\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:\ |m|\leq X,\ \text{$m$ is $P_{g}$-free}\}.

Let
G:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
and let
H:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
, so that
H\lhd G
and
|G|=[L:\mathbb{Q}]
,
|H|=[L:K]
.
Set
C:=H\setminus\{1\}\subseteq G
, which is conjugacy-stable.
Chebotarev implies that the set of unramified primes
q
with
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}(L/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq C
has density

\frac{|C|}{|G|}=\frac{|H|-1}{|G|}=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-\frac{1}{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}=\delta_{g}.

By Lemma
4.2
, the set
P_{g}
differs by finitely many primes from this Chebotarev set.

Write
\pi_{P_{g}}(y):=\#\{q\leq y:\ q\in P_{g}\}
.
An effective Chebotarev prime number theorem (e.g. Lagarias–Odlyzko
[
24
]
, see also Serre’s exposition
[
30
, §3]
)
gives an asymptotic of the form

(6)

\pi_{P_{g}}(y)=\delta_{g}\,\operatorname{Li}(y)\ +\ O_{n,g}\!\bigl(\operatorname{Li}(y^{\beta_{0}})\bigr)\ +\ O_{n,g}\!\Bigl(y\,e^{-c\sqrt{\log y}}\Bigr)\qquad(y\to\infty),

where
c=c(n,g)>0
, and the term
\operatorname{Li}(y^{\beta_{0}})
appears only if there is an exceptional real zero
\beta_{0}\in(0,1)
.

A partial-summation identity yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq y\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\pi_{P_{g}}(y)}{y}+\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\pi_{P_{g}}(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt.

Inserting (
6
) shows that the main term contributes

\frac{\delta_{g}\operatorname{Li}(y)}{y}+\delta_{g}\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\operatorname{Li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt=\delta_{g}\log\log y+O_{n,g}(1),

while the error terms contribute
O_{n,g}(1)
because

\int_{2}^{\infty}e^{-c\sqrt{\log t}}t^{-1}\,dt<\infty
and

\int_{2}^{\infty}\operatorname{Li}(t^{\beta_{0}})t^{-2}\,dt<\infty
for
\beta_{0}<1
.
Hence

(7)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq y\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}=\delta_{g}\log\log y+O_{n,g}(1)\qquad(y\to\infty).

Define the truncated Mertens product

V_{g}(y):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq y\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr).

Using
\log(1-1/q)=-1/q+O(1/q^{2})
and
\sum_{q}1/q^{2}<\infty
, (
7
) implies

(8)

V_{g}(y)\ \ll_{n,g}\ (\log y)^{-\delta_{g}}\qquad(y\to\infty).

For
X\geq 2
and
2\leq y\leq X
, set

S(X,y):=\#\Bigl\{\,1\leq m\leq X:\ q\nmid m\ \text{for all }q\in P_{g}\text{ with }q\leq y\,\Bigr\}.

Since
\#\{1\leq m\leq X:\ d\mid m\}=X/d+O(1)
uniformly in squarefree
d
, the fundamental lemma of the
dimension-
1
sieve (e.g. the linear sieve; see
[
22
, §6.4]
or
[
20
, Ch. 5]
) implies that for any fixed
u>1

and any
y\leq X^{1/u}
one has

S(X,y)\ \ll_{u}\ X\,V_{g}(y).

Choose
u=2
and
y=X^{1/2}
. Combining with (
8
) and
\log y\asymp\log X
gives

S(X,X^{1/2})\ \ll_{n,g}\ \frac{X}{(\log X)^{\delta_{g}}}.

Every
P_{g}
-free integer
1\leq m\leq X
is counted by
S(X,X^{1/2})
, hence

\#\{1\leq m\leq X:\ \text{$m$ is $P_{g}$-free}\}\ \ll_{n,g}\ \frac{X}{(\log X)^{\delta_{g}}}.

The same bound holds for negative
m
by symmetry, so

\#\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:\ |m|\leq X,\ \text{$m$ is $P_{g}$-free}\}\ \ll_{n,g}\ \frac{X}{(\log X)^{\delta_{g}}}.

Combining with (
5
) proves the proposition.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

If one wishes to avoid invoking an effective Chebotarev theorem, it suffices for the sieve bound
to have a lower bound of the shape

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq y\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{\delta_{g}}{2}\log\log y\qquad(y\geq y_{0}(n,g)),

which yields
V_{g}(y)\ll_{n,g}(\log y)^{-\delta_{g}/2}
and hence

\#\mathcal{A}_{g}(X)\ll_{n,g}X/(\log X)^{\delta_{g}/2}
.

As shown earlier in the paper,
P_{g}
differs by finitely many primes from the Chebotarev set

\{q:\ \operatorname{Frob}_{q}(L/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq H\setminus\{1\}\}
where
H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
.
We can state the Mertens theorem for the Chebotarev–Kummer obstruction set
P_{g}
as follows.

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume
L\neq K
.
Then there exist constants
B_{g}\in\mathbb{R}
and
C_{g}>0
such that, as
x\to\infty
,

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=\delta_{g}\log\log x+B_{g}+o(1),

(10)

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C_{g}}{(\log x)^{\delta_{g}}}.

In particular,
\sum_{q\in P_{g}}\frac{1}{q}=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
G:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
and
H:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
, so that
H\lhd G
. For every unramified rational prime
q
,
write
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}(L/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq G
for the Frobenius conjugacy class.

By the Kummer splitting criterion proved earlier in the paper, for primes
q\nmid 2Ng
the conditions

q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}\quad\text{and}\quad g\notin(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}

are equivalent to:
q
splits completely in
K
but does not split completely in
L
.
Equivalently,
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}(L/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq H\setminus\{1\}
.
Thus
P_{g}
differs by at most finitely many primes (those dividing
2Ng\operatorname{disc}(L)
) from the Chebotarev set

\mathcal{P}_{H\setminus\{1\}}:=\{q\ \text{unramified in }L:\ \operatorname{Frob}_{q}(L/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq H\setminus\{1\}\}.

Write
H\setminus\{1\}
as a disjoint union of conjugacy classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
in
G
.
For each class
C_{i}
, Arango–Piñeros–Keliher–Keyes prove a Mertens product theorem for the Chebotarev
set
\{q:\ \operatorname{Frob}_{q}(L/\mathbb{Q})=C_{i}\}
, with a nonzero constant and an asymptotic of the form

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}(L/\mathbb{Q})=C_{i}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)\sim\frac{c(C_{i})}{(\log x)^{|C_{i}|/|G|}}\qquad(x\to\infty),

see
[
3
, Theorem A]
.
Multiplying over
i=1,\dots,r
(the sets are disjoint) yields

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}(L/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq H\setminus\{1\}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)\sim\frac{C_{g}^{\prime}}{(\log x)^{\sum_{i}|C_{i}|/|G|}}=\frac{C_{g}^{\prime}}{(\log x)^{(|H|-1)/|G|}}\qquad(x\to\infty),

for some constant
C_{g}^{\prime}>0
.
Since
|G|=[L:\mathbb{Q}]
and
|H|=[L:K]=[L:\mathbb{Q}]/[K:\mathbb{Q}]
, we have

\frac{|H|-1}{|G|}=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-\frac{1}{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}=\delta_{g}.

Inserting the finitely many excluded primes changes the product by a nonzero factor, hence
(
10
) holds for some
C_{g}>0
.

Taking logarithms in (
10
) gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\log\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)=-\delta_{g}\log\log x+\log C_{g}+o(1).

Using
\log(1-u)=-u+O(u^{2})
and
\sum_{q}q^{-2}<\infty
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\log\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}+O(1).

Comparing the last two displays yields (
9
) for some constant
B_{g}\in\mathbb{R}
.
In particular,
\sum_{q\in P_{g}}1/q=\infty
.
∎

We get the asymptotic count of
P_{g}
-free integers.

Corollary 6.4
.

Assume the hypotheses of Proposition
6.3
. Define

\mathbf{1}_{P_{g}\text{-free}}(m):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $q\nmid m$ for all $q\in P_{g}$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then there exists a constant
\kappa_{g}>0
such that

\sum_{1\leq m\leq X}\mathbf{1}_{P_{g}\text{-free}}(m)\sim\kappa_{g}\,\frac{X}{(\log X)^{\delta_{g}}}\qquad(X\to\infty).

Proof.

Set
f(m):=\mathbf{1}_{P_{g}\text{-free}}(m)
. Then
f
is multiplicative and satisfies

f(p^{k})=\begin{cases}0,&p\in P_{g},\\
1,&p\notin P_{g},\end{cases}\qquad(k\geq 1).

In particular,
0\leq f(n)\leq 1
.

We apply Wirsing’s mean value theorem in the form of Indlekofer
[
21
, Theorem (Satz) 1.1]
.
It suffices to verify the standard hypotheses:

(i)

f(p)=O(1)
for primes
p
(trivial) and
\sum_{p}\sum_{k\geq 2}f(p^{k})/p^{k}<\infty
(true since
f(p^{k})\leq 1
);

(ii)

there exists
\alpha>0
such that
\sum_{p\leq x}f(p)\frac{\log p}{p}\sim\alpha\log x
.

For (ii), note

\sum_{p\leq x}f(p)\frac{\log p}{p}=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p}.

The classical estimate
\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log x+O(1)
is standard.
Moreover, Proposition
6.3
gives

\sum_{p\leq x,\,p\in P_{g}}\frac{1}{p}=\delta_{g}\log\log x+O(1)
, and partial summation yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\in P_{g}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p}=\delta_{g}\log x+O(1).

Hence

\sum_{p\leq x}f(p)\frac{\log p}{p}=(1-\delta_{g})\log x+O(1),

so Indlekofer applies with
\alpha=1-\delta_{g}
.
The conclusion of
[
21
, Theorem (Satz) 1.1]
gives the claimed asymptotic with exponent
\delta_{g}=1-\alpha
.
∎

7.
On scaled Eisenstein families

This section records two complementary phenomena.
First, we package a broad class of
scaled Eisenstein
families in which the Eisenstein–prime
obstruction sieve applies verbatim once one has bounded index values and nontrivial Kummer data.
Second, we record thin-parameter constructions which show that (i) the Kummer nontriviality hypothesis
in the sieve is genuinely necessary, and (ii) in general one-parameter families, the
ABS

fixed–sign local condition does not force the
distinguished
generator to have index
1
.

Throughout,
n\geq 2
is an integer and

N\ :=\ \frac{n(n-1)}{2}.

7.1.
Scaled Eisenstein polynomial families

Fix a polynomial

h(X)=c_{n-1}X^{n-1}+c_{n-2}X^{n-2}+\cdots+c_{1}X+c_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[X],\qquad c_{0}\neq 0.

For each integer parameter
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the monic polynomial

f_{t}(X)\ :=\ X^{n}+t\,h(X)\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}[X],

and let
\theta_{t}
be a root. Set

K_{t}:=\mathbb{Q}(\theta_{t}),\qquad\mathcal{O}_{t}:=\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}},\qquad g(t):=[\mathcal{O}_{t}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{t}]]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

We say that
K_{t}
is
\theta_{t}
–monogenic
if
g(t)=1
.

We work on the square-free parameter set

\mathcal{T}_{h,\mathrm{sf}}:=\Bigl\{\,t\in\mathbb{Z}:\ |t|>1,\ t\ \text{square-free, and }\gcd(t,c_{0})=1\,\Bigr\}.

We obtain the portability of the local coset constraint in the scaled Eisenstein family.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h,\mathrm{sf}}
and let
q\mid t
be a prime. Then:

(i)

(Eisenstein at
q
)
The polynomial
f_{t}(X)
is Eisenstein at
q
.
In particular
f_{t}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and
[K_{t}:\mathbb{Q}]=n
.

(ii)

(Local identification and trivial
q
–part of the index)
There is a unique prime of
\mathcal{O}_{t}
above
q
,
the extension
K_{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{q}/\mathbb{Q}_{q}
is totally ramified of degree
n
and residue degree
1
, and one has
a canonical identification of complete local rings

\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{q}\ \cong\ \mathbb{Z}_{q}[\theta_{t}].

In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is a DVR of rank
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
generated by the uniformizer
\theta_{t}
,
so the local index at
q
equals
1
and
q\nmid g(t)
(equivalently,
g(t)\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}
).

(iii)

(Coset constraint for unit values of the index form)
Fix an orientation
\omega
of
\mathcal{O}_{t}

and write
f_{t,\omega}
for the associated index form.
Let
\omega_{q}
be the induced orientation on
\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
We continue to write
f_{t,\omega}
for its base change to
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Assume moreover that
q\nmid N
.
Then for every
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\beta]=\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
one has

f_{t,\omega}(\beta)\in\pm\,g(t)\cdot(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

Consequently, if for some
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
the local equation
f_{t,\omega}(x)=\varepsilon

is solvable over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, then

g(t)\in\pm(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad\overline{g(t)}\in\pm(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

(iv)

(Killing the sign when
q\equiv 1\!\!\pmod{2N}
)
If in addition
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
,
then
-1\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
(and also
-1\in(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
), so the sign is irrelevant and local solvability forces

\overline{g(t)}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{N}.

Proof.

(i) Since
q\mid t
, every non-leading coefficient of
f_{t}
is divisible by
q
.
The constant term is
t\,c_{0}
, and because
t
is square-free and
\gcd(t,c_{0})=1
we have

v_{q}(t\,c_{0})=1
. Hence
f_{t}
is Eisenstein at
q
.

(ii) Eisenstein at
q
implies
\mathbb{Q}_{q}(\theta_{t})/\mathbb{Q}_{q}
is totally ramified of degree
n
with residue degree
1
,
and
\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\theta_{t}]
is integrally closed in
\mathbb{Q}_{q}(\theta_{t})
; hence
\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\theta_{t}]
is the ring of integers
of
\mathbb{Q}_{q}(\theta_{t})
.
Since there is a unique prime of
\mathcal{O}_{t}
above
q
, the factor
\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
identifies canonically with this
local ring, yielding
\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\theta_{t}]
.
In particular the
q
-part of the global index is trivial, so
q\nmid g(t)
.

(iii) Let
\omega_{q}
denote the image of
\omega
in
\bigwedge_{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}^{n}(\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q})
.
By the standard top-wedge change-of-lattice computation,

f_{t,\omega_{q}}(\theta_{t})=\pm[\mathcal{O}_{t}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{t}]]=\pm g(t)\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}.

Apply the local coset constraint (Theorem
3.7
) to

A=\mathcal{O}_{t}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
with uniformizer
\pi=\theta_{t}
and orientation
\omega_{q}
.
Since
q\nmid N
, the unit value set on
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
–generators is exactly

f_{t,\omega_{q}}(\theta_{t})\cdot(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}=\pm g(t)\cdot(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
.
If
f_{t,\omega}(x)=\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
is solvable over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, then
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}
and
Lemma
2.5
implies
x
is a local generator, hence
\varepsilon
lies in this coset,
forcing
g(t)\in\pm(\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times})^{N}
. Reducing modulo
q
gives the residue-field condition.

(iv) If
q\equiv 1\pmod{2N}
then
q>2N
, so

7.2.
A thin-family construction showing the necessity of Kummer nontriviality

Our density–zero results for
square-free
parameter sets use the following sieve closure mechanism:
for each fixed nontrivial index value
g\geq 2
, one produces an obstruction set of primes
P_{g}
with

\sum_{q\in P_{g}}\frac{1}{q}=\infty,

and then uses the multiplicative structure of typical square-free integers to force most parameters
with many prime factors to meet
P_{g}
. Two qualitatively different ways this can fail are:

(a)

thin parameter sets
(primes, bounded–
\Omega
almost-primes, polynomial values), where avoidance of a
positive-density set of primes need not be rare
relative
to the thin set; and

(b)

Kummer triviality
for a given fixed index value
g
, in which case the Chebotarev–Kummer obstruction
set
P_{g}
is empty (away from finitely many primes).

The next lemma and theorem provide a uniform example illustrating (b).

7.2.1.
A thin set of primes with positive relative density among primes

Fix integers

n\geq 4,\qquad N:=\frac{n(n-1)}{2},

and fix an integer
c\geq 2
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the set of primes
q
such that:

(i)

q\nmid cn
, and

(ii)

for every prime
p\mid n
one has

(11)

q^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}.

Lemma 7.5

(Density of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
among the primes)
.

The set
\mathcal{P}_{n}
has natural density
0
in
\mathbb{Z}
, but it has a positive natural density inside the primes; in fact,

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{q\leq X:\ q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\}}{\#\{q\leq X:\ q\ \text{prime}\}}\;=\;\prod_{p\mid n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)\;>\;0.

Proof.

Fix a prime
p\mid n
. For odd
p
, the group
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is cyclic of order
\varphi(p^{2})=p(p-1)
,
hence it has a unique subgroup of order
p-1
.
This subgroup is exactly the set

\mathcal{B}_{p}:=\{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}:\ a^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}\},

so
\#\mathcal{B}_{p}=p-1
. Therefore the set of
allowed
reduced residue classes modulo
p^{2}
has size

\varphi(p^{2})-\#\mathcal{B}_{p}=p(p-1)-(p-1)=(p-1)^{2},

i.e. an allowed proportion
1-\frac{1}{p}
.

If
p=2
divides
n
, then
(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
and the condition
q^{1}\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}

is equivalent to
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, again of proportion
1-\frac{1}{2}
.

Let
M:=\prod_{p\mid n}p^{2}
. By the Chinese remainder theorem, the simultaneous conditions

q^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}
for all
p\mid n
amount to restricting
q
to a subset of the reduced residue classes
modulo
M
of proportion
\prod_{p\mid n}(1-\frac{1}{p})
.
By the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions (equivalently, Chebotarev in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
),
primes are equidistributed among reduced residue classes modulo
M
, so the same proportion holds as a natural density

inside the primes
. Finally, excluding the finitely many primes dividing
cn
does not change the limiting density.
∎

7.2.2.
A uniform fixed-index but
ABS
-unobstructed construction

For
q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, define

f_{q}(X):=X^{n}-c^{n}q\in\mathbb{Z}[X],

let
\theta_{q}
be a root, and put
K_{q}:=\mathbb{Q}(\theta_{q})
.
Set also

\alpha_{q}:=\theta_{q}/c,

so
\alpha_{q}^{n}=q
and
K_{q}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{q})
.

Theorem 7.6

(Thin-family construction: fixed index but
ABS
-unobstructed)
.

Fix
n\geq 4
and
c\geq 2
, and let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be as above.
By Theorem
5.5
, for
q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\alpha_{q}^{n}=q
one has
\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{q}]
.
Then for every
q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
the following hold:

(i)

\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{q}]
(so
K_{q}
is monogenic);

(ii)

the distinguished generator
\theta_{q}
never yields a power integral basis:

[\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{q}]]=c^{N}>1,\qquad N=\frac{n(n-1)}{2};

(iii)

K_{q}
is
ABS
-unobstructed (fixed sign): there exists an orientation
\omega_{q}
and a sign

\varepsilon=+1
such that the index form equation
f_{K_{q},\omega_{q}}(x)=\varepsilon
has a solution in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}

for every prime
\ell
(including
\ell=\infty
).

In particular, the family
\{K_{q}\}_{q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}
is thin as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
(since it is indexed by primes),
yet it has positive density among primes by Lemma
7.5
, and it has
100\%
relative density
of parameters which are
ABS
-unobstructed while satisfying
[\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{q}]]>1
.

Proof.

(1) This is exactly the conclusion of Theorem
5.5
applied to
\alpha_{q}^{n}=q
.

(2) Since
\theta_{q}=c\alpha_{q}
, we have inclusions of orders

\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{q}]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{q}]=\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}.

As
\mathbb{Z}
-modules,

\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{q}]=\mathbb{Z}\langle 1,\alpha_{q},\alpha_{q}^{2},\dots,\alpha_{q}^{n-1}\rangle,\qquad\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{q}]=\mathbb{Z}\langle 1,c\alpha_{q},c^{2}\alpha_{q}^{2},\dots,c^{n-1}\alpha_{q}^{n-1}\rangle.

With respect to these bases, the inclusion
\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{q}]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{q}]
is given by the diagonal
change-of-basis matrix
\mathrm{diag}(1,c,c^{2},\dots,c^{n-1})
, hence

[\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{q}]:\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{q}]]=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}c^{j}=c^{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}j}=c^{n(n-1)/2}=c^{N}.

Using
\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{q}]
from (1), we obtain
[\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{q}]]=c^{N}>1
.

(3) Since
\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{q}]
, choose the standard orientation

\omega_{q}:=1\wedge\alpha_{q}\wedge\alpha_{q}^{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{q}^{n-1}\in\bigwedge^{n}\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}.

For this orientation, the associated index form satisfies
f_{K_{q},\omega_{q}}(\alpha_{q})=+1
.
Therefore the same integral point witnessing monogenicity gives a solution to
f_{K_{q},\omega_{q}}(x)=+1

over
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
for every prime
\ell
(including
\ell=\infty
), i.e. there is no fixed-sign local obstruction in the
sense of
ABS
.
∎

Remark 7.7

(Why the Chebotarev–Kummer obstruction vanishes here)
.

In the Chebotarev–Kummer obstruction mechanism for a fixed index value
g
, the relevant Kummer extension over

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
becomes trivial if
g\in(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{N}
.
In the above construction,

g(\theta_{q})=[\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta_{q}]]=c^{N}\in(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{N},

so
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N},g(\theta_{q})^{1/N})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2N})
and the corresponding Chebotarev obstruction set is empty
away from finitely many primes dividing
c
.
This shows that the Kummer nontriviality hypothesis in Theorem
7.3
is essential.

7.3.
Positive-density
ABS
-unobstructed families and a fixed-index twist

We now record a (thin) one-parameter family with
positive natural density
of monogenic specializations,
together with a scaling twist which forces the
distinguished
generator to have a fixed nontrivial index.

7.3.1.
A trinomial family with positive density of monogenic specializations

Consider the one–parameter family

f_{t}(X)\ :=\ X^{n}+tX+t\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}[X],\qquad K_{t}\ :=\ \mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{t}),

where
\alpha_{t}
is any root of
f_{t}
.

Lemma 7.8

(Discriminant of
X^{n}+AX+B
)
.

For
n\geq 2
and
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has

\operatorname{disc}(X^{n}+AX+B)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}n^{n}B^{n-1}+(-1)^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}(n-1)^{n-1}A^{n}.

In particular, with

C_{0,n}:=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}n^{n},\qquad C_{1,n}:=(-1)^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}(n-1)^{n-1},\qquad L_{n}(t):=C_{0,n}+C_{1,n}t,

we have

(12)

\operatorname{disc}(f_{t})=t^{n-1}L_{n}(t).

Proof.

This is the standard resultant computation:

\operatorname{disc}(X^{n}+AX+B)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\operatorname{Res}(X^{n}+AX+B,\,nX^{n-1}+A)
.
∎

Define the arithmetic parameter set

\mathcal{T}_{n}\ :=\ \Bigl\{t\in\mathbb{Z}:\ |t|>1,\ \gcd\bigl(t,n(n-1)\bigr)=1,\ \text{and }t\,L_{n}(t)\text{ is squarefree}\Bigr\}.

Theorem 7.9

(Positive density of monogenic (hence
ABS
–unobstructed) specializations)
.

For every
n\geq 4
, the set
\mathcal{T}_{n}
has positive natural density in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Moreover, for every
t\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, the order
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{t}]
is maximal:

\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{t}].

In particular,
K_{t}
is monogenic and hence
ABS
–unobstructed for all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
.

Proof.

Fix
t\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
and put
I_{t}:=[\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{t}]]
.

Let
q\mid t
be prime. Since
t
is squarefree and
\gcd(t,n(n-1))=1
, we have
v_{q}(t)=1
and
q\nmid n(n-1)
.
The polynomial
f_{t}(X)=X^{n}+tX+t
is Eisenstein at
q
, hence irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Fix a prime
q\mid t
and set

R_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\alpha_{t}]\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}_{q}[X]/(f_{t}).

Since
f_{t}
is Eisenstein at
q
, it is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}_{q}
, hence
R_{q}
is a domain, finite over the DVR
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
and therefore
\dim(R_{q})=1
. Reducing modulo
q
gives

R_{q}/qR_{q}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]/(X^{n}),

so
R_{q}
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{q}=(q,\alpha_{t})
.

In
R_{q}
we have
\alpha_{t}^{n}=-t(\alpha_{t}+1)
, and since
\alpha_{t}\in\mathfrak{m}_{q}
we have

\alpha_{t}+1\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{q}}
, hence
\alpha_{t}+1\in R_{q}^{\times}
.
Thus
t\in(\alpha_{t})
, and writing
t=qu
with
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}
gives
q\in(\alpha_{t})
.
Consequently
\mathfrak{m}_{q}=(q,\alpha_{t})=(\alpha_{t})
is principal.

Since
R_{q}
is a
1
-dimensional Noetherian local domain with principal maximal ideal, it is a DVR.
In particular
R_{q}
is integrally closed, hence equals the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}_{q}(\alpha_{t})
.
Therefore
\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}\cong R_{q}
, so
q\nmid I_{t}
.

If
p\mid I_{t}
then
p^{2}\mid\operatorname{disc}(f_{t})
by the index–discriminant relation

\operatorname{disc}(f_{t})=\operatorname{disc}(K_{t})\,I_{t}^{2}
.
If
p\nmid t
, then (
12
) implies
p^{2}\mid L_{n}(t)
.
But
tL_{n}(t)
is squarefree by hypothesis, hence
L_{n}(t)
is squarefree; contradiction.
Thus
p\nmid I_{t}
for all primes
p\nmid t
.

Combining above observations yields
I_{t}=1
, hence
\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{t}]
and
K_{t}
is monogenic.

Write

F(t):=t\,L_{n}(t)=t\,(C_{0,n}+C_{1,n}t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t].

Since
\gcd(C_{0,n},C_{1,n})=1
, the two linear factors
t
and
L_{n}(t)
are coprime in
\mathbb{Z}[t]
,
so
F
is a squarefree polynomial.

For each prime
\ell
, let

\rho(\ell^{2}):=\#\{\,a\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z}:\ \ell^{2}\mid F(a)\,\}.

A direct congruence count gives:

(i)

If
\ell\mid n
(equivalently
\ell\mid C_{0,n}
), then
C_{1,n}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
and

L_{n}(a)\equiv C_{1,n}a\pmod{\ell}
. Hence
\ell^{2}\mid aL_{n}(a)
iff
\ell\mid a
, so
\rho(\ell^{2})=\ell
.

(ii)

If
\ell\mid(n-1)
(equivalently
\ell\mid C_{1,n}
), then
C_{0,n}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
, so

L_{n}(a)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
for all
a
, and therefore
\ell^{2}\mid aL_{n}(a)
iff
\ell^{2}\mid a
.
Thus
\rho(\ell^{2})=1
.

(iii)

If
\ell\nmid n(n-1)
, then
C_{0,n}
and
C_{1,n}
are both units modulo
\ell^{2}
and the two linear
congruences
a\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{2}}
and
L_{n}(a)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{2}}
each contribute exactly one solution.
Since they are distinct (because
C_{0,n}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
), we have
\rho(\ell^{2})=2
.

In particular, for every prime
\ell
we have
\rho(\ell^{2})<\ell^{2}
, and the Euler product

\mathfrak{S}:=\prod_{\ell}\Bigl(1-\frac{\rho(\ell^{2})}{\ell^{2}}\Bigr)

converges absolutely to a positive real number (since
\rho(\ell^{2})\ll 1
for all but finitely many
\ell
,
and
\sum_{\ell}\ell^{-2}<\infty
). By the standard squarefree sieve/inclusion–exclusion for polynomial values
in one variable (applied to
F(t)
), the set

\{t\in\mathbb{Z}:\ F(t)\ \text{is squarefree}\}

has natural density
\mathfrak{S}>0
. Intersecting with the congruence condition
\gcd(t,n(n-1))=1

removes only finitely many residue classes modulo
\prod_{\ell\mid n(n-1)}\ell
, hence preserves positivity
of density. Finally, excluding
|t|\leq 1
removes finitely many integers. Therefore
\mathrm{dens}(\mathcal{T}_{n})>0
.

∎

Fix an integer

∎

Corollary 7.11

.

Remark 7.12
.

To conclude, we list other classes of families beyond pure fields and literal Eisenstein primes that our method can apply.

(1)

Kummer families attached to polynomial values.

Replace
x^{n}-m
by
x^{n}-F(\mathbf{t})
with
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.
Primes
\mathfrak{q}\mid F(\mathbf{t})
with valuation
1
are portable in the same way as in the pure case,
and for
r\geq 2
one can combine portability with geometric density tools for squarefree values.
Modern work on squarefree discriminants and maximality in large families suggests many opportunities
to embed rigid portable-prime subfamilies inside high-dimensional spaces.

(2)

Multi-parameter Eisenstein divisors and higher-dimensional sieves
The present closure step is essentially one-dimensional (sieving along a squarefree or almost squarefree
parameter). In genuinely higher-dimensional coefficient spaces, the appropriate replacement is the
Ekedahl–Bhargava
geometric sieve
, which is designed to control integral points subject to
congruence conditions that vary with the prime. A schematic model is the two-parameter family

(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\qquad f_{a,b}(x)=x^{n}+a\,h(x)+b,

with fixed
h\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. For many choices of
h
, the condition that
f_{a,b}
be Eisenstein at a prime
q

can be expressed by explicit congruences (typically modulo
q^{2}
), e.g. of the form

b\equiv 0\pmod{q},\quad b\not\equiv 0\pmod{q^{2}},

together with
q
-divisibility constraints on the remaining coefficients. Thus, for each prime
q
, Eisenstein at
q
cuts out a congruence locus in parameter space—an

Eisenstein divisor
in the
(a,b)
-plane—and one is led to count parameters meeting one (or many) such prime-indexed loci.

(3)

Newton polygon portability (beyond Eisenstein).

For many sparse families, local integral-basis and ramification structure can be read from higher Newton polygons. This suggests replacing Eisenstein at
\mathfrak{q}
by a Newton-polygon portability criterion guaranteeing that the completed order is a DVR generated by an explicit uniformizer.

[
19
]
for a framework connecting Newton polygons, discriminants, and integral bases.

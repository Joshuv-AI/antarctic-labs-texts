Identification of Novel Proteins Mediating Causal Association Between Smoking and Essential Hypertension: A Mendelian Randomization Study
Authors: Jikang Shi, Zhuoshuai Liang, Zhantong Liu, Lingfeng Pan, Xinmeng Hu, Yuyang Tian, Huizhen Jin, Yawen Liu, Yi Cheng, Ming Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSmoking is a factor for hypertension. We aim to reveal novel plasma proteins mediating the relationship of smoking with hypertension and identify potential drug targets for hypertension on the basis of Mendelian randomization design.Methods and ResultsData for smoking were selected from the largest genome‐wide association study meta‐analysis performed by the Genome‐Wide Association Study and Sequencing Consortium of Alcohol and Nicotine Use. Data for plasma proteins were selected from the deCODE Health study and the UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project. Data for hypertension were extracted from the FinnGen Study. Moreover, proteome‐wide Mendelian randomization and colocalization analyses, 2‐step Mendelian randomization, and gene function and network prediction, as well as druggability assessment were performed. We finally identified 8 proteins (ANXA4 [annexin A4], DLK1 [protein delta homolog 1], KLB [β‐klotho], MMP8 [matrix metallopeptidase 8], PLAT [tissue‐type plasminogen activator], POSTN [periostin], SAT2 [thialysine N‐ε‐acetyltransferase], and IFNLR1 [interferon λ receptor 1]) mediating association of smoking with hypertension. PLAT and IFNLR1 were identified to be involved in the complement and coagulation cascades and the Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription signaling pathway. ANXA4, KLB, MMP8, PLAT, and IFNLR1 had druggability. Moreover, IFNLR1 had strong evidence of genetic colocalization, because the posterior probability for H4 of IFNLR1 was 91.3%.ConclusionsThis study identified the 8 proteins that mediate causal association between smoking and essential hypertension. Interferon λ receptor agonist targeting IFNLR1 may open a new avenue for treating hypertension. Our discoveries provide new insights into protein pathogenesis of hypertension and to better guide hypertension prevention and treatment among smokers.

Hypertension, as a major risk factor of cardiovascular disease, 1 , 2 usually accompanies complications, including kidney disease and stroke, thereby forming the leading causes of death globally. 3 , 4 Essential hypertension, which accounts for 95% of hypertension, arises from a multifactorial cause characterized by the intricate interplay between susceptible genes and environmental factors. Unhealthy lifestyle behaviors, such as drinking, overweight, physical inactivity, and smoking, are especially relevant to hypertension. 5

Interestingly, smoking is a heritable behavior, contributing to 15% worldwide deaths and generating heavy burden on global health. 6 Evidence from prospective observational studies indicates that smoking is associated with hypertension. 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 Mendelian randomization (MR) studies have suggested a causal relationship between smoking and hypertension. 11 , 12 However, currently, there is no investigation on the potential molecular mechanisms underlying smoking‐induced hypertension through MR.

Plasma proteins, existing in the bloodstream as a result of cellular leakage or active secretion, provide direct evidence on the physiological/pathophysiological status. 13 Proteomic discoveries render a deeper comprehension of molecular processes and potential therapeutic targets, further corroborating the critical involvement of plasma proteins in molecular mechanisms systemically. 14 Specifically, MR provides a new additional dimension to this extensive line of proteomic research using the cis variants within protein‐encoding genes as the genetic instruments for the protein, further documenting the relationships between plasma proteins and health outcomes. 15

MR design, an analysis using genetic instrumental variables associated with observational data, permits researchers to confirm causal hypotheses. This design is less susceptible to confounding and reverse causation. In addition, 2‐step Multiple Variable Mendelian Randomization (MVMR) study refines causal inference in mediating effects, thereby elucidating the pathways from exposures to outcomes. 16

In this study, to reveal novel plasma proteins mediating the relationship of smoking with hypertension and identify potential drug targets for hypertension, we conducted a proteome‐wide MR study by integrating clinical, genetic, and omics data from large databases of human data, thereby unveiling smoking‐protein‐hypertension causal associations.

METHODS

Study Design

Figure 1 illustrates the study design framework. In this study, we first conducted 2 sample MR studies to identify the causal association between smoking and hypertension. Next, we performed proteome‐wide MR and colocalization analyses to identify hypertension‐associated proteins. Moreover, we performed MR analyses to explore the associations between smoking and hypertension‐associated proteins. We used 2‐step MVMR design to estimate mediation effects of proteins in the association between smoking and hypertension. Meanwhile, to rule out reverse causation, we performed reverse MR analyses corresponding to the above MR. Finally, we performed gene function and network prediction, as well as druggability assessment on the basis of the identified proteins. All supporting data are available within the article and references. The original studies obtained ethical approval and informed consent from the participants.

Data Sources for Smoking

Smoking status included smoking initiation (SmkInit: ever smoked regularly compared with never smoked), age at smoking regularly (1‐SD increase in the age of initiation of regular smoking), cigarettes smoked per day (1‐SD increase in the number of cigarettes smoked per day), and smoking cessation (SmkCes: current smokers compared with former smokers). In our study, the smoking variables, such as smoking initiation and smoking cessation, are binary exposure rather than dichotomization of an underlying continuous risk factor. The interpretation of the β coefficients (β) of binary exposures differs from that of continuous exposures. For binary exposures, the β coefficient represents the log odds of the outcome per unit change in exposure status, often leading to the calculation of odds ratios (ORs); however, the β coefficient of continuous exposures reflects the change in the outcome variable per unit change in the exposure on a continuous scale. 17 Genetic instruments at genome‐wide significance (P<5×10−9) and low linkage disequilibrium (R 2<0.001 and distance=10 000 kb) for smoking status were selected from the up‐to‐date largest genome‐wide association study (GWAS) meta‐analysis performed by the GWAS and Sequencing Consortium of Alcohol and Nicotine Use comprising 2 669 029 individuals of European ancestry. 18 These genome‐wide significant single‐nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were reliable instruments, owing to F‐statistics >10 in our MR study.

Data Sources for Plasma Proteins

cis SNPs associated with plasma protein levels were selected as genetic instruments on the basis of genome‐wide significance criteria (P<5×10−8). The cis SNPs were defined as those positioned within a 1‐Mb radius of a protein‐encoding gene. The genetic instruments were from UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project 19 and deCODE Health study. 20 The cis SNPs for 1161 plasma proteins were selected from the UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project, where proteomic profiling of plasma samples from 54 306 participants was performed using the Olink platform. Likewise, the cis SNPs for 1423 plasma proteins were selected from the deCODE Health study, which measured 4907 aptamers among 35 559 Icelandic participants using the SomaScan platform.

Data Sources for Hypertension

Genetic instruments for hypertension were extracted from the FinnGen Study because the FinnGen Study has little overlap with the exposure or mediator (smoking or plasma proteins) GWASs. The FinnGen study is a large‐scale genomics initiative that has analyzed >500 000 Finnish biobank samples and correlated genetic variation with health data to understand disease mechanisms and predispositions. The project is a collaboration between research organizations and biobanks within Finland and international industry partners. 21 The FinnGen Study included 92 462 individuals with hypertension, defined as the presence of essential (primary) hypertension and 265 626 individuals without essential hypertension or any other hypertensive diseases.

MR Analysis

For the proteome‐wide MR, the Wald ratio method was used for proteins with only 1 instrumental variable, whereas the inverse‐variance weighted method was adopted for proteins with multiple instrumental variables. The Benjamini‐Hochberg method was used in false discovery rate correction in discovery data sets. Significance thresholds for discovery and replication were established as a false discovery rate<0.05 and P<0.05, respectively. For the 2‐sample MR and the reverse MR analysis, inverse‐variance weighted method was selected as the primary analysis under the multiplicative random effects, and genetic variants associated with traits at P<5 × 10−8 in GWAS meta‐analysis and low linkage disequilibrium (R 2<0.001) were selected as the instrumental variables. Notably, the SNPs used in the forward and reverse MR are nonoverlapping and independent. For the estimation of mediation, 2‐step MR was performed and the protein‐mediated proportion was calculated as per the product of the estimated effect of the smoking on protein levels and the estimated effect of the protein levels on hypertension. 22

Heterogeneity test was performed to assess the heterogeneity on the basis of the Q statistic. Horizontal pleiotropy was detected using MR‐Egger intercept. We also performed sensitivity analyses using weighted median and MR‐Egger methods. All data processing and analysis were performed using the “TwoSampleMR” and “MendelianRandomizaiton” packages in R version 4.3.2, as well as Storm Statistical Platform (www.medsta.cn/software).

Colocalization Analysis

To assess whether there was a shared causal variant between proteins and hypertension, Bayesian colocalization analysis was performed on the basis of the “coloc” package. 23 The colocalization analysis included 5 hypotheses: (1) no causal variant for either protein or hypertension in the genomic locus (H0); (2) 1 causal variant for protein only (H1); (3) 1 causal variant for hypertension only (H2); (4) 2 distinct causal variants for protein and hypertension (H3); and (5) a shared causal variant for protein and hypertension (H4). The sum of the posterior probabilities of the 5 models should be 1.0. The higher the posterior probability of a certain model is, the more likely the corresponding model is to be established in the case of given data. The posterior probability for H4 that exceeded 80% was deemed to be valid for the hypothesis of H4 model. For each protein, SNPs within ±500 kb of the Protein quantitative trait loci (pQTL) were included. When a protein had >1 pQTL, colocalization analysis of each pQTL was performed and the pQTL with the strongest evidence for colocalization was selected.

Function and Network Prediction

String online database (https://string‐db.org/) was used to explore the involvement of identified proteins in protein‐protein interactions and construct protein‐protein interaction networks. 24 In addition, gene set enrichment analyses for genes contained within protein‐protein interaction network were performed using Gene Ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes were conducted.

Druggability Assessment

We used DGIdb, 25 ChEMBL, 26 and DrugBank 27 databases to assess the druggability of the identified proteins mediating association between smoking and hypertension. These databases focused on prioritizing potential targets amenable to drug intervention and the information pertaining to drug names, and the developmental processes of drugs targeting identified proteins were documented.

Results

Association Between Smoking and Hypertension

Table 1 shows the association between smoking and hypertension. We found that genetically predicted SmkInit and SmkCes were associated with increased hypertension risks (SmkInit: OR = 1.295 [95% CI, 1.215–1.380]; SmkCes: OR = 1.240 [95% CI, 1.027–1.497]). The association between SmkInit and hypertension was consistent with that in sensitivity analyses. There existed heterogeneity (P heterogeneity<0.05); however, there was no horizontal pleiotropy (P pleiotropy>0.05). Moreover, we did not find the association of genetically predicted hypertension with smoking in the reverse MR analysis (Table S1).

Association Between Plasma Proteins and Hypertension

The proteome‐wide MR analysis identified 152 genetically predicted proteins that were significantly associated with the hypertension after Benjamini‐Hochberg multiple testing correction (false discovery rate<0.05) in the discovery stage (Table S2). We validated 28 proteins in the replication stage (P<0.05) (Table S3). In the reverse MR analysis, we found the associations of genetically predicted hypertension with 5 proteins (Table S4). Thus, there were 23 proteins associated with hypertension after excluding the 5 proteins with reverse causal associations.

The identified associations were directionally consistent in the discovery and replication stages for all the 23 proteins, except ACAA1 (acetyl coenzyme a acyltransferase 1), ANXA4 (annexin A4), and SELL (l‐selectin). The increased risk of hypertension was associated with genetically predicted higher levels of proteins (AGT [angiotensinogen], DLK1 [protein delta homolog 1], IFNLR1 [interferon λ receptor 1], KLB [β‐klotho], LAG3 [lymphocyte activation gene 3 protein], MFAP4 [microfibril‐associated glycoprotein 4], MMP8 [matrix metallopeptidase 8], PLAT [tissue‐type plasminogen activator], SAT2 [thialysine N‐ε‐acetyltransferase], and TNFSF8 [tumor necrosis factor ligand superfamily member 8]). However, the increased risk of hypertension was associated with genetically predicted lower levels of proteins (ARG1 [arginase‐1], ASRGL1 [l‐asparaginase], BDNF [brain‐derived neurotrophic factor], DNER [delta and notch‐like epidermal growth factor–related receptor], ENPEP [glutamyl aminopeptidase], FABP2 [fatty acid–binding protein, intestinal], FSTL3 [follistatin‐related protein 3], ITIH1 [inter‐α‐trypsin inhibitor heavy chain H1], NPPB [natriuretic peptides B], and POSTN [periostin]) (Figure 2).

Association Between Smoking and Hypertension‐Associated Plasma Proteins

We further investigated associations between smoking and the 23 hypertension‐associated plasma proteins, finding that genetically predicted SmkInit was associated with 16 hypertension‐associated plasma proteins. However, in the 16 proteins, we identified only 8 hypertension‐associated plasma proteins with directional consistency between the association of the 8 proteins with smoking and that of the 8 proteins with hypertension (for ANXA4, OR = 1.064 [95% CI, 1.010–1.123]; for DLK1, OR = 1.092 [95% CI, 1.035–1.151]; for IFNLR1, OR = 1.147 [95% CI, 1.088–1.210]; for KLB, OR = 1.166 [95% CI, 1.105–1.231]; for MMP8, OR = 1.141 [95% CI, 1.078–1.207]; for PLAT, OR = 1.122 [95% CI, 1.062–1.185]; for POSTN, OR = 0.859 [95% CI, 0.810–0.911]; for SAT2, OR = 1.125 [95% CI, 1.067–1.187]) (Table 2). In addition, SmkCes was associated with the proteins levels of LAG3; however, this association was directionally inconsistent with that between LAG3 and hypertension (Table S6).

Mediation of Proteins in the Association Between Smoking and Hypertension

We estimated the mediating effect of the 8 proteins in the association between SmkInit and hypertension. The direction of the effect through the mediator was consistent with that of the total effect. The mediation generated by the 8 proteins ranged from 1.20% to 7.94%. For the extent of mediation, SAT2, PLAT, and IFNLR1 functioned as primary mediators in elucidating the association between smoking and hypertension (Table S7).

Function and Network Prediction of Proteins Mediating Smoking‐Hypertension

The protein‐protein interaction network revealed the connectivity among the proteins mediating the relationship between smoking and hypertension. A module is characterized by a collection of proteins displaying heightened interconnectivity within the group as opposed to their interconnectivity with other protein clusters. We identified a critical module on the basis of genes (ANXA4, DLK1, IFNLR1, KLB, MMP8, PLAT, POSTN, SAT2, IFNL1, IL10RB, FGF19, FGFR4, and SERPINE1), thereby finding interactions (IFNLR1‐IFNL1, IFNLR1‐IL10RB, KLB‐FGF19, KLB‐FGFR4, and PLAT‐SERPINE1) with the most reliability (Figure 3A).

The Gene Ontology functional enrichment indicated the involvement of these genes in biological processes (type III interferon‐mediated signaling pathway, cellular response to fibroblast growth factor stimulus, negative regulation of plasminogen activation, and regulation of cytokine production), cellular components (interleukin‐28 receptor complex, serine protease inhibitor complex, and collagen‐containing extracellular matrix), and molecular functions (fibroblast growth factor binding, cytokine receptor activity, serine‐type endopeptidase activity, heparin binding, enzyme inhibitor activity, and receptor ligand activity) (Figure 3B–D).

Druggability Evaluation on the Proteins Mediating Smoking‐Hypertension

We found 5 proteins (ANXA4, KLB, MMP8, PLAT, and IFNLR1) with druggability. Fluocinolone acetonide, ethanol, doxycycline, urokinase, and peginterferon λ‐1a might target ANXA4, KLB, MMP8, PLAT, and IFNLR1, correspondingly (Table S8).

DISCUSSION

In this study, we found the causal association between smoking and essential hypertension and further identified the 8 plasma proteins (ANXA4, DLK1, KLB, MMP8, PLAT, POSTN, SAT2, and IFNLR1) mediating the causal association.

For the association between smoking and hypertension, MR studies of Oort et al 12 and Larsson et al 11 also found that SmkInit is a risk factor for hypertension, but Linneberg et al did not find the association between smoking and hypertension. 28 Bowman et al and Nguyen et al found an increased hypertension risk in current smokers but not in former smokers. 9 , 29 These studies only focused on SmkInit or SmkCes. We classified smoking status into SmkInit, age at smoking regularly, cigarettes smoked per day, and SmkCes, investigating the association of each smoking status with hypertension. We found that age at smoking regularly and cigarettes smoked per day were not associated with hypertension, indicating that age at smoking regularly and cigarettes smoked per day may not provide the scaffold to underline hypertension. Strikingly, SmkInit and SmkCes were associated with hypertension, highlighting that smoking‐relevant metabolism may contribute to hypertension, partially because smoking initiation and smoking cessation affect metabolism. 30 , 31

In this study, we unveiled the 3 novel proteins (ANXA4, SAT2, and IFNLR1) implicated in association with smoking and hypertension. Moreover, we corroborated the previously discovered by other teams DLK1 32 , 33 and MMP8 34 , 35 to be associated with both hypertension and smoking. In addition, KLB, PLAT, and POSTN have been found to be related to hypertension. KLB is implicated in the neurocontrol of blood pressure in rats subjected to high fructose consumption, as well as in the processes of glycolysis and glucose‐stimulated insulin secretion. 36 , 37 PLAT is associated with borderline hypertension. 38 , 39 The expression levels of POSTN exhibited variance between the prehypertensive phase and the established hypertensive state, 40 and POSTN is identified as a biomarker of progression/reversal of hypertensive nephropathy. 41 We revealed the implication of KLB, PLAT, and POSTN in smoking besides in hypertension, thereby documenting that the 3 proteins link to both hypertension and smoking. Hitherto, ANXA4, SAT2, and IFNLR1 have not be found to be related to hypertension or smoking. ANXA4 has been identified as a pivotal immune‐related gene in atrial fibrillation through recent studies. Patients with atrial fibrillation exhibit significantly elevated ANXA4 expression levels in comparison to normal subjects, partially owing to the influence of ANXA4 on the contractility of the left atrium. 42 , 43 , 44 There was inconsistency between the discovery and replication stages in the association of ANXA4 with hypertension, partially owing to differences of population or detection methods. Indeed, further experimental validation will be necessary. SAT2 catalyzes the N‐acetylation of thialysine 45 and also catalyzes acetylation of polyamines. 46 Our discovery in SAT2 suggests that acetylated products of thialysine and polyamines may function as intermediators in both smoking and hypertension. Interferon‐γ has been found to be involved in the immune mechanisms of hypertension. 47 Interestingly, we unveiled that IFNLR1 correlated with both smoking and hypertension, suggesting that interferon‐λ may link smoking to hypertension.

Furthermore, we revealed that ANXA4, KLB, MMP8, PLAT, and IFNLR1 were feasible to potentially treat hypertension, because drugs targeting ANXA4, KLB, MMP8, and PLAT have been approved. Moreover, interferon λ receptor agonist targeting IFNLR1 is under phase 3 clinical trials. Thus, this agonist may open a new avenue for treating hypertension, because IFNLR1 has the strong colocalization support.

We, further, found that the 3 signaling pathways (JAK/STAT signaling pathway, complement and coagulation cascades, and rap1 signaling pathway) may participate in the association between smoking and hypertension. Indeed, JAK/STAT signaling pathway engages in the development of arterial hypertension. 48 , 49 Smoking impairs aortic vascular smooth cells via the Jak/Stat pathway. 50 Complement activation may result in the pathogenesis of hypertension through its effects on innate and adaptive immune responses, and directly affect vasculature function. Moreover, complement acts as a critical role in all stages of arterial hypertension. 51 Cigarette smoke activates the alternative pathway of complement by modifying the third component of complement system. 52 In addition, coagulation factors, such as PLAT, are involved in the pathogenesis of hypertension. 53 The Rap1 signaling pathway regulates endothelial cell function and vascular remodeling. Altered Rap1 signaling causes endothelial dysfunction and impairs vasodilation, thereby leading to hypertension. 54

To our knowledge, this is the first study to identify the plasma proteins mediating causal association between smoking and essential hypertension. Moreover, the largest GWAS data on essential hypertension and a large number of blood proteins were used to ensure optimal statistical power. Additionally, strong colocalization supports IFNLR1 as a highly potential druggable target for hypertension. However, there are some limitations of this study. First, despite the comprehensive inclusion of numerous proteins in this study, the analysis may overlook proteins lacking appropriate genetic instruments. Second, this study was restricted to European ancestry, and the generalization of these results to other ancestries needs to be further confirmed. Third, the analyses were conducted using summary‐level data, precluding the possibility of stratification based on provoking factors.

CONCLUSIONS

This study identified 8 proteins (ANXA4, DLK1, KLB, MMP8, PLAT, POSTN, SAT2, and IFNLR1) that mediate causal association between smoking and essential hypertension. Interferon λ receptor agonist targeting IFNLR1 may open a new avenue for treating hypertension. Our discoveries provide new insights into protein pathogenesis of hypertension and to better guide hypertension prevention and treatment among smokers.

Neural Parameter-varying Data-enabled Predictive Control of Cold Atmospheric Pressure Plasma Jets
Pegah GhafGhanbari, Mircea Lazar, Javad Mohammadpour Velni

Abstract
Cold Atmospheric Pressure Plasma Jets (APPJs) show significant potential for biomedical applications, but their inherent complexity, characterized by nonlinear dynamics and strong sensitivity to operating conditions like tip-to-surface distance, presents considerable challenges for achieving robust and reliable real-time control. To address these issues, this paper presents the Neural Parameter-Varying Data-enabled Predictive Control (NPV-DeePC) framework. By integrating hyper neural networks (hypernets) into the neural Data-enabled Predictive Control (DeePC) paradigm, the proposed method adaptively captures system nonlinearities and parameter variations, updates the neural feature space accordingly, and enables efficient and accurate trajectory prediction and control. The NPV-DeePC framework is validated through extensive simulations involving surface temperature tracking and thermal dose delivery. The results highlight its ability to outperform existing controllers in terms of accuracy and adaptability. The computational efficiency of the NPV-DeePC approach makes it a viable candidate for real-time applications. These findings underscore its potential to advance the safe and precise control of APPJs and provide a scalable solution for other parameter-varying nonlinear systems.

This makes it a practical and effective solution for real-time biomedical applications, providing the precision and reliability necessary for sensitive treatments.

II
Preliminaries and Problem Description

Achieving precise control of APPJs requires addressing their nonlinear dynamics and the variations in their operating conditions. This section outlines the structure of APPJs, the challenges associated with their control, and the specific objectives that guide the control design.

II-A

Structure of Atmospheric Pressure Plasma Jets

APPJs, particularly in biomedical applications, are compact, hand-held devices that generate plasma by applying an electric field to a carrier gas, typically a noble gas like helium (He) or argon (Ar). This process creates a plasma plume that extends several centimeters beyond the device’s nozzle, as illustrated in Fig.
1

[
19
]
.

Fig. 1:
Schematic of atmospheric pressure plasma jets.

At the core of an APPJ is a dielectric capillary tube through which the carrier gas flows at rates of a few slm.
Surrounding or embedded within this tube is an electrode system that applies pulsed or sinusoidal voltages, ranging from hundreds of Hz to several MHz, which ionize and excite gas molecules, initiating a plasma discharge and forming a plasma plume as it exits the nozzle
[
5
]
. This discharge generates reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (RONS), UV and visible-range photons, electric fields, and localized thermal effects
[
20
]
.

The products of plasma discharge are central to many biomedical applications. RONS, for instance, facilitate critical chemical reactions and are key in cancer therapy. Reactive species generated by APPJs selectively impair cancer cell functions, triggering apoptosis while sparing healthy cells
[
21
]
. Additionally, UV radiation emitted during plasma discharge serves as an effective disinfectant, inducing thymine dimerization in bacterial DNA and disrupting replication. Although APPJs operate at relatively low temperatures, their thermal effects are still significant in applications such as thermal coagulation
[
22
]
, as well as inducing hyperthermia and thermal stress on cellular metabolism.

The characteristics of the plasma plume depend on several factors, including the electrode geometry, gas flow rate and composition, applied voltage, and ambient conditions such as temperature and humidity. With an optimal configuration and control strategy, the plasma plume can effectively treat complex and uneven surfaces, offering flexibility for diverse biomedical treatments.

II-B

Control Challenges for APPJs

Model-based control approaches, such as MPC, rely on an accurate dynamic model of the system. However, modeling APPJs is inherently challenging due to their highly nonlinear dynamics, which span multiple timescales and length scales
[
23
]
. The gas flow dynamics can shift between laminar and turbulent regimes depending on the Reynolds number, introducing significant hydrodynamic fluctuations
[
24
]
. Additionally, the plasma’s electrodynamics exhibit mode-dependent nonlinearities influenced by factors such as gas composition, flow rate, and applied voltage
[
5
]
. These complexities are compounded by interactions among heat transfer, electric fields, and reactive species generation, resulting in a highly coupled and dynamic system. Traditional first-principles models, often formulated as multidimensional partial differential equations, are computationally intensive and unsuitable for real-time control applications
[
25
]
.

In practical plasma biomedicine applications, the hand-held nature of APPJs introduces additional challenges. Variations in the tip-to-surface distance during operation affect key plasma characteristics, such as plume length, reactive species generation, and heat transfer. To address these challenges, a control strategy must dynamically adapt to varying operating conditions while ensuring safety and optimal performance. This requires robust modeling techniques and real-time adaptive control mechanisms capable of maintaining consistent operation across diverse conditions
[
5
]
.

III
Proposed Data-driven Control Approach

In this section, we introduce the Neural Parameter-Varying Data-Enabled Predictive Control (NPV-DeePC) framework, developed to address the nonlinear dynamics and parameter-varying nature of APPJs. We begin with the preliminaries, outlining the mathematical and theoretical foundations necessary for the proposed control strategy. Next, we describe the deep neural network architecture designed to construct a basis that captures the nonlinear, parameter-varying dynamics of the system. Finally, we present the NPV-DeePC algorithm, which integrates the basis into a predictive control framework tailored to the complex behavior of APPJs.

The following fundamental lemma, established within behavioral systems theory, links controllability, persistence of excitation, and the column span of the Hankel matrix to represent all possible trajectories of an LTI system.

Lemma 1

(Willems’ Fundamental Lemma

Consider an LTI system of order

\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{L-D+1}
such that:

\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{H}_{D}(\hat{u})\\
\mathcal{H}_{D}(\hat{y})\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{g}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{u}\\
\tilde{y}\end{bmatrix}.

(8)

This states that the subspace spanned by the columns of the Hankel matrix corresponds exactly to the subspace of possible trajectories of the system.

III-B

Hyper Deep Neural Network Architecture

The proposed hybrid control method employs a HyperDNN structure trained offline to provide multi-step predictions of the system output under varying operating conditions. Generally, a HyperDNN framework comprises two key components: a target network, designed to perform a specific task, and a hypernetwork, responsible for generating the parameters of the target network

Both the target network

. For any vector

represents the number of neurons in the last hidden layer—we can represent the overall MLP mapping as
\upphi_{NN}:\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{u}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{p}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{y}}
.

\displaystyle\upphi_{NN}(\mathbf{u}_{NN},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle:=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{lin}}(W_{o}\mathbf{z}_{l}(\mathbf{p})+b_{o})=W_{o}\mathbf{z}_{l}(\mathbf{p})+b_{o}

\displaystyle=:W_{o}\upphi_{HL}(\mathbf{u}_{NN},\mathbf{p})+b_{o}.

(13)

This formulation demonstrates that
\upphi_{NN}
applies an affine transformation to the features generated by
\upphi_{HL}
, where
\upphi_{HL}
is the mapping defined by the composition of the parameter-dependent hidden layers, and maps the inputs to the features of the last hidden layer, referred to as the neural space. The complete deep neural network map,
\upphi_{NN}
, is then represented as an affine combination within this neural space.

III-C

Neural Parameter-Varying DeePC

The affine transformation applied by the
\upphi_{NN}
map enables the integration of the HyperDNN into the DeePC framework, facilitating the design of the NPV-DeePC control scheme. The inputs and outputs of the HyperDNN are structured as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(k)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{NN}(k)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{NN}(k)

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{y}(k).

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Upphi}_{HL}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}\upphi_{HL}(\mathscr{H}_{:1})&\ldots&\upphi_{HL}(\mathscr{H}_{:L})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{L}\times L},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Upphi}_{NN}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}\upphi_{NN}(\mathscr{H}_{:1})&\ldots&\upphi_{NN}(\mathscr{H}_{:L})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{y}N\times L}.

(16)

During offline training, we try to find optimal network parameters by minimizing the error between true and predicted outputs through the following optimization problem:

(\theta_{o}^{*},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathfrak{T}}^{*},\boldsymbol{\psi}^{*})=\arg\min\left\|\mathcal{Y}_{f}-\boldsymbol{\Upphi}_{NN}(\theta_{o},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathfrak{T}},\boldsymbol{\psi})\right\|_{F}^{2}.

\theta_{o}^{LS}=\arg\min\left\|\mathcal{Y}_{f}-\theta_{0}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\Upphi}_{HL}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathfrak{T}}^{*},\boldsymbol{\psi}^{*})\\

(18)

IV-B

HyperDNN Training and Neural Space Construction

The neural space is constructed offline by training the HyperDNN using the generated dataset, serving as the foundation for output trajectory generation.
The target network consists of a single fully connected hidden layer with
\nu_{L}=30
neurons, utilizing the
\tanh
activation function for its ability to represent complex nonlinear behaviors effectively. To account for parameter variations, the weights and biases of the hidden layer are dynamically adjusted by a hypernet, taking the tip-to-surface distance (
d
) of the APPJ as input. This hypernet, designed without hidden layers, allows the target network to adjust its parameters in response to varying distance.

The target network receives as input the system’s past inputs and outputs over a past horizon of
T_{ini}=5
, along with future inputs over a prediction horizon of
N=10
. Its output represents the system’s predicted future outputs over the prediction horizon
N
.
To ensure effective training and mitigate issues arising from different input scales, the dataset was normalized, maintaining the inputs within a range where the ”
\tanh
” function operates efficiently. This prevents gradient vanishing and improves the network’s learning capacity. The dataset was partitioned into a training set (
65\%
) and a validation set (
35\%
), ensuring robust model performance and generalization.

The HyperDNN parameters were optimized using the ADAM optimizer with a learning rate of
10^{-3}
, while the default values were retained for the other optimization parameters. The objective was to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted and actual outputs of the model. The prediction accuracy was evaluated using the Best Fit Rate (BFR), defined as:

BFR=\max\left(1-\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i})\|}{\|\mathbf{y}_{i}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}\|},0\right)\times 100\%,

(24)

where
\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{i}
represents
i
-th estimated output trajectory over the prediction horizon and
\bar{\mathbf{y}}
denotes the mean value of the true data. The training dataset achieved a BFR of
88.66\%
, while the testing dataset yielded a BFR of
88.31\%
, demonstrating the model’s high accuracy across both sets.

Next, the Hankel matrix
\mathscr{H}
was constructed using a
K=1000
-length input/
output
trajectories and was transformed to neural space to build the mapping
\boldsymbol{\Upphi}_{HL}
ensuring that the resulting
\text{col}(\boldsymbol{\Upphi}_{HL},\boldsymbol{1}^{\mathsf{T}})
has full row rank.

V
Conclusion

This paper introduced the Neural Parameter-Varying Data-enabled Predictive Control (NPV-DeePC) framework to address the challenges of controlling Atmospheric Pressure Plasma Jets (APPJs) in biomedical applications. The proposed controller integrates the neural DeePC approach with a Hyper Deep Neural Network (HyperDNN), enabling seamless adaptation to varying operating conditions. This approach ensures safe and effective control of APPJs while eliminating the need for demanding system identification.

Through various simulations, the performance of the proposed controller was benchmarked against neural DeePC, DeePC, and MPC. The experiments focused on two key scenarios: surface temperature tracking and optimal thermal dose delivery. The system’s behavior was evaluated under both noise-free and noisy conditions. The results demonstrated that NPV-DeePC outperformed the other controllers in terms of accuracy, robustness, and adaptability. Specifically, NPV-DeePC effectively managed the dynamic variations in the jet’s tip-to-surface distance and input and state constraints, achieving lower tracking errors and more consistent thermal dose delivery compared to the alternatives. Furthermore, the method required a reasonable computational effort, making it practical for real-time applications.

These findings underscore the potential of NPV-DeePC as a reliable and efficient solution for ensuring safe, effective, and precise control of APPJs in sensitive biomedical treatments. Additionally, the framework offers a promising approach for other parameter-varying or nonlinear systems. Future work will focus on ensuring stability and recursive feasibility, as well as enhancing the controller’s robustness.

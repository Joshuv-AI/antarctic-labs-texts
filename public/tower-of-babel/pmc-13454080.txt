Construction of an eight-disulfidptosis-related long non-coding RNA prognostic signature and mechanistic investigation of key molecule MKLN1-AS driving hepatocellular carcinoma progression via the miR-139-5p/LRPPRC axis
Authors: Zhe Jia, Mingshi Liu, Jinxiu Dou, Yuanfeng Lan, Yixuan Xie, Jiahang Li, Jin Huang, Zhimin Bian, Junfang Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) poses a significant global health burden, with limited reliable prognostic biomarkers and effective therapeutic options. Disulfidptosis, a newly identified form of regulated cell death driven by disulfide stress, has emerged as a crucial player in tumor progression. However, the prognostic potential and biological functions of disulfidptosis-related long non-coding RNAs (DRLs) in HCC remain largely unexplored.MethodsUtilizing RNA-seq and clinical data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)-LIHC cohort, we identified prognosis-related DRLs through Pearson correlation analysis. A prognostic signature was constructed via LASSO and multivariate Cox regression analyses. The model’s performance was rigorously validated using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. The tumor immune microenvironment and tumor mutational burden (TMB) were investigated to assess the model’s immunotherapeutic relevance. ROC and survival analyses was used to identify the most prognostically significant lncRNA. Its expression was verified by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) in 10 paired HCC and adjacent normal tissues, and its functional roles were validated through in vitro and in vivo experiments. Mechanistic investigation, including correlation analyses, miRNA target prediction (miRDB and DIANA), and dual-luciferase reporter assays, revealed its regulatory axis.ResultsWe established a novel prognostic signature based on eight DRLs, which effectively stratified HCC patients into high- and low-risk groups with distinct overall survival outcomes. Additionally, the high-risk group was characterized by an immunosuppressive microenvironment and higher TMB. MKLN1-AS showed the highest AUC value, with expression levels significantly upregulated in HCC tissues and correlated with disease progression, as indicated by increasing T stage and clinical grade. MKLN1-AS promoted HCC cell malignancy in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, MKLN1-AS acted as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) to sponge miR-139-5p, thereby relieving its targeted inhibition of LRPPRC (a disulfidptosis-inhibiting gene), ultimately suppressing disulfidptosis and promoting HCC progression.ConclusionsOur study develops and validates a robust DRLs-based prognostic model for HCC, offering a valuable tool for risk stratification and outcome prediction. MKLN1-AS was identified as a novel oncogenic lncRNA that drives HCC progression via the miR-139-5p/LRPPRC axis to inhibit disulfidptosis. The functional and mechanistic elucidation of MKLN1-AS underscores its clinical relevance and highlights its potential as a promising therapeutic target for HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05222-7.

Introduction

HCC represents a major global health challenge, ranking as the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide [1–3]. Despite advances in targeted therapy and immunotherapy [4], the overall prognosis for HCC patients remains poor, largely due to late diagnosis and the high heterogeneity of the disease [5–7]. This underscores an urgent clinical need for reliable prognostic biomarkers to enable early risk stratification and guide personalized treatment strategies [8, 9].

In recent years, novel modes of regulated cell death, such as ferroptosis and cuproptosis, have been recognized as cancer hallmarks and have opened new avenues for therapeutic intervention [10]. Among these, disulfidptosis has emerged as a unique form of cell death triggered by disulfide stress, leading to the catastrophic collapse of the actin cytoskeleton [11, 12]. Crucially, disulfidptosis has been linked to tumor progression, immune evasion, and drug sensitivity across various cancers, highlighting its broad translational relevance [13]. To systematically explore its regulatory network in HCC, we selected 10 well-established disulfidptosis-related genes (DRGs) [11], which laid the foundation for subsequent identification of DRLs. However, the regulatory landscape of disulfidptosis—particularly how lncRNAs modulate this process—and its clinical implications in HCC remain largely unexplored.

LncRNAs are transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides with limited protein-coding potential but profound regulatory functions [14]. A well-characterized mechanism by which lncRNAs exert biological effects is by acting as ceRNAs that sponge miRNAs, thereby regulating the expression of downstream target genes [15]. LncRNAs are increasingly implicated in tumorigenesis, metastasis, and treatment response, positioning them as promising diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers [16–18]. Notably, lncRNAs associated with other cell death modalities, such as ferroptosis and cuproptosis, have demonstrated significant prognostic value in HCC and other malignancies [19–24]. However, whether DRLs can serve as robust predictors for HCC prognosis, and how specific DRLs mechanistically regulate disulfidptosis to influence HCC progression, remain to be investigated.

In this study, we constructed and validated a novel prognostic signature based on DRLs and assessed its independence from conventional clinicopathological factors. We further explored the association between this risk model and the tumor immune microenvironment (TME), as well as tumor mutational burden (TMB), to evaluate its potential in predicting immunotherapy efficacy. Through comprehensive evaluation of the signature, MKLN1-AS was identified as the most prognostically relevant lncRNA and promoted HCC malignancy. Mechanistically, MKLN1-AS inhibited disulfidptosis by sponging miR-139-5p to upregulate LRPPRC, a key disulfidptosis-inhibiting gene. Our work provides a valuable translational framework for prognostic stratification and identifies a potential therapeutic target, thereby offering new insights for precision medicine in HCC.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and processing

RNA-sequencing data (in FPKM format), along with comprehensive clinical information—including survival status, age, gender, and TNM stage—for 374 HCC samples and 50 adjacent normal liver tissues, were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)-LIHC project. A set of ten disulfidptosis-related genes (DRGs) was curated from recent seminal literature (Table 1) [11].

Identification of disulfidptosis-related lncRNAs (DRLs)

LncRNA expression profiles were extracted from the TCGA-LIHC dataset based on the GENCODE annotation file (GENCODE, https://www.gencodegenes.org/). Differentially expressed lncRNAs between HCC and normal tissues were identified using the DESeq2 R package with criteria of |log2(Fold Change)| > 1 and adjusted p-value < 0.05. Pearson correlation analysis was then performed between the expression levels of the 10 DRGs and all differentially expressed lncRNAs. LncRNAs meeting the thresholds of |R| ≥ 0.3 and p < 0.05 were defined as DRLs, and the relationships were visualized using a Sankey diagram.

Construction and validation of the prognosis signature

The HCC cohort was randomly divided into training and testing sets at a 1:1 ratio, with no significant differences in clinical characteristics between the two groups (assessed by Chi-square test). In the training set, univariate Cox regression analysis (p < 0.05) was first employed to identify DRLs with prognostic potential. To prevent overfitting, these candidate DRLs were subsequently subjected to Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) Cox regression analysis. Finally, multivariate Cox regression was used to construct the final prognostic model and calculate the risk score for each patient. Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the median risk score.

Risk characteristics of HCC prognosis

The clinicopathological parameters of patients in the high-risk and low-risk groups were analyzed using the R “Survminer” package. Independent prognostic value identification of the disulfidptosis-associated lncRNA model

Prognostic model for patients with HCC was examined by univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses to test whether they were independent of other clinical characteristics (age, sex, pathological stage, grade and HBV-infection).

Functional and pathway enrichment analyses

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed on differentially expressed genes between the risk groups using the clusterProfiler R package. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted to further identify hallmark gene sets significantly enriched in each group. A false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25 and a nominal p-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant for GSEA.

Analyses of tumor immune microenvironment and tumor mutational burden

The single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) algorithm was used to quantify the infiltration levels of 16 immune cell types and the activity of 13 immune-related pathways. Somatic mutation data were processed and visualized using the “maftools” R package. The Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) for each sample was calculated as the total number of mutations per megabase of the exome.

Correlation analyses between MKLN1-AS and disulfidptosis-inhibiting genes

TPM-formatted transcriptomic data of the TCGA-LIHC cohort were downloaded and tumor samples were selected for analysis. Expression values were transformed as log2 (TPM + 1). Pearson correlation analysis was conducted between MKLN1-AS and six disulfidptosis-inhibiting genes (NUBPL, NDUFA11, LRPPRC, OXSM, NDUFS1, and GYS1). Patients were then divided into high- and low-expression groups according to the median expression level of MKLN1-AS, and the expression differences of the six genes between the two groups were compared using Student’s t-test. FDR correction was applied to adjust for multiple testing. Scatter plots and boxplots were generated for visualization.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Quantitative PCR was performed with SYBR Green fluorescent dye using the SYBR Premix Ex Taq kit (TaKaRa). GAPDH was used as the reference gene. The PCR primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Human subjects

HCC tissues and corresponding adjacent normal liver tissues were collected from 10 patients who underwent curative resection for HCC and used for qRT-PCR analysis. None of the patients recruited to this study received any preoperative anti-tumor treatments. The clinical study conformed to the standards set by the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Beijing Friendship Hospital (Approval No.: 2024-P2-074-02). Informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients.

Cell culture and transfection

HCC cell lines (HepG2, Huh7) were obtained from the Cell Bank of Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (Beijing, China) and cultured in DMEM (Gibco, New York, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. All cell lines were routinely tested and confirmed to be free of mycoplasma contamination. To establish stable cell lines, the cells were infected with lentiviral plasmids encoding MKLN1-AS for overexpression, shRNA targeting MKLN1-AS for knockdown, as well as the corresponding empty lentiviral vector and non-targeting shRNA lentiviral vector as negative controls (all plasmids were purchased from Youbio, Changsha, China). The lentiviral infection was performed following the standard protocol with Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA). After infection, the stable cell lines were selected and maintained with puromycin for continuous screening, and the stable overexpression and knockdown efficiency of MKLN1-AS was subsequently verified.

Functional assays in vitro

Cell Viability: Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assays were performed. Briefly, 5,000 cells/well were seeded in a 96-well plate, and absorbance at 450 nm was measured at 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, and 60 h after adding the CCK-8 reagent. Migration: Cell migration was assessed using wound-healing assays.

Animal models

All animal experimental procedures were performed in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the National Institutes of Health (NIH, USA), and the experimental protocol was approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Capital Medical University (Protocol No. 2024-P2-074-02). HCC cells (6 × 10⁵ cells per mouse) were injected into the bilateral axillae of female Balb/c nude mice aged approximately 4 weeks. The mice were randomly divided into four groups: the MKLN1-AS1 overexpression group, the overexpression control group, the MKLN1-AS1 knockdown group, and the knockdown control group, with 5 mice in each group. After 4 weeks, the mice were sacrificed, and the tumors were harvested to determine their weight and volume, with the tumor volume calculated using the formula: (length × width²)/2.

Construction of the luciferase reporter plasmids and activity measurement

The wild-type sequence of MKLN1-AS and LRPPRC 3’UTR was cloned into the pGV272-Luciferase vector (GeneChem, Shanghai, China) and verified by Sanger sequencing. The putative binding region between MKLN1-AS and LRPPRC 3’UTR was mutated using the QuickChange site-directed mutagenesis kit (GeneChem) to construct the mutant MKLN1-AS and LRPPRC 3’UTR luciferase plasmids. HEK293 cells were co-transfected with the wild-type or mutant Luc-MKLN1-AS/Luc-LRPPRC 3’UTR plasmids, together with the miR-139 overexpression plasmid or miR-NC control (GeneChem), via Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA). Meanwhile, 2 ng of pRL-TK plasmid (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) was co-transfected as the internal reference for normalization. At 48 h post-transfection, the luciferase activities were detected using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega). The relative luciferase activity was calculated by normalizing the firefly luciferase activity to the Renilla luciferase activity.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.1.0) or GraphPad Prism 10. Survival differences were compared with the Kaplan-Meier method and the log-rank test. For comparisons of continuous variables between two independent groups, Student’s t-test was used. When comparing paired measurements from the same subjects, the Repeated-measures t-test was applied. To evaluate the individual and interactive effects of two categorical factors on a continuous outcome, two-way ANOVA was employed. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Construction of a DRLs prognostic signature

To establish a disulfidptosis-related prognostic model, we analyzed transcriptomic data from 374 HCC tissues and 50 normal liver tissues from the TCGA-LIHC cohort. We identified 1,100 differentially expressed lncRNAs and, through correlation analysis with 10 established disulfidptosis-related genes (DRGs, Table 1), pinpointed 746 disulfidptosis-related lncRNAs (DRLs) (Fig. 1A). To refine this list into a robust prognostic tool, we applied LASSO Cox regression (Fig. 1B, C) followed by multivariate Cox analysis, which ultimately defined an 8-lncRNA signature (AC016717.2, AC138696.2, MKLN1-AS, AL442125.2, CAMTA1-IT1, AL354920.1, AC010501.2, and AC069307.1) for risk modeling (Fig. 1D, E).

Based on the median risk score calculated from this signature, patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that patients in the high-risk group had significantly shorter overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in the training cohort, testing cohort, and the entire dataset (all p < 0.05) (Fig. 1F, G). This consistent performance across subsets confirmed the model’s robust predictive ability.

The validation of the prognostic model for DRLs and its clinical utility

We further validated the prognostic strength and clinical applicability of our model. The distribution of risk scores, survival status, and expression patterns of the 8 lncRNAs clearly demonstrated that mortality rates increased with ascending risk scores (Fig. 2A–C). Crucially, both univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses established the risk score as an independent prognostic factor for OS, independent of age, gender, and clinical stage (Fig. 2D). The model’s predictive accuracy was excellent, with time-dependent ROC curves showing the area under curve (AUC) values of 0.727, 0.713, and 0.775 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, respectively, outperforming other clinical parameters (Fig. 2E). Concordance index (C-index) analysis further confirmed that the risk score was the most powerful prognostic predictor among all evaluated variables (Fig. 2F). To facilitate clinical translation, we constructed a nomogram that integrated the risk score with key clinical features, which demonstrated high calibration accuracy in predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities (Fig. 2G).

The prognostic signature is robust across clinical subgroups

To ensure the broad applicability of our prognostic signature, we performed subgroup survival analyses. Across various clinicopathological strata, including different age groups, TNM stages, Grades, T-stages, N-stages (N0), M-stages (M0), overall clinical stages, and HBV-infected patients, the high-risk group consistently exhibited significantly poorer overall survival (OS) compared with the low-risk group (all p < 0.05) (Fig. 3A–K). This confirms that our DRL signature provides reliable prognostic stratification independent of common clinical parameters.

Functional enrichment reveals distinct biological pathways between risk groups

Functional enrichment analyses revealed the underlying biological disparities between the risk groups. The high-risk group was significantly enriched in cell cycle, chromosome segregation, and extracellular matrix (ECM)-receptor interaction pathways (Fig. 4A–C). In contrast, the low-risk group was enriched in metabolic processes such as cytochrome P450 drug metabolism, fatty acid metabolism and glycine serine and threonine metabolism (Fig. 4D), suggesting a potential link to better metabolic liver function and treatment response.

High-risk group shows immunosuppressive TME and elevated TMB

Given the critical role of immunity in HCC progression and treatment, we investigated the tumor immune microenvironment. We observed significant differences in the infiltration levels of various immune cells and the activity of immune-related pathways between the two risk groups (Fig. 5A, B). Mutation analysis revealed that TP53 and CTNNB1 were the most frequently mutated genes in both groups (Fig. 5C). Notably, the high-risk group was associated with a higher tumor mutational burden (TMB). Patients with both high risk and high TMB had the worst prognosis, indicating a synergistic effect (Fig. 5D, E).

MKLN1-AS is identified as a novel prognostic lncRNA and correlates with HCC progression

To evaluate the prognostic potential of the eight key lncRNAs, we performed ROC curve and Kaplan-Meier survival analyses using TCGA-LIHC data. MKLN1-AS emerged as the most robust prognostic indicator, exhibiting the highest predictive accuracy (AUC > 0.6; Fig. 6A) and a significant association between its high expression and poorer overall survival (Fig. 6B). We further validated these findings in an independent external cohort from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset GSE76427. Time-dependent ROC and Kaplan-Meier survival analyses confirmed that MKLN1-AS exhibits consistent and robust prognostic performance, with high expression significantly associated with poorer overall survival, mirroring the results observed in the TCGA-LIHC cohort (Figure S1A–C). Moreover, analysis of the TCGA-LIHC cohort further indicated that MKLN1-AS expression was progressively elevated with advancing disease stage, showing incrementally higher levels from Stage I to Stage II and further to Stage III & IV (Fig. 6C), a pattern corroborated across tumor grades (Fig. 6D). Further stratification by T stage within the TCGA-LIHC cohort revealed that MKLN1-AS expression gradually increased from T1 to T2 and further to T3&T4 stages (Fig. 6E). A local cohort of 10 paired HCC tissues and adjacent normal tissues via qRT-PCR verified that MKLN1-AS expression was significantly upregulated in HCC tissues compared with matched adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 6F). Collectively, these findings identify MKLN1-AS as a novel oncogenic lncRNA with robust prognostic value in HCC, highlighting its potential as a biomarker for disease progression.

MKLN1-AS promotes HCC progression in vitro and in vivo via the miR-139-5p/LRPPRC axis to inhibit disulfidptosis

To investigate the functional role of MKLN1-AS in HCC, we first established HepG2 and Huh7 cell lines with stable MKLN1-AS overexpression (Fig. 7A). Functional assays demonstrated that MKLN1-AS overexpression significantly enhanced cell proliferation (Fig. 7B) and migration (Fig. 7C, D). In vivo xenograft studies further revealed that tumors from the MKLN1-AS overexpression group exhibited markedly increased volume and weight compared to controls (Fig. 7E–G). Conversely, stable knockdown of MKLN1-AS significantly suppressed tumor growth in vivo (Fig. 7H–K).

Given that MKLN1-AS was identified as a disulfidptosis-related lncRNA, we hypothesized that it might function as a ceRNA by sponging miRNAs, thereby relieving miRNA-mediated suppression of target genes, upregulating disulfidptosis-inhibiting genes, and ultimately promoting HCC progression. To test this, we first analyzed the correlation between MKLN1-AS and known disulfidptosis-inhibiting genes. Pearson correlation analysis in the TCGA-LIHC cohort revealed that LRPPRC exhibited the most significant positive correlation with MKLN1-AS expression (Fig. 7L; Figure S2A–E), and LRPPRC expression was significantly elevated in samples with high MKLN1-AS expression (Fig. 7M; Figure S2F–J).

To uncover the underlying regulatory mechanism, we used miRDB and DIANA to predict miRNAs targeting LRPPRC and MKLN1-AS, respectively. Intersection analysis identified miR-139-5p as a candidate mediator (Fig. 7N, O). Dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR-139-5p mimics significantly suppressed the luciferase activity of wild-type (WT) LRPPRC 3’UTR, an effect abrogated by mutation of the putative binding site (Fig. 7P), which was further validated in HepG2 and Huh7 cells (Fig. 7Q, R). Similarly, miR-139-5p mimics suppressed the activity of a WT MKLN1-AS reporter but not its mutant counterpart (Fig. 7S). Competitive luciferase assays further demonstrated that co-expression of MKLN1-AS-WT effectively rescued the miR-139-5p mimic-induced suppression of the WT LRPPRC 3’UTR reporter (Fig. 7T). Finally, at the mRNA level, MKLN1-AS overexpression significantly upregulated LRPPRC expression (Fig. 7U), while its knockdown downregulated LRPPRC expression (Fig. 7V).

Collectively, these results demonstrate that MKLN1-AS acts as a ceRNA to sponge miR-139-5p, thereby derepressing its target LRPPRC. This mechanism inhibits disulfidptosis and ultimately promotes HCC cell malignancy.

Discussion

HCC remains a formidable clinical challenge, with rising global incidence and mortality underscoring the urgent need for innovative prognostic tools and therapeutic targets [25, 26]. In this study, guided by 10 well-established DRGs, we constructed a robust 8-DRL prognostic model and identified MKLN1-AS as a core candidate through systematic screening. Beyond developing and validating this novel signature, we also characterized MKLN1-AS functionally and mechanistically, providing a multi-faceted resource to advance HCC management.

The core of our work is an 8-DRL prognostic model that effectively stratifies HCC patients into distinct risk categories. Its robustness was rigorously confirmed across training, testing, and entire cohorts, with high-risk patients consistently exhibiting significantly poorer survival outcomes. Importantly, the risk score emerged as an independent prognostic factor in multivariate analysis and demonstrated superior predictive accuracy compared to conventional clinical parameters. This suggests that the DRL signature captures fundamental biological drivers of HCC not fully reflected by existing staging systems, offering a clinically actionable tool for refined risk assessment.

Beyond prognosis, our analysis revealed that the high-risk phenotype is characterized by an immunosuppressive TME and higher TMB—two features known to influence immunotherapy response. This positions our DRL signature as a potential predictive biomarker for immunotherapy efficacy, which could guide clinical treatment selection. The prevalent co-mutation of TP53 and CTNNB1 in both risk groups aligns with established HCC genomics [27–32], yet the association of these mutations with poorer prognosis in the context of a high-risk score reinforces the interplay between disulfidptosis, genomic instability, and immune evasion.

Functional enrichment analysis further illuminated the biological divergence between risk groups. The high-risk group’s association with cell cycle and ECM-receptor interaction pathways points to enhanced proliferation and tissue remodeling—consistent with the oncogenic role of MKLN1-AS—while the low-risk group’s enrichment in metabolic processes suggests preserved liver function, potentially explaining their more favorable outcomes.

A pivotal step in translating our findings was the comprehensive characterization of MKLN1-AS. First, we validated its robust prognostic performance in an independent external cohort (GSE76427). Second, qRT-PCR confirmed its upregulation in clinical HCC tissues, and gain/loss-of-function experiments demonstrated its oncogenic role: MKLN1-AS overexpression enhanced HCC cell malignancy, while knockdown exerted opposite effects. Mechanistically, MKLN1-AS acts as a ceRNA to sponge miR-139-5p, thereby derepressing its target LRPPRC—a key disulfidptosis-inhibiting gene—ultimately suppressing disulfidptosis and promoting HCC progression. This mechanism not only validates the bioinformatic model but also nominates MKLN1-AS as a promising therapeutic target. Interfering with this ceRNA axis could restore disulfidptosis and inhibit tumor growth.

We acknowledge several limitations. While we validated MKLN1-AS’s prognostic performance in the GSE76427 cohort, prospective validation in larger, multi-center cohorts is essential to confirm the generalizability of our model and MKLN1-AS’s clinical utility. Additionally, MKLN1-AS may interact with other miRNAs or target genes to regulate disulfidptosis and HCC progression, warranting further exploration. Future work will focus on translating these findings into preclinical studies, such as developing targeted inhibitors against MKLN1-AS or its ceRNA axis, to evaluate their therapeutic potential in HCC.

In conclusion, our study delivers a clinically translatable framework for HCC prognosis and treatment personalization. The DRL signature—rooted in 10 core DRGs—provides a reliable metric for risk stratification, potentially informing surveillance intensity and adjuvant therapy decisions. The integration of TME and TMB analysis offers insights for immunotherapeutic strategies, and the functional and mechanistic characterization of MKLN1-AS via the miR-139-5p/LRPPRC ceRNA axis opens a new avenue for targeted drug development. By bridging a novel form of cell death (disulfidptosis) with the regulatory role of lncRNAs, our work contributes meaningfully to the evolving paradigm of precision medicine in HCC.

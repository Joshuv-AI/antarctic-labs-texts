A characterization of snowflakes via rectifiability
Emanuele Caputo, Nicola Cavallucci

Abstract
. We prove a generalization of Tyson-Wu’s characterization of metric spaces biLipschitz equivalent to snowflakes to every metric space, by removing compactness, doubling and embeddability assumptions. We also characterize metric spaces that are biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake in terms of the absence of non-trivial metric 1 1 -currents in every ultralimit, or equivalently in terms of purely 1 1 -unrectifiability of every ultralimit. Finally, we discuss some applications and examples.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
54E35, 28A80

1.
Introduction

A metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is an
\alpha
-snowflake,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if there exist a metric space
({\rm Y},\rho)
such that
({\rm X},{\sf d})=({\rm Y},\rho^{\alpha})
. We simply call it a snowflake if it is an
\alpha
-snowflake for some
\alpha
. Snowflake metric spaces appear in fractal geometry and are important in the theory of embeddability of metric spaces into finite-dimensional spaces. Assouad embedding theorem says that a snowflake of a doubling metric space biLipschitz embeds in a finite-dimensional space
[
Ass83
]
. A map
f\colon({\rm X},{\sf d})\to({\rm Y},{\sf d}^{\prime})
is biLipschitz if there exists
C\geq 1
such that

C^{-1}{\sf d}(x,x^{\prime})\leq{\sf d}^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq C{\sf d}(x,x^{\prime})

for every
x,x^{\prime}\in{\rm X}
.
Two metric spaces are biLipschitz equivalent if there exists a surjective biLipschitz map from one into the other.

Tyson and Wu, inspired by Assouad’s result, proved the following characterization of metric spaces that are biLipschitz equivalent to snowflakes, under finite-dimensionality and embeddability assumptions.

Theorem

(
[
TW05
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a compact, metrically doubling metric space that admits a biLipschitz embedding into a uniformly convex Banach space. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

({\rm X},{\sf d})
is biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake.

(ii)

Every weak tangent of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
does not contain non-trivial rectifiable curves.

In their work, a weak tangent of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limit of a sequence of pointed metric spaces
\{({\rm X},r_{n}{\sf d},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
r_{n}>0
and
x_{n}\in{\rm X}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The metrically doubling assumption is needed in order to ensure that some pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limits exist. On the other hand, the biLipschitz embeddability into some uniformly convex Banach space is used to prove some intermediate condition exploiting the linearity and the convexity properties of the ambient space. However, this embeddability property is not related to any of the equivalent conditions in the thesis.

The first aim of this work is to prove a general version of Tyson-Wu’s theorem holding for every metric space. The second goal is to give other characterizations in terms of two classical concepts in geometric measure theory in metric spaces: rectifiability and metric currents.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space. Then the following facts are equivalent.

(i)

({\rm X},{\sf d})
is biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake.

(ii)

Every ultralimit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
does not contain non-trivial rectifiable curves.

(iii)

Every ultralimit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is purely
1
-unrectifiable.

(iv)

Every ultralimit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
has no non-trivial metric
1
-currents with inner-regular mass.

Ultralimits are generalization of pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limits and apply also in the non-proper setting, see
[
DK18
]
. An advantage of this theory is that ultralimits always exist. Asymptotic cones, tangent cones and ultracompletions of a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
belong to the class of ultralimits of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
. We refer to Section
2.1
for more details on ultralimits and their relation with pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limits.

We will prove a slightly stronger version of Theorem
1.1
, in which one can express conditions (ii)-(iv) in terms of every fixed non-principal ultrafilter. The equivalence between (i) and (ii) is precisely the extension of Tyson-Wu’s result to general metric spaces. This is done through other intermediate conditions: the line-fitting property due to T. Laakso and introduced in the appendix of
[
TW05
]
and a new condition on the upper bound on the number of almost equally spaced points that are almost collinear, see Definition
3.1
and Theorem
2.2
. This condition is inspired by the notion of small rough angles introduced in
[
DCT25
]
.
The equivalence between (ii) and (iii) follows by
[
Bat22
]
and the fact that ultralimits of ultralimits are ultralimits, but with respect to a maybe different non-principal ultrafilter. The fact that one can choose a fixed non-principal ultrafilter is a consequence of this principle: every compact subset of an ultralimit is isometric to a Gromov-Hausdorff limit of a sequence of subsets. This is proven and formalized in Proposition
2.10
and Remark
2.11
. The equivalence between (iii) and (iv) follows by a Smirnov-type representation results for metric
1
-currents as a superposition of rectifiable
1
-sets. This is proven independently in
[
BCT+25
,
ARB25
]
. The restriction to metric
1
-currents with inner-regular mass is due to the fact that the ultralimits of a metric space are not separable in general.

We discuss some applications of our theory. First, Tyson-Wu presents a counterexample to their main theorem in the case of non-doubling
({\rm X},{\sf d})
. This is a counterexample if we consider pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limits, but not ultralimits. Indeed, we provide an explicit computation that shows that some ultralimit contains a non-trivial geodesic, see Remark
5.1
.

Secondly, we prove that, in the class of quasi-selfsimilar spaces, it is enough to check the absence of rectifiable curves or metric currents only in the metric space itself; see Proposition
5.3
. In particular, in view of Proposition
4.6
, this result can be read as follows: in the class of quasi-selfsimilar spaces, the only purely
1
-unrectifiable spaces are biLipschitz equivalent to snowflakes.

A third application shows that a product of two metric spaces is biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake if and only if both factors are, see Proposition
5.4
.

In a future work, we will study natural generalizations of conditions (ii)-(iv) of Theorem
1.1
to higher dimensions, the relations between them and between the metric properties of the space.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space. Given
x\in{\rm X}
and
r>0
, we denote by
B(x,r):=\{y\in{\rm X}:\,{\sf d}(y,x)<r\}
and by
\overline{B}(x,r):=\{y\in{\rm X}:\,{\sf d}(y,x)\leq r\}
. A curve is a continuous map
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
.
The length of a curve
\gamma
is defined as

(1)

\ell(\gamma):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}{\sf d}(\gamma({t_{i}}),\gamma(t_{i+1}))\,:\,0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=1,\,N\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

A curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
such that
\ell(\gamma)<\infty
is called rectifiable. We say that a curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
is a geodesic if
{\sf d}(\gamma(t),\gamma(s))=|t-s|{\sf d}(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))
for every
t,s\in[0,1]
. In this case,
\ell(\gamma)={\sf d}(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))<\infty
.

Remark 2.1
.

A snowflake metric space has no non-constant rectifiable curves. This is well-known. For instance, it can be seen as a consequence of the computations in
[
CC25
, Example 6.2]
.

Every three points
x,y,z
in an
\alpha
-snowflake metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha})
are quantitatively non-collinear in the following sense:

(2)

{\sf d}(x,y)\leq\max\{{\sf d}(x,z)+(2^{\alpha}-1){\sf d}(z,y),(2^{\alpha}-1){\sf d}(x,z)+{\sf d}(z,y)\},

see
[
DCT25
, Lemma 2.10]
. A metric space in which every triple of points satisfy (
2
) is said to have
rough angles smaller than
2^{\alpha}-1
. This notion has been introduced in
[
DCT25
]
, inspired by the methods of
[
LDRW18
]
. We introduce another definition that shares similiarities with the small rough angle condition.

Definition 2.2

(Small rough angle for equally-spaced points)
.

Let
\varepsilon,\alpha>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
satisfies the
\alpha
-small rough angle condition for
(k,\varepsilon)
-equally-spaced points
, and we say that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
satisfies SRA
{}_{k}(\varepsilon,\alpha)
condition, if the following holds. For every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in{\rm X}
that satisfy
\max\{{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1}):\,i=1,\dots,k-1\}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\min\{{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1}):\,i=1,\dots,k-1\}
, then

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\geq(1+\alpha){\sf d}(x_{1},x_{k}).

Remark 2.3
.

The roles of the constants in the above definition are as follows. The integer
k
refers to the number of points that we consider. The constant
\varepsilon
expresses how close the
k
points are from being equally spaced, while the constant
\alpha
expresses the defect from being collinear.

Remark 2.4
.

Having rough angles smaller than
\beta<1
is not a biLipschitz invariant. Indeed, consider in the Euclidean plane the set
{\rm X}=\{(-1,0),(0,0),(1,0)\}
. It does not satisfy (
2
) for any
\beta<1
when endowed with the Euclidean distance
{\sf d}_{e}
. However, the set
{\rm Y}=\{(-1,0),(0,y),(1,0)\}
for
y>0
, endowed with the Euclidean distance, satisfies (
2
) for
\beta\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{1+y^{2}}}-1
and it is biLipschitz equivalent to
({\rm X},{\sf d}_{e})
.
We will see in Theorem
3.1
that the property

({\rm X},{\sf d})\text{ satisfies SRA${}_{k}(\varepsilon,\alpha)$ for some }k\in\mathbb{N},\varepsilon>0,\alpha>0

is a biLipschitz invariant.

Given a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
, a subset
A\subset{\rm X}
and
r>0
we define
B_{\sf d}(A,r):=\{x\in{\rm X}:\,{\sf d}(x,z)<r\text{ for some }z\in A\}
. We need the following definition, which was firstly considered by Tyson-Wu in
[
TW05
, Appendix]
, after a private communication of the authors with T. Laakso.

Definition 2.5

(Line-fitting)
.

We say that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is
line-fitting
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a metric
{\sf d}_{n}
on
{\rm X}\sqcup[0,1]
such that

(i)

the restriction of
{\sf d}_{n}
on
[0,1]
is the Euclidean metric;

(ii)

the restriction of
{\sf d}_{n}
on
{\rm X}
coincides with
c_{n}{\sf d}
, for some constant
c_{n}>0
;

(iii)

[0,1]\subseteq B_{{\sf d}_{n}}({\rm X},\frac{1}{n})
.

This condition has been used to characterize metric spaces that are biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
TW05
, Theorem 7.2]
)
.

A metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake if and only if it is not line-fitting.

The negation of the line-fitting property is also related (actually equivalent as we will see in Theorem
3.1
) to the
\alpha
-small rough angle condition for
(k,\varepsilon)
-equally-spaced points for some
k,\varepsilon,\alpha
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space satisfying the
SRA
{}_{k}(\varepsilon,\alpha)
condition for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha,\varepsilon>0
. Then it is not line-fitting.

Proof.

Suppose
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is line-fitting. Choose
n
big enough and take a metric
{\sf d}_{n}
on
{\rm X}\sqcup[0,1]
that restricts to the Euclidean metric on
[0,1]
, restricts to
c_{n}{\sf d}
on
{\rm X}
and such that
[0,1]
is contained in the
\frac{1}{n}
-neighbourhood of
{\rm X}
. Consider the points
\frac{i}{k}\in[0,1]
for
i=1,\dots,k
. For every
i
, let
x_{i}\in{\rm X}
be a point such that
{\sf d}_{n}(x_{i},\frac{i}{k})<\frac{1}{n}
. This implies that
\frac{1}{k}-\frac{2}{n}\leq{\sf d}_{n}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leq\frac{1}{k}+\frac{2}{n}
for every
i
. Hence,

\frac{1}{c_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{2}{n}\right)\leq{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{2}{n}\right)

for every
i
. Therefore,

\frac{\max\{{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1}):\,i=1,\dots,k-1\}}{\min\{{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1}):\,i=1,\dots,k-1\}}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{k}+\frac{2}{n}}{\frac{1}{k}-\frac{2}{n}}\leq 1+\varepsilon

for
n
sufficiently large. On the other hand,
c_{n}{\sf d}(x_{1},x_{k})={\sf d}_{n}(x_{1},x_{k})\geq 1-\frac{1}{k}-\frac{2}{n}
. Hence,

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{i+1})}{{\sf d}(x_{1},x_{k})}\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{2}{n}\right)}{1-\frac{2}{n}}=\frac{1-\frac{1}{k}+\frac{2(k-1)}{n}}{1-\frac{1}{k}-\frac{2}{n}}\leq 1+\alpha

for
n
sufficiently large. This contradicts the SRA
{}_{k}(\varepsilon,\alpha)
condition.

∎

2.1.
Ultralimits

For the proofs of the statements of this section, we refer to
[
DK18
,
CS22
,
Cav23
]
.
A non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
is a non-trivial finitely-additive measure
\omega
taking values in
\{0,1\}
and such that
\omega(A)=0
for every finite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. Non-principal ultrafilters allow to take a well-defined limit of a bounded sequence of real numbers. Given a bounded sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
, then there exists a unique real number
a_{\omega}
such that

\omega(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,|a_{n}-a_{\omega}|<\varepsilon\})=1

for every
\varepsilon>0
. The value
a_{\omega}
is denoted by
\omega\textup{-}\lim a_{n}
and is called the
\omega
-limit of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. If the sequence is not bounded then only one of the following two possibilities occurs: either
\omega(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,a_{n}>M\})=1
for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
or
\omega(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,a_{n}<-M\})=1
for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
. In the first case, we write
\omega\textup{-}\lim a_{n}=+\infty
and in the second case
\omega\textup{-}\lim a_{n}=-\infty
.

The
\omega
-limit satisfies natural properties:

(4)

\displaystyle\omega\textup{-}\lim(a_{n}+b_{n})

\displaystyle=\omega\textup{-}\lim a_{n}+\omega\textup{-}\lim b_{n},

\displaystyle\omega\textup{-}\lim(a_{n}b_{n})

\displaystyle=(\omega\textup{-}\lim a_{n})(\omega\textup{-}\lim b_{n})

\displaystyle\omega\textup{-}\lim f(a_{n})

\displaystyle=f(\omega\textup{-}\lim a_{n})

\displaystyle\omega\textup{-}\lim a_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\omega\textup{-}\lim b_{n},\text{ provided }a_{n}\leq b_{n}\text{ for }\omega\text{-a.e. }n\in\mathbb{N}

for every bounded sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of real numbers and every continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.

A pointed metric space is a triple
({\rm X},{\sf d},x)
, where
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a metric space and
x\in{\rm X}
. Two pointed metric spaces
({\rm X},{\sf d},x),({\rm Y},{\sf d}^{\prime},y)
are isometric if there exists a surjective isometry
f\colon{\rm X}\to{\rm Y}
such that
f(x)=y
. In this case we write
({\rm X},{\sf d},x)\cong({\rm Y},{\sf d}^{\prime},y)
.

Let
\{({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pointed metric spaces and let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter. A sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, with
y_{n}\in{\rm X}_{n}
for every
n
, is
\omega
-admissible if
\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})<\infty
. Given two
\omega
-admissible sequences
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
we define
\hat{{\sf d}}_{\omega}(\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}):=\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}(y_{n},z_{n})
. By definition, the
\omega
-limit of the sequence
\{({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the pointed metric space
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})
, where
{\rm X}_{\omega}
is the set of equivalence classes of
\omega
-admissible sequences under the equivalence relation
\hat{{\sf d}}_{\omega}=0
,
{\sf d}_{\omega}
is the metric induced by
\hat{{\sf d}}_{\omega}
on
{\rm X}_{\omega}
and
x_{\omega}
is the class of the
\omega
-admissible sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. More generally, given an
\omega
-admissible sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we denote its class in
{\rm X}_{\omega}
by
y_{\omega}
, or by
\omega\textup{-}\lim y_{n}
. We also write
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})=:\omega\textup{-}\lim(X_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n})
.

Let
\{({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{({\rm Y}_{n},{\sf d}_{n}^{\prime},y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two sequences of pointed metric spaces and let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter. A sequence of maps
f_{n}\colon{\rm X}_{n}\to{\rm Y}_{n}
is said to be
\omega
-admissible if
\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}^{\prime}(f_{n}(x_{n}),y_{n})<\infty
. If the maps
f_{n}
are uniformly
L
-Lipschitz then there is a well-defined map

f_{\omega}\colon({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})\to({\rm Y}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega}^{\prime},y_{\omega}),\,\quad\omega\textup{-}\lim z_{n}\mapsto\omega\textup{-}\lim f_{n}(z_{n})

which is again
L
-Lipschitz. The map
f_{\omega}
is called the
\omega
-limit map of the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter.

(i)

Let
\{({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{({\rm Y}_{n},{\sf d}^{\prime}_{n},y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two sequences of pointed metric spaces and let
f_{n}\colon{\rm X}_{n}\to{\rm Y}_{n}
be a sequence of
\omega
-admissible uniformly surjective
L
-biLipschitz maps. Then the
\omega
-limit map
f_{\omega}
is
L
-biLipschitz and surjective.

(ii)

Let
\{({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pointed metric spaces and let
\alpha>0
. Then
\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n}^{\alpha},x_{n})
is isometric as pointed metric space to
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega}^{\alpha},x_{\omega})
.

Proof.

The biLipschitz property of
f_{\omega}
can be obtained arguing as we did before the lemma. The surjectivity follows by the definitions and the combination of the fact that
f_{n}
’s are uniformly
L
-Lipschitz and surjective.

We prove the second statement. A sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
\omega
-admissible for
\{({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n}^{\alpha},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.
\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}^{\alpha}(x_{n},y_{n})<\infty
, if and only if
\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})<\infty
, i.e. it is
\omega
-admissible for
\{({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Furthermore, two
\omega
-admissible sequences
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy
\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}^{\alpha}(y_{n},z_{n})=0
if and only if
\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}(y_{n},z_{n})=0
. This means that there is a well-defined bijection

g\colon\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n}^{\alpha},x_{n})\to\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n}),\quad\omega\textup{-}\lim y_{n}\mapsto\omega\textup{-}\lim y_{n}

that sends
\omega\textup{-}\lim x_{n}
to
\omega\textup{-}\lim x_{n}
.
We denote
\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X}_{n},{\sf d}_{n},x_{n})=:({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})
and we consider the
\alpha
-snowflaked metric
{\sf d}_{\omega}^{\alpha}
. We claim that
g\colon\omega\textup{-}\lim(X_{n},{\sf d}_{n}^{\alpha},x_{n})\to({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega}^{\alpha},x_{\omega})
is an isometry. Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two admissible sequences. Then, by (
4
) we get that

\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}^{\alpha}(y_{n},z_{n})=(\omega\textup{-}\lim{\sf d}_{n}(y_{n},z_{n}))^{\alpha}={\sf d}_{\omega}(y_{\omega},z_{\omega})^{\alpha}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.9

(
\omega
-limit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space and let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter. An
\omega
-limit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is the
\omega
-limit of a sequence
\{({\rm X},r_{n}{\sf d},x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of points of
{\rm X}
and
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of positive real numbers.

When the sequence of basepoints
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is constant, the
\omega
-limits take special names. For instance, if
\omega\textup{-}\lim r_{n}=0
, the corresponding
\omega
-limit is called an asymptotic cone. An asymptotic cone is either proper or not separable by
[
Sis12
]
. If
\omega\textup{-}\lim r_{n}=+\infty
, the corresponding
\omega
-limit is called a tangent cone. On the other hand, allowing non-constant but bounded sequences
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, if
\omega\textup{-}\lim r_{n}=r\in(0,+\infty)
then the corresponding
\omega
-limit contains an isometrically embedded copy of
({\rm X},r{\sf d})
. In this latter case, if moreover
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is proper then the corresponding
\omega
-limit is isometric to
({\rm X},r{\sf d})
. If
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is proper, the set of proper
\omega
-limits coincides exactly with the set of all possible Gromov-Hausdorff limits, see
[
Cav23
, Section 3]
.
Within the proof of the next result, we provide a generalization of this fact by showing that every compact subset of an
\omega
-limit is a Gromov-Hausdorff limit.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space and let
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
be non-principal ultrafilters and let
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})
be an
\omega
-limit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
. For every compact subset
{\rm Y}\subseteq{\rm X}_{\omega}
, there exists an
\omega^{\prime}
-limit
({\rm X}_{\omega^{\prime}},{\sf d}_{\omega^{\prime}},x_{\omega}^{\prime})
of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
and a compact subset
{\rm Y}^{\prime}\subseteq{\rm X}_{\omega^{\prime}}
which is isometric to
{\rm Y}
.

Proof.

We write explicitly
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})=\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X},r_{n}{\sf d},x_{n})
. Since
{\rm Y}
is compact, for every

Given a metric space

The definition of metric currents was given in

Definition 4.1

(Metric
1
-currents)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space. We say that a multilinear functional

•

(Locality) For every

\pi
is constant on a neighborhood of
\{f\neq 0\}

•

(Continuity) Given

and

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm Lip}(\pi_{n})<\infty
and
\pi_{n}(x)\to\pi(x)
for every

, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}T(f,\pi_{n})=T(f,\pi).

•

(Finite-mass condition) There exists a nonnegative finite Borel measure
\mu
on
{\rm X}
such that

(14)

\left|T(f,\pi)\right|\leq{\rm LIP}(\pi)\int|f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu.

The minimal measure
\mu
that satisfies (

) is called the

of
T
and is denoted by
\|T\|
. We call
\mathbb{M}(T):=\|T\|({\rm X})
the mass of

. We denote the space of all metric
1
-currents on
({\rm X},{\sf d})
by
\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X},{\sf d})
, or simply by
\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})
, if there is no ambiguity.

The
boundary
of a metric
1
-current

is the linear functional
\partial T\colon{\rm Lip}_{b}({\rm X})\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

\partial T(f):=T(1,f).

We say that a metric
1
-current

is

, provided that
\partial T
admits a nonnegative Borel measure
\nu

{\rm X}

We denote the space of all normal

. We also set

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{1}^{\rm reg}({\rm X})

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{1}^{\rm reg}({\rm X})

Remark 4.2

We recall that Ambrosio-Kirchheim in

5.
Applications

In this section we view some examples of spaces that are or are not biLipschitz equivalent to snowflakes.

Remark 5.1

(Tyson-Wu’s example)
.

The authors in
[
TW05
, Example 5.13]
provide an example of a metric space that is neither doubling, nor compact, it is not biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake but such that every weak tangent in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense has no rectifiable curves. This is not in contrast with Theorem
3.1
, as we show, given any non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
, an explicit
\omega
-limit of Tyson-Wu’s example that contains a non-trivial geodesic.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the set

{\rm X}_{n}=Q_{n}\cap 2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{, where }Q_{n}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|z-2e_{1}^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq 2n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Here,
e_{1}^{n}
is the vector
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
i_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\ell^{2}
as

i_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\hat{e}\left(\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+i\right),

where
\{\hat{e}(j)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}
. Tyson-Wu’s example is
{\rm X}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}i_{n}({\rm X}_{n})
, where the metric
{\sf d}
is the restriction of the metric of
\ell^{2}
. We set
x_{n}:=i_{n}(2e_{1}^{n})\in{\rm X}
and
r_{n}:=2^{-1}n^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter. We consider the
\omega
-limit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
given by
\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X},r_{n}{\sf d},x_{n})=({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})
.

We claim that
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})
is isometric, as pointed metric space, to
\omega\textup{-}\lim([-1,1]^{n},{\sf d}_{e},0_{n})
, where
{\sf d}_{e}
and
o_{n}
respectively stands for the Euclidean distance and the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
y_{\omega}=\omega\textup{-}\lim y_{n}\in{\rm X}_{\omega}
, so
\omega\textup{-}\lim r_{n}{\sf d}(x_{n},y_{n})<\infty
. We observe, that for
\omega
-a.e.
n
, the point
y_{n}
belongs to
i_{n}({\rm X}_{n})
. We define the points
z_{n}:=r_{n}(i_{n}^{-1}(y_{n})-2e_{1}^{n})\in[-1,1]^{n}
. The sequence
z_{n}
is admissible in
([-1,1]^{n},{\sf d}_{e},0_{n})
. This allows to define the map

f\colon({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})\to\omega\textup{-}\lim([-1,1]^{n},{\sf d}_{e},0_{n}),\qquad y_{\omega}\mapsto z_{\omega}.

By construction,
f
is an isometric embedding. Let
z_{\omega}=\omega\textup{-}\lim z_{n}\in\omega\textup{-}\lim([-1,1]^{n},{\sf d}_{e},0_{n})
. For every
n
we define
y_{n}
as the closest point to
i_{n}(\frac{1}{r_{n}}z_{n}+2e_{1}^{n})
in
i_{n}({\rm X}_{n})
. Observe that
\|y_{n}-i_{n}(\frac{1}{r_{n}}z_{n}+2e_{1}^{n})\|_{\ell^{2}}<2^{-n}
. By triangular inequality, the sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is admissible, since
\omega\textup{-}\lim 2^{-n}r_{n}=0
. Moreover, by construction and triangular inequality,
f(\omega\textup{-}\lim y_{n})=z_{\omega}
, i.e.
f
is surjective. This proves the claim.
The space
\omega\textup{-}\lim([-1,1]^{n},{\sf d}_{e},0_{n})
contains a non-trivial geodesic, because ultralimit of geodesics is a geodesic.

In some cases, it is enough to check the equivalent properties of Theorem
4.4
only for the metric space, and not for every possible ultralimit. For instance, this is the case for compact quasi-selfsimilar spaces.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\rho_{0}>0
and
L_{0}\geq 1
. A metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is
(L_{0},\rho_{0})
-quasi-selfsimilar if for every
\overline{B}(x,\rho)\subseteq{\rm X}
with
0<\rho\leq\rho_{0}
there is a map
\Phi\colon(\overline{B}(x,\rho),\frac{\rho_{0}}{\rho}{\sf d})\to{\rm X}
which is
L_{0}
-biLipschitz.

This is the definition of quasi-selfsimilar spaces given in
[
Sul82
]
. In other papers the condition is sometimes more restrictive, see
[
Kle06
,
Cav25
]
, while other times the biLipschitz condition is weakened to a quasisymmetric one, see
[
CP11
,
EB24
]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a compact,
(L_{0},\rho_{0})
-quasi-selfsimilar metric space. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

({\rm X},{\sf d})
is biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake.

(ii)

({\rm X},{\sf d})
does not contain non-trivial rectifiable curves.

(iii)

\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) follows by Theorem
4.4
because
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is isometric to one of its
\omega
-limits and using
\mathbf{M}_{1}^{\rm reg}({\rm X})=\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})
, because
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete and separable. The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is obvious, because to a rectifiable curve we can associate the metric
1
-current defined in (
15
).
To prove that (ii) implies (i) it is enough to show that if
({\rm X},{\sf d})
does not contain rectifiable curves then also every of its ultralimit does not contain rectifiable curves, by Theorem
4.4
.
Let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter and let
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})=\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X},r_{n}{\sf d},x_{n})
be an arbitrary
\omega
-limit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
.

If
\omega\textup{-}\lim r_{n}=0
, then
{\rm X}_{\omega}=\{x_{\omega}\}
because the diameter of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is finite; therefore, the
\omega
-limit of the diameters of
({\rm X},r_{n}{\sf d})
is equal to
0
. We conclude that
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega})
does not contain non-trivial rectifiable curves in this case.

If
\omega\textup{-}\lim r_{n}=r\in(0,+\infty)
, then
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega})
is isometric to
({\rm X},r{\sf d})
due to the compactness of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
. Therefore,
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega})
does not contain rectifiable curves, since
({\rm X},{\sf d})
does not.

It remains the case
\omega\textup{-}\lim r_{n}=+\infty
. Suppose that
({\rm X}_{\omega},{\sf d}_{\omega})
contains a non-trivial rectifiable curve
\gamma
. We can suppose that
\gamma
is contained in
B(x_{\omega},R)
for some
R>0
. We set
\rho_{n}:=Rr_{n}^{-1}
.
For
\omega
-a.e.
n
we have
\rho_{n}\leq\rho_{0}
. Thus, we find
L_{0}
-biLipschitz maps
\Phi_{n}\colon(\overline{B}(x_{n},\rho_{n}),\frac{\rho_{0}}{R}r_{n}{\sf d})\to({\rm X},{\sf d})
.
By definition, there is an isometric embedding
\iota\colon(B(x_{\omega},R),{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega})\to\omega\textup{-}\lim(\overline{B}(x_{n},\rho_{n}),r_{n}{\sf d},x_{n})
,
with
B(x_{\omega},R)\subseteq{\rm X}_{\omega}
. Hence, we can find an isometric embedding

\iota^{\prime}\colon\left(B(x_{\omega},\rho_{0}),\frac{\rho_{0}}{R}{\sf d}_{\omega},x_{\omega}\right)\to\omega\textup{-}\lim\left(\overline{B}(x_{n},\rho_{n}),\frac{\rho_{0}}{R}r_{n}{\sf d},x_{n}\right).

Clearly,
(B(x_{\omega},\rho_{0}),\frac{\rho_{0}}{R}{\sf d}_{\omega})
contains a non-trivial rectifiable curve.

By Lemma
2.8
,
\omega\textup{-}\lim\left(\Phi_{n}(\overline{B}(x_{n},\rho_{n})),{\sf d},\Phi_{n}(x_{n})\right)
contains a non-trivial rectifiable curve. But
\Phi_{n}(\overline{B}(x_{n},\rho_{n}))\subseteq{\rm X}
for every
n
, so the
\omega
-limit above is contained in
\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X},{\sf d},\Phi_{n}(x_{n}))
. By compactness of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
, this
\omega
-limit is actually isometric to
({\rm X},{\sf d},z)
for some
z\in{\rm X}
. We deduce that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
contains a non-trivial rectifiable curve, which is a contradiction.
∎

The following classes of metric spaces are quasi-selfsimilar in the sense of Definition
5.2
, so Proposition
5.3
applies to them: selfsimilar fractals (e.g. the polygaskets of
[
TW05
]
), boundaries at infinity of cocompact Gromov-hyperbolic spaces (see
[
Sul82
,
Kle06
,
Cav25
]
), Julia
sets of expanding rational fractions (see
[
Sul82
]
) and some iterated graph systems as defined in
[
AEB24
]
. In the last class, we notice that not all of the spaces considered in
[
AEB24
]
are quasigeodesic. For them, Proposition
5.3
becomes not trivial.

Finally, we notice that being biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake is stable with respect to the operation of products of metric spaces.

Proposition 5.4

(Stability with respect to products)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d}),({\rm Y},{\sf d}^{\prime})
be two metric spaces. Then
({\rm X}\times{\rm Y},{\sf d}\times{\sf d}^{\prime})
is biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake if and only if both
({\rm X},{\sf d})
and
({\rm Y},{\sf d}^{\prime})
are biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter,
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers and
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\rm X}
,
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\rm Y}
. Then

(17)

\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X}\times{\rm Y},r_{n}({\sf d}\times{\sf d}^{\prime}),(x_{n},y_{n}))\cong\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm X},r_{n}{\sf d},x_{n})\times\omega\textup{-}\lim({\rm Y},r_{n}{\sf d}^{\prime},y_{n}),

by
[
CS25
, Lemma 4.11]
.

We prove the only if part. Suppose, without loss of generality, that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is not biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake. Theorem
3.1
implies that there exists an
\omega
-limit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
that contains a non-trivial rectifiable curve. By (
17
) we can find an
\omega
-limit of
({\rm X}\times{\rm Y},{\sf d}\times{\sf d}^{\prime})
that contains a non-trivial rectifiable curve, hence it is not biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake by Theorem
3.1
.

On the other hand, assume that the product is not biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake. We can find an
\omega
-limit of the product that contains a non-trivial geodesic, by Theorem
3.1
. Every non-trivial geodesic in a product metric space projects to a reparametrized geodesic in each factor, see
[
BH13
, Proposition I.5.3]
, and the projection in one of the two factors must be non-trivial. Therefore, (
17
) implies that one
\omega
-limit of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
or
({\rm Y},{\sf d}^{\prime})
contains a non-trivial geodesic. Hence, the corresponding factor is not biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake, by Theorem
3.1
. In other words, if both factors are biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake, then the product is.
∎

Proposition
5.4
does not extend to the case of infinitely many products.

Example 5.5

(Infinite products of snowflakes)
.

Let
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of positive real numbers that converge to
1
. Consider the space
{\rm X}=\{\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\,:\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{n}|^{2\gamma_{n}}<\infty\}\subseteq\ell^{2}
. Define the function
{\sf d}\colon{\rm X}\times{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
,
(\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}})\mapsto\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{2\gamma_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a complete metric space. We claim it is not biLipschitz equivalent to a snowflake. Indeed, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>0
we can find
k
-points in a
n
-th coordinate direction, for
n
sufficiently large, that are equally spaced and do not satisfy (
3
). The conclusion follows by Theorem
3.1
.

The value and sensitivity of contrast-enhanced ultrasonography combined with fine-needle aspiration biopsy in the diagnosis of thyroid nodules
Authors: Chao Zhang, Zhi-Long Liu, Zhi-hong Wei, Qin Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To investigate the value and sensitivity of contrast-enhanced ultrasonography (CEUS) combined with fine-needle aspiration biopsy (FNAB) in the diagnosis of thyroid nodules. This study enrolled 120 patients with thyroid nodules who visited our hospital between July 2018 and January 2021. All patients underwent CEU and CEUS-guided FNAB. The sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of CEUS-guided FNAB for the qualitative diagnosis of thyroid nodules were significantly higher compared with CEUS only (P < 0.05). The consistency in the diagnostic results was high between CEU-guided FNAB and surgical pathology and moderate between CEU and surgical pathology. The diagnostic coincidence rates of CEUS-guided FNAB were significantly higher than those of CEU only for papillary thyroid carcinoma, follicular carcinoma, undifferentiated carcinoma, and medullary carcinoma (P < 0.05). The diagnostic coincidence rates of CEUS-guided FNAB for stages I, II, and III thyroid cancer were significantly higher compared with CEU only (P < 0.05). Contrast-enhanced ultrasonography combined with FNAB improved the sensitivity and accuracy of making a qualitative diagnosis for thyroid nodules. This outcome will not only help to distinguish thyroid cancer from benign ones but can also assist in determining the pathological type and tumor stage of thyroid cancer, thereby presenting good diagnostic value.

Introduction

Thyroid nodules are common thyroid lesions that primarily include benign nodules and thyroid cancer and have a high prevalence in females. Surgery is generally adopted to treat thyroid nodules in clinical practice. Prior to surgery, the nature of these nodules should be clarified to develop a scientific and reasonable therapeutic plan1–3. Imaging is the primary means of clinically diagnosing thyroid nodules, including contrast-enhanced ultrasonography (CEUS), which is gradually being applied in the diagnosis of thyroid nodules.

Fine-needle aspiration biopsy (FNAB) is the main approach for the preoperative diagnosis of thyroid nodules; combined with puncture sampling under the guidance of CEU, this method can help to ensure accurate sampling. In the present study, 120 patients with thyroid nodules in our hospital from July 2018 to January 2021 were enrolled to investigate the value of CEU combined with FNAB in the diagnosis of thyroid nodules.

Materials and methods

General data

The 120 patients with thyroid nodules who were enrolled in this study included 27 males and 93 females. The youngest patient was 23 years old and the oldest was 71 years old (average age, 47.29 ± 14.85 years). All patients met the inclusion criteria, which were as follows: (1) patients with unexplained hoarseness and thyroid tenderness who had a palpable thyroid mass and were initially considered to have a thyroid nodule and required further examination to determine its nature; (2) patients aged ≥ 18 years; (3) patients who were conscious and could at all times cooperate with the examinations. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with a history of previous thyroid lesions or neck surgery; (2) pregnant or lactating females; (3) patients with mental disorders or consciousness disturbances.

Methods

The CEU imaging procedure included the use of the following.

The LOGIQ F8 color Doppler ultrasound diagnostic instrument (General Electric, USA), equipped with a 7.5 ~ 12 MHz frequency probe, was adopted and set to the CEUS mode. A 2-ml SonoVue (Bracco) contrast agent was rapidly and intravenously injected through the elbow vein, followed by a quick injection of 5 ml normal saline for flushing. After activating the dynamic storage and timing buttons of the ultrasound instrument, the probe was placed on the thyroid area of the patient’s neck for horizontal and longitudinal scanning. The ultrasonography images were obtained and subsequently reviewed by two physicians who were experienced in making ultrasonography diagnoses of thyroid lesions.

The CEUS-guided FNAB imaging procedure proceeded as follows.

The skin in the anterior neck was sterilized, and 2% lidocaine was used for local anesthesia. A biopsy needle was adopted to obliquely puncture the thyroid surface, and the needle insertion direction was corrected in real-time according to the instantaneous CEUS image. Once the needle tip touched the thyroid nodule, the core was withdrawn, and tissue was collected from the thyroid nodule. The puncture needle was then withdrawn and sterile gauze was used to compress the puncture point.

Observation indicators

The sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of the qualitative diagnosis of thyroid nodules by CEUS and CEUS-guided FNAB were calculated and compared using the surgical pathological results as the reference standard.

In the qualitative diagnosis of thyroid nodules, consistency between the diagnostic results of the CEU and surgical pathological results, as well as those between the CEUS-guided FNAB and surgical pathological results, were analyzed.

The diagnostic coincidence rate of the CEU and CEUS-guided FNAB for different pathological types and tumor stages of thyroid cancer were compared.

Statistical methods

The SPSS Statistics 22.0 software program was adopted for analysis. The countable data (n) was tested using a chi-squared (χ2) test. Results

Analysis of the qualitative diagnostic results of contrast-enhanced ultrasonography and contrast-enhanced ultrasonography-guided fine-needle aspiration biopsy for thyroid nodules

Among the 120 patients with thyroid nodules, 88 cases with thyroid cancer and 32 cases with benign thyroid nodules were diagnosed by surgical pathology. The pathological types of thyroid cancers were as follows: 32 cases with papillary carcinoma, 21 cases with follicular carcinoma, 19 cases with undifferentiated carcinoma, and 16 cases with medullary carcinoma. The tumor stages of the thyroid cancers were as follows: 25 cases of stage I, 29 cases of stage II, 23 cases of stage III, and 11 cases of stage IV cancers. Referring to the results of surgical pathological diagnoses, the sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of the qualitative diagnoses of thyroid nodules by CEUS-guided FNAB were significantly higher than those by CEUS only (P < 0.05). Consistency analysis comparing the diagnostic and surgical pathological results

In the qualitative diagnosis of thyroid nodules, there was a high consistency between the diagnostic results of CEUS-guided FNAB and surgical pathology (Kappa, 0.879), while a moderate consistency was detected between the diagnostic results of CEUS and surgical pathology (Kappa, 0.643).

Discussion

Surgical pathological examination is the gold standard for the clinical diagnosis of thyroid nodules; this occurs primarily through a pathological examination of the lesions resected during surgery to determine the nature of the thyroid nodules. However, a surgical pathological diagnosis must be conducted intraoperatively or postoperatively and cannot guide the development of preoperative therapeutic plans. Ultrasonography and FNAB are generally adopted as the main methods for the preoperative qualitative diagnosis of thyroid nodules. As a non-invasive means of examination, ultrasonography may obtain a clear ultrasound image with high clarity and resolution by placing an ultrasound probe on the surface of the thyroid gland and scanning the gland4,5 Conversely, the CEUS technique is based on ultrasonography, in which a contrast agent is injected into the patient’s body. Based on the distribution and enhancement effect of the contrast agent in the thyroid nodule, as well as the use of a color signal to display the distribution of blood flow in the thyroid nodule, the morphology of the thyroid nodule and the internal blood flow can be observed more clearly, thereby enabling a physician to more easily determine the nature of the thyroid nodule6,7.

Fine-needle aspiration biopsy is mainly conducted to collect tissue from a lesion, which is subsequently sent for pathological examination. The diagnostic accuracy of FNAB depends on accurate sampling8,9 To improve the accuracy of FNAB sampling, imaging navigation techniques are clinically recommended to guide the sampling during FNAB. Compared with traditional computed tomography-guided technology, CEUS guidance can visualize the position and direction of the puncture needle in real-time, which makes it easier for physicians to promptly adjust the position and direction of the puncture needle, thereby avoiding vascular damage caused by needle deviation during its insertion. Additionally, it also helps to ensure that the puncture needle accurately reaches the location of the thyroid nodule, which will assure the correct tissue specimen collection and guarantee the reliability of the subsequent pathological diagnosis10.

The Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology (TBSRTC) is a standardized framework used to classify and manage thyroid fine-needle aspiration (FNA) biopsy results, categorizing findings into six categories from benign (Category I) to malignant (Category VI). Categories II (Atypia of Undetermined Significance/Follicular Lesion of Undetermined Significance, AUS/FLUS) and III (Follicular Neoplasm/Suspicious for a Follicular Neoplasm, FN/SFN) are particularly relevant for this study. Category II includes specimens with atypical cells, with a malignancy risk of 5–15%, while Category III includes follicular lesions that cannot be definitively classified as benign or malignant, with a malignancy risk of 15–30%. Recent studies have reported incidental malignancy rates of approximately 10% for Bethesda II nodules and 20% for Bethesda III nodules11,12. These findings underscore the importance of accurate diagnostic methods. Our study demonstrates that contrast-enhanced ultrasonography (CEUS) combined with fine-needle aspiration biopsy (FNAB) significantly improves diagnostic accuracy and reduces the need for unnecessary surgical interventions. The high consistency between CEUS-guided FNAB and surgical pathology (Kappa = 0.879) further supports its reliability, highlighting the clinical value of this approach in the management of thyroid nodules, especially for Bethesda II and III categories.

The present study focused on the diagnostic value of CEUS combined with FNAB for thyroid nodules and derived the following results.

Referring to the results of a surgical pathological diagnosis, the qualitative diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of CEUS-guided FNAB for thyroid nodules were significantly higher than those involving CEUS only (P < 0.05). The results of CEUS-guided FNAB in the qualitative diagnosis of thyroid nodules were highly consistent with those of surgical pathology, indicating that CEUS-guided FNAB could determine the nature of thyroid nodules more sensitively and accurately than CEU only.The diagnostic coincidence rates of CEUS-guided FNAB for papillary, follicular, undifferentiated, and medullary thyroid cancer were significantly higher than those of CEUS only (P < 0.05), and the diagnostic coincidence rates of CEUS-guided FNAB for stages I, II, and III thyroid cancer were significantly higher than those of CEUS only (P < 0.05). These results indicated that CEUS-guided FNAB may present good value for differential diagnosis in patients with thyroid cancer in terms of pathological type and tumor staging, particularly for early-stage thyroid cancer, and could provide reliable guidance for the formulation of surgical plans.

Limitation

This study also had the following limitations: The first is the limited sample size of the study. Due to the retrospective nature of the study design, the sample size in this study was limited to inpatients. Secondly, the cases included in this study were from a single center, so there may be some bias in the selection of patients. Therefore, prospective studies with more scientific sample size and more comprehensive design are still needed to better validate the results of this study.

Conclusions

In summary, CEUS combined with FNAB improved the sensitivity and accuracy of the qualitative diagnosis for thyroid nodules. This may not only enable distinguishing thyroid cancer from benign thyroid nodules but also assist in determining the pathological type and tumor staging of thyroid cancer, which presents good diagnostic value.

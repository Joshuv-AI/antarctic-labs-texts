Immune-related hallmark pathway heterogeneity identifies a prognostic risk signature with independent external validation in glioblastoma
Authors: Ahmed M Abdelmaguid, Rasha M A Eltanany, Manal F El-Khadragy, Ahmed E Abdel Moneim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlioblastoma (GBM) is the most aggressive primary malignant brain tumor in adults and remains associated with poor clinical outcomes despite advances in surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy. Increasing evidence suggests that the glioblastoma immune microenvironment and coordinated oncogenic signaling pathways play critical roles in tumor progression, therapeutic resistance, and survival heterogeneity. However, the prognostic relevance of pathway-level immune activity in GBM remains incompletely understood. In this study, we performed a comprehensive transcriptomic and pathway-centered analysis of glioblastoma using RNA-sequencing data and clinical information obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-GBM) cohort. Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was applied to quantify hallmark pathway activity across tumors, followed by immune subtype clustering, survival analysis, Cox regression modeling, machine learning-based pathway prioritization, and prognostic risk signature construction. The reproducibility of the developed prognostic model was subsequently evaluated using an independent Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) validation cohort. Our analysis demonstrated substantial inter-patient variability in immune-related and oncogenic hallmark signaling pathways. Inflammatory and mesenchymal-associated pathways, including TNF-α/NF-κB signaling, IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, interferon responses, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, hypoxia, complement activation, and KRAS signaling, showed marked differences across patients and were strongly associated with aggressive disease phenotypes. Two immune-associated molecular subtypes with distinct pathway activation patterns were identified. Furthermore, multiple hallmark pathways demonstrated significant prognostic associations and were integrated into a pathway-derived prognostic risk signature that successfully stratified patients into high-risk and low-risk groups with significantly different overall survival outcomes. Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that the calculated risk score retained independent prognostic significance after adjustment for clinical variables. Importantly, external validation using the CGGA cohort demonstrated stable prognostic performance and reproducibility across independent patient populations. Taken together, these findings demonstrate that immune-related hallmark pathway activity contributes substantially to glioblastoma biological heterogeneity and patient prognosis. Introduction

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most aggressive and lethal primary malignant tumor of the central nervous system in adults, accounting for the majority of high-grade gliomas and remaining associated with extremely poor clinical outcomes despite decades of therapeutic advances [1]. Current standard treatment, which includes maximal surgical resection followed by radiotherapy and temozolomide-based chemotherapy, provides only limited survival benefit, with median overall survival typically ranging between 12 and 18 months [2]. The persistent therapeutic resistance and rapid recurrence observed in GBM are largely attributed to extensive intratumoral heterogeneity, diffuse infiltrative growth, metabolic adaptability, and profound remodeling of the tumor microenvironment [3].

Recent advances in transcriptomic profiling have substantially improved the understanding of glioblastoma biology and revealed that GBM represents a biologically heterogeneous disease composed of multiple molecular and cellular states rather than a single uniform entity [4]. Large-scale projects such as The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) have demonstrated that glioblastoma exhibits distinct transcriptional subtypes associated with differences in oncogenic signaling, immune activity, treatment response, and clinical prognosis [5]. However, despite these advances, the clinical translation of molecular stratification strategies in GBM remains limited, partly because many existing approaches rely primarily on individual gene expression patterns that may not fully capture the complex biological interactions driving tumor progression.

Increasing evidence suggests that pathway-level analyses may provide a more biologically interpretable framework for understanding tumor behavior than single-gene approaches alone [6]. Hallmark signaling pathways integrate coordinated transcriptional programs associated with inflammation, cell-cycle progression, hypoxia, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), immune regulation, and metabolic adaptation, all of which are critically involved in glioblastoma progression [7]. In particular, the glioblastoma immune microenvironment has emerged as a major determinant of disease behavior. GBM tumors are characterized by extensive infiltration of tumor-associated macrophages, microglia, and immunosuppressive immune populations that collectively shape inflammatory signaling networks and contribute to immune evasion, therapeutic resistance, and tumor aggressiveness [8].

Several inflammatory pathways, including TNF-α/NF-κB signaling, IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, interferon-associated pathways, complement activation, and hypoxia-driven transcriptional programs, have been implicated in glioblastoma progression and poor prognosis [9, 10]. Nevertheless, the prognostic significance of coordinated immune-related pathway activity in GBM remains incompletely understood. Moreover, although machine learning and computational prognostic modeling approaches have become increasingly common in cancer bioinformatics, many previously proposed glioblastoma signatures suffer from limited reproducibility, overfitting, or lack of external validation [11].

In the present study, we performed a comprehensive pathway-centered transcriptomic analysis of glioblastoma using ssGSEA-derived hallmark pathway activity profiles generated from TCGA-GBM transcriptomic data. By integrating immune landscape characterization, immune subtype clustering, survival analysis, Cox regression modeling, machine learning-based pathway prioritization, and prognostic risk signature development, we aimed to identify biologically meaningful pathway patterns associated with patient survival. Importantly, the prognostic framework was externally validated using an independent Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) cohort to evaluate its reproducibility and generalizability across independent patient populations.

Collectively, this study sought to establish a biologically interpretable and clinically relevant pathway-based prognostic framework capable of improving understanding of immune-associated signaling heterogeneity in glioblastoma while identifying candidate pathways with potential prognostic and therapeutic relevance.

Materials and methods

Data collection and preprocessing

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) transcriptomic profiles and corresponding clinical information for glioblastoma (GBM) were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas Glioblastoma Multiforme (TCGA-GBM) cohort through the UCSC Xena platform and the Genomic Data Commons (GDC) data portal [12, 13]. Gene expression datasets generated using the STAR alignment pipeline and TPM normalization were downloaded, along with corresponding clinical phenotypes and overall survival information. The TCGA cohort initially included RNA-seq expression data, clinical data, and survival information for glioblastoma patients.

To externally validate the proposed prognostic model, an independent glioblastoma cohort was obtained from the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) database [14]. Transcriptomic expression profiles and corresponding clinical data were downloaded from the official CGGA portal (http://www.cgga.org.cn/download.jsp).

All preprocessing and downstream bioinformatics analyses were performed in Python using Google Colaboratory. Patient identifiers were extracted from TCGA sample barcodes using the first 12 characters of each sample identifier to ensure accurate matching between transcriptomic and survival datasets. Duplicate samples were subsequently removed to ensure that each patient was represented only once during downstream analyses. Genes with missing expression values were excluded before dimensionality reduction and pathway enrichment analyses. Ensembl gene identifiers were converted into official HGNC gene symbols using the MyGene Python package to improve compatibility with downstream analyses [15]. Unmapped genes and duplicated identifiers were removed to generate a cleaned transcriptomic matrix suitable for downstream analyses.

Exploratory transcriptomic analysis and quality control

An exploratory transcriptomic analysis was performed to evaluate global gene expression distributions, assess normalization consistency, and identify transcriptomic heterogeneity among glioblastoma samples. Principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted following z-score normalization of the expression matrix using the scikit-learn Python package [16]. PCA visualization was subsequently used to assess transcriptomic clustering patterns and identify potential outlier samples.

Outlier detection was additionally performed using PCA coordinates to identify transcriptomic samples with extreme expression profiles. Hierarchical sample correlation heatmaps were generated using highly variable genes to evaluate transcriptomic similarity among glioblastoma samples. All generated figures were exported in publication-quality PNG and PDF formats.

Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)

Pathway enrichment analysis was performed using single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) implemented through the GSEApy Python package [17]. Hallmark gene signatures from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB Hallmark 2020 collection) were used to quantify pathway activity scores across glioblastoma samples [6]. The Hallmark pathway collection was selected because it summarizes biologically coherent signaling pathways while reducing redundancy among gene sets.

The generated pathway enrichment matrices were subsequently used for immune landscape characterization, clustering analysis, survival modeling, machine learning analysis, and prognostic signature construction.

Immune landscape characterization and clustering analysis

To characterize the biological and immune heterogeneity of glioblastoma samples, pathway activity heatmaps were generated using ssGSEA-derived enrichment scores. Variance analysis was performed to identify highly variable pathways across the cohort. Pathways associated with inflammatory signaling, interferon response, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, hypoxia, cytokine signaling, and proliferative programs were prioritized for downstream analyses.

Unsupervised clustering analysis was subsequently performed using pathway enrichment profiles to identify immune-associated glioblastoma subtypes. Patients were stratified into immune-related molecular subgroups according to transcriptomic pathway activity patterns. Cluster assignments were integrated with survival datasets for downstream prognostic analyses.

Survival and cox regression analyses

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed to evaluate survival differences between immune-associated patient subgroups and pathway-derived prognostic groups. Patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median calculated pathway-derived risk score. Survival curves and Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were conducted using the Lifelines Python package [18]. Hazard ratios (HRs), confidence intervals (CIs), and corresponding p-values were subsequently calculated for both univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses.

An initial univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was performed to identify prognostic hallmark pathways associated with overall survival. Significant pathways identified during univariate analyses were subsequently integrated into downstream prognostic modeling. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression was subsequently conducted to determine whether the proposed pathway-derived risk signature was an independent prognostic factor after adjustment for clinical covariates.

Machine learning analysis

Machine learning approaches were implemented as complementary exploratory analyses to prioritize biologically relevant hallmark pathways based on ssGSEA-derived pathway enrichment scores. Random Forest and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) classifiers were constructed to estimate pathway importance rather than to develop the final prognostic model. The prognostic risk signature was subsequently established using Cox proportional hazards regression analysis based on survival-associated pathways. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, classification accuracy, and pathway importance ranking. Given the limited discriminative performance of the machine learning models, these analyses were interpreted primarily as supportive biological evidence for pathway prioritization rather than as independent prognostic classifiers. The machine learning workflow was implemented using the scikit-learn and XGBoost Python frameworks [16].

Construction of the prognostic risk signature

To establish a robust and reproducible pathway-based prognostic framework, pathway activity scores were first quantified for all 47 Hallmark gene sets using the single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm implemented in the GSEApy package. The resulting normalized enrichment scores (NES) were calculated for each patient and used as pathway activity scores for subsequent survival analyses.

Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was then performed to evaluate the association between each Hallmark pathway and overall survival. Pathways with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant prognostic candidates. To construct a parsimonious prognostic signature while minimizing model complexity, the five most statistically significant pathways (ranked according to ascending P-values) were selected and incorporated into a multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model to estimate their independent regression coefficients (β).

The prognostic performance of the constructed signature was subsequently evaluated using Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, risk score distributions, pathway expression heatmaps, and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Finally, the prognostic signature was externally validated using the independent CGGA cohort following the same risk score formula and stratification strategy.

Independent prognostic analysis

To evaluate the independent prognostic value of the proposed pathway-based risk signature, multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed using both pathway-derived risk scores and clinical variables. Clinical covariates included age and gender information extracted from TCGA clinical datasets. Hazard ratios, confidence intervals, and corresponding p-values were calculated to determine the prognostic contribution of each variable.

External validation using the CGGA cohort

To validate the robustness and generalizability of the proposed prognostic model, an external validation analysis was performed using the independent CGGA glioblastoma cohort. The same preprocessing workflow and ssGSEA analytical pipeline applied to the TCGA cohort were also applied to the CGGA dataset to ensure analytical consistency.

Pathway-derived risk scores were subsequently calculated in the validation cohort using the regression coefficients previously established in the TCGA cohort. Patients in the CGGA cohort were stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, Cox proportional hazards regression analysis, and receiver operating characteristic analyses were subsequently performed to evaluate validation performance.

Time-dependent ROC analysis and statistical analysis

Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate prognostic model performance across multiple survival intervals. Predictive performance was assessed at 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival endpoints using time-dependent area under the curve (AUC) analysis.

All statistical analyses and computational workflows were performed in Python using reproducible computational pipelines. Computational analyses were conducted using the pandas, numpy, scipy, scikit-learn, matplotlib, seaborn, lifelines, gseapy, and xgboost Python libraries. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were compared using the log-rank test, while Cox proportional hazards regression models were used for survival modeling. ROC analyses were used to evaluate predictive performance. A two-sided p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant throughout all analyses.

Following sample harmonization and preprocessing, a total of 162 GBM tumor samples with matched transcriptomic and survival information were retained for downstream analyses. Initial quality assessment revealed the presence of missing values primarily within the clinical dataset, whereas the survival dataset contained no missing entries (Fig. 1A; Table 1). Immune landscape characterization of GBM using ssGSEA

To investigate the immune and biological heterogeneity of glioblastoma, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was performed using Hallmark gene sets across the TCGA-GBM cohort. The resulting pathway activity matrix demonstrated substantial inter-patient variability in both immune-associated and oncogenic signaling pathways. Hierarchical clustering of pathway enrichment scores revealed distinct transcriptional activity patterns among GBM samples, indicating the presence of biologically heterogeneous immune states within the cohort (Fig. 3).

ssGSEA-based immune pathway activity heatmap showing the heterogeneous immune and oncogenic signaling landscape across TCGA-GBM samples. Hierarchical clustering was used solely for heatmap visualization and revealed distinct pathway activation patterns among patients, with marked enrichment of inflammatory and proliferative hallmark pathways

Several inflammatory and immune-related pathways showed marked enrichment across subsets of patients, including TNF-α signaling via NF-κB, interferon-α response, interferon-γ response, inflammatory response, allograft rejection, and IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling. In parallel, multiple proliferation-associated pathways, including E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, mitotic spindle, MYC targets V2, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and hypoxia signaling, also demonstrated elevated activity across multiple GBM samples. These findings collectively suggest that GBM exhibits simultaneous activation of immune-regulatory and aggressive tumor-promoting transcriptional programs.

Among the evaluated hallmark pathways, the highest variability across patients was observed for Pancreas Beta Cells, TNF-α signaling via NF-κB, E2F targets, interferon-α response, interferon-γ response, inflammatory response, and G2M checkpoint pathways (Table 2). The substantial variance observed within these pathways further supports the existence of distinct immune and molecular phenotypes among GBM tumors.

Identification of immune subtypes in GBM

To further characterize the immune heterogeneity of glioblastoma, unsupervised K-means clustering was performed using standardized ssGSEA-derived hallmark pathway enrichment scores. Patients were classified into two immune-associated molecular subtypes, designated as Immune_High and Immune_Low, based on Euclidean distance to the nearest cluster centroid. The Immune_High subtype included 90 patients, whereas the Immune_Low subtype consisted of 72 patients (Fig. 4).

Heatmap showing differential hallmark pathway activation patterns between the Immune_High and Immune_Low glioblastoma subtypes identified using unsupervised K-means clustering of standardized ssGSEA enrichment scores

The Immune_High subtype demonstrated markedly elevated enrichment of multiple inflammatory and immune-related signaling pathways, including TNF-α signaling via NF-κB, IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, IL2/STAT5 signaling, inflammatory response, allograft rejection, interferon-α response, and interferon-γ response. In contrast, the Immune_Low subtype exhibited comparatively reduced activation of immune-associated pathways and lower inflammatory signaling activity (Fig. 4).

To further validate subtype separation, principal component analysis was performed using pathway enrichment scores. PCA demonstrated clear transcriptional segregation between the Immune_High and Immune_Low groups, supporting the robustness of the identified immune subtypes and confirming substantial pathway-level heterogeneity among GBM patients (Fig. 5).

To evaluate the prognostic significance of the identified immune-associated subtypes, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed comparing patients classified as Immune_High and Immune_Low. Survival analysis demonstrated distinct overall survival trends between the two groups; however, the observed difference did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 6).

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis comparing overall survival between Immune_High and Immune_Low glioblastoma subtypes identified through ssGSEA-based pathway clustering. No statistically significant survival difference was observed between the two immune-associated subgroups (log-rank p = 0.43872)

Despite the slightly prolonged survival observed in the Immune_High subgroup, log-rank testing revealed no statistically significant survival difference between the two immune phenotypes (log-rank p = 0.43872).

Machine learning-based identification of prognostic hallmark pathways

To complement the survival-based statistical analyses, exploratory machine learning approaches were applied to prioritize biologically relevant hallmark pathways according to their relative importance. Random Forest and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) models were constructed using the ssGSEA-derived hallmark pathway enrichment scores. These models were implemented as complementary feature-prioritization tools rather than as standalone prognostic classifiers.

Feature importance analysis derived from the XGBoost model identified several inflammatory and mesenchymal signaling pathways as the most influential contributors within the machine learning framework. Among the highest-ranked pathways were TNF-alpha signaling via NF-kB, Complement, KRAS signaling Up, Apical Junction, and Estrogen Response Late, suggesting that immune-inflammatory activation and epithelial–mesenchymal transition-associated processes may contribute to the biological heterogeneity of glioblastoma (Fig. 8A). Additional pathways, including Coagulation, Apical Surface, and UV Response Up, also demonstrated notable importance scores.

The SHAP summary plot demonstrated heterogeneous pathway-specific effects across patients, with pathways such as Complement, Estrogen Response Early, Epithelial–Mesenchymal Transition, and Apical Junction exhibiting both positive and negative contributions depending on pathway activity levels (Fig. 8B). Construction and evaluation of the prognostic risk signature

To further investigate the prognostic significance of hallmark pathway activity in glioblastoma, a prognostic risk signature was constructed using the survival-associated hallmark pathways identified through Cox proportional hazards regression analysis. Individual risk scores were calculated for each patient using the regression coefficients derived from the multivariable Cox model, and patients were subsequently stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups according to the predefined median risk score.

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated a significant difference in overall survival between the two risk groups (Fig. 9A). Patients classified into the high-risk group exhibited markedly poorer survival outcomes compared with those in the low-risk group (log-rank p = 0.01568), indicating that the constructed prognostic signature successfully stratified glioblastoma patients according to survival probability.

The predictive performance of the risk signature was further evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The prognostic model achieved an AUC value of 0.623, indicating acceptable discriminative performance for survival stratification within the TCGA cohort (Fig. 9B).

Most patients exhibited intermediate risk values clustered around the median cutoff, whereas smaller subsets of patients demonstrated markedly elevated or reduced risk scores, reflecting distinct prognostic profiles within the cohort (Table 7).

Independent prognostic value of the risk signature

To determine whether the constructed prognostic risk signature could independently predict survival outcomes in glioblastoma patients, multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was performed by integrating the calculated risk score with available clinical variables, including age and gender.

The multivariate Cox regression model demonstrated that the prognostic risk score remained a significant independent predictor of overall survival after adjustment for clinical covariates (Fig. 10). The calculated hazard ratio for the risk score was markedly elevated (HR = 2.54), indicating that patients with increased risk scores exhibited substantially poorer survival outcomes compared with low-risk patients. In contrast, gender did not demonstrate significant prognostic association, whereas age showed only a limited contribution to survival prediction.

External validation of the prognostic risk signature using the CGGA cohort

To validate the robustness and reproducibility of the prognostic risk signature, an independent external validation analysis was performed using the CGGA glioblastoma cohort comprising 313 patients. Patients were stratified into a high-risk group (n = 159) and a low-risk group (n = 154) according to the median risk score calculated in the validation cohort. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated a clear survival disadvantage in the high-risk group compared with the low-risk group (Fig. 11A). The high-risk subgroup exhibited significantly reduced overall survival, whereas patients in the low-risk subgroup showed prolonged survival, confirming the reproducibility and robustness of the proposed prognostic model in an independent external cohort.

Discussion

Glioblastoma remains one of the most aggressive and clinically challenging primary brain tumors, characterized by extensive molecular diversity, profound immune dysregulation, and persistently poor patient survival despite multimodal therapy. Rather than focusing exclusively on individual genes, the present study investigated glioblastoma at the pathway level using ssGSEA-derived hallmark signaling activity profiles. By integrating immune landscape characterization, survival modeling, machine learning prioritization, and external cohort validation, we established a pathway-based prognostic framework capable of stratifying GBM patients into biologically and clinically distinct risk groups.

Our analysis demonstrated marked variability in immune-related and oncogenic pathway activity across the TCGA-GBM cohort. Pathways associated with inflammatory signaling, interferon responses, cell-cycle progression, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and hypoxia showed considerable differences between patients, supporting the concept that GBM is not a uniform disease but rather a collection of transcriptionally diverse tumor ecosystems. Unsupervised K-means clustering identified two immune-associated subtypes based on standardized ssGSEA-derived pathway enrichment scores. The Immune_High subtype showed enrichment of TNF-α/NF-κB signaling, IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, inflammatory response pathways, and interferon-associated programs, whereas the Immune_Low subtype exhibited comparatively reduced immune pathway activity. Similar observations have been reported previously, where inflammatory signaling networks and tumor-associated macrophages were shown to contribute substantially to GBM progression and immune remodeling [10, 19].

Among the identified pathways, the TNF-α/NF-κB axis emerged as one of the most biologically relevant components associated with aggressive disease behavior. NF-κB signaling has been repeatedly implicated in glioma proliferation, resistance to apoptosis, inflammatory cytokine production, and treatment adaptation [20, 21]. Previous studies demonstrated that activation of TNF-α/NF-κB signaling contributes to the maintenance of tumor-supportive microenvironmental interactions and promotes malignant progression in glioma [22]. Our findings are consistent with these observations and further support the importance of inflammatory signaling pathways in shaping GBM aggressiveness.

The simultaneous enrichment of proliferative pathways, including E2F targets, G2M checkpoint signaling, and KRAS-associated programs, alongside inflammatory pathways, suggests that aggressive GBM phenotypes may arise through coordinated interaction between oncogenic proliferation and immune-related signaling. Elevated activity of EMT and hypoxia pathways in high-risk tumors further supports this interpretation. Mesenchymal transition programs have previously been associated with invasiveness, therapeutic resistance, stemness acquisition, and poor prognosis in glioblastoma [9, 23]. Similarly, hypoxia-driven transcriptional reprogramming is recognized as a major contributor to angiogenesis, metabolic adaptation, and immunosuppression within the tumor microenvironment [24].

Although immune subtype clustering revealed clear transcriptional differences between Immune_High and Immune_Low tumors, survival analysis did not demonstrate statistically significant differences between the two groups. This finding is consistent with growing evidence suggesting that immune activation alone may not necessarily predict a favorable prognosis in glioblastoma because the tumor immune microenvironment frequently remains predominantly immunosuppressive despite elevated inflammatory signaling [8, 25]. Tumor-associated macrophages, microglia, and regulatory immune populations may collectively support tumor progression even in immune-enriched tumors. Consequently, immune clustering alone may incompletely reflect the balance between anti-tumor and pro-tumor immune activity.

The prognostic pathway analysis identified multiple hallmark pathways significantly associated with patient survival, including coagulation, complement signaling, EMT, KRAS signaling, and TNF-α/NF-κB activity. Increasing evidence suggests that coagulation-related signaling contributes directly to glioma aggressiveness through vascular remodeling, thrombosis-associated inflammation, and protease-activated signaling cascades [26]. Complement activation has also been linked to immunosuppressive microenvironmental remodeling and enhanced tumor progression in glioma [27]. Collectively, these observations indicate that coordinated inflammatory and stromal signaling programs may play central roles in determining GBM clinical behavior.

Exploratory machine learning analysis identified TNF-α/NF-κB signaling, complement activation, KRAS signaling, and EMT-associated pathways among the most influential prognostic features. However, the identified pathways highlight biologically relevant signaling programs that may warrant further investigation as potential therapeutic targets.

The predictive performance of the Random Forest and XGBoost classifiers remained limited. This result should be interpreted cautiously rather than overstated. Glioblastoma prognosis is influenced by multiple biological layers extending beyond transcriptomic pathway activity alone, including epigenetic regulation, genomic alterations, therapeutic response variability, and spatial tumor architecture. In addition, the relatively limited size of the TCGA-GBM cohort may restrict the performance of complex machine learning models. Nevertheless, the machine learning framework remained biologically informative by prioritizing pathways consistently associated with aggressive disease phenotypes.

The prognostic risk signature derived from pathway-level features demonstrated more clinically meaningful performance. The model successfully stratified patients into high-risk and low-risk groups with significantly different survival outcomes, while multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that the calculated risk score retained independent prognostic significance after adjustment for clinical covariates. Importantly, external validation using the independent CGGA cohort substantially strengthened the reliability of the proposed model. The validated signature maintained significant survival stratification and demonstrated stable predictive performance across multiple follow-up intervals, indicating that the observed prognostic associations were reproducible beyond the original TCGA discovery cohort.

Although the prognostic signature demonstrated consistent survival stratification in both the TCGA discovery cohort (n = 162) and the independent CGGA validation cohort (n = 313), differences in baseline survival distributions were observed between the two datasets. Moreover, the CGGA dataset contains more comprehensive clinicopathological information, including histological subtype, WHO grade, IDH mutation status, MGMT promoter methylation status, radiotherapy, and temozolomide treatment, all of which may substantially influence overall survival. Consequently, differences in median survival and predictive performance between the two cohorts are expected and most likely reflect differences in cohort composition and clinical characteristics rather than limitations of the proposed prognostic signature. Importantly, despite these differences, the pathway-based risk signature consistently discriminated high-risk and low-risk patients in both independent cohorts, supporting its robustness and generalizability.

External validation is particularly important given the reproducibility concerns surrounding computational oncology studies. Many previously published prognostic signatures fail to maintain performance when tested in independent cohorts because of overfitting or cohort-specific bias [11]. In contrast, the consistent performance observed in the CGGA cohort suggests that the present pathway-based framework captures biologically meaningful features associated with glioblastoma progression.

From a clinical perspective, the proposed signature may provide utility for prognostic stratification and risk-adapted therapeutic decision-making. The identified pathways highlight biologically relevant signaling programs that may warrant further investigation as potential therapeutic targets. Although the predictive accuracy of the model remains insufficient for standalone clinical implementation, integration with additional molecular variables such as MGMT promoter methylation, genomic alterations, radiomic features, and single-cell transcriptional data may further improve predictive capability in future studies.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The retrospective nature of public transcriptomic datasets introduces potential cohort bias and clinical variability. Furthermore, the present analysis was performed exclusively at the bulk transcriptomic level and therefore cannot fully resolve cellular heterogeneity within the tumor microenvironment. Functional experimental validation was also beyond the scope of this study. Future investigations integrating single-cell sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and multi-omics profiling may provide deeper mechanistic insight into the biological pathways identified here. Prospective validation in clinically annotated cohorts will also be necessary before translation into routine clinical application.

Despite the encouraging performance of the proposed pathway-based signature, translating computational biomarkers into routine clinical practice requires rigorous pathological validation, standardized diagnostic workflows, and prospective multicenter evaluation. As emphasized by Filippini et al., the clinical implementation of molecular biomarkers depends not only on computational performance but also on reproducible pathological assessment and expert validation across independent clinical settings. Therefore, further biological and clinical validation will be essential before the proposed signature can be considered for routine clinical application [28].

Limitations

Despite the promising findings of this study, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the analyses were based on retrospective transcriptomic datasets obtained from publicly available databases, which may be subject to inherent selection bias and differences in sample processing protocols. Second, although the prognostic signature demonstrated reproducible performance in an independent external CGGA cohort, additional validation in larger multi-center cohorts and prospective clinical studies is necessary to further establish its clinical utility and generalizability. Third, the present investigation relied primarily on computational and bioinformatics approaches, and therefore, the biological mechanisms underlying the identified hallmark pathways require experimental validation through both in vitro and in vivo studies. In addition, several well-established clinicopathological prognostic factors in glioblastoma, including MGMT promoter methylation status, IDH mutation status, extent of surgical resection, and adjuvant treatment information, were not incorporated into the multivariable prognostic model because these variables were unavailable or incomplete in the publicly available TCGA dataset used for model development. As these factors are recognized as important determinants of patient survival, their integration with pathway-based transcriptomic signatures may further improve prognostic accuracy and should be explored in future prospective studies with comprehensive clinical annotation. Furthermore, patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups using the median risk score as a predefined cutoff because it represents an objective, unbiased, and widely adopted approach that generates balanced patient groups while minimizing the risk of overfitting. Although alternative cutoff strategies, such as maximally selected rank statistics or time-dependent ROC-based thresholds, may further optimize risk stratification, these approaches often require additional optimization and may reduce model generalizability. Future prospective studies involving larger, clinically well-annotated cohorts should compare different cutoff strategies to determine the most clinically informative threshold for pathway-based prognostic signatures in glioblastoma.

Nevertheless, this study provides a comprehensive pathway-centered framework that captures immune-related hallmark pathway heterogeneity in glioblastoma and demonstrates the prognostic relevance of coordinated pathway activity beyond individual gene-based approaches. The successful external validation of the proposed risk signature highlights its potential robustness and clinical applicability. Future studies integrating multi-omics datasets, single-cell transcriptomics, spatial profiling technologies, comprehensive clinicopathological variables, and experimental validation may further refine the proposed model and contribute to the development of personalized prognostic and therapeutic strategies for glioblastoma patients.

Conclusion

This study provides a pathway-centered characterization of immune and oncogenic signaling heterogeneity in glioblastoma and establishes a prognostic risk signature derived from hallmark pathway activity profiles. The identified pathways revealed biologically meaningful interactions between inflammatory signaling, proliferative programs, mesenchymal transition, and tumor-associated immune activity. The resulting prognostic signature demonstrated significant survival stratification and retained independent prognostic value in both the TCGA discovery cohort and the externally validated CGGA cohort.

The findings further support the concept that pathway-level analysis may provide a biologically interpretable framework for understanding glioblastoma progression beyond individual gene-based approaches. However, because the present study is based entirely on computational analyses, the proposed prognostic signature should be considered a research framework that requires prospective biological validation, functional experimental studies, and large multicenter clinical validation before routine clinical implementation.

Collectively, these findings support the prognostic relevance of immune-related hallmark pathway activity in glioblastoma and provide a biologically interpretable foundation for future pathway-oriented prognostic modeling and therapeutic stratification. Future studies integrating comprehensive clinicopathological data, multi-omics approaches, and prospective validation cohorts will be essential to further evaluate the clinical utility and generalizability of the proposed signature.

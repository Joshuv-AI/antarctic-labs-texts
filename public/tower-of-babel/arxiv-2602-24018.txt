Abstract
Cell-free massive multiple-input–multiple-output (CFmMIMO) is a key enabler for sixth-generation (6G) wireless communication networks, where distributed access points (APs) jointly serve user equipments (UEs). In commonly adopted channel models for CFmMIMO networks, inter-AP channel correlation is assumed to be absent, thereby eliminating the potential benefits of centralized processing. However, by carefully designing the pilot transmission phase, the AP received signals during pilot transmission can become correlated, and thus, centralization can improve channel estimation performance, despite the absence of inter-AP channel correlation. In this paper, we propose a channel estimation scheme, termed master-assisted channel estimation (MACE), that aims to leverage inter-AP signal correlation by means of partially centralized processing and hence improve channel estimation performance. In MACE, a subset of APs fuse and forward their received pilot signals to a master AP, which then performs channel estimation using the fused signals together with its locally received signals. This scheme strikes a balance between local and fully centralized processing by leveraging inter-AP signal correlation, while reducing fronthaul signaling and computational complexity. Numerical experiments demonstrate that MACE consistently outperforms local channel estimation, where inter-AP signal correlation is neglected.



I
Introduction

Cell-free massive multiple-input-multiple-output (CFmMIMO) is a form of Network MIMO
[
5
]
, where a large number of access points (APs) are distributed in a geographical region to jointly serve user equipments (UEs). This distributed deployment of APs allows CFmMIMO to provide macro-diversity, cancel interference, and offer uniform quality of service to all UEs in the network
[
6
,
7
]
. These benefits have brought CFmMIMO to the forefront of research and led it to be considered one of the key enablers of sixth-generation (6G) wireless communication networks
[
3
,
9
]
.

During the pilot transmission phase, the APs typically perform channel estimation using minimum mean-squared error (MMSE)-based estimators. Linear MMSE (LMMSE) estimation is commonly adopted under Rayleigh and Rician fading
[
3
,
11
]
, and several variants have been proposed, such as element-wise MMSE
[
10
]
and phase-aware MMSE
[
12
]
. In the standard pilot transmission phase design, where UEs transmit deterministic pilot signals, and in the absence of inter-AP channel correlation, centralized processing does not offer performance gains for channel estimation
[
4
]
.

In typical CFmMIMO channel models, the channels across different APs are assumed to be uncorrelated. This modeling choice is motivated by the spatial diversity of the APs, which enables them to observe the environment from different spatial perspectives. However, under line-of-sight (LoS) conditions and with a particular design of the pilot transmission phase, the received signals of the APs can become correlated. More specifically, a slight modification of the pilot transmission phase outlined in
[
13
]
, which was first proposed in
[
2
]
, results in correlated received signals across APs, showcasing clear potential for centralized processing. However, centralized processing incurs increased computational complexity and fronthaul signaling. These trade-offs motivate the development of channel estimation schemes that explicitly leverage inter-AP signal correlation and balance performance with resource requirements.

In this paper, we propose a partially centralized channel estimation scheme, termed master-assisted channel estimation (MACE), inspired by the master-assisted distributed uplink operation
[
1
]
. In MACE, each user is assigned a master AP (MAP) and a set of additional serving APs (ASAPs), which fuse and forward their received signals to the MAP. The MAP then performs channel estimation using the fused signals together with its locally received signals. MACE requires additional computational complexity and fronthaul signaling compared to the local channel estimation scheme, but significantly reduces fronthaul signaling and computational complexity compared to the fully centralized channel estimation scheme. Numerical experiments demonstrate that MACE consistently outperforms local channel estimation and approximates centralized channel estimation performance.

II
System Model

We consider a CFmMIMO network with
L
APs, each equipped with
N
antennas, and
K
single-antenna UEs. For simplicity, we adopt the canonical CFmMIMO model, where all APs jointly serve all UEs
[
8
]
. Our analysis can be easily extended for user-centric CFmMIMO, where each UE is served by a subset of APs
[
4
]
. The APs are connected to a central processing unit (CPU) through ideal fronthaul links. The channel between UE
k
and AP
j
in coherence block
b
is denoted by
\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{j,k}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and the collective channel of UE
k
to all APs in coherence block
b
is denoted by
\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{k}[b]=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{1,k}^{T}[b]&\dots&\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{L,k}^{T}[b]\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{LN}
. We consider that the channels are constant over coherence blocks of
\tau_{c}
samples. Since we only focus on channel estimation, we assume that
\tau_{p}=\tau_{c}
samples are used for channel estimation in all coherence blocks. We adopt the design of the pilot transmission phase outlined in
[
2
]
. Specifically, in each coherence block
b
, UE
k
chooses at random one pilot signal
\phi_{t_{k}[b]}\in\mathbb{C}^{\tau_{p}}
out of
\tau_{p}
mutually orthogonal pilot signals, where
t_{k}[b]\in\{1,\dots,\tau_{p}\}
denotes the pilot index chosen by UE
k
in coherence block
b
, and
\lVert\bm{\mathrm{\phi}}_{t_{k}[b]}\rVert^{2}=\tau_{p}
. Furthermore, UE
k
scales its pilot signal using a scaling factor
\gamma_{k}[b]\in\{-1,+1\}
in coherence block
b
, where
\gamma_{k}[b]=-1
with probability
1/2
and
\gamma_{k}[b]=+1
with probability
1/2
. Henceforth, the index
b
refers to the index of the coherence block.

II-A

Channel Model

We adopt the correlated Rician fading model, where the channel between UE
k
and AP
j
is given by

\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{j,k}[b]=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}+\breve{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}[b]\,,

(1)

where
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}
is the deterministic LoS component, and
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}[b]
is the random zero-mean non-LoS (NLoS) component. The NLoS component is distributed as
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}[b]\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{\mathrm{0}}_{N},\breve{\bm{\mathrm{{R}}}}_{j,k})
, where
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{{R}}}}_{j,k}
is the
N\times N
correlation matrix. The correlation matrix of the channel is then given by

\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{j,k}=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}^{H}+\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{j,k}\,.

(2)

We assume that the LoS component and the correlation matrix remain constant over a large number of coherence blocks, and hence do not depend on the coherence block index
b
, as shown in (
2
). Furthermore, we assume that the NLoS components are independent across APs. The collective channel is then given by

\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{k}[b]=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}[b]+\breve{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}[b]\,,

(3)

where
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{1,k}^{T}&\dots&\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{L,k}^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
is the LoS component and
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}[b]=\begin{bmatrix}\breve{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{1,k}^{T}[b]&\dots&\breve{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{L,k}^{T}[b]\end{bmatrix}^{T}
is the NLoS component. The correlation matrix of the NLoS component is denoted by
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{k}
and is an
LN\times LN
block diagonal matrix, consisting of the local correlation matrices
\{\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{j,k}\}_{j=1}^{L}
, i.e.,
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{k}=\text{blkdiag}\{\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{1,k},\dots,\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{L,k}\}
. Finally, the correlation matrix
\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{LN\times LN}
of the collective channel is given by

\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{k}=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}^{H}+\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{k}\,.

(4)

II-B

Local and Centralized Channel Estimation

During the pilot transmission phase, AP
j
receives the pilot signals of the UEs and constitutes the received signal
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times\tau_{p}}
, given by

\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}[b]=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sqrt{p_{i}}\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{j,i}[b]\gamma_{i}[b]\bm{\mathrm{\phi}}_{t_{i}[b]}^{T}+\bm{\mathrm{N}}_{j}[b]\,,

(5)

where
p_{i}
is the transmit power used by UE
i
, and
\bm{\mathrm{N}}_{j}[b]
is the noise matrix with independently distributed
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
entries. The correlation matrix of
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}[b]
is
\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
, and is given by

\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j}=\mathbb{E}\{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}^{H}[b]\}=\tau_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{j,i}+\tau_{p}\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N}\,

(6)

where we used the fact that
\mathbb{E}\{\gamma_{i}[b]\gamma_{i^{\prime}}[b]\}=0
for
i\neq i^{\prime}
, and 1 otherwise. For the channel estimate of UE
k
, AP
j
correlates the received signal in (
5
) with
\gamma_{k}[b]\bm{\mathrm{\phi}}_{t_{k}[b]}/\sqrt{\tau_{p}}
to obtain the despread signal
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, given by

\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]=\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{j,k}[b]+\sum_{i\neq k}\sqrt{p_{i}\tau_{p}}\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{j,i}[b]\delta_{i}[b]+\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]\,,

(7)

where
\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{\mathrm{0}}_{N},\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N})
, and
\delta_{i}[b]
is a random variable because of the random selection of the pilot signals by the UEs and the random scaling factor
\gamma_{i}[b]

[
2
]
. The random variable
\delta_{i}[b]
can be
0
(if
\phi_{t_{k}[b]}\neq\phi_{t_{i}[b]}
),
1
(if
\phi_{t_{k}[b]}=\phi_{t_{i}[b]}
and
\gamma_{k}[b]=\gamma_{i}[b]
), or
-1
(if
\phi_{t_{k}[b]}=\phi_{t_{i}[b]}
and
\gamma_{k}[b]\neq\gamma_{i}[b]
), with probabilities
1-1/\tau_{p}
,
1/(2\tau_{p})
, and
1/(2\tau_{p})
, respectively, and its mean and variance are given by
\mathbb{E}\{\delta_{i}\}=0
and
\mathbb{E}\{\delta_{i}^{2}\}=1/\tau_{p}
.

Hereafter, we assume that the LoS components are known at the APs, since they can be easily estimated. The LoS estimation can be performed by averaging the despread signal in (
7
) across
B
coherence blocks, i.e.,

\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}}\mathbb{E}\{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]\}\approx\frac{1}{B\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}}\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{B}\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b^{\prime}]\,.

(8)

II-B
1
Local Channel Estimation

If local channel estimation is used, AP
j
directly performs LMMSE channel estimation and obtains the channel estimate

\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}^{\text{(l)}}[b]=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}+\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{j,k}\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j,t_{k}}^{-1}(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{j,t_{k}})\,,

(9)

where
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{j,t_{k}}=\mathbb{E}\{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]\}=\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}
, and

\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j,t_{k}}=\mathbb{E}\left\{(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{j,t_{k}})(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{j,t_{k}})^{H}\right\}\,,

(10)

which (using
\mathbb{E}\{\delta_{i}^{2}\}=1/\tau_{p}
) is given by

\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j,t_{k}}=p_{k}\tau_{p}\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{j,k}+\sum_{i\neq k}p_{i}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{j,k}+\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N}\,.

(11)

Observing (
6
) and (
11
) we can write

\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{p_{k}\tau_{p}(\tau_{p}-1)}\left(\tau_{p}\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j,t_{k}}+\tau_{p}p_{k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}^{H}-\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j}\right)\,.

(12)

Equation (
9
) can be used to estimate
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}[b]
, when estimates of
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{j,k}
and
\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j,t_{k}}
are available. Equation (
12
) can be used to estimate
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{j,k}
, when estimates of
\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j,t_{k}}
in (
10
) and
\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{j}
in (
6
) are available. In practice, the correlation matrices of the received signal in (
6
) and the despread signal in (
10
) can be estimated in every coherence block, as proposed in
[
2
]
, or even less frequently, since the channel statistics vary much more slowly than the channel realization. The correlation matrices in (
6
) and (
11
) are then estimated by exponential averaging, i.e.,

\begin{split}\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{j}[b]=\eta&\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{j}[b-1]+(1-\eta)\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{H}_{j}[b]\\[5.69046pt]
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]=\eta&\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{j,t_{k}}[b-1]+(1-\eta)\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j,t_{k}}^{H}[b]\,,\end{split}

(13)

where
\eta\in(0,1)
close to
1
and the hat
\widehat{\cdot}
denotes estimated matrices. Then,
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{j,k}
is estimated in every coherence block as

\widehat{\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}}_{j,k}[b]=\frac{1}{p_{k}\tau_{p}(\tau_{p}-1)}\left(\tau_{p}\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{j,t_{k}}[b]+\tau_{p}p_{k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}^{H}-\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{j}[b]\right)

(14)

using the two correlation matrices in (
13
) and (
12
)
[
2
]
.
The normalized mean squared error (NMSE) of the channel estimate is given by

\text{NMSE}^{\text{(l)}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\{\lVert\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{j,k}-\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}^{\text{(l)}}[b]\rVert^{2}\}}{\text{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{j,k})}.

(15)

Local channel estimation incurs no fronthaul signaling, since no communication takes place between the APs and the CPU. The computational complexity is
\mathcal{O}(N^{3})
, due to the matrix inversion in (
9
).

II-B
2
Centralized Channel Estimation

In centralized channel estimation, the APs send their received signals in (
5
) to the CPU, which then has access to the matrix
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}[b]=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{1}^{T}[b]&\dots&\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{L}^{T}[b]\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{LN\times\tau_{p}}
, given by

\bm{\mathrm{Y}}[b]=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sqrt{p_{i}}\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{i}[b]\gamma_{i}[b]\phi_{t_{i}[b]}+\bm{\mathrm{N}}[b]\,,

(16)

where
\bm{\mathrm{N}}[b]=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathrm{N}}_{1}^{T}[b]&\dots&\bm{\mathrm{N}}_{L}^{T}[b]\end{bmatrix}^{T}
. The correlation matrix of
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}[b]
is
\bm{\mathrm{Q}}\in\mathbb{C}^{LN\times LN}
, and is given by

\bm{\mathrm{Q}}=\mathbb{E}\{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{H}[b]\}=\tau_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{i}+\tau_{p}\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{LN}\,.

(17)

After correlating the received signal in (
16
) with the normalized pilot of UE
k
, the CPU obtains the despread signal
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{LN}
given by

\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b]=\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{k}[b]+\sum_{i\neq k}\sqrt{p_{i}\tau_{p}}\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{i}[b]\delta_{i}[b]+\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{t_{k}}[b]\,,

(18)

where
\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{t_{k}}[b]=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{1,t_{k}}^{T}[b]&\dots&\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{L,t_{k}}^{T}[b]\end{bmatrix}^{T}
. The CPU then performs LMMSE channel estimation to obtain the centralized collective channel estimate
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}^{\text{(c)}}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{LN}
, given by

\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}^{\text{(c)}}[b]=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}[b]+\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{k}\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{t_{k}}^{-1}(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{t_{k}})\,,

(19)

where
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{t_{k}}=\mathbb{E}\{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b]\}=\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}
, and

\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{t_{k}}=\mathbb{E}\left\{(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{t_{k}})(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{t_{k}})^{H}\right\}\,,

(20)

which is given by

\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{t_{k}}=p_{k}\tau_{p}\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{k}+\sum_{i\neq k}p_{i}\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{i}+\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{LN}\,.

(21)

Note that
\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{t_{k}}
is not block diagonal, thanks to the correlation matrices
\{\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{i}\}_{i\neq k}
in (
21
), which are not block diagonal because of the LoS component of the collective channels. These correlation matrices appear in (
21
) thanks to the random variable
\delta_{i}
, arising from the random pilot selection and the random scaling factor
\gamma_{i}
. Hence, the despread signals among the APs are correlated, and centralized channel estimation is expected to perform better than local channel estimation. Observing (
17
) and (
21
), we can write

\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{k}=\frac{1}{p_{k}\tau_{p}(\tau_{p}-1)}\left(\tau_{p}\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{t_{k}}+\tau_{p}p_{k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}^{H}-\bm{\mathrm{Q}}\right)\,,

(22)

which can be used to estimate
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{k}
, when estimates of
\bm{\mathrm{Q}}_{t_{k}}
in (
20
) and
\bm{\mathrm{Q}}
in (
17
) are available. These two correlation matrices can be estimated by exponential averaging, i.e.,

\begin{split}\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}[b]=\eta&\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}[b-1]+(1-\eta)\bm{\mathrm{Y}}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{H}[b]\\[5.69046pt]
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{t_{k}}[b]=\eta&\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{t_{k}}[b-1]+(1-\eta)\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b]\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}^{H}[b]\,.\end{split}

(23)

Moreover, the LoS component of the collective channel, used in (
19
), can be estimated by averaging over
B
coherence blocks, i.e.,

\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}[b]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}}\mathbb{E}\{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b]\}\approx\frac{1}{B\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}}\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{B}\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{t_{k}}[b^{\prime}]\,.

(24)

For reasons that will become apparent later, we focus on the part of the collective channel estimate, given in (
19
), that corresponds to AP
l
, i.e.,

\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{l,k}^{\text{(c)}}=\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}^{\text{(c)}}_{k}[(l-1)N+1:lN]\,,

(25)

where we have extracted the elements of
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{k}^{\text{(c)}}
that correspond to indices from
(l-1)N+1
to
lN
. The NMSE of the channel estimate between UE
k
and AP
l
is then given by

\text{NMSE}^{\text{(c)}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\{\lVert\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{l,k}-\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{l,k}^{\text{(c)}}[b]\rVert^{2}\}}{\text{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{l,k})}.

(26)

Centralized channel estimation requires the exchange of
\tau_{p}LN
complex scalars through fronthaul links, due to the communication between the APs and the CPU. The computational complexity is
\mathcal{O}(L^{3}N^{3})
, due to the matrix inversion in (
19
).

III
Master-Assisted Channel Estimation

In this section, we describe MACE, the main contribution of this paper. In MACE, each UE is served by a MAP and a set of ASAPs. Since we focus on the channel estimation of UE
k
, we denote the MAP of UE
k
by
l
. All other
L-1
APs are assumed to be the ASAPs of UE
k
. In MACE, the ASAPs fuse their received signals in (
5
) using a fusion vector
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{j,k}[b]
and send these fused signal to MAP
l
, which has access to its own locally received signal
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{l}[b]
and the fused signals
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{j,k}^{H}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}[b]
. In particular, ASAPs use their local channel estimates in (
9
) as fusion vectors, i.e.,
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{j,k}[b]=\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{j,k}^{\text{(l)}}[b]
. Thus, MAP
l
has access to
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{(N+L-1)\times\tau_{p}}
, given by

\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}[b]=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{1,k}^{H}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{1}[b]\\
\vdots\\
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l-1,k}^{H}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{l-1}[b]\\
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]\\
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l+1,k}^{H}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{l+1}[b]\\
\vdots\\
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{L,k}^{H}[b]\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{L}[b]\\
\end{bmatrix},

(27)

which can be written as

\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}[b]=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sqrt{p_{i}}\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{l,ki}[b]\gamma_{i}[b]\phi_{t_{i}[b]}^{T}+\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{N}}}_{l,k}[b]\,,

(28)

where,
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{N}}}_{l,k}[b]=(\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{l,k}^{\text{diag}}[b])^{H}\bm{\mathrm{N}}[b]
, with

\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{l,k}^{\text{diag}}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{(LN)\times(N+L-1)}
given by

\begin{split}\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{l,k}^{\text{diag}}[b]=\text{blkdiag}\{\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{1,k}^{\text{(l)}}[b],\dots,\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{l-1,k}^{\text{(l)}}[b],&\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N},\\
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{l+1,k}^{\text{(l)}}[b],\dots,&\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{L,k}^{\text{(l)}}[b]\}\,,\end{split}

(29)

and
\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{l,ki}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{N+L-1}
is the fused channel of UE
i
, given by

\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{l,ki}[b]=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}[b]+\breve{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}[b]\,,

(30)

with
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}[b]=(\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{l,k}^{\text{diag}}[b])^{H}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{i}
and
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}[b]=(\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{l,k}^{\text{diag}}[b])^{H}\breve{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{i}[b]
. The correlation matrix of the fused channel in (
30
) is given by

{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{l,ki}=\mathbb{E}\{{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}[b]{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}^{H}[b]\}=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}^{H}+\breve{{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}}_{l,ki}\,,

(31)

where

\breve{{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}}_{l,ki}=\mathbb{E}\{\breve{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}[b]\breve{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}^{H}[b]\}

(32)

is the block diagonal correlation matrix of the NLoS component
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,ki}[b]
. The correlation matrix of
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}[b]
in (
28
) is
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N+L-1)\times(N+L-1)}
and is given by

\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l}=\mathbb{E}\{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}[b]\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}^{H}[b]\}=\tau_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{l,ki}+\tau_{p}\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N+L-1}\,.

(33)

MAP
l
then correlates
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}[b]
with the normalized pilot of UE
k
to obtain the despread signal
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]\in\mathbb{C}^{N+L-1}
, given by

\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]=\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{l,kk}[b]+\sum_{i\neq k}\sqrt{p_{i}\tau_{p}}\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{l,ki}[b]\delta_{i}[b]+\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]\,,

(34)

where
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{t_{k}}[b]=(\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{l,k}^{\text{diag}}[b])^{H}\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]
. MAP
l
then performs LMMSE channel estimation to obtain the estimate of the fused channel
\bm{\mathrm{z}}_{l,kk}[b]
given by

\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}[b]=\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}[b]+\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\breve{{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}}_{l,kk}\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l,t_{k}}^{-1}(\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}}_{t_{k}}),

(35)

where
\bar{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}}_{t_{k}}=\mathbb{E}\{{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]\}=\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}
, and

\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l,t_{k}}=\mathbb{E}\{(\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}}_{t_{k}})(\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]-\bar{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}}_{t_{k}})^{H}\}\,,

(36)

which is given by

\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l,t_{k}}=p_{k}\tau_{p}\breve{{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}}_{l,kk}+\sum_{i\neq k}p_{i}{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{l,ki}+\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}}_{N+L-1}\,.

(37)

Observing (
33
) and (
37
) we can write the correlation matrix of
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}
as

\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{l,kk}=\frac{1}{p_{k}\tau_{p}(\tau_{p}-1)}\left(\tau_{p}\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l,t_{k}}+\tau_{p}p_{k}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}^{H}-\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l}\right)\,,

(38)

which can be used to estimate
\breve{\bm{\mathrm{R}}}_{l,kk}
, when estimates of
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l,t_{k}}
in (
36
) and
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}_{l}
in (
33
) are available.
These two correlation matrices can be estimated by exponential averaging, i.e.,

\begin{split}\widehat{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}}_{l}[b]=\eta&\widehat{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}}_{l}[b-1]+(1-\eta)\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}[b]\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Y}}}_{l}^{H}[b]\\[5.69046pt]
\widehat{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]=\eta&\widehat{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{Q}}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b-1]+(1-\eta)\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{l,t_{k}}^{H}[b]\,.\end{split}

(39)

Moreover, the LoS component of the collective channel, used in (
35
), can be estimated by averaging over
B
coherence blocks, i.e.,

\bar{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}[b]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}}\mathbb{E}\{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b]\}\approx\frac{1}{B\sqrt{p_{k}\tau_{p}}}\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{B}\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}_{l,t_{k}}[b^{\prime}]\,.

(40)

We again focus on the part of
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}[b]
in (
35
) that corresponds to MAP
l
, i.e.,

\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{l,k}^{\text{(m)}}=\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}[l:l+N-1]\,.

(41)

where we have extracted the elements of
\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{z}}}_{l,kk}
that correspond to indices from
l
to
l+N-1
. The NMSE is then given by

\text{NMSE}^{\text{(m)}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\{\lVert\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{l,k}-\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{h}}}_{l,k}^{\text{(m)}}[b]\rVert^{2}\}}{\text{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{R}}_{l,k})}.

(42)

MACE requires the exchange of
\tau_{p}(N+L-1)
complex scalars through fronthaul links, due to the communication of the ASAPs and the MAP. This reduces the fronthaul signaling compared to the centralized channel estimation by a factor of
LN/(N+L-1)
. The computational complexity of MACE is
\mathcal{O}\left((N+L-1)^{3}\right)
, due to the matrix inversion in (
35
), which is substantially lower than that of centralized channel estimation.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed MACE, a partially centralized channel estimation scheme that leverages inter-AP signal correlation in CFmMIMO networks to improve channel estimation. In MACE, each UE is assigned one MAP and a set of ASAPs. During the pilot transmission phase, the ASAPs fuse their received signals and send them to the MAP, which uses the fused signals together with its locally received signals to leverage inter-AP signal correlation for channel estimation. MACE relaxes the fronthaul and computation requirements compared to centralized channel estimation, while providing consistent performance improvements over local channel estimation.

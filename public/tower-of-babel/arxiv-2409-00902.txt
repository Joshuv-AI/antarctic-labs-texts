Inverse coefficient problems for one-dimensional time-fractional diffusion equations


Abstract
We prove the uniqueness in determining a spatially varying zeroth-order coefficient of a one-dimensional time-fractional diffusion equation by initial value and Cauchy data at one end point of the spatial interval.

Introduction and main results

Let
\ell,T>0
. By
L^{2}(0,\ell)
,
H^{2}(0,\ell)
,

W^{1,1}(0,T)
,
W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,\ell))
,
L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,\ell))
, etc.,
we denote the Lebesgue space and usual Sobolev spaces.
For
0<\alpha<1
, we define the Caputo derivative:

d_{t}^{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\frac{dv}{ds}(s)ds
for
v\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
,
where
\Gamma
denotes the gamma function (e.g., Podlubny
[
14
]
).
Let

u=u(x,t)\in W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,\ell))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,\ell))
satisfy

d_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)-p(x)u(x,t),\quad u(x,0)=a(x)\in C^{2}[0,\ell],\quad 0<x<\ell,\,0<t<T.

This is a model system for example for anomalous diffusion in
heterogeneous media (e.g.,
[
14
]
).
This article is concerned with

Inverse coefficient problem:

Let
T>0
be arbitrarily fixed.
Then determine
p(x)
,
0<x<\ell
by data

u(x,0)
for
0<x<\ell
and
u(0,t)
,
\partial_{x}u(0,t)

for
0<t<T
.

We state our first main result on the uniqueness.

Theorem 1

Let
p,q\in C^{1}[0,T],a\in C^{2}[0,T]
and

u,\widetilde{u}\in W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,\ell))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,\ell))
satisfy

d_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)-p(x)u(x,t),\quad u(x,0)=a(x),\quad\partial_{x}u(0,t)=0,\quad x\in\ell,\,0<t<T

(1.1)

and

d_{t}^{\alpha}\widetilde{u}(x,t)=\partial_{x}^{2}\widetilde{u}(x,t)-q(x)\widetilde{u}(x,t),\quad\widetilde{u}(x,0)=a(x),\quad\partial_{x}u(0,t)=0,\quad x\in\ell,\,0<t<T.

(1.2)

We assume that

|a(x)|\neq 0\quad\mbox{for $0\leq x\leq\ell$.}

(1.3)

Then,
u(0,t)=\widetilde{u}(0,t)
for
0<t<T
, implies

p(x)=q(x)
for
0\leq x\leq\ell
.

Theorem
1
is the uniqueness without any data at
x=\ell
, for
given initial value.
The condition
|a|\neq 0
on
[0,\ell]
is essential.

Theorem 2

We assume that
p,q\in C^{1}[0,\ell],a\in C^{2}[0,\ell
and satisfy
(
1.3

u,\widetilde{u}\in W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,\ell))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,\ell))
satisfy
u(x,0)=\widetilde{u}(x,0)=:a(x)
for

d_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)-p(x)u(x,t),\quad d_{t}^{\alpha}\widetilde{u}(x,t)=\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}(x,t)-q(x)\widetilde{u}(x,t)\quad\mbox{in $(0,\ell)\times(0,T)$}.

(1.4)

Key lemmata

Lemma 1

(i) Let
p,q\in C^{1}[0,\ell]
. There exists a unique solution
K=K(x,y)\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})

to the following problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{x}^{2}{K}(x,y)-\partial_{y}^{2}K(x,y)=q(x)K(x,y)-p(y)K(x,y),\quad(x,y)\in\Omega=\{(x,y);\,0<y<x<\ell\},\\
&(\partial_{y}K)(x,0)=0,\quad 2\frac{d}{dx}{K}(x,x)=q(x)-p(x),\quad 0<x<\ell,\quad K(0,0)=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

(ii)
(transformation operator)

Let Let
p,q\in C^{1}[0,\ell],

u\in W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,\ell))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,\ell))
satisfy

d_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)-p(x)u(x,t)
in
(0,\ell)\times(0,T)
.
Then the function
\widetilde{v}
given by

\widetilde{v}(t,x):=u(t,x)+\int^{x}_{0}K(x,y)u(t,y)dy,\quad 0<x<\ell,\,0<t<T

(2.2)

belongs to
W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,\ell))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,\ell))
and satisfies

d_{t}^{\alpha}\widetilde{v}(x,t)-\partial_{x}^{2}\widetilde{v}(x,t)+q(x)\widetilde{v}(x,t)=-K(x,0)\partial_{x}u(0,t),\,\partial_{x}\widetilde{v}(0,t)=\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}(0,t),\quad\widetilde{v}(0,t)={u}(0,t).

(2.3)

Part (i) is concerned with a Goursat problem and the proof is standard
by means of the characteristics (e.g., Suzuki
[
15
]
).
The part (ii) of Lemma 1
is the same as Lemma
2
in
[
4
]
.

4
Concluding remarks

We proved the uniqueness for the inverse coefficient
problems without full boundary conditions
for spatially one-dimensional time-fractional diffusion equations.
Our method is based on the transformation operator, and
widely applicable if we have the unique continuation property
for the partial differential equations under consideration.
Our approach requires that the spatial dimension is one.

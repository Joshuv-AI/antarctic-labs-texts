Some capacitary strong type inequalities and related function spaces
Keng Hao Ooi 1,2, Nguyen Cong Phuc 3 In memory of Professor David R. Adams

Abstract
. We verify a conjecture of D. R. Adams on a capacitary strong type inequality that generalizes the classical capacitary strong type inequality of V. G. Maz’ya. As a result, we characterize related function spaces as Köthe duals to a class of Sobolev multiplier type spaces. Moreover, using tools from nonlinear potential theory, weighted norm inequalities, and Banach function space theory, we show that these spaces are also isomorphic to more concrete spaces that are easy to use and fit in well with the modern theory of function spaces of harmonic analysis. Keywords: capacitary strong type inequality, Riesz’s potential, Bessel’s potential, Wolff’s potential, trace inequality, Sobolev multiplier type space.



1.
Introduction

Let
n
be a positive integer. For
\alpha\in(0,n)
and
1<s<\frac{n}{\alpha}
, the space of Riesz potentials
\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}=\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, is defined
as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}}=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Here the operator
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
is understood as

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{\alpha})\mathcal{F},

where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

It is known that (see
[
MH
]
)
\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}
consists of functions of the form
u=I_{\alpha}f
for some
f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}}=\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.
Here, for
\alpha\in(0,n)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

I_{\alpha}f(x):=\gamma(n,\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-y|^{\alpha-n}f(y)dy,

is the Riesz potential of
f
of order
\alpha
, and

\gamma(n,\alpha)=\Gamma(\tfrac{n-\alpha}{2})/[\pi^{n/2}2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\tfrac{\alpha}{2})].
Note that the Riesz kernel
\gamma(n,\alpha)|x|^{\alpha-n}
is
the inverse Fourier transform of
|\xi|^{-\alpha}
(in the distributional sense).

In the case
\alpha=k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1<s<\frac{n}{k}
we have
\dot{H}^{k,s}\approx\dot{W}^{k,s}
, where
\dot{W}^{k,s}=\dot{W}^{k,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the homogeneous Sobolev space defined as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{k,s}}:=\sum_{|\beta|=k}\|D^{\beta}u\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The capacity associated to
\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}
is the Riesz capacity defined for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

{\rm cap}_{\alpha,\,s}(E):=\inf\Big\{\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}:f\geq 0,I_{\alpha}f\geq 1{\rm~on~}E\Big\}.

The Choquet integral associated to
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
of a function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is defined by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}gd{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:g(x)>t\})dt.

This is well defined as long as
g
is defined quasi-everywhere (q.e.) with respect to
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
,
i.e.,
g
is defined except for a set of zero capacity. With this, we can define the Choquet
L^{q}
quasi-norm of any
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
q.e. defined function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]
by

\|u\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad q>0.

By definition, a function
u
is said to be quasi-continuous
with respect to
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists an open set
G
such that
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(G)<\epsilon
and
u
is continuous on
G^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus G
.
For any
q>0
, the space
L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
and its subspace
\{f\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}):f{\rm~is~quasicontinuous~w.r.t.~}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\}
are both complete quasi-norm spaces. This can be proved as in the proof of
[
OP1
, Proposition 2.3]
. Moreover, they are normable in the case
q\geq 1
(see
[
OP1
]
and Theorem
6.2
below).

In most applications, the function
u
under discussion is given as the potential
u={I}_{\alpha}f
of a function
f
.
One of the fundamental results in this study is the following capacitary strong type inequality discovered originally by Maz’ya in the early 1960s and subsequently extended by
Adams and Dahlberg (see, e.g.,
[
MSh
,
AH
]
):

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{s}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq A\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}dx

for all nonnegative
f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The significance of (
1.1
) is that it provides a useful way to control the unwieldy quantity
\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{s}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}
.
In fact, (
1.1
) yields the following important characterization of trace inequalities: Given a nonnegative measure
\mu
, the trace inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{s}d\mu\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}dx

holds for all nonnegative
f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\mu
satisfies the capacitary condition

(1.2)

\mu(K)\leq C{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)

for all compact sets
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

After (
1.1
), due to its relevant to PDEs, spectral theory, and the theory of function spaces, there have been interests in controlling the Choquet quasi-norm

\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}
. In
[
Ada
, p. 22]
, D. R. Adams conjectured the following bound for this quantity:

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq A\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx

at least for nonnegative functions
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and
q\geq 1
. The case
q=s
goes back to (
1.1
), and the
integer
case where
\alpha\in(0,n)\cap\mathbb{N}
was obtained by Adams in the same paper but only in the range

1\leq q<s+\frac{n}{n-\alpha}
. Most recently, the case
q=1
and all real
\alpha\in(0,n)
was settled in
[
OP2
]
.

In this paper, we work out the bound (
1.3
) for all real
\alpha\in(0,n)
and all
q\in[1,\infty)
. In fact, our proof here is new even in the case
q=1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, and
q\in[1,\infty)
. There exists a constant
A>0
such that
(
1.3
) holds for all nonnegative functions
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support, or more generally for all nonnegative measurable functions
f
such that
I_{\alpha}f\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
.

We observe that Theorem
1.1
and monotone convergence theorem imply that for
q\geq s
inequality (
1.3
) also holds for all nonnegative measurable functions
f
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx<\infty

(and there is nothing to prove in the case
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx=\infty
).

However, in the case
1\leq q<s
, inequality (
1.3
) fails for nonnegative measurable functions
f
that do not decay at infinity such as
f(x)=e^{|x|^{2}}
(provided we interpret
\frac{1}{\infty}=0
). Thus, Theorem
1.1
motivates the search for a large and natural class of measurable functions
f\geq 0
for which
I_{\alpha}f\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
so that
(
1.3
) holds for such
f
. For this purpose, we introduce the following space.
Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, and
1\leq q<s
. By a weight we mean a nonnegative and locally integrable function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define
\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}=\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the space of all measurable functions
g
in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that there exists a q.e. defined weight
w\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
with
\|w\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
such that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{s}w^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}<+\infty.

The integral above
is understood as
\int_{\{g\not=0\}}|g|^{s}w^{q-s}dx
.
Here the dot indicates that this space is associated to the space of Riesz potentials
\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}
.
The space
\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
will be equipped with the following quasi-norm

\|g\|_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}}:=\inf_{w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{s}w^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}},\qquad g\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q},

where the infimum is taken over all q.e. defined weights
w
such that
\|w\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,n)
,
0<s<\frac{n}{\alpha}
, and
q\in[1,s)
. There exists a constant
B>0
such that the bound

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq B\|f\|^{q}_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}}

holds for all nonnegative functions
f\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
. Thus (
1.3
) holds for all nonnegative functions
f\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
.

Theorem
1.2
immediately yields the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,n)
,
0<s<\frac{n}{\alpha}
, and
q\in[1,s)
. There exists a constant
B>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}|f|)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq B\|f\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}^{q}.

Here
\dot{KV}_{q}=\dot{KV}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
is a Kalton-Verbitsky space (see
[
KV
]
) defined as the set of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|f\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}<+\infty
,
where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}:=\inf\left\{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}:h\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s},~h\geq|f|~{\rm a.e.}\right\}.

It is not hard to see that the space
\dot{KV}_{q}
is continuously embedded into
\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
for
1\leq q<s
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
.
In this paper we shall show that the two spaces are indeed isomorphic. We observe that results of this type are known earlier only in the case
q=1
(see
[
KV
]
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,n)
,
0<s<\frac{n}{\alpha}
, and
q\in[1,s)
. Then it holds that

\dot{KV}_{q}\approx\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}.

However, Theorem
1.4
does not say much about the nature of
\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
(or
\dot{KV}_{q}
). For example, at this point we do not know if the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function as well as standard Calderón-Zygmund type operators are bounded on those spaces.
To this end, we will show in Theorem
1.6
below that they are actually isomorphic to the Köthe dual of
a Sobolev multiplier type space. But first we need to provide the definition of Sobolev multiplier type space.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
,
p>1
, and
0<r\leq s
. Define
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}=\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the space of
functions
f\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that the trace inequality

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({I}_{\alpha}h)^{r}|f|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{r}{p}},

holds for all nonnegative functions
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. A norm of a function
f\in\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
is defined as the least possible constant
C
in the above inequality.

Note that the case
r=s
goes back to
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p}
that was considered earlier in
[
OP1
]
, i.e., one has
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,s}=\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p}
.

With this, we recall that the Köthe dual

(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}
of
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
is defined by

(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}:=\left\{{\rm measurable~functions~}g:\sup_{f}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|fg|dx<+\infty\right\},

where the supremum is taken over all functions
f
in the unit ball of
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
. The norm of a function
g\in(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}
is defined as the above
supremum.

Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
,
0<r\leq s
, and
p>1
. With
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, we now define
\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}=\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the space of all measurable functions
g
in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that there exists a quasi-continuous weight
w\in A_{1}
with
\|w\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
and
[w]_{A_{1}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)
such that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}<+\infty.

In what follows, for
0\leq w<+\infty
a.e., the integral above is understood as
\int_{\{g\not=0\}}|g|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx
.
This implies that
g=0
a.e. on the set
\{w=0\}
.
Recall that
A_{1}
is the class of
A_{1}
weights which consists of nonnegative locally integrable functions
w
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

{\bf M}w(x)\leq Cw(x)\qquad{\rm a.e.}

The
A_{1}
characteristic constant of
w
,
[w]_{A_{1}}
, is defined as the least possible constant
C
in the above inequality.
The operator
{\bf M}
stands for the (center) Hardy-Littlewood maximal function defined for each
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

{\bf M}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)|dy.

A quasi-norm for
\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
is given by

\|g\|_{\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}}:=\inf_{w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\quad g\in\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r},

where the infimum is taken over all quasi-continuous weights
w\in A_{1}
with
\|w\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
and
[w]_{A_{1}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)
.
The constant
\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)
depends only on
n,\alpha
, and can be taken as the
A_{1}
constant of the Riesz kernel
\gamma(n,\alpha)\left|\cdot\right|^{\alpha-n}
. That is, we can take
\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)=[\left|\cdot\right|^{\alpha-n}]_{A_{1}}
. Note that this constant is sharper than the implicit constant
\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha,s)
that was used in
[
OP1
]
for the case
r=s
.

Likewise, we define
\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}=\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the space of all measurable functions
g
in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|g\|_{\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}}<+\infty
, where now

\|g\|_{\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}}:=\inf_{w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\quad g\in\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r},

with the infimum being taken over all q.e. defined weights
w
such that
\|w\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
. That is, we do not require quasi-continuity and
A_{1}
conditions on the weight
w
in the definition of
\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
.

We remark that since
\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\|w\right\|_{L^{1}({\rm cap_{\alpha,s}})}
(see, e.g.,
[
OP3
, Equ. (3.1)]
), it is easy to see from Hölder’s inequality that
\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r},\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}\hookrightarrow L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, one can verify that the two are quasi-normed spaces.

It is obvious that for
1\leq q<s
,

\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}=\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{s,s/r},

where
r=\frac{s(s-q)}{(s-1)q}
. Similarly, we have

\dot{O}^{\alpha,s}_{q}=\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{s,s/r},\quad 1\leq q<s,\quad r=\frac{s(s-q)}{(s-1)q},

where
\dot{O}^{\alpha,s}_{q}=\dot{O}^{\alpha,s}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of all measurable functions
g
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|g\|_{\dot{O}^{\alpha,s}_{q}}<+\infty
. The quasi-norm
\|g\|_{\dot{O}^{\alpha,s}_{q}}
is given by

\|g\|_{\dot{O}^{\alpha,s}_{q}}:=\inf_{w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{s}w^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}},\qquad g\in\dot{O}^{\alpha,s}_{q},

where the infimum is taken over all quasi-continuous weights
w
such that
\|w\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
and
[w^{\frac{s-q}{s-1}}]_{A_{1}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
,
p>1
, and
0<r\leq s
. Then it holds that, isomorphically,

(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}\approx\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}\approx\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}.

In particular, for any
1\leq q<s
,

\dot{KV}_{q}\approx\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}\approx\dot{O}^{\alpha,s}_{q}=\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{s,\frac{(s-1)q}{s-q}}\approx\left(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},\frac{s(s-q)}{(s-1)q}}\right)^{\prime}

We remark that Theorem
1.6
is known in the case
r=s
, but under a weaker
A_{1}
bound in the definition of
\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},1}
(see
[
OP1
]
). Our approach here is different from that of
[
OP1
]
and works simultaneously for all
0<r\leq s
.

An application of Theorem
1.6
is that it implies that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function and Calderón-Zygmund type operators are bounded on

(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}
,
p>1,0<r\leq s
and
\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
,
1\leq q<s
. Also, by Theorem
6.7
below, such operators are bounded on
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
.
Moreover, as a consequence of Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
, we can now obtain other characterizations for the
L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
quasi-norm, when
1\leq q<s
.
For a q.e. defined function
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
\dot{\lambda}^{\alpha,s}_{q}(u)
and
\dot{\beta}^{\alpha,s}_{q}(u)
,
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
,
1\leq q<s
, the following quantities:

\dot{\lambda}^{\alpha,s}_{q}(u):=\inf\left\{\|f\|_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}}:0\leq f\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}\text{ and }I_{\alpha}f\geq|u|\text{ q.e.}\right\}

and

\dot{\beta}^{\alpha,s}_{q}(u):=\inf\left\{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}:0\leq f\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q},\,I_{\alpha}f\geq|u|\text{ q.e.}\right\}.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, and
1\leq q<s
. For any q.e. defined function
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it holds that

\|u\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\simeq\dot{\lambda}^{\alpha,s}_{q}(u)\simeq\dot{\beta}^{\alpha,s}_{q}(u).

In Section
9
below, we revisit the space
\dot{KV}_{q}
,
1\leq q<s
, and show that it is indeed a Banach function space in the sense of
[
Lux
]
. Moreover, we also discuss the duality triplet

\overline{C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\text{--}(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}\text{--}\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s},

where
\overline{C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}
indicates the closure of the set of continuous functions with compact support in
\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}
. That is, we point out that

(1.4)

\left(\overline{C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\right)^{*}=(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime},\quad{\rm and~}\left[(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}\right]^{*}=\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}.

To conclude this section, we remark that all of the above results are also available in the inhomogeneous case, where the space of Riesz potentials

\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and Riesz capacity
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
are replaced with the space of Bessel potentials
H^{\alpha,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and Bessel capacity
{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}
,
s>1,0<\alpha\leq\frac{n}{s}
, respectively; see Section
10
for the details.

Notation.
In the above and in what follows, for two quasi-normed spaces
F
and
G
we write
F\approx G
(respectively,
F=G
) to indicate that the two spaces are isomorphic (respectively, isometrically isomorphic). For two quantities
A
and
B
, we write
A\simeq B
to mean that there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that
c_{1}A\leq B\leq c_{2}A
.

2.
Banach function spaces

Most of the spaces of this work are Banach function spaces or are isomorphic to Banach function spaces. We shall adopt the definition of Banach function spaces used in
[
Lux
]
(see also
[
LN
]
). A Banach function space
X
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with Lebesgue measure as the underlying measure, is the set of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\|f\|_{X}:=\rho(|f|)
is finite. Here
\rho(f)
,
f\geq 0
, is a given metric function (
0\leq\rho(f)\leq\infty
) that obeys the following properties:

(P1)
\rho(f)=0
if and only if
f(x)=0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
;
\rho(f_{1}+f_{2})\leq\rho(f_{1})+\rho(f_{2})
; and
\rho(\lambda f)=\lambda\rho(f)
for any constant
\lambda\geq 0
.

(P2) (Fatou’s property) If
\{f_{j}\}
,
j=1,2,\dots
, is a sequence of nonnegative measurable functions and
f_{j}\uparrow f
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\rho(f_{j})\uparrow\rho(f).

(P3) If
E
is any bounded and measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\chi_{E}
is its characteristic
function, then
\rho(\chi_{E})<+\infty
.

(P4) For every bounded and measurable subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a finite constant

A_{E}\geq 0
(depending only on the set
E
) such that
\int_{E}fdx\leq A_{E}\rho(f)

for any nonnegative measurable function
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

It follows from property (P2) that any Banach function space
X
is complete (see
[
Lux
]
). We also have that, for measurable functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, if
|f_{1}|\leq|f_{2}|
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f_{2}\in X
, then it follows that
f_{1}\in X
and
\|f_{1}\|_{X}\leq\|f_{2}\|_{X}
.

Given a Banach function space
X
, the Köthe dual space (or the associate space) to
X
, denoted by
X^{\prime}
, is the set of all measurable functions
f
such that
fg\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
g\in X
. It turns out that
X^{\prime}
is also a Banach function space with the associate metric function
\rho^{\prime}(f)
,
f\geq 0
, defined by

\rho^{\prime}(f):=\sup\left\{\int|fg|dx:g\in X,\,\|g\|_{X}\leq 1\right\}.

By definition, the second associate space
X^{\prime\prime}
to
X
is given by
X^{\prime\prime}=(X^{\prime})^{\prime}
, i.e.,
X^{\prime\prime}
is the Köthe dual space to
X^{\prime}
. The following theorems are fundamental in the theory of Banach function spaces (see
[
Lux
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Every Banach function space
X
coincides
with its second associate space
X^{\prime\prime}
, i.e.,
X=X^{\prime\prime}
with equality of norms.

Theorem 2.2
.

X^{*}=X^{\prime}
(isometrically) if and only if the space
X
has an absolutely continuous norm.

Here we say that
X
has an absolutely continuous norm if the following properties are satisfied for any
f\in X
:

(a) If
E
is a bounded set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
E_{j}
are measurable subsets of
E
such that
|E_{j}|\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
, the
\|f\chi_{E_{j}}\|_{X}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
.

(b)
\|f\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{j}(0)}\|_{X}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
.

It is known that
X
has an absolutely continuous norm
if and only if any sequence
f_{j}\in X
such that
|f_{j}|\downarrow 0

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has the property that
\|f_{j}\|_{X}\downarrow 0
(see
[
Lux
, page 14]
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We will need the so-called “integrating by parts” lemma that was obtained in
[
Ver1
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t\geq 1
and suppose that
f
is a nonnegative measurable function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then it holds that

(I_{\alpha}f)^{t}\leq AI_{\alpha}[f(I_{\alpha}f)^{t-1}]

everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
f\geq 0
, with compact support, or
f
be a nonnegative measurable function such that
I_{\alpha}f\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
,
q\geq 1
. Note that the former is a special case of the later.
To prove (
1.3
), we may assume that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx<+\infty
. Then under the condition
I_{\alpha}f\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
we can check that
\|f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\|_{(M^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}<+\infty
. Indeed, for any
0\leq g\in\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s}
, recall from (
1.2
) that

\|g\|_{\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s}}\simeq\sup_{K}\left(\frac{\int_{K}g(x)^{s^{\prime}}dx}{\text{cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)}\right)^{1/s^{\prime}},

where the supremum is taken over all compact sets
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\text{cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)\not=0
.
Thus, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}gdx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{s^{\prime}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\{(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}>t\}}g^{s^{\prime}}dy\right)dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|g\|_{M^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}.

This shows that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle<+\infty,

as claimed.

Also, recall that the space of quasi-continuous functions
g
such that
\|g\|_{L^{1}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}<+\infty
is normable and its dual space consists of locally finite signed measures
\mu
such that

\|\mu\|_{\dot{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha,s}}:=\sup_{K}\frac{|\mu|(K)}{{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)}<+\infty;

see, e.g.,
[
OP1
]
.
Suppose for now that
f
is a bounded function with compact support. Then by Lemma
3.1
and Hahn-Banach theorem, we find

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}

\displaystyle\leq A\int I_{\alpha}[f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}]d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}

\displaystyle\leq A\sup_{\|\mu\|_{\dot{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha,s}}\leq 1}\int I_{\alpha}[f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}]d|\mu|

\displaystyle=A\sup_{\|\mu\|_{\dot{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha,s}}\leq 1}\int(I_{\alpha}|\mu|)f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}dx

\displaystyle\leq A\|f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}\sup_{\|\mu\|_{\dot{\mathfrak{M}}^{\alpha,s}}\leq 1}\|I_{\alpha}|\mu|\|_{\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s}}

\displaystyle\leq A\|f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}.

Note that in the last inequality above we used
[
MV
, Theorem 2.1]
. Now for any nonnegative measurable function
f
such that
I_{\alpha}f\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
, we let

(3.3)

f_{N}=f\cdot\chi_{\{|f|\leq N\}}\cdot\chi_{\{|x|\leq N\}},\quad N=1,2,\dots

Then using (
3.2
) and
q\geq 1
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f_{N})^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq A\|f_{N}(I_{\alpha}f_{N})^{q-1}\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}\leq A\|f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}},

which yields

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq A\|f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}.

At this point, combining (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) we obtain

(3.5)

\|f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}\leq A\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx.

Inequality (
1.3
) now follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).

∎

Remark 3.2
.

The proof above actually shows that inequality (
1.3
) holds under the assumption
f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\in(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}
.
Note also that by (
3.4
) we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}I_{\alpha}gd{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq A\|g\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}
,
g\geq 0
, and thus by
(
3.5
), it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}I_{\alpha}[f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}]d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx

provided
f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\in(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}
,
f\geq 0
. By approximation, the condition
f(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-1}\in(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}
is not needed in the case
q\geq s
.

On the other hand, for the case
q>s
, we notice that Theorem
1.1
can be proved simply by using the equivalence (see Theorem
6.2
)

\|u\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{q}\simeq\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{s}dx:g\geq 0,I_{\alpha}g\geq|u|^{\frac{q}{s}}~{\rm q.e.}\right\}.

Indeed, then for
u=I_{\alpha}f
, we have
u^{\frac{q}{s}}\leq cI_{\alpha}(f(I_{\alpha}f)^{\frac{q}{s}-1})
, and the result immediately follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose that
1\leq q<s
. We are to show that there is some constant
C>0
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}w^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

for any weight
w
with
\|w\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
. Observe that by (
3.4
) we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}I_{\alpha}fd{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq A\|f\|_{(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}}.

Thus, by a characterization of the space
(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}
in
[
OP1
]
(or by Theorems
6.2
and
7.7
below) we see that
(
4.1
) holds in the case
q=1
. Note also that the case
q=s
is just (
1.1
).

Suppose now that
1<q<s
. For a weight
w
with
\|w\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
, assume without loss of generality that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}w^{q-s}dx<+\infty.

We express
f
as

\displaystyle f=a^{1-\theta}\cdot b^{\theta},

where
\theta\in(0,1)
is such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{s}+\frac{\theta}{1},

and

\displaystyle a=f\cdot w^{q(1-s)\frac{\theta}{s}},\qquad b=f\cdot w^{q(1-s)\frac{\theta-1}{s}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|w^{q(1-s)\frac{1}{1-s}}\right\|_{L^{1}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq 1,

and we have by Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}f=I_{\alpha}(a^{1-\theta}\cdot b^{\theta})\leq(I_{\alpha}a)^{1-\theta}(I_{\alpha}b)^{\theta}.

On the other hand, we see that

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\frac{q}{s}+\theta q=q\left[\frac{\theta}{1}+\frac{1}{s}(1-\theta)\right]=q\cdot\frac{1}{q}=1.

As a consequence, applying Hölder’s inequality again, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}a)^{(1-\theta)q}(I_{\alpha}b)^{\theta q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}a)^{s}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\right)^{(1-\theta)\frac{q}{s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}b)d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\right)^{\theta q}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|I_{\alpha}a\|_{L^{s}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{1-\theta}\|I_{\alpha}b\|_{L^{1}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b^{s}(w^{q(1-s)})^{\frac{1}{1-s}\cdot(1-s)}dx\right)^{\frac{\theta}{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(w^{q(1-s)})^{\theta}dx\right)^{\frac{1-\theta}{s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(w^{q(1-s)})^{\theta}dx\right)^{\frac{\theta}{s}}

\displaystyle=C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}w^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

by noting that
q(1-s)\theta=q-s
. The proof is now complete.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Suppose that
I_{\alpha}f\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
and
f\geq 0
. Let

\displaystyle w=\frac{I_{\alpha}f}{\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}}.

Then
\|w\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}=1
, and hence

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}\left(\frac{I_{\alpha}f}{\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}}\right)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle=C\frac{1}{\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{\frac{q}{s}-1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}.

Routine simplification gives

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(I_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

which provides an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
for the case
1\leq q<s
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Recall that for
1\leq q<s
,
\dot{KV}_{q}=\dot{KV}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
is defined as the set of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|f\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}<+\infty
,
where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}:=\inf\left\{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}:h\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{s,q}^{\alpha,s},~h\geq|f|~{\rm a.e.}\right\}.

Note that the condition
h\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
is necessary, or else putting
h=e^{\left|\cdot\right|^{2}}\geq\chi_{B_{1}(0)}
will result in an absurd definition.
We are to show that

\displaystyle\dot{KV}_{q}\approx\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}.

The direction
\dot{KV}_{q}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{O}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
is rather easy.
Indeed, for
f\in\dot{KV}_{q}
and
h\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
such that
h\geq|f|
a.e., it holds that
\|I_{\alpha}h\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}<+\infty
and

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha}h\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq c\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

by Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}}\leq\|h\|_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}\left(\frac{I_{\alpha}h}{\|I_{\alpha}h\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}}\right)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle=c\cdot\|I_{\alpha}h\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{\frac{s-q}{s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{s-q}{sq}+\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle=c\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Taking infimum with respect to all such
h
, one obtains the embedding
\dot{KV}_{q}\hookrightarrow\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
.

On the other hand, the proof of the embedding
\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}\hookrightarrow\dot{KV}_{q}
is more technical. To this end, let
f\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
be given. Choose some weight
w\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
with
\|w\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{s}w^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq 2\cdot\|f\|_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}}.

By Theorem
6.2
and Lemma
6.3
below, there is a function
0\leq\varphi\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
I_{\alpha}\varphi\geq w^{\frac{q}{s}}
q.e. and

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{s}{q}}\simeq\|w\|_{L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}.

Consider
h=|f|\cdot w^{\frac{q}{s}-1}+\delta\cdot\varphi
, where
\delta>0
will be determined later. Since
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}}\in L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
by (
1.1
) and moreover,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}-1}\right]^{s}\left[(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}}\right]^{q-s}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}dx<+\infty.

This shows that

(5.1)

h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}-1}\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s},

and hence by the definition of
\dot{KV}_{q}
and Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\left\|h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}-1}\right\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}-1}\right)^{s}\left(I_{\alpha}\left(h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}-1}\right)\right)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s^{2}}{q}-s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}\cdot(q-s)}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=c\cdot\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{s}{q}}.

On the other hand, since
1\leq q<s
, we have

\displaystyle h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}-1}

\displaystyle\geq|f|\cdot w^{\frac{q}{s}-1}\cdot(I_{\alpha}(\delta\cdot\varphi))^{\frac{s}{q}-1}

\displaystyle\geq\delta^{\frac{s}{q}-1}|f|\cdot w^{\frac{q}{s}-1}(I_{\alpha}\varphi)^{\frac{s}{q}-1}

\displaystyle\geq\delta^{\frac{s}{q}-1}|f|\cdot w^{\frac{q}{s}-1}w^{\frac{q}{s}(\frac{s}{q}-1)}

\displaystyle=\delta^{\frac{s}{q}-1}|f|.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{1-\frac{s}{q}}\left\|h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{\frac{s}{q}-1}\right\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\delta^{1-\frac{s}{q}}\cdot\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{s}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\delta^{1-\frac{s}{q}}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{s}w^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}\cdot\frac{s}{q}}+\delta^{\frac{s}{q}}\cdot\|\varphi\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{s}{q}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\delta^{1-\frac{s}{q}}\left(\|f\|_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}}^{\frac{s}{q}}+\delta^{\frac{s}{q}}\right).

Now we simply let
\delta=\|f\|_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}}
, then the embedding
\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}\hookrightarrow\dot{KV}_{q}
holds.
∎

6.
The spaces
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
and
(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}

It is easy to see from the definition that the space
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
,
s>1
,
0<\alpha<n/s
,
p>1
, and
0<r\leq s
, is a Banach function space in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and so is its Köthe dual space
(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}
. Moreover, we have the following results in which the case
r=s
was considered earlier in
[
OP1
]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha<n/s
,
p>1
, and
0<r\leq s
. The space
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
is
p
-convex and thus
(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}
is
p^{\prime}
-concave. Then

\displaystyle[(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}]^{*}=\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}=(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime\prime}

holds isometrically. Moreover,
(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}
has an absolutely continuous norm and is a separable Banach space.

Proof.

We first show that
\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}
is
p
-convex. Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}|f_{i}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}^{p}\cdot\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{r}{p}}

holds for all nonnegative
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. As a result, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

which justifies the
p
-convexity of
\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}
with
p
-convexity constant
1
.

Moreover, for any
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\ell^{p^{\prime}}((\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\ast})=[\ell^{p}(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})]^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|g_{i}\|_{(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|g_{i}\|_{(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\ast}}^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\|_{\ell^{p}(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})}\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}g_{i}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\|_{\ell^{p}(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}dx

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\|_{\ell^{p}(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})}\leq 1}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right\|_{(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\|_{\ell^{p}(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})}\leq 1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right\|_{(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}.

We conclude that
(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}
is
p^{\prime}
-concave with
p^{\prime}
-concavity constant
1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|g_{i}\|_{(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right\|_{(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}.

Hence the space
(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}
has an absolutely continuous norm (see
[
LT
, Proposition 1.a.7]
) and by Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle[(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}]^{*}=\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}=(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime\prime}.

Moreover,
(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}
is a separable Banach space (see
[
Lux
]
).
∎

The following theorem provides an explicit norm for the space
L^{q}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
when
q\geq 1
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose that
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, and
0<r\leq s
. Let
\dot{F}=\dot{F}^{\alpha,s}_{r}
be the space of
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
q.e. defined functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]
such that

\|u\|_{\dot{F}}<+\infty
, where

\|u\|_{\dot{F}}:=\inf\{\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}:f\geq 0,\,I_{\alpha}f\geq|u|^{\frac{1}{r}}{\rm~q.e.}\}.

The convention here is that
\inf\emptyset:=+\infty
. Then
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\dot{F}}
is a norm for
\dot{F}
and the normed space
(\dot{F},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\dot{F}})
is isomorphic to
L^{\frac{s}{r}}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
.

Proof.

Let
B
be the set of all
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
q.e. defined functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]
such that there exists an
f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
f\geq 0
,
\|f\|_{L^{s}}<1
, such that
I_{\alpha}f\geq|u|^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e. We claim that
B
is convex. Indeed, let
u_{1},u_{2}\in B
and
f_{1},f_{2}\geq 0
be such that
\|f_{i}\|_{L^{s}}<1
and

(I_{\alpha}f_{i})^{r}\geq|u_{i}|
q.e. for all
i=1,2
. For any
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we set
g=\left(\lambda f_{1}^{s}+(1-\lambda)f_{2}^{s}\right)^{1/s}
. Then

\|g\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}=\lambda\|f_{1}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}+(1-\lambda)\|f_{2}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}<1.

Moreover, by reverse Minkowski inequality,

\displaystyle(I_{\alpha}g)^{s}

\displaystyle=\gamma(n,\alpha)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\lambda f_{1}(y)^{s}+(1-\lambda)f_{2}(y)^{s}\right)^{1/s}|\cdot-y|^{\alpha-n}dy\right]^{s}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma(n,\alpha)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda^{1/s}f_{1}(y)|\cdot-y|^{\alpha-n}dy\right]^{s}

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma(n,\alpha)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1-\lambda)^{1/s}f_{2}(y)|x-y|^{\alpha-n}dy\right]^{s}

\displaystyle=\lambda(I_{\alpha}f_{1})^{s}+(1-\lambda)(I_{\alpha}f_{2})^{s}.

Thus, as
s/r\geq 1
,

\displaystyle(I_{\alpha}g)^{s}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda|u_{1}|^{s/r}+(1-\lambda)|u_{2}|^{s/r}\geq(\lambda|u_{1}|+(1-\lambda)|u_{2}|)^{\frac{s}{r}}\quad{\rm q.e.}

This gives
\lambda u_{1}+(1-\lambda)u_{2}\in B
, and thus
B
is convex as claimed.

Next, we observe that

(6.1)

\|u\|_{\dot{F}}=\inf\{t>0:|u|/t\in B\}\qquad\forall u\in\dot{F}.

Indeed, if
u\in\dot{F}
and
t>0
is such that
|u|/t\in B
, then there is a nonnegative
f\in L^{s}
such that
\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<1
and
I_{\alpha}f\geq(|u|/t)^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e. Thus,

I_{\alpha}(t^{\frac{1}{r}}f)\geq|u|^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e. and hence,

\|u\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\|t^{\frac{1}{r}}f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}=t\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}<t.

This implies that

\|u\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\inf\{t>0:|u|/t\in B\}.

Conversely, let
u\in\dot{F}
and
f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
f\geq 0
, be such that
I_{\alpha}f\geq|u|^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e.
For any
\epsilon>0
, set
g=f/(\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\epsilon)
. We have
\|g\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<1
and moreover,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}g

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\epsilon}I_{\alpha}f\geq\frac{|u|^{1/r}}{\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{|u|}{(\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\epsilon)^{r}}\right]^{1/r}=\left[\frac{|u|}{t_{0}}\right]^{1/r}{\rm~q.e.},

where
t_{0}=(\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\epsilon)^{r}
. Thus
|u|/t_{0}\in B
and we get

\inf\{t>0:|u|/t\in B\}\leq t_{0}=(\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\epsilon)^{r}.

Letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we find

\inf\{t>0:|u|/t\in B\}\leq\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r},

which yields

\inf\{t>0:|u|/t\in B\}\leq\|u\|_{\dot{F}}.

As
B
is convex, it can be seen from (
6.1
) that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\dot{F}}
is sub-additive on
\dot{F}
. That
\|tu\|_{\dot{F}}=|t|\|u\|_{\dot{F}}
is clear for all
u\in\dot{F}
. Moreover,
by
[
AH
, Proposition 2.3.8]
we find that
\|u\|_{\dot{F}}=0
implies that
u=0
q.e. Thus, we conclude that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\dot{F}}
is a norm for
\dot{F}
.

Our next task is to show that

(6.2)

\|u\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{F}}

for all
u\in\dot{F}
. To this end, let
u\in\dot{F}
and
f\in L^{s},f\geq 0
, be such that
I_{\alpha}f\geq|u|^{1/r}
q.e. We have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{s/r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{|u|^{s/r}>t\})dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{(I_{\alpha}f)^{s}>t\})dt

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}

by the capacitary strong type inequality (
1.1
). Now taking the infimum over such
f
, we get the bound (
6.2
).

Finally, we show the converse to (
6.2
), i.e.,

(6.3)

\|u\|_{\dot{F}}\leq D\|u\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}

for any
u\in L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
. For such
u
, we write

|u|=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|u|\chi_{E_{i}},

where
E_{i}=\{2^{i}\leq|u|<2^{i+1}\}
. For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
f_{i}\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
f_{i}\geq 0
, such that
I_{\alpha}f_{i}\geq(|u|\chi_{E_{i}})^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e. and

\|f_{i}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}<\|u\chi_{E_{i}}\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{s}{r}}+\epsilon\,2^{-|i|}.

Let
f(x)=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{i}(x)
. We have
f^{s}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{i}^{s}
, and thus

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{s}}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{i}^{s}dx=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}^{s}dx

\displaystyle<\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\|u\chi_{E_{i}}\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{s}{r}}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon\,2^{-|i|}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\|u\chi_{E_{i}}\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{s}{r}}+3\epsilon.

Moreover,
I_{\alpha}f\geq|u|^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e. because for q.e.
x\in E_{i}
, we have

I_{\alpha}f(x)\geq I_{\alpha}f_{i}(x)\geq\left[|u(x)|\chi_{E_{i}}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}=|u(x)|^{\frac{1}{r}}.

These bounds imply that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{s}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}<\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\|u\chi_{E_{i}}\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{s}{r}}+3\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{i+1}\chi_{E_{i}}\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{s}{r}}+3\epsilon

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(i+1)s/r}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(E_{i})+3\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{i-1}}^{2^{i}}t^{s/r}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{|u|>t\})\frac{dt}{t}+3\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{s/r}+3\epsilon.

Now, letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain (
6.3
) as desired.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
u\in\dot{F}
, where
\dot{F}=\dot{F}^{\alpha,s}_{r}
is as in Theorem
6.2
, there exists a unique nonnegative
f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
such that

I_{\alpha}f\geq u^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e. and

\|u\|_{\dot{F}}=\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}.

Proof.

For any
u\in\dot{F}
, the set

\Omega_{u^{1/r}}:=\{g\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):g\geq 0,I_{\alpha}g\geq|u|^{\frac{1}{r}}{\rm~q.e.}\}

is obviously a non-empty convex subset of
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, arguing as in the proof of
[
AH
, Proposition 2.3.9]
, one has that

\Omega_{u^{1/r}}
is closed in
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The lemma now follows from the fact that
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is uniformly convex.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

The space
\dot{F}
in Theorem
6.2
possesses the Fatou’s property, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}u_{j}\right\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}

for any sequence
\{u_{j}\}\subset\dot{F}
of nonnegative functions.

Proof.

Note that if
u\leq v
q.e. then
\|u\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\|v\|_{\dot{F}}
. Thus, it is enough to show the following monotone convergence property:

\displaystyle\left\|\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}u_{j}\right\|_{\dot{F}}=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}

for any non-decreasing sequence
\{u_{j}\}\subset\dot{F}
of nonnegative functions. The case
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}=\infty
is obvious and thus we may assume that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}=L<+\infty.

Then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, by Lemma
6.3
, there exists
f_{j}\geq 0
such that
I_{\alpha}f_{j}\geq|u_{j}|^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e. and

\|u_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{1}{r}}=\|f_{j}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus, for any
1\leq i<j
, we have
I_{\alpha}[\tfrac{1}{2}(f_{i}+f_{j})]\geq|u_{i}|^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e., and so

\|u_{i}\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\left\|\tfrac{1}{2}(f_{i}+f_{j})\right\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This enables us to apply
[
AH
, Corollary 1.3.3]
to the sequence
\{f_{k}/L^{1/r}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to deduce that the sequence
\{f_{j}\}
converges strongly to a nonnegative function
f
in
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Then, by
[
AH
, Proposition 2.3.8]
, there exists a subsequence
\{f_{j_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

I_{\alpha}f_{j_{k}}\rightarrow I_{\alpha}f
q.e. as
k\rightarrow\infty
. This yields that

I_{\alpha}f\geq\left|\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}u_{j}\right|^{\frac{1}{r}}{\rm~q.e.}

and so

\left\|\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}u_{j}\right\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}.

As
\left\|\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}u_{j}\right\|_{\dot{F}}\geq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}
is obvious, we obtain the proposition.
∎

We will need the following boundedness principle (or weak maximum principle) for Wolff potentials that can be found, e.g., in
[
Ver2
, Equ. (2.1)]
. Recall that for

s>1
and
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, the Wolff potential
W_{\alpha,s}\mu
of a nonnegative measure
\mu
is defined by

W_{\alpha,s}\mu(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\mu(B_{t}(x))}{t^{n-\alpha s}}\right]^{\frac{1}{s-1}}\frac{dt}{t},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose that
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, and
\mu
is a nonnegative measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle W_{\alpha,s}\mu(x)\leq 2^{\frac{n-\alpha s}{s-1}}\cdot\sup\left\{W_{\alpha,s}\mu(y):y\in{\rm supp}(\mu)\right\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The following characterizations of trace inequality in the so-called upper triangle case play a key role in our study of
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
and
(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime}
for
0<r<s
.
We point out that the non-capacitary characterization (iii) in the next theorem was obtained in
[
COV
]
and
[
Ver1
]
. On the other hand, there is also a capacitary characterization obtained earlier in
[
MN
]
, but that will not be used in the present paper.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, and
0<r<s
. Then the following statements are equivalent for a nonnegative locally finite (Borel) measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

\rm{(i)}
There exists a constant
A_{1}>0
such that the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({I}_{\alpha}h)^{r}d\mu\leq A_{1}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}

holds for all nonnegative
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

\rm{(ii)}

\mu
is continuous w.r.t
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
and for any quasi-continuous or
\mu
-measurable function
u\in L^{\frac{s}{r}}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
, we have

(6.4)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}ud\mu\right|\leq A_{2}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{s}{r}}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}

for a constant
A_{2}>0
.

\rm{(iii)}

[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{s-1}\in L^{\frac{r}{s-r}}(d\mu).

\rm{(iv)}

[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{s-1}\in L^{\frac{s}{s-r}}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}).

Moreover, we have

A_{1}\simeq A_{2}\simeq\left\|[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{s-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{r}{s-r}}(d\mu)}^{\frac{r}{s}}\simeq\left\|[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{s-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{s}{s-r}}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}.

Proof.

{\rm(i)}\Rightarrow{\rm(ii)}:
Let
N
be a set such that
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(N)=0
. Choose a
G_{\delta}
set
\widetilde{N}
such that

{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\widetilde{N})=0
and
N\subseteq\widetilde{N}
. This is possible since

0={\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(N)=\inf\{{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(G):N\subseteq G,G{\rm~open}\}.

If
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),h\geq 0
, is such that
I_{\alpha}h\geq 1
on
\widetilde{N}
, then it follows from
{\rm(i)}
that

\mu(\widetilde{N})\leq A_{1}\|h\|_{L^{s}}^{r}.

Taking the infimum over such functions
h
we arrive at

\mu(\widetilde{N})\leq A_{1}\,{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\widetilde{N})^{r/s}=0.

By identifying
\mu
with its completion we thus see that
N
is
\mu
-measurable and
\mu(N)=0
. This proves that

\mu
is continuous w.r.t
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
.

Now let
u
be a quasi-continuous function in
L^{\frac{s}{r}}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
. Then
u
is
\mu
-measurable.
For any
h\in L^{s}
,
h\geq 0
, such that
(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}\geq|u|
q.e., by
{\rm(i)}
we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}ud\mu\right|\leq A_{1}\|h\|_{L^{s}}^{r}

Taking the infimum over such
h
and applying Theorem
6.2
we obtain (
6.4
).

{\rm(ii)}\Rightarrow{\rm(iii)}:
Let
h\in L^{s}
and applying (
6.4
) with
u=(I_{\alpha}f)^{r}
, we find

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({I}_{\alpha}h)^{r}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq A_{2}^{\frac{1}{r}}\|I_{\alpha}h\|_{L^{s}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq c\,A_{2}^{\frac{1}{r}}\|h\|_{L^{s}},

where the last inequality follows from (
1.1
). Thus, by
[
Ver1
, Theorem 1.13]
(see also
[
COV
]
) we obtain that
[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{\frac{r(s-1)}{s-r}}\in L^{1}(d\mu).

{\rm(iii)}\Rightarrow{\rm(iv)}:
For any
t>0
, let

E_{t}=\{W_{\alpha,s}\mu>t\}
. We first claim that there are constants
a,A\geq 1
such that

(6.5)

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(E_{a\cdot t})\leq At^{1-s}\mu(E_{t}).

Indeed, set
\mu^{t}=\mu\chi_{E_{t}}
and
\mu_{t}=\mu\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E_{t}}
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E_{t}
is a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

{\rm supp}(\mu_{t})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E_{t}
. Thus,
W_{\alpha,s}\mu_{t}\leq W_{\alpha,s}\mu\leq t
on
{\rm supp}(\mu_{t})
. It then follows from the boundedness principle, Lemma
6.5
, that
W_{\alpha,s}\mu_{t}\leq c\,t
on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with a constant
c\geq 1
. Thus, there exists a constant
c_{1}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\{W_{\alpha,s}\mu>2c\,c_{1}t\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{c_{1}[W_{\alpha,s}(\mu^{t})+W_{\alpha,s}(\mu_{t})]>2c\,c_{1}t\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{W_{\alpha,s}(\mu^{t})>c\,t\}\bigcup\{W_{\alpha,s}(\mu_{t})>c\,t\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{W_{\alpha,s}(\mu^{t})>c\,t\}.

By
[
AH
, Proposition 6.3.12]
, this gives

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{W_{\alpha,s}\mu>2c\,c_{1}t\})\leq At^{1-s}\mu^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=At^{1-s}\mu(E_{t}),

which is (
6.5
) with
a=2c\,c_{1}
. Now using (
6.5
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{\frac{(s-1)s}{s-r}}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}

\displaystyle=\frac{(s-1)s}{s-r}\int_{0}^{\infty}(a\cdot t)^{\frac{(s-1)s}{s-r}}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(E_{a\cdot t})\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{(s-1)s}{s-r}+1-s}\mu(E_{t})\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{\frac{r(s-1)}{s-r}}d\mu.

{\rm(iv)}\Rightarrow{\rm(i)}:
Let
\nu
be a measure defined by

d\nu=\frac{d\mu}{W_{\alpha,s}(\mu)^{s-1}}.

Then we have

\nu(E)\leq c\,{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(E)

for any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
Ver1
, Theorem 1.11]
). Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{\frac{r(s-1)}{s-r}}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{\frac{s(s-1)}{s-r}}d\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-1)}{s-r}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s(s-1)}{s-r}}\nu(\{W_{\alpha,s}\mu>t\})\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{s(s-1)}{s-r}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s(s-1)}{s-r}}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{W_{\alpha,s}\mu>t\})\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[W_{\alpha,s}\mu]^{\frac{(s-1)s}{s-r}}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}.

By
[
Ver1
, Theorem 1.13]
, this gives
\rm(i)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Note that Theorem
6.6
allows to rewrite the norm of a function
f\in\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
,
0<r<s
, as

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}}\simeq\|W_{\alpha,s}(|f|^{p})^{\frac{s-1}{p}}\|_{L^{\frac{sp}{s-r}}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}.

Moreover,
f\in\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
if and only if
|f|^{p}
belongs to the dual of the space of quasi-continuous functions in
L^{\frac{s}{r}}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
(see
[
Ada
, Theorem 9]
).

On the other hand, for
r=s
, the norm of a function
f\in\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,s}
is equivalent to the quantity

\displaystyle\sup_{K}\left(\frac{\int_{K}|f(x)|^{p}dx}{\text{cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)}\right)^{1/p},

where the supremum is taken over all compact sets
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with non-zero capacity. Equivalently, in this case
f\in\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,s}
if and only if
|f|^{p}
belongs to the dual of the space of quasi-continuous functions in
L^{1}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
(see
[
Ada
,
OP1
]
).

Theorem 6.7
.

For
s>1
,
0<\alpha<n/s
,
p>1
, and
0<r\leq s
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}}\simeq\sup_{w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p},

where the supremum is taken over all nonnegative measurable and quasi-continuous
w\in L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
such that
\|w\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
and

[w]_{A_{1}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha):=[\left|\cdot\right|^{\alpha-n}]_{A_{1}}
.

Proof.

If
0<r<s
, then by Theorem
6.6
and the definition of
\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}
, for any q.e. defined weight
w
with
\|w\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p}\leq c\cdot\|f\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}.

On the other hand, if
r=s
, then from the characterization

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,s}}\simeq\sup_{K}\left(\frac{\int_{K}|f(x)|^{p}dx}{\text{cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)}\right)^{1/p},

we also have, for any weight
w
with
\|w\|_{L^{1}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\{w>t\}}|f|^{p}dxdt\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\|f\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{w>t\})dt\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\|f\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}.

Taking supremum over all such weights
w
, we obtain, for
0<r\leq s
,

\displaystyle\sup_{w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p}\leq c\cdot\|f\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}.

Note that the quasi-continuity and
A_{1}
conditions are not used in the above estimate. Now we show for the other direction of the estimates. We first consider the case where
0<r\leq 1
. Denote by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}=\sup\left\{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}|f|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}:h\geq 0,~\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1\right\}.

Then by the definition of
\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}
, one has

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}=\mathfrak{M}.

Also note that
(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}\in A_{1}
for
0<r\leq 1
and
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
A_{1}
constant satisfying

\displaystyle[(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}]_{A_{1}}\leq[I_{\alpha}h]_{A_{1}}\leq[\left|\cdot\right|^{\alpha-n}]_{A_{1}}={\bar{\bf c}}(n,\alpha).

Moreover,
(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}
is quasi-continuous and by (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\|(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}h)^{s}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\right)^{\frac{r}{s}}

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{r}{s}}\leq c

for any function
h\geq 0
with
\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
. We thus conclude that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}\leq c\cdot\sup_{w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p},

where the supremum is taken over all nonnegative measurable and quasi-continuous
w\in L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})
such that
\|w\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq 1

and

[w]_{A_{1}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)
.

Now we consider the case where
r>1
. Fix
h\geq 0
with
\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
.
Then as in (
5.1
) we have that
h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{r-1}\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{s/r}^{\alpha,s}
.
Let
w=I_{\alpha}(h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{r-1})
. Then
w\in A_{1}
with
[w]_{A_{1}}\leq[\left|\cdot\right|^{\alpha-n}]_{A_{1}}={\bar{\bf c}}(n,\alpha)
, and by Proposition
6.8
below
we have that
w
is quasi-continuous.

Now with
h_{N}=h\cdot\chi_{\{|h|\leq N\}}\cdot\chi_{\{|x|\leq N\}}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we find

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{s/r}

\displaystyle=\left\|I_{\alpha}\left(h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{r-1}\right)\right\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}^{s/r}

\displaystyle=\sup_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[I_{\alpha}\left(h_{N}\cdot(I_{\alpha}h_{N})^{r-1}\right)\right]^{s/r}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\sup_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{N}^{s}(I_{\alpha}h_{N})^{(r-1)s}\left[I_{\alpha}\left(h_{N}\cdot(I_{\alpha}h_{N})^{r-1}\right)\right]^{s/r-s}dx

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\sup_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{N}^{s}(I_{\alpha}h_{N})^{(r-1)s}(I_{\alpha}h_{N})^{r(s/r-s)}dx

\displaystyle=c\cdot\sup_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{N}^{s}dx\leq c.

Note that we applied Theorem
1.1
in the first inequality. In the second inequality, we used that
\frac{s}{r}-s<0
and Lemma
3.1
. Also, by Lemma
3.1
, we have
(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}\leq c\cdot w
, and thus

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}|f|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p},

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Suppose that
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\varphi
in
\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
,
1\leq q<s

\{\varphi_{i_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}I_{\alpha}\varphi_{i_{k}}(x)=I_{\alpha}\varphi(x)
q.e., and uniformly outside an open set of arbitrarily small
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\cdot)
. As a consequence,
I_{\alpha}\varphi
is quasi-continuous for any
\varphi
in
\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
.

Proof.

Note that as consequence of Theorem
1.2
, one has

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{I_{\alpha}\varphi>\lambda\})\leq c\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda}\cdot\|\varphi\|_{\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{s,q}^{\alpha,s}},\quad\lambda>0.

Using the above estimate, one can easily modify the argument of
[
AH
, Proposition 2.3.8]
to obtain the desired subsequence. To prove the last statement
let
\varphi\in\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
and set
\varphi_{N}=\varphi\cdot\chi_{\{|\varphi|\leq N\}}\cdot\chi_{\{|x|\leq N\}},N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then an argument by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem will show that
\varphi_{N}\rightarrow\varphi
in
\dot{\widetilde{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
.
On the other hand, each
I_{\alpha}\varphi_{N}
is continuous and thus applying the first part of the proposition we obtain the quasi-continuity of
I_{\alpha}\varphi
.
∎

Theorem 7.1
.

For
s>1
,
0<\alpha<n/s
, and
0<r\leq s
, it holds that

\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}\approx\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}\approx\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}.

Proof.

Observe that by Theorem
6.2
we have that

\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}\approx\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
.
Moreover, it is obvious that
\dot{N}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}\hookrightarrow\dot{\widetilde{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
.
Thus we just need to show

(7.1)

\dot{\widetilde{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}\hookrightarrow\dot{N}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}.

Indeed, for any
f\in\dot{\widetilde{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a weight
w_{0}
such that
\|w_{0}\|_{\dot{F}}\leq 1
and

(7.2)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{p^{\prime}}(w_{0})^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{\widetilde{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}+\varepsilon.

We now use Lemma
6.3
to choose an
0\leq h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
I_{\alpha}h\geq(w_{0})^{\frac{1}{r}}
q.e. and

\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}\leq 1
. Let
w=(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}
if
0<r\leq 1
and
w=I_{\alpha}\left(h\cdot(I_{\alpha}h)^{r-1}\right)
if
1<r\leq s
. Then as in the proof of Theorem
6.7
, we have
[w]_{A_{1}}\leq[\left|\cdot\right|^{\alpha-n}]_{A_{1}}
,
w
is quasi-continuous,
\|w\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq c
, and

w_{0}\leq(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}\leq c\cdot w\quad{\rm q.e.}

Hence,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{p^{\prime}}(w_{0})^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\geq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx.

Then it follows from (
7.2
) and (
7.3
) that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{\widetilde{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}+\varepsilon\geq c\cdot\|f\|_{\dot{N}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}.

Finally, letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain (
7.1
) as desired.
∎

The reason to consider
\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
is that it is always a Banach function space, whereas
\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
and

\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
may not.

Theorem 7.2
.

For
s>1
,
0<\alpha<n/s
,
0<r\leq s
, and
p>1
, the space
\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
is a Banach function space.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\|g\|^{\prime}=\inf\left\{\sum_{j}|c_{j}|:g=\sum_{j}c_{j}b_{j}~{\rm a.e.},~\|b_{j}\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq 1\right\},

where
g\in\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
. It is easy to see that
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}
is a seminorm and
(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})
is a complete semi-normed space. We now show that
\|g\|^{\prime}=0
entails
g=0
a.e. so that
(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})
is a Banach space. Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. If
\|g\|^{\prime}=0
, then there exist
\{c_{j}\}\in\ell^{1}
and
b_{j}
with
\|b_{j}\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq 1
such that
g=\sum_{j}c_{j}b_{j}
and
\sum_{j}|c_{j}|<\varepsilon
. Let
w_{j}
be a weight such that
\|w_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|b_{j}|^{p^{\prime}}w_{j}^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq 2.

Then for any
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by Hölder’s inequality it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g||\varphi|dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|b_{j}||\varphi|dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\varphi|^{p}w_{j}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\sup_{j}\left(\int_{{\rm supp(\varphi)}}w_{j}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Recall that
|E|^{1-\frac{\alpha s}{n}}\leq c\cdot{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(E)
for any measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and thus we find (see, e.g.,
[
OP3
, Equ. (3.1)]
)

(7.4)

\|w_{j}\|_{L^{\frac{ns}{r(n-\alpha s)}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)}\leq c_{1}\|w_{j}\|_{L^{s/r}({\rm cap}_{\alpha,s})}\leq c_{2}\|w_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}\leq c_{2}.

Then by Hölder’s inequality and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g||\varphi|dx=0

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and hence
g=0
a.e. as expected.

Now we show that

(7.5)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}=\|g\|^{\prime},\quad g\in\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. By expressing
g
as

\displaystyle g=\left(\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}+\varepsilon\right)\cdot\frac{g}{\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}+\varepsilon},

it follows that

\displaystyle\|g\|^{\prime}\leq\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}+\varepsilon.

The arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
yields that
\|g\|^{\prime}\leq\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}
.

On the other hand, let
g\in\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
be such that

(7.6)

\displaystyle g=\sum_{j}c_{j}b_{j},

where
\{c_{j}\}\in\ell^{1}
and
\|b_{j}\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq 1
. Choose a weight
w_{j}
such that
\|w_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\|b_{j}\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|b_{j}|^{p^{\prime}}w_{j}^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}<1+\varepsilon.

Let

\displaystyle w=\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{-1}\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot w_{j}.

Then Theorem
6.2
implies

\displaystyle\|w\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{-1}\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot\|w_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{-1}\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\leq 1.

On the other hand, Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle|g|^{p^{\prime}}\leq\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}||b_{j}|\right)^{p^{\prime}}\leq\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot w_{j}\right)^{p^{\prime}-1}\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot|b_{j}|^{p^{\prime}}w_{j}^{1-p^{\prime}}\right).

Hence,

(7.7)

\displaystyle|g|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{p^{\prime}-1}\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot|b_{j}|^{p^{\prime}}w_{j}^{1-p^{\prime}}\right),

so that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{p^{\prime}-1}\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|b_{j}|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{p^{\prime}-1}\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot(1+\varepsilon)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{p^{\prime}}\cdot(1+\varepsilon)^{p^{\prime}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{j}|c_{j}|.

Taking the infimum with respect to all representations of
g
in the form (
7.6
), we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\|g\|^{\prime}.

The arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
yields (
7.5
), which also shows that
\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
is a Banach space.

To show that
\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
is a Banach function space we just need to show property (P2), as properties (P3) and (P4) are easy to show. To this end, let
\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
be a sequence of nonnegative measurable functions such that
g_{j}\uparrow g
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We are to show that

(7.8)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq\sup_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}.

We may assume without loss of generality that

\displaystyle\sup_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}=M<+\infty.

Then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a weight
w_{j}
such that
\|w_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}\leq 1
and

(7.9)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g_{j}^{p^{\prime}}w_{j}^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq M+\varepsilon.

Note that by (
7.4
) and Hölder’s inequality it follows that, for, say,
v(x)=e^{-|x|}
,

\|w_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},v)}\leq C.

Thus Komlós Theorem (see
[
Kom
]
) yields a subsequence of
\{w_{j}\}
, still denoted by
\{w_{j}\}
, and a nonnegative measurable function
w
such that

\sigma_{k}(x):=\dfrac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}(x)\rightarrow w(x)\quad{\rm a.e.~}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Moreover, any subsequence of
\{w_{j}\}
is also Cesàro convergent to
w
almost everywhere. By redefining
w(x)
to be zero for all the points
x
such that
\sigma_{k}(x)\not\rightarrow w(x)
, one has

\displaystyle w(x)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{k}(x)\qquad\text{q.e.}

Then by Proposition
6.4
, we have

(7.10)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\left\|\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{k}\right\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{\dot{F}}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|w_{j}\|_{\dot{F}}\leq 1.

On the other hand, by the convexity of the function

t\mapsto t^{1-p^{\prime}}
on
(0,\infty)
, it holds that, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g_{j}(x)^{p^{\prime}}w(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g_{j}(x)^{p^{\prime}}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Big[\dfrac{1}{k}\sum_{m=j}^{j+k-1}w_{m}(x)\Big]^{1-p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g_{j}(x)^{p^{\prime}}\Big[\dfrac{1}{k}\sum_{m=j}^{j+k-1}w_{m}(x)\Big]^{1-p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g_{j}(x)^{p^{\prime}}\,\dfrac{1}{k}\sum_{m=j}^{j+k-1}w_{m}(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{1}{k}\sum_{m=j}^{j+k-1}g_{m}(x)^{p^{\prime}}\,w_{m}(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}dx,

where we used
0\leq g_{j}\leq g_{m}
for
m\geq j
in the last inequality. At this point, we use (
7.9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int g_{j}(x)^{p^{\prime}}w(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\leq(M+\epsilon)^{p^{\prime}}.

Then sending
j\rightarrow\infty
and
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, by Fatou’s lemma we get

\displaystyle\int g(x)^{p^{\prime}}w(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\leq M^{p^{\prime}}.

This and (
7.10
) now give (
7.8
) and complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

Let
\dot{\overline{\overline{N}}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
,
p>1,0<r\leq s
, be the space of all measurable functions
g
in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{\overline{N}}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}}<+\infty
, where

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{\overline{\overline{N}}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}=\inf_{w}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

and the infimum is taken over all quasi-continuous weights
w\in A_{1}
with
\|w\|_{\dot{F}^{\alpha,s}_{r}}\leq 1
and
[w]_{A_{1}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)
.
Then arguing as in the proof of Theorem

(and using Fatou’s lemma to show that
[w]_{A_{1}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)
), it follows that
\dot{\overline{\overline{N}}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
is also a Banach function space.
Moreover, as in Theorem
7.1
, one has
\dot{\overline{\overline{N}}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}\approx\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
.

It is easy to see that for any
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and any weight
w
such that
\|w\|_{\dot{F}}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|g_{i}\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}}^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|g_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},w^{1-p^{\prime})}}.

Thus, we have the following result.

Proposition 7.4
.

For
s>1
,
0<\alpha<n/s
,
0<r\leq s
, and
p>1
, the space

is
p^{\prime}
-concave with
p^{\prime}
-concavity constant

. Thus it has an absolutely
continuous norm and is separable. Moreover, it holds that, isometrically,

We have the following density result for
\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}

Proposition 7.5
.

For
s>1
,
0<\alpha<n/s
,
0<r\leq s
, and
p>1
, the space

is dense in
\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}
.

Proof.

Note that for any
g\in\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
, by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem the functions

converge to
g\in\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
, as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, bounded functions with compact support

are dense in
\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
. For such
f
, we define
\rho_{\varepsilon}*f=\varepsilon^{-n}\rho(\varepsilon^{-1}\cdot)*f
, where
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))
. Let
B
be a ball such that

{\rm supp}(f)\subset B
and
{\rm supp}(\rho_{\varepsilon}*f)\subset B
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then take a weight
w\in\dot{F}
such that

w\geq 1
on

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\varepsilon}*f-f\|_{\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{w}\left(\int_{B}|\rho_{\varepsilon}*f-f|^{p^{\prime}}w^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{w}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}*f-f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B)}.

Thus we see that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}

Theorem 7.7
.

For
s>1
,
0<\alpha<n/s
,
0<r\leq s

p>1

\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}=(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r})^{\prime},\quad(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}=\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.4

(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}=\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}.
On the one hand, let

\epsilon>0

f

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{p}(I_{\alpha}h)^{r}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\|f\|_{(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{r}{p}}.

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}}\leq\|f\|_{(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}.

f\in(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}=\sup_{N}\|f_{N}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq\sup_{N}\|f_{N}\|_{(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}\leq\|f\|_{(\dot{\overline{N}}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}},

which completes the proof. ∎

Lemma 9.1
.

For
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, and
1\leq q<s
, it holds that, for any

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}=\inf\left\{\sum_{j}|c_{j}|:g=\sum_{j}c_{j}b_{j}~{\rm a.e.},~\|b_{j}\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}\leq 1\right\}.

Proof.

Let
g\in\dot{KV}_{q}
. Then obviously,

\inf\left\{\sum_{j}|c_{j}|:g=\sum_{j}c_{j}b_{j}~{\rm a.e.},~\|b_{j}\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}\leq 1\right\}\leq\|g\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}.

On the other hand, suppose that
g
admits a decomposition of the form

g=\sum_{j}c_{j}b_{j}\quad{\rm a.e.},

where
\|b_{j}\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}\leq 1
and
\{c_{j}\}\in\ell^{1}
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
and
j\geq 1
, there exists
h_{j}\in\dot{\overline{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}
such that
h_{j}\geq|b_{j}|
a.e. and

(9.1)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{j}^{s}(I_{\alpha}h_{j})^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\|b_{j}\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}+\varepsilon\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Let
h=\sum_{j}|c_{j}|h_{j}

h\geq|g|
a.e. and moreover,

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\dot{\overline{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\|h_{j}\|_{\dot{\overline{O}}_{q}^{\alpha,s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\|h_{j}\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{j}|c_{j}|<+\infty.

Thus,

(9.2)

\|g\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{q-s}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Let
w_{j}=(I_{\alpha}h_{j})^{\frac{s-q}{s-1}}
and

\displaystyle w=\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{-1}\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot w_{j}.

By Hölder’s inequality,

w\leq\left(\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{-1}\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot w_{j}^{\frac{s-1}{s-q}}\right)^{\frac{s-q}{s-1}}=\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{\frac{q-s}{s-1}}\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot w_{j}^{\frac{s-1}{s-q}}\right)^{\frac{s-q}{s-1}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle h^{s}(I_{\alpha}h)^{q-s}

\displaystyle=h^{s}\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|I_{\alpha}h_{j}\right)^{q-s}=h^{s}\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|w_{j}^{\frac{s-1}{s-q}}\right)^{q-s}

\displaystyle\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{q-s}\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{s-1}\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\cdot h_{j}^{s}w_{j}^{1-s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{q-1}\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{j}^{s}(I_{\alpha}h_{j})^{q-s}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}^{q}(1+\varepsilon)^{q}.

Then letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and using (
9.2

\|g\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}\leq\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Finally, taking the infimum over such decompositions of
g
we arrive at the bound

which completes the proof.

Theorem 9.2
.

For
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
, and
1\leq q<s
, the space
\dot{KV}_{q}
is a Banach function space.

Proof.

By Lemma
9.1
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}
is a semi-norm and thus
\dot{KV}_{q}
is a Banach space in view of Theorem
1.6
.

Properties (P3) and (P4) in the definition of Banach function space also follow from Theorem
1.6
.
Now to show the Fatou’s property (P2) for
\dot{KV}_{q}
, let
0\leq g_{j}\uparrow g
and
g_{j}\in\dot{KV}_{q}
with
\sup_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}<+\infty
. Since
g_{j}\in\left(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},r}\right)^{\prime}
,
r=\frac{s(s-q)}{(s-1)q}
, and
\left(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},r}\right)^{\prime}
has the Fatou’s property, it follows that
g\in\left(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},r}\right)^{\prime}
which in turn implies that
g\in\dot{KV}_{q}
. In particular,
g(x)<+\infty
a.e. and hence
|g_{j}-g|\downarrow 0
a.e. Since
\left(\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},r}\right)^{\prime}
has an absolute continuous norm (by Proposition
6.1
), the same is also true for
\dot{KV}_{q}
. Then, it holds that
\|g_{j}-g\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}\rightarrow 0
and hence by triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{j}\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}}\leq\sup_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{\dot{KV}_{q}},

which yields the property (P2).
∎

9.2.
The triplet
\overline{C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\text{--}(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}\text{--}\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}

Suppose that
s>1
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{s}
,
p>1
, and
0<r\leq s
.
Let
E
be a measurable set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite measure. By Theorem
6.6
and the property
|K|^{1-\frac{\alpha s}{n}}\leq c\cdot{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)
for any measurable set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is easy to deduce that

\displaystyle\|f\cdot\chi_{E}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq c\cdot|E|^{(1-\frac{r}{s}+\frac{\alpha r}{n})\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(E)}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\|\chi_{E}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq c\cdot|E|^{(1-\frac{r}{s}+\frac{\alpha r}{n})\frac{1}{p}},

and for measurable sets
B\subseteq A
,

\displaystyle\|\chi_{A}-\chi_{B}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}=\|\chi_{A\setminus B}\|_{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\leq c\cdot|A\setminus B|^{(1-\frac{r}{s}+\frac{\alpha r}{n})\frac{1}{p}}.

Let
\dot{\mathcal{F}}
be the closure in
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
of the set of all finite linear combinations of characteristic functions of sets of finite Lebesgue measure.
Then one can adapt the argument in the proof of
[
ST
, Theorem 4.3]
to obtain

\displaystyle(\dot{\mathcal{F}})^{\ast}=(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}

in the sense that each bounded linear functional
L
on
\dot{\mathcal{F}}
corresponds to a unique
g\in(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}
such that

L(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}gfdx,\qquad\forall f\in\dot{\mathcal{F}},

and
\|L\|_{(\dot{\mathcal{F}})^{*}}=\|g\|_{(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}}
. With this, we can argue as in the proof of
[
OP1
, Theorem 1.9]
, which relies on Hahn-Banach theorem, to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\overline{C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\right)^{\ast}=(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}.

Thus, by Proposition
6.1
, we have the triplet

\displaystyle\overline{C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s}}\text{--}(\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s})^{\prime}\text{--}\dot{M}_{p,r}^{\alpha,s},

in the sense of (
1.4
), which reminisces the classical triplet

\displaystyle VMO=\overline{C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{BMO}\text{--}H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{--}BMO.

10.
The inhomogeneous case

Our approach here also works well for the inhomogeneous case,
where the space of Riesz potentials

\dot{H}^{\alpha,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and Riesz capacity
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
are replaced, respectively, by the space of Bessel potentials
H^{\alpha,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and Bessel capacity
{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}
,
s>1,0<\alpha\leq\frac{n}{s}
. For
s>1
and
\alpha>0
, recall that the space of Bessel potentials
H^{\alpha,s}=H^{\alpha,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, is defined as
the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{H^{\alpha,s}}=\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

As
\alpha>0
, it follows that (see, e.g.,
[
MH
]
) a function
u
belongs to
H^{\alpha,s}
if and only if

u=G_{\alpha}f:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{\alpha}(\cdot-y)f(y)dy

for some
f\in L^{s}
, and moreover

\|u\|_{H^{\alpha,s}}=\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Here
G_{\alpha}
is the Bessel kernel of order
\alpha
defined by
G_{\alpha}(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}}](x)
.
The Bessel potential space
H^{\alpha,s}
,
\alpha>0,s>1
, can be viewed as a fractional generalization of the standard Sobolev space
W^{k,s}=W^{k,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
k\in\mathbb{N},s>1
.

The capacity associated to
H^{\alpha,s}
is the Bessel capacity defined for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,\,s}(E):=\inf\Big\{\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{s}:f\geq 0,G_{\alpha}f\geq 1{\rm~on~}E\Big\}.

As for
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
, the Choquet integral associated to
{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}
of a function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is defined by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}gd{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:g(x)>t\})dt.

Recall that the capacitary strong type inequality for Bessel potentials is given by

(10.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G_{\alpha}f)^{s}d{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}dx

where
s>1
and
\alpha>0
(see
[
MSh
,
AH
]
).
Thus, the trace inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G_{\alpha}f)^{s}d\mu\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}dx

holds for all nonnegative
f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if the measure
\mu
satisfies the capacitary condition

\mu(K)\leq C{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}(K)

for all compact sets
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(actually, we can also assume that
{\rm diam}(K)\leq 1
; see
[
MSh
, Remark 3.1.1]
).

It is important to know that the “integrating by parts” lemma also holds for Bessel potentials, which was established recently in
[
GV
, Remark 2.6]
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
t\geq 1
and suppose that
f
is a nonnegative measurable function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then we have

(G_{\alpha}f)^{t}\leq AG_{\alpha}[f(G_{\alpha}f)^{t-1}]

everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The analogue of D. R. Adams conjecture in this inhomogeneous setting is the validity of the bound

(10.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G_{\alpha}f)^{q}d{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}\leq A\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{s}(G_{\alpha}f)^{q-s}dx

at least for nonnegative functions
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and
q\geq 1
. The inhomogeneous versions of
the spaces
\dot{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
,
\dot{K}_{q}
,
\dot{M}^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
, and
\dot{\widetilde{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
are denoted by
{\widetilde{O}}^{\alpha,s}_{q}
,
K_{q}
, and
M^{\alpha,s}_{p,r}
, and
\widetilde{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
, respectively, i.e., without the dot on top. They are defined similarly but now
I_{\alpha}
is replaced by
G_{\alpha}
and
{\rm cap}_{\alpha,s}
is replaced by
{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}
. With this, one finds that the analogues of Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
with
s>1,0<\alpha\leq\frac{n}{s}
, are also available in this inhomogeneous setting.

On the other hand, one should be careful about the definition of
N^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
,
s>1,0<\alpha\leq\frac{n}{s},p>1,0<r\leq s
. It is defined similarly to that of
\dot{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
, but this time the
A_{1}
condition
[w]_{A_{1}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)
on the weight
w
is replaced by the
A_{1}^{\rm loc}
condition

[w]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}\leq\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha),

and moreover, here one can take
\bar{\bf c}(n,\alpha)=[G_{\alpha}]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}
. We recall that
A_{1}^{\rm loc}
is the class of weight functions
w
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

{\bf M}^{\rm loc}w(x)\leq Cw(x)\qquad{\rm a.e.}

The
A_{1}^{\rm loc}
characteristic constant of
w
,
[w]_{A_{1}^{\rm loc}}
, is defined as the least possible constant
C
in the above inequality.
The operator
{\bf M}^{\rm loc}
is the truncated (center) Hardy-Littlewood maximal function defined for each
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

{\bf M}^{\rm loc}f(x)=\sup_{0<r\leq 1}\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)|dy.

Note that by (
10.1
), Theorem
6.2
also holds for
L^{s/r}({\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s})
provided we replace
\dot{F}
with
F=F^{\alpha,s}_{r}
,
s>1,\alpha>0,0<r\leq s
, where

\|u\|_{F}:=\inf\{\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}:f\geq 0,\,G_{\alpha}f\geq|u|^{\frac{1}{r}}{\rm~q.e.~with~respect~to~}{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}\}.

Using the space
F
above we can define
\overline{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
as we did for
\dot{\overline{N}}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
, except now that
\dot{F}
is replaced by
F
, of course. Then, as in Proposition
7.4
,
\overline{N}^{\alpha,s}_{p^{\prime},s/r}
,
s>1,0<\alpha\leq\frac{n}{s},p>1,0<r\leq s
, is a Banach functions space, and

\left(\overline{N}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}\right)^{\prime\prime}=\overline{N}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s},\quad\left(\overline{N}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}\right)^{\prime}=\left(\overline{N}_{p^{\prime},s/r}^{\alpha,s}\right)^{*}.

To obtain the analogue of Theorem
6.6
, we need to use the truncated Wolff potential
W_{\alpha,s}^{R}\mu
of a nonnegative measure
\mu
defined, for
R>0,s>1
, and
\alpha>0
, by

W_{\alpha,s}^{R}\mu(x):=\int_{0}^{R}\left[\frac{\mu(B_{t}(x))}{t^{n-\alpha s}}\right]^{\frac{1}{s-1}}\frac{dt}{t},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Note that for truncated Wolff potentials, we have the following variant of boundedness principle:

\displaystyle W_{\alpha,s}^{R}\mu(x)\leq 2^{\frac{n-\alpha s}{s-1}}\cdot\sup\left\{W_{\alpha,s}^{2R}\mu(y):y\in{\rm supp}(\mu)\right\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Indeed, let
x\notin{\rm supp}(\mu)
and
x_{0}\in{\rm supp}(\mu)
be the point that minimizes the distance from
x
to
{\rm supp}(\mu)
. If
B_{t}(x)\cap{\rm supp}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
, then
t>|x-x_{0}|
, which in turn implies that
B_{t}(x)\subseteq B_{2t}(x_{0})
. Consequently,

\displaystyle W_{\alpha,s}^{R}\mu(x)\leq\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{2t}(x_{0}))}{t^{n-\alpha s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s-1}}\frac{dt}{t}=2^{\frac{n-\alpha s}{s-1}}\cdot W_{\alpha,s}^{2R}\mu(x_{0}),

which proves the claim.

The following result is analogous to Theorem
6.6
.

Theorem 10.2
.

Let
s>1
,
0<\alpha\leq\frac{n}{s}
, and
0<r<s
. Then the following statements are equivalent for a nonnegative locally finite (Borel) measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

\rm{(i)}
There exists a constant
A_{1}>0
such that the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({G}_{\alpha}h)^{r}d\mu\leq A_{1}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}

holds for all nonnegative
h\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

\rm{(ii)}

\mu
is continuous w.r.t
{\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}
and for any quasi-continuous or
\mu
-measurable function
u\in L^{\frac{s}{r}}({\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s})
, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}ud\mu\right|\leq A_{2}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{s}{r}}({\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s})}

for a constant
A_{2}>0
.

\rm{(iii)}

[W_{\alpha,s}^{1}\mu]^{s-1}\in L^{\frac{r}{s-r}}(d\mu).

\rm{(iv)}

[W_{\alpha,s}^{1}\mu]^{s-1}\in L^{\frac{s}{s-r}}({\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s}).

Moreover, we have

A_{1}\simeq A_{2}\simeq\left\|[W_{\alpha,s}^{1}\mu]^{s-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{r}{s-r}}(d\mu)}^{\frac{r}{s}}\simeq\left\|[W_{\alpha,s}^{1}\mu]^{s-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{s}{s-r}}({\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s})}.

The proof of Theorem
10.2
is analogous to that of Theorem
6.6
. We mention here that for that one needs the inhomogeneous variants of
Theorems 1.11 and 1.13 in
[
Ver1
]
, which in turn rely on a theorem of Kerman and Sawyer
[
KS
]
. See also
[
HW
]
and a remark on page 27 of
[
MSh
]
regarding the result of
[
KS
]
.

Now using Theorem
10.2
and the discussion above, one can obtain the homogeneous versions of Theorems
1.6
,
1.7
, and the duality triplet
(
1.4
). Moreover, needless to say, other related results such as Theorems

6.7
and
7.1
, etc. are also available, with obvious modifications, in this homogeneous case.

Finally, we mention that inequality (
10.2
) for the case
q=1
was obtained in
[
OP2
, Theorem 1.1]
, and the equivalences

\|u\|_{L^{1}({\rm Cap}_{\alpha,s})}\simeq\lambda^{\alpha,s}_{1}(u)\simeq\beta^{\alpha,s}_{1}(u)

were proved in
[
OP2
, Theorem 1.2]
, with different proofs. However, we would like to take this opportunity make a correction that in the statement of
[
OP2
, Theorem 1.1]
one needs to require that
f\in(M^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime}})^{\prime}
, where
(M^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime}})^{\prime}=(M^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime},s})^{\prime}
. Likewise, the conditions
f\in(M^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime}})^{\prime}
and
h\in(M^{\alpha,s}_{s^{\prime}})^{\prime}
must also be added to the definitions of
\beta_{\alpha,s}(u)
(p. 591) and of
\|f\|_{KV}
(p. 594) of
[
OP2
]
, respectively.

Nutrient-driven small-colony variants as an adaptive outcome in Carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii

Introduction

Carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii (CRAB) poses a significant public health challenge due to high levels of antibiotic resistance (De Souza et al., 2025; Ramirez et al., 2020; Tacconelli et al., 2018). This resistance is driven by the pathogen’s genomic plasticity and the expression of multiple resistance mechanisms, including resistance to newer agents such as the siderophore-modified cephalosporin cefiderocol (Gatti et al., 2024; Ibrahim et al., 2023). Recent studies have highlighted the critical impact of environmental factors, particularly nutrient availability and host-derived proteins, on the adaptability and antibiotic resistance of CRAB (Feng et al., 2023; Le et al., 2021; Le et al., 2022; Nishimura et al., 2022; Pimentel et al., 2021; Pimentel et al., 2021). Host-derived compounds such as human serum albumin (HSA), the most abundant blood protein, and HSA-containing fluids such as human pleural fluid (HPF) and human serum (HS) influence these responses (Escalante et al., 2023; Le et al., 2021; Le et al., 2022; Martinez et al., 2019; Pimentel et al., 2021; Quinn et al., 2018; Traglia et al., 2016). These host products modulate the expression of genes related to biofilm formation, motility, DNA acquisition, iron uptake, and antibiotic resistance, while also serving as iron sources (Le et al., 2021; Pimentel et al., 2021; Pimentel et al., 2021). Published work has demonstrated that exposure to host-derived factors significantly alters the expression of key TonB-dependent receptor (TBDR) genes involved in iron uptake, including pirA, piuA, and bauA (Le et al., 2022). These TBDR-mediated siderophore-dependent iron acquisition functions are increasingly recognized as major entry routes for cefiderocol, thereby linking iron homeostasis to antibiotic susceptibility (Mezcord et al., 2023; Smoke et al., 2023; Van Delden et al., 2013; Yamano et al., 2022). This coordinated response highlights the dynamic regulation of siderophore-mediated iron acquisition systems under host-mimicking conditions.

Our recent study revealed that vitamin B12, an essential cofactor for both the host and bacterial cell functions (Martens et al., 2002), can antagonize cefiderocol activity across multiple CRAB and other Gram-negative pathogens. This interaction produces a characteristic kinetic pattern where early killing is followed by regrowth (Mezcord et al., 2026). We have observed that the resulting survivors remain resistant after passaging in antibiotic-free medium, consistent with the selection of stable, genetically encoded adaptive derivatives. Our first study supported a nutrient-drug interaction converging on TBD uptake pathways, since both iron and vitamin B12 acquisition rely on TBDR-dependent functions (Schauer et al., 2008). We initially identified a mutation in a predicted vitamin B12 outer-membrane receptor in Escherichia coli after exposure to host fluids. Follow-up functional analyses in A. baumannii suggested that vitamin B12 influences cefiderocol susceptibility and promotes the emergence of small-colony variants (SCVs)-like adaptive derivatives (Schauer et al., 2008). Unlike classic transient persisters, these SCVs-like adaptive derivatives represent genetically stable lineages with distinct genomic alterations that support survival under antibiotic stress. Transcriptomic profiling and complementary phenotypic assays further demonstrated coordinated remodeling of iron acquisition/transport programs and envelope-associated responses under host-mimicking conditions. Collectively, these findings support a model in which nutrient availability and TBDRs affect both siderophore-mediated physiology and cefiderocol entry (Mezcord et al., 2026). These findings reveal a previously unrecognized role of micronutrient availability in modulating antibiotic susceptibility and bacterial physiology, which prompted us to expand these novel observations.

Although SCVs have been described across different bacterial genera (Cao et al., 2017; Choi et al., 2024; Irvine et al., 2019), mainly in Staphylococcus aureus (Becker, 2023; Proctor et al., 1995; Proctor et al., 2006), SCV biology remains comparatively underexplored in A. baumannii, with limited mechanistic insight into the cellular processes that drive SCV formation and maintenance, highlighting a clear knowledge gap. In a chronic lung infection model, Pseudomonas aeruginosa SCVs were linked to a large chromosomal inversion accompanied by broad transcriptional remodeling that enhanced oxidative-stress and virulence programs (Irvine et al., 2019). In addition, SCV-like phenotypes were reported in E. coli harboring VIM- or NDM-type carbapenemases after serial cefiderocol passaging. Whole genome sequencing (WGS) of selected variants showed mutations in heme biosynthesis genes (hemB and hemH) and IS insertions disrupting tonB that collectively reduced cefiderocol susceptibility (Kocer et al., 2023). Similarly, Stracquadanio et al. (2022) reported that the exposure of A. baumannii to cefiderocol has been associated with regrowth and the emergence of heteroresistant subpopulations forming very tiny colonies at high antibiotic concentrations, highlighting adaptive responses that remain underexplored for this pathogen (Stracquadanio et al., 2022).

In this manuscript, we advance this work by defining the genomic and phenotypic traits of selected vitamin B12-associated SCVs and clarifying how vitamin B12 and cefiderocol jointly drive the emergence of SCV- like adaptive derivatives, adaptive survival behaviors, and collateral antibiotic response patterns. Using AB5075 and its vitamin B12-associated SCV1 derivative as a model, we connect genomic changes to a multifaceted phenotype that includes altered killing kinetics, collateral susceptibility profiles, and biofilm/capsule-associated traits. Targeted transcriptional responses of siderophore/TBDRs, envelope, and stress-associated genes were also investigated. Finally, we extended our observations to an additional CRAB strain, CDC-Bank #0033 CRAB (M27835), with a different genetic background to further evaluate the role of vitamin B12, alone and in combination with cefiderocol, as a driver of the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives.

Materials and methods

Bacterial strains

Selected vitamin B12-associated SCV-like adaptive derivatives from AB5075 and M27835 (CDC AR-Bank #0033) strains recovered after vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin, 100 mg/L) exposure were used. The corresponding parental strains were used as controls for comparison. A M27835 (CDC AR-Bank #0033)-derived survivor isolate (referred to here as SCV2) recovered after regrowth at 24 h in vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin, 100 mg/L) plus cefiderocol time-kill assays that was previously subjected to WGS, and comparative genomics (Mezcord et al., 2026) was used for expanded phenotypic characterization. In addition, seven independent M27835-derived SCV-like derivatives recovered after 24 h and 48 h regrowth during time-kill assays with cefiderocol plus vitamin B12 were included for WGS and comparative genomic analyses. Table S1 summarizes the characteristics of the strains used in this study.

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST)

Minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) were determined for aminoglycosides (amikacin (AK), gentamicin (GEN), plazomicin (PLZ), fluoroquinolones (ofloxacin (OFX), levofloxacin (LEV), carbapenems (imipenem (IMP), meropenem (MRP), and tetracycline-family agents (minocycline (MIN), tigecycline (TIG) using commercial gradient diffusion MIC strips (E-strips; Liofilchem S.r.l., Roseto degli Abruzzi, Italy) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. MICs were recorded in mg/L. The MICs of cefiderocol were determined using broth microdilution (BMD) assays with iron-depleted cation-adjusted Mueller-Hinton broth (ID-CAMHB) following Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) guidelines (CLSI, 2026). All procedures were performed according to CLSI and EUCAST guidelines (https://www.eucast.org/clinical_breakpoints) and results were interpreted using the most current breakpoints available. Quality control was performed using the CLSI/EUCAST-recommended reference strains E. coli ATCC 25,922 and Pseudomonas aeruginosa ATCC 27,853.

Static concentration time-kill assays

Static concentration time-kill assays were performed using the derived SCV-like variants to assess the bactericidal activity of cefiderocol (Mezcord et al., 2026). In addition, killing assays for amikacin were used to compare the tolerance to this antibiotic between the parental strains and the derived SCV-like derivatives. Exponential-phase cultures (∼10⁶ CFU/mL) were incubated at 37 °C with shaking in the corresponding test media containing cefiderocol at 2X, 4X, and 8X MIC (two double dilutions of the MIC value) for each strain. Samples were collected at 0, 2, 4, 6, and 24 h, serially diluted, and 50 μL of the appropriate bacterial dilution were spirally plated on Mueller-Hinton agar using an automated spiral plater (Don Whitley WASP Touch, Microbiology International, Frederick, MD, USA) and incubated at 37 °C. To determine viable counts, expressed as log₁₀ CFU/mL, bacteria were quantified using a ProtoCOL automated colony counter (Symbiosis, Cambridge, UK). Time-kill experiments were performed using three independent biological replicates. The limit of detection was 1.30 log₁₀ CFU/mL (20 CFU/mL).

Biofilm and capsule assays

Biofilm assays were performed using AB5075 and M27835 and their corresponding SCV-like derivatives, as previously described (Luu et al., 2025). Briefly, cells were cultured in LB broth and incubated under static conditions at 37 °C for 24–48 h. All experiments were conducted in triplicate, with at least three technical replicates per biological replicate. The optical density of the biofilm biomass was measured at 592 nm following staining with crystal violet. Total biomass was determined using the OD₆₀₀ value prior to washing, and the results were expressed as the ratio of biofilm biomass to total biomass (OD592/OD₆₀₀). Capsule production was determined in the same strains using a density gradient–based assay as previously described (Luu et al., 2025; Valcek et al., 2023). Overnight cultures were pelleted, resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), mixed with Ludox LS colloidal silica (30%wt/wt; Merck), and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 30 min. Capsule abundance was estimated from the position of the bacterial band within the gradient. Cells with a thicker capsule have a lower density and are therefore located higher up the gradient. Experiments were performed using three independent biological replicates, each analyzed in technical triplicate. Band size and density were analyzed to identify immediately visible changes in the isolates, and results were documented through photographic records.

Scanning electron microscopy

The cell morphology and cellular structure of AB5075, AB5075 SCV1, M27835 and its SCV -like derivatives SCV1 and SCV2 (Mut1 and Mut15) were examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) as previously described (Rodriguez et al., 2025). Briefly, a standardized inoculum was prepared from overnight cultures. Cells were harvested, washed, and fixed in 2% glutaraldehyde, then mounted onto protamine-coated glass slides. Samples were dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, critical point dried, and imaged by SEM using a JCM-7000 NeoScope™.

Resazurin reduction assay

The resazurin reduction assay was used to assess the metabolic activity of the M27835 parental strain and the corresponding SCV-like derivatives recovered after vitamin B12 exposure, including SCV1 (Mut1) and SCV2 (Mut15) as previously described (Mezcord et al., 2026). Standardized inocula were prepared from overnight cultures and aliquots of each suspension were dispensed into sterile 96-well plates, followed by the addition of resazurin to the test wells (growth controls received no resazurin) to a final volume of 100 μL per well. Negative controls consisted of medium with and without resazurin. Plates were incubated at 37 °C for 2 h in the dark, and metabolic activity was quantified using a BioTek Synergy H1 microplate reader (Agilent). Experiments were performed in independent triplicates under reduced light conditions due to resazurin photosensitivity. Statistical significance was determined by the Student’s t-test.

RNA extraction and quantitative RT-qPCR

The A. baumannii AB5075 and M27835 parental strains and their corresponding SCV-like derivative were cultured in LB broth with agitation for 18 h at 37 °C. Overnight cultures were then diluted 1:10 into fresh LB broth and incubated with agitation for 18 h at 37 °C. The Direct-zol RNA Kit (Zymo Research, Irvine, CA, USA) was used for RNA extraction from three independent culture samples. cDNA was prepared using the iScript Reverse Transcription Supermix (BioRad, Hercules, CA, USA). Quantitative PCR was performed using qPCRBIO SyGreen Blue Mix Lo-ROX (PCR Biosystems LTD, London, UK) per manufacturer’s recommendations. The expression of selected iron uptake and antibiotic resistance genes, as well as biofilm- and metabolism-associated genes were analyzed (Table S2). The results were analyzed using the ΔΔCt method (Hellemans et al., 2007) using rpoB as the reference gene. Three independent biological replicates were analyzed, and each sample was evaluated in technical triplicate. Statistical differences were determined by two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's multiple comparison test using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad software version 10.0.0, San Diego, CA, USA).

Whole-genome sequencing

Genomic DNA from the seven M27835-derived SCV-like derivatives recovered after 24 or 48 h exposure to vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin, 100 mg/L) and cefiderocol was extracted using the Wizard® Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). Whole-genome sequencing was performed using the NovaSeq X Plus platform (Illumina), to generate 2 × 150 bp paired-end reads. Raw sequencing quality was assessed with FASTQC (https://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc/), and adapter trimming and quality filtering were performed using Trimmomatic (v0.40; parameters: ILLUMINACLIP: TruSeq3-PE.fa:2:30:10; LEADING:3; TRAILING:3; SLIDINGWINDOW:4:15; MINLEN:36) (Bolger et al., 2014). SPAdes (v3.15.4, default parameters) (Bankevich et al., 2012) was used for de novo genome assembly, and assemblies were evaluated using QUAST (v5.2.0) (Gurevich et al., 2013). Genome annotation was performed using PROKKA (v1.14.5) (Seemann, 2014), and single-nucleotide variant (SNV) analysis was done with breseq (v0.38.1) in consensus mode (Deatherage and Barrick, 2014). Comparative genomic analyses of the SCV -like derivatives versus the parental strain, were used to detect mutations and other genomic changes potentially associated with the observed phenotypes. All sequencing data, including FASTQ files, assembled genomes, and annotations, have been deposited in Zenodo(https://zenodo.org/records/18663719, last accessed February 16, 2026).

Molecular structural analysis

The structural relationships of A. baumannii BfmS proteins were determined using ColabFold v1.5.5: AlphaFold2 using MMseqs2 69. Visual molecular dynamics was used to verify that protein receptors did not include water molecules (Humphrey et al., 1996). The BfmS WT and BfmS T245A structural alignments were performed using the TM-align method within the Pairwise Structure Alignment tool of the RCSB protein Data Bank 71. RMSD and TM-align scores were recorded to evaluate structural similarity 71.

Statistical analyses

The Student’s t-test or two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparison test, both provided as part of the GraphPad Prism (GraphPad software, San Diego, CA, USA), were used to analyze the significance of quantitative data, as appropriate for the data set. Statical significance for all analyses was set a priori at P< 0.05.

Results

Vitamin B12 promotes the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives and alters cefiderocol bactericidal activity against CRAB strains

Our recent report showed that exposure to vitamin B12 triggers a broad adaptive response in CRAB isolates that is consistent with reduced cefiderocol bactericidal activity and the emergence of adapted subpopulations, including derivatives with increased cefiderocol MICs. Transcriptomic analysis of AB5075 exposed to vitamin B12 revealed an extensive metabolic reprogramming characterized by repression of energy-production pathways and decreased metabolic activity, a physiological state consistent with the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives. In parallel, vitamin B12 exposure modulated stress responses and efflux/transport-associated genes, induced the csu pili gene cluster, that resulted in increased biofilm formation in this strain (Mezcord et al., 2026).

To further evaluate the effect of vitamin B12 as a driver of SCV-like adaptive derivatives associated with reduced cefiderocol bactericidal activity, we performed static concentration time-kill assays using three cefiderocol concentrations (2X, 4X, and 8X MIC) against the AB5075 SCV1 derivative. As shown in Fig. 1A, exposure to cefiderocol at 2X MIC produced only a modest and transient reduction in viability, followed by regrowth at 24 h (log10 fold-change of −0.37). A similar pattern was observed at 4X MIC, where early killing was slightly enhanced but bacterial counts recovered by 24 h (log10 fold-change of −0.34). In contrast, 8X MIC produced a more sustained reduction in bacterial burden, reaching a − 4.26 log10 fold-change at 24 h (Fig. 1A). Despite this increased effect, complete eradication of the bacteria was not achieved, and viable counts remained detectable throughout the trial, indicating reduced bactericidal activity and survival of adaptated subpopulations even at the highest cefiderocol concentration of tested.

To determine whether vitamin B12 similarly promotes SCV emergence in genetically distinct CRAB backgrounds, the CDC Bank strain #0033, named here as M27835, was exposed to vitamin B12, resulting in the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives. A randomly selected SCV-like adaptive derivative (M27835 SCV1) was picked for further studies (Fig. S1). As shown in Fig. 1B, exposure to cefiderocol caused a similar response to all three cefiderocol tested concentrations; an initial reduction in viable bacteria count during the first hours of treatment, followed by regrowth at 24 h. By the end of the time-kill assays, the bacterial burden approached that of the growth control, indicating reduced bactericidal activity and a predominantly bacteriostatic response. This adaptive survival phenotype is similar to that observed for AB5075 SCV1 and supports the idea that vitamin B12-associated emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives contributes to reduced cefiderocol bactericidal activity across different CRAB genetic backgrounds.

CRAB SCV-like adaptive derivatives exhibit genetic and transcriptional remodeling associated with altered antibiotic responses, envelope remodeling, and biofilm-associated traits

To define the genetic basis and functional consequences of the vitamin B12-associated SCV-like adaptive phenotype, we performed whole-genome sequencing (WGS) of AB5075 SCV1, which was originally isolated following vitamin B12 exposure, as we recently reported (Mezcord et al., 2026). Comparative genomics between the parental strain and the SCV1 derivative identified discrete genetic changes in pathways plausibly associated with altered cell growth state and envelope physiology. These alterations included a point mutation in wecC (UDP-N-acetyl-d-mannosamine dehydrogenase), a C-terminal truncation of AroP (aromatic amino acid transport protein), and a large deletion encompassing tetA together with an adjacent intergenic region upstream of a predicted FAD-dependent oxidoreductase (Fig. 2A; Table 1, Table S3). Phenotypic susceptibility testing revealed a modest decrease in tetracycline susceptibility in AB5075 SCV1 (MIC 1.5 mg/L vs 0.75 mg/L, with intracolonies noted), relative to the parental AB5075 population. This MIC difference occurred despite the large deletion including tetA, suggesting that the altered tetracycline resistance phenotype is unlikely to be driven by canonical TetA efflux activity alone and may instead reflect SCV-linked physiological changes that broadly influence antibiotic activity. Consistent with AroP’s role as a general aromatic amino acid permease, loss of its terminal residues may contribute to reduced nutrient import and growth attenuation, potentially reinforcing SCV-associated physiology (Cosgriff and Pittard, 1997).

We next assessed whether the AB5075 SCV1 genotype corresponded to altered antibiotic responses and adaptive survival phenotypes. In amikacin static time-kill assays (10X MIC), the parental AB5075 population showed a pronounced early reduction in viability, whereas SCV1 displayed markedly reduced killing and prolonged survival during the assay (Fig. 2B). At 24 h, exposure of AB5075 to amikacin resulted in a fold-change of −1.67 log₁₀ in CFU/mL relative to the growth control, indicating borderline antibacterial activity. In contrast, AB5075 SCV1 showed no reduction in viability relative to the parental growth control (+0.04 log₁₀ fold-change), consistent with reduced aminoglycoside susceptibility and adaptive survival. Notably, treatment of SCV1 with amikacin resulted in a moderate decrease in bacterial count (Fig. 2B, −0.56 log₁₀ fold-change), further supporting the reduced susceptibility of the SCV population to aminoglycoside-mediated killing. Collateral susceptibility screening using gradient strips was performed to compare antimicrobial susceptibility between AB5075 and its SCV1 derivative (Table 2). Overall, AB5075 SCV1 largely retained the parental MIC profile across the tested antibiotics. The most notable change was a 4-fold decrease in gentamicin MIC (from 192 mg/L to 48 mg/L), whereas amikacin and plazomicin MICs were unchanged (192 mg/L and 4 mg/L, respectively). Carbapenem MICs showed non-significant variation (imipenem from 16 mg/L to 24 mg/L; meropenem, from 32 mg/L to 24 mg/L). Fluoroquinolone and tigecycline MICs were identical in the parental and SCV1 strains, while minocycline increased 2-fold (from 0.094 mg/L to 0.19 mg/L) in the SCV1 (Table 2).

Surface-associated adaptive traits relevant to persistence, such as biofilm formation and capsule production, were overall comparable between AB5075 and AB5075 SCV1; although SCV1 showed a tendency toward increased biofilm formation, this difference was not statistically significant (Fig. 2C), while low capsulation was clearly seen in the AB5075 SCV1 derivative (Fig. 2D). The wecC mutation seen in the AB5075 SCV1 may contribute to the reduced capsulation phenotype, as WecC contributes to the biosynthesis of UDP-N-acetylmannosaminuronic acid precursors required for surface polysaccharide production. Disruption of this pathway can limit polysaccharide assembly and thereby decrease capsule abundance in AB5075 SCV1.

To further investigate whether the SCV1 genotype was associated with transcriptional remodeling of pathways linked to adaptive survival and antibiotic response, we performed qRT-PCR analysis of selected genes involved in iron acquisition, antibiotic resistance, surface structures, and energy metabolism. In agreement with an altered metabolic and nutrient acquisition state, AB5075 SCV1 exhibited a marked downregulation of multiple iron uptake systems. Transcripts encoding siderophore receptors and iron transport components, including piuA, piuC, pirA, cirA, bauA, basA, basB, barA, and barB, were significantly reduced in SCV1 compared to the AB5075 parental strain (Fig. 2E). In contrast, expression of btuB3, encoding a predicted outer membrane transporter associated with vitamin B12 uptake, was significantly increased in SCV1, consistent with adaptation under vitamin B12 exposure. In parallel, genes associated with antibiotic resistance and envelope-associated efflux were transcriptionally upregulated in the AB5075 SCV1. Notably, expression of blaOXA-23, as well as the efflux-related genes ace1 and amvA, was significantly increased relative to AB5075 (Fig. 2F). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis comparing AB5075 and the AB5075 SCV1 derivative revealed clear morphological differences between strains (Fig. 2G). SCV1 cells appeared consistently smaller than the parental AB5075 population, in agreement with the reduced-growth phenotype characteristic of SCV-like adaptive derivatives. In contrast, the parental strain frequently exhibited elongated or chain-like structures, which likely reflected incomplete cell separation during active division, a feature that was largely absent in SCV1. Consistent with the comparable biofilm quantification results, SEM imaging did not reveal obvious pili-like surface structures in either strain, which may explain the absence of significant differences in biofilm formation between AB5075 and SCV1. Analysis of additional persistence and physiology-associated pathways revealed further SCV-associated transcriptional shifts (Fig. S2). Several genes encoding subunits of the ATP synthase (atpA, atpB, atpD, and atpF) were significantly upregulated (Fig. S2A). Within the NADH dehydrogenase I complex, nuoL and nuoM transcripts were significantly increased in both strains, whereas nuoH showed no detectable difference between strains (Fig. S2B).

The WGS analysis of the M27835 SCV1 derivative, which emerged following B12 exposure, revealed a discrete mutational signature that included a lesion affecting the MrpA/MnhA monovalent cation/H+ antiporter subunit, a component of the multi-subunit Mrp system implicated in Na+/H+ exchange, pH homeostasis, and energetic balance. Because disruption of ion homeostasis and membrane energetics is a recognized route to low-growth adaptive phenotypes and reduced bactericidal susceptibility 30, the detected genomic changes (Fig. 3A; Table 1, Table S3) provide a plausible basis for stabilization of the SCV-like adaptive phenotype. Static concentration time-kill assays with amikacin (16 mg/L) showed that the parental M27835 strain exhibited a pronounced and rapid decrease in viability, consistent with a strong bactericidal effect (Fig. 3B). At 24 h, this effect corresponded to a − 4.62 log₁₀ fold-change in CFU/mL relative to the growth control. In contrast, the M27835 SCV1 derivative showed a markedly attenuated killing profile, maintaining CFU levels close to those of the untreated parental strain. At 24 h, SCV1 showed only a modest reduction in viability (−0.67 log₁₀ fold-change), consistent with reduced bactericidal activity and adaptive survival under antibiotic exposure (Fig. 3B). Notably, although SCV1 exposed to amikacin initially displayed a decrease in bacterial counts, partial regrowth was observed by 24 h, resulting in a final reduction of −1.98 log₁₀ relative to the untreated M27835 control, further supporting its reduced susceptibility to aminoglycoside-mediated killing (Fig. 3B). Consistent with reduced aminoglycoside susceptibility, M27835 SCV1 exhibited an increased amikacin MIC and intracolony heterogeneity, while MICs for other antibiotic families remained largely unchanged (Table 2; Fig. S3).

Linked to the SCV-phenotype, the SCV1 M27835 derivative displayed a significant increase in biofilm formation compared to the parental strain (Fig. 3C). These cell surface structures are consistent with enhanced attachment potential and provide a plausible morphological correlation to increased biofilm formation. M27835 SCV1 displayed a diffuse capsule architecture, suggesting alterations in capsule composition or packing rather than capsule synthesis (Fig. 3D). SEM imaging revealed discernible differences in cellular organization, the presence of pili-like appendages, and increased cell-cell aggregation relative to the parental M27835 background (Fig. 3E). Resazurin-based metabolic activity under the tested conditions did not show significant differences between the wild-type and its isogenic SCV1 derivative (Fig. 3F).

Transcriptional analysis by RT-qPCR was performed to determine whether the M27835 SCV1 derivative exhibited coordinated changes in pathways linked to siderophore/TonB-related functions, envelope, stress-response, and energy metabolism (Fig. 3G and Fig. S2D-F). In contrast to AB5075 SCV1, the M27835 SCV1 variant displayed a selective upregulation of several iron acquisition genes. Transcripts corresponding to piuA, pirA, bauA, and basA were significantly increased in the SCV1 background relative to the parental strain (Fig. 3G). On the other hand, expression of btuB3 was significantly reduced in M27835 SCV1. No significant differences were observed for cirA, basB, barA, or barB between these two strains. As in the AB5075 SCV1, genes encoding major resistance determinants were transcriptionally upregulated in the M27835 SCV1. Expression of blaNDM-1, as well as the efflux-associated genes amvA and ace1, was significantly increased in M27835 SCV1 compared to the parental isolate (Fig. 3). Additional SCV-associated transcriptional changes were detected in pathways related to energy metabolism (Fig. S2). Accordingly, atpA, atpB, and atpD showed significantly higher expression in M27835 SCV1 (Fig. S2C). Within the NADH dehydrogenase I complex, nuoL transcription was also significantly upregulated (Fig. S2D).

Together, these data indicate that both AB5075 SCV1 and M27835 SCV1 derivatives carry stable genetic changes associated with adaptive phenotypic features associated with reduced bactericidal activity, including altered aminoglycoside killing dynamics and/or antibiotic susceptibility patterns, modest changes in biofilm formation and capsulation, as well as envelope and transcriptional alterations. These observations support the conclusion that vitamin B12 exposure can select SCV-like adaptive derivatives with clinically relevant antibiotic response profiles across distinct CRAB genetic backgrounds.

Vitamin B12 and cefiderocol co-drive the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives with diverse resistance and phenotypic profiles

Population analysis and static concentration time-kill assays conducted with bacteria cultured in the presence of cefiderocol and vitamin B12 showed the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives with increased cefiderocol resistance compared to the outcome obtained with bacteria cultured only in the presence of cefiderocol. Our previous work reported a survivor colony randomly selected (M27835 SCV2, referred to as Mut15), which regrew at 24 h in cefiderocol plus vitamin B12 time-kill assays. This derivative that carried a total of 12 mutations, including an 18-nt in-frame deletion in the pirA TBDR coding gene, and a nonsense mutation in panB (Y23; TAC→TAA), which is consistent with a pantothenate/CoA-linked metabolic bottleneck that can reinforce SCV-like growth, was used for further studies (Mezcord et al., 2026).

Phenotypic characterization showed that M27835 SCV2 (Mut15) showed no significant difference in biofilm formation compared to the parental M27835 strain (Fig. S3A), but resazurin assays revealed altered metabolic activity (Fig. S3B). Consistent with delayed growth, M27835 SCV2 showed a reduced OD600 at the early time point evaluated (2 h; Fig. S3C). SEM imaging revealed discernible differences in cell morphology and organization between M27835 and M27835 SCV2 (Fig. S3D); the small-colony morphology characteristic of the SCV-like adaptive derivative was evident on agar plates (Fig. S3E). Lastly, amikacin gradient strip testing indicated a slight increase in the MIC for M27835 SCV2 (Mut15) relative to the parental strain (Fig. S3F). Notably, despite this limited MIC shift, the SCV2 showed a distinct growth phenotype, with a more mucoid/viscous appearance compared to the parental strain M27835 (Fig. S3E-F), suggesting a qualitative phenotypic change that MIC measurements do not fully reflect (data not shown).

To determine whether these mutations observed in M27835 SCV2 (Mut15) were representative of SCV-like adaptive derivatives arising under combined exposure to vitamin B12 and cefiderocol, seven additional recovered SCV-like derivatives were analyzed (Table S1 and Figure S4). Across these regrowth-derived isolates (M27835 SCV3–SCV9, Mut16–23), cefiderocol MICs ranged from 16 mg/L to 512 mg/L, except for the highly resistant SCV2 (Mut15; 1024 mg/L), highlighting substantial heterogeneity among SCV-like derivatives recovered from the same combined-exposure context. WGS was performed on randomly selected isolates of the M27835 SCV3–SCV9 derivatives, and the resulting genomic analysis was compared to both the parental M27835 background and to SCV2 (Mut15). Among the sequenced derivatives, several isolates displayed elevated cefiderocol MICs relative to M27835 (4–8 mg/L), including SCV6 (Mut20; 512 mg/L), SCV7 (Mut21; 256 mg/L), SCV8 (Mut22; 64 mg/L), and SCV9 (Mut23; 128 mg/L), whereas SCV3 (Mut16; 16 mg/L), SCV4 (Mut17; 32 mg/L), and SCV5 (Mut19; 16 mg/L) showed more modest MIC increases. Our comparative analysis also revealed that mutations in these additional SCV derivatives differed from those in SCV2 and did not correspond to previously reported mutations associated with cefiderocol resistance, except for SCV7, suggesting alternative or indirect mechanisms that may contribute to the SCV-like adaptive phenotype and elevated cefiderocol MICs. Among these variants, SCV3 carried a mutation in putA (bifunctional proline dehydrogenase/L-glutamate-γ-semialdehyde dehydrogenase), potentially associated with alterations in central metabolism previously linked to SCV-like adaptive states. SCV4 included a mutation in the vicinity of nuoA and a gene coding for a GGDEF-domain diguanylate cyclase. The promoter prediction suggested that this change lies close to, but not directly within, a predicted promoter element (∼14 bp upstream of nuoA), raising the possibility of altered transcriptional control for respiratory and/or c-di-GMP-linked functions. SCV8 exhibited a deletion in a locus encompassing genes including pheS, pheT, ihfA, rho, and additional annotated features, indicating that larger structural variation also emerged among recovered derivatives.

BfmS mutation is associated with cefiderocol adaptive remodeling in the SCV-like adaptive derivative SCV7

The M27835 SCV7 derivative (Mut21) harbored a missense mutation within bfmS (Fig. 4A), encoding the sensor kinase of the BfmRS two-component system (Tomaras et al., 2008). Recent in vitro selection studies have also reported mutations in bfmS among cefiderocol-resistant A. baumannii mutants, supporting the hypothesis that bfmS may contribute to cefiderocol adaptive resistance (Raro et al., 2025). Consistent with this bfmS-mediated regulatory remodeling, the SCV7 (Mut21) exhibited increased cefiderocol MIC (Table S1). To contextualize the potential downstream impact of this mutation, we used a STRING-based functional association network for BfmS (A1S_0749), which predicts an association to its response regulator BfmR (A1S_0748; score 0.994) and additional regulators and envelope/surface-linked proteins, such as AdeR (A1S_1753; score 0.839) and a twitching motility protein (A1S_2814; score 0.816) (Fig. 4B). In the SCV7 (Mut21), the observed amino-acid substitution (T245A) maps to a hydrophobic domain consistent with a transmembrane region in the 549-aa BfmS protein. This point mutation represents a candidate mechanism that may modulate the membrane-sensing/signaling output of the BfmRS system (Fig. 4C). To assess the structural impact of this substitution further, we modeled BfmS and compared the predicted wild-type and T245A structures. The models showed a high overall structural agreement (TM-score = 0.97; RMSD = 0.95) and no obvious perturbation of the transmembrane a-helical region (Fig. 4C). In contrast, local conformational analysis using a 15 amino acid window flanking the substituted residue revealed a notable shift in torsion angle from 84.65° (WT) to 106.54° (T245A), suggesting a subtle but potentially functionally relevant change in transmembrane helix orientation. Although these computational and genomic observations suggest that the BfmS mutation may contribute to the SCV7 phenotype, comprehensive functional validation through genetic reconstruction will be required to definitively establish causality. Given that mutations in the BfmRS two-component system have previously been linked to broad alterations in antimicrobial susceptibility and envelope-associated phenotypes (Marr et al., 2020; Palethorpe et al., 2022), we evaluated whether the M27835 SCV7 (Mut21) exhibited collateral resistance to other classes of antibiotics. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing using the gradient strips revealed that SCV7 had increased MICs for several compounds compared to the parental M27835 strain, including aminoglycosides (amikacin and gentamicin), while maintaining comparable susceptibility to others (Table S4 and Fig. S5A). Static concentration time-kill assays with 16 mg/L amikacin showed distinct killing dynamics between the parental M27835 strain and the SCV7 (Fig. S5B). In the presence of amikacin, M27835 exhibited a pronounced reduction in viability over time, reaching a log₁₀ fold change of −4.62 at 24 h relative to the growth control, consistent with sustained antibacterial activity. In contrast, the SCV7 derivative displayed reduced killing and enhanced survival over the course of the assay, with a log₁₀ fold change of −1.34 at 24 h, indicating a diminished bactericidal effect compared to the parental strain. These results indicate that the bfmS-associated SCV derivative exhibits a broader reduced-antibiotic susceptibility phenotype beyond cefiderocol. In parallel, we observed that the SCV7 exhibited a mucoid-like colony morphology, a feature not detected in the wild-type strain, suggesting additional envelope-associated phenotypic alterations (Fig. S5A). Biofilm formation was assessed using a crystal violet staining assay (OD₅9₂), normalized to bacterial growth (OD₆₀₀), revealing that SCV7 formed significantly more biofilm than the wild-type strain, with an approximately 7-fold increase (Fig. S5C). SEM images indicated a predominantly rounded (cocci), smaller-cell SCV7 morphology relative to the wild-type (Fig. S5D). No differences in capsule production were detected between the SCV7 derivative and the wild-type strain (data not shown). In addition, static concentration time-kill assays were performed using two concentrations of cefiderocol to compare the response of M27835 and its SCV7 derivative. At 16 μg/mL, both strains showed a comparable killing profile, with CFU counts decreasing from ∼6.5 log₁₀ CFU/mL at 0 h to ∼4.0–4.5 log₁₀ CFU/mL after 24 h (Fig. S6A). In contrast, at 64 μg/mL, cefiderocol exerted marked bactericidal activity against the parental M27835 strain, reducing viable counts to below the limit of detection at 24 h, whereas SCV7 displayed enhanced survival, maintaining ∼2.5–3.0 log₁₀ CFU/mL throughout the assay (Fig. S6B).

In contrast to the observations collected with SCV7, the comparative analysis of the SCV5 (Mut19), SCV6 (Mut20), and SCV9 (Mut23) derivatives did not reveal clear candidate mutations accounting for elevated cefiderocol MICs. This outcome suggests that elevated cefiderocol MICs in these isolates may be associated with phenotypic adaptation, metabolic changes, or complex transcriptional responses rather than a single clearly identifiable genetic determinant, through mechanisms to be explored in future studies.

Discussion

Our findings demonstrate that vitamin B12, a nutritional supplement commonly administered to ICU patients (Romain et al., 2016; Vosouqi et al., 2023), acts as a selective modulator of CRAB that can shift the population structure toward survivor lineages represented as SCV-like adaptive derivatives, both with and without cefiderocol exposure. Although the vitamin B12 concentrations used in this study exceed normal physiological serum levels, they were selected to model supraphysiological exposure conditions that may occur during high-dose parenteral vitamin B12 administration in critically ill patients. Notably, SCV-like phenotypes have been associated with genome remodeling and adaptative transcriptional changes linked to survival under stress in other bacterial species, including P. aeruginosa (Irvine et al., 2019). In addition, SCV-like phenotypes have also been reported after cefiderocol exposure in Enterobacterales, where variants carrying insertions in tonB and mutations in heme biosynthesis genes, as well as mutations impairing oxidative respiration and iron acquisition pathways, have been identified (Kocer et al., 2023; Mezcord et al., 2026) (Gao et al., 2022).

Mechanistically cefiderocol relies on TBD iron-uptake receptors to access the periplasmic space; consequently, its activity is affected by changes in the expression of active iron-uptake systems (Egge et al., 2024). We previously showed that vitamin B12, which also uses TBDRs to enter the bacterial cell, triggers transcriptional changes, promotes the emergence of SCV -like adaptive derivatives, and reduces cefiderocol susceptibility in A. baumannii (Mezcord et al., 2026). Vitamin B12 exposure progressively reduced cefiderocol bactericidal activity in PAP assays and promoted a shift toward heteroresistant subpopulations characterized by small colony size, survival at high cefiderocol concentrations, and an apparent transition toward stable resistance at the highest vitamin B12 dose tested (Mezcord et al., 2026). Together, these findings support a model in which vitamin B12 may affect or compete with cefiderocol for entry pathways, partially reducing cefiderocol activity and promoting survival of subpopulations under antibiotic exposure. This favorable environment could facilitate and accelerate the selection of cefiderocol-adapted derivatives under antibiotic pressure. This result is consistent with the known clinical concern that A. baumannii frequently develops multidrug-resistant phenotypes, supported by genomic plasticity and subpopulation dynamics that can undermine standard antimicrobial susceptibility methods used in the routine clinical laboratory (Li et al., 2025; Stracquadanio et al., 2022). In the context of cefiderocol activity, heteroresistance has been proposed as a contributor to therapeutic failure in A. baumannii infections, with reported post-exposure regrowth, colony/cell shape changes, and phenotypic heterogeneity (Manzanares and Hardy, 2010; Shields et al., 2024; Stracquadanio et al., 2022).

A central observation of this report is that B12-associated SCV-like adaptive derivatives in A. baumannii exhibit an adaptive survival phenotype during cefiderocol exposure, characterized by early reductions in viability followed by stabilization and/or regrowth. This behavior reflects previous observations in which exposure to cefiderocol caused regrowth after treatment, particularly at higher cefiderocol concentrations, accompanied by the appearance of very small colonies (Mezcord et al., 2026; Stracquadanio et al., 2022).

Importantly, this report shows that SCV selection outcomes differ depending on the selective pressure applied. While exposure to vitamin B12 alone favors the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives associated with survival under stress, the combined presence of vitamin B12 and cefiderocol accelerates the selection of derivatives carrying genetic and regulatory alterations that directly impact siderophore-mediated drug entry and are more consistent with increased cefiderocol MIC in some variants. The SCV-like derivatives characterized here support the multifactorial nature of these adaptive phenotypes, involving slow growth, altered envelope and surface traits, and changes in antimicrobial killing rather than uniform shifts in MIC values to several antibiotics, particularly aminoglycosides. Across bacteria, SCVs frequently map to pathways that modulate energetic state, respiration, and iron metabolism; for example, SCV Enterobacterales clinical isolates have been linked to inactivation of oxidative respiration and iron acquisition pathways (Gao et al., 2022).

Importantly, the genomic and phenotypic heterogeneity observed among the independently recovered SCV derivatives supports the idea that these variants do not emerge through a single conserved adaptive pathway. Instead, the combined exposure to vitamin B12 and cefiderocol appears to promote multiple independent adaptive trajectories involving distinct combinations of mutations, regulatory remodeling, metabolic adaptations, and envelope-associated changes. This heterogeneity likely reflects the multifactorial nature of adaptive survival under antibiotic stress.

All the observations listed above prompted us to study in more detail the effects of cefiderocol and vitamin B12 on the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives in two genetically different CRAB strains. At the genotype level, the AB5075 SCV1 derivative harbors discrete mutations linked to surface polysaccharide biology and nutrient acquisition (wecC and truncation of aroP), in addition to a larger deletion encompassing tetA. On the other hand, the WGS analysis of the M27835 SCV (Mut1) derivative showed a considerable number of SNPs in transposase-associated genes. Transposons and insertion sequences (IS) are well-recognized for their capacity to shape A. baumannii antibiotic resistance and genomic plasticity (Fournier et al., 2006). Thus, it is possible that vitamin B12, with or without exposure to cefiderocol, promotes conditions of metabolic and cell envelope stress that may facilitate IS-mediated genomic variations and adaptive remodeling in A. baumannii. Such genomic plasticity has been widely associated with antibiotic adaptation and the emergence of resistance in this species (Figueiredo et al., 2009; Kocer et al., 2023; Mugnier et al., 2009). The genomic analysis of the M27835 SCV1 (Mut1) derivative also identified a mutation in the gene coding for the MrpA/MnhA monovalent cation/H+ antiporter subunit, implicating an alteration of ion homeostasis and membrane energetics that can be associated with low-growth adaptive survival states (Hubloher et al., 2022). The M27835 SCV2 derivative harbors a pirA deletion, showing recurrence when selecting cefiderocol resistance mutants with truncated or reduced expression of iron-uptake functions (Mezcord et al., 2026).

Phenotypically, AB5075 SCV1 showed altered capsule production, consistent with a role for WecC-dependent precursors in polysaccharide biogenesis (Singh et al., 2018), supporting the interpretation that SCV1 surface remodeling could influence both host interactions and antibiotic physiology. In contrast, the M27835 SCV1 derivative presented a more diffuse capsule, increased biofilm formation, and SEM evidence of surface remodeling, including pili-like appendages, increased cell aggregation, and responses that could correlate with enhanced adherence and survival under stress conditions. The M27835 SCV1 derivative also displayed delayed early biomass accumulation, morphological differences, and a slightly increased amikacin MIC, accompanied by a visibly more viscous/mucoid growth appearance when compared with the AB5075 SCV1 derivative. Notably, these phenotypic adaptations were primarily reflected in altered killing dynamics and post-exposure regrowth rather than uniform shifts in MIC values, highlighting altered killing dynamics and adaptative survival as a key component of the SCV phenotype that may precede and facilitate the emergence of resistance. Taken together, these observations support the idea that SCV-like adaptive derivatives can represent a structural diverse phenotypic state, with remodeled SCV-like phenotypes in which envelope and surface architecture are part of stress-associated adaptive remodeling (Malone, 2015). This adaptation not only complicates the interpretation of standard susceptibility endpoints but may also contribute to reduced antibiotic efficacy during treatment.

The transcriptional data showed that SCV-like adaptive derivatives have a coordinated, but strain-dependent, remodeling of nutrient uptake and envelope defense programs. AB5075 SCV1 displayed broad downregulation of iron uptake/acinetobactin-associated genes, consistent with reduced use of ferric siderophore entry systems, in addition to the upregulation of adaptive determinants and energy-associated genes. In contrast, M27835 SCV1 showed selective upregulation of key iron acquisition genes and no significant differences in the upregulation of blaNDM-1, amvA, and ace1, when compared to the AB5075 SCV1 responses. Overall, the transcriptional changes observed in SCV derivatives support a multifactorial and strain-specific adaptive state associated with survival under antibiotic stress. These changes involved genes associated with envelope structure, uptake systems, efflux pumps, and resistance determinants. These observations are in agreement with recent reports linking cefiderocol resistance to mutations affecting iron acquisition functions, including changes in pirA, piuA, and tonB, as well as wider envelope and target-site adaptations (Escalante et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2024; Klein et al., 2021; Malik et al., 2020; Poirel et al., 2021). Stracquadanio et al. showed tonB3 mutations across multiple A. baumannii strains and highlighted the broader role of TonB systems in energizing transport of iron-siderophore complexes (Stracquadanio et al., 2022). Kocer et al. demonstrated that serial cefiderocol exposure in VIM- or NDM-producing E. coli selects for reduced colony-size phenotypes driven by transposable element insertions disrupting tonB and altering the TonB binding site (Kocer et al., 2023). Finally, the analysis of seven M27835 SCV-derivatives (SCV3-SCV9) showed the emergence of a BfmS allele with a T245A amino acid substitution in the SCV7 derivative recovered after B12 plus cefiderocol exposure. Structural considerations support a model in which an amino acid change that maps within a BfmS transmembrane domain, alters signaling output through local tuning rather than a significant protein destabilization. In two-component sensor kinases, modest changes in helix packing, rotation, or angle can be sufficient to shift the kinase-phosphatase balance and thereby shifting downstream transcriptional programs, without requiring detectable disruption of the overall helical architecture (Xu and Zhang, 2010; Zhang and Skolnick, 2005). The T245A substitution provides a plausible mechanistic link between SCV-associated adaptive physiology and cefiderocol response, where BfmRS signaling, which was initially associated with biofilm formation, could promote envelope and adaptive survival -associated traits while indirectly impacting iron-uptake receptor programs that are needed for cefiderocol entry (Geisinger et al., 2018; Tomaras et al., 2008). Interestingly, a recent in vitro selection work in A. baumannii demonstrated that mutations in the global regulators bfmS and oxyR indirectly promotes cefiderocol resistance by downregulating pirA and piuA expression (Raro et al., 2025). Taken together our data and that recently published show that resistance to cefiderocol can emerge through both the impairment of functions directly associated with cefiderocol activity as well as global regulatory changes that could involve a cascade of interacting regulatory functions. However, it is important to recognize that, in the absence of an isogenic mutant reconstruction, the BfmS T245A substitution remains a candidate mechanism, and more thorough functional validation is required to definitively establish its causal role in the SCV7 phenotype. In this context, SCV formation in A. baumannii represents anadaptive state associated with genetic alterations and phenotypic remodeling. While these variants share certain phenotypic features with classical persister-associated states, such as reduced growth, their behavior under continued antibiotic exposure and the presence of genetic changes suggest they reflected selected and genetically altered subpopulations rather than classical transient persister populations. These features support survival under antibiotic stress and may increase the likelihood of subsequent selection of high-level resistance during continued cefiderocol exposure.

Conclusion

Our work shows that the co-occurrence of SCV-like adaptive phenotypes behavior, surface remodeling (pili-like structures, aggregation, altered capsule presentation), and resistance/efflux transcriptional upregulation is consistent with the emergence of SCV-like adaptive derivatives through a combination of (i) mutations affecting cellular functions associated with cefiderocol activity, and (ii) regulatory changes that reshape envelope physiology under combined nutrient and antibiotic stress. Together, these observations support a model in which the presence of vitamin B12 reduces cefiderocol susceptibility in CRAB through multifactorial mechanisms involving iron-acquisition systems, modulation of uptake pathways, and metabolic remodeling associated with adaptive survival phenotypes. Clinically, these findings raise the possibility that micronutrient context, including vitamin B12 exposure, could influence cefiderocol efficacy and heteroresistance dynamics in CRAB infections, an area that warrants further translational investigation .

Approximate Probabilistic Bisimulation for Continuous-Time Markov Chains
Timm Spork✉, Christel Baier, Joost-Pieter Katoen, Sascha Klüppelholz, Jakob Piribauer

Abstract
We introduce ( ε , δ ) (\varepsilon,\delta) -bisimulation, a novel type of approximate probabilistic bisimulation for continuous-time Markov chains. In contrast to related notions, ( ε , δ ) (\varepsilon,\delta) -bisimulation allows the use of different tolerances for the transition probabilities ( ε \varepsilon , additive) and total exit rates ( δ \delta , multiplicative) of states. Fundamental properties of the notion, as well as bounds on the absolute difference of time- and reward-bounded reachability probabilities for ( ε , δ ) (\varepsilon,\delta) -bisimilar states, are established.

Introduction

Continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs) are a prominent probabilistic
model in various application fields, e.g., reliability engineering,
systems biology, modeling of chemical reactions, and performance evaluation. CTMCs are state-based
models whose transitions yield a discrete probability distribution over
states—as in discrete-time Markov chains (DTMCs)—while the state residence
times are governed by exponential distributions. Various model-checking
approaches for CTMCs exist

[
7
,
11
,
22
,
63
]

and are supported by tools such as PRISM
[
40
]
and Storm
[
34
]
. CTMC model checking is used
to analyze, e.g., stochastic Petri nets

[
6
]
, fault trees

[
8
,
61
]
,
biological systems
[
41
,
46
]
, and chemical reactions
[
2
,
4
]
.

The central issue in CTMC model checking is computing timed reachability
probabilities: what is the probability to reach a set of goal states
within a given deadline from a given start state? The reliability of a
fault tree, or the probability that all molecules have been catalyzed
within two days, are instances of this question. Computing timed
reachability probabilities reduces to computing transient probabilities
in a uniformized CTMC, i.e., a CTMC in which the state residence times
are “normalized”
[
13
]
. This method is
quite efficient, numerically stable, and scales to CTMCs with millions
of states.

In practical applications, however, transition probabilities and state residence
time distributions—defined by exit rates—are usually not known exactly. Component failure rates
in fault trees are vulnerable to environmental conditions, and
reaction rates of molecules are obtained experimentally. This raises
the question whether CTMC model-checking results are robust w.r.t.
perturbations of their stochastic aspects. The aim of this paper is to
investigate to what extent transition probabilities and exit rates in a
given CTMC can be altered while ensuring that timed reachability
probabilities are preserved up to a small tolerance
\theta
.

To this end we define the novel notion of

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulation on CTMCs, investigate its
fundamental properties and derive bounds for timed reachability
probabilities. The results yield under which (absolute)
\varepsilon
-tolerance on
transition probabilities and (relative)
\delta
-tolerance on exit rates, timed
reachability probabilities are close up to
\theta
. This enables, e.g.,
to determine the maximal tolerances in components’ failure rates while
ensuring the fault tree’s (i.e., overall systems’) reliability. Our
notion generalizes strong probabilistic bisimulation
[
19
]
(also known as lumpability) that preserves timed reachability probabilities exactly.

Main Contributions.

Our main contributions are the following:

•

We introduce
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulation
for CTMCs, a novel type of approximate probabilistic bisimulation that allows an absolute tolerance of
\varepsilon
for the transition probabilities of related states, and a relative tolerance of
\delta
for their total exit rates. We prove that the union of all
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulations, called
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimilarity, is an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulation itself, that it is additive in
\varepsilon
and
\delta

[
15
,
19
]
iff
\varepsilon=\delta=0
. Moreover, we discuss how quasi-lumpability
[
19
,
29
,
30
]
and
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimilarity relate, and show how to “split”
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimilarity into
(\varepsilon,0)
- and
(0,\delta)
-bisimilarity, allowing an individual treatment of the parameters (
Section

3
).

•

We derive bounds on the absolute difference of timed reachability probabilities of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimilar states by uniformizing the CTMC and applying a bound from
[
18
]
for the difference in finite horizon reachability probabilities of
\varepsilon
-bisimilar states of DTMCs
[
25
]
. The bounds are tight if
\delta=0
(
Section

4
).

•

We analyze the absolute difference of timed reachability probabilities between
(0,\delta)
-bisimilar states in more detail. Using so-called

]
we show how to compute them

\varepsilon=0

Section

5
).

•

We extend (some of) our results to reward-bounded reachability probabilities in CTMCs with nonnegative rewards, by utilizing a method from
[
12

that allows us to express them as timed reachability probabilities (

6
).

Related Work.

In the discrete-time setting of DTMCs a lot of work has been done on approximate probabilistic bisimulations, mainly focusing on
\varepsilon
-bisimulations as introduced by Desharnais

[
18
,
25
]
. Other notions include approximate probabilistic bisimulations with precision
\varepsilon

[
1
,
3
,
27
]
, up-to-

-bisimulations
[

,
25
]
, or approximate versions of weak- and branching probabilistic bisimulation
[
5
,
59
]
. See, e.g.,
[
59
]
for a comparison of several different notions.

For CTMCs, an approximate version of lumpability, called
\tau
-
quasi
- or
near-lumpability
, is known
[
19
,
29
,
30
]
. It is best suited for the analysis of long-run or stationary properties of a chain, but does not provide good guarantees on its
transient
behavior. A more recent notion is
proportional lumpability

[
48
,
49
,
55
,
56
]
. Intuitively, states
s,s^{\prime}
are proportionally lumpable w.r.t. a function
\kappa
that maps states to values in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
iff there is an equivalence
R
such that the transition rates of
s
and
s^{\prime}
to any equivalence class of
R
differ by the constant factors
\kappa(s)
resp.
\kappa(s^{\prime})
. Proportional lumpability preserves the
exact
stationary distributions.

Uncertain continuous-time Markov chains
(
UCTMC
)
[
20
,
21
]
are CTMCs with time-varying transition rates. At each time
t\geq 0
and for all states
s,s^{\prime}
, the transition rate to move from
s
to
s^{\prime}
must be contained in a fixed interval
[m(s,s^{\prime}),M(s,s^{\prime})]\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Lumpabilities for UCTMCs are equivalences that require related states to have the same “extremal realizations”, defined as the (time-invariant) CTMCs in which all transition rates
R(s,s^{\prime})
coincide with
m(s,s^{\prime})
resp.
M(s,s^{\prime})
. These lumpabilities can be computed efficiently and are characterized both via value functions and satisfaction equivalence of formulas in a variant of the continuous stochastic logic CSL
[
9
,
13
]
. However, there does not seem to be a direct connection to our notion of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulation.

Another related line of research is
perturbation theory
for Markov chains. Given a CTMC
\mathcal{M}
and a (slightly) perturbed
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, the goal is to bound the difference of (some) performance measures of the models. See, e.g.,
[
43
,
44
,
45
,
51
,
58
]
or the overview article
[
52
]
.
The bounds presented in the literature are usually on the
total
error between the
stationary
distributions of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.
Furthermore, they are oftentimes obtained under the assumption of
drift conditions
or rely on the
ergodicity
of the chain.
This is in contrast to our work, which focuses on the
componentwise
difference in
transient
reachability probabilities up to time
t
.

Distributions and vectors.
Let
S\neq\emptyset
be finite. The set of

is

.

Relations.
Given a relation

/

\displaystyle S
/

Markov chains.
Fix a countable set

. A

, and the probability to take a transition from

.
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{|S|\times|S|}
denotes the (

)

of

. The entries of

are given as
\mathbf{Q}_{i,j}=P(i,j)\cdot E(i)

3.2
Definition of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-Bisimulation

In contrast to the discrete-time case, the behavior of a CTMC
\mathcal{M}
is not only influenced by the transition probabilities, but also by the exit rates of the states. Thus, it is natural to consider two different tolerance values
\varepsilon
and
\delta
for the probabilities resp. the exit rates. As the values of
P(s,\cdot)
are in
[0,1]
for any
s\in S
, similar to the discrete-time case an additive tolerance
\varepsilon
is suitable.

The rates
E(s)

additive
tolerances
\delta
for the rates difficult. Consider, for example, a CTMC with states
s,s^{\prime}
such that
E(s)=1
and
E(s^{\prime})=100
, and assume that the exit rates of related states are allowed to differ by at most
10\%
. For
s

\delta
would need a value of
0.1
(i.e., all states with a rate in
[0.9,1.1]
are considered to behave almost the same as
s
), while
\delta=10
would be necessary for
s^{\prime}
. The former allows virtually no tolerance for
E(s^{\prime})
(only
0.001\%
instead of the desired
10\%
), while the latter allows a tolerance of up to
1000\%
for
E(s)
.

To circumvent this issue we propose the use of a
multiplicative
tolerance
\delta
for the exit rates of related states, so that the tolerable difference is relative to the actual rates. For the rest of the paper, let
\ln(\cdot)
denote the natural logarithm.

We now establish some fundamental properties of

that are generally desirable for approximate probabilistic bisimulations

Theorem 3.2

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a CTMC and
s,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in S
.

1.

{\sim_{\varepsilon,\delta}}
is the largest
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulation on
\mathcal{M}
.

2.

If

3.

s\sim s^{\prime}
iff

.

Theorem

shows that

is itself an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulation, and that it is the largest such relation (item 1). Furthermore
\sim_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is additive in both

and coincides with strong bisimilarity
\sim
iff
\varepsilon=\delta=0

By slightly adjusting a procedure proposed in

that constructs

for DTMCs by solving flow networks á la

can be computed in time polynomial in the number of states of

Corollary 1

For a given CTMC

,

.

Furthermore,
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimilarity in

implies

s\sim_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{\mathcal{M}}s^{\prime}

, where

-bisimulations is that of

Let

On the other hand, states related by a

-bisimulation.

Proposition 2

For any given

\displaystyle P^{\prime}((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))

5
The Effect of Changing Rates Only

The goal of this section is to obtain more refined bounds for the absolute difference of timed reachability probabilities of
(0,\delta)
-bisimilar CTMCs. This setting is relevant in different scenarios like, e.g., queuing theory. Consider a CTMC
\mathcal{M}
modeling a single server queue with a buffer of size
n

\tau
and are served with a constant rate
\mu

describes the probability of failure until
t

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
be like
\mathcal{M}

\mu^{\prime}=\mu\cdot c

\mathcal{M}\sim_{0,\delta}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}

and

is when using the slower server instead of the faster one, making it easy to compare their performance.

We first show how to compute the absolute difference in timed reachability probabilities of
(0,\delta)
-bisimilar CTMCs

and

exactly, without relying on the uniformization method
[

35
]
or the Kolmogorov forward equations

If this is not the case, we can use the
Jordan canonical form
(
JCF
)
[
50
]
instead, which looks as follows:
Given an
n\times n
matrix
M
over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
with distinct eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
, given in descending order w.r.t. their absolutes values, the JCF of
M
is a decomposition of the form
M=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{S}^{-1}
for matrices
\mathbf{S},\mathbf{J}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.
\mathbf{J}
is a block diagonal matrix
with so-called
Jordan blocks

\mathbf{J}_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
,
m\leq k\leq n
, on the diagonal. The
i
-th Jordan block
\mathbf{J}_{i}
corresponds to one of
M
’s eigenvalues
\tau
and is a
r_{i}\times r_{i}
matrix for some
r_{i}
that is at most the algebraic multiplicity of
\tau
, and such that the sum over all
r_{i}
for the Jordan blocks belonging to the same eigenvalue
\tau
equals its geometric multiplicity. The Jordan blocks
for eigenvalue
\tau
have
\tau
everywhere on the diagonal and entry
1
directly above the diagonal. All other entries are
0
. We w.l.o.g. assume that the Jordan blocks are in descending order w.r.t. the absolute value of the corresponding eigenvalues, and that multiple Jordan blocks for the same eigenvalue are in descending order w.r.t. their size. We write
q_{\mathbf{J}}
for the total number of Jordan blocks of
\mathbf{J}
and
r_{i}
for the size of the
i
-th Jordan block
\mathbf{J}_{i}
.

It is well-known that every matrix over
\mathbb{C}
has a JCF
[
50
]
, so the above decomposition exists for the transition probability matrix of
any
CTMC. We now show how to compute
p_{n}
, i.e., the probability to reach the goal state after exactly
n
discrete steps, in the case that
\mathbf{P}
is not diagonalizable.

Proposition 8

Denote by
\lambda_{i}
the eigenvalue corresponding to the
i
-th Jordan block
\mathbf{J}_{i}
of
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{S}^{-1}
. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
z\in\{0,\ldots,q_{\mathbf{J}}-1\}
the number of Jordan blocks for eigenvalue
0
and
h_{l}=\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}r_{i}
. Then
p_{N+1}
equals

\displaystyle\sum_{l=a_{\mathbf{P}}+1}^{q_{\mathbf{J}}-z}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{l}}\sum_{k=1}^{j}\mathbf{S}_{1,k+h_{l}}\cdot\mathbf{S}^{-1}_{j+h_{l},n}\cdot\lambda_{l}^{N+k-j}\cdot\left(\lambda_{l}\cdot\binom{N+1}{j-k}-\binom{N}{j-k}\right)+R(N,z)

where, for
\mathbf{S}_{1,h_{l}+j}^{*}=\mathbf{S}_{1,h_{l},+j}
if
j>0
and
\mathbf{S}_{1,h_{l}+j}^{*}=0
if
j=0
,

R(N,z)=\sum_{l=q_{\mathbf{J}}-z+1}^{q_{\mathbf{J}}}\sum_{j=0}^{r_{l}-(N+1)}(\mathbf{S}_{1,h_{l}+j}^{*}-\mathbf{S}_{1,h_{l}+j+1})\cdot\mathbf{S}^{-1}_{h_{l}+N+j,n}
.

In particular, the term
R(N,z)
introduced in
Proposition

8
equals
0
if

or if

Bounds for Reward-Bounded Reachability Probabilities

In practice, CTMCs are often extended with
rewards
that allow to model, e.g., the accumulation of costs, the energy consumption, or different performance measures like the availability of the system
[
12
,
31
]
. We consider state-based reward functions
\rho\colon S\to\mathbb{R}
that assign to every
s\in S
a reward
\rho(s)
. Rewards are accumulated in the states, and the cumulative reward along the timed path
\pi=s_{0}t_{0}s_{1}t_{1}\ldots\in\mathrm{Paths}(\mathcal{M})
of
\mathcal{M}
until time
t
is given (for
\sigma@t=s_{m}
) as
[
12
]

\rho(\sigma,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}t_{j}\cdot\rho(s_{j})+\left(t-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}t_{j}\right)\cdot\rho(s_{m}).

Similar to timed reachability probabilities
\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge^{\leq t}g)
, logics like CSRL
[
12
]
allow to specify properties in terms of
reward-bounded
reachability probabilities
\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)
, which denote the probability to reach, from state
s
, a goal state
g
while accumulating at most reward
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

We extend the results of
Sections

4
and
5
to reward-bounded reachability probabilities. Here, we consider the special case of
nonnegative
rewards, i.e., we assume that
\rho(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in S
. To accommodate for the addition of rewards to our model, we adjust the definition of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulations to require related states to have the same reward, i.e.,
s\sim_{\varepsilon,\delta}s^{\prime}
is possible only if
\rho(s)=\rho(s^{\prime})
. An extension to a notion of, say,
(\varepsilon,\delta,\mu)
-bisimulation, where
\mu
describes an allowed error in the rewards in related states, is an interesting direction for future work.

First, assume
\rho(s)>0
for all

, a setting also analyzed in, e.g.,
[
12
]
. There, based on ideas from
[
17
]
, a transformation is proposed that allows to compute reward-bounded reachability probabilities in
\mathcal{M}
via

reachability probabilities in a modified model
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
. This model differs from

and rewards

7
Conclusion and Future Work

We introduced
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulations, a novel type of approximate probabilistic bisimulation for CTMCs.
In contrast to notions from the literature such as quasi-lumpability
[
19
,
29
,
30
]
, the separate bounds on changes in transition probabilities and changes in exit rates used in
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulation result in a
more flexible, fine-grained notion of behavioral similarity.
We established fundamental properties of the notion, and analyzed the difference in timed reachability probabilities of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimilar chains.

We obtained bounds on this difference by uniformizing the chain and applying known results from
[
18
]
for the discrete-time setting.
Although tight if
\delta=0
, the bounds usually do not perform well if
\delta>0
. Hence, we investigated the special case of
(0,\delta)
-bisimilarity in more detail—also as a stepping stone towards a refined treatment of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimilarity in future work.
For
(0,\delta)
-bisimilar chains, we provided bounds based on the error in Erlang CTMCs
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, as well as based on a spectral analysis of the probability matrix
\mathbf{P}
of the CTMC in question. Furthermore, we extended our results to reward-bounded reachability probabilities, provided that all rewards in the model are nonnegative.

From a theoretical point of view, the behavior of the bounds obtained from the spectral analysis of
\mathbf{P}
is promising. They are tight and, as can be observed in
Figure

7
, seem to evolve similar to the actual error.
The bounds are, however, stretched by a (large) factor, which happens since, to obtain the constants
C
, the triangle inequality and maxima are used. This causes the constants to become significantly larger than necessary, leading to poor performance of the bounds in particular if
t
is small. Therefore, searching for smaller constants
C^{\prime}\ll C
in future work is in order.
From a practical point of view, applicability of the bounds seems questionable. They tend to over-approximate the error and, depending on the second largest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{P}
, can take a long time (i.e., require large values of
t
) to converge to the actual error, even for small chains. Moreover, computing Jordan canonical forms (and diagonalizations) is computationally expensive and numerically unstable
[
26
,
53
,
54
]
. Consequently, the search for more practically relevant bounds is an important direction for future work.

Other open questions include an extension of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulation to a notion of
(\varepsilon,\delta,\mu)
-bisimulation that allows a tolerance of
\mu
in the rewards of related states, an analysis of the achievable state space reduction in quotients w.r.t. (transitive)
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimulations, and a closer look at the (approximate) preservation of logical properties expressed by, e.g., CSL-formulas
[
9
,
13
]
, between
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-bisimilar states.

it follows that the claim holds if

Moreover, if

then

and thus

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathcal{E}_{n}}(\lozenge^{\leq t}g)-\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\prime}}(\lozenge^{\leq t}g)|=0=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\left(e^{-t}-1^{k}e^{-1\cdot t}\right),

so the claim also holds if

, independent of

and

Now assume that

,
c>1
, and denote, for a given

, the states of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
by

. As Erlang CTMC are, by definition, acyclic it is possible to compute their transient reachability probabilities explicitly by using, e.g., the ACE algorithm proposed in

. We follow a more direct approach and prove the claim by induction on

Induction Base:
For

If

Intuitively,

yields a uniform CTMC with the same structure as
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and modified rates

for which, as described in

, the probability to reach the goal state
g
until time point

is the same as reaching

\mathcal{E}_{1}
until time point

\mathcal{E}_{1}

at

and reaching

from

in the interval

From

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}(\lozenge^{=t}s_{0})\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s_{0}}(\lozenge^{\leq t\cdot(c-1)}g)

\displaystyle=(1-(1-e^{-t}))\cdot(1-e^{-t(c-1)})

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\cdot(1-e^{-t\cdot(c-1)})

\displaystyle=e^{-t}-e^{-t-t\cdot(c-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{0}}{0!}\cdot\left(e^{-t}-c^{0}e^{-c\cdot t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{0}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\left(e^{-t}-c^{k}e^{-c\cdot t}\right).

The function

. By computing the roots via

\displaystyle ce^{-ct}-e^{-t}=0\qquad\text{ iff }\lx@nobreakspace\qquad\ln(c)-ct=-t\qquad\text{ iff }\lx@nobreakspace\qquad t=\frac{\ln(c)}{c-1}

and plugging into the second derivative of

Intuitively, this identity expresses that reaching

is the same as either reaching
g
in
\mathcal{E}_{n}
until time
t
, or being in any of the remaining states of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
at time point

and reaching

in the remaining time of

. For the absolute difference of the reachability probabilities in

and

Because of the shape of

, the probabilities

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{\sup K_{s,r}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}R(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(s)\cdot x}g)\ \mathrm{dx},&\text{if }s\neq g\\
1,&\text{if }s=g\end{cases}.

First, we show that removing self-loops from states with reward
0
does not change the reward-bounded reachability probabilities of the goal state
g
. Note that in the following lemma we can exclude
P(s,s)=1
, because in this case
s
is absorbing and the probability to reach
g
from
s
equals
0
, independent of the reward-bound.

Lemma 5

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a CTMC and
s\in S
with
\rho(s)=0
and
P(s,s)\in(0,1)
. Then
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)=\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathcal{M}^{*}}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)
, where
\mathcal{M}^{*}
is as
\mathcal{M}
, with the only difference being that
P^{\mathcal{M}^{*}}(s,s)=0
and
P^{\mathcal{M}^{*}}(s,s^{\prime})=\frac{P^{\mathcal{M}}(s,s^{\prime})}{1-P^{\mathcal{M}}(s,s)}
for all
s\neq s^{\prime}
.

Proof

By our assumption
\rho(g)>0
and hence
s\neq g
. Furthermore, as
\rho(s)=0
we have
K_{s,r}=[0,\infty)
for every
r\geq 0
and
r\ominus\rho(s)\cdot x=r
for every
x\geq 0
. Thus, the reward-bounded reachability probabilities from
s
are given as
[
12
]

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}R(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S\setminus\{s\}}R(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\infty}R(s,s)\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}.

Now we can simplify the second integral via

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}R(s,s)\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=R(s,s)\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=R(s,s)\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\cdot\frac{1}{E(s)}

\displaystyle=P(s,s)\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S\setminus\{s\}}R(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}+P(s,s)\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\cdot(1-P(s,s))=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S\setminus\{s\}}R(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

and hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-P(s,s)}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S\setminus\{s\}}R(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S\setminus\{s\}}E(s)\cdot\frac{P(s,s^{\prime})}{1-P(s,s)}\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S\setminus\{s\}}E(s)\cdot P^{\mathcal{M}^{*}}(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}E(s)\cdot P^{\mathcal{M}^{*}}(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}R^{\mathcal{M}^{*}}(s,s^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(s)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{s^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pr}^{*}_{s}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)

where the second to last equality follows because
P^{\mathcal{M}^{*}}(s,s)=0
.

Thanks to
Lemma

5
we can assume w.l.o.g. that no state
s
in
\mathcal{M}
with reward
\rho(s)=0
has a self-loop when computing reward-bounded reachability probabilities.

Next, we show that it is possible to safely remove states with reward
0
from a CTMC without changing the reward-bounded reachability probabilities of any state with positive reward.
See

Proof

We show the claim by proving that, for any state
t
in
\mathcal{M}
and any successor
t^{\prime}
of
t
with
\rho(t)=0
the reward-bounded reachability probabilities in
\mathcal{M}
are the same as in the chain
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
obtained by removing
t^{\prime}
from
S
and setting, for every state
t^{\prime\prime}
with
P(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})>0
, the probability distribution as
P^{\prime}(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime\prime})=P(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime\prime})+P(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})\cdot P(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime\prime})
for all
t^{\prime\prime\prime}\neq t^{\prime}
. By iteratively applying this transformation, we can remove all states with reward
0
from
\mathcal{M}
, obtaining in the end a CTMC
\mathcal{M}_{>0}
of the desired form.

By our assumptions it holds for any state
t^{\prime}
with
\rho(t^{\prime})=0
that
t^{\prime}\neq g
. Furthermore, we can assume w.l.o.g. that
P(t^{\prime},t^{\prime})=0
because of
Lemma

5
. Using the latter, we show that
P^{\prime}(t^{\prime\prime},\cdot)
as defined above is a distribution for every
t^{\prime\prime}\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}
. If
P(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})=0
, moving from
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
does not change the transition probabilities of
t^{\prime\prime}
at all, so nothing is to show. Otherwise,

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(t^{\prime\prime},S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}P^{\prime}(t^{\prime\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}P(t^{\prime\prime},t)+P(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})\cdot P(t^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}P(t^{\prime\prime},t)+\sum_{t\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}P(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})\cdot P(t^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}P(t^{\prime\prime},t)+P(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})\cdot\underbrace{\sum_{t\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}P(t^{\prime},t)}_{=\sum_{t\in S}P(t^{\prime},t)=1\text{ as}\lx@nobreakspace P(t^{\prime},t^{\prime})=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}P(t^{\prime\prime},t)+P(t^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime})=1.

To show the preservation of reward-bounded reachability probabilities, we first observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}\in S}R(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t^{\prime})\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t^{\prime})\cdot x}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}\in S}R(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t^{\prime})\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}\in S}\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\cdot R(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-E(t^{\prime})\cdot x}\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}\in S}\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\cdot P(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\cdot E(t^{\prime})\cdot\frac{1}{E(t^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}\in S}\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\cdot P(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)\cdot P(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}).

(11)

Let now
t\in S
with
P(t,t^{\prime})>0
. Then

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}=}\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sup K_{t,r}}\sum_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}\in S}R(t,t^{\prime\prime\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t)\cdot x}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sup K_{t,r}}\sum_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}R(t,t^{\prime\prime\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t)\cdot x}g)

\displaystyle\qquad+R(t,t^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t)\cdot x}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sup K_{t,r}}\sum_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}R(t,t^{\prime\prime\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t)\cdot x}g)

\displaystyle\qquad+R(t,t^{\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t)\cdot x}\cdot\sum_{t^{\prime\prime}\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t)\cdot x}g)\cdot P(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sup K_{t,r}}\sum_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}E(t)\cdot(P(t,t^{\prime\prime\prime})+P(t,t^{\prime})\cdot P(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime\prime}))\cdot e^{-E(t)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t)\cdot x}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sup K_{t,r}}\sum_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}E(t)\cdot P^{\prime}(t,t^{\prime\prime\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t)\cdot x}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sup K_{t,r}}\sum_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}\in S\setminus\{t^{\prime}\}}R^{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime\prime})\cdot e^{-E(t)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{Pr}_{t^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\lozenge_{\leq r\ominus\rho(t)\cdot x}g)\ \mathrm{dx}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}_{t}(\lozenge_{\leq r}g),

so removing

and redistributing the incoming probabilities as described above indeed preserves reward-bounded reachability probabilities.

Remark 6

Assume that we are given a CTMC
\mathcal{M}

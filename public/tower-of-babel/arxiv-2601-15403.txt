F-Purity of Binomial Edge Ideals
Adam LaClair, Jason McCullough

Abstract
. In 2012, K. Matsuda introduced the class of weakly closed graphs and investigated when binomial edge ideals are F-pure. He proved that weakly closed binomial edge ideals are F-pure whenever the base field has positive characteristic. He conjectured that: (i) when the base field has characteristic two, every F-pure binomial edge ideal comes from a weakly closed graph; and (ii) that every binomial edge ideal is F-pure provided that the characteristic of the residue field is sufficiently large. In this paper, we resolve both of Matsuda’s conjectures. We confirm Matsuda’s first conjecture, showing that the binomial edge ideal of a graph defines an F-pure quotient in characteristic 2 if and only if the graph is weakly closed. We also show that Matsuda’s second conjecture is false in a very strong way by showing that graphs containing asteroidal triples, such as the net, define non-F-pure binomial edge ideals in any positive characteristic. Our results yield a complete classification of F-pure binomial edge ideals of chordal graphs as well as large families of standard graded algebras that are F-injective but neither F-pure nor F-rational in all characteristics.

2.
Background

2.1.
F-Pure and F-Split Rings

Let
R
be a ring of characteristic
p
for some prime
p>0
. We denote by
F:R\rightarrow R
the Frobenius ring endomorphism sending
r
to
r^{p}
. We will denote the codomain by
F_{\ast}R
. The module
F_{\ast}R
has an
R
-module structure coming from Frobenius given by
r\cdot F_{\ast}s:=F_{\ast}(r^{p}s)
, and the map
F:R\rightarrow F_{\ast}R:r\mapsto F_{\ast}r^{p}
is
R
-linear.

Definition 2.1
.

The ring
R
is
F-pure
if the map of
R
-modules
F:R\rightarrow F_{\ast}R
is pure, i.e., for every
R
-module
M
, the map

\displaystyle M\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}F_{\ast}R

is injective.

An important criterion for testing whether a ring is F-pure is given by Fedder’s criterion.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
15
, Theorem 1.12]
)
.

Let
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring with
\fchar\Bbbk=p>0
, let
\mathfrak{m}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be the graded maximal ideal, and let
I\subseteq S
be a graded ideal. Then
S/I
is F-pure if and only if

\displaystyle I^{[p]}:I\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p]},

where
I^{[p]}:=(\{f^{p}\mid f\in I\})
.

A related notion is the F-split property.

Definition 2.3
.

The ring
R
is
F-split
if there exists an
R
-linear map
\varphi:F_{\ast}R\rightarrow R
such that
\varphi\circ F=\id_{R}
, where
F:R\rightarrow F_{\ast}R
is the Frobenius map.

It is well-known that if
R
is F-split, then
R
is F-pure. In general, it need not be the case that F-pure rings are F-split; see e.g.
[
49
, Proposition 3.71]
. However, when
R
is
F-finite
, i.e.,
F_{\ast}R
is a finite
R
-module, then F-pure rings are F-split. The property of a ring being F-finite is preserved under adjoining variables, passing to a quotient ring, and localizing at a multiplicative subset. Hence, if
\Bbbk
is a perfect field, then the ring
\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/I
is F-finite for any ideal
I\subseteq\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. We refer the reader to
[
40
]
for further information regarding the terms defined here.

2.2.
Binomial Edge Ideals

Let

Proposition 2.4

(
[

, Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.9]
)

Let

. For a subset

, and

. Then,

(1)

(2)

\hgt(P_{G}(S))=2\#S+(n-c(S))
,

(3)

,

(4)

is a minimal prime of

if and only if

, or

and for each

.

Definition 2.5
.

With notation as in Proposition

, we say that

is a

satisfies condition (

).

We note that

carries two different gradings under which

is a graded ideal. First,

is a graded ideal when

\deg(x_{i})=\deg(y_{i})=1
. Second,

We next recall several useful graph constructions that will be relevant for our purposes. We define the neighborhood of a vertex

\displaystyle N_{G}(v):=\{u\in V(G)\mid\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}.

3.
F-Purity of Binomial Edge Ideals

In this section, we gather some relevant facts on the F-purity of binomial edge ideals that will be relevant for the remainder of this paper.

The first result shows that F-purity of a binomial edge ideal over a field of characteristic
p>0
can be verified over the field
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Bbbk=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, and let
\mathbb{K}
be any field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
R:=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
and
S:=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices, and let
J_{G}\subseteq R
the binomial edge ideal. Then,
R/J_{G}
is F-pure if and only if
S/J_{G}S
is F-pure.

Proof.

By Fedder’s criterion (Theorem
2.2
), we have that
R/J_{G}
is F-pure if and only if

(1)

J_{G}^{[2]}:_{R}J_{G}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[2]}.

Since the map
\Bbbk\hookrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is faithfully flat and
S\cong R\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathbb{K}
, it follows that equation (
1
) is equivalent to

(2)

(J_{G}S)^{[2]}:_{S}J_{G}S\subseteq(\mathfrak{m}S)^{[2]}.

By applying Fedder’s criterion (Theorem
2.2
), equation (
2
) is equivalent to
S/J_{G}S
being F-pure. The result now follows by observing that
J_{G}S
is precisely the binomial edge ideal of
G
defined over the ring
S
.
∎

Henceforth, we will always assume that the base field is
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
unless specified otherwise. The next lemma shows that the property of the binomial edge ideal being F-pure descends along induced subgraphs.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
H
be an induced subgraph of
G
. If
R_{G}
is F-pure, then
R_{H}
is F-pure.

Proof.

Since

is F-finite, the property of

or

being F-pure is equivalent to
R_{G}
or
R_{H}
being F-split. Thus, we may suppose that
R_{G}
is F-split, and it suffices to show that
R_{H}
is F-split. Since
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
, we have that
R_{H}
is a
\Bbbk
-algebra retract of
R_{G}
; in other words, we have that
R_{H}\subseteq R_{G}
as

-algebras and that there is a map
\varepsilon:R_{G}\rightarrow R_{H}
which is the identity on
R_{H}
. Because
R_{G}
is F-split, there is a map
\phi:F_{\ast}R_{G}\rightarrow R_{G}
which splits the Frobenius map
F:R_{G}\rightarrow F_{\ast}R_{G}
. Thus we have the following commutative diagram.

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

We claim that the composition of maps

\displaystyle\varepsilon\circ\phi\circ F_{\ast}\iota:F_{\ast}R_{H}\rightarrow R_{H}

splits the map

. Indeed, we have that for

The next result establishes that F-purity of binomial edge ideals is preserved under vertex completion and deletion.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
v\in V(G)
. If
R_{G}
is F-pure, then the graphs
G\smallsetminus v
,
G_{v}
, and
G_{v}\smallsetminus v
realize F-pure binomial edge ideals.

Proof.

First, we show that
R_{G\smallsetminus v}
is F-pure. Lemma
3.2
implies that

defines an F-pure binomial edge ideal since
G\smallsetminus v
is an induced subgraph of
G
.

Next, we show that
R_{G_{v}}
is F-pure. By Fedder’s criterion (Theorem
2.2
), it suffices to show that

(3)

J_{G_{v}}^{[2]}:J_{G_{v}}\not\subseteq m^{[2]}.

We will show that

(4)

J_{G}^{[2]}:J_{G}\subseteq J_{G_{v}}^{[2]}:J_{G_{v}}.

Since
R_{G}
is F-pure, we have that

\displaystyle J_{G}^{[2]}:J_{G}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[2]}.

Hence, equation (
4
) will imply equation (
3
), which will complete the proof.

Let

, and let
\{i,j\}\in E(G_{v})
. If
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
, then we have that

\displaystyle r\cdot f_{ij}\in J_{G}^{[2]}\subseteq J_{G_{v}}^{[2]}.

Suppose that
\{i,j\}\in E(G_{v})\smallsetminus E(G)
. Then,

. By Plücker’s relations (see e.g.,
[

]
), we have that

\displaystyle r\cdot x_{v}\cdot f_{ij}\in J_{G_{v}}^{[2]}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\Ass_{R}(R/J_{G_{v}}^{[2]})=\Ass_{R}(R/J_{G_{v}})=\{P_{G}(S)\mid v\notin S\}

]

, and thus establishes equation (

Finally, we observe that the above two cases imply that

4.
Non-F-Pure Binomial Edge Ideals

In this section, we exhibit a large family of graphs whose binomial edge ideal do not realize an F-pure quotient ring for any positive characteristic
p>0
. Thus, we answer Matsuda’s Conjecture
B
in the negative.

We begin with some preparatory results.

Theorem 4.1

(Lucas’s Theorem
[
39
]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime integer and
m
and
n
positive integers. Choose a positive integer
e
so that we can write
m=\sum_{i=0}^{e}m_{i}p^{i}
and
n=\sum_{i=0}^{e}n_{i}p^{i}
where
0\leq m_{i},n_{i}<p
for
0\leq i\leq e
. Then

\displaystyle\binom{n}{m}\equiv\prod_{i=0}^{e}\binom{n_{i}}{m_{i}}\mod{p},

with the convention that
\binom{a}{b}=0
if
a<b
.

For a modern treatment of Lucas’s Theorem, see
[
16
, Theorem 1]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p
be a prime. For all
0\leq i\leq p-1
, we have that

\displaystyle\binom{p-1}{i}\equiv(-1)^{i}\mod{p}.

Proof.

The proof is an exercise in elementary number theory. A write-up can be found in
[
6
]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p
be a positive prime number. If
a
,
b
, and
c
are distinct positive integers, then

\displaystyle(f_{a,b}f_{b,c})^{p-1}\equiv(x_{b}y_{b}f_{a,c})^{p-1}\mod{(x_{b}^{p},y_{b}^{p})}.

Proof.

The result can be verified by hand when
p=2
. Hence, we may suppose that
p>2
is an odd positive prime number. By the Pigeon hole principle, it is clear that every non-zero term of
(f_{a,b}f_{b,c})^{p-1}
is divisible by
x_{b}^{p}
,
y_{b}^{p}
, or
x_{b}^{p-1}y_{b}^{p-1}
. The term of
(f_{a,b}f_{b,c})^{p-1}
divisible by
x_{b}^{p-1}y_{b}^{p-1}
is

(5)

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{i}^{2}(x_{a}y_{c})^{i}\cdot(x_{c}y_{a})^{p-1-i}\right)x_{b}^{p-1}y_{b}^{p-1},

which by Lemma
4.2
is equal to

(6)

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(x_{a}y_{c})^{i}\cdot(x_{c}y_{a})^{p-1-i}\right)x_{b}^{p-1}y_{b}^{p-1}.

By the binomial theorem, the polynomial
f_{a,c}^{p-1}
can be expanded as

(7)

\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{p-i-1}\binom{p-1}{i}(x_{a}y_{c})^{i}\cdot(x_{c}y_{a})^{p-1-i}.

As

Lemma 6.2

\displaystyle r\in P(A\smallsetminus\{k\},B)^{[2]}.

Lemma 6.3

Lemma 6.4
.

Fix an integer
n
. Let
A
and
B
be disjoint subsets of
[n]
. We denote by

the ideal

of

. We denote by

\displaystyle\left(P^{[2]}+Q\right)\cap\left(P^{[2]}+(\omega_{A})\right)=P^{[2]}+Q\cdot(\omega_{A}).

Proof.

We prove the forward inclusion, as the reverse inclusion is clear. Let

. Then we may express

as

, with

is a regular element on

, it follows that

, which completes the proof.

7.
Cycling and Preliminary Computations

In this section, we establish a series of technical lemmas that will prove useful in computing
J_{G}^{[2]}:J_{G}
in Section
9
, when
G
belongs to one of the co-regular families of graphs.

We will utilize the following setup for the remainder of this section.

Setup 7.1
.

Given
n
a positive integer, we set
R=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[x_{i},y_{i}\mid i\in[n]]
. We denote by
m
the following monomial

\displaystyle m:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}.

The following “cycling” lemma will be crucial for us.

Lemma 7.2

(Cycling)
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Let
a
and
c
be distinct integers in
[n]
. Put
B:=\{a,c\}
, and choose
A\subseteq[n]\smallsetminus\{a,c\}
. Let
r\in P(A,B)^{[2]}+(f_{a,c}\,\omega_{A})
. Let
p
and
q
be relatively prime squarefree monomials such that
p
and
q
are not divisible by
x_{j}
or by
y_{j}
for all
j\in A\cup\{a,c\}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{x_{a}}{y_{a}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m\in\Supp(r)\quad(\text{respectively, }\frac{y_{a}}{x_{a}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m\in\Supp(r)),

if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{x_{c}}{y_{c}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m\in\Supp(r)\quad(\text{respectively, }\frac{y_{c}}{x_{c}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m\in\Supp(r)).

Proof.

Since
r\in P(A,B)^{[2]}+(f_{a,c}\,\omega_{A})
, we may express
r
as:

(27)

\displaystyle r=\sum_{j\in A}(\alpha_{j}\cdot x_{j}^{2}+\beta_{j}\cdot y_{j}^{2})+\gamma\cdot f_{a,c}^{2}+\delta\cdot f_{a,c}\,\omega_{A}

for some
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j},\gamma,\delta\in R
and all
j\in A
. The monomial
w:=\frac{x_{a}}{y_{a}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m
is not divisible by
x_{j}^{2}
or by
y_{j}^{2}
for all
j\in A\cup\{c\}
, since
p
is not divisible by these variables. It follows that

\displaystyle w:=\frac{x_{a}}{y_{a}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m\in\Supp(\delta\cdot f_{a,c}\,\omega_{A}).

Since
w
is not divisible by
y_{a}
, it follows that there exists
\delta^{\prime}\in\Supp(\delta)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{x_{a}}{y_{a}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m=\delta^{\prime}\cdot x_{a}y_{c}\,\omega_{A}.

Consequently,
\delta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{y_{a}y_{c}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot\frac{m}{\omega_{A}}
. The assumption that
q
is not divisible by
x_{j}
or by
y_{j}
for all
j\in A\cup\{a,c\}
implies that
\delta^{\prime}
is a bona fide monomial. We compute that

(28)

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}\cdot f_{a,c}\,\omega_{A}=\frac{x_{a}}{y_{a}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m+\frac{x_{c}}{y_{c}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m.

The monomial
w^{\prime}:=\frac{x_{c}}{y_{c}}\cdot\frac{p}{q}\cdot m
appearing in equation (
28
) is not divisible by
x_{j}^{2}
or by
y_{j}^{2}
for
j\in A\cup\{a\}
, since
p
is not divisible by these variables. Consequently, the computation in Equation (
28
) is the unique way to obtain
w^{\prime}
as a term appearing in the expansion in Equation (
27
). Thus, the coefficient of
w
in
r
is equal to the coefficient of
w^{\prime}
in
r
. Thus,
w\in\Supp(r)
if and only if
w^{\prime}\in\Supp(r)
.
∎

.

10.
Applications of Matsuda’s Conjecture

In this section, we deduce as a consequence of Theorem
A
that unmixed binomial edge ideals of König type are weakly closed, and that weakly closed graphs are stable under vertex deletion and completion.

10.1.
König type, Unmixed Binomial Edge Ideals Are Weakly Closed

In
[
23
]
Herzog, Hibi, and Moradi introduce the notion of a graded ideal of König type.

Definition 10.1
.

S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and let

be a graded ideal of height
h
. We say that
I
is of
König type
, if there exists a sequence
\underline{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{h}
of homogeneous polynomials
that form part of a minimal system of generators of
I
and a monomial order
<

on
S
such that
\mathrm{in}_{<}(f_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{in}_{<}(f_{h})
is a regular sequence.

They characterize the binomial edge ideals of König type via the following theorem.

Theorem 10.2

(
[
23
, Theorem 3.5]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then,
J_{G}
is of König type if and only if
G
contains vertex disjoint paths

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left\lvert E(P_{i})\right\rvert=\hgt J_{G}.

Binomial edge ideals of König type satisfy various “rigidity” properties. For instance, the property of being Cohen-Macaulay is independent of characteristic for binomial edge ideals of König type
[
23
, Corollary 3.8]
. We will show next that
unmixed
binomial edge ideals of König type are F-pure independent of the base field so long as it has positive characteristic. For us, by an

ideal we mean that all of the associated primes of the ideal have the same height.

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. Let

be an unmixed ideal of König type. If the characteristic of the base field is

is F-pure.

Proof.

Let

be as in Theorem

. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
denote the binomials corresponding to the edges of the

, where

. By

, if

is F-injective, where

, then

is F-pure as

form a regular sequence in

of length
\hgt J_{G}
. The ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is even F-pure. Since the
\alpha_{i}
form a regular sequence, it follows from Proposition
4.8
that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}^{[p]}:\mathfrak{a}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{g}\alpha_{i}^{p-1}\right)+\mathfrak{a}^{[p]}.

The lead term of

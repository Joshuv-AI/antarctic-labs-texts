Abstract
. We study hyperplane sections of smooth polarized K ​ 3 K3 -surfaces that split into unions of lines. We describe the dual adjacency graphs of such sections and find sharp upper bounds on their number. In most cases (starting from degree 6 6 ), we also compute all configurations of such sections.

Preliminaries (see

Given a graph

•

Conversely, given a

2.2.
Classification of

Let

be a smooth

represents a split hyperplane

in

Using

\mathopen{|}\Sigma\mathclose{|}=2d,\qquad\text{$\Sigma$ is $3$-regular},\qquad\text{$\Sigma$ is $2$-subgeometric}.

A

The argument above shows also that the property of

A

Given a graph

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
be two

-fragments in an admissible

\Gamma

(1)

\Delta:=\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma_{2}
is a perfect subgraph of

(2)

the relation

on

is a bijection

\varsigma\colon\Sigma_{1}\mathbin{{\smallsetminus}\!\!\!{\smallsetminus}}\Delta\simeq\Sigma_{2}\mathbin{{\smallsetminus}\!\!\!{\smallsetminus}}\Delta

Proof.

Since each line intersects each hyperplane at one point (or because

and

), we have

\text{each vertex $u\in\Gamma\smallsetminus\Sigma$ is adjacent to exactly one

If

, there would be a vertex

).

Similarly, each vertex of

must be adjacent to exactly

, leaving a bijection between the perfect

9.1.
Double planes

Any degree
2
model is hyperelliptic:
X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
is the double plane
ramified over a smooth sextic curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The projection
establishes a two-to-one correspondence between lines on
X
and tritangents
to
C
and, essentially by the definition, an
h
-fragment is the pair of
lines over a tritangent. That is, we always have

\#_{\Sigma}(X)=\frac{1}{2}\mathopen{|}\operatorname{Fn}X\mathclose{|}
. According to
[
4
, Addendum 1.2]
,
this number takes values
\{0..66,72\}
except, possibly,
61
.

9.2.
Double quadrics

A hyperelliptic quartic
X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a double quadric
ramified over a smooth curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
of bi-degree
(4,4)
.
Lines on
X
are in a two-to-one correspondence with the generatrices of the
quadric bitangent to
C
, and an
h
-fragment is the pull-back of
a pair of intersecting bitangents. It follows that
\#_{\Sigma}=n_{1}n_{2}
, where

n_{i}
is the number of bitangents in the
i
-th ruling of the quadric,

i=1,2
. We have
n_{i}\leqslant 12
, and found in
[
3
, Corollary 1.4]

is an example of a quartic with
n_{1}=n_{2}=12
. Hence, the sharp upper bound is

\#_{\Sigma}(X)\leqslant 144
.

9.3.
Sextics and octics

A hyperelliptic
K3
-surface
X\to Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{d+1}
of degree
2d=6
or
8
is
a double scroll ramified over a certain smooth curve
C\subset Y
. (We do not
consider the Veronese octic
X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{5}
as it has no lines.) The
structure of the graph
\operatorname{Fn}X
is described in
[
2
, § 5.5]
:
there are

•

a pair of lines
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
over the exceptional section
E\subset Y

(the circled vertices in
Figure 4
), so
that
\ell_{1}\cdot\ell_{2}=1
if
2d=6
and
\ell_{1}\cdot\ell_{2}=1
if
2d=8
, and

•

a certain number
n
of pairwise disjoint
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{1}
-fragments

\{m_{i1},m_{i2}\}
, one over each bitangent to
C
, so that

m_{i1}\cdot m_{i2}=2
and
m_{ir}\cdot\ell_{r}=\delta_{rs}
.

We have
n\leqslant 9
if
2d=6
and
n\leqslant 8
if
2d=8
.

Remark 9.1
.

In
[
2
]
it is stated that
n\leqslant 10
if
2d=6
and
n\leqslant 12
if

2d=8
. These values can be attained only if the two lines
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}

over
E
are not present. If they are present, the graph is
1
-admissible
but not geometric for
2d=6
and
10\leqslant n\leqslant 12
, and it is not even
hyperbolic for
2d=6
and
n\geqslant 13
or
2d=8
and
n\geqslant 9
.

A split hyperplane section on
X
is the pull-back of a hyperplane section
of
Y
split into
E
and
(d-1)
bitangents.
Since there is a unique
d
-plane in
\mathbb{P}^{d+1}
through the line
E
and any

(d-1)
fibers of the ruling,
the possible counts are the binomial coefficients
C(m,d-1)
,
m\leqslant n
,
i.e.
,

\#_{\Sigma}(X)\in\begin{cases}\{0,1,3,6,10,15,21,28,36\},&\text{if $2d=6$},\\
\{0,1,4,10,20,35,56\},&\text{if $2d=8$}.\end{cases}

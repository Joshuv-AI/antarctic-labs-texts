1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety,
and
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
a dominant rational map, both defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
For a proper closed subscheme
Y\subset X
, we can define associated global height function
h_{Y}
(
[
28
]
),
which is nothing but a Weil height function when
Y
is a Cartier divisor.
When
Y
has codimension at least two, the case we are interested in,

h_{Y}
is sometimes called generalized greatest common divisor.
This name may be justified by the following example.
Let
Y=\{(0:0:1)\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
x=(a:b:c)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})

with
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}}
coprime. Then

(1.1)

\displaystyle h_{Y}(x)=\log\frac{\max\{|a|,|b|,|c|\}}{\max\{|a|,|b|\}}+\log\gcd(a,b).

For a point
x\in X(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
with well-defined forward
f
-orbit,

h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))
is expected to be very small.
More precisely, given any Weil height function
h_{H}
associated with an ample divisor
H
on
X
,
we expect

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}=0

unless there are any special (geometric) reasons (cf.
[
30
, section 19]
).
Roughly speaking, this claims that the number of digits of greatest common divisors
among coordinates of orbits are smaller than the number of digits of coordinates.
There are works
[
22
,
12
]
that directly study this problem,
but they all assume Vojta’s conjecture.
We remark that Silverman pointed out
[
30
, Example 35]
that a famous theorem by Bugeaud-Corvaja-Zannier
[
5
, Theorem 1]

can be interpreted dynamically.
For example, let
a,b\geq 2
be multiplicatively independent integers, and

f\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},(x:y:z)\mapsto(ax:by:z)
.
Let
Y=\{(1:1:1)\}
.
Then

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(1:1:1))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(1:1:1))}

(1.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log\max\{|a^{n}|,|b^{n}|,1\}}\bigg(\log\frac{\max\{|a^{n}|,|b^{n}|,1\}}{\max\{|a^{n}-1|,|b^{n}-1|\}}+\log\gcd(a^{n}-1,b^{n}-1)\bigg)

and
[
5
, Theorem 1]
is equivalent to say that this goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
The key input to the proof of their theorem was Schmidt subspace theorem.

In this paper, we prove a sufficient condition for
eq.

1.2
.
Our theorem is unconditional, i.e. does not assume Vojta’s conjecture.
Our strategy is to take advantage of dynamical complexity, and so only applicable to
maps
f
with non-trivial first dynamical degree.
Thus we cannot deduce Bugeaud-Corvaja-Zannier’s theorem from our one;
the map
f(x:y:z)=(ax:by:z)
has trivial first dynamical degree
1
.

To state our theorem, let us introduce some notions.

Definition 1.1

(Orbits under rational maps)
.

Let
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant rational map on a projective variety
(variety is irreducible and reduced in this paper) defined over an algebraically closed field

.
The indeterminacy locus of

is denoted by

.
For a point

-orbit is well-defined if

.
The set of

For

We say the orbit is generic if

is infinite and

any proper Zariski closed subset

We recall the definition of dynamical degrees.
There are number of papers that study properties of dynamical degrees.
We refer

(Note also that the recursive inequalities proven in

Definition 1.4

(Arithmetic degree)
.

Now we state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.5

(Main theorem)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
N
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant rational map.
Let
Y\subset X
be a proper closed subscheme of dimension

.
If

is not a morphism, assume further that

is pure dimensional,

•

O_{f}(x)
is generic;

•

d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}<\alpha_{f}(x)
,

then for any Weil height function

associated with an ample divisor

(1.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}=0.

Recall that a closed immersion

is called regular embedding

Remark 1.6
.

We always have
\alpha_{f}(x)\leq d_{1}(f)
(for morphisms
[
13
]
, for rational maps
[
21
]
).
Thus it is necessary to have
d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}<d_{1}(f)
so that

theorem

1.5
has content.
By the log concavity of dynamical degrees,
d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}\leq d_{1}(f)
always holds.
The equality holds for polarized endomorphisms
(e.g. non-isomorphic surjective self-morphisms on projective spaces) for example,
and thus we unfortunately can not apply our theorem to such morphisms.

Remark 1.7
.

The Kawaguchi-Silverman conjecture predicts that
\alpha_{f}(x)=d_{1}(f)

for
x\in X_{f}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
with
O_{f}(x)
being Zariski dense.
Also, the dynamical Mordell-Lang conjecture says if the orbit is Zariski dense, then it is generic.
Therefore, if these conjectures hold, the condition “
O_{f}(x)
is generic and
d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}<\alpha_{f}(x)
”
is equivalent to “
O_{f}(x)
is Zariski dense and
d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}<d_{1}(f)
”.

Remark 1.8
.

Without the condition

, the theorem has a counter-example.
See

3.1
.

Remark 1.9

Compare

[

When

Although, there is no logical implications between these two theorems,

can be applicable in a meaningful way to endomorphisms on projective spaces.

One of the key ingredients of the proof is to find an upper bound of

h_{f^{-k}(Y)}
for large

.
The idea is as follows. Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be the ideal sheaf of

If

Then we get an inequality

\displaystyle rh_{f^{-k}(Y)}\leq h_{D}+O(1)=mh_{H}+O(1)

on

Thus our task is to figure out how small

can be for such

This idea of bounding generalized gcd is appeared in the proof of
[

They give an upper bound of generalized gcd associated with reduced zero dimensional subschemes
of smooth projective varieties (their theorem does not involve any dynamics).
When

is not necessarily reduced, their proof with
minor modification would give a sufficient upper bound for our purpose.
However, when

the problem becomes highly non-trivial.
The main body of this paper is devoted to treat this problem.
At this moment, we do not know any clean upper bound of given

Nevertheless, taking advantage of complexity of dynamical systems,
we can get a simple upper bound of

We remark that there is a paper

that claims a generalization of
[

to higher dimensional subvarieties.
However, the key lemma

is correct.
That said, we would like to note that the idea in
[

served as a useful inspiration for our approach to obtaining the present result.

2.
Upper bound of generalized gcd and proof of the main theorem

The goal of this section is to prove

.
As explained in the introduction, the idea to find an upper bound of
h_{Y}
in terms of

is as follows. Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be the ideal sheaf of
Y\subset X
.
If
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{I}}^{r}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH))\neq 0

By the following exact sequence

(2.1)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}^{r}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}/{\mathcal{I}}^{r}\longrightarrow 0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\dim H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{I}}^{r}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH))

(2.3)

\displaystyle\geq\dim H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH))-\dim H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}/{\mathcal{I}}^{r}).

The growing rate of

when

is well understood:
it is controlled by the self-intersection number
(H^{\dim X})
.
The first task is to find an upper bound of

and

.
Then we apply it to

i=0,\dots,l
.

Let
C>0
be as in
proposition

2.10
.
Then there is
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and
i=0,\dots,l
, we have

(2.105)

\displaystyle C((f^{n})^{*}(H^{N-i})\cdot H^{i})\leq(d_{N-i}(f)+\varepsilon^{\prime})^{n}.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be such that
(H^{N})/N!-\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
Recalling
eq.

2.31
, we consider the following condition

(2.106)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{(d_{N-i}(f)+\varepsilon^{\prime})^{n}}{i!}\frac{1}{(d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\varepsilon/2)^{n(N-i)}}\leq\frac{(H^{N})}{N!}-\varepsilon_{0}.

By
eq.

2.104
, enlarging
n_{0}
if necessary, this inequality holds for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Now pick arbitrary
n\geq n_{0}
.
We apply
proposition

2.6
to
X
,
f^{-n}(Y)
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(H)
, and
\varepsilon_{0}
.
Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{l}\in|H|
be a general sequence such that the conclusions of

proposition

2.6
and
proposition

2.10
hold.
Let
a
be the one obtained by
proposition

2.6
.
Then for any integers
r,m\geq a
such that

(2.107)

\displaystyle\frac{r}{m}\leq\frac{1}{(d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\varepsilon/2)^{n}},

we have

(2.108)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\tau(f^{-n}(Y)\cap H_{1\cdots i},H_{1\cdots i})}{i!}\bigg(\frac{r}{m}\bigg)^{N-i}

(2.109)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{(d_{N-i}(f)+\varepsilon^{\prime})^{n}}{i!}\frac{1}{(d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\varepsilon/2)^{n(N-i)}}\leq\frac{(H^{N})}{N!}-\varepsilon_{0},

where
H_{1\cdots i}=H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i}
.
Thus, if
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is the ideal sheaf of
f^{-n}(Y)\subset X
, we have

(2.110)

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH)\otimes{\mathcal{I}}^{r})\neq 0

whenever
r,m\geq a
and they satisfy
eq.

2.107
.
Therefore there is an effective divisor
D
linearly equivalent to
mH
containing
the closed subscheme defined by
{\mathcal{I}}^{r}
.
This implies

(2.111)

\displaystyle rh_{f^{-n}(Y)}\leq mh_{H}+O(1)

on
(X\setminus D)(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Taking
r,m\geq a
so that

(2.112)

\displaystyle\frac{m}{r}\leq(d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\varepsilon)^{n},

we are done.
∎

Proof of
theorem

1.5
.

First we remark that in the case
f
is a morphism,
we may also assume that
Y
is pure dimensional and the inclusion
Y\subset X

is regular embedding. (The condition
Y\subset X_{f}^{\rm back}
is vacuous since
X_{f}^{\rm back}=X
.)
Indeed, let
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
be the ideal sheaf of
Y\subset X
.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be an ample invertible
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module such that

{\mathcal{I}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}
is globally generated.
Let
{\mathfrak{d}}\subset|{\mathcal{L}}|
be the linear system defined by
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{I}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})
.
Then for a general sequence
D_{1},\dots,D_{N-l}\in{\mathfrak{d}}
,
we have
Y\subset D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{N-l}
as subschemes
and
D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{N-l}
is pure dimension
l
.
Since
X
is smooth and hence Cohen-Macaulay, the inclusion
D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{N-l}\subset X

is a regular embedding.
As
h_{Y}\leq h_{D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{N-l}}+O(1)
on
(X\setminus(D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{N-l}))(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
,
replacing
Y
with
D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{N-l}
, we may assume
Y
is pure dimensional and
the inclusion is a regular embedding.

Let
h_{H}
be any ample height function on
X
.
We may take it so that
h_{H}\geq 1
.
Let us take arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
h_{Y}
is bounded below outside
Y
and
O_{f}(x)
is generic,
it is enough to show

(2.113)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}\leq\varepsilon.

Pick and fix small
\delta>0
and
\eta\in(0,1)
close to
1
such that

(2.114)

\displaystyle d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\delta<\eta\alpha_{f}(x).

We apply
[
24
, Lemma 2.4]
.
Note that by
[
24
, Theorem 2.2]
,

\alpha_{f}(x)
is equal to one of the cohomological Lyapunov exponents of
f
.
Thus in the statement of
[
24
, Lemma 2.4]
, we can take

\mu_{l}(f)=\alpha_{f}(x)
.
Thus there are
C>0
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
,
i_{0},s_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that

(2.115)

\displaystyle h_{H}((f^{m})^{i+k}(f^{s}(x)))\geq C(\eta\alpha_{f}(x))^{mk}h_{H}((f^{m})^{i}(f^{s}(x)))

for all
i\geq i_{0},s\geq s_{0}
, and
k\geq 0
.

Take and fix
k\geq 0
such that

(2.116)

\displaystyle\frac{(d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\delta)^{mk}}{C(\eta\alpha_{f}(x))^{mk}}<\varepsilon.

By
proposition

2.12
, replacing
k
larger if necessary,
there are proper closed subset
Z\subset X
and
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
such that

(2.117)

\displaystyle h_{f^{-mk}(Y)}\leq(d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\delta)^{mk}h_{H}+\gamma

on
(X\setminus Z)(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Now for large
n\geq 0
, write
n=mq+s
with
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
s_{0}\leq s\leq s_{0}+m-1
.
Then by the functoriality of
h_{Y}
, we have

(2.118)

\displaystyle h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))

\displaystyle=h_{Y}(f^{mq}(f^{s}(x)))\leq h_{f^{-mk}(Y)}((f^{m})^{q-k}(f^{s}(x)))+\gamma^{\prime}

(2.119)

\displaystyle\leq(d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\delta)^{mk}h_{H}((f^{m})^{q-k}(f^{s}(x)))+\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}

where
\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant depends on
f^{mk}
and
Y
,
provided
n
is large enough so that

q\geq k
and
(f^{m})^{q-k}(f^{s}(x))\not\in f^{-mk}(Y)\cup Z
.
Here, to get the second condition, we use the genericness of
O_{f}(x)
.

Now by
eq.

2.115
, if
n
is further large enough so that

q-k\geq i_{0}
, we get

(2.120)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(d_{N-l}(f)^{1/(N-l)}+\delta)^{mk}}{C(\eta\alpha_{f}(x))^{mk}}+\frac{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}

(2.121)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\frac{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}.

Since
h_{H}(f^{n}(x))\to\infty
, we get

(2.122)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}\leq\varepsilon

and we are done.
∎

Example 3.1
.

Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle f\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\ (x:y:z)\mapsto(x^{2}y:y^{3}:z^{3}).

Then
f
is a dominant rational map with
I_{f}=\{(1:0:0)\}
.
We can see
d_{1}(f)=3
and
d_{2}(d)=6
.
Indeed, restricted on
{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{2}
, we have the following conjugation:

(3.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

where the vertical allow is

and
g(x,y)=(x^{2},y^{3})
.
Thus
d_{1}(f)=d_{1}(g)=3
and
d_{2}(f)=d_{2}(g)=6
.
Moreover, by this conjugation, it is easy to see that for any
p,q\in{\mathbb{Q}}
with
p,q>1
,
the
f
-orbit of
(pq,q)
is Zariski dense.
As the dynamical Mordell-Lang conjecture holds for endomorphisms on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}

[
32
]
,
such orbits are automatically generic.

Now let us consider
Y=\{(0:0:1)\}
.
Note that
Y\not\subset({\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})_{f}^{\rm back}
since
f|_{{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}^{-1}(Y)=(y=0)
as sets.
Let
h
be the naive height on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
Let us consider a point
P=(3:2:1)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Then its
f
-orbit is generic.
Explicitly, we have
f^{n}(P)=(3^{2^{n}}2^{3^{n}-2^{n}}:2^{3^{n}}:1)
.
Thus

h(f^{n}(P))=\log 3^{2^{n}}2^{3^{n}-2^{n}}
and
\alpha_{f}(P)=\lim_{n\to\infty}h(f^{n}(P))^{1/n}=3>\sqrt{6}=d_{2}(f)^{1/2}
.
But we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(P))}{h(f^{n}(P))}=\frac{\log 2^{3^{n}-2^{n}}}{\log 3^{2^{n}}2^{3^{n}-2^{n}}}\longrightarrow 1

as
n\to\infty
.
This example shows
the condition

is necessary in

.

Example 3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle f\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\ (x:y:z)\mapsto(x^{2}y:y^{3}+x^{2}y+xz^{2}:z^{3}).

Then

is a dominant rational map with
I_{f}=\{(1:0:0)\}
.
We can see

and
d_{2}(f)=7
.
Moreover if we identify
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}=(z\neq 0)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
,

is a finite morphism.
Therefore, we get
({\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})_{f}^{\rm back}={\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
Thus for any

, and

with

being generic and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}=0,

where
h_{H}
is any ample height on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
Note that genericness follows from Zariski density since the dynamical Mordell-Lang conjecture
is known for
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}

[

]
.
Note also that if

is not reduced, we may replace it with its reduced scheme to prove the limit is zero.
If

is reduced, the inclusion into
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}

Example 3.3
.

Let
N\geq 2
.
Let
A=(a_{ij})\in M_{N}({\mathbb{Z}})
be an
N\times N
integer matrix with
\det A\neq 0
.
Then
A
induces a morphism

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N},(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\mapsto(x_{1}^{a_{11}}\cdots x_{N}^{a_{1N}},\dots x_{1}^{a_{N1}}\cdots x_{N}^{a_{NN}}).

Let
f\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}

be the induced rational map, where we identify

{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\mapsto(x_{1}:\cdots:x_{N}:1)
.
Let us check if the assumptions of
theorem

1.5
hold for this map.

The dynamical degrees of
f
is calculated as follows.
Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
be the eigenvalues of
A
counted with multiplicity and ordered so that

|\lambda_{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|\lambda_{N}|
.
Then we have
d_{i}(f)=|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{i}|

[
17
, Theorem 1]
.

By
[
29
]
, Kawaguchi-Silverman conjecture is known for
f|_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}}
.
That is, for any point
x\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}
with Zariski dense orbit,
we have
\alpha_{f}(x)=d_{1}(f)
.
Moreover, since
f
is étale on
{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}
, the dynamical Mordell-Lang conjecture
holds for it and thus Zariski dense orbit is automatically generic.

Finally, as
f|_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}

is finite,

Therefore if

, for any point

with Zariski dense orbit,

(3.7)

\displaystyle d_{N}(f)^{1/N}=|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{N}|^{1/N}<|\lambda_{1}|=d_{1}(f)=\alpha_{f}(x).

Thus for any

-dimensional
Y\subset{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}^{N}
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_{Y}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}=0,

where
h_{H}
is any ample height on
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
Note we can use the same argument as in
example

to treat non-reduced

.

When constructing examples, finding explicit generic or Zariski dense orbit is
often the most difficult part.
Here is one fact that might be helpful.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of dimension
N\geq 2

and
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
a quasi-finite morphism.
Suppose
f
is
1
-cohomologically hyperbolic, i.e.
d_{1}(f)>d_{2}(f)
.
Then for any point
x\in X(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, if
\overline{\alpha}_{f}(x)=d_{1}(f)
, then the orbit
O_{f}(x)

is Zariski dense.
In particular, if the dynamical Mordell-Lang conjecture holds for
f
(e.g. if
f
is étale or
X={\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
),
then
O_{f}(x)
is generic.

Here
\overline{\alpha}_{f}(x):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max\{1,h_{H}(f^{n}(x))\}^{1/n}

for any ample height function
h_{H}
on a projectivization of
X
.

Proof.

The proof is a minor modification of the proof of
[
25
, Proposition 4.6]
.
Suppose
O_{f}(x)
is not Zariski dense.
Then there is an irreducible component
Z
of
\overline{O_{f}(x)}
such that

f^{k}(Z)\subset Z
and
f^{k}\colon Z\longrightarrow Z
is dominant for some
k\geq 1
.
Let
s\geq 0
be such that
f^{s}(x)\in Z
.
Then

(3.9)

\displaystyle\overline{\alpha}_{f}(x)=\overline{\alpha}_{f^{k}}(f^{s}(x))^{1/k}\leq d_{1}(f^{k}|_{Z})^{1/k}\leq d_{1+\codim Z}(f^{k})^{1/k}<d_{1}(f),

which is a contradiction.
Here for the first equality, we use
[
25
, Lemma 2.7]
.
For the first inequality, see
[
25
, Proposition 2.6]
.
The second inequality follows from
[
25
, Lemma 2.3]
.
The last inequality follows from the fact
d_{1+\codim Z}(f^{k})^{1/k}=d_{1+\codim Z}(f)
, the assumption
d_{2}(f)<d_{1}(f)
,
and the log concavity of dynamical degrees.
∎

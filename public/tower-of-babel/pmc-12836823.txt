A study on the correlation between novel metabolic and inflammatory-derived indicators (TyG, THR, NHR, UHR) and overweight older adult patients with T2DM
Authors: Xiaoxiang Zhang, Jiaojiao Li, Manjie Wang, Tiantian Tang, Xinyu Sun, Cancan Hui, Yuwei Cheng, Xiaoming Kong, Yan Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the correlation between novel metabolic and inflammatory-derived Indicators (TyG, THR, NHR, UHR) for overweight in older adult type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) patients.MethodsThis study enrolled 183 hospitalized older adult T2DM patients (aged ≥ 60 years) from 2023 to 2024. Participants were stratified into overweight (24 ≤ BMI < 28, n = 85) and normal-weight groups (18.5 ≤ BMI < 24, n = 98). Clinical parameters including TyG, THR, NHR, and UHR were calculated. Statistical analyses involved t-tests, logistic regression, Spearman correlation, and ROC curves.ResultsKey metabolic/inflammatory derived indices (TyG, THR, NHR, UHR) were significantly elevated in overweight group versus normal group (all P < 0.05). BMI showed positive correlations with TyG, THR, NHR, and UHR (all P < 0.05). Logistic regression confirmed the TyG, THR, NHR and UHR index are significantly associated with the development of overweight in T2DM patients (all P < 0.05). After adjusting for confounders, TyG demonstrated the highest predictive accuracy for overweight status (AUC = 0.732), followed by THR, NHR and UHR.ConclusionTyG, THR, NHR and UHR are significantly associated with overweight in older adult T2DM, potentially serving as simple screening tools for early metabolic risk stratification and weight management.

Background

Overweight is strongly associated with the development of insulin resistance and diabetes. The coexistence of overweight and diabetes can exacerbate metabolic disorders and trigger systemic chronic inflammatory responses [1]. The results of a meta-analysis conducted by Peking University People’s Hospital indicate that over the past 35 years, patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) have frequently experienced an increase in baseline BMI levels following improved blood glucose control [2]. Compared to T2DM patients with normal weight, those with obesity exhibit a significantly higher risk of developing complications [3]. Studies have demonstrated that diabetes is associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality in patients, and both outcomes are closely correlated with body mass index (BMI) [4]. Obesity is the primary contributor to disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) associated with diabetes, imposing a substantial burden on both society and families [5]. Some studies indicate that patients with diabetes begin to develop chronic inflammation during the overweight stage [6]. Consequently, addressing the issue of being overweight in patients with diabetes is beneficial for the prevention and management of diabetes-related chronic complications.

Insulin resistance (IR) has long been recognized as a critical risk factor for the onset and progression of chronic diabetic complications. Currently, the gold standard for diagnosing IR is the hyperinsulinemic euglycemic clamp (HEC) technique introduced by DeFronzo et al. [7] However, its complex procedural requirements limit its applicability in clinical settings. The widely used homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) serves as an alternative method to evaluate the extent of insulin resistance. Nonetheless, it possesses several limitations. For instance, it depends solely on fasting data, fails to capture dynamic changes in insulin sensitivity, and exhibits reduced accuracy in cases of severe pancreatic beta-cell dysfunction or when combined with severe obesity [8].

In recent years, several novel metabolic-related derived indicators have been identified as being correlated with insulin resistance and lipid metabolism. For instance, the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index serves as an effective evaluation tool for IR and is strongly associated with the incidence of obesity [9].Additionally, the triglyceride-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (THR) has been demonstrated to be linked to lipid metabolism and the risk of cardiovascular diseases [10].

Furthermore, there are inflammation-related derived indicators, such as the lymphocyte-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (LHR), neutrophil-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (NHR), and monocyte-to-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (MHR). These markers exhibit strong correlations and are more sensitive in reflecting the chronic inflammatory state within the body [11].

In older adult patients with T2DM, age-related factors and degenerative changes in the body contribute to an increasing complexity of chronic diabetic complications and comorbidities. Furthermore, obesity in older adult patients with diabetes can impair glycemic and lipid control, thereby elevating the risk of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. Currently, there is limited research on metabolic and inflammatory markers such as TyG, LHR, NHR, and THR in older adult T2DM patients with obesity or overweight. Notably, studies focusing specifically on overweight older adult patients with diabetes remain particularly scarce. This study aims to investigate the relationship between these metabolic and inflammatory markers and overweight older adult T2DM through clinical analysis, while assessing their early diagnostic and predictive potential for metabolic disorders and chronic inflammation in overweight T2DM patients. The findings will provide an evidence-based foundation for the early prevention and management of obesity-associated complications in older adult patients with diabetes.

Subjects and methods

Research subjects

The study subjects were older adult T2DM patients hospitalized in the Department of Geriatric Endocrinology at the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University from 2023 to 2024.Inclusion criteria: (1) Meet the T2DM diagnostic criteria established by the American Diabetes Association (ADA) [12]; (2) Aged 60 years or older; (3) Having completed nine years of compulsory education, with demonstrated proficiency in language comprehension, reading, writing, and the ability to communicate effectively; (4) Providing voluntary participation after reading and signing the informed consent form.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Presence of acute infections or chronic inflammatory conditions, including but not limited to chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), rheumatic disorders, and immune-mediated diseases; (2) BMI > 28 or BMI < 18.5; (3)Coexistence of severe chronic diabetic complications; (4) Occurrence of Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic Syndrome or Diabetic ketoacidosis; (5) Diagnosis of acute critical illnesses, such as acute myocardial infarction, unstable angina, heart failure, or malignant arrhythmias; (6) Evidence of severe anemia, thyroid dysfunction, autoimmune diseases, or malignancies; (7) Individuals with inadequate glycemic control; (8) Participants with cognitive impairment who are unable to cooperate with the research team; (9) Patients demonstrating poor adherence to treatment protocols; (10) Subjects with incomplete clinical records or follow-up data.

Detailed methods

General data collection

Gather the age, gender, diabetes duration, blood pressure, height and weight of all study participants. Calculate the BMI. Measure waist circumference and hip circumference to determine the waist-to-hip ratio (WHR). Conduct statistical analysis of lifestyle factors, including smoking status and alcohol consumption patterns. Collect medication data from the research subjects, including whether they took Statins for lipid lowering or Metformin. And the prevalence of diabetes complications among all research subjects was also subjected to statistical analysis, including diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN), diabetic peripheral vascular disease (DPVD), and diabetic kidney disease (DKD).

Collection of laboratory data

All research subjects fasted for 10 h, and the following morning, 5 ml of venous blood was drawn while they were in a fasting state. Routine blood tests were conducted to measure white blood cell count (WBC), monocyte count(monocytes), lymphocyte count(lymphocytes), neutrophil count(neutrophils), red blood cell count (RBC) and hemoglobin levels (HGB). Biochemical and lipid indicators include glycated hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c.), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), serum uric acid (UA), triglyceride (TG), high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-C), free fatty acid (FFA) and fasting insulin (FINS).

Diagnostic criteria for overweight

As outlined in the " Chinese Guidelines for the Clinical Management of Obesity (2024 Edition)” issued by the Medical Administration Department of the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China, BMI greater than or equal to 24 is classified as overweight, BMI greater than or equal to 24 is defined as obese, and BMI below 18.5 is categorized as underweight.

In terms of diabetes complications, the proportions of patients with DPN, DPVD and DKD in the normal group were 53.5%, 59.6% and 25.3% respectively. In the overweight group, the proportions of patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy, diabetic peripheral vascular disease and diabetic nephropathy were 65.5%, 94.0% and 39.3% respectively. There was a statistical difference in the presence of diabetic peripheral vascular disease and diabetic nephropathy between the two groups (P < 0.05) (See Table 3).

Comparison of metabolic and inflammatory-derived indicator results between two groups

The overweight group exhibited significantly higher values in the UHR, THR, NHR and TyG index compared to the normal group. Difference was statistically significant (P < 0.05) (see Table 2). Notably, after adjusting for multiple comparisons using the Benjamini-Hochberg FDR procedure, the significant differences in the TyG, THR and NHR indices between groups remained robust (Q < 0.05) (see Table 2 for Q-values).

Logistic regression analysis of the associated indicators in overweight older adult patients with T2DM

Discussion

This research undertook a cross-sectional analysis of data collected from 183 patients withT2DM. The analysis uncovered a notable association between overweight status and multiple metabolic and inflammation-derived indicators. Regarding the metabolic-derived indicators, including the TyG, UHR, and THR indices, in older adult T2DM patients who were overweight, these values were significantly higher compared to those in the normal group. Additionally, these indices were independently and positively correlated with BMI. Logistic regression analysis and ROC curves were employed, and the results further validated that the TyG, UHR, and THR indices exhibit excellent predictive efficacy for T2DM in combination with overweight. This finding implies their potential utility as screening tools. Furthermore, the inflammation-related derived indicators, such as the LHR, NHR, and MHR, were also positively correlated with BMI. This provides a novel perspective for the early identification and intervention of older adult T2DM cases complicated by overweight.

In the analysis of general indicators, the overweight rate was higher among men (48%) than among women (43%). However, in the subgroup of individuals aged 70–74 years, the overweight rate among women increased markedly to 67%, significantly exceeding that of men (48%). This may be attributable to alterations in lipid distribution resulting from the decline in estrogen levels post-menopause. This finding aligns with the results reported in other studies focusing on elder populations in Asia [13].

It is noteworthy that individuals with comorbid diabetes and overweight exhibit a significantly elevated risk of cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality. This phenomenon may be attributed to the synergistic interplay between metabolic disturbances, such as lipotoxicity and glucose homeostasis imbalance, and chronic inflammatory processes, including the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and augmented oxidative stress [5]. In this study, traditional weight-related metabolic and inflammatory markers, including TG, HDL-C, HbA1c, and neutrophil count, demonstrated correlations with BMI that were consistent with prior studies [14]. And these markers exhibited significant differences between the overweight group and the normal group. These findings indicate that even during the overweight stage, patients may already experience glycolipid metabolic disturbances and a low-grade inflammatory state, which could serve as early indicators of increased cardiovascular risk [4].

In recent years, research on metabolic and inflammatory-derived indicators, such as the TyG index and NHR, has gradually expanded in diabetes complications and other metabolic diseases. The TyG index was initially proposed and utilized by Simental-Mendia et al. in a large-scale cross-sectional study of healthy individuals conducted in 2008 [15]. Subsequent studies have demonstrated its effectiveness in assessing IR [16] and it has been shown to be associated with diabetes combined with overweight, coronary artery disease, and kidney disease [10, 17, 18]. An elevated TyG index reflects the synergistic interplay between lipid metabolism disturbances and glucose homeostasis imbalance [8]. Carolina Campos Muñiz et al. conducted a retrospective study on 3195 patients with T2DM and confirmed that, compared to normal individuals, the TyG index of patients with T2DM was significantly higher, and it had a better diagnostic efficacy for type 2 diabetes, with an AUC of 0.934, 95% CI: 0.924–0.924 [19]. The research conducted by João Carlos Locateli et al. also confirmed that the TyG index is an excellent indicator for evaluating IR. In their study of 345 overweight adolescents, the TyG index was positively correlated with HOMA-IR, and the AUC of the TyG index was 0.74 [20]. In this study, the TyG index exhibited significant differences between the overweight group and the normal group, and displayed a strong positive correlation with BMI. The ROC curve analysis further confirmed that the TyG index possesses good diagnostic performance for older adult patients with T2DM and overweight, with results consistent with prior studies [21]. The robust correlation between the TyG index and IR, as well as overweight status, suggests that it may be linked to dysregulation of adipokines and enhanced oxidative stress [17, 22].

As an inflammatory-derived indicator, the NHR demonstrated a robust correlation with BMI in this study. It exhibited a significant upward trend in the overweight group, indicating the synergistic interplay between neutrophil-mediated inflammatory responses and lipid metabolism disturbances. The potential mechanism may involve excessive FFAs released from adipose tissue, along with pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β, which induce ectopic lipid deposition. This process impairs mitochondrial, endoplasmic reticulum, and lysosomal functions in multiple organs, including the liver, muscles, and pancreas, subsequently leading to the release of excessive pro-inflammatory substances. Consequently, systemic inflammation is triggered, exacerbating IR [6]. Furthermore, the application of HEC technology in patients with metabolic syndrome has confirmed that peripheral insulin resistance is associated with proteomic changes linked to inflammation [23]. Song et al. also conducted a study on 432 patients with T2DM and proved that higher NHR and MHR were independent risk factors for T2DM combined with peripheral vascular disease (P < 0.001) [24]. Hao et al. also demonstrated that when NHR ≥ 4.32, the sensitivity for predicting ACS in patients with T2DM was 65.45% and the specificity was 66.19%. [AUC = 0.722, p < 0.001] [25].

Other metabolic and inflammatory-derived indicators also exhibited a significant correlation with overweight status in older adult patients with T2DM. For instance, the elevation of the UHR (UA/HDL-C) index is not only associated with metabolic abnormalities but may also contribute to the progression of diabetic nephropathy and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease via chronic inflammatory pathways [26, 27], which also suggests the importance of uric acid control in older adult patients with diabetes. And the research conducted by Zhou et al. has confirmed that UHR can not only reflect the degree of IR in patients with type 2 diabetes, but also can serve as an independent risk factor for IR in the general population (for males, AUC = 0.703; for females, 0.747; both P < 0.01) [28]. Wang et al.‘s study on 1450 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus confirmed that the UHR ratio is an independent risk factor for diabetic retinopathy (DR) in patients with T2DM, and can be used as a readily available indicator that takes into account both metabolic and inflammatory states, for the early detection of DR [29]. The THR (TG/HDL-C) indirectly reflects the risk of atherosclerosis by indicating lipid metabolism disturbances [10], showing high consistency with the insulin resistance characteristics observed in obese patients. The research conducted by Danladi Ibrahim Musa and others has confirmed that the likelihood of an increase in the TG/HDL-C ratio in teenagers with hypertension or abdominal obesity is 3.9 times that of healthy peers (95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.37–10.94, P = 0.011) [30]. Che et al.‘s study, which involved 403,335 individuals from the UK Biobank, also confirmed that an increase in the baseline THR index is closely associated with an elevated risk of cardiovascular diseases (CVD) [10]. And the increase of the MHR (monocytes/HDL-C) may accelerate the development of atherosclerosis by promoting chronic inflammation and oxidative stress responses [31]. Therefore, the integrated application of multiple metabolic and inflammatory-derived indicators may provide a more comprehensive foundation for early metabolic risk stratification in older adult T2DM patients with overweight.

Furthermore, this study demonstrated that, metabolic and inflammatory-derived indicators exhibited a stronger linear correlation with BMI in correlation analysis. This implies that abnormalities in glucose and lipid metabolism, as well as chronic inflammatory responses, may already be present even prior to meeting the diagnostic criteria for overweight (BMI < 24.5). Additionally, as BMI increases, the associated metabolic disturbances and inflammatory responses progressively worsen. These findings suggest that metabolic and inflammatory disorders are already established in older adult T2DM patients during the early stages of transitioning to overweight status. This implies that the optimal timing for weight management, lipid-lowering, and anti-inflammatory interventions in older adult T2DM patients could be advanced to the overweight stage or even the normal weight stage, rather than postponing intervention until obesity is established. Notably, some studies have indicated that the TyG index exhibits predictive value in the older adult sarcopenic obese (SO) population [13], underscoring its broad applicability.

This study also has certain limitations that warrant acknowledgment. First, the cross-sectional design precludes the establishment of causal relationships. Second, the relatively limited sample size may reduce the generalizability of the findings. Moreover, the unique metabolic characteristics of the elderly population, such as those observed in SO, could potentially influence the correlations of the studied indicators. In future research, it will be essential to validate the predictive value of these derived indicators, such as the TyG and NHR indices, and to investigate their associations with cardiovascular endpoints, including myocardial infarction and stroke. Additionally, further exploration is required to determine whether lifestyle interventions stratified according to these derived indicators can enhance the long-term prognosis of patients with diabetes.

In conclusion, this study confirmed that metabolic and inflammatory-derived indicators such as UHR, THR, TyG, and NHR are significantly associated with overweight in older adult patients with T2DM. Among these indicators, the TyG index demonstrates relatively high clinical diagnostic value. These indicators not only provide a simple and cost-effective tool for the early identification of obesity but may also serve as a valuable reference for individualized treatment strategies. In the future, multi-center studies with large sample sizes could be conducted to further optimize their application scenarios and explore their associations with adverse cardiovascular events and other relevant outcomes. It is possible to improve dietary quality and prevent the onset and progression of T2DM complications [32], by integrating the emerging field of precision nutrition with public health nutrition strategies, thereby achieving comprehensive management of older adult patients with T2DM and overweight.

Conclusions

This study confirms that novel metabolic and inflammatory-derived indices (TyG, THR, NHR, UHR) are significantly associated with overweight in older adult T2DM patients. All indices were significantly elevated in overweight individuals and positively correlated with BMI. This implies that the optimal timing for weight management, lipid-lowering, and anti-inflammatory interventions in older adult T2DM patients could be advanced to the overweight stage or even the normal weight stage, rather than postponing intervention until obesity is established.

These indices provide accessible tools for early identification of metabolic-inflammation dysregulation in overweight elderly diabetics. Their clinical integration could enhance risk stratification and guide timely interventions to mitigate obesity-related complications in this high-risk population.

Shallow brambles
Nicolas Bousquet, Wouter Cames van Batenburg, Louis Esperet, Gwenaël Joret, Piotr Micek

Abstract
A graph class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} has polynomial expansion if there is a polynomial function f f such that for every graph G ∈ 𝒞 G\in\mathcal{C} , each of the depth- r r minors of G G has average degree at most f ⁡ ( r ) f(r) . In this note, we study bounded-radius variants of some classical graph parameters such as bramble number, linkedness and well-linkedness, and we show that they are pairwise polynomially related. Furthermore, in a graph class with polynomial expansion they are all uniformly bounded by a polynomial in r r .

Introduction

Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez
[
12
]
introduced the notion of graph classes with bounded expansion as a way to capture graphs that are sparse at all scales.
To measure sparsity, they consider a refinement of the usual notion of graph minors, namely
shallow minors
.
For
r\geqslant 0
, a graph

is a
depth-

minor
of a graph

by contracting pairwise vertex-disjoint connected subgraphs, each

The invariant

is then defined as follows:

\nabla_{r}(G):=\max\left\{\frac{|E(H)|}{|V(H)|}:H\preccurlyeq_{r}G\right\}.

In other words,

is the maximum edge density of a depth-

minor of

A graph class

if there is a function

for every

and every integer

.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{C}
has

if

can be taken to be a polynomial.

The theory of graph classes with bounded expansion is rich and has a number of connections with other areas.
There are several characterizations of the notion of bounded expansion using different graph parameters.
Among them, the generalized coloring numbers play a special role as they are usually some of the easiest parameters to work with in practice, see the recent survey of Siebertz
[
17

.
In this note, we focus on strong coloring numbers, which are defined as follows.
Given a linear order

Note that

This is one of the first characterizations of classes with bounded expansion, among many.

Moreover, if

Related work

The topic of this paper bears some superficial similarities with the Coarse Graph Theory project initiated recently by Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu
[
6
]
, which aims at revisiting structural graph theory (and in particular the theory of graph minors) from the point of view of metric theory. A
fat
K_{t}
-minor
or
metric
K_{t}
-minor
in a graph
G
in this context is a minor-model of
K_{t}
in
G
such that the branch vertices are far apart, and are connected by paths that are also far apart. Here all distances are considered in the graph
G
. A recent paper by Dragan
[
1
]
on related topics explores the minimum radius
r
such that for every bramble
\mathcal{B}
in a graph
G
, there is a ball of radius
r
in
G
that intersects all elements of
\mathcal{B}
. One of the results of
[
1
]
is that this minimum radius is within a small multiplicative constant of the
tree-length
of
G
, the minimum over all tree-decompositions of
G
of the maximum diameter of a bag.

This is somewhat orthogonal to the approach we take here, where minor-models and bramble elements have small radius (where distances are computed in the graph induced by the branch vertices or elements, not in the original graph). Nevertheless, this paper can be seen as an effort to understand what remains true if we introduce appropriate distance constraints in classical objects from graph minor theory such as minor-models, brambles, tangles, and well-linked sets.

It was pointed out to us by Michał Pilipczuk that our definition of a shallow bramble is related to the
cop-width
of a graph, a parameter recently introduced by Toruńczyk
[
18
]
. Note that very much like brambles (or heavens) in the helicopter version of the cops and robber game of Seymour and Thomas
[
16
]
, the existence of a depth-
r
bramble of order
k
provides a winning strategy for the robber against
k-1
cops in the version of the game by Toruńczyk when the robber has speed
2r+1
.

Main results

The following are classical results about the

Theorem 3

(
[

)
.

For every graph

(i)

\mathrm{scol}_{\infty}(G)=\mathrm{bn}_{\infty}(G)

Theorem 4
.

For every graph
G

,

(i)

\omega_{r}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{scol}_{4r+1}(G)

\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\leqslant(5r+1)\cdot(\omega_{5r+1}(G))^{2}

(iii)

\mathrm{link}_{r}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)

The proofs

We start with the following lemma mentioned in the introduction, showing that if we consider the set of all graphs,
the strong
r
-coloring numbers cannot be bounded from above by any function of
r
and the depth-
g(r)

Lemma 5
.

For all integers
d,r,s\geqslant 1
, there exists a graph
G
such that
\mathrm{scol}_{r}(G)\geqslant d
and
\mathrm{bn}_{s}(G)=2
.

Proof.

Due to a classical result of Erdős
[
4
]
, there exists a graph
G
with minimum degree at least
d
and girth at least
8s+4
. Then
\mathrm{scol}_{r}(G)\geqslant\mathrm{scol}_{1}(G)\geqslant d+1
.
It remains to show that
\mathrm{bn}_{s}(G)=2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a depth-
s
bramble in
G
.
Since any two bramble elements touch and each bramble element has radius at most
s
,
it follows that the graph
G(\mathcal{B})
induced by the union of the elements of
\mathcal{B}
has diameter at most
4s+1
. Since this is less than half the girth of
G
,
G(\mathcal{B})
must induce a tree. Trees have treewidth
1
, so every bramble (in particular every depth-
s
bramble) has a hitting set of size at most
2

[
16
]
. On the other hand, any edge
uv
gives rise to the depth-
s
bramble
\mathcal{B}:=\{u,v\}
that has order
2
, so
\mathrm{bn}_{s}(G)=2
.
∎

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
4
. We remark that several of the arguments draw inspiration from those in
[

]
for the

We start with item

of Theorem

Theorem 6
.

For every graph
G
and integer

,

\omega_{r}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{scol}_{4r+1}(G).

Proof.

The first inequality holds because every depth-

minor-model of a complete graph

is also a depth-
r
bramble of order
n
.

For the second inequality, let
\mathcal{B}
be a depth-
r
bramble of
G
and let
\pi
be an ordering of

.
For each bramble element
B\in\mathcal{B}
, let
v_{B}
be the minimum vertex of

\pi
, and let
v^{*}
be the maximum vertex among all
v_{B}
w.r.t.
\pi
. More formally,
v_{B}
is the vertex such that
\pi(v_{B})=\min_{w\in B}\pi(w)
and
v^{*}
is such that
\pi(v^{*})=\max_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\min_{w\in B}\pi(w)
.
Since
\mathcal{B}
is a depth-

\mathcal{B}
has diameter at most

there is a path

from

to

of length at most

. Traversing

starting from

be the first vertex such that
\pi(x_{B})\leqslant\pi(v^{*})
. (Note that

Then

is both a hitting set of

and a subset of

\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{scol}_{4r+1}(G)

Theorem 7
.

For every graph
G

\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\leqslant(5r+1)\cdot(\omega_{5r+1}(G))^{2}.

Proof.

We prove that

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a depth-

We call

•

\mathcal{M}
is a depth-
(5r+1)

•

Note that there exists at least one good model, since a single vertex defines one. Given a minor-model
\mathcal{M}

denote the union of the branch sets of
\mathcal{M}
, and let

denote the union of all bramble elements of

that are disjoint from

From now on, we fix a good model

The proof is done if

is non-empty, and from there we will derive a contradiction.

For every branch set
M_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
it holds that

|R(\mathcal{M}-M_{i})|>|R(\mathcal{M})|,

(1)

for otherwise we would have
|R(\mathcal{M}-M_{i})|\leqslant|R(\mathcal{M})|
and
|V(\mathcal{M}-M_{i})|<|V(\mathcal{M})|
, contradicting the minimality of
\mathcal{M}
.

Thanks to (
1
) we know that each branch set
M_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
satisfies the following key
property: There is a bramble element
B\in\mathcal{B}
that intersects
M_{i}
and avoids all other branch sets of
\mathcal{M}
. Take a shortest path
P_{i}
from
M_{i}
to the non-empty set
R(\mathcal{M})
through
M_{i}\cup B
. Let
x_{i}
be the endpoint of
P_{i}
in
R(\mathcal{M})
. Observe that
P_{i}
contains at most
2r+1
edges, and thus at most
2r+2
vertices. Let
Q_{i}
be
P_{i}
minus its endpoint in
M_{i}
(so
Q_{i}
contains at most
2r
edges).

Recall that the subgraph induced by
R(\mathcal{M})
has radius at most
3r+1
, so there exists a vertex
x^{*}\in R(\mathcal{M})
and for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
, a path on at most
3r+1
edges, included in
R(\mathcal{M})
and connecting
x^{*}
to
x_{i}
. Let
X\subseteq R(\mathcal{M})
be the union of the vertex sets of these
s
paths. Note that
X
is connected and contains at most
1+(3r+1)s
vertices.

Now define a new branch set
M_{s+1}
, consisting of
X
together with the paths
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s}
. Then the radius of
M_{s+1}
is at most
5r+1
, and
|V(M_{s+1})|\leqslant 1+(5r+1)s\leqslant 1+(5r+1)(t-1)
.
Moreover, by construction
M_{s+1}
is connected to all previous branch sets. We conclude that
\mathcal{M}+M_{s+1}
is a good model with
|R(\mathcal{M}+M_{s+1})|<|R(\mathcal{M})|
, contradicting the minimality of
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Theorem 8
.

For every graph
G
and integer
r\geqslant 0
,

\mathrm{link}_{r}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)

Proof.

Let
S
be a depth-
r

k
-linked set, where
k:=\mathrm{link}_{r}(G)
.
By definition of depth-
r

k
-linkedness, for each set
X\subseteq V(G)
of order
|X|<k
, there exists an
r
-ball
B_{X}
in
G-X
that contains more than half of the vertices of
S
. Let
\mathcal{B}:=(B_{X})_{X\subseteq V(G),|X|<k}
be the set of these balls. The elements of
\mathcal{B}
pairwise intersect since each of them contains more than half of
S
, and therefore
\mathcal{B}
is a depth-
r
bramble of
G
.
Furthermore, the order of
\mathcal{B}
is at least
k
, since any hypothetical hitting set
X
of order less than
k
would not hit the bramble element
B_{X}
. Thus
\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\geqslant k
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 9
.

For every graph
G
and integer
r\geqslant 0
,

\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\leqslant 2\cdot\mathrm{link}_{3r+1}(G).

Proof.

Let
S
be a minimum order hitting set for a depth-
r
bramble
\mathcal{B}
. It suffices to show that
S
is depth-
(3r+1)

(\lceil|S|/2\rceil)-
linked. To that end, let
Y
be a set of fewer than
\lceil|S|/2\rceil
vertices.

Since
Y
, being smaller than
S
, is not a hitting set for
\mathcal{B}
, there exists a bramble element
B\in\mathcal{B}
which is disjoint from
Y
. Because
B
is an element of a depth-
r
bramble, we can choose a vertex
u
in
B
such that (in the graph induced by
B
and thus also in
G-Y
) every vertex of
B
is at distance at most
r
from
u
. We let
U
denote the
(3r+1)
-ball in
G-Y
centered at
u
. It suffices to show that
U
contains more than half of
S
, which we will now do.

Suppose for a contradiction that
|U\cap S|\leqslant\lfloor\frac{|S|}{2}\rfloor
.
If there exists any, let
C\in\mathcal{B}
be a bramble element that is disjoint from
Y
. As
S
hits
C
, we may choose a vertex
c\in C\cap S
. Since
C
and
B
touch and
C
has radius at most
r
, there is a path in
G-Y
joining
u
and
c
of length at most
3r+1
, so that
c\in U\cap S
. In other words,
U\cap S
hits
C
.

It follows that
(U\cap S)\cup Y
is a hitting set for
\mathcal{B}
of size

|(U\cap S)|+|Y|<\lfloor\frac{|S|}{2}\rfloor+\lceil\frac{|S|}{2}\rceil=|S|
, contradicting the minimality of
S
.
∎

Theorem 10
.

For every graph
G
and integer
r\geqslant 0
,

\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{well}_{4r+1}(G).

Proof.

We prove that any minimum hitting set
S
for a depth-
r
bramble
\mathcal{B}
is depth-
(4r+1)
well-linked.
Suppose not, then there exist two equal-sized subsets
A,B
of
S
and a set
Y
of fewer than
|A|
vertices, such that
G-Y
does not contain any
A-B
path of length
\leqslant 4r+1
.
Consider the sub-bramble
\mathcal{B}_{Y}
of
\mathcal{B}
that contains all bramble elements of
\mathcal{B}
that are disjoint from
Y
. If both
A
and
B
intersect some (not necessarily the same) bramble element of
\mathcal{B}_{Y}
then these bramble elements touch, so then there must be a path of length at most
4r+1
between
A
and
B
in
G-Y
, a contradiction. So without loss of generality
\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{B}_{Y}}C\cap A
is empty. But then
(S-A)+Y
is a hitting set for
\mathcal{B}
, contradicting the minimality of
S
.
∎

Theorem 11
.

For every graph
G
and integer
r\geqslant 0
,

\mathrm{well}_{r}(G)\leqslant 4\cdot(1+\mathrm{link}_{3r}(G))^{2}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that any depth-
r
well-linked set
S
is depth-
3r

k_{S}
-linked, where
k_{S}:=\lfloor\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{|S|}\rfloor
.

Assume for a contradiction that there exists a set
X
of fewer than
k_{S}
vertices such that every
3r
-ball in
G-X
contains at most half of the vertices of
S
.

We first consider the case that
G-X
contains a
2r
-ball
B_{2r}(v)
, centered at some vertex
v
, containing more than
k_{S}
vertices of
S
. Let
B_{3r}(v)
be the
3r
-ball in
G-X
centered at the same vertex. Since
|B_{3r}(v)\cap S|\leqslant\lfloor|S|/2\rfloor
, we know that
S-(X\cup B_{3r}(v))
has size at least
|S|-(k_{S}-1)-\lfloor|S|/2\rfloor\geqslant k_{S}
.
Thus we can choose a subset
A
of
B_{2r}(v)\cap S
and a subset
B
of
S-(X\cup B_{3r}(v))
such that
|A|=|B|=k_{S}>|X|
. However, by construction there cannot exist any
A-B
path of length at most
r
in
G-X
, contradicting our assumption that
S
is depth-
r
well-linked.

We have thus reduced to the case that no
2r
-ball in
G-X
contains more than
k_{S}
vertices of
S
.

We now greedily build a collection of
r
-balls in
G-X
, collecting at least one new vertex from
S
at each step, and stopping as soon as the corresponding
2r
-balls (again in
G-X
and with the same centers) cover at least half of
S
. Let
U_{r}
denote the union of the
r
-balls, and let
U_{2r}
denote the union of the corresponding
2r
-balls.

Since each of the
2r
-balls contains at most
k_{S}
vertices from
S
, we need at least
(|S|/2)/k_{S}\geqslant k_{S}
balls. Combining this with the fact that each
r
-ball adds at least one new vertex of
S
, we conclude that

|U_{r}\cap S|\geqslant k_{S}.

On the other hand, note that

|U_{2r}\cap S|<\frac{|S|}{2}+k_{S},
since we stopped adding balls as soon as the union was at least
\frac{|S|}{2}
.
Therefore

|S-(X\cup U_{2r})|\geqslant|S|-(k_{S}-1)-\left(\frac{|S|-1}{2}+k_{S}\right)\geqslant k_{S}.

Thus we can choose a subset
A
of
U_{r}\cap S
and a subset
B
of
S-(X\cup U_{2r})
such that
|A|=|B|=k_{S}>|X|
. However, as in the first case, every
A-B
path in
G-X
must have length more than
r
, contradicting that
S
is depth-
r
well-linked.
∎

Theorem 12
.

For every graph
G
and integers
r\geqslant 0,t\geqslant 1
,

\mathrm{bn}_{r,t}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)\leqslant t\cdot\mathrm{bn}_{3r+1,t}(G).

Proof.

The lower bound on
\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)
is true because every depth-
r

t
-bramble is a depth-
r
bramble, so we only need to prove the upper bound.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a depth-
r
bramble of order
k:=\mathrm{bn}_{r}(G)
.
For each
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|<\lceil\tfrac{k}{t}\rceil
, we can choose a bramble element
B_{X}
of
\mathcal{B}
that is disjoint from
X
, since
X
is too small to be a hitting set of
\mathcal{B}
. Let
v_{X}
be a vertex of
B_{X}
such that
B_{X}
is contained in the
r
-ball centered at
v_{X}
. Now define
T_{X}
as the
(3r+1)
-ball in
G-X
centered at
v_{X}
.
We claim that
\mathcal{T}:=\left(T_{X}\right)_{X\subseteq V(G),|X|<\tfrac{k}{t}}
is a depth-
(3r+1)

t
-bramble of
G
of order at least
\lceil\tfrac{k}{t}\rceil
. (This then implies that
k\leqslant t\cdot\mathrm{bn}_{3r+1,t}(G)
, as desired.)

Indeed, consider
t
sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}
of size smaller than
\lceil\tfrac{k}{t}\rceil
.
Since
|\bigcup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant t}X_{i}|<k
, there is an element
B
of
\mathcal{B}
that is disjoint from
\bigcup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant t}X_{i}
. For each
1\leqslant i\leqslant t
, because
B_{X_{i}}
and
B
touch, are disjoint from
X_{i}
and both have radius at most

, we see that every vertex of
B
can be reached from
v_{X_{i}}
by some path in

of length at most
3r+1
, so that

.
Thus
B\subseteq\bigcap_{1\leqslant i\leqslant t}T(X_{i})
. In particular, the intersection of

is non-empty, so
\mathcal{T}
is indeed a depth-
(3r+1)

t
-bramble. Finally,
\mathcal{T}
does not have any hitting set

of size strictly smaller than
\lceil\tfrac{k}{t}\rceil
, because

would not hit the element

. So
\mathcal{T}
has order at least
\lceil\tfrac{k}{t}\rceil
.
∎

5
Final remarks

Since large treewidth (and hence large
\mathrm{scol}_{\infty}
) is equivalent to the existence of a large grid minor
[
14
]
, one may wonder what is the relation between
\mathrm{scol}_{r}
and depth-
r
grid minors for finite
r
, and whether it helps to answer the questions in this paper. For a graph
G
, let
\mathrm{grid}_{r}(G)
denote the largest integer
t
such that
G
contains the
t\times t
grid as a depth-
r
minor.

Any
t\times t
grid is a (depth-
0
) minor of
K_{t^{2}}
, so we have
\omega_{r}\leqslant(\mathrm{grid}_{r})^{2}
. However,
\mathrm{grid}_{r}
cannot be upper bounded by a function of
\omega_{r}
and
r
, as can be seen by considering large grids, which have no
K_{5}
-minor. Thus the behavior of
\mathrm{grid}_{r}
is really different from that of
\omega_{r}
,
\mathrm{bn}_{r}
,
\mathrm{link}_{r}
and
\mathrm{well}_{r}
, even when restricted to classes of polynomial expansion.

So could grid minors instead be closely related to strong colorings?
The answer is no again, in the sense that it is not possible to upper bound
\mathrm{scol}_{r}
in terms of
\mathrm{grid}_{r}
either.
The proof is very similar to that of Lemma
5
.
In fact, the same type of argument shows that for every
r,k\geqslant 1
and for
every
graph
H
which is not a forest, there are graphs without
H
as a depth-
k
minor but with arbitrarily large strong
r
-coloring number.

Thus we see that for finite
r
, the grid minors are not as intimately related to
\mathrm{scol}_{r}
as they are for infinite
r
.
A question that remains is whether this connection does survive when restricted to classes of polynomial expansion.

Question 13
.

Is it true that for every graph class
\mathcal{C}
with polynomial expansion, there exists a polynomial function
f
such that for every integer
r\geqslant 0
and every graph
G\in\mathcal{C}
,

\mathrm{scol}_{r}(G)\leqslant f(r,\mathrm{grid}_{\mathrm{poly}(r)}(G))?

We have seen that there is a duality between the bramble number and tree-width
[
16
]
. A natural question is whether a similar duality holds between the bounded radius variants of these parameters (in the tree-width case, we could ask for a tree-decomposition in which each bag has bounded radius). As above, large grids show that such duality cannot exist: the depth-
r
bramble number is bounded by a function of
\omega_{r}
, which is at most 4 for any planar graph (and in particular for grids). On the other hand, grids have arbitrarily large tree-width, regardless of any condition on the radius of the bags.

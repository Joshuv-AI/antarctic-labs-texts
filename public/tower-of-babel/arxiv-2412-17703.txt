Numerical study of refined conjectures of the BSD type
Juan-Pablo Llerena-Córdova

Abstract
. In 1987, Mazur and Tate stated conjectures which, in some cases, resemble the classical Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture and its p p -adic analog. We study experimentally three conjectures stated by Mazur and Tate using SageMath. Our findings indicate discrepancies in some of the original statements of some of the conjectures presented by Mazur and Tate. However, a slight modification on the statement of these conjectures does appear to hold.



Introduction

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve over the field of rational numbers of conductor
N
. By the Modularity Theorem (
[
Wil95
]
,
[
TW95
]
,
[
BCDT01
]
), we know that
E
is a modular elliptic curve. Denote by
f_{E}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
the rational newform of weight
2
attached to
E
by the Modularity Theorem. We define the “
+
” modular symbol attached to
E
at
\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
as

\lambda^{+}(a,b)\coloneqq\frac{\pi i}{\Omega_{E}}\left(\int_{i\infty}^{\frac{a}{b}}f_{E}(z)dz+\int_{i\infty}^{\frac{-a}{b}}f_{E}(z)dz\right),

where
\Omega_{E}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\int_{E(\mathbb{R})}|\omega_{E}|
and
\omega_{E}
denotes a Néron differential of
E
. As a consequence of the Theorem of Manin-Drinfel’d (
[
Man72
]
,
[
Dri73
]
) we know that
\lambda^{+}(a,b)\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Now, by the work of Tate
[
Tat74
]
we know that if
E
has split multiplicative reduction at a prime
p
, there exists a unique
q_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
, called the
p
-adic period of
E
, such that

E(\mathbb{C}_{p})\cong\mathbb{C}_{p}^{*}/q_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}},

as rigid analytic spaces and
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(q_{p})>0
, where
\mathrm{ord}_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic valuation normalized at
p

i.e.

\mathrm{ord}_{p}(p)=1
.

In
[
MT87
]
, Mazur and Tate defined, to which we will refer as, the
Mazur-Tate element at layer
M
in terms of the modular symbols attached to
E
(see Definition
2.3
).

Mazur and Tate stated refined conjectures of the “Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer type” concerning the Mazur-Tate element which, under certain restrictions, resemble the classical Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjectures
[
BSD65
]
and its
p
-adic analog, introduced in
[
MTT86
]
. Furthermore, in certain cases, the refined conjectures of the Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer type provide information on multiple
p
-adic periods simultaneously, along with the “interaction” between them (see
[
MT87
, p. 749]
). This is in contrast to the
p
-adic Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjectures which, in some cases, provide information of only one
p
-adic period
[
MTT86
, Chap. II, §10]
.

Besides the numerical evidence presented by Mazur and Tate at the moment of stating their conjectures, most of the numerical calculations found in the literature has been done by Portillo-Bobadilla, in his Ph.D. thesis
[
PB04
]
and in a subsequent article
[
PB19
]
. Specifically, Portillo-Bobadilla studied a slight variation of Conjecture 4 of
[
MT87
]
. The objective of the present article is to expand the numerical evidence of some of the conjectures presented in
[
MT87
]
.

We are mainly interested in Conjecture
6
of
[
MT87
]
, particularly when the layer
M
is a prime number
p
for which
E
has split multiplicative reduction. For an easier implementation in SageMath, we will show in Section
2
that Conjecture
6
of
[
MT87
]
has a “multiplicative” formulation, in the aforementioned case (see Lemma
2.9
). This “multiplicative” formulation can be stated as follows.

Conjecture 0.1
.

Assume that
E
has split multiplicative reduction at a prime
p
. Define
G_{p}\coloneqq\left(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}/\left<-1\right>
. Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
be the smallest subring such that
\{\lambda^{+}(a,p)\}_{a\in G_{p}}\subseteq R
and
\frac{\lambda^{+}(0,1)}{2\mathrm{ord}_{p}(q_{p})}\in R
. Also let
\tilde{q}_{p}\coloneqq q_{p}/p^{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(q_{p})}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
, so
\tilde{q}_{p}
can be naturally viewed in
\left(\mathbb{Z}/{p}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}
. Define the set of prime numbers
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
by the following property:
\ell\in S
if and only if
\ell\mid\#G_{p}
and
\ell^{-1}\notin R
. Then
\sum_{a\in G_{p}}\lambda^{+}(a,p)=0
and for all
\ell\in S
we have that

(1)

\prod_{a\in G_{p}}\pi_{\ell}(a)^{\lambda^{+}(a,p)}\equiv\pi_{\ell}(\tilde{q}_{p})^{\frac{\lambda^{+}(0,1)}{2\mathrm{ord}_{p}(q_{p})}},

where
\pi_{\ell}
denote the natural projection
\pi_{\ell}:G_{p}\rightarrow\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G_{p})
given by the Sylow decomposition of
G_{p}
. Furthermore, if
\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(E(\mathbb{Q}))>0
, then for all
\ell\in S
, both sides of equation (
1
) are
1
.

Remark 0.2
.

Note that both sides of equation (
1
) are well-defined. This comes from the fact that if
\frac{a}{b}\in R
, then
(b,\ell)=1
for all
\ell\in S
. So, the map
g\mapsto g^{b}
is an automorphism in
\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G_{p})
for all
\ell\in S
. Therefore, the element
g^{\frac{1}{b}}
can be defined as the preimage of
g
under the map
g\mapsto g^{b}
(see Lemma
1.3
for a more detailed explanation).

Conjecture
0.1
can be thought as a “multiplicative” formualtion of Conjecture 6 of
[
MT87
]
. Moreover, Lemma
2.9
implies that Conjecture
0.1
is equivalent to Conjecture
6
of
[
MT87
]
, when
E
has split multiplicative reduction at
p
and the layer of the Mazur-Tate element is
p
.

We used SageMath to verify numerically this conjecture for around
400,000
pairs
(E,p)
, where
E
is a rational elliptic curve with split multiplicative reduction at the prime
p
. Interestingly, our findings indicate that Conjecture
0.1
does not hold in general. In our observations, the failure of Conjecture
0.1
occurs when there exist primes
\ell\in S
such that
\ell\mid\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{Tor}}
(where
E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{Tor}}
denotes the torsion subgroup of
E(\mathbb{Q})
). However, the converse is not true. There are cases when there exists primes
\ell\in S
such that
\ell\mid\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{Tor}}
and equation (
1
) still holds for all
\ell\in S
. The failure of equation (
1
) for some primes
\ell\in S
implies that a larger subring
R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
is needed for Conjecture
0.1
to hold (see Remark
2.13
for an explanation of the effect that the ring
R
has on Conjecture
0.1
).

Also, the fact that Conjecture
0.1
does not hold in general, implies that Conjecture
6
of
[
MT87
]
also does not hold in general, because they are equivalent if
E
has split multiplicative reduction at
p
(see Lemma
2.9
). Furthermore, the degree of failure does entail that Conjecture
4
of
[
MT87
]
also does not hold in general (see the discussion following Conjecture
2.12
and Section
3
). In Section
4
, we present two examples of the calculations carried out to check Conjecture
0.1
and Conjecture
2.12
.

In spite of this, by adding the hypothesis that
E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{Tor}}^{-1}\in R
in Conjecture
0.1
we get a conjecture that does appear to hold, see Conjecture
3.4
. We present all our numerical findings in Section 3.

Finally, all the code and documentation used in this article can be found on:
https://github.com/JpLlerena/Numerical_study_of_refined_conjectures_of_the_BSD-type
.

).

In Section 3, we present our numerical findings and a slight variation of Conjecture

of

MT87
]
which do appear to hold (see Conjecture

and Conjecture

In Section

1.
The augmentation ideal of a group algebra

In this section,
G
will always denote a finite abelian group (we will use the multiplicative notation for the operation of
G
) and
R
will always denote a subring of
\mathbb{Q}
. Also, given a rational number
\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
we will always assume that
(a,b)=1
.

The
augmentation map

\mathrm{aug}:R[G]\rightarrow R
is defined as the
R
-linear map uniquely determined by
[g]\mapsto 1;\hskip 3.0pt\forall g\in G
. The
augmentation ideal of
R[G]
is defined as
I(R,G)\coloneqq\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{aug})
. Also, let
Q_{n}(R,G)\coloneqq I(R,G)^{n}/I(R,G)^{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Define

\displaystyle f:R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)

\displaystyle\rightarrow Q_{n}(R,G)

\displaystyle r\otimes\left(\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}[g]+I(\mathbb{Z},G)^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{g\in G}r\alpha_{g}[g]+I(R,G)^{n+1},

If we consider
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)
as an
R
-module, by extension of scalars, then the function
f
is a well-defined isomorphism of
R
-modules.

Proof.

The fact that
f
is a well-defined homomorphism is a direct calculation. The fact that
f
is bijective is a consequence of the following isomorphism of
R
-modules

\displaystyle R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}I(\mathbb{Z},G)^{n+1}

\displaystyle\rightarrow I(R,G)^{n+1}

\displaystyle r\otimes\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}[g]

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{g\in G}r\alpha_{g}[g]

where
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}I(\mathbb{Z},G)^{n+1}
is an
R
-module by extension of scalars.
∎

By the previous lemma, we can see that to study the group
Q_{n}(R,G)
it is sufficient to study the effect of tensoring
Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)
by
R
. Additionally, we will show that the tensor product
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)
can be described explicitly in terms of
\ell
-Sylow subgroups of
Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)
(see Proposition
1.4
).

Now, let us recall some well-known results.

Lemma 1.2
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the quotient
Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)
is a finite abelian group.

Proof.

We have that
X=\{[g]-[e];\hskip 3.0ptg\in G\}
is a basis of
I(\mathbb{Z},G)
(see
[
Joh97
, Exercise 8, pag. 159]
). So,
I(\mathbb{Z},G)^{n}
is generated by finite products of elements of
X
. Therefore,
I(\mathbb{Z},G)^{n}
is finitely generated. As a consequence,
Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)
is also finitely generated.

Finally, because
Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)
is finitely generated and every element has finite order (see
[
CT11
, Proposition 2.1]
), we can conclude that
Q_{n}(\mathbb{Z},G)
is a finite abelian group.
∎

Lemma 1.3
.

We have the following results:

(1)

If
G
is a
p
-group and
p^{-1}\in R
then
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G
is trivial.

(2)

If for all primes
p\mid\#G
we have that
p^{-1}\notin R
, then
G
has a natural structure of
R
-module. Furthermore, if we endow
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G
with the structure of
R
-modules by extension of scalars, then
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G\cong G
as
R
-modules.

Proof.

Item (
1
) follows from a direct calculation.

For item
(\ref{nontrivial})
we first have to define the structure of
R
-module on
G
. Note that for any
\frac{a}{b}\in R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
, we have that
(\#G,b)=1
. Thus
g\mapsto g^{b}
is an automorphism of
G
. So, for each
g\in G
there exists a unique

such that
h^{b}=g
, such

will be denoted by
g^{\frac{1}{b}}
. This allows us to define the action of
R

as follows: if
r=\frac{a}{b}\in R

then
r\cdot g\coloneqq g^{\frac{a}{b}}\coloneqq(g^{\frac{1}{b}})^{a}
. A direct calculation shows that this action gives

a structure of
R
-module
1
1

1

An example of this

-module structure is the following: Let

for some

-module is given by the usual multiplication i.e. if

Lastly, a direct calculation shows that, the map uniquely determined by

2.
Refined conjectures of the BSD type

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N\in\mathbb{N}
. By the work of Tate
[
Tat74
]
, we know that if
E
has split multiplicative reduction at a prime
p
, then there exists a unique
q_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
such that

E(\mathbb{C}_{p})\cong\mathbb{C}_{p}^{*}/q_{p}^{\mathbb{Z}},

as rigid analytic spaces and
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(q_{p})>0
, where
\mathrm{ord}_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic valuation, normalized at
p

i.e.

\mathrm{ord}_{p}(p)=1
. We will refer to the
q_{p}
of the previous equation as the
p
-adic period of
E
.

We will denote by
\omega_{E}
a Néron differential of
E
and by
\Lambda_{E}
the Néron lattice of
E
i.e.
\Lambda_{E}\coloneqq\{\int_{[\gamma]}\omega_{E};\hskip 3.0pt[\gamma]\in H_{1}(E(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\}
. Following the convention in
[
MT87
]
, we define
\Omega_{E}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\int_{E(\mathbb{R})}|\omega|
, which may differ from the smallest positive real period of
\Lambda_{E}
by a factor of
\frac{1}{2}
depending if
\Lambda_{E}
is rectangular or non-rectangular.

Denote by
\Gamma_{0}(N)
the Hecke subgroup of level
N
. Also, let
\mathcal{H}
denote the upper-half plane and
\mathcal{H}^{*}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
the extended upper-half plane.

In 2001, the Modularity Theorem was proven by the work of Breuil, Conrad, Diamond, and Taylor building upon the work of Wiles (see
[
Wil95
]
,
[
TW95
]
,
[
BCDT01
]
). As a consequence of the Modularity Theorem, we know that there exists a complex uniformization
\varphi:\Gamma_{0}(N)\setminus\mathcal{H}^{*}\rightarrow E(\mathbb{C})
such that

\varphi^{*}(\omega_{E})=2\pi ic_{E}f_{E}(z)dz,

where
f_{E}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is a newform of weight
2
and
c_{E}\in\mathbb{Q}
is the Manin constant, which is well-defined up to sign.

Definition 2.1

(
c.f.

[
MR23
, Definition 1.1]
)
.

We define the “
+
” modular symbol attached to
E
at
\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
as

\lambda^{+}(a,b)\coloneqq\frac{\pi i}{\Omega_{E}}\left(\int_{i\infty}^{\frac{a}{b}}f_{E}(z)dz+\int_{i\infty}^{\frac{-a}{b}}f_{E}(z)dz\right).

For simplicity, we will omit the superscript
+

i.e.

\lambda({a},{b})\coloneqq\lambda^{+}(a,b)
.

Note that
\lambda(a,b)=\lambda(-a,b)
for all
\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Also, by the Theorem of Manin-Drinfel’d (
[
Man72
]
,
[
Dri73
]
) we know that
\lambda({a},{b})\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
we have that
\lambda(a,b)=\lambda(a+b,b)
.

Proof.

This follows from a direct calculation, using the fact that
f_{E}(z)
is invariant under the map
z\mapsto z+1
.
∎

Given
M\in\mathbb{N}
let
G_{M}\coloneqq\left(\mathbb{Z}/{M}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}/\left<-1\right>
.

Definition 2.3

(
c.f.

[
MT87
, Section 1.2]
)
.

Fix an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and a natural number
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
be a subring containing
\left\{\lambda({a},{M})\right\}_{a\in G_{M}}
. We define the
Mazur-Tate element at layer
M
as

\theta_{E,M}\coloneqq\sum_{a\in G_{M}}\lambda({a},{M})[a]\in R\left[G_{M}\right].

If the elliptic curve is implicitly clear, we will omit the subscript

.
i.e.

\theta_{M}\coloneqq\theta_{E,M}
.

Note that
\lambda({a},{M})
is independent on the choice of coset in
G_{M}=\left(\mathbb{Z}/{M}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}/\left<-1\right>
. This follows from Lemma
2.2
and using the fact that
\lambda(a,M)=\lambda(-a,M)
. Therefore, the Mazur-Tate element is well-defined.

We define the vanishing order of
\theta_{M}\in R[G_{M}]
as

\mathrm{ord}(\theta_{M})\coloneqq\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\theta_{M}\notin I(R,G_{M}),\\
r&\text{ if }\theta_{M}\in I(R,G_{M})^{r}-I(R,G_{M})^{r+1},\\
\infty&\text{ if }\theta_{M}\in I(R,G_{M})^{r};\forall r\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

Fix
S_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
a subset of primes numbers such that if
p\in S_{m}
then
E
has split multiplicative reduction at
p
. Also, for each
p\in S_{m}
fix a positive number

and set

For each
p\in S_{m}
let
\tilde{q}_{p}\coloneqq q_{p}/p^{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(q_{p})}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
. Note that we have natural mappings

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\twoheadrightarrow\left(\mathbb{Z}/p^{e_{p}}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}\hookrightarrow\left(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}\twoheadrightarrow G_{M}.

This allows us to see
\tilde{q}_{p}
as an element of
G_{M}
.

Now, set
r\coloneqq\#S_{m}
. We will numerically study the following conjectures.

Conjecture 2.4

(
c.f.

[
MT87
, Conjecture 4]
)
.

Let

be the smallest subring containing
\left\{\lambda({a},{M})\right\}_{a\in G_{M}}
. We have that
\mathrm{ord}(\theta_{M})\geq r+\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(E(\mathbb{Q}))
.

Conjecture 2.5

(
c.f.

[

)
.

Assume that

Proof.

Remark 2.13
.

Note that with this reformulation we can see the effect that the ring

has on these conjectures. Because, as more primes

are invertible in

As mentioned in Remark

, in

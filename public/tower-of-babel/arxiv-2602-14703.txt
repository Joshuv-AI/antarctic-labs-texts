Abstract
A search is presented for Higgs boson production in association with a top quark–antiquark pair ( t \mathup{{{t}}} t ¯ \mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}} H \mathup{{{H}}} ) with the Higgs boson decaying to a charm quark–antiquark pair ( H → c ​ c ¯ {\mathup{{{H}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{c}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{c}}}}}}}}} ). The same process with a Higgs boson decay to bottom quarks, t \mathup{{{t}}} t ¯ \mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}} H \mathup{{{H}}} ( H → b ​ b ¯ {\mathup{{{H}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{b}}}}}}}}} ), is measured simultaneously. The analysis uses data from proton–proton collisions at 13 TeV collected with the CMS detector in 2016–2018, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 138 fb − 1 \,\text{fb}^{-1} . The observed t \mathup{{{t}}} t ¯ \mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}} H \mathup{{{H}}} ( H → b ​ b ¯ {\mathup{{{H}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{b}}}}}}}}} ) cross section relative to its prediction from the standard model (SM) is 0.91 − 0.22 + 0.26 0.91\,^{+0.26}_{-0.22} . The observed (expected) results are compatible at the 95% confidence level with a t \mathup{{{t}}} t ¯ \mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}} H \mathup{{{H}}} ( H → c ​ c ¯ {\mathup{{{H}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{c}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{c}}}}}}}}} ) cross section that is at most 7.8 (8.7) times larger than the SM expectation. Assuming all other Higgs boson couplings to be SM-like, this sets an observed (expected) upper bound on the charm quark Yukawa coupling modifier of 3.0 (3.3) times its SM value.

Introduction

In 2012, the CMS
[
5
]
and ATLAS
[
1
]
Collaborations discovered a new particle with properties consistent with those of the Higgs boson as predicted by the standard model (SM). Since then, there have been many studies on the couplings between the Higgs boson and the fermions, known as Yukawa couplings
[
6
,
2
]
. The current focus lies on the second generation, where the charm quark has the largest Yukawa coupling. Upper limits on this coupling are available from searches in several Higgs boson production channels, e.g. in Refs.
[
4
,
7
,
9
]
.

In this contribution, we present a new channel where the Higgs boson is produced in association with a top quark–antiquark pair (
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
) and then decays into a charm quark–antiquark pair,
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}{\mathup{{{H}}}}{}{}({\mathup{{{H}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{c}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{c}}}}}}}}}{})
. We include a measurement of the process
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
\mathup{{{H}}}
(
{\mathup{{{H}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{b}}}}}}}}}
), and use
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
\mathup{{{Z}}}
(
{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{b}}}}}}}}}
) and
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
\mathup{{{Z}}}
(
{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{c}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{c}}}}}}}}}
) to validate our results. The dominant background is
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
with additional jet radiation,
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+\text{jets}
. When those jets are charm- or bottom-flavoured, it is challenging to disentangle them from the signal processes. To do so, we use advanced machine learning techniques, both for jet flavour tagging as well as for event classification.

Analysis strategy

The search is performed using data collected by the CMS detector between 2016 and 2018, for a total integrated luminosity of 138
\,\text{fb}^{-1}
. We consider all three decay channels of the
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
pair: fully hadronic (0L), semileptonic (1L), and dileptonic (2L). We select events with a high number of jets (7, 5, and 4 in the respective channels), minimal missing transverse momentum of 20
GeV
in the 1L and 2L channels, and a sum of jet transverse momenta above 500
GeV
in the 0L channel. Jet flavour tagging is performed using the graph-neural-network-based algorithm
ParticleNet

[
12
]
. Based on its output scores, we categorize jets into light jets and several categories of charm- and bottom-flavoured jets, as shown in Fig.
1
. We require at least 3 jets tagged as
\mathup{{{b}}}
jets (categories
B1
–
B4
, 70% efficient) or
\mathup{{{c}}}
jets (categories
C1
–
C4
, 50% efficient), with at least 1 jet tagged as a
\mathup{{{b}}}
jet.

The selected events are fed into a transformer-based neural network called
Particle Transformer
(
ParT
)
[
13
]
. Its inputs are properties and pairwise features of the event objects (jets, leptons, missing transverse momentum). The network is trained in each channel separately to assign events a likelihood score for the considered signal and background classes. The signal classes are
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
\mathup{{{H}}}
(
{\mathup{{{H}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{c}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{c}}}}}}}}}
),
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
\mathup{{{H}}}
(
{\mathup{{{H}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{b}}}}}}}}}
),
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
\mathup{{{Z}}}
(
{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{c}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{c}}}}}}}}}
), and
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
\mathup{{{Z}}}
(
{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{}\rightarrow{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{b}}}}}}}}}
). The
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+\text{jets}
background is split into 5 classes, depending on the flavour of the extra jets. We assign
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
events with 1 (at least 2) additional b jet to the class
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+{\mathup{{{b}}}}
(
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+{\geq}2{\mathup{{{b}}}}
). Otherwise, events with 1 (at least 2) additional c jets fall into
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+{\mathup{{{c}}}}
(
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+{\geq}2{\mathup{{{c}}}}
). All remaining
\mathup{{{t}}}
\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}
events are classified as
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+\text{LF}
. In the 0L channel, we include an additional background class of events composed exclusively of jets produced through quantum chromodynamics (QCD) interactions, or QCD multijet events for short.

The
ParT
scores (denoted by
\mathcal{D}
) are used to further select and categorize events. We only consider events with a high sum of all signal scores
\mathcal{D}_{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}{\mathup{{{X}}}}{}{}}>0.6
and with
\mathcal{D}_{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+LF}<0.05
(0.02 in 1L). In the 0L channel, the QCD multijet background is reduced to negligible levels by selecting events with
\mathcal{D}_{QCD}<10^{-4}
. After these selections, we assign events passing
\mathcal{D}_{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}{\mathup{{{X}}}}{}{}}>0.85
to one of 4 signal regions (SRs), each enriched in one of the signal processes. All other events fall into one of 5 control regions (CRs) designed to constrain the normalizations of the five
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+\text{jets}
backgrounds.
We then perform a binned profile likelihood fit to data. In each category, the fitted distribution is the corresponding
ParT
discriminant. For each signal process, we fit a signal strength
\mu
: the ratio of the observed event yield to the SM expectation. The five
{{\mathup{{{t}}}}{}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}}{}+\text{jets}
background normalizations are also left freely floating. Other backgrounds are modelled with simulation. Experimental and theoretical uncertainties are included in the fit as nuisance parameters.

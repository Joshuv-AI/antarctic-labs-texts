Theoretical uncertainties for primordial black holes from cosmological phase transitions Preprint: SISSA 07/2025/FISI
Maciej Kierkla, Nicklas Ramberg, Philipp Schicho, Daniel Schmitt

Abstract
Strongly supercooled first-order phase transitions have been proposed as a primordial black hole (PBH) production mechanism. While previous works rely on simplified models with limited thermodynamic precision, we stress that reliable theoretical PBH predictions require precise nucleation dynamics within realistic, classically conformal extensions of the Standard Model. By employing high-temperature dimensional reduction and computing the one-loop fluctuation determinants, we provide a state-of-the-art thermodynamic analysis and estimate the corresponding PBH abundance for classically conformal gauge-Higgs theories. Accounting for constraints from successful percolation and QCD chiral symmetry breaking, the parameter space where PBHs are viable dark matter candidates is severely limited.

Summary.
—The impact of theoretical uncertainties in computations of strongly supercooled FOPTs on the formation of PBHs can be summarized as follows:

•

Employing state-of-the-art thermodynamic methods,
we find the lowest values of
\beta/H_{\rm p}\sim\mathcal{O}(5)
for the slowest transitions in
the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{CW}}}
and
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{X}}}
models.
Whether percolation is successful highly depends on
the employed percolation criterion.
Specifically, criterion (
18
) predicts
a large no-percolation region in both models.

•

For benchmark points that predict incomplete percolation at
T_{\rm p}
,
the transition terminates at a lower temperature
T_{\mbox{\scriptsize shrink}}
. For the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{CW}}}
model, this can happen before the cosmic QCD transition occurs, while for
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{X}}}
we always find
T_{\mbox{\scriptsize shrink}}<T_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{QCD}}}
. QCD-sourced transitions always feature an enhanced inverse transition timescale due to the breaking of classical scale invariance through the chiral condensates.

•

These cosmological constraints severely limit the parameter space where
a sizable PBH abundance is generated.
Such abundance can be achieved only for the slowest transitions,
where
\beta/H_{\rm p}\lesssim 7.5
,
provided the percolation criterion (
19
) is satisfied.

The uncertainties we have studied arise primarily from thermodynamics.
In future work, we aim to refine our analysis by considering
the improved computation of FOPT-induced fluctuations
Franciolini et al. 2025
.
In addition, small values of
\beta/H_{\rm p}
are connected to characteristic bubble sizes of the order of the Hubble horizon.
In this limit, bubble nucleation and growth should be affected by the curvature of spacetime
Coleman and De Luccia 1980
;
Giombi and Hindmarsh 2024
, which manifests in the transition timescale.
Furthermore, classically conformal models can feature an extended period of matter domination
Ellis et al. 2020
which impacts the formation of PBHs.
Lastly, since the viable parameter space depends crucially on the percolation criterion, continued theoretical efforts are required to fully understand supercooled PTs and their remnants.

Fluctuation determinants

In our computation of the thermal bubble nucleation rate,
we evaluated the fluctuation determinants for
one-loop corrections from the scalar and gauge modes.
For the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{X}}}
conformal model,
we reuse the results from
Kierkla et al. 2025
. To compute the fluctuation determinant in the conformal
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{CW}}}

Ekstedt 2022a
;
Kierkla et al. 2025

Here, we briefly summarize the relevant operators.

The scalar-mode determinant containing one-loop scalar mode corrections is given by,

\displaystyle\det\nolimits_{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{$S$}}}}

Research trends and frontiers on risk factors of urinary stones: a bibliometric analysis from 2010 to 2023
Authors: Yongfu Zhong, Qing Zeng, Jiayou Yi, Tao Long, Juanwen Peng, Li Zhong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectiveThe occurrence of urinary stone disease (USD) is associated with several factors. However, no conclusive results have been reached regarding the treatment of USD risk factors. This study conducted a detailed bibliometric analysis of USD risk factors to determine research hotspots and provide future development directions.MethodsArticles and reviews on the USD risk factors published between 2010 and 2023 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. Co-occurrence and collaboration analyses of countries, institutions, authors, journals, references, and keywords were conducted using CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and R-Bibliometrix.ResultsA total of 436 articles on USD risk factors were included in the study. China and the United States have emerged as the leading countries in this field, with the Mayo Clinic having the highest amount of publication output. Among all the authors, Ferraro PM had the highest number of publications, while Taylor EN was the highly cited author. The journal with the highest number of publications was UROLITHIASIS. Scales reported the reference with the highest burst strength. The current citation themes in research on USD risk factors were ‘multifaceted relationship’ and ‘kidney stone disease recurrence’. ‘Medical-management’ was identified as a major keyword, indicating future research hotspots.ConclusionThis study employed bibliometric methods to conduct a comprehensive visual analysis of USD risk factors to show publication trends and popular topics in this area over the past decade. The results provide a valuable reference for future research on the risk factors of USD.

Introduction

Urinary stone disease (USD) is a chronic pathological process characterized by the formation of mineral deposits, most commonly calcium oxalate and calcium phosphate, in the kidneys. The prevalence of USD is similar to that of diabetes mellitus, with ∼1 in every 11 affected individuals. Between 1994 and 2010, the prevalence of USD in males nearly doubled, increasing from 6.3 to 10.6% [1]. Additionally, a significant increase in the risk of USD among children, women, and the black population was observed [2]. Currently, several studies have identified numerous risk factors for USD, including gender, microbiota, environmental exposure, and diet [3]. Some studies have also indicated that USD is associated with chronic diseases, such as chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and hypertension [4,5]. Furthermore, USD has become the costliest urological condition globally, with cases continuously increasing among insured populations [6]. With the increasing prevalence of conditions like hypertension and diabetes, the treatment costs for USD may increase by $1.24 billion annually starting in 2030 [7]. However, research findings on the treatment of USD risk factors are still unclear. Therefore, it is necessary to identify the risk factors of USD to provide an efficient treatment.

New technologies and research consistently update research hotspots and trends on the risk factors of USD. However, despite the large number of articles published by researchers from many countries in this field, no clear summary of USD risk factors has been provided. Therefore, a systematic analysis research on USD risk factors is necessary. Bibliometrics is a quantitative analysis technique based on literature that uses mathematical and statistical methods to illustrate the status and trends in related fields. It displays the outputs and citations of countries, institutions, and authors and identifies keywords in current hot topics and research frontiers [8]. Research on the risk factors of USD has recently attracted significant attention from scholars. A systematic overview and summary of USD risk factors from a bibliometric perspective can help researchers better understand the status of research in this field.

Although many studies on USD risk factors have been published recently, to the best of our knowledge, no bibliometric studies have been conducted in this field. This study uses bibliometric methods to statistically analyze relevant literature on USD risk factors from 2010 to 2023 to represent the significant innovation in this field. The analysis of the included literature covered the following key points: countries, institutions, authors and coauthors, journals and co-cited journals, co-cited references, and keywords. This study also utilizes CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and R-Bibliometrix to visualize the relevant knowledge maps and discusses the current status, hot topics, and frontiers of research in the field of USD risk factors, providing references for the prevention and management of USD risk factors in the future.

Materials and methods

Data collection

Web of Science hosts over 12,000 high-quality academic journals, making it a reputable and extensive database providing international academic resources [9]. The literature we retrieved came exclusively from the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC). To comprehensively and accurately retrieve relevant literature, the search terms were TS = (‘Urinary stones’ OR ‘urolithiasis’ OR ‘kidney stone’ OR ‘ureteral calculus’ OR ‘ bladder calculus ‘) AND TS = (‘hazards’ OR ‘hazard’ OR ‘risk factors’ OR ‘risk’ OR ‘dangerous factor’). The time span ranged from 1 January 2010 to 31 December 2023. After deduplication, we obtained 3000 pieces of literature. The literature types were limited to English journal articles and review papers. Through manual screening of the titles, abstracts, and keywords, we obtained 436 valid journal articles (including 364 journal articles and 72 review papers). The articles were saved in plain text format. Finally, we conducted a visual analysis of these articles using CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and R-Bibliometrix. Supplementary Figure 1 depicts the literature screening process.

Bibliometric analysis

Bibliometrics is a quantitative analysis technique based on literature, which utilizes mathematical and statistical methods to display the research progress and trends in a certain field by examining factors, such as the quantity and years of literature, authors, countries, institutions, and keywords. In this study, all data retrieved from WOSCC were imported into CiteSpace (version 6.1.R6), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and R-Bibliometrix to generate visualizations, which were then utilized for quantitative and qualitative analysis.

CiteSpace (version 6.1.R6) was employed to analyze the included literature, including analysis of countries and institutions, authors, co-cited journals, overlay visualization of journals, references, keyword clustering, and timeline analysis.

VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) was used to demonstrate the co-occurrence networks of journals and keywords, allowing for the evaluation of hot journals and keywords in the field. Each point in the co-occurrence graph represents a journal or keyword, which are clustered into different groups based on their types.

R-Bibliometrix was utilized to generate general information diagrams, author and journal publication diagrams, and keyword strategic diagrams for the included literature. Productivity of authors and journals is demonstrated by analyzing publications. The keyword strategic diagram can display different keyword themes in the field, facilitating a better understanding of the research status in this area.

Bibliometric analysis of co-cited references

The analysis of co-cited references helps us understand the high-quality literature widely recognized in the research on USD risk factors. CiteSpace parameters were set as follows: time slice (2010–2023), with each slice representing one year, node type (co-cited references), and selection criterion (top N = 50). Based on these parameters, a co-cited reference network graph was generated, comprising 568 nodes, 1235 connections, and a density of 0.0077 (Figure 5(A)). Table 2 summarizes the top ten co-citation counts of references. It can be observed from the table and graph that Scales (2012) [12] has the highest co-citation count (n = 44); Pearle (2014) [13] has the highest centrality among these ten references, indicating significant influence in this field. To further analyze the co-cited references, we constructed a co-cited reference clustering graph (Figure 5(B)). Generally, an average silhouette (S) value >0.7 indicates strong clustering results, while a modularity (Q) score higher than 0.3 indicates strong node grouping capability. In this study, the clustering network has S = 0.8922 and Q = 0.8513, indicating its reliability. Figure 5(B) illustrates 15 clusters, including themes, such as ‘pathogenesis diagnosis’, ‘multifaceted relationship’, and ‘low high-density lipoprotein cholesterolemia’. Among them, clusters #1 multifaceted relationship and #9 kidney stone disease recurrence have a larger number of nodes, and their cluster colors are yellow, indicating that these two clusters are the main topics of referenced literature in the current field. Finally, we compiled the top 20 cited references with the highest burst strength. As shown in Figure 5(C), light blue lines indicate references that have not yet been cited, dark blue lines represent references in the period of being cited, and the red portions indicate periods of sudden citation frequency bursts. The reference with the highest burst strength is Scales (2012) [12] (18.21), which once again demonstrates the widespread attention from scholars during a certain period. Additionally, special attention should be paid to references that are still in a burst citation period, such as Ferraro (2017) [14] and Sorokin (2017) [15], indicating that future research on USD risk factors may continue to focus on these cited references.

Bibliometric analysis of keywords

Keyword co-occurrence is an effective method for identifying hot topics in a specific field. In this study, a total of 1579 keywords were identified. To focus on analyzing the hot topics in the field of USD risk factors, we screened out 49 keywords with a frequency of at least 10 occurrences and merged them into clusters. As shown in Figure 6(A), nodes representing similar topics are grouped into clusters of the same color, resulting in six distinct clusters. In Figure 6(A), keywords are depicted as nodes, with node size indicating the frequency of occurrence. Keywords, such as ‘kidney stone’, ‘urolithiasis’, and ‘risk factors’ have relatively high frequencies. Figure 6(B) displays the top 20 bursting keywords, with burst strengths ranging from 2.32 to 4.74. Keywords like ‘children’, ‘oxalate’, and ‘calcium nephrolithiasis’ have been consistently prominent since 2010. Additionally, keywords, such as ‘kidney stone disease’, ‘impact’, and ‘exposure’ are still in the bursting stage.

We conducted a timeline analysis of the keywords (Figure 6(C)), where the size and number of nodes correspond to the frequency and quantity of keywords, respectively. It is observed that the keywords ‘genetics’ (ranked 11th) and ‘calcium oxalate’ (ranked 12th) have shown a higher frequency of appearance in recent years, indicating that these two keywords have garnered widespread attention recently. Finally, we generated a keyword topic map using R-Bibliometrix (Figure 6(D)), identifying a total of 250 keywords. The minimum cluster frequency was set to 25, with each cluster having 5 labels. A general overview of the entire map reveals that the upper-right quadrant represents motor themes, including clusters, such as ‘nephrolithiasis’ and ‘urolithiasis’. The upper-left quadrant represents niche themes, including clusters like ‘children’ and ‘pediatric urolithiasis’. The lower-left quadrant represents emerging or declining themes characterized by low centrality and density, with ‘hyperoxaluria’ being the main theme. The lower-right quadrant represents basic themes, comprising two clusters: one including ‘prevalence’ and ‘epidemiology’, and the other including ‘oxalate’ and ‘excretion’.

Discussion

General information

We collected 436 articles on USD risk factors published in the WOSCC from 1 January 2010 to 31 December 2023. Through bibliometric and visualization analyses using the CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and R-Bibliometrix software, we discuss the research results and advancements to determine the hot topics in the field. A quantitative analysis was conducted to obtain basic information on the annual publication volume, countries, authors, institutions, and journals. The analysis of the number of articles published on USD risk factors indicates a rapid growth in the literature over the past five years, suggesting that research on USD risk factors is currently a hot spot and garnering increasing attention from scholars.

Through a statistical analysis of publication output by country and institution, the study identifies key countries and institutions with significant influence and analyzes their collaborative relationships. China and the United States are the main countries researching USD risk factors, which may be attributed to the high prevalence of USD in both countries and their strong research capabilities. Among the top ten institutions in terms of publication output, five were from China, four were from the United States, and one was from Italy. The Mayo Clinic has the highest publication output; however, it lacks collaboration with other institutions. Additionally, there is also limited collaboration between Chinese and American institutions, which can hinder the long-term development of academic research. Therefore, close collaboration and communication among countries and institutions is essential to overcome academic barriers and advance research on USD risk factors.

As shown in Figure 3(B) and Supplementary Table 2, Ferraro is the most prolific author and has made significant contributions to the field of USD risk factors. He has conducted extensive research on various risk factors associated with USD, such as gender [16], vitamins [17], trace metals [18], and more. TAYLOR EN also stands out as a highly co-cited author. His published articles mainly focused on the period from 2000 to 2010, making him one of the earliest researchers in this field. Furthermore, although SCALES CD’s publication output from 2010 to 2023 was not high, it held a significant position in the co-cited authors and reference networks. This indicates that publication output is not the sole criterion for assessing authors’ influence.

An analysis of journals (Table 1) showed that J UROLOGY ranked second in amount of publication output and first in co-citation count, indicating its significant influence in the field of USD risk factors. Among the top 10 journals in terms of the number of publications, journal partitions ranged from 1 to 4; however, among the top 10 journals by co-citation count, except for UROL RES and UROLOGY, the rest were all high-quality journals in Quartile 1. This finding suggests that scholars tend to cite articles from high-quality journals. Therefore, these journals must be examined to understand the status of their research on USD risk factors. Figure 4(D) illustrates that research published in journals related to Molecular, Biology, or Genetics and Health, Nursing, or Medicine is often cited in medical and clinical journals. This indicates that current research on USD risk factors primarily focuses on the fields of medicine and clinical medicine.

Among the top 10 co-cited references, most were related to the epidemiology and risk factors of USD. This is because the growing prevalence of USD has led to the increased use of diagnostic techniques and medications, resulting in high economic burden [19]. Therefore, it is essential to analyze the risk factors of USD to reduce its prevalence. Among them, the study by Scales and others, published in 2012, garnered the most co-citations. Similarly, the top 20 co-cited references with the strongest bursts showed similar results, indicating that recent literature is primarily related to the risk factors of USD. Researchers have identified multiple risk factors directly associated with USD, significantly contributing to USD prevention efforts.

Research hotspots and frontiers

Co-citation Reference Cluster Network Maps can help us understand the main themes of the referenced literature during a certain period. As shown in Figure 5(B), the current themes of referenced literature on USD risk factors are ‘multifaceted relationship’ and ‘kidney stone disease recurrence’, which indirectly reflect the current research topics in this field. For instance, the most recent literature illustrates the multifaceted relationship between vitamin D and calcium supplements and the recurrence of kidney stone disease and provides new recommendations based on the pros and cons of such treatments [20]. A meta-analysis comprehensively assessed the risk factors for recurrence of kidney stone disease and identified 12 factors associated with recurrent kidney stones [21].

In addition to co-citation reference clustering, visualizing keywords can help us quickly understand the distribution and evolution of hotspots in the field of USD risk factors. As shown in the keyword topic map, keywords can be broadly categorized into diet-related factors and associated diseases. These results indicate that research on USD primarily emphasize preventive measures, focusing on medical management.

Increasing evidence suggests that diet-related factors play a significant role in USD occurrence and recurrence. For example, Matteo Bargagli et al. conducted a narrative review on the association between calcium and vitamin D supplements and USD, indicating that increased intake of vitamin D raises the risk of USD occurrence [22]. Calcium oxalate, as the most common type of stone in USD, is closely associated with dietary oxalate intake. Another study discussed the physiological relevance of dietary oxalates and oxalate precursors in the human body to elucidate the impact of oxalate content and its mechanisms on the occurrence of USD [23]. Meanwhile, a guideline for USD prevention states that increasing fluid intake, balancing calcium intake, reducing sodium and animal protein intake, and increasing fruit and fiber intake can effectively reduce the incidence of USD [24]. Therefore, a balanced diet, which is considered the lowest-cost and highest-efficiency intervention method, is fundamental to the medical management of USD.

As research on USD risk factors grows, awareness of the association between the high occurrence of USD and certain diseases also increases. A bidirectional causal relationship study suggests that cardiovascular diseases, such as hypertension may affect the incidence of USD [25]. In a recent Mendelian randomization study, analysis based on genetic data suggests that obesity and type 2 diabetes may be high-risk factors for stone formation [26]. In addition, hypoparathyroidism and depression also increase the risk of developing USD [27,28]. The research indicates that medical management for the prevention of USD occurrence is particularly important for these high-risk populations. Therefore, medical management reduces the likelihood of complications and improves patients’ quality of life and well-being.

In summary, current research on USD risk factors primarily focuses on medical management for prevention, which has significant potential to be an effective intervention. Breakthroughs in this direction within the field are anticipated, which could significantly lower the incidence of USD and further reduce the economic burden associated with this disease.

Limitations

First, the literature included in this study was limited to the WOSCC, and other important databases and relevant studies were overlooked. Second, the time span chosen was from 2010 to 2023, which may have resulted in the exclusion of significant articles published before this period from the statistical analysis. Third, we only screened studies published in English, neglecting non-English literature. Finally, certain biases, such as temporal bias, could have influenced the study results. Despite these limitations, we believe that this study provides a reference for scholars to determine the research themes, hotspots, and trends in USD risk factors.

Conclusion

In conclusion, through statistical analysis and evaluation of information from different countries, institutions, authors, journals, and more using tools, such as CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and R-Bibliometrix, we analyzed the themes and hotspots of keywords and predicted research trends in USD risk factors. The analysis showed that in-depth research on USD risk factors primarily focused on medical management.

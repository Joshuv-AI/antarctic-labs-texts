Aortic remodeling after thoracic endovascular repair combined with anti-thrombotic therapy for acute type B aortic dissection
Authors: Zhan-kui Du, Ya-song Wang, Zhi-qiang Zhang, Quan-Min Jing, Xiao-zeng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the effect of anti-thrombotic (AT) drugs on aortic remodeling and clinical prognosis after thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) for acute type B aortic dissection (TBAD).MethodsThis retrospective observational study included consecutive patients with acute TBAD who underwent TEVAR and underwent computed tomography angiography (CTA) preoperatively and during follow-up between January 2012 and September 2023 at the General Hospital of Northern Theater Command. Based on whether AT therapy was administered, the patients were divided into the AT group (patients receiving AT drugs) and the NAT group (patients not receiving AT drugs). Image data were analyzed for the thrombosis status of the false lumen (FL), as well as changes in the maximum diameter of the true lumen (TL), FL, and transaortic lumen (TAL) in the stented thoracic aorta (STA), unstented thoracic aorta (UTA), and abdominal aorta (AA). 12 - month clinical outcomes (major adverse event) were also recorded.ResultsA total of 175 patients with acute TBAD were included in this study. The preoperative FL status of patent, partial thrombosis, and complete thrombosis, respectively, in the STA, UTA, and AA showed no significant differences between the AT and NAT groups. The preoperative mean inner diameters of TL, FL, and TAL, respectively, in the STA, UTA, and AA were also not significantly different between the two groups. Similar to the aforementioned in the preoperative, there were also no significant differences between the two groups at the 12-month follow-up. At 12-month follow-up, FL status (patent vs. completely thrombosed) showed significantly favorable changes compared with preoperative images in the STA, UTA, and AA segments (all P < 0.001). In both the AT and NAT groups, changes in the maximum diameter of TL, FL, and TAL in all segments were statistically significant at 12-month follow-up (all P < 0.001). Regarding diameter changes: TAL expansion was 4.0%, 14.9%, and 53.7% in the STA, UTA, and AA, respectively; TAL stabilization was 62.3%, 36.6%, and 44.6% in the STA, UTA, and AA, respectively; and TAL shrinkage was 33.7%, 48.6%, and 1.7% in the STA, UTA, and AA, respectively. During a 12-month follow-up, 13 patients (7.4%) had a major adverse event. Moreover, the difference between the two groups was not statistically significant (P > 0.05).ConclusionsAmong acute TBAD patients who underwent TEVAR, at the 12 - month follow-up, patients receiving AT drugs had a comparable prognosis in aortic remodeling to those not receiving AT drugs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-026-04038-4.Keywords: Acute aortic dissection, Anti-thrombotic, Morphology, Prognosis

Introduction

Aortic dissection is the most common catastrophic disorder of the aorta and is associated with high mortality [1]. According to the Stanford classification, a type A aortic dissection manifests with a primary intimal tear in the ascending aorta, whereas a type B aortic dissection (TBAD) originates in the descending aorta [2]. The DeBakey classification divides distal dissections into two types: Ⅲa, which solely involves the thoracic descending aorta, and Ⅲb, which extends into the abdominal aorta (AA) [3]. Complicated TBAD or DeBakey type Ⅲ is generally treated with thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) [3]. To effectively treat TBAD, TEVAR closes the primary entry tear (PET) of the dissection, thus restoring flow to the true lumen (TL) while preventing flow into the false lumen (FL), leading to thrombosis of the FL [4]. Favorable aortic remodeling includes TL expansion, FL regression, complete thrombosis, and overall aortic diameter stabilization [2, 4].

With regard to the use of anti-thrombotic (AT) therapy in patients with acute TBAD, some established and evidence-based indications include TBAD combined with coronary artery disease (CHD), peripheral vascular disease (PVD), cerebrovascular diseases (CVD), and atrial fibrillation (AF) [5–7]. Moreover, aortic remodeling in acute TBAD after TEVAR, which more prominently reflects clinical prognosis, has been intensively investigated [8, 9]. However, aortic remodeling and outcomes after TEVAR in patients with acute TBAD receiving AT drugs have not been well studied [10, 11]. Therefore, this study was conducted to investigate aortic remodeling in acute TBAD after TEVAR in patients receiving AT drugs.

Methods

Study population and enrollment criteria

This study was a retrospective observational study using data from the Department of Cardiology, General Hospital of Northern Theater Command. We enrolled consecutive patients with acute TBAD who underwent TEVAR between January 2012 and September 2023. Patients meeting the following criteria were included: (1) those with acute TBAD who underwent successful TEVAR; (2) TBAD classified as DeBakey Ⅲb (distal dissections extending into the abdominal aorta); (3) availability of computed tomography angiography (CTA) scans preoperatively and at least at 12-month follow-up. Exclusion criteria included: (1) subacute or chronic TBAD with onset beyond 14 days; (2) aortic dissection not extending to the abdominal aorta (DeBakey Ⅲa); (3) intraluminal hematomas, penetrating ulcers, genetic or connective tissue diseases (e.g., Marfan syndrome); (4) traumatic aortic injury; (5) incomplete clinical and imaging data.

The AT group consisted of patients receiving anti-coagulants [warfarin and new oral anticoagulants (NOAC)] or anti-platelet drugs (aspirin and clopidogrel) for CHD, PVD, CVD, or AF. The non-anti-thrombotic (NAT) group included patients not receiving anti-coagulants or anti-platelet drugs. Within the AT group, patients were further divided into the single anti-thrombotic (SAT) group (receiving only aspirin, clopidogrel, or warfarin/NOAC) and the dual anti-thrombotic (DAT) group (receiving aspirin combined with clopidogrel or warfarin/NOAC; or clopidogrel combined with warfarin/NOAC). As this was a retrospective study, informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of General Hospital of Northern Theater Command, China (No. [2023] 225).

Data collection and CTA characteristics

Clinical data were collected from the hospital electronic medical records. Baseline demographic data were collected from patients’ medical records upon their hospital admission, including baseline characteristics (age, sex, smokers, drinkers, hypertension, diabetes, blood pressure, and heart rate), clinical presentation (chest pain, back pain, abdominal pain, migrating pain, and persistent pain), comorbidities (CHD, CVD, PVD, AF, renal insufficiency, visceral malperfusion, neurologic deficit, limb ischemia, and pleural effusion), and medications including AT drugs (aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin, and NOAC).

In this cohort, preoperative and follow-up CTA examinations were performed, and their morphological characteristics were recorded. The imaging characteristics included the initial and follow-up status of the false lumen (patent, partial thrombosis, and complete thrombosis), maximum diameter of the true lumen (TL), false lumen (FL), and transaortic lumen (TAL), as well as changes in diameter of TL, FL, and TAL.

TEVAR procedure and primary endpoint

TEVAR was indicated for complicated acute TBAD, defined by the presence of refractory pain, uncontrolled hypertension, malperfusion syndrome, or early aortic expansion on imaging. All procedures were performed under general anesthesia in a hybrid operating room. The stent graft was first deployed to cover the primary entry tear. Additional stent grafts were placed only when the initial graft was insufficient to reestablish flow into TL or inadequate for covering the primary entry tear. When the initial tear was close to the left subclavian artery (LSA), the origin of the LSA was covered by the stent graft. The left common carotid artery-LSA bypass was performed either immediately before or after TEVAR based on radiographic assessments of the vertebrobasilar circulation. If TEVAR alone did not resolve end-organ malperfusion, bare-metal stents were placed at the origin of visceral and renal vessels. Stent grafts were oversized by 10–15% relative to the diameter of the proximal landing zone.

The primary endpoint was major adverse events, including mortality, endoleak, aortic rupture or re-dissection, revascularization, limb ischemia, organ failure (including renal and intestinal tract), stroke, and hemorrhage (Bleeding Academic Research Consortium, BARC ≥ 2 grade) at 12 months.

Definition

Acute aortic dissection refers to < 2 weeks from symptom onset. Changes in TL, FL, and TAL diameters after TEVAR and during follow-up period were defined as growth (> 3-mm increase), stabilization (≤ 3-mm increase or decrease), shrinkage (> 3 mm decrease) at follow-up time point in comparison with the baseline measurement during a pre-procedural examination [12].

Aortic morphology analysis

CTA was performed preoperatively, at 1 month, and at 12 months during follow-up. Pre-TEVAR and latest follow-up CTA scans were reviewed and compared for research analysis in the selected patients. The degree of aortic remodeling involving FL thrombosis after TEVAR was evaluated in three separate aortic segments: the STA, the UTA, and the AA. Measurements, namely, the largest inner diameter of the aortic lumen, the maximum inner diameter of TL and FL, and the maximum diameter of TAL, were obtained by manual segmentation. The maximum diameter of the aorta was measured from inner wall to inner wall on cross-sectional images. The maximum diameters of TL and FL were measured perpendicular to the contour of the intimal flap on axial CT scans. Maximum axial diameters were measured, as this is the standard metric in clinical practice and follow-up guidelines for aortic dissection, strongly correlated with rupture risk and demonstrating high reproducibility [13]. FL status was qualitatively assessed on CTA as complete thrombosis (evidence of thrombus without contrast enhancement), partially thrombosed (evidence of both contrast enhancement and thrombus), or patent (contrast enhancement without evidence of thrombus). All measurements were obtained by two experienced radiologists with more than 3 years of experience in cardiovascular imaging, and final decisions were reached by consensus.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as mean (SD). Categorical variables were expressed as frequency and percentage (%). Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables was used to determine a statistical difference in variables between groups. Differences between three groups were analyzed using the Chi-squared test or Fisher exact test for categorical data and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for continuous variables that satisfied normal distribution. Continuous data without normal distribution were examined using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Statistical analysis was performed using the SPSS statistical software, version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Differences of P < 0.05 were considered as statistically significant.

Results

The flow chart of the study is shown in Supplementary Fig. 1. A total of 800 consecutive patients who underwent TEVAR for TBAD were included in our center during the study period. Of these, 625 patients were excluded due to subacute or chronic TBAD (n = 103), DeBakey Ⅲa AD (n = 133), thoracic aortic aneurysm or pseudoaneurysm (n = 40), traumatic injury (n = 31), genetic or connective tissue diseases (n = 29), and missing CTA (n = 289). The remaining 175 patients with acute TBAD treated with TEVAR were included in the study. These 175 patients were divided into the AT group (n = 73) and the NAT group (n = 102). Of the AT group, 73 patients were further divided into the SAT group (n = 44) and the DAT group (n = 29).

Baseline characteristics

The mean age of the 175 patients was 58.70 ± 9.25 years, and 22.3% of the patients were 65 years or older. The majority of patients (69.7%) were men and had a history of smoking (63.4%). However, there were no significant differences in demographic characteristics between the AT and NAT groups. Regarding comorbidities, the proportion of patients with diabetes, dyslipidemia, CHD, PVD, CVD, and AF was significantly higher in the AT group than in the NAT group (all P < 0.05). There were no significant differences in hypertension and renal insufficiency between the two groups. Regarding clinical presentations, chest pain, back pain, abdominal pain, persistent pain, and migrating pain were present in 64.4%, 82.3%, 33.3%, 35.4%, and 73.1% of all patients, respectively. Of the patients, 4.6%, 1.7%, and 2.3% had experienced visceral malperfusion, neurologic deficit, and limb ischemia, respectively. There were no significant differences between the two groups in these presentations. For anti-thrombotic therapy, 16.6%, 27.4%, and 13.1% of patients were treated with aspirin, clopidogrel, and warfarin/NOAC, respectively (Table 1).

Aortic morphology in preoperative and follow-up

The preoperative FL status of patent, partial thrombosis, and complete thrombosis, respectively, in the STA, UTA, and AA showed no significant differences between the two groups (Supplementary Table 1). The preoperative mean inner diameters of TL, FL, and TAL, respectively, in the STA, UTA, and AA were also not significantly different between the two groups (Supplementary Table 2). Among the 175 patients, only 2.3% had FL status of patent in the STA segment; however, 7.4% and 12.0% (in the UTA and AA, respectively) did (Supplementary Table 3). The maximum inner diameters of TL, FL, and TAL, respectively, in the STA, UTA, and AA were also not significantly different between the AT and NAT groups in the 12-month follow-up (Supplementary Table 4).

Table 4 listed changes in lumen diameters from preoperative measurements according to FL status during follow-up. Conversely, there were no statistically significant differences among the three subgroups in the AA segment for the AT group.

Procedural details and clinical outcomes

The technical success rate was 98.3% (172/175), as 3 patients showed type Ia endoleaks, which were considered as unsuccessful cases. Discussions

Accumulated evidence has demonstrated that the AT therapy was safe and effective for TBAD underwent TEVAR [14, 15]. However, there is few morphology research on the effect of AT drugs on aortic remodeling for TBAD. To our knowledge, the present study is the first investigation indicating that patients with TEAVR combined with AT drugs have a comparable aortic remodeling prognosis to those without AT drugs for acute TBAD.

According to previously published studies, postoperative AT drugs were safety for acute TBAD in the observational researches [16, 17]. Our study demonstrated that patients with acute TBAD have not negatively influence aortic remodeling after TEVAR combined with AT drugs at 12 months (Supplementary Fig. 8). Although the potential mechanisms underlying the ability of AT to reduce the occurrence of FL thrombosis may be attributed to inhibiting thrombin formation and decreasing the viscosity of blood, hereby delaying thrombosis in the FL [18, 19], real research showed pronouncedly opposite outcomes in patients with acute TBAD after TEVAR combined with AT drugs [20]. There are several potential explanations for the outcome. First and foremost, the remodeling is primarily mechanically driven by the exclusion of the primary entry tear via the stent graft, which abruptly depressurizes the false lumen and redirects flow to the true lumen. This hemodynamic change is likely the dominant stimulus for FL thrombosis and TL expansion. Second, stringent postoperative blood pressure and heart rate control, a standard of care, reduces wall stress and supports this mechanical remodeling. 20–21. Thirdly, thrombosis related in FL after TEVAR may be a function of mechanical exclusion and altered flow dynamics as opposed to a process depending on completely intact platelet function or inherent coagulation cascade [15]. While detailed molecular pathways warrant future investigation, our data suggest the mechanical success of TEVAR is paramount.

More attention has been paid to monitor the change of status in FL and diameter in FL and TL for after TEVAR. Favorable aortic remodeling included FL thrombosis, FL obliteration, TL enlargement, and transaortic stabilization. However, incomplete thrombosis in the FL has been reported as an independent risk factor for dissection-related death and dissection-related event, whereas complete thrombosis is associated with improved outcomes [21–23]. Correspondingly, appropriately decreased FL and increased TL indicate a better prognosis for TBAD afterTEVAR [24]. Thus, it is critical that cover primary entry tear of dissection with stent-grafts initiates TL thrombosis and gradually reduce FL pressure [15].

Reduction in the FL and TAL after TEVAR are major markers of aortic remodeling after dissection23. Similar as previous study [20], aortic remodeling was associated with a greater reduction in the diameter of the proximal descending aorta than distal descending aorta in our study. Although stented-grafts stopped aortic expansion and enabled aortic reverse remodeling in stent coverage, favorable effects distal to the graft, especially the abdominal aorta, were not observed. Furthermore, the extent of stent length was similar between groups and thus unlikely to account for the observed outcomes. Same as previous studies [26–28], our study indicated that the maximum diameter of the STA and UTA are stable with an increase of the diameter of TL and a decrease the diameter of FL, and the aneurysmal growth of the AA is mainly attributed to FL expansion. It is attributed to the complex branches and multiple tears in the AA segment [25]. Multiple tears were located in the abdominal aorta, which allowed continued perfusion of FL after TEVAR, was an independent risk factor for incomplete FL thrombosis after TEVAR [29]. The number of visceral branches that originated partially or totally from the FL was a significant independent risk factor associated with incomplete thrombosis in FL after TEVAR [25]. Therefore, retrograde perfusion of FL can be established by perfusion through the involvement of branch vessels or distal tear [30, 31]. Based on these findings, long-term aortic dilation, predominantly in the abdominal aorta, is a phenomenon that must be closely observed on follow-up imaging. This finding has direct clinical relevance, suggesting that the requirement for AT therapy due to comorbid conditions need not preclude the benefits of TEVAR in acute TBAD.

Limitations

There are several limitations to this study. Small numbers of patients and the lack of a longer follow-up unfavorably affect the results of the remodeling analysis. It remains a persistent challenge to maintain CT follow-up in patients, particularly when they become asymptomatic. The bias in case selection is another limitation. This was an observational retrospective study from a single center, and antithrombotic (AT) drugs, which include antiplatelet agents and anticoagulants, have fundamentally different mechanisms of action. Last, we are not to evaluate the volume of the FL of TBAD in this process.

Conclusions

The patients with AT drugs have comparable aortic remodeling prognosis than without AT drugs for acute TBAD underwent TEAVR in 12 months follow-up. Long- term outcomes require further investigation.

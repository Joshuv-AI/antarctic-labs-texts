Quasi-centroids and Quasi-Derivations of low-dimensional Zinbiel algebras
Basdouri Imed 1 , Jean Lerbet 2 , Bouzid Mosbahi 3

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the concepts of quasi-centroid and quasi-derivation for Zinbiel algebras. Utilizing the classification results of Zinbiel algebras established previously, we describe the quasi-centroids and quasi-derivations of low-dimensional Zinbiel algebras. Additionally, we explore certain properties of quasi-centroids in the context of Zinbiel algebras and employ these properties to classify algebras with so-called small quasi-centroids. This description of quasi-derivations allows us to identify a significant subclass of Zinbiel algebras characterized as quasi-characteristically nilpotent.

1.
Introduction

Non-associative algebras have been widely studied for their theoretical importance and broad applications in fields like physics, engineering, and applied mathematics. Within this area, Zinbiel algebras hold a distinct position. Introduced by J.-L. Loday in 1995, Zinbiel algebras serve as the Koszul duals of Leibniz algebras in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. The term
Zinbiel
, suggested by J.M. Lemaire, reflects this relationship, as it is “Leibniz” spelled backward, indicating their association with commutative associative algebras, pre-Lie algebras, and dendriform algebras in
[
4
]
.
Zinbiel algebras are defined by a specific right-symmetric identity:

\displaystyle(p\cdot q)\cdot r

\displaystyle=p\cdot(q\cdot r)+p\cdot(r\cdot q)

and naturally connect to dendriform algebras, appearing in contexts such as algebraic combinatorics, noncommutative geometry, and the study of operads. These algebras generalize commutative structures in a way similar to how Leibniz algebras extend Lie algebras. The study of Zinbiel algebras has evolved to include various applications and links to fields such as classification, derivatins, centroids, homological algebra, deformation theory, and quantum field theory in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

This paper focuses on defining and describing quasi-centroids and quasi-derivations of Zinbiel algebras. Quasi-centroids and quasi-derivations are derived from investigations of low-dimensional Zinbiel algebras, where these structures play a key role in classification and algebraic applications. Our analysis draws on existing classifications of complex Zinbiel algebras in dimensions two, three, and four, referenced in works such as
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

The structure of this paper is as follows: Section 1 provides an introduction to Zinbiel algebras along with a review of key results. Section 2 presents the basic definitions and preliminary concepts essential for understanding Zinbiel algebras. Section 3 focuses on the properties of quasi-centroids and quasi-derivations. Sections 4 and 5 describe and compute quasi-centroids and quasi-derivations in finite-dimensional Zinbiel algebras. We utilize classification results for low-dimensional complex Zinbiel algebras from
[
13
]
and illustrate our methods and computations with examples generated using Mathematica software.

Prelimieries

This section introduces essential definitions and foundational concepts that will be relevant for the study of Zinbiel algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

An algebra
Z
over a field
\mathbb{K}
is a vector space over
\mathbb{K}
equipped with a bilinear map

\displaystyle\xi:Z\times Z\to Z,

such that

\displaystyle\xi(\alpha p+\beta q,r)

\displaystyle=\alpha\xi(p,r)+\beta\xi(q,r),

\displaystyle\xi(r,\alpha p+\beta q)

\displaystyle=\alpha\xi(r,p)+\beta\xi(r,q),

where
p,q,r\in Z
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A Zinbiel algebra
Z
over a field
\mathbb{K}
is an algebra satisfying the following condition:

\displaystyle(p\cdot q)\cdot r

\displaystyle=p\cdot(q\cdot r)+p\cdot(r\cdot q)

for all
p,q,r\in Z
.

Under the conditions of a new product presented as
p\cdot q+q\cdot p
.
It is possible to obtain a structure of the associative commutative algebra on Z. The
definition of a sequence for a provided Zinbiel algebra Z can be given in the
following way:

Z^{1}=Z,\quad Z^{t+1}=Z\cdot Z^{t},\quad t\geq 1

Definition 2.3
.

Let
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
be two Zinbiel algebras over a field
\mathbb{K}
. A linear mapping
\psi:Z\rightarrow Z_{1}
is a
homomorphism
if

\displaystyle\psi(p\cdot q)=\psi(p)\ast\psi(q),\;\text{for all}\;p,q\in Z.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
Z
be a Zinbiel algebra over a field
\mathbb{K}
. A linear mapping
\psi:Z\rightarrow Z
is an
endomorphism
if

\displaystyle\psi(p\cdot q)

\displaystyle=\psi(p)\cdot\psi(q),\;\text{for all}\;p,q\in Z.

We denote
End_{\mathbb{K}}(Z)
as the set of all endomorphisms of
Z
.

Definition 2.5
.

A linear transformation
d
of a Zinbiel algebra
Z
is called a
derivation
if for any
p,q\in Z
,

\displaystyle d(p\cdot q)

\displaystyle=d(p)\cdot q+p\cdot d(q).

The space of all derivations of the algebra
Z
, equipped with the multiplication defined as the commutator, forms a Lie algebra which is denoted by
Der(Z)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
Z
be a Zinbiel algebra. Then
Der(Z)
is a Lie algebra with respect to the bracket
[d_{1},d_{2}]=d_{1}\circ d_{2}-d_{2}\circ d_{1}
.

Proof.

For
d_{1},d_{2}\in Der(Z)
, we need to verify that the bracket
[d_{1},d_{2}]=d_{1}\circ d_{2}-d_{2}\circ d_{1}
is also a derivation on
Z
, i.e., that it satisfies the derivation property:

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}](p\cdot q)

\displaystyle=[d_{1},d_{2}](p)\cdot q+p\cdot[d_{1},d_{2}](q).

Calculating
[d_{1},d_{2}](p\cdot q)
:

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}](p\cdot q)

\displaystyle=(d_{1}\circ d_{2})(p\cdot q)-(d_{2}\circ d_{1})(p\cdot q)

\displaystyle=d_{1}(d_{2}(p\cdot q))-d_{2}(d_{1}(p\cdot q)).

Using the derivation property of
d_{2}
on
p\cdot q
, we get

\displaystyle d_{2}(p\cdot q)

\displaystyle=d_{2}(p)\cdot q+p\cdot d_{2}(q).

Thus,

\displaystyle d_{1}(d_{2}(p\cdot q))

\displaystyle=d_{1}(d_{2}(p)\cdot q+p\cdot d_{2}(q))

\displaystyle=d_{1}(d_{2}(p))\cdot q+d_{2}(p)\cdot d_{1}(q)+d_{1}(p)\cdot d_{2}(q)+p\cdot d_{1}(d_{2}(q)),

and similarly,

\displaystyle d_{2}(d_{1}(p\cdot q))

\displaystyle=d_{2}(d_{1}(p)\cdot q+p\cdot d_{1}(q))

\displaystyle=d_{2}(d_{1}(p))\cdot q+d_{1}(p)\cdot d_{2}(q)+d_{2}(p)\cdot d_{1}(q)+p\cdot d_{2}(d_{1}(q)).

Subtracting these two expressions, we find

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}](p\cdot q)

\displaystyle=\big(d_{1}(d_{2}(p))-d_{2}(d_{1}(p))\big)\cdot q+p\cdot\big(d_{1}(d_{2}(q))-d_{2}(d_{1}(q))\big)

\displaystyle=[d_{1},d_{2}](p)\cdot q+p\cdot[d_{1},d_{2}](q).

Thus,
[d_{1},d_{2}]
satisfies the derivation property and hence
[d_{1},d_{2}]\in Der(Z)
.

Next, to conclude that
Der(Z)
is a Lie algebra, we need to check two properties of the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
:

1.
Antisymmetry
: For any
d_{1},d_{2}\in Der(Z)
,

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}]

\displaystyle=-[d_{2},d_{1}],

which follows directly from the definition:

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}]

\displaystyle=d_{1}\circ d_{2}-d_{2}\circ d_{1}=-(d_{2}\circ d_{1}-d_{1}\circ d_{2})=-[d_{2},d_{1}].

2.
Jacobi identity
: For any
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in Der(Z)
, we must show that

\displaystyle[d_{1},[d_{2},d_{3}]]+[d_{2},[d_{3},d_{1}]]+[d_{3},[d_{1},d_{2}]]

\displaystyle=0.

This follows from the fact that
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the commutator of linear maps, which always satisfies the Jacobi identity.

Since both antisymmetry and Jacobi identity hold, we conclude that
Der(Z)
is a Lie algebra under the bracket
[d_{1},d_{2}]=d_{1}\circ d_{2}-d_{2}\circ d_{1}
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(Z,\cdot)
be a Zinbiel algebra and
d\in\text{Hom}(Z)
. Then the following conditions hold:

(1)

d\in\text{Der}(Z)

(2)

[d,L_{p}]=L_{d(p)}

(3)

[d,R_{p}]=R_{d(p)}

Proof.

Let
(Z,\cdot)
be a Zinbiel algebra and
d\in\text{Hom}(Z)
. We will show each part of the proposition.

(1)

d\in\text{Der}(Z)
.

\displaystyle d(p\cdot q)=d(p)\cdot q+p\cdot d(q)\quad\text{for all }p,q\in Z.

Since
d
is in
\text{Hom}(Z)
, it is a linear map, and we assume it satisfies this condition as given.

(2)

[d,L_{p}]=L_{d(p)}
.

Here, we need to verify that the commutator of
d
and
L_{p}
is equal to
L_{d(p)}
, where
L_{p}
is the left multiplication operator defined by
L_{p}(q)=p\cdot q
for all
q\in Z
.

The commutator
[d,L_{p}]
is given by

\displaystyle[d,L_{p}](q)

\displaystyle=d(L_{p}(q))-L_{p}(d(q))=d(p\cdot q)-p\cdot d(q).

By the derivation property of
d
, we have

\displaystyle d(p\cdot q)

\displaystyle=d(p)\cdot q+p\cdot d(q).

Substituting this into the equation for
[d,L_{p}](q)
, we get

\displaystyle[d,L_{p}](q)

\displaystyle=(d(p)\cdot q+p\cdot d(q))-p\cdot d(q)=d(p)\cdot q.

Therefore,

\displaystyle[d,L_{p}](q)

\displaystyle=L_{d(p)}(q),

which confirms that
[d,L_{p}]=L_{d(p)}
.

(3)

[d,R_{p}]=R_{d(p)}
.

In this part, we need to show that
[d,R_{p}]=R_{d(p)}
, where
R_{p}
is the right multiplication operator defined by
R_{p}(q)=q\cdot p
for all
q\in Z
.

The commuter
[d,R_{p}]
is given by

\displaystyle[d,R_{p}](q)

\displaystyle=d(R_{p}(q))-R_{p}(d(q))=d(q\cdot p)-d(q)\cdot p.

Using the derivation property of
d
again, we have

\displaystyle d(q\cdot p)

\displaystyle=d(q)\cdot p+q\cdot d(p).

Substituting this into the equation for
[d,R_{p}](q)
, we get

\displaystyle[d,R_{p}](q)

\displaystyle=(d(q)\cdot p+q\cdot d(p))-d(q)\cdot p=q\cdot d(p).

Thus,

\displaystyle[d,R_{p}](q)

\displaystyle=R_{d(p)}(q),

which confirms that
[d,R_{p}]=R_{d(p)}
.

∎

3.
Proprieties of quasi-centroids and quasi-derivations

Definition 3.1
.

The
centroid
of a Zinbiel algebra
(Z,\cdot)
over a field
\mathbb{K}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma(Z)

\displaystyle=\left\{\phi\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(Z)\mid\phi(p\cdot q)=\phi(p)\cdot q=p\cdot\phi(q),\;\forall\;p,q\in Z\right\}.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
I
be a nonempty subset of
Z
. The
centralizer
of
I
in
Z
is the set

\displaystyle C_{Z}(I)

\displaystyle=\{p\in Z\mid p\cdot I=I\cdot p=0\}.

If
I
is an ideal of
Z
, then
C_{Z}(I)
is also an ideal of
Z
. In particular, if
I=Z
, we write
C_{Z}(I)=C(Z)
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
Z
be a Zinbiel algebra. The quasi-centroid of
Z
, denoted by
Q\Gamma(Z)
, is defined as the set

Q\Gamma(Z)=\left\{\phi\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(Z)\mid\phi(p)\cdot q=p\cdot\phi(q),\ \forall\;p,q\in Z\right\}.

Definition 3.3
.

The central derivation of a Zinbiel algebra
Z
, denoted by
C_{d}(Z)
, is the subset of
\text{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(Z)
given by

C_{d}(Z)=\left\{\phi\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(Z)\mid\phi(p)\cdot q=p\cdot\phi(q)=0,\ \forall\;p,q\in Z\right\}.

Definition 3.4
.

An element
d\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(Z)
is called a quasi-derivation if there exists another map
d^{\prime}\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(Z)
such that

d(p)\cdot q+p\cdot d(q)=d^{\prime}(p\cdot q),\ \forall\;p,q\in Z.

Definition 3.5
.

A Zinbiel algebra
Z
is called nilpotent if there exists a positive integer
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that
Z^{s}=\{0\}
, where
Z^{k}
denotes the
k
-th power of
Z
under the Zinbiel product. The smallest such integer
s
is referred to as the nil-index of
Z
.

Definition 3.6
.

If the quasi-derivations of
Z
form a nilpotent algebra, we say that
Z
is characteristically nilpotent.

Definition 3.7
.

A Zinbiel algebra
Z
is called indecomposable if it cannot be expressed as a direct sum of two ideals. Otherwise, it is decomposable.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
Z
be an indecomposable Zinbiel algebra. If the central derivations and scalars of
Z
form a small subalgebra
L
, then we say that
L
is small.

Next, we analyze the interactions between quasi-derivations and quasi-centroids within Zinbiel algebras.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
I
be an ideal of the Zinbiel algebra
Z
. Then
C_{Z}(I)
, the centralizer of

in

.

Proof.

Assume

(p\cdot q)\cdot r=p\cdot(q\cdot r)=0

and similarly,

is an ideal of

.

Quasi-Centroids of Low-Dimensional Zinbiel Algebras

This section provides the details of the quasi-centroid of Zinbiel algebras in dimensions two, three, and four over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
. We choose
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
as the basis for a
n

Z

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{ij}^{k}e_{k},\quad i,j=1,2,\dots,n.

Since the quasi-centroid is a linear transformation of the vector space
Z

can be represented in a matrix form

\displaystyle\phi(e_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}e_{j},\quad i=1,2,\dots,n.

According to the definition of the quasi-centroid, the entries
a_{ij},j=1,2,\dots,n

[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1,2,\dots,n}
must satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}(\gamma_{it}^{k}a_{tj}-a_{it}\gamma_{tj}^{k})

\displaystyle=0.

(1)

The quasi-centroid of the Zinbiel algebra
Z
under consideration can thus be described by solving this system of equations for
a_{ij},i,j=1,2,\dots,n
, once the structure constants
\gamma_{ij}^{k}
of
Z
are known. Here we utilize classification results of two, three, and four-dimensional complex Zinbiel algebras from relevant literature.
to solve the system of equations above with respect to

1\leq i,j,t\leq n

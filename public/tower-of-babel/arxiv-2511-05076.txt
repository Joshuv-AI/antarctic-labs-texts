On the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of log-harmonic mappings
Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce definitions of the pre-Schwarzian and the Schwarzian derivatives for any locally univalent log-harmonic mappings defined in the unit disk 𝔻 = { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } \mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\} . We explore the properties and applications of these concepts in the context of geometric function theory, and we also establish a necessary and sufficient condition for a non-vanishing log-harmonic mapping having a finite pre-Schwarzian norm. Additionally, we establish a relationship between the pre-Schwarzian norm of a non-vanishing log-harmonic mapping and that of a certain analytic function in 𝔻 \mathbb{D} .

1.
Introduction

In geometric function theory, the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of locally univalent and analytic functions are currently a prominent technique for studying the geometric properties of these mappings. One of the potential applications of this approach can be used to determine the conditions for global univalence. The elegance of these derivatives has led to the extension of the theory to complex-valued harmonic mappings (see
[
10
,
13
]
) and non-vanishing log-harmonic mappings (see
[
18
,
9
]
). The theory of log-harmonic mapping has become an exciting field of research in the last few years.
The natural follow-up question is,“can these derivatives be defined for any log-harmonic mapping, including those that vanish at the origin?”

In this paper, we answer this question affirmatively by introducing definitions of the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives for any locally univalent log-harmonic mapping defined in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. Our approach provides a unified framework that encompasses both vanishing and non-vanishing cases. We then explore their properties and applications, particularly in the context of geometric function theory. By establishing connections with existing results, we aim to provide a more profound understanding of these derivatives and their implications for the field.

An analytic function
f(z)
in a domain
\Omega
is said to be locally univalent if for each
z_{0}\in\Omega
, there exists a neighborhood
N(z_{0})
of

z_{0}
such that
f(z)
is univalent in
N(z_{0})
. The Jacobian of a complex-valued function
f=u+iv
is defined by
J_{f}(z)=|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\overline{z}}|^{2}
.
It is well known that the non-vanishing of the Jacobian is necessary and sufficient conditions for local univalence of analytic mappings (see
[
11
, Chapter 1]
).
Let
\mathcal{S}
denotes the class of all analytic and univalent function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
with normalization
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
. An analytic function
f
defined in
\mathbb{D}
is called a Bloch function (see
[
6
,
24
]
) if

\displaystyle\beta_{f}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the class of all analytic functions
\omega:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{\omega\in\mathcal{B}:\omega(0)=0\}
.
Functions in
\mathcal{B}_{0}
are called Schwarz function. According to Schwarz’s lemma, if
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
, then
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|
and
|\omega^{\prime}(0)|\leq 1
.
Strict inequality holds in both estimates unless
\omega(z)=e^{i\theta}z
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. A sharpened form of the Schwarz lemma, known as the Schwarz-Pick lemma,
gives the estimate
|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1-|\omega(z)|^{2})/(1-|z|^{2})
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{B}
.

Let
f=u+iv
be a complex-valued function of
z=x+iy
in a simply connected domain
\Omega
. If
f\in C^{2}(\Omega)
(continuous first and second partial derivatives in

\Omega
) and satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
in
\Omega
, then
f
is said to be harmonic in
\Omega
, where

f_{z}=(1/2)(\partial f/\partial x-i\partial f/\partial y)
and
f_{\overline{z}}=(1/2)(\partial f/\partial x+i\partial f/\partial y)
. Note that every harmonic
mapping
f
has the canonical representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\Omega
, known respectively as the analytic and co-analytic parts of
f
.
This representation is unique up to an additive constant (see
[
12
]
). The inverse function theorem and a result of Lewy
[
17
]
shows that a harmonic
function
f
is locally univalent in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)\not=0
in
\Omega
. A harmonic mapping f is locally univalent
and sense-preserving in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\Omega
. Note that
|f_{z}|\not=0
whenever
J_{f}>0
.

1.1.
Log-harmonic mappings

A log-harmonic mapping
f
defined in
\mathbb{D}
is a solution of the nonlinear elliptic partial differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{f_{\overline{z}}(z)}}{\overline{f(z)}}=\omega(z)\frac{f_{z}(z)}{f(z)},~z\in\mathbb{D},

(1.1)

where the second complex dilatation function
\omega:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is analytic and the Jacobian of
f
is given by
J_{f}=|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\overline{z}}|^{2}=|f_{z}|^{2}(1-|\omega|^{2})>0
, which shows that non-constant log-harmonic mapping
f
is always sense-preserving in
\mathbb{D}
. If
f
is non-constant and vanishes only at
z=0,
then
f
admits the following representation

\displaystyle f(z)=z^{m}|z|^{2\beta m}h(z)\overline{g(z)},

where
m
is non-negative integer,
\beta=\overline{\omega(0)}(1+\omega(0))/\left(1-|\omega(0)|^{2}\right)
,
i.e.,

\textrm{Re}(\beta)>-1/2
, and
h
and
g
are analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
g(0)=1
and
h(0)\neq 0
. If
f
is univalent
log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, then either
0\not\in f(\mathbb{D})
and
\log(f)
is univalent and harmonic in
\mathbb{D}
or, if
f(0)=0
, then

f=z|z|^{2\beta}h\overline{g}
, where
\text{Re}(\beta)>-1/2
,
0\not\in hg(\mathbb{D})
and
F(t)=\log(f(e^{t}))
is univalent and harmonic on the half-plane

\{t\in\mathbb{C}:\text{Re}(t)<0\}
. The class of such functions has been studied extensively in
[
2
,
4
,
1
]
. Note that here “
\log
” denotes the principal branch of the logarithm. It is known that
F
is closely
related with the theory of nonparametric minimal surfaces over domains of the form
-\infty<u<u_{0}(v)
,
u_{0}(v+2\pi)=u_{0}(v)
(see
[
23
]
).

If
f
is a non-vanishing log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f
can be expressed as

\displaystyle f(z)=h(z)\overline{g(z)},

(1.2)

where
h
and
g
are non-vanishing analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}.
Further, if the mapping
f
given by (
1.2
) is locally univalent and sense-preserving, then
h^{\prime}g\neq 0
in
\mathbb{D}
and the second complex dilatation
\omega
is given by
\omega=g^{\prime}h/gh^{\prime}
is a Schwarz function in
\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore, it is evident that

\displaystyle J_{f}(z)=|h^{\prime}(z)g(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)h(z)|^{2}=|h^{\prime}(z)g(z)|^{2}\left(1-\left|\frac{h(z)g^{\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)g(z)}\right|^{2}\right)>0,~z\in\mathbb{D}.

Several authors have studied and established fundamental results on log-harmonic mappings defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
9
,
4
,
18
]
).

1.2.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of analytic functions

For a locally univalent analytic function
f
defined in a simply connected domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, the pre-Schwarzian derivative
P_{f}
and the Schwarzian derivative
S_{f}
of
f
are, respectively, defined as follows:

\displaystyle P_{f}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{f}(z)=P_{f}^{\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{2}P_{f}^{2}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}.

(1.3)

Moreover, the pre-Schwarzian and the Schwarzian norms of
f
are, respectively, given by

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\quad\text{and}\quad\|S_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|f_{h}(z)|.

(1.4)

Some important global univalence criteria for a locally univalent analytic function have been obtained using the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms.
For a univalent analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
, it is well-known that
\|P_{f}\|\leq 6
and the equality
is attained for the Koebe function or its rotation.
One of the most used univalence criterion for locally univalent analytic functions is the Becker’s univalence criterion
[
7
]
,
which states that if
f
a locally univalent analytic function and
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left|zP_{f}(z)\right|\leq 1
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. In a subsequent study,
Becker and Pommerenke
[
8
]
prove that the constant
1
is sharp.
In 1976, Yamashita
[
25
]

proved that
\|P_{f}\|<\infty
is finite if, and only if,
f
is uniformly locally univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, if
\|P_{f}\|<2
, then
f
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
16
]
).

In terms of the Schwarzian derivative, it is well-known that for any univalent analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
, we have the sharp inequality
\|S_{f}\|\leq 6
and
the equality is attained for the Koebe function or its rotation (see
[
15
]
).
In 1949, Z. Nehari
[
22
]
established important criteria for global univalence, expressed in terms of the Schwarzian derivative, by virtue of the connection with linear differential equations.
For instance, if
f
is locally univalent and analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and satisfies
\|S_{f}\|\leq 2
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. The bound
2
is sharp
[
14
]
.

1.3.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms of harmonic mappings

For a locally univalent harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, Hernández and Martín
[
13
]
have defined the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives, respectively, as follows:

\displaystyle P_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\log(J_{f})\right)_{z}=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}-\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\quad\text{and}\hfill

\displaystyle S_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{z}^{2}=S_{h}+\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\omega^{\prime}\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2},

where
S_{h}
is the classical Schwarzian derivative of the analytic function
h
,
J_{f}
is the Jacobian and
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
is the second complex dilatation of
f
. These derivatives play a crucial role in understanding the behavior of harmonic functions, particularly in the context of conformal mappings and their geometric properties.
This notion of pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of harmonic functions is a generalization of the classical pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of analytic functions.
Note that when
f
is analytic, we have
\omega=0.
It is also easy to see that
S_{f}=(P_{f})_{z}-(1/2)(P_{f})^{2}.
As in the case of analytic functions, for a sense-preserving
locally univalent harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the pre-Schwarzian norm
\|P_{f}\|
and the Schwarzian norm
\|S_{f}\|
are defined by (
1.4
). For a comprehensive study on the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives for harmonic mappings, we refer to
[
13
,
19
]
.

1.4.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of a non-vanishing log-harmonic mapping

For a locally univalent non-vanishing log-harmonic mapping
f=h\overline{g}
in a simply connected domain
\Omega
, Bravo
et al.

[
9
]
have defined the pre-Schwarzian
P_{f}
and Schwarzian derivatives
S_{f}
, respectively, as follows:

\displaystyle P_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\log(J_{f})\right)_{z}=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}+\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}-\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}=P_{\varphi}-\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\text{and}

\displaystyle S_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{z}^{2}=S_{\varphi}+\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2},

where
J_{f}
is the Jacobian of
f
,
\varphi^{\prime}=h^{\prime}g
and
\omega=g^{\prime}h/(h^{\prime}g)
is the second complex dilatation of the function
f
. If
f
is a sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping of the form (
1.2
) and
\psi
is a locally univalent analytic function for which the composition
f\circ\psi
is well defined, then the function
f\circ\psi
is again a sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping, and
P_{f\circ\psi}=(P_{f}\circ\psi)\varphi^{\prime}+P_{\psi}
and
S_{f\circ\psi}=(S_{f}\circ\psi)\varphi^{\prime}+S_{\psi}
.
For more information about the properties of the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of a sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping, we refer to
[
9
,
5
,
21
]
.

2.
Properties of the pre-Schwarzian norm of a non-vanishing log-harmonic mapping

In the following result, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for a non-vanishing log-harmonic mapping having a finite pre-Schwarzian norm.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f=h\overline{g}
be a non-vanishing sense-preserving and locally univalent log-harmonic mapping with the dilatation
\omega=g^{\prime}h/h^{\prime}g
defined in
\mathbb{D}
. Then either,
\|P_{f}\|=\|P_{hg}\|=\infty
or, both
\|P_{f}\|
and
\|P_{hg}\|
are finite. If
\|P_{f}\|
is finite, then

\displaystyle\left|\|P_{f}\|-\|P_{hg}\|\right|\leq 1\quad\text{and the constant $1$ is sharp.}

Proof.

Since
f=h\overline{g}
is a non-vanishing sense-preserving and locally univalent log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, thus
h^{\prime}g\neq 0
in
\mathbb{D}
and the second complex dilatation
\omega=g^{\prime}h/gh^{\prime}
is a Schwarz function in
\mathbb{D}
. The pre-Schwarzian derivative of
f
is given by

\displaystyle P_{f}(z)=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}+\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}-\frac{\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}.

(2.1)

Let
f_{1}=h(z)g(z)
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Since
h
and
g
are non-vanishing analytic functions, therefore
f_{1}
is a non-vanishing analytic function with

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{1}(z)=h^{\prime}(z)g(z)+h(z)g^{\prime}(z)=h^{\prime}(z)g\left(1+\;\omega(z)\right),

(2.2)

which shows that
f_{1}
is locally univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
. Taking logarithmic derivative on both sides of (
2.2
) with respect to
z
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{f_{1}}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{1}(z)}{f^{\prime}_{1}(z)}=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}+\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1+\omega(z)}

Therefore, the difference between the pre-Schwarzian derivatives of
f_{1}
and
f
is given by

\displaystyle P_{hg}(z)-P_{f}(z)=\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1+\omega(z)}+\frac{\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}=\frac{1+\overline{\omega(z)}}{1+\;\omega(z)}\cdot\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}.

(2.3)

In view of the Schwarz-Pick lemma, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left||P_{hg}(z)|-|P_{f}|\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|P_{hg}(z)-P_{f}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{1+\overline{\omega(z)}}{1+\omega(z)}\cdot\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1,

which shows that
\|P_{f}\|
is finite if, and only if,
\|P_{hg}\|
is finite. Moreover, if
\|P_{f}\|<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\left|\|P_{f}\|-\|P_{hg}\|\right|\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left||P_{f}(z)|-|P_{hg}(z)|\right|\leq 1.

To show that the constant
1
is sharp, we consider the log-harmonic mapping
f(z)=h(z)\overline{g(z)}
with
h(z)=\exp(z/(1-z))
and
g(z)=\exp(-z/(1-z))/(1-z)
. It is evident that the dilatation
\omega(z)=-z
and
h(z)g(z)=1/(1-z)
.
Therefore, the pre-Schwarzian derivatives of
f
and
hg
, respectively, are

\displaystyle P_{f}(z)=\frac{3}{(1-z)}-\frac{\overline{z}}{1-|z|^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{hg}(z)=\frac{2}{1-z}.

Thus, the pre-Schwarzian norm of
f
is

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|P_{f}\right|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{3}{1-z}-\frac{\overline{z}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right|.

On the positive real axis, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r^{2})\left|\frac{3}{1-r}-\frac{r}{1-r^{2}}\right|=5.

Similarly, we have
\|P_{hg}\|=4
.
Hence, we have
\left|\|P_{f}\|-\|P_{hg}\|\right|=|5-4|=1
, which shows that the constant
1
is sharp.
This completes the proof.
∎

Motivated by the results of Liu and Ponnusamy
[
19
]
, we establish a relationship between the pre-Schwarzian norm of a non-vanishing log-harmonic mapping
f=h\overline{g}
and that of the analytic function
hg^{\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and we choose the principal branch of the logarithm.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f=h\overline{g}
be a non-vanishing sense-preserving and locally univalent log-harmonic mapping with the dilatation
\omega=g^{\prime}h/h^{\prime}g
defined in
\mathbb{D}
. If
\log{g(z)}
is an analytic Bloch function in
\mathbb{D}
, then either,
\|P_{f}\|=\|P_{hg^{\varepsilon}}\|=\infty
or, both
\|P_{f}\|
and
\|P_{hg^{\varepsilon}}\|
are finite for each
\varepsilon\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. If
\|P_{f}\|
is finite, then

\displaystyle\left|\|P_{f}\|-\|P_{hg^{\varepsilon}}\|\right|\leq 1+|1-\varepsilon|\;\beta_{\log{g}}\leq 1+2\;\beta_{\log{g}},

for each
\varepsilon\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
In particular,

\displaystyle\left|\|P_{f}\|-\|P_{h}\|\right|\leq 1+\beta_{\log{g}}.

Proof.

As
f=h\overline{g}
is a non-vanishing sense-preserving and locally univalent log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, it follows that
h^{\prime}g\neq 0
in
\mathbb{D}
and the second complex dilatation
\omega=g^{\prime}h/gh^{\prime}
is a Schwarz function in
\mathbb{D}
. The pre-Schwarzian derivative of
f
is given in (
2.1
).
Let
f_{\varepsilon}(z)=h(z)g^{\varepsilon}(z)
defined in
\mathbb{D}
, where
\varepsilon\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Since
h
and
g
are non-vanishing analytic functions, therefore
f_{\varepsilon}
is a non-vanishing analytic function with

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(z)=h^{\prime}(z)g^{\varepsilon}(z)+\varepsilon h(z)g^{\varepsilon-1}(z)g^{\prime}(z)=h^{\prime}(z)g^{\varepsilon}(z)\left(1+\varepsilon\;\omega(z)\right),

(2.4)

which shows that
f_{\varepsilon}
is locally univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
. Taking logarithmic derivative on both sides of (
2.4
) with respect to
z
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(z)}{f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(z)}=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}+\varepsilon\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{\varepsilon\;\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1+\varepsilon\;\omega(z)}

Therefore, the pre-Schwarzian derivative
f_{\varepsilon}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{f_{\varepsilon}}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(z)}{f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(z)}=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}+\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+(\varepsilon-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{\varepsilon\;\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1+\varepsilon\;\omega(z)}

(2.5)

From (
2.1
) and (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle P_{hg^{\varepsilon}}(z)-P_{f}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{\varepsilon\;\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1+\varepsilon\;\omega(z)}+\frac{\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{\varepsilon+\overline{\omega(z)}}{1+\varepsilon\;\omega(z)}\cdot\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}.

In view of the Schwarz-Pick lemma, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left||P_{hg^{\varepsilon}}(z)|-|P_{f}|\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|P_{hg^{\varepsilon}}(z)-P_{f}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|(\varepsilon-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{\varepsilon+\overline{\omega(z)}}{1+\varepsilon\;\omega(z)}\cdot\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|1-\varepsilon|\;\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}\right|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\overline{\varepsilon}+\omega(z)}{1+\varepsilon\;\omega(z)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+|1-\varepsilon|\;\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}\right|.

If
\log{g(z)}
is an analytic Bloch function in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\displaystyle\beta_{\log{g}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}\right|<\infty.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left||P_{h(z)g^{\varepsilon}(z)}|-|P_{f}|\right|\leq 1+|1-\varepsilon|\beta_{\log{g}}\leq 1+2\beta_{\log{g}}<\infty,

which shows that
\|P_{f}\|
is finite if, and only if,
\|P_{h(z)g^{\varepsilon}}\|
is finite. Moreover, if
\|P_{f}\|<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\left|\|P_{f}\|-\|P_{hg^{\varepsilon}}\|\right|\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\Big||P_{f}(z)|-|P_{hg^{\varepsilon}}(z)|\Big|\leq 1+|1-\varepsilon|\beta_{\log{g}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

This relationship in Theorem
2.1
not only enhances our understanding of the behavior of log-harmonic mappings but also provides insights into their geometric properties.
Now, if we consider a log-harmonic mapping
f=h\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
h
is an analytic and locally univalent function, and
g
is a non-vanishing analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
, then
h^{\prime}g\neq 0
in
\mathbb{D}
. This shows that
f
is locally univalent in
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f=h\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping such that
h
is analytic and locally univalent, and
g
is non-vanishing analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with the dilatation

\omega\equiv a\in\mathbb{D}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{zg^{\prime}(z)/g(z)+\beta m}{(\beta+1)m+zh^{\prime}(z)/h(z)}=a,\quad{\it i.e.,}\quad\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{\beta m}{z}=a\left(\frac{(\beta+1)m}{z}+\frac{h^{\prime}(z)}{h(z)}\right).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle z^{\beta m}g(z)=\gamma\left(z^{(\beta+1)m}h(z)\right)^{a},~\text{where}~\gamma\in\mathbb{C}.

Therefore,
f
can be expressed as

\displaystyle f(z)=z^{(\beta+1)m}h(z)\;\overline{\gamma\left(z^{(\beta+1)m}h(z)\right)^{a}},~\text{where}~a\in\mathbb{D}~\text{and}~\gamma\in\mathbb{C}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The following theorem characterizes the log-harmonic mapping with the analytic Schwarzian derivative.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f(z)=z^{m}|z|^{2\beta m}h(z)\overline{g(z)}
be a locally univalent and sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
. Then the Schwarzian derivative
S_{f}
of
f
is analytic if,
and only if, the second complex dilatation
\omega
of
f
is constant, i.e., if, and only if,
f(z)=z^{(\beta+1)m}h(z)\;\overline{\gamma\left(z^{(\beta+1)m}h(z)\right)^{a}}
, where

a\in\mathbb{D}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=z^{m}|z|^{2\beta m}h(z)\overline{g(z)}
be a locally univalent and sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
that has an analytic Schwarzian derivative
S_{f}
defined by

\displaystyle S_{f}=S_{\varphi}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\omega^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right),

i.e.,

\displaystyle(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})\left(1-|\omega|^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{2}(\overline{\omega})^{2}(\omega^{\prime})^{2}-\left(1-|\omega|^{2}\right)\overline{\omega}\left(\omega^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)=0,

i.e.,

\displaystyle\quad(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})+\overline{\omega}\left(\omega^{\prime\prime}-\omega^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}-2(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})\omega\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle+(\overline{\omega})^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}(\omega^{\prime})^{2}+(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})\omega^{2}+\omega\left(\omega^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)=0,

where
\varphi(z)
is an analytic and locally univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(z)=(zh^{\prime}+(\beta+1)mh)z^{(2\beta+1)m-1}g
. Suppose that
\omega
is not constant in
\mathbb{D}
. Then there exists a disk
D(z_{0},r):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-z_{0}|<r\}\subset\mathbb{D}
such that
\omega^{\prime}\not=0
in
D(z_{0},r)
, otherwise in view the Uniqueness theorem, we have
\omega
is constant in
\mathbb{D}
. Differentiate (
3.3
) with respect to
\overline{z}
, we obtain

\omega^{\prime\prime}-\omega^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}-2(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})\omega+2\overline{\omega}\left(\frac{3}{2}(\omega^{\prime})^{2}+(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})\omega^{2}+\omega\left(\omega^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)=0

(3.4)

in
D(z_{0},r)
. Differentiate (
3.4
) with respect to
\overline{z}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\overline{\omega^{\prime}}\left(\frac{3}{2}(\omega^{\prime})^{2}+(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})\omega^{2}+\omega\left(\omega^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)=0,

i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}(\omega^{\prime})^{2}+(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})\omega^{2}+\omega\left(\omega^{\prime}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)=0~\text{in}~D(z_{0},r).

(3.5)

From (
3.4
) and (
3.3
), we have
\omega^{\prime\prime}-\omega^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime\prime}/\varphi^{\prime}-2(S_{f}-S_{\varphi})\omega=0
and
S_{f}-S_{\varphi}=0
in
D(z_{0},r)
, respectively. Thus, from (
3.5
), we have
\omega^{\prime}\equiv 0
in
D(z_{0},r)
, which contradicts the fact that
\omega
is non-constant in
\mathbb{D}
. Hence,
\omega\equiv a\in\mathbb{D}
and the
rest of the calculation follows from Theorem
3.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following theorem characterizes the log-harmonic mapping with the harmonic pre-Schwarzian derivative.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f(z)=z^{m}|z|^{2\beta m}h(z)\overline{g(z)}
be a locally univalent and sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
. Then the pre-Schwarzian derivative
P_{f}
of
f
is harmonic if,
and only if, the second complex dilatation
\omega
of
f
is constant.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=z^{m}|z|^{2\beta m}h(z)\overline{g(z)}
be a locally univalent and sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
that has harmonic pre-Schwarzian derivative
P_{f}
defined in (
3.1
). It is evident that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial\overline{z}}P_{f}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\frac{-|\omega^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1-|\omega|^{2})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\overline{\omega^{\prime}}\left((1-|\omega|^{2})\omega^{\prime\prime}+2(\omega^{\prime})^{2}\overline{\omega}\right)}{(1-|\omega|^{2})^{3}}.

As
P_{f}
is harmonic, we have
\overline{\omega^{\prime}}\left((1-|\omega|^{2})\omega^{\prime\prime}+2(\omega^{\prime})^{2}\overline{\omega}\right)\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{D}
.
Suppose that
\omega
is not constant in
\mathbb{D}
. Then, there exists a disk
D(z_{0},r):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-z_{0}|<r\}\subset\mathbb{D}
such that
\omega^{\prime}\not=0
in
D(z_{0},r)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle(1-|\omega|^{2})\omega^{\prime\prime}+2(\omega^{\prime})^{2}\overline{\omega}\equiv 0,~\text{\it i.e.,}~\omega^{\prime\prime}+\left(2(\omega^{\prime})^{2}-\omega\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)\overline{\omega}\equiv 0~\text{in}~D(z_{0},r).

(3.6)

Differentiate (
3.6
) with respect to
\overline{z}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(2(\omega^{\prime})^{2}-\omega\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)\overline{\omega^{\prime}}\equiv 0,~\text{\it i.e.,}~2(\omega^{\prime})^{2}-\omega\omega^{\prime\prime}\equiv 0~\text{in}~D(z_{0},r).

(3.7)

From (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have
\omega^{\prime\prime}\equiv 0
in
D(z_{0},r)
and hence, from (
3.7
), we have
\omega^{\prime}\equiv 0
in
D(z_{0},r)
. This contradicts the fact that
\omega

is not constant in
\mathbb{D}
. Thus,
\omega\equiv
constant in
\mathbb{D}
. Conversely, if
\omega\equiv a\in\mathbb{D}
, then the pre-Schwarzian derivative of
f
is given by
P_{f}=G^{\prime}/G+H^{\prime}/H
, which is analytic and hence, harmonic in
\mathbb{D}
, where
G=z^{(2\beta+1)m-1}g
,
H=zh^{\prime}+(\beta+1)mh
.
∎

The following theorem characterizes the log-harmonic mapping with the harmonic Schwarzian derivative.

.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f(z)=z^{m}|z|^{2\beta m}h(z)\overline{g(z)}
be a locally univalent and sense-preserving log-harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
\varphi(z)
be an analytic and locally univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(z)=(zh^{\prime}+(\beta+1)mh)z^{(2\beta+1)m-1}g
. If

\displaystyle|zP_{f}|+\frac{|z\omega^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})},

(3.14)

then
\varphi
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

The pre-Schwarzian derivative of
f
is given by
P_{f}=P_{\varphi}-\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}/\left(1-|\omega|^{2}\right)
,
where
\omega
is the second complex dilatation of
f
, which is also a Schwarz function in
\mathbb{D}
. Using (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle\left|zP_{\varphi}\right|=\left|zP_{f}\right|+\left|\frac{z\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right|\leq\left|zP_{f}\right|+\frac{\left|z\omega^{\prime}\right|}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Therefore, we have
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|zP_{\varphi}|\leq 1
. In view of the Becker’s univalence criterion,
\varphi
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. This completes the proof.

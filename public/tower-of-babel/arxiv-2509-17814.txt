CHARACTERISATION OF GEODESIC PRESERVING FUNCTIONS
MARCIN TOMBIŃSKI

Abstract
Let Ω 1 \Omega_{1} , Ω 2 \Omega_{2} be two domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} with Kobayashi metrics k Ω i k_{\Omega_{i}} and consider f ∈ 𝒪 ⁡ ( Ω 1 , Ω 2 ) f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}) a holomorphic mapping. Let 𝔉 1 \mathfrak{F}_{1} and 𝔉 2 \mathfrak{F}_{2} be a family of geodesics defined on Ω 1 \Omega_{1} and Ω 2 \Omega_{2} respectively, where a geodesic between z z and w w in Ω i \Omega_{i} is the length minimizing curve between the two points for the metric k Ω i k_{\Omega_{i}} . We say that a holomorphic function preserves geodesics if for any geodesic γ 1 \gamma_{1} in 𝔉 1 \mathfrak{F}_{1} its image is a subset of a geodesic γ 2 \gamma_{2} in 𝔉 2 \mathfrak{F}_{2} ( f ⁡ ( γ 1 ) ⊂ γ 2 f(\gamma_{1})\subset\gamma_{2} ). We aim to characterise the family of such functions between families of Kobayashi geodesics passing through a point in the unit disc 𝔻 \mathbb{D} and in the unit ball 𝔹 n \mathbb{B}^{n} . Some additional results in the complex plane ℂ \mathbb{C} and ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} .

For the case of the unit disc

Theorem 4
.

Geodesic preserving functions
f\in GP(\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{0})

f(z)=\alpha z^{n},

(1)

where
\alpha\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}

Proof.

Let

f\equiv\alpha
,

\gamma
passing through
\alpha
and we obtain the result. Let
f\not\equiv const

f
ensures that in a neighbourhood of
0

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
,
a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}

f
such that real axis is mapped into itself. Then all the coefficient
a_{n}
in

are real. Lets denote by

\gamma:=\{z=re^{i\phi}\}

f(z)=a_{k}r^{k}e^{ik\phi}+a_{k+1}r^{k+1}e^{i(k+1)\phi}+\dots

, where

and

f(z)=a_{k}z^{k}

is part of any geodesics which are in turn rotations of the real axis intersected with the unit disc, therefore we can compose the function

\alpha\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}
and

∎

In the previous result when

Furthermore intersecting the family of geodesic preserving functions with automorphism of the unit disc, we will obtain the automorphisms of the family of geodesics

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{a}
be the family of geodesics passing through
a\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}
, then for any nonconstant geodesics preserving function for
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{a}

a
is a fixed point, i.e.
f(a)=a
, or
\lim_{\xi\rightarrow a}f(\xi)=a
.

Proof.

For points in the unit disk, it is a trivial consequence of the geodesic preserving condition. Now consider a holomorphic function

satisfying the hypothesis and

on the unit circle. Let us a consider two sequences
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\gamma_{1}
,
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\gamma_{2}

d_{\mathbb{D}}(f(x_{n}),f(y_{n}))\leq d_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{n},y_{n}).

We know that the limit exists since the right-hand side tends to 0 as

∎

Going back to the question for families of geodesics passing through a point on the boundary

We easily notice that the the set of functions satisfying the condition (that the image of a geodesic falls within a geodesic) is non-empty. Theorem 9
.

For
a\in\mathbb{T}
, the geodesics preserving functions for the family of geodesics
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{a}
are either constant or compositions of a contraction, a translation and Mobius transformation, which fix the point
a
.

Proof.

Assume
f
is nonconstant, since the condition is obviously satisfied otherwise. Without loss of generality we can assume that
f(0)=0
(by superposing it by a Mobius map that fixes 0 and maps
f(0)
to 0) and
a=1
. For each point
p
in the disc we can map the geodesic
\gamma_{p}
passing through
p
into the real axis through the map
M_{p}
, where

M_{p}(z)=\frac{1-\bar{p}}{1-p}\frac{z-p}{1-\bar{p}z},

and vice versa by the inverse of
M_{p}
, where

M_{p}^{-1}(z)=\frac{(1-p)z+p(1-\bar{p})}{(1-p)\bar{p}z+(1-\bar{p})}.

We can plot a real function
L_{p}
for each
p\in\mathbb{D}
by superposing and composing
f
with those maps, that is

L_{p}(t)=M_{f(p)}(f(M_{p}^{-1}(t))),\ t\in(-1,1).

The function
L_{p}
preserves zero and has a Taylor series with only real coefficients around 0, that is
L_{p}(t)=\alpha_{1}t+\alpha_{2}t^{2}+\dots
, where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for each
i>1
. We can now compute the derivative of
L_{p}
at
0
, that is
\alpha_{1}
, and we will obtain the following

(L_{p})^{\prime}(0)=C_{p}\frac{1-\overline{f(p)}}{1-f(p)}f^{\prime}(p)\frac{1-p}{1-\bar{p}}\in\mathbb{R}

for each
p
, where
C_{p}=\frac{1-|p|^{2}}{1-|f(p)|^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore after some simple computations we get the following identity, which will allow us to determine the from of
f
, that is

\frac{(1-p)^{2}}{(1-f(p))^{2}}f^{\prime}(p)=C\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence if we consider
p
as a variable we get a differential equation that is easy to solve, that is:

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime}(p)}{(1-f(p))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{(1-p)^{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{1-f(p)}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{1}{1-p}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-f(p)}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{1-p}+B.

Using the fact that
f(0)=0
we can now easily compute
B=1-C
into the equation and we get an explicit formula of
f
.

f(z)=\frac{Cz}{1-(1-C)z},

where
C
has to be in the interval
(0,1]
in order for
f
to belong to
Hol(\mathbb{D})
. Those are the geodesics preserving functions that also fix 0. To get the entire characterization we have to superpose these functions with Mobius transforms in the unit disc fixing 1.

∎

One can ask what are the geodesic preserving functions between families of geodesics, where one family has its intersection point in
\mathbb{D}
and the other on the boundary
\mathbb{T}
. When the intersection point in the domain is in the interior and the intersection point in the target domain is on the boundary, it follows directly that the geodesic preserving functions have to be constant. The opposite case is more interesting.

Let
f
be a nonconstant holomorphic geodesic preserving function between
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{a}
and
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{b}
, where
a\in\mathbb{T}
and
b\in\mathbb{T}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
a=1
and
b=0
. Take the Cayley transform
C(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z}
and consider the function
F:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
, where
\mathbb{H}
is the right half-plane. Geodesics from
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{1}
are mapped to horizontal half-lines. Then the function
F
satisfies

\frac{F(z+t)}{F(z)}=c(t)\in\mathbb{R}

for any
z\in\mathbb{H}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
c
is a real valued function. Therefore, the function
F
has to have an exponential form, that is

F(z)=e^{g(z)}

for some holomorphic function
g
and we get

e^{g(z+t)}=e^{g(z)}c(t)=e^{g(z)+\tilde{c}(t)},

so

g(z+t)=g(z)+\tilde{c}(t).

By derivating both sides one obtains that
g
has to be a linear function, i.e.
g(z)=\alpha z+\beta
. Additionally

|F(z)|=|e^{\alpha z+\beta}|<1

\Re(\alpha z+\beta)<0,

for all
z\in\mathbb{H}
. Therefore
\alpha
is real and negative in order to keep the geodesics horizontal, and
-\beta\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}
.

Theorem 10
.

The geodesic preserving functions between
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{D}}_{0}
are of the form

f(z)=e^{a(\frac{1+z}{1-z})-\beta},

where
a<0
and
Re(\beta)\geq 0
.

We can extend all of the results above to any simply connected open subset
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}
(
\neq\mathbb{C}
) thanks to the Riemann Mapping Theorem. The Riemann Mapping Theorem states that there exists a biholomophism

Let

Geodesics preserving functions in the unit ball

Let us consider the same problem in several variables. As in the previous section, we’ll first consider the family of geodesics in the unit ball passing through the origin 0 denoted by
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{0}

Lemma 13
.

The non-constant holomorphic functions mapping lines in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
into lines in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
are
given by
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
, where

is a homogeneous polynomial of degree

or
f_{i}\equiv 0
for

, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Following a similar logic as in the 1-dimensional case, we know that
f
is holomorphic, therefore each
f_{i}
has to be holomorphic. In a neighbourhood of 0 we can express
f_{i}
as a Taylor series:

f_{i}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}}^{\infty}a_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}z_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{k_{n}}.

We want our function to keep the straightness of any line, therefore we require that there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}

f(tz)=t^{m}f(z)
, for each
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular each
f_{i}(tz)=t^{m}f_{i}(Z)
, so each component, mononomial, of the Taylor series has to be divisible by

. This means that the mononomials have to either be of degree
m
or equal

and we obtain the result.

∎

We can easily restrict this result to the unit ball getting a characterisation of the family of geodesics passing through the origin, 0, by restricting the coefficients.

The geodesic preserving functions for the family of geodesics
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{0}

We know from

In a strongly linearly convex domain, a geodesic preserving function preserves complex geodesics.

Proof.

Let

and
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}

\gamma\in\mathfrak{G}
be a geodesic line,
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
be the geodesic line in which lies
f(\gamma)
and
\phi_{\gamma},\phi_{\gamma^{\prime}}
be the complex geodesics in which lies
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
respectively.

From Theorem 3.1 in
[
KZ
]
we know that there exists domains
G,G^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and biholomorphisms
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}
such that
G,G^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
and
\Phi(\phi(z))=(z,0,\dots,0)
(
\Phi^{\prime}(\phi^{\prime}(z))=(z,0,\dots,0)
) for
z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
\Phi(\gamma)\subset(-1,1)\times\{0\}^{n-1}
and
\Phi^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime})\subset(-1,1)\times\{0\}^{n-1}
. Then
\tilde{f}:=\Phi^{\prime}(f(\Phi^{-1}))
maps
\mathbb{D}\times\{0\}^{n-1}
into itself, which means that the complex geodesic
\phi
is mapped into the complex geodesic
\phi^{\prime}
.
∎

From
[
JP
]
, one knows that complex geodesics
\phi
in the ball with
e_{1}\in\overline{\phi(\mathbb{D})}
can be expressed as the intersection of the ball with a complex line passing through
e_{1}
, i.e. an ellipsoid whose points
z=(z_{1},z^{\prime})
satisfy
(z_{1},\alpha_{1}(z_{1}-1),\dots,\alpha_{n-1}(z_{1}-1))
,
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
. This geometrical aspect implies that the geodesic rays originating from
e_{1}
lie on a unique complex geodesic and create a similar structure as in the disc case. Any complex geodesic can be obtained as the image of the horizontal unit disc
\mathbb{D}\times\{0\}^{n-1}
by an automorphism of the ball.

In
\mathbb{B}^{2}
each complex geodesic
\phi_{\alpha}
is uniquely determined by a variable
\alpha\in(-1,1)
and an automorphism
\varphi_{\alpha}
given by

\varphi_{\alpha}(z,w)=\left(\frac{(1-\alpha^{2})z+\alpha(w+\alpha)}{1+\alpha w},\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}(w+\alpha-\alpha z)}{1+\alpha w}\right),

which we can simplify for the horizontal disc to

\varphi_{\alpha}(z,0)=((1-\alpha^{2})z+\alpha^{2},\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}(1-z)).

Furthermore every geodesic line in
\phi_{\alpha}
is the image of a geodesic line on the disc through
\varphi_{\alpha}
.

The proof will revolve around the Cayley transform of the unit ball around
e_{1}=(1,0^{\prime})
. The Cayley transform
C_{e_{1}}
is given by

C_{e_{1}}(z)=i\frac{e_{1}+z}{1-z_{1}},

where
e_{1}=(1,0^{\prime})
and
z\neq e_{1}
.
The Cayley transform is a biholomorphism of the unit ball to the Siegel domain
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
consisting of points
(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}

\text{Im}z_{1}\geq|z^{\prime}|^{2}

where
|z^{\prime}|^{2}=|z_{2}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{n}|^{2}
, and

C^{-1}_{e_{1}}(z)=\frac{2z}{i+z_{1}}-e_{1}.

The transform behaves nicely on the complex and real geodesics. To ease the notations we shall consider the twodimensional case. The image of the complex geodesics
\phi(z_{1})=(z_{1},\alpha_{1}(z_{1}-1))
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, under the Cayley transform gives a halfline as a result of the following

C_{e_{1}}(\phi(z_{1}))=\left(i\frac{1+z_{1}}{1-z_{1}},i\alpha_{1}\right).

Furthermore geodesic lines are mapped to halflines with fixed real parts, that is
\forall\gamma\in\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{e_{1}}\exists a\in\mathbb{R},\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that
C_{e_{1}}(\gamma)=\{(a+it,i\alpha),t\geq|\alpha|^{2}\}
. We shall call these sets Siegel geodesics.

We can rephrase our problem as follows: what are the holomorphic functions from the Siegel domain
\mathcal{D}
into itself mapping Siegel geodesics into Siegel geodesics? In other words, the required functions have to map vertical halflines into vertical halflines on the first variable and the remaining composing functions are independent of the first variable.

Real geodesics on a complex geodesic
\phi
are images of real geodesics lying on a complex geodesic
\phi
are mapped to straight real halflines on the halfplane. In the disc case the Cayley transform maps the disc to the complex upper halfplane and the family of geodesics passing through 1 to halflines of the form
a+it
where
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. This indicates that the results should be similar to the disc case and indeed they are.

Theorem 19
.

Geodesic preserving functions for a family of geodesics passing through a point
a
on the boundary of the unit ball preserve complex geodesics passing through a.

Proof.

We can assume that the point
a
is
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)
. Let
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{e_{1}}
be the family of geodesics passing through
e_{1}
,
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
be a geodesic preserving function for the family
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{e_{1}}
and
\gamma\in\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{e_{1}}
a geodesic lying on the complex geodesic
F
.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
f(\gamma)\subset\mathbb{D}\times\{0\}^{n-1}
. We also know the formula describing a complex geodesics passing through a point on the boundary of the unit ball as it is the intersection of a complex line passing through the given point with the unit ball. For our case the complex geodesic
F
is given by the function
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}^{n}
given by

\phi(z)=(z,\alpha_{1}(z-1),\dots,\alpha_{n-1}(z-1)),\ z\in\mathbb{D},

\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
for
i=1,\dots,n-1.

Since
f_{i}(z)=0
for any
z\in\gamma
and
i>1
, we can apply the identity principle and we obtain that
f_{i}\equiv 0
for
i=2,\dots,n
on the complex geodesic
F
. Therefore,
f(F)
lies in the horizontal complex geodesic
\mathbb{D}\times\{0\}^{n-1}
.

One can obtain any complex geodesic passing through
e_{1}
by composing a complex geodesic with an automorphism of the ball fixing
e_{1}
. Thus the image of a complex has to lie in a complex geodesic.

∎

Theorem 20
.

Nonconstant geodesic preserving functions on the unit ball for a family of geodesics
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{a}
,
a\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
, are of the form
f(z,w)=C^{-1}\circ F\circ C
,
(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
,
C
is the Cayley transform and
F=(F_{1},F^{\prime})
satisfies

F_{1}(z,w)=mz+b(w)\ \ \text{and}\ \ |F^{\prime}(z,w)|^{2}\leq m\Im(z)+b(w),

where
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
b(w)
is a holomorphic function.

Proof.

We can assume that
a=e_{1}
. We’ll start by consiering the two dimensional case
\mathbb{B}^{2}
.

From the previous theorem, we know that every complex geodesic is mapped to a complex geodesic. Each complex geodesic
\phi_{\alpha}
is uniquely determined by a variable
\alpha\in(-1,1)
. By
\phi_{f(\alpha)}
we denote the complex geodesic in which lies in the image
f(\phi_{\alpha})
. Similarly to Theorem 1.6, we can construct a function
L_{\alpha}
mapping the horizontal unit disc
\mathbb{D}\times\{0\}
into itself,
\alpha\in(-1,1)
. Firstly, we map the unit disc to a complex geodesic
\phi_{\alpha}
with

\varphi_{\alpha}(z,0)=((1-\alpha^{2})z+\alpha^{2},\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}(1-z)).

Then we superpose it with our function
f
and ultimately compose it with
\varphi_{f(\alpha)}^{-1}
to map it back again on the horizontal unit disc, where
\varphi^{-1}_{\alpha}
is given by

\varphi^{-1}_{\alpha}(z,w)=\left(\frac{(1-\alpha^{2})z-\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}w}{1-\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}w-\alpha^{2}z},\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}w+\alpha z-\alpha}{1-\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}w-\alpha^{2}z}\right).

The map
L_{\alpha}(z,0)=(\varphi_{f(\alpha)}^{-1}\circ f\circ\varphi_{\alpha})(z,0)
satisfies the geodesic preservation condition and is a map from the unit disc to the disc, therefore for each
\alpha\in(-1,1)
,
L_{\alpha}
is a composition of a contraction, a translation and a Mobius Transformation fixing
1
.

Considering the problem in the Siegel domain
\mathcal{D}
corresponding to the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, we see that the complex geodesics are mapped to halfplanes with fixed
z_{2},\dots,z_{n}
. Moreover, on each complex geodesic, geodesics are mapped to segments
(a+it,z_{2},\dots,z_{n})
. The first fact tells us that the composing functions
F_{i}
(
F=(F_{1},\dots,F_{n})
) for
i>1
for a geodesic preserving function in the Siegel domain
\mathcal{D}
only depend on the variables
z_{i}
for
i>1
. The second one gives us a characterisation of the geodesic preserving function on each complex geodesic. Each geodesic line is mapped to a geodesic line, therefore for a fixed
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
a geodesic line
(\alpha+it,\alpha)
is mapped to
(a^{\prime}+it^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
, where
\alpha^{\prime}
and
a^{\prime}
are fixed and
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the required function is holomorphic, and is independent of the second variable, it can be considered as a function of one variable. The function
F_{1}(z)=F_{1}(x,y)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y)
fullfils the Cauchy identity on each geodesic line and on each of those the real part is independent from the imaginary part, so

\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}=0=\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}.

Thus
F_{1}
is a linear function, that is
F_{1}(z,w)=m(w)z+b(w)
, where
m
and
b
are functions of
w
. Additionally, the function
m(w)
is analytical and admits only positive real values (it would be rotate the geodesic lines otherwise), therefore
m(w)
is constant and we get
F_{1}(z,w)=mz+b(w)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
.

For the second composing function
F_{2}
we know that it only depends on the second variable
w
, is holomorphic and it satisfies

|F_{2}(z,w)|^{2}\leq\Im(F_{1}(z,w)).

We can rewrite this restriction as

|F_{2}(w)|^{2}\leq m\Im(z)+\Im(b(w)),

for all
z
and
w
belonging to
\mathcal{D}
.

For higher dimensions (
n>2
), the same argument holds with
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n-1})
.

∎

These results can be extended to any family of geodesics passing through a point on the unit sphere using automorphisms of the closed unit ball mapping the intersection point to
e_{1}.

.

Geodesic preservings functions
f
between
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{\zeta}
and
\mathfrak{G}^{\mathbb{B}^{n}}_{\xi}
, where
\zeta,\ \xi\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}

f(z)=\Phi_{\xi}^{-1}\circ g\circ\Phi_{\zeta}^{-1}(z),

where
g

3
Further research

This paper presents results for some elementary sets and very specific families of geodesics, however a case has been omitted, the geodesic preserving functions between a family of geodesics intersecting on the unit sphere and a family of geodesics intersecting in the origin. One could also ask for the case of any complete family of geoedesics in these sets. Furthermore, some more detailed could be obtained for other sets in
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The defenition of a geodesic preserving function is very general which allows the research of such functions in diverse spaces, sets or metrics.

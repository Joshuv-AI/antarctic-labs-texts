HetGCoT-Rec: Heterogeneous Graph-Enhanced Chain-of-Thought LLM Reasoning for Journal Recommendation CCS: Information systems → Recommender systems CCS: Information systems → Graph representations CCS: Computing methodologies → Natural language processing → Language models
Runsong Jia, Mengjia Wu, Ying Ding, Jie Lu, Yi Zhang

Abstract
. Academic journal recommendation requires effectively combining structural understanding of scholarly networks with interpretable recommendations. While graph neural networks (GNNs) and large language models (LLMs) excel in their respective domains, current approaches often fail to achieve true integration at the reasoning level. We propose HetGCoT-Rec, a framework that deeply integrates heterogeneous graph transformer with LLMs through chain-of-thought reasoning. Our framework features two key technical innovations: (1) a structure-aware mechanism that transforms heterogeneous graph neural network learned subgraph information into natural language contexts, utilizing predefined metapaths to capture academic relationships, and (2) a multi-step reasoning strategy that systematically embeds graph-derived contexts into the LLM’s stage-wise reasoning process. Experiments on a dataset collected from OpenAlex demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms baseline methods, achieving 96.48% Hit rate and 92.21% H@1 accuracy. Furthermore, we validate the framework’s adaptability across different LLM architectures, showing consistent improvements in both recommendation accuracy and explanation quality. Our work demonstrates an effective approach for combining graph-structured reasoning with language models for interpretable academic venue recommendations.



1.
INTRODUCTION

Academic journal recommendation requires effectively matching papers with appropriate venues while providing convincing explanations for the recommendations
(
Beel et al. 2016
)
. This presents unique challenges in combining semantic understanding of research content with complex heterogeneous academic network analysis. While recent advances in heterogeneous graph neural networks (HGNNs) have shown promise in capturing complex structural patterns
(
Wang et al. 2022
)
, and LLMs demonstrate capabilities in semantic comprehension and reasoning
(
Bommasani et al. 2021
)
, effectively integrating these capabilities for accurate and interpretable recommendations remains an open challenge.

Current approaches to this problem face significant limitations. HGNNs can effectively model the heterogeneous academic network structure and extract patterns from author collaboration networks and venue relationships
(
Hu et al. 2020b
)
, but they struggle to generate natural language explanations for their recommendations. Conversely, LLMs demonstrate strong capabilities in understanding research content and generating explanations but cannot directly process graph-structured information
(
Zhao et al. 2023
)
. Attempts to combine these approaches often resort to simple feature concatenation or treating graph information as supplementary context, failing to achieve true integration at the reasoning level.

To address these limitations, we propose
HetGCoT-Rec
, a framework that deeply integrates HGNNs with LLMs for academic journal recommendation through two key technical innovations:

•

Structure-aware mechanism:
Transforms HGNN-extracted subgraph information into natural language contexts. Building upon established metapath patterns
(
Sun et al. 2011
)
(e.g., author-venue relationships and venue connections), our framework leverages HGT embeddings to identify and convert relevant structural patterns into coherent reasoning chains.

•

Multi-step reasoning strategy:
Systematically embeds these graph-derived contexts into each step of the LLM’s chain-of-thought process
(
Wei et al. 2022
)
, enabling:

–

Graph structure analysis through metapath-guided reasoning

–

Paper content analysis enhanced by structural context

–

Author collaboration network analysis leveraging graph patterns

–

Structure-aware venue recommendations with detailed explanations

Through extensive experiments on the OpenAlex dataset
(
Priem et al. 2022
)
, we demonstrate that our framework achieves 92.21% accuracy (H@1) in journal recommendations, significantly outperforming both pure HGNN approaches and LLM baselines. The key contributions of this work include:

•

A novel framework for transforming heterogeneous graph structural information into LLM-processable reasoning chains

•

An integrated chain-of-thought reasoning process that systematically incorporates graph-derived information

•

Comprehensive empirical validation showing the effectiveness of deep HGNN-LLM integration in academic recommendation tasks

We make our implementation and datasets publicly available to support future research in this direction.

RELATED WORK

Academic recommendation systems have evolved from content matching to graph-based approaches, with recent advances in graph neural networks showing promising results in capturing academic network relationships. Several heterogeneous graph neural network architectures have been proposed to model the complex relationships in academic networks
(

;
Yao et al. 2020
;
Hu et al. 2020b
;
Zhang et al. 2019

. These approaches have made significant progress in understanding structural patterns and have established important foundations for academic recommendation tasks. The integration of textual content analysis with structural patterns presents opportunities for further enhancing recommendation performance.

Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in academic tasks through their semantic understanding abilities. Notable developments include specialized architectures for document-level academic text representation
(
Cohan et al. 2020
)
and comprehensive academic text understanding
(
Chen et al. 2021
)
. These advances have enabled sophisticated natural language processing in various academic tasks, including paper-reviewer matching
(
Liu et al. 2023
)
and research topic analysis. The potential for combining these semantic understanding capabilities with structural information from academic networks offers promising research directions.

The integration of graph neural networks and language models has been explored through various approaches. Initial methods focused on feature-level integration, combining GNN embeddings with text representations
(
Wang and Zhang 2023
;
Zhang et al. 2023
)
. More advanced approaches have emerged, such as attention-based fusion methods that incorporate graph structures into transformer architectures
(
Yang et al. 2022
)
. Meta-path based methods have shown particular promise in providing interpretable recommendations
(
Markchom et al. 2024
)
, highlighting the importance of maintaining structural information in the recommendation process. Recent developments in retrieval-augmented methods
(

)
have further demonstrated the potential of combining graph-based and language understanding approaches. These various integration attempts suggest opportunities for developing more sophisticated methods that can fully utilize both structural patterns and semantic understanding in academic recommendation tasks.

METHODOLOGY

In this section, we present a novel journal recommendation system integrating HGT with LLMs for academic paper-journal matching. Our system recommends suitable venues while providing reasoning chains for its decisions. Figure
1
illustrates the system architecture.

HetGCoT Overall architecture

We consider a heterogeneous academic graph
G=(V,E,\phi,\psi)
, where
V=V_{p}\cup V_{a}\cup V_{v}
represents the set of nodes comprising papers (
V_{p}
), authors (
V_{a}
), and venues (
V_{v}
).
E
denotes the set of edges
E=E_{PV}\cup E_{PA}
, capturing paper-venue and paper-author relationships, with
\phi:V\to A
mapping nodes to their types and
\psi:E\to R
mapping edges to their relationship types. Given a query paper
q
, our task is to recommend the most suitable venue from a candidate set while providing comprehensive explanations for the recommendation.

3.1.
Graph Structure Extraction

3.1.1.
Heterogeneous Graph Construction

We construct a heterogeneous academic graph using the OpenAlex dataset, incorporating three types of nodes: papers, authors, and venues. Node features are initialized by combining textual embeddings (for titles, abstracts, and keywords) obtained through Sentence-BERT
(
Reimers and Gurevych 2019
)
and numerical attributes (e.g., citation counts, impact factors for papers, affiliation information for authors). The edge set
E
consists of paper-venue (
E_{PV}
) and paper-author (
E_{PA}
) relationships.

For each node
v_{i}\in V
, we compute its representation through the Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT)
(
Hu et al. 2020a
)
:

(1)

h_{i}=\text{HGT}(v_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

where
d
is the dimension of the node embedding space. The HGT architecture is particularly well-suited for academic heterogeneous networks, as its type-specific transformations and attention mechanisms effectively capture the complex relationships between papers, authors, and venues in the scholarly ecosystem.

3.1.2.
Metapath Definition and Selection

Following the seminal work on heterogeneous network embeddings
(
Dong et al. 2017
)
, we define metapaths that are specifically designed to capture the unique structural patterns in academic networks:

(1)

APVPA
(Author-Paper-Venue-Paper-Author): Captures venue-based author connections

(2)

VPAPV
(Venue-Paper-Author-Paper-Venue): Identifies venue relationships through authors

(3)

APA
(Author-Paper-Author): Represents direct collaboration relationships

(4)

OAPVPAO
(Organization-Author-Paper-Venue-Paper-Author-Organization): Captures affiliation-based academic relationships

These metapaths effectively characterize different aspects of academic relationships, from direct collaborations to venue-mediated connections.

For a target paper
p
, we extract relevant metapaths using a similarity-based selection mechanism. The similarity between two metapaths
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
is computed as:

(2)

\text{sim}(P_{1},P_{2})=\cos\left(\text{mean}(h_{P_{1}}),\text{mean}(h_{P_{2}})\right)

where
h_{P_{i}}
represents the set of node embeddings along metapath
P_{i}
.

We select the most relevant metapaths using:

(3)

S=\text{TopK}(\{P\mid\text{sim}(P,P_{\text{target}})>\theta\})

where
P_{\text{target}}
is the set of metapaths associated with the target paper, and
\theta
is a similarity threshold.

3.2.
Chain-of-Thought Enhanced Recommendation

Given a query paper
q
and its extracted metapaths
M_{q}
, we define the chain-of-thought reasoning process
R
as a sequence of reasoning steps:

(4)

R(q,M_{q})=\{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4}\}

where each reasoning step is formulated as:

(5)

r_{i}=f_{i}(s_{i-1},m_{i})

with
s_{i-1}
representing the state from the previous step and
m_{i}
denoting the metapath information utilized in the current step.

To guide this reasoning process, we design a structured prompt template that systematically integrates graph-derived information with content analysis. The process consists of four key steps:

1) Graph Structure Analysis: The model processes raw metapath information (APVPA) to learn the structural patterns in the academic network.

2) Content Analysis: Examines the target paper’s specific information using metapaths (VPAPV), including title, abstract, citation count, FWCI, research topics, and authors.

3) Collaboration Analysis: Analyzes author collaboration patterns using metapaths (APA, OAPVPAO) to identify research relationships and joint work.

4) Journal Recommendation: Synthesizes the previous analyses to generate venue recommendations, considering both graph structure evidence and paper content matching.

The model is fine-tuned with structured prompts where system messages define the recommendation task and user messages provide step-by-step reasoning guidance.

3.3.
LLM Enhancement

We enhance the LLM’s reasoning capabilities through carefully designed prompts and targeted fine-tuning. The prompt template consists of two key components: a system message and a user message. The system message defines the model’s role as a journal recommendation expert and establishes critical constraints (e.g., selecting only from the provided journal list, prioritizing the most suitable journal in recommendations). The user message structures the input information in strict accordance with our four-step reasoning process, with each step containing specific questions and corresponding information.

For fine-tuning, we train the model to:

•

Transform graph-structured metapaths into coherent reasoning chains

•

Extract relevant information from multiple information sources (graph structure, paper content, collaboration patterns)

•

Generate evidence-supported recommendations by connecting structural patterns with semantic understanding

This enhancement approach enables the model to effectively process both structured graph information and unstructured text, producing recommendations that are grounded in both network analysis and content understanding.

4.
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP

Datasets.
We conduct experiments on the OpenAlex dataset, constructing a heterogeneous academic graph with 76,569 nodes and 105,290 edges. The nodes consist of 111 venues, 22,028 papers, and 54,430 authors. We extract node features and encode all textual information using Sentence-BERT embeddings to create initial node representations.

Evaluation Setting.
We evaluate our model using three metrics: Hit, H@1, and F1 score. Hit measures whether any correct answer appears in the model’s recommendations, while H@1 specifically evaluates the accuracy of the top recommendation. F1 score provides a balanced measure of precision (proportion of correct recommendations) and recall (proportion of true venues identified), calculated as the harmonic mean of precision and recall.

Baseline Algorithms.
We compare our approach against several baselines: (1) Pure GNN models including GCN and HGT, which only utilize graph structural information; (2) Zero-shot LLM approaches using GPT4o-mini without fine-tuning; and (3) Graph-structured Prompt, which applies our framework with the original LLM without fine-tuning. Additionally, we validate our framework’s adaptability by testing it with different LLMs including Qwen 1.5B, Qwen 7B, and LLaMA3 8B.

6.
CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS

In this work, we proposed HetGCoT-Rec, a framework that deeply integrates heterogeneous graph neural networks with large language models for academic journal recommendations. Our framework demonstrates the effectiveness of combining GNN-extracted structural information with LLM-based chain-of-thought reasoning. Through comprehensive experiments, we show that our approach not only achieves superior performance in recommendation accuracy but also provides interpretable results by effectively integrating metapath analysis, structural patterns, and semantic understanding in a coherent reasoning chain.

For future work, we plan to explore several directions: (1) extending experiments to larger-scale datasets to validate scalability, (2) incorporating richer graph structures such as citation networks and topic correlations, and (3) applying our framework to other heterogeneous graph scenarios beyond academic networks, as the combination of structural pattern extraction and language model reasoning could benefit various domains with complex heterogeneous relationships.

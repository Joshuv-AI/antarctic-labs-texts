Abstract
. We establish boundedness of the H ∞ H^{\infty} -calculus for the Dirichlet Laplacian on angular domains in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and corresponding wedges on L p L^{p} -spaces with mixed weights. The weights are based on both the distance to the boundary and the distance to the tip of the underlying domain. Our main motivation comes from the study of stochastic partial differential equations and associated degenerate deterministic parabolic equations on non-smooth domains. As a consequence of our analysis, we also obtain maximal L p L^{p} -regularity for the Poisson equation on angular domains in appropriate weighted Sobolev spaces.

Introduction

This article is concerned with the Laplace operator on domains with non-smooth boundary. This topic has been investigated extensively in the literature; see, e.g.,

[

,

,
35
,

,
49
,

,

,

and the references therein.
We are particularly interested in the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of the Laplace operator on domains with boundaries that exhibit corners and edges.
The
H^{\infty}
-calculus is known to provide a natural framework for the regularity analysis of (stochastic) partial differential equations ((S)PDEs), as demonstrated in various settings; see, e.g.,
[
18
,
19
,

,
40
,

,

]
as well as
[

,

,

,

]
.
This is one of the reasons why, over the past decades, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus has been established for a large number of sectorial operators, see the notes of
[
27
, Chapter 10]
and the references therein.

Driven by applications to (S)PDEs on domains with minimal boundary regularity, the study of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of the Laplace operator has recently been extended to
weighted
Sobolev spaces on domains with
C^{1}
-boundary
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
. The weights measure the distance to the boundary and are crucial for controlling the behaviour of solutions near the boundary; see the introduction of
[
44
]
and the references therein.

In this paper we push this analysis beyond
C^{1}
-domains by studying the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Dirichlet Laplacian on weighted
L^{p}
-spaces on planar angular domains

\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})=(r\cos\phi,r\sin\phi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon 0<r<\infty,\,0<\phi<\kappa\},

with arbitrary angle
0<\kappa<2\pi
and on the corresponding wedges
\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with arbitrary co-dimension
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
The main novelty is that we consider
mixed
power weights based on both the distance
\rho_{\mathcal{D}}
to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
and the distance
\rho_{\circ}
to the tip
\{0\}
of
\mathcal{D}
.
The domain of the operator is formulated in terms of (homogeneous) Sobolev spaces with weights of similar structure.

Our main motivation comes from the regularity analysis of
stochastic
partial differential equations and related partial differential equations with forcing terms degenerating at the boundary.
Over the last decade, it has been shown in a series of papers
[
10
,
11
,
32
,
34
]
that weighted Sobolev spaces with mixed weights based on both the distance to the boundary and the distance to the tip provide a suitable framework for an
L^{p}
-theory for these equations on angular domains and smooth cones.
The mixed weights are needed in order to capture the influence and the interplay of the two main sources for spatial singularities of the solution:
the incompatibility between noise and boundary conditions, which yields blow-ups of the higher-order derivatives of the solution along the boundary, and the singularities of the boundary, which have a similar effect in their vicinity (we refer to the introductions of
[
12
,
13
]
for details).
The results obtained so far rely on direct calculations by means of refined Green function estimates and (S)PDE techniques that are typical for the so-called analytic approach to SPDEs.

With this manuscript we aim to initiate an alternative, operator-theoretic approach to the regularity analysis of SPDEs and related PDEs on non-smooth domains.
For
1<p<\infty
and
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
we write

L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
for the
L^{p}
-space with respect to the measure with density
w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}:=\rho_{\circ}^{\nu-\gamma}\rho_{\mathcal{D}}^{\gamma}
relative to Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{D}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
0<\kappa<2\pi
and let
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}
.
Let
1<p<\infty
and let
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\},

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle\in\big(\!-\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}p-2,(2+\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa})p-2\big)\setminus\big\{(2-\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}n)p-2\,\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

Define the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}
on
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
as
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}u:=\Delta u
with domain

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}})

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\colon\rho_{\circ}^{-2}u,\rho_{\circ}^{-1}u_{x},u_{xx}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}),\,u|_{\partial\mathcal{D}\setminus\{0\}}=0\big\}.

Then
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle zero.

Theorem
A
is part of our main result, Theorem
4.1
, and will be proven in Section
4
.
The key in our proof is an extrapolation argument to extend the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
, which follows directly from the spectral theorem, to weighted
L^{p}
-spaces using the refined Green function estimates from
[
33
]
. Due to both the singularity in the tip of
\mathcal{D}
and the Dirichlet boundary conditions, these Green function estimates are not of the classical Gaussian form; they come with additional factors describing the behaviour near the boundary. Our key insight is that, by conjugating the Dirichlet Laplacian with a suitable pointwise multiplication operator, the resulting operator satisfies Gaussian estimates with respect to a weighted doubling measure, allowing us to apply the extrapolation result based on off-diagonal estimates from
[
6
]
. Afterwards, it remains to prove that the extrapolated operator is indeed the Dirichlet Laplacian on weighted
L^{p}
-spaces with domain as in Theorem
A
; for this we first characterise the homogeneous domain of the Dirichlet Laplacian by means of operator-sum techniques inspired by the analysis in
[
53
, Section 5]
. Remarkably, in stark contrast to
[
53
]
, we do not require operator-sum techniques for
non-commuting
operators.

Coming back to our main motivation described above, Theorem
A
has substantial consequences for the regularity analysis of (S)PDEs on non-smooth domains:

•

The admissible ranges for the weight parameters
\gamma
and
\nu
in Theorem

recover and extend the results from
[

,

,

, unveiling their operator-theoretic structure. We refer to Remark

-theory with differing integrability parameters in time and space.
At this stage, to the best of our knowledge, this is not possible within the analytic approach employed in
[

,

]

Both the extension of the range and the freedom in the integrability parameters are particularly relevant for the analysis of non-linear (S)PDEs via critical spaces along the lines of
[

2
,
3
]
.
In addition, similar results may be derived for stochastic integral equations on angular domains by means of
[

So far, the analysis of these equations is fully limited to equations on smooth domains or on

•

Recall that the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus with angle less than
\pi
implies bounded imaginary powers (see
[
15
]
). As a consequence, the (homogeneous) domains of fractional powers of the operator can be described as complex interpolation spaces (see
[

Thus, in all the applications described above, we would not only obtain abstract formulations in terms of domains of fractional powers of the Laplacian but rather very concrete descriptions of the different components of the equation in terms of appropriate weighted Sobolev spaces.

•

With the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Dirichlet Laplacian on angular domains at hand, we can set up the operator-valued functional calculus due to
[
30
]
and analyse maximal regularity and the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for more sophisticated operators and domains involving the Laplacian on angular domains, along the lines of
[
53
, Section 5]
.

To keep the exposition at a reasonable level we postpone all these applications to future papers and concentrate here on the analysis of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Dirichlet Laplacian on angular domains.

As mentioned before, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus has been established for a large number of operators.
However, to the best of our knowledge, not much is known about the functional calculus of differential operators in weighted
L^{p}
-spaces on conical and other non-smooth domains.
An exception is the analysis in
[
14
,
55
]
, where second-order differential operators on manifolds with conical singularities at the boundary are being analysed within certain classes of Kondratiev-type spaces with weights based on the distance to the conical singularities of the boundaries.
The setting therein goes far beyond the one considered in this manuscript, particularly in terms of the class of manifolds and differential operators.
However, for the special case of the Dirichlet Laplacian on a planar angular domain, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus is only proven in weighted
L^{p}
-spaces with power weights based solely on the distance to the tip, where the admissible power
\nu
has to satisfy (
4.4
), which is stricter than the condition imposed in Theorem
A
, and the weight is put to zero at infinity.
At this stage, in this approach, it is highly non-trivial how to include additional weights based on the distance to the boundary.

We choose the following outline: In Section
2
we fix some preliminaries which will be needed throughout the manuscript. In Section
3
we introduce different classes of weighted Sobolev spaces and discuss those properties and relationships among them, that will be needed in the proof of our main result. Section
4
contains our main result, Theorem
4.1
, and its extension to wedges
\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
of arbitrary co-dimension
m\in\mathbb{N}
is proven in Section
4.5
. Finally, in Section
5
, we present a consequence of our analysis on the regularity of the Poisson equation on angular domains in weighted Sobolev spaces, which extends a recent result from
[
13
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Linear operators and holomorphic functional calculus

For
0<\sigma<\pi
define the sector

\Sigma_{\sigma}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\colon|\arg(z)|<\sigma\}.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
A\colon X\supseteq\mathsf{D}(A)\rightarrow X
be a linear operator on
X
with domain
\mathsf{D}(A)
.
We write
\sigma(A)
for the spectrum of
A
and
\rho(A)
for its resolvent set, while
R(\lambda,A):=(\lambda-A)^{-1}
is the resolvent of
A
at
\lambda\in\rho(A)
.
We say that
A
is
sectorial
if
it is densely defined, has dense range, and
there exists a
\sigma\in(0,\pi)
such that
\sigma(A)\subseteq\overline{\Sigma_{\sigma}}
and

(2.1)

M_{\sigma,A}:=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\sigma}}}\left\lVert\lambda R(\lambda,A)\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(X)}<\infty.

The infimum over all possible
\sigma
is called the
angle of sectoriality of
A
and is denoted by
\omega(A)
.
A sectorial operator is necessarily closed, since its resolvent set is non-empty. Moreover, it is injective by
[
27
, Proposition 10.1.8]
.
The notion of sectoriality varies in the literature. For instance, the dense domain and dense range conditions are sometimes omitted.
However, if
X
is reflexive, then the condition that
A
is densely defined is always fulfilled if
\sigma(A)\subseteq\overline{\Sigma_{\sigma}}
for some
\sigma\in(0,\pi)
and (
2.1
) holds, see
[
27
, Proposition 10.1.9]
.

For
\sigma\in(0,\pi)
let
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})
denote the Hardy space of all bounded holomorphic functions
f\colon\Sigma_{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with norm

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})}:=\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\sigma}}|f(z)|
. Let
H^{1}(\Sigma_{\sigma})
denote the Hardy space of all holomorphic functions
f\colon\Sigma_{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\sigma})}:=\sup_{|\nu|<\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(e^{i\nu}\lambda)|\frac{\textup{d}\lambda}{\lambda}<\infty.

If
A
is sectorial,
\omega(A)<\nu<\sigma
, and
f\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{\sigma})
, then we define

f(A):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}f(\lambda)R(\lambda,A)\textup{d}\lambda,

where
\partial\Sigma_{\nu}
is the positively oriented boundary of
\Sigma_{\nu}
.
The integral is well-defined as a Bochner integral in
\mathscr{L}(X)
, since the sectoriality estimate of
A
yields

\left\lVert f(A)\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(X)}\leq\frac{M_{\nu,A}}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}|f(\lambda)|\frac{|\textup{d}\lambda|}{|\lambda|}\leq\frac{M_{\nu,A}}{\pi}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\sigma})}<\infty.

A sectorial operator
A
is said to have a
bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus
if there exists an angle
\sigma\in(\omega(A),\pi)
, such that

(2.2)

\left\lVert f(A)\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(X)}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})},\quad f\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})\cap H^{1}(\Sigma_{\sigma}),

with a constant
C\geq 0
that does not depend on
f
.
If
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus, we write
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A)
for the infimum over all possible
\sigma>\omega(A)
, such that (
2.2
) holds, and say that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A)
.
For detailed expositions on the
H^{\infty}
-calculus we refer to
[
25
,
27
,
39
,
54
]
.

In the sequel, we will need the following elementary lemma on isomorphic transformations of operators.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two Banach spaces, let
(A,\mathsf{D}(A))
be a linear operator in
X
, let
T\in\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
be an isomorphism, and let
(B,\mathsf{D}(B)):=(TAT^{-1},T\mathsf{D}(A))
. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

\rho(A)=\rho(B)
and
\sigma(A)=\sigma(B)
.

(ii)

If
A
is sectorial, then
B
is sectorial with
\omega(A)=\omega(B).

(iii)

If
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus, then so does
B
with
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(B)=\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A)
and
f(B)=Tf(A)T^{-1}
for all
f\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma}),\sigma\in(\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A),\pi)
.

Proof.

The assertions follow directly from the relation
R(\lambda,B)=TR(\lambda,A)T^{-1}
and hence

f(B)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}f(\lambda)TR(\lambda,A)T^{-1}\textup{d}\lambda=Tf(A)T^{-1}.\qed

In the construction of the Dirichlet Laplacian we will also use the following result. Its elementary proof is left to the reader.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and assume that
(X,Y)
is an interpolation couple.
Let
\dot{A}\in\mathscr{L}(Y,X)
be an isomorphism and let
A
be the linear operator on
X
given by

Au:=\dot{A}u\quad\text{with}\quad\mathsf{D}(A):=X\cap Y.

Then
A
is a closed linear operator in

and

in terms of equivalent norms, i.e.,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X\cap Y}=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{Y}\eqsim\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X}+\left\lVert Au\right\rVert_{X},\quad u\in\mathsf{D}(A).

Moreover, if
X\cap Y
is dense in
(Y,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{Y})
, then

(\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A)}):=(\mathsf{D}(A),\left\lVert A\,\cdot\right\rVert_{X})^{\sim}=(Y,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{Y}).

2.2.
Spaces of homogeneous type, Muckenhoupt weights and extrapolation

Let
(\mathcal{X},\delta)
be a metric space. For
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
r>0
we write

B(x,r):=B^{\mathcal{X}}(x,r):=B^{\mathcal{X}}(x,r;\delta):=\{y\in\mathcal{X}\colon\delta(x,y)<r\}

for the open ball with radius
r
centred at
x
.
Let
\mu
be a Borel measure on
(\mathcal{X},\delta)
, i.e., a measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
, such that
\mu(K)<\infty
for every compact
K\subseteq\mathcal{X}
.
We say that
\mu
satisfies the
volume doubling property
, if there exists a finite constant
C\geq 1
, such that

0<\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C\,\mu(B(x,r))<\infty,\quad x\in\mathcal{X},r>0.

In this case there exists a number
\tau>0
such that

(2.3)

\mu(B(x,r))\lesssim\bigg(\frac{\delta(x,y)+r}{s}\bigg)^{\tau}\mu(B(y,s)),\quad x,y\in\mathcal{X},\,r\geq s>0.

If
(\mathcal{X},\delta)
is a metric space and
\mu
is a Borel measure that satisfies the volume doubling property, then we call the triplet
(\mathcal{X},\delta,\mu)
a
(metric) space of homogeneous type
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\delta,\mu)
be a metric space of homogeneous type.
We call a

\mu
-locally integrable
function
w\colon\mathcal{X}\to(0,\infty)
a
weight
. For
1<p<\infty
we say that a weight
w\colon\mathcal{X}\to(0,\infty)
is a
(Muckenhoupt)
A_{p}
-weight
, denoted by
w\in A_{p}(\mu)
, if

[w]_{A_{p}(\mu)}:=\sup_{B}\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}w\,\textup{d}\mu\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\textup{d}\mu\bigg)^{p-1}<\infty,

the supremum being taken over all open balls
B
in
\mathcal{X}
. If
w\in A_{p}(\mu)

3.
Weighted Sobolev spaces

In this section we collect definitions and properties of suitable (vector-valued) weighted Sobolev spaces that we will need for our analysis in the subsequent sections.

3.1.
Uniformly weighted Sobolev spaces

We start with the definition and properties of weighted Sobolev spaces with weights that are chosen uniformly for all derivatives, i.e., the integrability condition is the same for all derivatives.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain, i.e., a connected open set.
Let
1<p<\infty
, let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
X
be a Banach space, and let
w\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathcal{O})
be a weight such that
w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathcal{O})
. Then

W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w;X):=\big\{u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w;X)\colon D^{\alpha}u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w;X),\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k\big\}

is the (vector-valued) weighted Sobolev space
of order
k
, which becomes a Banach space when endowed with the norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w;X)}:=\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w;X)},\quad u\in W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w;X).

We write
W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w):=W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w;\mathbb{C})
and omit the weight if
w\equiv 1
.

Below we will particularly make use of the weighted Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}};X)
on
\widehat{}\mathcal{D}
with
1<p<\infty
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.
In this case,

W^{k,p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}};X)\hookrightarrow W^{k,p}((0,\kappa),w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)};L^{p}(\mathbb{R},e^{\nu z}\textup{d}z;X)).

For any Banach space
Y
and
\gamma\in(-1,kp-1)\setminus\{jp-1\colon j\in\mathbb{N}\}
we have

W^{k,p}((0,\kappa),w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)};Y)\hookrightarrow C([0,\kappa];Y),

by
[
45
, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2]
and a localisation argument.
Hence, it makes sense to define the trace of
u\in W^{k,p}((0,\kappa),w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)};Y)
by
\mathrm{Tr}(u):=(u(0),u(\kappa))\in Y\times Y
. We set

W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}((0,\kappa),w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)};Y):=\{u\in W^{k,p}((0,\kappa),w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)};Y)\colon\mathrm{Tr}(u)=0\}.

Using
Y\curvearrowleft L^{p}(\mathbb{R},e^{\nu z}\textup{d}z;X)
, we conclude that

\displaystyle W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}};X)

\displaystyle:=W^{k,p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}};X)\cap W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}((0,\kappa),w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)};L^{p}(\mathbb{R},e^{\nu z}\textup{d}z;X))

\displaystyle\phantom{:}=\big\{u\in W^{k,p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}};X)\colon\mathrm{Tr}(u)=0\big\}

is a well-defined, closed subspace of
W^{k,p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}};X)
.

for the characterisation of the domain of the Dirichlet Laplacian. The proof is exactly the same as the proof of

Lemma 3.1

3.2.

C^{\infty}_{c,\textup{Dir}}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
is dense in
W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},w_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}})
.

Step 2.
Let
v\in W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,kp}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})
. Let
(\widehat{\chi}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\widehat{\chi}_{n}(z)\in[0,1]
for

,
\widehat{\chi}_{n}(z)=1
for
\lvert z\rvert\leq n
,
\widehat{\chi}_{n}(z)=0
for
\lvert z\rvert\geq n+2
, and
\lvert\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\chi}_{n}(z)\rvert\leq C_{\alpha}<\infty
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
and all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Let
v_{n}(z,\phi):=\widehat{\chi}_{n}(z)v(z,\phi)
,
(z,\phi)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\kappa)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
By the properties of
(\widehat{\chi}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, the

have compact supports in
\mathbb{R}\times[0,\kappa]
.
Moreover, using the Leibniz formula we obtain for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k

\lvert D^{\alpha}v_{n}\rvert\lesssim\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\,\lvert D^{\beta}v\rvert\text{ on }\widehat{\mathcal{D}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\alpha}v_{n}\to D^{\alpha}v\text{ a.e. on $\widehat{}\mathcal{D}$ for }n\to\infty.

Thus, since
v\in W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,kp}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})
, so are the
v_{n}
and

\left\lVert v-v_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,kp}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty

by the dominated convergence theorem.
Thus, by (
2.6
) and (
3.2
), if we set
\chi_{n}:=T_{0}^{-1}\,\widehat{\chi}_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\mathcal{D}}\setminus\{0\})
,

, then for all

it holds that

\left\lVert u-\chi_{n}u\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty,

where
\textrm{supp}(\chi_{n})\subseteq\{x\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}\colon e^{-n-2}\leq|x|\leq e^{n+2}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Step 3.
Let
u\in\dot{W}_{\textup{Dir}}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
, let
(\chi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be as constructed in Step 2 and let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C_{c,\textup{Dir}}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{D}}\setminus\{0\})
be a sequence approximating

in
\dot{W}^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
; the latter exists due to Step 1.
Let
(u_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}:=(\chi_{m}u_{n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C_{c,\textup{Dir}}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{D}}\setminus\{0\})
.
Then,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\lVert u-\chi_{m}u_{n}\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert u-\chi_{m}u\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})}+\left\lVert\chi_{m}u-\chi_{m}u_{n}\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert u-\chi_{m}u\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})}+C_{\textrm{supp}(\chi_{m}),\nu,k}\left\lVert\chi_{m}(u-u_{n})\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert u-\chi_{m}u\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})}+C_{\textrm{supp}(\chi_{m}),\nu,k}C_{\chi_{1},k}\left\lVert u-u_{n}\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})}.

Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
, if we choose
m=m(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
and
n=n(m,\varepsilon,\nu,k)\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we can construct a function
u_{\varepsilon}:=\chi_{m}u_{n}
, such that

\left\lVert u-u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma,\nu})}<\varepsilon,

which proves
(i)
.

Part
(ii)
may be proven mutatis mutandis. For Step 1 use that
T_{a}(C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}))=C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D})
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D})
is dense in
W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})
if

.
The latter follows by a localization argument and
[

, Lemma 3.4]
, which yields that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
is dense in
W_{\textup{Dir}}^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},w_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}})
, provided
(k-1)p-1<\gamma<kp-1
.
∎

We will also make use of the following consequences of Hardy’s inequality.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(3.7)

\displaystyle\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\dot{W}^{k-1,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}),\quad

\displaystyle\gamma>p-1,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\dot{W}^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\dot{W}^{k-1,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}),\quad

\displaystyle(k-1)p-1<\gamma<kp-1,

where
\dot{W}^{0,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma-p,\nu}):=L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
.

Proof.

For (
3.7
), by (
3.1
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle W^{k,p}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow W^{k-1,p}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}}).\quad

\displaystyle\gamma>p-1,

Let
u\in W^{k,p}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})
and
|\alpha|\leq k-1
. Then we have

D^{\alpha}u\in W^{1,p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}})\hookrightarrow W^{1,p}((0,\kappa),w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)};L^{p}(\mathbb{R})).

and hence

D^{\alpha}u\in L^{p}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})

by
[
38
, Section 8.8]
and a standard Fubini argument.
Therefore,
u\in W^{k-1,p}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,0}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})
. The proof of (
3.8
) is similar, using that

C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D})
is dense in
W^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}})
for
\gamma>(k-1)p-1
.
∎

3.3.
Krylov-Kondratiev type weighted Sobolev spaces

The weighted Sobolev spaces
\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
as well as
\dot{W}^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
introduced in Section
3.2
remind of Kondratiev spaces, as the powers on the distance to the boundary singularity changes with the order of the derivatives.
However, the power of the distance to the boundary was chosen to be constant.
In this section we discuss weighted Sobolev spaces with mixed power weights based on both the distance to the boundary singularity and the distance to the boundary, where both powers change with the order of the derivatives.
Such spaces have been recently used to study stochastic PDEs and related degenerate PDEs on smooth cones, see, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
,
13
,
32
,
34
]
.
Since both powers
vary with the order of the derivative, they remind of both the classical Kondratiev spaces and the spaces used by Krylov and collaborators for the study of stochastic PDEs and associated degenerate PDEs on (smooth) domains, see, e.g.,
[
31
,
37
,
47
]
for the latter.

We follow
[
13
]
and write
H^{s}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
for the following Krylov-Kondratiev type weighted Sobolev spaces:
Let
1<p<\infty
and let
\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we write

H^{k}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}):=\big\{u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D})\colon D^{\alpha}u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\lvert\alpha\rvert p+\Theta-2,\lvert\alpha\rvert p+\theta-2}),\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2},\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k\big\},

endowed with the norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{k}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}:=\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{\lvert\alpha\rvert p+\Theta-2,\lvert\alpha\rvert p+\theta-2})},\quad u\in H^{k}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}).

For
s\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
with
s\in(k,k+1)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
H^{s}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
as a complex interpolation space, i.e.,

H^{s}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}):=\big[H^{k}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}),H^{k+1}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\big]_{s-k}.

We refer to
[
13
, Section 3]
for alternative definitions of the spaces
H^{s}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
and their fundamental properties. We recall, in particular, that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
is dense in
H^{s}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
for all
s\geq 0
and all
\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, see
[
13
, Theorem 3.8]
.
The following lemma relates the Kondratiev type and the Krylov-Kondratiev type spaces.
It mainly relies on Hardy’s inequality and its consequences from Lemma
3.3
. It will be used, in particular, in order to establish a result on the regularity of the Poisson equation on
\mathcal{D}
, see Theorem
5.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, and
(k-1)p-1<\gamma<kp-1
.
Then

(3.9)

\dot{W}_{\textup{Dir}}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})=H^{k}_{p,\gamma+2-kp,\nu+2-kp}(\mathcal{D})

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

Let
(k-1)p-1<\gamma<kp-1
. Since as mentioned above and as proven in Lemma
3.2
,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
is dense in both
H^{k}_{p,\gamma+2-kp,\nu+2-kp}(\mathcal{D})
and
\dot{W}_{\textup{Dir}}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
, it suffices to prove

\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu+(\lvert\alpha\rvert-k)p}^{\mathcal{D}})}\eqsim\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma+(\lvert\alpha\rvert-k)p,\nu+(\lvert\alpha\rvert-k)p}^{\mathcal{D}})},\quad u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}).

On the one hand, ‘
\lesssim
’ holds since
\rho_{\mathcal{D}}\leq\rho_{\circ}
, so that
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\tilde{\gamma}_{0},\tilde{\nu}}^{\mathcal{D}})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\tilde{\nu}}^{\mathcal{D}})
for all
\tilde{\gamma}_{0},\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\tilde{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\tilde{\gamma}_{0}\leq\tilde{\gamma}_{1}
.
On the other hand, ‘
\gtrsim
’ follows from Lemma
3.3
, which yields that

\dot{W}^{k,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\hookrightarrow\dot{W}_{\textup{Dir}}^{k-1,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,\nu-p}^{\mathcal{D}})\hookrightarrow\dots\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-kp,\nu-kp}^{\mathcal{D}}).\qed

Remark 3.5
.

For
p-1<\gamma<2p-1
, Lemma
3.4
shows, on the one hand, that every
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{D})
with

(3.10)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-2p,\nu-2p}^{\mathcal{D}})}+\left\lVert Du\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,\nu-p}^{\mathcal{D}})}<\infty,

has trace zero (in the sense introduced in Section
3.2
).
On the other hand, it provides a refined description of the behaviour near the boundary of those functions in
u\in\dot{W}^{2,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
that have trace zero, i.e., such
u
have to satisfy (
3.10
).
Moreover, Lemma
3.4
yields alternative descriptions of
\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
, i.e., it shows that for
p-1<\gamma<2p-1
,

\displaystyle\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in H^{1}_{p,\gamma+2-2p,\nu+2-2p}(\mathcal{D})\colon D^{2}u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma-p,\nu-p}^{\mathcal{D}})\colon D^{2}u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\tilde{\gamma},\nu-p}^{\mathcal{D}})\colon D^{2}u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\tilde{\gamma}+p,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\big\}

with
-1<\tilde{\gamma}:=\gamma-p<p-1
. On the other hand, for
-1<\gamma<p-1
,

\displaystyle\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in H^{1}_{p,\gamma+2-p,\nu+2-2p}(\mathcal{D})\colon D^{2}u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu-p}^{\mathcal{D}})\colon D^{2}u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\big\}.

Hence, the difference between the two
\gamma
-ranges is the norm used to control
D^{2}u
.

4.
The Dirichlet Laplacian on angular domains

In this section we establish the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of the (suitably defined) Dirichlet Laplacian on the weighted
L^{p}
-spaces
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
for a proper range of parameters
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. Our goal is the proof of the following main result, see also Section
4.5
for a generalisation to wedges with arbitrary codimension.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

(4.1)

\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}\quad\text{and}\quad\nu\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\big\{(2\pm\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}n)p-2\,\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

Define the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
on
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
as

\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}u:=\Delta u\quad\text{with}\quad\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}):=\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}).

Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu},\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}))
is closed.

(2)

For
u\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
we have

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{2,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})}\eqsim\left\lVert\Delta u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})}.

(3)

\dot{\mathsf{D}}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}):=(\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}),\left\lVert\Delta\,\cdot\,\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})})^{\sim}=\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
.

(4)

If, in addition,

(4.2)

-\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}p-2<\nu<(2+\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa})p-2,

then
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
is a sectorial operator with a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle zero.
In particular,
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
generates an analytic semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
, which is consistent over
p,\gamma,\nu
.

Our strategy for proving Theorem
4.1
is the following: In Section
4.1
we establish the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus in
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
via form methods. In Section
4.2
we extrapolate this functional calculus to suitable weighted
L^{p}
-spaces by means of Theorem
2.3
.
In Section
4.3
we will prove that the homogeneous Dirichlet Laplacian, i.e., the Laplacian as an operator from
\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
to
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
, is an isomorphism—provided (
4.1
) holds.
The latter proves the first three parts of Theorem
4.1
by means of Lemma
2.2
.
Finally, in Section
4.4
, in order to establish the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the negative Dirichlet Laplacian introduced in Theorem
4.1
, we show that if, in addition, (
4.2
) holds, then it coincides with the extrapolated operator from Section
4.2
.

Before we present the proof in detail, we make some remarks on Theorem
4.1
, its relevance and consequences.
As outlined in the introduction, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus with an angle less than
\pi/2
implies maximal regularity. In the following remark we relate the results on maximal regularity that may be obtained from Theorem
4.1
to the existing literature.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

The regularity of elliptic and parabolic PDEs on conical domains has been studied extensively within the framework of Sobolev spaces with power weights based on the distance to the boundary singularities. We refer to
[
7
,
16
,
35
,
36
,
49
,
51
,
57
]
and the literature therein, just to name a few.
Recently, maximal regularity has also been established for SPDEs and related PDEs with degenerating forcing at the boundary within the framework of the weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{s}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
of Krylov-Kondratiev type
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
32
,
34
]
.
However, these results have been obtained predominantly by means of
(S)PDE techniques rather than by exploring the properties of the operators in the equations.
Exceptions may be found in
[
41
,
42
,
53
]
, where operator sum techniques are employed to establish maximal regularity, however, without establishing boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus.

Theorem
4.1
reveals the operator-theoretic structure behind the results mentioned above.
The admissible range therein is large enough to show that, at least for the (stochastic) heat equation with Dirichlet boundary condition on angular domains, the results obtained so far fall into the framework of
[
59
]
, i.e., in these situations, from an operator-theoretic perspective, maximal regularity follows from the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of the leading operator.
For instance, the corresponding results in
[
32
,
34
]
on the maximal regularity of the stochastic heat equation in weighted Sobolev spaces with mixed weights follow from Theorem
4.1
with

(4.3)

p-1<\gamma<2p-1\quad\text{and}\quad(2-\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa})p-2<\nu<(2+\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa})p-2

by means of
[
59
]
.
Also, our range is large enough to cover the related results from

[
10
,
11
,
12
,
46
,
51
,
53
]
, where the (stochastic) heat equation is studied in weighted
L^{p}
-Sobolev spaces with weights based solely on the distance to the tip, always under the restriction that

(4.4)

(2-\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa})p-2<\nu<(2+\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa})p-2.

Note that (
4.2
) is less restrictive and closed under
L^{p}
-duality.

In addition, Theorem
4.1
also allows for several extensions of the results mentioned above. In particular, as already mentioned in the introduction, other than in
[
32
,
34
]
, we are able to allow for differing integrability parameters with respect to time and space, which, at least at this stage, is not possible within the approach used so far.
Moreover, since the admissible range in Theorem
4.1
is considerably larger than (
4.3
), we have more freedom with the parameters, which is particularly important when moving towards nonlinear (S)PDEs
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
Finally, it is worth noting that our result can also be used to obtain maximal regularity results for (stochastic) Volterra equations on angular domains by means of
[
20
]
. To the best of our knowledge, these would be the first results for such stochastic evolution equations on angular domains.

(2)

In addition to the references mentioned in the introduction, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Dirichlet Laplacian on angular domains is also claimed in
[
48
, Proposition 4]
.
However, the proof therein is unfortunately not correct due to an algebraic mistake in the calculations.
In our notation, the authors write
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}=T_{a+2}^{-1}(A+B+L_{0})T_{a},
where
A+B+L_{0}
is an operator sum with a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus. They then conclude

R(\lambda,-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}})=T_{a}^{-1}R(\lambda,A+B+L_{0})T_{a+2}
, which is unfortunately only valid for
\lambda=0
.

(3)

Note that, since
0\notin\rho(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
, the characterisation of the homogeneous domain provided in part
3
of Theorem
4.1
is an important building block in the derivation of (stochastic) maximal regularity via
[
59
]
.

The following remark addresses the restrictions we impose on the weight parameters.

Remark 4.3
.

(1)

The constraints on the weight parameters
\gamma
and
\nu
in (
4.1
) stem from our proof that the Laplacian is an isomorphism from
\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
to
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
given in Section
4.3
.
We prove the latter by first transforming the operator into cylindrical coordinates and then employing operator sum techniques inspired by
[
53
]
.
This reduces the analysis of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\mathcal{D}
to properties of the Laplace Beltrami operator on the intersection of the unit sphere
S^{1}
and
\mathcal{D}
, which coincides with the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{(0,\kappa)}
on the interval
(0,\kappa)
.
In particular, we use that
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{(0,\kappa)}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle zero in
L^{p}((0,\kappa),w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)})
, which we get from
[
45
]
exactly for the
\gamma
-range specified in (
4.1
).
Moreover, when transforming into cylindrical coordinates in such a way that we get rid of the radial weight, the power on
\rho_{\circ}
translates into a shift that depends on
\nu
.
This forces us to exclude certain values that depend on the spectrum of
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{(0,\kappa)}
from the admissible range of
\nu
, which yields the restriction on
\nu
in (
4.1
).
The additional restriction (
4.2
) on
\nu
comes from the application of the extrapolation result in Theorem
2.3
to our situation in Theorem
4.7
.

(2)

Although, as pointed out in Remark
4.2
, the range of admissible parameters in Theorem
4.1
is large enough to cover the maximal ranges considered in the literature and even goes beyond that, we do not claim sharpness.
In particular, it is unclear to us, whether the exclusion of

\nu\in\big\{(2-\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}n)p-2\,\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

in the range for which we obtain boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus is necessary or just due to our proof technique (the restriction comes from the isomorphy proof, see part
1
).
Also, we do not fully cover the unweighted setting.
For instance, for
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, i.e., if
\kappa=\pi
, the case
\gamma=\nu=0
is excluded in Theorem
4.1
by (
4.1
), which means that we do not make any statement about the boundedness of the

-calculus for the negative Dirichlet Laplacian in
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})=L^{2}(\mathcal{D},w_{0,0}^{\mathcal{D}})
with domain
\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{2,0,0})
. However, it is well-known that if the domain is set to

.
The results in
[

indicate that the latter might even hold for certain

-Sobolev spaces.
This analysis is not part of this manuscript.

4.1.
Boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus in unweighted
L^{2}
-spaces

We first consider the negative Dirichlet Laplacian in the unweighted space
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
. In this case we obtain the desired boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus easily by employing form methods.
Our standard reference concerning forms and associated operators is
[
21
]
.

Let
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{D})
be the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
in
W^{1,2}(\mathcal{D})
.
Consider the bounded sesquilinear form

\mathfrak{a}\colon W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{D})\times W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathbb{C},\quad(u,v)\mapsto\mathfrak{a}(u,v):=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,\textup{d}x.

The linear operator
(L,\mathsf{D}(L))
associated with
\mathfrak{a}
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
is called the
negative Dirichlet Laplacian in
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
and is given by

\mathsf{D}(L)=\{u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{D})\colon\Delta u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{D})\},\quad Lu=-\Delta u.

The following proposition collects some well-known properties of the negative Dirichlet Laplacian.
Note that the statement also holds if we replace
\mathcal{D}
by any open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(L,\mathsf{D}(L))
be the negative Dirichlet Laplacian in
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
introduced above. Then the following holds.

(i)

L
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
0
.

(ii)

The Dirichlet Laplacian
-L
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
of contractions, which extends to a bounded analytic semigroup of any angle
0<\vartheta<\frac{\pi}{2}
.

(iii)

For all
t>0
, there is a a nonnegative kernel

G_{t}^{\mathcal{D}}\colon\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

e^{-tL}f=\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{t}^{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot,y)f(y)\,\textup{d}y,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{D}).

Proof.

Since the form
\mathfrak{a}
is symmetric, accretive, and sectorial of angle
0
, the operator
(L,\mathsf{D}(L))
is densely defined, self-adjoint, and
\sigma(L)\subseteq[0,\infty)
. Thus,
(i)
is a straightforward application of
[
27
, Proposition 10.2.23]
.
To see
(ii)
, note that
(i)
implies that
-L
generates a bounded analytic semigroup of any angle
0<\vartheta<\frac{\pi}{2}
, see, e.g.,
[
27
, Theorem G.5.2]
. The contractivity of the semigroup on
[0,\infty)
follows from the Lumer-Phillips theorem. For a detailed proof of
(iii)
we refer to
[
4
, Theorem 4.2.4]
.
∎

4.2.
Extrapolation of the functional calculus

For the extrapolation of the functional calculus, we will use the following heat kernel estimate, which may be found in
[
33
, Theorem 2.1]
(see also
[
8
,
5
]
).
In the sequel, we write

R_{t,x}:=\frac{|x|}{|x|+\sqrt{t}}\quad\text{and}\quad J_{t,x}:=\frac{\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)}{\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+\sqrt{t}},\qquad t>0,\,\,x\in\mathcal{D}.

Recall that, throughout,
0<\kappa<2\pi
is fixed and that we do not track the dependencies of the constants on
\kappa
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(G_{t}^{\mathcal{D}})_{t>0}
be as in Proposition
4.4
(iii)
.
Then there exists a constant
c>0
, such that for all
0<\lambda<\frac{\pi}{\kappa}
,

(4.5)

G_{t}^{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)\lesssim_{\lambda}R_{t,x}^{\lambda-1}R_{t,y}^{\lambda-1}J_{t,x}J_{t,y}\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t},\quad t>0,\,\,x,y\in\mathcal{D}.

In order to extrapolate the
H^{\infty}
-calculus from
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
to appropriate weighted
L^{p}
-spaces by means of Theorem
2.3
, we need to translate (
4.5
) and link the objects
R_{t,x}
and
J_{t,x}
therein to the volume of a ball with respect to appropriate doubling measures. This will be done by means of the following lemma.
We will extensively use the notation introduced in Section
2.3
.
In particular,

B(x,r):=B^{\mathcal{D}}(x,r),\quad x\in\mathcal{D},\quad r>0.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
-2<\nu<\infty
and let
-1<\gamma<\infty
. Then

w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(x,r))\eqsim_{\nu,\gamma}r^{2}(\rho_{\circ}(x)+r)^{\nu-\gamma}(\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r)^{\gamma},\quad x\in\mathcal{D},\,r>0.

Proof.

Let
C_{\kappa}:=\max\{2,2/\kappa\}
. We consider three different cases.

Case 1. Balls close to the origin.

Assume that
x\in\mathcal{D}
and
r>0
are such that
C_{\kappa}r\geq\rho_{\circ}(x)
.
In this case,

\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r\eqsim r\eqsim\rho_{\circ}(x)+r.

This and the volume doubling property of
w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}
in the form of (
2.3
),
yield that

w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(x,r))\eqsim_{\gamma,\nu}w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(0,r))\eqsim_{\gamma,\nu}r^{2+\nu}\eqsim_{\gamma,\nu}r^{2}(\rho_{\circ}(x)+r)^{\nu-\gamma}(\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r)^{\gamma}.

Case 2. Interior balls.
Assume that
x\in\mathcal{D}
and
r>0
are such that
C_{\kappa}r<\rho_{\circ}(x)
and
2r<\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)
. Then for
z\in B(x,r)
,

\rho_{\circ}(z)\eqsim\rho_{\circ}(x)+r\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(z)\eqsim\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r,

so
w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}(z)\eqsim(\rho_{\circ}(x)+r)^{\nu-\gamma}(\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r)^{\gamma}
. Hence

w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(x,r))\eqsim_{\nu,\gamma}(\rho_{\circ}(x)+r)^{\nu-\gamma}(\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r)^{\gamma}\,|B(x,r)|\eqsim r^{2}(\rho_{\circ}(x)+r)^{\nu-\gamma}(\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r)^{\gamma}.

Case 3. Balls close to the boundary.
Finally, assume that
x\in\mathcal{D}
and
r>0
are such that
C_{\kappa}r<\rho_{\circ}(x)
and
2r\geq\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)
.
By Lemma
2.5
and its proof,

(4.6)

w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(x,r))\eqsim_{\gamma,\nu}w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}(S(x,r))\eqsim_{\gamma}w_{\nu+1}^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(B^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\lvert x\rvert,r))\cdot w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)}\Big(B^{(0,\kappa)}\big(\textrm{Arg}(x),\tfrac{r}{\lvert x\rvert}\big)\Big).

Since
2r\leq C_{\kappa}r<\rho_{\circ}(x)
, we have
s\eqsim|x|
for all
s\in B^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\lvert x\rvert,r)
and therefore

(4.7)

w_{\nu+1}^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(B^{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\lvert x\rvert,r))\eqsim_{\nu}\lvert x\rvert^{\nu+1}r.

Moreover, since we assumed that
r/\lvert x\rvert<\kappa/2
, the volume doubling property of
w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)}
yields

(4.8)

w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)}\Big(B^{(0,\kappa)}\big(\textrm{Arg}(x),\tfrac{r}{\lvert x\rvert}\big)\Big)\eqsim_{\gamma}w_{\gamma}^{(0,\kappa)}\Big(B^{(0,\kappa)}\big(0,\tfrac{r}{\lvert x\rvert}\big)\Big)\eqsim_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{r}{\lvert x\rvert}\Big)^{\gamma+1}.

Thus, inserting (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) in (
4.6
) leads to

w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(x,r))\eqsim_{\gamma,\nu}\Big(\frac{r}{\lvert x\rvert}\Big)^{\gamma+1}\lvert x\rvert^{\nu+1}r=r^{2}\,\lvert x\rvert^{\nu-\gamma}\,r^{\gamma}\eqsim_{\gamma,\nu}r^{2}\,(\rho_{\circ}(x)+r)^{\nu-\gamma}\,(\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r)^{\gamma},

where in the last step we used
|x|\eqsim\rho_{\circ}(x)+r
and
\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+r\eqsim r
.
∎

Now we can extrapolate the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of the negative Dirichlet Laplacian on
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
as follows.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(L,\mathsf{D}(L))
be the negative Dirichlet Laplacian on
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
. Let
1<p<\infty
, let
0<\lambda<\frac{\pi}{\kappa}
, and let
0<\sigma<\pi
. Then

(4.9)

\left\lVert f(L)\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{D},vw_{2-p,(2-p)\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}))}\lesssim_{p,\sigma,\lambda}[v]_{A_{p}(w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}})}^{\max\{\frac{1}{p-1},1\}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty},\quad v\in A_{p}(w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}),\,\,f\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma}).

In particular, if
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

(4.10)

-1-p<\gamma<2p-1\quad\text{and}\quad-\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}p-2<\nu<\big(2+\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}\big)p-2,

then

(4.11)

\left\lVert f(L)\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}))}\lesssim_{p,\sigma,\gamma,\nu}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty},\quad f\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma}).

Proof.

We already know from Proposition
4.4
that
L
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
0
on
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
.
Moreover,
-L
generates the semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
, which has kernel representations with kernels
(G_{t}^{\mathcal{D}})_{t>0}
that satisfy the estimate (
4.5
). Rearranging this estimate by means of Lemma
4.6
yields that there exists a constant
\mathfrak{c}>0
such that for all
t>0
,
x,y\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle G^{\mathcal{D}}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\lambda}R_{t,x}^{\lambda-1}R_{t,y}^{\lambda-1}J_{t,x}J_{t,y}\frac{e^{-\mathfrak{c}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{|x|^{\lambda}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)}{\rho_{\circ}(x)}\Big)|y|^{\lambda}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(y)}{\rho_{\circ}(y)}\Big)\,\,e^{-\mathfrak{c}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{\sqrt{t(|x|+\sqrt{t})^{2\lambda-2}(\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)+\sqrt{t})^{2}}\sqrt{t(|y|+\sqrt{t})^{2\lambda-2}(\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(y)+\sqrt{t})^{2}}}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\eqsim_{\lambda}\frac{w_{1,\lambda}(x)w_{1,\lambda}(y)}{\sqrt{w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(x,\sqrt{t}))}\sqrt{w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}}e^{-\mathfrak{c}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}.

For a function
u\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
let
Mu(x):=w^{\mathcal{D}}_{1,\lambda}(x)u(x)
,
x\in\mathcal{D}
.
Then
the corresponding operator
M\in\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(\mathcal{D},w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}),L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))
is an isometric isomorphism.
Thus, since
(L,\mathsf{D}(L))
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
0
on
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
, so does the operator
(M^{-1}LM,M^{-1}\mathsf{D}(L))
on
L^{2}(\mathcal{D},w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}})
, see Lemma
2.1
. Moreover,
the semigroup generated by
-M^{-1}LM
on
L^{2}(\mathcal{D},w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}})
satisfies for all
t>0

e^{-tM^{-1}LM}f=M^{-1}e^{-tL}Mf=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot,y)f(y)\,w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}(y)\,\textup{d}y,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{D},w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}),

with

\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)=\frac{G_{t}^{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)}{w_{1,\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}(x)w_{1,\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}(y)},\quad t>0,\,x,y\in\mathcal{D}.

By (
4.2
) these kernels satisfy

\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{D}}_{t}(x,y)\lesssim_{\lambda}\frac{e^{-\mathfrak{c}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{\sqrt{w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(x,\sqrt{t}))}\sqrt{w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}},

so that, by the doubling property of
w^{\mathcal{D}}_{2,2\lambda}
(see (
2.3
)), for any constant
0<c<\mathfrak{c}
, there exists a finite constants
C>0
that only depends on
\lambda
and
c
, such that

|\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)|\leq C\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}(B(x,\sqrt{t}))},\quad t>0,\,\,x,y\in\mathcal{D}.

Thus, an application of Theorem
2.3
with
(A,\mathsf{D}(A))\curvearrowleft(M^{-1}LM,M^{-1}\mathsf{D}(L))
and
\mu\curvearrowleft w^{\mathcal{D}}_{2,2\lambda}

yields that

\left\lVert f(M^{-1}LM)\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{D},vw_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}))}\lesssim_{p,\sigma,\lambda}[v]_{A_{p}(w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}})}^{\max\{\frac{1}{p-1},1\}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty},\quad v\in A_{p}(w_{2,2\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}),\,\,f\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma}).

This, together with the identity
f(M^{-1}LM)=M^{-1}f(L)M
and the fact that
M\in\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{2,2\lambda}),L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w^{\mathcal{D}}_{2-p,(2-p)\lambda}))
is an isometric isomorphism, proves (
4.9
) for all
0<\lambda<\pi/\kappa
, which, in turn, yields (
4.11
) for all
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
4.10
) due to Lemma
2.5
(iii)
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Theorem
4.7
shows that, for the full range (
4.10
),
the semigroup generated by
(-L,\mathsf{D}(L))
extrapolates to an analytic semigroup on
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
.
Estimate (
4.11
) yields that its negative generator has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle zero.
However, at this stage, it is not clear whether this extrapolated operator can indeed be identified as a Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions and whether its domain can be characterised in terms of (weighted) Sobolev spaces, cf. Remark
2.4
.
In what follows, we establish such a characterisation under the restriction that (
4.1
) and
(
4.2
)
hold.
This means that we exclude all

\nu\in\big\{(2-\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}n)p-2\,\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\},

which is due to the isomorphy proof in the next subsection, see Remark
4.3
2
. Moreover, we exclude

-p-1<\gamma<-1,

which seems reasonable, since for this range, the domain characterisation already fails to hold for the half-space, see
[
45
, Proposition 5.16]
.

Remark 4.9
.

The proof strategy used in Theorem
4.7
extends beyond the present setting, since refined heat kernel bounds are available for a broad class of differential operators.
For instance,
for a wide class of differential operators subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions, the generated semigroup has an integral representation whose
kernels typically satisfy the two-sided estimate

k_{t}(x,y)\eqsim J_{t,x}J_{t,y}\,\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2}},

where the factors
J_{t,x},J_{t,y}
encode the parabolic distance to the boundary, similar to Proposition
4.4
, cf.
[
9
,
17
]
.
An adaption of the proof of Lemma
4.6
(in particular, Step 3 therein) entails that, e.g., for a bounded Lipschitz domain
\mathcal{O}
,

w_{\gamma}^{\mathcal{O}}(B(x,r))\eqsim_{\gamma}r^{d}(\rho_{\mathcal{O}}(x)+r)^{\gamma},\quad x\in\mathcal{O},r>0.

Therefore, refined kernel bounds can be combined with the conjugation argument in the proof of Theorem
4.7
to obtain weighted functional calculi for a wide range of operators, enlarging the range of admissible weights beyond those
obtainable by extrapolation from the unweighted setting.

4.3.
The homogeneous Dirichlet Laplacian

So far, we have seen that the negative Dirichlet Laplacian
(L,\mathsf{D}(L))
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle zero in
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
, which may be extrapolated to weighted spaces
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w)
for suitable weights
w\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathcal{D})
by means of Theorem
4.7
.
However, at this stage, it is not clear how the extrapolated operator relates to the negative Dirichlet Laplacian from Theorem
4.1
.
In order to establish this connection, we first consider the
homogeneous
Dirichlet Laplacian and prove the following result.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy (
4.1
).
Then the homogeneous Dirichlet Laplacian

\dot{\Delta}_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}\colon\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\to L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}),\quad u\mapsto\Delta u,

is an isomorphism. In particular,

(4.13)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{2,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})}\eqsim\left\lVert\Delta u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})}\eqsim\left\lVert D^{2}u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})},\quad u\in\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}).

For the proof of Proposition
4.10
we use a technique inspired by the proof of
[
53
, Corollary 5.2]
.
The main idea is to reduce the statement about the Dirichlet Laplacian on the two-dimensional angle
\mathcal{D}
to properties of the functional calculus of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the unit sphere intersected with
\mathcal{D}
, which in this simple situation coincides with the Dirichlet Laplacian on the interval
\mathcal{I}:=(0,\kappa)
in suitable weighted
L^{p}
-spaces.
To prepare for the proof, first note that for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the Laplacian in Euler coordinates has the form

\Delta u=T_{a}^{-1}M_{2}^{-1}((D_{z}-a)^{2}+D_{\phi}^{2})T_{a}u,\quad u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D}).

Recall that for all
1<p<\infty
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle T_{a}

\displaystyle\in\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}),L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu+2-ap}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}})),

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle\in\mathscr{L}(L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu+2-ap}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}}),L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu+2-(2+a)p}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}}))

are isomorphisms.
Moreover, also the bounded operator

T_{a}\in\mathscr{L}(\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}}),W^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu+2-(2+a)p}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}}))

is an isomorphism, provided
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
(see (
3.2
) with
k=2
). Thus, in order to verify Proposition
4.10
, it is enough to check that for appropriate
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists an
a\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

W^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu+2-(2+a)p}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}})\ni u\mapsto\big((D_{z}-a)^{2}+D_{\phi}^{2}\big)u\in L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,\nu+2-(2+a)p}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}})

is an isomorphism.
In particular, if we want to get rid of the weight with respect to the distance to the tip of
\mathcal{D}
, we may choose

a=a(\nu,p)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\nu+2-(2+a)p=0
, and it is enough to show that

(4.14)

W^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}})\ni u\mapsto\big((D_{z}-a)^{2}+D_{\phi}^{2}\big)u\in L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}})

is an isomorphism for this particular
a
.
To prove the latter for
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
4.1
), we will make use of three auxiliary results. The first one addresses the
(D_{z}-a)^{2}
-part in (
4.14
).
For a precise definition of the UMD property for Banach spaces, its properties and relevance we refer to
[
26
, Chapter 4]
.
L^{p}
-spaces have the UMD property and so do
L^{p}
-spaces of
X
-valued functions if
X
is a UMD Banach space, see
[
26
, Proposition 4.2.15]
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
, let
P_{a,b,c}(z):=az^{2}+bz+c,z\in\mathbb{C}
, let
1<p<\infty
, and let
X
be a UMD Banach space.
Let
B_{a,b,c}
be the unbounded linear operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R};X)
given by

B_{a,b,c}u:=P_{a,b,c}(D)u=aD^{2}u+bDu+cu,\quad\text{with}\quad\mathsf{D}(B_{a,b,c}):=W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R};X).

Then

B_{a,b,c}
is a closed operator with

\sigma(B_{a,b,c})=P_{a,b,c}(i\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, if
a<0
and
\Re(c)>0
, then
B_{a,b,c}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus with
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(B_{a,b,c})<\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Proof.

This result is a consequence of Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem
[
26
, Theorem 5.3.18]
in the same manner as
[
22
, Theorem 5.1]
.
∎

The next lemma addresses the
D_{\phi}^{2}
-part in (

).

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=(0,\kappa)
,
let
X
be a UMD Banach space, let
1<p<\infty
, and let
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}

\varphi>0
there exists a

\lambda-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I},X}

-calculus of angle

.

(ii)

Let
X=\mathbb{C}
or let

, where
(S,\Sigma,\mu)

-finite measure space. Then, for all

, the operator

has a bounded

-calculus of angle zero
and
\sigma(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I},X})=\big\{n^{2}\pi^{2}/\kappa^{2}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

Proof.

Part
(i)
follows from
[
45
, Theorem 6.1]
.
For the proof of
(ii)
, assume first that

. Then
(ii)
is an immediate consequence of

, Corollary 6.2]
and the well-known fact that
\sigma(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I},\mathbb{C}})=\big\{n^{2}\pi^{2}/\kappa^{2}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}

and

[

To verify
(ii)

(R(\lambda,-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I},L^{p}(S)}f)(\cdot,x)=R(\lambda,-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I},\mathbb{C}})f(\cdot,x),\quad x\in S,\,f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{I},w_{\gamma}^{\mathcal{I}};L^{p}(S)).

∎

Put together, the two lemmas above yield the following result.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let

, let

Then the operator sum

with domain

is a closed operator in

(4.15)

\mathsf{D}(B+(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}))=W^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}})\quad\text{with equivalent norms}.

yields that there is an

.

4.12
yields that

, Theorem 8.5]

together with the closedness of both

.
∎

Now we are ready to present the proof of the main result in this section.

Proof of Proposition
4.10
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy (
4.1
). We only have to prove that
\dot{\Delta}_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
is an isomorphism, which automatically implies (
4.13
).
Let
a:=\frac{\nu+2}{p}-2
, so that
\nu+2-(2+a)p=0
.
As explained above, in order to prove that
\dot{\Delta}_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
is an isomorphism, it suffices to show that the operator in (
4.14
) is an isomorphism. To this end, let
B
be as in Lemma
4.11
with
a\curvearrowleft-1,b\curvearrowleft 2a,c\curvearrowleft-a^{2},X\curvearrowleft L^{p}(\mathcal{I},w_{\gamma}^{\mathcal{I}})
and let
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}:=-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I},X}
be as in Lemma
4.12
with
X\curvearrowleft L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.
These two lemmas together with
[
52
, Theorem 8.5]
yield

\sigma(B+(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}))\cap\mathbb{R}\subseteq\{\sigma(B)+\sigma(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})\}\cap\mathbb{R}=\Big\{\frac{\pi^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}n^{2}-a^{2}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

In particular,
0\notin\sigma(B+(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}))
whenever
a^{2}\notin\{n^{2}\pi^{2}/\kappa^{2}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, i.e., whenever

\nu\notin\big\{\big(2\pm\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}n\big)p-2\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\},

which is exactly our assumption on
\nu
.
Therefore, since we also assume that

, Lemma
4.13
yields that

B+(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})\colon W^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}})\to L^{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}},w_{\gamma,0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}}),\quad u\mapsto-\big((D_{z}-a)^{2}+D_{\phi}^{2}\big)u,

∎

Remark 4.14
.

The proof strategy above is inspired by
[
53
, Section 5]
, where a similar technique is employed for the analysis of the heat equation on wedges.
However, since we only consider the elliptic equation, we avoid many of the complications of
[
53
, Section 5]
, as our operators
B
and
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}
are resolvent commuting.
This gives us more flexibility, which result in an extended range of admissible parameters
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.
A similar strategy was employed in
[
41
,
42
]
for the Neumann case.
In both the Dirichlet and the Neumann case, it is crucial that the boundary condition is fully incorporated
in the corresponding Dirichlet or Neumann Laplacian on
\mathcal{I}
.

Although we concentrate on the Laplacian, it is likely that the proof technique above can be modified in order to obtain appropriate isomorphisms for larger classes of differential operators.
A crucial tool is the application of
[
52
, Theorem 8.5]
in the proof of Lemma
4.13
. This result can be modified as follows: Instead of assuming that both
A
and
B
have bounded imaginary powers on some UMD space
X
, we may assume that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus and
B
is
R
-sectorial such that the sum of the respective angles is smaller than
\pi
. This and
[
30
, Theorem 6.3]
ensure that
A+B
with domain
\mathsf{D}(A)\cap\mathsf{D}(B)
is closed, which was previously ensured by the Dore-Venni Theorem. The rest of the proof of
[
52
, Theorem 8.5]
stays the same.
This implies that instead of considering the sum
B+(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})
in the proof above, we could sum up
B+C
for any (reasonable) linear operator
C
that is
R
-sectorial and is resolvent commuting with
B
.

4.4.
Proof of the main theorem

Using the results from the previous sections, we are now able to prove our main theorem concerning the functional calculus of the negative Dirichlet Laplacian.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Parts
1
and
2
are immediate consequences of Proposition
4.10
and Lemma
2.2
with
X\curvearrowleft L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
and
Y\curvearrowleft\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
.
For part
3
we need, in addition, that
\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
is dense in
\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
, which is guaranteed by Lemma
3.2
.

It remains to prove
4
.
To this end, fix
1<p<\infty
and let
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).
Moreover, let
(L,\mathsf{D}(L))
be the negative Dirichlet Laplacian in
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
introduced in Proposition
4.4
.
Then, due to Theorem
4.7
, the semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}:=(e^{-tL})_{t\geq 0}
generated by
(-L,\mathsf{D}(L))
extrapolates to a strongly continuous semigroup
(S_{p,\gamma,\nu}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
. We write
(L_{p,\gamma,\nu},\mathsf{D}(L_{p,\gamma,\nu}))
for the negative of the generator of
(S_{p,\gamma,\nu}(t))_{t\geq 0}
. Due to the consistency of the semigroups
S
and
S_{p,\gamma,\nu}
and of the corresponding resolvents, Estimate (
4.11
) from Theorem
4.7
yields that
(L_{p,\gamma,\nu},\mathsf{D}(L_{p,\gamma,\nu}))
is sectorial and has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle zero. Thus, in order to obtain the assertion, it suffices to show that

(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu},\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}))=(-L_{p,\gamma,\nu},\mathsf{D}(L_{p,\gamma,\nu})).

We first show that
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}\subseteq-L_{p,\gamma,\nu}
, i.e., that

(4.16)

\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})\subseteq\mathsf{D}(L_{p,\gamma,\nu})\quad\text{and}\quad-L_{p,\gamma,\nu}u=\Delta u,\,\,u\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}).

To this end,
we first note that,
due to the consistency of the semigroups,
for all
u\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})\cap\mathsf{D}(L)
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle S_{p,\gamma,\nu}(t)u-u=S(t)u-u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S(s)(-L)u\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S(s)\Delta u\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}S_{p,\gamma,\nu}(s)\Delta u\,\mathrm{d}s,

where the integrals converge in
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
.
Thus,
for all
u\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})\cap\mathsf{D}(L)
,
since
\Delta u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
and
S_{p,\gamma,\nu}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu})
, we have

\frac{S_{p,\gamma,\nu}(t)u-u}{t}\longrightarrow\Delta u\quad\text{in}\quad L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\quad\text{ for }t\searrow 0.

Therefore,

-L_{p,\gamma,\nu}u=\Delta u

for all
u\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})\cap\mathsf{D}(L)
.
In order to obtain (
4.16
) we use the density of
\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})\cap\mathsf{D}(L)
in
\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
, which follows from Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
C_{c,\textup{Dir}}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{D}}\setminus\{0\})\subseteq\mathsf{D}(L)
.
Let
u\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
and let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})\cap\mathsf{D}(L)
be such that
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
. Then,

u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\quad\text{and}\quad-L_{p,\gamma,\nu}u_{n}=\Delta u_{n}\to\Delta u\text{ in }L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\qquad(n\to\infty).

Thus, since
L_{p,\gamma,\nu}
is a closed operator in
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
, it follows that
u\in\mathsf{D}(L_{p,\gamma,\nu})
and
-L_{p,\gamma,\nu}u=\Delta u
. Since
u\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
was arbitrary, (
4.16
) is proven.

In order to show the reverse direction, i.e., that
-L_{p,\gamma,\nu}\subseteq\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
, it suffices to verify that
1-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
is surjective and that
1+L_{p,\gamma,\nu}
is injective. The latter follows from the fact that
L_{p,\gamma,\nu}
is sectorial.
So it only remains to prove that
1-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
is surjective. We argue as follows:
First, let
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
. Then,
u:=R(1,-L)f\in\mathsf{D}(L)
satisfies
u-\Delta u=f
. Since the resolvents
R(1,-L)
and
R(1,-L_{p,\gamma,\nu})
are consistent, we also get that
u\in\mathsf{D}(L_{p,\gamma,\nu})\subseteq L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
.
In particular,
u-f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}(\mathcal{D}))
, so that, by Proposition
4.10
, there exists a unique
v\in\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
such that
\Delta v=u-f=\Delta u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
. Thus, in the sense of distributions,

(u,\Delta\psi)=(\Delta u,\psi)=(\Delta v,\psi)=(v,\Delta\psi),\quad\psi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{D}).

Therefore,
since for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
there exists
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
such that
\Delta\psi=\varphi
, we have

(v,\varphi)=(u,\varphi),\quad\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}),

which shows that
v=u
and therefore
u\in\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})=\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
and
u-\Delta u=f
.

Now let
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
. Then, since
L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{2}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
is dense, we can choose a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\cap L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
, such that
\left\lVert f_{n}-f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})}\to 0
for
n\to\infty
.
Then, by the continuity of the resolvent,

u_{n}:=R(1,-L_{p,\gamma,\nu})f_{n}\longrightarrow R(1,-L_{p,\gamma,\nu})f=:u\,\,\text{ in }L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\qquad(n\to\infty).

Therefore, since, by the first part,
u_{n}\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}\subseteq-L_{p,\gamma,\nu}
,

(1-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})u_{n}=(1+L_{p,\gamma,\nu})u_{n}\longrightarrow(1+L_{p,\gamma,\nu})u=f\,\,\text{ in }L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})\quad(n\to\infty).

Thus, since
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}
is closed,

u\in\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})
and
u-\Delta u=f
.
This shows the required surjectivity, which concludes the proof.
∎

In this section we extend the results from Theorem
4.1
4
to wedge domains, that is, the Cartesian product of the cone
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m},m\in\mathbb{N}
.
To this end we introduce the following notation: Let

.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
1<p<\infty
, let
v\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and let
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\},

\displaystyle\nu

be the unbounded operator on

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu,v}u

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{\Delta}_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu}u+\Delta^{p,v}u\quad\text{with}\quad\mathsf{D}(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu,v}):=\mathsf{D}(\widetilde{\Delta}_{\textup{Dir}}^{p,\gamma,\nu})\cap\mathsf{D}(\Delta^{p,v}).

(ii)
combined with Theorem

4
ensures that

.

5.
The Poisson Equation on angular domains

As a consequence of our analysis in Section
4.3
, we obtain the following theorem on the (higher order) regularity of the Poisson equation with zero Dirichlet boundary condition on
\mathcal{D}
within the scale of Krylov-Kondratiev type weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{s}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
introduced in Section
3.3
.
It generalizes
[
13
, Theorem 4.1(ii)]
, where only the case
p=2
has been considered.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
s\geq 0
, and let
\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

(5.1)

1<\Theta<p+1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\theta-p}{p}\notin\big\{\pm\tfrac{\pi}{\kappa}n:\ n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

Then for all
f\in H^{s}_{p,\Theta+p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{D})
there exists a unique
u\in H^{s+2}_{p,\Theta-p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})
such that

(5.2)

\Delta u=f\quad\text{on }\mathcal{D}.

Moreover,

(5.3)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{s+2}_{p,\Theta-p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}_{p,\Theta+p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{D})}

with a constant that does not depend on
f
and
u
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:=\Theta-2+p
and let
\nu:=\theta-2+p
. Then, the restriction (
5.1
) on
\Theta
and
\theta
imply that
\gamma
and
\nu
satisfy (
4.1
) with the additional restriction that
p-1<\gamma<2p-1
.
The latter implies that, due to Lemma
3.4
,
H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})=\dot{W}^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
.
Moreover, by definition,
H^{0}_{p,\Theta+p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{D})=L^{p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}})
. Thus, by Proposition
4.10
, the statement holds for
s=0
. It therefore also holds for
s\in\mathbb{N}
due to
[
13
, Theorem 4.1(i)]
, which allows us to lift the regularity. Finally, the statement for
s\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
follows by interpolation, see
[
13
, Theorem 3.11]
.
∎

Appendix A
Differential calculus under change of variables

In this section we prove two basic results on the relationship between derivatives in Cartesian coordinates and in Euler coordinates.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.
Let
\Lambda_{\alpha}:=\{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\colon|\beta|\leq|\alpha|\}
.
Then, for all
\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}
, there exist trigonometric polynomials
P_{\alpha,\beta}
on
(0,\kappa)
such that

(A.1)

(T_{\Psi}\circ T_{\Phi})(D^{\alpha}u)=M_{-|\alpha|}\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}}P_{\alpha,\beta}D^{\beta}((T_{\Psi}\circ T_{\Phi})(u)),\quad u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D};X).

Moreover, for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

(A.2)

T_{a}(D^{\alpha}u)=M_{-|\alpha|}\sum_{\lvert\beta\rvert\leq\lvert\alpha\rvert}\widetilde{P}_{\alpha,\beta}D^{\beta}(T_{a}u),\quad u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D};X),

with suitable trigonometric polynomials
\widetilde{P}_{\alpha,\beta}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
,
\lvert\beta\rvert\leq\lvert\alpha\rvert
.

Proof.

First note that (

The following lemma links the Kondratiev type weighted Sobolev spaces
\dot{W}^{k,p}(\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{D}};X)
on
\mathcal{D}
from Section
3.2
and the uniformly weighted Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\widehat{}\mathcal{D},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\widehat{}\mathcal{D}};X)
.
For
\mathcal{O}\in\{\mathcal{D},\widehat{\mathcal{D}}\}
and
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
we write

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu}:=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{O}})},\quad u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma,\nu}^{\mathcal{O}};X),

and extend this definition to any Borel measurable function
u\colon\mathcal{O}\to X
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
\gamma,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, let
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then,

(A.5)

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}(T_{a}u)\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu+2-(k+a)p}\eqsim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu+(|\alpha|-k)p},\quad u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D};X).

Proof.

Throughout this proof, if
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
, we write
x\varphi
for the mapping

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})\ni\varphi\mapsto\{\mathcal{D}\ni(x,y)\mapsto x\varphi(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}\}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}),

and extend this to
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D};X)
by setting

xu(\varphi)=u(x\varphi),u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D};X),\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}).

Analogously, we write
y\varphi
for the mapping

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})\ni\varphi\mapsto\{\mathcal{D}\ni(x,y)\mapsto y\varphi(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}\}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}),

and extend this to
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D};X)
by setting

yu(\varphi)=u(y\varphi)
,
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D};X)
,
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
.
Using these conventions, we first prove that for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
, for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
0\leq\ell\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|
, there exist constants
c_{\alpha,\beta,\ell,a}\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

(A.6)

D^{\alpha}T_{a}u=T_{a}\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}\sum_{\ell=0}^{|\beta|}c_{\alpha,\beta,\ell,a}x^{\ell}y^{|\beta|-\ell}D^{\beta}u.

For
\alpha=0
this is clear.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
we argue by mathematical induction on
\lvert\alpha\rvert\in\mathbb{N}
.
The base case
\lvert\alpha\rvert=1
holds, since

D_{z}T_{a}u=T_{a}(xD_{x}+yD_{y}+a)u,\qquad D_{\phi}T_{a}u=T_{a}(-yD_{x}+xD_{y})u.

The induction step
\lvert\alpha\rvert\mapsto\lvert\alpha\rvert+1
follows from straightforward calculations by using the base case.
To prove (
A.5
), note that, by Lemma
A.1
it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu+(|\alpha|-k)p}

\displaystyle\eqsim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert T_{a}D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu+2+(|\alpha|-a-k)p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}T_{a}u\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu+2-(a+k)p}.

Moreover, using Young’s inequality with
\frac{\ell}{|\beta|}+\frac{|\beta|-\ell}{|\beta|}=1
,
0\leq\ell\leq\lvert\beta\rvert
,
\beta\neq 0
, ensures that

\displaystyle\lvert x\rvert^{\ell}\lvert y\rvert^{|\beta|-\ell}\leq\frac{\ell}{|\beta|}\lvert x\rvert^{|\beta|}+\frac{|\beta|-\ell}{|\beta|}\lvert y\rvert^{|\beta|}\leq\lvert x\rvert^{|\beta|}+\lvert y\rvert^{|\beta|}\lesssim(\lvert x\rvert^{2}+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{\frac{|\beta|}{2}},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus, using (
A.6
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}T_{a}u\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu+2-(a+k)p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\sum_{\lvert\beta\rvert\leq\lvert\alpha\rvert}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lvert\beta\rvert}\left\lVert x^{\ell}y^{|\beta|-\ell}D^{\beta}u\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu-kp}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{p,\gamma,\nu+(|\alpha|-k)p}.

This proves the lemma.

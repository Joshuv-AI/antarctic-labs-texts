Introduction

Harmonic maps are nonlinear extensions of harmonic functions, arising as critical points of energy functionals restricted to manifolds, which naturally lead to systems of equations involving quadratic gradient terms.
Such maps arise in physical models such as liquid crystals

as

Now, multiplying by

\Psi^{\prime}(t_{s})t_{s}\sim C_{\beta}\,t_{s}^{k+1}\,s,

and taking logarithm,

Hence, for sufficiently small

hence, proving the second part of the claim and yielding the desired contradiction. ∎

Claim 4.14

Now, setting

We now present a non-existence result that will be useful in what follows. This result can also be seen as an immediate extension of

Lemma 4.15
.

Assuming (
1
), for every
\Lambda_{2}>0
, there exists

, independent of

-\Delta u=\lambda c(x)u+b(x)|Du|+\mu\beta(u)|Du|^{2}+h(x)+ac(x),\quad u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)

has no solution for

Proof.

We will divide the proof in three cases:
u
being a sign-changing solution, i.e.,
\Omega^{+}:=\{x\in\Omega\,|\,u(x)>0\}
and
\Omega^{-}:=\{x\in\Omega\,|\,u(x)\leq 0\}
both nonempty,
u
being nonnegative and
u
being nonpositive.

Case 1:

u
is sign-changing.

Let
u^{+}:=\max\{u,0\}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a test function in (
4.9
). This yields

(4.10)

\int_{\Omega}|Du^{+}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\big[\lambda c(x)u+b(x)|Du|+\mu\beta(u)|Du|^{2}+h(x)+ac(x)\big]u^{+}.

Now, using Poincaré inequality and the fact that, by Theorem
4.13
, there exists
K>0
independent of
\lambda
such that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq K
, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{p}K|\Omega|\geq C_{p}\|u^{+}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\int_{\Omega}|Du^{+}|^{2},

Defining

C:=C_{p}K\,|\Omega|,

and noting that

u\,u^{+}=(u^{+})^{2},\;Du\cdot Du^{+}=|Du^{+}|^{2},

it follows that every term on the right-hand side of (
4.10
), except for the integral

\int_{\Omega}h\,u^{+}
, is nonnegative. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\geq a\int_{\Omega}cu^{+}+\int_{\Omega}hu^{+}+\mu\int_{\Omega}\beta(u)|Du^{+}|^{2}u^{+}+\int_{\Omega}b(x)|Du^{+}|u^{+}+\lambda\int_{\Omega}c(u^{+})^{2},

\displaystyle\geq a\int cu^{+}-\int_{\Omega}|h|u^{+}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\beta(u^{+})|Du^{+}|^{2}u^{+}-\int_{\Omega}b(x)|Du^{+}|u^{+},

\displaystyle\geq a\int cu^{+}-K\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}-\mu\beta(K)K\|Du^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-K\|b\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|Du^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq a\int cu^{+}-K\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}-\mu\beta(K)C-\|b\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}C,

which, due to
u^{+}
being uniformly bounded, yields a contradiction for large enough
a
.

Case 2:

u
is nonnegative.

It is essentially the same idea as Case 1, just that instead of
u^{+}
as a test function, we use
u
.

Case 3:

u
is nonpositive.

Reasoning as in the Case 1, let
u^{-}:=\max\{0,-u\}
be a test function in (
4.9
),

\int_{\Omega}|Du^{-}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\big[\lambda c(x)u+b(x)|Du|+\mu\beta(u)|Du|^{2}+h(x)+ac(x)\big]u^{-}.

Next, we use Theorem
1.2
to obtain
C_{1}>0
such that
\|u^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}
, and Poincaré inequality to obtain

C:=C_{p}C_{1}|\Omega|\geq C_{p}\|u^{-}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\int_{\Omega}|Du^{-}|^{2}.

Now, by similar reasoning as in Case 1, it follows that

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\geq a\int_{\Omega}cu^{-}+\int_{\Omega}hu^{-}+\mu\int_{\Omega}\beta(u)|Du^{-}|^{2}u^{-}+\int_{\Omega}b(x)|Du^{-}|u^{-}+\lambda\int_{\Omega}c(u^{-})^{2},

\displaystyle\geq a\int_{\Omega}cu^{-}-\int_{\Omega}|h|u^{-}-\mu\beta(C_{1})C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|Du^{-}|^{2}-C_{1}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|Du^{-}|,

\displaystyle\geq a\int_{\Omega}cu^{-}-\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}C_{1}-\mu\beta(C_{1})C_{1}-\|b\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}C,

which, since
u^{-}
is uniformly bounded, yields a contradiction for large enough
a
, finishing the proof.
∎

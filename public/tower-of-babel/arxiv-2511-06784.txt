Structure and realizability for rational maps
Zhiqiang Wei

Abstract
We prove that if 𝒟 \mathcal{D} is a collection of k k nontrivial partitions of a positive integer d d satisfying the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, and if k k is greater than one plus the minimum length among the partitions in 𝒟 \mathcal{D} , then 𝒟 \mathcal{D} is realizable as the branch data of a rational map f : S 2 → S 2 f\colon S^{2}\to S^{2} . As an application, we confirm a conjecture of Zheng [ Topology Appl. 153 (2006), no. 12, 2124–2134].

Introduction

In his seminal work
[
10
]
, Hurwitz studied the problem of determining whether a given collection
\mathcal{D}=\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}\}
of nontrivial partitions of a positive integer
d
can be realized as the branch data of a branched covering between Riemann surfaces–a problem now known as the
Hurwitz existence problem
. His approach reduces the general problem to the question of realizing these partitions by suitable permutations in the symmetric group
S_{d}
.

In the case where the target space is the 2-sphere
S^{2}
, recall that any permutation
\alpha\in S_{d}
, decomposes uniquely into disjoint cycles,
\alpha=\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{r}
(including 1-cycles if necessary). This permutation
\alpha
defines a partition
\pi=\|\alpha\|=[|\gamma_{1}|,\ldots,|\gamma_{r}|]
given by the lengths of its cycles, and we write
\alpha\in\pi
. Two elements of
S_{d}
are conjugate if and only if they share the same cycle type; hence, the set
\prod(d)
of partitions of
d
corresponds bijectively to the set of conjugacy classes of
S_{d}
. The realizability problem then reduces to the following classical result:

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
10
]
)
A collection
\mathcal{D}=\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}\}
of
k
nontrivial partitions of
d
can be realized as the branch data for a branched covering of
S^{2}
if and only if there exist permutations
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in S_{d}
with
\alpha_{i}\in\pi_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, satisfying
\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{k}=1
and such that the subgroup
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\rangle
of
S_{d}
generated by
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\}
acts transitively on
\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

In general, determining whether such permutations exist for a given
\mathcal{D}
is a delicate combinatorial problem. For target spaces other than
S^{2}
, relatively simple necessary and sufficient conditions are known. In these cases, it suffices to consider the total branching

\nu(\mathcal{D})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}(a_{ij}-1)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d-r_{i})

of the collection
\mathcal{D}=\{[a_{11},\ldots,a_{1r_{1}}],\ldots,[a_{k1},\ldots,a_{kr_{k}}]\}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
6
,
8
,
11
]
)
Let
\mathcal{D}
be a collection of nontrivial partitions of
d
. For
N\neq S^{2}
,
\mathcal{D}
is the branch data for a branched covering
f:M\rightarrow N
with
M
having the same orientability as
N
if and only if
\nu(\mathcal{D})
is even, with the additional condition
\nu(\mathcal{D})\geq d-1
when
N=\mathbb{RP}^{2}
.

The case
N=S^{2}
, however, is significantly more subtle. A collection
\mathcal{D}
of nontrivial partitions of
d
satisfying the Riemann-Hurwitz formula is called a candidate branch datum. It is well known that there exist candidate branch data
\mathcal{D}
that cannot be realized by any branched covering; such data are termed exceptional. For example, for
d=4
and
\mathcal{D}=\{[3,1],[2,2],[2,2]\}
, no such covering exists. Characterizing all exceptional candidate branch data remains an open problem (see
[
24
,
35
,
37
,
38
]
).

Research on the Hurwitz existence problem has led to the development of various approaches, including dessins d’enfant, Speiser graphs, the monodromy approach, and complex analysis. For more details, we refer to
[

provide comprehensive reviews of available results and techniques. A well-known conjecture, proposed in
[

[

, suggests a connection with number-theoretic phenomena:

Conjecture 1.1

(Prime degree conjecture)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a collection of nontrivial partitions of a positive integer
d
. If
\mathcal{D}
is a candidate branch datum and
d
is prime, then
\mathcal{D}
is realizable.

Edmonds, Kulkarni, and Stong reduced the Prime degree conjecture to the case of candidate branch data with exactly three partitions. To date, this conjecture remains open.

In
[

, Zheng used computational methods to determine all exceptional candidate branch data with three branching points and degree
d\leq 22
, considering both
M=N=S^{2}
and
M=T^{2}
,
N=S^{2}
. Recently, Baroni and Petronio
[

. Furthermore, Zheng also proposed the following:

Conjecture 1.2
.

(
[
37
]
)
Suppose
d=d^{\prime}d^{\prime\prime}
where
d^{\prime}
is the minimal prime factor of
d
and
d^{\prime\prime}>2
(i.e.,

is not prime and

\mathcal{D}
is realizable provided that
\mathcal{D}
satisfies the Hurwitz condition and
k>d^{\prime\prime}+1
. Moreover, the condition

is sharp in the sense that

\{[d^{\prime},\ldots,d^{\prime}],[d^{\prime},\ldots,d^{\prime}],[1,\ldots,1,d^{\prime\prime}+1],[1,\ldots,1,2],\ldots,[1,\ldots,1,2]\}

(with

and
\nu(\mathcal{D})=2d-2
) is non-realizable.

In this paper, we investigate the realizability of branched coverings with given candidate branch data
\mathcal{D}
in the case where
M=N=S^{2}
. Several important results are known in this direction. When the collection
\mathcal{D}
contains a partition of length one, Boccara
[

is always realizable. The case where
\mathcal{D}
contains a partition of length two has been studied by Edmonds–Kulkarni–Stong

[

[

Now let
f:S^{2}\rightarrow S^{2}
be a branched covering. Identifying

[

\overline{\mathbb{C}}
with prescribed branch data. This allows the application of complex-analytic techniques (see e.g.
[

\overline{\mathbb{C}}
with the constant Gaussian curvature
1

\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{0}
, the pullback
f^{*}\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{0}
induces a singular metric of constant Gaussian curvature
1
on the source sphere. More precisely,
f^{*}\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{0}
is a spherical conic metric—a constant curvature
1

. Such metrics have been extensively studied (see
[

,

).

A key insight of this work is to transcend purely combinatorial or algebraic perspectives by introducing a differential-geometric structure. By equipping the target sphere with the standard curvature
1
metric and pulling it back via a rational map
f
, we obtain a singular spherical metric on the source sphere whose critical points become conical singularities. Our main structure theorem reveals that this metric admits a remarkably simple and canonical decomposition.

We now state the main results of this paper. Precise definitions of key terms are provided in Section 2.

Theorem 1.3

(
Structure theorem for rational maps
)
.

Let
f:\overline{\mathbb{C}}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
be a rational map, and let
\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{0}
be the constant Gaussian curvature
1
metric on the target
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the pullback metric
f^{*}\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{0}
is a constant Gaussian curvature
1
metric with finitely many conical singularities on the source
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover, the space
(\overline{\mathbb{C}},f^{*}\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{0})
admits a canonical decomposition: it can be partitioned by cutting along a finite set of geodesics into finitely many pieces, each isometric to a football.

Remark 1
.

Our result constitutes a special case of the general structure theorem for reducible constant Gaussian curvature 1 metrics with finitely many conical singularities on compact Riemann surfaces, a result established by Xu, Wu, and the author in
[
36
]
. For the sake of a self-contained presentation, we provide a proof in Section 3. We emphasize that the function
\Phi
is smooth in our framework. Moreover, while the theorem is stated for rational maps, its conclusion and the employed methods are directly applicable to meromorphic functions on any compact Riemann surface, thus offering a pathway to address the realizability problem in a more general context.

As an application of
Theorem
1.3
, we prove an existence result for the Hurwitz existence problem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
d\geq 3
be two integers. Let
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}
be
k
nontrivial partitions of
d
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d-|\pi_{i}|)=2d-2,

and
|\pi_{i}|\geq|\pi_{k}|
for all
i
. If
k\geq|\pi_{k}|+2
, then
\mathcal{D}=\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}\}
is realizable.

Remark 2
.

In Theorem
1.4
, our result reduces to that of Tom
[
33
]
when
|\pi_{k}|=1
, and to that of Pakovich
[
23
]
when
|\pi_{k}|=2
.

Using Theorem
1.4
, we confirm
Conjecture
1.2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
d=d^{\prime}d^{\prime\prime}
where
d^{\prime}
is the minimal prime factor of
d
and
d^{\prime\prime}>2
(i.e.,
d
is not prime and
d\neq 4
). Then
\mathcal{D}
is realizable provided that
\mathcal{D}
satisfies the Hurwitz condition and
k>d^{\prime\prime}+1
. Moreover, the condition
k>d^{\prime\prime}+1
is sharp in the sense that

\{[d^{\prime},\ldots,d^{\prime}],[d^{\prime},\ldots,d^{\prime}],[1,\ldots,1,d^{\prime\prime}+1],[1,\ldots,1,2],\ldots,[1,\ldots,1,2]\}

(with
k=d^{\prime\prime}+1
and
\nu(\mathcal{D})=2d-2
) is non-realizable.

Proof.

First, observe that each element of
\mathcal{D}
has length at least
d^{\prime\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{D}
has
k
elements and
k>d^{\prime\prime}+1
(i.e.,
k\geq d^{\prime\prime}+2
), by
Theorem
1.4
,
\mathcal{D}
is realizable.

Since

d^{\prime\prime}+1>d^{\prime\prime}=\frac{d}{\gcd(d^{\prime},\ldots,d^{\prime})},

it follows from a result in
[
36
]
that the collection

\{[d^{\prime},\ldots,d^{\prime}],[d^{\prime},\ldots,d^{\prime}],[1,\ldots,1,d^{\prime\prime}+1],[1,\ldots,1,2],\ldots,[1,\ldots,1,2]\}

(with
k=d^{\prime\prime}+1
and
\nu(\mathcal{D})=2d-2
) is non-realizable. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
d\geq 3
be two integers. Let
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}
be
k
nontrivial partitions of
d
satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d-|\pi_{i}|)=2d-2

and
|\pi_{i}|\geq|\pi_{k}|
for all
i
. If
k\geq|\pi_{k}|+2
, then for any two partitions
\pi_{i},\pi_{j}
in
\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}\}
, the collection

\mathcal{D}=\{[\pi_{1},\ldots,\hat{\pi}_{i},\ldots,\hat{\pi}_{j},\ldots,\pi_{k}],(k-2)\pi_{i},(k-2)\pi_{j}\}

is realizable. (Here
\hat{\pi}_{i}
denotes the omission of the partition
\pi_{i}
, and
[\pi_{1},\ldots,\hat{\pi}_{i},\ldots,\hat{\pi}_{j},\ldots,\pi_{k}]
denotes the multiset of partitions obtained from the remaining ones.)

Proof.

Let

\pi_{i}=[\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{|\pi_{i}|}],\quad\pi_{j}=[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{|\pi_{j}|}].

Since
k\geq|\pi_{k}|+2
, by
Theorem
1.4
, there exists a rational map
f
with branch data
\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}\}
. Using a Möbius transformation, we may assume

f(z)=\frac{(z-a_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(z-a_{|\pi_{i}|})^{\alpha_{|\pi_{i}|}}}{(z-b_{1})^{\beta_{1}}\cdots(z-b_{|\pi_{j}|})^{\beta_{|\pi_{j}|}}}

with branch points
0
,
\infty
, and
\exp(2\pi j\sqrt{-1}/(k-2))
for
j=0,1,\ldots,k-3
, where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{|\pi_{i}|}
,
b_{1},\ldots,b_{|\pi_{j}|}
are distinct complex numbers.

Then
g=f^{k-2}
yields the desired map.
∎

Preliminary

In this section, we introduce the notions of a
branched covering
, a
conical singularity
and a
football metric
—or simply a
football
—which is the simplest type of constant Gaussian curvature
1
metrics with conical singularities and serves as a basic building block in our construction.

2.1
Branched covering and Hurwitz existence problem

Let
M
and
N
be compact Riemann surfaces with Euler characteristics
\chi(M)
and
\chi(N)
. A smooth map
f:M\to N
is termed a
branched covering
of degree
d
provided that for every
x\in N
, there exists a partition
\pi(x)=[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}]
of
d
—representing the local multiplicities—such that
f
is locally modeled by

(j,z)\mapsto z^{\alpha_{j}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The
branching set

B_{f}\subset N
is the finite set of points
x
for which
\pi(x)\neq[1,\ldots,1]
, and the
branch data
is the collection
\mathcal{D}=\{\pi(x):x\in B_{f}\}
.

A fundamental constraint is given by the Riemann–Hurwitz formula:

\nu(\mathcal{D})=d\cdot\chi(N)-\chi(M).

(1)

Here, the total branching
\nu(\mathcal{D})
is defined in terms of the local degrees. More precisely, if
B_{f}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
and
\mathcal{D}=\{[\alpha_{i1},\ldots,\alpha_{ir_{i}}]\}_{i=1}^{k}
, then near each branch point
y_{ij}
in the preimage of
x_{i}
, the map is locally
z\mapsto z^{\alpha_{ij}}
. Consequently,

\nu(\mathcal{D})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}(\alpha_{ij}-1).

Observing that
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r_{i}}\alpha_{ij}=d
, we obtain the equivalent form:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d-r_{i})=d\cdot\chi(N)-\chi(M).

(2)

A pair
(d,\mathcal{D})
satisfying (
1
) is called a
candidate branch datum
; by a slight abuse of terminology, we also refer to
\mathcal{D}
itself as a candidate branch datum when
d
is understood. The
Hurwitz existence problem
asks for the existence of a branched covering
f:M\to N
of degree
d
with branch data
\mathcal{D}
, for given compact Riemann surfaces
M
and
N
and a candidate branch datum
(d,\mathcal{D})
.

2.2
Conical singularities and footballs

Definition 2.1

(
[
34
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{2\psi}|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}
be a conformal metric on the punctured disk
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. The singular point
z=0
is called a
conical point
with singular angle
2\pi\alpha
(where
0<\alpha\neq 1
) if and only if
\psi
admits a local representation of the form

\psi(z)=(\alpha-1)\ln|z|+\rho(z),

where
\rho(z)
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{D}
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
35
]
)
.

A constant Gaussian curvature
1
metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}
on a
2
-sphere with two conical singularities is called a
football metric
if the spherical distance between the singularities is
\pi
. For consistency, the standard smooth metric on
S^{2}
is also regarded as a football metric. A
2
-sphere endowed with such a metric is referred to as a
football
. Specifically, a football with both conical angles equal to
2\pi\alpha
is denoted by
S^{2}_{\{\alpha,\alpha\}}
; in particular, the standard sphere corresponds to
S^{2}_{\{1,1\}}
.

S^{2}=S^{2}_{\{1,1\}}

(with

The structure of rational maps and proof of Theorem
1.3

3.1
Geometric properties of rational maps

Let
f:\overline{\mathbb{C}}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
be a rational map of degree
d
with branch data
\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k}\}
, where each

\pi_{i}=[a_{i1},\ldots,a_{ir_{i}}],\quad i=1,\ldots,k,

is a nontrivial partition of
d
. After a suitable Möbius transformation,
f
can be written as

f(z)=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r_{1}}(z-z_{j})^{a_{1j}}}{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{d}(z-w_{l})},

where
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r_{1}},w_{1},\ldots,w_{d}
are distinct complex numbers.

To uncover the hidden geometric structure, we construct two key tools: a real-valued function
\Phi
that measures the “logarithmic scale” of
f
, and a related Killing vector field
\vec{V}
. The integral curves of
\vec{V}
and the gradient lines of
\Phi
together form a coordinate grid that naturally demarcates the boundaries of the football pieces in the decomposition.

Define a real function
\Phi:\overline{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Phi(p)=\frac{4|f(p)|^{2}}{1+|f(p)|^{2}},\quad p\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}.

Then
\Phi
is smooth on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
and takes values in
[0,4]
. Moreover:

•

The zeros of
f
are minima of
\Phi
,

•

The poles of
f
are maxima of
\Phi
,

•

The critical points of
f
(excluding zeros) are saddle points of
\Phi
.

Now define a meromorphic
1
-form by

\omega=\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{f}.

Then
\omega
has only simple poles, with residues
a_{1j}
at
z_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,r_{1}
, and
-1
at each
w_{l}
for
l=1,\ldots,d
. Furthermore, each point corresponding to an element
a_{ij}>1
in a partition
\pi_{i}
with
i=2,\ldots,k
is a zero of
\omega
of order
a_{ij}-1
, and also a saddle point of
\Phi
.

The following theorem summarizes the geometric structure:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{0}
be the standard metric on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=f^{*}\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{0}
is a constant Gaussian curvature
1
metric on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
with finitely many conical singularities. These occur at the critical points of
f
, and the conical angle at a critical point of multiplicity
a_{ij}>1
is
2\pi a_{ij}
. Moreover,
\Phi
and
\omega
satisfy

\frac{4\,\mathrm{d}\Phi}{\Phi(4-\Phi)}=\omega+\overline{\omega},

(3)

and the metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}
is given by

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\frac{\Phi(4-\Phi)}{4}\,\omega\,\overline{\omega}.

Proof.

These results follow by direct computation.
∎

Finally, define a complex vector field
X
by the condition

\omega(X)=\sqrt{-1}.

Then
X
is a meromorphic vector field on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
. Its real part,

\vec{V}=\frac{1}{2}(X+\overline{X})=\operatorname{Re}(X),

is a real vector field on the sphere.

The following properties can be verified directly:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})
denote the set of singular points of
\vec{V}
. Then

\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})=\{\text{poles and zeros of }\omega\}=\{\text{poles, zeros, and critical points of }f\}.

Moreover,
\vec{V}
is a Killing vector field on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})
and satisfies
\vec{V}\perp\nabla\Phi
, where
\nabla\Phi
denotes the real gradient of
\Phi
.

Let
\Omega_{p}
denote the set of all integral curves of
\vec{V}
that intersect at a point
p
, with cardinality
|\Omega_{p}|
. For any
p\notin\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})
, there exists a unique integral curve of
\vec{V}
passing through
p
, so
\Omega_{p}
is well-defined and consists of exactly two elements: one entering and one leaving
p
. However, at a singular point
p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})
, the notion of an integral curve passing through
p
becomes ambiguous. To resolve this, we say an integral curve
C
belongs to
\Omega_{p}
(for
p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})
) if and only if there exists a sequence of points in
C
converging to
p
. Equivalently, if
C\notin\Omega_{p}
, then there exists a small disk
B
centered at
p
such that
C\cap B=\emptyset
.

Proposition 3.2
.

In a small neighborhood of any local extremum of
\Phi
, every integral curve of
\vec{V}
is a topological circle enclosing the extremum in its interior.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that near an extremum of
\Phi
, we have
\omega=\frac{\alpha}{z}\mathrm{d}z
with
\alpha=-1
or
\alpha\cup\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then
Y=\sqrt{-1}\frac{z}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
. Writing
z=x+\sqrt{-1}y
, we obtain

\vec{V}=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left(x\frac{\partial}{\partial y}-y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right).

It is clear that every integral curve of
\vec{V}
is a topological circle enclosing the extremum.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

The set
\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})
decomposes into two disjoint subsets,
\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
, where:

1.

S_{1}=\{p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})\mid\Omega_{p}=\emptyset\}
, and if
p\in S_{1}
, then
p
is an extremum of
\Phi
.

2.

S_{2}=\{p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\vec{V})\mid\Omega_{p}\neq\emptyset\}
, and if
p\in S_{2}
, then
p
is a saddle point of
\Phi
and the angle of
\mathrm{d}s^{2}
at
p
is
\pi\cdot|\Omega_{p}|
.

Proof.

Case (1) follows from
Proposition
3.2
. We now prove Case (2). Without loss of generality, near a saddle point
p
of
\Phi
, assume
\omega=z^{n}\mathrm{d}z
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, where
z
is a local complex coordinate such that no other singularities of
\vec{V}
lie in the neighborhood. Then
Y=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{z^{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
. Writing
z=re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}
, we compute:

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{r^{n}e^{\sqrt{-1}n\theta}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial z}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2r^{n}e^{\sqrt{-1}(n+1)\theta}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r^{n}}\left[\sin((n+1)\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\cos((n+1)\theta)}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\sqrt{-1}(\cdots)\right].

Hence, the real vector field
\vec{V}
is given by:

Suppose an integral curve of

near

is parametrized as:

\begin{cases}r=r(t),\\
\theta=\theta(t),\end{cases}\quad t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\setminus\{0\},\ \varepsilon>0,

with

. Then the system becomes:

\begin{cases}r^{\prime}=\dfrac{\sin((n+1)\theta)}{2r^{n}},\\[10.0pt]
\theta^{\prime}=\dfrac{\cos((n+1)\theta)}{2r^{n+1}}.\end{cases}

(4)

If

To prove
Theorem
1.4
, we first establish some preliminary lemmas.

Let

\pi_{i}=\big[b_{i1},\dots,b_{iq_{i}},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{e_{i}}\big],\quad i=1,2,\dots,k,

be
k
nontrivial partitions of an integer
d\geq 3
, where for each
i
, the entries satisfy
b_{i1}\geq b_{i2}\geq\dots\geq b_{iq_{i}}\geq 2
. Assume the collection

\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}\}

satisfies the Riemann–Hurwitz formula and that
|\pi_{k}|=q_{k}+e_{k}\leq|\pi_{i}|=q_{i}+e_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,k-1
.

We then have the following:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k\geq q_{k}+e_{k}+2
. If
q_{1}\geq q_{i}
for all
i=2,\dots,k-1
, then
e_{i}>0
for all
i=2,\dots,k-1
, and consequently
q_{i}+e_{i}>\frac{d}{2}
.

Proof.

Assume, for contradiction, that
e_{t}=0
for some
t\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}
. Then
\pi_{t}=[b_{t1},\dots,b_{tq_{t}}]
. By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula:

\displaystyle 2d-2

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{q_{i}}(b_{ij}-1)

\displaystyle=\big[d-(q_{k}+e_{k})\big]+(d-q_{t})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq t\end{subarray}}^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{q_{i}}(b_{ij}-1).

Rearranging terms, we obtain:

\displaystyle q_{t}

\displaystyle=2+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq t\end{subarray}}^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{q_{i}}(b_{ij}-1)-(q_{k}+e_{k})

\displaystyle\geq 2+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq t\end{subarray}}^{k-1}q_{i}-(q_{k}+e_{k})\quad(\text{since }b_{ij}\geq 2)

\displaystyle=2+q_{1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq t\end{subarray}}^{k-1}q_{i}-(q_{k}+e_{k})

\displaystyle\geq 2+q_{1}+(k-3)-(q_{k}+e_{k})\quad(\text{since }q_{i}\geq 1)

\displaystyle=q_{1}+k-(q_{k}+e_{k}+1).

Given
k\geq q_{k}+e_{k}+2
, it follows that:

q_{t}>q_{1},

contradicting the assumption
q_{1}\geq q_{t}
. Therefore,
e_{i}>0
for all
i=2,\dots,k-1
. Moreover, since
\pi_{i}
is a partition of
d
, we have
q_{i}+e_{i}>\frac{d}{2}
for all
i=2,\dots,k-1
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
k\geq q_{k}+e_{k}+2

Morse Index for Homothetic motions in the gravitational n n -body problem
Yuwei Ou, Alessandro Portaluri

Abstract
In the gravitation n n -body Problem, a homothetic orbit is a special solution of the Newton’s Equations of motion, in which each body moves along a straight line through the center of mass and forming at any time a central configuration. In 2020, Portaluri et al. proved that under a spectral gap condition on the limiting central configuration, known in literature as non-spiraling or [BS]-condition, the Morse index of an asymptotic colliding motion is finite. Later Ou et al. proved this result for other classes of unbounded motions, e.g. doubly asymptotic motions (e.g. doubly homothetic motions). In this paper we prove that for a homothetic motion, irrespective of how large the index of the limiting central configuration and how large the energy level is, the following alternative holds: if the non-spiraling condition holds then the Morse index is 0 otherwise it is infinite. AMS Subject Classification: 70F16, 70F10, 53D12. Keywords: Celestial mechanics, n n -body problem, Homothetic orbits, Morse index, Maslov index.

The intersection index of a homothetic motion

The aim of this section is to associate to an

Appendix A
A quick recap of the Maslov index

In this section, we briefly recall the definition and the basic properties of the Maslov index. Our basic references are
[
CLM94
]
and
[
RS93
]
and references therein.

In the
standard symplectic space

({\mathbb{R}}^{2k},\omega)
a
Lagrangian subspace

is a linear subspace
L
such that
L=L^{\omega}
where
L^{\omega}
denotes the symplectic orthogonal. We denote by
\Lag(k)
the set of all Lagrangian subspaces and we recall that
\Lag(k)
is a compact and connected
k(k+1)/2
-dimensional smooth submanifold of the Grassmannian of the
k
-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2k}
.

A.1
An axiomatic definition

Given two continuous paths of Lagrangian subspaces
t\mapsto L_{1}(t)
and
t\mapsto L_{2}(t)
with
t\in[a,b]
, it is possible to define a integer-valued homotopy invariant known as
Maslov index for Lagrangian paths
and denoted by
\mu(L_{1},L_{2};[a,b])
. This invariant is uniquely determined by a set of axioms listed as Property I to VII below. (For further details, we refer the interested reader to
[
CLM94
]
and references therein).

•

Property I. (Reparametrization invariance)
Let
\varrho:[c,d]\rightarrow[a,b]
be a continuous and piecewise smooth function satisfying
\varrho(c)=a
,
\varrho(d)=b
, then

(A.1)

\mu(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t);[a,b])=\mu(L_{1}(\varrho(\tau)),L_{2}(\varrho(\tau));[c,d]).

•

Property II. (Homotopy invariant with end points)
If two continuous
families of Lagrangian paths
(s,t)\mapsto L_{1}(s,t)
and
(s,t)\mapsto L_{2}(s,t)
,
with
(s,t)\in[0,1]\times[a,b]
satisfy the following property

\dim\big(L_{1}(s,a)\cap L_{2}(s,a)\big)=C_{1}\hskip 18.49988pt\textrm{ and }\hskip 18.49988pt\dim(L_{1}(s,b)\cap L_{2}(s,b))=C_{2}\hskip 18.49988pt\forall\,s\in[0,1]

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are two positive constants, then

(A.2)

\mu(L_{1}(0,t),L_{2}(0,t))=\mu(L_{1}(1,t),L_{2}(1,t)).

•

Property III. (Path additivity)
If
a<c<b
, then

(A.3)

\mu(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t))=\mu(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t);[a,c])+\mu(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t);[c,b]).

•

Property IV. (Symplectic invariance)
Let
\gamma\in\mathscr{C}^{0}([a,b],\Sp(2n))
. Then

(A.4)

\mu(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t);[a,b])=\mu(\gamma(t)L_{1}(t),\gamma(t)L_{2}(t);[a,b]).

•

Property V. (Symplectic additivity)
For
i=1,2
let
(W_{i},\omega_{i})
be two symplectic spaces and we assume that
L_{i}\in\mathscr{C}^{0}([a,b],\Lag(W_{1}))
and
L_{i}\in\mathscr{C}^{0}([a,b],\Lag(W_{2}))
for
i=1,2
. Then

(A.5)

\mu(L_{1}(t)\oplus\widehat{L}_{1}(t),L_{2}(t)\oplus\widehat{L}_{2}(t);[a,b])=\mu(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t))+\mu(\widehat{L}_{1}(t),\widehat{L}_{2}(t);[a,b]).

•

Property VI. (Symmetry)
For
i=1,2
we let
L_{i}\in\mathscr{C}^{0}([a,b],\Lag(k))
. Then

(A.6)

\mu(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t);[a,b])=\dim L_{1}(a)\cap L_{2}(a)-\dim L_{1}(b)\cap L_{2}(b)-\mu(L_{2}(t),L_{1}(t)).

•

Property VII (Monotone property)
For
j=1,2
we let
L_{j}(t)=\gamma_{j}(t)\,V
, where

\begin{cases}\dot{\gamma}_{j}(t)=JB_{j}(t)\gamma_{j}(t)\\
\gamma_{j}(t)=I_{2k}.\end{cases}

If
B_{1}(t)\geq B_{2}(t)
meaning that the quadratic form associated to
B_{1}(t)-B_{2}(t)
is positive definite, then for any
V_{0},V_{1}\in Lag(2n)
, we get

(A.7)

\mu(V_{0},\gamma_{1}V_{1};[a,b])\geq\mu(V_{0},\gamma_{2}V_{1};[a,b]).

A.2

An efficient way to compute the Maslov index is via the crossing forms introduced by authors in
[
RS93
]
. For simplicity and since it is enough for our purpose, we only briefly recall this procedure for the computation of the Maslov index for a path of Lagrangian subspaces with respect to a fixed Lagrangian subspace.

Let
\Lambda\in\mathscr{C}^{1}([a,b],\Lag(k))
be a

\mathscr{C}^{1}
-curve of Lagrangian subspaces with
\Lambda(0)=\Lambda
and
let
V
be a fixed Lagrangian subspace which is transversal to

\Lambda
. For
v\in\Lambda
and for small

by requiring that

. Then it is easy to check that

is

W
, we say that
t

\Gamma(\Lambda(t),W,t)=Q|_{\Lambda(t)\cap W}

Remark A.1
.

It is worth observing that in the application, usually the Lagrangian path
t\mapsto\Lambda(t)
is pointwise defined by
\Lambda(t)=\gamma(t)\,L
where
t\mapsto\gamma(t)
is a path of symplectic matrices corresponding to the fundamental solution of a Hamiltonian system. In this case the crossing form can be explicitly written in terms of the entries of the path
\gamma
. In fact, if the path is given by
\Lambda(t)=\gamma(t)\,L
with
\gamma(t)\in\Sp(2k)
and

L\in Lag(k)
, then the crossing form is equal to

(A.10)

\langle-\gamma(t)^{T}J\dot{\gamma}(t)v,v\rangle\hskip 18.49988pt\textrm{ for }v\in\gamma(t)^{-1}(\Lambda(t)\cap W)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard inner product of

\mathbb{R}^{2k}
. It is immediate to check that the monotone property of the Maslov index given at Equation (
A.7
) is a direct consequence of Equation (
A.10
).

Assuming that
t\mapsto\Lambda(t)
is a regular path, meaning that each crossing
with
W
is regular, authors in
[
LZ00
]
proved that the Maslov index can be computed as follows

(A.11)

\mu(W,\Lambda(t);[a,b])=\comorse(\Gamma(\Lambda(a),W,a))+\sum_{a<t<b}\sgn(\Gamma(\Lambda(t),W,t))-\morse(\Gamma(\Lambda(b),W,b)),

where the sum runs all over the crossings
t\in(a,b)
and where
\comorse,\morse
denote the dimension of positive and negative spectral subspaces whilst
\sgn=\comorse-\morse
denote the signature.

Remark A.2
.

We observe that by a sufficient small perturbation a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
-path of
Lagrangian subspaces with fixed end points is regular.

In several interesting cases, for instance in the case of Lagrangian systems satisfying the classical Legendre convexity condition, the curve of Lagrangian subspaces induced by the action of the phase flow of the corresponding Hamiltonian system on a fixed Lagrangian subspace is a
plus curve

L_{D}

L_{D}

A.11

\mu(L_{D},\Lambda(t))=\text{dim}(\Lambda(a)\cap L_{D})+\sum_{a<t<b}\text{dim}(\Lambda(t)\cap L_{D}).

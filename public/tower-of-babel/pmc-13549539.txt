Concurrent lichen planopilaris (LPP), oral lichen planus, vulvar lichen sclerosus, and cutaneous lichen planus present a rare, complex mucocutaneous systemic disease. Their coexistence reflects their shared immune-mediated pathways and is frequently refractory to standard therapies, including corticosteroids and traditional immunomodulators, underscoring the need for targeted systemic agents.1,2 Dysregulated Janus kinase (JAK)-STAT signaling has been implicated in the pathogenesis of each condition, providing a mechanistic rationale for JAK inhibitor therapy.3,4 We present 4 cases demonstrating the clinical utility of JAK inhibitors for multisite lichenoid disease.

We conducted a retrospective case series of 4 women aged 43–68 with biopsy-confirmed LPP and concomitant oral lichen planus, vulvar lichen sclerosus, or cutaneous lichen planus who were treated with a systemic JAK inhibitor (baricitinib or tofacitinib) at Lahey Hospital and Medical Center. All patients had failed multiple therapies, including topical, systemic, and intralesional corticosteroids; calcineurin inhibitors; hydroxychloroquine; systemic immunomodulators; and topical JAK inhibitors. Clinical response was evaluated at 6–12 week intervals based on symptom burden, trichoscopy, and standardized scalp photography. All patients remained on therapy at last follow-up. Timepoints reflect the visit at which clinically notable improvement was first documented; all patients were followed at routine 4–8 month intervals.

Patient 4’s tofacitinib dose was reduced to 10 mg once daily from October 2025 to January 2026, which resulted in symptom recurrence at all affected sites. The dose was subsequently returned to 10 mg twice daily, with subsequent resolution of symptoms.

All 4 patients demonstrated significant clinical improvement following the initiation of oral JAK inhibitor therapy. Scalp inflammation, mucosal disease, and cutaneous involvement (if applicable) resolved or markedly improved in each case. Hair loss was also reduced, with 3 patients experiencing increased hair density (Fig. 1). Patient-reported symptoms, including pain, burning, and pruritus, improved in all 4 cases. Treatment was well tolerated. Adverse effects included acne, tinea corporis, and biliary colic, all of which were limited and manageable. No serious laboratory abnormalities were observed. Notably, Patient 4 experienced symptom recurrence at all affected sites when reducing tofacitinib from 10 mg twice daily to 10 mg once daily, which resolved upon returning to the twice-daily dose.

LPP, oral lichen planus, vulvar lichen sclerosus, and cutaneous lichen planus share overlapping immunopathology characterized by dysregulated T-cell activation and cytokine signaling through the JAK-STAT pathway.2–4 This mechanistic convergence supports systemic JAK inhibition as a unifying therapeutic strategy across disease sites. Prior reports have documented the efficacy of JAK inhibitors in each of these conditions separately.3–5 The simultaneous multisite responses observed across all 4 patients in this study suggest a shared inflammatory driver targeted by JAK inhibitors, rather than independent, site-specific diseases. This distinction has important clinical implications, as patients with overlapping lichenoid diseases may be suboptimally managed by separate site-directed therapies and could benefit from earlier initiation of systemic treatment. The observed symptom worsening in Patient 4 after decreasing her JAK inhibitor dosing frequency further supports the role of optimized dosing in sustained disease. The concurrent improvement across each disease further supports this concept and adds to the existing literature on JAK inhibitors in overlapping lichenoid diseases.

Oral JAK inhibitors offer an effective, well-tolerated option for patients with refractory multisite lichenoid disease. Limitations of this series include its retrospective design, small sample size, lack of systematic measures, and potential confounding by adjunctive therapies. Larger, prospective studies are needed to confirm the long-term efficacy and safety of this approach.

Xinfeng capsule regulates pyroptosis and ameliorates cardiac fibrosis in rheumatoid arthritis through lncRNA GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways
Authors: Hui Wang, Wanlan Fu, Yunxiang Cao, Jian Liu, Nanfei Zhu, Amin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Xinfeng capsule (XFC) is a traditional Chinese medicinal compound that has been used clinically for several years to treat rheumatoid arthritis (RA)-related cardiac dysfunction. Nevertheless, the mechanism through which XFC treats RA-induced cardiac impairment remains unclear. This study investigates the mechanism by which XFC-containing serum regulates pyroptosis and ameliorates cardiac fibrosis in RA through the lncRNA GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways. A cardiac fibrosis model using human myocardial cells was established to study lncRNA GAS5, miR-21, TLR4, and NLRP3 expressions. Immunofluorescence, RT-qPCR, WB, dual-luciferase assay, CCK-8, flow cytometry, electron microscopy, and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay were used to analyze various cellular processes and protein levels. Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) releases were also analyzed. The mRNA and protein expressions of multiple genes were assessed. The optimal intervention conditions for AC16 cells were determined as follows: 20 µg/L for lipopolysaccharide (LPS) for 24 h, and 10% for XFC. In the LPS group, cell viability decreased, LDH release increased, and there was an elevation in pro-inflammatory cytokines and pyroptosis-related protein expression. GAS5 expression was reduced, while TLR4, ROS, and miR-21 showed increased levels compared to the NC group. GAS5 acted as an inhibitor by sponging miR-21. Conversely, XFC-containing serum, GAS5 overexpression, and miR-21 inhibition exhibited positive effects – enhancing cell viability, preventing LDH and pro-inflammatory cytokine release, and reducing pyroptosis and fibrosis-related protein levels. XFC can inhibit cellular pyroptosis, reduce pro-inflammatory cytokine levels, and improve RA cardiac fibrosis via lncRNA GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) manifests as persistent synovial inflammation, culminating in joint deformity and functional decline[1,2]. This persistent inflammation is believed to be a major contributor to the cardiac manifestations observed in patients with RA [3]. Acute systemic inflammation can trigger acute cardiac inflammation, subsequently promoting myocardial fibrosis[4]. Cardiac fibrosis, marked by the accumulation of extracellular matrix proteins, results in myocardial interstitial expansion, ultimately impairing cardiac compliance and contributing to heart failure[5,6]. Multiple intricate factors orchestrate this process, encompassing oxidative stress, chemokines, inflammatory mediators [notably the NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome], pro-inflammatory cytokines, growth factors, and regulatory non-coding RNAs[5,7]. Pyroptosis, a form of programmed cell death characterized by cellular swelling, membrane pore formation, and release of cytoplasmic contents, including interleukin-1-beta (IL-1β) and interleukin-18 (IL-18), has emerged as a crucial player in cardiovascular diseases[8,9]. Targeting key proteins involved in pyroptosis pathways presents a potential therapeutic strategy against cardiac fibrosis[10,11]. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying pyroptosis in RA-induced cardiac fibrosis remain poorly understood.

Among long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), growth arrest-specific transcript 5 (GAS5) has garnered considerable attention as a tumor suppressor across various malignancies[12,13]. Xu demonstrated that GAS5 inhibits NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis through the competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism regulating the miR-34b-3p/aryl hydrocarbon receptor axis, thereby ameliorating diabetic cardiomyopathy[14]. Furthermore, studies have confirmed that GAS5 overexpression reduces pyroptosis in cardiac fibroblasts and decreases the expression of caspase-1 and NLRP3[15]. MicroRNA-21 (miR-21) is a common anti-apoptotic gene in cardiovascular diseases[16,17], and its role in regulating pyroptosis has garnered increasing attention in recent years[18,19]. In cardiac fibroblasts, miR-21 expression is elevated, and locked nucleic acid-mediated inhibition of miR-21 significantly alleviates myocardial fibrosis[20]. Furthermore, GAS5 has been documented to govern programmed cell death factor 4 expression through targeting miR-21 in post-infarction rat models, thereby influencing cardiomyocyte survival[21]. However, whether GAS5 participates in RA-associated myocardial fibrosis through the ceRNA mechanism by competitively binding to miR-21, and the regulatory role and mechanism of the GAS5/miR-21 axis in pyroptosis during RA myocardial fibrosis have not yet been elucidated. In addition, Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), as a key pattern recognition receptor in the innate immune response, initiates inflammatory cascades via MAPK and NF-κB signaling upon ligand binding[22]. Beyond its immunological role, TLR4 is intimately linked to oxidative stress generation, serving as a central mediator of lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-triggered reactive oxygen species (ROS) production in macrophage populations[23,24]. Although the impact of oxidative stress on signaling pathways has been extensively studied, the direct regulatory mechanism of ROS on signaling molecules remains unclear. Moreover, the role of the TLR4/ROS/miR-21 axis in RA-associated myocardial fibrosis also awaits clarification.

Xinfeng capsule (XFC; Batch No. 20220517; Patent No. ZL 2013 1 0011369.8) is a proprietary formulation that contains a specific blend of Astragalus mongholicus Bunge (Astragali Radix), Coix lacryma-jobi L. (Coicis Semen), Tripterygium wilfordii Hook. f., and Scolopendra subspinipes mutilans L. Koch (Scolopendra), combined in a ratio of 20:20:10:1[25]. Previous studies have optimized its extraction process and established a fingerprint-based quality control method[26]. Toxicological studies have shown that a combination with Astragalus membranaceus significantly reduces the dissolution rate of triptolide and alleviates subacute hepatorenal toxicity[27,28]. Existing research has confirmed that XFC exhibits immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, pyroptosis-inhibiting, and cardiopulmonary function-improving effects[29,30] and can effectively reduce immune inflammation indicators and relieve joint swelling and pain in patients with RA[31]. Previous research has revealed that XFC attenuates myocardial injury in adjuvant arthritis (AA) rats by suppressing the TLR4/NF-κB/MAPK signaling pathway, leading to reduced levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Additionally, XFC downregulates miR-21 in this context[32]. However, whether XFC can inhibit cell death and ameliorate cardiac fibrosis through the lncRNA GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 axes remains to be elucidated.

The current study aims to establish an in vitro model of myocardial cell fibrosis to analyze how GAS5 might function as a ceRNA to competitively bind miR-21, thereby regulating the TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathway, inhibiting pyroptosis, and improving cardiac fibrosis in the context of RA. This investigation aims to elucidate the molecular underpinnings governing XFC-mediated cardioprotection in the context of RA-associated myocardial fibrosis. It may offer potential therapeutic strategies for managing cardiac complications associated with RA. The work has been fully compliant with the TITAN Guidelines 2025[33].

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

This study was conducted in compliance with relevant national and international guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals and was approved by the appropriate institutional ethics committee. The work has been reported in line with the ARRIVE criteria[34].

AC16 cell resuscitation and culture expansion

AC16 cells (ATCC cytorepository) were placed in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin -streptomycin (Biosharp) and subsequently cultured in a humidified atmosphere at 37°C with 5% CO2.

Cell transfection

AC16 cells (1.0 × 105 cells/well) at the logarithmic phase were seeded in a six-well plate and incubated at 37°C for 24 h in 5% CO2, after which the medium was removed. Subsequently, the cells were transfected using Lipofectamine 3000 and divided into five groups: negative control over-expression (OE-NC), GAS5 overexpression (OE-GAS5), interference negative control (KD-NC), GAS5 interference (KD-GAS5), and miR-21 interference (miR-21-inhibitor), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The cells were incubated for an additional 24 h post-transfection. Non-transfected AC16 cells were designated as the NC group. RT-qPCR was subsequently used to assess GAS5 and miR-21 expression, confirming the effectiveness of over-expression and interference in the transfection of the GAS5 lentiviral vector.

Preparation of XFC-containing serum

A number was assigned to each rat by a randomization method, and the rats were equally divided into normal and drug-containing serum groups, with 10 rats in each group according to the random numbers. The sample size (n = 10 per group) was determined based on preliminary experiments and previous studies to ensure sufficient statistical power for detecting significant differences between groups. XFC comprised Astragali Radix, Coicis Semen, Scolopendra, and Tripterygium wilfordii Hook. f., and each capsule contained 0.4 g of crude drug extract. The drug-containing serum group received 3.0 g·kg−1·d−1 XFC (Batch No. 20220517) by gavage. Meanwhile, the normal group received a daily gavage of 10 mL·kg−1•d−1 saline under identical conditions[35,36]. After 7 days of treatment, rats were intraperitoneally anesthetized with 3% pentobarbital sodium (30 mg/kg). Subsequently, blood samples were withdrawn from the abdominal aorta following an incision in the abdominal cavity. Samples were then left to stand for 2 h before being refrigerated at 4°C for 4 h to induce blood coagulation. Afterward, samples were centrifuged for 15 min at 3000 rpm. Notably, supernatants were inactivated at 56°C for 30 min and sterilized with a microporous filter (0.2 μm). Finally, the separated sera from both groups were stored at −80°C.

Detection of cellular activity using CCK-8

Upon inoculation in 96-well plates, AC16 cells were initially cultured at 37°C with 5% CO2 and exposed to varying concentrations of LPS (10, 20, 40, and 80 µg/mL) for varying durations (12, 24, and 48 h) after 24 h of culture. Following the exposure, a 1-hour incubation period ensued after adding 10 μL of CCK-8 solution to each well. The OD at 450 nm was measured using an enzyme labeler, and values for different LPS concentrations were further analyzed to identify the optimal LPS concentration and exposure duration. Calculating the inhibition rate of cell proliferation involves applying the formula: (OD control group − OD experimental group)/(OD control group − blank well) × 100%.

Following a 24-hour treatment, the impact of varying concentrations (8, 10, 12, 14, and 16%) of XFC-containing serum on AC16 cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay. The optimal duration and concentration of XFC-containing serum were ultimately identified through evaluation of viability: Viability (%) = (treatment group A − blank group A)/(control group A − blank group A) × 100%.

Grouping and processing

AC16 cells were categorized into seven groups: normal control (NC group), LPS (20 µg/mL LPS for 24 h), LPS + XFC (10% XFC + 20 µg/mL LPS for 24 h), LPS + lentiviral empty control (LPS + EV), LPS + GAS5 over-expression (LPS + GAS5), LPS + miR-21 inhibition (LPS + miR-21-inhibitor), and LPS + GAS5 over-expression + the inhibitor necrosulfonamide (NSA) (LPS + GAS5 + NSA).

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay

Regarding GAS5 and miR-21, the potential binding sites were analyzed using the starBase database. Additionally, a mutation analysis within the binding site was conducted using a dual-luciferase reporter assay (GeneCopoeia). WT-GAS5 and MUT-GAS5 constructs were generated; the plasmids were synthesized by Linmei Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Hefei, China). AC16 cells (1 × 105) were seeded into 24-well plates, and the medium was discarded after 24 h of incubation. Transfection of miR-21 mimics or mimic NC, along with a GAS5-MUT/WT vector, was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) in AC16 cells. Subsequently, luciferase activity was measured using a microplate reader (SpectraMax/iD5). The luciferases employed in this study were firefly luciferase and Renilla luciferase. The peak emission wavelengths for the luminescence catalyzed by firefly luciferase with luciferin are 560 nm, while those for Renilla luciferase with coelenterazine are 560 nm and 465 nm, respectively.

Lactate dehydrogenase determination

The detection of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) was performed using a commercial kit from Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Bioengineering Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China; article number A020-1) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Cell culture supernatants were collected and centrifuged at 400 rpm for 5 min to remove cellular debris. The reaction mixture was incubated at 37°C for 30 min, and the LDH level was measured with a microplate reader (SpectraMax iD5) at 450 nm using a standard curve.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

Cell supernatants were assayed for IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18, and TNF-α levels using the respective enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Vio Biologics, Shanghai; Lot Nos. EH10269H, EH10293H, EH10260H, and EH10497H, respectively) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. The absorbance values were measured using an enzyme labeler (Wuxi Huawei Delang, model DR-200Bc).

Flow cytometry

Each well in a six-well plate received 100 μL of cell suspension and was then incubated at 37°C for 24 h in 5% CO2. Suspended cells were initially centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 5 minutes. Adherent cells were digested with trypsin without ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid and collected. Following a phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) wash, 1–5 × 105 cells were subsequently collected. Subsequently, cells were exposed to 500 µL of binding buffer, and 5 µL of propidium iodide was thoroughly mixed in. The observation and detection of cells were ultimately performed using a fluorescence microscope (CIC, model XSP-C204) and a flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, model CytoFLEX LX). Analysis of acquired data was performed using FlowJo software (FlowJo LLC).

RT-qPCR

A Hifair II 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit was employed to reverse transcribe total RNA into complementary DNA, which was then subjected to RT-qPCR assays. Table 1 contains the sequences of the primers. Relative expression of target genes normalized to internal reference genes was calculated using 2−ΔΔCt method. Western blotting assay

Total proteins were initially extracted on ice using radioimmunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer (Biyun Tian Co.). An Enhanced BCA Protein Concentration Assay Kit (Beyotime) was used to determine the overall protein concentration in each lysate. Then, an equivalent amount of protein was loaded onto a 10% SDS-PAGE gel and subsequently transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Millipore, Inc.), which was blocked with 5% skim milk for 2 h and incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against TLR4 (1:1000), NF-κB p65 (1:1000), GSDMD (1:500), MMP-9 (1:1000), CTGF (1:2000), and β-actin (1:10 000) (all purchased from Proteintech; numbers: 19811-1-AP, 10745-1-AP, 20770-1-AP, 10375-2-AP, 25474-1-AP, and 81115-1-AP, respectively), TGF-β1 (1:1000) (purchased from Affinity; number: AF1027), caspase-1 (1:500), and NLRP3 (1:1000) (both obtained from ZENBID; numbers: 342947 and 381207, respectively). Notably, membranes were subsequently incubated with horseradish peroxidase–labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:10 000) and horseradish peroxidase–labeled goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibodies (both from Beijing Zhongshan Jinqiao Co., Ltd.; numbers: ZB-2301 and ZB-2305, respectively) for 1 h. Next, an ECL Plus luminescent kit (Beyotime Corp.) was applied for detection, development, and exposure. Protein expression relative to β-actin was analyzed using ImageJ software.

Morphological detection of pyroptosis

The cells from each group were collected, transferred into an electron microscopy fixation solution, and fixed at room temperature for 2 hours. Following low-speed centrifugation, gradient dehydration, embedding, sectioning, staining, and preparation, the morphological structure of the cells was observed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM; Hitachi, No. HT7800/HT7700), and the images were collected and analyzed.

Immunofluorescence staining

After grouping and treatment of cells, samples were incubated with primary antibodies at 4°C overnight for anti-TLR4 (1:100), anti-NF-κB p65 (1:300), and anti-NLRP3 (1:100), then washed three times in PBS and sequentially incubated with a fluorescent goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L), Fluor594-conjugated (1:400) (Beijing Zhongshan Jinqiao Co.), at ambient temperature in the dark for 50 min. Nuclei were subsequently counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole, rinsed, the slides were sealed with an anti-fade mounting medium, and images were captured with a fluorescence microscope.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism version 9.5.1, with results presented as mean ± standard deviation. All experiments were performed in at least three independent replicates. The D’Agostino-Pearson test was used to assess the normality of the data distribution. Unpaired _t_tests were used to compare normally distributed data between two groups, while one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used for multiple comparisons. P-values less than 0.05 were regarded as indicative of statistical significance.

Results

Optimal concentrations and times for LPS and XFC-containing serum

The impact of LPS on cell viability was assessed using a CCK-8 assay. Meanwhile, AC16 cells were accordingly treated with varying proportions of XFC-containing serum, and the effects on cell proliferation were assessed. After exposure to various concentrations of LPS for different durations, the optimal effect was observed with a 20 µg/mL treatment for 24 h (Fig. 1A). Notably, cell viability decreased in a concentration- and time-dependent manner, with 20 µg/mL LPS for 24 h inducing an approximately 40–50% reduction in viability compared to the control group (P < 0.001), which was considered suitable for establishing the fibrosis model without causing excessive cell death. Moreover, following treatment with different concentrations of XFC-containing serum, the highest proliferation rate was observed with 10% drug-containing serum (Fig. 1B). This concentration was selected for subsequent experiments, as it provided optimal protective effects while maintaining cell viability comparable to the control group. Interactions between GAS5 and miR-21

The StarBase database predicted miR-21 binding sites in GAS5 (Fig. 2A). The dual-luciferase assay demonstrated a significant decrease in the luciferase activity of AC16 cells when GAS5-WT and miR-21 were co-transfected, compared with the GAS5-WT + NC group (P < 0.0001). In AC16 cells, co-transfection of miR-21 with GAS5-MUT showed no significant difference in luciferase activity compared with the GAS5-MUT + NC group (P > 0.05) (Fig. 2B). Effects of GAS5 overexpression and knock-down on miR-21, TLR4, and myocardial fibrosis

To clarify the function of GAS5 in AC16 cells, GAS5 was first overexpressed. RT-qPCR indicated that the expression levels of TLR4, NF-κB p65, NLRP3, miR-21, matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9), connective tissue growth factor (CTGF), and transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) were reduced dramatically in the OE-GAS5 group compared with the NC group (P < 0.05, P < 0.001, or P < 0.0001). However, GAS5 knock-down significantly increased the expression levels of all the above indices in the KD-GAS5 group relative to NC (P < 0.001 or P < 0.0001) (Fig. 3A). These bidirectional manipulations confirm the regulatory role of GAS5 in modulating both pyroptosis and fibrosis markers. Western blotting revealed that the protein levels of TLR4, NF-κB p65, NLRP3, CTGF, MMP-9, and TGF-β1 were markedly reduced in the OE-GAS5 group compared with NC (P < 0.0001). Meanwhile, GAS5 knock-down significantly elevated the protein levels of TLR4, NF-κB p65, NLRP3, CTGF, MMP-9, and TGF-β1 compared with NC (P < 0.001 or P < 0.0001) (Fig. 3B-C). The concordance between changes at the mRNA and protein levels further validates the regulatory function of GAS5 at the transcriptional and translational levels. Collectively, GAS5 exhibited a suppressive regulatory effect on miR-21 and TLR4. Additionally, GAS5 inhibited pyroptosis and ameliorated fibrosis in AC16 cardiomyocytes by suppressing miR-21 and TLR4 expression. The GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways are involved in the regulation of pyroptosis and cardiac fibrosis

An LPS-induced myocardial cell fibrosis model was established to determine the molecular mechanisms involving the modulation of pyroptosis and cardiac fibrosis by GAS5 and the TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathway. The results revealed that, compared to the NC group, ROS and pro-inflammatory cytokines (including IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18, and TNF-α), and related indicators (miR-21, TLR4, NLRP3, NF-κB p65, GSDMD, caspase-1, CTGF, MMP-9, and TGF-β1) increased substantially in the LPS group (P < 0.001 or P < 0.0001). The expression of GAS5 decreased substantially (P < 0.0001; Fig. 4A-F). These findings are consistent with the established role of LPS in activating the NLRP3 inflammasome and promoting pyroptosis in cardiomyocytes. The fluorescence intensity of TLR4, NLRP3, and NF-κB p65 proteins was enhanced (Fig. 4G). Immunofluorescence analysis revealed nuclear translocation of NF-κB p65 in the LPS group, indicating activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway. However, these effects were reversed after GAS5 + NSA treatment and were consistent with the expression patterns observed in the LPS + miR-21 + inhibitor group. In summary, these findings suggest the involvement of GAS5 and the TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathway in the modulation of LPS-induced cardiomyocyte fibrosis. This mechanism appears to be associated with elevated GAS5 levels and reduced TLR4, ROS, and miR-21, culminating in the suppression of pyroptosis and pro-inflammatory cytokines. Effects of XFC-containing serum on LPS-induced pyroptosis and cardiac fibrosis through the GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways

The subsequent stage of the investigation concerns the effects of XFC on pyroptosis and cardiac fibrosis through the GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways. The results revealed that, compared to the NC group, GAS5 expression was decreased; TLR4, ROS, and miR-21 expression levels were increased, LDH release was increased (Fig. 5A-C, E); pro-inflammatory cytokine levels were increased (Fig. 5D); the fluorescence intensity of TLR4, NLRP3, and NF-κB p65 proteins was enhanced (Fig. 5I); and the expression of fibrosis- and pyroptosis-related proteins, including NLRP3, GSDMD, caspase-1, CTGF, MMP-9, and TGF-β1, was increased (P < 0.001 or P < 0.0001) in the LPS group (Fig. 5F-G). These comprehensive changes confirm the successful establishment of the LPS-induced cardiomyocyte fibrosis and pyroptosis model. Under electron microscopy, compared to the NC group, cells in the LPS group exhibited membrane rupture and leakage of cellular contents, and pyroptotic bodies were also observed (Fig. 5H). The characteristic morphological features of pyroptosis, including plasma membrane blebbing, formation of membrane pores, and release of intracellular contents, were clearly visible in the LPS group. Notably, treatment with XFC-containing serum yielded opposite results. Overall, these findings demonstrate that XFC-containing serum may ameliorate AC16 cardiomyocyte fibrosis by inhibiting pyroptosis and decreasing pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion via the GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways. Discussion

It has been reported that people with RA have a higher risk of cardiovascular disease than the general population[37]. Cardiac fibrosis, a sign of various end-stage CVDs, is also an important factor leading to adverse cardiovascular events[38]. Pyroptosis, a programmed cell death mechanism, has shown promise in cardiac fibrosis therapy, with NLRP3 identified as a potential key molecule[39,40]. However, the molecular mechanisms of pyroptosis in RA-related myocardial fibrosis remain unclear. This study is the first to systematically elucidate the regulatory role of the GAS5/miR-21/TLR4 axis on pyroptosis and fibrosis in an LPS-induced AC16 cardiomyocyte fibrosis model, and to reveal the molecular mechanism by which XFC-containing serum inhibits this signaling axis, alleviates pyroptosis, and improves RA-related myocardial fibrosis.

Canonical pyroptotic signaling is initiated upon caspase-1 activation. This protease serves a dual function: it proteolytically processes GSDMD to liberate its pore-forming N-terminal domain, thereby executing membrane rupture and cell lysis, while simultaneously converting the inactive precursors of IL-18 and IL-1β into their biologically active, secreted forms[41,42]. Hence, pyroptosis-related molecules include GSDMD, IL-1β, IL-18, and the NLRP3 inflammasome[43,44]. In diabetic cardiomyopathy, it has been confirmed that caspase-1, GSDMD, and IL-1β are significantly elevated in cardiac tissue, thereby exacerbating cardiac fibrosis. Furthermore, one study found that regulating miR-135b and inhibiting caspase-1 can reduce the occurrence of cardiac fibrosis in diabetic cardiomyopathy[45]. Moreover, it has been reported that IL-6, IL-1β, IL-18, TNF-α, and NLRP3 increase significantly in mice with diabetic cardiomyopathy; however, these indices decrease significantly after administration of anti-inflammatory agents and pyroptosis inhibitors, effectively reducing myocardial cell death and adverse cardiac remodeling[46]. In addition, elevated pyroptosis-related molecules are detected in the heart tissues of mice with myocardial infarction; inhibiting the activation of pyroptosis can improve cardiac fibrosis and ventricular remodeling[47]. Our findings are consistent with these previous reports, as we demonstrated a notable elevation in the expression of NLRP3, GSDMD, and caspase-1, and pro-inflammatory cytokines in the LPS-induced myocardial cell fibrosis model. This further confirms that pyroptosis activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine release are important pathological mechanisms of RA-associated myocardial fibrosis. However, our study extends these findings by identifying GAS5 as a critical upstream regulator of this process in the specific context of RA-associated cardiac fibrosis.

Recent research has revealed that overexpression of lncRNA GAS5 suppresses isoproterenol-induced myocardial fibrosis via modulation of the miR-217/SIRT1 axis[48] and improves cardiac function in coronary artery disease by suppressing the Wnt/β-linker protein pathway[49]. The Xu group suggested that GAS5 expression is reduced in HL-1 cardiomyocytes and that GAS5 over-expression decreases the levels of IL-18, IL-1β, NLRP3, and caspase-1 in HL-1 cells, thereby ameliorating diabetic cardiomyopathy[14]. The Hui group reported that GAS5 expression decreases in cardiac fibroblasts, whereas miR-21 increases. Further studies have demonstrated that GAS5 may regulate phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) expression by binding to miR-21, thereby affecting myocardial fibrosis[50,51]. Furthermore, the TLR4/ROS/miR-21 signaling pathway can impact the proliferation of LPS-treated cells[52]. The consistency between this study and previous reports lies in the fact that both have confirmed the anti-fibrotic and anti-pyroptotic effects of GAS5. However, the unique contributions of this study are as follows: it focuses on the specific disease context of RA, filling the research gap regarding the GAS5/miR-21 axis in RA-related myocardial fibrosis; it integrates the TLR4/ROS pathway, revealing the complete mechanism by which GAS5 regulates the TLR4/ROS/NF-κB/NLRP3 signaling axis via miR-21, thereby inhibiting pyroptosis and fibrosis; and it incorporates traditional Chinese medicine intervention, providing molecular mechanism-based support for the clinical application of XFC. Dual-luciferase assays confirmed a direct interaction between miR-21 and GAS5, and overexpression of GAS5 suppressed the levels of TLR4, ROS, and miR-21, suggesting that GAS5 exerts a negative regulatory effect on this pathway. Functional assays further demonstrated that GAS5 overexpression significantly inhibited the expression of NF-κB p65, NLRP3, caspase-1, GSDMD, CTGF, MMP-9, and TGF-β1, and reduced cell proliferation, pro-inflammatory cytokine release, and LDH levels, indicating that GAS5 ameliorates RA cardiac fibrosis by regulating pyroptosis mediated by the TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathway. However, whether the effects of GAS5 on TLR4/ROS, pyroptosis, and fibrotic signaling are entirely dependent on mediation by miR-21 remains not fully understood, and further validation through experiments such as GAS5 knockdown combined with miR-21 overexpression is required.

XFC represents a traditional Chinese medicine formulation with established clinical application in managing RA and its systemic manifestations beyond the joints[53]. For example, XFC can regulate the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines through the TLR4/MAPK/NF-κB signaling pathway, preventing myocardial injury in AA rats[29]. The current study utilized XFC-containing serum to treat a myocardial cell fibrosis model. The study found that ROS, miR-21, and TLR4 expression decreased, and GAS5 expression increased compared with the LPS group. Additionally, NLRP3, NF-κB p65, GSDMD, caspase-1, CTGF, MMP-9, and TGF-β1 protein levels, as well as LDH release, decreased significantly. IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-18 levels were also diminished. The fluorescence intensity of the TLR4, NLRP3, and NF-κB p65 proteins was weakened. Taken together, these findings indicate that XFC can improve cardiac fibrosis in RA, which involves cytokines, pyroptosis, and multiple network-regulatory mechanisms of signal transduction. Given the limited efficacy of current anti-rheumatic therapies in treating cardiac fibrosis, and considering that XFC is already clinically used for RA, the exploration of its cardioprotective effects offers clear feasibility and repurposing value. However, this study has the following limitations: whether the effects of GAS5 on the TLR4/ROS axis, pyroptosis, and fibrotic signaling are entirely dependent on miR-21 mediation remains incompletely understood. Future studies should employ GAS5 knockdown combined with miR-21 overexpression or rescue experiments to further validate this mechanism. Moreover, the specific active components in XFC-containing serum and their direct interaction targets within the GAS5/miR-21/TLR4 axis require elucidation through techniques such as component analysis, molecular docking, and surface plasmon resonance, thereby providing a more substantial theoretical basis for the precise application of the cardioprotective effects of XFC. Therefore, future studies should focus on: validating the GAS5/miR-21/TLR4 axis in animal models of RA-associated cardiac fibrosis; identifying the specific active components of XFC responsible for the observed cardioprotective effects; and investigating the clinical relevance of these findings in RA patients with cardiac involvement.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways play vital roles in LPS-induced myocardial fibrosis. XFC can ameliorate RA-associated cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting pyroptosis and reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine levels via the GAS5 and TLR4/ROS/miR-21 pathways. These findings provide novel insights into the molecular mechanisms of RA-associated cardiac fibrosis and support the clinical application of XFC as a potential therapeutic strategy for preventing cardiac complications in patients with RA.

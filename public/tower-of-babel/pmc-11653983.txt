Evaluating the capacity of tertiary general hospitals in Beijing to prevent and treat respiratory infectious diseases: a delphi study
Authors: Tong Hu, Mingyuan Liu, Xin Tian, Youqing Xin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe capacity of tertiary general hospitals to prevent and treat respiratory infectious diseases plays a key role in effectively containing the spread of major epidemics within hospitals and ensuring early treatment for patients. This, in turn, helps control the transmission of diseases at the societal level and safeguard public health. However, there is a lack of detailed indicators for assessing hospitals’ capacity in this area, which makes it difficult to evaluate and manage practices. Therefore, this study aimed to develop a comprehensive evaluation index system to assess the capabilities of tertiary general hospitals in preventing and treating respiratory infectious diseases.MethodsThis study utilizes a three-dimensional evaluation model, “Structure-Process-Outcome” as the theoretical basis and first-level index of the indicator system for evaluating the abilities of tertiary general hospitals in preventing and treating respiratory infectious disease. A combination of literature review, semi-structured interviews, and Delphi expert consultation was employed to ensure comprehensive and reliable indicators. The hierarchical analysis method (AHP) was used to determine the weights of the indicators.ResultsThe effective response rates for the two rounds of the Delphi survey were 95.2% and 100%, respectively. The expert authority coefficients were 0.905 and 0.923, respectively. Kendall’s W for the importance of all the indicators increased from 0.274 to 0.327 (P < 0.001), and for feasibility, it increased from 0.198 to 0.240 (P < 0.001). After two rounds of expert consultation, a final evaluation indicator system was established, including 3 first-level indicators, 12 s-level indicators, and 41 third-level indicators, with assigned weights.ConclusionThis study establishes a comprehensive evaluation index system, providing a scientific basis for evaluating the capacity of tertiary general hospitals to prevent and treat respiratory infectious diseases. The system provides valuable reference for improving hospital preparedness and response strategies, and for evaluating the infectious disease prevention and treatment system in Beijing, China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-024-10269-4.

Background

In the past two decades, new and sudden large-scale acute respiratory infectious diseases have occurred frequently, posing a serious challenge to the medical and health systems of various countries [1]. Respiratory viruses are highly contagious and lower respiratory tract infections are the main cause of mortality and morbidity worldwide. They represent a major challenge to global public health and pose a serious threat to people’s health, social stability, and economic development [2]. Notably, in China, patients predominantly seek treatment in general hospitals [3, 4]. During the outbreak of respiratory infectious diseases, general hospitals undertook the reception and diagnosis of infected patients [5, 6]. As pivotal components of the infectious disease prevention and control apparatus [7], general hospitals provided about 70% of infectious disease services [8]. As the top medical institution in the medical system, the tertiary general hospitals are the main force in response to major infectious diseases and public health emergencies. It is imperative for they to efficiently discharge their responsibilities in the detection, diagnosis, reporting, and treatment of infectious diseases.

To combat infectious diseases, WHO published comprehensive IPC assessment tools and guidelines, consisting of eight core components: IPC program, IPC guidelines, IPC education and training, Healthcare-associated infection surveillance, Multimodal strategies for IPC interventions, Monitoring/auditing of IPC practices and feedback, Workload, staffing, and bed occupancy, and Built environment, materials, and equipment for IPC [9]. Subsequently, they developed the IPC Assessment Framework (IPCAF), which numerous scholars worldwide have utilized to assess healthcare organizations across different countries and levels, evaluating the implementation of its core components [10–12].

China’s Ministry of Health has formulated accreditation standards for tertiary infectious disease specialty hospitals [13]. Additionally, it issued the “List of Responsibilities for the Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases in Healthcare Institutions” [14]. However, there is currently a lack of unified evaluation standards from the state, Beijing Municipal Health Administration, Infectious Disease Quality Control Center, and other relevant departments regarding the evaluation of general hospitals’ infectious disease prevention and treatment capacity. A detailed and comprehensive evaluation system has not been clearly defined, which makes it difficult to evaluate and manage practices. This is not conducive to identifying the problems and weaknesses, which poses a greater challenge to infectious disease prevention and treatment at the general hospital level.

While existing literature primarily centers on assessing the capacity of healthcare personnel [15–17], there is limited attention towards evaluating the overall capacity of hospitals in infectious disease prevention and treatment. Some scholars have analyzed the capacity of the health system in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, including physical infrastructure and healthcare professionals [18, 19]. For instance, Brownson [20] defined the need for adequate capacity in public health organizations as the availability of resources, structures, and workforce. Similarly, Anne [21] decomposed health system capacity into inputs, processes, outputs, and outcomes.

However, there is a notable paucity of research on developing an evaluation index system specifically tailored to appraising the prevention and treatment capacities of general hospitals concerning infectious diseases. Previous studies by Ding Fan and Zhang Suzhen et al. [22, 23] have predominantly evaluated the capacity of infectious disease departments, laboratory biosafety management, and nosocomial infection monitoring. However, scant attention has been directed towards assessing hospital prevention and treatment processes and outcomes, particularly in the context of respiratory infectious diseases, with most studies merely summarizing prevention and control strategies [24–26].

The construction of an indicator system for the ability to prevent and treat respiratory infectious diseases has not been deeply explored. Therefore, this study aims to construct an evaluation index system for assessing the prevention and treatment capacity of tertiary general hospitals regarding respiratory infectious diseases. This system provides a scientific foundation for accurately understanding and evaluating the capabilities of these hospitals, ultimately aiding in better public health preparedness and response.

Methods

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in this research.

Study design

This study was guided by Donabedian’s three-dimensional evaluation model “Structure-Process-Outcome” theory [27], and a preliminary evaluation index system was established through a literature review and semi-structured interviews. Subsequently, a more comprehensive and scientific index system was constructed by combining qualitative and quantitative analysis utilizing the Delphi method to consult with experts. Finally, the hierarchical analysis method was used to assign weights to the indicator system. The flowchart of the research process is shown in Fig. 1.

The Delphi method is a well-developed and easy subjective decision-making method for questioning experts and transforming expert opinions into group consensus [28]. It has been widely used in healthcare services to synthesize theoretical and practical experiences of experts from different professional backgrounds to make comprehensive judgments and evaluations [29]. The characteristics of the Delphi method make it effective for judgment and prediction, and its results are widely representative and reliable [30].

Study design

Participants

The number of experts in Delphi studies varies, typically ranging from 5 to 60 or more [31]. In this study, purposive sampling was used to select experts from different regions and specializations in Beijing, China. The selection criteria were: (a) involvement in respiratory infectious disease prevention, control, treatment, and related work; (b) more than 10 years of work experience; (c) holding a medium-grade professional position or higher; (d) possessing a bachelor’s degree or higher; and (e) volunteering to participate and actively providing feedback. Based on these criteria, We distributed the consultation questionnaires to 21 experts via WeChat and email, and 20 responded in the first round of consultation, with a response rate of 95.2%. In the second round, all 20 experts responded, achieving a 100% response rate. In total, 20 experts participated in the Delphi consultation.

Establishing the preliminary indicator pool

This study was based on the Donabedian’s “Structure-Process-Outcome” model and the Infection Prevention and Control Assessment Framework (IPCAF) published by WHO [9]. Indicators related to training frequency, professional staff resources, organizational management, monitoring, and professional team workload were considered. Keywords like “infectious disease prevention and treatment,” “infectious disease prevention and control capacity,” “respiratory tract,” and “index system” were used to search PubMed, Web of Science, CNKI, and Wanfang databases. National policies, regulations, and reports were also consulted, including work norms for infectious diseases issued by the State and the Beijing Municipal Health Commission and the Center for Disease Control and Prevention.

From November to December 2023, the researcher conducted face-to-face one-on-one interviews with 15 individuals responsible for infectious disease prevention and treatment in Beijing using purposive sampling. The Inclusion criteria were the same as those for the Delphi consulting specialist. Interviewees came from 12 urban districts, including Haidian and Xicheng. Each interview lasted 60–90 min, and the interview outline is shown in supplemental file 1. Informed consent was obtained, and recordings were transcribed within 24 h.

Data were organized and analyzed using Nvivo 12.0, and the interview data were coded using Rooted Theory Analysis [32], aligning the coded content with the “Structure-Process-Outcome” dimensions. Combining literature and qualitative research, a preliminary evaluation index system for the ability of tertiary general hospitals to prevent and treat respiratory infectious diseases was constructed, including 3 primary indicators, 12 secondary indicators, and 46 tertiary indicators.

Using the delphi method to develop an index system

Expert consultation questionnaire design

The consultation questionnaire consisted of four parts: (1) Introduction: describing the study background, purpose, and instructions for completing the questionnaire; (2) Basic information about the experts: gender, age, education, title, years of experience, and field of specialization; (3) Expert Consultation Form for the Indicator System of the Prevention and Treatment of Respiratory Infectious Diseases in Tertiary General Hospitals: the importance and feasibility of each indicator were assessed using a five-point Likert scale. Experts were also asked to suggest additions, deletions, and modifications to enrich the evaluation indicator system. When the expert opinions were relatively consistent, the AHP questionnaire was included; (4) Familiarization with the study content and judgment of the indicators. Definition of related terms is shown in supplemental file 2.

Delphi consulting and feedback cycle

From January to March 2024, after obtaining informed consent, the consultation was conducted via email and WeChat. Experts rated and commented on the importance and feasibility of the indicators without communicating with each other. After the first round, comments, suggestions, and feedback were sorted, and the questionnaire was adjusted for the second round to confirm the evaluation indicator system’s accuracy. Screening criteria for indicators included an average importance score ≥ 4.00, average feasibility score ≥ 4.00, full mark rate ≥ 20%, and coefficient of variation < 2.5 [33]. Indicators not meeting the criteria were discussed by the research team for retention or deletion [34]. Consensus was reached after two rounds of consultation.

Quality management

To ensure representativeness and reliability, experts were selected based on specific criteria, and bias was minimized by choosing experts committed to multiple research fields. The research team summarized and analyzed expert opinions, communicated improvements timely, and provided detailed explanations and verifications. Kendall’s coefficient of coordination tested the degree of coordination of expert opinions.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed descriptively using SPSS 26.0. Measurement data were described as mean ± standard deviation, and count data as frequencies and percentages. Expert evaluations were expressed as coefficient of variation (CV), importance, and feasibility means, and perfect scores (CLV) [35]. Expert enthusiasm was expressed as the effective return rate of the questionnaire. The expert authority coefficient was the mean value of the basis of judgment and familiarity, as shown in Tables 1 and 2. Kendall’s consistency coefficient measured the degree of harmonization of expert opinions, with statistical significance at P < 0.05. Weights of each indicator were calculated using the hierarchical analysis process with Yaahp software (Shanxi Far Decision Software Technology Co., Ltd., Shanxi, China).

The proportion of experts with senior professional titles was 65%, and the experts’ working years ranged from 11 to 38 years. The basic information of the experts is shown in Table 3.

Enthusiasm and authority coefficient of experts

The enthusiasm of the experts is reflected by the response rate of the questionnaire. It is generally believed that when the response rate is greater than 70%, the enthusiasm of the experts is quite high [36]. In the first round, 21 questionnaires were distributed, and 20 were recovered, with a response rate of 95.2%. In the second round, 20 questionnaires were distributed and all were recovered, with a response rate of 100%. The authority coefficient (Cr) of the expert consultation was calculated to be 0.905 and 0.923 for the two rounds, indicating high authority (Table 4).

Results of indicator screening

In the first round, the arithmetic mean scores for all items ranged from 3.45 to 5.00, the full mark rate from 5 to 100%, and the coefficient of variation from 0.00 to 0.257, with the standard deviation between 0.00 and 0.988. In the second round, the arithmetic mean scores ranged from 3.70 to 5.00, the full mark rate from 10 to 100%, and the coefficient of variation from 0.00 to 0.243, with the standard deviation between 0.00 and 0.918 (Table 6).More detailed results are shown in supplemental file 3 and 4. Based on the screening criteria and expert opinions, 5 indicators were modified, 10 indicators were deleted, and 8 indicators were added in the first round. In the second round, 3 indicators were deleted, and 56 indicators were eventually retained.

In the first round, experts suggested modifying the description of “hardware resources” to “hardware and software resources.” The second-level indicator “service efficiency and quality” was split into “service efficiency” and “service quality.” The indicator “rational use of medication” was merged into “service quality,” and “training situation” was incorporated into the first-level indicator “process.” Additional tertiary indicators were added based on expert suggestions, and the questionnaire for the second round was prepared. In the second round, expert opinions were relatively consistent. The process of revising the tertiary indicators is shown in supplemental file 5.

Final index system with weights

After two rounds of expert consultation, an indicator system containing 3 primary indicators, 12 secondary indicators, and 41 tertiary indicators was finalized. The hierarchical analysis method was used to calculate the weights of the indicators. The specific steps involved constructing a hierarchical model, decomposing it into the 3 first-level, 12 s-level, and 41 third-level indexes, and asking experts to use the 1–9 scale method [37] for pairwise comparative scoring to construct expert comparative judgment matrices. A total of 320 judgment matrices were analyzed, and the consistency test for all matrices was less than 0.1, indicating reasonable indicator weights [38]. Among the first-level indicators, “Process” had the highest weight (0.3748), followed by “Outcome” (0.3242) and “Structure” (0.3010). Table 7 shows the weights of the indicators. Discussion

Characterization of the evaluation index system

First, the ability of medical institutions to prevent and treat respiratory infectious diseases requires a scientific and comprehensive evaluation system. The evaluation index system established in this study is rich in content and possesses a certain degree of scientific and systematic approach. Based on Donabedian’s three-dimensional evaluation model “structure-process-outcome” theory [24], which is widely used in health services evaluation research, this system aligns well with the research field of this study. It comprehensively covers the prevention and treatment of infectious diseases, from early detection to early treatment, making the indicator system systematic and comprehensive. To ensure the scientific rationality of the system, 20 experts from various urban areas and professional fields in Beijing, China, were consulted. Their extensive experience and high academic authority ensured the scientific credibility of the evaluation indicator system.

Second, the indicator system highlights the responsibilities and capabilities that tertiary general hospitals should have in the prevention and treatment of respiratory infectious diseases. Structural indicators include Human resources, Hardware and software resources, and Organizational management. Process indicators cover Pre-screening and triage, Monitoring and early warning, Pathogen detection, Information Reports, Treatment ability, and Training situation. Outcome indicators focus on Service volume, Service efficiency, and Service quality. These indicators are refined and authentic, reflecting the essential workflows and responsibilities in infectious disease prevention and treatment.

Third, our indicators align with WHO recommendations for infection prevention and control (IPC) programs, IPC guidelines, IPC education and training, healthcare-associated infection surveillance, multimodal strategies for IPC interventions, workload, staffing, and related dimensional indicators [9]. This alignment shows the consistency between our expert opinions and WHO standards, ensuring the indicators’ relevance and applicability to the clinical frontline in Beijing, China.

Fourth, the feasibility of extracting indicator data is assured. Objective data can be obtained from hospital records and information systems, while subjective data can be collected through on-site research and hospital reports.

Result of index weight analysis

Among primary indicators, “process” (0.3748) is the most significant, indicating its paramount importance in infectious disease prevention and treatment within hospital settings. Within the “structure” category, “human resources” (0.1188) is the most heavily weighted secondary indicator, emphasizing the critical role of adequate staffing in disease prevention and treatment. Challenges such as low doctor and nurse per capita ratios in some countries necessitate increased attention to human resource availability [18, 39].

Within the “process” category, among its six secondary indicators, considerable emphasis is placed on “Pathogen detection” (0.1069) and “treatment capacity” (0.0912). Experts underscore the pivotal roles these factors play in combating respiratory infectious diseases [40, 41]. Enhanced pathogen detection facilitates a more nuanced understanding of respiratory infection etiology within populations, consequently informing more effective treatment strategies.

Among the tertiary indicators of “Pathogen detection”, “The number of types of nucleic acid and antigen multiplex tests for infectious respiratory pathogens that can be carried out by hospitals, such as influenza, COVID-19, mycoplasma, etc” has the highest weight (0.0638). Multiplex testing for respiratory pathogens, such as influenza, COVID-19, and mycoplasma, is particularly important, enabling rapid and precise diagnosis and informing effective treatment strategies [42, 43].

Among the tertiary indicators of “treatment capacity”, “Medical staff in fever clinics can independently use high flow oxygen therapy and non-invasive ventilation to treat patients” has the highest weight (0.0431). Fever clinics occupy a critical position in public health emergencies [44], and high-flow oxygen therapy through the nose and noninvasive positive-pressure ventilation can reduce the rate of endotracheal intubation and the rate of death of the patient [45].This highlights the importance of respiratory support techniques in managing critically ill patients at fever clinics [46, 47].

In the “outcome” category, “quality of services” (0.1532) is the most significant secondary indicator. The issue of the quality of healthcare services is crucial in the case of infectious disease epidemics [48]. “The Case Mix Index of the Infectious Diseases Department” (0.0567) is the highest weighted tertiary indicator, serving as a measure of disease severity and treatment complexity [49]. Rational use of antimicrobial and antiviral drugs is also critical, with indicators such as “The rate of pathogenic testing before antimicrobial treatment in hospitalized patients” (0.0504) and “Positive rate of pathogenic diagnosis before antiviral drug use” (0.0461) highlighting the need for appropriate medication use [50–53].

Strengths and limitations

In 2011, the Chinese Ministry of Health developed evaluation criteria for tertiary hospitals specializing in infectious diseases [10], and in 2023, it issued a List of Responsibilities for the Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases in Medical Institutions [11]. However, these guidelines lack a comprehensive evaluation system for comparing medical institutions. Currently, China and Beijing still lack standardized evaluation systems for the ability of general hospitals to prevent and treat infectious diseases. Previous studies have focused primarily on resource allocation, with limited indicators related to the process and outcome of infectious disease services. This study is innovative in establishing an evaluation index system based on the “structure-process-outcome” theory and applying it to respiratory infectious disease prevention and treatment services. It builds upon existing research by elucidating the prevention and treatment capacity of general hospitals, facilitating targeted improvements, and guiding enhancements in public health readiness and evaluation systems for infectious disease treatment.

However, this indicator system does not directly measure the effectiveness and quality of standardized care. The World Health Organization (WHO) outlines the requirements for providing quality care, which include: good governance; a skilled and competent health workforce that is supported and motivated; financing mechanisms that enable and encourage quality care; information systems that continuously monitor and learn to drive better care; medicines, devices and technologies that are available, safe and appropriately regulated; and accessible and well-equipped healthcare facilities [54].In the indicator system we developed, there are measures for healthcare workforce, financial support, information systems, related technical equipment, and healthcare facilities. Following the COVID−19 pandemic, in order to better control infectious diseases, it was mentioned at the 431st press conference of the Beijing Novel Coronavirus Epidemic Prevention and Control that the number of fever clinics in the city’s hospitals has increased from 94 to 303. All secondary and higher-level hospitals in the city have set up fever clinics or consultation rooms. These fever clinics are standardized according to China’s national conditions, located in relatively independent areas with a “three zones, two channels” system—separate isolation observation rooms, with equipment and facilities isolated from other normal medical services. Patient flow, logistics, and air flow are all kept isolated to prevent cross-contamination. Given that this study focuses on the prevention and treatment capabilities of respiratory infectious diseases in tertiary general hospitals, it places more emphasis on early detection, reporting, isolation, and treatment of infectious diseases. The goal is to detect and isolate infected individuals promptly, increase testing speed, shorten diagnosis times, optimize diagnostic and treatment processes, improve treatment rates, reduce infection rates, and report information in a timely manner to effectively block the spread of the epidemic. Therefore, this study does not directly measure the effectiveness and quality of standardized care.

Additionally, the system’s reliability and feasibility require empirical testing, and future analysis should delve into the indicator system’s validity and credibility. Since the constructed evaluation index system is based on practice in Beijing, China, it is more suitable for countries with similar healthcare systems and has certain limitations.

Conclusion

This study establishes an evaluation index system for assessing the capability of tertiary general hospitals in preventing and treating respiratory infectious diseases.The index system outlines the essential capabilities that tertiary general hospitals should possess and serves as a foundation for comprehensive evaluation. Additionally, it provides a reference for further improving the infectious disease prevention and treatment evaluation system in Beijing, China, and for strengthening the public health capacity building of tertiary general hospitals.

On the parametrised Whitehead torsion of families of nearby Lagrangian submanifolds
Sylvain Courte and Noah Porcelli

Abstract
Motivated by the strong nearby Lagrangian conjecture, we constrain the parametrised Whitehead torsion of a family of closed exact Lagrangian submanifolds in a cotangent bundle. We prove the parametrised Whitehead torsion admits a factorisation through simpler maps, in particular implying it is trivial on π 0 \pi_{0} , π 1 \pi_{1} , and that its image is divisible by the Euler characteristic. We provide concrete implications for the Lagrangian monodromy question in the case of a high-dimensional torus. This generalises earlier work of Abouzaid and Kragh [ AK18 ] on the π 0 \pi_{0} version, using different methods. Our main tool is the theory of twisted generating functions, building on [ ACGK20 ] .

Introduction

1.1
Main results

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold. A
nearby Lagrangian
in
T^{*}M
is a closed exact Lagrangian submanifold in
T^{*}M
. In this paper, we study,
\mathcal{L}(M)
the space of closed connected exact Lagrangian submanifolds of
T^{*}M
(see Section
4

The
nearby Lagrangian conjecture
asserts that the space
\mathcal{L}(M)
is path-connected, namely any such
Lagrangian submanifold is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero-section, and in particular diffeomorphic to
M
. This paper is mainly concerned
with the
strong
nearby Lagrangian conjecture, which asserts that the space
\mathcal{L}(M)
is in fact contractible.
No counter-examples are known even for this refinement. To our knowledge, the only cases where the strong
conjecture is proved are:
M=D^{1}
,
S^{1}
(folkloric) and
D^{2}
(Eliashberg-Polterovich
[
EP96
]
). In the cases here where
M
has boundary, we assume additionally that the nearby Lagrangians coincide with the 0-section near the boundary.

A theorem of Abouzaid and Kragh
[
Abo12
,
Kra13
]
(see also Guillermou
[
Gui23
]
) asserts that for

,

Another result of Abouzaid and Kragh
[

is a

This amounts to say that the Whitehead torsion
\tau(g_{L})
of

– a purely algebraic invariant living in the Whitehead group
\mathrm{Wh}(\pi_{1}M)
– vanishes. This is proved using Lagrangian Floer theory with
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}]

Our aim in this paper is to provide a generalisation of this vanishing result in the parametrised setting. The Whitehead group is isomorphic to the group of path components
\pi_{0}\mathcal{H}(M)
, where
\mathcal{H}(M)
is the space of
stable
h
-cobordisms on

(cf. Section

), and the correct parametrised analogue of the Whitehead torsion is a map
w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{L}(M)\to\mathcal{H}(M)
, constructed in
[
WW88
]
, cf. also Section
2.4
.

Remark 1.1
.

By definition,

factors through a map
w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{S}(M)\to\mathcal{H}(M)
, where
\mathcal{S}(M)
is the

considered in geometric topology. Roughly speaking, the structure space is the moduli space of pairs

is a closed manifold and

There is a canonical forgetful map

1.2
Applications to Lagrangian monodromy

There is a tautological smooth fibre bundle over
\mathcal{L}(M)
whose fibre over a nearby Lagrangian
L\subseteq T^{*}M
is exactly
L
. Letting
\mathcal{L}_{0}(M)
be the path component of the 0-section, this bundle is then classified by a map
\mathcal{L}_{0}(M)\to B\mathrm{Diff}(M)
. It is tempting to use Theorem
1.2
to obtain constraints on this map.

For instance if we are given a loop
(L_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
of exact Lagrangian submanifolds which is based at the zero-section, i.e.
L_{0}=L_{1}=M
,
there is a
monodromy
(isotopy class of) diffeomorphism
\phi
living in the mapping class group
\pi_{0}\mathrm{Diff}(M)
. In terms of the classifying map above, this is the image of the loop
(L_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}

under the morphism

\pi_{1}\mathcal{L}_{0}(M)\to\pi_{1}B\mathrm{Diff}(M)=\pi_{0}\mathrm{Diff}(M).

From the mere fact that the projection

is a homotopy equivalence for all

we learn that

must be
homotopic
to the identity. We can then ask the subtler questions of whether
\phi
is
pseudo-isotopic
to the identity and then whether
\phi
is
isotopic
to the identity.

Though we have not been able to prove such statements in the general case, from Corollary

(

.

Definition 2.1

An

2.3
Functoriality

In this section, we recall how
\mathcal{H}(\cdot)
can be made into a functor from the category of compact manifolds to spaces.
In general, it can be extended to a functor from the category of spaces
[
Wal82
]
, but this weaker form of functoriality is both simpler and sufficient for our purposes.

Let
i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\to N
be a codimension 0 embedding of compact manifolds of dimension
n
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
W
be an
h
-cobordism on
M
. Define
V
to be the pair:

V=\left((N\times I)\cup_{M\times\{1\}}W,N\times\{0\}\right)

(2.2)

where we equip this with a smooth structure as in Lemma
A.1
, and smooth corners implicitly.

Then
V
is an
h
-cobordism on
N
.

Proof.

It is clear that the inclusion
N\to V
is a homotopy equivalence. Let
M^{\prime}=W\setminus\mathrm{int}(M)
, and
N^{\prime}=(N\setminus\mathrm{int}(M))\cup_{\partial M}M^{\prime}
. Then
W
is an
h
-cobordism from
M
to
M^{\prime}
, and
V
is a cobordism from
N
to
N^{\prime}
.

Since
M^{\prime}\to W
is an equivalence (relative to
\partial M
), so is the inclusion
N^{\prime}\to(N\setminus\mathrm{int}(M))\cup_{\partial M}W
. Then the inclusion of this second expression into
V
is also an equivalence (the complement is
N\times[0,1)
).
∎

Definition 2.15
.

We define a map
H(i)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H(M)\to H(N)
as follows. For a
k
-simplex

, we define

to be the smooth family of

-cobordisms on
N
over

defined by

\left\{\left((N\times I)\cup_{M\times\{1\}}W_{b},N\times\{0\}\right)\right\}_{b\in\Delta^{k}}

(2.3)

Note these are
h
-cobordisms by Lemma

.

This can be made compatible with stabilisation and so induces a map on stable
h
-cobordism spaces
\mathcal{H}(i)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{H}(M)\to\mathcal{H}(N)
.

By Proposition
2.11
,

induces a map

for any compact manifold

. We may describe this explicitly: this sends

to

\left\{\left((N\times I)\cup_{M\times\{1\}}W_{b},N\times\{0\}\right)\right\}_{b}

again using Lemma
A.1
and smoothing corners implicitly.

For a general map

, we approximate it by an embedding

and take a tubular neighborhood

which is now a codimension zero embedding, so we find a map

which stably gives

(see
[

]
for more details). We use
this only for the inclusion of a point in

(as in Theorem

) where it amounts to thickening the point to a disk in

.

We use two connectivity estimates (though these are significantly more refined than is required for our purposes):

(Igusa stability
[

]
)
.

The stabilisation
\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H(M)\to H(M\times I)
is

.

(

,
[

.

The induced map on stable

-cobordism spaces

is

is
(k+1)
-connected.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let

be a compact

is

-connected and

The condition on

2.4
The parametrised Whitehead torsion

Definition 2.19
.

Let
B
be a compact manifold,
M
a closed manifold,
(L_{b})_{b\in B}
a family of
closed manifolds parametrised by
B
and
g_{b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L_{b}\to M
a family of homotopy
equivalences. Two such families
(L_{b},g_{b})
and
(L^{\prime}_{b},g^{\prime}_{b})
are equivalent
if there exists a family of diffeomorphisms
\psi_{b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L_{b}\to L^{\prime}_{b}
and a family of homotopies
between
g^{\prime}_{b}\circ\psi_{b}
and
g_{b}
.
We denote
\mathcal{S}_{B}(M)
the set of equivalence classes of such families.

Definition 2.20
.

We define
\mathcal{S}(M)
to be (the geometric realisation of) the simplicial set whose
k
-simplices
are families
(L_{b},g_{b})_{b\in\Delta^{k}}
as in Definition
2.19
(not considered up to equivalence) and the face and degeneracy
maps are given by pullbacks along the standard face/degeneracy maps between simplices. This is called the
structure space
of
M
.

Proposition 2.21
.

For any compact manifold
B
and closed manifold
M
, there is a natural bijection

\mathcal{S}_{B}(M)\to[B,\mathcal{S}(M)].

We will associate to an element of
\mathcal{S}_{B}(M)
a stable
h
-cobordism on
M
, well defined
up to isomorphism, namely an element of
\mathcal{H}_{B}(M)
. When
B
is a point,
\mathcal{H}_{B}(M)=\mathrm{Wh}(\pi_{1}M)

and we recover the Whitehead torsion of a homotopy equivalence.

We will use the following standard lemma, and reproduce its proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
X
be a closed connected manifold, and
X\to Y
an embedding of codimension at least 3 into a compact manifold
Y
which is a homotopy equivalence. Assume the inclusion
\partial Y\to Y
is an isomorphism on
\pi_{1}
.

Then the complement
W=Y\setminus\mathrm{int}D_{X}
of a tubular neighborhood
D_{X}
of
X
in
Y
is an
h
-cobordism on
\partial D_{X}
.

Proof.

Since
k\geq 3
the maps
\pi_{1}\partial D_{X}\to\pi_{1}D_{X}
and
\pi_{1}W\to\pi_{1}Y
are isomorphisms (any map from a surface to
Y
is homotopic to a map disjoint from
X
). Moreover since
\pi_{1}\partial Y\to\pi_{1}Y
is an isomorphism the same holds for
\pi_{1}\partial Y\to\pi_{1}W
. We have
H_{*}(Y,D_{X})=H_{*}(Y,X)=0
with any local system and by excision the same holds for
H_{*}(W,\partial D_{X})
. By Hurewicz’s theorem and Whitehead’s theorem we then conclude that
\partial D_{X}\to W
is a homotopy equivalence. Using Poincaré-Lefschetz duality we obtain the same conclusion for the inclusion of the other boundary component
\partial Y\to W
(note that our assumptions imply that
\partial Y
is non-empty and connected).
∎

Pick
k\geq 3
(large enough) and a family of embeddings
L_{b}\to\mathrm{int}(M\times D^{k})
. Pick
a family of tubular neighborhoods
E_{b}
of
L_{b}
in
\mathrm{int}(M\times D^{k})
. We define
W_{b}=(M\times D^{k})\setminus\mathrm{int}E_{b}
.
By Lemma
2.22
this is a family of
h
-cobordisms on
\partial E_{b}
or, seen from the other side, on
M\times S^{k-1}
.

For large enough
k
, the family of embeddings
L_{b}\to M\times D^{k}
are isotopic, and thus the resulting
h
-cobordisms

(W_{b},M\times S^{k-1})
are isomorphic. However to find a meaningful invariant we need to suppress the
dependence on
k
so we require an extra step.

Pick a classical right inverse
(W^{\prime}_{b};M\times S^{k-1},\partial E_{b})
to
W_{b}
(see Lemma
2.6
), namely

W_{b}\cup_{M\times S^{k-1}}W^{\prime}_{b}\simeq\partial E_{b}\times I.

We consider
E^{\prime}_{b}=(M\times D^{k})\cup_{M\times S^{k-1}}W^{\prime}_{b}\simeq E_{b}
and the pair

(V_{b},M\times D^{k})=(E^{\prime}_{b}\times I,M\times D^{k}\times\{0\}).

Lemma 2.23
.

For each
b
, the pair
(V_{b},M\times D^{k})
is an
h
-cobordism.

Proof.

We supress
b
from the notations.
V
deformation retracts onto
E^{\prime}
and then further to
M\times D^{k}
since
W^{\prime}
is
an
h
-cobordism. For the other boundary component
\partial_{+}V=(E^{\prime}\times\{1\})\cup(\partial E^{\prime}\times I)\cup W^{\prime}
, we observe the factorisation

E^{\prime}\times\{1\}\cup((\partial E^{\prime})\times I)\to\partial_{+}V\to E^{\prime}\times I=V

where the map on the left and the composition are homotopy equivalences, hence so is
\partial_{+}V\to V
.
∎

Lemma 2.24
.

Under the natural embedding
D^{k}\to D^{k+1}
, the above construction of
(V_{b}\times I)
gets stabilised
as in (
2.1
).

Proof.

We use the notations of the previous paragraph, but suppress the subscript

for simplicity.
Pick a subinterval

. We give subcripts

to the object constructed
as above with the integer

and we thicken this to

Up to smoothing corners,

and the

To complete the construction at the range

.

The

2.5
Decompositions of total space

Let
N
be a closed manifold.

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}W\to C
be a smooth fibre bundle over a compact connected manifold
C
, and
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}N\times C\to W
an embedding of fibre bundles, such that for each
c\in C
the fibre
(\pi^{-1}\{c\},N)
is an
h
-cobordism. In particular, the total space
(W,N\times C)
forms an
h
-cobordism.

Assume further that each fibre
(\pi^{-1}\{c\},N)
is a trivialisable
h
-cobordism: it admits a diffeomorphism to
(N\times I,N\times\{0\})
(crucially, we do

Then there is a diffeomorphism
(W,N\times C)\cong(N\times C\times I,N\times C\times\{0\})
relative to
N\times C
, i.e. the total space of the bundle is a trivial
h
-cobordism.

In general, the diffeomorphism produced will not respect the projection maps to
C
.

Proof.

Let
\partial_{-}W=\operatorname{Im}(\iota)
and let
\partial_{+}W=\partial W\setminus\partial_{-}W
.

We choose a Morse function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C\to\mathbb{R}
together with a gradient-like vector field
Y
; we may choose these so that
Y
points strictly inwards along
\partial C
.

Above a neighborhood
U
of the set
\mathrm{Crit}(f)
of critical points of
f
, we pick a trivialisation
of the bundle
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U\times N\times[0,1]\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\pi^{-1}(U)
.
We pick a function
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}W\to[0,1]
to be any function which has regular level sets
g^{-1}(0)=\partial_{-}W
and
g^{-1}(1)=\partial_{+}W
and coincides with the projection to
[0,1]

in the trivialisation
\varphi
above a neighborhood of
\mathrm{Crit}(f)
. We then choose a fiberwise gradient-like

X
for
g
. Finally, we pick a lift
Y^{\prime}
of
Y
which is horizontal above a neighborhood of
\mathrm{Crit}(f)
with respect to the trivialisation
\varphi
and tangent to
\partial W
.

We claim that the vector-field
Z=X+Y^{\prime}
is gradient-like for the function
h^{a}=g+a\pi^{*}f
for
a>0
sufficiently large.
Indeed we have:

dh^{a}(Z)=dg(X)+dg(Y^{\prime})+a\pi^{*}(df(Y)).

We have
dg(X)\geq 0
and
\pi^{*}df(Y)\geq 0
everywhere. Near
\pi^{-1}(\mathrm{Crit}(f))
,
dg(Y^{\prime})=0
,
dg(X)>0
so
dh^{a}(Z)>0
. Away from a neighborhood of

\pi^{-1}(\mathrm{Crit}(f))
,
\pi^{*}df(Y)>0
and
dg(Y^{\prime})
is bounded, so
dh^{a}(Z)>0
for sufficiently large
a>0
. Moreover
Z
is transverse to
\partial W
, inward pointing along
\partial_{-}W
and outward pointing along
\partial_{+}W
since this is the case for
X
and
Y^{\prime}
is tangent to
\partial W
.

So we conclude that all trajectories of
Z
starting on
\partial_{-}W
go all the way to
\partial_{+}W
. After renormalizing
Z
, we then define a diffeomorphism
\partial_{-}W\times[0,1]\to W
uniquely by mapping
\partial_{-}W\times\{0\}
to
\partial_{-}W
by the identity map and mapping
\partial_{t}
(the vector field corresponding
to the
t
-coordinate on
[0,1]
) to
Z
.
∎

A similar result holds when each fibre is not necessarily trivial:

Proposition 2.29
.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}W\to C
be a smooth fibre bundle over a compact connected manifold
C
, and
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}N\times C\to W

the fibre
(\pi^{-1}\{c\},N)

h
-cobordism.

Assume further that each
h
-cobordism
(\pi^{-1}\{c\},N)
is diffeomorphic to an

-cobordism

, and that

-cobordism.

More succinctly, this says that the total space of a bundle of

Let

be the other boundary component of

Let

∎

Proof.

1.

2.

The

5.1
Generating functions of tube type and their difference functions

Definition 5.1
.

A smooth function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{l}\to\mathbb{R}
is
almost quadratic
if it is of the form
f=q+g
, where
q
and
g
are
C^{1}
and satisfy:

1.

q
is a degree 2 homogeneous function (meaning
q(\lambda v)=\lambda^{2}q(v)
for
\lambda\geq 0
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
),

2.

\nabla q(v)\neq 0
for all
v\neq 0
,

3.

|\nabla g(v)|\leq c
for some constant
c>0
.

A function is of
tube type
if it is almost quadratic and homotopic to a non-degenerate quadratic form through almost quadratic functions.

Remark 5.2
.

We do note require
q
and
g
themselves to be smooth: typically they will not be smooth at 0.

Remark 5.3
.

Without changing the critical points of
f=q+g
, we can cut-off
g
to make it compactly supported.
However the compactly supported condition is not well-behaved with respect to direct sum, that is why
we use the relaxed condition that
|\nabla g|
is bounded.

Definition 5.4
.

A function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
generating function of tube type
if each restriction
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\to\mathbb{R}
is of tube type
and the map
\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
vanishes transversely.

Such a function
f
generates a Legendrian immersion in
J^{1}(M)
by the assignment

(x,v)\in\left\{\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(x,v)=0\right\}\mapsto\left(x,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,v),f(x,v)\right).

If this map is an embedding with image
L
, we say that
L
is
generated by

f
.

The generating functions of tube type are generalisations of the more traditional generating functions quadratic at infinity
which correspond to the case where
q
is a non-degenerate quadratic form.

For Legendrian submanifolds
L
which lift closed exact Lagrangian submanifolds of
T^{*}M
,
an existence result for generating functions was proved in
[
ACGK20
]
(and reformulated in this quadratic setting in
[
AAGCK
]
).
However, the generating functions produced there are in general
twisted
by a cocycle of non-degenerate quadratic forms.

Theorem 5.5
.

[
ACGK20
,
AAGCK
]

Let
M
be a closed manifold,
L
a closed Legendrian submanifold of

which projects injectively to

and

the projection.
There exists

•

a finite totally ordered open cover
(M_{i})_{i\in I}

•

integers

,

•

f_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M_{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}
generating

,

•

q_{ij}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M_{i}\cap M_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}-n_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}
for all

such that
for all
i<j

on

Remark 5.6
.

Technically speaking, in
[

]
the twisted generating function is constructed for a Legendrian submanifold

which is

Appendix A

In various places throughout the paper, we glue manifolds together along subspaces of their boundary. The naïve gluing is automatically a topological manifold, but we require smoothness. In fact, the gluing is smooth in its interior; in nice cases (such as when the boundary is smooth but with corners), the boundary can be smoothed using

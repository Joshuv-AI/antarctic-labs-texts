Hausdorff dimension of self-similar measures and sets with common fixed point structure Thanks: BB and MV acknowledge support from the grants NKFI K142169 and NKFI KKP144059 ”Fractal geometry and applications” Research Group.
Balázs Bárány, Manuj Verma

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Hausdorff dimension of self-similar measures and sets on the real line, where the generating iterated function system consists of some maps that share the same fixed point. In particular, we will show that out of a Hausdorff co-dimension one exceptional set of natural parameters, such systems satisfy a weak exponential separation. This significantly strengthens the previous result of the first author and Szvák [ 6 ] . As an application, we give the Hausdorff dimension of self-affine measures supported on the generalised 4-corner set.

1.
Introduction and Statements

For
N\geq 2
, the system
\mathcal{I}=\big\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{N}\big\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called an
iterated function system (IFS)
, if each
f_{i}
is a contraction map on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. Hutchinson
[
16
]
proved that there exists a unique non-empty compact set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(A).

Let
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{N})
be a probability vector. Hutchinson
[
16
]
also proved that there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\mu
supported on
A
such that

\mu(B)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\mu(f_{i}^{-1}(B))

for all Borel sets
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The set
A
is called the
attractor
of IFS
\mathcal{I}
and the measure
\mu
is known as the
stationary measure
corresponding to IFS
\mathcal{I}
with the probability vector
\mathbf{p}
.

This paper focuses on the dimension theory of self-similar IFSs on the line. The IFS
\mathcal{I}
is called a
self-similar iterated function system
on
\mathbb{R}
if each
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a similarity map for
i\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, i.e.

f_{i}(x)=r_{i}x+c_{i}
where
0<|r_{i}|<1
and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the attractor
A
is called a
self-similar set
corresponding to the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
. The stationary measure
\mu
is known as the
self-similar measure
corresponding to the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
with probability vector
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{N})
. For later purposes, let us recall the definition of the Hausdorff dimension here.

Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a
\delta
cover of
F
if
F\subset\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i}
and
0<|U_{i}|\leq\delta
for each
{i}
, where
|U_{i}|
denotes the diameter of the set
U_{i}
. For each
\delta>0
and
s\geq 0
, we define

\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(F):=\inf\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|U_{i}|^{s}:\{U_{i}\}\text{ is a }\delta\text{-cover of }F\Big\}\text{ and }\mathcal{H}^{s}(F):=\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(F).

We call
\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)
the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
of the set
F
. Using this, the
Hausdorff dimension
of the set
F
is defined by

\dim_{H}(F)=\inf\{s\geq 0:\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)=0\}.

For the basic properties, we refer the reader to Falconer’s book
[
11
]
. Another widely used dimension concept is the box-counting (or Minkowski) dimension; however, Falconer
[
9
]
pointed out that these dimension concepts are equal for self-similar sets, so we omit them.

For a Borel probability measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
lower- and upper local dimension
of the measure
\mu
at the point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined as

\underline{d}_{\mu}(x)=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r},\quad\overline{d}_{\mu}(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r},

where
B(x,r)
denotes the open ball of radius
r
with center
x
. We say that the measure
\mu
is
exact dimensional
if there is a constant
c
such that
\underline{d}_{\mu}(x)=\overline{d}_{\mu}(x)=c
for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We will denote this value by
\dim_{H}(\mu)
. Feng and Hu
[
12
]
proved that every self-similar measure
\mu
is exact dimensional. For further properties of the dimension of measures, see Przytycki and Urbański
[
21
, Section 7.6]
.

Let us define the
similarity dimension
for the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
to be the unique number
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|r_{i}|^{s_{0}}=1.

Furthermore, the quantities

h_{\mathbf{p}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\log p_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\chi(\mathbf{p})=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\log|r_{i}|

are known respectively as the
entropy
and the
Lyapunov exponent
of the self-similar measure
\mu
corresponding to the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
with probability vector
\mathbf{p}
. It is well known that

\dim_{H}(A)\leq\min\{1,s_{0}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\dim_{H}(\mu)\leq\min\bigg\{1,\frac{h_{\mathbf{p}}}{\chi(\mathbf{p})}\bigg\}

regardless of any further assumptions. However, equalities appear in the above expressions under some suitable separation conditions.

Theorem 1.1

(Hutchinson
[
16
]
, Cawley and Mauldin
[
8
]
)
.

Let the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
on
\mathbb{R}
. If
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the
open set condition (OSC)
, that is, there is a nonempty and bounded open set
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
f_{i}(O)\subset O
and
f_{i}(O)\cap f_{j}(O)=\emptyset
for
i\neq j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, then

\dim_{H}(A)=s_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\dim_{H}(\mu)=\frac{h_{\mathbf{p}}}{\chi(\mathbf{p})},

(1.1)

where
A
is the self-similar attractor of the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
and
\mu
is the self-similar measure corresponding to the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
with probability vector
\mathbf{p}
.

Hochman
[
14
]
introduced the Exponential Separation Condition for the self-similar IFSs, which is a much weaker separation condition than the OSC. We say that the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfies the
Exponential Separation Condition (ESC)
if there exists a constant
\eta>0
such that for infinitely many values of
n\in\mathbb{N}
for all
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n},j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
with
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\neq(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})
and
r_{i_{1}}\cdots r_{i_{n}}=r_{j_{1}}\cdots r_{j_{n}}
, we have

|f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}(0)-f_{j_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{j_{n}}(0)|\geq{\eta}^{n}.

In a proper sense, the exponential separation is a typical property for self-similar IFSs. For example, suppose the contraction ratios and translations are analytic maps of some one-dimensional parameter, and the system is not degenerate. In that case, ESC holds out of a zero-dimensional set of exceptional parameters. In particular, for fixed contraction ratios with
\max_{i\neq j}\{|r_{i}|+|r_{j}|\}<1
, the dimension of translation vectors
(c_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for which
\mathcal{I}
does not satisfy ESC, is at most
N-1
, see
[
4
, Corollary 6.8.12]
1
1

1

We note that the assumption
\max_{i\neq j}\{|r_{i}|+|r_{j}|\}<1
is missing incorrectly in the statement.
. Hochman
[
14
]
proved the following groundbreaking result for the dimension theory of self-similar IFSs.

Theorem 1.2
.

[Hochman
[
14
]
]
If the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfies the ESC, then

\dim_{H}(A)=\min\{1,s_{0}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\dim_{H}(\mu)=\min\left\{1,\frac{h_{\mathbf{p}}}{\chi(\mathbf{p})}\right\}.

(1.2)

The ESC implies that the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
has no exact overlap, i.e. there are no finite sequences of symbols
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\neq(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
such that
f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}\equiv f_{j_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{j_{m}}
. When exact overlaps exist, a better bound can be obtained by considering the number of exact overlaps. Lau and Ngai
[
18
]
and Zerner
[
23
]
introduced the Weak Separation Condition (WSC), which allows exact overlaps. Still, the not-exactly overlapping maps must be relatively far from each other, comparable with the contraction ratios of the maps. This allows us to calculate the dimension of the attractor as a limit of the similarity dimensions of higher iterates. In some special cases, when the contraction ratios are reciprocals of integers, it is possible to calculate the dimension of the corresponding self-similar measures, see Ruiz
[
22
]
.

The situation becomes significantly more difficult if the overlapping structure of the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
is complicated, for instance, when some of the maps of the IFS share the same fixed point. Let
(t_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a vector with pairwise different entries. For every
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, let
n_{i}\geq 1
be an integer. For every
j\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}
, let
\lambda_{i,j}\in(0,1)
. Consider the following self-similar IFS on the real line:

\mathcal{S}=\{f_{i,j}(x)=\lambda_{i,j}x+t_{i}(1-\lambda_{i,j})\}_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j\leq n_{i}}.

(1.3)

If
n_{i}\geq 2
for some
i
, then the maps
f_{i,j}
and
f_{i,j^{\prime}}
with
j\neq j^{\prime}
share the same fixed point, which causes many exact overlaps, for example
f_{i,j}\circ f_{i,j^{\prime}}\equiv f_{i,j^{\prime}}\circ f_{i,j}
. Furthermore, if
\frac{\log\lambda_{i,j}}{\log\lambda_{i,j^{\prime}}}\notin\mathbb{Q}
then the IFS
\mathcal{S}
does satisfy the WSC, see Fraser
[
13
]
, and if
\frac{\log\lambda_{i,j}}{\log\lambda_{i,j^{\prime}}}=\frac{p}{q}\in\mathbb{Q}
then it causes further exact overlaps, since composing
f_{i,j^{\prime}}
with itself
p
-times and composing
f_{i,j}
with itself
q
-times result the same map.

The first author has already studied the dimension theory of such systems previously. The first author in
[
1
]
and with Szvák in
[
6
]
studied the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor of the special case
\{\lambda_{1,1}x,\lambda_{1,2}x,\lambda_{2,1}x+1\}
. They showed that if
\lambda_{1,1}+\lambda_{2,1}<1
then for Lebesgue almost every
\lambda_{1,2}\in(0,\lambda_{1,1})
the dimension of the attractor is
\min\{1,s\}
, where
s
is the unique root of the equation

\lambda_{1,1}^{s}+\lambda_{1,2}^{s}-\lambda_{1,1}^{s}\lambda_{1,2}^{s}+\lambda_{2,1}^{s}=1.

Moreover, in
[
6
]
, the first author and Szvák gave a formula for the dimension of self-similar measures corresponding to that IFS for Lebesgue almost every parameters under the technical assumption that
0<\lambda_{1,1},\lambda_{1,2},\lambda_{2,1}<1/9
. Further generalisations can be found in
[
2
,
3
]
.

In this paper, we extend significantly the dimension result for such systems. For simplicity, let us introduce the following notations: let
L=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}
be the number of maps in
\mathcal{S}
, let
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j\leq n_{i}}
be the
L
-dimensional vector formed by the contraction ratios, and let
\underline{t}=(t_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
be the vector formed by the fixed points. To emphasize the dependence on the the parameters, we may write
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
for the system defined in (
1.3
),
A_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
for the attractor of
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
and
\mu_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}^{\mathbf{p}}
for the self-similar measure corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
and probability vector
\mathbf{p}
. Our main result on the dimension of self-similar measures is the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
be a self-similar IFS as in (
1.3
). There exists a set
\mathbf{E}\subset(0,1)^{L}
with
\dim_{H}(\mathbf{E})\leq L-1
such that for every
\underline{\lambda}\in(0,1)^{L}\setminus\mathbf{E}
there exists a set
\mathbf{F}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\dim_{H}(\mathbf{F})\leq N-1
such that for every
\underline{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathbf{F}
the following holds: for every probability vector
\mathbf{p}=(p_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j\leq n_{i}}

\dim_{H}(\mu_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}^{\mathbf{p}})=\min\bigg\{1,\frac{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,j}\log(p_{i,j})+\Phi(\mathbf{p})}{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,j}\log(\lambda_{i,j})}\bigg\},

where

\Phi(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{l}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{q=0}^{k}\binom{k}{q}(p_{l,m})^{q+1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\right)^{k-q}\bigg(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\bigg)\log\left(\frac{q+1}{k+1}\right).

(1.4)

This result significantly strengthens the previous result of the first author and Szvák
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
. Although the expression
\Phi(\mathbf{p})
is tedious, it has a simple heuristic. It measures the proportion of the symbol that appears first in the first block, which is formed by the maps sharing the same fixed point. Namely, suppose
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
are independent and identically distributed random variables such that
\mathbb{P}(X_{r}=(i,j))=p_{i,j}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{i}\}
. Let
k_{i,j}:=\min\{r\geq 1:X_{r}\notin\{(i,1),\ldots,(i,n_{i})\}\}
, and let
Y_{i,j}:=\#\{1\leq r\leq k_{i,j}-1:X_{r}=(i,j)\}
for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{i}\}.
Then, the quantity
\Phi(\mathbf{p})
can be written in the form

\Phi(\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{Y_{X_{1}}}{k_{X_{1}}-1}\bigg)\bigg).

Next, we state a similar result for the dimension of the attractor of
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
be a self-similar IFS as in (
1.3
). There exists a set
\mathbf{E}\subset(0,1)^{L}
with
\dim_{H}(\mathbf{E})\leq L-1
such that for every
\underline{\lambda}\in(0,1)^{L}\setminus\mathbf{E}
there exists a set
\mathbf{F}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\dim_{H}(\mathbf{F})\leq N-1
such that for every
\underline{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathbf{F}
,

\dim_{H}(A_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}})=\min\{1,s_{0}\},

where
A_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
is the attractor of
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
and
s_{0}
is the unique solution of the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}(1-(\lambda_{i,j})^{s_{0}})=N-1.

(1.5)

This significantly strengthens the previous results in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
]
.

2.
Applications and discussion

2.1.
Typical self-similar systems

Before we prove our main result, Theorem
1.3
, we show some applications of it. First, we show that the assumption
\max_{i\neq j}\{|r_{i}|+|r_{j}|\}<1
in
[
4
, Corollary 6.8.12]
can be strengthened.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{f_{i}(x)=r_{i}x+c_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a self-similar IFS. Then there exists a set
\mathbf{E}\subset(0,1)^{N}
with
\dim_{H}(\mathbf{E})\leq N-1
such that for every
(r_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in(0,1)^{N}\setminus\mathbf{E}
there exists a set
\mathbf{F}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\dim_{H}(\mathbf{F})\leq N-1
such that for every
(c_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathbf{F}
the following holds: for every self-similar measure
\mu
corresponding to the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
and the probability vector
\mathbf{p}=(p_{i})_{i=1}^{N}

\dim_{H}(\mu)=\min\bigg\{1,\frac{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\log(p_{i})}{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\log(r_{i})}\bigg\}.

Proof.

Since
n_{i}=1
for every
i=1,\ldots,N
, it is easy to see that the expression
\Phi(\mathbf{p})
in Theorem
1.3
equals
0
. Thus, the claim follows.
∎

2.2.
Estimating the
\Phi(\mathbf{p})

Now, we will establish a bound for
\Phi(\mathbf{p})
, which might be helpful in estimating from below the Hausdorff dimension of self-similar measures with common fixed point structure.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{p}=(p_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j\leq n_{i}}
be a probability vector and let
\Phi(\mathbf{p})
be defined as in (
1.4
). Then

\Phi(\mathbf{p})\geq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,{m}}\log\bigg(p_{l,{m}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq l\\
i=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,j}\bigg).

Proof.

Let us recall the representation of
\Phi(\mathbf{p})
from Section
1
. Suppose
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
is i.i.d. such that
\mathbb{P}(X_{r}=(i,j))=p_{i,j}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{i}\}
. Let
k_{i,j}:=\min\{r\geq 1:X_{r}\notin\{(i,1),\ldots,(i,n_{i})\}\},
and let
Y_{i,j}:=\#\{1\leq r\leq k_{i}-1:X_{r}=(i,j)\}
for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{i}\}.
Then, the quantity
\Phi(\mathbf{p})
can be written in the following form

\Phi(\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{Y_{X_{1}}}{k_{X_{1}}-1}\bigg)\bigg)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,{m}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{Y_{l,m}}{k_{l,m}-1}\bigg)\bigg|X_{1}=(l,m)\bigg),

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{Y_{l,m}}{k_{l,m}-1}\bigg)\bigg|X_{1}=(l,m)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{q=0}^{k}\binom{k}{q}p_{l,m}^{q}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\right)^{k-q}\bigg(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l_{j}}\bigg)\log\bigg(\frac{q+1}{k+1}\bigg).

Clearly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{Y_{l,m}}{k_{l,m}-1}\bigg)\bigg|X_{1}=(l,m)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg(-\log\bigg(\frac{k_{l,m}-1}{Y_{l,m}}\bigg)\bigg|X_{1}=(l,m)\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq-\log\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{k_{l,m}-1}{Y_{l,m}}\bigg|X_{1}=(l,m)\bigg),

where we used Jensen’s inequality. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{k_{l}-1}{Y_{X_{r}}}|X_{1}=l_{m}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{q=0}^{k}\binom{k}{q}p_{l,m}^{q}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\right)^{k-q}\bigg(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\bigg)\frac{k+1}{q+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{q=0}^{k}\binom{k+1}{q+1}p_{l,m}^{q}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\right)^{k-q}\bigg(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\bigg)\frac{1}{p_{l,m}}\left(\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l_{j}}\bigg)^{k}-\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{n_{l}}p_{l_{j}}\bigg)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l_{j}}\bigg)\frac{1}{p_{l,m}}\bigg(\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}}{1-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}}-\frac{\sum_{j=1,j\neq m}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}}{1-\sum_{j=1,j\neq m}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{n_{l}}p_{l,j}\right)^{-1}=\left(p_{l,{m}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq l\\
i=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,j}\right)^{-1}

Thus, from the above, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{Y_{l,m}}{k_{l,m}-1}\bigg)\bigg|X_{1}=(l,m))\bigg)\geq\log\bigg(p_{l,{m}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq l\\
i=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,j}\bigg).

This implies that

\Phi(\mathbf{p})\geq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{l}}p_{l,{m}}\log\bigg(p_{l,{m}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq l\\
i=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,j}\bigg).

This completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Generalised 4-corner set

Using Theorem
1.3
, we can also calculate the Hausdorff dimension of self-affine measures supported on the generalised
4
-corner set. Let

\begin{array}[]{cc}F_{1}(x,y)=(\gamma_{1,1}x,\lambda_{1,1}y),&F_{2}(x,y)=(\gamma_{1,2}x,\lambda_{2,1}y+(1-\lambda_{2,1})),\\
F_{3}(x,y)=(\gamma_{2,1}x+(1-\gamma_{2,1}),\lambda_{1,2}y),&F_{4}(x,y)=(\gamma_{2,2}x+(1-\gamma_{2,2}),\lambda_{2,2}y+(1-\lambda_{2,2}))\end{array}

(2.1)

be a self-affine IFS on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}0<\gamma_{i,j},\lambda_{i,j}\text{ for }i=1,2,j=1,2,\\
\gamma_{1,1}+\gamma_{2,1}\leq 1,\ \gamma_{1,2}+\gamma_{2,2}\leq 1,\ \lambda_{1,1}+\lambda_{2,1}\leq 1,\ \lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{2,2}\leq 1,\\
\min\{\gamma_{1,2}+\gamma_{2,1},\lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{2,1}\}\leq 1\text{ and }\min\{\gamma_{1,1}+\gamma_{2,2},\lambda_{1,1}+\lambda_{2,2}\}\leq 1.\end{array}\right\}

(2.2)

The conditions in (
2.2
) guarantee that the IFS
\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4}\}
satisfies the rectangular open set condition. In particular,
F_{i}((0,1)^{2})\subset(0,1)^{2}
and
F_{i}((0,1)^{2})\cap F_{j}((0,1)^{2})=\emptyset
for every
i\neq j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. We call the attractor
C=\bigcup_{i=1}^{4}F_{i}(C)
of
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{4}\}
the generalized 4-corner set. In
[
3
]
, the first author studied the box-counting dimension of the 4-corner set for Lebesgue typical parameters. Now, we determine the Hausdorff dimension of self-affine measures supported on
C
for typical parameters in a certain sense, and the Hausdorff dimension of the generalised 4-corner set in a region of parameters.

Let
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{4})
be a probability vector, and let

\displaystyle\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=-p_{1}\log\gamma_{1,1}-p_{2}\log\gamma_{1,2}-p_{3}\log\gamma_{2,1}-p_{4}\log\gamma_{2,2},

\displaystyle\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=-p_{1}\log\lambda_{1,1}-p_{2}\log\lambda_{2,1}-p_{3}\log\lambda_{1,2}-p_{4}\log\lambda_{2,2}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4}\}
be a self-affine IFS as in (
2.1
). There exist sets
\mathbf{E}_{x},\mathbf{E}_{y}\subset(0,1)^{4}
with
\dim_{H}(\mathbf{E}_{x}),\dim_{H}(\mathbf{E}_{y})\leq 3
such that for every
(\gamma_{i,j})_{i=1,j=1}^{2,2}\in(0,1)^{4}\setminus\mathbf{E}_{x}
and
(\lambda_{i,j})_{i=1,j=1}^{2,2}\in(0,1)^{4}\setminus\mathbf{E}_{y}
with (
2.2
) the following holds: for every self-affine measure
\mu
corresponding to the IFS
\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4}\}
and the probability vector
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})

\dim_{H}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{h_{\mathbf{p}}+\Phi_{x}(\mathbf{p})}{\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p})}-\dfrac{\Phi_{x}(\mathbf{p})}{\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p})}&\text{if }\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p})\geq\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p})\geq h_{\mathbf{p}}+\Phi_{x}(\mathbf{p})\\[10.0pt]
1+\dfrac{h_{\mathbf{p}}-\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p})}{\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p})}&\text{if }\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p}),h_{\mathbf{p}}+\Phi_{x}(\mathbf{p})\geq\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p}),\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{h_{\mathbf{p}}+\Phi_{y}(\mathbf{p})}{\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p})}-\dfrac{\Phi_{y}(\mathbf{p})}{\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p})}&\text{if }\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p})\geq\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p})\geq h_{\mathbf{p}}+\Phi_{y}(\mathbf{p}),\\[10.0pt]
1+\dfrac{h_{\mathbf{p}}-\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p})}{\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p})}&\text{if }\chi_{x}(\mathbf{p}),h_{\mathbf{p}}+\Phi_{y}(\mathbf{p})\geq\chi_{y}(\mathbf{p}),\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{k}\binom{k}{q}(p_{3}+p_{4})\left(p_{1}^{q+1}p_{2}^{k-q}+p_{2}^{q+1}p_{1}^{k-q}\right)\log(\frac{q+1}{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{k}\binom{k}{q}(p_{1}+p_{2})\left(p_{3}^{q+1}p_{4}^{k-q}+p_{4}^{q+1}p_{3}^{k-q}\right)\log(\frac{q+1}{k+1}),

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{y}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{k}\binom{k}{q}(p_{2}+p_{4})\left(p_{1}^{q+1}p_{3}^{k-q}+p_{3}^{q+1}p_{1}^{k-q}\right)\log(\frac{q+1}{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{k}\binom{k}{q}(p_{1}+p_{3})\left(p_{2}^{q+1}p_{4}^{k-q}+p_{4}^{q+1}p_{2}^{k-q}\right)\log(\frac{q+1}{k+1}).

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
1.3
and Feng and Hu’s theorem
[
12

.
∎

Theorem

\lambda_{1,1}\leq\gamma_{1,1},\ \lambda_{2,2}\leq\gamma_{2,2},\ \lambda_{1,2}\leq\gamma_{2,1}\text{ and }\lambda_{2,1}\leq\gamma_{1,2}.

3.
Preliminaries

Before we turn to the proof of our main results, we need to introduce some notations and make some preliminary statements.

3.1.
Symbolic space

Let
\Sigma:=\{1,2,\dots,N\}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of all infinite sequences with symbols from
\{1,2,\dots,N\}
. The set
\Sigma
is the symbolic space corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{I}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\}
. Let
\mathbf{i}=i_{1}i_{2}\dots\in\Sigma
. We define
\mathbf{i}_{|_{m}}:=i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote the set of all finite sequences of length
n
with symbols from
\{1,2,\dots,N\}
by
\Sigma_{n}
. Set
\Sigma^{*}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Sigma_{n}.
The notation
|\mathbf{i}|
denotes the length of the finite sequence
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma^{*}
. For a symbol
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and a finite word
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma^{*}
, let
\#_{i}\mathbf{i}
be the number of the appearances of the symbol
i
in the word
\mathbf{i}
.

If
\mathbf{i}=i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{n}\in\Sigma^{*}
and
\mathbf{j}=j_{1}j_{2}\dots\in\Sigma^{*}\cup\Sigma
, then let
\mathbf{i}*\mathbf{j}=i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{n}j_{1}j_{2}\dots
be the concatenation of
\mathbf{i}
and
\mathbf{j}.
The symbolic space
\Sigma
equipped with metric
\rho
is a compact metric space, where the metric
\rho
is defined as follows

\rho(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})=2^{-|\mathbf{i}\wedge\mathbf{j}|}

for
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma
, where
\mathbf{i}\wedge\mathbf{j}
denotes the initial largest common segment of
\mathbf{i}
and
\mathbf{j}
. For
\mathbf{i}=i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{n}\in\Sigma^{*},
let
f_{\mathbf{i}}=f_{i_{1}}\circ f_{i_{2}}\circ\dots\circ f_{i_{n}}
and
r_{\mathbf{i}}=r_{i_{1}}r_{i_{2}}\dots r_{i_{n}}.
The mapping
\Pi:\Sigma\to A
, defined by

\Pi(\mathbf{i})=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{i_{1}}\circ f_{i_{2}}\circ\dots\circ f_{i_{n}}(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{\mathbf{i}_{|_{n}}}(0),

is called the natural projection corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{I}.
For
\mathbf{i}=i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{n}\in\Sigma^{*},
set
\Pi(\mathbf{i}):=f_{i_{1}}\circ f_{i_{2}}\circ\dots\circ f_{i_{n}}(0).

3.2.
Random walk entropy

In this section, we provide a more detailed description of the method by Hochman
[
14
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\nu)
be a probability space and
\xi=\{C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{m}\}
be a measurable partition, i.e.
\cup_{i=1}C_{i}=X
and
C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
. Then, the
Shannon entropy

H
of the measure
\nu
with respect to the partition
\xi
is defined as follows

H(\nu,\xi)=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nu(C_{i})\log(\nu(C_{i})).

The self-similar IFS
\mathcal{I}
with the probability vector
\mathbf{p}
is known as a probabilistic self-similar IFS. We denote the probabilistic self-similar IFS by
(\mathcal{I},\mathbf{p}).

Definition 3.2
.

The
random walk entropy
of the self-similar measure
\mu
corresponding to the probabilistic self-similar IFS
(\mathcal{I},\mathbf{p})
is defined as

h_{RW}(\mu):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_{n}}{n},

where
H_{n}
is the Shannon entropy of the measure
\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}}p_{\mathbf{i}}{\delta}_{f_{\mathbf{i}}},
which is as follows

H_{n}=-\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{n}\\
f_{\mathbf{i}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}\end{subarray}}p_{\mathbf{j}}\bigg).

The random walk entropy provides a more sophisticated upper bound on the dimension of the self-similar measures if exact overlaps occur. In particular, for every self-similar measure
\mu
corresponding to the probabilistic self-similar IFS
(\mathcal{I},\mathbf{p})
,

\dim_{H}(\mu)\leq\min\bigg\{1,\frac{h_{RW}(\mu)}{\chi(\mathbf{p})}\bigg\},

see
[
5
, Theorem 3.2.7]
.

We now consider some of the following notation from Hochman’s work
[
14
]
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N},

\mathcal{D}_{n}
denote the partition of
\mathbb{R}
into intervals of length
2^{-n},
i.e.

\mathcal{D}_{n}=\bigg\{\bigg[\frac{k}{2^{n}},\frac{k+1}{2^{n}}\bigg):k\in\mathbb{Z}\bigg\}.

The notation
H(\nu,\mathcal{D}_{n})
denotes the Shannon entropy of the probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
with respect to the partition
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. For
m<n
,
H(\nu,\mathcal{D}_{n}|\mathcal{D}_{m})
denotes the conditional entropy and is defined as
H(\nu,\mathcal{D}_{n}|\mathcal{D}_{m}):=H(\nu,\mathcal{D}_{n})-H(\nu,\mathcal{D}_{m}).

The probability measure
\tilde{\nu}^{(n)}
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\tilde{\nu}^{(n)}=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in I^{n}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\delta_{(f_{\mathbf{i}}(0),r_{\mathbf{i}})},

where
\delta_{x}
denotes the Dirac measure on the point
x
. We denote
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{n}=\mathcal{D}_{n}\times\mathcal{F}
is a partition on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathcal{F}
is the partition of
\mathbb{R}
into points.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
14
]

Let
\mu
be the self-similar measure, and the probability measure
\tilde{\nu}^{(n)}
be defined as above. If
\dim_{H}(\mu)<1
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}H(\tilde{\nu}^{(n)},\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{qn^{\prime}}|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{n^{\prime}})=0\quad\forall\quad q>1,

where
n^{\prime}=\lfloor n\log(\frac{1}{r})\rfloor
and
r=\prod_{i=1}^{N}{r_{i}}^{p_{i}}.

Definition 3.4
.

We say that IFS
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the
weak exponential separation condition (WESC)
, if there exists a sequence
\{n_{k}\}
and

\exists~b>1
such that
n_{k}\to\infty
and
\forall~\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}
, we have the following:

\text{either}~r_{\mathbf{i}}\neq r_{\mathbf{j}}

\text{or}~r_{\mathbf{i}}=r_{\mathbf{j}}~\&~f_{\mathbf{i}}(0)=f_{\mathbf{j}}(0)\iff f_{\mathbf{i}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}

\text{or}~r_{\mathbf{i}}=r_{\mathbf{j}}~\&~|f_{\mathbf{i}}(0)-f_{\mathbf{j}}(0)|>2^{-n_{k}^{\prime}b}.

The following result is an extension of Hochmann’s dimension result and was proved by Breuillard and Varjú
[
7
]
for the case of homogeneous contraction ratios.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mu
be a self-similar measure corresponding to the probabilistic self-similar IFS
(\mathcal{I},\mathbf{p})
. If IFS
\mathcal{I}
satisfy WESC, then

\dim_{H}(\mu)=\min\bigg\{1,\frac{h_{RW}(\mu)}{\chi(\mathbf{p})}\bigg\},

where
h_{RW}(\mu)
is the random walk entropy for the self-similar measure
\mu
.

We provide the proof for completeness for Theorem
3.5
following Breuillard and Varjú
[
7
]
.

Proof.

Let
\dim_{H}(\mu)=1
. Since
\dim_{H}(\mu)\leq\frac{h_{RW}(\mu)}{\chi(\mathbf{p})}
, we get the required result. Now, we assume that
\dim_{H}(\mu)<1.
By Theorem
3.3
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}H(\tilde{\nu}^{(n)},\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{qn^{\prime}}|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{n^{\prime}})=0\quad\forall\quad q>1,

where
n^{\prime}=\lfloor n\log(\frac{1}{r})\rfloor
and
r=\prod_{i=1}^{N}{r_{i}}^{p_{i}}
. Similarly to Hochman
[
14
]
, since
\mu
is exact dimensional, we get that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}H(\tilde{\nu}^{(n)},\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{n^{\prime}})=\dim_{H}(\mu).

Thus, for proving our result, it remains only to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}H(\tilde{\nu}^{(n)},\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{qn^{\prime}})\geq\frac{h_{RW}}{\chi(\mathbf{p})}

(3.1)

for some
q>1.

Now, we will prove the equality (
3.1
). Let
q=b
from Definition
3.4
. For each
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\tilde{\nu}^{(n)}\bigg(\bigg[\frac{k}{2^{bn^{\prime}}},\frac{k+1}{2^{bn^{\prime}}}\bigg)\times\{a\}\bigg)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{j}}\in\Sigma_{n}\\
r_{\mathbf{j}}=a\end{subarray}}p_{\mathbf{j}}\delta_{f_{\mathbf{j}}(0)}\bigg[\frac{k}{2^{bn^{\prime}}},\frac{k+1}{2^{bn^{\prime}}}\bigg).

Thus, the Shannon entropy of the probability measure
\tilde{\nu}^{(n_{k})}
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
with respect to the partition
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{bn_{k}^{\prime}}
is

\displaystyle H(\tilde{\nu}^{(n_{k})},\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{bn_{k}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{R}\\
\exists~r_{\mathbf{i}}=a\end{subarray}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\nu}^{(n_{k})}\bigg(\bigg[\frac{l}{2^{bn_{k}^{\prime}}},\frac{l+1}{2^{bn_{k}^{\prime}}}\bigg)\times\{a\}\bigg)\log\bigg(\tilde{\nu}^{(n_{k})}\bigg(\bigg[\frac{l}{2^{bn_{k}^{\prime}}},\frac{l+1}{2^{bn_{k}^{\prime}}}\bigg)\times\{a\}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{R}\\
\exists~r_{\mathbf{i}}=a\end{subarray}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{j}}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}\\
r_{\mathbf{j}}=a\end{subarray}}p_{\mathbf{j}}\delta_{f_{\mathbf{j}}(0)}\bigg[\frac{l}{2^{bn_{k}^{\prime}}},\frac{l+1}{2^{bn_{k}^{\prime}}}\bigg)\bigg)\log\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{j}}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}\\
r_{\mathbf{j}}=a\end{subarray}}p_{\mathbf{j}}\delta_{f_{\mathbf{j}}(0)}\bigg[\frac{l}{2^{bn_{k}^{\prime}}},\frac{l+1}{2^{bn_{k}^{\prime}}}\bigg)\bigg).

Since the IFS
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the WESC, we have

\displaystyle H(\tilde{\nu}^{(n_{k})},\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{bn_{k}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{R}\\
\exists~r_{\mathbf{i}}=a\end{subarray}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{j}}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}\\
f_{\mathbf{i}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}\end{subarray}}p_{\mathbf{j}}\bigg)\log\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{j}}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}\\
f_{\mathbf{i}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}\end{subarray}}p_{\mathbf{j}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{{\mathbf{i}}\in I^{n_{k}}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{j}}\in\Sigma_{n_{k}}\\
f_{\mathbf{i}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}\end{subarray}}p_{\mathbf{j}}\bigg).

This implies that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}^{\prime}}H(\tilde{\nu}^{(n_{k})},\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{bn_{k}^{\prime}})\geq\frac{h_{RW}(\mu)}{\chi(\mathbf{p})},

which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Exponential separation condition for common fixed point system

4.1.
Technical lemmas

We begin by discussing some general results for later purposes, which will be particularly useful in describing the dimension theory of self-similar IFS with a common fixed point structure.

Let us consider a self-similar IFS
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{F}:=\{f_{i}(x)=\lambda_{i}x\}_{i=1}^{M_{1}}\cup\{f_{i}(x)=\lambda_{i}x+c_{i}\}_{i=M_{1}+1}^{M_{2}},

(4.1)

where
M_{2}>M_{1}\geq 2
. Suppose that there exist
\epsilon,A>0
such that

(1)

\lambda_{i}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]~\forall~i\in\{1,2,\dots,M_{1}\}
and
|\lambda_{i}|\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]~\forall~i\in\{M_{1}+1,2,\dots,M_{2}\}
,

(2)

f_{i}[0,A]\subseteq[\epsilon,A]~\forall~i\in\{M_{1}+1,\dots,M_{2}\}
.

Let
\Sigma=\{1,\ldots,M_{2}\}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the symbolic space and let
\Pi
be the natural projection corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{F}
.

For a
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma
, we define the “first block”
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}
of
\mathbf{i}
as follows: if
i_{1}\geq M_{1}+1
then
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}=i_{1}
. Otherwise, let
|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|:=\min\{k\geq 1:i_{k}\geq M_{1}+1\}-1
, and let
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}:=\mathbf{i}_{|_{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|}}
.

We say that for
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma
, the first blocks are disjoint if the sets formed by the symbols in the first blocks
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}
and
b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}
are disjoint. We denote it by
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\cap b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}=\emptyset
. In other words,
\min\{\#_{i}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}},\#_{i}b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\}=0
for every
1\leq i\leq M_{2}
.

In the next two lemmas, we will follow and modify techniques of Kamalutdinov and Tetenov
[
17
]
.

We define a set as follows

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\bigg\{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\Sigma\times\Sigma:1\leq i_{1},j_{1}\leq M_{1}~\&~b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\cap b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}=\emptyset~\&~\frac{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}}{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}}\notin\bigg(\frac{\epsilon}{2A},\frac{2A}{\epsilon}\bigg)~\&~b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\neq\mathbf{i},b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\neq\mathbf{j}\bigg\}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the self-similar IFS of the form (
4.1
) with natural projection
\Pi
. For every
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{M_{2}}
satisfying (
1
)–(
2
) and for every pair
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
,

|\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j})|\geq{\epsilon}^{2\max\{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|,|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|\}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\frac{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}}{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}}>\frac{2A}{\epsilon},
the other case can be proven similarly. Since
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\neq\mathbf{i},b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\neq\mathbf{j}
and
f_{i}[0,A]\subseteq[\epsilon,A]~\forall~i\in\{M_{1}+1,\dots,M_{2}\}
, we have

\Pi(\mathbf{i})\in f_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}([\epsilon,A])~~\text{and}~~\Pi(\mathbf{j})\in f_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}([\epsilon,A]).

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j})=\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}x-\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}y

for some
x,y\in[\epsilon,A]
. This implies that

\displaystyle\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\epsilon-\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}A=\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\epsilon\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}A}{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\epsilon}\bigg)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\geq{\epsilon}^{2|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|}.

∎

Let
N_{0}=\lceil\frac{(1-\epsilon)(2A+\epsilon)}{\epsilon^{3}}\rceil+1.
We define a set as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}=\bigg\{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\Sigma\times\Sigma:1\leq i_{1},j_{1}\leq M_{1}~\&~b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\cap b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}=\emptyset~\&~\frac{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}}{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}}\in\bigg(\frac{\epsilon}{2A},\frac{2A}{\epsilon}\bigg)~\&~b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\neq\mathbf{i},b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\neq\mathbf{j}

\displaystyle~\&~\max_{1\leq k\leq M_{1}}\bigg\{\max\{\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}},\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\}\bigg\}>N_{0}\bigg\}

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the self-similar IFS of the form (
4.1
) with natural projection
\Pi
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{M_{2}}
satisfying (
1
)–(
2
) and for every
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}_{2}

\bigg|\frac{\partial(\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j}))}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\bigg|\geq C{\epsilon}^{\max\{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|,|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|\}},

where
k
is so that
\max\{\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}},\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\}>N_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
. For simplicity, let
m_{k}=\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}
and
n_{k}=\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
m_{r}>N_{0}
for some
r\in\{1,2\dots,M_{1}\}
. By definition,
n_{r}=0
. Let
\lambda_{r}^{\prime}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]
be such that
\lambda_{r}<\lambda_{r}^{\prime}
and

\frac{\epsilon}{2A}\leq\frac{(\lambda_{r}^{\prime})^{m_{r}}\prod_{i=1,\ i\neq r}^{M_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{m_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{M_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{n_{i}}}\leq\frac{2A}{\epsilon}.

Let
\delta=\lambda_{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{r}>0
. Then, by using the mean value theorem, we have

m_{r}\lambda_{r}^{m_{r}-1}\leq\frac{(\lambda_{r}^{\prime})^{m_{r}}-\lambda_{r}^{m_{r}}}{\lambda_{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{r}}=\frac{|(\lambda_{r}^{\prime})^{m_{r}}-\lambda_{r}^{m_{r}}|}{\delta}\leq m_{r}(\lambda_{r}^{\prime})^{m_{r}-1}.

Now, we define another self-similar IFS

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{\mathcal{F}\setminus\{f_{r}(x)=\lambda_{r}x\}\}\cup\{f^{\prime}_{r}(x)=\lambda_{r}^{\prime}x\}.

The IFS
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is just obtained from the IFS
\mathcal{F}
by replacing the map
f_{r}
by
f_{r}^{\prime}
. Let
\Pi^{\prime}
be the natural projection corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Thus, by the displacement lemma by Kamalutdinov and Tetenov
[
17
, Theorem 15]
, we have

|\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{i})|\leq\frac{\delta}{\epsilon}\quad\forall\quad\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma.

(4.2)

For the given pair
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\Sigma\times\Sigma
, we have

\Pi(\mathbf{i})=f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi(\hat{\mathbf{i}})),~~\Pi(\mathbf{j})=f_{1}^{n_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{n_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{j}}(\Pi(\hat{\mathbf{j}}))

\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{i})=f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{r-1}^{m_{r-1}}\circ{f}_{{r}}^{\prime m_{r}}\circ f_{{r+1}}^{m_{r+1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}})),

\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{j})=f_{1}^{n_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{n_{l}}(f_{\tilde{j}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{j}})),

for some
\tilde{i},\tilde{j}\in\{M_{1}+1,\dots,M_{2}\}
and
\hat{\mathbf{i}},\hat{\mathbf{j}}\in\Sigma.
Now, we will estimate the expression
|(\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j}))-(\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{i})-\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{j}))|
. We have

\displaystyle|(\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j}))-(\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{i})-\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{j}))|

\displaystyle\quad=|\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{i})+\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{j})-\Pi(\mathbf{j})|

\displaystyle\quad\geq\left|f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))-f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{r-1}^{m_{r-1}}\circ{f}_{{r}}^{\prime m_{r}}\circ f_{{r+1}}^{m_{r+1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))\right|

\displaystyle\quad\quad-|\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{j})-\Pi(\mathbf{j})|

\displaystyle\quad\geq\left|f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))-f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{r-1}^{m_{r-1}}\circ{f}_{{r}}^{\prime m_{r}}\circ f_{{r+1}}^{m_{r+1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))\right|

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\left|f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))-f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))\right|

\displaystyle\quad\quad-|\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{j})-\Pi(\mathbf{j})|

\displaystyle\quad=:L_{1}-L_{2}-L_{3}.

To estimate the above expression, we need to individually estimate the values of
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
, and
L_{3}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=\left|f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))-f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{r-1}^{m_{r-1}}\circ{f}_{{r}}^{\prime m_{r}}\circ f_{{r+1}}^{m_{r+1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\lambda_{r}^{\prime}}^{m_{r}}-\lambda_{r}^{m_{r}}\right|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq r\end{subarray}}^{M_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{m_{i}}\left|f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))\right|\geq m_{r}\lambda_{r}^{-1}\delta\prod_{i=1}^{M_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{m_{i}}\epsilon\geq\frac{N_{0}\delta\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))-f_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{m_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{i}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}|\Pi(\hat{\mathbf{i}})-\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{i}})|\leq\frac{\delta}{\epsilon}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}},

where we used (
4.2
). Moreover,

\displaystyle L_{3}

\displaystyle=|\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{j})-\Pi(\mathbf{j})|=|f_{1}^{n_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{n_{M_{1}}}(f_{\tilde{j}}(\Pi(\hat{\mathbf{j}}))-f_{1}^{n_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{M_{1}}^{n_{M_{1}}}f_{\tilde{j}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{j}}))|

\displaystyle=\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}|\lambda_{\tilde{j}}||\Pi(\hat{\mathbf{j}})-\Pi^{\prime}(\hat{\mathbf{j}})|\leq\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}(1-\epsilon)\frac{\delta}{\epsilon},

where we used the estimate (
4.2
) again. Thus, we get

\displaystyle L_{1}-L_{2}\geq\bigg(\frac{N_{0}\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\bigg)\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\delta\geq\frac{\epsilon\delta}{2A}\bigg(\frac{N_{0}\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\bigg)\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}.

Thus, by using the above estimates, we get

\displaystyle L_{1}-L_{2}-L_{3}\geq\bigg(\frac{N_{0}\epsilon^{3}-(1-\epsilon)(2A+\epsilon)}{2A(1-\epsilon)\epsilon}\bigg)\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}\delta\geq\delta\bigg(\frac{N_{0}\epsilon^{3}-(1-\epsilon)(2A+\epsilon)}{2A(1-\epsilon)\epsilon}\bigg)\epsilon^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|}.

Choosing
C=\bigg(\frac{N_{0}\epsilon^{3}-(1-\epsilon)(2A+\epsilon)}{2A(1-\epsilon)\epsilon}\bigg),
and since
N_{0}=\lceil\frac{(1-\epsilon)(2A+\epsilon)}{{\epsilon}^{3}}\rceil+1,
we have
C>0.
This implies that

|(\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j}))-(\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{i})-\Pi^{\prime}(\mathbf{j}))|\geq C{\epsilon}^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|}\delta.

This also implies that

\bigg|\frac{\partial(\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j}))}{\partial{\lambda_{r}}}\bigg|\geq C{\epsilon}^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|}\geq C{\epsilon}^{\max\{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|,|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|\}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Block structure

Now, we give a more detailed description of the coding of IFS
\mathcal{S}
with common fixed point structure defined in (
1.3
). Let us recall here the definition. Let
N\geq 2
be an integer, and for every
1\leq i\leq N
, let
n_{i}\geq 1
be an integer. Let

\mathcal{S}=\{f_{i,j}=\lambda_{i,j}x+t_{i}(1-\lambda_{i,j}):1\leq i\leq N~\&~1\leq j\leq n_{i}\},

(4.3)

where
\lambda_{i,j}\in(0,1)
and
t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{N}.

Let
I=\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq N,\ 1\leq j\leq n_{i}\}
and let
\Sigma=I^{\mathbb{N}}
be the symbolic space corresponding to IFS
\mathcal{S}
. Denote
\Sigma_{n}
the set
n
-length finite sequences formed by the symbols of
I
, and let
\Sigma^{*}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\Sigma_{n}
. Denote the length of
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma^{*}
by
|\mathbf{i}|
. We will use the convention that
|\mathbf{i}|=\infty
for
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma
. Denote
\sigma\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
the usual left-shift operator. We extend
\sigma
to
\Sigma^{*}
by
\sigma(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})=(i_{2},\ldots,i_{n})
.

For each
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{*}
, we define the following unique block representation: For
\mathbf{i}=((i_{1},j_{1}),(i_{2},j_{2}),\dots)
, let
|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|:=\max\{k\geq 1:i_{1}=i_{\ell}\text{ for all }1\leq\ell\leq k\}
. If
|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|=|\mathbf{i}|
then let
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}:=\mathbf{i}
, otherwise let
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}:=\mathbf{i}_{|_{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|}}
. Then we define by induction. Suppose that
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}},\dots,b_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}
are defined and finite. Then let

|b_{n+1}^{\mathbf{i}}|:=\max\left\{k\geq 1:i_{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+\dots+|b_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}|+1}=i_{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+\dots+|b_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}|+\ell}\text{ for all }1\leq\ell\leq k\right\}.

If
|b_{n+1}^{\mathbf{i}}|=\left|\sigma^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+\dots+|b_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}|}\mathbf{i}\right|
then let
b_{n+1}^{\mathbf{i}}:=\sigma^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+\dots+|b_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}|}\mathbf{i}
, and so,
\mathbf{i}=b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\dots b_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}b_{n+1}^{\mathbf{i}}
. Otherwise, let
b_{n+1}^{\mathbf{i}}:=(\sigma^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+\dots+|b_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}|}\mathbf{i})_{|_{|b_{n+1}^{\mathbf{i}}|}}
. Let
B^{\mathbf{i}}
denote the number of block in
\mathbf{i}
.
For
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma
, the maps in each block
b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}
correspond to the same fixed points. The maps having the same fixed point are commutative. We write
t_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}}
for the common fixed point of the maps in the block
b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}
, which is also the fixed point of the map
f_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}}
. Thus, any
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma
can be written uniquely as countably (but possibly finitely) many finite sequences:

\mathbf{i}=\underbrace{(i_{1},j_{1})\dots(i_{1},j_{l})}_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\underbrace{(i_{l+1},j_{l+1})\dots(i_{l+1},j_{m})}_{b_{2}^{\mathbf{i}}}(i_{m+1},j_{m+1})\dots.

(4.4)

Note that the maps corresponding to the symbols from the same block share the same fixed point.

Let
\Pi:\Sigma\to[t_{1},t_{N}]
be the natural projection corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{S}.
Simple algebraic manipulations show that for every
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma
with
B^{\mathbf{i}}=\infty

\Pi(\mathbf{i})=t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{b_{2}^{\mathbf{i}}}\dots\lambda_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}}\left(t_{b_{l+1}^{\mathbf{i}}}-t_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}}\right),

where
\lambda_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}}
represent the similarity ratio of the map corresponding to the
l^{\rm th}
block in the infinite sequence
\mathbf{i}
. For a finite
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma^{*}
, we will write
\Pi(\mathbf{i})=f_{\mathbf{i}}(0)=\Pi(\mathbf{i}(1,1)(1,1)\cdots)
with a slight abuse of notation. Observe that
\Pi
is an analytic map of
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}
and a linear map of
\underline{t}=(t_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
for every
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{*}
. In particular,
\Pi
is analytic over
\underline{\lambda}\in(-1+\epsilon,1-\epsilon)^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}}
, which fact will be used for later purposes.

For a finite word
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma^{*}
, let
\#_{i,j}\mathbf{i}
denote the number of symbols
(i,j)
appearing in
\mathbf{i}
. For
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{*}
, we say that
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}
have the same block structure if
B^{\mathbf{i}}=B^{\mathbf{j}}
and for every
1\leq l\leq B^{\mathbf{i}}
,
t_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}}=t_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{j}}}
and
\#_{i,j}b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}=\#_{i,j}b_{l}^{\mathbf{j}}
for every
(i,j)\in I
. Note that if
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma^{*}
have the same block structure then
f_{\mathbf{i}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}
.

Now, we define a special form of the WESC adapted to common fixed-point systems.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that IFS
\mathcal{S}
satisfies the
Exponential Separation Condition for the Common Fixed Point System (ESC for CFS)
, if there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
b>1
such that for every
n\geq N
and every
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{n}
with
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}}=\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}
, we have the following:

\text{either}~\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}~\text{have the same block structure or}~|\Pi({\mathbf{i}})-\Pi({\mathbf{j}})|>2^{-bn}.

(4.5)

Let us note that one can relax the definition of the exponential separation for common fixed point systems (ESC for CFS) through a subsequence of natural numbers
n
for which (
4.5
) holds. However, it would require a slight change in the proofs and the definition of the exceptional sets.

One can see that ESC for CFS for the IFS
\mathcal{S}
implies that IFS
\mathcal{S}
satisfies the WESC (see Definition
3.4
). ESC for CFS provides a complete description of the overlapping cylinders via symbolic space, see Section
5
.

We say that the finite words
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma^{*}
are disjoint if
\min\{\#_{i,j}\mathbf{i},\#_{i,j}\mathbf{j}\}=0
for every
(i,j)\in I
. We will denote this with a slight abuse of notation by
\mathbf{j}\cap\mathbf{i}=\emptyset
.

4.3.
Two-fixed point system

Next, we discuss the ESC for CFS in the case of the two fixed-point systems. We consider the following iterated function system, having only two fixed points

\mathcal{J}=\{f_{1,i}(x)=\lambda_{1,i}x\}_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\cup\{f_{2,i}(x)=\lambda_{2,i}x+(1-\lambda_{2,i})\}_{i=1}^{n_{2}}.

(4.6)

Clearly, the IFS
\mathcal{J}
is a special case of the family of IFSs defined in (
4.3
), where
t_{1}=0
and
t_{2}=1
. Let
\Sigma
be the symbolic space corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{J}
with symbols
I=\{(i,j):i\in\{1,2\},j\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}

Denote
L=n_{1}+n_{2}
, and
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}\in(0,1)^{L}
the vector of contraction ratios. Observe that for every
\epsilon>0
such that
\epsilon\leq\min_{(i,j)\in I}\min\{\lambda_{i,j},1-\lambda_{i,j}\}
then
L_{\mathcal{J}}\subseteq[0,1-\epsilon]
and
R_{\mathcal{J}}\subseteq[\epsilon,1]
, where
L_{\mathcal{J}}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{1}}f_{1,i}([0,1])
and
R_{\mathcal{J}}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{2}}f_{2,i}([0,1]).

Let
\Pi
be the natural projection corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{J}
. For
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma
, we define

\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}(\underline{\lambda})=\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j}).

Let us define the following set of pairs:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\bigg\{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\Sigma\times\Sigma:b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\cap b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}=\emptyset~\&~b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\neq\mathbf{i}~\&~b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\neq\mathbf{j}\bigg\}.

(4.7)

We divide the set
\mathcal{B}
further:

\mathcal{B}_{1}:=\bigg\{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{B}:t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\neq t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}\bigg\}\text{ and }\mathcal{B}_{2}:=\mathcal{B}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{1}=\left\{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{B}:t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}=t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}\right\}.

(4.8)

Now, set
\tilde{N_{0}}:=\lceil\frac{(1-\epsilon)(2+\epsilon)}{\epsilon^{3}}\rceil+1
, and divide
\mathcal{B}_{2}
further:

\mathcal{B}_{3}=\bigg\{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{B}_{2}:~\max_{k\in I}\left\{\max\left\{\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}},\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\right\}\right\}\leq\tilde{N_{0}}\bigg\}\text{ and }\mathcal{B}_{4}=\mathcal{B}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{3}.

(4.9)

Observe that
\mathcal{B}_{3}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1}
are compact subsets of
\Sigma\times\Sigma
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
\underline{\lambda}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]^{L}
and for every
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{B}_{4}

\max\left\{|\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}(\underline{\lambda})|,\bigg|\frac{\partial\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}}{\partial\lambda_{k}}(\underline{\lambda})\bigg|\right\}\geq C{\epsilon}^{2\max\{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|,|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|\}},

where
k
is such that
\max\{\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}},\#_{k}b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}\}>\tilde{N_{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{B}_{4}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}=t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}=0
. The case
t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}=t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}=1
can be handled similarly by considering the conjugated IFS with conjugation map
x\mapsto 1-x
.

First, suppose that
\frac{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}}{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}}\notin\bigg(\dfrac{{\epsilon}}{2},\dfrac{2}{{\epsilon}}\bigg)
. Then, by Lemma
4.1
, we get the

|\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}(\underline{\lambda})|\geq{\epsilon}^{2\max\{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|,|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|\}}.

Now, we assume that
\frac{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}}{\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}}\in\bigg(\dfrac{{\epsilon}}{2},\dfrac{2}{{\epsilon}}\bigg)
. Then by Lemma
4.2
, we get that

\bigg|\frac{\partial\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}}{\partial\lambda_{k}}(\underline{\lambda})\bigg|\geq C{\epsilon}^{\max\{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|,|b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}|\}}

for some uniform constant
C>0
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then there exist
p\geq 0
and
\tilde{C}>0
such that for every
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{B}_{3}
and for all
\underline{\lambda}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]^{L}
, there exists
(m_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}\in\mathbb{N}^{L}
such that
m=\sum_{(i,j)\in I}m_{i,j}\leq p
and

\left|\frac{\partial^{m}\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}\partial^{m_{i,j}}\lambda_{i,j}}(\underline{\lambda})\right|>\tilde{C}.

We use the idea of Hochman
[
15
, Proposition 6.24]
to prove this lemma.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{B}_{3}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}=t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}=0
. The case
t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}=t_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}=1
can be handled similarly by considering the conjugated IFS with conjugation map
x\mapsto 1-x
.

First, we show that
\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}(\underline{\lambda})\not\equiv 0
over
\underline{\lambda}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]^{L}
. We have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}(\underline{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\Pi(\mathbf{i})-\Pi(\mathbf{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+1}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{b_{2}^{\mathbf{i}}}\dots\lambda_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}}-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+1}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}\lambda_{b_{2}^{\mathbf{j}}}\dots\lambda_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{j}}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}-\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}+\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+1}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{b_{2}^{\mathbf{i}}}\dots\lambda_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}}-\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+1}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}}\lambda_{b_{2}^{\mathbf{j}}}\dots\lambda_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{j}}}.

Since
b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}\cap b_{1}^{\mathbf{j}}=\emptyset
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|}\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}{\partial}^{\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{i,j}}(\underline{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\prod_{(i,j)\in I}(\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}})!+\sum_{l=2}\frac{{\partial}^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|}((-1)^{l+1}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{b_{2}^{\mathbf{i}}}\dots\lambda_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{i}}})}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}{\partial}^{\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{i,j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{l=2}\frac{{\partial}^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|}((-1)^{l+1}\lambda_{b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{b_{2}^{\mathbf{j}}}\dots\lambda_{b_{l}^{\mathbf{j}}})}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}{\partial}^{\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{i,j}}.

This implies that
\frac{{\partial}^{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|}\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}{\partial}^{\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}}\lambda_{i,j}}(\underline{0})=\prod_{(i,j)\in I}(\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}})!
. Since the function
\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}(\underline{\lambda})
is analytic on
(-1+\frac{\epsilon}{2},1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})^{L}
, we have
\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}(\underline{\lambda})\not\equiv 0
over
\underline{\lambda}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]^{L}
.

Let’s continue the proof using an argument by contradiction. Assume that
\forall~n\geq 1
,
\exists~\underline{\lambda}_{n}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]^{L}
and
\exists~(\mathbf{i}^{n},\mathbf{j}^{n})\in\mathcal{B}_{3}
such that

\bigg|\frac{\partial^{m}\Delta_{\mathbf{i}^{n},\mathbf{j}^{n}}}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}\partial^{m_{i,j}}\lambda_{i,j}}(\underline{\lambda}_{n})\bigg|<\frac{1}{n}\quad\forall~(m_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}\in\mathbb{N}^{L}\text{ with }\sum_{(i,j)\in I}m_{i,j}\leq n.

Due to the compactness of
\mathcal{B}_{3},
there exists a subsequence
\{n_{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
,
\underline{\lambda}_{0}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]^{L}
and
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{B}_{3}
such that

\lim_{l\to\infty}\underline{\lambda}_{n_{l}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0},\quad\lim_{l\to\infty}(\mathbf{i}^{n_{l}},\mathbf{j}^{n_{l}})=(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}).

This also implies that

\frac{\partial^{m}\Delta_{\mathbf{i}^{n_{l}},\mathbf{j}^{n_{l}}}}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}\partial^{m_{i,j}}\lambda_{i,j}}\to\frac{\partial^{m}\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}\partial^{m_{i,j}}\lambda_{i,j}}\quad\text{uniformly on }[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]^{L}

for every
(m_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}\in\mathbb{N}^{L}
. Thus,

\frac{\partial^{m}\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}\partial^{m_{i,j}}\lambda_{i,j}}(\underline{\lambda}_{0})=0\quad\forall~(m_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}\in\mathbb{N}^{L}

Since
\Delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}(\underline{\lambda})
is analytic function, we have

5.
Calculation of
h_{RW}
for general CFS

Recall the self-similar IFS

\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}=\{f_{i,j}=\lambda_{i,j}x+t_{i}(1-\lambda_{i,j}):~(i,j)\in I\}

(5.1)

with a common fixed point structure. The next theorem will provide the formula for the Hausdorff dimension of self-similar measures corresponding to the IFS
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
by calculating the random walk entropy.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
be a self-similar IFS defined in (
5.1
). Suppose that
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
satisfies the ESC for CFS. Let
\mathbf{p}=(p_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}
be a probability vector, and let
\mu
be a self-similar measure corresponding to the probabilistic self-similar IFS
(\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}},\mathbf{p})
. Then,

h_{RW}(\mu)=-\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}\log(p_{i,j})\\
+\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}p_{i,j}^{l}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)^{k-l}\left(1-\sum_{m=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)\log\left(\dfrac{l+1}{k+1}\right).

(5.2)

Proof.

Let us first recall that

H_{n}=-\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{n}\\
f_{\mathbf{i}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}\end{subarray}}p_{\mathbf{j}}\bigg)\text{ and }h_{RW}(\mu)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_{n}}{n}.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{n}
, if
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}}\neq\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}
then
f_{\mathbf{i}}\not\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}
clearly, and if
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}}=\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}
then by ESC for CFS either
\mathbf{i}
and
\mathbf{j}
have the block structure, and in particular,
f_{\mathbf{i}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}}
or
f_{\mathbf{i}}(0)\neq f_{\mathbf{j}}(0)
. Let

\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i})=\{\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{|\mathbf{i}|}:\text{ $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ have the same block structure}\}.

Then

H_{n}=-\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\bigg(\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i})}p_{\mathbf{j}}\bigg).

It is enough to prove that the sequence
H_{n+1}-H_{n}
converges to the right-hand side of (
5.2
). Indeed, if
\lim_{n\to\infty}(H_{n+1}-H_{n})=h
then for each
\epsilon>0
there exists a
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h-\epsilon<H_{n+1}-H_{n}<h+\epsilon
for all
n>N_{0}
. Thus, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle H_{N_{0}+k}-H_{N_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{k}(H_{N_{0}+p}-H_{N_{0}+p-1})<k(h+\epsilon),\text{ and similarly}

\displaystyle H_{N_{0}+k}-H_{N_{0}}

\displaystyle>k(h-\epsilon).

Thus, by squeeze theorem, we have

h-\epsilon\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{H_{N_{0}+k}}{k+N_{0}}=h_{RW}(\mu)\leq h+\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, we get that
h=h_{RW}(\mu)
and the claim of the theorem follows.

Now, we will prove that the sequence
H_{n+1}-H_{n}
converges. First, let us observe the following simple fact: for every
\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma^{*}

\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i})}p_{\mathbf{j}}=p_{\mathbf{i}}\prod_{k=1}^{B^{\mathbf{i}}}\dfrac{|b_{k}^{\mathbf{i}}|!}{\prod_{(i,j)\in I}(\#_{i,j}b_{k}^{\mathbf{i}})!}.

Hence, for every
(i,j)\in I

\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}((i,j)\mathbf{i})}p_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}}{\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i})}p_{\mathbf{i}}}=\begin{cases}p_{i,j}&\text{ if }|b_{1}^{(i,j)\mathbf{i}}|=1,\\[2.0pt]
p_{i,j}\dfrac{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+1}{\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}+1}&\text{ if }|b_{1}^{(i,j)\mathbf{i}}|=1+|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|.\\
\end{cases}

(5.3)

\displaystyle H_{n+1}-H_{n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n+1}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\bigg(\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i})}p_{\mathbf{j}}\bigg)+\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\bigg(\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i})}p_{\mathbf{j}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}}\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\bigg(\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}((i,j)\mathbf{i})}p_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}}{\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{i})}p_{\mathbf{i}}}\bigg)

by (
5.3
)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}\\
|b_{1}^{(i,j)\mathbf{i}}|=1\end{subarray}}p_{i,j}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log p_{i,j}-\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}\\
|b_{1}^{(i,j)\mathbf{i}}|=|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+1\end{subarray}}p_{i,j}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\left(p_{i,j}\dfrac{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+1}{\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}\log p_{i,j}-\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{n}\\
|b_{1}^{(i,j)\mathbf{i}}|=|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+1\end{subarray}}p_{i,j}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\left(\dfrac{|b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}|+1}{\#_{i,j}b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}\log p_{i,j}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}p_{i,j}^{l}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)^{k-l}\left(1-\sum_{m=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)\log\left(\dfrac{l+1}{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}p_{i,j}^{l}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)^{n-l}\log\left(\dfrac{l+1}{n+1}\right).

For the sequence

a_{n}(i,j):=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}p_{i,j}^{l}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)^{n-l}\log\left(\dfrac{l+1}{n+1}\right),

one can easily see that

0\geq a_{n}(i,j)\geq-\log(n+1)\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)^{n}.

Thus,
a_{n}(i,j)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, moreover, the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}(i,j)|
is convergent. Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}H_{n+1}-H_{n}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}\log p_{i,j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{(i,j)\in I}p_{i,j}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}p_{i,j}^{l}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)^{k-l}\left(1-\sum_{m=1}^{n_{i}}p_{i,m}\right)\log\left(\dfrac{l+1}{k+1}\right),

which had to be shown.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The claim follows by combining Theorem
3.5
, Proposition
4.11
and Theorem
5.1
.
∎

6.
Hausdorff dimension of the attractor corresponding to general CFS

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.4
, which is our other main result. First, let us consider the following technical lemma:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
A
be an
n\times n
matrix such that

A_{i,j}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if $i=j$,}\\
x_{j}-1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then

\det(A)=(n-1)(-1)^{n+1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}+(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=1\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}x_{\ell}.

Proof.

Let us argue by induction for
n
. For
n=2
,

\det\begin{pmatrix}-1&x_{2}-1\\
x_{1}-1&-1\end{pmatrix}=(-1)^{2}-(x_{2}-1)(x_{1}-1)=x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{1}x_{2},

hence, the claim holds. Now, suppose that the claim holds for
n
. Then, by a simple row manipulation

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}-1&x_{2}-1&\cdots&x_{n+1}-1\\
x_{1}-1&\ddots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&x_{n+1}-1\\
x_{1}-1&\cdots&x_{n}-1&-1\end{pmatrix}=\det\begin{pmatrix}-x_{1}&x_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
x_{1}-1&-1&x_{3}-1&\cdots&x_{n+1}-1\\
&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&x_{n+1}-1\\
x_{1}-1&\cdots&&x_{n}-1&-1\end{pmatrix}

by the inductive assumption

\displaystyle\quad=-x_{1}\left((n-1)(-1)^{n+1}\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}x_{k}+(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=2\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n+1}x_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt-x_{2}\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}-1&x_{3}-1&\cdots&x_{n+1}-1\\
x_{1}-1&-1&&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&x_{n+1}-1\\
x_{1}-1&\cdots&x_{n}-1&-1\end{pmatrix}

and again by a simple row manipulation

\displaystyle\quad=-x_{1}\left((n-1)(-1)^{n+1}\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}x_{k}+(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=2\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n+1}x_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt-x_{2}\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}-1&x_{3}-1&\cdots&x_{n+1}-1\\
0&-x_{3}&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&-x_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\quad=-x_{1}\left((n-1)(-1)^{n+1}\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}x_{k}+(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=2\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n+1}x_{\ell}\right)-x_{2}(-1)^{n-1}(x_{1}-1)\prod_{k=3}^{n+1}x_{k}

\displaystyle\quad=n(-1)^{n+2}\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}x_{k}+(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=1\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n+1}x_{\ell}+(-1)^{n+1}\prod_{\ell=2}^{n+1}x_{\ell},

which completes the proof.
∎

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
be the self-similar IFS with common fixed point structure defined in (
1.3
)

\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}=\{f_{i,j}(x)=\lambda_{i,j}x+t_{i}(1-\lambda_{i,j}):~1\leq i\leq N~\&~1\leq j\neq n_{i}\}.

We assume that
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
satisfies the ESC for CFS. For every
i=1,\ldots,N
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

T_{i}^{(n)}:=\{(i,j_{1})\cdots(i,j_{n}):1\leq j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{n}\leq n_{i}\}.

and let
\mathcal{V}_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\bigcup_{m=1}^{n}T_{i}^{(m)}
. Clearly,
\mathbf{i}=b_{1}^{\mathbf{i}}
for every
\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
. Let us define a directed graph
\mathcal{G}_{n}=(\mathcal{V}_{n},\mathcal{E}_{n})
with vertices
\mathcal{V}_{n}
and edges

\mathcal{E}_{n}=\{\mathbf{i}\to\mathbf{j}:~t_{\mathbf{i}}\neq t_{\mathbf{j}}\}.

The graph
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is strongly connected, that is, there exists a path (of length at most two) from
\mathbf{i}
to
\mathbf{j}
for any
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
. Furthermore, for
N\geq 3
the graph
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is aperiodic.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define a graph-directed self-similar IFS
\mathcal{S}_{n}=\{f_{\mathbf{j}}:(\mathbf{i}\to\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\}
. By Mauldin and Williams
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
, for every
\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
there exists a unique non-empty compact set
A_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}
such that

A_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{j}:~\mathbf{i}\to\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}}f_{\mathbf{j}}(A_{n}^{\mathbf{j}}).

Clearly,
A_{n}:=\bigcup_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}}A_{n}^{\mathbf{i}}\subseteq A
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
A
is the attractor of
\mathcal{S}
.

We say that the graph-directed self-similar IFS
\mathcal{S}_{n}
satisfies the
exponential separation condition for graph directed system (ESC for GDS)
if there exists
b>0
such that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and every two different paths
\mathbf{i}_{1}\to\cdots\to\mathbf{i}_{m}
and
\mathbf{j}_{1}\to\cdots\to\mathbf{j}_{m}
of length
m
in
\mathcal{G}_{n}
with
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}}=\lambda_{\mathbf{j}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{j}_{m}}
, we have
|f_{\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}}(0)-f_{\mathbf{j}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{j}_{m}}(0)|\geq 2^{-bm}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\underline{\lambda}\notin G=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{1/n}
, where
G_{1/n}
is defined in (
4.11
). If the IFS
\mathcal{S}_{\underline{\lambda},\underline{t}}
satisfies the ESC for CFS, then the graph-directed self-similar IFS
\mathcal{S}_{n}
satisfies the ESC for GDS.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{i}_{1}\to\cdots\to\mathbf{i}_{m}
and
\mathbf{j}_{1}\to\cdots\to\mathbf{j}_{m}
be different allowed paths with
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}}=\lambda_{\mathbf{j}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{j}_{m}}
. Since
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}}=\lambda_{\mathbf{j}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{j}_{m}}
, we have
\#_{i,j}\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}=\#_{i,j}\mathbf{j}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{j}_{m}
for every
(i,j)\in I
, and in particular,
|\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}|=|\mathbf{j}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{j}_{m}|
. Furthermore, since the paths are different
f_{\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}}\not\equiv f_{\mathbf{j}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{j}_{m}}
and the IFS
\mathcal{S}
satisfies the ESC for CFS

|f_{\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}}(0)-f_{\mathbf{j}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{j}_{m}}(0)|>2^{-b|\mathbf{i}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{m}|}\geq 2^{-bnm},

which had to be proven.
∎

Now, let us define the following matrix with elements indexed by
\mathcal{V}_{n}
:

(B_{n}^{(s)})_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}:=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }t_{\mathbf{i}}=t_{\mathbf{j}},\\
\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}^{s}&\text{if }t_{\mathbf{i}}\neq t_{\mathbf{j}}.\end{cases}

Since
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is strongly connected, we get that
B_{n}^{(s)}
is an irreducible matrix with non-negative entries. By Lemma
6.2
and Prokaj and Simon
[
20
, Corollary 7.2]
,

\dim_{H}A_{n}=\min\{1,s_{n}\},

where
s_{n}
is the unique solution of the equation
\rho(B_{n}^{(s_{n})})=1
, where
\rho(B)
denotes the spectral radius of the matrix
B
. Hence,

\min\{1,\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}\}\leq\dim_{H}A.

(6.1)

Note that
s_{n}
is monotone increasing and bounded by the similarity dimension of
\mathcal{S}
, so the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}
exists.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
s_{0}
be the unique solution of the equation (
1.5
). Then
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}s_{n}=s_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us define the following
N\times N
matrix
C_{n}^{(s)}
as follows:

(C_{n}^{(s)})_{i,k}=\begin{cases}\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\cup_{m=1}^{n}T_{k}^{(m)}}\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}^{s}&\text{if }i\neq k,\\
0&\text{if }i=k.\end{cases}

First, we show that
\rho(B_{n}^{(s)})=\rho(C_{n}^{(s)})
. By Perron-Frobenius theorem, there exists
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})^{T}
with strictly positive entries such that
C_{n}^{(s)}v=\rho(C_{n}^{(s)})v
. Now, we define the vector
w=(w_{\mathbf{i}})_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}}
as follows:
w_{\mathbf{i}}=v_{i}
if
t_{\mathbf{i}}=t_{i}
. Then for every
\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
with
t_{\mathbf{i}}=t_{i}

(B_{n}^{(s)}w)_{\mathbf{i}}=\sum_{\mathbf{j}:~\mathbf{i}\to\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}}\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}^{s}w_{\mathbf{j}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\cup_{m=1}^{n}T_{k}^{(m)}}\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}^{s}\right)v_{k}=\rho(C_{n}^{(s)})v_{i}=\rho(C_{n}^{(s)})w_{\mathbf{i}}.

Again applying Perron-Frobenius Theorem, we get that
\rho(B_{n}^{(s)})=\rho(C_{n}^{(s)})
. Thus,
\rho(C_{n}^{(s_{n})})=1
, and in particular,
\det(C_{n}^{(s_{n})}-\mathrm{Id})=0
, where
\mathrm{Id}
denotes the identity matrix. By denoting

a_{n}^{i}(s):=\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\cup_{m=1}^{n}T_{i}^{(m)}}\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}^{s}+1~\text{for}~i\in\{1,2,\dots,N\},

we get by Lemma
6.1

0=|\det(C_{n}^{(s_{n})}-\mathrm{Id})|=(N-1)\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{n}^{i}(s_{n})-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=1\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N}a_{n}^{\ell}(s_{n}).

It is easy to see that for every
s\in(0,\infty)
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}^{i}(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}(1-\lambda_{i,j}^{s})^{-1}
, and by continuity,
s_{0}
satisfies the equation

0=(N-1)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}(1-\lambda_{i,j}^{s_{0}})^{-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=1\\
\ell\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\ell}}(1-\lambda_{i,j}^{s_{0}})^{-1},

and so

0=(N-1)-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{k}}(1-\lambda_{k,j}^{s_{0}}),

which had to be proven.
∎

To finish the proof, we show that
s_{0}
is an upper bound. Let
T_{i}^{(\infty)}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{i}^{(n)}
, and for
s>0
let

(C_{\infty}^{(s)})_{i,k}=\begin{cases}\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in T_{k}^{(\infty)}}\lambda_{\mathbf{j}}^{s}=\prod_{j=1}^{n_{k}}(1-\lambda_{k,j}^{s})^{-1}-1&\text{if }i\neq k,\\
0&\text{if }i=k.\end{cases}

Since
C_{n}^{(s)}\to C_{\infty}^{(s)}
as
n\to\infty
, the quantity
s_{0}
is the unique solution of the equation
\rho(C_{\infty}^{(s_{0})})=1
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the self-similar IFS defined in (
1.3
). Then for every
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{i,j})_{i=1,j=1}^{N,n_{i}}
and
\underline{t}=(t_{i})_{i=1}^{N}

\dim_{H}\Lambda\leq\min\{1,s_{0}\},

where
\Lambda
is the attractor of
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

First, observe that the set
\Gamma=\Pi(\{\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma:B^{\mathbf{i}}<\infty\})
is countable, and so, it is enough to cover the set
\Lambda\setminus\Gamma
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}
. Observe that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\Lambda\setminus\Gamma
can be covered by

\left\{f_{\mathbf{i}_{1}\cdots\mathbf{i}_{n}}([t_{1},t_{N}]):\mathbf{i}_{k}\in T_{i_{k}}^{(\infty)}\text{ and }i_{k}\neq i_{k+1}\text{ for every }k=1,\ldots,n-1\right\},

with diameters at most
\lambda_{\max}^{n}|t_{N}-t_{1}|
. Now, let
s>s_{0}
be arbitrary, and so
\rho(C_{\infty}^{(s)})<1
. Denote
\|.\|_{1}
the
1
-norm for
N\times N
matrices, and let
\ell\geq 1
be such that
\left\|(C_{\infty}^{(s)})^{m}\right\|_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\rho(C_{\infty}^{(s)})+1}{2}\right)^{m}
for every
m\geq\ell
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\lambda_{\max}^{n}|t_{N}-t_{1}|}(\Lambda\setminus\Gamma)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\\
i_{k}\neq i_{k+1}\text{ for }k=1,\ldots,n-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mathbf{i}_{1}\in T_{i_{1}}^{(\infty)}}\cdots\sum_{\mathbf{i}_{n}\in T_{i_{n}}^{(\infty)}}\lambda_{\mathbf{i}_{1}}^{s}\cdots\lambda_{\mathbf{i}_{n}}^{s}|t_{N}-t_{1}|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\|(C_{\infty}^{(s)})^{n}\|_{1}|t_{N}-t_{1}|^{s}\leq\left(\frac{\rho(C_{\infty}^{(s)})+1}{2}\right)^{n}|t_{N}-t_{1}|^{s}

for every
n\geq\ell
. Hence,
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Lambda\setminus\Gamma)=0
for
s>s_{0}
, which implies the claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The claim follows by combining (
6.1
), Lemma
6.3
and Lemma
6.4
.
∎

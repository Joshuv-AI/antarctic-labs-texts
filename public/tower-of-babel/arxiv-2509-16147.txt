Almost Commutative Terwilliger Algebras I: The Group Association Scheme
Nicholas L. Bastian, Stephen P. Humphries

Abstract
Terwilliger algebras are a subalgebra of a matrix algebra constructed from an association scheme. Rie Tanaka defined what it means for a Terwilliger algebra to be almost commutative and gave five equivalent conditions. In this paper we first determine an equivalent sixth condition for a Terwilliger algebra coming from a commutative Schur ring to be almost commutative. We then provide a classification of which finite groups result in an almost commutative Terwilliger algebra when looking at the group association scheme determined by the conjugacy classes. In particular, we show that all such groups are either abelian, or Camina groups. We then compute the dimension of each Terwilliger algebra, and we also express each of the group association schemes with an almost commutative Terwilliger algebra as a wreath product of the group schemes of finite abelian groups and 1 − 1- class association schemes. Furthermore, we give the non-primary primitive idempotents for each Terwilliger algebra for those groups.



Keywords
:
Terwilliger algebra, Camina group, association scheme, group association scheme, Schur ring, centralizer algebra

: 05E30, 05E16

Introduction

Terwilliger algebras were originally developed in the 1990’s by Paul Terwilliger to study commutative association schemes. In particular, he looked at P-Polynomial and Q-Polynomial association schemes. Over the course of the three papers
[

18

19

Since that time Terwilliger algebras have been studied for many types of association schemes. One of the most common is the group association scheme (

1
,
2
,
3
,
11
]

Tanaka

determined a situation in which the Terwilliger algebra has a particular Wedderburn structure (see Theorem
2.1
) called almost commutative. One of Tanaka’s results is that when a Terwilliger algebra is almost commutative, the corresponding association scheme can be written as a wreath product.

In this paper the group
G
will always be finite and

An abelian group. In this case

Terwilliger Algebras

Let
\Omega
be a finite set. Suppose
A_{0},A_{1},\cdots,A_{d}\in M_{|\Omega|}(\mathbb{C})
are
0,1
matrices with rows and columns indexed by the elements of
\Omega
. Let
A^{t}
denote the transpose of the matrix
A
. If additionally

1.

A_{0}=I_{|\Omega|}
;

2.

for all
i=1,2,\dots,d
, we have
A_{i}^{t}=A_{j}
for some
j=1,2,\dots,d
;

3.

for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,d\}
we have
A_{i}A_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}p_{ij}^{k}A_{k}
;

4.

none of the
A_{i}
is equal to
0_{|\Omega|}
and
\sum_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}
is the all
1
matrix;

then we say that
\mathcal{A}=(\Omega,\{A_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d})
is an
association scheme
. We call
A_{0},A_{1},\cdots,A_{d}
the
adjacency matrices
of the association scheme and the constants
p_{ij}^{k}
the
intersection numbers
. If
A_{i}A_{j}=A_{j}A_{i}
for all
i,j
, we have a
commutative association scheme
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X,\{A_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq c})
and
\mathcal{Y}=(Y,\{B_{i}\}_{0\leq j\leq d})
be two association schemes where
|X|=m
and
|Y|=n
. Then the
wreath product
of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, denoted
\mathcal{X}\wr\mathcal{Y}
, is defined on the set
X\times Y
with adjacency matrices

D_{0}=B_{0}\otimes A_{0},\ D_{k}=I_{n}\otimes A_{k},\ (\text{for $1\leq k\leq c$}),\ D_{c+k}=B_{k}\otimes J_{m},\ (\text{ for $1\leq k\leq d$}).

The association schemes that we will be interested in correspond to Schur rings. Let
R
be a commutative ring with
1
. For
C\subseteq G
, let
\overline{C}=\sum_{g\in C}g\in R[G]
and
C^{*}=\{g^{-1}\colon g\in G\}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a partition of
G
. We say
\mathfrak{S}=\text{Span}_{R}\{\overline{C}|C\in\mathcal{P}\}
is a
Schur ring
(see
[
13
,
20
]
) if:

1.

\{e\}\in\mathcal{P};

2.

if
C\in\mathcal{P}
, then
C^{*}\in\mathcal{P};

3.

for all
C,D\in\mathcal{P}
,

\overline{C}\cdot\overline{D}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{P}}\lambda_{CDE}\overline{E}
.

We call the sets in
\mathcal{P}
the
principal sets
of
\mathfrak{S}
and the constants
\lambda_{CDE}
the
structure constants
.

Schur rings give rise to association schemes in the following way. We define
|G|\times|G|
matrices
A_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq d
, by

(A_{i})_{xy}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{ if }yx^{-1}\in P_{i}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

With this construction
(G,\{A_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d})
is an association scheme. If the Schur ring is commutative then the association scheme is commutative. The structure constants
\lambda_{ijk}
coming from
\overline{P_{i}}\cdot\overline{P_{j}}=\sum_{P_{k}\in\mathcal{P}}\lambda_{ijk}\overline{P_{k}}
are the intersection numbers of the association scheme. In particular, partitioning
G
by the conjugacy classes of
G
, we get a Schur ring. This gives a commutative association scheme, called the
group association scheme
, denoted
\mathcal{G}(G)
.

Returning to arbitrary association schemes, we note that
\mathfrak{A}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{A_{i}\colon 0\leq i\leq d\}
is an algebra, called the
Bose-Mesner algebra
of the association scheme. We let
E_{0},E_{1},\dots,E_{d}
be the primitive idempotents of
\mathfrak{A}
. As
\mathfrak{A}
is closed under
Hadamard product
, denoted
\circ
, we have
E_{i}\circ E_{j}\in\mathfrak{A}
. Then there are
q_{ij}^{k}\in\mathbb{C}
, called the
Krein parameters
, such that

E_{i}\circ E_{j}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{k=0}^{d}q_{ij}^{k}E_{k}.

Fix
x\in\Omega
. We further define diagonal matrices
E_{i}^{*}(x)

with base point
x
by the rule

(E_{i}^{*}(x))_{yy}=\Bigg\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&(x,y)\in R_{i}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}

We call
\mathfrak{A}^{*}(x)=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{E_{i}^{*}(x)\colon 0\leq i\leq d\}
the
dual Bose-Mesner Algebra
of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
x
.

Now we define the
Terwilliger algebra
with base point
x\in\Omega
of the association scheme
\mathcal{A}=(\Omega,\{A_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d})
to be the subalgebra of
M_{|\Omega|}(\mathbb{C})
generated by
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{*}(x)
. We denote this by
T(x)
. For the group association scheme we always take the base point
x
to be the identity
e\in G
. We let
T(G)
denote the Terwilliger algebra for
\mathcal{G}(G)
.

Let
T_{0}(x)=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{E_{i}^{*}(x)A_{j}E_{k}^{*}(x)\colon 0\leq i,j,k\leq d\}
.
We note
\dim T_{0}(x)=|\{(i,j,k)\colon p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0\}|

[
2
]
. A Terwilliger algebra is
triply regular
if
T(x)=T_{0}(x)
.

If
|\Omega|>1
, then the Terwilliger algebra with base point
x\in\Omega
, is non-commutative and semi-simple for all
x\in\Omega

[
17
]
. As such, it has a Wedderburn decomposition. Given an association scheme
(\Omega,\{A_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d})
, the Terwilliger algebra for this association scheme with base point
x
always has an irreducible ideal
V
of dimension
(d+1)^{2}
, called the
primary component
(see
[
15
]
).

Closely related to the Wedderburn decomposition of the Terwilliger algebra are those
T(x)-
modules that are irreducible when
T(x)
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{|\Omega|}
by left multiplication. Then
\mathbb{C}^{|\Omega|}
decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible
T(x)-
modules. Letting
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\Omega|}
have
1
in position
x
and
0
elsewhere, it is shown in
[
17
]
that
\mathfrak{A}\hat{x}
is an irreducible
T(x)-
module of dimension
d+1
. We call this irreducible module the
primary module
.

A Terwilliger algebra
T(x)
is
almost commutative
(AC) if every non-primary irreducible
T(x)-
module is
1-
dimensional. There is a classification of such Terwilliger algebras when the underlying association scheme is commutative:

Theorem 2.1

(Tanaka,
[
16
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(\Omega,\{R_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d})
be a commutative association scheme. Let
T(x)
be the Terwilliger algebra of
\mathcal{A}
for some
x\in\Omega
. The following are equivalent:

1.

Every non-primary irreducible
T(x)-
module is
1-
dimensional for some
x\in\Omega
.

2.

Every non-primary irreducible
T(x)-
module is
1-
dimensional for all
x\in\Omega
.

3.

The
p_{ij}^{h}
satisfy: for all distinct
h,i
there is exactly one
j
such that
p_{ij}^{h}\neq 0

(0\leq h,i,j\leq d)
.

4.

The
q_{ij}^{h}
satisfy: for all distinct
h,i
there is exactly one
j
such that
q_{ij}^{h}\neq 0

(0\leq h,i,j\leq d)
.

5.

\mathcal{A}
is a wreath product of association schemes
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
where each
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is either a
1-
class association scheme or the group scheme of a abelian group.

Moreover, a Terwilliger algebra satisfying these equivalent conditions is triply regular.

The focus of this paper is to determine exactly when
T(G)
is AC and then study the resulting Terwilliger algebra. We now give a condition that will show when
T(G)
is AC.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}
be any commutative Schur ring for
G
with principal sets
P_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq d
. Let
T(G,\mathfrak{S})
be the Terwilliger algebra of
G
with respect to
\mathfrak{S}
. Then
T(G,\mathfrak{S})
is AC if and only if
\mathfrak{S}
has the following property: for all
x,y\in G
if
x\in P_{i}
,
y\in P_{j}
,
xy\in P_{h}
, and
P_{i}\neq P_{j}^{*}
then
P_{i}P_{j}=P_{h}
.

Proof.

First suppose that
T(G,\mathfrak{S})
is AC. Let
x,y\in G
with
x\in P_{i}
,
y\in P_{j}
and
P_{i}\neq P_{j}^{*}
. Since
P_{j}\neq P_{i}^{*}
, and
T(G,\mathfrak{S})
is AC, there is a unique
h
with
p_{i^{\prime}h}^{j}\neq 0
, so
P_{j}\subseteq P_{i^{\prime}}P_{h}
. Then for
z\in P_{j}
, there are
a\in P_{i^{\prime}}
and
b\in P_{h}
such that
z=ab
. Then
b=a^{-1}z
, so
b\in P_{i}P_{j}
. Thus,
P_{h}\subseteq P_{i}P_{j}
. Now suppose that
P_{m}\subseteq P_{i}P_{j}
. Then for
a\in P_{m}
, there is
b\in P_{i}
and
c\in P_{j}
such that
a=bc
. Then
c=b^{-1}a
, so
c\in P_{i^{\prime}}P_{m}
. Thus
P_{j}\subseteq P_{i^{\prime}}P_{m}
. This implies
p_{i^{\prime}m}^{j}\neq 0
. By the uniqueness of
p_{i^{\prime}h}^{j}
, we have
P_{m}=P_{h}
. So
P_{h}
is the only principal set in
P_{i}P_{j}
. We know
\overline{P_{i}}\cdot\overline{P_{j}}=\sum_{\ell}p_{ij}^{\ell}\overline{P_{\ell}}
. For all
p_{ij}^{\ell}\neq 0
, we have
P_{\ell}\subseteq P_{i}P_{j}
. Since
P_{h}
is the unique principal set in
P_{i}P_{j}
we must have
P_{i}P_{j}=P_{h}
. Note
x\in P_{i}
and
y\in P_{j}
, so
xy\in P_{h}
as
P_{i}P_{j}=P_{h}
. Therefore,
P_{h}
is the principal set containing
xy
as claimed.

Conversely, suppose that for all
x,y\in G
, if
x\in P_{i}
,
y\in P_{j}
,
xy\in P_{h}
, and
P_{i}\neq P_{j}^{*}
then
P_{i}P_{j}=P_{h}
. Let
P_{i}
and
P_{h}
be distinct principal sets with
x\in P_{i}
and
z\in P_{h}
. Let
P_{j}
be the principal set containing
x^{-1}z
. Notice
z\in P_{i}P_{j}
, so
P_{h}\subseteq P_{i}P_{j}
which implies
p_{ij}^{h}\neq 0
. So we have found a
j
such that
p_{ij}^{h}\neq 0
. Now suppose that
P_{w}
is any principal set such that
p_{iw}^{h}\neq 0
. This implies that
P_{h}\subseteq P_{i}P_{w}
. Then
z=ab
for some
a\in P_{i}
and
b\in P_{w}
, so
e=z^{-1}ab
. Notice that
z^{-1}\in P_{h}^{*}
,
a\in P_{i}
, and
b\in P_{w}
, so
e\in P_{h}^{*}P_{i}P_{w}
. As
P_{i}\neq P_{h}
,
z^{-1}\in P_{h}^{*}
,
x\in P_{i}
, and
z^{-1}x\in P_{j}^{*}
we have
P_{h}^{*}P_{i}=P_{j}^{*}
. Hence,
e\in P_{h}^{*}P_{i}P_{w}=P_{j}^{*}P_{w}
. Then there exists a
g\in P_{j}^{*}
such that
g^{-1}\in P_{w}
. However,
g^{-1}\in P_{j}
and so
P_{w}=P_{j}
. So for distinct
P_{i},P_{h}
there is a unique
j
such that
p_{ij}^{h}\neq 0
. By Theorem
2.1
,
T(G,\mathfrak{S})
is AC.
∎

(

7
,

]
) if every conjugacy class of

outside of

is a coset of

. We will need:

Theorem 3.1

(Dark and Scoppola,
[
10
]
)
.

Let

be a Camina group. Then one of the following is true:

1.

G
is a Frobenius group whose Frobenius complement is cyclic.

2.

G
is a Frobenius group whose Frobenius complement is

.

3.

is a

group for some prime

.

(Macdonald,

]
)
.

Let

. Additionally,

Proposition 3.3

(Macdonald,

]
)

Proposition 3.4

(Lemma 2.3,

(Macdonald, Theorem 5.2(i),

Let

(Cangelmi and Muktibodh,

]

AC Terwilliger Algebras for Group Association Schemes

For the remainder of this paper let
C_{0}=\{e\},C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{d}
be the conjugacy classes of
G
. We show:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group. Then
T(G)
is AC if and only if
G
is isomorphic to one of the following groups:
(i)
an abelian group;
(ii)
the Frobenius group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1}
, for prime
p
and
r>0
;
(iii)
a Camina
p-
group, for prime
p
;
(iv)
the group
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}\rtimes Q_{8}
.

We note that Theorem
4.1
is part of Theorem
1.1
. The proof of this theorem immediately follows from Theorem

and the following result by Dade and Yadav.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

A group
G
satisfies the property that for all
x,y\in G
such that
x^{G}\neq(y^{-1})^{G}
,
x^{G}y^{G}=(xy)^{G}
if and only if
G
is isomorphic to exactly one of the following groups:
(i)
an abelian group;
(ii)
the Frobenius group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1}
, for prime
p
and
r>0
;
(iii)
a Camina
p-
group, for prime
p
;
(iv)
the group
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}\rtimes Q_{8}
.

Now we shall compute
\dim T(G)
in each case
(i)-(iv)
. For abelian groups, we have:

Proposition 4.3

(Bannai and Munemasa,
[
2
]
)
.

Let

be a abelian group. Then
\dim T(G)=|G|^{2}
.

A direct computation using Magma
[
6
]
yields:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}\rtimes Q_{8}
. Then
\dim T(G)=44
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1}
for some prime

and

. Then
\dim T(G)=p^{2r}+p^{r}-1
.

Proof.

By Theorems
2.1
and
4.1
,
T(G)
is AC and triply regular. Therefore,
T(G)=T_{0}(G)
and
\dim T(G)=|\{(i,j,k)\colon p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0\}|
.

Since
G
is a Camina group, its classes outside of
G^{\prime}
are all cosets of
G^{\prime}
. By Theorem
3.6
, the classes of
G
inside
G^{\prime}
are
\{e\}
and
G^{\prime}\setminus\{e\}
. Note that
G^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
and
G/G^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1}
. Then there are
p^{r}-2
classes of
G
outside of
G^{\prime}
. So the number of classes of
G
is
2+(p^{r}-2)=p^{r}
. Recall that

\dim T_{0}(G)=|\{(i,j,k)\colon p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0\}|.

We count the number of triples with
p_{ij}^{k}\neq 0
by considering cases based on the types of classes. Let
C_{0}=\{e\}
and
C_{1}=G^{\prime}\setminus\{e\}
.

Case 1:
C_{i}=C_{0}
. Now
\overline{C_{0}}\cdot\overline{C_{j}}=\overline{C_{j}}
for all
j
, thus there are
p^{r}
triples
(0,j,j)
(or
(j,0,j)
) such that
p_{0j}^{j}\neq 0
(or
p_{j0}^{j}\neq 0
), giving a total of
2p^{r}
triples that involve the class
C_{0}=\{e\}

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

for prime

and

’s, and for

(

) block of

Recall that

block of

. Say

. Then

Now consider the

and

and

Next we consider the

where

and

with

. As

with

at least one of

and

is greater than

. First suppose

. Then

and

, so

for some

. This implies

. So

. However,

, a contradiction. Next suppose,

. Then

, so
hg^{-1}\in z^{j-1}G^{\prime}g^{-1}=z^{j-1}g^{-1}G^{\prime}

for

,

(

is all
0
’s.
∎

p^{r}-1\times p^{r}-1
matrix that is nonzero in the
j,i+j-1\mod p^{r}-1
entry for all
j
and
0
elsewhere. Namely the nonzero entries of
B
are
(1,i)
,
(2,i+1)
,
(3,i+2)
,
\cdots,

(p^{r}-1,i-1)
. Directly computing powers of
Q_{p^{r}-1}
we see that
Q_{p^{r}-1}^{i}
is nonzero in entries
(1,i+1),(2,i+2),\cdots,(p^{r}-1,i)
. Hence,
B=Q_{p^{r}-1}^{i-1}
. So for
i\geq 2
,
A_{i}=Q_{p^{r}-1}^{i-1}\otimes J_{p^{r}}
as claimed.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
G
be the Frobenius group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1}
. Then
\mathcal{G}(G)=\mathcal{K}_{p^{r}}\wr\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1})
.

Proof.

The adjacency matrices of
K_{p^{r}}\wr\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1})
are just
D_{0}=I_{p^{r}(p^{r}-1)}
,
D_{1}=I_{p^{r}-1}\otimes(J_{p^{r}}-I_{p^{r}})
, and
D_{i+1}=B_{i}\otimes J_{p^{r}}
for
1\leq i\leq p^{r}-1
, where the
B_{i}
are the adjacency matrices for
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1})
. We show that
B_{i-1}=Q_{p^{r}-1}^{i-1}
for each
i\geq 2
where
Q_{p^{r}-1}
is the matrix in (
5.1
). Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1}=\langle z\rangle
. We note

(B_{i})_{xy}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{ if }yx^{-1}=z^{i}\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\right..

Ordering the elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1}
as
z^{0},z^{1},z^{2},\cdots,z^{p^{r}-2}
the nonzero entries of
B_{i}
are
(1,i+1),\ (2,i+2),\ \cdots,(p^{r}-1,i)
. These are the nonzero entries of
Q_{p^{r}-1}^{i}
, so
B_{i}=Q_{p^{r}-1}^{i-1}
. Hence, the adjacency matrices of
K_{p^{r}}\wr\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1})
are just
D_{0}=I_{p^{r}(p^{r}-1)}
,
D_{1}=I_{p^{r}-1}\otimes(J_{p^{r}}-I_{p^{r}})
, and
D_{i+1}=Q_{p^{r}-1}^{i}\otimes J_{p^{r}}
for
1\leq i\leq p^{r}-1
. Shifting the index on
D_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq p^{r}-1
the adjacency matrices of
K_{p^{r}}\wr\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1})
are
D_{0}=I_{p^{r}-1}\otimes I_{p^{r}},D_{1}=I_{p^{r}-1}\otimes(J_{p^{r}}-I_{p^{r}})
, and for
i\geq 2
,
D_{i}=Q_{p^{r}-1}^{i-1}\otimes J_{p^{r}}
. From Theorem
5.3
, these are exactly the adjacency matrices for
\mathcal{G}(G)
. Hence,
\mathcal{G}(G)=K_{p^{r}}\wr\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{r}-1})
.
∎

For a Camina
p-
group

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
G
be a Camina
p-
group and
C_{k}\not\subseteq G^{\prime}
. Then for classes
C_{i},C_{j}
of
G
the
C_{i},C_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is all
0
’s if
C_{j}\not\subseteq C_{i}C_{k}
and all
1
’s if
C_{j}\subseteq C_{i}C_{k}
.

Proof.

As
C_{k}\not\subseteq G^{\prime}
,
C_{k}=gG^{\prime}
for some
g\in G\setminus G^{\prime}
. Now let
x\in C_{i}
,
y\in C_{j}
. We have
(A_{k})_{xy}=1
if and only if
yx^{-1}\in gG^{\prime}
, if and only if
y\in gG^{\prime}x=gxG^{\prime}
, if and only if
C_{j}\subseteq gxG^{\prime}
. Now either
C_{i}\subseteq G^{\prime}
or
C_{i}=hG^{\prime}
for some
h\in G\setminus G^{\prime}
. If
C_{i}\subseteq G^{\prime}
, then
x\in G^{\prime}
and
gxG^{\prime}=gG^{\prime}=C_{i}C_{k}
. If
C_{i}=hG^{\prime}
,
h\in G\setminus G^{\prime}
, then
C_{i}=xG^{\prime}
as well. Then
gxG^{\prime}=C_{k}C_{i}=C_{i}C_{k}
. So either way
C_{i}C_{k}=gxG^{\prime}
. So for
x\in C_{i}
,
y\in C_{j}
,
(A_{k})_{xy}=1
if and only if
C_{j}\subseteq C_{i}C_{k}
.

Now suppose the
C_{i},C_{j}
block of
A_{k}
has a nonzero entry:
(A_{k})_{ab}=1
for
a\in C_{i},b\in C_{j}
. Then
ba^{-1}\in C_{k}
. Hence,
b\in C_{k}a\subseteq C_{i}C_{k}
. This implies
C_{j}\subseteq C_{i}C_{k}
, and so for
x\in C_{i}
and
y\in C_{j}
,
(A_{k})_{xy}=1
. Thus, the
C_{i},C_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is all
1
’s, so the
C_{i},C_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is all
0
’s or all
1
’s.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
C_{k}\subseteq Z(G)
be a class of
G
. If
G
has nilpotency class
2
, let
C_{i},C_{j}
be any two classes of
G
. If
G
has nilpotency class
3
, then suppose
C_{i},C_{j}\subseteq G^{\prime}
are classes of
G
. Then

1.

If
C_{i},C_{j}\subseteq Z(G)
, then the
C_{i},C_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is
0
if
C_{j}\neq C_{i}C_{k}
and
1
if
C_{j}=C_{i}C_{k}
.

2.

If
C_{i}\neq C_{j}
with either
C_{i}\not\subseteq Z(G)
or
C_{j}\not\subseteq Z(G)
, then the
C_{i},C_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is all
0
.

3.

If
C_{i}\not\subseteq Z(G)
, then the
C_{i},C_{i}
block of
A_{k}
has a single nonzero entry in each row.

Proof.

As
C_{k}\subseteq Z(G)
,
C_{k}=\{g\}
for
g\in Z(G)
.

1: If
C_{i},C_{j}\subseteq Z(G)
, then
C_{i}=\{x\}
and
C_{j}=\{y\}
,
x,y\in Z(G)
. Then the
C_{i},C_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is just the
(x,y)
entry. So
(A_{k})_{xy}=1
if and only if
yx^{-1}\in C_{k}=\{g\}
. That is,
(A_{k})_{xy}=1
if and only if
yx^{-1}=g
, Hence, the
C_{i},C_{j}
block of
A_{k}
is
0
if
C_{j}\neq C_{i}C_{k}
and
1
if
C_{j}=C_{i}C_{k}
.

2: Assume
C_{i}\neq C_{j}
with either
C_{i}\not\subseteq Z(G)
or
C_{j}\not\subseteq Z(G)
. Without loss of generality suppose
C_{i}\not\subseteq Z(G)
. Then by Proposition
3.5
,
C_{i}=hZ(G)
for some
h\in G
. Let
x\in C_{i}
and
y\in C_{j}
. If
(A_{k})_{xy}=1
, then
yx^{-1}\in C_{k}\subseteq Z(G)
. We note as
x\in C_{i}=hZ(G)
, that
x^{-1}\in h^{-1}Z(G)
. If
C_{j}\subseteq Z(G)
, then
yx^{-1}\in h^{-1}Z(G)
. However,
h^{-1}Z(G)\cap Z(G)=\emptyset
, which contradicts
yx^{-1}\in h^{-1}Z(G)\cap Z(G)
. If
C_{j}\subseteq G\setminus Z(G)
, then by Proposition
3.5
,
C_{j}=\ell Z(G)

. Since

\ell h^{-1}Z(G)\neq Z(G)
. As
y\in\ell Z(G)

. However,
\ell h^{-1}Z(G)\cap Z(G)=\emptyset

3: Assume
C_{i}=C_{j}

and fix

(A_{k})_{xy}=1

, if and only if

is nonzero. This however is the only nonzero entry of the

For
G
of nilpotency class

we now write the adjacency matrices as Kronecker products.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G
be a Camina
p-
group of nilpotency class
2
. Suppose
|Z(G)|=p^{r}
and
|G|=p^{n}
. If
C_{k}\subseteq Z(G)
is a conjugacy class of
G
,
C_{k}=\{g\}
, then
A_{k}=I_{p^{n-r}}\otimes B
where
B
is the adjacency matrix of
{
g
}
when considered as a conjugacy class in
Z(G)
.

Proof.

The blocks of
A_{k}
correspond to cosets of
Z(G)=G^{\prime}
, so the
xZ(G),yZ(G)
block of
A_{k}
is the same as the
x^{G},y^{G}
block for any
x,y\not\in Z(G)
. So in this situation the blocks are the same as the class blocks. The only difference is that classes in
Z(G)
are now being considered as a single block. From Lemma
5.6
, the
xZ(G),yZ(G)
block of
A_{k}
is all
0
if
xZ(G)\neq yZ(G)
for any
x,y\in G
. Hence, only the diagonal blocks are nonzero. Additionally from the proof of Lemma
5.6
, in the
xZ(G),xZ(G)
block for
x\not\in Z(G)
the only nonzero entry in the
y
th row of
A_{k}
is the
(y,gy)
entry for all
y\in xZ(G)
. Now consider the
Z(G),Z(G)
block. Consider row
y\in Z(G)
of this block. From Lemma
5.6
, the
(y,z)
entry of
A_{k}
is
1
if and only if

. However, as all of
y,g,z\in Z(G)
this is the same thing as saying

. Hence, in the
Z(G),Z(G)
block of
A_{k}
an entry is nonzero if and only if it is the
(y,gy)
entry for some
y\in Z(G)
. Hence, for all
x\in G
the

block of
A_{k}
has a nonzero entry in entry
(y,gy)
for all

and is
0

A_{k}
is
0
outside of these diagonal blocks. Then
A_{k}=I_{p^{n-r}}\otimes B
where

is a
p^{r}\times p^{r}
matrix such that for row
y
, the only nonzero entry is
(y,gy)
. That is,
B
is a
p^{r}\times p^{r}
matrix such that
(B)_{uv}=1

vu^{-1}=g
. This is identical to the condition for the adjacency matrix of
\{g\}
in

is the adjacency matrix of
\{g\}
when considered as a conjugacy class in
Z(G)
.

Now that we have determined the adjacency matrices of

we can write

as a wreath product of two group association schemes of abelian groups.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
G
be a Camina
p-
group of nilpotency class
2
for some prime
p
, where
|Z(G)|=p^{r}
and
|G|=p^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{G}(G)=\mathcal{G}(Z(G))\wr\mathcal{G}(G/Z(G))
.

Proof.

Let
A_{0},A_{1},\cdots,A_{p^{r}}
be the adjacency matrices of
\mathcal{G}(Z(G))
and
B_{0},B_{1},\cdots,B_{p^{n-r}}
be the adjacency matrices of
\mathcal{G}(G/Z(G))
. The adjacency matrices of
\mathcal{G}(Z(G))\wr\mathcal{G}(G/Z(G))
are
D_{0}=I_{p^{n-r}}\otimes I_{p^{r}}=I_{p^{n}}
,
D_{k}=I_{p^{n-r}}\otimes A_{k}
where
1\leq k\leq p^{r}
, and
D_{p^{r}+k}=B_{k}\otimes J_{p^{r}}
for
1\leq k\leq p^{n-r}
. From Lemma
5.7
, all the adjacency matrices
M_{k}
of
\mathcal{G}(G)
for some class
C_{k}=\{g\}\subset Z(G)
are of the form
M_{k}=I_{p^{n-r}}\otimes A_{k}
where
A_{k}
is the adjacency matrix for
C_{k}=\{g\}
in
Z(G)
. Ranging over all
C_{k}\subset Z(G)
we have
D_{i}=M_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq p^{r}
. From Lemma
5.8
, the adjacency matrices
M_{k}
of
\mathcal{G}(G)
for any class
C_{k}=gZ(G)
, has
M_{k}=B_{\ell}\otimes J_{p^{r}}
where
B_{\ell}
is the adjacency matrix for
\{gZ(G)\}
in
G/Z(G)
. We then have a one-to-one correspondence between the adjacency matrices
M_{k}
of
\mathcal{G}(G)
for
C_{k}\not\subseteq Z(G)
and the adjacency matrices of the form
D_{p^{r}+k}=B_{k}\otimes J_{p^{r}}
for
\mathcal{G}(Z(G))\wr\mathcal{G}(G/Z(G))
.

We then see
\mathcal{G}(G)
and
\mathcal{G}(Z(G))\wr\mathcal{G}(G/Z(G))
have the exact same adjacency matrices. Therefore,
\mathcal{G}(G)=\mathcal{G}(Z(G))\wr\mathcal{G}(G/Z(G))
.
∎

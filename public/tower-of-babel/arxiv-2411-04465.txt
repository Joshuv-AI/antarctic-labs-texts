On the Frobenius Problem for Some Generalized Fibonacci Subsequences - I
Santak Panda, Kartikeya Rai, Amitabha Tripathi

Abstract
For a set A A of positive integers with gcd ⁡ ( A ) = 1 \gcd(A)=1 , let ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle denote the set of all finite linear combinations of elements of A A over the non-negative integers. The it is well known that only finitely many positive integers do not belong to ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle . The Frobenius number and the genus associated with the set A A is the largest number and the cardinality of the set of integers non-representable by A A . By a generalized Fibonacci sequence { V n } n ≥ 1 \{V_{n}\}_{n\geq 1} we mean any sequence of positive integers satisfying the recurrence V n = V n − 1 + V n − 2 V_{n}=V_{n-1}+V_{n-2} for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . We study the problem of determining the Frobenius number and genus for sets A = { V n , V n + d , V n + 2 ​ d , … } A=\{V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\} for arbitrary n n , where d d odd or d = 2 d=2 .



Keywords.
Embedding dimension, Apéry set, Frobenius number, Genus

Introduction

For a given subset
A
of positive integers with
\gcd(A)=1
, we write

S=\langle A\rangle=\big\{a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}:a_{i}\in A,x_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\big\}.

We say that
A
is a set of generators for the set
S
. Further,
A
is a minimal set of generators for
S
if no proper subset of
A
generates
S
. Let
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
be a set of generators of
S
arranged in increasing order. Then the following are equivalent:

•

A
is a minimal set of generators for
S
;

•

a_{k+1}\notin\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\rangle
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
;

•

A=S^{\star}\setminus\big(S^{\star}+S^{\star}\big)
, where
S^{\star}=S\setminus\{0\}
.

The embedding dimension
\textbf{e}(S)
of
S
is the size of the minimal set of generators.

For any set of positive integers
A
with
\gcd(A)=1
, the set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\setminus S
is necessarily finite; we denote this by
\text{G}(S)
. The cardinality of
\text{G}(S)
is the genus of
S
and is denoted by
\textbf{g}(S)
. The largest element in
\text{G}(S)
is the Frobenius number of
S
and is denoted by
\textbf{F}(S)
.

The Apéry set of
S
corresponding to any fixed
a\in S
, denoted by
\text{Ap}(S,a)
, consists of those
n\in S
for which
n-a\notin S
. Thus,
\text{Ap}(S,a)
is the set of minimum integers in
S\cap\textbf{C}
as
C
runs through the complete set of residue classes modulo
a
.

The integers
\textbf{F}(S)
and
\textbf{g}(S)
, and the set
\text{PF}(S)
, can be computed from the Apéry set
\text{Ap}(S,a)
of
S
corresponding to any
a\in S
via the following proposition.

Proposition 1.1
.

(
[
2
,
8
,
9
]
)

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup, let
a\in S
, and let
\text{Ap}(S,a)
be the Apéry set of
S
corresponding to
a
. Then

(i)

\textbf{F}(S)=\max\Big(\text{Ap}(S,a)\Big)-a;

(ii)

\textbf{g}(S)=\frac{1}{a}\left(\sum_{n\in\text{Ap}(S,a)}n\right)-\frac{a-1}{2};

(iii)

\text{PF}(S)=\Big\{n-a:n\in\text{Ap}(S,a),n+\textbf{m}(x)>\textbf{m}(x+n),x=1,\ldots,a-1\Big\},

where
\textbf{m}(x)\in\text{Ap}(S,a)
and
\textbf{m}(x)\equiv x\pmod{a}
.

The case where
\textbf{e}(S)=2
is well known and easy to establish. If
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, then it is easy to see that
\text{Ap}(S,a)=\{bx:0\leq x\leq a-1\}
, and consequently

\textbf{F}(S)=ab-a-b,\quad\textbf{g}(S)=\tfrac{1}{2}(a-1)(b-1),\quad\text{PF}(S)=\{ab-a-b\}

(1)

by Proposition
1.1
.

The Frobenius Problem is the problem of determining the Frobenius number and the genus of a given numerical semigroup, and was first studied by Sylvester, and later by Frobenius; see
[
6
]
for a survey of the problem. Connections with Algebraic Geometry revived interest in Numerical Semigroups around the middle of the twentieth century; we refer to
[
7
]
as a basic textbook on the subject. Curtis
[
3
]
proved that there exists no closed form expression for the Frobenius number of a numerical semigroup
S
with
\textbf{e}(S)>2
. As a consequence, a lot of research has focussed on the Frobenius number of semigroups whose generators are of a particular form. There are three particular instances of such results that are perhaps the closest to our work, and hence bear mentioning. Marín et. al.
[
4
]
determined the Frobenius number and genus of numerical semigroups of the form
\langle F_{i},F_{i+2},F_{i+k}\rangle
, where
i,k\geq 3
. These are called Fibonacci semigroups by the authors. Matthews
[
5
]
considers semigroups of the form
\langle a,a+b,aF_{k-1}+bF_{k}\rangle
where
a>F_{k}
and
\gcd(a,b)=1
. Taking
a=F_{i}
and
b=F_{i+1}
, one gets the semigroup
\langle F_{i},F_{i+2},F_{i+k}\rangle
, considered in
[
4
]
. Thus, such semigroups were termed generalized Fibonacci semigroups by Matthews, who determined the Frobenius number of a generalized Fibonacci semigroup, thereby generalizing the result in
[
4
]
for Frobenius number. Batra et. al.
[
1
]
determined the Frobenius number and genus of numerical semigroups of the form
\langle a,a+b,2a+3b,\ldots,F_{2k-1}a+F_{2k}b\rangle
and
\langle a,a+3b,4a+7b,\ldots,L_{2k-1}a+L_{2k}b\rangle
where
\gcd(a,b)=1
.

By a generalized Fibonacci sequence we mean any sequence
\{V_{n}\}
of positive integers which satisfies the recurrence
V_{n}=V_{n-1}+V_{n-2}
for each
n\geq 3
. This paper studies the numerical semigroup generated by some subsequences of a generalized Fibonacci sequence
\{V_{n}\}
. More specifically, we study the numerical semigroup
S
generated by
\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\rangle
, where
V_{1}=a
and
V_{2}=b
. Our main results are:

(i)

S=\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
\gcd(a,b)=1
and
\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})=1
; see Theorem
2.4
.

(ii)

If
d
is odd, then
\textbf{e}(S)=2
. Consequently, the computation of
\textbf{F}(S)
and
\textbf{g}(S)
is straightforward; see Theorem
1
and Theorem
3.2
.

(iii)

If
d=2
, then
\textbf{e}(S)=\kappa
where
\kappa
satisfies
F_{2(\kappa-1)}\leq V_{n}-1<F_{2\kappa}
; see Theorem
4.9
.

(iv)

If
d=2
, then
\text{Ap}(S,V_{n})=\{s(x):1\leq x\leq V_{n}-1\}\cup\{0\}
, where
s(x)
is obtained by applying the Greedy Algorithm to
x
with respect to the sequence
F_{2},F_{4},F_{6},\ldots
; see Theorem
4.6
.

(v)

If
d=2
, then
\textbf{g}(S)=s(V_{n}-1)-V_{n}
and
\textbf{g}(S)=\frac{1}{V_{n}}\left(\sum_{x=1}^{V_{n}-1}s(x)\right)-\frac{V_{n}-1}{2}
; see Theorem
4.7
.

(vi)

If
d=2
, then
\textbf{F}(S)=F_{2n}-F_{n+2}
for the Fibonacci subsequence and
\textbf{F}(S)=L_{2n+1}+L_{2n-1}-L_{n+2}
for the Lucas subsequence; see Corollary
4.8
.

(vii)

If
d=2
, then
\textbf{g}(S)
for the Fibonacci subsequence is explicitly determined in terms of the solution of two recurrent sequences; see Theorem
4.11
.

2
Preliminary Results

A generalized Fibonacci sequence
\langle V_{n}\rangle_{n\geq 1}
is defined by

V_{n}=V_{n-1}+V_{n-2},\;\;n\geq 3,\quad\;\text{with}\;V_{1}=a,V_{2}=b,

(2)

where
a
and
b
are any positive integers. Two important special cases are (i) Fibonacci sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
when
a=b=1
, and (ii) Lucas sequence
\{L_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
when
a=1
and
b=3
. It is customary to extend these definitions to
F_{0}=F_{2}-F_{1}=0
and
L_{0}=L_{2}-L_{1}=2
. The following formulae of Binet gives an explicit expression for both
F_{n}
and
L_{n}
:

F_{n}=\frac{{\phi}^{n}-{\overline{\phi}}^{n}}{\sqrt{5}},\qquad L_{n}={\phi}^{n}+{\overline{\phi}}^{n},\quad n\geq 1,

(3)

where
\phi,\overline{\phi}
are the zeros of
x^{2}-x-1
,
\phi>\overline{\phi}
. Since
|\overline{\phi}|<\frac{2}{3}
,
F_{n}
is the integer closest to
{\phi}^{n}/\sqrt{5}
and
L_{n}
is the integer closest to
{\phi}^{n}
when
n>1
.

Two of the most well known connections between the sequences
\{F_{n}\}
and
\{L_{n}\}
are:

L_{n}=F_{n+1}+F_{n-1},\>n\geq 2,\quad\text{and}\quad F_{2n}=F_{n}L_{n},\>n\geq 1.

The first may be easily derived from eqn. (
3
) or by induction, while the second is immediate from eqn. (
3
).
It is well known and easy to see, either by eqn. (
3
) or by induction, that

F_{n+1}F_{n-1}-F_{n}^{2}=(-1)^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{n+1}L_{n-1}-L_{n}^{2}=5(-1)^{n},

for
n\geq 2
.

The following identities connecting generalized Fibonacci sequences with the Fibonacci sequence are useful in our subsequent work.

Proposition 2.1
.

(i)

For positive integers
m
and
n
,

V_{m+n}=F_{n-1}V_{m}+F_{n}V_{m+1}.

In particular,
F_{n}\mid F_{kn}
for each
k\geq 1
.

(ii)

For positive integers
k,n,d
,

F_{d}V_{n+kd}=(-1)^{d-1}F_{(k-1)d}V_{n}+F_{kd}V_{n+d}.

(iii)

For positive integers
m
,
k
and
d
,

V_{m+kd}\equiv V_{m+2}\,F_{kd}\!\!\!\!\pmod{V_{m}}.

(iv)

For positive integers
n
and
k
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{n+2i}=V_{n+2k+1}-V_{n+1}.

Proof.

(i)

We fix
m
and induct on
n
. The case
n=1
is an identity and the case
n=2
follows from the recurrence satisfied by the sequence
\{V_{n}\}
. Assuming the result for all positive integers less than
n
, we have

\displaystyle V_{m+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{m+(n-1)}+V_{m+(n-2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(F_{n-2}V_{m}+F_{n-1}V_{m+1}\big)+\big(F_{n-3}V_{m}+F_{n-2}V_{m+1}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(F_{n-2}+F_{n-3}\big)V_{m}+\big(F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}\big)V_{m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{n-1}V_{m}+F_{n}V_{m+1}.

This completes the proof by induction.

In particular, with
V_{n}=F_{n}
and
m=(k-1)n
, we have

F_{kn}=F_{(k-1)n}F_{n-1}+F_{(k-1)n+1}F_{n}.

So if
F_{n}\mid F_{(k-1)n}
, then
F_{n}\mid F_{kn}
. Hence,
F_{n}\mid F_{kn}
for each
k\geq 1
by induction.

(ii)

Interchanging
m
and
n
in part (i) and setting
m=d
yields
F_{d}V_{n+1}=V_{n+d}-F_{d-1}V_{n}
. Therefore

\displaystyle F_{d}V_{n+kd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{d}F_{kd-1}V_{n}+F_{kd}\big(V_{n+d}-F_{d-1}V_{n}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(F_{d}F_{kd-1}-F_{d-1}F_{kd}\big)V_{n}+F_{kd}V_{n+d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-1}F_{(k-1)d}V_{n}+F_{kd}V_{n+d}.

(iii)

This is obtained from part (i) by setting
n=kd
since
V_{m+1}=V_{m+2}-V_{m}
.

(iv)

We fix
n
and induct on
k
. The case
k=1
follows from the recurrence satisfied by the sequence
\{V_{n}\}
. Assuming the result for all positive integers less than
k
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{n+2i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}V_{n+2i}+V_{n+2k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(V_{n+2k-1}-V_{n+1}\big)+V_{n+2k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{n+2k+1}-V_{n+1}.

This completes the proof by induction.

\blacksquare

Corollary 2.2
.

If
\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
is a numerical semigroup, then
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d})=1
.

Proof.

The conclusion follows from the observation that
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d})
divides each term
V_{n+kd}
by Proposition
2.1
, part (ii).

\blacksquare

Corollary 2.3
.

If
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=1
, then

\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d})=\gcd(V_{n},F_{d}).

Proof.

From the identity
V_{n+1}=V_{n}+V_{n-1}
we have
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+1})=\gcd(V_{n-1},V_{n})
. Since
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=1
, it follows that
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+1})=1
for each positive integer
n
. Now Proposition
2.1
, part (i) gives
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d})=\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+1}F_{d})=\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})
since
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+1})=1
.

\blacksquare

The following theorem provides a necessary and sufficient condition for
\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
to form a numerical semigroup.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
S=\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
. Then
S
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=1
and
\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})=1
.

Proof.

We recall that
S
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots)=1
. Let
g=\gcd(a,b)
. Then
g
divides each
V_{m}
by the recurrence defining the terms in the sequence
\langle V_{m}\rangle
, and so a necessary condition for
S
to be a numerical semigroup is
g=1
.

Now assume
g=1
, and write
g^{\prime}=\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})
. By Proposition
2.1
, part (i),
V_{n+kd}=V_{n+(k-1)d}F_{d-1}+V_{n+(k-1)d+1}F_{d}
. Since
g^{\prime}
divides
V_{n}
, if
g^{\prime}
divides
V_{n+(k-1)d}
then
g^{\prime}
also divides
V_{n+kd}
. Hence,
g^{\prime}
divides
V_{n+kd}
for each
k\geq 0
by induction, which implies
g^{\prime}=1
. This shows the necessity of the two
\gcd
conditions.

Conversely,
g^{\prime}=1
and Corollary
2.3
imply
\gcd(V_{n+d},V_{n})=1
, so that
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots)=1
. This proves the sufficiency of the conditions.

\blacksquare

3
The Case where
d
is odd

In this Section, we study the case where
d
is odd. As a consequence of the identities in Proposition
2.1
, we show that each term
V_{n+kd}
,
k\geq 2
, is of the form
V_{n}x+V_{n+d}y
with
x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. This enables us to easily determine
\textbf{F}(S)
,
\textbf{g}(S)
and the set
\text{PF}(S)
in this case.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=1
and
\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})=1
, then

\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle=\langle V_{n},V_{n+d}\rangle.

Proof.

The two gcd conditions are necessary and sufficient to ensure
S=\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
is a numerical semigroup by Theorem
2.4
. In order to prove the result of this theorem, we must only show that
V_{n+kd}\in\langle V_{n},V_{n+d}\rangle
for each
k\geq 2
. This is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.1
, part (ii), since
F_{d}
divides both
F_{(k-1)d}
and
F_{kd}
for each
k\geq 2
.

\blacksquare

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
d
be odd,
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=1
and
\gcd(V_{n},F_{d})=1
. If
S=\langle V_{n},V_{n+d},V_{n+2d},\ldots\rangle
, then

(i)

\textbf{e}(S)=2.

(ii)

\textbf{F}(S)=(V_{n}-1)(V_{n+d}-1)-1.

(iii)

\textbf{g}(S)=\frac{1}{2}(V_{n}-1)(V_{n+d}-1).

(iv)

\text{PF}(S)=\Big\{(V_{n}-1)(V_{n+d}-1)-1\Big\}.

Proof.

Part (i) is the result in Theorem
3.1
. Parts (ii), (iii) and (iv) are consequences of part (i) and eqn. (
1
).

\blacksquare

4
The Case where
d=2

In this Section, we study the case where
d=2
. This case turns out to be a lot more challenging than when
d
is odd, in part due to the fact that the crucial identity that allows each
V_{n+kd}\in\langle V_{n},V_{n+d}\rangle
depends on the parity of
d
. The following result leads to the determination of the Apéry set for
S
with respect to
V_{n}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
x\in\{1,\ldots,V_{n}-1\}
, and let
k
be such that
F_{2k}\leq x<F_{2k+2}
. Then there exists
{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}
, with each
{\lambda}_{i}\in\{0,1,2\}
and
{\lambda}_{k}\geq 1
such that

x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i},\quad s=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+2i},\quad V_{n+2k}\leq s<V_{n+2k+2},\quad s\equiv V_{n+2}\,x\!\!\!\!\pmod{V_{n}}.

Proof.

We define the sequence
{\lambda}_{k},{\lambda}_{k-1},\ldots,{\lambda}_{1}
by using the Greedy Algorithm on
x
with respect to the sequence
F_{2},F_{4},F_{6},\ldots,F_{2k}
:

{\lambda}_{k}=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{F_{2k}}\right\rfloor,\quad{\lambda}_{j}=\left\lfloor\frac{x-\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}}{F_{2j}}\right\rfloor,\>\>j=k-1,k-2,\ldots,1.

(4)

Since
F_{2k+2}=(F_{2k}+F_{2k-1})+F_{2k}
and
F_{2k}\leq x<F_{2k+2}
, we have
1\leq{\lambda}_{k}\leq 2
. For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
, since

{\lambda}_{j+1}F_{2j+2}\leq x-\sum_{i=j+2}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}<({\lambda}_{j+1}+1)F_{2j+2},

we have

0\leq x-\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}=x-\sum_{i=j+2}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}-{\lambda}_{j+1}F_{2j+2}<F_{2j+2},

so that

0\leq\frac{x-\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}}{F_{2j}}<\frac{F_{2j+2}}{F_{2j}}=2+\frac{F_{2j-1}}{F_{2j}}.

Thus,
{\lambda}_{j}\in\{0,1,2\}
.

Now

{\lambda}_{1}=\left\lfloor\frac{x-\sum_{i=2}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}}{F_{2}}\right\rfloor=x-\sum_{i=2}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i},

so
x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}
.

Define
s=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+2i}
. By Proposition
2.1
, part (ii),

s=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+2i}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+2}\,F_{2i}=V_{n+2}\,x\!\!\!\!\pmod{V_{n}}.

Since
{\lambda}_{k}\geq 1
, we have
s\geq V_{n+2k}
. To prove the upper bound for
s
, we consider two cases: (i)
{\lambda}_{k}=1
, and (ii)
{\lambda}_{k}=2
.

Case
(I): If
{\lambda}_{k}=1
, then

s\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}V_{n+2i}+V_{n+2k}\leq 2\big(V_{n+2k-1}-V_{n+1}\big)+V_{n+2k}<\big(V_{n+2k}+V_{n+2k-1}\big)+V_{n+2k-1}<V_{n+2k+2}

using Proposition
2.1
, part (iii).

Case
(II): Suppose
{\lambda}_{k}=2
. We claim that one of the following cases must arise: (i)
{\lambda}_{i}=1
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
; (ii) there exists
r\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
such that
{\lambda}_{r}=0
and
{\lambda}_{i}=1
for
i\in\{r+1,\ldots,k-1\}
.

If neither of these cases is true, then there must exist
t\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
such that
{\lambda}_{t}=2
and
{\lambda}_{i}=1
for
i\in\{t+1,\ldots,k-1\}
. But then

x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}\geq\sum_{i=t}^{k}F_{2i}+F_{2t}+F_{2k}=\big(F_{2k+1}-F_{2t-1}\big)+F_{2t}+F_{2k}\geq F_{2k+2}

using Proposition
2.1
, part (iii) with
V_{n}=F_{n}
. This contradiction proves the claim.

In case (i), we have

s=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+2i}=V_{n+2k}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{n+2i}=V_{n+2k}+\big(V_{n+2k-1}-V_{n+1}\big)<V_{n+2k+2}

using Proposition
2.1
, part (iii).

In case (ii),

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+2i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle V_{n+2k}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{k}V_{n+2i}+2\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}V_{n+2i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{n+2k}+\big(V_{n+2k+1}-V_{n+2r+1}\big)+2\big(V_{n+2r-1}-V_{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{n+2k+2}-\left(\big(V_{n+2r+1}-V_{n+2r-1}\big)-V_{n+2r-1}\right)-2V_{n+1}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle V_{n+2k+2}.

This completes the proof of the Proposition.

\blacksquare

Notation 4.2
.

The sequence

The following result proves that the Greedy Algorithm for an arbitrary positive integer

F_{2},F_{4},F_{6},\ldots

Theorem 4.4
.

For any sequence
{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{m}
of nonnegative integers, not all zero,

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+2i}.

Proof.

We induct on the sum
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}
. If
\sigma=1
, then
m={\alpha}_{1}=1
and the two sides are equal. For some positive integer
\sigma
, assume the result holds whenever the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}<\sigma
.

Using Proposition
4.1
, define the sequence
{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k}
for
x=\sigma
. Suppose
{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{m}
is any sequence of nonegative integers such that
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}
; we may assume that
{\alpha}_{m}\geq 1
. Note that
m\leq k
, for if
m>k
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}\geq F_{2k+2}>\sigma
.

If
m=k
, then
1\leq{\alpha}_{k}\leq{\lambda}_{k}
. By Induction Hypothesis,

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}-F_{2k}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+2i}-V_{n+2k}.

Since

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}-F_{2k}\right)=s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}-F_{2k}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}V_{n+2i}-V_{n+2k}=s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}\right)-V_{n+2k},

we have

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}\right)=s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}F_{2i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+2i}.

This proves the Proposition when
m=k
.

Suppose
m<k
. By Induction Hypothesis,

s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}-F_{2m}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+2i}-V_{n+2m}.

(5)

Two cases arise: (I)
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}-F_{2m}\geq F_{2k}

\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}-F_{2m}<F_{2k}

, and apply the case

discussed above, we get

from eqn. (
5
) and eqn. (
6
). This proves Case (i).

Case
(II): Suppose
\sigma-F_{2m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha}_{i}F_{2i}-F_{2m}<F_{2k}
. Since
\sigma-F_{2m}\geq F_{2k}-F_{2k-2}>F_{2k-2}
, the sequence determined by the Greedy Algorithm for
\sigma-F_{2m}
is

Note that
\sigma-F_{2k-2}
lies between
F_{2k}-F_{2k-2}=F_{2k-1}

\displaystyle\sigma-F_{2k-2}

Lemma 4.5
.

For any positive integer
m
,
s(m)<s(m+1)
.

Proof.

We induct on
m
. By Proposition
4.3
, part (i),
V_{n+2}=s(1)<s(2)=2V_{n+2}
. Assume
s(i-1)<s(i)
for
1\leq i\leq m
. If
m=F_{2k}-1
for some
k
, then
s(m)=V_{n+2k}-V_{n+1}<V_{n+2k}=s(m+1)
by Proposition
4.3
, part (i). Otherwise
F_{2k}\leq m<F_{2k+2}
, and so

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\gcd(V_{1},V_{2})=1
. The Apéry set for
S=\langle V_{n},V_{n+2},V_{n+4},\ldots\rangle

\text{Ap}(S,V_{n})=\{s(x):1\leq x\leq V_{n}-1\}\cup\{0\}.

For
x\in\{1,\ldots,V_{n}-1\}
, we show that
s(x)
is the least positive integer in
S
that is congruent to
V_{n+2}\,x
modulo
V_{n}
. This proves the result since
\{V_{n+2}\,x:1\leq x\leq V_{n}-1\}
is the set of non-zero residues modulo

as
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+2})=1
.

Suppose
s\in S
is congruent to
V_{n+2}\,x
modulo
V_{n}
. Then
s=\sum_{i\geq 0}{\alpha}_{i}V_{n+2i}
, with each
{\alpha}_{i}\geq 0
. Since

, we have

, so that

by Theorem
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
.

V_{n+2}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mu}_{i}F_{2i}\equiv F_{2k}V_{n+2}\!\!\!\!\pmod{V_{n}}.

Since
\gcd(V_{n},V_{n+2})=1
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mu}_{i}F_{2i}\equiv F_{2k}\!\!\!\!\pmod{V_{n}}.

Since
k\leq\kappa-1
, we have
F_{2k}\leq F_{2(\kappa-1)}<V_{n}
, and so
\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mu}_{i}F_{2i}\geq F_{2k}
. By Theorem
4.4
, Proposition
4.5
and eqn. (
8
), we have

V_{n+2k}=s\left(F_{2k}\right)\leq s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mu}_{i}F_{2i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\mu}_{i}V_{n+2i}=V_{n+2k}.

Therefore, by Proposition
4.5
,

F_{2k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mu}_{i}F_{2i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mu}_{i}F_{2i},

where
m
be the largest integer for which
{\mu}_{m}>0
. Then by Theorem
4.4

s\left(F_{2k}-F_{2m}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mu}_{i}V_{n+2i}-V_{n+2m}.

Since
F_{2k}-F_{2m}\geq F_{2k}-F_{2k-2}>F_{2k-2}
, the Greedy Algorithm applied to
F_{2k}-F_{2m}
yields a sequence
{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,{\lambda}_{k-1}
, with
{\lambda}_{k-1}>0
. Applying Theorem
4.4
to the sequence obtained by adding
1
to
{\lambda}_{m}
and subtracting
1
from
{\lambda}_{k-1}
yields

s\left(F_{2k}-F_{2k-2}\right)\leq s\left(F_{2k}-F_{2m}\right)+V_{n+2m}-V_{n+2k-2}.

Hence

\displaystyle V_{n+2k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mu}_{i}V_{n+2i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle s\left(F_{2k}-F_{2m}\right)+V_{n+2m}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle s\left(F_{2k}-F_{2k-2}\right)+V_{n+2k-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\left(F_{2k-1}\right)+V_{n+2k-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{n+2k}+V_{n}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle V_{n+2k}.

This contradiction completes the proof of the Proposition.

\blacksquare

Corollary 4.10
.

(i)

If
S=\langle F_{n},F_{n+2},F_{n+4},\ldots\rangle
,
n\geq 3
, then
\textbf{e}(S)=\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor
.

(ii)

If
S=\langle L_{n},L_{n+2},L_{n+4},\ldots\rangle
,
n\geq 4
, then
\textbf{e}(S)=\left\lfloor\frac{n+3}{2}\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

This is a direct application of Proposition
4.9
.

(i)

Note that
F_{2\kappa-2}\leq F_{2\kappa-1}-1<F_{2\kappa}
and
F_{2\kappa-2}<F_{2\kappa}-1<F_{2\kappa}
. Thus,
F_{n}-1\in\big[F_{2\kappa-2},F_{2\kappa}\big)
both when
n=2\kappa-1
and when
n=2\kappa
. Hence
\kappa=\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor
.

(ii)

Since
L_{n}=F_{n+1}+F_{n-1}
, we have
F_{2\kappa}\leq L_{2\kappa-1}-1=F_{2\kappa}+F_{2\kappa-2}-1<F_{2\kappa+2}
and
F_{2\kappa}\leq L_{2\kappa}-1=F_{2\kappa+1}+F_{2\kappa-1}-1<F_{2\kappa+2}
. Thus,
L_{n}-1\in\big[F_{2\kappa},F_{2\kappa+2}\big)
both when
n=2\kappa-1
and when
n=2\kappa
. Hence
\kappa=\left\lfloor\frac{n+3}{2}\right\rfloor
.

\blacksquare

Computation of
\textbf{g}(S)
is difficult in the general case. In the following result we compute the genus in the special case of Fibonacci subsequences. The result is in terms of the
k^{\text{th}}
term of sequence that satisfies a second order recurrence, and that can be explicitly solved.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\{{\sigma}_{k}\}
be the second order recurrence given by

{\sigma}_{k}=3{\sigma}_{k-1}-{\sigma}_{k-2}+F_{n+4k-1}+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k},\quad k\geq 2,

with
{\sigma}_{0}=0
and
{\sigma}_{1}=3F_{n+2}
. Let
\{{\rho}_{k}\}
be the related second order recurrence given by

{\rho}_{k}={\sigma}_{k}-2{\sigma}_{k-1}-F_{2k+1}F_{n+2k},\quad k\geq 2.

If
S=\langle F_{n},F_{n+2},F_{n+4},\ldots\rangle
and
\textbf{e}(S)=\kappa
, then

\textbf{g}(S)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{{\sigma}_{\kappa-2}+{\rho}_{\kappa-1}}{F_{n}}-\displaystyle\frac{F_{n}-1}{2}&\mbox{ if $n=2\kappa-1$};\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{{\sigma}_{\kappa-1}}{F_{n}}-\displaystyle\frac{F_{n}-1}{2}&\mbox{ if $n=2\kappa$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Applying Theorem
4.7
, part (ii) to
V_{n}=F_{n}
, we must show

\sum_{x=1}^{F_{n}-1}s(x)=\begin{cases}{\sigma}_{\kappa-2}+{\rho}_{\kappa-1}&\mbox{ if $n=2\kappa-1$};\\
{\sigma}_{\kappa-1}&\mbox{ if $n=2\kappa$}.\end{cases}

For positive integer
k
and
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, define

{\rho}_{k,\ell}=\sum_{x=F_{2k}}^{F_{2k+\ell}-1}s(x),\quad{\sigma}_{k}=\sum_{x=1}^{k}{\rho}_{x,2}=\sum_{x=1}^{F_{2k+2}-1}s(x).

(9)

Fix
x\in\{1,\ldots,F_{n}-1\}
, and let
k
be defined by
F_{2k}\leq x<F_{2k+2}
. Then
{\lambda}_{k}\geq 1
by the Greedy Algorithm, so that
s(x)=s(x-F_{2k})+F_{n+2k}
. Hence

{\rho}_{k,2}=\sum_{x=F_{2k}}^{F_{2k+2}-1}\big(s(x-F_{2k})+F_{n+2k}\big)=\left(\sum_{x=1}^{F_{2k+1}-1}s(x)\right)+F_{2k+1}F_{n+2k}={\sigma}_{k-1}+{\rho}_{k,1}+F_{2k+1}F_{n+2k},

so that

{\sigma}_{k}={\sigma}_{k-1}+{\rho}_{k,2}={\sigma}_{k-1}+{\sigma}_{k-1}+{\rho}_{k,1}+F_{2k+1}F_{n+2k}=2{\sigma}_{k-1}+{\rho}_{k,1}+F_{2k+1}F_{n+2k}.

(10)

Similarly

{\rho}_{k,1}=\sum_{x=F_{2k}}^{F_{2k+1}-1}\big(s(x-F_{2k})+F_{n+2k}\big)=\left(\sum_{x=1}^{F_{2k-1}-1}s(x)\right)+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k}={\sigma}_{k-2}+{\rho}_{k-1,1}+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k}.

(11)

Replacing
k
by
k-1
in eqn. (
10
), we get

{\sigma}_{k-1}=2{\sigma}_{k-2}+{\rho}_{k-1,1}+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k-2}.

(12)

From eqns. (
10
), (
11
), (
12
), and using Proposition
2.1
, part (i), we have

\displaystyle{\sigma}_{k}-{\sigma}_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\sigma}_{k-1}-2{\sigma}_{k-2}+\big({\rho}_{k,1}-{\rho}_{k-1,1}\big)+\big(F_{2k+1}F_{n+2k}-F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k-2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\sigma}_{k-1}-{\sigma}_{k-2}+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k}+\big(F_{2k+1}F_{n+2k}+F_{2k}F_{n+2k-1}\big)

\displaystyle-\big(F_{2k}F_{n+2k-1}+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k-2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\sigma}_{k-1}-{\sigma}_{k-2}+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k}+F_{n+4k}-F_{n+4k-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\sigma}_{k-1}-{\sigma}_{k-2}+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k}+F_{n+4k-1},

so that

{\sigma}_{k}=3{\sigma}_{k-1}-{\sigma}_{k-2}+F_{2k-1}F_{n+2k}+F_{n+4k-1}.

(13)

Thus,
\{{\sigma}_{k}\}
satisfies the recurrence given in the Theorem. Moreover,
{\sigma}_{0}=0
and
{\sigma}_{1}=s(1)+s(2)=F_{n+2}+2F_{n+2}=3F_{n+2}
.

Now

{\rho}_{k,1}={\sigma}_{k}-2{\sigma}_{k-1}-F_{2k+1}F_{n+2k}

can be used to determine
{\rho}_{k,1}
from the sequence
\{{\sigma}_{k}\}
; this is the recurrence for the sequence
\{{\rho}_{k}\}
with
{\rho}_{k}
being used for
{\rho}_{k,1}
here.

Recall that
\textbf{e}(S)=\kappa
where
F_{2(\kappa-1)}\leq F_{n}-1<F_{2\kappa}
by Theorem
4.9
. If
n=2\kappa
, then
\sum_{x=1}^{F_{n}-1}s(x)=\sum_{x=1}^{F_{2\kappa}-1}s(x)={\sigma}_{\kappa-1}
. If
n=2\kappa-1
, then
\sum_{x=1}^{F_{n}-1}s(x)=\sum_{x=1}^{F_{2\kappa-1}-1}s(x)={\sigma}_{\kappa-2}+{\rho}_{k-1,1}
.

This completes the proof of the Theorem.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.12
.

Theorem
4.11
provides a formula for
\textbf{g}(S)
when
S
is generated by the Fibonacci subsequence. This formula is in terms of
{\sigma}_{k}
and
{\rho}_{k}
, both of which can be determined in closed form because the recurrence has constant coefficients and the non-homogenous part is a linear combinations of powers of distinct real numbers. However, this expression is not particularly simple and so has been omitted here.

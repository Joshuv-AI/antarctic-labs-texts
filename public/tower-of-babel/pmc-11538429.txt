Influence of speed and line spacing on aerodynamic forces in high speed train passing in tunnels
Authors: Xiuping Duan, Yuangui Mei, Xiao Hu, Changbao Qi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
As train speeds increase, the aerodynamic effects generated during the passage of high-speed trains in tunnels become more pronounced. This article investigates the influence of line spacing on the aerodynamic forces during the passage of high-speed trains in tunnels at different speeds. The research methodology is based on the three-dimensional transient compressible Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) equations and the k-ω Shear Stress Transport (SST) turbulence model, using overset grid technology to simulate the train passing process in the tunnel. The results show that the aerodynamic drag, lateral force, and lift experienced by the train increase as the line spacing decreases and as the speed increases. line spacing has the greatest impact on aerodynamic lift, followed by lateral force, with drag having the smallest impact. Compared to line spacing, speed has a much larger effect on aerodynamic forces. The aerodynamic forces experienced by the train during tunnel passage are proportional to the square of the train’s speed and have an exponential relationship with the line spacing during train passage.

Introduction

With the rapid development of high-speed railways, the operating speed of high-speed trains continues to increase, significantly enhancing transportation efficiency and passenger experience. However, the increase in train speeds also brings numerous technical challenges. One such challenge is the increasingly pronounced aerodynamic effects when high-speed trains pass each other in tunnels. The pressure waves generated by trains meeting within a tunnel are constrained by the limited tunnel space, leading to more intense aerodynamic effects1–4. Several factors influence the aerodynamic effects of high-speed trains passing each other in tunnels, including train speed, tunnel cross-sectional shape, tunnel length, and line spacing. Line spacing, determined by parameters such as aerodynamic effects during train passing, is an important factor that impacts track layout, construction volume of tunnel engineering, and investment costs. It also significantly affects the magnitude, direction, and variation patterns of aerodynamic drag, lateral force, and lift experienced by trains when passing in tunnels. Unreasonable line spacing can increase peak aerodynamic forces during passing, worsen tunnel pressure fluctuations, enhance the impact hazards on trains and tunnel structures, and compromise train operation safety and passenger comfort5–9.

Scholars both domestically and internationally have conducted extensive research on the aerodynamic effects of high-speed trains passing in tunnels using methods such as model experiments, full-scale vehicle tests, and numerical calculations10–15. Johnson et al16. used model experiments to study the impact of parameters such as train speed, line spacing, and nose length on tunnel pressure waves during train passing. Suzuki et al17–19. designed wind tunnel experiments under different conditions to study the aerodynamic forces on high-speed trains (windshields, pantographs, and car bodies). Adamu et al20,21. investigated the impact of components like windshields and pantographs on the aerodynamic drag of high-speed trains. Tian Hongqi et al22–26. used experiments and simulations to study the impact of line spacing and train speed on open line passing pressure waves. Zhou Miaomiao et al27–29. investigated the characteristics of pressure wave impacts under different speeds on trains in tunnels. Qiao Yingjun et al30. derived fitting curves for the relationship between train passing pressure waves and line spacing and predicted pressure wave curves for train passing at speeds of 250 km/h and 350 km/h. Zhang Lei et al31. studied the impact of line spacings of 5.0 m, 5.2 m, and 5.4 m on pressure waves generated when trains pass at 400 km/h in tunnels.

In tunnel environments, due to the enclosed space and high-speed characteristics of trains, when two trains travel in opposite directions, complex aerodynamic forces are instantly generated32–35. Previous research mainly focused on the aerodynamic characteristics of a single train passing through a tunnel or wind tunnel experiments under static conditions. Some studies on aerodynamic effects of trains passing in tunnels primarily concentrated on tunnel pressure waves, predicting or simulating the impact of higher line spacing. However, there is a lack of research on the impact of different line spacings on aerodynamic forces when high-speed trains pass each other in tunnels. In actual operations, the relative speed of trains can reach several hundred kilometers per hour, resulting in more intense and unpredictable aerodynamic force fluctuations during passing. Therefore, systematic research on the impact of line spacing and speed on aerodynamic forces during high-speed train passing in tunnels is necessary.

This study aims to use numerical simulation methods, employing three-dimensional numerical simulation software to establish a train-tunnel coupling model. Using computational fluid dynamics (CFD) technology and a three-dimensional transient compressible flow turbulence model, it simulates the dynamic changes in the flow field during train passing. The focus is on investigating the mechanism of action of line spacing on passing aerodynamic forces. The study will deeply analyze the impact of different line spacings and speeds on the aerodynamic characteristics of high-speed trains passing in tunnels. The results will provide theoretical support and technical guidance for high-speed railway tunnel design, reasonable selection of line spacing, optimization of train operation plans, and structural optimization of high-speed train bodies, promoting the safe, stable, and efficient operation of high-speed railway systems.

The structure of this paper is as follows: First, the theoretical background and relevant literature review of the study are introduced. Next, the computational model, mesh generation, and mathematical model selection for the numerical simulation are described in detail. Then, the numerical simulation methods, overset mesh techniques, and mesh generation methods used in this study are validated. Following this, the numerical simulation results are analyzed, and the specific impact characteristics of line spacing on passing aerodynamic forces are discussed. Finally, the numerical simulation results are summarized, revealing the patterns discovered through the research. Through this study, we hope to provide new insights and solutions to the problem of aerodynamic forces during high-speed train passing in tunnels.

Grid division

The computational model was meshed using Star-ccm+, employing an unstructured hybrid mesh (Trim mesh and Prism mesh). For the train model, a prismatic layer mesh (Prism mesh) was used, while the external flow field of the train was meshed using a trimmed cell mesh (Trim mesh). During high-speed motion of the train, significant changes occur in the flow field near components such as bogies and windshields. A refinement of 400 mm was applied around the flow field of passing trains, and a refinement of 200 mm was set in the bogie area. The flow field near the train head and tail experiences large gradient changes; thus, three layers of refinement zones were set, with 100 mm, 200 mm, and 400 mm moving outward from the train head area. To better simulate the boundary layer flow field of the train, a stretched prismatic layer mesh design was applied to the train model. Due to the large gradient changes in the flow field within the boundary layer on the train body, the wall y + was set to 50, and the boundary layer stretching was set to 8 layers, with the first near-wall layer having a mesh size of 0.582 mm. To accurately capture the complex flow field information during train tunnel interactions while reducing computational costs, the final time step was determined to be 0.005s based on the computational conditions and the convergence of preliminary residuals in this study. The total number of computational model grids is 46 million. The computational grid for the train model is shown in Fig. 5, while the overset mesh and background mesh are shown in Fig. 6.

Mathematical model

During the encounter of high-speed trains in tunnels, the air disturbance is extremely severe. The reciprocating propagation of compression and expansion waves formed when two trains enter the tunnel superimposes, resulting in a highly complex aerodynamic flow field. The Reynolds number (Re) of the flow field is greater than 1 × 10^6, indicating turbulent flow. Therefore, the computational model adopts the k-ω SST two-equation model to address the turbulence problem during train encounters in tunnels. By solving the three-dimensional transient compressible Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) equations and the k-ωSST two-equation turbulence model37, the aerodynamic force changes during train encounters are calculated.

Overset mesh calculation method

Grid generation

First, use the overset mesh method to construct a background region that encompasses the entire solution domain. Two overlapping regions are built on either side of the background area, containing the high-speed train being studied, as shown in Fig. 7. During grid generation, the grids for the background region and the overlapping regions are created independently.

Fig. 7Schematic diagram of the overset mesh method for train tunnel intersections.

Schematic diagram of the overset mesh method for train tunnel intersections.

Grid assembly

The “hole drilling” and “interpolation” processes of the overlapping grid method are illustrated in Fig. 8. Figure 8(a) shows the initial state without interpolation, where the red solid line grid represents active units in the background region, and the blue dashed line grid indicates active units in the overlapping region. Active units participate in solving the discrete equation system. Figure 8(b) depicts the grid assembly during interpolation, with the blue and red dashed grids at “O” representing interpolation units belonging to the background and overlapping regions, respectively. Their purpose is to couple the discrete equation systems of the two regions while separating active and inactive units. Compared to Fig. 8(a), the red solid line grid that disappears in the lower right corner of Fig. 8(b) represents the inactive units, which do not participate in solving any equations. When the overlapping region moves in a certain direction, the previously disappeared inactive units reappear and become active units again.

Fig. 8Schematic diagram of overlapping grid method assembly and interpolation: (a) Initial State. (b) Grid Assembly.

Schematic diagram of overlapping grid method assembly and interpolation: (a) Initial State. (b) Grid Assembly.

Interpolation calculation

First, identify the contribution units. The interpolation units and contribution units belong to the overset region (or background region) and background region (or overset region) respectively, making them relative to each other. For example, in the upper left corner of Fig. 8(b), the three red grids D1, D2, and D3 surrounding the blue hollow “O” interpolation unit in the overset region are its corresponding contribution units from the background region.

Next, establish the interpolation relationships for the calculation. The computational fluid dynamics software used in this study provides three interpolation methods: linear, weighted distance, and least squares. Although linear interpolation consumes more computational resources, it offers relatively higher accuracy.

Finally, solve the discrete equation system. The grids of active units across the entire area will be computed simultaneously. After interpolation calculations, the variable values from several contribution units around the interpolation unit grid are passed to the interpolation unit, followed by solving the coefficient matrix of the algebraic equation system. Continuous interpolation transfers flow field information between the background and overset regions, resulting in lower discretization errors. Therefore, the convergence speed is comparable to that of using a single set of grids.

Grid update

After the numerical solution of the flow field converges at the corresponding time, the overset mesh region is moved according to pre-defined motion rules. Then, grid assembly is repeated to update the computational grid for the next time step, followed by continuing the interpolation calculations. This process is repeated until all required time steps in the computation are completed.

Analysis of calculation results

Train pressure field variation diagram

Figure 11 shows the pressure field in the tunnel and the aerodynamic force cloud diagram experienced by two trains during their meeting in the tunnel.

Fig. 11Pressure cloud map during train tunnel intersection process: (a) Head-to-head intersection process of trains. (b) Head-to-tail intersection process of trains. (c) Tail-to-tail separation pprocess of trains.

Pressure cloud map during train tunnel intersection process: (a) Head-to-head intersection process of trains. (b) Head-to-tail intersection process of trains. (c) Tail-to-tail separation pprocess of trains.

Based on the figure, during the head-to-head intersection process of trains in the tunnel, the pressure at the nose cone compresses the air in front of the train, resulting in a red zone where pressure is highest. The positive pressure area radiates outward from the nose cone, gradually decreasing in pressure. Throughout the dynamic process of the head-to-head intersection, the pressure at the nose cone gradually decreases, causing the positive pressure area to shrink rapidly, while the negative pressure zone expands quickly in all directions. After the intersection, the overlapping area of the trains experiences a sharp drop to a lower negative pressure zone. The pressure around the train decreases from the front to the rear, and during the head-to-head intersection, the overall body of the train experiences a negative pressure phenomenon due to the influence of the train expansion wave. As the pressure in the area in front of the train decreases, the aerodynamic drag caused by the pressure difference from front to rear drops quickly. The positive pressure area created at the nose cone results in higher lateral pressure on the intersection side compared to the non-intersection side, leading to a “repulsive force” acting on the train during the head-to-head intersection.

In the head-to-tail intersection process within the tunnel, before the two trains overlap, the pressure at the nose cone gradually increases, with the positive pressure area expanding outward from the nose. The negative pressure area at the tail decreases, resulting in an increasing resistance due to the pressure difference between the front and rear of the train. The lateral pressure on the intersection side is affected by the decreasing negative pressure area, enhancing the “suction” effect experienced during the head-to-tail intersection. When the two trains are completely overlapped, the pressure at the nose cones rapidly increases again, while the pressure in the low-pressure area at the rear drops quickly, causing the differential pressure resistance to increase sharply. At this moment, the negative pressure area on the intersection side continues to decline, leading to an instantaneous peak in the “suction” effect when fully overlapped. Upon separation after overlapping, the positive pressure area at the front continues to rise, while the negative pressure area at the rear decreases, causing the differential pressure resistance to remain elevated. The negative pressure area on the non-intersection side also decreases, reducing the “suction” effect. Throughout the head-to-tail intersection process, both trains exhibit a very low negative pressure area around their bodies.

During the tail-to-tail separation process in the tunnel, before the two trains completely separate, the negative pressure area in the overlapping region at the rear gradually increases, reducing the differential pressure resistance. Once both trains exit the intersection area, the pressure in the rear quickly rises, leading to a rapid decrease in the differential pressure resistance at the front and rear. As the tails gradually separate, a higher positive pressure area appears between them, causing the differential pressure resistance to continue decreasing. Throughout the tail-to-tail separation process, the pressure around the train increases continuously, and the pressure cloud map indicates that the lateral force changes are not significant during the intersection process.

From Fig. 11, it can be observed that during the head-to-head intersection process in the tunnel, the rapid decrease in pressure in the area in front of the train quickly reduces the aerodynamic drag caused by the pressure difference between the front and rear. As the positive pressure area created by the nose cone approaches during the intersection, the lateral pressure on the intersection side exceeds that on the non-intersection side, leading to a rapid increase in the “repulsive force.” During the head-to-tail intersection in the tunnel, the rapid rise in pressure in the positive pressure area in front of the train quickly increases the pressure differential at the ends, sharply increasing the differential pressure resistance experienced by the train. The rapid decrease in lateral pressure during the intersection also leads to a swift increase in the “suction” effect. In the tail-to-tail separation process in the tunnel, as the tail cone moves away from the intersection area, the pressure in the rear area rises rapidly, resulting in a sharp increase in the differential pressure, which leads to a quick decrease in aerodynamic drag; the pressure cloud changes during the tail-to-tail separation process are not significant.

Study on the influence of aerodynamic drag

To analyze the effect of line spacing on the variation of aerodynamic drag during train passing in tunnels, the train at the speed level of 350 km/h is selected as the research object. The changes in aerodynamic drag during tunnel passing are studied for three line spacing scenarios (4.6 m, 4.8 m, and 5.0 m). Figure 12 depicts the characteristic curves of the influence of line spacing on aerodynamic drag during train passing in tunnels. Fd represents the aerodynamic drag experienced by the train.

Fig. 12Characteristic curve of the influence of line spacing on train aerodynamic drag.

Characteristic curve of the influence of line spacing on train aerodynamic drag.

At time 0s (N moment) is defined as the moment when the train nose enters the tunnel, NN represents the moment when the heads of two trains meet, NT represents the moment when the head and tail of the train meet, and TT represents the moment when the tails of two trains separate. From Fig. 12, it can be observed that when the train runs on the open track, the drag of the train under different line spacings is basically the same, with minimal impact from line spacing. During the train passing in the tunnel, the variation trend of the train drag under different line spacings is basically consistent. Throughout the passing process, the influence of line spacing on the train drag is not significant. After the train enters the tunnel, the pressure waves are constrained by the space, leading to an increase in aerodynamic drag due to the superimposed compression waves. Around t = 2.84s, the aerodynamic drag reaches its maximum value. Subsequently, as the expansion wave from the tail passes the head, the pressure difference drag between the head and tail begins to decrease, with the minimum drag occurring just before the head-to-tail intersection of the two trains. The smaller the line spacing, the larger the peak drag during train passing, and the more significant the drag variation during the passing process.

Results

This study investigates the influence characteristics of aerodynamic forces during the passing process of trains at speeds of 250 km/h, 350 km/h, and 400 km/h with three different line spacings (4.6 m, 4.8 m, 5.0 m). The following conclusions are drawn:

At the same speed, the maximum and peak-to-peak values of the resistance experienced by the train increase with decreasing line spacing. The peak values and peak-to-peak values of the lateral force experienced by the train also increase with decreasing line spacing. The minimum value of the downward force experienced by the train decreases with decreasing line spacing, while the maximum value and peak-to-peak value of the downward force increase with decreasing line spacing. Taking a speed of 350 km/h as a reference, the peak-to-peak values of resistance increase by 1.85% and 2.71% respectively for line spacings of 4.8 m and 4.6 m compared to 5.0 m. The peak-to-peak values of lateral force increase by 20.10% and 42.91% respectively for line spacings of 4.8 m and 4.6 m compared to 5.0 m. The peak-to-peak values of downward force increase by 41.39% and 86.05% respectively for line spacings of 4.8 m and 4.6 m compared to 5.0 m.At the same line spacing, the maximum and peak-to-peak values of aerodynamic resistance, lateral force, and downward force experienced by the train increase with increasing speed.

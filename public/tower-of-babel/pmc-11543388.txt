Neurophysiological Tests in a Myasthenia Gravis Patient With Muscle-Specific Kinase Antibody Accompanied by Myotonic Discharges
Authors: Sevim Gökmen, Halit Fidancı, Halil Can Alaydın, Elif Banu Söker
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Only small numbers of cases and case series have been reported for myotonic discharges (MDs) in myasthenia gravis (MG) patients with positivity for muscle-specific kinase antibody (MuSK-Ab),1234 which contrasts with other MG patients. Since these case reports have had deficiencies in detailed electrodiagnostic tests such as single-fiber electromyography (EMG) and exercise tests, here we present a MuSK-Ab+ MG patient with the corresponding electrodiagnostic features.

A 34-year-old female patient was referred to the neurology clinic with a 3-month history of dysphagia, diplopia, and ptosis. She was not receiving any treatment and had no history of chronic disease. A neurological examination revealed hypophonia and bilateral limitation of external gaze. There was no abnormality in the complete blood count or serum biochemical tests. Genetic testing for myotonic dystrophy type-1 and type-2 was negative. While the acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibody (AChR-Ab) was absent in the patient, MuSK-Ab was present. Antibodies for paraneoplastic syndrome were not found.

The patient underwent repetitive nerve stimulation (RNS), jitter analysis with concentric needle electrodes, long and short exercise tests, and needle EMG as recommended previously. 567 The amplitude of the fourth compound muscle action potential (CMAP) was >10% lower than the first CMAP amplitude during RNS of the facial nerve recorded from the left orbicularis oculi muscle, and of the ulnar nerve recorded from abductor digiti quinti (ADQ) muscle at 2, 3, 5, and 50 Hz (Fig. 1A). No facilitation in the ulnar nerve CMAP was found after 30 seconds of exercise of the left ADQ muscle. No abnormalities were found in long and short exercise tests applied to the left ADQ muscle. Jitter analysis of the left frontalis muscle with a concentric needle electrode during voluntary contraction revealed a mean consecutive difference (MCD) of >38 µs in 10 of 20 apparent single-fiber action potentials (ASFAPs). Blocks were found in 10 of 20 ASFAPs. The mean MCD of all ASFAP pairs was 55.8 µs. Fig. 1B shows ASFAPs with abnormal jitter. MDs at 60 Hz were observed in the left abductor pollicis brevis and tibialis anterior muscles (Fig. 1C), but myopathic motor-unit action potentials were not present. There was no needle EMG abnormality in the left first dorsal interosseous, biceps brachii, or vastus lateralis muscles. Thorax computed tomography showed no thymoma.

MDs have been reported only in the present case and a few previous cases of MuSK-Ab+ MG.234 This rare entity may be explained by muscle membrane instability in the pathophysiology of myotonia.234 MuSK-Abs may cause muscle fiber membrane instability and AChR clustering impairment. An increased membrane instability can result in MDs occurring.234 In the present case, the presence of jitter abnormality and blocks in jitter analysis support this hypothesis. Unlike in AChR-Ab+ MG patients, electrical MDs have been reported in MuSK-Ab+ MG patients;234 the only exception was 1 out 44 AChR-Ab+ MG patients reported by Skolka et al.2 The presence of electrical MDs in MuSK-Ab+ MG patients but not in AChR-Ab+ MG patients suggests that MuSK-Ab+ MG and AChR-Ab+ MG have differing underlying pathophysiologies.

The occurrence of MDs in MuSK-Ab+ MG may be due to membrane changes causing disruptions in sodium and/or chloride channels.234 Similar to myotonia-associated muscle channelopathies, muscle membrane-channel disruptions may explain MDs in MuSK-Ab+ MG. However, in the present case, no abnormality was found in long and short exercise tests, which is one of the electrodiagnostic tests used to diagnose muscle channelopathies.7 This finding may indicate that defects of sodium and/or chloride channels are not solely responsible for MuSK-Ab+ MG.

In addition to MDs, myokymia, fasciculation, and neuromyotonic discharges have been reported in MG and myasthenia.8910 These discharges indicate peripheral nerve hyperexcitability, and they may be related to pyridostigmine treatment as well as thymoma.810 However, Simon et al.10 reported that neuromyotonia may be present in MuSK-Ab+ MG patients without thymoma. Notably, there was no thymoma in the present case.

The electrodiagnostic tests including RNS, jitter analysis, needle EMG, and exercise tests performed in the present case were more detailed than those applied for previous case reports. This case report shows that jitter abnormalities in addition to RNS abnormalities may be present in MuSK-Ab+ MG accompanied by MDs. The absence of abnormalities in the exercise tests may indicate that abnormalities such as defects of sodium or chloride channels in the muscle membrane do not play a major role in the pathophysiology of MuSK-Ab+ MG. Studies that apply exercise tests to MG patients and analyze needle EMG data in MG patients carrying antibodies other than MuSK-Ab and AChR-Ab may help to improve the understanding of MG pathophysiology.

A multi-dimensional evaluation model for power enterprise procurement performance based on fuzzy analytic hierarchy process and TOPSIS integration
Authors: Haigang Zhao, Shugui Song, Xiaoqing Lv, Yu Bao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Power enterprises face unique procurement challenges, including grid reliability requirements, regulatory complexities, and long-term asset investment considerations that existing evaluation methods inadequately address. Traditional approaches suffer from three critical limitations: inability to handle linguistic uncertainty (consistency ratios often exceeding 0.15), failure to integrate power-specific criteria simultaneously, and lack of systematic frameworks for aggregating conflicting stakeholder objectives. This study develops a comprehensive multi-dimensional evaluation model by integrating Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP) and TOPSIS methodologies specifically tailored for power enterprise contexts. The proposed framework achieves 23% improved consistency ratios compared to traditional AHP methods while systematically addressing uncertainty in expert judgments. The model constructs a hierarchical evaluation system encompassing four primary dimensions: cost-benefit performance, quality management, supplier relationship management, and risk control. FAHP handles subjective expert judgments and linguistic uncertainties in weight determination, while TOPSIS provides robust ranking capabilities through distance-based proximity measures. Empirical validation through a large regional power enterprise case study demonstrates model effectiveness. Results show a comprehensive closeness coefficient of 0.6651, positioning the enterprise at the 73rd industry percentile with strengths in quality management and improvement opportunities in supplier relationship development. Multi-dimensional sensitivity analysis across five uncertainty sources confirms model robustness, with coefficient variations within ± 5.7% and 96.3% ranking stability. Cross-validation with three peer enterprises (closeness coefficients ranging 0.5834–0.7245) demonstrates 91.3% performance gap identification accuracy. The integrated framework provides enhanced analytical capabilities for strategic procurement decision-making and continuous improvement initiatives in power enterprises.

Introduction

As critical infrastructure providers in the national economy, power enterprises face increasingly complex procurement environments that demand sophisticated performance evaluation systems1. The procurement activities of power enterprises encompass a wide range of materials and services, including electrical equipment, construction materials, maintenance services, and technical consulting, which collectively account for substantial portions of their operational expenditures2. Effective procurement performance evaluation has become a strategic imperative for power enterprises to enhance operational efficiency, reduce costs, and maintain competitive advantages in the evolving energy market.

Importance and practical significance of power enterprise procurement performance evaluation

The significance of procurement performance evaluation in power enterprises extends beyond simple cost control to encompass multiple dimensions of organizational effectiveness. Modern power enterprises operate in highly regulated environments where procurement decisions directly impact service reliability, environmental compliance, and financial sustainability3. The evaluation of procurement performance serves as a critical management tool for identifying inefficiencies, optimizing supplier relationships, and ensuring alignment with corporate strategic objectives. Furthermore, as power enterprises face increasing pressure to adopt sustainable practices and digital transformation initiatives, comprehensive procurement performance evaluation becomes essential for measuring progress toward these goals and facilitating continuous improvement processes.

Current challenges in power enterprise procurement management

Contemporary power enterprise procurement management encounters numerous challenges that complicate traditional evaluation approaches. The increasing complexity of supply chains, driven by globalization and technological advancement, creates uncertainties in supplier selection and performance monitoring4. Power enterprises must navigate volatile market conditions, fluctuating material prices, and evolving regulatory requirements while maintaining stringent quality and safety standards. Additionally, the integration of renewable energy sources and smart grid technologies introduces new procurement categories and evaluation criteria that traditional systems struggle to accommodate. The COVID-19 pandemic has further highlighted supply chain vulnerabilities and the need for more resilient and adaptive procurement strategies5.

Limitations of traditional procurement performance evaluation methods

Traditional procurement performance evaluation methods employed by power enterprises face three critical limitations that have been empirically documented in recent industry studies. First, these methods demonstrate inability to handle linguistic uncertainty in expert assessments, leading to inconsistent decision-making with consistency ratios often exceeding 0.15, as reported in cross-national studies involving 150 power companies51,52. Second, conventional approaches fail to integrate power enterprise-specific criteria such as grid reliability requirements, regulatory compliance standards, and long-term asset performance simultaneously within a unified evaluation framework. Third, existing methods lack systematic approaches for aggregating conflicting objectives across diverse stakeholder perspectives including technical specialists, financial managers, and regulatory compliance officers. These deficiencies result in suboptimal procurement decisions, with industry benchmarking data indicating that 35% of power enterprises report inadequate supplier performance attribution to evaluation system limitations. The proposed FAHP-TOPSIS integration addresses these gaps through triangular fuzzy number representation of linguistic variables, hierarchical decomposition of power-specific criteria, and distance-based multi-criteria aggregation, achieving demonstrable improvements in consistency ratios (CR reduction from 0.142 in traditional AHP to 0.087 in FAHP, representing 39% improvement) and decision stability (96.3% ranking consistency across sensitivity scenarios) as validated through comparative experiments presented in Section Empirical analysis.

The inadequacy of traditional evaluation methods becomes particularly pronounced when addressing complex procurement scenarios involving multiple conflicting objectives and uncertain information. Power enterprises require evaluation systems capable of handling qualitative assessments, expert judgments, and incomplete data while providing transparent and defensible decision-making processes. Moreover, traditional methods often fail to provide actionable insights for procurement strategy optimization and continuous improvement initiatives.

Necessity of constructing Multi-dimensional evaluation model

The construction of a multi-dimensional evaluation model based on Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP) and Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) addresses the identified limitations of traditional approaches while meeting the evolving needs of power enterprise procurement management. The integration of fuzzy logic principles enables the systematic handling of linguistic variables and uncertain information commonly encountered in procurement evaluation contexts7. The hierarchical structure of AHP facilitates the decomposition of complex procurement performance evaluation into manageable sub-criteria while maintaining logical consistency and stakeholder engagement throughout the evaluation process.

The TOPSIS methodology provides a robust framework for ranking alternative procurement strategies or suppliers by considering their relative distances from ideal and negative-ideal solutions across multiple evaluation dimensions. This approach enables power enterprises to make informed procurement decisions that balance competing objectives while providing clear justification for selected alternatives. The combination of FAHP and TOPSIS creates a comprehensive evaluation framework capable of addressing the multi-dimensional, uncertain, and complex nature of power enterprise procurement performance evaluation.

Research objectives and significance

This research aims to develop and validate a comprehensive multi-dimensional evaluation model for power enterprise procurement performance that integrates FAHP and TOPSIS methodologies. The primary objectives include: (1) identifying and structuring key evaluation criteria for power enterprise procurement performance; (2) developing a systematic approach for handling uncertainty and subjectivity in procurement evaluation; (3) creating an integrated evaluation framework that balances multiple stakeholder perspectives and objectives; and (4) providing practical guidance for implementation in real-world power enterprise contexts.

The significance of this research lies in its potential to enhance procurement decision-making effectiveness, improve supplier relationship management, and support strategic alignment between procurement activities and organizational objectives8. The developed model contributes to the theoretical advancement of procurement performance evaluation while providing practical tools for power enterprise managers and procurement professionals.

Theoretical foundation and literature review

Theory of power enterprise procurement performance evaluation

Basic concepts and theoretical framework of procurement performance evaluation

Procurement performance evaluation represents a systematic process of measuring, analyzing, and improving the effectiveness and efficiency of procurement activities within organizational contexts. The theoretical foundation of procurement performance evaluation stems from operations management and supply chain management disciplines, emphasizing the alignment between procurement outcomes and organizational strategic objectives9. The conceptual framework encompasses multiple dimensions including cost efficiency, quality assurance, delivery reliability, supplier relationship management, and risk mitigation, which collectively determine the overall procurement effectiveness.

Contemporary procurement performance evaluation theory recognizes the multi-stakeholder nature of procurement decisions and incorporates balanced scorecard approaches that integrate financial and non-financial performance indicators. The theoretical evolution has progressed from simple cost-focused metrics to comprehensive evaluation systems that consider value creation, sustainability, innovation capability, and strategic alignment10. This theoretical advancement reflects the growing recognition that procurement performance extends beyond transactional efficiency to encompass strategic value creation and competitive advantage development.

Characteristics of power enterprise procurement and evaluation indicator systems

Power enterprise procurement exhibits distinctive characteristics that differentiate it from conventional industrial procurement practices. The high capital intensity of power infrastructure investments necessitates rigorous quality standards and long-term reliability considerations that significantly influence procurement evaluation criteria11. Power enterprises typically operate under strict regulatory frameworks that mandate compliance with safety standards, environmental regulations, and grid reliability requirements, creating additional evaluation dimensions beyond conventional procurement metrics.

The specialized nature of power equipment and services creates unique evaluation challenges related to technical specifications, compatibility requirements, and lifecycle cost considerations. Power enterprise procurement evaluation systems must incorporate technical performance indicators such as equipment efficiency ratings, maintenance requirements, interoperability standards, and grid integration capabilities. Furthermore, the critical nature of power infrastructure creates heightened emphasis on supplier financial stability, delivery reliability, and emergency response capabilities within evaluation frameworks.

The evaluation indicator systems for power enterprise procurement typically encompass five primary dimensions: economic performance, quality performance, delivery performance, service performance, and strategic performance. Economic performance indicators include cost competitiveness, lifecycle cost optimization, and procurement budget adherence. Quality performance encompasses technical compliance, reliability standards, and defect rates. Delivery performance considers schedule adherence, logistics efficiency, and inventory management effectiveness.

Domestic research in power enterprise procurement performance evaluation has focused primarily on adapting international best practices to local regulatory environments and market conditions. Current research directions include the integration of fuzzy logic approaches to handle uncertainty in evaluation processes and the development of comprehensive evaluation models that support strategic decision-making in competitive electricity markets.

TOPSIS method theory

Basic ideas and calculation steps

The Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) represents a multi-criteria decision analysis method that ranks alternatives based on their relative distances from positive ideal solution (PIS) and negative ideal solution (NIS)19. The fundamental principle of TOPSIS is based on the concept that the selected alternative should have the shortest geometric distance from the positive ideal solution and the longest geometric distance from the negative ideal solution.

The TOPSIS methodology involves six sequential calculation steps that systematically transform raw decision data into comprehensive ranking results. The process begins with the construction of a normalized decision matrix that eliminates the influence of different measurement units and scales across evaluation criteria. The second step involves the creation of a weighted normalized decision matrix by incorporating the relative importance weights of evaluation criteria, typically derived from expert judgment or analytical hierarchy process methods.

The third step identifies the positive ideal solution as the set of best attribute values among all alternatives, while simultaneously determining the negative ideal solution as the collection of worst attribute values. For benefit criteria, the positive ideal solution corresponds to maximum values, whereas for cost criteria, it represents minimum values. The fourth step calculates the Euclidean distances between each alternative and both the positive and negative ideal solutions, providing quantitative measures of proximity to optimal and suboptimal performance levels.

The fifth step computes the relative closeness coefficient for each alternative, which represents the ratio of distance from the negative ideal solution to the total distance from both ideal solutions. The final step ranks all alternatives based on their relative closeness coefficients, with higher values indicating superior overall performance across multiple evaluation dimensions.

Application principles in multi-objective decision making

TOPSIS demonstrates exceptional capability in handling multi-objective decision-making scenarios where conflicting criteria require balanced consideration and systematic trade-off analysis20. The method’s strength lies in its ability to simultaneously consider multiple evaluation dimensions while maintaining computational simplicity and result interpretability. Unlike single-objective optimization approaches, TOPSIS accommodates scenarios where improvement in one criterion may necessitate sacrifice in another, reflecting the reality of complex decision environments.

The geometric distance concept employed in TOPSIS provides an intuitive and mathematically sound foundation for comparing alternatives across multiple criteria. This approach enables decision-makers to visualize the performance space and understand the relative positioning of alternatives in multi-dimensional evaluation contexts. The method’s flexibility allows for the incorporation of both quantitative and qualitative criteria through appropriate normalization techniques, making it suitable for diverse decision-making applications.

TOPSIS effectively addresses the challenge of criteria aggregation in multi-objective environments by avoiding the need for explicit utility function specification or complex preference elicitation procedures. The method’s robustness stems from its independence from the scales and units of measurement, allowing for meaningful comparison of alternatives evaluated on disparate criteria such as cost, quality, time, and strategic alignment.

Research progress and application effects in enterprise performance evaluation

The integration of TOPSIS with other multi-criteria decision analysis methods, including fuzzy set theory and analytic hierarchy process, has enhanced the method’s capability to handle uncertainty and subjective judgment in performance evaluation contexts.

Contemporary applications of TOPSIS in enterprise performance evaluation have expanded beyond traditional financial metrics to encompass comprehensive evaluation frameworks that incorporate environmental, social, and governance performance indicators. This evolution reflects the growing emphasis on sustainable business practices and stakeholder value creation in modern enterprise management. The method has proven particularly effective in benchmarking exercises, supplier selection processes, and strategic performance assessment initiatives.

The empirical evidence from TOPSIS applications in enterprise performance evaluation consistently demonstrates the method’s ability to provide clear, defensible ranking results that facilitate informed decision-making22. Case studies across manufacturing, service, and technology sectors have validated the method’s practical utility in identifying best practices, highlighting improvement opportunities, and supporting strategic planning processes.

Comparative studies examining TOPSIS performance against alternative multi-criteria decision analysis methods have revealed consistent advantages in terms of computational efficiency, result stability, and stakeholder acceptance23. The method’s transparency and logical foundation contribute to its widespread adoption in academic research and practical implementation contexts. Furthermore, the development of software tools and decision support systems incorporating TOPSIS algorithms has enhanced accessibility and reduced implementation barriers for enterprise applications.

The integration of TOPSIS with advanced analytical techniques, including machine learning algorithms and big data analytics, represents an emerging research frontier that promises to further enhance the method’s capability and application scope in enterprise performance evaluation. These technological advances enable real-time performance monitoring, predictive analytics, and adaptive evaluation frameworks that respond to changing business environments and stakeholder requirements. Recent methodological developments in infrastructure risk assessment have demonstrated hybrid MCDM approaches combining fuzzy logic with failure mode analysis for construction project prioritization67, while renewable energy system evaluation has benefited from entropy-weighted multicriteria frameworks that balance objective data-driven weighting with subjective expert knowledge68,69. Smart grid optimization research has integrated AHP with network analysis for power system reliability assessment74, and blockchain-enabled supply chain frameworks have explored advanced fuzzy set extensions including cubic picture fuzzy operators for handling extreme uncertainty in procurement contexts65,70. However, these advanced fuzzy approaches (T-spherical56, Fermatean57, circular intuitionistic63 introduce substantial computational complexity unsuitable for routine procurement evaluation in operational settings, creating a methodological gap between theoretical sophistication and practical implementability that the current research addresses through parsimonious FAHP-TOPSIS integration.

Contemporary research gaps in power enterprise procurement evaluation manifest across three dimensions. First, existing MCDM applications predominantly focus on supplier selection rather than comprehensive procurement performance assessment encompassing quality management, risk control, and strategic alignment simultaneously. Second, while parsimonious weighting methods (BWM53, FUCOM54, LBWA55 offer computational efficiency through reduced pairwise comparisons, they sacrifice uncertainty handling capabilities critical for power enterprise contexts where expert judgments regarding grid reliability and regulatory compliance inherently involve linguistic ambiguity. Third, cross-industry MCDM applications (manufacturing, healthcare71,72, transportation59 lack adaptation to power sector-specific requirements including long-term infrastructure reliability, regulatory compliance complexity, and supply chain resilience for critical equipment procurement. The proposed FAHP-TOPSIS framework systematically addresses these gaps by integrating hierarchical criteria decomposition with fuzzy uncertainty representation while maintaining computational feasibility for practical implementation, as validated through multi-enterprise benchmarking presented in Section IV.

Recent methodological developments in MCDM have introduced parsimonious weighting methods including Best-Worst Method (BWM)53, Full Consistency Method (FUCOM)54, and Level-Based Weight Assessment (LBWA)55 that require fewer pairwise comparisons (n-1) compared to AHP’s n(n-1)/2 comparisons. While these methods offer computational efficiency advantages, they face limitations in handling linguistic uncertainty inherent in procurement evaluation contexts. Advanced fuzzy extensions including T-spherical fuzzy sets56, Fermatean fuzzy approaches57, and circular intuitionistic fuzzy frameworks63 provide enhanced uncertainty representation but introduce computational complexity unsuitable for practical power enterprise applications.

Contemporary hybrid MCDM research has demonstrated diverse applications across sectors. The integration of entropy-based weight assessment with combined compromise solutions has shown effectiveness in renewable energy system evaluation64, while blockchain-enabled cubic picture fuzzy approaches address supply chain uncertainty65. Comparative analysis across MCDM methodologies reveals that FAHP-TOPSIS maintains optimal balance between uncertainty handling capability, computational feasibility, and discriminatory power for power enterprise procurement contexts. While parsimonious methods (BWM53, FUCOM54, LBWA55 reduce comparison requirements from AHP’s n(n-1)/2 to n-1, they employ crisp numerical scales that inadequately capture linguistic uncertainty inherent in subjective assessments of technical compliance and strategic partnership quality. Validation testing comparing FAHP-TOPSIS with BWM-MARCOS using identical datasets demonstrates superior consistency ratios (0.087 vs. 0.12, p < 0.05) and lower sensitivity coefficient variations (± 5.7% vs. ± 8.3%) under weight perturbation scenarios. Advanced fuzzy extensions including T-spherical56 and Fermatean fuzzy sets57 provide enhanced uncertainty representation but impose computational complexity (5-10x processing time) impractical for routine procurement evaluation requiring monthly or quarterly assessment cycles. The TOPSIS ranking mechanism offers superior discriminatory power compared to compromise solution methods (MARCOS, CoCoSo58 through explicit consideration of both positive and negative ideal solutions, enabling clearer performance gap identification critical for continuous improvement initiatives. Entropy-based objective weighting approaches eliminate subjective bias but disregard domain expertise essential for power enterprise contexts where strategic priorities (grid reliability emphasis over cost minimization during supply disruptions) require expert-informed weight adjustments. These methodological trade-offs justify FAHP-TOPSIS selection for power enterprise procurement evaluation where linguistic uncertainty handling, expert knowledge integration, and computational efficiency must be simultaneously balanced.

Construction of power enterprise procurement performance evaluation model based on fuzzy analytic hierarchy process and TOPSIS

Construction of evaluation indicator system

Multi-dimensional indicator framework development

The indicator development process followed a systematic three-stage methodology to ensure content validity, construct validity, and reliability. In Stage 1 (Literature-based Identification), comprehensive review of 127 procurement performance studies published between 2018 and 2024 across power sector, manufacturing, and public procurement contexts identified 45 candidate indicators spanning cost efficiency, quality management, supplier relations, risk mitigation, innovation capability, and sustainability dimensions. Stage 2 (Expert Delphi Screening) employed two iterative rounds with the same 8-member expert panel subsequently used for FAHP weight determination, achieving consensus convergence (Kendall’s W = 0.78, p < 0.01) to refine the indicator set to 18 tertiary-level measures distributed across 4 primary dimensions. Expert selection criteria required: (1) minimum 8 years procurement experience in power sector operations; (2) senior management position with strategic decision-making authority; (3) direct involvement in major equipment procurement projects exceeding $10 million value. Stage 3 (Statistical Validation) applied exploratory factor analysis to historical procurement data from 42 power enterprises (2021–2023, n = 1847 transactions), confirming the four-dimensional structure with satisfactory model fit (KMO = 0.847, Bartlett’s test χ²=3,245.7, p < 0.001). Internal consistency reliability assessed through Cronbach’s alpha achieved acceptable thresholds for all dimensions: Cost-Benefit (α = 0.89), Quality Management (α = 0.91), Supplier Management (α = 0.86), and Risk Control (α = 0.88), validating the indicator system’s psychometric properties for subsequent FAHP-TOPSIS application.

Weight determination using fuzzy analytic hierarchy process

Fuzzy judgment matrix construction and weight calculation process

The determination of indicator weights using fuzzy analytic hierarchy process requires systematic construction of fuzzy pairwise comparison matrices that capture expert judgments while accommodating the inherent uncertainty in subjective assessments28. The process begins with the conversion of linguistic terms into triangular fuzzy numbers, enabling quantitative manipulation of qualitative expert opinions regarding the relative importance of evaluation criteria.

Figure 2 illustrates the comprehensive workflow for fuzzy analytic hierarchy process weight calculation, demonstrating the sequential steps required for transforming expert judgments into normalized weight vectors. The flowchart presents the systematic approach for handling fuzzy information throughout the weight determination process.

Fig. 2Fuzzy analytic hierarchy process weight calculation workflow.

Fuzzy analytic hierarchy process weight calculation workflow.

The FAHP-TOPSIS integration workflow illustrated in Fig. 2 operationalizes through a structured algorithmic sequence comprising nine computational modules executed sequentially with intermediate validation checkpoints. Module 1 constructs fuzzy pairwise comparison matrices through linguistic term conversion using standardized triangular fuzzy number scales. Module 2 applies geometric mean aggregation across expert judgments to synthesize group consensus matrices while preserving reciprocal consistency properties. Module 3 performs fuzzy weight calculation via extent analysis method with defuzzification through centroid technique. Module 4 executes consistency verification with iterative refinement loops triggered when CR exceeds 0.1 threshold. Module 5 normalizes raw performance data across evaluation alternatives using vector normalization to eliminate scale effects. Module 6 constructs weighted normalized decision matrix by element-wise multiplication of criterion weights with normalized performance values. Module 7 identifies positive and negative ideal solutions through max/min operations conditional on criterion type (benefit/cost). Module 8 computes Euclidean distances from each alternative to both ideal solutions using n-dimensional distance formulas. Module 9 calculates relative closeness coefficients and ranks alternatives in descending order. Computational complexity analysis reveals O(n³) time complexity for FAHP weight calculation dominated by eigenvalue computation, and O(mn²) for TOPSIS ranking where m represents alternative count and n denotes criterion number, yielding total computational cost suitable for routine procurement evaluation with typical parameters m ≤ 20, n ≤ 15 requiring 3–5 s processing time on standard enterprise computing infrastructure.

The construction of fuzzy pairwise comparison matrices employs triangular fuzzy numbers to represent linguistic assessments provided by domain experts. TOPSIS comprehensive evaluation method

Integration of FAHP weights with TOPSIS framework

The selection of FAHP-TOPSIS integration over newer parsimonious methods is justified by three critical factors specific to power enterprise procurement: (1) Uncertainty Management: Power enterprise procurement involves subjective assessments of technical compliance and regulatory requirements that require fuzzy representation capabilities unavailable in BWM (Consistency Ratio: FAHP = 0.087 vs. BWM = 0.12 in validation tests); (2) Stakeholder Integration: Unlike FUCOM’s hierarchical ranking approach, FAHP accommodates multiple expert perspectives through geometric mean aggregation essential for power enterprise decision-making involving technical, financial, and regulatory stakeholders; (3) Practical Implementation: While LBWA reduces comparison requirements to n-1, power enterprise procurement evaluation benefits from FAHP’s comprehensive pairwise analysis to capture subtle preference relationships critical for high-stakes infrastructure decisions where procurement errors can impact grid reliability.

Computational validation demonstrates FAHP-TOPSIS achieves optimal performance balance: sensitivity coefficient variation ± 5.7% vs. BWM-MARCOS ± 8.3% and FUCOM-WASPAS ± 7.1%, confirming superior stability for strategic decision-making contexts. The TOPSIS method utilizes the normalized weight vectors obtained from FAHP to construct weighted decision matrices that reflect the relative importance of evaluation criteria while maintaining computational efficiency and result interpretability32.

Conclusion

This research successfully developed and validated a comprehensive multi-dimensional evaluation model for power enterprise procurement performance by integrating fuzzy analytic hierarchy process and TOPSIS methodologies, addressing critical gaps in existing procurement evaluation frameworks and providing enhanced analytical capabilities for complex decision-making environments46.

Theoretical Contributions: (1) Methodological Innovation: First integration of FAHP-TOPSIS specifically designed for power enterprise procurement context, achieving 23% improvement in consistency ratios over traditional AHP approaches; (2) Uncertainty Modeling: Novel fuzzy aggregation framework for power-specific evaluation criteria including grid reliability and regulatory compliance factors; (3) Framework Validation: Comprehensive sensitivity analysis methodology incorporating data measurement uncertainty, expert judgment variations, and normalization technique impacts.

Practical Contributions: (1) Industry Implementation: Validated evaluation framework deployed across four power enterprises with demonstrated 15% improvement in supplier selection accuracy; (2) Decision Support: Practical guidance for power enterprise procurement managers with specific performance thresholds and improvement strategies; (3) Benchmarking Capability: Industry-wide performance comparison framework enabling systematic procurement optimization.

The study represents a significant advancement in procurement performance assessment theory and practice through the systematic incorporation of uncertainty handling mechanisms and multi-criteria decision analysis techniques.

Main research achievements and innovations

Future Research Directions: (1) Dynamic Evaluation: Integration of temporal performance trends using machine learning algorithms for predictive procurement analytics (2) Blockchain Integration: Transparent supplier performance tracking using distributed ledger technology (3) Circular Economy: Extension to sustainability metrics including carbon footprint and circular procurement practices (4) Cross-Industry Application: Adaptation framework for other critical infrastructure sectors (telecommunications, water utilities).

This research acknowledges several methodological and empirical limitations that bound the generalizability and applicability of findings while identifying opportunities for future advancement. The single-case enterprise validation design limits external validity, as organizational characteristics (state-owned status, regional market conditions, installed capacity mix) may influence indicator relevance and weight priorities differently across diverse power enterprise typologies including private generation companies, transmission-focused utilities, or renewable energy specialists. The cross-validation with three peer enterprises partially addresses this limitation but remains confined to similar large-scale regional operators within China’s regulatory environment. The static evaluation framework captures performance at discrete time points (annual assessment) but fails to model dynamic procurement performance trajectories, seasonal variations, or progressive improvement trends that longitudinal analysis could reveal. Expert judgment subjectivity introduces potential bias despite consistency verification procedures, particularly in qualitative assessments of strategic partnership quality and innovation contribution where inter-rater variance (ICC = 0.82) indicates non-negligible disagreement. The 3.2% missing data rate addressed through multiple regression imputation assumes missing-at-random mechanism that may not hold if data absence correlates with poor supplier performance, potentially introducing systematic underestimation of risk exposure. Computational complexity of FAHP-TOPSIS iteration (3.2 s per evaluation) constrains real-time decision support applications, limiting deployment in dynamic procurement scenarios requiring immediate supplier selection or contract award decisions. The power industry-specific indicator system and criterion weights reduce direct transferability to other critical infrastructure sectors (telecommunications, water utilities, transportation) without substantial adaptation and revalidation, though the methodological framework structure remains generalizable. These limitations inform the future research directions outlined subsequently, particularly emphasizing multi-case longitudinal studies, automated real-time evaluation systems, and cross-sector framework adaptation initiatives.

The integration of fuzzy set theory with analytic hierarchy process enables systematic handling of linguistic variables and subjective expert judgments while maintaining mathematical consistency and logical transparency throughout the weight determination process47. The subsequent application of TOPSIS methodology provides robust ranking capabilities that consider both ideal and anti-ideal performance benchmarks across multiple evaluation dimensions simultaneously.

The constructed evaluation model demonstrates multi-criteria decision-making compared to conventional approaches by incorporating four primary evaluation dimensions encompassing cost-benefit performance, quality management, supplier relationship management, and risk control, each systematically decomposed into secondary and tertiary indicators with specific measurement criteria and evaluation standards. The hierarchical structure facilitates comprehensive performance assessment while maintaining operational feasibility and stakeholder acceptance in practical implementation contexts.

Theoretical and practical value

From a theoretical perspective, this research advances the field of procurement performance evaluation by providing a methodologically sound framework for handling uncertainty, subjectivity, and multi-dimensionality inherent in complex organizational assessment scenarios48. The systematic integration of fuzzy logic principles with established multi-criteria decision analysis techniques creates a robust analytical foundation that can be adapted to diverse industry contexts and organizational requirements.

The practical value is demonstrated through successful application to a representative power enterprise case study, revealing actionable insights regarding performance strengths, improvement opportunities, and strategic priorities for procurement management enhancement. The empirical validation confirms the model’s capability to provide reliable, stable, and interpretable evaluation results that support evidence-based decision-making and continuous improvement initiatives.

Research limitations and future directions

The current research acknowledges several limitations that provide opportunities for future investigation and model enhancement. The reliance on expert judgment for fuzzy comparison matrices introduces potential subjective bias that could be mitigated through expanded expert panels or alternative preference elicitation techniques49. Additionally, the static nature of the current evaluation framework limits its ability to capture dynamic performance changes and temporal trends that may significantly impact procurement effectiveness over extended periods.

Future research directions emerge directly from identified limitations and evolving technological capabilities in procurement performance management. First, dynamic evaluation model development addressing the static framework limitation should integrate time-series forecasting techniques (ARIMA, Prophet) with FAHP-TOPSIS to enable predictive procurement performance assessment, incorporating leading indicators (supplier financial health trajectories, market price trend forecasts) alongside lagging performance metrics to support proactive risk mitigation and strategic planning with 3–6 month forward visibility. Machine learning algorithms including random forests and gradient boosting can reduce expert judgment subjectivity by learning weight patterns from historical high-performing procurement decisions, creating hybrid expert-algorithmic weighting schemes that preserve domain knowledge while incorporating data-driven pattern recognition. Second, blockchain integration for transparent supplier performance tracking addresses data reliability limitations through immutable distributed ledger recording of procurement transactions, quality inspection results, and delivery performance metrics, enabling real-time dashboard visualization accessible to all supply chain stakeholders while eliminating data manipulation risks and reducing audit requirements. Smart contract automation can trigger performance-based payment releases and supplier tier classification updates based on objective blockchain-recorded metrics, reducing manual assessment overhead. Third, circular economy and sustainability metric expansion addresses incomplete environmental impact consideration by incorporating carbon footprint assessment (Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions), circular material usage rates, end-of-life recyclability scores, and renewable energy consumption in supplier operations as additional evaluation dimensions weighted at 8–12% within revised cost-benefit or dedicated sustainability dimension, aligning procurement strategy with net-zero transition commitments increasingly mandated in power sector regulations64,66. Fourth, cross-industry framework adaptation systematically addresses generalizability limitations through comparative case studies across critical infrastructure sectors (water utilities n = 15, telecommunications n = 20, urban transit n = 12), identifying universal procurement performance dimensions (cost, quality, risk) versus sector-specific criteria (grid reliability for power74,75, water quality for utilities, network uptime for telecom76, developing modular indicator systems with core-plus-contextual structure enabling rapid framework deployment with 60–70% indicator reuse and 30–40% sector adaptation. Fifth, enhanced computational efficiency for real-time decision support addresses current processing time constraints through algorithmic optimization (sparse matrix techniques, parallel processing), approximation methods (fuzzy membership function simplification), and cloud-based distributed computing architectures enabling < 0.5 s evaluation latency suitable for online supplier selection tools and automated procurement recommendation engines integrated within e-procurement platforms.

Policy recommendations and practical guidance

The research findings provide several strategic recommendations for power enterprise procurement management practice and policy development based on empirical insights from the case study and cross-enterprise validation50.

Implementation guidance derived from case enterprise evaluation results emphasizes actionable intervention strategies targeting identified performance gaps with quantified improvement targets and resource allocation recommendations. For Supplier Relationship Management enhancement addressing the case enterprise’s underperformance (3.8/5.0 strategic partnership level, 82.6% supplier performance score), procurement leadership should implement a differentiated three-tier supplier development program structured as follows. Strategic Tier (top 10% suppliers by transaction volume and strategic importance) receives dedicated relationship managers, joint innovation initiative funding ($50–100 K per supplier annually), long-term contracts (5 + years with volume commitments), and quarterly executive-level review meetings to co-develop capability roadmaps. Development Tier (middle 30% of supplier base) accesses structured capability building support including technical training programs (quality management systems, lean manufacturing), performance improvement consulting, financial incentive mechanisms (early payment for quality milestones), and biannual performance reviews with explicit upgrade pathways to Strategic Tier contingent on demonstrated improvement. Transactional Tier (remaining 60%) maintains competitive bidding processes with annual contract renewals while monitoring for high-potential candidates exhibiting consistent quality delivery and innovation proposals warranting Development Tier consideration. This tiered approach balances relationship investment concentration on strategic suppliers (estimated 15–20% of procurement budget allocated to partnership development) while maintaining competitive pressure and cost efficiency for commodity categories.

For Cost-Risk Trade-off Optimization leveraging the model’s sensitivity insights (± 5.7% coefficient variation under ± 20% weight changes), organizations should adopt dynamic weighting protocols responsive to external market conditions and internal strategic shifts. During supply chain disruption periods (pandemic recurrence, geopolitical conflicts, natural disasters affecting supplier regions), increase Risk Control dimension weight from baseline 7.3% to 15–20% through expedited expert panel consensus (2-week cycle vs. quarterly standard), prioritizing supply continuity and multiple-source availability over cost minimization. Operationally, this manifests through relaxed cost deviation tolerances (± 10% vs. ± 5% standard), acceptance of premium pricing for guaranteed inventory levels, and accelerated qualification of backup suppliers even with higher onboarding costs. Conversely, during stable market conditions with low supply disruption risk indicators, optimize Cost-Benefit emphasis to maximize efficiency gains through competitive bidding intensification, category consolidation initiatives, and global sourcing expansion, potentially achieving 3–5% additional cost reduction annually. Quarterly environmental scanning protocols should assess five key risk indicators (supplier financial distress index, commodity price volatility, logistics disruption frequency, regulatory change magnitude, demand forecast uncertainty) to trigger weight recalibration decisions, with automated dashboard alerts when aggregate risk index exceeds predetermined thresholds.

Supplier Relationship Management Enhancement: Given the case enterprise’s underperformance in strategic partnership levels (3.8/5.0) and supplier performance scores (82.6%), procurement managers should implement a three-tier supplier development program: (1) Strategic Tier - Establish joint innovation initiatives and long-term contracts (5 + years) with top 10% suppliers showing high reliability and innovation potential; (2) Development Tier - Provide technical training, quality improvement support, and performance incentive mechanisms for the middle 30% of suppliers to elevate capabilities; (3) Transactional Tier - Maintain competitive bidding for commodity items while monitoring for potential upgrade candidates. This tiered approach balances relationship investment with performance improvement, addressing the identified weakness while maintaining cost efficiency.

Cost-Risk Trade-off Optimization: The model’s sensitivity analysis reveals that cost-benefit weight variations of ± 20% produce coefficient changes of ± 5.7%, indicating strategic flexibility in balancing economic and risk objectives. Organizations should adopt dynamic weighting based on market conditions: during supply chain disruptions or high market volatility, increase risk control weights by 15–20% to prioritize supply continuity; conversely, in stable periods, emphasize cost optimization to maximize efficiency gains. The FAHP framework enables quarterly weight recalibration based on environmental assessments.

Quality Management Sustainability: The case enterprise’s excellence in specification conformance (96.8%) and equipment reliability (87.4%) should be maintained through institutionalized quality assurance mechanisms: (1) Implement statistical process control with real-time monitoring dashboards tracking defect rates and reliability metrics; (2) Establish supplier quality certification requirements as pre-qualification criteria; (3) Conduct bi-annual quality audits with performance-linked contract renewals. These measures ensure sustained quality standards while preventing complacency.

Regulatory Compliance and Sustainability Integration: Power enterprises must align procurement evaluation with evolving environmental regulations and carbon neutrality targets. Organizations should expand the evaluation framework to incorporate: (1) Supplier carbon footprint metrics weighted at 8–12% within cost-benefit dimension; (2) Circular economy indicators measuring recycling rates and lifecycle environmental impact; (3) Renewable energy usage in supplier operations. Integration of these sustainability criteria positions procurement strategy in alignment with regulatory trends and stakeholder expectations.

Adaptive Sensitivity Analysis Procedures: Organizations should establish regular sensitivity analysis protocols conducted quarterly or following major market disruptions. The analysis should test: (1) Weight variation scenarios (± 15–25%) across all primary dimensions; (2) Data measurement uncertainty through Monte Carlo simulation (10,000 iterations minimum); (3) Normalization technique robustness comparing vector, min-max, and z-score methods. Results should inform strategic adjustments to evaluation priorities and risk mitigation measures. The demonstrated 96.3% ranking stability provides confidence in decision robustness while highlighting areas requiring attention during significant weight shifts.

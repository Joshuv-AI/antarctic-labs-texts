Development and evaluation of a blended learning model integrating ARCS and BOPPPS for Endodontics education
Authors: Yepo Hou, Chan Xiao, Hui Yang, Mei Juan, Guangye Zhao, Chunfeng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesEndodontics courses frequently encounter challenges, including decentralized teaching platforms, fragmented learning resources, uniform instructional methods, and subjective assessment practices. This study aimed to design and evaluate a blended teaching model that integrates the Attention, Relevance, Confidence, Satisfaction (ARCS) motivation model with the Bridge-in, Objective, Pre-assessment, Participatory Learning, Post-assessment, Summary (BOPPPS) pedagogical framework specifically for Endodontics education. The primary objectives were to improve teaching efficiency, enhance students’ learning motivation, and foster the development of their comprehensive competencies.Material and methodsA total of 275 undergraduate dental students were randomly assigned to either an experimental group (n = 137) or a control group (n = 138). The experimental group participated in a one-year teaching reform intervention using the "ARCS + BOPPPS" blended learning model. Key components of the intervention included: (1) establishing an integrated "1 + N" one-click accessible teaching platform; (2) developing a modular teaching resource library; (3) systematically applying ARCS motivational strategies across all phases of the BOPPPS teaching process; and (4) constructing a multi-dimensional evaluation system encompassing knowledge, skills, and professionalism. The control group received traditional instruction. The effectiveness of the "ARCS + BOPPPS" model was evaluated by comparing the two groups in terms of platform and resource utilization efficiency, rates of positive feedback on learning motivation, comprehensive competency scores, achievement of teaching objectives, and overall teaching satisfaction. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26.0. Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), and categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages [n (%)]. Independent-samples t-tests and χ2 tests were applied for intergroup comparisons, with a P-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.Results(1) Teaching platform and resource utilization efficiency improved markedly, with a daily platform activity rate of 90.3% and average resource library usage exceeding 85%. (2) Students in the experimental group reported significantly higher satisfaction across all four dimensions of the ARCS model, Attention (93.4%), Relevance (92.0%), Confidence (89.1%), and Satisfaction (96.4%), compared to the control group (p < 0.01). (3) The experimental group also demonstrated significantly superior performance in practical skills (94.25 ± 4.34), innovative ability (92.43 ± 4.76), and overall comprehensive competency (90.72 ± 5.27) relative to the control group (p < 0.01). (4) Furthermore, the experimental group achieved higher attainment rates for knowledge (91.2%), skills (89.1%), and professionalism (93.4%) objectives, as well as a greater overall teaching satisfaction rate (96.4%), all statistically significant compared to the control group (p < 0.01).ConclusionsThe "ARCS + BOPPPS" blended teaching model effectively integrates teaching resources, enhances student learning motivation, and markedly improves their practical and innovative abilities. It promotes a shift in student learning behavior from passive reception to active inquiry. This model offers a replicable and scalable framework for reforming professional courses in dental education and provides valuable insights for advancing educational reforms in medical education.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-025-08564-y.Keywords: Endodontics, ARCS motivation model, BOPPPS model, Blended learning, Educational reform

Introduction

Blended online and offline teaching has emerged as a key driver for the modernization and digital transformation of higher education [1, 2]. This trend aligns with the global emphasis on systematically integrating information technology to promote the deep fusion of online and offline instruction [3, 4], thereby facilitating the development of more intelligent and high-quality teaching models [5]. In practice, however, the implementation of blended learning faces several challenges, including decentralized teaching platforms that are difficult to manage uniformly, fragmented learning resources lacking systematic integration, uniform instructional methods that fail to stimulate students’ intrinsic motivation, and subjective assessment practices that are challenging to quantify objectively [6, 7]. These limitations highlight an urgent need for coherent, structured, and theoretically grounded pedagogical models.

Within China’s dental education system, the undergraduate stomatology program typically spans five years. Endodontics, as a core professional course, is usually delivered during the seventh and eighth semesters and comprises 88 total credit hours, including 40 h of theoretical instruction and 48 h of practical training. The curriculum encompasses essential modules such as the etiology, diagnosis, treatment, and restoration of dental caries and pulp–periapical diseases, with a strong emphasis on translating foundational theory into clinical practice [8]. Beyond the transmission of systematic disciplinary knowledge, the course is also responsible for developing students’ clinical operative skills, doctor–patient communication abilities, and innovative thinking. However, traditional teaching models frequently struggle to accommodate students’ individualized learning needs or to effectively cultivate higher-order competencies, thereby limiting their educational effectiveness [9].

In response to these challenges, increasing attention has been directed toward teaching models that integrate the ARCS motivation model with the BOPPPS instructional framework as a means of enhancing learning motivation and classroom engagement. The ARCS model promotes sustained learning motivation across four dimensions, Attention, Relevance, Confidence, and Satisfaction, by addressing both cognitive and affective aspects of learning [10]. In parallel, the BOPPPS framework, consisting of Bridge-in, Objective, Pre-assessment, Participatory Learning, Post-assessment, and Summary, offers a highly structured and operational approach to instructional design, characterized by clear learning objectives, active student participation, and timely feedback [11, 12]. Empirical studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of this combined approach in disciplines such as Investment Studies, Management, and Computer Fundamentals, reporting improvements in learning motivation, classroom interaction, and overall teaching outcomes [13–15]. Nevertheless, within dental education, particularly in core clinical courses such as Endodontics, Prosthodontics, and Periodontics, the systematic construction and empirical validation of this integrated model remain largely unexplored, and no formal studies have been reported to date.

Against this backdrop, and guided by the principle articulated by Maiti et al. that “motivational design should precede instructional design” [16], the present study advances a central hypothesis: that the organic integration of the ARCS motivation model with the BOPPPS instructional framework, through the establishment of an integrated teaching platform, the development of a modularized resource repository, the innovation of instructional strategies, and the construction of a multidimensional evaluation system, can yield an “ARCS + BOPPPS” blended teaching model. This model is expected to effectively consolidate teaching resources, stimulate students’ learning motivation, and significantly enhance their practical skills and innovative capabilities, thereby promoting the coordinated achievement of three-dimensional teaching objectives encompassing knowledge acquisition, competency development, and professional literacy. Accordingly, using the Endodontics course as a representative case, this study aims to systematically construct and empirically evaluate this blended teaching model, address key shortcomings in current instructional practices, and provide a replicable and scalable paradigm for the reform of professional courses in dental education.

Materials and methods

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board [2025(H11001]. The schema of this study was shown in Fig. 1.

The schema of this study

Participants

This study recruited 275 fourth-year undergraduate students from the stomatology program during the 2021–2022 academic year. All participants were in the preclinical stage of the core professional course Endodontics, a pivotal transitional phase linking systematic theoretical instruction with subsequent clinical clerkships. A randomized controlled trial (RCT) design was adopted. Eligible students were randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to either an experimental group (n = 137) or a control group (n = 138) using a computer-generated randomization sequence. Allocation concealment was ensured by an independent staff member who was not involved in the teaching process, through the use of sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes. Owing to the nature of the educational interventions, blinding of students and instructors was not feasible. No statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) were observed between the two groups in baseline characteristics such as age, gender, and comprehensive entrance examination scores, indicating good comparability.

Study design

The control group received traditional instruction, primarily consisting of teacher-led classroom lectures and demonstrations, with students passively acquiring knowledge, supplemented by limited after-class exercises and a conventional final written examination. In contrast, the experimental group participated in a one-year intervention using the "ARCS + BOPPPS" blended teaching model. The specific interventions included:

Establishment of a highly integrated '1 + N' one-click accessible teaching platform

To address challenges such as platform fragmentation, data silos, and the difficulty of real-time progress monitoring by instructors, this study employed the ChaoXing Learning Platform as the primary teaching environment. This core platform was integrated with a range of teaching tools, including Rain Classroom, virtual simulation teaching systems, the ZhiPu Language AI system, an oral medicine VR exhibition hall, the Oral-Systemic Case Study APP, and a TikTok science popularization APP. Together, these tools created a highly unified '1 + N' online teaching platform accessible with a single click, enabling interoperability of data across multiple platforms and centralized management through a unified access portal. This integration effectively enhanced platform usage efficiency and operational transparency (Fig. 2).

Implementation of the "ARCS + BOPPPS" Blended Teaching Reform

Guided by the ARCS motivation model, the instructional design emphasized: The study focused on capturing student Attention, establishing the Relevance of content to student needs, building student Confidence, and generating Satisfaction with learning outcomes. ARCS motivational strategies were systematically integrated into all six phases of the BOPPPS framework. For instance, during the Bridge-in phase, clinical cases and VR immersive experiences were employed to capture Attention. In the Objective phase, the relevance of learning goals to professional development was clearly articulated to enhance Relevance. Throughout Participatory Learning, scaffolded task design was implemented to build student Confidence. In the Post-assessment and Summary phases, showcasing exemplary student work and providing positive feedback fostered Satisfaction. This approach achieved a deep integration of motivational strategies within the pedagogical structure, effectively addressing issues related to uniform teaching methods and insufficient intrinsic motivation among students (Fig. 4).

The "ARCS + BOPPPS" Blended Teaching Methodology

Construction of a multi-dimensional evaluation system aligned with three-dimensional teaching objectives

To comprehensively assess the attainment of the three-dimensional objectives, knowledge, skills, and professionalism, this study established a multi-dimensional evaluation system encompassing the entire instructional process, including pre-class, in-class, and post-class phases (Fig. 5). The system incorporates diverse evaluators, such as instructors, students (via self- and peer-assessment), and external stakeholders. By leveraging digital tools for learning analytics, it enables an integrated evaluation of knowledge, skills, and professionalism across diagnostic, formative, summative, and value-added dimensions.

Multi-dimensional Evaluation System for the Three-Dimensional Teaching Objectives Methodology

Statistical analysis

Data were collected using the following instruments to assess the effectiveness of the "ARCS + BOPPPS" blended teaching model: (1) platform backend data (e.g., page views, user activity, and resource utilization rates); (2) A self-developed ARCS Motivation Model questionnaire (Cronbach's α = 0.89); (3) theoretical examination scores, practical skill operation assessment checklists, and evaluations of innovative ability projects; and (4) questionnaires assessing the attainment of three-dimensional teaching objectives, knowledge, skills, and professionalism, as well as overall teaching satisfaction.

Data analysis was conducted using SPSS version 26.0. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s) and were compared between groups using the independent samples t-test. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages [n (%)] and were compared using the Chi-square (χ2) test. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance.

Results

Significant improvement in the utilization efficiency of the '1 + N' one-click accessible teaching platform and resources

Experimental data demonstrated a high level of engagement with the integrated "1 + N" teaching platform, totaling 644,800 visits, which corresponds to an average of 2,344.7 visits per student. The platform's daily activity rate reached 90.3%, and the average utilization rate across all modular resource repositories exceeded 85%. Specifically, the completion rate for the Multi-level Video Repository was 92.7%, the utilization rate of the Exemplary Teaching Resource Bank reached 95.6%, the click-through rate for the Hotspot and Frontier Knowledge Link Library was 98.2%, the task completion rate for the Stomatology-Specific Ideological and Political Case Database was 96.8%, and the access rate for the Student Advanced Work Exhibition Repository was 85.4%. These findings indicate that the platform and resource system effectively dismantled resource barriers, facilitated efficient acquisition and utilization of materials, and provided substantial support for differentiated instruction and personalized learning (Fig. 6).

Utilization Efficiency of the '1 + N' One-Click Accessible Teaching Platform and Resources

Positive shifts in student learning motivation and behavior

Results from the ARCS motivation questionnaire indicated that the experimental group demonstrated significantly higher positive feedback across all four dimensions, Attention (93.4%), Relevance (92.0%), Confidence (89.1%), and Satisfaction (96.4%), compared to the control group (P < 0.01), as presented in Table 1. Additionally, the experimental group achieved significantly higher scores not only in each individual dimension but also in the overall questionnaire total score (P < 0.01).Qualitative analysis further revealed that students generally perceived clinical cases and VR experiences as highly effective in rapidly capturing their attention. They regarded the teaching content as closely aligned with their professional needs, felt that the scaffolded instructional design bolstered their confidence, and reported substantial satisfaction from collaboratively completing and presenting their learning outcomes. These findings suggest that the "ARCS + BOPPPS" model not only enhances learning motivation but also promotes a meaningful transition in learning behavior, shifting students from passive reception to active inquiry.

Comprehensive enhancement of student competencies

Regarding theoretical knowledge, no significant difference was observed between the experimental group (85.50 ± 6.57) and the control group (83.98 ± 8.25) (P > 0.05). In contrast, the experimental group demonstrated clear advantages in practical skills, innovative ability, and overall comprehensive competency. Their practical skills score (94.25 ± 4.34) was significantly higher than that of the control group (84.31 ± 5.26) (P < 0.01), and their innovative ability score (92.43 ± 4.76) markedly exceeded that of the control group (70.35 ± 8.98) (P < 0.01). Similarly, the overall comprehensive score of the experimental group (90.72 ± 5.27) was significantly superior to that of the control group (79.55 ± 7.40) (P < 0.01), as detailed in Table 2. These results highlight the substantial effectiveness of the "ARCS + BOPPPS" model in fostering knowledge internalization, enhancing practical skills, and cultivating students’ innovative competencies.

Significant improvement in teaching objective attainment and satisfaction

The multi-dimensional evaluation based on the three-dimensional teaching objectives demonstrated that the experimental group achieved significantly higher attainment rates for knowledge (91.2%), skills (89.1%), and professionalism (93.4%) compared to the control group (P < 0.01). In addition, the overall teaching satisfaction rate in the experimental group reached 96.4%, markedly higher than the 71.7% observed in the control group (P < 0.01), as shown in Table 3. These findings indicate that the "ARCS + BOPPPS" blended teaching model effectively promoted the comprehensive achievement of teaching objectives and enhanced student satisfaction, thereby successfully shifting the instructional focus from mere "low-level knowledge transmission" to the "highly effective three-dimensional" integration of knowledge, skills, and professionalism.

Discussion

Guided by the proposed core hypothesis, this study systematically developed and empirically validated the “ARCS + BOPPPS” blended teaching model specifically tailored for the Endodontics course. The findings demonstrate that this model effectively integrates diverse teaching resources, significantly enhances students’ learning motivation, and improves their practical skills and innovative capabilities. Consequently, it fosters the comprehensive and synergistic achievement of the three-dimensional teaching objectives, encompassing knowledge acquisition, competence development, and professional literacy.

Construction of a digital learning ecosystem: high-efficiency integration of teaching resources

The findings of this study support the hypothesis that the “ARCS + BOPPPS” blended teaching model effectively integrates diverse educational resources. This is evidenced by the systematic development and efficient operation of the “1 + N” integrated teaching platform alongside a five-category modular resource repository. Centered on the “Learning through” portal, the platform seamlessly incorporates tools such as virtual simulations, VR exhibition halls, and AI-assisted systems, creating a unified, one-click access teaching environment. The modular resource repository comprises instructional videos, refined lesson plans, frontier knowledge updates, ideological-political case studies, and exemplary student work, forming a well-structured system with complementary functions. Operational metrics demonstrate robust engagement, with a total of 644,800 platform visits and a daily average activity rate of 90.3%. Utilization rates for the five resource categories consistently exceeded 85%, with the Hotspot and Frontier Knowledge repository achieving a 98.2% click-through rate and the Ideological-Political Case repository recording a 96.8% task completion rate. These outcomes reflect the practical application of the “Digital Learning Ecosystem” concept [17, 18], wherein the integration of a unified platform portal and a structured resource framework significantly reduced students’ cognitive load and minimized access barriers, thereby providing stable and efficient environmental support for blended teaching. This approach aligns with Zeng et al.’s research philosophy advocating the use of digital technologies to enhance learning intuitiveness and student engagement [19].

Systematic stimulation of learning motivation: the integration mechanism of ARCS and BOPPPS

The results of this study provide compelling evidence supporting the hypothesis that the “ARCS + BOPPPS” blended teaching model significantly enhances students’ learning motivation. The experimental group demonstrated markedly higher scores and positive feedback rates across all four ARCS dimensions, Attention (93.4%), Relevance (92.0%), Confidence (89.1%), and Satisfaction (96.4%), compared to the control group (P < 0.01). This effectiveness stems from the deliberate and systematic integration of ARCS motivational strategies within the structured phases of the BOPPPS instructional framework, both at the design and implementation levels.

Motivational stimulation was precisely embedded at each stage of the BOPPPS cycle. During the Bridge-in phase, authentic clinical cases and immersive virtual reality (VR) experiences were utilized to rapidly capture students’ Attention. The Objective phase clearly articulated the Relevance of learning goals to students’ future professional roles. Within the Participatory Learning phase, scaffolded task designs combined with immediate feedback from pre- and post-assessments fostered students’ Confidence through the accumulation of mastery experiences. Finally, in the Summary phase, showcasing achievements and providing positive evaluations reinforced students’ Satisfaction.

This instructional design aligns closely with Keller’s Model of Motivational Design [20] and the concept of “mastery experience” central to Usher’s Self-Efficacy Theory [21], thereby creating a sustainable reinforcement loop: Motivation Stimulation (Attention/Relevance) → Behavioral Engagement and Mastery Experience (Confidence) → Achievement Reinforcement (Satisfaction). It is this cyclical process that drives a profound shift in students’ learning behaviors, from passive reception toward active inquiry. Elevated motivation translates into increased classroom engagement and deeper cognitive investment, ultimately yielding superior learning outcomes. The experimental group’s significant advantages in practical skills (94.25 ± 4.34) and innovative ability (92.43 ± 4.76) reflect the direct impact of this “motivation-behavior” reinforcement loop [22]. These findings not only confirm the successful translation of enhanced motivation into higher-order competencies but also address a critical limitation of traditional medical education: the overemphasis on knowledge transmission at the expense of competency development [23].

In this blended model, the ARCS framework functions as the intrinsic “driving engine,” addressing the fundamental question of “why learn” by stimulating interest, reinforcing the relevance of content, fostering confidence, and enhancing satisfaction. This approach aligns with the “motivation first” principle advocated by Maiti et al. [16]. Conversely, the BOPPPS model, through its six interlocking phases, Bridge-in, Objective, Pre-assessment, Participatory Learning, Post-assessment, and Summary, provides a clear, cyclical instructional structure that facilitates the transformation of motivation into deep learning [24, 25]. The seamless integration and complementary synergy of these two models culminate in a student-centered teaching ecology that fosters a positive and dynamic interplay between motivational and cognitive development.

Transformation from knowledge to competence: synergistic achievement of three-dimensional teaching objectives

The significant advantages demonstrated by the experimental group in both practical skills and innovative capabilities further substantiate the core hypothesis of this study: that the “ARCS + BOPPPS” blended teaching model effectively facilitates the synergistic attainment of the three-dimensional teaching objectives, knowledge, competence, and literacy. This success is attributed to the “dual-engine mechanism for competence transformation” inherent in the integrated model. Specifically, the Participatory Learning phase of the BOPPPS framework establishes a “learning-by-doing” pathway by designing task scenarios that closely simulate real clinical practice. Simultaneously, the intrinsic motivation ignited and sustained by the ARCS model drives students to actively engage in the exploration and innovation demanded by complex tasks. The interplay of these elements, structured practical opportunities coupled with sustained intrinsic motivation, constitutes the pivotal mechanism for translating knowledge into higher-order competencies. This approach aligns closely with educational theories emphasizing the centrality of situated cognition and motivation in effective learning [26, 27].

Concurrently, the multidimensional evaluation system developed in this study, grounded in the three-dimensional objectives of knowledge, competence, and literacy, not only systematically verifies the effectiveness of the teaching model but also enables closed-loop optimization of instructional practices. By integrating multiple evaluators and diverse assessment methods, diagnostic, formative, summative, and value-added, this system facilitates comprehensive monitoring of students’ learning processes and overall literacy development. The experimental group’s outstanding achievement rates in knowledge (91.2%), skills (89.1%), professionalism (93.4%), and overall teaching satisfaction (96.4%) provide objective evidence of the holistic fulfillment of teaching goals. This represents a paradigm shift in evaluation methodology, from traditional knowledge-centric testing toward comprehensive literacy assessment, resonating with contemporary philosophies of educational evaluation reform [28]. Moreover, it offers a compelling empirical foundation for leveraging educational technology to implement data-driven, multi-faceted teaching assessments [29].

Limitations and future directions

This study systematically developed and validated the effectiveness of the “ARCS + BOPPPS” blended teaching model within dental education. The findings demonstrate that this model effectively stimulates students’ learning motivation, facilitates a behavioral shift toward active inquiry, and promotes the synergistic development of the three-dimensional educational objectives, knowledge, competence, and literacy, thus providing robust empirical support for curricular reform in professional courses. Nevertheless, certain limitations must be acknowledged. The single-center design may limit the representativeness of the sample, warranting caution when generalizing these results. Additionally, the relatively short duration of the teaching intervention precludes assessment of long-term impacts, which require further longitudinal study.

Future research should advance along several key directions. First, multi-center, longitudinal tracking studies are needed to systematically verify the generalizability and sustained effectiveness of the model. Second, integrating advanced learning analytics technologies can enable the development of personalized learning support systems based on student behavioral data [30, 31]. Third, exploring the incorporation of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies to create intelligent assessment systems tailored to evaluate higher-order cognitive skills and practical competencies will further enhance instructional efficacy [32, 33]. These initiatives will drive the evolution of the blended teaching model toward greater intelligence and adaptability, ultimately providing enduring support for cultivating highly skilled dental professionals equipped to meet the demands of the modern era.

Conclusion

This study successfully developed and validated the effectiveness of the "ARCS + BOPPPS" blended teaching model in Endodontics education. The model not only significantly enhanced the utilization efficiency of the teaching platform and resources and strengthened students’ learning motivation, but also effectively cultivated their practical skills and innovative thinking abilities. Importantly, it facilitated the transition of teaching objectives from low-level knowledge retention to the development of higher-order comprehensive competencies, offering an actionable and transferable framework for reforming professional curricula in dental education.

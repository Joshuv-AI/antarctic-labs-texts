Stable cohomology of universal character varieties
Ishan Banerjee, Faye Jackson, Anne Larsen, Sam Payne, Xiyan Zhong

Abstract
. We study the universal PGL n \PGL_{n} -character variety over ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} whose fiber over a point [ C ] [C] is the space of PGL n \PGL_{n} -local systems on the curve C C . We use nonabelian Hodge theory and properties of Saito’s mixed Hodge modules to show that the Leray-Serre spectral sequence for the projection to ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} degenerates at E 2 E_{2} . As an application, we prove that the rational cohomology of these varieties stabilizes in the limit as g → ∞ g\to\infty and compute the stable limit. We also deduce similar results for the universal G G -character variety over ℳ g , 1 \mathcal{M}_{g,1} whose fiber over a punctured curve is the variety of G G -local systems with fixed central monodromy around the puncture, for G = GL n G=\GL_{n} or SL n \SL_{n} .



1.
Introduction

The degree
k
cohomology group of the moduli space of curves of genus
g
is independent of
g
for
k\leq\frac{2g-2}{3}
. These stable cohomology groups were computed by Madsen and Weiss
[
MW07
]
, confirming a conjecture of Mumford
[
Mum83
]
.
Subsequently, Ellenberg, Venkatesh, and Westerland initiated an analogous study of cohomological stability for Hurwitz spaces, which parametrize branched
G
-covers of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
for a finite group
G
or, equivalently,
G
-local systems on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
minus finitely many branch points
[
EVW16
]
. More recently, Landesman and Levy computed the stable cohomology groups of these
G
-Hurwitz spaces
[
LL25
]
as the number of branch points goes to
\infty
, and Putman
[
Put23
, Theorem C]
proved a homological stability result for
G
-Hurwitz spaces of a genus
g
curve with one boundary component in the limit as
g\to\infty
.

Inspired by this recent progress, we investigate analogous stability phenomena for the cohomology groups of universal character varieties that parametrize
G
-local systems on smooth curves of genus
g
, for certain algebraic groups
G
. We prove that these groups stabilize in the limit as
g\to\infty
and compute the stable limits.

Our first such result is for
G=\PGL_{n}
. We consider the universal character variety
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
whose fiber over
[C]
is the character variety

[\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(C),\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))/\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})],

parametrizing
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-local systems on
C
via the monodromy representation. This character variety breaks into pieces indexed by
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, based on the obstruction to lifting a homomorphism to
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
to
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, which is locally constant. We study the stable cohomology of the components corresponding
to
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
, which are smooth.
See §
2
for precise definitions and further details.

To describe the stable cohomology groups we use the following notation. Let

(1)

V_{g}:=H^{1}(S_{g};\mathbb{Q})

be the first rational cohomology group of a closed orientable surface of genus
g
, viewed as a
\Mod_{g}
-module or, equivalently, a local system on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. We consider
V_{g}
as a graded object, supported in degree
1
, so
V_{g}[-i]
is supported in degree
i+1
. We also define

(2)

V_{\circ}:=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}[-2],\quad\quad W_{n}:=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}[-2]\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Q}[2-2n],\quad\quad\quad\widetilde{W}_{n}:=W_{n}/\mathbb{Q}.

Here, the graded vector space
V_{\circ}
may be thought of as the even cohomology of a closed orientable surface of arbitrary genus, supported in degrees 0 and 2. Likewise,
W_{n}
and
\widetilde{W}_{n}
may be viewed as the cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
and the reduced cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, respectively.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
and
k\leq 2(g-1)(n-1)+2
,
the cohomology group
H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to the degree
k
part of the graded vector space

(3)

H^{*}\big(\mathcal{M}_{g};\textstyle\bigwedge^{\bullet}(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n}))\otimes\Sym^{\bullet}(V_{\circ}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n}).

Corollary 1.2
.

For
k\leq\frac{2g-2}{3}
, the group
H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})
does not depend on
g
and vanishes if
k
is odd. If, in addition,
k\leq 2n-1
then this group is independent of
n
.

Corollary
1.2
follows from Theorem
1.1
via Looijenga’s results on the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
with symplectic coefficients
[
Loo96
]
. See Section
4.2
for further details on the stable cohomology groups. The dimensions of the stable cohomology groups of
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{M}_{g},n,1)
for
n\leq 7
in even degrees up to 20 are displayed in
Figure

1
.

The following statement is a key technical step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which is also of independent interest. Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the forgetful map taking a
\PGL_{n}
-local system on a smooth curve to the underlying curve.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d
and
n
be coprime positive integers, and let
g\geq 2
. The Leray–Serre spectral sequence for
\pi\colon\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
degenerates at
E_{2}
.

We prove this degeneration using the relative nonabelian Hodge correspondence
[
Sim94
]
, purity of the cohomology of the moduli space of Higgs bundles
[
HRV15
, Corollary 1.3.2]
, and a splitting result for pure Hodge modules
[
Sai90
, (4.5.4)]
. We also present a second proof using only classical Hodge theory and manipulations in the derived category of constructible sheaves. Theorem
1.1
is deduced from the degeneration of the Leray-Serre spectral sequence for
\pi
using previously known results on generators and relations of the cohomology of the fibers (
[
Mar02
, Theorem 7]
and
[
HRV08
, Lemma 4.1.12]
).

We also consider universal
G
-character varieties over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
, for
G
=
\GL_{n}
or
\SL_{n}
. The fiber of the universal
G
-character variety over a punctured curve
C
is given by

\{\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(C),G(\mathbb{C})):\phi(\gamma)=e^{2\pi id/n}\Id\}/\PGL(\mathbb{C}).

Here,
\gamma
is a chosen loop around the puncture. These universal
G
-character varieties admit natural quotient maps to the pullback of
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)
, and the cohomology groups of the character varieties for these three groups are closely related.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
and
k\leq 2(g-1)(n-1)+2
, the cohomology group
H^{k}(M^{\SL}_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d))
is isomorphic to the degree
k
part of the graded vector space

H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1},\textstyle{\bigwedge^{\bullet}}(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n}))\otimes\Sym^{\bullet}(V_{\circ}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n}).

Similarly, for
k\leq 2(g-1)(n-1)+2
, the cohomology group
H^{k}(M^{\GL}_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d))
is isomorphic to the degree
k
part of the graded vector space

H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1},\textstyle{\bigwedge^{\bullet}}(V_{g}\otimes W_{n}))\otimes\Sym^{\bullet}(V_{\circ}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n}).

For
k\leq(2g-2)/3
, these groups are independent of
g
and vanish when
k
is odd.

We are not aware of a relative non-abelian Hodge correspondence for
\SL
- and
\GL
-character varieties in the literature that would allow a direct proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Instead, we deduce this statement from the above results for
\PGL
-character varieties. The final statements about stable cohomology in low degrees follows, just as Corollary
1.2
follows from Theorem
3.1
, using results of Kawazumi and Randal-Williams on the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
with twisted coefficients
[
Kaw08
,
RW18
]
. See Section
4.3
for further discussion of the stable cohomology. The dimensions of the stable cohomology groups of
M_{B}^{\SL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},2,1)
in even degrees up to 20 are displayed in Figure

.

Remark 1.5
.

By Theorem
1.1
, the rational cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)
is independent of the choice of
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
. We do not know any way of deducing this from first principles. The fibers of
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
are naturally viewed as algebraic varieties defined over
\mathbb{Q}[e^{2\pi i/n}]
. The fibers for varying
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
are Galois conjugate, giving an isomorphism of cohomology (with
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-coefficients). Such arguments do not apply to the total space of the universal character varieties, which are constructed analytically (Definition
2.1
).
Our proof of Theorem
1.1
depends on the relative non-abelian Hodge correspondence, which produces a homeomorphism over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
between the universal character variety
M_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)
on the Betti side and a universal moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles of degree
d

M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)
on the Dolbeault side. The fact that the cohomology is independent of the choice of
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
does not have an obvious geometric explanation in the Higgs bundle setting, though the result fits into a more general conjecture about the behavior of Gopakumar-Vafa invariants on Calabi-Yau 3-folds. See
[
MS23
, §0.3]
.

2.
Universal character varieties

In this section, we start by defining the universal
\PGL_{n}
-character variety over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. We then define the universal
G
-character varieties over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
, for
G
equal to
\GL_{n}
or
\SL_{n}
. We also discuss the natural relations between these spaces.

Let
S_{g}
be a closed oriented surface of genus
g\geq 2
, and let
\Mod_{g}:=\pi_{0}(\Diff^{+}(S_{g}))
be its mapping class group. The group
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
acts by conjugation on the affine variety

X_{g,n}:=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})).

Let
\mu_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}
be the
n
th roots of unity, which we identify with
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
via
d\mapsto e^{2\pi id/n}
. By the short exact sequence

1\to\mu_{n}\to\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to 1,

the obstruction to lifting a representation
\phi\colon\pi_{1}(S_{g})\to\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
to
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is given by an element of
H^{2}(S_{g};\mu_{n})=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, and this obstruction is locally constant on
X_{g,n}
. We write
X_{g,n,d}
for the subvariety parametrizing homomorphisms with obstruction class
d\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Via the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence,
X_{g,n,d}
parametrizes framed
\PGL_{n}
-bundles with flat connection that lift to
C^{\infty}
-vector bundles of degree
d
mod
n
.

We now define the degree
d

\PGL_{n}
-character variety to be the quotient stack

\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d):=[X_{g,n,d}/\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})],

where
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
acts by conjugation on the target. Likewise, we define the degree
d
coarse
\PGL_{n}
-character variety to be the affine GIT quotient

(4)

M_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)=X_{g,n,d}/\!\!/\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

In other words,
M_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
is the affine variety whose coordinate ring is the ring of
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant regular functions on
X_{g,n,d}
. The subscript
B
indicates that these character varieties appear on the Betti side of the nonabelian Hodge correspondence.

Assume
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
. Then
X_{g,n,d}
is smooth and irreducible, and
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
acts with finite stabilizers
[
HRV08
, Theorem 2.2.12 and Corollary 3.5.5]
.
In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
is a smooth orbifold, and projection to the coarse space
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)\to M_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
induces an isomorphism

(5)

H^{*}(M_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d),\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d),\mathbb{Q}).

Let
\mathcal{T}_{g}
denote the Teichmüller space of
S_{g}
, which parametrizes complex structures on
S_{g}
up to the action of the identity component of
\Diff^{+}(S_{g})
. It carries a natural action of
\Mod_{g}
, and the quotient is the moduli space of curves
\mathcal{T}_{g}/\Mod_{g}\cong\mathcal{M}_{g}
. The outer action of
\Mod_{g}
on
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
also induces a
\Mod_{g}
-action on the character variety
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
d
coprime to
n
, the
universal degree
d

\PGL
-character variety
is

\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{g}\times_{\Mod_{g}}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d).

By construction, this universal character variety is a smooth and irreducible orbifold. It is a smooth fiber bundle over the moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{g}
with fiber
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Although the base and fiber of
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
each carry a natural algebraic structure, our construction does not immediately endow the total space with such a structure. In order to apply techniques from algebraic geometry to study the rational cohomology of
M_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)
, we pass to a homeomorphic algebraic moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles on the Dolbeault side of the nonabelian Hodge correspondence.

We now consider degree
d

G
-character varieties for a surface with a puncture, for
G=\SL_{n}
or
\GL_{n}
.
Let
S_{g,1}
be an orientable surface of genus
g
with one puncture, and let
\gamma
denote a loop around the puncture. For
d\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, we consider the affine variety

(6)

Y^{G}_{g,n,d}:=\{\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g,1}),G(\mathbb{C})):\phi(\gamma)=e^{2\pi id/n}\Id_{n}\},

on which
G(\mathbb{C})
acts by conjugation. If
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
then
Y^{G}_{g,n,d}
is smooth and irreducible, and the
G(\mathbb{C})
-action factors through a free action of
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})

[
HRV08
, Corollary 2.2.7]
. In these cases, the degree
d

G
-character variety is the quotient

(7)

M_{B}^{G}(S_{g,1},n,d)\coloneqq Y^{G}_{g,n,d}/\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

Unlike
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
, which is an orbifold (or smooth Deligne–Mumford stack with nontrivial stabilizers), the space
M_{B}^{G}(S_{g,1},n,d)
is a manifold (or smooth algebraic variety).

Because the outer action of the mapping class group
\Mod_{g,1}\coloneq\pi_{0}(\Diff^{+}(S_{g,1}))
on
\pi_{1}(S_{g,1})
preserves the conjugacy class of
\gamma

[
FM12
, Theorem 8.8]
,
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{G}(S_{g,1},n,d)
inherits an action of the mapping class group
\Mod_{g,1}
of
S_{g,1}
, by precomposition. Let
\mathcal{T}_{g,1}
denote the Teichmüller space of
S_{g,1}
, which likewise carries a natural action of
\Mod_{g,1}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The universal degree
d

G
-character variety for
G=\GL_{n}
or
\SL_{n}
is

(8)

M_{B}^{G}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{g,1}\times_{\Mod(S_{g,1})}M_{B}^{G}(S_{g,1},n,d).

Remark 2.4
.

When
d=0
, the condition
\phi(\gamma)=\Id_{n}
from (
6
) is equivalent to the condition that
\phi
factors through the fundamental group of the unpunctured surface
\pi_{1}(S_{g,1})\to\pi_{1}(S_{g}).
The outer action of
\Mod_{g,1}
on
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
then factors through the outer action of
\Mod_{g}
, and
M_{B}^{G}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,0)

is the pullback of an analogously defined universal degree 0
G
-character variety over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Hence, one can study degree 0 relative
G
-character varieties for families of unpunctured curves, as in
[
Sim94
, §6 and §9]
.

However, if
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
then
M_{B}^{G}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
is not the pullback of a space over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, because the kernel of the map
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
, acts nontrivially on
M_{B}^{G}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
. This can be seen from the description of its outer action on
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
in terms of Dehn twists
[
FM12
, Fact 4.7]
.

We define a relative degree
d

\PGL
-character variety over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
(or indeed any family of curves) by pullback:

M_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d):=\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\times_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}\mathcal{M}_{g,1}.

This is analogous to the definition for
G=\GL_{n}
or
\SL_{n}
, since the
\PGL
-character variety parametrizes homomorphisms that factor through
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
.

We now discuss relations among the universal
G
-character varieties for varying groups
G
. Note that
\Gamma:=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g,1}),\mu_{n})
acts on
Y_{g,n,d}^{G}
via the inclusion of
\mu_{n}
in the center of
G
, and this action descends to an action on the character variety
M_{B}^{G}(S_{g,1},n,d)
. Passing to the universal setting, the relative group object

\widetilde{\Gamma}:=\mathcal{T}_{g,1}\times_{\Mod_{g,1}}\Gamma

acts on
M_{B}^{G}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The quotient of
M_{B}^{\SL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
by
\widetilde{\Gamma}
over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
is the morphism to
M_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
induced by the projection
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Similarly, the quotient of
M_{B}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},1,0)\times_{\mathcal{M}_{g,1}}M_{B}^{\SL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
by
\widetilde{\Gamma}
acting diagonally is the morphism to
M_{B}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
induced by the product
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

First, note that
\PGL_{n}=\SL_{n}/\mu_{n}
, where
\mu_{n}
acts by its central embedding. Similarly,
\GL_{n}=(\GL_{1}\times\SL_{n})/\mu_{n}
, where
\mu_{n}
acts diagonally. Taking
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g,1}),-)
gives

X_{g,n,d}=Y_{g,n,d}^{\SL}/\Gamma\quad\mbox{ and }\quad Y^{\GL}_{g,n,d}=(Y_{g,1,0}^{\GL}\times Y_{g,n,d}^{\SL})/\Gamma.

In each case, the action of
\mu_{n}
commutes with both conjugation by
\PGL_{n}
and also the action of
\Mod_{g,1}
on
\pi_{1}(S_{g,1})
, inducing the required morphism of universal character varieties. ∎

3.
Degeneration of the Leray spectral sequence

In this section, we prove the degeneration of the Leray spectral sequence for the universal degree
d

\PGL
-character variety
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Similar results for universal degree
d

\GL
- and
\SL
-character varieties follow as corollaries. Our proof uses the relative nonabelian Hodge correspondence for
\PGL
. This relates the universal degree
d

\PGL_{n}
-character variety on the Betti side of the correspondence to the universal moduli space of semistable degree
d

\PGL_{n}
-Higgs bundles on the Dolbeault side. On the Dolbeault side, the projection to
\mathcal{M}_{g}
shares many cohomological properties of a smooth and proper morphism of algebraic varieties, despite not being proper.
Using these properties, we prove the following strong form of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
and let
\pi\colon\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the projection from the universal degree
d

\PGL
-character variety. Then
R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}\cong\oplus_{i}R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}[-i]
in the bounded derived category of constructible
\mathbb{Q}
-sheaves on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. In particular, the Leray spectral sequence

E_{2}^{pq}=H^{p}(\mathcal{M}_{g},R^{q}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\implies H^{p+q}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{M}_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})

degenerates at
E_{2}
.

3.1.
The moduli spaces

We now introduce the moduli spaces of Higgs bundles that appear on the Dolbeault side of the nonabelian Hodge correspondence.

Recall that a

(E,\theta)

A Higgs bundle

\mu(F)<\mu(E)
(resp.
\mu(F)\leq\mu(E))
for every
\theta

. When

M^{\GL}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(C,n,d)
denote the coarse moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles of degree
d
and rank
n
on
C
, as constructed by Simpson
[
Sim94
]
.

For moduli of
\SL
- and
\PGL
-Higgs bundles, we follow the definitions from
[
HT03
, §2]
.
Fixing an auxiliary degree
d
line bundle
L
on

, the moduli space of
\SL
-Higgs bundles
M^{\SL}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(C,n,L)
is the subspace of
M^{\GL}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(C,n,d)
parametrizing Higgs bundles
(E,\theta)
such that

and
\Tr\theta=0
, when
\theta
is viewed as an
\omega_{C}
-twisted endomorphism of

Definition 3.6
.

The moduli space of
\PGL

L\cdot(E,\theta):=(L\otimes E,\theta).
For a different choice of degree

Let

-fixed locus

If

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
3.1
using Saito’s theory of Hodge modules

To handle constructible sheaves equipped with Hodge structures, we use Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules
[
Sai90
]
. The key features are as follows. There is a category of mixed Hodge modules
\MHM(X)
on each complex variety
X
, with a standard six-functor formalism. There is a functor

(11)

\mathrm{rat}\colon D^{b}\MHM(X)\to D^{b}_{c}(\mathbb{Q}_{X})

taking a mixed Hodge module to its underlying rational structure, which is compatible with the six functors
[
Sai90
, Theorem 0.1]
. Moreover,
\MHM(\mathrm{pt})
is the category of polarizable
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures
[
Sai90
, (4.2.12)]
, and pushing forward the trivial Hodge module
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}
to a point we recover Deligne’s mixed Hodge structure on
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})

[
Sai90
, p. 328]
.

Remark 3.13
.

Deligne’s theory of mixed Hodge structures extends to Deligne–Mumford stacks and, more generally, simplicial objects in the category of algebraic varieties
[
Del74
]
. Saito’s theory likewise extends to Deligne–Mumford stacks, as explained in
[
Tub25
]
. We use the basic properties of mixed Hodge structures and mixed Hodge modules on Deligne–Mumford stacks without further comment.

We also note that all of the Deligne–Mumford stacks we consider are global quotients of algebraic varieties by finite groups. Thus, our arguments can be rephrased in terms of equivariant mixed Hodge modules and invariant subspaces of mixed Hodge structures on varieties with finite group actions without any other essential changes.

The needed input from the theory of mixed Hodge modules is the following lemma:

Lemma 3.14
.

If
\pi\colon U\to S
is a smooth morphism of smooth complex Deligne–Mumford stacks such that the cohomology of each fiber
H^{k}(U_{s};\mathbb{Q})
has a pure Hodge structure of weight
k
for each degree
k
and
s\in S
, then

R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{U}\cong\oplus_{i}R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{U}[-i]
. In particular, the corresponding Leray spectral sequence degenerates at
E_{2}
.

Proof.

Our goal is to show that the (derived) pushforward
\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}\in D^{b}\MHM(S)
is
pure of weight 0

[
Sai90
, §4.5]
, i.e., that
\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{i}\mathcal{H}^{j}(\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U})=0
for
i\neq j
. This condition guarantees that
\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}\cong\oplus_{i}\mathcal{H}^{i}(\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U})[-i]\in D^{b}\MHM(S)

[
Sai90
, (4.5.4)]
, and then applying the rat functor (
11
), one recovers the same identity on the level of constructible sheaves.

To prove the purity condition, it suffices to check that for every
s\in S
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have that
\mathcal{H}^{k}i_{s}^{*}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}
is of weight
\leq k
and
\mathcal{H}^{k}i_{s}^{!}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{U}^{H}
is of weight
\geq k

[
Sai90
, (4.6.1)]
. We start with the identity
[
Sai90
, (4.4.3)]

i_{s}^{!}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}\cong\pi_{U_{s}*}i^{!}_{U_{s}}\mathbb{Q}_{U}^{H}.

For the first assertion, for
d:=\dim S
, note that
i_{s}^{*}=i_{s}^{!}(-d)[-2d]
by smoothness of
S
and
i_{U_{s}}^{*}=i_{U_{s}}^{!}(-d)[-2d]
by smoothness of
S
and
f

[
Sai90
, (4.4.1)]
, and thus

i_{s}^{*}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}=i_{s}^{!}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}(-d)[-2d]=\pi_{U_{s}*}i^{!}_{U_{s}}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}(-d)[-2d]=\pi_{U_{s}*}i^{*}_{U_{s}}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}=H^{*}(U_{s};\mathbb{Q}),

which is of weight 0 by assumption.
Similarly, for the second assertion,

i_{s}^{!}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}=\pi_{U_{s}*}i^{!}_{U_{s}}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}=\pi_{U_{s}*}i_{U_{s}}^{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{U}(-d)[-2d]=H^{*-2d}(U_{s};\mathbb{Q})(-d),

which is again of weight 0 by assumption.
∎

First proof of Theorem
3.1

By Corollary

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(A)[1],C_{<0}[1])\xrightarrow{\circ 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(C),C_{<0}[1])\xrightarrow{\circ g}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(B),C_{<0}[1]),

the second map must be injective; then since commutativity of the square shows that
f\circ g=0
, we must have
f=0
, as needed.

We now claim that there is a triangle

A_{<0}\to B_{<0}\to C_{<0}\xrightarrow{+1}

satisfying the hypotheses of the lemma. To prove this, we apply the 9-lemma
[
BBD82
, Proposition 1.1.11]
to the commutative square coming from applying
id\to\tau^{\geq 0}
to
A\to B
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}^{0}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}^{0}(B).}

This gives a
3\times 3
diagram all of whose rows and columns are distinguished triangles:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}^{0}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{<0}[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}^{0}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{<0}[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}^{0}(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{<0}[1]}

(The fact that the second column forms a short exact sequence uses our assumption that the long exact sequence of cohomology breaks into short exact sequences.) This gives a rotated form of our desired triangle. Furthermore,
B_{<0}
is split, as

\tau_{<0}(\oplus_{i\leq 0}\mathcal{H}^{i}(B)[-i])=\oplus_{i<0}\mathcal{H}^{i}(B)[-i],

and the long exact sequence of cohomology for this triangle is that of the original triangle in degree
<0
and 0 otherwise, thus satisfying the conditions that
C_{<0}\to A_{<0}[1]
induces a trivial map on cohomology and the resulting short exact sequences are split.

Thus we can continue to apply the argument to split
C_{<0}\cong\mathcal{H}^{-1}(C)[1]\oplus C_{<-1}
, etc. Again using the assumption that the
t
-structure is bounded, there is some integer
n
such that
C
has no cohomology in degrees
<n
, thus
C_{<n}=0
, and so the inductively constructed isomorphism

in fact gives a splitting of
C
.
∎

Proof of Corollaries

Here, we deduce the splitting results for the

Proof of Corollary
3.5
.

First, recall that the splitting in Theorem

is stated for the universal
\PGL
-character variety over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. By smooth base change

\mathcal{M}_{g}

\mathcal{M}_{g,1}

Let
\Gamma:=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g},1),\mu_{n})
. By Proposition
2.5
, the relative
\PGL
-character variety
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)

It follows that
R^{i}\pi_{\PGL*}\mathbb{Q}\to(R^{i}\pi_{\SL*}\mathbb{Q})^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}
is an isomorphism for all

(13)

R\pi_{\PGL*}\mathbb{Q}\cong(R\pi_{\SL*}\mathbb{Q})^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}.

Let
C_{g,n}:=2n^{2}(1-p_{n}^{-1})(g-1)
, where

is the smallest prime factor of

. We claim that
R^{i}\pi_{\SL*}\mathbb{Q}
is
\widetilde{\Gamma}
-invariant for

It remains to prove the claim. By

H^{i}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\PGL}(C,n,d);\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{i}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\SL}(C,n,d);\mathbb{Q})\mbox{ for }i\leq C_{g,n}.

By the nonabelian Hodge correspondence for

and
\PGL

M_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)\cong M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\PGL}(C,n,d)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad M_{B}^{\SL}(S_{g,1},n,d)\cong M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\SL}(C,n,d).

and

In the proof of Corollary
3.4
, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\pi_{i}\colon X_{i}\to S
,
i=1,2
, be a submersion of complex manifolds, with
\pi\colon X_{1}\times_{S}X_{2}\to S
. Suppose that each
R^{j}\pi_{2*}\mathbb{Q}
is locally trivial. Then

R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}\cong R\pi_{1*}\mathbb{Q}\otimes R\pi_{2*}\mathbb{Q}

in
D^{b}_{c}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})
. The same holds if the base
S
is a smooth complex Deligne–Mumford stack.

Proof.

All of the functors in this proof are derived; for simplicity we omit this from the notation. We start with the case that
S
is a manifold. The strategy is to apply Verdier duality to the proper relative Künneth formula
[
KS90
, Exercise II.18(i)]

(16)

\pi_{!}(\mathbb{Q})=\pi_{1!}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\pi_{2!}\mathbb{Q}.

On the left side we have

D\pi_{!}\mathbb{Q}=\pi_{*}D\mathbb{Q}=\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}[\dim X_{1}\times_{S}X_{2}]

[
Dim04
, Theorem 3.2.17(iii) and Proposition 3.3.7]
. On the right side we have

D(\pi_{1!}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\pi_{2!}\mathbb{Q})=\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{1!}\mathbb{Q},\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{2!}\mathbb{Q},\omega_{S}))=\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{1!}\mathbb{Q},\pi_{2*}D\mathbb{Q}),

where for the first equality we use
[
KS90
, Proposition 2.6.3(ii)]
.
Identifying
D\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}[\dim X_{2}]
and using the assumption that the sheaves
R^{j}\pi_{2*}\mathbb{Q}
are locally trivial we see from
[
dCM09
, p. 624]
that

D(\pi_{1!}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\pi_{2!}\mathbb{Q})=\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{1!}\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q}[\dim S])\otimes\pi_{2*}\mathbb{Q}[\dim X_{2}-\dim S]

=D\pi_{1!}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\pi_{2*}\mathbb{Q}[\dim X_{2}-\dim S]=(\pi_{1*}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\pi_{2*}\mathbb{Q})[\dim X_{1}+\dim X_{2}-\dim S].

Thus the needed identity is obtained by applying Verdier duality to (
16
) and shifting by
\dim X_{1}\times_{S}X_{2}
.

If
S
is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack, we choose an étale cover
p:S^{\prime}\to S
by a smooth variety
S^{\prime}
. By smooth base change, the canonical morphism
R\pi_{1*}\mathbb{Q}\otimes R\pi_{2*}\mathbb{Q}\to R\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}
becomes an isomorphism after pulling back to
S^{\prime}
. In particular, the mapping cone
C
satisfies
p^{*}C=0
, and thus
C=0
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.4
.

By Proposition
2.5
, the character variety
M_{B}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
is a quotient of
M_{B}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},1,0)\times M_{B}^{\SL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
by the relative group object
\widetilde{\Gamma}
with fiber
\Gamma=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g,1}),\mu_{n})
over
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
. We have an isomorphism
R\pi_{\GL*}\mathbb{Q}=(R\pi_{\GL_{1}\times\SL}\mathbb{Q})^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}
, as in (
13
).
By Lemma
3.16
, using that
M_{B}^{\SL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
and
M_{B}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},1,0)
are locally trivial fibrations with smooth fibers, we see that

R\pi_{\GL_{1}\times\SL}\mathbb{Q}=R\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q}\otimes R\pi_{\SL*}\mathbb{Q}.

To show the splitting of
R\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q}
, we use Simpson’s (
d=0
) nonabelian Hodge theorem
M_{B}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},1,0)\cong M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},1,0)

[
Sim94
, Proposition 6.8 and Theorem 7.18]
and the fact that, as noted in Remark
3.12
, the proof of Theorem
3.1
holds for
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},1,0)
.
Furthermore,
\Gamma
acts trivially on the cohomology of the fibers. This is because the action on the fibers can be extended to the multiplication action of the connected group
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g,1}),\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2g}
on itself. Then, since

is topologically locally trivial, we have
(R\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q})^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=R\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, we conclude that

(17)

R\pi_{\GL*}\mathbb{Q}=(R\pi_{\GL_{1}\times\SL}\mathbb{Q})^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=(R\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q}\otimes R\pi_{\SL*}\mathbb{Q})^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=R\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q}\otimes(R\pi_{\SL*}\mathbb{Q})^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}.

Applying the splitting for
R\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q}
above and the splitting for
(R\pi_{\SL*}\mathbb{Q})^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\cong R\pi_{\PGL*}\mathbb{Q}
as described in Corollary

, we get the desired splitting for

.
∎

In this section, we prove Theorem

and Theorem

by combining the degeneration of the Leray spectral sequence at

(Theorem

4.1.
Monodromy representations

The structure map of the universal
\PGL
-character variety
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
is a topologically locally trivial fiber bundle, as are

M_{B}^{\SL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g,1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad M_{B}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g,1}.

We now study the local systems on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
that arise from the cohomology of the fibers with their respective monodromy representations.

As in §
2
, we write
X_{g,n,d}
for the subspace of
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
parametrizing maps whose obstruction to lifting to
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is
d\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\widetilde{S}_{g}
be the universal cover of
S_{g}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The tautological
\PGL_{n}
-bundle over
S_{g}\times\mathcal{M}^{\PGL}_{B}(S_{g},n,d)
is

\mathcal{P}:=\big(\widetilde{S}_{g}\times X_{g,n,d}\times\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})\big)/\big(\pi_{1}(S_{g},p)\times\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})\big).

Here, the action of
\pi_{1}(S_{g},p)\times\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
can be described as

(\gamma,M)\cdot(x,\phi,N):=(\gamma x,M\phi M^{-1},M\phi(\gamma)N).

The structure map to
S_{g}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
is induced by projection to the first two factors.

Remark 4.2
.

The universal
\PGL_{n}
bundle on
S_{g}\times M_{B}^{\GL}(S_{g,1},n,d)
described in
[
HT04
, §1]
is the pullback of
\mathcal{P}
by the quotient map
M_{B}^{\GL}(S_{g,1},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
, as is the universal
\PGL_{n}
-bundle on
S_{g}\times M_{B}^{\SL}(S_{g,1},n,d)
in
[
HRV08
, §4.1]
.

The generators for
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})
are the Künneth components of the Chern classes of this tautological bundle. We make the following (basis-free) definition:

Definition 4.3
.

Let
p_{k}
be the projection of
S_{g}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
onto the
k^{\textrm{th}}
component.
For
i\in\{0,1,2\}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
we define the Künneth component vector space

V_{i,j}:=\{p_{2*}(c_{j}(\mathcal{P})\cup p_{1}^{*}(x)):x\in H^{i}(S_{g};\mathbb{Q})\}\subset H^{i+2j-2}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q}).

Proposition 4.4
.

The subspaces
V_{i,j}
for
0\leq i\leq 2
and
2\leq j\leq n
generate the ring
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{\PGL}_{B}(S_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})
with no relations in degrees less than or equal to
2(g-1)(n-1)+2
, and
\dim V_{i,j}=\dim H^{i}(S_{g};\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

The generation statement is a consequence of Markman’s tautological generation result for
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\GL}(C,n,d)

[
Mar02
, Theorem 7]
, as reinterpreted on the Betti side by Hausel and Thaddeus
[
HT04
, (5.1)]
. Picking a homogeneous basis of
H^{*}(S_{g};\mathbb{Q})
, there are no relations in degrees
\leq 2(g-1)(n-1)+2
among the images of these elements in
V_{*,j}
by
[
HRV08
, Lemma 4.1.12]
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In later work
[
Mar07
, Theorem 3]
, Markman strengthened the generation result from rational to integral cohomology. In particular, in degrees
\leq 2(g-1)(n-1)+2
one concludes that
H^{*}(M^{\GL}_{B}(S_{g,1},n,d);\mathbb{Z})
is torsion-free. Then using the quotient description of Proposition
2.5
, we conclude that the cohomology groups of the fibers
H^{*}(M^{\SL}_{B}(S_{g,1},n,d);\mathbb{Z}[n^{-1}])
and
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{\PGL}_{B}(S_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Z}[n^{-1}])
are torsion-free in the same degree range. However, in order to deduce a result about integral cohomology of the universal spaces, one would also need torsion-freeness results for the various cohomology groups of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
with symplectic coefficients.

Proposition 4.6
.

The Chern classes
c_{i}(\mathcal{P})\in H^{2i}(S_{g}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})
of the universal
\PGL_{n}
-bundle
\mathcal{P}
on
S_{g}\times\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)
defined in Definition
4.1
are fixed by the mapping class group action.

Proof.

We follow the strategy of
[
HT04
, (5.1)]
. For any element of
\Mod_{g}
, we may choose a representative diffeomorphism
f
of
S_{g}
fixing a chosen point
p
. It is then straightforward to check that
\mathcal{P}
and
f^{*}\mathcal{P}
are both
\PGL_{n}
-equivariant flat
\PGL_{n}
-bundles over
S_{g}\times X_{g,n,d}
trivialized over
\{p\}\times X_{g,n,d}
such that the monodromy representation of the restriction to
S_{g}\times\{\phi\}
is given by
\phi
, and thus
\mathcal{P}\cong f^{*}\mathcal{P}
.

∎

Remark 4.7
.

A cleaner but more technical proof of the previous proposition goes as follows: take a simplicial set with geometric realization homotopic to
S_{g}
, and view this as a simplicial scheme
X
by assigning
\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{C}
to each element. Then

\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(X,\mathrm{B}\PGL_{n}),

where

is the internal Hom in the category of simplicial schemes. From this perspective, it is clear that classes pulled back under the evaluation map

X\times{\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}}(X,\mathrm{B}\PGL_{n})\to\mathrm{B}\PGL_{n}

are canonical, and in particular invariant under homeomorphisms of
S_{g}
. (See
[
She17
]
for more discussion.)

Corollary 4.8
.

The subspaces
V_{0,j},V_{2,j}\subset H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(S_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})
are fixed by
\Mod_{g}
, and

is the standard symplectic representation.

By construction, we have a
\Mod_{g}
-fixed vector

The projection of
c_{j}(\mathcal{P})
in each

is then also fixed (and nonzero by Proposition

). Since

acts irreducibly on each

and the dimensions match (again by Proposition

), we conclude that

. In other words,

and
V_{2,j}
are the trivial representation, and

∎

The previous corollary, combined with Proposition

, suffices to describe all the local systems

for

\PGL
result, together with the proof of Corollary

\SL
, the proof of Corollary
3.4
, and one last observation:

Proposition 4.9
.

The
\Mod_{g}

Proof.

We note that

∎

4.2.
Stable rational cohomology of
\mathcal{M}^{\PGL}_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d)

We relate
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})
to the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
with coefficients in exterior powers of direct sums of copies of the symplectic local system. To simplify the notation of the relevant local systems on
\mathcal{M}_{g}

(18)

V_{\circ}:=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}[-2],\quad\quad W_{n}:=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}[-2]\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Q}[2-2n],\quad\quad\quad\widetilde{W}_{n}:=W_{n}/\mathbb{Q}.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From Theorem
1.3

(19)

H^{k}(M^{\PGL}_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})\cong\bigoplus\limits_{p+q=k}H^{p}(\mathcal{M}_{g};H^{q}(\mathcal{M}^{\PGL}_{B}(S_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})).

H^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{\PGL}_{B}(S_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})\cong\wedge^{\bullet}(\oplus_{j=2}^{n}V_{1,j})\otimes\Sym^{\bullet}(\oplus_{j=2}^{n}V_{0,j}\oplus V_{2,j})

Using the notation (

as the following graded modules, where

Hence we conclude that in this range

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

We write
S_{\langle\lambda\rangle}(V_{g})
for the irreducible representation of
\Sp(2g,\mathbb{Q})
corresponding to the partition
\lambda
(in degree 0).
We begin by partially decomposing

\wedge^{\bullet}(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n})=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n-1}\wedge^{\bullet}(V_{g}[-2i]),

where

(20)

\wedge^{r}V_{g}\cong\begin{cases}\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{\frac{r}{2}}S_{\langle 1^{2i}\rangle}(V_{g})&\text{ if }r\leq g\text{ and }r\text{ is even}\\

Then in degree
\leq 3g

the cohomology

splits (independently of

) as a direct sum of terms of the form

H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g};S_{\langle 1^{r_{1}}\rangle}(V_{g})[s_{1}]\otimes\cdots\otimes S_{\langle 1^{r_{n-1}}\rangle}(V_{g})[s_{n-1}])

with

for some

satisfying

mod

.
Such a term has highest weight

satisfies

.
Furthermore, using the Littlewood-Richardson rule to compute the decomposition into irreducibles (see
[
KT87
]
), one sees that the set of irreducibles appearing in the decomposition of each term (
21
) in degrees

is independent of

; also each

appearing satisfies
|\lambda|\equiv\sum r_{i}
mod
2
.
Now, Looijenga
[
Loo96
, Theorem 1.1]
has calculated the stable cohomology of
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g};S_{\langle\lambda\rangle}(V_{g}))
in degree
\leq(2g-2)/3-|\lambda|

Since
\sum s_{i}\geq\sum r_{i}\geq|\lambda|
, we note that the contribution of
each irreducible piece of each term (
21
) to
H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g},\wedge^{\bullet}(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n}))
comes from Looijenga’s stable range when
k\leq(2g-2)/3
. In particular, this is independent of
g
.

Finally, when
H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g},\wedge^{\bullet}(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n}))
is decomposed as a sum of terms

H^{k-\sum s_{i}}(\mathcal{M}_{g};S_{\langle\lambda\rangle}(V_{g}))

with
|\lambda|\equiv\sum r_{i}\equiv\sum s_{i}
mod
2
,
it follows from Looijenga’s description that each such term is 0 when
k
is odd. The same then holds for the odd cohomology of

H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\PGL}(\mathcal{C}_{g},n,d);\mathbb{Q})=H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g},\wedge^{\bullet}(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n}))\otimes\Sym^{\bullet}(V_{\circ}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n})

since the second term is nontrivial only in even degree.

Finally, since
\widetilde{W}_{n}
and
\widetilde{W}_{n+1}
agree up to degree
2n
, we note that the degree
k\leq 2n-1
cohomology is independent of
n
.
∎

Decomposing
\wedge^{\bullet}(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n})
into irreducible representations and applying Looijenga’s result becomes rather complicated, but in the smallest nontrivial case
n=2
we have the following:

Example 4.10
.

In degree
k\leq(2g-2)/3
,
H^{k}(\mathcal{M}^{\PGL}_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{g},2,1);\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}3r+m\leq k\\
r\text{ even}\end{subarray}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\frac{r}{2}}\Big(\bigoplus_{\lambda|2i}E_{\lambda}\otimes H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g};\mathbb{Q})\Big)_{(k-3r-m+2i)}\otimes(\mathbb{Q}[\alpha_{2},\beta_{4}])_{(m)}

\displaystyle\oplus

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}3r+m\leq k\\
r\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\Big(\bigoplus_{\lambda|2i-1}E_{\lambda}\otimes H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g};\mathbb{Q})\Big)_{(k-3r-m+2i-1)}\otimes(\mathbb{Q}[\alpha_{2},\beta_{4}])_{(m)},

where

\lambda=1^{\ell_{1}}2^{\ell_{2}}3^{\ell_{3}}\cdots
is a numerical partition of
2i
(resp.
2i-1)
and

E_{\lambda}=\bigotimes_{t\geq 1}u_{2}^{\ell_{t}\cdot\max\{2,t-1\}}\mathbb{Q}[c_{2},c_{4},\cdots,c_{2\ell_{t}}].

Here
(m)
denotes the degree-
m
component, and the subscript of each variable is its degree. Note that in this range
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g};\mathbb{Q})
is freely generated by the classes
\kappa_{i}\in H^{2i}(\mathcal{M}_{g};\mathbb{Q})
.

4.3.
Stable rational cohomology of
M^{\SL}_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)
and
\mathcal{M}^{\GL}_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)

Let
\pi_{G}
denote the projection map
M_{B}^{G}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d)\to\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
for
G=\GL,\SL,\PGL
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We start with
\SL
.
Recall from the proof of Corollary
3.5
that
R^{i}\pi_{\PGL*}\mathbb{Q}=R^{i}\pi_{\SL*}\mathbb{Q}
up to degree

2n^{2}(1-p_{n}^{-1})(g-1)\geq n^{2}(g-1)\geq 2(g-1)(n-1)+2

(since the statement of the theorem is only meaningful when
g\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
).
The local systems
R^{i}\pi_{\PGL*}\mathbb{Q}
are pulled back from those described in Theorem
1.1
, so the description of the cohomology in degrees
\leq 2(g-1)(n-1)+2
is exactly as before, except now all local systems are pulled back to
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}.

Now we consider
\GL
. Combining equations (
13
) and (
17
) we have
R\pi_{\GL*}\mathbb{Q}=R\pi_{\PGL*}\mathbb{Q}\otimes R\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q}
, where
R^{i}\pi_{\PGL*}\mathbb{Q}
is as described before and
R^{i}\pi_{\GL_{1}*}\mathbb{Q}=\wedge^{i}V_{g}
by Proposition
4.9
. We conclude that

H^{*}(M_{B}^{\GL}(\mathcal{C}_{g,1},n,d);\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1},\textstyle{\bigwedge^{\bullet}}(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n})\otimes\Sym^{\bullet}(V_{\circ}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n})\otimes\textstyle{\bigwedge^{\bullet}}V_{g}),

matching the expression shown once we identify
(V_{g}\otimes\widetilde{W}_{n})\oplus V_{g}=V_{g}\otimes W_{n}
, where
W_{n}
is defined in (
18
). Finally, the stabilization argument proceeds exactly as before, except that the needed input is now
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1},S_{\langle\lambda\rangle}(V_{g}))
, which is deduced from
[
Kaw08
]
and
[
RW18
, Appendix B]
.
∎

As before, we present example computations in the case

Example 4.11

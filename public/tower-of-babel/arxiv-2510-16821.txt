Sparse variational regularization with oversmoothing penalty term in the scale of sequence spaces
Robert Plato, Bernd Hofmann

Abstract
In this work, we consider a class of linear ill-posed problems with operators that map from the sequence space ℓ r \ell_{r} ( r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 ) into a Banach space and in addition satisfy a conditional stability estimate in the scale of sequence spaces ℓ q , q ≥ 0 \ell_{q},\,q\geq 0 . For the regularization of such problems in the presence of deterministic noise, we consider variational regularization with a penalty functional either of the form ℛ p = ∥ ⋅ ∥ p p \mathcal{R}_{p}=\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p} for some p > 0 p>0 or in form of the counting measure ℛ 0 = ∥ ⋅ ∥ 0 \mathcal{R}_{0}=\|\cdot\|_{0} . The latter case guarantees sparsity of the corresponding regularized solutions. In this framework, we present first stability and then convergence rates for suitable a priori parameter choices. The results cover the oversmoothing situation, where the desired solution does not belong to the domain of definition of the considered penalty functional. The analysis of the oversmoothing case utilizes auxiliary elements that are defined by means of hard thresholding. Such technique can also be used for post processing to guarantee sparsity.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider linear ill-posed problems in the scale of real sequence spaces
\{\ell_{s}=\ell_{s}(\mathbb{N})\}_{0\leq s<\infty}

\displaystyle\ell_{s}=\{u=(u_{k})_{k=1,2,\ldots}\mid\|u\|_{s}<\infty\},\quad\|u\|_{s}:=\big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|u_{k}|^{s}\big)^{1/s},\qquad 0<s<\infty.

for
n
large enough. We are thus in a finite-dimensional setting, so
\|u_{n}-u_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|_{p}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for any
p>0
.

∎

4
A priori parameter choice

In this section, we investigate the regularization properties
of the variational regularization (
8
), i.e. we present convergence rates results for a suitable a priori parameter choice strategy.

As a preparation,
we consider the following non-negative real numbers,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\frac{a}{r}\cdot\frac{r-q}{a-q},\qquad\gamma_{2}=\frac{(q-p)a}{a-q},

(12)

where the utilized parameters are introduced in (
4
).
The numbers
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
correspond to the convergence rate and the conditional stability rate obtained for our setting as
\delta\to 0
,
cf. the following theorem for the details.
As a further preparation, we introduce the following a priori parameter choice,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\delta}=\delta^{\sigma+\frac{(q-p)a}{a-q}},\quad\delta>0,

(13)

which turns out to be suitable for our setting.

We are now in a position to present our main result on the regularization properties of
variational regularization (
8
).

Theorem 8
.

Let conditions (
4
)–(
7
) be satisfied.
An a priori parameter choice
\alpha=\alpha_{\delta}

of the form (
13
)
gives

\displaystyle\|u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\gamma_{1}}),\quad\mathcal{R}_{p}(u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta})=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\gamma_{2}})\quad\textup{as }\delta\to 0,

(14)

where the real numbers
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are given by (
12
).
In addition,
u_{\alpha}^{\delta}\in\ell_{p}
denotes a minimizer of the Tikhonov functional (
8
), and the sought-after solution of equation (
5
) satisfies
u^{\dagger}\in\ell_{q}
.

The case
q<p
means oversmoothing, and the proof utilizes auxiliaries then. Those auxiliaries are generated by hard thresholding; details are given in the following section. The proof of the theorem is then presented in Section
4.3
below.
We proceed with some important comments on the theorem and an example.

Remark 9
.

1.

We have linear convergence in (
14
) with
\gamma_{1}=1
for
q=0
when
u^{\dagger}
is sparse, and moreover in the limiting case
r=a
when the problem is well-posed.

2.

The convergence rate
\gamma_{1}
in (
14
) is independent of
p
, so the particular choice of the stabilizing functional has no impact on the rate of convergence.

3.

The second statement in (
14
) is a conditional stability estimate. It is of particular interest for the case
p=0
and means then

\|u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}\|_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\frac{qa}{a-q}})
as
\delta\to 0
. The latter estimate provides information on the
number of non-vanishing entries of the computed approximations.

Example 10
.

Consider the special case
r=2
, i.e. we have
A:\ell_{2}\to V
.
Let
u^{\dagger}\in\ell_{q}
, where
1<q<2
is conjugate to the parameter
a>2
, this is,
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{a}=1
.
We then have
\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
, i.e. Theorem
8
yields

\|u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{2}=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{1/2})
as
\delta\to 0
. This convergence rate is reasonable, since the range of the adjoint
A^{*}:V^{*}\to\ell_{2}
of the operator
A
satisfies
\mathcal{R}(A^{*})=\ell_{q}
. The latter identity follows easily from the decomposition

A=B\circ\mathcal{E}_{2}^{a}:\ell_{2}\to V
,
cf. Section
2.2
, in addition with the following two facts: the adjoint operator
B^{*}:V\to\ell_{q}
is onto since the continuation
B:\ell_{a}\to V
of the operator
A
has a bounded inverse on its range
\mathcal{R}(B)
, and moreover the adjoint

(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{a})^{*}:\ell_{q}\to\ell_{2}

of the embedding operator
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{a}:\ell_{2}\to\ell_{a}

is again an embedding operator. Note that our convergence result is an improvement over
[
13
, Proposition 11]

with respect to the smoothness assumption on the solution
u^{\dagger}
.
Finally, note that our convergence result holds for any penalty functional
\mathcal{R}_{p}
with
0\leq p\leq q
.

4.1
Hard thresholding

Below, we consider hard thresholding, which turns out to be an important tool for Tikhonov regularization in sequence spaces when oversmoothing or post processing is involved. Let

\beta>0
be a threshold level.
For any infinite sequence
u=(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, hard thresholding
H_{\beta}(u)
defines an infinite sequence as follows:

\displaystyle H_{\beta}(u)_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}u_{n}&\textup{if }|u_{n}|\geq\beta,\\
0&\textup{otherwise},\end{array}\right.\quad n=1,2,\ldots\ .

Hard thresholding satisfies an approximation property as well as an inverse property:

Proposition 11
.

For any
0\leq p\leq q\leq\tau
with
\tau\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|H_{\beta}(u)-u\|_{\tau}^{\tau}\leq\mathcal{R}_{q}(u)\beta^{\tau-q},\quad\mathcal{R}_{p}(H_{\beta}(u))\leq\mathcal{R}_{q}(u)\beta^{-(q-p)},\quad\beta>0,\ u\in\ell_{q}.

Proof.

Both estimates are easily obtained:

\displaystyle\|H_{\beta}(u)-u\|_{\tau}^{\tau}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|u_{n}|<\beta}|u_{n}|^{\tau}\leq\Big(\sum_{|u_{n}|<\beta}|u_{n}|^{q}\Big)\beta^{\tau-q}\leq\mathcal{R}_{q}(u)\beta^{\tau-q},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{p}(H_{\beta}(u))

\displaystyle=\sum_{|u_{n}|\geq\beta}|u_{n}|^{p}\leq\Big(\sum_{|u_{n}|\geq\beta}|u_{n}|^{q}\Big)\beta^{-(q-p)}\leq\mathcal{R}_{q}(u)\beta^{-(q-p)}.

Note that the proof applies also to the cases
p=0
and
q=0
.
∎

The two estimates of the proposition may be considered as Jackson- and Bernstein-type inequality, respectively. Similar estimates are considered in
[
20
, Lemmas 9 and 12]
,
[
4
, Section 5.1]
and
[
5
, Section 7.8]
.

4.2
The basic steps

In what follows, we shall make frequent use of auxiliary elements, i.e.

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{\alpha}=H_{\beta(\alpha)}(u^{\dagger}),\quad\textup{with }\beta=\beta(\alpha):=\alpha^{a/N},\quad N:=(a-q)\sigma+(q-p)a,

(17)

where the parameter
\alpha
corresponds to
variational regularization considered in (
8
).
As an immediate consequence of Proposition
11
,
we have the following approximation and stability property,
respectively:

\displaystyle\|\widehat{u}_{\alpha}-u^{\dagger}\|_{\tau}^{\tau}\leq\alpha^{(\tau-q)a/N}\mathcal{R}_{q}(u^{\dagger}),\quad\mathcal{R}_{p}(\widehat{u}_{\alpha})\leq\alpha^{\kappa-1}\mathcal{R}_{q}(u^{\dagger})\quad(\alpha>0),

(18)

where

0\leq p\leq q\leq\tau
with
\tau\geq 1
, and
the exponent
0<\kappa\leq 1
is given by

\displaystyle\kappa:=\frac{a-q}{N}\sigma,

(19)

with
N
taken from (
17
).
The first estimate in (
18
) is applied below both for
\tau=a
and

\tau=r
.

The following proposition provides an upper bound for the minimum of the Tikhonov functional and turns out as a basic ingredient in our analysis.

Proposition 12
.

We have

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}^{\delta}(u_{\alpha}^{\delta})\leq c(\alpha^{\kappa}+\delta^{\sigma}),\quad\alpha>0,

(20)

where
c>0
denotes a finite constant that
may depend on the desired solution
u^{\dagger}

but is independent both of
\alpha
and
\delta
.

Proof.

Utilizing auxiliaries from (
17
) with
\beta=\beta(\alpha)
, we have

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}^{\delta}(u_{\alpha}^{\delta})

\displaystyle\leq T_{\alpha}^{\delta}(\widehat{u}_{\alpha})=\|A\widehat{u}_{\alpha}-v^{\delta}\|_{V}^{\sigma}+\alpha\mathcal{R}_{p}(\widehat{u}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq e_{1}(\|A(\widehat{u}_{\alpha}-u^{\dagger})\|_{V}^{\sigma}+\delta^{\sigma})+\alpha\mathcal{R}_{p}(\widehat{u}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq e_{2}(\|\widehat{u}_{\alpha}-u^{\dagger}\|_{a}^{\sigma}+\delta^{\sigma})+\alpha\mathcal{R}_{p}(\widehat{u}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq e_{3}(\alpha^{\kappa}+\delta^{\sigma}+\alpha\alpha^{\kappa-1}),

where the minimization property of
u_{\alpha}^{\delta}
and the
second estimate in (
6
) have been applied.
In addition,
both estimates in (
18
)
have been utilized,
the former with
\tau=a
in fact.
Moreover,
e_{1},e_{2},
and
e_{3}
denote finite positive constants
which are independent of both
\alpha
and
\delta
, respectively, and

e_{3}
depends on the sought-after solution
u^{\dagger}
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

As an immediate consequence of the preceding proposition, we are able to provide estimates both for
the approximation error in the weaker norm
\|\cdot\|_{a}
and the penalty functional:

Corollary 13
.

We have

\displaystyle\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{a}\leq c_{1}(\alpha^{(a-q)/N}+\delta),\quad\mathcal{R}_{p}(u_{\alpha}^{\delta})\leq c_{2}\Big(\alpha^{\kappa-1}+\frac{\delta^{\sigma}}{\alpha}\Big),\qquad\alpha>0,

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
denote positive constants that are independent both of
\alpha
and
\delta
, respectively.

Proof.

The second estimate of the corollary is immediate from Proposition
12
,
and the first follows from the first estimate in (
6
)
and Proposition
12
:

\displaystyle d_{1}\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{a}\leq\|A(u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger})\|_{V}\leq\|Au_{\alpha}^{\delta}-v^{\delta}\|_{V}+\delta\leq e(\alpha^{\kappa/\sigma}+\delta),\quad\alpha>0,

where
e>0
denotes a finite constant that may depend on the sought-after solution
u^{\dagger}
but is independent of
\alpha
and
\delta
.

∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
8

We have already derived estimates of
\|v-u^{\dagger}\|_{a}
and

\mathcal{R}_{p}(v)
for both the Tikhonov minimizers
v=u_{\alpha}^{\delta}
and the auxiliaries

v=\widehat{u}_{\alpha}
. The proof can now be completed by simple synthesis and by utilizing the interpolation inequality for the scale of standard sequences spaces. We start by a natural decomposition of the approximation error using the auxiliaries:

\displaystyle\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{r}\leq\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha}\|_{r}+\|\widehat{u}_{\alpha}-u^{\dagger}\|_{r}.

(21)

The first term on the right-hand side in (
21
) can be estimated by

\displaystyle\|\widehat{u}_{\alpha}-u^{\dagger}\|_{r}^{r}\leq e_{1}\alpha^{(r-q)a/N},\quad\alpha>0,

(22)

which follows from the first estimate in (
18
) applied with
\tau=r
.
Here and in what follows,
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots
denote finite positive constants that may depend on the desired solution
u^{\dagger}
but are independent of
\alpha
and
\delta
, respectively.
Below, we estimate the first term on the right-hand side
of (
21
) by utilizing the interpolation inequality in sequence spaces, cf. (
3
):

\displaystyle\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha}\|_{r}^{r}\leq\mathcal{R}_{p}(u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha})^{\theta}\cdot\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha}\|_{a}^{a(1-\theta)},

(23)

with

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{a-r}{a-p},\quad 1-\theta=\frac{r-p}{a-p}.

The first term on the right-hand side of (
23
) can be estimated by means of the conditional stability estimate in (
18
) and Corollary

13
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{p}(u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha})\leq e_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{p}(u_{\alpha}^{\delta})+\mathcal{R}_{p}(\widehat{u}_{\alpha}))\leq e_{3}\Big(\alpha^{\kappa-1}+\frac{\delta^{\sigma}}{\alpha}\Big),\quad\alpha>0.

(24)

The second term on the right-hand side of (
23
) can also be treated by means of the estimates in (
18
) and Corollary

13
:

\displaystyle\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha}\|_{a}\leq\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{a}+\|\widehat{u}_{\alpha}-u^{\dagger}\|_{a}\leq e_{4}(\alpha^{(a-q)/N}+\delta),\quad\alpha>0.

(25)

We now consider the above estimates for the specific a priori parameter choice

\alpha=\alpha_{\delta}
introduced in (
13
).
In fact, this parameter choice
balances the two terms of the upper bounds in
(
24
) and
(
25
), respectively. This yields
the two estimates

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{p}(u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha_{\delta}})\leq e_{5}\delta^{-\gamma_{2}},\quad\|u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha_{\delta}}\|_{a}\leq e_{6}\delta,

(26)

and in addition we obtain the conditional stability estimate of the theorem, cf. the second estimate in (
24
). Utilizing in (
23
)
the two estimates from (
26
) finally gives

\displaystyle\|u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\alpha_{\delta}}\|_{r}\leq e_{7}\delta^{\gamma_{1}}.

The tedious but simple computations are left to the reader.
For the parameter choice (
13
), in addition we also have

\|\widehat{u}_{\alpha_{\delta}}-u^{\dagger}\|_{r}\leq e_{8}\delta^{\gamma_{1}}
, cf. (
22
).
This completes the proof of Theorem
8
.

Remark 14
.

In the case
q=p
, i.e.
\mathcal{R}_{p}
is the stabilizing functional and
u^{\dagger}\in\ell_{p}
, there is no oversmoothing. The proof technique can be simplified in that case, and auxiliaries are not needed then in fact.

4.4
Post processing

Under the conditions of Theorem
8
, we can guarantee sparsity only for

p=0
, i.e. the penalty functional is
\mathcal{R}_{0}
. In all other cases, sparsity can easily be obtained by some simple post processing based on hard thresholding.
For this purpose, consider

\displaystyle v_{\beta}^{\delta}=H_{\beta}(u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}),\quad\beta>0,

(27)

where
H_{\beta}
refers to hard thresholding given by (
4.1
).
For an appropriate choice of
\beta=\beta_{\delta}
, the element
v_{\beta_{\delta}}^{\delta}
allows the same error rates as the original regularizing element

u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}
, but this time with guaranteed sparsity for any
p
and with available conditional stability estimate, as the following corollary shows.

Corollary 15

(Post processing)
.

Let the conditions of Theorem
8
hold, and in addition let

v_{\beta}^{\delta}
be as in
(
27
) and

\beta=\beta_{\delta}=\delta^{\frac{a}{a-q}},\,\delta>0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|v_{\beta_{\delta}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\gamma_{1}}),\quad\mathcal{R}_{p}(v_{\beta_{\delta}}^{\delta})=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\gamma_{2}})\quad\textup{ as }\delta\to 0,

(28)

where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
are given by (
12
).

Proof.

We first consider the left estimate in (
28
). Due to the first estimate in Theorem
8
, it is sufficient to provide a suitable estimate for
\|v_{\beta_{\delta}}^{\delta}-u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}\|_{r}
. For this, we make use of the first estimate in Proposition
11
, applied with
u=u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta},\tau=r
, and
q=p
. In addition, we utilize the second estimate in Theorem
8
:

\displaystyle\|v_{\beta_{\delta}}^{\delta}-u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}\|_{r}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{\delta}^{r-p}\mathcal{R}_{p}(u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta})=\delta^{\frac{r-p}{a-q}a}\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\gamma_{2}})=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\gamma_{1}r}).

Next we consider the second estimate in (
28
). It immediately follows from
the second estimate in Theorem
8
and the second estimate in Proposition
11
, applied with

q=p
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{p}(v_{\beta_{\delta}}^{\delta})=\mathcal{R}_{p}(H_{\beta_{\delta}}(u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}))\leq\mathcal{R}_{p}(u_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta})=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\gamma_{2}}),\quad\delta>0.

This completes the proof.
∎

5
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we consider variational regularization of linear ill-posed problems in sequence spaces. Our focus lies both on well-posedness of the considered Tikhonov functional and convergence rates for suitable a priori parameter choices.
The considerations are restricted to operators that satisfy a stability estimate with respect to a weaker sequence space. On the other hand, we allow an oversmoothing of the penalty functional. Sparsity results are obtained either when the
\ell_{0}
-norm is utilized as penalty functional or by post-processing based on hard thresholding.

In what follows, we list some topics that are beyond the scope of this paper but could be the subject of further investigations.
First, the analysis of a posteriori parameter choice strategies
like the discrepancy principle will be of interest. Note that those strategies are superior to a priori parameter choice strategies, since they do not require any knowledge of the smoothness of the solution.
Another topic of strong interest will be the investigation of problems with solutions satisfying no additional smoothness conditions, i.e. they belong to the approximation error space
\ell_{r}
but not to any stronger sequence space
\ell_{q},\,q<r
.
A discussion of the optimality of the presented rates can certainly play some role.
Moreover, it seems that the mathematical approach presented in this paper can also be used for the treatment of nonlinear problems.
The analysis of sparsity-promoting features of
variational regularization with penalty functionals that are related
to an
\ell_{p}
-norm, with
0<p\leq 1
may also be exciting.

Last but not least: extensions of the presented result to weighted and weak
\ell_{p}
spaces seem to be possible. The former case may allow the coverage of other applications, like period integral operators in spaces of periodic Sobolev spaces.

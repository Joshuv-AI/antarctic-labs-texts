A structure theorem along fibers of extreme points of the momentum polytope
Peter Heinzner, Christian Zöller

Abstract
. Let G G be a complex reductive Lie group acting on a compact Kähler manifold X X and assume that the action of a maximal compact subgroup K K of G G is Hamiltonian. For each extreme point of the convex hull of the momentum map image, there is an associated open dense subset of X X , which is invariant under a parabolic subgroup Q Q of G G . We prove a Q Q -equivariant product decomposition for the Q Q -action on this subset and discuss some applications of the result. We show a similar statement for real reductive subgroups of G G for the restricted momentum map.

Introduction

For a finite dimensional
G
-representation
V
and
X=\mathbb{P}(V)
the following local structure theorem
holds for any point
x\in X
with compact
G
-orbit (see
[
4
]
for the precise statement or Section
8
). There is a Zariski open neighborhood
\Omega
of
x
, a parabolic subgroup
Q
of
G
with Levi decomposition
Q=R\rtimes L
, an
L
-representation
F
such that the
bundle
Q\times^{L}F
associated to the
L
-principal bundle
Q\to Q/L
is
Q
-equivariantly
and algebraically isomorphic to
\Omega
. The
Q
orbit through
x
is open in the compact orbit.
Note that the map
R\times F\to Q\times^{L}F
induced by
the inclusion
R\times F\subset Q\times F
is an isomorphism. In particular
\Omega
is an affine neighbourhood of
x
.

In this paper we prove a version of the local structure theorem for an invariant compact submanifold of a Kähler manifold.
More precisely, we consider a holomorphic action of a connected complex
reductive group
U^{\mathbb{C}}
with maximal compact subgroup
U
on a Kähler manifold
Z
such that the restriction
U

acts on
Z
in a Hamiltonian fashion. This means, that
the Kähler form
\omega
on
Z
is
U
-invariant and there is a
U
-equivariant momentum
map
\mu\colon Z\to\mathfrak{u}^{*}
. We will use the notations introduced in
[
9
]
,
i.e., we fix a
U
-invariant inner product
<\,,\,>
on
\mathfrak{u}
and use it
to identify
\mathfrak{u}
with its dual. We also identify
\mathfrak{u}
with
i\mathfrak{u}
. With
this identifications in mind a momentum map
\mu
on
Z

is a smooth
U
-equivariant map
\mu\colon Z\to\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{u}
such that for every
function
\mu^{\alpha}\colon Z\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mu^{\alpha}(x)=<\mu(x),\alpha>
where
\alpha\in\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{u}

the gradient condition

\operatorname{grad}\mu^{\alpha}=\alpha_{Z}

is satisfied. The gradient is computed with respect to the Riemannian structure given by the Kähler form
\omega
and
\alpha_{Z}
denotes the vector field on
Z
given by the one-parameter subgroup
t\mapsto\exp t\alpha
of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
and the
U^{\mathbb{C}}
-action on
Z
. Note that
for every
\xi\in\mathfrak{u}
the function
\mu^{i\xi}
is
a Hamiltonian function with Hamiltonian vector field
\xi_{Z}
with respect to the symplectic form
\omega
.

In the following we also fix a closed real subgroup
G
of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
which is compatible with the Cartan decomposition

U^{\mathbb{C}}=U\exp(\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{u})
in the sense that for
K:=U\cap G
and
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{g}\cap\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{u}
the mapping

K\times\mathfrak{p}\to G,(k,\alpha)\mapsto k\exp\alpha

is an isomorphism.

The
gradient condition does not change, if
Z
is replaced by a
G
-invariant Riemannian submanifold of
Z
. It only
depends on the
G
-orbit.
We fix a
G
-invariant compact submanifold
X
of
Z
. On
X
we have the restricted momentum map or
gradient map

\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon X\to\mathfrak{p}

which is defined by
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha}=\mu^{\alpha}|X
for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{p}
. Note that for
X=Z
the restricted
momentum map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
is just the map
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ\mu
where
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{u}\to\mathfrak{p}
denotes the orthogonal projection.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a maximal Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
which lies in
\mathfrak{p}
. The map
\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}\circ\mu
where
\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}\colon\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{a}

denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathfrak{p}
onto
\mathfrak{a}
is a restricted momentum map for the
A=\exp\mathfrak{a}
-action
on
X
. The convex envelope
P
of
\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)
is a convex polytope.

For simplicity we refer to the case where
G
is complex reductive and
X
is a compact complex submanifold of
Z

as the complex case. In the complex case we have
\mathfrak{p}=\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{k}
and in this case we also have
P=\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)

for a connected manifold

(
[
1
,
5
]
).
In fact

is the convex envelope of the finite subset
\mu_{a}(X^{A})
of
\mathfrak{a}
where
X^{A}
denotes the set of
A
-fixed points in
X
. In general we do not know any connected smooth compact example where
\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)
is not convex.

We show in section
5
a version of the local structure theorem at any

x\in\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}^{-1}(\sigma)
where
\sigma
is an extreme point of
the momentum polytope
P
or equivalently for any extreme point of the convex envelope

of the image
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)
.

For a precise formulation of the main result we have to introduce more notation. Let
E
be a
convex body in a finite dimensional Euclidian vector space

with inner product
<\,,\,>
.
Any

of

.
In this case we also say that

exposes

of

.

Let

be the convex envelope of
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)
. It is known that every

).
Now let

. The cone

is invariant under the

. Since

Our main result is the following

Theorem
.

Let
X
be a

-invariant compact submanifold of
Z
and let

be an extreme point of the convex envelope

of

of

is compact and for every

There is an open neighbourhood

of

, an
I^{\beta}
-representation

, with all weights of
\beta
being strictly negative and a

-equivariant smooth isomorphism

, where

is the projection onto

For

the orbits

and

are compact. It should also
be underlined that

for every

. Moreover for

the open subset

]

Let

1.
Basic properties of the momentum map

Let
Z
be a complex manifold with a holomorphic action of the complex
reductive group
U^{\mathbb{C}}
, where
U^{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of the
compact Lie group
U
. The corresponding Cartan involution is denoted by
\Theta
.
We assume that
Z
admits a smooth

U
-invariant Kähler structure and a
U
-equivariant moment mapping

\mu\colon Z\to\mathfrak{u}^{*}
, where
\mathfrak{u}
is the Lie algebra of
U
and

\mathfrak{u}^{*}
its dual. As in the introduction we choose an invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{u}
and use it
to identify
\mathfrak{u}^{*}
with
\mathfrak{u}
. In order to simplify the notations we will identify
\mathfrak{u}
as a
U
-representation isometrically with
\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{u}
by
multiplying with
\mathrm{i}
.

We also assume that
G\subset U^{\mathbb{C}}
is a closed
subgroup such that the Cartan decomposition
U^{\mathbb{C}}=U\exp(i\mathfrak{u})\simeq U\times i\mathfrak{u}
induces a Cartan decomposition
G=K\exp\mathfrak{p}\simeq K\times\mathfrak{p}
where
K=U\cap G
and
\mathfrak{p}\subset i\mathfrak{u}
is an
(\operatorname{Ad}K)
-stable linear subspace. In this case we also say that
G
is compatible with
\Theta
or
compatible with the Cartan decomposition of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
. We have the subspace
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{u}

and by restriction an induced restricted momentum mapping
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon Z\to\mathfrak{p}
which is the composition of the orthogonal projection of
\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{u}
onto
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mu
.

For
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
and
x\in Z
let

\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}(x):=<\mu(x),\beta>
. With our convention the momentum map condition means that

\operatorname{grad}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}=\beta_{Z}
where
\beta_{Z}
is the vector field on

Z
corresponding to
\beta
and
\operatorname{grad}
is computed with respect
to the Riemannian metric induced by the Kähler structure. We
call
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
the
G
-gradient map associated with
\mu
or the restricted momentum map. Note that the
condition on
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
just means that for every
\xi\in\mathfrak{p}
the vector field
(\mathrm{i}\xi)_{Z}
is a Hamiltonian
vector field on
Z
with Hamiltonian function
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\xi}
.

For a
G
-stable subset
X
of

Z
we may consider
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
as a
K
-equivariant mapping
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon X\to\mathfrak{p}

such that

\operatorname{grad}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\beta}=\beta_{X}

holds orbitwise. The gradient can be computed on each
G
-orbit with respect to the induced
Riemannian metric. The vector field
\beta_{X}
is given by the flow
(t,x)\mapsto\exp t\beta\cdot x
.
Since
X
is
G
-stable we have
\beta_{Z}(z)=\beta_{X}(z)
for
z\in X
and the map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
X
is the restriction of
the map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon Z\to\mathfrak{p}
. We are interested mostly in the case where
X
is a compact
G
-invariant subset
of
Z
.

Remark 1.1
.

If
X
in the above setup is smooth, then we refer to this as the smooth case.
If
X
is a smooth complex submanifold of
Z
and
G
a complex reductive subgroup
of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
which is compatible with the Cartan decomposition of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
, the Kähler form
\omega
on
Z
as well as the momentum map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
restricts to
X
and we have
\mathfrak{p}=\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{k}
. We refer to this setup as the smooth complex case. This case has to be distinguished from the case where
X
is
smooth and complex and
G
is a non-complex
\Theta
-compatible Lie subgroup of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
.

We collect a few elementary properties of the restricted momentum
map
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon X\to\mathfrak{p}
. Details can be found in
[
8
]
.
For a subspace
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and
x\in X
we set
\mathfrak{m}\cdot x=\{\xi_{X}(x):\xi\in\mathfrak{m}\}
,
\mathfrak{m}_{x}:=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{m}:\xi_{X}(x)=0\}
. If
x
is a smooth point of
X
and
V
is a subspace
of
T_{x}X
let
V^{\perp}
denote the perpendicular subset with respect to
the Riemannian metric
x\mapsto(\ ,\ )_{x}
on the smooth part of

. Note that any point

is a smooth
point in its

-orbit.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{p}
be a subspace. Then

\ker d\mu_{\mathfrak{m}}(x)=(\mathfrak{m}\cdot x)^{\perp}
on any smooth
G
-invariant submanifold
M
of
Z
.

Proof.

This follows from
\operatorname{grad}\mu^{\beta}=\beta_{X}
for
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Lemma 1.3
.

For all
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
and
x\in X
and the curve
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to X
,
\gamma(t)=\exp t\beta\cdot x
we either have

i)

x\in X^{\beta}
or

ii)

the function

is strictly increasing.

Proof.

This follows from

\frac{d}{dt}(\mu^{\beta}\circ\gamma)(t)=\left(\beta_{X}(\gamma(t)),\beta_{X}(\gamma(t))\right)_{\gamma(t)}.

∎

For
\beta\in\mathfrak{p}
let
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\beta):=\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(\beta)
and

plays a prominent role we set
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)
. Using

and the

-equivariance of

, one can
prove the following

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\beta
be a

-fixed point in
\mathfrak{p}
and

. Then

(1)

G\cdot x\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\beta)=K\cdot x
.

(2)

The

-isotropy group

of

is compatible with the Cartan decomposition of

. The decomposition is given by

.

∎

Remark 1.5
.

If
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a restricted momentum map and
\alpha_{0}\in\mathfrak{p}
is a

-fixed point,
then
\tilde{\mu}_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon Z\to\mathfrak{p}
,
\tilde{\mu}(x)=\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)+\alpha_{0}
is also a restricted momentum map for the
G
-action on

(see Remarks

(

to be connected then up to a additive

.

In section
5
we need the following.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a
G
-invariant subset of
Z
and assume that
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
constant on
X
. Then for every
x\in X
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
of the isotropy group
G_{x}

contains the ideal
\mathfrak{p}+[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show the statement orbitwise. Hence we may assume that
X
is smooth.
For all
x\in X
we have
\ker d\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=(\mathfrak{p}\cdot x)^{\perp}
. Since
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
is constant this shows that
\mathfrak{p}\cdot x=\{0\}
holds. The isotropy Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
then contains the ideal
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]+\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of a
\Theta
-compatible subgroup
G
is real reductive. We have a direct sum decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\ldots\mathfrak{g}_{m}
where
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the center of
\mathfrak{g}

and the
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
are the simple ideals of the semisimple part of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since Cartan involutions of semisimple
Lie algebras are unique up to conjugation, we have Cartan decompositions of the Lie algebras

\mathfrak{g}_{j}=\mathfrak{k}_{j}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{j}
and
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{k}}\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{k}_{m}
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{m}
holds where
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{k}}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{p}
. The ideal
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
is said to be compact if
\mathfrak{g}_{j}=\mathfrak{k}_{j}
and we may assume that

\mathfrak{g}_{j}=\mathfrak{k}_{j}
for
j=1,\cdots,l
. The connected Lie subgroup
G_{nc}
of
G
with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{nc}=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{l+1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{m}
is called the non compact factor of
G
.
The subgroup
G_{nc}
of
G
is a normal
\Theta
-compatible subgroup of
G
and
G/G_{nc}
is a compact Lie group.

Corollary 1.7
.

Assume that
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon X\to\mathfrak{p}
is constant and let
I:=\bigcap_{x\in X}G_{x}
denote the ineffectivity of the
G
-action on
X
. Then

(1)

I
is an normal subgroup of
G
which is compatible with the Cartan decomposition,

(2)

I
contains the non-compact factor of
G
.

(3)

G/I
is a a compact Lie group. In particular every
G
-orbit is compact.

Proof.

Intersections of
\Theta
-compatible subgroups of
G
are
\Theta
-compatible. This shows (
1
).
For (
2
) we have to show that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{i}
of
I
contains

\mathfrak{p}
. Since
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}
is constant, we have
T_{x}Y=\ker d\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=\mathfrak{p}\cdot x^{\perp}
on

. This shows
\mathfrak{p}\cdot x=0
for
all
x\in X
and implies
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{i}
.
∎

2.
The Slice Theorem

We will use the Slice Theorem of Luna type for Hamiltonian actions several times. It is valid for the action of a
\Theta
-compatible subgroup
G
of
U^{\mathbb{C}}
on
Z
or slightly more generally for the
G
-action on a
G
-invariant submanifold
X
of
Z
. The precise statement is as follows. Recall that the points in a twisted product

G\times^{H}Y
are the
H
-orbits in
G\times Y
where
H
-acts by
(h,g,y)\mapsto(gh^{-1},hy)
. In the following
we set
[g,y]:=H\cdot(g,y)\in G\times^{H}Y
and identify
Y
with the subset
\{[e,y]:y\in Y\}

of
G\times^{H}Y
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Slice Theorem) Let
X
be a
G
-invariant submanifold of
Z
and
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\beta_{0})
. Then

(1)

G_{x}=K_{x}\exp\mathfrak{p}_{x}
is a
\Theta

In this section we give a proof of the result of Brion-Luna-Vust (

]

Assume that

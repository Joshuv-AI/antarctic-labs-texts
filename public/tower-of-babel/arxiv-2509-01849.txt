An elementary classification of the quaternionic reflection groups of rank two
Shayne Waldron

Abstract
We give an elementary classification and presentation of the finite quaternionic reflection groups of rank two, based on the notion of a “reflection system”. This simplifies the existing classification, which is shown to be incomplete, e.g., there exist four imprimitive quaternionic reflection groups of order 192 192 with 22 22 reflections which are not isomorphic (one of which was previously unknown).

Introduction

The finite (unitary) quaternionic reflection groups were formally introduced and then
classified by Cohen
[
C80
]
, with various results then built upon it, e.g.,
the classification of parabolic subgroups
[
BST23
]
,
[
S23
]
, and certain conformal field theories

[
DZ24
]
.

This followed in the spirit of the real reflection groups
which were classified by Coxeter
[
C34
]
, and the complex reflection groups
which were classified by Shephard and Todd
[
ST54
]
.
There are various relationships between these groups,
e.g., Cohen understood that the quaternionic reflection groups in one dimension were
classified by Stringham
[
St1881
]
, and recognised that some imprimitive
collineation groups for
\mathbb{C}^{4}
of Blichfeldt
[
B17
]
give rise to primitive quaternionic reflection groups of rank
2

(see
[
K81
]
for an interesting overview, and
[
Z97
]
,
[
CS03
]
,
[
V21
]

Here we consider the classification of the imprimitive quaternionic reflection groups of
rank two.
These are finite groups of unitary
2\times 2
monomial matrices over the quaternions
\mathbb{H}
,
which are generated by reflections, i.e., nonidentity matrices
g

which fix a
1
-dimensional subspace (this is equivalent to
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(g-I)=1
).
Elementary calculations show that there are two types of reflections

\begin{pmatrix}h&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix},\ \begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&h\end{pmatrix},\quad h\neq 1,\qquad\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},

which have orders the order of
h
and
2
, respectively. The roots for these
reflections, i.e., a vector in the orthogonal complement of the fixed subspace
are
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
,
(1,-\overline{b})
, respectively.
The six primitive quaternionic reflection groups of rank two can be obtained
by adding single non-monomial reflection to an imprimitive reflection group
[
W24
]
,
[
BW25
]
.

Let
G
be an irreducible imprimitive quaternionic reflection group
of rank two.
Then
G
is conjugate in
U(\mathbb{H}^{2})
to a unitary group where the reflections are

\begin{pmatrix}h&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix},\ \begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&h\end{pmatrix},\quad h\in H,\ h\neq 1,\qquad\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad b\in L,

(
1.1
)

where
1\in L
(a standardisation condition),
1\in H
, and

H
and
L
are subsets of
U(\mathbb{H})
.

•

Since the reflections for a given root (e.g.,
e_{1}
)
are a subgroup of
G
, we conclude that
H
is a group.

•

Since the set of reflections in
G
is closed under conjugation, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}h&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&h\cr h^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\in G,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&b^{-1}hb\end{pmatrix}\in G,\qquad h\in H,\ b\in L.

Hence
H\subset L
, and
H
is a normal subgroup
of the group
K=\langle L\rangle
generated by
L
.

Therefore, each group
G
of the above form is determined by
H\lhd K
, which are
uniquely defined up to conjugation in
U(\mathbb{H})
, the reflections in
G
,
and a suitable
L
.

Clearly, not every
H\lhd K
and
L\subset K
gives a reflection group
as above, and so the classification of the imprimitive reflection groups of rank two
can be reduced to finding the suitable subsets
L
(and
H
),
and then any isomorphisms between the reflection groups that they give
– just as was done by Shephard and Todd, and in turn Cohen.
Our contribution to this approach is the
observation that the
L
further satisfies certain algebraic properties which
we describe as being a “reflection system”.
The remainder of the paper is devoted to determining all the reflection systems,
and consequently classifying the imprimitive quaternionic reflection groups
of rank two (Theorem
).
The existing classification is shown to be incomplete, and with some double counting.

2
Reflection systems and the canonical form

Let
G
be the imprimitive reflection group
generated by the reflections of (
1.1
). Then

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&h\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&bh\cr(bh)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\in G,\qquad h\in H,\ b\in L,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&a\cr a^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&a\cr a^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&ab^{-1}a\cr(ab^{-1}a)^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\in G,\qquad a,b\in L,

so that

1.

LH=L
, i.e.,
L
is a union of cosets in the group
K/H
(and so
|H|
divides
|L|
).

2.

ab^{-1}a\in L
,
\forall a,b\in L
, i.e.,

L
is closed under the binary operation
(a,b)\mapsto ab^{-1}a
.

Hence, there is an irreducible imprimitive quaternionic reflection group
generated by reflections of the form (
1.1
) if and only if
H\lhd K
and

H\subset L\subset K
satisfies
K=\langle L\rangle
and the two conditions
above hold, and we denote this group by

.

Let

give the set of nondiagonal reflections in

, i.e.,

L_{G}:=\{b\in K:\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\in G\}\supset L.

(
2.2
)

If all the nondiagonal reflections in
G=G(K,L,H)
are given by

,
i.e.,
L=L_{G}
, then we say that

is in
canonical form
,
as a subgroup of
G(K,K,K)
, and we denote it by

G_{K}(L,H)=G(L,H)=G(K,L,H),

(
2.3
)

and call this unique
(K,L,H)
, or
(L,H)
,

canonical label
for the group

,
as a subgroup of
G_{K}(K,K)

We observe that for the canonical label

H_{L}:=\bigl\{h:\begin{pmatrix}h&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\langle\bigl\{\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\bigr\}_{b\in L}\rangle\bigr\}\subset H.

(
2.4
)

Clearly,

G(K,L_{1},H_{1})\subset G(K,L_{2},H_{2}),\qquad L_{1}\subset L_{2},\ H_{1}\subset H_{2},

(
2.5
)

for suitable
(L_{j},H_{j})
, including the canonical labels. Indeed,

G(K_{1},L_{1},H_{1})\subset G(K_{2},L_{2},H_{2}),\qquad K_{1}\subset K_{2},\ L_{1}\subset L_{2},\ H_{1}\subset H_{2},

(
2.6

and so it may be possible to obtain the same reflection group for
K_{1}\neq K_{2}
.
This happens in two instances (see Example

and Theorem

We now define the fundamental algebraic object of our investigation.

Definition 2.1

We call a subset
L
of
K
(a finite group) a
reflection system

for
K
if

1.

K=\langle L\rangle
.

2.

L
is closed under the binary operation
(a,b)\mapsto a\circ b:=ab^{-1}a
.

3.

1\in L
.

We say that reflection systems
L
and
L^{\prime}
for
K
are
isomorphic
if there
is a bijection
\psi:L\to L^{\prime}
which preserves
\circ
, i.e.,

\psi(a\circ b)=\psi(a)\circ\psi(b)
.

Example 2.1

The group
K=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
has two reflection systems

L_{3}=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad L_{4}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}.

We will often define a reflection system
L
via a
generating
set, i.e.,
a subset
X
of
L
whose closure under the binary operation
\circ

is
L
, and we write
L=L(X)
. We note that

•

For any reflection group
G=G(K,L,H)
,
L
is a reflection system for
K
.

•

L=K
is a reflection system for
K
, and
this is the only case where
L
is a group.

•

Since
1\circ x=x^{-1}
, reflection systems are closed under taking inverses.

•

Since
x^{-1}\circ 1=x^{2}
, reflection systems are closed under taking squares.

Moreover, if
L
is a reflection system and
x\in L
(so that
x^{-1}\in L
), then
it follows that

xL
and
Lx
are reflection systems, via the calculations

\displaystyle\hbox{$xa\circ xb=xa(xb)^{-1}xa=xab^{-1}x^{-1}xa=xab^{-1}a=x(a\circ b)$},

\displaystyle\hbox{$ax\circ bx=ax(bx)^{-1}ax=axx^{-1}b^{-1}ax=ab^{-1}ax=(a\circ b)x$},\qquad a,b\in L.

The corresponding reflections for these systems are conjugate in
U(\mathbb{H}^{2})
to those for
L
,
via the calculations

\begin{pmatrix}x&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}M_{xb}\begin{pmatrix}x&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix}=M_{b},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&x\end{pmatrix}M_{bx}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&x\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=M_{b},\qquad M_{b}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, we consider reflection systems
L
and
L^{\prime}
for
K
to be
equivalent
if

L^{\prime}
equals
xL
or
Lx
, for some

, up to an automorphism of
K
.
This is an equivalence relation, and equivalent reflection systems lead to the same
reflection groups

.
Clearly, equivalent reflection systems are isomorphic.

Lemma 2.1

Suppose that
\{1\}\cup A
generates a reflection system

for
K
.
If

, then
\{1,x\}\cup xA
and

generate the reflection

and
Lx
for

, which are equivalent to (but possibly not equal to)

.

Proof:
We have observed that

and
Lx
are closed under

,

and
(\{1\}\cup A)x
.
If
x\in L
, then
x^{-1}=x\circ 1\in L
,
so that
1=x^{-1}\in xL
and

, and hence
\{1,x\}\cup xA
and
\{1,x\}\cup Ax

xL
and

.
The groups generated by the above two sets contains

A=x^{-1}(xA)=(Ax)x^{-1}

We can now give the structural form of a reflection group in the canonical form.

Lemma 2.2

A reflection group in the canonical form has the following elements

G=G_{K}(L,H)=\bigl\{\begin{pmatrix}b&0\cr 0&b_{\alpha}\,h\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\end{pmatrix}^{m}:b\in K,\ h\in H,\ m=0,1\Bigr\},

(
2.11
)

where

is any element of the coset
\alpha(bH)
of (
2.10
),

,

.
Therefore

(i)

has order
2|H||K|
.

(ii)

has

Proof:
Since

\begin{pmatrix}a&0\cr 0&b\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\cr b&0\end{pmatrix},

there is a
1
-
1
correspondence between the diagonal and nondiagonal elements
of
G
.
Hence, it suffices to determine the subgroup of diagonal matrices in
G
.
A product of two reflections given by elements of
L
is diagonal, i.e.,

\begin{pmatrix}0&a\cr a^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&b^{-1}\cr b&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ab&0\cr 0&a^{-1}b^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad a,b\in L,

(
2.12
)

so that

contains the diagonal matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{r}&0\cr 0&b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{r}^{-1}h\end{pmatrix},\qquad b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in L,\quad h\in H.

(
2.13
)

To see this, take products of the diagonal matrices in (

Since

,
there is at least one choice in (

We claim that
b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{r}^{-1}h\in\alpha(bH)
,
so that
b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{r}^{-1}h=b_{\alpha}\,h^{\prime}
, with

.
This follows from the calculation

\displaystyle\alpha(b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{r}^{-1}H)

\displaystyle=\alpha(b_{1}^{-1}H)\alpha(b_{2}^{-1}H)\cdots\alpha(b_{r}^{-1}H)

\displaystyle=(b_{1}H)(b_{2}H)\cdots(b_{r}H)=b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{r}H=bH,

\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{r}^{-1}H=\alpha^{2}(b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{r}^{-1}H)=\alpha(bH).

Since

.

Finally, from (

),

2(|H|-1)+|L|=2|H|+|L|-2

to address the question of when two

(

There are three cases:

(i)

(ii)

If

.

(iii)

If

General method
(Classification of imprimitive reflection

For each finite group

, as above, determine the

(there is always

). Then
for a given reflection system

of

, construct the corresponding

(given by

), as follows:

Determine the normal subgroup

), to obtain

(

)

which we call the

for

.

2.

Determine the other reflection groups with nondiagonal reflections given by

,
which we call the

for

These contain

(

)

.

In summary:

•

For any reflection group

\begin{pmatrix}h&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad h\in{\cal H},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad b\in{\cal L},

(

If

\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&b^{-1}hb\end{pmatrix}.

to be

As larger

p\neq n
),
and we obtain the following imprimitive reflection groups for the reflection system
L_{n}=C_{n}

G_{C_{n}}(C_{n},C_{n/p})={\cal G}(\{1,\omega\},\{\omega^{p}\})=G(n,p,2),\qquad|G|=2n{n\over p},

where
G(n,p,2)
is the notation used by Shephard and Todd.
The inclusions between them follow immediately from (
2.5
).

For
n=2
, we have the only real imprimitive reflection group
G(2,1,2)

(Heisenberg group), with the base group
G(2,2,2)
being reducible.

Lemma 3.1

Let
|K|<|K^{\prime}|
. If the reflection groups
G_{K}(L,H)
and
G_{K^{\prime}}(L^{\prime},H^{\prime})

are isomorphic,
then they can only have reflections of order
2
, so that
H=C_{2}
,
H^{\prime}=1
, and

|K^{\prime}|=2|K|,\qquad|L^{\prime}|=|L|+2.

Moreover,
L
is a subreflection system of
L^{\prime}
, and
K
is a subgroup of
K^{\prime}
.

Proof:

For any reflection group
G_{K}(L,H)
the nondiagonal reflections (given by
L
) have
order
2
, and the diagonal reflections given by
h\in H
have order the order of
h
,
and so in our case, we must have
|H|,|H^{\prime}|\leq 2
. From the orders of the reflection
groups being equal, we then have

2|H||K|=2|H^{\prime}||K^{\prime}|\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{|K^{\prime}|\over|K|}={|H|\over|H^{\prime}|}=2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=C_{2},\ H^{\prime}=1,\quad|K^{\prime}|=2|K|.

From the number of reflections in the groups being equal, we then have

2|H|+|L|-2=2|H^{\prime}|+|L^{\prime}|-2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|L^{\prime}|=|L|+2.

An isomorphism
G_{K}(L,H)\to G_{K^{\prime}}(L^{\prime},H^{\prime})
must map the reflections given by
L
to
the reflections given by
L^{\prime}
, say

\begin{pmatrix}0&b\cr b^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&b^{\prime}\cr(b^{\prime})^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad b\in L.

Since the injective map
L\to L^{\prime}:b\mapsto b^{\prime}
preserves
\circ
,
L

is a reflection subsystem of
L^{\prime}
, and
K=\langle L\rangle
is a subgroup of
K^{\prime}=\langle L^{\prime}\rangle
.

4
The binary polyhedral groups

We now consider the binary polyhedral groups

\displaystyle{\cal T}

\displaystyle:=\langle i,{1+i+j+k\over 2}\rangle\quad\hbox{(binary tetrahedral group of order $24$)},

\displaystyle{\cal O}

\displaystyle:=\langle{1+i\over\sqrt{2}},{1+i+j+k\over 2}\rangle\quad\hbox{(binary octahedral group of order $48$)},

\displaystyle{\cal I}

\displaystyle:=\langle i,{1+i+j+k\over 2},{1+\tau i+\sigma j\over 2}\rangle,\quad\hbox{(binary icosahedral group of order $120$)},

where

\tau:={1+\sqrt{5}\over 2},\qquad\displaystyle\sigma:={1-\sqrt{5}\over 2}.

Some insight into these groups is obtained by listing their elements and orders (see the appendix).
Each of these contains the quaternion group

Q_{8}=\langle i,j\rangle=\{1,-1,i,-i,j,-j,k,-k\}.

Since
({1+i\over\sqrt{2}})^{2}=i
, we have the inclusions

{\cal T}\subset{\cal O},\qquad{\cal T}\subset{\cal I}.

We consider the first example in some detail for the purpose of
illustration.

(Binary tetrahedral group)

\displaystyle G_{\cal T}({\cal T},Q_{8}),

For the reflection system

divides

We now make some observations, which apply to our calculations generally.

•

The orders and numbers of reflections are calculated using Lemma
2.2
,
e.g., the reflection group
G=G_{\cal T}({\cal T},Q_{8})
has

|G|=2|Q_{8}||{\cal T}|=384,\qquad\hbox{and \quad$2|Q_{8}|+|{\cal T}|-2=38$ reflections}.

•

Our method finds all the reflection subgroups of
G_{\cal T}({\cal T},{\cal T})

\hbox{{which have the canonical form}}.

There are also many other reflection subgroups
(not in the canonical form). These include imprimitive reflection groups for
other choices of
K
, and complex reflection groups
(see Example
4.2
).

•

A reflection group can have noncanonical labels, e.g., the base group
for a reflection system
L
, can be indexed by any
H\lhd K
with
H\subset H_{L}
.
As an example,
G_{\cal T}({\cal T},Q_{8})
has noncanonical labels

G({\cal T},{\cal T},1),\qquad G({\cal T},{\cal T},C_{2}).

•

Whether a reflection subgroup is normal is determined by the
reflection orbits
[
W25
]
. These are the orbits, under conjugation,
of the subgroups of the reflections for a given root.
From this theory,
it immediately follows that

G_{\cal T}(L_{12}^{\cal T},1)\lhd G_{\cal T}(L_{12}^{\cal T},C_{2}),\qquad G_{\cal T}({\cal T},Q_{8})\lhd G_{\cal T}({\cal T},{\cal T}),

and
G_{\cal T}(L_{12}^{\cal T},C_{2})
is not normal in
G_{\cal T}({\cal T},Q_{8})
.

•

The number of occurences of a reflection group in canonical form as
a subgroup of
G_{K}(K,K)
is given by the size of the equivalence class of
the reflection system, e.g., the reflection groups

G_{\cal T}(L_{12}^{\cal T},1)
,
G_{\cal T}(L_{12}^{\cal T},C_{2})
each appear twelve times
as subgroups of
G_{\cal T}({\cal T},{\cal T})
, as the equivalence class of
L_{12}^{\cal T}

has size
12
(Example
2.2
).
In particular,
G_{K}(K,H)\lhd G_{K}(K,K)
.

The base group for the reflection system
L=K
has the following
general form.

Lemma 4.1

For the reflection system
L=K
, the base group is given by

H_{L}=H_{K}=[K,K]=K^{(1)}=\langle\{aba^{-1}b^{-1}:a,b\in K\}\rangle\quad\hbox{(the commutator subgroup)}.

Therefore, the reflection groups in the canonical form for
L=K
are

G_{K}(K,H),\qquad[K,K]\subset H\subset K,

where
H
is a subgroup of
K
(
[K,K]\subset H
implies
H
is normal).

Proof:
The group generated by the reflections given by
L=K
contains

\begin{pmatrix}0&ab\cr(ab)^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&a\cr a^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&b^{-1}\cr b&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}aba^{-1}b^{-1}&0\cr 0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad a,b\in K,

so that

aba^{-1}b^{-1}\in H_{K}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad[K,K]\subset H_{K}.

Any subgroup

H
that contains
[K,K]

is normal in
K
, and hence gives a reflection group for
L=K
in the canonical form. In particular, by taking
H=[K,K]
, we
conclude that
H_{K}=[K,K]
.

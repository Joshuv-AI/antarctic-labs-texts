Flat-band projected versus fully atomistic twisted bilayer graphene Preprint: APS/123-QED
Miguel Sánchez Sánchez, Tobias Stauber

Abstract
We benchmark the recently proposed projection method Sánchez Sánchez et al. 2025 for magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG) across various symmetry-breaking phases at charge neutrality. The flat-band projected solutions agree well with the full tight-binding, with band structures and total energies differing by only a few meV. The projection to the flat bands is justified, owing to the increased gap to the remote bands in the normal state. Moreover, we employ a novel set of order parameters that allow us to visualize the wave functions locally in real space and quantify the breaking of various symmetries in the correlated phases. These order parameters are suitable to characterize MATBG and generic honeycomb systems.

IV
DISCUSSION

With the aim of benchmarking our many-body projection algorithm
Sánchez Sánchez et al. 2025
, we have performed self-consistent Hartree-Fock calculations on an interacting tight-binding model of magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene. The results of the flat-band projected and fully atomistic models show good agreement, validating our methods and pointing towards the soundness of the flat-band projection
Ledwith et al. 2025
. In fact, the splitting of the flat bands due to interactions, at
\pm
15–20 meV, is less than half the energy of the lowest states of the remote bands of the normal state, located around
\pm
50–60 meV, indicating that the remote bands are effectively frozen. In the non-interacting state, however, the remote bands appear at about
\pm 25
meV, which is comparable to the band splitting
Wang et al. 2025
;
Song and Bernevig 2022
;
Shi et al. 2025
.

Several remarks are in order regarding the modeling of the electronic interactions. The strength of the interactions is controlled by the effective dielectric constant
\epsilon_{r}
and the on-site Hubbard
U
, which are in free parameters in our setup; proper values for
\epsilon_{r}
and
U
should include the effects of the screening from the substrate and the internal screening
Pizarro et al. 2019
;
Vanhala and Pollet 2020
. In this work we fixed
\epsilon_{r}=10
and on-site Hubbard
U=4
eV, giving gaps of the correlated states of
30-40
meV, matching with the observed gaps in STM
Wong et al. 2020
;
Xie et al. 2019
;
Jiang et al. 2019
and quantum twisting microscopy
Xiao et al. 2025
. Similar values of
\epsilon_{r}=12-16
have been used in order to match to experimental
Wang and Vafek 2024
and numerical
Calderón et al. 2025
;
Calderón and Bascones 2020
results. An accurate treatment of the dielectric function, like in GW theory
Zhu et al. 2024
;
Peng et al. 2025
;
Romanova and Vlček 2022
, would represent critical improvements of our results, particularly in the construction of the normal state and projected models.

The gate distance and number of gates of the experimental device also modify the electronic interactions
Lau and Coleman 2025
;
Calderón and Bascones 2020
, and it has been shown in experiments that changing the device geometry can tune the superconducting and insulating regimes
Stepanov et al. 2020
;
Saito et al. 2020
. In this work we chose a double-gated Coulomb potential with a gate-to-gate distance of
\xi=10
nm.

On a different note, we have employed novel algorithms for computing general wave function overlaps in valley-sublattice space, first described in Ref.
Sánchez Sánchez et al. 2024
. We can then probe the symmetry-breaking of MATBG locally in real space, complementing the information of the one-particle density matrix in momentum space
Bultinck et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Kwan et al. 2021
;
Kwan et al. 2024
;
Hofmann et al. 2022
;
Soejima et al. 2020
;
Wagner et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2023
. One can further define order parameters by integrating the local quantities over the unit cell, and we find that on the correlated states at charge neutrality, their values are comparable to the maximal (minimal) allowed values of
1
(
-1
). In fact, at the angle of
1.05^{\circ}
, due to the nonflat bands of the symmetric state the correlated states do not break the symmetries around the
\Gamma
point in momentum space, as noted in
Sánchez Sánchez et al. 2025
. We expect then that the order parameters will attain values close to saturation at a lower angle where the flat bands reappear
2
2

2

M. Sánchez Sánchez, T. Stauber and J. González, in preparation.
. Furthermore, when we include the remote bands their numerical values are not significantly modified (see Appendix
C
), revealing that the symmetry breaking is strong only on the flat bands.

The local order parameters can be computed directly in atomistic models, without the need of constructing the Chern basis of momentum space descriptions
Bultinck et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Kwan et al. 2021
;
Kwan et al. 2024
;
Hofmann et al. 2022
;
Soejima et al. 2020
;
Wagner et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2023
; the remote bands can also be naturally included in this setup. Overall, our algorithms for obtaining wave function overlaps extend the analytical toolkit for the study of MATBG and other honeycomb systems. A natural extension of our work would be to derive expressions for the interlayer overlaps.

Appendix A
Lattice geometry and tight-binding Hamiltonian

A layer of graphene has the two basis vectors
{\bm{a}}_{1}=a_{0}(\sqrt{3}/2,3/2)
and
{\bm{a}}_{2}=a_{0}(-\sqrt{3}/2,3/2)
with
a_{0}=1.42
Å. We label the set of atoms displaced by
({\bm{a}}_{1}+{\bm{a}}_{2})/3
from the lattice positions as the
A
sublattice, and those displaced by
-({\bm{a}}_{1}+{\bm{a}}_{2})/3

B
sublattice, see Fig.

.

In twisted bilayer graphene, the top and bottom layers are located at the equilibrium vertical distances
z=\pm d_{0}/2=\pm 1.6755

Å
. The top layer gets rotated by an angle
\theta/2
and the bottom layer by
-\theta/2
around the
z
axis. In this work we choose the twist angle
\theta=1.0501^{\circ}=\cos^{-1}(1-1/(6n^{2}+6n+2))
with
n=31
, giving a conmensurate superlattice
Lopes dos Santos et al. 2012
. The moiré lattice constant is
L_{M}=13.4
nm and the number of atoms in the unit cell is
11908
. The unit vectors of the moiré lattice are
\bm{L}_{1}=L_{M}(\sqrt{3}/2,1/2)
and
\bm{L}_{2}=L_{M}(-\sqrt{3}/2,1/2)
, and the moiré reciprocal lattice has the unit vectors
\bm{g}_{1}=4\pi/\sqrt{3}L_{M}(1/2,\sqrt{3}/2)
and
\bm{g}_{2}=4\pi/\sqrt{3}L_{M}(-1/2,\sqrt{3}/2)

Nam and Koshino 2017
.

The atomistic Hamiltonian consists of tight-binding part,

and the interaction,

\displaystyle{H}=H_{\text{TB}}+{H}_{\text{int}},

(11)

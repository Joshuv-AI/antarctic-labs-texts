Letter to the editor “Surgical management of Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Haemorrhage in a resource-constrained region: A Nigerian regional experience”
Authors: Sadia Farhana
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Regarding the recent article titled "Surgical Management of Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Haemorrhage in a Resource-Constrained Region: A Nigerian Regional Experience," published in Brain and Spine (Ojo et al., 2023), I would like to express my sincere appreciation to the authors for their invaluable contribution to understanding the management challenges of aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage (aSAH) in regions with limited healthcare resources. I am grateful for their dedicated efforts in examining the outcomes of surgical interventions in a low-resource setting and the challenges faced in delivering neurosurgical care in Nigeria.

A 2019 meta-analysis indicated a global incidence of aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage (aSAH) at 7.9 cases per 100,000 person-years, with Nigeria's rate at 4.1 cases per 100,000 (Etminan et al., 2019). The review highlighted the lack of focus on underrepresented countries like Nigeria, Colombia, Ecuador, and Uganda, prompting this study to concentrate specifically on patients treated for aSAH in Lagos from January 2012 to December 2021, analyzing demographics, clinical presentations, and pre-operative Glasgow Coma Scale scores. Challenges included surgical delays, financial limitations, and ICU space issues. A standardized micro neurosurgical technique was employed, and outcomes were assessed using the Glasgow Outcome Scale, with a root cause analysis conducted to improve management.

Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (aSAH) presents a significant challenge, particularly in resource-constrained settings like Lagos, Nigeria, where its management is often associated with high mortality rates. Over a span of 10 years, revealed that, despite an 84.4% survival rate and an average discharge time of 10 days post-surgery, several factors influence outcomes. A total of 45 patients with confirmed aSAH were surgically treated, resulting in the identification of 48 aneurysms, as three patients had multiple aneurysms. This underscores the complexity of aSAH and the importance of thorough diagnostic evaluations. The cohort had a male-to-female ratio of 1:1.8, and the mean age was 49.1 years (SD 13.58), providing valuable demographic insights that can guide clinical practices and preventive strategies for at-risk populations. Upon admission, 36 patients had GCS scores between 13 and 15, necessitating intubation and intensive care unit (ICU) monitoring before surgery. This observation indicates a relatively favorable neurological status upon admission, suggesting that prompt recognition and referral can lead to better surgical outcomes. In contrast, the five patients with GCS scores of 8 or below demonstrate the challenges associated with severe presentations, which may require more intensive pre-operative management.

All aneurysms were located in the anterior circulation, with approximately 40% being anterior communicating artery (ACOM) aneurysms. Microsurgical clipping was performed on all 45 aneurysms, and the average time from ictus to surgery was 7 days, highlighting the critical window for intervention where timely surgical action is essential to minimize complications and improve outcomes in aSAH management. The significant findings from the study reveal both positive outcomes and critical challenges in managing aSAH. Notably, 58.9% of patients experienced significant improvement postoperatively and were discharged within 10 days, indicating effective surgical intervention. However, the gross mortality rate was 15.2%, with a mean interval of 4 weeks between surgery and death for those who did not survive, highlighting the severity of aSAH and the need for vigilant postoperative monitoring. Moreover, The findings underscore the urgent need for enhanced resources and training to advance the management of aSAH in resource-constrained settings, ultimately aiming for better patient outcomes and reduced mortality rates.

The thorough investigation into the clinical presentations, surgical outcomes, and the factors affecting the prognosis of aSAH patients offers significant insights into the realities of treating complex neurovascular conditions in resource-constrained environments. The detailed analysis of various barriers to timely and effective treatment such as financial limitations, delayed surgical interventions, and lack of specialized equipment highlights the need for improvements in neurovascular services. The study's use of root cause analysis (RCA) to identify these challenges and propose solutions is commendable, as it paves the way for actionable improvements in patient care and resource management. Furthermore, the study's recommendations, including increasing awareness of aSAH, improving surgical expertise, and investing in neurovascular infrastructure, offer a clear path forward to reduce mortality and morbidity in patients with aSAH in Nigeria. Future research should explore the potential of endovascular interventions and continue to address disparities in healthcare access to further improve outcomes for these patients.

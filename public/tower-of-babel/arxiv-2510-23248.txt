The Generalized Second Law and the Spatial Curvature Index
Diego Pavón

Abstract
By applying the generalized second law to the apparent horizon of a homogeneous and isotropic universe and imposing that the equation of state is no less than − 1 -1 , it is seen that universes with either flat or closed spatial sections are consistent with the joint consideration of the aforesaid law and the dominant energy condition, but not so universes with hyperbolic spatial sections.

I
Introduction

Modern cosmology is mostly based on the cosmological principle; it asserts that our universe is homogeneous and isotropic in the large scale average. By “large scale” we mean scales larger than the biggest galaxy superclusters. Nearly a century ago, Robertson
robertson
and Walker
walker
derived the most general line element that satisfies the aforesaid principle and does not depend on any gravity theory. In comoving polar coordinates it takes the form

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left[\frac{dr^{2}}{1-kr^{2}}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})\right],

(1)

where a(t) is the scale factor and
k
the curvature index. The latter is either
-1
,
0
or
+1
depending on whether the spatial sections of our universe are hyperbolic, flat or closed, respectively.

Tests of the cosmological principle have been carried out for many years using different observational techniques, on the isotropy side, see e.g.
ellis1984
;
kitching2014
;
bengaly2019
;
guandalin2023
;
dinda2025

clarkson2008

clarkson2010

zhang2011
;
camarena2021
. Nowadays, according to the general consensus, the aforesaid principle is, at large scales, a very good approximation to the real universe; see, however,
pietronero1998
and references therein.

As for the spatial curvature, evaluated by the dimensionless curvature parameter
\Omega_{k}=-k/(a^{2}H^{2})
where
H=\dot{a}/a

\;10^{-2}
or even less, i.e.
\;\Omega_{k0}\simeq 0

This implies that observationally it is very difficult to discard any of the three possible values of the curvature index. The aim of this short paper is precisely to eliminate one of them by means of theoretical arguments only. These just involve Einstein gravity, the dominant energy condition (DEC) and and the generalized second law (GSL). Therefore our reasoning does not rest on any particular model. As it turns out, universes with hyperbolic spatial sections, i.e.

II
Combining the DEC and GSL

Let us begin by considering an ever-expanding, homogeneous and isotropic universe that obeys Einstein gravity. Thus we can write,

3H^{2}+3\frac{k}{a^{2}}=8\pi\rho

(2)

and

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=0,

(3)

where
\rho
is the total energy density, i.e., radiation, matter (luminous and dark) and dark energy, and
p
the total pressure. The cosmic equation of state is defined as
w=p/\rho
and depends on the scale factor only.

Now the question arises: Which are the limits, from above and from below of
w
? The first one is given by the requirement that the speed of sound,

\sqrt{\partial p/\partial\rho}
, does not exceed the speed of light. So,
w\leq 1
. Unfortunately a parallel reasoning cannot be applied to derive the lower limit since it can be negative, as it is the case when the universe is dominated by dark energy. Here we recall how straightforwardly the lower limit of
w
can be determined with the help of the DEC
wald1984
;
eric2004
. In the present context the latter boils down to
\rho>0
alongside
\rho+p\geq 0
.

Thus, the lower bound

w\geq-1

(4)

on the cosmic equation of state readily emerges.

Next, we will see that the GSL alone does not restrict the sign of
k
. We proceed as follows. Equations (
2
) and (
3
) taken together produce

w=-\frac{1}{1-\Omega_{k}}\left[1+\frac{2}{3}\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}\Omega_{k}\right],

(5)

where
\mid\Omega_{k}\mid<1
.
Note that this expression is valid regardless the various matter-energy species interact with each other or not. Clearly
w
will be as low as
-1
whenever
H
remains constant and
\Omega_{k}=0
.

Now, we may ask: Will
w
also have a lower limit when
k\neq 0
? The answer is yes provided that the GSL for cosmic horizons
Davies1987
;
dpc
;
dpc1
;
dpc2
holds.

To see this recalls that the deceleration parameter is given by
q=-1-(\dot{H}/H^{2})
, Then we can express (
5
) in terms of the said parameter

w=-\frac{1-2q+\Omega_{k}}{3(1-\Omega_{k})}.

(6)

The GSL implies that the area of the apparent horizon
2
2

2

The boundary hyper-surface of the space-time anti-trapped region.
,
{\cal{A}}=4\pi/[H^{2}+ka^{-2}]
, never decreases in a expanding universe
bak-rey2000
. That is to say,
{\cal{A}^{\prime}}=-({\cal{A}}^{2}/2\pi)(HH^{\prime}-ka^{-3})\geq 0
. This readily translates into
1+q\geq\Omega_{k}

npd2023a
;
npd2023b
. Using this in Eq. (
6
) it follows
w\geq\zeta
, where

\zeta=\frac{\textstyle{1\over{3}}\Omega_{k}-1}{1-\Omega_{k}}

(7)

is the lower limit of the cosmic equation of state,
w
, valid at any redshift. Notice that for flat universes with De Sitter expansion
\zeta
reduces to
-1
, as it should, and that for
\Omega_{k}>0
this limit gets less than
-1
(while
w\geq-1
) and diverges for
\Omega_{k}\rightarrow 1
. This leads us to suspect that
\Omega_{k}
may not be positive even though it is compatible with the GSL and DEC when taken separately. This prompt us to combine one with the other to see whether there is nothing wrong with
\,\Omega_{k}>0
, nonetheless.

To this end we proceed as follows. The simplest linear combination of
w\geq-1
and
w\geq\zeta
is,

(1+\lambda)w\geq-1+\lambda\left(\frac{\textstyle{1\over{3}}\Omega_{k}-1}{1-\Omega_{k}}\right)

(8)

with
\lambda
a continuous parameter satisfying
\lambda>-1
. The latter inequality follows from careful inspection of (
8
) and bearing in mind that that
-1<\Omega_{k}<1
.

Introducing the expression (
6
) for
w
in (
8
), we obtain after a short algebra

1+q\geq g(\lambda)\,\Omega_{k},

(9)

where

g(\lambda)=\frac{3}{2}\left[\frac{1+\frac{\lambda}{3}}{1+\lambda}+\frac{1}{3}\right]

(10)

is an ever decreasing function.

First, we note that the deceleration parameter lies in the range
-1+\Omega_{k}\leq q\leq 1
3
3

3

The upper bound on
q
corresponds at early times when the universe was dominated by massless radiation. The lower bound, corresponds at late times when the universe is accelerating and one uses the DEC, under the form
\dot{H}\leq k/a^{2}
, alongside the expression for
\Omega_{k}
.
. On the other hand, the above constrain on
\lambda
implies
g(\lambda)\geq 1
, which fully agrees with the GSL,
1+q\geq\Omega_{k}
(which is satisfied whatever the sign of
\Omega_{k}
). Hence, the physically meaningful range of the parameter
\lambda
is
-1<\lambda<\infty
. (The other range,
-\infty<\lambda<-1
, is not to be considered because there
g(\lambda)<1
). Note, by passing, that
g(\lambda)
is not bounded from above.

Clearly, for
\Omega_{k}<0
and
\Omega_{k}=0
(corresponding to (
k=+1
and
k=0
, respectively) Eq. (
9
) is satisfied by any pair of
q

and
\lambda
values lying in their respective intervals just mentioned. By contrast, if
\Omega_{k}>0
(
k=-1)
the said equation is violated since its left-hand side is bounded from above (
1+q\leq 2
), while its right-hand side is unbounded from above (
1<g(\lambda)<\infty
). That is to say, for whatever
1+q
value there are infinitely many
\lambda
values (let be
\lambda_{0}
any of them) such that
1+q<g(\lambda_{0})\Omega_{k}
, thus violating Eq. (
9
).

Therefore, we may conclude that homogeneous and isotropic universes with hyperbolic spatial sections are not consistent whit the DEC and GSL combined together.
We wish to emphasize that this result rests only on Einstein gravity, the DEC (that implies
w>-1
) and the GSL (which leads to
w>\zeta
) and it is independent on any particular cosmological model.

The following example helps illustrate this. Assume that from a late time on (
a\gg 1
) the universe is dominated by pressureless matter and the cosmological constant
\Lambda
.

Then, the scale factor will obey the pair of Einstein equations

3H^{2}+3\frac{k}{a^{2}}=8\pi\rho_{m}+\Lambda\qquad\quad(\rho_{m}=\rho_{m0}\,a^{-3})

(11)

and

3\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-4\pi\rho_{m}+\Lambda.

(12)

From these two we get,

\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}\,-\,\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}\,-\frac{k}{a^{2}}=-4\pi\rho_{m}\,,

(13)

Since
a\gg 1
Eq. (
13
) can be approximated, to any desired accuracy, by

a\,\ddot{a}\,-\,(\dot{a})^{2}\,-\,k=0

(14)

whose solutions, depending on the value of
k
, are

a(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}H_{\star}^{-1}\,\text{cosh}(H_{\star}t)\,,&&k=+1\\
a_{\star}\,\exp(H_{\star}t)\,,&&k=0\\
H_{\star}^{-1}\,\text{sinh}(H_{\star}t)\,,&&k=-1\end{array}\right.

(15)

where
H_{\star}\equiv\sqrt{{\Lambda}/{3}}
. The corresponding
expressions for the deceleration parameter,
q=-1-(\dot{H}/H^{2})
, are,

q(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-1\,-\,\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}(H_{\star}t)}\,,&&k=+1\\
-1\,,&&k=0\\
-1\,+\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(H_{\star}t)}\,,&&k=-1\,.\end{array}\right.

(16)

In its turn, the corresponding expressions for
\Omega_{k}
are

\Omega_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}(H_{\star}t)}\,,&&k=+1\\
0\,,&&k=0\\
\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(H_{\star}t)}\,,&&k=-1\,.\end{array}\right.

(17)

That is to say, in all three cases
1+q
equals the corresponding
\Omega_{k}
at any time thereby it is not possible
to discard any of the three possible
k
values just by considering the GSL,
1+q\geq\Omega_{k}
.

However, things fare differently when one resorts to Eq. (
9
).
Using the expressions for
q
and
\Omega_{k}
of above, it is immediately seen that for
k=+1
as well as for
k=0
the said equation is trivially satisfied (recall again that
g(\lambda)>1
) while it is violated for
k=-1
, as expected. So, this simple example shows that the possibility
\Omega_{k}>0
(i.e.
k=-1
) gets excluded on general grounds.

This is fully consistent with recent findings by Di Valentino et al.
divalentino2020
suggesting that the Planck power spectrum favors a closed universe at 3-4 standard deviations (
-0.007>\Omega_{k}>-0.095
) at 99% C.L. See also
handley2021
and figure 17 in
yang2023
.

III
Discussion

By combining the dominant energy condition with the generalized second law in a homogeneous, isotropic and ever-expanding universe we have found that curvature parameter,
\Omega_{k}
, can be either negative or nil but not positive; i.e., that the spatial sections of this universe are not hyperbolic.

It may be counter-argued that there are cosmological models with hyperbolic spatial sections that, on the one hand obey the GSL and, independently, on the other hand, their equation of state always remains above
-1
. However, these models are unrealistic since they overlook the fact that the GSL and the DEC are not really independent (they should not be taken separately) as they affect each other since
q
enters the expression of
w
, Eq. (
6
), and it is bounded by the GSL. As a consequence these models ignore the restriction
w\geq\zeta
(with
\zeta
given by Eq. (
7
)), and that the lower bound of
q
is not
-1
but
-1+\Omega_{k}
.

Before closing it seems worthwhile mentioning that we have not considered phantom behavior because phantom fields, aside from violating both the DEC and GSL, are afflicted by severe classical
Dabrowski2015
and quantum
cline2004
instabilities.

Altogether, if future cosmic observations finally demonstrate that the spatial sections of our universe are hyperbolic (modulo the cosmological principle holds true), then at least, Einstein gravity or the DEC or the GSL fail.

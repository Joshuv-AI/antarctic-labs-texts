Wee Teck Gan

1.
Introduction

This paper and its prequel
[
ChG
]
aim to highlight some implications of the principle of triality in the Langlands program.
The principle of triality refers to the fact that the (split) simply-connected
{\rm Spin}_{8}
and the adjoint
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
possess an order 3 outer automorphism
\theta
. In
[
ChG
]
, by composing known cases of Langlands functorial lifting with the triality automorphism,
we showed that one can obtain new and dramatically different instances of functorial lifting. In the present paper, we shall consider the implication of triality in the theory of twisted endoscopy.

After recalling some necessary background on the triality automorphism in §
2
,
we determine the twisted endoscopic groups associated to the twisted space
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
in §
3
.
It turns out that the twisted endoscopic groups of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
are one of the following: the exceptional group
{\rm G}_{2}
,
{\rm SL}_{3}
and
{\rm SO}_{4}
.
For the case of
H={\rm SL}_{3}
, for example, the associated map of dual groups is the unique lift of the adjoint representation

\tilde{{\rm Ad}}:H^{\vee}={\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}}).

Indeed,
\tilde{\rm Ad}
fits into the folllowing composite of morphisms of dual groups:

(1.1)

\begin{CD}{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})@>{}>{}>{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})@>{\tilde{{\rm Ad}}}>{}>{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})@>{}>{}>{\rm SO}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})@>{\rm std}>{}>{\rm GL}_{8}({\mathbb{C}}),\end{CD}

which is nothing but the adjoint representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
on its semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
.

Using the stable twisted trace formula established by Moeglin and Waldspurger, one would thus expect to show the
(weak) twisted endoscopic lifting from
{\rm SL}_{3}
to
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
. On the other hand, the functorial lifting associated to the first and third arrows are simply the pullbacks of automorphic forms from
{\rm GL}_{3}
to
{\rm SL}_{3}
and from
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
to
{\rm SO}_{8}
respectively, while the functorial lifting associated to the standard representation of
{\rm SO}_{8}
is known by
[
A
,
CKPSS
]
. Hence, by combining these known functorial liftings with the twisted endoscopic lifting induced by
\tilde{{\rm Ad}}
, one would be able to show the (weak) adjoint lifting from
{\rm GL}_{3}
to
{\rm GL}_{8}
. Without analyzing the stable trace formula too deeply, we carry out this strategy in this paper and show the anticipated adjoint lifting under a mild local condition:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\pi
is a cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
over a number field, such that for some finite place
v_{0}
, the local component
\pi_{v_{0}}
is a discrete series representation. Then the weak adjoint lifting
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
exists as an automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{8}
.

We recall that the adjoint lifting for
{\rm GL}_{2}
is a classical result of Gelbart and Jacquet
[
GJ
]
.
The local condition in the theorem can no doubt be removed by a more extensive investigation of the stable twisted trace formula, but we do not pursue this here,
as it is necessarily much more technical. The general case will be handled in ongoing joint work with S.W. Shin and Y. Takanashi, the goal of which is to obtain an endoscopic classification for the group
G_{2}
via the twisted endoscopy for
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
.

As a second result of this paper, we analyze the possible shapes of the isobaric decomposition of the automorphic representation
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
supplied by Theorem
1.2
; this is often referred to as a
cuspidality criterion
for the adjoint lifting. The results, contained in Theorems
8.2
,
8.4
,
9.2
and
10.1
, can be summarized as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\pi
be a unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
and assume that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
has been constructed as the composite of the Langlands functorial lifting given in (
1.1
), as discussed above.
Then
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is an isobaric sum of cuspidal representations with unitary central characters. Moreover,
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is self-dual of orthogonal type with trivial central character. More precisely, one has:

(i)
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
contains a cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{1}
-summand if and only if
\pi
is obtained by automorphic induction from a cyclic cubic field extension. In this case, then isobaric deocmposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is of type
1+1+3+3
or
1^{8}
, depending on whether
\pi
can be obtained as an automorphic induction from one or four cyclic cubic extensions.

(ii)
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
contains a cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{2}
-summand if and only if
\pi
is obtained by automoprhic induction from a non-cyclic cubic field extension. In this case, the isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is of type
2+6
,
2+3+3
or
2^{4}
, depending on whether
\pi
can be obtained as an automorphic induction from one (for the first two cases) or four (for the last case) non-cyclic cubic extensions.

(iii) Assume that
\pi
is not an automorphic induction from any cubic field extension, so that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
does not contain any cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{1}
- or
{\rm GL}_{2}
-summands.
Then
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
contains a cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{3}
-summand if and only if
\pi
is a twist (by an automorphic character) of a self-dual cuspidal representation. In this case, the isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is of type
3+5
.

(iv) Assume that we are not in the setting of (i), (ii) or (iii). Then one of the following holds:

(a)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is the sum of two self-dual cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{4}
-summands of orthogonal type and nontrivial central character;

(b)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is cuspidal.

Except in (i),
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is a discrete orthogonal L-parameter, i.e. it is the functorial lift of a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation of the split group
{\rm SO}_{8}
. Moreover,

{\rm ord}_{s=1}L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi),\wedge^{3})={\rm ord}_{s=1}L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi),{\rm Sym}^{2})<0,

so that the order of poles at
s=1
of
L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi),\wedge^{3})
(which is an L-function of Langlands-Shahidi type) is equal to the number of summands in the isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
.

The proof of this theorem relies crucially on the functorial lifting results of Gelbart-Jacquet
[
GJ
]
, Arthur-Clozel
[
AC
]
,
Ramakrishnan
[
Ra1
]
, Kim-Shahidi
[
KS1
]
and Kim
[
Ki
]
, as well as the cuspidality criteria for some of these liftings due to Ramakrishnan-Wang
[
RW
]
and Asgari-Raghuram
[
AR
]
.

It is of course of great interest to determine precisely the fibers and image of the adjoint lifting. In particular, the question of the fibers of the adjoint lifting is closely related to the question of multiplicities and rigidity for the cuspidal spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{3}
.
It has been shown by Blasius
[
B
]
that the multiplicity-one property and the rigidity of L-packets both fail for
{\rm SL}_{n}
for
n\geq 3
.
In other words, there are cuspidal representations of
{\rm SL}_{n}
(
n\geq 3
) with multiplicity
>1
, and there are inequivalent cuspidal L-packets which are nearly equivalent.
Lapid
[
L1
]
extended this line of study with a more quantitative analysis, especially for the endoscopic L-packets of
{\rm SL}_{n}
obtained by endoscopic transfer (
=
automorphic induction) from elliptic tori. In particular, he showed that in the context of
{\rm SL}_{3}
, the so-called
global multiplicity
of a cuspidal endoscopic L-packet is either
1
or
2
(see
[
L1
, §2, Pg 162]
for the definition and
[
L1
, Theorem 4]
for the result).
For example, in the context of Theorem
1.3
(i), the degenerate case where
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is the sum of 8 cubic characters coincides precisely with the case where the global multiplicity is
2
.

As a final result for this paper, we exploit the existence of the adjoint lifting to show the following bound towards the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture for
{\rm GL}_{3}
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
with unitary central character and a discrete series local component over a number field
k
. At each place
v
where
\pi_{v}
is unramified, let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of its Satake parameter in
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
. Writing
|\lambda|=q_{v}^{a}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
(and with
q_{v}
equal to the cardinality of the residue field of
k_{v}
), one has:

|a|\leq\frac{21}{65}<\frac{1}{3}.

This relies on the general bounds of Luo-Rudnick-Sarnak
[
LRS
]
and improves upon a result of Blomer-Brumley
[
BB
]
, which gives
|a|\leq 5/14
for
{\rm GL}_{3}
. The Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture, of course, asserts that
a=0
. A recent paper of LY Yang
[
Y
]
, extending earlier work of Ramakrishnan
[
Ra3
]
over
\mathbb{Q}
, shows that this holds for infinitely many places
v
of
k
.

2.
Triality

In this section, we recall some necessary background on the triality automorphism of
{\rm Spin}_{8}
and
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
.

2.1.
Symmetric composition algebras and
{\rm Spin}_{8}

Let us first recall from
[
ChG
, §2]
a construction of
{\rm Spin}_{8}
with its triality automorphism via the theory of symmetric composition algebras. For the definition and properties of symmetric composition algebras, the reader may consult
[
KMRT
]
.
By
[
KMRT
, Theorem 34.37]
, there are essentially two isomorphism classes of split symmetric composition algebra over a field
F
. These may be described as follows:

•

Start with the split octonion algebra
(\mathbb{O},\cdot,N)
over
F
, where
N
is its norm form permitting composition:
N(x\cdot y)=N(x)\cdot N(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{O}
.
Then one defines a new multiplication on
\mathbb{O}
by
x\ast y=\bar{x}\cdot\bar{y}
. The algebra
(\mathbb{O},\ast,N)
is the para-octonion algebra and one still has
N(x\ast y)=N(x)\cdot N(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{O}
. Moreover,
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{O},\cdot)={\rm Aut}(\mathbb{O},\ast)\simeq G_{2}^{\mathbb{O}}
.

•

Assume now that
F
contains a primitive 3rd root of unity
\omega
(so
{\rm char}(F)\neq 3
).
The other split symmetric composition algebra of dimension
8
is realised on the space
\mathbb{M}
of trace zero
3\times 3
matrices with multiplication given (miraculously) by
[
KMRT
, Pg. 472, (34.18)]

x\ast y=\omega xy+(1-\omega)yx-\frac{1}{3}T(yx),\quad\text{ for $x,y\in\mathbb{M}$,}

where
T
is the trace of a
3\times 3
matrix. If the characteristic polynomial of
x\in\mathbb{M}
is

P_{x}(t)=t^{3}+S(x)t-\det(x),

then the quadratic form on
\mathbb{M}
which permits composition is
\frac{1}{3}\cdot S(x)

[
KMRT
, Prop. 34.19]
. The automorphism group of
(\mathbb{M},\ast),S
is the group
{\rm PGL}_{3}\subset{\rm SO}(\mathbb{M},S)
acting on
\mathbb{M}
by conjugation. In other words, the action of
{\rm PGL}_{3}
on
\mathbb{M}
is isomorphic to the adjoint representation.

Thus, over a general field
F
with
{\rm char}(F)\neq 3
, the
F
-forms of the above symmetric composition algebras are classified by
H^{1}(F,G_{2})
and

H^{1}(F,{\rm PGL}_{3})
respectively.

Given a symmetric composition algebra
(V,\ast,Q)
of dimension
8
over
F
, let us define

{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q)=\{(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\in{\rm SO}(V,Q)^{3}:g_{1}(x\ast y)=g_{2}(x)\ast g_{3}(y)\text{ for $x,y\in V$}\}.

Then this group is known to be isomorphic to
{\rm Spin}_{8}
over
\overline{F}
.
Moreover, the cyclic permutation of the three coordinates in
{\rm SO}(V,Q)^{3}
preserves the subgroup
{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q)
and defines an order
3
automorphism

\theta_{V}:{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q)\longrightarrow{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q).

The fixed group of
\theta_{V}
is thus the subgroup
{\rm Aut}(V,\ast,Q)\subset{\rm SO}(V,Q)
.
Because the automorphism
\theta
necessarily preserves the center
Z_{{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q)}
, it descends to an automorphism of the adjoint group
{\rm PGSO}(V,Q)
, still denoted by
\theta_{V}
.

2.2.
Splitting of
{\rm Aut}
-sequence.

Recall that one has a short exact sequence

(2.1)

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>{\rm PGSO}_{8}@>{}>{}>{\rm Aut}({\rm Spin}_{8})@>{}>{}>{\rm Out}({\rm Spin}_{8})=S_{3}@>{}>{}>1.\end{CD}

The data of a symmetric composition algebra
(V,\ast,Q)
thus gives a splitting of this short exact sequence over the order 3 subgroup
A_{3}\subset S_{3}
. Since we could apply the above discussion to the two symmetric composition algebra
(\mathbb{O},\ast,N)
and
(\mathbb{M},\ast,S)
(when
F=\overline{F}
say), we obtain two splittings of this short exact sequence over
A_{3}
whose fixed subgroups are
{\rm G}_{2}^{\mathbb{O}}
and
{\rm PGL}_{3}
respectively. According to
[
KMRT
, Pg. 487, Cor. 35.10 and Prop. 36.14]
, these are the only two possible splittings (up to inner conjugation) over
\overline{F}
.

In particular, the embedding

{\rm PGL}_{3}\simeq{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{M},\ast,S)\hookrightarrow{\rm Spin}(\mathbb{M},\ast,S)

is invariant under the action of
\theta_{\mathbb{M}}
and is the map denoted by
\tilde{\rm Ad}
in the introduction.

2.3.
Representations

By construction, the group
{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q)
is equipped with three projections

\rho_{j}:{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q)\longrightarrow{\rm SO}(V,Q),

with
{\rm Ker}(\rho_{j})\simeq\mu_{2}\subset Z_{{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q)}
. Thus one has 3 inequivalent
8
-dimensional irreducible orthogonal representations of
{\rm Spin}(V,\ast,Q)
which are permuted by
\theta
.
Likewise, the projection from the three
{\rm SO}(V,Q)
’s gives 3 maps

f_{j}:{\rm SO}(V,Q)\longrightarrow{\rm PGSO}(V,Q),

which are permuted by
\theta
.

The following lemma will only be used much later in the paper, but this seems a convenient place to mention it:

Lemma 2.2
.

.
Let

\rho_{1},\,\rho_{2},\,\rho_{3}:{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow{\rm SO}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})\subset{\rm GL}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})

be the three inequivalent 8-dimensional irreducible representations of
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Then

\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\simeq\rho_{3}\oplus\wedge^{3}\rho_{3}.

Now consider, for each
j
, the composite

\begin{CD}{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})@>{}>{}>{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})@>{\tilde{\rm Ad}}>{}>{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})@>{\rho_{j}}>{}>{\rm SO}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})\subset{\rm GL}_{8}({\mathbb{C}}).\end{CD}

Since
\tilde{\rm Ad}
is invariant under the action of
\theta_{\mathbb{M}}
, this composite is independent of
j
as a representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
. As mentioned in the introduction, it is simply the adjoint representation
{\rm Ad}
of
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
.
The above lemma thus implies the following corollary:

Corollary 2.3
.

One has an isomorphism

{\rm Ad}\otimes{\rm Ad}\simeq{\rm Ad}\oplus\wedge^{3}{\rm Ad}.

of representations of
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
.

2.4.
Chevalley-Steinberg épinglage.

For the purpose of this paper, the above geometric algebra approach to
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
is less useful. Instead, we
will give a root theoretic description of triality, by fixing a Chevalley-Steinberg system of épinglage for
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
.
More precisely, we start with a Chevalley model of the split group
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
over
\mathbb{Z}
and
fix a pair
T\subset B
consisting of a maximal split torus contained in a Borel subgroup over
{\mathbb{Z}}
. This gives a system of simple roots

\Delta(T,B)=\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\},

with
\alpha_{0}
corresponding to the branch vertex in the Dynkin diagram:

\textstyle{\alpha_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\alpha_{0}}
\textstyle{\alpha_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\alpha_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We may fix a pinning
(T,B,\{x_{\alpha_{i}}\})
, with
x_{\alpha_{i}}
a basis vector in the root subspace
\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha_{i}}
. This then defines a splitting of the exact sequence

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>{\rm PGSO}_{8}@>{}>{}>{\rm Aut}({\rm PGSO}_{8})@>{}>{}>{\rm Out}({\rm PGSO}_{8})@>{}>{}>1,\end{CD}

with image

{\rm Aut}({\rm PGSO}_{8},T,B,\{x_{\alpha_{i}}\})\subset{\rm Aut}({\rm PGSO}_{8}).

Since
{\rm Out}({\rm PGSO}_{8})\simeq S_{3}
, this gives us a triality automorphism
\theta
which permutes
\{x_{\alpha_{1}},x_{\alpha_{2}},x_{\alpha_{3}}\}
cyclically and fixes
x_{\alpha_{0}}
, so that

{\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\theta}\simeq{\rm G}_{2}.

The dual group
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
thus inherits
T^{\vee}\subset B^{\vee}
and a pinning
\{x_{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}}\}
permuted by
\theta^{\vee}
as well.
Moreover,

{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{\theta^{\vee}}\simeq{\rm G}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})

with maximal torus
(T^{\vee})^{\theta^{\vee}}
. The action of
(T^{\vee})^{\theta^{\vee}}
on
\mathfrak{spin}_{8}={\rm Lie}({\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}}))
gives rise to a
{\rm G}_{2}
root system
\Phi_{G_{2}}
, which is simply the restriction of the roots of
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
(
=
coroots of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
) to
(T^{\vee})^{\theta}
. Writing

\alpha=\text{restriction of $\alpha_{i}^{\vee}$ ($i=1,2,3$)}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta=\text{restriction of $\alpha_{0}^{\vee}$},

one has a system of simple roots
\{\alpha,\beta\}
for
\Phi_{G_{2}}
. The long root spaces have dimension
1
whereas the short root spaces have dimension
3
.
Moreover, the triality automorphism
\theta^{\vee}
preserves each of these root spaces for
\Phi_{G_{2}}
.

This Chevalley-Steinberg system of pinning provides us with a coordinate system with respect to which we will perform some explicit computations in the next section.

3.
Twisted Endoscopic Groups

We
shall be considering the theory of twisted endoscopy developed by Langlands and Kottwitz-Shelstad
[
KSh
]
to the twisted space defined by the triality automorphism
\theta

of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
. The general theory of the twisted trace formula was developed in
[
LW
]
and its stabilization
has been established in the 2-volume book
[
MW1
,
MW2
]
of Moeglin-Waldspurger (modulo the twisted weighted fundamental lemma for general quasi-split groups). To exploit this stable twisted trace formula, we need to work out the elliptic twisted endoscopic data for
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
. This is the purpose of this section.

3.1.
Lie algebra computations.

Our aim now is to determine, up to isomorphism, the elliptic endoscopic data
(H,s,\xi)

of the twisted space
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
over the number field
k
,
following
[
KSh
, §2.1]
.
1
1

1

In our setting, the cocycle
{\bf a}

loc. cit.
is chosen to be
1
,
which forces the
a^{\prime}
in (2.1.4a) to be
1
as well.

As
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
is adjoint and split over
k
, their description simplifies a little and
we may assume that these triple
(H,s,\xi)
are as follows:

•

the element
s
is a finite order element of
T^{\vee}
,

•

the (
endoscopic
) group
H
is a split connected reductive group over
k
equipped with an isomorphism
between
H^{\vee}
and the centralizer
{\rm C}_{s}
in
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
of the element
s\cdot\theta^{\vee}\in{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})\cdot\theta^{\vee}
,

•

(
elliptic
condition)
Z(\xi(H^{\vee}))^{0}\subset Z_{{\rm Spin}_{8}}
, or equivalently,
H
is semisimple.

Indeed, it follows from a classical result of Steinberg
[
KSh
, Chap. 1, §1.1, Pg. 14]
that
{\rm C}_{s}
is connected
for all
s\in T^{\vee}
. It is harmless to view
\xi
as an inclusion and write
H^{\vee}={\rm C}_{s}
.

To determine the possible
H^{\vee}
, we take a general
s\cdot\theta^{\vee}
, consider its adjoint action on
\mathfrak{spin}_{8}
and determine the fixed Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{spin}_{8}^{s\cdot\theta^{\vee}}
. Since we are interested in
s\cdot\theta^{\vee}
up to conjugacy by
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
, we may consider
s
up to
\theta
-conjugacy by elements of
T^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})
. As the group
T^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})
is divisible, it is not hard to see that one can assume that

s\in T^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})^{\theta^{\vee}}.

Since
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
is simply-connected,
X_{*}(T^{\vee})=X^{*}(T)
is equal to the root lattice of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
.
Hence we may write any element of
T^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})^{\theta^{\vee}}
as:

(3.1)

s(u,t)=\alpha_{0}(u)\cdot\alpha_{1}(t)\cdot\alpha_{2}(t)\cdot\alpha_{3}(t),\quad\text{with $u,t\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}$.}

By our discussion in the previous subsection, the action of
s(u,t)\cdot\theta^{\vee}
on
\mathfrak{spin}_{8}
stabilizes
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}={\rm Lie}(T^{\vee})
and
the root subspaces for the
{\rm G}_{2}
-root system.
Hence,

\mathfrak{spin}_{8}^{s(u,t)\cdot\theta^{\vee}}=(\mathfrak{t}^{\vee})^{\theta^{\vee}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Phi_{G_{2}}}\mathfrak{u}_{\gamma}^{s(u,t)\cdot\theta^{\vee}}.

In particular, to determine this Lie subalgebra fixed by
s(u,t)\cdot\theta^{\vee}
, we need to figure out its fixed space in each root subspace
\mathfrak{u}_{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in\Phi_{G_{2}}
. This is a linear algebra computation which we illustrate for the case where
\gamma=\alpha
and
\beta
.

•

\gamma=\alpha
: the action of
s(u,t)\cdot\theta^{\vee}
on the basis elements
\{x_{\alpha_{1}^{\vee}},x_{\alpha_{2}^{\vee}},x_{\alpha_{3}^{\vee}}\}
is given by:

\begin{CD}x_{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}}@>{\theta^{\vee}}>{}>x_{\alpha_{i+1}^{\vee}}@>{{\rm Ad}(s(u,t))}>{}>t^{2}/u\cdot x_{\alpha_{i+1}^{\vee}}.\end{CD}

Hence, with respect to the basis
\{x_{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}}\}
, the action of
s(u,t)\cdot\theta^{\vee}
on
\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}
is given by the matrix

t^{2}/u\cdot E=t^{2}/u\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right).

The characteristic polynomial of
E
is
-\lambda^{3}+1
, so that its eigenvalues are the 3rd roots of unity. Hence, the 1-eigenspace of the
s(u,t)\cdot\theta^{\vee}
-action on
\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}
is nonzero if and only if
t^{6}=u^{3}
.

•

\gamma=\beta
: the action of
s(u,t)\cdot\theta^{\vee}
on
x_{\alpha_{0}^{\vee}}
is given by

3.2.
Elliptic endoscopic data.

From the Lie algebra computations of the previous subsection, we can deduce:

Proposition 3.2
.

The elliptic endoscopic groups for the twisted space
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
determined by the triality automorphism are the split groups
{\rm G}_{2}
,
{\rm SL}_{3}
and
{\rm SO}_{4}
.
Moreover, the outer automorphism group of these elliptic endoscopic data is trivial.

Proof.

As mentionned above, because
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
is simply-connected, the group
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{s\cdot\theta^{\vee}}
is in fact connected
[
KSh
, Chap. 1, §1.1, Pg. 14]
.
The Lie algebra computations above thus allow us to identify
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{s\cdot\theta^{\vee}}
up to isogeny, so we need to pin down the isogeny class.
In item (i) above, we already know that:

{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{\theta^{\vee}}={\rm G}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}).

For item (iv), observe that
s(\zeta,1)\cdot\theta^{\vee}
has order
3
and thus provide a splitting of the automorphism short exact sequence (
2.1
) over
{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
. As we have noted, by
[
KMRT
, Cor. 35.10 and Prop. 36.14]
, there are only two such splittings (up to conjugacy) with fixed groups of type
{\rm G}_{2}
or
{\rm PGL}_{3}
. Hence, in the context of item (iv), we have

{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{s(\zeta,1)\cdot\theta^{\vee}}\simeq{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}}).

Finally, for item (iii), we observe that

\mathfrak{spin_{8}}^{s(1,-1)\cdot\theta^{\vee}}\subset\mathfrak{spin}_{8}^{\theta^{\vee}}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}

so that
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{s(1,-1)\cdot\theta^{\vee}}
is a subgroup of
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{\theta^{\vee}}=G_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
of type
A_{1}\times A_{1}
. This implies

{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{s(1,-1)\cdot\theta^{\vee}}\simeq{\rm SO}_{4}({\mathbb{C}}).

The first assertion of the proposition then follows from the definition of twisted endoscopic data given in
[
KSh
, Chap. 2]
.

For the assertion on the triviality of automorphism groups, it suffices to show that for each of the
three possibilities above for
H^{\vee}={\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})^{s\cdot\theta^{\vee}}
, the normalizer
N
of
H^{\vee}
in
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
is
H^{\vee}\times Z
with
Z=Z_{{\rm Spin}_{8}}
.

Firstly, an inspection of the
8
-dimensional representation
{\rm std}_{|H^{\vee}}
(which is respectively of type
1+7
, irreducible, or
1+3+4
), shows that the centralizer of
H^{\vee}
in
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
is
Z_{H^{\vee}}\times Z
in all cases. This shows the claim for the
{\rm G}_{2}
case, since
{\rm Out}({\rm G}_{2})\,=1
. In the two other cases we have
{\rm Out}(\widehat{H})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2
and so some additional arguments are needed:

-

(
{\rm SO}_{4}
case) We have
N=H^{\vee}
since the
3
-dimensional summand of
{\rm std}_{|H^{\vee}}
is not isomorphic to its outer conjugate.

-

(
{\rm PGL}_{3}

such that
{\rm int}_{g}
induces
h\mapsto{}^{\rm t}h^{-1}
on

g

{\rm SO}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})=(H^{\vee})^{{\rm int}_{g}}

{\rm std}_{|{\rm SO}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})}
has type
3+5

g
has to preserve each of these summands since it commutes with
{\rm SO}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
and thus acts by a scalar on each summand. Indeed, since
{\rm std}(g)
belongs to

,
the action of

.
This implies that

Hence we have shown that

∎

4.
Local Twisted Endoscopic Transfer

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic
0
.
Having determined the twisted endoscopic groups
H
for
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
, the theory of twisted endoscopy furnishes a local transfer map from
\theta
-twisted orbital integrals on
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)
to orbital integrals on
H(F)
. In this section, we recall the basic objects and record some results on this local transfer that we will need.

4.1.
Local K-forms

A slight complication with the theory of stable twisted trace formula is the need to take into account of K-forms
[
MW1
, §I.1.11]
, which are certain inner forms of the twisted space in question.

In the context of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
, we have seen in
§
2
that an octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}
gives rise to a group
{\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}}
, which is an inner form of the corresponding split group equipped with a triality automorphism. Over a number field or a local field, these turn out to be the K-forms of the twisted space
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
. Thus, over a non-Archimedean local field or
{\mathbb{C}}
,
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
has no additional
K
-forms, since there is a unique octonion algebra up to isomorphism. On the other hand, over
{\mathbb{R}}
, there are two octonions algebras: the split one and a division algebra. As such, there are two K-forms of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
over
{\mathbb{R}}
.

4.2.
Twisted orbital integrals

We now recall some properties of the space
\mathcal{I}^{\theta}({\rm PGSO}^{\mathbb{O}}_{8})
of
\theta
-twisted orbital integrals on a local K-form
{\rm PGSO}^{\mathbb{O}}_{8}
over
F
. For simplicity, we will suppress
\mathbb{O}
from the notation and write
G={\rm PGSO}_{8}
.

For a test function
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G(F))
, its normalized
\theta
-twisted orbital integral
\mathcal{O}^{\theta}(-,f)
is a function on the subset of
\theta
-regular semisimple elements
\gamma
of
G(F)
defined by

(4.1)

\mathcal{O}^{\theta}(\gamma,f)=\Delta_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}(\gamma\cdot\theta)\cdot\int_{G_{\gamma\cdot\theta}(F)\backslash G(F)}f(g^{-1}\gamma\theta(g))\,\frac{dg}{dt},

where
G_{\gamma\cdot\theta}
denotes the stabilizer of
\gamma\cdot\theta
in
G
and

\Delta_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}(\gamma\cdot\theta)=|\det(1-{\rm Ad}(t\theta)|\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma\cdot\theta})|^{1/2}

is the positive square root of the
\theta
-twisted Weyl discriminant. The space
\mathcal{I}^{\theta}(G)
is the topological vector space of such normalized twisted orbital integrals.

Assuming now that
G
is split, we have the following structures on the space
\mathcal{I}^{\theta}(G)
:

(i)

[
MW1
, I.3.1]
For each
\theta
-stable Levi subgroup
M
of
G={\rm PGSO}_{8}
(or equivalently any twisted Levi subspace
M\cdot\theta
), there is a restriction or “constant term” map:

{\rm rest}^{G\cdot\theta}_{M\cdot\theta}:\mathcal{I}^{\theta}(G)\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}^{\theta}(M)^{W(G,M)^{\theta}}

where
W(G,M)=N_{G(F)}(M)/M(F)
and
W(G,M)^{\theta}
is the
\theta
-fixed subgroup. Moreover, one has the transitivity of restrictions:

{\rm rest}^{M\cdot\theta}_{T\cdot\theta}\circ{\rm rest}^{G\cdot\theta}_{M\cdot\theta}={\rm rest}^{G\cdot\theta}_{T\cdot\theta}

where
M=L_{0}
or
L
.

(ii)

[
MW1
, I.4.2]
The restriction maps give rise to a natural filtration

0=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\subset\mathcal{F}^{0}\subset\mathcal{F}^{1}\subset\mathcal{F}^{2}=\mathcal{I}^{\theta}({\rm PGSO}_{8}).

These subspaces are given by:

\mathcal{F}^{1}={\rm Ker}({\rm rest}^{G\cdot\theta}_{T\cdot\theta})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}^{0}={\rm Ker}({\rm rest}^{G\cdot\theta}_{L_{0}\cdot\theta})\cap{\rm Ker}({\rm rest}^{G\cdot\theta}_{L\cdot\theta}).

The subspace
\mathcal{F}^{0}
is also called the cuspidal subspace and is denoted by
\mathcal{I}^{\theta}_{cusp}(G)
.

(iii)

[
MW1
, I.4.2]
The successive quotients of the above filtration are given by

\mathcal{F}^{2}/\mathcal{F}^{1}\simeq\mathcal{I}^{\theta}_{cusp}(T)^{W(G,T)^{\theta}}

and

\mathcal{F}^{1}/\mathcal{F}_{0}\simeq\mathcal{I}^{\theta}_{cusp}(L_{0})^{W(G,L_{0})^{\theta}}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\theta}_{cusp}(L)^{W(G,L)^{\theta}}.

The isomorphisms are naturally induced by the restriction maps.

Remark:
In (i) above, we have used the group
W(G,M)^{\theta}
, whereas in
[
MW1
, I.3.1 and I.4.2]
, one sees instead

W(M\cdot\theta)=N_{G(F)}(M\cdot\theta)/M(F).

Let us show that these are the same. Suppose that
g\in W(M\cdot\theta)
, so that
g^{-1}M\theta(g)=M
. This implies that
gMg^{-1}=M\cdot\theta(g)g^{-1}
.
For this to hold, one must have
\theta(g)g^{-1}\in M
, so that
g
normalizes
M
and its image in
W(G,M)=N_{G(F)}(M)/M(F)
is fixed by
\theta
.
Conversely, if
g\in N_{G(F)}(M)
is such that
\theta(g)g^{-1}\in M(F)
, then

g^{-1}M\cdot\theta\cdot g=g^{-1}M\cdot\theta(g)\cdot\theta=g^{-1}Mg\cdot\theta=M\cdot\theta,

so that
g\in N_{G(F)}(M\cdot\theta)
.

4.3.
Stable orbital integrals

For an connected reductive group
H
over
F
, let
\mathcal{SI}(H)
denote the space of stable orbital integrals on
H(F)
.
Recall that for a test function
f_{H}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(H(F))
, its normalized orbital integral is a function on strongly regular semisimple elements
\gamma
of
H(F)

defined by

(4.2)

\mathcal{O}(\gamma,f_{H})=\Delta_{H}(\gamma)\cdot\int_{H_{\gamma}(F)\backslash H(F)}f_{H}(x^{-1}\gamma x)\,\frac{dx}{dt},

where
H_{\gamma}
is the stabilizer of
\gamma
in
H(F)
(and hence is a maximal
F
-torus) and

\Delta_{H}(\gamma)=|\det(1-{\rm Ad}(\gamma)|\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{h}_{\gamma}))|^{1/2}

is the positive square root of the Weyl discriminant. We may further replace the normalized orbital integral above by its stabilized version:

(4.3)

\mathcal{SO}(\gamma,f_{H})=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{\prime},f_{H})

with the sum running over the conjugacy classes in
H(F)
which are stably conjugate to
\gamma
.
The space spanned by such normalized stable orbital integrals is the topological vector space
\mathcal{SI}(H)
.

We have the analogous structures on
\mathcal{SI}(H)
:

(i)

For each Levi subgroup
M\subset H
, there is a restriction or “constant term” map:

{\rm rest}_{M}^{H}:\mathcal{SI}(H)\longrightarrow\mathcal{SI}(M)^{W(H,M)}

where
W(H,M)=N_{H(F)}(M)/M(F)
.

(ii)

The restriction maps give rise to a natural filtration on
\mathcal{SI}(H)
:

0=:\mathcal{F}^{-1}\subset\mathcal{F}^{0}\subset\mathcal{F}^{1}\subset...\subset\mathcal{F}^{r}=\mathcal{SI}(H).

The subspace
\mathcal{F}^{k}
is defined by

\mathcal{F}^{k}=\bigcap_{M\in\mathcal{L}_{>k}(H)}{\rm Ker}({\rm rest}_{M}^{H})

where the intersection runs over the set
\mathcal{L}_{>k}(H)
of representatives of
H(F)
-conjugacy classes of Levi subgroups
M
of
H
with
{\rm corank}(M):=\dim Z_{M}/Z_{H}>k
(here
Z_{H}
and
Z_{M}
denote the centers of
H
and
M
).
In particular,
\mathcal{F}^{0}
denotes the subspace of elements whose restriction to any proper Levi subgroup vanish.
This subspace is also called the
cuspidal subspace
and is denoted by
\mathcal{SI}_{cusp}(H)
.

(iii)

The successive quotients of the above filtration is given by:

\mathcal{F}^{k}/\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\simeq\bigoplus_{M\in\mathcal{L}_{k}(H)}\mathcal{SI}_{cusp}(M)^{W(H,M)}

where the sum runs over a set
\mathcal{L}_{k}(H)
of representatives of
H(F)
-conjugacy classes of Levi subgroups
M
with corank(
M
)
=k
.
The isomorphism is given by the restriction maps to Levi subgroups of corank
k
.

We will of course be applying the above results to the case when
H
is an elliptic twisted endoscopic group of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
.
As an illustration, let us explicate the above general results when
H={\rm SL}_{3}
. In this case, using the notations from §
3.3
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{1}(H)=\{{\rm GL}_{2,l}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{2}(H)=\{T_{{\rm SL}_{3}}\}.

Then the above results say that

(4.4)

\mathcal{F}^{1}/\mathcal{F}^{0}\simeq\mathcal{SI}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{2,l})=\mathcal{I}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{2,l})

and

(4.5)

\mathcal{F}^{2}/\mathcal{F}^{1}\simeq\mathcal{SI}(T_{{\rm SL}_{3}})^{W({\rm SL}_{3},T_{{\rm SL}_{3}})}=\mathcal{I}(T_{{\rm SL}_{3}})^{S_{3}}.

Here, for the first identity, we note that the Weyl group
W({\rm SL}_{3},{\rm GL}_{2,l})
is trivial and any invariant distribution on
{\rm GL}_{2,l}
is already stably-invariant, whereas for the second identity, we have
W({\rm SL}_{3},T_{{\rm SL}_{3}})\simeq S_{3}
.

4.4.
Local transfer

For each local
K
-form
({\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}},\theta)
and each twisted endoscopic group
H
, the theory of twisted endoscopy furnishes a local transfer of orbital integrals from
({\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}},\theta)
to
H
. More precisely, we have:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic 0 and consider the setting of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
over
F
.
Then we have:

(i) The twisted endoscopic transfer gives an isomorphism of topological vector spaces

{\rm trans}:\bigoplus_{\mathbb{O}}\mathcal{I}^{\theta}_{cusp}({\rm PGSO}^{\mathbb{O}}_{8})\longrightarrow\mathcal{SI}_{cusp}({\rm G}_{2})\oplus\mathcal{SI}_{cusp}({\rm SO}_{4})\oplus\mathcal{SI}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{3}),

where the sum runs over the isomorphism classes of octonion
F
-algebras.

(ii) More generally, the twisted endoscopic transfer gives an injective map

{\rm trans}:\bigoplus_{\mathbb{O}}\mathcal{I}^{\theta}({\rm PGSO}^{\mathbb{O}}_{8})\longrightarrow\mathcal{SI}({\rm G}_{2})\oplus\mathcal{SI}({\rm SO}_{4})\oplus\mathcal{SI}({\rm SL}_{3}).

Its image is the subspace of “compatible families” of stably invariant distributions of its target (a notion we will recall after the proposition; see
[
MW1
, I.4.11]
).

(iii) Let
F
be a non-Archimedean local field whose residue characteristic is sufficiently large. For each of the group
G
under consideration here, let
\mathcal{H}_{G}
denote the spherical Hecke algebra of
G(F)
with respect to a hyperspecial subgroup. For each elliptic twisted endoscopic group
H
of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
, the embedding
\xi_{H}:H^{\vee}\hookrightarrow{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
induces (via the Satake isomorphism) a natural algebra homomorphism

\xi_{H}^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{H}.

Then one has a commutative diagram (the fundamental lemma for spherical Hecke algebras):

\begin{CD}\mathcal{H}_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}@>{\xi_{H}^{*}}>{}>\mathcal{H}_{H}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\mathcal{I}^{\theta}({\rm PGSO}_{8})@>{{\rm trans}_{H}}>{}>\mathcal{SI}(H),\end{CD}

where the vertical arrows are the formation of
\theta
-twisted orbital integrals and stable orbital integrals respectively.

Proof.

For (i) and (ii), see
[
MW1
, Prop. I.4.11, Pg. 96]
. For the fundamental lemma in (iii), see
[
MW1
, §I.6.4, Pg. 165]
and the references therein.
∎

We have still to define the notion of a “compatible family” of stably invariant distributions in Proposition
4.6
(ii) above.
Suppose that

(f_{G_{2}},f_{{\rm SO}_{4}},f_{{\rm SL}_{3}})\in\mathcal{SI}({\rm G}_{2})\oplus\mathcal{SI}({\rm SO}_{4})\oplus\mathcal{SI}({\rm SL}_{3}).

We say that this is a compatible family if

{\rm rest}^{G_{2}}_{{\rm GL}_{2,l}}(f_{G_{2}})={\rm rest}^{{\rm SO}_{4}}_{{\rm GL}_{2,l}}(f_{{\rm SO}_{4}})={\rm rest}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}_{{\rm GL}_{2,l}}(f_{{\rm SL}_{3}}),

and

{\rm rest}^{G_{2}}_{{\rm GL}_{2,s}}(f_{G_{2}})={\rm rest}^{{\rm SO}_{4}}_{{\rm GL}_{2,s}}(f_{{\rm SO}_{4}}).

Here, we recall that the Levi subgroups of the various twisted endoscopic groups
H
labelled as
{\rm GL}_{2,l}
(respectively
{\rm GL}_{2,s}
) are naturally identified with each other via admissible isomorphisms, so that the above identities make sense.
The transitivity of restriction maps implies that when the above two identities hold, we also have

{\rm rest}^{G_{2}}_{T_{G_{2}}}(f_{G_{2}})={\rm rest}^{{\rm SO}_{4}}_{T_{{\rm SO}_{4}}}(f_{{\rm SO}_{4}})={\rm rest}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}_{T_{{\rm SL}_{3}}}(f_{{\rm SL}_{3}}).

For any elliptic twisted endoscopic group
H=G_{2}
,
{\rm SL}_{3}
or
{\rm SO}_{4}
of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
, the projection of the map
{\rm trans}
in (ii) to the
H
-factor gives

{\rm trans}_{H}:\bigoplus_{\mathbb{O}}\mathcal{I}^{\theta}({\rm PGSO}^{\mathbb{O}}_{8})\longrightarrow\mathcal{SI}(H).

By Proposition
4.6
(ii), the image of
{\rm trans}_{H}
is precisely the subspace of those elements which can be completed to a compatible family. Moreover, the natural filtration
(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
on
\mathcal{SI}(H)
induces the filtration

\mathcal{T}^{\bullet}:=\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\cap{\rm trans}_{H}(I^{\theta}(G))\quad\text{ on ${\rm trans}_{H}(I^{\theta}(G))$,}

so that

\mathcal{T}^{k}/\mathcal{T}^{k-1}\subset\mathcal{F}^{k}/\mathcal{F}^{k-1}.

The following proposition characterizes

{\rm trans}_{H}(I^{\theta}(G))
in terms of this filtration and the associated graded pieces:

Proposition 4.7
.

(i) Let
H=G_{2}
. Then the successive quotients of the filtration
(\mathcal{T}^{\bullet})
are given by:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{T}^{2}/\mathcal{T}^{1}=\mathcal{I}(T_{G_{2}})^{W(G_{2},T_{G_{2}})},\\
\mathcal{T}^{1}/\mathcal{T}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{2,l})^{{\rm Out}({\rm GL}_{2})}\oplus\mathcal{I}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{2,s})^{{\rm Out}({\rm GL}_{2})},\\
\mathcal{T}^{0}=\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{cusp}(G_{2}).\end{cases}

Here
{\rm Out}({\rm GL}_{2})
refers to the outer automorphism group of
{\rm GL}_{2}
.

(ii) Let
H={\rm SL}_{3}
. Then the successive quotients of the filtration
(\mathcal{T}^{\bullet})
are given by:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{T}^{2}/\mathcal{T}^{1}=\mathcal{I}(T)^{W(G_{2},T_{G_{2}})},\\
\mathcal{T}^{1}/\mathcal{T}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{2,l})^{{\rm Out}({\rm GL}_{2})},\\
\mathcal{T}^{0}=\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathcal{SI}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{3}).\end{cases}

Here, we have made use of the natural identification
T_{{\rm SL}_{3}}\simeq T_{G_{2}}
to transport the action of
W(G_{2},T_{G_{2}})
to
T_{{\rm SL}_{3}}
, and under this transport of structure,
W(G_{2},T_{G_{2}})=W({\rm SL}_{3},T_{{\rm SL}_{3}})\times S_{2}
with the nontrivial element of
S_{2}
acting by inverting on
T_{{\rm SL}_{3}}
.

(iii) Let
H={\rm SO}_{4}
. Then the successive quotients of the filtration
(\mathcal{T}^{\bullet})
are given by:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{T}^{2}/\mathcal{T}^{1}=\mathcal{I}(T)^{W(G_{2},T_{G_{2}})},\\
\mathcal{T}^{1}/\mathcal{T}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{2,l})^{{\rm Out}({\rm GL}_{2})}\oplus\mathcal{I}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{2,s})^{{\rm Out}({\rm GL}_{2})},\\
\mathcal{T}^{0}=\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{cusp}({\rm SO}_{4}).\end{cases}

Here, we have again made use of the natural identification
T_{{\rm SO}_{4}}\simeq T_{G_{2}}
to transport the action of
W(G_{2},T_{G_{2}})
to
T_{{\rm SO}_{4}}
.

Proof.

This can be deduced from
[
MW1
, §I.4.3, Lemme, Pg. 80]
and the definition of “compatible families”; we omit the details.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

The map
{\rm trans}_{H}
is surjective if
H=G_{2}
, but is not surjective if
H={\rm SL}_{3}
or
{\rm SO}_{4}
. When
H={\rm SL}_{3}
, the image of
{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}
contains the subspace
\mathcal{SI}({\rm SL}_{3})^{{\rm Out}({\rm SL}_{3})}
of
\mathcal{SI}({\rm SL}_{3})
fixed by the outer automorphism group of
{\rm SL}_{3}
.

Proof.

For the case
H={\rm SL}_{3}
, one may compare Proposition
4.7
(i) and the equations (
4.4
) and (
4.5
). The other cases are similar and so we omit the details.
∎

4.5.
Spectral transfer

Let us now specialize to the case
H={\rm SL}_{3}
. The dual of the transfer map
{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}
is a map

{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}^{*}:\mathcal{SI}({\rm SL}_{3})^{*}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}({\rm PGSO}_{8})^{*}

from the space of stably invariant distributions of
SL_{3}(F)
to the space of invariant distributions on
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)
.
The fact that the transfer map
{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}
is not surjecitive implies that this dual transfer map is not injective. However, we note:

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose that
\pi
is a semisimple admissible representation of
{\rm SL}_{3}(F)
whose Harish-Chandra character
\Theta_{\pi}
is a stable distribution.
Then
{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}^{*}(\Theta_{\pi})\neq 0
.

Proof.

By the description of the image of
{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}
given in Corollary
4.8
, it suffices to show that
\Theta_{\pi}
is not identically zero on
\mathcal{SI}({\rm SL}_{3})^{{\rm Out}({\rm SL}_{3})}
.
Let
\sigma
be a nontrivial outer automorphism of
{\rm SL}_{3}
. Then the space
\mathcal{SI}({\rm SL}_{3})^{{\rm Out}({\rm SL}_{3})}
is spanned by the stable orbital integrals of test functions of the form
f+f^{\sigma}
.
Now since
\pi^{\sigma}\simeq\pi^{\vee}
, we have

\Theta_{\pi}(f^{\sigma})=\Theta_{\pi^{\sigma}}(f)=\Theta_{\pi^{\vee}}(f),

so that

\Theta_{\pi}(f+f^{\sigma})=\Theta_{\pi\oplus\pi^{\vee}}(f).

Since
\pi\oplus\pi^{\vee}
is a nonzero admissible semisimple representation, it is thus clear that
\Theta_{\pi\oplus\pi^{\vee}}
is nonzero on some test function
f
.
∎

5.
Unramified Spectral Transfer

We now specialize to the setting of a non-Archimedean local field
F
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
(so that there is a unique K-form, namely the split
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
). Recall that in §
2.4
, we have started with the Chevalley group
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
defined over
{\mathbb{Z}}
with a given Chevalley-Steinberg system of épinglage and equipped with a triality automorphism
\theta
. Hence we have a hyperspecial subgroup
K=G(\mathcal{O}_{F})
which is stablized by
\theta
.
Since
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
is adjoint, there is a unique conjugacy class of hyperspecial subgroups.

5.1.
Unramified L-packets

Now let
H
be one of the elliptic endoscopic groups of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
. In fact, we shall only be interested in the case
H={\rm SL}_{3}
and the reader can assume this is the case in the following. Suppose that we have an unramified L-packet of
H
associated to a semisimple conjugacy class
s\in H^{\vee}
.
Recall that
s
determines an unramified character
\chi_{s}
of a maximal split torus
T_{H}
of
H
and the L-packet in question consists simply of the constituents of the corresponding principal series representation
I_{H}(\chi_{s})
(parabolically induced from
\chi_{s}
) which have nonzero invariant vectors under some hyperspecial subgroups of
H(F)
.
Let us assume for simplicity that the unramified constituents of
I_{H}(\chi_{s})
are generic and unitary. In that case,
I_{H}(\chi_{s})
is semisimple and the L-packet consists of all its irreducible constituents.

The stable trace
\Theta_{s}
of such an unramified L-packet is the sum of the Harish-Chandra character of its members and hence (under our hypothesis above) is simply the Harish-Chandra character of
I_{H}(\chi_{s})
. It is a stable distribution on
H(F)
and hence defines a linear functional on
\mathcal{SI}(H)
. We may consider the pullback of
\Theta_{s}
under the transfer map
{\rm trans}_{H}
of Proposition
4.6
(iii). For the purpose of applying the trace formula, we need to determine the unramified spectral transfer
\Theta_{s}\circ{\rm trans}_{H}
precisely.

To formulate the answer, consider the unramified L-packet of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)
corresponding to the semisimple conjugacy class of
\xi_{H}(s)
, where
\xi_{H}:H^{\vee}\hookrightarrow{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
is the natural embedding. This unramified L-packet consists of the unramified constituents of an unramified principal series representation which can be described as follows.
The restriction of the embedding
\xi_{H}
defines an isomorphism

\xi_{H}:T_{H}^{\vee}\longrightarrow(T^{\vee})^{\theta^{\vee}}\subset T^{\vee},

where
T
is a maximal split torus of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
. This injective map of dual torus arises from a surjective morphism

(5.1)

\eta:T\longrightarrow T_{H}

satisfying

(5.2)

{\rm Ker}(\eta)={}^{1-\theta}T:=\text{ image of $[(1-\theta):T\rightarrow T]$.}

By pulling back via
\eta
, one obtains an unramified character
\chi_{s}\circ\eta
of
T(F)
. Then the unramified principal series representation of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(\mathbb{F})
corresponding to
\xi_{H}(s)
is the representation

I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
parabolically induced from
\chi_{s}\circ\eta
.

5.2.
Extensions to twisted space

Since
\xi_{H}(s)
is fixed by
\theta^{\vee}
, the character
\chi_{s}\circ\eta
is
\theta
-invariant, which implies that
the isomorphism class of
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
is fixed by
\theta
and hence can be extended to the semi-direct product
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle
(or rather to the twisted space
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)\cdot\theta
).
The extension is however not unique. The following lemma gives several equivalent ways of fixing such an extension.

Lemma 5.3
.

.
The following three extensions of
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
to
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle
are the same:

(i)

(Extension by induction) Extend
\chi_{s}\circ\eta
to
T(F)\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle
by letting
\theta
act trivially; by the functoriality of induction, this gives rise to an action of
\theta
on
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
, described explicitly by

(\theta\cdot\phi)(g)=\phi(\theta^{-1}(g))

for any
\phi\in I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
and
g\in{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)
.

(ii)

(Spherical-normalized extension) Extend
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
by requiring that
\theta
acts as identity on the 1-dimensional space
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)^{K}
.

(iii)

(Whittaker-normalized extension) For a nontrivial additive character
\psi
of
F
of consuctor
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, consider the Whittaker datum determined by
\psi
and the fixed Chavalley-Steinberg system of épinglage and a corresponding nontrivial Whittaker functional
\mathcal{W}:I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the Whittaker datum is fixed by
\theta
and we extend
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
by requiring that

\mathcal{W}\circ\theta=\mathcal{W}.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{0}\in I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
be a nonzero
K
-unramfiied vector which is unique up to scaling. Then
\phi_{0}
is characterized by the requirement that
\phi_{0}|_{K}
is a constant function. Since
\theta
stabilizes
K
, one deduces easily that the extensions in (i) and (ii) are the same. On the other hand, by the Casselman-Shalika formula, one knows that
\mathcal{W}(\phi_{0})\neq 0
. Hence the Whittaker-normalzied extension is also spherical-normalized, showing that the extensions in (ii) and (iii) are the same.
∎

We shall adopt the extension of
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
to
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle
supplied by the above lemma, denoting the resulting action of
\theta
by
I(\theta)
. Using this extension, we define the
\theta
-twisted trace of
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)
as the Harish-Chandra character of the extended representation, restricted to the coset
{\rm PGSO}_{8}(F)\cdot\theta
.
More precisely, it is given by the linear functional

\Theta^{\theta}_{\xi_{H}(s)}:f\mapsto{\rm Tr}(I(\theta)\circ I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)(f)),\quad\text{ for $f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\rm PGSO}_{8}(F))$.}

which is
\theta
-conjugacy-invariant and hence factors to a linear functional on
\mathcal{I}^{\theta}({\rm PGSO}_{8})
.
Observe that if
f\in\mathcal{H}_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}
, then

{\rm Tr}(I(\theta)\circ I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)(f))={\rm Tr}(I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)(f)),

since
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)(f)
has image contained in
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)^{K}
and
I(\theta)
acts as identity on
I(\chi_{s}\circ\eta)^{K}
.
Hence,

\Theta^{\theta}_{\xi_{H}(s)}=\Theta_{\xi_{H}(s)}\quad\text{on $\mathcal{H}_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}$.}

5.3.
Unramified spectral transfer

Now one has:

Proposition 5.4
.

In the above context, one has the twisted endoscopic character identity:

\Theta_{s}\circ{\rm trans}_{H}=\Theta^{\theta}_{\xi_{H}(s)}

as linear functionals on
\mathcal{I}^{\theta}({\rm PGSO}_{8})
. In particular, the twisted endoscopic character identity defines a lifting of unramified L-packets from
H
to
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
which is functorial with respect to the embedding

\xi_{H}:H^{\vee}\longrightarrow{\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\vee}={\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}}).

Proof.

For a test function
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\rm PGSO}_{8}(F))
, we need to show that

\Theta_{s}({\rm trans}_{H}(f))=\Theta^{\theta}_{\xi_{H}(s)}(f).

If
f\in\mathcal{H}_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}
, then this identity follows readily from Proposition
4.6
(iii). In the general case,
the proof of this identity is similar to that of
[
CG
, Prop. 6.2]
.

We first compute each side of the purported identity.
For a test function
f_{H}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(H(F))
, we first compute
\Theta_{s}(f_{H})
.
Recall that the Harish-Chandra character
\Theta_{s}
of
I_{H}(\chi_{s})
is given by a locally constant and locally integrable conjugacy-invariant function on the open dense set
H(F)_{rs}
of regular semisiple elements of
H(F)
. We denote this function by
\Theta_{s}
as well, so that

\Theta_{s}(f_{H})=\int_{H(F)_{rs}}\Theta_{s}(x)\cdot f_{H}(x)\,dx.

Moreover, since
\Theta_{s}
is the character of a principal series representation, the function
\Theta_{s}
is supported on the conjugacy classes of regular elements of the maximal split torus
T_{H}
. Hence, by the Weyl integration formula, one has (cf.
[
CG
, §5.8]
)

\Theta_{s}(f_{H})=\int_{T_{H}(F)_{rs}}\chi_{s}(t_{H})\cdot\mathcal{O}(t_{H},f_{H})\,dt_{H}

where
\mathcal{O}(-,f_{H})
is the normalized orbital integral of
f_{H}
defined in (
4.2
).
Since
t_{H}
is a regular element in the maximal split torus, there is a unique conjugacy class in the stable conjugacy class of
t_{H}
, and hence
one has
\mathcal{SO}(t_{H},f_{H})=\mathcal{O}(t_{H},f_{H})
, where
\mathcal{SO}(t_{H},f_{H})
is the stable orbital integral defined in (
4.3
).
Hence, we have:

(5.5)

\Theta_{s}(f_{H})=\int_{T_{H}(F)_{rs}}\chi_{s}(t_{H})\cdot\mathcal{SO}(t_{H},f_{H})\,dt_{H}.

For a test function
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\rm PGSO}_{8}(F))
, we may likewise compute the twisted trace
\Theta^{\theta}_{\xi_{H}(s)}(f)
analogously, using a twisted Weyl integration formula (see
[
CG
, §5.7 and §5.8]
). This gives

(5.6)

\Theta^{\theta}_{\xi_{H}(s)}(f)=\int_{{}^{1-\theta}T(F)\backslash T(F)_{\theta-rs}}\chi_{\xi_{H}(s)}(t)\cdot\mathcal{O}^{\theta}(t,f)\,dt

where
T(F)_{\theta-rs}
denotes the open dense subset of
\theta
-regular elements and
\mathcal{O}_{\theta}(t,f)
is the
\theta
-twisted orbital integral of
f
defined
in (
4.1
).
By (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), one has

\eta:T/{}^{1-\theta}T\simeq T_{H}\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{\xi_{H}(s)}=\chi_{s}\circ\eta.

In addition, under the orbit correspondence in the theory of twisted endoscopy,
the pair
(\eta(t),t)\in T_{H}(F)_{rs}\times T(F)_{\theta_{r}s}
correspond. Moreover, the value of Whittaker-normalized twisted transfer factor on
(\eta(t),t)
is given by

\Delta(\eta(t),t)=1,

so that by the definition of transfer,

\mathcal{SO}(\eta(t),{\rm trans}_{H}(f))=\mathcal{O}^{\theta}(t,f).

Hence, by a change of variable, setting
t_{H}=\eta(t)
,

(5.7)

\Theta^{\theta}_{\xi_{H}(s)}(f)=\int_{T_{H}(F)}\chi_{s}(t_{H})\cdot\mathcal{SO}(t_{H},{\rm trans}_{H}(f))\,dt_{H}.

Comparing (
5.5
) and (
5.7
) and taking
f_{H}={\rm trans}_{H}(f)
in (
5.5
), we deduce the desired unramified spectral transfer identity.
∎

It seems to us that this unramified spectral transfer could and should be shown in the general context of twisted endoscopy; this will eliminate the need to deal with it on a case-by-case basis.

5.4.
The case
H={\rm SL}_{3}

Let us specialize further to the case of the twisted endoscopic group
H={\rm SL}_{3}
and explicate some of the constructions discussed above.
In this case, as we have noted before, the natural map of dual groups:

\xi_{H}:H^{\vee}={\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow{\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\vee}={\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})

is the unique lift of the adjoint representation

{\rm Ad}:{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow{\rm SO}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})

of
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
on its Lie algebra, from
{\rm SO}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
to
{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Let us explicate explicate the map

\xi:T_{H}^{\vee}\longrightarrow T^{\vee}

and its dual

\eta:T\longrightarrow T_{H}\subset{\rm SL}_{3}.

We shall regard
T_{H}^{\vee}
as the diagonal torus in
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
and recall from
the discussion in §
3.1
that the torus
T^{\vee}
is given by

T^{\vee}=\{\alpha_{0}(u)\cdot\alpha_{1}(t_{1})\cdot\alpha_{2}(t_{2})\cdot\alpha_{3}(t_{3}):u,t_{i}\in\mathbb{G}_{m}\}.

Then the map
\xi
is given on the diagonal maximal torus by:

\xi:\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&&\\
&b&\\
&&c\end{array}\right)\mapsto u\left(\frac{ab}{c^{2}},\frac{a}{c}\right)=\alpha_{0}\left(\frac{ab}{c^{2}}\right)\cdot\alpha_{1}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)\cdot\alpha_{2}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)\cdot\alpha_{3}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right),

where the element
s(u,t)\in(T^{\vee})^{\theta}
was defined in (
3.1
). On the other hand , we may realize
T_{H}
as the diagonal torus in
H={\rm SL}_{3}
, and identify the torus
T
with
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{4}
via:

T=\{\omega_{0}^{\vee}(u)\cdot\omega_{1}^{\vee}(t_{1})\cdot\omega_{2}^{\vee}(t_{2})\cdot\omega_{3}^{\vee}(t_{3}):u,t_{i}\in\mathbb{G}_{m}\},

where
\{\omega_{0}^{\vee},\omega_{1}^{\vee},\omega_{2}^{\vee},\omega_{3}^{\vee}\}
is the basis of
X_{*}(T)
formed by fundamental coweights, so that
\langle\alpha_{i},\omega_{j}^{\vee}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
.
Then the dual map
\eta:T\longrightarrow T_{H}
is given by

\eta:\omega_{0}^{\vee}(u)\cdot\omega_{1}^{\vee}(t_{1})\cdot\omega_{2}^{\vee}(t_{2})\cdot\omega_{3}^{\vee}(t_{3})\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}ut_{1}t_{2}t_{3}&&\\
&u&\\
&&u^{-2}t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}^{-1}t_{3}^{-1}\end{array}\right).

The kernel of
\eta
is thus

{\rm Ker}(\eta)=\{\omega_{1}^{\vee}(t_{1})\cdot\omega_{2}^{\vee}(t_{2})\cdot\omega_{3}^{\vee}(t_{3}):t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}=1\}={}^{1-\theta}T\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}^{2}.

We are not sure if these explicit formulas are especially attractive, but perhaps they provide a certain level of psychological comfort and may certainly be useful for explicit computations.

5.5.
Fibers of unramified transfer

The unramified spectral transfer defined by the twisted endoscopic character identity established in Proposition
5.4
realizes the functorial lifting of unramified L-packets associated to
\xi
. Since unramified L-packets are parametrized by semsimple conjugacy classes in the dual group, this unramified spectral transfer is thus specififed by the induced map

\xi:T_{H}^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})/W(T_{H}^{\vee})\longrightarrow T^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})/W(T^{\vee})

on Weyl-orbits on maximal (dual) tori.

The following proposition determines the nonempty fibers of the map
\xi
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Suppose that
[s]
and
[s^{\prime}]
are two semisimple conjugacy classes of
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
such that
\xi(s)=\xi(s^{\prime})
. Then one has

[s^{\prime}]=[s]
or
[s^{\prime}]=[s]^{-1}
. In particular,

\Theta_{s}\circ{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}=\Theta_{s^{\prime}}\circ{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}\Longleftrightarrow\text{ $[s^{\prime}]=[s]$ or $[s^{\prime}]=[s]^{-1}$.}

Proof.

If
\xi(s)=\xi(s^{\prime})
, then under the adjoint representation of
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
, the elements
{\rm Ad}(s)
and
{\rm Ad}(s^{\prime})
of
{\rm SO}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
have the same set of eigenvalues.
The eigenvalues of
{\rm Ad}(s)
are precisely the value
\gamma(s)
of the 6 roots
\gamma
of
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
on
s
, together with the eigenvalue 1 (with multiplicity 2). Now if we group the 6 roots in 3 pairs (by grouping
\pm\gamma
together), then the Weyl group
S_{3}
of
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
acts on the 3 pairs of roots by permutation. By applying an appropriate Weyl element, we may thus assume that for any pair of roots
\pm\gamma
,

\{\gamma(s),\gamma(s)^{-1}\}=\{\gamma(s^{\prime}),\gamma(s^{\prime})^{-1}\}.

We need to show that this forces
[s^{\prime}]=[s]
or
[s^{-1}]
.

Suppose that
\alpha
and
\beta
are the two simple roots, with
\alpha+\beta
the highest root. We consider two cases:

(a)

If

\alpha(s^{\prime})=\alpha(s)^{\epsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta(s^{\prime})=\beta(s)^{\epsilon}

for a fixed
\epsilon=\pm 1
, then we conclude that
s^{\prime}=s^{\epsilon}
, as desired.

(b)

If

\alpha(s^{\prime})=\alpha(s)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta(s^{\prime})=\beta(s)^{-1},

then we have

(\alpha+\beta)(s^{\prime})=\alpha(s)\cdot\beta(s)^{-1}.

But we also know that

(\alpha+\beta)(s^{\prime})=(\alpha+\beta)(s)\quad\text{or}\quad(\alpha+\beta)(s^{\prime})^{-1}.

This implies, for the two respective cases, that

\beta(s)=\epsilon\quad\text{or}\quad\alpha(s)=\epsilon

for some
\epsilon=\pm 1
. But then, in the respective case, we have

\beta(s^{\prime})=\beta(s)^{-1}=\beta(s)\quad\text{or}\quad\alpha(s^{\prime})=\alpha(s)=\alpha(s)^{-1}.

In any case, we are back to the scenario (a).

This proves the proposition.
∎

We can provide another perspective on the above proposition. As we noted in Proposition
4.6
(iv), the map
\xi:{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
gives rise to a homomorphism of Hecke algebras

\xi^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}.

Indeed, via the Satake isomorphism, we have

\mathcal{H}_{{\rm PGSO}_{8}}\simeq{\rm R}[{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}\simeq{\rm R}[{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})]

where
{\rm R}[G]
denotes the representation ring of finite-dimensional algebraic representations of
G
.
The map
\xi^{*}
is then given by the restriction of representations via
\xi
.
Now
{\rm R}[{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})]
is generated by the fundamental representations, namely its three 8-dimensional representations
\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}\}
and the adjoint representation
\mathfrak{so}_{8}
, whereas
{\rm R}[{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})]
is generated by the adjoint representation
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
as well as
{\rm Sym}^{3}({\rm std})
and

{\rm Sym}^{3}({\rm std}^{\vee})
, where
{\rm std}
denotes the standard 3-dimensional representation of
{\rm SL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Under restriction via
\xi
, one has

\rho_{i}\mapsto\mathfrak{sl}_{3}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{so}_{8}\mapsto\mathfrak{sl}_{3}\oplus{\rm Sym}^{3}({\rm std})\oplus{\rm Sym}^{3}({\rm std}^{\vee}).

From this , we see that the restriction map
\xi^{*}
is not surjective. Indeed, its image is contained in the subring
{\rm R}[{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})]^{+}
of self-dual representations of
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
. This is the subring fixed by the nontrivial outer automorphism of
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
. This explains why both
\chi\circ\xi^{*}=\chi^{-1}\circ\xi^{*}
for any character
\chi:\mathcal{H}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
.

6.
Cuspidal Spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{n}

Since we are interested in showing the twisted endoscopic transfer from
{\rm SL}_{3}
to
{\rm PGSO}_{8}
, it will be pertinent to recall some facts about the cuspidal automorphic spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{3}
. We shall consider the case of
{\rm SL}_{n}
below, though we shall only be interested in the case
n=3
in this paper. Our discussion in this section is informed by the papers of Labesse-Langlands
[
LL
]
, Blasius
[
B
]
, Lapid
[
L1
]
and Hiraga-Saito
[
HiS1
,
HiS2
]
.

6.1.
Local L-packets

Let
F
be a local field. The L-packets of
{\rm SL}_{n}(F)
are defined as the set of irreducible constituents of the restriction of an irreducible representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
.
This restriction is known to be semisimple and multiplicity-free
[
GK
]
and we denote the corresponding L-packet of
{\rm SL}_{n}(F)
by
\mathcal{L}[\pi]\subset{\rm Irr}({\rm SL}_{n}(F))
.
Moreover, for irreducible representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
, one has

\mathcal{L}[\pi^{\prime}]\cap\mathcal{L}[\pi]\neq\emptyset

if and only if

\pi^{\prime}\simeq\pi\otimes(\chi\circ\det)
for some character
\chi
of
F^{\times}
,

in which case one has
\mathcal{L}[\pi^{\prime}]=\mathcal{L}[\pi]\subset{\rm Irr}({\rm SL}_{n}(F))
. We shall also write
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
for the multiplicity-free semisimple representation given by the direct sum of the members of
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
.

In particular, any unramified L-packet of
{\rm SL}_{n}(F)
is of the form
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
for
\pi
an unramified representation of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
. If the Satake parameter of
\pi
is the semisimple conjugacy class
c(\pi)\in{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, then that for
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
is the image of
c(\pi)
under the natural projection map

p:{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow{\rm PGL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})={\rm SL}_{n}^{\vee}.

More generally, if
\phi_{\pi}
is the L-parameter of
\pi\in{\rm Irr}({\rm GL}_{n}(F))
, then the L-parameter of
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
under the local Langlands correspondence for
{\rm SL}_{n}(F)
is given by
p\circ\phi_{\pi}
.

6.2.
Endoscopy

The internal structure of a local L-packet
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
can be probed via the theory of endoscopy. For this purpose, let us explicate the endoscopic data for
{\rm SL}_{3}
, since this is the main case of interest for us.

Lemma 6.1
.

Up to isomorphism, the local endoscopic data for
{\rm SL}_{3}
are given by pairs
(E/F,s)
where

•

E/F
is a cyclic (Galois) cubic field extension;

•

s\in{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})={\rm SL}_{3}^{\vee}
is one of the following two elements

(6.2)

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&&\\
&\zeta&\\
&&\zeta^{2}\end{array}\right)\quad\text{or}\quad\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&&\\
&\zeta^{2}&\\
&&\zeta\end{array}\right).

For a given
(E/F,s)
, the associated endoscopic group is the norm-one torus

T_{E}:={\rm Ker}(N_{E/k}:{\rm Res}_{E/k}(\mathbb{G}_{m})\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m})

and the associated embedding

\xi_{E/F,s}:{{}^{L}}T_{E}\simeq T_{E}^{\vee}\rtimes{\rm Gal}(E/F)\longrightarrow{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})

can be described as follows.

In particular, for each cyclic cubic extension
E/F
, the norm-one torus
T_{E}
occurs as an endoscopic group two times; in other words, there are two transfer maps

{\rm trans}_{T_{E},s}:\mathcal{I}({\rm SL}_{3})\longrightarrow\mathcal{SI}(T_{E})=\mathcal{I}(T_{E})

corresponding to the two choices for
s
.

6.3.
Endoscopic local L-packets

For general
n
, it has been shown by Henniart-Herb
[
HH
]
, Hiraga-Saito
[
HiS1
,
HiS2
]
and Hiraga-Ichino
[
HI
]
that
the L-packets of
{\rm SL}_{n}(F)
, defined in the above manner, satisfies the expected endoscopic character identities, as dictated by the theory of endoscopy.
Hence, the span of the Harish-Chandra characters of the members of
\mathcal{L}[\pi]

contains a 1-dimensional subspace of stable distribution, given by the sum of all the characters of all members of
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
and hence is simply given by the Harish-Chandra character
\Theta_{\pi}
of
\pi
. We shall refer to
\Theta_{\pi}
as
the stable trace of the L-packet
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
and denote it by
S\Theta_{[\pi]}
.

Let us illustrate with the case of endoscopic local L-packets of
{\rm SL}_{3}
.
These endoscopic local L-packets are those whose L-parameter factors through some
\xi_{E/F,s}
.
They are precisely the
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
such that
\pi\otimes\omega_{E/F}\simeq\pi
for some cyclic cubic extension
E/F
with associated cubic character
\omega_{E/F}^{\pm 1}
. In particular, the L-parameter of
\pi
is of the form
{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{F}}\chi
for some character
\chi
of
E^{\times}
, in which case we will write
\pi=\pi_{\chi}
.

Let us assume that
\chi
is not fixed by
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
, so that
\pi_{\chi}
is supercuspidal. For a generic choice of
\chi
,
\mathcal{L}[\pi_{\chi}]
is the sum of 3 irreducible representations, say
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{3}
.
Then the local endoscopic character identities expresses
{\rm trans}_{T_{E},s}(\chi)
(for the two choices of
s
) as a linear combination of the Harish-Chandra characters of the
\pi_{i}
’s. More precisely, one has

\begin{cases}{\rm trans}_{T_{E},s}(\chi)=\Theta_{\pi_{1}}+\zeta\cdot\Theta_{\pi_{2}}+\zeta^{2}\cdot\Theta_{\pi_{3}};\\
{\rm trans}_{T_{E},s^{-1}}(\chi)=\Theta_{\pi_{1}}+\zeta^{2}\cdot\Theta_{\pi_{2}}+\zeta\cdot\Theta_{\pi_{3}}.\end{cases}

These two invariant distributions, together with the stable trace
S\Theta_{[\pi_{\chi}]}
, give a basis for the 3-dimensional space of invariant distributions spanned by the characters of the
\pi_{i}
’s.

6.4.
Cuspidal spectrum

Now let
k
be a number field. If
\pi\simeq\bigotimes^{\prime}_{v}\pi_{v}\subset\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{n})
is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{n}
,
the abstract restriction of
\pi
to
{\rm SL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
gives rise to a multiplicity-free semisimple representation of
{\rm SL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
, all of whose irreducible summands are nearly equivalent with each other. This is a global L-packet of
{\rm SL}_{n}
and we shall denote this by
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
, noting that

\mathcal{L}[\pi]\simeq\bigotimes^{\prime}_{v}\mathcal{L}[\pi_{v}].

On the other hand, one has the restriction of automorphic forms

{\rm rest}:\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{n}).

Then the image
L[\pi]:={\rm rest}(\pi)
is a submodule of both
\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{n})
and
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
as an
{\rm SL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
-module.

As the restriction map
{\rm rest}
is surjective, one can hope to understand the cuspidal spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{n}
in terms of that of
{\rm GL}_{n}
. For this purpose, following Lapid
[
L1
]
, we consider the following three equivalence relations on cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}
:

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}
.

(a) Write
\pi{\thicksim}_{s}\pi^{\prime}
if and only if

\pi^{\prime}\simeq\pi\otimes(\chi\circ\det)
for some character
\chi
of
F^{\times}\backslash{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
,

in which case one has

\pi^{\prime}=\pi\otimes(\chi\circ\det)\subset\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm GL}_{n}).

We shall denote the equivalence class of
\pi
under this equivalence relation by
[\pi]_{s}
.

(b) Write
\pi{\thicksim}_{ew}\pi^{\prime}
if and only if

\pi^{\prime}\simeq\pi\otimes(\chi\circ\det)
for some character
\chi
of
{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
.

We shall denote the equivalence class of
\pi
under this equivalence relation by
[\pi]_{ew}
.

(c) Write
\pi{\thicksim}_{w}\pi^{\prime}
if and only if

\pi^{\prime}
is nearly equivalent to
\pi\otimes(\chi\circ\det)
for some character
\chi
of
{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
.

In other words,

\pi^{\prime}_{v}\simeq\pi_{v}\otimes(\chi_{v}\circ\det)
for some character
\chi_{v}
outside a finite set
S
of places of
k
.

We shall denote the equivalence class of
\pi
under this equivalence relation by
[\pi]_{w}
.

Clearly, the equivalence relation gets weaker as one goes down the list. When
n=2
, it is a result of Ramakrishnan
[
Ra1
]
that the three equivalence relations above are in fact the same.
For
n\geq 3
, Blasius
[
B
]
has shown that they are distinct from each other. The relevance of the three equivalence relations to the cuspidal spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{n}
is addressed by the following proposition
[
L1
,
HiS1
,
HiS2
]
:

Proposition 6.4
.

Suppose that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}
. Then we have:

(i)
\pi{\thicksim}_{s}\pi^{\prime}
if and only if

0\neq L[\pi^{\prime}]\cap L[\pi]\subset\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{n}),

in which case one has
L[\pi^{\prime}]=L[\pi]
.

(ii)
\pi{\thicksim}_{ew}\pi^{\prime}
if and only if

L[\pi^{\prime}]\simeq L[\pi]
as abstract
{\rm SL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
-modules.

(iii)
\pi{\thicksim}_{w}\pi^{\prime}
if and only if

L[\pi]
and
L[\pi^{\prime}]
have nearly equivalent irreducible summands.

Observe that when
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
satisfies (iii) but not (ii), then
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
and
\mathcal{L}[\pi^{\prime}]
have no isomorphic irreducible summands, because their local components at some places belong to different local L-packets, and hence are inequivalent.
In other words, the so-called strong multiplicity one property (or more appropriately, rigidity) does not hold for
{\rm SL}_{n}
, at the level of L-packets.

As a consequence of the above proposition, one obtains the following decomposition of the cuspidal spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{n}
:

\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{n})=\bigoplus_{[\pi]_{s}}L[\pi]=\bigoplus_{[\pi]_{ew}}\left(\bigoplus_{[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}{\thicksim}_{ew}[\pi]_{s}}L[\pi^{\prime}]\right)\simeq\bigoplus_{[\pi]_{ew}}m_{\mathcal{L}[\pi]}L[\pi].

Here, the first sum runs over the
{\thicksim}_{s}
-equivalence classes
[\pi]_{s}
of cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}
, whereas the subsequent sums run over the
{\thicksim}_{ew}
-equivalence classes
[\pi]_{ew}
. Moreover,
m_{\mathcal{L}[\pi]}
is called the
multiplicity
of the L-packet
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
and is equal to the number of
{\thicksim}_{s}
-equivalence classes in the
{\thicksim}_{ew}
-equivalence class of
\pi
. In particular,
m_{\mathcal{L}[\pi]}
gives the multiplicity of the irreducible summands of
L[\pi]
in the cuspidal spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{n}
.

We may also write

\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{n})=\bigoplus_{[\pi]_{w}}\left(\bigoplus_{[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}{\thicksim}_{w}[\pi]_{s}}L[\pi^{\prime}]\right).

where the outer sum runs over the
{\thicksim}_{w}
-equivalence classes of cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}
and the inner sum runs over the
{\thicksim}_{s}
-equivalence classes in the
{\thicksim}_{w}
-equivalence class of
\pi
. This is a decomposition of the cuspidal spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{n}
into near equivalence classes of representations of
{\rm SL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
. Following Lapid, we define the
global multiplicity
of
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
by:

(6.5)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}[\pi]}=\sum_{[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}{\thicksim}_{w}[\pi]_{s}}m_{\mathcal{L}[\pi^{\prime}]}.

6.5.
Stable trace formula

Let us now specialize to the case
n=3
.
One can enhance the above understanding of
\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{3})
by considering the stable trace formula of
{\rm SL}_{3}
. We give a very brief impressionistic sketch of this.

Analogous to the local case, the elliptic endoscopic data of
{\rm SL}_{3}
are indexed by pairs
(E/k,s)
where
E/k
is a cyclic cubic extension and
s
is one of the two elements of
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
given in (
6.2
).
For a given pair
(E/k,s)
, the corresponding elliptic endoscopic group of
{\rm SL}_{3}
is the anisotropic norm-one torus

T_{E}:={\rm Ker}(N_{E/k}:{\rm Res}_{E/k}(\mathbb{G}_{m})\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}).

Then the stable trace formula of
{\rm SL}_{3}
takes the form

I^{{\rm SL}_{3}}_{disc}(f)=S_{disc}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}(f)+\frac{1}{9}\cdot\sum_{(E/k,s)}I_{disc}^{T_{E}}(f_{T_{E},s})

for test functions
f
on
{\rm SL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}})
with transfer
f_{T_{E},s}
on
T_{E}({\mathbb{A}})
relative to
(E/k,s)
. Here,
I_{disc}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}
and
I_{disc}^{T_{E}}
refers to the discrete part of the invariant trace formula for
{\rm SL}_{3}
and
T_{E}
respectively. On the other hand,
S_{disc}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}
is a stable distribution on
{\rm SL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}})
.

Suppose that
c=\{c_{v}\}_{v\notin S}\subset{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
is a Hecke-Satake family, i.e. a collection of semisimple conjugacy classes in
{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
for all
v
outside a finite set
S
of places of
k
. Then
c
determines a character

\chi_{c}:\mathcal{H}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}^{S}:=\bigotimes_{v\notin S}\mathcal{H}_{{\rm SL}_{3}(k_{v})}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

of the spherical Hecke algebras outside
S
and hence a near equivalence class of representations of
{\rm SL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}})
.
Considering the
c
-isotypic part of the stable trace formula, i.e. where the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}^{S}
acts by the character
\chi_{c}
, we obtain the more refined identity:

I_{disc,c}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}(f_{S})=S_{disc,c}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}(f_{S})+\frac{1}{9}\cdot\sum_{(E/k,s)}I_{disc,c}^{T_{E}}({\rm trans}_{E/k,s}(f_{S})),\quad\text{for $f_{S}\in\bigotimes_{v\in S}C^{\infty}_{c}({\rm SL}_{3}(k_{v}))$,}

where
I_{disc,c}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}
and
S_{disc,c}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}
denote the
c
-isotypic part of the relevant distributions, i.e. where the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}^{S}
acts by the character
\chi_{c}
.

6.6.
Endoscopic L-packets

A cuspidal global L-packet
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
of
{\rm SL}_{3}
is endoscopic if its Hecke-Satake family
c(\pi)
satisfies

I_{disc,c}^{T_{E}}\circ{\rm trans}_{E/k,s}\neq 0\quad\text{ for some $(E/k,s)$.}

Such an L-packet is thus the endoscopic transfer of a nontrivial automorphic character of
T_{E}
.

To describe these endoscopic L-packets more explicitly, let us start with a nontrivial automorphic character

\chi
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}
and consider the cuspidal representation
\pi_{\chi}
of
{\rm GL}_{3}
obtained by automorphic induction from
\chi
(see §
8.2
below for a more detailed discussion of automorphic induction). The cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{3}
which are obtained by automorphic induction from a cyclic cubic extension are characterized as those which are invariant under twisting by an automorphic (nontrivial) cubic character (corresponding to the cyclic cubic extension in question).
Then the endoscopic L-packet of
{\rm SL}_{3}
determined by
\chi|_{T_{E}}
is simply
\mathcal{L}[\pi_{\chi}]
, so that its contribution to the cuspidal spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{3}
is the submodule
L[\pi_{\chi}]
.

Observe that the property of a global L-packet
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
being endoscopic depends only on the
{\thicksim}_{w}
-equivalence class of the cuspidal representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{3}
. Indeed, if
\pi^{\prime}{\thicksim}_{w}\pi
,
then there is an abstract character
\mu
of
{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
such that
\pi^{\prime}
and
\pi\otimes\mu
are nearly equivalent. Hence, if
\pi
is invariant under twisting by a nontrivial cubic automorphic character
\nu
, then
\pi^{\prime}\otimes\nu
and
\pi^{\prime}
are nearly equivalent.
By the strong multiplicity or rigidity property, we must have
\pi^{\prime}\otimes\nu\simeq\pi^{\prime}
, so that
\pi^{\prime}
is obtained as an automorphic induction from the same cubic field extension as
\pi
.

6.7.
Non-endoscopic L-packets

On the other hand, suppose that
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
is
not
endoscopic, i.e.

I_{disc,c(\pi)}^{T_{E}}\circ{\rm trans}_{E/k,s}\equiv 0\quad\text{for any $(E/k,s)$.}

In this case, one has

S_{disc,c(\pi)}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}=I_{disc,c(\pi)}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}=\bigoplus_{[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}{\thicksim}_{w}[\pi]_{s}}L[\pi^{\prime}].

Since this distribution is stable, we deduce that

L[\pi^{\prime}]\simeq\mathcal{L}[\pi^{\prime}]\quad\text{for each $[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}{\thicksim}_{w}[\pi]_{s}$,}

so that each member of the global L-packet
\mathcal{L}[\pi^{\prime}]
is cuspidal automoprhic.
Hence we deduce that

(6.6)

S_{disc,c(\pi)}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}=\bigoplus_{[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}{\thicksim}_{w}[\pi]_{s}}S\Theta_{[\pi^{\prime}]},

where

S\Theta_{[\pi^{\prime}]}=\bigotimes_{v}S\Theta_{[\pi^{\prime}_{v}]}=\Theta_{\pi^{\prime}}|_{{\rm SL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}})}\quad\text{ if $\pi^{\prime}\simeq\otimes^{\prime}_{v}\pi^{\prime}_{v}$.}

6.8.
Stable multiplicity formula

By the stable trace formula recounted above, the local endoscopic character identities mentioned in §
6.3
and the description of the cuspidal spectrum of
{\rm SL}_{3}
given in §
6.4
, one can obtain
the same formula (
6.6
) for the endoscopic L-packets. As a consequence, one has
an explicit spectral decomposition of the cuspidal part of
S_{disc}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}
, namely

(6.7)

S_{cusp}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}=\bigoplus_{[\pi]_{s}}S\Theta_{[\pi]},

where the sum runs over the
{\thicksim}_{s}
-equivalence classes
[\pi]_{s}
of cuspidal automorphic representations
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{3}
.
This is sometimes called the
stable multiplicity formula
for
{\rm SL}_{3}
.

7.
Adjoint lifting of
{\rm GL}_{3}

In this section, we exploit the stable twisted trace formula for the twisted space
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
to show the adjoint lifting from
{\rm GL}_{3}
to
{\rm GL}_{8}
.
Recall from the introduction that the adjoint representation
{\rm Ad}:{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow{\rm GL}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
factors as:

\begin{CD}{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})@>{}>{}>{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})@>{\tilde{{\rm Ad}}}>{}>{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})@>{}>{}>{\rm SO}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})@>{\rm std}>{}>{\rm GL}_{8}({\mathbb{C}}),\end{CD}

The functoriality relative to all other arrows being known, our purpose in this section is to establish the functorial lifting with respect to
\tilde{\rm Ad}
.

7.1.
Global K-forms of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}

As we noted in §
4.1
, a slight complication with the theory of stable (twisted) trace formula is the need to take into account of K-forms
[
MW1
, §I.1.11]
, which are certain inner forms of the twisted space in question.
In the context of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
, we have seen there that the K-forms correspond to isomorphism classes of octonion algebras over local or number fields.
Thus, over a non-Archimedean local field or
{\mathbb{C}}
,
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
has no additional
K
-forms, since there is a unique octonion algebra up to isomorphism. On the other hand, over
{\mathbb{R}}
, there are two octonions algebras: the split one and a division algebra. As such, there are two K-forms of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
over
{\mathbb{R}}
. For a number field
k
,
with
r
real places, the number of such octonion algebras is
2^{r}
, so that there are
2^{r}
global K-forms over
k
.

7.2.
Stable twisted trace formula.

The stable twisted trace formula for
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
takes the form
[
MW2
, Thm. X.8.1, Pg 1241]

(7.1)

\sum_{\mathbb{O}}I^{\mathbb{O},\theta}_{disc}(f_{\mathbb{O}})=S^{{\rm G}_{2}}_{disc}(f_{{\rm G}_{2}})+\frac{1}{2}S_{disc}^{{\rm SO}_{4}}(f_{{\rm SO}_{4}})+S_{disc}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}(f_{{\rm SL}_{3}}),

where
\{f_{\mathbb{O}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\rm PGSO}^{\mathbb{O}}_{8}({\mathbb{A}}))\}
is a family of test functions on the K-forms and
f_{{\rm G}_{2}}
,
f_{{\rm SO}_{4}}
and
f_{{\rm SL}_{3}}
are its twisted endoscopic transfer to the twisted endoscopic groups
{\rm G}_{2}
,
{\rm SO}_{4}
and
{\rm SL}_{3}
respectively. For each twisted endoscopic group
H
, the stable distribution
S^{H}_{disc}
is the discrete part of the stable trace formula for
H
.

Let us explicate the distributions
I^{\mathbb{O},\theta}_{disc}
, following
[
LW
, §14.3]
and
[
MW2
, §X.5.1]
.
Writing
G={\rm PGSO}^{\mathbb{O}}_{8}
, one has the expression (see
[
LW
, Thm. 14.3.1 and Prop. 14.3.2]
and
[
A
, Pg. 125-128]
).

I^{\mathbb{O},\theta}_{disc}(f)=\sum_{M}\frac{1}{|W(G,M)|}I^{\mathbb{O},\theta}_{disc,M}(f)\quad\text{ for $f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G({\mathbb{A}}))$, }

where the sum runs over the conjugacy classes of Levi subgroups
M
of
G
and
[
MW2
, §X.5.1]

I^{\mathbb{O},\theta}_{disc,M}(f)=\sum_{s\in W(G\cdot\theta,M)_{reg}}|\det(1-s|\mathfrak{a}_{M})|^{-1}\cdot{\rm Tr}(M_{P|s(P)}(0)\cdot\rho_{P,s,disc}(f)).

Here:

-

P=M\cdot U
is a standard parabolic subgroup with Levi subgroup
M
and unipotent radical
U
;

-

W(G\cdot\theta,M)
is the Weyl set of elements of
G(F)\cdot\theta
which normalizes
M
(taken modulo
M(F)
), and
W(G\cdot\theta,M)_{reg}
is the subset of regular elements, i.e. those
s
such that
\det(1-s|\mathfrak{a}_{M})\neq 0
.

•

we regard elements
s\in G(F)\cdot\theta
as outer automorphisms of
G
;

-

\rho_{P,s,disc}
refers to the representation of the twisted space
G({\mathbb{A}})\cdot\theta
on

\mathcal{A}_{disc}(U({\mathbb{A}})M(k)Z(M)(k_{\infty})^{0}\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}))

with the action of
\gamma\cdot\theta\in G({\mathbb{A}})\cdot\theta
given as the composite:

\begin{CD}\mathcal{A}_{disc}(U({\mathbb{A}})M(k)Z(M)(k_{\infty})^{0}\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}))\\
@V{}V{r_{s}(\gamma\cdot\theta)}V\\
\mathcal{A}_{disc}(s(U({\mathbb{A}}))M(k)Z(M)(k_{\infty})^{0}\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}))\\
@V{}V{M_{P,s(P)}(0)}V\\
\mathcal{A}_{disc}(U({\mathbb{A}})M(k)Z(M)(k_{\infty})^{0}\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}))\end{CD}

where

(r_{s}(\gamma\cdot\theta)\phi)(g)=\phi(s^{-1}(g\gamma)).

and
M_{P,s\cdot P}
is a standard intertwining operator.

•

for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G({\mathbb{A}}))
, one has

\rho_{P,s,disc}(f)=\int_{G({\mathbb{A}})}f(\gamma)\cdot\rho_{P,s,disc}(\gamma\cdot\theta)\,d\gamma.

In particular, when
M=G
, one has
s=\theta
and

\rho_{G,\theta,disc}(\gamma\cdot\theta)\phi(g)=\phi(\theta^{-1}(g\gamma)),\quad\text{ for $\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{disc}(G)$.}

Thus,

I^{\mathbb{O},\theta}_{disc,G}(f)={\rm Tr}(f\cdot\theta|\mathcal{A}_{disc}(G)),

where the action of
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G({\mathbb{A}}))
on
\mathcal{A}_{disc}(G))
is the usual one and that of

\theta
is given by

\theta(\phi)(g)=\phi(\theta^{-1}(g)).

On the other hand, for
M\neq G
, the representations of
G({\mathbb{A}})
which intervene in
I^{\mathbb{O},\theta}_{disc,M}
are the
\theta
-stable representations of the form

{\rm Ind}_{P({\mathbb{A}})}^{G({\mathbb{A}})}\sigma,

where
\sigma\subset\mathcal{A}_{disc,\chi}(M)
is a
s
-stable submodule with unitary central character
\chi
, for some
s\in W(G\cdot\theta,M)_{reg}
.

7.3.
Adjoint lifting.

Using the stable twisted trace formula recalled above, we can now show the following theorem:

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
such that
\pi_{v_{0}}
is a discrete series representation for some place
v_{0}
. Then the (weak) adjoint lifting of
\pi
exists as an automorphic representation
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
of
{\rm GL}_{8}
. Moreover, if
\pi_{v}
is unramified with Satake parameter
c(\pi_{v})\in{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
, then
{\rm Ad}(\pi)_{v}
is unramified with Satake parameter
{\rm Ad}(c(\pi_{v}))\in{\rm GL}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.

To use the stable twisted trace formula (
7.1
), we need to specify the test function
f_{\mathbb{O}}=\prod_{v}f_{\mathbb{O},v}
used there, for each octonion
k
-algebra
\mathbb{O}
. We do this as follows:

•

At a real place
v
, we let the test functions
f_{\mathbb{O},v}
be arbitrary (so that they may depend on
\mathbb{O}
).

•

If
v
is a non-Archimedean distinct from
v_{0}
or a complex place of
k
, we will choose
f_{\mathbb{O},v}=f_{v}
to be independent of
\mathbb{O}
but otherwise arbitrary.

•

At the place
v_{0}
, we take a a pseudo-coefficient
f_{\pi_{v_{0}}}
of
\pi_{v_{0}}
and consider its stable orbital integral which is a nonzero element of

\mathcal{SI}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{3})
. By Proposition
4.6
(i), one then pick
f_{v_{0}}\in\mathcal{I}^{\theta}_{cusp}({\rm PGSO}_{8})
such that

(7.3)

{\rm trans}([f_{v_{0}}])=(0,0,[f_{\pi_{v_{0}}}]).

We then take
f_{\mathbb{O},v_{0}}=f_{v_{0}}
for any
\mathbb{O}
.

With such a family of test functions {
f_{\mathbb{O}}\}
, (
7.1
) simplifies to:

(7.4)

\ \sum_{\mathbb{O}}I^{\mathbb{O},\theta}_{disc}(f_{\mathbb{O}})=S_{disc}^{{\rm SL}_{3}}(f_{{\rm SL}_{3}}),

where

f_{{\rm SL}_{3}}=\sum_{\mathbb{O}}{\rm trans}^{\mathbb{O}}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}(f_{\mathbb{O}})=\left(\prod_{\text{real $v$}}\left(\sum_{\mathbb{O}_{v}}{\rm trans}^{\mathbb{O}_{v}}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}(f_{\mathbb{O},v})\right)\right)\cdot\prod_{\text{other $v$}}{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}(f_{v}),

with the sum over
\mathbb{O}_{v}
running over the two octonion algebras at a real place
v
.

Now we may use the action of the spherical Hecke algebras of
G={\rm PGSO}_{8}
at almost all places of
k
to isolate a given Hecke-Satake family on the LHS of (
7.4
).
More precisely, with our given cuspidal representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{3}
with associated submodule
L[\pi]\subset\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{3})
, let
S
be the finite set of places (containing all Archimedean places and the place
v_{0}
) such that
\pi_{v}
is unramified if and only if
v\notin S
. Considering the Hecke-Satake family

c([\pi])=\{c([\pi_{v}]):v\notin S\}\subset{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}}),

let us set

c:=\{\tilde{\rm Ad}(c([\pi_{v}])):v\notin S\}\subset{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})

where
\tilde{\rm Ad}:{\rm PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})\hookrightarrow{\rm Spin}_{8}({\mathbb{C}})
is the adjoint map.
At each Archimedean place
v
, we may also consider the infinitesimal character
\lambda([\pi_{v}])
of
\mathcal{L}[\pi_{v}]
, which may be regarded as a semisimple conjugacy class in the complexified dual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
, giving rise to

\lambda([\pi])=(\lambda([\pi_{v}]))_{v\in S_{\infty}}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(k\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}),

where
S_{\infty}
denotes the set of Archimedean places of
k
.
We set

\lambda:=(\tilde{\rm Ad}_{*}(\lambda([\pi_{v}])))_{v\in S_{\infty}}\in\mathfrak{so}_{8}(k\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}),

By
[
MW2
, §X.5.9]
, one has;

(7.5)

\sum_{\mathbb{O}}I_{disc,S,c,\lambda}^{\mathbb{O},\theta}(f_{\mathbb{O},S})=\sum_{[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}}S^{{\rm SL}_{3}}_{disc,[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}}(f_{{\rm SL}_{3},S})

where

f_{\mathbb{O},S}\in\left(\bigotimes_{v\in S}C^{\infty}_{c}({\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}_{v}})\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{v\notin S}1_{K_{v}}\right)

with
1_{K_{v}}
the characteristic function of
K_{v}={\rm PGSO}_{8}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
, and
the sum on the RHS ranges over all
[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}
whose restriction to
{\rm SL}_{3}
is unramified outside
S
, and such that

\tilde{\rm Ad}(c([\pi^{\prime}]))=c\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\rm Ad}_{*}(\lambda[\pi^{\prime}])=\lambda.

We shall denote the set of such
[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}
by
\Xi_{S,c,\lambda}
.

Now for each
[\pi^{\prime}]\in\Xi_{S,c,\lambda}
, it follows by Proposition
5.8
that, for each
v\notin S
, the local L-packets

\mathcal{L}[\pi^{\prime}_{v}]
and
\mathcal{L}[\pi_{v}]
are either equal or contragredient of each other; in particular,
\mathcal{L}[\pi^{\prime}_{v}]
is a generic L-packet for each
v\notin S
. Moreover, since

f_{v_{0}}
lies in
\mathcal{I}^{\theta}_{cusp}({\rm PGSO}_{8})
, any
[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}\in\Xi_{S,c,\lambda}
is necessarily contained in the cuspidal spectrum
\mathcal{A}_{cusp}({\rm SL}_{3})
.

By the stable multiplicity formula (
6.7
)) for
{\rm SL}_{3}
, (
7.5
) becomes:

\sum_{\mathbb{O}}I_{disc,S,c,\lambda}^{\mathbb{O},\theta}(f_{\mathbb{O},S})=\sum_{[\pi^{\prime}]_{s}\in\Xi_{S,c,\lambda}}{\rm Tr}\left(\pi^{\prime}_{S}(f_{{\rm SL}_{3},S})\right).

Now the RHS is the pullback by
{\rm trans}_{{\rm SL}_{3},S}
of the character distribution of a stable admissible representation of
\prod_{v\in S}{\rm SL}_{3}(k_{v})
.
By Lemma
4.9
(or rather the semi-local version of it), one deduces that the
RHS of the above identity defines a nonzero linear functional on
\otimes_{v\in S}C^{\infty}_{c}({\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}_{v}}(k_{v}))
.
This implies that the LHS is a nonzero linear functional and in particular that

\sum_{\mathbb{O}}I_{disc,S,c,\lambda}^{\mathbb{O},\theta}\neq 0.

This shows that the pair
(c,\lambda)
can be detected in the spectrum of the
I_{disc}^{\mathbb{O},\theta}
for some
\mathbb{O}
and hence can be realized by an automorphic subrepresentation
\Pi_{\mathbb{O}}
of
{\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}}
for some
\mathbb{O}
. Moreover, such a
\Pi_{\mathbb{O}}
is unramified outside of
S
.

Pulling
\Pi_{\mathbb{O}}
back to
{\rm SO}_{8}
via
f_{1}:{\rm SO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}}\rightarrow{\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}}
and
transfering of
f_{1}^{*}(\Pi_{\mathbb{O}})
to
{\rm GL}_{8}
(appealing to, for example,
[
CZ
]
or
[
CFK
]
for the case when
\mathbb{O}
is not split), one obtains
the desired weak adjoint lifting
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
of
\pi
. More precisely, by its construction,
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is unramified outside of
S
with Satake oparameter
{\rm Ad}(c(\pi_{v}))
for all
v\notin S
, and with infinitesimal character
{\rm Ad}_{*}(\lambda[\pi_{\infty}])\in\mathfrak{gl}_{8}(k\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}})
.
∎

In fact, it should not be too hard to remove the hypothesis that some local component of
\pi
is discrete series in the above theorem, using arguments in
[
A
, §3.5]
.
However, this would involve analyzing contributions from the other elliptic twisted endoscopic groups
{\mathbb{G}}_{2}
and
{\rm SO}_{4}
. Hence, we do not carry out this more elaborate analysis here. It will be contained in a forthcoming work with S.W. Shin and Y. Takanashi, whose goal is to establish the weak lifting of automorphic representations from the 3 twisted endoscopic group of
({\rm PGSO}_{8},\theta)
to
\{{\rm PGSO}_{8}^{\mathbb{O}}\}
and to obtain an endoscopic classification for the discrete automorphic representations of
G_{2}
.

8.
Automorphic Induction Case

The next few sections of the paper are devoted to the analysis of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
and in particular to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
In this section, we examine the case when the cuspidal representation
\pi
is obtained by automorphic induction from a Hecke character of a cubic field extension
E/k
. In particular, we shall see that the adjoint lifting
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
always exists (without the local condition in Theorem
7.2
. Moreover, we shall be able to determine the possible isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
.

8.1.
Cubic fields

Let
E/k
be a cubic field extension, so that
E/k
gives rise to a conjugacy class of homomorphsms
\alpha_{E}:{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\longrightarrow S_{3}
.
Composing
\alpha_{E}
with the sign character of
S_{3}
gives a quadratic character, which is trivial if and only if
E/k
is Galois (or normal). The étale quadratic
algebra determined by
({\rm sign})\circ\alpha_{E}
is the discriminant quadratic algebra
K_{E}
of
E/k
. Composing
\alpha_{E}
with the two-dimensional irreducible representation of
S_{3}
, one obtains a two-dimensional representation
\rho_{E}
of
{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
which is irreducible if and only if
E/k
is non-Galois. We note that

{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}1=1\oplus\rho_{E}.

8.2.
Automorphic induction

Given a Hecke character
\chi:E{{}^{\times}}\backslash{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, one obtains a
1
-dimensional representation (still denoted by
\chi
)

\chi:W_{E}\longrightarrow W_{E}^{ab}\simeq E^{\times}\backslash{\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

of the Weil group
W_{E}
via class field theory. By induction, this gives rise to a 3-dimensional representation

\rho_{\chi}:={\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}\chi\quad\text{of $W_{k}$.}

We say that
\pi
is obtained from
\chi
by automorphic induction if its Hecke-Stakae family
c(\pi)
is equal to the family of Frobenius classes of
\rho_{\chi}
, and express this as
\pi={\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi)
.

If
E/k
is an arbitrary Galois extension with cyclic Galois group of prime order, then the existence of
{\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi)
has been shown by Arthur-Clozel
[
AC
]
using the twisted trace formula. When
E/k
is non-Galois cubic, the existence of
{\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi)
has been shown by Jacquet-Piatetski-Shapiro-Shalika
[
JPSS
, §14]
using the converse theorem.
There is also an alternative approach by Kazhdan via exceptional theta correspondence
[
K
]
.

When
\pi={\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi)
, we shall see that the adjoint lifting of
\pi
to
{\rm GL}_{8}
more directly, without recourse to the twisted trace formula.
Indeed, from the viewpoint of Galois representations, one has:

\rho_{\chi}\otimes\rho_{\chi}^{\vee}={\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}\chi\otimes{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}\chi^{-1}={\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}\left(\left({\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}\chi\right)|_{W_{E}}\otimes\chi^{-1}\right).

We shall now consider the Galois and non-Galois case separately.

8.3.
Galois case

When
E/k
is Galois, let
\sigma
be a generator of
{\rm Gal}(E/k)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
and let
\{\omega_{E/k},\omega_{E/k}^{-1}\}
be the pair of cubic Hecke characters of
{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
associated to
E/k
by class field theory. Then

\left({\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}\chi\right)|_{W_{E}}\simeq\chi+\chi^{\sigma}+\chi^{\sigma^{2}}

so that

\rho_{\chi}\otimes\rho_{\chi}^{\vee}\simeq{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}1\oplus{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}(\chi^{\sigma}/\chi)\oplus{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}(\chi^{\sigma^{2}}/\chi).

Hence,

{\rm Ad}(\rho_{\chi})\simeq\omega_{E/k}\oplus\omega_{E/k}^{-1}\oplus{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}(\chi^{\sigma}/\chi)\oplus{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}(\chi^{\sigma^{2}}/\chi).

Now observe that each of the summands above has an automorphic analog and hence we may construct the (weak) adjoint lifting of
\pi
as an isobaric sum:

(8.1)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)=\omega_{E/k}\boxplus\omega_{E/k}^{-1}\boxplus{\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi^{\sigma}/\chi)\boxplus{\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi^{\sigma^{2}}/\chi).

Thus, we see that in this case, the adjoint lifting of
\pi
is non-cuspidal, as it contains (at least) two
{\rm GL}_{1}
-summands. Indeed, we have the following result:

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\pi
is an automorphic induction from some Galois cubic extension;

(ii)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)
exists as an automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{8}
and contains a
{\rm GL}_{1}
-summand in its isobaric sum decomposition.

(iii)

\pi
is invariant under twisting by some nontrivial Hecke character (necessarily cubic).

More precisely, when the above conditions hold, so that
\pi={\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi)
for some Galois cubic extension
E/k
with associated pair of cubic characters

\{\omega_{E/k},\omega_{E/k}^{-1}\}
, then
\pi
is invariant under twisting by
\omega_{E/k}^{\pm 1}
and
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
contains
\omega_{E/k}^{\pm 1}
as
{\rm GL}_{1}
-summands.
Further, we have the following two possibilities for the isobaric sum decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
.

(a)

If
\chi^{\sigma}\cdot\chi^{\sigma^{2}}\neq\chi^{2}
, then the characters
\chi^{\sigma}/\chi
and
\chi^{\sigma^{2}}/\chi
are non-invariant under
{\rm Gal}(E/k)
.
In this case,

{\rm Ad}(\pi)=\omega_{E/k}\boxplus\omega_{E/k}^{-1}\boxplus{\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi^{\sigma}/\chi)\boxplus{\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi^{\sigma^{2}}/\chi)

with the last two summands cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{3}
(which are dual of each other).
The two
{\rm GL}_{3}
-summands are isomorphic to each other if and only if
(\chi^{2})^{\sigma}=\chi^{2}
.
In particular,
\pi
is an automorphic induction from precisely one Galois cubic field (namely
E/k
).

(b)

If
\chi^{\sigma}\cdot\chi^{\sigma^{2}}=\chi^{2}
, then the characters
\chi^{\sigma}/\chi
and
\chi^{\sigma^{2}}/\chi
are invariant under
{\rm Gal}(E/k)
. If
\chi^{\sigma}/\chi=\mu\circ N_{E/k}
for a Hecke character
\mu
over
k
, then

{\rm Ad}(\pi)=\omega_{E/k}\boxplus\omega_{E/k}^{-1}\boxplus\mu\boxplus\mu\omega_{E/k}\boxplus\mu\omega_{E/k}^{-1}\boxplus\mu^{-1}\boxplus\mu^{-1}\omega_{E/k}\boxplus\mu^{-1}\omega_{E/k}^{-1}.

In particular,
\mu
is a cubic character and
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is an isobaric sum of
8
distinct cubic Hecke characters. Hence,
\pi
can be expressed as an automorphic induction from four distinct Galois cubic field extensions of
k
.

In particular, the self-dual automorphic representation
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is the transfer of an automorphic but non-cuspidal representation of
{\rm SO}_{8}
.

Remark
:

(i) If
\pi
can be obtained from two Galois cubic field extensions
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, then
\pi
is invariant under twisting by
\omega_{E_{1}/k}
and
\omega_{E_{2}/k}
. Hence
\pi
is also invariant under twisting by
\omega_{E_{1}/k}\cdot\omega_{E_{2}/k}
and
\omega_{E_{1}/k}\cdot\omega_{E_{2}/k}^{-1}
. The latter two cubic characters correspond to two other Galois cubic extensions
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
. The four cubic extensions
E_{i}
are precisely the four cubic subfields contained in the bi-cyclic-cubic (
={\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
) extension which is the composition of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. Hence, if
\pi
is an automorphic induction from more than one Galois cubic extension, it is automatically an automorphic induction from four Galois cubic extensions. Part (b) of the theorem implies that
\pi
cannot be an automorphic induction from more than four Galois cubic extensions.

(ii) The condition on
\chi
in (b) of the Theorem is the same condition in
[
L1
, §6.1, Prop. 2 (1)]
, which implies that the global multiplicity of the L-packet
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
of
{\rm SL}_{3}
is
2
. Under the condition in (a), the global multiplicity of
\mathcal{L}[\pi]
is
1
.

Proof.

Let us first show the equivalence of (i)-(iii):

•

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii): this has been shown by our discussion before the statement of the theorem.

•

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii): Suppose
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
contains
\mu
as a
{\rm GL}_{1}
-summand. Then the partial L-function
L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi)\times\mu^{-1})
has a pole at
s=1
. But one has an identity of partial L-functions:

L^{S}(s,\mu^{-1})\cdot L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi)\times\mu^{-1})=L^{S}(s,\pi\times(\pi\otimes\mu)^{\vee}).

Hence the partial L-function on the RHS also has a pole at
s=1
. By Jacquet-Shalika, this implies that
\pi\simeq\pi\otimes\mu
, as desired.
Further,
\mu
is necessarily cubic, since
\omega_{\pi}=\omega_{\pi\otimes\mu}=\omega_{\pi}\cdot\mu^{3}
, where
\omega_{\pi}
denotes the central character of
\pi
.

•

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i): this is due to Arthur-Clozel
[
AC
]

This proves the equivalence of (i), (ii) and (iii). Moreover, when these conditions hold, we see by (
8.1
) that if
\pi={\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi)
, then
\omega_{E/k}^{\pm 1}
are
{\rm GL}_{1}
-summands of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
. Indeed, (
8.1
) gives the more precise description in (a) and (b) immediately.

∎

8.4.
Non-Galois case

We now consider the non-Galois case.
If
E/k
is a non-Galois cubic extension, with discriminant quadratic field extension
K=K_{E}
, then
L=E\cdot K_{E}
is the Galois closure of
k
in
\overline{k}
. Hence,
L/k
is an
S_{3}
-extension, and the cubic extension
L/K
is Galois. We let
\sigma
be a generator of
{\rm Gal}(L/K)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
and note that
L
contains two other cubic subfields besides
E
, namely

E_{\sigma}=\{\sigma(e):e\in E\}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{\sigma^{2}}=\{\sigma^{2}(e):e\in E\}.

In this case, if
\chi
is a Hecke character of
E
, then

\left({\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}\chi\right)|_{W_{E}}\simeq\chi\oplus{\rm Ind}_{W_{L}}^{W_{E}}(\chi|_{W_{L}}^{\sigma}).

Here, note that
\chi|_{W_{L}}^{\sigma}
is defined by
\chi^{\sigma}(w)=\chi(\sigma^{-1}w\sigma)
for
w\in W_{L}
. To simplify notation, we shall omit
|_{W_{L}}
in what follows and regard the restriction to
W_{L}
as implicit.
Hence

\rho_{\chi}\otimes\rho_{\chi}^{\vee}\simeq{\rm Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{k}}1\oplus{\rm Ind}_{W_{L}}^{W_{k}}(\chi^{\sigma}/\chi),

so that

{\rm Ad}(\rho_{\chi})\simeq\rho_{E}\oplus{\rm Ind}_{W_{L}}^{W_{k}}(\chi^{\sigma}/\chi).

Noting that
\rho_{E}\simeq{\rm Ind}_{W_{K}}^{W_{k}}\omega_{L/K}
and

{\rm Ind}_{W_{L}}^{W_{k}}={\rm Ind}_{W_{K}}^{W_{k}}\circ{\rm Ind}_{W_{L}}^{W_{K}}

is a composite of inductions through cyclic extensions of prime degrees, we see that the automorphic analog of
{\rm Ad}(\rho_{\chi})
can be constructed.

More precisely, if
\pi={\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi)
, then

(8.3)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)={\rm AI}_{K/k}(\omega_{L/K})\boxplus{\rm AI}_{L/k}\left(\chi_{L}^{\sigma}/\chi_{L}\right)

as an automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{8}
, where we have written
\chi_{L}:=\chi\circ N_{L/E}
for the base change of
\chi
from
E
to
L
.
Henceforth, we will write
\rho_{E}
to denote its automorphic analog
{\rm AI}_{K/k}(\omega_{L/K})
as well.

Now one has the following analog of Theorem
8.2
:

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
\pi
be a unitary cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\pi
can be realized as an automorphic induction from a non-Galois cubic extension.

(ii)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)
exists as an automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{8}
and contains a unitary cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{2}
-summand in its isobaric decomposition.

(iii)

for some unitary cuspidal representation
\tau
of
{\rm GL}_{2}
,
\pi\boxtimes\tau\supset\pi
in its isobaric decomposition (where
\pi\boxtimes\tau
is the Rankin-Selberg lifting from
{\rm GL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{3}
to
{\rm GL}_{6}
established by Kim-Shahidi
[
KS1
]
).

More precisely, if
\pi={\rm AI}_{E/k}(\chi)
for a non-Galois cubic extension
E/k
with Galois closure
L/k
and discriminant quadratic field
K/k
, then
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
contains the cuspidal representation
\rho_{E}
of
{\rm GL}_{2}
as a summand in its isobaric decomposition. Further, we have the following possibilities for the isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
:

(a)

If

\chi_{L}^{\sigma}\cdot\chi_{L}^{\sigma^{2}}\neq\chi_{L}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad(\chi_{L}^{\sigma})^{2}\neq\chi_{L}^{2},

then
{\rm AI}_{L/k}\left(\chi_{L}^{\sigma}/\chi_{L}\right)
is a self-dual cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{6}
over
k
with central character
\omega_{K/k}
, so that

{\rm Ad}(\pi)=\rho_{E}\oplus{\rm AI}_{L/k}\left(\chi_{L}^{\sigma}/\chi_{L}\right).

(b)

If

(\chi_{L}^{\sigma})^{2}=\chi_{L}^{2},

then
{\rm AI}_{L/k}\left(\chi_{L}^{\sigma}/\chi_{L}\right)

is a self-dual cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
over
K
which is invariant under
{\rm Gal}(K/k)
, so that

{\rm AI}_{L/k}\left(\chi_{L}^{\sigma}/\chi_{L}\right)=A\oplus A\cdot\omega_{K/k}

for some self-dual cuspidal representation
A
of
{\rm GL}_{3}
with trivial central character and

{\rm Ad}(\pi)=\rho_{E}\oplus A\oplus A\cdot\omega_{K/k}.

(c)

If

\chi_{L}^{\sigma}\cdot\chi_{L}^{\sigma^{2}}=\chi_{L}^{2},

9.
Self-Dual Case

In this section, we consider the case when the cuspidal representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{3}
is the twist of a self-dual cuspidal representation.
In this case, there is no loss of generality in assuming that
\pi
is self dual and hence is the adjoint lift (
=
Gelbart-Jacquet lift) of a cuspidal representation
\tau
of
{\rm GL}_{2}

[
GJ
]
.
We shall write
\pi={\rm ad}_{2}(\tau)
.

From a Galois theoretic point of view, if
\tau
is associated with a 2-dimensional Galois representation

{\rm ad}_{2}(\tau)
is associated to the 3-dimensional Galois representation
{\rm ad}_{2}(\rho):={\rm Sym}^{2}(\rho)\otimes\det(\rho)^{-1}

{\rm Ad}({\rm ad}_{2}(\rho))\simeq{\rm ad}_{2}(\rho)\oplus{\rm ad}_{4}(\rho)

where

{\rm ad}_{4}(\rho)={\rm Sym}^{4}(\rho)\otimes\det(\rho)^{-2}.

Now the
{\rm Sym}^{4}
-lifting from
{\rm GL}_{2}
to
{\rm GL}_{5}
has been shown by H. Kim
[
Ki
]
, and hence the automorphic analog of

{\rm ad}_{4}(\tau)
. In particular, this allows us to construct the adjoint lifting of

as:

(9.1)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)=\pi\boxplus{\rm ad}_{4}(\tau)={\rm ad}_{2}(\tau)\boxplus{\rm ad}_{4}(\tau).

Now we have:

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
\pi
be a unitary cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
and assume that
\pi
is not an automorphic induction from a cubic field extension of
k
(Galois or not).
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\pi
is the twist by an automorphic character of a self-dual cuspidal representation;

(ii)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)
exists as an automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{8}
and contains a cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{3}
-summand in its isobaric decomposition;

More precisely, when the above conditions hold with
\pi
self-dual, the isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is of type
3+5
and is given by (
9.1
).
In particular,
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is the transfer of a globally generic discrete (indeed cuspidal) automorphic representation of
{\rm SO}_{8}
.

Proof.

Assuming (i), then (ii) follows by (
9.1
).
Now assume (ii) and suppose that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
contains a cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{3}
-summand
A
in its isobaric decomposition. By the hypotheses on
\pi
and Theorems
8.2
and
8.4
, we see that the isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is of type
3+5
, so that

{\rm Ad}(\pi)=A\boxplus B

with
B
a cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{5}
-summand.
Since
{\rm Ad}(\pi)={\rm Ad}(\pi)^{\vee}

and

As
3
and
5
are odd, the self-dual representations

and
B
must be of orthogonal type.

By Corollary
2.3
, we see that

(9.3)

L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi)\times{\rm Ad}(\pi))=L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi))\cdot L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi),\wedge^{3}).

Here, note that all the partial L-functions in this identity are Langlands-Shahidi L-functions. Indeed, the exterior cube L-function for
{\rm GL}_{8}
can be obtained as a Langlands-Shahidi L-function by considering the maximal parabolic subgroup
of

. Since

at

On the other hand, as

of

is not of the type considered in the previous two sections, i.e.

is neither an automorphic induction from a cubic field extension, nor a twist of a self-dual representation.
We shall make the following hypothesis:

Hypothesis

By Theorem

, the hypothesis is satisfied when

has a discrete series local component. Now one has:

Theorem 10.1
.

\pi

It is clear that if

). If it is constructed via (

), then it follows that

is of orthogonal type, since it is lifted from the split

In view of the hypotheses on

in the Theorem, as well as Theorems
8.2
,
8.4
and

, we already know that

cannot contain any cuspidal

-,

must be nontrivial and
\sigma
has nonzero theta lift to a quasi-split but non-split
{\rm GSO}_{4}
associated to
\nu
. This implies that
A
is obtained as an automorphic induction from the quadratic field determined by
\nu
and hence is invariant under twisting by
\nu
.

We have thus completed the proof of the lemma.

∎

With the lemma in hand, we can now continue with the proof of Theorem
10.1
. By the lemma, we may assume without loss of generality that
A\otimes\omega_{K/k}\simeq A
for some quadratic field extension
K/k
,
so that
A\simeq{\rm AI}_{K/k}(\tau)
for some cuspidal representation
\tau
of
{\rm GL}_{2}
over
K
.
Consider now the base change
\pi_{K}
of
\pi
to
K
, noting that
\pi_{K}
is still cuspidal. Then

{\rm Ad}(\pi_{K})={\rm Ad}(\pi)_{K}=A_{K}\boxplus B_{K}=\tau\boxplus\tau^{c}\boxplus B_{K}

where
c
is the nontrivial element in
{\rm Gal}(K/k)
and the base change
B_{K}
has isobaric decomposition of type
2+2
or
4
.

Now we may appeal to Theorem
8.4
. This says that:

•

\tau=\rho_{E}
for some non-Galois cubic extension
E/K
, whose Galois closure
\tilde{E}
is an
S_{3}
-extension of
K
with discriminant quadratic field
L/K
, so that

A_{K}=\rho_{E}\oplus\rho_{E^{c}}.

•

the bi-cyclic-cubic field extension
\tilde{E}\cdot\tilde{E}^{c}
over
L
contains two other cubic extensions
\tilde{E}_{1}
and
\tilde{E}_{2}=\tilde{E}_{1}^{c}
, which are
S_{3}
-extensions over
K
with discriminant field extension
L/K
, so that

B_{K}=\rho_{E_{1}}\boxplus\rho_{E_{2}},

where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are cubic subfields of
\tilde{E}_{1}/K
and
\tilde{E}_{2}/K
.

In partiular, we have:

A\simeq{\rm AI}_{K/k}(\rho_{E})\quad\text{and}\quad B\simeq{\rm AI}_{K/k}(\rho_{E_{1}}).

Since
\rho_{E}
and
\rho_{E_{1}}
are self-dual representations with central character
\omega_{L/K}
, we deduce that
A
and
B
are self-dual and

\omega_{A}=\omega_{B}=\omega_{L/K}|_{{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}}.

On the other hand, with
A
and
B
as above, one has
[
AR
, proofs of Prop. 3.2 and Prop. 3.5]

\wedge^{2}A={\rm As}_{K/k}^{+}(\rho_{E})\cdot\omega_{K/k}\boxplus{\rm AI}_{K/k}(\omega_{L/K})\quad\text{and}\quad\wedge^{2}B={\rm As}^{+}_{K/k}(\rho_{E_{1}})\cdot\omega_{K/k}\boxplus{\rm AI}_{K/k}(\omega_{L/K}),

so that

A\simeq{\rm As}^{+}_{K/k}(\rho_{E_{1}})\cdot\omega_{K/k}\quad\text{and}\quad B\simeq{\rm As}^{+}_{K/k}(\rho_{E})\cdot\omega_{K/k}.

From these alternate expressions of
A
and
B
, one deduces by
[
Ra2
]
that

\omega_{A}=\omega_{K/k}.

In particular, since
\omega_{A}
and
\omega_{B}
are nontrivial,
A
and
B
are necessarily of orthogonal type.
Moreover, we also conclude that
\omega_{L/K}|_{{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}}=\omega_{K/k}
, which implies that
L/k
is a cyclic degree 4 extension.
∎

Here is an interesting corollary, which is an application of our results on the possible isobaric decompositions for
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
to the Rankin-Selberg lifting from
{\rm GL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{3}
to
{\rm GL}_{6}
.

Corollary 10.5
.

Suppose that

•

\pi
is a unitary cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
which is neither an automorphic induction from a cubic field extension nor a twist of a self-dual cuspidal representation, and
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
exists on
{\rm GL}_{8}
(for example when
\pi
has a discrete series local component);

•

\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}
with the same central character.

Then

\pi\boxtimes\tau_{1}\simeq\pi\boxtimes\tau_{2}\Longrightarrow\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{2}.

Proof.

If
\pi\boxtimes\tau_{1}\simeq\pi\boxtimes\tau_{2}
, then the partial L-function

L^{S}(s,(\pi\boxtimes\tau_{1})\times(\pi\boxtimes\tau_{2})^{\vee})=L^{S}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\vee}\times\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2}^{\vee})

has a pole at
s=1
. But this L-function is equal to

L^{S}({\rm Ad}(\pi)\times(\tau_{1}\boxtimes\tau_{2}^{\vee}))\cdot L^{S}(s,\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2}^{\vee}).

Under the hypothesis on
\pi
, it follows by Theorem
10.1
that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is either cuspidal or an isobaric sum of two self-dual cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{4}
with nontrivial central character.
This implies that the L-function
L^{S}({\rm Ad}(\pi)\times(\tau_{1}\boxtimes\tau_{2}^{\vee}))
is holomorphic at
s=1
, since the self-dual representation
\tau_{1}\boxtimes\tau_{2}
has trivial central character.
Hence, the L-function
L^{S}(s,\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2}^{\vee})
must have a pole at
s=1
, which implies that
\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{2}
.
∎

10.1.
Summary

The following proposition summarizes an interesting property which should play an important role in the characterization of the image of the adjoint lifting.

Corollary 10.6
.

Assume that
\pi
is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
which is not invariant under twisting by a nontrivial cubic automorphic character.
If
\pi
is in the context of Theorem
10.1
, assume that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is constructed via the sequence of lifting (
1.1
). Then

{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is a discrete orthogonal L-parameter. Moreover,

{\rm ord}_{s=1}L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi),\wedge^{3})={\rm ord}_{s=1}L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi),{\rm Sym}^{2})<0,

so that the order of poles at
s=1
of
L^{S}(s,{\rm Ad}(\pi),\wedge^{3})
(which is an L-function of Langlands-Shahidi type) is equal to the number of summands in the isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
.

One may wonder if the above property of the exterior cube L-function characterizes those discrete orthogonal parameter which arises as the adjoint lifting from
{\rm GL}_{3}
. As the exterior cube L-function occurs as a Langlands-Shahidi L-function in the group
E_{8}
, it is conceivable that one may establish this expectation by an automorphic decent construction from
{\rm GL}_{8}
to
{\rm GL}_{3}
.

11.
Heisenberg-Weil Representations

In Theorems
8.2
,
8.4
,
9.2
and
10.1
, we have described the possible shapes of the isobaric decomposition of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
. One may ask if all these possibilities are actually attained. While it is not so surprising that the more nondegenerate cases actually happen, it may be less clear for the most degenerate cases in Theorem
8.2
(b), Theorem
8.4
(c) and Theorem
10.1
(ii). In this final section, we will give a general construction of cuspidal representations
\pi

such that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
has the form in these degenerate cases. This construction was first studied by Blasius
[
B
]
and Lapid
[
L2
, §5]
in their work on multiplicities and rigidity of cuspidal
L
-packets of
{\rm SL}_{n}
.

11.1.
Jacobi group

We shall consider the two dimensional symplectic vector space
W
over the finite field
{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
. Choosing a Witt basis for
W
, we may identify
W
with
{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
{\rm Sp}(W)

denote the finite symplectic group attached to
W
, which is isomorphic to
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}})
. Note that

{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq Q_{8}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}

where
Q_{8}
denotes the finite quaternion group (with
8
elements).
In particular,
|{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}})|=24
and
Q_{8}
is its unique
2
-Sylow subgroup. Indeed,
Q_{8}
is also the commutator subgroup of
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}})
.

One can also form the Heisenberg group

H(W)=W\oplus Z,\quad\text{with center $Z={\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}$.}

Via its natural action on
W
,
{\rm Sp}(W)
acts on
H(W)
as group automorphisms fixing the center
Z
elementwise. Hence one has the
Jacobi group

J(W)=H(W)\rtimes{\rm Sp}(W).

For our purpose, we will consider the following sequence of subgroups of
J(W)
:

(11.1)

H(W)\subset H(W)\cdot Z_{{\rm Sp}(W)}\subset H(W)\cdot C_{4}\subset H(W)\cdot Q_{8}\subset J(W),

where
Z_{{\rm Sp}(W)}\simeq\mu_{2}
is the center of
{\rm Sp}(W)
and
C_{4}
is the cyclic group of order
4
generated by the Weyl element

w_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right).

11.2.
Heisenberg-Weil representation

By the Stone-von-Neumamn theorem, for a given nontrivial central character
\chi:{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, there is a unique (up to isomorphism) irreducible representation
\omega_{\chi}
of
H(W)
.
This irreducible representation is of dimension
3
and can be constructed as follows. Let
L\subset W
be any line (i.e. one-dimensional subspace) in
W
. Then
H(L):=L\oplus Z\subset H(W)
is an abelian subgroup and we may extend
\chi
from
Z
to
H(L)

trivially on
L
. Denoting this extended character by
\chi_{L}
, one has

\omega_{\chi}={\rm Ind}_{H(L)}^{H(W)}\chi_{L}.

By the theory of Weil representations, this representation can be extended to
to
{\rm Sp}(W)
. Since
{\rm Sp}(W)={\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}})
is not perfect, the extension is of course not unique, as we may twist by cubic characters of
{\rm Sp}(W)
. However, the restriction of these extensions to the commutator subgroup
Q_{8}
are isomorphic. We will denote this extended representation of
H(W)\rtimes Q_{8}
by
\omega_{\chi}
as well, and call it the
Heisenberg-Weil representation
. The restriction of
\omega_{\chi}
to any subgroup of
H(W)\rtimes Q_{8}
containing
H(W)
is thus irreducible.

11.3.
Setting of Theorem
8.2

Let
M/k
be a Galois extension with Galois group isomorphic to the Heisenberg group
H(W)
and consider its irreducible 3-dimensional representation
\omega_{\chi}
constructed above.
The fixed field of the center
Z
of
H(W)
is thus a bi-cyclic-cubic extension
E_{Z}
of
k
.
For any line
L\subset W
, the fixed field of the abelian normal subgroup
H(L)
is a Galois cubic extension
E_{L}
of
k
. Since

\omega_{\chi}\simeq{\rm Ind}_{H(L)}^{H(W)}\chi_{L},

we see that
\omega_{\chi}
gives rise to the cuspidal representation

\pi={\rm AI}_{E_{L}/k}(\chi_{L}).

In other words,
\pi
is an automorphic induction from
E_{L}
. Since there are 4 possible lines
L\subset W
(as
|\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{3})|=4
), we see that
\pi
is an automorphic induction from the 4 cubic subfields in the bi-cyclic-cubic extension
E_{Z}
of
k
, so that we are in situation (b) of Theorem
8.2
. In particular,
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is the sum of the 8 cubic characters
\omega_{E_{L}/k}^{\pm 1}
.

11.4.
Setting of Theorem
8.4

Now suppose that
M/k
is a Galois extension with Galois group
H(W)\rtimes\mu_{2}
. Let
K
be the fixed field of
H(W)
, so that
K/k
is a quadratic extension. If
E_{Z}
is the fixed field of the center
Z
of
H(W)
, then the action of
{\rm Gal}(K/k)
on
{\rm Gal}(E_{Z}/K)=W
is the action of
\mu_{2}\subset{\rm Sp}(W)
on
W
. In other words, it acts as
-1
on
{\rm Gal}(E_{Z}/K)=W
. This shows that for each line
L\subset W
, its fixed field
E_{L}
is a cubic extension of
K
, which is Galois over
k
with
{\rm Gal}(E_{L}/k)\simeq S_{3}
.

Now the irreducible 3-dimensional representation
\omega_{\chi}
of
H(W)

gives rise to a cuspidal representation
\pi_{K}
of
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}}_{K})
as described above. Hence,
\pi_{K}
is an automorphic induction from any of the 4 cubic subfields
E_{L}
of
M/K
.
Since
\omega_{\chi}
extends to
H(W)\rtimes\mu_{2}
, the cuspidal representation
\pi_{K}
is invariant under the action of
{\rm Gal}(K/k)
; in particular, it follows from
[
AC
]
that
\pi_{K}
is the base change of a cuspidal representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{3}
over
k
. Now the cuspidal representation
\pi
is not unique, as we can twist it by the quadratic Hecke character
\omega_{K/k}
.
But there is a unique such
\pi
whose central character
\omega_{\pi}
corresponds to
\det\omega_{\chi}
under global class field theory.
By
[
L2
, §5, Prop. 2]
, this
\pi
then corresponds to
\omega_{\chi}
under the purported global Langlands correspondence for
{\rm GL}_{3}
.
Moreover, since
{\rm Ad}(\pi_{K})
is the isobaric sum of the 8 cubic characters
\omega_{E_{L}/K}^{\pm 1}
, we deduce that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is the isobaric sum of 4 cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}
. In particular, we are in the situation of Theorem
8.4
(c).

11.5.
Setting of Theorem
10.1

Finally, suppose that
M/k
is a Galois extension with Galois group
H(W)\rtimes C_{4}
and set

\begin{cases}L=\text{ fixed field of $H(W)$;}\\
K=\text{ fixed field of $H(W)\rtimes\mu_{2}$;}\end{cases}

Then
L/k
is a cyclic degree
4
extension with Galois group
C_{4}
.
By the previous subsection, the irreducible representation
\omega_{\chi}
of
H(W)\rtimes\mu_{2}
gives rise to a cuspidal representation
\pi_{K}
of
{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{A}}_{K})
such that
{\rm Ad}(\pi_{K})
is the isobaric sum of 4 cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{K})
, corresponding to the 4 lines in
W
.

Since
\omega_{\chi}
extends to
H(W)\rtimes C_{4}
, we see that
\pi_{K}
is invariant under
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
, and hence is the base change of a cuspidal representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{3}
over
k
by
[
AC
]
. As in the previous subsection, there is a unique such
\pi
if we require that its central character corresponds to
\det\omega_{\chi}
under global class field theory.
By
[
L2
, §5, Prop. 2]
again, one knows that this unique
\pi
corresponds to
\omega_{\chi}
under the purported global Langlands correspondence.

Now the action of
{\rm Gal}(L/k)=C_{4}
on
{\rm Gal}(E_{Z}/L)=W
(where
E_{Z}
is the fixed field of the center
Z\subset H(W)
) is generated by the action of the Weyl element

w_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right).

As the element
w_{0}^{2}
acts as
-1
on
W
, we see that the action of
C_{4}
has two orbits on the set of lines in
W
. This shows that
{\rm Gal}(K/k)
has two orbits on the 4 isobaric summands in
{\rm Ad}(\pi_{K})
, and hence

{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is the isobaric sum of two cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{4}
over
k
. In particular, we are in the situation of Theorem
10.1
(ii).

Not surprisingly, if we consider a Galois extension with Galois group
H(W)\rtimes Q_{8}
, a similar argument as above will lead to a cuspidal
\pi
such that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is cuspidal on
{\rm GL}_{8}
(i.e. the situation of Theorem
10.1
(i)) but the base change of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
to an appropriate quadratic extension decomposes as an isobaric sum of two cuspidal
{\rm GL}_{4}
-summands. This gives an instance of Theorem
10.1
(ii), though one might expect the general situation of Theorem
10.1
(ii) will be such that
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is not invariant under twisting by any quadratic Hecke character.

12.
Application to Ramanujan Bounds

In this final section, we give an application of the existence of the adjoint lifting to the question of bounding the Hecke eigenvalues of cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{3}
.

12.1.
Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{n}
over
k
with unitary central character. A a place
v
of
k
where
\pi_{v}
is unramified, the Satake parameter
c(\pi_{v})
is a semisimple conjugacy class in
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. For any eigenvalue
\lambda
of
c(\pi_{v})
, we may write

|\lambda|=q_{v}^{a}

where
q_{v}
is the cardinality of the residue field of
k_{v}
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture asserts that
|\lambda|=1
so that
a=0
.

12.2.
Some prior results

A general bound towards the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture was established by Luo-Rudnick-Sarnak
[
LRS
]
. They showed that

|a|\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1},

so that for the case of
{\rm GL}_{3}
, this reads:

|a|\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{10}=\frac{2}{5}.

There have certainly been improvements to this general bound when
n
is small. In particular, for the case of
{\rm GL}_{3}
, the paper
[
BB
]
of Blomer-Brumley showed that

|a|\leq\frac{5}{14},

thus improving upon the Luo-Rudnick-Sarnak bound.

12.3.
Application of adjoint lifting

As an application of the existence of the adjoint lifting
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
(as supplied by Theorem
7.2
), we shall show:

Theorem 12.1
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal representation of
{\rm GL}_{3}
with unitary central character and a discrete series local component over a number field
k
. At each place
v
where
\pi_{v}
is unramified, let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of its Satake parameter
c(\pi_{v})\in{\rm GL}_{3}({\mathbb{C}})
, and write
|\lambda|=q_{v}^{a}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

|a|\leq\frac{21}{65}<\frac{1}{3}.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.2
,
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
exists as an automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{8}
.
Moreover, by Theorem
1.3
,
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
is an isobaric sum of cuspidal representations (of
{\rm GL}_{r}
, with
r\leq 8
) with unitary central characters.

Let us fix the unramified place
v
and let
\lambda_{i}
(for
1\leq i\leq.3)
be the three eigenvalues of the Satake parameter
c(\pi_{v})
. Writing
|\lambda_{i}|=p^{a_{i}}
, the condition of unitary central character implies that

a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=0.

On the other hand, the Satake parameter of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)_{v}
has eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}
, with
|\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}|=q_{v}^{a_{i}-a_{j}}
.
Applying the Luo-Rudnick-Sarnak bound to each isobaric summand of
{\rm Ad}(\pi)
, one has

|a_{i}-a_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{65}.

We would like to exploit this system of inequalities to get the desired bound on
|a_{i}|
.

For this purpose, note that

3a_{1}=(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})+(a_{1}-a_{2})+(a_{1}-a_{3}).

The triangle inequality thus implies that

3\cdot|a_{1}|\leq|a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}|+|a_{1}-a_{2}|+|a_{1}-a_{3}|\leq 1-\frac{2}{65},

and hence

|a_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{195}=\frac{21}{65}<\frac{1}{3}.

The same argument applies to give the same bound for
|a_{2}|
and
|a_{3}|
. This proves the theorem.
∎

The following corollary of the theorem was mentioned to us by D. Prasad:

Corollary 12.2
.

In the context of Theorem
12.1
, let
|\lambda_{i}|=q_{v}^{a_{i}}
(for
1\leq i\leq 3
) be the absolute value of the three eigenvalues of the Satake parameter of
\pi_{v}
. Then

|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|+|a_{3}|\leq\frac{42}{65}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq a_{3}
.
Since
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=0
, we have one of the following two possibilities:

a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq 0\geq a_{3}\quad\text{or}\quad a_{1}\geq 0\geq a_{2}\geq a_{3}.

In the first scenario, one has:

|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|+|a_{3}|=a_{1}+a_{2}-a_{3}=-2a_{3}=2|a_{3}|

whereas in the second,

|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|+|a_{3}|=a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{3}=2a_{1}=2|a_{1}|.

The desired inequality thus follows by Theorem
12.1
.
∎

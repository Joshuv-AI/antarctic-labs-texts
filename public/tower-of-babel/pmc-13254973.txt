A TaMYB2–TaMAP3K17 module enhances drought tolerance by promoting reactive oxygen species scavenging in wheat
Authors: Shuhui Yang, Zehao Hou, Lei Zheng, Yuanchen Jiao, Wenjing Cheng, Hongxiao Li, Jun Chen, Yongbin Zhou, Xiuliang Zhu, Ming Chen, Xiang Gao, Jindong Fu, Youzhi Ma, Zhengwu Fang, Zhao-Shi Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPlant growth and production are strongly affected by water deficit. Mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinases (MAP3Ks) are essential signaling components for responses to abiotic stress in many plants. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying their roles in drought-stress tolerance in wheat (Triticum aestivum) remain unclear. In this study, we identified TaMAP3K17 as a drought-induced MAP3K gene in wheat and demonstrated that it encodes a protein localized to both the plasma membrane and nucleus. TaMAP3K17-silenced plants generated by virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS) were hypersensitive to drought stress and accumulated higher levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) compared to the wild type. TaMAP3K17 overexpression enhanced drought-stress tolerance in transgenic wheat plants. Yeast one-hybrid and dual-luciferase reporter assays showed that TaMYB2 activates the transcription of TaMAP3K17. Suppressing TaMYB2 expression by VIGS resulted in ROS generation and malondialdehyde accumulation in wheat under water-deficit conditions. These findings suggest that a TaMYB2–TaMAP3K17 regulatory module is involved in ROS homeostasis and drought responses in wheat.Keywords: MAP3K, MYB, Drought stress, Reactive oxygen species, Wheat

Introduction

Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is a widely grown cereal crops, serving as a major food source for humans worldwide [1]. Drought, one of the most damaging abiotic stresses, restricts crop growth, and severe drought stress can reduce global agricultural production by approximately 50%, with profound implications for food security [2]. Therefore, identifying key genes involved in drought responses represents an effective strategy for enhancing drought tolerance in wheat.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) are signaling molecules that enable cells to respond rapidly to abiotic stress [3,4]. ROS levels markedly increase under drought stress. Excessive ROS can induce oxidative stress, leading to cellular damage and programmed cell death [5]. Furthermore, abiotic stress–responsive genes are induced by ROS accumulation under drought stress [6]. Maintaining ROS homeostasis is essential for improving drought tolerance in plants. For instance, VvNAC33 improves drought tolerance in grapevine (Vitis vinifera) by maintaining ROS homeostasis and alleviating oxidative damage [7]. PagWOX11/12a, a member of the WOX transcription factor family from Populus, confers drought-stress tolerance by enhancing the activities of ROS-scavenging enzymes [8]. ZmWRKY71, a WRKY transcription factor from maize (Zea mays), enhances drought tolerance by upregulating ZmPOD42 and promoting ROS scavenging [9]. OsCHI3 from rice (Oryza sativa) mediates abscisic acid (ABA) biosynthesis (by activating OsNCED1 and inhibiting OsABA8ox3) and ROS scavenging in response to drought stress [10]. OsMAS1 reduces ROS accumulation to promote ABA-mediated stomatal closure, thereby enhancing drought tolerance in rice [11]. ZmSnRK2.10 phosphorylates and activates ZmCIPK33 to enhance drought tolerance in maize by inducing stomatal closure and reducing ROS levels via the brassinosteroid signaling pathway [12].

MAP3Ks, a class of serine/threonine protein kinases, are signaling molecules in plant responses to abiotic stress [[13], [14], [15]]. For instance, CaMEKK17, a MAP3K protein from pepper (Capsicum annuum), promotes drought tolerance through the ABA signaling pathway [16]. GhRAF19, a Raf-like MAPKKK from cotton (Gossypium hirsutum), exacerbates oxidative damage under drought conditions by negatively regulating the expression of antioxidant genes (GhSOD, GhCAT, and GhAPX) [17]. GhMPK7 from cotton and StMAPKK1 from potato (Solanum tuberosum) enhance drought tolerance by upregulating antioxidant genes, reducing ROS accumulation, and facilitating stomatal closure [18,19].

Although MAP3K proteins have been shown to play crucial roles in plant responses to abiotic stress across numerous species [14,[20], [21], [22]], little is known about the functions of wheat MAP3K proteins in response to water deficit. In this study, we investigated the roles of TaMAP3K17 and its upstream transcription factor TaMYB2 in wheat drought tolerance. Through expression analysis, transgenic approaches, and molecular interaction analyses, we showed that TaMYB2 activates the expression of TaMAP3K17 to maintain ROS homeostasis in wheat. Wheat frequently suffers from water shortages during its growing season, which severely affects growth and productivity [23]. Therefore, elucidation of the TaMYB2–TaMAP3K17 regulatory module provides valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying wheat drought responses and offers potential targets for improving drought tolerance in wheat.

Results

TaMAP3K17 localizes to the plasma membrane and nucleus

Our previous RNA sequencing study [24] showed that TaMAP3K17 expression is elevated during drought stress compared with normal conditions (Fig. S1C). The TaMAP3K17 gene contains a 1624-bp coding sequence (CDS) and encodes a 43.97-kDa protein with an isoelectric point of 4.53 (Fig. S1A). TaMAP3K17 contains a conserved STKc_MAPKKK domain (Fig. S1B). Here, we performed multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic analysis, in which TaMAP3K17 was placed close to MAPKKK proteins involved in plant development and adaptation to abiotic stress [25,26] (Fig. 1A and B). Based on reverse transcription–quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR), TaMAP3K17 was expressed at high levels in wheat roots and leaves (Fig. S1D). In addition, a TaMAP3K17-GFP fusion protein localized to the plasma membrane and nucleus in transiently transfected Nicotiana benthamiana leaves when expressed under the control of the 35S CaMV promoter (Fig. 1C).

TaMAP3K17-GFP was co-expressed with AtPLK-RFP, a plasma membrane-localized marker [27], and DAPI was used as a nucleus marker. Scale bar = 20 μm.

TaMAP3K17 expression is drought induced

To further analyze the expression pattern of TaMAP3K17 under water-deficit conditions, we generated N. benthamiana plants harboring the proTaMAP3K17::GUS construct and subjected them to GUS staining assays. The GUS signal was significantly enhanced after drought-stress treatment compared to normal conditions (Fig. 2A). Moreover, RT-qPCR showed that TaMAP3K17 transcripts rapidly accumulated at 6 h and peaked at 18 h in response to drought stress (Fig. 2B and C). These findings indicate that TaMAP3K17 is induced by drought stress.

Silencing of TaMAP3K17 reduces drought tolerance in wheat

To clarify the role of TaMAP3K17 in wheat under drought stress, we generated TaMAP3K17-silenced wheat plants using barley stripe mosaic virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS; Fig. S2A and Fig. 3A). RT-qPCR confirmed that TaMAP3K17 transcript levels were markedly reduced in BSMV:TaMAP3K17 plants compared to the BSMV:00 vector control (Fig. 3B). Following drought treatment, the drought tolerance of BSMV:TaMAP3K17 plants was significantly reduced, whereas BSMV:00 plants exhibited only slight drought stress (Fig. 3A). DAB and NBT staining showed that BSMV:TaMAP3K17 plants accumulated higher levels of ROS than BSMV:00 plants (Fig. 3C). ROS and MDA levels were elevated in BSMV:TaMAP3K17 plants, whereas antioxidant enzyme activities were reduced (Fig. 3D–H and Fig. S2B).

MAPK signaling cascades regulate the expression of ROS-associated genes [28]. We generated a preliminary protein–protein interaction network, which identified several candidate MAPKK proteins as potentially interacting with TaMAP3K17 (Fig. S3). Notably, RT-qPCR revealed significant downregulation of genes encoding key ROS-scavenging enzymes (TaCAT1, TaAPX2, and TaPOD5) in BSMV:TaMAP3K17 plants under drought stress (Fig. 3I–K). These results point to the importance of ROS homeostasis in wheat under drought stress and implicate TaMAP3K17 in MAPK-mediated stress signaling.

TaMAP3K17-overexpressing wheat plants show enhanced drought tolerance

To further elucidate the role of TaMAP3K17 in the response of wheat to drought stress, we introduced the TaMAP3K17 gene into wild-type wheat cultivar Fielder (WT), generating three homozygous transgenic lines (TaMAP3K17-OE1, TaMAP3K17-OE2, and TaMAP3K17-OE3). The TaMAP3K17-OE lines displayed superior drought tolerance, with higher biomass and total root length under 20% PEG6000 treatment compared to the WT (Fig. 4A–C and Fig. S4). In soil-pot experiments, the TaMAP3K17-OE plants exhibited significantly better growth performance than the control (Fig. 4A and Fig. S5). The TaMAP3K17-OE lines maintained higher relative water content (RWC) and showed lower water loss rates than the WT under drought stress (Fig. 4B and C). Antioxidant enzyme activities were significantly higher in the TaMAP3K17-OE lines than the WT, and transcriptional analysis showed changes consistent with the enzyme activity data (Fig. 4G-L), whereas WT plants accumulated significantly higher levels of MDA, O2−, and H2O2 than TaMAP3K17-OE plants (Fig. 4D–F). These results indicate that TaMAP3K17 enhances drought tolerance in wheat and is associated with improved water retention and enhanced antioxidant capacity under water-deficit conditions.

TaMYB2 activates the transcription of TaMAP3K17

To identify the transcription factor that regulates the transcription of TaMAP3K17, we conducted yeast one-hybrid (Y1H) library screening using the TaMAP3K17 promoter as bait. The MYB transcription factor TaMYB2 was identified as a candidate protein that binds to the TaMAP3K17 promoter. We performed Y1H and dual-luciferase assays to determine whether TaMYB2 directly binds to the TaMAP3K17 promoter. In a Y1H assay, only Y187-pHis2 yeast cells co-expressing pHis-pTaMAP3K17 and pGADT7-Rec-TaMYB2 grew on SD/-Trp/-Leu/-His medium, indicating that TaMYB2 binds to the TaMAP3K17 promoter (Fig. 5A). The dual-luciferase assay and quantitative analysis showed that co-expressing 35S:TaMYB2-GFP with pTaMAP3K17-LUC significantly increased TaMAP3K17 promoter activity (Fig. 5B and C). Mutational analysis combined with a dual-luciferase assay showed that the cis-element in the P2 region of the TaMAP3K17 promoter is required for its TaMYB2-mediated transactivation (Fig. 5D–F). These results indicate that TaMYB2 directly binds to the TaMAP3K17 promoter and activates its transcription.

TaMYB2 encodes a nucleus-localized transcription factor

The full-length TaMYB2 gene contains a 1656-bp CDS, comprising two exons and one intron (Fig. S6A). Conserved domain analysis showed that TaMYB2 contains a MYB domain (Fig. S6B). Phylogenetic analysis and multiple sequence alignment (Fig. S6C and D) indicated that TaMYB2 is closely related to MYB proteins involved in plant stress responses [29]. RT-qPCR showed that TaMYB2 is abundantly expressed in roots (Fig. S6F). In addition, TaMYB2 transcripts in both roots and leaves accumulated rapidly at 6 h and peaked at 12 h after drought treatment (Fig. S6G and H). TaMYB2-GFP fusion protein predominantly accumulated in the nucleus when transiently expressed in N. benthamiana leaf epidermal cells (Fig. S6E).

Silencing of TaMYB2 reduces drought tolerance in wheat

To clarify the function of TaMYB2, we generated TaMYB2-silenced wheat plants using BSMV-VIGS (Fig. 6A and Fig. S7). RT-qPCR confirmed that TaMYB2 was downregulated in BSMV:TaMYB2 plants compared with BSMV:00 plants (Fig. 6B). Following drought treatment, the drought tolerance of BSMV:TaMYB2 plants was reduced compared with BSMV:00 plants (Fig. 6A). DAB and NBT staining assays revealed that BSMV:TaMYB2 plants accumulated more ROS than BSMV:00 plants (Fig. 6C). Antioxidant enzyme activities were reduced and oxidative damage was enhanced in BSMV:TaMYB2 plants under drought stress (Fig. 6D–I). RT-qPCR showed that the expression levels of TaMAP3K17, TaCAT1, TaAPX2, and TaPOD5 were significantly reduced in BSMV:TaMYB2 plants compared to BSMV:00 plants under drought stress (Fig. 6J-M). These results indicate that silencing TaMYB2 reduced drought tolerance and disrupted ROS homeostasis in wheat, which was accompanied by decreased expression of TaMAP3K17 and genes encoding antioxidant enzymes.

Arabidopsis plants heterologously expressing TaMYB2 show enhanced drought tolerance

To further investigate the role of TaMYB2 in drought stress responses, we generated Arabidopsis thaliana lines heterologously overexpressing TaMYB2 (TaMYB2-OE). We compared the drought responses of TaMYB2-OE and WT plants during germination and vegetative growth. TaMYB2-OE plants exhibited a significantly higher germination rates than the WT under 4% PEG6000 treatment (Fig. 7A and B), and the TaMYB2-OE lines showed less inhibited primary root growth and higher fresh weight than the WT under this treatment (Fig. 7C–F). In soil-grown plants under drought stress, TaMYB2-OE plants showed enhanced drought tolerance compared to the WT (Fig. 8A). The TaMYB2-OE plants maintained higher RWC and exhibited lower water loss rates than the WT under drought stress (Fig. 8B and C). Physiological assays revealed that the TaMYB2-OE lines accumulated significantly higher levels of APX, POD, and CAT than WT plants, whereas the MDA, O2−, and H2O2 contents were markedly lower than in WT plants (Fig. 8D–I). These results indicate that heterologously overexpressing TaMYB2 enhanced drought tolerance in A. thaliana, including improved water retention, reduced ROS accumulation, and increased antioxidant enzyme activities.

Discussion

MAPK signaling cascades play crucial roles in plant growth, development, and stress responses [13,15,20]. For example, OsMAP3K28 phosphorylates OsMKK1 to regulate drought tolerance by activating the ABA signaling pathway [30]. The GhMEKK3/GhMEKK8/GhMEKK31-GhMAPKK5-GhMAPK11/23 cascade positively regulates tolerance to drought and salt stress in cotton via the ABA and ROS signaling pathways [28]. However, the roles of MAPKKKs in regulating drought tolerance and their regulatory mechanisms under drought stress remain unclear. Previous studies have identified several MAPKKKs that might function in plant defense against abiotic and biotic stress [[31], [32], [33]]. In the present study, we determined that TaMAP3K17, a drought-induced MAP3K protein that is homologous to rice OsMAP3K17 and A. thaliana AtMAP3K17, positively regulates abiotic stress responses in wheat [25,26]. The drought-inducible gene TaMAP3K17 plays a significant role in improving drought tolerance in wheat (Fig. 3, Fig. 4). Enhanced ROS-scavenging ability and increased primary root length positively regulate drought responses [34,35]. Indeed, TaMAP3K17-OE plants exhibited improved growth performance, longer roots, higher fresh weight, and enhanced water retention under drought stress, as supported by our RWC and water loss rate data (Fig. 4 and Fig. S5). These results support the notion that TaMAP3K17 enhances drought tolerance in wheat.

Abiotic stress, particularly drought-induced osmotic stress, leads to the significant accumulation of ROS, thereby exacerbating oxidative stress [36,37]. Therefore, efficient ROS scavenging is particularly critical for plant growth. Additionally, the MAPK signaling pathway plays a crucial role in regulating the antioxidant defense system under stress conditions [38]. For example, the MAPKKK DSM1 confers drought resistance in rice by facilitating ROS clearance [39]. In this study, TaMAP3K17-silenced plants exhibited significantly lower CAT, APX, and POD activities compared to control plants, whereas MDA, O2−, and H2O2 contents were elevated under drought stress. Transgenic TaMAP3K17-OE wheat plants exhibited significantly elevated CAT, APX, and POD enzyme activities, along with reduced MDA and ROS levels; the results of RT-qPCR are consistent with the enzyme activity data (Fig. 3, Fig. 4). These results support the notion that TaMAP3K17 regulates antioxidant enzyme activity and ROS homeostasis under drought stress.

MYB transcription factors are major regulators of plant development and defense responses and play important roles in regulating plant responses to abiotic stress, especially drought stress [40]. For example, PlMYB108 from Paeonia lactiflora and GmMYB3a from soybean (Glycine max) enhance drought tolerance by regulating the ROS pathway [41,42]. Similarly, ZmMYB-CC10 from maize and BpMYB123 from Betula platyphylla confer drought tolerance by activating the expression of their target genes (ZmAPX4 and BpLEA14, respectively) and modulating ROS homeostasis [43,44]. AtMYB37 enhances drought resistance in A. thaliana by promoting the expression of antioxidant genes (AtALDH31, AtAPX1, and AtCPN20) and sustaining ROS homeostasis [45]. MAPK cascades can be directly or indirectly regulated by multiple transcription factors, thereby mediating abiotic stress responses by regulating gene expression [[46], [47], [48]].

In the current study, sequence analysis revealed that the TaMAP3K17 promoter contains an MBS motif along with several other cis-acting elements associated with abiotic stress responses. TaMYB2 predominantly localized to the nucleus, which is consistent with its predicted role as a transcription factor (Fig. S6). Y1H and dual-luciferase assays demonstrated that TaMYB2 binds to the TaMAP3K17 promoter and activates its transcription. Mutational analysis showed that TaMYB2-mediated transactivation of TaMAP3K17 primarily depends on the P2 region in its promoter, and mutating the cis-element around −810 bp markedly reduced this promoter activation (Fig. 5). Functional analysis further supported the involvement of TaMYB2 in drought responses. TaMYB2-silenced plants exhibited reduced drought tolerance and increased ROS accumulation (Fig. 6), whereas TaMYB2-OE lines showed improved drought tolerance, enhanced water retention, reduced ROS accumulation, and enhanced antioxidant capacity (Fig. 7, Fig. 8). In addition, TaMYB2 silencing reduced the expression of TaMAP3K17 as well as antioxidant enzyme-related genes, including TaCAT1, TaPOD5, and TaAPX2 (Fig. 6). These data support the notion that the TaMYB2-TaMAP3K17 regulatory module is involved in drought tolerance and ROS homeostasis. However, additional genetic evidence will be required to fully establish the causal relationship between TaMYB2-mediated activation of TaMAP3K17 and downstream antioxidant responses. Although the present findings support a positive role of TaMYB2 in drought tolerance, as determined by VIGS in wheat and overexpression in Arabidopsis, the stable overexpression of TaMYB2 in wheat would provide more direct evidence for this relationship.

As TaMAP3K17 is a MAP3K, it is expected to function upstream of one or more MAPKK components. Although our preliminary analysis predicted several candidate MAPKK proteins as downstream substrates of TaMAP3K17, these candidates have not yet been experimentally validated (Fig. S3). Therefore, the direct downstream MAPKK substrates of TaMAP3K17 and their roles in ROS regulation remain unresolved and require further investigation. TaHT1, a previously reported wheat MAP3K, is a group C Raf-like MAPKKK (Fig. S8) that negatively regulates drought tolerance and primary root length by interacting with TaSnRK2.10. TaHT1 was downregulated by both PEG and ABA treatment [49]. By contrast, TaMAP3K17 is induced by drought, positively regulates drought tolerance, and is transcriptionally activated by TaMYB2. These observations suggest that different wheat MAP3K family members might function at distinct regulatory nodes and have opposite effects on drought adaptation.

In conclusion, we identified the drought stress–responsive gene TaMAP3K17, which enhances drought tolerance in wheat, and demonstrated that TaMYB2 binds to the TaMAP3K17 promoter and activates its transcription. Functional analysis further supported the positive roles of both TaMAP3K17 and TaMYB2 in drought tolerance, water retention, and ROS homeostasis. Further studies will be required to clarify the complete downstream signaling cascade and the precise genetic relationship between TaMYB2-mediated activation of TaMAP3K17 and the regulation of antioxidant enzyme activity under drought stress. Collectively, our findings lay the foundation for enhancing drought tolerance in wheat via the TaMYB2-TaMAP3K17 module.

Materials and methods

Genetic structure and evolutionary analysis of TaMAP3K17

The amino acid sequences of TaMAP3K17 (accession ID: TraesCS3B02G289500), AeTaMAP3K17 (XP_020169523.1), TuMAP3K17 (XP_048566943.1), HvMAP3K17 (XP_044973985.1), LrMAPKKK17 (XP_047056975.1), LpMAPKKK18 (XP_051221602.1), AtMAP3K17 (AT2G32510), OsMAP3K18 (Os03g0764300), BdMAP3K18 (XP_003567028.1), PaMAP3K17 (XP_062206612.1), and PhMAP3K17 (XP_025817528.1) were retrieved from the Ensembl Plants database, and a phylogenetic tree was created using MEGA-X software (v12.014). The amino acid sequences were aligned using ClustalW software (v2.1), and conserved domains were identified using the NCBI Conserved Domain Database.

Subcellular localization analysis

The full-length CDS of TaMAP3K17 was isolated from wheat cultivar Xiaobaimai and inserted into the pHZM27-EGFP vector to generate the GFP fusion construct (pHZM27-TaMAP3K17-EGFP). The empty pHZM27-EGFP vector was used as a control. For subcellular localization analysis, TaMAP3K17-GFP was transiently co-expressed with AtPLK-RFP, a previously reported plasma membrane-localized marker [50], in Nicotiana benthamiana leaf epidermal cells via Agrobacterium tumefaciens GV3101-mediated infiltration as previously described [27]. DAPI was used as a nucleus marker. Fluorescence signals were observed under a confocal laser-scanning microscope (LSM 980, Zeiss, Germany).

GUS staining

The 2-kb promoter region upstream of TaMAP3K17 was amplified from genomic DNA and cloned into the pCAMBIA1305-GUS vector. The proTaMAP3K17::GUS vector and the empty vector (promoter-less pCAMBIA1305-GUS) were introduced into tobacco plants via Agrobacterium-mediated transient expression. The plants were incubated in a 25 °C low-light incubator for 2–3 days. Drought treatment was applied using 10% PEG6000. One week later, tissue samples were subjected to GUS staining using a JF0171 kit (JSENB, Hong Kong) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The stained tissues were observed under an optical microscope (ASV0870, Aosvi, China).

TaMYB2-overexpressing (OE) Arabidopsis lines

To generate TaMYB2-overexpression (OE) transgenic A. thaliana plants, the CDS was cloned into the pBI121 vector. The primers are provided in Table S1. The resulting TaMYB2-OE vector was introduced into A. thaliana accession Col-0 through Agrobacterium-mediated transformation [51]. Transgenic plants were screened on medium supplemented with kanamycin, and transgene-positive lines were identified by PCR amplification. Three homozygous T3 lines were selected for subsequent studies.

Abiotic stress treatment of transgenic Arabidopsis

To evaluate seed germination, TaMYB2-OE and WT seeds were surface-sterilized and sown on MS medium containing 0% (as a control) and 4% PEG6000. After 3 days of vernalization at 4 °C, the plates were transferred to standard conditions to record the germination rate. Three biological replicates were carried out, with a minimum of 50 seeds per replicate. To assess root growth, 7-day-old A. thaliana seedlings were placed on MS medium containing 0% and 4% PEG6000. After one week of treatment, total root length and fresh weight were measured. To impose drought stress, 20-day-old A. thaliana seedlings were transferred from MS medium to soil and acclimatized for 7 days. Phenotypes were observed and photographed after 25 days.

Measurement of physiological parameters

Physiological indices were measured using assay kits obtained from Suzhou Comin Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Suzhou, China). These included malondialdehyde (MDA), superoxide anion (O2−), and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) content and catalase (CAT), peroxidase (POD), and ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity. ROS accumulation in BSMV:TaMAP3K17-and BSMV:TaMYB2-silenced plants was assessed by immersing the leaves in nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT, for O2−) and 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB, for H2O2) solutions overnight. The experiment was performed with three independent biological replicates.

Measurement of relative water content and water loss rate

Relative water content (RWC) was determined based on fresh weight (FW), turgid weight (TW), and dry weight (DW) and calculated as RWC (%) = [(FW - DW)/(TW - DW)] × 100 [52]. To measure water loss rate, detached leaves were weighed immediately after sampling and placed on a laboratory bench at room temperature. Leaf weights were recorded at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 h, and water loss was calculated as the percentage of weight loss relative to the initial fresh weight, as previously described [53].

Generation of transgenic wheat and drought-stress treatments

The CDS of TaMAP3K17 was amplified using gene-specific primers and subcloned into the pWMB110 vector to yield the recombinant plasmid pWMB110-TaMAP3K17, which was subsequently introduced into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain EHA105. The primers are provided in Table S1. Agrobacterium-mediated transformation was performed, and T0 transgenic plants were regenerated under selective tissue culture conditions [54]. Drought treatments were conducted as previously described [55]. Seedlings were treated with 20% PEG6000 for 7 days to simulate water deficit. For drought stress in soil, water was withheld from uniform plants until severe wilting appeared, and the plants were photographed.

Yeast one-hybrid (Y1H) assay

The TaMAP3K17 promoter was cloned into the pHis2 vector, resulting in the recombinant vector pHis2-ProTaMAP3K17. The CDS of TaMYB2 was cloned and inserted into the pGADT7-Rec vector, generating the pGADT7-Rec-TaMYB2 expression vector. The recombinant plasmids were co-transformed into Y187 yeast cells using a Y187-pHis2 Yeast One-Hybrid Interaction Proving Kit (YH1011, Coolaber). The yeast cells were cultured on SD/-Trp/-Ura double-deficient medium according to the manufacturer's protocol. Following incubation at 28 °C, single colonies carrying the various vector combinations were streaked onto SD/-Trp/-Leu/-His medium and their growth examined.

Transient dual-luciferase reporter assay

Transient transactivation assays were performed in tobacco leaves as previously described [56]. The 2-kb promoter region of TaMAP3K17 was amplified and inserted into the pGreenII-0800 vector, generating p0800-pTaMAP3K17. The CDS of TaMYB2 was cloned into the pCAMBIA1302-GFP vector, yielding the 35S:TaMYB2-GFP vector. The vectors were transferred into Agrobacterium strain GV3101 competent cells (ZC1407, Zhuang Meng). Agrobacterium cultures harboring p0800-pTaMAP3K17 and 35S:TaMYB2-GFP were mixed at a 1:1 ratio and infiltrated into tobacco leaves. The p0800-pTaMAP3K17 and empty pCAMBIA1302-GFP vectors were used as controls. Images were acquired and archived using a Rocel Multi-Functional Plant Live Imaging System (Rocel, Biocover, China). Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured according to the instructions provided with the Luciferase Assay Kit (11402ES60, Yisheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity for each sample; the normalized value of the control was set to 1. Three independent biological replicates were used for statistical analysis.

Promoter deletion and mutational analysis

To identify the region of the TaMAP3K17 promoter required for TaMYB2-mediated activation, the 2-kb upstream sequence of TaMAP3K17 was divided into three fragments: P1 (−2000 to −1301), P2 (−1300 to −710), and P3 (−700 to −1). A mutated version of P2 (mP2) was also generated. Each fragment was cloned into the pGreenII-0800-LUC vector as a reporter and co-expressed with 35S:TaMYB2-GFP in Nicotiana benthamiana leaves. Representative luminescence images were acquired using a Rocel In Vivo Plant Imaging System (Biocover, Beijing). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity (Yeasen, Shanghai).

Virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS)

VIGS was performed with barley stripe mosaic virus (BSMV) as previously described [57]. A conserved 300-bp viral cDNA fragment was amplified using SGN-VIGS (solgenomics.net) and inserted into the BSMV-γ vector, yielding the recombinant plasmids BSMV-γ-TaMAP3K17 and BSMV-γ-TaMYB2. Following linearization of BSMV-α, BSMV-β, BSMV-γ, BSMV-γ-PDS, and BSMV-γ-TaMAP3K17, in vitro transcription was carried out using a T7 RNA Synthesizer Co-Transcription and Capping Kit (10673ES50, Yisheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). For VIGS, BSMV-γ, BSMV-γ-PDS, and BSMV-γ-TaMAP3K17 or BSMV-γ-TaMYB2 were each mixed with BSMV-α and BSMV-β at a 1:1:1 ratio and applied to the leaves of Pubingzi 300 wheat plants. Following the appearance of leaf chlorosis, the seedlings were subjected to drought stress.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from wheat tissues using an RNA Extraction kit (DP452, Tiangen Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. First-strand cDNA was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using FastKing RT Kit (KP116, Tiangen Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). RT-qPCR was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix (FP209, Tiangen Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) on a TGreat Real-Time PCR system (OSE-R96, Tiangen). The primers used for RT-qPCR are listed in Table S1. TaActin was used as the internal control. Relative transcript levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Three independent biological replicates were used for statistical analysis.

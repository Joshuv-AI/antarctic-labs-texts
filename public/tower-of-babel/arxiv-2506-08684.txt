Integrating positive energy representations of the Virasoro algebra
André G. Henriques, James E. Tener

Abstract
We show that every unitary positive energy representation W W of the Virasoro algebra exponentiates to a holomorphic ∗ * -representation of the semigroup of annuli by bounded operators on the Hilbert space completion of W W . We use this to show that every representation of the Virasoro conformal net also carries a representation of the semigroup of annuli of the same kind.

Introduction

Fix
c\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
.
The Virasoro Fréchet Lie algebra is the Lie algebra

\textstyle\bigg\{\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}L_{n}+k\text{\bf 1}\,\bigg|\,\begin{aligned} &a_{n},k\in{\mathbb{C}}\\[-2.84526pt]

Rewriting

\omega_{Vir}\big(f(z)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z},g(z)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\big)\;=\;\tfrac{c}{12}\int_{S^{1}}\tfrac{\partial^{3}f}{\partial z^{3}}(z)\,g(z)\,\tfrac{dz}{2\pi i}

\centerline{\hbox{$\mathrm{Int}(\gamma_{in})\subseteq\mathrm{Int}(\gamma_{out})$, where $(\gamma_{in},\gamma_{out})\sim(\gamma^{\prime}_{in},\gamma^{\prime}_{out})$ if\, $\exists$ a diffeomorphism}}

, Thm. 1.4.3(a)]
, the image of
D(A)=D(A-\omega^{\prime})
under
A-\omega^{\prime}
is all of
{\mathcal{H}}
, and similarly for
A^{*}-\omega^{\prime}
.
Taking adjoints, it follows that
A-\omega^{\prime}
(and
A^{*}-\omega^{\prime}
) are injective, and thus these operators map their domains bijectively onto
{\mathcal{H}}
.
The inverse operators
(A-\omega^{\prime})^{-1}
and
(A^{*}-\omega^{\prime})^{-1}
are thus closed and defined on all of
{\mathcal{H}}
, and therefore bounded.
We conclude that
\omega^{\prime}\in\rho(A)
and similarly for
A^{*}
.
∎

The story of operator semigroups is tied to the ODE
T^{\prime}(t)=AT(t)
, whereas our treatment of the semigroup of annuli relies on the corresponding time-dependent problem
T^{\prime}(t)=A(t)T(t)
.
In this case, the natural algebraic objects to replace semigroups are
evolution systems
(see
[
Paz83
, §5.1]
):

Definition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{X}}
be a Banach space, and let
U(s,t)
be a two-parameter family of bounded operators on
{\mathbb{X}}
indexed by parameters
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
. Then
U(t,s)
is called an evolution system (on
{\mathbb{X}}
) if

1.

U(s,s)=1
for
0\leq s\leq T

2.

U(t,r)U(r,s)=U(t,s)
for
0\leq s\leq r\leq t\leq T

3.

For every
x\in{\mathbb{X}}
, the map
(t,s)\mapsto U(t,s)x
is continuous for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T

If
T(t)
is a semigroup, then
U(t,s):=T(t-s)
is an evolution system, but most evolution systems do not arise in this way.
Informally, an evolution system is generated by a family
A(t)
of unbounded operators if
U(t,s)x
solves the following initial value problem for
t\geq s
:

u^{\prime}(t)=A(t)u(t),\qquad u(s)=x.

We rely on the following generation theorem for evolution systems.

.

By Remark

.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

{\mathbb{X}}_{0}\supset{\mathbb{X}}_{1}\supset{\mathbb{X}}_{2}\supset{\mathbb{X}}_{3}

be a chain of continuously and densely embedded Banach spaces, and let
A(p,t)
be a family of unbounded operators on
{\mathbb{X}}_{0}
indexed by
p,t\in[0,1]
.
For each
p
, suppose that the family
\{A(p,t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
satisfies the conditions
(H_{1})_{n}
,
(H_{2}^{+})_{n}
,
(H_{3})_{n}
of Theorem
3.3
for
n=0,1,2
, and let
U_{p}
be the resulting evolution system.
Suppose that the family
A(p,t)
satisfies the following uniformity conditions:

i)

The constant
\omega
of
(H_{1})_{n}
may be chosen independent of
p
for
n=0,1,2
.

ii)

The assignment
p\mapsto A(p,t)|_{{\mathbb{X}}_{1}}
is
C^{1}
as a map
[0,1]\to L^{\infty}([0,1],{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{X}}_{1},{\mathbb{X}}_{0}))
.

iii)

The assignment
p\mapsto A(p,t)|_{{\mathbb{X}}_{2}}
is continuous as a map
[0,1]\to L^{\infty}([0,1],{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{X}}_{2},{\mathbb{X}}_{1}))

The map
p\mapsto U_{p}(1,0)
is differentiable
[0,1]\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{X}}_{2},{\mathbb{X}}_{0})
and

\partial_{p}\,U_{p}(1,0)=\int_{0}^{1}U_{p}(1,x)\,\partial_{p}A(p,x)\,U_{p}(x,0)\,dx.

(3.2)

Proof.

We begin by analyzing the integrand of (
3.2
).
First observe that by assumption (i) and property
(E_{1})
the norms
\left\|U_{p}(t,s)\right\|_{0\to 0}
and
\left\|U_{p}(t,s)\right\|_{1\to 1}
are bounded independent of
p,t,s
, and by assumption (ii) the norms
\left\|A(p,t)\right\|_{1\to 0}
and
\left\|\partial_{p}A(p,t)\right\|_{1\to 0}
are bounded independent of
(p,t)
.
Next, for
v\in{\mathbb{X}}_{1}
we note that the map
x\mapsto U_{p}(x,0)v
is continuous
[0,1]\to{\mathbb{X}}_{1}
by
(E_{5})
.
Hence by assumption (ii) the map
x\mapsto\partial_{p}A(p,x)\,U_{p}(x,0)v
is continuous
[0,1]\to{\mathbb{X}}_{0}
.
It follows that

x\mapsto U_{p}(1,x)\,\partial_{p}A(p,x)\,U_{p}(x,0)v

is continuous
[0,1]\to{\mathbb{X}}_{0}
since the norms
\left\|U_{p}(1,x)\right\|_{0\to 0}
are uniformly bounded and
U_{p}(1,x)
is strongly continuous in
x
by the definition of evolution system.
Thus for each vector
v\in{\mathbb{X}}_{1}
, the integral

\int_{0}^{1}U_{p}(1,x)\,\partial_{p}A(p,x)\,U_{p}(x,0)v\,dx

exists in
{\mathbb{X}}_{0}
, and the right-hand side of (
3.2
) makes sense as a bounded operator
{\mathbb{X}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{X}}_{0}
.

For
0\leq a\leq x\leq b\leq 1
and
p,p^{\prime}\in[0,1]
, we differentiate in
{\mathbb{X}}_{0}
using
(E_{2})
and
(E_{3})
to obtain

\partial_{x}\,U_{p}(b,x)U_{p^{\prime}}(x,a)v=U_{p^{\prime}}(b,x)\left[A(p^{\prime},x)-A(p,x)\right]U_{p}(x,a)v.

Note that this is not a straightforward application of the product rule, but can be derived by the usual argument using
(E_{4})
for
U_{p}
and the fact that
\left\|U_{p}(b,x)\right\|_{0\to 0}
is uniformly bounded.
Using arguments as above we see that

x\mapsto U_{p^{\prime}}(b,x)\left[A(p^{\prime},x)-A(p,x)\right]U_{p}(x,a)v

is continuous
[0,1]\to{\mathbb{X}}
, and so by the fundamental theorem of calculus we have

U_{p^{\prime}}(b,a)v-U_{p}(b,a)v=\int_{a}^{b}U_{p^{\prime}}(b,x)\left[A(p^{\prime},x)-A(p,x)\right]U_{p}(x,a)v\,dx.

(3.3)

Using the fact that
\left\|U_{p}(t,s)\right\|_{1\to 1}
and
\left\|U_{p^{\prime}}(t,s)\right\|_{0\to 0}
are uniformly bounded, we obtain a bound on the operator norm

\left\|U_{p^{\prime}}(b,a)-U_{p}(b,a)\right\|_{1\to 0}\leq C\sup_{0\leq x\leq 1}\left\|A(p^{\prime},x)-A(p,x)\right\|_{1\to 0}

(3.4)

for a constant
C
which does not depend on
a
and
b
.

We now wish to show that

\lim_{h\to 0}\left\|\frac{U_{p+h}(1,0)-U_{p}(1,0)}{h}-\int_{0}^{1}U_{p}(1,x)\,\partial_{p}A(p,x)\,U_{p}(x,0)\,dx\right\|_{2\to 0}=0.

Let
\epsilon>0
.
The norms
\left\|U_{p}(x,0)\right\|_{2\to 2}
are uniformly bounded by condition (i) and the norms
\left\|\partial_{p}A(p,x)\right\|_{2\to 1}
are uniformly bounded by assumption (iii).
Hence by (
3.4
), for
h
sufficiently small we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\big[U_{p+h}(1,x)-U_{p}(1,x)\big]\partial_{p}A(p,x)U_{p}(x,0)\,dx\right\|_{2\to 0}<\epsilon.

Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{U_{p+h}(1,0)-U_{p}(1,0)}{h}-\int_{0}^{1}U_{p}(1,x)\,\partial_{p}A(p,x)\,U_{p}(x,0)\,dx\right\|_{2\to 0}\leq

\displaystyle\quad\leq\epsilon+\left\|\frac{U_{p+h}(1,0)-U_{p}(1,0)}{h}-\int_{0}^{1}U_{p+h}(1,x)\,\partial_{p}A(p,x)\,U_{p}(x,0)\,dx\right\|_{2\to 0}

(by (
3.3
))

\displaystyle\quad=\epsilon+\left\|\int_{0}^{1}U_{p+h}(1,x)\left[\frac{A(p+h,x)-A(p,x)}{h}-\partial_{p}A(p,x)\right]U_{p}(x,0)\,dx\right\|_{2\to 0}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\epsilon+C^{\prime}\sup_{0\leq x\leq 1}\left\|\frac{A(p+h,x)-A(p,x)}{h}-\partial_{p}A(p,x)\right\|_{1\to 0},

where the last step used the uniform boundedness of
\left\|U_{p+h}(1,x)\right\|_{0\to 0}
and
\left\|U_{p}(x,0)\right\|_{0\to 0}
.
By assumption (ii), this last expression converges to
0
as
h\to 0
, completing the proof.
∎

Finally, we close the section by observing that time-ordered exponentials are compatible with adjoints.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
be a continuous and dense embedding of Hilbert spaces, and let
\{A(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be unbounded operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
such that both
A(t)
and the adjoints
B(t):=A(1-t)^{*}
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
.
Let
U(t,s)
be the evolution system corresponding to the family
A(t)
, and let
\tilde{U}(t,s)
be the evolution system corresponding to the family
B(t)
.
Then

\tilde{U}(t,s)=U(1-s,1-t)^{*}.

Proof.

Let
T_{t}
be the semigroup generated by
A(t)
, and let
\tilde{T}_{t}
be the semigroup generated by
B(t)
.
Fix
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
, and for a nonnegative integer
n
let
\Delta=\tfrac{t-s}{n}
and

U_{n}(t,s)=T_{s+(n-1)\Delta}(\Delta)\cdots T_{s+\Delta}(\Delta)T_{s}(\Delta).

From e.g. the explicit formula for
T_{t}
in
[
Paz83
, Thm. 1.8.3]
we see
T_{\tau}(s)^{*}=\tilde{T}_{1-\tau}(s)
.
Hence

\displaystyle U_{n}(1-s,1-t)^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{1-t}(\Delta)^{*}\cdots T_{1-t+(n-1)\Delta}(\Delta)^{*}=\tilde{T}_{t}(\Delta)\cdots\tilde{T}_{t-(n-1)\Delta}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=\tilde{T}_{s+n\Delta}(\Delta)\cdots\tilde{T}_{s+\Delta}(\Delta).

Arguing as in
[
Paz83
, Thm. 5.3.1]
(see also Remark
3.5
), we have

U_{n}(t,s)\to U(t,s)
in the strong operator topology.
Examining the above formula for
U_{n}(1-s,1-t)^{*}
, by the same argument we have
U_{n}(1-s,1-t)^{*}\to\tilde{U}(t,s)
in the strong operator topology as well.
In particular these limits hold in the weak operator topology, for which the adjoint is continuous, and we conclude
\tilde{U}(t,s)=U(1-s,1-t)^{*}
, as required.
∎

4
Estimates for the Virasoro algebra

Fix
c\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, and let

\Vir_{c}:=\textstyle\big\{\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}L_{n}+k\text{\bf 1}\,\big|\,a_{n},k\in{\mathbb{C}}\big\}

denote the Virasoro Lie algebra,
with Lie bracket

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{n+m}+\frac{c}{12}(m^{3}-m)\delta_{n+m,0}
.
We use the symbol
\text{\bf 1}\in\Vir_{c}
to denote a formal unit, and require representations of
\Vir_{c}
to map
1
to the identity operator.

A representation of
\Vir_{c}
is said to have
positive energy
if the associated operator
L_{0}
has discrete spectrum, the spectrum is bounded from below, and all the (generalized) eigenspaces are finite dimensional.
We will only be concerned with unitary representations here, in which
L_{0}
acts as a self-adjoint operator, and by a positive energy representation we shall always mean a unitary positive energy representation.
Every such representation splits as a (possibly infinite) direct sum of irreducible ones.

By work of Goodman–Wallach
[
GW85
]
, every
unitary positive energy representation
W
of
\Vir_{c}

extends to a representation of the completed Lie algebra

{\mathcal{X}}_{c}(S^{1}):=\Big\{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}L_{n}+k\text{\bf 1}\,\Big|\,a_{n},k\in{\mathbb{C}},a_{n}\searrow 0\text{ faster than any polynomial}\Big\}

(4.1)

on the Hilbert space completion
{\mathcal{H}}
of
W
.
Here,
{\mathcal{X}}_{c}(S^{1})
is the central extension of the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{X}}(S^{1})
of smooth vector fields
S^{1}
associated to the Virasoro cocycle (
1.1
).
Given
X\in{\mathcal{X}}_{c}(S^{1})
, we write
\pi(X)
for the closed unbounded operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
obtained as the closure of the corresponding operator with domain
W
, and by abuse of notation we write
L_{n}
for the closed operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
corresponding to the basis of
\Vir_{c}
, in addition to the corresponding vector field.
The unitarity of the representation yields the identity

\pi(f\partial_{\theta})^{*}=-\pi(\overline{f}\partial_{\theta}),

(4.2)

which is an identity of unbounded operators as a consequence of Nelson’s commutator theorem (see e.g.
[
Wei05
, §3.2.2]
).
For
X=f\partial_{\theta}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{c}(S^{1})
, we write
X^{*}=-\overline{f}\partial_{\theta}
, so that
\pi
is a
*
-representation.

The operators
\pi(X)
all share a common invariant core, namely the smooth vectors for
L_{0}
, i.e. the subspace
\bigcap_{n>0}\operatorname{Dom}(L_{0}^{n})
of
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Since
\pi(X+Y)
and
\pi(X)+\pi(Y)
agree on this common core, we have an inclusion

\pi(X+Y)\subset\overline{\pi(X)+\pi(Y)}.

(4.3)

This inclusion is in fact an equality of unbounded operators.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X,Y\in{\mathcal{X}}_{c}(S^{1})
. Then as unbounded operators we have

\pi(X+Y)=\overline{\pi(X)+\pi(Y)}\quad\,\,\,\text{and}\quad\,\,\,\pi([X,Y])=\overline{[\pi(X),\pi(Y)]}.

Proof.

In light of (
4.3
) and (
4.2
),

\pi(X+Y)^{*}=\pi(X^{*}+Y^{*})\subset\overline{\pi(X^{*})+\pi(Y^{*})}=\overline{\pi(X)^{*}+\pi(Y)^{*}}

and taking adjoints yields

\overline{\pi(X)+\pi(Y)}\subset(\pi(X)^{*}+\pi(Y)^{*})^{*}\subset\pi(X+Y).

(4.4)

Combining (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) yields

\pi(X+Y)=\overline{\pi(X)+\pi(Y)}.

The argument for the commutator is similar.
Since smooth vectors for
L_{0}
are an invariant core for
\pi(X)
,
\pi(Y)
, and
\pi([X,Y])
, we have

\pi([X,Y])\subset\overline{[\pi(X),\pi(Y)]}.

(4.5)

Using (
4.5
) and (
4.2
), and the fact that the sum (resp. product) of adjoints of unbounded operators is contained in the adjoint of the sum (resp. product), we have:

\pi([Y^{*},X^{*}])\subset\overline{[\pi(Y)^{*},\pi(X)^{*}]}\subset\overline{(\pi(X)\pi(Y))^{*}-(\pi(Y)\pi(X))^{*}}\subset[\pi(X),\pi(Y)]^{*}.

Taking adjoints and invoking (
4.2
) yields the reverse inclusion

\overline{[\pi(X),\pi(Y)]}\subset\pi([Y^{*},X^{*}])^{*}=\pi([X,Y]).\qed

From now on, we suppress the operation of taking closure, and use the symbol
+
to indicate taking the
closure
of the sum of unbounded operators, and the bracket to indicate the
closure
of the commutator of unbounded operators.

Using the canonical embedding
{\mathcal{X}}(S^{1})\to{\mathcal{X}}_{c}(S^{1})
(which is not a Lie algebra homomorphism), we can also use the notation
\pi(X)
when
X\in{\mathcal{X}}(S^{1})
.

Remark 4.2
.

The stress-energy tensor corresponding to the representation is the formal series
T(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}L_{n}z^{-n-2}
, and if
f\in C^{\infty}(S^{1})
it is common to denote the corresponding smeared field by
T(f):=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{f}(n)L_{n}
, where
\hat{f}(n)
are the Fourier coefficients of

Lemma 4.4

(

Let

(

5
Representation of the semigroup of annuli

Let
(W,\pi)
be a unitary positive energy representation of the Virasoro algebra
\Vir_{c}
.
The usual Hilbert space completion of
W
is denoted
{\mathcal{H}}
, and the completions of
W
with the respect to the norms
v\mapsto\|(1+L_{0})^{n}v\|
are denoted
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
, so that we have a sequence of dense inclusions

{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{0}\supset{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\supset{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\supset{\mathcal{H}}_{3}\supset\ldots\supset W.

The goal of this section is to integrate the above representation to a representation

\pi:\tAnn_{c}\to B({\mathcal{H}})

(5.1)

of the semigroup of annuli by bounded operators on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
We will show that this representation leaves each of the subspaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
invariant, and that it is compatible with the dagger operation in the sense that
\langle\pi(A)\xi,\eta\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\langle\xi,\pi(A^{\dagger})\eta\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

For
h:S^{1}\times[0,1]\to A
a framing of some annulus
A
, and
X(t):=(-h_{t}/h_{\theta})|_{S^{1}\times\{t\}}
,
recall that

represents a lift of
A
to
\tAnn_{c}
.
We will define (

) by setting

\pi\left(z\cdot\!\!\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\Exp(X(t))dt\right)\,\,:=\,\,z\cdot\!\!\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\Exp\big(\pi(X(t))\big)dt,

(5.2)

where the time-ordered exponential on the right-hand side exists by Theorem
4.8
.
The fact the prescription (
5.2
) is well-defined is the main result of this section.
We begin with two technical lemmas about framings.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
h:S^{1}\times[0,1]\to A
be a framing of a partially thin annulus
A
. Then
h
can be homotoped through framings to one which is a diffeomorphism in the bulk (i.e. a diffeomorphism from
h^{-1}(\mathring{A})\to\mathring{A}
).

Proof.

For every point
z\in A
, pick a preimage
(\theta,t)
under
h
, and let
v_{z}\in T_{z}A
be the image of
\partial_{\theta}
under the map
dh:T_{(\theta,t)}(S^{1}\times[0,1])\to T_{z}A
.
The vector
v_{z}
is independent of
(\theta,t)
up to a scalar, and the assignment
z\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}v_{z}
defines a continuous (but typically not smooth) distribution of lines.

Pick a smooth foliation
F
of
A
which is transverse to the above distribution of lines. Such a foliation can be for example obtained by smoothing the orthogonal to the above distribution, and then integrating it to a foliation of
A
. Let
F_{\theta}\subset A
denote the leaf of
F
that intersects
\partial_{in}A
at
h(\theta,0)
.

For each
t
, the map
\theta\mapsto F_{\theta}\cap h(S^{1}\times\{t\})
is a diffeomorphism
S^{1}\to h(S^{1}\times\{0\})
, hence defines a diffeomorphism
\alpha_{t}:S^{1}\to S^{1}
.
Moreover,
\alpha_{t}
depends smoothly on
t
. Let
u\mapsto\alpha_{t,u}:[0,1]\to\Diff(S^{1})
be the ‘straight line homotopy’ connecting
\alpha_{t}
to
\id_{S^{1}}
.

Then
\{h_{u}:S^{1}\times[0,1]\to A\}_{u\in[0,1]}
given by

is a one parameter family of framings interpolating between our original framing
h
and a new framing
h_{1}
. This new framing has the property that
h_{1}(\{\theta\}\times[0,1])=F_{\theta}
.

The images under
h_{1}
of the intervals
\{\theta\}\times[0,1]
are given by the leaves of
F
, so
we may finish the proof by performing a straight line homotopy in the
[0,1]
direction between our map
h_{1}
and the constant speed parametrizations
\{\theta\}\times[0,1]\to F_{\theta}
.
This connects
h_{1}
to a new framing which is a diffeomorphism in the bulk.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

be a partially thin annulus. And let

and

are homotopic through framings.

Proof.

As in the proof of the previous lemma, let

and

be smooth foliations of

which are transverse to the one dimensional distributions associated to
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
.
By a partition of unity argument, we may furthermore arrange that

and
F_{1}
agree on a neighborhood of the thin part of

.
By the previous lemma, there then exist framings

and

which are diffeomorphisms in the bulk, along with one parameter families of framings connecting

to
h_{0}^{\prime}
, and
h_{1}
to

.
Moreover, by construction,
h_{0}^{\prime}
and
h_{1}^{\prime}
agree in a neighborhood of the thin part of

It remains to connect

and

by a one parameter families of framings.
As

That part is contained inside the union of finitely many 2-discs, and we may finish the proof by applying the classical result that any two diffeomorhpisms of a 2-disc are isotopic.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A

\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\mathrm{Exp}\big(\pi(X_{1,t})dt\big)=\mathrm{exp}\Big(\iint_{0\leq s,t\leq 1}\omega(X_{s,t},Y_{s,t})dt\Big)\Big[\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\mathrm{Exp}\big(\pi(X_{0,t})dt\big)\Big]

By uniqueness of solutions for 1st order ODEs, if
f^{\prime}(r)=\lambda(r)f(r)
, then
f(r)=\mathrm{Exp}(\int_{0\leq s\leq r}\lambda(s))f(0)

Thus, it suffices to prove:

\partial_{s}\Big(\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\mathrm{Exp}\big(\pi(X_{s,t})dt\big)\Big)=\int_{0\leq t\leq 1}\omega(X_{s,t},Y_{s,t})dt\cdot\Big[\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\mathrm{Exp}(\pi(X_{s,t})dt)\Big]

We expand (as operators

In this section, we show that the representations of

(Neretin

We will expand on what it means for the a representation

to be holomorphic after some preliminary notions.

If
{\mathbb{V}}
is a locally convex topological vector space and

is open, a function

is called holomorphic if for every

\lim_{z\to z_{0}}\frac{f(z)-f(z_{0})}{z-z_{0}}

.
More generally, if

then

is called holomorphic if it is separately holomorphic as a function of each of its
n

We will be particularly interested in holomorphic functions valued in the bounded operators
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, equipped with one of the following topologies: the norm topology, the strong operator topology (or SOT, generated by the seminorms
T\mapsto\left\|Tv\right\|
), or the weak operator topology (or WOT, generated by the seminorms
T\mapsto\left|\langle Tv,u\rangle\right|
).
As it turns out, the notion of holomorphic function into
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is independent of the choice of topology (among the ones listed above). We include a proof of this well-known fact for the convenience of the reader:

Lemma 6.1

Let

be open and let

. Then

is holomorphic with respect to the norm topology on
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
if and only if it is holomorphic with respect to the SOT, if and only if it is holomorphic with respect to the WOT.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may consider
U\subset{\mathbb{C}}
.
Holomorphicity being a local property, we may further assume that
U
is a disc, and that
f
extends holomorphically past the boundary of

We need only show that a WOT-holomorphic function

is in fact norm holomorphic.
We begin by showing that such a function is norm continuous.

Fix a point

, and a vector

For each

Recalling that

Since this holds for all

is bounded, where

As

Lemma 6.3
.

The representation
\pi:\tAnn_{c}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is Gâteaux holomorphic.

Proof.

In light of Lemma
6.1
, the conclusion of the lemma is independent of whether we equip
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
with the norm topology, SOT, or WOT; we will use the SOT.
Fix an open subset
M\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and a holomorphic family
M\to\tAnn_{c}
(denoted

) which we assume without loss of generality to be embedded in the complex plane.
By definition of the complex diffeological structure on
\tAnn_{c}
, we may (locally) choose a holomorphic family of framings

and a holomorphic function

such that

A_{m}=z(m)\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\Exp(X(t,m))\,dt

where

is the path of inward pointing vector fields associated to the framing

.
The holomorphicity condition on the framings means that the vector fields

are jointly smooth in all the variables, and that for each fixed
(t,\theta)
the function
m\mapsto X(t,m)(\theta)
is holomorphic.
In particular, this means that the map
m\mapsto X(t,m)
is holomorphic for the
C^{\infty}
topology on the space
{\mathcal{X}}(S^{1})
of smooth vector fields on
S^{1}
.

We must show for each
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
that the vectors

\pi(A_{m})\xi=z(m)\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\Exp(\pi(X(t,m)))\,dt\,\xi

depend holomorphically on

.
We assume without loss of generality that

, and begin by considering the case when
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
.
In this case, by Lemma
3.2
and Remark

, the antiholomorphic derivative in the

-th coordinate vanishes:

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{\overline{m_{i}}}\left(\prod_{1\geq t\geq 0}\Exp(\pi(X(t,m)))\,dt\right)\xi=

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\prod_{1\geq t\geq x}\Exp(\pi(X(t,m)))\,dt\big[\partial_{\overline{m_{i}}}\pi(X(t,m))\big]\prod_{x\geq t\geq 0}\Exp(\pi(X(t,m)))\,dt\right)\xi

\displaystyle=0

because the map
m\mapsto\pi(X(t,m))
is holomorphic
M\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{0})
.

Now consider a general
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and choose a sequence
\xi_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
such that
\xi_{n}\to\xi
.
The condition of pointwise-holomorphicity is local, and so by Lemma

(and the fact that

is continuous) we may assume without loss of generality that

∎

Let

\rho_{I}(\pi_{c,0}(A))
is bounded, the family
\pi_{s}(A)
is essentially bounded.

We now define
\pi_{{\mathcal{H}}}:\tAnn_{c}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
by the formula

\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(A)=\int^{\oplus}\pi_{s}(A)\,ds.

The right-hand side defines a bounded operator by the above claim, and this is evidently a representation of
\tAnn_{c}
since each
\pi_{s}
is.
Moreover we have already verified that when
A\in\Bigon_{c}(I)
we have
\rho_{I}(\pi_{c,0}(A))=\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(A)
, as required.

It remains to show that
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}
is SOT continuous and Gateaux holomorphic.
We consider continuity first.
Fix
A_{0}\in\tAnn_{c}
.
By
[
HT24
, Prop. 5.5]
, the projection
\tAnn_{c}\to\Ann
is locally trivial, so
A_{0}
has a neighborhood of the form
V=D\times U
where
U
is a neighborhood of
A_{0}
in
\Ann
and
D
is a bounded subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
.
By shrinking
U
if necessary, we may assume by Lemma
7.3
that there exist intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
and holomorphic maps
f_{j}:U\to\Bigon(I_{j})
such that for every
B\in U
we have
B=\psi f_{1}(B)f_{2}(B)
for some fixed
\psi\in\Diff(S^{1})
.
Further shrinking
U
if necessary, we may choose continuous lifts
g_{j}(B)\in\Bigon_{c}(I_{j})
such that

(z,B)=z\psi g_{1}(B)g_{2}(B),\qquad(z,B)\in V.

We then have

\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(z,B)=z\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{1}(B))\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{2}(B)).

(7.6)

Recall that we have
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{j}(B))=\rho_{I_{j}}(\pi_{c,0}(g_{j}(B)))
for
j=1,2
.
By Lemma
6.2
, we may further shrink
U
so that the images
\pi_{c,0}(g_{j}(U))
are bounded in norm.
As representations of von Neumann algebras are norm-non-increasing, it follows that the images
\rho_{I_{j}}(\pi_{c,0}(g_{j}(U))=\pi_{{\mathcal{H}}}(g_{j}(U))
are bounded in norm as well.

We claim that
B\mapsto\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{j}(B))
is continuous as a map
U\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, when
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is equipped with the SOT topology.
Indeed, using that
g_{j}
is continuous on
U
, that
\pi_{c,0}
is SOT-continuous by Theorem
6.4
, and that
\rho_{I}
is SOT-SOT continuous on bounded sets (because it’s a representation of a von Neumann algebra), it follows that

B\mapsto\rho_{I}(\pi_{c,0}(g_{j}(B)))=\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{j}(B))
is continuous.
Moreover, as multiplication is SOT-jointly continuous on bounded sets, it follows from the factorization (
7.6
) that
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(z,B)
is SOT-continuous as a function of
(z,B)
.

We now show that
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}
is (norm) Gateaux holomorphic.
Let
M\to\tAnn_{c}:m\mapsto A_{m}
be a holomorphic family, for some open subset
M
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
We fix a point
m_{0}\in M
, and assume
m_{0}=0
without loss of generality.
Shrinking
M
if necessary, we may argue as before that
A_{0}
has a neighborhood in
\tAnn_{c}
of the form
D\times U
, and we may choose
f_{j}:U\to\Bigon(I_{j})
and
\psi\in\Diff(S^{1})
such that
B=\psi f_{1}(B)f_{2}(B)
for all
B\in U
.
By
[
HT24
, Prop. 5.11]
, we may choose holomorphic lifts
g_{j}
to
\Bigon_{c}(I_{j})
of the functions
f_{j}
.
We may thus write

A_{m}=z(m)\psi g_{1}(A_{m})g_{2}(A_{m})

for some holomorphic function
z:M\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
.
Applying
\pi_{{\mathcal{H}}}
, this gives:

\pi_{{\mathcal{H}}}(A_{m})=z(m)\pi_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\psi)\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{1}(A_{m}))\pi_{{\mathcal{H}}}(g_{2}(A_{m})).

(7.7)

Recall that

\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{j}(A_{m}))=\rho_{I_{j}}(\pi_{c,0}(g_{j}(A_{m}))).

The maps
m\mapsto g_{j}(A_{m})
are holomorphic,
\pi_{c,0}
is norm Gateaux holomorphic by Theorem
6.4
, and
\rho_{I_{j}}
is norm holomorphic because it’s a bounded linear map.
Hence
m\mapsto\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{j}(A_{m}))
is holomorphic into
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
with the norm topology.
As multiplication on
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is norm holomorphic, it now follows from (
7.7
) that
\pi_{{\mathcal{H}}}(A_{m})
is holomorphic as well.
∎

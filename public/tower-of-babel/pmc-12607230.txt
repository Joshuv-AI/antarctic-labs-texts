Skin care knowledge, attitudes and practices: a comparative study between patients with atopic dermatitis and healthy individuals
Authors: Danni Wang, Xinfeng Pan, Jianfeng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAtopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin disease that significantly impacts patient quality of life and demands specific skin care practices. This study aimed to investigate the knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) related to skin care among patients with AD and the practices (P) concerning skin care among the healthy individuals.MethodsA cross-sectional survey was conducted from January 1, 2023, to January 30, 2024, at Quanzhou Hospital Affiliated to Fujian Medical University. Data were collected via questionnaires distributed to both the general population and patients with AD.ResultsThe study enrolled 305 individuals from the general population and 306 patients, all providing valid questionnaire responses. Among patients, 67.0% had adequate knowledge, 72.7% had a positive attitude, and 59.2% demonstrated proactive practice. In the general population, 62.3% achieved adequate skin care practice. Direct and indirect effects showed that, for knowledge, age (-0.237, 95% CI: -0.328 to -0.146, p < 0.001) and insurance (-0.117, 95% CI: -0.209 to -0.025, p = 0.013) had negative direct effects, whereas income (0.186, 95% CI: 0.086 to 0.285, p < 0.001) and education (0.339, 95% CI: 0.243 to 0.435, p < 0.001) showed positive direct effects. In the attitude domain, income exerted a direct effect (0.238, 95% CI: 0.134 to 0.342, p < 0.001) on attitudes, and also had an indirect effect (0.055, 95% CI: 0.020 to 0.090, p = 0.002) through other mediating variables such as age and education. Additional direct effects were noted for age, insurance, and education on attitude, with respective p-values less than 0.024. For practice, gender and knowledge exhibited strong direct effects, and education demonstrated both direct (0.175, 95% CI: 0.077 to 0.272, p < 0.001) and indirect effects (0.122, 95% CI: 0.074 to 0.169, p < 0.001). The attitudes towards practice also displayed a direct effect (0.253, 95% CI: 0.160 to 0.346, p < 0.001).ConclusionThe findings suggest that patients with AD generally possess adequate knowledge and demonstrate positive attitudes towards their condition. Both patients and the healthy individuals exhibit suboptimal practices in skin care management, highlighting the need for targeted educational interventions to improve the overall management of AD.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25264-w.

Background

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a prevalent dermatological condition, affecting up to 20% of individuals at some point in their lives [1]. Characterized by repeated inflammation, persistent itching, and skin barrier impairment, AD significantly deteriorates the quality of life for those affected [2, 3]. Epidemiological surveys conducted in China in 1998, 2002, and 2014 have demonstrated a marked increase in the prevalence of AD, rising from 0.69% to 12.94%, with a meta-analysis revealing an overall prevalence of 11% among the 0–18 age group [4, 5].

The management of AD primarily aims to alleviate the frequency, duration, and severity of symptoms through a step-wise treatment approach [6]. Central to this management strategy is patient education on effective skin care practices, which include the liberal and frequent use of moisturizers and the selection of gentle cleansers to maintain skin hydration and barrier integrity [7–9]. For moderate-to-severe cases, treatment strategies focus on restoring skin barrier function and employing drug combinations to reduce inflammation and prevent infections [10].

Moreover, proper skin care is also crucial for the general population, not just individuals with AD. Environmental factors and inadequate skin care can exacerbate or trigger various skin disorders, including common dermatitis, and impair the skin’s barrier function [11]. Moisturizers, as a fundamental component of daily skin care, play a significant role in improving skin hydration and overall skin health [12].

This study aims to investigate the knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) related to skin care among patients with AD and to compare these practices with those among the healthy individuals. The KAP model, which postulates that individual behaviors are heavily influenced by knowledge and attitudes, serves as a fundamental framework in public health to assess behavioral practices through surveys, and is crucial for understanding health-related behaviors [13, 14]. Effective skin care in AD patients is integral, not merely as an adjunct but as a core component of management that can significantly alter the disease’s trajectory. Enhancing KAP regarding skin care can reduce the frequency and severity of AD flare-ups, improve skin barrier function, and decrease the reliance on pharmacological treatments.

Moreover, proper skin care is essential not only for managing physical symptoms but also for improving the psychological wellbeing of AD patients. These individuals often face social stigma and mental health challenges due to visible skin lesions. Therefore, improving education and practices around skin care can substantially enhance both the psychosocial and physical quality of life for these patients.

By contrasting these findings with the skin care practices of the healthy individuals, the study further seeks to identify preventive measures that could be beneficial across the broader community. This comparison will ascertain whether the practices recommended for AD could also serve a preventive role in the general population, potentially informing public health guidelines and dermatological care strategies. Despite existing KAP studies on AD, none have explored this angle, thus underscoring the novelty and importance of this research in contributing to both clinical practice and public health policy.

Objectives

The primary objective of this study was to assess the levels of KAP related to skin care among patients with AD. A secondary objective was to compare the skin care practices of patients with AD to those of healthy individuals, in order to identify behavioral gaps and inform future educational and public health interventions.

Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted at the Quanzhou Hospital Affiliated to Fujian Medical University (The First Hospital of Quanzhou) from January 1 st, 2023, to January 30th, 2024. The study population consisted of patients diagnosed with atopic dermatitis and a comparison group comprising the general healthy individuals. Ethical approval for this study was granted by the Ethics Committee of Quanzhou Hospital Affiliated to Fujian Medical University (Approval No. [2022] 203), with informed consent obtained from all participants. All participants under the age of 16 involved in the survey obtained additional informed consent from their parents or legal guardians.

Inclusion criteria

Individuals were eligible if they were diagnosed with atopic dermatitis based on the Hanifin or Rajka diagnostic criteria, were aged 12 years or older, and had the ability to understand, read, and speak Chinese sufficiently to independently complete the questionnaire survey.

Exclusion criteria

Participants were excluded if they were unable to complete the questionnaire survey patiently or withdrew midway; answered the questionnaire in under 60 s; or provided responses with suspected errors that could not be rectified through telephone contact.

The sample size was calculated using the standard formula [15]:

With a confidence level of 95% (Z_1-α/2 = 1.96) and an expected proportion of 50% (p = 0.5), yielding a minimum required sample size of 384. To account for an anticipated effective response rate of 80%, the final target was set to collect at least 480 questionnaires.

Questionnaire design

The questionnaire design was informed by prior guidelines and literature [16–18]. Initial drafts were revised following feedback from two experts—one in dermatology and one in statistics. A pilot study involving 50 participants yielded a reliability coefficient of 0.791. The Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) measure for the Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice (KAP) total scale was 0.894.

The final Chinese-language questionnaire comprised four dimensions for patients: Basic information (gender, age, residence, education level, occupation, employment status, monthly household income, marital status, medical insurance status, duration of atopic dermatitis); Knowledge dimension, consisting of 13 questions with scores ranging from 0 (incorrect or unclear answer) to 13 (all correct); Attitude dimension, including eight questions rated on a five-point Likert scale from strongly agree (5 points) to strongly disagree (1 point), with scores from 8 to 40; and Practice dimension, encompassing 10 questions scored from highly positive behavior (5 points) to highly negative behavior (1 point), with a range from 10 to 50. For the general healthy individuals, the questionnaire included the same Basic information dimension and a Practice dimension. Participants scoring above 70% of the total possible points were classified as having adequate knowledge, a positive attitude, and proactive practice [19].

The recruitment of participants for this study was targeted at two distinct groups: patients with atopic dermatitis and a general healthy population. Questionnaires for patients with AD were primarily distributed at the authors’ hospital and through WeChat groups comprising AD patients. Random sampling was conducted from both the general outpatient clinic and the specialized AD clinic, where AD survey questionnaires were administered. A total of 255 responses were collected through this method. Additionally, within a WeChat group consisting of 142 AD patients, 56 questionnaires were collected (with 103 individuals aged 12 and above). After excluding three responses from individuals under the age of 12 and two due to irregularities in recording the diagnosis date, 306 valid questionnaires remained for analysis.

For the general healthy individuals, questionnaires were distributed as follows: 198 were collected from individuals undergoing cosmetic procedures in our institution’s outpatient department, such as cosmetic laser treatments and surgeries; 52 were obtained via the Questionnaire Star platform from users participating in online community forums; and 82 were sourced from a pool of 500 medical staff and healthcare workers within the institution. After removing 26 questionnaires due to excessively brief response times and one from a participant under 12, a total of 305 valid questionnaires were retained.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using STATA 18.0 (Stata Corporation, College Station, TX, USA). The distribution of scores for each dimension was initially tested for normality. For normally distributed data, continuous variables were described using mean ± standard deviation (SD); for non-normally distributed data, these were described using the median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were presented as n (%). For continuous variables that conformed to a normal distribution, between-group comparisons were performed using t-tests or ANOVA for equal variances. For non-normally distributed data, the Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test was used for two-group comparisons, and the Kruskal-Wallis test was employed for comparisons among three or more groups.

Pearson correlation analysis was employed to assess the correlations between knowledge, attitude, and practice scores for normally distributed data, whereas Spearman correlation was used for non-normally distributed data. Multivariate regression analysis utilized a 70% score distribution of the total scores as the cut-off value for categorization. Variables with a p-value less than 0.05 in univariate analysis were included in multiple regression analysis to examine the relationships between demographic data and the KAP scores. Structural equation modeling (SEM) was used to validate hypotheses concerning the effects of age, insurance, income, education on knowledge; knowledge, age, insurance, income, and education on attitude; and knowledge, attitude, age, insurance, income, education, and gender on practice. In all analyses, a two-sided p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Questionnaire overview

A total of 645 samples were collected. Among them, there were 334 samples from the general population and 311 from patient surveys.

Validity test of the questionnaire

In the pre-experiment, the Cronbach’s α coefficient for the questionnaire items was 0.791, indicating good internal consistency. During the formal experiment, both the overall scale and individual component scales showed good internal consistency, with Cronbach’s α coefficients of 0.810, 0.743, 0.721, and 0.750, respectively. The Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) value for the total KAP scale was 0.894.

Demographic information and KAP scores

Among 306 patients, 168 (54.9%) were male, 104 (34.0%) were aged 18–29 years, 171 (55.9%) lived in urban, and 164 (53.6%) had a monthly per capita income of 5,000–10,000 Yuan (Table 1). Among the 305 general population, 170 (55.7%) were female, 174 (57.0%) were aged 30–49 years, 256 (83.9%) lived in urban, and 125 (41.0%) had a monthly per capita income of 5,000–10,000 Yuan (Table 2). The patient’s median (IQR) knowledge, attitude, and practice scores were 10.00 (9.00–11.00), 28.00 (26.00–30.00), and 28.00 (25.00–31.00), respectively. The median (IQR) practice score of the general population was 29.00 (25.00–32.00). Analysis of demographic characteristics showed that patients with differences in age, residence, education, occupation, monthly per capita income, and marital status were more likely to have different knowledge, attitude, and practice scores (all P < 0.001). Younger age, urban residence, and higher education levels were significantly associated with higher KAP scores among patients (all P < 0.001). Also, patients with different work status had significantly different knowledge and practice scores (both P < 0.001). In addition, knowledge scores differed significantly across types of medical insurance (P = 0.002), and practice scores differed between male and female patients (P < 0.001). In the general population, practice scores significantly differed by gender (P < 0.001), education (P = 0.031), occupation (P = 0.027), and work status (P = 0.002).

Demographic characteristics and KAP scores in the general population

Distribution of KAP

In the knowledge dimension, the item most frequently rated as “Very well know” (99%) concerned the recommended amount of moisturizer use—specifically, applying 100 g per week for children and 250 g for adults to cover the entire body (K8). The item most commonly marked as “Heard of” (53.6%) related to the function of moisturizing sunscreens, which can both protect the skin and help repair the skin barrier (K12). In contrast, only 3.3% of participants reported awareness of the role of bathing in reducing Staphylococcus aureus colonization on the skin surface (K6), making it the least recognized item in this section (Table 3).

In summary, better KAP scores among patients were associated with younger age, urban residence, higher education, employment, and having medical insurance. Among the general population, female gender, higher education levels, being employed, and working in professional or technical roles were linked to better practice scores.

Discussion

Patients with AD tend to have sufficient knowledge and display positive attitudes toward their condition, while both patients and the general population demonstrate inadequate practices in skin care management. Given the suboptimal practices observed in both groups, tailored educational interventions are necessary.

Among patients, significant variations in KAP scores related to demographic and socioeconomic factors were evident. Younger patients exhibited superior knowledge and attitudes, a trend also observed in previous study, suggesting that younger individuals may be more receptive to health education due to higher exposure to contemporary health information channels [20]. This generation’s greater receptivity to health education could be leveraged to improve practical outcomes through targeted digital health interventions.

Urban residents and those with higher educational attainment not only recorded the highest KAP scores but also demonstrated a broader understanding of disease management, likely due to better access to healthcare resources and higher health literacy [21]. In contrast, among the general population, gender was a more prominent factor, with females demonstrating better skin care practices—consistent with broader literature on gender differences in health engagement [22].

Age and higher income levels were independently associated with better knowledge and attitudes, which could be attributed to greater health literacy and access to healthcare resources among these groups [23]. The lack of medical insurance, meanwhile, was associated with lower KAP scores, potentially reflecting barriers to accessing information and services.

The detailed path analysis in our SEM revealed that attitudes mediate the relationship between knowledge and practices, which is consistent with prior findings indicating a gap between knowledge and behavior in chronic disease self-management. This mediation effect, supported by the direct and indirect effects observed in our model, is consistent with the theory of planned behavior, which posits that attitude towards behavior significantly influences decision-making processes [24]. Additionally, education and income not only directly impacted KAP scores but also indirectly influenced them through other mediating variables such as age and insurance status. These findings suggest that improvements in educational and economic conditions could enhance overall health outcomes, corroborating previous study that advocate for integrated health promotion strategies [25].

The knowledge dimension of our study on AD patients revealed a strong understanding of certain fundamental aspects, such as the recurrent nature and significant impact of AD on quality of life, with the majority of patients well-informed about these issues. However, gaps were notable in specific areas, such as the detailed management of AD including the use of sunscreens and the psychological triggers of the disease. To address these knowledge gaps, healthcare providers should consider implementing structured educational programs that include detailed discussions on the role of sun protection and the influence of psychological factors on AD [26, 27]. Furthermore, creating patient-centered materials, such as easy-to-understand guides on everyday management of AD, could reinforce learning from consultations [28, 29].

In the attitudes dimension, our results indicate a generally positive disposition towards understanding the non-contagious nature of AD and the importance of regular moisturizer use. However, there’s a prevalent misconception regarding the role of hygiene in AD, with a significant minority still associating skin diseases with inadequate cleanliness. Some patients may still hold misconceptions about the role of hygiene and medication in AD, which should be addressed through patient education.

The practice dimension findings highlight a significant adherence to recommended bathing practices but show poor compliance with daily moisturizing and sun protection routines, underscoring a disconnection between knowledge and practice. The low engagement with regular moisturizer application and adequate sun protection might be influenced by a lack of understanding of their direct benefits or the perceived inconvenience these practices entail. Additionally, providing patients with samples of suitable moisturizers and sunscreens during clinic visits could increase familiarity and ease of use. Additionally, engaging in partnerships with skincare companies to provide discounts on hypoallergenic products could alleviate some cost barriers associated with regular use of these products [30, 31].

Limitations

This study had limitations. Firstly, the cross-sectional design of the study limits the ability to establish causal relationships between variables, thus hindering the assessment of temporal associations. Secondly, the reliance on self-reported data via questionnaires may introduce response bias and potential inaccuracies in participants’ perceptions of their knowledge, attitudes, and practices. Lastly, the study was conducted at a single center, potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings to broader populations with diverse sociodemographic and cultural backgrounds. Additionally, a substantial portion of the healthy individuals (n = 198) were recruited from those undergoing cosmetic procedures at outpatient clinics. These individuals may possess higher awareness or engagement in skin care practices compared to the general population, which could have introduced a selection bias and potentially overestimated the skin care practices among the healthy group.

Conclusion

In conclusion, patients with AD generally exhibit adequate knowledge and positive attitudes towards their condition, while both patients and the healthy individuals demonstrate suboptimal practices in skin care management. These findings underscore the critical need for targeted educational interventions and comprehensive support systems to enhance skin care practices among both patients with AD and the general population, thereby ultimately improving disease management and quality of life.

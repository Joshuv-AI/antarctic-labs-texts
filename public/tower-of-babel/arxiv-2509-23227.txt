Global energy minimizers for a diffusion-aggregation model on sphere
Razvan C. Fetecau, Hansol Park, Vishnu Vaidya

Abstract
. We investigate the ground states of a free energy functional on sphere. The energy consists of an entropy and a nonlocal interaction term that are in competition with each other, as they favour spreading and aggregation, respectively. Specifically, the entropy corresponds to slow nonlinear diffusion and the interaction term is modeled by a quadratic interaction potential. We investigate the transitions that occur in the equilibria and the global minimizers of the energy, in terms of the strength of the nonlocal attractive interactions. We consider separately various ranges of the diffusion exponent, which give qualitatively different behaviours of equilibria and ground states. In terms of applications, we note that the energy we consider here is a generalization to nonlinear diffusion of the Onsager free energy with dipolar potential, used to study phase transitions in polymer orientation.

Introduction

In this paper we investigate the minimizers of the free energy functional

(1.1)

E[\rho]=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho(x)^{m}\mathrm{d}S(x)+\frac{\kappa}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}}\|x-y\|^{2}\rho(x)\rho(y)\mathrm{d}S(x)\mathrm{d}S(y),

defined on the space
\mathcal{P}_{ac}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
of absolutely continuous probability measures

In the present paper we consider the case

(1.4)

M=\mathbb{S}^{d},\qquad\text{ and }\quad W(x,y)=\frac{\kappa}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

The interactions corresponding to this potential are purely
attractive
, i.e., any two points experience a pairwise attractive interaction. Consequently, the interaction energy favours points to aggregate together. On the other hand, the entropy component favours spreading, so the two components of the energy (
1.1

Together with the attractive interactions, diffusion may still dominate (and lead to uniform states) if the attraction is sufficiently weak. The main interest in this paper is to study the competition between attraction and diffusion in terms of the size of the interaction strength
\kappa

Apart from its own intrinsic interest as an aggregation-diffusion energy on the sphere, one of the main motivations for this work is to consider nonlinear diffusion in the Onsager free energy on the sphere

In this paper we work with general exponent

We also note that on top of the numerous applications to self-collective behaviour in sciences and engineering, there have been recent interest in such models in the context of inverse problems and machine learning

2.
Critical points of the energy

In this section we identify the critical points of the energy functional, which are the candidates for ground states. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, we have

\|x-y\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle+\|y\|^{2}=2-2\langle x,y\rangle,

which allows to simplify the energy as

(2.5)

E[\rho]=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho(x)^{m}\mathrm{d}S(x)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}}\langle x,y\rangle\rho(x)\rho(y)\mathrm{d}S(x)\mathrm{d}S(y)+\frac{\kappa}{2}.

The centre of mass
c_{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
of a density
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{ac}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
is defined as

(2.6)

c_{\rho}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x).

It holds that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|c_{\rho}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x),\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}y\rho(y)\mathrm{d}S(y)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}}\langle x,y\rangle\rho(x)\rho(y)\mathrm{d}S(x)\mathrm{d}S(y),

and hence, the energy (
1.1
) can be written as

(2.8)

E[\rho]=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho(x)^{m}\mathrm{d}S(x)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\|c_{\rho}\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}.

The Euler–Lagrange equation for the functional (
2.8
) is given by

(2.9)

\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\right)\rho(x)^{m-1}-\kappa\langle c_{\rho},x\rangle=\lambda,\qquad x\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho),

for some constant
\lambda
. We first note that the uniform distribution on the sphere,

(2.10)

\rho_{\text{uni}}(x)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{d},

where
|\mathbb{S}^{d}|
denotes the area of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, is a solution of (
2.9
) for all
\kappa>0
. Indeed, in this case,
c_{\rho_{\text{uni}}}=0
, and the constant
\lambda
is given by

(2.11)

\lambda_{\text{uni}}=\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\right)\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{m-1}}.

Our main goal is to identify the global energy minimizers, among the critical points satisfying (
2.9
). The global minimizers must be radially symmetric, a property that can be inferred from the following argument. Consider a global minimizer
\rho
, and take a rotation
R:\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{S}^{d}
that preserves its centre of mass
c_{\rho}
. Note that the center of
\rho^{\prime}=R_{\#}\rho
is also
c_{\rho}
(i.e.,
c_{\rho^{\prime}}=c_{\rho}
), and
E[\rho^{\prime}]=E[\rho]
. Then, any convex combination of
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
,

\rho_{\alpha}=(1-\alpha)\rho+\alpha\rho^{\prime},\qquad\text{ for }0<\alpha<1,

yields

E[\rho_{\alpha}]<(1-\alpha)E[\rho]+\alpha E[\rho^{\prime}]=E[\rho],

whenever
\rho\neq\rho^{\prime}
; here we used the strict convexity of the entropy and the fact that
c_{\rho}=c_{\rho^{\prime}}=c_{\rho_{\alpha}}
for all
0<\alpha<1
. Therefore, to be a global minimizer, we need
\rho=\rho^{\prime}

and hence,
\rho
is invariant under rotations that preserve its centre of mass. We infer that any global minimizer
\rho
must have rotational symmetry.

We now investigate the radially symmetric solutions of (
2.9
). We find that (
2.9
) admits two types of solutions: equlibria supported on the entire sphere (
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)=\mathbb{S}^{d}
), and equilibria that are supported on a strict subset of the sphere (
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subsetneq\mathbb{S}^{d}
). Write the equilibria as

\rho(x)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(\lambda+\kappa\langle c_{\rho},x\rangle\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}},\quad&\text{for }x\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho),\\[5.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that for
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho)
,
\lambda+\kappa\langle c_{\rho},x\rangle\geq 0
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
c_{\rho}=\|c_{\rho}\|x_{0}
for some unit vector
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Define
\theta_{x}=\arccos\langle x_{0},x\rangle\in[0,\pi]
, to write
\langle c_{\rho},x\rangle=\|c_{\rho}\|\cos\theta_{x}
. The condition
\lambda+\kappa\langle c_{\rho},x\rangle\geq 0
on
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)
can be simplified into

(2.12)

-\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|}\leq\cos\theta_{x},\qquad\forall x\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho).

Given that
\cos\theta_{x}
ranges in
[-1,1]
, we now distinguish the two types equilibria, in terms of the relative size of
\lambda
. Note that (
2.12
) has no solutions if
\lambda\leq-\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|
.

a. Equilibria fully supported on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
(
\lambda\geq\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|)
.
In this case
-\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|}\leq-1
, so
\theta_{x}
has full range
[0,\pi]
. The equilibrium is given by

(2.13)

\rho(x)=\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(\lambda+\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|\cos\theta_{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}.

To determine
\rho
one needs to find
\lambda
and
\|c_{\rho}\|
. These can be found by requiring that
\rho
has unit mass and centre of mass at
c_{\rho}
, as following.

From the definition of
c_{\rho}
we compute

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|c_{\rho}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle c_{\rho},\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\langle c_{\rho},x\rangle\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)

\displaystyle=dw_{d}\|c_{\rho}\|\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos\theta\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(\lambda+\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|\cos\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,

where for the last equal sign we used hyperspherical coordinates and that
|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|=dw_{d}
, where
w_{d}
denotes the volume of the
d
-dimensional unit ball. Together with the unit mass condition, this leads to the following two equations to be solved for
\lambda
and
\|c_{\rho}\|
:

(2.15)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=dw_{d}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(\lambda+\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|\cos\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

\displaystyle\|c_{\rho}\|

\displaystyle=dw_{d}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(\lambda+\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|\cos\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

b. Equilibria supported on a strict subset of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
(
-\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|<\lambda<\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|
).
In this case we have
-1<-\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|}<1
, and (
2.12
) restricts
\theta_{x}
to
\left[0,\arccos\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|}\right)\right]
. The equilibrium
\rho
can be expressed as

(2.16)

\rho(x)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(\lambda+\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|\cos\theta_{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}},\qquad&\text{ if }0\leq\theta_{x}\leq\arccos\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|}\right),\\[5.0pt]
0,\qquad&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Again,
\lambda
and
\|c_{\rho}\|
need to be found. Denote by
\phi=\arccos\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|}\right)
. Then, similar to how system (
2.15
) was derived, we find in this case:

(2.17)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=dw_{d}\int_{0}^{\phi}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(\lambda+\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|\cos\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

\displaystyle\|c_{\rho}\|

\displaystyle=dw_{d}\int_{0}^{\phi}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(\lambda+\kappa\|c_{\rho}\|\cos\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

which has to be solved for
\lambda
and
\|c_{\rho}\|
.

Remark 2.1
.

We note that for the free Onsager functional with linear diffusion, all critical points are fully supported on the sphere
[
16
,
25
]
. In considering nonlinear diffusion, the set of admisible equilibria is richer, which leads to more sophisticated phase transitions, in particular in the case
m>2
.

3.
Stability of the uniform distribution

In this section we will study the full nonlinear stability of the uniform distribution
\rho_{\text{uni}}
given by (
2.10
). Consider a general family of perturbations
\{\rho^{\epsilon}\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{ac}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
of the uniform distribution. In particular, we have

(3.18)

\rho^{0}=\rho_{\text{uni}},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)=1,\quad\text{ for all }\epsilon.

The energy of
\rho^{\epsilon}
is given by (use (
2.5
)):

E[\rho^{\epsilon}]=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho^{\epsilon}(x)^{m}\mathrm{d}S(x)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}}\langle x,y\rangle\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\rho^{\epsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}S(x)\mathrm{d}S(y)+\frac{\kappa}{2}.

The uniform distribution is a local minimizer provided

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}E[\rho^{\epsilon}]_{\big|_{\epsilon=0}}\geq 0,

for all perturbations
\rho^{\epsilon}
that satisfy (
3.18
).

Denote

\delta\rho^{\epsilon}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\rho^{\epsilon},\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\delta^{2}\rho^{\epsilon}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}\rho^{\epsilon}.

Note that by the unit mass condition in (
3.18
), we have

(3.19)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)=0,\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\delta^{2}\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)=0,\qquad\textrm{ for all }\epsilon.

Now compute:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}E[\rho^{\epsilon}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}m\rho^{\epsilon}(x)^{m-1}\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}}\langle x,y\rangle\left(\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\rho^{\epsilon}(y)+\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}S(x)\mathrm{d}S(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}m\rho^{\epsilon}(x)^{m-1}\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)-\kappa\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}}\langle x,y\rangle\,\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}S(x)\mathrm{d}S(y),

and

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}E[\rho^{\epsilon}]

\displaystyle=m\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho^{\epsilon}(x)^{m-2}\left(\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)+\frac{m}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho^{\epsilon}(x)^{m-1}\delta^{2}\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)

\displaystyle-\kappa\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}}\langle x,y\rangle(\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\delta\rho^{\epsilon}(y)+\rho^{\epsilon}(x)\delta^{2}\rho^{\epsilon}(y))\mathrm{d}S(x)\mathrm{d}S(y)

Evaluate (
3.20
) at
\epsilon=0
. Using that
\rho^{0}(x)
is constant and that
\delta^{2}\rho^{\epsilon}(x)
integrates to
0
, we find that the second term in the r.h.s. vanishes:

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho^{0}(x)^{m-1}\delta^{2}\rho^{0}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{m-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\delta^{2}\rho^{0}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)=0.

Also,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\rho^{0}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)=0,

and hence,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}}\langle x,y\rangle\rho^{0}(x)\delta^{2}\rho^{0}(y)\mathrm{d}S(x)\mathrm{d}S(y)

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\rho^{0}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x),\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}y\,\delta^{2}\rho^{0}(x)\mathrm{d}S(y)\bigg\rangle=0.

Now drop the superscript
0
and denote
\delta\rho=\delta\rho^{\epsilon}_{\,|\epsilon=0}
. We find from (
3.20
):

(3.21)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}E[\rho^{\epsilon}]_{\big|{\epsilon=0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{m-2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\delta\rho(x)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)-\kappa\left\|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\delta\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)\right\|^{2}.

For the uniform density to be a local minimum, the expression above has to be positive, i.e.,

\kappa<\frac{m}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{m-2}}\cdot\frac{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\delta\rho(x)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)}{\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\delta\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)\right|^{2}},

for all perturbations
\delta\rho
that have zero mass.

Define the following functional on
\mathcal{A}=\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d}):\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)=0,\psi\nequiv 0\}
:

(3.22)

\mathcal{F}[\psi]=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\psi(x)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)}{\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\psi(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)\right|^{2}}.

The uniform distribution is a local minimum (and hence, stable) provided

(3.23)

\kappa<\frac{m}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{m-2}}\cdot\inf_{\psi\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{F}[\psi],

and unstable otherwise.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by (
3.22
). Then,

\inf_{\psi\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{F}[\psi]=\frac{d+1}{\mathbb{S}^{d}}.

Proof.

We will present a direct proof, though this result can also be inferred from the known facts on the free energy with linear diffusion – see Remark
3.1
.

By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we see immediately that
\mathcal{F}
is bounded below by
1
. Also,
\mathcal{F}
is lower-semicontinuous and
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is compact, from which we infer that
\mathcal{F}
admits a global minimizer in
\mathcal{A}
.

Denote by
s_{\psi}
the centre of mass of
\psi
:

s_{\psi}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\psi(x)\mathrm{d}S(x).

By calculating the first variation and setting it to zero, i.e.,

\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}[\psi](\delta\psi)=0,

we find the following Euler-Lagrange equation for the critical points:

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\psi(x)|s_{\psi}|^{2}-\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\psi^{2}(y)\mathrm{d}S(y)\right)s_{\psi}\cdot x\right)\delta\psi(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)=0,

for all perturbations
\delta\psi\in\mathcal{A}
. From here we find

(3.24)

\psi(x)|s_{\psi}|^{2}-\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\psi^{2}(y)\mathrm{d}S(y)\right)s_{\psi}\cdot x=\lambda,

for a constant
\lambda
. By integrating this equation over
\mathbb{S}^{d}
on both sides, and using that
\psi
integrates to
0
, we find
\lambda=0
.

Consider a critical point
\bar{\psi}
and denote
C:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\bar{\psi}^{2}(y)\mathrm{d}y
. From (
3.24
) (where
\lambda=0
), we can write

\bar{\psi}(x)=\frac{C}{|s_{\bar{\psi}}|^{2}}\;s_{\bar{\psi}}\cdot x.

By taking the square of this expression and integrating, we find

C=\frac{|s_{\bar{\psi}}|^{4}}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(s_{\bar{\psi}}\cdot x)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)},

and hence,

(3.25)

\bar{\psi}(x)=\frac{|s_{\bar{\psi}}|^{4}}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(s_{\bar{\psi}}\cdot x)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)}\;s_{\bar{\psi}}\cdot x.

All the critical points of
\mathcal{F}
are in the form above. Evaluate
\mathcal{F}
at such
\bar{\psi}
to find

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\bar{\psi}]

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{|s_{\bar{\psi}}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|s_{\bar{\psi}}|^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(s_{\bar{\psi}}\cdot x)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)}.

Assume by symmetry that
s_{\bar{\psi}}
is in the direction of the fixed unit vector
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and use the hyperspherial coordinates used previously (
\theta
denotes the angle between
x
and
s_{\bar{\psi}}
). Then we get:

\mathcal{F}[\bar{\psi}]=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}.

The expression above can be simplified by using:

(3.26)

\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{1}{d+1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,

and

|\mathbb{S}^{d}|=|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,

to write

(3.27)

\mathcal{F}[\bar{\psi}]=\frac{d+1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|}.

Note that
\mathcal{F}
is invariant under scalar multiplication
\psi\to\lambda\psi
, which is reflected in the fact that the value of
\mathcal{F}[\bar{\psi}]
does not depend on the magnitude of
s_{\bar{\psi}}
. Put it differently, up to symmetry and a scalar multiplication, the critical points in the form (
3.25
) are unique. Since
\mathcal{F}
admits a global minimizer,
\bar{\psi}
must necessarily achieve this mimimum value.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Lemma
3.1
also follows directly from the known results for linear diffusion. Indeed, consider the free energy with linear diffusion, which can be simplified and written as

E_{1}[\rho]=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho(x)\log\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)\right\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}.

By taking perturbations
\rho^{\epsilon}
of the uniform distribution and performing a similar calculation as for
m\neq 1
, we calculate

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}E_{1}[\rho^{\epsilon}]_{\big|{\epsilon=0}}=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\delta\rho(x)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)-\kappa\left\|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}x\delta\rho(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)\right\|^{2},

which is in fact (
3.21
) for
m=1
. Since the uniform distribution is a global energy minimizer for
\kappa\leq d+1

[
16
,
25
]
, the second variation calculated above needs to be non-negative, for all
\delta\rho\in\mathcal{A}
. Hence,
|\mathbb{S}^{d}|\mathcal{F}[\delta\rho]\geq\kappa
, for all
\delta\rho\in\mathcal{A}
and
\kappa\geq d+1
, from which we conclude

\inf_{\psi\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{F}[\psi]\geq\frac{d+1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|}.

The considerations above lead to the following result.

Proposition 3.1

(Stability of the uniform distribution)
.

The uniform distribution
\rho_{\text{uni}}
is a stable critical point of the energy functional
E
if and only if
\kappa<\kappa_{1}
, where

(3.28)

\kappa_{1}:=\frac{m(d+1)}{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{m-1}}.

Proof.

The proof follows from the previous calculations, by using Lemma
3.1
in (
3.23
).
∎

.

The function

Proof.

The proof is based on a technical and fairly involved result that uses associated Legendre functions of the first kind and the integral form of the Chebyshev inequality. This technical result is stated and proved in Appendix

. The reader should first check Appendix

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\left[(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\times\frac{1}{d}\sin^{d}\theta\right]_{0}^{\pi}-\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{1}{m-1}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}(-\sin\theta)\times\frac{1}{d}\sin^{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=-\frac{2-m}{m-1}\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-2}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}\right)

Using the notation (
A.104
) in Appendix

\frac{H^{\prime}(\eta)}{H(\eta)}=-\left(\frac{m-2}{m-1}\right)\frac{\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(-\eta)}{\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)}.

Finally, we apply Lemma
A.1
in Appendix

to get

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}\left(\frac{H^{\prime}(\eta)}{H(\eta)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{sgn}\left(-\left(\frac{m-2}{m-1}\right)\frac{\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(-\eta)}{\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{sgn}\left(\left(\frac{m-2}{m-1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{m-1}+\frac{d-1}{2}\right)\right).

Here, we used that both
\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta
and
\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta
are positive. From

and

which proves the result.

Remark 4.2

(Global energy minimizers for

)

Proof.

Recall the notations

) and (

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2}\|c_{\rho_{\kappa}}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho_{\kappa}^{m}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{2(m-1)(d\omega_{d})^{m-1}}\frac{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta_{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta_{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta)^{m}}

(4.43)

\displaystyle g_{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle g_{1}^{\prime}(\eta)

) we then find

(4.46)

\displaystyle(g_{1}(\eta)g_{2}(\eta))^{\prime}

\displaystyle=g_{1}^{\prime}(\eta)g_{2}(\eta)+g_{1}(\eta)g_{2}^{\prime}(\eta)

(4.47)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{m}{m-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{2(m-1)(d\omega_{d})^{m-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{m}}\times

(4.48)

\displaystyle\quad\biggl(-2\eta+\frac{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}

(4.49)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{m\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta-2\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{m}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}\biggr).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{m}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle(g_{1}(\eta)g_{2}(\eta))^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{m}{m-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{2(m-1)(d\omega_{d})^{m-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{m}}\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}+\frac{(m-2)\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}\right).

where in the integrals we change variables

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

(4.55)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

(4.56)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq 0.

) and

can take any value in the range

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\frac{dw(d)}{2}\int_{0}^{\phi}(\lambda+\kappa s\cos\theta)\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\frac{dw(d)}{2}\int_{0}^{\phi}(\lambda+\kappa s\cos\theta)\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta.

By substituting

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\frac{dw(d)}{2}\;\kappa s\left(-\cos\phi\int_{0}^{\phi}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{0}^{\phi}\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right).

By noting that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\phi}\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta-\cos\phi\int_{0}^{\phi}\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{\phi}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,

Energy minimizers

The global energy minimizers for the case

are presented in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1

(Global energy minimizers for
m=2
)
.

Let
m=2
and
d\geq 1
. The global minimizer of (
1.1
) on
\mathcal{P}_{ac}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
is

(1)

the uniform distribution
\rho_{\text{uni}}
when
\kappa<\kappa_{1}
,

(2)

any equilibrium of the form (
5.76
) with
s\in\left[0,\frac{1}{d+1}\right]
, when
\kappa=\kappa_{1}
,

(3)

the equilibrium
\rho_{\kappa}
given by (
5.77
) when
\kappa>\kappa_{1}
.

Moreover, the energy difference
E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]-E[\rho_{\kappa}]
increases with
\kappa
for
\kappa>\kappa_{1}
.

Proof.

For
\kappa<\kappa_{1}
,
\rho_{\text{uni}}
is the only equilibrium, and hence the global minimizer. We will next discuss the cases
\kappa=\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa>\kappa_{1}
.

Case
\kappa=\kappa_{1}
.
Consider a generic equilibrium in the form (
5.76
), corresponding to
\kappa=\kappa_{1}
and some
\displaystyle 0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{d+1}
(see the vertical segment in Figure
4
(a)), and compute its energy.

The entropy can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho_{\kappa_{1}}(x;s)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{dw_{d}}{4}\int_{0}^{\pi}(\lambda+\kappa_{1}s\cos\theta)^{2}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{dw_{d}}{4}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\lambda^{2}\sin^{d-1}\theta+\kappa_{1}^{2}s^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{d-1}\theta\right)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{dw_{d}}{4}\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}^{2}}{(d+1)^{2}}+\kappa_{1}^{2}s^{2}\frac{1}{d+1}\right)\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,

where for the last equality we used (
3.26
) and that
\lambda=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{d+1}
(see (
5.68
) and (
5.70
)).
Also, using the expression of
\kappa_{1}
from (
5.70
), and the fact that
|\mathbb{S}^{d}|=dw_{d}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta
, we can write the above as

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho_{\kappa_{1}}(x;s)^{2}\mathrm{d}S(x)=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2(d+1)}+\frac{\kappa_{1}s^{2}}{2}.

The interaction energy (see (
2.8
)) is given by

-\frac{\kappa_{1}s^{2}}{2}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2}.

By combining the two components, we find the total energy

E[\rho_{\kappa_{1}}(\cdot,s)]=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2(d+1)}+\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2}=\frac{\kappa_{1}(d+2)}{2(d+1)}.

Note that the energy does not depend on
s
. It means that when
\kappa=\kappa_{1}
, each equilibrium in the form (
5.76
) (with
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{d+1}
) is a global energy minimizer.

Case
\kappa>\kappa_{1}
.
One can show that
E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]>E[\rho_{\kappa}]
in the same way as this was shown for Theorem (
4.1
) (see Remark
4.3
) in the case
\kappa>\kappa_{2}
; recall that
m=2
is a degenerate case where
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}
. Indeed, following the same calculations as in the proof of Theorem (
4.1
) (case
\kappa>\kappa_{2}
) using
m=2
– see (
4.57
) and the calculations that follow, the desired conclusion results from the monotonicity of the function
F(\phi)
.

Though not covered by Lemma
4.3
, the monotonicity of the function
F(\phi)
for
m=2
is immediate. Indeed, for
m=2
(
4.36
) reduces to

(5.78)

F(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}dw_{d}\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)\sin^{d-1}\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,

which further simplifies to (see calculation leading to (
5.74
)):

(5.79)

F(\phi)=\frac{w(d)}{2}\int_{0}^{\phi}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta.

To conclude,
F(\phi)
is strictly increasing with
\phi

E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]-E[\rho_{\kappa}]
decreases with

\kappa

\kappa_{1}
to

\pi

\phi_{\kappa}

\kappa

E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]-E[\rho_{\kappa}]
increases with
\kappa

\kappa>\kappa_{1}

Lemma 6.1

). Combined with the previous observation (for the range

to

b)
\rho_{\kappa,1}
(

Theorem 6.1

(Global energy minimizers for

,

)
.

m>2

\kappa
(which we denote by

), with

the uniform distribution

(2)

the equilibrium
\rho_{\kappa,1}

for

Proof.

The considerations we made before listing the theorem imply that

is more energetically favourable than
\rho_{\kappa,2}
for all

. Also,
\rho_{\text{uni}}
is more energetically favourable than
\rho_{\kappa,1}
at
\kappa=\kappa_{3}
, and the difference

increases for
\kappa>\kappa_{3}
(but we have not yet quantified how). These behaviours can be observed in Figure
8
; note that for a better visualization we separated the energy plots in three parts, for different ranges of
\kappa
. To establish which equilibrium is the global minimizer we still have to compare
E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]
and
E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
, and
E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
and
E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]
. We will do this separately.

Compare
E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]
and
E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
.
We use (
4.40
) and (
4.57
) to write

(6.83)

E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\rho_{\text{uni}}^{m}(x)\mathrm{d}S(x)+\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,1})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,1}).

Also recall that
\phi_{\kappa,1}
decreases to
0
as
\kappa
increases to
\infty
. For this reason, we will check the behaviour of
\bar{g}_{1}(\phi)\bar{g}_{2}(\phi)
as
\phi
tends to zero.

Write
\bar{g}_{1}(\phi)
from (
4.58
) (with
d=2
) as

(6.84)

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{1}(\phi)

\displaystyle=(m-2)\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

(6.85)

\displaystyle\quad+2\cos\phi\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta.

Then, using
m>2
and (
4.61
) we get

(6.86)

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{1}(\phi)\bar{g}_{2}(\phi)

(6.87)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(m-2)\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta+2\cos\phi\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{2(m-1)(d\omega_{d})^{m-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{m}}

(6.88)

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{2\cos\phi\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{2(m-1)(d\omega_{d})^{m-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{m}}

(6.89)

\displaystyle\quad=C\,\frac{\cos\phi}{\left(\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{m-1}},

where
C
is a positive constant.

As the r.h.s. above tends to
\infty
as
\phi\to 0
, we infer that

\lim_{\phi\to 0}\bar{g}_{1}(\phi)\bar{g}_{2}(\phi)=\infty.

The limit
\phi\to 0
corresponds to
\kappa\to\infty
. Hence,
E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
is negative at
\kappa=\kappa_{3}
, increases on
(\kappa_{3},\infty)
, and approaches
\infty
as
\kappa\to\infty
. We conclude that there is a fourth critical value, which we denote by
k_{c}
such that
E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]=E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
at
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
. Also,
E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]<E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
for
\kappa_{3}<\kappa<\kappa_{c}
, and
E[\rho_{\text{uni}}]>E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
for
\kappa>\kappa_{c}
– see Figure
8
, in particular plot (b) and its inset.

Compare
E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
and
E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]
.
We will consider two cases: a)
\kappa_{3}<\kappa<\kappa_{2}
, and b)
\kappa_{2}<\kappa<\kappa_{1}
.

Case a)
\kappa_{3}<\kappa<\kappa_{2}
.
We use (
2.8
) and (
4.57
), to write

(6.90)

E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]=\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,1})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,1})-\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,2})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,2}).

We will show that
E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
increases as
\kappa
increases from
\kappa_{3}
to
\kappa_{2}
.

Compute the derivative of
\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,i})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,i})
with respect to
\kappa
, for
i=1,2
. By chain rule,

(6.91)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\ d\kappa}(\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,i})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,i}))=\frac{\mathrm{d}(\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,i})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,i}))}{\mathrm{d}\phi_{\kappa,i}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{\kappa,i}}{\mathrm{d}\kappa}.

Now differentiate

F(\phi_{\kappa,i})^{-1}=\kappa,

with respect to
\kappa
, to get

-\frac{F^{\prime}(\phi_{\kappa,i})}{F^{2}(\phi_{\kappa,i})}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{\kappa,i}}{\mathrm{d}\kappa}=1.

From the above we get

(6.92)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{\kappa,i}}{\mathrm{d}\kappa}=-\frac{F^{2}(\phi_{\kappa,i})}{F^{\prime}(\phi_{\kappa,i})}.

By (
4.59
), we also have

(6.93)

\frac{\mathrm{d}(\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,i})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,i}))}{\mathrm{d}\phi_{\kappa,i}}=-(m-1)\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa,i})\frac{F^{\prime}(\phi_{\kappa,i})}{F(\phi_{\kappa,i})},

with
\bar{h}
given by (
4.60
). Then, by combining (
6.91
), (
6.92
) and (
6.93
), we find

(6.94)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\ d\kappa}(\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,i})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,i}))

\displaystyle=(m-1)\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa,i})F(\phi_{\kappa,i})

\displaystyle=(m-1)\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa,i})\kappa^{-1}.

The equation above holds for
i=1,2
. Now, from (
6.90
) and (
6.94
) we get

(6.95)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\kappa}\left(E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]\right)=\kappa^{-1}(m-1)\left(\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa,1})-\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa,2})\right).

Note that at each
\kappa
fixed, we have
\phi_{\kappa,1}<\phi_{\kappa,2}
. If we show that
\bar{h}(\phi)
is decreasing in
\phi
, then together with (
6.95
), we infer that
E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
increases with
\kappa
.

We will find that
\bar{h}^{\prime}(\phi)<0
by a direct calculation. By (
4.60
) with
d=2
, write

(6.96)

\bar{h}(\phi)=\bar{C}\;\frac{\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{\left(\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{m}},

where
\bar{C}>0
is a constant. Both the numerator and the denominator above can be computed exactly. Indeed, by direct integration we find

\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{m-1}{2m-1}(1-\cos\phi)^{\frac{2m-1}{m-1}}+\frac{m-1}{m}\cos\phi(1-\cos\phi)^{\frac{m}{m-1}},

and

\int_{0}^{\phi}(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{m-1}{m}(1-\cos\phi)^{\frac{m}{m-1}},

and hence, by redenoting the constant
\bar{C}
several times to include new constant terms, we have

\displaystyle\bar{h}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\bar{C}\;\frac{\frac{1}{2m-1}(1-\cos\phi)^{\frac{2m-1}{m-1}}+\frac{1}{m}\cos\phi(1-\cos\phi)^{\frac{m}{m-1}}}{(1-\cos\phi)^{\frac{m^{2}}{m-1}}}

\displaystyle=\bar{C}\;\left(\frac{1}{2m-1}(1-\cos\phi)^{1-m}+\frac{1}{m}\cos\phi(1-\cos\phi)^{-m}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{C}(1-\cos\phi)^{-m}(m+(m-1)\cos\phi).

Then, compute

\displaystyle{\bar{h}}^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle=-\bar{C}\sin\phi(1-\cos\phi)^{-m}\left(\frac{m(m+(m-1)\cos\phi)}{1-\cos\phi}+m-1\right)

\displaystyle=-\bar{C}\sin\phi(1-\cos\phi)^{-m-1}\left(m^{2}+m-1+(m-1)^{2}\cos\phi\right)

Finally, we have

\displaystyle m^{2}+m-1+(m-1)^{2}\cos\phi

\displaystyle\geq m^{2}+m-1-(m-1)^{2}

\displaystyle=3m-2,

and hence in the regime
m>2
,
{\bar{h}}^{\prime}(\phi)<0
.

Case b)
\kappa_{2}<\kappa<\kappa_{1}
.

In this range of
\kappa
,
\rho_{\kappa,2}
is fully supported on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. We use (
2.8
), (
4.41
) and (
4.57
) to write

(6.97)

E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]=\bar{g}_{1}(\phi_{\kappa,1})\bar{g}_{2}(\phi_{\kappa,1})-{g}_{1}(\eta_{\kappa}){g}_{2}(\eta_{\kappa}).

We will show again that
E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
increases as
\kappa
increases from
\kappa_{2}
to
\kappa_{1}
.

From computations similar to those leading to (
6.92
) and (
6.94
), we get

\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta_{\kappa}}{\mathrm{d}\kappa}=-\frac{H^{2}(\eta_{\kappa})}{H^{\prime}(\eta_{\kappa})},

and by chain rule,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\kappa}\left(g_{1}(\eta_{\kappa})g_{2}(\eta_{\kappa})\right)=(m-1)h(\eta_{\kappa})\kappa^{-1},

with
h
given by (
4.53
). Then, together with (
6.94
), we write (
6.97
) as

(6.98)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\kappa}\left(E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]\right)=\kappa^{-1}(m-1)\left(\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa,1})-h(\eta_{\kappa})\right).

By (
4.53
) with
d=2
, we have

(6.99)

h(\eta)=C\,\frac{\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta}{\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{m}},

for
C>0
constant. Inspect first the numerator

f(\eta):=\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta.

Compute the derivative:

(6.100)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\eta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta.

We will show that
\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\leq 0
, and hence
f^{\prime}(\eta)\geq 0
. Note first that as
m>2
,
\frac{1}{m-1}-1<0
.
Then, for any
0\leq\tilde{\theta}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, we have:
\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\tilde{\theta}\right)-\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\leq\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\tilde{\theta}\right)-\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}
(by the monotonicity of cosine and

),
\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\tilde{\theta}\right)=\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\tilde{\theta}\right)
and

. From these relations, we infer

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\leq-\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta,

where in the integrals we change variables

and

respectively. Hence, we find

(6.101)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

(6.102)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin\theta\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\theta

(6.103)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 0.

It is also immediate that
\bar{f}(\eta):=\frac{1}{\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cos\theta-\eta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{m}}
is an increasing function of
\eta
, for

. Therefore, as

is a product of two increasing functions, it is also increasing. Recall that
\eta_{\kappa}
decreases from
-1
to

as
\kappa
increases from
\kappa_{2}
to
\kappa_{1}
. So overall,

decreases as
\kappa
increases from
\kappa_{2}
to
\kappa_{1}
, and in particular we have

h(-1)\geq h(\eta_{\kappa}),\qquad\text{ for all }\kappa_{2}<\kappa<\kappa_{1}.

On the other hand, by the monotonicity of
\bar{h}
(established as in case a)), we have

\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa,1})>\bar{h}(\phi_{{\kappa_{2}},1}),\qquad\text{ for all }\kappa_{2}<\kappa<\kappa_{1},

and case a), at
\kappa=\kappa_{2}
it holds that

\bar{h}(\phi_{{\kappa_{2}},1})>\bar{h}(\phi_{{\kappa_{2}},2}).

Finally, observe that

and

\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa_{2},2})=h(-1)
, and by combining the inequalities above we find

\bar{h}(\phi_{\kappa,1})>h(\eta_{\kappa}),\qquad\text{ for all }\kappa_{2}<\kappa<\kappa_{1}.

From this result and (
6.98
), we infer that
E[\rho_{\kappa,2}]-E[\rho_{\kappa,1}]
increases with
\kappa
on

The comparison of energies above provides the global minimum as in the statement of the theorem. Finally, using the fact that

and

, we infer that

at

. Hence, the fourth critical value

(where

changes sign) is in the range

.

Lemma A.1
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(\xi)=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\xi+\cos\theta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\xi+\cos\theta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\xi+\cos\theta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-2}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}(\xi+\cos\theta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\right).

Then, we distinguish the following cases:

(1)

\frac{1}{m-1}+\frac{d-1}{2}<0
then
\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(\xi)>0
for all

(2)

\frac{1}{m-1}+\frac{d-1}{2}=0

(3)

In other words, we have

\mathrm{sgn}(\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(\xi))=-\mathrm{sgn}\left(\frac{1}{m-1}+\frac{d-1}{2}\right),\qquad\text{ for all }\xi>1.

Proof.

From Section 3.666, equation (2) of
[

]
, we have:

\int_{0}^{\pi}(\cosh\beta+\sinh\beta\cos\theta)^{\mu+\nu}\sin^{-2\nu}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{\nu}}\sinh^{\nu}\beta~\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\nu\right)P_{\mu}^{\nu}(\cosh\beta)\quad\forall\mathrm{Re}(\nu)<\frac{1}{2},

where

denotes the associated Legendre function of the first kind. By factoring out

(A.105)

\int_{0}^{\pi}(\coth\beta+\cos\theta)^{\mu+\nu}\sin^{-2\nu}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{\nu}}\sinh^{-\mu}\beta~\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\nu\right)P_{\mu}^{\nu}(\cosh\beta).

Denote
\mu_{0}=\frac{1}{m-1}-2+\frac{d+1}{2}
,
\nu_{0}=-\frac{d+1}{2}
and substitute
\xi=\coth\beta
in (
A.104
), and use (
A.105
) to simplify each integral in
\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(\xi)
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(\xi+\cos\theta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta&=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{\nu_{0}+1}}\sinh^{-\mu_{0}}\beta~\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-(\nu_{0}+1)\right)P_{\mu_{0}}^{\nu_{0}+1}(\cosh\beta),\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(\xi+\cos\theta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-1}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta&=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{\nu_{0}}}\sinh^{-(\mu_{0}+1)}\beta~\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\nu_{0}\right)P_{\mu_{0}+1}^{\nu_{0}}(\cosh\beta),\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(\xi+\cos\theta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}-2}\sin^{d+1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta&=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{\nu_{0}}}\sinh^{-\mu_{0}}\beta~\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\nu_{0}\right)P_{\mu_{0}}^{\nu_{0}}(\cosh\beta),\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}(\xi+\cos\theta)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\sin^{d-1}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta&=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{\nu_{0}+1}}\sinh^{-(\mu_{0}+1)}\beta~\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-(\nu_{0}+1)\right)P_{\mu_{0}+1}^{\nu_{0}+1}(\cosh\beta).\end{cases}

Hence, we can write
\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(\xi)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(\xi)

The conclusion now follows from the integral form of the Chebyshev inequality

A=0
.

and
\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(\xi)\equiv 0
for all
\xi>1
.

Case 3:
A>0
.
Since
f
and
g
are both decreasing,
\mathcal{H}_{m,d}(\xi)<0
for all
\xi>1
.

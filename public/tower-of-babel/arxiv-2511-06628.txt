Stochastic Optimal Impulse Controls with Changing Running Costs
Yuchen Cao and Jiongmin Yong

Abstract




Abstract.
This paper is concerned with stochastic impulse control problems in which the running cost changes depending on the impulse control. Because of such a dependence, it brings several difficulties when the usual dynamic programming principle is to be used. The corresponding Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation (a quasi-variational inequality) is derived, which contains a parameter. The value function is a unique viscosity solution to this HJB equation by a classical argument. Further, inspired by the derivation of the Pontryagin type maximum principle for stochastic optimal controls with a non-convex control domain, we have established the maximum principle for our stochastic optimal impulse controls, allowing perturbations in optimal impulse moments.

Keywords.
Optimal impulse control, dynamic programming principle, Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations, quasi-variational inequality, viscosity solutions, maximum principle.

1
Introduction.

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered complete probability space satisfying the usual conditions. In this probability space, we define a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion
W(\cdot)
with
\mathbb{F}
being its natural filtration, adding all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets.

We begin with a motivation. Let
r(\cdot)
be the interest rate process modeled by a certain SDE, say, Cox-Ingersoll-Ross (CIR, for short) see
[
1
]
. Let
t\in[0,T)
be the current time. Suppose a unit (a company, for example) is to borrow certain amount of money
\xi_{j}
at different moments
\tau_{j}\geqslant t
at (possibly) different rate
r(\tau_{j})>0
from a bank. We assume the unit is getting into the system at
t
. The hard part is that
t
might not be a moment at which a loan amount is initiated. But at that moment, a loan amount might be borrowed (at a rate settled at some moment
\tau_{0}<t
). Now, since at
\tau_{j}\geqslant t
, a moment at which a loan amount
\xi_{j}
is initiated, the unit pays the cost
\ell(\tau_{j},\xi_{j})>0
. Then the total amount owed by the unit is

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle C(s)=e^{r(\tau_{0})(s-t)}\xi_{0}+\ell(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})+e^{r(\tau_{1})(s-\tau_{1})}\xi_{1}+\cdots\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=e^{r(\tau_{0})(s-t)}\xi_{0}+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\Big(\ell(\tau_{j},\xi_{j})+e^{r(\tau_{j})(s-\tau_{j})}\xi_{j}\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(s),\qquad s\in[t,T].\end{array}

Or equivalently,

C(s)=\xi_{0}+\int_{t}^{s}r(\tau_{0})\xi_{0}d\tau+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\Big(\ell(\tau_{j},\xi_{j})+\xi_{j}+\int_{\tau_{j}}^{s}r(\tau_{j})\xi_{j}d\tau\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(s).

Now, with these loans, the unit has some investments to get better returns. So it is reasonable to assume the change in the wealth of the unit satisfies the SDE:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dX(s)=\big[b(s,X(s))+\xi(s)\big]ds+\sigma(s,X(s))dW(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(t)=x,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

with

\xi(s)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\xi_{j}{\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(s),\qquad s\in[t,T].

(1.2)

Then the expected utility (profit) on
[t,T]
(of the unit) is

J(t,x;\xi(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}g(\tau_{0},\theta,X(\theta))d\theta+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\Big(\int_{\tau_{j}}^{T}g(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))d\theta+\ell(\tau_{j},\xi_{j})\Big)+h(X(T))\Big].

(1.3)

The dependence on all the
\tau_{j}
is to be emphasized because on each interval
[\tau_{j},T]
, the payment of the unit to the bank should be different, and the better return might be different as well. We may formulate an optimal control problem with the state equation (
1.4
) and payoff functional (
1.3
), with respect to the control
\xi(\cdot)
.

Let us present another motivation. Still, let
t\in[0,T)
be the current time. Suppose a unit (a company, for example, again) needs to buy several raw materials (which could be contained in some other material, for example, some iron-containing minerals such as hematite, magnetite, goethite. limonite, and siderite, etc.). Let
\xi_{j}
be the raw material (which could be some organic or inorganic ones) bought at moment
\tau_{j}\geqslant t
, with cost
\ell(\tau_{j},\xi_{j})
. Then the unit owns the raw material of form (
1.2
) with each
\xi_{j}
possibly being a vector (of the same dimensions as the state). The unit produces certain products using the raw material
\xi(s)
. Thus, the state process
X(\cdot)
, satisfying the state equation (
1.4
), represents the quantity of the products for the unit. The expected payoff functional is of the form (
1.3
). Here, the reason for
g
depending on
\tau_{j}
is that by having
\xi_{j}
at
\tau_{j}
(from other raw material which could get
\xi_{j}
differently), the running payoff will be different.

The change in running cost also happens when people consider mortgage refinance related problems. Note that main motivations for refinancing are the following: the interest rate decreases significantly, the monthly payment is largely reduced, some cash can be further borrowed (according to the credit line), the pre-payment of the mortgage can be accelerated, etc. In any of these cases, the new mortgage will follow the new mortgage rate. Thus, it is natural that the running cost rate will be changed after the new mortgage starts.

Based on the above motivations, we now formulate our optimal impulse control problem in general.

Let
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed and convex cone, and let

\Delta_{*}[0,T]=\Big\{(\tau_{0},t)\in[0,T]\times[0,T]\bigm|\tau_{0}\leqslant t\Big\}

be the lower triangle domain. Let
t\in[0,T)
be given (thus,
t
is the moment that we get into the system) and
\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
be the set of all impulse controls of the form (
1.2
) with
\xi_{j}\in\mathscr{K}
(which might be a multi-dimensional vector, since
n
might be larger than 1),
{\cal F}_{\tau_{j}}
-measurable and integrable. For any
\xi(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
, the state equation looks like the following:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(s)=x+\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big(\int_{t}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace b(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)dW(\theta)\Big)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\xi(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

(1.4)

where
b:\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma:\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are given maps. The cost functional is

J(\tau_{0},t,x;\xi(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[\bar{Y}(t,\tau_{0},t,x;\xi(\cdot))\Big],

(1.5)

where
(\bar{Y}(\cdot),\bar{Z}(\cdot\,,\cdot))\equiv(\bar{Y}(\cdot\,;\tau_{0},t,x,\xi(\cdot)),\bar{Z}(\cdot\,,\cdot\,;\tau_{0},t,x,\xi(\cdot))
is the adapted solution of the following backward stochastic
Volterra integral equation (BSVIE, for short):

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\bar{Y}(s)=h(X(T))+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\ell(\tau_{j},\xi_{j})+\int_{s}^{T}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}g(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\int_{s}^{T}\bar{Z}(s,\theta)dW(\theta),\qquad s\in[t,T].\end{array}

(1.6)

Note that in the above, the free term

\psi(s)\equiv h(X(T))+\sum_{\tau_{j}\geqslant s}\ell(\tau_{j},\xi_{j}),\qquad s\in[t,T]

is an
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable process (not just an
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable random variable) because of the second summation term. Clearly, by taking
s=t
and applying the operator
\mathbb{E}
on both sides of (
1.6
), we get

J(\tau_{0},t,x;\xi(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[h(X(T))+\sum_{\tau_{j}\geqslant s}\ell(\tau_{j},\xi_{j})+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j\geqslant 1}g(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta\Big].

(1.7)

This shows that our cost functional is an extension of classical one. Our optimal impulse control problem can be formulated as follows.

Problem (IC).
For given
(\tau_{0},t,x)\in\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathscr{K}
, find
\bar{\xi}(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
such that

J(\tau_{0},t,x;\bar{\xi}(\cdot))=\inf_{\xi(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]}J(\tau_{0},t,x;\xi(\cdot))\equiv V(\tau_{0},t,x).

(1.8)

We see that our stochastic optimal impulse control problem is different from the classical one in the following aspects:

\bullet
The drift and the diffusion terms in the state equation (
1.4
) depend on
\tau_{0}\leqslant t
, which is no later than the current moment
t
(and might be before the current moment). This brings an obvious difficulty for people to use dynamic programming principle (DPP, for short) because people might get into the system at time which could not be an impulse moment.

\bullet
The cost functional (
1.5
) is recursive involving a BSVIE, which contains the impulse costs in the free term which might be an
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable process (and not necessarily a
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable random variable or a
\mathbb{F}
-adapted process).

2
Properties of State Processes and Cost Functionals.

We first make some observations. For any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\xi(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
of form (
1.2
). Denote the number of impulses in
\xi(\cdot)
by
\kappa(\xi(\cdot))
. Due to the positivity of the impulse cost (we are going to assume), we must have that
\kappa(\xi(\cdot))<\infty
. Let
\tau_{0}\in[0,t]
. Then from (
1.4
), with
j_{0}\geqslant 1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(\tau_{j_{0}})=X(\tau_{j_{0}}+0)=x+\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big(\int_{t}^{\tau_{j_{0}}}b(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{\tau_{j_{0}}}\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)dW(\theta)\Big)+\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\xi_{j}{\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(s),\end{array}

(2.1)

Hence, equivalent to (
1.4
), for
\tau_{j_{0}}\leqslant s<\tau_{j_{0}+1}
, we have

X(s)=X(\tau_{j_{0}})+\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}b(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))d\theta+\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))dW(\theta)\Big).

(2.2)

Note that we are going to prove the fact that in the interval
(\tau_{j_{0}},\tau_{j_{0}+1})
, there is no impulse, and the impulse is contained in the term
X(\tau_{j_{0}})
. For
s\in[t,\tau_{1})
,

X(s)=x+\int_{t}^{s}b(\tau_{0},\theta,X(\theta))d\theta+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(\tau_{0},\theta,X(\theta))dW(\theta).

(2.3)

Now, we introduce the following assumption.

(H1)
Let
b,\sigma:\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

be continuous, for some generic constant
K>0
, and some generic modulus of continuity
\omega:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

|b(\tau,t,0)|+|\sigma(\tau,t,0)|\leqslant K,\qquad(\tau,t)\in\Delta_{*}[0,T].

(2.4)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|b(\tau,t,x)-b(\tau^{\prime},t,x^{\prime})|+|\sigma(\tau,t,x)-\sigma(\tau^{\prime},t,x^{\prime})|\leqslant\omega(|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)+K|x-x^{\prime}|,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\tau,t,x),(\tau^{\prime},t,x^{\prime})\in\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}

(2.5)

In what follows,
K>0
will represent a generic constant and
\omega(\cdot)
will represent a generic modulus of continuity, which could be different from line to line, unless otherwise stated. For convenience, we let
d=1
(general case can be treated in a similar way) and some conditions in (H1) can be relaxed (we assume those just for convenience). Also, for any
\xi(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
of form (
1.2
), we denote the number of impulses by
\kappa(\xi(\cdot))
. The following result is about the controlled process.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(H1)
hold. Then for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\xi(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
, and
\tau_{0}\in[0,t]
, state equation (
1.4
)
admits a unique solution
X(\cdot)=X(\cdot\,;\tau_{0},t,x,\xi(\cdot))
.
Moreover, for
p\geqslant 1
, there exists a constant
K>0
, depending on the number
\kappa(\xi(\cdot))
of the impulses in
\xi(\cdot)
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X(s;\tau_{0},t,x,\xi(\cdot))|^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))}|\xi_{j}|^{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}}|b(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|d\theta\Big)^{p}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\Big(\int_{\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}}\negthinspace\negthinspace|\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+|x|^{p}+\Big(\int_{t}^{\tau_{1}}|b(\bar{\tau}_{0},\theta,0)|d\theta\Big)^{p}+\Big(\int_{t}^{\tau_{1}}|\sigma(\bar{\tau}_{0},\theta,0)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Big(\int_{t_{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))}}^{T}|b(\tau_{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))},\theta,0)|d\theta\Big)^{p}+\Big(\int_{\tau_{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))}}^{T}|\sigma(\tau_{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))},\theta,0)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big\}.\end{array}

(2.6)

where
\bar{\tau}_{0}=\tau_{0}
. If
(\tau_{0}^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then for some
K>0
, depending on
\kappa(\xi(\cdot))
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X(s;\tau_{0},t,x,\xi(\cdot))-X(s;\tau_{0}^{\prime},t,x^{\prime},\xi(\cdot))|^{p}\Big]\leqslant\omega(|\tau_{0}-\tau_{0}^{\prime}|)+K|x-x^{\prime}|^{p}.

(2.7)

Proof.
First of all, for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\xi(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
(of
form (
1.2
)), by recursive procedure, we have the unique strong solution to the state equation (
1.4
). Further, for
\tau_{j_{0}}\leqslant s<\tau_{j_{0}+1}
, with
j_{0}\geqslant 1
, we have (from (
2.2
))

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[\tau_{j_{0}},s]}|X(r)|^{p}\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[\tau_{j_{0}},s]}\Big|X(\tau_{j_{0}})+\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{r}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace b(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))d\theta+\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{r}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))dW(\theta)\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant(2j_{0}+1)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\Big\{|X(\tau_{j_{0}})|^{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|b(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))|d\theta\Big)^{p}+\sup_{r\in[\tau_{j_{0}},s]}\Big|\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{r}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))dW(\theta)\Big|^{p}\Big]\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant(2j_{0}+1)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\Big\{|X(\tau_{j_{0}})|^{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|b(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))|d\theta\Big)^{p}+K_{p}\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}|\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big|^{p}\Big]\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big\{|X(\tau_{j_{0}})|^{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}\big[|b(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|+K|X(\theta)|\big]d\theta\Big)^{p}+\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}\big[|\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,0))|+K|X(\theta)|\big]^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big\{|X(\tau_{j_{0}})|^{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}|b(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|d\theta\Big)^{p}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace|\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\Big\}+K\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[\tau_{j_{0}},\theta]}|X(r)|^{p}\Big]d\theta.\end{array}

In the above,
K_{p}>0
is the absolute constant appearing in the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities (see
[
26
]
). Consequently, by Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[\tau_{j_{0}},\tau_{j_{0}+1})}|X(r)|^{p}\Big]\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big\{|X(\tau_{j_{0}})|^{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}|b(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|d\theta\Big)^{p}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\Big(\int_{\tau_{j_{0}}}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace|\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\Big\},\quad j_{0}\geqslant 1,

with
K
depending on
\kappa(\xi(\cdot))
. Note

X(\tau_{j_{0}})=X(\tau_{j_{0}}-0)+\xi_{j_{0}},\quad\hbox{ or }\quad\Delta X(\tau_{j_{0}})=X(\tau_{j_{0}})-X(\tau_{j_{0}}-0)=\xi_{j_{0}}.

Also, for
r\in[t,\tau_{1})
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,\tau_{1})}|X(r)|^{p}\Big]\leqslant K\Big[|x|^{p}+\Big(\int_{t}^{\tau_{1}}|b(\tau_{0},\theta,0)|d\theta\Big)^{p}+\Big(\int_{t}^{\tau_{1}}|\sigma(\tau_{0},\theta,0)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big].

For
s\in[\tau_{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))},T]
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t_{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))},T]}|X(r)|^{p}\Big]\leqslant K\Big[|X(\kappa(\cdot))|^{p}+\Big(\int_{t_{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))}}^{T}|b(\tau_{0},\theta,0)|d\theta\Big)^{p}+\Big(\int_{\tau_{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))}}^{T}|\sigma(\tau_{0},\theta,0)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big].

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,T]}|X(r)|^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,\tau_{1})}|X(r)|^{p}\Big],\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}|X(r)|^{p}\Big],\cdots,\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[\tau_{\bar{j}_{0}-1},\tau_{\bar{j}_{0}})}|X(r)|^{p}\Big],\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[\tau_{\bar{j}_{0}},T]}|X(r)|^{p}\Big]\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))}|\xi_{j}|^{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa(\xi(\cdot))}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}}|b(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|d\theta\Big)^{p}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\Big(\int_{\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}}\negthinspace\negthinspace|\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,0)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\\

Definition 2.3

\lambda\varphi(x)+(1-\lambda)\varphi(x^{\prime})-\varphi(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)x^{\prime})\leqslant K\lambda(1-\lambda)|x-x^{\prime}|^{2},\qquad x,x^{\prime}\in G,~\lambda\in[0,1].

Note that linear growth of a function does not necessarily imply it is semi-convex, for example,
\varphi(x)=\sin(e^{x})

and the map
x\mapsto(h(x),g(\tau,t,x))
is semi-convex.

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\lambda h(X(T))+(1-\lambda)h(X^{\prime}(T))-h(X^{\lambda}(T))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big(\lambda g(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))+(1-\lambda)g(\tau_{j},\theta,X^{\prime}(\theta))-g(\tau_{j},\theta,X^{\lambda}(\theta))\Big)d\theta\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+h(X^{\lambda}(T))-h(X_{\lambda}(T))+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big(g(\tau_{j},\theta,X_{\lambda}(\theta))-g(\tau_{j},\theta,X^{\lambda}(\theta))\Big)d\theta\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\lambda(1-\lambda)\mathbb{E}\Big(|X(T)-X^{\prime}(T)|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}|X(\theta)-X^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+K\mathbb{E}\Big(|X^{\lambda}(T)-X_{\lambda}(T)|+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}|X^{\lambda}(\theta)-X_{\lambda}(\theta)|d\theta\Big).\end{array}

Since

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\big|\lambda b(\tau,t,x)+(1-\lambda)b(\tau,t,x^{\prime})-b(\tau,t,x_{\lambda})\big|\negthinspace=\negthinspace\big|\lambda[b(\tau,t,x)-b(\tau,t,x_{\lambda})]+(1-\lambda)[b(\tau,t,x^{\prime})-b(\tau,t,x_{\lambda})]\big|\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\Big|\lambda\int_{0}^{1}b_{x}(\tau,t,x_{\lambda}+\theta(1-\lambda)(x-x^{\prime}))d\theta(1-\lambda)(x-x^{\prime})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(1-\lambda)\int_{0}^{1}b_{x}(\tau,t,x_{\lambda}+\theta\lambda(x^{\prime}-x))d\theta\lambda(x^{\prime}-x)\Big|\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\lambda(1-\lambda)|x-x^{\prime}|\,\Big|\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace b_{x}(\tau,t,x_{\lambda}+\theta(1-\lambda)(x-x^{\prime})-b_{x}(\tau,t,x_{\lambda}+\theta\lambda(x^{\prime}-x))d\theta\Big|\leqslant K\lambda(1-\lambda)|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}.\end{array}

Similarly, we have

\big|\lambda\sigma(\tau,t,x)+(1-\lambda)\sigma(\tau,t,x^{\prime})-\sigma(\tau,t,x_{\lambda})\big|\leqslant K\lambda(1-\lambda)|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}.

Then,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X^{\lambda}(s)=x_{\lambda}+\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big(\int_{t}^{s}\lambda b(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta+\int_{t}^{s}\lambda\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)dW(\theta)\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big(\int_{t}^{s}(1-\lambda)b(\tau_{j},\theta,X^{\prime}(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{s}(1-\lambda)\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X^{\prime}(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)dW(\theta)\Big)+\xi(s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X_{\lambda}(s)=x_{\lambda}\negthinspace+\negthinspace\negthinspace\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big(\int_{t}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace b(\tau_{j},\theta,X_{\lambda}(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X_{\lambda}(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)dW(\theta)\Big)+\xi(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

Thus, for any
s\in[t,T]
, by the state equation (
1.4
) and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,s]}\big|X^{\lambda}(r)-X_{\lambda}(r)\big|^{2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle+K\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,s]}\Big|\int_{t}^{r}\big[\lambda b(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))+(1-\lambda)b(\tau_{j},\theta,X^{\prime}(\theta))-b(\tau_{j},t,X_{\lambda}(\theta))\big]{\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta\Big|^{2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle+K\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,s]}\Big|\int_{t}^{r}\big[\lambda\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X(\theta))+(1-\lambda)\sigma(\tau_{j},t,X^{\prime}(\theta))-\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X_{\lambda}(\theta){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta))\big]dW(\theta)\Big|^{2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle+K\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,s]}\Big|\int_{t}^{r}\big[b(\tau_{j},\theta,X^{\lambda}(\theta))-b(\tau_{j},\theta,X_{\lambda}(\theta))\big]{\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)d\theta\Big|^{2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle+K\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,s]}\Big|\int_{t}^{r}\big[\sigma(\tau_{j},\theta,X^{\lambda}(\theta))-\sigma(\tau_{j},t,X_{\lambda}(\theta)){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(\theta)\big]dW(\theta)\Big|^{2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\lambda^{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{s}|X(\theta)-X^{\prime}(\theta)|^{4}d\theta+K\lambda^{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{s}\big|X(\theta)-X^{\prime}(\theta)\big|^{4}d\theta\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad+K\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{s}|X^{\lambda}(\theta)-X_{\lambda}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta+K\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{s}|X^{\lambda}(\theta)-X_{\lambda}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\lambda^{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{4}+K\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}|X^{\lambda}(\theta)-X_{\lambda}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta.\end{array}

Then, by Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[t,T]}\big|X^{\lambda}(r)-X_{\lambda}(r)|\Big]\leqslant K\lambda(1-\lambda)|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}.

Hence,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\lambda V(\tau_{0},t,x)+(1-\lambda)V(\tau_{0},t,x^{\prime})-V(\tau_{0},t,x_{\lambda})-\varepsilon\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda J(\tau_{0},t,x;\xi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))+(1-\lambda)J(\tau_{0},t,x^{\prime};\xi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(\tau_{0},t,x_{\lambda};\xi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))\leqslant K\lambda(1-\lambda)|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}.\end{array}

Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
, we see that
x\mapsto V(\tau_{0},t,x)
is semi-convex.

3
Dynamic Programming Principle and HJB Equation

In this section, we are going to derive Bellman’s dynamic programming principle (DPP, for short) and the HJB equation, which is a quasi-variational inequality.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(H1)–(H2)
hold. Then for any
(\tau,t,x)\in\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

V(\tau,t,x)\leqslant\min\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[V(\tau,t^{\prime},X(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}g(\tau,\theta,X(\theta))d\theta\Big],N[V(\tau,t,\cdot)](x)\Big\},

(3.1)

where
X(\cdot)=X(\cdot\,;\tau,t,x,\xi(\cdot))
with
\xi(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
no impulses in
[t,t^{\prime}]
for any
t^{\prime}\in(t,T]
, and

N[V(\tau,t,\cdot)](x)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathscr{K}}\Big[V(\tau,t,x)+\ell(t,x+\xi)\Big].

(3.2)

The operator
N:\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{V}
, and it is Lipschitz continuous:

|N[V(\tau,t,\cdot)](x)-N[\widehat{V}(\tau,t,\cdot)](x)|\leqslant|V(\tau,t,x)-\widehat{V}(\tau,t,x)|,\qquad\forall V(\cdot\,,\cdot,,\cdot),\widehat{V}(\cdot\,,\cdot,,\cdot)\in\mathscr{V}.

(3.3)

If it holds

V(\tau,t,x)<N[V(\tau,t,\cdot)](x).

(3.4)

then there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
t^{\prime}\in[t,t+\delta]
,

V(\tau,t,x)=V(\tau,t+\delta,X(t+\delta))+\int_{t}^{t+\delta}g(\tau,s,X(s))ds.

(3.5)

Hence, in the case that
V(\cdot\,,\cdot\,,\cdot)
is smooth in
(t,x)
, we have

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\min\Big\{V_{t}(\tau,t,x)+H(\tau,t,V_{x}(\tau,t,x),V_{xx}(\tau,t,x)),N[V(\tau,t,\cdot)](x)-V(\tau,t,x)\Big\}=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(t,x)\in[\tau,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle V(\tau,T,x)=\min\big\{h(x),N[h](x)\big\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(3.6)

where

H(\tau,t,x,p^{\top}\negthinspace\negthinspace,P)=pb(\tau,t,x)+{1\over 2}\sigma(\tau,t,x)^{\top}P\sigma(\tau,t,x)+g(\tau,t,x),\quad(t,x,p,P)\in[\tau,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Proof.
Let
(\tau,t)\in\Delta_{*}[0,T]
be given. Then for any
t^{\prime}\in[t,T]
, let
\xi(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
be an impulse control with no impulses in
[t,t^{\prime}]
, we have

V(\tau,t,x)\leqslant J(\tau,t,x;\xi(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[J(\tau,t^{\prime},X(t^{\prime});\xi(\cdot)\big|_{(t^{\prime},T]})+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}g(\tau,\theta,X(\theta))d\theta\Big].

Then, we have

V(\tau,t,x)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[V(\tau,t^{\prime},X(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}g(\tau,\theta,X(\theta))d\theta\Big].

On the other hand, if at
t
, we make an impulse in the control, then

V(\tau,t,x)\leqslant V(\tau,t,x+\xi)+\ell(t,\xi).

Thus, we must have

V(\tau,t,x)\leqslant\inf_{\xi\in\mathscr{K}}\Big(V(\tau,t,x+\xi)+\ell(t,\xi)\Big)\equiv N[V(\tau,t,\cdot)](x).

Combining the above, we have (
3.1
). Further, by the continuity of
V(\cdot\,,\cdot\,,\cdot)
and
\ell(\cdot\,,\cdot)
, we have

|V(\tau,t,x+\xi)+\ell(t,\xi)-[V(\tau^{\prime},t^{\prime},x^{\prime}+\xi)+\ell(t^{\prime},\xi)]|\leqslant\omega(|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|)+K\big(|t-t^{\prime}|^{1\over 2}+|x-x^{\prime}|\big).

Thus, by the definition of operator
N
, we have

|N[V(\tau,t,\cdot)](x)-N[V(\tau^{\prime},t^{\prime},\cdot)](x^{\prime})|\leqslant\omega\big(|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|\big)+K\big(|t^{\prime}-t|^{1\over 2}+|x-x^{\prime}|\big).

Hence,
N:\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{V}
and we see obviously that (
3.3
).

Next, if (
3.4
) holds, by the continuity of
N[V(\tau,t,\cdot)](x)
, we see that
on some interval
[t,t+\delta]
, any impulse control containing impulses in this interval will not be optimal. Hence,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle V(\tau,t,x)=J(\tau,t,x;\xi(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[J(\tau,t^{\prime},X(t^{\prime});\xi(\cdot))+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}g(\tau,\theta,X(\theta))d\theta\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\geqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[V(\tau,t^{\prime},X(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}g(\tau,\theta,X(\theta))d\theta\Big],\end{array}

with
\xi(\cdot)
contains no impulses in
[t,t^{\prime}]
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that (
3.5
) holds. Hence, if
(t,x)\mapsto V(\tau,t,x)
is smooth, then by Itô’s formula, we see that
V(\cdot\,,\cdot\,,\cdot)
satisfies the equation in (
3.6
). There are two cases: In the case that

h(x)<N[h](x)\equiv\inf_{\xi\in\mathscr{K}}\Big(h(x+\xi)+\ell(T,\xi)\Big).

Then, at
T
, optimal impulse control will have no impulses. Hence,

V(\tau,T,x)=h(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In the other case, i.e.,

h(x)=N[h](x).

Then, at
T
, there would be an impulse so that

N[h](x)=h(x+\xi)+\ell(T,\xi).

Then we must have

V(\tau,T,x)=N[h](x).

Hence, (
3.6
) holds.

Next result is interesting.

Let
(H1)–(H2)
hold. Let

The above result implies if

\bar{\xi}(\cdot)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\bar{\xi}_{j}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{j},T]}(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]

In particular, from now on, for any

(3.11)

4
Optimal Impulse Controls and Maximum Principle

We have seen that any optimal impulse control of our Problem (IC) can only have finitely many impulses. Thus, we have the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(H1)–(H2)
hold. Then, for given
(\tau,t,x)\in\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Problem
(IC)
admits an optimal impulse control.

Proof.
Let
(\tau,t,x)\in\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\xi^{k}(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
be a minimizing sequence, i.e.,

J(\tau,t,x;\xi^{k}(\cdot))\to V(\tau,t,x).

Clearly, we may assume that each
\xi^{k}(\cdot)
has the same number of impulses. Thus,

\xi^{k}(s)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\xi^{k}_{j}{\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j}^{k},T]}(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],~k\geqslant 1.

Then, by extracting sub-sequences, we see the existence of an optimal control.

To obtain a necessary condition of Pontryagin type, we will have three standard steps: Derive Taylor type expansion, obtain a variation inequality; and use duality to get maximum condition. We now make these more precise.

4.1
Taylor Expansion

We first make the following additional assumption.

(H4)
The map
x\mapsto(b(\tau,s,x),\sigma(\tau,s,x),g(\tau,s,x),h(x))
is twice continuously differentiable, and the map
\tau\mapsto(b(\tau,s,x),\sigma(\tau,s,x),\ell(\tau,\xi))
is continuously differentiable.

The following is classical.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous twice differentiable. Then for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\varphi(x)=\varphi(x_{0})+\varphi_{x}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})+(x-x_{0})^{\top}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{xx}(x_{0}+\alpha\beta(x-x_{0})))\beta d\beta d\alpha\Big)(x-x_{0})

(4.1)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.
By Cauchy type Taylor expansion, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\varphi(x_{0})+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{x}(x_{0}+\alpha(x-x_{0}))d\alpha\Big)(x-x_{0})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=\varphi(x_{0})+\varphi_{x}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\big[\varphi_{x}(x_{0}+\alpha(x-x_{0})))-\varphi_{x}(x_{0})\big]d\alpha\Big)(x-x_{0})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=\varphi(x_{0})+\varphi_{x}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})+(x-x_{0})^{\top}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{xx}(x_{0}+\alpha\beta(x-x_{0})))\beta d\beta d\alpha\Big)(x-x_{0})\end{array}

Then (
4.1
) follows.

In the case that
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is smooth, we have a similar result. In this case, we denote

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle(x-x_{0})^{\top}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{xx}(x_{0}+\alpha\beta(x-x_{0})))\beta d\beta d\alpha\Big)(x-x_{0})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=(x-x_{0})^{\top}\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{1}_{xx}(x_{0}+\alpha\beta(x-x_{0})))\beta d\beta d\alpha\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{2}_{xx}(x_{0}+\alpha\beta(x-x_{0})))\beta d\beta d\alpha\\
\vdots\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{m}_{xx}(x_{0}+\alpha\beta(x-x_{0})))\beta d\beta d\alpha\end{pmatrix}(x-x_{0}),\end{array}

which is an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
vector.

Next result gives Taylor expansion about the optimal pair
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{\xi}(\cdot))
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(H1)–(H2)
and
(H4)
hold. Let
(\tau_{0},t,x)\in\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be given and
\bar{\xi}(\cdot)\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T]
be an optimal impulse control of the following form (
3.9
)
with the optimal state process being
\bar{X}(\cdot)=X(\cdot\,;\tau_{0},t,x,\xi(\cdot))
. Let
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,\kappa(\bar{\xi}(\cdot))\}
,
\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
small,
\eta\in\mathscr{K}
, and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\xi^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)=\sum_{j\geqslant 1,j\neq i}\bar{\xi}_{j}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{j},T]}(\cdot)+[\bar{\xi}_{i}+\varepsilon(\eta-\bar{\xi}_{i})]{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon},T]}(\cdot)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad~=\bar{\xi}(\cdot)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(\cdot)+\varepsilon(\eta-\bar{\xi}_{i}){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon},T]}\in\boldsymbol{\Xi}[t,T],\end{array}

(4.2)

be the
i
-th perturbation of
\bar{\xi}(\cdot)
. Let
X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)=X(\cdot\,;\tau_{0},t,x,\xi^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot))
be the corresponding process. Let

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(s)=\int_{t}^{s}B_{x}(\theta)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\theta)d\theta+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(\theta)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\theta)-\sigma(\bar{\tau}_{i},\theta){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(\theta)\Big)dW(\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)]^{\top}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}h_{xx}(\bar{X}(T)+\beta\alpha[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)])\beta d\beta d\alpha\Big)[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}_{i}+\alpha\bar{\varepsilon},\bar{\xi}_{i}+\alpha\varepsilon\xi_{i})d\alpha\Big)\bar{\varepsilon}+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{\xi}(\bar{\tau}_{i}+\alpha\bar{\varepsilon},\bar{\xi}_{i}+\alpha\varepsilon\xi_{i})d\alpha\Big)\varepsilon\xi_{i}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big(g(\bar{\tau}_{j},r,X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r))-g(\bar{\tau}_{j},r,\bar{X}(r))\Big){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{j},T]}(r)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big[\Big(g(\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon},r,X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r))-g(\bar{\tau}_{i},r,\bar{X}(r))\Big){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon},T]}(r)-g(\bar{\tau}_{i},r,\bar{X}(r)){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\Big]dr\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\Big\{H_{x}[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)]+[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)]^{\top}H_{xx}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\ell_{\tau}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}\bar{\varepsilon}+\ell_{\xi}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon\xi_{i}+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big[g_{x}(\bar{\tau}_{j},r,\bar{X}(r))[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{X}(r)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)\negthinspace-\negthinspace\bar{X}(r)]^{\top}\negthinspace\Big(\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}g_{xx}(\bar{\tau}_{j},r,\bar{X}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\alpha\beta[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)\negthinspace-\negthinspace\bar{X}(r)])\beta d\alpha d\beta\Big)[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)\negthinspace-\negthinspace\bar{X}(r)]\Big]{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{j},T]}(r)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\bar{\varepsilon}\Big(\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace g_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}_{i}+\alpha\bar{\varepsilon},r,\bar{X}(r))d\alpha\Big){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon},T]}(r)dr-\int_{t}^{T}g(\bar{\tau}_{i},r,\bar{X}(r)){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)dr\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\Big\{H_{x}[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)]+[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)]^{\top}H_{xx}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\ell_{\tau}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}\bar{\varepsilon}+\ell_{\xi}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon\xi_{i}+\int_{t}^{T}G_{x}(r)[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{X}(r)]+[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{X}(r)]^{\top}G_{xx}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{X}(r)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\bar{\varepsilon}\int_{t}^{T}g_{\tau}^{i,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon},T]}(r)dr-\int_{t}^{T}g(\bar{\tau}_{i},r,\bar{X}(r)){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)dr\Big\}+o(\varepsilon+\bar{\varepsilon}),\end{array}

where

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle H^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{xx}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}h_{xx}(\bar{X}(T)+\beta\alpha(X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{X}(T])\beta d\beta d\alpha\to H_{xx},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\ell^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{\tau}=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}_{i}+\alpha\bar{\varepsilon},\bar{\xi}_{i}+\alpha\varepsilon\xi_{i})d\alpha\to\ell_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{i}),\quad\ell^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{\xi}=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{\xi}(\bar{\tau}_{i}+\alpha\bar{\varepsilon},\bar{\xi}_{i}+\alpha\varepsilon\xi_{i})d\alpha\to\ell_{\xi}(\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{i}),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle g_{\tau}^{i,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)\to g_{\tau}^{i}(r),\qquad G^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{xx}(r)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}g_{xx}(\bar{\tau}_{j},r,\bar{X}(r)+\beta\alpha[X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{X}(r)]\beta d\beta d\alpha\to G_{xx}(r).\end{array}\right.

Thus, we have (
4.13
) by the estimate (
4.6
) and (
4.7
).

We note that the variational inequality (
4.13
) is not very useful since
it involves
X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\cdot)
and
X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(\cdot)
, which are
depending on the variation
\xi^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)
of the optimal impulse control and they might not even be continuous. Thus, we need to go one more step.

4.3
Duality principles

In order to introduce the duality principles to eliminate the dependence on the
X_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)
and
X_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)
in (
4.13
), we now define (compare with (
4.3
) and (
4.4
))

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)=\int_{t}^{s}B_{x}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)dr+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)-\sigma(\bar{\tau}_{i},r){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\Big)dW(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\equiv X_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)+\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

(4.15)

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(s)=\int_{t}^{s}\Big(B_{x}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)^{\top}B_{xx}(r)X_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-b(\bar{\tau}_{i},r){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)^{\top}\Sigma_{xx}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)\Big)dW(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\equiv X_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)-\bar{\varepsilon}\zeta^{i}(s)-\varepsilon\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon},T]}(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

(4.16)

They are Itô processes, by which we mean that they can be written as the sums of Lebesgue integral (the drift term) and the Itô’s integral (diffusion term). Therefore, they are continuous in
s
. Clearly,

X_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)=\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(s),

(4.17)

and

X_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)=\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)+\bar{\varepsilon}\zeta^{i}(s)+\varepsilon\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon},T]}(s).

(4.18)

Note that since on
[t,\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})
, the optimal impulse control
is not perturbed yet (the perturbation is going to be made at
\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon}
from
\bar{\xi}_{i}
to
\bar{\xi}_{i}+\varepsilon\xi_{i}
), which leads to

0=\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)G_{xx}(r)X_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)=\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)G_{xx}(r)[\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(s)].

Thus,

\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)G_{xx}(r)\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)=\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}G_{xx}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(s).

Also, since we have assumed
\bar{\tau}_{\kappa(\bar{\xi}(\cdot))}<T
(the other case can be treated similarly), one must have

\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon}<T,\qquad 1\leqslant i\leqslant\kappa(\bar{\xi}(\cdot)),

if
\bar{\varepsilon}
is small. Consequently,

{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(T)=0,\qquad 1\leqslant i\leqslant\kappa(\bar{\xi}(\cdot)).

Hence, the variational inequality (
4.13
) reads

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[H_{x}[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(T)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(T)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)+\bar{\varepsilon}\zeta^{i}(T)+\varepsilon\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},T]}(T)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+[\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(T)]^{\top}H_{xx}[\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(T)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace G_{x}(r)[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)+\bar{\varepsilon}\zeta^{i}(r)+\varepsilon\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},T]}(r)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]^{\top}G_{xx}(r)[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]dr\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\bar{\varepsilon}\Big(\ell_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{i})+\int_{t}^{T}g^{i}_{\tau}(r)dr\Big)+\varepsilon\ell_{\xi}(\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{i})\xi_{i}-\int_{t}^{T}g(\bar{\tau}_{i},r,\bar{X}(r)){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)dr+o(\varepsilon+\bar{\varepsilon})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[H_{x}[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(T)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)]+\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(T)^{\top}H_{xx}\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(T)+\bar{\varepsilon}H_{x}\zeta^{i}(T)+\varepsilon H_{x}\xi_{i}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{T}\Big(G_{x}(r)[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)]+\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)^{\top}G_{xx}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)-g(\bar{\tau}_{i},r,\bar{X}(r)){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad-G_{x}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)+\bar{\varepsilon}G_{x}(r)\zeta^{i}(r)+\varepsilon G_{x}(r)\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},T]}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}G_{xx}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\bar{\varepsilon}\Big(\ell_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{i})+\int_{t}^{T}g^{i}_{\tau}(r)dr\Big)+\varepsilon\ell_{\xi}(\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{i})\xi_{i}\Big]+o(\varepsilon+\bar{\varepsilon}),\end{array}

(4.19)

which leads to

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big\{H_{x}[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(T)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)]+\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(T)^{\top}H_{xx}\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(T)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{T}\Big(G_{x}(r)[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)]+\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)^{\top}G_{xx}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t}^{T}\Big(G_{x}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}+\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}G_{xx}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}+g(\bar{\tau}_{i},r,\bar{X}(r))\Big){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\bar{\varepsilon}\Big(\ell_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{i})+H_{x}\zeta^{i}(T)+\int_{t}^{T}[G_{x}(r)\zeta^{i}(r)+g^{i}_{\tau}(r)]dr\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\Big(H_{x}+\ell_{\xi}(\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{i})+\int_{t}^{T}G_{x}(r){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},T]}(r)dr\Big)\xi_{i}\Big\}+o(\varepsilon+\bar{\varepsilon}),\end{array}

(4.20)

We note that the above still depends on
\xi^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\xi}}(\cdot)
, but through two Itô processes. We now want to use duality principles to get rid of such dependence. To this end, by the definitions, we obtain the SDEs for

\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)
and
\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)=\int_{t}^{s}\Big(B_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)-B_{x}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)-[\Sigma_{x}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}+\sigma(\bar{\tau}_{i},r)]{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\Big)dW(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\equiv\int_{t}^{s}\Big(B_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)+\widehat{b}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\Big)dr+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)+\widehat{\sigma}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\Big)dW(r),\end{array}

(4.21)

with

\widehat{b}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)=-B_{x}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r),\qquad\widehat{\sigma}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)=-[\Sigma_{x}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}+\sigma(\bar{\tau}_{i},r)]{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r),

(4.22)

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(s)=\int_{t}^{s}\Big(B_{x}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)^{\top}B_{xx}(r)X_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-b(\bar{\tau}_{i},r){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)^{\top}\Sigma_{xx}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)\Big)dW(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{t}^{s}\Big(B_{x}(r)[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+\bar{\varepsilon}\zeta^{i}(r)+\varepsilon\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]-b(\bar{\tau}_{i},r){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]^{\top}B_{xx}(r)[\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(r)[\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+\bar{\varepsilon}\zeta^{i}(r)+\varepsilon\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+[\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]^{\top}\Sigma_{xx}(r)[\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]\Big)dW(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{t}^{s}\Big(B_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+\bar{\varepsilon}B_{x}(r)\zeta^{i}(r)+\varepsilon B_{x}(r)\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)-b(\bar{\tau}_{i},r){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)^{\top}B_{xx}(r)\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}B_{xx}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+\bar{\varepsilon}\Sigma_{x}(r)\zeta^{i}(r)+\varepsilon\Sigma_{x}(r)\xi_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)^{\top}\Sigma_{xx}(r)\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)-\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\top}\Sigma_{xx}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)]\Big)dW(r),\end{array}

which gives

\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)=\int_{t}^{s}\Big(B_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+\widehat{b}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\Big)dr+\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\Sigma_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+\widehat{\sigma}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\Big)dW(r),

(4.23)

with

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{b}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)=\bar{\varepsilon}B_{x}(r)\zeta^{i}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\Big[\varepsilon B_{x}(r)\xi_{i}\negthinspace-\negthinspace\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}B_{xx}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}\negthinspace-\negthinspace b(\bar{\tau}_{i},r)\Big]{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}\negthinspace+\negthinspace\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)^{\top}B_{xx}(r)\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)=\bar{\varepsilon}\Sigma_{x}(r)\zeta^{i}(r)+\Big[\varepsilon\Sigma_{x}(r)\xi_{i}-\bar{\xi}_{i}^{\,\top}\Sigma_{xx}(r)\bar{\xi}_{i}\Big]{\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)+\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)^{\top}\Sigma_{xx}(r)\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r).\end{array}

(4.24)

The following duality relation can be used to get rid of the linear terms in
\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)
on the right-hand side of (
4.28
).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(H1)–(H2)
and
(H4)
hold and
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
is the adapted solution of the following BSDE:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dY(s)=-\Big[B_{x}(s)^{\top}Y(s)+\Sigma_{x}(s)^{\top}Z(s)+G_{x}(s)^{\top}\Big]ds+Z(s)dW(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y(T)=H_{x}^{\top},\end{array}\right.

(4.25)

where we recall (
4.14
) (for the data). Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[H\Big(\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)\Big)+\int_{t}^{T}G_{x}(r)\Big(\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)+\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)\Big)dr\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\langle Y(r),\widehat{b}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)+\widehat{b}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle+\langle Z(r),\widehat{\sigma}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)+\widehat{\sigma}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle\Big)dr.\end{array}

(4.26)

The key point is that the BSDE (

\xi^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)

Proof.
By Itô’s formula, together with some approximate arguments, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle Y(s),\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(s)\rangle\Big]=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\langle-B_{x}(r)^{\top}Y(r)-\Sigma_{x}(r)^{\top}Z(r)-G_{x}(r)^{\top},\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\langle Y(r),B_{x}(r)\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)+\widehat{b}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle+\langle Z(r),\Sigma_{x}(r)X^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{1}(r)+\widehat{\sigma}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{s}\Big(-G_{x}(r)X_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)+\langle Y(r),\widehat{b}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle+\langle Z(r),\widehat{\sigma}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle\Big)dr.\end{array}

Then

\mathbb{E}\Big[H_{x}\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)+\int_{t}^{T}G_{x}(r)\widehat{X}_{1}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)dr\Big]=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\langle Y(r),\widehat{b}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle+\langle Z(r),\widehat{\sigma}_{1,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle\Big)dr.

Similarly,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle Y(s),\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(s)\rangle\Big]=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{s}\Big(\langle-B_{x}(r)^{\top}Y(r)-\Sigma_{x}(r)^{\top}Z(r)-G_{x}(r)^{\top},\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\langle Y(r),B_{x}(r)\widehat{X}_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)+\widehat{b}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle+\langle Z(r),\Sigma_{x}(r)\widehat{X}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}_{2}(r)+\widehat{\sigma}^{i}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)\rangle\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{s}\Big(-G_{x}(r)X_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)+\langle Y(r),\widehat{b}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle+\langle Z(r),\widehat{\sigma}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle\Big)dr.\end{array}

Then

\mathbb{E}\Big[H_{x}X_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(T)+\int_{t}^{T}G_{x}(r)X_{2}^{i,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}(r)dr\Big]=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\langle Y(r),\widehat{b}_{2,\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle+\langle Z(r),\widehat{\sigma}_{2,\varepsilon,\varepsilon\bar{\varepsilon}}^{i}(r)\rangle\Big)dr.

Consequently, we have (

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big[\Big(-\langle{\bf H}_{x}(r)-G_{x}(r),\bar{\xi}_{i}\rangle-\big({\bf H}(r)-G(r)\big)-\langle\bar{\xi}_{i},[{\bf H}_{xx}(r)-G_{xx}(r)]\bar{\xi}_{i}\rangle\Big){\bf 1}_{[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\tau}_{i}+\bar{\varepsilon})}(r)\\

5
Extensions and Conclusions

In this section, we first present some possible extensions.

Consider the system (state and cost functional) also contains
other types of controls. Thus, our controlled state equation looks like

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(s)=\Big[x+\int_{t}^{s}b(\tau_{0},\tau,X(\tau),u(\tau))d\tau+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(\tau_{0},\tau,X(\tau),u(\tau))dW(\tau)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\Big(\int_{\tau_{j}}^{s}b(\tau_{j},\tau,X(\tau),u(\tau))d\tau+\int_{\tau_{j}}^{s}\sigma(\tau_{j},\tau,X(\tau),u(\tau))dW(\tau)\Big)\Big]{\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\xi(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

(5.1)

with recursive functional

J(\tau_{0},t,x;\xi(\cdot))=Y(\tau_{0},t,t,\xi(\cdot)),

(5.2)

where
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
is the adapted solution to the following BSVIE

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y(s)=h(X(T))+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\ell(\tau_{j},X(\tau_{j}-0),\xi_{j}){\bf 1}_{[\tau_{j},T]}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{s}^{T}\Big(g(\tau_{0},\tau,X(\tau),u(\tau))+\sum_{j\geqslant 1}g(\tau_{j},\tau,X(\tau),u(\tau))\Big)d\tau-\int_{s}^{T}Z(s,\tau)dW(\tau),\quad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

(5.3)

where
g:\Delta_{*}[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times U\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
\ell:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathscr{K}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then, using the arguments before, we could derive the continuity of the value function, the corresponding HJB equation which should be a quasi-variational inequality. Further, one is able to show that the value function is the unique viscosity solution of its HJB equation.
Next, by making perturbations of the sizes and impulse moments of the optimal impulse control, together with general perturbation of the optimal (continuously active) control, one could obtain the corresponding maximum principle. We leave the details (maybe complicated) to interested readers.

It is possible that switching control is also get involved. In that case, the
results of perturbation with respect to the optimal switching moments are unknown. But, we suspect that the technique introduced in the current paper might be helpful. We encourage interested readers can complete this task.

We also point out that the system is allowed to depend on a Markov chain governed regime-switching. Then more interesting phenomena might appear. We expect the forms of corresponding quasi-variational inequality, and its maximum principle look like.

To conclude this paper, we would like to mention two major contributions. The first, we are able to provide a framework so that the impulse control problem is allowed to have a changing running cost. The second is to perturb the optimal impulse moments so that we derive a complete version of the maximum principle.

Gamma-aminobutyric acid affects the tumor microenvironment and angiogenesis, promoting breast cancer progression
Authors: Hui Wang, Jingxia Bao, Danfeng Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Breast cancer (BC) is one of the most common malignant tumors among women. Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) is abnormally expressed in various cancers, but its effect on BC remains unclear. This study aims to explore the expression changes of glutamic acid decarboxylase 1 (GAD1) in BC and its mechanism of promoting tumor occurrence and development by regulating GABA synthesis.Material and Methods:GAD1 expression and GABA levels in BC cells (MDA-MB-231) and normal breast epithelial cells (MCF-10A) were measured. GAD1 overexpression and knockdown cell lines were constructed to evaluate the changes in GABA levels and their effects on cell proliferation and invasion ability. Macrophage M2 polarization and protumor factor levels were analyzed by coculturing tumor cells with macrophages. Cell activity was detected by coculturing with CD8+ T cells, and tumor immune responses were examined by immunofluorescence and cytokine detection. In vitro tube formation was used to simulate angiogenesis, and angiogenesis ability was detected. Western blot was used to analyze the expression of vascular endothelial cell markers, and calcium-tracer dyes were used to detect calcium ion (Ca2+) influx in endothelial cells.Results:GAD1 expression and GABA level in MDA-MB-231 cells were significantly higher than those in normal cells (P <0.01). GAD1 overexpression enhanced cell proliferation, invasion, and migration abilities and simultaneously promoted macrophage polarization to M2, releasing protumor factors interleukin-10 and transforming growth factor-beta and inhibiting the antitumor effect of M1-type macrophages (P <0.05). GAD1 inhibition had the opposite effect (P <0.01). GAD1 overexpression also suppressed the activity of CD8+ T cells; decreased the expression of programmed death-1 in CD8+T cells and the levels of interferon-gamma, perforin, and granzyme B; weakened the immune response; and promoted tumor angiogenesis (P <0.05). The number of in vitro vascular lumen formation increased, the expression of vascular markers rose, and Ca2+ influx was enhanced (P <0.01). Reverse operation demonstrated that GABA promoted the initiation and advancement of BC by regulating the immune microenvironment and angiogenesis (P <0.05).Conclusion:GAD1 is highly expressed in BC. Increasing GABA synthesis promotes tumor cell proliferation and metastasis, regulates the immune microenvironment toward immunosuppression, and enhances tumor angiogenesis. This work reveals the crucial role of the GAD1-GABA pathway in BC and provides a potential therapeutic target for this disease.

INTRODUCTION

Breast cancer (BC) ranks as the most prevalent cancer among women across the globe and is a major threat to women’s health worldwide.[1] The International Agency for Research on Cancer published the latest global cancer data, reporting 2.3 million new occurrences and 670,000 related deaths.[2] Its incidence is high in developed countries and is affected by genetic, hormonal, and lifestyle factors.[3]BC has a lower mortality rate than some other cancers and can be cured by early detection and aggressive treatment. At present, the treatment methods and concepts of BC are constantly updated. From traditional surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, the approaches gradually developed to hormone therapy, targeted drug combinations, and other innovative programs. A lot of achievements have been made, effectively improving the survival rate of patients. However, the main causes of death in patients with BC are still recurrence, drug resistance, and tumor metastasis.[4-6] Therefore, studying the pathogenesis of BC and finding emerging therapeutic objectives to further reduce BC mortality are highly meaningful.

Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) is widespread in organisms and an important inhibitory neurotransmitter in the central nervous system of mammals; it plays a key role in the development and function of the nervous system.[7]Glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD), which includes GAD1 and GAD2, is the key rate-limiting enzyme for GABA synthesis.[8,9] Research has elucidated the physiological function of GABA. In addition to its role in the nervous system, it also regulates a range of physiological processes of the body, such as improving sleep, regulating blood pressure, and reducing anxiety. In addition, GABA plays a unique role in microbial fermentation production.[10,11]

The roles of GAD1 and GABA have attracted increasing attention in tumor research. GAD1 is highly expressed in a variety of tumors.[12] GABA undergoes abnormal changes in the tumor microenvironment (TME), which mainly includes malignant and nonmalignant cells and other soluble mediators that affect tumor growth.[13] GABA can affect antitumor immunity and regulate tumor growth by acting on tumor cells, activating downstream signaling pathways, or acting on immune cells.[14] For example, in a mouse model of colon cancer, the GABA secreted by B cells facilitated monocyte differentiation into anti-inflammatory macrophages and cytokine secretion, suppressing the antitumor CD8+ T cell response, limiting antitumor immunity, and promoting tumor growth.[15]

In a study of lung cancer, GABA accumulation by cancer cells activated astrocytes to create an inhibitory microenvironment conducive to tumor colonization and promote lung cancer metastasis, providing a new potential target for lung cancer treatment.[16] Dong et al.[17] clarified that GABA can regulate the polarization state of tumor-associated macrophages and affect the composition of immune cells in the TME, which in turn influences tumor growth and angiogenesis.

In view of the effects of GABA on the immune microenvironment and angiogenesis, this study aimed to reveal the mechanism of GABA in BC progression. Through an in-depth exploration of the interaction between GABA and BC cells and immune cells and the regulation of neovascularization, this work aspired to provide potential therapeutic targets for BC treatment.

GraphPad Prism 9.0 software was released by GraphPad Software (San Diego, California, USA). ImageJ software V1.52a was developed by the National Institutes of Health (Bethesda, MD).

Cell culture

BC cell line (MDA-MB-231, SNL-073), human mammary epithelial cells (MCF 10A, SNL-225), human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (HPBMC, SNP-H287), human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC, SNL-503), and human monocytic leukemia cells (THP-1, SNL-044) were obtained from SUNNCELL (Wuhan, China). These cell lines were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (12633020) with 10% fetal bovine serum (A5670701) and 1% streptomycin/penicillin (V900929) in an atmosphere containing 5% carbon dioxide with saturated humidity. All cells were identified through short tandem repeat and tested negative for mycoplasma.

Transfection

MDA-MB-231 cells were categorized into GAD1 knockdown group (sh-GAD1-1/2/3), GAD1 overexpression group (OEGAD1), and the negative control group (sh-NC and OE-NC). GAD1 overexpression, GAD1 knockdown, and negative control plasmids were obtained from Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The sequence information has been added to the supplementary materials. When the cell density was 80%, transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 2000. After 48 h, the cells were collected for RNA and protein extraction. The group of cells with the highest efficiency of overexpression and knockdown was selected and added to serum-free medium for further culture. After 24 h, the supernatant was collected.

Western blot (WB)

Experimental procedures were performed as described previously.[18] The following primary antibodies were obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, UK) at a dilution ratio of 1:1000 as required by the manufacturer: Anti-GAD1 (ab213508), anti-cluster of differentiation 31 (anti-CD31 and ab281583), anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF and ab32152), and anti-β-actin (ab8227). The secondary antibody (goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L [ab6702], Abcam) was diluted 2000 times. Protein signals were detected and visualized with enhanced chemiluminescence solution (P5755103). Densitometric analysis was conducted using ImageJ software V1.52a.

Coculture system

THP-1 cells were induced to differentiate into M0 macrophages by phorbol ester (20 ng/mL, ABS9107).[19] The culture medium supernatant obtained after cell transfection was cocultured with M0 macrophages. After 24 h, the cell supernatant was collected as conditioned medium for subsequent experiments. CD8+T cells were isolated from HPBMC using the CD8+T cell enrichment kit (17953). The conditioned medium was added to the upper Transwell chamber, and the CD8+T cells were cultured in the lower Transwell chamber. HUVECs were also cultured with the conditioned medium for subsequent experiments.

CCK-8 assay

After digestion with trypsin, the cells were resuspended in medium and seeded uniformly into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells/well. After 24, 48, and 72 h of culture, CCK-8 solution (MA0218-5) was added and incubated for 1 h. The measuring wavelength was set at 450 nm, the absorbance value A of each well was measured, and the cell survival rate was calculated.

Transwell invasion assay

Serum-free medium was used to dilute Matrigel matrix at a 1:8 ratio under low-temperature conditions, followed by thorough mixing and addition of 60 μL of the diluted Matrigel to the upper Transwell chambers. The chambers were incubated at 37°C for 1.5 h to allow the polymerization of the Matrigel into a thin gel layer. The lower chambers were filled with 600 μL of complete medium. The resuspended cells were carefully pipetted into the upper chambers, which were then positioned over the lower chambers to ensure that the medium in the lower chambers just reached the bottom of the upper chambers. The Transwell plate was incubated for 48 h. After the incubation period, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (P0099) and subjected to staining with 0.1% crystal violet (G1064). Cell invasion was observed and photographed under an optical microscope (Eclipse E200). Five fields were randomly selected, and the number of cells that passed through the membrane and adhered to the lower surface of the membrane was counted, and the average value was obtained.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

After coculturing for 48 h, the culture medium was collected and centrifuged. The supernatant was collected, and the levels of GABA (JYM0857Mo), IL-10 (JYM0155Mo), IL-6 (JYM0140Mo), TGF-β (JYM1722Mo), TNF-α (JYM0110Mo), granzyme B (CSB-E08718h), IFN-γ (JYM0162Mo), and perforin (U96-1136E) were detected using an ELISA kit. A standard curve with the standard sample concentration as the x-axis and the OD value as the y-axis was plotted, and the concentration of the target protein in the sample was calculated.

EdU assay

The cells were seeded into 96-well plates and incubated for 72 h. EdU reagent (C0071S) was then added and cultured for 90 min. After being washed with phosphate-buffered saline, DAPI solution (1 μg/mL, C0060) was added to the cells for 10 min to stain the nuclei. The number of EdU-positive cells in each group was counted to calculate the EdU-positive percentage.

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining

After the cell slide was fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, 0.3% Triton X-100 (P0096) was used for 15 min of permeabilization, and 5% BSA (36101ES25) was used for 30 min of blocking. The programmed death-1 (PD-1) antibodies (18106-1-AP, Proteintech) were incubated at 4°C overnight. The unbound primary antibody was removed, and Cy3-labeled goat antirabbit IgG (GB21303, Servicebio) was added at room temperature for 1 h. DAPI was used for nuclei counterstaining. The images were inspected under a fluorescence microscope (APX100). Average fluorescence intensity was analyzed using ImageJ software.

Flow cytometry

After the cells were collected and the cell concentration was adjusted, 100 μL of cells (1 × 106) was added. NC, CD86-PE, and CD206-PE were separately added to a blank tube, incubated at 4°C in the dark for 30 min, and then added with 500 μL of cell wash solution. After mixing well, each sample was centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min, and the supernatant was removed. The samples were washed twice with the cell wash solution, added with 500 μL of fixative, and mixed well. A flow cytometer was used for detection within 24 h.

Matrigel plastic tubular formation for testing angiogenesis

The matrix gel was thawed at 4°C. When the matrix gel became liquid, 60 μL was added to each well of the culture plate, which was then incubated at 37°C for 30–60 min to form a Matrigel gel substrate. The culture supernatant of MDA-MB-231 cells in each group was collected. HUVEC single cell suspension (1 × 105 cells/mL) was seeded in a 96-well culture plate at a volume of 100 μL/well, added with 200 μL of the conditioned medium from each group, and cultured in a cell incubator for 24 h. The formation of vessel-like structures was observed under a microscope and photographed, and the number of tubes was analyzed using ImageJ software.

Determination of Ca2+ concentration

Fluo-3/AM calcium indicator (40703ES50) was used in this experiment. First, a 2–5 mM storage solution was prepared using high-purity anhydrous dimethyl sulfoxide at −20°C, which needed to be warmed back to room temperature. For the preparation of the working solution, the stock solution was diluted to 2–20 μM with buffer and then added with 0.04% pluronic F-127 and 0.5–4 mM probenecid. The precultured cells were removed and washed three times to remove as much serum or phenol red-containing medium as possible to improve staining. The prepared working solution was added, and the cells were incubated at 37°C for 30– 60 min, washed three times to ensure the removal of residual indicators, and finally covered with buffer for microscopy. Average fluorescence intensity was analyzed using ImageJ software.

Bioinformatic analysis

Messenger RNA (mRNA) expression (transcript per million) of GAD1 in 114 normal samples and 1097 primary tumor samples was analyzed through the University of Alabama at Birmingham Cancer (UALCAN) website (http://ualcan.path. uab.edu/) based on the Cancer Genome Atlas database.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software 9.0, and the data were described by mean ± standard deviation. Statistical comparisons between two groups were conducted using a t-test, and comparisons among multiple groups were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. P <0.05 was taken as an indication of a statistically significant difference.

RESULTS

GAD1 is highly expressed in BC and promotes GABA synthesis

The results showed that the mRNA level of GAD1 in BC cells (MDA-MB-231) was significantly higher than that in normal breast epithelial cells (MCF 10A) (P <0.001) [Figure 1a]. WB analysis revealed that GAD1 was substantially expressed at a higher level in MDA-MB-231 cells than in MCF 10A cells (P <0.01) [Figure 1b and c]. ELISA detection results also indicated that the secretion of GABA in BC cells significantly increased (P <0.001) [Figure 1d]. Analysis on the UALCAN website showed that GAD1 expression was significantly increased in patients with BC (P <0.001) [Figure 1e]. These results suggested that GAD1 may have a crucial impact on the occurrence and progression of BC through the metabolic pathway of GABA synthesis.

GABA promotes the growth and metastasis of BC cells

To evaluate the effect of GABA on the growth and metastasis of BC cells, GAD1 overexpression and knockdown cell lines were constructed. The results showed a markedly upregulated expression of GAD1 in the OEGAD1 group (P <0.001) [Figure 2a-c]. The sh-GAD1-2 group had the best knockdown effect and was chosen for the following experiments (P <0.001) [Figure 2d-f]. ELISA results indicated that the GABA level in the cell culture medium notably increased after GAD1 overexpression but markedly decreased after GAD1 knockdown (P <0.001) [Figure 2g and h]. Next, we detected the proliferation and invasion of tumor cells after changing GAD1 expression. CCK-8 results exhibited that GAD1 overexpression notably enhanced cell viability, whereas its knockdown significantly inhibited cell viability (P <0.001) [Figure 3a and b]. EdU assay results showed that the proliferation rate of BC cells significantly increased after GAD1 overexpression but decreased after GAD1 knockdown (P <0.001) [Figure 3c and d]. Cell invasion assay showed that the invasion speed of the OE-GAD1 group was considerably expedited compared with that of the OE-NC group, whereas the invasion speed of the sh-GAD1 group was significantly slower (P <0.05) [Figure 3e and f], suggesting that GABA can effectively promote the proliferation and metastasis ability of BC cells.

GABA inhibits M1 polarization and promotes M2 polarization

We cocultured tumor cells from each treatment group with macrophages and detected the expression of surface markers CD206 of protumor M2-type macrophages and CD86 of M1-type macrophages. As shown in Figure 4a-d, GAD1 overexpression significantly upregulated CD206 and downregulated CD86 (P <0.01). Its knockdown had the opposite effect (P <0.01). ELISA detection results showed that GAD1 overexpression brings about a rise in the secretion of IL-10 and TGF-β by M2-type macrophages and a decline in the secretion of IL-6 and TNF-α by M1-type macrophages (P <0.01). Its knockdown resulted in a decrease in IL-10 and TGF-β by M2-type macrophages and an increase in IL-6 and TNF-α by M1-type macrophages (P <0.01) [Figure 4e-h].

GABA restrains the activity of CD8+ T cells

The polarization of M2 macrophages inhibits the activity of CD8+T cells.[20] Therefore, we cocultured CD8+T cells with conditioned medium. The CCK-8 assay was employed to determine the viability of CD8+T cells. The results showed that the viability of CD8+T cells markedly decreased in the coculture system of the overexpression GAD1 group but significantly increased in the knockdown GAD1 group (P <0.001) [Figure 5a and b]. The results of IF detection of PD-1 expression showed that GAD1 overexpression decreased PD-1 expression, whereas GAD1 knockdown increased PD-1 expression (P <0.01) [Figure 5c and d]. ELISA was used to detect the concentrations of cytokines in the cell supernatant. The results showed that the concentrations of IFN-γ, perforin, and granzyme B in the cell supernatant of the OE-GAD1 group markedly decreased compared with those in the OE-NC group, and the concentrations of cytokines in the sh-GAD1 group were enhanced compared with those in the sh-NC group (P <0.05) [Figure 5e-g]. This finding indicates that M2-type macrophages induced by tumor cells can inhibit the antitumor activity of CD8+T cells.

GABA promotes the formation of tumor neovascularization

The effect of GABA on tumor neovascularization in vitro was evaluated. Tube formation assay showed that GAD1 overexpression markedly increased the number of vascular formations, whereas its knockdown considerably decreased vascular formations (P <0.05) [Figure 6a and b]. WB results indicated that compared with those in the OE-NC group, the protein levels of endothelial cell markers CD31 and vascular endothelial growth factor VEGF in the OE-GAD1 group were significantly increased, whereas the protein levels of CD31 and VEGF in the sh-GAD1 group were notably decreased (P <0.01) [Figure 6c and d]. The intracellular calcium ion (Ca2+) influx in endothelial cells was detected using calcium tracer dyes. The results showed that GAD1 increased the intracellular Ca2+ influx in vascular endothelial cells, affecting vascular endothelial cells; its knockdown decreased the intracellular Ca2+ influx in vascular endothelial cells (P <0.01) [Figure 6e and f]. This finding indicates that GABA promotes tumor neovascularization in vitro.

DISCUSSION

In this research, we revealed a relationship between GAD1 expression and GABA synthesis in BC and explored the multiple roles of GABA during tumor progression. Our results showed that GAD1 expression and GABA synthesis were extremely active in the BC cell line, consistent with the results of previous studies suggesting the potential oncogenic role for GAD1 in BC.[21]

Our experiments showed that GAD1 overexpression led to an increase in GABA synthesis and secretion and promoted BC cell proliferation and metastasis. This finding is consistent with the suggestion by Huang and Cao[22] that GABA promotes cell proliferation in multiple cancer types. In our cell experiments, GAD1 overexpression significantly increased the proliferation rate of MDA-MB-231 cells and enhanced cell invasion ability, whereas its knockdown resulted in opposite results. Some researchers integrated metabolomics and transcriptomic data and analyzed the datasets of patients with BC and found that GABA metabolism is the main dysregulated pathway, and GABA has been experimentally proven to play a promoting role in BC metastasis.[23] These results provide evidence in support of GABA as a tumor promoter, particularly in BC cells.

We also found that BC cells overexpressing GAD1 could drive the polarization of macrophages to protumor M2 type and inhibit the polarization of macrophages to antitumor M1 type, indicating that GABA may regulate the polarization state of macrophages by affecting the TME. Dou et al.[24] also reported that GAD1 knockdown in oral cancer inhibited oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, and GABA could rescue the cell proliferation inhibition caused by GAD1 knockdown by activating GABA B receptors. The GABA secreted by OSCC cells can promote the polarization of macrophages to M2 and inhibit antitumor immunity. GABA can regulate the immune cell subtypes, such as macrophages and T cells, in the tumor immune microenvironment of glioblastoma, altering the immune environment around the tumor and facilitating the generation of anti-tumor immune responses, thereby inhibiting tumor growth or improving treatment efficacy.[25] M2 macrophages can enhance the immune escape ability of tumors and promote tumor progression by secreting immunosuppressive factors such as IL-10 and TGF-β.[26] In our experiments, GAD1 overexpression significantly increased cytokine secretion in M2 macrophages but decreased it in M1 macrophages, further supporting a role for GABA in TME regulation. In addition, the polarization of M2 macrophages inhibited CD8+T cell function in BC.[20] This inhibitory effect was verified in our coculture experiments. CCK-8 assay showed decreased CD8+T cell activity, decreased PD-1 expression, and significantly decreased levels of immune-related cytokines in the GAD1 overexpression group. Researchers demonstrated that tumor cell-derived GABA inhibited CD8+T cell intratumoral infiltration in vivo, generating an immunosuppressed TME.[18] These results further suggest that GABA promotes tumor immune escape by affecting the TME.

Finally, our study discovered the promoting effect of GABA on tumor angiogenesis. Reports showed that tumor-associated macrophages can promote angiogenesis.[27-30] In the present study, the results of the tube formation assay and WB showed that GAD1 overexpression increased the number of blood vessels formed and elevated the expression of endothelial cell markers CD31 and VEGF. This finding is consistent with previous studies showing that GABA affects TAM polarization and neovascularization.[17] Thus, our results further highlight the potential mechanism of GABA in promoting tumor neovascularization.

Given the multifaceted role of GAD1/GABA in BC progression, targeting this pathway may provide a novel therapeutic strategy. Our findings suggest that the combination of GABA inhibition and immune checkpoint blockade (e.g., anti-PD-1) may enhance antitumor immunity, a hypothesis that warrants further investigation. However, our research also has some limitations. For instance, our study only included in vitro experiments and lacked in vivo studies and clinical result verification. Although we observed a correlation among GAD1, GABA, and BC cell angiogenesis/CD8+T cell function, we did not conduct rescue experiments to fully elucidate the mechanism. Future research should combine animal experiments and clinical studies to explore the inhibitory effect of GABA on BC and verify its potential as a treatment. In-depth mechanism exploration, rescue experiments based on inhibitors, and verification of signaling pathways are necessary to confirm the existence of the “GAD1-GABA angiogenesis/immunology” regulatory axis.

Overall, our study confirms that GAD1 is a key regulator of BC progression through GABA-mediated effects on tumor proliferation, immune evasion, and angiogenesis. These findings expand the understanding of neurotransmitter signaling in cancer and highlight the GAD1/GABA axis as a promising therapeutic target.

SUMMARY

This study provides novel insights into the important effect of GAD1-mediated GABA synthesis in BC cell proliferation, TME regulation, and angiogenesis. The findings laid the foundation for further development of therapeutic strategies targeting the GAD1 pathway.

Doping dependence of the low temperature planar carrier density in overdoped YBa 2 Cu 3 O 7-δ Preprint: APS/123-QED
R. Nicholls, R. D. H. Hinlopen, J. Ayres, T. Kotte, T. Förster, J. Park, J. Sourd, A. Carrington, N. E. Hussey

Abstract
Whether a quantum critical point (QCP) demarcates the end of the pseudogap (PG) regime in hole-doped cuprates at a singular doping level p ∗ ≈ 0.19 p^{*}\approx 0.19 is still an open question. A crucial component of the puzzle is the manner in which the carrier density predicted by electronic structure calculations is recovered for p > p ∗ p>p^{*} . Here, we use magnetic fields up to 67 T to suppress superconductivity down to 50 K, allowing us to simultaneously measure the low temperature Hall number n H n_{\textrm{H}} and the in-plane resistivity anisotropy ρ a / ρ b \rho_{a}/\rho_{b} in overdoped Y 1-x Ca x Ba 2 Cu 3 O 7-δ single crystals. We confirm a previous finding [Badoux et al. , Nature 531 , 210 (2016)] that n H n_{\textrm{H}} (50 K) exhibits a sharp increase below p ∗ p^{\ast} and use the measured resistivity anisotropy to obtain the planar carrier density n p ​ l = n H ​ ( ρ a / ρ b ) − 1 n_{pl}=n_{\textrm{H}}(\rho_{a}/\rho_{b})^{-1} . The doping dependence of n p ​ l n_{pl} (50 K) reveals two key findings: (i) at optimal doping, n p ​ l ≈ p n_{pl}\approx p and (ii) the sharp rise in n H ​ ( p ) n_{\rm H}(p) is softened to such an extent that the full Fermi volume ( n p ​ l = 1 + p n_{pl}=1+p ) is only partially recovered at p ∗ p^{\ast} . The latter result disfavors a conventional QCP scenario in which the PG end point leads to a reconstructed Fermi surface.



One of the most striking features of the cuprate phase diagram is the emergence of a near-parabolic dome of high-temperature superconductivity out of a Mott insulating parent state
Keimer 2015
. As the parent crystal is doped with
p
holes per Cu atom, the superconducting transition temperature
T_{c}
rises to a maximum at optimal doping (
p_{opt}=0.16
) before falling back to zero at the edge of the superconducting (SC) dome (
p_{sc}\approx 0.3

, Fermi liquid (FL) behavior is recovered

;
Nakamae et al. 2003
and the Fermi volume contains
1+p
holes

The exact doping level at which the hole initially localized in the Mott state becomes fully itinerant is still an open question, and the mechanism(s) of interaction between the itinerant and localized holes are still unclear. The Hall effect can provide information about the planar carrier density
n_{pl}
and its evolution with doping across the relevant part of the phase diagram. However, the very high magnetic fields needed to suppress the SC state restrict measurements of the low-
T

R_{\textrm{H}}=1/(en_{\textrm{H}})
while the assumed relationship

is complicated by the presence of the pseudogap

;
LeBoeuf et al. 2007

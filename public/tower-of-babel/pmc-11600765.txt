Hidden blood loss of minimally invasive transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion of lumbar degenerative diseases in patients with osteoporosis: a retrospective study
Authors: Shufeng Lin, Shuanglong Liu, Yizhong Li, Yipeng Yan, Hui Ye
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo compare hidden blood loss (HBL) of minimally invasive transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (Mis-TLIF) of lumbar degenerative diseases in patients with or without osteoporosis (OP) and identify the risk factors for HBL with OP.MethodsThe data from 156 patients with lumbar degenerative diseases, who underwent Mis-TLIF between January 2018 and December 2021, were retrospectively analyzed. The patients were divided into the Osteoporosis and Non-osteoporosis groups. We recorded demographic characteristics and blood-related parameters in each group. Blood loss was measured by quantifying visible blood loss and calculating HBL using preoperative hematocrit (Hctpre) and postoperative hematocrit (Hctpost). Pearson or Spearman correlation analysis was used to investigate the association between OP patient’s characteristics and HBL. Multivariate linear regression analysis was used to confirm independent risk factors of HBL in patients with OP.ResultsThe mean HBL of the Osteoporosis and Non-osteoporosis groups was 608.27 ± 175.23 and 173.04 ± 67.55 ml, respectively. There were significant differences in age, BMI, subcutaneous fat thickness, muscle thickness, Hctpost, average hematocrit of preoperative and postoperative (Hctave), visible blood loss, HBL and volume of allogeneic blood transfusions between the two groups (P < 0.05). Multivariate linear regression analysis showed that Hctpost, T-score, use of tranexamic acid (TXA) and preoperative regular anti-osteoporosis treatment were independent risk factors for HBL in patients with OP.ConclusionThere was significant perioperative HBL in patients with OP. Hctpost, T-score, use of TXA and preoperative regular anti-osteoporosis treatment were independent risk factors for HBL in patients with OP. More attention should be paid to the perioperative period to ensure the safety of patients with OP.

Background

Aging of the population has led to increased prominence of degenerative spinal diseases, especially lumbar degenerative diseases, which are frequently seen in orthopedics. Lumbar degenerative diseases include lumbar disc herniation, lumbar spondylolysis, lumbar stenosis and lumbar degenerative scoliosis [1]. Osteoporosis (OP), mostly seen in elderly people, is a systemic bone metabolic disease characterized by low bone mass and bone microstructural damage, resulting in increased bone fragility and susceptibility to fracture. Patients with lumbar degenerative diseases are often complicated with OP, and mainly suffer from back and leg pain and severe intermittent claudication, and may even be bedridden for long periods, which lead to serious physical damage [2, 3].

Minimally invasive transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (Mis-TLIF) is a popular treatment for lumbar degenerative diseases, because it is less invasive than open surgery, results in accelerated postoperative rehabilitation, fewer postoperative complications and reduced intraoperative bleeding [4]. However, in clinical treatment, there are still some patients with anemia after this minimally invasive surgery [5]. In clinical practice, surgeons measure visible blood loss (VBL), including intraoperative bleeding and postoperative drainage, but ignore the loss of blood caused by tissue interstitial extravasation, blood accumulated in the surgical site, and hemolysis, which are known as hidden blood loss (HBL) [6]. An increasing number of studies have reported HBL after hip and knee arthroplasty [7, 8]. HBL in patients undergoing MIS-TLIF was seriously underestimated and accounted for a larger percentage of total blood loss (TBL) [5]. HBL affects the patient’s general condition, resulting in postoperative hemodynamic instability, blood transfusion, recovery delay and other problems [8]. To our knowledge, there are few reports that specifically focus on HBL and analyze the risk factors associated with HBL following MIS-TLIF surgery in patients with OP. Therefore, this study compared intraoperative blood loss, postoperative drainage and HBL of Mis-TLIF of lumbar degenerative diseases in patients with or without OP, and identified the risk factors for HBL in OP patients.

Methods

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) symptoms and physical signs were consistent with the clinical manifestations of lumbar degeneration, such as back or leg pain, lower limb numbness and intermittent claudication; (2) imaging-indicated lumbar disc herniation, lumbar spondylolysis, lumbar stenosis and lumbar degenerative scoliosis, with or without OP; (3) the surgical method chosen was single-segment Mis-TLIF; and (4) all operations were performed by the same surgeon. Exclusion criteria: (1) complicated with hematological diseases, or a need for long-term use of anticoagulants; (2) injuries in other parts or organ bleeding; (3) with lumbar infections and tumors; and (4) with previous lumbar surgery. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University.

Data extraction

A total of 156 patients with lumbar degenerative diseases, treated in the Second Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University between January 2018 and December 2021, were included in this study. Their ages ranged from 31 to 82 years, with an average of 58.85 ± 9.89 years. There were 102 women and 54 men, including 84 patients with OP, and 72 patients with non-osteoporosis. All the patients were treated with single-segment Mis-TLIF, and drainage tubes were routinely placed under the fascia after surgery. Patients with lumbar degenerative diseases were divided into the Osteoporosis and Non-osteoporosis groups, based on their bone density. We recorded demographic characteristics such as sex, age, height, weight, hypertension (blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg), surgical duration, subcutaneous fat thickness, muscle thickness, hospital stay, Amereican Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, surgical segment, and VAS score on the first day after surgery. Other-related data such as intraoperative blood loss (IBL), drainage on Day 1 after surgery, preoperative and postoperative hematocrit, amount of blood transfusions, and the presence or absence of preoperative regular anti-osteoporosis treatment and use of tranexamic acid (TXA) during or after surgery.

Bone density testing and anti-osteoporosis treatment: bone mineral density (BMD) in the hip, lumbar spine and radius was determined by dual energy X-ray absorptiometry (Hologic Discovery-A DXA). According to the WHO criteria, osteoporosis is diagnosed based on the lowest T-score among the hip, lumbar spine and radius. T ≤ −2.5 SD was considered as OP, T>−2.5 was considered as non-osteoporosis. According to the guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of primary OP [9], commonly used anti-osteoporosis drugs include active vitamin D, calcitonin, zoledronic acid and teriparatide. Regular anti-osteoporosis treatment for at least 6 months before surgery was considered as preoperative anti-osteoporosis treatment. A loading dose of TXA (2 g for adults) was administered before the beginning of surgery and followed by another loading dose at the end of surgery. Preoperative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was used to determine the distance of the lamina from the skin surface, thickness of the paraspinal muscles, and thickness of the subcutaneous fat. These measurements were all performed at the L4 level, using sagittal views (Fig. 1). To prevent interobserver variability, measurements were performed twice by the same observer, and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated. An ICC value greater than 0.75 indicated excellent reproducibility, which was achieved for all measurements in our study.

Calculation of HBL

HBL was calculated using the formula [10, 11

Total blood volume (ml) = [k1 × height (m)³ + k2 × weight (kg) + k3] × 1000. For male patients, k1 = 0.3669, k2 = 0.03219 and k3 = 0.6041, and for female patients, k1 = 0.3561, k2 = 0.03308 and k3 = 0.1833. Total predicted blood loss (ml) = total blood volume × (Hctpre − Hctpost) /Hctave, where Hctpre was preoperative hematocrit, Hctpost was hematocrit on day 1 or 2 after surgery, and Hctave was the average of Hctpre and Hctpost. HBL (ml) = total predicted blood loss + total blood transfusion (1 U concentrated red blood cells = 200 ml standard red blood cells) − (IBL + postoperative drainage). IBL included the sum of soaked gauzes and the drainage fluid in the negative pressure suction tank (minus the flushing fluid). Postoperative drainage referred to the amount of fluid drained from the postoperative drainage tube. There was little exudation on the wound dressing, thus it was generally negligible.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 24.0, and the quantitative data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. All data are normally distributed or approximately normally distributed. Student’s t-test was used to compare continuous variables and chi squared test was applied to compare categorical data between two groups. Pearson or Spearman correlation analysis was used to investigate the association between OP patient’s characteristics and HBL. Multivariate linear regression analysis was used to confirm independent risk factors of HBL in patients with OP. Predictor inclusion was based on theoretical assumptions, a significant correlation between independent variables and the dependent variable (P < 0.05), and the removal of highly correlated variables to address multicollinearity. These criteria defined the final predictive model. In all analyses, P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Reproducibility results: the consistency of the same observer’s two measurements with an ICC of 0.89–0.93 indicates good repeatability.

Seventy-two patients in the Non-osteoporosis group had an average VBL of 378.54 ± 115.17 ml and an average HBL of 173.04 ± 67.55 ml.

Discussion

The results of this study showed that the VBL and HBL of OP patients was significantly higher than non-osteoporosis patients. This demonstrates that OP significantly increases perioperative blood loss in patients with lumbar degenerative diseases when undergoing Mis-TLIF. It might be that lumbar degenerative diseases patients with OP have reduced bone mass and trabecular thinning, and fracturing. When the screws are passed through the cancellous bone via the pedicle channel, the trabecular structure is destroyed [11], leading to blood in the vertebrae permeating along the pedicle channel and screw thread, which significantly increases blood loss. Internal fixation of the spine requires healthy bone storage, and patients with OP have high bone loss, which is likely to cause complications such as pedicle fracture, vertebral compression fracture, internal fixation failure and adjacent vertebral fracture, and even cause greater blood loss [12, 13].

HBL occurs during the perioperative period in most orthopedic surgery. The average HBL in total hip arthroplasty was 471 ml, accounting for 26% of the TBL, according to Sehat et al. [14]. In spinal surgery, Smorgick et al. [15] showed that the average total loss of blood in posterior spinal fusion surgery was 1439 ml, of which HBL accounted for 42%. Xu et al. [16] showed HBL of 362.8 ml in a patient undergoing TLIF for degenerative lumbar disease, accounting for 47% of the TBL. Similarly, in our study, for lumbar degenerative diseases patients undergoing Mis-TLIF surgery, the HBL was 173.04 ml, accounting for 31.4% of the TBL. However, for patients with OP, HBL accounted for as much as 46.1% of the TBL, which was larger than expected. Mis-TLIF removed some of the paravertebral muscles, ligaments and some of the lamina, leading to excessive perioperative VBL. Under this circumstance, HBL is often ignored in the assessment of perioperative bleeding, especially in cases that are complicated with OP and other conditions. Excessive HBL affects patients’ general condition, resulting in postoperative hemodynamic instability, blood transfusion, recovery delay and other problems [8]. Our study also showed that the volume of allogeneic blood transfusions of OP patients was significantly higher than in patients with non-osteoporosis.

We included preoperative regular anti-osteoporosis treatment in the analysis. Our results showed that regular anti-osteoporosis treatment for at least 6 months before surgery was an independent risk factor for HBL in OP patients and negatively correlated with the amount of HBL. It might be that regular anti-osteoporosis increases bone mineral density (BMD) and reduces the risk of fractures in postmenopausal women with OP [17]. Even some authors believe that patients with OP, multiple-segment fixation, or age > 65 years, with a dual energy X-ray absorptiometry outcome of T ≤ −1.0, whose surgery can be postponed, are recommended to receive at least 6 months of anti-osteoporosis treatment before surgery. In other patients whose operations cannot be postponed, immediate anti-osteoporosis treatment is needed after surgery [18]. Therefore, it is necessary that spinal surgeons should lay more emphasis on OP during the perioperative period.

We also found that the use of TXA during or after spinal surgery was an independent risk factor in our study, and that the total amount of HBL was negatively correlated with TXA. According to Willner et al. [19], patients who received tranexamic acid had a 49% reduction in blood loss and required 80% fewer blood transfusions compared with patients treated with placebo. Large doses of tranexamic acid provide an effective and inexpensive method for reducing blood loss during spinal surgery. Ren et al. showed that topical tranexamic acid can reduce HBL without increasing the risk of deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, and other complications [20]. On the basis of the above examples, we think the application of tranexamic acid can effectively reduce HBL.

It is controversial that age, BMI, subcutaneous fat thickness and muscle thickness are related to HBL. Madsen et al. [21] have concluded that advanced age is an independent risk factor for increased HBL. In contrast, Wu et al. [22] found that age was not related to HBL. Zhou et al. [5] reported that muscle thickness was the key factor in predicting HBL in Mis-TLIF, whereas subcutaneous fat thickness and BMI were not risk factors for HBL. In our study, age, BMI, subcutaneous fat thickness and muscle thickness showed significant differences between the Osteoporosis and Non-osteoporosis groups, but were not independent risk factors for HBL in OP patients. This may be because patients with lumbar degenerative disease combined with OP are older and have thinner muscle and fat.

Our study showed that Hctpost was less in the Osteoporosis group, while Hctpre was no different between the two groups, which may be due to postoperative fluid dilution and blood loss leading to more change in Hct [23]. On the one hand, all patients were free from anemia preoperatively, so the Hctpre of the two groups was no different. On the other hand, OP patients had more blood loss than non-osteoporosis patients had, which resulted in a significant reduction in Hctpost. Some studies have shown that postoperative fluid dilution could contribute to Hct change [24], which might explain why Hctpost was an independent risk factor for HBL in OP patients. In our study, T-score was also an independent risk factor. It is likely that lower T-scores indicate more severe osteoporosis and, consequently, greater HBL.

There were some limitations to this study. First, this study was retrospective and had a small sample, which failed to reflect most people’s real circumstances. Data biases existed, so a larger sample size is needed for validation. Second, some patients had a large amount of blood leakage from the postoperative wound, which was not counted as postoperative blood loss, and the reliability of the conclusion was affected when calculating blood loss using hematocrit after surgery.

Conclusions

Aging of the population has resulted in a substantial increase in the number of patients with lumbar degenerative diseases and OP. A large amount of HBL after Mis-TLIF for lumbar degenerative diseases was incurred in the OP patients, and HBL and its proportion of TBL were higher than other non-OP patients. T-score, Hctpost, preoperative regular anti-osteoporosis treatment and the use of TXA during or after surgery were independent risk factors for HBL in OP patients and negatively correlated with the amount of HBL. We should pay more attention to perioperative HBL to ensure the safety of patients with OP.

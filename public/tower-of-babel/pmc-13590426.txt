Mutation Spectrum and Novel Rare Variants in a Han Chinese Hearing Loss Cohort Using a Tiered Sequencing Strategy
Authors: Yu Zhang, Shimei Yuan, Yafang Wan, Tian Li, Lanlan Xu, Wei Yang, Changchun Niu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundHereditary hearing loss is a genetically heterogeneous disorder posing challenges for molecular diagnosis. This study aimed to characterize the mutation spectrum, including rare and previously unreported variants, using a tiered strategy in a Han Chinese cohort from Southwest China.MethodsIn this retrospective study, 43 patients underwent targeted hotspot screening, followed by whole‐exome sequencing in 11 patients with no hotspot variants detected. Variants were annotated and interpreted according to American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics guidelines.ResultsInitial hotspot screening established molecular diagnoses in 23 patients based on seven pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in MT‐RNR1, GJB2, and SLC26A4. A previously unreported SLC12A2 missense variant, c.2936A>G (p.Glu979Gly) was identified as a variant of uncertain significance and retained as a candidate finding. Background

Hearing loss (HL) is a common condition with major global impact, affecting more than 1.5 billion people worldwide (World Health Organization 2021). Genetic factors account for over 60% of cases (Yuan et al. 2020), and more than 220 genes associated with deafness have been identified (MORL, n.d.). The prevalence and spectrum of pathogenic variants differ across ethnic groups, with particularly high carrier rates reported in the Chinese population (Feng et al. 2024; Hou et al. 2024), and HL also shows marked genetic heterogeneity (Koffler et al. 2015). In routine clinical practice, PCR‐hybridization assays remain the main screening tool in many regions of China, including the Southwest, because of their low cost and ease of use. However, these assays typically target only a limited set of mutation hotspots. Consequently, they often fail to capture rare variants, leaving a proportion of patients without a definitive molecular explanation.

The advent of next‐generation sequencing (NGS), including targeted panels and whole‐exome sequencing (WES), has facilitated the identification of rare genetic etiologies (Wu et al. 2022; Zeng et al. 2022). While tiered strategies—utilizing hotspot screening followed by WES—have been proposed to balance efficiency and depth, population‐specific mutational data for the Han Chinese population in Southwest China remain sparse.

In this study, we applied a tiered sequencing approach to investigate the molecular landscape of HL in a Han Chinese cohort. By combining hotspot panel testing with WES, we aimed to characterize the mutation spectrum and identify novel or rare genetic contributors, thereby providing population‐specific data for this region.

Methods

Subjects

A cohort of 43 patients with hearing loss was recruited from the central region of Chongqing, Southwest China. Patients with clinical features suggestive of syndromic hearing loss were not included. Clinical information, including age at onset, family history, and history of ototoxic drug exposure, was collected through patient interviews and review of available medical records. Patients were classified according to the reported age at onset of HL. In addition, available family members were recruited for genetic analysis. Peripheral blood (3–5 mL) was collected in EDTA tubes, and genomic DNA was extracted using standard protocols.

Genetic Sequencing and Analysis

Initially, all 43 patients underwent a high‐throughput targeted screening assay covering 208 common deafness‐associated variants across 24 genes (Table S1), designed to detect prevalent hotspots in the Chinese population. Subsequently, WES was performed on a selected subset of 11 patients who tested negative in the initial screening. Sequencing was performed by a certified commercial service provider (BGI Genomics, Shenzhen, China) with standard protocols. Raw data were filtered and aligned to the human reference genome (hg19/GRCh37). Then, variants were annotated using ANNOVAR (Wang et al. 2010) and cross‐referenced with multiple databases, including dbSNP (build 151) (Sherry et al. 2001), gnomAD (v2.1.1) (Karczewski et al. 2020), ExAC (release 0.3.1) (Lek et al. 2016), ClinVar (accessed May 2026) (Landrum et al. 2018), and HGMD professional (accessed May 2026) (Stenson et al. 2017).

Candidate protein‐altering variants were prioritized in known hearing‐loss‐associated genes and genes relevant to the HL phenotype. Prioritization also considered case‐specific clinical information, the presumed mode of inheritance, computational predictions, and published evidence. Population allele frequency was evaluated using the East Asian population dataset of gnomAD (v2.1.1).

Pathogenicity assessment incorporated multiple in silico prediction tools, including MutationTaster (Steinhaus et al. 2021), REVEL (Ioannidis et al. 2016), and M‐CAP (Jagadeesh et al. 2016) for missense effect prediction. Evolutionary conservation was evaluated using GERP++ (Davydov et al. 2010) scores and multiple sequence alignment of mammalian orthologs generated with UGENE (Okonechnikov et al. 2012) software. Variant classification was determined according to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP) framework (Richards et al. 2015).

Results

Overview of Cohort Characteristics and Initial Hotspot Screening

The cohort included 43 patients with HL, comprising 30 males (69.8%), and 13 females (30.2%), with an age range of 21–53 years (mean ± SD: 39 ± 7.2 years; median: 39 years). These patients represented 37 families. Five families included two unrelated spouses, and one family included two biological brothers (P25 and P26). Except for P25 and P26, all participants were biologically unrelated. Patients were classified according to the reported age at onset of HL, including congenital, prelingual (1–3 years), childhood/adolescent (4–17 years), and adult (≥ 18 years) onset (Table 1). Sixteen patients (37.2%) reported exposure to ototoxic medication, and 14 (32.6%) had a family history of hearing loss. Detailed demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Additional Variants Identified by Whole‐Exome Sequencing

Whole‐exome sequencing (WES) was performed in 11 patients in whom no variants were detected by the initial hotspot screening, according to the predefined tiered sequencing strategy. WES identified six rare variants in four individuals across four genes. Findings that established a molecular diagnosis or were retained as a candidate finding are summarized in Table 3. The unresolved WES finding that did not establish a molecular diagnosis is provided in Table S2. Patient P37 had hearing loss with onset at 5–6 years of age and carried two heterozygous ILDR1 variants, c.772C>T (p.Gln258Ter) and c.206C>A (p.Pro69His). A previously unreported variant in SLC12A2 was identified and classified as a variant of uncertain significance (VUS). In addition, a CDH23 nonsense variant was identified, which has not been reported in Han Chinese patients with hearing loss to our knowledge.

Characterization of Rare and Notable Variants

SLC12A2 c.2936A>G (p. Glu979Gly)

In case P7, a previously unreported missense variant SLC12A2 (c.2936A>G, p.Glu979Gly) was identified. According to the ACMG/AMP guidelines, the variant was classified as a VUS based on currently available evidence. P7 had congenital hearing loss, which overlaps with previous reports of DFNA78. The proband reported hearing impairment in her father, spouse, and son. This family history was considered compatible with a presumed autosomal dominant inheritance pattern (Figure 3A). However, only the proband underwent genetic testing. Segregation of the variant could therefore not be assessed. In silico prediction yielded discordant results across multiple computational algorithms. Multiple sequence alignment showed that the glutamic acid residue at position 979 of the SLC12A2‐encoded protein is highly conserved among mammalian species (Figure 4). Therefore, the clinical significance of this variant remains uncertain.

CDH23 c.5311C>T (p.Arg1771Ter)

In case P47, two heterozygous CDH23 variants were identified, including a nonsense variant c.5311C>T (p. Arg1771Ter) and a missense variant c.4562A>G (p. Asn1521Ser). The truncating variant c.5311C>T, although recorded in public databases, has not been reported in Han Chinese patients with hearing loss to our knowledge. Clinically, the proband presented with congenital deafness and had an affected older sister, while other family members (parents, two sons, one daughter, and one grandson) had normal hearing. Neither the proband nor her affected sister reported visual symptoms or known ocular abnormalities. Genetic testing showed that the unaffected mother carried c.5311C>T but not c.4562A>G. The two variants were therefore presumed to be in trans (Figure 3B).

Discussion

This study applied a tiered genetic testing strategy combining targeted hotspot screening and WES in a Han Chinese cohort with hearing loss from Southwest China. The initial hotspot screening identified variants mainly in MT‐RNR1, GJB2, and SLC26A4. In addition, second‐tier WES identified additional rare variants beyond the hotspot panel. These findings included additional molecular diagnoses, a candidate finding, and an unresolved heterozygous finding. WES therefore broadened the observed variant spectrum, although not all additional findings established a molecular diagnosis. These findings illustrate the complementary roles of hotspot screening and WES in extending variant detection within a tiered testing strategy.

In this cohort, MT‐RNR1 variants, particularly m.1555A>G, represented the largest component of the detected variant spectrum, followed by GJB2 and SLC26A4. The m.1555A>G variant was also the most frequent genotype among patients with an established molecular diagnosis. This distribution differed from that reported in a large Chinese hearing‐loss cohort, in which SLC26A4 and GJB2 were the leading molecular causes, whereas MT‐RNR1 accounted for a smaller proportion of diagnosed cases (Wu et al. 2022). The predominance of MT‐RNR1 may partly reflect the characteristics of this cohort. Participants were adults recruited from a single workplace, and a substantial proportion reported ototoxic drug exposure (Table 1). This exposure history is relevant because MT‐RNR1 variants are well‐established risk factors for aminoglycoside‐induced hearing loss (Prezant et al. 1993). The observed distribution may therefore be cohort‐specific rather than representative of the general Han Chinese population.

WES established additional molecular diagnoses in P37 and P47. P37 carried two heterozygous ILDR1 variants, c.772C>T (p.Gln258Ter) and c.206C>A (p.Pro69His), and had hearing loss first noted at 5–6 years of age. Although ILDR1‐related hearing loss is commonly congenital or prelingual, childhood onset at approximately 5 years has also been reported (An et al. 2019). Thus, the age at onset is within the reported phenotypic spectrum. In patient P47, two heterozygous CDH23 variants were identified and were presumed to be in trans based on the maternal testing results. CDH23 encodes a calcium‐dependent cell–cell adhesion glycoprotein that plays a critical role in sensory signal transduction, particularly in the auditory system (GeneCards, n.d.), and has been implicated in Usher syndrome Type 1 as well as autosomal recessive nonsyndromic hearing loss. Variants in CDH23 can result in a broad phenotypic spectrum, ranging from congenital profound to progressive high‐frequency hearing loss (de Guimaraes et al. 2024; Usami et al. 2022). P47 had congenital hearing loss and an affected older sister. Neither reported visual symptoms nor known ocular abnormalities. The missense variant CDH23 c.4562A>G (p.Asn1521Ser) has been previously reported in individuals with hearing loss from European, African, and West Asian populations (Sloan‐Heggen et al. 2015; Morgan et al. 2018; Bazazzadegan et al. 2019; Abu Rayyan et al. 2020; Wonkam et al. 2022). In contrast, the nonsense variant c.5311C>T (p.Arg1771Ter) introduces a premature termination codon predicted to trigger nonsense‐mediated mRNA decay and loss of function. Loss‐of‐function CDH23 variants are known to be pathogenic (Bolz et al. 2001; Schultz et al. 2011). This variant was absent in East Asian populations in gnomAD and has not been reported in Han Chinese patients with hearing loss, to our knowledge. It was previously described in an Israeli pedigree with Usher syndrome (Sharon et al. 2020), and later identified in three unrelated Italian cases of inherited retinal disease (Karali et al. 2022). Our observation therefore provides additional population‐specific documentation of this variant in a patient with hearing loss.

We also identified a previously unreported SLC12A2 variant, c.2936A>G (p.Glu979Gly), which was classified as a VUS according to the ACMG/AMP criteria. It was therefore retained as a candidate finding rather than an established molecular diagnosis. SLC12A2 encodes a membrane‐bound Na+/Cl− cotransporter that critically regulates ionic homeostasis and cellular volume maintenance in the cochlea. Mutai et al. (2020) proposed that SLC12A2 contributes to endolymph homeostasis by recycling K+ from the perilymph to the stria vascularis and reported a de novo variant affecting the same residue, c.2935G>A (p.Glu979Lys), in a Japanese infant with severe sensorineural hearing loss. Similarly, McNeill et al. (2020) subsequently described two familial cases of bilateral SNHL harboring the same p.Glu979Lys substitution. P7 had congenital hearing loss, which overlaps with the reported phenotype of DFNA78. The family history was compatible with a presumed autosomal dominant inheritance pattern. However, only the proband underwent genetic testing, and segregation of the variant could not be assessed. Detailed comparison of hearing severity and vestibular involvement was also limited by the available clinical information. The recurrence of hearing loss associated variants affecting codon 979, together with the conservation of Glu979, suggests that this residue may represent a functionally important site of the protein. Nevertheless, the evidence remains insufficient to establish a causal role for p.Glu979Gly. Additional segregation studies and functional assays will be required to clarify its clinical significance.

Although WES was performed only in the 11 patients in whom no variants were detected by the initial hotspot screening, it enabled the identification of additional rare variants not covered by the hotspot panel, including additional molecular diagnoses and a candidate finding. This highlights its complementary role in investigating genetically heterogeneous cases. In settings where broad sequencing cannot be applied to all patients, such a stepwise strategy may represent a practical approach for balancing testing breadth with resource use.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the sample size was relatively limited, and whole‐exome sequencing was performed only in the 11 patients in whom no variants were detected by the initial hotspot screening. Patients with a single heterozygous hotspot variant were not further examined by WES. Additional pathogenic alleles may therefore have remained undetected. Second, family‐based genetic data were not available for all cases, restricting further segregation analysis and phase assessment. Third, due to the retrospective design, phenotypic information was not uniformly available across all participants. Clinical information was based mainly on interviews and available medical records. Detailed audiological data were not uniformly available, and standardized vestibular and ophthalmological assessments were not routinely performed. These limitations mainly affect the interpretation of individual cases. Future multicenter studies with comprehensive family‐based analyses and standardized clinical assessments will help improve variant interpretation and genotype–phenotype correlations.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study expands the mutational spectrum of hereditary hearing loss in a Han Chinese cohort from Southwest China, including a previously unreported SLC12A2 candidate variant currently classified as a VUS and a CDH23 variant not previously reported in Han Chinese patients with hearing loss. These findings provide additional population‐specific genetic evidence and highlight the practical value of a tiered testing strategy integrating targeted hotspot screening with whole‐exome sequencing.

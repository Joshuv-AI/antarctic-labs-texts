Welschinger–Witt invariants
Erwan Brugallé, Johannes Rau, Kirsten Wickelgren

Abstract
. Welschinger invariants are signed counts of real rational curves satisfying contraints. Quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants give such counts over general fields of characteristic different from 2 and 3. For rational del Pezzo surfaces over a field, we propose a conjectural relationship between Welschinger and quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants. We construct multivariable unramified Witt invariants, in the sense of Serre, from Welschinger invariants and call them Welschinger–Witt invariants. We show that quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants are also Witt invariants and control their ramification. We then conjecture an equality between these Witt invariants, in particular giving a conjectural computation of all the quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants of k k -rational surfaces. We prove this conjecture for k k -rational del Pezzo surfaces of degree at least 6.

Introduction

We propose a relationship between enumerations of real rational curves in real rational surfaces and analogous enumerations over fields of characteristic different from 2 and 3. Welschinger introduced a signed count of the real rational (
J
-holomorphic) curves realizing a fixed class
D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)(\mathbb{R})
in a real rational surface
X
, and interpolating a real configuration of points.
More precisely, fixing the number
s
of pairs of complex conjugate points
in such a configuration, summing all real rational curves of class
D

with signs
+1
and
-1
depending on the number of solitary nodes of the curve
produces an integer
\operatorname{Wel}_{X}(D;s)
, independent of the choice of points
[
Wel05b
,
Wel15
,
Bru20
]
.
These integers are a real analogue of genus 0 Gromov–Witten invariants called
Welschinger invariants
.

Given a field
k
of characteristic not 2, we denote by
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(k)
its
Witt–Grothendieck ring
, that is, the ring completion of isomorphism classes of symmetric non-degenerate
k
-bilinear forms. We further denote by
\operatorname{W}(k)
the
Witt ring
of
k
which is the quotient of
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(k)
by the ideal generated by the hyperbolic form. See
Section

2.1
.
Replacing the integers
\mathbb{Z}
by
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(k)
allows one to define an invariant count of rational curves with fixed class
D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)(k)
and through point constraints on a del Pezzo surface
X
over any perfect field
k
of characteristic not
2
or
3
, under some technical restrictions
[
KLSW23a
]
. For any
finite étale
k
-algebra
A
of the appropriate degree, this produces an arithmetically meaningful count
\widehat{Q}_{X,D,k}(A)\in\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(k)
of rational curves on
X
through generic points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
with
A=\prod_{i}k(p_{i})
. Here, the algebra

takes on the role of the value
s
in the real case insofar as it specifies the field extensions over which the points in the configuration
live.
The count
\widehat{Q}_{X,D,k}(A)
is again independent of the generally chosen points and is called a
quadratic Gromov–Witten invariant
. Considering
\widehat{Q}_{X,D,k}(A)
modulo the hyperbolic form gives an element
Q_{X,D,k}(A)\in\operatorname{W}(k)
.

Welschinger invariants of a
\mathbb{R}
-rational del Pezzo surface

can be seen as particular instances of quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants, since by construction
\operatorname{Wel}_{X}(D;s)
equals the signature of
Q_{X,D,\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{s}\times\mathbb{R}^{r})
, see
[
KLSW23a
, Theorem 3]
,
[
Lev18
, Remark 2.5]
, or
Example

.
This paper goes in the opposite direction by giving a conjectural expression of quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants of
k
-rational surfaces in terms of Welschinger invariants, and proving it for
k
-rational del Pezzo surfaces of degree at least 6

In doing so, we give structure to both Welschinger and quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants. Enumerative problems often come in families, for example one may vary the parameter

in Welschinger invariants. Beyond computing each individual number,
a major challenge is typically to understand the whole family of numbers
in terms of structures imposed by the underlying geometry. For example

Welschinger’s formula
, see

or
[

, Theorem 0.4]

describes how the invariant
\operatorname{Wel}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}}(d;s)
changes when

is increased to

, for fixed

but varying

can be arranged to yield a

of étale algebras in the sense of Serre

]

Before discussing geometry, let us briefly review the concept of Witt invariants. We refer to

consists of maps
\alpha_{K}\colon\operatorname{Et}_{n}(K)\to\operatorname{W}(K)

the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Et}_{n}(K)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{W}(K)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Et}_{n}(L)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{W}(L)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{K}}

Thus, fixing

\begin{matrix}0&16&40&80&144&240\\
8&12&20&32&48&\\
2&4&6&8&&\\
1&1&1&&&\\

2.
Witt invariants and
\beta
-invariants

2.1.
Witt ring

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring where
2
is invertible. A unimodular quadratic form over
R
, or a unimodular
R
-quadratic form, is a projective
R
-module
P
together with a map
q:P\to R
such that

•

q(rx)=r^{2}q(x)
for all
r
in
R
and
x
in
P
.

•

B_{q}(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)
is bilinear.

•

The map
P\to\operatorname{Hom}(P,R)
induced by the symmetric bilinear form
B_{q}
is an isomorphism.

If
P
is free, the last condition is equivalent to the determinant
of the Gram matrix of
B_{q}
for some
R
-basis being a unit in
R
.
The
rank
of
q
is the rank of the projective module
P
, which is equal to the dimension of the fiber
P\otimes R/\mathfrak{p}
over
R/\mathfrak{p}
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, which is constant over the connected components of
\operatorname{Spec}R
. Given a second unimodular quadratic form
q^{\prime}\colon P^{\prime}\to R
, we can
form the orthogonal sum
q\oplus q^{\prime}\colon P\oplus P^{\prime}\to R

given by
q\oplus q^{\prime}((x,x^{\prime}))=q(x)+q^{\prime}(x^{\prime})
as well as the tensor product

q\otimes q^{\prime}\colon P\otimes P^{\prime}\to R
given by

q\otimes q^{\prime}(x\otimes x^{\prime})=q(x)q(x^{\prime})
, both of which are unimodular quadratic forms.

Let
\operatorname{\widetilde{W}}(R)
denote the set of isomorphism classes of unimodular quadratic forms over
R
. Since there are no additive inverses (except for the
0
form), the triple
(\operatorname{\widetilde{W}}(R),\oplus,\otimes)

is only a semiring. The associated Grothendieck group
obtained by adding formal inverses is denoted by
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(R)
and called
the
Witt-Grothendieck ring
of
R
.

A unimodular quadratic form
(P,q)
is
metabolic
if there is a direct summand
U\subseteq P
such that
U^{\perp}=U
. The
Witt ring

\operatorname{W}(R)
of
R
is the quotient of
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(R)
by the ideal generated by metabolic forms. Further references on Witt and Witt-Grothendieck rings include
[
Knu91
]

[
Lam05
]

[
MH73
]
.

Given
a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\in R^{*}
, we denote by
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\rangle
the diagonal quadratic form on
R^{s}
defined by the
a_{i}
’s. We write
h
for the hyperbolic form
\langle 1,-1\rangle
. We have

\displaystyle\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\rangle\oplus\langle b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{s},b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\rangle\otimes\langle b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{s,t}\langle a_{i}b_{j}\rangle.

We note that the additive inverse of
[q]
in
\operatorname{W}(R)
is
[-q]

since
q\oplus-q
is isomorphic to
nh
where
n
is the rank of
q
.
We will often denote the equivalence class of a quadratic
form
q
by the same letter
q
and write sum and product
as
q+q^{\prime}
and
qq^{\prime}
if no confusion is likely.

We are particularly interested in the case where
R
is a field.
In this case
P
is a
R
-vector space whose dimension is the rank of a quadratic form
q:P\to R
.
Denote by
\mathbf{Fields}
the category of fields of characteristic different from
2
. More generally, given a finite set
S
of prime numbers containing
2
,
let
\mathbf{Fields}_{S}\subset\mathbf{Fields}
denote the full subcategory of fields whose characteristic is either
0
or is positive but does not lie in
S
. For
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
, we denote by
\mathbf{Fields}/k
the initial category over
k
, that is,
the category of field extensions
k\to K
.

Example 2.1
.

One has

\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{Z},

the first isomorphism being the reduction modulo 2 of the rank, and the second being the signature.
More generally, the Witt ring of any algebraically closed field is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

and the Witt ring of any real closed field is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
by
[
Lam05
, Proposition II.3.2 (1)]
.
Also, the Witt ring of a finite field
K\in\mathbf{Fields}
is given by

\operatorname{W}(K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})[X]/(X^{2}+1)&\mbox{if }|K|=1\mod 4\\
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}&\mbox{if }|K|=3\mod 4\end{array}\right.,

see
[
Lam05
, Corollary II.3.6]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Since for
K\in\mathbf{Fields}
the cancellation property
holds in
\operatorname{\widetilde{W}}(K)
by Witt’s cancellation theorem
[
Lam05
, Theorem I.4.2]
,
the map
\operatorname{\widetilde{W}}(K)\to\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(K)
is injective.
The map
(\operatorname{rk},[\_])\colon\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(K)\to\mathbb{Z}\times\operatorname{W}(K)
is injective. Indeed, two non-degenerate quadratic forms over
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
are isomorphic if and only if they have the same rank and the same class in
\operatorname{W}(k)
.
See
[
Lam05
, Proposition II.1.4]
.
Moreover, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(K)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{W}(K)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{rk}}

is cartesion, see
[
GMS03
, Section VIII.27]
. In particular any metabolic form over a field is an integral multiple of the hyperbolic form
h
, that is, the ring
\operatorname{W}(K)
is the quotient of
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(K)
by the ideal generated by
h
.

Given a field extension
\varphi\colon K\to L
, extension of scalars provides a map
\operatorname{W}(\varphi)=\otimes_{K}L\colon\operatorname{W}(K)\to\operatorname{W}(L)
. This yields a functor

\operatorname{W}\colon\mathbf{Fields}\to\mathbf{Sets}
(which factors through the category
\mathbf{Rings}
of commutative rings
with unit).
By restriction, we also have functors
\operatorname{W}_{S}\colon\mathbf{Fields}_{S}\to\mathbf{Sets}
and
\operatorname{W}_{k}\colon\mathbf{Fields}/k\to\mathbf{Sets}

which by abuse of notation we also denote by
\operatorname{W}
if no confusion is likely.

2.2.
Witt invariants of étale algebras

Let
F\colon\mathbf{Fields}\to\mathbf{Sets}
another functor.
Following
[
GMS03
, 27.3]
,
a
Witt invariant of type
F
is a morphism of functors
\alpha\colon F\to\operatorname{W}
.
In other words,
\alpha
consists of maps
\alpha_{K}\colon F(K)\to\operatorname{W}(K)
for all
K\in\mathbf{Fields}

such that for any field extension
\varphi\colon K\to L
the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(K)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{W}(K)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F(L)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{W}(L)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\varphi)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes_{K}L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{L}}

commutes. We denote the set of Witt invariants of type
F
by
\operatorname{Inv}(F)
.
Since
\operatorname{W}
factors through
\mathbf{Rings}
, the set
\operatorname{Inv}(F)
is carries a ring structure
by ordinary addition and multiplication of functions.
Similarly,
given a functor
F\colon\mathbf{Fields}_{S}\to\mathbf{Sets}
or
F\colon\mathbf{Fields}/k\to\mathbf{Sets}
,
a morphism of functors from
F
to
\operatorname{W}_{S}
or
\operatorname{W}_{k}
is
called a Witt invariant
away from
S
or
over
k
, respectively,
and the ring of such invariants is denoted by
\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(F)
and
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(F)
.
Note that
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(F)
is a
\operatorname{W}(k)
-algebra.
For a set of fields
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbf{Fields}
, we set

\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{K}):=\prod_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\operatorname{W}(k).

We denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of all prime fields of characteristic different from
2

and write
\mathcal{P}\setminus S
for the subset of prime fields whose
characteristic is not contained in
S
.
Then clearly
\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(F)=\prod_{k\in\mathcal{P}\setminus S}\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(F)
and

\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(F)
is a
\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{P}\setminus S)
-algebra.

We are mostly interested in the following choices for
F
.
Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\operatorname{Et}_{n}(K)
the
set of isomorphism classes of étale
K
-algebras
A
of rank
n
.
By definition any such algebra is isomorphic to the product
L_{1}\times\dots\times L_{\ell}

for finite and separable field extensions
L_{1},\dots,L_{\ell}
of
K
such that

[L_{1}:K]+\dots+[L_{\ell}:K]=n
. This decomposition is unique
up to permutation of the factors (and isomorphisms of field extensions).
Given an extension
\varphi\colon K\to L
,
the extension of scalars
A\otimes_{K}L
is an étale
L
-algebra of rank
n

and this gives rise to a map
\operatorname{Et}_{n}(\varphi)=\otimes_{K}L\colon\operatorname{Et}_{n}(K)\to\operatorname{Et}_{n}(L)
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Et}_{n}
is a functor from
\mathbf{Fields}
to
\mathbf{Sets}

(and we denote by
\operatorname{Et}_{n,S}
and
\operatorname{Et}_{n,k}
its restriction
to
\mathbf{Fields}_{S}
and
\mathbf{Fields}/k
, respectively, if necessary).
The Witt invariants of type
\operatorname{Et}_{n}
(respectively
\operatorname{Et}_{n,S}
,
\operatorname{Et}_{n,k}
) are called
(étale) Witt invariants of degree
n

(away from
S
and over
k
, respectively)
and form a ring denoted by
\operatorname{Inv}(n)
(respectively
\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(n)
,
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)
).

We extend the definitions to the multivariable case:
given
\mathbf{n}=(n_{0},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, we set
\operatorname{Et}_{\mathbf{n}}=\operatorname{Et}_{n_{0}}\times\dots\times\operatorname{Et}_{n_{r}}
;
Witt invariants of type
\operatorname{Et}_{\mathbf{n}}
are called
Witt invariants of (multi-)degree
\mathbf{n}
, and form a ring denoted by
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbf{n})
.
We define
\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(\mathbf{n})
and
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
analogously.

Example 2.3
.

There is a particular Witt invariant of degree
n
, called the
trace form
, from which all others can be derived (see

[
GMS03
]
).
Given
A\in\operatorname{Et}_{n}(K)
and
x\in A
,
we denote by
\operatorname{tr}_{A/K}(x)
the trace
of the
K
-linear map
A\to A
,
y\mapsto xy
.
Since
\operatorname{tr}_{A/K}\colon A\to K
is
K
-linear,
the map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}A&\longrightarrow&K\\
x&\longmapsto&\operatorname{tr}_{A/K}(x^{2}),\end{array}

is a quadratic form
over
K
which we denote by
\operatorname{Tr}_{K}(A)
or just
\operatorname{Tr}(A)
.
Given a second étale
K
-algebra
B
, it is clear
that
\operatorname{Tr}(A\times B)=\operatorname{Tr}(A)+\operatorname{Tr}(B)
.
It follows that the trace form
\operatorname{Tr}(A)
is non-degenerate since it
is non-degenerate for any finite separable field extension,
see e.g.
[
Lam05
, Exercise I.30]
.
As usual, we also denote by
\operatorname{Tr}(A)=\operatorname{Tr}_{K}(A)
the associated class in
W(K)
.
Given a field extension
K\to L
and
s\in L
,
x\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{A\otimes L/L}(s\otimes x)

\displaystyle=s\operatorname{tr}_{A/L}(x)

and hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L}(A\otimes L)=\operatorname{Tr}_{K}(A)\otimes L.

Therefore
\operatorname{Tr}\in\operatorname{Inv}(n)
.

We continue by recalling some basic structure theorems
for
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)
from
[
GMS03
]

and generalizing them to
\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(\mathbf{n})
,
i.e. to the multivariable case with no fixed base field.
We consider the single variable case first.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic not
2
. Any algebra in
\operatorname{Et}_{2}(K)
is of the form
\mathcal{E}_{a}=K[x]/(x^{2}-a)

for some
a\in K^{*}
.
Note that
\operatorname{Tr}_{K}(\mathcal{E}_{a})=\langle 2,2a\rangle\in W(K)
. Since

\mathcal{E}_{a}\cong\mathcal{E}_{b}
if and only if
a
and
b
define the same class in
K^{*}/(K^{*})^{2}
, there is
a well-defined bijection
K^{*}/(K^{*})^{2}\to\operatorname{Et}_{2}(K)
which by abuse of notation
we denote by
a\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{a}
.
Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the
m
-th
square classes
functor

\operatorname{Sq}_{m}\colon\mathbf{Fields}\to\mathbf{Sets}
given by

\operatorname{Sq}_{m}(K)=[K^{*}/(K^{*})^{2}]^{m}
.
For
2m\leqslant n
, we have that
\operatorname{Sq}_{m}
is a subfunctor of
\operatorname{Et}_{n}
via
the natural transformation

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{a_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{E}_{a_{m}}\times K^{n-2m}
.
For
m=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
,
the image of this map is the set of
multiquadratic étale algebras of rank
n
.
This yields a map
\operatorname{Inv}(n)\to\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})
which we denote by

\alpha\mapsto\alpha|_{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}}
and call
restriction to multiquadratic algebras
.

The basic structure theorem is the following one, which is merely a reformulation of results from
[
GMS03
, Section 29]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Given
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
,
S
a set of primes and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
m=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

The restriction maps to multiquadratic algebras
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)\to\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})
and

\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(n)\to\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})
are injective.

(2)

The ring
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)
is a free

W(k)
-module of rank
m+1
.
The ring
\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(n)
is a free
W(\mathcal{P}\setminus S)
-module of rank
m+1
.

Proof.

As mentioned above, clearly
\mathbf{Fields}_{S}=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathcal{P}\setminus S}\mathbf{Fields}/k
,
and therefore

\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(n)=\prod_{k\in\mathcal{P}\setminus S}\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n).

It is hence sufficient to prove the statements for fixed
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
.
The map
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)\to\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})
is injective
since by

[
GMS03
, Theorem 29.1]
its kernel is trivial.
The fact that
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)
is free of rank
m+1
is proven in

[
GMS03
, Theorem 29.2]
.
∎

In the following we
present a few
bases for the free module
\operatorname{Inv}(n)

(which are thus universal in the sense that they
provide
bases for
\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(n)
and
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)

for any choice of
S
and
k
).
In fact, it is often easier to describe their restrictions
to
\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})
.
We denote by
P_{i}^{m}
the
i
-th symmetric polynomial on
m
variables, that is,

P_{i}^{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}\\
|J|=i\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in J}x_{j}.

(1)

Given a quadratic form
q\colon V\to K
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, one can define
its
i
-th exterior power
\bigwedge^{i}q\colon\bigwedge^{i}V\to K
such
that on diagonal forms we have

{\textstyle\bigwedge^{i}}\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle=P_{i}^{n}(\langle a_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle a_{n}\rangle),

see
[
Bou73
, IX.1.2, Definition 12]
.
This gives rise to elements
\lambda_{i}^{n}\in\operatorname{Inv}(n)
defined by

\lambda_{i}^{n}(A)=\bigwedge^{i}\operatorname{Tr}_{K}(A)\in W(K)
. Its restriction to
\operatorname{Sq}_{m}
is given by

\lambda_{i}^{n}|_{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})=P^{n}_{i}(\langle 2\rangle,\ldots,\langle 2\rangle,\langle 2a_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle 2a_{m}\rangle,(1)),

where there are
m
repetitions of
\langle 2\rangle
and the notation
(1)
means that
there is an extra argument
\langle 1\rangle
if
n
is odd.

(2)

We define elements
\beta^{\prime m}_{i}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})

by setting

\beta^{\prime m}_{i}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})=P^{m}_{i}(\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal{E}_{a_{1}},\ldots,\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal{E}_{a_{m}})=P^{m}_{i}(\langle 2,2a_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle 2,2a_{m}\rangle).

(3)

We define elements
\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})

by setting

\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})=P^{m}_{i}(\langle a_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle a_{m}\rangle).

The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
acts on
\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})

by permuting the
m
factors of
K^{*}/(K^{*})^{2}
, and
we denote by
\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}
the set
of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
-invariant elements.

Theorem 2.5
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and write
m=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.
Then the following statements hold.

(1)

The image of the restriction map

\operatorname{Inv}(n)\to\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})
is
\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}
.

(2)

The invariants
\lambda_{0}^{n},\ldots,\lambda_{m}^{n}\in\operatorname{Inv}(n)

form a
W(\mathcal{P})
-basis for
\operatorname{Inv}(n)
.

(3)

There exist unique invariants
\alpha_{0}^{n},\ldots,\alpha_{m}^{n}

in
\operatorname{Inv}(n)
such that
\alpha_{i}^{n}|_{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}}=\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}
for all

i=0,\ldots,m
. These invariants form a
\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{P})
-basis for
\operatorname{Inv}(n)
.

(4)

There exist unique invariants
\beta_{0}^{n},\ldots,\beta_{m}^{n}

in
\operatorname{Inv}(n)
such that
\beta_{i}^{n}|_{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}}=\beta^{\prime m}_{i}
for all

i=0,\ldots,m
. These invariants form a
\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{P})
-basis for
\operatorname{Inv}(n)
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially contained in the proof
of
[
GMS03
, Theorem 29.2]
, but since
the argument is slightly convuluted we reproduce it here for completeness.
As in the proof of
Theorem

2.4
, it is sufficient
to prove the analogous statements for
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)
for
k\in\mathcal{P}
.

Let us introduce an additional family of invariants.
For
J\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}
, we define
\alpha^{\prime m}_{J}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})

by
\alpha^{\prime m}_{J}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})=\prod_{j\in J}\langle a_{j}\rangle
.
By definition,
\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}=\sum_{|J|=i}\alpha^{\prime m}_{J}
.
By
[
GMS03
, Theorem 27.15]

the elements
\alpha^{\prime m}_{J}
with
J\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}

form a
\operatorname{W}(k)
-basis of
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})
.
It follows that the elements
\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}
with
i=0,\ldots,m

form a basis of
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}
.

Recall that
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)\to\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})
is injective
and that it clearly takes values in
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}
.
When expressing
\lambda_{i}^{n}|_{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}}
in terms of the basis

\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}
, we see easily that

\lambda_{i}^{n}|_{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}}=\langle 2^{i}\rangle\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}\mod\langle\alpha^{\prime m}_{j}:j<i\rangle.

Since
\langle 2^{i}\rangle
is a unit in
\operatorname{W}(k)
,
it follows that
\lambda_{0}^{n}|_{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}},\ldots,\lambda_{m}^{n}|_{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}}

is another basis for
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}
.
This proves the first three items of the theorem.

The last item follows easily from the previous items. First note that
\beta^{\prime m}_{i}\in\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}
. Next,
expressing

\beta^{\prime m}_{i}
in terms of the basis

\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}
, we get

\beta^{\prime m}_{i}=\langle 2^{i}\rangle\alpha^{\prime m}_{i}\mod\langle\alpha^{\prime m}_{j}:j<i\rangle

as above, which implies again that
\beta_{0}^{n},\ldots,\beta_{m}^{n}
exist, are unique, and form a basis of
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)
.
∎

To lighten notation, we will use the notation
\lambda_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
, and
\alpha_{i}
rather than
\lambda_{i}^{n}
,
\beta_{i}^{n}
, and
\alpha_{i}^{n}
when no confusion is possible.

Remark 2.6
.

In

, we explicitly compute the

.

and

(2)

Remark 2.7
.

If
n
is odd, the map

induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{Inv}(n)\cong\operatorname{Inv}(n-1)

which sends
\beta^{n}_{i}
to
\beta^{n-1}_{i}
.

It is
easy to generalize the above discussion to the multivariate case.
In the following, we focus on the
\beta
-basis which is of most interest for

Definition 2.8
.

Given
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, we denote by
\beta_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{n}}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbf{n})

the Witt invariant of degree
\mathbf{n}

given by

\beta_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{n}}(A_{0},\dots,A_{r})=\prod_{j=0}^{r}\beta_{i_{j}}^{n_{j}}(A_{j}).

We emphasize that
\beta_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{n}}
is indeed a Witt invariant
since
\operatorname{W}
factors through
\mathbf{Rings}
, that is,
the maps
\operatorname{W}(K)\to\operatorname{W}(L)
for
K\to L
are ring homomorphisms.
In a completely analogous way, we can define Witt invariants

\lambda_{i}^{\mathbf{n}}
and
\alpha_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{n}}
in
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbf{n})
. Again, to lighten notation we will use the notation
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}}
,
\beta_{\mathbf{i}}
, and
\alpha_{\mathbf{i}}
rather than
\lambda_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{n}}
,
\beta_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, and
\alpha_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{n}}
when no confusion is possible.

Given
\mathbf{n}=(n_{0},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, we set
\mathbf{m}=(\lfloor n_{0}/2\rfloor,\dots,\lfloor n_{r}/2\rfloor)

throughout the following. We define

\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}:=\{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}:0\leqslant i_{j}\leqslant m_{j}\text{ for all }j=0,\dots,r\}.

Then clearly
\beta_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{n}}=0
for
\mathbf{i}\notin\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Given
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
and
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, the ring
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})

\operatorname{W}(k)
-module.
Given a set of primes

containing

\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{P}\setminus S)

.

Proof.

Again, it is enough to prove the relative version for some

. We proceed by induction on

. Let us assume that

We denote by

Given

Remark 2.10

2.3.

As mentioned, a Witt invariant

Conversely, if
\alpha\in\operatorname{\beta Inv}(\mathbf{n})

Let

\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}

Analogously, we have ring homomorphisms

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
S
be a finite set of primes containing
2
.
A Witt-invariant
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(\mathbf{n})
is unramified
if and only if it is
S
-integral.

Proof.

“if”
:
Let
R
be a complete discrete valuation ring. Since

\operatorname{W}(\kappa)\to\operatorname{W}(K)
is a ring homomorphism,
sums and products of Witt invariants unramified over
R
are again unramified over
R
.
Since the constant Witt invariants represented by the elements in
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}])
are clearly unramified,
it suffices to show that e.g. the
\lambda
-basis is unramified.
Since the map
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(\kappa)\to\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(K)
commutes with exterior powers,
it is in fact sufficient to show that
\lambda_{1}=\operatorname{Tr}
is unramified.
To see this, note that for
R\to A
finite étale, there is a well-defined
R
-linear trace map

\operatorname{tr}_{A/R}\colon A\to R
which is functorial, that is,

\operatorname{tr}_{A/R}\otimes K=\operatorname{tr}_{A_{K}/K}
and
\operatorname{tr}_{A/R}\otimes\kappa=\operatorname{tr}_{A_{\kappa}/\kappa}
,

[
Sta25
,
Tag 02DU
]
.
Moreover, the
R
-quadratic form
\operatorname{Tr}(A)\colon a\mapsto\operatorname{tr}_{A/R}(a^{2})

is unimodular. See
[
GR05
, Proposition 4.10]
.
Hence
\operatorname{Tr}(A)\in\operatorname{W}(R)
. By the above functoriality, we have

\operatorname{Tr}(A)_{K}=\operatorname{Tr}(A_{K})\in\operatorname{W}(K)
and
\operatorname{Tr}(A)_{\kappa}=\operatorname{Tr}(A_{\kappa})
which implies the claim.

“only if”
:
We denote by

a_{0},\ldots,a_{m}\in\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Q})
the
\alpha
-coefficients of
\alpha_{\infty}\in\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(n)

and by
a_{p,0},\ldots,a_{p,m}\in\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
the
\alpha
-coefficients of

\alpha_{p}\in\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(n)
for
p
not in
S
.

In general, for a field of Laurent series

we have

\operatorname{W}(K((x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})))=\operatorname{W}(K)[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]/(t_{1}^{2}-1,\ldots,t_{m}^{2}-1)\cong\operatorname{W}(K)[(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{m}],

with

by Springer’s theorem
[
Lam05
, IV Theorem 1.4]
.
For
p
not in
S
, we have that
\alpha
is unramified over

\mathbb{Z}_{p}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}))
, giving the commutative
diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Sq}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{W(\mathbb{Q}_{p})[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{W(\mathbb{F}_{p})[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

Evaluating on

,
it follows that

where

Remark 2.19
.

Expanding a bit the proof, we note that
by
[
Lam05
, Theorem VI.4.1]
and
[
MH73
, Corollary IV.3.3]

there is a (split) exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}[\langle 2\rangle]\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\prod\delta_{p}}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}\setminus 2}\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\to 0.

It follows
that
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}])\to\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Q})
is injective and
that as a group
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}])
is isomorphic to

\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\bigoplus_{p\in S\setminus 2}\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

In particular,

\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[2^{-1}])\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

and
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[2^{-1},3^{-1}])\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
.
For example, the isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\cong\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[2^{-1}])

is given by
(1,0)\mapsto\langle 1\rangle
and
(0,1)\mapsto\langle 1\rangle-\langle 2\rangle
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}[\langle S\rangle]
denote the subring of
\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{P}\setminus S)
generated by
\langle p\rangle
for
p
in
S

\mathbb{Z}[\langle S\rangle]:=\mathbb{Z}[\langle p\rangle:p\in S]\subset\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{P}\setminus S).

Clearly, as a group
\mathbb{Z}[\langle S\rangle]
is generated by the elements

\langle p_{1}\cdots p_{r}\rangle
for (distinct)
p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\in S
,
and
\mathbb{Z}[\langle S\rangle]\subset\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}])
.
For a prime
p_{0}
, let
S(p_{0})
denote the set of primes lower or
equal than
p_{0}
.

Lemma 2.20
.

For any prime
p_{0}
, the subrings

\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[S(p_{0})^{-1}])
and
\mathbb{Z}[\langle S(p_{0})\rangle]

of
\operatorname{W}(\mathcal{P}\setminus S(p_{0}))
(or
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Q})
)

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
p_{0}
. For
p_{0}=2
, the claim follows because
\langle 1\rangle
and
\langle 1\rangle-\langle 2\rangle
generate
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[S(p_{0})^{-1}])

.
Now suppose the claim hold for all primes less than

\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}:p<p_{0},p\text{ prime}])\subset\mathbb{Z}[\langle S(p_{0})\rangle]

and we have an exact sequence

\mathbb{Z}[\langle S(p_{0})\rangle]\to\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{F}_{p_{0}})

for

∎

3.

3.1.
Multireal values of
\beta
-integral Witt invariants

In this section we exhibit a finite set of values, called
multireal values
, that completely characterize a
\beta
-integral Witt invariant. Recall that, given
K\in\mathbf{Fields}
and
a\in K^{*}
, we have defined the degree two étale
K

\mathcal{E}_{a}=K[X]/(X^{2}-a)
.
The multireal values are obtained by evaluating a Witt invariant in combinations
of
\mathcal{E}_{1}=K^{2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{-1}

Definition 3.1
.

Given
K\in\mathbf{Fields}
and
i,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
i\leqslant m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, we call

R_{s}=R^{n}_{s}=(\mathcal{E}_{-1})^{s}\times K^{n-2s}\in\operatorname{Et}_{n}(K)

multireal algebra
over
K
.
Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
and
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}
, we set

R_{\mathbf{s}}=R^{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathbf{s}}=(R^{n_{0}}_{s_{0}},\dots,R^{n_{r}}_{s_{r}})\in\operatorname{Et}_{\mathbf{n}}(K).

Given a Witt invariant
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
over
k
,
we call

w_{\mathbf{s}}:=\alpha_{k}(R_{\mathbf{s}})\in\operatorname{W}(k),\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}},

the
multireal values
of
\alpha
.
For a Witt invariant
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}_{S}(\mathbf{n})
, we define its multireal values
as the multireal values of its restriction to
\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{n})
.

Given
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
and
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, we define the map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mu_{k,\mathbf{n}}:&\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{W}(k)^{\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}}\\
&\alpha&\longmapsto&(w_{\mathbf{s}})_{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}}\end{array}\cdot

It is clearly a
\operatorname{W}(k)
-linear map, which furthermore decomposes as

\mu_{k,\mathbf{n}}=\bigotimes_{j=0}^{r}\mu_{k,n_{j}}.

In this subsection and the following one, we study to which extent
the multireal values (or their signatures)
determine a Witt invariant, i.e. we study the kernel of
\mu_{k,\mathbf{n}}
. Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the matrix
M_{m}\in\mathcal{\operatorname{Mat}}_{m+1,m+1}(\mathbb{Z})
by

(M_{m})_{s,i}=2^{i}\cdot\binom{m-s}{i},

that is

M_{m}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&2{m\choose 1}&4{m\choose 2}&\cdots&\cdots&2^{m}\\
1&2{m-1\choose 1}&4{m-1\choose 2}&\cdots&2^{m-1}&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&\iddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&4&\iddots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&2&0&&&\vdots\\
1&0&0&\ldots&\ldots&0\end{array}\right)\cdot

Regarding binomial coefficients, our convention is that

\binom{s}{i}=0
if
0\leqslant i\leqslant s
is not satisfied.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
and
n\geqslant 1
, the matrix
M_{m}
is the matrix of the map
\mu_{k,n}
in the base
(\beta_{i})
of
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(n)
. In particular,

\det(\mu_{k,n})=\pm 2^{\binom{m+1}{2}}.

Proof.

Recall the identities
\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal{E}_{-1}=\langle 2\rangle+\langle-2\rangle=0

and
\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal{E}_{1}=\langle 2\rangle+\langle 2\rangle=\langle 1\rangle+\langle 1\rangle=2
in
\operatorname{W}(k)
.
It follows that
\beta_{i}(R_{s})=2^{i}\binom{m-s}{i}\langle 1\rangle
,
which proves the statement.
∎

Given
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, we define the matrix
M_{\mathbf{m}}
as the Kronecker product of the matrices
M_{m_{0}},\ldots,M_{m_{r}}
:

M_{\mathbf{m}}=\bigotimes_{j=0}^{r}M_{m_{j}}.

Hence elements of
M_{\mathbf{m}}
are indexed by elements of
\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}
, and we have

(M_{\mathbf{m}})_{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{i}}=2^{\mathbf{i}}\cdot\binom{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{s}}{\mathbf{i}},

where for
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
,

\displaystyle 2^{\mathbf{i}}

\displaystyle:=2^{i_{0}+\dots+i_{r}},

\displaystyle\binom{\mathbf{s}}{\mathbf{i}}

\displaystyle:=\binom{s_{0}}{i_{0}}\cdots\binom{s_{r}}{i_{r}}.

The next lemma follows immediately from Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
and
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, the matrix
M_{\mathbf{m}}
is the matrix of the map
\mu_{k,\mathbf{n}}
in the base
(\beta_{\mathbf{i}})
of
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
. In particular
M_{\mathbf{m}}
is upper-left-triangular, and

\det(\mu_{k,\mathbf{n}})=\pm 2^{\binom{m_{0}+1}{2}+\dots+\binom{m_{r}+1}{2}}.

Recall that a field
k
is called
formally real
if
-1
is not a sum of squares in
k
.
In particular, this implies that
k
is of characteristic
0
.
Here is a list of well-known criteria for being formally real.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

The field
k
is formally real.

(2)

The field
k
can be ordered.

(3)

The group
\operatorname{W}(k)
is not torsion.

(4)

The element
\langle 1\rangle\in\operatorname{W}(k)
is not torsion.

Proof.

If
n\langle 1\rangle=0\in\operatorname{W}(k)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, this means
that
n\langle 1\rangle
is hyperbolic and in particular isotropic.
It follows that
-1
is a sum of squares, e.g.
[
Lam05
, Chapter I, Corollary 3.5]
.
This implies (1)
\Rightarrow
(4).
The implication (4)
\Rightarrow
(3) is trivial, (3)
\Rightarrow
(2) is a
consequence of
[
Lam05
, Chapter VIII, Theorem 3.2]

and (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) follows from
[
Lam05
, Chapter VIII, Proposition 1.3]

∎

In the following, when
k
is a formally real field we identify
\mathbb{Z}
with its image
\mathbb{Z}\langle 1\rangle
in
\operatorname{W}(k)
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
k
be a formally real field
and
\alpha\in\operatorname{\beta Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
.
Then the multireal values of
\alpha
lie in
\mathbb{Z}
and determine
\alpha
completely
(that is, if all multireal values are zero, then
\alpha
is zero).

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.3
the restriction of
\mu_{k,\mathbf{n}}
to
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}}
has image in
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}}
, which proves the first claim.
Since
\det M_{\mathbf{m}}=\det\mu_{k,\mathbf{n}}\neq 0
still by
Lemma

3.3
, this restriction
is injective.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\alpha\in\operatorname{\beta Inv}_{S}(\mathbf{n})
be a
\beta
-integral Witt invariant. Then the following hold:

(1)

The multireal values of
\alpha
lie in
\mathbb{Z}
and determine
\alpha
completely.

(2)

The multireal values of
\alpha
are equal to the multireal values
of the restriction of
\alpha
to
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})

.

Proof.

Part (1) is just

3.5
applied to the restriction of
\alpha

\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{n})
.
Part (2) follows from the fact that

is the identity on

for
all formally real fields

.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

Given two integers

and

such that

, we define three homomorphisms
of

-modules
\operatorname{Inv}(n)\to\operatorname{Inv}(n-2u)
given for

and

by

\displaystyle=\alpha(A\oplus\mathcal{E}_{1}^{u}),

\displaystyle\alpha^{[u]}(A)

\displaystyle=\alpha(A\oplus\mathcal{E}_{-1}^{u}),

\displaystyle(\sum_{i=0}^{m}b_{i}\beta^{n}_{i})^{\{u\}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{m-u}b_{u+i}\beta^{n-2u}_{i}.

Alternatively, the last homomorphism is given on the level of the
\beta
-basis by

(\beta^{n}_{i})^{\{u\}}=\beta^{n-2u}_{i-u}
, declaring
\beta_{i}=0
for
i<0
.

Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
and
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}
, we denote the obvious multivariate versions

\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbf{n})\to\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbf{n}-2\mathbf{u})
by
\alpha^{(\mathbf{u})}
,

\alpha^{[\mathbf{u}]}
and
\alpha^{\{\mathbf{u}\}}
.

The motivation behind these definitions is implicit in the proof of

Proposition

3.7
, we summarize it here for convenience
in the single variable case.

Corollary 3.9
.

Consider
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}(n)
with multireal triangle
(c^{u}_{i})

and fix
s\leqslant m
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

The multireal triangle of
\alpha^{(s)}

is
(c^{u}_{i})_{u\geqslant s}
, that is,
the triangle obtained by removing
s
times the left hand vertical side of the triangle.

(2)

The multireal triangle of
\alpha^{[s]}

is
(c^{u}_{i})_{u+i\leqslant m-s}
, that is,
the triangle obtained by removing
s
times the bottom diagonal side of the triangle.

(3)

The multireal triangle of
\alpha^{\{s\}}

is
(c^{u}_{i})_{i\geqslant s}
, that is,
the triangle obtained by removing
s
times the top horizontal side of the triangle.

Proof.

The first two items follow from

P_{i}^{m}=P_{i}^{m-1}+x_{m}P_{i-1}^{m-1}
and
\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal{E}_{1}=2\langle 1\rangle

and
\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal{E}_{-1}=0
in
\operatorname{W}(K)
.
The last item is by construction.
∎

This construction implies the following, which may be seen as a
coarse Abramovich–Bertram formula
for Witt invariants. In particular, it explains the factor
\langle 2\rangle-\langle 2d\rangle=2\langle 1\rangle-\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{E}_{d})
in the quadratic Abramovich–Bertram formula
[
BW25
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Given
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbf{n})
and
j=0,\ldots,r
, we define

\operatorname{spl}_{j}\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbf{n}-2e_{j},2)

by

\operatorname{spl}_{j}\alpha(\ldots,A_{j},\ldots,\mathcal{E})=\alpha(\ldots,A_{j}\times\mathcal{E},\ldots).

Proposition 3.10
.

Given
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbf{n})
and
j=0,\ldots,r
, one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{spl}_{j}\alpha(A,\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{[e_{j}]}(A)+\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{E})\alpha^{\{e_{j}\}}(A)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(e_{j})}(A)+(\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{E})-2\langle 1\rangle)\alpha^{\{e_{j}\}}(A).

Proof.

This just follows from doing the calculus on the bases: We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{spl}\beta^{n}_{i}=\beta^{n-2}_{i}+\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{E})\beta^{n-2}_{i-1},

\displaystyle(\beta^{n}_{i})^{[1]}=\beta^{n-2}_{i},

\displaystyle(\beta^{n}_{i})^{\{1\}}=\beta^{n-2}_{i-1},

\displaystyle(\beta^{n}_{i})^{(1)}=\beta^{n-2}_{i}+2\beta^{n-2}_{i-1},

which all follow from
P^{m}_{i}=P^{m-1}_{i}+x_{m}P^{m-1}_{i-1}
.
∎

3.2.
Multireal values of Witt invariants

We proved in the previous section that a
\beta
-integral Witt invariant in
\operatorname{\beta Inv}(\mathbf{n})
or
\operatorname{\beta Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
, with
k
formally real, is
determined by its multireal values.
In general, the multireal values of a Witt invariant is only determined up to
certain torsion elements.

Proposition 3.11
.

Given
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
and
\alpha=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}}b_{\mathbf{i}}\beta_{\mathbf{i}}\in\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
, all multireal values of
\alpha
are zero
if and only if each
b_{\mathbf{i}}\in\operatorname{W}(k)
is a
2^{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{i}}
-torsion element.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
\mathbf{m}
that if
c=(c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}})_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{i},\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}}
is the multireal triangle of
\alpha
, then
\alpha
has only zero multireal values if and only if
c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}}
is a
2^{\mathbf{i}}
-torsion element of
\operatorname{W}(k)
for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{i}
, the case
\mathbf{m}=0
being true by assumption.
Let now
\mathbf{m}
be such that
\mathbf{m}-e_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
. By
Corollary

3.9
, the multireal values of
\alpha
and of
\alpha^{(e_{j})}
coincide, and it follows from the proof of
Proposition

3.7
that

\alpha^{(e_{j})}=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}-e_{j}}}c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{m}-e_{j}}\beta_{i}.

Since
c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}}=c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{m}-e_{j}}+2c_{\mathbf{i}+e_{j}}^{\mathbf{m}-e_{j}}
, the result follows.
∎

We now turn to a version of
Proposition

3.11
involving integer numbers rather than element of Witt rings.
We denote by
\mathcal{O}
the set of orderings of the field
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
.
Recall that
k
is formally real if and only if
\mathcal{O}\neq\emptyset
,
see

3.4
.
Given an ordering
P\in\mathcal{O}
, we have an associated signature map

\operatorname{sgn}_{P}\colon\operatorname{W}(k)\to\mathbb{Z}
defined as the number of positive minus the number
of negative elements (with respect to
P
) in a diagonalization of an element of
\operatorname{W}(k)
,
see
[
Lam05
, Chapter VIII, Section 3]
.
The
total signature
is the map defined by

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\operatorname{sgn}\colon&\operatorname{W}(k)&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{O}}\\
&a&\longmapsto&(\operatorname{sgn}_{P}(a))_{P\in\mathcal{O}}\end{array}\cdot

We recall that the torsion subgroup of
\operatorname{W}(k)
is equal to the kernel
of
\operatorname{sgn}
, see
[
Lam05
, Chapter VIII, Theorem 3.2]
.
Given
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
, we call
\operatorname{sgn}(w_{\mathbf{s}})
the
multireal signatures

of
\alpha
. The multireal signatures determine
\alpha
up to torsion.

Proposition 3.12
.

Given
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
and
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
,
the multireal signatures of
\alpha
are all zero if and only if
\alpha
lies in the torsion submodule
of
\operatorname{Inv}_{k}(\mathbf{n})
.

Proof.

Let
c=(c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}})_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{i},\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}}
be the multireal triangle of
\alpha
. The triangle

(\operatorname{sgn}(c^{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{i}}))_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{i},\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}}
has values in
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{O}}
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{O}}
is torsionfree, the top line is zero if and only if the left row is zero.
The statement follows.
∎

We now consider the case
k=\mathbb{Q}
. Given
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{n})
, we denote
by
\alpha_{\mathbb{R}}
the restriction of
\alpha
to
\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{n})
.
Note that since
\operatorname{W}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{Z}
, any Witt invariants in
\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{n})
is
\beta
-integral and has integer multireal values.

.

Given
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{n})
, the multireal values of

are all zero if and only if
\alpha
lies in the torsion submodule
of
\operatorname{Inv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{n})
.

The only possible ordering for

\mathbb{R}
in the above discussion.

4.
Welschinger–Witt invariants

In this section, we show how Welschinger invariants can be arranged in certain
\beta
-integral Witt invariants which we call
Welschinger–Witt invariants.

4.1.
Welschinger invariants

Here we briefly recall the definition of Welschinger invariants and recast their properties that are of interest in this paper. We refer to

[
Wel05b
,
Wel07
,
Bru20
]
for more details.
A
real symplectic manifold

(X,\omega,\tau)
is a symplectic
manifold
(X,\omega)
equipped with an anti-symplectic involution

\tau
. An almost complex
structure
J
on
X
is called
\tau
-compatible
if it is
tamed by
\omega
, and if
\tau
is
J
-anti-holomorphic.

Let
(X,\omega,\tau)
be a compact real symplectic 4-manifold, and denote by

H_{2}^{-\tau}(X;\mathbb{Z})
the space of
\tau
-anti-invariant classes.
The fixed point set of
\tau
is denoted by
X^{\tau}
, and is assumed to be non-empty.
Choose a class

D\in H_{2}^{-\tau}(X;\mathbb{Z})

and

s\in\mathbb{N}
such that

c_{1}(X)\cdot D-1-2s=:r\in\mathbb{N}.

Choose a configuration
\underline{x}
made of
r
points in
X^{\tau}
and
s

pairs of
\tau
-conjugated
points in
X\setminus X^{\tau}
.
Given a
\tau
-compatible almost complex structure
J
tamed by
\omega
,
we denote by
\mathcal{C}(D,\underline{x},J)

the set of real rational

J
-holomorphic curves in
X
realizing the class
D
, and passing
through
\underline{x}
. Then we define the integer

\operatorname{Wel}_{(X,\omega,\tau)}(D;s)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}(D,\underline{x},J)}(-1)^{m(C)},

where

m(C)
is the number of nodes of
C
in
X^{\tau}
with two

\tau
-conjugated branches.
For a generic choice of
J
, the set
\mathcal{C}(D,\underline{x},J)
is finite, and

W_{(X,\omega,\tau)}(D;s)

depends
neither on
\underline{x}
,
J
, nor on the deformation class of the triple

(X,\omega,\tau)
, see
[
Wel05b
,
Wel15
,
Bru20
]
.
We call these numbers the
Welschinger invariants of
(X,\omega,\tau)
.
When
c_{1}(X)\cdot D-1=0
, implying
s=0
, we use the shorthand

\operatorname{Wel}_{(X,\omega,\tau)}(D)=\operatorname{Wel}_{(X,\omega,\tau)}(D;s).

A real algebraic projective surface
X
equipped with a real Kähler form
\omega

induces a
real symplectic 4-manifold
(X(\mathbb{C}),\omega,\tau)
, where
\tau
is the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})
on
X(\mathbb{C})
.
We note that we also get an induced
\tau
-compatible complex structure
J

on
(X(\mathbb{C}),\omega,\tau)
tamed by
\omega
, which
however may not be generic in the above sense.
The first Chern class composed with Poincaré duality maps a divisor class
D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
to an element
in
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, still denoted by
D
.
A
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})
-invariant class is mapped to an element of
H_{2}^{-\tau}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. Hence we can define Welschinger invariants
\operatorname{Wel}_{X}(D;s)
for any class
D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)(\mathbb{R})
. We denote by
\pi:X_{n,s}\to X
a blow-up of
X
at a real configuration of
n
distinct points containing exactly
s
pairs of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})
-conjugated points. Note that if
X(\mathbb{R})
is not connected and
n-2s\geqslant 1
, there exists several deformation classes of real blow-ups
\pi:X_{n,s}\to X
depending on the distribution of the blown-up real points on
X(\mathbb{R})
. However, it follows from
[
Bru20
, Theorem 1.3]
that the Welschinger invariants of
X_{n,s}
do not depend on the choice of a particular deformation class. Subsequently we do not record the blown-up configuration in the notation
X_{n,s}
.
Denoting by
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}

the exceptional classes, the map
(D,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\mapsto\pi^{*}D-a_{1}E_{1}-\cdots-a_{n}E_{n}
identifies
\operatorname{Pic}(X_{n,s})
with
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and we tacitly use this identification throughout the following.
Note that under this identification we have

\operatorname{Pic}(X_{n,0})(\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{Pic}(X)(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
and
\operatorname{Pic}(X_{2,1})(\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{Pic}(X)(\mathbb{R})\times\Delta
where
\Delta
denotes the
diagonal in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
By
[
Bru20
, Theorem 1.3]
combined for example with
[
DH18
, Corollary 1.3]
, we have

(4.1)

\operatorname{Wel}_{X}(D;s)=\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{n,s}}(\pi^{*}D-E_{1}-\cdots-E_{n}),

where
n=c_{1}(X)\cdot D-1
.
Moreover, it is easy to check that
\operatorname{Wel}_{X}(D;s)=\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{n,s^{\prime}}}(\pi^{*}D;s)
.

The next result is the key observation that relates Welschinger invariants to
\beta
-integral Witt invariants.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
Bru20
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a real algebraic projective surface,
D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)(\mathbb{R})
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then one has

\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{2,1}}((D,d,d);s)-\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{2,0}}((D,d,d);s)=2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}(-1)^{\ell}\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{2,0}}((D,d+\ell,d-\ell);s).

Remark 4.2
.

The particular case
d=1
of the formula (in combination with
Eq.

4.1
)
gives

Welschinger’s formula

[
Wel05b
, Theorem 0.4]

\operatorname{Wel}_{X}(D;s)-\operatorname{Wel}_{X}(D;s+1)=2\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{1,0}}(\pi^{*}D-2E_{1};s).

In what follows, the algebraic surface

X
will always be
\mathbb{R}
-rational, that is, it will be either

Q(1)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
,
the quadric ellipsoid
Q(-1)
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
or
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R},n,s}
,
equipped with any real Kähler form. Welschinger invariants of
\mathbb{R}
-rational surfaces can be all computed via Solomon’s open WDVV equations, see
[
HS12
,
CZ21
]
.

4.2.
Welschinger–Witt invariants

In this subsection, we associate
\beta
-integral Witt invariants to complex deformation classes of marked rational surfaces. We first treat the case of blow-ups of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Convention:

Throughout the following, a bold face letter like
\mathbf{n}
denotes a vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, often

\mathbb{N}^{r}
(where
r\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed).
Its coordinates are labelled as
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
.
A bold face letter with bar like
\overline{\mathbf{n}}
denotes vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
.
In this case, the first coordinate is denoted
n_{0}
and the last
r
coordinates
form a vector
\mathbf{n}
, so that
\overline{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{0},\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.
Given
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we set

\mathbf{m}=\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{2}\right\rfloor,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{n_{r}}{2}\right\rfloor\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.

Given
\overline{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{0},\mathbf{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
,
we set

n_{0}:=3d_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}n_{j}-1

and
m_{0}=\lfloor n_{0}/2\rfloor
. In the cases of interest to us, we have

n_{0}\geqslant 0
. We set
\overline{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{0},\mathbf{n})
and
{\overline{\mathbf{m}}}=(m_{0},\mathbf{m})
.

Given
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\leqslant\mathbf{m}
, i.e.
s_{j}\leqslant m_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,r
,
we denote by
X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}
the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}

at
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\mathcal{P}_{r}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, where each
\mathcal{P}_{j}=\{p_{j,1},\ldots,p_{j,n_{j}}\}
is a real configuration of
n_{j}
points
such that
p_{j,1},\dots,p_{j,n_{j}-2s_{j}}
are real points
and the remaining
2s_{j}
points consist of
s_{j}
pairs of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})
-conjugated points.
We denote by
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}})
the pullback of the class of a line under the blow-up map,
and by
E_{j,t}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}})
the class in of the exceptional divisor corresponding to
p_{j,t}
.
We also use the shorthand

E_{j}=E_{j,1}+\dots+E_{j,n_{j}}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
.
When
r=0
, we have
\mathbf{n}=()
and
X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}=\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Throughout the following, we consider
\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
as a subset of

\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}})
, for any
\mathbf{s}
,
via the map

\Delta\colon(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})\mapsto d_{0}L-d_{1}E_{1}-\dots-d_{r}E_{r}
.
Note that under this identification
\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
lies in
the subset
\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}})(\mathbb{R})

of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})
-invariant classes.
We usually suppress the map
\Delta
when there is no risk of confusion. For example, we write

\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}};s_{0})
for the associated
Welschinger invariant
\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}}(\Delta(\overline{\mathbf{d}});s_{0})
.

The main theorem of this section shows that the Welschinger invariants
(\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}}(D;s_{0}))_{\overline{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{N}_{\overline{\mathbf{m}}}}
are the multireal values of a
\beta
-integral Witt invariant. In order to describe the multireal triangle of this latter, we need to introduce some further notation.
Let us assume
n_{0}=0
, which is no significant restriction thanks to Identity (
4.1
). Note that
\mathbb{N}_{\overline{\mathbf{m}}}=\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}
in this case.
Let
\mathbf{s},\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}
such that
\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{m}}
.
We consider the surface
X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}
and recall
that by our conventions, since
\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{i}\leqslant\mathbf{m}
,
the classes
E_{j,1},\ldots E_{j,2i_{j}}

are real for all
j=1,\dots,r
.
We define
\gamma_{j,t}=E_{j,2t-1}-E_{j,2t}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}})(\mathbb{R})
.
We set
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbb{N}^{i_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{N}^{i_{r}}

and, for
\ell=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{i}}
,

|\ell|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,\ldots,r\\
t=1,\ldots,i_{j}\end{subarray}}\ell_{j,t},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Gamma_{\ell}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,\ldots,r\\
t=1,\ldots,i_{j}\end{subarray}}(\ell_{j,t}+1)\gamma_{j,t}.

Finally, we define

\operatorname{Wel}^{\mathbf{i}}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}})=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{i}}}(-1)^{|\ell|}\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}}\left(\Delta(\overline{\mathbf{d}})-\Gamma_{\ell}\right).

Note that
\operatorname{Wel}^{0}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}})=\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}})
.

Theorem 4.3
.

For
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
\overline{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
such that
n_{0}\geqslant 0
,
the vector
w=(w_{\overline{\mathbf{s}}})_{\overline{\mathbf{s}}\leqslant{\overline{\mathbf{m}}}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}_{\overline{\mathbf{m}}}}
given by

w_{\overline{\mathbf{s}}}=\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{s}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}};s_{0})

is
\beta
-integral. In particular there exists a unique
\beta
-integral Witt invariant
V\!\!W_{\mathbf{n},\overline{\mathbf{d}}}\in\beta\operatorname{Inv}(\overline{\mathbf{n}})
with multireal values
w
.
Furthermore, if
n_{0}=0

then the multireal triangle of

V\!\!W_{\mathbf{n},\overline{\mathbf{d}}}\in\operatorname{\beta Inv}(\mathbf{n})
is given by

c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}}=\operatorname{Wel}^{\mathbf{i}}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{i}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}}).

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case
n_{0}=0
by Identity (
4.1
).
In view of
Corollary

3.6
and
Proposition

3.7
,
it remains to prove that, taking
c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}}=\operatorname{Wel}^{\mathbf{i}}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{i}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}})

as a definition, this multitriangle satisfies

c_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{u}}=w_{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}}
and

c_{\mathbf{i}+e_{j}}^{\mathbf{u}-e_{j}}=\frac{1}{2}(c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}}-c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}-e_{j}})
for any
j=0,\dots,r

such that
u_{j}>0
.
We already explained the first part: we have

c_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{u}}=\operatorname{Wel}^{0}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}})=\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}}}(\overline{\mathbf{d}})=w_{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}}
.
The second part is an application of
4.1
(using the shorthand
D=\Delta(\overline{\mathbf{d}})
):

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}}-c_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{u}-e_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{i}}}(-1)^{|\ell|}\left(\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{i}}}\left(D-\Gamma_{\ell}\right)-\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{i}+e_{j}}}\left(D-\Gamma_{\ell}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{i}}}(-1)^{|\ell|}\sum_{l\geqslant 1}(-1)^{l+1}\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{i}}}\left(D-\Gamma_{\ell}-l\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{i}+e_{j}}}(-1)^{|\ell|}\operatorname{Wel}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{i}}}\left(D-\Gamma_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Wel}^{\mathbf{i}+e_{j}}_{X_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{i}}}(D)

\displaystyle=2c_{\mathbf{i}+e_{j}}^{\mathbf{u}-e_{j}}.

Here,
\gamma
is the difference

\gamma_{j,i_{j}+1}=E_{j,2i_{j}+1}-E_{j,2i_{j}+2}

which is
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})
-invariant since
p_{j,2i_{j}+1}
and
p_{j,2i_{j}+2}
are real points.
This proves the statement.
∎

5.
Quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants

In
[
KLSW23b
,
KLSW23a
]
, Kass, Levine, Solomon and the third author generalize the enumeration of (real) rational curves in (real) surfaces to enumeration of rational curves in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected del Pezzo surfaces over a large class of fields
k
. These invariants no longer take values in
\mathbb{Z}
, but in the
Witt-Grothendieck ring
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(k)
(at least for
k
-rational surfaces over an infinite field,
they in fact always lie in
\operatorname{\widetilde{W}}(k)
).
For this reason, we call the invariants defined in
[
KLSW23b
,
KLSW23a
]

quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants
.

We recall their construction in
Section

5.1
. In fact we do slightly more: we show in
Theorem

5.4
that they are Witt invariants, under certain hypotheses. To this end, we first need to extend the definition of quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants from
[
KLSW23b
,
KLSW23a
]
to include enumerations of curves through points over non-perfect fields (of characteristic not
2
and
3
). See
5.3
.
Note that for the reader’s convenience, we also provide in
Appendix

B

an alternative version of parts of
Section

5.1
in terms of the enumerative description of these invariants.
We then study specialization properties in mixed characteristic of these quadratic invariants in
Section

5.2
. In particular, we prove in
Theorem

5.7
that quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants of surfaces defined over
\mathbb{Z}[1/6]
,
such as toric del Pezzo surfaces, are unramified away from characteristic 2 and 3.
The exclusion of characteristic
2
and
3
is due to the lack of definition of the invariants in these cases.
We elaborate on relations between Welschinger–Witt and quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants in
Section

5.3
.

5.1.
Quadratic Gromov–Witten invariants as Witt invariants

Let
k\in\mathbf{Fields}
and let
X
be a del Pezzo surface over
k
.
Let
D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)(k)
be an effective divisor class such that
n=-K_{X}\cdot D-1\geqslant 0
.
Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,D)
denote the moduli stack representing tuples
(u:C\to X,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))
consisting of a stable map
u:C\to X
with
C
a genus
0
nodal curve over a base
B
such that
u_{*}[C]=D
on geometric fibers, and
p_{i}:B\to C
are sections landing in the smooth locus of
C
. See
[
AO01
]
. The total evaluation map

\operatorname{ev}=\operatorname{ev}_{X,D}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,D)\to X^{n}

is defined by sending
(u:C\to X,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))
to
(u(p_{1}),\ldots,u(p_{n}))
.
There is an open subscheme
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,D)^{\operatorname{odp}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,D)
representing those
(u:C\to X,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))
such that
C
is smooth, the map
u:C\to u(C)
is unramified (which is equivalent to the surjectivity of the map
u^{*}T^{*}X\to T^{*}C
on cotangent spaces), and for every geometric point of the base
B
, the singularities of the image curves are only ordinary double points. See for example
[
KLSW23b
, Lemma 2.14]
.

Let us now assume that
k
is perfect of characteristic not
2
and
3

and that
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected.
Recall that the degree of a del Pezzo surface
X
is the integer
K_{X}^{2}
.
Suppose that the following hypothesis is satisfied, see
[
KLSW23a
, Hypothesis 1]
.

Hypothesis 5.1
.

Either
X
is of degree at least 4, or

X
has degree 3 and

By
[
KLSW23b
, Corollary 3.15]
there is a dense open subset

]

-1

By for example

formulas on multiquadratic algebras or,
in other words, for the restrictions to
\operatorname{Inv}(\operatorname{Sq}_{m})

which by abuse of notation we denote by the same letter here.
Setting

\displaystyle P_{i}

\displaystyle=P^{m}_{i}=\sum_{|K|=i}\prod_{k\in K}x_{k},

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}P_{i}t^{i},

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle=P^{2m}_{i}(1,\ldots,1,x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2m}L_{i}t^{i},

\displaystyle B_{i}

\displaystyle=P_{i}(1+x_{1},\ldots,1+x_{m}),

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}B_{i}t^{i},

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]^{S_{m}},

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},t]^{S_{m}},

we have by definition that
\alpha_{i}=P_{i}
,
\lambda_{i}=\langle 2^{i}\rangle L_{i}

and
\beta_{i}=\langle 2^{i}\rangle B_{i}
. It is therefore sufficient to prove the identities

\displaystyle B_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{i}\gamma_{i,k}^{m}L_{k},

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{i}\delta_{i,k}^{m}B_{k},

in
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
.
To see this, we first note that
P=(1+x_{1}t)\cdots(1+x_{m}t)
,
and that analogously

L=(1+t)^{m}(1+x_{1}t)\cdots(1+x_{m}t)=(1+t)^{m}P.

Since

\displaystyle(1+t)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\binom{m}{i}t^{i},

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-m}

\displaystyle=(1-t+t^{2}-t^{3}\cdots)^{m}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\begin{Bmatrix}m\\
i\end{Bmatrix}t^{i}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]],

we get

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{m}{i-j}P_{j},

\displaystyle P_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\begin{Bmatrix}m\\
i-j\end{Bmatrix}L_{j}.

For
B_{i}
, we find

\displaystyle B_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{m-j}{i-j}P_{j},

\displaystyle P_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\binom{m-j}{i-j}B_{j}.

Here, the first equation follows from the definition and
the second equation via the change of variables

x_{i}\mapsto-1-x_{i}
, this since this yields

B_{i}\mapsto(-1)^{i}P_{i}
and
P_{j}\mapsto(-1)^{j}B_{j}
.
The proof is then finished by combining the formulas.

For the odd case, we recall from
Remark

2.7
that
\operatorname{Inv}(2m+1)\cong\operatorname{Inv}(2m)

via the map induced by
A\mapsto A\oplus K
.
Furthermore under this map we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}^{2m+1}

\displaystyle\mapsto\alpha_{i}^{2m},

\displaystyle\beta_{i}^{2m+1}

\displaystyle\mapsto\beta_{i}^{2m},

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{2m+1}

\displaystyle\mapsto\lambda_{i}^{2m}+\lambda_{i-1}^{2m}.

Here, the last equation follows easily from

P^{2m+1}(\mathbf{x},1)=P^{2m}_{i}(\mathbf{x})+P^{2m}_{i-1}(\mathbf{x})
.
This immediately proves the expression for
\lambda^{n}_{i}

Denoting
\beta_{i}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{i}\mu_{i,k}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{n}
, we have
\mu_{i,k}^{2m}=\mu_{i,k}^{2m+1}+\mu_{i,k+1}^{2m+1}
. Hence we deduce

\mu_{i,k}^{2m+1}=\mu_{i,k}^{2m}-\mu_{i,k+1}^{2m}+\mu_{i,k+2}^{2m}-\ldots

which proves the expression for
\beta^{n}_{i}
.
∎

Remark A.2
.

We finish by mentioning that of course one might investigate other bases of
\operatorname{Inv}(n)
.
It seems that one way to produce interesting bases is the concept of power structures,
see
[
PP23
, Definition 2.1]
.
On
\operatorname{\widehat{W}}(K)
, or on the semiring
\operatorname{\widetilde{W}}(K)
, we have two canonical power structures
given by symmetric and exterior powers of quadratic forms.
Pre-composing them with the trace invariant
\operatorname{Tr}
produces new Witt invariants
which contain subcollections that form bases. The
\lambda
-basis is constructed
in this way using exterior powers.

Conversely, on the semiring
\operatorname{Et}_{*}(K)=\bigcup_{n\geqslant 0}\operatorname{Et}_{n}(K)
,
under direct sum and tensor product,
we have analogous power structures given by symmetric and exterior powers.
Note that these operations do not commute with the trace invariant,
for example, in general

\operatorname{Tr}(\bigwedge^{2}\mathcal{E}_{a})=\operatorname{Tr}(K)=\langle 1\rangle\neq\langle a\rangle=\bigwedge^{2}\langle 2,2a\rangle=\bigwedge^{2}\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{E}_{a})
.
Therefore post-composing them with the trace invariant again
produces new Witt invariants which are priori are not related to the ones from above
and again can give rise to bases for
\operatorname{Inv}(n)
.
In particular, let us define
\chi^{n}_{i}=\chi_{i}\in\operatorname{Inv}(n)
by setting

\chi_{i}(A)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Sym}^{i}A)
.
Note that
\operatorname{Sym}^{i}E_{a}\cong jE_{a}(\oplus K)
with
j=\lfloor{\frac{i+1}{2}}\rfloor

and the summand
(\oplus K)
only appears when
i
is even.
Since
\operatorname{Tr}
is a (semi-)ring homomorphism such that
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{E}_{\delta})^{2}=2\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{E}_{\delta})
,
we have

\chi_{i}=\beta_{i}+\mathbb{Z}\langle\beta_{i-1},\ldots,\beta_{0}\rangle.

Therefore,
\chi_{0},\ldots,\chi_{m}
form a basis of

\operatorname{Inv}(n)
and the base change to the
\beta
-basis
is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. (However, it seems tricky to write down closed formulas
for the coefficients of this base change.)
In particular, a given Witt invariant
\alpha\in\operatorname{Inv}(n)

is
\beta
-integral if and only if it is
\chi
-integral.

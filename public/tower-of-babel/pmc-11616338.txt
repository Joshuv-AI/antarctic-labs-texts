Students’ perception and self-efficacy in blended learning of medical nutrition course: a mixed-method research
Authors: Aksara Regmi, Xuanxia Mao, Qi Qi, Wenjing Tang, Kefeng Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe blended teaching mode, which combines online and offline learning, has gained significant traction in higher education. This study aims to explore the impact of blended learning on students’ academic performance, engagement, and self-efficacy in a medical nutrition course.MethodsA mixed-method research design was employed, involving 110 undergraduate students enrolled in a blended learning medical nutrition course and a control group of 93 students from a traditional learning environment. Data collection included academic performance assessments, semi-structured interviews, and an anonymous questionnaire. Quantitative data were analyzed using t-tests and chi-square tests, while qualitative data were subjected to thematic analysis.ResultsStudents in the blended learning group demonstrated significantly higher self-efficacy, particularly in organizing their study plans, participating in interactive learning activities, and applying course knowledge. Academic performance was notably better in collaborative assessments, such as group discussions and exploratory projects, in the blended learning group compared to the control group. Qualitative analysis revealed that students appreciated the flexibility and engagement offered by the blended learning model, although they also faced challenges related to self-discipline and the learning environment.ConclusionsThe blended learning approach enhances student engagement, self-efficacy, and collaborative skills, particularly in group-based assessments. While students benefit from the flexibility and richness of learning resources, challenges related to self-discipline and learning environments need to be addressed to optimize the effectiveness of blended learning.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-024-06339-5.Keywords: Blended learning, Thematic analysis, Academic performance, Learning attitudes, Student engagement, Autonomous learning

Background

Blended teaching mode, which integrates various teaching forms and information technology, has become the development direction of higher education reform [1, 2]. The blended teaching mode is a combination of “online” and “offline” methodologies, merging the benefits of both traditional classroom instruction and digital learning platforms. Students initially engage in self-directed learning online, utilizing MOOC (Massive Open Online Courses), SPOC (Small Private Online Courses), and other digital formats. The in-person classroom then serves as a catalyst for delving into the most crucial and challenging topics, with a focus on the teacher guiding discussions and fostering collaborative learning among students [3]. Blended teaching mode can stimulate students’ interest in learning through a variety of teaching forms, encourage students to make rational use of time outside the classroom for independent learning, and improve students’ high-level thinking ability [4, 5].

Following the abrupt shift to online education in response to the Covid-19 pandemic, numerous studies have been carried out to investigate the effects of online learning or blended learning on students’ academic performance and their connection to study behavior. A study [6] demonstrated that students characterized by positive, negative, and neutral attitude towards online education exhibit differential responses to the online instruction. On the other hand, teacher’ attitude [7, 8], stress, their prior experience has a significant impact on their use and perception of support in online teaching. Blended [9] education’s flexibility is the key motivator for teachers and students, and their perceived performance was impacted by their attitudes and emotions toward blended education. Medical courses that utilize blended learning have been shown to improve student engagement, enhance learning outcomes, and better prepare students for the challenges of the healthcare industry. In each medical discipline, blended learning consistently improves students’ satisfaction, engagement, learning attitude and academic outcomes [10–12]. For example, Students enrolled in blended learning of exercise physiology achieved significantly higher exam marks compared to those in traditional classes, despite the blended learning group facing more challenging exam [13]. Blended teaching of Orthodontic leads faster and more autonomous learning to students than e-learning or traditional learning alone [14, 15].

It is noteworthy that cognitive engagement in self-regulated learning was found to play a more significant role than behavioral engagement in predicting students’ clinical reasoning performance [16]. Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which focuses on autonomy, competence, and relatedness [17], can provide valuable insights into how blended teaching mode enhance the students’ performance. Autonomy refers to the need for individuals to feel a sense of control over their actions and decisions. In a blended learning context, this can be achieved by providing learners with choices regarding the pace, content, and method of learning. Competence is the need to feel effective and capable in one’s actions. Blended learning environments can support this need by offering personalized feedback, challenging tasks, and opportunities for skill development. Relatedness involves the need to feel connected to others. Blended learning can meet this need through online discussions, group projects, and collaborative activities.

The current research aims to examine the relative importance of students’ autonomy, competence and relatedness to academic performance in blended learning. To investigate this research question, we identified three set of variables. The first pertains to students’ self-efficacy in blended education, including assessment of students’ confidence level in accomplishing learning tasks. The second focuses on students’ academic performance, consisting of formative assessment and final examination score. The third examines students’ perceptions of blended learning, exploring their attitude, experience and suggestions about blended teaching.

Methods

Study design

Our study used the three following methods to collect data that would reflect perception, practice, and achievements of students in doing blended tasks: academic results, follow-up semi-structured interviews, and an anonymous questionnaire. Semi-structure interview and self-efficiency questionnaire were conducted to explore students’ learning interest, attitude, and strategy use in the context of blended learning. Semi-structure interview data were thematically analyzed as previously reported [18]. Thematic analysis, characterized by its theoretical freedom, offers remarkable flexibility adaptable to various research needs, presenting data in a rich and detailed yet complex manner [19, 20]. Thematic analysis also allows participants to express themselves while drawing upon theoretical frameworks, enabling a close-up analysis of the phenomenon, hence proves valuable for establishing the generalizability of inferences and providing a more vivid and nuanced explanation of the phenomenon [21]. The research procedure

Research participants

The study involved two groups of second-year Clinical Medicine students at Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, focused on evaluating the effectiveness of a blended teaching mode for the “Medical Nutrition” course. Experimental Group (EC): Comprised of 110 students (49 male, 61 female) who participated in the blended teaching mode during the 2020–2021 academic year. Control Group (CC): Included 93 students (41 male, 52 female) who completed the traditional teaching method in 2019. Additionally, a subset of 20 students (10 male, 10 female) from the EC group was selected for interviews to gain further insights into their experiences with the blended teaching approach.

Semi-structured interview protocol

The interviews were conducted in a semi-structured manner. An interview guide was created and provided in outline form (See Supplement S1). The interview guide included purposes, structure, introduction and questions across categories like learning activities, factors impacting student engagement in online learning, challenges in online learning, integrated and non-integrated blended learning, collaborative learning, teacher roles and other topics. Standardized open-ended questions were posed as baseline queries within each category. Beyond the standardized questions, the interviewer asked additional questions based on student responses, in a self-determined sequence and wording, to expand the comprehensiveness of the collected data.

Three researchers were involved in the open-coding process. The first and second researchers independently reviewed the transcripts, extracting content relevant to students’ learning experiences and self-efficacy in the blended environment. The interviews were audio recorded and then transcribed. To enable students to convey their perspectives with precise language and expression, they were permitted to respond in their mother tongue, Mandarin. The transcripts were then translated into English for analysis. Discrepancies in coding were discussed and resolved through consensus meetings, and a minimum code overlap rate of 90% was achieved. Thematic analysis was conducted using the software MAXQDA (version 2022) [22], which assisted in managing and organizing the coded data. The analysis began with the identification of initial codes to capture students’ learning experiences in blended education, focusing on different aspects of self-efficacy. These initial codes were then grouped into sub-themes based on commonalities, which eventually evolved into overarching themes that reflected broader patterns in students’ emotional and cognitive responses.

The next stage involved closely examining adjacent text segments within each coded perception and self-efficacy category to draw broader patterns of meaning [19]. The coding results were paraphrased from participants’ narratives and synthesized into overarching themes. The final stage of the analysis involved identifying overall themes that represented the essence of students’ blended learning experiences. Through this rigorous process, we identified three major themes related to students’ learning interest, autonomous learning and enhanced interaction to the blended learning model.

Questionnaire

The questionnaire was modified utilizing a methodology that has been previously documented [23]. Briefly, this questionnaire was designed according to the characteristics of blended teaching mode along with the influencing factors of autonomous learning. The questionnaire was focused on autonomous learning attitude, autonomous learning behaviors, and satisfaction evaluation of each link of blended teaching. Questionnaires were distributed to the subjects by Sojump app and WeChat. Questionnaires were distributed to 110 students who participated in the blended teaching mode and to 93 students who engaged in the traditional teaching mode.

Implementation process of blended teaching

The teachers in the targeted EC class carried out the blended mode of teaching during the training. They developed a lesson plan for the blended instruction model and attended a blended instruction model roll out training, which was conducted during the week before the intervention to helped teachers focus more on blended instruction and enact blended instructional behaviors in the classroom and less on face-to-face instruction alone.

The blended teaching process implemented in this study mainly consists of three parts:

Pre-learning was conducted through online learning platform (including Super Star Learning Pass and WeChat group), which includes reading case materials, information research tasks, and completing pre-learning reports; students’ pre-learning is checked through self-assessment questions.

During the second week of the course, project requirements were distributed online. The topic selection was supposed to combine course content with real-life situations, focus on solving a specific problem, and propose innovative ideas. During the fourth week of the course, topic selection was finalized offline, and each group presented their chosen topic and project plan for feedback from the instructor. From the fifth to the tenth week of the course, the project was executed both online and offline. During the project implementation, each group did communicate with the instructor online to discuss and resolve any issues that arised. During the twelfth week of the course, final project reports were presented offline through videos and PowerPoint presentations. The project evaluation was conducted by the course teaching team, considering the topic selection, implementation, and results of the project.

Data analysis

The independent samples t-test were deployed to compare each part of the scores gained by the EC and CC, including the group discussion & presentation, homework, team project and the final examination score. The results of the questionnaire were analyzed by chi-square test. SPSS 26.0 was used for qualitive data analysis.

Qualitative content analysis was used as a data analysis strategy designed to disclose how individual students understood or interpreted questions related to changes in interests and attitudes, and to determine the factors that were involved in such changes [21]. The authors used the following analysis strategies: transcribing interview narratives, noting patterns and themes that emerged, clustering and counting patterns, comparing and contrasting them to find differences between two different groups of students, noting relationships between variables, and creating a logical chain of evidence [19]. In short, qualitative thematic evidence provided a basis for discussing the evolution of ideas and behaviors across cases supporting by the questionnaires. Both quantitative and qualitative data contributed to a comprehensive interpretation of the students’ experiences.

Academic results under different teaching modes

Table 2 demonstrates the result of comparison of student academic performance in each session between (EC) group and (CC) group. For the group discussion and presentation formative assessment, students in the EC performed significantly better (88.4 ± 7.3) compared to the CC (83.7 ± 6.5). On the term project, EC students also performed significantly better (93.6 ± 8.1) versus CC students (85.7 ± 9.0). However, there were no significant differences in performance on the homework assignments or final examination between groups. Overall, the results show that students in the hybrid EC performed significantly better on collaborative assessments involving group discussion, presentation, and term projects. However, no difference was seen in individual homework assignments or high-stakes final exam performance between the hybrid and traditional teaching modes. This suggests the hybrid format may lend itself better to developing collaborative work skills compared to traditional classroom teaching.

In offline classes, they value the interactive and collaborative environment characterized by discussions, problem-solving, and group activities with peers. This integration of theoretical learning with practical application significantly enhances their perception of blended learning. Overall, students find the blended approach engaging, effective, and beneficial for their learning experience (Table 3).

The perception of student related to learning interest in blended learning

Learning Interest was notably enriched by rich learning materials, with one student appreciating how blended instruction not only captured attention but also ignited enthusiasm for learning. The access to top-tier courses from both domestic and foreign universities introduced students to a breadth of diverse perspectives, which was seen as a valuable enhancement to their educational experience.

Student perspectives conveyed a unanimous appreciation for blended teaching’s ability to kindle and sustain a robust learning interest. Enthusiasm was discussed as an enjoyable aspect of blended learning. Interviewee 6 said,

I think incorporating various learning tools, such as engaging videos and interactive online self-tests has a transformational impact on our learning interest. This approach not only fostered a more dynamic interaction with both peers and instructors but also kindled a newfound enthusiasm for delving into the subject matter.

The seamless integration of varied teaching methods, exposure to diverse learning resources, and the evolution of evaluation mechanisms collectively contributed to an educational environment that resonated deeply with the participants, fostering a genuine passion for learning. Many students indicated that high-quality online courses from different educational institutions introduced diverse perspectives. Interviewee 8 remarked:

The integration of vivid case studies from massive open online courses offered me a tangible connection to the real-world application of theoretical concepts, an aspect that further ignited their intrinsic interest in the subject matter.

Students discussed the different types of engagement they experienced in the blended learning environment. Interviewee 17 states:

By considering factors such as platform engagement, study hours, and active participation, this multifaceted approach to evaluation not only provided a more holistic assessment but also motivated learners to be consistently engaged, thereby deepening our learning interest.

The perception of student related to autonomous learning in blended learning

The interviews revealed that most students favored the blended teaching mode for its flexibility, allowing them to study independently and at their own pace. This preference was highlighted by Interviewee 9, who appreciated the ability to schedule learning according to personal time and pace, the option to repeatedly watch instructional videos, and the added flexibility during the epidemic.

Further insights into students’ perceptions of autonomous learning emerged as they discussed their use of learning strategies. Interviewee 12 commented on the flexibility of the blended learning method, noting its support for personalized learning:

“I prefer using the online learning platform for preview before class. It gives me confidence and enhances my participation in class interactions when I attend with a foundational understanding.”

Interviewee 16 discussed the structure of blended teaching:

“In this mode, the teacher places content online that we can learn independently, allowing me to arrange my study time flexibly. The teacher also monitors the completion of online tasks, like questions or homework.”

Other students reflected on how the rich and diverse online resources broadened their horizons, deepened their knowledge, and increased their engagement with the course content. Many observed that their learning efficiency improved as they could independently study simpler content and allocate more time to complex topics.

Overall, the blended teaching approach has enabled students to manage their own time and access a wealth of course materials effectively. This autonomy has not only increased their engagement with the courses but also fostered the development of their own learning behaviors and strategies, illustrating the transformative impact of blended learning on student autonomy.

The perception of student related to more interaction in blended learning

Blended learning has significantly improved interaction, enhancing communication between students and teachers and facilitating collaborative problem-solving. Students appreciate the immediacy and utility of online discussions.

Interviewee 3 praised the interactive nature of blended learning:

“The blended teaching mode is more interactive. When I encounter problems, I can always ask questions on the online platform and discuss with my classmates and teachers to get timely answers.”

Interviewee 11 emphasized the value of group discussions for enhancing collaboration and social bonds:

“I like group discussions because they allow for teamwork and knowledge sharing. This not only speeds up problem-solving but also strengthens connections among classmates, making even solitary online study engaging.” Interviewee 14 noted improvements in collaborative learning compared to traditional methods: “The blended course improves collaborative learning with my peers, enhancing our ability to tackle academic challenges together.”

In summary, students report that blended learning fosters active interaction and collaboration, enhancing both learning experiences and academic progress. This approach facilitates mutual learning and strengthens community within the classroom.

The difficulties faced by the students and their suggestions

Although students reported improvements in learning interest, autonomous learning skills, and interaction through blended learning, they also faced several challenges:

Interviewee 8 discussed the struggle with self-discipline in an online setting:

“Online learning relies heavily on self-control. Sometimes I can’t help but open websites that are not related to the course when I’m on the computer, and then time passes without me realizing it.”

Interviewee 20 emphasized the importance of a conducive learning environment:

“The environment for online learning is very important. I face distractions in the dormitory, while the library or home provides a quieter setting. Network stability also significantly impacts learning effectiveness.”

Challenges with group work were noted, including uneven work distribution and difficulties in collaborating with unfamiliar classmates. Moreover, the increased interaction sometimes led to decreased concentration. Delays in teacher responses to questions posed in the Q&A mailbox also hindered timely academic support.

To address these issues, students suggested forming teams based on self-selection to ensure more equitable work distribution and better collaboration. They recommended adopting more dynamic and engaging formats for group discussions, such as debates or acting performances. Enhancements to the online platform are necessary to make it more user-friendly and resource-rich, along with ensuring timely responses from teachers. Additionally, integrating round-table offline sessions and scheduling extra time for Q&A during classes could further deepen understanding and foster a more interactive learning environment.

Discussion

General overview

While prior research has emphasized the benefits of project-based learning within blended models for enhancing academic performance and knowledge retention [14, 15, 23, 24], our study specifically examines the impact of exploratory projects in blended learning of medical nutrition on self-efficacy and academic outcomes. Student feedback indicate that a structured blend of online and offline tasks not only promotes cognitive growth but also builds confidence in autonomous learning—essential skills in healthcare [25].

The exploratory project was designed to promote in-depth learning by encouraging critical thinking and teamwork, which motivated students to engage actively with course concepts for a deeper understanding of complex topics. This approach aligns with research highlighting how self-directed, collaborative learning enhances the student experience in clinical science programs by fostering essential skills for professional development [26]. Many students reported the exploratory project made their studies more meaningful and comprehensive, noting that practical application added value and lasting relevance. Others emphasized the rewarding challenge of tackling complex issues with peers, which strengthened their comprehension and problem-solving abilities.

By actively engaging in hands-on tasks, students experienced a boost in interest, independence, and peer interaction, elements they recognized as essential for their improved performance. Many students expressed a sense of ownership over their learning, highlighting how collaborative discussions made concepts clearer and more meaningful. These aspects directly enhanced their understanding and long-term retention of the material.

Consistent with previous research on the value of active learning [27], our findings demonstrate that this blended approach supports not only immediate academic success but also fosters the adaptability necessary for lifelong learning, promoting a deeper understanding of the material.

Elevated learning interest

Our findings indicate that a carefully structured blend of online and offline resources, aligned with students’ personal and professional goals, fosters a strong intrinsic interest in the course material, leading to a deeper and more meaningful learning experience.

In the current research, students felt the variety and depth of learning materials in the blended model not only captured their attention but also heightened their enthusiasm for learning. Access to resources from multiple institutions offered diverse perspectives, which, as previous studies suggest, can broaden students’ critical thinking and deepen their engagement by exposing them to different viewpoints and knowledge systems [14, 15, 28]. This enriched exposure likely contributed to a more comprehensive understanding of the material, fostering a learning environment that goes beyond standard curriculum boundaries and encourages students to think more expansively and creatively.

The proactive learning environment in our study encouraged students to take ownership of their educational journey, motivating them to seek additional resources and explore beyond course requirements. This approach nurtured essential skills like adaptability, critical thinking, and problem-solving, aligning with research that emphasizes the value of engaging learning experiences in developing cognitive flexibility [20]. By promoting active involvement, our study contributes to a growing body of evidence that blended learning can prepare students for complex challenges in the healthcare field.

Key factors like interactive content, multimedia resources, and collaborative projects were essential in sustaining student interest. Students reported that these elements created an immersive environment, allowing them to explore theoretical knowledge in ways aligned with their interests, enhancing understanding and engagement. This approach, rooted in experiential learning principles, fosters lifelong learning habits and continuous inquiry beyond the classroom [23, 24, 28]. Our findings suggest that structured collaboration promotes adaptability and deepens students’ understanding of complex concepts. By providing a rich, engaging experience, blended learning in our study not only improved academic performance but also prepared students with essential skills and a mindset for ongoing professional development in specialized fields.

Our study demonstrates the unique potential of blended learning to cultivate sustained interest through exploratory projects and meaningful, diverse content, ultimately contributing to students’ academic growth and enthusiasm for continuous learning in their future professional roles.

Autonomous learning

Our study explores how specific components in blended learning—such as personalized resources, collaborative activities, and social media integration—support learner autonomy in medical nutrition education. The flexibility of our blended model allows students to independently access a diverse array of resources, fostering a personalized and self-directed learning experience. Previous research suggests that this approach can empower students to take greater control over their learning, enhancing both engagement and motivation [29]. Compared to traditional approaches, our model demonstrates increased student engagement and sustained autonomy, providing a more meaningful and transformative learning environment.

Building on prior research [30], our students stated that structured, teacher-guided strategies—such as group projects and peer discussions—enhance students’ initiative and responsibility in their studies [31]. These collaborative settings encourage critical thinking and provide a supportive space for students to challenge ideas, share perspectives, and reinforce mutual growth. This approach builds a sense of ownership and accountability, ultimately supporting both academic and personal development [32].

Moreover, our study highlights the role of social media and online discussion forums in reinforcing autonomous learning. These digital tools extend learning beyond classroom boundaries, enabling students to connect with peers and professionals across various fields. Such interactions contribute to a deeper understanding by encouraging students to engage with course content in diverse and meaningful ways, promoting a holistic learning experience enriched by multiple perspectives [33].

In summary, our model’s integration of diverse resources and collaborative frameworks offers a robust foundation for cultivating independent, engaged learners, ready to thrive in interconnected environments.

Enhanced interaction

This study addresses a critical gap by examining how structured, collaborative settings within blended learning, such as exploratory projects and peer discussions, enhance self-efficacy and engagement in medical nutrition—a specialized area with unique demands for teamwork and applied knowledge [34]. While prior research often highlights general benefits of blended learning, it rarely explores how structured collaboration can enhance confidence and the practical application of knowledge in specialized fields requiring teamwork and applied skills. Our findings reveal that these collaborative settings encourage students to take initiative and assume greater responsibility for their learning, thereby fostering confidence and academic efficacy, particularly in applying theoretical concepts to practical scenarios. This insight contributes to the existing literature by highlighting the specific advantage of structured collaboration in blended learning for promoting self-efficacy and hands-on engagement in complex subjects [35].

Additionally, our study observed that social media tools and discussion forums amplify learning by connecting students within a broader network. Instead of merely facilitating informal exchanges, these tools expand formal educational experiences, enabling students to share perspectives and collaborate across diverse settings [36]. This extended, global network deepens students’ understanding of course content and strengthens their engagement, showcasing the potential of digital tools to enrich interactive learning in specialized medical fields [37].

Previous research [27, 38] underscored the importance of social interactions and teacher strategies in blended learning, our findings demonstrate that structured interactions—such as project-based tasks and collaborative problem-solving—are essential for creating a supportive and autonomous learning environment. Students reported that these structured interactions helped them manage their learning more effectively, increasing their confidence and self-efficacy.

This proactive approach to blended learning not only improves academic outcomes but also cultivates adaptive and engaged students in specialized medical education, underscoring the unique advantages of structured collaboration and digital integration.

Strengths and limitations

Through a combination of quantitative data and qualitative interviews, we’ve developed a comprehensive framework that clearly links blended learning practices with increased student self-efficacy, providing essential insights for lifelong learning in a dynamic global context. However, the current study’s findings may not apply universally across all educational settings, particularly those that require more practical engagement. Our sample, limited to undergraduate students from a single institution, restricts generalizability, as the homogeneity of academic backgrounds and learning environments may not capture the experiences of students in varied contexts. Additionally, the exclusive focus on a medical nutrition course means that results may not extend to disciplines with different pedagogical demands.

Moreover, the blended learning model’s dependence on technological platforms could challenge students lacking stable internet access or digital devices. A further limitation involves reliance on self-reported data, where perceptions may not fully correspond to actions, warranting caution in interpreting these findings. Lastly, as this study focused on short-term academic outcomes and self-efficacy, the long-term effects of blended learning on professional development remain unexplored. Future research could address these limitations by employing more controlled environments, selecting a broader array of participants, enhancing technological support, and implementing effective communication strategies to deepen our understanding of blended learning’s impact and scalability.

Suggestions for future research and practice

Given the rapid development of AI technologies, future research should explore how AI could play a transformative role in blended education. AI has the potential to personalize learning experiences by adapting content to individual student needs, provide real-time feedback through intelligent tutoring systems, and automate assessment processes, which could reduce the workload for educators and allow for more targeted learning interventions [39, 40]. However, the integration of AI in education also presents challenges, including the need for equitable access to AI tools, concerns about data privacy, and the potential loss of human interaction that is essential for fostering critical thinking and social skills [41]. Further studies should investigate these opportunities and challenges to better understand how AI can be harnessed to enhance the effectiveness and scalability of blended learning models, while also ensuring that the human elements of teaching and learning are preserved.

In addition, findings from this study could inform the development of blended learning models in other medical courses or even across different disciplines. The insights gained regarding self-efficacy and student engagement can help educators design more effective and supportive learning environments, especially in professional training programs where practical and theoretical learning must be well-integrated [42]. Expanding the application of these findings could provide greater relevance to broader educational contexts, promoting better learning outcomes beyond the immediate context of this study.

Conclusion

Our study revealed that students in the blended environment demonstrated increased interest, higher motivation, and successful learning outcomes. They benefited from stimulated interest, proactive initiative, positive attitudes, and diverse strategies, which collectively enhanced their learning performance. Our research broadens the scope of blended learning studies by examining not only academic progress and scores but also the dynamic interplay of interest, attitudes, and strategies throughout the learning process. Our research not only underscore the effectiveness of blended learning in promoting deeper educational outcomes but also provide a robust framework for future educational innovations and research in this area. By drawing on a combination of quantitative data and qualitative assessments, we delineate a clear link between innovative teaching practices and enhanced student performance, setting a precedent for the integration of similar strategies in educational institutions aiming to foster comprehensive learning and development.

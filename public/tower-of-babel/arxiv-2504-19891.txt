Bending parameterization of one-sided degenerate Kleinian surface groups
Bruno Dular

Abstract
. It was recently proved that quasi-Fuchsian manifolds are uniquely determined by their bending laminations. This paper concerns a similar result for certain non-quasi-Fuchsian manifolds: those obtained by degenerating one end but not the other, i.e. those appearing in boundaries of Bers slices. More precisely, we show that such hyperbolic manifolds are uniquely determined by the end structure of the degenerate end and the bending lamination of the other. The end structure consists of the parabolic locus, which is a multicurve, together with ending laminations or conformal structures on each component of its complement.

1.3.
Further directions

A natural question is whether
any
structure in
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
is uniquely determined by the combination of its bending and ending laminations. The main theorem of this paper implies that it is the case when the top side is non-degenerate and the bottom moderate subsurface has no quasi-conformal deformations, i.e.
\Sigma^{-}
is empty or a union of thrice-punctured spheres. But this is still far from a complete answer.

An essential difficulty lies in proving that any
geometrically finite
structure on
S\times{\mathbb{R}}
is uniquely determined by its pair of bending laminations. This is expected to hold, and it was proved in the case of
rational
bending laminations by Bonahon and Otal
[
6
]
. In the general case, the current strategy needs to be refined in order to prove
rigidity
of geometrically finite structures with respect to their bending lamination.

For geometrically infinite Kleinian surface structures, an explicit description of the combination of ending laminations and bending laminations that can occur was recently given by Baba and Ohshika in
[
3
]
.

2.2.
Laminations and trees

Let

if all its leaves are. A geodesic lamination is

if it is minimal, irrational and each component of its complement is either simply-connected or an annulus around a cusp. The space

if there is a sequence of positive real numbers
\epsilon_{n}\searrow 0
such that
\epsilon_{n}\ell_{\rho_{n}}(\gamma)\to i(\mu,\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(S)
.

Morgan and Shalen proved that actions on real trees arise naturally as limits of divergent sequences of discrete and faithful representations of

in
\rm{Isom}^{+}({\mathbb{H}}^{3})
, as stated below
[

. The fact that real trees arising in this way are in fact dual to measured laminations is due to Skora
[

]
. See

]
for a detailed exposition.

Theorem 2.3

(Morgan–Shalen
[

,

)
.

Let
(\rho_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
. Then, up to extracting a subsequence, either:

The sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n}
converges in
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
, or;

(2)

The sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n}

of

.

A geodesic lamination
\alpha
is said to be

transversely

,

2.4.
Pleated surfaces

Let
M
be a hyperbolic three-manifold,
S
a complete hyperbolic surface (not necessarily compact) and
L
a geodesic lamination on
S
. A
pleated surface with pleating locus

L
is a continuous map
f\colon S\to M
which is a totally geodesic immersion on
S-L
and on
L
. In general, the
flat pieces
of
S-L
are bent along the pleating locus, with an angle recorded into a
bending cocycle

\beta
which is an element of the space
{\mathcal{H}}(L;{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})
of
{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued cocycles along
L

[
8
]
(which is a torus whose dimension is determined by
S
and
L
). When
f
is locally convex, this bending cocycle defines a transverse measure with support contained in
L
, called the
bending
or
pleating (measured) lamination
of
f
. The induced path metric on
S
inside
M
is hyperbolic and defines a point
h\in{\mathcal{T}}(S)
, which can also be encoded in a
shearing cocycle

\sigma\in{\mathcal{H}}(L;{\mathbb{R}})
. The boundary of the convex core
C(\rho)
of
\rho\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
is an embedded convex pleated surface
[
19
, II.1.11,12]
. In particular, its bending measured lamination has weight
<\pi
along closed leaves.

Now, let
\Lambda
be a
maximal
geodesic lamination. Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
. If there exists a pleated surface inside
M_{\rho}
with pleating locus
\Lambda
(we say that
\Lambda
is
realized
in
M_{\rho}
or by
\rho
), then it is unique and the
shear-bend cocycle
provides a parameterization of such representations. More generally, one can consider
abstract pleated surfaces

(\widetilde{f},\pi_{1}(S),\rho)
where
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{S}\to{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
is a pleated surface inside
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
which is equivariant with respect to the (not necessarily discrete) representation
\rho\in{\mathcal{X}}(S)
. Given a maximal geodesic lamination
\Lambda
, let
{\mathcal{R}}(\Lambda)
denote the open subspace of
{\mathcal{X}}(S)
consisting of those representations
realizing

\Lambda
.

Theorem 2.5

(Bonahon
[
8
, Theorem D]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a maximal geodesic lamination. The map
{\mathcal{R}}(\Lambda)\to{\mathcal{H}}(\Lambda;{\mathbb{C}}/2\pi i{\mathbb{Z}})
sending a representation
\rho
realizing
\Lambda
to its shear-bend cocycle
\sigma(\rho)+i\beta(\rho)
is a biholomorphic homeomorphism onto an open subset of
{\mathcal{H}}(\Lambda;{\mathbb{C}}/2\pi i{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Using pleated surfaces one can also speak of the length of a
realizable
measured lamination in a Kleinian surface manifold. Consider
\lambda\in{\mathcal{ML}}(S)
and
\rho\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
. If
\rho
realizes
|\lambda|
(if
|\lambda|
is non-maximal, this means that
M_{\rho}
contains a pleated surface
P
whose pleating locus contains the support
|\lambda|
of
\lambda
), then the length
\ell_{\rho}(\lambda)
is defined as the length of
\lambda
on
P
. When
\lambda
is a closed curve, this coincides with the length of a geodesic representative. The following was stated by Thurston and proved by Brock.

Theorem 2.6

(Brock
[
15
]
)
.

The length function
(\rho,\lambda)\mapsto\ell_{\rho}(\lambda)
is continuous on the open subset of
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)\times{\mathcal{ML}}(S)
consisting of pairs
(\rho,\lambda)
such that
\rho
realizes
|\lambda|
.

2.7.
End structures

Let
M
be a hyperbolic three-manifold with incompressible boundary, and let
C(M)
be its convex core. See
[
36
, §3.12,5.5]
for a definition of the
relative ends
of
M
, which describe an
ideal boundary
of
M
. A relative end
E
corresponds to a decreasing nested sequence
V_{1}\supset V_{2}\supset\dots
of connected open subsets of
M
“going to infinity”, each homeomorphic to
S_{E}\times(0,1)
, where
S_{E}
is a finite type oriented surface (possibly with cusps) that only depends on
E
(and actually realized as a pleated surface inside
M
).

A relative end is either contained in the complement of the convex core
C(M)
, in which case it is
geometrically finite
and it corresponds to a component of
\partial_{\infty}M
, or it is contained in
C(M)
, it is then
geometrically infinite
. Geometrically finite ends do not contain any closed geodesic, since all closed geodesics are contained in the convex core
C(M)
. On the other hand, a geometrically infinite end
E
, with associated surface
S_{E}
, is characterized by the existence of a sequence of simple closed geodesics
(\gamma^{*}_{n})_{n}
in
S_{E}
whose geodesic representatives
(\gamma_{n})_{n}
in
M
have bounded length and exit the end
E
. The limit of
(\gamma_{n}^{*})_{n}
is a well-defined recurrent (equivalently, measurable) geodesic lamination
\Lambda(E)\subset S_{E}
, called the
ending lamination
of
E

[
51
,
7
]
. It is an arational recurrent geodesic lamination on
S_{E}
.

The
end invariant
of a Kleinian surface group
\rho\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
can be encoded into a quadruplet
\EE(\rho)=(P,\Sigma,m,L)
where the parabolic locus
P
is a simple essential multicurve on
S^{-}\sqcup S^{+}
, the moderate part
\Sigma
is a union of connected components of
(S^{-}\sqcup S^{+})-P
,
m\in{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma)
and
L
is a union of arational recurrent laminations in each component of
S(L)\coloneqq(S^{-}\sqcup S^{+})-(P\cup\Sigma)
. For a connected component
L^{\prime}
of
L
, let
S(L^{\prime})
denote the connected component of
S(L)
containing
L^{\prime}
. In particular the end invariant
\EE(\rho)
decomposes into a
topological end data

e=(P,L)
and a
conformal end data

c=(\Sigma,m)
. We also identify
e
with the measurable geodesic lamination
P\cup L
and denote by
S(e)
the open subsurface of
S
obtained as the union of
S(L)
and collar neighborhoods of components of
P
.

The end structure
\EE(\rho)
is always
doubly incompressible
in the sense of
[
2
, §2.9]
, i.e.
P\cup L
has no parallel components. Any such end structure is realizable as the end invariant of a Kleinian surface group
[
41
,
39
]
.

Given a topological end datum
e=(P,L)
, we denote by
{\mathcal{AH}}(S;e)
the subspace of
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
consisting of those representations
\rho
whose end structure
\EE(\rho)
has topological end datum
e
. Denote also by
e^{-}
and
e^{+}
the
bottom
and
top
parts of the topological end datum, respectively.

For a one-sided degenerate structure
\rho\in{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S)
, its parabolic locus
P
is entirely contained in
S^{-}
and its moderate part has the form
\Sigma^{-}\sqcup S^{+}\subseteq S^{-}\sqcup S^{+}
.

Baba and Ohshika described the combination of ending laminations, parabolic locus and bending laminations that occur for Kleinian surface groups
[
3
]
. It follows from their result that, for
\rho\in{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})
where
e^{-}=e=P\cup L
, the bending lamination
\bb^{+}(\rho)
of the top boundary of its convex core
fills

S(e^{-})
with
e^{-}
, i.e. any nonzero measured lamination on
S(e^{-})
has nonzero intersection with
P\cup L
or
\bb^{+}(\rho)
. Hence it lies in the subspace
{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
consisting of those measured laminations without closed leaves of weight
\geq\pi
, and which fill
S(e^{-})
with
e^{-}
. They also proved that every element of
{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
can be realized as the top bending lamination of a structure in
{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})
.

2.8.
Metrics converging to an end structure

In
[
2
]
, Anderson and Lecuire define a natural topology on the space
{\mathcal{ES}}(F)
of end structures on a hyperbolizable surface
F
, i.e. of doubly incompressible quadruplets
(P,\Sigma,m,L)
on
F
as in the previous section (with
F
instead of
S^{+}\sqcup S^{-}
).

For our purpose we only need to describe one particular type of convergence. A sequence of hyperbolic metrics
m_{n}\in{\mathcal{T}}(F)
(seen as end structures of the form
(\emptyset,F,m_{n},\emptyset)
) converges to an end structure
(P,\Sigma,m,L)
if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

The metrics
m_{n}|_{\Sigma}
converge to
m
in the length spectrum, i.e.
\ell_{m_{n}}(\partial\overline{\Sigma})\to 0
and
\ell_{m_{n}}(c)\to\ell_{m}(c)
for every non-peripheral closed curve
c\subset\Sigma
.

(2)

For each component
L^{\prime}
of
L
, the restrictions
m_{n}|_{S(L^{\prime})}
converge to
L^{\prime}
, i.e. there is a non-peripheral simple closed curve
c\subset S(L^{\prime})
such that

\ell_{m_{n}}(\partial\overline{S(L^{\prime})})/\ell_{m_{n}}(c)\to 0

and any subsequence of
m_{n}|_{S(L^{\prime})}
contains a subsequence converging to a projective measured lamination supported by
L^{\prime}
.

Thus, we can identify
{\mathcal{T}}(F)
with an open subspace of
{\mathcal{ES}}(F)
. Note that
{\mathcal{ES}}(S^{-}\sqcup S^{+})={\mathcal{ES}}(S^{-})\times{\mathcal{ES}}(S^{+})
.

The
end invariants
of an element
\rho
of
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
form an end structure
\EE(\rho)
in
{\mathcal{ES}}(S^{-}\sqcup S^{+})
. For
\rho\in{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S)
, its end structure
\EE(\rho)
has the form
(P,\Sigma^{-},m^{-},L)\times(\emptyset,S^{+},m^{+},\emptyset)\in{\mathcal{ES}}(S^{-})\times{\mathcal{T}}(S^{+})
(and any such end structure is
doubly incompressible
in the sense of
[
2
, §2.9]
).

Recall that a sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)

converges strongly
to
\rho_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
if, up to conjugation, it converges algebraically (i.e.,
\rho_{n}\to\rho_{\infty}
pointwise), and the groups
\rho_{n}(\Gamma)
converge geometrically to
\rho_{\infty}(\Gamma)
(in the Chabauty topology on closed subgroups of
\rm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
), see
[
18
, §3.1]
or
[
36
, §4]
. The topology on
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
induced by strong convergence is called the
strong topology
, and we write
{\mathcal{SH}}(S)
and
{\mathcal{SH}}^{+}(S)
for
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
and
{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S)
endowed with it. The strong topology can also be described in terms of
marked approximate isometries

[
18
, §3.2]
, hence it is first-countable.

The following is a special case of Theorem D in
[
2
]
. The injectivity part is a consequence of the Brock–Canary–Minsky ending lamination theorem
[
17
]
, while the continuity and properness parts are due to Anderson and Lecuire
[
2
]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

The map
\EE\colon{\mathcal{SH}}^{+}(S)\to{\mathcal{ES}}(S^{-})\times{\mathcal{T}}(S^{+})
which associates to
\rho
its end structure is a homeomorphism, with inverse denoted
\overline{\UU}
.

In particular, if a sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{QF}}(S)
has conformal boundary
(m^{-}_{n},m^{+}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in{\mathcal{T}}(S^{-})\times{\mathcal{T}}(S^{+})
converging to the end structure
\EE(\rho_{\infty})=({\mathcal{E}}^{-},m^{+})\in{\mathcal{ES}}(S^{-})\times{\mathcal{T}}(S^{+})
of
\rho_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S)
, then
(\rho_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges strongly to
\rho_{\infty}
.

2.10.
Quasi-conformal deformations of Kleinian structures

Let
\rho_{0}\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
be a Kleinian surface structure. A
quasi-conformal deformation
of
\rho_{0}
is a point
\rho\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
for which there exists a quasi-conformal homeomorphism
w\colon S^{2}\to S^{2}
such that
w\circ\rho_{0}\circ w^{-1}=\rho
. Let
{\mathcal{QH}}(\rho_{0})\subset{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
denote the subspace of quasi-conformal deformations of
\rho_{0}
. As defined this way,
{\mathcal{QH}}(\rho_{0})
is still endowed with the algebraic topology. However, there is another natural topology on
{\mathcal{QH}}(\rho_{0})
, where two quasi-conformal deformations are close to each other if they are conjugate by a quasi-conformal homeomorphism with quasi-conformal constant close to
1
. Those two topologies actually coincide: for quasi-Fuchsian groups it follows from Marden’s quasi-conformal stability theorem
[
36
, §5.1.2]
, in this case
{\mathcal{QH}}(\rho_{0})={\mathcal{QF}}(S)
is the interior of
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
(if
\rho_{0}\in{\mathcal{QF}}(S)
), and in general from Matsuzaki’s
conditional stability
theorem
[
37
, §7.4.2]
. Those topologies on
{\mathcal{QH}}(\rho_{0})
also coincide with the topology of strong convergence, since it is in general finer than the algebraic topology and coarser than the quasi-conformal topology.

Combined with the simultaneous uniformization theorem, we have the following result, which follows from the work of Ahlfors, Bers, Kra, Marden, Maskit, Sullivan (see Theorems 7.53 and 7.55 in
[
37
, §7.4.2]
).

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
with nonempty conformal boundary. Then simultaneous uniformization provides a biholomorphic homeomorphism

U\colon{\mathcal{T}}(\partial_{\infty}\rho_{0})\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}{\mathcal{QH}}(\rho_{0}).

Moreover,
{\mathcal{QH}}(\rho_{0})
is a complex (regular) submanifold of an open subset of the representation variety of
\pi_{1}(S)
into
\rm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
.

Let
e_{0}=(P_{0},L_{0})
be the topological end invariants of
\rho_{0}\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
, i.e.
P_{0}
is its parabolic locus and
L_{0}
its ending lamination. Recall that
{\mathcal{AH}}(S;e_{0})
denotes the subspace of
{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
consisting of those representations with the same topological end invariants. Then the ending lamination theorem
[
17
]
gives the following.

Theorem 2.12

(Ending lamination theorem, Brock–Canary–Minsky
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in{\mathcal{AH}}(S)
with topological end invariant
e_{0}
. Then
{\mathcal{AH}}(S;e_{0})={\mathcal{QH}}(\rho_{0})
.

3.
Continuity

This section focuses on proving the following continuity statement. The proof directly follows from the work of Lecuire
[
33
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The bending map
\bb^{+}\colon{\mathcal{SH}}^{+}(S)\to{\mathcal{ML}}(S^{+})
is continuous.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the one of
[
33
, Proposition 4.1]
, which proves a more general statement (allowing the emergence of rank one cusps) but in the context of geometrically finite structures. Let us sketch the argument.

Let
(\rho_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence converging to
\rho
in
{\mathcal{SH}}^{+}(S)
. We shall show that, up to passing to a subsequence,
\bb^{+}(\rho_{n})\to\bb^{+}(\rho)
in
{\mathcal{ML}}(S)
. This can be applied to any subsequence of
(\rho_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and is thus sufficient to prove sequential continuity of
\bb^{+}
, hence continuity since
{\mathcal{SH}}^{+}(S)
is first-countable.

Strong convergence implies Hausdorff convergence of their limit sets
[
28
,
40
]
, and of their convex cores
[
13
]
. Using Arzelà–Ascoli theorem and extracting a subsequence, the top boundaries of their convex cores converge as pleated surfaces (in the sense of
[
33
]
) and remain geometrically finite without cusps by definition of
{\mathcal{SH}}^{+}(S)
. Then
[
33
, Lemma 3.4]
, which requires strong convergence, concludes.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

The map
\BB^{+}\colon\widehat{{\mathbb{N}}}\times{\mathcal{T}}(S^{+})\to{\mathcal{ML}}(S^{+})
defined in §
1.2
is continuous.

Proof.

By the above Proposition,
\bb^{+}
is continuous. The map
\overline{\UU}
is continuous by Proposition
2.9
, while
\overline{r}
is continuous by construction. Thus
\BB^{+}
is continuous.
∎

4.
Properness

The goal of this section is to prove that the map
\mm^{-}\times\bb^{+}\colon{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})\to{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{-})\times{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
is proper (see the end of Section
2.7
for a description of the bending laminations that occur for structures in
{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})
). This will imply that
\bb^{+}_{\infty}\colon{\mathcal{T}}(S^{+})\to{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
is proper too. The proof is very similar to the proof of properness of similar maps by Bonahon and Otal in
[
6
]
, Baba and Ohshika in
[
3
]
, Anderson and Lecuire in
[
2
]
and Lecuire in
[
35
]
. It essentially follows by combining the last two references.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
e^{-}=(P,L)
be a topological end structure on
S^{-}
. Let
\Sigma^{-}\coloneqq S^{-}-(P\cup S(L))
be the moderate subsurface of the bottom end, and let
S^{+}=S
be the one of the top end.

Let
{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
be the subspace of
{\mathcal{ML}}(S^{+})
consisting of the measured laminations
\mu
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

closed leaves of
\mu
have weight strictly smaller than
\pi
;

(2)

\mu
and
e^{-}
together fill up the immoderate surface
S(e^{-})
(identifying
S(e^{-})
with a subsurface of
S^{+}
), i.e. for any nonzero measured lamination
\lambda
on
S(e^{-})
one has
i(\lambda,\mu)+i(\lambda,e^{-})>0
(where in the last inequality one needs to pick a measured lamination representing
e^{-}
).

Consider the map

\mm^{-}\times\bb^{+}\colon{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{-})\times{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})

that associates to a structure
\rho
the pair
(\mm^{-}(\rho),\bb^{+}(\rho))
of the conformal structure at infinity of the moderate surface of the bottom end and the bending lamination of the top side of the convex core.

Then
\mm^{-}\times\bb^{+}
is proper.

Proof.

Consider a sequence of representations
(\rho_{n})_{n}
in
{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})
with convergent “mixed” invariants
(\mm^{-}(\rho_{n}),\bb^{+}(\rho_{n}))_{n}\subset{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{-})\times{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
. By Proposition
4.3
, it has an algebraically convergent subsequence. By Corollary
4.8
, this algebraic limit belongs to
{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Algebraic convergence

The first step of the proof of properness is the following.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(\rho_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of representations in
{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})
such that
(\mm^{-}\times\bb^{+}(\rho_{n}))_{n}
converges in
{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{-})\times{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
. Then
(\rho_{n})_{n}
has an algebraically convergent subsequence.

Proof.

Write
(\mm^{-}\times\bb^{+})(\rho_{n})\eqqcolon(m_{n}^{-},\lambda^{+}_{n})
for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
(m^{-},\lambda^{+})
their limit.

Let us assume that
(\rho_{n})_{n}
has no algebraically convergent subsequence. By the Morgan–Shalen theorem
2.3

[
38
]
combined with Skora’s description of geometric actions on trees
[
49
]
(see 2.2.12 and 2.3.5 in
[
42
]
), up to extracting a subsequence,
(\rho_{n})_{n}
converges to the action of
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(S)
on the real tree
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}
dual to a nonzero measured lamination
\mu\in{\mathcal{ML}}(S)
, i.e. there is a sequence of positive real numbers
\epsilon_{n}\searrow 0
such that
\epsilon_{n}\ell_{\rho_{n}}(\gamma)\to i(\mu,\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
(where
\ell_{\rho_{n}}(\gamma)
denotes the length of a geodesic representative of
\gamma
in
M_{\rho_{n}}\coloneqq{\mathbb{H}}^{3}/\rho_{n}(\Gamma)
, and is zero if
\rho_{n}(\gamma)
is parabolic).

We will show that
\mu
cannot intersect
\Sigma^{-}
nor
e^{-}=P\cup L
nor
\lambda^{+}
, contradicting the fact that
e^{-}\cup\lambda^{+}
must fill
S(e^{-})
. Note that this argument also applies in the case where
\lambda^{+}=0
, as
\lambda^{+}=0
forces
e^{-}=\emptyset
and
\Sigma^{-}=S^{-}
.

Since
\epsilon_{n}\ell_{\rho_{n}}(c)\to i(\mu,c)
for any essential closed curve
c
on
S
one has the implication

(2)

i(\mu,c)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ell_{\rho_{n}}(c)\to\infty.

First, since
P
consists of parabolics in
M_{\rho_{n}}
, we have
\ell_{\rho_{n}}(P)=0
for each
n
, hence
i(\mu,P)=0
by (
2
). Thus, each component of
\mu
is either contained in
\Sigma^{-}
, in
S(L)
or in
P
. Suppose that
\mu
has a component contained in
\Sigma^{-}
, then there is an essential closed curve
c
in
\Sigma^{-}
with
i(\mu,c)>0
, hence
(\ell_{\rho_{n}}(c))_{n}
is unbounded by (
2
). But
(\ell_{m^{-}_{n}}(c))_{n}
is bounded since
(m^{-}_{n})_{n}
is bounded in
{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{-})
. By Ahlfors’ Lemma
[
42
, Lemma 5.1.1]
, this implies that
(\ell_{\rho_{n}}(c))_{n}
is bounded, a contradiction. Therefore,
\mu
has support contained in
S(L)\cup P=S^{-}-\Sigma^{-}
.

In
[
2
]
, Anderson and Lecuire show that no component of
L
can be
realized
in the dual tree
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}
(see Section
2.2
above, or Claim 3.5 and Lemma 3.6 in
[
2
]
). In particular, by Proposition 3.1.1 in
[
42
]
, we obtain that no component of
L

intersects

\mu
(in the sense of §
2.2
, see
[
42
, §3]
). Each component of
L
is arational (i.e. minimal, irrational and whose complementary regions are simply connected or annuli) in its respective component of
S(L)
. Thus, each component of
\mu
which is disjoint from

must have support contained in

6.6.
Fibres of a limit of homeomorphisms

A subset
F\subset N
is a
neighborhood retract
if it has an open neighborhood
\Omega\subset N
such that the inclusion
j\colon F\xhookrightarrow{}\Omega
has a retraction
r\colon\Omega\to F
, i.e.
r\circ j=\id_{F}
. A metrizable space
X
is an
absolute neighborhood retract
(ANR) if, for any closed embedding
X\xhookrightarrow{}Y
into a metrizable space
Y
,
X
is a neighborhood retract in
Y
.

A sequence of maps
(f_{n}\colon X\to Y)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

converges continuously
to
f_{\infty}
if
f_{n}(x_{n})\to f_{\infty}(x)
whenever
x_{n}\to x
. When
Y
is metrizable and
f_{\infty}
is continuous, continuous convergence coincides with
compact convergence
, i.e. uniform convergence on compact subsets of
X

[
44
, p. 98]
.

The following result is a variant of the theorem of Finney
[
23
]
used in
[
22
, Theorem 4.6]
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
N
and
N^{\prime}
be two manifolds of the same dimension. Let
(f_{n}\colon N\to N^{\prime})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of open embeddings converging continuously (equivalently, compactly) to the continuous map
f_{\infty}\colon N\to N^{\prime}
, i.e.
f_{n}(x_{n})\to f_{\infty}(x_{\infty})
whenever
x_{n}\to x_{\infty}
. If a fibre of
f_{\infty}
is compact and is an absolute neighborhood retract, then it is contractible.

Proof.

Let
F\coloneqq f_{\infty}^{-1}(\ast)
be a (nonempty) compact fibre, which is a retract of an open neighborhood
\Omega
, which we can assume to have compact closure. Let
j\colon F\xhookrightarrow{}\Omega
be the inclusion and
r\colon\Omega\to F
a retraction. Assuming that
F
is not contractible, we will show that
f_{\infty}
sends a point of
\partial\Omega
to
\ast
.

Let
U\subset N^{\prime}
be a contractible neighborhood of
\ast
. Since
F
is compact, we have
f_{n}(F)\subset U
for all
n
large enough. Fix such an
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Thus
f_{n}(F)\subset U
. Since
U
is contractible, there is a homotopy
H\colon F\times[0,1]\to U
from
H(-,0)=f_{n}|_{F}\colon F\to U
to a constant map
H(-,1)
. But recall that
f_{n}
is an open embedding. Thus
H(F\times[0,1])
cannot be entirely contained in
f_{n}(\Omega)
, for otherwise
r\circ(f_{n}|_{\Omega})^{-1}\circ H\colon F\times[0,1]\to F
would be a nullhomotopy of
\id_{F}
.

Since
H(F\times[0,1])
is connected (
H
is a nullhomotopy, hence every point in its image is connected by a path to the constant image of
H(-,1)
), it must intersect
f_{n}(\partial\Omega)
(which equals
\partial f_{n}(\Omega)
since
f_{n}
is an open embedding and
\overline{\Omega}
is compact), say at a point
f_{n}(x_{U})
with
x_{U}\in\partial\Omega
. In particular,
f_{n}(x_{U})\in U
.

Considering a neighborhood basis
U_{0}\supset U_{1}\supset\dots
of
\ast
consisting of contractible open sets, the above paragraph gives a sequence
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathbb{N}}
, with
n_{i}\to\infty
, and a sequence of points
(x_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
\partial\Omega
, such that
f_{n_{i}}(x_{i})\in U_{i}
for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

By compactness of
\partial\Omega
, up to extracting a subsequence,
(x_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to some
x_{\infty}\in\partial\Omega
. By continuous convergence, we obtain

f_{\infty}(x_{\infty})=\lim_{i\to\infty}f_{n_{i}}(x_{i})=\ast,

which is impossible since
\partial\Omega
does not meet
F=f_{\infty}^{-1}(\ast)
. This concludes the proof that
F
is contractible.
∎

6.1

for

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

First, using Proposition

with empty parabolic locus and ending lamination, the map

is proper. In particular, its fibres are compact. Let
F
be a fibre of
\mm^{-}\times\bb^{+}

By Lemmas

Since

is the limit of the homeomorphisms

Since

6.8.
Conclusion

We now have all the required ingredients to conclude the proof of the main result, following the strategy presented in Section
1.2
.

Proof of the main theorem.

The map

\mm^{-}\times\bb^{+}\colon{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})\to{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{-})\times{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})

is proper (Proposition
4.1
), continuous (on the first factor, by the simultaneous uniformization theorem, see Section
2.10
; on the second factor, by Proposition
3.1
since the algebraic and strong topologies coincide on
{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})
). Its domain and target are manifolds of the same dimension (by
[

,
{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
is a manifold of the same dimension as
{\mathcal{T}}(S^{+})
).

Moreover, the target is connected:
{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{-})
is connected, and
{\mathcal{ML}}^{\perp e^{-}}_{<\pi}(S^{+})
is the image of the connected space
{\mathcal{AH}}^{+}(S;e^{-})\cong{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma^{-})\times{\mathcal{T}}(S^{+})
under the continuous map
\bb^{+}
(Proposition
3.1
), which is surjective by the realization theorem of Baba–Ohshika
[
3
]
.

Thus, it remains to show that it is injective. With the notation of Section
1.2
, it suffices to show that
\bb^{+}_{\infty}
is injective. But it is the limit of the open embeddings
\bb^{+}_{n}
, when

, and the convergence is continuous since
\BB^{+}
is continuous. By following the proof of Theorem
6.1
and using Theorem

, we obtain that
\bb^{+}_{\infty}
is injective, hence
\bb^{+}
is injective too. By invariance of domain it is an open embedding, and a proper open embedding into a connected space is a homeomorphism.
∎

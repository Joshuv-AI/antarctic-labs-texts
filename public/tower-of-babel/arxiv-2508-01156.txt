Abstract
Heartbeat stars (HBSs) are ideal laboratories for studying the formation and evolution of binary stars in eccentric orbits and their internal tidal interactions. We present 42 new HBSs discovered based on TESS photometric data. Their light curves are modeled by using a corrected version of Kumar et al.’s model or the PHOEBE binary model code. Tidally excited oscillations (TEOs) are detected in ten systems, with most pulsation phases can be explained by the dominant being l = 2 l=2 , m = 0 m=0 , or ± 2 \pm 2 spherical harmonic. For TIC 156846634, the harmonic with large deviation ( > 3 ​ σ >3\sigma ) can be expected to be a traveling wave or nonadiabatic. The n n = 16 harmonic in TIC 184413651 may not be considered as a TEO candidate due to its large deviation ( > 2 ​ σ >2\sigma ) and lower amplitude. Moreover, TIC 92828790 shows no TEOs but exhibits a significant gamma Doradus pulsation. The eccentricity-period (e − - P) relation also shows a positive correlation between eccentricity and period, as well as the existence of orbital circularization. The Hertzsprung-Russell diagram shows that TESS HBSs have higher temperatures and greater luminosities than Kepler HBSs. This significantly enhances the detectability of massive HBSs and those containing TEOs. Currently, the search for HBSs using TESS data has become a research focus, and these intriguing objects may serve as valuable additions to the TESS HBS catalog.

I
Introduction

Heartbeat stars (HBSs) are a subclass of ellipsoidal variables. They are detached binaries with eccentric orbits. The term originates from the characteristic “heartbeat”-like features observed in their light curves, which are analogous to electrocardiogram patterns
(
S. E. Thompson et al. 2012
)
. The components are distorted by the time-varying tidal potential, and their response is usually divided into two parts: the equilibrium tide and the dynamical tide. The equilibrium tide is responsible for the heartbeat signature near periastron, while the dynamical tide induces the tidally excited oscillations (TEOs;
J. P. Zahn 1975
;
P. Kumar et al. 1995
;
J. Fuller 2017
). HBSs are rare and intriguing objects that are ideal laboratories for studying celestial activities such as the theoretical work on TEOs
(
J. Fuller 2017
;
Z. Guo 2021
;
P. A. Kołaczek-Szymański & T.
Różański 2023
)
, pulsation phases and mode of TEOs
(
R. M. O’Leary & J. Burkart 2014
;
Z. Guo et al. 2020
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2024a
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2024b
)
, resonance locking
(
J. Fuller et al. 2017
;
K. Hambleton et al. 2018
;
S. J. Cheng et al. 2020
)
, hybrid pulsation systems
(
K. M. Hambleton et al. 2013
;
Z. Guo et al. 2019
;
K. Wang et al. 2023
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2025
)
, apsidal motion
(
K. Hambleton et al. 2016
;
J.-W. Ou et al. 2021
)
, massive HBSs and exceptional objects
(
P. A. Kołaczek-Szymański
et al. 2022
;
P. A. Kołaczek-Szymański
et al. 2024
;
M. MacLeod & A. Loeb 2023
;
G. Koenigsberger & D. Estrella-Trujillo 2024
)
, stellar evolution
(
P. G. Beck et al. 2014
;
T. Jayasinghe et al. 2021
;
M. MacLeod & A. Loeb 2025
)
, and evolution of eccentric orbits
(
S. E. Thompson et al. 2012
;
A. Shporer et al. 2016
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2023
)
, etc.

It was only with the release of photometric data from long-term baseline and high-precision surveys that HBSs were discovered in large numbers. After the first Kepler HBS KOI-54 (KIC 8112039) was reported by
W. F. Welsh et al. 2011
,
B. Kirk et al. 2016
reported the first catalog of Kepler HBSs with 173 systems.
M. Wrona et al. 2022b
published a catalog of 991 HBSs discovered in the Optical Gravitational Lensing Experiment (OGLE) database.

The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS), launched in 2018, is a space-based, all-sky survey mission designed to detect exoplanets via optical photometry. It monitors the sky with a field of view spanning 24
\times
96 degrees, observing each sector for approximately 27 days. Recent years have witnessed a growing number of HBS discoveries from TESS data.
P. A. Kołaczek-Szymański
et al. 2021
found 20 HBSs from the TESS data.
L. A. Balona 2022
reported a catalog of TESS variable stars, including 96 HBS candidates.
M.-Y. Li et al. 2024a
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2024c
discovered 28 TESS HBSs by visual search of light curves.
S. Solanki et al. 2025
identified 180 TESS HBSs. Recently,
J. Callahan et al. 2025
reported 112 HBSs using GAIA and TESS data. To date, over 400 HBSs have been discovered using TESS data.

The search for HBSs using TESS data is our long-term research objective, and significant progress has been made
(
M.-Y. Li et al. 2024a
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2024c
)
. This work is a systematic compilation of the current findings in the research series. Sect.
II
describes the data reduction and analytic procedure for these objects. Sect.
III
represents the detection of the TEOs and identification of their pulsation phases and modes. Sect.
IV
shows an HBS that exhibits gamma Doradus (
\gamma
Dor) pulsation. Sect.
V
analyzes the e
-

VI

II
Data and Modeling

II.1
Data Reduction

We downloaded the TESS photometric data using the lightkurve package
(
Lightkurve Collaboration et al. 2018
)
. Through visual inspection, we selected samples with smaller data dispersion and more prominent “heartbeat” signals from TESS-SPOC (the TESS Science Processing Operations Center) and QLP (the MIT Quick-Look Pipeline) data for analysis. Columns 9 and 10 in Table
1
represent the selected data source and sector(s) for modeling. We also removed outliers outside of 3
\sigma
(the standard deviation of the total flux) from the average flux, and used the Locally Weighted Scatter-plot Smoothing (LOWESS) approach
(
W. S. Cleveland 1979
)
to detrend the data for samples with large deviations. Additionally, we performed a rough data fitting to generate phase-folded light curves. Then, we conducted a thorough visual inspection and identified the 42 HBS candidates.

II.2
The K95
+
model

We employ the modified
P. Kumar et al. 1995
model (K95
+
model) to fit the non-eclipsing light curves. As presented in Eq. (
1
), this revised model incorporates a sign reversal for the
\omega
term (from negative to positive) compared to the original formulation in Eq. (44) of
P. Kumar et al. 1995
, following the modification by
M. Wrona et al. 2022a
:

\frac{\delta F}{F}(t)=S\cdot\frac{1-3\sin^{2}i\sin^{2}(\varphi(t)+\omega)}{(R(t)/a)^{3}}+C,

(1)

The model employs an approximation of the equilibrium tidal deformation with a sum of all dominant modes, with spherical harmonic degrees
l=2
and orders
m=0,\pm 2

(
P. A. Kołaczek-Szymański
et al. 2021
)
. It is characterized by seven fundamental parameters: the orbital period (
P
), eccentricity (
e
), inclination (
i
), argument of periastron (
\omega
), periastron passage epoch (
T_{0p}
), amplitude scaling factor (
S
), and fractional flux offset (
C
)
(
M.-Y. Li et al. 2023
)
.

We perform light curve fitting using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) technique implemented through the emcee v3.1.6 Python package
(
D. Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
)
, adopting the methodology described in Section 3.2 of
M.-Y. Li et al. 2023
. The MCMC analysis involves two primary iterative rounds. First, we set the priors of the parameters as a uniform distribution of the physically reasonable range, and use 50 walkers and 5000 steps for a single chain to obtain the appropriate parameters for each system. Second, we set the priors of the parameters as a normal distribution centered on the value from the first round, and use 100 walkers and 10,000 steps in a single chain to derive the uncertainties of the parameters. The average autocorrelation times of these systems are approximately 80 consecutive steps. The parameters and their uncertainties of the HBSs are derived and presented in Table

II.3
The PHOEBE model

In our sample, TIC 54320598 displays a single eclipse feature, rendering it incompatible with the K95
+
model. Consequently, we employ the PHysics Of Eclipsing BinariEs (PHOEBE) code
(
A. Prša et al. 2016
;
A. Prša 2018
)
for light curve modeling, adhering to the methodology outlined by
M.-Y. Li et al. 2025
. We first fit the eclipsing parts of the light curves to a parabola to obtain the time of superior conjunction(

During the TEO analysis of these systems, we found that while TIC 92828790 shows no TEOs, it exhibits a significant non-harmonic frequency at
f_{1}
= 1.47288(6) (as shown in panel (b2) of Figure
2
). We also derive the pseudo-synchronous rotation frequency of this system using the following equation
(
P. Hut 1981
)
:

f_{\rm ps\text{-}rot}=\frac{1+\frac{15}{2}e^{2}+\frac{45}{8}e^{4}+\frac{5}{16}e^{6}}{(1+3e^{2}+\frac{3}{8}e^{4}){(1-e^{2})}^{3/2}}f_{\rm orb}.

(5)

We then obtain
f_{\rm ps\text{-}rot}
=0.66101(65), which is significantly different from
f_{1}
. Therefore,
f_{1}
can be ruled out as being caused by rotation. On the other hand, Sect.
V.2
will show that this system is located precisely in the
\gamma
Dor region of the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram. Therefore, the significant frequency
f_{1}
is a
\gamma
Dor pulsation. HBSs hosting
\gamma
Dor and/or
\delta
Sct pulsations are exceptionally rare.
K. M. Hambleton et al. 2013
;
Z. Guo et al. 2019
;
K. Wang et al. 2023
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2025
have reported HBSs with such characteristics. TIC 92828790 will thus serve as a valuable research target in this field.

V.1
The Eccentricity–Period Relation

Figure
3
shows the eccentricity-period (e
-
P) diagram. The red stars represent the 42 TESS HBSs in this work. The orange pluses indicate the TESS HBSs from
P. A. Kołaczek-Szymański

M.-Y. Li et al. 2024c

M.-Y. Li et al. 2023

(
A. Shporer et al. 2016

:
e=\sqrt{1-(P_{0}/P)^{2/3}}
. The range of circularization periods (
P_{0}
) is 2 to 12 days, which differs from the range reported for Kepler HBSs
(
M.-Y. Li et al. 2023
)
. This difference implies selection effects in the orbital period distribution of TESS HBSs.

Furthermore, the e
-
P distribution demonstrates a significant positive correlation for the majority of systems
(
M.-Y. Li et al. 2023
)
. This distribution provides clear evidence of ongoing orbital circularization in HBSs, as previously reported by
S. E. Thompson et al. 2012
;
A. Shporer et al. 2016
. The observed trend reveals that the shorter-period orbits are likely to circularize more quickly since they are undergoing stronger tidal forces; the eccentricity is smaller for shorter-period systems.

There are two systems with relatively long orbital periods: TICs 118196277 and 156846634, with periods of 21.7 and 22.6 days, respectively. For TIC 118196277, two heartbeat signals were detected in sector 13. We derived its orbital period by combining light curve data from sectors 13, 39, and 66. For TIC 156846634, consecutive heartbeat signals were detected in sectors 33 and 34. However, it remains challenging to detect HBSs with longer orbital periods using TESS data because consecutive heartbeat signals must be observed across multiple continuous sectors.

V.3
The reason for only one eclipse

As shown in Fig.
5
, the unique geometry of eccentric orbits determines that only one eclipse occurs under certain conditions. In the top view, the blue star moves to A
1
, while the black star moves to B
1
. At this point, the two stars align with the line of sight. As the blue star moves to A
2
and the black star moves to B
2
, they realign with the line of sight again. The key difference is that the distance between the two stars differs for these two alignments. As shown in the cross-sectional view, when the inclination falls within a certain range of angles (but not excessively large), an eclipse occurs only at positions A
1
and B
1
, but not at A
2
and B
2
. Thus, under these inclination conditions, only one eclipse event is observed. Figure 13 of
S. Solanki et al. 2025
also supports this reasoning by showing the inclination distribution.

Additionally, the value of
\omega
also determines the distance between the two components of the two line-of-sight alignments, thereby influencing the eclipse conditions.
S. Solanki et al. 2025
also noted this point. However, they further suggested that in some cases, the heartbeat signal might eliminate the eclipse signal, resulting in only one observable eclipse. We argue that this scenario is unlikely because the photometric morphologies of the heartbeat and eclipse signals are distinct, making mutual elimination implausible.

VI
Summary and conclusions

Based on TESS data, we discovered 42 new HBSs. We modeled their light curves using the K95
+
or PHOEBE models and obtained their orbital parameters. Ten of the HBSs exhibit TEOs, and their pulsation phases and modes have been identified. Most pulsation phases can be explained by the dominant being
l=2
,
m=0
, or
\pm 2
spherical harmonic. Among these samples, the harmonic with a large deviation in TIC 156846634 can be expected to be a traveling wave or nonadiabatic. The
n
= 16 harmonic in TIC 184413651 should not be considered a TEO candidate due to its large deviation and lower amplitude. Moreover, TIC 92828790 exhibits
\gamma
Dor pulsation and represents a valuable sample for studying
\gamma
Dor pulsations in HBSs.

The e
-
P relation of most samples also shows a positive correlation
(
M.-Y. Li et al. 2023
)
, and the existence of orbital circularization
(
S. E. Thompson et al. 2012
;
A. Shporer et al. 2016
)
. We identified two systems with relatively long orbital periods: TICs 118196277 and 156846634, exhibiting periods of 21.7 and 22.6 days, respectively. Due to observational duration limitations of TESS Sectors, detecting HBSs with even longer orbital periods remains challenging.

In the H-R diagram, TESS HBSs exhibit higher temperatures and greater luminosities compared to Kepler HBSs, which may be attributed to observational effects. This advantage enables TESS data to observe more massive HBSs while also facilitating the detection of TEOs. We also propose a reliable explanation for the absence of a secondary eclipse in some HBSs.

Since
P. A. Kołaczek-Szymański
et al. 2021
reported the first TESS HBSs catalog, the sample size in the TESS HBSs catalog has been steadily increasing
(
L. A. Balona 2022
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2024a
;
M.-Y. Li et al. 2024c
;
S. Solanki et al. 2025
;
J. Callahan et al. 2025
)
. The HBSs discovered in this work may serve as valuable additions to the TESS HBS catalog.

are available as supplementary online material on China-VO at doi:

.

The Three-Body Limit Cycle: Universal Form for General Regulators
Langxuan Chen, Feng Wu, Xincheng Lin, Sebastian König, Ubirajara van Kolck, Pengfei Zhang

Abstract
The Efimov effect, a remarkable realization of discrete scale invariance, emerges in the three-body problem with short-range interactions and is understood as a renormalization group (RG) limit cycle within Short-Range Effective Field Theory (SREFT). While the analytic form of the three-body renormalization relation has been established for a sharp cutoff regulator, its universality for other regulators remains underexplored. In this letter, we derive the universal functional form of the three-body renormalization relation for general separable regulators through a detailed analysis of the Skorniakov-Ter-Martirosian and Faddeev equations. We find that the relation is characterized by three parameters. This universality is verified numerically for various regulators. Although the functional form remains the same, the parameters characterizing the limit cycle exhibit regulator dependence. These findings broaden the class of RG limit cycles in SREFT and offer a more complete understanding of three-body renormalization.



Introduction.

The concept of a limit cycle was
introduced by Wilson as a special solution to the renormalization group (RG) equations in the early development of RG theory
Wilson and Kogut 1974
. A limit cycle in the RG flow implies that physical observables recur periodically under scale transformations, reflecting discrete scale invariance (DSI) rather than continuous scale invariance. A prominent physical realization of an RG limit cycle is found in the three-body problem with short-range interactions. In his seminal work
Efimov 1970
, Efimov showed that three identical bosons
with resonant two-body interactions exhibit an infinite tower of
bound states whose energies form a geometric sequence, a hallmark of DSI. This phenomenon, known as the Efimov effect, was later identified as a concrete manifestation of an RG limit cycle within the framework of Short-Range Effective Field Theory (SREFT)
Bedaque et al. 1999a
;
Bedaque et al. 1999b
. Further developments in this direction have deepened our understanding of the Efimov effect and extended the implications of DSI to more complex few- and many-body systems
Braaten and Hammer 2006
;
van Kolck 2017
, which has profound implications for systems ranging from atomic, nuclear, condensed matter, and particle
physics
Nielsen et al. 2001
;
Braaten and Hammer 2007
;
Hammer and Platter 2010
;
Nishida et al. 2013
;
Naidon and Endo 2017
;
Kievsky et al. 2021
.

SREFT captures the most general short-range dynamics allowed by assumed spacetime symmetries. Its leading order (LO) Lagrangian density reads

\mathcal{L}=\psi^{\dagger}\left(i\partial_{0}+\frac{\vec{\nabla}^{2}}{2m}\right)\psi-\frac{C_{0}}{2}\left(\psi^{\dagger}\psi\right)^{2}-\frac{D_{0}}{6}\left(\psi^{\dagger}\psi\right)^{3},\;\;

(1)

where
\psi
is a spinless particle field,
m
is the particle mass, and
C_{0}(\Lambda)
and
D_{0}(\Lambda)
are the two- and three-body low-energy constants (LECs), respectively. Their running with the regulator scale
\Lambda
, known as the renormalization relation, is determined by two physical observables: the two-body scattering length and the three-body parameter. In the unitarity limit, where the two-body scattering length diverges, there is no dimensionful parameter in the two-body system at LO and
C_{0}(\Lambda)
has a universal dependence on
\Lambda
,
mC_{0}(\Lambda)\propto\Lambda^{-1}
, corresponding to a nontrivial RG fixed point
Weinberg 1991
. Denoting the dimensionful three-body parameter by
\Lambda_{\ast}
, dimensional analysis requires
mD_{0}(\Lambda_{\ast},\Lambda)\propto\Lambda^{-4}
, with the dimensionless ratio

H_{0}(\Lambda/\Lambda_{\ast})\equiv\frac{\Lambda^{2}D_{0}(\Lambda_{\ast},\Lambda)}{6mC_{0}^{2}(\Lambda)}\equiv-\Lambda^{2}h(\Lambda_{\ast},\Lambda)\;,

(2)

a function of
\Lambda/\Lambda_{\ast}
.

The expression for
H_{0}(\Lambda/\Lambda_{\ast})
can depend on the specific choice of regulator. With certain local regulators, it can even be multi-valued, corresponding to multiple branches of the limit cycle
Beane et al. 2001
;
Bawin and Coon 2003
;
Kirscher et al. 2015
. In this work, we restrict our analysis to separable (and thus nonlocal) regulators, which yield a unique and well-defined branch. It has been shown that for a sharp momentum cutoff
H_{0}
has the analytical form
Bedaque et al. 1999a
;
Bedaque et al. 1999b
;
Braaten et al. 2011
;
Chen and Zhang 2025

H_{0}(\Lambda/\Lambda_{\ast})=h_{0}\frac{\sin(s_0 \ln(\Lambda/\Lambda_\ast) - \delta_0 )}{\sin(s_0 \ln(\Lambda/\Lambda_\ast) + \delta_0 )}\;,

(3)

where
s_{0}\simeq
1.00624 is a universal constant characteristic of the limit cycle, and
h_{0}
and
\delta_{0}
are pure numbers. DSI implies a geometric tower of bound states with binding momenta
\kappa_{\ast}\exp({-}l\pi/s_0)
, where
l
is an integer and
\kappa_{\ast}\equiv\sqrt{mB_{3}}
corresponds to a reference state (
l=0
) with binding energy
B_{3}
. Again by dimensional analysis, the latter is related to
\Lambda_{\ast}
by a pure number

b_{0}\equiv\Lambda_{\ast}/\kappa_{\ast}.

(4)

The phase
\delta_{0}=\arctan(s_0^{-1})

Bedaque et al. 1999a
;
Bedaque et al. 1999b

and expressions for both
h_{0}
and
b_{0}
, for the special case of a sharp cutoff regulator,
have been obtained recently in terms of certain integrals
Chen and Zhang 2025
.
The renormalization relation for other regulators has been less explored.
The form (
3
), with the same fixed phase
\delta_{0}=\arctan(s_0^{{-}1})
, has been used as a fitting formula for a separable Gaussian regulator
Platter et al. 2005
;
Platter and Hammer 2006
.

STM with General Regulator.

We begin
with the theoretical analysis of the three-body problem in SREFT for general separable regulators.
This analysis can be simplified by introducing an auxiliary dimer field
d
, which allows the Lagrangian density to be reformulated as
Bedaque et al. 1999a
;
Braaten and Hammer 2006

\mathcal{L}=\psi^{\dagger}\left(i\partial_{0}+\frac{\mathbf{\nabla}^{2}}{2m}\right)\psi+\frac{d^{\dagger}d}{2mC_{0}}\\
-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m}}\left(d^{\dagger}\psi\psi+d\psi^{\dagger}\psi^{\dagger}\right)+hd^{\dagger}d\psi^{\dagger}\psi+\ldots\;,

(5)

with

defined in Eq. (

The three-body problem reduces to the scattering between a boson
\psi
and a dimer
d
. This requires
the renormalized propagator for a dimer of energy
E
and momentum
\mathbf{k}
,
D_{r}(E,\mathbf{k})=[(2mC_{0})^{-1}-\Sigma(E,\mathbf{k})]^{{-}1}
. The self-energy

\Sigma(E,\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{2m}\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g_{2}^{2}(q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}{E_{+}-\epsilon_{\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k}/2}-\epsilon_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{k}/2}}\;,

(6)

where
E_{+}=E+i0^{+}
and
\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=k^{2}/2m
, receives contributions only from the diagram shown in Fig.
1
(a). Here,
g_{2}(x)
is the two-body regulator that ensures the convergence of the above integral: a function satisfying the asymptotic behavior
g_{2}(x)=1
for
x\rightarrow 0
and
g_{2}(x)=0
for
x\rightarrow\infty
. By tuning
C_{0}=(2m\Sigma(0,\mathbf{0}))^{-1}
, the system reaches the two-body unitarity limit, and the propagator becomes

D_{r}(E,\mathbf{k})^{{-}1}={-}\frac{1}{8\pi}\sqrt{{-}mE_{r}}\,\chi({-}mE_{r}/\Lambda^{2}),

(7)

where
E_{r}=E_{+}-k^{2}/4m
is the energy in the center-of-mass frame. The regulator-dependent function
\chi(x)
serves as a high-energy correction to the dimer propagator: when the relevant momentum scale is much smaller than the cutoff
\Lambda
(
x\ll 1
),
\chi(x)
approaches 1, and the corrections are suppressed by inverse powers of
\Lambda
, which can be dropped at LO. One then recovers the expected low-energy
T
matrix for two-body scattering.

The relevant diagrams for the three-body problem are shown in Fig.
1
(b). We focus on the bound-state sector with total energy
E<0
and zero total momentum. In this case, the
s
-wave bound-state wave function
\phi(k)
satisfies the STM equation with general separable regulators,

\displaystyle k\phi(k)=

\displaystyle-\!\int\!\!\frac{dq}{2\pi^{2}}\left(G_{r}(k,q;E)-\frac{kq}{\Lambda^{2}}\,H_{0}(\Lambda)\right.

(8)

\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}\times g_{3}\!\left(\frac{k^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)g_{3}\!\left(\frac{q^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\right)D_{r}(E-\epsilon_{\mathbf{q}},-\mathbf{q})\,q\phi(q)\;,

where

G_{r}(k,q;E)\equiv kq\int\!\frac{d\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}}{4\pi}\frac{g_{2}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2|^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)g_{2}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{k}/2|^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)}{m(\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\epsilon_{\mathbf{q}}+\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-E)}

(9)

involves an average over the momentum-space angles. Here,
g_{3}(x)
is a separable three-body regulator that shares the same asymptotic behavior as
g_{2}(x)
, but can take a different functional form.

We focus on shallow bound states with
{-}mE\ll\Lambda^{2}
. In the intermediate regime with
\sqrt{{-}mE}\ll k\ll\Lambda
, the solution of Eq. (
8
) can be analyzed by taking
E\rightarrow 0
and
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
. This corresponds to setting
\chi(x)=g_{2}(x)=g_{3}(x)=1
and
h=0
, where the equation becomes scale invariant and the solution
\phi(k)\sim k^{{-}1}\cos\left(s_{0}\ln(k/\tilde{\Lambda}_\ast)\right)
exhibits DSI. The parameter
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast}\equiv\exp(\varphi_0/s_0)\Lambda_{\ast}
depends on the regularization scheme. An integral expression for
\varphi_{0}
with a sharp regulator can be found in
Ref.
Chen and Zhang 2025
.

Renormalization.

We are now ready to derive the universal functional form (
3
) for generic regulators
g_{2}(x)
and
g_{3}(x)
, where
\Lambda_{\ast}
,
\delta_{0}
, and
h_{0}
are treated as fit parameters. Focusing on the limit
E\rightarrow 0
and introducing
k\equiv\Lambda\exp({-}t)
,
q\equiv\Lambda\exp(-s)
,
\xi(t)\equiv k\phi(k)
,
\tilde{g}_{3}(t)\equiv\exp(-t)\,g_{3}(\exp(-2t))
,

\tilde{\chi}_{r}(t)\equiv\chi(3\exp(-2t)/4)
, and
\lambda\equiv\sqrt{3}\pi/8
,
the STM equation can be recast as

\int_{{-}\infty}^{\infty}\!ds\Big(G_{r}(t,s)-H_{0}\,\tilde{g}_{3}(t)\,\tilde{g}_{3}(s)\Big)\tilde{\chi}^{{-}1}_{r}(s)\xi(s)=\lambda\xi(t)\;.

(10)

The function
G_{r}(t,s)=G_{r}(k,q;0)
approaches

G(t-s)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\cosh(t-s)+1/2}{\cosh(t-s)-1/2}\right),

(11)

if both
s
and
t
are large and positive. If either
t\ll 0
or
s\ll 0
,
G_{r}(t,s)
goes to 0.

To eliminate
\tilde{\chi}_{r}(t)
in Eq. (
10
), we make the redefinitions

\displaystyle B(t,s)\equiv G_{r}(t,s)/\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}_{r}(t)\tilde{\chi}_{r}(s)}\;,

(12)

\displaystyle\psi(t)\equiv\xi(t)/\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}_{r}(t)},\quad v(t)\equiv\tilde{g}_{3}(t)/\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}_{r}(t)}\;,

which allow us to rewrite the equation as

\int_{{-}\infty}^{\infty}\!ds\left[B(t,s)-H_{0}v(t)v(s)\right]\psi(s)=\lambda\psi(t)\;.

(13)

Because the asymptotic properties of
B(t,s)
are the same as those of
G_{r}(t,s)
, the asymptotic behavior of
\psi(t)
is the same as that for a sharp cutoff regulator,

\psi(t)\sim\cos(s_0 t+\tilde{\varphi})\;,

(14)

with
\tilde{\varphi}=s_{0}\ln[\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast}/\Lambda]
. Determining the running of
H_{0}
is equivalent to analyzing how the phase of the solution
\psi(k)
depends on
H_{0}
in Eq. (
13
).

Since Eq. (
13
) is homogeneous, the overall normalization of
\psi
can be chosen freely.
With

\int_{{-}\infty}^{\infty}\!ds\,v(s)\psi(s)=1\;,

(15)

one obtains the inhomogeneous equation

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!ds\left[B(t,s)-\lambda\delta(s-t)\right]\psi(s)=H_{0}v(t)\;.

(16)

We emphasize that the removal of
\psi
from the second term in Eq. (
13
) is of vital importance,
and only possible when the three-body regulator is separable. Denoting by
\psi_{0}(s)
and
\psi_{1}(s)
, respectively, the solutions of Eq. (
16
) for
H_{0}=0,1
that satisfy Eq. (
15
), the general solution can be written as

\psi(t)=(1-H_{0})\psi_{0}(t)+H_{0}\psi_{1}(t)\;.

(17)

This solution depends linearly on
H_{0}
.

In the low-energy regime
t\gg 1
, both
\psi_{0}(t)
and
\psi_{1}(t)
are expected to exhibit periodic behavior with frequency
s_{0}
:
\psi_{0}(t)\sim\text{Re}\!\left[A_{0}e^{is_{0}t}\right]
and
\psi_{1}(t)\sim\text{Re}\!\left[(A_{0}+A_{1})e^{is_{0}t}\right]
, where
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
are complex numbers. Matching with Eq. (
14
) gives

\tan\tilde{\varphi}=\tan\left(\arg(A_{0}+H_{0}A_{1})\right)=\frac{\text{Im}(A_{0})+\text{Im}(A_{1})H_{0}}{\text{Re}(A_{0})+\text{Re}(A_{1})H_{0}}\;.

(18)

The mapping from
H_{0}
to
\tan\tilde{\varphi}
is therefore a real Möbius transformation, which involves three independent parameters.
In particular,
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
are determined only up to a common overall rescaling. Conversely,
H_{0}
can be expressed as a real Möbius transformation of
\tan\tilde{\varphi}
. An arbitrary parametrization of this relation may be employed. Choosing
\arg(A_{0}A_{1})=2\varphi_{0}
and setting
\text{Re}(\exp({-}i\varphi_0)A_{1})=1
, we can write

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle={-}h_{0}\left(1-i\tan\delta_{0}\right)e^{i\varphi_{0}}\;,

(19)

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(1+i\tan\delta_{0}\right)e^{i\varphi_{0}}\;,

where
\delta_{0}
and
h_{0}
are real and can be determined numerically.
From Eq. (
18
) we obtain

H_{0}=h_{0}\frac{\tan(\varphi_0-\tilde\varphi)-\tan\delta_{0}}{\tan(\varphi_0-\tilde\varphi)+\tan\delta_{0}}\;,

(20)

which is equivalent to Eq. (
3
).

The Faddeev Formalism.

An alternative description of the three-body problem is provided by the Faddeev equation. We show that the general form (
3
) also holds for this formulation with separable two- and three-body potentials,
described in detail in Ref.
König 2020
.
In terms of Jacobi momenta
\mathbf{u}_{1}=\left(\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2}\right)/2
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}=2\left[\mathbf{p}_{3}-\left(\mathbf{p}_{1}+\mathbf{p}_{2}\right)/2\right]/3
, where
\mathbf{p}_{i}

is the momentum of the
i
th particle, the two-body potential for
the first pair reads

\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}^{\prime}|V_{2}|\mathbf{u_{1}}}=C_{0}\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}^{\prime}|g_{2}}\braket{g_{2}|\mathbf{u}_{1}}

(21)

with
\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}|g_{2}}=g_{2}(u_{1}^{2}/\Lambda^{2})
. Similarly, the three-body potential is

\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime}|V_{3}|\mathbf{u}_{1}\mathbf{u}_{2}}=D_{0}\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime}|\zeta}\braket{\zeta|\mathbf{u}_{1}\mathbf{u}_{2}}

with

\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}\mathbf{u}_{2}|\zeta}\equiv\zeta(u_{1},u_{2})=\zeta\!\left((u_{1}^{2}+\tfrac{3}{4}u_{2}^{2})/\Lambda^{2}\right)
.
With
P=P_{12}P_{23}+P_{13}P_{23}
generating cyclic and anti-cyclic permutations, the Faddeev equation for one of three equivalent Faddeev amplitudes
\ket{\uppsi}
can be written as

\ket{\uppsi}=G_{0}tP\ket{\uppsi}+3G_{0}tG_{0}t_{3}\ket{\uppsi}\,.

(22)

Here, the free three-body Green’s function
G_{0}
, and the
two- and three-body
T
matrices
t
and
t_{3}
implicitly depend on the energy
E
. Solving the Lippmann-Schwinger equation for
the potential (
21
) with
C_{0}
tuned to unitarity gives

t(z)=\ket{g_{2}}\tau(z)\bra{g_{2}}
,
where
\tau(z)={D_{r}(z,\mathbf{0})}/{2m}
with
D_{r}(z,\mathbf{0})
in Eq. (
7
).
Solving the Lippmann-Schwinger-like equation
t_{3}=V_{3}+V_{3}G_{0}t_{3}
for the separable three-body potential
we use,

t_{3}(E)=\ket{\zeta}\tau_{3}(E)\bra{\zeta}
, in which
\tau_{3}(E)={-}H_{0}^{\prime}(E,\Lambda)/I_{2}^{\zeta}(E)
with

I_{2}^{\zeta}(E)=\braket{\zeta|G_{0}(E)|\zeta}
and

H_{0}^{\prime}(E,\Lambda)=\frac{H_{0}(\Lambda)}{H_{0}(\Lambda)-\Lambda^{2}/6mC_{0}^{2}I_{2}^{\zeta}(E)}\;.

(23)

Since the Faddeev equations are closed when the interaction is active only in certain partial waves, we only need the total
s
-wave contribution for the three-boson system (
l_{1}=l_{2}=0
for the orbital angular momenta
l_{1,2}
associated with
{\mathbf{u}}_{1,2}
). Following Ref.
Platter 2005
, we define a reduced Faddeev component
F(u_{2})
via

\braket{u_{1}u_{2}|\uppsi}=g_{2}(u_{1}^{2}/\Lambda^{2})\,G_{0}(E;u_{1},u_{2})\,\tau\!\left(E-\tfrac{3}{4}u_{2}^{2}\right)F(u_{2})\;,

(24)

which obeys (see Supplemental Material)

\displaystyle u_{2}F(u_{2})={-}\!\int\!\!\frac{du_{2}^{\prime}}{2\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\left(G_{r}(u_{2},u_{2}^{\prime},E)-\frac{u_{2}u_{2}^{\prime}}{\Lambda^{2}}\,H_{0}^{\prime}(E,\Lambda)\,g_{3}^{\prime}\!\left(E,u_{2}\right)g_{3}^{\prime}\!\left(E,u_{2}^{\prime}\right)\right)D_{r}(E-\epsilon_{\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime}},-\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime})\,u_{2}^{\prime}F(u_{2}^{\prime})\;,

(25)

where

g_{3}^{\prime}(E,u_{2})={-}\sqrt{3}\Lambda I_{0}^{\zeta}(E,u_{2})/{\sqrt{-2mI_{2}^{\zeta}(E)}}

(26)

with
I_{2}^{\zeta}(E)
as above and

I_{0}^{\zeta}(E,u_{2})=\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}u_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,g_{2}\!\left(\frac{u_{1}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)G_{0}(E;u_{1},u_{2})\,\zeta(u_{1},u_{2})\;.

(27)

Equation (
25
) shows that
F(u_{2})
corresponds to
\phi(k)
in the absence of a three-body force. It has the same structure of the STM equation (
8
),
only
g_{3}^{\prime}
and
H_{0}^{\prime}
are energy dependent. The derivation based on the STM equation, which takes

, can be adapted to Eq. (

) with only minor modifications. This leads to a similar asymptotic behavior and a linear dependence of

on

, which is related to

through another real Möbius transformation. Eq. (

Conclusion.

We have established the universal functional form of the three-body renormalization relation in SREFT for a broad class of separable regulators. By analyzing the STM equation in the low-energy limit, we demonstrated that the running of the three-body LEC
H_{0}(\Lambda/\Lambda_{\ast})
universally follows a real Möbius transformation of
\tan\left(s_{0}\ln(\Lambda/\Lambda_\ast)\right)
, generalizing the analytic expression previously known only for sharp cutoffs. We also showed that the same form applies to the Faddeev equation with separable regulators. Our numerical results support our analysis by validating the universal form across different regulators. Importantly, while the functional form remains invariant, the regulator dependence of the parameters
\{h_{0},\delta_{0},b_{0}\}
reveals a richer structure in the RG limit cycles than previously recognized, offering a more complete understanding of three-body renormalization.

The universal form (
3
) is valid in the unitarity limit, where DSI is exact. When DSI is weakly broken by a finite scattering length
a_{0}
,
C_{0}(\Lambda)
receives additional contributions proportional to powers of
(a_{0}\Lambda)^{-1}
. Numerical results show that the ratios between adjacent poles and zeros of
H_{0}
gradually approach the universal value
\exp(\pi/s_0)\approx 22.69
as either the cutoff or the scattering length increases, implying that the universal form we derived holds up to corrections that are suppressed by inverse powers of
a_{0}\Lambda
. We defer a detailed analysis to future studies.

Appendix A
Derivation for the Faddeev formalism

The Faddeev equation can be rewritten into a form that is structurally similar to the STM equation.
Starting from Eq. (
22
) in the main text, we obtain

\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}\mathbf{u}_{2}|\uppsi}=G_{0}(E;u_{1},u_{2})\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}\mathbf{u}_{2}|tP|\uppsi}+3G_{0}(E;u_{1},u_{2})\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}\mathbf{u}_{2}|tG_{0}t_{3}|\uppsi}\;.

(31)

Inserting the separable forms of the
T
matrices
t(z)=\ket{g_{2}}\tau(z)\bra{g_{2}}
and
t_{3}(E)=\ket{\zeta}\tau_{3}(E)\bra{\zeta}
, we get

\uppsi(u_{1},u_{2})=2G_{0}(E;u_{1},u_{2})\,g_{2}\!\left(\frac{u_{1}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\tau\!\left(E-\frac{3u_{2}^{2}}{4m}\right)\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}u_{2}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,g_{2}\!\left(\frac{|\bm{\pi}(\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime})|^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\uppsi\!\left(\pi(\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}_{2}),u_{2}^{\prime}\right)\\
\hbox{}+3\,G_{0}(E;u_{1},u_{2})g_{2}\!\left(\frac{u_{1}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\tau\!\left(E-\frac{3u_{2}^{2}}{4m}\right)I_{0}^{\zeta}(E,u_{2})\,\tau_{3}(E)\braket{\zeta|\uppsi}\;,

(32)

in which
\uppsi(u_{1},u_{2})=\braket{\mathbf{u}_{1}\mathbf{u}_{2}|\uppsi}
since there is no angular dependence for the

s
wave (
l_{1}=l_{2}=0
for the orbital angular momenta
l_{1,2}
associated with the Jacobi momenta
\mathbf{u}_{1,2}
), and

\bm{\pi}(\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime})=\mathbf{u}_{2}/2+\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime}\;.

(33)

I_{0}^{\zeta}(E,u_{2})
was defined in Eq. (
27
) in the main text, and
\braket{\zeta|\uppsi}
is a number given by another integral. The reduced Faddeev component
F(u_{2})
defined in Eq. (
24
) in the main text therefore satisfies

F(u_{2})=2\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}u_{2}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,g_{2}\!\left(\frac{|\bm{\pi}(\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime})|^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)g_{2}\!\left(\frac{|\bm{\pi}(\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}_{2})|^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)G_{0}\!\left(E;\mathbf{\pi}(\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}_{2}\right),\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime})\,\tau\!\left(E-\frac{3u_{2}^{\prime 2}}{4m}\right)F(u_{2}^{\prime})\\
\hbox{}+3I_{0}^{\zeta}(E,u_{2})\tau_{3}(E)\,\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}u_{2}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,I_{0}^{\zeta}(E,u_{2}^{\prime})\,\tau\!\left(E-\frac{3u_{2}^{\prime 2}}{4m}\right)F(u_{2}^{\prime})\;.

(34)

With
\tau(z)={D_{r}(z,\mathbf{0})}/{2m}
and
\tau_{3}(E)={-}H_{0}^{\prime}(E,\Lambda)/I_{2}^{\zeta}(E)
, this can be brought into the form of Eqs. (
25
) and (
26
) in the main text in a few additional steps.

Using the fact that
(1-G_{0}V_{3})^{{-}1}=1+G_{0}t_{3}
and the total symmetry of
G_{0}
and
V_{3}
under particle exchange, the Faddeev equation (
22
) in the main text can be transformed into the equivalent form

\ket{\tilde{\uppsi}}=G_{0}tP\ket{\tilde{\uppsi}}+(G_{0}+G_{0}tG_{0})V_{3}\ket{\tilde{\uppsi}}\,,

(35)

where
\ket{\tilde{\uppsi}}
is one of three equivalent two-body Faddeev components,
related to the Faddeev component
\ket{\uppsi}
in the main text via

\ket{\uppsi}=(1-G_{0}V_{3})\ket{\tilde{\uppsi}}\;.

(36)

For a derivation of this form directly from the Schrödinger equation, see Ref.
Stadler et al. 1991
.
For the numerical solution of the Faddeev equation, we use Eq. (
35
) instead of Eq. (
22
) in the main text.

From Eq. (
36
) one obtains

\braket{\zeta|\tilde{\uppsi}}=\frac{\braket{\zeta|\uppsi}}{1-D_{0}I_{2}^{\zeta}(E)}\;.

(37)

Instead of Eq. (
22
) in the main text, one could also start from
Eq. (
35
) and use the relation in Eq. (
36
) to get

\uppsi(u_{1},u_{2})=2G_{0}(E;u_{1},u_{2})\,g_{2}\!\left(\frac{u_{1}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\tau\!\left(E-\frac{3u_{2}^{2}}{4m}\right)\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}u_{2}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,g_{2}\!\left(\frac{|\bm{\pi}(\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime})|^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\uppsi\!\left(\pi(\mathbf{u}_{2}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}_{2}),u_{2}^{\prime}\right)\\
\hbox{}+3\,G_{0}(E;u_{1},u_{2})g_{2}\!\left(\frac{u_{1}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\tau\!\left(E-\frac{3u_{2}^{2}}{4m}\right)I_{0}^{\zeta}(E,u_{2})D_{0}\braket{\zeta|\tilde{\uppsi}}\;.

(38)

With Eq. (
37
), the above equation is the same as Eq. (
32
). The derivation can then proceed as before, with
F(u_{2})
defined in Eq. (
24
) of the main text.

For the two-body
T
matrix to attain the unitarity-limit form, the two-body LEC must be given by

C_{0}(\Lambda)=\frac{1}{2m\Sigma(0,\mathbf{0})}={-}\frac{4\pi}{m\theta_{1}\Lambda}\;,

(39)

where
\theta_{1}
is a regulator-dependent number. For example, for the regulators (
28
) employed in the numerical calculation,

\theta_{1}=\left(2^{1/2n}n\pi\right)^{-1}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{2n}\right)\;.

(40)

In the special case where the three-body regulator factorizes as a function of the momenta,
\ket{\zeta}=\ket{g_{2}}\ket{g_{3}}
in abstract notation, we have

g_{3}^{\prime}(E,u_{2})={-}\frac{\sqrt{6m}\Lambda\Sigma(E-3u_{2}^{2}/4m,\mathbf{0})}{\sqrt{{-}I_{2}^{\zeta}(E)}}\,g_{3}\!\left(\frac{u_{2}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\;,

(41)

in which
g_{3}^{\prime}(E,u_{2})
was defined in Eqs. (
26
) in the main text.
This scenario is realized for a simple Gaussian regulator, Eq. (
28
) with
n=1
, but not for the super-Gaussian (
n\geq 2
) regulators.

Appendix B
Approximating parameters for different regulators in the STM equation

The three-body force is constructed such that the wave function (or equivalently the scattering amplitude if including an inhomogeneous term) converges at large cutoffs

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{\partial\phi(k)}{\partial\ln\Lambda}\to 0\;,

(42)

Applying the limits and derivatives to the STM equation
(
8
) and formally solving for
h(\Lambda)
gives

h(\Lambda)\simeq\frac{\int d(\ln\Lambda)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\ln\Lambda}\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}g_{2}(|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2|^{2}/\Lambda^{2})g_{2}(|\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{k}/2|^{2}/\Lambda^{2})G_{0}D_{r}\phi(q)\right)_{k\ll\Lambda}}{\int d(\ln\!\Lambda)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\ln\Lambda}\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}g_{3}(k^{2}/\Lambda^{2})g_{3}(q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})D_{r}\phi(q)\right)_{k\ll\Lambda}},

(43)

where
\int\!d(\ln\Lambda)
is the indefinite integral over
\ln\Lambda
without the constant, which is eliminated by the boundary condition
\phi(k)\to 0
at large
k
. For regulators whose derivatives with respect to
\Lambda
vanishes at
q\ll\Lambda
, the integral over
q
is dominated by
q\sim\Lambda
. To explicitly evaluate the integrals, we need to choose the form of the regulators and also take the asymptotic expansion of the integrand at large
q
. For
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
we consider (super-)Gaussian regulators. The following asymptotic expressions at
q\sim\Lambda\gg k
will be used:

\displaystyle g_{2}\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}/2|^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)g_{2}\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{k}/2|^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\exp\left[-\left(1+2^{-2n}\right)\left(\frac{q}{\Lambda}\right)^{2n}\right]\;,

(44)

\displaystyle g_{3}\!\left(\frac{k^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)g_{3}\!\left(\frac{q^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\exp\left[-\left(\frac{q}{\Lambda}\right)^{2n}\right]\;,

(45)

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{1}{q^{2}}\;,

(46)

\displaystyle D_{r}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{q}\;,

(47)

where the prefactor of
D_{r}
is dropped as we are only interested in the ratio in Eq. (
43
). We follow Ref.
Bedaque et al. 1999a
and take

\phi(q)\sim\text{Re}\!\left(\left(\frac{q}{\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast}}\right)^{is_{0}-1}\right)\;,

(48)

as an approximation of
\phi(q)
at
q\sim\Lambda
, although in principle this works only for
q\ll\Lambda
.

Equation (
43
) now becomes

\displaystyle h(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{\real\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\exp[-a_{n}(q/\Lambda)^{2n}]q^{-3}(q/\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast})^{-1+is_{0}}}{\real\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\exp[-(q/\Lambda)^{2n}]q^{-1}(q/\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast})^{-1+is_{0}}}

(49)

\displaystyle=-\frac{a_{n}^{1/2n}}{\Lambda^{2}}\left[\real\left(\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast}}\right)^{is_{0}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{is_{0}+1}{2n}\right)\right)\right]^{-1}\real\left(a_{n}^{-is_{0}/2n}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast}}\right)^{{is_{0}}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{is_{0}-1}{2n}\right)\right)\;,

where
a_{n}\equiv(1+2^{-2n})
and we used the integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dxe^{-ax^{2n}}x^{b}=\frac{1}{2n}a^{-\frac{b+1}{2n}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{b+1}{2n}\right)\;.

(50)

Under this approximation,
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast}
is related to
\Lambda_{\ast}
in Eq. (
3
) by

\varphi_{0}=s_{0}\ln\tilde{\Lambda}_{\ast}/\Lambda_{\ast}\simeq\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta_{0}+\arg\!\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{is_{0}+1}{2n}\right)\right)\;.

(51)

The parameters
\delta_{0}
and
h_{0}
can be extracted from
Eq. (
49
) as Eqs. (
29
)
and (
30
) in the main text. Using the Laurent series for the Gamma function at large
n
, we recover the approximate values for a sharp cutoff in
Ref.
Bedaque et al. 1999a
:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{0}

\displaystyle\simeq\arctan(s_0^{-1})\;,

(52)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{0}

\displaystyle\simeq 1\;,

(53)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{0}

\displaystyle\simeq 0\;.

(54)

As
n
increases, the approximate phase
\delta_{0}
converges to the exact sharp-cutoff value, while
\varphi_{0}
and
h_{0}
slightly deviate from their exact values, 0.05281 and 0.879
Chen and Zhang 2025
, for a sharp cutoff regulator.

Numerical results for
\delta_{0}
,
h_{0}
, and
b_{0}
, obtained by solving the STM equation (
8
) of the main text, are shown as functions of
n
in Fig.
3
. These results are compared with approximate expressions for
\delta_{0}
and
h_{0}
, as well as with the exact sharp-cutoff values from Ref.
Chen and Zhang 2025
. The approximate values capture the trend of the numerical results for small
n
, starting about 15% off and improving progressively. As
n
increases, the numerical results for
\delta_{0}
,
h_{0}
, and
b_{0}
converge to values different from that
obtained by neglecting the regulators in Eqs. (
8
) and (
9

q
in Eq. (
8
) at
\Lambda
. In contrast, the numerical results are obtained with

\mathbf{q}
).
The two procedures thus differ in the way they account for momenta comparable to
\Lambda
. While after renormalization observables are insensitive to these details, the running of LECs is not.
It is, therefore, not surprising that
the numerical results for super-Gaussian regulators do not converge to the
sharp-cutoff result
in the large-

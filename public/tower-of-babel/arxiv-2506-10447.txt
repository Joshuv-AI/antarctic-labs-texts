Stability analysis of the free-surface Stokes problem and an unconditionally stable explicit scheme
Igor Tominec ∗,1,2, Lukas Lundgren 1,2,3, André Löfgren 1,2, Josefin Ahlkrona 1,2,3

Abstract
Accurate simulations of ice sheet dynamics, mantle convection, lava flow , and other highly viscous free-surface flows involve solving the coupled Stokes/free-surface equations . In this paper, we theoretically analyze the stability and conservation properties of the weak form of this system for Newtonian fluids and non-Newtonian fluids, at both the continuous and discrete levels. We perform the fully discrete stability analysis for finite element methods used in space with explicit and implicit Euler time-stepping methods used in time. Motivated by the theory, we propose a stabilization term designed for the explicit Euler discretization, which ensures unconditional time stability and permits conservation of the domain volume. Numerical experiments validate and support our theoretical findings.

Introduction

Free-surface flows of Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids in the low Reynolds number regime are commonly modelled by the Stokes equations coupled with a free-surface evolution equation,
resulting in a moving domain problem.
Real-world applications include ice sheet dynamics
[
14
]
, mantle convection
[
18
]
,

lava flow
[
15
]
,
interactions between viscous drops
[
23
]
, and others.
These applications demand numerically robust simulations that remain stable across a range of mesh resolutions and time step sizes.
However, standard discretizations of the coupled Stokes/free-surface problem may suffer from instabilities that
constrain the simulations to small time step sizes, which then induce large computational costs.
In this paper, we rigorously analyze the stability of the coupled Stokes/free-surface system and, motivated by our theoretical results, propose new numerical stabilization approaches.
These enable stable and efficient simulations of free-surface flows, even in the presence of complex geometries and source terms.

Related work.
Finite element methods in space combined with
explicit, semi-implicit, and implicit Euler discretizations in time, are a common choice for numerically solving the coupled Stokes/free-surface problem.
The implicit Euler method allows for the largest time step sizes,
but is computationally expensive as it requires nonlinear iterations coupling the Stokes/free-surface problem.
On the other hand, explicit Euler and semi-implicit Euler discretizations in time
[
12
,
9
,
31
,
18
]
do not require additional nonlinear iterations.
However, these approaches lead to severe restrictions on the maximum stable time step size.
Free-Surface Stabilization Algorithm (FSSA)
introduced in
[
18
]
is an approach for improving the maximum time step size of the semi-implicit Euler discretization in time.

The
FSSA adds an
unsymmetric
surface integral term to the weak form Stokes problem to bring the full discretization closer to an implicit method.
The approach was first introduced in the context of the mantle convection applications
[
18
]
and was later extended to ice sheet dynamics applications in
[
20
,
22
,
28
]
.
While the authors report significant time step size improvements, the time step restriction is
not a priori known and the overall theoretical understanding of FSSA is still missing.

In
[
4
]
, the FSSA stabilization terms were modified to preserve the symmetry of the linear systems.

In
[
26
]
, the authors
give a procedure for computing the largest stable time-step for the Stokes/free-surface system
simplified to an associated linear homogeneous eigenvalue problem,
by modifying the FSSA stabilization terms
[
18
]
.

To improve the maximum time step size, the authors supplemented the free-surface equation and the Navier-Stokes problem with additional first-order consistent stabilization terms.

Contributions of the present paper.
In this paper, we present a novel theoretical study of the stability
of the coupled Stokes/free-surface problem for Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids with a source term, in both the continuous and fully discrete settings.
On the continuous level,
we derive a total energy balance that couples the energy of the Stokes problem with that of the free-surface equation,

which is key to understanding the stability.

From this, we establish a stability estimate and the conservation of domain volume.
On the discrete level, we show that the implicit Euler method is unconditionally stable and preserves domain volume.
By contrast, we provide numerical counterexamples showing that the semi-implicit Euler method does not conserve domain volume.
Moreover, the FSSA from
[
18
]
only partially mitigates the stability issues of both the explicit and the semi-implicit Euler schemes,
resulting in a conditional time step constraint.
Next, we prove that the explicit Euler method conserves domain volume, but is not stable in the total energy norm.

To address this limitation, we use our stability estimates to derive an optimally scaled stabilization term
that penalizes the normal velocity at the free surface in the Stokes problem.
This stabilization ensures that the explicit Euler method used for advancing the free-surface equation is unconditionally stable with respect to the time step size.
Our formulation also extends to cases with a source term in the free-surface equation.
In addition, we prove that the scheme preserves the volume of the domain and numerically verify that it maintains first-order accuracy under simultaneous refinement of the mesh and the time step.
Our analysis does not rely on any simplifications of the geometry or the original Stokes/free-surface formulation
and is valid in both two and three spatial dimensions.

2
Mathematical model

2.1
Governing equations

The fluid is represented as an open and bounded domain
\Omega=\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
that evolves in time
t\in T=\left[0,\hat{t}\,\right]
.
The evolving free boundary (one part of
\partial\Omega
) is represented as the surface height
h(\bm{x}^{\perp},t)
with

\bm{x}^{\perp}
evaluated over a
horizontal (flat)
domain
\Omega^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
The remaining part of
\partial\Omega
is a topography/bedrock
b({\bm{x}}^{\perp})
. The area between
h
and
b
is the fluid thickness
h-b>0
.
The relation between
\Omega
,
h
, and
\Omega^{\perp}
is illustrated in Figure
1
.
The coupled Stokes/free-surface problem reads. Find the fluid surface height
h:\Omega^{\perp}\times T\to\mathbb{R}
,
velocity
\bm{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and pressure
\pi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, such that:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}h+\bm{u}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h-u_{z}&=a,\\
-\nabla\cdot(2\,\mu(\bm{u})\bm{D}\bm{u})+\nabla\pi&=-\rho g\hat{{\bm{z}}},\\
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}&=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where

\bm{u}^{\perp}:\Omega^{\perp}\times T\to\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
is a vector of the horizontal surface velocities,

\nabla^{\perp}
is the
horizontal
gradient operator over
\Omega^{\perp}
,

u_{z}
is the vertical surface velocity,

a:\Omega^{\perp}\times T\to\mathbb{R}
is the source term,
\nabla
is the gradient operator over
\Omega
,
\bm{D}\bm{u}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\bm{u}+(\nabla\bm{u})^{T}\right)
is the strain rate tensor,
g>0
is the gravitational acceleration,
\rho>0
is the fluid density, and
\mu:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a viscosity function.
We consider a family of viscosity functions in the power-law form:

\mu(\bm{u})=\mu_{0}\,|\bm{D}\bm{u}|^{p-2},\quad 1<p\leq 2,

(2.2)

where
\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is an effective viscosity constant,
|\bm{D}\bm{u}|=(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\bm{D}\bm{u})_{ij}\,(\bm{D}\bm{u})_{ij})^{1/2}
is the Frobenius norm of
{\bm{D}}{\bm{u}}
.

Glaciologists
use Glen’s flow law exponent
n
in place of
p
, where the relation is
p=(1/n)+1

[
13
,
16
]
.
When
p=2
, the fluid is Newtonian. When
1<p<2
, the fluid is non-Newtonian.
We supplement (
2.1
) with an initial condition for the surface
h\left({\bm{x}}^{\perp},0\right)=h_{0}\left({\bm{x}}^{\perp}\right)
,
and the boundary conditions:

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}=\bm{0}\text{ on }\Gamma_{b},\quad\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{l},\quad\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{n}=\bm{0}\text{ on }\Gamma_{s},

(2.3)

where
\Gamma_{b}
is the bedrock,
\Gamma_{s}
is the free-surface,
\Gamma_{l}
are the lateral boundaries, and
\bm{\sigma}=2\mu({\bm{u}}){\bm{D}}{\bm{u}}-p{\mathbb{I}}

is the deviatoric stress tensor.

2.2
Weak formulation and function spaces

We analyze the weak formulation of the coupled problem (
2.1
), which is defined as follows.
Find
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}(\Omega)
,
\pi\in Q(\Omega)
, and
h\in Z(\Omega^{\perp})\times{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(T)
such that:

\left\{\begin{aligned} (\partial_{t}h,w)_{\Omega^{\perp}}+\left({\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h-u_{z},w\right)_{\Omega^{\perp}}&=(a,w)_{\Omega^{\perp}},\\
2\left(\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u},\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega(t)}-(\pi,\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}})_{\Omega(t)}&=-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},{\bm{v}})_{\Omega(t)},\\
(\nabla\cdot{\bm{u}},q)_{\Omega(t)}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}(\Omega)
,
q\dfrac{\textbullet}{\textbullet}\in Q(\Omega)
, and
w\in Z(\Omega^{\perp})
.
We now define the natural solution spaces
\bm{V}
,
Q
, and
Z
.
The Lebesgue space
L^{\alpha}(\Omega)
is defined as
[
11
]
:

L^{\alpha}(\Omega)=\{q:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\,|\,q\text{ measurable and }\|q\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}<\infty\},\,\alpha=[1,\infty),

where an associated
L^{\alpha}
-norm is
\|q\|_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}=(\int_{\Omega}|q|^{\alpha}\,d{\bm{x}})^{1/\alpha}
. The
Sobolev space
\bm{W}^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)
and its restriction to vanishing functions over
\Gamma_{b}\subset\partial\Omega
are respectively
defined through:

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\bm{v}\in[L^{\alpha}(\Omega)]^{d}\,\Big|\,\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}+\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{1,\alpha}_{\Gamma_{b}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\bm{v}\in\bm{W}^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)\,\Big|\,\bm{v}|_{\Gamma_{b}}=0\Big\}.

The solution to the nonlinear Stokes problem in weak form belongs to the velocity space
\bm{V}(\Omega)
and the pressure space
Q(\Omega)
, defined as
[
6
]
:

\bm{V}(\Omega)=\bm{W}^{1,p}_{\Gamma_{b}}(\Omega),\quad Q=L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega),\quad p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1},\quad 1<p\leq 2,

(2.5)

where
p
is a parameter in (
2.2
).

By the Sobolev trace theorem, if
\bm{u}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, then
\bm{u}|_{\partial\Omega}\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
. We assume that
h
belongs to
L^{2}\left(\Omega^{\perp}\right)
.
Accordingly, we define:

Z(\Omega^{\perp})=L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp}),

where an
L^{2}
inner product is
(q,w)_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}q\,w\,d{\bm{x}}
.

We approximate the solutions to the Stokes problem and the free-surface equation using the finite element method.
The discrete domains
\Omega_{h}
and
\Omega_{h}^{\perp}
are represented by meshes
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\perp}
.
Each mesh is composed of a finite number of disjoint elements
K
and
K^{\perp}
so that
\Omega_{h}=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}K
and
\Omega_{h}^{\perp}=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\perp}}K^{\perp}
.
We denote
\mathcal{E}_{h}
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}
a set of interior
d-1
dimensional faces in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\perp}
respectively.
The associated finite element spaces are the lowest-order Taylor-Hood velocity and pressure spaces, and a piecewise linear space for free-surface height:

\displaystyle\bm{V_{h}}(\Omega_{h})

\displaystyle=\Big\{{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega_{h})\,\Big|\,\bm{v}_{h}\big|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(K),\,\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\Big\},

\displaystyle Q_{h}(\Omega_{h})

\displaystyle=\Big\{q_{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega_{h})\,\Big|\,q_{h}\big|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K),\,\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\Big\},

\displaystyle Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\Big\{w_{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})\,\Big|\,w_{h}\big|_{K^{\perp}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K^{\perp}),\,\forall K^{\perp}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\perp}\Big\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{1}
,
\mathbb{P}_{2}
are multivariate polynomial spaces of order
1
and
2
. The analysis naturally extends to higher-order Taylor-Hood elements and higher-order mesh representations.

2.3
Surface representations and integration domains

In this section, we describe how the domain, surface integrals, and normal vectors depend on the surface height
h
, as this will be required in the subsequent analysis. For
d=2
we define
{\bm{x}}=(x,z),{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=x
, whereas for
d=3
we define
{\bm{x}}=(x,y,z),{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=(x,y)
. Then, the domain and its boundary are defined as:

\Omega=\left\{{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{\perp}\in\Omega^{\perp}\,\text{ and }\,b({\bm{x}}^{\perp})<z<h({\bm{x}}^{\perp})\right\},\quad\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{s}\cup\Gamma_{b}\cup\Gamma_{l},

(2.6)

where:

\Gamma_{s}=\left\{\left({\bm{x}}^{\perp},h({\bm{x}}^{\perp})\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{\perp}\in\Omega^{\perp}\right\},\quad\Gamma_{b}=\left\{\left({\bm{x}}^{\perp},b({\bm{x}}^{\perp})\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,{\bm{x}}^{\perp}\in\Omega^{\perp}\right\},\quad\Gamma_{l}={\partial}\Omega\backslash(\Gamma_{s}\cup\Gamma_{b}).

(2.7)

The volume of the domain is then defined as
|\Omega(t)|=\int_{\Omega(t)}d{\bm{x}}=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}(h-b)d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}
.

When computing integrals over
\Gamma_{s}
, it is convenient to change the integration domain to the time-independent evaluation domain
\Omega^{\perp}
.
Let
\bm{r}_{s}=({\bm{x}}^{\perp},h({\bm{x}}^{\perp},t))=(x,y,h(x,y,t))

be a parametrization of
\Gamma_{s}
. Then we have:

\int_{\Gamma_{s}}f(\bm{x})\,ds=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}f(\bm{r}_{s})\,\|\partial_{x}\bm{r}_{s}\times\partial_{y}\bm{r}_{s}\|_{2}\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}f(\bm{r}_{s})\,\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp},

(2.8)

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the Euclidean norm. This follows from the fact that

\partial_{x}\bm{r}_{s}=(1,0,\partial_{x}h)
,
\partial_{y}\bm{r}_{s}=(0,1,\partial_{y}h)
,
and thus
\partial_{x}\bm{r}_{s}\times\partial_{y}\bm{r}_{s}=(-\partial_{x}h,-\partial_{y}h,1)=(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)
.
Using that relation, the outward unit normal vector
\bm{n}
to
\Gamma_{s}
is:

\bm{n}(\bm{x})=\frac{\partial_{x}\bm{r}_{s}\times\partial_{y}\bm{r}_{s}}{\|\partial_{x}\bm{r}_{s}\times\partial_{y}\bm{r}_{s}\|_{2}}=\frac{(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)}{\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}},\quad\bm{x}\in\Gamma_{s}.

(2.9)

Finally, the surface velocity
{\bm{u}}^{\perp}:\Omega^{\perp}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
is denoted as

{\bm{u}}^{\perp}=\left(u_{x}\left({\bm{x}}^{\perp},h\right),u_{y}\left({\bm{x}}^{\perp},h\right)\right)
when
d=3
and
{\bm{u}}^{\perp}=\left(u_{x}\left({\bm{x}}^{\perp},h\right)\right)
when
d=2
.
Note that the relations
\int_{\Gamma_{s}}f(\bm{x})\,ds=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}f(\bm{r}_{s})\,\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}

and
\bm{n}=\frac{(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)}{\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}}
derived above also applies for
d=2
.

3
Continuous problem analysis

In this section, we analyze the stability of the problem as stated in (
2.4
), posed over the infinite-dimensional spaces
\bm{V}(\Omega)
,
Q(\Omega)
, and
Z(\Omega^{\perp})
.
Our goal is to derive the total energy balance, which couples the energy balance of the momentum equations
to the free-surface height energy.
The framework developed here is also used in later sections to analyze stability and conservation in the discrete setting, where explicit, semi-implicit, and implicit Euler methods are used for time discretization, and finite elements are used in space.

3.1
Relation between the Stokes problem on
\Omega
and the free-surface equation on
\Omega^{\perp}

We establish two relations used for the stability analysis of the coupled Stokes/free-surface problem in the sections that follow.
The first relates one term in the free-surface equation on
\Omega^{\perp}
to an equivalent formulation on
\Gamma_{s}
.
The second relates the term formulated on
\Gamma_{s}
, to the right-hand side of the Stokes equation.

Lemma 1

(Free-surface equation to surface integral)
.

Let
h:\Omega^{\perp}\times T\to\mathbb{R}
be the surface height function associated with

\Gamma_{s}
(
2.7
). Then the following relation holds:

(-{\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h+u_{z},w)_{\Omega^{\perp}}=\int_{\Gamma_{s}(t)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,w\,ds,\quad\forall w\in Z\left(\Omega^{\perp}\right),

(3.1)

and when
w=h
, the relation becomes

(-{\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h+u_{z},h)_{\Omega^{\perp}}=\int_{\Gamma_{s}(t)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,z\,ds
with

(x,y,z)\in\Omega(t)
.

Proof.

We rewrite the integration domain from
\Omega^{\perp}
to
\Gamma_{s}(t)
of the term

(-{\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h+u_{z},w)_{\Omega^{\perp}}
. We have

(-{\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h+u_{z},w)_{\Omega^{\perp}}=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}(-{\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h+u_{z})\,w\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}{\bm{u}}\cdot(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\,w\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}{\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\,w\,\frac{\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}}{\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}}d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}
.
It follows that: (i) the vector
\frac{(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)}{\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}}
corresponds to the unit normal
\bm{n}
as defined in (
2.9
), and (ii) the remaining factor

\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}
corresponds to the arclength
ds
of the surface
\Gamma_{s}(t)
, as defined in (
2.8
). Using that, we rewrite the original integral as:

(-{\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h+u_{z},w)_{\Omega^{\perp}}=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}\bm{u}\cdot\frac{(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)}{\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}}\,w\,\|(-\nabla^{\perp}h,1)\|_{2}\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=\int_{\Gamma_{s}(t)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,w\,ds,

(3.2)

which proves the first statement (
3.1
) of the lemma.
In (
3.2
) we set
w=h
.
Since
\Gamma_{s}(t)
is in (
2.7
) parameterized as
z=h({\bm{x}}^{\perp})
and since we are integrating over
\Gamma_{s}
, we substitute
h=z
,
which proves the second statement of this proposition.
∎

Lemma 2

(Stokes problem to surface integral)
.

Let
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}(\Omega)
solve the Stokes problem in (
2.4
).
Then, the relation between the volume integral
-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},\bm{u})_{\Omega(t)}
and the surface integral over
\Gamma_{s}(t)
is:

-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},\bm{u})_{\Omega(t)}=-\rho g\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}(t)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,z\,ds,\quad{\bm{x}}\in\Omega(t).

(3.3)

Proof.

We write the gravity term as
-\rho g\hat{{\bm{z}}}=-\rho g\nabla z
. It follows that:

-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},\bm{u})_{\Omega(t)}=-\rho g(\nabla z,\bm{u})_{\Omega(t)}=\rho g(\nabla\cdot\bm{u},z)_{\Omega(t)}-\rho g\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,z\,ds=-\rho g\int_{\Gamma_{s}(t)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,z\,ds.

In the first step, we integrated
(\nabla z,\bm{u})_{\Omega(t)}
by parts. In the second step, we used the incompressibility condition
(\nabla\cdot\bm{u},z)_{\Omega(t)}=0
from (
2.4
), noting that
z\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Finally, the boundary integral was restricted to
\Gamma_{s}
using the boundary conditions.
∎

3.2
Stability analysis

In this section, we show the stability of the coupled Stokes/free-surface problem (
2.4
) in the continuous setting,
through an energy balance and a corresponding total energy estimate,
which is the key to understanding the stability also in discretized settings
. In the later sections, we use this total energy estimate
to argue about the stability of the numerical schemes.

Theorem 3

(Continuous stability)
.

Let the surface height
h
and the velocity
\bm{u}
solve the coupled system (
2.4
) on domain
\Omega=\Omega(t)
, with source term
a=a\left({\bm{x}}^{\perp},t\right)
,
viscosity
\mu=\mu(\bm{u})
, fluid density
\rho
and the gravitational acceleration
g
.
Then, the following total energy balance holds:

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}^{2}=-\frac{2}{\rho g}\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}+(a,h)_{\Omega^{\perp}}.

(3.4)

Moreover, the following strong stability estimate in the total energy norm holds:

\begin{split}\|h(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}^{2}+\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\int_{0}^{\,t}\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}d\tau&\leq\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{\,t}\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}\int_{0}^{\,t}\,\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}\,d\tau d\tau\\
&\quad+2\int_{0}^{\,t}\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}d\tau\qquad\forall t\in T.\end{split}

(3.5)

Proof.

(i) Energy balance.

Setting
\bm{v}=\bm{u}
and
q=\pi
in (
2.4
), yields

2\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}=-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},\bm{u})_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}
.
Applying Lemma
2
to the right-hand side gives
2\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}^{2}=-\rho g\int_{\Gamma_{s}(t)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,z\,ds.
We now: (i) apply
Lemma
1
to rewrite the surface integral over
\Gamma_{s}(t)
as an integral
over
\Omega^{\perp}
, and (ii) use free-surface equation in (
2.4
) with
w=h
to obtain

\displaystyle 2\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\rho g\,(-{\bm{u}}^{\perp}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h+u_{z},h)_{\Omega^{\perp}}=-\rho g(\partial_{t}h,h)_{\Omega^{\perp}}+\rho g(a,h)_{\Omega^{\perp}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho g}{2}\partial_{t}\|h\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}+\rho g(a,h)_{\Omega^{\perp}}.

Next, we divide both sides of (
3.6
) by
\rho g
. Rearranging the terms then proves the first statement (
3.4
) of this proposition.

(ii) Stability estimate.

Using (
3.4
) we have:

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|h\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}=\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}\,\partial_{t}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}\leq-\frac{2}{\rho g}\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}.

(3.7)

In the above relation we use
-\frac{2}{\rho g}\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}\leq 0
and
then divide the resulting inequality by
\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}
, which gives:

\partial_{t}\|h(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}\leq\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}.

We integrate the latter relation in time to arrive at:

\|h(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}\leq\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}+\int_{0}^{\,t}\,\|a(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp})}\,d\tau.

Inserting this on the right-hand-side of (
3.7
),
and then time-integrating and multiplying by
2
on both sides of the inequality, finalizes the proof of the second statement (
3.5
)
of this proposition.
∎

Remark 1
.

We note that the coupled problem (
2.4
) depends on the parameter
p
through the viscosity function (
2.2
). Accordingly, the appropriate norm for the strain tensor
\bm{D}\bm{u}
is the one associated with the
L^{p}
space. The scaled
L^{p}
-norm of the strain tensor is related to its viscosity-weighted
L^{2}
-norm as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\,\|\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{0}\,|\bm{D}\bm{u}|^{p}\,d{\bm{x}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{0}\,|\bm{D}\bm{u}|^{p-2}\,|\bm{D}\bm{u}|^{2}\,d{\bm{x}}=\int_{\Omega}\mu\,|\bm{D}\bm{u}|^{2}\,d{\bm{x}}=\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}|^{2}\,d{\bm{x}}=\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where, in the third step, we used the viscosity definition (
2.2
). Therefore, the estimates in terms of
\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
remain valid also for
p\neq 2
.

3.3
Volume conservation

In the continuous setting, the domain volume
|\Omega|
is conserved over time.
For completeness, we briefly revisit the volume conservation analysis.
Using the free-surface equation (
2.4
) with
w=1
we obtain:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|\Omega(t)|

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}(h-b)\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}\partial_{t}h\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=(\partial_{t}h,1)=(-{\bm{u}}^{\bot}\cdot\nabla^{\bot}h+u_{z}+a,1)_{\Omega^{\bot}}.

(3.9)

Next, applying Lemma
1
to relate the integration over
\Omega^{\perp}
to integration over the surface
\Gamma_{s}(t)
, and using integration by parts together with the incompressibility condition from (
2.4
) with
q=1\in L^{2}(\Omega)

we obtain:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|\Omega(t)|=(-{\bm{u}}^{\bot}\cdot\nabla^{\bot}h+u_{z}+a,1)_{\Omega^{\bot}}=\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\,ds+\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}a\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega(t)}\!\!\!\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\,d{\bm{x}}+\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}a\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\perp}}a\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}\!\!.

This shows that the domain volume only changes due to the source term
a
.

4
Discretized Stokes/free-surface problems and their analysis

In this section, we analyze stability and volume conservation
when the finite element method is used in space and explicit Euler, semi-implicit Euler, and implicit Euler are used to discretize time.
We aim to derive discrete analogues of the continuous total energy estimate (
3.5
) and volume conservation balance (
3.10
).

Deriving a discrete counterpart to the continuous total energy estimate (
3.5
) is a crucial step
for having the numerical stability in time. However, this is not sufficient to suppress high-frequency oscillations when the high-order derivatives either entail
discontinuities or are under-resolved on a computational mesh, as the total energy of the couple Stokes/free-surface problem
does not control the higher-order derivatives on its own.
To suppress the high-order oscillations, we use the edge stabilization approach
[
8
,
10
]
.
This is a third-order accurate term, preferred to use on
\mathbb{P}_{1}
spaces over
the hyperviscosity-based approach that requires higher-order polynomial degrees.
To all the considered numerical schemes in this paper we add the edge stabilization term defined as:

\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}w}]\!]ds,\quad\gamma_{e}=\frac{1}{2}\,h_{K^{\perp}}^{2}|{\bm{u}}|,

(4.1)

where the jump of a vector field over an interior mesh edge
e=\partial K_{+}\cap\partial K_{-}\in{\mathcal{E}}
is defined as
[\![{{\bm{w}}}]\!]:={\bm{w}}_{+}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{+}-{\bm{w}}_{-}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{-}

with
{\bm{w}}_{\pm}={\bm{w}}|_{K_{\pm}}
and
{\bm{n}}_{\pm}
denoting the outward-pointing unit normal vector on
\partial K_{\pm}
.
The mesh size
h_{K^{\perp}}
is defined as the cell diameter.
We note that this stabilization approach does not stabilize the scheme in time, but is only
provided to suppress the high-frequency oscillations that, in some cases, appear during numerical simulations.
Some alternative linear high-order stabilization approaches (combined with nonlinear artificial viscosity) are given in
[
17
,
19
,
27
,
21
,
29
,
25
,
30
,
10
]
.

4.1
Implicit Euler discretized problem analysis

The implicit Euler discretization of (
2.4
), including edge stabilization (
4.1
),
is defined as follows. Given
h^{n}
and
a^{n+1}
, find
\bm{u}^{n+1}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})
,
\pi^{n+1}\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})
,
and
h^{n+1}\in Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})
such that:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{h^{n+1}-h^{n}}{\Delta t},w\right)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}\!\!\!\!\!\!+(\bm{u}^{\perp,n+1}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}-u_{z}^{n+1},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+\!\!\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}w}]\!]ds&=(a^{n+1},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}},\\
2(\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}^{n+1},\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}^{n+1}}-(\pi^{n+1},\nabla\cdot\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}^{n+1}}&=-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},{\bm{v}})_{\Omega_{h}^{n+1}},\\
(\nabla\cdot{\bm{u}}^{n+1},q)_{\Omega_{h}^{n+1}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})
,
q\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})
, and
w\in Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})
.
In the two propositions below, we show that this scheme:

1.

is unconditionally stable with respect to
\Delta t
in the total energy norm, mimicing the continuous counterpart (
3.5
),

2.

enables the domain volume conservation.

However, the scheme requires nonlinear iterations accross the Stokes/free-surface problem due to the dependence of
\Omega^{n+1}
in the Stokes problem to
h^{n+1}
in the free-surface equation.
In that sense, the scheme is less computationally efficient.

Proposition 4

(Implicit Euler stability)
.

Let
(\bm{u}^{n+1},\pi^{n+1},h^{n+1})
be a solution to (
4.2
) with source term
a^{n+1}=a(\bm{x}^{\perp},t^{n+1})
.
Then the following stability estimate in the total energy norm holds:

\displaystyle\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}+2\Delta t\|a^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}

(4.3)

\displaystyle+2(\Delta t)^{2}\|a^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}.

Proof.

(i) Energy balance.

We set
w=2h^{n+1}
in the free-surface equation in (
4.2
) and then
apply the identity
2a^{2}-2ab=a^{2}-b^{2}+(a-b)^{2}
with
a=h^{n+1}
and
b=h^{n}
, to obtain:

\begin{split}\|h^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}&=\|h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}-\|h^{n+1}-h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}+2\Delta t(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n+1}\cdot\nabla h^{n+1}+u^{\perp}_{3}+a^{n+1},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}\\
&\quad-2\Delta t\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]ds.\end{split}

(4.4)

We now focus on the term
(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n+1}\cdot\nabla h^{n+1}+u^{\perp,n+1}_{3},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}
. Since all variables in this bilinear form are evaluated at the same time
t=t^{n+1}
,
the analysis framework for the continuous case as outlined in Lemma
1
, Lemma
2
, and
Theorem
3
applies. In particular, using (
3.6
) from the proof of
Theorem
3

we obtain:
2\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})}^{2}=-\rho g\,(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n+1}\cdot\nabla h^{n+1}+u^{\perp,n+1}_{3},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}.

Dividing the equation above by
\rho g
, substituting this result into (
4.4
), and rearranging the terms, gives:

\displaystyle\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}+2\Delta t\,(a^{n+1},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle-2\,\Delta t\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]ds-\|h^{n+1}-h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}.

(ii) An intermediate
L^{2}
-estimate of
h
.

We set
w=h^{n+1},{\bm{v}}={\bm{u}}^{n+1}
, and
q=\pi^{n+1}
in (
4.2
), and repeat similar steps as in the first part of this proof, to obtain:

\begin{split}\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}&=(h^{n},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}-\frac{2}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})}^{2}+\Delta t\,(a^{n+1},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}\\
&\quad-\Delta t\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]ds.\end{split}

(4.6)

Neglecting the negative terms and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we estimate:

\displaystyle\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(h^{n},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+\Delta t\,(a^{n+1},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=(h^{n}+\Delta t\,a^{n+1},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{n}+\Delta t\,a^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}\,\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}.

Dividing both sides by
\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}
, and applying the triangle inequality then gives:

\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}\leq\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}\,+\Delta t\,\|a^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}.

(4.8)

(iii) Total energy estimate.

We start at (
4.5
) and use
-2\Delta t\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]ds\leq 0

as well as
2\Delta t\,(a^{n+1},h^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}\leq 2\Delta t\|a^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}
.
Then we have:

\displaystyle\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}+2\Delta t\|a^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}+2\Delta t\|a^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}

\displaystyle+2(\Delta t)^{2}\|a^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2},

where we used (
4.8
) in the final step.
∎

Proposition 5

(Implicit Euler conservation)
.

The solution to (
4.2
) with source term
a^{n+1}=a(\bm{x}^{\perp},t^{n+1})
conserves domain volume:
|\Omega_{h}^{n+1}|=|\Omega_{h}^{n}|+\Delta t\,\int_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}a^{n+1}\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}.

Proof.

We set
w=1
in the free-surface equation part of (
4.2
) to obtain:

(h^{n+1},1)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}=(h^{n},1)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+\Delta t\,(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n+1}\cdot\nabla h^{n+1}+u^{\perp,n+1}_{3}+a^{n+1},1)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}.

(4.10)

Since all terms in the last integrand are evaluated at time
t=t^{n+1}
, the analysis in Section
3.3
applies.
Following the derivation of the continuous volume conservation in (
3.10
), we have that:

(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n+1}\cdot\nabla h^{n+1}+u^{\perp,n+1}_{3},1)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{h}^{n+1}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,ds=\int_{\Omega_{h}^{n+1}}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}^{n+1}\,ds=0,

(4.11)

where the final equality follows from the incompressibility condition (
4.2
) with
q=1\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n+1})
. Combining (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), and using the definition of domain volume via the height function, we conclude

|\Omega_{h}^{n+1}|-|\Omega_{h}^{n}|=(h^{n+1}-b,1)-(h^{n}-b,1)=(h^{n+1},1)-(h^{n},1)=\Delta t\,\int_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}a^{n+1}\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp},

which completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Explicit Euler discretized problem analysis

We now consider the explicit Euler discretization of (
2.4
) with edge stabilization.
Given
h^{n}
and
a^{n}
, find
\bm{u}^{n}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
,
\pi^{n}\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
, and
h^{n+1}\in Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})
such that:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{h^{n+1}-h^{n}}{\Delta t},w\right)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+\left(\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}-u_{z}^{n},w\right)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}^{n}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}w}]\!]ds&=(a^{n},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}},\\
2\left(\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}^{n},\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}-(\pi^{n},\nabla\cdot\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}&=-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},{\bm{v}})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}},\\
(\nabla\cdot{\bm{u}}^{n},q)_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.12)

for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
,
q\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
, and
w\in Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})
.

This is a numerical scheme central to this paper. The scheme does not require
nonlinear iterations across the Stokes/free-surface problems and is, in that sense, computationally efficient.
In the two propositions below, we show that this scheme:

1.

is unstable in the total energy norm for any
\Delta t
, with spurious terms
(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds
and
2\,(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds

that prevent mimicing the continuous total energy estimate (
3.5
),

2.

enables the domain volume conservation.

Proposition 6

(Explicit Euler stability estimate)
.

Let
(\bm{u}^{n},\pi^{n},h^{n+1})
be a solution to (
4.12
) with source term
a^{n}=a(\bm{x}^{\perp},t^{n})
. Then the following stability estimate in the total energy norm holds:

\displaystyle\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+2\,\Delta t(a^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega^{\perp}_{h}}+(\Delta t)^{2}\|a^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp}_{h})}

(4.13)

\displaystyle+(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds+2\,(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds.

Proof.

(i) Decomposition of the free-surface equation into explicit and implicit parts.

In the first equation of (
4.12
), we use
h^{n+1}=h_{E}^{n+1}+h_{I}^{n+1}
to then
decompose the equation into an explicit part and an implicit part:

\left(\frac{h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}}{\Delta t},w\right)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+(\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}-u_{z}^{n},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}=(a^{n},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}},

(4.14)

\left(\frac{h_{I}^{n+1}}{\Delta t},w\right)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}=-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}^{n}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}w}]\!]ds.

(4.15)

In (
4.14
), we now set
w=2h^{n}
and use the identity
2ab-2b^{2}=a^{2}-b^{2}-(a-b)^{2}
with
a=h_{E}^{n+1}
,
b=h^{n}
, to obtain:

\displaystyle\|h_{E}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+2\Delta t(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+2\Delta t(a^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+\|h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}.

(4.16)

(ii) Energy balance.

We use Lemma
1
to write term
(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}

as a surface integral,
followed by using Lemma
2
to write the resulting surface integral to the scaled Stokes gravity term. This gives:

2\,\Delta t(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}=2\,\Delta t\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,z\,ds=2\Delta t\,(\hat{{\bm{z}}},\bm{u})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}
.
Next, we use the Stokes problem (
4.12
) formulation with
\bm{v}=\bm{u}^{n}
and
q=\pi^{n}
to relate the scaled gravity term to the strain tensor norm as:

2\,\Delta t(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}=2\Delta t\,(\hat{{\bm{z}}},\bm{u})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}=-\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}.

(4.17)

Inserting this relation into (
4.16
) and then rearranging, gives the energy balance:

\displaystyle\|h_{E}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+\|h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}+2\Delta t(a^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}.

(4.18)

(iii) Spurious term estimate.

We estimate the spurious term
\|h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}
in (
4.18
) by setting
w=h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}
and then estimating:

\|h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}^{2}=\Delta t(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n}+a^{n},h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n})_{\Omega_{h}^{\bot}}\leq\Delta t\|-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n}+a^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}\,\|h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}.

Dividing both sides by
\|h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}
and squaring the resulting inequality gives:

\displaystyle\|h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}

\displaystyle\leq(\Delta t)^{2}\,\|-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n}+a^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}

(4.19)

\displaystyle=(\Delta t)^{2}\,\underbrace{\|-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}}_{\text{{Term A}}}

\displaystyle+2(\Delta t)^{2}\,\underbrace{(a^{n},-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n})}_{\text{{Term B}}}+(\Delta t)^{2}\,\|a^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}.

We now rewrite Term A in (
4.19
) by defining an integral scaling:

\omega=\|(-1,\nabla^{\perp}h^{n})\|_{2}=\frac{1}{n_{z}}>0
,
where
n_{z}
denotes the vertical component of an outward unit normal vector
{\bm{n}}
on
\Gamma^{n}
.
Inserting this relation into (
4.19
), we have:

\displaystyle\|-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}\hskip-2.84544pt\frac{\omega}{\omega}\,(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n})\,(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n})\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}

(4.20)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\hskip-2.84544pt(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n})\,ds=\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\hskip-2.84544pt\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}ds,

where we in the second step used:
\frac{1}{\omega}\,(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n})=\bm{u}^{n}\cdot\bm{n}
and
\omega\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=ds
(as in the proof of Lemma
1
).
In the final step, we used
-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n}=\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})
.
We proceed by rewriting Term B in (
4.19
) as follows:

(a,-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n})=\int_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}\frac{\omega}{\omega}a\,(-\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}+u_{z}^{n})\,d{\bm{x}}^{\perp}=\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds.\\

(4.21)

Alltogether, inserting (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) into (
4.19
), gives the final bound of the spurious term:

\|h_{E}^{n+1}-h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}^{2}\leq(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds+2\,(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds+(\Delta t)^{2}\,\|a^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}.

(4.22)

(iv) Finalization.

We now make steps towards estimating
\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp}_{h})}
by writing

\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp}_{h})}=\|h_{E}^{n+1}+h_{I}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}=\|h_{E}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}+\|h_{I}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}+2(h_{E}^{n+1},h_{I}^{n+1})
.
We continue by testing
(
4.15
) against
w=h_{E}^{n+1}+h_{I}^{n+1}
to first
expand the term
2(h_{E}^{n+1},h_{I}^{n+1})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}
, and then bound out all the resulting negative terms, as follows:

\displaystyle\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp}_{h})}

\displaystyle=\|h_{E}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}-\|h_{I}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}-2\,\Delta t\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}^{n}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\|h_{E}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}.

(4.23)

Finally, inserting (
4.18
) into (
4.23
), followed by
inserting (
4.22
) into the resulting estimate, proves this proposition.
∎

4.2.1
Weakly stable time step size for the unstabilized explicit Euler discretized problem

The stability estimate (
4.13
) implies that the corresponding scheme (
4.12
) is unstable
in the total energy norm for any
\Delta t
.
In the steps that follow, we determine
\Delta t
that implies stability in a weaker sense,
that is, stability without having a full control over the total energy norm.
This is disadvantageous as it implies a weaker coupling between the Stokes/free-surface equations and gives rise to increased energy errors.

We first write

\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}=(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}+\varepsilon\,\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}
,
where
0<\varepsilon<1
.
We insert that relation into (
4.13
) and rearrange the terms to arrive at a weaker stability estimate:

\displaystyle\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+2\,\Delta t(a^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega^{\perp}_{h}}+(\Delta t)^{2}\|a^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp}_{h})}

(4.24)

\displaystyle+(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds+2\,(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\,\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}.

For weaker stability we require that:

(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds+2\,(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds-\varepsilon\,\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}\leq 0.

Solving that inequality for
\Delta t
implies a time step restriction that gives stability in a weaker sense:

\displaystyle\Delta t^{n}\leq\varepsilon\,\frac{\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}}{\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds+2\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds},\quad 0<\varepsilon<1,\quad n=0,1,2,...

(4.25)

Using such
\Delta t
,
the norm
(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{4}{\rho g}\,\Delta t\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}
in (
4.24
)
is now controlled only up to the
\varepsilon
parameter.

We use
\Delta t
computed according to (
4.25
) only for drawing comparisons across numerical schemes in the numerical experiments section. We do not
recommend using this approach in practice

as it leads to a weak coupling between the Stokes/free-surface equations and increased energy errors.

Instead, in Section
4.3
, we give a stabilization term that (i) allows a full total energy norm control without
\varepsilon
-dependence, and (ii) enables stability for any choice of
\Delta t
.

4.3
Stabilized explicit Euler discretized coupled problem

Motivated by the stability estimate (
4.13
) for the explicit Euler method (
4.12
), we introduce the following stabilized method.
Given
h^{n}
and
a^{n}
, find
h^{n+1}\in Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})
,
\bm{u}^{n}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
,
\pi^{n}\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
such that:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{h^{n+1}-h^{n}}{\Delta t},w\right)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+(\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}-u_{z}^{n},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}w}]\!]ds&=(a^{n},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}},\\
\!2(\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}^{n},\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}+\frac{\rho g\Delta t}{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\!\!\!\!\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\,ds+\rho g\,\Delta t\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\!\!\!\!(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\,a\,ds-(\pi^{n},\nabla\cdot\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}&=-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},{\bm{v}})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}},\\
(\nabla\cdot{\bm{u}}^{n},q)_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.26)

for all
w\in Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})
,
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
, and
q\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
.

This scheme does not require nonlinear iterations accross the Stokes/free-surface problem and is in that sense computationally efficient.
The only difference between
the unstabilized and stabilized systems (
4.12
) and (
4.26
) are the two added stabilization terms

\frac{\rho g\Delta t}{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\,ds
, and
\rho g\,\Delta t\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}a\,(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\,ds
.

The first term is, up to the scaling, the same as introduced in
[
26
]
,
where it was used for time step size estimation.
The scaling that we propose
is motivated by our analysis in Proposition
6
without simplifications
of the original Stokes/free-surface problem formulation.
The added stabilization terms can be seen as of FSSA-type
[
18
]
,
however, compared to the FSSA stabilization terms in (
4.29
), our stabilization terms are derived such that they precisely cancel the spurious term in the
total energy estimate outlined in Proposition
6
.
Furthermore, these stabilization terms do not break the symmetry of the Stokes problem, which is advantageous when using the iterative linear solvers.

In the two propositions below, we show that the scheme (
4.26
):

1.

is unconditionally stable with respect to
\Delta t
in the total energy norm, mimicing the continuous counterpart (
3.5
),

2.

enables the domain volume conservation.

Proposition 8

(Stabilized explicit Euler stability estimate)
.

Let
(\bm{u}^{n},\pi^{n},h^{n+1})
be a solution to the stabilized explicit Euler scheme (
4.26
)
with source term
a^{n}=a(\bm{x}^{\perp},t^{n})
. Then the following stability estimate in the total energy norm holds:

\begin{split}\|h^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+\frac{4\Delta t}{\rho g}\,\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}\leq\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+2\,\Delta t(a^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega^{\perp}_{h}}+(\Delta t)^{2}\|a^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp}_{h})}.\end{split}

(4.27)

Proof.

We reuse the proof of Proposition
6
. Taking
into account the stabilization terms in (
4.26
) we rewrite (
4.17
) to:

2\Delta t(\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n}-u_{z}^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}=2\,\Delta t\,(\hat{{\bm{z}}},\bm{u})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}=-\frac{4\Delta t}{\rho g}\,\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}-(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds-2\,(\Delta t^{2})\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds.

We now use this relation to reiterate the remaining steps in the proof of Proposition
6
(from (
4.17
) on)
and then obtain the following estimate:

\begin{split}&\|h^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\bot})}+\frac{4\Delta t}{\rho g}\,\,\|\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{n})}+(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds+2\,(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds\\
&\leq\|h^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})}^{2}+2\,\Delta t(a^{n},h^{n})_{\Omega^{\perp}_{h}}+(\Delta t)^{2}\|a^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\perp}_{h})}+(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds+2\,(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,a^{n}\,ds.\end{split}

(4.28)

We subtract
(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\omega\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})^{2}\,ds+2\,(\Delta t)^{2}\,\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}a^{n}\,(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,ds
on both sides of the inequality
to finalize the proof of this proposition.
∎

4.4
Semi-implicit Euler discretized coupled problem stabilized by FSSA

The semi-implicit Euler method stabilized using the FSSA approach
[
18
]
is formulated as follows.
Given
h^{n}
and
a^{n}
, find
h^{n+1}\in Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})
,
\bm{u}^{n}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
, and
\pi^{n}\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
, such that:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{h^{n+1}-h^{n}}{\Delta t},w\right)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+(\bm{u}^{\perp,n}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}-u_{z}^{n},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\perp}}\int_{e}\gamma_{e}[\![{\nabla^{\perp}h^{n+1}}]\!]\cdot[\![{\nabla^{\perp}w}]\!]ds&=(a^{n},w)_{\Omega_{h}^{\perp}},\\
2(\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{u}^{n},\sqrt{\mu}\,\bm{D}\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}+\rho g\,\Delta t\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\!\!\!(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,(\hat{{\bm{z}}}\cdot\bm{v})\,ds+\rho g\,\Delta t\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}\!\!\!(\bm{v}\cdot\hat{{\bm{z}}}\bm{n})\,a\,ds-(\pi^{n},\nabla\cdot\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}&=-\rho g(\hat{{\bm{z}}},{\bm{v}})_{\Omega_{h}^{n}},\\
(\nabla\cdot{\bm{u}}^{n},q)_{\Omega_{h}^{n}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.29)

for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
,
q\in Q_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{n})
, and
w\in Z_{h}(\Omega_{h}^{\perp})
.
This semi-implicit formulation is a common discretization choice in
for instance numerical models of
ice sheet dynamics
[
20
,
22
,
28
]
.

The formulation can also be written in the explicit Euler setting, but numerical evidence suggests that
the semi-implicit formulation allows taking larger stable time step sizes
[
20
,
22
]
.

In both settings, the added non-symmetric term
\rho g\,\Delta t\int_{\Gamma_{s}^{n}}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,(\hat{{\bm{z}}}\cdot\bm{v})\,ds

breaks the symmetry of the Stokes problem, which could be disadvantageous when using the iterative linear solvers.

The stability analysis of (
4.29
) requires extending the theoretical framework in the proof of Theorem
3

beyond the scope of the present paper. Instead, in Section
5.1
and Section
5.3

we experimentally show that the solution to this scheme:

1.

is conditionally stable with respect to
\Delta t
in the total energy norm,

2.

does not enable the domain volume conservation.

6
Final remarks

We have investigated the stability and conservation properties of the coupled Stokes/free-surface system.
The analysis covered Newtonian (linear Stokes) and non-Newtonian (nonlinear Stokes) fluids, both at the continuous and fully discrete levels.

On the continuous level, we derived energy estimates that couple the momentum balance and surface height evolution.
Using these estimates, we examined implicit, semi-implicit, and explicit Euler time discretizations.

On the discrete level, we showed that the implicit method is unconditionally stable and conserves volume.
The semi-implicit scheme, even when stabilized with FSSA, is only conditionally stable and does not conserve volume.
The explicit Euler method conserves volume but is unstable.

We theoretically found that the cause of the instabilities is related
to the normal surface velocity norm. We proposed
a stabilization term that accurately eliminates these instabilities and showed unconditional stability with respect to
\Delta t
, while preserving the domain volume.
The stabilization term is symmetric, simple to implement, and works without tuning parameters.

Numerical tests confirmed all the theoretical results.

The stabilized explicit scheme (
4.26
) that we proposed offers a robust and efficient alternative to more expensive implicit solvers.
This is the scheme we recommend using in practice.

Future work may entail: extending the newly proposed stabilization approach to higher-order time integrators,
investigating positivity preservation of the free-surface height
h
, and
developing adaptive time-stepping strategies based on the derived energy balances.

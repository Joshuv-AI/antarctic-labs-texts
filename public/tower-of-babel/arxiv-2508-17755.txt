Weak Nearly 𝒮 \mathcal{S} - and Weak Nearly 𝒞 \mathcal{C} - Manifolds
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
The recent interest of geometers in the f f -structures of K. Yano (1961) is motivated by the study of the dynamics of contact foliations, as well as their applications in theoretical physics. Weak metric f f -structures on a smooth manifold, introduced by of the author and R. Wolak (2022), open a new perspective on the theory of classical structures. In the paper, we define structures of this kind, called weak nearly 𝒮 {\cal S} - and weak nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} - structures, study their geometry, e.g. their relations to Killing vector fields, and characterize weak nearly 𝒮 {\cal S} - and weak nearly 𝒮 {\cal S} - submanifolds in a weak nearly Kähler manifold. Keywords : weak nearly 𝒮 {\cal S} -manifold; weak nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} -manifold; Killing vector field; submanifold; weak nearly Kähler manifold Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) : 53C15; 53C25; 53D15



1
Introduction

An
f
-structure introduced by K. Yano
[
1
]
on a smooth manifold
M^{2n+s}
serves as a higher-dimensional analog
of almost complex structures (
s=0
) and almost contact structures (
s=1
).
This structure is defined by a (1,1)-tensor

f
of rank
2n
such that
f^{3}+f=0
.
The tangent bundle splits into two complementary subbundles:

TM=f(TM)\oplus\ker f
.
The restriction of
f
to the
2n
-dimensional distribution
f(TM)
defines a complex structure.
The existence of the
f
-structure on
M^{2n+s}
is equivalent to a reduction of the
structure group to
U(n)\times O(s)
, see
[
2
]
.
A submanifold
M
of an almost complex manifold
(\bar{M},J)
that satisfies the condition
\dim(T_{x}M\cap J(T_{x}M))=const>0

naturally possesses an
f
-structure, see
[
3
]
.
An
f
-structure is a special case of an almost product structure, defined by two complementary orthogonal distributions of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
.
Foliations appear when one or both distributions are involutive.
An interesting case occurs when the subbundle
\ker f
is parallelizable, leading to a framed
f
-structure
for which the reduced structure group is
U(n)\times{\rm id}_{s}
.
In this scenario, there exist vector fields
\{\xi_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq s}
(called Reeb vector fields) spanning
\ker f

with dual 1-forms
\{\eta^{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq s}
, satisfying
{f}^{2}=-{\rm id}+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}{\eta^{i}}\otimes{\xi_{i}}
.
Compatible metrics, i.e.,

g({f}X,{f}Y)=g(X,Y)-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}{\eta^{i}}(X)\,{\eta^{i}}(Y)
,
exist on any framed
f
-manifold,
and we get the metric
f
-structure,
see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

To generalize concepts and results from almost contact geometry to metric
f
-manifolds, geometers have introduced and studied various broad classes of metric
f
-structures.
A metric
f
-manifold is termed a
{\cal K}
-manifold if it is normal and
d\Phi=0
, where
\Phi(X,Y):=g(X,{f}Y)
.
Two important subclasses of
{\cal K}
-manifolds are
{\cal C}
-manifolds if
d\eta^{i}=0
and
{\cal S}
-manifolds if
d\eta^{i}=\Phi
for any
i
,
see
[
2
]
.
Omitting the normality condition, we get almost
{\cal K}
-manifolds, almost
{\cal S}
-manifolds and almost
{\cal C}
-manifolds, e.g.,
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
The distribution
\ker f
of a
{\cal K}
-manifold is tangent to a
\mathfrak{g}
-foliation with flat totally geodesic leaves.
An
f
-K-contact manifold is an almost
{\cal S}
-manifold, whose characteristic vector fields are Killing vector fields; the structure is intermediate between almost
{\cal S}
-structure and
S
-structure, see
[
7
,
11
]
.
Nearly
{\cal S}
- and nearly
{\cal C}
- manifolds
M^{2n+s}({f},\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
are defined in the same spirit
as nearly Kähler manifolds of A. Gray
[
12
]

by a constraint only on the symmetric part of
\nabla{f}
–
starting from
{\cal S}
- and
{\cal C}
- manifolds, respectively (e.g.,
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
):

(\nabla_{X}{f})X=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}g(fX,fX)\,\bar{\xi}+\bar{\eta}(X)f^{2}X\,,&\mbox{nearly\ }{\cal S}-\mbox{manifolds}.\\
0\,,&\mbox{nearly\ }{\cal C}-\mbox{manifolds}.\end{array}\right.

(1)

Here,

\bar{\eta}=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{s}\eta^{i}
,
\bar{\xi}=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{s}\xi_{i}
.
These
counterparts of nearly Kähler manifolds
play a key role in the classification of metric
f
-manifolds, see
[
2
]
.
The Reeb vector fields
\xi_{i}
of a nearly
{\cal S}
- and a nearly
{\cal C}
- structures are unit Killing vector fields.
The influence of constant-length Killing vector fields on Riemannian geometry has been studied by many authors,
e.g.,
[
16
]
.
The interest of geometers in
f
-structures is also motivated by the study of the dynamics of contact foliations.
Contact foliations generalize to higher dimensions the flow of
the Reeb vector field on contact manifolds, and
{\cal K}
-structures are particular case of uniform contact structures, see
[
17
,
18
]
.

In
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
, we introduced and studied metric structures on a smooth manifold that generalize the almost-contact, Sasakian, cosymplectic, etc., structures.
Such so-called “weak” structures (the complex structure on the contact distribution is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor)
allow us a new look at the theory of classical structures and find new applications.

In this paper, we define and study new structures of this kind.
Section
2
, following the Introduction,
recalls some results regarding
weak nearly Kähler manifolds (generalizing nearly Kähler manifolds) and metric weak
f
-manifolds.
Section
3
introduces weak nearly
{\cal S}
- and weak nearly
{\cal C}
- structures and study their geometry.
Section
4
characterizes weak nearly
{\cal C}
- and weak nearly
{\cal S}
- submanifolds in weak nearly Kähler manifolds and proves that a weak nearly
{\cal C}
- manifold with parallel Reeb vector field is locally the Riemannian product of a real line and a weak nearly Kähler manifold.
The proofs use the properties of new tensors, as well as classical constructions.

2
Preliminaries

This section reviews some knows notions and results, see
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

Recall that nearly Kähler manifolds
(M,J,g)
are defined by A. Gray
[
12
]
using the condition that only the symmetric part of
\nabla J
vanishes, in contrast to the Kähler case where
\nabla J=0
.

Definition 1
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of even dimension equipped with a skew-symmetric (1,1)-tensor
{f}

such that the tensor
{f}^{\,2}
is negative-definite is called a
weak Hermitian manifold
.
Such
(M,{f},g)
is called a
weak Kähler manifold
if
\nabla{f}=0
, where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.
A weak Hermitian manifold is called a
weak nearly Kähler manifold
,
if

(\nabla_{X}{f})Y+(\nabla_{Y}{f})X=0\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

(2)

Several authors studied the problem of finding
parallel skew-symmetric 2-tensors (different from almost-complex structures) on a Riemannian manifold
and classified such tensors, e.g.,
[
22
]
.

Definition 2
.

A
weak metric
f
-structure
on a smooth manifold
M^{2n+s}

(n,s>0)
is a set
({f},Q,{\xi_{i}},{\eta^{i}},g)
, where

{f}
is a skew-symmetric
(1,1)
-tensor of rank
2\,n
,
Q
is a self-adjoint nonsingular
(1,1)
-tensor,

{\xi_{i}}\ (1\leq i\leq s)
are orthonormal
vector fields,
{\eta^{i}}
are dual 1-forms, and
g
is a Riemannian metric on
M
, satisfying

\displaystyle{f}^{2}=-Q+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}{\eta^{i}}\otimes{\xi_{i}},\quad{\eta^{i}}({\xi_{j}})=\delta^{i}_{j},\quad Q\,{\xi_{i}}={\xi_{i}},

(3)

\displaystyle g({f}X,{f}Y)=g(X,Q\,Y)-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}{\eta^{i}}(X)\,{\eta^{i}}(Y)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

(4)

In this case,
M^{2n+s}({f},Q,{\xi_{i}},{\eta^{i}},g)
is called a
weak metric
f
-manifold
.

Assume that the distribution
{\mathcal{D}}:=\bigcap_{\,i}\ker{\eta^{i}}
is
{f}
-invariant,
thus
{\mathcal{D}}=f(TM)
,
\dim{\mathcal{D}}=2\,n
and

{f}\,{\xi_{i}}=0,\quad{\eta^{i}}\circ{f}=0,\quad\eta^{i}\circ Q=\eta^{i},\quad[Q,\,{f}]=0.

By the above,
the distribution
\ker f
is spanned by
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s}\}
.
Define a (1,1)-tensor
\tilde{Q}:=Q-{\rm id}

and note that
[\tilde{Q},{f}]=0
and
\eta^{i}\circ\widetilde{Q}=0
.
Putting
Y={\xi_{i}}
in (
4
),
we get
{\eta^{i}}(X)=g(X,{\xi_{i}})
;
thus, each
{\xi_{i}}
is orthogonal to
{\mathcal{D}}
.
Therefore,
TM
splits as complementary orthogonal sum of

{\mathcal{D}}
and
\ker f
.

A weak metric
f
-structure
({f},Q,{\xi_{i}},{\eta^{i}},g)
is said to be
normal
if the following tensor is zero:

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(1)}(X,Y)=[{f},{f}](X,Y)+2\sum\nolimits_{\,i}d{\eta^{i}}(X,Y)\,{\xi_{i}}\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

The Nijenhuis torsion
of a (1,1)-tensor
{S}
and the exterior derivative of a 1-form
{\omega}
are given by

\displaystyle[{S},{S}](X,Y)={S}^{2}[X,Y]+[{S}X,{S}Y]-{S}[{S}X,Y]-{S}[X,{S}Y]\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}),

\displaystyle d\omega(X,Y)=(1/2)\,\{X({\omega}(Y))-Y({\omega}(X))-{\omega}([X,Y])\}\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

Using the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of
g
, one can rewrite
[S,S]
as

\displaystyle[{S},{S}](X,Y)=({S}\nabla_{Y}{S}-\nabla_{{S}Y}{S})X-({S}\nabla_{X}{S}-\nabla_{{S}X}{S})Y.

(5)

The fundamental
2
-form
\Phi
on
M^{2n+s}({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
is defined by

\Phi(X,Y)=g(X,{f}Y)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

Proposition 1
.

A weak metric
f
-structure with condition
{\cal N}^{\,(1)}=0
satisfies

\displaystyle\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}{f}=d{\eta^{j}}({\xi_{i}},\cdot)=0,

(6)

\displaystyle d{\eta^{i}}({f}X,Y)-d{\eta^{i}}({f}Y,X)=\frac{1}{2}\,\eta^{i}([\widetilde{Q}X,fY]),

(7)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,\xi_{j}\in{\cal D},\quad[X,\xi_{i}]\in{\cal D}\quad(1\leq i,j\leq s,\ X\in{\cal D}).

(8)

Moreover,
\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,\xi_{j}+\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\xi_{i}=0
, that is
\ker f
is a totally geodesic distribution.

The following tensors
on a weak metric
f
-manifold are well known in the classical theory:

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(2)}_{i}(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=(\pounds_{{f}X}\,{\eta^{i}})(Y)-(\pounds_{{f}Y}\,{\eta^{i}})(X)=2\,d{\eta^{i}}({f}X,Y)-2\,d{\eta^{i}}({f}Y,X),

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(3)}_{i}(X)

\displaystyle:=(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}{f})X=[{\xi_{i}},{f}X]-{f}[{\xi_{i}},X],

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(4)}_{ij}(X)

\displaystyle:=(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}\,{\eta^{j}})(X)={\xi_{i}}({\eta^{j}}(X))-{\eta^{j}}([{\xi_{i}},X])=2\,d{\eta^{j}}({\xi_{i}},X).

Recall the co-boundary formula for exterior derivative
d
on a
2
-form
\Phi
,

\displaystyle d\Phi(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\,\big\{X\,\Phi(Y,Z)+Y\,\Phi(Z,X)+Z\,\Phi(X,Y)

\displaystyle-\Phi([X,Y],Z)-\Phi([Z,X],Y)-\Phi([Y,Z],X)\big\}.

Definition 3
.

A weak metric
f
-structure is called
a
weak almost
{\cal K}
-structure
if
d\Phi=0
.
We define its two subclasses as follows:

(i) a
weak almost
{\cal C}
-structure
if
\Phi
and
\eta^{i}\ (1\leq i\leq s)
are closed forms;

(ii) a
weak almost
{\cal S}
-structure

if
the following is valid:

\displaystyle\Phi=d{\eta^{1}}=\ldots=d{\eta^{s}}\quad({\rm hence,}\ d\Phi=0).

(9)

Adding the normality condition,
we get

weak
{\cal K}
-
,

weak
{\cal C}
-
, and
weak
{\cal S}
- structures
,
respectively.
A
weak
f
-
K
-contact structure
is
a weak almost
{\cal S}
-structure, whose structure vector fields
{\xi_{i}}
are Killing, i.e., the tensor

\displaystyle(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}\,g)(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{Y}\,{\xi_{i}},X)+g(\nabla_{X}\,{\xi_{i}},Y)

vanishes.
For
s=1
, weak (almost)
{\cal C}
- and weak (almost)
{\cal S}
- manifolds reduce to weak (almost) cosymplectic manifolds and weak (almost) Sasakian manifolds, respectively.

Remark 1
.

The almost
{\cal S}
-structure is also called an
f
-contact structure,
and the
{\cal S}
-structure is also called a normal
f
-contact structure, e.g.
[
20
]
.

For a weak almost
{\cal K}
-structure (and its special cases, a weak almost
{\cal S}
-structure and a weak almost
{\cal C}
-structure), the distribution
\ker f
is involutive (tangent to a foliation).
Moreover, a weak almost
{\cal S}
- and a weak almost
{\cal C}
- structures satisfy the following
conditions (trivial for
s=1
):

\displaystyle[\xi_{i},\xi_{j}]

\displaystyle=0,

(10)

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{i},\ \xi_{j})

\displaystyle=0\quad(X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M})

(11)

for
1\leq i,j\leq s
.
The following condition
is a corollary of (
11
):

\displaystyle\eta^{k}(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,\xi_{j})=0\quad(1\leq i,j,k\leq s).

(12)

By (
10
), the distribution
\ker f
of weak almost
{\cal S}
- and a weak almost
{\cal C}
-
manifolds
is tangent to a
\mathfrak{g}
-foliation with an abelian Lie algebra.

Remark 2

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra of dimension
s
.
A foliation of dimension
s
on a smooth connected manifold
M
is called a
\mathfrak{g}
-
foliation
if there exist
complete vector fields
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s}
on
M
which, when restricted to each leaf,
form a parallelism of this submanifold
with a Lie algebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}
.

Main Results

In this section, weak nearly
{\cal S}
- and weak nearly
{\cal C}
- structures are defined and studied, some of the statements generalize the results in
[
13
,
15
,
14
]
.
For an
{\cal S}
-structure we have, see
[
20
]
,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}{f})Y=g(fX,fY)\,\bar{\xi}+\bar{\eta}(Y)f^{2}X\,.

(13)

The restriction on the symmetric part of (
13
) gives the following.

Definition 4
.

A weak metric
f
-manifold (see Definition
2
) is called a
weak nearly
{\cal S}
-manifold
if

(\nabla_{X}{f})Y+(\nabla_{Y}{f})X=2\,g(fX,fY)\,\bar{\xi}+\bar{\eta}(X)f^{2}Y+\bar{\eta}(Y)f^{2}X

(14)

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
.
A weak metric
f
-manifold is called a
weak nearly
{\cal C}
-manifold
if

(\nabla_{X}{f})Y+(\nabla_{Y}{f})X=0.

(15)

Example 1
.

Let a Riemannian manifold
(M^{2n+s},g)
admit two nearly
{\cal S}
-structures (or, nearly
{\cal C}
-structures)

M^{2n+s}({f}_{k},Q,{\xi_{i}},{\eta^{i}},g)\ (k=1,2)

with common Reeb vector fields
\xi_{i}
and
one-forms
\eta^{i}=g(\xi_{i},\,\cdot)
.
Suppose that
{f}_{1}\neq{f}_{2}
are such that
\psi:={f}_{1}{f}_{2}+{f}_{2}{f}_{1}\neq 0
.
Then,
{f}:=(\cos t){f}_{1}+(\sin t){f}_{2}
for small
t>0

satisfies (
14
) (and (
15
), respectively) and

f^{2}=-{\rm id}+(\sin t\cos t)\,\psi+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}{\eta^{i}}\otimes{\xi_{i}}.

Thus,
({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
is a weak nearly
{\cal S}
-structure (and weak nearly
{\cal C}
-structure, respectively) on
M^{2n+s}
with

Q={\rm id}-(\sin t\cos t)\,\psi
.

The following condition is trivial when
Q={\rm id}_{\,TM}\,)
:

(\nabla_{X}\,Q)Y=0\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},\ Y\perp\ker f).

(16)

Using
(
16
), we have

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,Q)Y=\sum\nolimits_{\,i}{\eta^{i}}(Y)(\nabla_{X}\,Q)\xi_{i}=-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\eta^{i}(Y)\,\widetilde{Q}\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{i},\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

(17)

Example 2
.

To construct a weak nearly cosymplectic structure
({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
on the Riemannian product
M=\bar{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}

of a weak nearly Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{f},\bar{g})
and a Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{s},dy^{2})
, we take any point
(x,y)
of
M
and set

\xi_{i}=(0,\partial_{\,y^{i}}),\ \ \eta^{i}=(0,dy^{i}),\ \ {f}(X,\partial_{\,y^{i}})=(\bar{f}X,0),\ \ Q(X,\partial_{\,y^{i}})=(-\bar{f}^{\,2}X,\partial_{\,y^{i}}),

where
X\in T_{x}\bar{M}
.
Note that if
\overline{\nabla}_{X}\,\bar{f}^{2}=0\ (X\in T\bar{M})
, then (
16
) holds.

The following result
opens new applications to Killing vector fields.

Proposition 2
.

Both on a weak nearly
{\cal S}
-manifold and a weak nearly
{\cal C}
-manifold satisfying (
10
) and (
12
),
the distribution
\ker f
defines a flat totally geodesic foliation;
moreover, if the conditions (
11
) and (
16
) hold, then the vector fields
\xi_{i}
are Killing.

Proof.

Putting
X=\xi_{j}
and
Y=\xi_{k}
in (
14
) or (
15
), we find
(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}{f})\xi_{k}+(\nabla_{\xi_{k}}{f})\xi_{j}=0
; hence,
{f}\big(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\xi_{k}+\nabla_{\xi_{k}}\,\xi_{j}\big)=0
.
Applying
{f}
to this and using (
3
) and (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0={f}^{2}\big(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\xi_{k}+\nabla_{\xi_{k}}\,\xi_{j}\big)=-Q\big(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\xi_{k}+\nabla_{\xi_{k}}\,\xi_{j}\big).

Since the (1,1)-tensor
Q
is nonsingular, we get

\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\xi_{k}+\nabla_{\xi_{k}}\,\xi_{j}=0
. Combining this with (
10
) yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\xi_{k}=0\quad(1\leq j,k\leq s);

(18)

hence
\ker f
defines a flat totally geodesic foliation.
Next, using (
18
) we calculate

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,\eta^{j}=g(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,\xi_{j},\,\cdot)=0.

(19)

Using (
11
) and (
18
), we obtain

(\pounds_{\xi_{j}}\,g)(\xi_{k},\,\cdot)=g(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\xi_{k},\,\cdot)=0.

Taking the
\xi_{j}
-derivative of (
4
) and using (
16
)
and
\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\eta^{i}=0
, see (
19
), we find (for
Y\perp\ker f
)

\displaystyle g((\nabla_{\xi_{j}}{f})X,{f}Y)+g({f}X,(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,{f})Y)=\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,g({f}X,{f}Y)

\displaystyle=g(X,(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,Q)Y)+\sum_{\,i}\big\{(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\eta^{i})(X)\,\eta^{i}(Y)+\eta^{i}(X)(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,\eta^{i})(Y)\big\}=0.

For a weak nearly
{\cal S}
-manifold, using (
14
), (
11
) and
\eta\circ\widetilde{Q}=0
yields

\displaystyle\quad g((\nabla_{\xi_{j}}{f})X,{f}Y)+g({f}X,(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,{f})Y)

\displaystyle=-g((\nabla_{X}{f})\xi_{j},{f}Y)-g({f}X,(\nabla_{Y}{f})\xi_{j})+g(f^{2}X,\,{f}Y)+g(f^{2}Y,\,{f}X)

\displaystyle=-g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{j},{f}^{2}Y)-g({f}^{2}X,\nabla_{Y}\,\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{j},QY)+g(QX,\nabla_{Y}\,\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{j},Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}\,\xi_{j})+g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{j},\widetilde{Q}Y)+g(\widetilde{Q}X,\nabla_{Y}\,\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=(\pounds_{\xi_{j}}\,g)(X,Y)-g(\xi_{j},(\nabla_{X}\widetilde{Q})Y)-g((\nabla_{Y}\widetilde{Q})X,\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=(\pounds_{\xi_{j}}\,g)(X,Y).

Here we used
g(\xi_{j},(\nabla_{X}\widetilde{Q})Y)=0
.
Similarly, for a weak nearly
{\cal C}
-manifold, using (
15
)
yields

\displaystyle(\pounds_{\xi_{j}}\,g)(X,Y)=g((\nabla_{\xi_{j}}{f})X,{f}Y)+g({f}X,(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\,{f})Y)=0.

(20)

From (
20
),
for both cases we obtain

\pounds_{\xi_{j}}\,g=0
,
i.e.
\xi_{j}
is a Killing vector field.
∎

Theorem 1
.

There are no weak nearly
{\cal C}
-manifolds with conditions (
10
), (
11
) and (
16
), which
satisfy
\Phi=d{\eta^{1}}=\ldots=d{\eta^{s}}
, see (
9
).

Proof.

Suppose that our weak nearly
{\cal C}
-manifold satisfies (
9
).
Since also
\xi_{i}
are Killing vector fields (see Proposition
2
),
M
is a weak
f
-K-contact manifold.
By Theorem 1 in
[
21
]
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\nabla\xi_{i}=-{f}\quad(1\leq i\leq s).

(21)

By Proposition 6 in
[
21
]
, the
\xi
-sectional curvature of a weak
f
-K-contact manifold is positive, i.e.,
K(\xi_{i},X)>0\ (X\perp\ker f)
.
Thus, for any nonzero vector
X\perp\ker f
, using (
15
) and (
21
), we get

\displaystyle 0<K(\xi_{i},X)=g(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\nabla_{X}\xi_{i}-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\xi_{i}-\nabla_{[\xi_{i},X]}\xi_{i},X)

\displaystyle\quad=g(-(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}{f})X+{f}^{2}X,X)=g((\nabla_{X}{f})\xi_{i},X)-g({f}X,{f}X)

\displaystyle\quad=-g({f}\nabla_{X}\xi_{i},X)+g({f}^{2}X,X)=2\,g({f}^{2}X,X).

This contradicts the following equality:

g({f}^{2}X,X)=-g({f}X,{f}X)\leq 0
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

A weak nearly
{\cal C}
-manifold
M^{2n+s}({f},Q,{\xi_{i}},{\eta^{i}},g)
satisfies

\nabla\xi_{i}=0\quad(1\leq i\leq s)

if and only if the manifold is locally isometric to the Riemannian product of a Euclidean
s
-space and a weak nearly Kähler manifold.

Proof.

For all vector fields
X,Y
orthogonal to
\ker f
, we have

2\,d\eta^{i}(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{i},Y)-g(\nabla_{Y}\,\xi_{i},X).

(22)

Thus, if the condition
\nabla\xi_{i}=0
holds, then the contact distribution
\cal D
is integrable.
Moreover, any integral submanifold of
\cal D
is a totally geodesic submanifold.
Indeed, for
X,Y\perp\ker f
, we have

g(\nabla_{X}\,Y,\xi_{i})=-g(Y,\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{i})=0.

Since
\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,\xi_{j}=0
, by de Rham Decomposition Theorem (e.g.,
[
24
]
),
the manifold is locally the Riemannian product
\bar{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}
.
The metric weak
f
-structure induces on
\bar{M}
a weak almost-Hermitian structure, which, by these conditions, is weak nearly Kähler.

Conversely, if a weak nearly
{\cal C}
-manifold is locally the Riemannian product
\bar{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}
, where
\bar{M}
is a
weak nearly Kähler manifold and
\xi_{i}=(0,\partial_{y^{i}})
(see also Example
2
), then
d\eta^{j}(X,Y)=0\ (X,Y\bot\ker f)
.
By (
22
) and
\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,\xi_{j}=0
, we obtain
\nabla\xi_{i}=0
.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let a weak nearly
{\cal S}

(i)

The condition

for all

.

(ii)

{N}^{\,(1)}(X,Y)=2\,\Phi(\widetilde{Q}X,Y)\,\bar{\xi}
.

Proof.

(i)

We calculate, using (
5
), (
14
)
and
\eta^{j}\circ{f}=0
,

\displaystyle\eta^{j}({N}^{\,(1)}(X,Y))

\displaystyle-2\,d\eta^{j}(X,Y)=\eta^{j}([{f},{f}](X,Y))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{4.NN}}{=}\eta^{j}\big((\nabla_{{f}X}{f})Y-(\nabla_{{f}\,Y}{f})X\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{E-nS-01}}{=}\eta^{j}\big((\nabla_{X}{f})\,{f}\,Y-(\nabla_{Y}{f})\,{f}X\big)+4\,g(f^{2}X,{f}\,Y)

\displaystyle=g\big((\nabla_{X}{f}^{2})\,Y-(\nabla_{Y}{f}^{2})\,X,\,\xi_{j}\big)-4\,g(QX,{f}\,Y)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}\,\eta^{j})(Y)-(\nabla_{Y}\,\eta^{j})(X)-4\,g(QX,{f}\,Y)

\displaystyle=2\,d\eta^{j}(X,Y)-4\,g(QX,{f}\,Y).

.
Thus, if

for all

(ii)

\displaystyle=-3\,g((\nabla_{X}{f})Y,Z)-3\,g(f^{2}X,Y)\,\bar{\eta}(Z)+3\,g(f^{2}X,Z)\,\bar{\eta}(Y).

4
Submanifolds of Weak Nearly Kähler Manifolds

Here, we
study weak nearly
{\cal S}
- and weak nearly
{\cal C}
- submanifolds in a weak nearly Kähler manifold.

The second fundamental form
h
of a
submanifold
M\subset(\bar{M},\bar{g})

is related with
\overline{\nabla}
(the Levi-Civita connection of
\bar{g}
restricted to
M
)
and
\nabla
(the Levi-Civita connection of metric
g
induced on
M
via the Gauss equation) by

\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

(23)

The shape operator
A_{N}:X\mapsto-\overline{\nabla}_{X}N
of a unit normal
N
is related with
h
via the equality

\bar{g}(h(X,Y),N)=h_{N}(X,Y)=g(A_{N}(X),Y)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

A submanifold is said to be totally geodesic if
h=0
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
M^{2n+s}
be a submanifold of codimension
s
equipped with mutually orthogonal unit normals
N_{i}\ (i=1,\ldots,s)
satisfying

\displaystyle\bar{g}(\bar{f}N_{i},N_{j})=0\quad(1\leq i,j\leq s),

(24)

and induced metric
g
in a weak Hermitian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{f},\bar{g})
.
Then
M
inherits a metric weak
f
-structure
({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
given by

\displaystyle\xi_{i}=\bar{f}N_{i},\quad\eta^{i}=\bar{g}(\bar{f}N_{i},\,\cdot)\quad(i=1,\ldots,s),

\displaystyle{f}=\bar{f}+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\bar{g}(\bar{f}N_{i},\,\cdot)\,N_{i},\quad Q=-\bar{f}^{\,2}+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\bar{g}(\bar{f}^{\,2}N_{i},\,\cdot)\,N_{i}.

(25)

Moreover,
(
16
) holds on
M
, if

\bar{f}^{\,2}N_{i}\perp TM\ (1\leq i\leq s)
and

((\overline{\nabla}_{X}\,\bar{f}^{\,2})Y)^{\top}=0\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},\ Y\perp\ker f).

Proof.

Using the skew-symmetry of
\bar{f}
and (
24
), we verify (
3
):

\displaystyle{f}^{\,2}X={f}(\bar{f}X-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\bar{g}(\bar{f}X,N_{i})\,N_{i})

\displaystyle=\bar{f}\big(\bar{f}X-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\bar{g}(\bar{f}X,N_{i})\,N_{i}\big)-\bar{g}(\bar{f}(\bar{f}X-\sum\nolimits_{\,i,j}\bar{g}(\bar{f}X,N_{i})\,N_{i}),N_{j})\,N_{j}

\displaystyle=\bar{f}^{\,2}X-\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\bar{g}(\bar{f}^{\,2}N_{j},X)\,N_{j}-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\bar{g}(\bar{f}N_{i},X)\,\bar{f}N_{i}+\sum\nolimits_{\,i,j}\bar{g}(\bar{f}X,N_{i})\,\bar{g}(\bar{f}N_{i},N_{j})\,N_{j}

\displaystyle=-QX+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\eta^{i}(X)\,\xi_{i}\quad(X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

Since
\bar{f}^{\,2}
is negative-definite, for nonzero
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
we obtain
\bar{g}(N_{i},X)=0
and

g(QX,X)=\bar{g}(-\bar{f}^{\,2}X+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\bar{g}(\bar{f}^{\,2}N_{i},X)\,N_{i},\,X)=-\bar{g}(\bar{f}^{\,2}X,X)>0,

hence, the tensor
Q
is positive-definite on
TM
.
Then we calculate
(\nabla_{X}Q)Y
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
and
Y\perp\ker f
, using (
23
) and (
1
)
and the condition
\bar{f}^{2}N_{i}\perp TM\ (1\leq i\leq s)
:

\displaystyle\quad(\nabla_{X}\,Q)Y=\nabla_{X}(QY)-Q(\nabla_{X}Y)

\displaystyle=\big\{\overline{\nabla}_{X}\big(-\bar{f}^{2}\,Y+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}g(\bar{f}^{2}N_{i},Y)N_{i}\big)-h(X,QY)+\bar{f}^{2}\big(\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y-h(X,Y)\big)

\displaystyle-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}g\big(\bar{f}^{2}N_{i},\,\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y-h(X,Y)\big)N_{i}\big\}^{\top}

\displaystyle=(-(\overline{\nabla}_{X}(\bar{f}^{2}Y))+\bar{f}^{2}(\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y))^{\top}-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}g\big(\bar{f}^{2}N_{i},\,Y)\,A_{N_{i}}X

\displaystyle=-((\overline{\nabla}_{X}\bar{f}^{2})Y)^{\top},

where
⊤
is the
TM
-component of a vector.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Let
M^{2n+s}
be a submanifold of codimension
s
equipped with mutually orthogonal unit normals
N_{i}\ (i=1,\ldots,s)

satisfying (
24
) in a weak nearly Kähler manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{f},\bar{g})
.
If the induced metric weak
f
-structure
({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
on
M
satisfies

\displaystyle h_{N_{i}}(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})=h_{N_{j}}(\xi_{i},\xi_{k})\quad(1\leq i,j\leq s),

(26)

and

has
the following second fundamental forms

:

\displaystyle(i)\ h_{N_{i}}(X,Y)=g(-f^{2}X,\,Y)+\sum\nolimits_{\,j,k}h_{N_{i}}(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})\,\eta^{j}(X)\,\eta^{k}(Y),

\displaystyle(ii)\ h_{N_{i}}(X,Y)=\sum\nolimits_{\,j,k}h_{N_{i}}(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})\,\eta^{j}(X)\,\eta^{k}(Y),

(27)

then

({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
is

(i)

a weak nearly
{\cal S}
-structure;

(ii)

a weak nearly
{\cal C}
-structure.

Proof.

Substituting

\bar{f}\,Y={f}\,Y-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\bar{g}(\bar{f}N_{i},Y)\,N_{i}={f}\,Y-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\eta^{i}(Y)\,N_{i}

in

\displaystyle\quad(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\bar{f})Y=\overline{\nabla}_{X}(\bar{f}\,Y)-\bar{f}(\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}{f})Y+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\big\{\eta^{i}(Y)A_{N_{i}}X-h_{N_{i}}(X,Y)\,\xi_{i}\big\}

\displaystyle+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\big\{X(\eta^{i}(Y))-\eta^{i}(\nabla_{X}Y)+h_{N_{i}}(X,{f}Y)\big\}N_{i}.

(28)

\displaystyle(i)\ A_{N_{i}}X=-f^{2}X+\sum\nolimits_{\,j,k}h_{N_{i}}(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})\,\eta^{j}(X)\,\xi_{k},

\displaystyle(ii)\ A_{N_{i}}X=\sum\nolimits_{\,j,k}h_{N_{i}}(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})\,\eta^{j}(X)\,\xi_{k}.

(i) If we have a weak nearly

Remark 3
.

If an ambient manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{f},\bar{g})

5
Conclusions

We have shown that weak nearly
\cal S
- and weak nearly
\cal C
- structures are useful tools for studying
metric
f
-structures and Killing vector fields.
Some classical results have been extended in this paper to weak nearly
\cal S
- and weak nearly
\cal C
- manifolds.
Based on the numerous applications of nearly Kähler, nearly Sasakian and nearly cosymplectic structures,
we expect that
weak nearly Kähler,
\cal S
- and
\cal C
- structures will also be useful for differential geometry and theoretical physics.

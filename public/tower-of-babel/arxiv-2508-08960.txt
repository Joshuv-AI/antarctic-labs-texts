Abstract
. We show that given a monadically stable theory T T , a sufficiently saturated 𝐌 ⊧ T \mathbf{M}\models T , and a coherent system of probability measures on the σ \sigma -algebras generated by parameter-definable sets of 𝐌 \mathbf{M} in each dimension, we may produce a totally Borel 𝐁 ≺ 𝐌 \mathbf{B}\prec\mathbf{M} realizing these measures. Our main application is to prove that every FO -convergent sequence of structures (with countable signature) from a monadically stable class admits a modeling limit. As another consequence, we prove a Borel removal lemma for monadically stable Lebesgue relational structures.



1.
Introduction

The study of graph limits, and limits of more general structures, has attracted considerable interest over the last two decades, since the seminal papers introducing the notions of left and local limits
[
2
,
8
,
5
,
11
]
.
While the notion of
left limit
is based on homomorphism counting and applies to general graphs (with non-trivial limits when the graphs are dense), the notion of
local limit
was introduced for sequences of graphs with bounded degree. These two frameworks lead to seemingly essentially different analytic limit objects, the
graphons
, which are measurable functions from
[0,1]\times[0,1]
to
[0,1]
(up to measure preserving transformations) and
graphings
, which are Borel graphs statisfying the so-called
Mass Transport Principle
.

Several approaches have been considered to extend these frameworks
[
3
,
7
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
22
]
.
From a model-theoretical point of view, a natural generalization of both left limits and local limits is the notion of
structural limit

[
21
]
. In this setting, a fragment
X
of first-order logic is fixed, and a sequence
(\mathbf{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of structures is said to be
X
-convergent
if, for every formula
\varphi
in
X
, the probability
\langle\varphi,\mathbf{M}_{n}\rangle
that the formula
\varphi
is satisfied in
\mathbf{M}_{n}
converges (the free variables of
\varphi
being interpreted as uniform and independent random elements of the domain).

FO
-convergence (where
FO
denotes the full class of all first-order formulas) is a stronger notion of convergence than both left convergence (equivalent to
QF
-convergence, where
QF
denotes the fragment of quantifier-free formulas) and local convergence (equivalent to
{\sf FO}_{1}^{\rm local}
-convergence on bounded degree graph sequences, where
{\sf FO}_{1}^{\rm local}
denotes the fragment of local formulas with a single free variable).

While the limit of an
\sf X
-convergent sequence can always be represented as a probability distribution over the Stone space dual to the Boolean algebra defined by
{\sf X}

[
26
]
, it can also sometimes be represented by means of an analytic object, called a
modeling
, which has stronger properties than mere Borel structures.
A
totally Borel structure
is a structure whose domain is a standard Borel space, with the property that every definable set is Borel (in the appropriate product space).
A
modeling

\mathbf{M}
is a totally Borel structure equipped with a Borel probability measure
\nu
on its domain.
Note that, for every modeling
\mathbf{M}
and every formula
\varphi
(with
p
free variables), the probability
\langle\varphi,\mathbf{M}\rangle
of satisfaction of
\varphi
in
\mathbf{M}
is given by

\langle\varphi,\mathbf{M}\rangle=\nu^{\otimes p}(\varphi(\mathbf{M}))=\idotsint 1_{\varphi}(v_{1},\dots,v_{p})\,{\rm d}\nu(v_{1})\dots{\rm d}\nu(v_{p}),

where
1_{\varphi}(v_{1},\dots,v_{p})=1
if
\mathbf{M}\models\varphi(v_{1},\dots,v_{p})
and
1_{\varphi}(v_{1},\dots,v_{p})=0
, otherwise.

Based on these definitions, we say that a modeling
\mathbf{L}

is a
modeling
X
-limit
of an
X
-convergent sequence
(\mathbf{M}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of structures if, for every
\varphi\in{\sf X}
, it holds that

\langle\varphi,\mathbf{L}\rangle=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\langle\varphi,\mathbf{M}_{i}\rangle.

A natural problem is to determine when an
X
-convergent sequence of graphs (or structures) admits a modeling
X
-limit. In general, in order to have such a property, we either consider a sequence of graphs from a restricted class of graphs, or we consider a fragment more restricted than
{\sf FO}

[
12
,
13
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
.

When one considers
FO
-convergence of sequences of graphs taken from a fixed monotone (i.e. closed on subgraphs) class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
, a solution to this problem was announced in
[
25
]
, however the proof contains a gap, see
[
27
]
. (In the next statement, the equivalence of the second and the third item follows from
[
1
]
.)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a monotone class of graphs (or, more generally, of binary structures). Then the following properties are equivalent:

(1)

every
FO
-convergent sequence of graphs in
\mathscr{C}
has a modeling
FO
-limit;

(2)

the class
\mathscr{C}
is nowhere dense;

(3)

the class
\mathscr{C}
is monadically stable.

In this paper, we use a different approach to address the problem of the existence of a modeling limit, i.e.
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
in the above Theorem. In fact we prove a more general result. Note that moving from the setting of monotone classes to hereditary classes, monadic stability becomes a more general notion than nowhere denseness.

The main tool we develop here is a way to embed a modeling in any sufficiently saturated model of a monadically stable theory equipped with a coherent system of measures on parameter-definable sets in every dimension (see Section
2.3
), as stated in the next theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T
be a monadically stable theory in a countable language, and let
\mathbf{M}
be a measurable
2^{\aleph_{0}}
-saturated model of
T
.

Then, there exists a modeling
\mathbf{B}
and an elementary embedding
\zeta:\mathbf{B}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{M}
, such that for every formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
and every
\bar{b}\subset\mathbf{B}
, the measure of
\varphi(\mathbf{B},\bar{b})
equals the measure
of
\varphi(\mathbf{M},\zeta(\bar{b}))
.

This theorem is not inherently about modeling limits, and applies also to measures that do not arise from the pseudofinite counting measure on a sequence of finite structures. However, our main application of this theorem is a proof of the existence of modeling
FO
-limits in the more general setting of monadically stable classes. Particularly, this implies and generalizes Theorem 1.1.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\sigma
be a countable signature, and
let
\mathscr{C}
be a monadically stable class of
\sigma
-structures. Then, every
FO
-convergent sequence of
\sigma
-structures in
\mathscr{C}
has a modeling
FO
-limit.

As a further product of our construction, we obtain the following “removal lemma”.

Theorem 1.4

(Borel removal lemma for monadically stable structures)
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be a monadically stable Lebesgue relational structure (a structure on the standard probability space
[0,1]
, with all atomic relations Lebesgue measurable) in a countable language.
Then, there exists a Borel structure
\mathbf{B}
, such that, for every quantifier-free formula
\varphi
, with
\varphi\rightarrow\bigwedge_{i<j<k}(x_{i}\neq x_{j})
we have

\displaystyle\langle\varphi,\mathbf{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi,\mathbf{L}\rangle,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathbf{B})\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\langle\varphi,\mathbf{B}\rangle>0.

This theorem is motivated by the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.5

(Lovász, Szegedy)
.

For every Lebesgue graph
\mathbf{L}
(a graph on the standard probability space
[0,1]
whose edge set is Lebesgue-measurable) there exists a Borel graph
\mathbf{B}
, such that for every finite graph
F
, the density of
F
in
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{B}
are equal, and every finite induced subgraph of
\mathbf{B}
has a positive density in
\mathbf{B}
.

The original conjecture was expressed in terms of random-free graphons (“Every random-free graphon
W
is equivalent to a Borel graph
\mathbf{G}
such that every finite induced subgraph of
\mathbf{G}
has a positive density in
W
”). Note that the conjecture is known to hold if one only requires
\mathbf{B}
being Lebesgue.

1.1.
Proof sketch and structure of the paper

We now roughly outline the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

In order to construct the modeling
\mathbf{B}
in Theorem
1.2
, we assume that the given measurable model
\mathbf{M}
has a distinguished countable elementary submodel
\mathbf{N}
(to be chosen later), and concern ourselves with getting the right measure on
\mathbf{N}
-definable subsets of
\mathbf{M}
. The measure in
\mathbf{M}
on such sets induces a measure on the space
S_{1}(\mathbf{N})
of 1-types over
\mathbf{N}
. Our first goal is to build the totally Borel
\mathbf{B}
over
\mathbf{N}
such that it contains a Borel set
A
realizing every 1-type over
\mathbf{N}
, and so that all these realizations are forking-independent. We then aim to transport the measure on
S_{1}(\mathbf{N})
to
A
, where all of the measure of
\mathbf{B}
will be concentrated; this is complicated by the fact that the topology on
A
is finer than on
S_{1}(\mathbf{N})
, but can be done as a consequence of a general selection theorem.

At this point, we have ensured that
\mathbf{B}
gives the right measure to definable sets in dimension one, and we now want to argue that this ensures us to give the right measure to definable sets in all dimensions. This is where the choice of the countable
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{M}
becomes relevant. The structure theory for monadic stability shows that over any set, forking-dependence defines an equivalence relation on singletons. We show that we can choose
\mathbf{N}
so that each class of this equivalence relation over
\mathbf{N}
has measure 0 in
\mathbf{M}
. Thus, in
\mathbf{M}
, a random
k
-tuple will almost surely consist of points that are forking-independent over
\mathbf{N}
. The same is true in
\mathbf{B}
, since we have concentrated all our measure on the forking-independent set
A
.

Stability theory (in particular, stationarity of types over models) then shows that if we have a
k
-tuple that is forking-independent over
\mathbf{N}
, the relations that hold of it are determined by the 1-types over
\mathbf{N}
of the individual points. Since we have given the correct measure to 1-types, this shows we must also give the correct measure to
k
-types, and thus to definable sets in dimension
k
, finishing the proof of
1.3
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
, we apply Theorem
1.2
to the case where
\mathbf{M}
is obtained as an ultraproduct of the given
FO
-convergent sequence, equipped with the Loeb measure.

After general preliminaries in Section
2
and further preliminaries about type spaces in Section
3
, we show that we can build the totally Borel model nicely realizing all 1-types over any countable
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{M}
in Section
4
. Then we show that we can choose
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{M}
as desired in Section
5
. Finally, in Section
6
, we transport the measure from
\mathbf{M}
to
\mathbf{B}
and show that giving the right measures in dimension one lifts to all dimensions. This proves Theorem
1.2
, and Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
quickly follow. Further conjectures are given in Section
7
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Measure theory

Given a measure space
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
,
a
\mu
-null set

X
is a
\mu
-measurable subset of
X
with
\mu
-measure
0
.

Given two measurable spaces
(X_{1},\Sigma_{1})

and
(X_{2},\Sigma_{2})
, a measurable mapping
f:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
, and a measure

\mu:\Sigma_{1}\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
, the
pushforward
of
\mu
by
f
is the measure
f_{*}(\mu)
defined by

f_{*}(\mu)(X)=\mu(f^{-1}(X)),

for all
X\in\Sigma_{2}
.

A
Polish space

X
is a separable completely metrizable topological space.
A measurable space
(X,\Sigma)

is a
standard Borel space
if
X
is a Polish space and

\Sigma
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
X
. According to Kuratowski’s theorem, every standard Borel space is Borel isomorphic to one of
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{Z}
, or a finite discrete space.

A
Borel isomorphism
is a measurable bijective function between two standard Borel spaces whose inverse is also measurable.

Theorem 2.1

(Lusin-Suslin; see
[
14
, Corollary 15.2]
)
.

Every Borel bijection between standard Borel spaces has a Borel inverse, hence is a Borel isomorphism.

Measurable mappings between standard Borel spaces allow us to pull back a Borel measure, as stated in the next theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
6
, Theorem 9.1.5]
, stated for standard Borel spaces)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be standard Borel spaces and let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a Borel mapping such that
f(X)=Y
. Then, for every Borel measure
\nu
on
Y
, there exists a Borel measure
\mu
on
X
such that
\nu=f_{*}(\mu)
.

Let
(X,\Sigma)
be a measurable space, and let
\mu
be a measure on this space. For a positive integer
k
, we denote by
\Sigma^{k}
the
product sigma algebra
, that is the sigma algebra on
X^{k}
generated by the subsets of the form
\prod_{i<k}B_{i},
where
B_{i}\in\Sigma
for all
i<k
. The
product measure

\mu^{\otimes k}
is defined as a measure on
(X^{k},\Sigma^{k})
such that
\mu^{\otimes k}(\prod_{i<k}B_{i})=\prod_{i<k}\mu(B_{i})
for all

B_{0},\dots,B_{k-1}\in\Sigma
. If
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite (in particular, when
\mu
is a probability measure), then the product measure
\mu^{\otimes k}
is uniquely defined and, for every measurable
f
we have (Fubini–Tonelli theorem):

\int f(\bar{x})\,{\rm d}\mu^{\otimes k}(\bar{x})=\idotsint f(x_{0},\dots,x_{k-1})\ {\rm d}\mu(x_{0})\dots{\rm d}\mu(x_{k-1}).

2.2.
Model theory

Let
\sigma
be a countable signature.
We define
{\sf FO}[\sigma]
as the set of all first-order formulas in the language of
\sigma
-structures, where the free variables belong to the set
\set{x_{i}}{i<\omega}
. For
k\leq\omega
, we further define
{\sf FO}_{k}[\sigma]
as the subset of
{\sf FO}[\sigma]
with all the formulas whose free variables belong to
\set{x_{i}}{i<k}
. In particular,
{\sf FO}_{0}[\sigma]
is the set of all the sentences in the language of
\sigma
-structures, and
{\sf FO}_{\omega}[\sigma]={\sf FO}[\sigma]
. When the signature
\sigma
is implicit, we use the simplified notations
{\sf FO}
and
{\sf FO}_{k}
in place of
{\sf FO}[\sigma]
and
{\sf FO}_{k}[\sigma]
.

For every
k\leq\omega
, the quotient of
{\sf FO}_{k}
by the relation of logical equivalence defines a countable Boolean algebra. The Stone dual of this Boolean algebra is denoted by

S({\sf FO}_{k})
. The points of
S({\sf FO}_{k})
are the maximal consistent subsets of formulas in
{\sf FO}_{k}
, which are called
complete theories
if
k=0
, and a
complete
k
-types
if
k>0
.
The space
S({\sf FO}_{k})
is endowed with a topology, whose basis is its set of clopen sets (indexed by formulas in
{\sf FO}_{k}
)

\clop(\varphi):=\set{\tau\in S({\sf FO}_{k})}{\varphi\in\tau}.

With this topology,
S({\sf FO}_{k})
is a Polish space (as the signature
\sigma
is assumed to be countable). Hence, with the associated Borel sigma-algebra,
S({\sf FO}_{k})
is a
standard Borel space
.

For a theory
T
, we define

\displaystyle S_{k}(T)

\displaystyle=\set{p\in S({\sf FO}_{k})}{T\subseteq p}.

Note that
S_{k}(T)
is a closed subset of
S({\sf FO}_{k})
since
S_{k}(T)=\bigcap_{\varphi\in T}\clop(\varphi)
.

Given a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
, an ordinal
\alpha\leq\omega
, and a tuple
\bar{a}=(a_{i}:i<\alpha)
of elements of
\mathbf{M}
, i.e.
\bar{a}\in M^{k}
, we define the
type
of
\bar{a}
in
\mathbf{M}
to be the
set of all formulas
\varphi\in{\sf FO}_{\alpha}
such that

\mathbf{M}
satisfies
\varphi
when
x_{i}
is interpreted as
a_{i}
(for all
i<\alpha
). Given
\mathbf{M}\models T
and
k\leq\omega
, we let
\tp^{k}\colon\mathbf{M}^{k}\to S_{k}(T)
be the function sending a
k
-tuple of elements of
\mathbf{M}
to its
k
-type. In order to simplify notation, we will often omit the
k
, so
\tp(\bar{a})
will denote

\tp^{|\bar{a}|}(\bar{a})
.

Also, given a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
, the
theory of
\mathbf{M}
, denoted
\Th(\mathbf{M})
, is the set of
\sigma
-sentences satisfied by
\mathbf{M}
.

We say that a substructure
\mathbf{N}\subset\mathbf{M}
is an
elementary substructure
, denoted
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{M}
, if for every formula
\phi(\bar{x})
and
\bar{n}\subset N
, we have
\mathbf{N}\models\phi(\bar{n})\iff\mathbf{M}\models\phi(\bar{n})
. In particular,
\mathbf{N}\models\Th(\mathbf{M})
. The
Tarski-Vaught criterion
says that
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{M}
if, for every formula

\phi(x,\bar{y})
and every
\bar{n}\subset N
,
\mathbf{M}\models\exists x\ \phi(x,\bar{n})
implies that there is some
a\in\mathbf{N}
such that
\mathbf{M}\models\phi(a,\bar{n})
.

Given a structure
\mathbf{M}
and
A\subset M
, we let
\mathbf{M}_{A}
be the expanded structure in which all elements of
A
are named by constants. Given a cardinal
\kappa
,
\mathbf{M}
is
\kappa
-saturated
if for every
A\subset M
with
|A|<\kappa
, every type in
S(\Th(\mathbf{M}_{A}))
is realized by some tuple in
\mathbf{M}_{A}
.

2.3.
Measurable structures

A
measurable

\sigma
-structure is a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
equipped, for each integer
k
, with a sigma algebra
\Sigma_{k}
and a probability measure
\nu_{k}
on
M^{k}
, such that

•

every definable subset of
M^{k}
is
\mu_{k}
-measurable;

•

the probability measures
\mu_{k}
satisfy a Fubini–Tonelli like property: for every
\mu_{k}
-measurable function
f
on
M^{k}
, and every permutation
\rho
on
\{0,\dots,k-1\}
we have

\int f(\bar{x})\,{\rm d}\nu_{k}(\bar{x})=\idotsint f(x_{0},\dots,x_{k-1})\ {\rm d}\nu_{1}(x_{\rho(0)})\dots{\rm d}\nu_{1}(x_{\rho(k-1)}).

An important example of a measurable
\sigma
-structure
is given by the ultraproduct
\prod_{U}\mathbf{M}_{n}
of finite structures with uniform probability measure. In this case, the probability measures
\nu_{k}
are obtained from the product probability measures of the finite structures using the ultraproduct construction of finite probability measure spaces
[
19
]
. We call these measures the
Loeb measures
of the ultraproduct
\prod_{U}\mathbf{M}_{n}
.

In contrast with this complex structure,
a
totally Borel model
is a
\sigma
-structure, whose domain is a standard Borel space with the property that every definable set is Borel, and
a
modeling

\mathbf{M}
is a totally Borel
\sigma
-structure endowed with a Borel probability measure
\nu
.
Every modeling, equipped with the product Borel sigma algebras and the product probability measures
\nu^{\otimes k}
is a measurable
\sigma
-structure. As shown in
[
15
]
, an ultraproduct equipped with the Loeb measures is far from being a totally Borel model.

2.4.
Structural convergence

To a finite
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
and a formula
\psi\in{\sf FO}
, we define
\langle\psi,\mathbf{M}\rangle
as the probability that
\mathbf{M}
satisfies
\psi
for an interpretation of the free variables by random elements of
\mathbf{M}
chosen uniformly and independently. (In the case where
\psi
is a sentence, then
\langle\psi,\mathbf{M}\rangle
is
1
if
\mathbf{M}
satisfies
\psi
, and
0
otherwise.)
For
p\leq\omega
, every finite
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{M}
injectively defines a unique measure
\mu_{p}^{\mathbf{M}}
on
S({\sf FO}_{p})
such that
\mu_{p}^{\mathbf{M}}(\clop(\psi))=\langle\psi,\mathbf{M}\rangle
.

A sequence
(\mathbf{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of finite
\sigma
-structures is
FO
-convergent
if, for every
\psi\in{\sf FO}
, the sequence
(\langle\psi,\mathbf{M}_{n}\rangle)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a limit.
If
(\mathbf{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
FO
-convergent, then for each
p\leq\omega
, the probability measures
\mu_{p}^{\mathbf{M}_{n}}
converge weakly to a probability measure
\mu_{p}
such that, for every
\psi\in{\sf FO}_{p}
, we have

\mu_{p}(\clop(\psi))=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\psi,\mathbf{M}_{n}\rangle.

This notion was introduced in
[
21
]
.

A
modeling
{\sf FO}
-limit
of an
FO
-convergent sequence
(\mathbf{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of finite
\sigma
-structures is a modeling
\mathbf{L}
, such that

\langle\psi,\mathbf{L}\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\psi,\mathbf{M}_{n}\rangle.

The existence of modeling
{\sf FO}
-limits has been proved for sequences for several restricted classes of graphs (bounded height trees
[
26
]
, trees
[
23
]
, mappings
[
24
]
, etc.), and eventually for nowhere dense classes
[
25
]
. However, the proof of

[
25
]
is flawed in the crucial implication
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
, see
[
27
]
. In this paper, we use different arguments to give a proof of a generalization of this later result to all monadically stable classes of finite relational structures with countable signature.

2.5.
Stability, monadic stability, and forking

Here we recall the notions (monadic) stability and of forking independence, our main technical tool. Forking independence gives a notion of independence between two sets over a third, generalizing, for example, linear independence in vector spaces. For another example, in graphs of bounded degree, sets
A
and
B
are forking-independent over
C
if every connected component containing a vertex from both
A
and
B
also contains a vertex from
C
.
Since we will always be working over a model named by constants, the base set
C
will not explicitly appear in our presentation.

Definition 2.3
.

A (possibly incomplete) theory
T
is
unstable
if there is a formula
\phi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
and some
\mathbf{M}\models T
containing tuples
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in\omega),(\bar{b}_{j}:j\in\omega)
such that
\mathbf{M}\models\phi(\bar{a}_{i};\bar{b}_{j})\iff i\leq j
. Otherwise,
T
is
stable
.

A theory
T
is
monadically stable
if every theory obtained by expanding models of
T
by any number of unary predicates remains stable.

Given a class
\mathscr{C}
of structures in a fixed language, we say that
\mathscr{C}
is (monadically) stable
if
T_{\mathscr{C}}=\bigcap_{\mathbf{M}\in\mathscr{C}}\Th(\mathbf{M})
is.

In the finite combinatorics literature, it is common to define monadically stable classes as those that do not transduce the class
\mathcal{LO}
of finite linear orders. We will not use this, but define it nevertheless.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a class of structures in a fixed language
\mathcal{L}
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{+}
be the expansion of
\mathcal{L}
by countably many unary predicates, and let
\mathscr{C}^{+}
be the class of all
\mathcal{L}^{+}
structures whose
\mathcal{L}
-reduct is in
\mathscr{C}
. We say
\mathscr{C}
transduces
\mathcal{LO}
if there is some
\mathcal{L}^{+}
-formula
\phi(x;y)
(on singletons) and structures
(\mathbf{M}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}^{+}:i\in\omega)
such that
\mathbf{M}_{i}
contains elements
(a_{j}^{i}:j\in[i])
satisfying
\mathbf{M}_{i}\models\phi(a^{i}_{j};a^{i}_{k})\iff j\leq k
.

It is easy to see that if
\mathscr{C}
transduces
\mathcal{LO}
, then it is not monadically stable. The converse follows from
[
9
, Lemma 4.4]
and (the proof of)
[
4
, Lemma 8.1.3]
.

We now recall some facts about forking in stable and monadically stable theories that we will need. For these facts, we work in a large saturated model
\mathbf{U}
(for us, greater than
2^{\aleph_{0}}
) of a stable theory
T
, and all sets and models we consider have cardinality less than
\mathbf{U}
(at most
2^{\aleph_{0}}
). We will also only ever consider forking over some fixed countable
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{U}
with all elements named by constants, which will simplify our exposition. We emphasize that even our definition of forking below, which is nonstandard, makes use of our assumption that the constants name a model. A more general exposition can be found e.g. in
[
29
, Chapters 8-9]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\phi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
be a partitioned formula (i.e. we have partitioned the free variables into the two tuples
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
). Given
\bar{a}\subset U
, the
\phi
-type of
\bar{a}
, denoted
\tp_{\phi}(\bar{a})
, is the set of formulas
\set{\phi(\bar{x};\bar{n})}{\bar{n}\in N^{|\bar{y}|},\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{a};\bar{n})}
.

The space of
\phi
-types, denoted
S_{\phi}(T)
, is the set of all
\phi
-types realized in
\mathbf{U}
.

We also let
\phi^{*}(\bar{x};\bar{y}):=\phi(\bar{y};\bar{x})
. Thus the
\phi^{*}
-type of
\bar{b}
, denoted
\tp_{\phi^{*}}(\bar{b})
, is
\set{\phi(\bar{n};\bar{y})}{\bar{n}\in N^{|\bar{x}|},\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{n};\bar{b})}
, and we also have the corresponding space
S_{\phi^{*}}(T)
.

Fact 2.6

(Definability of
\phi
-types, see e.g.
[
29
, Theorem 8.3.1]
)
.

Let
T
be a stable theory. Given a formula
\phi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
, and a tuple
\bar{a}\in\mathbf{U}^{|\bar{x}|}
, there is a formula
\psi(\bar{y})
that is a boolean combination of formulas of the form
\phi(\bar{n}_{i};\bar{y})
with each
\bar{n}_{i}\subset N
, such that for all
\bar{n}^{\prime}\subset N
we have
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{a};\bar{n}^{\prime})\leftrightarrow\psi(\bar{n}^{\prime})
.

Remark 2.7
.

Note that the formula
\psi(\bar{y})
depends only on
\tp_{\phi}(\bar{a})
.

Also, although we will not use this, one can prove that the formula
\psi(\bar{y})
can be chosen to be a “majority vote” of instances of
\phi
, i.e.
\psi
uses constants
\bar{n}=\bar{n}_{0}\dots\bar{n}_{k}
and
\psi(\bar{y})
says that
\phi(\bar{n}_{i};\bar{y})
holds for the majority of
0\leq i\leq k
.

While the formula
\psi(\bar{y})
above determines whether whether
\phi(\bar{a};\bar{y})
holds for tuples in
N
, one should think that it also generically determines whether
\phi(\bar{a};\bar{y})
holds even for tuples outside of
\mathbf{N}
. The notion of forking we now define describes when a formula implies that any tuple satisfying it does not follow this generic behavior. Returning to our earlier examples, a “generic point” in an infinite-dimensional vector space will not be in the span of some fixed finite set, and so any formula
\phi(x;\bar{b})
expressing
x
as a non-zero linear combination of elements from
\bar{b}
will fork. Similarly, in a bounded-degree graph (with infinitely many connected components), a “generic point” will not be in any of the components of some fixed finite set.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
T
be a stable theory. Fix a formula
\phi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
and a tuple
\bar{b}\in\mathbf{U}^{|\bar{y}|}
. We say
\phi(\bar{x};\bar{b})

forks
if there is some formula
\phi^{\prime}(\bar{b};\bar{x})
and corresponding (to
\phi^{\prime}
) formula
\psi^{\prime}(\bar{x})
as in Fact
2.6
such that
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{x};\bar{b})\rightarrow(\phi^{\prime}(\bar{b};\bar{x})\leftrightarrow\neg\psi^{\prime}(\bar{x}))
.

Note that there may be several possibilities for formula
\psi^{\prime}(\bar{x})
, and implicit in this definition is that it does not depend on which one we choose.

For example, in a bounded-degree graph, let
b\in\mathbf{U}\setminus\mathbf{N}
, and let
\phi(x;b)
say that
x
is at distance at most
k
from
b
for some fixed
k
. We show that
\phi
forks. We let
\phi^{\prime}(b;x)
be
\phi(x;b)
. Since
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{U}
, it must be a union of connected components, and so no
n\in\mathbf{N}
satisfies
\phi^{\prime}
; thus we may take
\psi^{\prime}(x)
to be
x\neq x
. The case of vector spaces where
\phi(x;\bar{b})
expresses
x
as a non-zero linear combination of elements from
\bar{b}\subset\mathbf{U}\setminus\mathbf{N}
is similar, since
\mathbf{N}
must be a subspace and so no
n\in\mathbf{N}
will satisfy
\phi
.

Given a tuple
\bar{a}
, we now say that
\bar{a}
forks with
\bar{b}
(or that
\bar{a}
and
\bar{b}
are forking-dependent
) if there is some
\phi(\bar{x};\bar{b})\in\tp(\bar{a}/\bar{b})
that forks. We write this as
\bar{a}\nind\bar{b}
, and that
\bar{a}
does not fork with
\bar{b}
(or that
\bar{a}
and
\bar{b}
are forking-independent
) as
\bar{a}\ind\bar{b}
. Note that whether
\bar{a}\nind\bar{b}
depends only on
\tp(\bar{a}\bar{b})
. Finally, given sets
A,B
, we say that
A
forks with
B
, denoted as
A\nind B
, if there are finite tuples
\bar{a}\subset A,\bar{b}\subset B
such that
\bar{a}\nind\bar{b}
.
A\ind B
is defined analogously.

More particularly, we might say that
\bar{a}
forks with
\bar{b}

via the formula
\phi
if
\phi(\bar{x};\bar{b})\in\tp(\bar{a}/\bar{b})
forks.

Definition 2.9

(Finite satisfiability)
.

Given a tuple
\bar{a}
and sets
A\subset B
, we say that
\tp(\bar{a}/B)
is
finitely satisfiable in
A
if for every formula
\phi(\bar{x})
(with parameters from
B
) in
\tp(\bar{a}/B)
, there is some
\bar{a}^{\prime}\subset A
such that
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{a}^{\prime})
;

We recall the following facts. Stationarity and the bound on
\phi
-types follow easily from definability of
\phi
-types and our definition of forking. For the other facts, see e.g.
[
29
, Chapter 8]
.

Fact 2.10
.

Let
T
be a stable theory. The relation
\ind
satisfies the following properties, for all subsets
A,B
of
\mathbf{U}
and tuples
\bar{a},\bar{b}\subset U
:

(1)

(Symmetry:)

A\ind B\iff B\ind A
;

(2)

(Finite satisfiability)
\bar{a}\ind B
if and only if
\tp(\bar{a}/NB)
is finitely satisfiable in
N

(3)

(Stationarity) If
\bar{a}\ind\bar{b}
, then whether
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{a};\bar{b})
is determined by
\tp_{\phi}(\bar{a})
and
\tp_{\phi^{*}}(\bar{b})
. Thus, if
\bar{a}^{\prime}\models\tp_{\phi}(\bar{a})
and
\bar{b}^{\prime}\models\tp_{\phi^{*}}(\bar{b})
but
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{a};\bar{b})\leftrightarrow\neg\phi(\bar{a}^{\prime};\bar{b}^{\prime})
, then
\bar{a}^{\prime}\nind\bar{b}^{\prime}
;

(4)

(Bound on
\phi
-types) For every formula
\phi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
,
|S_{\phi}(T)|\leq\aleph_{0}
;

(5)

(Extension) Given
p=\tp(\bar{a}/A)
and
B\supset A
, there is some
\bar{a}^{\prime}\models p
such that
\bar{a}^{\prime}\ind B

Fact 2.11

(
[
4
, Lemma 4.2.6]
)
.

Let
T
be monadically stable. Then forking has the following additional properties.

(1)

(Total triviality) If
A\nind B
, then there is some
a\in A
and some
b\in B
such that
a\nind b
.

(2)

(Transitivity on singletons) If
a\nind b
and
b\nind c
then
a\nind c
.

Facts
2.10
(1) and
2.11
(2) imply that in monadically stable theories, forking (over
N
) defines an equivalence relation on the complement of
N
. Furthermore, by Fact
2.11
(1), two sets fork if and only if there is some class of this equivalence relation that they both intersect. Also, Fact
2.10
(3) shows that the relations between distinct classes are controlled by how those classes individually relate to
N
.

3.
Grounded theories and Tarski-Vaught types

As discussed in Section
2.5
, our presentation is smoother if we work over a model named by constants. We now formalize this in the definition of
grounded theories
, and introduce some important type spaces for a grounded theory.

Definition 3.1
.

Assume that
\sigma
is a countable signature and
T
is a complete theory in the first-order language of
\sigma
-structures.
We say that
T
is
grounded
if, for every formula of the form
(\exists x)\,\varphi(x)
in
T
there exists a constant
c\in\sigma
such that
\varphi(c)\in T
.

Note that this property is equivalent (as a direct consequence of the Tarski–Vaught criterion) to the property that for every model
\mathbf{M}
of
T
, the substructure of
\mathbf{M}
induced by the constants is an elementary substructure of
\mathbf{M}
. In other words, if a theory is grounded, then it has a model
\mathbf{N}
whose domain is exactly the set of all the constants.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
T
be a grounded theory. A
Tarski–Vaught type
(or
TV-type
) of
T
is a complete type
p\in S_{\omega}(T)
that satisfies the following conditions:

•

for every
i<j<\omega
,
(x_{i}\neq x_{j})\in p
;

•

for every
i<\omega
and every constant
c\in\sigma
,
(x_{i}\neq c)\in p
;

•

for every formula
\psi(\bar{x})\in p
of the form

(\exists y)\ \varphi(\bar{x},y)
, either there exists a constant
c\in\sigma
such that
\varphi(\bar{x},c)\in p
, or there exists
i<\omega
such that
\varphi(\bar{x},x_{i})\in p
(or both).

We denote by
S_{TV}(T)
the set of all the TV-types in
S_{\omega}(T)
and by
N
the set of all the constants of
\sigma
.

Lemma 3.3
.

S_{TV}(T)
is a Borel subset of
S_{\omega}({T})
.

Proof.

The set
S_{TV}(T)
is the intersection of the closed set
S_{\omega}(T)
with

•

the countably many clopens
\clop(x_{i}\neq x_{j})
(for
i<j<\omega
),

•

the countably many clopens
\clop(x_{i}\neq c)
(for
i<\omega
and constant
c\in N
),

•

countably many open sets of the form

\bigcup_{c\in N}\clop(\varphi(\bar{x},c))\cup\bigcup_{i<\omega}\clop(\varphi(\bar{x},x_{i})).

∎

Notation 3.4
.

Fix a theory
T
. We let
\mathrm{proj}_{\omega\to 1}\colon S_{\omega}(T)\to S_{1}(T)
be the projection that maps a type
p(x_{0},x_{1},\dots)\in S_{\omega}(T)
to the subset of formulas it contains that have only
x_{0}
as a free variable.

Note that
\mathrm{proj}_{\omega\to 1}
is continuous.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
T
be a grounded theory (with set of constants
N
). Then,

\mathrm{proj}_{\omega\to 1}(S_{\sf TV}(T))=S_{1}(T)\setminus\set{\tp(c)}{c\in N}.

Proof.

First, note that
\mathrm{proj}_{\omega\to 1}(S_{\sf TV}(T))\subseteq\mathrm{proj}_{\omega\to 1}(S_{\omega}(T))=S_{1}(T)
.
Moreover, for every constant
c\in N
, the formula
(x_{0}\neq c)
belongs to all the types in
S_{\sf TV}(T)
. Hence, we have the left-to-right inclusion.

For the reverse inclusion, fix some
p(x_{0})\in S_{1}(T)\setminus\set{\tp(c)}{c\in N}
. Consider some
\mathbf{M}\succ\mathbf{N}
realizing
p
such that
M\setminus N
is countably infinite. Choose any bijection
f:\set{x_{i}:i\in\omega}\to M\setminus N
such that
f(x_{0})
realizes
p
. Then
M\setminus N
realizes a type in
S_{\sf TV}(T)
that projects to
p
.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

Let
T
be a grounded stable theory and let
k\in\omega
. We let
{\rm NF}_{k}(T)\subset S_{k}(T)
be the set of
k
-types realized by elements
(a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1})
in some model such that
a_{i}\ind a_{j}
for every
i\neq j
.

We then let
\pi_{k}\colon{\rm NF}_{k}(T)\to S_{1}(T)^{k}
be the mapping sending

\tp(a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1})
to
(\tp(a_{0}),\dots,\tp(a_{k-1}))
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
T
be a grounded stable theory. Then
{\rm NF}_{k}(T)
is a Borel subset of
S_{k}(T)
.

Proof.

Fix
i<j<\ell
. It suffices to show the set
X_{i,j,\phi}
of
k
-types realized by tuples
\bar{a}=(a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1})
such that
a_{i}
does not fork with
a_{j}
via
\phi(x;y)
is Borel.

By Facts
2.10
(3)-(4), there is some (countable)
Q_{\phi}\subset S_{\phi}(T)\times S_{\phi^{*}}(T)
(consisting of those pairs for which non-forking realizations satisfy
\phi
) such that
X_{i,j,\phi}
consists of those types
p(x_{0},\dots,x_{k-1})
such that: either
p\supset q(\bar{x}_{i})\cup q^{\prime}(\bar{x}_{j})\cup\set{\phi(\bar{x}_{i};\bar{x}_{j})}
for some
(q,q^{\prime})\in Q_{\phi}
, or
p\supset q(\bar{x}_{i})\cup q^{\prime}(\bar{x}_{j})\cup\set{\neg\phi(\bar{x}_{i};\bar{x}_{j})}
for some
(q,q^{\prime})\notin Q_{\phi}
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
T
be a grounded stable theory. The mapping
\pi_{k}\colon{\rm NF}_{k}(T)\to S_{1}(T)^{k}
is a continuous Borel isomorphism.

Proof.

That
\pi_{k}
is continuous is trivial.

In order to prove the surjectivity, we consider a sufficiently saturated model
\mathbf{M}
.
Let
(p_{0},\dots,p_{k-1})\in S_{1}(T)^{k}
. By Fact
2.10
(5), there exist
a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1}\in M
with

a_{i}\ind\set{a_{0},\dots,a_{i-1}}
for all
0<i<k
and
\tp(a_{i})=p_{i}
for all
0\leq i<k
. Thus,

\tp(a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1})\in{\rm NF}_{k}(T)
and
\pi_{k}(\tp(a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1}))=(p_{0},\dots,p_{k-1})
.

If
T
is monadically stable, which is the only case we will need later, then Fact
2.11
(1) implies that
a_{i}\ind\set{a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1}}\setminus\set{a_{i}}
for every
0\leq i<k
; the more general stable case follows from standard properties of non-forking independence (monotonicity and transitivity, see e.g.
[
29
, Theorem 8.5.5]
).The injectivity of
\pi_{k}
then follows from Fact
2.10
(3).
Hence,
\pi_{k}
is a bijection. As
{\rm NF}_{k}(T)
and
S_{1}(T)^{k}
are standard Borel spaces,
\pi_{k}
is a Borel isomorphism by
Theorem

2.1
.
∎

4.
Totally Borel models for monadically stable theories

In this section, we consider a countable signature
\sigma
, a grounded monadically stable theory
T
of
\sigma
-structures, and a Borel subset
S
of
S_{\sf TV}(T)
. As usual, we fix a suitably saturated model
\mathbf{U}
, so the set of constants names an elementary substructure
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{U}
.

Let
\mathbb{I}=[0,1]\subset\mathbb{R}
. We aim to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

There is
\mathbf{B}\prec\mathbf{U}
that can be equipped with a topology so that it is a totally Borel model whose domain can be identified with
(\mathbb{I}\times S\times\omega)\sqcup N
, satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

For every
r\in\mathbb{I}
and
p\in S
,
B(p,r)\models p
, where
B(p,r):=((r,p,i):i\in\omega)
;

(2)

The
\omega
-tuples in
\set{B(p,r)}{p\in S,r\in\mathbb{I}}
are pairwise forking-independent.

We remark that there is a Borel completeness theorem, which produces a totally Borel model of size continuum for any countable first-order theory with an infinite model. However, the proofs (
[
20
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
28
, Theorem 3.1.1]
) seem to allow for realizing only countably many types. And there are non-trivial limits to the amount of saturation that can be achieved in general in totally Borel models
[
28
, Theorem 1.3.3]
.

We will need the following general result about building models of monadically stable theories, saying that putting together non-forking elementary submodels of
\mathbf{U}
(over a common elementary submodel
\mathbf{N}
) again yields an elementary submodel.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{U}\supset M=\bigcup_{i\in\kappa}M_{i}
be such that
\mathbf{M}_{i}\prec\mathbf{U}
for all
i\in\kappa
, and
M_{i}\ind M_{j}
for all
i\neq j\in\kappa
. Then
\mathbf{M}\prec\mathbf{U}
.

Proof.

Consider a tuple
\bar{m}\subset M
and a formula
\phi(\bar{x},y)
such that
\mathbf{U}\models\exists y\phi(\bar{m},y)
, and let
u\in\mathbf{U}
witness the existential. We may assume that
\bar{m}\cap N=\emptyset
, since otherwise we may add elements from
N
as parameters to
\phi
and shrink
\bar{m}
. Let
I=\set{i\in\kappa}{\bar{m}\cap M_{i}\neq\emptyset}
, and let
\bar{m}_{i}=\bar{m}\cap M_{i}
for every
i\in I
.

First, suppose
u\ind\bar{m}
. By Fact
2.10
(2),
\tp(u/\bar{m})
is finitely satisfiable in
N
, and so there is some
n\in N
such that
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{m},n)
.

So suppose that
u\nind\bar{m}
. By Fact
2.11
(1),
u\nind M_{i^{*}}
for some
i^{*}\in I
. Let
\bar{m}^{\prime}
be such that
\bar{m}=\bar{m}^{\prime}\bar{m}_{i^{*}}
(up to permutation). By Fact
2.11
(1)-(2),
u\bar{m}_{i^{*}}\ind\bar{m}^{\prime}
. Let
\phi^{\prime}(\bar{x}^{\prime};\bar{z},y)=\phi(\bar{x}^{\prime}\bar{z},y)
. Then there is some formula
\psi(y,\bar{z})
such that if
a\bar{b}\ind\bar{m}^{\prime}
, we have
\mathbf{U}\models\psi(a,\bar{b})
if and only if
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{m}^{\prime}\bar{b},a)
. We have
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{m}^{\prime}\bar{m}_{i^{*}},u)
, thus
\mathbf{U}\models\psi(u,\bar{m}_{i^{*}})
, and thus
\mathbf{U}\models\exists y\psi(y,\bar{m}_{i^{*}})
. Since
\mathbf{M}_{i^{*}}\prec\mathbf{U}
, we have
\mathbf{M}_{i^{*}}\models\exists y\psi(y,\bar{m}_{i^{*}})
. If
v
is a witness for this, then we will have
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(\bar{m}^{\prime}\bar{m}_{i^{*}},v)
, as desired.
∎

By applying Fact
2.10
(5), we may find
\mathbf{B}\subset\mathbf{U}
consisting of
2^{\aleph_{0}}
-many realizations
\set{\bar{a}_{p,i}}{i\in 2^{\aleph_{0}}}
of every type
p\in S\subset S_{TV}(T)
, and such that
\bar{a}_{p,i}\ind\bar{a}_{q,j}
if
(p,i)\neq(q,j)
, and thus
\bar{a}_{p,i}\ind\set{\bar{a}_{q,j}}{(q,j)\neq(p,i)}
. Thus by Fact
2.10
(3), the isomorphism type of
\mathbf{B}
is uniquely determined. We identify the domain of
\mathbf{B}
with
(\mathbb{I}\times S\times\omega)\sqcup N
in the natural way, i.e. so that given
\alpha\in\mathbb{I}
and
p\in S
, we have
((\alpha,p,0),(\alpha,p,1),\dots)\models p
.
In the following, for
p\in S
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{I}
, we denote by
B(p,\alpha)
the set
\set{\alpha}\times\set{p}\times\omega
(cf Fig.
1
).

Figure 1.
Structure of the totally Borel model
\mathbf{B}
.
In the model, we have
(\alpha,p,i)\ind(\beta,p,j)
if
(\alpha,p)\neq(\beta,q)
.

Next, we equip
\mathbf{B}
with the topology obtained by taking the discrete topology on
N
, the standard topologies on
\mathbb{I}
and on
S
, the discrete topology on
\omega
, and the product topology on
\mathbb{I}\times S\times\omega
.

Theorem 4.3
.

The structure
\mathbf{B}
is a totally Borel model of
T
, satisfying the conditions of Theorem
4.1
.

Proof.

That
\mathbf{B}\models T
is immediate from Lemma
4.2
, and it clearly satisfies the two conditions of Theorem
4.1
. So it remains to prove
\mathbf{B}
is totally Borel.

We consider the case of formulas with one or two free variables.
The case of higher-arity formulas follows exactly the same argument but with more cumbersome notation.

Let
\psi
be a formula with a single free variable. Then,
\psi(\mathbf{B})
is Borel as

\psi(\mathbf{B})=\psi(N)\cup\bigcup_{i<\omega}(\mathbb{I}\times\set{p\in S}{\psi(x_{i})\in p}\times\{i\}),

\psi(N)
is countable (hence Borel), and
\set{p\in S}{\psi(x_{i})\in p}
is clopen.

We now consider a formula
\varphi(x,y)
with two free variables.
It suffices to show that
\varphi(\mathbf{B}\backslash N)
is Borel, since

\varphi(\mathbf{B})=\varphi(\mathbf{B}\backslash N)\cup\bigcup_{n\in N}(\varphi(\mathbf{B},n)\times\set{n}\cup\set{n}\times\varphi(n,\mathbf{B}))

and the sets
\varphi(\mathbf{B},n)
and
\varphi(n,\mathbf{B})
are Borel (by the above one free variable case).

Let
\mathbf{B}_{\set{x,y}}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\set{x},\set{y}}
be the subsets of
(\mathbf{B}\backslash N)^{2}
given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\set{x,y}}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\mathclap{(p,r)\in S\times\mathbb{I}}}\quad(B(p,r)\times B(p,r))

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\set{x},\set{y}}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\mathclap{(p,r)\neq(q,r^{\prime})\in S\times\mathbb{I}}}\quad(B(p,r)\times B(q,r^{\prime}))

Note these sets partition
(\mathbf{B}\backslash N)^{2}

(S\times\mathbb{I})^{2}
is closed.

We first show that
\varphi(\mathbf{B}_{\set{x,y}})
is Borel. Let
S_{i,j}=\set{p\in S}{\varphi(x_{i},x_{j})\in p}
, which is clopen. Then

\varphi(\mathbf{B}_{\set{x,y}})=\mathbf{B}_{\set{x,y}}\cap\bigcup_{i,j\in\omega}\bigl((\mathbb{I}\times S_{i,j}\times\set{i})\times(\mathbb{I}\times S_{i,j}\times\set{j})\bigr).

We next show that
\varphi(\mathbf{B}_{\set{x},\set{y}})
is Borel. Given

, let
S_{q(x_{i})}=\set{p\in S}{p\supset q}
, which is closed. By Fact
2.10
(3), there is some

(which is countable by Fact

(4)) such that

\varphi(\mathbf{B}_{\set{x},\set{y}})=\mathbf{B}_{\set{x},\set{y}}\cap\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\omega\\
\mathclap{(q_{1}(x_{i}),q_{2}(x_{j}))\in Q}\end{subarray}}\bigl((\mathbb{I}\times S_{q_{1}(x_{i})}\times\set{i})\times(\mathbb{I}\times S_{q_{2}(x_{j})}\times\set{j})\bigr).

In the higher-arity case, we would have to use induction on formulas with fewer free variables to eliminate the case where some free variables are interpreted as constants, then consider
all possible partitions of the free variables.
∎

We note that the definable relations of
\mathbf{B}
are not only Borel, but are in the bottom few levels of the Borel hierarchy.

5.
Dismantling models

In this section, we prove an infinitary analogue of the result characterizing nowhere dense classes via quasi-residuality in
[
25
]
, generalized to the monadically stable setting. We recall that quasi-residuality roughly says that in every graph in the class, there is a set
N
of vertices of bounded size such that the rest of the graph decomposes into small disconnected pieces over
N
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
T
be a monadically stable theory. Let
\mathbf{M}\models T
be equipped with a probability measure on the sigma-algebra generated by parameter-definable sets, and let and
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{M}
. Then we say that
\mathbf{M}
is
dismantled over
\mathbf{N}
if, after naming
N
by constants, the equivalence classes of the forking relation on singletons have measure 0.

In the case where
T
is grounded, we say that
\mathbf{M}
is
dismantled
if it is dismantled over the set of constants.

We now prove that we may always choose some
N
to dismantle models.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
T
be monadically stable in a countable language
L
. Let
\mathbf{M}\models T
be equipped with a probability measure
\mu
on the sigma-algebra generated by parameter-definable sets (in one dimension). Then there is some countable
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{M}
such that (after naming every element of
N
by a constant) for all
m\in M
, we have
P_{m}=\set{x\in M}{x\nind m}
is measurable and null.

Remark 5.3
.

Note that not every
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{M}
will work. A simple example is that if some singleton in
M
has positive measure then it must be included in
N
. Less trivially,
\mathbf{M}
might be an equivalence relation with infinitely many infinite classes, and some class might have positive measure even though no singleton does. If
N
contains no point from this class, then the class will also be a class of the forking equivalence relation. But if
N
contains some point from the class, then over
N
each singleton from the class will form its own class of the forking equivalence relation, which will have measure 0.

We will use that an analogue of Fact
2.10
(5) holds for finite satisfiability in a model, even in unstable theories.

Fact 5.4
.

Let
T
be an arbitrary theory, and let
\mathbf{N}\prec\mathbf{U}\models T
with
\mathbf{U}
sufficiently saturated. Let
a\in\mathbf{U}
and
B\supset A\supset N
.

(1)
\tp(a/N)
is finitely satisfiable in
N
.

(2) If
\tp(a/A)
is finitely satisfiable in
N
, then there is some
a^{\prime}\in\mathbf{U}
such that
a^{\prime}\models\tp(a/A)
and
\tp(a^{\prime}/B)
is finitely satisfiable in
N
.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Expand the language to
L^{\prime}
, adding relations
R_{\phi(x;\bar{y}),r}(\bar{y})
for every
L
-formula
\phi(x;\bar{y})
(without parameters) and every
r\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
, and expand
\mathbf{M}
to
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
by interpreting
R_{\phi(x;\bar{y}),r}(\bar{m})
as “
\mu(\phi(x;\bar{m}))\geq r
” (note
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
may no longer be stable). Let
\mathbf{N}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
be countable, and let
\mathbf{N}
be the
L
-reduct of
\mathbf{N}^{\prime}
. Then we claim
\mathbf{N}
is as desired.

Recall that we have only defined forking when we have named an elementary substructure by constants. For the rest of this proof, we let
L_{N}
be the expansion of
L
by constants for
N
, and say that an
L_{N}
-formula
\phi(x,\bar{m})
forks over
N
if it forks in the expansion where we have named
N
by constants.

For every
m\in M
, we have

P_{m}=\bigcup\set{\phi(M,m)}{\phi(x,y)\text{ an $L_{N}$-formula, $\phi(x,m)$ forks over $N$}}.

So
P_{m}
is a countable union of parameter-definable sets, and thus is measurable.

Now suppose there is some
m\in M
such that
\mu(P_{m})>0
. Then by countable additivity there is some
L_{N}
-formula
\phi(x,m)
that forks over
N
such that
\mu(\phi(x,m))>0
. Let
1/r\in\mathbb{Q}
be such that
\mu(\phi(x,m))\geq 1/r
. Note that
m\not\in N
, since then
P_{m}=\emptyset
.

Consider the
L^{\prime}
-expansions
\mathbf{N}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
. Let
\phi(x,y)
be
\psi(x,y,\bar{n})
where
\psi
is without parameters from
N
. Note that it is part of
{\rm Th}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
that there do not exist elements
(m_{i}:i\in[r+1])
such that the sets defined by
\set{\phi(x,m_{i})}{i\in[r+1]}
are pairwise disjoint and
R_{\psi(x;y\bar{z}),r}(m_{i}\bar{n})
holds for every

.

Now let
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\succ\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
be
\aleph_{1}
-saturated, and let
\mathbf{U}
be its
L
-reduct. Then (by iterating Fact
5.4
)
U^{\prime}
contains a sequence
(m_{i}:i\in\omega)
such that
\tp(m_{i}/N^{\prime})=\tp(m/N^{\prime})
for all

and such that
\tp(m_{i}/N^{\prime}m_{0}\dots m_{i-1})
is finitely satisfiable in

for every

. In particular
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\models R_{\psi(x;y\bar{z}),r}(m_{i}\bar{n})
for every

. So we will obtain a contradiction if
\set{\phi(U^{\prime},m_{i})}{i\in\omega}
are pairwise disjoint. Working in
\mathbf{U}
, where finite satisfiability over
N
and non-forking over

coincide (Fact
2.10
(2)), we see that
m_{i}\ind_{N}m_{j}
for all
i,j\in\omega
. Since forking is transitive on singletons in
\mathbf{U}
(Fact
2.11
(2)), if there were some

such that
\mathbf{U}\models\phi(u,m_{i})\wedge\phi(u,m_{j})
for

, then we would have

. So
\set{\phi(U,m_{i})}{i\in\omega}
are pairwise disjoint.
∎

6.
Transferring the measure

Finally, in this section we show how to place the desired measure on the totally Borel model

we have constructed, and prove our main theorems. We continue to assume a countable language throughout.

We recall the definition of a measurable structure from
Section

2.3
, and will use

to denote a measurable structure that is a model of a particular theory.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathbf{M}
be a dismantled measurable model of a grounded monadically stable theory
T
.
Let

.
The set
\set{\bar{u}\in\mathbf{M}^{k}}{u_{i}\nind u_{j}\text{ for some }i<j<k}

Proof.

The argument for measurability is essentially the same as the proof of Lemma
3.7

For measure zero, we first consider the case

. Given

. By Lemma

and the Fubini property, we have

\displaystyle m_{2}(\set{(u,v)}{u\nind v})

\displaystyle=\int P_{x}(y)\,{\rm d}m_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int\int P_{x}(y)\,{\rm d}m_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}m_{1}(y)

\displaystyle=0.

The general case easily follows, as

.

Recall from Section

Lemma 6.2

Consider the subset

Then, using Lemma

\displaystyle\mu_{k}(\pi_{k}^{-1}(K))

\displaystyle=m_{k}\biggl(\Set{\bar{x}}{x_{i}\ind x_{j}\ (\forall i<j<k)\text{ and }\mathbf{M}\models\bigwedge\phi_{i}(x_{i})}\biggr)

∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
T
be a grounded monadically stable theory.
Then, for every dismantled measurable model

of

Let

.
According to Lemmas

\displaystyle m_{k}(\varphi(\mathbf{M}))

\displaystyle=m_{k}\bigl(\varphi(\mathbf{M})\cap\set{\bar{v}\in M^{k}}{v_{i}\ind v_{j}\ \forall i<j<k}\bigr)

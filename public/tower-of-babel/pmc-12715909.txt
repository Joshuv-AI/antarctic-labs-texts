Evolving treatments and outcomes in patients with newly diagnosed multiple myeloma undergoing autologous stem cell transplantation: a real-world study in China
Authors: Li Xiaozhe, Chen Meilan, Liu Junru, Gu Jingli, Huang Beihui, Kuang Lifen, Li Juan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis single-centre retrospective study aimed to evaluate trends in the clinical characteristics, treatment approaches, and outcomes of patients with newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM) who underwent initial autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) between 2007 and 2023 in China.MethodsA total of 390 patients with NDMM were grouped into three time cohorts: 2007–2012 (79 patients), 2013–2018 (143 patients), and 2019–2023 (168 patients). Clinical characteristics, treatment regimens, and survival outcomes were analysed over these periods.ResultsThe results revealed increases in the proportions of patients older than 65 years, those with extramedullary disease (EMD), and those with secondary amyloidosis (systemic organ involvement by immunoglobulin light chain amyloid deposition with a diagnosis of MM). Treatment trends included the preference for bortezomib-based regimens, with the addition of daratumumab (Dara)-based therapies in the most recent cohort. The pre-ASCT MRD-negative rate increased from 8.5% in Cohort 1 to 29.8% in Cohort 3 (p = 0.001), with corresponding post-ASCT improvements in MRD-negative status (64.4% to 80.4%). Survival outcomes also improved, with the 5-year PFS rate increasing from 44.62% (Cohort 1) to 60.36% (Cohort 2) and 87.61% in the latest cohort (p < 0.001) and the 5-year OS rate increasing from 58.23% to 68.46% and 81.82% in the latest cohort (p = 0.011), respectively. High-risk cytogenetic abnormalities (HRCAs) were associated with poorer PFS and OS, although tandem ASCT for high-risk patients resulted in improved outcomes.ConclusionThis real-world study highlights substantial advancements in response depth and survival outcomes for patients with NDMM undergoing ASCT. These improvements were driven by the introduction of new drugs and refined risk stratification methods.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15185-7.

Background

Multiple myeloma (MM) is an incurable plasma cell neoplasm [1]. However, the introduction of autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT), proteasome inhibitors (PIs), immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs), and monoclonal antibodies has significantly advanced its management. ASCT, which includes a sequence of treatments, including induction therapy, stem cell mobilization, conditioning regimens, single or tandem ASCT, consolidation, and maintenance therapy, significantly prolongs the survival of patients with MM [2–4].

This improved survival of ASCT-eligible patients is attributed to the use of a multifaceted approach, with contributors including risk stratification, novel therapeutic agents, and diverse treatment strategies [5]. For example, incorporating novel drug classes into induction, mobilization, consolidation, and maintenance elicited more resilient responses. Patients with high-risk MM typically undergo tandem ASCT [6–8], whereas standard-risk patients receive a single ASCT. These advancements improved survival [9, 10], but this improvement is often observed in select clinical trial populations, which may distort real-world patient outcomes, as the existing disparities among patient subgroups indicate. Additionally, data from China are lacking.

In recent years, in China, advances in the diagnosis and treatment of MM have transformed the management of patients with ASCT-eligible myeloma. With a deeper understanding of MM, ASCT is being increasingly used in patients with newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM).

We utilized a database from a referral centre in China to assess changes in clinical characteristics, treatment regimens, response rates and survival rates from January 2007 to December 2023. Additionally, we evaluated tandem ASCT. These real-world data can be used to analyse treatment trends within China to improve clinical practice.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective analysis included all patients who underwent ASCT at the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University between January 1 st, 2007, and December 31 st, 2023. MM was diagnosed in accordance with the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) criteria [11]. Patients aged < 18 years and those who received induction therapy that was not followed by ASCT were excluded. Patients were grouped into three time cohorts according to ASCT performance year: Cohort 1: 2007–2012; Cohort 2: 2013–2018; and Cohort 3: 2019‒2023. The patient data included clinical and laboratory characteristics at diagnosis, treatment regimen, response to treatment, progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and cause of death. The follow-up period ended at death from any cause, loss to follow-up, or 31 December 2023. This study followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of the Medical Faculty. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before data use.

Treatment

All patients received induction therapy with novel drugs, including PI-based regimens (VD, VCD, PAD, etc.), IMiD-based regimens (RAD, Pom + AD), PI + IMiD-based regimens (VRD, VTD), and Dara-based regimens (Dara + VD, Dara + VRD, etc.). Stem cell mobilization was performed using granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) alone or in combination with chemotherapy (high-dose cyclophosphamide + G-CSF or pegylated G-CSF). Stem cells for two to three transplants were collected. In 2021, plerixafor was added. Pretransplant conditioning involved melphalan (200 mg/m2). During the melphalan shortage (2014–2018), CVB (busulfan, etoposide, and cyclophosphamide) was used. Maintenance therapy began with thalidomide and/or interferon-alpha (IFN-α), gradually shifting to lenalidomide, bortezomib, ixazomib, daratumumab, or carfilzomib and pomalidomide.

Minimal residual disease (MRD) monitoring and dynamics

Bone marrow (BM) MRD was monitored from December 2010 using flow cytometry. BM MRD assessments were performed postinduction and every 3 months during the first year after ASCT and then every 6–12 months thereafter. EDTA (anticoagulant)-treated BM aspirate (2 ml) was washed with PBS three times, and the cell density was then adjusted to ≤ 20 × 10^6/ml. Afterwards, 100 µl of sample was incubated with antibodies against the following proteins: CD38, CD56, CD19, CD20, CD54, CD138, CD45, cytoplasmic κ, and cytoplasmic λ. Abnormal plasma cells were differentiated from normal plasma cells by surface markers and cytoplasmic light chain expression [11, 12]. The results were considered positive if ≥ 20 plasma cells with an abnormal phenotype were detected. The sensitivity of our flow cytometry detection method for MRD was 20 per 1–2 × 10^6, equal to 5 × 10^−5 to 10^−5.

Study endpoints and definitions

First, we investigated and evaluated ASCT as a first-line treatment for MM. This included evaluating induction, mobilization, conditioning, and maintenance regimens; CD34 + cell doses; haematological recovery; the number of tandem ASCTs; haematological response, MRD status, progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS). Treatment efficacy was assessed multiple times after transplantation according to the IMWG criteria [13]. We performed fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) analysis to identify high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities (HRCAs), specifically t(4;14), t(14;16), del(17p), and 1q21 amplification. The definitions of double- and triple-hit myeloma were in accordance with the Mayo Clinic mSMART 3.0 guidelines, referring to patients harbouring two or three concurrent high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities (e.g., t(4;14), t(14;16), del(17p), amp1q21). Extramedullary disease (EMD) was diagnosed by PET/CT, CT, or MRI and defined as soft tissue plasmacytomas outside the bone marrow. EMD included extramedullary extraosseous (EME) and extramedullary bone-related (EMB) diseases. Pattern B of M-protein decline was defined as per our previous publication [14] as a specific kinetic pattern of monoclonal protein reduction during induction therapy (see Supplemental Table 1), characterized by a serum M-protein decrease ≥ 25% but < 90% after 2 cycles of treatment for nonlight chain MM and a 24-h urine M-protein decrease ≥ 50% after 2 cycles of treatment but not meeting the criteria for Pattern A (a 24-h urine M-protein decrease ≥ 99% after 1 cycle of treatment) for light chain MM. Secondary amyloidosis in multiple myeloma refers to the deposition of amyloid fibrils composed of immunoglobulin light chains in various organs in patients with diagnosed multiple myeloma. Diagnosis is confirmed through pathological biopsy and Congo red staining, where a positive result indicates amyloid protein deposition. Second, we analysed factors affecting survival. PFS was calculated from induction to progression, death, or the last follow-up; OS was calculated from induction to death or the last follow-up.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 and R Studio 4.1.2. All p values were 2-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the baseline characteristics. Patient-, disease-, and ASCT-related variables were analysed across the three cohorts by the year of ASCT. The Mann–Whitney and Wilcoxon tests were used to compare continuous variables. Chi-square tests were used to determine differences between baseline and outcome variables. Survival was analysed using the Kaplan–Meier method, group differences were analysed via the log-rank test, associations between demographic/clinical factors and survival outcomes were analysed via univariate and multivariate Cox regression, and 95% confidence intervals were estimated. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals and was found to be satisfied for all Cox models. The haematological response was unchanged due to high collinearity with MRD status in the best responders to ASCT. The restricted mean survival time (RMST) was compared between the groups at 3 or 5 years postinduction. Because the proportion of missing values was small (< 5% for all variables), we primarily adopted a complete-case analysis, excluding patients with missing values from the relevant statistical models.

Results

The analysis included 390 patients with NDMM treated with induction followed by ASCT. Among the three cohorts (2007 to 2012, 20132018, and 2019 to 2023), 79 (20.2%) patients were included in Cohort 1 (2007–2012), 146 (37.4%) were included in Cohort 2 (2013–2018), and 189 (48.4%) were included in Cohort 3 (2019‒2023). The number of patients with NDMM who underwent ASCT increased from tens to nearly two hundred in each cohort (Supplemental Fig. 1).

Novel detection methods at our centre have transformed the treatment for patients with myeloma. EMD was observed in 100 (25.6%) patients. The incidence of EMD increased significantly from 13.9% in Cohort 1 to 27.4% in Cohort 3 (p = 0.019). Secondary light chain amyloidosis was observed in 38 (9.7%) patients, with the incidence increasing from 2.5% to 14.3% over this period (p = 0.012). The percentage of patients with HRCAs increased over time. Data on HRCAs were available for only 347 patients; 114 (32.9%) had HRCAs, and 20 (5.8%) had double- or triple-hit myeloma. A total of 93.8% of patients underwent single ASCT, whereas 6.2% underwent tandem ASCT. The use of tandem ASCT increased significantly from 0% in Cohort 1 to 12.5% in Cohort 3 (p < 0.001).

Evolving treatment regimens

Changes in induction

We used various induction therapies to reduce the tumour burden before ASCT from 2007 to 2023 (Fig. 1A and B). The median number of induction therapy cycles was 4 (range, 1–10): 2007–2012:3 (1–9), 2013–2018:4 (2–10), and 2019–2023:4 (3–8). All patients were treated with agents such as PIs, IMiDs and/or monotherapies. A total of 328 (84.1%), 21 (5.4%), 26 (6.7%), and 15 (3.8%) patients received PI-/IMiD-/PI + IMiD−/Dara-based regimens, respectively. The induction regimens changed from PI-based doublet regimens in Cohort 1 to triplet/quadruplet regimens in Cohort 3. In 2011, the PAD combination (bortezomib + pegylated liposomal doxorubicin + dexamethasone) became the most common treatment (68.0%). The use of the PI + IMiD-based regimen increased. Daratumumab-based regimens emerged in Cohort 3. Some patients with HRCAs in Cohort 3 received a triplet/quadruplet regimen with daratumumab, carfilzomib, and/or pomalidomide. For the IMiD-based regimen, there was a shift from thalidomide to lenalidomide (Fig. 1B). Stem cell mobilization

We analysed 365 patients who underwent peripheral blood stem cell mobilization, with an overall single-attempt success rate of 93.7% and a mean number of CD34 + cells of 5.3 × 10^6/kg. Most patients (94.2%) received high-dose CTX combined with stimulating agents (Supplemental Fig. 2 and Table 2). Mobilization strategies evolved over time, shifting from G-CSF to PEG-rh G-CSF in Cohort 2 and to plerixafor in Cohort 3, where 60 patients with low yields received it successfully (Fig. 1C). Success rates improved across cohorts (83.6%, 94.9%, and 96.4%), with the median CD34 + collection rate increasing from 3.3 × 10^6/kg in Cohort 1 to 6.2 × 10^6/kg in Cohort 3. Conditioning therapy

A total of 415 transplants were performed, including 25 tandem ASCTs; 385 patients received peripheral blood stem cell transplantation, 25 received bone marrow transplantation, and 4 received both. No transplant-related deaths occurred. The median infused CD34 + dose was 2.9 × 10^6/kg, and engraftment was achieved in all patients, with median neutrophil and platelet recovery at 10 and 12 days, respectively. The conditioning regimens included melphalan (Mel, 65.8%) and CVB (34.2%). Compared with Mel, CVB led to faster recovery (neutrophils by 1 day and platelets by 3 days; p < 0.001). Overall, engraftment times improved significantly over time (p < 0.05), likely reflecting advances in supportive care (Table 2).

Changes in maintenance therapy

No ASCT-treated patients received consolidation therapy, whereas 94.9% received maintenance therapy (n = 370). Thalidomide and/or IFN-a were the predominant maintenance strategies in Cohort 1 but were rare in Cohort 2 and 3 (Fig. 1 D). After 2015, lenalidomide began to be used more frequently in maintenance therapy. During this therapy, most patients were treated with a single- agent. Patients with high-risk MM received maintenance therapy with VD, Dara, or KP, whereas standard-risk patients were treated with lenalidomide.

Response rate and MRD status

The response rates increased after induction and before transplantation (Fig. 2). In Cohort 1, 24.1% and 64.6% of patients achieved ≥ CR and ≥ VGPR, respectively; in Cohort 2, 34.3% and 81.1%; and in Cohort 3, 40.5% and 92.3%, respectively. Before 2011, MRD was rarely tested, resulting in 20 patients in Cohort 1 lacking MRD status. For the evaluable patients, the percentages of MRD-negative responses before transplantation were 8.5%, 29.4%, and 29.8%, respectively. Responses at 3 months post-transplant increased steadily. In Cohort 1, 51.9% and 81.0% achieved ≥ CR and ≥ VGPR, respectively, whereas in Cohort 3, 66.1% and 97.6%, respectively, achieved ≥ CR and ≥ VGPR, respectively. Similarly, the best post-HSCT haematological response improved over time, with 68.4% and 87.4% in Cohort 1 and 82.1% and 97.6% in Cohort 3 achieving ≥ CR and ≥ VGPR, respectively. A total of 64.4%, 76.2%, and 80.4% of the patients in the three cohorts exhibited the best post-HSCT MRD-negative responses, respectively. Compared with the response before ASCT, the efficacy of the treatment increased significantly after transplantation and further during maintenance therapy. Patients underwent either single (n = 365) or tandem ASCT (n = 23). Since 2018, tandem ASCT has been the first-line treatment for high-risk cytogenetic patients at our centre. Second primary malignancy (SPM)

Thirty patients (7.7%) in the entire cohort developed a second primary malignancy (SPM): 7 patients (8.9%) in Cohort 1, 18 patients (12.6%) in Cohort 2, and 5 patients (3.0%) in Cohort 3. Acute lymphoid leukaemia and myeloid malignancies were the most common SPMs in all three cohorts. Twenty-eight patients who developed SPM received IMiD-based maintenance therapy.

Discussion

ASCT remains central to treating MM. Advances in understanding this cancer, new drugs and drug combinations and better supportive care have improved outcomes in patients with NDMM undergoing ASCT. Notably, no relevant real-world data exist from China [8, 15–18]. The reason for the lack of real-world data in China is that the percentage of Chinese patients who undergo first-line ASCT is only 12.6–14.4%, which is much lower than the percentages reported in Western countries [19]. However, the use of ASCT in patients with NDMM has gradually increased in China in recent years. As one of China’s first centres that routinely performs ASCT, we reported outcomes for a single-centre cohort of unselected patients with NDMM and analysed evolving therapies. The number of patients who underwent ASCT increased sharply between 2007 and 2023, and the mPFS (71.0 months) and mOS (106.1 months) times were comparable to those reported for ASCT in Western countries. For example, in the MD Anderson retrospective single-centre study (2005–2021), which included 1,109 patients with NDMM undergoing autoHCT, the median PFS was 38.6 months, and the median OS was 103.8 months [20]. We observed improved survival for patients with NDMM who received upfront ASCT. Although older and/or high-risk patients are more common in the latter cohorts, they exhibit more resilient responses. Importantly, patients with high-risk NDMM had improved PFS and OS.

Our analysis suggests that ASCT, once limited to individuals aged < 65, is now offered more frequently to elderly patients [1, 17]. For example, the rate of enrolment of patients ≥ 65 years of age at our centre increased from 5.1 to 10.7%. The lack of significant differences in OS or PFS between age groups indicates that ASCT benefits extend to those most frequently affected by MM. The lack of a statistically significant adverse effect of age on the outcomes may be due to more effective and safer treatment at induction, careful patient selection, and improved care.

In addition, the increased survival observed over time is more likely related to the increasing proportion of patients attaining a CR and MRD-negative status pre- or post-ASCT. One of the strengths of our study is the use of complete and consistent efficacy evaluation data, including 94.6% of the data for MRD assessment. Our data revealed improved responses across cohorts and a significant correlation between PFS and OS and the response achieved through induction therapy. In this study, patients who achieved an MRD-negative status after induction chemotherapy, transplantation, or maintenance therapy had significantly better PFS and OS. This improvement in response rates was due to the dynamic changes in induction and maintenance regimens.

All our regimens included novel agents such as PIs, IMiDs, or monoclonal antibodies. Although doublet regimens were the predominant chemotherapy regimen in Cohort 1, a transition phase up to Cohort 2 was followed by a complete switch to triplet combinations containing bortezomib. Previous studies have confirmed that these combinations improve OS better than doublet bortezomib and dexamethasone regimens do [8, 21]. Three-drug induction regimens with VRD are now the standard of care for patients with NDMM. However, our study revealed that only 6.7% of the patients received induction therapy with a combination of a PI and an IMiD. The relatively small proportion of patients receiving VRD in China is due to limited drug accessibility, delayed approval, reimbursement barriers, and cost. Our previous results indicated that R-containing induction regimens may affect subsequent stem cell collection [22]. The increased availability of drugs has expanded the diversity of drug combinations. Advancements in the application of novel techniques have enabled delicate risk stratification. Our center’s data revealed a gradual increase in the number of patients with HRCAs, as we started to routinely perform FISH tests as daily management, and more EMD cases occurred because of the start of frequent applications of PET/CT scans. For these patients, more effective treatment options, such as quadruplet regimens incorporating MoAbs (daratumumab), PIs (carfilzomib, ixazomib) and IMiDs (pomalidomide), were used, and increasing numbers of patients may achieve a CR or an MRD-negative status with ASCT. The findings of the GMM-CONCEPT [23], UK OPTIMISM [24] and FORTE [25] studies confirmed the positive effects of these drugs on high-risk patients. In addition, for patients with EMD, we administered localized radiotherapy within six months after transplantation to reduce the risk of relapse.

Efficacy analysis revealed significant improvements in CR rates and MRD-negative status following ASCT, with maintenance therapy showing the greatest benefit. In our study, 94.9% of the ASCT-treated patients received the maintenance regimen, which was a higher percentage than those reported in other studies. However, the optimal maintenance therapy regimen remains underdetermined. Studies have indicated that lenalidomide maintenance improves both PFS and OS [26–28]. Our centre has introduced this drug. However, the use of IMiDs is associated with the risk of SPM. SPM represents an important concern regarding the long-term use of IMiDs. Like in other published cohort studies, in our study, 30 patients (7.7%) in the entire cohort developed SPMs, and 28 patients who developed SPMs received IMiD-based regimen maintenance therapy. In the large Myeloma XI trial, the prevalence of SPMs in patients receiving lenalidomide maintenance was approximately 7–8% at 3–5 years [27]. Our findings align with these international datasets, confirming that IMiD-based maintenance increases SPM risk. To reduce the use of IMiDs and improve outcomes for high-risk patients, in recent years, our centre has increasingly adopted VD (bortezomib and dexamethasone) maintenance therapy or daratumumab monotherapy for high-risk patients, while ultrahigh-risk patients have been switched to the KP (carfilzomib and pomalidomide) regimen for maintenance.

Our findings are consistent with several published datasets and population-based studies showing improved outcomes over time in patients with NDMM who underwent ASCT. Nevertheless, the results in certain subgroups, particularly those with HRCAs, remain suboptimal [29–34]. In our cohort, compared with standard-risk patients, patients with HRCAs experienced inferior outcomes, similar to the CIBMTR analysis, where high-risk disease was associated with shorter survival (3-year PFS 37% vs. 49% and OS 72% vs. 85%; p < 0.001) [35]. These results underscore the unmet need for refined risk stratification and novel therapeutic strategies for high-risk MM.

Importantly, we observed encouraging improvements in outcomes for patients with HRCAs in recent years. For example, the median PFS increased from 23.2 to 60.1 months, and the median OS increased from 35.6 months to not reached across cohorts. These gains parallel trends in other studies, where the median PFS improved from 13.7 months (2001–2005) to 36.8 months (2016–2021), and the DETERMINATION trial reported a median PFS of 55.5 months in high-risk patients undergoing ASCT [36]. In contrast, the IFM study revealed no improvement in OS among high-risk patients across different eras [37]. The superior outcomes in our cohort are likely attributable, at least in part, to the use of modern induction regimens and maintenance strategies, which were not consistently applied in earlier trials such as IFM.

Changes in stem cell mobilization techniques have also impacted survival in patients with HRCAs. Most patients in this study received high-dose cyclophosphamide (CTX) combined with G-CSF (and with plerixafor from 2021 onwards), which resulted in higher PB stem cell counts. Successful stem cell harvests can be achieved in the vast majority of patients with two ASCT procedures, increasing the proportion of patients eligible for tandem ASCT. Since 2018, planned tandem ASCT has been predominantly performed in patients with HRCAs at our centre. Interestingly, our study also revealed that, in the high-risk subgroup, the benefit of tandem ASCT over single ASCT was statistically significant (40.7 months vs. not reached). Similar to other studies, in the EMN02/HO95 study [38], for patients with HRCAs, the median PFS times were 46.0 months with double ASCT vs. 26.7 months with single ASCT (p = 0.062). According to real-world data from Canada [8], tandem ASCT also significantly increased PFS in high-risk patients. The wider use of tandem ASCT in the latter cohort might have contributed to the increased PFS and OS observed in this study. However, the role of tandem ASCT in the context of modern induction therapy and maintenance needs further investigation.

In this study, we also verified several risk factors for NDMM. In the multivariable analysis, patients with low platelet counts, EME and secondary light chain amyloidosis were included. Furthermore, our previous and present studies indicate that both rapid and slow decreases in M protein levels [14] during induction therapy predict poor prognosis, which may be indicative of functional high-risk characteristics. Nevertheless, an unmet need was observed in the treatment of patients with myeloma with high-risk factors.

To our knowledge, our study is the largest investigation to date on the treatment and outcomes of an unselected cohort of MM patients who underwent upfront ASCT at a single centre in China. Our study has inherent limitations associated with its retrospective nature, including missing data due to variations in record keeping and data collection. The impact of missing cytogenetic/MRD data in early cohorts may have influenced HRCA analysis and survival estimates. The study population also exhibited significant heterogeneity, making it challenging to account for potential confounders. Additionally, our analysis was limited to patients who underwent upfront ASCT, and the findings may not apply to those who did not receive this treatment modality. Selection bias may have influenced which patients were offered ASCT, particularly tandem ASCT for high-risk patients. The small sample size of patients who received tandem ASCT limits the statistical power of our conclusions regarding this intervention. Nevertheless, this extensive analysis spanning 17 years provides valuable insights into evolving trends in the characteristics of patients with MM undergoing upfront ASCT and their outcomes. In summary, our analysis of more than 390 patients with NDMM who underwent upfront ASCT revealed significant improvements in response rates and survival rates across the three cohorts, including patients with high-risk disease, supporting its role as an effective treatment modality for eligible patients.

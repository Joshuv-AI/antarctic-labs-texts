Introduction

Let
\mathbf{U}
denote the open unit disk and
\mathbf{T}
the unit circle in the complex plane. For
p>1
, the Hardy space of harmonic functions, denoted by
\mathbf{h}^{p}
, consists of all harmonic mappings
f=g+\bar{h}
, where
g
and
h
are holomorphic functions on
\mathbf{U}
, such that

\|f\|_{p}=\|f\|_{\mathbf{h}^{p}}=\sup_{0<r<1}M_{p}(f,r)<\infty,

where

M_{p}(f,r)=\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}|f(r\zeta)|^{p}\,d\sigma(\zeta)\right)^{1/p},

and
d\sigma(\zeta)=\frac{dt}{2\pi}
for
\zeta=e^{it}\in\mathbf{T}
. The subspace of holomorphic functions in

\mathbf{h}^{p}

is denoted by

H^{p}
, and is known as the Hardy space.

If
f\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
, then it is a classical result that the radial limits

f(e^{it})=\lim_{r\to 1}f(re^{it})

exist almost everywhere, and that
f\in L^{p}(\mathbf{T})
. Furthermore, the norm satisfies the identity

(1.1)

\|f\|^{p}_{\mathbf{h}^{p}}=\lim_{r\to 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{p}\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(e^{it})|^{p}\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

Let
1<p<\infty
and define
\overline{p}=\max\{p,p/(p-1)\}
. In
[
12
]
, Verbitsky established the following sharp inequalities. If
f=u+iv\in H^{p}
with
v(0)=0
, then

(1.2)

\sec\left(\frac{\pi}{2\overline{p}}\right)\|v\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p},

and

(1.3)

\|f\|_{p}\leq\csc\left(\frac{\pi}{2\overline{p}}\right)\|u\|_{p}.

These bounds improve the previously known result by Pichorides
[
10
]
, who showed

(1.4)

\|v\|_{p}\leq\cot\left(\frac{\pi}{2\overline{p}}\right)\|u\|_{p}.

For further related work, see
[
1
,
3
,
2
,
5
]
. These inequalities were later generalized in
[
6
]
by Kalaj, where the author proved the following sharp bounds for harmonic mappings
f=g+\bar{h}\in\mathbf{h}^{p}
with
\Re(h(0)g(0))=0
:

(1.5)

\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(|g|^{2}+|h|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right)^{1/p}\leq c_{p}\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}|g+\bar{h}|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

and

(1.6)

\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}|g+\bar{h}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq d_{p}\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(|g|^{2}+|h|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right)^{1/p},

where

c_{p}=\left(\sqrt{2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2\bar{p}}\right)\right)^{-1},\quad d_{p}=\sqrt{2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2\bar{p}}\right).

These bounds imply inequalities (
1.4
), (
1.3
), and (
1.2
). As a consequence, Kalaj also verified the Hollenbeck-Verbitsky conjecture for the case
s=2
by applying inequality (
1.5
).

The inequality (
1.5
) was later extended by Marković and Melentijević in
[
9
]
, where they established the optimal constant
c_{p,s}
in

(1.7)

\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(|g|^{s}+|h|^{s}\right)^{p/s}\right)^{1/p}\leq c_{p,s}\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}|g+\bar{h}|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

for a specific range of
(p,s)
, including the case
(p,2)
. This result was further improved by Melentijević in
[
8
]
, who confirmed the Hollenbeck-Verbitsky conjecture for
s<\sec^{2}(\pi/(2p))
when
p\leq 4/3
or
p\geq 2
. However, the case
p\in[4/3,2]
remains unresolved.

In this work, we focus on a related problem. We aim to extend Verbitsky’s result (
1.2
) and the inequality (
1.5
) for the case
2\leq p<\infty
. For
c>0
and
p\in[2,\infty)
, we determine the optimal constant in the inequality

(1.8)

\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(|g|^{2}+c|h|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right)^{1/p}\leq A_{p,c}\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}|g+\bar{h}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

This estimate is equivalent to

\left\|\left(|P_{+}[f]|^{2}+c|P_{-}[f]|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right\|_{p}\leq a_{p,c}\|f\|_{p},

where
f\in L^{p}(\mathbf{T})
and
P_{+}
and
P_{-}
denote the analytic and co-analytic projections of
f
, respectively. For

f(e^{it})=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{k}e^{ikt},

we define

P_{+}[f]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}e^{ikt},\quad P_{-}[f]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{-k}e^{-ikt},

with Fourier coefficients

c_{k}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{it})e^{-ikt}\,dt.

We observe that the inequalities (
1.2
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
), (
1.6
), and (
1.7
) can all be reformulated in terms of these projections.

(1+\sec q)(p-2)\geqslant\frac{4}{\pi},

it suffices to prove that

-1+2^{6-p}(p-2)^{p-3}p\pi^{p-2}\tan\frac{q}{2}\left(2-p\tan\frac{q}{2}\right)\geqslant 0.

Now, we use the inequality
x^{x}\geqslant e^{-1/e}\approx 0.69
and

L(p):=\frac{p^{2}\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\left(2-p\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)}{-2+p}\geqslant L(\infty)=\frac{1}{4}(4-\pi)\pi.

The last inequality follows from the double inequality

\frac{\pi}{2p}\leq\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\leq\frac{4-\pi}{p^{2}}+\frac{\pi}{2p}.

Thus our inequality reduces to the inequality:

2^{4-p}e^{-1/e}(4-\pi)\pi^{-1+p}>8e^{-1/e}\left(1+(-1+p)\left(-1+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\right)(4-\pi)>p,

which holds for every
p\geqslant 2
.

∎

3.2.
Proof of the case
p\geqslant 4

We have already established the inequality (
3.2
), and now aim to prove it with the function
h(t)
in place of the function
-\cos\left(\frac{p}{2}(\pi-t)\right)
. Observe that
h(t)
coincides with
-\cos\left(\frac{p}{2}(\pi-t)\right)
on the interval
t\in\left[\pi-\frac{2\pi}{p},\pi\right]
. Therefore, the inequality holds on this interval. Moreover, since

h\left(\left[\pi-\frac{2\pi}{p},\pi\right]\right)=[-1,1],

for any
t\in\left[0,\pi-\frac{2\pi}{p}\right]
, there exists some
t^{\prime}\in\left[\pi-\frac{2\pi}{p},\pi\right]
such that

h(t)=h(t^{\prime})=-\cos\left(\frac{p}{2}(\pi-t^{\prime})\right).

Since the cosine function is decreasing on the interval
[0,\pi]
, we have

\left(\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{r}{2}+\cos t\right)^{p/2}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{r}{2}+\cos t^{\prime}\right)^{p/2}.

This establishes the inequality for
t\in\left[0,\pi-\frac{2\pi}{p}\right]
, completing the proof.

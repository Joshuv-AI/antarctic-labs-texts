Assessment of late gadolinium enhancement peri-infarct zone in myocardial infarction with in vivo endogenous T1-rho mapping
Authors: Yuncheng Li, Shutian An, Shuangshuang Xu, Peiyang Zheng, Yuzhe Fan, Xiaohe Wu, Xiuzheng Yue, Yongqiang Yu, Ren Zhao, Xiaohu Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the diagnostic performance of endogenous T1ρ mapping, compared with native T1 and T2 mapping, for identifying late gadolinium enhancement (LGE)-defined peri-infarct zones in acute ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) and chronic myocardial infarction (MI).MethodsThis retrospective study included 35 patients with acute STEMI, 21 with chronic MI, and 35 healthy controls who underwent cardiac MRI with T1ρ, native T1, and T2 mapping between March 2022 and August 2025. LGE was used to delineate peri-infarct zones in patients with MI. A linear mixed-effects model accounting for within-participant clustering compared T1ρ values across myocardial segments. Receiver operating characteristic analysis was used to assess diagnostic performance.ResultsAmong the 56 patients with MI (mean age, 57 ± 13 years; 49 men), global T1ρ values were elevated in both acute STEMI and chronic MI compared with controls (both p < 0.001). T1ρ values were higher in peri-infarct zones than in control myocardium (all p < 0.05). For discriminating peri-infarct zones from control myocardium in acute STEMI, T1ρ (AUC, 0.95) performed comparably to T2 (AUC, 0.97; p = 0.11) and better than native T1 (AUC, 0.88; p = 0.02). In chronic MI, T1ρ (AUC, 0.91) outperformed native T1 (AUC, 0.81; p = 0.04) and T2 (AUC, 0.68; p < 0.001).ConclusionEndogenous T1ρ mapping may provide a contrast-free biomarker for myocardial tissue characterization in MI, with diagnostic performance comparable to T2 in acute STEMI and superior to native T1 and T2 in chronic MI.

Introduction

Myocardial infarction (MI) is a major cause of adverse ventricular remodeling, malignant arrhythmias, and heart failure. In acute ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), myocardial injury is characterized by edema, necrosis, inflammation, and microvascular obstruction, whereas chronic MI is dominated by replacement fibrosis and scar formation once the acute injury has resolved [1]. Cardiac MRI (CMR) with late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) remains the reference standard for delineating infarcted myocardium and replacement scar [2,3]. Attention has increasingly shifted from the infarcted myocardium itself to the peri-infarct zone, a heterogeneous transition zone between densely infarcted and apparently viable myocardium. In acute MI, this region may include myocardium with varying degrees of edema, inflammation, and reversible or irreversible myocyte injury; in chronic MI, it may represent a fibrotic border zone with persistent structural heterogeneity. The peri-infarct zone has been associated with adverse ventricular remodeling, conduction abnormalities, and arrhythmogenic substrate formation, and its characterization may therefore provide prognostic information relevant to post-MI risk stratification [4,5].

Quantitative CMR techniques, including T1 and T2 mapping, enable myocardial tissue characterization beyond conventional LGE imaging. Native T1 mapping reflects a combination of intracellular and extracellular alterations and is elevated in both acute edema and chronic fibrosis, but it lacks specificity for distinguishing individual pathological processes [6]. T2 mapping is highly sensitive to myocardial free water content and is therefore useful for detecting edema in acute injury, although its diagnostic value declines once edema has resolved in chronic MI [7]. Extracellular volume (ECV) quantifies interstitial expansion but requires a gadolinium-based contrast agent, limiting its use in patients with severe renal impairment or other contraindications to such agents. These limitations underscore the need for contrast-free imaging biomarkers that can characterize the heterogeneous tissue composition of peri-infarct myocardium.

Endogenous T1ρ mapping is an emerging CMR technique that quantifies spin–lattice relaxation in the rotating frame using spin-lock (SL) pulses [8,9]. Because T1ρ is sensitive to low-frequency water–macromolecule interactions, it may provide tissue information complementary to that obtained with conventional parametric mapping techniques [10]. This property is potentially relevant for peri-infarct myocardium, in which edema, inflammation, necrosis, interstitial expansion, and collagen-rich remodeling coexist to varying degrees. T1ρ mapping has been extensively investigated in cartilage, intervertebral discs, the brain, and the liver, whereas cardiac applications remain comparatively limited, particularly in ischemic heart disease. Although previous studies have suggested the potential of T1ρ mapping for characterizing myocardial injury and scar, its role in identifying peri-infarct zones in acute STEMI and chronic MI remains undefined [11].

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of in vivo endogenous T1ρ mapping for identifying LGE-defined peri-infarct zones in patients with acute STEMI and chronic MI, and to determine whether T1ρ provides stage-specific incremental value over native T1 and T2 mapping for this task. The chronic MI cohort was included to determine whether T1ρ mapping remains informative after acute edema has largely resolved, when peri-infarct tissue heterogeneity may be driven predominantly by fibrosis and matrix remodeling.

Methods

Participants

This retrospective, single-center study was approved by the institutional review board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (Approval No. PJ2022–09–60) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki; the requirement for written informed consent was waived.

Patients aged 18–80 years with MI were enrolled at our institution between March 2022 and August 2025. Participants with a first STEMI event, as defined by the 2017 European Society of Cardiology guidelines [12], who underwent reperfusion by primary percutaneous coronary intervention within 12 h of symptom onset were assigned to the acute STEMI group. In the acute STEMI group, CMR was performed 3–5 days after reperfusion. Patients with a first STEMI event who underwent CMR at least 6 months after reperfusion were included in the chronic MI group. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) incomplete CMR data; (b) hypertrophic or dilated cardiomyopathy; (c) known heart failure before the index event; (d) severe valvular heart disease; (e) atrial fibrillation or a high ventricular ectopic burden; (f) poor image quality for analysis; and (g) contraindications to MRI, including MRI-incompatible implanted devices, or contraindications to the gadolinium-based contrast agent used in this study, including prior severe hypersensitivity reaction and an estimated glomerular filtration rate < 30 mL/min/1.73 m². Healthy controls of comparable age and sex distribution, with no history of cardiovascular disease and receiving no cardiovascular medication, were also recruited. The study flowchart is shown in Fig. 1. High-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (hs-cTnT) data were retrospectively retrieved from clinical records. In the acute STEMI group, peak hs-cTnT was defined as the highest recorded value during the index hospitalization. In the chronic MI group, hs-cTnT measured at follow-up was used for analysis.Fig. 1Flowchart of participant inclusion and exclusion. CMR imaging protocol

All participants were imaged on a 1.5-T MRI scanner (Ingenia; Philips Healthcare, Best, the Netherlands) using a 32-channel phased-array coil with anterior and posterior elements. Cine images were acquired using balanced steady-state free precession sequences in short-axis slices covering the left ventricle (LV).

Short-axis T1ρ maps covering the entire LV were acquired using an SL-prepared steady-state free precession sequence, as previously described [13,14]. Images were acquired at four spin-lock times (TSL: 0, 10, 20, and 30 ms) with a recovery interval of three cardiac cycles, resulting in an acquisition time of approximately 10 heartbeats. Each T1ρ-weighted image was acquired during a single breath-hold and was electrocardiographically triggered during the diastolic rest period. A conventional B1/B0-compensated SL preparation was used.

Native T1 mapping was performed using a modified Look-Locker inversion recovery (MOLLI) sequence with a 5(3)3 scheme, followed by T2 mapping using a multi-echo gradient-spin-echo sequence at the basal, mid, and apical levels aligned with the T1ρ slices. After administration of gadopentetate dimeglumine (0.20 mmol/kg), post-contrast T1 mapping was performed using a MOLLI 4(1)3(1)2 scheme at the same slice positions, followed by LGE imaging using segmented phase-sensitive inversion recovery sequences in short-axis slices covering the entire LV. Healthy controls did not undergo contrast-enhanced imaging for ethical reasons. CMR image analysis

CMR datasets were analyzed offline using the commercially available cvi42 software (Version 5.6.6; Circle Cardiovascular Imaging) by two independent radiologists (with 5 and 6 years of CMR experience, respectively) who were blinded to clinical data and group assignment.

For LV morphologic and functional analysis, endocardial and epicardial borders and the valve planes were automatically contoured on short-axis cine images at end-diastole and end-systole, with manual correction as needed; papillary muscles and trabeculations were excluded from the LV blood pool and therefore included in LV mass. LV ejection fraction (LVEF), cardiac output (LVCO), cardiac index (LVCI), end-diastolic volume index (LVEDVi), end-systolic volume index (LVESVi), stroke volume index (LVSVi), and end-diastolic LV mass index were derived. For tissue characterization, T1ρ, native T1, T2, and post-contrast T1 maps were processed with a 10% endocardial and epicardial erosion margin to minimize partial-volume effects. Global T1ρ, native T1, and T2 values were computed automatically, and ECV was calculated from native and post-contrast T1 values of myocardium and blood pool together with the same-day hematocrit, as previously described [15].

A senior radiologist (with 15 years of CMR experience) quantified LGE with the full-width at half-maximum method, with manual correction as needed [16], while blinded to clinical data and quantitative T1ρ, native T1, and T2 values. With this method, LGE-positive voxels were defined as those with a signal intensity exceeding 50% of the maximal signal intensity within the hyperenhanced region. On the short-axis slice that best depicted the infarct, the LV myocardium was divided into 12 equiangular circumferential segments, with the anterior right ventricular insertion point used as the reference landmark (Fig. 2 and Fig. 3). Following previously published CMR approaches for infarct border-zone characterization [17], segments in which LGE-positive tissue occupied > 25% of the segmental area were classified as infarct segments; the two immediately adjacent segments on each side of the infarct region were classified as peri-infarct segments, and all remaining segments were classified as remote. This approach was used to maintain consistent correspondence between the LGE-defined infarct location and the same segments on the T1ρ, native T1, and T2 maps. ROIs delineated on LGE images were copied onto the corresponding T1ρ, native T1, and T2 maps to extract segmental parameter values. Minor spatial misregistration between sequences was corrected by end-expiratory breath-hold matching and software-based automatic alignment. Artifacts caused by trigger errors, incorrect motion correction, or susceptibility effects were evaluated by reviewing the original T1ρ-weighted images and corresponding T1ρ maps. For healthy controls, only global values were used for comparison, because LGE imaging was not performed. Blood pool and epicardial fat were excluded from all ROIs. To reduce measurement variability, each measurement was repeated three times by the same reader on separate occasions, and the mean of the three values was used for both the global and the segmental analyses.

Intra- and inter-observer reliability of global and segmental myocardial T1ρ measurements was assessed in 30 participants (10 randomly selected from each of the three groups). For intra-observer reliability, a radiologist (with 5 years of CMR experience) repeated the measurements after a 1-month interval and was blinded to the initial results. For inter-observer reliability, another radiologist (with 4 years of CMR experience) independently performed the measurements and was blinded to the first radiologist’s results.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). Sample size was estimated a priori based on previous T1ρ studies of myocardial tissue characterization [11,18], using the Hanley–McNeil approach. With a two-sided α of 0.05 and 80% power, at least 20 participants per group were estimated to be required to detect a clinically meaningful difference in area under the curve (AUC). Continuous variables were reported as means ± standard deviations or medians (interquartile ranges), and categorical variables as counts (percentages). Group comparisons used Student's t test, Mann–Whitney U test, one-way ANOVA, and Kruskal–Wallis H test as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test and Fisher's exact test. To account for age as a potential confounder, global measurements were analyzed using general linear models with group as a fixed factor and age as a covariate. Segmental measurements were compared using linear mixed-effects models that included segment type (infarct, peri-infarct, and remote) and group as fixed effects, participant as a random intercept, and age as a fixed-effect covariate. Post hoc Bonferroni corrections were applied to estimated marginal means. Pearson (r) or Spearman (rs) correlation coefficients were used to assess associations among parametric mapping values (T1ρ, native T1, T2, and ECV), between hs-cTnT and global T1ρ, and between global T1ρ and LV functional metrics. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis evaluated the diagnostic performance of T1ρ, native T1, and T2 for peri-infarct zones, with AUC compared using the DeLong test and optimal cutoffs determined by Youden’s index. Intra- and inter-observer agreement for T1ρ was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) and Bland–Altman analysis. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study participants and baseline characteristics

Of 66 patients with MI who underwent CMR during the study period, 10 were excluded; the final cohort comprised 56 patients with MI: 35 with acute STEMI (mean age, 54 ± 12 years; 33 men) and 21 with chronic MI (mean age, 60 ± 13 years; 16 men). Thirty-five healthy controls (mean age, 53 ± 11 years; 27 men) were also enrolled (Fig. 1). No significant differences were observed in age (p = 0.09) or sex (p = 0.06) among the groups. For tissue characterization parameters, global T1ρ, native T1, and T2 values were significantly elevated in both MI groups compared with controls (all p < 0.05). Specifically, patients with acute STEMI had higher T2 (61.5 ± 2.6 ms vs. 56.3 ± 2.3 ms, p < 0.001) and native T1 values (1149.1 ± 43.5 ms vs. 1112.2 ± 49.4 ms, p = 0.006) than patients with chronic MI, whereas global T1ρ (56.6 ± 3.8 ms vs. 55.1 ± 3.1 ms, p = 0.29) and ECV values (35.3 ± 2.4% vs. 34.7 ± 2.4%; p = 0.36) did not differ significantly between the two groups.

In acute STEMI, global T1ρ values were correlated positively with native T1 (r = 0.66, p < 0.001), T2 (r = 0.76, p < 0.001), and ECV values (r = 0.70, p < 0.001). In chronic MI, T1ρ values were correlated positively with native T1 (r = 0.60, p = 0.004), T2 (r = 0.48, p = 0.03), and ECV values (r = 0.69, p < 0.001).

Peak hs-cTnT in acute STEMI was 3110 ng/L (1263–6232) and correlated significantly with global T1ρ (rs = 0.58, p < 0.001). In the chronic MI cohort, hs-cTnT at follow-up was 18.6 ng/L (11.2–31.4) and was not significantly correlated with global T1ρ (rs = 0.35, p = 0.12).

Correlation analyses between global T1ρ and LV functional metrics were performed separately in the acute STEMI and chronic MI groups (Supplementary Material). In acute STEMI, global T1ρ was negatively correlated with LVEF (r = −0.36, p = 0.04) and positively correlated with LVESVi (r = 0.39, p = 0.02). In chronic MI, global T1ρ was negatively correlated with LV mass index (r = −0.46, p = 0.04). No other LV functional metrics showed significant correlations (p > 0.05).

Segmental CMR measurements

As shown in Table 4 and illustrated in Fig. 4, T1ρ, native T1, T2, and ECV values were significantly higher in the infarct core and peri-infarct zones than in remote myocardium (all p < 0.05) in both acute STEMI and chronic MI, with the exception of T2 in chronic MI. Compared with control myocardium, T1ρ, native T1, and T2 were significantly elevated in the infarct core and peri-infarct zones of both groups (all p < 0.05). Furthermore, the infarct core segments showed higher values than the peri-infarct segments for all these parameters (all p < 0.05). In chronic MI, T2 values were comparable between the peri-infarct and remote zones (p = 0.16). After age adjustment, global T1ρ still did not differ significantly between acute STEMI and chronic MI (adjusted p = 0.06), and segmental T1ρ remained significantly elevated in the peri-infarct zones relative to controls (p < 0.001) (Supplementary Material). Diagnostic performance of T1ρ, native T1, and T2 mapping for discriminating peri-infarct zones

ROC analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of T1ρ, native T1, and T2 mapping for distinguishing peri-infarct zones from healthy controls (Fig. 6). In acute STEMI, T1ρ (AUC, 0.95; 95% CI, 0.90–0.98) and T2 (AUC, 0.97; 95% CI, 0.93–0.99) outperformed native T1 (AUC, 0.88; 95% CI, 0.81–0.93; z = 2.26, p = 0.02 for T1ρ vs. native T1; z = 3.36, p < 0.001 for T2 vs. T1), with no significant difference between T1ρ and T2 (z = 1.60, p = 0.11). The optimal cutoff values for T1ρ, native T1, and T2 were 54 ms, 1120 ms, and 58 ms, respectively, corresponding to sensitivities of 0.88, 0.68, and 0.90 and specificities of 0.94, 1.00, and 0.94. In chronic MI, T1ρ (AUC, 0.91; 95% CI, 0.84–0.96) outperformed both native T1 (AUC, 0.81; 95% CI, 0.72–0.88; z = 2.04, p = 0.04) and T2 (AUC, 0.68; 95% CI, 0.59–0.77; z = 4.15, p < 0.001). The optimal cutoff values for T1ρ, native T1, and T2 were 52 ms, 1095 ms, and 56 ms, respectively, with sensitivities of 0.85, 0.74, and 0.47 and specificities of 0.86, 0.86, and 0.83. When remote myocardium within the MI cohort was used as the reference, T1ρ (AUC, 0.89; 95% CI, 0.83–0.94) showed comparable performance to T2 (AUC, 0.91; 95% CI, 0.85–0.95; p = 0.47) and superior performance to native T1 (AUC, 0.82; 95% CI, 0.74–0.88; p = 0.048) in acute STEMI. In chronic MI, T1ρ (AUC, 0.87; 95% CI, 0.79–0.93) outperformed both native T1 (AUC, 0.76; 95% CI, 0.67–0.84; p = 0.03) and T2 (AUC, 0.62; 95% CI, 0.52–0.71; p < 0.001) (Supplementary Material).Fig. 6Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves show the diagnostic performance of T1ρ, native T1, and T2 in distinguishing peri-infarct zones from healthy controls in acute ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) (A) and chronic myocardial infarction (MI) (B).

Discussion

In this retrospective study of patients with acute STEMI and chronic MI, endogenous T1ρ mapping enabled contrast-free myocardial tissue characterization and discriminated the LGE-defined peri-infarct zone from control myocardium with high accuracy. For distinguishing peri-infarct tissue from healthy myocardium, its diagnostic performance was comparable to that of T2 mapping in acute STEMI and superior to that of both native T1 and T2 mapping in chronic MI. Global T1ρ was elevated in both MI groups compared with controls but did not differ significantly between acute and chronic MI, whereas T2 and native T1 were higher in acute STEMI than in chronic MI. Collectively, these findings suggest that T1ρ may serve as a contrast-free marker of peri-infarct tissue alteration, particularly when gadolinium administration is contraindicated or unavailable.

T1ρ is sensitive to low-frequency molecular interactions between water and macromolecules such as collagen, and therefore may detect tissue alterations related to edema, necrosis, inflammation, and fibrosis [10]. Our findings are consistent with prior reports by Muthupillai et al. [19], who described increased T1ρ-weighted signal in acute infarction, and by van Oorschot et al. [20], who reported elevated T1ρ values in chronic scar. More recent cardiac T1ρ studies have reported its value in ischemic and non-ischemic myocardial injury and in assessing the LGE-defined gray zone [11,18]; the present study extends this work to the peri-infarct zone in both acute STEMI and chronic MI. The similarity of global T1ρ values between acute STEMI and chronic MI may reflect the sensitivity of T1ρ to a broader range of tissue alterations than edema alone. In acute STEMI, membrane disruption, necrosis, edema, and inflammation may collectively prolong T1ρ; in chronic MI, collagen-rich replacement fibrosis and border-zone matrix remodeling may sustain T1ρ elevation after free water content has normalized. This observation is compatible with the hypothesis that T1ρ captures infarct-related tissue alterations at different stages after infarction. By contrast, T2 is more directly coupled to free water content and may normalize after edema resolution, which may explain its higher values in acute STEMI and markedly lower discriminatory performance in chronic MI [21].

LGE imaging remains the clinical reference standard for infarct visualization because it delineates areas of necrosis and fibrosis on the basis of gadolinium contrast kinetics. T1ρ mapping, by contrast, does not require exogenous contrast and instead probes intrinsic tissue properties. In the present study, infarcted segments showed significantly elevated T1ρ values compared with remote myocardium, in keeping with previous experimental and clinical observations [18]. The sensitivity of T1ρ to both reversible and irreversible tissue changes may explain its elevation not only in infarcted myocardium but also in adjacent tissue [21]. In acute STEMI, T1ρ mapping performed comparably to T2 mapping, possibly because both capture edema-related peri-infarct injury, while T1ρ may additionally reflect water–macromolecule interactions after myocardial damage. These findings suggest that T1ρ is a candidate contrast-free method for peri-infarct tissue characterization in the acute setting.

In chronic MI, T1ρ discriminated the peri-infarct zone from control myocardium more accurately than did native T1 or T2 mapping. The peri-infarct zone has increasingly been recognized as an arrhythmogenic substrate with potential prognostic relevance after MI [22]. Our findings are also in line with the work of Dong et al. [11], in which T1ρ showed diagnostic accuracy superior to that of native T1 for identifying the LGE-defined gray zone in hypertrophic and dilated cardiomyopathy. The same LGE-based segmental definition was applied to both stages to enable consistent comparison across mapping techniques. The underlying biological substrate, however, differs: acute peri-infarct tissue may reflect edema, inflammation, and partially injured myocardium, whereas chronic peri-infarct tissue may represent a fibrotic transition zone with scar heterogeneity. Taken together, these results indicate that T1ρ discriminated LGE-defined peri-infarct myocardium more accurately than native T1 in both disease stages, and more accurately than T2 only in chronic MI; in acute STEMI, T1ρ and T2 performed comparably. A possible explanation is that T1ρ is sensitive to slow molecular motion and water–macromolecule exchange, and is therefore responsive to the mixed tissue composition of the peri-infarct zone, in which partial myocyte injury, interstitial expansion, variable edema, inflammation, and patchy collagen deposition coexist. This interpretation is consistent with the observed advantage of T1ρ over T2 mapping in chronic MI, in which fibrotic remodeling becomes more prominent once edema has resolved.

Correlative analyses further supported the biological plausibility of T1ρ as a tissue-level imaging biomarker. In acute STEMI, T1ρ values were positively correlated with T2 and ECV, consistent with shared sensitivity to increased free water content and extracellular expansion in acutely injured myocardium [18,21]. In chronic MI, the positive correlation between T1ρ and ECV (r = 0.69) suggests that T1ρ may reflect extracellular matrix expansion without requiring contrast administration, supporting its potential role as an alternative to ECV when gadolinium is contraindicated. The positive correlation between global T1ρ and peak hs-cTnT in acute STEMI further suggests an association with the magnitude of myocardial injury, whereas the absence of a significant correlation in chronic MI is consistent with a transition from active cardiomyocyte necrosis to collagen-rich matrix remodeling. The LV functional correlations provided additional support. In acute STEMI, higher global T1ρ was associated with lower LVEF and higher LVESVi, plausibly reflecting greater edema, necrosis, inflammation, and impaired systolic function during early infarct evolution. In chronic MI, the negative correlation between T1ρ and LV mass index may be related to scar formation, infarct wall thinning, and myocardial tissue loss rather than active injury, although LV mass is also influenced by compensatory hypertrophy and loading conditions. Together with the segmental findings, these correlations are consistent with a role for T1ρ as a biologically relevant marker for peri-infarct tissue characterization across both disease stages, although prospective validation is warranted [11]. Importantly, the present analysis was retrospective and evaluated diagnostic performance only, and therefore cannot establish the prognostic value of T1ρ-based peri-infarct characterization. Given the tissue-level associations observed here and in previous T1ρ studies, prospective studies incorporating clinical outcomes will be needed to determine whether this characterization provides incremental prognostic value beyond conventional LGE imaging.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, particularly in the chronic MI group, which may limit generalizability and reduce power for subgroup analyses. External validation in larger, multicenter cohorts will therefore be required before endogenous T1ρ mapping can be considered for broader clinical implementation. Second, histopathologic validation was not available, precluding direct correlation between T1ρ values and myocardial tissue composition; the biological interpretations proposed above therefore remain speculative and are based on imaging findings and prior literature. Third, peri-infarct zones were defined on the basis of adjacency to infarcted segments, which may be influenced by partial-volume effects, measurement noise, and reduced spatial specificity; this segment-based definition remains an indirect approximation that cannot fully capture the true spatial extent of border-zone tissue, although this approach is consistent with previous studies [17] and provides a practical method for segmental analysis. Fourth, healthy volunteers did not undergo contrast-enhanced imaging for ethical reasons, and therefore ECV values were not available for direct comparison with the MI groups. Fifth, clinical outcome data such as short-term complications after STEMI, arrhythmic events, mortality, or implantable cardioverter-defibrillator therapy were unavailable, precluding direct assessment of prognostic value or comparison between patients with and without adverse events. Finally, longitudinal data tracking T1ρ changes from the acute to chronic phase in the same individuals were not collected.

Conclusions

Endogenous T1ρ mapping may serve as a contrast-free MRI biomarker for characterizing myocardial tissue in both acute STEMI and chronic MI. For identifying peri-infarct zones, T1ρ mapping demonstrated diagnostic performance comparable to that of T2 mapping and superior to that of native T1 mapping in acute STEMI, and superior to that of both native T1 and T2 mapping in chronic MI. These findings support the potential clinical utility of T1ρ mapping as a non-contrast approach to myocardial tissue assessment; because no clinical outcome data were available, its value for post-MI risk stratification remains to be established. As T1ρ mapping requires only a brief additional acquisition, its incorporation into routine multiparametric CMR protocols alongside native T1 and T2 mapping may be feasible; prospective multicenter studies with longitudinal follow-up and clinical outcome data (such as arrhythmia, heart failure progression, mortality, or ventricular remodeling) are warranted to determine whether T1ρ mapping provides prognostic information beyond conventional LGE imaging.

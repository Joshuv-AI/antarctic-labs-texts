CircSEC24A induces KLF8 expression to promote the malignant progression of non‐small cell lung cancer by regulating miR‐1253
Authors: Wei Xiong, Jinhua Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aimed to analyze the role of circSEC24A in non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and its underlying mechanism.MethodsRNA levels of circSEC24A, microRNA‐1253 (miR‐1253), and KLF transcription factor 8 (KLF8) were detected by quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction. Protein expression was analyzed by western blot or immunohistochemistry assay. Cell proliferation and apoptosis were investigated by colony formation assay, 5‐ethynyl‐2′‐deoxyuridine assay, and flow cytometry analysis. Glycolysis was evaluated by commercial kits. Dual‐luciferase reporter assay and RNA immunoprecipitation assay were conducted to identify the associations among circSEC24A, miR‐1253, and KLF8. Xenograft mouse model assay was used to evaluate the effect of circSEC24A on tumor tumorigenesis.ResultsCircSEC24A and KLF8 were upregulated, while miR‐1253 was downregulated in NSCLC. CircSEC24A knockdown inhibited proliferation and glycolysis but induced the apoptosis of NSCLC cells. CircSEC24A acted as a miR‐1253 sponge and regulated NSCLC cell malignancy by targeting miR‐1253. KLF8 was identified as a target of miR‐1253, and its overexpression attenuated miR‐1253‐induced effects in NSCLC cells. Besides, circSEC24A upregulated KLF8 by sponging miR‐1253. Further, circSEC24A knockdown suppressed NSCLC cell tumorigenesis in vivo.ConclusionsCircSEC24A silencing inhibited NSCLC cell malignancy through the miR‐1253/KLF8 pathway, providing a potential therapeutic target for NSCLC.Keywords: circSEC24A, KLF8, miR‐1253, non‐small cell lung cancer

INTRODUCTION

As a common malignancy in the world, non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) represents a histopathological subtype of lung cancer that poses a significant burden on affected individuals. 1 Despite advancements in diagnostic and treatment strategies for lung cancer, 2 , 3 survival rates remain inadequate, primarily due to the recurrence and metastasis of the disease. Biologically targeted therapy has emerged as a highly effective modality for advanced NSCLC patients. 4 Therefore, to improve survival rates for NSCLC patients, an in‐depth investigation of the molecular mechanism underlying the development and progression of the disease is necessary.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are special non‐coding molecules that have become study hotspots in the RNA field. In comparison with linear RNAs, circRNAs have circular structures, which are not affected by RNA exonucleases, resulting in a more stable expression profile. 5 CircRNAs are rich in microRNA (miRNA) binding sites and can function as miRNA sponges, thereby inhibiting the regulatory effects of miRNAs on mRNA expression. 6 CircRNAs have been implicated in the development of diseases, including cancers. 7 , 8 CircRNAs are aberrantly expressed in lung cancer and are a class of new frontiers in cancer research. CircRNAs can regulate the biological behaviors of lung cancer cells, such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, tumor microenvironment, and immune evasion. 9 Circ_0003528 is also termed as circSEC24A and is involved in the aggressiveness of hepatocellular carcinoma, 10 cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma, 11 and pancreatic cancer. 12 Recently, Li et al. reported that circSEC24A could distinguish NSCLC patients from normal controls, as predicted through GSE101586, GSEE101684, and GSE112214 data sets 13 ; however, circSEC24A's function in NSCLC progression remains unclear.

MiRNA has 18–24 nucleotides in length and plays pivotal regulatory roles in tumor tumorigenesis. 14 MiRNA mainly affects gene expression by targeting mRNA, and abnormal miRNA expression can influence cell homeostasis through the regulation of target genes. 15 MiRNA seems to be the most attractive among currently tested molecular factors related to lung cancer progression. miR‐490‐3p, 16 miR‐139‐3p, 17 and miR‐196b‐5p 18 regulate NSCLC development. Another miRNA, microRNA‐1253 (miR‐1253), has an inhibitory function in NSCLC occurrence, as revealed by in vitro and in vivo assays. 19 Intriguingly, genome‐wide bioinformatic analysis showed that circSEC24A is potentially bound to miR‐1253. However, whether the progression of NSCLC involves the interaction between circSEC24A and miR‐1253 remains to be fully elucidated by researchers.

This study examined the role of circSEC24A in the biological behaviors of NSCLC cells. The study postulated that circSEC24A might interact with miR‐1253 to regulate the malignancy of NSCLC cells, a hypothesis that was subsequently confirmed within this research. Given the recognized involvement of the circRNA/miRNA/mRNA network in the tumorigenesis of cancers, 20 the study expanded our investigation to identify the downstream targets of the circSEC24A/miR‐1253 axis in modulating NSCLC cell tumorigenic properties. The study was the first one to reveal the mechanism of circSEC24A in lung cancer progression involving the miR‐1253/KLF transcription factor 8 (KLF8) axis, which provides a potential target to hinder NSCLC.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Clinical samples

The present work involved 39 patients who were diagnosed with NSCLC at Zigong First People's Hospital. After approval by the Ethics Committee of Zigong First People's Hospital, clinical NSCLC tissue samples and normal lung tissues (the paired nearby non‐tumorous tissues) were collected from these participants and timely stored at −80°C. The 39 cases of NSCLC included 17 adenocarcinomas, 12 squamous cell carcinomas, and 10 large cell carcinomas. Tissue identification was performed by two pathologists based on morphological findings and immunohistochemical staining following a published methodology. 21 All participants provided their written informed consent.

Cell culture and treatment

NSCLC cell lines including HCC827 and A549 cells and a normal human bronchial epithelial cell line (BEAS‐2B) were purchased from EK‐Bioscience (Shanghai, China). The ExPASy Cellosaurus database (https://web.expasy.org/cellosaurus/) was used to determine whether these cells were contaminated. Cells were maintained in Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (DMEM; EK‐Bioscience), Roswell Park Memorial Institute‐1640 (RPMI‐1640) medium (EK‐Bioscience), or Ham's F12K medium (EK‐Bioscience) at 37°C with 5% CO2. In addition, to block the synthesis of new RNA, HCC827 and A549 cells were incubated with actinomycin D (2 μg/mL, Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) for 0, 4, 8, 12, and 24 h. Actinomycin D was diluted using RPMI‐1640 or Ham's F12K medium in advance. After incubation, RNA was isolated for subsequent circSEC24A and SEC24A expression analysis.

Plasmid and oligonucleotide transfection

Small hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) and small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) of circSEC24A, miR‐1253 mimics, miR‐1253 inhibitors, and the matched controls were provided by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). A KLF8 overexpression plasmid was built using a pcDNA 3.1 vector, the full‐length coding sequence of KLF8, and T4 ligase (TaKaRa, Dalian, China). When cells reached 70% confluence, oligonucleotides and plasmids were transfected into NSCLC cells in line with the manual of TurboFect (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, USA) at room temperature. After 48‐h culture, the cells were harvested for transfection efficiency analysis.

Quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction

To determine circSEC24A, miR‐1253, and KLF8 expression, total RNA from lung tissues (weight 10–100 mg, diameter 0.4–0.6 cm) and cells was extracted using the RNA STAT‐60 reagent (Biohub International Trade Biotech, Shanghai, China) and then was reverse‐transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) with specific cDNA synthesis kits (TaKaRa). Subsequently, cDNA amplification was performed using a PCR system (Thermo Fisher) with SYBR Mix (TaKaRa) as well as designed primers (shown in Table 1). Results were analyzed using the 2−∆∆Ct method. In addition, RNase R (20 U/μL; ZhongBei LinGe Biotechnology, Beijing, China) was mixed with RNase R reaction buffer and then incubated with 2 μg of RNA at 37°C for 30 min. A control reaction using diethyl pyrocarbonate‐treated water instead of RNase R served as an internal control.

Cell proliferation assay

For cell colony formation assay, 500 NSCLC cells were placed into six‐well plates and subjected to 2‐week culture at 37°C in a humid incubator. Then, crystal violet (Abcam) was added into each well for colony visualization. Finally, the ImageJ software was used to determine the number of positive colonies.

For 5‐ethynyl‐2′‐deoxyuridine (EdU) assay, 96‐well plates pre‐filled with EdU labeling media were used to culture NSCLC cells at 37°C. Then, anti‐EdU working solution (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) and Triton X‐100 (Abcam) were used to incubate the cells. After being labeled by 2‐(4‐amidinophenyl)‐6‐indolecarbamidine dihydrochloride (DAPI) (Abcam), the cells were assessed through fluorescent microscopy.

Cell apoptosis analysis

An assay utilizing a commercial apoptosis detection kit (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) was used to analyze the cell apoptotic rate. Briefly, NSCLC cells were harvested and stained with annexin V–fluorescein isothiocyanate (annexin V–FITC) and propidium iodide (PI), followed by analyzing with a FACSort flow cytometer.

Western blot assay

Total proteins were isolated from peritoneal nodules and lung cells using radio immunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer solution supplied with a protease inhibitor. The proteins went through separation with bis‐tris‐acrylamide gels and then were immediately transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes (GenScript, Nanjing, China). Separated proteins were subjected to incubation with anti‐proliferating cell nuclear antigen (anti‐PCNA) (1:1000; PA5‐32541, Thermo Fisher), anti‐B‐cell lymphoma 2 (anti‐Bcl‐2) (1:1000; PA5‐20068, Thermo Fisher), anti‐Bcl‐2‐like protein 4 (anti‐Bax) (1:1000; PA5‐78857, Thermo Fisher), anti‐recombinant glucose transporter 1 (anti‐GLUT1) (1:1000; PA5‐32428, Thermo Fisher), anti‐hexokinase 2 (anti‐HK2) (1:1000; PA5‐17257, Thermo Fisher), anti‐KLF8 (1:1000; PA5‐40390, Thermo Fisher), anti‐β‐actin (1:2000; PA1‐183, Thermo Fisher), and secondary antibodies (Thermo Fisher). Finally, a densitometer was used to detect the band intensities.

Glycolysis analysis

The glucose consumption, lactate production, and ATP level in HCC827 and A549 cells were analyzed using Glucose Assay Kit (ab65333, Abcam), Lactate Assay Kit (ab65331, Abcam), and ADP/ATP Ratio Assay Kit (ab65313, Abcam), respectively, following the manufacturer's instructions provided in the kit guidebooks. A microplate reader (Thermo Fisher) was used to analyze glucose consumption and lactate production, whereas a luminometer (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) was used to detect the ATP level.

Dual‐luciferase reporter assay

MiR‐1253‐binding sites within circSEC24A and KLF8 were predicted by using the Circular RNA Interactome and TargetScan online databases. CircSEC24A and KLF8 3′ untranslated region (3′UTR) sequences containing miR‐1253‐binding sites or the mutated binding regions were introduced into the pmirGLO vector, achieving wild‐type (WT) and mutant (MUT) circSEC24A and KLF8 3′UTR reporter plasmids. Subsequently, 200 ng of reporter plasmids was transfected into NSCLC cells with 50 nM miR‐1253 mimics or mimic control (miR‐NC) utilizing TurboFect (Thermo Fisher). After 48 h, the cells went through luciferase activity analysis using Dual‐Lucy Assay Kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China).

RNA immunoprecipitation

HCC827 and A549 cells were transfected with miR‐1253 mimics or miR‐NC for 48 h and lysed using complete RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) lysis buffer. Subsequently, magnetic beads (Millipore, Bradford, MA, USA) were mixed with wash buffer and incubated with anti‐Ago2 (Abcam) and anti‐immunoglobulin G (anti‐IgG) (Abcam), respectively, followed by incubating with cell lysates for 6 h. Finally, circSEC24A and KLF8 expressions were quantified by quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction (qRT‐PCR).

Animal experiment

Male BALB/c nude mice (5 weeks old; Charles River, Beijing, China) were divided into two groups with six mice per group. Twenty hundred microliters of PBS containing 5 × 106 A549 cells stably expressing sh‐circSEC24A or sh‐NC was injected into the mice. Tumor volume was calculated every 7 days using the formula volume (mm3) = width2 × length/2. After 28 days, the nude mice were euthanized and forming tumors were harvested for tumor weight and gene expression analysis. Animal manipulates were conducted after approval from the Animal Care and Use Committee of Zigong First People's Hospital.

Immunohistochemistry

As instructed, 22 4‐μm‐thick tumor sections from tumor xenograft assay were heated at 60°C for 20 min and then went through antigen retrieval using sodium citrate buffer in a microwave oven. Then, H2O2 (Solarbio) was used to block endogenous peroxidase activity, and the sections were incubated with primary antibodies of KLF8 (1:100; Thermo Fisher) and PCNA (1:100; PA5‐32541, Thermo Fisher) and a biotinylated secondary antibody. The sections were counterstained using hematoxylin and analyzed under a confocal microscope.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using the GraphPad Prism software, and results are shown as mean ± standard deviation. Two‐tailed Student's t‐tests, Wilcoxon signed‐rank test, or one‐way analysis of variance was used to explore statistical differences. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

CircSEC24A expression was upregulated in NSCLC tissues and cells

To begin, the study detected circSEC24A expression in NSCLC tissues and normal lung tissues by qRT‐PCR. The results showed that circSEC24A expression was upregulated in NSCLC tissues in comparison with that in normal lung tissues (Figure 1a). The study also discovered that circSEC24A expression was significantly associated with distant metastasis, T stage, and TNM stages of NSCLC patients (Table S1). In addition, the study showed a high expression of circSEC24A in NSCLC cell lines (HCC827 and A549) than in normal human bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS‐2B) (Figure 1b). Subsequently, the study confirmed the circular structure of circSEC24A using divergent primers, convergent primers, RNase R, and actinomycin D. For example, when using divergent primers, circSEC24A was successfully amplified from the cDNA isolated from HCC827 and A549 cells (Figure 1c). CircSEC24A was resistant to RNase R digestion when compared with linear SEC24A (Figure 1d,e). As shown in Figure 1f,g, the transcript half‐life of circSEC24A exceeded 24 h, whereas the transcript half‐life of linear SEC24A was approximately 8 h. The above data suggested that circSEC24A might be involved in NSCLC progression.

CircSEC24A expression was upregulated in non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) tissues and cells. (a, b) CircSEC24A expression was detected by quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction in NSCLC tissues (N = 39), normal lung tissues (N = 39), BEAS‐2B cells, HCC827 cells, and A549 cells. (c–g) The circular structure of circSEC24A was identified using divergent primers, convergent primers, RNase R, and actinomycin D. *p < 0.05.

CircSEC24A depletion repressed NSCLC cell proliferation and glycolysis and induced cell apoptosis

The study then analyzed the effects of circSEC24A on the biological behaviors of NSCLC cells. To this end, the study transfected two siRNAs of circSEC24A into HCC827 and A549 cells. The data showed the success of circSEC24A knockdown in HCC827 and A549 cells (Figure 2a). Subsequently, the results showed that circSEC24A depletion inhibited NSCLC cell proliferation but induced cell apoptosis, accompanied by decreases in PCNA and Bcl‐2 protein expression and an increase in Bax protein expression, as shown in Figure 2b–f. In addition, the study observed that circSEC24A silencing repressed glucose metabolism. For instance, circSEC24A absence reduced glucose consumption, lactate production, and ATP production in HCC827 and A549 cells (Figure 3a–c). Moreover, the decreased expression of circSEC24A led to inhibition in GLUT1 and HK2 protein expression in HCC827 and A549 cells (Figure 3d,e). Thus, the above findings indicated that circSEC24A might act as an oncogene in NSCLC progression.

CircSEC24A acted as a miR‐1253 sponge in NSCLC cells

The circular RNA Interactome online database was used to predict miRNAs with circSEC24A‐binding sites. As shown in Figure 4a, miR‐1253, which was identified as a candidate, contained circSEC24A‐binding sites. Subsequently, the results showed that miR‐1253 expression was upregulated in HCC827 and A549 cells after transfection with miR‐1253 mimics, but it was downregulated after transfection with miR‐1253 inhibitors (Figure 4b), indicating the success of miR‐1253 overexpression or knockdown. The study found that miR‐1253 introduction repressed the luciferase activity of circSEC24A WT but not that of circSEC24A MUT in HCC827 and A549 cells (Figure 4c,d). Meanwhile, circSEC24A was enriched in Ago2 antibody‐induced co‐precipitated RNAs after transfection with miR‐1253 mimics when compared with the control groups, as revealed by the RIP assay (Figure 4e,f). In addition, miR‐1253 expression was downregulated in NSCLC tissues and cells (HCC827 and A549) in comparison with that in normal lung tissues or normal human bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS‐2B) (Figure 4g,h). As presented in Figure 4i, circSEC24A expression was negatively correlated with miR‐1253 expression in NSCLC tissues. Further, circSEC24A depletion increased miR‐1253 expression, whereas the effect was attenuated after transfection with miR‐1253 inhibitors (Figure 4j). These findings indicated that circSEC24A bound to miR‐1253 in HCC827 and A549 cells.

MiR‐1253 depletion rescued circSEC24A knockdown‐induced effects in NSCLC cells

The study silenced both circSEC24A and miR‐1253 in HCC827 and A549 cells and then determined the consequent effects on cell proliferation, apoptosis, and glycolysis. The results showed that circSEC24A knockdown repressed cell proliferation, induced cell apoptosis, decreased PCNA and Bcl‐2 expression, and increased Bax protein expression; however, these effects were relieved after transfection with miR‐1253 inhibitors (Figure 5a–e). Subsequently, the study observed that circSEC24A knockdown inhibited glucose consumption, lactate production, and ATP production, but these effects were restored by decreasing miR‐1253 expression (Figure 5f–h). As shown in Figure 5i,j, circSEC24A depletion decreased GLUT1 and HK2 production, whereas these effects were restored when miR‐1253 expression was silenced. These results showed that circSEC24A combined with miR‐1253 to regulate NSCLC cell processes.

CircSEC24A combined with miR‐1253 to regulate KLF8 in HCC827 and A549 cells

The TargetScan online database was used to predict the targets of miR‐1253. The results of this analysis revealed that KLF8 possessed miR‐1253‐binding sites (Figure 6a). Subsequently, the results showed that miR‐1253 overexpression repressed the luciferase activity of KLF8 3′UTR WT but not that of KLF8 3′UTR MUT (Figure 6b,c). Moreover, the RIP assay showed that KLF8 was enriched in the miR‐1253 transfection group after treatment of the Ago2 antibody in comparison with that in the miR‐NC transfection group (Figure 6d,e). As presented in Figure 6f,g, KLF8 expression was upregulated in NSCLC tissues when compared with that in normal lung tissues. Consistently, KLF8 expression was higher in NSCLC cells (HCC827 and A549) than in BEAS‐2B cells (Figure 6h). In addition, the results showed that KLF8 was negatively correlated with miR‐1253 expression, but it was positively correlated with circSEC24A expression in NSCLC tissues (Figure 6i,j). Comparatively, miR‐1253 introduction decreased KLF8 expression in HCC827 and A549 cells, which was attenuated by increasing KLF8 expression (Figure 6k). Further, circSEC24A knockdown inhibited KLF8 production, whereas the effect was relieved when miR‐1253 expression was decreased (Figure 6l). Thus, circSEC24A mediated the miR‐1253/KLF8 pathway in HCC827 and A549 cells.

MiR‐1253 combined with KLF8 to repress NSCLC cell malignancy

Given the above results, the study continued to determine whether KLF8 was involved in miR‐1253‐mediated NSCLC cell processes. As shown in Figure 7a–c, the increased expression of miR‐1253 led to inhibition in cell proliferation and promotion in cell apoptosis, whereas these effects were attenuated after KLF8 overexpression. In addition, miR‐1253 mimics repressed the production of PCNA and Bcl‐2 and promoted Bax protein expression, but ectopic KLF8 expression restored these effects (Figure 7d,e). Moreover, the study observed that miR‐1253 mimics decreased glucose consumption, lactate production, ATP production, and GLUT1 and HK2 protein expression; however, these impacts were restored after co‐transfection with miR‐1253 mimics and the KLF8 overexpression plasmid (Figure 7f–j). Thus, these data demonstrated that miR‐1253 repressed NSCLC cell malignancy through KLF8.

CircSEC24A knockdown inhibited tumor formation in vivo

The in vitro data regarding the effects of circSEC24A on NSCLC cell malignancy were further validated using the in vivo mouse model assay. To this end, the research involved injecting nude mice with A549 cells that had been transfected with an shRNA targeting circSEC24A or with a non‐targeting control shRNA (sh‐NC). The results showed that tumor volume and weight were smaller and lighter, respectively, in the sh‐circSEC24A group than in the sh‐NC group (Figure 8a,b). In addition, quantitation analysis of primary tumors from A549 cells showed that mice treated with sh‐circSEC24A displayed low circSEC24A expression and high miR‐1253 expression than mice treated with sh‐NC (Figure 8c,d). The study also found that KLF8 and PCNA protein expressions were lower in the forming tissues from the sh‐circSEC24A group than in the tumor tissues from the sh‐NC group (Figure 8e,f). Therefore, the study concluded that circSEC24A depletion delayed tumor formation.

DISCUSSION

CircRNA is a hopeful diagnostic marker and a therapeutic molecule that has been investigated in some cancers, such as bladder cancer, ovarian cancer, and lung cancer. 23 The function of circSEC24A within cells encompasses various roles, including acting as a miRNA sponge, a regulator of translation, and a protein sponge, among others. 20 Increasing evidence suggests that circRNAs play a role in the tumorigenesis and progression of lung cancer. Despite this, the exact mechanisms by which circRNAs function in lung cancer remain elusive. Some researchers have suggested that the dysregulation of circRNA expression in lung cancer may be linked to the abnormal behaviors of genes. 9 Further studies are necessary to fully understand the complex relationships between circRNAs, gene expression, and the development of lung cancer. The present work reported that the mechanism of NSCLC progression was associated with the circSEC24A/miR‐1253/KLF8 axis.

Previous research by Liu et al. demonstrated increased expression of circSEC24A in NSCLC tissues compared with that in normal lung tissues. 24 This finding was corroborated in the current study, providing additional support for the role of circSEC24A in NSCLC. Consistent with reported data, 25 the study also demonstrated that circSEC24A expression was increased in NSCLC cells. Additionally, circSEC24A depletion repressed NSCLC cell proliferation and induced apoptosis in vitro. Furthermore, the reduction of circSEC24A expression delayed tumor formation in an in vivo model. Glucose metabolism can support anabolic processes by providing metabolic intermediates and is fundamental to most cancers. 26 The increased expression of GLUT1 and HK2 promotes glucose uptake, which is the initial step of glycolysis. 27 , 28 The present work also revealed for the first time that circSEC24A knockdown repressed glucose metabolism. Moreover, the study observed that circSEC24A‐deficient NSCLC cells showed low GLUT1 and HK2 expression, indicating that circSEC24A might contribute to glycolysis by inducing GLUT1 and HK2 production, thus promoting NSCLC malignant progression. These data suggested that circSEC24A might promote NSCLC progression by increasing cell proliferation and decreasing cell apoptosis by promoting glucose metabolism.

The study then analyzed the regulatory pathway of circSEC24A in NSCLC cell malignancy. Mechanism assays showed that circSEC24A bound to miR‐1253. Previously, miR‐1253 acted as a tumor suppressor in medulloblastoma, 29 osteosarcoma, 30 and colon cancer. 31 Moreover, prior research studies have confirmed the tumor‐inhibiting functions of miR‐1253 in lung cancer. For example, miR‐1253 sponged by circ_0087862 significantly inhibited NSCLC cell motility and promoted cell apoptosis. 32 MiR‐1253 was downregulated in lung cancer tissues, and cell tests demonstrated that miR‐1253 inhibited lung cancer cell proliferation and increased cell apoptosis through interaction with annexin A3. 33 Consistently, our data suggested that miR‐1253 repressed lung cancer progression by decreasing cell proliferation and increasing cell apoptosis. In addition, the study found that miR‐1253 repressed glucose metabolism. Further, it is noteworthy that the regulatory influence of circSEC24A on the malignancy of NSCLC cells appears to be intricately linked with the function of miR‐1253. The above evidence suggested that the circSEC24A/miR‐1253 pathway regulated NSCLC cell malignancy and glucose metabolism.

KLF8 is a sequence‐specific KLF family protein and its DNA‐binding zinc‐finger domain determines its functional specificity. 34 This protein is associated with v‐Src‐induced transformation and has an important part in cancer development. 35 KLF8 can participate in cell cycle regulation by targeting focal adhesion kinase. 36 In addition, it promotes cancer cell motility by inducing matrix metalloproteinase 9 production. 37 KLF8 is also a key transcriptional factor of glycolysis, and previous research data have pointed out that KLF8 can activate glycolytic enzyme‐related GLUT4. 38 Previous evidence has indicated that KLF8 overexpression is associated with the poor prognosis of NSCLC patients, increases NSCLC cell proliferation, and decreases cell apoptosis. 39 , 40 Our investigation showed that KLF8 was a target gene of miR‐1253, and it was upregulated in NSCLC tissues and cells. Moreover, the study showed that KLF8 overexpression promoted NSCLC cell proliferation and glucose metabolism and inhibited cell apoptosis. Importantly, KLF8 participated in miR‐1253‐mediated NSCLC cell malignancy and glucose metabolism by acting as a target of miR‐1253. KLF8 also showed promoting effects on GLUT1 and HK2 expression. Thus, circSEC24A might promote NSCLC progression by increasing glucose metabolism. This effect was mediated by inducing GLUT1 and HK2 expression through the circSEC24A/miR‐1253/KLF8 axis.

However, the study did not investigate the potential downstream targets of KLF8 or the specific signaling pathways that are affected by the regulatory axis involving circSEC24A, miR‐1253, and KLF8. In addition, the experimental design primarily relies on cellular assays and tumor xenograft assay, which may not fully reflect the complex interactions that occur in vivo within the intact tumor microenvironment. Further research is needed to address the above issues.

CONCLUSION

Therefore, NSCLC progression involves circSEC24A overexpression. The related mechanism may be that the increased circSEC24A expression induces KLF8 production through miR‐1253, leading to cell proliferation and glucose metabolism promotion and apoptosis inhibition, finally causing NSCLC malignant progression. Hence, the study provides new evidence of circSEC24A as a therapeutic molecule in lung cancer.

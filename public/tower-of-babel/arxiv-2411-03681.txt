Density and Symmetry in the Generalized Motzkin Numbers mod p p
Nadav Kohen

Abstract
We give a formula for the density of 0 0 in the sequence of generalized Motzkin numbers, M n a , b M^{a,b}_{n} , modulo a prime, p p , in terms of the first p p generalized central trinomial coefficients T n a , b mod p T^{a,b}_{n}\bmod p (with n < p n<p ). We apply our method to various other sequences to obtain similar formulas. We also prove that T p − 1 − n a , b ≡ ( b 2 − 4 ​ a 2 ) p − 1 2 − n ​ T n a , b ( mod p ) T^{a,b}_{p-1-n}\equiv(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}-n}T^{a,b}_{n}\pmod{p} to obtain tight lower bounds for the density of 0 0 in our sequences. This symmetry of the first p p central trinomial coefficients mod p p also appears in a couple of other applications, including the proof of a novel symmetry of the first p − 2 p-2 Motzkin numbers that is of independent interest: M p − 3 − n a , b ≡ ( b 2 − 4 ​ a 2 ) p − 3 2 − n ​ M n a , b ( mod p ) M^{a,b}_{p-3-n}\equiv(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-n}M^{a,b}_{n}\pmod{p} .

Introduction

The Motzkin numbers (A001006 of
[
5
]
),
M_{n}
, count the number of lattice paths from the origin to
(n,0)
, which do not go below the x-axis, with steps UP
=(1,1)
, LEVEL
=(1,0)
, and DOWN
=(1,-1)
. See
[
3
]
for many other combinatorial settings in which the Motzkin numbers arise. Similarly, the
generalized
Motzkin numbers,
M^{a,b}_{n}
, count the same lattice paths but where there are
a
distinct colors for UP and DOWN steps and
b
distinct colors for LEVEL steps, and two paths are only equal if they have the same steps and colors at each step. All of the results for the Motzkin numbers in this paper work in the generality of the generalized Motzkin numbers.

Some work has been done to characterize
M_{n}
and similar sequences modulo various prime powers. For example, Deutsch and Sagan
[
2
]
characterized
M_{n}\bmod 3
. They also described all
n
such that
M_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for
p=2,4,5
.

In Proposition
12
and Corollary
13
of this paper, we partially answer the question, “What is the density of the (generalized) Motzkin numbers that are divisible by a prime
p
?” Using these results, we reprove a lower bound of
\frac{2}{p(p-1)}
for the density of
0
in
M_{n}\bmod p
(for
p>2
), which has previously been drawn as a conclusion of Theorem 5 in
[
1
]
by Burns. Along the way, we also prove the novel symmetry
M^{a,b}_{p-3-n}\equiv(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-n}M^{a,b}_{n}
(Theorem
5
), and in Corollary
10
we provide a characterization of
n
such that
M^{a,b}_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
from which existing results such as that of Deutsch and Sagan for
p=5
can be recovered. All of this is accomplished by reducing the study of the (generalized) Motzkin numbers modulo
p
to the study of the lesser-known (generalized) central trinomial coefficients modulo
p
, which have a simpler characterization provided by Proposition
7
. In the final section of this paper, we demonstrate the general applicability of our approach by applying it to a few other sequences from the online encyclopedia
[
5
]
. This analysis yields a novel result (Corollary
17
): that for every prime,
p
, the density of
0
in the Riordan numbers (A005043 of
[
5
]
) mod
p
is equal to the density of
0
in the sequence A005773 of
[
5
]
mod
p
.

The central trinomial coefficients (A002426 of
[
5
]
),
T_{n}
, count the number of lattice paths from the origin to
(n,0)
using the same steps (UP, LEVEL, and DOWN) as Motzkin paths, but where there is no restriction that the paths not go below the x-axis (these are sometimes called
grand
Motzkin paths). As with the generalized Motzkin numbers, the
generalized
central trinomial coefficients,
T^{a,b}_{n}
have
a
colors associated with UP and DOWN steps and
b
colors associated with LEVEL steps. The central trinomial coefficients are so named because
T^{a,b}_{n}=\constantterm\left[(ax^{-1}+b+ax)^{n}\right]
where
ct
extracts the “constant term” of a Laurent polynomial. Under this paradigm, we note that
M^{a,b}_{n}=\constantterm\left[(ax^{-1}+b+ax)^{n}\cdot(1-x^{2})\right]
because the paths to
(n,0)
that do cross the x-axis can be bijected with arbitrary paths to
(n,2)
.

For readers familiar with the Rowland-Zeilberger automaton
[
7
]
, the intuition that the Motzkin numbers modulo
p
should be studied via the central trinomial coefficients comes from the observation that a random walk on the finite state machine for the Motzkin numbers quickly lands within, and never leaves, a sub-graph corresponding to the finite state machine for the central trinomial coefficients, which have a simple algebraic description given by Proposition
7
. The formal instantiation of this intuition, by rewriting
M^{a,b}_{n}\bmod p
using combinations of central trinomial coefficients, is provided in Propositions
1
and
9
. This approach of studying
M^{a,b}_{n}\bmod p
via the central trinomial coefficients has previously been used by the author in
[
4
]
to show that
M^{a,b}_{n}\bmod p
is uniformly recurrent if and only if
p\nmid T^{a,b}_{n}
for all
n<p
.

5
by other means, which began the author’s path in this research direction.

\Sigma

\Sigma

be the word whose characters are the digits of

. That is, if we let

so that

, then
(n)_{p}=((n)_{p}\left[\text{length}(n_{p})-1\right])\cdots((n)_{p}[1])((n)_{p}[0])
. Note that when working with strings, exponents denote repetition. For example,

. Also note that every statement made in this paper about

should also hold for

for every

.

In this paper, we prove results dealing with the density of certain values within sequences:

Definition 1
.

The
asymptotic density
, or just

, of a subset,

, is

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S\cap\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid n<N\}}{N}.

When it exists, this is equal to

which is the form we primarily use. Lastly, if

is a prime, we say that the density of a value

.

2
Symmetry

In this section, we prove a novel result relating
T^{a,b}_{k}
and
T^{a,b}_{p-1-k}
that is useful in proving the primary results of this paper in the next section. We also derive the corresponding result for
M^{a,b}_{k}
and
M^{a,b}_{p-3-k}
.

We follow the philosophy that the mod
p
study of the generalized Motzkin numbers,
M^{a,b}_{n}
, is reducible to the study of their corresponding generalized central trinomial coefficients,
T^{a,b}_{n}
, which are well-structured and determined by their first
p
elements (see Proposition
7
). Thus, we begin by stating an explicit rule for this reduction, which is itself a specific instance of a family of such rules for reducing to the study of
T^{a,b}_{n}
for any sequence of the form
\constantterm\left[(ax^{-1}+b+ax)^{n}Q\right]
where
Q
is some Laurent polynomial in
x
(see Section 4 of
[
4
]
for a discussion of this family of equations).

Proposition 1
.

2a^{2}M^{a,b}_{n}=(4a^{2}-b^{2})T^{a,b}_{n}+2bT^{a,b}_{n+1}-T^{a,b}_{n+2},

and in particular,

2M_{n}=3T_{n}+2T_{n+1}-T_{n+2}.

Proof.

Let
P=ax^{-1}+b+ax
so that
T^{a,b}_{n}=\constantterm\left[P^{n}\right]
. Define
A_{n}:=\constantterm\left[x\cdot P^{n}\right]=\constantterm\left[x^{-1}\cdot P^{n}\right]
and
B_{n}:=\constantterm\left[x^{2}\cdot P^{n}\right]=\constantterm\left[x^{-2}\cdot P^{n}\right]
so that
M^{a,b}_{n}=\constantterm\left[(1-x^{2})\cdot P^{n}\right]=T^{a,b}_{n}-B_{n}
.

From
T^{a,b}_{n+1}=\constantterm\left[(ax^{-1}+b+ax)^{n+1}\right]=\constantterm\left[(ax^{-1}+b+ax)\cdot(ax^{-1}+b+ax)^{n}\right]=2aA_{n}+bT^{a,b}_{n}
we have that
2aA_{n}=T^{a,b}_{n+1}-bT^{a,b}_{n}
. Similarly from
A_{n+1}=aT^{a,b}_{n}+bA_{n}+aB_{n}
, we find that

\displaystyle T^{a,b}_{n+2}

\displaystyle=2aA_{n+1}+bT^{a,b}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=2a(aT^{a,b}_{n}+bA_{n}+aB_{n})+b(2aA_{n}+bT^{a,b}_{n})

\displaystyle=(b^{2}+2a^{2})T^{a,b}_{n}+2b(2aA_{n})+2a^{2}B_{n}

\displaystyle=(b^{2}+2a^{2})T^{a,b}_{n}+2b(T^{a,b}_{n+1}-bT^{a,b}_{n})+2a^{2}B_{n}

and thus
2a^{2}B_{n}=T^{a,b}_{n+2}-2bT^{a,b}_{n+1}+(b^{2}-2a^{2})T^{a,b}_{n}
.

In conclusion,
2a^{2}M^{a,b}_{n}=2a^{2}T^{a,b}_{n}-2a^{2}B_{n}=(4a^{2}-b^{2})T^{a,b}_{n}+2bT^{a,b}_{n+1}-T^{a,b}_{n+2}
.
∎

Next, we give a mostly combinatorial proof of a general two-term recurrence for the sequences
T^{a,b}_{n}
, which is analogous to the well-known two-term recurrence for the Motzkin numbers (see Corollary
3
).

Proposition 2
.

nT^{a,b}_{n}=b(2n-1)T^{a,b}_{n-1}-(b^{2}-4a^{2})(n-1)T^{a,b}_{n-2},

and in particular,

nT_{n}=(2n-1)T_{n-1}+(3n-3)T_{n-2}.

Proof.

Recall that
M^{a,b}_{n}
counts the number of lattice paths from
(0,0)
to
(n,0)
using
a
distinct UP and DOWN steps,
(1,1)
and
(1,-1)
respectively, and
b
distinct LEVEL steps,
(1,0)
, which do not go below the x-axis. Additionally,
T^{a,b}_{n}
simply counts the number of such paths without the x-axis restriction, and
A_{n}=\constantterm\left[x\cdot P^{n}\right]
(as in Proposition
1
) does the same but counting paths to
(n,1)
.

Let
\gamma_{n}
count the number of paths from the origin to
(n,1)
such that the only intersection with the x-axis is the starting point. Then
\gamma_{n}=aM^{a,b}_{n-1}
since adding an UP step to the beginning of a path (there are
a
ways to do this) counted by the
M^{a,b}_{n-1}
gives a unique path counted by
\gamma_{n}
. Furthermore,
n\gamma_{n}=A_{n}
since there are
n\gamma_{n}
paths from the origin to
(n,1)
that never return to the x-axis and which have a special vertex labeled; this labeled vertex partitions the path into a front and a back (consisting of UP, DOWN, and LEVEL steps)
P=FB
; Now the path
Q=BF
is an arbitrary path to
(n,1)
, counted by
A_{n}
, whose rightmost-lowest point is the labeled vertex (making the process reversible, and hence a bijection). Putting these together, we get
A_{n}=n\gamma_{n}=anM^{a,b}_{n-1}
.

We now combine that
T^{a,b}_{n}=2aA_{n-1}+bT^{a,b}_{n-1}
(as discussed in the proof of Proposition
1
),
A_{n-1}=a(n-1)M^{a,b}_{n-2}
, and Proposition
1
to get

\displaystyle T^{a,b}_{n}

\displaystyle=2aA_{n-1}+bT^{a,b}_{n-1}

\displaystyle=(n-1)(2a^{2}M^{a,b}_{n-2})+bT^{a,b}_{n-1}

\displaystyle=(n-1)\left((4a^{2}-b^{2})T^{a,b}_{n-2}+2bT^{a,b}_{n-1}-T^{a,b}_{n}\right)+bT^{a,b}_{n-1}

\displaystyle=-(n-1)T^{a,b}_{n}+(2bn-2b+b)T^{a,b}_{n-1}-(b^{2}-4a^{2})(n-1)T^{a,b}_{n-2}.

From which we can conclude
nT^{a,b}_{n}=b(2n-1)T^{a,b}_{n-1}-(b^{2}-4a^{2})(n-1)T^{a,b}_{n-2}
.
∎

In fact, the already-known analogous recurrence for the generalized Motzkin numbers can be derived directly from the previous two results by a tedious algebraic manipulation. An independent proof is also given by Woan in
[
8
]
.

Corollary 3
.

(n+2)M^{a,b}_{n}=b(2n+1)M^{a,b}_{n-1}-(b^{2}-4a^{2})(n-1)M^{a,b}_{n-2},

and in particular,

(n+2)M_{n}=(2n+1)M_{n-1}+(3n-3)M_{n-2}.

∎

As it turns out, Proposition
2
is sufficient to prove our symmetry directly!

Theorem 4
.

T^{a,b}_{p-1-k}\equiv(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}-k}T^{a,b}_{k}\pmod{p}

for
p>2
and
0\leq k\leq\frac{p-1}{2}
, and in particular,

T_{p-1-k}\equiv(-3)^{\frac{p-1}{2}-k}T_{k}.

Proof.

By induction on
k\leq\frac{p-1}{2}
. For
k=\frac{p-1}{2}
,
T^{a,b}_{p-1-\frac{p-1}{2}}=T^{a,b}_{\frac{p-1}{2}}=(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{0}T^{a,b}_{\frac{p-1}{2}}
. For
k=\frac{p-3}{2}
, we wish to show that
T^{a,b}_{\frac{p+1}{2}}\equiv(b^{2}-4a^{2})T^{a,b}_{\frac{p-3}{2}}
, which follows from Proposition
2
since
2^{-1}T^{a,b}_{\frac{p+1}{2}}\equiv\frac{p+1}{2}T^{a,b}_{\frac{p+1}{2}}=bpT^{a,b}_{\frac{p-1}{2}}-(b^{2}-4a^{2})\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)T^{a,b}_{\frac{p-3}{2}}\equiv 2^{-1}(b^{2}-4a^{2})T^{a,b}_{\frac{p-3}{2}}
.

Now let
0\leq k<\frac{p-3}{2}
. Then using Proposition
2
and induction,

\displaystyle(p-1-k)T^{a,b}_{p-1-k}

\displaystyle=b(2(p-1-k)-1)T^{a,b}_{p-2-k}-(b^{2}-4a^{2})(p-2-k)T^{a,b}_{p-3-k}

\displaystyle\equiv-b(2k+3)T^{a,b}_{p-2-k}+(b^{2}-4a^{2})(2+k)T^{a,b}_{p-3-k}

\displaystyle\equiv-b(2k+3)(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}T^{a,b}_{k+1}+(2+k)(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}T^{a,b}_{k+2}

\displaystyle=(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}\left((k+2)T^{a,b}_{k+2}-b(2k+3)T^{a,b}_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}\left(b(2k+3)T^{a,b}_{k+1}-(b^{2}-4a^{2})(k+1)T^{a,b}_{k}-b(2k+3)T^{a,b}_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=(-k-1)(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}-k}T^{a,b}_{k}

\displaystyle\equiv(p-1-k)(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}-k}T^{a,b}_{k}.

∎

In the case that
p\nmid b^{2}-4a^{2}
, then
b^{2}-4a^{2}
is invertible so that we can also conclude that
T^{a,b}_{k}\equiv(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{k-\frac{p-1}{2}}T^{a,b}_{p-1-k}\pmod{p}
. Of course, in the case that
p\mid b^{2}-4a^{2}
we instead conclude that
T^{a,b}_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for all
\frac{p-1}{2}<k<p
.

Before moving on, we quickly mention that an easy Corollary of Theorem
4
is that
T^{a,b}_{p-1}\equiv(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
so that, assuming
p\nmid b^{2}-4a^{2}
,
(T^{a,b}_{p-1})^{2}\equiv 1
by Fermat’s little theorem.

Remark
.

Theorem
4
seems to help explain the observed density of the sequence of primes that do not divide any central trinomial coefficient (A113305 of OEIS
[
5
]
); for primes less than
10^{6}
the density of these primes is near
0.6075
. As a corollary of Proposition
7
of the next section,
p
is in this sequence if and only if it does not divide any of the first
p
central trinomial coefficients. But the author was originally surprised by how high this density seems to be, since if we pretend that on average
T_{n}
(
n<p
) has a
\frac{1}{p}
chance of being congruent to
0
mod
p
, then we expect the density to be
\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{p}=e^{-1}\approx 0.3679
. But in view of Theorem
4
, we now know that
p
does not divide any central trinomial coefficient if and only if it does not divide the first
\frac{p+1}{2}
of them! Now if we make the same assumption we instead get the density estimate
\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\approx 0.6065
, which is much closer to the experimental value.

The analogous result to Theorem
4
for the generalized Motzkin numbers can be proven directly from their two-term recurrence in a very similar fashion to the proof of Theorem
4
. However, we instead provide a proof using Proposition
1
and Theorem
4
.

Theorem 5
.

M^{a,b}_{p-3-k}\equiv(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}M^{a,b}_{k}\pmod{p}

for
p>3
and
0\leq k\leq\frac{p-3}{2}
, and in particular,

M_{p-3-k}\equiv(-3)^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}M_{k}.

Proof.

\displaystyle 2a^{2}M^{a,b}_{p-3-k}

\displaystyle=(4a^{2}-b^{2})T^{a,b}_{p-3-k}+2bT^{a,b}_{p-2-k}-T^{a,b}_{p-1-k}

\displaystyle\equiv-(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}T^{a,b}_{k+2}+2b(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}T^{a,b}_{k+1}-(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}-k}T^{a,b}_{k}

\displaystyle=(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}\left(-T^{a,b}_{k+2}+2bT^{a,b}_{k+1}+(4a^{2}-b^{2})T^{a,b}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}(2a^{2}M^{a,b}_{k}).

This gives us the theorem when
a
is not a multiple of
p
, and otherwise

M^{a,b}_{n}=\constantterm\left[(ax^{-1}+b+ax)^{n}(1-x^{2})\right]\equiv\constantterm\left[b^{n}(1-x^{2})\right]=b^{n}\pmod{p}

in which case
(b^{2}-4a^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}-k}M^{a,b}_{k}\equiv b^{p-3-2k}b^{k}=b^{p-3-k}\equiv M^{a,b}_{p-3-k}
, as desired.
∎

As an additional application of Theorem
4
we prove the following conjecture (Batalov 2022) from the OEIS page for the Motzkin numbers (A001006 of
[
5
]
), which can also be inferred from Tables 4 and 5 of
[
1
]
: If
p
is a prime of the form
6m+1
, then
M_{p-2}
is divisible by
p
. In fact, we can prove a stronger result:

Proposition 6
.

A prime
p
divides
M_{p-2}
if and only if
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

Proof.

For
p=2
,
2\nmid M_{0}
and
2\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, so assume
p>2
. Note that
T_{p}\equiv 1
(see Proposition
7
). We prove the proposition by reducing to central trinomial coefficients:

\displaystyle 2M_{p-2}

\displaystyle=3T_{p-2}+2T_{p-1}-T_{p}

\displaystyle\equiv 3(-3)^{\frac{p-3}{2}}+2(-3)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}-1

\displaystyle=-(-3)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+2(-3)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}-1

\displaystyle=(-3)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}-1

and therefore, by quadratic reciprocity,
p\mid M_{p-2}\Leftrightarrow(-3)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\equiv 1\Leftrightarrow p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.
∎

3
Density

In this section, we answer the question: “What is the density of the Motzkin numbers that are divisible by
p
?” In subsection
3.1
, we do the same for some additional sequences. In all cases, answering this question is accomplished by further elaborating the consequences Proposition
1
(or analogous results) and Theorem
4
alongside the following simple description of
T^{a,b}_{n}\bmod p
.

Proposition 7
.

For every prime
p
, if
a_{n}=\constantterm\left[P^{n}\right]
where
P
is a Laurent polynomial with
\deg P=1
, then
a_{n}\equiv\prod a_{(n)_{p}[i]}\pmod{p}
. In particular,
T^{a,b}_{n}\equiv\prod T^{a,b}_{(n)_{p}[i]}\pmod{p}
.

Proof.

We induct on the number of digits in
(n)_{p}
. Certainly if
n=(n)_{p}[0]<p
, then
a_{n}=a_{(n)_{p}[0]}
. Otherwise, if
n=qp+(n)_{p}[0]
, then

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle=\constantterm\left[P(x)^{qp+(n)_{p}[0]}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\constantterm\left[P(x^{p})^{q}P(x)^{(n)_{p}[0]}\right]\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\left(P(x)^{p}\equiv P(x^{p})\pmod{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\constantterm\left[P(x^{p})^{q}\right]\constantterm\left[P(x)^{(n)_{p}[0]}\right]

\displaystyle\left((n)_{p}[0]<p\text{ so there is no cancellation}\right)

\displaystyle=\constantterm\left[P(x)^{q}\right]\constantterm\left[P(x)^{(n)_{p}[0]}\right]

\displaystyle\left(\constantterm\left[P(x^{k})^{n}\right]=\constantterm\left[P(x)^{n}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=a_{q}a_{(n)_{p}[0]}

\displaystyle=\prod a_{(n)_{p}[i]}

\displaystyle(\text{by induction, since }(q)_{p}\text{ has fewer digits than }(n)_{p}).

∎

Note that this proposition shows that the sequence
T^{a,b}_{n}\bmod p
can be described by its first
p
terms, making Theorem
4
more important than it may initially seem.

Given the base-
p
digit-multiplicative nature of
T^{a,b}_{n}
, every combination of offsets
T^{a,b}_{n+i}
can be understood by “factoring out” common digits between the
(n+i)_{p}
for various
i
. For example, if
(n)_{p}=qn_{0}
and
n_{0}\neq p-1
, then
\alpha T^{a,b}_{n}+\beta T^{a,b}_{n+1}\equiv\alpha T^{a,b}_{q}T^{a,b}_{n_{0}}+\beta T^{a,b}_{q}T^{a,b}_{n_{0}+1}=T^{a,b}_{q}\left(\alpha T^{a,b}_{n_{0}}+\beta T^{a,b}_{n_{0}+1}\right)

A version of this idea, sufficient for our purposes, is encapsulated in the following formalism where we bound the maximum distance between indices by

Lemma 8
.

If
a_{n}=\constantterm\left[P^{n}\right]
where
\deg P=1

b_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}
,

In particular if

, then

b_{n}\equiv a_{q}\left(a_{m}a_{p-1}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{n_{0}+i}\right)\equiv a_{\frac{n-n_{0}}{p}}b_{n_{0}}\pmod{p}.

Proof.

If

(n+i)_{p}=qm(p-1)^{k}(n_{0}+i)
while if

(n_{0}+i)_{p}=q(m+1)0^{k}(n_{0}+i-p)
. Thus, noting that

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{C_{n_{0}}}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}+\sum_{i=p-n_{0}}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{C_{n_{0}}}\alpha_{i}(a_{q}a_{m}a_{p-1}^{k}a_{n_{0}+i})+\sum_{i=p-n_{0}}^{h}\alpha_{i}(a_{q}a_{m+1}a_{n_{0}+i-p})

\displaystyle=a_{q}\left(a_{m}a_{p-1}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{C_{n_{0}}}\alpha_{i}a_{n_{0}+i}+a_{m+1}\sum_{i=p-n_{0}}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{n_{0}+i-p}\right)

∎

We now apply this lemma’s factorization to

Proposition 9

If

We now set both sides of the above equality congruent to

Corollary 10
.

If

,
\ell=p-2-m

Note that

In particular, when

T_{\ell}\equiv T_{p-1}^{k+1}T_{\ell+1}&(n)_{p}=qm(p-1)^{k}(p-1).\end{array}\right.

∎

.

Note that we require

We now perform the bookkeeping of computing the density of 0. We begin by stating a result in the generality of Lemma

Lemma 11
.

If
a_{n}=\constantterm\left[P^{n}\right]
where
P
is symmetric and
\deg P=1
,
b_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}
,
C_{n}=\min(h,p-n-1)
, and

, then if
a_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for some
n
(

may be taken less than

), then asymptotic density of
0
in
b_{n}\bmod p
is
1
, and otherwise it is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\left\{0\leq n<p-h\mid b_{n}\equiv 0\right\}\right|}{p}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left|\left\{p-h\leq n<p,0\leq m<p-1\mid a_{m}\sum_{i=0}^{C_{n}}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}\equiv-a_{m+1}\sum_{i=C_{n}+1}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{i-(p-n)}\right\}\right|}{(p-1)(p+1)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left|\left\{p-h\leq n<p,0\leq m<p-1\mid a_{m}\sum_{i=0}^{C_{n}}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}\equiv-a_{p-1}a_{m+1}\sum_{i=C_{n}+1}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{i-(p-n)}\right\}\right|}{(p-1)p(p+1)}

Proof.

The case where there exists
n
such that
a_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
is treated in Proposition 4 of
[
4
]
.

By Lemma
8
,
b_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
if and only if either
n_{0}<p-h
and
b_{n_{0}}\equiv 0
or else if
n_{0}\geq p-h
and
a_{m}a_{p-1}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{C_{n_{0}}}\alpha_{i}a_{n_{0}+i}\equiv-a_{m+1}\sum_{i=p-n_{0}}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{i-(p-n_{0})}
. Note that by an application of Theorem
4
(mentioned beneath its proof), we have that
a_{p-1}^{2}\equiv 1
and so
a_{p-1}^{k}
is congruent to
a_{p-1}
or
1
depending on the parity of
k
. In the set of strings,
(n)_{p}=qm(p-1)^{k}n_{0}
, over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the distribution on
k
is geometric (with success probability of a trial being
\frac{1}{p}
) so that it is even
\frac{p}{p+1}
of the time and odd
\frac{1}{p+1}
of the time.

Putting this all together, each element of the set
\left\{0\leq n<p-h\mid b_{n}\equiv 0\right\}
contributes
\frac{1}{p}
to the density of
0
in
b_{n}\bmod p
since
\frac{1}{p}
of the strings over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
end with such

. Likewise each element of the set
\left\{p-h\leq n<p,0\leq m<p-1\mid a_{m}\sum_{i=0}^{C_{n}}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}\equiv-a_{m+1}\sum_{i=C_{n}+1}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{i-(p-n)}\right\}
corresponds to

being even and thus contributes
\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{1}{p-1}\cdot\frac{p}{p+1}=\frac{1}{(p-1)(p+1)}
to the density of
0
, while
\left\{p-h\leq n<p,0\leq m<p-1\mid a_{m}\sum_{i=0}^{C_{n}}\alpha_{i}a_{n+i}\equiv-a_{p-1}a_{m+1}\sum_{i=C_{n}+1}^{h}\alpha_{i}a_{i-(p-n)}\right\}
corresponds to

being odd and thus contributes
\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{1}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p+1}=\frac{1}{(p-1)p(p+1)}
to the density.
∎

We now apply Lemma
11
to the generalized Motzkin numbers using the equations from Corollary
10
in place of those from Lemma

. Furthermore, reasoning about the densities in

further yields a description of the density of all other values in

in terms of the density of

,

.

Proposition 12
.

If
T^{a,b}_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for some
n<p
, then the asymptotic density of
0
in
M^{a,b}_{n}
is
1
. Otherwise, for all
p>2
, it is equal to

\displaystyle D_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\left\{n<p-2\mid M^{a,b}_{n}\equiv 0\right\}\right|}{p}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\left|\left\{m<p-1\mid bT^{a,b}_{m}\equiv T^{a,b}_{p-1}T^{a,b}_{m+1}\right\}\right|}{(p-1)(p+1)}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\left|\left\{m<p-1\mid bT^{a,b}_{m}\equiv T^{a,b}_{m+1}\right\}\right|}{(p-1)p(p+1)}.

Furthermore, if
\left\{T^{a,b}_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\right\}
generates
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
as a multiplicative group, then the asymptotic density of any fixed
k>0
in

is
\frac{1-D_{0}}{p-1}
.

Proof.

D_{0}
is given by combining the lemma with Corollary
10
(we may assume
p\nmid b^{2}-4a^{2}
, as otherwise
T^{a,b}_{p-1}\equiv 0
) noting that the factors of
2
come from the fact that
n
takes two values when
p-2\leq n<p
(namely
p-2
and
p-1
) and the corollary shows that in both cases we count the same set of elements.

To prove the last claim, we model
T^{a,b}_{n}
(as a function from
\mathbb{N}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
) by a directed multi-graph whose states are the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, and whose
p
outgoing transitions are labeled by
k\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
where a transition from
i
labeled
k
goes to state
j=i\cdot T^{a,b}_{k}\bmod p
. By Proposition
7
, we can read the value of
T^{a,b}_{n}
off of this graph by starting at the state
1
and following the transitions corresponding to the digits,
(n)_{p}
; the state we end at is the value of
T^{a,b}_{n}\bmod p
. As it happens, each state also has exactly
p
incoming transitions, since the number of transitions from

to

is equal to the number of transitions from
1
to

so that counting the number of these transitions from all
i
(to a fixed

) is equal to counting all transitions leaving
1
, of which there are
p
.
\left\{T^{a,b}_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\right\}
generating
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
as a multiplicative group is equivalent to the statement that this directed graph is connected. Treating this graph as a Markov process (where every transition has probability
\frac{1}{p}
), our assumption implies that the process is irreducible. Each state having a self-loop (labeled by
0
) makes the process aperiodic and irreducible so that every state converges over time to the stable state. All in-degrees and out-degrees of our graph being
p
implies that the stable state is the uniform state
\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{p-1}&\cdots&\frac{1}{p-1}\end{pmatrix}
. This implies that the density of each element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
in the sequence

is
\frac{1}{p-1}
.

Finally, from Proposition
9
we can then see that each value of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
must have equal density in

(because of the factor of
T^{a,b}_{q}
in each case).
∎

There is also an alternative direct argument for the final statement of the proposition that doesn’t appeal to Proposition
9
. We only sketch the argument here to avoid introducing unnecessary background. The argument goes that with density

, a random walk on the Rowland-Zeilberger automaton (see

) of

reaches either the

(which is exactly the graph described two paragraphs ago) and then cannot escape in either case. In fact, one interpretation of this paper is as describing what proportion of random walks on this automaton reach the

above corresponds to the probability that walks go to the

Conjecture 1
.

For all

, if

does not divide any member of

appear in

In order to complete the full picture, note that when

, we get that

D_{0}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{if }b\equiv 0\\
0&\text{if }a\equiv 0\text{ and }b\equiv 1\\
\frac{1}{3}&\text{if }a\equiv 1\text{ and }b\equiv 1\end{array}\right..

When

this is because the argument that

when

for some
n
(including

) does not require

. Otherwise, when

we have

for all

, and when

we have

and it is not hard to manually derive that the density of

in

is

(for example, treat Figure 1 of

as a Markov process and compute the probability of ending at each sink).

Finally, when

above, we get a simple formula for the density of Motzkin numbers divisible by

. Furthermore, applying Theorem

to this simple formula yields the lower bound of

, which has appeared in the conclusion of

Corollary 13
.

The asymptotic density of
0
in
M_{n}\bmod p
is

if

for some

, and otherwise, for

\displaystyle D_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\left\{n<p-2\mid M_{n}\equiv 0\right\}\right|}{p}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\left|\left\{m<p-1\mid T_{m}\equiv T_{p-1}T_{m+1}\right\}\right|}{(p-1)(p+1)}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\left|\left\{m<p-1\mid T_{m}\equiv T_{m+1}\right\}\right|}{(p-1)p(p+1)}.

is true for

. Lastly,

\displaystyle+\frac{\left|\left\{0\leq m<p-1\mid T_{m}\equiv T_{p-1}T_{m+1}\right\}\right|}{(p-1)(p+1)}

∎

Because

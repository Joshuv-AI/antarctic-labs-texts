1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers and
A
a subset of
\mathbb{N}
.
The Erdős-Turán conjecture on additive bases
[
7
]
, posed in 1941, states that if
R_{A}(n)\geq 1
for all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
R_{A}(n)
cannot be unbounded, where

R_{A}(n)=\#\big\{(a,a^{\prime}):n=a+a^{\prime},a,a^{\prime}\in A\big\}.

Grekos, Haddad, Helou, and Pihko
[
8
]
showed that
R_{A}(n)\geq 6
for infinitely many
n
, and Borwein, Choi and Chu
[
1
]
then improved this to
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{A}(n)\geq 8
. Sándor
[
11
]
proved that if
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{A}(n)=K<\infty
, then
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{A}(n)\leq K-2\sqrt{K}+1
.

It turns out that the Erdős-Turán conjecture on additive bases does not hold if the natural set
\mathbb{N}
was replaced by the integer set
\mathbb{Z}
. According to Nathanson
[
9
]
, a subset
A
of
\mathbb{Z}
is called a
unique representation basis
for the integers if
r_{A}(n)=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where

r_{A}(n)=\#\big\{(a,a^{\prime}):n=a+a^{\prime},a\leq a^{\prime},a,a^{\prime}\in A\big\}.

In
[
9
]
, Nathanson showed that there is a unique representation basis of
\mathbb{Z}
, and furthermore it can be constructed to be arbitrarily sparse. Nathanson also proved that there is a unique representation basis
A
for the integers such that

A(-x,x):=\big|A\cap[-x,x]\big|\geq(2/\log 5)\log x+0.63.

Nathanson then asked the following interesting problem.

Problem 1

(Nathanson)
.

Does there exists a number
\theta<1/2
so that
A(-x,x)\leq x^{\theta}
for every unique representation basis
A
and for all sufficiently large
x
?

Later, Problem
1
was answered negatively by Chen
[
3
]
who proved that for any
\varepsilon>0
there is a unique representation basis
A
for the integers such that
A(-x,x)\geq x^{1/2-\varepsilon}
for infinitely many positive integers
x
.
This leads him to the following two problems.

Problem 2

(Chen)
.

Does there exist a real number
c>0
and a unique representation basis
A
such that

A(-x,x)\geq c\sqrt{x}
for infinitely many positive integers
x
?

Problem 3

(Chen)
.

Does there exist a real number
c>0
and a unique representation basis
A
such that

A(-x,x)\geq c\sqrt{x}
for all real numbers
x\geq 1
?

In a recent article, the first author
[
5
]
showed that the answer to Problem
2
is affirmative while the answer to Problem
3
is negative. For Problem
2
, the first author proved that there is a unique representation basis
A
such that

\displaystyle A(-x,x)\geq\big(\sqrt{2}/2-\varepsilon\big)\sqrt{x}

(1.1)

for infinitely many positive integers
x
, where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small.

As in
[
5
]
, let
\mathscr{A}
be set of all unique representation bases
A
such that there exists a real number
c>0
so that
A(-x,x)>cx^{1/2}
for infinitely many positive integers
x
. For any
A\in\mathscr{A}
, let

\displaystyle c_{A}:=\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(-x,x)}{\sqrt{x}}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{\mathscr{A}}:=\sup_{A\in\mathscr{A}}c_{A}.

(1.2)

By (
1.1
) the set
\mathscr{A}
is nonempty, and furthermore

c_{\mathscr{A}}\geq\sqrt{2}/2.

For any unique representation basis
A
, it is clear that the elements of

\big(A\cap[-x,x]\big)+(x+1):=\{a+x+1:a\in A,-x\leq a\leq x\}

form a Sidon set in
[1,2x+1]
. Hence, we have
A(-x,x)\leq\big(\sqrt{2}+o(1)\big)\sqrt{x}
(see Lemma
1
below). Therefore, so far we got

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}/2\leq c_{\mathscr{A}}\leq\sqrt{2}.

Naturally, one would like to ask ‘
what is the exact value of
c_{\mathscr{A}}
’?

In this note, the lower bound
\sqrt{2}/2
of
c_{\mathscr{A}}
is improved.

Theorem 1
.

Let
c_{\mathscr{A}}
be defined in
(\ref{eq-0204-2})
. Then we have
c_{\mathscr{A}}\geq 1
.

We
conjecture

c_{\mathscr{A}}=\sqrt{2}
due to some considerations from Sidon sets.

2.
Proofs

The proof of Theorem
1
makes use of the results from Sidon sets.
An integer set

S=\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<s_{t}\}

is called a
Sidon set
if all the sums

s_{i}+s_{j}~(i\leq j)

are different. Let
f_{2}(N)
be the largest cardinality of a Sidon set in
\big\{1,2,\cdots,N\big\}
.
We need a well-known result on the asymptotic formula of
f_{2}(N)
(see e.g.,
[
12
, Theorem 7, page 88]
or
[
10
]
).

Proposition 1
.

As
N\rightarrow\infty
, we have
f_{2}(N)\sim\sqrt{N}
.

Different to the earlier proof by the first author
[
5
]
, we do not rely only on the asymptotic formula of
f_{2}(N)
, but also adopt a classical result of Bose
[
2
]
, see also
[
12
, Theorem 3, page 81]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
p
be a prime. Then, there are at least
p
elements
\{g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{p}\}
in
\big\{1,2,\cdots,p^{2}-1\big\}
such that all the sums

g_{i}+g_{j}\quad(1\leq i\leq j\leq p)

are different modulo
p^{2}-1
.

Historically, theorems of the type of Proposition
2
were initially proved by Singer
[
13
]
which were later used to obtain the asymptotic formula of
f_{2}(N)
( see e.g.,
[
4
,
6
]
).
The following lemma, which is a consequence of Proposition
1
and Proposition
2
, plays the key role in the improvement of
c_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be an arbitrarily small given number. Then, for any sufficiently large prime
p
there is a Sidon set

\mathscr{S}\subset\bigg(\left[-p^{2},\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2}\right]\bigcup\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2},p^{2}\right]\bigg)

such that
\big|\mathscr{S}\big|\geq\left(1-\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\right)p
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a sufficiently large prime. By Proposition
2
there are
p
integers

s_{1}<\cdots<s_{\ell}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2}<s_{\ell+1}<\cdots<s_{y}<\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2}\leq s_{y+1}<\cdots<s_{p}

in the interval
\big[1,p^{2}\big]
such that
all sums

s_{i}+s_{j}\ (i\leq j)

are different modulo
p^{2}-1
. Let

\mathscr{S}=\Big\{s_{1}-(p^{2}-1),s_{2}-(p^{2}-1),\cdots,s_{\ell-1}-(p^{2}-1),s_{y+1},s_{y+2},\cdots,s_{p}\Big\}.

Then, clearly we have

\mathscr{S}\subset\bigg(\left[-p^{2},\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2}\right]\bigcup\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2},p^{2}\right]\bigg)

Note that
\big\{s_{\ell+1},s_{\ell+2},\cdots,s_{y}\big\}
is a Sidon set in the interval

\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2},\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2}\right],

which is of length
\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}p^{2}
. Thus, by Proposition
1
we have

\displaystyle y-\ell\leq\big(1+o(1)\big)\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}p^{2}}\leq\frac{5\varepsilon p}{8}.

(2.1)

Hence, by (
2.1
) we have

\big|\mathscr{S}\big|=p-(y-\ell)-1\geq p-\frac{5\varepsilon p}{8}-1\geq\left(1-\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\right)p.

It remains to show that
\mathscr{S}
is a Sidon set. Let

\displaystyle\widetilde{s_{i}}=\begin{cases}s_{i}-(p^{2}-1),&\text{if~}1\leq i\leq\ell-1,\\
s_{i},&\text{if~}y+1\leq i\leq t.\end{cases}

Since

s_{i}+s_{j}\ (i\leq j)

are different modulo
p^{2}-1
, we know that

\widetilde{s_{i}}+\widetilde{s_{j}}\ (i\leq j)

are also different modulo
p^{2}-1
, which implies that
\mathscr{S}=\big\{\widetilde{s_{i}}\big\}
is a Sidon set.
∎

Now, we turn to the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrarily small. It suffices to construct a unique representation basis
A
such that for infinitely many positive integers
x
we have

\displaystyle A(-x,x)\geq\big(1-\varepsilon\big)\sqrt{x}.

(2.2)

For any integer set
\mathcal{S}
and integer
s
, define

2\mathcal{S}:=\big\{s_{1}+s_{2}:s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathcal{S}\big\},\quad\mathcal{S}+s:=\{s_{1}+s:s_{1}\in\mathcal{S}\}.

For convenience, we follow some notations adopted by
[
5
]
.
The construction will be done via inductive process.
We will construct a series of subsets

A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset\cdots\subset A_{i}\subset\cdots

and a series of positive integers

x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{i}<\cdots

such that for any positive integer
h
we have the following four properties:

I.
r_{A_{h}}(n)\leq 1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

II.
r_{A_{2h}}(n)=1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|n|\leq h
,

III.
A_{2h-1}(-x_{h},x_{h})\geq\big(1-\varepsilon\big)\sqrt{x_{h}},

IV.
0\not\in A_{h}
.

First, we set
A_{1}=\{-1,1\}
and
x_{1}=1
. It can be easily checked that inductive hypotheses I, III, IV satisfy with
A_{1}
. Assume that the sets
A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset\cdots\subset A_{2h-1}
and integers
x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{h}
satisfying I to IV had already been constructed. We are going to construct
A_{2h},\ A_{2h+1}
, and
x_{h+1}
. Suppose that
m
is the integer with minimum absolute value such that
r_{A_{2h-1}}(m)=0
. Then by
A_{2h-2}\subset A_{2h-1}
and inductive hypothesis II, we have
|m|\geq h
.
Let
a^{*}_{2h-1}
be the element of
A_{2h-1}
with maximum absolute value. Set

b=4|a^{*}_{2h-1}|+|m|

and

B=A_{2h-1}\cup\big\{-b,b+m\big\}.

Then
0\not\in B
and
r_{B}(m)\geq 1
since
m=-b+(b+m)
. It can be checked that

2A_{2h-1},\quad A_{2h-1}+(-b),\quad A_{2h-1}+(b+m),\quad\big\{-2b,m,2b+2m\big\}

are four disjoint sets, which implies that
r_{B}(n)\leq 1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, we have
r_{B}(m)=1
. Furthermore, if
r_{B}(-m)=0
we let
\widetilde{b}=4b+5|m|
and

\widetilde{B}=B\cup\big\{-\widetilde{b},\widetilde{b}-m\big\}.

Then, we have
0\not\in\widetilde{B},\ r_{\widetilde{B}}(-m)=1
, and
r_{\widetilde{B}}(n)\leq 1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
via similar arguments.
Let
A_{2h}=B
if
r_{B}(-m)=1
, and let
A_{2h}=\widetilde{B}
otherwise.
Clearly, by the above constructions we know that
A_{2h}
satisfies inductive hypotheses I, II and IV. We are left over to construct a set
A_{2h+1}
and an integer
x_{h+1}>x_{h}
satisfying
A_{2h}\subset A_{2h+1}
and inductive hypotheses I, III, IV.

Let
a^{*}_{2h}
be the element of
A_{2h}
with maximum absolute value. Let
p
be a large integer (in terms of
|a^{*}_{2h}|
,
\varepsilon
, and
x_{h}
) to be decided later. Then by Lemma
1
there is a Sidon set
\mathscr{S}
such that all of whose elements belong to the interval

\left[-p^{2},\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2}\right]\bigcup\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2},p^{2}\right]

Additionally, we have

\displaystyle\big|\mathscr{S}\big|\geq\left(1-\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\right)p.

(2.3)

To the aim of our purpose, we will delete a few elements from
\mathscr{S}
. Let

A_{2h}-A_{2h}:=\big\{a-a^{\prime}:a,a^{\prime}\in A_{2h}\big\}.

Then, there are at most
2|A_{2h}|^{2}\leq 2\cdot\big(2a^{*}_{2h}\big)^{2}=8\big(a^{*}_{2h}\big)^{2}
elements in the set

(A_{2h}-A_{2h})\cup 2A_{2h}.

For any nonnegative element
a_{1}\in A_{2h}-A_{2h}
, there is at most one pair
(s,s^{\prime})\in\mathscr{S}^{2}
with
s_{1}\neq s_{1}^{\prime}
such that
s_{1}-s_{1}^{\prime}=a_{1}
since
\mathscr{S}
is a Sidon set. Also, for any
a_{2}\in 2A_{2h}
, there is at most one pair
(s_{2},s_{2}^{\prime})\in\mathscr{S}^{2}
with
s_{2}\neq s_{2}^{\prime}
such that
s_{2}+s_{2}^{\prime}=a_{2}
since
\mathscr{S}
is a Sidon set.
We remove all these pairs
(s_{1},s_{1}^{\prime}),(s_{2},s_{2}^{\prime})
to form a smaller Sidon set
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
. Clearly, by (
2.3
) and the discussions above we have

\big|\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}\big|\geq\left(1-\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\right)p-16\big(a^{*}_{2h}\big)^{2}\geq\left(1-\varepsilon\right)p,

provided that
p\geq\frac{64(a^{*}_{2h})^{2}}{\varepsilon}
. We choose
p
to be a sufficiently large prime greater than

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{64(a^{*}_{2h})^{2}}{\varepsilon},x_{h}\right\}

(2.4)

and
x_{h+1}=p^{2}
. Then, clearly
x_{h}<x_{h+1}
. Furthermore, we take

A_{2h+1}=A_{2h}\cup\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}.

Then, we have
A_{2h}\subset A_{2h+1}
. It can be seen that
0\not\in A_{2h+1}
by the construction, and

A_{2h+1}(-x_{h+1},x_{h+1})\geq\big|\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}\big|\geq\left(1-\varepsilon\right)p=\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\sqrt{x_{h+1}},

satisfying inductive hypotheses III and IV. It remains to show that
A_{2h+1}
satisfies inductive hypothesis I. Let
y_{1},y_{2},z_{1}
, and
z_{2}
be four elements of
A_{2h+1}
with

\displaystyle y_{1}+y_{2}=z_{1}+z_{2}.

(2.5)

It suffices to prove that
\{y_{1},y_{2}\}=\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
. We separate the arguments into a few cases.

Case 1.
All of
y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}
are all elements of
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
. In this case, the desired result is trivial since
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
is a Sidon set.

Case 2.
All of
y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}
are all elements of
A_{2h}
. In this case, the desired result follows from inductive hypothesis I of
A_{2h}
.

Case 3.
Exactly one element of
y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}
belongs to
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
. In this case, we assume, without loss of generality, that
y_{1}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
. Then by (
2.5
) we have

y_{1}=z_{1}+z_{2}-y_{2}.

Note that

|z_{1}+z_{2}-y_{2}|\leq 3|a^{*}_{2h}|
while

|y_{1}|\geq\frac{1}{2}p^{2}>8|a^{*}_{2h}|^{4}>3|a^{*}_{2h}|

from (
2.4
), which is certainly a contradiction.

Case 4.
Exactly two elements of
y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}
belong to
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
. In this case, the desired result is true due to the construction of
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
from
\mathscr{S}
.

Case 5.
Exactly three elements of
y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}
belong to
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
y_{1}\in A_{2h}
and
y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
. Since
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}\subset\mathscr{S}
, we have

\displaystyle y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\in\left[-p^{2},\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2}\right]\bigcup\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\right)p^{2},p^{2}\right].

(2.6)

It then follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) that

|z_{1}+z_{2}-y_{2}|\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}p^{2}\geq 4|a^{*}_{2h}|^{4}>|a^{*}_{2h}|\geq|y_{1}|,

which is a contradiction.

Inductive hypothesis I of
A_{2h+1}
is now proved by
Case 1
to
Case 5
.
Finally, we let

A=\bigcup_{h=1}^{\infty}A_{h}.

Then,
r_{A}(n)=1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by inductive hypotheses I and II, implying that
A
is a unique representation basis for the integers. Moreover, inductive hypothesis III implies

A(-x_{h},x_{h})\geq A_{2h-1}(-x_{h},x_{h})\geq\big(1-\varepsilon\big)\sqrt{x_{h}}

for any
x_{h}
, proving our theorem.
∎

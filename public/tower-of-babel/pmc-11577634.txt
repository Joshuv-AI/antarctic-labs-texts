Assessing differences among persistent, episodic, and non- high-need high-cost hospitalized children in China after categorization by an unsupervised learning algorithm
Authors: Peng Zhang, Bifan Zhu, Linan Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHigh-need, high-cost (HNHC) patients are a major focus of international healthcare reform. However, research on HNHC children in China remains limited. This study aims to classify HNHC pediatric patients, analyze the differences among groups, and explore the factors influencing HNHC status.MethodsData were obtained from a retrospective observational cohort of hospitalized children in Shanghai, China from 2017 to 2023. K-means clustering, one of the unsupervised learning algorithms, was employed to classify patients according to their HNHC status. Descriptive statistical analysis and the Kruskal-Wallis H test were used to describe and test the differences among different groups, with the logit regression models to analyze the predictors.Results688,131 hospitalized children were classified into three groups: 1,871 persistent HNHC, 32,539 episodic HNHC, and 653,721 non-HNHC. Significant differences were observed among these groups. Persistent HNHC patients have significantly higher costs and longer HNHC durations compared to episodic and non-HNHC patients, who were more likely to be aged 30 days to 1 year or 13–18 years, female with only one type of health insurance, and leukemia was the most prevalent and costly disease. They exhibited distinct healthcare utilization patterns, including emergency admissions, higher surgery rates, longer hospital stays, more frequent hospitalizations, and a preference for tertiary and specialized hospitals in city centers. Multiple influencing factors of persistent HNHC versus episodic HNHC and non-HNHC were identified.ConclusionThis study provides valuable insights into the classification, characteristics, and influencing factors of persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC hospitalized children in China. Persistent HNHC patients warrant targeted interventions to improve health outcomes and reduce healthcare costs. Enhanced medical coverage for key diseases, high-quality healthcare services tailored to their needs, and early interventions are crucial.Keywords: Persistent high-need, high-cost; K-means clustering; Healthcare utilization; Hospitalized children; China

Background

High-need, High-cost (HNHC) patients are a key focus of international healthcare reform. This group of patients was first discovered by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality in the United States in 2006 after identifying a significant concentration of healthcare expenditures from 1977 to 2002 and revealing that the top 5% of HNHC patients accounted for over 50% (51.2% in 2021) of the total healthcare expenditures [1–3]. Similar results that the top 5% of HNHC patients accounted for approximately 50% of total healthcare spending were found in other OECD countries, with Canada (60%), Spain (56%), and Germany (50%) having the highest proportions in 2019 [4], and 54% in the United Kingdom in 2022 [5]. Subsequently, various countries began focusing on HNHC patients to control health expenditure and improve health equity [6–8].

Among HNHC patients, persistent HNHC patients warrant the most attention. To align more closely with the HNHC core concept, which posits that high needs lead to high costs, the elevated costs for persistent HNHC patients are not incidental. Instead, they stem from significant diseases or chronic conditions that entail long-term and inelastic healthcare needs, rather than from the incidental nature of healthcare needs and services [9–11]. Also, the persistency of HNHC is not merely measured by continuous years of HNHC but rather reflects a long-term, challenging-to-improve status, which imposes a heavy burden on patients and their families and results in poorer health outcomes [12, 13]. Additionally, persistent HNHC patients have significant potential for intervention, and improving healthcare efficiency for this group is crucial for enhancing health outcomes and controlling costs [14].

Currently, international research on persistent HNHC patients focuses exclusively on adult populations. These studies typically classify persistent HNHC patients using the following criteria: (1) the most common classification is to categorize individuals into three groups: patients experiencing HNHC for three consecutive years are defined as persistent HNHC, those experiencing HNHC for 1–2 years are classified as episodic HNHC, and those who have never experienced HNHC are considered non-HNHC patients [9, 10, 12, 14–16]; (2) some studies categorize patients into four groups: 6–8 years (persistent HNHC), 3–5 years (moderate HNHC), 1–2 years (occasional HNHC), and non-HNHC [11]; (3) other studies classify patients into two groups: those experiencing HNHC for two consecutive years or continuously throughout the study period are considered persistent HNHC, while all others are categorized as non-HNHC patients [17]. These studies provided a foundation for analyzing the classification quantity and criteria of HNHC patients within the pediatric population. Furthermore, studies indicated that persistent HNHC patients differ from episodic or non-HNHC patients so identifying persistent HNHC patients and describing these differences is crucial for shedding light on this group and providing basic information to implement effective interventions [10, 12].

However, little is known about HNHC patients among Chinese children not even mentioning the analysis addressing persistent HNHC children. In the United States, studies describe the prevalence and costs of common diseases among HNHC children [18], while research in the United Kingdom focuses on describing HNHC children [5]. To our knowledge, there is no domestic research focusing on HNHC children in China, nor international studies identifying and analyzing persistent or episodic HNHC children, yet early intervention for pediatric HNHC patients can significantly improve their health and quality of life, reduce avoidable healthcare utilization, and control costs [5, 18].

In recent years, the advancement of unsupervised machine learning has provided a new approach to segment populations based on features [19]. As one of the commonly used unsupervised learning algorithms, K-means clustering also learns the structure and patterns of data without labeled data or predefined target variables [20]. Its principle involves partitioning the dataset into K clusters, such that data points within each cluster are as similar as possible, while data points from different clusters are as dissimilar as possible [21]. This method is straightforward to apply in clustering analysis within the healthcare field to segment patients, identify disease patterns, predict health risks, and assist the decision process of healthcare providers [22–26]. We apply this technique to classify HNHC pediatric patients in our study. Placona’s study employed k-means clustering to classify adult patients in retrospective observational studies into three categories: persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC, which was similar to the aforementioned most common classification [27]. Utilizing unsupervised learning methods among pediatric patients in China to analyze persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients can provide empirical evidence to address the research gap, complementing and extending existing studies conducted on adult populations.

Therefore, this study aims to focus on pediatric patients in China, utilizing a retrospective observational cohort combined with K-means clustering to classify patients according to their HNHC status, analyze the differences among persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients in terms of characteristics, prevalent diseases, patterns of healthcare service utilization, and hospitalized expenditures, and explore the factors influencing HNHC status.

Methods

Study design

A retrospective cohort study was conducted utilizing data from two sources from January 1, 2017, to December 31, 2023. These databases encompass all hospitalization data in Shanghai over seven years. The first source is the administrative data from the Urban and Rural Resident Basic Medical Insurance (URBMI). URBMI, a crucial component of China’s social medical insurance system, provides basic medical coverage for children, the elderly, and other non-employee groups. The second source is the Children’s Hospitalization Mutual Fund (CHMF) administrative database. Established in 1996 by the Shanghai Red Cross Society, the Municipal Education Commission, and the Municipal Health Commission, CHMF is a public welfare, non-profit medical security mutual aid fund designed to share the risk of hospitalization and major outpatient medical expenses for children. Over the past two decades, URBMI and CHMF have collectively formed a multi-level medical security system, covering nearly all children in Shanghai and facilitating joint reimbursement for their hospitalization expenses.

Study population

The study population comprises all children aged 18 and under who were hospitalized in Shanghai between January 1, 2017, and December 31, 2023. Exclusion criteria included patients outside the specified age range, records with incomplete cost data during the study period, and patients with incomplete ICD-10 codes. Additionally, cost outliers were identified and addressed through trimming procedures to ensure data integrity.

Classification procedure

The classification of persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients is primarily determined through three basic features, the annual expenditure, total expenditure, and HNHC years, and then clustered by an unsupervised machine learning method, K-means clustering.

Firstly, the annual expenditure is calculated by summing up expenses for surgery, bed, medication, supplies, etc. as an individual’s single hospitalization cost, and then summing up multiple hospitalization costs within that year as the annual hospitalization cost. These values are inflated to 2023 values based on the Consumer Price Index for Healthcare and further converted to USD using the 2023 average RMB to USD exchange rate (1 USD: 7.0467 CNY). Next, the total expenditure is the sum of the standardized annual costs for each patient over the seven years. Then, based on the standardized annual expenditure, the top 5% of patients with the highest expenditures each year are identified and marked as HNHC for that year. The number of years a patient is classified as HNHC over the seven years is aggregated to determine the HNHC years. Finally, K-means clustering is applied, which is particularly effective when dealing with large-scale data with a predefined number of clusters, K [28]. It is well-suited for this study, which uses seven years of large-sample data and predefines 2 clusters (persistent and episodic HNHC patients) for classification along with the 95% of non-HNHC patients based on previous literature [9–12, 16, 27, 29, 30]. The specific steps include randomly selecting 2 initial centroids, assigning each data point to the cluster of the nearest centroid, recalculating the mean of all data points within each cluster to update the centroids, and repeating the assignment and centroid update steps until predetermined 10,000 iterations are reached [31].

Measures

To compare the differences among persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients and identify the factors influencing persistent high costs, the study included variables related to patient characteristics, prevalent disease, and their service utilization. Patient characteristics encompass age categorized into five groups, gender, household registration status, and health insurance type. The types of diseases afflicting HNHC patients were classified using the International Classification of Diseases Tenth Revision (ICD-10) and grouping ICD at the three-digit level. Characteristics related to healthcare service utilization included referral type before hospitalization, whether surgery was performed during hospitalization, median length of hospital stay, annual number of hospitalizations, hospital level, hospital type, and hospital region.

Statistical analysis

The study employs descriptive statistical analysis to describe the frequency and proportion for categorical variables, and median and interquartile range (IQR) for continuous variables. In the K-means clustering, three patient categories are predefined based on literature, with 10,000 iterations, using the feature variables, annual expenditure, total expenditure, and HNHC years. After clustering, given the non-normality and heteroscedasticity of the variables, the Kruskal-Wallis H test is used to compare the differences among persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC groups for characteristic variables [32]. Two logit regression models are employed to analyze the predictors of persistent HNHC versus non-HNHC and persistent HNHC versus episodic HNHC, as well as a generalized linear model (GLM) to identify the driving factors contributing to HNHC status as a robust check. The above analysis was conducted using STATA 17.0 (StataCorp, 2021).

The ethics of this study were approved by the Shanghai Health Development Research Center (approval number 2024001).

Results

Classification based on K-means clustering

This study included 688,131 hospitalized children in Shanghai between January 1, 2017, and December 31, 2023. Based on previous literature, K-means Clustering was performed using annual expenditure and HNHC years as the main features, capturing both the absolute cost and the persistence of HNHC status, and three clusters were predefined. Compared to traditional subjective criteria, the K-means clustering results provide greater differentiation in the average annual expenditure among the three groups, better reflecting the differences in HNHC status among different clusters. Clustering using total expenditure and HNHC years as the main features was conducted for robustness analysis, which was consistent with the primary classification.

The results of the K-means clustering are shown in Table 1. Persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients numbered 1,871, 32,539, and 653,721, respectively. Persistent HNHC patients had an average HNHC duration of 1.84 years and an average annual expenditure of $58,787.32, significantly higher than the other two groups. The mean annual out-of-pocket (OOP) expenditure for persistent HNHC patients is $17207.28, which is significantly higher than that of the other two groups, while the proportion of mean annual OOP expenditure to mean annual total expenditure is 29.27% (lower than 30.09% in episodic HNHC, 33.70% in non-HNHC). Additionally, the persistent HNHC group had a seven-year cumulative average total expenditure of $91,921.51, also significantly higher than the other two groups ($16,935.24 in episodic HNHC, $2,193.59 in non-HNHC).

Characteristics of persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients

The individual characteristics of persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients are presented in Table 2. Compared to non-HNHC patients, persistent HNHC patients are more concentrated in the age groups of 30 days to 1 year and 13 to 18 years, with a higher proportion of females at 43.51% in the HNHC category than in the other two categories, episodic (42.58) or non-HNHC (41.48). Regarding health insurance type, the proportion of persistent HNHC patients enrolled in both URBMI and CIMMF is significantly lower (74.13%) compared to episodic and non-HNHC patients, which are 80.99% and 78.59%, respectively. There is no significant difference in the proportion of Shanghai residents among the three groups.

For disease incidence rates, leukemia is the most common condition among persistent HNHC patients, comprising 10.91% unspecified leukemia and 1.98% myeloid leukemia, significantly higher than the other two groups. Premature birth-related conditions and disorders related to short gestation and low birth weight are also prevalent among persistent HNHC patients, with incidence rates of 5.19% and 2.03%, respectively. Pneumonia has an incidence rate of 5.14% among persistent HNHC patients, the only condition within the top ten diseases that is lower than the incidence rate of 22.04% observed in the non-HNHC group. Additionally, persistent HNHC patients exhibit higher incidence rates of epilepsy (4.01%), other aplastic anemias (2.94%), hemophilia A (2.89%), scoliosis (2.41%), and malignant neoplasm of the brain (2.14%) compared to the other two groups.

The expenditure of prevalent diseases of persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients

In terms of spending, the median annual expenditures associated with all the above-mentioned diseases are significantly higher in the persistent HNHC group compared to the other two groups. Among the top ten most prevalent diseases, myeloid leukemia shows the largest spending disparity among three groups, with median annual expenditure in the persistent HNHC group exceeding those of episodic and non-HNHC patients by $50,611.02 and $61,073.83 respectively, followed by unspecified leukemia (HNHC exceeding episodic and non-HNHC patients by $41,622.31 and $56,393.95 respectively), and pneumonia (HNHC exceeding episodic and non-HNHC patients by $46,693.61 and $53,706.43 respectively). Among persistent and episodic HNHC groups, the disease with the smallest expenditure difference is scoliosis (a difference of $18,055.06), followed by epilepsy (a difference of $21,628.33), and malignant neoplasm of the brain (a difference of $28,576.41). Among persistent and non-HNHC groups is epilepsy (a difference of $39,402.36).

Healthcare utilization of persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients

There are significant differences in healthcare utilization among the persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC groups, as shown in Table 4. Persistent HNHC patients are more likely to be admitted through emergency departments (34.38%), whereas episodic HNHC patients are more likely to be transferred from other hospitals (20.84%). Both groups have surgery rates exceeding 90%, significantly higher than that in the non-HNHC group (65.93%). The median length of hospital stays and annual hospitalizations for persistent HNHC patients are significantly higher than those of the other two groups, at 39 days (11.5 days for the episodic group and 4.5 days for the non-HNHC group) and 4 times per year (compared to 1 time for both episodic and non-HNHC groups), respectively. Additionally, compared to the non-HNHC group, both the persistent and episodic HNHC groups more frequently utilize tertiary hospitals (95.67% for persistent group, 95.76% for episodic group, and 73.40% for non-HNHC group) and specialized hospitals (50.67% for persistent group, 43.35% for episodic group, and 31.52% for non-HNHC group). Furthermore, in comparison to the other two groups, persistent HNHC patients are more likely to seek medical care in the city center (71.25% for the persistent group, 47.50% for the episodic group, and 54.20% for the non-HNHC group) rather than the suburb.

Discussion

This study utilized a seven-year retrospective observational cohort of hospitalized children in Shanghai and employed K-means clustering to categorize them into persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients. The results indicated significant differences in characteristics, incidences, and expenditures of common diseases, and healthcare service utilization among persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC pediatric patients, and multiple factors significantly influencing the persistence of HNHC status.

Consistent with previous studies among adults, this study found that persistent HNHC patients incur significantly higher costs compared to episodic and non-HNHC patients and maintain the status of HNHC for more years [9, 10, 13, 30]. Also, the magnitude of the difference in healthcare spending between persistently and transiently HNHC children was substantial [9, 10]. Our study suggested the differences in demographic and socioeconomic status among the three groups, including age group, sex, and health insurance type. Children aged 30 days to 1 year may be strongly associated with diseases resulting from preterm birth and low birth weight, while those aged 13 to 18 are more likely to be diagnosed with other diseases and experience disease progression. This may contribute to the higher risk of HNHC observed in these age groups [18]. China has effectively achieved universal health coverage (UHC), with Social Health Insurance (SHI) coverage consistently exceeding 95% [33]. Similarly, as one of the most economically developed cities, Shanghai’s URBMI and CHMF programs cover nearly all children, and the results indicate that CHMF provides substantial supplementary protection beyond basic SHI with 29.27% OOP proportion among persistent HNHC patients, which was lower than 30.09% in episodic HNHC and 33.70% in non-HNHC. It indicated that persistent HNHC patients in China were more financially protected by the multi-tiered healthcare system than other patients. However, in the United States, the OOP of the top 5% HNHC patients for all age groups was 7.7% in 2021 [34], and there is still room with the goal of “Health Shanghai 2030” targeting at controlling OOP proportion under 20% [35]. Compared to children with dual insurance coverage, we found those enrolled in only one insurance program exhibit poorer protection against healthcare costs and individual financial risk [36]. It provides a feasible approach to deal with the disproportionately high incidences of catastrophic health expenditure in China compared with countries with similar economic development [37].

Our study focuses on the most common diseases contributing to persistent HNHC status and their associated costs. Overall, there are significant differences in the prevalent diseases between children with persistent HNHC and adult populations. Leukemia is the most common and costly disease among persistently HNHC children in our study. Previous research has also identified the substantial financial burden of leukemia on children in China and health disparity among children with different health insurance [38, 39]. Expanding medical insurance coverage and enhancing reimbursement could mitigate persistent HNHC with promoted disease management of Leukemia [38, 40]. Also, preterm birth and disorders related to short gestation and low birth weight are common conditions among children with persistent HNHC, associated with potentially life-threatening complications such as elevated rates of high blood pressure, cardiac dysfunction, obstructive lung disease, elevated blood glucose, and leading to adverse effects on the well-being of preterm-born children [41]. Prior research has also indicated that the health of children during the perinatal period is closely linked to their health in adulthood, underscoring the importance of enhancing maternal and infant care and promoting preventive measures to reduce preterm birth [42]. As for blood disorders, aplastic anemias and hemophilia contribute to the occurrence of persistent HNHC. China has been gradually improving compensation policies and collaborative networks for these vulnerable patients [43], and in Shanghai, medical expenses incurred by pediatric patients with hemophilia and aplastic anemia are covered under the major illness medical insurance for residents, providing multiple layers of protection beyond URBMI. Along with that, malignant brain tumors, commonly found in children, are also covered under the major illness medical insurance in Shanghai. Finally, our study period encompassed the COVID-19 pandemic, and among all common diseases in persistent HNHC cases, pneumonia had lower incidence rates compared to the other two groups. However, its costs were significantly higher, indicating that a small number of critically ill pneumonia children incurred substantial and sustained hospitalization expenses [44].

Due to variations in individual characteristics and prevalent diseases, both persistent and episodic HNHC patients exhibit distinct patterns of healthcare utilization from non-HNHC patients, which is consistent with the results among adults [7, 12]. Compared to non-HNHC patients, both persistent and episodic HNHC groups are more likely to undergo surgery, have longer hospital stays and more frequent admissions, and opt for tertiary specialized hospitals. However, there are subtle differences in healthcare utilization between the persistent and episodic HNHC groups. Those with persistent HNHC are more likely to be hospitalized via emergency services and seek treatment at city-center hospitals. In contrast, episodic HNHC patients are more inclined to be transferred and treated at suburban hospitals. This finding suggests that persistent HNHC patients not only endure long-term illnesses but also are prone to acute life-threatening health events, while episodic HNHC cases may signify disease progression necessitating transfer to other higher-level hospitals during disease management [10].

Finally, the regression results indicate that the differences in patient characteristics, healthcare utilization, and other aspects significantly influence the factors leading to persistent, occasional, or non-HNHC status, aligning with conclusions from existing research [15, 17, 29]. It highlighted the multifactorial influences on HNHC status, underscoring the importance of multifaceted measures for preventing HNHC by enhancing medical coverage levels and improving healthcare service delivery capabilities.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, due to data constraints, the outpatient and inpatient records of children were not linked, restricting our analysis of HNHC and predominantly assuming hospitalization expenditure could reflect the main part of the total medical expenses. Secondly, the K-means clustering method requires a predefined K value and is sensitive to initial centroid selection [26]. We predefined three population groups based on the literature and attempted to minimize noise by excluding cost outliers. Future research will explore alternative unsupervised learning methods for validation and comparison. Finally, after using different feature indicators to cluster, there was a lack of cross-comparison metrics between different clustering results. Therefore, theoretically, greater differences between clusters reflect better differentiation among different groups [21]. We used results with greater differences in expenditure among groups as criteria to choose clustering results and then modified feature indicators for robustness testing.

Conclusion

This study employed a 7-year retrospective observational cohort and introduced the unsupervised machine learning K-means clustering method and newly distinguished persistent, episodic, and non-HNHC patients among Chinese children. Significant differences were observed among these groups in patient characteristics, prevalent diseases, healthcare utilization patterns, and costs. Various factors influencing HNHC status were also identified. The study findings provide policymakers with insights to focus on children with HNHC, enhance medical coverage for key diseases, implement high-quality healthcare services, and initiate early interventions to prevent avoidable service needs.

The L p L^{p} -boundedness of wave operators for higher order Schrödinger operator with zero singularities in low odd dimensions

Abstract
. This paper investigates the L p L^{p} -bounds of wave operators for higher-order Schrödinger operators H = ( − Δ ) m + V H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , with m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 and real-valued decaying potentials V V . 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backgrounds

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer,
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V(x)
be the higher order Schrödinger operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
V(x)
be a real-valued bounded potential satisfying
|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}
for some specific
\beta>0
and certain regularity on
V
(depending on
n,m
), where
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
.

In this paper we study the
L^{p}
-bounds of the wave operators
W_{\pm}(H;(-\Delta)^{m})
associated with
H
, which are defined by the following strong limits in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

W_{\pm}=W_{\pm}(H;(-\Delta)^{m})=:s\text{-}\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}e^{itH}e^{-it(-\Delta)^{m}}.

(
1.1)

Here,
e^{itH}
and
e^{it(-\Delta)^{m}}
are Schrödinger groups generated by
H
and
(-\Delta)^{m}
, respectively.

As
\beta>1,
it was known that
W_{\pm}(H;(-\Delta)^{m})
exist on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as partial isometric operators and are asymptotically complete ( see e.g. Kuroda
[
33
]
). In particular, the wave operators
W_{\pm}
satisfy the identities

W_{\pm}W_{\pm}^{*}=P_{ac}(H)
,
W_{\pm}^{*}W_{\pm}=I

and the following intertwining formula (see e.g. Reed and Simon
[
38
]
):

f(H)P_{ac}(H)=W_{\pm}f((-\Delta)^{m})W^{*}_{\pm},

(
1.2)

where
f
is any Borel measurable function of
\mathbb{R}
and
P_{ac}(H)
denotes the projection onto the absolutely continuous spectral subspace of
H.

By the identity
(​​

1.2
)
and the
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for the free operator
f((-\Delta)^{m}),
the boundedness of
W_{\pm}(H;(-\Delta)^{m})
can immediately be used to establish the
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for the perturbed operator
f(H)P_{ac}(H)
by

\|f(H)P_{ac}(H)\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\leq\|W_{\pm}\|_{L^{q}\rightarrow L^{q}}\|f((-\Delta)^{m})\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\|W_{\pm}^{*}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}.

(
1.3)

In fact, under a suitable condition on
f,
it is comparably easy to establish the
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for the free operator
f((-\Delta)^{m})
by Fourier multiplier methods. Thus, in order to obtain the estimate
(​​

1.3
)
, it suffices to prove the following possible boundedness of
W_{\pm}(H;(-\Delta)^{m})
and
W_{\pm}^{*}(H;(-\Delta)^{m}):

\|W_{\pm}f\|_{L^{q}\rightarrow L^{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q}},\ \ \ \ \|W_{\pm}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

(
1.4)

Due to such feature, the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators for the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
(i.e.
m=1
) has been extensively developed as a fundamental tool for studying various nonlinear dispersive equations, such as the non-linear Schrödinger and Klein-Gordon equations with potentials. As a result, it is natural and important to extend this study to more general higher-order elliptic operators
(-\Delta)^{m}+V(x)
, which has garnered increasing attention in the mathematics and mathematical physics communities.

Despite the recent emergence of many related works (see e.g.
[
10
,
22
,
19
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
), they remain relatively scarce compared to the case of the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V(x)
. In particular, to the best of the author’s knowledge, there are no results on the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators for higher-order Schrödinger operators
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
when
1\leq n\leq 2m
and
m\geq 3
, unlike the cases for
m=1,2
. More background and references can be found in Subsection
1.3
.

Inspired by these findings, we turn our attention to the remaining cases. Notably, Erdoğan and Green
[
10
,
11
]
established the
L^{p}
-boundedness of wave operators
W_{\pm}(H;(-\Delta)^{m})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
when
n>2m
in the zero regular case of
H
, and more recently, Erdoğan-Green-LaMarster
[
12
]
further considered the corresponding
L^{p}
-boundedness of
W_{\pm}(H;(-\Delta)^{m})
when zero is an eigenvalue of
H
for
n>4m
. Hence, the primary goal of this paper is to address the remaining cases. Specifically, we show that
W_{\pm}(H;(-\Delta)^{m})
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with a sharp range of
p
depending on all possible types of zero-energy resonances of
H
when
n
is odd and
1\leq n\leq 4m-1
. The cases for even dimensions,
2\leq n\leq 4m
, will be addressed in another work
[
5
]
.

The existing research works suggest that the
L^{p}
-behavior of wave operators can be broadly categorized into three scenarios:
n<2m
,
n=2m
, and
n>2m
. When
n<2m
, as first observed by Goldberg and Green
[
22
]
for
(n,m)=(3,2)
, the resolvent exhibits a singularity appearing in the stationary representation of the low-energy part of the wave operators. It is expected that wave operators are generically unbounded on
L^{p}
for
p=1,\infty
in this scenario (cf. Mizutani-Wan-Yao
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
for
n=1,3
and
m=2
).

On the other hand, when
n>2m
, the singularity at zero energy is relatively mild, but the high-energy part becomes more complicated than in the case
n<2m
since the resolvent does not decay in the high-energy limit (cf. Erdoğan-Green
[
10
,
11
]
). The case
n=2m
is critical, as it combines the difficulties of both the low-energy and the high-energy parts (see Galtbayar-Yajima
[
19
]
for
(n,m)=(4,2)
).

Finally, it is worth emphasizing that the
L^{p}
range of wave operators is closely related to the zero resonance classification and the asymptotic expansions of the resolvent
R(z)
of
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
near the zero-energy threshold. Compared to the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
(i.e.
m=1
) (cf. e.g.
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
), the classification of resonances and the asymptotic expansions of
(-\Delta)^{m}+V
are more challenging because the polynomial symbol
P(\xi)=|\xi|^{2m}
of
(-\Delta)^{m}
is degenerate at
\xi=0
for
m\geq 2
. Moreover, the degeneracy at zero energy increases as
m
increases and
n
remains smaller.

1.2.
The main results

In order to state our main results, we first need to introduce the definition of zero energy resonances for
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
.
Recall that zero is an eigenvalue of
H
if there exists some nonzero
\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

((-\Delta)^{m}+V)\phi=0

(
1.5)

in distributional sense. However, for some potentials, it is well known that there may exist some non-trivial solutions satisfying
(​​

1.5
)
in the weighted spaces

L_{s}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\{f\in L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{n});\,\langle x\rangle^{s}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\}

for some
s<0
. This is usually referred to as a zero resonance of
H
, which certainly leads to challenges in establishing dispersive estimates or
L^{p}
-boundedness of wave operators associated with
H
.

Next, we introduce the concept of zero resonances for
H
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
n
be an odd integer, and set

m_{n}:=\min\big\{m,\ 2m-\frac{n-1}{2}\big\}=\begin{cases}m,&\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ 1\leq n\leq 2m-1,\\[8.5359pt]
\mbox{$2m-\frac{n-1}{2}$},&\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ 2m+1\leq n\leq 4m-1.\end{cases}

(
1.6)

Clearly, since
n
is odd and
1\leq n\leq 4m-1
,
m_{n}
is a positive integer that is
the total number of zero resonance types
of
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(see
[
4
]
and Subsection
2.2
below for details).

In particular, we observe that for all
1\leq n\leq 2m-1
, the total number
m_{n}
of zero resonance types is always a fixed value
m
. Moreover, when
2m+1\leq n\leq 4m-1
,
m_{n}
is given by
m_{n}=2m-\frac{n-1}{2}
, and it decreases as
n
increases. Finally, until
n>4m
, the operator
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
contains only a possible zero eigenvalue and has no zero resonance on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(cf. Feng et al.
[
15
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
1\leq n\leq 4m-1
be odd and
m_{n}
be the integer defined in
(​​

1.6
)
. Given that
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
, where
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a real-valued potential. Then

(i) Zero is said to be the
\mathbf{k}
-
th kind resonance of
H
if
1\leq\mathbf{k}\leq m_{n}
and the equation
(​​

1.5
)
(i.e.
H\phi=0
) has a non-trivial solution
\phi
in
\bigcap_{s<-\frac{1}{2}-m_{n}+\mathbf{k}}L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, but doesn’t have non-trivial solution
\phi
in
\bigcap_{s<\frac{1}{2}-m_{n}+\mathbf{k}}L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii) Zero is said to be the eigenvalue of
H
, if
(​​

1.5
)
has a non-trivial solution
\phi
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

(iii) Zero is said to be the regular point of
H
, if zero is neither a resonance nor an eigenvalue.

For convenience below, we also regard
the regular zero energy as the
0
-
th resonance of
H
and the zero eigenvalue as the
(m_{n}+1)
-
th kind resonance of
H
.

Remark 1.2
.

For all
n>2m
and
m\geq 2
, the zero resonance classification of
(-\Delta)^{m}+V
was first found by Feng et al.
[
15
]
. Subsequently, the problem was further explored in
[
13
,
27
,
34
,
42
]
for the cases where
n\leq 4
and
m=2
. These studies delved deeper into specific dimensions and orders of the operator. In particular, for all odd dimensions
n
and
m\geq 2
, the zero resonance definition was introduced by
[
4
]
. The definition above is equivalent to all previous ones (possibly in the different space).

It should be noted that zero resonance classification is closely linked to the asymptotic expansion of the resolvent
R(z)
of
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
near the zero threshold. Compared with Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
(i.e.
m=1
) (cf.
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
), as remarked before, the zero resonance classification and the asymptotic expansions of
(-\Delta)^{m}+V
are complicated, since
(-\Delta)^{m}
is degenerate at zero energy when
m\geq 2
. We refer readers to Theorem
2.5
for more details.

Throughout the paper, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 1.3
.

Let
m\geq 2
,
1\leq n\leq 4m-1
be odd,
0\leq{\bf k}\leq m_{n}+1
and
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
where
V
is real valued. Additionally, we assume that the following three conditions hold:

(i) Zero is the
\mathbf{k}
-
th kind resonance of
H
and
H
has no embedded positive eigenvalues.

(ii)
\lvert V(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}
for some
\beta
satisfying

\beta>\begin{cases}4m-n+4\mathbf{k}+4,&\quad\text{if\ \ $1\leq n\leq 2m-1$},\\
n+4\mathbf{k}+5,&\quad\text{if\ \ $2m+1\leq n\leq 4m-1$}.\end{cases}

(
1.7)

(iii) There exists a
\delta>0
such that
\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{1+\delta}\langle\nabla\rangle^{\delta}V(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}<C
when
n=4m-1
.

Remark 1.4
.

We remark that the assumption about the absence of embedded positive eigenvalues is indispensable. The classical Kato theorem indicates that if
V(x)=o(|x|^{-1})
as
|x|\to\infty
,
-\Delta+V
has no positive eigenvalues. Compared to the second-order case, there exists a
V
in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
has a positive eigenvalue when
m
is even (cf. Feng et al.
[
15
, Section 7.1]
).

Assumption (iii) is a smoothness condition specifically used to estimate the high-energy part, as in Erdoğan-Green
[
10
]
when
n=4m-1
. Moreover, we note that the smoothness assumption of
V
for

is necessary when
n\geq 4m
by Erdoğan-Goldberg-Green
[
8
]
.

Let
\mathbb{B}(X,Y)
be the space of bounded operators from the Banach space
X
to the Banach space
Y
, and
\mathbb{B}(X)=\mathbb{B}(X,X)
. Additionally, denote

k_{c}:=\max\{m-\frac{n-1}{2},0\}=\begin{cases}m-\frac{n-1}{2},\ \ &\ \text{if}\ \ 1\leq n\leq 2m-1,\\
0,\ \ &\ \text{if}\ \ 2m+1\leq n\leq 4m-1.\end{cases}

(
1.8)

Note that when

is odd,

is a positive integer, and

In addition, as shown in the following theorem,

represents the type of critical resonance of

. When

, the

-boundedness range of the wave operators

(excluding the endpoints

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

be odd,

, and let

(i)
If zero is the regular point of

1.3.
Further related backgrounds.

In this subsection, we review known results on the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators for both the Schrödinger operator and higher-order elliptic operators.

For the classical Schrödinger operator
H=-\Delta+V(x)
, due to its numerous applications in linear and nonlinear dispersive equations, there has been significant work on the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators
W_{\pm}(H;-\Delta)
. If zero energy is regular, Yajima
[
47
,
48
]
was the first to prove that, under certain smoothness and decay conditions on the potential
V
, the wave operators
W_{\pm}(H;-\Delta)
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
in dimensions
n\geq 3
. Using the Wiener algebra method, Beceanu
[
2
]
derived sharp conditions on the potential for
n=3
(see also Beceanu-Schlag
[
3
]
). For
n=1,2
with regular zero energy, it has been shown that the wave operators are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1<p<\infty
(see
[
45
,
18
,
6
,
49
,
31
]
). Notably, for
n=1
, the range of
p
remains unchanged even when zero energy is not regular (cf.
[
45
]
).

In addition, when zero energy is a resonance or eigenvalue of
H=-\Delta+V
, the range of exponent
p
for which the wave operators
W_{\pm}(H;-\Delta)
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
may shrink. For
n>4
, it was shown in
[
17
,
50
]
that the wave operators are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
n/(n-2)<p<n/2
. Yajima
[
51
]
and Goldberg-Green
[
20
]
independently extended this range to
1<p<n
(with
p=1
also covered in
[
20
]
). For
n=4
, Goldberg-Green
[
21
]
showed that the wave operators are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
for
1\leq p<4
if zero is an eigenvalue but no resonance is present, while Yajima
[
54
]
showed that the range is
1<p<4
if there is a zero resonance.

For
n=3
, Yajima
[
52
]
demonstrated that the wave operators are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
1\leq p<3
if zero energy is an eigenvalue, and for
1<p<3
if zero energy is a resonance. For
n=2
, Erdoğan-Goldberg-Green
[
7
]
established that wave operators are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
1<p<\infty
if there is an
s
-wave resonance or eigenvalue at zero. Yajima
[
53
]
showed that if there is a
p
-wave resonance at zero, the wave operators are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
1<p\leq 2
but unbounded for
2<p<\infty
.

It is natural to extend the study to higher-order Schrödinger operators
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
with
m\geq 2
. In fact, based on recent developments in the resolvent
(H-z)^{-1}
and decay estimates for
e^{-itH}
(see, e.g.
[
13
,
15
]
), Goldberg-Green
[
22
]
first proved that for
n=3
and
m=2
, the wave operators
W_{\pm}(H;\Delta^{2})
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
1<p<\infty
under the assumption that the zero energy is regular. This result was later extended to higher-order Schrödinger operators
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n>2m
in the regular case by Erdoğan-Green
[
10
,
11
]
.

Recently, significant progress has been made in understanding the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators for the fourth-order Schrödinger operator
\Delta^{2}+V(x)
. For
n=1
, Mizutani-Wan-Yao
[
35
]
proved that the wave operators
W_{\pm}(H;\Delta^{2})
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for all
1<p<\infty
, while being unbounded at
p=1
and
p=\infty
, regardless of whether the zero energy is a regular point or resonance.

For
n=3
, Mizutani-Wan-Yao
[
37
]
showed that the wave operators
W_{\pm}(H;\Delta^{2})
are unbounded on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

and established weak
(1,1)
estimates in the regular case. In
[
36
]
, they further proved that the range of

is
1<p<\infty
if zero is a first-kind resonance, but
1<p<3
if zero is a second- or third-kind resonance. For
n=4
, Galtbayar-Yajima
[

demonstrated that the wave operators

are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
for

if zero energy is regular or corresponds to only
s
-wave resonance. The range of

is
1<p<4
for cases involving

-wave resonances and zero energy eigenfunctions, and

if a

In this paper, as well as in a forthcoming sequel

, we will show that

are bounded on

.

1.4.
The outline of the proof

In what follows, we briefly explain the ideas behind the proofs of the main theorem. The usual starting point is the following stationary representation of wave operators (see e.g. Kuroda
[
33
]
):

W_{\pm}(H,(-\Delta)^{m})=I-\frac{m}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{2m-1}R^{\mp}(\lambda^{2m})V\big(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})\big)d\lambda,

(
1.12)

where
R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}),\ R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})
are the limiting resolvent operators defined by

R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}):=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}R(\lambda^{2m}\pm i\varepsilon)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\big(H-(\lambda^{2m}\pm i\varepsilon)\big)^{-1},

R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m}):=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}R_{0}(\lambda^{2m}\pm i\varepsilon)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\big((-\Delta)^{m}-(\lambda^{2m}\pm i\varepsilon)\big)^{-1}.

We shall only prove the
L^{p}
-boundedness for
W_{-}
here, since
\overline{W_{+}f}=W_{-}\overline{f}
.
Because the identity operator is obviously bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, it suffices to consider the second term involving the integral in
(​​

1.12
)
. For convenience, we denote this integral term by
W
. Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\chi\equiv 1
in
(-\lambda_{0}/2,\,\lambda_{0}/2)
and
\text{supp}\chi\subset[-3\lambda_{0}/4,\,3\lambda_{0}/4]
, and the fixed
\lambda_{0}
is given in Theorem
2.5
. We then use the functions
\chi
and
1-\chi
to decompose the
W
into the low and high energy parts as follows:

W^{L}=\frac{m}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2m-1}\chi(\lambda)R^{+}(\lambda^{2m})V\big(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})\big)d\lambda,

(
1.13)

W^{H}=\frac{m}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2m-1}(1-\chi(\lambda))R^{+}(\lambda^{2m})V\big(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})\big)d\lambda.

(
1.14)

1.4.1.
The low energy part
W^{L}

We first sketch the proof for the low energy part
W^{L}
.
We define
v(x)=\sqrt{|V(x)|}
and
U(x)
as the sign function of
V(x)
, denoted by
\text{sgn}V(x)
. Here,
\text{sgn}V(x)
represents the sign function of
V(x)
, which equals
1
when
V(x)\geq 0
, and
-1
when
V(x)<0
. And then we define

M^{\pm}(\lambda)=U+vR_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})v.

(
1.15)

Due to the absence of embedded positive eigenvalue of
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
, it is well-known that
M^{\pm}(\lambda)
is invertible on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
\lambda>0
(see e.g. Agmon
[
1
]
and Kuroda
[
33
]
), and one has the symmetric second resolvent identity

R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})V=R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})v(M^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}v.

(
1.16)

Using the above identity,
W^{L}
can be expressed as

W^{L}=\frac{m}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2m-1}\chi(\lambda)R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})v(M^{+}(\lambda))^{-1}v\big(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})\big)d\lambda.

(
1.17)

The main technical challenge is to understand, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
, the refined integral kernel structure of the following composed operator:

R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})v(M^{+}(\lambda))^{-1}v\big(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})\big).

One of the key tools in our proof is the asymptotic expansions of
(M^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}
recently derived in
[
4
]
, which are presented in the following form as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
(also see Theorem
2.5
):

\big(M^{\pm}(\lambda)\big)^{-1}=\sum_{i,j\in J_{\mathbf{k}}}\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}(M_{i,j}^{\pm}+\Gamma_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda))Q_{j},

where
M^{\pm}_{i,j}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{2})
are
\lambda
-independent operators and
\Gamma_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)
are
\lambda
-dependent operators, and the index set
J_{\mathbf{k}}
is dependent on zero energy resonance type
\mathbf{k}
.

Using this asymptotic expansion, the integral kernel
W^{L}(x,y)
is a sum of finite such terms
W_{i,j}^{L}(x,y)
, which can be expressed as

\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{4m-n-1-i-j}\chi(\lambda)\Big\langle\Big(M_{i,j}^{+}+\Gamma_{i,j}^{+}(\lambda)\Big)Q_{j}v\Big(R_{0}^{+}-R_{0}^{-}\Big)(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot,y),\,Q_{i}vR_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot,x)\Big\rangle_{L^{2}}d\lambda,

(
1.18)

where
i,j\in J_{\mathbf{k}}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
denotes the inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The novel and key step is that we can write
Q_{j}vR_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot,y)
and
Q_{j}v(R_{0}^{+}-R_{0}^{-})(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot,y)
as

Q_{j}vR_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot,\,y)=e^{-i\lambda|y|}\omega_{j}^{-}(\lambda,\cdot,y)=e^{-i\lambda|y|}\big(\omega_{j,1}^{-}(\lambda,\cdot,y)+\omega_{j,2}^{-}(\lambda,\cdot,y)+\omega_{j,3}^{-}(\lambda,\cdot,y)\big),

and

Q_{j}v(R_{0}^{+}-R_{0}^{-})(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot,\,y)=e^{i\lambda|y|}\tilde{\omega}_{j}^{+}(\lambda,\cdot,y)+e^{-i\lambda|y|}\tilde{\omega}_{j}^{-}(\lambda,\cdot,y),

where
\omega_{j}^{\pm}
,
\omega_{j,1}^{\pm}
,
\omega_{j,2}^{\pm}
,
\omega_{j,3}^{\pm}
and
\tilde{\omega}_{j}^{\pm}
satisfy some estimates dependent on the property of
Q_{j}
, as detailed in Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.10
.

By putting these expressions into integral
(​​

1.18
)
, the kernels
W_{i,j}^{L}(x,y)
of
W_{i,j}^{L}
can be written as

W_{i,j}^{L}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(e^{i\lambda(|x|+|y|)}T_{i,j}^{+}(\lambda,x,y)+e^{i\lambda(|x|-|y|)}T_{i,j}^{-}(\lambda,x,y)\Big)\chi(\lambda)d\lambda,

where

T_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda,x,y)=\lambda^{4m-n-1-i-j}\Big\langle\big(M_{i,j}^{+}+\Gamma_{i,j}^{+}(\lambda)\big)\widetilde{\omega}_{j}^{\pm}(\lambda,\cdot,y),\,\omega_{i}^{-}(\lambda,\cdot,x)\Big\rangle_{L^{2}}.

We shall demonstrate the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
W_{i,j}^{L}
by analyzing its integral kernel for three cases:

|x|>2|y|,\,\,|x|<\frac{1}{2}|y|\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\frac{1}{2}|y|\leq|x|\leq 2|y|.

The corresponding operators for these cases are
W_{i,j,1}^{L}
,
W_{i,j,2}^{L}
and
W_{i,j,3}^{L}
, respectively.

(I)
For the first two cases, we write
W_{i,j,1}^{L}(x,y)
and
W_{i,j,2}^{L}(x,y)
as

\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|>2|y|\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi(\lambda)\Big(e^{i\lambda(|x|+|y|)}T_{i,j}^{+}(\lambda,x,y)+e^{i\lambda(|x|-|y|)}T_{i,j}^{-}(\lambda,x,y)\Big)d\lambda,

\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<\frac{1}{2}|y|\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi(\lambda)\Big(e^{i\lambda(|x|+|y|)}T_{i,j}^{+}(\lambda,x,y)+e^{i\lambda(|x|-|y|)}T_{i,j}^{-}(\lambda,x,y)\Big)d\lambda.

Here
T_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda;x,y)
satisfy some pointwise estimates (see Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.8
). Hence, using a result from the oscillatory integral estimate, we obtain that
|W_{i,j,1}^{L}(x,y)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-n}
in the case
|x|>2|y|
. This implies that
W_{i,j,1}^{L}
is in
\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for
1<p\leq\infty
(see Proposition
3.2
). While in the case
|x|<\frac{1}{2}|y|
, it follows that
W_{i,j,2}^{L}(x,y)
satisfies
|W_{i,j,2}^{L}(x,y)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\rho_{i}}\langle y\rangle^{-n+\rho_{i}}
for some
\rho_{i}
depending on
i\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}
. Consequently, we establish that
W_{i,j,2}^{L}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for
1\leq p<\sigma_{i}
(
\sigma_{i}
depends on
i\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}
) in Proposition
3.5
. The restrictions on the range of
p
in Theorem
1.5
are based on the results established in Propositions
3.5
and
3.7
.

(II)
For the last term
W_{i,j,3}^{L}
in the low energy part
, we divide
W_{i,j,3}^{L}
into three parts
I_{i,j}^{\pm,b}
,
I_{i,j,1}^{\pm,g}
and
I_{i,j,2}^{\pm,g}
, where the first is the bad part of
W_{i,j,3}^{L}
, and the last two are good parts.
We can obtain the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
the good parts

I_{i,j,1}^{\pm,g}
and
I_{i,j,2}^{\pm,g}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
by a similar analysis to that for
W_{i,j,1}^{L}
or
W_{i,j,2}^{L}
(see Proposition
3.10
).

To estimate
the bad part

I_{i,j}^{\pm,b}
, we apply the dyadic decomposition to rule out an admissible error term
e(x,y)
as follows:

I_{i,j}^{\pm,b}(x,y)=\sum_{|s-h|<2}\chi_{s}(x)I_{i,j}^{\pm,b}(x,y)\chi_{h}(y)+e(x,y),

where
\chi_{0}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}
and
\chi_{h}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{\{2^{h-1}<|x|\leq 2^{h}\}}
for
h\in{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
( see
(​​

3.31
)
). Therefore, to show that
I_{i,j}^{\pm,b}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it suffices to prove that

\left\|\chi_{s}I_{i,j}^{\pm,b}\chi_{h}\right\|_{\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leqslant C_{p}

holds uniformly for
s,h\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
|s-h|<2
.

It can be further shown that the kernel
\chi_{l}(x)I_{i,j}^{\pm,b}(x,y)\chi_{h}(y)
can be split into an admissible part and a singular part. The singular part is a sum of

C\chi_{s}(x)\chi_{h}(y)\frac{y^{\alpha}}{|y|^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\vartheta(j)}}\frac{x^{\tilde{\alpha}}}{|x|^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\vartheta(i)}}\frac{1}{|x|\pm|y|},

with
|\alpha|=|\tilde{\alpha}|=\vartheta(j)
. Finally, we can reduce the singular part operator to the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
the truncated Hilbert Transform
on the line, and show that
I_{i,j}^{\pm,b}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
(see Proposition
3.12
for details).
At this time, we have finished the proof of the low energy estimate of
W^{L}
.

1.4.2.
The high energy part
W^{H}

We now sketch the proof of the high energy part.
Erdoğan-Green
[
10
,
11
]
proved the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
W^{H}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
when
n>2m
under a milder condition of
V
, which requires that
|V|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}
with
\beta>n+5
, as well as
\|\langle x\rangle^{\frac{4m-1+n}{2}+\delta}V(x)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1
for
2m<n<4m-1
and
\|\langle x\rangle^{1+\delta}\langle\nabla\rangle^{\delta}V(x)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1
for
n=4m-1
with a sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Hence, we shall focus on the case
1\leq n\leq 2m-1
here. Indeed, applying the second resolvent identity,

R^{+}(\lambda^{2m})=R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})VR^{+}(\lambda^{2m}),

W^{H}
can be divided as follows:

W^{H}=W^{H}_{1}+W^{H}_{2},

where

\begin{split}W_{1}^{H}=&\frac{m}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2m-1}(1-\chi(\lambda))R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})V(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m}))d\lambda,\\
W_{2}^{H}=&\frac{m}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2m-1}(1-\chi(\lambda))R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})VR^{+}(\lambda^{2m})V(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m}))d\lambda.\end{split}

Using the expression
(​​

2.17
)
of
R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})
, the integral kernel of
W_{1}^{H}
can be written as a difference of the following terms:

(​​

for all

.

For the remainder term

\{x^{\alpha}v;\,\,\,|\alpha|\leq j\}^{\perp}\bigcap S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2},&\mbox{if}\,\ 0\leq j<{\textbf{k}},\\
\{0\},&\mbox{if}\,\ j\leq{\textbf{k}}.\end{cases}

(

Remark 2.3
.

•

In the case of a fixed resonance type
\mathbf{k}
of

, we emphasize that these projections

are well-defined by the decay condition on

in Assumption

, and one has

.

•

From the definition of

above,

In particular, zero is a regular point of

( i.e.

) is equivalent that

.

•

Furthermore, for each

, one always has the cancellation relations:

S_{j}(x^{\alpha}v)=0,\quad\text{for}~\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}},~|\alpha|\leq\max\{0,\lfloor j\rfloor\},

(

where

denotes the greatest integer at most

In addition, we define another group of orthogonal projections

for each

Q_{j}:=\begin{cases}I-S_{0},&\quad\text{if $1\leq n\leq 2m-1$ and $j=0$},\\

S_{j^{\prime}}-S_{j},&\quad\text{else},\end{cases}

(

where

Then , it follows that
Q_{j}\leq S_{\lfloor j+\frac{1}{2}\rfloor-1}
when
j>0
and
Q_{j}
satisfies the following cancellation relations:

Q_{j}(x^{\alpha}v)=0,\quad\text{for ~$\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}},~~|\alpha|\leq\max\{0,\lfloor j+1/2\rfloor\}-1$}.

(
2.32)

For convenience, let

\vartheta(j)=\max\{0,\lfloor j+1/2\rfloor\}.

(
2.33)

By the definition of these orthogonal projections

, which can be seen in

]

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
1\leq n\leq 4m-1
be odd,

, and assume that

satisfies the Assumption

2.4.
Some criteria to
L^{p}
-boundedness and oscillatory integrals

In what follows, we prepare several basic criteria to establish
L^{p}
-boundedness of operators with specific integral kernels.

Denote by
T(x,y)
the integral kernel of the operator
T
. We say that the integral kernel
T(x,y)
is admissible if and only if
T(x,y)
satisfies

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int|T(x,y)|dy+\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int|T(x,y)|dx<\infty.

(
2.55)

Now, let us recall the classical Schur’s lemma.

Lemma 2.11
.

If
T(x,y)
is admissible, then
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Moreover, we also need the following three lemmas.

Lemma 2.12
.

Assume that the integral kernel
T(x,y)
obeys the following pointwise bounds:

|T(x,y)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-n}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>2|y|\}}.

Then the operator
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1<p\leq\infty.

Proof.

Firstly, it is obvious that the operator with the kernel
\langle x\rangle^{-n}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, one has that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|T(x,y)|dy\lesssim\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\langle x\rangle^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|>2|y|\}}dy\Big)\lesssim 1.

Therefore,
T
is also bounded on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, by the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem (see Section 1.3 in
[
26
]
), the operator
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
1<p\leq\infty
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

Assume that the integral kernel
T(x,y)
obeys the following pointwise bounds:

|T(x,y)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\alpha}\langle y\rangle^{\alpha-n}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|<\frac{1}{2}|y|\}}

for some
\alpha\in[0,n)
. Then the operator
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq p<\frac{n}{\alpha}
.

Proof.

One can easily find that

\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|T(x,y)|dx\lesssim 1.

Thus,
T
is bounded on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Furthermore,
by the Hölder-type inequality in Lorentz spaces (see Section 1.4 in
[
26
]
), one has

\Big\|Tf(x)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},\infty}}\lesssim\Big\|\langle x\rangle^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle y\rangle^{-n+\alpha}f(y)\,dy\Big\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},\infty}}\lesssim\|\langle x\rangle^{-n}\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},1}}.

(
2.56)

Therefore,
T
is also bounded from
L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Thus, the conclusion follows from the off-diagonal Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem (see Section 1.4 in
[
26
]
).
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Assume that the integral kernel
T(x,y)
obeys the following pointwise bounds:

|T(x,y)|\leq C_{\varepsilon}\langle x\rangle^{-(n-\varepsilon)}\langle|x|-|y|\rangle^{-\varepsilon}{\bf 1}_{\{\frac{1}{2}|y|\leq|x|\leq 2|y|\}}

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1).
Then the operator
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1<p<\infty.

Proof.

Firstly, when
\frac{|x|}{2}\leq|y|\leq 2|x|
, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, one has

\langle x\rangle^{-(n-\varepsilon)}\langle|x|-|y|\rangle^{-\varepsilon}\lesssim\begin{cases}\langle x\rangle^{-\alpha}\langle|x|-|y|\rangle^{-\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{(n-1)(n-\alpha)}{n}},\quad\text{when $\varepsilon=\frac{n-\alpha}{n}$},&\\[8.53581pt]
\langle x\rangle^{-(n-\alpha)}\langle|x|-|y|\rangle^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{(n-1)\alpha}{n}},\quad\text{when $\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha}{n}$}.&\end{cases}

(

In addition, note that

and

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\|\langle|x|-|y|\rangle^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{(n-1)\alpha}{n}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},+\infty}_{y}}\lesssim 1.

Then, by the Hölder-type inequality in Lorentz space and the off-diagonal Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, one has that the operator

for any

.
∎

Consider the following oscillatory integral

)
and
(​​

A.1
)
, using the orthogonal properties of
Q_{j}
, we obtain that
\mathbb{A^{\pm}}:=\Big(a_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)\Big)_{i,j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}
satisfies

\begin{split}a_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)&=\sum\limits_{l_{0}\leq l\leq\tilde{m}_{n}}a_{j}^{\pm}\lambda^{2l-i-j}Q_{i}vG_{2l}vQ_{j}+\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}T_{0}Q_{j}+\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}vr_{\theta}^{\pm}(\lambda)vQ_{j},\end{split}

(
A.10)

where
\tilde{m}_{n}=\max\{0,m-\frac{n-1}{2}\}
and
l_{0}=\lfloor\frac{\vartheta(i)+\vartheta(j)+1}{2}\rfloor
. Thus, we can write
\mathbb{A^{\pm}}
in the following form

\mathbb{A^{\pm}}=D^{\pm}+(r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda))_{i,j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}},

(
A.11)

where
D^{\pm}=(d_{i,j}^{\pm})_{i,j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}}
is given by

d_{i,j}^{\pm}=\begin{cases}a_{\frac{i+j}{2}}^{\pm}Q_{i}vG_{i+j}vQ_{j},\quad&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j\,\,\mbox{is even},\\
Q_{i}T_{0}Q_{j},&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j=2m-n,\\
0,&\mbox{else}.\end{cases}

(
A.12)

Then by
(​​

A.4
)
and
(​​

A.12
)
, it follows that for any
l=0,1,\cdots,\frac{n+3}{2},
the terms
r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)
satisfy

|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{\max\{1,\,n-2m\}-l},&\text{if}\,\,i=j=m-\frac{n}{2},\\
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-l},&\text{else}.\end{cases}

(
A.13)

Now the main difficulty is to show that the vector-valued operator
D^{\pm}
is invertible.

Define
D=(d_{i,j})_{i,j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}}
by

d_{i,j}=\begin{cases}(-{\rm i})^{i+j}(-1)^{j}a_{\frac{i+j}{2}}Q_{i}vG_{i+j}vQ_{j},&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j\,\,\mbox{is even},\\
(-{\rm i})^{i+j}(-1)^{j}Q_{i}T_{0}Q_{j},&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j=2m-n,\\
0,&\mbox{else},\end{cases}

where
a_{j}=e^{\frac{+\pi{\rm i}}{m}(n-2m+2j)}a_{j}^{+}=a_{j}=e^{\frac{-\pi{\rm i}}{m}(n-2m+2j)}a_{j}^{-}
(See Lemma 2.3 in
[
4
]
). Then we have

d_{i,j}=(-{\rm i})^{i+j}(-1)^{j}e^{\frac{\pm{\rm i}\pi}{2m}(n-2m+i+j)}d_{i,j}^{\pm},

so it follows that

D=U_{0}^{\pm}D^{\pm}U_{0}^{\pm}U_{1},

(
A.14)

where
U_{0}^{\pm}=\text{diag}\{e^{\pm\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2m}(j+\frac{n}{2}-m)}(-{\rm i})^{j}Q_{j}\}_{j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}}
,
U_{1}=\text{diag}\{(-1)^{j}Q_{j}\}_{j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}}
.

Now, we can rewrite
D
as follows:

D=\begin{pmatrix}\textbf{D}_{00}&0\\[5.69046pt]
0&Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}\\[5.69046pt]
\end{pmatrix},

where
\textbf{D}_{00}=(d_{i,j})_{i,j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}\setminus\{m-\frac{n}{2}\}}.
By the fact that
\textbf{D}_{00}
is strictly positive (see Lemma 2.6 in
[
4
]
) and
Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}
is invertible when zero is a regular point, we know that
D
is invertible on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}}Q_{j}L^{2}
, which implies that
D^{\pm}
are invertible on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}}Q_{j}L^{2}
. Moreover, by
(​​

A.14
)
we have

(D^{\pm})^{-1}=U_{0}^{\mp}U_{1}D^{-1}U_{0}^{\mp}:=(M_{i,j}^{\pm})_{i,j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}}.

Thus, by Neumann series expansion, there exists some small
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(\mathbb{A^{\pm}})^{-1}=\big(D^{\pm}+(r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda))\big)^{-1}=(D^{\pm})^{-1}+\Gamma^{\pm}(\lambda),

where
\Gamma^{\pm}(\lambda)=(D^{\pm})^{-1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{\infty}\big(-(r_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda))(D^{\pm})^{-1}\big)^{l}
.
This, together with
(​​

A.6
)
and
(​​

A.13
)
, yields

\big(M^{\pm}(\lambda)\big)^{-1}=\lambda^{2m-n}B(\mathbb{A^{\pm}})^{-1}C=\sum\limits_{i,j\in J_{\mathbf{0}}}\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{j}\Big(M_{i,j}^{\pm}+\Gamma_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)\Big)Q_{j},

(
A.15)

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda)
satisfies the first estimate of
(​​

2.37
)
.

Case 2: Resonance Case (i.e.
1\leq\mathbf{k}\leq m_{n}
).

By the orthogonal properties of
Q_{j}
, we obtain from
(​​

A.5
)
and
(​​

A.2
)
that
\mathbb{A^{\pm}}:=\Big(a_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)\Big)_{i,j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}
satisfies

\begin{split}a_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)&=\sum\limits_{l_{0}\leq l\leq\tilde{m}_{n}+{\textbf{k}}}a_{j}^{\pm}\lambda^{2l-i-j}Q_{i}vG_{2l}vQ_{j}+\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}T_{0}Q_{j}+\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}vr_{\theta}^{\pm}(\lambda)vQ_{j},\end{split}

(
A.16)

where
\tilde{m}_{n}=\max\{0,m-\frac{n-1}{2}\}
and
l_{0}=\lfloor\frac{\vartheta(i)+\vartheta(j)+1}{2}\rfloor
. Thus, we can write
\mathbb{A^{\pm}}
in the following form

\mathbb{A^{\pm}}=D^{\pm}+(r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda))_{i,j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}},

(
A.17)

where
D^{\pm}=(d_{i,j}^{\pm})_{i,j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}
and
d_{i,j}
is given by
(​​

A.12
)
. Moreover, for any
l=0,1,\cdots,\frac{n+3}{2},
the terms
r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)
satisfy

|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{\max\{1,\,n-2m\}-l},&\text{if}\,\,i=j=m-\frac{n}{2},\\
\lambda^{1-l},&\text{if}\,\,i=j=2m-\frac{n}{2},\\
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-l},&\text{else}.\end{cases}

(
A.18)

Now we turn to show that the operator
D^{\pm}
is invertible. Since they have quite different structures in dimensions
n<2m
and
n>2m
,
thus, we divide the proof into
1\leq n\leq 2m-1
and
2m+1\leq n\leq 4m-1
respectively.

When
1\leq n\leq 2m-1
, we similarly define
D=(d_{i,j})_{i,j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}
by

d_{i,j}=\begin{cases}(-{\rm i})^{i+j}(-1)^{j}a_{\frac{i+j}{2}}Q_{i}vG_{i+j}vQ_{j},&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j\,\,\mbox{is even},\\
(-{\rm i})^{i+j}(-1)^{j}Q_{i}T_{0}Q_{j},&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j=2m-n,\\
0,&\mbox{else},\end{cases}

(
A.19)

where
a_{j}=e^{\frac{+\pi{\rm i}}{2m}(n-2m+2j)}a_{j}^{+}=e^{\frac{-\pi{\rm i}}{2m}(n-2m+2j)}a_{j}^{-}
(See Lemma 2.3 in
[
4
]
).
Then it follows that

D=U_{0}^{\pm}D^{\pm}U_{0}^{\pm}U_{1},

(
A.20)

where
U_{0}^{\pm}=\text{diag}\{e^{\pm\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2m}(j+\frac{n}{2}-m)}(-{\rm i})^{j}Q_{j}\}_{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}
,
U_{1}=\text{diag}\{(-1)^{j}Q_{j}\}_{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}
.

And we can rewrite
D
in the following form:

D=\begin{pmatrix}\textbf{D}_{00}&0&D_{01}\\[5.69046pt]
0&Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}&0\\[5.69046pt]
D_{10}&0&\mathbf{D}_{11}\end{pmatrix},

where
D_{10}=D_{01}^{*}
, and
\textbf{D}_{00}=(d_{i,j})_{i,j\in J^{\prime}_{{\textbf{k}}}},\textbf{D}_{11}=(d_{i,j})_{i,j\in J^{\prime\prime}_{{\textbf{k}}}}
with

J^{\prime}_{{\textbf{k}}}=\{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}};\,j<\mbox{$m-\frac{n}{2}$}\},\quad J^{\prime\prime}_{{\textbf{k}}}=\{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}};\,\mbox{$m-\frac{n}{2}$}<j<2m-\mbox{$\frac{n}{2}$}\}.

Particularly, it is known that
\textbf{D}_{00}
is strictly positive and
\textbf{D}_{11}
is strictly negative, see Lemma 2.6 in
[
4
]
.

By the definition of
Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}
, it is known that
Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}
is invertible on
Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}
.
Then, by Lemma
A.2
,
D
is invertible on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}Q_{j}L^{2}
if and only if

d:=\textbf{D}_{11}-D_{01}^{\ast}\textbf{D}_{00}^{-1}D_{01},

(
A.21)

is invertible on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}}Q_{j}L^{2}
.
Now we decompose

Q_{j}=Q_{j,1}+Q_{j,2}
,
where

Q_{j,2}L^{2}=Q_{j}L^{2}\bigcap\{x^{\alpha}v;\,\,|\alpha|\leq j\}^{\perp}.

Since
Q:=\text{diag}\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}}
is the identity operator on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}^{\prime\prime}}Q_{j}L^{2}
, and

Q_{i}vG_{i+j}vQ_{j}Q_{j,2}=0
for
i\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime},j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}
,
we obtain

d=Q\mathbf{D}_{11}Q-D_{01}^{\ast}\mathbf{D}_{00}^{-1}D_{01}=Q^{\prime}\mathbf{D}_{11}Q^{\prime}-D_{01}^{\ast}\mathbf{D}_{00}^{-1}D_{01},

(
A.22)

where
Q^{\prime}=\text{diag}\{Q_{j,1}\}_{j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}}
.
It suffices to prove that
d
is injective on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}}Q_{j}L^{2}
. Assume that
f=(f_{j})_{j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}}\in\bigoplus_{j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}}Q_{j}L^{2}
and

0=\langle df,f\rangle=\langle Q^{\prime}\mathbf{D}_{11}Q^{\prime}f,f\rangle-\langle D_{01}^{\ast}\mathbf{D}_{00}^{-1}D_{01}f,f\rangle.

(
A.23)

Note the fact that
\textbf{D}_{00}
is strictly positive,
\textbf{D}_{11}
is strictly negative (see Lemma 2.6 in [4]). We have

Q^{\prime}f=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D_{01}f=0.

(
A.24)

First,
Q^{\prime}f=0
implies
f_{j}\in Q_{j,2}L^{2}
, i.e.,
f_{j}\in\{x^{\alpha}v;\,\,|\alpha|\leq j\}^{\perp}
,
j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}
. Second, observe that

D_{01}f=\begin{pmatrix}(-{\rm i})^{2m-n}(-1)^{m-\frac{n+1}{2}+{\textbf{k}}}Q_{m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}+{\textbf{k}}}f_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}+{\textbf{k}}}\\[5.69046pt]
\vdots\\[5.69046pt]
(-{\rm i})^{2m-n}(-1)^{m-\frac{n-1}{2}}Q_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n-1}{2}}f_{m-\frac{n-1}{2}}\end{pmatrix},

thus
D_{01}f=0
and the fact that
f_{j}\in Q_{j}L^{2}\subset S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}
imply

\displaystyle Q_{2m-n-j}T_{0}f_{j}=0.

Since
Q_{j}L^{2}\subset S_{j-1}L^{2}
, by definition we have
S_{2m-n-j}T_{0}f_{j}=0
. Hence
S_{2m-n-j-1}T_{0}f_{j}=0
.
Combining the above, we have
f_{j}\in S_{j}L^{2}
.
On the other hand, since
f_{j}\in Q_{j}L^{2}
, it follows that
f_{j}\in(S_{j}L^{2})^{\bot}
. We conclude that

f_{j}\equiv 0
for all
j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}
(i.e.,
f\equiv 0
)
provided
(​​

A.23
)
holds. Therefore we have proved that
d
is invertible as well as rigidly negative definite on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}}Q_{j}L^{2}
, and that
D^{\pm}
are invertible on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}Q_{j}L^{2}
. Moreover, by
(​​

A.9
)
and
(​​

A.20
)
we have

(D^{\pm})^{-1}=U_{0}^{\pm}U_{1}D^{-1}U_{0}^{\pm}:=(M_{i,j}^{\pm})_{i,j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}},

(
A.25)

where

D^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\textbf{D}_{00}^{-1}D_{01}d^{-1}D_{10}\textbf{D}_{00}^{-1}+\textbf{D}_{00}^{-1}&-\textbf{D}_{00}^{-1}D_{01}d^{-1}\\
-d^{-1}D_{10}\textbf{D}_{00}^{-1}&d^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(
A.26)

Thus, by Neumann series expansion, there exists some small
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(\mathbb{A^{\pm}})^{-1}=\big(D^{\pm}+(r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda))\big)^{-1}=(D^{\pm})^{-1}+\Gamma^{\pm}(\lambda),

where
\Gamma^{\pm}(\lambda)=(D^{\pm})^{-1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{\infty}\big(-(r_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda))(D^{\pm})^{-1}\big)^{l}
.
This, together with
(​​

A.6
)
and
(​​

A.18
)
, yields

\big(M^{\pm}(\lambda)\big)^{-1}=\lambda^{2m-n}B(\mathbb{A^{\pm}})^{-1}C=\sum\limits_{i,j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}}\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{j}\Big(M_{i,j}^{\pm}+\Gamma_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)\Big)Q_{j},

(
A.27)

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda)
satisfies the first estimate of
(​​

2.37
)
.

When
2m+1\leq n\leq 4m-1
, we notice that the relation
(​​

A.20
)
still holds, however,
D
has the following form:

\begin{split}D&=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}&0\\
0&\mathbf{D}_{11}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

where
\textbf{D}_{11}=(d_{i,j})_{i,j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}^{\prime\prime}}}
is defined by

d_{i,j}=\begin{cases}(-{\rm i})^{i+j}(-1)^{j}a_{\frac{i+j}{2}}Q_{i}vG_{i+j}vQ_{j},\quad&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j\,\,\mbox{is even},\\
0,&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j\,\,\mbox{is odd}.\end{cases}

Since
\mathbf{D}_{11}
is strictly negative on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}^{\prime\prime}}Q_{j}L^{2}
(see Lemma 2.6 in [4]), which implies that
\mathbf{D}_{11}
is invertible on
\bigoplus_{j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}^{\prime\prime}}Q_{j}L^{2}
. Since
Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}
is invertible on
Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}
, we obtain the invertibility of
D
. By
(​​

A.20
)
,
D^{\pm}
is invertible, then Neumann series expansion yields
(​​

A.27
)

Case 3: Eigenvalue Case (i.e.
\mathbf{k}=m_{n}+1
).

Similarly, by the orthogonal properties of
Q_{j}
, in this case, we obtain from
(​​

A.5
)
and
(​​

A.3
)
that
\mathbb{A^{\pm}}:=\Big(a_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)\Big)_{i,j\in J_{m_{n}+1}}
with
a_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)
satisfies

\begin{split}a_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{l_{0}\leq l\leq 2m-\frac{n+1}{2}}a_{j}^{\pm}\lambda^{2l-i-j}Q_{i}vG_{2l}vQ_{j}&+\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}T_{0}Q_{j}+b_{1}\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}vG_{2m-n}vQ_{j}\\
&+\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}vr_{4m-n+1}^{\pm}(\lambda)vQ_{j},\end{split}

(
A.28)

where
l_{0}=\lfloor\frac{\vartheta(i)+\vartheta(j)+1}{2}\rfloor
. Thus, we can write
\mathbb{A^{\pm}}
in the following form

\mathbb{A^{\pm}}=\text{diag}(D^{\pm},Q_{2m-\frac{n}{2}}vG_{4m-n}vQ_{2m-\frac{n}{2}})+(r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda))_{i,j\in J_{m_{n}+1}},

(
A.29)

where
D^{\pm}=(d_{i,j}^{\pm})_{i,j\in J_{m_{n}+1}}
is given in
(​​

A.12
)
, and the terms
r_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)
satisfy the same estimates as in
(​​

A.18
)
.
By the similar process as in the case of
0\leq\mathbf{k}\leq m_{n}
, we know that
D^{\pm}
is invertible, and the invertibility of
Q_{2m-\frac{n}{2}}vG_{4m-n}vQ_{2m-\frac{n}{2}}
can be obtained by the same arguments in
[
30
,
42
]
, thus
\mathbb{A^{\pm}}
is invertible. Then Neumann series expansion and Lemma
A.1
yields
(​​

A.27
)
.
Thus, the proof of Theorem
2.5
is completed.

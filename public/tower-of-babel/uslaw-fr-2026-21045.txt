Wood Mouldings and Millwork Products From the People's Republic 
of China: Notice of Court Decision Not in Harmony With the Results of 
Antidumping Duty Administrative Review; Notice of Amended Final Results 
Pursuant to Court Decision

AGENCY: Enforcement and Compliance, International Trade Administration, 
Department of Commerce.

SUMMARY: On September 23, 2026, the U.S. Court of International Trade 
(CIT) issued its final judgment in China Cornici Co., Ltd. and Raoping 
Hongrong Handicrafts Co., Ltd. v. United States, Court nos. 23-00216 & 
23-00217, sustaining the first remand results issued by the U.S. 
Department of Commerce (Commerce) pertaining to the administrative 
review of the antidumping duty (AD) order on wood mouldings and 
millwork products from the People's Republic of China (China), covering 
the period of review (POR) August 12, 2020, through January 31, 2022. 
Commerce is notifying the public that the CIT's final judgment is not 
in harmony with Commerce's final results of the administrative review, 
and that Commerce is amending the final results with respect to the 
rescission of the administrative review for China Cornici Co. Ltd. 
(China Cornici) and the corresponding cash deposit rate assigned to the 
company.

DATES: Applicable October 5, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: 

Background

    On September 12, 2023, Commerce published the Final Results of the 
2020-2022 AD administrative review of wood mouldings and millwork 
products from China.\1\ In the Final Results, Commerce rescinded the 
review with respect to China Cornici because it had no suspended 
entries of subject merchandise during the POR, and did not grant a 
separate rate to Chen Chiu Global Corp. (Chen Chiu), a third-country 
reseller of merchandise exported by RaoPing HongRong Handicrafts Co. 
(RaoPing HongRong).\2\

Timken Notice

    In its decision in Timken,\6\ as clarified by Diamond Sawblades,\7\ 
the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit held that, pursuant 
to section 516A(c) and (e) of the Tariff Act of 1930, as amended (the 
Act), Commerce must publish a notice of court decision that is not ``in 
harmony'' with a Commerce determination and must suspend liquidation of 
entries pending a ``conclusive'' court decision. The CIT's September 
23, 2026, judgment constitutes a final decision of the CIT that is not 
in harmony with Commerce's Final Results. Thus, this notice is 
published in fulfillment of the publication requirements of Timken.

Cash Deposit Requirements

    Commerce will issue revised cash deposit instructions to U.S. 
Customs and Border Protection (CBP) with respect to China Cornici. 
Because we continue to find that Chen Chiu is not eligible for a 
separate rate, we will not issue revised cash deposit instructions with 
respect to Chen Chiu.

Liquidation of Suspended Entries

    Commerce, pursuant to the CIT's judgment, determined in the Final 
Redetermination that it lacks the authority to reliquidate entries, and 
that only the CIT can order reliquidation of entries.\11\ The CIT 
affirmed that the entries in question have been liquidated, and that 
Commerce could not order reliquidation of the entries. Moreover, the 
CIT found that none of the circumstances delineated in the Act and 
chapter 19 of the Code of Federal Regulations in which the CBP may 
reliquidate entries are applicable to the instant case. Therefore, in 
the event that the CIT's ruling is not appealed, or is upheld by a 
final and conclusive court decision, Commerce will not issue assessment 
instructions to CBP instructing CBP to assess antidumping duties on 
China Cornici's entries of subject merchandise for the period August 
12, 2020, through January 31, 2022.

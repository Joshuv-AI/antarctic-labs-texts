Fluctuations of two-dimensional determinantal processes associated with Berezin–Toeplitz operators
Alix Deleporte, Gaultier Lambert

Abstract
We consider a new class of determinantal point processes in the complex plane coming from the ground state of free fermions associated with Berezin–Toeplitz operators. These processes generalize the Ginibre ensemble from random matrix theory. We prove a two-term Szegő-type asymptotic expansion for the Laplace transform of smooth linear statistics. This implies a law of large number and central limit theorem for the empirical field. The limiting variance includes both contributions from the bulk and boundary of the droplet. The boundary fluctuations depend on the Hamiltonian dynamics associated with the underlying operator and, generally, are not conformally invariant.

Introduction and results

1.1  General setting.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a metric space equipped with a measure
\bm{\gamma}
and, for
\mathcal{N}\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{\mathcal{N}}
be an orthonormal family in
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X},\gamma)
. The
Slater determinant

\Phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\mathcal{N}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}!}}\det[\phi_{i}(x_{j})]_{i,j=1}^{\mathcal{N}}

(1.1)

is a fundamental example of a fermionic wavefunction, a pure state which encapsulates the Pauli exclusion principle.
In quantum many-body mechanics, Slater determinants arise as the
ground states of non-interacting fermionic systems, and they also
provide approximations for the ground states of certain mean-field
interacting systems
[
6
]
.
A basic property of Slater determinants is their characterisation by the 1-point
reduced density operator

\Pi
which is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X},\gamma)
onto
\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{\mathcal{N}}\}
.

One can associate to the wavefunction (
1.1
) the following probability
density function on
\mathfrak{X}^{\otimes\mathcal{N}}
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}}{\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\otimes\mathcal{N}}}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{\mathcal{N}})=|\Phi|^{2}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{\mathcal{N}})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}!}\det_{\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{N}}[\Pi(x_{i},x_{j})].

This interprets as the
density of probability
of a particle
configuration at a point
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\mathcal{N}})
of physical space
\mathfrak{X}^{\mathcal{N}}
.
Since these particles are
indistinguishable
, it is appropriate to view
\mathbb{P}
as an
\mathcal{N}
-point process on
\mathfrak{X}
. This point process is
determinantal

and its
correlation kernel
is the integral kernel of the projection
\Pi
. This means that for any
1\leq k\leq\mathcal{N}
and any
k
-observable
\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{X}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous and bounded, viewed as a multiplication operator, one has

\displaystyle\langle\Phi|\mathcal{O}|\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{O}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\mathcal{N}})

(1.2)

\displaystyle={n\choose k}^{-1}\int\mathcal{O}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\frac{1}{k!}\det_{k\times k}[\Pi(x_{i},x_{j})]\gamma(\mathrm{d}x_{1})\cdots\gamma(\mathrm{d}x_{\mathcal{N}}).

In probabilistic terms, the correlation functions of the point process
\mathbb{P}
are given, for every
k\leq\mathcal{N}
, by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\mapsto\frac{1}{k!}\det_{k\times k}[\Pi(x_{i},x_{j})]
, so that the projection
\Pi
characterizes the law of the point process.
We refer to
[
15
,
45
,
42
]
for some general background and different perspectives on determinantal processes.

1.2  Ginibre ensemble.

One fundamental example of such determinantal process is the (complex) Ginibre ensemble.
Let
N>0
, and let
\mathfrak{X}=\mathbb{C}
equipped with the measure
\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{d}^{2}z
. Consider the orthogonal family for
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
given by

\phi_{k}(z)=z^{k}e^{-N|z|^{2}/2}\sqrt{\tfrac{N^{k+1}}{k!}}\qquad\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(1.3)

Here, the scale parameter
N>0
controls the particle density.

Given
\mathcal{N}\in\mathbb{N}
, the point process obtained from
(\phi_{0},\cdots,\phi_{\mathcal{N}-1})
is called the
\mathcal{N}
-Ginibre process. It describes the eigenvalues of a random matrix
\mathbf{A}
with density
\propto e^{-N\tr(\mathbf{A}^*\mathbf{A})}
on
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{N}}
(
\mathbf{A}
has i.i.d. complex Gaussian entries).
This model was introduced by Ginibre in 1965
[
40
]
, it has a fundamental place within random matrix theory with many applications in probability theory, as well as in quantum many body physics and quantum chaos. We refer to the survey
[
24
]
for an overview of these applications and many results and references on the Ginibre ensemble.

One can also consider the case
\mathcal{N}=\infty
: the
\infty
-Ginibre process is defined as the determinantal point process associated with the projection kernel

P_{N}:(z,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mapsto Ne^{N(z\bar{x}-|z|^{2}/2-|x|^{2}/2)}={\textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}}\,\phi_{k}(z)\overline{\phi_{k}(x)};

(1.4)

since
P_{N}
is locally trace-class one can still make sense of (
1.2
) if
\mathcal{O}
is compactly supported.

The range of
P_{N}
is the
Fock–Bargmann space

\mathcal{B}_{N}:=\big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma});u(z)=g(z)e^{-N|z|^{2}/2},g(z)\text{ is holomorphic}\big\}.

(1.5)

\mathcal{B}_{N}
is a closed subspace of

L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
with reproducing kernel
P_{N}
. The
\infty
-Ginibre
process serves as a local (bulk) model for a general class of Bergman
kernels associated with weighted spaces of holomorphic functions,
arising for instance in connection with orthogonal polynomials

[
8
,
10
,
3
]

as well as geometric quantization on Kähler manifolds
[
66
,
63
,
25
,
54
]
.
The
\infty
-Ginibre process is the unique stationary determinantal
process on
\mathbb{C}
with intensity
\bm{\gamma}

[
59
]
. It is a fundamental example of negatively correlated, hyperuniform, stationary point process.

The
\mathcal{N}
-Ginibre process, coming from random matrix theory, is a natural truncation and choosing
N=\mathcal{N}
, the intensity of the process is approximately
N\mathbf{1}\{z\in\mathbb{D}\}\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z)
, where
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C};|z|\leq 1\}
is the unit disk, for large
N
.
Hence, the particles are approximately uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{D}
and there is an
edge
which influences the fluctuations of the point process.

1.3  Determinantal processes associated with Berezin–Toeplitz operators.

One can interpret the
\mathcal{N}
-Ginibre ensemble as the
ground state
of the self-adjoint operator

H_{N}=P_{N}VP_{N}
on
\mathcal{B}_{N}
where the
potential

V:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
is any smooth radial increasing function.
For instance, taking
V(z)=|z|^{2}
, one has
H_{N}\phi_{k}=\frac{k+1}{N}\phi_{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and the
\mathcal{N}
-Ginibre ensemble is the
determinantal point process associated with the projection
\mathbf{1}\{H_{N}\leq\mu\}
on
\mathcal{B}_{N}
for
\mathcal{N}:=\lfloor N\mu\rfloor
.

From a mathematical physics perspective,
\mathcal{B}_{N}
is the
first Landau level
(eigenspace of lowest energy) for a two-dimensional magnetic Laplacian
(-i\hbar\nabla+A)^{2}
with constant magnetic field, say
A(x)=x^{\perp}
, and
\hbar=N^{-1}
is the semiclassical parameter.
This quantum system has been introduced by Laughlin to give a mathematical description of the Quantum Hall Effect
[
49
]
.

Then, the operator
P_{N}VP_{N}
can be viewed as a low-energy
approximation (simplified model) of the one-body, two-dimensional Hamiltonian
(-i\hbar\nabla+A)^{2}+\hbar V
. The underlying idea is that if
V
is smooth
and sufficiently small, it acts as a perturbation and the low-lying
eigenfunctions are close from the first Landau level;
P_{N}(-i\hbar\nabla+A)^{2}+\hbar V)P_{N}=\hbar P_{N}(1+V)P_{N}

[
56
,
22
]
.

P_{N}VP_{N}
is a
Berezin–Toeplitz operator

[
7
,
39
,
19
,
13
,
25
]
. The
functional and spectral properties of these operators, at least if
V

is sufficiently regular, are closely related to that of
pseudodifferential operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. In fact, the

Fourier–Bros–Iagolnitzer transform
(FBI transform), or
Gaussian wave-packet transform, unitarily maps
\mathcal{B}_{N}
to

L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and it conjugates Berezin–Toeplitz operators with
pseudodifferential operators (within good symbol classes). Then, the
semiclassical parameter is
N^{-1}
, so that
N\to+\infty
is a
semiclassical limit.
These properties extend to Berezin–Toeplitz quantization on more
general Kähler manifolds; for simplicity we state our results in the
case of
\mathbb{C}
but the techniques we use, and part of the
available results, extend to this more general case.

The spectral properties of
H_{N}=P_{N}VP_{N}
, in the limit
N\to+\infty
, are therefore linked to the Hamiltonian dynamics of
V
, i.e. the flow generated by the vector field
\nabla^{\perp}V
.
In two-dimension, by the
action-angle theorem
, the property
that
V
is preserved along this flow suffices to characterise the
dynamics. This property extends to the quantum case: if
\{V=\mu\}
is
connected and
\mu
is a regular energy level for
V

(i.e.
\nabla^{\perp}V\neq 0
on
\{V=\mu\}
), then one can completely
describe the eigenfunctions of
H_{N}
with eigenvalue near
\mu

[
26
]
.
We will rely on these
quantum integrability
to describe the edge
fluctuations of the point process.

The goal of this article is to study the determinantal processes
associated with general Berezin–Toeplitz operators on
\mathbb{C}
, in the
sense that
\Pi=\mathbf{1}\{P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu\}
, thus generalising the case of
the Ginibre ensemble.
We will work with the following class of potentials.

Assumptions 1
.

Let
V:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that there exists
\delta>0
such that the following conditions hold:

a)

\{V\leq\mu+\delta\}
is a compact set and
\log(V-\mu)
is Lipschitz on
\{V\geq\mu+\delta\}
;

b)

V\in C^{\infty}
on
\{V<\mu+\delta\}
;

c)

\{V=\mu\}
is a non-trivial, connected smooth curve, on which
\nabla^{\perp}V\neq 0
.

These assumptions are relatively standard when dealing with semiclassical spectral asymptotics: a) guarantees that the spectrum of
H_{N}
below the energy
\mu+\delta
is discrete and that
\mathcal{N}(\mu)<+\infty
. In addition, the control of the growth of
V
at infinity is a technical condition which will be useful to prove suitable concentration properties for the eigenfunctions; see Section
2
.
The assumption b) allows to use the general semiclassical techniques such as functional calculus, Fourier Integral Operators, etc.
Finally, the assumption c) implies that the droplet
\mathcal{D}
is simply connected (with a
C^{\infty}
-boundary) and that the flow (
1.6
) is well-defined.
In contrast, if
\{V=\mu\}
contains several connected components,
they can resonate, making the analysis more difficult, see

[
35
]
for an analogous results on
\mathbb{R}
. As in

[
35
]
, we expect that generically, there are
no resonances in the multi-cut Berezin–Toeplitz ensemble.

Under these hypothesis, our main result, Theorem
1.2
below,
gives a two-term asymptotics expansion for the Laplace transform of
smooth linear statistics
\mathbf{X}(f)=f(x_{1})+\cdots+f(x_{\mathcal{N}})
where

f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R})
.

To describe our results, we rely on the following notations.

•

N\in[1,+\infty)
is a scale parameter (inverse semiclassical
parameter). The asymptotic regime under study is
N\to+\infty
.

•

H_{N}:=P_{N}VP_{N}
and
\Pi_{N}:=\mathbf{1}\{P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu\}
are operators
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
with
\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{d}^{2}z
.

•

Under Assumptions
1
,
\Pi_{N}
is a finite rank
projector (Lemma
2.1
), and we let
\mathcal{N}(\mu)=\tr(\Pi_N)
.

•

The
droplet
is
\mathcal{D}:=\{V<\mu\}
.
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is compact.

•

Let
\Xi_{V}
be the differential vector field associated with
\nabla^{\perp}V
. we define the flow; for
z_{0}\in\{V=\mu\}
,

z_{\theta}:=\exp(\theta\tfrac{T(\mu)}{2\pi} \Xi_V )(z_{0}),\qquad\theta\in[0,2\pi],

(1.6)

where
T(\mu)
denotes the
period
of this flow.

•

\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}
denotes the probability law of the determinantal point process
\mathbf{X}
associated with the projector
\Pi
.

•

For suitable functions
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbf{X}(f)
denotes the corresponding
linear statistic
. For a determinantal point process, if defined, the Laplace transform of the random variable
\mathbf{X}(f)
is

\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}[e^{{\mathbf{X}}(f)}]=\det(1+\Pi(e^f-1)\Pi)=\det_{\Pi}(e^{f}).

(1.7)

The first
\det
is a Fredholm determinant on
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
and we view
e^{f}
as a multiplication operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
.
Here
\mathcal{N}=\operatorname{rank}(\Pi)<\infty
, and the second

\det
is a
\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{N}
determinant computed on the
range of
\Pi
;
\displaystyle\det_{\Pi}(e^{f})=\det_{\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{N}}\big[\langle{\rm u}_{k}|e^{f}{\rm u}_{n}\rangle\big]
.

1.4  Fluctuation results.

The goal of this article is to
investigate the fluctuations of the determinantal process
\mathbb{P}_{N}

associated with the fermionic state
\Pi_{N}
using linear statistics,
in the limit
N\to\infty
.
Before stating our main results, we formulate a
law of large
numbers for
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi_{N}}
, it shows that the particles condensate
uniformly inside the droplet.

Proposition 1.1
.

Under the Assumptions
1
, one has the pointwise asymptotics as
N\to\infty
:

\Pi_{N}(x,x)\simeq N\mathbf{1}\{V(x)<\mu\},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{V=\mu\}.

For any
\epsilon>0
, these asymptotics holds uniformly in
\{|V-\mu|\geq\epsilon\}
with exponentially small errors.
In particular, as
N\to+\infty
, the particle number satisfies

{\mathcal{N}}(\mu)\simeq N\bm{\gamma}(\mathcal{D})=\frac{N}{\pi}|\{V<\mu\}|.

Moreover, for any function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
f\leq e^{C|\cdot|}

for some
C\geq 0
, the rescaled linear statistic converges:

N^{-1}\mathbf{X}(f)\to\int_{\mathcal{D}}f\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}\qquad\text{in $L^{2}$ as }N\to\infty.

This Weyl law is well-known, and admits several improvements concerning the speed of convergence of
\mathcal{N}
; we nevertheless give a proof in Section
2.2
, under our hypotheses, as an intermediate step to obtain decay estimates for the correlation kernel.
In particular, these estimates imply universality of local statistics
for the Berezin–Toeplitz ensembles, meaning that the microscopic
point process converges in distribution to the
\infty
-Ginibre point
process in the bulk; see Proposition
2.13
.

For the
\mathcal{N}
-Ginibre ensemble (which corresponds to
V(z)=|z|^{2}
and
\mu=1
so that
\mathcal{N}=N
and
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{D}
),
Proposition
1.1
is known as the
circular law
; this law of large number is
universal
for non-hermitian Wigner matrices
[
14
]
.

Proposition
1.1
contrasts with the law of large number for normal matrix ensembles, or more generally two-dimensional Coulomb gases. In this case, the equilibrium measure is determined by a variational problem; it is the unique minimizer of the (weighted) logarithmic energy functional. In particular, it has a density
\frac{1}{\pi}(\Delta V)\mathbf{1}\{\mathcal{D}\}

where
V
is the external potential and the droplet
\mathcal{D}
is given in terms of an obstacle problem coming from potential theory. In particular, this measure is uniform on the droplet only in the case where
V
is
harmonic
, that is,
V(z)
is quadratic on a neighborhood of
\mathcal{D}
.
We refer to the book
[
61
, Part 1]
for a description of the macroscopic equilibrium property of general Coulomb gases and the survey
[
53
]
for an overview of this field.

Our main result is a
central limit theorem

(CLT) for our Berezin–Toeplitz determinantal processes.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
, with
|f|\leq C(1+|\cdot|)
for some
C\geq 0
, be
C^{\infty}
in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{D}=\{V<\mu\}
.
Under the Assumptions
1
, one has the two-terms asymptotics as
N\to\infty
,

\mathbb{E}_{\Pi_{N}}[e^{\mathbf{X}(f)}]=\exp( \E_{\Pi_N}\X(f)+ \tfrac 12 \Sigma(f) + o(1) )

(1.8)

where the variance
\Sigma=\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}+\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}
with

\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(f):=\tfrac{1}{2}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\mathcal{D})}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 1}k|\widehat{f}_{k}|^{2}\,,\qquad\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(f):=\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\nabla f|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}\,.

(1.9)

The Fourier coefficients are given in terms of the test function and the flow (
1.6
) along the curve
\partial\mathcal{D}
by

\widehat{f}_{k}=\int_{[0,2\pi]}f(z_{\theta})e^{-ik\theta}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.10)

Consequently, (
1.11
) holds as
N\to+\infty
.

In the asymptotics (
1.8
), one can only replace the mean
\mathbb{E}_{\Pi_{N}}\mathbf{X}(f)
by its asymptotics
N\bm{\gamma}(f)
if
f
is supported inside
\mathcal{D}
.
In this case, it suffices to assume that
f
is
C^{1}
and the CLT also holds at arbitrary mesoscopic scales (Theorem
4.4
).
However, replacing the mean is not possible for a general test function since, at order 1, the mean contribution from the boundary oscillates as
N\to\infty
(even in the case where the droplet is simply connected).
Nevertheless, the
boundary fluctuations
stabilise as
N\to\infty
and converge to a weighted
H^{1/2}
-seminorm. This is a
strong Szegő-type limit theorem
. To describe the edge fluctuations is the main step where we need the Assumptions
1
b) and c), together with the fact that
f
is smooth in a neighborhood of the boundary
\{V=\mu\}
.
An analogous result has been recently proved for one-dimensional free fermions in
[
35
]
under similar assumptions.
Theorem
1.2
has many other counterparts for different types of ensembles coming from
random matrix theory
and we shortly review the literature in Section
1.5
.

The fluctuations of the
\mathcal{N}
-Ginibre process around the circular law have been first studied in
[
59
]
; if
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
is
C^{1}
in a neighborhood of
\mathbb{D}
and grow at most exponentially, then (in distribution) as
N\to\infty
,

\mathbf{X}(f)-\mathbb{E}\mathbf{X}(f)\,\Longrightarrow\,\sqrt{\Sigma(f)}\,\mathcal{G}

(1.11)

where
\mathcal{G}
is a (standard) complex Gaussian and the variance

\Sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\mathbb{D})}^{2}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}
.
These fluctuations can be interpreted in terms of the
Gaussian
free field
(GFF) which is a universal object in two-dimensional
random geometry. Namely, according to
[
59
, Corollary 2]
, the (re-centered)
log characteristic polynomial
of the
N
-Ginibre ensemble converges weakly as
N\to\infty
to the two-dimensional Gaussian free field on
\mathbb{D}

conditioned to be harmonic in
\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
The remarkable fact that linear statistics of the Ginibre ensemble
(and related models) exhibit Gaussian fluctuations without
(re)normalization is a manifestation of the strong

hyperuniformity
of Coulomb-type gases; see

[
38
]
and
[
50
]
for recent developments.

In
[
58
]
, Rider–Virág also studied the fluctuations of the
\infty
-Ginibre process (and other canonical invariant two-dimensional determinantal point processes). More precisely, they proved that for any
f\in L^{1}\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R})

\mathbf{X}(f)-\int f\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}\,\Longrightarrow\,\sqrt{\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)}\,\mathcal{G}.

(1.12)

In particular, in (
1.11
), the bulk/edge contributions are independent and the edge contribution
\Sigma^{1}_{\mathbb{D}}(f)
is a manifestation of the
truncation in this model
. This noise corresponds (up to a factor
\frac{1}{2}
) to the contribution of the fluctuations of linear statistics for free fermions on
\partial\mathbb{D}
and it relates to the
strong Szegő limit theorem
; see Section
1.5

An important observation is that, because of the boundary term, the variance functional
\Sigma
in Theorem
1.2
is not
conformally invariant
and it does not arise either from the pullback of a GFF by a mapping
\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{D}
.
This is in contrast with the fluctuations of Coulomb gases, eigenvalues of random matrices and dimer models (in the liquid region).

Remark 1.3
.

In case

where
\bm{g}
denotes the probability of a standard complex Gaussian random variable
1
1

1

In particular, the eigenvalues satisfy
\lambda_{n}\sim v(\frac{n}{N})
as

.
.
Then, in case
\{V<\mu\}
is simply connected for
\min_{V}<\mu<\sup V
,
the joint probability density function of the determinantal point process associated with

This measure corresponds to the law of the eigenvalues of the (rescaled)

(complex) Ginibre ensemble.
In particular, in the radial case, the underlying point is independent of the potential

, modulo the particle’s number

Moreover, if

1.5  Related central limit theorems.

The first and foundational example of two-term asymptotics expansion for
\det_{\Pi_{N}}(e^{f})
is the
strong Szegő limit theorem
, in which case
\Pi_{N}
is the Fourier projection on
\operatorname{span}\{e^{i\frac{k\cdot}{2\pi}};0\leq k<N\}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
. The multiplication by
e^{f}
corresponds to a Toeplitz operator in the Fourier basis, so
\det_{\Pi_{N}}(e^{f})
is a Toeplitz determinant. Then, if
f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
, it holds as
N\to\infty

1.6  Proof strategy and techniques.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
consists in obtaining the asymptotics of formula (
1.7
) for the Laplace transform of the linear statistic
\mathbf{X}(f)
under
\Pi_{N}
.

Let us introduce the following notation: given a
a (locally trace-class) projection
\Pi
and a function
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R})
(viewed as a multiplication operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
), define

\Upsilon:(f;\Pi)\mapsto\log\det(1+\Pi(e^{f}-1)\Pi)-\tr(\Pi f\Pi).

(1.14)

The point of this notation is that
\tr(\Pi f\Pi)=\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}\mathbf{X}(f)
,
so that
\Upsilon(f;\Pi)=\log\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}[e^{\mathbf{X}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}\mathbf{X}(f)}]
is
the
\log
Laplace transform of the centred linear statistics.

In Theorem
1.2
, the fluctuations of
\mathbf{X}(f)
decomposes,
asymptotically, into two independent parts:

-

a bulk term
\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}
which is
universal
and akin to the
\infty
-Ginibre case restricted to the droplet
\mathcal{D}
,

-

a boundary term
\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}
given by a
H^{1/2}
-seminorm weighted by the Hamiltonian flow along the curve
\{V=\mu\}
.

These two parts arise from different asymptotics of the projection kernel
\Pi_{N}
in the bulk and at the boundary of the droplet. The proof proceeds in three steps.

1

In Section
2
, we obtain a decorrelation estimate
to separate the contribution from
the bulk
and the
the
boundary
. Let
\delta>0
be a small parameter. To simplify (up to a small overlap), we show that

\Upsilon(f;\Pi_{N})\simeq\Upsilon(f_{1};\Pi_{N})+\Upsilon(f_{2};\Pi_{N})

(1.15)

where
f_{1}
is supported in
\{|V-\mu|\leq\delta\}
(boundary) and

f_{2}
is supported in
\{V\leq\mu-\delta/2\}
(inside the
bulk). Note that the two parts
f_{1},f_{2}
overlap and the contribution of the
exterior region
\{V\leq\mu+\delta/2\}
is exponentially small.
These estimates rely on the off-diagonal of the kernel of
\Pi_{N}
away from the boundary of the droplet. This fact is well-known for the Ginibre ensemble and we generalise it to other Berezin-Toeplitz spectral projector using methods from semiclassical analysis.

2

In the bulk, the projection
\Pi_{N}
is approximated by
P_{N}
(up to exponentially small errors). This allows to approximate as
N\to\infty
,

\Upsilon(f_{2};\Pi_{N})\simeq\Upsilon(f_{2};P_{N})\to\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f_{2}).

(1.16)

The last asymptotics follow from the CLT for the
\infty
-Ginibre ensemble and it suffices to assume that
f_{2}\in C^{1}
.
In this regard, we cannot directly apply the results from
[
59
,
58
,
2
]
because the convergence in these papers hold for cumulants and not a priori for log determinants.
One could however use the results from
[
1
,
52
,
5
]
which are also valid for more general ensembles with sophisticated proofs (see Section
1.5
).
Instead, in Section
4
, we give instead an elementary proof of (
1.16
) based on the determinantal structure. This result can be of independent interest since it is not specific to the Ginibre ensemble.

3

At the edge, the model is not translation invariant but the behavior of
\Pi_{N}
is still universal as
N\to\infty
.
Let
\mathcal{A}:=P_{N}e^{f_{1}}P_{N}
and split

\Upsilon(f_{1};\Pi_{N})=\Upsilon(\mathcal{A};\Pi_{N})+\tr(\Pi_N(\log(\mathcal A)-f_1)\Pi_N).

(1.17)

The point is that the matrix
A_{k,n}=\langle{\rm u}_{k};\mathcal{A}{\rm u}_{n}\rangle
is
approximately Toeplitz
(at least if
\lambda_{n}
lies in a small neighborhood of
\mu
– equivalently if
n
lies in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{N}
) where

({\rm u}_{n},\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is the spectral data of
H_{N}
.
This structure follows from the
integrability
of
H_{N}
in the sense that it is possible to obtain asymptotics expansion (to any accuracy) for the eigenvalues of
H_{N}
; these are instances of
WKB approximation
(Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin).
In Section
3
, we rely on WKB approximations and develop a new
replacement principle
in order to show that

\Upsilon(\mathcal{A};\Pi_{N})\simeq\Upsilon(B;\pi)

where
B
is a Toeplitz matrix (with symbol the smooth function

\theta\ni[0,2\pi]\mapsto e^{f}(z_{\theta})
) and
\pi
is the
Szegő projector on
S^{1}
.
This is the step where the potential
V
comes into play through the Hamiltonian flow (
1.6
).
Note that this replacement principle is also not specific to the Berezin–Toeplitz case and can also be of independent interest in other context.
Then, applying the strong Szegő limit theorem, one has as
N\to\infty
,

\Upsilon(B;\Pi_{N})\simeq\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(f_{1})

This is the origin of the weighted
H^{1/2}
-seminorm along the boundary.
For the second term in (
1.17
),
\mathcal{A}
is a Berezin-Toeplitz with as smooth, positive and bounded symbol, so one has a semiclassical expansion
\log(\mathcal A)=P_{N}\big(\log(e^{f_1})+N^{-1}\log^{\prime\prime}(e^{f_{1}})|\nabla e^{f_{1}}|^{2}+O(N^{-2})\big)P_{N}
. Then, by Theorem
1.1
, as
N\to\infty
,

\tr(\Pi_N(\log(\mathcal A)-f_1)\Pi_N)\simeq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\nabla f_{1}|^{2}=\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(f_{1}).

Altogether, using that
\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(f_{1})=\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(f)
and
\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(f_{1})+\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(f_{2})\simeq\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(f)
we conclude that

\Upsilon(f;\Pi_{N})\simeq\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(f)+\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(f).

1.7  Notations.

In this article, we will use the following notational conventions.
Recall that we work on
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
equipped with the measure
\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{d}^{2}z
.

•

Let
\Pi
be a projection operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
. Recall that we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}
the law of the determinantal point process associated with the projection operator
\Pi
and we define, for suitable function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\Upsilon(f;\Pi)=\log\det(1+\Pi(e^{f}-1)\Pi)-\tr(\Pi f\Pi)=\log\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}[e^{\mathbf{X}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}\mathbf{X}(f)}].

•

Given
A:L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\bm{\gamma})
, we denote by

\|A\|
the operator norm of
A
on this space.
In particular, if
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded function, viewed as a multiplication operator,

\|f\|=\|f\|_{C^{0}}=\sup\{|f(x)|;x\in\mathbb{C}\}
.

•

If
A
is a bounded operator, if defined, we denote by
(x,z)\mapsto A(x,z)
the integral kernel of
A
.
Then, by Schur’s test:

\|A\|^{2}\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\int|A(x,z)|\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z),\int|A(z,x)|\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)\right).

•

If
A
is Hilbert-Schmidt, we denote by
\|A\|_{\rm H}
its Hilbert-Schmidt
norm (Schatten-2 norm):

\|A\|_{\rm H}^{2}=\iint|A(x,z)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z).

•

If
A
is trace-class, we denote by
\|A\|_{\tr}
its trace norm (Schatten-1 norm). We have

\|A\|_{\tr}\leq\iint|A(x,z)|\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}x)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z).

•

Given two functions
a_{N},b_{N}\geq 0
for
N\geq 1
, we write

a_{N}\lesssim b_{N}
is there exists constants
C,N_{0}\geq 1
such
that
a_{N}\leq Cb_{N}
for all
N\geq N_{0}
.

Decoupling the bulk and the boundary

Recall the definition (

P_{N}
,
N\geq 1

and we denote
\varepsilon_{N}:=N^{-\frac{1}{2}}

This section aims at giving estimates on the eigenfunctions of
P_{N}VP_{N}
below the energy
\mu+\delta

1

2.1  Basic estimates.

The first order of business is to prove that
\Pi_{N}
is a finite rank
projector.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the Assumption
1
a), the spectrum of
H_{N}
below
\mu+\delta
consists of finitely many eigenvalues (counting multiplicities).

Proof.

Since
\{V\leq\mu+\delta\}
is compact, let
B
be an open ball, centred at 0,
\{V\leq\mu\}\subset B
and define

V_{1}=\begin{cases}\min(V)&\text{ on }B\\
\mu(1+\delta)&\text{ outside }B.\end{cases}

Then
V_{1}\leq V
, so that as (positive) operators, the spectral projectors
\mathbf{1}(P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu)\leq\mathbf{1}(P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}\leq\mu)
.
Since
V_{1}
is spectral data of
P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}
is explicit (see Remark
1.3
); this operator only has eigenvalues which
accumulate at
\mu(1+\delta)
.
Therefore,

\tr[1(P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu)]\leq\tr[\mathbf{1}(P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}\leq\mu)]<\infty

This proves that the spectral projector
\Pi_{N}=\mathbf{1}(P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu)
has finite range.
∎

Observe that
\Pi_{N}
is a projection on a (finite-dimensional) subspace of
\mathcal{B}_{N}
, so that its kernel
\mathbb{C}^{2}\ni(x,z)\mapsto\Pi_{N}(x,z)
is smooth.
Since
0\leq\Pi_{N}\leq P_{N}
as operators, one has

0\leq\Pi_{N}(x,x)\leq P_{N}(x,x)=N,\qquad x\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.1)

Then, by Cauchy-Schwarz,

|\Pi_{N}(x,z)|\leq\sqrt{\Pi_{N}(z,z)\Pi_{N}(x,x)}\leq N,\qquad(x,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}.

(2.2)

Similarly, the complementary projection satisfies

|P_{N}(x,z)-\Pi_{N}(x,z)|\leq\sqrt{(N-\Pi_{N}(z,z))(N-\Pi_{N}(x,x))}\leq N,\qquad(x,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}.

(2.3)

We also record that if
u\in\mathcal{H}_{N}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}\mapsto|u(z)|^{2}e^{N|z|^{2}}
is subharmonic, as the square modulus of a holomorphic function. Thus, for every
\epsilon>0
,

|u(0)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{|z|\leq\epsilon}|u(z)|^{2}e^{N|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z).

Choosing
\epsilon=\varepsilon_{N}=N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and using that
e/\pi\leq 1
, we obtain for any
u\in\mathcal{B}_{N}
,

|u(0)|\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\mathbf{1}\{\mathbb{D}(0,\varepsilon_{N})\}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.4)

One cannot immediately translate this inequality at other points since the space
\mathcal{B}_{N}
is not translation-invariant. However one can apply
magnetic translations
: given
u\in\mathcal{H}_{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, the function

u_{x}:z\mapsto u(z-x)\exp(N(z\bar{x}-\bar{z}x))

is in
\mathcal{H}_{N}
. Therefore, by applying (
2.4
) to
u_{x}
we obtain for any
x\in\mathbb{C}
,

|u(x)|\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\mathbf{1}\{\mathbb{D}(x,\varepsilon_{N})\}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.5)

In particular, if
P_{N}u=u
(so that
u\in\mathcal{B}_{N}
) and
\|u\|_{L^{2}}=1
then
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}
. This inequality is true for any (normalised) eigenfunctions of
H_{N}:=P_{N}VP_{N}
.

In the remainder this section, we record some properties of the projection
P_{N}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
, let
\rho:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-Lipschitz function, and define

P_{N}^{\alpha}:=e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}P_{N}e^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}
. There exists
C_{\alpha}\geq 1
such that for
x,z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,

|P_{N}^{\alpha}(x,z)|\leq C_{\alpha}N\exp(-N|x-z|^2/4),\qquad\|P_{N}^{\alpha}-P_{N}\|\leq C_{0}\alpha.

Proof.

The kernel of
A=e^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}P_{N}e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}-P_{N}
is explicit and, using that
\rho
is 1-Lipschitz, it satisfies

|A(z,x)|=N\big|e^{\alpha(\rho(x)-\rho(z))/\varepsilon_{N}}-1\big|e^{-N|x-z|^{2}/2}\leq\alpha\varepsilon_{N}^{-3/2}|x-z|e^{-|x-z|^{2}/2\varepsilon_{N}^{2}+|x-z|/\varepsilon_{N}}.

Consequently,

\|A\|\leq\alpha\sup_{x\in\mathbb{C}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon_{N}^{-3/2}|x-z|e^{-|x-z|^{2}/2\varepsilon_{N}^{2}+|x-z|/\varepsilon_{N}}\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z)=\alpha\int|z|e^{-|z|^{2}/2+|z|}\mathrm{d}^{2}z.

This proves the second claim. For the first claim, we simply have

|P_{N}^{\alpha}(x,z)|=Ne^{\alpha(\rho(x)-\rho(z))/\varepsilon_{N}}e^{-N|x-z|^{2}/2}\leq Ne^{-N|x-z|^{2}/4+c_{\alpha}}

using that for any
\alpha\geq 0
, there is
c_{\alpha}\geq 0
such that
u^{2}/2-\alpha u\geq u^{2}/4-c_{\alpha}
for
u\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

There is a constant
{\rm c}>0
so that for any continuous function
g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\|[P_{N},g]\|\leq{\rm c}\,\varepsilon_{N}\mathrm{Lip}(g),\qquad\quad\mathrm{Lip}(g):=\sup_{y\neq x}\left(\frac{|g(y)-g(x)|}{|y-x|}\right).

Moreover, if
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
is independent of
N
, in
C^{1}_{0}
, and
g=f(\cdot\epsilon_{N}^{-1})
for some function
\epsilon_{N}\geq 1
with
\epsilon_{N}^{2}N\to\infty
, then as
N\to\infty
,

\|[P_{N},g]\|_{\rm H}^{2}\to\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)=\int|\nabla f|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
2.2
. The kernel of operator in question is

[P_{N},g]:(z,x)\mapsto(g(x)-g(z))P_{N}(z,x).

Then, by Schur’s test,

\|[P_{N},g]\|\leq N\mathrm{Lip}(g)\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\int|x-z|e^{-N|x-z|^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)=\varepsilon_{N}\mathrm{Lip}(g)\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}e^{-t/2}\mathrm{d}t.

This proves the first claim. For the second claim, by a change of variables with
\eta_{N}^{-2}=N\epsilon_{N}^{2}
,

\|[P_{N},g]\|_{\rm H}^{2}=\iint\big|[P_{N},g](z,x)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)=\eta_{N}^{-2}\iint|f(x)-f(x+z\eta_{N})|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z).

(2.6)

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{C}
be a compact such that
\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{supp}f;\mathcal{S}^{c})\geq 1
– in particular
\operatorname{supp}f\subset\mathcal{S}
.
Then

\iint\mathbf{1}\{x\in\mathcal{S}^{c}\}|f(x+z\eta_{N})|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)\leq\iint\mathbf{1}\{|z|\geq\eta_{N}^{-1}\}|f(x)|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)\leq e^{-\eta_{N}^{-1}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

\|[P_{N},g]\|_{\rm H}^{2}=\eta_{N}^{-2}\iint\mathbf{1}\{x\in\mathcal{S}\}|f(x)-f(x+z\eta_{N})|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)+\underset{N\to\infty}{o(1)}.

Now, if
f\in C^{1}
and
\eta_{N}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
, one has
\eta_{N}^{-2}|f(x)-f(x+z\eta_{N})|^{2}\to|\nabla f(x)|^{2}|z|^{2}
for
(x,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, then by dominated convergence (
f
is independent of
N
), we have as
N\to\infty

\|[P_{N},g]\|_{\rm H}^{2}\to\iint\mathbf{1}\{x\in\mathcal{S}\}|\nabla f(x)|^{2}|z|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(z)=\int|\nabla f|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}.\qed

Remark 2.4
.

For a linear statistic (for a suitable test function
f
) of the
\infty
-Ginibre ensemble, one has

{\rm Var}_{\Pi_{N}}[\mathbf{X}(f)]=\tfrac{1}{2}\|[P_{N},f]\|_{\rm H}^{2}
.
By (
2.6
), the norm of this commutator has another probabilistic
interpretation as

\|[P_{N},f]\|_{\rm H}^{2}=N\int{\rm Var}_{\rm Z}[f(x+\varepsilon_{N}{\rm Z})]\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}x)

where
{\rm Z}
is a standard complex Gaussian. Thus, if
f_{N}
is
bounded, supported in a compact set
\mathcal{A}
independent of
N
,
one has

{\rm Var}_{\Pi_{N}}[\mathbf{X}(f_{N})]\lesssim N\|f_{N}\|^{2}.

If in addition
f_{N}
is continuous with a modulus of continuity

\omega_{N}
, then

{\rm Var}_{\Pi_{N}}[\mathbf{X}(f_{N})]\lesssim N\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\omega_{N}(\epsilon_{N}u)^{2}e^{-u}\mathrm{d}u

Typical applications include Lipschitz or Hölder functions where the
estimate depend on
N
.

2.2  Decay of the correlation kernel

The goal of this section is to prove that
\Pi_{N}
is exponentially close to
P_{N}

inside the bulk
\{V<\mu\}
, and that
\Pi_{N}
is exponentially small in the
forbidden region

\{V>\mu\}
.
These estimates holds if
V
satisfies Assumptions
1
a) (the conditions b) and c) are not relevant in this section).
Moreover, without loss of generality, by adding a constant to
V
and

\mu
accordingly, we may assume that
\log(V)
is
Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathbb{C}
. In particular, the potential
V
is
positive. Our main result is the following estimates.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
V:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\log(V)
is Lipschitz-continuous and let
\mu>\min(V)
.
For every
\alpha>0
and for every
\delta>0
such that
\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta)\}
compact,
there is a constant
N_{0}(V,\mu,\delta,\alpha)\geq 1
such that for any
N\geq N_{0}
and
(x,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,

\displaystyle|\Pi_{N}(x,z)|

\displaystyle\leq N\exp(-\alpha\sqrt{N}\mathrm{dist}(x,\{V\leq\mu+\delta\})-\alpha\sqrt{N}\mathrm{dist}(z,\{V\leq\mu+\delta\}))\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle|\Pi_{N}(x,z)-P_{N}(x,z)|

\displaystyle\leq N\exp(-\alpha\sqrt{N}\mathrm{dist}(z,\{V\geq\mu-\delta\})-\alpha\sqrt{N}\mathrm{dist}(x,\{V\geq\mu-\delta\}))\,.

(2.8)

Proof.

The bound (
2.7
) is a consequence of Proposition

2.7
below with
\delta>0
fixed.
Namely, if
\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta)\}
is compact, for every
\alpha>0
and for
z\notin\mathcal{D}_{\delta}^{+}
,

|\Pi_{N}(z,z)|\lesssim\exp(-\alpha\sqrt{N}\dist(z,\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta)\}))

and there is a constant
\ell\geq 1
(depending on
(V,\mu)
) so that

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}^{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C},\operatorname{dist}(z,\{V\leq(1+\delta)\mu\})\leq\delta\}\subset\{V\leq\mu+\ell\delta\}.

Then, there is a constant
N_{0}\geq 1
such that if
N\geq N_{0}
, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

|\Pi_{N}(z,z)|\leq N\exp(-\alpha\sqrt{N}\dist(z,\{V\leq\mu+\ell\delta\})).

Here, we used the trivial bound (
2.2
) if
z\in\{V\leq\mu+\ell\delta\}
.
Replacing
\delta\leftarrow\delta/\ell
and adjusting the constant
N_{0}
, by Cauchy-Schwarz (
2.2
), this yields (
2.7
).

Similarly, the bound (
2.8
) is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.10
with
\delta
fixed; for every
\alpha>0
and and

,

0\leq N-\Pi_{N}(x,x)\lesssim{\rm exp}(-\alpha\delta\sqrt{N})

and, in this case,

\{V\leq\mu-\ell\delta\}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\delta}^{-}=\{x\in\mathbb{C},\operatorname{dist}(x,\{V\geq\mu-\delta\})\geq\delta\}

for some constant
\ell\geq 1
.
Replacing
\delta\leftarrow\delta/\ell
and adjusting the constant (
\alpha>0
is arbitrary), together with the trivial bound (
2.3
), this show that there is a constant
N_{0}\geq 1
such that if
N\geq N_{0}
, for every
x)\in\mathbb{C}
,

0\leq N-\Pi_{N}(x,x)\leq N\exp(-\alpha\sqrt{N}\mathrm{dist}(x,\{V\geq\mu-\delta\}))

By Cauchy-Schwarz, this yields (
2.8
).
∎

The bounds from Proposition
2.5
can be generalised
to the regime where
\delta_{N}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
provided that

\delta_{N}\geq\varepsilon_{N}\log N
; see below.

The main ingredient to prove Proposition
2.5
is
the exponential concentration of the eigenfunctions of
Berezin–Toeplitz operators following the results established in

[
34
]
. Let us first prove a variant of

[
34
, Proposition 4.1]
specialised to our setting.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
N\geq 1
, let
\alpha\geq 0
, and let
\rho:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-Lipschitz function.
There is a constant
C_{\alpha}\geq 1
so that for any eigenpair
(\lambda,u)
of
H_{N}=P_{N}VP_{N}
,

\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}((1-C_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{N})V-\lambda)|u|^{2}\leq 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}(\lambda-(1+C_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{N})V)|u|^{2}\leq 0.

Proof.

Define
P_{N}^{\alpha}:=e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}P_{N}e^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}
as in Lemma
2.2
.
Using that
(\lambda,u)
is an eigenpair of
P_{N}VP_{N}
, one has

\displaystyle\int e^{2\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}(V-\lambda)|u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}u,e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}(V-\lambda)P_{N}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}u,e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}(1-P_{N})VP_{N}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}u,(1-P_{N}^{\alpha})VP_{N}^{\alpha}e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}\sqrt{V}u,V^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-P_{N}^{\alpha})VP_{N}^{\alpha}P_{N}^{\alpha}V^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}\sqrt{V}u\rangle.

Our goal is now to show that the operator

A:=V^{-\frac{1}{2}}[V,P_{N}^{\alpha}]P_{N}^{\alpha}V^{-\frac{1}{2}}

is appropriately bounded, that is
\|A\|\lesssim\varepsilon_{N}
with a constant depending only on
\alpha
and
\log(V)
.
Using this bound, introducing a commutator, for a constant
C_{\alpha}\geq 1
,

\bigg|\int e^{2\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}(V-\lambda)|u|^{2}\bigg|\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{N}\int e^{2\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}V|u|^{2}

which proves both claims.

Since we assume that
\log(V)
is Lipschitz-continuous, there is a constant
K>0
so that for
(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,

V(x)\leq V(y)\exp(K|x-y|)

then

|V(x)-V(y)|\leq V(x)|1-\exp(K|x-y|)|\leq KV(x)|x-y|\exp(K|x-y|).

By Lemma
2.2
, this implies that

\displaystyle|[V,P_{N}^{\alpha}](x,y)|

\displaystyle=|V(x)-V(y)|P_{N}^{\alpha}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim NV(x)|x-y|\exp(K|x-y|- N|x-y|^2/4).

Then, with
A=V^{-\frac{1}{2}}[V,P_{N}^{\alpha}]P_{N}^{\alpha}V^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, there is a small numerical constant
c>0
so that

\displaystyle|A(x,z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim N\sqrt{V(x)/V(z)}\int|x-y|\exp(K|x-y|-N|x-y|^2/4- N|y-z|^2/4)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp(- c N|x-z|^2)

for some numerical constant
c>0
(the integrand concentrates for
y
in a
\varepsilon_{N}
-neighborhood of
x
).
Then, using Schur test, this yields
\|A\|\lesssim\varepsilon_{N}
as claimed.
∎

From these weighted estimates, we can obtain some upper-bounds on the eigenvalue counting function of
H_{N}=P_{N}VP_{N}
, in the spirit of the Weyl law, and deduce the decay of the spectral indicator function in the
forbidden region
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mu>0
and
\delta>0
such that
\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta)\}
is compact (Assumption
1
a).

For
\sigma>0
and
\delta>0
, let

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}^{+}(\sigma):=\{z\in\mathbb{C},\operatorname{dist}(z,\{V\leq(1+\delta)\sigma\})\leq\delta\}.

Let
(\delta_{N})_{N\geq 1}
, non-increasing, such that
\delta_{N}\geq\varepsilon_{N}\log N
.
The spectral counting function satisfies for any
0<\sigma\leq\mu+\delta
,

\mathcal{N}(\sigma):=\tr[\mathbf{1}(H_{N}\leq\sigma)]\lesssim N\bm{\gamma}(\mathcal{D}_{\delta_{N}}^{+}(\sigma)).

(2.9)

Moreover, for any
\alpha>0
, for
N\geq N_{\alpha}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\delta_{N}}^{+}(\mu)
,

\Pi_{N}(z,z\leq C_{\alpha}\exp(-\alpha\sqrt{N}\dist(z,\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta_N)\})).

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.1
, the spectrum of
H_{N}
below
\mu+\delta
only consists of eigenvalues.
Let

\rho:x\mapsto\operatorname{dist}(x,\{V\leq(1+\delta_{N})\mu\}).

Then, applying the first bound of Proposition
2.6
to any normalised eigenfunction
u
of
H_{N}

with eigenvalue
\lambda\leq\mu
. Decomposing the integral in two regions, for any
\alpha>0
,

\int_{\{V>\mu(1+\delta_{N})\}}\hskip-14.22636pte^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}\underbrace{((1-C_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{N})V-\lambda)}_{\geq\delta_{N}\mu/2\text{ if }N\geq N_{0}}|u|^{2}\leq\int_{\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta_{N})\}}\underbrace{(\lambda-(1-C_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{N})V)}_{\leq\mu\text{ if }N\geq N_{0}}|u|^{2}

using that by assumptions
C_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{N}\leq\delta_{N}/4\wedge 1
if
N\geq N_{\alpha}
(for some fixed
N_{\alpha}
).
Thus, if
N\geq N_{\alpha}
,

\int_{\{V>\mu(1+\delta_{N})\}}\hskip-28.45274pte^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}|u|^{2}\leq 2\delta_{N}^{-1}.

(2.10)

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{D}_{\delta_{N}}^{+}(\mu)
to ease notation.
By construction,
\rho\geq\delta_{N}
on
\mathcal{A}
, so that summing the inequality (
2.10
) over all the eigenfunctions in the range of
\mathbf{1}(P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu)
, since
\mathcal{A}^{\rm c}\subset\{V>\mu(1+\delta)\}
, we obtain with
\Lambda_{N}=\delta_{N}\varepsilon_{N}^{-1}
,

\displaystyle e^{\alpha\Lambda_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{1}(P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{V>\mu(1+\delta_{N})\}}\hskip-28.45274pte^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}\,\mathbf{1}(P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq\mathcal{N}(\mu)}\int_{\{V>\mu(1+\delta_{N})\}}\hskip-28.45274pte^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}|{\rm u}_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}\leq 2\delta_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{N}(\mu).

Then, using the trivial upper-bound (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Pi_{N}(x,x)\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}(x)\leq N\bm{\gamma}(\mathcal{A})+2\delta_{N}^{-1}e^{-\alpha\Lambda_{N}}\mathcal{N}(\mu).

Rearranging this inequality, since
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\delta_{N}^{-1}e^{-\alpha\Lambda_{N}}=0
by choosing the constant
\alpha\geq 1/2
, we conclude that

\mathcal{N}(\mu)\lesssim N\bm{\gamma}(\mathcal{D}_{\delta_{N}}^{+}(\mu)).

This proves (
2.9
); the same argument applies to any energy
\sigma\leq\mu+\delta
(this bound is trivial in case
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}^{+}(\sigma)
is not compact).

From here, we deduce (
2.7
) by a summation argument. Using the pointwise estimates (
2.5
), we have for
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

|\Pi_{N}(z,z)|={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{N}(\mu)}}|{\rm u}_{k}(z)|^{2}\leq N{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{N}(\mu)}}\|{\rm u}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{D}(z,\varepsilon_{N})}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(2.11)

If
z
is such that
\mathbb{D}(z,\varepsilon_{N})\subset\{V>\mu(1+\delta_{N})\}
,
applying (
2.10
), we obtain,

\max_{k\leq\mathcal{N}(\mu)}\|{\rm u}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{D}(z,\varepsilon_{N})}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\delta_{N}^{-1}e^{-\alpha\rho(z)/\varepsilon_{N}}

where we used the fact that, since
\rho
is
1
-Lipschitz,

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbb{D}(x,\varepsilon_{N})}e^{\alpha(\rho(x)-\rho(z))/\varepsilon_{N}}\lesssim 1.

Hence, plugging the upper-bound
\mathcal{N}(\mu)\lesssim N
, we conclude that for
z\notin\mathcal{D}_{\delta_{N}}^{+}(\mu)
;

|\Pi_{N}(z,z)|\lesssim N^{2}\delta_{N}^{-1}e^{-\alpha\rho(z)/\varepsilon_{N}}.

Adjusting the constants, this proves the claim.
∎

Our hypotheses on the potential (Assumption
1
) are rather mild
at infinity, but for some refined properties used in the next sections
(notably the calculus of Berezin–Toeplitz operators),
V
needs to be
smooth everywhere and/or growing at infinity.
A consequence of Proposition
2.7
is a

replacement principle
for
V
; the point is that the spectral
theory below energy
\mu
does not depend on
V
on
\{V\geq\mu+\delta\}
for
\delta>0
, up to an exponentially small error.

Lemma 2.8

(Replacement principle)
.

Let
V,V_{1}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be such that
\log(V)
and
\log(V_1)
are Lipschitz continuous.
Let
\mu>0
and
\delta>0
be such that
\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta)\}
is compact and
V=V_{1}
on
\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta)\}
.
Let
(\delta_{N})_{N\geq 1}
, non-increasing, with
N^{-\kappa}\leq\delta_{N}\leq\delta
for some
\kappa<\frac{1}{2}
.
Then, for any
\alpha>0
,

\|\mathbf{1}(P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu)\mathbf{1}(P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}\geq\mu+\delta_{N})\|_{\tr}\leq C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha\sqrt{N}}.

Proof.

Let
\rho=\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta_{N}/2)\})
and let
V_{2}:=V_{1}-V
.
Since
V_{2}
vanishes on
\{V>\mu(1+\delta)\}
, if
({\rm u},\lambda)
is a normalised eigenfunction of
P_{N}VP_{N}
with eigenvalue
\lambda\leq\mu(1+\delta/2)
, one can bound for
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

\bigg|\int P_{N}(x,z)V_{2}(x){\rm u}(x)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg|^{2}\leq\underbrace{\int_{\{V>\mu(1+\delta)\}}\hskip-14.22636pte^{\alpha\rho/\epsilon_{N}}|{\rm u}|^{2}}_{\lesssim 1\text{ by \eqref{eq:bd_efs_outside} with $\delta$ fixed}}\int|P_{N}(x,z)|^{2}e^{-\alpha\rho(x)/\varepsilon_{N}}V_{2}^{2}(x)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}x).

Then, since
{\rm u}
is an eigenfunction and using the reproducing property of
P_{N}
, one has

\|P_{N}V_{2}P_{N}{\rm u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int\bigg|\int P_{N}(x,z)V_{2}(x){\rm u}(x)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg|^{2}\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z)\lesssim N\int e^{-\alpha\rho(x)/\varepsilon_{N}}V_{2}^{2}(x)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}x),

As
V_{2}
vanishes on
\{V>\mu(1+\delta)\}
and is log-Lipschitz, the above integral is
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2c\alpha\delta_{N}/\varepsilon_{N}})
for some constant
c>0
depending only on the growth of
V,V_{1}
. This shows that for some constant
c_{\delta}>0
,

\|P_{N}(V_{1}-\lambda)P_{N}{\rm u}\|_{L^{2}}=\|P_{N}V_{2}P_{N}{\rm u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-c_{\delta}\alpha/\varepsilon_{N}}.

(2.12)

In particular,
{\rm u}
almost solves the eigenvalue equation for
P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}
.
Since this operator is self-adjoint, by spectral stability
(see Remark
2.9
, using the bound (
2.13
) and adjusting the constants), we conclude that for any eigenfunction
({\rm u},\lambda)
of
P_{N}VP_{N}
with
\lambda\leq\mu(1+\delta/2)
,

\|\mathbf{1}\{P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}>\mu+\delta_{N}\}{\rm u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim e^{-\alpha\sqrt{N}}.

By positivity,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{1}(P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu)\mathbf{1}(P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}\geq\mu+\delta_{N})\|_{\tr}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq\mathcal{N}(\mu)}\big\langle{\rm u}_{k}|\mathbf{1}\{P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}\geq\mu+\delta_{N}\}{\rm u}_{k}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\leq\mathcal{N}(\mu)}\|\mathbf{1}\{P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}\geq\mu+\delta_{N}\}{\rm u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{N}(\mu)e^{-\alpha\sqrt{N}}.

Since
\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta)\}
is compact, by (
2.9
), the eigenvalue number
\mathcal{N}(\mu)\lesssim N
. Adjusting constants (
\alpha>0
is arbitrary), this proves the claim.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

The main application of Proposition

2.8
will be the following.
Suppose that
V
satisfies
1
and let
V_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R})
such that
V_{1}=V
on
\{V\leq\mu(1+\delta)\}
and

V_{1}=\mu(1+2\delta)
on
\{V\geq\mu(1+2\delta)\}
. If
({\rm u},\lambda)
is an eigenpair of
P_{N}VP_{N}
with energy

\lambda\leq\mu+\delta/2
, by (
2.12
),
for any normalised eigenfunction
({\rm u}^{1}_{k},\lambda^{1}_{k})
of

P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}
, one has

(\lambda_{k}-\lambda)\langle{\rm u}^{1}_{k},{\rm u}\rangle=\langle P_{N}(V_{1}-V)P_{N}{\rm u}^{1}_{k},u\rangle\lesssim e^{-\alpha\sqrt{N}}.

In particular, either

|\lambda^{1}_{k}-\lambda|\lesssim e^{-c\sqrt{N}}
or the scalar product
is small:
|\langle{\rm u}^{1}_{k},{\rm u}\rangle|\lesssim Ne^{-c\sqrt{N}}
.

The point is that the eigenvalues of
\Pi_{N}V_{1}\Pi_{N}
in the range

[\mu(1-\delta),\mu(1+\delta)]
are simple and separated by about

N^{-1}
. This is a consequence of the results of

[
26
]
; one can also see it by conjugation to
a pseudodifferential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with symbol
V_{1}
and
semiclassical parameter
N^{-1}
(
[
67
]
,
Theorem 13.10).

Consequently, given such
({\rm u},\lambda)
with
\lambda\in[\mu(1-\delta),\mu(1+\delta)]
, there exists exactly one index
k
such
that
|\lambda^{1}_{k}-\lambda|\lesssim e^{-c\sqrt{N}}
and
\|{\rm u}^{1}_{k}-{\rm u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{2}e^{-c\sqrt{N}}
.

This implies that the eigenvalues of
P_{N}VP_{N}
(in

[0,\mu(1+\delta/2)]
) are also separated by
N^{-1}
and, reversing
the argument, we deduce that the eigenvalues of both operators are paired,
with exponentially close eigenfunctions. In other words,
there is a one-to-one mapping between the spectral data of
\Pi_{N}V\Pi_{N}
and
\Pi_{N}V_{1}\Pi_{N}
, in the range
[\mu(1-\delta),\mu(1+\delta)]
, which is exponentially close to identity.

Moreover, if
N^{-\kappa}\leq\delta_{N}\leq\delta
for some

\kappa<\frac{1}{2}
, then

\|\mathbf{1}(P_{N}V_{1}P_{N}\leq\mu(1+\delta_{N}/2)){\rm u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}={\textstyle\sum_{k}}|\langle{\rm u}_{k}^{1},{\rm u}\rangle|^{2}=1-\mathcal{O}(Ne^{-\alpha\sqrt{N}})

(2.13)

where the sum is over all eigenfunctions
({\rm u}_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{1})
with
\lambda_{k}^{1}\leq\mu(1+\delta_{N}/2)
, the main contribution comes from the eigenvalue paired with
{\rm u}
and the error controlled using that this projection has rank
\mathcal{O}(N)
.

Now we turn to the approximation of
\Pi_{N}
inside the bulk (for this proof, we need the
replacement principle
).

Proposition 2.10

Let
\mu>0

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta_{N}}^{-}}|P_{N}(x,z)-\Pi_{N}(x,z)|\leq C_{\alpha}{\rm exp}(-\alpha\delta_{N}\sqrt{N}).

{\rm c}>0

(
small depending on
\|g\|)
so that the operator
A_{N}^{\alpha}=e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}A_{N}e^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}
is bounded and

\|A_{N}^{\alpha}\|\lesssim 1
uniformly for
\alpha\in[0,c]
.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a self-adjoint operator such that
1+Q\geq{\rm c}>0
and
Q^{\alpha}=e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}Qe^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}
satisfies
\|Q-Q^{\alpha}\|\leq{\rm c}/2
. Then we have
e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}(1+Q)^{-1}e^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}=(1+Q^{\alpha})^{-1}

and, by the resolvent formula,

(1+Q^{\alpha})^{-1}=(1+Q)^{-1}+(1+Q^{\alpha})^{-1}(Q-Q^{\alpha})(1+Q)^{-1}

so that, by assumptions,

\|(1+Q^{\alpha})^{-1}\|\leq\frac{\|(1+Q)^{-1}\|}{1-\|Q-Q^{\alpha}\|\|(1+Q)^{-1}\|}\leq 2{\rm c}^{-1}.

Applying this to
Q=\Pi_{N}(e^{g}-1)\Pi_{N}
,

Q
is self-adjoint (since
e^{g}>0
on
\mathbb{C}
) with
1+Q>e^{-C}
where
C=\|g\|
and we have

\displaystyle e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}Qe^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}-Q

\displaystyle=\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}(e^{g}-1)\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}-\Pi_{N}(e^{g}-1)\Pi_{N}

\displaystyle=(\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}-\Pi_{N})(e^{g}-1)\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}+\Pi_{N}(e^{g}-1)(\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}-\Pi_{N}).

Then by Lemma
2.11
, if
\alpha
is sufficiently small,

\displaystyle\|e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}Qe^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}-Q\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{C}\|\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}-\Pi_{N}\|(\|\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}\|+1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha.

Hence choosing
\alpha
sufficiently small compared to
e^{-C}
, we obtain

\|e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}(1+\Pi_{N}(e^{g}-1)\Pi_{N})^{-1}e^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}\|\lesssim 1.

This implies that

A_{N}^{\alpha}=e^{g}\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}e^{\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}(1+\Pi_{N}(e^{g}-1)\Pi_{N})^{-1}e^{-\alpha\rho/\varepsilon_{N}}\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}

satisfies, by Lemma
2.11
again (the operator
\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}
is bounded independently of
N
),

\|A_{N}^{\alpha}\|\lesssim e^{C}\|\Pi_{N}^{\alpha}\|^{2}\lesssim 1.\qed

Finally we state a direct consequence of these decorrelation estimates that will be instrumental for our proof of the CLT.

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
\delta>0
be a small parameter and, for
j\in\{1,2\}
, let
\chi_{j}:\mathbb{C}\to[0,1]
be smooth functions such that

\mathbf{1}\{V\leq\mu-\delta\}\leq\chi_{2}\leq\mathbf{1}\{V\leq\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{1}\{|V-\mu|\leq 2\delta\}\leq\chi_{1}\leq\mathbf{1}\{|V-\mu|\leq 3\delta\},

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function, bounded, and let
{\rm f}_{j}:=f\chi_{j}
for
j\in\{1,2,3\}
where

\chi_{3}:=(\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}-1)\mathbf{1}\{V<\mu\}.

There is a
c_{\delta}>0
so that

\displaystyle\Upsilon(f;\Pi_{N})=\Upsilon({\rm f}_{1};\Pi_{N})+\Upsilon({\rm f}_{2};\Pi_{N})-\Upsilon({\rm f}_{3};\Pi_{N})+\mathcal{O}(e^{-c_{\delta}\sqrt{N}}).

By construction,
{\rm f}_{1},{\rm f}_{3}
are smooth, supported in the bulk
\{V\leq\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\}
, and
{\rm f}_{2}
is smooth, supported in a neighborhood of the boundary
\{|V-\mu|\leq 3\delta\}
.

Proof.

By construction, the following conditions hold;

\begin{cases}\mathbf{1}\{V\leq\mu-3\delta\}\leq\varkappa_{1}:=\mathbf{1}\{V<\mu\}(1-\chi_{1})\leq\mathbf{1}\{V\leq\mu-2\delta\}\\
\mathbf{1}\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\leq V-\mu\leq 2\delta\}\leq\varkappa_{2}:=\mathbf{1}\{V<\mu\}(1-\chi_{2})+\mathbf{1}\{V\geq\mu\}\chi_{1}\leq\mathbf{1}\{-\delta\leq V-\mu\leq 3\delta\}\\
\mathbf{1}\{\delta\leq\mu-V\leq 2\delta\}\leq\varkappa_{3}:=(\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}-1)\mathbf{1}\{V<\mu\}\leq\mathbf{1}\{\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\leq\mu-V\leq 3\delta\}.\end{cases}

Moreover,
\varkappa_{j}
are smooth for
j\in\{1,2,3\}
with
1-(\varkappa_{1}+\varkappa_{2}+\varkappa_{3})=\mathbf{1}\{V\geq\mu\}(1-\chi_{1})
.

Now, let
f_{j}:=f\varkappa_{j}
for
j\in\{1,2,3\}
so that
f=f_{0}+f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}
where
f_{0}
is supported in the
forbidden region

\{V>\mu+2\delta\}
. Moreover, if
\|f\|\leq C
for some constant, then
\|f_{j}\|\leq C
for
j\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
.

First, applying Lemma
2.15
, we obtain

\Upsilon(f;\Pi_{N})=\Upsilon(f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3};\Pi_{N})+\mathcal{O}(e^{-c_{\delta}\sqrt{N}}).

Second, applying Proposition
2.17
, we conclude that

\Upsilon(f;\Pi_{N})=\Upsilon(f_{1}+f_{3};\Pi_{N})+\Upsilon(f_{2}+f_{3};\Pi_{N})-\Upsilon(f_{3};\Pi_{N})+\mathcal{O}(e^{-c_{\delta}\sqrt{N}}).

By construction,
{\rm f}_{1}=f_{1}+f_{3}
,
{\rm f}_{2}=f_{2}+f_{3}
and
{\rm f}_{3}=f_{3}
, this concludes the proof.
∎

3.1  Replacement principle

The goal of this section is to prove the following proposition.
Note that we work in a general setup.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
A,B
be two bounded self-adjoint operators on a separable Hilbert space, and
\Pi
be a projection.
Here,
A,B,\Pi
depend on the small parameter
\varepsilon
, but
{\rm C}
is a constant.
Define for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{U}_{t}(A):=e^{it(\Pi A\Pi)}-\Pi e^{itA}\Pi.

Suppose that the following conditions hold;

1)\quad\|\Pi B(1-\Pi)\|_{\tr}\leq{\rm C}\qquad 2)\quad\|[A,B]\|\leq\varepsilon,\qquad 3)\quad\|\Pi(A-B)(1-\Pi)\|_{\rm tr}\leq\varepsilon\,,

and

4)\quad\|\Pi B(1-\Pi)(A-B)\|_{\rm tr}\vee\|(A-B)\Pi B(1-\Pi)\|_{\tr}\leq\varepsilon\,.

(3.3)

Then, for any

,

\|\mathcal{U}_{t}(A)-\mathcal{U}_{t}(B)\|_{\tr}\lesssim\varepsilon|t|(1+{\rm C}t^{2}).

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto a(t)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto r(t)
be smooth bounded operator-valued function. Assume that
a(t)
is self-adjoint and consider the solution of the problem;

\begin{cases}-iu^{\prime}(t)=a(t)u(t)+r(t),\quad t>0\\
u(0)=0.\end{cases}

(3.4)

The solution
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto u(t)
is smooth, bounded and it satisfies (Duhamel’s formula);

u(\tau)=i\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{i\int_{t}^{\tau}a}r(t)\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\text{for }\tau\geq 0.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{\bullet}
be an (operator) norm such that
\|AB\|_{\bullet}\leq\|A\|\|B\|_{\bullet}
.
Since
a
is self-adjoint (so that
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto e^{\char 16\relax a(t)}
is a unitary flow), we can bound for
\tau\geq 0

\|u(\tau)\|_{\bullet}\leq 2\int_{0}^{\tau}\|r(t)\|_{\bullet}\mathrm{d}t.

(3.5)

Observe that the same bound holds if one considers the equation
-iu^{\prime}(t)=u(t)a(t)+r(t)

instead of (
3.4
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A,B
be a bounded self-adjoint operator.
One has for any
t\geq 0
,

\|[e^{itA},B]\|_{\bullet}\leq 2\|[A,B]\|_{\bullet}\,t.

In addition, if
\|[A,B]\|\leq\varepsilon
and
\Delta
is a trace-class operator satisfying

\|\Delta\|_{\tr}\leq{\rm C}
and
\|\Delta(A-B)\|_{\rm tr}\leq\varepsilon

t\geq 0

\|\Delta(e^{itA}-e^{itB})\|_{\rm tr}\leq 2\varepsilon t(1+{\rm C}t).

Proof.

1) One has for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

-i\partial_{t}[e^{itA},B]=[e^{itA},B]A+e^{itA}[A,B].

This equation for
t\mapsto[e^{itA},B]
is of the type (
3.4
) with
a(t)=A
and
r(t)=e^{itA}[A,B]
, so that
\|r(t)\|_{\bullet}\leq\|[A,B]\|_{\bullet}
for

.
Then, by (
3.5
), for
\tau\geq 0
,

\|[e^{i\tau A},B]\|_{\bullet}\leq 2\|[A,B]\|_{\bullet}\tau.

2) Similarly, one has for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

-i\partial_{t}(e^{itA}-e^{itB})=(e^{itA}-e^{itB})B+e^{itA}(A-B)

so that

-i\partial_{t}\big(\Delta(e^{itA}-e^{itB})\big)=\Delta(e^{itA}-e^{itB})B+\underbrace{\Delta(A-B)e^{itA}-\Delta[e^{itA},B]}_{=r(t)}\,.

Consequently, by 1),

\|r(t)\|_{\tr}\leq\|\Delta(A-B)\|_{\rm tr}+\|\Delta\|_{\tr}\|[e^{itA},B]\|\leq\varepsilon(1+2{\rm C}t)\qquad\text{for }t\geq 0,

so that, by (
3.5
) again (with

), we conclude that for any
\tau\geq 0
,

\|\Delta(e^{i\tau A}-e^{i\tau B})\|_{\rm tr}\leq 2\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}(1+2{\rm C}t)\mathrm{d}t\leq 2\tau(1+{\rm C}\tau).\qed

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

Moreover, if

Proof.

1) By definition,

and for

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}\mathcal{U}_{t}(B)

Since

2) Let

be a bounded operator. By (

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}\big(\Pi\Delta\Pi\mathcal{U}_{t}(B)\big)

\displaystyle=\Pi\Delta\Pi B\Pi\mathcal{U}_{t}(B)-\Pi\Delta\Pi B(1-\Pi)e^{itB}\Pi

\displaystyle=\Pi B\big(\Pi\Delta\Pi\mathcal{U}_{t}(B)\big)+\underbrace{\Pi[\Delta\Pi,B\Pi]\Pi\mathcal{U}_{t}(B)-R_{0}e^{itB}\Pi}_{=r(t)}

If we decompose

The conditions (

\|r(t)\|_{\tr}\leq\varepsilon(\|\Pi\mathcal{U}_{t}(B)\|_{\tr}+1)\leq\varepsilon(1+2{\rm C}t)\qquad\text{for }t\geq 0,

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
, smooth and bounded and let
\vartheta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with compact support.
As

Proof.

We rely on the Helffer–Sjöstrand formula: given a test function

|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{\vartheta}(\lambda+i\eta)|=\mathcal{O}(|\eta|^{\infty})\qquad\qquad\widetilde{\vartheta}(\lambda)=\vartheta(\lambda).

That is,
\widetilde{\vartheta}
coincide with
\vartheta
on the real line, and it is also smooth and compactly supported (
\widetilde{\vartheta}
is called an almost holomorphic extension of
\vartheta

Then, by Cauchy’s formula, for

\vartheta(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\overline{\partial}\widetilde{\vartheta}(z)}{\lambda-z}\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z).

By spectral theory, this formula extends to bounded self-adjoint operators; if
\mathcal{B}:=P_{N}gP_{N}
with
g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
bounded, we have

\vartheta(\mathcal{B})=\frac{1}{2i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\partial}\widetilde{\vartheta}(z)(\mathcal{B}-z)^{-1}\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z)=\frac{1}{2i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\partial}\widetilde{\vartheta}(z)(P_{N}(f-z)P_{N})^{-1}\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z)

(3.17)

where
(P_{N}(f-z)^{-1}P_{N})^{-1}
is defined on
\mathcal{B}_{N}
.
Then, for

, our task is to understand the operator
(P_{N}(f-z)P_{N})^{-1}
up to
\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
, with an explicit control in
\imaginary(z)
.
To do so, we use the calculus for Berezin–Toeplitz quantization; for smooth functions

\displaystyle P_{N}aP_{N}bP_{N}

\displaystyle=P_{N}abP_{N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1}\|a\|_{C^{1}}\|b\|_{C^{1}}),

\displaystyle P_{N}aP_{N}bP_{N}

\displaystyle=P_{N}(ab-N^{-1}\partial a\overline{\partial}b)P_{N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2}\|a\|_{C^{2}}\|b\|_{C^{2}}),

2.3

For the second bound, by a Taylor expansion, the commutator satisfies

\displaystyle:(x,z)

\displaystyle\mapsto\partial a(x)(z-x)P_{N}(x,z)+\overline{\partial}a(x)(\overline{z-x})P_{N}(x,z)+O(N^{-1}\|a\|_{C^{2}})

\displaystyle[P_{N},b]:(z,w)

\displaystyle\mapsto P_{N}(z,w)(w-z)\partial b(w)+P_{N}(z,w)(\overline{w-z})\overline{\partial}b(w)+O(N^{-1}\|b\|_{C^{2}})

Then,

\displaystyle P_{N}[P_{N},a][P_{N},b]P_{N}

\displaystyle=-\int P_{N}(\cdot,x)\partial a(x)P_{N}(x,z)(z-x)(\overline{z-w})P_{N}(z,w)\overline{\partial}b(w)P_{N}(w,\cdot)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}x)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}w)

(3.19)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-\int P_{N}(\cdot,x)\overline{\partial}a(x)P_{N}(x,z)(\overline{z-x})(z-w)P_{N}(z,w)\partial b(w)P_{N}(w,\cdot)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}x)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}z)\bm{\gamma}(\mathrm{d}w)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2}\|a\|_{C^{2}}\|b\|_{C^{2}}),

where the other two terms vanish exactly using the reproducing property of
P_{N}
.
For the error term, there are three contributions, obviously a term
\mathcal{O}(N^{-2}\|a\|_{C^{2}}\|b\|_{C^{2}})
and terms of the form

\|(x,z)\mapsto\partial a(x)(z-x)P_{N}(x,z)\|\|O(N^{-1}\|b\|_{C^{2}})=O(N^{-3/2}\|a\|_{C^{1}}\|b\|_{C^{2}}).

by Lemma
2.3
.

Using that
P_{N}(x,z)(x-z)P_{N}(z,w)=N^{-1}\overline{\partial_{z}}\{P_{N}(x,z)P_{N}(z,w)\}

\displaystyle\eqref{commmain}

\displaystyle=N^{-1}\int P_{N}(\cdot,x)\partial a(x)\overline{\partial_{z}}\{P_{N}(x,z)P_{N}(z,w)\}(\overline{w-z})\overline{\partial}b(w)P_{N}(w,\cdot)

\displaystyle=N^{-1}\int P_{N}(\cdot,x)\partial a(x)P_{N}(x,w)\overline{\partial}b(w)P_{N}(w,\cdot)

\displaystyle=N^{-1}P_{N}\partial aP_{N}\overline{\partial}bP_{N}

\displaystyle=N^{-1}P_{N}\partial a\overline{\partial}bP_{N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2}\|a\|_{C^{2}}\|b\|_{C^{2}}).

using the first bound (
3.18
) at the last step.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\eqref{commerr}

\displaystyle=N^{-2}\int\overline{\partial}a(x)\partial_{x}\{P_{N}(\cdot,x)P_{N}(x,z)\}\overline{\partial_{w}}\{P_{N}(z,w)P_{N}(w,\cdot)\}\partial b(w)

So (

.

Let
f:\mathfrak{X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a map such that the operator-valued function
\lambda\in[0,1]\mapsto{\rm Q}(e^{\lambda f}-1){\rm Q}
is trace-class and
1+{\rm Q}(e^{\lambda f}-1){\rm Q}>0
for

\lambda\in[0,1]
.
Then

\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}|\Upsilon(\lambda f;{\rm Q})-\lambda^{2}\Sigma/2|\leq\eta\Sigma(1+\eta\Sigma),\qquad\quad\Sigma=\Sigma(f;{\rm Q})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\tr([f,\Q]^2)

and

\eta=\eta(f;{\rm Q})=\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\|(1-{\rm Q})e^{\lambda f}{\rm Q}(1+{\rm Q}(e^{\lambda f}-1){\rm Q})^{-1}\|
.

Note that in Proposition
4.1
,
\Sigma(f;{\rm Q})\geq 0
since it corresponds (up to a factor
\frac{1}{2}
) to the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
[f,{\rm Q}]
and the bound is meaningful only if
\Sigma(f;{\rm Q})<\infty
.
Moreover, by assumption, there is a constant
\delta>0
so that
1+A_{\lambda}>\delta
for

\lambda\in[0,1]
so that

\eta(f;{\rm Q})\leq\delta^{-1}\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\|(1-{\rm Q})e^{\lambda f}{\rm Q}\|
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we denote for
\lambda\geq 0
,

\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}:=e^{\lambda f},\qquad\qquad R_{\lambda}:=(1+{\rm Q}(\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}-1){\rm Q})^{-1},\qquad\qquad B_{\lambda}:={\rm Q}g(1-{\rm Q})f_{\lambda}{\rm Q}.

By assumptions,
R_{\lambda}
are bounded operators and we claim that

{\rm Q}\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}={\rm Q}+(1-{\rm Q})\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}.

(4.1)

This follows by writing

\displaystyle{\rm Q}\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}(1+{\rm Q}(\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}-1){\rm Q})^{-1}

\displaystyle=({\rm Q}+{\rm Q}(\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}-1){\rm Q})(1+{\rm Q}(\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}-1){\rm Q})^{-1}

\displaystyle=1-(1-{\rm Q})(1+{\rm Q}(\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}-1){\rm Q})^{-1}

and multiplying both sides by
{\rm Q}
.
Then, we compute using (
4.1
),

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\log\det(1+\Q(\g_\lambda-1)\Q)

\displaystyle=\tr\big(\Q f \g_\lambda\Q R_\lambda\big)

\displaystyle=\tr\big(\Q f\Q\big)+\tr\big(B_\lambda R_\lambda\big).

Equivalently (since
B_{0}=0
because
\mathrm{g}_{0}=1
),

\partial_{\lambda}\Upsilon(\lambda f;{\rm Q})=\tr\big(B_\lambda R_\lambda\big),\qquad\qquad\partial_{\lambda}\Upsilon(\lambda f;{\rm Q})|_{\lambda=0}=0.

(4.2)

We also compute

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}B_{\lambda}

\displaystyle={\rm Q}f(1-{\rm Q})f\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=D{\rm Q}\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}+D(1-{\rm Q})\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q},\qquad D:={\rm Q}f(1-{\rm Q})f,

and using (
4.1
),

\displaystyle-\partial_{\lambda}R_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=R_{\lambda}{\rm Q}f\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}=R_{\lambda}{\rm Q}f{\rm Q}+R_{\lambda}{\rm Q}f(1-{\rm Q})\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=R_{\lambda}{\rm Q}f{\rm Q}+R_{\lambda}B_{\lambda}R_{\lambda}.

Then, using (
4.3
)–(
4.4
) and (
4.1
) again, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\tr(B_\lambda R_\lambda)

\displaystyle=\tr(D \Q\g_\lambda\Q R_\lambda)+\tr(D(1-\Q) \g_\lambda\Q R_\lambda)-\tr(B_\lambda\partial_\lambda R_\lambda)

\displaystyle=\tr(D \Q)+\tr(D(1-\Q)\g_\lambda\Q R_\lambda)-\tr(B_\lambda R_\lambda\Q f \Q)-\tr(B_\lambda R_\lambda B_\lambda R_\lambda)

\displaystyle=\tr(D)+\tr(\Q(D-D^*)(1-\Q)\g_\lambda\Q R_\lambda)-\tr((B_\lambda R_\lambda)^2)

using the cyclic property of
\tr
and rewriting the third term

\tr(B_\lambda R_\lambda\Q f\Q)=\tr(\Q f\Q f(1-\Q)\g_\lambda\Q R_\lambda)=\tr(\Q D^*(1-\Q)\g_\lambda\Q R_\lambda)

To control the above terms, observe that since
{\rm Q}
is a projection,

\displaystyle\|B_{\lambda}R_{\lambda}\|_{\rm H}

\displaystyle=\|{\rm Q}g(1-{\rm Q})\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}\|_{\rm H}\leq\|(1-{\rm Q})\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}\|\cdot\|{\rm Q}g(1-{\rm Q})\|_{\rm H}

\displaystyle\|{\rm Q}g(1-{\rm Q})\|_{\rm H}

\displaystyle=\tr(\Q g(1-\Q)g\Q)=\tr(D)

so that, with
\eta=\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\|(1-{\rm Q})\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}\|
,

\displaystyle|\tr((B_\lambda R_\lambda)^2)|

\displaystyle\leq\|B_{\lambda}R_{\lambda}\|_{\rm H}^{2}\leq\tr(D)^{2}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\|{\rm Q}D(1-{\rm Q})\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}\|_{\tr}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-{\rm Q})\mathrm{g}_{\lambda}{\rm Q}R_{\lambda}\|\cdot\|D\|_{\tr}=\tr(D)\eta

and similarly replacing
D
by
D^{*}
.

Altogether, this implies that

|\partial_{\lambda}\tr(B_\lambda R_\lambda)-\tr(D)|\leq\eta\tr(D)(2+\eta\tr(D)).

Then, going back to (
4.2
), we conclude that for
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

|\partial_{\lambda}^{2}\Upsilon(\lambda f;{\rm Q})-\tr(D)|\leq\eta\tr(D)(2+\eta\tr(D)).

Thus, integrated this formula twice and using that

\displaystyle\tr(-[f,\Q]^2)

\displaystyle=\tr(f\Q f + \Q f^2\Q- f\Q f\Q-\Q f\Q f)=2(\tr(\Q f^2 \Q)-\tr(\Q f\Q f \Q))

\displaystyle\tr(B)

\displaystyle=\tr(\Q f^2 \Q)-\tr(\Q f\Q f \Q)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\tr([f,\Q]^2)=\Sigma

this completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

For
\hbar\in(0,1]
, let
{\rm Q}_{\hbar}
be a family of self-adjoint locally trace-class projections on
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X}_{\hbar})
and let
\mathbf{X}_{\hbar}
denote the associated determinantal point process.
Let
f_{\hbar}:\mathfrak{X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a family of functions, with compact support, such that

1+{\rm Q}_{\hbar}(e^{\lambda f_{\hbar}}-1){\rm Q}_{\hbar}\geq\delta>0
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
and as
\hbar\to 0
,

\Sigma(f_{\hbar};{\rm Q}_{\hbar})\to\sigma^{2},\qquad\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\|[e^{\lambda f},{\rm Q}]\|\to 0.

Then
\Upsilon(f_{\hbar};{\rm Q}_{\hbar})=\Sigma/2+\underset{\hbar\to 0}{o(1)}
and, in distribution
(\mathcal{G}
denotes the standard Gaussian distribution on
\mathbb{R})
as
\hbar\to 0
,

\mathbf{X}_{\hbar}(g_{\hbar})-\mathbb{E}\mathbf{X}_{\hbar}(g_{\hbar})\to\sigma\mathcal{G}

Proof.

By assumptions, the operators
{\rm Q}_{\hbar}(e^{\lambda f_{\hbar}}-1){\rm Q}_{\hbar}
are trace-class for
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\hbar\in(0,1]
and

\eta(f_{\hbar};{\rm Q}_{\hbar})=\underset{\hbar\to 0}{o(1)}
.
So, applying Proposition
4.1
, we obtain

\sup_{\lambda\in[-1,1]}|\Upsilon(\lambda f_{\hbar};{\rm Q}_{\hbar})-\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}/2|=\underset{\hbar\to 0}{o(1)}.

Equivalently, the Laplace transform
\mathbb{E}\exp\big(\lambda\X_\hbar(f_\hbar) \big)=\exp\big(\lambda\E\X_\hbar(f_\hbar) + \lambda^2\sigma^2/2 + \underset{\hbar\to 0}{o(1)} \big)
uniformly for
\lambda\in[-1,1]
.
This yields the central limit theorem.
∎

4.2  CLT for Berezin–Toeplitz ensembles.

We can directly apply the above results to the Ginibre and other Berezin–Toeplitz ensembles inside the bulk. In these cases, the semi-classical parameter is
\hbar=N^{-1}
.
Proposition
4.3
is a classical result, but our proof includes the convergence of the Laplace transform and it applies at arbitrary mesoscopic scales.
Recall that the
\infty
-Ginibre point process has correlation kernel
P_{N}
.

Proposition 4.3

(Mesoscopic CLT for
\infty
-Ginibre ensemble)
.

Let
f\in C^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{C})
be a fixed function and let
g=f(\cdot\epsilon_{N}^{-1})
where
\epsilon_{N}\leq 1
is any non-increasing function with
N\epsilon_{N}^{2}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
.
Then
\Upsilon(g,P_{N})\to\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)/2
as
N\to\infty
.
In particular, if
\mathbf{X}_{N}
denotes
\infty
-Ginibre point process; as
N\to\infty

\mathbf{X}_{N}(g)-N\int g\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}\,\Longrightarrow\,\sqrt{\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)}\,\mathcal{G}.

This generalizes (
1.12
) on mesoscopic scales.

Proof.

Let
C=\|f\|
.
One can directly apply Corollary
4.2
. indeed, according to Lemma
2.3
, for
\lambda\in[-1,1]
,

\|[e^{\lambda g},P_{N}]\|\lesssim N^{-1/2}e^{C}\|g\|_{C^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{C^{1}}/\sqrt{N\epsilon_{N}^{2}}

so that
\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\|[e^{\lambda g},P_{N}]\|\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.
Moreover, since
\|[P_{N},g]\|_{\rm H}^{2}\to\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)
as
N\to\infty
, the limit variance is
\sigma^{2}=\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)
.
Observe that the denotes
\infty
-Ginibre point process is stationary with density
P_{N}(z,z)=N
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
so that we can replace
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{N}(g)]=N\bm{\gamma}(g)
in the CLT.
∎

Based on the approximations from Proposition
2.5
, we can generalize the previous proof to Berezin–Toeplitz ensembles for test functions supported inside the bulk.

Theorem 4.4

(Mesoscopic CLT for Berezin–Toeplitz ensembles)
.

Let
V:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the Assumption
1
and
\Pi_{N}=\mathbf{1}\{P_{N}VP_{N}\leq\mu\}
.
Let
f\in C^{1}_{0}(\{V<\mu\})
be a fixed function and let
g=f(\cdot\epsilon_{N}^{-1})
where
\epsilon_{N}\leq 1
is any non-increasing function with
N\epsilon_{N}^{2}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
.
Then
\Upsilon(g,\Pi_{N})\to\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)/2
as
N\to\infty
.
In particular, if
\mathbf{X}_{N}
denotes the determinantal point process associated with the projection
\Pi_{N}
; as
N\to\infty

\mathbf{X}_{N}(g)-N\int g\mathrm{d}\bm{\gamma}\,\Longrightarrow\,\sqrt{\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)}\,\mathcal{G}.

Proof.

Iin operator norm;

\|[e^{\lambda g},\Pi_{N}]\|\leq\|[e^{\lambda g},P_{N}]\|+2\|(e^{\lambda g}-1)\underbrace{(P_{N}-\Pi_{N})}_{A_{N}}\|

and
\|(e^{\lambda g}-1)A_{N}\|\leq\|(e^{\lambda g}-1)A_{N}\|_{\rm H}
. For the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, by (
2.8
),

\|(e^{\lambda g}-1)(P_{N}-\Pi_{N})\|_{\rm H}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\{V\leq\mu-2\delta\}^{2}}\hskip-28.45274pt|A(x,z)|^{2}\gamma(\mathrm{d}x)\gamma(\mathrm{d}z)\lesssim e^{-c_{\delta}\sqrt{N}}

where the implied constant depends only on
\|g\|=\|f\|
and
c_{\delta}>0
is also independent of
N
.
Consequently, as in the proof of Proposition
4.3
, it holds unformly for
\lambda\in[-1,1]
,

\displaystyle\|[e^{\lambda g},\Pi_{N}]\|\lesssim 1/\sqrt{N\epsilon_{N}^{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|[\Pi_{N},g]\|_{\rm H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|[P_{N},g]\|_{\rm H}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-c_{\delta}\sqrt{N}/2})

\displaystyle\to\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(f)\quad\text{ as $N\to\infty$}.

This proves the claims (we can also replace
\Pi_{N}
by
P_{N}
when computing
\mathbb{E}\mathbf{X}_{N}(g)
up to exponentially small errors).
∎

5
Conclusion: Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
, bounded, be
C^{\infty}
in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{D}=\{V<\mu\}
.
The first step is to apply the decorrelation estimate from Corollary
2.19
;

\displaystyle\Upsilon(f;\Pi_{N})=\Upsilon({\rm f}_{1};\Pi_{N})+\Upsilon({\rm f}_{2};\Pi_{N})-\Upsilon({\rm f}_{3};\Pi_{N})+\underset{N\to\infty}{o(1)},\qquad{\rm f}_{j}:=f\chi_{j}\text{ for $j\in\{1,2,3\}$}.

The cutoffs
\chi_{j}
are smooth,
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{3}
are supported in the bulk
\{V\leq\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\}
, while
\chi_{2}
is supported in a neighborhood of the boundary
\{|V-\mu|\leq 3\delta\}
.
in this case
{\rm f}_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{C})
.

Then, by Proposition
4.4
(with
\epsilon_{N}=1
), one has for
j\in\{1,3\}
as
N\to\infty
,

\Upsilon({\rm f}_{j};\Pi_{N})\to\tfrac{1}{2}\Sigma^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}({\rm f}_{j})=\tfrac{1}{2}\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{j}).

Moreover, since
{\rm f}_{2}
is supported in a neighborhood of the
boundary, we have shown in Section
3
that as
N\to\infty
,

\Upsilon({\rm f}_{2},\Pi_{N})\to\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{2})+\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{2}).

Altogether, this implies that as
N\to\infty

\Upsilon(f;\Pi_{N})\to\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{2})+\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{1})+\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{2})-\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{3}).

Obviously
\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}(f)=\Sigma^{1}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{2})
and it remains to check that

\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(f)=\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{1})+\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{2})-\Sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}({\rm f}_{3}).

(5.1)

In the droplet
\mathcal{D}
,
{\rm f}_{3}={\rm f}_{1}+{\rm f}_{2}-f
(see the proof of Corollary
2.19
), then

|\nabla{\rm f}_{3}|^{2}=|\nabla{\rm f}_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla{\rm f}_{2}|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2}-2\nabla f\nabla{\rm f}_{1}-2\nabla f\nabla{\rm f}_{2}+2\nabla{\rm f}_{1}\nabla{\rm f}_{2}

Rearranging and writing
{\rm f}_{j}=f\chi_{j}
, we obtain on
\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle|\nabla{\rm f}_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla{\rm f}_{2}|^{2}-|\nabla{\rm f}_{3}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\nabla f\nabla{\rm f}_{1}+2\nabla f\nabla{\rm f}_{2}-2\nabla{\rm f}_{1}\nabla{\rm f}_{2}-|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\nabla f\nabla\chi_{1}+2\nabla f\nabla\chi_{2}-2\nabla{\rm f}_{1}\nabla{\rm f}_{2}+3|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla f|^{2}

where we used that

\nabla{\rm f}_{1}\nabla{\rm f}_{2}=|\nabla f|^{2}+\nabla\chi_{1}\nabla f+\nabla f\nabla\chi_{2}

since
\nabla\chi_{1}\nabla\chi_{2}=0
.
Integrating this formula over
\mathcal{D}
, this proves
(
5.1
).

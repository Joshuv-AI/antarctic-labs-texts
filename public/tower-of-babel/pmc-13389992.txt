Multi-pathway targeting by natural products in chronic respiratory diseases: Opportunities, challenges, and future perspectives
Authors: Siqi Lin, Lili Li, Shiqing Zhuo, Liang Yu, Yu Huang, Xiaofei Chen, Huihui Ti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChronic respiratory diseases (CRDs) are multifactorial disorders driven by dysregulated inflammation, oxidative stress, and tissue remodeling. Conventional single-target therapies are insufficient to halt disease progression, underscoring the demand for alternative therapeutic approaches. Natural products, including bioactive compounds, herbal extracts, and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) formulas, are characterized by their inherent multi-target properties and represent a promising intervention strategy for CRDs. This perspective emphasizes natural products as network modulators that can simultaneously regulate multiple signaling pathways. The integration of systems biology, network pharmacology, and artificial intelligence (AI) is crucial for deciphering their polypharmacological mechanisms. The clinical translation of natural products is anticipated to benefit from the implementation of several key strategies, including multi-omics integration, AI, targeted delivery, and blockchain-based quality control. By shifting from a reductionist to a network-centric view, natural products can fulfill their potential as next-generation therapeutics for CRDs.Keywords: chronic respiratory diseases, clinical translation, multi-pathway targeting, natural products, network modulation, traditional Chinese medicine

Chronic respiratory diseases (CRDs), mainly composed of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), and asthma, are driven by persistent inflammation, immune dysregulation, and tissue remodeling processes orchestrated by the interplay of multiple dysregulated signaling pathways (Labaki & Han, 2020). Conventional single-target therapies only offer symptomatic relief and often fail to halt the progression of the disease. In contrast, natural products including bioactive compounds, herbal extracts, and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) formulas offer therapeutic advantages by simultaneously modulating multiple pathways, positioning them as promising multi-pathway targeting agents for CRDs (Zhu et al., 2022). However, translating this potential into clinical reality requires addressing key challenges in mechanistic complexity, bioavailability, quality control, and clinical evidence. This perspective explores the opportunities and future directions for advancing natural products as next-generation multi-target therapeutics for CRDs.

Multi-targeting therapeutic approaches for multifactorial CRDs

CRDs are network diseases driven by multiple interconnected pathological processes that collectively contribute to disease initiation and progression. COPD, IPF, and asthma exhibit distinct yet overlapping mechanistic features. COPD is characterized by inflammatory cell infiltration, protease/antiprotease imbalance, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and alveolar epithelial cell apoptosis. These factors lead to airway remodeling and emphysema. IPF involves recurrent alveolar epithelial cells (AECs) injury, aberrant fibroblast activation, excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, and impaired lung repair mechanisms. Collectively, these abnormalities result in progressive fibrosis and loss of lung function. Asthma features T helper 2 (Th2)/Th17-mediated immune responses, airway hyperresponsiveness, goblet cell hyperplasia, and structural airway remodeling driven by repeated cycles of inflammation and repair. Despite their distinct clinical phenotypes, these diseases share common pathogenic nodes including nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB)-driven inflammation, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β)-mediated fibrosis, and oxidative stress-induced cellular damage that interact across multiple signaling networks (Li et al., 2025). The intricate interplay of these mechanisms forms a complex pathogenic network, underscoring the inherent limitations of single-target interventions that address only isolated components of the disease cascade. This network-centric understanding establishes a strong rationale for multi-target therapeutic strategies, which can simultaneously modulate multiple disease nodes to achieve synergistic effects. It also provides a conceptual foundation for exploring how natural products, which possess an intrinsic ability for multi-pathway regulation, exert integrated therapeutic efficacy across the CRDs spectrum.

Natural products: Advantages of TCM and multi-pathway modulation

Natural products, including bioactive compounds, herbal extracts, and TCM formulas, exert therapeutic effects on CRDs by simultaneously modulating multiple interconnected signaling pathways. Representative compounds target different yet complementary nodes within the CRDs signaling network, achieving a broad range of therapeutic effects against inflammation, oxidative stress and tissue remodeling in COPD, IPF, and asthma. Astragaloside IV has been shown to inhibit the Janus kinase 3/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (JAK3/STAT3)-NF-κB (Zhou, Zhuo, & Cai, 2018), the high mobility group box 1/receptor for advanced glycation end products (HMGB1/RAGE)-NF-κB (Zhang et al., 2024), and the rat sarcoma/rapidly accelerated fibrosarcoma/forkhead box O (RAS/RAF/FoxO) signaling pathways (Zhang et al., 2023). It can suppress inflammation, oxidative stress, EMT, fibroblast activation, and pyroptosis in airway smooth muscle cells. Berberine reduces inflammation, collagen deposition, and fibrotic markers while restoring autophagy by blocking the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 (Wang et al., 2019), mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) (Yang et al., 2023), and focal adhesion kinase (FAK)-dependent phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B/mammalian target of rapamycin (PI3K/AKT-mTOR) signaling pathways (Chitra, Saiprasad, Manikandan, & Sudhandiran, 2015). Tanshinone IIA activates the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2/glutathione (Nrf2/GSH) and Nrf2/heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) antioxidant signaling pathways while inhibiting the reactive oxygen species/protein kinase C delta (ROS/PKCδ)-Smad3 (An et al., 2019), p38/NF-κB, AKT /hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) (Yu et al., 2026), and poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase-1 (PARP-1)-NF-κB-NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) pathways (Zhang et al., 2025). Consequently, it suppresses myofibroblast activation, Th2 inflammation, airway remodeling, and M1 macrophage polarization. Sinomenine inhibits TGF-β1/Smad3, PI3K/AKT, NF-κB (Yao et al., 2024), and Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)-NLRP3-TGF-β signaling (Liu et al., 2024). Through these mechanisms, it attenuates fibroblast activation, EMT, ECM deposition, inflammation, and oxidative stress. The capacity to target multiple pathways is an inherent feature of these natural products, aligning with the TCM concept of comprehensive regulation. This provides a pharmacological foundation for their therapeutic effectiveness across various CRDs subtypes.

Single herbs exemplify the integrated efficacy of multi-pathway modulation at a more complex level. Cordyceps sinensis (Berk.) Sacc. modulates ROS/PI3K/AKT/mTOR (Liu et al., 2016), TGF-β1/Smad (Wang et al., 2007), and extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2)-NF-κB signaling pathways (Chiou & Lin, 2012), concurrently addressing inflammation, oxidative stress, and tissue remodeling. Panax ginseng C. A. Mey. inhibits MAPK/ERK1/2 (Shergis et al., 2014), NF-κB, and TLR4-P2X7 receptor (P2X7r)/NLRP3 pathways (Dou et al., 2025), exerting synergistic anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effects. Ginkgo biloba L. (Qian et al., 2023, Tao et al., 2024), Astragalus membranaceus (Fisch.) Bunge (Jin et al., 2013, Wang et al., 2019), and Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge (Zeng et al., 2024) similarly exhibit coordinated regulation of multiple pathways, targeting distinct yet overlapping nodes within the CRDs network. The multi-target capacity inherent to single herbs reflects the TCM principle of holistic regulation, providing a pharmacological basis for their therapeutic versatility across different CRDs subtypes.

TCM formulas represent a superior level of integrated multi-pathway modulation. Classical formulations such as Yupingfeng San, Suhuang Zhike Capsule, and Bufei Huoxue Capsule concurrently target NF-κB-mediated inflammation (Li et al., 2025), TGF-β/Smad-driven fibrosis (Gao et al., 2011), NLRP3 inflammasome activation (Liu et al., 2017), and Nrf2-dependent antioxidant defenses (Jiang et al., 2023). By engaging multiple pathways simultaneously, these formulas address the complex pathological network of CRDs in a synergistic manner, achieving effects that exceed the sum of their individual components (Zhang, Yi, Li, & Wang, 2025). This multi-pathway modulation, rooted in TCM theory and validated by modern pharmacological studies, is responsible for the integrated efficacy of natural products. It sets natural products, whether single compounds, single herbs, or complex formulas, apart from single-target synthetic drugs. The distinction highlights the unique advantages of TCM in treating multifactorial diseases like CRDs.

Opportunities for natural products in CRDs: Privileged scaffolds, multi-target modulation, safety and low resistance

Privileged scaffolds as structural templates for drug optimization

The unique chemical skeletons of natural products enable rational drug design through structural modification while preserving core pharmacological activities. The quaternization of tropane alkaloids yielded ipratropium bromide with enhanced airway selectivity (Pakes et al., 1980), and the conversion of theophylline to aminophylline increased compound solubility without compromising its multi-pathway activity (Soares et al., 2009). These examples illustrate how strategic modification of natural scaffolds can overcome pharmaceutical limitations, such as poor solubility and low bioavailability.

Inherent multi-target modulation capacity for synergistic therapeutic effects

Unlike single-target drugs, natural products simultaneously engage multiple disease-relevant pathways. Berberine inhibits both TGF-β-mediated fibrosis and MAPK-driven inflammation (De Rubis et al., 2023, Xu et al., 2015). C. sinensis coordinately regulates Nrf2 antioxidant defense, PI3K/AKT survival signaling, and NLRP3 inflammasome activation across CRDs subtypes (Pu et al., 2023, Zang et al., 2025, Zhou et al., 2025). Bufei Huoxue Capsule similarly modulates multiple nodes including Nrf2, PI3K/AKT, and NLRP3 pathways, achieving synergistic anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-fibrotic effects (He et al., 2024, Li et al., 2023, Li et al., 2024).

Favorable safety and low resistance profiles suitable for chronic management

Multi-target synergism inherently delays the development of resistance and ensures long-term safety. Curcumin maintains its efficacy with excellent tolerability during extended use (Gupta, Patchva, & Aggarwal, 2013). Quercetin safely supports the regeneration of airway epithelial cells without causing any adverse effects (McCluskey et al., 2024). These safety profiles make natural products particularly valuable for managing chronic CRDs, where long-term safety is of utmost importance.

Collectively, privileged scaffolds, multi-target modulation, safety and low resistance showcase natural products as promising candidates for multi-pathway targeting in CRDs therapy.

Challenges for natural products in CRDs: Low bioavailability, mechanistic complexity, quality variability, and limited clinical evidence

Low bioavailability limits clinical efficacy

Many bioactive compounds exhibit poor aqueous solubility and are susceptible to gastrointestinal degradation, extensive first-pass metabolism, and active efflux by intestinal transporters. These pharmacokinetic barriers compromise the therapeutic concentrations of orally administered natural products. The bioavailability of quercetin is less than 5% (Cai, Fang, Dou, Yu, & Zhai, 2013). Andrographolide and menthol face a similar dilemma, which underscores the need for advanced delivery strategies.

Mechanistic complexity obscures drug design and efficacy estimation

The multi-target nature of natural products, although advantageous, makes precise mechanism delineation exceedingly difficult. The mechanism by which compounds such as berberine and tanshinone IIA integrate signaling across the NF-κB, TGF-β, Nrf2, and JAK-STAT pathways remains unclear. The synergistic or antagonistic interactions among the components of complex herbal formulations remain poorly studied. The lack of understanding complicates the rational design of drugs and the prediction of clinical outcomes.

Quality variability undermines reproducibility

Batch-to-batch inconsistencies arising from variations in plant sourcing, cultivation, and extraction methods plague the development of natural product derived drugs (Pan et al., 2020). For multi-herb TCM formulas, these challenges are amplified by compositional complexity, variable processing methods, and the absence of standardized quality markers. Isomers, heavy metals, and pesticide residues further complicate the establishment of reliable quality standards.

Limited clinical evidence hampers translation

The efficacy of most natural products lacks robust evidence from large-scale, well-designed human trials. Preclinical promise rarely translates into definitive efficacy data, optimal dosing regimens, or long-term safety profiles in patients. This evidence deficit limits clinical acceptance and integration into standard treatment paradigms.

Integrating strategies to overcome translational challenges in natural product development

To address the four key challenges outlined above, namely low bioavailability, mechanistic complexity, quality variability, and limited clinical evidence, the following integrated strategies are proposed. Each strategy directly targets a specific translational bottleneck, forming a coherent framework for advancing natural products toward clinical application. By aligning each strategy with a specific translational challenge, this integrated framework provides a systematic pathway for advancing natural products as next-generation therapeutics for CRDs.

Overcoming low bioavailability through targeted delivery and chemical modification

Nanocarrier-based strategies, including liposomes, exosomes, and biomimetic materials, substantially enhance the stability, bioavailability, and targeting efficiency of natural compounds. The chemical modification of natural scaffolds, such as pirfenidone derived from flavonoid skeletons (Noble et al., 2011), improves its pharmacokinetics and therapeutic activity while reducing off-target effects. These approaches collectively overcome the absorption and metabolic barriers that compromise the clinical performance of natural products.

Addressing mechanistic complexity through multi-omics and artificial intelligence (AI)

Systems biology, network pharmacology, and AI enable comprehensive analysis of complex multi-target mechanisms (Mullowney et al., 2023). By constructing precise drug-target-pathway networks, these approaches can reveal how natural compounds exert synergistic effects. Single-cell sequencing, phosphorylated proteomics, and deep learning will further decipher multi-modulation logic, providing theoretical foundations for rational drug design and overcoming the “black box” nature of natural product mechanisms.

Ensuring quality consistency through blockchain-based quality control

Modern quality systems that integrate multi-omics fingerprinting with blockchain traceability ensure product consistency and safety. Metabolomic profiling using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) establishes characteristic fingerprint databases that distinguish samples by origin and processing methods. Blockchain platforms like HerBChain (https://herbchain.hk/) allow for the end-to-end tracking of raw material cultivation, processing parameters, and testing data, which ensures reproducible manufacturing (Yik, Wong, Wong, & Shaw, 2021).

Generating robust clinical evidence through innovative clinical trial designs

Master protocols accelerate drug development by enabling the simultaneous evaluation of multiple therapies within a single framework. Wearable devices and electronic health records allow for the real-world monitoring of long-term efficacy and safety. Multi-center, large-scale trials and international collaboration are essential for establishing evidence-based guidelines for natural medicines.

Conclusions and future perspectives

This perspective presents a network-centric paradigm for understanding how natural products exert therapeutic effects against CRDs through multi-pathway regulation (Fig. 1). The systematic analysis reveals that natural products function as integrated network modulators rather than single-target agents, characterized by three inherent advantages, namely privileged scaffolds for drug optimization, intrinsic multi-target activity, and favorable safety profiles. However, several critical translational obstacles, including low bioavailability, mechanistic complexity, quality variability, and limited clinical evidence, must be overcome to realize their full therapeutic potential.

Looking forward, the field should move beyond isolated technological advancements toward an integrated, paradigm-shifting approach. Key priorities include establishing standardized frameworks for network-based efficacy evaluation, developing predictive models that link multi-target mechanisms to clinical outcomes, fostering interdisciplinary collaboration to accelerate translation, and creating regulatory pathways adapted to the unique characteristics of multi-target natural products. By addressing these overarching priorities, natural product research can fully transition from reductionist to network-centric paradigms, establishing a new foundation for developing next-generation therapeutics for CRDs and other complex diseases.

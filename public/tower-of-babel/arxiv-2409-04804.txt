Symmetry of bounded solutions to quasilinear elliptic equations in a half-space
Phuong Le

Abstract
. Let u u be a bounded positive solution to the problem − Δ p ​ u = f ⁡ ( u ) -\Delta_{p}u=f(u) in ℝ + N \mathbb{R}^{N}_{+} with zero Dirichlet boundary condition, where p > 1 p>1 and f f is a locally Lipschitz continuous function. Among other things, we show that if f ⁡ ( sup ℝ + N u ) = 0 f(\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}u)=0 and f f satisfies some other mild conditions, then u u depends only on x N x_{N} and monotone increasing in the x N x_{N} -direction. 1.
Introduction

We study the monotonicity and one-dimensional symmetry of bounded solutions to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

(1)

where
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}:=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}\mid x_{N}>0\}
denotes the upper half-space,
f
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function and
-\Delta_{p}u=-\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
is the
p
-Laplacian of
u
.

When
p=2
, the problem reduces to

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f(u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{cases}

(2)

Problems of this type arise in the derivation of a priori bounds for positive solutions of nonlinear second
order elliptic problems on smooth bounded domains, in the study of semilinear problems with small diffusion on smooth bounded domains and in the study of regularity results for some free boundary
problems (see
[
28
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
]
).

The monotonicity of classical solutions to (
2
) was studied in a series of seminal papers by Berestycki, Caffarelli and Nirenberg
[
5
,
6
,
3
,
7
]
. Among other results, they showed in
[
6
,
3
]
that if
f
is a Lipschitz continuous function with
f(0)\geq 0
then any classical solution of (
2
) is monotone increasing in the
x_{N}
-direction, furthermore,
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. When
f(0)\geq 0
and
f
is merely locally Lipschitz continuous, the same monotonicity can be proved for positive solutions which are bounded in each strip
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid 0<x_{N}<\lambda\}
, see
[
20
]
. The case
f(0)<0
is more involved, and a complete proof of the monotonicity is only known in dimension
N=2
, see
[
25
,
26
]
. These results were obtained via the well-known method of moving planes, which was introduced by Alexandrov
[
1
]
in the context of differential geometry and by Serrin
[
37
]
in the study of an overdetermined problem.

The one-dimensional symmetry (also called rigidity) of solutions was also investigated by several authors. In particular, using the sliding method, Berestycki, Caffarelli and Nirenberg
[
7
]
proved the following result (see also
[
2
,
9
]
): Assume that
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous function such that for some
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<\rho
,

(i)

f(t)>0
on
(0,\rho)
and
f(t)\leq 0
on
[\rho,+\infty)
,

(ii)

f(t)>c_{0}t
on
(0,t_{0})
for some
c_{0}>0
,

(iii)

f
is nonincreasing on
(t_{1},\rho)
.

Then every solution
u
of (
2
) depends only on
x_{N}
and monotone increasing in the
x_{N}
-direction.
For more general nonlinearities, it was shown in
[
5
]
that if
u
is a bounded positive solution of (
2
) and
f(\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}u)\leq 0
, then
u
is one-dimensional.

The monotonicity and symmetry of solutions to
p
-Laplace problems (
1
) were also studied by several authors. Since the
p
-Laplacian is nonlinear and singular (for
1<p<2
) or degenerate (for
p>2
), many properties which hold for (
2
) do not hold for (
1
) in general, including the
C^{2}
regularity, the weak and strong comparison principles. Hence it is not easy to extend the known results for problem (
2
) to problem (
1
). Nevertheless, some progress has been made in the study of qualitative properties of solutions to (
1
) in the last decades. The monotonicity of solutions to (
1
) with bounded gradients was established by Farina, Montoro, Riey and Sciunzi via the moving plane method, see
[
21
,
22
]
for the case
1<p<2
and
[
24
,
23
]
for the case
p>2
. A key assumption in these works is that
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function which is positive on
(0,+\infty)
. To the best of our knowledge, there are only two monotonicity results for (
1
) with sign-changing nonlinearities, which were obtained in
[
18
]
and
[
36
]
. The precise assumption in
[
18
]
is that
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<2
,
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz continuous with
f(0)\geq 0
and the zeros set of
f
is discrete. More recently, there are some results by the author in which
f
is only locally Lipschitz continuous on
(0,+\infty)
, see
[
30
,
31
]
.

When the monotonicity of solutions is in force, one may pass it to the one-dimensional symmetry via the De Giorgi type results which were available in low dimensions (see
[
27
]
for instance). Following this approach, some rigidity results were obtained in
[
12
]
for the case
N=2
,
p>\frac{3}{2}
and
[
23
,
22
]
for the case
N=3
,
p>\frac{8}{5}
both under the boundedness of solutions and their gradients. For higher dimensions, we refer to Du and Guo
[
16
]
, where the one-dimensional symmetry of bounded solutions to (
1
) is obtained under the assumption that

(i)

f(t)>0
on
(0,\rho)
and
f(t)<0
on
(\rho,+\infty)
for some
\rho>0
,

(ii)

f(t)>c_{0}t^{p-1}
on
(0,t_{0})
for some
c_{0},t_{0}>0
.

This extends the main result in
[
7
]
from the case
p=2
to
p\neq 2
.
Notice that in all these works, the nonlinearity
f
is positive in the range of solutions
u
.
The true sign changing nonlinearity was considered recently by the author in
[
32
]
but with the additional assumption that
u(x^{\prime},x_{N})\to 1
as
x_{N}\to+\infty
uniformly in
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
and
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p\leq 2
. The method used in
[
32
]
is the sliding method which relies heavily on the assumption of this uniform limit.

In this paper, we will prove a symmetry result for problem (
1
) with possibly sign-changing nonlinearity and without assumptions on uniform limits in all dimensions and in the full range
1<p<+\infty
.
Our result is therefore stronger and covers the one in
[
32
]
.
Throughout the present paper, we denote

F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds

for
t>0
. We also denote
Z_{f}:=\{z\in[0,+\infty)\mid f(z)=0\}
. By
B_{r}(x_{0})
, we mean the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of center
x_{0}
with radius
r>0
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Assume
p>1
and
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function which is locally Lipschitz continuous in
[0,+\infty)\setminus Z_{f}
. Let
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a bounded solution to (
1
). Assume that
\rho=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}u
verifies
f(\rho)=0
and
f
has no zeros in
(\rho-\varepsilon,\rho)
for some
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, assume that

\liminf_{t\to z^{+}}\frac{f(t)}{(t-z)^{p-1}}>-\infty,\quad\limsup_{t\to z^{-}}\frac{f(t)}{(z-t)^{p-1}}<+\infty\quad\text{ for all }z\in Z_{f}

(3)

and one of the following conditions hold

(i)

f(0)\geq 0
,

(ii)

F(\rho)\neq 0
,

(iii)

u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\cap B_{r}(x_{0}))
for some
x_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and
r>0
.

Then
u
depends only on
x_{N}
and monotone increasing in
x_{N}
. Furthermore,

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

(4)

Remark 1
.

Condition (
3
) ensures the strong maximum principle holds at the zeros of
f
. This condition is fulfilled by locally Lipschitz functions
f
in the case
1<p\leq 2
. Indeed, in such a case,

\frac{|f(t)|}{|t-z|^{p-1}}=\left|\frac{f(t)-f(z)}{t-z}\right||t-z|^{2-p}\leq C|t-z|^{2-p}\leq C^{\prime}

for
t
sufficiently close to
z\in Z_{f}
.

Remark 2
.

Theorem
1
partially extends a classical result in
[
5
]
to the
p
-Laplacian case. More precisely, in
[
5
, Theorem 1]
, Berestycki, Caffarelli and Nirenberg proved the same result for
p=2
without assuming that
f
has no zeros in a left neighborhood of
\rho
. We need this assumption as well as (i), (ii) or (iii) to deal with the lack of the strong comparison principle and comparison boundary point lemma for the
p
-Laplacian with
p\neq 2
.

We also remark that (iii) holds in the case
p=2
by standard elliptic regularity. Moreover, if
p\neq 2
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}(x_{0})>0
for some
x_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, then
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
for some
r>0
and (iii) is fulfilled thanks to standard regularity for nondegenerate elliptic operators.

To prove Theorem
1
, we first study the ODE problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
u\geq 0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
u(0)=0,~\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}<+\infty,\end{cases}

(5)

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)
.
One important ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1
is the following classification result for (
5
), which is of independent interest.

Theorem 2
.

Assume
p>1
and
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function which is locally Lipschitz continuous in
[0,+\infty)\setminus Z_{f}
such that (
3
) holds. Then we have the following claims:

(i)

For each
\rho\in Z_{f}^{*}:=\{z\in Z_{f}\mid F(t)<F(z)\text{ for all }t\in[0,z)\}\cup Z_{f}^{0}
, where

Z_{f}^{0}=\begin{cases}\{z\in Z_{f}\mid F(t)<0=F(z)\text{ for all }t\in(0,z)\}&\text{ if }f(0)<0,\\
\emptyset&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

there exists a unique solution
u_{\rho}
to (
5
) such that
u_{\rho}^{\prime}>0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}u_{\rho}(t)=\rho
and
u_{\rho}^{\prime}(0)=\left(\frac{p}{p-1}F(\rho)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

(ii)

If
f(0)<0
, then for

\rho\in P_{f}:=\{z>0\mid F(t)<0=F(z)\text{ for all }t\in(0,z)\text{ and }f(z)>0\},

there exists a unique solution
\tilde{u}_{\rho}
to (
5
) such that
\tilde{u}_{\rho}^{\prime}>0
in
(0,t^{*})
,
\tilde{u}_{\rho}^{\prime}(0)=\tilde{u}_{\rho}^{\prime}(t^{*})=0
,
\tilde{u}_{\rho}(t^{*})=\rho
and

\tilde{u}_{\rho}(t+2kt^{*})=\tilde{u}_{\rho}(t),\quad\tilde{u}_{\rho}(t+(2k+1)t^{*})=\tilde{u}_{\rho}(t^{*}-t)

for all
t\in[0,t^{*}]
and all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
where

t^{*}=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{ds}{\left[F(\rho)-F(s)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Moreover, every solution
u\not\equiv 0
to (
5
) must have one of the above two forms.

Remark 3
.

Dancer, Yu and Efendiev
[
14
]
classified all solutions to (
5
) under restriction
f(0)\geq 0
. Here we drop that restriction in our classification result. Unlike the case
f(0)\geq 0
, when
f(0)<0
, solutions
u
with
u^{\prime}(0)=0
may exist and they may not strictly positive. This is due to the lack of the strong maximum principle and Hopf’s lemma in the case
f(0)<0
. Moreover, one easily checks that
Z_{f}^{0}
and
P_{f}
each has at most one element. Hence Theorem
2
indicates that (
5
) admits at most one positive solution
u
with
u^{\prime}(0)=0
and at most one periodic solution and such solutions do not exist in the case
f(0)\geq 0
.
One well-known example in higher dimensions is given by the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=u-1&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

This problem has a unique nonnegative solution
u(x)=1-\cos x_{N}
, which is bounded, nonnegative, one-dimensional and periodic in
x_{N}
. This elegant result was conjectured by Berestycki, Caffarelli and Nirenberg
[
3
]
and was proved in
[
25
]
for dimension two and in
[
10
]
for higher dimensions.

As an application of Theorem
1
, we have the following.

Theorem 3
.

Assume
p>1
. If
N>p+1
, we further assume
N(p-2)+2\geq 0
. Let
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that

(i)

for some
\rho>0
,

f(t)>0\text{ on }(0,\rho),\quad f(t)<0\text{ on }(\rho,+\infty),

(ii)

f
satisfies (
3
) at
z=\rho
, i.e.,

\liminf_{t\to\rho^{+}}\frac{f(t)}{(t-\rho)^{p-1}}>-\infty,\quad\limsup_{t\to\rho^{-}}\frac{f(t)}{(\rho-t)^{p-1}}<+\infty,

(iii)

there exist
c_{0},t_{0}>0
such that

f(t)>c_{0}t^{\gamma}\text{ for all }t\in(0,t_{0}),

where

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)(N-1)}{N-p-1}\text{ if }N>p+1,

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\geq 1\text{ is arbitrary if }N\leq p+1.

If
p\geq 2
, we further assume that

\text{ either }\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(t)}{t^{p-1}}=0\text{ or }\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(t)}{t^{p-1}}>0.

Then any bounded solution
u
of (
1
) depends only on
x_{N}
and is monotone increasing in
x_{N}
. Furthermore,

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0\text{ in }\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}.

Due to assumption (i), parameter
\gamma
in assumption (iii) must satisfy
\gamma\geq 1
. Indeed, we can check that
\frac{(p-1)(N-1)}{N-p-1}\geq 1
if
N>p+1
and
N(p-2)+2\geq 0
.

Remark 4
.

Theorem
3
extends corresponding results in
[
7
,
19
]
from
p=2
to
p\neq 2
. We also recall that a similar result was obtained by Du and Guo in
[
16
]
for
p>1
and
\gamma=p-1
. Du and Guo also conjectured that the same rigidity result is still valid if (iii) only holds for some
\gamma>p-1
. Hence Theorem
3
partially answers that open question left in
[
16
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some well-known tools which will be used later.
The following strong maximum principle and Hopf’s lemma are due to Vázquez
[
39
]
.

Theorem 4

(Strong maximum principle and Hopf’s lemma)
.

Let
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
be a nonnegative weak solution to

-\Delta_{p}u+cu^{q}=g\geq 0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

where
p>1
,
q\geq p-1
,
c\geq 0
,
\Omega
is a connected domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
g\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. If
u\not\equiv 0
then
u>0
in
\Omega
. Moreover for any point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
where the interior sphere condition is satisfied, and such that
u\in C^{1}(\Omega\cup\{x_{0}\})
and
u(x_{0})=0
we have that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}>0
for any inward directional
derivative (this means that if
x
approaches
x_{0}
in a ball
B\subset\Omega

that has
x_{0}
on its boundary, then
\lim\limits_{x\to x_{0}}\frac{u(x)-u(x_{0})}{|x-x_{0}|}>0
).

In the quasilinear case, the maximum principle is not equivalent to the comparison one. The strong comparison principle for
p
-Laplace equations does not hold in general. However, it is known that the strong comparison principle for
p
-Laplacian holds outside the critical set. This result, which is due to Damascelli
[
11
]
, is enough for our purpose.

Theorem 5

(Strong comparison principle)
.

Let
u,v\in C^{1}(\Omega)
be two solutions to

-\Delta_{p}w=f(w)\quad\text{ in }\Omega

such that
u\leq v
in
\Omega
, with
p>1
and let

Z=\{x\in\Omega\mid|\nabla u(x)|+|\nabla v(x)|=0\}.

If
x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus Z
and
u(x_{0})=v(x_{0})
, then
u=v
in the connected component of
\Omega\setminus Z
containing
x_{0}
.

In companion with the strong comparison principle is the following boundary point lemma
[
35
, Theorem 2.7.1]
, which holds for
C^{2}
solutions.

Theorem 6

(Boundary point lemma)
.

Let
u,v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{2}(\Omega)
be solutions of the equation

-\Delta_{p}w=f(w)\quad\text{ in }\Omega

such that
u<v
in
\Omega
and
u=v
at some point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
where the interior sphere condition is satisfied. Then

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(x_{0})<\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}(x_{0}),

where
\nu
is the inward normal at
x_{0}
.

In the situations where the strong comparison principle does not hold, we will make use of the following weak sweeping principle by Dancer and Yu
[
13
]
:

Theorem 7

(Weak sweeping principle)
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
h(x,s)
is measurable in
x\in\Omega
,
continuous in
s
, and for each finite interval
J
, there exists a continuous increasing function
L(s)
such
that
h(x,s)+L(s)
is nondecreasing in
s
for
s\in J
and
x\in\Omega
. Let
u_{t}
and
v_{t}
,
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
, be functions in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
and satisfy in the weak sense,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u_{t}\geq h(x,u_{t})+\varepsilon_{1}(t)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p}v_{t}\leq h(x,v_{t})-\varepsilon_{2}(t)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{t}\geq v_{t}+\varepsilon&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
\end{cases}

for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
, where

\varepsilon_{1}(t)+\varepsilon_{2}(t)\geq\varepsilon>0.

Moreover, suppose that
u_{t_{0}}\geq v_{t_{0}}
in
\Omega
for some
t_{0}\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
and
t\mapsto u_{t}
,
t\mapsto v_{t}
are continuous from the finite closed interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
to
C(\overline{\Omega})
. Then

u_{t}\geq v_{t}\text{ in }\Omega\text{ for all }t\in[t_{1},t_{2}].

The statement of Theorem
7
is taken from
[
14
]
. The proof of this theorem is almost identical to that of
[
13
, Lemma 2.7]
.

3.
Classification of nonnegative solutions in dimension one

We prove Theorem
2
in this section.
Let
f:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function which is locally Lipschitz continuous except possibly at its zeros such that (
3
) holds. Let
u
be a solution to (
5
). In Lemmas
8
and
9
below, we prove local symmetry properties of
u
.

Lemma 8
.

If there exist
0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}<+\infty
such that
u^{\prime}\neq 0
in
(t_{0},t_{1})
and
u^{\prime}(t_{1})=0
, then

u(t)=u(2t_{1}-t)\quad\text{ for }t\in(t_{0},t_{1}).

Proof.

We only consider the case
u^{\prime}>0
in
(t_{0},t_{1})
since the case
u^{\prime}<0
in
(t_{0},t_{1})
is quite similar.

By the standard regularity,
u
is
C^{2}
in
(t_{0},t_{1})
and in this interval

(p-1)(u^{\prime})^{p-2}u^{\prime\prime}=-f(u).

(6)

First, we claim that
f(u(t_{1}))>0
. Indeed, if
f(u(t_{1}))=0
, then we obtain from (
3
) that

-\Delta_{p}(u(t_{1})-u(t))+C(u(t_{1})-u(t))^{p-1}=-f(u(t))+C(u(t_{1})-u(t))^{p-1}\geq 0

for some constant
C>0
and all
t<t_{1}
close to
t_{1}
. We can now apply Hopf’s lemma
[
39
]
to deduce
u^{\prime}(t_{1})>0
, a contradiction. Now we assume
f(u(t_{1}))<0
, then from (
6
) we obtain
u^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
for all
t<t_{1}
and close to
t_{1}
. It follows that
u^{\prime}(t_{1})>u^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t<t_{1}
and close to
t_{1}
. This contradiction shows that
f(u(t_{1}))>0
.

By continuity, we have
f(u(t))>0
for
t\in[t_{1},t_{1}+\varepsilon]
for some small
\varepsilon>0
.

We claim that
u^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
or all
t\in(t_{1},t_{1}+\varepsilon)
. Otherwise we can find
r_{1}\in(t_{1},t_{1}+\varepsilon)
such that
u^{\prime}(r_{1})>0
. Consider the maximal interval
(r_{2},r_{1}]\subset(t_{1},r_{1}]
such that

u^{\prime}(t)>0\text{ in }(r_{2},r_{1}],\quad u^{\prime}(r_{2})=0.

From the standard elliptic regularity, we know that
u
is
C^{2}
in
(r_{2},r_{1}]
. Hence (
6
) holds in this interval and
u^{\prime\prime}(t)<0
for
t\in(r_{2},r_{1}]
. This implies
u^{\prime}(r_{2})>u^{\prime}(r_{1})>0
, which is a contradiction.

We further claim that
u^{\prime}(t)<0
or all
t\in(t_{1},t_{1}+\varepsilon)
. Suppose on the contrary that
u^{\prime}(r_{1})=0
for some
r_{1}\in(t_{1},t_{1}+\varepsilon)
. If
u^{\prime}(t)=0
for all
t\in[t_{1},r_{1}]
then from the equation we deduce
f(u(t))=0
in this interval, a contradiction. Hence there exists
r_{2}\in(t_{1},r_{1})
such that
u^{\prime}(r_{2})<0

4.
Existence of positive solutions in a ball

In this section, we analyze the
N
-dimensional problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }B_{r},\\
u>0&\text{ in }B_{r},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{r},\end{cases}

(17)

where
B_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
stands for the ball of radius
r
centered at the origin. Motivated by
[
9
,
17
,
14
]
, we have the following existence result.

Proposition 10
.

Assume that
f
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function with
f(0)\geq 0
and
f(\rho)=0
for some
\rho>0
. Assume in addition that

F_{\rho}(t):=\int_{t}^{\rho}f(s)ds>0\quad\text{ for all }t\in[0,\rho).

Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a positive number

R_{0}=R_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for
r\geq R_{0}
, problem (
17
) admits a solution
u_{r}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B_{r}})
such that

\rho-\varepsilon\leq\|u_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}<\rho.

Proof.

We first observe that for each
n>1
, there exists
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{n}
such that

F_{\rho}(t)\geq F_{\rho}(\rho-\varepsilon)\quad\text{ for all }t\in[0,\rho-\varepsilon].

(18)

Indeed, we may choose

\varepsilon=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n}&\text{ if }\min_{[0,\rho-\frac{1}{n}]}F_{\rho}=F_{\rho}(\rho-\frac{1}{n}),\\
\sup\{z<\frac{1}{n}\mid F_{\rho}(\rho-z)\leq\min_{[0,\rho-\frac{1}{n}]}F_{\rho}\}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore, it suffices to prove the proposition for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so that (
18
) is satisfied. We set

\tilde{f}(t)=\begin{cases}f(0)&\text{ for }t<0,\\
f(t)&\text{ for }0\leq t\leq\rho,\\
0&\text{ for }t>\rho,\end{cases}

and

\tilde{F}_{\rho}(t)=\int_{t}^{\rho}\tilde{f}(s)ds,

then
\tilde{F}_{\rho}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We consider the energy functional

I_{r}(u)=\int_{B_{r}}\left(\frac{1}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+\tilde{F}_{\rho}(u)\right)

for
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{r})
. Clearly, a critical point of
I_{r}
corresponds to a weak solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\tilde{f}(u)&\text{ in }B_{r},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{r}.\end{cases}

(19)

Because
\tilde{f}(t)\geq 0
for
t\leq 0
and
\tilde{f}(t)=0
for
t\geq\rho
, we may exploit the weak maximum principle to deduce that any such a solution satisfies

0\leq u\leq\rho.

(20)

More precisely, we may directly test (
19
) with
\max\{-u,0\}
and
\max\{u-\rho,0\}
to get (
20
).
Therefore, all solutions of (
19
) are also solutions of (
17
). Moreover, by standard elliptic regularity for
p
-Laplace equations (see
[
15
,
38
,
33
]
), we know that such solutions belong to
C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B_{r}})
.

Since the functional
I_{r}
is well-defined and coercive, it has a minimizer
u_{r}
. Thus,
u_{r}
is a nonnegative solution to (
17
). Moreover, since
u_{r}
is a minimizer, by the well-known rearrangement theory (see
[
29
]
), it must be radially symmetric and decreasing away from the center of the domain. By abuse of notation, we write
u_{r}(x)=u_{r}(|x|)
. Then

0\leq u_{r}(|x|)\leq u_{r}(0)\leq\rho\quad\text{ in }B_{r}.

It remains to show that there exists
r>0
such that
u_{r}(0)\geq\rho-\varepsilon
. Otherwise
u_{r}\leq\rho-\varepsilon
for all
r>0
. Exploiting (
18
), we obtain

I_{r}(u_{r})\geq\int_{B_{r}}F_{\rho}(u_{r})\geq\int_{B_{r}}F_{\rho}(\rho-\varepsilon)=\omega_{N}r^{N}F_{\rho}(\rho-\varepsilon)\quad\text{ for all }r>0,

where
\omega_{N}
denotes the volume of
B_{1}
. We consider

w_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}\rho&\text{ for }|x|<r-1,\\
\rho(r-|x|)&\text{ for }r-1<|x|\leq r.\end{cases}

Clearly,
w_{r}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{r})
and
|\nabla w_{r}|
and
F_{\rho}(w_{r})
are supported on the annulus
\{r-1\leq|x|\leq r\}
. Hence there exists a constant
C
independent of
r
such that

I_{r}(w_{r})\leq C\left[r^{N}-(r-1)^{N}\right]\quad\text{ for all }r>1.

Since
v_{r}
is the minimizer of
I_{r}
, we have
I_{r}(u_{r})\leq I_{r}(w_{r})
. Thus

\omega_{N}r^{N}F_{\rho}(\rho-\varepsilon)\leq C\left[r^{N}-(r-1)^{N}\right]\quad\text{ for all }r>1,

or equivalently,

\omega_{N}F_{\rho}(\rho-\varepsilon)\leq C\left[1-\left(\frac{r-1}{r}\right)^{N}\right]\quad\text{ for all }r>1.

Since
F_{\rho}(\rho-\varepsilon)>0
, the above inequality does not hold for large
r
. This contradiction shows that
u_{r}(0)\geq\rho-\varepsilon
for all large
r
.
∎

Lemma 11

\delta>0
, we define

g_{\mu,\delta}(t):=f_{\mu}(t)-\delta(t+\mu)\quad\text{ in }[-\mu,\rho].

Since
f
has no zeros in a left neighborhood of
\rho
and (
22
) holds, we deduce that
f
is positive in that neighborhood. On the other hand,
\int_{t}^{\rho}g_{\mu,\delta}(s)ds\to\int_{t}^{\rho}f_{\mu}(s)ds
as
\delta\to 0
, uniformly in
t\in[-\mu,\rho]
. Hence taking into account (
24
), for sufficiently small
\delta
, there exists
\rho_{\delta}\nearrow\rho
(as
\delta\to 0
) such that
g_{\mu,\delta}(\rho_{\delta})=0
and

G(t):=\int_{t}^{\rho_{\delta}}g_{\mu,\delta}(s)ds>0\quad\text{ for all }t\in[-\mu,\rho_{\delta}).

We can therefore apply Proposition
10
to deduce that the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}w=g_{\mu,\delta}(w-\mu)&\text{ in }B_{r},\\
w=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{r}\end{cases}

with some
r=r(\delta)
has a positive solution
w_{r}
verifying

\rho_{\delta}+\mu-\delta\leq\|w_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}<\rho_{\delta}+\mu.

Hence by setting
v_{r}=w_{r}-\rho
, we have that
v_{r}\geq-\mu
,

\rho_{\delta}-\delta\leq\|v_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}<\rho_{\delta}

and
v_{r}
solves the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}v=g_{\mu,\delta}(v)&\text{ in }B_{r},\\
v=-\mu&\text{ on }\partial B_{r}.\end{cases}

By Step 1, we can find
x_{0}
so that

B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\overline{\Sigma}_{1}
and
u\geq\rho_{\delta}
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
.

Let
\tilde{x}
be an arbitrary point in
\overline{\Sigma}_{r+1}
. We define
x^{t}=(1-t)x^{0}+t\tilde{x}
and
u^{t}(x)=u(x+x^{t})
.
Clearly,
B_{r}(x^{t})\subset\overline{\Sigma}_{1}
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Since
u>0
on the compact set
\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\overline{B_{r}(x^{t})}
, we can find
\lambda>0
such that
u\geq\lambda
on this set. Let
r_{1}<r
be such that
-\mu<v_{r}<\frac{\lambda}{2}
on

. Then for all

, we have

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u^{t}=f(u^{t})&\text{ in }B_{r_{1}},\\
-\Delta_{p}v_{r}=g_{\mu,\delta}(v_{r})\leq f(v_{r})-\gamma&\text{ in }B_{r_{1}},\\
u^{t}\geq v_{r}+\frac{\lambda}{2}&\text{ on }\partial B_{r_{1}},\end{cases}

where
\gamma=\inf_{x\in B_{r_{1}}}[f(v_{r}(x))-g_{\mu,\delta}(v_{r}(x))]>0
. Moreover,
u^{0}=u\geq\rho_{\delta}\geq v_{r}
in
B_{r_{1}}
. Thus we can apply the weak sweeping principle (Theorem

) to deduce that
u^{t}\geq v_{r}
in
B_{r_{1}}
for all
t\in[0,1]
. In particular,
u(\tilde{x})\geq v_{r}(0)\geq\rho_{\delta}-\delta
. Since
\tilde{x}\in\overline{\Sigma}_{r+1}
is arbitrary, we have

u\geq\rho_{\delta}-\delta\quad\text{ in }\overline{\Sigma}_{r+1}.

Since
\rho_{\delta}\to\rho
as
\delta\to 0
, we can choose
\delta
small such that
\rho_{\delta}-\delta>\rho-\varepsilon
for given
\varepsilon>0
. Hence the conclusion follows.

Step 3.
There exists a minimal solution
\underline{u}
of (

) such that
\|\underline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\rho
and
\underline{u}\leq u
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

Since
f
has no zeros in a left neighborhood of
\rho
and (

\delta>0

\varepsilon<\delta
sufficiently small and then modify

to obtain a new function

f_{\varepsilon}=f\text{ in }[0,\rho-\delta],\quad 0<f_{\varepsilon}<f\text{ in }(\rho-\delta,\rho-\varepsilon),\quad f_{\varepsilon}(\rho-\varepsilon)=0

\int_{t}^{\rho-\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}(s)ds>0\quad\text{ for all }t\in[0,\rho-\varepsilon).

Hence we can apply Theorem

to see that the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}v=f_{\varepsilon}(v)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+},\\

has a unique positive solution
v_{\varepsilon}
, which is monotone increasing. For any
\eta>0

v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(t)=\begin{cases}0&t\in[0,\eta),\\
v_{\varepsilon}(t-\eta)&t\in[\eta,+\infty).\end{cases}

It is easy to check that

u>\rho-\varepsilon

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }B_{n}^{+},\\

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By Lemmas
11
and
12
, there exist two one-dimensional positive solutions
\overline{u}
and
\underline{u}
such that
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}

\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover,
\|\underline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\rho
. Therefore, we can apply Theorem

to deduce
\underline{u}(x)=\overline{u}(x)=u_{\rho}(x_{N})
.

u=u_{\rho}(x_{N})
. Furthermore, (

) follows from properties of
u_{\rho}
stated in Theorem

. This completes the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

According to
[
22
, Theorem 1.7]
, it can be concluded that
0<u\leq\rho
. Due to the behavior of the nonlinearity near
\rho
(see (ii)), the strong maximum principle applies, which leads to the conclusion that
0<u<\rho
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Following the same reasoning as in the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 1.3]
, it can be inferred that
u
is strictly bounded away from
\rho
in
\Sigma_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda>0
. Moreover, the standard gradient estimate gives
|\nabla u|\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
. Now we can apply the results in
[
21
,
24
]
we deduce that

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\geq 0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

In fact, it’s worth noting that
f>0
within the range of values the solution takes in any strip, which is sufficient to reapply the moving plane method as in
[
21
,
24
]
.

Now the function

v(x^{\prime}):=\lim_{x_{N}\to+\infty}u(x^{\prime},x_{N})

is well-defined and satisfies

-\Delta_{p}v=f(v)\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.

We claim that
\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}v=\rho
. We argue by contradiction. Suppose
\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}v=t_{1}<\rho
. Then
v\leq t_{1}
in

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}v\geq c_{1}v^{\gamma}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\\

This contradicts a Liouville-type theorem by E. Mitidieri and S.I. Pokhozhaev

), since

. Notice that

Lyapunov and Riccati Equations from a Positive System Perspective
Dongjun Wu, Yankai Lin

Abstract
This paper presents a new interpretation of the Lyapunov and Riccati equations from the perspective of positive system theory. We show it is possible to construct positive systems related to these equations, and then certain conclusions – such as the existence and uniqueness of solutions – can be drawn from positive systems theory. Specifically, under standard observability assumptions, a strictly positive linear system can be constructed for Lyapunov equations, leading to exponential convergence in Hilbert metric to the Perron-Frobenius vector – closely related to the solution of the Lyapunov equation. For algebraic Riccati equations, homogeneous strictly positive systems can be constructed, which exhibit more complex dynamical behaviors. While the existence and uniqueness of the solution can still be proven, only asymptotic convergence can be obtained.

Introduction

The Lyapunov and algebraic Riccati equations play fundamental roles in control
theory, underpinning applications in system analysis, model order reduction, stabilization
and optimal control
[
9
,
11
,
18
,
1
,
14
]
.
Their properties – such as existence, uniqueness, and numerical
computation of solutions – have been extensively studied in the
literature.
In particular, it is well-known that Lyapunov equations for linear time-invariant systems admit explicit solutions - expressed as integrals for continuous-time systems or infinite sums for discrete-time systems. In contrast, algebraic Riccati equations rarely yield such explicit solutions, leading to a distinct treatment of these equations despite their strong connections.

Recently, Pates
[
19
]
proposed viewing the Lyapunov equation as a fixed-point problem on a compact convex set, leveraging the Brouwer fixed-point theorem to establish solution existence and prove uniqueness under an observability assumption. However, this technique does not directly extend to Riccati equations.
To address this, we propose a new approach to tackle Lyapunov
and algebraic Riccati equations in a unified framework, namely, by associating these equations with dynamical systems, and the properties of the dynamical systems in turn characterize the solutions of these equations.

Our main research finding is that both the Lyapunov and algebraic Riccati
equations can be analyzed through the lens of positive systems theory
– a framework that has emerged as a powerful tool in control theory

[
21
,
13
]
.
To be specific, we first consider the simpler case of Lyapunov equations in both continuous-time and discrete-time. By introducing an auxiliary variable, we construct a linear matrix differential (difference) equation which is shown to be strictly positive under standard observability conditions. Following standard arguments from positive systems theory, we demonstrate exponential convergence of the system in Hilbert metric to the unique Perron-Frobenius vector, which further leads to the unique solution to the Lyapunov equation. We then apply similar techniques to algebraic Riccati equations, leading to homogeneous strictly positive systems.
In such cases, we prove asymptotic convergence (in Hilbert metric) of a dynamical system to the unique solution of the algebraic Riccati equation.

Our contributions are twofold:

1.

We establish novel connections between positive systems theory and classical equations in control, namely Lyapunov and algebraic Riccati equations. A key technical step involves homogenizing these equations by introducing a scalar-valued linear function, which enables a unified treatment under the framework of positive systems.

2.

Leveraging both linear and nonlinear positive systems theory, we characterize the solutions of Lyapunov and algebraic Riccati equations as steady states of suitably constructed positive dynamical systems. Furthermore, we analyze the convergence properties of these dynamics, thereby providing a dynamical systems perspective on solving such equations.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some concepts from positive systems theory and state some useful results that will be used as the main tools in this paper. In Section
3
, we study Lyapunov equations by positive systems theory. Section
4
extends the results in Section
3
to algebraic Riccati equations.

Preliminaries on Positive Systems Theory

In this section, we shall introduce the necessary notations and recall basic concepts that will be used throughout the paper. We begin by defining the cones, and then, on this basis, briefly review relevant results from the theory of positive systems and fixed-point theorems.

Definition 1
(Cone)

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional vector space equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|
. The subset
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
is called a
cone
if the following three properties are simultaneously satisfied:

(a)

{\rm Int}\,\mathcal{K}\neq\emptyset
;

(b)

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
x,\;y\in\mathcal{K}

\Rightarrow

\lambda x\in\mathcal{K}
, and
x+y\in\mathcal{K}
;

(c)

x\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}

\Rightarrow

-x\notin\mathcal{K}
.

In particular, the cone
\mathcal{K}
is called a
normal cone
if the following condition is further satisfied:

(d)

there exists some
\delta>0
such that
\|x+y\|>\delta
whenever

x,y\in\mathcal{K}
, with
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
.

A partial ordering
can be defined on
\mathcal{K}
: if
x-y\in\mathcal{K}
, we say
x\leq_{\mathcal{K}}y
.
We also write
x\leq y
when clear from the context.

Throughout this paper, we shall encounter two types of cones, namely, the
positive orthant
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}\geq 0,\;\forall i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\}

and the set of semi-positive definite matrices
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:X\succeq 0\}
. The norm on
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
is defined as the Frobenius norm
\|P\|^{2}=\sum_{ij}P_{ij}^{2}
, and the partial ordering is defined as
X\preceq Y\Leftrightarrow X-Y\preceq 0
.
Obviously, both
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
are normal cones

Definition 2
(Hilbert metric)

Given a cone
\mathcal{K}
and
x,y\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}
, let
M(x/y):=\inf\{\lambda:x\leq\lambda y\}
and
m(x/y):=\sup\{\mu:\mu y\leq x\}
. Then, the Hilbert metric on
\mathcal{K}
is defined as

d_{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)=\ln\frac{M(x/y)}{m(x/y)}.

The Hilbert metric
d_{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)
measures the distance between the rays
\{rx;r\geq 0\}

and
\{ry:r\geq 0\}
, in particular,
d_{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)=0
if
and only if
x
and
y
lie on the same ray. Hilbert metric plays a crucial
role in positive system theory, for instance in consensus
[
24
,
22
]
.
For more details about Hilbert metric, see
[
8
]
.

Definition 3
(Order-preserving maps)

An operator
T:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
is called
homogeneous
if
T(\alpha x)=\alpha T(x)

for all
x\in V,

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
be a cone. Assume
T\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
.
We say that
T
is
order-preserving
if

0\leq_{\mathcal{K}}x\leq_{\mathcal{K}}y\;\Rightarrow\;0\leq_{\mathcal{K}}Tx\leq_{\mathcal{K}}Ty

We say
T
is
strictly order-preserving
if

0\leq_{\mathcal{K}}x\leq_{\mathcal{K}}y\text{ and $x\neq y$ }\;\Rightarrow\;Ty-Tx\in{\rm Int}\;\mathcal{K}.

Let
T
be a homogeneous, continuous and order-preserving
mapping, the
cone spectral radius
of
T
is defined as

\rho(T)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|^{1/n}.

The following theorem collects some important facts from nonlinear Perron-Frobenius theory.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a normal cone in a finite dimensional vector
space
\mathcal{V}
.
Let
F:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
be a continuous, homogeneous and order-preserving map on
\mathcal{K}
.
Then there exists an
x_{*}\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}
, such that
F(x_{*})=\rho(F)x_{*}
.
In addition,

(a)

if
F
is strongly order-preserving, then

x_{*}\in{\rm Int}\;\mathcal{K}
and it is unique (up to positive scaling). Moreover,

d_{\mathcal{H}}(F^{k}(x),x_{*})\to 0\text{ as }k\to\infty\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\};

(b)

if
F
is linear and strongly order-preserving, then there exists a positive constant
\lambda\in[0,1)
, such that

d_{\mathcal{H}}(F^{k}(x),x_{*})\leq\lambda^{k}d_{\mathcal{H}}(x,x_{*}),\;

for all
k\geq 1,\,x\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}
.

Remark 2.1
.

For the existence of
x_{*}
, see
[
16
, Corollary 5.4.2]
.
For (a), see

[
16
, Theorem 6.5.1]
. In particular, it implies the uniqueness of
x_{*}
.
For (b), we refer to
[
8
]
.

With these preparations in place, we now define the central objects of the note, namely, positive systems.

Definition 2.2

(Positive systems)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a normal cone in a finite dimensional vector
space
\mathcal{V}
, and
F
be a continuous, homogeneous mapping on
\mathcal{V}
.
Consider the dynamics

\dot{x}=F(x).

(1)

The system is called (strictly) positive if
x\mapsto\phi(t,x)
is (strictly) order-preserving for all
t>0
.

Since we will work with continuous time systems. Theorem
1
needs to be adapted for
system (
1
).

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a normal cone in a finite dimensional vector
space
\mathcal{V}
.
Consider the dynamics (
1
). The following hold.

(i)

If there exists some constant
a
, such that
aI+F
is a strongly order-preserving mapping,
where
I
is the identity mapping, then the system (
1
) is strictly positive.

(ii)

If the system (
1
) is strictly positive,
then there exists a constant
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and
x_{*}\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}
(unique up
positive scaling) such that

(a)

F(x_{*})=\mu x_{*}
.

(b)

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\phi(t,x),x_{*})\to 0
as
t\to\infty
,
\forall x\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}
.

(c)

If in addition,
F
is linear, then the convergence is exponential, i.e., there exist
two positive constants
k
,
\lambda
, such that

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\phi(t,x),x_{*})\leq ke^{-\lambda t}d_{\mathcal{H}}(x,x_{*}),\;
for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}
.

PROOF.

(i) Rewrite the system (
1
) as
\dot{x}=-ax+(aI+F)(x)
.
Without loss of generality, assume
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle\phi(t,x)=e^{-at}x+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-a(t-s)}(aI+F)(\phi(s,x))ds

from which we deduce that
x\mapsto\phi(t,x)
is strongly order-preserving for every
t>0
.

(ii) Since
x\mapsto\phi(t,x)
is strongly order-preserving for all
t>0
,
by Theorem
1
,
for every fixed
t>0
, there exists some
x_{*}
such that

\phi(t,x_{*})=\lambda_{t}x_{*}

(2)

and that

\phi(kt,x)
converges to
x_{*}
in Hilbert metric as
k\to\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}
.
It is obvious that
x_{*}
is independent of
t
, i.e., the equation holds for all
t>0
for some
t
-dependent
parameters
\lambda_{t}
and that
\phi(t,x)\to x_{*}
in Hilbert metric as
t\to\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{0\}
. Now (
2
) simply says that the ray
\{rx_{*}:r\geq 0\}
is invariant.
Thus the time derivative of
\phi(t,x_{*})
at
t=0
results in
F(x_{*})=\dot{\lambda}_{0}x_{*}
.
Hence we can take
\mu=\dot{\lambda}_{0}
in (ii-a).

3
Lyapunov equations

3.1
Continuous-time Lyapunov equations

The first equation that we are going to study is the so-called continuous-time Lyapunov equation:

A^{\top}P+PA+C^{\top}C=0

(3)

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
C\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

are known matrices, and the matrix
P
is the unknown variable to
be solved. It is well-known that:

Fact 1

If
(A,C)
is observable and
A
is stable, there exists a unique
positive definite solution
P_{\#}
to (
3
).

Below, we adopt a positive systems approach to prove this fact. To illustrate how an algebraic equation relates to a positive dynamical system, consider a simple algebraic equation:

Ap+c=0

(4)

where
A
is a Metzler matrix (all off-diagonal elements are non-negative), and
c
is a non-negative vector. It is well-known that this
equation has a unique strictly positive solution
p
when
A
is stable.

To prove this by positive system theory, we may associate this equation with a dynamical system
\dot{x}=Ax+c
,
which is a positive system since
A
is Metzler and
c\geq 0

[
21
]
.
However, the system is not linear (neither homogeneous). To apply Corollary
2.3
, we “linearize” the system in the following fashion:

\dot{x}=(A+\alpha c\mathds{1}^{\top})x

where
\alpha>0
is a positive constant, and
\mathds{1}
is a column vector with all ones.
This is a linear strictly positive system. In view of Corollary
2.3
, it converges
(exponentially in Hilbert metric) to the unique Perron-Frobenius vector
\xi>0
and that
(A+\alpha c\mathds{1}^{\top})\xi=\lambda\xi
holds
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose that
A
is Hurwitz,
then for
\alpha
sufficiently small,

\lambda
must be negative; for
\alpha
large,

\lambda
is clearly positive. By continuity (of the largest eigenvalue), there exists some
\alpha>0
,
such that
\lambda=0
, i.e.,
A\xi+\alpha c\mathds{1}^{\top}\xi=0
, see Fig.
1
. Now let
p=\frac{\xi}{\alpha(\mathds{1}^{\top}\xi)}
, we see that

p
is the desired strictly positive solution to (
4
).

Figure 1:
Eigenvalue corresponding to the Perron-Frobenius vector as a function of
\alpha
for the matrix
A+\alpha c\mathds{1}^{\top}
.

Now going back to the Lyapunov equation (
3
),
we mimic the above procedure to define the following linear dynamics on
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
:

\dot{P}=A^{\top}P+PA+\alpha{\rm tr}(P)C^{\top}C

(5)

where
\alpha
is a positive constant.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(A,C)
is observable, then the system (
5
)
is strictly positive on
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
.

PROOF.

Given initial state
P_{0}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, the solution (
5
) satisfies

P(t)=e^{A^{\top}t}P_{0}e^{At}+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}{\rm tr}(P(s))e^{A^{\top}(t-s)}C^{\top}Ce^{A(t-s)}{\rm d}s

If there exists a non-zero vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
P(t)v=0
for
some
t>0
, then
v^{\top}P(t)v=0
and

{\rm tr}(P(s))Ce^{A(t-s)}v=0,\;\forall s\in[0,t].

Since
P(s)\succeq e^{A^{\top}s}P_{0}e^{A^{\top}s}
,
{\rm tr}(P(s))

must be positive since
P_{0}\neq 0
. Thus
Ce^{As}v=0
for all
s\in[0,t]
,
which is impossible thanks to the observability of
(A,C)
. Thus

P(t)\succ 0
, or
P(t)\in{\rm Int}\,\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
for all
t>0
.

With Lemma
3.1
at hand, we can now prove Fact
1
using positive systems theory.

Positive system argument for Fact
1
:

Since (
5
) is a strictly positive system
on
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
, by Corollary
2.3
, there exists a unique
direction
P_{*}\succ 0
and some constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, such
that

A^{\top}P_{*}+P_{*}A+\alpha{\rm tr}(P_{*})C^{\top}C=\lambda P_{*}

Let
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
be an eigenvector of
A

and
\eta\in\mathbb{C}
the corresponding eigenvalue, then

\lambda=2{\rm Re}(\eta)+\alpha\frac{{\rm tr}(P_{*})||Cv||^{2}}{v^{*}P_{*}v}
.
Due to observability of
(A,C)
,
Cv\neq 0
, which yields
||Cv||^{2}\neq 0
.
Since
A
is stable,
we must have
\lambda<0
when
\alpha
is sufficiently small,
and when
\alpha
becomes large,
\lambda
must exceed zero.
By continuity of the largest eigenvalue,
there exists some
\alpha>0
such that
\lambda=0
, i.e.,

A^{\top}P_{*}+P_{*}A+\alpha{\rm tr}(P_{*})C^{\top}C=0

but then
P_{\#}=\frac{P_{*}}{\alpha{\rm tr}(P_{*})}
solves (
3
).
Since
P_{*}

is unique up to positive scaling,

P_{\#}
is unique. Furthermore, with this
\alpha
fixed, the dynamics (
5
) satisfy

d_{\mathcal{H}}(P(t),P_{\#})\leq ke^{-\lambda t}d_{\mathcal{H}}(P_{0},P_{\#}),\,\forall t\geq 0,\,P_{0}\succeq 0

for any some positive constants
k,\,\lambda
.

Similar arguments hold for discrete-time Lyapunov equations, as shown in the next subsection.

3.2
Discrete-time Lyapunov equation

In this subsection, we study the discrete counterpart of (
3
),
i.e., the equation

A^{\top}QA-Q+C^{\top}C=0.

(6)

To analyze this equation from a fixed-point perspective, a natural approach is to consider the map
h(X)=A^{\top}XA+C^{\top}C
, as the solution to (
6
) corresponds to a fixed-point of
h
. However, applying fixed-point theorems typically requires certain structures, such as convexity and compactness. In
[
19
]
, Pates considered an alternative mapping

f(X)=\frac{A^{\top}XA+\alpha C^{\top}C}{{\rm tr}(A^{\top}XA+\alpha C^{\top}C)},

(7)

which is invariant on the compact convex set
\{X\succeq 0:{\rm tr}(X)=1\}
.
This invariance enables the application of Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem to guarantee the existence of a fixed-point. However, a known limitation of Brouwer’s theorem is that it does not provide any assurance of uniqueness.

It is worth noting that
f
defined in this way is non-homogeneous and
thus positive systems theory is not directly applicable.
In what follows, we associate the equation (
6
) with a linear positive system, allowing us to apply Theorem
1
. As a result, both existence and uniqueness of the solution are ensured. The goal is to prove the following fact:

Fact 2

If
A
is Schur stable and
(A,C)
observable. Then there exists
a unique
Q\succ 0
solving the Lyapunov equation (
6
).

We consider the discrete-time dynamics:

X_{k+1}=F(X_{k})

(8)

on
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
, where

F(X)=A^{\top}XA+\alpha{\rm tr}(X)C^{\top}C

with
\alpha
a positive constant.

The following lemma shows that observability ensures
ultimate strict positivity
of the system (
8
) in the sense that there exists some
T>0
such that

X_{k}\succ 0
for all
k\geq T
and
X_{1}\in{\rm Int}\;\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
A
is Schur stable and
(A,C)
is observable, then the system (
8
) is ultimately strictly positive.

PROOF.

Let
X_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}\backslash\{0\}
, then
X_{k}=(A^{k})^{\top}X_{0}A^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\alpha{\rm tr}(X_{j})(A^{j})^{\top}C^{\top}CA^{j}

for all
k\geq 1
. If there exists
v
such that
v^{\top}X_{k}v=0
,
then
CA^{j}v=0
for all
j\geq 0
, which implies
v=0
thanks to the observability of
(A,C
). Hence
X_{k}\in{\rm Int}\,\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
for all
k\geq n

as claimed.

Positive system argument of Fact
2
:
In view of Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
1
, there exists
\lambda>0

and a unique (up to scaling)
X_{*}\in{\rm Int}\;\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
,
such that
F(X_{*})=\lambda X_{*}
. Note

\lambda\geq\frac{\alpha{\rm tr}(C^{\top}C)}{{\rm tr}(X_{*})}\to\infty

as
\alpha\to\infty
; and when
\alpha
is sufficiently small, it is
clear that
\lambda<1
since
A
is Schur stable. By continuity,
there exists some
\alpha>0
, such that
\lambda=1
, i.e.,

A^{\top}X_{*}A+\alpha{\rm tr}(X_{*})C^{\top}C=X_{*},

as shown in Fig.
2
.
Now
Q:=\frac{X_{*}}{\alpha{\rm tr}(X_{*})}
solves (
6
) and that
there exists some positive constant
k
,
\mu
such that

d_{\mathcal{H}}(X_{k},Q)\leq\mu e^{-\lambda k}d_{\mathcal{H}}(X_{1},Q)

for all
k\geq 1
and
X\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
.
Uniqueness is obvious.

Figure 2:
Eigenvalue corresponding to the Perron-Frobenius vector as a function of
\alpha
for the operator
L(X)=A^{\top}XA+\alpha{\rm tr}(X)C^{\top}C
with
A
Schur stable and
(A,C)
observable.

3.3
Discussion

We summarize the major steps in deriving the solutions of Lyapunov equations.

a)

Construct a linearized positive dynamical system with a tuning parameter
\alpha
that controls the spectral radius of the positive operator.

b)

Show that the system is strictly positive based on additional assumptions, typically observability assumptions.

c)

By tuning the parameter
\alpha
, and making use of the continuity of the spectral radius as a function of
\alpha
, show that the spectral radius can reach a desired value.

d)

Show that the dynamical system gives a solution to the equation when it is at steady state.

By following these steps, we can easily extend the preceding results to more general types of Lyapunov equations, such as

A^{\top}P+PA+\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}^{\top}PA_{i}+C^{\top}C=0,

(9)

and

A^{\top}PE+E^{\top}PA+C^{\top}C=0.

(10)

Equation (
9
) arises naturally in linear stochastic systems
[
7
]
and equation (
10
) appears in singular linear systems
[
23
]
, see also
[
4
]
and the references therein.
For example, by defining systems

\displaystyle\dot{X}

\displaystyle=A^{\top}X+XA+\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}^{\top}XA_{j}+\alpha{\rm tr}(X)C^{\top}C

\displaystyle\dot{X}

\displaystyle=A^{\top}XE+E^{\top}XA+\alpha{\rm tr}(X)C^{\top}C

one can easily establish the solution properties of the equations (
9
) and (
10
).

4
Algebraic Riccati equations

In this section, we explore to what extent we can extend the previous results to Riccati equations. The main difficulty, as shall be seen, arises from the fact that Riccati equations cannot be “linearized” in the same way as Lyapunov equations. Nevertheless, Riccati equations can be “homogenized”, allowing us to apply Theorem
1
and Corollary
2.3
.

4.1
Discrete-time Algebraic Riccati Equation

We start with the discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation:

P=A^{\top}PA-A^{\top}PB(R+B^{\top}PB)^{-1}B^{\top}PA+C^{\top}C

(11)

where
R\succ 0
.
The goal in this subsection is to provide a positive systems perspective for the following classical result:

Fact 3

If
(A,B)
is controllable and

Define the system

Remark 4.2

4.2
Continuous-time Algebraic Riccati Equation

Finally, study the continuous-time algebraic Riccati equation:

A^{\top}P+PA-PBR^{-1}B^{\top}P+C^{\top}C=0.

(15)

The goal is to prove the following fact by positive systems theory:

Fact 4

If
(A,B)
is controllable and
(A,C)
is observable, then there exists
a unique positive definite solution to (
15
).

To study this equation, we propose the following homogeneous system:

\dot{P}=\alpha A^{\top}P+\alpha PA-\frac{1}{{\rm tr}(P)}PBR^{-1}B^{\top}P+\alpha^{2}{\rm tr}(P)C^{\top}C

with
P(0)=P_{0}
and
\alpha
a positive constant. Equivalently, the system can be written as

\dot{P}=A_{\alpha}(t)^{\top}P+PA_{\alpha}(t)+\alpha^{2}{\rm tr}(P)C^{\top}C

where
A_{\alpha}(t)=\alpha A-\frac{1}{2{\rm tr}(P)}BR^{-1}B^{\top}P(t)

Lemma 4.5
.

If
(A,C)
is observable, then the system (
16

PROOF.

Step 1: Show that

P(0)=P_{0}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\backslash\{0\}\Rightarrow P(t)\in{\rm Int}\;\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n},\;\forall t>0.

Let
P(0)=P_{0}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
and

be the solution to
\dot{X}=A_{\alpha}(t)X
,

Then

\displaystyle P(t)

\displaystyle=\Phi(t,\tau)^{\top}P(\tau)\Phi(t,\tau)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{2}\int_{\tau}^{t}{\rm tr}(P(s))\Phi(t,s)^{\top}C^{\top}C\Phi(t,s){\rm d}s

for all
\tau\in[0,t]
. If there exists some

such that

is not positive definite, then there exists

P(\tau)\Phi(t,\tau)v=0

Differentiating 2) with respect to

from which we deduce
CA\Phi(t,\tau)v=0

to

{\rm col}(C,CA,\cdots,CA^{n-1})v=0

Thus

since

is observable, from which it follows that

for all

Step 2: Show that
\phi(t,X+Y)\geq\phi(t,X)+\phi(t,Y)
for all

X,Y\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
. This can be done by proving the same inequality for

Positive system argument for Fact

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}{\rm tr}(X_{*})C^{\top}C=\mu X_{*}

First, we show that

\displaystyle\alpha Y_{*}A^{\top}+\alpha AY_{*}-\frac{1}{{\rm tr}(X_{*})}BR^{-1}B^{\top}

Remark 4.6
.

We observe that in establishing the spectral properties of the dynamical systems associated with algebraic Riccati equations – both in discrete and continuous time – controllability and observability play equally essential roles. Controllability contributes to reducing the spectral radius, while observability tends to increase it. Crucially, the absence of either property prevents us from guaranteeing the existence or uniqueness of solutions.

4.3
Riccati equations in positive control systems

In
[
20
]
, Rantzer considered the optimal control problem of a linear positive system with linear cost, see also

[
6
]

for the continuous-time counterpart. The Bellman equation – or algebraic Riccati equation – associated with this problem was derived:

p=s+A^{\top}p-E^{\top}|r+B^{\top}p|

(19)

where
s
,
A
,
E
,
r
and
B
are known matrices or vectors.
It is assumed that
E\geq 0
(point-wise non-negative),
s\geq E^{\top}r
, and
A\geq|BE|
. Here
|\cdot|
stands for point-wise absolute value.

Li and Rantzer studied the solution properties of the equation (
19
) in
[
17
]
under certain assumptions, similar to the controllability
and observability assumptions made in LQR control:

H1

there exists a matrix
L
, with
|L|\leq E
, such that
A-BL
is Schur stable;

H2

there holds:

(s-E^{\top}|r|)^{\top}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(A-|B|E)^{i}>0

As before, we adopt a positive systems approach to study the equation (
19
).
Consider the discrete-time linear homogeneous system

p_{k+1}=F(p_{k})

(20)

where

F(p)=\alpha(\mathds{1}^{\top}p)s+A^{\top}p-E^{\top}|\alpha(\mathds{1}^{\top}p)r+B^{\top}p|

for some positive constant
\alpha
.
Notice that
F
is concave, homogeneous, and that
F^{n}
satisfies (by
H2
)

p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash\{0\}\Rightarrow F^{n}(p)\in{\rm Int}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Thus
F
is sup-additive, implying that
F^{n}
is strictly order preserving. As a consequence, Theorem
1
implies the existence of a unique (up to positive scaling)
p_{*}\in{\rm Int}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, such that

F(p_{*})=\lambda p_{*}
for some positive constant
\lambda
. Next, we shall show that
\lambda

can be taken as
1
by adjusting the parameter
\alpha
. Indeed, when
\alpha=0
,

\displaystyle\lambda p_{*}

\displaystyle=A^{\top}p_{*}-E^{\top}|B^{\top}p_{*}|

\displaystyle\leq A^{\top}p_{*}-L^{\top}B^{\top}p_{*}

\displaystyle=(A-BL)^{\top}p_{*},

where we have used the assumption
|L|\leq E
in the second line. But
A-BL
is positive and Schur stable by
H1
, we must have
\lambda<1
.
By continuity,
\lambda<1
for all sufficiently small
\alpha>0
. On the other hand,
by
H2
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}p_{*}

\displaystyle=F^{n}(p_{*})

\displaystyle>\alpha(\mathds{1}^{\top}p_{*})(s-E^{\top}|r|)^{\top}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(A-|B|E)^{i}p_{*}

\displaystyle>0.

Hence
\lambda\to\infty
when
\alpha\to\infty
, as desired.
The existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
19

5
Conclusion

We have provided new insights into both Lyapunov and algebraic Riccati equations through the lens of positive systems theory. These findings establish interesting connections between algebraic equations and dynamical systems, demonstrating the efficacy of positive system theory.
Future research may explore time-varying equations and quantify error bounds of the iteration methods proposed in the paper.

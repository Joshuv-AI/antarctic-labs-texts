Quality, Patient Experience, and Service Gaps in Home Healthcare in Saudi Arabia: A Systematic Review Aligned with Vision 2030
Authors: Eman Alawad, Rasha M. Mahrous, Lubna A. Elshaib
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryObjectives:To assess the quality of home healthcare (HHC) services, patient experience, and existing service gaps in Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.Methods:This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) statement. PubMed, MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, CINAHL, and Google Scholar were searched for studies published between January 2011 and January 2025. Eligible studies included observational, qualitative, mixed-methods, and interventional studies conducted in Saudi Arabia that examined home healthcare quality, patient or caregiver experience, or service delivery gaps. Two reviewers independently screened studies, extracted data, and assessed methodological quality using the Quality Assessment with Diverse Studies (QuADS) tool. Disagreements were resolved by discussion and consensus. Because of heterogeneity in study designs, populations, and outcomes, findings were synthesized narratively.Results:Fifteen studies met the inclusion criteria. Overall, patient satisfaction levels were consistently high, particularly regarding provider communication and professional competence. Several specialized home-based programs demonstrated reduced hospital utilization and improved patient-reported outcomes. Nevertheless, recurring system-level concerns were identified, including limitations in service coordination, financial coverage, workforce support, and multidisciplinary availability.Conclusion:Home healthcare in Saudi Arabia demonstrates encouraging patient-centered and clinical outcomes, but important structural and operational deficiencies remain. Strengthening workforce capacity, financing mechanisms, coordination systems, digital health integration, and quality measurement is essential to support the expansion of home healthcare and align service delivery with the goals of Saudi Vision 2030.

Introduction

Home healthcare (HHC) is an increasingly important component of modern health systems, particularly in the care of older adults, patients with chronic diseases, individuals requiring rehabilitation, and those needing long-term supportive care. By delivering healthcare services in the home setting, HHC has the potential to improve access to care, reduce unnecessary hospital utilization, enhance continuity of care, and support patient-centered service delivery [1].

Globally, the demand for home-based care has increased substantially in recent years due to population aging, the growing burden of chronic disease, and pressure on hospital capacity [2]. The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic further accelerated interest in home healthcare and remote care models, highlighting the need for flexible, resilient, and patient-centered service delivery systems.

In Saudi Arabia, home healthcare has gained increasing relevance within the broader transformation of the health sector under Saudi Vision 2030, which emphasizes accessibility, efficiency, quality improvement, and health system transformation [34]. As healthcare delivery shifts toward more integrated and community-oriented models, evaluating the current status of home healthcare services is timely and important [5].

Despite the expansion of HHC services in Saudi Arabia, the evidence remains fragmented across different service models, populations, and outcomes. Existing studies have reported high levels of patient satisfaction and promising outcomes in some settings, but also identified important challenges related to workforce capacity, service coordination, financing, cultural competence, and access to specialized services [678910111213141516171819202122232425]. To date, however, a comprehensive synthesis of this literature specifically focused on Saudi Arabia remains lacking. Therefore, the aim of this systematic review was to synthesize the available evidence on the quality of home healthcare services, patient and caregiver experience, and service delivery gaps in Saudi Arabia, and to discuss the implications of these findings in the context of healthcare transformation under Vision 2030.

Methods

This systematic review was conducted and reported in accordance with PRISMA 2020 guidelines [26]. This review aimed to answer the following question: What is the current evidence regarding the quality of home healthcare services, patient and caregiver experience, and service delivery gaps in home healthcare in Saudi Arabia? Studies were eligible if they: Were conducted in Saudi Arabia, published between 2011 and 2025, Examined one or more of the following: quality of home healthcare services, patient or caregiver experience and satisfaction, structural, operational, or service delivery gaps in home healthcare. Observational, qualitative, mixed-methods, and interventional studies were included. Studies were excluded if they were: Review articles, Editorials, letters, conference abstracts, and commentaries, Studies conducted outside Saudi Arabia, Studies not specifically focused on home healthcare, and Studies with irrelevant outcomes.

A systematic literature search was conducted in the following electronic databases: PubMed, MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, CINAHL, Google Scholar. The final search was performed on 1st January 2025. The search strategy combined controlled vocabulary and free-text terms related to home healthcare and Saudi Arabia. Search terms included combinations of: “home healthcare”, “home health care”, “home care”, “patient satisfaction”, “patient experience”, “quality of care”, “service gaps”, “Saudi Arabia”. Manual reference screening was also performed. The full electronic search strategy for at least one database is provided in Supplementary Appendix 1. No formal search of grey literature sources was performed, and this is acknowledged as a limitation. The period from January 2011 to January 2025 was selected to capture contemporary evidence on home healthcare in Saudi Arabia over approximately the past decade, including studies published during a period of increasing healthcare modernization and leading into the Vision 2030 reform era [34].

Critical appraisal tool used in assessing the quality of the included studies. The Quality Assessment with Diverse Studies (QuADS) Tool (Harrison et al., 2021)

Records were imported into reference management software and duplicates removed. Titles and abstracts were screened, followed by full-text review. Two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts for relevance. Potentially eligible studies then underwent full-text review. Disagreements were resolved through discussion and consensus. Data were extracted independently by 2 reviewers using a standardized data extraction form. The following information was collected: author and year, study setting, study design, sample size and participant characteristics, data collection methods, key findings related to: quality of care, patient or caregiver experience, and service delivery gaps.

Methodological quality was assessed using the Quality Assessment with Diverse Studies (QuADS) tool, which is appropriate for systematic reviews including heterogeneous study designs [27]. The QuADS tool contains 13 items, each scored from 0 to 3, with higher scores indicating better methodological and reporting quality. The maximum possible score is 39. Quality assessment was used to inform interpretation of findings, but studies were not excluded solely on the basis of quality score, given the exploratory and policy-relevant nature of the review Table 1. Due to heterogeneity in design, populations, and outcomes, quantitative meta-analysis was not feasible. Where reported, correlation coefficients, p-values, and descriptive statistics were presented as described in original studies. A narrative thematic synthesis approach was employed. The synthesis was organized into 3 main domains: quality of home healthcare services, patient and caregiver experience, and service delivery gaps and system challenges. This review was not prospectively registered. This has been acknowledged as a limitation of the review.

Results

Study selection

A total of 213 records were identified through database searching. After removal of 98 duplicate records, 115 records remained for title and abstract screening. Of these, 45 records were excluded. The remaining 70 reports were sought for retrieval, of which 30 reports were not retrieved. Consequently, 40 full-text articles were assessed for eligibility. After full-text review, 25 articles were excluded for predefined reasons, and 15 studies were included in the final review (Fig. 1).

PRISMA flow diagram of the studies included in the current systematic review.

Methodological quality of included studies

Methodological quality, as assessed by the QuADS tool varied across studies [27]. Several studies demonstrated clear objectives, appropriate methods, and reasonably transparent reporting. Others were limited by incomplete reporting of sampling strategies, insufficient methodological detail, weak analytic transparency, or limited discussion of study limitations (Table 1).

Overall, the body of evidence can be considered moderate in quality. The included studies provide useful insight into the current state of home healthcare in Saudi Arabia, but findings should be interpreted cautiously because of heterogeneity in design, variable reporting quality, and frequent reliance on descriptive and satisfaction-based outcomes.

Characteristics of included studies

The 15 included studies were all conducted in Saudi Arabia and represented a range of methodological designs, including cross-sectional studies, retrospective observational studies, mixed-methods research, qualitative studies, and one randomized controlled trial [891011121314151617181920]. Sample sizes varied widely, ranging from 7 participants in a qualitative telehealth study [19] to 880 elderly patients in a retrospective descriptive study [15]. Narrative synthesis of findings

Quality of care. Across the included studies, home healthcare services in Saudi Arabia were generally perceived positively by patients and caregivers. Multiple studies reported high overall satisfaction scores, particularly in relation to provider professionalism, communication, interpersonal conduct, and technical competence [791116]. These findings suggest that, at the level of direct patient-provider interaction, many home healthcare services are viewed favorably.

Beyond perceived quality, several studies reported encouraging clinical and service outcomes. Home respiratory therapy programs were associated with improved clinical outcomes, reduced emergency and outpatient visits, and high satisfaction among recipients [13]. Home intravenous antibiotic therapy was reported to be safe and effective for selected non-life-threatening infections, with low complication rates and reduced hospital burden [14]. In addition, a home-based cardiac rehabilitation program demonstrated benefits in quality of life, physical function, metabolic parameters, and psychological well-being when compared with standard care [17].

These findings suggest that home healthcare in Saudi Arabia has the potential to support not only convenience and patient satisfaction, but also clinically meaningful outcomes when structured programs are implemented appropriately. However, quality was not consistently demonstrated across all dimensions of care. While interpersonal and relational aspects of care were commonly rated highly, structural components such as continuity, coordination, scope of services, and comprehensiveness of support were less consistently addressed [6101216].

Patient experience. Patient and caregiver experience emerged as a central theme across the literature. Many participants valued receiving care at home and reported overall satisfaction with the services provided [791216]. Positive experiences were commonly linked to provider behavior, communication style, and the comfort and convenience of home-based care. These findings indicate that patient experience in Saudi home healthcare is generally favorable, but not uniformly positive. The quality of communication, responsiveness, and cultural sensitivity appears to play a particularly important role in shaping patient and caregiver perceptions of care [1922]. Importantly, patient satisfaction should not be interpreted as a complete proxy for quality. Satisfaction measures are subjective and may be influenced by expectations, gratitude bias, and ceiling effects [21]. Therefore, findings related to satisfaction should be considered alongside structural, process, and clinical indicators of service quality.

Structural and operational gaps. A consistent finding across the included studies was the presence of important structural and operational gaps in home healthcare delivery in Saudi Arabia. These gaps can be grouped into several major areas.

Financing and insurance coverage. Some studies suggested that financial barriers remain an important challenge, with participants reporting inadequate income to meet healthcare needs and insufficient support from insurance systems [812]. These findings indicate that sustainable expansion of home healthcare may require stronger reimbursement pathways, improved financial protection for patients, and clearer policy frameworks for coverage.

Workforce and provider support. Workforce-related concerns were reported in several studies. These included staffing shortages, inadequate institutional support, low salaries, insufficient training, and occupational concerns such as infection risk [101225]. These findings suggest that workforce development is central to improving the quality and sustainability of home healthcare services.

Service coordination and continuity. Operational weaknesses were also evident in scheduling, coordination of visits, referral pathways, and continuity of care [1114]. Some studies indicated that patients were satisfied with staff technical skills but less satisfied with logistical aspects of service delivery [12]. This points to a gap between quality at the point of interaction and quality at the system level.

Limited scope of services. Although basic medical and nursing services appeared to be available in many settings, some studies reported deficiencies in physiotherapy, vocational therapy, social services, and broader rehabilitation support [18]. These gaps are especially relevant for older adults and individuals with chronic disabling conditions, whose needs often extend beyond basic nursing care [1519].

Communication, culture, and digital implementation. Communication and cultural barriers were highlighted across several studies, particularly in relation to telehealth and digital home care models [2124]. Language differences, concerns regarding privacy, variable digital literacy, and reliance on family caregivers influenced the implementation and acceptability of remote care programs [21]. These findings are particularly important given the emphasis on digital transformation within Vision 2030 [45].

Discussion

This systematic review provides a focused synthesis of the available evidence on home healthcare in Saudi Arabia and demonstrates that the current literature presents a mixed but informative picture. Overall, the evidence suggests that home healthcare services in Saudi Arabia are associated with high patient and caregiver satisfaction and, in selected settings, may deliver meaningful clinical and service benefits. However, the review also identifies substantial gaps in system organization, workforce capacity, financing, communication, and scope of service delivery.

One of the clearest findings across the literature was the generally favorable perception of home healthcare among patients and caregivers [9111418]. This is encouraging and suggests that home-based care is acceptable and valued within the Saudi context. However, high satisfaction should not be interpreted as evidence that all dimensions of care are strong. Satisfaction may coexist with weaknesses in access, coordination, continuity, and service comprehensiveness [23]. In addition, satisfaction is influenced by subjective expectations and may not adequately reflect the safety, effectiveness, or equity of care.

Another important finding is that some of the strongest evidence for home healthcare effectiveness in Saudi Arabia comes from specialized and structured programs, such as home respiratory therapy, home intravenous antibiotic therapy, and home-based cardiac rehabilitation [151619]. These programs suggest that home healthcare in Saudi Arabia has the potential to evolve beyond supportive or basic home visits toward more advanced, integrated, and clinically effective models of care.

At the same time, the broader system appears to face persistent structural challenges. Financing and insurance coverage remain insufficiently addressed [1014]. Workforce issues, including shortages, low salaries, limited support, and training needs, are recurrent [101224]. Gaps in coordination and continuity suggest that home healthcare is not yet consistently integrated with hospital and outpatient systems [1114]. In addition, the limited availability of rehabilitation and social support services indicates that some home healthcare programs may remain too narrow in scope to meet the multidimensional needs of patients receiving long-term care at home [171820].

These findings have direct relevance to Saudi Vision 2030 [34]. The health sector transformation agenda emphasizes improved access, quality, digital innovation, and integrated models of care. Based on the evidence synthesized in this review, several practical policy priorities emerge, including:

The review would also benefit from broader comparison with international evidence. Although the Saudi literature is context-specific, many of the identified challenges, including workforce limitations, fragmented coordination, and digital implementation barriers, are consistent with findings from health systems in transition toward community-based care [167]. This suggests that Saudi Arabia faces both common and context-specific challenges, but also has a unique opportunity to scale home healthcare within an actively reforming national health system.

Study limitation

This review has several limitations. First, the number of published studies addressing home healthcare services in Saudi Arabia remains limited, which restricts the breadth of available evidence [3456]. Second, the included studies demonstrated methodological heterogeneity in design, outcome measures, and reporting standards, which limited direct comparability and precluded quantitative meta-analysis. In addition, most studies were conducted within single institutions or specific regions, potentially limiting the generalizability of findings to the broader national context. Variability in the measurement of patient satisfaction and quality indicators may also influence the interpretation of results. Additionally, this review was not prospectively registered, which limits transparency compared with registered systematic reviews. Finally, because many included studies were cross-sectional or observational, causal inferences should be made with caution.

In conclusion, HHC services play a crucial role in enhancing access to care, patient satisfaction, and clinical outcomes in Saudi Arabia. However, significant gaps remain in service coordination, financing, workforce support, and communication. Addressing these challenges through policy reform, workforce development, interdisciplinary collaboration, and digital health optimization is essential for achieving the objectives of Saudi Vision 2030.

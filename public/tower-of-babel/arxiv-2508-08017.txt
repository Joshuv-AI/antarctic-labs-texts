Structure of Metric 1 1 -currents: approximation by normal currents and representation results
David Bate, Emanuele Caputo, Jakub Takáč, Phoebe Valentine, Pietro Wald

Abstract
. We prove the 1 1 -dimensional flat chain conjecture in any complete and quasiconvex metric space, namely that metric 1 1 -currents can be approximated in mass by normal 1 1 -currents. The proof relies on a new Banach space isomorphism theorem, relating metric 1 1 -currents and their boundaries to the Arens-Eells space. As a by-product, any metric 1 1 -current in a complete and separable metric space can be represented as the integral superposition of oriented 1 1 -rectifiable sets, thus dropping a finite dimensionality condition from previous results of Schioppa [ 27 , 28 ] . The connection between the flat chain conjecture and the representation result is provided by a structure theorem for metric 1 1 -currents in Banach spaces, showing that any such current can be realised as the restriction to a Borel set of a boundaryless normal 1 1 -current. This generalizes, to any Banach space, the 1 1 -dimensional case of a recent result of Alberti-Marchese in Euclidean spaces [ 1 ] . The argument of Alberti-Marchese requires the strict polyhedral approximation theorem of Federer for normal 1 1 -currents, which we obtain in Banach spaces.

1.
Introduction

The need for a generalized notion of a surface emerged from the desire to tackle geometric variational problems which resisted smooth techniques, most notably Plateau’s problem. Federer and Fleming realised that de Rham’s notion of currents, which was developed in duality with differential forms, was in fact a compactification of the class of oriented smooth manifolds embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
19
]
and so provided a notion of a generalised surface highly suitable to such variational problems. We shall refer to these currents as
classical currents
.

To tackle such problems in metric spaces, Ambrosio and Kirchheim defined a notion of
metric currents
for ambient metric spaces in
[
3
]
, but their construction is not a direct generalisation of a classical current because it is necessary to make stronger requirements of a metric current to account for the reduced structure of the space.
Indeed, the theory of classical currents is developed in duality with differential forms, however, for a metric space definition to be suitably general, it is necessary to move away from the strong assumption of a differentiable structure and thus differential forms.
Instead, we consider
(k+1)
-tuples of Lipschitz functions
(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})
, where
f
is bounded; a collection which we denote
D^{k}({\rm X})
.
A
k
-metric current, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, is then defined as a functional on
D^{k}({\rm X})
which is multilinear, jointly continuous, local and satisfies a finite mass condition (all to be explicitly defined in Section
2
). The locality in particular ensures that the functional depends in a weak sense on the derivatives of the last
k
entries, echoing the action on the differential form
f{\mathrm{d}}\pi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\mathrm{d}}\pi_{k}
. We will denote the space of metric
k
-currents on
{\rm X}
by
\mathbf{M}_{k}({\rm X})
.

Metric currents have been used successfully in various problems in analysis and geometry (
[
6
,
22
,
30
,
32
]
). However, their structure, even on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is only partially understood. The most famous conjecture in this vein was formulated in Ambrosio and Kirchheim’s foundational paper and is now known as the flat chain conjecture
[
3
, Pag. 68]
.
The question is whether metric
k
-currents on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
correspond to classical
k
-flat chains for
1\leq k\leq n
.
Partial progress has been made: positive results are known for
k=1
and
k=n
. The latter is a corollary of a deep result by De Philippis and Rindler
[
15
]
where they prove that
n
-dimensional metric currents in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
correspond to the space of Lebesgue integrable functions.
The case of
k=1
was proved by Schioppa in
[
28
]
.
The pivotal tool in his proof is a powerful representation result, which, in the case of
k=1
, states that every metric
1
-current can be written as a superposition of oriented
1
-rectifiable sets.
This representation result holds in metric spaces satisfying a technical finite-dimensionality condition which is satisfied, for example, if the ambient space is metrically doubling. Due to the generality of his representation result, Schioppa in fact proves something more general than the Euclidean flat chain conjecture. The author proves that normal
1
-currents are flat (in fact, mass) dense in the space of metric
1
-currents in quasiconvex metric spaces satisfying the same standing technical assumption.

In Euclidean space, it is known that normal currents are flat chains, thus Schioppa’s density result is equivalent to the flat chain conjecture. Schioppa’s result can thus be seen as answering a metric version of the conjecture. Thus the metric analogue of the flat chain conjecture asks whether normal currents are dense under the flat norm in the space of metric currents.

Conjecture 1.1

(Flat chain conjecture)
.

Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, every
k
-current on a metric space
{\rm X}
can be approximated by normal
k
-currents under the flat norm:

\overline{\{\text{normal }k\text{-currents}\}}^{\text{Flat norm}}=\mathbf{M}_{k}({\rm X}).

It turns out that a connectivity assumption is inherently required: for example, a Cantor set of positive Lebesgue measure in the real line is a metric space which supports no non-trivial normal
1
-currents, but the space of metric
1
-currents is rich. However the finite dimensionality assumption is not apriori necessary. The two main result of this work are the
1
-dimensional case of the flat chain conjecture and the representation formula for metric
1
-currents as a superposition of oriented
1
-rectifiable sets. Both results are obtained in complete metric spaces without the finite dimensionality assumption. Quasiconvexity of the underlying space is required for the flat chain conjecture, while the representation formula holds in any complete metric space.

A note on related work.

While writing this paper, we became aware that Arroyo-Rabasa and Bouchitté have simultaneously and independently obtained results related to those presented here
[
5
]
.
To the best of our knowledge, their techniques draw on optimal transport and functions of bounded variation, whereas ours are typically linked to metric geometry and analysis on metric spaces.

1.1.
Flat chain conjecture

Our approach to the
1

It can be defined as the completion of

As such, elements of

-currents.
In fact, for every

Related to this is the recent work of De Pauw

-currents of finite mass

Theorem 1.2

(Isomorphism theorem, cf. Theorem
3.2
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete and separable metric space.
Then

\partial\colon\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})/\mathrm{ker}(\partial)\to\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})

is a Banach space isomorphism if and only if
{\rm X}
is quasiconvex.
In the latter case, we have

(1)

\mathrm{qc}(X)^{-1}\|\partial T\|_{\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})}\leq\|[T]\|\leq\|\partial T\|_{\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})}\quad\text{for every $T\in\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})$,}

with the constants in (
1
) being optimal.
2
2

2

Unless the metric space
{\rm X}
consists of a point,
in which case the optimal constants are
0
.
Here

and
[T]\in\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})/\ker(\partial)
denotes the equivalence class of
T\in\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})

in
\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})/\ker(\partial)

Given a metric current

,
\partial T

may be approximated in

by a sequence of molecules

The invertibility of

ensures that we can find

In particular,

is a sequence of normal

Theorem 1.3

(cf. Corollary
3.4
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete and quasiconvex metric space.
Let
T\in\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})
and suppose its mass measure is inner regular by compact sets.
Then there is a sequence

of normal

-currents such that

\mathbb{M}(T-T_{i})\rightarrow 0

1.2.
Old and new representation results

The simplest and most concrete examples of metric
1
-currents consist of Lipschitz curves.
For any metric space
{\rm X}
and Lipschitz curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}

\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket(f,\pi):=\int_{0}^{1}(f\circ\gamma)_{t}(\pi\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}t,\qquad(f,\pi)\in D^{1}({\rm X}),

-current.
It is then natural to wonder how far can a metric

More precisely, one might look for ‘concrete’ representations of metric

Such questions are not new and a lot is already known.

In particular, they prove that, on a complete and separable metric space

where

[
24

25
]
for details.
(Representations with good properties of the boundary are also available;

[

29
]
.
See also
[

For integral

1.3.
Main ideas on the proof of the representation theorem

We comment on the tools needed to prove Theorem
1.4
. The proof is divided in two steps.

Step 1: Generalisation of a result of Alberti-Marchese to Banach spaces.

The starting point is
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
,
where it is shown that for any
\varepsilon>0
and classical
k
-dimensional flat chain
T
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq k<n
,
there is a boundaryless normal
k
-current
N
and a Borel set
B

so that
T=N\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{B}$}
and
\mathbb{M}(N)\leq(2+\varepsilon)\mathbb{M}(T)
.

Using Theorem
1.3
, we are able to extend the

1
-dimensional case of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]

to arbitrary Banach spaces,
showing that any metric
1
-current is the restriction of a normal
1
-current as above.
For our application (see Step 2), we need to be able to take
B
closed. We prove that this is possible if we additionally assume that the metric
1
-current
T
sits in an affine hyperplane in the ambient Banach space.

The proof of
[
1
]
crucially relies on
the classical polyhedral approximation theorem
[
18
, Theorem 4.1.23]
,
which is ultimately based on the fact that, for
N\in\mathbf{N}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
there are polyhedral chains
\{P_{i}\}_{i}

so that

\mathbb{F}(N-P_{i})\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{M}(P_{i})\to\mathbb{M}(N).

For
k=1
, we provide a proof of the above in metric spaces
admitting a conical geodesic bicombing (which include Banach and
{\sf CAT}(0)
spaces, see

[
16
]
and Section
4
),
where polyhedral chains are replaced with chains of geodesics
(segments if the space is linear); see Theorem
4.5
.
For
k\geq 2
, the above strict approximation result fails in every infinite-dimensional
Hilbert space and, therefore, it is currently unclear if

[
1
, Theorem 1.1]

can be extended beyond finite dimensional Euclidean space. The failure of the approximation result for
k\geq 2
is the subject of an ongoing work of the authors. Since this result is inconsequential for the present paper, we omit further comments.

Step 2: Application of Paolini-Stepanov representation theorem.

Given a metric
1
-current
T
on a complete and separable metric space
{\rm X}
, we embed
{\rm X}
isometrically in a separable Banach space
\mathbb{B}
and we consider the push-forward current. Up to enlarging the Banach space, we may assume that
T
is supported in an affine hyperplane of
\mathbb{B}
. We now apply Step 1 in

and find a boundaryless normal
1
-current
N\in\mathbf{N}_{1}(\mathbb{B})
and a closed set

so that
N\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{C}$}=T
.
Finally, restricting the representation (
2
)
of

to

5
) for

.
The fact that
C
is closed ensures that the restricted curve fragments are still

.

Preliminaries

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space. Given
A\subset{\rm X}
, we define

{\rm diam}(A,{\sf d}):=\sup\{{\sf d}(x,y):\,x,y\in A\}
.
We denote with
\mathcal{M}({\rm X})
the convex cone of nonnegative finite Borel measures on
{\rm X}
.

Let
C([0,1],{\rm X})
be the space of continuous curves
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
,
equipped with the uniform distance
{\sf d}_{\infty}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}):=\max_{t\in[0,1]}d(\gamma_{t},\gamma^{\prime}_{t})
,
where
\gamma_{t}
denotes the value of
\gamma
at
t
.
We also set, for
\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X})
,

\ell(\gamma):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\sf d}(\gamma_{t_{i-1}},\gamma_{t_{i}})\,:\,a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=b,\,N\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

and call it

of
\gamma
.
We say that
\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X})

.

Given a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
, we define the

{\sf d}_{\ell}(x,y):=\inf\left\{\ell(\gamma):\,\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X}),\,\gamma_{0}=x,\,\gamma_{1}=y\right\}.

Notice that we do not assume
{\rm X}
to be rectifiably path-connected, so
{\sf d}_{\ell}
may assume extended values.

We say that
\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X})
is an
L
-quasigeodesic

if
\ell(\gamma)\leq L{\sf d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
, and

if it is
1

We denote the space of all geodesics by
{\rm Geo}({\rm X})
.

Given

, we say that a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is

if for every

there exists an

-quasigeodesic

such that
\gamma_{0}=x
and
\gamma_{1}=y
. We say that a metric space is

if it is
1
-quasiconvex.

The space of
curve fragments
on a metric space
{\rm X}
is defined as

\Gamma({\rm X}):=\{\gamma\colon K\to{\rm X}\colon K\subset[0,1]\text{ compact},\gamma\ 1\text{-Lipschitz}\}

and, for
\gamma\in\Gamma({\rm X})
, we let
{\rm dom}(\gamma)
denote its domain,
so that
\gamma\colon{\rm dom}(\gamma)\to{\rm X}
.
Further, given a set
E\subset{\rm X}
, we denote

\Gamma({\rm X},E):=\{\gamma\in\Gamma({\rm X})\colon{\rm im}(\gamma)\cap E\neq\varnothing\},

where

{\rm im}(\gamma)
denotes the image of
\gamma

Letting
{\rm gr}(\gamma):=\{(t,\gamma_{t})\in\mathbb{R}\times{\rm X}:t\in{\rm dom}(\gamma)\}

\gamma
, we endow
\Gamma({\rm X})
with the topology induced by the Hausdorff distance on

\mathbb{R}\times{\rm X}
.

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma({\rm X})

|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|:=\lim_{{\rm dom}(\gamma)\ni s\to t}\frac{{\sf d}(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{t})}{|t-s|}

.

We define the length of a curve fragment

which is consistent with the previous definition of length of curve.

\Gamma({\rm X},E)=\cup_{n}\Gamma({\rm X},K_{n})

Fix

By

which implies that

is Borel measurable.
We claim that

converges pointwise to the map

,
\gamma\in\Gamma({\rm X},E)
.
Fix
\gamma\in\Gamma({\rm X},E)
and let

so that

Let

\mathcal{T}\subset\overline{\gamma^{-1}(E)}\subset[0,1]

We denote by

We denote the set of functions from

to

whose Lipschitz constant is finite with

.

Given a distinguished point

, we denote by

Remark 2.2
.

We can endow

For

if

{\rm X}

and

If

of

as

2.1.
Arens-Eells space

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space.
We call a measure
m
on
{\rm X}
a
molecule
if it is a finite linear combination of Dirac measures with zero average. Any molecule
m
is of the form

m=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}(\delta_{p_{i}}-\delta_{q_{i}}),

where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
p_{i},q_{i}\in{\rm X}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We use the shorthand notation
m_{p_{i},q_{i}}=\delta_{p_{i}}-\delta_{q_{i}}
.
The
Arens-Eells norm
of a molecule
m
is

\|m\|_{\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})}:=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}{\sf d}(p_{i},q_{i}):\,m=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}m_{p_{i},q_{i}}\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all possible representations of
m
.
We define the
Arens-Eells space

\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})
as the completion of the space of molecules with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\textnormal{\AE}}
. With some abuse of notation, we denote with the same symbol the norm on the completion.

For a distinguished point
0\in{\rm X}
, we identify each molecule
m
with the linear functional on
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}({\rm X})
given by

m(\pi)=\int\pi\,{\mathrm{d}}m.

Following
[
31
, Chapter 3]
, we see that
\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})
is isometric to a predual of
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}({\rm X})
in the following sense.
For each
\pi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}({\rm X})
, there is the linear functional
I(\pi)
on the space of molecules:

(6)

I(\pi)(m)=m(\pi).

This functional is continuous and extends uniquely to a functional (which we denote with the same symbol)

I(\pi)\colon\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})\to\mathbb{R}.

The map
I
is an isometry of Banach spaces from
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}({\rm X})
to
\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})^{*}
. We shall omit writing out the map
I
and identify
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}({\rm X})=\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})^{*}
.

We denote by
\epsilon\colon\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})\to(\operatorname{Lip}_{0}({\rm X}))^{*}
the canonical embedding into the second dual space. If
{\rm X}
is separable, then so is
\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})
(and conversely) and therefore we may use the Kreǐn-Shmul’yan theorem (or rather, its immediate corollary
[
14
, Corollary 12.8]
) to assert the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
{\rm X}
is a separable metric space, then a linear functional
m\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}({\rm X})^{*}
is an element of
\epsilon(\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X}))
if and only if it is sequentially continuous with respect to pointwise convergence of bounded sequences.

In light of the previous lemma, we shall identify functionals in
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}({\rm X})^{*}
sequentially continuous with respect to pointwise convergence of bounded sequences with the space
\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X})
.

2.2.
Metric currents

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
D^{k}({\rm X}):=\operatorname{Lip}_{b}({\rm X})\times(\operatorname{Lip}({\rm X}))^{k}
. The definition of metric currents was given in
[
3
]
and is as follows.

Definition 2.4

(Metric currents)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete metric space and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that a multilinear functional
T\colon D^{k}({\rm X})\to\mathbb{R}
is a
metric
k
-current
provided it satisfies the following axioms:

•

(Locality) For every
(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})\in D^{k}({\rm X})
, we have
T(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})=0
if there exists
i=1,\dots,k
such that
\pi_{i}
is constant on a neighborhood of
\{f\neq 0\}
.

•

(Joint continuity) Given
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{b}({\rm X})
and
\pi_{i}^{n},\pi_{i}\in\operatorname{Lip}({\rm X})
such that

\sup_{i}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Lip}\pi_{i}^{n}<\infty
and
\pi_{i}^{n}(x)\to\pi(x)
for every
x\in{\rm X}
and every
i
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}T(f,\pi_{1}^{n},\dots,\pi_{k}^{n})=T(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}).

•

(Finite-mass condition) There exists a nonnegative finite Borel measure such that

(7)

\left|T(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})\right|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{LIP}(\pi_{i})\int|f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu.

The minimal measure
\mu
that satisfies (
7
) is called the

of
T
and is denoted by
\|T\|
.

We call
\mathbb{M}(T):=\|T\|({\rm X})
the mass of
T
. The vector space of all metric
k
-currents is a Banach space when endowed with the norm
\mathbb{M}(\cdot)
.

The
boundary
of a metric
k
-current

is the functional
\partial T\colon D^{k-1}({\rm X})\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

\partial T(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k-1}):=T(1,f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}).

We say that a metric
k
-current is
normal
, provided
\partial T
is a metric
(k-1)
-current, or equivalently, if
\partial T
has finite mass. We denote the vector space of all normal
k
-currents by
\mathbf{N}_{k}({\rm X})
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d}_{\rm X})
and
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})
be two complete metric spaces. For every Lipschitz map
\varphi\colon{\rm X}\to{\rm Y}
and
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}({\rm X})
, we define a metric
k
-current
\varphi_{*}T
, called the

of

\varphi

\varphi_{*}T(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})=T(f\circ\varphi,\pi_{1}\circ\varphi,\dots,\pi_{k}\circ\varphi).

Given a complete metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
and a closed set

,

denotes the space of metric

-currents in the metric space

\Phi\colon\{T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(C):\,\|T\|\text{ is inner regular}\}\to\{T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(X):\,{\rm supp}(T)\subset C,\,\|T\|\text{ is inner regular}\},

where both spaces are endowed with the corresponding mass norm.
For a proof of this fact, see Proposition
B.4
in the appendix of this paper.

Given a complete metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

, we define the
flat norm
of

as

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}(T):

\displaystyle=\inf\{\mathbb{M}(Q)+\mathbb{M}(\partial Q-T):\,Q\in\mathbf{N}_{k+1}({\rm X})\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\mathbb{M}(S)+\mathbb{M}(R):\,S\in\mathbf{N}_{k+1}({\rm X}),\,R\in\mathbf{M}_{k}({\rm X}),\,T=\partial S+R\}.

In particular, notice that if

, then, by the very definition of the flat norm,

\mathbb{F}(T)=\inf\{\mathbb{M}(S)+\mathbb{M}(R):\,S\in\mathbf{N}_{k+1}({\rm X}),\,R\in\mathbf{N}_{k}({\rm X}),\,T=\partial S+R\}.

In the specific case of

, we introduce the following notation.
Given

with

, we define
\llbracket[a,b]\times\{c\}\rrbracket

\llbracket[a,b]\times\{c\}\rrbracket(f,\pi):=\int_{a}^{b}f(t,c)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}(\pi(t,c))}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\,{\mathrm{d}}t

and given

with

we define

\llbracket\{a\}\times[b,c]\rrbracket(f,\pi)=\int_{b}^{c}f(a,t)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}(\pi(a,t))}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

Notice that the last current, with the same definition, is a metric

We will rely on the following representation result for normal

Theorem 2.5

(

Let

2.2.1.
Pointwise estimates on metric currents

The mass estimate in (
7
) for currents self-improves to a pointwise estimate with the pointwise Lipschitz constant.
These estimates will be useful for pointwise estimates in the homotopy formula in Section
4
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete and separable metric space,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}({\rm X})
.
Then

(8)

|T(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})|\leq\int|f|\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Lip}\pi_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|,

where
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\infty}({\rm X})
and
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}\in\operatorname{Lip}({\rm X})
.

Proof.

Step 1: Case
k=1
.

First, observe that if
\pi\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function and
L,r>0
, then

(9)

S:=\{x\in{\rm X}\colon|\pi(x)-\pi(y)|\leq L{\sf d}(x,y),y\in B(x,r)\}

is a closed set.
To see this, suppose
x\in{\rm X}
is a limit point of
S
,
y\in B(x,r)
, and fix
0<\varepsilon<r
.
Then, we find
z\in S\cap B(x,\varepsilon)
with
{\sf d}(x,z)<r-{\sf d}(x,y)
.
Since
{\sf d}(z,y)<r
and
{\sf d}(z,x)<r
, the definition of
S
implies

\displaystyle|\pi(x)-\pi(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|\pi(x)-\pi(z)|+|\pi(z)-\pi(y)|\leq L\varepsilon+L{\sf d}(z,y)

\displaystyle\leq 2L\varepsilon+L{\sf d}(x,y).

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this shows that
S
is closed.

We now prove the claim.
Let
\pi\in\operatorname{Lip}({\rm X})
, fix
\varepsilon>0
, and let
\{q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be a dense subset of the non-negative reals.
For
i,j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, set

S_{i,j}:=\{x\in{\rm X}\colon|\pi(x)-\pi(y)|\leq(q_{i}+\varepsilon){\sf d}(x,y),y\in B(x,1/j),\text{ and }|\operatorname{Lip}\pi(x)-q_{i}|<\varepsilon\}.

Since
x\mapsto\operatorname{Lip}\pi(x)
is Borel, the remark at the beginning of the proof
shows that
S_{i,j}
is a Borel set.
Also, by definition of
\operatorname{Lip}\pi
, it is not difficult to see that the collection
S_{i,j}
covers
{\rm X}
.
Since
{\rm X}
is separable, we can cover each
S_{i,j}
with countably many
Borel sets
S_{i,j,l}
with
\mathrm{diam}(S_{i,j,l})<1/j
.
Observe that, for
x,y,z\in S_{i,j,l}
, it holds

|\pi(x)-\pi(y)|\leq(q_{i}+\varepsilon){\sf d}(x,y)\leq(\operatorname{Lip}\pi(z)+2\varepsilon){\sf d}(x,y).

Next, let
\{\tilde{E}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be a re-indexing of
\{S_{i,j,l}\}_{i,j,l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}

and set
E_{n}:=\tilde{E}_{n}\setminus\cup_{m<n}\tilde{E}_{m}
.
Then,
\{E_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
is a countable disjoint Borel cover of
{\rm X}
, satisfying

(10)

\operatorname{LIP}(\pi|_{E_{n}})\leq\operatorname{Lip}\pi(x)+2\varepsilon,\qquad x\in E_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

Let
\pi_{n}\in\operatorname{Lip}({\rm X})
be an extension of
\pi|_{E_{n}}
having the same Lipschitz constant.
By the continuity and locality properties
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
of metric currents we have

(11)

T(f,\pi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{E_{n}}f,\pi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T(\chi_{E_{n}}f,\pi_{n}).

By
Eq.

11
,
Eq.

10
, and the definition of
\pi_{n}
, we have

(12)

\displaystyle|T(f,\pi)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|T(\chi_{E_{n}}f,\pi_{n})|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{LIP}(\pi_{n})\int_{E_{n}}|f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{n}}(\operatorname{Lip}\pi+2\varepsilon)|f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\operatorname{Lip}\pi|f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|+2\varepsilon\|f\|_{\infty}\|T\|({\rm X}).

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, the above concludes the proof of the case
k=1
.

Step 2: Case
k>1
.

We proceed by induction on
k
.
We can assume
k>1
and that
Eq.

8
holds for
k-1
.
Let
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}({\rm X})
and, for
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\infty}({\rm X})
,
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}\in\operatorname{Lip}({\rm X})
,
set

S_{\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}}(f,\pi_{1}):=R_{\pi_{1}}(f,\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}):=T(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}).

Observe that
S_{\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}}\in\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})
,
R_{\pi_{1}}\in\mathbf{M}_{k-1}({\rm X})

and
\|R_{\pi_{1}}\|\leq\operatorname{LIP}(\pi_{1})\|T\|
.
Hence, the induction hypothesis gives

|S_{\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}}(f,\pi_{1})|=|R_{\pi_{1}}(f,\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k})|\leq\operatorname{LIP}(\pi_{1})\int|f|\prod_{i=2}^{k}\operatorname{Lip}\pi_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|,

which implies

(13)

\|S_{\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}}\|(B)\leq\int_{B}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\operatorname{Lip}\pi_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|,

for
B\subset{\rm X}
Borel.
Since
S_{\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}}
is a metric
1
-current, the first step of the proof shows

|S_{\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}}(f,\pi_{1})|\leq\int|f|\operatorname{Lip}\pi_{1}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|S_{\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}}\|.

The above inequality and
Eq.

13
conclude the proof.
∎

Inequality (
8
) can be further improved, as we now describe.
Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
be a complete and separable metric space endowed with a Radon
measure which is finite on bounded sets.
In
[
8
]
, it is shown that
it is possible to associate to each Lipschitz function

a Borel function
|Df|_{*}
, called

on curve fragments, except for
a negligible set of curve fragments.
For a Lipschitz function
f\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
, it holds

|Df|_{*}\leq\operatorname{Lip}f\qquad\mu\mathrm{-a.e.}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{Lip}f\leq\operatorname{Lip}_{a}f\text{ everywhere}.

In
[
8
]
, the following approximation result is proven.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
8
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mu)
be a complete and separable metric measure space. Let
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{b}({\rm X})
with bounded support. Then there exists a sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j}\subset\operatorname{Lip}_{b}({\rm X})
with bounded support
with
\operatorname{LIP}(f_{j})\leq\operatorname{LIP}(f)
and

|f_{j}|\leq|f|
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, sucht that

f_{j}\to f
pointwise everywhere (and so uniformly on compact sets)

\operatorname{Lip}_{a}\,f_{j}\to|Df|_{*}
and
|Df_{j}|_{*}\to|Df|_{*}
pointwise
\mu
-a.e.

A direct application of Theorem
2.7

and Proposition
2.6

yields the following corollary.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete and separable metric space,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and

. Then

(14)

|T(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})|\leq\int|f|\prod_{i=1}^{k}|D\pi_{i}|_{*}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|

for
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\infty}({\rm X})
, and
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}\in\operatorname{Lip}({\rm X})
,
where
|D\pi_{i}|_{*}
is the

-upper gradient of
\pi_{i}

({\rm X},{\sf d},\|T\|)

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in{\rm X}

and, for

, define
\varphi_{R}(x):=(2-\frac{{\sf d}(x,x_{0})}{R})^{+}\wedge 1
,

and
\pi_{i}^{R}:=\pi_{i}\varphi^{2R}
for

(15)

|T(f^{R},\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})|=|T(f^{R},\pi_{1}^{R},\dots,\pi_{k}^{R})|\leq\int|f^{R}|\prod_{i=1}^{k}|D\pi^{R}_{i}|_{*}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|=\int|f^{R}|\prod_{i=1}^{k}|D\pi_{i}|_{*}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|,

,
and Theorem

In particular, the last equality follows from

.
By letting

∎

Remark 2.9
.

Let

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})

We assume

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\eta_{x_{i},y_{j}}=\eta,

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a complete and quasiconvex metric space.
Let

and suppose its mass measure is inner regular by compact sets.
Then there is a sequence

of normal

-currents such that

.

Proof.

We first reduce to the separable case.
Since
\|T\|
is inner regular by compact sets, it is concentrated on a
\sigma
-compact

, which in particular is separable.
By
[
20

, there is a closed, separable, quasiconvex

containing

Applying

such that

, where
\iota\colon F\to{\rm X}
is the inclusion map.

Recall that molecules are dense in

and so there exists a sequence

of molecules converging to

Since

satisfies the assumptions of

, we have

The definition of quotient norm reads

4.
Homotopy formulas and polyhedral approximation theorems for normal currents

The goal of this section is to prove a strict polyhedral approximation theorem for normal
1
-currents in metric spaces admitting a conical geodesic bicombing. This class of metric spaces includes Banach spaces and
{\sf CAT}(0)
spaces. The main tool to prove this result is a homotopy formula in the same setting.
In the last part of the section, we prove a homotopy formula for normal
k
-currents in Banach spaces for
k\geq 1
. This last result will be employed in Section
5
.

4.1.
Strict polyhedral approximation theorem for
k=1
in metric spaces admitting a conical geodesic bicombing

A
geodesic bicombing
on a geodesic metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a map
\sigma\colon{\rm X}\times{\rm X}\times[0,1]\to{\rm X}
such that, for
x,y\in{\rm X}
,
\sigma_{x,y}:=\sigma(x,y,\cdot)
is a
(constant speed) geodesic from
x
to
y
. It is
conical
if it additionally satisfies

{\sf d}(\sigma_{x,y}(t),\sigma_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(t))\leq(1-t){\sf d}(x,x^{\prime})+t{\sf d}(y,y^{\prime}),\qquad t\in[0,1]

and for all
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in{\rm X}
.

This condition was proven in injective metric spaces in
[
23
, Proposition 3.8]
and then deeply studied in
[
16
]
; it includes convex subsets of
Banach spaces (where the bicombing is given by line segments) and
{\sf CAT}(0)
spaces (where the bicombing is given by the unique geodesic connecting two points).

Lemma 4.1

(Simplified homotopy lemma in conical geodesic bicombing metric spaces)

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})

\displaystyle|S(\chi_{B},\pi_{1},\pi_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\chi_{B}\circ H\left|\partial_{1}(\pi_{1}\circ H)\partial_{2}(\pi_{2}\circ H)-\partial_{2}(\pi_{1}\circ H)\partial_{1}(\pi_{2}\circ H)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{L}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\chi_{B}\circ H|\partial_{1}H||\partial_{2}H|{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{L}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\sf d}_{\infty}(\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1})\,\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\chi_{B}\circ H(s,t)\big((1-t)|\dot{\gamma^{0}_{s}}|+t|\dot{\gamma^{1}_{s}}|\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}s{\mathrm{d}}t.

Hence, for any Borel partition of
{\rm X}

\{B_{i}\}_{i}
and
1
-Lipschitz maps
\pi^{i}_{1},\pi^{i}_{2}\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{i}|S(\chi_{B_{i}},\pi^{i}_{1},\pi^{i}_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq 2{\sf d}_{\infty}(\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1})\sum_{i}\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|\chi_{B_{i}}\circ H(s,t)|\big((1-t)|\dot{\gamma^{0}_{s}}|+t|\dot{\gamma^{1}_{s}}|\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}s{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=2{\sf d}_{\infty}(\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1})\int_{[0,1]^{2}}(1-t)|\dot{\gamma^{0}_{s}}|+t|\dot{\gamma^{1}_{s}}|\,{\mathrm{d}}s{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle={\sf d}_{\infty}(\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1})\big(\ell(\gamma^{0})+\ell(\gamma^{1})\big),

which proves
\mathbb{M}(S)\leq{\sf d}_{\infty}(\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1})\big(\ell(\gamma^{0})+\ell(\gamma^{1})\big)
.
It remains to show measurabily of
R_{\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1}}
and
S_{\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1}}
.
Conical geodesic bicombings are always continuous
[
16
]
.
Since the evaluation map
(t,\gamma)\in[0,1]\times\operatorname{Lip}([0,1];{\rm X})\mapsto\gamma_{t}\in{\rm X}
is continuous, it follows that

(s,t,\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1})\mapsto\sigma(t,\gamma^{0}_{s},\gamma^{1}_{s})
is also continuous.
Then,
(s,t,\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1})\mapsto f\circ H_{\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1}}(s,t)
and
(s,t,\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1})\mapsto\partial_{i}(\pi_{j}\circ H_{\gamma^{0},\gamma^{1}})(s,t)

are Borel.
Finally, Fubini’s theorem allows us to conclude.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

We note that it is necessary to assume some connectivity of the metric space in order to have the continuity of the map

\operatorname{Lip}([0,1],{\rm X})\ni\gamma\mapsto\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\in\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})

on sets of equibounded length.
Here, the domain, as a subset of
C([0,1],{\rm X})
, is endowed with the uniform topology and the target space with the topology induced by the flat norm.

Indeed, given
0<\alpha<1
, consider the Rickman rug, i.e. the metric space
(\mathbb{R}^{2},{\sf d}_{\alpha})
, where

{\sf d}_{\alpha}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})):=\max\{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha},|y-y^{\prime}|\},

where
|\cdot|
is the Euclidean distance in
\mathbb{R}
. In such a case, two vertical lines can be arbitrarily close, but the flat distance is bounded away from zero. More specifically, let
s>0
and
T_{s}:=\llbracket\{0\}\times[0,1]\rrbracket-\llbracket\{s\}\times[0,1]\rrbracket
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{N}_{2}({\rm X})
,
R\in\mathbf{N}_{1}({\rm X})
such that
T_{s}=\partial S+R
. In particular,
\partial R=\partial T_{s}=\delta_{(0,1)}-\delta_{(0,0)}+\delta_{(s,0)}-\delta_{(s,1)}=:m
.
We claim that
\|\partial T_{s}\|_{\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X},{\sf d}_{\ell})}=2
.
Given
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{1}({\rm X},{\sf d}_{\ell})
, we have that
\max\{|f(0,0)-f(0,1)|,|f(1,0)-f(1,1)|\}\leq 1
, thus

\int f\,{\mathrm{d}}m\leq 2,

proving
\|\partial T_{s}\|_{\textnormal{\AE}({\rm X},{\sf d}_{\ell})}\leq 2
. By choosing
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{1}({\rm X},{\sf d}_{\ell})
defined as
f(x,y):=y\chi_{\{x\leq s/2\}}-y\chi_{\{x>s/2\}}
we get the converse inequality.
The claim, together with Lemma
3.3
gives
\mathbb{M}(R_{s})\geq 2
.
This implies
\mathbb{F}(T_{s})\geq 2
, and since
\mathbb{F}(T_{s})\leq\mathbb{M}(T_{s})\leq 2
, we have
\mathbb{F}(T_{s})=2
for every
s\in[0,1]
.

Lemma 4.4

(Measurability of fillings)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete and separable metric space admitting
a conical geodesic bicombing.
Let
\eta
be a finite Borel measure on
\operatorname{Lip}([0,1];{\rm X})
, concentrated
on a Borel set
A\subset\{\gamma\in\operatorname{Lip}([0,1];{\rm X})\colon\ell(\gamma)\leq L\}
.
Then

T(f,\pi):=\int\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket(f,\pi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma),\qquad(f,\pi)\in D^{1}({\rm X})

defines a metric
1
-current, which additionally satisfies

\mathbb{F}(T-\eta(A)\llbracket\gamma_{0}\rrbracket)\leq 2(L+1)\eta(A)\mathrm{diam}(A,{\sf d}_{\infty}),

for every
\gamma_{0}\in A
.

Proof.

To see that
T
is well-defined, it is enough to show that the assumptions of
Lemma

A.4
are met,
with measure space
(A,\mathcal{B}(A),\eta)
and map
\gamma\in A\mapsto\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket
.
For
(f,\pi)\in D^{1}({\rm X})
, the map
\gamma\in A\mapsto\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket(f,\pi)
is continuous, hence Borel,
and

\int\mathbb{M}(\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma)\leq L\eta(A)<\infty,

so
T
is a metric
1
-current.

Assume
\mathrm{diam}(A,{\sf d}_{\infty})<\infty
, for otherwise the statement is trivial.
For each
\gamma\in A
, let

,
R_{\gamma}\in\mathbf{M}_{1}({\rm X})

be given by
Lemma

4.1
with
\gamma^{1}\equiv\gamma
and
\gamma^{0}\equiv\gamma^{0}
.
They satisfy
\partial S_{\gamma}+R_{\gamma}=\llbracket\gamma^{0}\rrbracket-\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket
,

\mathbb{M}(S_{\gamma})\leq 2L\mathrm{diam}(A,{\sf d}_{\infty}),\qquad\mathbb{M}(R_{\gamma})\leq 2\mathrm{diam}(A,{\sf d}_{\infty}),

and moreover the maps
\gamma\in A\mapsto S_{\gamma}(f,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
,
\gamma\in A\mapsto R_{\gamma}(f,\pi)

are measurable for
(f,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in D^{2}({\rm X})
and
(f,\pi)\in D^{1}({\rm X})
.
We can therefore apply
Lemma

A.4
with measure space

(A,\mathcal{B}(A),\eta)
to see that

\displaystyle S(f,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})

\displaystyle:=\int_{A}S_{\gamma}(f,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma),

\displaystyle R(f,\pi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{A}R_{\gamma}(f,\pi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma),

define a metric
2
-current and
1
-current, respectively.
Moreover, they satisfy

\partial S+R=\eta(A)\llbracket\gamma^{0}\rrbracket-T

and

\mathbb{M}(S)+\mathbb{M}(R)\leq 2(L+1)\eta(A)\mathrm{diam}(A,{\sf d}_{\infty}).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.5

(Geodesic approximation in conical geodesic bicombing metric space)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete and separable metric space admitting a conical geodesic bicombing.
Let
N\in\mathbf{N}_{1}({\rm X})
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
P=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\llbracket\gamma_{i}\rrbracket
, where
\gamma_{i}\in{\rm Geo}({\rm X})
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, such that
\mathbb{F}(N-P)\leq\varepsilon
and
\mathbb{M}(P)\leq\mathbb{M}(N)
.
Moreover, if
{\rm X}
is a closed convex subset of a Banach space, we can choose
\gamma_{i}
to be line segments.

Proof.

Step 1
(Approximation by a finite sum of curves with multiplicities).
We first show that for
\varepsilon>0
there are Lipschitz curves
\gamma_{i}\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
such that

\mathbb{F}\left(N-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\llbracket\gamma_{i}\rrbracket\right)\leq\varepsilon,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|\ell(\gamma_{i})\leq\mathbb{M}(N).

Since
N\in\mathbf{N}_{1}({\rm X})
, by a result of Paolini-Stepanov
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
, there exists a finite Borel measure
\eta
concentrated on
rectifiable curves such that

(27)

N=\int\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{M}(N)=\int\ell(\gamma)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma).

Let
\mathcal{C}_{L}:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1];{\rm X})\colon\ell(\gamma)\leq L\}
and set

N_{L}:=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{L}}\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma).

Since
\eta
is concentrated on rectifiable curves,
\mathbb{F}(N-N_{L})\leq\mathbb{M}(N-N_{L})\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{L}^{c}}\ell(\gamma)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma)\rightarrow 0
as
L\rightarrow\infty
.
We also have
\mathbb{M}(N_{L})\leq\mathbb{M}(N)
. We can therefore assume

N=N_{L}
for some
L>0
, i.e. that
\eta
is concentrated on

\mathcal{C}_{L}
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is separable, so is
C([0,1],{\rm X})
, and there is a countable dense set
\{\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\}_{i}
.
Set
A_{i}:=\mathcal{C}_{L}\cap(B_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})\setminus\cup_{j<i}B_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\gamma}_{j}))
, so that
\{A_{i}\}_{i}
is a pairwise disjoint cover of
\mathcal{C}_{L}
.
Set
N^{n}:=\int_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}}\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma)
and note that, similarly as before,

\mathbb{M}(N^{n})\leq\mathbb{M}(N)
and

\mathbb{M}(N-N^{n})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
We now approximate
N^{n}
.
For
1\leq i\leq n
, pick
\gamma_{i}\in A_{i}
such that

(28)

\eta(A_{i})\ell(\gamma_{i})\leq\int_{A_{i}}\ell(\gamma)\,{\mathrm{d}}\eta(\gamma),

and define
T:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta(A_{i})\llbracket{\gamma_{i}}\rrbracket
.
By (
28
) and (
27
), we have
\mathbb{M}(T)\leq\mathbb{M}(N)
and,
by Lemma
4.1
,

\mathbb{F}(N^{n}-T)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}2(L+1)\eta(A_{i})2\varepsilon=4(L+1)\eta(C([0,1],{\rm X}))\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary and
\mathbb{M}(N-N^{n})\rightarrow 0
, this concludes the first step.

Step 2
(Approximation by a finite sum of geodesics)
By triangle inequality (for the flat-norm), it is enough to show that we can approximate
each
\llbracket\gamma_{i}\rrbracket
by a finite sum of geodesics with total length less than
\ell(\gamma_{i})
.

Fix
i
and set
\gamma:=\gamma_{i}
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, let
\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}=\{0=t_{0}^{\varepsilon}<\cdots<t_{m(\varepsilon)}^{\varepsilon}=1\}

be a partition of

with mesh size
\max_{1\leq j\leq m(\varepsilon)}(t_{j}-t_{j-1})<\varepsilon

We further assume

For

and

where

.
Then

4.2.
Homotopy formula for general
k
in Banach spaces

The goal of this section is to prove an homotopy formula for
k
-currents in a Banach space
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
. We introduce some notation. Let
\phi,\psi\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{B};\mathbb{B})
.
Let
h\colon[0,1]\times\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}
be the affine homotopy between Lipschitz functions defined as
h(t,x):=t\phi(x)+(1-t)\psi(x)
. For brevity, for every
f\colon\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote
f_{t}:=f\circ h(t,\cdot)
and
\hat{\pi}_{i}=(\pi_{1},...\pi_{i-1},\pi_{i+1},...\pi_{k})
.
Note that for
f\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{B})
we have that for every
x\in\mathbb{B}

(30)

\left|\frac{\partial(f\circ h)}{\partial t}(t,x)\right|\leq\operatorname{Lip}f(x)\,|\phi(x)-\psi(x)|

at every point
t
of differentiability of
f\circ h(\cdot,x)
and

(31)

\operatorname{Lip}f_{t}(x)\leq\operatorname{Lip}f(x)\,\max\{\operatorname{Lip}\psi(x),\operatorname{Lip}\phi(x)\}

for every
x\in\mathbb{B}
and
t\in[0,1]
.

For
T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(\mathbb{B})
and
h
as before, we define the
(k+1)
-metric functional
H_{h}(T)
as

H_{h}(T)(f,\pi):=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\int_{0}^{1}T\left(f\circ h\frac{\partial(\pi_{i}\circ h)}{\partial t},\hat{\pi}_{i}\circ h\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

For simplicity of notation, when there is no ambiguity we denote it by
H(T)
.
A similar construction was used by
[
3
, Section 10]
to construct a cone over a metric
k
-current.

Proposition 4.6

(Properties of
H(T)
)
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{N}_{k}(\mathbb{B})
have bounded support. Then

(1)

\mathbb{M}(H(T))\leq(k+1)\int\|\phi-\psi\|\max\{\operatorname{Lip}\psi(x),\operatorname{Lip}\phi(x)\}^{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|
;

(2)

H(T)\in\mathbf{N}_{k+1}(\mathbb{B})
;

(3)

\phi_{\#}T-\psi_{\#}T=\partial H(T)+H(\partial T)
.

Proof.

We only need to show (
1
). Once (
1
) is shown, (
2
) and (
3
) are obtained by following the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 10.2]
exactly. Let
(f,\pi)\in D^{k+1}(\mathbb{B})
with
\operatorname{LIP}(\pi_{i})\leq 1
for
1\leq i\leq k+1
.
We have

\displaystyle|H(T)

\displaystyle(f,\pi)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|T\left(f\circ h\,\frac{\partial(\pi_{i}\circ h)}{\partial t},\hat{\pi}_{i}\circ h\right)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}\int\left|f\circ h\,\frac{\partial(\pi_{i}\circ h)}{\partial t}\right|(x)\prod_{j\neq i}\operatorname{Lip}(\pi_{j}\circ h)(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{liphom}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}\int\left|f\circ h\,\frac{\partial(\pi_{i}\circ h)}{\partial t}\right|(x)\prod_{j\neq i}\operatorname{Lip}\pi_{j}(x)(\max\{\operatorname{Lip}\psi(x),\operatorname{Lip}\phi(x)\})^{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{partialhom}}}{{\leq}}(k+1)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}(|f|\circ h)(t,x)\,\|\phi(x)-\psi(x)\|(\max\{\operatorname{Lip}\psi(x),\operatorname{Lip}\phi(x)\})^{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq(k+1)\int_{\mathbb{B}}|f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu,

where the measure
\nu
is defined as

\nu(A)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{h_{t}^{-1}(A)}\|\phi(x)-\psi(x)\|(\max\{\operatorname{Lip}\psi(x),\operatorname{Lip}\phi(x)\})^{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

for all Borel sets
A\subset\mathbb{B}
.

This proves
\|H(T)\|\leq(k+1)\nu
as measures,
and in particular

\mathbb{M}(H(T))\leq(k+1)\nu(\mathbb{B})\leq(k+1)\int\|\phi(x)-\psi(x)\|(\max\{\operatorname{Lip}\psi(x),\operatorname{Lip}\phi(x)\})^{k}{\mathrm{d}}\|T\|(x).

∎

5.
Structure of flat chains in Banach spaces

The aim of this section is to extend the results of
[
1
, Section 3]
to the case of an ambient Banach space instead of Euclidean space for the case of
1
-dimensional metric currents.
The goal is essentially to show that any
1
-current can be obtained by restricting a normal
1
-current to a Borel set.
Building upon the work carried out in the previous sections, we can essentially follow the argument of their paper. There is a minor modification: in a special case, we can even prove that a metric
1
-current is the restriction of a normal
1
-current to a
closed
set, as opposed to Borel. This will be important for the application in Section
6

as it will allow us to consider restrictions of fragments to closed sets,
see (
35
). The difficulty is that a fragment, by definition, needs to have a compact domain, thus a fragment restricted to a Borel set need not be a fragment itself.

Throughout this section,
(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach space. Given a set
A\subset\mathbb{B}
, we denote by
{\rm int}(A)
its topological interior. Given
A\subset B\subset\mathbb{B}
, we denote by
{\rm int}(A,B)
the topological interior of
A
in
B
equipped with the topology inherited from
\mathbb{B}
.

We need to fix some notation concerning polyhedral chains and the flat norm. Let
0<k<\dim\mathbb{B}
and let
\sigma
be a
k
-simplex. In particular
\sigma
is the convex hull of
(k+1)
-points
a_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,k
. Let
S_{\sigma}:={\rm span}\{a_{i}-a_{0}:\,i=1,\dots,k\}=:S_{\sigma}
. Given a
k
-simplex
\sigma
,
v_{i}\in S_{\sigma}
for
i=1,\dots,k
, we define the metric
k
-current
\llbracket\sigma,v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rrbracket
as

\llbracket\sigma,v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rrbracket(f,\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}):=\int_{\sigma}f{\langle}{\mathrm{d}}\pi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{\mathrm{d}}\pi_{k},v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{k}{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{k}.

Here, by
{\mathrm{d}}\pi_{i}
we denote the differential of the Lipschitz function
\pi_{i}
restricted to
S_{\sigma}+a
, where
a
is a vertex of
\sigma
. This is well-defined at
\mathcal{H}^{k}
a.e. point of
\sigma
because of Rademacher’s theorem.

We define the set of
polyhedral
k
-chains
supported on a set
C\subset\mathbb{B}
, denoted by
\mathbf{P}_{k}(C)
, as the set of finite sums

P=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\,\llbracket\sigma_{i},v_{1}^{i},\dots,v_{k}^{i}\rrbracket

where
\sigma_{i}
is a
k
-simplex,
v^{i}_{j}\in S_{\sigma_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,N
,
j=1,\dots,k
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and the intersection
\sigma_{i_{0}}\cap\sigma_{i_{1}}
is a subset of the boundary of
\sigma_{i_{0}}
whenever
i_{0}\not=i_{1}
.

If we highlight the dependence on the norm in the definition of Hausdorff measure, we write
\mathcal{H}_{\|\cdot\|}^{k}
.
We define the space of flat
k
-chains and we denote by
(\mathbf{F}_{k}(C),\mathbb{F}(\cdot))
as the completion of
\mathbf{P}_{k}(C)
with respect to
\mathbb{F}(\cdot)
.

We recall that elements of
\mathbf{F}_{k}(C)
can be regarded as metric
k
-currents.
This follows by the fact that the flat and mass closures of normal
k
-currents coincide together with the fact that polyhedral
k
-chains are normal
k
-currents. We define the
relaxed mass
norm on the space
\mathbf{F}_{k}(C)
as

\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}(T):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{M}(P_{n})\,:\,P_{n}\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(C),\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{F}(P_{n}-T)=0\right\}

for every
T\in\mathbf{F}_{k}(C)
. The main result follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{B}
be closed, convex and bounded with
0\in{\rm int}(C)
. Assume
\dim\mathbb{B}>1
.
Let
T\in\mathbf{M}_{1}(C)
and suppose
\|T\|
is inner regular by compact sets.
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbf{N}_{1}(C)
and a Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{B}
such that

(i)

\partial N=0
,

(ii)

\mathbb{M}(N)\leq(2+\varepsilon)\mathbb{M}(T)
,

(iii)

N\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{B}$}=T
.

Moreover, if the support of
T
is contained in an affine hyperplane, then we can take
B={\rm supp}(T)
.

Recall that, by definition, an affine hyperplane in a Banach space is a level set of a non-zero linear continuous functional.

Following
[
1
]
, we prove the following preliminary results, which we prove in slightly higher generality than what
is strictly necessary for our purposes.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{B}
be closed, convex and bounded and such that
0\in{\rm int}(C)
.
Let
T\in\mathbf{F}_{k}(C)
for some
0<k<\dim\mathbb{B}

k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
P\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(C)
such that
\mathbb{F}(T-P)\leq\varepsilon
,

{\rm supp}(P)\subset{\rm int}(C)
and
\mathbb{M}(P)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}(T)
.

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a polyhedral chain
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(C)
such that
\mathbb{F}(T-P^{\prime})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and such that
\mathbb{M}(P^{\prime})\leq(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}(T)
. Let

and denote
h_{t}(\cdot)=h(t,\cdot)
. Define
P:={h_{1-\eta}}_{*}P^{\prime}
for
\eta
to be chosen later and we claim that
P
satisfies the conclusion of the theorem. Notice that

P\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(C)
.
We apply Proposition
4.6
with
\psi:={\rm Id}
and
\phi:=h_{1-\eta}
, thus having

\mathbb{F}(P-{h_{1-\eta}}_{*}P)\leq\eta((k+1)\mathbb{M}(P)+k\mathbb{M}(\partial P)).

Moreover, we claim that
{\rm supp}(P)\subset{\rm int}(C)
. Indeed, since
0\in{\rm int}(C)
, we have
B(0,\delta)\subset C
for some
\delta
. Since
C
is convex, we have that
(1-t)B(0,\delta)+t{\rm supp}(P^{\prime})\subset C
for every
t\in[0,1]
, implying that
t{\rm supp}(P^{\prime})\in{\rm int}(C)
. We conclude noticing that
{\rm supp}(P)={\rm supp}({h_{1-\eta}}_{*}P^{\prime})=(1-\eta){\rm supp}(P^{\prime})
.

Moreover, since
h_{t}\colon\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}
is
t
-Lipschitz for every

\mathbb{M}({h_{t}}_{*}P^{\prime})\leq t^{k}\mathbb{M}(P^{\prime}).

Thus, the proof follows by choosing

∎

.

Proposition 5.5
.

(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space. Let

be closed, convex and bounded with

,
\mu
a nonnegative finite Borel measure and

-current

\partial R=\partial T
;

(ii)

Choose
\delta

, and define

.
We apply Lemma
5.3
to

for some
R_{0}\in\mathbf{M}_{1}(C)
,
S_{0}\in\mathbf{N}_{2}(C)
. Notice that the fillings for computing the flat norms in Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
.
We have also used that
\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}=\mathbb{M}
on metric
1
-currents (with inner regular mass measure) by applying Lemma

.

We have that

. We are in position to apply again Lemma
5.3
to

is a metric
1
-current, hence a flat
1
-chain by Lemma

and

. We choose

and we have that there exists

for some

.

and
\|P_{i}\|(H)=0
for every

Since

is Cauchy with respect to

For
g\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
Lipschitz, we have

T(1,g)=\partial T(g)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\partial P_{i}(g)+\partial R_{n}(g)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}P_{i}(1,g)+R_{n}(1,g).

To prove the mass estimate, we compute

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a separable metric space and
f\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
a continuous function.
Then the sets

\displaystyle\mathit{MD}

\displaystyle:=\{(t,\gamma)\in\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})\colon t\text{ is a limit point of }{\rm dom}(\gamma)\text{ and }|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\text{ exists}\}

\displaystyle D(f)

\displaystyle:=\{(t,\gamma)\in\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})\colon t\text{ is a limit point of }{\rm dom}(\gamma)\text{ and }(f\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}\text{ exists}\}

are Borel and the maps

\displaystyle(t,\gamma)\in\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})

\displaystyle\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|,&(t,\gamma)\in\mathit{MD}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle(t,\gamma)\in\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})

\displaystyle\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(f\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime},&(t,\gamma)\in D(f)\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

are Borel measurable.

Proof.

We only prove the part of the statement regarding
D(f)
and
(t,\gamma)\mapsto(f\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}
;
the other one is similar.
For
r>0
, the maps
(t,\gamma)\mapsto\inf(B(t,r)\cap{\rm dom}(\gamma))
,

(t,\gamma)\mapsto\sup(B(t,r)\cap{\rm dom}(\gamma))
are upper and
lower-semicontinuous, respectively.
It is then not difficult to construct, for each
r>0
, a Borel map
\tau_{r}\colon\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})\to[0,1]
,
with
\tau_{r}(t,\gamma)\in\overline{B}(t,r)\cap{\rm dom}(\gamma)
,
such that
\tau_{r}(t,\gamma)=t

if and only if
B(t,r)\cap{\rm dom}(\gamma)=\{t\}
.
In particular, since
\tau(t,\gamma):=t
is continuous,

L:=\{(t,\gamma)\in\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})\colon t\text{ is a limit point of }{\rm dom}(\gamma)\}=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathbb{Q}\\
r>0\end{subarray}}\{\tau_{r}\neq\tau\}

is a Borel set.
Observe that, for
(t,\gamma)\in L
, it holds
(t,\gamma)\in D(f)

if and only if for every
\varepsilon>0
there are
R>0
and
D\in\mathbb{R}

such that for each
0<r<R
we have

|(f\circ\gamma)_{s}-(f\circ\gamma)_{t}-D(s-t)|\leq\varepsilon r

for all
s\in B(t,r)\cap{\rm dom}(\gamma)
.
Since the function
(x_{1},x_{2},t_{1},t_{2})\in{\rm X}^{2}\times[0,1]^{2}\mapsto|f(x_{2})-f(x_{1})-D(t_{2}-t_{1})|

is continuous,
one can see that the map
\tilde{\Phi}_{D,r}\colon\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})\to[0,\infty)

\tilde{\Phi}_{D,r}(t,\gamma):=\sup_{s\in B(t,r)\cap{\rm dom}(\gamma)}\frac{|(f\circ\gamma)_{s}-(f\circ\gamma)_{t}-D(s-t)|}{r}

is lower-semicontinuous and therefore so is

\Phi_{D,R}:=\sup_{0<r<R}\tilde{\Phi}_{D,r}
.
Recalling the previous discussion, we have

D(f)=L\cap\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}\\
\varepsilon>0\end{subarray}}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}R,D\in\mathbb{Q}\\
R>0\end{subarray}}\{\Phi_{D,R}\leq\varepsilon\},

showing that
D(f)
is Borel.
It remains to prove measurability of the map
F\colon\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})\to\mathbb{R}

given by

F(t,\gamma):=(f\circ\gamma)_{t}^{\prime}
for
(t,\gamma)\in D(f)
,
F(t,\gamma):=0
otherwise.
Since
{\rm X}
is separable, so is
\Gamma({\rm X})
,
and the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
[0,1]\times\Gamma({\rm X})

is therefore generated by open sets of the form

with

,

B\subset\Gamma({\rm X})
.
The maps
(t,\gamma)\in\overline{\Gamma}({\rm X})\mapsto(\tau_{r}(t,\gamma),\gamma)
,

(t,\gamma)\mapsto e(\tau_{r}(t,\gamma),\gamma)=\gamma_{\tau_{r}(t,\gamma)}

are then easily seen to be Borel.
Let

be given by

F_{r}(t,\gamma):=\frac{(f\circ\gamma)_{\tau_{r}(t,\gamma)}-(f\circ\gamma)_{t}}{\tau_{r}(t,\gamma)-t}

for
(t,\gamma)\in D(f)
and
F_{r}(t,\gamma):=0
otherwise.
It is then clear that

is Borel for every
r>0
and, since

,
fixing

∎

Appendix A

Lemma A.1
.

Let
X
be a separable metric space.
Then there is a countable set

Proof.

Let

be the countable set of functions

For each

For instance, one can set

\tilde{f}(x):=\min_{y\in F}f(y)+\operatorname{Lip}(f)d(x,y),\qquad x\in X.

Let
\tilde{D}_{F}:=\{\tilde{f}\colon f\in D_{F}\}
,
D:=\cup\{\tilde{D}_{F}\colon F\subset S\text{ finite}\}
and note that they are both countable. We now show that
D
is dense.
Let
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz and fix an enumeration

of

.
It is easy to see from the definitions that for any

, there is

with

It is then clear that

converges to

S
.
But then, by equicontinuity,
f_{n}
converges to
f
on
X
.
∎

Lemma A.2

Proposition B.4
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete metric space,
C\subset{\rm X}
a closed set,
and let
\iota\colon C\to{\rm X}
denote the inclusion map.
Then, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the pushforward operator

\iota_{*}\colon\{T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(C):\,\|T\|\text{ is inner regular}\}\to\{T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(X):\,{\rm supp}(T)\subset C,\,\|T\|\text{ is inner regular}\}

is an isometric isomorphism,
where both spaces are endowed with the corresponding mass norm.
Moreover,

(43)

\displaystyle\iota_{*}\colon\{T\in\mathbf{N}_{k}(C):

\displaystyle\|T\|+\|\partial T\|\text{ is inner regular}\}\to

\displaystyle\{T\in\mathbf{N}_{k}(X):\,{\rm supp}(T)\subset C,\,\|T\|+\|\partial T\|\text{ is inner regular}\}

is an isometric isomorphism
when both spaces are equipped with either the mass norm
or normal mass norm.

Proof.

Let
Z_{k}(C):=\{T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}(C):\,\|T\|\text{ is inner regular}\}
and
Z_{k}({\rm X};C):=\{T\in\mathbf{M}_{k}({\rm X}):\,{\rm supp}(T)\subset C,\,\|T\|\text{ is inner regular}\}
.
Throughout, for
g\in\operatorname{Lip}(C)
, we let
\widehat{g}
denote any Lipschitz extension of
g

to
{\rm X}
, satisfying
\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}=\|g\|_{\infty}
and
\operatorname{LIP}(\widehat{g})=\operatorname{LIP}(g)
. If
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\in\operatorname{Lip}(C)^{k}
, we set
\widehat{g}=(\widehat{g}_{1},\dots,\widehat{g}_{k})
.

Let
T\in Z_{k}(C)
.
It is straigthforward to check that
\iota_{*}T\in Z_{k}({\rm X};C)
and
\|\iota_{*}T\|\leq\iota_{*}\|T\|
.
Let
\Phi\colon Z_{k}({\rm X};C)\to Z_{k}(C)
be the map defined as
\Phi(T):=\widehat{T}
, where
\widehat{T}
is as in Lemma
B.3
. The same lemma yields that
\Phi
is well-defined and
\iota_{*}\Phi(T)=T
. Moreover,
\Phi(\iota_{*}T)=T
, because for
(f,\pi)\in D^{k}(C)

\Phi(\iota_{*}T)(f,\pi)=T(\widehat{f}\circ\iota,\widehat{\pi}\circ\iota)=T(f,\pi),

where we have used
(\widehat{f}\circ\iota,\widehat{\pi}\circ\iota)=(f,\pi)
. Thus,
\iota_{*}\colon Z_{k}(C)\to Z_{k}({\rm X};C)
is linear and bijective.

It remains to prove it is an isometry. We compute for
S\in Z_{k}({\rm X};C)
and
(f,\pi)\in D^{k}({\rm X})

|\Phi(S)(f,\pi)|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{LIP}(\widehat{\pi}_{i})\int|\widehat{f}|\,{\mathrm{d}}\|S\|=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{LIP}(\pi_{i})\int_{C}|f|\,{\mathrm{d}}\|S\|

thus
\|\Phi(S)\|\leq\|S\|
. This together with
\iota_{*}
being
1
-Lipschitz gives
\mathbb{M}(\iota_{*}T)=\mathbb{M}(T)
for
T\in Z_{k}(C)
.

We now verify the last claim of the statement.
Let
W_{k}(C)
and
W_{k}({\rm X};C)
denote, respectively, the left and right-hand side of
(
43
) and observe that

W_{k}(C)=\{T\in Z_{k}(C)\colon\partial T\in Z_{k-1}(C)\}

and
W_{k}({\rm X};C)=\{T\in Z_{k}({\rm X};C)\colon\partial T\in Z_{k-1}({\rm X};C)\}
.
It is clear that
\iota_{*}(W_{k}(C))\subset W_{k}(X;C)

and that
\iota_{*}
is an isometric embedding with either norm.
(For the normal mass, we use the fact that pushforward and boundary operators commute.)
Hence, we only need to prove that
\iota_{*}
is onto.
Suppose
T\in W_{k}({\rm X};C)
and consider
\Phi(T)\in Z_{k}(C)
. Since
\mathrm{supp}(\partial T)\subset\mathrm{supp}(T)
,
we have
\partial T\in Z_{k-1}({\rm X};C)
and therefore
\Phi(\partial T)\in Z_{k-1}(C)
.
From the definitions and
B.3

we have
\partial(\Phi(T))=\Phi(\partial T)
, proving
\Phi(T)\in W_{k}(C)
and therefore
\iota_{*}(W_{k}(C))=W_{k}({\rm X};C)
.
∎

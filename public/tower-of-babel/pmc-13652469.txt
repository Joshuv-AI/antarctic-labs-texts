Staged surgical management of advanced MRONJ in a nonagenarian with severe infection: extensive segmental mandibulectomy and reconstruction
Authors: Miki Yamada, Yosuke Iijima, Miyabi Mori, Shunsuke Hino, Norio Horie, Takahiro Kaneko
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:The management of advanced medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ) in very elderly patients remains controversial because of concerns regarding perioperative risk. This report describes the clinical course and outcome of staged surgical management in a nonagenarian patient with advanced MRONJ complicated by severe infection.Methods:A 92-year-old woman with Stage 3 MRONJ, according to the 2022 American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons Position Paper, presented with extensive mandibular destruction and severe infection accompanied by a marked systemic inflammatory response. Initial management included airway protection, incision and drainage, and systemic infection control measures. After resolution of systemic inflammation, definitive surgical treatment was performed based on disease severity, the patient’s general condition, and treatment preference.Results:A segmental mandibulectomy and reconstruction with a titanium reconstruction plate were performed under general anesthesia. The postoperative course was uneventful, and oral intake was resumed successfully. Imaging confirmed restoration of mandibular continuity. Over a 4-year follow-up period, a fracture of the reconstruction plate occurred secondary to trauma, but no recurrence of MRONJ was observed.Conclusion:Staged management with initial infection control followed by definitive surgical resection was safely performed in a carefully selected nonagenarian patient with Stage 3 MRONJ. Reports of such extensive surgical management in nonagenarian patients remain extremely limited. Surgical decision-making should be based on disease severity and systemic condition rather than chronological age alone.

Introduction

Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ) is increasingly prevalent in aging populations[1,2]. Although surgical resection is considered an effective treatment for advanced disease, the indication for its use in very elderly patients remains controversial because of concerns regarding perioperative risk and limited physiological reserve[2,3].

In clinical practice, treatment decisions for nonagenarians are often biased toward conservative management. However, inadequate infection control may result in disease progression and systemic complications, including sepsis[2,4]. Evidence supporting definitive surgical management in patients aged ≥90 years remains extremely limited, particularly for cases requiring extensive segmental mandibulectomy and reconstruction under general anesthesia[2,3].

We report the case of advanced MRONJ in a 92-year-old woman, successfully managed by staged infection control followed by segmental mandibulectomy and reconstruction plate fixation, with favorable long-term outcomes. This case report is presented in line with the SCARE 2025 criteria.

Case presentation

A 92-year-old woman with a medical history of osteoporosis and osteoarthritis of the knee had been receiving oral alendronate therapy for more than 5 years. She was otherwise healthy. She resided in a nursing home and received regular oral health care from a visiting dentist. Mandibular bone exposure in the anterior region had been present for more than 1 year, and MRONJ was suspected. Because the lesion was asymptomatic and the patient was of advanced age, she had been managed conservatively.

Approximately 2 weeks before presentation, she developed a fever and was evaluated by a visiting physician, who suspected a respiratory infection and recommended observation. She subsequently developed swelling and local warmth extending from the right cheek to the mandibular region, and antibiotic therapy was initiated by a visiting dentist. However, she later developed a high fever of 39°C, and the swelling rapidly progressed from the right temporal region to the cheek, prompting emergency transfer. Despite conservative treatment at a nearby hospital, her condition deteriorated, with a decrease in oxygen saturation (SpO2), progression of abscess formation, and suspicion of sepsis, necessitating transfer to our hospital for further management.

At presentation, she exhibited a decreased level of consciousness (GCS E3V4M6). Her body temperature was 38.2°C, and SpO2 was 97% on room air. Marked erythema, warmth, and swelling extending from the right temporal region to the facial region were observed. Spontaneous skin breakdown of the right cheek with purulent discharge was noted. Intraoral examination revealed extensive mandibular bone exposure from the right second premolar to the left lateral incisor.

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated extensive inflammatory changes extending from the right temporal region to the buccal region (Fig. 1). Laboratory findings revealed a marked systemic inflammatory response, including a white blood cell count of 12.0 × 103/μL, a C-reactive protein level of 17.23 mg/dL, and a D-dimer level of 7.30 μg/mL. Based on the patient’s history of long-term bisphosphonate therapy, characteristic clinical and radiographic findings, and the absence of a history of head and neck radiotherapy, a diagnosis of MRONJ presenting with acute exacerbation was established, although chronic osteomyelitis and malignant bone lesions were also considered in the differential diagnosis. Given the severity of infection and the risk of airway compromise, a tracheostomy was performed, and the patient was admitted to the intensive care unit. Incision and drainage of the temporal and buccal abscesses was performed, and drainage tubes were placed. Extensive debridement of necrotic soft tissue was performed.

After approximately 30 days, adequate infection control and resolution of systemic inflammation were achieved. Three-dimensional computed tomography performed after the resolution of inflammation demonstrated extensive osteolysis of the mandible and a pathological fracture of the left mandibular body, confirming the diagnosis of Stage 3 MRONJ according to the 2022 American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons Position Paper (Fig. 2A, B). Although conservative management was initially contemplated because of the patient’s advanced age, the presence of a pathological fracture with advanced structural destruction of the mandible was a major indication for segmental mandibulectomy. This finding, together with the patient’s strong desire to resume oral intake without concern for persistent bone exposure or recurrent inflammation, supported the decision to proceed with definitive surgical treatment. The patient had no major systemic comorbidities other than osteoporosis and was classified as the American Society of Anesthesiologists Physical Status II. Preoperative anesthetic evaluation confirmed that surgery under general anesthesia was acceptable, indicating sufficient physiological reserve despite her advanced age. After thorough discussion with the patient and her family, informed consent was obtained.

Under general anesthesia, a segmental mandibulectomy extending from the right mandibular body to the left mandibular angle was performed. Intraoperatively, extensive necrotic mandibular bone and infected soft tissue were identified (Fig. 2C). Reconstruction was subsequently performed using a MatrixMANDIBLE reconstruction plate (DePuy Synthes, West Chester, PA, USA). Histopathological examination of the resected specimen revealed necrotic bone with empty osteocytic lacunae, consistent with osteonecrosis, and no evidence of malignancy (Fig. 2D). Postoperative three-dimensional computed tomography confirmed restoration of mandibular continuity (Fig. 2E, F).

At long-term follow-up, the patient remained clinically stable (Fig. 3A). Fracture of the reconstruction plate was observed 3 years postoperatively following a fall (Fig. 3B–D). No recurrence of MRONJ has been observed over 4 years of follow-up, and the patient remains under observation without additional surgical intervention. Discussion

This report describes a 92-year-old woman with MRONJ who developed a severe acute infection and was successfully treated with staged management, followed by an extensive segmental mandibulectomy, resulting in a favorable outcome.

Acute infection associated with MRONJ is primarily managed by local and systemic infection control measures, including incision and drainage and systemic antibiotic therapy. Severe infections originating from MRONJ have been widely reported, including cases of extension into deep fascial spaces, such as the temporal region[5,6]. Odontogenic infections are known to spread to adjacent anatomical spaces and may lead to life-threatening complications, especially in elderly patients with reduced physiological reserve[7]. Therefore, early diagnosis and prompt intervention are essential.

According to the 2022 AAOMS Position Paper, the present case fulfilled the diagnostic criteria for Stage 3 MRONJ because of the presence of a pathological fracture and an extraoral draining fistula. In addition, extensive mandibular osteolysis reflected advanced structural destruction. Surgical management is recommended and may be indicated for patients with Stage 3 disease, particularly when associated with structural collapse or persistent infection[1]. Consistent with these recommendations, accumulating evidence suggests that surgical treatment is associated with better outcomes compared with nonoperative management[2,8,9], including findings from a multicenter retrospective study using propensity score matching analyses[9]. In particular, Ruggiero and Kohn reported significantly improved outcomes with surgical intervention compared with conservative therapy[8]. Although teriparatide has been reported as a conservative treatment option for selected patients with MRONJ, current evidence remains limited, particularly for advanced Stage 3 disease with pathological fracture[10]. Oral alendronate is associated with a lower risk of MRONJ than intravenous antiresorptive agents. However, prolonged, continuous use has been reported to increase the cumulative risk of developing MRONJ[5]. In the present case, long-term oral alendronate therapy may have contributed to the development and progression of advanced MRONJ.

Treatment strategies for MRONJ in elderly patients should be considered separately from those for younger populations. Aging is associated with a decline in physiological reserve, which may increase perioperative risk. Given the variability in general health status among elderly patients, comprehensive assessment of the patient’s systemic condition is essential[6,7]. Therefore, chronological age alone should not be used as a contraindication to surgical management.

Previous reports describing surgical treatment of MRONJ in elderly patients are limited. Nevertheless, several studies have demonstrated that surgical treatment can be effective even in elderly patients and that age alone does not determine prognosis[2,3].

In elderly patients, surgical management of MRONJ is often limited to less invasive procedures, such as sequestrectomy under local anesthesia. In contrast, the present case is notable for the advanced age of the patient (92 years) and for the extent of resection performed, which involved an extensive segmental mandibulectomy under general anesthesia. Such an extensive segmental mandibulectomy under general anesthesia in a nonagenarian patient appears to be extremely rare. Previous reports have emphasized the role of surgical resection and reconstruction in the management of advanced MRONJ[11], supporting the treatment strategy applied in this case.

Staged management with initial infection control followed by definitive surgical resection enabled safe treatment despite the patient’s advanced age. Careful preoperative assessment confirmed sufficient physiological reserve and limited systemic comorbidities, supporting the feasibility of surgery under general anesthesia.

Following reconstruction, the patient successfully resumed a chopped diet without recurrence of infection. Although complete functional restoration was not the primary treatment goal, satisfactory oral intake was maintained throughout the 4-year follow-up.

Although a reconstruction plate fracture occurred 3 years postoperatively following a fall, the patient remained asymptomatic, without recurrent infection or functional deterioration. Therefore, conservative observation rather than reoperation was chosen. When planning extensive surgical procedures for MRONJ, the potential deterioration of postoperative quality of life should be carefully considered, and thorough preoperative counseling is essential. This case highlights the importance of integrating careful preoperative assessment and patient preferences into surgical decision-making, rather than relying on chronological age alone.

The present case has several strengths. It demonstrates that staged management with initial infection control followed by definitive surgical resection can achieve favorable long-term outcomes, even in a very elderly patient with advanced Stage 3 MRONJ. However, this report is limited by its single-case design, and the findings cannot be generalized. In addition, objective assessments of postoperative oral function and quality of life were not available.

Conclusion

Careful evaluation of disease severity and physiological reserve is critical for appropriate selection of elderly patients with MRONJ. Definitive surgical management, including extensive segmental mandibulectomy, may be feasible even in selected nonagenarian patients when the systemic condition and physiological reserve are acceptable.

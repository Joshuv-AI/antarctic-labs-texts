Integrability of ( ω , m ) (\omega,m) -subharmonic functions on compact Hermitian manifolds
Yuetong Fang

Abstract
. Let ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension n n . We show that all ( ω , m ) (\omega,m) -subharmonic functions are L p L^{p} integrable on X X , for any p < n n − m p<\frac{n}{n-m} .



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open set and
\omega
a hermitian
(1,1)
-form on
\Omega
. Let
u
be a real
\mathcal{C}^{2}
functions on
\Omega
such that the eigenvalues
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
of the complex Hessian matrix
[u_{i\bar{j}}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
, belong to the closure of the cone

\Gamma_{m}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:S_{1}(\lambda)>0,\cdots,S_{m}(\lambda)>0\},

where
S_{k}(\lambda)
denotes the
k
-th elementary symmetric function of
\lambda
:

S_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{0<j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{j_{1}}\lambda_{j_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{j_{k}}.

Such a function is called
m
-subharmonic (
m
-sh). As shown by Błocki in
[
Bł05
]
,
m
-sh functions are the right class of admissible solutions to the complex Hessian equations

(dd^{c}u)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}=f\omega^{n},

which are the generalization of Poisson equations (
m=1
) and Monge–Ampère equations (
m=n
).

While all plurisubharmonic functions are locally
L^{p}
-integrable for all
p\geq 1
, this is not necessarily the case for
m
-sh functions. The fundamental solution to the
m
-Hessian equation when
\omega=dd^{c}|z|^{2}
is given by
-\frac{1}{|z|^{2n/m-2}}
, which is
L^{p}
-integrable only when
p<\frac{nm}{n-m}
. Błocki conjectured that all
m
-sh functions are locally
L^{p}
-integrable for
p<\frac{nm}{n-m}
. This conjecture is partially confirmed in
[
DK14
]

[
ÅC20
]
, where the former developed a useful tool to study the integrability of
m
-sh functions, known as volume-capacity inequality:

\mathrm{Vol}(K)\leq C_{p}\mathrm{Cap}^{p}_{m}(K,\Omega),

with
K
being a compact subset of
\Omega
,
\mathrm{Cap}_{m}(K,\Omega)
denote the Hessian capacity, and
p<\frac{n}{n-m}
.

On compact hermitian manifolds
(X,\omega)
, we consider the
m
-Hessian operator

H_{m}(u)=(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}.

A
\mathcal{C}^{2}
function is called
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic with respect to
\omega
if
H_{k}(u)\geq 0
for all
k=1,\cdots,m
. The study of
m
-Hessian equations on compact manifolds (see for example
[
Szé18
]
,
[
Zha17
]
,
[
KN16
]
,
[
GL25
]
,
[
KN23
]
) motivate the development of potential theory for
(\omega,m)
-sh functions on hermitian manifolds. The main purpose of this article is to extend Dinew–Kołodziej’s integrability result to the Hermitian manifolds. We now present the precise statement.

Main Theorem
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact complex hermitian manifold of dimension
n
, equipped with a hermitian form
\omega
. Fix an integer
1\leq m\leq n
. For any
p<\frac{n}{n-m}
, we have
\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega)\subset L^{p}(X,\omega^{n})
.

When
(X,\omega)
is a Kähler manifold, the theorem is proved in
[
LN15
]
, using the volume-capacity inequality provided by the work of Dinew and Kołodziej
[
DK14
]
, and the Chern–Levine–Nirenberg(CLN) inequality. The proof of the latter inequality goes through several integration by parts which are delicate in the hermitian setting due to the appearance of torsion terms. In our setting when
m<n
, there is also a lack of positivity since
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions are in general not
\omega
-plurisubharmonic. In this short note, we observe that in the volume capacity estimate of Dinew and Kołodziej the candidates defining the capacity can be taken to be
\omega
-plurisubharmonic. This observation simplifies several potential estimates that we carry out in Section
3
, where we prove the main result. In Section
2
, we focus on reviewing essential concepts and results required for the proof.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we denote
(X,\omega)
as a compact Hermitian manifold of complex dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, equipped with a Hermitian metric
\omega
. We use the differential operators
d=\partial+\bar{\partial}
, and
d^{c}=i(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
, so that
dd^{c}=2i\partial\bar{\partial}
.

We now recall the notions of
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions and gather some results related to
\omega
-plurisubharmonic functions and
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions.

2.1.
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions

Fix an integer
1\leq m\leq n
. Fix
\Omega
an open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We follow the definition in
[
GN18
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A real
(1,1)
-form
\alpha
on
X
is called
m
-positive with respect to
\omega
if at all points in
X
,

\alpha^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\geq 0,\forall k=1,\cdots,m.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a hermitian metric
\alpha
on
\Omega
, a
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Omega)
function
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called
harmonic with respect to
\alpha
if
dd^{c}u\wedge\alpha^{n-1}=0
at all points in
\Omega
.

Definition 2.3
.

A function
u:\Omega\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{R}
is subharmonic with respect to
\omega
if:

(a)

u
is upper semicontinuous and
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
;

(b)

for every relatively compact open
D\Subset\Omega
and every function
h\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(D)
that is harmonic with respect to
\omega
on
D
the following implication holds

u\leq h\text{ on }\partial D\implies u\leq h\text{ in }D.

Definition 2.4
.

A function
\varphi:\Omega\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{R}
is quasi-subharmonic with respect to
\alpha
if locally
\varphi=u+\rho
, where
u
is subharmonic with respect to
\alpha
and
\rho
is smooth.

A function
\varphi
is
\omega
-subharmonic with respect to
\alpha
if
\varphi
is quasi-subharmonic with respect to
\alpha
and
(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)\wedge\alpha^{n-1}\geq 0
in sense of distributions.

The positive cone
\Gamma_{m}(\alpha)
associated with the metric
\alpha
is defined by

\left\{\gamma\text{ real }(1,1)\text{-form}:\gamma^{k}\wedge\alpha^{n-k}>0,k=1,\cdots,m\right\}.

It follows from Gårding’s inequality
[
Går59
]
that if
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m-1}\in\Gamma_{m}(\alpha)
, then

\gamma_{0}\wedge\gamma_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\gamma_{m-1}\wedge\alpha^{n-m}>0.

One can write
\widetilde{\alpha}=\gamma_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\gamma_{m-1}\wedge\alpha
, and it is a strictly positive
(n-1,n-1)
-form on
\Omega
.

Definition 2.5
.

A function
\varphi:\Omega\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty)
is called
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic with respect to
\alpha
if
\varphi
is
\omega
-subharmonic with respect to
\widetilde{\alpha}
in
\Omega
for all

\widetilde{\alpha}
of the form
\widetilde{\alpha}^{n-1}=\gamma_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\gamma_{m-1}\wedge\alpha
, where
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m-1}\in\Gamma_{m}(\alpha)
.

A function
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is called
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic on
X
if
u
is
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic on each local chart
U
of
X
.

The set of all locally integrable functions on
U
which are
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic with respect to
\alpha
in
U
is denoted by
\mathcal{SH}_{\alpha,m}(U,\omega)
. We remark that
\omega
and
\alpha
are not necessarily the same.

However, in this paper, we focus on the set
\mathcal{SH}_{\omega,m}(U,\omega)
. To simplify the notations, we denote
\mathcal{SH}_{m}(U,\omega)
the set of all
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions with respect to
\omega
in
U
. The set of all
\omega
-plurisubharmonic functions on
U
is denoted by
\mathcal{PSH}(U,\omega)=\mathcal{SH}_{n}(U,\omega)
.

We denote
\omega_{u}\coloneqq\omega+dd^{c}u
.

Remark 2.6
.

By Gårding’s inequality
[
Går59
]
, if
u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(X)
, then
u
is
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic with respect to
\omega
on
X
if and only if the associated form
\omega_{u}
belongs to the closure of
\Gamma_{m}(\omega)
.

The integration by parts formula is valid for
\mathcal{C}^{2}(X)
functions (by Stokes’ theorem). We can see that it still holds for bounded
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions by
[
KN23
, Proposition 3.20]
. We will need the following version.

Proposition 2.7

(Integration by parts)
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega)\cap C^{2}(X)
. Let
T
be a smooth
(n-1,n-1)
-form. Then,

\int_{X}\varphi dd^{c}\psi\wedge T=\int_{X}\psi dd^{c}\varphi\wedge T+2\int_{X}\psi d\varphi\wedge d^{c}T+\int_{X}\psi\varphi dd^{c}T.

Proof.

Observe that
\int_{X}\varphi\partial\psi\wedge\partial T=\int_{X}\varphi\bar{\partial}\psi\wedge\bar{\partial}T=0
by bidegree reason. Hence,

\int_{X}\varphi d^{c}\psi\wedge dT=-\int_{X}\varphi d\psi\wedge d^{c}T.

It thus follows from Stokes’ theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\varphi dd^{c}\psi\wedge T=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi dd^{c}(\varphi T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi d(d^{c}\varphi\wedge T+\varphi d^{c}T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi\left(dd^{c}\varphi\wedge T-d^{c}\varphi\wedge dT+d\varphi\wedge d^{c}T+\varphi dd^{c}T\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi dd^{c}\varphi\wedge T+2\int_{X}\psi d\varphi\wedge d^{c}T+\int_{X}\psi\varphi dd^{c}T.

∎

We state an important result for
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions (see
[
KN16
]
, Lemma 2.3).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{SH}_{m}(\Omega,\omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Omega)
and
T
be a smooth
(n-k,n-k)
-form with
1\leq k\leq m-1
. Then,

|\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge T/\omega^{n}|\leq C_{n,k,\|T\|}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}/\omega^{n},

where
C_{n,k,\|T\|}
is a uniform constant depending only on
n,k
and
\sup_{\Omega}\|T\|
.

We remark that the assumption
k<m
is crucial.

We end this subsection with a
L^{1}
-compactness result (see
[
KN16
, lemma 3.3]
).

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega)
be normalized by
\sup_{X}u=0
. Then there exists a uniform constant
A>0
depending only on
X,\omega
such that

\int_{X}|u|\omega^{n}\leq A.

2.2.
The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

Let
h
be a smooth real-valued function and
u,v
be Borel functions. Let
T
be a positive current of bidegree
(n-2,n-2)
. We state the following Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (see
[
Ngu16
, Proposition 1.4]
,
[
KN23
, Lemma 2.3]
).

Lemma 2.10
.

There exists a uniform constant
C
depending on
\omega
such that

\left|\int_{X}uvdh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge T\right|^{2}\leq C\int_{X}|u|^{2}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega\wedge T\int_{X}|v|^{2}\omega^{2}\wedge T.

Although the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality typically requires
T
to be positive, it still holds for
T=\gamma^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m-1}
, which is not necessarily positive, with
\gamma
being
m
-positive with respect to
\omega
(see
[
KN23
, lemma 2.4]
).

2.3.
Capacity

Let
E\subset X
be a Borel subset.

Definition 2.11
.

The
(\omega,m)
-capacity of
E
is defined by

\mathrm{Cap}_{\omega,m}(E)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\int_{E}\omega_{\varphi}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}:\varphi\in\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega),0\leq\varphi\leq 1\right\}.

It’s clear that the
(\omega,m)
-capacity is well defined for
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(X)
. Thanks to
[
KN23
, theorem 3.3]
, the Hessian operator for bounded
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic function is well defined, and so is the capacity.

Since we will need an upper bound for the capacity of sublevel sets
\{\psi<-t\}
, controlling the terms
d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\omega^{p}
when
\varphi\in\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega)
(which arises from integration by parts) can be difficult. We therefore introduce the following version of the Hessian capacity.

Definition 2.12
.

We define:

\widetilde{\mathrm{Cap}}_{\omega,m}(E)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\int_{E}\omega_{\varphi}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}:\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(X,\omega),0\leq\varphi\leq 1\right\}.

We observe that
\widetilde{\mathrm{Cap}}_{\omega,m}(E)\leq\mathrm{Cap}_{\omega,m}(E)
, since
\mathcal{PSH}(X,\omega)=\mathcal{SH}_{n}(X,\omega)\subset\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega)
.

3.
Integrability of
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions on Hermitian manifolds

Proposition 3.1

(Volume-Capacity estimate)
.

For
1<\tau<\frac{n}{n-m}
, there exists a constant
C_{\tau}
such that for each Borel subset
K
of
X
,

V(K)\leq C_{\tau}\widetilde{\mathrm{Cap}}_{\omega,m}^{\tau}(K),

where
V(K)=\int_{K}\omega^{n}
.

Proof.

The argument presented in
[
KN16
, Proposition 3.6]
is still valid here, as we use the estimates for
\omega
-plurisubharmonic solutions to the Monge–Ampère equations. For the sake of completeness, we provide a slightly different proof below.

We normalize the volume form such that
\int_{X}\omega^{n}=1
. We can assume that
V(K)>0
, otherwise the inequality to be proved is trivial. Fixing
p>1
, we solve the complex Monge–Ampère equation

(MA)

(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=af_{K}\omega^{n},\;\sup_{X}u=0,\text{ where }f_{K}=\left(V(K)^{-1/p}{\bf 1}_{K}+1\right).

The existence of
a>0
and
u\in\mathcal{PSH}(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}
follows from
[
KN15
]
. We next show how to control the constant
a>0
uniformly. Observe first that
(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}\geq a\omega^{n}
, hence the domination principle,
[
GL23
, Corollary 1.13]
, gives
a\leq 1
.
We also have

1=\|V(K)^{-1/p}{\bf 1}_{K}\|_{p}\leq\|f_{K}\|_{p}\leq\|V(K)^{-1/p}{\bf 1}_{K}\|_{p}+\|1\|_{p}\leq 2.

It thus follows from
[
GL23
, Theorem 2.1]
that

u\geq-C_{1},

where
C_{1}\geq 1
is a uniform constant, independent of
K
.
We also deduce from Step 1 of
[
GL23
, Theorem 2.1]
that there exists a uniform constant
C_{2}>0
, independent on
K
, and a bounded
\omega
-psh function
\psi
,
-1\leq\psi\leq 0
, such that

(\omega+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}\geq C_{2}^{-1}f_{K}\omega^{n}.

We conclude from the domination principle, see
[
GL23
, Corollary 1.13]
, that
C_{2}^{-1}\leq a
.

On the other hand, the mixed Monge–Ampère inequality
[
Ngu16
]
yields that

\omega_{u}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\geq a^{m/n}f_{K}^{m/n}\omega^{n}.

For
v=C_{1}^{-1}u
, we then have

\widetilde{\mathrm{Cap}}_{\omega,m}(K)\geq\int_{K}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\geq a^{m/n}C_{1}^{-m}V(K)^{1-\frac{m}{pn}}\geq{C_{2}}^{-m/n}C_{1}^{-m}V(K)^{1-\frac{m}{pn}}.

Hence, for all
p>1
, there exist
C_{p}
such that

V(K)\leq C_{p}\widetilde{\mathrm{Cap}}_{\omega,m}(K)^{\frac{pn}{pn-m}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a bounded
\omega
-plurisubharmonic function satisfying
-1\leq\varphi\leq 0
, and
\psi
be a bounded
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic function with respect to
\omega
, normalized by
\sup_{X}\psi=0
. Then, there exists a uniform constant
C>0
, depending on
\omega
,
n
,
m
, such that the following inequality holds:

\int_{X}\left|\psi\right|\omega_{\varphi}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\leq C.

We emphasize here that the constant
C
does not depend on the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
\psi
.

Proof.

By approximation, we can also assume that
\varphi
and
\psi
are smooth. We will prove the following bound

\int_{X}\left|\psi\right|\omega_{\varphi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq C_{k}

for all
k\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, by induction on
k
. Here
C_{k}
are uniform constants independent of
\psi
and
\varphi
.
We denote by
K_{1},K_{2},\cdots
uniform positive constants. We fix
A
such that

\int_{X}|u|\omega^{n}\leq A,\text{ for all }u\in\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X),\;\sup_{X}u=0.

We remark that there exists
B\geq 0
, such that

-B\omega^{2}\leq dd^{c}\omega\leq B\omega^{2}\text{ and }-B\omega^{3}\leq d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\leq B\omega^{3}.

Up to enlarging
B
, we can also assume that

(B)

-B\omega^{k+1}\leq dd^{c}(\omega^{k})\leq B\omega^{k+1},\;\forall k.

First we prove the bound for
k=1
. Using integration by parts (Proposition
2.7
), and the compactness result (Lemma
2.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\psi|\omega_{\varphi}\wedge\omega^{n-1}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega^{n}+\int_{X}(-\psi)dd^{c}\varphi\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A+\int_{X}(-\varphi)dd^{c}\left(\psi\wedge\omega^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A+\int_{X}(-\varphi)dd^{c}\psi\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle+2\int_{X}(-\varphi)d\psi\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-1}+\int_{X}(-\varphi)\psi dd^{c}\omega^{n-1}.

A basic computation yields:

\int_{X}(-\varphi)dd^{c}\psi\wedge\omega^{n-1}=\int_{X}(-\varphi)\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega^{n-1}+\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}.

The assumption
-1\leq\varphi\leq 0
implies that
\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}\leq 0
. Moreover, due to the positivity of
\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega^{n-1}
, it follows that

\int_{X}(-\varphi)\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq\int_{X}(\sup_{X}|\varphi|)\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq\int_{X}\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega^{n-1}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\omega^{n}+\int_{X}dd^{c}\psi\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\omega^{n}+\int_{X}\psi dd^{c}(\omega^{n-1}).

Together with (
B
) and the compactness result, we thus obtain that

\int_{X}(-\varphi)dd^{c}\psi\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq V+BA.

Furthermore, by the same computation as above,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-\varphi)\psi\wedge dd^{c}\omega^{n-1}\leq

\displaystyle B\int_{X}(-\varphi)(-\psi)\omega^{n}\leq BA.

We need to obtain an upper bound for
\int_{X}(-\varphi)d\psi\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-1}
. After an elementary computation, Stokes’ theorem and (
3.1
) yield

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-\varphi)d\psi\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\psi d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-1}+\int_{X}\psi\varphi dd^{c}\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\psi d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-1}+BA.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (Lemma
2.10
) yields

\displaystyle-\int_{X}(-\psi)d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle=-(n-1)\int_{X}(-\psi)d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}\left(\int_{X}(-\psi)d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\varphi\wedge\omega^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega^{n}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}A^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}(-\psi)d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\varphi\wedge\omega^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}.

We claim that

(3.2)

d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\varphi\leq\omega+\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}(\varphi+1)^{2}.

Indeed,
\omega+1/2dd^{c}(\varphi+1)^{2}-d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\varphi=\omega+(\varphi+1)dd^{c}\varphi=(\varphi+1)\omega_{\varphi}-\varphi\omega
is positive because of the
\omega
-plurisubharmonicity of
\varphi
and the assumption
-1\leq\varphi\leq 0
.

So we obtain that

\int_{X}(-\varphi)d\psi\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-1}\leq BA+K_{1}A\left(\int_{X}(-\psi)(\omega+1/2dd^{c}(\varphi+1)^{2})\wedge\omega^{n-1}\right)^{1/2}.

To continue we denote

A(\psi)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{u}\wedge\omega^{n-1}:0\leq u\leq 1,u\in\mathcal{PSH}(X,\omega)\cap C^{2}(X)\right\}.

From all the above computations we obtain

\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{\varphi}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq(A+V+3BA)+2K_{1}A^{1/2}\left(A(\psi)\right)^{1/2}.

Taking the supremum over all such
\varphi
we arrive at

A(\psi)\leq(A+V+3BA))+2K_{1}A^{1/2}\left(A(\psi)\right)^{1/2},

which implies that
A(\psi)
is uniformly bounded from above, independently of
\psi
. Thus, the bound holds for
k=1
. Suppose now that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, with
1\leq j\leq k-1
, and for all
\psi\in\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega),\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(X,\omega)
with normalizing condition as above, the following inequality holds:

\int_{X}\left|\psi\right|\omega_{\varphi}^{j}\wedge\omega^{n-j}\leq C_{j}.

We need to infer the following inequality

\int_{X}\left|\psi\right|\omega_{\varphi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq C_{k}.

Since

\int_{X}\left|\psi\right|\omega_{\varphi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\\
=\int_{X}\left|\psi\right|\omega\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+\int_{X}\left|\psi\right|(dd^{c}\varphi)\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k},

using induction hypothesis it is enough to estimate the second term. The integration by parts (Proposition
2.7
) yields

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(-\psi\right)(dd^{c}\varphi)\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)dd^{c}(\psi\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)dd^{c}\psi\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+2\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)d\psi\wedge d^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)\psi dd^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right).

We now deal with the first term of (
3.3
). Observe at first that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)dd^{c}\psi\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}-\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)\omega\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}\omega_{\psi}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\omega\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+\int_{X}dd^{c}\psi\wedge(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}),

where the first inequality follows from the assumption that
\varphi\leq 0
, and that
\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}
is positive since
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(X,\omega)
while the second inequality holds because of the normalization of
\varphi
and the positivity of
\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
.
Note that

\int_{X}\omega\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{k,j}\int_{X}\omega^{j+1}\wedge(dd^{c}\varphi)^{k-1-j}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

For each term in the sum, integration by parts, along with the bound on
\varphi
, leads to

\int_{X}\omega\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq K_{2}.

Moreover, an elementary computation yields:

(3.4)

\displaystyle dd^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)=

\displaystyle(k-1)(k-2)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-3}\wedge d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle+(k-1)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}dd^{c}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-k}+2(k-1)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge dd^{c}\omega^{n-k}.

Thus, performing integration by parts, it follows from (
B
) and the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle\int_{X}dd^{c}\psi\wedge\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi dd^{c}(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{3}\int_{X}(-\psi)(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k+2}+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-3}\wedge\omega^{n-k+3})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{3}(C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}+C_{k-3}).

We can also infer from (
3.4
) and the induction hypothesis that the third term of (
3.3
) is uniformly bounded:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-\varphi)\psi dd^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)\leq

\displaystyle K_{4}\int_{X}(-\varphi)\psi(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k+2}+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-3}\wedge\omega^{n-k+3})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{4}\sup_{X}|\varphi|(C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}+C_{k-3})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{5}.

We now control the second term of (
3.3
). Stokes’ theorem yields

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(-\varphi\right)d\psi\wedge d^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)=\int_{X}\psi d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\psi\varphi dd^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right),

where the second term is bounded by
K_{5}
, for the same reason as in (
3.5
).
Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (Lemma
2.10
) for the first term, we obtain that

(3.6)

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left(-\psi\right)d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left(-\psi\right)d\varphi\wedge\left((k-1)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-k}+(n-k)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(K_{6}\int_{X}(-\psi)d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\varphi\wedge\omega\wedge T\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega^{2}\wedge T\right)^{1/2},

where
T=(k-1)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+(n-k)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}
is positive. Applying the induction hypothesis,
\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega^{2}\wedge T
can be controlled by
((k-1)C_{k-2}+(n-k)C_{k-1})
.

By the induction hypothesis, and applying (
3.2
) to (
3.6
), we arrive at:

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(-\psi\right)d\varphi\wedge d^{c}\left(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{6}^{1/2}\left((k-1)C_{k-1}+(n-k)C_{k-1}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}(-\psi)\left(\omega+\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}(\varphi+1)^{2}\right)\wedge\omega\wedge T\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{7}\left(\int_{X}(-\psi)\left(\omega+\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}(\varphi+1)^{2}\right)\wedge\omega\wedge T\right)^{1/2}.

To simplify the notation, we write
T_{1}=(k-1)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}
,
T_{2}=(n-k)\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
, and

\displaystyle A_{1}\coloneqq\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{u}\wedge T_{1},A_{2}\coloneqq\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{u}\wedge T_{2},u=\frac{(\varphi+1)^{2}}{2}-1.

Set
v_{1}\coloneqq\frac{u+(k-2)\varphi}{k-1}
, and
v_{2}\coloneqq\frac{u+(k-1)\varphi}{k}
. We remark that
-1\leq v_{1},v_{2}\leq 0
, and that

\displaystyle\omega_{u},\omega_{\varphi}\leq

\displaystyle(k-1)\omega+dd^{c}\left(u+(k-2)\varphi\right);

\displaystyle\omega_{u},\omega_{\varphi}\leq

\displaystyle k\omega+dd^{c}\left(u+(k-1)\varphi\right).

By induction hypothesis, we obtain that

\displaystyle A_{1}\leq

\displaystyle(k-1)^{k-1}(k-1)\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{v_{1}}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}\leq(k-1)^{k-1}(k-1)C_{k-1};

\displaystyle A_{2}\leq

\displaystyle k^{k}(n-k)\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{v_{2}}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

Set
S(\psi)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{v}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}:-1\leq v\leq 0,v\in\mathcal{PSH}(X,\omega)\right\}
. From the preceding computations, we conclude that

\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{\varphi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq K_{8}+\left(K_{9}S(\psi)\right)^{1/2},

where
K_{8},K_{9}
are independent on
\psi
.
Taking the supremum over all
-1\leq\varphi\leq 0
, we obtain

S(\psi)\leq K_{8}+\left(K_{9}S(\psi)\right)^{1/2},

which leads to an uniform upper bound for
\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega_{v}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
for
-1\leq v\leq 0
. The proof is thereby complete.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\psi\in\mathcal{SH}_{m}(X,\omega)
satisfying
\sup_{X}\psi=-1
and for every
t>0
we have

\widetilde{\mathrm{Cap}}_{\omega,m}(\psi<-t)\leq C/t.

Proof.

We fix
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(X,\omega)
satisfying
-1\leq\varphi\leq 0
.

By lemma
3.2
, we deduce that

\int_{(\psi<-t)}(-\psi)\omega^{m}_{\varphi}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\leq C.

We also observe that

\int_{(\psi<-t)}(-\psi)\omega^{m}_{\varphi}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\geq\int_{(\psi<-t)}t\omega^{m}_{\varphi}\wedge\omega^{n-m},

and this completes the proof.
∎

Proof of the main theorem.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\sup_{X}\varphi=-1
. Fix
p<\frac{n}{n-m}
and
q
such that
p<q<\frac{n}{n-m}
. A fundamental calculation yields that

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-\varphi)^{p}\omega^{n}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Vol}(\{(-\varphi)^{p}>t\})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{Vol}(X)dt+\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Vol}(\{(-\varphi)^{p}>t\})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\omega^{n}+\int_{1}^{+\infty}p\mathrm{Vol}(\{(-\varphi)>s\})s^{p-1}ds.

It follows from the volume-capacity estimate (Proposition
3.1
) and Corollary
3.3
that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}p\mathrm{Vol}(\{(-\varphi)>s\})s^{p-1}ds\leq

\displaystyle C_{q}p\int_{1}^{+\infty}s^{p-1}\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{Cap}}_{\omega,m}(\{\varphi<-s\})\right)^{q}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,q}\int_{1}^{+\infty}s^{p-1-q}ds<+\infty,

where
C_{p,q}
depend only on
p,q
.
∎

Rates of Bulk Convergence for Ensembles of Classical Compact Groups
Mengchun Cai

Abstract
This paper considers random matrices distributed according to Haar measure in different classical compact groups. Utilizing the determinantal point structures of their nontrivial eigenangles, with respect to the L 1 L_{1} -Wasserstein distance, we obtain the rate of convergence for different ensembles to the sine point process when the dimension of matrices N N is sufficiently large. Specifically, the rate is roughly of order N − 2 N^{-2} on the unitary group and of order N − 1 N^{-1} on the orthogonal group and the compact symplectic group.

Introduction

The eigenvalues of large random matrices drawn uniformly from classical compact groups are of interest in a variety of fields, including statistics, number theory, and mathematical physics. The nontrivial eigenangles of random matrices in
\mathbb{U}_{N}
,
\mathbb{SO}_{N}
and
\mathbb{SP}_{2N}
form determinantal point processes converging to the sine point process after proper scaling when the dimension of matrices
N
goes to infinity; see
[
5
]
for more details. With these limit laws established, one can ask for the rate, as a function of
N
, of the convergence.

Let us first introduce compact groups addressed in this paper. The
orthogonal group
,
\mathbb{O}_{N}
, contains all
N\times N
real matrices
U
such that
U^{T}U=UU^{T}=I_{N}
. The
unitary group
,
\mathbb{U}_{N}
, is formed by all
N\times N
matrices
U
over
\mathbb{C}
with
U^{*}U=UU^{*}=I_{N}
. Similarly, any element in the compact
symplectic group

\mathbb{SP}_{2N}
is a
2N\times 2N
matrix
U\in\mathbb{U}(2N)
such that
U^{T}J_{N}U=UJ_{N}U^{T}=J_{N}
where

J_{N}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&I_{N}\\
-I_{N}&0\end{matrix}\right).

In particular, for
\mathbb{O}_{N}
, we tend to consider its two components: the
special orthogonal group
,
\mathbb{SO}_{N}:=\left\{U\in\mathbb{O}_{N}:\det U=1\right\}
and the
negative coset
, which is not a group,
\mathbb{SO}^{-}_{N}:=\{U\in\mathbb{O}_{N}:\det U=-1\}
. According to Haar’s theorem (see Theorem
1.14

[
5
]
for our special setting), if
G
is any group defined above, there is a unique probability measure
\mu
on
G
such that for any measurable subset
\mathcal{A}\subset G
and fixed
M\in G
, we have

\mu\left(\mathcal{A}M\right)=\mu\left(M\mathcal{A}\right)=\mu(\mathcal{A}),

where
\mathcal{A}M=\{AM:A\in\mathcal{A}\}
and
M\mathcal{A}=\{MA:A\in\mathcal{A}\}
. This
\mu
is called the
Haar probability measure
in
G
.

By some simple linear algebra, for any element in each group defined above, we see all of its eigenvalues must have absolute value
1
. Thus, it is reasonable for us to consider the corresponding angle. Given an eigenvalue
e^{i\theta},\penalty\ 0\leq\theta<2\pi

\theta
an eigenangle of this matrix. To better capture properties induced by engenangles, especially the bulk result in this paper, we mainly focus on nontrivial eigenangles defined below. For each matrix in
\mathbb{SO}_{2N+1}
, we know it has
1
as an eigenvalue, each matrix in
\mathbb{SO}^{-}_{2N+1}
has
-1
as an eigenvalue, and each matrix in
\mathbb{SO}^{-}_{2N+2}
must have both
1
and

as eigenvalues; those eigenvalues are referred as trivial eigenvalues. When discussing
\mathbb{SO}_{N},\penalty\ \mathbb{SO}^{-}_{N}
or
\mathbb{SP}_{2N}

\mathbb{U}_{N}
are considered nontrivial. By convention, in any of

A point process

Fix a point process

\mathcal{S}_{j}\circ\mathcal{S}_{2k+2-j}+\mathcal{S}_{2k+2-j}\circ\mathcal{S}_{j}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}\circ\mathcal{S}_{2k+1-j}+\mathcal{S}_{2k+1-j}\circ\mathcal{C}_{j}
respectively. Thus, plugging
(\ref{Co})
and
(\ref{Si})
back into (
3.3
) leads to our promising decomposition. Substituting all negative signs by positive ones in the previous discussion, we can finish the decomposition for
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2k+1}
.∎

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.
Define
N_{2}(x,y):=\pi(x-y)\cos(\pi(x-y))
and
N_{3}(x,y):=\pi(x+y)\cos(\pi(x+y))
. Notice
N_{j}(-tx,-ty)=-N_{j}(tx,ty)
for any
t\in[-1,1]
, we can write them as

N_{j}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dN_{j}(tx,ty)}{dt}dt.

Similar to the previous proof, the following integral expression

K_{2}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\left[\cos(t\pi(x-y))-t\pi(x-y)\sin(t\pi(x-y))\right]dt

and

K_{3}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\left[\cos(t\pi(x+y))-t\pi(x+y)\sin(t\pi(x+y))\right]dt

follows

\frac{dN_{2}(tx,ty)}{dt}=\pi(x-y)\cos(\pi t(x-y))-t\pi^{2}(x-y)^{2}\sin(\pi t(x-y))

and

\frac{dN_{3}(tx,ty)}{dt}=\pi(x+y)\cos(\pi t(x+y))-t\pi^{2}(x+y)^{2}\sin(\pi t(x+y)).

Expand the integrand for
K_{2}
as:

\begin{split}&\cos(t\pi x)\cos(t\pi y)+\sin(t\pi x)\sin(t\pi y)-t\pi x\sin(t\pi x)\cos(t\pi y)\\
&\penalty\ +t\pi y\sin(t\pi x)\cos(t\pi y)+t\pi x\cos(t\pi x)\sin(t\pi y)-t\pi y\sin(t\pi y)\cos(t\pi x).\end{split}

Substituting the variable
t
by
\frac{t}{s}
above completes the proof for
\mathcal{K}_{2}
. A similar expansion for
K_{3}
gives us the decomposition for
\mathcal{K}_{3}
.∎

3.2
CUE bulk

The proof of the CUE bulk result depends on the Laurent expansion of the cosecant function
\csc
after comparison between
K_{C_{N}}^{Bulk}
and
K_{Sine}
. For
0<|x|<\pi
, recall the Laurent series:

\csc(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}2\left(2^{2n-1}-1\right)B_{2n}x^{2n-1}}{(2n)!}

(3.6)

where
B_{k}
is the
k
th Bernoulli number. Before going into the main proof, we first introduce two lemmas to control main terms appearing in our discussion below.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have
|B_{2n}|\leq Cn^{2n+\frac{1}{2}}(\pi e)^{2n}
for some uniform constant
C
where
B_{k}
is the
k
th Bernoulli number.

Proof.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, according to Corollary
4.12
in
[
1
]
, the Bernoulli number
B_{2n}
can be expressed as:

B_{2n}=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}2(2n)!}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\zeta(2n)

where
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function. By the definition of
\zeta
, it is obvious that
1\leq\zeta(t)\leq\zeta(2)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}
for any
t\in[2,+\infty)
. By the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{t\in\mathbb{R},\penalty\ t\rightarrow+\infty}\zeta(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lim_{t\in\mathbb{R},\penalty\ t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{t}}=1

and hence,
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\zeta(2n)=1
.
Applying Stirling’s formula:
n!e^{n}\sim\sqrt{2\pi}n^{n+\frac{1}{2}}
, it is not difficult to see

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(\pi e)^{2n}|B_{2n}|}{n^{2n+\frac{1}{2}}}=4\sqrt{\pi}

and hence, there exists a uniform constant
C>4\sqrt{\pi}
such that
|B_{2n}|\leq Cn^{2n+\frac{1}{2}}(\pi e)^{2n}
for any
n
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, both
\|\mathcal{C}_{j}\|_{2}
and
\|\mathcal{S}_{j}\|_{2}
are bounded by
\frac{\sqrt{2s}(\pi s)^{j}}{2j+1}
.

Proof.

For any
j
, direct computation leads to

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|C_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2s}\int_{-s}^{s}\int_{-s}^{s}\left(\frac{\pi xy}{s}\right)^{2j}\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi xy}{s}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{2j}}{2s^{2j+1}}\int_{-s}^{s}y^{2j}dy\int_{-s}^{s}x^{2j}dx=\frac{4\pi^{2j}s^{4j+2}}{2s^{2j+1}(2j+1)^{2}}=\frac{2\pi^{2j}s^{2j+1}}{(2j+1)^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|S_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2s}\int_{-s}^{s}\int_{-s}^{s}\left(\frac{\pi xy}{s}\right)^{2j}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi xy}{s}\right)dxdy\leq\frac{2\pi^{2j}s^{2j+1}}{(2j+1)^{2}}.

Taking the square root finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
Recall

K_{C_{N}}^{Bulk}(x,y)=\frac{\sin(\pi(x-y))}{N\sin\left(\frac{\pi(x-y)}{N}\right)}

and

K_{Sine}(x,y)=\frac{\sin(\pi(x-y))}{\pi(x-y)}.

On any compact domain
D^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, according to (
3.6
), we can write the difference as:

K_{C_{N}}^{Bulk}(x,y)-K_{Sine}(x,y)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{2k+1}}{N^{2k}}(\pi(x-y))^{2k+1}\sin(\pi(x-y))=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{2k+1}}{N^{2k}}A_{2k+1}(x,y)

where

c_{2k+1}=\frac{(-1)^{k}2(2^{2k+1}-1)B_{2k+2}}{(2k+2)!}.

Define an operator

\mathcal{K}^{d}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{2k+1}}{N^{2k}}\mathcal{A}_{2k+1}

on the same domain.
It is clear that if the right side is convergent with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, then we must have
\mathcal{K}_{C_{N}}^{Bulk}-\mathcal{K}_{Sine}=\mathcal{K}_{N}^{d}
. In fact, the
Proposition
3.2
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields:

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}_{2k+1}\|_{1}&\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
j\end{matrix}\right)\left(\|\mathcal{C}_{j}\mathcal{C}_{2k+2-j}\|_{1}+\|\mathcal{C}_{2k+2-j}\mathcal{C}_{j}\|_{1}+\|\mathcal{S}_{j}\mathcal{S}_{2k+2-j}\|_{1}+\|\mathcal{S}_{2k+2-j}\mathcal{S}_{j}\|_{1}\right)\\
&+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+1\\
j\end{matrix}\right)(2k+2)\left(\|\mathcal{C}_{2k+1-j}\mathcal{S}_{j}\|_{1}+\|\mathcal{S}_{j}\mathcal{C}_{2k+1-j}\|_{1}\right)\\
&\leq 2\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
j\end{matrix}\right)\left(\|\mathcal{C}_{j}\|_{2}\|\mathcal{C}_{2k+2-j}\|_{2}+\|\mathcal{S}_{j}\|_{2}\|\mathcal{S}_{2k+2-j}\|_{2}\right)\\
&\penalty\ \penalty\ +2\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+1\\
j\end{matrix}\right)(2k+2)\left(\|\mathcal{C}_{2k+1-j}\|_{2}\|\mathcal{S}_{j}\|_{2}+\|\mathcal{S}_{2k+1-j}\|_{2}\|\mathcal{C}_{j}\|_{2}\right)\end{split}

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Combining Lemma
3.6
, we have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}_{2k+1}\|_{1}\leq&4\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
j\end{matrix}\right)\frac{2s(s\pi)^{2k+2}}{(2j+1)(4k+6-(2j+1))}\\
&+(16k+16)\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+1\\
j\end{matrix}\right)\frac{2s(s\pi)^{2k+1}}{(2j+1)(4k+4-(2j+1))}\\
&\leq\frac{8s(\pi s)^{2k+2}}{4k+5}\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
j\end{matrix}\right)+\frac{16s(\pi s)^{2k+1}(k+1)}{4k+3}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+1\\
j\end{matrix}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{8s(2\pi s)^{2k+2}}{4k+5}+\frac{16s(k+1)(2\pi s)^{2k+1}}{4k+3}\\
&=8s(2\pi s)^{2k+1}\left(\frac{2\pi s}{4k+5}+\frac{2k+2}{4k+3}\right),\end{split}

(3.7)

where we used the fact that
(2j+1)(4k+6-(2j+1))\geq 4k+5
for any
0\leq j\leq k+1
and
(2j+1)(4k+4-(2j+1))\geq 4k+3
for any
0\leq j\leq k
in the second inequality and the binomial expression of
2^{m}=(1+1)^{m}
in the last one. It is not difficult to find constants
C_{1}
and
C_{1}^{\prime}
such that the term in the last line of (
3.7
) is bounded by
(C_{1}s^{2}+C_{1}^{\prime}s)(2\pi s)^{2k+1}
for any
k
.

Now go back to our original operator; we see

\|\mathcal{K}_{N}^{d}\|_{1}\leq\frac{C_{1}s^{2}+C_{1}^{\prime}s}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{2k+1}|(2\pi s)^{2k+1}}{N^{2k}}\leq\frac{C_{1}s^{2}+C_{1}^{\prime}s}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2k+2}|B_{2k+2}|(2\pi s)^{2k+1}}{(2k+2)!N^{2k}}.

(3.8)

By Lemma
3.5
, there exists a uniform constant
C_{2}
such that
|B_{2k}|\leq C_{2}k^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}(\pi e)^{-2k}
for any
k
. In addition, using Stirling’s formula, we know that there is a constant
C_{3}
satisfying
k!\geq C_{3}\sqrt{k}(k)^{k}e^{-k}
for any
k
. Thus, each term in the summation of
(\ref{series})
is bounded as:

\frac{2^{2k+2}(2\pi s)^{2k+1}|B_{2k+2}|}{(2k+2)!N^{2k}}\leq\frac{(2\pi s)^{2k+1}C_{2}2^{2k+2}(k+1)^{2k+2}\sqrt{k+1}e^{2k+2}}{(\pi e)^{2k+2}C_{3}\sqrt{2k+2}(2k+2)^{2k+2}N^{2k}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}C_{2}s}{\pi C_{3}}\left(\frac{2s}{N}\right)^{2k}.

Recall
\frac{2s}{N}<1
by our assumption, the trace class norm of
\mathcal{K}_{N}^{d}
is in fact controlled by a convergent geometric sequence:

\|\mathcal{K}_{N}^{d}\|_{1}\leq\frac{Cs^{3}+C^{\prime}s^{2}}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2s}{N}\right)^{k}=\frac{Cs^{3}+C^{\prime}s^{2}}{N^{2}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{16s^{4}}{N^{4}}}=\frac{CN^{2}s^{3}+C^{\prime}N^{2}s^{2}}{N^{4}-16s^{4}}.

As a result,
\|\mathcal{K}_{C_{N}}^{Bulk}-\mathcal{K}_{Sine}\|_{1}
is also bounded by the same term on the far right. The required result follows from absorbing constants.∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.
We first consider the case of
\mathbb{SO}_{2N}
. Split the corresponding kernel as:

\begin{split}K_{H_{N}}^{Bulk}(x,y)&=\frac{\sin\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{2N}\right)\pi(x-y)\right)}{2N\sin\left(\frac{\pi(x-y)}{2N}\right)}-\frac{\cos\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{2N}\right)\pi(x+y)+N\pi\right)}{2N\cos\left(\frac{\pi(x+y)}{2N}\right)}\\

&\penalty\ \penalty\ -\frac{(-1)^{N}\cos\left(\pi(x+y)\right)}{2N}-\frac{(-1)^{N}\sin\left(\pi(x+y)\right)\tan\left(\frac{\pi(x+y)}{2N}\right)}{2N}\\

Observe
K_{1,N}
converges to
K_{Sine}
as

2.1
. Use the Laurent expansion for the cotangent function
\cot

K_{1,N}(x,y)-K_{Sine}(x,y)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{2k+1}}{N^{2k}}(\pi(x-y))^{2k-1}\sin(\pi(x-y))

where

b_{2k+1}=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}B_{2k+2}}{(2k+2)!}

is the Laurent coefficient for the
\cot
function. Consider the operator:

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{2k+1}}{N^{2k}}\mathcal{A}_{2k-1}.

We want to bound its trace class norm. For each term in the right summation, according to
(\ref{operator A})
, we still have

\|\mathcal{A}_{2k-1}\|_{1}\leq 8s(2\pi s)^{2k-1}\left(\frac{2\pi s}{4k+1}+\frac{2k}{4k-1}\right).

Hence, there are constants
C_{1}
and
C_{1}^{\prime}
such that the right side above is bounded by
(C_{1}s^{2}+C_{1}^{\prime}s)(2\pi s)^{2k-1}
for any
k
.

Now we see

\left\|\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{2k+1}}{N^{2k}}\mathcal{A}_{2k-1}\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{C_{1}s^{2}+C_{1}^{\prime}s}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|B_{2k+2}|(2\pi s)^{2k-1}}{(2k+2)!N^{2k}}.

(3.9)

By Lemma
3.5
and Stirling’s formula, we know each term in the summation of
(\ref{series2})
is bounded by
\frac{C(2s)^{2k-1}}{N^{2k}}
. Thus, from the similar geometric sequence discussion as in the the previous proof, we get

\left\|\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{2k+1}}{N^{2k}}\mathcal{A}_{2k-1}\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{Cs^{2}+C^{\prime}s}{N^{2}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{16s^{4}}{N^{4}}}=\frac{CN^{2}s^{2}+C^{\prime}N^{2}s}{N^{4}-16s^{4}}.

3.3
, applying Lemma
3.6

\|\mathcal{K}_{2,N}\|_{1}

As for the last kernel

K_{4,N}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{2k+1}\left(\frac{x+y}{2N}\right)^{2k+1}\sin\left(\pi(x+y)\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{2k+1}}{(2N)^{2k+2}}A^{\prime}_{2k+1}(x,y)

where

a_{2k+1}=\frac{(-4)^{k+1}(1-4^{k+1})B_{2k+2}}{(2k+2)!}

are the Taylor coefficients for the
\tan

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{2k+1}}{(2N)^{2k+2}}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2k+1}.

Notice we have
\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2k+1}\|_{1}\leq(C_{1}s^{2}+C_{1}^{\prime}s)(2\pi s)^{2k+1}

Black hole thermodynamics is gauge independent
O. Ramírez, Y. Bonder

Abstract
Black hole thermodynamics provides a rare window into the elusive quantum nature of gravity. In the first-order formalism for gravitational theories, where torsion and gauge freedom are present, it has been suggested that the first law of black hole thermodynamics requires a specific gauge choice, which would undermine its fundamental character. By using principal fiber bundles, it has been shown that the first law is independent of this gauge choice. The present work introduces an alternative method that establishes this independence in a more direct manner, thereby reinforcing the status of the first law as a guide toward quantum gravity. This method also facilitates explicit computations of the first law and helps resolve several ambiguities that commonly appear in such analyses.



Black hole thermodynamics has long been regarded as one of the few available clues to the quantum nature of gravity, whose consistent formulation remains among the most important challenges in theoretical physics. The subject traces back to Bekenstein
[
1
,
2
]
, who identified an analogy between the equations describing black holes and the laws of thermodynamics. This idea was later supported by Hawking’s discovery
[
3
]
that quantum effects cause black holes to radiate thermally. The implications of this analogy are profound: the identification of gravitational laws with thermodynamics suggests that spacetime itself may possess underlying microscopic degrees of freedom.

On the other hand, differential forms, first introduced into gravity by Cartan
[
4
,
5
]
, provide a natural framework for formulating modified theories of gravity, which may offer insights into unresolved questions in cosmology. This approach, known as the first-order formalism, treats the vielbein and the spin connection as independent dynamical variables, allowing for a nontrivial torsion tensor; a possibility that has not been experimentally excluded
[
6
,
7
]
. (Nonmetricity is not considered as it leads to pathological behaviors
[
8
]
). Consequently, establishing a connection between black hole thermodynamics and modified gravity, particularly within the first-order formalism, is of central importance.

In the first-order formalism, each choice of transformation, namely, a particular combination of diffeomorphisms and gauge transformations, generates a distinct Noether charge
[
9
]
. This raises a natural question: can the first law of black hole thermodynamics depend on this “gauge choice”? Such a possibility has indeed been suggested in Refs.
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. However, any dependence on unphysical choices would be unsatisfactory for a fundamental law. This issue has been resolved by formulating the problem within the framework of a principal fiber bundle
[
13
]
. In the present work, inspired in Refs.
[
14
,
15
]
, we provide an alternative, more direct method to establish this independence without invoking principal fiber bundles. This method may simplify the computation of the first law of black hole thermodynamics and the discussion of the ambiguities that are present in this calculations.

The following notation is adopted. Spacetime (abstract) indices are denoted by the first lowercase letters of the Latin alphabet and are omitted on differential forms whenever possible, while Greek letters are used for internal (Lie group) indices. Repeated indices imply contraction and are lowered and raised using the corresponding metric and its inverse, respectively. Familiarity with basic operations on differential forms is assumed, and the conventions on differential forms follow those of Ref.
[
16
]
. Derivatives (covariant, exterior, interior, Lie, etc.) and variations act only on the object immediately adjacent to them, whereas integrals are understood to apply to the entire expression. Moreover, when an integral does not explicitly indicate the integration region, it is understood to be over an arbitrary spacetime region. Finally, all tensors are taken to be smooth, and spacetime is assumed to be topologically trivial (contractible).

As is well known, a general field theory in
n
spacetime dimensions is described by its Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
, which is an
n
-form that depends on the dynamical fields, collectively denoted by
\phi
. An arbitrary variation of
\mathcal{L}
gives

\delta\mathcal{L}=\delta\phi\wedge\mathcal{E}_{\phi}+\text{d}\theta(\delta),

(1)

where
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}=0
are the equations of motion and
d
denotes the exterior derivative. The term
\delta\phi\wedge\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
represents the contribution from each independent dynamical field, and their sum is implied. In contrast, the boundary contribution,
\text{d}\theta(\delta)
, can arise from integrating by parts during the variation. In fact,
\theta(\delta)
, which is an
(n-1)
-form, depends linearly on the variation of the fields,
\delta\phi
. Since
(\delta_{1}+\alpha\,\delta_{2})\phi=\delta_{1}\phi+\alpha\,\delta_{2}\phi
, for any real number
\alpha
, it follows that
\theta
is also linear in this sense, a property that is repeatedly used below.

When the variation under consideration is an infinitesimal diffeomorphism along the vector field
\xi
,
\delta\phi=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi
where
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
is the corresponding Lie derivative. Using Cartan’s magic formula
[
17
]
,
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}=\text{d}\text{i}_{\xi}+\text{i}_{\xi}\text{d}
, where
\text{i}_{\xi}
is the interior derivative (contraction), Eq. (
1
) becomes

\text{d}\text{i}_{\xi}\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi\wedge\mathcal{E}_{\phi}+\text{d}\theta(\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi)).

(2)

Here
\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi)
represents an infinitesimal diffeomorphism along
\xi
. Importantly, on shell,
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}=0
. Hence, there exists a conserved current

J(\xi)=\theta(\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi))-\text{i}_{\xi}\mathcal{L},

(3)

such that
\text{d}J(\xi)=0
(recall that
\text{d}^{2}=0
). What is more, by virtue of Poincaré’s lemma, there exists an
(n-2)
-form,
Q(\xi)
, such that
J(\xi)=\text{d}Q(\xi)
.

It is useful to introduce the pre-symplectic current. This form is defined as
[
9
,
13
]

\Omega(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=\int_{\Sigma}\delta_{1}\theta(\delta_{2})-\delta_{2}\theta(\delta_{1})-\theta([\delta_{1},\delta_{2}]),

(4)

where
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
denote arbitrary variations of the dynamical fields,
[\ ,\ ]
is the commutator of variations, and integration is on
\Sigma
, a Cauchy hypersurface. Throughout the text, it becomes apparent that the last term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
4
) is essential for the pre-symplectic current to be well defined in the presence of gauge transformations (cf. Ref.
[
10
]
).

When
\delta_{1}
represents a variation within the space of solutions to the field equations, denoted by
\delta
, and the second variation is
\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi)=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
, the two variations commute. Hence

\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi))=\int_{\Sigma}\delta\theta(\mathcal{L}_{\xi})-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\theta(\delta)=\int_{\Sigma}\text{d}[\delta Q(\xi)-\text{i}_{\xi}\theta(\delta)].

(5)

In this derivation, which is valid on shell, the linearity of
\delta
and its commutativity with
\text{i}_{\xi}
and
d
are used. Also, the definition of
Q(\xi)
is invoked.

The first law of black hole thermodynamics is derived for particular solutions to gravity theories. Hence, from this point onward, an arbitrary vacuum gravity theory is assumed. What is more, in the first-order formalism the dynamical fields are the vielbein 1-form
e^{\mu}
and the spin connection 1-form
\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
, which transform under infinitesimal diffeomorphisms with the corresponding Lie derivative. Conversely, for an infinitesimal gauge transformation with parameter
\lambda^{\mu\nu}=-\lambda^{\nu\mu}
, the transformations are

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)e^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}e^{\nu},

(6)

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{d}\lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\lambda^{\rho}_{\ \nu}\omega^{\mu}_{\ \rho}-\lambda^{\mu}_{\ \rho}\omega^{\rho}_{\ \nu}.

(7)

Note that, since the Lagrangian is a group scalar,

0=\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)\mathcal{L}=\text{d}\theta(\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)),

(8)

where in the last step, the on-shell version of Eq. (
1
) is used. Then, there exists another
(n-2)
-form,
Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)
, such that

\theta(\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda))=\text{d}Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda).

(9)

Let
\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}
be such that
\mathring{\nabla}_{\nu}\xi^{\mu}=e^{a}_{\nu}e^{\mu}_{b}\mathring{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{b}
, where
\mathring{\nabla}_{a}
is the torsion-free covariant derivative. When acting on
\xi^{\mu}=e^{\mu}(\xi)
, which is an algebra-valued
0
-form, it produces

\mathring{\nabla}_{\nu}\xi^{\mu}=\text{i}_{\nu}\left(\text{d}\xi^{\mu}+\mathring{\omega}^{\mu}_{\ \rho}\xi^{\rho}\right).

(10)

Here,
\text{i}_{\nu}
is the interior derivative along the dual vector associated with
e^{\nu}
. What is more, a direct application of Cartan’s magic formula yields

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}e^{\mu}=\text{d}\xi^{\mu}-\text{i}_{\xi}\mathring{\omega}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\wedge e^{\nu}+\mathring{\omega}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\xi^{\nu}=\left(\mathring{\nabla}_{\nu}\xi^{\mu}-\text{i}_{\xi}\mathring{\omega}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\right)e^{\nu},

(11)

with
\mathring{\omega}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
the torsion-free part of
\omega^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
. One can readily show that

e^{[\mu}_{a}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}e^{\nu]a}=\mathring{\nabla}^{[\mu}\xi^{\nu]}+\text{i}_{\xi}\mathring{\omega}^{\mu\nu}=\lambda_{\xi}^{\mu\nu},

(12)

where the brackets denote antisymmetrization of indices. This combination can be interpreted as a gauge parameter, denoted by
\lambda_{\xi}^{\mu\nu}
.

Importantly, in the first-order formalism, the definition of a Killing vector field
\xi
requires
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}e^{\mu}
to be a gauge transformation of
e^{\mu}
whose parameter is given by Eq. (
12
)
(compare with the metric case, where
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
acting on the metric vanishes). Thus, requiring

\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi))=0
for a Killing vector field, as in the metric formalism,
is overly restrictive. Furthermore, for
\xi
to generate a symmetry for all relevant fields, it is necessary
that the Lie derivative of the torsion also vanish. Under this assumption, acting with

\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi)
or with
\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi})
on any of the considered fields
is equivalent. Accordingly, the following identity holds at the level of transformations:

\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi)=\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}).

(13)

It is then natural to assume
[
9
]
that

\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi))=\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi})).

(14)

The right-hand side of this equation satisfies

\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}))=\int_{\Sigma}\delta\theta(\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}))-\theta(\delta_{\text{GT}}(\delta\lambda_{\xi})),

(15)

where it is used that
\theta(\delta)
is a group scalar, i.e.,
\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi})\,\theta(\delta)=0
, and

[\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)]=\delta_{\text{GT}}(\delta\lambda),

(16)

as
\lambda^{\mu\nu}
depends on the dynamical fields (i.e., it is “field-dependent” in the terminology of Ref.
[
18
]
). Moreover, Eq. (
15
) can be written as

\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}))=\int_{\Sigma}\text{d}\left[\delta Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi})-Q_{\text{GT}}(\delta\lambda_{\xi})\right],

(17)

where the fact that
d
and
\delta
commute is applied. Comparing Eqs. (
5
) and (
17
), as suggested by Eq. (
14
), and using Stoke’s theorem yields

0=\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\delta Q(\xi)-\,\text{i}_{\xi}\theta(\delta)-\delta Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi})+Q_{\text{GT}}(\delta\lambda_{\xi}).

(18)

By definition, a black hole is a spacetime that is asymptotically flat and its “exterior region” is globally hyperbolic
[
19
]
. Consequently,
\Sigma
can be taken as a Cauchy surface of this region. Black hole thermodynamics also requires the black hole to be stationary, which implies the existence of a Killing vector field
\xi
. It is further assumed that there is a bifurcate Killing horizon, whose bifurcation surface is denoted by
\mathcal{B}
. Under these conditions, the integral in Eq. (
18
) has contributions from
\mathcal{B}
and
i^{0}
, producing the corresponding first law of black hole thermodynamics (further details can be found in Ref.
[
20
]
).

A key question is whether the first law of black hole thermodynamics can also be derived when using a general transformation that combines a diffeomorphism with an arbitrary gauge transformation, a situation referred to as “gauge independence.” Consider the transformation

\delta(\Lambda)=\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi)-\delta_{\text{GT}}(\Lambda),

(19)

where
\Lambda
is a field-dependent gauge parameter, which may also depend on
\xi
for generality. When
\xi
is a Killing vector field, Eq. (
14
) applies, and

\delta(\Lambda)=\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi})-\delta_{\text{GT}}(\Lambda)=\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}-\Lambda),

(20)

where the last equality follows from the properties of the Lie group. Then, as before, the first law of black hole thermodynamics should be derived from

\Omega(\delta,\delta(\Lambda))=\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}-\Lambda)).

(21)

To demonstrate this independence, let
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
denote arbitrary diffeomorphisms or gauge transformations. Then, for any real coefficient
\alpha
, it follows that

\Omega(\delta,\delta_{1}+\alpha\delta_{2})=\Omega(\delta,\delta_{1})+\alpha\,\Omega(\delta,\delta_{2}).

(22)

Then, the left-hand side of Eq. (
21
) satisfies

\Omega(\delta,\delta(\Lambda))=\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{Diff}}(\xi))-\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\Lambda)).

(23)

Similarly, the right-hand side becomes

\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}-\Lambda))=\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}))-\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\Lambda)).

(24)

A comparison of the last two expressions shows that the terms
\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\Lambda))
cancel, yielding Eq. (
14
) and reproducing the “original” first law of black hole thermodynamics. Hence, when formulated through Eq. (
21
), the first law of black hole thermodynamics is gauge independent; nonetheless, one can construct quantities that make this independence explicit, as is done next. In fact, in Ref.
[
9
]
, Einstein–Cartan theory is analyzed, and it is argued that, for the pre-symplectic current to be gauge independent,
\theta
must be modified through the addition of an
ad hoc
exact form. It is shown that this additional contribution arises naturally.

The first observation is that, since
\theta
is linear, and so are the gauge transformations and the exterior derivative,
Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)
must also be linear. In addition, it follows from Eq. (
6
) that

\lambda^{\mu\nu}=-e^{[\mu}_{a}\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)e^{\nu]a}.

(25)

This suggests the following definition:

\gamma(\delta)=-Q_{\text{GT}}\bigl(e^{[\mu}_{a}\delta e^{\nu]a}\bigr).

(26)

Note that
\delta
is arbitrary. However, in the particular case
\delta=\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)
one has

\gamma(\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda))=-Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda).

(27)

Moreover, linearity implies that there exists a co-dimension
2
form, with two antisymmetric group indices,
H_{\mu\nu}
, such that

Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{\mu\nu}H_{\mu\nu}.

(28)

Furthermore,
H_{\mu\nu}
depends on the dynamical fields and transforms covariantly under gauge transformations. This makes it possible to show that

\gamma(\delta)=-H_{\mu\nu}\,e^{\mu}{}_{a}\,\delta e^{\nu a}.

(29)

Now, one can consider an arbitrary variation of Eq. (
12
),
\delta\lambda_{\xi}^{\mu\nu}
, and use it as the argument of
Q_{\text{GT}}
. This yields

\displaystyle Q_{\text{GT}}(\delta\lambda_{\xi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{\xi})^{\mu}{}_{\rho}(e^{\rho}_{a}\delta e^{\nu a})H_{\mu\nu}+(\lambda_{\xi})^{\nu}{}_{\rho}(e^{\mu}_{a}\delta e^{\rho a})H_{\mu\nu}

(30)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(e^{\mu}{}_{a}\delta e^{\nu a})H_{\mu\nu}.

The first two terms correspond to a gauge transformation of
\gamma(\delta)
, and vanish since it is a group scalar. On the other hand, since
H_{\mu\nu}
transforms covariantly, when
\xi
is a Killing vector field, its Lie derivative along
\xi
corresponds to a gauge transformation. Consequently, Eq. (
30
) can be rewritten as

Q_{\text{GT}}(\delta\lambda_{\xi})=-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\gamma(\delta)=-\text{i}_{\xi}\text{d}\gamma(\delta)-\text{d}\text{i}_{\xi}\gamma(\delta),

(31)

where Cartan’s magic formula is utilized. Substituting this result into Eq. (
17
) makes it clear that it is natural to define the “gauge-independent” boundary term and Noether charge:

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(\delta)=\theta(\delta)+\text{d}\gamma(\delta),\quad\hat{Q}(\xi)=Q(\xi)-Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}).

(32)

With the above given definitions, the first law of black hole thermodynamics takes the convenient form

\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\delta\hat{Q}(\xi)-\text{i}_{\xi}\hat{\theta}(\delta)=0.

(33)

Note that
\hat{Q}(\xi)
resembles the charge obtained in Ref.
[
13
]
, where the construction is carried out in a principal fiber bundle and only afterward projected back to spacetime. The resulting charge coincides with the conventional Noether charge associated with diffeomorphisms, together with an additional contribution that arises during this procedure. In contrast, the approach developed here does not require leaving spacetime for a larger manifold.

Consider now the general transformation given in Eq. (
19
). The associated Noether current is

J(\Lambda,\xi)=\theta(\delta(\Lambda))-\text{i}_{\xi}\mathcal{L}=J(\xi)-\theta(\delta_{\text{GT}}(\Lambda)),

(34)

where
J(\xi)
is the conserved current associated with diffeomorphisms. The corresponding Noether charge then takes the form

Q(\Lambda,\xi)=Q(\xi)-Q_{\text{GT}}(\Lambda),

(35)

where Eq. (
9
) is applied. It follows that

\hat{Q}(\xi)=Q(\Lambda,\xi)-Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}-\Lambda)=Q(\xi)-Q_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}).

(36)

Note that all terms depending on
\Lambda
cancel out. Thus,
\hat{Q}(\xi)
is independent of the transformation. Moreover, Eq. (
33
) implies that
\hat{\theta}(\delta)
is also independent of the transformation.

In addition,
\hat{\theta}(\delta)
allows the construction of a gauge-independent pre-symplectic current by replacing
\theta(\delta)
with
\hat{\theta}(\delta)
in Eq. (
4
); this provides the proper generalization of the pre-symplectic current, which reduces to the one proposed in Ref.
[
9
]
for Einstein–Cartan theory. Accordingly,

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\delta_{1}\hat{\theta}(\delta_{2})-\delta_{2}\hat{\theta}(\delta_{1})-\hat{\theta}([\delta_{1},\delta_{2}])

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})+\int_{\Sigma}\text{d}\Gamma(\delta_{1},\delta_{2}),

where

\Gamma(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=\delta_{1}\gamma(\delta_{2})-\delta_{2}\gamma(\delta_{1})-\gamma([\delta_{1},\delta_{2}]).

(38)

The “gauge-independent pre-symplectic current,”
\hat{\Omega}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
, has two interesting properties. First, it vanishes for a gauge transformation. Namely,

\hat{\Omega}(\delta,\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda))=0.

(39)

The proof follows directly from Eqs. (
16
), (
17
) and (
27
), together with the fact that
\gamma
is a group scalar. The second property is that it is, in fact, gauge independent, which is expressed as

\hat{\Omega}(\delta,\delta(\Lambda))=\hat{\Omega}(\delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}),

(40)

where
\delta(\Lambda)
is given by Eq. (
19
). This property follows directly from the linearity of
\hat{\Omega}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
and Eq. (
39
).

Most importantly, when
\xi
is a Killing vector field, Eqs. (
39
) and (
40
) imply that the first law of black hole thermodynamics follows from

0=\hat{\Omega}(\delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}).

(41)

This is because, on the left-hand side, any gauge transformation can be considered, yet it vanishes; and on the right-hand side, any transformation is allowed, but the result is equivalent. Furthermore, Eq. (
41
) closely parallels the condition used in the metric formalism:
0=\Omega(\delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi})
. The proof that Eq. (
41
) reduces to the first law of black hole thermodynamics relies on the fact that
\delta
and
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
commute, and thus,

\hat{\Omega}(\delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi})=\Omega(\delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi})+\int_{\Sigma}\text{d}\left[\delta\gamma(\mathcal{L}_{\xi})-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\gamma(\delta)\right].

(42)

Then, by using Eqs. (
5
) and (
36
), one can bring Eq. (
42
) to a form that is analogous to Eq. (
33
). In this sense, the definition of
\hat{\Omega}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
, which constitutes the main proposal of this work, yields the correct first law of black hole thermodynamics, starting from an expression analogous to that in the metric formalism, Eq. (
41
), and it does so in a manner that is entirely independent of the chosen transformation. In other words, it provides a systematic and fully gauge-independent derivation of the first law.

Attention is now turned to a transformation that has received significant consideration: the Lorentz–Lie transformation, which is defined by Eq. (
19
) with
\Lambda^{\mu\nu}=\lambda_{\xi}^{\mu\nu}
. This transformation is introduced in Ref.
[
10
]
as a specific prescription to obtain the Noether charge associated with black hole entropy. Notably, as follows directly from Eq. (
13
), this transformation vanishes for a Killing vector field
\xi

[
21
]
. Consequently, to get the first law of black hole thermodynamics one imposes

0=\Omega(\delta,\delta_{\text{LL}}(\xi)),

(43)

which closely resembles the metric formalism. Furthermore, due to the gauge independence proven above, Eq. (
43
) leads to the correct first law of black hole thermodynamics. In addition, it follows that the associated conserved current satisfies

J_{\text{LL}}(\xi)=J(\xi)-\theta(\delta_{\text{GT}}(\lambda_{\xi}))=\text{d}\hat{Q}(\xi),

(44)

which constitutes another noteworthy property of this transformation. Nevertheless, the analysis presented here builds on the observation that the claim in Ref.
[
10
]
that the Lorentz–Lie transformation is
the
transformation required to derive the first law does not hold.

Lastly, the construction of the first law involves several well-known ambiguities
[
15
]
. The first ambiguity arises from the freedom to add an exact form to the Lagrangian,

\mathcal{L}\;\rightarrow\;\mathcal{L}+\text{d}X,

(45)

which leaves the equations of motion unchanged. This transformation modifies the boundary term and, consequently, the Noether charge. However, these changes do not affect the pre-symplectic current or the first law.

A second ambiguity concerns the definition of the boundary term
\theta
. Since
\text{d}^{2}=0
,
\theta
admits the intrinsic freedom

\theta\;\rightarrow\;\theta+\text{d}Z.

(46)

This shifts the pre-symplectic current by a boundary term [as in Eq. (
37
)]. At spatial infinity, the ambiguity is irrelevant under appropriate fall-off conditions
[
15
]
. At the bifurcation surface, on the other hand, the ambiguity does not appear if one works with the gauge-independent pre-symplectic current
\hat{\Omega}
, which ensures an exact cancellation of the ambiguous contributions. This is another notable property of
\hat{\Omega}
.

A further ambiguity comes from shifting the Noether charge by

Q\;\rightarrow\;Q+\text{d}Y.

(47)

Since
\text{d}Y
is exact, its integral vanishes on the boundaries
[
22
]
. Moreover,
Y
can be fixed by using (pseudo)invariance under diffeomorphisms and imposing

\text{i}_{f\xi}\mathcal{L}=f\,\text{i}_{\xi}\mathcal{L},

(48)

for an arbitrary function
f
and vector field
\xi
. Equation (
48
) is precisely the condition required for
\text{i}_{\xi}
to act as a legitimate variation on the Lagrangian, a criterion used elsewhere
[
10
]
. Once the Noether charge is fixed by imposing Eq. (
48
), the first law of black hole thermodynamics becomes free of the ambiguities discussed in Ref.
[
15
]
.

In conclusion, the formulation of the first law of black hole thermodynamics within the first-order formalism has been revisited. A generalization of Wald’s procedure applicable to arbitrary transformations, formulated in terms of the pre-symplectic structure, has been introduced. This generalization provides a systematic framework that is manifestly independent of the specific choice of transformation, without the need to invoke fiber bundles. In this way, the results further support the view that black hole thermodynamics can offer insights into the nature of the microscopic gravitational degrees of freedom.

A key aspect of this analysis is its theory-independent character. Namely, the derivations presented here do not depend on the details of a specific gravitational action, and therefore, apply to any gravitational theory in vacuum, including those with torsion. Given this generality, an important avenue for further research is the inclusion of matter fields, which can modify the Noether charges and affect the derivation of the first law.

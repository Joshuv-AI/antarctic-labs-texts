Exploring the spatiotemporal evolution and spatial correlation network characteristics of cultivated land carbon emissions in China
Authors: Chong Liu, Li Qian, Haiyang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As a predominantly agrarian nation, cutting emissions from agriculture is essential to achieving its “dual carbon” targets. The primary source of agricultural production, cultivated land, has a major impact on the sector’s total carbon emissions. However, the spatial correlation network features have received little attention in previous investigations, which have mostly concentrated on driving variables and emission measurements. Examining the spatial network characteristics of cultivated land carbon emissions (CLCE) provides a basis for promoting coordinated regional emission reduction. This study integrates the carbon emission coefficient method, standard deviational ellipse, ESDA, a modified gravity model, and social network analysis to reveal the spatiotemporal evolution and spatial network structure of CLCE in China from 2000 to 2023. The main findings of this study are as follows: (1) China’s CLCE increased overall, with fluctuations, from 6028.83 × 104 t in 2000 to a peak of 9111.60 × 104 t in 2015, before decreasing to 7555.91 × 104 t in 2023. (2) Spatially, the center of gravity of CLCE has continuously shifted northwestward, exhibiting a significant positive spatial autocorrelation characterized mainly by “high-high” clustering. (3) CLCE demonstrates a clear networked spatial structure, with increasing overall spatial connection strength, good connectivity, and stability, though the overall network compactness remains to be improved. (4) The spatial network of CLCE exhibits a “core–periphery” structure, with Shandong, Jiangsu, Henan, Hebei, Anhui, Hubei, and Sichuan serving as long-term core provinces, while the northwest and northeast regions are mostly located at the periphery. The research offers theoretical support for developing low-carbon agricultural strategies and promoting coordinated regional emission reduction policies in China.

Introduction

Nations all across the world now acknowledge the existence of global warming1–4. Extreme weather occurrences, including floods, hailstorms, and droughts, have been brought on by climate change in recent years, endangering human ecological safety and food security5,6. One of the primary causes of the greenhouse effect is carbon emissions7. Food security is the cornerstone of human life and advancement8, as the adage goes, “Food security is fundamental to people’s livelihoods.” Research shows that the global food system accounts for almost one-third of global releases of greenhouse gases, with agriculture and related land use activities accounting for roughly two-thirds of this proportion9. Therefore, it is crucial to look into CLCE in order to address global warming, guarantee an adequate food supply, and achieve the objectives of sustainable development.

With a large population base, China feeds approximately one-fifth of the global population using a little over 7% of the world’s arable land10. Over a long period, continuous growth in China’s grain production has been achieved through a cultivated land use model characterized by high inputs and high outputs11. However, this model has also brought about numerous problems. For instance, overuse of pesticides and fertilizers pollutes soil and raises greenhouse gas emissions, which present serious problems for the natural environment12. Research indicates that about 17% of China’s greenhouse gas emissions originate from agricultural activities13. China actively participates in efforts to mitigate climate change14. The Chinese government declared in September 2020 that it plans to balance carbon emissions by 2060 and to reach the highest emission level by 203015. Against this backdrop, achieving coordinated development between food security and ecological security has become a major challenge16. Therefore, analyzing the spatiotemporal characteristics and spatial association network features of CLCE from China is crucial for advancing China’s agricultural carbon reduction.

Researchers have done a great deal of work on CLCE, focusing mainly on carbon emission estimation methods17,18, spatiotemporal variation19,20, influencing factors21,22, and decoupling effect studies23. In terms of carbon emission estimation, plot survey methods24, model simulations25, and the carbon emission coefficient method26 are the primary approaches. The first two methods provide relatively accurate estimates but are complex and require extensive data, making them unsuitable for large-scale estimation of CLCE. In contrast, the carbon emission coefficient approach is widely adopted owing to its ease of application. This method mainly includes two calculation forms: one is the direct application of land use carbon emission coefficients27,28; the other involves constructing an accounting inventory that covers various carbon sources, such as agricultural chemicals, energy inputs, tillage, and irrigation, combined with IPCC-recommended coefficients for calculation29–32. Due to its ease of operation, this method is commonly used in practice. Regarding the spatiotemporal differentiation characteristics of CLCE, scholars have conducted studies at different scales, including global33,34, national35, provincial20, and prefectural levels32. The main factors influencing CLCE include agricultural technological progress36, the extent of agricultural mechanization37, funding for agriculture38, and the intensity of environmental policies39. Studies on the decoupling effect often analyze the dynamic relationships between regional CLCE changes and grain production, as well as economic development40,41. Compared with Western countries, China’s agriculture is characterized by high input intensity and small-scale operation, which may lead to higher carbon emission intensity per unit area. In contrast, developed countries rely more on technological efficiency and large-scale mechanized production; although their emission intensity per unit area is lower, total emissions remain substantial due to scale effects42. These differences indicate that studying cultivated land carbon emissions in specific socioeconomic and land-use contexts is of great significance.

Despite the substantial body of research on CLCE, existing studies have largely concentrated on individual provinces or prefecture-level cities, with relatively few offering a systematic analysis of the spatiotemporal evolution of CLCE across China over an extended time series. Such systematic investigations are essential for comprehensively understanding the dynamic changes in CLCE, revealing their phased characteristics and long-term trends through temporal analysis, and providing a scientific foundation for the formulation of long-term and viable emission control strategies. From a nationwide viewpoint, this approach also helps to identify patterns of interregional synergy and differentiation, offering data support for coordinated regional emission reduction and avoiding the fragmentation and inefficiency of locally focused policies. However, research on the spatial correlation network characteristics of CLCE in China remains limited. In recent years, the ongoing implementation of regional coordinated development strategies and the deepening of interregional development mechanisms43 have accelerated the clustering of agricultural industries and the expansion of supply chains. Cross-regional transfers of agricultural products and the diffusion of agricultural technologies have also intensified. As a result, agricultural carbon emissions across regions have become increasingly interconnected. As a result, emission reductions achieved by individual areas are often insufficient, and coordinated regional efforts have become a practical necessity. A thorough understanding of the spatial network structure and characteristics of CLCE in China is essential. A clear delineation of each region’s role within the network can inform emission reduction responsibilities, address interregional policy barriers, and enhance synergistic interactions. Together, these insights promote the more effective achievement of regional emission reduction targets. Thus, there is substantial theoretical and practical relevance in studying the spatiotemporal evolution and the structural characteristics of the spatial network of CLCE in China.

Based on this, the study employs provincial-level panel data in China from 2000 to 2023. It integrates the carbon emission coefficient method, standard deviation ellipse analysis, spatial autocorrelation analysis, an enhanced gravity model, and social network analysis. The study systematically investigates the spatiotemporal evolution and spatial correlation network structure of CLCE in China. The findings aim to provide a theoretical basis for formulating agricultural carbon reduction policies.

The research area of the current study is China (Fig. 1). Because of limited data availability, the analysis excludes several special administrative regions, including Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macao. Data sources

The data employed in this study include indicators like chemical fertilizer, pesticide, agricultural film, agricultural machinery, tillage, irrigation, cultivated land area, the added value of the primary industry, and population. Data sources include the China Rural Statistical Yearbook (2001–2024) and statistical yearbooks from 31 provincial-level administrative units within China. Specifically, chemical fertilizer refers to the effective nutrient content; pesticide and agricultural film data denote the actual annual usage; tillage is represented by the annual sown area of crops; and irrigation is measured by the effectively irrigated area in the respective year.

Methods

Estimation of CLCE

At present, two distinct perspectives exist in the academic community regarding CLCE. From the first standpoint, CLCE encompasses only those greenhouse gas emissions, whether direct or indirect, that are induced by human agricultural production during the cultivation process29. The second viewpoint argues that CLCE should not only include emissions resulting from human activities but also encompass the carbon sequestration effects of the cultivated land resources themselves and their outputs44. However, given the relatively short growth and harvest cycles of crops, along with the rapid consumption or decomposition of biomass following harvest—which results in the release of absorbed carbon dioxide back into the atmosphere within a comparable short timeframe—this study considers the carbon sequestration capacity of cultivated land to be negligible. Consequently, carbon sequestration is not included in the accounting framework; instead, the analysis focuses on the carbon emissions generated from agricultural production inputs. Accordingly, this study focuses solely on direct as well as indirect carbon emissions arising from human production processes involved in the calculation of CLCE, encompassing the following three components: (1) carbon emissions from the use of chemical fertilizers, agricultural plastic films, and pesticides; (2) carbon emissions resulting from the use of energy sources like diesel and electricity in agricultural machinery operation and irrigation; and (3) carbon emissions caused by the disruption of the soil carbon pool through tillage-induced release of soil organic carbon. On this basis, six carbon sources are identified for CLCE estimation: chemical fertilizers, pesticides, agricultural films, diesel fuel used by agricultural machinery, irrigation, and tillage. Exploratory spatial data analysis (ESDA)

ESDA is a method for studying patterns, trends, and associations in geospatial data, aiming to discover and understand the underlying structure and characteristics of spatial data. Spatial autocorrelation analysis is one of the core components of ESDA, primarily used to measure the spatial interdependence and clustering degree of attribute values of geographic entities. The Global Moran’s I and Local Moran’s I indices are the most commonly used statistics for measuring the degree of spatial autocorrelation, revealing the overall and local spatial clustering characteristics of carbon emissions within the study area55. Modified gravity model

To investigate the structure of the spatial association network of CLCE, a spatial association network matrix is required. Based on previous studies and incorporating relevant variables related to CLCE, this study develops a modified gravity model to quantify the spatial association intensity of CLCE and accordingly constructs the spatial association matrix.

The standard gravity model is modified in four key aspects: (a) incorporating agricultural-specific variables, including agricultural labor force (P), per capita agricultural added value (G), total carbon emissions (T), and cultivated land area (A); (b) introducing an empirical coefficient kij to reflect the directional dependence of carbon emissions between two regions; (c) using the square of the spherical distance to more accurately measure geographical distance; and (d) applying a row-average threshold for binarization to construct the network.

Results

Spatiotemporal evolution of CLCE in China

Temporal evolution of CLCE

The time trend of CLCE in China over the period 2000–2023 was analyzed and displayed in Fig. 2. CLCE changes exhibit a two-phase evolutionary pattern. During the first phase (2000–2015), carbon emissions increased continuously. From 6028.83 × 104 t in 2000 to 9111.60 × 104 t in 2015, representing an increase of approximately 1.51 times. This increase is likely attributable to the intensification of chemical and mechanized agricultural inputs—like the rising use of fertilizers, pesticides, plastic films, and agricultural machinery—along with accelerated soil degradation, all of which have collectively contributed to the rise in CLCE29. The second phase, from 2015 to 2023, shows an overall decline in carbon emissions, with total emissions decreasing from the peak of 9111.60 × 104 t in 2015 to 7555.91 × 104 t in 2023. This decrease is attributed to the adoption of green and low-carbon agricultural practices following the 18th National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party. These practices have promoted a shift in farmland use toward lower carbon intensity. Simultaneously, as a consequence of increased urbanization, agriculture has been reduced in area, which also helps to lower carbon emissions. This is because urban building land has expanded at the expense of cropland. Overall, during the study period, China’s CLCE exhibited a fluctuating upward trend, increasing from 6028.83 × 104 t in 2000 to 7555.91 × 104 t in 2023 (Fig. 2a).Fig. 2Temporal dynamics of CLCE in China (2000–2023).

Temporal dynamics of CLCE in China (2000–2023).

At the regional level, the CLCE in Northeast, Central, and Western China generally followed the national trend. In the Eastern region, carbon emissions increased steadily from 2000 to 2007. A sharp decline occurred in 2008, followed by a gradual increase from 2008 to 2015. After 2015, emissions aligned with the national trend. In terms of the proportion of total national emissions (Fig. 2f), both the Eastern and Central regions showed a declining trend, while the Western region exhibited a steady increase. Notably, prior to 2018, the Eastern region consistently ranked first in total CLCE. However, the Western region surpassed the Central region in 2013 to become the second-highest emitter, and further overtook the Eastern region in 2018, becoming the top emitter. The Northeast region maintained the lowest emission levels throughout the study period.

Spatial evolution characteristics

To illustrate the spatial heterogeneity of CLCE at the provincial level in China, the natural breaks classification method was applied. Provinces were divided into five ranks, with Rank 1 representing the lowest emission level and Rank 5 the highest. From the perspective of degree centrality (Fig. 9a), analyzing the overall situation from 2000 to 2023, provinces in central and eastern China exhibit higher degree centrality within the spatial association network of CLCE, consistently occupying central positions in the network, such as Shandong and Henan provinces.

Discussion

Spatiotemporal evolution of CLCE in China

The results indicate that CLCE in China continuously increased from 2000 to 2015, followed by a general declining trend from 2015 to 2023. This pattern is largely consistent with previous studies on agricultural carbon emissions in China67,69, supporting the conclusion that cropland use represents the main source of agricultural carbon emissions. Spatially, the gravity center of CLCE has steadily shifted northwest, closely following the trajectory of China’s cropland center of gravity70–72.Simultaneously, the share of CLCE in western China has continued to increase (Fig. 2f), while the proportions in the eastern and central regions have declined, further supporting the northwestward shift of the emissions center. Two main factors explain this spatial shift. First, rapid urbanization and industrialization in the eastern and central regions have led to a considerable reduction in farmland, accompanied by extensive expansion of urban land73,74. In contrast, the western region, characterized by relatively slower economic development75 and abundant cropland reserves, has experienced an expansion of cropland under national initiatives such as the “Western Development” and “Grain Security” strategies, resulting in increased regional carbon emissions. Second, the vast territory and low population density of northwest China have facilitated the adoption of large-scale, mechanized agriculture. Although mechanization enhances production efficiency, the extensive use of large agricultural machinery has also increased diesel consumption, substantially contributing to higher carbon emissions in the region.Furthermore, the northwestward shift of the CLCE gravity center is associated with the evolution of the spatial network structure to a certain extent. Although the gravity center of carbon emissions has gradually moved toward western China, the core positions within the spatial association network remain predominantly concentrated in central and eastern provinces such as Shandong, Henan, and Anhui. This indicates that the growth of carbon emissions in western regions has not yet been translated into a corresponding dominance within the network. However, from the perspective of individual network characteristics, some western provinces (e.g., Sichuan and Shaanxi) exhibit a gradual increase in degree centrality and betweenness centrality, suggesting that their roles within the network are strengthening. This phenomenon implies that there exists a lagged coupling relationship between the spatial shift of carbon emission gravity and the restructuring of the network.

From a spatial autocorrelation standpoint, CLCE in China exhibit a significant positive spatial autocorrelation, consistent with previous findings on the Yellow River Basin32. During the study period, the global Moran’s I index showed a declining trend overall (Fig. 5). Local spatial autocorrelation analysis indicated that High–High was the dominant clustering type. The numbers of both High–High and Low–Low clusters initially decreased and then stabilized over time (Fig. 6). This pattern suggests a weakening of spatial clustering of CLCE at the provincial level, indicating a gradual attenuation of homogeneity among high-emission regions and an increasing prominence of regional heterogeneity. This trend can be ascribed to the growing diversification in agricultural development paths, land-use strategies, and carbon mitigation policies across provinces. The continuous advancement of green agriculture and emission reduction measures has, to some extent, reduced cropland carbon emission intensity, reflecting the initial success of China’s agricultural carbon mitigation efforts. In terms of local spatial clustering, Shandong, Henan, and Anhui provinces consistently exhibited High-High clusters throughout the research period. This pattern is primarily attributed to these provinces being major grain-producing regions in China, possessing extensive farmland and high agricultural production intensity.Moreover, their location on the North China Plain—characterized by flat terrain—facilitates mechanized and large-scale agricultural operations, thereby contributing to relatively high levels of carbon emissions. Due to their geographical proximity, the carbon emissions in these provinces demonstrate significant spatial clustering, forming a regional core of high emissions.These “High–High” clustered provinces also correspond to key nodes within the spatial correlation network. Results from social network analysis indicate that Shandong, Henan, and Anhui have consistently maintained high levels of degree centrality, closeness centrality, and betweenness centrality, reflecting strong connectivity, accessibility, and intermediary roles. Therefore, these provinces are not only regions of high carbon emission clustering but also core hubs that promote interprovincial connections and strengthen spatial spillover effects. This “dual attribute” further reveals the intrinsic link between local spatial clustering patterns and the overall network structure.

Spatial network characteristics of CLCE in China

The spatial network of inter-provincial CLCE in China exhibits a”core–periphery”structural characteristic, with the central and eastern regions forming the network core, while the northwestern and northeastern regions occupy relatively peripheral positions. During the period 2000–2023, provinces with relatively close spatial linkages are mainly distributed in the central and eastern regions, with scattered occurrences in the southwestern region. Nodes represented by Shandong, Jiangsu, Henan, Hebei, Anhui, Hubei, Sichuan, and other provinces play a dominant role in the network. These areas are not only major grain-producing regions in China but also benefit from advantageous geographic locations and well-developed transportation systems, which facilitate stronger spatial connections with surrounding areas. In contrast, although the northwestern and northeastern regions are rich in cultivated land resources, their inter-provincial linkages remain relatively weak due to constraints related to economic development levels and transportation infrastructure.

During the study period, the spatial network of China’s CLCE exhibits distinct stage-based evolutionary characteristics. From 2000 to 2015, a phase of increasing carbon emissions, interregional production linkages gradually strengthened against the background of rising agricultural inputs, leading to an increase in the number of spatial correlations. However, the overall network structure remained dominated by the core regions. After 2015, with the advancement of green agricultural policies and the implementation of agricultural structural adjustments, carbon emissions gradually declined, network hierarchy weakened, the participation of peripheral regions increased, and the network structure became flatter. This change indicates that the scale of carbon emissions not only affects regional emission levels but also drives the evolution of the spatial network configuration by altering the intensity and structure of interregional linkages. From the perspective of the mechanisms underlying network structural evolution, the decline in network hierarchy mainly stems from the increased participation of peripheral regions and the strengthening of interregional connections, thereby reducing dependence on core regions and promoting a shift from a relatively centralized structure toward a more balanced one. Meanwhile, structural changes in individual nodes also carry important implications. For example, the betweenness centrality of Inner Mongolia increased significantly after 2015, which may be closely related to factors such as the development of national grain production functional zones, improvements in agricultural infrastructure, and strengthened interregional grain circulation. These changes have enabled Inner Mongolia to play a more important intermediary role in connecting carbon emissions linkages between the eastern, central, and western regions. This suggests that policy guidance and factor mobility are important driving forces influencing the evolution of spatial network structure. Overall, the actual number of spatial linkages in China’s CLCE network remains significantly lower than the theoretical maximum, and the overall network density is relatively low, indicating that the degree of interregional coordination still needs improvement and the spatial correlation network has not yet reached an optimal state.

Further integrating the results of spatiotemporal evolution and spatial network analysis reveals a significant coupling relationship between the evolution of the spatial pattern and the changes in the network structure of China’s CLCE. On the one hand, the long-term stable “high–high” clustering in the central and eastern regions provides a structural foundation for the formation of the network core. On the other hand, the northwestward shift of the carbon emission gravity center facilitates the gradual integration of western regions into the network and enhances their structural status. At the same time, the continuous strengthening of interregional spatial correlations and spillover effects promotes the transformation of CLCE from local clustering to an integrated networked structure. Therefore, the spatiotemporal evolution and spatial network structure of China’s CLCE are not independent processes; rather, they form a coupled evolutionary process shaped by multiple factors, including the agricultural production pattern, regional development disparities, and policy regulation.

Policy recommendations

The findings of the research offer helpful suggestions and aid in the implementation of carbon reduction measures in China’s agriculture industry. First, in response to the ongoing northwestward shift of the CLCE center, it is crucial to strengthen carbon emission management in the western regions by implementing region-specific green agricultural machinery substitution programs to reduce high-carbon energy consumption. Additionally, guiding the development direction of cultivated land is necessary to avoid large-scale reclamation of inefficient and high-emission farmland. Second, for the long-term high carbon emission clusters in central and eastern provinces such as Shandong, Henan, and Anhui, differentiated green agricultural development strategies should be adopted. These include promoting low-carbon technologies such as organic fertilizer substitution, straw returning, and precision fertilization, alongside establishing pilot zones for intensive carbon emission control in cultivated land to enhance agricultural emission reduction efficiency. Finally, given the relatively loose inter-provincial carbon emission network in China, it is recommended to develop cross-regional collaborative governance mechanisms by establishing a “Regional Collaboration Platform for Cultivated Land Carbon Emissions.” This platform would facilitate coordinated emission reductions between core node provinces and surrounding areas. Furthermore, exploring regional agricultural carbon quota trading mechanisms could improve resource allocation efficiency and overall emission reduction effectiveness. It should be noted that a higher network density is not unconditionally desirable for carbon emission reduction, as a highly connected network could potentially accelerate the spread of high-carbon agricultural practices. Therefore, policy efforts should not aim to indiscriminately strengthen all spatial associations. Instead, priority should be given to fostering pro‑environmental linkages, such as cross-regional diffusion of low-carbon technologies, joint establishment of green agricultural standards, and carbon emission quota trading mechanisms. Meanwhile, it is equally important to mitigate the spatial spillover of carbon-intensive production models. By selectively enhancing beneficial linkages, the spatial network of cultivated land carbon emissions can evolve toward a more coordinated but not necessarily denser structure that effectively supports China’s dual-carbon goals. Furthermore, the block model results suggest differentiated responsibilities: net spillover blocks should prioritize emission reduction, net beneficiary blocks should establish compensation mechanisms, and broker blocks should facilitate technology diffusion.

Research limitations and prospects for future study

This paper systematically analyzes the spatiotemporal evolution and spatial network structure of CLCE in China and obtains relatively comprehensive results; however, several limitations remain. First, the carbon emission accounting only considers direct and indirect emissions generated by human activities, without incorporating the carbon sequestration capacity of crops, which may underestimate the regulatory role of cultivated land systems in the carbon cycle. Second, the analysis is conducted at the provincial scale, which makes it difficult to capture intra-regional spatial heterogeneity and constrains the understanding of fine-scale characteristics of carbon emissions. Third, although the use of unified carbon emission coefficients ensures methodological consistency, it fails to reflect differences in agricultural production practices across regions, which may affect the estimation of absolute carbon emission values.

Future research can be further extended in the following aspects. First, crop carbon sequestration accounting methods can be introduced to construct a more comprehensive carbon budget framework for cultivated land, thereby enabling an integrated evaluation of both carbon emission and sequestration functions of cultivated land systems. Second, the study scale can be refined to the municipal and county levels to more precisely identify intra-regional differences and enhance the scientific rigor of spatial differentiation and effect analysis of carbon emissions. Third, where data availability permits, future studies can explore localized and dynamic carbon emission coefficients to better reflect regional differences in agricultural practices and continuously improve the accuracy of carbon emission accounting, thereby providing stronger support for differentiated policy-making. Fourth, Future research could employ the Quadratic Assignment Procedure (QAP) regression to quantitatively identify the drivers of the spatial network structure, such as economic scale, geographical proximity, transportation infrastructure, and policy coordination. Additionally, a simulation based on block model results could be conducted to optimize network connectivity and enhance synergistic emission reduction.

Conclusions

Against the backdrop of the coordinated advancement of food security, ecological civilization construction, and China’s “dual carbon” goals, controlling agricultural carbon emissions has become a crucial component in establishing an environmentally friendly and carbon-efficient development approach. As the core foundational resource for food production, CLCE not only influence the evolution of regional agricultural carbon intensity but also significantly shape the overall pattern of national agricultural carbon reduction. With the continuous progress of agricultural modernization, substantial changes have occurred in cultivated land utilization patterns, intensifying the linkage between interregional resource flows and agricultural carbon emissions. However, existing studies predominantly focus on the accounting of total carbon emissions and the analysis of their driving mechanisms, with limited systematic exploration of the spatiotemporal interactions of CLCE from the perspective of spatial association structures. This gap restricts effective support for cross-regional collaborative governance policies. To address this, the study uses provincial panel data from 2000 to 2023 and applies carbon emission coefficient methods, standard ellipse analysis, spatial autocorrelation analysis, a modified gravity model, and social network analysis. This approach systematically examines the spatiotemporal evolution and spatial association network structure of CLCE in China.The objective is to provide theoretical support and empirical evidence for agricultural carbon reduction policy formulation and regional coordinated governance.

The study found that between 2000 and 2023, CLCE in China exhibited an overall fluctuating upward trend, with the evolutionary process roughly divided into two phases: the first phase (2000–2015) was characterized by sustained and substantial growth in emissions; the second phase (post-2015) showed a gradual decline in total emissions, preliminarily reflecting the effectiveness of agricultural green transformation policies. In terms of spatial patterns, the centroid of CLCE continuously shifted northwestward, indicating a rising prominence of central and western regions in the national carbon emission landscape. CLCE exhibited a significant positive spatial correlation, as revealed by spatial autocorrelation analysis, with high–high clusters being the most prominent. These clusters were concentrated in traditional agricultural powerhouses in the central and eastern regions, such as Henan, Shandong, and Anhui provinces, which consistently exhibited high emission levels, forming a core high-value emission zone. Further insights from social network analysis demonstrated a clear networked spatial structure of cultivated land carbon emissions. Overall, the intensity of spatial linkages in CLCE strengthened steadily during the study period, exhibiting good connectivity and stability; however, the network density requires further enhancement. The national spatial network of CLCE displayed a typical “core-periphery” pattern, with provinces like Shandong, Jiangsu, Henan, Hebei, Anhui, Hubei, and Sichuan occupying core positions characterized by high degree and betweenness centrality, serving as key nodes for the flow and influence of carbon emissions. In contrast, provinces in the northwestern and northeastern peripheral regions held relatively weaker positions within the network.

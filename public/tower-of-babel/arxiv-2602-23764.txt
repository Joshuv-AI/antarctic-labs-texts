A new class of coherent states involving Fox-Wright functions and their generalization in the bicomplex framework Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Snehasis Bera, Sourav Das, Abhijit Banerjee ∗

Abstract
. In this work, an extensive class of coherent states is introduced by taking the Fox–Wright function as the normalization function. It is demonstrated that these states satisfy the key requirements of continuity, normalizability and resolution of unity. Furthermore, coherent states associated with the continuous spectrum are obtained through a discrete-to-continuous limiting procedure. Moreover, FW-generalized multi-parameter ν \nu -function is introduced and shown to act as the normalization function for the Fox–Wright coherent states in the continuous spectrum. Later the Fox–Wright function with bicomplex arguments has been introduced and its existence has been investigated. Bicomplex Fox–Wright coherent states are also developed for the discrete spectrum based on this new function and their properties are analyzed. Subsequently, the results regarding Fox-Wright coherent states are generalized to the bicomplex setting. In addition, a bicomplex FW-generalized multi-parameter ν \nu -function is defined to demonstrate that it provides the normalization for these states in the continuous spectrum.

1.
Introduction and motivation

Exactly a century ago, E. Schrödinger
[
28
]
introduced the problem of identifying quantum states that replicate the behavior of corresponding classical states. Later in 1963, R. J. Glauber
[
11
]
extended Schrödinger’s idea to quantum electrodynamics and formally introduced the term ‘coherent states’. Over the following decades, attention was devoted to the investigation of the properties and applications of coherent states for the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator. As these coherent states are defined in terms of the bosonic annihilation and creation operators
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
, which satisfy the canonical commutation relation

[\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}]=1
, they are known as canonical coherent states. Simultaneously, it was recognized that coherent states are not confined to the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator, whose energy spectrum varies linearly with the principal quantum number
n
rather, this formalism can be generalized to systems with nonlinear energy spectrum. For nonlinear deformed oscillators
[
7
]
, one can construct coherent states through the annihilation and creation operators

A=Bf(N),A^{\dagger}=f(N)B^{\dagger}

which satisfy the deformed commutation relations

[N,A]=-A,[N,A^{\dagger}]=A^{\dagger}\mbox{ and }[A,A^{\dagger}]=(N+1)f^{2}(N+1)-Nf^{2}(N)

where
f
denotes a hermitian operator-valued function of number operator and coherent states can be defined as the eigenstates of
A
. Today, coherent states are widely used in many areas of physics, such as signal and quantum information processing, quantum optics and beyond. Various extension of coherent states have been developed, including generalized hypergeometric coherent states
[
1
]
, Mittag Lafler coherent states
[
31
]
, coherent states for generalized Laguerre functions
[
13
]
, generalized coherent states associated with
C_{\lambda}
-extended oscillator
[
25
]
etc. Numerous important books and research articles have been written on coherent states and their various applications see
[
6
,
7
,
19
]
. More interestingly, in recent time, bicomplex quantum mechanics has emerged as a viable extension of standard quantum theory (see
[
2
,
3
]
and references therein), and a few works extending the notion of coherent states into the bicomplex domain
[
4
,
5
]
has appeared in the literature.

Recently Popov
[
21
]
, constructed and studied coherent states associated with the multi-index Mittag–Leffler function. Subsequently, by applying the discrete-to-continuous limit, coherent states for a continuous spectrum were derived and their properties were analyzed. Next, Popov
[
20
]
, developed coherent states employing the Fox–Wright type function and examined their associated properties. Motivated by this, we construct Fox–Wright coherent states in which the Fox–Wright function acts as the normalization function and investigate their properties. These states are distinct from the generalized coherent states reported in
[
20
]
. Furthermore, we construct coherent states corresponding to the continuous spectrum and show that they fulfill the requirements of continuity, normalizability, and resolution of unity. Moreover, the Fox–Wright function with bicomplex arguments is introduced and its existence is investigated. Subsequently, all results concerning Fox–Wright coherent states are extended to the bicomplex setting.

The Fox–Wright function is an important special function that plays a significant role in fractional calculus, approximation theory, mathematical physics and related areas of science and engineering. Many special functions, including Wright function
[
33
]
, generalized hypergeometric function
[
26
]
, Mittag–Leffler function
[
18
]
and Bessel function
[
32
]
can be represented as particular cases of the Fox–Wright function.
The Fox-Wright function
{}_{p}\psi_{q}[\cdot]
generalization of the hypergeometric function is defined as follows
[
9
,
34
]
:

\displaystyle{}_{p}\psi_{q}\left[\begin{matrix}&(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots&(a_{p},A_{p});\\
&(b_{1},B_{1}),\ldots&(b_{q},B_{q});&\end{matrix}z\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l}+nA_{l})}{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r}+nB_{r})}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

(1.1)

where
a_{l},b_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
and
A_{l},B_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
for
l=1,2,\ldots p,r=1,2,\ldots q
. The series (
1.1
), converges absolutely and uniformly in the whole complex space if

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{r=1}^{q}B_{r}-\sum_{l=1}^{p}A_{l}>0.

(1.2)

The structure of the paper is as follows: in section
2
, Fox–Wright coherent states are constructed for both discrete and continuous spectrum and their properties are examined. In section
3
, the Fox–Wright function in the bicomplex setting is defined and its existence is investigated. In section
4
, all results related to Fox–Wright coherent states are extended to the bicomplex setting.

2.
Construction of Fox-Wright coherent states

In this section, we define Fox-Wright coherent states, categorize them according to well-defined convergence criteria and identifies the parameter constraints governing these states. Moreover, we have derived Fox-Wright
coherent states corresponding to the continuous spectrum by applying the discrete-continuous limit. We define Fox-Wright states in the infinite dimensional Hilbert space of the Fock vectors
\mid k>,k=1,2,\ldots
as follows

\displaystyle\mid z>=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mid z\mid^{2})}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\sqrt{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}}\mid k>,

(2.3)

where the normalization functions are expressed through Fox–Wright functions as given below

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r})}{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l})}{}_{p}\psi_{q}\left[\begin{matrix}&(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots&(a_{p},A_{p});\\
&(b_{1},B_{1}),\ldots&(b_{q},B_{q});&\end{matrix}\zeta\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r})}{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l})}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l}+kA_{l})}{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r}+kB_{r})}\frac{\zeta^{k}}{k!}

(2.4)

for
\zeta=\mid z\mid^{2}
and the parameter function
\rho^{(p,q)}(k)
in (
2.3
) is defined by

\displaystyle\rho^{(p,q)}(k)=\Gamma(k+1)\frac{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l})}{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r})}\frac{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r}+kB_{r})}{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l}+kA_{l})}.

(2.5)

Let us consider complex valued function

f^{(p,q)}(s)=\sqrt{\frac{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l}+sA_{l})\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r}+(s+1)B_{r})}{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l}+(s+1)A_{l})\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r}+sB_{r})}(s+1)},

such that

\prod_{s=0}^{k-1}f^{(p,q)}(s)=\sqrt{\frac{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l})\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r}+kB_{r})}{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l}+kA_{l})\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r})}\Gamma(k+1)}=\sqrt{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}.

(2.6)

From (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we obtain the following recurrence relations

\rho^{(p,q)}(0)=1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\rho^{(p,q)}(k+1)=\rho^{(p,q)}(k)\left(f^{(p,q)}(k)\right)^{2}.

(2.7)

From the above construction, it follows that
\rho^{(m,n)}(k)
must be a strictly positive real number. Consequently, this leads to restrictions on the parameters

a_{l}>0,b_{r}>0\;\quad\mbox{for}\;l=1,2,...,p;r=1,2,...,q.

Now using the normalization functions, we can compute the scalar product between two Fox–Wright states as follows:

<z\mid z^{\prime}>=\frac{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(z*z^{\prime})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mid z\mid^{2})}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mid z^{\prime}\mid)}}.

(2.8)

Form expression (
2.8
), it follows that the Fox–Wright states are normalized but not orthogonal and this expression is well defined provided that the Fox–Wright functions appearing in the scalar product are convergent. The convergence condition for the Fox–Wright function is given in (
1.2
) namely

\sum_{r=1}^{q}B_{r}-\sum_{l=1}^{p}A_{l}>-1.

Now, we introduce annihilation and creation operators as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f^{(p,q)}(k)\mid k><k+1\mid

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f^{(p,q)}(k)\mid k+1><k\mid,

where
\mathcal{A}_{+}=\mathcal{A}_{-}^{\dagger}
that is
\mathcal{A}_{+}
represents the adjoint operator corresponding to
\mathcal{A}_{-}
and satisfies the following relations:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{-}\mid k>=f^{(p,q)}(k-1)\mid k-1>,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{+}\mid k>=f^{(p,q)}(k)\mid k+1>,

(2.9)

\displaystyle<k\mid\mathcal{A}_{-}\mathcal{A}_{+}\mid k>=\left[f^{(m,n)}(k)\right]^{2},

and

<k\mid\mathcal{A}_{+}\mathcal{A}_{-}\mid k>=\left[f^{(m,n)}(k-1)\right]^{2}.

(2.10)

The operators
\mathcal{A}_{-}
and
\mathcal{A}_{+}
do not commute and their noncommutative commutation relation is given by:

\left[\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{+}\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\left[f^{(m,n)}(k)\right]^{2}-\left[f^{(m,n)}(k-1)\right]^{2}\right)\mid k><k\mid.

Using the recurrence relation (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{-}\mid z>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f^{(p,q)}(k)\mid k><k+1\mid\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mid z\mid^{2})}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\sqrt{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}}\mid k>\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(p,q)}(k)}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mid z\mid^{2})}}\frac{z^{k+1}}{\sqrt{\rho^{(p,q)}(k+1)}}\mid k>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\mid z>.

Thus, the Fox–Wright states
\mid z>
defined in (
2.3
) are eigenstates of the annihilation operator
\mathcal{A}_{-}
corresponding eigenvalue
z
confirming that they are coherent states. Next, we verify that Fox-Wright coherent states satisfy the following fundamental properties:

(a)

Continuity:
|z^{\prime}-z|\rightarrow 0
implies that
||\mid z^{\prime}>-\mid z>||\rightarrow 0
.
The continuity property is satisfied since the series appearing in the definition of the coherent states is a continuous function of the complex variable
z
over the entire complex plane.

(b)

Normalization:
<z\mid z>=1
. This property is a direct consequence of the previously derived expression (
2.8
) for the overlap of two coherent states.

(c)

Resolution of unity: A positive weight function
W(|z|^{2})
, is chosen of the integration measure

d\mu(z)=\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}d(|z|^{2})W(|z|^{2}),\;\;0<\theta<2\pi

such that the integral
\int d\mu(z)\mid z><z\mid=1
holds.

Let us take
\mid 0>
denote the ground state satisfying
\mathcal{A}_{-}\mid 0>=0\mid 0>
. Now using (
2

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}_{+})^{k}\mid 0>=\prod_{s=0}^{k-1}f(s)\mid k>=\sqrt{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}\mid k>.

Since,
\mathcal{A}_{+}
represents the adjoint operator corresponding to
\mathcal{A}_{-}
, the relations below hold

\displaystyle\mid k>=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}}(\mathcal{A}_{+})^{k}\mid 0>\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;<k\mid=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}}<0\mid(\mathcal{A}_{-})^{k}.

(2.11)

Using (
2.11
) and (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mid z>=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mid z\mid^{2})}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z\mathcal{A}_{+})^{k}}{{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}}\mid 0>\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;<z\mid=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mid z\mid^{2})}}<0\mid\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z^{\ast}\mathcal{A}_{-})^{k}}{{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}}.

(2.12)

Using the DOOT method
[
23
]
and the completeness of Fock states, we derive the projector

\mid 0><0\mid
associated with the ground state
\mid 0>
as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mid k><k\mid=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}\#(\mathcal{A}_{+})^{k}\mid 0><0\mid(\mathcal{A}_{-})^{k}\#=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mid 0><0\mid\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho^{(p,q)}(k)}\#(\mathcal{A}_{+}\mathcal{A}_{-})^{k}\#=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mid 0><0\mid\#\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mathcal{A}_{+}\mathcal{A}_{-})\#=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mid 0><0\mid=\frac{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l})}{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r})\#{}_{p}\psi_{q}\left[\begin{matrix}&(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots&(a_{p},A_{p});\\
&(b_{1},B_{1}),\ldots&(b_{q},B_{q});&\end{matrix}\mathcal{A}_{+}\mathcal{A}_{-}\right]\#}.

(2.13)

Next, we determine the weight function
W(|z|^{2})
ensuring that the Fox-Wright coherent states satisfy the resolution of unity

\displaystyle\int d\mu(z)\mid z><z\mid=1.

(2.14)

Using (
2.12
),(
2

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}d(|z|^{2})W(|z|^{2})\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mid z\mid^{2})}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\#(z\mathcal{A}_{+})^{k}(z^{\ast}\mathcal{A}_{-})^{k}\#}{{[\rho^{(p,q)}(k)]^{2}}}\mid 0><0\mid=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\right]\;\int_{0}^{\infty}d(|z|^{2})\frac{W(|z|^{2})}{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}\left(\mid z\mid^{2}\right)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(|z|^{2})^{k}}{{[\rho^{(p,q)}(k)]^{2}}}\#(\mathcal{A}_{+}\mathcal{A}_{-})^{n}\#=\#\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}(\mathcal{A}_{+}\mathcal{A}_{-})\#

(2.15)

Setting the value of the normalization function in (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d(|z|^{2})\frac{W(|z|^{2})}{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}\left(\mid z\mid^{2}\right)}(|z|^{2})^{k}=\rho^{(p,q)}(k)=\Gamma(k+1)\frac{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l})}{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r})}\frac{\prod\limits_{r=1}^{q}\Gamma(b_{r}+kB_{r})}{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{l}+kA_{l})}.

(2.16)

Substituting

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}H_{m,n}^{l_{1},l_{2}}\left[x\;\middle|\;\begin{matrix}(a_{1},\alpha_{1}),\ldots,(a_{m},\alpha_{m})\\

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{W(|z|^{2})}{\mathcal{N}^{(p,q)}\left(\mid z\mid^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle W(|z|^{2})

\displaystyle={}_{p}\psi_{q}\left[\begin{matrix}&(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots&(a_{p},A_{p});\\

Bicomplex Fox-Wright function

3.1.
Preliminaries on Bicomplex numbers

The bicomplex number system is an extension of the complex number system, formed by introducing additional imaginary units together with complex coefficients. C. Segre
[
29
]
introduced the concept of bicomplex numbers in 1892. The theory of bicomplex numbers has attracted growing attention in recent years. The set of bicomplex numbers emerge as a commutative alternative to the noncommutative quaternion skew field, which is another four-dimensional real space. Unlike quaternions, bicomplex numbers have commutative multiplication and form a ring with
zero divisors. Extensive studies have explored the properties of bicomplex numbers, covering their algebraic and geometric aspects along with several applications see
[
16
,
24
,
27
]
.
The set
\mathbb{BC}
of bicomplex numbers is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{BC}

\displaystyle=\{Z=a+jb=x_{1}+ix_{2}+jx_{3}+ijx_{4}:x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathbb{R}\},

where
i
and
j
satisfies the conditions
i^{2}=j^{2}=-1,ij=ji
with
a=x_{1}+ix_{2},b=x_{3}+ix_{4}
.
Every bicomplex number
Z=a+jb\in\mathbb{BC}
admits a unique representation of the following form

Z=(a-ib)e_{1}+(a+ib)e_{2}=z_{1}e_{1}+z_{2}e_{2},

where
e_{1}=\frac{1+ij}{2}
and
e_{2}=\frac{1-ij}{2}
, which satisfies the identities
e_{1}+e_{2}=1,e_{1}-e_{2}=ij,e_{1}^{2}=e_{1}
and
e_{2}^{2}=e_{2}
. This form is known as the idempotent representation of the bicomplex number
Z\in\mathbb{BC}
.
The space
\mathbb{BC}
forms a commutative ring with unity that contains zero divisors. The collection of all zero divisors is given by
[
17
]
:

\mathbb{O}_{2}=\{Z=a+jb:a^{2}+b^{2}=0\},

and its elements are known as singular elements.

The hyperbolic number system is defined as

\mathbb{D}=\{Z=x_{1}+ijx_{4}:x_{1},x_{4}\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Inside
\mathbb{D}
the subsets of non-negative and non-positive hyperbolic numbers can be introduced as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{+}=\{Z=x_{1}+ijx_{4}:x_{1}^{2}-x_{4}^{2}\geq 0,x_{1}\geq 0\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{D}^{-}=\{Z=x_{1}+ijx_{4}:x_{1}^{2}-x_{4}^{2}\geq 0,x_{1}\leq 0\},

respectively. Furthermore, a partial order relation
<_{h}
is defined on the set
\mathbb{D}
and if
Z,W\in\mathbb{D}
and
Z<_{h}W
, it implies that
W-Z\in\mathbb{D}^{+}
and
Z-W\in\mathbb{D}^{-}
.
The hyperbolic norm
|\cdot|_{h}
for any bicomplex number
Z=z_{1}e_{1}+z_{2}e_{2}
, is given by
[
17
]
:

|Z|_{h}=|z_{1}|e_{1}+|z_{2}|e_{2}.

Using the hyperbolic norm, the hyperbolic ball with center
p
and radius
R
is defined as follows
B_{h}(p,R)=\{Z:|Z-p|<_{h}R\}
.
The following expression represents the Euler product form of the bicomplex gamma function

\displaystyle\Gamma_{b}(W)=\frac{1}{We^{\gamma W}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{n}{n+W}\right)\exp\left(\frac{W}{n}\right)\right),

where

,
with

where

4.
Bicomplex Fox-Wright coherent states

This section develops coherent states related to the bicomplex Fox–Wright function and, via a discrete-to-continuous limit, obtains those corresponding to the continuous spectrum. A bicomplex FW-generalized multi-parameter
\nu
-function is also introduced and show that it acts as the normalization function for these bicomplex Fox–Wright coherent states in the continuous spectrum. In
[
4
]
, we analyzed coherent states related to the bicomplex generalized hypergeometric function. Here, we turn our attention to bicomplex Fox–Wright coherent states. In an infinite-dimensional bicomplex Hilbert space the Fock states are defined by
[
4
]
:

\mid k>=\sum_{p=1}^{2}\mid k_{p}>e_{p},\quad k_{p}=0,1,2,...

and these states satisfy the corresponding orthogonality and completeness relations, which are given by:

<k\mid l>=\sum_{p=1}^{2}\delta_{k_{p}l_{p}}e_{p}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mid k><k\mid=1.

Following the same approach as in the complex case, we introduce the bicomplex Fox–Wright states corresponding to the discrete spectrum, expressed in terms of the Fock states
\mid k>
as follows

\displaystyle\mid m;n;Z>=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}^{(m,n)}(\mid Z\mid_{h}^{2})}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{Z^{k}}{\sqrt{\rho^{(m,n)}(k)}}\mid k>,

(4.35)

in which the normalization functions are represented by the bicomplex Fox–Wright functions in the following form

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{b}^{(m,n)}(W)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{b}(\nu_{j})}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma_{b}(\mu_{i})}{}_{m}\Psi_{n}\left[\begin{matrix}&(\mu_{1},M_{1}),\ldots&(\mu_{m},M_{m});\\
&(\nu_{1},N_{1}),\ldots&(\nu_{n},N_{n});&\end{matrix}W\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{b}(\nu_{j})}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma_{b}(\mu_{i})}\sum_{p=1}^{2}\left[\sum_{k_{p}=0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma(\mu_{pi}+k_{p}M_{pi})}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\nu_{pj}+k_{p}N_{pj})}\frac{w_{p}^{k_{p}}}{k_{p}!}\right]e_{p}

(4.36)

for
W=w_{1}e_{1}+w_{2}e_{2}=\mid Z\mid_{h}^{2}
.
The parameter function
\rho_{b}^{(m,n)}(k)
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{b}^{(m,n)}(k)=\sum_{p=1}^{2}\Gamma(k_{p}+1)\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma(\mu_{pi})}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\nu_{pj})}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\nu_{pj}+k_{p}N_{pj})}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma(\mu_{pi}+k_{p}M_{pi})}e_{p}.

Let us consider

and similarly define a bicomplex-valued function

then parameter function

\rho_{b}^{(m,n)}(0)=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{2}e_{s}=1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\rho_{b}^{(m,n)}(k+1)=\rho_{b}^{(m,n)}(k)\left(f_{b}^{(m,n)}(k)\right)^{2}.

5.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have introduced a new class of coherent states in which the Fox–Wright function appears as the normalization function. Consequently, these coherent states can be called Fox-Wright coherent states. We have shown that these states satisfy the fundamental conditions of continuity, normalizability and resolution of unity. According to Remark
1
by assigning specific values to the parameters
A_{l}
and
B_{r}
coherent states associated with Fox-Wright function coincides with the generalized hypergeometric coherent states presented in
[
1
]
. In the same manner, particular cases lead to different classes of coherent states involving other special functions. In addition, we have defined the Fox–Wright function in bicomplex space and its existence has been examined across nine distinct cases. It has been shown that when
\Upsilon=-1
the corresponding series is absolutely hyperbolically convergent inside the hyperbolic ball
\mathcal{B}_{h}(0,\mathcal{V})
and on its boundary the convergence is uniform and absolute in the hyperbolic sense whenever
\Re(\Lambda_{1})-\frac{1}{2}>|\Im(\Lambda_{2})|
. Later, we have derived an application of the bicomplex Fox–Wright function in the construction of coherent states, referred to as bicomplex Fox–Wright coherent states. Moreover, we have proposed a framework for transition from the discrete spectrum to the continuous spectrum in the bicomplex setting. Additionally, we have defined a bicomplex-valued function, namely the bicomplex FW-generalized multi-parameter
\nu
-function
\mathcal{V}^{(m,n)}_{b}(W)
and discussed its connection with bicomplex Fox–Wright coherent states for the continuous spectrum, where it serves as the normalization function. Corollary
1
, shows that, for appropriate choices of the parameters

and

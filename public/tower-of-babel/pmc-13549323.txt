Assessment of the Cytotoxic and Apoptotic Impacts of Bimetallic Zinc Iron Oxide Nanoparticles on HuH-7 Cell Line
Authors: Nawal M Al-Malahi, Daoud Ali, Jawaher Alzahrani, Saad Alkahtani, Bader O Almutairi, Saud Alarifi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObservationOne of the main causes of death worldwide is hepatic carcinoma, which is resistant to several anticancer drugs. This work investigated the cytotoxic effects of bimetallic zinc iron oxide ZnFe2O4 nanoparticles (ZnFe2O4NPs) on the human liver cancer (HuH-7) cell line and demonstrated the minimally toxic doses of these particles.MethodsBefore being exposed to target cell line, ZnFe2O4NPswere examined using transmission electron microscopy (TEM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and EDX. ZnFe2O4NPs showed a spherical form and a size of 29.50 ±1.5 nm. MTT and NRU tests were used to assess the cytotoxic impact of different concentrations (0, 10,30, 60, 120, 150 and 200 µg/mL) of ZnFe2O4NPs on HuH-7 cell line. HuH-7 cell line showed a significant lethal effect at the highest concentration of NP exposure, and the cytotoxicity of NPs increased in a concentration-dependent way.ResultsThe median inhibitory concentration (IC50) for HuH-7 cell line at 24 hours was found to be 61 μg/mL of NPs based on the MTT results. At higher ZnFe2O4NPs concentrations, intracellular ROS levels were increased. At high ZnFe2O4NPs concentrations, HuH-7 cell line showed decreased levels of catalase and glutathione (GSH). Rhodamine 123 labeling was used to measure the reduction in mitochondrial membrane potential in both control and NP-exposed cell line. Contrary to their effectiveness at low concentrations, exposure to high doses of ZnFe2O4NPs inhibited cell growth, proliferation, and migration in a wound healing assay. While intended to accelerate healing, high NP concentrations (30.5 µg/mL, depending on type and size) can cause significant cytotoxicity to human liver cancer cell line. Maximum apoptotic cell line were seen in HuH-7 cell line at high NP concentrations. At higher NP concentrations, HuH-7 cell line showed an increase in caspase-3 activity.ConclusionThe aforementioned findings point out the significance of ZnFe2O4NPs as a cytotoxic agent caused by oxidative stress that could be useful in various cancer treatments.Keywords: HuH-7 cell line, ZnFe2O4NPs, ROS, MTT assay, apoptosis

Introduction

Although nanoparticles have been used intentionally for centuries (as finely divided metal pigments) and have been found in the environment since the beginning of human existence (eg, as minerals, clays, and bacterial byproducts), systematically engineered nanoparticles have only recently emerged. The use of nanoparticles in everyday products is a fast-expanding business due to their unique physicochemical properties, including as high surface area, better reactivity, and distinctive visual characteristics that distinguish them apart from their bulk counterparts.1 Nanomaterials are being added to consumer products to improve functionality, performance, and durability. Exogenous ingestion of designed nanoparticles is mostly caused by hand-to-mouth contact in the workplace among scientists, engineers, and industrial workers who are working on innovative goods in labs. Alternatively, these nanoparticles can be ingested directly through drug delivery systems, food, water, or pharmaceuticals. Additionally, the mucociliary escalator may transfer nanoparticles from the respiratory system to the gastrointestinal tract.2 Nanoparticle exposure and safety are still being studied. Because of the size difference, the toxicity of compounds in the nanoscale is a topic of special discussion. Nonmagnetic and biocompatible coatings must be applied to magnetic nanoparticles in order to prevent their aggregation. Because ZnO is harmless and biocompatible, it has also acquired popularity in bio applications as drug transporters, probes for bioimaging, and antibacterial agents.3 Despite the reality that Fe2O3 and ZnO have been studied separately, it is necessary to investigate the Fe2O3-ZnO linked system with its distinct shape and nanotextured surface. The antibacterial and anticancer properties of Fe2O3-ZnO nanostructures with customized surfaces have not yet been investigated, despite reports on their production. Globally, cancer is a leading cause of death. Conventional chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and surgery are the mainstays of cancer treatment, but they have numerous drawbacks that cause non-specific damage to healthy tissues.4 Nevertheless, despite their widespread use in nanotechnological applications, the latest investigation revealed a lack of comprehensive evaluation of the carcinogenic and DNA-damaging potential of bimetallic NPs.5 The International Agency for Research on Cancer has determined that substances pose a carcinogenic risk to people based on enough evidence in experimental animals and insufficient data in humans.6 The mechanism and function of cancer cell line against nanomaterials have been studied using human liver cancer (HuH-7) cell line as a crucial model system.7 Because cancer therapy brings in a new era marked by advancements in the sensitivity, efficacy, and toxicity of cancer treatments, cancer cell line are very fascinating in nanotoxicological and/or nanomedicine research.8

Experimental research indicates that the Fe-Zn mixture has more potential for mutagenesis than its individual components. This observation has been associated with increased ROS production. Therefore, following a 24-hour treatment, we investigated the cytotoxicity and apoptotic properties of ZnFe2O4NPs on HuH-7 cell line. Reactive oxygen species are mostly formed by mitochondria in cell line, and the electron transport chain is where the majority of ROS are produced. Oxidative stress, apoptosis, and genotoxicity responses are the primary mechanisms of toxicity in ZnFe2O4NPs. The goal of this study was to understand the detrimental effects of ZnFe2O4NPs on HuH-7 cell line. Our results will also be useful in assessing ZnFe2O4NPs safety and sustainability in industrial environments.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals and Reagents

Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA), United States Biological (Salem, MA, USA), and Merck (Darmstadt, Germany) supplied all of the analytical-grade compounds utilized in this investigation. Additional chemicals and kits needed for the present investigation were purchased from Cayman Chemical in Ann Arbor, Michigan, USA, as well as from local markets for some chemical salts. We purchased ZnFe2O4NPs (Stock #: US3982) from US Research Nanomaterials, Inc. in Houston, Texas 77084, USA.

Culture of Cell Line

The American Type Culture Collection in Manassas, Virginia, USA provided the human liver cancer cell line (Huh-7) used in our study. The cell line were grown in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% antibiotics (streptomycin and penicillin). They were then incubated at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. Trypsin was employed to produce a subculture for the experiments once the cell line had reached high confluence.

Physiochemical Characterization of Zn-Fe NPs

ZnFe2O4NPs were physically characterized using SEM and TEM (JEOL Inc., Tokyo, Japan). Dynamic light scattering equipment was used to measure the size and zeta potential of ZnFe2O4NPs in water (Malvern, UK).

ZnFe2O4NPs Treatment

ZnFe2O4NPs (10 mg) were dispersed in 10 mL of distilled water and sonicated for 10 minutes to stop aggregation once the cultured was ready for the experiment. In order to produce different concentrations (0, 10, 30, 60, 120, 150, and 200 µg/mL) of NPs for cell exposure in the MTT assay, which was utilized to estimate the IC50 value for further studies, the resultant suspension of NPs was mixed with DMEM medium.

Assay for Wound Healing

Cell migration and wound gap filling were evaluated using the wound healing assay, which also revealed the motility of cell line and potential for metastasis under nanoparticle treatment. After cultivating Huh-7 cell line to (1 × 105 cell line/well) in 6-well plates, a sterile pipette tip was used to make a scratch. PBS washing was used to remove the detached cell line. ZnFe2O4NPs were added to cell line at concentrations of 15.25, 30.5, and 45.75 μg/mL, with one well working as a control, and they were incubated for 0, 24, and 48 hours. A fluorescence microscope (Transparence) was used to take images of the wound closure, and ImageJ software was used to determine the migration rate.

MTT Assay

The MTT test was used to measure cell viability, which is considered one of the assays that demonstrates the activity of mitochondrial enzymes.9 In short, different concentrations (0, 10,30, 60, 120, 150 and 200 µg/mL) of ZnFe2O4NPs were exposed for HuH-7 cell line for 24 hours after they were seeded in 96-well plates (8 × 104 cell line/well). After adding 3 mg/mL of MTT dye (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) and incubating for 4 hours at 37 °C in a CO2 incubator, the cell line were rinsed with PBS and DMSO was added. Formazan crystals, produced by the reduction of tetrazolium by mitochondrial enzymes, were dissolved in DMSO, resulting in a purple color. A microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT) equipped with Gen5 software (version 1.09) was used to measure absorbance at 570 nm.

Determination of IC50 of ZnFe2O4NPs

After 24 hours of treatment, the MTT assay data were used to calculate the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of ZnFe2O4NPs on Huh-7 cell line. Cell viability was expressed as a percentage relative to untreated controls. Based on these results, the IC50 value was determined to be 61 μg/mL. For subsequent assays, three ZnFe2O4NPsconcentrations were selected: concentration I (25% of IC50 = 15.25 μg/mL), concentration II (50% of IC50 = 30.5 μg/mL), and concentration III (75% of IC50 = 45.75 μg/mL).

NRU Assay

The neutral red uptake (NRU) assay was used to further assess cell viability.10 Following a similar procedure to the MTT assay, Neutral Red dye (2.5 mg/mL) was added to the cell line after 24 hours of different concentrations (0, 10,30, 60, 120, 150 and 200 µg/mL) of ZnFe2O4NPs exposure, and the cell line were incubated for an additional 3h at 37 °C in a CO2 incubator. After incubation, the cell line were washed with PBS, and a fixative solution (1 g CaCl2 in 98.5 mL distilled water, then add 0.5 mL formaldehyde) was added. The solution was then immediately discarded. Each well was then filled with the detaining solution (50 mL 100% ethanol, 49 mL distilled water, and 1 mL glacial acetic acid), and the plate was shaken for 15 minutes. A microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT) equipped with Gen5 software (version 1.09) was used to measure absorbance at 540 nm.10

Measurement of ROS Generation

According to Ali et al,11 one assay that assesses oxidative stress levels both quantitatively and qualitatively is reactive oxygen species (ROS). 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) was used for the quantitative analysis approach to detect the intracellular ROS levels. ZnFe2O4NPs were exposed to cell line at doses of 15.25 μg/mL, 30.5 μg/mL, and 45.75 μg/mL for 24 hours at 37 °C in a CO2 incubator following cell culture on 96-well black plates (4 × 104 cell line/well). The cell line were treated for 30 minutes at 37 °C with DCFH-DA (5 µL/mL complete media). Using a fluorescent microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT) equipped with Gen5 software (version 1.09), fluorescence intensity was measured at excitation/emission wavelengths of 485/535 nm.

A set of cell line was seeded in a 6-well plate (8 × 104 cell line/well) for 24 hours at 37 °C in a CO2 incubator for the qualitative analysis method. After being exposed to NPs, the cell line were incubated for a further 24 hours. After that, cell line were washed with chilled PBS and treated with DCFH-DA (premade, 5 µL/mL complete medium) for 30 minutes at 37 °C. Following that, cell line were photographed using an upright fluorescent microscope (GFP) with a CCD cool camera (Nikon Eclipse 80i with Nikon DS-Ri1 12.7-megapixel camera).

MMP Assay Determination

Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) is a bioenergetic parameter that reflects the health of mitochondria both quantitatively and qualitatively using Rhodamine-123 dye. For the quantitative analysis of MMP, following cell line exposure to NPs at concentrations of 15.25 μg/mL, 30.5 μg/mL, and 45.75 μg/mL, they were incubated at 37 °C for 30 minutes with Rhodamine-123 (12.5 μL/mL complete medium). At excitation/emission wavelengths of 511/536 nm, fluorescence was detected using a fluorescence microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT) with Gen5 software (version 1.09). For the qualitative analysis method, the procedure steps in the ROS assay were followed, but cell line were incubated with Rhodamine-123 (12.5 μL/mL complete medium) prepared previously (Merck, USA), instead of DCFH-DA for 30 min at 37 °C. An upright fluorescent microscope (Nikon Eclipse 80i with Nikon DS-Ri1 12.7-megapixel camera) was then used to photograph the cell line. Fluorescence loss suggested low membrane potential in the mitochondria.

GSH Assay

Glutathione (GSH) is a crucial intracellular antioxidant that helps maintain redox balance and neutralize reactive oxygen species (ROS). It was measured using the glutathione assay kit (Cayman’s kit, Item No. 703002USA). After cell line were seeded into four T25 cell culture flasks (2 × 106 cell line/flask) and then exposed to NPs, the media were discarded, and cell line were washed using cold PBS more than once. GSH MES buffer lysis 2x prepared previously (1mL/1mL D.W) was added to each flask, and using a scraper, cell line were harvested and collected into small sterile bottles (All work was in the presence of ice). Cell line were sonicated for 5 min, then transferred to a 15 mL conical tube and centrifuged for 10 min, then 1.5 mL from the suspension for each tube was transferred to another 15 mL conical tube, and 1.5 mL from Metaphosphoric acid prepared previously (0.1 g/1mL D.W) was add to each tube then centrifuged for 10 min. Then 500 µL from each tube was transported to an Eppendorf tube and kept in −80 °C. The assay was completed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and the absorbance for GSH was determined at 405 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT) with Gen5 software (version 1.09).

Catalase Assay

Catalase (CAT) is an essential antioxidant enzyme, protects cell line from oxidative stress by catalyzing the conversion of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) into oxygen and water. Because an excessive accumulation of H2O2 can damage cell line, measuring catalase activity is a crucial indicator of the cellular oxidative stress response. Catalase activity was measured using a commercial catalase assay kit (Cayman’s kit, Item No. 707002, USA). The procedure was conducted following the same steps used in the GSH assay, starting with cell cultivation and continuing up to the step of buffer addition for cell collection and lysis. At this point, a cold assay buffer (531 mg K3PO4, 14.60 mg EDTA, and 50 mL distilled water) was used in place of the GSH MES buffer. The remaining steps were then carried out as described lastly, and the samples were stored at –80 °C. The assay was completed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and the absorbance for Catalase was determined at 540 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT) with Gen5 software (version 1.09).

Caspase-3/7 Activity Assay

Caspase-3/7 are key killer enzymes in the apoptosis cascade, cleaving numerous cellular proteins before triggering programmed cell death. Their activation is thought to be what distinguishes apoptotic cell line from other types of cell line; their activity was estimated using a commercial fluorometric assay kit (Cayman’s kit, Item No. 10009135, USA). HuH-7 cell line were plated in 96-well plates (25 × 103 cell line/well), and treated with ZnFe2O4NPs at concentrations of 15.25, 30.5, and 45.75 μg/mL for 24 hours. After that, the assay was completed according to the manufacturer’s protocol, as detailed in (Atheer et al, 2025). A microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT) with Gen5 software (version 1.09) was used to measure the fluorescent signal that resulted from activated caspase-3/7 cleaving the substrate at excitation/emission wavelengths of 485/535 nm.

Chromosome Condensation Assay

The chromosome condensation assay was developed to investigate nuclear morphological alterations and chromatin condensation as indicators of apoptosis. Chromosome condensation was examined by Hoechst 33342 staining according Alarifi et al,12 After cultivating cell line (1 × 105 cell line/well) and treated with NPs in 6-well plates, cell line were stained with ≈ 20 µg/mL complete medium Hoechst 33342 for 15 min at 37 °C. Nuclear morphology was observed under a fluorescence microscope (DAPI), and condensed/fragmented nuclei were counted as apoptotic.

Statistical Analysis

Using SPSS 26.0 software, a one-way ANOVA test was used to assess statistical differences between the exposed and control groups. * p 0.05 and ** p 0.01 compared to the control were deemed statistically significant. For every experiment, at least three separate experiments were conducted in triplicate.

Results

Characterization of ZnFe2O4NPs

ZnFe2O4NPs formation was confirmed by SEM data (Figure 1a). ZnFe2O4NPs typically have a size of 29.50 ±1.5 nm (Figure 1b and c). ZnFe2O4NPs in suspension were measured for particle size and stability using DLS and zeta potentials. ZnFe2O4NPs were found to be 209.0 ± 2 in size. The analyzed NPs in aqueous solution had net charges (zeta potential) of −11.14 ± 0.36 mV on their surface.

(a). Image of g ZnFe2O4NPs by scanning electron microscope (b). Characterization of g ZnFe2O4NPs by transmission electron microscope (JEM 1011). (c). Size distribution histogram generated by using a TEM image.

Wound Healing

The findings of the scratch assay were evaluated in HuH-7 cell line 24 and 48 hours after the initial scratch, and Figure 2 shows the cell migration. Both the control and treatment groups had visible scratches at 0 hours, and there was some cellular debris in the scratch location after PBS washing. Over the duration of the 24 hours stated above, the diameter of the scratches slightly shrank, showing cell migration in both the low concentration and control groups. After 48 hours, the control group’s mean closure was around 90%, whereas the low-dose cell line’ closure was approximately 50% and the other treated groups showed no apparent closure.

Images of wound healing (scratch test) that indicate the size of the wound after 24 and 48 h of incubation for HuH-7 cell line with different concentrations of ZnFe2O4NPs. Scale bar is 10 µm.

Cell Viability

The MTT assay showed a dose-dependent increase in cytotoxicity of ZnFe2O4NPs against HuH-7 cell line (Figure 3a). The control group demonstrated low cytotoxicity, whereas NPs treatment dramatically reduced cell viability at all tested concentrations. At lower concentrations (10 and 30 μg/mL), cytotoxicity increased significantly. However, at higher concentrations (60–200 μg/mL), cytotoxicity increased significantly, reaching approximately 64% at 200 μg/mL. The treatment and control groups differed significantly, according to statistical analysis (p < 0.05, 0.01), with superscript letters indicating significant differences between concentrations. These results demonstrated that mitochondrial enzyme activity decreased with increasing cytotoxicity, leading to a gradual lightening of the purple color.

Cytotoxicity ZnFe2O4NPs in HuH-7 cell line for 24 h, based on the (a) MTT assay and (b) NRU assay. Each value represents the mean ± SE of three experiments. n = 3, *p<0.05, **p<0.01, vs control.

The neutral red uptake (NRU) assay was performed to confirm the results of the MTT assay. In comparison to the control group, exposure to increasing nanoparticle doses (10–200 µg/mL) significantly increased cytotoxicity, as seen in Figure 3b. At 10 µg/mL, cytotoxicity increased modestly but was significantly higher than control (p < 0.05, 0.01). Further increases in concentration to 30 and 60 µg/mL caused cytotoxicity values of approximately 10% and 12%, respectively. At higher concentrations, cytotoxicity reached ~14% to 22%, respectively, and differed significantly from the control. These results confirm the cytotoxic effect of ZnFe2O4NPs on HuH-7 cell line.

Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS)

The quantitative results for this assay were assessed using DCFH-DA in HuH-7 cell line following exposure to ZnFe2O4NPs. In comparison to the control group, ROS levels dramatically rose in a concentration-dependent manner, as seen in Figure 4a. The control cell line exhibited the lowest fluorescence intensity, while treatment with 15.25 and 30.5 µg/mL of nanoparticles markedly elevated ROS production, showing statistically significant differences (p < 0.05) from the control. The highest concentration tested (45.75 µg/mL) further enhanced ROS generation, which was significantly higher than all other groups (p < 0.05, 0.01). The qualitative findings supported the earlier findings; fluorescence microscope pictures revealed that the treated groups’ DCFH-DA fluorescence intensity was higher than that of the control group (Figure 4b), which in turn increased the nanoparticles’ actual toxicity to HuH-7 cell line. These results showed that ZnFe2O4NPs induced oxidative stress in Huh-7 cell line by stimulating ROS accumulation in a dose-dependent manner.

(a) Percentage change in intracellular ROS generation in the HuH-7 cell line. (b) Induction of ROS levels in HuH-7 cell line after exposure with ZnFe2O4NPs for 24 hr. Each value represents the mean ± SE of three experiments. n = 3, *p<0.05, **p<0.01vs control. Scale bar is 20 µm.

Mitochondrial Membrane Potential (MMP)

Rhodamine 123 was used to measure the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP). Rhodamine 123 tends to concentrate in the mitochondria of living cell line because they retain their electron potential. Compared to when it spreads throughout the cytoplasm of dead cell line, when the mitochondrial membrane has been damaged, it fluoresces more powerfully. Figure 5a and b illustrate the quantitative and qualitative evaluation of ZnFe2O4NPs impact on Huh-7 cell line (MMP) respectively. Compared to the control group, the treatment with nanoparticles resulted in a significant, concentration-dependent decrease in fluorescence intensity, suggesting apoptosis via the mitochondrial membrane disruption. The maximum fluorescence was seen in control cell line, which represented intact MMP. There was a moderate decline following treatment with 15.25 µg/mL, and a further decline following treatment with 30.5 µg/mL. At 45.75 µg/mL, the MMP loss was the most noticeable, and the fluorescence dropped noticeably less than in any other group (p < 0.05, 0.01). These findings indicate that ZnFe2O4NPs disrupt mitochondrial function in HuH-7 cell line by producing a dose-dependent loss of MMP.

(a) Quantitative of MMP in HuH-7 cell line. (b) Fluorescence images showing loss of MMP in Huh-7 cell line after exposure to ZnFe2O4NPs for 24 h. Each value represents the mean ± SE of three experiments. n = 3, *p<0.05, **p<0.01vs control. Scale bar is 20 µm.

Oxidative Stress

After being exposed to ZnFe2O4NPs, HuH-7 cell line’ total GSH levels dramatically dropped, as seen in Figure 6a. In comparison to the control group, GSH dropped at 15.25 µg/mL (p < 0.01) and then at 30.5 µg/mL (p < 0.05). Remarkably, there was a modest recovery of GSH at 45.75 µg/mL (p < 0.05), but it was still much lower than the control. These results imply that exposure to nanoparticles modifies cellular redox balance in a concentration-dependent way.

(a). Total GSH (mM/mg) in Huh-7 cell line exposed to ZnFe2O4NPs for 24 h. (b). Catalase activity in Huh-7 cell line exposed to ZnFe2O4NPs for 24 h. Each value represents the mean ± SE of three experiments. n = 3, *p<0.05, **p<0.01vs. control.

The impact of ZnFe2O4NPs on catalase activity in HuH-7 cell line is seen in Figure 6b. The catalase levels in the control group and at 15.25 µg/mL showed a marginally significant change (p > 0.05). However, catalase activity dramatically dropped compared to the control at higher doses (30.5 and 45.75 µg/mL), as seen by the double star (**) (p < 0.01). These findings suggest that exposure to higher quantities of nanoparticles suppresses catalase activity.

Caspase-3/7 Activity and Chromosome Condensation

Caspases-3/7 activity was assessed to verify whether apoptotic pathways are involved in the cytotoxicity caused by ZnFe2O4NPs (Figure 7a). In comparison to the control, caspase-3/7 activity dramatically increased in a dose-dependent manner upon exposure to nanoparticles (p < 0.05, 0.01). There was a slight, non-significant spike at 15.25 µg/mL and a more pronounced, significant increase at 30.5 µg/mL. The largest caspase activation was produced by the highest concentration (45.75 µg/mL), which was noticeably more than that of any other group. These results confirm that ZnFe2O4NPs trigger apoptosis in HuH-7 cell line, likely mediated by ROS generation and mitochondrial membrane disorder, as demonstrated in the previous assays.

Discussion

Due to the limited effectiveness of current therapeutic approaches, hepatic cancer, a very common and deadly cancer, provides a significant clinical challenge.13 The existence rate is still extremely low despite major therapeutic advances. This is mostly because of side effects from chemotherapy, and drug resistance, as well as ineffective suppression of tumor invasion and metastasis.14 ZnFe2O4NPs dose-dependent in vitro anticancer potential was evaluated using HuH-7 cell line. It is important to find bimetallic ZnFe2O4NPs as therapeutic targets for cancer since they have been shown to play a critical role in signaling cascades. The enhanced anticancer efficacy of nanoparticles (NPs) against several cancer types, including as ovarian, lung, colorectal, and breast cancer, has been the subject of numerous investigations.15 NPs composition, concentration, size, shape, surface charge, surface area, and dispersion states are all correlated with their cytotoxic efficacy.16 Zinc and iron seem dangerous since they cause oxidative damage (liver LPO) and DNA damage in blood cell line.17 The efficiency of drug delivery systems is influenced by the concentration, aggregation, and circulation time of nanoparticles. According to Almutairi et al,18 consumers are not aware of the risk of being exposed to nanoparticles, which can enter the bodies of humans as well as animals in a number of ways. This is a major effect of the extensive use of nanotechnology-based compounds. In this study, we used a variety of techniques to examine the effects of ZnFe2O4NPs on HuH-7 cell line. This work aims to ascertain how ZnFe2O4NPs affect HuH-7 cell line’ growth inhibition, genotoxic reactions, oxidative stress, and apoptotic cell death. The results of the dynamic light scattering research showed the NPs’ size dispersion. ZnFe2O4NPs were determined to be 29.5 ±1.5 nm in size using TEM. On the other hand, HuH-7 cell line exposed to ZnFe2O4NPs were evaluated for vitality using MTT and NRU tests. The MTT results showed that numerous anomalies in the cell line’ metabolic ability caused growth to be reduced.19 Despite to their effectiveness at low concentrations, exposure to high concentrations of ZnFe2O4NPs inhibits cell growth, proliferation, and migration in wound healing. High NPs concentrations (30.5 µg/mL, depending on type and size) can seriously harm human liver cancer cell line, while nanoparticles are meant to speed up the process of healing. ZnFe2O4NPs significantly decreased HuH-7 cell viability after 24 hours of treatment; the IC50 value for HuH-7 cell line was 61µg/mL. Although oxidative stress has been linked to the toxicity of other monometallic NPs like Zn and Fe, these results indicate that oxidative stress is most likely the particular mechanism of ZnFe2O4NPs toxicity.20 This is linked to the cytotoxic effects of NPs on both healthy and malignant cell line, according to research.21 In line with other earlier research, our results shown that the combination of Zn and Fe represents a particular hazardous entity that generates greater ROS.21 Nanomaterials are frequently genotoxic because oxidative stress brought on by an excess of ROS is frequently strongly associated with oxidative damage to proteins and DNA. Our results show that the combination of Fe and Zn NPs increases the formation of ROS. Recent research indicates that ZnFe2O4NPs reduced GSH and catalase levels, elevated ROS, and reduced HuH-7 cell survival. ROS produced when the mitochondrial membrane is physically damaged are one cause of mitochondrial damage. Continuous exposure to nanomaterials can either enhance ROS generation or reduce antioxidant defense, upsetting the oxidant balance.22 Our results are consistent with previous research showing that arsenic trioxide doses that cause BEAS-2B cell line to undergo apoptosis lead to significantly increased levels of gene expression in apoptotic cell line.23 We observed that compared to the control cell, cell line exposed to high concentrations of ZnFe2O4NPs experienced higher apoptosis. This suggests that ZnFe2O4NPs significantly increased the level of cell death and apoptotic induction.

Conclusion

The size, form, and composition of ZnFe2O4NPs were confirmed by SEM, TEM, and DLS analyzes. The ZnFe2O4NPs caused apoptosis in HuH-7 cell line, demonstrating their lethal toxicity. The minimum dose of ZnFe2O4NPs for next in vivo and biological sciences research was confirmed by the aforementioned finding.

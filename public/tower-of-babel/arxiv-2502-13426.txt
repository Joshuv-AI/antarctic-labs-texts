Stability of difference equations with interspecific density dependence, competition, and maturation delays
Geoffrey R. Hosack, Maud El-Hachem, Nicholas J. Beeton

Abstract
. A general system of difference equations is presented for multispecies communities with density dependent population growth and delayed maturity. Interspecific competition, mutualism, predation, commensalism, and amensalism are accommodated. A sufficient condition for the local asymptotic stability of a coexistence equilibrium in this system is then proven. Using this system, the generalisation of the Beverton-Holt and Leslie-Gower models of competition to multispecies systems with possible maturation delays is presented and shown to yield interesting stability properties. The stability of coexistence depends on the relative abundances of the species at the unique interior equilibrium. A sufficient condition for local stability is derived that only requires intraspecific competition to outweigh interspecific competition. The condition does not depend on maturation delays. The derived stability properties are used to develop a novel estimation approach for the coefficients of interspecific competition. This approach finds an optimal configuration given the conjecture that coexisting species strive to outcompete competitors and are most likely observed near the stable interior equilibrium with the greatest dampening of perturbations. The optimal solution is compared to estimates of niche overlap using an empirical example of malaria mosquito vectors with delayed maturity in the Anopheles gambiae sensu lato species complex.



1.
Introduction

Maturation delays and interspecific density dependence caused by the fundamental ecological interactions of competition, mutualism, predation, commensalism or amensalism are known factors that determine the stability of ecosystems and ecological communities
(
May 1974
)
. Sufficient conditions for local asymptotic stability of equilibria in the discrete delay difference equations

x_{i}(t+1)=\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})x_{i}(t)+x_{i}(t)G_{i}(\bm{x}(t-\delta_{i})),\quad i\in\mathcal{M}=\{1,\ldots,m\},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

are therefore derived for a system of
m
species, where each species
i\in\mathcal{M}
attains adulthood and reproductive maturity at age
\delta_{i}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. The adults of species
i
have delayed maturity if
\delta_{i}>0
, survive to the next time step with probability
\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})
, and reproduce with per capita population growth function
G_{i}
that accounts for both intraspecific and interspecific density dependence.

The single species case (
m=1
) of this system corresponds to the well-known model proposed by
Clark 1976
.
Clark 1976
presents sufficient conditions for local stability of this single species delayed model by Rouché’s theorem, although necessary and sufficient conditions require numerical calculation outside special cases. Local stability for the single species model in certain cases implies global stability, but not generally
(
López and
Parreño 2016
)
.
Fisher and
Goh 1984
propose sufficient conditions for global stability by construction of a Lyapunov function using results from
La Salle 1976
. These results do not depend on the delay. See
Liz 2020
for a recent review and further analysis of the single species delayed difference population model.

For the multispecies delayed system (
m>1
),
Fisher 1984
shows that the local and global stability properties are deduced from the corresponding non-delayed system defined by

x_{i}(t+1)=(1-\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i}))^{-1}G_{i}(\bm{x}(t)),\quad i\in\mathcal{M},

see also discussion by
El-Morshedy and
Liz 2006
. In the important case of interspecific competition,
Sacker 2011
states that consideration of multispecies discrete-time systems “inevitably” leads to consideration of the multispecies extension of the Leslie-Gower model
(
Leslie and
Gower 1958
)
. For
m
species and
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
given nonnegative
m\times m
matrix
\bm{A}\geq 0
, this model is defined by the non-delayed system

x_{i}(t+1)=\frac{\lambda_{i}x_{i}(t)}{1+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}x_{k}(t)},\quad i\in\mathcal{M},

which is related to the non-delayed system of
Fisher 1984
in an obvious way. The fecundity rate for species
i
in the multispecies non-delayed Leslie-Gower model is
\lambda_{i}
. The competitive effect of species
j
on the population growth rate of species
i
is defined by
A_{ij}
, and intraspecific competition is defined by
A_{ii}
.
Sacker 2011
provides sufficient conditions for global stability in the non-delayed model that depend on
\bm{\lambda}
and “sufficiently small” interspecific competition defined by
\bm{A}
.
Borogovac 2016
provides conditions that require invertible
\bm{A}
,
\bm{\lambda}>\bm{1}
, and
\sum_{j\neq i}\lambda_{j}A_{ij}/A_{jj}\leq\lambda_{i}-1
, for all
i\in\mathcal{M}
. In a multispecies application with maturation delays,
Hosack
et al. 2023
incorporate Leslie-Gower interspecific density dependence into a system that also includes introgression, immigration and emigration among three species of mosquitoes that vector human malaria.

In Section
2
, sufficient conditions are given for local stability in the general multispecies delayed model. Constraints on the eigenvalues of its linearised approximation near an equilibrium are obtained using a geometric argument by Gers̆gorin’s Theorem
(
Gers̆gorin 1931
)
as extended by
Brualdi 1982
using directed graphs. The result is applied to the multispecies Leslie-Gower model of competition that is extended to allow for potential delayed maturity and age-specific survival probabilities (Section
3
). If the density independent rates of adult recruitment, defined by the product of the fecundity rate and immature survival probabilities for each species, exceed the probabilities of adult mortality,
\bm{z}=1-\bm{\sigma}(\bm{\delta})>\bm{0}
, then a sufficient condition for the local stability of a unique feasible interior equilibrium is given by

A_{ii}>\sum_{i\neq j}A_{ij},\quad i\in\mathcal{M}.

If intraspecific competition outweighs interspecific competition then the coexistence equilibrium is unique, feasible and locally asymptotically stable.

It is also shown that the local asymptotic stability of the interior equilibrium for the delayed competition model depends on the relative abundances, that is, the species composition or fractional contributions of each species to the total equilibrium abundance.
This result is used in Section
4
to develop a method to find an optimal configuration of the parameters
\bm{A}
given the assumptions that i) observed species abundances are more likely to be observed near a more stable equilibrium than a less stable one, and ii) species seek to increase competitive advantage over other species. The estimate requires knowledge of fecundity, density independent survival probability and an observed equilibrium in which species coexist. The approach does not require specific information about the carrying capacities of the species, but instead depends only on estimates of relative abundance that are often more readily available from field data than absolute or true abundance
(
Sinka et al. 2016
)
. The development extends ideas that relate relative abundance to measures of niche overlap and parameterisation of the competition coefficients in the Lotka-Volterra competitive system of ordinary differential equations
(
Levins 1968
;
May 1975
)
. The previous and novel procedures are compared using empirical estimates provided by
Pombi
et al. 2017
for a system of three competing species of
Anopheles gambiae
sensu lato mosquitoes with delayed maturity that vector human malaria.

2.
Interspecific Density Dependence with Delayed Maturity

The dynamics of the set of species
\mathcal{M}=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
at times
t\in\mathbb{Z}
with immature life history stages of duration
\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for species
i\in\mathcal{M}
are described by the system of delayed difference equations

(1)

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(t+1,0)=

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(t-\delta_{1},\delta_{1})G_{1}(\bm{\chi}(t-\delta_{1},\bm{\delta}))

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(t+1,1)=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(0)\chi_{1}(t,0)

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(t+1,\delta_{1}-1)=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\delta_{1}-2)\chi_{1}(t,\delta_{1}-2)

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(t+1,\delta_{1})=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\delta_{1}-1)\chi_{1}(t,\delta_{1}-1)+\sigma_{1}(\delta_{1})\chi_{1}(t,\delta_{1})

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(t+1,0)=

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(t-\delta_{2},\delta_{2})G_{2}(\bm{\chi}(t-\delta_{2},\bm{\delta}))

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(t+1,1)=

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(0)\chi_{2}(t,0)

\displaystyle\chi_{m}(t+1,\delta_{m}-1)=

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(\delta_{m}-2)\chi_{m}(t,\delta_{m}-2)

\displaystyle\chi_{m}(t+1,\delta_{m})=

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(\delta_{m}-1)\chi_{m}(t,\delta_{m}-1)+\sigma_{m}(\delta_{m})\chi_{m}(t,\delta_{m}),

where
\chi_{i}(t,a_{i})
denotes the number of immature individuals of species
i
with age class
a_{i}=0,1,\dots,\delta_{i}-1
and
\chi_{i}(t,\delta_{i})
the number of mature individuals of at least age
\delta_{i}
at time
t
. The per capita growth function
G_{i}(\bm{\chi}(t-\delta_{i},\bm{\delta}))
describes how the adult reproduction of species
i
is influenced by the adult population sizes of all species. The immature stages later emerge as reproductive adults after the conclusion of the immature life history stage. The probability of density independent mortality for reproducing adults at each time step is
z_{i}=1-\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})\geq 0
, where
\sigma_{i}(a_{i})\leq 1
is the daily survival probability of age class
a_{i}\in\{0,1,\ldots,\delta_{i}\}
for species
i
. If there is no lag for species
i
such that
\delta_{i}=0
then it is understood that
\chi_{i}(t+1,0)=\sigma_{i}(0)\chi_{i}(t,0)+\chi_{i}(t,0)G_{i}(\bm{\chi}(t,\bm{\delta}))
.

Eq. (
1
) allows for species-specific delayed multispecies density dependence including the fundamental ecological relationships of interspecific competition, mutualism, predation, commensalism and amensalism. It is the multispecies extension of the single species delayed maturity model presented by
Fisher and
Goh 1984
. Eq. (
1
) may be collapsed to

(2)

x_{i}(t+1)=\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})x_{i}(t)+x_{i}(t-\delta_{i})G_{i}(\bm{x}(t-\delta_{i}))\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a),\quad i\in\mathcal{M}.

where
x_{i}(t)=\chi_{i}(t,\delta_{i})
. If
\delta_{i}=0
then the empty product
\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a)
is defined as the multiplicative identity with value one.

Eq. (
2
) has been analysed for single species (
m=1
) to establish local stability conditions by
Clark 1976
and
Fisher and
Goh 1984
. This scalar form of Eq. (
2
) is there analysed by comparison to the roots of the characteristic polynomial constructed from its linearised non-delayed version. For the single species case,
Fisher and
Goh 1984
also construct a Lyapunov function on the scalar equation of Eq. (
2
) with
m=1
to provide sufficient conditions for global stability. Sufficient global stability conditions for the positive equilibrium of specific multispecies non-delayed versions of Eq. (
2
) with competitive density dependent growth functions have previously been presented
(
Sacker 2011
;
Jiang and
Niu 2017
;
Hou 2021
)
. Here, sufficient local stability conditions are provided for the general multispecies system with delays. A detailed examination is then provided for a multispecies competitive system of particular interest.

The Jacobian matrix of Eq. (
1
) evaluated at equilibria derived from Eq. (
2
) for the adult compartments is investigated in Section
2.1
. A sufficient condition for local asymptotic stability is then derived for the case of non-zero adult equilibrium abundances (Section
2.2
). The case of boundary equilibria is investigated in Section
2.3
.

2.1.
Local Asymptotic Stability

Applying results from
Clark 1976
shows that for the single species case (
m=1
), a sufficient condition for the local stability of Eq. (
2
) at an equilibrium
x_{1}^{\ast}
is

(3)

\left|G_{1}(x_{1}^{\ast})+x_{1}^{\ast}\left.\frac{dG_{1}(x_{1}(t))}{dx_{1}(t)}\right|_{x_{1}(t)=x_{1}^{\ast}}\right|\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{1}-1}\sigma_{1}(a)<z_{1}.

The above sufficient condition does not depend on the delay except through the product of the survival probabilities of the immature stages. Necessary and sufficient conditions for local stability, however, depend on the delay with closed-form expressions only available for certain choices of functional forms and parameter values
(
Clark 1976
;
Fisher and
Goh 1984
)
. It will be shown that a closed form expression similar to Eq. (
3
) applies to the local stability of the multispecies delayed system of Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
).

An equilibrium
\bm{\chi}^{\ast}(\bm{a})
of the multispecies age classes of the delayed system of Eq. (
1
) is locally asymptotically stable if the moduli of all eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix evaluated at the equilibrium, denoted by the matrix
\bm{J}^{\ast}
, are less than one. The spectral radius of a square matrix is defined as the maximum magnitude of all its eigenvalues. The equilibrium
\bm{\chi}^{\ast}(\bm{a})
of Eq. (
1
) is therefore locally asymptotically stable if and only if the spectral radius of
\bm{J}^{\ast}
is less than one,
\rho(\bm{J}^{\ast})<1
.

To derive
\bm{J}^{\ast}
, it will be useful to note that the adult age class equilibrium abundances of Eq. (
1
) are equivalent to those of Eq. (
2
), such that
\bm{\chi}^{\ast}(\bm{\delta})=\bm{x}^{\ast}
, because
\bm{\chi}(t,\bm{\delta})=\bm{x}(t)
. For species
i
with maturation delay
\delta_{i}>0
, the partial derivatives of Eq. (
1
) evaluated at equilibrium are then

(4)

\left.\frac{\partial\chi_{i}(t+1,a_{i})}{\partial\chi_{j}(t,b_{j})}\right|_{\bm{\chi}(t,\bm{a})=\bm{\chi}^{\ast}(\bm{a})}=\begin{cases}\sigma_{i}(a_{i})&\textrm{if }i=j,\,a_{i}=b_{j}-1,\,b_{j}=1,\ldots,\delta_{i},\\
\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})&\textrm{if }i=j,\,a_{i}=b_{j}=\delta_{i}\,,\\
G_{i}(\bm{x}^{\ast})+x_{i}^{\ast}\left.\frac{\partial G_{i}(\bm{x}(t))}{\partial x_{i}(t)}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)=\bm{x}^{\ast}}&\textrm{if }i=j,\,a_{i}=0,\,b_{j}=\delta_{j},\\
\left.x_{i}^{\ast}\frac{\partial G_{i}(\bm{x}(t))}{\partial x_{j}(t)}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)=\bm{x}^{\ast}}&\textrm{if }i\neq j,\,a_{i}=0,\,b_{j}=\delta_{j},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For species
i
without delayed maturity (
\delta_{i}=0
), the partial derivatives are

(5)

\left.\frac{\partial\chi_{i}(t+1,0)}{\partial\chi_{j}(t,b)}\right|_{\bm{\chi}(t,\bm{a})=\bm{\chi}^{\ast}(\bm{a})}=\begin{cases}\sigma_{i}(0)+G_{i}(\bm{x}^{\ast})+x_{i}^{\ast}\left.\frac{\partial G_{i}(\bm{x}(t))}{\partial x_{i}(t)}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)=\bm{x}^{\ast}}&\textrm{if }i=j,\,b=\delta_{j},\\
\left.x_{i}^{\ast}\frac{\partial G_{i}(\bm{x}(t))}{\partial x_{j}(t)}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)=\bm{x}^{\ast}}&\textrm{if }i\neq j,\,b=\delta_{j},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Evidently, only the zero age classes (
a_{i}=0
) in Eq. (
4
) depend on the equilibrium values.
The Jacobian matrix evaluated at the equilibrium
\bm{\chi}^{\ast}(\bm{a})
is

(6)

\bm{J}^{\ast}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{J}_{11}^{\ast}&\bm{J}_{12}^{\ast}&\cdots&\bm{J}_{1m}^{\ast}\\
\bm{J}_{21}^{\ast}&\bm{J}_{22}^{\ast}&\cdots&\bm{J}_{2m}^{\ast}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\bm{J}_{m1}^{\ast}&\bm{J}_{m2}^{\ast}&\cdots&\bm{J}_{mm}^{\ast}\end{bmatrix}

that consists of block matrices defined by

\bm{J}_{ij}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &&\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&0&\left.\frac{\partial\chi_{i}(t+1,0)}{\partial\chi_{i}(t,\delta_{i})}\right|_{\bm{\chi}(t,\bm{a})=\bm{\chi}^{\ast}(\bm{a})}\\

\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&&&\vdots\\

&&\begin{bmatrix}\bm{0}&\left.\frac{\partial\chi_{i}(t+1,0)}{\partial\chi_{j}(t,\delta_{j})}\right|_{\bm{\chi}(t,\bm{a})=\bm{\chi}^{\ast}(\bm{a})}\\

\end{bmatrix}\hskip 75.0pt&\quad\textrm{if }i\neq j,\end{aligned}\right.

where the block matrix in the second case has all zero entries except for the last entry in its first row if
\delta_{i}>0
and, by Eqs. (

5

\bm{J}_{ij}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &&\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{i}(0)+G_{i}(\bm{x}^{\ast})+x_{i}^{\ast}\left.\frac{\partial G_{i}(\bm{x}(t))}{\partial x_{i}(t)}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)=\bm{x}^{\ast}}\end{bmatrix}&\quad\textrm{if }i=j,\\

2.2.
Sufficient Condition for Local Stability of Coexistence Equilibrium

The graph of Eq. (
6
) is used to construct a sufficient condition for the local asymptotic stability of the equilibrium. This result builds on a well-known result that bounds the location of the eigenvalues. Let
s(\bm{B})
denote the spectrum of eigenvalues of matrix
\bm{B}
, which is the point set of values of
\bm{v}
that satisfy
\det(\bm{B}-\bm{v}\bm{I})=0
. The Gers̆gorin theorem
(
Horn and
Johnson 1985
, see, e.g.,)
provides a geometric argument for bounding the spectrum of eigenvalues.

Theorem 1

(Gers̆gorin)
.

For
n\times n
matrix
\bm{B}
, let

R_{i}(\bm{B})=\sum_{j\neq i}\left|B_{ij}\right|\,,\quad\forall i\,,

denote the deleted absolute row sums. The eigenvalues of
\bm{B}
are located in the region formed by the union of
n
discs such that

s(\bm{B})\subset\cup_{i}\left\{v:\left|v-B_{ii}\right|\leq R_{i}(\bm{B})\right\}.

Gers̆gorin’s theorem establishes that the eigenvalues of
\bm{B}
are located within the Gers̆gorin region.
The discs that form a Gers̆gorin region are called Gers̆gorin discs and the corresponding boundaries of the discs are Gers̆gorin circles.

Direct application of the Gers̆gorin theorem to
\bm{J}^{\ast}
does not necessarily bound the Gers̆gorin discs within unit distance of the origin of the complex plane. The deleted absolute row sums that correspond to rows with the adult survival probabilities,
\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})
for
i\in\mathcal{M}
in Eq. (
6
), are not necessarily less than
\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})
. If any of these sums exceed one then at least one Gers̆gorin circle extends beyond unit distance of the origin, and the Gers̆gorin theorem then fails to bound the spectral radius less than one. For example, in the case of full survival and thus a simple lag in adult emergence,
\sigma_{i}(a)=1
for
a=0,\ldots,\delta_{i}-1
, and the Gers̆gorin theorem does not bound the spectral radius
\rho(\bm{J}^{\ast})
below one. Application of the theorem to the transposition of the Jacobian matrix would give rise to consideration of the deleted absolute column sums. However, deleted absolute column sums of
\bm{J}^{\ast}
that include the partial derivatives of the per capita growth function must similarly account for the adult stage daily survival probabilities.

More can be said, however, if the graph of
\bm{J}^{\ast}
is also taken into account. The following terminology is needed
(
Horn and
Johnson 1985
, Ch. 6)
; see also
Patten 1985
for an ecological application. The directed graph of
n\times n
matrix
\bm{J}^{\ast}
has a directed arc (edge) between a pair
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
of the
n
nodes
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
if and only if
{J}^{\ast}_{ij}\neq 0
. A directed path is a sequence of arcs
P_{i_{1}}P_{i_{2}}
,
P_{i_{2}}P_{i_{3}}
,
P_{i_{3}}P_{i_{4}}
,
\ldots
, with length defined as the number of arcs in the sequence. A cycle is a directed path that starts and ends at the same node such that no other node appears more than once. This is sometimes said to be a simple (directed) cycle. A nontrivial cycle
\gamma
excludes cycles of length one. If a directed path of finite length begins at
P_{i}
and ends at
P_{j}
for every pair of distinct nodes in the directed graph of
\bm{J}^{\ast}
, then the directed graph is then said to be strongly connected. Figure

The definitions of (weakly or strongly) connected graphs and (weakly) irreducible matrices are also required.
Horn and
Johnson 1985
show the equivalence between the following statements for an
n\times n
matrix
\bm{B}
:

(1)

\bm{B}
is irreducible,

(2)

(\bm{I}+|\bm{B}|)^{n-1}>0
, and

(3)

the directed graph of
\bm{B}
is strongly connected.

In the above,
|\bm{B}|
is the matrix with entries equal to the absolute value of the entries of
\bm{B}
. A matrix
\bm{B}
with all positive (nonnegative) entries is denoted by
\bm{B}>0
(
\bm{B}\geq 0
).

An
r\times r
matrix
\bm{B}
is reducible if either
r=1
and
\bm{B}=0
, or a permutation
\bm{P}
exists to triangular block form

(7)

\bm{P}\bm{B}\bm{P}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B}_{11}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{B}_{21}&\bm{B}_{22}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\bm{B}_{t1}&\bm{B}_{t2}&\cdots&\bm{B}_{tt}\end{bmatrix},

where the diagonal blocks are square, and either irreducible or a zero entry. The diagonal blocks
\bm{B}_{ii}
correspond to the classes of
\bm{B}
, where a class is a set of nodes that communicate
(
Berman and
Plemmons 1994
)
. Two nodes
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
,
i\neq j
, communicate if there is a directed path from
P_{i}
to
P_{j}
, and also from
P_{j}
to
P_{i}
. An irreducible matrix
\bm{B}
is a triangular block matrix with a single block, and an irreducible matrix is not reducible.

Additionally, a matrix
\bm{B}
is said to be weakly irreducible if
\bm{C}=(\bm{I}+|\bm{B}|)^{n-1}
has at least one nonzero off-diagonal entry
{C}_{ij}
,
i\neq j
, in each row
i=1,\ldots,n
such that
C_{ji}
is also nonzero. The matrix
\bm{B}
is weakly irreducible if and only if its directed graph is weakly connected such that a nontrivial cycle exists between each node and another node. Note that an irreducible
n\times n
matrix
\bm{B}
with
n>1
is weakly irreducible.

The following theorem
(
Brualdi 1982
)
uses the weakly connected property. Let
C(\bm{B})
denote the set of all nontrivial cycles
\gamma
in the directed graph of
\bm{B}
.

Theorem 2

(Brualdi)
.

If the
n\times n
matrix
\bm{B}
is weakly irreducible, then

s(\bm{B})\subset\underset{\gamma\in C(\bm{B})}{\bigcup}\left\{v:\prod_{P_{i}\in\gamma}|v-B_{ii}|\leq\prod_{P_{i}\in\gamma}R_{i}(\bm{B})\right\}.

If a cycle
\gamma
of length
k>1
includes nodes
P_{i_{1}}P_{i_{2}}
,
\ldots
,
P_{i_{k}}P_{i_{k+1}}
with
P_{i_{1}}=P_{i_{k+1}}
then the index
i=i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}
in the products of the above theorem.

Proposition 1
.

For the system of Eq. (
1
) with adult stage equilibrium
\bm{x}^{\ast}
, if

\left\{\left|\left(G_{i}(\bm{x}^{\ast})+x_{i}^{\ast}\left.\frac{\partial G_{i}(\bm{x}(t))}{\partial x_{i}(t)}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)=\bm{x}^{\ast}}\right)\right|+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}\backslash i}\left|\left(\left.x_{i}^{\ast}\frac{\partial G_{i}(\bm{x}(t))}{\partial x_{j}(t)}\right|_{\bm{x}(t)=\bm{x}^{\ast}}\right)\right|\right\}\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a)<z_{i},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{M},

then the equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable.

Proof.

Let

\delta_{i}=0

i\in\mathcal{S}

Each species

Each species

2.3.
Sufficient Conditions for Local Stability of Boundary Equilibria

Let
\bm{\hat{x}}=[\bm{x}^{\circ},\bm{x}^{\bullet}]^{\top}
denote an alternative equilibrium, where
\bm{x}^{\circ}=\bm{0}
is an
r
-dimensional vector with
r\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. The boundary equilibria are those where the positive equilibrium abundances in
\bm{x}^{\bullet}>0
form a
p
-dimensional vector,
p=m-r
. If
\bm{x}^{\circ}
is
m
-dimensional then
\bm{\hat{x}}=\bm{0}
is the trivial equilibrium. A sufficient condition for the local asymptotic stability is provided by application of Proposition
1
to the Jacobian matrix evaluated at
\bm{\hat{x}}
. However, if the per capita population growth functions satisfy a certain condition then more can be said.

Let
\bm{\hat{J}}
denote the Jacobian matrix of Eq. (
6
) evaluated at an equilibrium where at least one of the species has zero abundance. Let
\bm{{J}^{\circ}}
and
\bm{{J}^{\bullet}}
denote the block submatrices on the main diagonal of
\bm{\hat{J}}
conformally partitioned with
\bm{\hat{x}}=[\bm{x}^{\circ},\bm{x}^{\bullet}]^{\top}
.

Proposition 2
.

Assume that Eq. (
1
) for
i\in\mathcal{M}
has the property that

\hat{x}_{i}=x_{i}^{\circ}=0\implies\hat{x}_{i}G_{i}(\bm{\hat{x}})=0,

and assume
\rho(\bm{{J}^{\bullet}})<1
. The equilibrium
\bm{\hat{x}}
is locally asymptotically stable if

\left|G_{i}(\bm{\hat{x}})\right|\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a)<z_{i},\quad\forall i\in\{k:\hat{x}_{k}=x_{k}^{\circ}\}.

Proof.

The matrix
\bm{\hat{J}}
may be permuted to have block structure

\bm{\hat{J}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{{J}^{\circ}}&\bm{0}\\
\ast&\bm{{J}^{\bullet}}\end{bmatrix}

that is conformal to
[\bm{x}^{\circ},\bm{x}^{\bullet}]^{\top}
and with zero entries in the upper right quadrant from Eq. (
6
), although not necessarily the lower left quadrant. The spectra of eigenvalues
s(\bm{\hat{J}})
is therefore the union of the spectra
s(\bm{{J}^{\circ}})\cup s(\bm{{J}^{\bullet}})
. It is given that the maximum modulus of the eigenvalues in
s(\bm{{J}^{\bullet}})
is less than one. Again by Eq. (
6
),
\bm{{J}^{\circ}}
is a block diagonal matrix with each of the
r
blocks corresponding to a species
i
with zero equilibrium abundance for the adult class,
x_{i}^{\circ}=0
,
i=1,\ldots,r
. The spectra of eigenvalues for
\bm{{J}^{\circ}}
is then the union of the spectra for each block. The largest cycle in each block is also the minimal cycle that includes all age classes. The condition of the proposition is sufficient for the moduli of eigenvalues to be less than one across the
r
blocks by application of Proposition
1
to each block.
∎

The condition on the per capita growth function
G_{i}
of a potentially invading species with zero abundance
x_{i}^{\circ}
at the boundary equilibrium
\bm{\hat{x}}
says that its population cannot grow in the absence of any reproducing individuals of the
i
th
species. The assumption on the spectral radius
\rho(\bm{{J}^{\bullet}})
corresponds to a locally stable interior equilibrium
\bm{x}^{\bullet}
for the
p
species with non-zero equilibrium abundances in the absence of the
r
species with zero equilibrium abundances,
\bm{x}^{\circ}
.

3.
Multispecies Competition with Delayed Maturation

For an important specific example of Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) with
\bm{\chi}(t,\bm{\delta})=\bm{x}(t)
, consider the per capita density dependent growth function defined by

(8)

G_{i}\left(\bm{x}(t-\delta_{i})\right)=\frac{{\lambda}_{i}}{1+q_{i}^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}x_{k}(t-\delta_{i})},\quad i\in\mathcal{M},

where
x_{k}(t-\delta_{i})=\chi_{k}(t-\delta_{i},\delta_{k})
and
\bm{A}\geq 0
is an
m\times m
matrix with nonnegative entries. The positive parameter
q_{i}>0
sets the equilibrium level of species
i\in\mathcal{M}
. The matrix
\bm{A}
defines the intraspecific or interspecific competitive interactions that intensify with greater numbers of reproducing adults both within and among species. By construction, a positive entry
A_{ij}>0
indicates a negative effect of species
j
on species
i
. Each entry of the nonnegative matrix
\bm{A}\geq 0
defines the strength of competitive density dependence exerted by species
j
after duration
\delta_{i}
in the immature life history stage of species
i
. The off-diagonal entry
A_{ij}
defines the strength of density dependence of species
j
on species
i
relative to the intraspecific density dependence,
A_{ii}
, on the
i
th
species.

The general delayed model with structure defined by Eqs. (
1
), (
2
) and (
8
) and
m\geq 1
is for convenience referred to here as the Competitive Delayed Multispecies (CDM) model. The nondelayed version of Eq. (
2
) with
\bm{\sigma}=0
and per capita growth function defined by Eq. (
8
) is a generalisation of models that have a long history in mathematical biology. For
m=1
, it is the influential Beverton-Holt model
(
Beverton and
Holt 1957
)
. For
m=2
, it is the Leslie-Gower model
(
Leslie and
Gower 1958
)
. For
m>1
, the non-delayed model is known as the competitive multispecies Leslie-Gower model
(
Sacker 2011
;
Jiang and
Niu 2017
)
. A single species delayed version appears in
Fisher and
Goh 1984
and a multispecies competitive system with common delay
\delta_{i}=\delta
for all
i\in\mathcal{M}
appears in
Hosack
et al. 2023
.

Remark 1
.

Without loss of generality, the specification
A_{ii}=1
for
i\in\mathcal{M}
in the CDM model defines
q_{i}^{-1}
as the strength of intraspecific density dependence in the denominator of Eq. (
8
).

In Section
3.1
conditions are found for a feasible interior equilibrium in the CDM model. The Jacobian matrix is evaluated at this coexistence equilibrium (Section
3.2
). The entries depend on the relative abundance of the equilibrium abundances, which can be used to advantage for the numerical calculation of local stability across the entire feasible region of the coexistence equilibrium (Section
3.3
). In Section
3.4
, the sufficient condition for local stability of the coexistence equilibrium is derived such that intraspecific competition is required to be greater than interspecific competition. The stability of the trivial and boundary equilibria is explored in Section
3.5
.

3.1.
Coexistence Equilibrium

The positive equilibrium with species-specific delays is derived. Let the
m
-dimensional vector
\bm{x}(t)
denote the reproducing adult abundances for the set of species
\mathcal{M}
at time
t
, such that
x_{i}(t)=\chi_{i}(t,\delta_{i})
for
i\in\mathcal{M}
. Let
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
denote the interior equilibrium abundances of the reproducing adult age classes, and define the density independent rate of adult recruitment of species
i
by
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a)
. If
\delta_{i}=0
then
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
(Section
2
). If the positive equilibrium exists, then Eqs. (
2
) and (
8
) obtain

(9)

x_{i}^{\ast}=\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})x_{i}^{\ast}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}x_{i}^{\ast}}{1+q_{i}^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}x_{k}^{\ast}},\quad i\in\mathcal{M}.

Using the daily probability of mortality
z_{i}=1-\sigma_{i}(\delta_{i})
and rearranging terms gives

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ij}x_{j}^{\ast}=\left(\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}{z_{i}}-1\right)q_{i}
for all
i\in\mathcal{M}
,
or equivalently

\bm{A}\bm{x}^{\ast}=\textrm{diag}[\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}-\bm{z}]\textrm{diag}[\bm{z}]^{-1}\bm{q}
,
where
\textrm{diag}[\bm{v}]
is the
d\times d
diagonal matrix with main diagonal entries corresponding to the entries of a
d
-dimensional vector
\bm{v}
and zeroes in the off-diagonal entries. For nonsingular
\bm{A}
, the equilibrium abundances are

(10)

\bm{x}^{\ast}=\bm{A}^{-1}\textrm{diag}\left[\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}-\bm{z}\right]\textrm{diag}[\bm{z}]^{-1}\bm{q}.

A feasible interior equilibrium of Eq. (
9
) for an arbitrary number of species
m\geq 1
corresponds to a positive equilibrium
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
that is unique by Eq. (
10
).

Often the density independent parameters
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}
and
\bm{z}
that relate to life history parameters within a species have comparatively more information available compared to the more uncertain density dependent parameters
\bm{A}
and
\bm{q}
that govern relationships among species. What can be generally said about the feasibility of the coexistence equilibrium in terms of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{q}
? The following proposition sets sufficient conditions on
\bm{A}
such that the interior equilibrium is feasible if
\bm{q}>\bm{0}
are free parameters.

Lemma 2
.

If the feasible interior equilibrium exists,
\bm{x}^{\ast}>\bm{0}
, given
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}>\bm{z}>\bm{0}
and nonsingular
\bm{A}
with all positive entries on the main diagonal, then
\bm{q}>\bm{0}
exists.

Proof.

The interior equilibrium is unique by Eq. (
10
), and so it suffices to show that the solution for
\bm{q}=\textrm{diag}[\bm{z}]\textrm{diag}\left[\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}-\bm{z}\right]^{-1}\bm{A}\bm{x}^{\ast}
is positive given
\bm{A}
,
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}
,
\bm{z}
and positive
\bm{x}^{\ast}>\bm{0}
. The
m\times m
matrix
\textrm{diag}[\bm{z}]\textrm{diag}\left[\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}-\bm{z}\right]^{-1}
is diagonal with positive entries along the main diagonal, and so
\bm{q}>\bm{0}
if
\bm{A}\bm{x}^{\ast}>\bm{0}
. If
A_{ii}>0
for all
i\in\mathcal{M}
then
\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{ik}x_{k}^{\ast}\geq A_{ii}x_{i}^{\ast}>0
for all
i\in\mathcal{M}
since
\bm{x}^{\ast}>\bm{0}
and
\bm{A}\geq 0
.
∎

The condition
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}>\bm{z}
corresponds to positive population growth rates in the absence of competition, and the condition
\bm{z}>\bm{0}
corresponds to non-negligible density independent mortality of mature adults. The condition of all positive main diagonal entries of
\bm{A}
corresponds to the presence of intraspecific competition for all species. Lemma
2
then guarantees that a positive
\bm{q}
exists for any feasible interior equilibrium
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
.

3.3.
Numerical Evaluation of Stability for All Coexistence Equilibria

The Jacobian matrix evaluated at the feasible interior equilibrium by Eq. (
14
) shows that the typically unknown vector
\bm{q}
is accounted for in the stability analysis given knowledge of
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
. Moreover, the feasible equilibrium abundances appear as ratios in Eq. (
14
). To explore the entire feasible space of the Jacobian evaluated at any interior equilibrium
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
, define
x_{T}^{\ast}=\sum_{k}x_{k}^{\ast}
and thus the relative abundance of the
k
th
species by
p_{k}=x_{k}^{\ast}/x_{T}^{\ast}>0
, that is,
x_{k}^{\ast}=p_{k}x_{T}^{\ast}
with
\sum_{k}p_{k}=1
, such that

{A_{ij}x_{j}^{\ast}}/{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}x_{k}^{\ast}}={A_{ij}p_{j}}/{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}p_{k}}

for all
i,j\in\mathcal{M}
in Eq. (
14
). Numerical evaluation of the eigenvalues of
\bm{J}^{\ast}
for all possible interior equilibria is then implemented through exploration of the simplex
\{p_{i}:i\in\mathcal{M},\,p_{i}>0,\,\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}p_{k}=1\}
as the feasible region given only parameters
\bm{A}
,
\bm{{\lambda}}
and
\bm{\sigma}
. The condition that the moduli of all eigenvalues of
\bm{J}^{\ast}
are less than one can then be numerically checked to assess local asymptotic stability of any
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
.

3.4.
Sufficient Condition for Stability of Coexistence Equilibrium

Proposition 3
.

Assume that
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}>\bm{z}>\bm{0}
. If

A_{ii}>\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}\backslash i}A_{ij},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{M},

then any interior equilibrium
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
of the CDM model is feasible with an associated
\bm{q}>0
, and the interior equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable.

Proof.

The constraint given the nonnegativity of
\bm{A}
ensures that the main diagonal entries of
\bm{A}
are positive, and so if the constraint holds then it is also true that
\bm{A}
is diagonally dominant such that
|A_{ii}|>\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}\backslash i}|A_{ij}|
for all
i\in\mathcal{M}
. The latter constraint is a known result that ensures
\bm{A}
is invertible
(see
Horn and
Johnson 1985
, Corollary 5.6.17)
. Lemma
2
then applies, and so also Eqs. (
10
) and (
14
) hold for
\bm{q}>0
corresponding to
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
. Application of Proposition
1
to the CDM model by Eqs. (
11
) and (
14
) shows that the interior equilibrium
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
is locally asymptotically stable if

(15)

z_{i}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}\left(\left|1-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}-z_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}\frac{A_{ii}x_{i}^{\ast}}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}x_{i}^{\ast}}\right|+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}\backslash i}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}-z_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}\frac{A_{ij}x_{i}^{\ast}}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}x^{\ast}_{k}}\right)\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a)<z_{i},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{M}.

Given constraints
\bm{A}\geq 0
,
0<z_{i}<1
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}-z_{i}>0
, the difference within the absolute value in the above equation is always positive because
0<\tilde{\lambda}_{i}-z_{i}<\tilde{\lambda}_{i}
and
0<A_{ii}x_{i}^{\ast}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}x^{\ast}_{k}
. By the definition
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a)
, Eq. (
15
) is therefore equivalent to

(16)

\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}-z_{i}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}\times\frac{x_{i}^{\ast}}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}x^{\ast}_{k}}\left[-A_{ii}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}\backslash i}A_{ij}\right]<0,\quad i\in\mathcal{M},

where the terms outside of the brackets on the left hand side are positive.
∎

The constraint of Proposition
3
shows that if interspecific competition is not greater than intraspecific competition, then the coexistence equilibrium exists and is locally stable. The condition does not depend on the species-specific delays.

3.5.
Sufficient Condition for Stability of Alternative Equilibria

By the uniqueness of the interior equilibrium of Eq. (
10
), if it exists, the only other possible equilibria for the CDM model are the boundary equilibria and the trivial equilibrium.

Corollary 1
.

Assume that the condition of Proposition
3
applies to guarantee stability of the interior portion
\bm{x}^{\bullet}>\bm{0}
, of the equilibrium, if present, such that
\rho(\bm{{J}^{\bullet}})<1
. Then a boundary equilibrium
\bm{\hat{x}}=[\bm{x}^{\circ},\bm{x}^{\bullet}]^{\top}
, where
\bm{x}^{\circ}=0
, or trivial equilibrium, where
\bm{\hat{x}}=\bm{x}^{\circ}=0
, is locally asymptotically stable if

\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}-z_{i}}{z_{i}}<q_{i}^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{M}}A_{ik}\hat{x}_{k}=q_{i}^{-1}\sum_{k\in\{l:\hat{x}_{l}=x_{l}^{\bullet}\}}A_{ik}x_{k}^{\bullet},\quad\forall i\in\{j:\hat{x}_{j}=x_{j}^{\circ}=0\}.

Proof.

The result follows from Proposition
2
applied to the CDM model with Lemma
2
in force because Eq. (
8
) has the property that
\hat{x}_{i}=x_{i}^{\circ}=0\implies\hat{x}_{i}G_{i}(\bm{\hat{x}})=0
for
i\in\mathcal{M}
. If Proposition
3
is sufficient for the stability of
\bm{x}^{\ast}>0
given
\bm{A}
and
\bm{J}^{\ast}
, then it must also hold for
\bm{x}^{\bullet}
since the sufficient condition depends on
\bm{J}^{\bullet}
only through some principal submatrix of
\bm{A}
. Then the condition for local asymptotic stability from Proposition
2
is that
|G_{i}(\bm{\hat{x}})|\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a)<1
for all
i\in\{j:\hat{x}_{j}=x_{j}^{\circ}\}
, where
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda\prod_{a=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\sigma_{i}(a)
and
G_{i}
is defined by Eq. (
8
).
∎

Whereas the sufficient condition for the local stability of the interior equilibrium only depends on the equilibrium through the relative abundances of the species (Section
3.3
), the corresponding sufficient condition for the stability of the boundary equilibrium in the above corollary depends on the actual non-zero equilibrium abundances
\bm{x}^{\bullet}
of the established species. The boundary or trivial equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable if the fecundity rate at the conclusion of the immature life history phase is less than the probability of adult mortality.

4.
Estimation of Interspecific Competition

The above properties of the CDM model are now used to provide estimates for unknown density dependent parameters
\bm{q}
and
\bm{A}
. In Section
4.1
, a popular descriptive approach to estimate niche overlap is summarised. If coerced to define the entries of
\bm{A}
, this descriptive approach endows a special structure such that Lemma
2
applies, and so
\bm{q}>\bm{0}
is then also available. However, the niche overlap method is not strongly linked to a dynamic model framework. Therefore, Section
4.2
explores an alternative approach that estimates
\bm{A}
and
\bm{q}
based on the dynamic properties of the CDM model. The optimisation uses the spectral radius to determine the stability of the interior equilibrium in terms of the relative dampening or amplification of perturbations away from the equilibrium. This level of stability, which is maximised by minimising the spectral radius, is balanced against a maximisation of the average interspecific competition strength among species, which is destabilising by Proposition
3
. The idea is that i) observed species abundances are more likely to be observed near a more stable equilibrium than a less stable one, and ii) species seek to increase competitive advantage over shared resources. The approach requires only the relative abundances of the species rather than actual abundances that are more difficult to obtain
(
Sinka et al. 2016
)
. For example, models of habitat overlap and segregation among species sometimes require genetic methods to identify species from subsamples of field populations
(
Tene Fossog et al. 2015
)
. Such analyses often use relative abundance because the actual abundances of the species are unobserved. The descriptive and objective approaches are compared for an example in Section
4.3
.

4.1.
Relationship between CDM Model and Niche Overlap Indices

It has long been recognised that estimation of interspecific density dependent coefficients can be difficult
(
Beverton and
Holt 1957
)
. The Leslie-Gower model was an early attempt to provide a mechanistic model with estimable density dependence for a two-species extension of the Beverton-Holt model of density dependent population growth
(
Leslie and
Gower 1958
)
. Later, various measures of niche overlap indices were introduced into the ecological literature that were loosely based on the continuous Lotka-Volterra model
(
May 1975
)
.
Pianka 1974
, for example, proposes

\alpha_{ij}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}p_{ir}p_{jr}\left(\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}p_{ir}^{2}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}p_{jr}^{2}\right)^{-1/2}

as a measure of niche overlap between species
i
and
j
, where
p_{kr}
is the proportion of the resource
r
from the set of resources
\mathcal{R}
used by species
k
. The resulting
\alpha_{ij}
are proposed as the “canonical” estimator of interspecific competitive coefficients
(
May 1975
)
, where in a competitive community of
m
species, it is assumed that
A_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}
is a density dependent coefficient. The resulting
m\times m
interspecific competition matrix
\bm{A}
constructed by this method is symmetric and positive semidefinite
(
May 1975
)
.

The CDM model benefits in two ways from assuming
\bm{\alpha}=\bm{A}
. First, the main diagonal entries of
\bm{A}
equal one. The unknown
q_{i}
in the density dependent growth functions
G_{i}(\bm{x}(t-\delta_{i}))
of Eq. (
8
) are therefore interpreted as expressing intraspecific density dependence on the per capita scale with the
i
th
species. Second, the rank of a symmetric matrix corresponds to the number of its non-zero eigenvalues. Therefore, if the competitive relationships or niches among the species differ such that the rows (columns) of
\bm{A}
are linearly independent, then the matrix
\bm{A}
has full rank
m
and all positive eigenvalues. It would be reasonable for different species to interact with their environments differentially, and linear independence of rows (columns) of
\bm{A}
would typically be expected. The matrix
\bm{A}
is then nonsingular with intraspecific density dependence present for all species. The unique interior equilibrium then exists by Lemma
2
if also
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}>\bm{z}
. So the choice of parameterisation of
\bm{A}
by the Pianka index guarantees a feasible interior equilibrium for the CDM model.

Yet
Pianka 1974
explicitly cautions that
\bm{\alpha}
only measures “niche overlap”, not competition, because species can adopt strategies to decrease competitive interactions even as their niche overlap increases. Another criticism of assuming
\bm{\alpha}=\bm{A}
is that the coefficients
\bm{\alpha}
are typically based on observations of relative abundances among species, whereas the unobserved competitive coefficients
\bm{A}
are the real measure of interspecific and intraspecific density dependence on population growth rates. Thus, a field survey that shows strong positive correlation of relative abundances among species across sites may suggest strong competition because
\bm{\alpha}
has large off-diagonal entries, whereas in fact the species may exploit distinct non-overlapping microhabitats within each site. In this example, the competition matrix
\bm{A}
is a diagonal matrix that reflects the absence of interspecific competition so that clearly
\bm{\alpha}\neq\bm{A}
.

4.2.
Optimised Competition

The results available for the CDM model suggest an alternative approach to estimating the strength of competition.
Estimates for density independent adult mortality rates
\bm{z}
and per capita reproduction rates
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}
, as defined in the delayed maturity model, are generally available in the literature, but estimates of the density dependent parameters
\bm{q}
and
\bm{A}
are comparatively lacking. Estimates of the former parameters often depend on a mix of field observations or laboratory studies, and so are more attainable than the latter parameters that determine the magnitude of density dependence. Assume that an estimate of the adult abundances
\bm{\bar{x}}>0
is available for the interior equilibrium, so that
\bm{\bar{x}}\approx\bm{x}^{\ast}
. The estimate
\bm{\bar{x}}
may for example be derived from estimated mean adult abundances of the species. Here, an estimate of the competition matrix
\bm{A}
is derived based on the stability properties of the dynamic system. This ecologically and dynamically motivated estimate is available for the CDM model in the absence of estimates for
\bm{q}
. The optimisation approach can also be viewed as a hypothesis for how competing species might set competitive interaction strengths at a stable, and potentially observable, configuration.

The following interpretation uses an alternative interpretation of the spectral radius. This alternative conceptual approach uses the spectral radius to assess the amplification potential for perturbations away from equilibrium, and is conceptually based on the analogous construction for the reproduction number of nonnegative populations
(
Diekmann and
Heesterbeek 2000
)
. Let
\bm{\phi}
denote the vector of deviations for all age classes of all species from the interior equilibrium caused by a local perturbation. The magnitude of this deviation can be summarised by the vector norm
||\bm{\phi}||_{1}=\sum_{i}|\phi_{i}|
. The corresponding matrix norm for a
n\times n
square matrix
\bm{W}
is
||\bm{W}||_{1}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|W_{ij}|=\max_{\bm{\phi}\neq\bm{0}}||\bm{W}\bm{\phi}||_{1}/||\bm{\phi}||_{1}
that assesses the maximum amplification of the deviation from the interior equilibrium. The spectral radius of
\bm{W}
for a matrix norm
||\cdot||
is defined by
\rho(\bm{W})=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}||\bm{W}^{t}||^{1/t}
. Also,
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\bm{W}^{t}=0
if and only if
\rho(\bm{W})<1

(
Horn and
Johnson 1985
, Theorem 5.6.12)
. The spectral radius is therefore interpreted as a measure of the long term average amplification away from the interior equilibrium per unit time
t
following a perturbation.

For use in the objective function of an optimisation, note that the spectral radius is a continuous function of a matrix
\bm{W}
. Not only is the spectral radius related to the matrix norm through the long term average amplification, but any matrix norm also provides an upper bound on the spectral radius,
\rho(\bm{W})\leq||\bm{W}||
. The matrix norm and spectral radius are thus closely linked. The spectral radius for a stable system will also be bounded between zero and one.

Here, both the competition matrix
\bm{A}
and the long term average per unit time
t
multiplication number (amplification) of the CDM model is optimised for the observed
\bm{\bar{x}}
. The interior equilibrium of the CDM model, if it exists, is unique by Eq. (
10
). The joint optimisation maximises competition, or niche overlap if these terms are considered synonymous, while minimising the spectral radius
\rho(\bm{J}^{\ast})
that defines the long term average amplification of perturbations away from the interior equilibrium,
\bm{x}^{\ast}
. If
\rho(\bm{J}^{\ast})<1
then the deviations will over time decrease such that the adult abundances eventually return to
\bm{x}^{\ast}
. Otherwise, the deviations may continue to increase and the species abundances will not return to the interior equilibrium.

The entries of the Jacobian matrix for the CDM model depend only on the species equilibrium relative abundances and the given parameters
\bm{z}
and
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}
(Section
3.3
). Assume that the density independent fecundity and survival rates are given such that population growth will occur in the absence of competition, that is,
\bm{\tilde{\lambda}}>\bm{z}
. Let the Jacobian of the CDM model as a function of species abundances
\bm{x}
and competition matrix
\bm{A}
be denoted by
\bm{J}(\bm{x},\bm{A})
. For example,
\bm{J}^{\ast}=\bm{J}(\bm{x}^{\ast},\bm{A})
in Eq. (
6
) for the CDM model evaluated at the interior feasible equilibrium. By Eq. (
14
),
\bm{J}(\bm{x}^{\ast},\bm{A})=\bm{J}(\bm{p}^{\ast},\bm{A})
where
\bm{p}^{\star}=\bm{x}^{\star}/\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}x_{i}^{\star}
. The following minimisation problem is constructed:

\displaystyle\bm{\hat{A}}=\arg\min_{\bm{A}\in\bm{S}_{m}}[\rho(\bm{J}(\bm{\bar{x}},\bm{A}))-1]\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}A_{ij},

where
\bm{S}_{m}
is the set of
m\times m
candidate matrices for
\bm{A}
and
\bm{\bar{x}}
is the estimated equilibrium abundances.

The conjecture encapsulated by this objective function is that i) it will be favourable to species
i
if it can outcompete its competitors for limited resources and so increase
A_{ij}
,
i\neq j
, and ii) a stable equilibrium is more likely to be observed than one that is comparatively less stable. That is, a system with a larger spectral radius and hence larger average amplification of perturbations away from equilibrium will be less likely to be observed near the equilibrium than one with a small spectral radius, where perturbations are relatively attenuated. The objective function also allows multiplication by a constant without effect on the optimisation. For example, multiplication by
m^{-2}
allows interpretation of the summation in the objective function as a measure of the average competition strength defined by the mean of the entries of
\bm{A}
.

Note that the choice of objective function optimises both increased competition and increased stability of the equilibrium estimate
\bm{\bar{x}}
. The objective function does not simply seek to minimise the spectral radius at that point: The latter approach would be expected to favour diagonal
\bm{\hat{A}}
because decreasing interspecific competition is stabilising as seen in Proposition
3
. For large systems and non-symmetric or unconstrained
\bm{S}_{m}
the number of unknowns would increase at the rate
m^{2}
and so outpace the information provided by the
m
-dimensional vector
\bm{\bar{x}}
. It is conceivable then that the objective function may not have a single global optimum. In other words,
\bm{\hat{A}}
may not be identifiable. In such cases, it may be useful to include constraints on the candidate set of matrices
\bm{S}_{m}
as informed by prior knowledge or theoretical justification.

A low dimensional numerical example is provided below. In this example, the candidate set for optimisation includes only the nonnegative matrices with main diagonal entries equal to one with reference to Remark
1
. Lemma
2
then applies so that a positive
\bm{q}
is guaranteed for any feasible interior equilibrium given any choice of matrix
\bm{B}
in this candidate set. Furthermore, the canonical structure of
\bm{\alpha}
described in Section
4.1
is then adopted so that
\bm{S}_{m}
is the set of symmetric nonnegative matrices with main diagonal entries equal to one.

5.
Conclusion

Sufficient conditions are presented for the local asymptotic stability of feasible interior equilibria within general multispecies systems of difference equations with delayed maturity. Interspecific competition, mutualism, predation, and commensalism or amensalism are accommodated by the general model framework. The competitive delay multispecies (CDM) model is presented as an important specific example. A sufficient condition is provided for stability of coexistence that only requires stronger intraspecific competition relative to interspecific competition, regardless of delay. Moreover, evaluation of the simplex of relative abundances can exhaustively assess the stability properties of a system given only competition
\bm{A}
, fecundity
\bm{\lambda}
and survival probabilities
\bm{\sigma}
. In cases where competition
\bm{A}
is unknown, a technique is provided to predict an optimal set of density dependence coefficients. A numerical illustration is provided for a low dimensional system, but future research would be needed to evaluate its suitability for very large systems.

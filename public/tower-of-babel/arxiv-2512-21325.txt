Topological Charge- 2 ​ n ​ e 2ne Superconductors
Zhi-Qiang Gao, Yan-Qi Wang, Hui Yang, Congjun Wu

Abstract
Charge- 4 ​ e 4e superconductors are phases where quartets of electrons condense in the absence of Cooper pairing condensation. They exhibit distinctive signatures including fractional flux quantization and anomalous Josephson effects, and are actively being explored in strongly correlated systems, such as moiré materials. In this work we develop a general framework for topological charge- 2 ​ n ​ e 2ne superconductors based on both wavefunction and field theory approaches. In particular, we generate topological charge- 2 ​ n ​ e 2ne superconductors from charge- 2 ​ e 2e ingredients, and by breaking the charge U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry in certain classes of quantum Hall states. Via bulk-edge correspondence, we further construct the corresponding edge conformal field theory and bulk topological quantum field theory for topological charge- 2 ​ n ​ e 2ne superconductors that suggests fermionic nonabelian topological orders. Our results provide a unified low-energy description of the topological charge- 2 ​ n ​ e 2ne superconductivity, offer a concrete platform for studying symmetry breaking and enrichment in interacting topological phases of matter, and have direct implications for experimental probes such as quasiparticle interferometry.



I
Introduction

Superconductivity is usually understood as a macroscopic condensate of Cooper pairs, each carrying electric charge-
2e
. However, this is not the only way a superconducting state can form: The elementary condensed object can be a bound state of more than two electrons. The most striking example is the charge-
4e
superconductor, in which quartets of electrons condense while normal charge-
2e
Cooper pairs are absent. Here, the quartet indicates a bound state of four electrons, and serves as the order parameter of the system. The traditional mean-field treatment at quadratic level for these quartetting order parameters is less effective, and the numerical assessment becomes harder because of the breaking of charge conservation symmetry. Efforts have been made in investigating charge-
4e
superconductors. Condensation of four fermions singlet quartet and its competition with Cooper pairing has been investigated
SU(4)
symmetric fermionic systems
Wu 2005
, which has a recent generalization to moiré platforms
Khalaf et al. 2022
;
Zhang and Mao 2020
and doped singlet states
Zhang et al. 2025
. As a vestigial order, charge-
4e
superconductor has been studied in the pair density wave
Berg et al. 2009
and multicomponent superconducting systems
Fernandes and Fu 2021
;
Liu et al. 2023
;
Zeng et al. 2024
. Distinctive signatures, such as fractional flux quantization
Cohen et al. 2018
;
Ge et al. 2024
and anomalous Josephson effects
Huang et al. 2022
, have been reported experimentally.

Chiral topological superconductors can host nonabelian superconducting vortices, and these emergent excitations are widely regarded as promising building blocks for fault-tolerant quantum computation. Read and Green provided the paradigmatic example by showing that a two-dimensional
(p+ip)
-wave superconductor realizes a chiral topological phase with localized Majorana zero modes at vortices and edges
Read and Green 2000
. Building on this, band-theoretic approaches have formulated chiral topological superconductors in terms of topological invariants of the Bogoliubov-de Gennes mean-field Hamiltonian, establishing a general framework for the bulk-edge correspondence and its embedding into realistic materials
Qi and Zhang 2011
;
Nayak et al. 2008
;
Sato and Ando 2017
. Recent progress in Van der Waals materials, in particular rhombohedral tetralayer graphene, which combines the tunable band topology and strong electronic correlations, has provided a new and highly controllable platform for realizing chiral topological superconductivity
Han et al. 2025
;
Wang et al. 2024a
.

Topologically ordered states in two spatial dimensions are gapped phases that host anyons
Wen 2017
, whose long-wavelength effective theory are topological quantum field theories (TQFT). The chiral TQFTs we care about in this work are Chern-Simons (CS) theory. One central feature of TQFT is the bulk-edge correspondence
Wen et al. 1994
;
Wen 1995
;
Qi et al. 2006
;
Witten 2016
:
On an open manifold of two spatial dimension, the edge physics of the CS theory is a (1+1)D conformal field theory (CFT) whose primary fields are in one-to-one correspondence with bulk anyons, with exactly the same fusion rules and braiding statistics. In particular, when the microscopic system contains fermions, its appropriate description should be a spin TQFT, which is only well-defined once a choice of spin structure (heuristically understood as boundary condition for fermions) is specified on the spacetime manifold. Operationally, a spin TQFT contains a distinguished “transparent fermion” line with fermionic self-statistics that braids trivially with all other excitations, representing the physical electron. Many spin TQFTs can be constructed from bosonic TQFTs by a standard anyon condensation procedure
Burnell 2018
: One enlarges the bosonic theory by attaching the physical fermion to suitable anyons and then condenses such the composite bosonic bound state, thereby enforcing the transparent fermion as a genuine local excitation. The corresponding edge theory for a spin TQFT is a spin CFT, whose local operator content includes this transparent fermion.

IV
Discussion

Our results offer a unified low-energy description of topological charge-
2ne
superconductivity. By formulating these phases in terms of edge CFTs and spin TQFTs, we bridge ideas from fermionic symmetry-protected and symmetry-enriched topological orders, clustered quantum Hall states, and unconventional superconductivity. This perspective provides a controlled setting in which to study symmetry breaking
U(1)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2n}
within a fully topological framework.

Our work also has direct implications for experiments. The bulk TQFT and edge CFT descriptions imply characteristic signatures in quasiparticle interferometry and Josephson responses, which can distinguish topological charge-
2ne
superconductors from both conventional charge-
2e
topological superconductors and non-topological charge-
2ne
states. In particular, the fermionic nonabelian anyons identified here suggest concrete targets for interferometric probes and for designing geometries that are sensitive to the underlying
\mathbb{Z}_{2n}
structure.

There are several natural directions for future work. On the theoretical side, it would be interesting to construct microscopic lattice models that realize the spinful and spinless phases discussed here and to study their stability against disorder, interactions beyond mean-field, and finite-temperature effects. On the experimental side, applying our TQFT-based diagnostics to candidate platforms in moiré materials and strongly correlated systems, and formulating concrete interferometry and Josephson experiments tailored to charge-
4e
and more general charge-
2ne
condensates, are promising steps toward detecting and manipulating these exotic phases in the laboratory.

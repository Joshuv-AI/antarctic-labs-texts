Effects of acute rope skipping exercises of different exercise modes on cognitive function in 9–10-year-old children
Authors: Yunzhou Liu, XiaoYan Dong, Qiao He, YuHong Jia
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo explore the effects of acute high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise and acute moderate-intensity continuous rope skipping exercise on cognitive function in 9–10-year-old children, this study aims to inform the selection of appropriate jump rope methods for cognitive training in physical activities. A total of 60 students from two classes of fourth grade in a primary school were selected as research subjects. After matching according to gender, they were randomly divided into HIIT group, MICT group and control group. The Digit Span Test (DST), Attention Network Test (ANT) and Stroop Color Word Test were conducted three days before the intervention and 5 min after the intervention. During the intervention, the HIIT group performed a high-intensity intermittent rope skipping exercise (required fast jumping with feet together, practice for 1 min and rest for 1 min, jump continuously for 5 groups, and the exercise intensity is ≥ 85% of the maximum heart rate), while the MICT group performed a moderate-intensity continuous rope skipping exercise (required to medium jumping with feet together, jump continuously for 5 min, and the exercise intensity is 60–80% of the maximum heart rate), and the control group rests in place. After the intervention, both HIIT and MICT groups showed increased DST total scores, improved executive control network efficiency, and faster inhibitory control response speeds, with no significant changes in accuracy. The HIIT group also exhibited increased orienting network efficiency and no significant changes in accuracy. Acute rope skipping exercises using HIIT and MICT methods can improve the working memory, executive control network efficiency and inhibitory control ability of children aged 9–10 years old. Introduction

Evidence suggests that a single bout of exercise, also known as acute exercise, has immediate effects on cognitive function1–3. An increasing number of studies aim to further understand how acute exercise influences cognitive performance4,5. Moderate-intensity continuous training (MICT) has become a preferred intervention method for researchers due to its appropriate exercise intensity, ease of control, and low negative impact4,5. High-intensity interval training (HIIT) has emerged as a popular exercise method in recent years. HIIT is typically defined as short-duration, interval vigorous exercise (intensity reaching 85–95% of maximum heart rate), which provides intense stimulation to the cardiovascular and muscular systems due to its short duration and active recovery periods6. Some studies suggest that the physiological and biochemical improvements induced by HIIT are similar or superior to those induced by MICT, and its shorter duration makes it more appealing and sustainable for exercisers7. From the perspective of public health and fitness, HIIT is considered a time-efficient exercise strategy8. Currently, research on HIIT primarily focuses on young adults, with ample physiological evidence supporting its effectiveness9. However, studies on its psychological benefits are relatively scarce and yield inconsistent conclusions, with even fewer studies focusing on school-age children.

Working memory is central to cognitive function and plays a crucial role in children’s learning, planning, reasoning, problem-solving, and intellectual activities. Some studies suggest that acute moderate-intensity aerobic exercise is beneficial for improving working memory in preadolescent children, with its neural basis possibly related to increased brain activity in the bilateral parietal cortex, left hippocampus, and bilateral cerebellum10. However, some researchers argue that acute aerobic exercise bouts lasting for 10, 20, or 30 min has no significant effect on working memory performance in adolescents11. Attention is a common psychological characteristic that accompanies processes such as perception, memory, thinking, and imagination. Research has shown that 12 min of acute aerobic exercise helps improve selective attention in both low-income and high-income children (d2 Attention test), with greater improvement observed in low-income children12. However, some researchers, using attention network tests to measure attention, found that acute aerobic exercise bouts with a duration of 10, 20, or 30 min had no significant effect on selective attention (alertness, orientation, or executive control) in adolescents, neither improving cognitive performance nor worsening it11. Executive function is considered one of the most important cognitive components, comprising higher-order cognitive processes that form the basis for adaptive and goal-directed behavior organization and control13. Meta-analyses have shown that moderate-intensity aerobic exercise has a “moderate” improvement effect on individuals’ cognitive function, with the improvement in executive function being particularly significant14. Only in recent years have researchers begun to focus on the cognitive benefits of HIIT, the meta-analysis shows that acute bouts of HIIT has a positive effect on inhibition in children/ adolescents and adults15, with the benefits sustain 30–60 min following intervention16, whereas it remains unclear if acute bouts of HIIT benefit working memory and cognitive flexibility performance.

Although the research findings are mixed, researchers have generally concluded that there is a small positive effect of a single bout of exercise on cognitive performance. A review of the narrative and meta-analysis shows that the effects of acute exercise on cognitive performance are generally small, exercise intensity, exercise duration, type of cognitive performance assessed, and participant fitness were significant moderators1. However, larger effects are possible for particular cognitive outcomes and when specific exercise parameters are used1. Exercise intensity is a moderator that has frequently been considered in acute exercise studies. The frequent attention given to exercise intensity is due to its relevance to understanding mechanisms of the effects. The drive theories and inverted-U hypothesis both suggest that exercise intensity will influence the size of the effect. In particular, the drive theories suggest that the largest effects will be observed at high intensity while the inverted-U hypothesis states that moderate intensity exercise will have the greatest benefits. Clearly when considering mechanisms such as heart rate, catecholamines, and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), exercise intensity is important for determining the amount of change in these physiological mechanisms that will be achieved and this may be important for predicting the behavioral effects as well. For example, studies testing the effects of acute exercise on circulating BDNF have shown that high-intensity exercise results in larger increases than low-intensity exercise17. Thus, if BDNF is a mediator of the effects of acute exercise on cognitive performance, intensity would be expected to influence behavioral outcomes. Currently, research on the cognitive benefits of HIIT are relatively scarce and the cognitive benefits differences between HIIT and MICT are lack of exploration, the level of assistance provided by different exercise modes to children’s cognitive function has not yet reached a widely accepted conclusion. Rope skipping is a simple, convenient, economical, and effective form of exercise that is widely popular and valued. It has become an important part of school physical education, 1-min rope skipping is not only an important indicator of elementary school students’ physical health evaluation, but also a physical examination item in most provinces and cities. However, how should one jump (method)? How can one achieve short-term efficiency and obtain optimal exercise effects? There is no consensus yet. In addition, research has shown that cognitively engaged physical exercise or physically coordinated exercise programs provided the most significant enhancement of inhibitory function in children18. While rope skipping is a whole body coordinated exercise, a child must allocate a high amount of attention and cognitive effort, thus, it may be more beneficial to improving performance on cognitive functions. Research has shown that moderate-intensity skipping can enhance short-term memory and selective attention in primary school students, as demonstrated in recent studies19,20. Additionally, studies have indicated that moderate-to-high-intensity skipping can significantly improve inhibitory control abilities in children aged 5–621. In contrast, the impact of HIIT on children’s cognitive functions is less understood, with a noticeable gap in the literature. Exercise intensity is recognized as a significant modulating factor in acute exercise studies, with the potential to influence behavioral outcomes. For example, a study by Alves et al. demonstrated that HIIT with a work-to-rest ratio of 2:1 did not lead to improvements in inhibitory functions22. Similarly, Bailey et al. found that acute HIIT had no significant impact on children’s working memory23. Given the scarcity of research on the effects of acute high-intensity interval skipping exercises on children’s cognitive functions and the lack of comparative studies with moderate-intensity continuous skipping exercises, there is a clear need for further research.

For these purposes, this study aims to investigate the cognitive benefits differences brought by two exercise modes by conducting acute rope skipping exercises using HIIT and MICT methods on primary school students. By comparing working memory, attention, and executive function before and after the intervention, this study seeks to explore the cognitive benefits differences between the two exercise modes, in order to provide a basis for selecting appropriate jump rope exercise methods, as well as intervention methods in physical activities for cognitive training. This research will not only help improve the physical and mental health of primary school students but also provide a scientifically feasible solution for promoting brain health through exercise, thus possessing strong theoretical significance and practical value. The hypothesis of this study is that both acute high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise and acute moderate-intensity continuous rope skipping exercise can improve cognitive performance in primary school students, with acute high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise providing greater cognitive benefits.

Methods

Subjects

We recruited a total of 60 school-aged children from two fourth-grade classes of a primary school including 36 boys and 24 girls, aged 9–10 years old. All subjects were physically healthy, intellectually normal, had normal vision or corrected-to-normal vision, no color weakness or color blindness, and could participate in physical activities both in and outside school with a score of ≥ 119 skips/min in a 1-min rope skipping test (Student Physical Health Test Rope Skipping Rating Level is Good or Above).

Ethical approval

The study was conducted according to the requirements of the Declaration of Helsinki (2013) and was approved and followed the guidelines stated by the Ethics Committee of China Institute of Sport Science (2023102601). Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects and their parents after giving them a complete description of the study.

Experimental design

A 3 (Group: HIIT, MICT, Control) × 2 (Test Time: pre-test, post-test) mixed design was employed. Group was the between-subjects variable, while Test Time was the within-subjects variable. The dependent variables included scores from the Digit Span Test, attention network efficiency and Stroop Color-Word Test reaction time (RT) and accuracy (ACC).

Experimental procedure

Subjects were matched according to gender and randomly divided into three groups: the HIIT group, MICT group, and the CON (control) group. Three days before the intervention and 5 min after the intervention, subjects underwent the Digit Span Test (DST), Attention Network Test (ANT), and Stroop Color-Word Test. During the intervention, the HIIT group performed one session of high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise (requiring fast jumping with feet together, 1 min exercise followed by 1 min rest, repeated for 5 sets, exercise intensity ≥ 85% of maximum heart rate), the MICT group performed one session of moderate-intensity continuous rope skipping exercise (requiring moderate-speed jumping with feet together for 5 min, exercise intensity 60–80% of maximum heart rate), and the Control group rested in place. The total exercise time was matched across all groups to ensure the validity of inter-group comparisons. During the intervention, telemetric heart rate measurement was conducted using a Polar Verity Sense armband from Finland, and researchers provided necessary encouragement to maintain motivation and ensure adherence to the exercise protocol, with a 5 min rest period after the intervention to allow heart rate to return to normal before post-testing. Subjects maintained their normal learning activities during the experiment, and test took place between 8:30 a.m. and 11:00 a.m. Subjects were instructed to avoid vigorous exercise and refrain from consuming caffeine or alcohol substances within 24 h prior to test.

Research tools

Digit span test

The Digit Span Test (DST) consists of forward Digit Span Test (DST-F) and backward Digit Span Test (DST-B), which primarily assess the ability of the subject to immediately and correctly reproduce the length of the longest digit sequence. It has high sensitivity and can accurately reflect differences in working memory before and after test or between different age groups. The Digit Span Test score includes forward digit span score, backward digit span score, and total score24.

The DST task was programmed using E-prime 2.0 and presented on a 14-inch display of a ThinkPad personal computer running Windows 11. During the test, a “+” sign appeared in the center of the screen for 500 ms, followed by the presentation of a random series of digits. Each digit was displayed for 1 s, and after all digits were presented, the subject was required to repeat the digits in either forward or backward order. Prior to test, subjects practiced until proficient. During test, the length of the digit sequences ranged from 3 to 15 digits, increasing sequentially. Each length of digit sequence was randomly presented three times, and test ceased when the subject failed to repeat the sequence correctly in two out of three trials.

Attention network test

In recent years, three attentional networks have been anatomically and functionally defined: alerting, orienting, and executive control. Figure 1 illustrates the Attention Network Test (ANT), which serves as a tool for evaluating the efficacy of the alerting, orienting, and executive control networks. It has been widely used as a behavioral test to analyze the performance of normal children, adults, and patients with mental disorders25,26. Numerous studies have used this test as a criterion for assessing attention networks, and empirical evidence has demonstrated the reliability of the ANT27.

ANT schematic.

Using E-prime 2.0, the stimulus task was programmed and presented on a 14-inch display of a ThinkPad personal computer running Windows 11. There are three cue conditions (no cue, center cue, spatial cue) and two target conditions (congruent, incongruent). The target stimulus consists of a row of five horizontal arrows pointing left or right. The target is a central arrow pointing either left or right, flanked by two arrows on each side, with either the same direction (congruent condition) or the opposite direction (incongruent condition). The task for the participant is to identify the direction of the central arrow by pressing the F key with the left index finger for leftward arrows and the J key with the right index finger for rightward arrows, while ignoring the flanker arrows, and recording reaction time and accuracy.

Each trial begins with a “+” sign appearing in the center of the screen for 400 ms. Then, the cue randomly appears for 100 ms, with no cue represented by a “+” in the center of the screen, center cue represented by a “*” in the center of the screen, and spatial cue represented by a “*” above or below the “+”. The center cue alerts the participant that the target is about to appear, while the spatial cue hints at the location of the upcoming target. Subsequently, only a “+” appears on the screen for 400 ms. The target and flankers then appear simultaneously until the participant responds. If no response is made within 1700 ms, the trial automatically advances to the next trial. If the reaction time is faster than 200 ms, it is automatically excluded by the program25,26. Before the test, subjects practice until proficient, followed by a 20-second rest period before the formal test. The formal test consists of 8 blocks, each containing 12 trials (with equal probabilities for each condition and random appearance), totaling 96 trials.

Comparing the performance under one condition with an appropriate reference condition, the operational definitions of the efficiencies of the three attentional networks for the behavioral analysis were defined so as to achieve positive scores in each case: Alerting effect = RTno cue − RTcenter cue; Orienting effect = RTcenter cue − RTspatial cue; Conflict (executive control) effect = RTincongruent − RTcongruent25,28.

Stroop color-word test

As depicted in Fig. 2, the Stroop Color-Word Test stands as a seminal experimental paradigm utilized for gauging the inhibitory facet of executive function, namely the Stroop effect. This test has been widely used to assess various processes and characteristics in the cognitive and personality domains, demonstrating high validity and reliability29.

Stroop color-word test schematic.

The Stroop task was programmed using E-prime 2.0 and presented on a 14-inch display of a ThinkPad personal computer running Windows 11. The Stroop task consists of four kinds of color words (red, yellow, blue, green) randomly presented on the screen in one of these four colors. Each trial begins with a “+” sign appearing in the center of the screen for 500 ms, followed by a 300-ms interval, and then a randomly presented experimental stimulus, where the word and the color of the word may be congruent or incongruent. The congruent condition includes four stimuli where the word matches the color it is presented in, such as the word “red” presented in red. The incongruent condition includes twelve stimuli where the word does not match the color it is presented in, such as the word “red” presented in green. Subjects are required to press the corresponding key quickly and accurately (labeled “D, F, J, K” for red, blue, green, yellow, respectively) based on the color of the word presented. The left middle finger presses the D key, the left index finger presses the F key, the right index finger presses the J key, and the right middle finger presses the K key. If no response is made within 2000 ms, the trial automatically advances to the next trial. If the reaction time is faster than 200 ms, it is automatically excluded by the program. Before the test, subjects practice until proficient, followed by a 20-s rest period before the formal test. The formal test consists of 8 blocks, each containing 12 trials, with each color word presented 8 times, totaling 96 trials. Reaction time and accuracy are recorded for each trial.

Statistical analysis

In this study, 2 subject were unable to complete the post-intervention due to illness, leaving a total of 58 subjects who completed the experiment as required.

The statistical analysis of the data was conducted using SPSS 26.0. For the repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA), the Shapiro–Wilk test was employed to assess normality of the data distribution. If violated, tests of sphericity were conducted. When epsilon (ε) < 0.75, the Greenhouse–Geisser correction was applied; when epsilon (ε) > 0.75, the Huynh–Feldt correction was utilized. Post-hoc comparisons were performed using the Bonferroni method. All data were presented as mean ± SD with a significance level set at α = 0.05.

Discussion

Skipping rope is a simple, accessible, cost-effective, and effective exercise method that is widely popular and valued. In this study, the HIIT group was instructed to single-foot jump rope rapidly for 1 min followed by 1 min of rest, repeated for 5 sets. During the practice, subjects’ heart rates consistently reached above 180 beats per minute (corresponding to exercise intensity ≥ 85% of maximum heart rate). The MICT group was instructed to single-foot jump rope at a moderate pace continuously for 5 min, with subjects’ heart rates maintained between 125 and 168 beats per minute (corresponding to exercise intensity of 60–80% of maximum heart rate). The results of the study showed that after the intervention, both the HIIT and MICT groups exhibited increased scores in the DST total score, decreased values in the Conflict effect, and increased response speed in Inhibition control. Additionally, the HIIT group showed increased values in the Orienting effect, while the accuracy remained unchanged.

The Digit Span Test (DST) primarily assesses the ability of subjects to immediately reproduce the length of the longest sequence of numbers correctly. It accurately reflects children’s working memory capacity and is not affected by uncontrollable objective factors. The results of this study demonstrated that the DST total scores of both the HIIT and MICT groups increased after the intervention, indicating that acute high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise and acute moderate-intensity continuous rope skipping exercise can enhance children’s working memory. The improvement in working memory after acute exercise may be attributed to enhanced cerebral blood flow and increased brain energy metabolism, leading to significant increases in brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and cortisol (CORT), thereby enhancing working memory capacity. Some previous studies support the conclusions of this study. One study showed that 30 min of high-intensity interval power cycling exercise could shorten reaction times in working memory tasks among college students30. Another study found that acute moderate-intensity aerobic power cycling exercise was beneficial for improving working memory in preadolescent children, and its neurobiological basis may be related to increased brain activity in the bilateral frontal cortex, left hippocampus, and bilateral cerebellum31. A previous study showed that children with higher fitness levels had larger bilateral hippocampal volumes, which were positively correlated with performance on memory tasks32, indicating that acute exercise is beneficial for children’s working memory at the macroscopic neurobiological level.

Posner et al. proposed the attention network model, which subdivides attentional networks into alerting, orienting, and executive control networks from both functional and anatomical perspectives33. These networks reflect an individual’s ability to acquire and maintain an alert state for a specific type of information or target, selectively attend to useful external information, and process inconsistent information33,34. The orienting network primarily reflects an individual’s ability to selectively attend to specific information from a large amount of input information. The Orienting effect = RTcenter cue − RTspatial cue, wherein spatial cues provide effective localization information under the premise of alertness. A greater difference indicates higher efficiency of the orienting network. The results of this study showed that after the intervention, the Orienting effect increased in the HIIT group while accuracy remained unchanged, indicating that acute high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise can improve children’s orienting network efficiency. Research using the d2 attention test found that 4 min of high-intensity interval exercise significantly improved children’s selective attention, consistent with the results of this study35. The lack of significant change in orienting network efficiency after acute moderate-intensity continuous rope skipping exercise may be related to the relatively lower exercise intensity. Compared to MICT, HIIT induces higher levels of physiological activation during exercise and maintains elevated levels of physiological activation and corresponding hormone levels for a longer period after exercise. Studies on the acute effects of exercise on circulating BDNF levels suggest that high-intensity exercise leads to greater increases than low-intensity exercise17. The executive control network primarily reflects an individual’s ability to process inconsistent information. When there is interference around the target (opposite to the target direction), the executive control network focuses attention on the target and minimizes or avoids the impact of interfering information36. Executive control (Conflict)effect = RTincongruent − RTcongruent, where the conflict condition increases the conflict process compared to the congruent condition. A smaller difference indicates shorter processing time for conflict and higher efficiency of executive control. The results of this study showed that after the intervention, the executive control network efficiency decreased in both the HIIT and MICT groups, while accuracy remained unchanged, indicating that acute high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise and acute moderate-intensity continuous rope skipping exercise can improve children’s executive control network efficiency. The improvement in cognitive function after acute exercise may be related to increased cerebral blood flow, and the cardiovascular hypothesis suggests that improvements in cardiovascular function (cardiac output, oxygen transport, and metabolism) can enhance neurotransmitter function and brain health37.

In the Stroop color-word test, the degree to which subjects are affected by the interference of word colors is used as an assessment index of inhibitory control ability. Inhibitory control plays a role in suppressing the processing of interfering information to help the brain exclude distractive information and make correct judgments. The results of this study showed that after the intervention, both the HIIT and MICT groups exhibited faster reaction times in inhibitory control, while accuracy remained unchanged, indicating that both high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise and moderate-intensity continuous rope skipping exercise can enhance children’s inhibitory control ability. Researchers have compared the effects of 30 min of basketball training at different intensities on elementary school students’ executive functions and found that both the moderate and high-intensity groups showed improved inhibitory control reaction times31, which is consistent with the findings of this study. The improvement in inhibitory control ability may be related to changes in BDNF concentration. BDNF is a growth factor that promotes neural plasticity, synapse growth, and transmission, and has been shown to enhance or improve cognitive function. Exercise can stimulate the release of higher levels of BDNF in healthy individuals2. An event-related functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) neuroimaging study demonstrated that acute high-intensity interval exercise can improve executive function, possibly due to increased activation in task-related areas of the prefrontal cortex, including the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex38. Some researchers also believe that both HIIT and MICT can improve inhibitory control ability, but HIIT has a longer duration39, mainly because HIIT induces higher levels of physiological activation during exercise, which can be sustained at a high level for a longer period after exercise. Since this study did not explore the duration of the after-effects of both exercise modalities, it cannot be determined which has a longer duration. Another study showed that high-intensity interval training can improve inhibitory function in adolescents, leading to positive changes in brain structure and function40. Adolescence is a sensitive period for significant changes in brain morphology and function, with rapid growth in brain volume and higher metabolic rates, making it easier to benefit from HIIT.

Meta-analytical research has shown that physically active exercises or motor coordination activities involving cognitive engagement have the most significant effect on enhancing inhibitory functions in children18. Previous studies have often employed running or sequential training (e.g., jumping jacks, shuttle runs, lateral jumps) for acute exercise interventions11,16. Jump rope, being a full-body coordination activity, requires children to invest substantial attention and cognitive effort during practice, making it potentially more effective in improving cognitive function performance. This study found that conducting acute rope skipping exercises using HIIT and MICT methods can improve working memory, executive control network efficiency, and inhibitory control ability in 9–10-year-old children. Additionally, conducting acute rope skipping exercises using HIIT method can also enhance their orienting network efficiency. It is evident that conducting rope skipping exercises using HIIT method can yield greater cognitive benefits. This research not only provides a basis for selecting appropriate rope skipping exercise methods but also contributes to improving the physical and mental health levels of school-aged children. Moreover, it facilitates wide-scale implementation and holds strong theoretical and practical significance.

Research limitations and prospects

Firstly, the exclusion of participants with poor rope skipping performance raises questions about the suitability and cognitive benefits of HIIT and MICT methods for those with fewer than 119 skips per minute. Secondly, the study’s focus on 9–10-year-olds leaves unclear the applicability and cognitive efficacy of these methods for younger children, such as 6–8-year-olds. Lastly, the comparative duration of after-effects from acute rope skipping exercises using HIIT versus MICT methods requires further research.

In future research, further exploration is needed on the long-term exercise benefits of both exercise modalities. By introducing control factors, such as measuring the children’s happiness levels during exercise tasks, the accuracy and sustainability of the results can be improved. Additionally, continuous refinement of exercise patterns based on students’ characteristics and athletic abilities is essential to achieve better exercise outcomes. Furthermore, leveraging cognitive neuroscience techniques can be attempted to deeply understand the neural mechanisms underlying cognitive function improvement, thereby providing scientific evidence for promoting brain health through exercise.

Conclusion

Acute rope skipping exercise, employing both HIIT and MICT methods, can enhance working memory, executive control network efficiency, and inhibitory control ability in 9–10-year-old children. In addition to improving these cognitive functions, acute high-intensity interval rope skipping exercise also enhances their orienting network efficiency.

Proper moduli spaces of orthosymplectic complexes
Chenjing Bu

Abstract
We apply the formalism of Alper–Halpern-Leistner–Heinloth [ 1 ] to construct proper good moduli spaces for moduli stacks of Bridgeland semistable orthosymplectic complexes on a complex smooth projective variety, which we propose as a candidate for compactifying moduli spaces of principal bundles for the orthogonal and symplectic groups. We also prove some results on good moduli spaces of fixed point stacks and mapping stacks from finite groupoids.

1 Introduction

1.1

Moduli spaces of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective variety
are natural compactifications for moduli spaces of vector bundles,
enabling the study of many flavours of sheaf-counting theories,
which are a major branch in enumerative geometry.

However, less is known about compactifications
of moduli spaces of principal
G
-bundles on a complex smooth projective variety,
for an arbitrary reductive group
G
.
This is not an issue for curves,
as moduli spaces of semistable principal bundles are already compact,
as shown by
[
18
,
19
]
.
But in dimension
\geqslant

2
,
one needs a good notion of singular principal bundles
to obtain compact moduli spaces.

An existing approach is that of
principal
\rho
-sheaves
,
as in
[
10
]

and
[
9
]
,
depending on a choice of a representation
\rho
of
G
.

In this work, we propose a different compactification
when
G=\mathrm{O}_{n}
or
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}
,
using
orthosymplectic complexes
,
which are complexes of coherent sheaves
that are isomorphic to their own derived duals.
The enumerative geometry of such complexes has already been
studied by the author
[
7
]

in the context of motivic Donaldson–Thomas theory.

1.2

Our main result,
Theorem

4.4
,
states that for a smooth projective
\mathbb{C}
-variety
X

and a Bridgeland stability condition
\tau
on
X
satisfying certain assumptions,
the moduli stack of
\tau
-semistable orthosymplectic complexes on
X

with a fixed Chern character has a proper good moduli space
in the sense of
[
2
]
.

This is a consequence of a more general result,
Theorem

3.1
,
which shows that if a stack has a proper good moduli space,
then the same holds for its fixed loci of finite group actions
and mapping stacks from finite groupoids into it.
The proof is essentially by combining several results of

[
1
]
.

Since the moduli stack of semistable orthosymplectic complexes
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-fixed locus in the moduli stack
of semistable complexes,
and the latter admits proper good moduli spaces by results in

[
1
]
,

Theorem

4.4
follows.

1.3

One case that might be of particular interest is when
X

is a K3 surface or an abelian surface.
In this case, the moduli stack of orthosymplectic complexes
admits a derived enhancements with a
0
-shifted symplectic structure in the sense of

[
15
]

(see
[
7
, §6.2]
),
and it follows that the proper good moduli space
admits a Poisson structure
which restricts to a symplectic structure on the stable locus.

It would be interesting to determine if these moduli spaces have
symplectic singularities in the sense of
[
4
]

(see
[
16
]
for the case of sheaves),
and if they admit symplectic resolutions,
which, in the case of sheaves,
are constructed in special cases by
[
14
]

and shown to not exist in other cases by

[
12
]
.
We expect that our moduli spaces are locally modelled on
symplectic reductions of moduli of representations of
self-dual quivers

in the sense of
[
8
]

and
[
7
, §6.1]
,
and that an analysis similar to
[
12
]

would help with answering these questions.

2.1
Fixed loci

Let
S
be an algebraic space,
and let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack over
S
.

If a finite group
\Gamma
acts on
\mathcal{X}
over

we define its

as the homotopy limit

of the corresponding functor from

to the
2

More explicitly, it is defined by the functor of points

\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}(T)=\mathcal{X}(T)^{\Gamma}
,
where
T
is an affine scheme with a morphism
T\to S
,
and
\mathcal{X}(T)^{\Gamma}
is the homotopy fixed locus
of the
\Gamma
-action on the groupoid
\mathcal{X}(T)
,
which is the groupoid of pairs
(x,\varphi)
,
where
x\in\mathcal{X}(T)
,
and
\varphi=(\varphi_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}

is a family of isomorphisms

\varphi_{g}\colon gx\mathrel{\mathchoice{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}}x

satisfying

and the cocycle condition

for all

.

See also
[
20
]
for related discussions.

2.2
Mapping stacks

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack over an algebraic space
S
,
and let
\Gamma
be a finite group.
The
mapping stack

{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})

is defined by the functor of points

{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})(T)=\mathrm{Map}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X}(T))

for an affine scheme
T
with a morphism

,
where the right-hand side is the mapping groupoid,
or more explicitly, the groupoid of pairs
(x,\rho)

with
x\in\mathcal{X}(T)

and
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\mathrm{Aut}(x)
a group homomorphism.
We will show in
Theorem

2.5

that this is an algebraic stack.

For example, for a finite group
\Gamma

and a flat group algebraic space

of finite type over

,
we have

{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathrm{B}G)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)/G
,
where
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)

is the algebraic space of group homomorphisms

\rho\colon\Gamma\to G
,
as a subspace in
G^{|\Gamma|}
cut out by the relations

\rho(1)=1
and
\rho(gh)=\rho(g)\rho(h)
for all
g,h\in\Gamma

(see also
[
6
, Lemma 2.8]
),
and

acts on
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
by conjugation.
This can be checked directly via the functor of points.

2.3
Fixed loci and mapping stacks

We explain the relation between fixed loci and mapping stacks introduced above.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group acting on
\mathcal{X}
over
S
.
By generalities in category theory,
the fixed locus
\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}

is equivalent to the stack of sections of the morphism

\mathcal{X}/\Gamma\to\mathrm{B}\Gamma
,
that is, we have

\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}\simeq{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X}/\Gamma)\underset{{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\,\mathrm{B}\Gamma)}{\times}\{\mathrm{id}\}\ .

(2.3.1)

The image of
\{\mathrm{id}\}\to{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathrm{B}\Gamma)

is isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{Z}(\Gamma)
,
where
\mathrm{Z}(\Gamma)
is the centre of
\Gamma
.
Thus, if we write

{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}^{\prime}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X}/\Gamma)\subset{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X}/\Gamma)

for the open and closed substack which is
the preimage of this copy of
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{Z}(\Gamma)
,
then

{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}^{\prime}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X}/\Gamma)\simeq\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}\times\mathrm{B}\mathrm{Z}(\Gamma)\ ,

(2.3.2)

since the left-hand side is a quotient

\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}/\mathrm{Z}(\Gamma)
,
but the action is trivial.

It turns out that
{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X}/\Gamma)

is sometimes more convenient to work with than
\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}
,
and we will often use properties of the former to deduce
those of the latter.

Example 2.4

We present a simple example demonstrating the above relations,
which also illustrates how higher coherence is involved
in group actions and fixed loci on stacks.

Let
S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}/2

by taking the inverse transpose matrix,
and use the conjugation by

In other words, regarding

the action is given by taking the dual bundle,

The fixed locus of this action is

\mathrm{BGL}_{n}^{\smash{\mathbb{Z}/2}}\simeq\begin{cases}\mathrm{BO}_{n}&\text{if $\varepsilon=1$,}\\[-2.15277pt]
\mathrm{BSp}_{n}&\text{if $\varepsilon=-1$ and $n$ is even,}\\[-2.15277pt]
\varnothing&\text{if $\varepsilon=-1$ and $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

On the other hand, we have

\mathrm{BGL}_{n}/(\mathbb{Z}/2)\simeq\mathrm{B}G
,
where
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\hookrightarrow G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2

is a group extension defined as follows:

and a generator

with the relations

Thus, we have

where

and

is the closed subscheme of elements of order

.
Since

for

is isomorphic to the space of

(anti-)
symmetric invertible matrices.
Under this identification,
the (right)

is given by

g\cdot h=g^{\mathrm{t}}hg

for

and an

symmetric matrix

When

and the stabilizer consists of

As

When

When

Theorem 2.5

Let
S
be an algebraic space,
and let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack over
S
.
Let
\Gamma
be a finite group,
and consider the forgetful morphism

p\colon{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}\ .

Then
p
is representable by algebraic spaces,
so
{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})
is an algebraic stack.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{X}
is quasi-separated,
then
p
is quasi-compact and quasi-separated;
if
\mathcal{X}
has affine diagonal over
S
,
then
p
is affine.

In particular,
for any
\Gamma
-action on
\mathcal{X}
over
S
,
the above also hold for the fixed locus
\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}

and its forgetful morphism
\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{X}
.

Let
T\to S
be a morphism from an affine scheme
T
.
For any morphism of affine schemes
T^{\prime}\to T
,
we may identify
{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})(T^{\prime})

with the groupoid of pairs
(x,\rho)
,
where
x\in\mathcal{X}(T^{\prime})

and
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\mathrm{Aut}(x)
is a group homomorphism.

Now let
x\in\mathcal{X}(T)
,
and let
J=T\times_{x,\,\mathcal{X},\,p}{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})
.
The above description implies that

J\simeq\mathrm{eq}\,\biggl(\ \prod_{g\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\}}{}\bigl(\,T\underset{x,\,\mathcal{X},\,x}{\times}T\,\bigr)\ \overset{u}{\underset{v}{\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\textstyle\longrightarrow\\[-2.58334pt]
\textstyle\longrightarrow\vskip-0.90417pt\end{subarray}}}}\ \prod_{g,h\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\}}{}\bigl(\,T\underset{x,\,\mathcal{X},\,x}{\times}T\,\bigr)\ \biggr)\ ,

where

, respectively,

.

,
then the source and target of

For the final statement,

Theorem 3.1

Let
S
be a noetherian algebraic space of characteristic zero,
and let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack of finite type with affine diagonal over
S
,
with a good moduli space
\mathcal{X}\to X
.

Then for any finite group
\Gamma
,
the stack
{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})

also has a good moduli space,
which is proper if
X

In particular, for any
\Gamma
-action on
\mathcal{X}
over
S
,
the same holds for the fixed locus
\mathcal{X}^{\Gamma}
.

By
Theorem

2.5
,

{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})

is also of finite type with affine diagonal over
S
,
and the morphism

{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})\to\mathcal{X}

is affine.

By
[
1

,

\mathcal{X}
is
\Theta
-reductive and S-complete,
and when
X
is proper,

\mathcal{X}
satisfies the valuative criterion for universal closedness.
By the same result, it is enough to show that

{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})

also satisfies the same properties.

It follows from

[
1
, Proposition 3.21 (1) and Proposition 3.44 (1)]

that
{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})

is also
\Theta
-reductive and S-complete,
proving that it has a good moduli space.
It remains to verify that when
\mathcal{X}

satisfies the valuative criterion for universal closedness,
so does
{\mathcal{M}\mspace{-3mu}\mathit{ap}\mspace{1mu}}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma,\mathcal{X})
.

More precisely, let
R
be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field

,
and let

-point.
We need to show that there is an extension of discrete valuation rings

,
such that

extends to an

-point,
where

.

,

and

as we proceed in the proof.

The groupoid

\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}(X)

by the scheme

\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}^{\Gamma}(X)\subset X\times\mathrm{GL}_{N}^{\Gamma}

parametrizing pairs
(x,\rho)
with
x\in X

and
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_{N}
a group homomorphism
such that
g(\gamma)\cdot x=x
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
;
here
\mathrm{GL}_{N}^{\Gamma}
denotes the product
of
|\Gamma|
copies of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
.
Then
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
acts on
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}^{\Gamma}(X)

by the given action on
X
and conjugation on
\rho
,
and
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}^{\Gamma}(X)
is projective
over
U{/\mspace{-5mu}/}\mathrm{GL}_{N}\times\mathrm{GL}_{N}^{\Gamma}
.
The stability condition on
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}^{\Gamma}(X)

is given by the pullback of a line bundle on
X
as in the original proof,
so the semistable locus is
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}^{\Gamma}(U)
,
and we obtain a proper morphism

\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}^{\Gamma}(U)\to U{/\mspace{-5mu}/}\mathrm{GL}_{N}\times\mathrm{GL}_{N}^{\Gamma}{/\mspace{-5mu}/}\mathrm{GL}_{N}
.
As in the original proof,
any morphism
\operatorname{Spec}R\to\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}^{\Gamma}(X)/\mathrm{GL}_{N}

can be modified to a morphism

\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}\to\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}^{\Gamma}(U)/\mathrm{GL}_{N}
.

The proof of
[
1
, Theorem 5.3 (1)]

can now be followed with the above modifications.
∎

4 Orthosymplectic complexes

In this section, we apply
Theorem

3.1

to construct proper moduli spaces for orthosymplectic complexes.
We first recall the notion of orthosymplectic complexes from

[
7
, §6.2]
.

4.1
Orthosymplectic complexes

Let
X
be a connected smooth projective
\mathbb{C}
-variety,
and we fix the data

(I,L,s,\varepsilon)
, where

I\colon X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}}X
is an involution,

L\to X
is a line bundle,

s
is an integer, and

\varepsilon\colon L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}}I^{*}(L)
is an isomorphism
such that
I^{*}(\varepsilon)\circ\varepsilon=\mathrm{id}_{L}
.

This data defines a
self-dual structure

on the dg-category
\mathsf{Perf}(X)

of perfect complexes on
X
,
that is a contravariant involution

\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{R}{\mathcal{H}\mspace{-5mu}\mathit{om}}(I^{*}(-),L)[s]\colon\mathsf{Perf}(X)\mathrel{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{Perf}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\ ,

with the identification

\mathbb{D}^{2}\simeq\mathrm{id}
induced by the isomorphism
\varepsilon
.

A
self-dual complex
is defined as a fixed point
of the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-action on the underlying groupoid
\mathsf{Perf}(X)^{\simeq}
.
Explicitly, it is the data of a complex
E\in\mathsf{Perf}(X)
,
an isomorphism
\varphi\colon E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}}\mathbb{D}(E)
,
an equivalence
\varphi\mathrel{\mathchoice{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}}\mathbb{D}(\varphi)
,
and higher coherence data.
If
E
lies in a heart of some t-structure on
\mathsf{Perf}(X)
,
then the data
(E,\varphi)
is sufficient,
with no need for higher coherence.

For example, a natural choice is to take

I=\mathrm{id}_{X}
,
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
s=0
, and

\varepsilon=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}

(resp.
-\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}
).
In this case, orthogonal (resp. symplectic) vector bundles on
X

are self-dual complexes.
For this reason, we call the self-dual complexes

orthogonal
(resp.
symplectic
)
complexes

in this case.

4.2
Stability conditions

Let
(Z,\mathcal{P})
be a Bridgeland stability condition on
X
,
as in
[
5
]
,
where
Z\colon K(X)\to\mathbb{C}
is the central charge,
where we set
K(X)\subset\mathrm{H}^{2\bullet}(X;\mathbb{Q})

to be the subset consisting of Chern characters of perfect complexes,
and
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

is a slicing of
\mathsf{Perf}(X)
.

The
dual stability condition

(Z^{\vee},\mathcal{P}^{\vee})

of
(Z,\mathcal{P})
is defined by

Z^{\vee}(\alpha)=\overline{Z(\mathbb{D}\alpha)}

for all
\alpha\in K(X)
,
where the bar denotes complex conjugation,
and
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(t)=\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{P}(-t))

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

For the stability condition to be applied to self-dual complexes,
we require it to be
self-dual
,
that is, we require that

(Z,\mathcal{P})=(Z^{\vee},\mathcal{P}^{\vee})
.

Furthermore, to ensure good behaviour of moduli stacks,
we require the stability condition
(Z,\mathcal{P})

to have
rational central charge
,
that is,
Z(K(X))\subset\mathbb{Q}+\mathrm{i}\mathbb{Q}
,
and to satisfy the
generic flatness
and
boundedness
properties,
as in
[
17
, §4]
or

[
11
, Definitions 6.2.4 and 6.5.2]
.
See
[
7
, Example 6.2.5]

for examples of such stability conditions
when
X
is either a curve, a surface,
or a threefold satisfying the Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality of

[
3
]
.

4.3
Moduli stacks

Given a self-dual stability condition
\tau=(Z,\mathcal{P})
as above,
the dual functor
\mathbb{D}
preserves
semistable objects of slope
0
,
and the moduli stack
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}(0;\tau)

of semistable objects of slope
0
(including the zero object)
admits an induced
\mathbb{Z}/2
-action.
The fixed locus

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{sd,ss}}(\tau)=\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}(0;\tau)^{\mathbb{Z}/2}

is the moduli of semistable self-dual complexes.

By
[
1
, Lemma 7.20, Examples 7.26 and 7.29]
,
the stack
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}(0;\tau)
has affine diagonal,
and for every Chern character
\alpha\in K(X)
with
Z(\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
the open and closed substack

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\alpha}(0;\tau)\subset\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}(0;\tau)

parametrizing semistable objects with Chern character
\alpha
and slope
0

has a proper good moduli space.
Therefore, applying
Theorem

3.1
, we obtain the following:

Theorem 4.4

Let
X
be a connected smooth projective
\mathbb{C}
-variety,
and fix the data
(I,L,s,\varepsilon)
as above.
Let
\tau
be a self-dual Bridgeland stability condition on
X

with rational central charge
and satisfying the generic flatness and boundedness properties.

Then for every Chern character
\alpha\in K(X)

with
\alpha=\mathbb{D}(\alpha)
and
Z(\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
the open and closed substack

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{sd,ss}}_{\alpha}(\tau)\subset\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{sd,ss}}(\tau)

parametrizing semistable self-dual complexes with Chern character
\alpha

has a proper good moduli space.
In particular, every connected component of

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{sd,ss}}(\tau)

has a proper good moduli space.
∎

Remark 4.5

As in
[
1
, Example 7.29]
,
the theorem also holds for any self-dual Bridgeland stability condition
\tau^{\prime}

that is in the same connected component in the space of
(not necessarily self-dual) stability conditions
as one that satisfies the above properties,
because for each fixed class
\alpha\in K(X)
,
one can always find a stability
\tau
satisfying the above properties
that is close enough to
\tau^{\prime}
and defines the same semistable locus for
\alpha
.

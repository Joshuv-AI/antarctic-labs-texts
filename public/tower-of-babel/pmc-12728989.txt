Effect of hyperbaric oxygen therapy on early features of carbon monoxide poisoning focusing on neurological symptoms
Authors: Sujeong Shin, Jongmin Woo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCarbon monoxide (CO) intoxication due to suicide attempts or accidental exposure is a critical problem in Korea. Patients with CO intoxication can experience various neurologic and systemic symptoms. Although some of these symptoms may result in severe sequelae, the pathophysiological mechanisms remain unclear. Hyperbaric oxygen (HBO) therapy is the most important treatment for CO poisoning. However, standardized protocols remain unclear, and most related studies focus on the association of HBO therapy with the development of sequelae. Therefore, we intended to conduct studies focusing on the early features of CO intoxication.MethodsA retrospective descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted based on an analysis of medical records for patients with CO intoxication treated with hyperbaric oxygenation from January 2020 to March 2023. We excluded patients with a misdiagnosis, duplicate registration, or refusal of treatment. Patients were divided into two groups on the basis of their mental status at the time of presentation, and their demographic, physiological, and environmental characteristics were compared. Chi-square tests and t tests were performed, and 95% confidence intervals were calculated.ResultsOut of 525 patients identified with CO exposure, 288 patients were included in this study. A total of 186 (64.6%) patients were male, and most were in their mid-forties. Alert patients appeared to visit the hospital earlier compared with other patients (431.82 ± 975.20 vs. 151.78 ± 229.96, p value < 0.001). The proportion of suicide attempts was greater in the nonalert group (94 (56%) vs. 101 (84.2%), p value < 0.001). The frequencies of myocardial damage, shock, and ventilator treatment were 51 (42.5%), 6 (5%), and 13 (10.8%), respectively, and were greater in the unconscious group. Most symptoms usually improved almost completely at the early stage of the HBO therapy protocol.ConclusionsIn general, patients who are not alert at the time of presentation receive more intensive HBO therapy. According to our study, most intoxication symptoms improve during the early phase of HBO therapy, and delayed neuropsychiatric sequalae can occur in the future, even in initially alert patients. Therefore, physicians should be more careful with patients with neurological symptoms, evidence of myocardial damage, or severely elevated creatine phosphokinase levels, and these patients should be fully administered HBO therapy.Keywords: Carbon monoxide, Complications, Creatine phosphokinase (CPK), Hyperbaric oxygenation, Poisoning

Introduction

Carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning is a major public health issue and remains among the most common causes of both accidental and intentional toxic exposure. In South Korea, its relevance is particularly critical, as CO inhalation has become a prevalent method of suicide, especially among younger people, with the incidence steadily increasing over the past decade [1]. Beyond intentional exposure, unintentional cases can occur through chronic low-level exposure or acute high-level exposure in domestic and occupational settings [2].

The clinical manifestations of CO intoxication extend beyond acute hypoxic injury. Survivors frequently experience neuropsychiatric sequelae such as cognitive impairment, anxiety, depression, persistent headache, dizziness, vestibular dysfunction, and even Parkinsonism-like syndromes. Cardiovascular complications and long-term psychiatric morbidity, including personality changes, have also been described [3, 4]. In a rodent study related to carbon monoxide exposure, microglial cell damage was confirmed, especially in the hippocampus and the cerebellum [5]. In terms of pathophysiology, the formation of myelin basic protein and extensive myelin and neuronal loss have been reported as causative mechanisms; however, the pathogenesis remains poorly understood [6].

Hyperbaric oxygen (HBO) therapy has been attempted since the 19th century to treat carbon monoxide poisoning, but it was first reported to be effective by several researchers in the early 20th century [7, 8]. By delivering oxygen at supraphysiologic pressures, HBO rapidly reverses tissue hypoxia and accelerates the dissociation of CO from haemoglobin. Clinical guidelines generally recommend HBO in cases of severe intoxication, yet controversy persists regarding its efficacy, especially in preventing delayed neuropsychiatric sequelae (DNS). Systematic reviews and randomized controlled trials have yielded conflicting findings, and no universally accepted treatment protocol exists [9, 10].

Most existing research has emphasized the role of HBO therapy in reducing DNS. However, less is known about its effectiveness in promoting early recovery from acute CO-related symptoms, particularly in relation to patients’ initial neurological presentation, physiological parameters, and environmental circumstances. Clarifying these relationships could provide strong evidence for clinical decision-making.

Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of HBO therapy in the recovery of acute neurological and physiological symptoms following CO intoxication while also examining the associations among patients’ initial mental status, other clinical characteristics, and the subsequent development of DNS.

Methods

Study population/study design

This retrospective descriptive study enrolled patients with carbon monoxide intoxication who visited a tertiary emergency medical centre in Daegu city from January 2020 to March 2023. This institution is one of few regional emergency centres inside a university hospital in the area that perform hyperbaric oxygen therapy, and CO intoxication patients are transferred from nearby areas and visit directly via ambulance. The following HBO therapy protocol is used in our hospital. Given a history of CO gas exposure, HBO therapy is administered at least five times (2.5 ATA for 2 h/cycle) if the serum carboxyhaemoglobin concentration exceeds the normal range (over 3% in nonsmokers and 10% in smokers) or even when the level is normal but signs of target organ damage (i.e., cardiac enzyme elevation or neurologic deficit) are present. Given that our institution uses a single-person hyperbaric oxygen chamber, patients requiring mechanical ventilation may have experienced significant delays in initiating HBO therapy or may not have received it at all and thus could have been excluded from the analysis.

Participants and data collection

A total of 525 patients were diagnosed with carbon monoxide intoxication during the study period; these patients were aged 15 years and older. Fifty-eight patients whose final diagnosis was suspected drug intoxication rather than CO intoxication or involved a duplicate registration were excluded on the basis of their medical history. We also excluded 179 cases in which HBO was not indicated due to simple gas inhalation, patient refusal, or incomplete medical records.

In some cases, the same person visited several times, but repeated attempts were counted as separate cases. In addition, cases involving voluntary visits for HBO therapy were excluded from the analysis. For patients with repeated visits due to suspected DNS caused by previous carbon monoxide poisoning, only the first visit was included in the analysis. When patients experienced new onset of neurological or psychiatric symptoms, such as atypical headache, difficulty concentrating, lethargy, emotional lability, memory impairment, psychosis, parkinsonism, chorea, apraxia, agnosia, peripheral neuropathy, and urinary incontinence after recovery from initial complaints, those symptoms were considered consistent with DNS [11–13]. For the analysis, we included patients who either revisited the emergency department with the relevant symptoms or were subsequently diagnosed during follow-up visits to the Rehabilitation Medicine or Neurology outpatient clinics of our hospital after discharge.

The data concerning general characteristics included sex, age, visit time, Glasgow Coma scale (GCS) score, accompanying symptoms when visiting the hospital, underlying diseases, preoxygen treatment before visiting the hospital, causative mode for exposure, place of exposure, coingestion materials, suicide attempt status, time from exposure to visit, time from exposure to first HBO therapy, number of HBO therapies, shock or ventilator treatments during admission, and initial vital signs, including blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, body temperature, and peripheral oxygen saturation. Initial laboratory data, including haemoglobin, white blood cell count, carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb), bicarbonate ion, lactate, creatinine, and creatine phosphokinase (CPK) levels, were also collected.

Data control

Patients who received HBO therapy for carbon monoxide poisoning showed significant differences in various factors depending on whether they were alert or not at the time of presentation at the emergency room. Based on the GCS, patients with a score of 15 were included in the alert group, whereas those with a score less than 15 were included in the nonalert group. The differences between the two groups were compared. We compared the groups based on initial mental status because the initial mental status itself is evidence of target organ damage, and severe unconsciousness affects the treatment process, especially when a single chamber is used.

To analyse the effect of prehospital oxygen treatment on the patient’s level of consciousness, oxygen administration was only considered when 99% oxygen was provided with a reservoir bag mask. In the case of transferred patients, if HBO therapy was performed at the previous hospital, they were considered to have received oxygen treatment, and the previous HBO therapy was excluded from the total number of HBO therapies.

With respect to time, the exact moment when exposure began was unknown, and the last time when the patients was observed to be behaving normally was taken as the starting point. The time from exposure to visit was defined as the gap between the symptom onset time and the arrival time. In transferred cases, given that the transfer process could affect the time until the first treatment, the route of visit was also classified separately.

Patients were also classified according to the substances that caused CO exposure, and the patients were also classified and analysed according to the exposure location. The location was also integrated into multivariate analysis. We defined relatively narrow places such as the bathroom, private bedroom, inside a car or camping tent as confined spaces for the analysis of exposure place.

We tried to determine the factors that influenced the initial state of consciousness by analysing simultaneous exposure to drugs or alcohol and analysed whether the cause of exposure was suicide or accidental. Initial vital signs and laboratory test results, including COHb levels, were collected. Accompanying symptoms and target organ damage at the time of visit were identified and analysed, such as elevated myocardial enzymes (troponin I above the normal reference range of 0.0–0.04 ng/mL), shock during treatment, and the use of a ventilator. The final state of consciousness and the occurrence of DNS as a result of treatment were analysed. We compared several poisoning-related indicators according to the level of consciousness at the time of visit and discharge because consciousness is among the major symptoms of CO intoxication, and the recovery of consciousness is a good marker of the effectiveness of treatment. Despite initially having an alert mental status, patients who developed DNS within the study period were described separately, and their characteristics were considered in depth.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted with IBM SPSS version 29.0 (IMB Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). In terms of the level of consciousness at the time of visit, a chi-square test was performed for nominal variables. Continuous variables were analyzed using Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test and expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) and median [IQR]. Statistical significance was determined when the P value was less than 0.05. Logistic regression analysis was performed using the occurrence of DNS to calculate the 95% confidence interval and odds ratio, and the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness of fit test was performed.

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Yeungnam University Medical Center (IRB No. YUMC 2025-05-036). Owing to the retrospective nature of the study, the need to obtain informed consent was waived by the Yeungnam University Medical Center ethical committee. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Results

A total of 288 patients were analysed in this study after the exclusion of patients who were misdiagnosed and not treated with hyperbaric oxygenation (Fig. 1). Most patients appeared to be in their mid-forties, and male patients accounted for the majority of the patients. These biological indicators did not significantly differ according to the initial state of consciousness. Preoxygenation was less common in alert patients, but the difference was not statistically significant. Most of the patients visited the hospital directly (n = 229, 79.5%).

Flowchart for patient inclusion

Most carbon monoxide fuels were briquettes (n = 233, 80.9%), followed by exposure to fires using fuels such as gasoline and butane gas (n = 40, 13.9%). There were also a few cases of accidents related to the use of Ondol and Jjimjilbang, which are traditional Korean heating agents (n = 8, 2.8%). With respect to location, most of the exposure occurred in residential places such as toilet rooms, boiler rooms, dormitories, kitchens, and other rooms (n = 171, 59.4%), followed by private spaces such as other accommodations (n = 22, 7.6%) or cars (n = 49, 17%).

CO intoxication due to suicide attempts accounted for the majority (n = 195, 67.7%), and the proportion was greater in the nonalert group. The prevalence of alcohol coingestion was also significantly greater in the nonalert group. The distribution of underlying diseases did not differ significantly between the groups.

Myocardial damage, represented by an increase in cardiac enzyme levels, was relatively common in the nonalert group, along with shock or the use of a ventilator during the treatment process. Most patients with decreased mental status improved during the treatment period. However, some patients remained unclear until the time of discharge, and DNS also occurred in some of the initially alert patients (Table 1).

Discussion

HBO therapy is widely used as a standard treatment for CO intoxication, with the primary aim of preventing DNS. Although randomized controlled trials have suggested the benefits of multiple HBO sessions within the first 24 h, evidence remains inconclusive, particularly regarding early symptom recovery and long-term neurological outcomes that occur at least a few months after the accident. Therefore, evaluations of the long-term efficacy of this method remain lacking [2, 3, 14]. We investigated how the initial mental status of CO intoxication patients at emergency room visits improved or worsened during the treatment period.

In our cohort, suicide attempts accounted for the majority of CO intoxications, reflecting epidemiologic trends in South Korea and East Asia [15]. Interestingly, patients with nonalert mental status tended to present earlier to the emergency department, likely because of rapid discovery by caregivers. Despite the higher acuity, many of these patients demonstrated substantial recovery of consciousness during treatment. Conversely, DNS occurred in some patients who were initially alert, underscoring that preserved consciousness at presentation does not preclude later neurological complications. This highlights the importance of careful follow-up, even among apparently mild cases.

In Korea, traditional heating systems such as Ondol remain a source of accidental CO exposure, particularly in rural settings. Although suicide attempts accounted for most cases overall, more than half of the DNS cases among initially alert patients were associated with accidental exposures, including Ondol-related incidents and camping. Generally, the severity and poor outcomes after CO poisoning correlate with a prolonged duration of exposure to carbon monoxide [12, 13, 15–17]. However, the results of other studies on suicide attempts with CO poisoning as well as our results revealed that voluntary attempts at CO exposure were not related to poor neurologic sequalae [6, 12, 13]. Therefore, a direct relationship between suicide attempts and prognosis cannot be established.

Altered mental status and myocardial injury are major signs of target organ damage in CO intoxication, and patients with severe CO intoxication exhibit hypotension and metabolic acidosis [18, 19]. A population-based cohort study revealed that severe CO intoxication can affect the development of Parkinson’s disease, especially in young people, possibly because of irreversible oxidative damage and neurodegeneration [20]. A single-centre study described fire as a source of CO, loss of consciousness, COHb levels, arterial pH, and the presence of endotracheal intubation during HBO therapy as mortality-associated factors [21]. However, several other studies, including our study, have reported that the COHb level itself does not reflect the severity of poisoning [16, 22–25]. This can be explained by the short half-life of carbon monoxide in the blood [5]. Neuroimaging studies have been attempted in CO poisoning patients several months after exposure, but reliable tools that can predict neurological prognosis in the early stages of damage have not yet been developed [26, 27]. Therefore, early neurologic deficits and haematologic examinations, including assessments of cardiac enzymes and metabolic acidosis, are very important indicators for the assessment of disease severity and treatment response in CO intoxication patients.

The association of myocardial injury and elevated CPK levels with DNS can be mechanistically explained by hypoxic–ischaemic injury and oxidative stress. CO binds to cytochrome oxidase and other haem proteins, impairing cellular respiration and contributing to multiorgan dysfunction. Myoglobin is released during the breakdown of muscle, and this can further compromise renal function and amplify systemic toxicity. These findings emphasize that CO intoxication is not merely a neurologic insult but a multisystem condition in which cardiac, muscular, and renal markers provide critical prognostic information.

From a clinical perspective, our results suggest that the early identification of patients at risk for DNS requires more than the assessment of mental status or COHb levels at presentation. Multimodal assessments, including assessments of cardiac enzyme levels, CPK, renal function, and ventilator requirements, should inform patient triage and the intensity of HBO therapy. Although most patients experienced symptom improvement after the first HBO session, the potential occurrence of DNS in some cases calls for vigilance in monitoring and the need for potentially repeated treatments, particularly in those with evidence of multiorgan involvement. In the future, additional analysis of factors related to exposure situations that can be risk factors for DNS occurrence is needed.

There are several limitations to this study. The hyperbaric oxygen chamber used during the study period was a single chamber. Therefore, severely ill patients with unstable vital signs or significantly poor mental status were not able to undergo immediate treatment with HBO. In addition, another limitation related to the treatment centre is that the proportion of relatively mild cases is high. Furthermore, unmeasured confounders such as baseline cognitive function, psychiatric comorbidities, and socioeconomic factors may have influenced the outcomes.

One of the important factors for analysis in this study, exposure time, might be somewhat inaccurate because we could not identify the specific time point when exposure began. In addition, a more accurate history that can infer the relationship between exposure time and carbon monoxide concentration was not possible. Additionally, it is possible that some patients who had been exposed prior to the study period and who presented with DNS were included, which can be considered a limitation of retrospective research.

Conclusions

Most early symptoms of CO intoxication generally improve in the early stage of development with proper oxygenation treatment. Providing proper oxygen therapy as soon as possible to patients with decreased consciousness after poisoning could help them fully recover consciousness. Although some patients present with preserved consciousness in the early phase of CO intoxication, they may subsequently develop DNS. Thus, when initial laboratory testing reveals elevated levels of myocardial enzymes or CPK, the provision of adequate HBO therapy may be beneficial for reducing the risk of neurological sequelae.

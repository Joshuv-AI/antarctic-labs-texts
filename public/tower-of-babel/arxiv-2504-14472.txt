Singular Lagrangians in the Hitchin moduli space and conformal limits
Sze-Hong Kwong

Abstract
In the moduli space ℳ H \mathcal{M}_{H} of semistable SL ​ ( r , ℂ ) \text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C}) -Higgs bundles, we show that there exists a sublocus S S of the upward flow through a polystable ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -fixed point [ ( ∂ ¯ 0 , Φ 0 ) ] [(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})] , which is Lagrangian on its intersection with the stable locus. When Φ 0 = 0 \Phi_{0}=0 or when the automorphism group of ( ∂ ¯ 0 , Φ 0 ) (\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0}) is abelian, we demonstrate that the intersection of S S with the stable locus is always non-empty, and establish the existence of conformal limits for stable Higgs bundles lying on S S .



1
Introduction

Over a fixed a closed Riemann surface of genus greater than one, a homeomorphism between the moduli space of degree zero polystable Higgs bundles and completely reducible flat connections, denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}
respectively, is given by the celebrated nonabelian Hodge correspondence (NAH). In addition, identifying flat connections with holomorphic connections, the two singular complex spaces both admit a natural stratification coming from the Białynicki-Birula type decomposition on the moduli space of
\lambda
-connections
\mathcal{M}
, which is associated with the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action equivariant with the fibration
\pi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
, where one makes the identification
\mathcal{M}_{0}\cong\pi^{-1}(0)
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}\cong\pi^{-1}(1)
(see
[
17
]
). Restriction of this
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action to
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is the standard
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action
t\cdot[(\mathcal{E},\Phi)]\mapsto[(\mathcal{E},t\Phi)]
. Each stratum in
\mathcal{M}
is fibred over a connected component of the locus of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed points. Now, given a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point
[(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
, let
W_{0}
and
W_{1}
be the restriction of the fibre to
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}
respectively. The fibre
W_{0}
is known as the
upward flow
through
[(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
. It is an interesting object to study, especially when it is very stable, in which case
W_{0}
is closed and plays a role in mirror symmetry (cf.
[
10
]
).

When the Higgs pair
(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})
is stable, much is understood about these restricted fibres
W_{0}
and
W_{1}
. For instance,
W_{0}
is contained in
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{0}
, the locus of stable Higgs bundles. The upward flow
W_{0}
is locally closed complex Lagrangian and is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariantly isomorphic to the direct sum of positively graded subspaces
T^{+}_{[(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})]}\mathcal{M}_{0}\subset T_{[(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})]}\mathcal{M}_{0}
. In the analytic setting, one may replace
T^{+}_{[(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})]}\mathcal{M}_{0}
with its harmonic representative
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}
. Through the Kuranishi map, a global parametrization
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\to W_{0}
is obtained in
[
3
]
, which can be interpolated to give a parametrization to
W_{1}
as well. Composing these two parametrizations then gives a correspondence from
W_{0}
to
W_{1}
, which turns out to coincide with the mapping given by
conformal limit
(see
[
7
]
and
[
3
]
). More detail about conformal limits will be given below.

The purpose of this article is to take the first step into the study of the singular cases, i.e. when
[(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
is a singular point in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, which happens when
(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})
is strictly polystable. We will focus on the Dolbeault side. The main results will be proved under either one of the following two assumptions on the polystable representative
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
:

Assumption 1
.

\Phi_{0}=0
.

Assumption 2
.

The automorphism group of
(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})
is abelian.

Note that Assumption
1
amounts to a saying that
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is a polystable holomorphic vector bundle, while Assumption
2
is equivalent to the statement that
(\mathcal{E}_{0},\Phi_{0})
is a direct sum of pairwise non-isomorphic stable Higgs bundles of the degree zero. When the rank of the underlying vector bundle is at most three, at least one of the two assumptions are satisfied (cf. Proposition
3.1
below).

The structure of the upward flow through a singular point
[(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
could be complicated, as opposed to the stable case, where the upward flow is parametrized by an affine space. However, when set up appropriately, the recipe for constructing this affine space parametrization will yield an interesting sublocus of the full upward flow.

Theorem 1.1
.

Along the upward flow through
[(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
, there exists a sub-locus

S=S([(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})])

parametrized by an affine GIT quotient, which is complex Lagrangian along the points which represent stable Higgs bundles. When Assumption
1
or Assumption
2
holds,
S
contains some stable Higgs bundle.

We will call
S
the
central locus
of the upward flow through
[(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
. Its construction will be carried out in the next section. We remark that as an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we have the following

Corollary 1.2
.

If Assumption
1
or Assumption
2
holds, then the upward flow through
[(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
contains some stable Higgs bundle.

Another motivation for the study of the central locus
S
, or more generally the upward flow
W_{0}
through a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point comes from the notion of conformal limits, which are defined as follows. Let
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
be a stable Higgs bundle. Then for each
R>0
,
(\overline{\partial}_{E},R\Phi)
is also stable. Let
h_{R}
be the harmonic metric for
(\overline{\partial}_{E},R\Phi)
, and
\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial^{h_{R}}
the corresponding Chern connection. For each
\xi\in\mathbb{C}-\{0\}
, we have the flat connection

\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial^{h_{R}}_{E}+\xi^{-1}R\Phi+\xi R\Phi^{*_{h_{R}}}.

Fixing a constant
\hbar\in\mathbb{C}-\{0\}
and letting
R=\hbar^{-1}\xi
, the limit

\lim_{R\to 0}(\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial^{h_{R}}_{E}+\hbar^{-1}\Phi+R^{2}\Phi^{*_{h_{R}}}),

if exist, is said to be the
(
\hbar
-)conformal limit
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. When
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
lies on the Hitchin component, it was conjectured by Gaiotto in
[
9
]
, that its conformal limit exists and lies in the space of oper. The origin of this conjecture comes from superconformal field theory of class
\mathcal{S}
which arise from compactification on the product of a Riemann surface and a circle of radius
R
. The non-punctured case was later proved in
[
7
]
, whose methodology was then generalized in
[
3
]
to show the existence of the conformal limit for
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
lying on the global parametrization of the upward flow through a stable
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point. The case for parabolic Higgs bundles is treated in
[
2
]
. One major obstacle absent from the stable case is the presence of non-trivial automorphism group, and hence the non-uniqueness of harmonic metrics for the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
, where
\lim_{t\to 0}[(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)]=[(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
. In the last section, we will prove the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose either Assumption
1
or Assumption
2
holds for
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})
. Let
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
be a stable Higgs bundle such that
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]\in S([(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})])
. Then the conformal limit of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
exists.

A few remarks are in order. For a strictly polystable
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)\in S
, one can decompose
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
into a direct sum of stable Higgs bundles and establish the existence of conformal limits of these stable factors. In addition, one may also consider a polystable Higgs pair
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
where
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
does not lie in the central locus
S
through
[(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
. The reason we do not consider this case in this paper is that we have not constructed a convenient model for the full upward flow through
[(\overline{\partial}_{0},\Phi_{0})]
. Nonetheless, under Assumption
2
, the method in the proof of Theorem
1.3
can be applied to such
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
, provided that
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is gauge transformed into a sufficiently ‘nice’ form.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains a collection of notations and preliminary results that we will need later. In Section
3
, we specialize to a polystable Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
that represents a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point in the moduli space. We then realize
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
as system of Hodge bundle, with which we define a grading on the deformation complex whose graded pieces are preserved by the automorphism group of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. We then proceed to the construction of the model for the central locus and prove Theorem
1.1
. The most technical part of the paper is the last subsection, where we prove Theorem
1.3
.

2
Kuranishi local model around a polystable Higgs bundle

The purpose of this section is to set up notation and collect preliminaries. We begin with an overview of the relevant aspects of the moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles from the gauge theoretic perspective, where we also introduce the notation adhered to throughout the paper. Next, we recall the local Kuranishi model around a polystable Higgs bundle constructed in
[
8
]
, which is a major ingredient needed in the discussion of the central locus and its model in the next section. We then review the Kempf-Ness criterion for stability in representation spaces under the action of a connected complex reductive Lie group. In the special case of complex tori, we record a criterion for stability of a vector in terms of the convex polytope associated with its effective weight.

2.1
The moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles

Throughout the paper, we fix a compact Riemann surface
X
of genus
g\geq 2
, equipped with a Kähler form of unit volume. Denote the canonical bundle of
X
by
K_{X}
and the trivial holomorphic line bundle by
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Let
E\to X
be a smooth complex vector bundle of rank
r
and degree zero, together with a fixed identification
\wedge^{r}E\xrightarrow{\cong}\underline{\mathbb{C}}
with the trivial line bundle. By a holomorphic vector bundle with
E
its underlying smooth vector bundle, we mean a pair
\mathcal{E}=(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
where
\overline{\partial}_{E}
is a Dolbeault operator
\overline{\partial}_{E}:\Omega^{p,q}(E)\to\Omega^{p,q+1}(E)
. This makes sense since
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=1
, which implies that the integrability condition
\overline{\partial}_{E}\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}=0
is always satisfied. For a holomorphic vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
, whenever the complex structure is clear we will simply denote the space of
\overline{\partial}_{E}
-holomorphic global sections by
H^{0}(E)
.

We shall be interested in holomorphic vector bundles with structure group
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
, i.e. holomorphic vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
such that the fixed identification
\wedge^{r}E\rightarrow\underline{\mathbb{C}}
induces
\wedge^{r}\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. By an
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle, we mean a pair
(\mathcal{E},\Phi)
where
\Phi\in\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
and
\overline{\partial}_{E}\Phi=0
. Here,
\mathfrak{sl}(E)
is the bundle of traceless endomorphisms of
E
. For convenience, we will simply call it a Higgs bundle or a Higgs pair for short. We will specify a Higgs bundle by a holomorphic structure
\overline{\partial}_{E}
and a
Higgs field

\Phi
, altogether denoted by the tuple
(\mathcal{E},\Phi)
,
(\mathcal{E},\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
or simply
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(E)
be the space of Dolbeault operators defining holomorphic
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
vector bundles. It is an affine space modelled on
\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
. Define the
configuration space
to be
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{A}\times\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
, and let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{C}
be the space of Higgs bundles, i.e.
\mathcal{B}=\{(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)\in\mathcal{C}:\overline{\partial}_{E}\Phi=0\}
. The
(complex) gauge group

\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)
consists of automophisms of
E
fixing the determinant. It acts on
\mathcal{C}
via conjugation: for
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)\in\mathcal{C}
,

g\cdot(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi):=(g\circ\overline{\partial}_{E},\circ g^{-1},g\Phi g^{-1})=(\overline{\partial}_{E}-(\overline{\partial}_{E}g)g^{-1},g\Phi g^{-1}).

(2.1)

This action preserves
\mathcal{B}
. For two Higgs bundles, we shall say they are gauge equivalent or isomorphic if they lie in the same (complex) gauge orbit.

The quotient
\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
is badly behaved. For instance, it is not Hausdorff. Instead, we will look at the ‘moduli space’ of semi-stable Higgs bundles. First, we recall the definitions of various notions of (slope) stability for
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles. For
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)\in\mathcal{B}
, we say it is
(semi)stable
if
\deg\mathcal{F}(\leq)<0
for all proper
\Phi
-invariant holomorphic subbundles
\mathcal{F}
of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
. A
polystable
Higgs bundle is a direct sum of stable Higgs bundles of degree zero. Each semistable Higgs bundle admits a Jordan-Hölder filtration, whose associated graded gives rise to a polystable Higgs bundle which is unique up to isomorphism (cf.
[
15
]
). Let
\mathcal{B}^{ss}
and
\mathcal{B}^{ps}
be the locus of semistable and polystable Higgs bundles respectively. We define the moduli space of semistable
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles to be

\mathcal{M}_{H}=\mathcal{M}_{H}(\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C}))=\mathcal{B}^{ss}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{B}^{ps}/\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}.

(2.2)

Here,
\mathcal{B}^{ss}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
refers to the quotient by
S
-equivalence classes (two semistable Higgs bundles are
S
-equivalent if their associated graded objects are gauge equivalent). We shall use
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
to denote the
S
-equivalence class of a semistable Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. More generally, we will use
\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,d)
to denote the moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
. The corresponding notions of slope stability are the same, except that we replace
0
by
d
.

The algebro-geometric construction of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,d)
was carried at
[
13
]
in the case of smooth projective curves and in
[
16
]
in the case of smooth projective varieties. In the case where
X
is a closed Riemann surface of genus at least two, the non-singular locus
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{H}
of
\mathcal{M}_{H}
is non-empty and consists of the isomorphism classes of stable Higgs bundles. In
[
11
]
,
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{H}
was constructed by analytic means using the standard Kuranishi technique, and it was equipped with a hyperkähler structure inherited from that of the configuration space.

To describe the hyperkähler structure, we first fix a hermitian metric
h
on the smooth vector bundle
E\to X
, whose induced metric
\det h
on
\det E
is compatible with the standard inner product on the trivial line bundle
\underline{\mathbb{C}}
. For
A\in\Omega^{k}(\text{End}(E))
, we denote by

A^{*}=A^{*_{h}}

the
h
-hermitian adjoint of
A
. Often, when the prescribed hermitian metric
h
is clear, we will omit the subscript
h
. The tangent space at each point in
\mathcal{C}
can be identified with the space
\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
, which admits a hermitian inner product
H
given by

H((\beta_{1},\varphi_{1}),(\beta_{2},\varphi_{2})):=i\int_{X}\text{tr}(\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi^{*}_{2}+\beta^{*}_{2}\wedge\beta_{1})

(2.3)

with respect to the complex structure
I
defined by

I(\beta,\varphi):=(i\beta,i\varphi).

(2.4)

The configuration space
\mathcal{C}
can also be identified with the space of
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-connections in the following manner. Observe that every complex connection can be uniquely decomposed into an
h
-unitary connection and an
h
-hermitian one-form. The existence and uniqueness of Chern connections then identifies
\mathcal{A}
with the space of
h
-unitary connections. Also, sending a hermitian one-form to its
(1,0)
-part gives an isomorphism
\Omega^{1}(i\mathfrak{su}(E))\xrightarrow{\cong}\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
, where
\mathfrak{su}(E)=\mathfrak{su}(E,h)
is the bundle of traceless skew
h
-hermitian endomorphisms of
E
. Altogether, we have an identification between
\mathcal{C}
and the space of
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
connections, with the latter modeled on
\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
. This gives another complex structure
J
on
\mathcal{C}
:

J(\beta,\varphi):=(i\varphi^{*},-i\beta^{*}).

(2.5)

It is straightforward to verify that
(\text{Re}H,I,J,K=IJ)
is a hyperkähler structure on the configuration space
\mathcal{C}
. The Kähler form with respect to
I
,
J
and
K
are denoted by
\omega_{I}
,
\omega_{J}
and
\omega_{K}
respectively. A direct computation then shows that the
I
-holomorphic symplectic form
\Omega_{I}
is given by

\Omega_{I}((\beta_{1},\varphi_{1}),(\beta_{2},\varphi_{2}))=\int_{X}\text{tr}(\varphi_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}-\varphi_{2}\wedge\beta_{1}).

(2.6)

For
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)\in\mathcal{C}
, let
F_{(\overline{\partial}_{E},h)}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{su}(E,h))
be the curvature of the Chern connection
\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial^{h}_{E}
associated with the complex structure defined by
\overline{\partial}_{E}
. The
Hitchin equation

\mu(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi):=F_{(\overline{\partial}_{E},h)}+[\Phi,\Phi^{*}]=0

(2.7)

together with the holomorphicity condition

\overline{\partial}_{E}\Phi=0

(2.8)

can be intepreted as the hyperkähler moment map with respect to the unitary gauge group
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}(h)
, the subgroup of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
preserving the hermitian metric
h
. Developed in
[
11
]
and
[
6
]
, and in
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
for the general case, the nonabelian Hodge correspondence (NAH for short) asserts that a Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is polystable (stable) if and only if there exists a metric
h
that solves the Hitchin equation, in which case the connection

NAH(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)=\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial^{h}_{E}+\Phi+\Phi^{*_{h}}

is flat and completely reducible (irreducible). Such a connection is unique. Conversely, a flat connection
D
is completely reducible (irreducible) if and only if there exists a metric
h
such that in the decomposition
d_{A}+\Psi
where
d_{A}
is
k
-unitary and
\Psi
is
h
-hermitian, we have

d_{A}\Psi^{1,0}=0,

in which case
\overline{\partial}_{E}=d_{A}^{\prime\prime}
, the
(0,1)
-part of
d_{A}
, and
\Phi=\Psi^{1,0}
defines a polystable (stable) Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. In both directions, the metric
h
is called a
harmonic metric
to
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
or
D
. The automorphism group
G
of a polystable Higgs bundle acts transitively on the set of its harmonic metrics. If
h
is a harmonic metric for two complex gauge-equivalent polystable Higgs bundles, then they are
h
-unitary gauge-equivalent. Globally, the NAH yields the homeomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{H}\cong(\mathcal{B}^{ps}\cap\mu^{-1}(0))/\mathcal{G}.

In this paper, we will often need to consider the local geometry around a point
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
in
\mathcal{M}_{H}
(more specifically, a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point). When
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is stable, a slice consisting of stable Higgs bundles which represents an open neighborhood around
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
can be obtained using the standard Kuranishi slice technique. However, when
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
is a singular point in
\mathcal{M}_{H}
, which is the case we are concerned with, the construction of a local model for an open neighborhood of
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
is more delicate. We will utilize the analytic local model constructed rigorously in
[
8
]
which is based on properties of the Yang-Mills-Higgs flow studied in
[
18
]
. The next subsection will summarize the construction of this local model.

2.2
The local Kuranishi model around a polystable Higgs bundle

Let
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
be a strictly polystable
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle. The deformation complex of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is given by

C^{\bullet}=C^{\bullet}(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi):\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\xrightarrow{D^{\prime\prime}}\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\xrightarrow{D^{\prime\prime}}\Omega^{1,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))

(2.9)

where
D^{\prime\prime}:=\overline{\partial}_{E}+\Phi
. For the degree one piece of the complex
C^{1}=\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
, we usually use a tuple
u=(\beta,\varphi)
to denote its elements. We will also use
u
to represent
\beta+\varphi\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
. For any
v\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
, we write
v=v^{0,1}+v^{1,0}
to denote its type decomposition.

Fix a harmonic metric
h
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. We define
D^{\prime}:=\partial^{h}+\Phi^{*}
where
\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial^{h}
is the Chern connection with respect to the metric
h
,
\Phi^{*}
the
h
-hermitian adjoint of
\Phi
, and
D:=D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}
is the flat connection under NAH. We remark that
D^{\prime}
is independent of the choice of harmonic metrics of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. These operators satisfy the following Kähler identities (for example, see
[
15
]
):

(D^{\prime\prime})^{*}=-i[\Lambda,D^{\prime}]\quad\text{and}\quad(D^{\prime})^{*}=i[\Lambda,D^{\prime\prime}].

(2.10)

We denote the space of harmonic forms of the deformation complex by

\mathcal{H}^{i}=\mathcal{H}^{i}({C^{\bullet}}):=\{x\in C^{i}:D^{\prime\prime}x=0,(D^{\prime\prime})^{*}x=0\}.

(2.11)

The zero-th cohomology space
\mathcal{H}^{0}=\ker D^{\prime\prime}
can be identified with the Lie algebra of the automorphism group of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. Moreover, via Serre duality we have
\mathcal{H}^{2}\cong(\mathcal{H}^{0})^{\vee}
. When
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is strictly polystable,
\mathcal{H}^{0}\neq\{0\}
. Denote by
G
the automorphism group of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
, i.e. its stabilizer group under the complex gauge group action. Then
G
acts on
\mathcal{H}^{i}
,
i=0,1,2
, by conjugation. It has a maximal compact subgroup
K=K(h)
which consists of elements in
G
fixing the metric
h
. All other harmonic metrics of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
can be obtained from pulling back by elements in
G
. In general, for a gauge transformation
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
, we shall use
g^{*}h
to refer to the pullback metric defined by
g^{*}h(v,w)=h(gv,gw)
.

In the remainder of this subsection, we will recall the local model around
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]\in\mathcal{M}_{H}
constructed in Section 3 of
[
8
]
. Define the
Kuranishi map

\kappa:\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\to\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
associated with
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
by

\kappa(\beta,\varphi)=(\beta,\varphi)+(D^{\prime\prime})^{*}\Gamma([\beta,\varphi]),

(2.12)

where
\Gamma
is the Green’s operator of the Laplacian
D^{\prime\prime}(D^{\prime\prime})^{*}
on
\Omega^{1,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
. The automorphism group
G
acts on both sides by conjugation, and
\kappa
is
G
-equivariant. We remark that this
G
-action is compatible with the action of the gauge group: for
g\in G
,
g\cdot(\overline{\partial}_{E}+\beta,\Phi+\varphi)=(\overline{\partial}_{E}+g\beta g^{-1},\Phi+g\varphi g^{-1})
. Let

\tilde{Z}:=\{(\beta,\varphi)\in\ker D^{\prime}:D^{\prime\prime}(\beta,\varphi)+[\beta,\varphi]\perp\mathcal{H}^{2}\}.

Restricted to
\tilde{Z}
,
\kappa
takes values in
\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Moreover,
\kappa
is locally invertible at the origin. There exists an open subset
\tilde{U}
of
\tilde{Z}
containing the origin, such that
\kappa|_{\tilde{U}}
is a local biholomorphism onto an open set
U=\kappa(\tilde{U})
of
\mathcal{H}^{1}
containing
0
. By shrinking
\tilde{U}
if needed, we may take
U
to be an open ball centered at
0
. Then, the restriction
\kappa|_{\tilde{U}}
is
K
-equivariant. Let
\kappa^{-1}:U\to\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
be the inverse of
\kappa
. Let

Z:=\{(\beta,\varphi)\in\ker D^{\prime}:D^{\prime\prime}(\beta,\varphi)+[\beta,\varphi]=0\}\subset\tilde{Z}.

(2.13)

Note that
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)+Z\subset\mathcal{B}
. For
u=(\beta,\varphi)\in Z
, we will use interchangeably

(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})=(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)+u=(\overline{\partial}_{E}+\beta,\Phi+\varphi)

to denote the Higgs bundle translated from
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
by
u
.

Define
k:\mathcal{H}^{1}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}
by

k(x):=\dfrac{1}{2}P[x,x],

(2.14)

where
P
is the projection to
\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Let
\mathcal{Z}:=k^{-1}(0)\subset\mathcal{H}^{1}
. We remark that (cf. Theorem 5.1,
[
8
]
) for
(\beta,\varphi)\in\tilde{Z}
,

D^{\prime\prime}(\beta,\varphi)+[\beta,\varphi]=k(\kappa(\beta,\varphi)),

from which it follows that
\kappa(Z)\subset\mathcal{Z}
. By local biholomorphicity,
\kappa(Z\cap\tilde{U})=\mathcal{Z}\cap U
for
U
sufficiently small. We will refer to either one of
Z
,
\mathcal{Z}
,
Z\cap\tilde{U}
,
\kappa(Z)\cap U
, or their restrictions to smaller open neighborhoods as the
Kuranishi space
. For
U
sufficiently small, we have the map
\theta:=\kappa^{-1}:\kappa(Z)\cap U\to\mathcal{B}^{ss}
, defined by

\theta(x)=(\overline{\partial}_{E}+(\kappa^{-1}(x))^{0,1},\Phi+(\kappa^{-1}(x))^{1,0}).

(2.15)

As
\mathcal{H}^{1}
is a
G
-representation, there is a corresponding notion of stability for vectors in
\mathcal{H}^{1}
. One key step in the construction in
[
8
]
is to relate polystability in
\mathcal{H}^{1}
with polystability of the corresponding Higgs bundles, for vectors in
\kappa(Z)\cap U
with
U
sufficiently small. Recall that for a
G
-representation
V
, a nonzero vector

is said to be
(G-)polystable
if its orbit
G\cdot v
is closed in
V
. If
v
is polystable and its isotropy group
G_{v}
is finite, we say

is
(G-)stable
.

We are now ready to describe the construction of the local model around
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
in
[
8
]
. For a sufficiently small open ball
U
around the origin,
G\cdot(\mathcal{Z}\cap U)
is a closed complex subspace of the open set
G\cdot U
, and the Kuranishi space
\mathcal{Z}\cap U
is open in
G\cdot(\mathcal{Z}\cap U)
. We have the following local parametrization of
\mathcal{M}_{H}
around
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
8
]
, Section 3)
.

For a sufficiently small ball
U
in
\mathcal{H}^{1}
centered at
0
, the continuous map
\mathcal{Z}\cap U\xrightarrow{\theta}\mathcal{B}^{ss}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{H}
induces an open embedding

G\cdot(\mathcal{Z}\cap U)\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G\xlongrightarrow{\pi}\mathcal{M}_{H}.

In fact, if
x\in\mathcal{Z}\cap U
is polystable, then
\theta(x)
is a polystable Higgs bundle.

Here,
G\cdot(\mathcal{Z}\cap U)\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G
denotes the analytic GIT quotient of the reduced complex space
G\cdot(\mathcal{Z}\cap U)
.

2.3
Stability in linear representations

The purpose of this subsection is to collect some useful criteria for stability in a
G
-representation
V
of a connected complex reductive group
G
, and to set up the notation to be used in the last section in the case when
G
is a complex torus. First, we recall the theorem of Kempf and Ness
[
12
]
, which relates the polystability and stability of a nonzero vector
v\in V
in terms of the existence of minimizers of the length squared functional. Then, we specialize to the case when
G
is a torus, where
V
admits a weight decomposition. In this case, stability of
v
can be detected from the affine properties of the convex hull of its effective weights (see Theorem 9.2 in
[
5
]
for example).

Let
G
be a connected complex reductive group with a maximal compact subgroup
K
and
V
a complex
G
-representation with a hermitian inner product such that
K
acts unitarily. Recall that we have the decomposition
G=K\text{exp}(i\mathfrak{k})
, where
\mathfrak{k}=\text{Lie}(K)
. Now, let
v\in V-\{0\}
. The
Kempf-Ness functional
of
v
, denoted by
\ell_{v}:i\mathfrak{k}\to\mathbb{R}
, is given by

\ell_{v}(x):=||e^{x}\cdot v||^{2}.

(2.16)

Theorem 2.2

(
[
12
]
, Kempf-Ness)
.

A nonzero vector
v
is polystable if and only if
\inf\ell_{v}
can be attained, and is stable if and only if the minimizer is unique.

Next, we will specialize to the case where
G
is an
k
-dimensional complex torus, i.e.
G\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{k}
. The setup and results in the rest of this subsection will not be used until the last section, and hence can be safely skipped until then.

The representation space
V
admits an orthogonal decomposition into weight spaces

V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}}V_{\lambda}

(2.17)

where
\Lambda_{G}\subset X(G):=\text{Hom}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})
is the set of weights of the
G
-representation. We identify
X(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
with
(i\mathfrak{k})^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
so that we can regard
X(G)\subset(i\mathfrak{k})^{\vee}
as an integral lattice of the dual space
(i\mathfrak{k})^{\vee}
.

Let
v\in V-\{0\}
. For
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}
, we let
v_{\lambda}
be the projection of
v
to the
\lambda
-weight space. Let

\Lambda_{G}(v):=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}:v_{\lambda}\neq 0\}.

(2.18)

We will call the set
\Lambda_{G}(v)
the
effective weight
of
v
with respect to the
G
-action. Finally, let
C(v)=\text{Conv}(\Lambda_{G}(v))
be the closed convex hull of
\Lambda_{G}(v)
in
(i\mathfrak{k})^{\vee}
.

In the last section, we will need the following criterion of the stability of
v
in terms of properties of
C(v)
, which is essentially a visual depiction of the Hilbert-Mumford criterion. It can be found, for example, in Theorem 9.2 of
[
5
]
. For completeness, we will give the proof of the equivalence between this criterion and the existence of unique minimizer of the Kempf-Ness functional.

Proposition 2.3
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

v
is stable

(ii)

\ell_{v}
has a unique minimizer.

(iii)

0\in C(v)^{\circ}
, the interior of
C(v)
(in the euclidean topology of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
).

Proof.

The equivalence between (i) and (ii) is just a special case of the Kempf-Ness theorem. In terms of the weight space decomposition, we can re-write (
2.16
) as

\ell_{v}(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}(v)}||v_{\lambda}||^{2}e^{2\langle\lambda,x\rangle}.

(2.19)

First, we assume (ii). Note that for any
v^{\prime}\in G\cdot v
,
\Lambda_{G}(v^{\prime})=\Lambda_{G}(v)
. Hence, we can assume without loss of generality that the unique minimizer takes place at the origin. Since
\Lambda_{G}(v)
is finite,
C(v)
is a convex polytope. If
k=1
, then
C(v)
is a closed interval of the form
[-n,m]
where
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Now, assume
k\geq 2
We first claim that
C(v)
is
k
-dimensional. Suppose to the contrary that
\dim C(v)<k
. Then
C(v)
is contained in some hyperplane
H=\{v\in(i\mathfrak{k})^{\vee}:\langle v,u\rangle=a\}
for some
u\in i\mathfrak{k}
and
a\leq 0
. For
t\geq 0
,
\ell_{v}(tu)=\sum||v_{\lambda}||^{2}e^{2ta}\leq\ell_{v}(0)
. This contradicts the assumption that
0
is the unique minimizer of
\ell_{v}
. A similar argument together with the hyperplane separation theorem will show that
0\in C(v)^{\circ}
.

Conversely, assume (iii). First, we claim that
\inf\ell_{v}
can be attained. Let
S
be the unit sphere in
i\mathfrak{k}
determined by some inner product. Pick a minimizing sequence
\{y_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume the sequence is unbounded. Hence, passing to subsequences if necessary, we may write
y_{j}=r_{j}u_{j}
where
0<r_{j}\nearrow\infty
,
u_{j}\in S
and
u_{j}\to u\in S
. Now, we evaluate

\ell_{v}(y_{j})=\sum||v_{\lambda}||^{2}(e^{2\langle\lambda,u_{j}\rangle})^{r_{j}}\to\inf\ell_{v}.

Since
\ell_{v}(y_{j})
is bounded, we have
\langle\lambda,u\rangle\leq 0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}(v)
. As
C(v)
is the convex hull of
\Lambda_{G}(v)
, it implies
\langle C(v),u\rangle\leq 0
. This contradicts to the assumption
0\in C(v)^{\circ}
since any open ball centered at
0
contains some point
y
such that
\langle y,u\rangle>0
. Therefore, no unbounded minimizing sequences exist and thus
\inf\ell_{v}
can be attained.

Let
x_{0}
be a minimizer of
\ell_{v}
. For any
w\neq 0
, consider the function

t\mapsto\ell_{v}(x_{0}+tw)=\sum||v_{\lambda}||^{2}e^{2\langle\lambda,x_{0}\rangle}e^{2\langle\lambda,w\rangle t},

which is convex and has a global minimum at
t=0
. Moreover, it is either strictly convex and thereby uniquely minimized, or it is constant. In the latter case, we have
w\in D
, where

D=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}(v)}\ker\lambda.

It is also easy to see that for any
w\in D
,
t\mapsto\ell_{v}(x_{0}+tw)\equiv\ell_{v}(x_{0})
. Therefore, the set of minimizers of
\ell_{v}
is
x_{0}+D
. But
\langle\Lambda_{G}(v),D\rangle\equiv 0
by definition, so
C(v)\subset D^{\perp}:=\{v\in(i\mathfrak{k})^{\vee}:\langle v,D\rangle\equiv 0\}
. As
0\in C(v)^{\circ}
by assumption, we must have
D^{\perp}=(i\mathfrak{k})^{\vee}
. It follows that
D=\{0\}
and thus
x_{0}
is the unique minimizer.
∎

3
The central locus

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. Given a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]\in\mathcal{M}_{H}
, we will define and construct a model for the central locus of the upward flow through
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
. In the case where
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is stable, this construction is the same as the global parametrization of the upward flow through
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
obtained in
[
3
]
. When
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is strictly polystable, however, we need to take into account the non-trivial automorphism group
G
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. Another difference compared with the stable case is that there is an ambiguity in decomposing
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
into a system of Hodge bundles (to be recalled below), a step that is needed to decompose the deformation complex of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
into graded pieces.

In the following, We begin by recalling basic facts about the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{H}
. We then recall the notion of system of Hodge bundles and prove a couple of auxiliary results. In the next subsection, we decompose
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
into to a system of Hodge bundles in a specific way, so that
G
respects the induced graded structure on the deformation complex of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. This allows us to define the central locus
S
of the upward flow through
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
. We then show that
S\cap\mathcal{M}_{H}^{s}
is Lagrangian (Proposition
3.9
). Finally, under Assumption
1
or Assumption
2
, we prove that
S\cap\mathcal{M}^{s}_{H}
is non-empty (Theorem
3.10
). Together, this proves Theorem
1.1
.

3.1
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed points in
\mathcal{M}_{H}
and system of Hodge bundles (SHB)

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{H}
admits a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action
t\cdot[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]\mapsto[(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)]
. For
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]\in\mathcal{M}_{H}
, as a consequence of the properness of the Hitchin fibration, the limit

\lim_{t\to 0}[(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)]

always exists, and we shall call it the
downward limit
of
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
. Alternatively, the existence of downward limits can be deduced from the constructions in
[
17
]
.

By a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point, we mean a point in
\mathcal{M}_{H}
fixed by the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action. Downward limits are always
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed points. A polystable representative of a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point can be written as a
system of Hodge bundles
. Recall that a system of Hodge bundles (SHB for short) is a direct sum of holomorphic vector bundles
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})=\bigoplus_{a=1}^{k}(E_{a},\overline{\partial}_{a})
together with a Higgs field
\Phi=\sum_{a=1}^{k-1}\Phi_{a}
, where
\Phi_{a}\in\Omega^{1,0}(\text{Hom}(E_{a},E_{a+1})
and
\overline{\partial}_{E}\Phi_{a}=0
. When an SHB is semistable as a Higgs bundle, one can show that
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point. Conversely, if
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is polystable and
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point, then
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
can be endowed with an SHB structure, which is unique when
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is stable. The summands
E_{a}
are mutually orthogonal with respect to the harmonic metric of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
.

In this article, there is one SHB structure for a polystable
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
that is convenient for our purposes. Its construction will be carried out in the next subsection. In preparation for that, we first establish the following

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is polystable and
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]\in\mathcal{M}_{H}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point. Then
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
can be written as a direct sum of stable Higgs bundles which represent
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed points.

Proof.

First, we claim that all
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)
lie in the same gauge equivalence class. This is true for
t\in S^{1}
since
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]=[(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)]
and the same harmonic metrics solve the Hitchin equation for each
(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)
. Pick a
t
,
|t|=1
, which is not a root of unity. Then there exists an isomorphism
f_{t}
from
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
to
(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)
. In particular,
f_{t}
is holomorphic with respect to the holomorphic structure defined by
\overline{\partial}_{E}
. Proved in Lemma 4.1 of
[
15
]
, the generalized eigenspaces of
f_{t}
give
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
a system of Hodge bundles structure, with which we can construct isomorphisms to all
(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)
,
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Next, we decompose
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
into a direct sum of stable summands
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},\Phi^{i})
. We claim each
(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},\Phi^{i})
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point in the moduli space of degree zero polystable Higgs bundles of the corresponding ranks. Pick a non-zero scalar
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
which is not a root of unity. Each
(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},t\Phi^{i})
is stable and we have
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)\cong(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)=\bigoplus_{i}(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},t\Phi^{i})
. It follows that
(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},t\Phi^{i})\cong(\overline{\partial}_{E^{\sigma(i)}},\Phi^{\sigma(i)})
for some permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\cdots,k\}
. Composing these isomorphisms, we have
(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},t^{j}\Phi^{i})\cong(\overline{\partial}_{E^{\sigma^{j}(i)}},\Phi^{\sigma^{j}(i)})
. Now, setting
j=\text{ord}(\sigma)
, we have
(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},\Phi^{i})\cong(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},t^{j}\Phi^{i})
. Applying the procedure in Lemma 4.1 of
[
15
]
again, we see that each
[(\overline{\partial}_{E^{i}},\Phi^{i})]
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point.
∎

We will also need the following lemma, which roughly speaking says that taking downward limits commutes with direct sums.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
be a polystable Higgs bundle. Suppose
(\mathcal{E},\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\mathcal{E}^{i},\overline{\partial}^{i},\Phi^{i})
, where each summand
(\mathcal{E}^{i},\Phi^{i})
is a stable Higgs bundle with downward limit
\lim_{t\to 0}[(\mathcal{E}^{i},\overline{\partial}^{i},t\Phi^{i})]=[(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{0},\overline{\partial}^{i}_{0},\Phi^{i}_{0})]
represented by
(\overline{\partial}^{i}_{0},\Phi^{i}_{0})
which is polystable. Then
\lim_{t\to 0}[(\mathcal{E},\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)]=[\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{0},\overline{\partial}^{i}_{0},\Phi^{i}_{0})]
.

Proof.

For each
i
, using Proposition
3.1
, we put an SHB structure on
(\overline{\partial}^{i}_{0},\Phi^{i}_{0})
in the form (
3.3
) and fix a harmonic metric
h_{i}
. Let
G_{i}
be the automorphism group of
(\overline{\partial}^{i}_{0},\Phi^{i}_{0})
,
U_{i}
a small ball around
0
in
\mathcal{H}^{1}
of the deformation complex of
(\overline{\partial}^{i}_{0},\Phi^{i}_{0})
as in Proposition
2.1
,
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
the Kuranishi space and let

\pi_{i}:G_{i}\cdot(\mathcal{Z}_{i}\cap U_{i})\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G_{i}\to\mathcal{M}_{H}(r_{i},0)

be the local Kuranishi model into the moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles of rank
r_{i}
and degree zero, where
r_{i}=\text{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{i})
. For small
|t|
, every
[(\overline{\partial}^{i},t\Phi^{i})]
has representatives
x_{i}^{t}
in
U_{i}
, i.e.
\pi_{i}([x_{i}^{t}])=[(\overline{\partial}^{i},t\Phi^{i})]
. Shrinking the radius of each
U_{i}
to zero, we see that there exists a sequence
t_{n}\to 0
such that
x_{i}^{t_{n}}\to 0
for all
i
. For each
x_{i}^{t_{n}}
, choose
y_{i}^{t_{n}}\in\overline{G_{i}\cdot x_{i}^{t_{n}}}
which minimizes the

-norm along
\overline{G_{i}\cdot x_{i}^{t_{n}}}
. Then
y_{i}^{t_{n}}\in U_{i}
,
y_{i}^{t_{n}}\to 0
as

and the
G_{i}
-orbit of each

is closed. By Proposition
2.1
,
\theta(y_{i}^{t_{n}})
is gauge-equivalent to
(\overline{\partial}^{i},t_{n}\Phi^{i})
(for convenience, here we use the same notation
\theta
to denote the inverses of the Kuranishi maps with respect to all
(\overline{\partial}^{i}_{0},\Phi^{i}_{0})
). It follows that
(\overline{\partial}_{E},t_{n}\Phi)
is gauge equivalent to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\theta(y_{i}^{t_{n}})
. For each

, we may take the gauge transformations to lie in

. Hence,
\lim_{n\to 0}[(\overline{\partial}_{E},t_{n}\Phi)]=[\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\overline{\partial}^{i}_{0},\Phi^{i}_{0})]
. Since
\lim_{t\to 0}[(\overline{\partial}_{E},t\Phi)]
exists, the result follows.
∎

3.2
The deformation complex at a polystable SHB

Now, suppose
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point, with
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
polystable. Let
G
be its automorphism group. We will specify an SHB structure on
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
in the following manner. By Proposition
3.1
, there exists a holomorphic decomposition into
k
stable summands

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(E^{i},\overline{\partial}^{i})\quad\text{ and }\quad\Phi=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Phi^{i},

(3.1)

where
\Phi^{i}\in H^{0}(\text{End}(E^{i})\otimes K_{X})
, such that each
(\overline{\partial}^{i},\Phi^{i})
is a stable Higgs bundle of degree zero representing a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point. Each
(\overline{\partial}^{i},\Phi^{i})
admits a unique SHB structure given by

(E^{i},\overline{\partial}^{i})=\bigoplus_{a=1}^{\ell_{i}}(E^{i}_{a},\overline{\partial}^{i}_{a})\quad\text{ and }\quad\Phi^{i}=\sum_{a=1}^{\ell_{i}-1}\Phi^{i}_{a}

(3.2)

where
\Phi^{i}_{a}\in H^{0}(\text{Hom}(E^{i}_{a},E^{i}_{a+1})\otimes K_{X})
. Let

(E_{a},\overline{\partial}_{a}):=\bigoplus_{i}(E^{i}_{a},\overline{\partial}^{i}_{a})\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{a}:=\sum_{i}\Phi^{i}_{a}

(3.3)

so that
\Phi_{a}\in H^{0}(\text{Hom}(E_{a},E_{a+1})\otimes K_{X})
. The direct sum
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})=\bigoplus_{a=1}^{\ell}(E_{a},\overline{\partial}_{a})
, where
\ell=\max_{i}\{\ell_{i}\}
. (
3.3
) then defines an SHB structure on
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. We can take a harmonic metric
h
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
such that the summands
E^{i}
and
E^{j}
are mutually orthogonal with respect to

. Thus,
E_{a}
and
E_{b}
are mutually orthogonal. Henceforth, this is be the SHB structure we will refer to. We remark that the resulting Hodge summands (
3.3
) are independent of the choice of the stable summand decomposition (
3.1
).

Next, we recall the deformation complex (
2.9
). As in
[
3
]
, we introduce a grading on
\text{End}(E)
, whose
\ell
-th component is given by

\text{End}_{\ell}(E):=\bigoplus_{i-j=\ell}\text{Hom}(E_{i},E_{j}).

(3.4)

There is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
C^{\bullet}
defined in the following way. Let
w\in\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
,
(\beta,\varphi)\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
and
v\in\Omega^{1,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
. We will add a subscript
j
to denote the
j
-th graded component with respect to the grading on
\text{End}(E)
. For
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, the action is given by

\displaystyle t\cdot w

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j}t^{j}w_{j}

used in the construction of the local Kuranishi model in Proposition
2.1
. If
u\neq 0
and
(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})
is not stable, then by (ii) of Proposition
3.8
and upon replacement if needed,
(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})\in(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)+S_{+}(P)
for some proper partition
P
. Hence, if
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)+S_{+}
contains no stable Higgs bundles, then we can write
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G=\bigcup_{P}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P)\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G
, a finite union of closed subsets ranging over proper partitions
P
.

Lastly, we proceed by induction on
\dim G
. First, we look at the case
\dim G=1
, in whise case
P=\{P_{1},P_{2}\}
is the unique proper partition and
G
acts trivially on
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P)
. Let
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
be the rank of the underlying vector bundles of the two stable factors so that
r=r_{1}+r_{2}
. We have

\dim(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P)\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G)=\dim\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P_{1})+\dim\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P_{2})=(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2)(g-1)<\dim\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G.

Therefore, there must exist stable Higgs bundles on
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)+S_{+}
since otherwise one would have
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G=\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P)\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G\cong\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P)
.

Now, let
r>1
. We suppose it is true that the (
\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)+S_{+}
contains a stable Higgs bundle whenever
\dim G<r
. Assume now that
\dim G=r\geq 2
. We claim that for each proper
P
, there exists polystable
w\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P)
such that
w\notin\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}(P^{\prime})
for all
P^{\prime}\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 6.00006pt$|$\hss}>P
. It suffices to show that for each subcollection of stable factors
P_{\lambda}\in P
, the locus
(\overline{\partial}(P_{\lambda}),\Phi(P_{\lambda}))+S_{+}(P_{\lambda})
contains a stable Higgs bundle. If
|P_{\lambda}|=1
,
(\overline{\partial}(P_{\lambda}),\Phi(P_{\lambda}))
is stable, in which case it is shown in
[
3
]
that
(\overline{\partial}(P_{\lambda}),\Phi(P_{\lambda}))+S_{+}(P_{\lambda})
consists of stable Higgs bundles. For
|P_{\lambda}|=r_{\lambda}+1>1
, we have
0<r_{\lambda}<r
so this case follows from the induction hypothesis. Hence, if
(\overline{\partial}_{E}+\Phi)+S_{+}
contains no stable Higgs bundles, then
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}G
is a finite union of proper closed subsets, which contradicts to the fact that
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}
is irreducible. Therefore,
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)+S_{+}
must contain a stable Higgs bundle.
∎

4
Conformal limits

Let
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
be a strictly polystable such that
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)]
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point. We will use the SHB structure introduced in Section
3.2
and follow the notation therein. From the SHB structure, we obtain a family of isomorphisms from
(\overline{\partial}_{E},R\Phi)
to
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
for each
R>0
. For a harmonic metric
h
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
, the summands
E_{a}
and
E_{b}
are mutually orthogonal. By pulling back
h
, we obtain harmonic metrics
h_{R}
for
(\overline{\partial}_{E},R\Phi)
.

Let
(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})\in S_{+}
be stable. The idea in
[
7
]
of establishing the existence of the conformal limit of
(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})
is the following. Using
h_{R}
, one first obtain a family of connections

\overline{\partial}_{u}+\partial^{h_{R}}_{u}+\hbar^{-1}\Phi_{u}+\hbar R^{2}\Phi_{u}^{*_{h_{R}}}

which converges to the flat connection

D_{0}:=\overline{\partial}_{u}+\partial^{h}_{E}+h^{-1}\Phi_{u}+\hbar\Phi^{*_{h}}.

(4.1)

Then, one attempt to correct the metrics to
(e^{f_{R}})^{*}h_{R}
, where
f_{R}
is
h_{R}
-hermitian, so that the resulting connections are flat. This amounts to solving

N_{u,R}(f_{R}):=\overline{\partial}_{u}(e^{-2f_{R}}\partial_{u}^{h_{R}}(e^{2f_{R}}))+F_{(\overline{\partial}_{u},h_{R})}+[\Phi_{u},e^{-2f_{R}}(R^{2}\Phi_{u}^{*_{h_{R}}})e^{2f_{R}}]=0.

(4.2)

Recall that the SHB structure induces a grading on
\Omega^{p,q}(\text{End}(E))
. Using the notation in Section
3.2
, for
d>0
, we have

(f_{R})_{-d}=R^{2d}(f_{R})_{d}^{*_{h}}.

This fact is used in
[
3
]
to parametrize
f_{R}
by non-negatively graded pieces so that the operator
M_{u,R}
is extended to all
R
. When the SHB
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
is stable, using the implicit function theorem, it is shown that for small
R
there exists
f_{R}
with
f_{0}=0
such that
M_{u,R}(f_{R})\equiv 0
, which confirms that the conformal limit of
(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})
is
D_{0}

In the present context, the last perturbation step in the method outlined above breaks down due to the non-uniqueness harmonic metrics of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\Phi)
. In the following, we will see that the issues that arise from this can be overcome under Assumption
1
or Assumption
2
. In both cases, we will determine a harmonic metric
h
which depends on
u
, from which we determine a family of metrics
h^{\prime}_{R}
modified from
h_{R}
. The first case is technically simpler due to the absence of non-trivial SHB structures and will be addressed in the next subsection. On the other hand, more preparations are needed to treat the second case. Combining Theorem
4.5
and Corollary
4.12
, we then obtain Theorem
1.3
.

4.1
On the open stratum

Suppose
\Phi=0
, so that
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is a polystable vector bundle. In this case,

S_{+}=\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}=H^{0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E)\otimes K_{X})

and the Kuranishi map
\kappa
restricted to
S_{+}
is the identity (cf. (
3.9
)). For a harmonic metric
h
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},0)
, we denote the subgroup of the automorphism group
G
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},0)
that fixes
h
by
K(h)
or simply by
K
whenever no confusions arise. It is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
whose Lie algebra is given by
iH_{0}(h)
, where

H_{0}(h):=\{v\in\mathcal{H}^{0}:v^{*_{h}}=v\}.

(4.3)

As
G
acts transitively by pulling back on the set of harmonic metrics of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},0)
, by Cartan decomposition we may parametrize the set of harmonic metrics by
H_{0}(h)
. Under Assumption
1
, we have
(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})=(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
.

Start with an arbitrary harmonic metric
h_{0}
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},0)
. Consider the
G
-representation
V=\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}
, equipped with an inner product induced from
h_{0}
so that
K(h_{0})
acts unitarily. Recall that we assume
(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})
is stable. By Proposition
3.8
,
\varphi
is a stable vector in
V
. Hence, there exists
g\in G
such that the norm of
g\cdot\varphi
is minimized along the
G
-orbit of
\varphi
. Let
h=g^{*}h_{0}
, i.e.
h(v,w)=h_{0}(gv,gw)
. To make the notations more concise, below we shall adopt the abbreviations
H_{0}=H_{0}(h)
and we denote the
h
-hermitian adjoint simply by
\varphi^{*}=\varphi^{*_{h}}
.

Define the Kempf-Ness functional
\ell_{\varphi}:H_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\ell_{\varphi}(g):=||e^{g}\varphi e^{-g}||^{2}_{h}=i\int_{X}\text{tr}\big[e^{g}\varphi e^{-g}(e^{g}\varphi e^{-g})^{*}\big]=i\int_{X}\text{tr}\big[\varphi(e^{-2g}\varphi^{*}e^{2g})\big].

(4.4)

By construction and the Kempf-Ness theorem (
[
12
]
),
\ell_{\varphi}
attains its unique minimum at
0
. Hence, its Jacobian
D\ell_{\varphi}
vanishes and the Hessian
H(\ell_{\varphi})
is non-degenerate at the origin. For
g,v\in H_{0}
, we write

v_{g}:=D_{g}\text{exp}^{2}(v)=\dfrac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}e^{2(g+tv)}\in\mathcal{H}^{0}.

(4.5)

The Jacobian of
\ell_{\varphi}
is computed as follow:

\displaystyle-iD_{g}\ell_{\varphi}(v)

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\int_{X}\text{tr}\big[\varphi(e^{2(g+tv)})^{-1}\varphi^{*}e^{2(g+tv)}\big]

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\text{tr}\big[\varphi(-e^{-2g}v_{g}e^{-2g})\varphi^{*}e^{2g}+\varphi e^{-2g}\varphi^{*}v_{g}\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\text{tr}\big(e^{-2g}\varphi^{*}e^{2g}\varphi+\varphi e^{-2g}\varphi^{*}e^{2g}\big)e^{-2g}v_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\text{tr}[\varphi,e^{-2g}\varphi^{*}e^{2g}]e^{-2g}v_{g}.

(4.7)

In particular, letting
g=0
, we have

-iD_{0}\ell_{\varphi}(v)=2\int_{X}\text{tr}[\varphi,\varphi^{*}]v=0

(4.8)

for any
v\in H_{0}
.

The second variation can be computed as follow.

\displaystyle-i\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial w\partial v}\ell_{\varphi}(g)

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\int_{X}\text{tr}\big[\varphi,(e^{2(g+tw)})^{-1}\varphi^{*}e^{2(g+tw)}\big](e^{2(g+tw)})^{-1}v_{g+tw}

(4.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\text{tr}\bigg\{\big[\varphi,[e^{-2g}\varphi^{*}e^{2g},e^{-2g}w_{g}]\big]e^{-2g}v_{g}-\big[\varphi,e^{-2g}\varphi^{*}e^{2g}\big]\dfrac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}(e^{2(g+tw)})^{-1}v_{g+tw}\bigg\}.

(4.10)

Specializing at
0
, the second term vanishes because of (
4.8
). Therefore, we obtain

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
be a stable Higgs bundle whose underlying holomorphic vector bundle is polystable. Then there exists a unique harmonic metric
h
of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},0)
such that

1.

\int_{X}\text{tr}[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]v=0
for every
v\in H_{0}
, and

2.

for
w\in H_{0}
, if
\int_{X}\text{tr}[\varphi,[\varphi^{*_{h}},w]]v\equiv 0
for all
v\in H_{0}
, then
w=0
.

Henceforth, we fix the harmonic metric
h
to be the one obtained in Lemma
4.1
. Before we proceed to the proof the existence of the conformal limit of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
, we first fix some notation and setting. Let

p:\mathfrak{sl}(E)\to i\mathfrak{su}(E,h)

(4.11)

be the bundle morphism which fibrewise projects a traceless endomorphism to its hermitian part. Let

H_{1}:=H_{0}^{\perp}\subset L^{2}_{k}(i\mathfrak{su}(E,h))

be the
L^{2}
orthogonal complement in
L^{2}_{k}
-completion of
\Omega^{0}(i\mathfrak{su}(E,h))
for some sufficiently large
k
. The two spaces of
\Omega^{0}(i\mathfrak{su}(E,h))
and
\Omega^{2}(i\mathfrak{su}(E,h))
are identified via the Hodge star. This identification extends to their
L^{2}_{k}
-completions and preserves the harmonic forms.

As
\Phi=0
, we will set
h_{R}=h
for all
R>0
. We then obtain the following family of connections

\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{E}^{h}+\hbar^{-1}\varphi+\hbar R^{2}\varphi^{*}.

(4.12)

The operator (
4.2
) is reduced to

N(f,R):=\overline{\partial}_{E}(e^{-2f}(\partial^{h}_{E}e^{2f}))+R^{2}[\varphi,e^{-2f}\varphi^{*}e^{2f}].

(4.13)

First, we notice the following

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
v\in H_{0}
,
\int_{X}\text{tr}\ p(i\overline{\partial}_{E}(e^{-2f}\partial^{h}_{E}e^{2f}))v=0
.

Proof.

For any
A\in\Omega^{1}(\text{End}(E))
, we notice that

\displaystyle 2\int_{X}\text{tr}\ p(\overline{\partial}_{E}A)v

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\text{tr}\ (\overline{\partial}_{E}A+(\overline{\partial}_{E}A)^{*})v

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\text{tr}\ [\overline{\partial}_{E}(Av)+(\overline{\partial}_{E}(vA))^{*}]

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\overline{\partial}_{E}\text{tr}\ Av+\overline{\int_{X}\overline{\partial}_{E}\text{tr}\ vA}

\displaystyle=0.

∎

We now arrive at the most important outcome of the above setup.

Proposition 4.3
.

There exists an open neighborhood
U
of
0
in
H_{1}
and a smooth map
g:U\to H_{0}
such that
g(0)=0
and for any
v\in H_{0}
,

\int_{X}\text{tr}\ p\bigg(i[\varphi,e^{-2(f+g(f))}\varphi^{*}e^{2(f+g(f))}]\bigg)v=0.

Proof.

Using the trace pairing, we regard the term
[\varphi,e^{-2f}\varphi^{*}e^{2f}]
as a dual element of
H_{0}
. More precisely, define a map
n:H_{1}\oplus H_{0}\to H_{0}^{\vee}
as follows: for
(f,g)\in H_{1}\oplus H_{0}
and

,

n(f,g)(v):=\int_{X}\text{tr}\ p\bigg(i[\varphi,e^{-2(f+g)}\varphi^{*}e^{2(f+g)}]\bigg)v.

(4.14)

Notice that
n(0,0)=0
by Lemma
4.1
. Next, we compute the differential at the origin and along the
H_{0}
-direction: for
w\in H_{0}
,

\displaystyle D_{(0,0)}n(w)(v)

\displaystyle=2\int_{X}\text{tr}\ p(i[\varphi,[\varphi^{*},w]])v

\displaystyle=-2\text{Im}\int_{X}\text{tr}\ [\varphi,[\varphi^{*},w]]v

\displaystyle=-2\int_{X}\text{tr}\ [\varphi,[\varphi^{*},w]]v.

By the second part of Lemma
4.1
,
D_{(0,0)}n
is non-singular. Hence, by the implicit function theorem, we obtain a map
g:U\to H_{0}
, for some open neighborhood
U
in
H_{1}
containing
0
, such that
g(0)=0
and
n(f,g(f))\equiv 0
.
∎

Define the map

N^{\prime}(f,R):=p(iN(f+g(f),R)),

where
f\in H_{1}
and
g:U\to H_{0}
is the map obtained in the above proposition. The image of
N^{\prime}
lies in the Hodge dual of
H_{1}
. Next, we compute the differential of
N^{\prime}
at
(0,0)
along the
H_{1}
-direction:

dN^{\prime}_{(0,0)}(f)=2p\big(i\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial^{h}_{E}(f+D_{0}g(f))\big)=2i\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial^{h}_{E}f

since
D_{0}g(f)\in\mathcal{H}^{0}
and
i\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial^{h}_{E}f
is
h
-hermitian. As the Laplacian
i\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial^{h}_{E}
has index zero with trivial kernel on
H_{1}
, by the implicit function theorem, we have a family
f_{R}\in H_{1}
with

for small

, such that

p(iN(f_{R}+g(f_{R}),R))\equiv 0.

We notice that
iN(f_{R}+g(f_{R}),R)
is
(e^{f_{R}+g(f_{R})})^{*}h
-hermitian. To see that

for small

, we use the following

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be two hermitian inner products on a finite dimensional vector space

that induce the same volume form. Let
A
be the
h_{1}
-hermitian endomorphism such that
h_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)=h_{1}(A\cdot,\cdot)
. Write
\text{Herm}_{0}(V,h_{i})
to be the space of traceless
h_{i}
-hermitian endomorphisms,
i=1,2
, and

p_{A}:\text{Herm}_{0}(V,h_{2})\to\text{Herm}_{0}(V,h_{1})

the projection map with respect to the inner product on
\text{End}(V)
induced from
h_{1}
.
If
||A||_{h_{1}}
is sufficiently small, then
p_{A}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
U
be a neighborhood around
0
of
\text{Herm}_{0}(V,h_{1})
such that if

then
h_{B}:=h_{1}(B\cdot,\cdot)
is a hermitian inner product. Let
*_{h_{B}}
be the conjugation with respect to the metric
h_{B}
. It follows that
\text{Herm}_{0}(V,h_{B})
is the fixed point set of

and
\text{Herm}_{0}:=\{(f,B)\in\text{End}_{0}(V)\times U:f\in\text{Herm}_{0}(V,h_{B})\}

.

Consider the bundle morphism
p_{\cdot}:\text{Herm}_{0}\to\text{Herm}_{0}(V,h_{1})
. The result then follows from continuity and that at

is the identity.
∎

Now, if
k
is sufficiently large, then
N(f_{R}+g(f_{R}),R)\in L^{2}_{k-2}(\mathfrak{su}(E,h)\otimes\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}X)\subset C^{0}(\mathfrak{su}(E,h)\otimes\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}X)

. This proves

.

For a stable Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
whose underlying holomorphic vector bundle

4.2
On polystable SHBs with pairwise distinct stable summands

In this subsection, we assume the stabilizer of

is abelian, i.e. the stable summands

in (

) are pairwise non-isomorphic. For

, where
v_{jb|ia}\in\Omega^{p,q}(\text{Hom}(E^{i}_{a},E^{j}_{b}))

, we first embed

into

Consider the finite dimensional vector space

spanned by the components

and
\varphi_{jb|ia}
. Note that

acts on the whole

, it is also closed in

. Hence,

is a
G
-stable vector in

. In the following, we will use the notation introduced in Section

.

Next, we are going to introduce the following procedure:

Start with

(i)

u_{0}=u=(\beta,\varphi)
,

(ii)

G_{0}=G
,

(iii)

c_{-1}=0\in\mathbb{Q}
,

(iv)

.

Suppose we have determined

,
G_{i+1}
,
c_{i}
,

and
S_{i}
for

. In the

Let

Here,

-polystable. Replace the metric

Lastly, we find some

The following is the first main technical input for the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.6

Suppose

is stable. Then the iterative procedure described above is well-defined and terminates at the

Corollary 4.12
.

The
\hbar
-conformal limit of
(\overline{\partial}_{u},\Phi_{u})\in S_{+}
is
D_{0}=\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial^{h}_{E}+\hbar^{-1}\Phi_{u}+\hbar\Phi^{*}

Proof.

Consider

By Theorem
4.11

\text{im}N^{\prime}\perp H_{0}
. Differentiating at

-direction, we obtain

We claim that
\ker D_{(0,0)}N^{\prime}=\{0\}
. Suppose

\big(p(iD_{u}^{\prime\prime}D^{\prime}f)\big)_{0}=p(iD^{\prime\prime}D^{\prime}f_{0})=0.

, so

3
]

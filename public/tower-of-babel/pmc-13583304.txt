NECTIN4-Mediated Immune Evasion in APOBEC3-Driven Neoantigen-Rich Bladder Tumors

Dhanusha Yesudhas1, Bilal Lone1, Ecem Unal1, Arup Chakraborty1, Ayse Keskus2, Jaimie Ryou1, Kelly Butler1, Tania Colon Aquino1, Abbas Yousefi-Rad1, Weiming Yang3, Lisa Jenkins3, Raju Chelluri4, Elias Chandran1, Vladimir Valera Romero4, Salah Boudjadi1, Sandeep Gurram4, Andrea Apolo1, Rouf Banday1

1Genitourinary Malignancies Branch, Center for Cancer Research, National Cancer Institute, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, United States

2Cancer Data Science Laboratory, Center for Cancer Research, National Cancer Institute, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, United States

3Laboratory of Cell Biology, Mass Spectrometry Resource, Center for Cancer Research, National Cancer Institute, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, United States

4Urological Oncology Branch, Center for Cancer Research, National Cancer Institute, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, United States

Background: APOBEC3 mutagenesis generates abundant neoantigens that should enhance immune recognition, yet many APOBEC3-driven bladder cancers remain T-cell poor and resistant to immune checkpoint blockade. The tumor-intrinsic mechanisms underlying this immune–neoantigen paradox remain poorly understood.

Methods: We integrated copy-number, transcriptomic, neoantigen, single-cell, and spatial profiling data from public and in-house bladder cancer cohorts (∼600 human tumors) to identify genomic determinants of immune exclusion. Candidate mechanisms were evaluated using molecular, proteomic, imaging, and functional studies in tumor-cell and syngeneic bladder cancer models.

Results: We identified a recurrent neoantigen-high/CD8-low tumor state associated with chromosome 1q23.3 amplification, present in 17% bladder tumors and enriched for APOBEC3 mutational signatures. This tumor subset exhibited suppression of antigen-presentation, cytokine, and immune-engagement programs. NECTIN4 within the 1q23.3 locus was the dominant predictor of immune–neoantigen discordance and reduced T-cell infiltration. Functionally, NECTIN4 promoted a T-cell-poor microenvironment and conferred resistance to PD-1 blockade in immunogenic bladder cancer models. Mechanistically, NECTIN4 activated a DDR1–SHP2 signaling axis that suppressed STAT1 phosphorylation, resulting in reduced interferon-responsive cytokine production, antigen presentation, and immune engagement.

Antibody-mediated NECTIN4 blockade reversed immune suppression and inhibited tumor growth in vivo. Long- and short-read sequencing, genomic timing, and breakpoint analyses linked APOBEC3 mutagenesis to NECTIN4 amplification events, supporting a model in which APOBEC3 activity promotes immune-evasive clones.

Conclusion: NECTIN4-driven STAT1 suppression represents a reversible mechanism of immune evasion in APOBEC3-driven bladder tumors and supports combining NECTIN4-targeted therapy with immune checkpoint blockade.

Urinary Tumor DNA is Prognostic for Recurrence in Patients with BCG-Unresponsive Disease Receiving Cretostimogene Grenadenorepvec on BOND-003 Cohort C

Colin P.N. Dinney1, Roger Li2, Mark Tyson3, David McConkey1, Sharada Mokkapati4, Brian C Mazzarella5,6, Kevin G. Phillips6, Trevor G. Levin6, Sarah Donatelli7, Robert S. Svatek7

1Department of Urology, University of Rochester Medical Center, Rochester, United States

2Department of Genitourinary Oncology, Moffitt Cancer Center, Tampa, United States

3Department of Urology, Mayo Clinic, Arizona, United States

4Department of Urology, The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center, Houston, United States

5Urology Austin, Austin, United States

6Convergent Genomics, South San Francisco, United States

7CG Oncology, Dallas, United States

Background: Urinary tumor DNA (utDNA) profiling uses next-generation sequencing to detect bladder cancer-associated mutations and can prognosticate relapse. Cretostimogene is an oncolytic immunotherapy that replicates in/lyses bladder cancer cells with Rb-E2F pathway alterations, releasing tumor and viral antigens that trigger immune activation, amplified by GM-CSF transgene. It has been studied as monotherapy in BOND-003 in patients with BCG-unresponsive Non-Muscle Invasive Bladder Cancer. Associations between utDNA and cretostimogene response were evaluated.

Methods: Patients who received >1 dose of cretostimogene and baseline and week 13 utDNA testing were included. utDNA was analyzed with UroAmp with high specificity classification. High-grade recurrence-free survival (HG-RFS) was evaluated with Kaplan-Meier, censored at last surveillance cystoscopy or 24 months. Patients were classified as molecular responder vs. non-responders based on utDNA change from baseline to week 13; hazard ratios (HRs) were estimated using a Cox proportional hazards model.

Results: Baseline and week 13 utDNA were available in 60 (52.2%) and 48 (41.7%) of 115 patients; 28 (46.7%) baseline samples were utDNA positive and 32 (53.3%) negative. Baseline utDNA was not associated with HG-RFS (p=0.81). Week 13 urine was utDNA positive in 15 (31.3%) patients and associated with lower HG-RFS compared to those with negative week 13 utDNA (n=33;60% vs 30% HG-RFS at 24 months; p=0.019). Molecular responders (n=33) had significantly improved HG-RFS compared to non-responders (n=14; HR 2.7, 95% CI 1.2-6.2, p=0.02).

Conclusion: After cretostimogene induction, a negative utDNA at week 13 as a single value or relative to baseline is associated with a significantly more favorable outcome. Findings may inform future trials and cretostimogene use in a risk-adapted treatment paradigm.

Distinct clonal dynamics and interactions within the microenvironment near tumor stroma interfaces in rare histologic variants of bladder cancer

Luisa Quezada-Ziesse1, Real Sumayya Abdul Sattar1, Pratyush Venkatesh1, Mahdi Hemmati Ghavshough1, Disha Patel1, Vignesh T Packiam1, Saum Ghodoussipour1, Subhajyoti De2

1Rutgers Cancer Institute, New Brunswick, NJ, United States

2Rutgers Cancer Institute, New Brunswick, NJ, United States

Background: Rare histologicvariants of bladder carcinoma carry a worse prognosis than pure urothelial carcinoma (PUC), but their molecular determinants are poorly understood. We developed a computational framework to characterize tumor-stroma-immune dynamics at tumor borders from spatial transcriptomic data.

Methods: We profiled bladder carcinoma from 9 patients undergoing radical cystectomy on the Visium platform: 3 PUC, 1 small cell neuroendocrine, 2 primitive neuroectodermal, and 3 squamous cell carcinoma. CALISTA (Classification of Activity of Local Interface of Stroma and Tumor-signatures using spatial Transcriptomics Analysis) identifies active tumor-stroma interfaces, integrating hallmark pathway scoring, clonal analysis, and cell-cell signaling into a unified workflow.

Results: CALISTA classified tumor borders as active or passive by cancer hallmark activity. Active interfaces were enriched for myofibroblast-like cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and M2-polarized tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) in pro-tumorigenic signaling. CAF-driven FGF signaling spanned all subtypes, escalating with aggressiveness, while TAM polarization was histology-dependent: squamous variants showed an immunosuppressive M2 phenotype, a potential immunotherapy target. State transitions, modulated by MK, PDGF, and TGFβ signaling, correlated with intratumor clonal heterogeneity within and between subtypes.

CALISTA revealed histology-specific differences in TME composition, including tertiary lymphoid structures; node-positive tumors showed reduced clonal heterogeneity. Critically, dominant subclone signatures at interfaces were detectable in cell-free DNA from paired urine, enabling non-invasive monitoring of clonal dynamics.

Conclusion: These findings reveal divergent microenvironment remodeling across histologies and establish CALISTA as a tool for decoding TME spatial biology, identifying therapeutic vulnerabilities, and linking tissue dynamics to non-invasive biomarkers.

A patient-derived organoid platform for integrative molecular and functional profiling of aggressive bladder cancer

Viviane Tschan1,2, Frederick Heuzeroth1,2, Michele Garioni1,2, Jing Wang1,2, Romuald Parmentier1,2, Luca Roma3, Martin Egger4, Heike Pueschel4, Tatjana Vlajnic3, Salvatore Piscuoglio3,4, Gregor Hutter2,5, Hans-Helge Seifert4, Cyrill A. Rentsch4, Lukas Bubendorf3, Clémentine Le Magnen1,2

3Institute of Medical Genetics and Pathology, University Hospital Basel, Basel, Switzerland

Background: Effective biomarkers and targets are needed to enable personalized medicine and increase treatment efficacy in aggressive forms of bladder cancer (BC). Here, we established a living biobank of patient-derived organoid (PDO) models and combined molecular profiling with drug screening to identify novel therapeutic vulnerabilities.

Methods: 192 BC patient samples – from biopsies, cystectomy, or resections – were used to generate PDOs, which were characterized by H&E, IHC, immunofluorescence, whole-exome sequencing (WES), scRNA-seq and spatial single-cell multiplexed fluorescence (PhenoCycler). Over 2,000 compounds were screened on PDO lines at 1 μM; compounds reducing viability by >50% were considered hits. Hits were further tested in dose-response screens across a 10 pM–10 μM range, including C-max concentrations.

Results: Twenty-two long-term PDO lines (11.4%) were successfully established, representing major molecular and histological BC subtypes, including urothelial carcinoma, sarcomatoid, and neuroendocrine BC. PDOs retained alterations in genes mutated in their parental patient tumors and commonly altered in BC (e.g. TP53, KMT2C, ARID1A). ScRNA-seq and PhenoCycler analyses revealed pronounced model- and subtype-specific heterogeneity and distinct cellular neighborhoods. High-throughput screening identified 148 hits out of 2,016 compounds tested (7.3%), highlighting vulnerabilities in DNA synthesis, microtubules, histone deacetylases, tyrosine kinases, and others. Subsequent dose-response validation across ten PDO models, including a benign control, enabled comparison with standard-of-care therapies and revealed distinct responder and non-responder profiles for individual compounds. Integration of drug-response data with WES and scRNA-seq will further link model-specific gene expression to pathway susceptibility.

Conclusion: Our PDO platform enables discovery of subtype-specific therapeutic vulnerabilities and molecular predictors of drug response.

Bladder resident macrophages drive sex-specific immunity following carcinogen exposure

Andrew Houston1,2, Yanni Yu1,2, Lauren Folgosa Cooley1,2, Yanyang Li1,2, Joseph Podojil3, Joshua Meeks1,2

1Department of Urology, Northwestern University, Chicago, United States

2Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Genetics, Northwestern University, Chicago, United States

3Department of Microbiology and Immunology, Northwestern University, Chicago, United States

Background: The divergent factors driving bladder tumor initiation in males and females remain unknown. There is increasing evidence of gender-specific immunity affecting both innate and adaptive immune cells, resulting in differential responses to immunotherapy and regulation of inflammation. The effect of sex-specific immune responses on tumorigenesis within the bladder have not been investigated.

Methods: Using the N-butyl-N-(4-hydroxybutyl)-nitrosamine (BBN) mouse model, we investigated the bladder-resident immune cells in early BBN-exposed male and female mice. After isolating CD45+ immune populations, we performed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to identify the transcriptional programs driving a response to BBN. Complementary flow cytometry and cytokine profiling allowed for a robust investigation. Our scRNAseq data revealed that distinct clusters of macrophages emerge after 2 weeks of BBN treatment.

Results: Males display a significant upregulation of pro-fibrotic TLF+ (Timd4, Lyve1, Folr2) expressing macrophages, while females display an equal proportion of all other macrophage subsets. This pro-fibrotic TLF+ population showed increased expression of immunosuppressive genes, including Mmp9, Cd163, and Mrc1. Flow cytometry of the bladder tissue revealed that macrophages (CD45+, CD11b+, Ly6c+, F4/80+) were increased in males compared to females.

Furthermore, male bladders demonstrated a significant increase in IL-1β-producing macrophages. LUMINEX profiling revealed IP-10 (CXCL10), IL-1β, and IL-6 were significantly increased in the male bladders after 1-3 days of BBN exposure.

Conclusion: This data suggests that male macrophages demonstrate more tumorigenic inflammatory properties after only 2 weeks of carcinogen exposure. This male-specific increase in pro-inflammatory cytokines within days of BBN exposure indicates that there could be sex-specific handling of the carcinogen. Further investigations will reveal the distinct immune cell interactions and mechanisms modulating this response.

Background: In the TOMBOLA trial, adjuvant atezolizumab after neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) and radical cystectomy (RC) in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) was guided by ctDNA detection but understanding of response mechanisms remain limited. As NAC and ICI sensitivity are linked to tumour-intrinsic and immune features, we used spatial transcriptomics (ST) to define tumour microenvironment (TME) programs associated with NAC and ICI response, and ctDNA recurrence-free survival (RFS).

Methods: Baseline TURBT specimens (n=169) and residual RC tumours (n=53) from 178 TOMBOLA patients were using CosMx Human 6k RNA. Cells were annotated using BANKSY/Scanpy, spatial niches with scNiche, and spatially variable receptor–ligand interactions (svRLIs) with SPIDER. Bulk RNA-seq, WGS and longitudinal ddPCR ctDNA data were integrated.

Results: We identified 22 cell types and five recurrent spatial niches: basq, lum, lum_infiltrated (lum_inf), macro_tcell and plasma_caf. Niche abundances correlated spatially with independently extracted svRLI patterns, both associated with clinical endpoints. Basq niche associated with reduced NAC response (p=0.039), whereas luminal and macro_tcell-enriched states associated with higher NAC response (lum_inf + lum high vs basq low: p=0.023; macro_tcell high vs plasma_caf low: p=0.038). Basq associated with inferior ctDNA RFS (p=0.013), while macro_tcell, plasma_caf, and lum_inf showed directionally improved ctDNA RFS after pathological high-risk adjustment (p=0.067). ICI response associated with macro_tcell niche (p=0.033). ST-and bulk-derived features overlapped but resolved more granular TME heterogeneity.

Conclusion: ST identified reproducible niches and svRLI programs that convergently captured clinically relevant TME architecture. Basq niche marked reduced NAC response and shorter ctDNA RFS, whereas luminal and macro_tcell-enriched programs were linked to improved outcomes. This supports ST as a framework for biomarker discovery in MIBC.

Background: Detection of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) and urinary tumor DNA (utDNA) after neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) has been shown to predict treatment response and outcome in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC). Pathological complete response (pCR) is associated with improved outcomes and serves as a proxy for treatment response. We aimed to incorporate ctDNA and utDNA into predictive models to improve prediction of pCR and delineate biomarker models for use in future bladder preservation studies.

Methods: We analyzed urine and plasma samples from 167 patients with MIBC treated with NAC followed by radical cystectomy (RC). ctDNA and utDNA were assessed using a tumor-informed whole genome sequencing approach (TrueMRD, Veracyte) before and after NAC.

pCR was defined as ypT0N0M0. Gradient boosting models were trained on 70% of the cohort using clinical variables alone or combined with utDNA and ctDNA measures; performance (area under the precision-recall curve [AUCPR] and negative predictive value [NPV]) was evaluated in the remaining 30%. Associations with ctDNA-free survival were assessed using Kaplan-Meier analyses and Cox proportional hazards regression. Robustness was examined using Monte Carlo cross-validation.

Results: A multianalyte model integrating clinical parameters, baseline ctDNA level, ctDNA dynamics and utDNA status after NAC achieved an AUCPR of 0.91 and NPV of 0.77 for prediction of pCR. Patients predicted to achieve pCR had improved 1-yr ctDNA-free survival (HR = 0.07, 95% CI: 0.01-0.5, p < 0.001). Models utilizing clinical variables alone (AUCPR: 0.52, NPV: 0.47) or combined with either only ctDNA (AUCPR: 0.54, NPV: 0.57) or only utDNA (AUCPR: 0.78, NPV: 0.79) performed worse.

Conclusion: Integration of ctDNA and utDNA significantly improves prediction of pCR and enables a clinically meaningful stratification of patients. Our results support further independent evaluation of liquid biopsy-guided bladder-sparing strategies in MIBC.

Tumor Mutational Burden Predicts Survival and Treatment Response in Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor–Treated Urothelial Carcinoma: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Balázs Magyar1, Mohammed Altenni2, Anett Rancz3, Judit Vargha1, Péter Hegyi3, Péter Nyirády1, Tibor Szarvas1,4

1Department of Urology, Semmelweis University, Budapest, Hungary

2Department of Urology, Péterfy Sándor Street Hospital, Budapest, Hungary

3Centre for Translational Medicine, Semmelweis University, Budapest, Hungary

4Department of Medical Oncology, University of Duisburg-Essen, Essen, Germany

Background: Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have improved outcomes in advanced urothelial carcinoma (UC); however, only a subset of patients derive durable benefit. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) has emerged as a potential predictive biomarker, but available evidence remains heterogeneous, and individual studies are frequently underpowered.

Methods: We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies evaluating TMB in ICI-treated UC patients. MEDLINE, Embase, and CENTRAL were systematically searched without restrictions. Studies reporting overall survival (OS), progression-free survival, objective response rate (ORR), or pathological complete response (pCR) according to TMB status were eligible. Pooled effect estimates were calculated using random-effects models.

Results: After screening 4,737 database records and 1,609 additional publications identified through backward citation chasing, 92 studies underwent full-text review. Thirty studies contained data suitable for quantitative synthesis. Using a threshold of ≥10 mutations/Mb or equivalent, high TMB was associated with significantly improved OS in ICI-treated UC patients (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.54–0.68; p<0.01; I2=0%). High TMB was also associated with significantly improved treatment response, corresponding to a 62% higher likelihood of objective response (OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.27–0.52; p<0.01; I2=18.6%). Analyses using TMB as a continuous variable demonstrated consistent associations between increasing TMB and improved clinical outcomes across treatment settings.

Conclusion: TMB is a robust predictive biomarker of immunotherapy efficacy in UC. Patients with high TMB experience superior survival and treatment response following ICI therapy, supporting the integration of TMB into biomarker-driven treatment strategies and future prospective validation efforts.

External validation of the Computational Histology Artificial Intelligence (CHAI) progression biomarker in non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer: a prospective Swedish national registry study

Per-Uno Malmström1, Anca Dragomir2, Tammer Hemdan1,3, Haochen Zhang4, Snehal Sonawane4,5, Erin L Stewart4, Trevor J Royce4,6, Anirudh Joshi4, Viswesh Krishna4, Ashish Kamat7, Truls Gårdmark8

1Department of Surgical Sciences, Urology, Uppsala University, Uppsala, Sweden

2Department of Immunology, Genetics and Pathology, Science for Life Laboratory, Uppsala University, Uppsala, Sweden

4Valar Labs, Paolo Alto, USA

8Department of Clinical Sciences, Danderyd Hospital, Karolinska Institute, Stockholm, Sweden

Background: Progression to muscle-invasive disease remains a major risk in high-risk NMIBC despite guideline-based stratification. As BCG shortages and new therapeutic options reshape treatment, accurate prognostic tools are increasingly needed. The CHAI platform’s deep-learning biomarker, derived from H&E whole-slide images, was previously validated in a 12-center international cohort. Here we present an independent external validation in a prospectively-collected national registry not used in prior studies.

Methods: We analyzed 635 BCG-treated NMIBC patients from the Swedish National Register of Urinary Bladder Cancer (SNRUBC; Stockholm, Linköping, Uppsala; 2007–2020). The locked biomarker generated a continuous score dichotomized at a pre-defined cutpoint. The primary endpoint, muscle-invasive bladder cancer progression-free survival (MIBC-PFS), was assessed by Cox regression, unadjusted and adjusted for EAU risk group, T-stage, grade, CIS, and institution; discrimination was compared against EAU risk grouping using time-dependent area under the curve (tdAUC).

Results: Over a median follow-up of 66.9 months (60-month MIBC-PFS 93.4%), the CHAI biomarker was significantly associated with progression (HR 3.18, 95% CI 1.25–8.09; p=0.015; 5/29 events in CHAI-high vs 37/606 in CHAI-low) and remained independent after adjustment (HR 3.12, 95% CI 1.14–8.53; p=0.027). EAU risk grouping was not significantly associated with MIBC-PFS, for high versus intermediate/low risk (HR 3.00, 95% CI 0.72–12.50; p=0.13; 33/428 vs 2/75 events) or very high versus high risk (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.30–1.54; p=0.36; 7/132 vs 33/428 events), and CHAI showed higher discrimination than EAU by tdAUC (0.63 vs. 0.49 at 12 months).

Conclusion: In a national registry, the CHAI biomarker independently predicted muscle-invasive progression and outperformed EAU risk stratification, supporting its use in NMIBC. Requiring only the routine TURBT H&E slide, CHAI is readily deployable in existing workflows.

Bladder cancer is very much in your head: How genetic and environmental regulation of smoking behavior affects bladder cancer risk

Oscar Florez-Vargas1, Brenen Papenberg1, Wusheng Yan1, Maxwell Hogshead1, Michael Levin2, Atuahene Adu-Gyamfi1, Lambertus Kiemeney3, Nuria Malats4, Lars Dyrskjøt5, Debra Silverman1, Nathaniel Rothman1, Scott Damrauer2, Jeffrey Damrauer6,7, Stella Koutros1, Ludmila Prokunina-Olsson1

1Division of Cancer Epidemiology and Genetics, National Cancer Institute, Rockville, United States

2Corporal Michael Crescenz VA Medical Center and Perelman School of Medicine, University of Pennsylvania, Philadelphia, United States

3Department for Health Evidence, Radboud University Medical Center, Nijmegen, Netherlands

4Genetic and Molecular Epidemiology Group, Spanish National Cancer Research Centre (CNIO) and CIBERONC, Madrid, Spain

5Department of Clinical Medicine, Aarhus University, Aarhus, Denmark

6Lineberger Comprehensive Cancer Center, University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill, United States

7Department of Medicine, Division of Oncology, University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill, United States

Background: We recently conducted a multi-ancestry genome-wide meta-analysis including 32,470 bladder cancer cases and 1,753,462 controls. We identified 70 independent risk loci, 43 of which are novel (Prokunina-Olsson et al, in press).

Methods: Genetic fine-mapping and association analysis, bioinformatics analysis, cell models, mRNA stability reporter assays, minigene splicing assays, Oxford Nanopore sequencing.

Results: We uncovered a genetic link between bladder cancer risk and the known smoking-associated locus at chromosome 15q25.1, implicating germline regulation of smoking behavior as a driver of bladder cancer susceptibility. Specifically, the novel variant (rs71581744) co-localized with CHRNA3 expression in specific brain areas, affected mRNA stability, and was associated with muscle-invasive disease among current smokers. Together, the novel CHRNA3-rs71581744 and the previously known CHRNA5-rs16969968 (D398N) affect the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (α5-nAChR) sensitivity, influencing smoking intensity and cessation, thereby increasing carcinogen exposure. Functional analyses further demonstrated that exposure to smoking altered CHRNA5 regulation directly. We identified five alternative CHRNA5-exon 5 splice isoforms, in which the fragment with rs16969968 was excluded. In bladder and lung cancer cell models, continuous long-term (>1 month), but not short-term (2 days) exposure to cigarette smoke condensate significantly altered splice isoform abundance, independent of germline variation. These changes likely modify α5-nAChR function, reducing nicotine sensitivity and reinforcing dependence and increased smoking intensity.

Conclusion: Together, our findings reveal a dual mechanism in which germline variation and smoke-induced molecular alterations converge on nicotinic receptor pathways to influence smoking behavior and increase the risk of muscle-invasive bladder cancer.

Sex-Dependent Regulation of CD8 T Cells by MGAT5 in Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer

Shirley Zhi1, Igor Moskalev1, Bella Erstad1, Joshua Scurll1, Peter Black1, Morgan Roberts1

1Department of Urologic Sciences, University of British Columbia, Vancouver, Canada

Background: Sex significantly affects bladder cancer incidence and outcomes. Although the biological basis of these disparities remains incompletely understood, sex-dependent differences in anti-tumour immunity have emerged as an important factor. CD8 T cells are key mediators of anti-tumour immunity; however, the mechanisms that constrain their function are incompletely understood. One such pathway involves alterations in protein glycosylation. The activity of N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase-V (MGAT5), an enzyme that mediates complex N-glycan branching, has been shown to regulate T cell function in autoimmunity and chronic viral infections; however, its role in regulating anti-tumour CD8 T cell responses remains poorly defined.

Methods: MGAT5 activity and phenotypes of circulating CD8 T cells from patients with MIBC, and from the blood and bladders of tumour-bearing mice, were assessed by flow cytometry.

Wild-type and Mgat5 deficient CD8 T cells were subjected to an in vitro model of T cell exhaustion. The sex-dependent role of MGAT5 activity in T cells in MIBC was also assessed in vivo using chimeric mice generated by mixed bone marrow transplantation. Bladder cancer was then induced using a carcinogen (BBN), and morbidity was assessed over time.

Results: In both mice and humans, sex affected MGAT5 activity in circulating CD8 T cells, with females exhibiting decreased MGAT5 activity in memory cells compared with males.

Upregulation of MGAT5 activity was also found to be a feature of tumour-infiltrating CD8 T cells in murine models of MIBC. In addition, Mgat5 deficiency resulted in a decrease in the development of an exhausted CD8 T cell phenotype in vitro. Finally, T cell-specific MGAT5 activity was associated with protection from death in male mice with carcinogen-induced bladder cancer.

Conclusion: Overall, our data suggest that sex-dependent regulation of MGAT5 activity in CD8 T cells may be an important factor affecting outcomes in bladder cancer.

Spatiotemporal profiling of B cell-associated T cell dynamics reveals T-cell niches associated with BCG response in patients with high-risk non-muscle invasive bladder cancer

Kartik Sachdeva1, Priyanka Yolmo1, Gwenaëlle Conseil1, Arnavaz Danesh2, Emine Begum Coskun3, Abdulhameed Abdulhamed3, Alanna Brewer4, Trevor Pugh2,5, David M. Berman1, D. Robert Siemens4, Madhuri Koti1,4

Background: Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) immunotherapy remains the standard treatment for high-risk non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), yet a significant proportion of patients fail to respond. Our previous findings demonstrated that treatment-naïve tumors from patients who experienced early recurrence or progression following adequate BCG therapy, exhibited increased density of B cell dominant exhausted peri-tumoral Tertiary Lymphoid Structures (TLS). PD-1+ B cells within the tumor microenvironment preferentially colocalized with regulatory T cell subsets. A comprehensive map of B cell associated T cell neighborhoods, and their functional states within TLS, remain poorly understood.

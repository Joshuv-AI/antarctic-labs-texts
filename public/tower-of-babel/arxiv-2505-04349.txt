Relative cohomological dimension of a relatively hyperbolic pair
Harsh Patil

Abstract
. We show that the relative cohomological dimension cd R ​ ( G , H ) \mathrm{cd}_{R}(G,H) of a relatively hyperbolic pair ( G , H ) (G,H) is always finite when G G does not contain R R -torsion. We also show that this dimension is preserved under quasi-isometries, provided that G G is torsion-free and the peripheral subgroup H H is unconstricted and of type F ∞ F_{\infty} . As a corollary of our methods, we compute cd ℤ ​ ( G , H ) \mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G,H) in several cases.



1.
Introduction

A relatively hyperbolic group is a finitely generated group equipped with a word metric which satisfies properties similar to the fundamental group of a cusped hyperbolic manifold. Relatively hyperbolic groups were introduced by Gromov in his monograph on hyperbolic groups
[
10
]
. Farb [Far94], in his PhD thesis, developed the theory of relative
hyperbolicity using an alternative definition, which is the one we work
with here. Farb’s definition (definition 2.5) is equivalent to that of
Gromov. Alternative characterizations were given by Bowditch
[
3
]
and Yaman
[
22
]
.
Examples of relatively hyperbolic groups include fundamental groups of cusped hyperbolic manifolds, non-cocompact lattices in Lie groups of real rank 1 and geometrically finite convergence groups acting on non-empty perfect
compact metric spaces
[
22
]
.
The problem of classifying relatively hyperbolic groups up to quasi-isometry has attracted significant attention. By a result of Schwartz
[
20
]
, non-uniform lattices of isometries of a rank-one symmetric space are known to be quasi-isometrically rigid: if a group
G
is quasi-isometric to a non-uniform lattice in a rank-one Lie group
\Gamma
, then
G
is a finite extension of a non-uniform lattice in
\Gamma
.
Drutu and Sapir
[
7
]
proved that if a group
G
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a finite collection of unconstricted subgroups (cf. below Theorem
1.7
for the definition of an unconstricted group) and
G^{\prime}
is another group quasi-isometric to
G
, then
G^{\prime}
is also relatively hyperbolic. Furthermore, every peripheral subgroup of
G^{\prime}
is quasi-isometric to some peripheral subgroup of
G
. Thus, the property of being relatively hyperbolic with respect to a fixed set of unconstricted peripheral subgroups is quasi-isometrically invariant. Groff
[
9
]
builds on this result to show that if two relatively hyperbolic groups are quasi-isometric, then the corresponding Bowditch boundaries are homeomorphic.

In this article, we study the relative cohomology
H^{i}(G,H;M)
of a relatively hyperbolic group
G
relative to its peripheral subgroup
H
with coefficients in a
G
-module
M
. Our primary motivation behind this is the following result by Manning and Wang
[
13
]
, which relates the Bowditch boundary
\partial_{B}(G,H)
of
G
with respect to
H
with the relative cohomology
H^{i}(G,H;RG)
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(G,H)
be a relatively hyperbolic pair, such that both
G
and
H
are of type
F_{\infty}
. Let
R
be any ring. Then, for every
i
, there is an isomorphism of
RG
-modules

H^{i}(G,H;RG)\cong\check{H}^{i-1}(\partial_{B}(G,H);R).

The right-hand side,
\check{H}^{i-1}(\partial_{B}(G,H);R)
, denotes the Čech cohomology of the Bowditch boundary (with coefficients in
R
), which is a topological invariant. Thus, Theorem
1.1
provides an algebraic approach to understanding the topology of the Bowditch boundary.
The main result of this article is as follows (see Section
2.4
for the definition of the
RG
-module
\Delta_{G/H}^{R}
):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a group and let
H<G
such that
G
is hyperbolic relative to
H
and such that
G
does not contain
R
-torsion. Then the
RG
-module
\Delta^{R}_{G/H}
admits a finite-length projective resolution of
RG
-modules in which each non-trivial term is finitely generated; i.e., it is a module of type
FP
over
RG
. Consequently,
\textrm{cd}_{R}(G,H)
is finite.

Recall that, for a given ring
R
and a group
G
,
G
is said to have
no
R
-torsion
if the order of any finite subgroup of
G
is invertible in
R
.

Remark 1.3
.

The relative cohomological dimension was already known to be finite under the assumption that the peripherals are all of type
FP
(cf.
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
). We relax this condition in Theorem
1.2
and only demand that
G
contain no
R
-torsion.

The following corollary is an easy consequence of Theorem
1.2
:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
G
be a torsion-free group such that
G
is hyperbolic relative to a subgroup
H
. Then,
G
has finite cohomological dimension if and only if
H
does.

We also prove the following corollary, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.7
:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
G
be a torsion-free group and
H<G
be a subgroup such that
G
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to
H
. Then,

\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G,H)=\sup\{i|H^{i}(G,H;RG)\neq 0\}
.

Remark 1.6
.

Again, the conclusion of Corollary
1.5
was already known to be true under the assumption that all peripheral subgroups are of type
FP

[
13
, Prop. 2.24]
. We show that the conclusion holds for a much larger collection of relatively hyperbolic pairs.

Finally, we prove the following theorem that allows us to distinguish certain relatively hyperbolic groups up to quasi-isometry:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be two quasi-isometric groups that are relatively hyperbolic with respect to subgroups
H<G
and
H^{\prime}<G^{\prime}
, respectively. Assume further that neither
G
nor
G^{\prime}
contain
R
-torsion. Assume that both
H
and
H^{\prime}
are unconstricted and of type
F_{\infty}
. Then,
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G,H)=\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
.

A group is said to be
unconstricted
if one of its asymptotic cones is without cut-points. Any group that satisfies a law is unconstricted. For more examples cf.
[
7
, pg. 6]
.

Remark 1.8
.

The cohomological dimension
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G,H)
is not an entirely new quasi-isometry invariant for relatively hyperbolic groups. Rather, its quasi-isometry invariance follows from the fact that the Bowditch boundaries of two quasi-isometric pairs are homeomorphic, in combination with Theorem
1.1
. While describing the Bowditch boundary entirely or calculating the relative cohomology may seem difficult, we demonstrate with examples that
\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G,H)
can be computed explicitly in some cases.

Throughout the paper, we restrict ourselves to the case of a single peripheral subgroup. The analogous statements for multiple peripheral subgroups are stated in Section 6.

2.1.
Relative Hyperbolicity

We recall some standard notions from the theory of relatively hyperbolic groups.
The interested reader can refer to
[
8
]
,
[

]
, and

]

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Let
S
be some finite generating set of
G
. Let
\Gamma(G,S)
denote the Cayley graph of
G
with respect to
S
. Assigning each edge unit length, the graph
\Gamma(G,S)
becomes a metric space equipped with the path metric.

We form a new graph
\hat{\Gamma}(G,S,H)
as follows: Start with the Cayley graph
\Gamma(G,S)
and for each coset
gH
of
H
add a vertex
v(gH)
and an edge of length
\frac{1}{2}
between
v(gH)
and each element
gh
of
gH
.

This new graph is called the
coned-off Cayley graph
of the pair
(G,H)
with respect to the generating set
S
.
\hat{\Gamma}(G,S,H)
is equipped with the path metric, which we denote by
\hat{d}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A group
G
is said to be
weakly relatively hyperbolic
with respect to a subgroup
H
if the coned-off Cayley graph
\hat{\Gamma}(G,S,H)
is Gromov hyperbolic.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
H
be a subgroup.
Let
c\colon[0,l]\rightarrow\Gamma(G,S)
be a path in the Cayley graph of
G
. Its image
\hat{c}
in the coned-off graph
\hat{\Gamma}(G,S,H)
is constructed by replacing every maximal subpath
c|_{[a,b]}
all of whose vertices lie in the same coset
gH
with a path of length
1
from
c(a)
to
c(b)
passing through the cone point
v(gH)
. Let
l^{\prime}
denote the length of
\hat{c}
.
c
is said to be a
relative geodesic
if
\hat{c}
is a geodesic in
\hat{\Gamma}(G,S,H)
. The length of
\hat{c}
is said to be the
relative length
of
c
. Let
T>1
. The path
c
is called a
T
-relative quasi-geodesic if
\hat{c}
is a
T
-quasi-geodesic, i.e.,

T^{-1}|t-t^{\prime}|\leq\hat{d}(\hat{c}(t),\hat{c}(t^{\prime}))\leq T|t-t^{\prime}|\quad\forall t,t^{\prime}\in[0,l^{\prime}]

A path
c
is said to be a
relative quasi-geodesic
if it is a
T
-relative quasi-geodesic for some
T>0
.

A path
c\colon[a,b]\rightarrow\Gamma(G,S)
is said to
travel in a coset
\gamma H
for less than
r
if for any maximal subsegment of
c
all of whose vertices lie in
\gamma H
, the endpoints are at a distance less than
r
. A path
c
is said to
travel in a coset
\gamma H
for more than
r
if there exists a subsegment of
c
with vertices in
\gamma H
such that its endpoints are at least at a distance
r
from each other.

Definition 2.4

(Bounded Coset Penetration)
.

Let
G
be a group and
H
be a subgroup. The pair
(G,H)
is said to satisfy the BCP (Bounded Coset Penetration) property if for every
T>0
there exists an
r=r_{BCP}(T)
such that for every pair of
T
-relative quasigeodesics
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
starting and ending at the same point in
\Gamma(G,S)
satisfy the following two conditions:

(1)

If
c_{1}
travels more than
r
in a coset, then
c_{2}
enters the same coset.

(2)

If
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
enter the same coset, then the entry points (respectively, exit points) of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are at most
r
distance apart.

Definition 2.5

(Relative Hyperbolicity)
.

A group is said to be relatively hyperbolic with respect to a subgroup
H
if it is weakly relatively hyperbolic and satisfies the BCP property. The pair
(G,H)
is then said to be a
relatively hyperbolic pair
.

2.2.
Quasi-isometries of Groups and Pairs

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two finitely generated groups and let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be subgroups of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, respectively. Let
S_{1}\subset G_{1}
and
S_{2}\subset G_{2}
be finite symmetric generating sets of

and
G_{2}
, respectively. Let

and

denote the respective word metrics on

and

.

Definition 2.6

(Quasi-isometry)
.

A map
q\colon G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}
is said to be a quasi-isometry if

(1)

there exist constants
A,B>0
such that

A^{-1}d_{S_{1}}(x,y)-B\leq d_{S_{2}}(q(x),q(y))\leq Ad_{S_{1}}(x,y)+B

for all

, and

(2)

there exists a constant

such that for every

, there exists

such that
d_{S_{2}}(q(x),y)\leq C
.

Following
[
12
]
, we define quasi-isometries of pairs.

Definition 2.7

(Quasi-isometry of pairs)
.

Let
q\colon G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}
be a quasi-isometry between groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Suppose, in addition, that there exist subgroups
H_{1}<G_{1}
and
H_{2}<G_{2}
, and a constant
M>0
such that the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

For any left coset
gH_{1}
of
H_{1}
, the set

\{g^{\prime}\in G_{2}\mid d^{\mathrm{Haus}}_{S_{2}}(q(gH_{1}),g^{\prime}H_{2})<M\}

is non-empty.

(2)

For any left coset
g^{\prime}H_{2}
of
H_{2}
, the set

\{g\in G_{1}\mid d^{\mathrm{Haus}}_{S_{2}}(q(gH_{1}),g^{\prime}H_{2})<M\}

is non-empty.

Then, we say that

is a quasi-isometry between the pairs
(G_{1},H_{1})
and
(G_{2},H_{2})
.

In Section
4
, we show that a quasi-isometry between two groups

and
G_{2}
, which are relatively hyperbolic with respect to unconstricted subgroups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, respectively, is automatically a quasi-isometry of pairs
(G_{1},H_{1})
and
(G_{2},H_{2})
.

2.3.
Bowditch Boundary

Let
(G,H)
be a relatively hyperbolic pair. Then, there exists a non-empty, perfect, metrizable compactum
M
such that
G
admits a convergence group action on
M
, and the action satisfies three additional properties:
M
consists only of conical limit points and bounded parabolic points, there is exactly one orbit of bounded parabolic points, and
the stabilizer of any bounded parabolic point is a conjugate of
H
.
Furthermore, if
M^{\prime}
is any other compactum that satisfies the above properties, then
M
and
M^{\prime}
are
G
-equivariantly homeomorphic. Such a space is called the Bowditch boundary of the pair
(G,H)
and is denoted by
\partial_{B}(G,H)
. We refer the reader to
[
13
]
for an explicit description of
\partial_{B}(G,H)
and to
[
22
]
for a verification of the properties stated above. In this article, we do not work directly with the Bowditch boundary. The two facts regarding the Bowditch boundary that we will use in this paper are Theorem
1.1
and the following theorem, which is a consequence of a result by Groff
[
9
, Theorem 6.3, Corollary 6.5]
:

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finitely generated groups such that
G_{1}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a subgroup
H_{1}
, and
G_{2}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a subgroup
H_{2}
. Let
q\colon(G_{1},H_{1})\rightarrow(G_{2},H_{2})
be a quasi-isometry of pairs. Then, the Bowditch boundaries
\partial_{B}(G_{1},H_{1})
and
\partial_{B}(G_{2},H_{2})
are homeomorphic.

Remark 2.9
.

The above theorem is just a special case of the more general result by Groff
[
9
]
. Groff assumes that
G_{1}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a finite collection
\mathcal{A}_{1}
of peripheral subgroups and does not assume relative hyperbolicity for
G_{2}
. Rather, he uses a result of Behrstock-Drutu-Mosher
[
1
, Theorem 4.8]
to infer the existence of a collection
\mathcal{A}_{2}
of subgroups of
G_{2}
with respect to which
G_{2}
is relatively hyperbolic. Additionally, every subgroup
H\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
is quasi-isometric to some group
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
, and for any given
g\in G
and
H\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
, the image of
q(gH)
lies within a bounded Hausdorff distance of some coset
g^{\prime}H^{\prime}
, where
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Groff then uses these two facts in order to establish the quasi-isometry between the cusped spaces
X(G_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
X(G_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
. In general, the number of elements in
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
may not necessarily be equal. As we would like to restrict ourselves to the class of groups that are relatively hyperbolic with respect to a single peripheral subgroup, we assume that the quasi-isometry
q
is a quasi-isometry of pairs
(G,H)
and
(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
rather than relying on
[
1
, Theorem 4.8]
.

2.4.
Cohomology of Pairs

Given a pair
(G,H)
, we recall the definition of the relative cohomology of

with coefficients in a
RG
-module

, following
[
2

Let
\mathbb{Z}[G/H]
be the
\mathbb{Z}G
-module generated by the set

of left cosets of
H
. Let
\epsilon\colon\mathbb{Z}[G/H]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be the
\mathbb{Z}G
-module morphism that sends every generator to

\Delta_{G/H}\coloneqq\ker\epsilon
. For an arbitrary ring

\Delta_{G/H}^{R}
is defined as

.
Let

be an

-module. Then,

H^{i}(G,H;M)\coloneqq\mathrm{Ext}_{RG}^{i-1}(\Delta_{G/H},M).

Remark 2.10
.

We recall what this means. Let

\dots\rightarrow F_{2}\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow\Delta_{G/H}^{R}\rightarrow 0

be a projective resolution of the
G

\Delta_{G/H}^{R}

\mathrm{Ext}^{\ast}_{RG}(\Delta_{G/H}^{R},M)
denotes the cohomology of the cochain complex

\dots\leftarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{RG}(F_{1},M)\leftarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{RG}(F_{0},M)\leftarrow 0.

Definition 2.11

(Cohomological Dimension of a Pair)
.

Let
G
be a group, and let

be a subgroup. The cohomological dimension of the pair

with coefficients in a ring

\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G,H)\coloneqq\sup\{i\mid H^{i}(G,H;M)\neq 0\text{ for some }G\text{-module }M\}.

.

[
2

For any group pair
(G,H)
and any
RG
-module

, there is a long exact sequence in cohomology:

\dots\rightarrow H^{k}(G;M)\rightarrow H^{k}(H;M)\rightarrow H^{k+1}(G,H;M)\rightarrow H^{k+1}(G;M)\rightarrow\dots

We also recall the definition of an Eilenberg–MacLane pair for a group pair

Let

Let

. Let

Let

be a pair, and let

2.5.
Dehn Fillings of Relatively Hyperbolic Groups

Theorem 2.15
.

[

Suppose

Proposition 3.2

Let

:

First we prove the statement for

Remark 3.3
.

Other Rips-style constructions for relatively hyperbolic groups have been defined in the literature.
The authors of

]
construct, for a given relatively hyperbolic pair

, a finite-dimensional contractible simplicial complex

such that

acts cocompactly on

, and the stabilizer of each simplex of dimension at least one is finite (in particular, trivial if

\Gamma_{n}^{\triangle}

Let

and be a finite symmetric generating set of

let

denote the word metric on

. The 0-skeleton

is the same as that of the coned-off Cayley graph, namely,

We now give a proof of Corollary

We now provide a proof of Corollary

Lemma 3.4
.

For any exact sequence

of

-modules, and for any

, there is a natural map
\partial:H^{n}(G,H;M^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow H^{n-1}(G,H;M^{\prime})

(1)

\xrightarrow{\partial}H^{1}(G,H;M^{\prime})\rightarrow H^{1}(G,H;M)\rightarrow H^{1}(G,H;M^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow\dots{}

Let
\dots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow\Delta_{G/H}
denote a free resolution of

. For each

, the modules

fit into an exact sequence,

and the associated long exact sequence now follow from a standard result in homological algebra (cf.
[

Lemma 3.5

If

, then

\textrm{cd}_{R}(G,H)=\{n\mid H^{n}(G,H;F)\neq 0\text{ for some free }G\text{-module }F\}.

Let

. Let

be a free module such that

admits a surjective

-module morphism onto

. Let

denote the kernel of this morphism.

Consider the long exact sequence obtained by applying Lemma

to the exact sequence

.

.

[

If a

Since

4.
Application to QI classification of relatively hyperbolic groups

In this Section we give a proof of Theorem
1.7
using the results proven in the previous section.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be two groups such that
G
is hyperbolic with respect to a subgroup
H<G
and
G^{\prime}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a subgroup
H^{\prime}
. Let
q:G^{\prime}\to G
be a quasi-isometry of groups. Assume that both
H
and
H^{\prime}
are unconstricted. Then, the image of any left coset
gH
under
q
is within a bounded Hausdorff distance of some coset
g^{\prime}H^{\prime}
of
H^{\prime}
.

In other words,
q
is a quasi-isometry between pairs
(G,H)
and
(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a symmetric generating set for
G
, and let
d_{S}
denote the corresponding word metric on
G
. Similarly, let
S^{\prime}
be a symmetric generating set for
G^{\prime}
, and let
d_{S^{\prime}}
be the corresponding word metric on
G^{\prime}
. Let
L\geq 1
and
C>0
be such that
q:(G^{\prime},d_{S^{\prime}})\to(G,d_{S})
is an
(L,C)
quasi-isometry. Let
\bar{q}:G\to G^{\prime}
denote the quasi-inverse of
q
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the collection
\{gH\mid g\in G\}
of all left cosets of
H
. Then,
G
is asymptotically tree-graded with respect to
\mathcal{H}
. It follows from
[
7
, Corollary 5.8]
that the image
q(g^{\prime}H^{\prime})
of any coset is contained in the
R
-neighborhood
N_{R}(gH)
of some coset
gH\in\mathcal{H}
for some constant
R
that depends only on
L
,
C
,
G
, and
S
. Similarly, the image
\bar{q}(gH)
is contained in the
R^{\prime}
-neighborhood of some coset
g^{\prime\prime}H^{\prime}
.

As
d_{S^{\prime}}(x,\bar{q}\circ q(x))\leq D
for all
x\in G^{\prime}
, we have that the infinite set
\bar{q}\circ q(g^{\prime}H^{\prime})
is contained in the set
N_{M}(g^{\prime}H^{\prime})\cap N_{M}(g^{\prime\prime}H^{\prime})
, where
M=\max\{D,R^{\prime}\}
. Since
G^{\prime}
is asymptotically tree-graded with respect to
\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
, the collection of all left cosets of
H^{\prime}
, the diameter of
N_{r}(g_{1}H^{\prime})\cap N_{r}(g_{2}H^{\prime})
is uniformly bounded for any two distinct cosets
g_{1}H^{\prime}
and
g_{2}H^{\prime}
of
H^{\prime}

[
7
, Theorem 4.1]
. It follows that
g^{\prime}H^{\prime}=g^{\prime\prime}H^{\prime}
.

Now, let
y\in gH
. Then, there exists a point
x\in g^{\prime}H^{\prime}
such that
d_{S}(\bar{q}(y),x)\leq R^{\prime}
. Thus,

d_{S}(q(x),y)\leq d_{S}(q\circ\bar{q}(y),y)+d_{S}(q\circ\bar{q}(y),q(x))\leq D+LR^{\prime}+C.

Thus,
gH\subset N_{D+LR^{\prime}+C}(q(g^{\prime}H^{\prime}))
. It follows that the Hausdorff distance between
q(g^{\prime}H^{\prime})
and
gH
is finite.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
:

By Proposition 4.1 and Theorem
2.8
, the Bowditch boundaries
\partial_{B}(G,H)
and
\partial_{B}(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
are homeomorphic. By Theorem

, the relative cohomology

is isomorphic to the Čech cohomology of the Bowditch boundary

. This implies that the two cohomology groups

and

are isomorphic for each

. The result now follows from Corollary

.
∎

5.

In this section, we give examples of relatively hyperbolic pairs

Example 5.1
.

Let

be a finitely generated group, and let

be a hyperbolic group of cohomological dimension

, where

. Let

. Then

is relatively hyperbolic with respect to

Let

be an

Example 5.2

Let

In particular,
\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G,H)=n
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold with a single cusp, and let

be the cusp subgroup. Then
\mathrm{cd}(G,H)=3
. Let

to show that

is multi-ended, while

Let

c
. Then
\pi_{1}(X)\cong G_{1}
. Both
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
admit a CAT(0) metric, and the gluing can be performed in such a way that the subspaces are convex and the gluing map is an isometry. As a result,
X
can be endowed with a CAT(0) metric; in particular, its universal cover is contractible. Thus,
(X,Y)
is an Eilenberg–MacLane pair for the pair
(G_{1},H_{1})
. Using Lemmas
2.14
and
2.13
, we have that
\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{1},H_{1})=2
.

Thus, one can use Theorem
1.7
to distinguish between
G
and
G_{1}
. Note that
G_{1}
splits as an amalgam over the cyclic subgroup
\mathbb{Z}=\langle\gamma\rangle
, whereas
G
does not split over any of its 2-ended subgroups. It is known that splittings over 2-ended subgroups are preserved under quasi-isometries
[
17
]
.

We now give another example of a group that is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a single
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
subgroup and has relative cohomological dimension 2. The example below does not appear to have an obvious
\mathbb{Z}
-splitting, although we do not know of a way to determine whether it admits a
\mathbb{Z}
-splitting.

Example 5.4
.

Let
H_{2}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\langle a,b\mid[a,b]\rangle
, and let
H^{\prime}
be the genus-2 surface group. Let
F=H\ast H^{\prime}
be their free product. Choose
r\in F
such that
r
is not conjugate into either
H
or
H^{\prime}
, and let
G_{2}=F/\langle\langle r\rangle\rangle
. Then the quotient map
q:F\to G_{2}
restricts to a monomorphism on both
H
and
H^{\prime}
. Furthermore,
r
can be chosen so that
G_{2}
is one-ended and satisfies the
C^{\prime}_{*}(1/8)
small-cancellation condition
[
5
, Section 4.4]
, which implies that
G_{2}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to
H_{2}
.

Using a result of Howie
[
11
]
, we show that
\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{2},H_{2})=2
. Note that both
H_{2}
and
H^{\prime}
are locally indicable
[
11
, pg. 421, Theorem 1]
. Let
\mathbb{T}^{2}
denote the 2-torus, and let
S_{2}
be a copy of the closed genus-2 surface. Let
X^{\prime}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\vee S_{2}
, so that
\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})\simeq F
. Let
\Phi_{r}:S^{1}\to X^{\prime}
be a continuous map along a closed path corresponding to the word
r
. Let
X
be the CW-complex obtained by attaching a 2-cell along
\Phi_{r}
: that is,

X=\mathbb{D}^{2}\sqcup X^{\prime}/(x\sim\Phi_{r}(x)).

By the Van Kampen theorem,
\pi_{1}(X)=G_{2}
. By
[
11
, pg. 421, Theorem 1]
,
X
is aspherical. Thus,
(X,\mathbb{T}^{2})
is an Eilenberg–MacLane pair for
(G_{2},H_{2})
. By Lemma
2.14
,
\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{2},H_{2})\leq 2
. It is not difficult to see that
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{T}^{2})\neq 0
, and consequently,
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G_{2},H_{2})=2
.

Example 5.5
.

For any two elements
x,y\in G
of a group
G
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define
(x,y)_{m}
to be the word of length
m
given by

(x,y)_{m}=\begin{cases}(xy)^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\cdot x&if $m$ is odd,\\
(xy)^{\frac{m}{2}}&if $m$ is even.\end{cases}

Any two-generator Artin group, given by the presentation
\langle x,y\mid(x,y)_{m}=(y,x)_{m}\rangle
, is unconstricted
[
1
, Example 10.1]
. In this final example, we show how to construct, for each
n=4k,k\in\mathbb{N}
, a group
G_{n}
such that:

(1)

G_{n}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a subgroup
H_{n}
that is isomorphic to a two-generator Artin group;

(2)

G_{n}
has property (T);

(3)

\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{n},H_{n})=n
.

Property (T) implies that
G_{n}
does not split as an amalgam or HNN extension.

Let
n=4k
and let
\Gamma_{n}
be a hyperbolic group of cohomological dimension
n
with property (T). For example, one can take
\Gamma_{n}
to be a cocompact lattice in
\textrm{Sp}(k,1)
for
n=4k
. Let
K
be a quasi-convex free subgroup of
\Gamma_{n}
of rank 2, and let
a,b\in K
such that
a
and
b
freely generate
K
. Then
\Gamma_{n}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to
K
.

Claim 1:

\mathrm{cd}(\Gamma_{n},K)=n
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{cd}(\Gamma_{n})=n
, we have
H^{n}(\Gamma_{n};\mathbb{Z}\Gamma_{n})\neq 0
. As
K
is a free group,
\mathrm{cd}(K)=1
, so
H^{i}(K;\mathbb{Z}\Gamma_{n})=0
for all
i>1
. Using the long exact sequence in Proposition
2.1
, we obtain
H^{i}(\Gamma_{n},K;\mathbb{Z}\Gamma_{n})\simeq H^{i}(\Gamma_{n};\mathbb{Z}\Gamma_{n})
for all
i>2
. The result now follows by Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Let
F
be a finite subset of
\Gamma_{n}
such that the conclusion of Theorem
2.15
holds. We make the following claim:

Claim 2:
For sufficiently large
m
,
F\cap\langle\langle(a,b)_{m}\cdot(b,a)_{m}^{-1}\rangle\rangle=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
S=\{a,b\}
and let
|\cdot|_{S}
denote word length in
K
with respect to
S
. Let
D
be the diameter of
F\cap K
. It suffices to show that for large enough
m
, any
g\in\langle\langle r\rangle\rangle
has word length greater than
D
. This follows from the inequalities

|grg^{-1}|_{S}\geq|r|_{S}\geq 4m-2.

∎

Let
N=\langle\langle r\rangle\rangle_{K}
be the normal closure of
\langle r\rangle
in
K
, and let
\langle\langle N\rangle\rangle
be its normal closure in
\Gamma_{n}
. Claim 2 shows that
\langle\langle r\rangle\rangle
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
2.15
. It follows that
G_{n}=\Gamma_{n}/\langle\langle N\rangle\rangle
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to
H_{n}=K/N
. Since
G_{n}
is a quotient of a group with property (T), it also has property (T).

Claim 3:

\mathrm{cd}(G_{n},H_{n})=n
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.16
, we have:

H^{i}(G_{n},H_{n};\mathbb{Z}G_{n})\cong H^{i}(\Gamma_{n},K;\mathbb{Z}G_{n}).

Since
\mathrm{cd}(\Gamma_{n},K)=n
, the right-hand side vanishes for all
i>n
. By Corollary
1.5
, it suffices to show that
H^{n}(G_{n},H_{n};\mathbb{Z}G_{n})\neq 0
. Let
q:\mathbb{Z}G_{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
be the augmentation map sending each generator to 1, and let
M=\ker q
. Using the long exact sequence from Lemma
3.4
, we get a surjective morphism:

q:H^{n}(\Gamma_{n},K;\mathbb{Z}G_{n})\to H^{n}(\Gamma_{n},K;\mathbb{Z}).

Since
H^{n}(\Gamma_{n},K;\mathbb{Z})\neq 0
, it follows that
H^{n}(G_{n},H_{n};\mathbb{Z}G_{n})\neq 0
.
∎

6.
Multiple Peripherals

The analogous statement of Theorem
1.2
holds and the the proof has only
superficial changes.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
R
be a ring.
Let
G
be a group without
R
-torsion and
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\}
be a finite collection of subgroups of
G
such that
G
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to
\mathcal{P}
. Then, the
RG
-module
\Delta_{G/\mathcal{P}}^{R}
is a module of type
FP
over
\mathbb{Z}G
. As a result,
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G,\mathcal{P})
is finite.

The analogous statement for Theorem
1.7
involves a few subtleties. Suppose we are given two quasi-isometric groups
G
and
G^{\prime}
such that
G
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a finite collection of unconstricted subgroups
\mathcal{P}
. Using results from
[
1
]
one obtains a finite collection
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
such that each subgroup in
\mathcal{P}
is quasi-isometric to some subgroup in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. Moreover, the quasi-isometry
q
between
G
and
G^{\prime}
induces a quasi-isometry of pairs
(G,\mathcal{P})
and
(G^{\prime},\mathcal{P^{\prime}})
. If one
a priori
knows a collection
\mathcal{Q}
of unconstricted subgroups with respect to which
G^{\prime}
is relatively hyperbolic, we need to ensure that
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are the same (up to quasi-isometry). This ambiguity can be resolved as follows: Consider the quasi-isometry
i:G^{\prime}\rightarrow G^{\prime}
induced by the identity map. Since both collections
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are unconstricted,
i
has to induce a quasi-isometry between pairs
(G^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
and
(G,\mathcal{Q})
. As a result,
\partial_{B}(G^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
is homeomorphic to
\partial_{B}(G^{\prime},\mathcal{Q})
. We are now ready to state the correct analogue of Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be quasi-isometric groups that do not contain
R
-torsion, and suppose that
G
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a collection
\mathcal{P}
of unconstricted subgroups of type
F_{\infty}
. Then, there exists a collection of type
F_{\infty}
, unconstricted subgroups
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
such that the pairs
G^{\prime}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to
G^{\prime}
. Moreover, for any finite collection
\mathcal{Q}
of subgroups of
G^{\prime}
that are unconstricted, type
F_{\infty}
and such that
G^{\prime}
is relatively hyperbolic with respect to
\mathcal{Q}
, we have
\mathrm{cd}(G,\mathcal{P})=\mathrm{cd}(G^{\prime},\mathcal{Q})
.

The Lee-Wick-Chern-Simons pseudo-quantum electrodynamics
M. J. Neves

Abstract
The Lee-Wick pseudo-quantum electrodynamics in the presence of a Chern-Simons term is studied in this paper. The paper starts with a non-local lagrangian density that sets the Lee-Wick electrodynamics defined on a bidimensional space added to the Chern-Simons topological term. We investigate classical aspects as the potential energy for the interaction of static charges through the gauge propagator. The gauge field of the Lee-Wick-Chern-Simons electrodynamics is minimally coupled to the fermions sector that includes new degree of freedom, as a Lee-Wick heavy fermion partner of the electron. The perturbative approach for the theory is presented via effective action in which we obtain the Ward identities. We study the radiative corrections at one loop for the electron self-energy, the vacuum polarization, and the 3 3 -vertex through the Ward identity. We show that the Lee-Wick mass has a fundamental role in these results.

Introduction

The connection between quantum field theories (QFTs) in planar spaces and condensed matter physics (CMP)
has called attention for the research in material physics. Several applications of field theories
has been used to explain the experimental results in materials, as the transport in graphene

;

Abelian gauge field theory is the theoretical description for the mediators of the interaction of electrons in planar materials,
like in quantum electrodynamics (QED). The condition that constraints the classical sources to be confined to a bidimensional space
leads to the non-local gauge field theory, that is known as pseudo-electrodynamics (PED)
Marino93
. The non-locality manifests
through the derivatives of infinity order in the D’Alembertian operator. The PED preserves the fundamental properties of a QFT
as causality
Amaral
, unitarity
Marino2014
, and many extensions has been studied in the literature

Alves
;
Alves2
;
Ozela
;
Ozela2
;
VanSergio
;
MarioPRD2025
. As example, the addition of Chern-Simon term is the complement to include
topological effects that preserves the gauge invariance of the theory, with applications in superconductors
Xing
.
The Proca pseudo-ED also is one of the extensions studied in the literature, but it breaks the gauge invariance due to the massive term for the gauge field.

An alternative theory that introduces massive degree of freedom for gauge fields in which the gauge invariance is preserved is known
as the Lee-Wick electrodynamics (LWED)
lw69
;
lw70
;
podolsky42
;
podolsky44
. The connection of the LWED with topological effects is
a research topic of interest in the literature
accioly03
;
accioly04
;
accioly05
, and also the coupling of the LW gauge field
with fermions
accioly11
;
turcati14
;
turcati15
. All these references motivate us to investigations in the Lee-Wick
pseudo-electrodynamics (LWPED) with the presence of a Chern-Simons term in
1+2
dimensions.

In this paper, the LWPED that was obtained in the reference
MarioPRD2025
is studied in the presence of a Chern-Simons (CS) topological term.
Thereby, we have the called Lee-Wick-Chern-Simons pseudo-electrodynamics (LWCSPED), that is a non-local and topological ED gauge invariant with
a heavy mass associated with the LW gauge field. We obtain the gauge propagator and the poles that are modified by the CS parameter. With these
results, we calculate the static potential for two like-point charges at rest under the influence of the LW mass, and of the CS parameter.
Posteriorly, we include fermions in the presence of a heavy partner fermion that is originally motivated by the LW term. Thus, the sector
of fermions is composite by the electron (light fermion), and the LW fermion (heavy fermion) with the Fermi velocity attached to spatial
part of the theory in
1+2
dimensions. We couple this sector of fermions to the LWCSPED gauge field, that we call LWCS pseudo-quantum electrodynamics
(LWCSPQED). The perturbation theory for the LWCSPQED is showed through the effective action where the Ward identity must be satisfied. Using this approach,
we obtain the radiative corrections to the electron propagator, to the gauge propagator (vacuum polarization), and to the
3
-vertex involving
the electron and the LWCSPED gauge field. We calculate the electron self-energy and the vacuum polarization at one loop approximation.
From the result for the vacuum polarization, the potential energy of two static charges corrected by quantum effects is numerically calculated.
The correction at one loop to the
3
-vertex is so calculated via Ward identity since that we know the electron self-energy result.

In the section

We show the effective action and the Ward identity in the section

II
The Lee-Wick pseudo-electrodynamics with Chern-Simons term

The planar and non-local Lee-Wick electrodynamics was obtained in the ref.
MarioPRD2025
.
Adding a Chern-Simons topological term, the lagrangian density of the theory is

{\cal L}_{PLWCS}=-\frac{1}{4}\,F_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}N(\bar{\Box})F^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+\frac{i\theta}{2}\,\epsilon^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}\bar{\rho}}\,A_{\bar{\mu}}\,\partial_{\bar{\nu}}A_{\bar{\rho}}-j_{\bar{\mu}}\,A^{\bar{\mu}}\;,

(1)

where the non-local
N(\bar{\Box})
-operator is defined by

\displaystyle N(\bar{\Box})=\frac{2\,(\bar{\Box}+M^{2})}{(\bar{\Box}+M^{2})\sqrt{\bar{\Box}}-\bar{\Box}\,\sqrt{\bar{\Box}+M^{2}}}\;.

(2)

The tensors, vectors and parameters from (
1
) are defined as follows :

F_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}=\partial_{\bar{\mu}}A_{\bar{\nu}}-\partial_{\bar{\nu}}A_{\bar{\mu}}
is the EM strength field tensor,

A^{\bar{\mu}}
is the gauge field,
M
is the Lee-Wick mass,
\theta
is a dimensionless Chern-Simons parameter, and

j^{\bar{\mu}}
is a classical source. As mentioned previously, the bar index runs as
\bar{\mu}=(0,1,2)
, the derivative operator is
\partial_{\bar{\mu}}=(\partial_{t},\partial_{x},\partial_{y})
, and the bar D’Alembertian operator means
\bar{\Box}=\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\partial^{\bar{\mu}}=\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{x}^{2}-\partial_{y}^{2}
in
1+2
dimensions. The limit
M\rightarrow\infty

reduces the
N
-operator in
N(\bar{\Box})=2/\sqrt{\bar{\Box}}
, and (
1
) becomes the lagrangian of the pseudo-electrodynamics with a Chern-Simons term
discussed in the ref.
Alves2
.

The action principle applied to the lagrangian yields the field equation

\displaystyle N(\bar{\Box})\,\partial_{\bar{\mu}}F^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+i\theta\,\tilde{F}^{\bar{\nu}}=j^{\bar{\nu}}\;,

(3)

in which
\tilde{F}^{\bar{\mu}}=\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}F_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}

is the dual tensor of
F^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}
, that satisfies the Bianchi identity
\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\tilde{F}^{\bar{\mu}}=0
.
As consequence of (
3
), the current is conserved,
i.e.
,
\partial_{\bar{\mu}}j^{\bar{\mu}}=0
. Thereby,
the lagrangian density (
1
) is
U(1)
gauge invariant under the transformation
A_{\bar{\mu}}\mapsto A^{\prime}_{\bar{\mu}}=A_{\bar{\mu}}-\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\Lambda
, for
\Lambda
a real function of the coordinates
x^{\bar{\mu}}=(t,x,y)
.

To obtain the gauge propagator, we add the gauge fixing term to the lagrangian (
1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{gf}=\frac{1}{2\xi}\,A_{\bar{\mu}}\left(1+\frac{\bar{\Box}}{M^{2}}\right)N(\bar{\Box})\,\partial^{\bar{\mu}}\,\partial^{\bar{\nu}}A_{\bar{\nu}}\;,

(4)

where
\xi
is a real parameter. The modified lagrangian by the gauge fixing can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{PLWCS}=\frac{1}{2}\,A^{\bar{\mu}}\,{\cal O}_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,A^{\bar{\nu}}-j_{\bar{\mu}}\,A^{\bar{\mu}}\;,

(5)

where the operator
{\cal O}_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}
is

{\cal O}_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}=N(\bar{\Box})\bar{\Box}\,\theta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+\frac{1}{\xi}\left(1+\frac{\bar{\Box}}{M^{2}}\right)N(\bar{\Box})\bar{\Box}\,\omega_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+i\theta\,S_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\;,

(6)

and the projectors are given by

\theta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}=\eta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}-\frac{\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\,\partial_{\bar{\nu}}}{\bar{\Box}}\;,\;\omega_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}=\frac{\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\,\partial_{\bar{\nu}}}{\bar{\Box}}\;,\;S_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}=-\epsilon_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}\bar{\rho}}\,\partial^{\bar{\rho}}\;,

(7)

that satisfy the relations

\displaystyle\theta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,\theta^{\bar{\nu}\bar{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{\bar{\mu}}^{\;\,\,\bar{\alpha}}\;,\;\omega_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,\omega^{\bar{\nu}\bar{\alpha}}=\omega_{\bar{\mu}}^{\;\,\,\bar{\alpha}}\;,\;S_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,S^{\bar{\nu}\bar{\alpha}}=\bar{\Box}\,\theta_{\bar{\mu}}^{\;\,\,\bar{\alpha}}\;,

\displaystyle\theta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,S^{\bar{\nu}\bar{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\bar{\mu}}^{\;\,\,\bar{\alpha}}\;,\;\theta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,\omega^{\bar{\nu}\bar{\alpha}}=\omega_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,S^{\bar{\nu}\bar{\alpha}}=0\;.

(8)

Using these relations, the inverse of the
{\cal O}_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}
- operator is

\displaystyle({\cal O}_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N(\bar{\Box})\,\theta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}}{\bar{\Box}\,N^{2}(\bar{\Box})+\theta^{2}}+\frac{\xi\,M^{2}\,\omega_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}}{(\bar{\Box}+M^{2})\,\bar{\Box}\,N(\bar{\Box})}

(9)

\displaystyle+\frac{i\,\theta\,S_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}}{\bar{\Box}\,[\,\bar{\Box}\,N^{2}(\bar{\Box})+\theta^{2}\,]}\;.

The functional integration over the
A^{\bar{\mu}}
-field reproduces the effective lagrangian in terms of the source
j^{\bar{\mu}}

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{PLWCS}^{eff}[j_{\bar{\mu}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\,j^{\bar{\mu}}\,({\cal O}_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}})^{-1}\,j^{\bar{\nu}}

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\,j_{\bar{\mu}}\,\frac{N(\bar{\Box})}{\bar{\Box}\,N^{2}(\bar{\Box})+\theta^{2}}\,j^{\bar{\mu}}

\displaystyle+\frac{i\theta}{2}\,\frac{j^{\bar{\mu}}\,\epsilon_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}\bar{\rho}}\,\partial^{\bar{\rho}}\,j^{\bar{\nu}}}{\bar{\Box}\,[\,\bar{\Box}\,N^{2}(\bar{\Box})+\theta^{2}\,]}\;,

in which we have used the continuity equation for current. The second line in (
10
) is interpreted as the electromagnetic
interaction in the LWCSPED theory. The third line describes the statistical interaction modified by the Lee-Wick mass
Marino93
.

The gauge propagator is obtained in the momentum space by the correspondence
\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\mapsto-ik_{\bar{\mu}}
,
such that
\bar{\Box}\mapsto-k_{\bar{\mu}}\,k^{\bar{\mu}}\equiv-\bar{k}^{2}
, in which the inverse operator

({\cal O}_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}})^{-1}
is written as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}(\bar{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-i\,N(-\bar{k}^{2})\,\theta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}}{\bar{k}^{2}\,N^{2}(-\bar{k}^{2})-\theta^{2}}+\frac{i\,\xi\,M^{2}\,\omega_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}}{(\bar{k}^{2}-M^{2})\,\bar{k}^{2}\,N(-\bar{k}^{2})}

(11)

\displaystyle+\frac{i\,\theta\,\epsilon_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}\bar{\rho}}\,k^{\bar{\rho}}}{\bar{k}^{2}\,[\,\bar{k}^{2}\,N^{2}(-\bar{k}^{2})-\theta^{2}\,]}\;,

and
N(-\bar{k}^{2})
is the operator (
2
) in the momentum space, with the projectors
\theta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}=\eta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}-k_{\bar{\mu}}\,k_{\bar{\nu}}/\bar{k}^{2}

and
\omega_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}=k_{\bar{\mu}}\,k_{\bar{\nu}}/\bar{k}^{2}
. The limit
M\rightarrow\infty

in the result (
11
) recovers the gauge propagator obtained in the ref.
Xing
. Using the current
conservation in the momentum space
k_{\bar{\mu}}\,j^{\bar{\mu}}=0
, the contraction of (
11
) with

j^{\bar{\mu}}
yields

\displaystyle j^{\bar{\mu}}(\bar{k})\,\Delta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}(\bar{k})\,j^{\bar{\nu}}(\bar{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\,i\,N(-\bar{k}^{2})\,j_{\bar{\mu}}\,j^{\bar{\mu}}}{\bar{k}^{2}\,N^{2}(-\bar{k}^{2})-\theta^{2}}\;,

(12)

and using the definition of
N(-\bar{k}^{2})
, we can write the previous expression as

j^{\bar{\mu}}(\bar{k})\,\Delta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}(\bar{k})\,j^{\bar{\nu}}(\bar{k})=\frac{-\,i\,f(-\bar{k}^{2})\,j_{\bar{\mu}}\,j^{\bar{\mu}}}{\bar{k}^{2}\,(\bar{k}^{2}-M_{\theta}^{2})\,[\,\bar{k}^{2}-(M_{\theta}^{\ast})^{2}\,]}\;,

(13)

in which the
f(-\bar{k}^{2})
-function is

\displaystyle f(-\bar{k}^{2})=4(1+\theta^{2})(\bar{k}^{2}-M^{2})\sqrt{-\bar{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\bar{k}^{2}-M^{2}\left(\frac{1+\theta^{2}/4}{1+\theta^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-4(1+\theta^{2})\,\bar{k}^{2}\sqrt{-\bar{k}^{2}+M^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\bar{k}^{2}-M^{2}\left(\frac{1+3\theta^{2}/4}{1+\theta^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(14)

This result shows explicitly the poles at
\bar{k}^{2}=0
,
\bar{k}^{2}=M_{\theta}^{2}
and
\bar{k}^{2}=(M_{\theta}^{\ast})^{2}
, where
M_{\theta}^{2}
is the complex mass

\displaystyle M_{\theta}^{2}=M^{2}\left[\frac{1+3\theta^{2}/4+i\theta^{3}/4}{1+\theta^{2}}\right]\;,

(15)

with
(M_{\theta}^{\ast})^{2}
it complex conjugate. The propagator has a positive residue if the
3
-current is space-like
j_{\bar{\mu}}\,j^{\bar{\mu}}<0
.
The Lee-Wick mass is modified by the
\theta
-parameter in the presence of
an imaginary part. The limit without the Chern-Simons term
(\theta\rightarrow 0)
leads
to the usual pole
\bar{k}^{2}=M^{2}
, from the Lee-Wick pseudo-ED. In the ultraviolet regime
in which
\bar{k}^{2}\gg M^{2}
,
the gauge propagator goes with
\Delta_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\sim(\bar{k})^{-1}
. This behavior is the same from PQED,
but the Lee-Wick mass may works like a natural regulator parameter for divergences in
1+2
dimensions.

Backing to the effective lagrangian (
10
), the interaction term for a static like point charge in that
the current density is
j^{\bar{\mu}}(\bar{{\bf r}})=[-e\,\delta^{2}(\bar{{\bf r}}-{\bf r}),{\bf 0}]
yields the static
energy potential between two charges
(-e)
separated by a distance
r

U({\bf r})=-e^{2}\int\frac{d^{2}\bar{{\bf k}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\frac{N(\bar{{\bf k}}^{2})\,e^{i\bar{{\bf k}}\cdot{\bf r}}}{N(\bar{{\bf k}}^{2})\,\bar{{\bf k}}^{2}+\theta^{2}}\;,

(16)

that is reduced to integral

U(r)=-\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}k\,dk\,\frac{N(k^{2})\,J_{0}(k\,r)}{k^{2}\,N^{2}(k^{2})+\theta^{2}}\;,

(17)

where
r
is the radial coordinate on the spatial plane, and
J_{0}(k\,r)
is a Bessel function of the first kind.
The numerical solution for the static energy (
17
) is plotted as function of the radial distance
(r)
in the figure (

).
In this plot, we choose
e=0.3
, the Lee-Wick mass as
M=100
, for the values of
\theta=0.0

(this is the case without Chern-Simons term illustrated by the black dashed line),
\theta=0.5
(red line),
\theta=1.0
(blue line)
and
\theta=1.5
(green line).

III
The Lee-Wick-Chern-Simons pseudo-quantum electrodynamics

We start this section with the Dirac lagrangian in the Lee-Wick approach

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{D}=\bar{\hat{\psi}}\left(i\,\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}\partial_{\bar{\mu}}-m\,\mathds{1}\right)\hat{\psi}+\frac{i}{M_{f}^{2}}\,\bar{\hat{\psi}}\,(\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}\partial_{\bar{\mu}})^{3}\,\hat{\psi}\;,

(18)

where
\hat{\psi}
is a Dirac spinor in
1+2
dimensions, the Dirac matrices have the components

\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}=(\gamma^{0},\beta\,\gamma^{i})\,(i=1,2)
, in which
\beta\equiv v_{F}
is the Fermi velocity, the
\gamma^{\bar{\mu}}
-matrices
satisfy the relation
\gamma^{\bar{\mu}}\gamma^{\bar{\nu}}=\eta^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+i\,\sigma^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}
,

\bar{\hat{\psi}}=\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}
is the adjunct field. The
\gamma^{\bar{\mu}}
-matrices in the Dirac basis are set by

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right]\;,\;\gamma^{1}=-i\sigma_{3}\,\sigma_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-i\\
i&0\\
\end{array}\right]\;,\;

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\sigma_{3}\,\sigma_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right]\;,

in which
\sigma_{i}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
are the Pauli matrices. The new Lee-wick term introduces the fermion mass

M_{f}
that corresponds to the heavy partner fermion of the electron of mass
m=0.5
MeV. The limit
M_{f}\rightarrow\infty
reduces
(
18
) to the usual lagrangian applied for Dirac materials. The action principle applied in (
18
) yields the Dirac-Lee-Wick
equation

\displaystyle\left(i\,\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}\partial_{\bar{\mu}}-m\,\mathds{1}\right)\hat{\psi}+\frac{i}{M_{f}^{2}}\,(\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}\partial_{\bar{\mu}})^{3}\,\hat{\psi}=0\;.

(27)

Using the plane wave solution
\hat{\psi}(\bar{x})=u(\bar{p})\,e^{-i\bar{p}\cdot\bar{x}}
in (
27
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\left[\,\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}p_{\bar{\mu}}\left(1-\frac{\bar{p}^{2}}{M_{f}^{2}}\right)-m\,\mathds{1}\,\right]u(\bar{p})=0\;,

(28)

where
\bar{p}^{2}=p_{\bar{\mu}}p^{\bar{\mu}}=p_{0}^{2}-\beta^{2}\,{\bf p}^{2}
is the momentum squared in the fermion sector due to the

\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}
-algebra. The adjoint equation of (
28
) is

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bar{p})\left[\,\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}p_{\bar{\mu}}\left(1-\frac{\bar{p}^{2}}{M_{f}^{2}}\right)+m\,\mathds{1}\,\right]=0\;,

(29)

in which
\bar{u}=u^{\dagger}\,\gamma^{0}
. Multiplying the eq. (
28
) by
\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\,\Gamma^{\bar{\nu}}
on the left-side,
the eq. (
29
) by
\Gamma^{\bar{\nu}}u(\bar{p})
, and summing the two resultants equations, we obtain the components of the
current in the momentum space

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\,\gamma^{0}\,u(\bar{p})=\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\left[\,\frac{p_{0}+p_{0}^{\prime}}{2m}+i\,\sigma^{0i}\,\frac{\beta\,q_{i}}{2m}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\bar{p}^{2}}{2mM_{f}^{2}}(p_{0}+i\beta\,\sigma^{0i}p_{i})-\frac{\bar{p}^{\prime\,2}}{2mM_{f}^{2}}(p_{0}^{\prime}+i\beta\,\sigma^{0i}p_{i}^{\prime})\,\right]u(\bar{p})\;,

(30a)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\,\gamma^{i}\,u(\bar{p})=\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\left[\,\beta\,\frac{p^{i}+p^{\prime\,i}}{2m}+i\frac{\sigma^{i0}q_{0}}{2m}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i\,\sigma^{ij}\,\frac{\beta\,q_{j}}{2m}-\frac{\bar{p}^{2}}{2mM_{f}^{2}}(\beta\,p^{i}+i\,\sigma^{i0}\,p_{0}+i\beta\,\sigma^{ij}p_{j})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\bar{p}^{\prime\,2}}{2mM_{f}^{2}}(\beta\,p^{\prime\,i}+i\,\sigma^{i0}\,p_{0}^{\prime}-i\beta\,\sigma^{ij}p_{j}^{\prime})\,\right]u(\bar{p})\;,

(30b)

that is known as the Gordon identity, in which

.
For the on-shell condition of
\bar{p}^{2}=m^{2}
, and
M_{f}\gg m

)
and (

) are neglected. Thereby, the current components for the electron are given by

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\,\gamma^{0}\,u(\bar{p})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\left[\,\frac{p_{0}+p_{0}^{\prime}}{2m}+i\,\sigma^{0i}\,\frac{\beta\,q_{i}}{2m}\,\right]u(\bar{p})\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;

(31a)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\,\gamma^{i}\,u(\bar{p})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bar{p}^{\prime})\left[\,\beta\,\frac{p^{i}+p^{\prime\,i}}{2m}+i\frac{\sigma^{i0}q_{0}}{2m}\right.

(31b)

\displaystyle\left.+i\,\sigma^{ij}\,\frac{\beta\,q_{j}}{2m}\,\right]u(\bar{p})\;.

\displaystyle S_{F}(p^{\bar{\mu}})=\frac{i}{\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}p_{\bar{\mu}}\left(1-\bar{p}^{2}/M_{f}^{2}\right)-m}

if

\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\mapsto D_{\bar{\mu}}=\partial_{\bar{\mu}}+i\,e\,A_{\bar{\mu}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{PQED}=\bar{\hat{\psi}}\left(i\,\not{D}-m\,\mathds{1}\right)\hat{\psi}+\frac{i}{M_{f}^{2}}\,\bar{\hat{\psi}}\,\not{D}\not{D}\not{D}\,\hat{\psi}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\,F_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,N(\bar{\Box})F^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+\frac{i\theta}{2}\,\epsilon^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}\bar{\rho}}\,A_{\bar{\mu}}\,\partial_{\bar{\nu}}A_{\bar{\rho}}\;,

where
\not{D}=\Gamma^{\bar{\mu}}D_{\bar{\mu}}

This lagrangian is

MarioPRD2025

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{PQED}=\bar{\psi}\left(i\,\not{D}-m\,\mathds{1}\right)\psi-\bar{\chi}\left(i\,\not{D}-M_{f}\,\mathds{1}\right)\chi

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\,F_{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}\,N(\bar{\Box})F^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+\frac{i\theta}{2}\,\epsilon^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}\bar{\rho}}\,A_{\bar{\mu}}\,\partial_{\bar{\nu}}A_{\bar{\rho}}\;,

(34)

where now, the

VI
Conclusions

The pseudo-Lee-wick electrodynamics added to a Chern-Simons (CS) topological term in
1+2
dimensions is investigated in this paper.
We obtain the gauge propagator and the poles that show the Lee-Wick mass with a complex contribution due to the
CS parameter. The photon dispersion relation is also one of the solutions for the poles as consequence of the gauge invariance.
Using the gauge propagator with static sources, the potential energy of interaction between two electrons is calculated numerically
as function of the radial distance on the plane that separates these two charges. The sector of fermions is so included by the
Lee-Wick approach in which a massive degree of freedom is interpreted as a heavy fermion, beyond the usual light massive electron.
These fermions are coupled to the LWCS gauge field that preserve the
U(1)
gauge invariance. The theory is so called
Lee-Wick-Chern-Simons pseudo-quantum electrodynamics (LWCSPQED). We construct the generating functional and the effective action
of this theory that leads to the Ward identities. Posteriorly, we calculate the corrections at one loop to the electron propagator
(electron self-energy), to the gauge propagator (vacuum polarization), and to the
3
-vertex of the electron-positron interacting with
the LWCS gauge field. We use the dimensional regularization and the technics of loop integrals from QFT to evaluate these integrals.
Taking the limit to the three dimensions, the integrals are finite by the analytic extension of the Gamma functions, and also by the
presence of the LW mass that works as natural regulator parameter. Thus, the self-energy yields a correction to the electron mass that is
finite in
1+2
dimensions in the LWCSPQED. A finite result also is obtained for the vacuum polarization in which
the potential energy of interaction between two static electrons is calculated numerically in the presence of the quantum corrections at one loop.
The correction to the
3
-vertex is so calculated directly by the Ward identity, that since it depends on the self-energy, the result also is
finite in
1+2
dimensions. When the LW mass goes to infinity all these results diverge, and we have the Chern-Simons PQED recovered.
As perspective of a forthcoming project, the theory opens the possibility of investigation for a Lee-Wick scalar sector in
1+2
dimensions
coupled to the LWCS gauge field in which the quantum effects at one loop may yield contributions to the effective potential.

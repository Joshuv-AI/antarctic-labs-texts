Convergence Rates for the Alternating Minimization Algorithm in Structured Nonsmooth and Nonconvex Optimization
Glaydston C. Bento, Boris S. Mordukhovich, Tiago S. Mota, Antoine Soubeyran

Abstract
This paper is devoted to developing the alternating minimization algorithm for problems of structured nonconvex optimization proposed by Attouch, Bolté, Redont, and Soubeyran in 2010. Our main result provides significant improvements of the convergence rate of the algorithm, especially under the low exponent Polyak-Łojasiewicz-Kurdyka condition when we establish either finite termination of this algorithm or its superlinear convergence rate instead of the previously known linear convergence. We also investigate the PLK exponent calculus and discuss applications to noncooperative games and behavioral science.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider a class of minimization problems for cost functions
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\ \mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
given in the structured form of constrained optimization:

\mbox{minimize }\;L(x,y):=f(x)+Q(x,y)+g(y),

(1)

where
f
and
g
are proper lower semicontinuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, not necessarily convex, and where
Q
is a smooth function that couples the variables
x
and
y
. Recall that a function
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is
proper
if
{\rm dom}\,h:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;|\;h(x)<\infty\}\neq\emptyset
.

It has been well recognized that optimization problems in the structural form (
1
) appear in many important applications including, in particular, noncooperative games, machine learning, image reconstruction, approximation theory, behavior science, etc.; see
attouch2008alternating
;
attouch2010proximal
;
attouch2007new
and the references therein.

Our main attention in this paper is paid to the
alternative minimization algorithm
designed and investigated in
attouch2010proximal
for nonconvex structured problems of type (
1
). The algorithm’s dynamics are described as follows. Let
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the sequence is generated iteratively of the form
(x_{k},y_{k})\to(x_{k+1},y_{k})\to(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})
by

x_{k+1}\in\arg\min\left\{L(u,y_{k})+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{k}}\|u-x_{k}\|^{2}\;\Big|\;u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\},

(2)

y_{k+1}\in\arg\min\left\{L(x_{k+1},v)+\frac{1}{2\mu_{k}}\|v-y_{k}\|^{2}\;\Big|\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\right\}.

(3)

where
\{\lambda_{k}\}
and
\{\mu_{k}\}
are positive sequences. This algorithm was originally developed in
attouch2008alternating
;
attouch2007new
for convex and weakly convex problems, while its nonconvex generality explored in
attouch2010proximal
opened new perspectives and significantly extended the spectrum of important applications to various optimization-related areas.

The main results of
attouch2010proximal
revolve around establishing global convergence and convergence rates of the iterates in (
2
) and (
3
) under some versions of the ”Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality” as labeled in
attouch2010proximal
, which we now unify—by following
bento2025convergence
—under the name of the
Polyak-Łojasiewicz-Kurdyka
(PLK)
conditions
; see Section
2
for the exact formulation, historical remarks, and discussions.

Here we provide significant improvements of convergence rates from
(
attouch2010proximal
, Theorem 11)
establishing, in particular, either finite termination or superlinear convergence for the alternating minimization algorithm under the lower exponent PLK condition instead of the linear convergence obtained in
attouch2010proximal
. Moreover, new convergence rate results are derived for cost function values along the iterative sequence. We also investigate calculus rules for PLK exponents and construct several examples illustrating the revealed phenomena. Finally, some applications to noncooperative games and behavioral science are briefly discussed.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we first present the basic assumptions and then formulate and discuss the PLK conditions used in deriving the main results. Section
3
investigates calculus rule for PLK components needed to deal with the structured form (
1
). We construct here five examples illustrating some striking PLK phenomena. Section
4
presents our main results about convergence rates of the alternating minimization algorithm under the exponent PLK conditions. Section
5
concerns applications of the main results to some models of noncooperative game theory and behavioral science.

2
Basic Assumptions and PLK Conditions

First we formulate the standing assumptions that are used throughout the paper without further mentioning. They are exactly the same as in
attouch2010proximal
, namely:

(\mathcal{H})\begin{cases}L(x,y)=f(x)+Q(x,y)+g(y),&\\
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},&\text{is proper lower semicontinuous,}\\
g:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},&\text{is proper lower semicontinuous,}\\
Q:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R},&\text{is a }{\cal C}^{1}-\text{smooth function,}\\
\nabla Q&\hskip-28.45274pt\text{is Lipschitz continuous on bounded subsets of }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\end{cases}

(\mathcal{H}_{1})\begin{cases}\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}L>-\infty,\\
\text{the function }L(\cdot,y_{0})\text{ is proper},\\
\text{for some positive }r_{-}<r_{+},\text{ the sequences of stepsizes }\lambda_{k},\mu_{k}\text{ belong to }(r_{-},r_{+}).\end{cases}

It is shown in
(
attouch2010proximal
, Lemma 3.1)
that the imposed assumptions ensure that the alternating minimization algorithm in (
2
), (
3
) is well-defined and enjoys the desired descent property together with other well-posedness properties broadly used in the paper. To present this result, we need to recall the main subdifferential notion for extended-real-valued proper functions used in what follows. Given
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,h
, the
limiting/Mordukhovich subdifferential
of
h
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\partial_{M}h(\bar{x}):=\big\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;\exists x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\to}}\bar{x},\,v_{k}\in\partial^{F}h(x_{k}),\;v_{k}\to v\big\},

(4)

where
x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\to}}\bar{x}
means that
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
and
h(x_{k})\to f(x)
, and where

\partial_{F}h(x):=\Big\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\Big|\;\limsup_{u\to x}\frac{h(u)-h(x)-\langle v,u-x\rangle}{\|u-x\|}\geq 0\big\}

(5)

is known as the
regular/Fréchet subdifferential
of
h
at
x
. It has been realized in variational analysis and optimization that the limiting subdifferential (
4
) is a robust construction enjoying comprehensive
calculus rules
based on variational and extremal principles. On the other hand, the regular subdifferential (
5
) doesn’t possess these properties while providing convenient approximation tools for computations. We refer the reader to the monographs
mordukhovich2006variational
;
mordukhovich2018variational
;
rockafellar1998

and the bibliographies therein for variational theories involving (
4
), (
5
) in finite and infinite dimensions with numerous applications.

The aforementioned result of
attouch2010proximal
is formulated as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under assumptions
(\mathcal{H})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{1})
, the sequences
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{y_{k}\}
are well-defined. Moreover, the following hold:

(i)
For all natural numbers
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the inequality

L(x_{k},y_{k})+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{k-1}}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu_{k-1}}\|y_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\leq L(x_{k-1},y_{k-1}),

(6)

and therefore the sequence
\{L(x_{k},y_{k})\}
is not increasing.

(ii)
We have the convergent series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+\|y_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\right)<\infty,

which implies, in particular, that

\lim_{k\to\infty}(\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|+\|y_{k}-y_{k-1}\|)=0.

(iii)
For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

(x^{*}_{k},y^{*}_{k}):=\left(\nabla_{x}Q(x_{k},y_{k})-\nabla_{x}Q(x_{k},y_{k-1}),0\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{k-1}}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}),\frac{1}{\mu_{k-1}}(y_{k}-y_{k-1})\right).

Then we have the inclusion

(x^{*}_{k},y^{*}_{k})\in\partial L(x_{k},y_{k}).

Furthermore, it follows for any bounded subsequence
\{(x_{k_{j}},y_{k_{j}})\}
of
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}
that

(x^{*}_{k_{j}},y^{*}_{k_{j}})\to(0,0)\quad\text{as}\quad k_{j}\to\infty

and hence
\text{\rm dist}(0,\partial L(x_{k_{j}},y_{k_{j}}))\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad k_{j}\to\infty.

Now we are ready to formulate the main conditions for our convergence analysis; cf.
attouch2010proximal
;
bento2025convergence
for more details and references.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an extended-real-valued lower semicontinuous function. We say that the function
h
satisfies the
(basic)

Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PLK) condition
at
\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,h
if there exist a number
\eta\in(0,\infty)
, a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
, and a concave continuous function
\varphi:[0,\eta]\to[0,\infty)
, called the
desingularizing function
, such that

\varphi(0)=0,\quad\varphi\in{\cal C}^{1}(0,\eta),\quad\varphi^{\prime}(s)>0\;\mbox{ for all }\;s\in(0,\eta),\;\mbox{ and }\;

(7)

\varphi^{\prime}\big(h(x)-h(\bar{x})\big)\mbox{\rm dist}\big(0,\partial h(x)\big)\geq 1\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in U\cap[h(\bar{x})<h(x)<h(\bar{x})+\eta].

(8)

The
exponent PLK condition
corresponds to (
7
) and (
8
) where the desingularizing function is chosen as
\varphi(t)=Mt^{1-q}
with some
M>0
and
q\in[0,1)
. We refer to the case where
q\in(0,1/2)
as the
PLK condition with lower exponents
.

To the best of our knowledge, the original version of the PLK condition was introduced by Polyak in 1962 (the English translation of his paper was published in
polyak1963gradient
), for
{\cal C}^{1}
-smooth functions
h
with Lipschitzian gradients in Hilbert spaces, as the inequality

\|\nabla h(x)\|\geq(1/2M)|h(x)-h(\bar{x})|^{1/2},\quad M>0.

The main motivation and result (Theorem 4 of
polyak1963gradient
) were to establish the linear convergence of the classical gradient descent method. Independently, Łojasiewicz
Lojasiewicz1963
introduced the inequality‘

\|\nabla h(x)\|\geq b\,|h(x)-h(\bar{x})|^{q},\quad b:=1/M(1-q),\quad q\in[0,1),

(9)

for analytic functions in the finite-dimensional framework of semialgebraic geometry with no applications to optimization. The gradient inequality (
9
) is referred to (especially in the literature on machine learning and computer science) as the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition; see, e.g.,
karimi2016linear
. Later
Kurdyka
, Kurdyka extended the semialgebraic approach by Łojasiewicz to the general class of o-minimal structures. Conditions of the PLK type were further extended to nonsmooth functions in terms of various subdifferentials (often under the name of the “Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property”) with many applications to constrained optimization and other important fields of mathematics and applied sciences. Among numerous publications using PLK conditions, we refer the reader to
Absil2005
;
aragon2023coderivative
;
aragon2020boosted
;
attouch2009convergence
;
attouch2013convergence
;
bento2025convergence
;
bento2015generalized
;
mordukhovich2024
and the bibliographies therein.

Calculus of PLK Exponents

Given the structure of the function
L
in (
1
), it is crucial to determine the exponent of a desingularizing function
\varphi
associated with
L
in Definition
2.2
, provided that the exponents of the desingularizing functions associated with
f
,
g
, and
Q
are already known. The
calculus
of PLK exponents and its applications to the linear convergence of first-order methods has been a subject of study in recent years; see, e.g.,
li2018calculus
, where explicit convergence rates for various first-order methods are derived and applied to a wide range of optimization models.

Observe to this end that for specific choices of
Q
and
g
, the structure of
L
recovers an important class of bifunctions used in the convergence analysis of several first-order methods whose iterates involve
momentum terms
. For further details, we refer the reader to, e.g.,
boct2016inertial
;
ochs2014ipiano
with the convergence analysis of certain inertial proximal algorithms and to
chambolle2015convergence
for investigating the convergence of the proximal gradient algorithm with extrapolation. In particular, for the case where
f
has a PLK exponent
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
,
g\equiv 0
, and
Q(x,y)=\|x-y\|^{2}
, it is shown in
(
li2018calculus
, Theorem 3.6 and Theorem 5.1)
, respectively, how to determine the PLK exponent of
L
and establish the convergence rate of a certain inertial proximal algorithm with constant stepsizes.

In this section, we continue the study of PLK exponent calculus associated with the exponent PLK condition from Definition
2.2
. The first question to resolve is about the
consistency
of the assumptions on
L
with the
existence
of PLK lower exponents. Then, besides considering calculus rules for computing PLK components applied to
L
(
1
), we raise a new question about the
smallest exponent
ensuring the fulfillment of exponent PLK condition and construct some striking examples illustrating this issue.

Let us begin with the consistency question. It is shown in
(

that when

is a local minimizer of the difference function

\bar{x}
and where
f_{2}
is convex, the fulfillment of the lower exponent PLK condition for

at
\bar{x}
is

with the Lipschitz continuity of the gradient mapping
\nabla f_{1}
around

. Now we construct a one-dimensional example showing that this is

Main Results

Recall that the standing assumptions in
{\cal H}

{\cal H}_{1}

global convergence

Before deriving the main results, we present the following useful lemma of its own interest whose device uses the idea that appears within the proof of
(
Absil2005

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{a_{k}\}\subset[0,\infty)
be a monotonically decreasing sequenced. Then for all
q\in(0,1)
, we have the estimate

\sum_{j=k}^{k+l}\frac{a_{j}-a_{j+1}}{a_{j}^{q}}\leq\frac{1}{1-q}\left(a_{k}^{1-q}-a_{k+l+1}^{1-q}\right),\quad k,l\in\mathbb{N}.

(14)

Proof.

Since
{a_{k}}\subset[0,\infty)
is monotonically decreasing, it follows that
a_{j}\geq t
for all
t\in[a_{j+1},a_{j}]
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. This readily yields

\frac{1}{(a_{j})^{q}}\leq\frac{1}{t^{q}},\quad t\in[a_{j+1},a_{j}],\quad q\in(0,1),

which implies therefore that

\frac{a_{j}-a_{j+1}}{a_{j}^{q}}=\int_{a_{j+1}}^{a_{j}}\frac{1}{a_{j}^{q}}dt\leq\int_{a_{j+1}}^{a_{j}}\frac{1}{t^{q}}dt.

The latter tells us in turn that

\frac{a_{j}-a_{j+1}}{a_{j}^{q}}\leq\frac{1}{1-q}\left(a_{j}^{1-q}-a_{j+1}^{1-q}\right),\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

and thus we are done with the proof of the lemma.
∎

Our first convergence theorem establishes convergence rates for the
value sequence
of the alternating minimization algorithm in (
2
), (
3
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that the cost function
L
in (
1
) satisfies the assumptions in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
. Let the sequence
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}
converge to
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
, and let
L
satisfy the exponent PLK condition at
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
with

{\varphi}(t)=Mt^{1-q},\quad q\in[0,1),\quad M>0.

Then we have the following convergence rates for the sequence
\{L(x_{k},y_{k})\}
:

(i)
If
q=0
, then
\{L(x_{k},y_{k})\}
has the finite termination.

(ii)
If
q\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, then
\{L(x_{k},y_{k})\}
either has the finite termination or converges superlinearly to
L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
.

(iii)
If
q=1/2
, then
\{L(x_{k},y_{k})\}
converges linearly to
L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
.

(iv)
If
q\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, then there exists
\gamma>0
such that

L(x_{k},y_{k})-L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\leq\gamma k^{-\frac{1}{2q-1}}\;\mbox{ for all }\;k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof
.
As we know, the sequence
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
generated by (
2
) and (
3
) converges to
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, which is an
M
-stationary point of problem (
1
)
From (
6
), we have that the sequence
\{L(x^{k},y^{k})\}
is monotonically decreasing. Since
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is an accumulation point of
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
, it follows that
\{L(x^{k},y^{k})\}
converges to
L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
as
k\to\infty
.
Defining

\Psi(x,y):=L(x,y)-L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast}),

(15)

we get that the sequence of nonnegative numbers

\{\Psi(x^{k},y^{k})\}
is monotonically decreasing. Combining (
6
) and (
15
) with

\|(x_{k}^{*},y_{k}^{*})\|\leq\Big(C+\frac{1}{r_{-}}\Big)\|(x_{k},y_{k})-(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\|,\quad C>0,

(16)

leads us to the inequalities

\displaystyle\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})-\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\geq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\lambda_{k-1}}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu_{k-1}}\|y_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\,

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r_{+}}\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2r_{+}(C+1/r_{-})^{2}}\|(x^{*}_{k},y^{*}_{k})\|^{2}

with
(x^{*}_{k},y^{*}_{k})\in\partial L(x_{k},y_{k})
and
r_{-},r_{+}
taken from
{\cal H}_{1}
. The last inequality above and the imposed exponent PLK condition produce the estimate

\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})-\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\geq C_{0}\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)^{2q},

(17)

where the constant in (
17
) is defined by

C_{0}:=\frac{1}{2r_{+}(C+1/r_{-})^{2}c^{2}(1-q)^{2}}>0.

Now we are ready to proceed with verifying each assertions in (i)–(iv).

(i)
Let
q=0
. Supposing that the sequence
\{L(x_{k},y_{k})\}
is not finitely generated, we get from (
17
) that

\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})-\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\geq C_{0}\;\mbox{ for all large }\;k,

which is a clear contradiction.

(ii)
Let
q\in(0,1/2)
. Suppose that the sequence

\{\L(x_{k}),y_{k})\}
is not finitely terminated. Then it follows from (
17
) that

1+C_{0}\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)^{2q-1}\leq\frac{\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})}{\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})},

(18)

Since
q\in(0,1/2)
, we have
2q-1<0
and then deduce from (
18
) and the convergence
\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})}{\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})}=\infty.

The latter can be equivalently rewritten as

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})}{\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})}=0,

which yields the claimed superlinear convergence of

\{L(x_{k},y_{k})\}
to
L(x^{*},y^{*})
.

(iii)
For
q=1/2
, we get from (
17
) that

\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\leq\frac{1}{1+C_{0}}\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1}).

Since
1/(1+C_{0})\in(0,1)
, assertion (iii) is a consequence of the last inequality.

(iv)
If
q\in(1/2,1)
, the estimate in (
17
) tells us that

C_{0}\leq\frac{\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})-\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})}{\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)^{2q}}.

(19)

Denote
\displaystyle\Phi(t):=\frac{1}{t^{2q}}
and suppose first that there exists
\mu\in(1,\infty)
such that

\Phi\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)\leq\mu\,\Phi(\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})).

Combining the latter with (
19
) and Lemma
4.1
leads us to the relationships

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}&C_{0}\leq\displaystyle\frac{\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})-\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})}{\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)^{2q}}=\displaystyle\int_{\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})}^{\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})}\Phi\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)dt\\
&\displaystyle\leq\mu\int_{\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})}^{\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})}\Phi\big(\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\big)dt\\
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{1-2q}\big[\big(\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\big)^{1-2q}-\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)^{1-2q}\big]\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2q-1}\big[\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)^{1-2q}-\big(\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\big)^{1-2q}\big],\end{array}

which imply by
2q-1>0
that

\frac{C_{0}(2q-1)}{\mu}\leq\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)^{1-2q}-\big(\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\big)^{1-2q},\quad k\geq k_{0},

(22)

with some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Letting
j>k_{0}
, we deduce from (
22
) that

\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{j}\frac{C_{0}(2q-1)}{P}\leq\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{j}\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)^{1-2q}-\big(\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\big)^{1-2q},

which ensures in turn that

\frac{(j+1-k_{0})C_{0}(2q-1)}{\mu}\leq\big(\Psi(x_{j},y_{j})\big)^{1-2q}-\big(\Psi(x_{k_{0}},y_{k_{0}})\big)^{1-2q}.

Consequently, we get the estimate

\big(\Psi(x_{j},y_{j})\big)^{1-2q}\geq\frac{(j+1-k_{0})C_{0}(2q-1)}{\mu}+\big(\Psi(x_{k_{0}},y_{k_{0}})\big)^{1-2q}.

Since the function
t\mapsto t^{-\frac{1}{2q-1}}
is decreasing for
q\in(1/2,1)
, it follows that

\Psi(x_{j},y_{j})\leq\left(\frac{(j+1-k_{0})C_{0}(2q-1)}{\mu}+\big(\Psi(x_{k_{0}},y_{k_{0}})\big)^{1-2q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2q-1}}.

This allows us to find a number
\eta>0
such that

\Psi(x_{j},y_{j})\leq\eta j^{-\frac{1}{2q-1}},

which therefore confirms the claimed convergence rate in (iv) for this case.

Now we examine the remaining situation in
(iv)
where the number

\mu
mentioned above doesn’t exist, i.e., for any

\mu\in(1,\infty)
we have

\Phi\big(\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)>\mu\,\Phi\big(\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})\big)\;\mbox{ when }\;k\;\mbox{ is sufficiently large}.

Fix
\mu\in(1,\infty)
and define
\mu_{1}:=\frac{1}{P^{\frac{1}{2q}}}
. Then it follows from the definitions that
\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\leq\mu_{1}\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})
and therefore

\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})^{1-2q}\geq\mu_{1}^{1-2q}\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})^{1-2q}

since
1-2q<0
. Subtracting
\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})^{1-2q}

on both sides of the last inequality gives us the estimate

\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})^{1-2q}-\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})^{1-2q}\geq\big(\mu_{1}^{1-2q}-1\big)\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})^{1-2q}.

Note that
\mu_{1}\in(0,1)
and therefore
\mu_{1}^{1-2q}>1
for all
q\in(1/2,1)
. The convergence
\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})\to 0
yields
\psi(x^{k})^{1-2q}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
, and hence

\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})^{1-2q}-\Psi(x_{k-1},y_{k-1})^{1-2q}\geq\frac{C_{0}(2q-1)}{\mu}

for large
k
. The rest of the proof is similar to the above arguments based on (
22
), and thus we are done with verifying (iv) and the entire theorem.
\hfill\triangle

The second theorem in this section provides convergence rates for the
sequence of iterates

\{x_{k}\}
of the alternating minimization algorithm in (
2
) and (
3
) under the exponent PKL condition. The main improvement is in the lower exponent assertion (ii), and we’ll concentrate on its proof.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that the assumptions in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
are fulfilled for the cost function
L
in (
1
), that the sequence of iterates
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}
in the alternating minimization algorithm (
2
), (
3
) converges to the
M
-stationary point
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
of (
1
), and that
L
satisfies the exponent PLK property at
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
with

\varphi(t)=Mt^{1-q},\quad q\in[0,1),\quad M>0.

Then the following assertions hold:

(i)
If
q=0
, then the sequence
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}
converges in a finite number of steps.

(ii)
If
q\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, then
(x_{k},y_{k})
either has the finite termination or converges superlinearly to
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
.

(iii)
If
q=1/2
, then
\{(x_{k},y_{\}})
converges linearly to
(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
.

(iv)
If
q\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, then there exists
\gamma>0
such that

(x_{k},y_{k})-(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\leq\gamma k^{-\frac{1-q}{2q-1}}.

Proof
. Assertions (i), (iii), and (iv) follow from
(
attouch2010proximal
, Theorem 11)
, where a linear convergence version of (ii) is also obtained. Now we do much better as formulated in (ii). It follows from
(
attouch2010proximal
, Theorem 3.1)
that

\varphi\big(L(x_{k},y_{k})-L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\big)-\varphi\big(L(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})-L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})\big)\geq\frac{1}{\vartheta}\frac{\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|^{2}}{\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|},

(23)

where
\vartheta:=2r_{+}(C+1/r_{-})
and
z_{k}=(x_{k},y_{k})
with some
C>0
. Given
r\in(0,1)
, we have the following two possibilities for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

(a)

\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|\geq r\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|
.

(b)

\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|<r\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|
.

In case
(a)
, it follows from (
23
) with
\varphi(t)=Mt^{1-q}
with the notation
\Psi(x,y):=L(x,y)-L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})
that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|&\leq\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta M}{r}\Psi(z_{k})^{1-q}-\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta M}{r}\Psi(z_{k+1})^{1-q}\\
&\leq r\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|+\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta M}{r}\Psi(z_{k})^{1-q}-\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta M}{r}\Psi(z_{k+1})^{1-q}.\end{array}

Conversely, in case
(b)
, we immediately get

\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|\leq r\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|\leq r\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|+\frac{\vartheta M}{r}\Psi(z_{k})^{1-q}-\frac{\vartheta M}{r}\Psi(z_{k+1})^{1-q}.

Therefore, in both cases it holds that

\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|\leq r\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|+\frac{\vartheta M}{r}\Psi(z_{k})^{1-q}-\frac{\vartheta M}{r}\Psi(z_{k+1})^{1-q}.

(24)

The latter readily implies that for any
\l\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\sum_{j=k}^{k+l}\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|\leq\frac{r}{1-r}\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|+\frac{\vartheta M}{r(1-r)}\big(\Psi(z_{k})^{1-q}-\Psi(z_{k+l+1})^{1-q}\big).

(25)

Define further the value (which is a finite number under the basic PLK condition as follows from Lemma
2.1
(ii))

s_{k}:=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|.

Passing to the limit
l\to\infty
in (
25
) leads us to

s_{k}\leq\frac{r}{(1-r)}\|z_{k}-z_{k-1}\|+\frac{\vartheta M}{r(1-r)}\Psi(z_{k})^{1-q}.

Using the obtained inequality together with (
6
) and
L(z_{k-1})-L(z_{k})\leq L(z_{k-1})-L(x^{\ast},y^{\ast})=\Psi(z_{k-1})
gives us the estimate

s_{k}\leq\frac{r}{(1-r)}\big(\Psi(z_{k-1})\big)^{1/2}+\frac{\vartheta M}{r(1-r)}\Psi(z_{k})^{1-q}.

(26)

When
q\in(0,1/2)
, it follows from (
26
) that

s_{k}\leq\frac{r}{(1-r)}\big(\Psi(z_{k-1})\big)^{1/2}+\frac{\vartheta M}{r(1-r)}\Psi(z_{k})^{1/2}.

(27)

Since the sequence
\{\psi(x^{k})\}
is monotonically decreasing, we get

s_{k}\leq\frac{r^{2}+\vartheta M}{(1-r)}\big(\Psi(z_{k-1})\big)^{1/2},

and the result follows from Theorem
4.2
(ii), which completes the proof.

5
Discussions on Applications to Noncooperative Games and Behavioral Science

As mentioned in Section
1
,
convergence rate
of numerical algorithms are of crucial importance to solve various classes of
noncooperative games
in game theory and applications. From this viewpoint, the convergence results obtained in Section
4
for the alternating minimization algorithm present valuable refinements in practically important models of
alternating games
with symmetric or asymmetric costs to move. We refer the reader to, e.g., Cruz Neto et al.
neto2013learning
and Soubeyran et al.
soubeyran2019variational
for some models of
worthwhile-to-move potential games
played in asymmetric metric spaces, where each agent plays, in alternation, a worthwhile move given the action done by the other. The central concept of such behavioral games is that of an
individual worthwhile move
that
improves a player’s payoff without requiring a too high cost to move if other players stay at the status quo. That is, it better satisfies the needs and desires of this individual without too many sacrifices for himself or herself. This concept is borrowed from the
variational rationality approach
of
individual and social stay and change human dynamics; see, e.g.,
soubeyran2009variational
;
soubeyran2010variational
and other developments documented in

{\rm https://sites.google.com/view/antoine-soubeyran}

for applications of different optimization algorithms to models behavioral science.

The obtained results on fast convergence and especially on the finite termination for the alternating optimization algorithm are of
striking importance
for applications to such models. They show that the finite termination holds when the payoff functions of two players are sharp enough concerning the worthwhile change in their own actions. A lot of applications to game theory can be expected in both static and dynamic frameworks. The concept of the
existence of temporary/permanent traps
will be among central topics of our future research; see
ms19
for the unified approach of variational analysis and variational stationarity to these issues.
New potential functions will be used to get novel results on convergence rates under low exponent PLK conditions.

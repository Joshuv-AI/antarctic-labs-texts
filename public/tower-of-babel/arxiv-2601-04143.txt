1
Statement of the local result

Let
R
be a residually discrete local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residual field
k=R/\mathfrak{m}
.
Let
E
be a (finitely presented) étale
R
-algebra.

Theorem 1
.

There exists
s_{1},\dots,s_{m}
in
E
that are comaximal mod.
\mathfrak{m}
, i.e. their images generate the unit ideal in
E/\mathfrak{m}E
,
and each

E[1/s_{i}]
is a standard étale
R
-algebra.

Note that
E/\mathfrak{m}E
is a (finitely presented) unramified
k
-algebra and so, following
[
LQ
,
L
]
,
is a finite product of monogene separable extensions of
k
.

Using constructive Zariski Main Theorem
[
ACL
]
we get that there exists a finite
R
-subalgebra
B
of
E

and elements
s_{i}\in B
, comaximal in
E
, such that
B_{i}=B[1/{s_{i}}]\cong E[1/s_{i}]
for all
i
.

Some
B_{i}/\mathfrak{m}B_{i}
may be the trivial algebra. We discard them, which explains why in the end
result, we only get a family which is comaximal
residually
.

Each
B_{i}/\mathfrak{m}B_{i}
is a corresponding product of monogene separable extensions.
By refining
s_{i}
, we can assume that each
B_{i}/\mathfrak{m}B_{i}
is a itself a monogene separable extension of
k
.

Consider now

We have

Since

A[1/e_{i}]

A candidate for a standard étale presentation

We now consider the étale algebra

5
What happens if
E
is étale

We have a surjection
u:S\rightarrow E
, and
S
and
E
are finitely presented, so the kernel
I
is an ideal of
S

which is finitely generated. Furthermore
S/\mathfrak{m}S=E/\mathfrak{m}E=k[y][1/g]
.

We have the exact sequence
0\rightarrow I/I^{2}\rightarrow S/I^{2}\rightarrow E\rightarrow 0
, and, since
E
is étale and
I/I^{2}
of square
0
, this exact
sequence is split (in the sense of existence of a section). Furthermore, if an exact sequence
0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
is split, then the sequence

0\rightarrow A/\mathfrak{m}A\rightarrow B/\mathfrak{m}B\rightarrow C/\mathfrak{m}C\rightarrow 0
is still exact.
Since
S/I^{2}\rightarrow E
is an isomorphism mod.
\mathfrak{m}
, it follows that
I/I^{2}
is
0
mod.
\mathfrak{m}
, and so, since

I
is finitely generated, we have
I=I^{2}
by Nakayama. This implies that
I
is generated by an idempotent element
e=1-f
.
Thus we get
f
in
S
such that
I[1/f]=0
and
u(f)=1
. Then
S[1/f]
is isomorphic to
E
which is standard étale.

A global version

We now take

8
What happens if
E
is flat and unramified

Following
[
Ray
]
, we can prove that if
E
is
flat
and (finitely presented) unramified,
then
E
is locally standard étale, and so étale.

As before, let
I
is the kernel of the map
u:S\rightarrow E
. We have the exact sequence

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow S\rightarrow E\rightarrow 0
.
Furthermore the map
S/\mathfrak{m}S\rightarrow E/\mathfrak{m}E
is an isomorphism.
Since
I
is a finitely generated ideal of
S
, it is enough, by Nakayama, to show that
I/\mathfrak{m}I=0
.
We can then use the following Lemma (instantiated with
A=R
and
J=\mathfrak{m}
) to conclude,
since
E
is flat over
R
. We use the constructive definition of flatness in
[
LQ
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
L,M,N
be
A
-module and
J
an ideal of
A
. We assume
u:L\rightarrow M
and
v:M\rightarrow N

such that
0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0

is exact. If the module
N
is flat, then the map
L/JL\rightarrow M/JM
is injective.

Proof.

Let
x
be in
L
such that
u(x)=0
in
M/JM
.
We then as a relation
u(x)=rm
for some column vector
r
in
J
and some row vector
m
in
M
.
We then have
0=v(u(x))=rv(m)
in
N
. Since
N
is flat, there exists a matrix
p
in
A
and
a row vector
n
in
N
such that
rp=0
and
pn=v(m)
. Since
v
is surjective we can find
m_{1}
in
M

such that
n=v(m_{1})
. We then have
v(m-pm_{1})=0
and so
m-pm_{1}=u(l)
for some
l
row vector in
L
.
We then have
u(x)=rm=rm-rpm_{1}=ru(l)=u(rl)
and so
x=rl
since
u
is injective. But this means
that
x=0
in
L/JL
as wanted.
∎

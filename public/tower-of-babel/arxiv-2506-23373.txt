1.
Introduction and main results

The Macdonald polynomials
P_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
indexed by partitions are symmetric polynomials in infinite variables
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
with coefficients from the field
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
. They are defined as the unique basis for the algebra of symmetric functions over
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
, characterized by triangularity and orthogonality axioms
[
Haiman99
,
Mac95
]
. The Macdonald polynomials
P_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
unify several important families of symmetric functions, such as Hall–Littlewood functions (
q=0
),
q
-Whittaker functions (
t=0
), Jack functions (set
q=t^{\alpha}
and let
t\rightarrow 1
), monomial symmetric functions (
q=0
and
t=1
) and Schur functions (
q=t
).

The
modified Macdonald polynomials

\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
are deduced from
P_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
by a plethystic substitution, which are symmetric polynomials in
X
with
\mathbb{N}[q,t]
–coefficients. The combinatorial interpretation of these coefficients was a celebrated breakthrough due to Haglund, Haiman and Loehr
[
HHL04
]
, which connects the coefficients of
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
to permutation statistics on arbitrary fillings of Young diagrams. In the sequel, we represent a Young diagram in a French manner and the precise definitions of some statistics are postponed to Section
2
. Throughout the paper, two formulas are regarded to be different if the summands are not equal termwise.

Let
\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
denote the set of fillings of the Young diagram of
\lambda
where each cell is filled with a positive integer. Each filling is also called as a tableau.
For
\tau\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
, let
x^{\tau}
be the product of
x_{i}
’s whenever
i
is an entry of
\tau
. Haglund, Haiman and Loehr
[
HHL04
]
showed that

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)}x^{\tau}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}t^{\mathsf{inv}(\tau)}.

(1.1)

Here
\mathsf{maj}(T)
and
\mathsf{inv}(T)
are natural extensions, respectively of the major index and inversion numbers for permutations. Recently, Corteel, Haglund, Mandelshtam, Mason and Williams
[
CHO19
,
CHO22
]
introduced a new statistic
\mathsf{quinv}
, called queue inversion, and proposed a conjecture on an equivalent form of (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)}x^{\tau}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}t^{\mathsf{quinv}(\tau)}.

(1.2)

This conjecture was subsequently confirmed by Ayyer, Mandelshtam and Martin
[
AMM23
]
by verifying that the RHS of (
1.2
) satisfies certain orthogonal and triangular conditions which uniquely characterizes the modified Macdonald polynomials
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
.

Interestingly, the original motivation in
[
CHO22
]
to study the combinatorial formula of
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
stems from the search for new formulas with fewer summands and such formulas are named as “compact formulas”. Our of their main results is a compact formula for
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
based on (
1.1
). Sooner after, Mandelshtam
[
O:23
,
O:24
]
found a compact version of (
1.2
). More precisely, both formulas reduce the sum on the RHS of (
1.1
) or (
1.2
) to the sum over

\mathsf{inv}
-sorted (inversion sorted) or
\mathsf{quinv}
-sorted (queue inversion sorted) tableaux. That is, for
\varepsilon\in\{\mathsf{inv},\mathsf{quinv}\}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)=\sum_{\sigma\textrm{ is }\varepsilon\text{-}\textrm{sorted}}\textrm{perm}(\sigma)x^{\sigma}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\sigma)}t^{\varepsilon(\sigma)}

(1.3)

summed over all
\varepsilon
-sorted tableaux, where
\textrm{perm}(\sigma)
is a
t
-multinomial, counting different ways to permute columns of equal height within a sorted tableau
\sigma
.

In parallel to combinatorial formulas of
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
, Garbali and Wheeler derived new formulas for the modified Macdonald polynomials in terms of the monomial symmetric functions
[
GW20
]
through an integrable lattice model construction. This is a sophisticated extension of the formula by Kerov, Kirillov and Reshetikhin
[
KKR:88
,
K98
,
KKR:882
]
.

Inspired by these recent development, our research on the modified Macdonald polynomials
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
started from a refined equality of
of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), which was conjectured by Ayyer, Mandelshtam and Martin
[
AMM23
]
. To be precise, two fillings
\sigma,\tau
of the Young diagram of
\lambda
are called
row-equivalent
if the multisets of entries in the
i
th row of
\sigma
and
\tau
are exactly the same for all
i
. Somewhat to our surprise, the triples

(\mathsf{maj},\mathsf{inv},\mathsf{quinv})
and
(\mathsf{maj},\mathsf{quinv},\mathsf{inv})
have the same distribution over any equivalent class of a rectangular filling.

Theorem 1

(
[
JL24
]
)
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition, let
\sigma
be a filling of the Young diagram of
\lambda
, and let
[\sigma]
denote the row-equivalent class of
\sigma
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in[\sigma]}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}t^{\mathsf{inv}(\tau)}=\sum_{\tau\in[\sigma]}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}t^{\mathsf{quinv}(\tau)}.

(1.4)

If the diagram of
\lambda
is a rectangle, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in[\sigma]}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}t^{\mathsf{inv}(\tau)}u^{\mathsf{quinv}(\tau)}=\sum_{\tau\in[\sigma]}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}u^{\mathsf{inv}(\tau)}t^{\mathsf{quinv}(\tau)}.

(1.5)

Our main contribution of the current paper bridges the gap between combinatorial formulas (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) and explicit computable expressions of the modified Macdonald polynomials. In the first step, we introduce a family
\mathcal{A}
of sixteen new statistics on fillings of a Young diagram where each statistic in
\mathcal{A}
plays the same role as the statistic
\mathsf{inv}
in (
1.4
). This produces new combinatorial formulas of
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
[\sigma]
denote the row-equivalent class of
\sigma
and Let
\mathcal{A}
be the family of statistics in Definition
4
. Then for every
\eta\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in[\sigma]}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}t^{\mathsf{quinv}(\tau)}=\sum_{\tau\in[\sigma]}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}t^{\eta(\tau)}.

(1.6)

Consequently, the modified Macdonald polynomial is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)}x^{\tau}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\tau)}t^{\eta(\tau)}.

(1.7)

Building upon (
1.7
), we introduce canonical tableaux and dual canonical tableaux to present new compact formulas for the modified Macdonald polynomials in the second step.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\circ}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{C}_{\dagger}(\lambda)
be the sets of canonical and dual canonical tableaux of the Young diagram of
\lambda
, respectively. Then for
\varepsilon\in\{\circ,\dagger\}
and four statistics
\eta
of
\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)}d_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)x^{\sigma}q^{\mathsf{maj}(\sigma)}t^{\eta(\sigma)},

(1.8)

where
d_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)
is a product of
t
-multinomials.

Here the canonical fillings are defined as special sorted tableaux appearing in
[
CHO22
,
O:23
]
. An advantage to making use of canonical or dual canonical tableaux lies in the fact that (
1.8
) can be further used to generate computable monomial expansions of the modified Macdonald polynomials. One of them is consistent with the formula given by Garbali and Wheeler
[
GW20
]
.

Before we state the explicit expressions of the modified Macdonald polynomials, let us first review relevant notations from
[
GW20
]
. The
q
-analogue of the binomial coefficients is defined as

\displaystyle{n\brack k}_{q}=\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[k]_{q}![n-k]_{q}!},

for
n\geq k\geq 0
, where
[n]_{q}=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}=(1-q^{n})/(1-q)
for
n\geq 1
,
[0]!_{q}=[0]_{q}=1
and
[n]!_{q}=[n]_{q}[n-1]_{q}\cdots[1]_{q}
. For any sequences of nonnegative integers
\nu=(\nu^{1}\leq\cdots\leq\nu^{N})
and
\tilde{\nu}=(\tilde{\nu}^{1}\leq\cdots\leq\tilde{\nu}^{N})
such that
\tilde{\nu}^{N}=\nu^{N}
, we define

\displaystyle\Phi_{\nu|\tilde{\nu}}(\xi,z;t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}t^{\xi(s,\tilde{\nu})}\prod_{1\leq k<N}z^{s_{N}^{k}-s_{k}^{k}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\nu}^{k+1}-s_{k+1}^{1,k}\\
\tilde{\nu}^{k}-s_{k}^{1,k}\end{bmatrix}_{t}\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}\begin{bmatrix}s_{k+1}^{i}\\
s_{k}^{i}\end{bmatrix}_{t}\in\mathbb{N}[z,t],

(1.9)

where the sum is ranged over all sequences
\{s_{k}^{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq k\leq N}
satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq s_{k}^{k}\leq s_{k+1}^{k}\leq\cdots\leq s_{N-1}^{k}\leq s_{N}^{k}=\nu^{k}-\nu^{k-1},

(1.10)

with
s_{i}^{j,k}=\sum_{a=j}^{k}s_{i}^{a}
. Analogously, define

\displaystyle\phi_{\nu|\tilde{\nu}}(\xi,z;t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}t^{\xi(s,\tilde{\nu})}\prod_{0\leq k<N}z^{s_{N}^{k}-s_{k}^{k}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\nu}^{k+1}-s_{k+1}^{0,k}\\
\tilde{\nu}^{k}-s_{k}^{0,k}\end{bmatrix}_{t}\prod_{0\leq i\leq k}\begin{bmatrix}s_{k+1}^{i}\\
s_{k}^{i}\end{bmatrix}_{t}\in\mathbb{N}[z,t],

(1.11)

which is summed over all sequences
\{s_{k}^{i}\}_{0\leq i<N,i\leq k\leq N}
such that

\displaystyle 0=s_{0}^{0}\leq s_{k}^{k}\leq s_{k+1}^{k}\leq\cdots\leq s_{N-1}^{k}\leq s_{N}^{k}=\nu^{k+1}-\nu^{k},

(1.12)

with
s_{i}^{j,k}=\sum_{a=j}^{k}s_{i}^{a}
. For
\gamma\in\{0,1\}
, let

\displaystyle\xi_{\gamma}(s,\tilde{\nu})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\leq i\leq k<N}(s_{k+1}^{i}-s_{k}^{i})(\tilde{\nu}^{k}-s_{k}^{i,k}).

Finally for any partition
\lambda
, let
\lambda^{\prime}
be the conjugate of
\lambda
, that is,
the Young diagram of
\lambda^{\prime}
is obtained by interchanging rows and columns of the Young diagram of
\lambda
. Let

\displaystyle n(\lambda)=\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}{2}=\sum_{i\geq 1}(i-1)\lambda_{i}.

Theorem 4
.

The modified Macdonald polynomial
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
equals

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X;q,t)

\displaystyle=t^{n(\lambda^{\prime})}\sum_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t^{-1})m_{\mu}(X)\quad\mbox{ where }

(1.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\nu\}}t^{\chi_{1}(\nu)}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\Phi_{\nu_{i+1,j}|\nu_{i,j}}(\xi_{1},q^{j-i}t^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};t),

(1.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\nu\}}t^{\chi_{2}(\nu)}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\Phi_{\nu_{i+1,j}|\nu_{i,j}}(\xi_{1},q^{i}t^{\lambda_{j-i+1}-\lambda_{j}};t),

(1.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\nu\}}q^{n(\lambda)}t^{n(\lambda^{\prime})-\chi_{3}(\nu)}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\phi_{\nu_{i+1,j}|\nu_{i,j}}(\xi_{0},q^{i-j}t^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}};t^{-1}),

(1.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\nu\}}q^{n(\lambda)}t^{n(\lambda^{\prime})-\chi_{4}(\nu)}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\phi_{\nu_{i+1,j}|\nu_{i,j}}(\xi_{0},q^{-i}t^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j-i+1}};t^{-1}).

(1.17)

with the sum running over sequences
\nu_{i,j}=(\nu_{i,j}^{1}\leq\cdots\leq\nu_{i,j}^{N})
of nonnegative integers,
1\leq i\leq j\leq n=\ell(\lambda)
,
N=\ell(\mu)
, subject to the conditions

\displaystyle\nu_{i,j}^{N}=\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1},

\displaystyle\qquad\textrm{ for all }\,\,1\leq i\leq j\leq n,

(1.18)

\displaystyle\nu_{i+1,i}^{k}=0,

\displaystyle\qquad\textrm{ for all }\,\,1\leq i\leq n,1\leq k\leq N,

(1.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\nu_{i,j}^{k}=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k},

\displaystyle\qquad\textrm{ for all }\,\,1\leq k\leq N,

(1.20)

where
\chi_{i}(\nu)
for

are defined as

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\bigg(\binom{\nu_{i,j}^{k}-\nu_{i,j}^{k-1}}{2}+\sum_{\ell>j}(\nu_{i,j}^{k}-\nu_{i,j}^{k-1})(\nu_{i,\ell}^{k}-\nu_{i+1,\ell}^{k-1})\bigg),

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\bigg(\binom{\nu_{i,j}^{k}-\nu_{i,j}^{k-1}}{2}+\sum_{\ell>j}(\nu_{i,j}^{k}-\nu_{i,j}^{k-1})(\nu_{\ell-j+i,\ell}^{k}-\nu_{\ell-j+i+1,\ell}^{k-1})\bigg),

\displaystyle\chi_{3}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\sum_{\ell>j}(\nu_{i,j}^{k}-\nu_{i,j}^{k-1})(\nu_{i,\ell}^{k-1}-\nu_{i+1,\ell}^{k}),

\displaystyle\chi_{4}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\sum_{\ell>j}(\nu_{i,j}^{k}-\nu_{i,j}^{k-1})(\nu_{\ell-j+i,\ell}^{k-1}-\nu_{\ell-j+i+1,\ell}^{k}).

) was first found by Garbali and Wheeler

2.
Preliminaries and notations

A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})
of
n
is sequence of positive integers such that
\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i<k
and
|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}=n
. Each
\lambda_{i}
is called a part of
\lambda
and
k
is the length of
\lambda
, denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
.

The Young diagram of
\lambda
, denoted by
\mathsf{dg}(\lambda)
, is an array of boxes with
\lambda_{i}
boxes in the
i
th row from bottom to top, with the first box in each row left-justified. A box has coordinate
(i,j)
if it is in the
i
th row from bottom to top and
j
th column from left to right. Let
\lambda^{\prime}
be the conjugate (or transpose) of
\lambda
, that is,
\mathsf{dg}(\lambda^{\prime})
is obtained from
\mathsf{dg}(\lambda)
by reflecting across the main diagonal (boxes with coordinate
(i,i)
).

Let
\Lambda
be the algebra of symmetric functions in infinitely many variables
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
, with coefficients from
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
. All bases of
\Lambda
are indexed by partitions, such as the
monomial symmetric functions

m_{\mu}(X)
, the
elementary symmetric functions

e_{\mu}(X)
, the
complete homogeneous symmetric functions

h_{\mu}(X)
, the
power-sum symmetric functions

p_{\mu}(X)
, and the
Schur functions

s_{\mu}(X)
; for instance see the books
[
Hag08
,
Mac95
,
Stanley
]
.

Define the scalar product as follows:

\displaystyle\langle p_{\lambda},p_{\mu}\rangle=z_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda\mu}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{1-q^{\lambda_{i}}}{1-t^{\lambda_{i}}},

Dynamic prediction of adolescent sports injury probability based on the improved ARIMA model in the background of digital physical education teaching
Authors: Kai Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aims to solve the problem of dynamic prediction of sports injuries in adolescent physical education teaching. By integrating multi-source heterogeneous data and improving time series modeling, a real-time early warning system suitable for digital education scenarios is constructed. In view of the limitations of traditional ARIMA models in capturing the suddenness and multi-frequency characteristics coordination of injury events, this study innovatively integrates Time-Varying Filtering Empirical Mode Decomposition (TVF-EMD) and Mixed-Frequency Data Sampling (MIDAS) techniques to achieve separate modeling of injury trend and fluctuation terms, and establish cross-scale correlation between second-level sports data and quarterly physical fitness assessment. In an empirical study involving 217 middle school student athletes, the model significantly improved prediction accuracy (multi-scenario average RMSE reduced by > 40%), with an accuracy rate of 86.2% in high-intensity basketball training, a false alarm rate of 5.2% for ACL injuries in football, and the critical threshold of fracture risk was determined when the monthly running distance exceeded 280 km in track and field training. According to the research, Key technical breakthroughs include dynamically optimizing model parameters through particle filtering to adapt to sudden changes in teaching load, using LightGBM residual correction mechanism to improve the ability to capture sudden injury events, and verifying the reliability of three-dimensional motion trajectory monitoring in virtual reality teaching (error < 12.3 mm). The research results provide core methodological support for constructing a real-time and personalized campus sports health protection system, and will be further validated through multi-center cohort studies to promote clinical translational applications.

Introduction

The rapid development of digital sports technology offers new possibilities for adolescent sports health management. With the widespread use of smart wearables, motion sensors and virtual reality technology, real-time monitoring of students’ movement status has become an important part of educational practice1,2. However, the incidence of sports injuries among teenagers remains high, with about 30% of middle school students worldwide having their normal studies affected by sports injuries3. Traditional physical education models, which rely on experience to assess injury risk, struggle to meet individualized training needs. While digital technology can collect massive amounts of sports data, it still faces challenges in extracting effective features from unstructured information and building dynamic predictive models. For example, the time series characteristics of indicators such as heart rate variability and movement stability have not been fully exploited4. In this context, exploring dynamic injury prediction methods based on time series analysis is of urgent significance for optimizing digital physical education teaching models.

Existing prediction models have significant limitations when dealing with complex sports scenarios. Although the classical ARIMA model can capture linear trends, it is insufficient in handling sudden injury events and non-stationary data5. Studies have shown that ARIMA models built solely based on historical damage rates have a prediction error of up to 28%, failing to meet the requirements of real-time early warning6. As show in Fig. 1, In addition, adolescent sports injuries are influenced by multiple dimensions, including functional movement deficits, environmental mutations, and cumulative training load effects7. For example, insufficient core stability can increase the risk of knee joint injury by 2.3 times8. Existing studies mostly model with a single data source, ignoring the synergistic effects of multi-frequency features such as daily load versus monthly physical fitness tests9. Therefore, there is an urgent need to build a hybrid prediction framework that combines biomechanical indicators with dynamic environmental variables to break through the static analysis limitations of traditional models.

This study innovative proposes an improved ARIMA prediction system based on multi-source data fusion. By integrating time-varying filtering empirical mode decomposition (TVF EMD) with the particle filter algorithm, separate modeling of damage risk trend term and wave term was achieved for the first time6,10. Introduce the Mixed Frequency Data Sampling (MIDAS) technique to incorporate high-frequency motion data of wearable devices (such as acceleration per second) and low-frequency functional assessment metrics (such as quarterly FMS scores) into a unified analysis framework11. Experiments show that the accuracy of the model is 42.8% higher than that of conventional ARIMA (validation set RMSE 0.142 vs. 0.29, p < 0.01) [reference Table 6 data]12. As show in Fig. 2, By dynamically adjusting the weight distribution of the LightGBM sub-model, the system can capture the nonlinearity risk caused by sudden changes in training intensity13. This hierarchical modeling strategy provides adaptive early warning mechanisms for digital physical education classes, such as automatically triggering intervention programs when the coefficient of variation of real-time heart rate exceeds the threshold4.

Literature review

Limitations of traditional ARIMA models in sports injury prediction

Traditional ARIMA models are widely used in the field of health risk prediction due to their adaptability to linear time series data, but they show weakness in complex sports scenarios18. The study found that the non-stationarity and sudden fluctuations of adolescent sports injury data led to an ARIMA prediction error rate of up to 28%, especially with a significant increase in the misjudgment rate in acute injury events19. Meniscus injury predictions for adolescent football players, for example, show that traditional ARIMA models can only capture seasonal trends and fail to identify the acute risk window within 24 h after high-intensity training20. This defect stems from the model’s insufficient ability to integrate nonlinear relationships and multi-source heterogeneous data, such as ignoring the interaction between real-time exercise load collected by wearable devices and long-term biomechanical indicators21. As a result, improving the ARIMA model to fit dynamic motion scenarios has become the focus of research.

Breakthrough progress in multi-model fusion technology

In recent years, hybrid prediction frameworks have significantly improved prediction accuracy by fusing ARIMA with machine learning models19. Studies have confirmed that combining ARIMA with long Short-Term memory networks (LSTM) can simultaneously capture linear trends and nonlinear residuals of injury rates22, reducing the root mean square error in basketball player ankle sprain prediction by 19%21. Another study of junior track and field athletes showed that after introducing gradient boosting decision trees (LightGBM) to process ARIMA resists, the model’s early warning accuracy for stress fractures caused by over training increased to 81%12. The core advantage of such hybrid models lies in breaking through the limitations of a single algorithm, such as parsing multivariate temporal dependencies through LSTM or using LightGBM to screen high-dimensional biomechanical features23. However, existing research has mostly focused on competitive sports scenarios, and the fusion methods for multi-frequency data (such as daily exercise load and monthly physical fitness assessment) in digital physical education teaching still need to be further developed.

Dynamic parameter optimization and cross-domain method transfer

Dynamic parameter optimization techniques such as particle filter provide new ideas for the improvement of the ARIMA model6. In lithium-ion battery life prediction, by adjusting the ARIMA coefficients in real time through particle filtering, the prediction error of the model for the decay trajectory was reduced to 3.5%, providing a reference for the parameter drift problem in sports injury prediction6. The study confirmed that the risk of sports injury in adolescents is affected by time-varying factors such as environmental temperature and course intensity, and dynamically updating the difference order of ARIMA can improve the weekly prediction stability by 22%11. In addition, the mixed-frequency data sampling (MIDAS) technique developed in the field of economics has been introduced into sports science, successfully integrating high-frequency heart rate data with low-frequency functional assessment indicators, improving the timeliness of shoulder injury prediction for swimmers by 35%9. The application of these cross-domain methods indicates that dynamic optimization and multi-frequency data fusion are key paths to improving the predictive performance of ARIMA.

Data-driven intervention and adaptation to educational scenarios

The digital physical education teaching scenario poses unique requirements for prediction models, which need to take into account both the timeliness and operability of educational intervention16. Real-time early warning systems based on the ARIMA improved model can reduce teachers’ decision response time to within 10 min, such as automatically pushing adjusted training programs when core stability drops sharply17. An empirical study of middle school basketball classes showed that the intervention system combined with the ARIMA LightGBM hybrid model reduced the incidence of muscle strain by 27% while maintaining an 85% completion rate of teaching progress15. However, the existing models still have insufficient adaptability to educational scenario-specific data, such as the unclear association mechanism between three-dimensional motion trajectories and injury risk in virtual reality training4. Future research needs to further explore the deep integration of multi modal data, such as visual motion capture and physiological signals, and develop lightweight models to meet the application requirements in regions with uneven educational resources16.

Methodology: construction of an improved hybrid ARIMA framework driven by multi-source data

Multi-source data acquisition and sensor configuration

This study included 217 adolescent athletes (128 males and 89 females; age 14.2 ± 1.3 years), with data collection period from September 2023 to March 2024. Inclusion criteria for participants: ① Regular training ≥ 4 times per week; ② No chronic motor system diseases (confirmed by the school doctor’s physical examination); ③ The guardian signs the informed consent form. Exclusion criteria: ① Major sports injuries within the past 6 months (requiring training cessation for at least 2 weeks); ② Sensor wearing compliance < 90%. The definition of injury events adopts a dual verification mechanism: (a) triggering an alarm threshold by wearable devices (such as knee joint inversion angle > 28 ° for 0.2 s); (b) Acute injuries confirmed by the school doctor’s clinical diagnosis requiring cessation of training for ≥ 24 h, or stress fractures confirmed by imaging (abnormal T2 signal on tibial MRI ≥ Grade 2). The data collection environment covers basketball courts (wooden floors), track and field courts (plastic tracks), and VR classrooms (temperature 22 ± 2 ° C).

Three level criteria were used for the diagnosis of acute injury: (1) biomechanical threshold triggered by the device (such as knee varus angle > 28 ° for 0.2 s); (2) clinical diagnosis basis: Ottawa rule was used for ankle/foot injury (sensitivity 98%); Other joints were examined by special examination (such as Lachman test of knee joint); (3) MRI confirmed suspected stress fracture (fredericson grade ≥ 2).

Nonlinear residual correction (LightGBM Integration)

The baseline parameters of LightGBM were determined through grid search: learning rate of 0.05 (range 0.01–0.1), tree depth of 5 (range 3–7), and feature sampling ratio of 80%. These initial values were based on the performance on the validation set in the preliminary experiment (RMSE = 0.23→0.18).

The state space model of explicit particle filtering includes ARIMA parameters (autoregressive order, differential order, moving average order), which are updated in real-time based on input features (heart rate variability, ambient temperature, joint angle covariance). The covariance of process noise is set as a fixed diagonal matrix, and the observation matrix is assigned differentiated weight coefficients for different parameters. The determination of injury events adopts a three-level medical standard: firstly, the device triggers a biomechanical threshold alarm (such as when the knee joint inversion angle exceeds 28 degrees and lasts for 0.2 s), secondly, clinical diagnosis is made by the school doctor based on the Ottawa ankle joint rules (this standard has a sensitivity of 98% for acute injuries), and finally MRI imaging confirmation is performed on suspected stress fracture cases (which must meet the Fredericson T2 signal intensity grading of grade 2 or above for the tibia).

Elucidate the cross frequency correlation principle between quarterly physical fitness assessment data (such as FMS scores) and second level motion signals: By using the exponential decay kernel function of MIDAS technology, low-frequency physical fitness indicators are dynamically mapped to high-frequency data streams. The latest FMS data within 72 h is assigned the highest weight of 67%, and the weight gradually decreases over time. This design enables changes in functional indicators (such as a 20% decrease in shoulder joint flexibility score) to adjust the risk prediction coefficient in real time (such as a 0.15% increase in upper limb movement risk). Specifically, when the core stability is found to be insufficient during quarterly testing, the model will automatically increase the risk sensitivity of trunk swing angle in real-time movements.

Model training and hyper parameter optimization

Tables 6, 7 and 8 shows the hyper parameter optimization and modal feature association mechanism of the improved ARIMA LightGBM hybrid model. Table 6shows that the (2,1,1,0.05,5) parameter combination is optimal when the validation set RMSE = 0.142, with 320 MB of memory to meet the deployment requirements of the educational scenario, reducing memory consumption by 33.3% compared to the complex combination (3,1,2,0.10,6). Particle filter dynamic optimization (Table 7) converges the parameter variance to the range of 0.001–0.004, and although it takes 145 s to reach 500 particle size, it optimizes RMSE from 0.142 to 0.138, demonstrating the adaptability of dynamic tracking to sudden changes in teaching load. TVF EMD decomposition (Table 8) revealed that the IMF1 component (0.85 Hz) was strongly associated with acute injury (r = 0.72, p < 0.01), and its 38.2% energy share verified the dominance of the high-frequency shock signal; Although the IMF3 component (0.12 Hz) had only 15.4% energy, its significant association with chronic damage (p < 0.05) indicated the delayed characteristics of the low-frequency cumulative effect. The three-table linkage analysis showed that after the model locked the basic parameter framework through grid search, the particle filter dynamically adjusted the ARIMA coefficient (such as the D-order from 1 to 2 to deal with the mid-semester load surge), combined with the physical meaning mode separation of TVF EMD, formed a three-layer optimization system of “static structure + dynamic correction + feature decoupling”. The training efficiency of the model is 42% higher than that of a single parametric model.

Multi-scenario application

In basketball high dynamic scenarios (Fig. 4), the improved model RMSE = 0.17 is 45.2% lower than the traditional ARIMA, and the dynamic load error is 8.7%, reflecting its precise capture of sudden stop changes of direction (such as an instantaneous acceleration of 6.2 g). The 2.4-hour early warning time enabled teachers to implement the group rotation strategy, reducing the incidence of classroom injuries from 12.3% to 5.1%. The direction accuracy rate of 86.2% validated the model’s ability to predict damage evolution trends, such as triggering a secondary warning when three consecutive jumps with a height drop of more than 15% were detected. The dynamic load error metric innovative quantified the model’s response delay to the impact peak (> 5 g), with an error rate of 8.7% indicating that the system was able to identify dangerous movements (such as ankle inversion > 28°) within 0.2 s. Compared with the LSTM model, the improved model showed significant improvements in intervention success rate (92.3% vs. 84.7%) and timeliness (early warning 2.4 h vs. 1.8 h), attributed to TVF EMD’s advanced separation of shock signals and LightGBM’s real-time correction of electromyogram signal residual.

Research conclusions

This study has been approved by the ethics committee and strictly adheres to the guidelines of the Helsinki Declaration. The legal guardians of the participants sign the informed consent form, and the data is stored on the campus encrypted server (AES-256 standard) after being de identified. Access is limited to members of the research team. The data collection process follows the GB/T 35,273 − 2020 Personal Information Security Specification, where environmental sensor data (temperature/humidity) and biometric data (heart rate/acceleration) are stored separately, and TLS 1.3 encryption protocol is used for transmission.

The ARIMA LightGBM hybrid model constructed in this study demonstrates good potential for injury prediction in digital physical education teaching scenarios. In a queue of 217 adolescent athletes (aged 14.2 ± 1.3 years), the model significantly reduced prediction errors: the RMSE for high-intensity basketball training scenarios was 0.17 (45.2% lower than traditional ARIMA, 95% CI: 38.7–51.8), the specificity for predicting ACL injuries in football was 93.1% (90.3–95.9), and the cumulative load error for track and field stress fractures was controlled at 6.2% (5.1–7.3). Technological innovation is reflected in three aspects: ① Effective separation of acute injury high-frequency signals (0.85 Hz) and chronic risk low-frequency fluctuations (0.12 Hz) through TVF-EMD; ② Particle filter dynamic optimization adapts ARIMA parameters to sudden changes in teaching load (reducing error by 11.3% when adjusting differential order d from 1 to 2); ③ MIDAS technology successfully integrates second level exercise data with quarterly physical fitness assessment (FMS score weight attenuation coefficient θ ₀=0.67). These findings provide a new paradigm for the application of time series analysis in educational data science.

The model demonstrated clear clinical application value in empirical research: the real-time warning system shortened the response time of teacher intervention to 9.2 ± 1.3 min, and the incidence of injury in the experimental group decreased by 62.4% compared to the control group (4.3% vs. 11.7%, RR = 0.37). However, the following limitations must be emphasized: firstly, the model takes 145 s (i7-11800 H processor) in the particle filter optimization stage, which has not yet met the real-time decision-making requirement of < 10 s; Secondly, the samples were all from eastern coastal cities (with an average altitude of less than 5 m), and their generalizability needs to be verified in high-altitude areas (such as Kunming Sports School’s preliminary experiment showing a 23.7% increase in RMSE at an altitude of 1890 m) and special populations (such as the subgroup of obese adolescents with BMI > 30); Finally, virtual reality scenes did not fully consider differences in vestibular function (with a 12.7% increase in prediction error for patients with motion sickness). This delay stems from the search in the parameter space on the scale of 200 particles, which can be optimized through the following scheme:①Model level: Hierarchical particle filtering29 is adopted, with 50% high-weight particles retained in the pre-screening stage.②Hardware level: Deploy GPU acceleration (NVIDIA T4 can be shortened to 28 s in actual testing).③Algorithm level: Set dynamic particle size (100 particles for regular training, increased to 200 particles during sudden load).

The results of this study reveal that the mechanism of muscle stiffness variation (r = 0.71) uncovered by Włodarczyk et al.30 manifests in this model as follows: a correlation between high-frequency acceleration signals (0.85 Hz) and electromyographic envelope amplitude (β = 0.32, p < 0.05); dynamic load parameters automatically incorporate stiffness-derived indicators (such as the ground reaction force rise rate); similarly, the quadriceps force fluctuations (300–400 Hz) reported by Lemos et al.31 are integrated through the following approach: MIDAS technology maps electromyographic features to a 50 Hz IMU data stream, and the LightGBM model captures the nonlinear relationship between force fluctuations and knee varus torque (feature importance 28%).

Cross-disciplinary research provides important technical references for this model and clarifies the boundaries of application. Migliorini et al.32 pointed out in their systematic review that the evaluation of ACL reconstruction effects needs to be combined with the developmental stage of the growth plate, which requires the integration of bone age parameters in adolescent injury prediction. Blake et al.24 confirmed in their digital intervention experience in chronic pain management that user compliance (> 80%) is the key to technology implementation, which is in synergy with the optimization of vertigo scores in VR scenes in this study (1.8/5). However, it should be noted that Tedeschi’s33 trigger point therapy and Hassard’s34 educational scenario research suggest that customized solutions are needed for interventions for special populations to avoid insufficient model generalization.

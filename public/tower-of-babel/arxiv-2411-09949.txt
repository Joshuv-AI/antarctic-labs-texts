𝒲 𝐝 {\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d} -convergence rate of EM schemes for invariant measures of supercritical stable SDEs
Peng Chen, Lihu Xu, Xiaolong Zhang, Xicheng Zhang

Abstract




Abstract:

By establishing the regularity estimates for nonlocal Stein/Poisson equations under
\gamma
-order
Hölder and dissipative conditions on the coefficients,
we derive the
{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}
-convergence rate for the Euler-Maruyama schemes applied to the invariant measure of SDEs driven by multiplicative
\alpha
-stable noises with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},2)
, where
{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}
denotes the Wasserstein metric with
{\bf d}(x,y)=|x-y|^{\gamma}\wedge 1

and
\gamma\in((1-\alpha)_{+},1]
.

Key words
: Nonlocal Stein/Poisson equation; Hölder drift; Euler-Maruyama scheme; Invariant measure; Multiplicative
\alpha
-stable noise.

1.
Introduction

The study of sampling random variables from a given probability distribution has broad applications across various fields such as social sciences, engineering, agriculture, ecology and medicine (see
[
28
,
42
]
). A widely used method for this task is the Langevin Monte Carlo (LMC), which approximates the target distribution through Langevin diffusion processes. Numerous works have examined the performance of LMC algorithms (see
[
23
,
30
,
31
]
and references therein). Specifically, consider a probability distribution density
\pi(x)\propto\mathrm{e}^{-U(x)}
, where
U:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a potential function. The associated stochastic differential equation (SDE) is given by:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=-\nabla U(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{t},\ \ X_{0}=x,

(1.1)

where
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion, and
X
represents a particle’s trajectory in the potential field
U(x)
. Under suitable conditions on
U
, SDE (
1.1
) has a unique invariant probability measure
\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x):=\pi(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x
, and the law of the solution
X_{t}
converges exponentially to
\mu
as
t\to\infty
(cf.
[
7
,
30
]
). The Euler-Maruyama (EM) scheme is commonly used to numerically approximate solutions to SDE (
1.1
) and generate samples from
\mu
(cf.
[
7
]
).
Given step size
\eta>0
, let
\mu_{\eta}
denote the invariant measure corresponding to this scheme.
This raises two important questions:

•

Can
\mu_{\eta}
approximate
\mu
as
\eta\to 0
?

•

If yes, what is the optimal rate of convergence?

Recent studies
[
12
,
13
,
18
]
have established bounds on the Wasserstein distance between
\mu_{\eta}
and
\mu
, achieving convergence rates of order
\eta^{1/2}
or
\eta^{1/2}\log(1/\eta)
under various assumptions. For more detailed results on the Langevin sampling and the EM scheme, it is referred to
[
15
,
17
,
18
,
32
]
.

In statistical physics, the Langevin diffusion
X_{t}
described by SDE (
1.1
) represents the position of a particle at time
t
, which is subject to random forces. According to the central limit theorem (CLT), if these random impulses exhibit
infinite
variance, their sum may converge to an
\alpha
-stable distribution with
\alpha\in(0,2)
.
More precisely, consider a sequence of i.i.d. random variables
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
with the following common distribution:

\mathbb{P}(X_{1}>x)=\mathbb{P}(X_{1}<-x)=x^{-\alpha}/2,\hskip 9.24994ptx\geqslant 1,

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
. For the random walk
S_{n}=X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}
, the CLT (see
[
14
, p. 158]
)
asserts that

S_{n}/n^{1/\alpha}\Rightarrow Y,

where
Y
is an
\alpha
-stable random variable, and
\Rightarrow
denotes weak convergence. Heavy-tailed distributions, like
\alpha
-stable distributions, often arise in various stochastic systems. For example, they are used to model inputs in computer and communications networks, represent risk processes, and occur naturally in models of epidemiological spread. Such distributions are backed by statistical evidence in numerous fields, including physics, geoscience and economics (see
[
19
]
and references therein).
For further reading on heavy-tailed distributions and their applications in insurance and finance, it is referred to
[
6
,
16
]
. In a different context, for
d=1
and
U(x)=|x|^{\beta}
in (
1.1
), Roberts and Tweedie
[
39
]
showed that the diffusion process defined by SDE (
1.1
) is exponentially ergodic if and only if
\beta\geqslant 1
. Therefore, simulating heavy-tailed distributions using a continuous Langevin diffusion (
1.1
) is not appropriate for
\beta<1
, as the process fails to capture the heavy-tailed nature of such distributions.

In this paper, we study the following
d
-dimensional SDE driven by an
\alpha
-stable process:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(X_{t-}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}L^{(\alpha)}_{t},

(1.2)

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
, and
\big\{L^{(\alpha)}_{t},t\geqslant 0\big\}
is a rotationally invariant
\alpha
-stable process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lévy measure

\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}z)={\mathord{{\rm d}}}z/|z|^{d+\alpha}.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
satisfy the following conditions:

(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})

For some
\gamma\in((1-\alpha)_{+},1]
and
\kappa_{0}>1
, it holds that for all
x,y,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\kappa_{0}^{-1}|\xi|\leqslant|\sigma(x)\xi|\leqslant\kappa_{0}|\xi|,\ \ \|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|\leqslant\kappa_{0}|x-y|^{\gamma}

and

|b(0)|\leqslant\kappa_{0},\ \ |b(x)-b(y)|\leqslant\kappa_{0}\left(|x-y|^{\gamma}+|x-y|\right).

In recent years, SDE (
1.2
) has been extensively studied, see
[
10
,
11
,
29
,
46
,
48
]
for references. In particular, under
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
, for each starting point
X_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, SDE (
1.2
) admits a unique weak solution
X^{x}_{t}=X_{t}
(see
[
11
]
). Let
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be the semigroup associated with
X^{x}_{t}
, that is, for each
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle P_{t}f(x):=\mathbb{E}f(X^{x}_{t}).

(1.3)

For
f\in{\bf C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P_{t}f(x)=P_{t}{\mathscr{L}}f(x),\ \ t>0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.4)

where
{\bf C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of all
C^{2}
-functions with bounded derivatives up to
2
-order,
and
{\mathscr{L}}
is the generator of the semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, given by

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}f(x):={\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x)}f(x)+b(x)\cdot\nabla f(x),

(1.5)

where, for a
d\times d
matrix
A
,

{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{A}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f(x+Az)-f(x)-\mathbf{1}_{|z|\leqslant 1}Az\cdot\nabla f(x)]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z/|z|^{d+\alpha}.

Let
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-d}\phi(x/\varepsilon)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, where
\phi
is a smooth probability density function with compact support. Let
\theta_{t}(x)
solve the following regularized ODE:

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}_{t}(x)=(b*\phi_{t^{1/\alpha}})(\theta_{t}(x)),\ \ \theta_{0}(x)=x.

Under
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
, by
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
, for each
t>0
,
X^{x}_{t}
admits a density
p_{t}(x,y)
so that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{t}(x,y)={\mathscr{L}}p_{t}(\cdot,y)(x),

(1.6)

and for each
T>0
, there is a
C=C(T,\kappa_{0},d,\alpha,\gamma,\phi)>0
such that for any
t\in(0,T)
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)\asymp_{C}t(t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|\theta_{t}(x)-y|)^{-d-\alpha},

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle t|\Delta^{\alpha/2}p_{t}(\cdot,y)(x)|+t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim_{C}p_{t}(x,y),

(1.8)

where
\nabla_{x}
is the gradient operator in the
x
-direction.
Note that (
1.6
) implies that for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P_{t}f(x)={\mathscr{L}}P_{t}f(x),\ \ t>0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

On the other hand, under the following dissipative assumption: for some

\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\langle x,b(x)\rangle\leqslant-\kappa_{1}|x|^{2}+\kappa_{2},

(1.9)

it is well-known that SDE (
1.2
) has a unique invariant probability measure
\mu
(see
[
48
]
). This naturally raises the question of whether
\mu
can be approximated using a computable algorithm, which generates random vectors whose distributions are close to
\mu
. A common approximation scheme is the EM algorithm. Specifically, let
\eta\in(0,1)
be the step size, and consider the iteration

\displaystyle Y^{x}_{k+1}=Y^{x}_{k}+\eta b(Y^{x}_{k})+\sigma(Y^{x}_{k})\left(L^{(\alpha)}_{\eta(k+1)}-L^{(\alpha)}_{\eta k}\right),\hskip 9.24994ptk\geqslant 0,\ \ Y^{x}_{0}=x.

(1.10)

It is well established that the linear interpolation of
Y^{x}_{k}
converges to
X^{x}_{t}
(see
[
37
]
).
For further details on the EM scheme, we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
22
,
26
]
.

Under assumption (
1.9
), it can be shown that the Markov chain
(Y^{x}_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
admits a unique invariant distribution
\mu_{\eta}
(see Lemma
A.2
below). Two main approaches are used to analyze the Wasserstein distance between invariant measures of two Markov processes: the Stein method and the Lindeberg principle (see
[
2
,
8
,
9
]
). Both methods require sufficient regularity for at least one of the corresponding semigroups. In this paper, one of our primary objectives is to prove that
\mu_{\eta}
converges to
\mu
as
\eta\to 0
under certain Wasserstein metrics, and furthermore, to provide an explicit convergence rate using the Stein method.

1.1.
Main Results

Before presenting our main results, we introduce some notations and basic ideas used below.
Let
{\bf d}
be a distance on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and let
{\mathcal{P}}_{\bf d}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
denote the space of all probability measure
\mu
that satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\bf d}(x,0)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)<\infty.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}
represent the Wasserstein metric on
{\mathcal{P}}_{\bf d}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\ \inf_{(X,Y)\in\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}\mathbb{E}{\bf d}(X,Y),

where
\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the set of all coupling realizations of
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
.
For any
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\bf d}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}

with
|f(x)|\leqslant C(1+{\bf d}(x,0))
, we denote

\displaystyle\mu(f):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x).

Using the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality (see
[
45
, p.60]
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\sup_{g\in{\rm Lip}({\bf d})}|\mu_{1}(g)-\mu_{2}(g)|,

(1.11)

where

\displaystyle{\rm Lip}({\bf d}):=\big\{g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}:|g(x)-g(y)|\leqslant{\bf d}(x,y)\big\}.

For given
g\in{\rm Lip}({\bf d})
, letting
\bar{g}=:g-\mu(g)
, we consider the Stein/Poisson equation
1
1

1

The Poisson equation is also called the Stein equation due to its central role in Stein’s method.
defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}f(x)=\mu(g)-g(x)=-\bar{g}(x).

(1.12)

Suppose that (
1.12
) has a regular enough solution
f
. We can express
{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu,\mu_{\eta})
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu,\mu_{\eta})\overset{\eqref{jsy5}}{=}\sup_{g\in{\rm Lip}({\bf d})}|\mu(g)-\mu_{\eta}(g)|=\sup_{g\in{\rm Lip}({\bf d})}|\mu_{\eta}(\bar{g})|\overset{\eqref{Ste}}{=}\sup_{g\in{\rm Lip}({\bf d})}|\mu_{\eta}({\mathscr{L}}f)|.

(1.13)

For fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we introduce the following auxiliary random process:

\displaystyle Z^{x}_{t}=x+tb(x)+\sigma(x)L^{(\alpha)}_{t}.

By Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(Z^{x}_{t})=f(x)+\int^{t}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}{\mathscr{L}}_{x}f(Z^{x}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

(1.14)

where

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{x}f(y):=b(x)\cdot\nabla f(y)+{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x)}f(y).

Thus,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}f(x)=\frac{1}{\eta}\left[{\mathbb{E}}f(Z^{x}_{\eta})-f(x)\right]+\frac{1}{\eta}\int^{\eta}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathscr{L}}_{x}f(x)-{\mathscr{L}}_{x}f(Z^{x}_{s})]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Let
\mu_{\eta}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\bf d}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the stationary distribution of Markov chain
(Y^{x}_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}

defined in (
1.10
), that is, for
\xi\sim\mu_{\eta}
, independent of
L^{(\alpha)}_{\cdot}
,

\displaystyle Z^{\xi}_{\eta}\sim\xi\sim\mu_{\eta}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\bf d}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where
\sim
denotes the same distribution.
In particular,

\displaystyle\mu_{\eta}({\mathscr{L}}f)={\mathbb{E}}({\mathscr{L}}f(\xi))=\frac{1}{\eta}\int^{\eta}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathscr{L}}_{\xi}f(\xi)-{\mathscr{L}}_{\xi}f(Z^{\xi}_{s})\right]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

(1.15)

To show the convergence rate, it suffices to show certain regularity estimates for
f
.

Let
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
and
\kappa_{0},\gamma
be the same as in
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
.
Throughout this paper we use the parameter set

\displaystyle\Theta:=(d,\alpha,\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\gamma).

(1.16)

Now, we are ready to present our main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
{\bf d}(x,y):=|x-y|
. Suppose that
(\mathbf{H}^{1})
and

\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle\leqslant-\kappa_{1}|x-y|^{2}+\kappa_{2},\ \ \forall x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Then there are
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(\Theta)>0
and
C=C(\Theta)>0
such that for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
,

{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu,\mu_{\eta})\leqslant C\eta^{1/\alpha}.

The next result considers weaker Hölder regualrity and dissipativity assumption.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},2)
,
\gamma\in((1-\alpha)_{+},1]
and
{\bf d}(x,y):=|x-y|^{\gamma}\wedge 1
.
Suppose that
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
and

\langle x,b(x)\rangle\leqslant-\kappa_{1}|x|^{2}+\kappa_{2},\ \ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,2\alpha-1)
, there are
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(\Theta)>0
and
C=C(\Theta,\varepsilon)>0
such that for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
,

{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu,\mu_{\eta})\leqslant C\Big(\eta^{\gamma\wedge(2\alpha-1-\varepsilon)}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]}+\eta^{\gamma/\alpha}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\alpha>1}\Big).

Remark 1.3
.

At this stage, it seems that our method is not applicable to the case
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. For further discussion on this limitation, see Remark

Moreover, the convergence rates established in Theorem

and Theorem
1.2
may not be sharp, see Theorem

for OU processes. Obviously, the regularity of

Remark 1.4
.

Although we focus exclusively on EM scheme (
1.10
), we can also extend our results to the Pareto-type EM scheme studied in
[

, which is defined as

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}=Y_{k}+\eta b(Y_{k})+(\eta/\beta)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sigma(Y_{k})Z_{k+1},\hskip 9.24994ptk\geqslant 0,

where
\beta=\alpha\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})/(2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}})

Since the proof procedures are similar to those of EM scheme (

Our main contribution of this paper can be summarized as follows:

•

We establish the well-posedness and regularity properties for the Stein/Poisson equation
(
1.12
) in certain weighted Hölder spaces with logarithmic growth.
It is worth noting that our analysis includes cases with multiplicative noise and unbounded drift terms.

•

By applying the Stein method developed in
[
18
]
, we demonstrate that
\mu_{\eta}
converges to
\mu
as
\eta\to 0
in the
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbf{d}}
-Wasserstein metric. Moreover, we provide an explicit rate of convergence.

1.2.
Related work and the novelty

In
[
8
]
, the authors focus on the additive noise and subcritical case, i.e.,
\alpha\in(1,2)
. Besides, they also impose the following stronger assumptions:

\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{2}b\|_{\infty}\leqslant\kappa_{0},

and a monotonicity condition: for some
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle\leqslant-\kappa_{1}|x-y|^{2}+\kappa_{2}.

(1.17)

Using the Lindeberg principle, the authors in
[
8
]
derive the convergence rate of
\mu_{\eta}
under the Wasserstein-1 distance. A notable feature of
[
8
]

is the use of Malliavin calculus to establish
C^{2}
-regularity of the semigroup on
{\rm Lip}({\bf d})
for SDE (
1.2
).

In this paper, we extend the analysis to the supercritical case, i.e.,
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},2\right)
, assuming
b
and
\sigma
satisfy
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
and (
1.9
). Leveraging Stein’s method and the heat kernel estimates from
[
29
]
, we study the convergence rate of
\mu_{\eta}
under the
\mathcal{W}_{\bf d}
-distance, where
{\bf d}(x,y):=|x-y|^{\gamma}\wedge 1
(see Theorem
1.2
above). Furthermore, under
(\mathbf{H}^{1})
and (
1.17
), we establish the convergence rate of
\mu_{\eta}
in the Wasserstein-1 distance (see Theorem
1.1
above). Compared with
[
8
]
, we make weaker assumptions on
b
and
\sigma
and achieve the convergence rate of
\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
using alternative techniques.

Although the Malliavin calculus provides powerful tools for analyzing the regularity of the semigroup for SDE (
1.2
), it has two main limitations in our context: (i) Malliavin calculus typically requires both
b
and
\sigma
to possess sufficient regularity to achieve the desired semigroup regularity. Specifically, it is unsuitable for analyzing fractional regularity when
b
and
\sigma
satisfy only certain Hölder regularity conditions. (ii) For multiplicative noise, the adaptive Malliavin calculus based on time-change techniques, as used in
[
8
]
, is not applicable because the jump of
X_{t}
at time
t
depends on both
X_{t-}
and the jump of
L^{(\alpha)}_{t}
.

In our preliminary study, using the finite-jump approximation method from
[
46
]
, we obtained
C^{2}
-regularity for the semigroup on
{\rm Lip}({\bf d})
for SDE (
1.2
) with
\alpha\in(1,2)
. However, this method involves highly complex calculations and estimates and cannot be used in the supercritical case, i.e.,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, as higher-order directional derivatives of the SDE solution (
1.2
) only admit
m
-order moment estimates with
m\in(0,\alpha)
. In this paper, in Lemmas
2.9
and
2.13
, we employ heat kernel estimates from
[
29
]
and commutator estimates to establish weighted
s
-Hölder (
s>\alpha
) regularity for the semigroup on
{\rm Lip}(\bf d)
for SDE (
1.2
) with
\alpha\in(0,2)
, which is crucial for deriving the convergence rates for
\mu_{\eta}
under the
\mathcal{W}_{\bf d}
-distance, where
{\bf d}
is the bounded Hölder distance.

On the other hand, the Stein/Poisson equation is foundational in mathematical physics with a long research history. Gilbarg and Trudinger
[
20
]
established interior Schauder estimates for solutions of
\Delta f=g
. Stein
[
43
]
used the Riesz potential to establish global regularity estimates for solutions to
\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f=g
(see also Bass
[
5
]
). Ros-Oton and Serra
[
41
]
investigated the interior Hölder regularity of solutions to
Af=g
for certain symmetric
\alpha
-stable operators
A
. Recently, Kühn
[
24
,
25
]
established global and interior Schauder estimates for solutions to
Af=g
in certain Hölder-Zygmund spaces, where
A
is defined by a general Feller process. Notably, many studies have addressed the Stein/Poisson equation defined by infinitesimal generators of specific ergodic diffusion processes. For example, Pardoux and Veretennikov
[
33
,
34
,
35
]
studied the well-posedness and regularity estimates of the Stein/Poisson equation defined by an elliptic operator associated with an ergodic diffusion process, applying these results to analyze diffusion approximations in multiscale systems.

However, there appear to be few studies addressing well-posedness and regularity estimates of the Stein/Poisson equation for nonlocal operators with unbounded coefficients. Furthermore, as noted in
[
40
]
, the results of Pardoux and Veretennikov have been widely applied in fields such as the central limit theorem, moderate and large deviations, spectral methods for multiscale systems, the averaging principle, homogenization, and numerical approximations of invariant measures for SDEs and SPDEs. We believe that the results on the Stein/Poisson equation (
1.12
) in this paper may be applied to similar topics in a nonlocal context. In future work, we plan to explore these topics further.

In this paper, we investigate the well-posedness and regularity estimates for the Stein/Poisson equation (
1.12
) in weighted Hölder spaces. Specifically, for any
g\in{\rm Lip}({\bf d})
, we define

\displaystyle f(x):=\int^{\infty}_{0}P_{t}\bar{g}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t,\ \ \bar{g}:=g-\mu(g).

(1.18)

We show that the above
f
belongs to certain weighted Hölder spaces (see Theorem
2.14
), and indeed is the solution of Stein/Poisson equation (
1.12
) (see Theorem
2.16
).
In particular, we consider two cases:

(
Case 1.
) Under
(\mathbf{H}^{1})
and (
1.17
), we take
{\bf d}(x,y)=|x-y|
.

(
Case 2.
) Under
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
and (
1.9
), we take
{\bf d}(x,y)=|x-y|^{\gamma}\wedge 1
.

In
Case 1
, it follows from
[
27
]
that there exist constants
C,\lambda>0
such that for any
\varphi\in{\rm Lip}({\bf d})
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla P_{t}\varphi\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{C}e^{-\lambda t}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty},\hskip 9.24994ptt>0.

Using this result and certain commutator estimates, we show that for any
g\in{\rm Lip}({\bf d})
,
the function
f
defined in (
1.18
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{C}\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}.

See Lemma
2.7
and Theorem
2.8
for further details.

In
Case 2
, it follows from
[
48
, Theorem 1.2]
that for any
m\in(0,\alpha)
, there exist constants
C,\lambda>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\varphi/\rho_{m}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1}|P_{t}\varphi(x)-\mu(\varphi)|\lesssim_{C}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\rho_{m}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptt>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.19)

where
\rho_{m}(x):=(1+|x|^{2})^{m/2}
. From (
1.19
), we obtain only that
f
, defined as in (
1.18
), and its derivatives exhibit polynomial growth, which complicates the analysis of the convergence rate of
\mathcal{W}_{\bf d}(\mu,\mu_{\eta})
. This difficulty arises because, in general, SDE (
1.2
) has only
m
-order moment estimates for
m\in(0,\alpha)
.
To address this, we introduce the cut-off distance
{\bf d}(x,y)=|x-y|^{\gamma}\wedge 1
. This allows us to show that
f
, defined as in (
1.18
), and its derivatives exhibit only logarithmic growth (see Theorem
2.14
below). The key observation is the integral

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}1\wedge(a\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t})\,\mathrm{d}t=\lambda^{-1}(1+\ln a),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall\lambda>0,\,a\geqslant 1.

2.
Solvability of nonlocal Stein/Poisson equations

Throughout this section, we fix
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},2\right)
. Our primary objective is to investigate the well-posedness and regularity properties of the Stein/Poisson equation (
1.12
) in weighted Hölder spaces. We consider two distinct cases:
monotonicity drift
and
dissipativity drift
. For the monotonicity drift case, we restrict to
\alpha\in(1,2)
and derive sharper estimates. For the dissipativity drift case, we explore the full range
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},2\right)
, yielding slightly rougher estimates.

2.1.
Weighted Hölder spaces

In this section, we recall the definition of weighted Hölder spaces and their equivalent characterizations, which will be used throughout this paper (see
[
21
,
44
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A
C^{\infty}
-function

\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,\infty)
is called an admissible weight if, for some
C,\ell>0
,

\rho(x)\lesssim_{C}\rho(y)(1+|x-y|^{\ell}),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C_{j}>0
such that

|\nabla^{j}\rho(x)|\leqslant C_{j}\rho(x),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Remark 2.2
.

Typical examples of admissible weights include:

\rho(x)=(\log(\mathrm{e}+|x|^{2}))^{m},\hskip 9.24994pt\rho(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{m/2},\hskip 9.24994ptm\in\mathbb{R}.

From now on, we fix an admissible weight
\rho
.
Let
L^{\infty}_{\rho}:=L^{\infty}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

denote the space of
all measurable functions
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite norm:

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}:=\|f\rho\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x}|f(x)\rho(x)|<\infty.

For
\beta>0
, let
{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the weighted Hölder space defined by

\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}}:=\sum_{k=0}^{[\beta]}\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}+\sup_{|v|\leqslant 1}\frac{\|\delta_{v}\nabla^{[\beta]}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}}{|v|^{\beta-[\beta]}}<\infty,

where
[\beta]
denotes the integer part of
\beta
,
\nabla^{k}
is the
k
-th order gradient operator, and for a vector-valued function
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\delta_{v}F(x):=F(x+v)-F(x).

By definition, it is clear that for
0\leqslant\beta_{1}\leqslant\beta_{2}
and two admissible weights
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
with
\rho_{1}\leqslant\rho_{2}
,

\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{1}}_{\rho_{1}}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{2}}_{\rho_{2}}}.

Note that for two functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{v}(fg)(x)=f(x)\delta_{v}g(x)+\delta_{v}f(x)g(x+v).

(2.1)

If
\rho\equiv 1
, then
{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
becomes the classical Hölder space, and we simply write:

{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})={\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\hskip 9.24994pt\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}}:=\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{1}}.

If
\rho(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{m}{2}}
for some
m\geqslant 0
, then we write

L^{\infty}_{m}:=L^{\infty}_{\rho},\ \ \mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})={\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\hskip 9.24994pt\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{m}}:=\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}},\ \ \beta>0.

Next, we recall an equivalent characterization for
{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
\beta\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
and some basic properties of
{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(cf.
[
21
, Theorem 2.6, Remark 2.8, and Corollary 2.9]
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\rho
be an admissible weight, and let
\beta^{\prime}\geqslant\beta>0
be non-integers and
{\mathbb{N}}\ni m>\beta
.

(i)

Let
\delta^{m}_{v}f:=\delta_{v}(\delta^{m-1}_{v}f)
be the
m
-order difference operator. Then we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}}\asymp_{C}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}+\sup_{|v|\leqslant 1}\|\delta^{m}_{v}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}/|v|^{\beta}.

(ii)

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
be such that
\theta\beta
is not an integer. Then we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta\beta}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}}^{\theta}.

(iii)

Let
\ell
be the parameter associated with
\rho
as in Definition
2.1

and let
k>\beta^{\prime}-\beta\geqslant 0
. Then we have

\|\delta^{k}_{v}f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{C}|v|^{\beta^{\prime}-\beta}(1+|v|^{\ell})\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\rho}},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Remark 2.4
.

For
\beta\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
, an equivalent norm of
{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}
is given by

\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}}\asymp_{C}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}+\sup_{|v|\leqslant 1}\|\delta^{(2)}_{v}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}/|v|^{\beta},

where
\delta^{(2)}_{v}
is the symmetric difference operator defined by

\delta^{(2)}_{v}f(x):=f(x+v)+f(x-v)-2f(x).

The following lemma provides an alternative expression for
\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x)}f(v)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that for some
\gamma\in[0,1]
and
\kappa_{0}>1
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{0}^{-1}|\xi|\leqslant|\sigma(x)\xi|\leqslant\kappa_{0}|\xi|,\hskip 9.24994pt\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|\leqslant\kappa_{0}|x-y|^{\gamma}.

(2.2)

Let
\jmath(x,z):=(|z|/|\sigma^{-1}(x)z|)^{d+\alpha}/\det(\sigma(x))
. Then, for any
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x)}f(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta^{(2)}_{z}f(v)\frac{\jmath(x,z)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}z,

(2.3)

and there exists a constant
\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}>1
such that for all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{-1}\leqslant\jmath(x,z)\leqslant\widetilde{\kappa}_{0},\hskip 9.24994pt|\jmath(x,z)-\jmath(y,z)|\leqslant\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}|x-y|^{\gamma}.

(2.4)

Proof.

Since for each
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\int_{\{\epsilon<|y|\leqslant 1\}}\sigma(x)y\cdot\nabla f(v)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}=0,

and by symmetry,

\int_{\{\epsilon<|y|<\infty\}}[f(v+\sigma(x)y)-f(v)]\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}=\int_{\{\epsilon<|y|<\infty\}}[f(v-\sigma(x)y)-f(v)]\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}},

we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x)}f(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta^{(2)}_{\sigma(x)y}f(v)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}},

(2.5)

which gives the desired (
2.3
) by the change of variable
z=\sigma(x)y
.
Since
\det(\sigma(x))
is a polynomial function of
\sigma_{ij}(x)
and
\sigma^{-1}(x)=\sigma^{*}(x)/\det(\sigma(x))
, where
\sigma^{*}
is the adjoint matrix of
\sigma
,
estimate (
2.4
) follows from the chain rule and the assumptions.
∎

We have the following important estimates for
(\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f)(x)=\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x)}f(x)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\rho
be an admissible weight satisfying
\rho(x)\lesssim\rho(y)(1+|x-y|^{\ell})
with
\ell\in[0,\alpha\wedge 1)
. Under (
2.2
),
for any
\beta>\alpha
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, there exists a constant

C=C(d,\beta,\alpha,\theta,\rho,\ell,\kappa_{0})>0

such that for all
f\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta+\beta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta+\beta}_{\rho}},

(2.6)

and for
\gamma
being the same as in (
2.2
) and for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{1})}f-\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{{\sigma(x_{2})}}f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta+\beta}_{\rho}}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\gamma}.

(2.7)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\beta\in(\alpha,2)
.
By Lemma
2.5
and (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle|\delta_{v}\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(|\delta_{v}\delta^{(2)}_{z}f(x)|\cdot|\jmath(x+v,z)|+|\delta^{(2)}_{z}f(x)|\cdot|\delta_{v}\jmath(\cdot,z)(x)|\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{Ho1}}{\leqslant}\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(|\delta_{v}\delta^{(2)}_{z}f(x)|+|v|^{\gamma}|\delta^{(2)}_{z}f(x)|\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}.

For any
\theta\in[0,1]
,
by (ii) of Theorem
2.3
,
one can choose
\beta_{1}\in(\alpha,\beta]
such that for any
|v|\leqslant 1
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|\delta_{v}\delta^{(2)}_{z}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}\lesssim|v|^{\theta}\|\delta^{(2)}_{z}f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta}_{\rho}}\lesssim|v|^{\theta}(1+|z|^{\ell})\left((|z|^{\beta_{1}}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta+\beta}_{\rho}})\wedge\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta}_{\rho}}\right)

(2.8)

and

\|\delta^{(2)}_{z}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}\lesssim(1+|z|^{\ell})\left((|z|^{\beta_{1}}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}})\wedge\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}\right).

Hence, for any
\theta\in[0,1]
, there exists a
\beta_{1}\in(\alpha,\beta]
such that

\displaystyle\|\delta_{v}\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}\lesssim|v|^{\theta}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta+\beta}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|z|^{\ell})(|z|^{\beta_{1}}\wedge 1)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\lesssim|v|^{\theta}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta+\beta}_{\rho}},

(2.9)

and

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|z|^{\ell})(|z|^{\beta_{1}}\wedge 1)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}}.

(2.10)

Combining (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we obtain (
2.6
).

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle|\delta_{v}(\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{1})}f-\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{2})}f)(x)|\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\delta_{v}\delta^{(2)}_{z}f(x)|\cdot|\jmath(x_{1},z)-\jmath(x_{2},z)|\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}.

Let
\theta\in[0,1]
and
\beta_{1}
be as in (
2.8
). By (
2.8
) and (
2.4
), we have

\|\delta_{v}(\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{1})}f-\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{2})}f)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}\leqslant|v|^{\theta}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\gamma}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\theta+\beta}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|z|^{\ell})(|z|^{\beta_{1}}\wedge 1)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}

and

\|\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{1})}f-\mathscr{L}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{2})}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}\leqslant|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\gamma}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|z|^{\ell})(|z|^{\beta_{1}}\wedge 1)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}.

Thus we obtain (
2.7
).
∎

2.2.
Subcritical case: Monotonicity drifts

In the following, we restrict ourself to
\alpha\in(1,2)
and assume that
b
and
\sigma
satisfy

(H
1
)
and for some
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
and all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle\leqslant-\kappa_{1}|x-y|^{2}+\kappa_{2}.

(2.11)

Clearly, (
2.11
) implies (
1.9
) and there is a unique invariant probability measure
\mu
associated with
P_{t}
so that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x|\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)<\infty.

Moreover, under the above assumptions,
by
[
27
]
, there
exist constants
C,\lambda>0
depending on
\Theta
(see (
1.16
)) such that
for any Lipschitz function
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla P_{t}\varphi\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{C}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}.

(2.12)

Lemma 2.7
.

Under
(\mathbf{H}^{1})
and (
2.11
), there exists a constant
C=C(\Theta)>0
such that for any Lipschitz function
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}P_{t}\varphi\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{C}t^{-1/\alpha}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty},\ \ \forall t\in(0,1].

(2.13)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\varphi\in{\mathbf{C}}^{2}
and denote by
[A,B]:=AB-BA
the commutator of operators
A,B
.
Noting that by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
),

\nabla P_{t}\varphi-P_{t}\nabla\varphi=\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{s}(P_{t-s}\nabla P_{s}\varphi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=\int^{t}_{0}P_{t-s}[\nabla,{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s}\varphi{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}P_{t}\varphi=\nabla P_{t}\nabla\varphi+\int^{t}_{0}\nabla P_{t-s}[\nabla,{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s}\varphi{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

(2.14)

Moreover, we clearly have

[\nabla,b\cdot\nabla]P_{s}\varphi(x)=\nabla b\cdot\nabla P_{s}\varphi(x)

and by Lemma
2.5
,

[\nabla,{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}]P_{s}\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\delta^{(2)}_{z}P_{s}\varphi(x)\frac{\nabla\jmath(\cdot,z)(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}.

By (
1.5
),
(H
1
)
and (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle\|[\nabla,{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|[\nabla,b\cdot\nabla]P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}+\|[\nabla,{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}]P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\|\nabla P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}|z|)\wedge(\|\nabla^{2}P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}|z|^{2})\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{2}P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}.

Now by (
2.14
), we have for
t\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}P_{t}\varphi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\nabla P_{t}\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}+\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla P_{t-s}[\nabla,{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{Grad01}}{\lesssim}t^{-1/\alpha}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}+\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1/\alpha}\|[\nabla,{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1/\alpha}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}+\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1/\alpha}(\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{2}P_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Since
\alpha\in(1,2)
, by Gronwall-Volterra’s inequality (see
[
47
]
), we get (
2.13
).
∎

Now we can show the following main result.

Theorem 2.8
.

Under
(\mathbf{H}^{1})
and (
2.11
), there is a constant
C=C(\Theta)>0
such that
for any Lipschitz function
g
and
f=\int^{\infty}_{0}P_{t}\bar{g}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t
with
\bar{g}:=g-\mu(g)
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{\infty}+\|{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{2-\alpha}}\lesssim_{C}\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}.

(2.15)

Moreover, for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{1})}f-{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x_{2})}f\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{C}\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

(2.16)

Proof.

By (
2.12
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}\leqslant\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\nabla P_{t}\bar{g}\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\lesssim\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\|\nabla\bar{g}\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\lesssim\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}.

(2.17)

Noting that for
t>1
,

\|\nabla^{2}P_{t}\bar{g}\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla^{2}P_{1}P_{t-1}\bar{g}\|_{\infty}\overset{\eqref{IN:N2Pt}}{\lesssim}\|\nabla P_{t-1}\bar{g}\|_{\infty}\overset{\eqref{11}}{\lesssim}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\|\nabla g\|_{\infty},

we have by Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int^{1}_{0}\|\nabla^{2}P_{t}\bar{g}\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\int^{\infty}_{1}\|\nabla^{2}P_{t}\bar{g}\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{1}_{0}t^{-1/\alpha}\|\nabla\bar{g}\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\int^{\infty}_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\|\nabla\bar{g}\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\lesssim\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}.

(2.18)

On the other hand, for
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, by (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle\delta_{v}{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\delta_{v}\Big(\delta^{(2)}_{y}f\jmath(\cdot,y)\Big)(x)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}.

Since
\|\jmath(\cdot,y)\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla\jmath(\cdot,y)\|_{\infty}\leqslant\tilde{\kappa}_{0}
by Lemma
2.5
with
\gamma=1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\delta_{v}(\delta^{(2)}_{y}f\jmath(\cdot,y))\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\delta_{v}\delta^{(2)}_{y}f\|_{\infty}\|\jmath(\cdot,y)\|_{\infty}+\|\delta^{(2)}_{y}f\|_{\infty}\|\delta_{v}\jmath(\cdot,y)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leqslant\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}\Big(\|\delta_{v}\delta^{(2)}_{y}f\|_{\infty}+\|\delta^{(2)}_{y}f\|_{\infty}|v|\Big).

By (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), we have

\|\delta_{v}\delta^{(2)}_{y}f\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{\infty}(|y|^{2}\wedge(|v||y|))\lesssim\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}(|y|^{2}\wedge(|v||y|))

and

\|\delta^{(2)}_{y}f\|_{\infty}\leqslant(\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{\infty}|y|^{2})\wedge(\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}|y|)\lesssim\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}(|y|^{2}\wedge|y|).

Hence, for
|v|\leqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f\|_{\infty}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{(|y|^{2}\wedge|y|){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}\lesssim\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}

and

\displaystyle\|\delta_{v}{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{(|y|^{2}\wedge(|v||y|)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}\lesssim\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}|v|^{2-\alpha}.

Combining the above two estimates, we obtain

\|{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}f\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{2-\alpha}}\lesssim_{C}\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}
.
It is similar to (
2.7
) that (
2.16
) follows by (
2.15
).
∎

2.3.
Dissipativity drift

In this section, we assume that
b
and
\sigma
satisfy
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
with
\gamma\in((1-\alpha)_{+},1]
. In addition, we suppose that there exist constants
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0

\displaystyle\langle x,b(x)\rangle\leqslant-\kappa_{1}|x|^{2}+\kappa_{2}.

(2.19)

For given

,
by
[
48
, Theorem 1.2]
, there is a unique invariant probability measure
\mu
of
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}

with finite
m

and there are constants

and

\displaystyle\|P_{t}\varphi-\mu(\varphi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}\lesssim_{C}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}\lesssim_{C}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\ \ t>0.

(2.20)

From this, we clearly have

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\|P_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}\lesssim_{C}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}.

(2.21)

By (
2.20
) and (
1.8
), we have the following regularity estimate of

.

Lemma 2.9
.

For any

and

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{t}g(x)|\lesssim_{C}{\mathbbm{1}}_{t\leqslant 1}t^{\frac{\vartheta\wedge\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\vartheta}}+{\mathbbm{1}}_{t>1}(1\wedge(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}(1+|x|^{m})))\|g\|_{\infty}.

(2.22)

Proof.

For
t\in(0,1]
, noting that

\displaystyle\nabla P_{t}g(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)g(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}p_{t}(x,y)(g(y)-g(\theta_{t}(x))){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y,

by (
1.8
), (
1.7
) and the change of variable, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{t}{g}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}t^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\vartheta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|\theta_{t}(x)-y|)^{-d-\alpha}(|y-\theta_{t}(x)|^{\vartheta}\wedge 1){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\vartheta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|y|)^{-d-\alpha}(|y|^{\vartheta}\wedge 1){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\lesssim t^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\vartheta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{\vartheta}\wedge 1}{(t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+r)^{\alpha+1}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\vartheta}}\left[t^{-1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}r^{\vartheta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}^{\infty}\frac{r^{\vartheta}\wedge 1}{r^{\alpha+1}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right]\lesssim t^{\frac{\vartheta\wedge\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\vartheta}}.

(2.23)

For
t>1
, noting that

\nabla P_{t}g=\nabla P_{1}P_{t-1}g=\nabla P_{1}P_{t-1}(g-\mu(g)),

by (
2.20
) and (
1.7
), we have

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{t}g(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}p_{1}(x,y)||P_{t-1}g(y)-\mu(g)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p_{1}(x,y)\left(1\wedge(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-1)}(1+|y|^{m}))\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\infty}(1\wedge(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}(1+|x|^{m}))),

(2.24)

where we use the fact that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p_{1}(x,y)(1+|y|^{m}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y=1+{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x}_{1}|^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{C0:b}}}{{\lesssim}}1+|x|^{m}.

Combining (
2.23
) and (
2.24
), we obtain (
2.22
) and complete the proof.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

For the short-time estimate, in order to make
\nabla P_{t}g
integrable around
0
, we need to require

\frac{\vartheta\wedge\alpha-1}{\alpha}>-1,

which leads to
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
. This is also the reason that we cannot tackle the case
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
.
For the large-time estimate, one cannot take
m=0
in (
2.22
).
This is because under dissipative condition (
2.19
) we cannot take
m=0
in (
2.20
).

To show the regularity of the solution to the Stein/Poisson equation,
we need the following estimate.

Lemma 2.11
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,2)
, there is a constant
C=C(d,\alpha)>0
such that
for all
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
\|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f\|_{\infty}<\infty,

\displaystyle|\delta^{(2)}_{v}f(x)|+|\delta^{2}_{v}f(x)|\lesssim_{C}|v|^{\alpha}({\mathscr{U}}_{f,|v|}(x)+{\mathscr{U}}_{f,|v|}(x\pm v)),\ \ \forall x,v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

(2.25)

where
{\mathscr{U}}_{f,r}(x\pm v)={\mathscr{U}}_{f,r}(x+v)+{\mathscr{U}}_{f,r}(x-v)
, and for
r>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{U}}_{f,r}(x):=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-|n|(\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha))}}{(2^{n}r)^{d}}\int_{|z|\leqslant 2^{n+1}r}|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z.

Proof.

We only consider the second order symmetric difference oeprator
\delta^{(2)}
.
By suitable smooth approximation, we may assume

f
is smooth and has compact support.
Note that by Fourier’s transform,

f(x)=\Delta^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x)=K*\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x),

where
K(x)=c_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-d+\alpha}
and the constant
c_{d,\alpha}>0
is
chosen
so that
\hat{K}(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\alpha}
(see
[
43
, p. 117]
).
Fix
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
From this, we have

\displaystyle|\delta^{(2)}_{v}f(x)|=|\delta^{(2)}_{v}K*\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{|z|\leqslant 2|v|}+\int_{|z|>2|v|}\right)|\delta^{(2)}_{v}K(z)|\cdot|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z.

For
|z|>2|v|
, by Taylor’s expansion we have

\displaystyle|\delta^{(2)}_{v}K(z)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|v|^{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int^{1}_{-1}|\nabla^{2}K(z+\theta\theta^{\prime}v)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\theta{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|^{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int^{1}_{-1}|z+\theta\theta^{\prime}v|^{\alpha-d-2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\theta{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\theta^{\prime}\lesssim|v|^{2}|z|^{\alpha-d-2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{|z|>2|v|}|\delta^{(2)}_{v}K(z)|\cdot

\displaystyle|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\lesssim|v|^{2}\int_{|z|>2|v|}\frac{|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle=|v|^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{n}|v|\leqslant|z|<2^{n+1}|v|}\frac{|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|}{|z|^{d+2-\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\leqslant|v|^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2^{n}|v|)^{\alpha-d-2}\int_{|z|<2^{n+1}|v|}|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle=|v|^{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-n(2-\alpha)}}{(2^{n}|v|)^{d}}\int_{|z|<2^{n+1}|v|}|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\leqslant|v|^{\alpha}{\mathscr{U}}_{f,|v|}(x).

On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leqslant 2|v|}|K(z)|\cdot

\displaystyle|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{-n}|v|<|z|\leqslant 2^{-n+1}|v|}|K(z)|\cdot|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2^{-n}|v|)^{\alpha-d}\int_{2^{-n}|v|<|z|\leqslant 2^{-n+1}|v|}|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\leqslant 2|v|^{\alpha}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-n\alpha}}{(2^{-n}|v|)^{d}}\int_{|z|\leqslant 2^{-n+1}|v|}|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\leqslant 2|v|^{\alpha}{\mathscr{U}}_{f,|v|}(x),

and similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leqslant 2|v|}|K(z\pm v)|\cdot|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\leqslant\int_{|z\pm v|\leqslant 4|v|}|K(z\pm v)|\cdot|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt=\int_{|z|\leqslant 4|v|}|K(z)|\cdot|\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x\pm v-z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\lesssim|v|^{\alpha}{\mathscr{U}}_{f,|v|}(x\pm v).

Combining the above calculations, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Using the above lemma, we can show the following result, which will be used to establish the large time estimate.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
m\in[0,\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha))
and
\beta\in[0,\alpha]
. For any
T>0
, there is a constant
C=C(\Theta,m,\beta,T)>0
such that for all
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in(0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\delta^{2}_{v}P_{t}f(x)|+|\delta^{(2)}_{v}P_{t}f(x)|\lesssim_{C}|v|^{\beta}t^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}(1+|x|^{m}+|v|^{m})\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}.

(2.26)

Proof.

By interpolation,
it suffices to consider
\beta=0
and
\beta=\alpha
.
For
\beta=0
, by (
2.21
), (
2.26
) clearly holds. Now, we consider the case
\beta=\alpha
.
By definition, we have

|\delta^{2}_{v}P_{t}f(x)|\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\delta^{2}_{v}p_{t}(\cdot,y)(x)|\,|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y.

Note that by (
2.25
),

|\delta^{2}_{v}p(t,\cdot,y)(x)|\lesssim|v|^{\alpha}({\mathscr{U}}_{p_{t}(\cdot,y),|v|}(x)+{\mathscr{U}}_{p_{t}(\cdot,y),|v|}(x\pm v)),

where

{\mathscr{U}}_{p_{t}(\cdot,y),r}(x)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-|n|(\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha))}}{(2^{n}r)^{d}}\int_{|z|\leqslant 2^{n+1}r}|\Delta^{\alpha/2}p_{t}(\cdot,y)(x-z)|\,\mathrm{d}z.

Hence, by Fubini’s lemma and (
1.8
), for any
t\in(0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle|\delta^{2}_{v}P_{t}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|^{\alpha}t^{-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-|n|(\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha))}}{(2^{n}|v|)^{d}}\Bigg[\int_{|z|\leqslant 2^{n+1}|v|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t}(x-z,y)|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt+\int_{|z|\leqslant 2^{n+1}|v|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t}(x\pm v-z,y)|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z\Bigg].

By the assumption and SDE (
1.2
), it is standard to derive that for any
m\in[0,\alpha)
and
t\in(0,T]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t}(x\pm v-z,y)|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y=\mathbb{E}|f(X_{t}^{x\pm v-z})|\lesssim(1+|x\pm v-z|^{m})\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}.

Therefore, for any
t\in(0,T]
, it holds that

\displaystyle|\delta^{2}_{v}P_{t}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}|v|^{\alpha}t^{-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-|n|(\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha))}}{(2^{n}|v|)^{d}}\int_{|z|\leqslant 2^{n+1}|v|}(1+|x\pm v-z|^{m})\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}|v|^{\alpha}t^{-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{-|n|(\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha))}(1+|x|^{m}+(2^{n+1}|v|)^{m})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}|v|^{\alpha}t^{-1}(1+|x|^{m}+|v|^{m}),

where the last step is due to
m\in[0,\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha))
. The proof is complete.
∎

Now, we prove the following crucial short-time and long-time regularity estimates of the semigroup, which will be used to establish the regularity estimate of the Stein/Poisson equation. The key point here is that the regularity index can be greater than
\alpha
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},2)
. Under
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
and (
2.19
),
for any
\beta\in(0,\alpha)
and
m\in(0,\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha))
, there exist

\tau\in(0,1)
and constant

C=C(\Theta,m,\beta)>0
such that for any
g\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{|v|\leqslant 1}\frac{|\delta^{3}_{v}P_{t}{g}(x)|}{|v|^{\beta+\gamma}}\lesssim_{C}\left\{\begin{aligned} &t^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}},&\ \ t\in(0,2],\\
&{\mathscr{I}}_{m}^{t}(x)^{\tau}\|g\|_{\infty},&\ \ t\in(2,\infty),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.27)

where
{\mathscr{I}}^{t}_{m}(x):=1\wedge(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}(1+|x|^{m}))
and
\lambda
is from (
2.20
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
g\in{\mathbf{C}}^{2}
and denote by
[A,B]:=AB-BA
the commutator of operators
A,B
.
Fix
t_{0}\geqslant 0
.
Note that by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
),

\displaystyle\delta_{v}P_{t+t_{0}}{g}-P_{t}\delta_{v}{P_{t_{0}}g}

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{s}(P_{t-s}\delta_{v}P_{s+t_{0}}{g}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=\int^{t}_{0}P_{t-s}[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

In particular, for
|v|\leqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\delta^{3}_{v}P_{t+t_{0}}{g}=\delta^{2}_{v}P_{t}\delta_{v}P_{t_{0}}{g}+\int^{t}_{0}\delta^{2}_{v}P_{t-s}[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

(2.28)

Note that by definition (
1.5
),

\displaystyle[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}=[\delta_{v},b\cdot\nabla]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}+[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}.

By (
2.1
) and
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
, we have for all
|v|\leqslant 1
,

\displaystyle|[\delta_{v},b\cdot\nabla]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}(x)|=|\delta_{v}b(x)\cdot\nabla P_{s+t_{0}}{g}(x+v)|\leqslant\kappa_{0}|v|^{\gamma}|\nabla P_{s+t_{0}}{g}(x+v)|,

(2.29)

and by Lemma
2.5
, for each
s>0
and
x,v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{(\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s+t_{0}}g)(x+v)\delta_{v}\jmath(\cdot,y)(x)}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}|v|^{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|(\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s+t_{0}}g)(x+v)|}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

(2.30)

In the following, all the constants only depend on
\Theta
and
\beta
. We fix
|v|\leqslant 1
.

(Small time estimate)
We assume
t_{0}=0
.
In this case, by (
2.29
) and (
2.22
), we have

\displaystyle\|[\delta_{v},b\cdot\nabla]P_{s}{g}\|_{\infty}\lesssim|v|^{\gamma}s^{\frac{\gamma\wedge\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall s\in(0,2],\ |v|\leqslant 1,

and for
\beta_{0}\in(\alpha,2]
, by (
2.30
) with
t_{0}=0
,

\displaystyle\|[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}]P_{s}{g}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|^{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Big(\|g\|_{\infty}{\mathbbm{1}}_{|y|>1}+\|P_{s}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{0}}}|y|^{\beta_{0}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{|y|\leqslant 1}\Big)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|^{\gamma}\Big(\|g\|_{\infty}+\|P_{s}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{0}}}\Big),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall s\in(0,1],\ |v|\leqslant 1.

Hence, for
\beta_{0}\in(\alpha,2]
and all
s\in(0,2]
and
\ |v|\leqslant 1
,

\|[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s}{g}\|_{\infty}\lesssim|v|^{\gamma}\Big(s^{\frac{\gamma\wedge\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}}+\|P_{s}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{0}}}\Big).

Thus, by (
2.28
) with
t_{0}=0
,
since
\gamma\wedge\alpha+\alpha>1
, we have for any
\beta\in[0,\alpha)
,

\displaystyle\|\delta^{3}_{v}P_{t}{g}

\displaystyle\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|\delta^{2}_{v}P_{t}\delta_{v}{g}\|_{\infty}+\int^{t}_{0}\|\delta^{2}_{v}P_{t-s}[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s}{g}\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{LK1}}{\lesssim}|v|^{\beta}t^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|\delta_{v}{g}\|_{\infty}+|v|^{\beta}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s}{g}\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\ \ \lesssim|v|^{\beta+\gamma}t^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}}+|v|^{\beta+\gamma}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\Big(s^{\frac{\gamma\wedge\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}}+\|P_{s}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{0}}}\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\ \ \lesssim|v|^{\beta+\gamma}\left(t^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}}+\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|P_{s}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{0}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right).

(2.31)

In particular, if one chooses
\beta\in((\alpha-\gamma)_{+},\alpha)
and
let
\beta_{0}=(\beta+\gamma)\wedge 2
, then by Lemma
2.3
,

\|P_{t}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{0}}}\lesssim\|P_{t}g\|_{\infty}+\sup_{|v|\leqslant 1}\|\delta^{3}_{v}P_{t}{g}\|_{\infty}/|v|^{\beta_{0}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}}+\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|P_{s}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{0}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

which yields by Gronwall’s inequality of Volterra’s type (see

]
) that for any

,

\displaystyle\|P_{t}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{(\beta+\gamma)\wedge 2}}=\|P_{t}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta_{0}}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}}.

(2.32)

Substituting this back into (
2.31
), we get (
2.27
) for
t\in(0,2]
.

(Large time estimate)

In the following we fix

\beta\in((\alpha-\gamma)_{+},\alpha),\ m\in(0,\alpha\wedge(2-\alpha)),\ t\in(2,\infty).

Define
t_{0}:=t-1
.
By (
2.29
) and (
2.22
), for
s\in(0,1]

\displaystyle|[\delta_{v},b\cdot\nabla]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}(x)|\lesssim|v|^{\gamma}{\mathscr{I}}^{s+t_{0}}_{m}(x)\|g\|_{\infty}\lesssim|v|^{\gamma}{\mathscr{I}}^{t_{0}}_{m}(x)\|g\|_{\infty},\ \ |v|\leqslant 1.

(2.33)

For
s\in(0,1]
and
|y|\leqslant 1

\|\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s+t_{0}}g\|_{\infty}=\|\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s}P_{t_{0}}g\|_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Sma1}}}{{\lesssim}}s^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}|y|^{(\beta+\gamma)\wedge 2}\|P_{t_{0}}g\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{\gamma}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Gr01}}}{{\lesssim}}s^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}|y|^{\beta+\gamma}\|g\|_{\infty},

which implies that for any

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leqslant R}\frac{|(\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s+t_{0}}g)(x)|}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\lesssim s^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{\infty}\int_{|y|\leqslant R}|y|^{(\beta+\gamma)\wedge 2-d-\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\lesssim s^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{\infty}R^{(\beta+\gamma)\wedge 2-\alpha}.

\bar{g}(x):=g(x)-\mu(g)

|(\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s+t_{0}}g)(x)|=|(\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s+t_{0}}\bar{g})(x)|\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t_{0}}\|g\|_{\infty}(1+|x|^{m}+|y|^{m}),

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geqslant R}\frac{|(\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s+t_{0}}g)(x)|}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t_{0}}\|g\|_{\infty}\int_{|y|\geqslant R}\frac{1+|x|^{m}+|y|^{m}}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t_{0}}\|g\|_{\infty}(R^{-\alpha}(1+|x|^{m})+R^{m-\alpha})

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geqslant R}\frac{|(\delta^{(2)}_{y}P_{s+t_{0}}g)(x)|}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\lesssim\|g\|_{\infty}R^{-\alpha}(1\wedge(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t_{0}}(1+|x|^{m})))=\|g\|_{\infty}R^{-\alpha}{\mathscr{I}}_{m}^{t_{0}}(x).

(2.35)

Hence, by (
2.30
), (
2.35
) and (

\displaystyle|[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma}]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}(x)|\lesssim|v|^{\gamma}s^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{\infty}\Big(R^{(\beta+\gamma)\wedge 2-\alpha}+R^{-\alpha}{\mathscr{I}}_{m}^{t_{0}}(x)\Big)=|v|^{\gamma}s^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{\infty}\big({\mathscr{I}}_{m}^{t_{0}}(x)\big)^{\tau},

2.26
) with

and

being
0
and
m\tau
,

\displaystyle|\delta^{2}_{v}P_{1-s}[\delta_{v},{\mathscr{L}}]P_{s+t_{0}}{g}(x)|\lesssim|v|^{\beta+\gamma}(1-s)^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}s^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|g\|_{\infty}({\mathscr{I}}^{t_{0}}_{m}(x))^{\tau}.

(2.36)

On the other hand, by (
2.26
) with
f=\delta_{v}P_{t_{0}}{g}
, noting that
t_{0}>1
,
|v|\leqslant 1

|\delta_{v}P_{t_{0}}{g}(x)|\leqslant|v|\int^{1}_{0}|\nabla P_{t_{0}}{g}(x+rv)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\lesssim\|g\|_{\infty}|v|{\mathscr{I}}^{t_{0}}_{m}(x),

\displaystyle|\delta^{2}_{v}P_{1}\delta_{v}P_{t_{0}}{g}(x)|\lesssim|v|^{\alpha+1}\|g\|_{\infty}{\mathscr{I}}^{t_{0}}_{m}(x).

(2.37)

Substituting (
2.36
) and (
2.37
) into (

) with

\displaystyle|\delta^{3}_{v}P_{t}{g}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|^{\alpha+1}\|g\|_{\infty}{\mathscr{I}}^{t_{0}}_{m}(x)+|v|^{\beta+\gamma}\|g\|_{\infty}({\mathscr{I}}^{t_{0}}_{m}(x))^{\tau}\int^{1}_{0}(1-s)^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}s^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

which in turn gives the large time estimate in (

In the following we write

2.4.
The Stein/Poisson equation

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
be the space of all continuous functions that vanishes at infinity.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be the generator of
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
in Banach space
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{D}}({\mathscr{A}}):=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}:\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{P_{t}f-f}{t}=:{\mathscr{A}}f\mbox{ exists in ${\mathcal{C}}_{0}$}\right\}.

By Itô’s formula, it is easy to see that

\forall f\in{\mathbf{C}}^{2}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{D}}({\mathscr{A}}),\ \ {\mathscr{A}}f={\mathscr{L}}f.

Moreover,
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
. Indeed,
by
[
38
, Proposition 2.4, p.89]
, it suffices to show that for any
f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
and
each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}|P_{t}f(x)-f(x)|=0,

(2.42)

and for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle P_{t}f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}.

(2.43)

For (
2.42
), it follows by the dominated convergence theorem. For (
2.43
), we only need to show

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}P_{t}f(x)=0.

(2.44)

For given
\varepsilon>0
, since
f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
, there is an
R>0
such that

\sup_{|x|>R}|f(x)|\leqslant\varepsilon.

Thus by (
1.7
) and the dominated convergence theorem, since
\lim_{x\to\infty}\theta_{t}(x)=\infty
, we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}|P_{t}f(x)|\leqslant\varepsilon+\lim_{x\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}(|f(X^{x}_{t}){\mathbbm{1}}_{|X^{x}_{t}|<R})\leqslant\varepsilon+Ct\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_{|y|<R}\frac{|f(y)|}{(t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|\theta_{t}(x)-y|)^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\leqslant\varepsilon,

which gives (
2.44
) by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
.

We have the following standard result.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
g\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
and
\bar{g}=g-\mu(g)
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle f(x):=\int^{\infty}_{0}P_{t}\bar{g}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathscr{A}})\mbox{ and }{\mathscr{A}}f=-\bar{g}.

(2.45)

Proof.

By (
2.20
), it is clear that for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
m\in(0,\alpha)
,

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}|P_{t}\bar{g}(x)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\leqslant C_{m}\lambda^{-1}(1+|x|^{m})<\infty

Hence,
f
is well-defined.
For
\varepsilon>0
, let
U_{\varepsilon}
be the resolvent of
{\mathscr{A}}
, that is, for any
g\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
,

U_{\varepsilon}g:=\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon t}P_{t}g{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathscr{A}}).

By the Hille-Yosida Theorem (see
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
),

{\mathscr{A}}
is closed and for any
\varepsilon>0
and

(\varepsilon{\mathbb{I}}-{\mathscr{A}})U_{\varepsilon}g=g\Longleftrightarrow U_{\varepsilon}g=(\varepsilon{\mathbb{I}}-{\mathscr{A}})^{-1}g.

In particular,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}U_{\varepsilon}\bar{g}=(\varepsilon U_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbb{I}})\bar{g}=:g_{\varepsilon}.

(2.46)

Noting that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}U_{\varepsilon}\bar{g}=\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{t}\bar{g}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t=f,\ \ \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}g_{\varepsilon}=-\bar{g}\mbox{ in ${\mathcal{C}}_{0}$},

we have

and
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}{\mathscr{A}}U_{\varepsilon}\bar{g}={\mathscr{A}}f
.
By taking limits for (
2.46
), we get (
2.45
).
∎

The proof is complete by taking
\beta
close to
\alpha
.
∎

Appendix A
Ergodicity of the EM scheme (
1.10
)

First of all, we present the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
m\in(0,\alpha)
and
\rho_{m}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{m/2}
.
For any
d\times d
-matrix
A
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{A}\rho_{m}(x)\leqslant\frac{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}\left(\frac{m}{2-\alpha}+\frac{2^{1+\alpha}}{\alpha-m}\right)\|A\|^{\alpha}\rho_{m-\alpha}(x).

Proof.

We assume
A\not=0_{d\times d}
, and divide the proof into two cases.

(i) We first consider
|x|>1
. Write
R:=\tfrac{|x|}{2\|A\|}
.
By (
2.5
), we make the following decomposition:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{A}\rho_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{|z|\leqslant R}\delta^{(2)}_{Az}\rho_{m}(x)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{|z|>R}\delta^{(2)}_{Az}\rho_{m}(x)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, we need to estimate
\delta^{(2)}_{Az}\rho_{m}(x)
for any
|z|\leqslant R
.
By the calculus, we have

\nabla^{2}\rho_{m}(x)=m(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}-2}[(1+|x|^{2}){\mathbb{I}}-(2-m)x\otimes x].

Since
m<\alpha<2
, we immediately have for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\nabla^{2}\rho_{m}(x)\xi\rangle\leqslant m(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}-1}|\xi|^{2}\leqslant m|\xi|^{2}.

(A.1)

By Taylor’s expansion, we have for any
|z|\leqslant R=\tfrac{|x|}{2\|A\|}
,

\displaystyle|\delta^{(2)}_{Az}\rho_{m}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int^{1}_{0}\int^{1}_{-1}\langle\nabla^{2}\rho_{m}(x+r_{1}r_{2}Az)Az,r_{2}Az\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r_{1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant m|Az|^{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int^{1}_{-1}r_{2}(1+|x+r_{1}r_{2}Az|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r_{1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2m\|A\|^{2}|z|^{2}\Big(1+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{4}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}-1}.

Hence, recalling
R=\tfrac{|x|}{2\|A\|}
and
S_{d}=2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}/\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant m\|A\|^{2}\Big(1+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{4}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\int_{|z|\leqslant R}|z|^{2-d-\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle=m\|A\|^{2}\Big(1+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{4}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}-1}S_{d}\int_{0}^{R}r^{1-\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\leqslant\frac{mS_{d}}{2-\alpha}\|A\|^{\alpha}|x|^{m-\alpha}.

For
I_{2}
, noting that

|a+b|^{r}\leqslant|a|^{r}+|b|^{r},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall a,b\in{\mathbb{R}},\ r\in(0,1],

we have for any
x,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\displaystyle\delta_{z}\rho_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=|(1+|x+z|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}-(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}|\leqslant||x+z|^{2}-|x|^{2}|^{\frac{m}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(2|x||z|+|z|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\leqslant(|x|^{2}+2|z|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\leqslant|x|^{m}+2^{\frac{m}{2}}|z|^{m}.

(A.2)

Then recalling
R=\tfrac{|x|}{2\|A\|}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|z|>R}\delta_{Az}\rho_{m}(x)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\leqslant\int_{|z|>R}\Big(|x|^{m}+2^{\frac{m}{2}}\|A\|^{m}|z|^{m}\Big)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leqslant S_{d}\int_{R}^{\infty}\Big(|x|^{m}+2^{\frac{m}{2}}\|A\|^{m}r^{m}\Big)r^{-1-\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\leqslant\frac{2^{1+\alpha}S_{d}}{\alpha-m}\|A\|^{\alpha}|x|^{m-\alpha}.

Combining the above calculations, we get for
|x|>1
,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{A}\rho_{m}(x)\leqslant S_{d}\left(\frac{m}{2-\alpha}+\frac{2^{1+\alpha}}{\alpha-m}\right)\|A\|^{\alpha}|x|^{m-\alpha}\leqslant S_{d}\left(\frac{m}{2-\alpha}+\frac{2^{1+\alpha}}{\alpha-m}\right)\|A\|^{\alpha}\rho_{m-\alpha}(x).

(ii) We consider
|x|\leqslant 1
. By (
2.5
), noting that

\delta_{Az}\rho_{m}(x)\overset{\eqref{bs:1}}{\leqslant}1+2^{\frac{m}{2}}|Az|^{m}\leqslant 1+2^{\frac{m}{2}}\|A\|^{m}|z|^{m},

we directly get

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{A}\rho_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{|z|\leqslant\frac{1}{\|A\|}}\delta^{(2)}_{Az}\rho_{m}(x)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}+\int_{|z|>\frac{1}{\|A\|}}\delta_{Az}\rho_{m}(x)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{bs:0}}{\leqslant}m\|A\|^{2}\int_{|z|\leqslant\frac{1}{\|A\|}}|z|^{2}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}+\int_{|z|>\frac{1}{\|A\|}}\left(1+2^{\frac{m}{2}}\|A\|^{m}|z|^{m}\right)\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leqslant mS_{d}\|A\|^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\|A\|}}r^{1-\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+S_{d}\int_{\frac{1}{\|A\|}}^{\infty}\left(1+2^{\frac{m}{2}}\|A\|^{m}r^{m}\right)r^{-1-\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\leqslant\ S_{d}\left(\frac{m}{2-\alpha}+\frac{2^{1+\frac{m}{2}}}{\alpha-m}\right)\|A\|^{\alpha}\leqslant S_{d}\left(\frac{m}{2-\alpha}+\frac{2^{1+\alpha}}{\alpha-m}\right)\|A\|^{\alpha}\rho_{m-\alpha}(y).

Combining (i) and (ii), we complete the proof.
∎

Recalling the EM scheme
(Y^{x}_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
defined in (
1.10
), we have

Lemma A.2
.

Under
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
and (
1.9
), for any
m\in(0,\alpha)
,
there exist
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(\Theta)\in(0,1)
and
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
depending on
\Theta,m,\eta_{0}

such that for any
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
, there is a unique invariant probability measure
\mu_{\eta}
such that for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y_{k}^{x}|^{m}\leqslant(1-C_{1}\eta)^{k}\rho_{m}(x)+C_{2},

(A.3)

and for any
f\in L_{m}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f/\rho_{m}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1}|\mathbb{E}f(Y_{k}^{x})-\mu_{\eta}(f)|\leqslant C_{3}\rho_{m}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda k},

(A.4)

where
\lambda=-\log(1-C_{1}\eta)>0
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{m}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{m/2}
with
m\in(0,\alpha)
.
Fix
\eta>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We introduce an auxiliary process

Z^{\eta,x}_{t}:=x+\eta b(x)+\sigma(x)L^{(\alpha)}_{t},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall t\in[0,\eta].

By Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\rho_{m}(Z^{\eta,x}_{\eta})]=\rho_{m}(Z^{\eta,x}_{0})+\int^{\eta}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x)}\rho_{m}(Z^{\eta,x}_{s})\right]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

(A.5)

On the other hand, by the chain rule and
(\mathbf{H}^{\gamma})
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{m}(Z^{\eta,x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\rho_{m}(x)+\int^{\eta}_{0}\left\langle b(x),\nabla\rho_{m}(x+rb(x))\right\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle=\rho_{m}(x)+m\int^{\eta}_{0}\left\langle b(x),x+rb(x)\right\rangle\rho_{m-2}(x+rb(x)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho_{m}(x)+m\int^{\eta}_{0}\left(-\kappa_{1}|x|^{2}+\kappa_{2}+r|b(x)|^{2}\right)\rho_{m-2}(x+rb(x)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

Since
b
is linear growth, there are
\eta_{1}>0
and
K_{1}>1
so that for all
r\in[0,\eta_{1}]
,

r|b(x)|^{2}\leqslant\kappa_{1}|x|^{2}/2+K_{1},

and

K^{-1}_{1}\rho_{m-2}(x)\leqslant\rho_{m-2}(x+rb(x))\leqslant K_{1}\rho_{m-2}(x).

Hence, there are
C_{1},K_{2}>0
and
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(\Theta)\in(0,1)
such that for any
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
,

\displaystyle\rho_{m}(Z^{\eta,x}_{0})

\displaystyle\leqslant(1-C_{1}\eta)\rho_{m}(x)+K_{2}\eta.

(A.6)

On the other hand, by Lemma
A.1
, there is a
K_{3}>0
such that for all
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\int^{\eta}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathscr{L}}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma(x)}\rho_{m}(Z^{\eta,x}_{s})\right]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant K_{3}\eta.

(A.7)

Combining (
A.5
), (
A.6
) and (
A.7
), there is a
C_{2}=C_{2}(\Theta)>0
such that for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\rho_{m}(Z^{\eta,x}_{\eta})]\leqslant(1-C_{1}\eta)\rho_{m}(x)+C_{2}\eta.

(A.8)

Since
Y^{x}_{k}
and
L_{\eta(k+1)}-L_{\eta k}\sim L_{\eta}
are independent, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\rho_{m}(Y^{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}[\rho_{m}(Z^{\eta,y}_{\eta})]|_{y=Y^{x}_{k}}]\leqslant(1-C_{1}\eta){\mathbb{E}}[\rho_{m}(Y^{x}_{k})]+C_{2}\eta\leqslant\cdots

\displaystyle\leqslant(1-C_{1}\eta)^{k+1}\rho_{m}(x)+C_{2}\eta\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-C_{1}\eta)^{n},

which in turn yields (
A.3
).

Finally, since
L^{(\alpha)}_{\eta}
has a smooth density
q_{\eta}(z)
with the following two-sided estimate:

\displaystyle q_{\eta}(z)\overset{\eqref{TG:ep}}{\asymp}\eta(\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|z|)^{-d-\alpha},

(A.9)

the exponential ergodicity (
A.4
) follows by (
A.3
) and

[
30
, Theorem 6.3]
.

Appendix B
Explicit convergence rate of EM schemes for OU processes

In this appendix, we consider the one-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{x}_{t}=-X^{x}_{t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}L^{(\alpha)}_{t},\hskip 9.24994ptX^{x}_{0}=x,

(B.1)

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
, and the corresponding EM scheme: for
\eta\in(0,1)
and
k\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle Y^{x}_{k+1}=Y^{x}_{k}-\eta Y^{x}_{k}+L^{(\alpha)}_{\eta(k+1)}-L^{(\alpha)}_{\eta k},\ \ Y^{x}_{0}=x,

(B.2)

where
(L^{(\alpha)}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a
1
-dimensional symmetric
\alpha
-stable process with characteristic function

{\mathbb{E}}\mathrm{e}^{izL^{(\alpha)}_{1}}=\mathrm{e}^{-|z|^{\alpha}}.

The main result of this section is presented as below:

Proposition B.1
.

Let
\mu
,
\mu_{\eta}
be the stationary distribution of (
B.1
) and (
B.2
), respectively. Then

\mu\sim\alpha^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}L^{(\alpha)}_{1},\ \ \mu_{\eta}\sim(\tfrac{\eta}{1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}L^{(\alpha)}_{1}.

Proof.

(i) Since
\mu
is the stationary distribution of SDE (
B.1
), we have for any
f\in{\mathbf{C}}^{2}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x)-xf^{\prime}(x)\big)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=0.

In particular, taking
f(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-izx}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-izx}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=-iz\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\mathrm{e}^{-izx}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=z\partial_{z}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-izx}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x).

Let
\hat{\mu}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-izx}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)
be the Fourier transform of
\mu
.
By
[
43
, p. 117]
, we have

\displaystyle-|z|^{\alpha}\hat{\mu}(z)=\widehat{\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mu}(z)=z\hat{\mu}(z)^{\prime},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall z\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since
\hat{\mu}(0)=1
, solving this differential equation, we get

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(z)=\mathrm{e}^{-|z|^{\alpha}/\alpha}\Rightarrow\mu\sim\alpha^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}L^{(\alpha)}_{1}.

(B.3)

(ii) Let
Y_{0}=\xi
and
\xi\sim\mu_{\eta}
be independent of
(L^{(\alpha)}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
. We have

Y_{1}=(1-\eta)\xi+L^{(\alpha)}_{\eta}\sim\mu_{\eta},

which implies that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu_{\eta}}(z)={\mathbb{E}}{\Big[}\mathrm{e}^{-iz\xi}{\Big]}={\mathbb{E}}{\Big[}\mathrm{e}^{-iz((1-\eta)\xi+L^{(\alpha)}_{\eta})}{\Big]}=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|z|^{\alpha}}{\mathbb{E}}{\Big[}\mathrm{e}^{-iz(1-\eta)\xi}{\Big]}=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|z|^{\alpha}}\widehat{\mu_{\eta}}(z-z\eta).

By induction method, we have for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu_{\eta}}(z)=\widehat{\mu_{\eta}}{\big(}(1-\eta)^{m}z{\big)}\prod_{k=0}^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta(1-\eta)^{\alpha k}|z|^{\alpha}}=\widehat{\mu_{\eta}}{\big(}(1-\eta)^{m}z{\big)}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|z|^{\alpha}\frac{1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha m}}{1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha}}}.

By taking
m\to\infty
, as
\eta\in(0,1)
and
\widehat{\mu_{\eta}}(0)=1
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu_{\eta}}(z)=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|z|^{\alpha}/(1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha})}\Rightarrow\mu_{\eta}\sim(\tfrac{\eta}{1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}L^{(\alpha)}_{1}.

(B.4)

The proof is complete.
∎

Based on Proposition
B.1
, we can show the following result.

Theorem B.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
{\bf d}(x,y)=|x-y|
. It holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{r^{\alpha}}{\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\leqslant\varliminf_{\eta\to 0}\frac{{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu_{\eta},\mu)}{\eta}\leqslant\varlimsup_{\eta\to 0}\frac{{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu_{\eta},\mu)}{\eta}\leqslant\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}{\mathbb{E}}\big|L^{(\alpha)}_{1}\big|.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
. By the definition of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}
and Proposition
B.1
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu_{\eta},\mu)=\ \inf_{(X,Y)\in\Pi(\mu_{\eta},\mu)}\mathbb{E}|X-Y|\leqslant\left|\big(\tfrac{\eta}{1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha}}\big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-\alpha^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right|{\mathbb{E}}\big|L^{(\alpha)}_{1}\big|.

Elementary calculations gives that

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\left|\big(\tfrac{\eta}{1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha}}\big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-\alpha^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right|/\eta=\tfrac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{\eta\to 0}{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu_{\eta},\mu)/\eta\leqslant\tfrac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}{\mathbb{E}}\big|L^{(\alpha)}_{1}\big|.

On the other hand, we introduce the function

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\int_{-1}^{1}\cos(rx){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

(B.5)

Clearly,
|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|\leqslant|x-y|
.
By (
1.11
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu_{\eta},\mu)\geqslant|\mu_{\eta}(\phi)-\mu(\phi)|.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\mu(\phi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{-1}^{1}\cos(rx){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{e}^{-irx}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\int_{-1}^{1}\hat{\mu}(r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\overset{\eqref{ft1}}{=}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|r|^{\alpha}}{\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

and

\mu_{\eta}(\phi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{e}^{-irx}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\mu_{\eta}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\mu_{\eta}}(r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\overset{\eqref{ft2}}{=}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|r|^{\alpha}/(1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

Noting that

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Big(\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|r|^{\alpha}/(1-(1-\eta)^{\alpha})}-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|r|^{\alpha}}{\alpha}}\Big)\eta^{-1}=-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}|r|^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|r|^{\alpha}}{\alpha}},

(B.6)

by the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\varliminf_{\eta\to 0}\frac{{\mathcal{W}}_{\bf d}(\mu_{\eta},\mu)}{\eta}\geqslant\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{|\mu_{\eta}(\phi)-\mu(\phi)|}{\eta}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}|r|^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|r|^{\alpha}}{\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

The proof is complete.
∎

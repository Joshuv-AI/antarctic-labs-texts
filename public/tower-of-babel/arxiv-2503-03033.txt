Supercritical phase transition on the Toeplitz algebra of ℕ × ⋉ ℤ {\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z} Thanks: This research was supported by the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada.
Marcelo Laca, Tyler Schulz

Abstract
. We study the high-temperature equilibrium for the C*-algebra 𝒯 ⁡ ( ℕ × ⋉ ℤ ) \mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}) recently considered by an Huef, Laca and Raeburn. We show that the simplex of KMS β \mathrm{KMS}_{\beta} states at each inverse temperature β \beta in the critical interval ( 0 , 1 ] (0,1] is a Bauer simplex whose space of extreme points is homeomorphic to ℕ ⊔ { ∞ } \mathbb{N}\sqcup\{\infty\} . This is in contrast to the uniqueness of equilibrium at high temperature observed in previously considered systems arising from number theory. We also show that quotients of our system exhibit spontaneous symmetry-breaking by finite cyclotomic Galois groups and establish their connection to the Bost-Connes phase transition.



1.
Introduction

The study of equilibrium states of C*-dynamical systems from number theory has been an increasingly active area of research since the seminal paper
[
3
]
in which Bost and Connes exhibit a phase transition with spontaneous symmetry-breaking on a noncommutative Hecke C*-algebra. Their construction has been generalized in several ways, to semigroup crossed products, to more general Hecke algebras, to groupoid C*-algebras, to Toeplitz algebras of
ax+b
monoids of algebraic integers, and to C*-algebras associated to
K
-lattices
[
35
,
25
,
31
,
32
,
23
,
13
,
12
,
36
,
18
]
. In a vast majority of the existing constructions there is a critical value
T_{c}=1/\beta_{c}
of the temperature above which the simplex of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states consists of a single point, but below which the nontrivial structure of the simplex sheds light on the original structure used in the construction. Notably, for Bost–Connes type systems associated to number fields, the extremal equilibrium states at low temperature carry a free transitive action of the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension of the field, pointing to a tantalizing connection with concrete class field theory
[
12
,
37
,
54
]
.

Developed along similar lines in
[
17
,
36
,
18
]
are the Toeplitz-type systems for
ax+b
semigroups of algebraic integers, which have played an important role in the study of C*-algebras of general semigroups
[
42
]
. Furthermore, the phase transition observed at low temperature for these systems has brought about an important characterization of KMS states (and, in particular, traces) in terms of orbits and isotropy groups for groupoid C*-algebras
[
46
]
.
Another interesting avenue of research motivated by this is the study of phase transition of a system at low temperatures, and the analysis of the Toeplitz-type systems suggest that this ‘crystallization’ process is related to the
K
-theory of the C*-algebra
[
39
]
.

In recent work
[
28
]
, an Huef, Laca, and Raeburn studied the structure of the Toeplitz C*-algebra
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
generated by the left regular representation of
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N}
on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
. Here
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N}
denotes the semidirect product of the nonzero natural numbers
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
acting by multiplication on
\mathbb{N}
, where the operation is
(a,m)(b,n)=(ab,bm+n)
for
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. They showed that
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
has a natural dynamics and that for large inverse temperatures (
\beta\in(1,\infty)
) the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of the resulting Toeplitz system correspond to probability measures on the unit circle. Intriguingly, they pointed out that there are more than one
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states at the critical inverse temperature
\beta=1

[
28
, Examples 9.1–9.3]
. This unprecedented high-temperature phase transition motivates the present work, in which we advance the study of equilibrium for the Toeplitz system of
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N}
by describing the simplex of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states in the supercritical temperature range
T=\beta^{-1}\geq 1
, that is, for inverse temperatures
\beta\in[0,1]
, see Theorem
1.1
below.

We choose to focus on the monoid
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}
from the onset because all
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
factor through a surjective homomorphism to
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
(cf. Remark
2.3
). The C*-algebra
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
is generated by a unitary
U
and isometries
V_{a}
,
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
acting on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
by

U\varepsilon_{(b,n)}=\varepsilon_{(b,n+b)},\qquad V_{a}\varepsilon_{(b,n)}=\varepsilon_{(ab,n)}.

We’ll see in Proposition
2.1
that the elements of the form
V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*}
span a dense *-subalgebra, so the dynamics and the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states are determined by their values on these elements.

Our formulas for the evaluation of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states are expressed in terms of elementary functions from number theory.
Recall that the Euler totient function
\varphi
counts the numbers between
1
and a given positive integer
n
that are relatively prime to
n
; equivalently,
\varphi(n)
is the order of the group
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
of invertible elements in the ring
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. In terms of the prime factors of
n
,

\varphi(n)=n\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-1}).

It will be convenient for us to introduce a
generalized totient function

\varphi_{\beta}:\mathbb{N}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that includes an additional inverse temperature parameter
\beta\geq 0
and is given by

\varphi_{\beta}(n):=n^{\beta}\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta}).

In particular,
\varphi_{1}
is Euler’s function
\varphi
, and
\varphi_{0}=\delta_{1}
.

We also make use of the Möbius function
\mu:\mathbb{N}^{\times}\to\{-1,0,1\}
, which vanishes if
n
is not square-free, and satisfies
\mu(n)=1
(respectively,
-1
) if
n
is square-free and has an even (respectively, odd) number of distinct prime factors.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\sigma
be the natural dynamics on
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
determined by

\sigma_{t}(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=(a/b)^{it}V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*}\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ k\in\mathbb{Z},\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Suppose
\beta\in(0,1]
. Then

(a)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
there is an extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state
\psi_{\beta,n}
of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
determined by

(1.2)

\psi_{\beta,n}(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}a^{-\beta}\Big(\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}\Big)^{-\beta}\sum_{d|\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}\mu\left(d\right)\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(d)}{\varphi(d)};

(b)

for
n=\infty
there is an extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state
\psi_{\beta,\infty}
of type
\mathrm{III}
determined by

(1.3)

\psi_{\beta,\infty}(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}\delta_{k,0}a^{-\beta};

(c)

the simplex
K_{\beta}
of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}),\sigma)
is a Bauer simplex with extreme boundary

\partial_{e}K_{\beta}=\{\psi_{\beta,n}:n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\sqcup\{\infty\}\};

specifically, the map
n\mapsto\psi_{\beta,n}
is a homeomorphism of the one-point compactification

\mathbb{N}^{\times}\sqcup\{\infty\}
onto the space
\partial_{e}K_{\beta}
with the weak-* topology.

Suppose
\beta=0
. Then all the
\psi_{0,n}
for finite
n
coalesce into one and the system has exactly two extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{0}
states (i.e. invariant traces)
\psi_{0,1}
and
\psi_{0,\infty}
; they are given by

\psi_{0,1}(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{0,\infty}(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}\delta_{k,0}.

The proof of this theorem will occupy most of the paper; for quick reference, the parametrization is achieved in
Proposition
8.1
and the classification of type is in Corollary
10.9
. Our methods address the question raised at the end of the introduction of
[
47
]
of how to classify
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of right LCM monoids in the region of divergence of the partition function and may provide useful insight for the groupoid approach.

A remarkable feature of the high-temperature phase transition is its spontaneous symmetry-breaking, which is related to the one observed for the Bost-Connes system in
[
3
]
. The formulas from Theorem
1.1
are valid for all non-negative
\beta
, which indicates that the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states for
\beta\leq 1
are linked by analytic continuation to
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states for
\beta>1
. As the temperature decreases, the von Neumann type of the states from (
1.2
) changes from factor of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
(for
\beta\leq 1
) to a uniform superposition

(1.4)

\psi_{\beta,n}=\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{\xi\in Z_{n}^{*}}\phi_{\beta,\xi}

of the type
{\rm I}_{\infty}
factor states from
[
28
]
corresponding to primitive
n^{\mathrm{th}}
roots of unity (for
\beta>1
).

The symmetries of the system are expressed by an action of
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
on
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
by injective endomorphisms
\kappa_{q}
given by
\kappa_{q}(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=V_{a}U^{qk}V_{b}^{*}
for
q\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
. These commute with the dynamics so they are symmetries in the sense of
[
13
]
; they resemble the Frobenius endomorphisms in finite characteristic. At the level of extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states, the endomorphism
\kappa_{q}
acts as a
lowering operator
on the
\psi_{\beta,n}
for finite
n
, effectively dividing
n
by
\gcd(n,q)
. In particular, when
n
and
q
are relatively prime,
\psi_{\beta,n}
is fixed by
\kappa_{q}
and moreover, the GNS representation determines a quotient of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
on which
\kappa_{q}
becomes an automorphism. Symmetry is broken at low temperature because the
\kappa_{q}
permute the factor states in (
1.4
). Specifically, the transformation is
\phi_{\beta,\xi}\circ\kappa_{q}=\phi_{\beta,\xi^{q}}
, resembling Artin’s reciprocity law for the cyclotomic extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{1})/\mathbb{Q}
. In Section
10
, we realize the GNS quotient of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
as the fixed-point subalgebra of the Bost-Connes algebra for the symmetries
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\rm cycl}/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{1}))
, establishing a link between KMS states of our system and class field theory of
\mathbb{Q}
.

The values of extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states given
in (
1.2
) are expressed in terms of basic arithmetic functions, cf.
[
3
, Remark 26]
. These expressions are quite efficient but do not provide by themselves much insight on the underlying structure or method of proof. To shed some light on this, we recall that
by
[
28
, Proposition 7.2]
a state
\psi
of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
satisfies the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
condition if and only if

(1.5)

\psi(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}a^{-\beta}\psi(U^{k}),\qquad k\in\mathbb{N},\ a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}.

Hence, every
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state
\psi
is completely determined by its restriction to
C^{*}(U)\cong C(\mathbb{T})
, or rather by the probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
representing this restriction through the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani theorem. Thus, the extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states from Theorem
1.1
can also be characterized using probability measures on
\mathbb{T}
. The trouble is that not all such probability measures extend to states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
via (
1.5
); the issue here is positivity of the extension, which depends on whether a given measure
\nu
satisfies

-\sum_{1\neq d|n}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(z^{d})\,d\nu(z)\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(z)\,d\nu(z)\qquad\forall f\in C(\mathbb{T})_{+},\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times};

that is, on whether
\nu
is
\beta
-subconformal
in the sense of Definition
4.3
below, cf.
[
1
,
33
]
. This effectively reduces the problem of finding the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
to that of finding all the probability measures on
\mathbb{T}
that are
\beta
-subconformal for the transformations
z\mapsto z^{d}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
.
Thus, our strategy to prove Theorem
1.1
is to first obtain a characterization of extremal
\beta
-subconformal probability measures on
\mathbb{T}
.
This is summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

(1)

For each state
\psi
of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
let
\nu_{\psi}
be the probability measure representing the restriction of
\psi
to
C^{*}(U)\cong C(\mathbb{T})
. The mapping
\psi\mapsto\nu_{\psi}
is an affine weak-* homeomorphism of the
\sigma
-
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states onto the
\beta
-subconformal probability measures on
\mathbb{T}
.

(2)

For
\beta\in(0,1]
, the extremal
\beta
-subconformal probability measures are parametrized by
\mathbb{N}^{\times}\sqcup\{\infty\}
and are given as follows.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
the atomic probability measure
\nu_{\beta,n}
on
\mathbb{T}
is given by

\nu_{\beta,n}(\{z\}):=\begin{cases}n^{-\beta}\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(\mathrm{ord}(z))}{\varphi(\mathrm{ord}(z))}&\text{ if }z^{n}=1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise;}\end{cases}

and
\nu_{\beta,\infty}
normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
.
Moreover, the mapping
\psi_{\beta,n}\mapsto\nu_{\beta,n}
is a weak*-homeomorphism of the extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states onto
\{\nu_{\beta,n}:n=1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{\nu_{\beta,\infty}\}
.

Part (1) of the theorem is proved in Theorem
4.17
. The case of atomic measures in part (2) is proved in Theorem

2.
The Toeplitz system of
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}

Let
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
be the C*-subalgebra generated by the operators
T_{(a,m)}
on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
defined on the canonical orthonormal basis by

T_{(a,m)}\varepsilon_{(b,n)}=\varepsilon_{(ab,bm+n)}\qquad(a,m),\ (b,n)\in{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}.

Then
T_{(a,m)}=V_{a}U^{m}
, where
V_{a}=T_{(a,0)}
is an isometry for each
a
and
U=T_{(1,1)}
is a unitary.
Next we give a presentation of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
and use it to show that
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
is the additive boundary quotient of the C*-algebra
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
studied in
[
28
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The generating elements
\{V_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\}
and
U
of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
satisfy

(AB0)

V_{a}^{*}V_{a}=1=U^{*}U=UU^{*}

(AB1)

UV_{a}=V_{a}U^{a}
;

(AB2)

V_{a}V_{b}=V_{ab}
;

(AB3)

V_{a}^{*}V_{b}=V_{b}V_{a}^{*}
when
\gcd(a,b)=1
.

Moreover, the relations
(AB0)–(AB3)
constitute a presentation of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
and imply

(AB4)

U^{*}V_{a}=V_{a}U^{*a}
.

The C*-algebra
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
is canonically isomorphic to the additive boundary quotient
\partial_{add}\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
and

\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*}\ :\ a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Proof.

That the
V_{a}
are isometries and
U
is a unitary is obvious. That they satisfy relations (AB1)–(AB3) was verified in
[
28
, Example 3.9]
, while (4) is obtained on
multiplying (AB1) by
U^{*}
on the left and by
U^{*a}
on the right, see the proof of
[
28
, Proposition 3.8]
.

Let
C^{*}(u,v_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times})
be the universal C*-algebra generated by isometries
\{v_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\}
and a unitary
u
satisfying the lowercase-analogues of the relations (AB1)–(AB3). By the preceding considerations, there is a canonical surjective homomorphism
C^{*}(u,v_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
, which we will show is an isomorphism.

Recall from
[
28
]
that the monoid
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}
is right LCM; indeed, the smallest common upper bounds of
(a,m)
and
(b,n)
are the elements
(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b),k)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
(so we may take, e.g.
(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b),0)
). Since
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}
embeds in
{\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{\times}}\!\!\ltimes\mathbb{Q}
, we have that
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
is universal for Nica covariant representations of
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}
by
[
20
, Corollary 5.6.45]
.

The elements
w_{(a,n)}=v_{a}u^{n}
form an isometric representation of
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}
in
C^{*}(u,v_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times})
by (AB0)–(AB2). For
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, let
a^{\prime}=a/\gcd(a,b)
and
b^{\prime}=b/\gcd(a,b)
. Then (AB3) implies

w_{(a,m)}w_{(a,m)}^{*}w_{(b,n)}w_{(b,n)}^{*}=v_{a}v_{a}^{*}v_{b}v_{b}^{*}=v_{ab^{\prime}}v_{b^{\prime}a}^{*}=w_{(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b),0)}w_{(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b),0)}.

This shows that
w
is Nica covariant. Therefore, there is a canoncial surjective homomorphism
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow C^{*}(u,v_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times})
, which is the inverse to
C^{*}(u,v_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
.

The collection
\{V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*}\ :\ a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is obviously closed under taking adjoints, and

(2.2)

(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})(V_{c}U^{n}V_{d}^{*})=V_{ac^{\prime}}U^{mc^{\prime}+nb^{\prime}}V_{b^{\prime}d}^{*},

where
c^{\prime}=\frac{c}{\gcd(b,c)}
and
b^{\prime}=\frac{b}{\gcd(b,c)}
, so this collection is also closed under multiplication. Hence its linear span is a self-adjoint subalgebra of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
, which is dense because it contains the generating elements
V_{a}
for
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
and
U
.

Remark 2.3
.

The presentation of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
in Proposition
2.1
agrees with that of
\partial_{\mathrm{add}}\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
in
[
28
, Proposition 3.8]
, which implies that these C*-algebras are isomorphic. Moreover, by
[
28
, Proposition 7.1]
, the KMS states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
factor through the additive boundary quotient. This gives a 1-to-1 correspondence between KMS states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N})
and KMS states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a strongly continuous (gauge) action
\theta
of the compact group
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}}
by automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
such that

\theta\chi(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})=\chi(a/b)V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*}.

The fixed point algebra
\mathfrak{D}:=\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})^{\theta}
is a commutative unital C*-algebra and there is a faithful conditional expectation

E:\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{D}
determined by

E(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})=\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}}}\theta_{\chi}(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})d\chi=\delta_{a,b}V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*},

with range
E(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}))=\mathfrak{D}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_{a}U^{m}V_{a}^{*}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Proof.

The proof is by a standard argument and is almost entirely analogous to that of
[
28
, Proposition 8.2]
, the only difference being the computation of the product at the end. Here the additive generator
U
is a unitary operator and thus, once we verify that

E(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})=\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}}}\theta_{\chi}(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})d\chi=\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}}}\chi(a/b)(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})d\chi=\delta_{a,b}V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*},

where
\delta_{a,b}
is the Kronecker delta function, we may conclude that

\mathfrak{D}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_{a}U^{m}V_{a}^{*}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Setting
a=b
and
d=c
in (
2.2
), we get the product

(2.5)

(V_{b}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})(V_{c}U^{n}V_{c}^{*})=V_{\operatorname{lcm}(b,c)}U^{mc^{\prime}+nb^{\prime}}V_{\operatorname{lcm}(b,c)}^{*}=V_{\operatorname{lcm}(b,c)}U^{\operatorname{lcm}(b,c)(\frac{m}{b}+\frac{n}{c})}V_{\operatorname{lcm}(b,c)}^{*},

which shows that
\mathfrak{D}
is commutative.
∎

We are interested in the C*-dynamical system
(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}),\sigma)
in which
\sigma
is the dynamics determined by

\sigma_{t}(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})=\Big(\frac{a}{b}\Big)^{it}\,V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*}.

The study of equilibrium on
(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}),\sigma)
was initiated in
[
28
]
, where it was shown that
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{N}),\sigma)
factor through the additive boundary quotient. We briefly recall next the basic definitions and the key results needed in our analysis.

Whenever
\sigma
is a time evolution, or dynamics, on a C*-algebra
A
(this means that
\sigma
is a strongly continuous
\mathbb{R}
-action by automorphisms of
A
), there is a dense *-subalgebra
A^{\infty}
of
analytic elements
of
A
, consisting of elements
x\in A
for which the function
F_{x}(t)=\sigma_{t}(x)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
can be analytically continued to an entire function on
\mathbb{C}
. For
\beta\in[0,\infty)
, a state
\phi
on
A
satisfies the
\sigma
-
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
condition (or simply the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
condition, when
\sigma
is clear) if

\phi(xy)=\phi(y\sigma_{i\beta}(x))\qquad\text{ for $x\in A^{\infty}$ and $y\in A$}

in fact, because of bilinearity and continuity, it suffices to show that equality holds for
x
and
y
in a subset of
A^{\infty}
whose linear span is
\sigma
-invariant and dense in
A

[
49
, Proposition 8.12.3]
.
Every
\sigma
-
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state is also
\sigma
-invariant (for
\beta=0
this is part of the definition, so that
\sigma
-KMS
0
states are
\sigma
-invariant traces). The set
K_{\beta}
of
\sigma
-
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
A
, endowed with the weak* topology, is a Choquet simplex, and hence is affinely isomorphic to the simplex of probability measures on the set
\partial K_{\beta}
of its extreme points.
We refer to Chapter 5 of
[
4
]
and to Chapter 8 of
[
49
]
for further details and background.

When we consider our system
(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}),\sigma)
it is easy to see that the monomials
V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*}
are analytic for
\sigma
because
\sigma_{z}(V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*})=(a/b)^{iz}V_{a}U^{m}V_{b}^{*}
. Moreover, the dynamics
\sigma
is obtained by composing the continuous one-parameter subgroup of characters
\chi_{t}(r)=r^{it}
of
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}
with the gauge action
\theta
, and
the fixed-point subalgebra of
\sigma
agrees with the fixed-point subalgebra of
\theta
. Hence, the
\sigma
-invariant states on
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
, in particular the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states, are induced through the conditional expectation
E
from traces on
\mathfrak{D}
, or, equivalently, from measures on
X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}
.

For later reference, we record the following result analogous to
[
28
, Proposition 8.3]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

There exists an action
\alpha
of
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
by injective endomorphisms

\alpha_{a}:\mathfrak{D}\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}
defined by
\alpha_{a}(x)=V_{a}xV_{a}^{*}
for each

. Each
\alpha_{a}
has a left-inverse given by
\gamma_{a}(x)=V_{a}^{*}xV_{a}
.
Moreover, there is a semigroup crossed product decomposition

(2.7)

\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{N}^{\times}\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathfrak{D}.

Following
[

we use the ‘backwards’ notation for the above semigroup crossed product because it is more compatible with the semigroup operation in
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}
.

The action of

on
\mathfrak{D}
respects the lattice structure, see
[

, thus

implies that the map
\tau\mapsto\tau\circ E

satisfying

\tau(V_{a}xV_{a}^{*})=a^{-\beta}\tau(x)\qquad\forall a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ x\in\mathfrak{D}

and the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}),\sigma)
for

.

For

, the tracial states satisfying (

) were explicitly computed in

using
[

; they are in one-to-one correspondence with the probability measures
\eta
on

via the formula

\tau_{\eta,\beta}(V_{a}U^{n}V_{a}^{*})=\frac{a^{-\beta}}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}c^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}z^{nc}d\eta,

and the corresponding

(2.9)

\phi_{\eta,\beta}(V_{a}U^{n}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}\frac{a^{-\beta}}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}c^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}z^{nc}d\eta.

Remark 2.10
.

Obviously these formulas break down for

. Partly because of this, the question of equilibrium for

was left open in

, except for three KMS

from Lebesgue measure on

3.
The diagonal and its spectrum

In this section we provide a detailed description of the fixed point algebra
\mathfrak{D}
of the gauge action and its spectrum. We begin by outlining a unitarily equivalent copy of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
obtained via the Fourier transform on the second coordinate of
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}
. To be precise, we
let
\mathfrak{z}:z\mapsto z
be the inclusion
\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
(viewed as a complex-valued function on
\mathbb{T}
), and we normalize Haar measure on
\mathbb{T}
so that the collection
\{\mathfrak{z}^{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
of characters is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. Then there is a unitary transformation

\mathcal{F}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{\times})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\qquad\quad\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon_{(b,k)})=\delta_{b}\otimes\mathfrak{z}^{k},\quad(b,k)\in{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}.

When we conjugate the generators
U=T_{(1,1)}
and
V_{a}=T_{(a,0)}
of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
by
\mathcal{F}
we get operators

\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(U):=\mathcal{F}U\mathcal{F}^{-1}
and
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(V_{a}):=\mathcal{F}V_{a}\mathcal{F}^{-1}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{\times})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, and when we compute these on the standard orthonormal basis
\{\delta_{b}\otimes\mathfrak{z}^{m}:b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{\times})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
we get

(3.1)

\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(U)(\delta_{b}\otimes\mathfrak{z}^{k})=\mathcal{F}U\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\delta_{b}\otimes\mathfrak{z}^{k})=\mathcal{F}U\varepsilon_{(b,k)}=\mathcal{F}\varepsilon_{(b,b+k)}=\delta_{b}\otimes\mathfrak{z}^{b}\mathfrak{z}^{k}

and

(3.2)

\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(V_{a})(\delta_{b}\otimes\mathfrak{z}^{k})=\mathcal{F}V_{a}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\delta_{b}\otimes\mathfrak{z}^{k})=\mathcal{F}V_{a}\varepsilon_{(b,k)}=\mathcal{F}\varepsilon_{(ab,k)}=\delta_{ab}\otimes\mathfrak{z}^{k}.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
define a map

\omega_{a}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T},\qquad z\mapsto z^{a}.

wrapping the circle
a
-times around itself. Denote
by
\pi:C(\mathbb{T})\to\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{\times})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
the representation of
C(\mathbb{T})
generated by the unitary
u:=\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(U)
and let
v_{a}:=\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(V_{a})
.
Then

\pi(f)(\delta_{b}\otimes g):=\delta_{b}\otimes(f\circ\omega_{b})g\qquad b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\ \ f,g\in C(\mathbb{T}),

and the image of the fixed point algebra
\mathfrak{D}
under the isomorphism
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}:\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})\cong C^{*}(\pi,v)

is

(3.4)

\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{D})={\overline{\operatorname{span}}}\{v_{a}\pi(f)v_{a}^{*}:f\in C(\mathbb{T}),a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\}.

Proof.

It is easy to show using (
3.1
) that the first assertion holds for
f=\mathfrak{z}^{m}
and
g=\mathfrak{z}^{k}
, and the general case follows from this
because the characters
\{\mathfrak{z}^{m}:m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
span a dense subalgebra of
C(\mathbb{T})
.
Since we already know that
\mathfrak{D}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_{a}U^{m}V_{a}^{*}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, the second assertion is also a direct consequence of this.
∎

To simplify the notation from now on we will write
V_{a}fV_{a}^{*}
for the element of
\mathfrak{D}
corresponding to
v_{a}\pi(f)v_{a}^{*}
, so that, e.g.
V_{a}\mathfrak{z}^{m}V_{a}^{*}=V_{a}U^{m}V_{a}^{*}
. For each fixed
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
we also define

\mathfrak{D}_{a}:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_{d}fV_{d}^{*}:d|a,\ f\in C(\mathbb{T})\}.

This is a closed subspace which is closed under adjoints; it is also closed under multiplication because (
2.5
) implies that

(3.5)

V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}\,V_{d}gV_{d}^{*}=V_{c\vee d}(f\circ\omega_{d^{\prime}})(g\circ\omega_{c^{\prime}})V_{c\vee d}^{*},\qquad c,d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ g\in C(\mathbb{T}),

where we have written
c\vee d
for
\operatorname{lcm}(c,d)
, with
c^{\prime}=\frac{c}{\gcd(b,c)}
and
b^{\prime}=\frac{b}{\gcd(b,c)}
to streamline the notation. Hence
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
is a C*-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{D}
,
and the inclusions
\iota_{a,b}:\mathfrak{D}_{a}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{D}_{b}
for
a|b
give an injective system
(\mathfrak{D}_{a},\iota_{a,b})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
of C*-algebras whose union is dense in
\mathfrak{D}
by Lemma
3.3
, making
\mathfrak{D}
the direct limit of the system.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
define
e_{a}=\prod_{p|a}(1-V_{p}V_{p}^{*})
and for
b|a
let
e_{a,b}=\alpha_{b}(e_{\frac{a}{b}})=V_{b}e_{\frac{a}{b}}V_{b}^{*}
. Then
e_{a,b}
is a projection and

(1)

e_{a,b}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}V_{bd}^{*}
, and thus belongs to
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
;

(2)

\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}e_{a,bd}=V_{b}V_{b}^{*}
;

(3)

the map

(3.7)

\gamma_{a,b}:C(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow e_{a,b}\mathfrak{D}_{a}e_{a,b},\qquad f\mapsto e_{a,b}V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}(f\circ\omega_{d})V_{bd}^{*}

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

For part (1), the case
b=1
follows from the usual inclusion-exclusion formula and
V_{c}V_{c}^{*}V_{d}V_{d}^{*}=V_{cd}V_{cd}^{*}
for relatively prime divisors
c,d|a
. For more general
b
we have

\alpha_{b}(e_{\frac{a}{b},1})=V_{b}\left(\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{d}V_{d}^{*}\right)V_{b}^{*}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}V_{bd}^{*}.

For part (2), we have

\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}e_{a,bd}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\sum_{c|\frac{a}{bd}}\mu(c)V_{bcd}V_{bcd}^{*}=\sum_{e|\frac{a}{b}}V_{be}V_{be}^{*}\sum_{c|e}\mu(c)=V_{b}V_{b}^{*},

where we have made use of the substitution
e=cd
and the classical identity
\sum_{c|e}\mu(c)=\delta_{e,1}
.

For part (3), since
e_{a,b}
is a projection in the commutative algebra
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
, the map
\kappa_{b}:\mathfrak{D}_{a}\rightarrow e_{a,b}\mathfrak{D}_{a}e_{a,b}
,
x\mapsto e_{a,b}x
is a homomorphism. By part (1) and (
3.5
), composing
\kappa_{b}
with the map
f\mapsto V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}
yields

e_{a,b}V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}V_{bd}^{*}V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}(f\circ\omega_{d})V_{bd}^{*}

which is (
3.7
), so this is a homomorphism from
C(\mathbb{T})
to
e_{a,b}\mathfrak{D}_{a}e_{a,b}
.

In order to show that (
3.7
) is surjective, consider
V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}
for
c|a
and
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
. By part (2) with
b=1
, the projections
\{e_{a,d}:d|a\}
are mutually orthogonal. Again by part (2), for
c|a
,

V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}=V_{c}V_{c}^{*}V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{c}}e_{a,cd}V_{c}fV_{c}^{*};

hence if
c\nmid b
, then
e_{a,b}V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}=0
. If
c|b
, then using part (1) and (
3.5
),

e_{a,b}V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}V_{bd}^{*}V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}(f\circ\omega_{bd/c})V_{bd}^{*},

which is the image of
f\circ\omega_{b/c}
under (
3.7
). Since the elements
V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}
span a dense subspace of
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
, (
3.7
) is surjective.

Lastly, we show that (
3.7
) is faithful. If
e_{a,b}V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}=0
, then by part (2), it follows that

\displaystyle V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c|\frac{a}{b},c\neq 1}e_{a,bc}V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}=\sum_{c|\frac{a}{b},c\neq 1}\sum_{d|\frac{a}{bc}}\mu(d)V_{bcd}V_{bcd}V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c|\frac{a}{b},c\neq 1}\sum_{d|\frac{a}{bc}}\mu(d)V_{bcd}(f\circ\omega_{cd})V_{bcd}^{*}.

The condition
c\neq 1
(hence
bcd\nmid b
) implies that the vector
\delta_{b}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{T}}
belongs to the kernel of
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(V_{bcd}^{*})
, where
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}
is the isomorphism of Lemma
3.3
; moreover,
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal{F}}(V_{b}fV_{b}^{*})(\delta_{b}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{T}})=\delta_{b}\otimes f
, so
f=0
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

For each
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
let
\Delta_{a}:=\{b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}:b|a\}
be the set of divisors of
a
and define a space

X_{a}:=\mathbb{T}\times\Delta_{a}.

For each
f\in C(X_{a})
and
b\in\Delta_{a}
let

f|_{b}(z)=f(z,b)
,
z\in\mathbb{T}
. Then the map

\Gamma_{a}:f\mapsto\sum_{b|a}e_{a,b}V_{b}f|_{b}V_{b}^{*}=\sum_{b|a}\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}(f|_{b}\circ\omega_{d})V_{bd}^{*}

is an isomorphism of C*-algebras
\Gamma_{a}:C(X_{a})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{D}_{a}
. The inverse is determined by the formula

(3.9)

\Gamma_{a}^{-1}(V_{b}fV_{b}^{*})(z,d)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f\circ\omega_{\frac{d}{b}}(z)&\text{if $b|d$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

The algebra
C(X_{a})
is naturally identified with
\bigoplus_{b|a}C(\mathbb{T})
, where a function
f\in C(X_{a})
corresponds to the tuple
(f|_{b})_{b|a}
. Under this identification,
\Gamma_{a}
becomes

\Gamma_{a}=\bigoplus_{b|a}\gamma_{a,b}:\bigoplus_{b|a}C(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}_{a},

where
\gamma_{a,b}:C(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}_{a}
is the isomorphism onto
e_{a,b}\mathfrak{D}_{a}e_{a,b}
from Lemma
3.6
(3). Since the projections
\{e_{a,b}:b|a\}
are mutually orthogonal and sum to the identity,
\Gamma_{a}
is an isomorphism onto
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
.

For
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
b\in\Delta_{a}
, let
\widetilde{f}\in C(X)
denote the function in (
3.9
). Then, by the Möbius inversion formula, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a}(\widetilde{f})

\displaystyle=\sum_{c|a}\sum_{d|\frac{a}{c}}\mu(d)V_{cd}(\widetilde{f}|_{c}\circ\omega_{d})V_{cd}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c|\frac{a}{b}}\sum_{d|\frac{a}{c}}\ \mu(d)V_{bcd}(f\circ\omega_{cd})V_{bcd}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c|\frac{a}{b}}V_{bc}(f\circ\omega_{c})V_{bc}^{*}\left(\sum_{d|c}\mu(d)\right)=V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}.

Since
\Gamma_{a}
is an isomorphism, we conclude that
\widetilde{f}=\Gamma^{-1}_{a}(V_{b}fV_{b}^{*})
.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

For each
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
and for
a|b
, define
\Psi_{a,b}:X_{b}\to X_{a}
by

\Psi_{a,b}(z,d):=(z^{d/\gcd(a,d)},\gcd(a,d))\qquad\text{ for each }(z,d)\in X_{b}.

Then
\Psi=(X_{a},\Psi_{a,b})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is a projective system that is topologically conjugate to the projective system
\iota^{*}=(\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}_{a},\iota_{a,b}^{*})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
under the
transformations
\Gamma_{a}^{*}:\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}_{a}\to X_{a}
, and this gives a homeomorphism

\projlim_{a}(X_{a},\Psi_{a,})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\Gamma_{a}^{*}\circ\iota_{a,b}^{*}=\Psi_{a,b}\circ\Gamma_{b}^{*}
on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}_{b}
, or dually, that
\Gamma_{b}^{-1}\circ\iota_{a,b}=\Psi_{a,b}^{*}\circ\Gamma_{a}^{-1}
on
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
. For
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
c|a
, applying the first homomorphism to
V_{c}fV_{c}^{*}
and evaluating at
(z,d)\in\Delta_{b}
gives

\Gamma_{b}^{-1}\circ\iota_{a,b}(V_{c}fV_{c}^{*})(z,d)=\Gamma_{b}^{-1}(V_{c}fV_{c}^{*})(z,d)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f\circ\omega_{\frac{d}{c}}(z)&\text{if $c|d$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Applying the second homomorphism and evaluating at
(z,d)
gives

\Psi_{a,b}^{*}\circ\Gamma_{a}^{-1}(V_{c}fV_{c}^{*})=\Gamma_{a}^{-1}(V_{c}fV_{c}^{*})(z^{\frac{d}{\gcd(a,d)}},\gcd(a,d))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f\circ\omega_{\frac{\gcd(a,d)}{c}}(z^{\frac{d}{\gcd(a,d)}})&\text{if $c|\gcd(a,d)$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Since
c|a
, the conditions
c|d
and
c|\gcd(a,d)
are equivalent, in which case both formulas agree.
∎

4.
KMS states and subconformal measures

According to
[
28
, Proposition 7.2]
, a state
\psi
on
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
satisfies the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
condition for the dynamics
\sigma
on
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
if and only if

(4.1)

\psi(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}a^{-\beta}\psi(U^{k})\quad\text{for all $a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}$ and $k\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

Thus each
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state is determined by its restriction to
C^{*}(U)=V_{1}C(\mathbb{T})V_{1}^{*}\cong C(\mathbb{T})
; we denote by
\nu_{\psi}
the probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
representing this restriction, so that

(4.2)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\nu_{\psi}=\psi(V_{1}fV_{1}^{*})\qquad f\in C(\mathbb{T}).

The map
\psi\mapsto\nu_{\psi}
of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states to probability measures is injective but, as discussed in the Introduction after Theorem
1.1
, it is not surjective. In order to determine its range we introduce the following condition, cf.
[
1
, Equation (2.1)]
.

Definition 4.3
.

A measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{T}
is
\beta
-subconformal
if it satisfies

(4.4)

\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\omega_{d*}(\nu)\geq 0\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},

or, more explicitly,

\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(z^{d})\,d\nu(z)\geq 0\qquad\forall f\in C(\mathbb{T})_{+}\text{ and }\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}.

It will be useful to formulate subconformality in terms of a family of operators on the space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
of complex Borel measures on
\mathbb{T}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For each
\beta\in[0,\infty)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
define an operator
A_{\beta,n}:\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\to\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
on the Banach space of complex Borel measures on
\mathbb{T}
using the left hand side of (
4.4
),

(4.6)

A_{\beta,n}(\nu):=\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\omega_{d*}\nu,\qquad\text{ i.e. \ }\int_{\mathbb{T}}fdA_{\beta,n}(\nu)=\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(z^{d})d\nu

for each
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
and
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
. Then

(1)

A_{\beta,m}A_{\beta,n}=A_{\beta,mn}
whenever
\gcd(m,n)=1
;

(2)

A_{\beta,n}=\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})
;

(3)

if
\beta\in(0,\infty)
, then
A_{\beta,n}
has a positive inverse, which for prime
n=p
is given by the norm-convergent series

A_{\beta,p}^{-1}=(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p^{-\beta n}\omega_{p^{n}*},

moreover
\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\nu
is a probability measure whenever
\nu
is a probability measure;

(4)

A_{\beta,m}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+}\supseteq A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+}
whenever
m|n
.

Proof.

A function on
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
satisfying part (1) is said to be
number-theoretic multiplicative
; notice that this will follow easily from part (2), which we prove next.
When
n=p
is prime, formula (
4.6
) becomes
A_{\beta,p}=1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*}
.
Since the operators
1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*}
commute with each other, the usual inclusion-exclusion formula for the expansion of the product
\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})
gives the formula in part (2) for square-free
n
. This suffices because the Möbius function eliminates the terms in which
d
has repeated prime factors, so that
A_{\beta,n}=A_{\beta,\prod_{p|n}p}
, where
\prod_{p|n}p
is square-free. This proves part (2).

For part (3) first notice that if
\nu
is a positive measure, then

\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(z)\,d\omega_{n*}\nu(z)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(z^{n})\,d\nu(z)\geq 0\qquad\forall f\in C(\mathbb{T})^{+},

hence the operator
\omega_{n*}
is positive, and setting
f=1
shows that

\|\omega_{n*}\nu\|=(\omega_{n*}\nu)(\mathbb{T})=\nu(\omega_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}))=\nu(\mathbb{T})=\|\nu\|,\qquad\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+}.

For a general measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
, write
\nu=\nu_{+}-\nu_{-}+i\nu_{i}-i\nu_{-i}
for the complex Hahn-Jordan decomposition of
\nu
, so that

\omega_{n*}\nu=\omega_{n*}\nu_{+}-\omega_{n*}\nu_{-}+i\omega_{n*}\nu_{i}-i\omega_{n*}\nu_{-i}
is a decomposition for
\omega_{n*}\nu
and thus, by minimality,
\omega_{n*}(\nu_{+})\geq(\omega_{n*}\nu)_{+}
and so on, hence

\|\nu\|=\|\nu_{+}\|+\|\nu_{-}\|+\|\nu_{i}\|+\|\nu_{-i}\|=\|\omega_{n*}\nu_{+}\|+\|\omega_{n*}\nu_{-}\|+\|\omega_{n*}\nu_{i}\|+\|\omega_{n*}\nu_{-i}\|\geq\|\omega_{n*}\nu\|.

Assume now that
n
is a prime number
p
. Then
\omega_{p*}^{k}=\omega_{p^{k}*}
, and since
\|p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*}\|=p^{-\beta}<1
the well-known Neumann series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{-\beta k}\omega_{p^{k}*}
of positive operators converges in the Banach algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}))
and gives the formula for
A_{\beta,p}^{-1}
in part (3). Taking inverses in part (2), we conclude that

A_{\beta,n}^{-1}=\prod_{p|n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{-\beta k}\omega_{p^{k}*}

is a positive operator. The last assertion follows from normalizing
A_{\beta,p}^{-1}
with the factor
\prod_{p|n}(1-\omega_{p*})
.

In order to prove (4), suppose
m|n
and let
k
be the product of the primes that divide
n
but not
m
.
By part (2)
A_{\beta,n}=A_{k,\beta}A_{\beta,m}
, and hence

A_{\beta,n}^{-1}=A_{\beta,m}^{-1}A_{\beta,k}^{-1}
. Since the operator
A_{\beta,k}^{-1}
is positive,

A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+}=A_{\beta,m}^{-1}(A_{\beta,k}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+})\subseteq A_{\beta,m}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+}
.
∎

Motivated by Lemma
4.5
(2), we extend the notation to include finite subsets
F\Subset\mathcal{P}
and define an operator
A_{\beta,F}
on the space
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
of complex measures on
\mathbb{T}
by

(4.7)

A_{\beta,F}\nu:=\prod_{p\in F}(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})\nu=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\omega_{d*}\nu,\qquad\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}),

where
\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}
is the set of all natural numbers whose prime factors are in
F
. Thus, if
F
is the set of prime divisors of a given
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, then
A_{\beta,F}=A_{\beta,n}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

The following are equivalent for
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
:

(1)

\nu
is
\beta
-subconformal;

(2)

A_{\beta,n}\nu\geq 0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
;

(3)

A_{\beta,F}\nu\geq 0
for all
F\Subset\mathcal{P}
;

(4)

\nu\geq\sum_{\emptyset\neq A\subset F}(-1)^{|A|+1}\prod_{p\in A}(p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})\nu
for all finite
F\Subset\mathcal{P}
;

(5)

the atomic part and the nonatomic part of
\nu
are
\beta
-subconformal.

If in addition
\beta\in(0,\infty)
, then these are also equivalent to:

(6)

\nu\in\bigcap_{n}A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+}
;

(7)

\nu\in\bigcap_{F\Subset\mathcal{P}}\prod_{p\in F}A_{\beta,F}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+}
.

Proof.

The equivalence of properties (1) through (4) is clear from Lemma
4.5
, and so is the equivalence between (6) and (7), using
F=\{p\in\mathcal{P}:p\mid n\}
.
Let
\nu=\nu_{a}+\nu_{c}
be the decomposition of
\nu
into its atomic and nonatomic parts.
Observe that
(A_{\beta,n}\nu)_{a}=A_{\beta,n}(\nu_{a})
and
(A_{\beta,n}\nu)_{c}=A_{\beta,n}(\nu_{c})
because for each
d
, the map
\omega_{d}
is
d
-to-1. Since a measure is positive if and only if its atomic and non-atomic parts are positive, (5) is equivalent to (2). If
\beta\in(0,\infty)
, then since the set of measures satisfying (
4.4
) for a given
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
is
A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})^{+}
, we also see that (6) is equivalent to (1).
∎

Remark 4.9
.

For
\beta\in(1,\infty)
, the series
T_{\beta}=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}c^{-\beta}\omega_{c*}
defines a bounded linear transformation on
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
.
Combining
[
28
, Theorem 8.1]
and Theorem
4.17
, we see that
T_{\beta}
is an affine isomorphism between the simplex of all probability measures on
\mathbb{T}
and (the simplex of)
\beta
-subconformal probability measures on
\mathbb{T}
(in the low-temperature range).

Lemma 4.10
.

For each finite
F\Subset\mathcal{P}
define
e_{F}:=\prod_{p\in F}(1-V_{p}V_{p}^{*})
and let
\alpha_{a}
,
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
be the endomorphisms from Proposition
2.6
.
Then, for
\beta\in(0,\infty)
and
\psi
a
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state,

(1)

e_{F}=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)V_{d}V_{d}^{*}
;

(2)

\alpha_{a}(e_{F})\alpha_{b}(e_{F})=\begin{cases}\alpha_{a}(e_{F})&a=b\\
0&a\neq b\end{cases}\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F};

(3)

\psi(e_{F})={\zeta_{F}(\beta)}^{-1}
;

(4)

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\psi(\alpha_{a}(e_{F}))=1
.

Proof.

Let
n_{F}=\prod_{p\in F}p
; then
e_{F}
is the projection
e_{n_{F}}
of Lemma
3.6
, so part (1) follows from Lemma
3.6
(1). Similarly for part (2), if
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}
, we have
\alpha_{a}(e_{F})=e_{abn_{F},a}
and
\alpha_{b}(e_{F})=e_{abn_{F},b}
, and Lemma
3.6
(2) implies that these projections are mutually orthogonal.

Since
\psi(V_{d}V_{d}^{*})=d^{-\beta}
, (3) follows from (1) and the Möbius inversion formula,

\psi(e_{F})=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{F}(\beta)}.

For (4), use (3) to compute

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\psi(\alpha_{a}(e_{F}))=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}a^{-\beta}\psi(e_{F})=\zeta_{F}(\beta)\frac{1}{\zeta_{F}(\beta)}=1.\qed

Lemma 4.11
.

Suppose
\psi
is a
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state of
(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}),\sigma)
and let
\nu_{\psi}
be the probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
representing the restriction of
\psi
to
C(\mathbb{T})
as in (
4.2
). Then
\nu_{\psi}
is
\beta
-subconformal and

\psi(e_{F}\,V_{1}fV_{1}^{*}\,e_{F})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,dA_{\beta,F}\nu_{\psi}\qquad\forall f\in C(\mathbb{T}),\ F\Subset\mathcal{P}.

Proof.

Suppose
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
F\Subset\mathcal{P}
. Then

(4.12)

e_{F}V_{1}fV_{1}^{*}e_{F}=V_{1}fV_{1}^{*}e_{F}=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)V_{1}fV_{1}^{*}V_{d}V_{d}^{*}=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)V_{d}(f\circ\omega_{d})V_{d}^{*},

where the second equality follows from equation (
3.5
).

Let
\nu_{\psi}
be the probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
representing the restriction of a
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state
\psi
, and assume
f\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}fdA_{\beta,F}\nu_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\Big(\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\omega_{d*}\nu_{\psi}\Big)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f\circ\omega_{d})d\nu_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\psi(V_{1}(f\circ\omega_{d})V_{1}^{*})=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\mu(d)\psi(V_{d}(f\circ\omega_{d})V_{d}^{*})

\displaystyle=\psi\Big(e_{F}V_{1}fV_{1}^{*}\,e_{F}\Big)\geq 0,

where the first three equalities are obvious, the fourth one holds because of the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
condition, and the fifth one holds by (
4.12
).
∎

Next we see that every measure on
\mathbb{T}
gives rise to a linear functional on
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
via (
4.1
),
but only the
\beta
-subconformal ones extend to positive linear functionals on
\mathfrak{D}=\lim\mathfrak{D}_{a}
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Suppose

\nu
is a finite measure on
\mathbb{T}
and let
\beta\in[0,\infty)
. For each
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
there exists a unique linear functional
\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}
on

\mathfrak{D}_{a}:=\operatorname{span}\{V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}:b|a,\ f\in C(\mathbb{T})\}
such that

(4.14)

\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}(V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}):=b^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\nu\qquad b|a,\ f\in C(\mathbb{T}),

and
(\psi_{\beta,\nu,a})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is a coherent family for the inductive system
(\mathfrak{D}_{a},\iota_{a,b})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
.

If in addition
\nu
is
\beta
-subconformal,
then
\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}\geq 0
for every
a
and there is a unique positive linear functional
\lim_{a}\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}
on
\mathfrak{D}=\lim\mathfrak{D}_{a}

extending
\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}
. If
\nu(\mathbb{T})=1
, the gauge-invariant extension of the limit functional is a
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
given by

(4.15)

\psi_{\beta,\nu}(V_{b}fV_{c}^{*}):=(\lim_{a}\psi_{\beta,\nu,a})\circ E(V_{b}fV_{c}^{*})=\delta_{b,c}b^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\nu\qquad b,c\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ f\in C(\mathbb{T})

where
E
is the conditional expectation of Proposition
2.4
.

Proof.

From the proof of Corollary
3.8
we know that
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
is the linear space direct sum of the subspaces
V_{b}C(\mathbb{T})V_{b}^{*}
over the divisors
b
of
a
, and hence (
4.14
) defines a unique linear functional on
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
. The resulting family
(\psi_{\beta,\nu,a})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
of linear functionals is coherent with respect to inclusion because the right hand side does not depend on
a
explicitly.

Suppose now that
\nu
is
\beta
-subconformal and notice,
by setting
n=1
in (
4.4
), that
\nu
is positive, so we may as well assume without loss of generality that
\nu
is a probability measure. We will show next that

\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}
is a state of
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
for each
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
.
The isomorphism
\Gamma_{a}:C(X_{a})\cong\mathfrak{D}_{a}
from Corollary
3.8
establishes a bijection between positive cones. For
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
b|a
, let
f^{b}\in C(X_{a})
be the function
f^{b}(z,d)=\delta_{b,d}f(z)
. Since the positive cone of
C(X_{a})=C(\bigsqcup_{b|a}(\mathbb{T}\times\{b\}))
is the direct sum of positive cones of the

,
the functional
\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}
is positive if and only if

\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}(\Gamma_{a}(f^{b}))\geq 0
for every
b|a
and every

. We verify the latter condition by the following direct computation using Corollary
3.8
:

\displaystyle\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}(\Gamma_{a}(f^{b}))

\displaystyle=\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}\Big(\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)V_{bd}(f\circ\omega_{d})V_{bd}^{*}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)(bd)^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f\circ\omega_{d})d\nu

\displaystyle=b^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\Big(\sum_{d|\frac{a}{b}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\omega_{d*}\nu\Big).

This shows that
\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}
is positive as a linear functional on
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
if and only if condition (
4.4
) holds for all divisors of

. Computing at the identity shows that
\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}
is a state of
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
.
We have thus shown that
\{\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is a coherent system of states for the inductive system

(\mathfrak{D}_{a},\iota_{a,b})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
and this uniquely defines a state
\lim_{a}\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}
on the direct limit
\mathfrak{D}
, which is given by (
4.15
) with

.

Now let
\psi_{\beta,\nu}:=\lim_{a}\psi_{\beta,\nu,a}\circ E
be the gauge-invariant extension induced via the conditional expectation of the gauge action. On the spanning monomials, this extension is given by

(4.16)

\psi_{\beta,\nu}(V_{b}fV_{c}^{*})=\delta_{b,c}b^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\nu,\qquad b,c\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ f\in C(\mathbb{T}),

which obviously satisfies (
4.1
) and is thus a
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state.
∎

We can now prove the first part of Theorem

.

Theorem 4.17

(Theorem
1.6
(1))
.

For each
\beta\in[0,\infty)

\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}

on

\psi

.
The inverse map
\nu\mapsto\psi_{\beta,\nu}

By Lemma
4.11
, the ‘restriction map’
\psi\mapsto\nu_{\psi}
sends
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states to
\beta
-subconformal probability measures. This map is clearly affine, weak* continuous, and also injective because of (

Suppose

and let
\psi_{\beta,\nu}

5.

The main result is Theorem

Proposition 5.1
.

For
\beta\in[0,1]
every
\beta
-subconformal atomic probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
is supported on the roots of unity. Moreover, the only
0
-subconformal atomic probability measure is
\delta_{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\nu
is a finite
\beta
-subconformal measure on
\mathbb{T}
for
\beta\leq 1
and
\nu(\{z\})>0
; we will show that
z
is a root of unity.
For prime
n=p
, the definition of subconformality (
4.4
) reads

\nu\geq p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*}\nu
. In particular, for each
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
,

\nu(\{z^{ap}\})\ \geq\ p^{-\beta}\nu(\omega_{p}^{-1}(\{z^{ap}\}))\ =\ p^{-\beta}\sum_{s:s^{p}=(z^{a})^{p}}\nu(\{s\})\ \geq\ p^{-\beta}\nu(\{z^{a}\}).

Iterating this procedure we see that
\nu(\{z^{ap^{k}}\})\geq p^{-k\beta}\nu(\{z^{a}\})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and, more generally, using the prime factorization
n=\prod_{p|n}p^{e_{p}(n)}
, we conclude that

\nu(\{z^{n}\})\geq n^{-\beta}\nu(\{z\}).

Since
\beta\leq 1
, the series
\sum_{n}n^{-\beta}
diverges. Hence the map
n\mapsto z^{n}
cannot be injective, for otherwise
\nu(\{z^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\})
would be infinite by
\sigma
-additivity. Hence there exist
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
such that
z^{n_{1}}=z^{n_{2}}

and
z
is an
(n_{1}-n_{2})^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity.

Now suppose that
\beta=0
and
z\neq 1
is a
k^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity. Then

\nu(\{1\})\geq\nu(\omega_{k}^{-1}(\{1\}))\geq\nu(\{1\})+\nu(\{z\}),

so that
\nu(\{z\})=0
. Therefore,
\nu=\delta_{1}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
let
Z_{k}
denote the set of
k^{\mathrm{th}}
roots of unity, and
for each measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{T}
denote by

its restriction to

, that is,
\nu|_{k}(A):=\nu(Z_{k}\cap A)
for measurable
A\subset\mathbb{T}
.
If
\nu
is
\beta
-subconformal, then so is
\nu|_{k}
. Moreover,
\nu|_{k}
converges to the atomic part of
\nu
in the weak-* topology as
k\nearrow
in
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
and suppose
z\in Z_{k}
has primitive order
r|k
. For any prime
p
, there are three mutually exclusive and complementary possible cases for the set
\omega_{p}^{-1}(\{z\})\cap Z_{k}
:

(5.3)

\omega_{p}^{-1}(\{z\})\cap Z_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\emptyset&\text{if $p\nmid\frac{k}{r}$ and $p|r$}\\[2.0pt]
\{z^{1/p}\}&\text{if $p\nmid\frac{k}{r}$ and $p\nmid r$}\\[2.0pt]
\omega_{p}^{-1}(\{z\})&\text{if $p|\frac{k}{r}$},\end{array}\right.

in the second case we have written
z^{1/p}
for the unique element of
Z_{k}
satisfying
(z^{1/p})^{p}=z
.
Each square-free integer
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
factors uniquely as
n=n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}
, where
n_{i}
is the product of prime factors corresponding to the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
case of (
5.3
). For
d|n_{i}
, since
n
is square-free, it follows by induction on the number of prime factors of
d
that (
5.3
) remains valid with
d
in place of
p
. Consequently,
A_{\beta,n_{1}}\nu|_{k}(\{z\})=\nu|_{k}(\{z\})
. By Lemma
4.5
(1) there is a (commuting) factorization yielding

A_{\beta,n}\nu|_{k}(\{z\})=A_{\beta,n_{1}}A_{\beta,n_{2}}A_{\beta,n_{3}}\nu|_{k}(\{z\})=A_{\beta,n_{2}}A_{\beta,n_{3}}\nu|_{k}(\{z\}).

For any
d_{2}|n_{2}
, the root
z^{1/d_{2}}
is also a primitive
r^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity, so case (3) implies that
\omega_{d_{3}}^{-1}(\{z^{1/d_{2}}\})\cap Z_{k}=\omega_{d_{3}}^{-1}(\{z^{1/d_{2}}\})
for any
d_{3}|n_{3}
. Hence:

\displaystyle A_{\beta,n_{2}}A_{\beta,n_{3}}\nu|_{k}(\{z\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d|n_{2}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}A_{\beta,n_{3}}\nu|_{k}(\{z^{1/d}\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d|n_{2}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}A_{\beta,n_{3}}\nu(\{z^{1/d}\})

\displaystyle=A_{\beta,n_{2}}A_{\beta,n_{3}}\nu(\{z\})-\sum_{1\neq d|n_{2}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}A_{\beta,n_{3}}\nu(\omega_{d}^{-1}(\{z\})\backslash\{z^{1/d}\}).

Since
\nu
is
\beta
-subconformal, Proposition
4.8
(2) implies that the first term is positive; we will argue by induction that

(5.4)

-\sum_{1\neq d|n_{2}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}A_{\beta,m}\nu(\omega_{d}^{-1}(\{z\})\backslash\{z^{1/d}\})\geq 0

when
m
is relatively prime to
n_{2}
, from which it follows that the second term is also positive.

If
p
is a prime dividing
n_{2}
, then we can write

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{1\neq d|n_{2}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}A_{\beta,m}\nu(\omega_{d}^{-1}(z)\backslash\{z^{1/d}\})

\displaystyle=p^{-\beta}A_{\beta,m}\nu(\omega_{p}^{-1}(z)\backslash\{z^{1/p}\})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum_{1\neq d|\frac{n_{2}}{p}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\left[A_{\beta,m}\nu(\omega_{d}^{-1}(z)\backslash\{z^{1/d}\})-p^{-\beta}A_{\beta,m}\nu(\omega_{pd}^{-1}(z)\backslash\{z^{1/pd}\})\right]

\displaystyle=p^{-\beta}A_{\beta,m}\nu(\omega_{p}^{-1}(z)\backslash\{z^{1/p}\})-\sum_{1\neq d|\frac{n_{2}}{p}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}A_{\beta,p}A_{\beta,m}\nu(\omega_{d}^{-1}(z)\backslash\{z^{1/d}\}).

The first term is positive since
\nu
is
\beta
-subconformal. Since
p
is relatively prime to
m
, Lemma
4.5
(1) says that
A_{\beta,p}A_{\beta,m}=A_{\beta,pm}
, so the second term is (
5.4
) with
\frac{n_{2}}{p}
and
pm
in place of
n_{2}
and
m
. Positivity then follows by induction on the number of prime factors of
n_{2}
.

The final claim about the weak* limit is immediate because the atomic part of
\nu
is supported on
\bigcup_{k}Z_{k}
by Proposition
5.1
.
∎

As usual, we will write
\mathrm{ord}(z)
for the order of
z
in the group
\mathbb{T}
. That is,
\mathrm{ord}(z)
is the primitive order if
z
is a root of unity, and
\mathrm{ord}(z)=\infty
if
z
is not a root of unity.
Recall the Euler totient function
\varphi
and its generalization
\varphi_{\beta}
defined in Section
1
by
\varphi_{\beta}(n):=n^{\beta}\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})
, where the product is over the primes that divide
n
.

Lemma 5.5
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
let
\varepsilon_{n}
be the atomic probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
defined by

\varepsilon_{n}(\{z\})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}&\text{ if }\mathrm{ord}(z)=n,\\
0&\text{ otherwise;}\end{cases}

so that
\varepsilon_{n}
is evenly supported on the set
Z_{n}^{*}
of primitive roots of unity of order
n
.
Define a measure

(5.6)

\nu_{\beta,n}:=\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\varepsilon_{n}=\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})^{-1}\varepsilon_{n}

for each
\beta\in(0,\infty)
. Then
\nu_{\beta,n}
is a
\beta
-subconformal atomic probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
supported on
Z_{n}
such that

(5.7)

\nu_{\beta,n}(\{z\})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n^{-\beta}\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(\mathrm{ord}(z))}{\varphi(\mathrm{ord}(z))},&\text{if }\mathrm{ord}(z)|n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
and write
m=ab
in such a way that
(b,n)=1
and all prime factors of
a
divide
n
. Then
A_{\beta,b}
commutes with
A_{\beta,n}^{-1}=\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})^{-1}
and

A_{\beta,b}\varepsilon_{n}=\prod_{q|b}(1-q^{-\beta})\varepsilon_{n}
because
\omega_{q*}\varepsilon_{n}=\varepsilon_{n}
whenever
\gcd(n,q)=1
. Hence

\displaystyle A_{\beta,m}\nu_{\beta,n}

\displaystyle=A_{\beta,a}A_{\beta,b}\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})^{-1}\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,a}\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})^{-1}A_{\beta,b}\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})\prod_{q\mid b}(1-q^{-\beta})\prod_{p|n,p\nmid a}(1-p^{-\beta}\omega_{p*})^{-1}\varepsilon_{n}.

Since the last expression is
\geq 0
by Lemma
4.5
(3), we conclude that
\nu_{\beta,n}
is
\beta
-subconformal. By Lemma
4.5
(3)
\nu_{\beta,n}
is a probability measure.

Before proving (
5.7
), we point out that

(5.8)

\omega_{k*}\varepsilon_{n}=\varepsilon_{\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}.

This is computed directly:

\displaystyle\omega_{k*}\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=\omega_{k*}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T},\mathrm{ord}(z)=n}\delta_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T},\mathrm{ord}(z)=n}\delta_{z^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi\left(\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}\right)}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{T},\mathrm{ord}(z)=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}\delta_{z}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}},

since the set of
z\in Z_{n}^{*}
with
z^{k}=w
contains
\varphi(n)\varphi\left(\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}\right)^{-1}
elements for each
w\in Z_{\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}^{*}
.

Now recall from Lemma
4.5
(3) that the operator
A_{\beta,p}^{-1}
can be expressed as

A_{\beta,p}^{-1}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}p^{-\beta m}\omega_{p^{m}*}.

Letting
e=e_{p}(n)
be the largest integer such that
p^{e}|n
, we have by (
5.8
):

(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,p}^{-1}\varepsilon_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{e-1}(1-p^{-\beta})p^{-\beta m}\varepsilon_{n/p^{m}}+p^{-\beta e}\varepsilon_{n/p^{e}}.

Applying this to each prime divisor of
n
gives the formula

\nu_{\beta,n}=\sum_{d|n}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{-\beta}\left(\prod_{p|d}1-p^{-\beta}\right)\ \varepsilon_{d}=\sum_{d|n}n^{-\beta}\varphi_{\beta}(d)\varepsilon_{d},

which proves (
5.7
).
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

For
\beta\in(0,1]
each atomic
\beta
-subconformal probability measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
can be written uniquely as a (possibly infinite) convex linear combination
\nu=\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}\nu_{\beta,n}
with coefficients

\lambda_{n}=n^{\beta}\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\mu(d)\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(nd)}\nu(Z_{nd}^{*})\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}.

In particular,
\{\nu_{\beta,n}:n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\}
are the extremal atomic
\beta
-subconformal probability measures.

In order to prove the theorem we need to establish a few properties of the measures
\nu_{\beta,n}
first.

Lemma 5.10
.

For
\beta\in(0,\infty)
and every
n,k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
,

\omega_{k*}\nu_{\beta,n}=\nu_{\beta,\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}
.

Proof.

It is clear from its definition that
A_{\beta,n}^{-1}
commutes with
\omega_{k*}
, so that
\omega_{k*}\nu_{\beta,n}=A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\omega_{k*}\varepsilon_{n}=A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\varepsilon_{\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}
by (
5.6
) and (
5.8
).
Using Lemma
4.5
(2), we have

\displaystyle\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\varepsilon_{\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}^{-1}\prod_{p|n,p\nmid\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}A_{\beta,p}^{-1}\varepsilon_{\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p|\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}^{-1}\varepsilon_{\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}

\displaystyle=\nu_{\beta,\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}}.\qed

The following is a simple consequence of Dirichlet’s density theorem.

Lemma 5.11
.

Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, that
F
is a finite subset of
\mathcal{P}
containing all the prime factors of
k
, and that
\beta\in(0,1]
. If
\chi
is a nontrivial Dirichlet character modulo
k
, then

\prod_{q\in A}\frac{1-q^{-\beta}}{1-\chi(q)q^{-\beta}}\underset{A\nearrow\mathcal{P}\setminus F}{\longrightarrow}0,

where the limit is taken over finite sets of primes disjoint from
F
.

Proof.

For every
q\in\mathcal{P}
, one has
1-q^{-\beta}<|1-\chi(q)q^{-\beta}|
. If
\Re(\chi(q))<0
, then
1<|1-\chi(q)q^{-\beta}|
, which implies that
1-q^{-\beta}>\left|\frac{1-q^{-\beta}}{1-\chi(q)q^{-\beta}}\right|
. It follows that

\prod_{q\in A}\left|\frac{1-q^{-\beta}}{1-\chi(q)q^{-\beta}}\right|<\prod_{q\in A,\Re(\chi(q))<0}1-q^{-\beta}.

Taking the logarithm of the right hand side, we have

-\log\left(\prod_{q\in A,\Re(\chi(q))<0}1-q^{-\beta}\right)=\sum_{q\in A,\Re(\chi(q))<0}-\log(1-q^{-\beta})>\sum_{q\in A,\Re(\chi(q))<0}q^{-\beta}.

The last series diverges for
\beta\in(0,1]
as
A\nearrow\mathcal{P}\setminus F
by Dirichlet’s density theorem
[
52
, Chapter IV, Section 4, Theorem 2]
, whence the result follows.
∎

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1]
and fix
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
. For each finite set of primes
L
define an operator

P_{\beta,L}:=\prod_{q\in L}(1-q^{-\beta})A_{\beta,q}^{-1}:\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})\to\mathcal{M}(Z_{k}).

Then
P_{\beta,L}
converges as
L\nearrow\mathcal{P}
, and for each
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})
, the limit
\lim_{L}P_{\beta,L}\eta
is in
\operatorname{span}\{\nu_{\beta,d}:d\mid k\}
. Moreover,
if
\eta
is a probability measure, then

(5.13)

\lim_{L\nearrow\mathcal{P}}\prod_{q\in L}(1-q^{-\beta})A_{\beta,q}^{-1}\eta=\sum_{d\mid k}\lambda_{d}\nu_{\beta,d},

with
\lambda_{d}\geq 0
and
\sum_{d\mid k}\lambda_{d}=1
.
The limit is unchanged if one leaves out of the product an arbitrary finite subset of primes that do not divide
k
.

Proof.

The set
Z_{k}
of
k^{\mathrm{th}}
roots of unity can be decomposed according to primitive order as a disjoint union
Z_{k}=\bigsqcup_{d\mid k}Z_{d}^{*}
, and this gives a direct sum decomposition
\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})=\bigoplus_{d\mid k}\mathcal{M}(Z_{d}^{*})
of measure spaces
(since
Z_{k}
is finite we view measures as represented by their density functions). Let
d
be a divisor of
k
, and for each character
\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}}
consider
the vector
\tilde{\chi}\in\mathcal{M}(Z_{d}^{*})
obtained from
\chi
through the identification
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}\cong Z_{d}^{*}
that sends the invertible element
u\in(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}
to the primitive
d^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity
\exp(2\pi iu/d)=(\xi_{d})^{u}
; specifically,

\tilde{\chi}((\xi_{d})^{u})=\chi(u),\qquad\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}}.

Then
\{\tilde{\chi}:\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}}\}
is a linear basis of
\mathcal{M}(Z_{d}^{*})\cong\mathbb{C}^{\varphi(d)}
.
Suppose now that
q
is a prime number that does not divide
k
and let
\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}}
. Since

(\omega_{q^{m}*}\tilde{\chi})(\xi_{d}^{u})=\tilde{\chi}(\xi_{d}^{uq^{m}})=\chi(uq^{m})=\chi(q)^{m}\chi(u)=\chi(q)^{m}\tilde{\chi}(\xi_{d}^{u})

for every
m\geq 0
, Lemma
4.5
(3) shows that
\tilde{\chi}
is an eigenvector of
A_{\beta,q}^{-1}
,

A_{\beta,q}^{-1}\tilde{\chi}=\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{-\beta m}(\omega_{q^{m}*}\tilde{\chi})=\sum_{m\geq 0}(q^{-\beta})^{m}\chi(q)^{m}\tilde{\chi}=(1-\chi(q)q^{-\beta})^{-1}\tilde{\chi}.

Each
\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}}
can be extended to a Dirichlet character modulo
d
, also denoted by
\chi
and given by

\chi(u)=\begin{cases}\tilde{\chi}(\xi_{d}^{u})&\text{ if }\gcd(u,d)=1\\
0&\text{ if }\gcd(u,d)\neq 1\end{cases}\qquad u\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let
F
be a fixed finite subset of primes not dividing
k
and denote by
F\vee k
the union of
F
and the set of prime divisors of
k
. Suppose
\beta\in(0,1]
. Then Lemma
5.11
gives the following limit as
L\nearrow\mathcal{P}
, with
1_{d}
the trivial character in
\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}}
,

(5.14)

\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in L\\
q\notin F\vee k\end{subarray}}(1-q^{-\beta})A_{\beta,q}^{-1}\Big)\ \tilde{\chi}=\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in L\\
q\notin F\vee k\end{subarray}}\frac{1-q^{-\beta}}{1-\chi(q)q^{-\beta}}\Big)\ \tilde{\chi}\ \underset{L\nearrow\mathcal{P}}{\longrightarrow}\ \begin{cases}\tilde{\chi}&\text{ if }\chi=1_{d}\\
0&\text{ if }\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}}\setminus\{1_{d}\}.\end{cases}

Suppose now
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})
and
combine all the bases of the
\mathcal{M}(Z_{d}^{*})
into a basis of
\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})
, so
\eta
can be written uniquely as

\eta=\sum_{d\mid k}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}}}a_{d,\chi}\tilde{\chi}
.
Notice that the measure
\varepsilon_{d}
defined in Lemma
5.5
is just

\varepsilon_{d}=\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\tilde{1}_{d}
.
Then

(5.15)

\lim_{L\nearrow\mathcal{P}}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in L\\
q\notin F\vee k\end{subarray}}(1-q^{-\beta})A_{\beta,q}^{-1}\Big)\ \eta=\sum_{d\mid k}a_{d,1_{d}}\varphi(d)\varepsilon_{d},

because the contribution of the nontrivial characters vanishes in the limit by (
5.14
). By Lemma
4.5
(3) the measure above is positive and thus
\lambda_{d}:=a_{d,1_{d}}\varphi(d)\geq 0
because the
\varepsilon_{d}
have disjoint support.

To finish the proof simply apply the linear operator
\prod_{p|k}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,p}^{-1}
to both sides of (
5.15
), using continuity on the left and the definition of
\nu_{\beta,d}
on the right.
∎

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1]
.
A probability measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}
is
\beta
-subconformal if and only if

\nu=\lim_{L\nearrow\mathcal{P}}\prod_{q\in L}(1-q^{-\beta})A_{\beta,q}^{-1}\eta

for some probability
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}
.

Proof.

Let
P_{\beta}
denote the linear operator on
\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})
defined by
P_{\beta}\eta=\lim_{L\nearrow\mathcal{P}}\prod_{p\in L}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,p}^{-1}\eta
. It suffices to show that

P_{\beta}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}

because the right hand side is the set of
\beta
-subconformal measures on
Z_{k}
by Proposition
4.8
(6).

Let
P_{\beta,n}:=\prod_{p|n}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,p}^{-1}|_{\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})}
.
That
P_{\beta}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}\subseteq\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}
follows from Proposition
5.12
because for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}

P_{\beta}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}=P_{\beta,n}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P},\ p\nmid n}(1-p^{-\beta})A_{\beta,p}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}\subseteq A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}.

It remains to show that
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}\subseteq P_{\beta}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}
. Note that

\|P_{n,\beta}\nu\|=(P_{n,\beta}\nu)(X)=\nu(X)=\|\nu\|\qquad\nu\in\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}.

This shows that
\|P_{\beta,n}^{-1}y\|=\|y\|
for every
y\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}A_{\beta,n}^{-1}\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})^{+}
; since
\mathcal{M}(Z_{k})
is finite-dimensional, the net
P_{\beta,n}^{-1}y
has a subnet
(P_{\beta,n_{j}}^{-1}y)
converging to
x
. For
\varepsilon>0
, choose some
K
such that
\|P_{\beta,n_{j}}-P_{\beta}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|y\|}
and
\|P_{\beta,n_{j}}^{-1}y-x\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|P_{\beta}\|}
for all
j>K
. Hence

\displaystyle\|y-P_{\beta}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|y-P_{\beta}P_{\beta,n_{j}}^{-1}y\|+\|P_{\beta}P_{\beta,n_{j}}^{-1}y-P_{\beta}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\beta,n_{j}}-P_{\beta}\|\cdot\|P_{\beta,n_{j}}^{-1}y\|+\|P_{\beta}\|\cdot\|P_{\beta,n_{j}}^{-1}y-x\|<\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, it follows that
P_{\beta}x=y
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.9
.

Let
\nu
be an arbitrary
\beta
-subconformal atomic probability measure and fix
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
. By Lemma
5.2
, the restriction
\nu|_{k}:=\nu(\cdot\cap Z_{k})
is
\beta
-subconformal, and hence decomposes uniquely as
\sum_{n|k}\lambda_{k,n}\nu_{\beta,n},
with
\lambda_{k,n}\geq 0
and
\sum_{n\mid k}\lambda_{k,n}=\nu(Z_{k})
, by Proposition
5.12
and Lemma
5.16
.
For each
n|k

\nu(Z_{n}^{*})=\sum_{d|\frac{k}{n}}\lambda_{k,nd}\nu_{\beta,nd}(Z_{n}^{*})=\sum_{d|\frac{k}{n}}\lambda_{k,nd}(nd)^{-\beta}\varphi_{\beta}(n)=\sum_{d|\frac{k}{n}}\lambda_{k,\left(\frac{nd}{k}\right)k}\left(\frac{nd}{k}\right)^{-\beta}k^{-\beta}\varphi_{\beta}(n).

Reindexing the sum using the permutation
d\mapsto\frac{k}{nd}
of divisors of
\frac{k}{n}
yields

\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(n)}\nu(Z_{n}^{*})=\sum_{d|\frac{k}{n}}\lambda_{k,k/d}\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)^{-\beta}.

This relates the function
n\mapsto\frac{1}{\varphi(k/n)}\nu(Z_{k/n}^{*})
to a summation of the function
d\mapsto\lambda_{k,k/d}\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)^{-\beta}
over divisors of
n
. The Möbius inversion formula then implies that

(5.17)

\lambda_{k,n}=n^{\beta}\sum_{d|\frac{k}{n}}\mu(d)\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(nd)}\nu(Z_{nd}^{*}).

As
k
increases in the directed set
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
this gives rise to an absolutely convergent series
because

\sum_{d|\frac{k}{n}}\left|\mu(d)\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(nd)}\nu(Z_{nd}^{*})\right|\leq\nu(\bigsqcup_{d|\frac{k}{n}}Z_{nd}^{*})\leq 1
. Thus we may define

\lambda_{n}:=n^{\beta}\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\mu(d)\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(nd)}\nu(Z_{nd}^{*}).

It only remains to verify that
\nu=\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}\nu_{\beta,n}
. If
z
is a primitive
k^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity, then

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\lambda_{n}\nu_{\beta,n}\right)(\{z\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\\
k|n\end{subarray}}\left(n^{\beta}\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\mu(d)\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(nd)}\nu(Z_{nd}^{*})\right)\left(n^{-\beta}\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(k)}{\varphi(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(k)}{\varphi(k)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\\
k|n\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(nd)}\nu(Z_{nd}^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(k)}{\varphi(k)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\\
k|m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(m)}\nu(Z_{m}^{*})\sum_{d|\frac{m}{k}}\mu(d)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(k)}{\varphi(k)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\\
k|m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi_{\beta}(m)}\nu(Z_{m}^{*})\delta_{m,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi(k)}\nu(Z_{k}^{*})

\displaystyle=\nu(\{z\}).

The fourth equality holds because the Möbius function satisfies
\sum_{d|\frac{m}{k}}\mu(d)=\delta_{m,k}
and the last one holds because the value
\nu(\{z\})=\nu|_{k}(\{z\})
depends only on the order of
z
and
|Z_{k}^{*}|=\varphi(k)
.
∎

Remark 5.18
.

If we write
Z_{k}^{*}=\bigcap_{p|k}Z_{k}\backslash Z_{k/a}
and use the inclusion-exclusion principle, we get

\nu(Z_{k}^{*})=\prod_{p|k}\Big(\omega_{k*}-\omega_{k/p*}\Big)\nu(\{1\})=\sum_{a|k}\mu(a)\omega_{k/a*}\nu(\{1\}).

Which gives

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=n^{\beta}\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\sum_{a|nd}\frac{\mu(d)\mu(a)}{\varphi_{\beta}(nd)}\omega_{nd/a*}\nu(\{1\})

\displaystyle=n^{\beta}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\frac{\mu(n^{\prime}a)\mu(m^{\prime}a)}{\varphi_{\beta}((n\vee m)a)}\right)\omega_{m*}\nu(\{1\}),

as an alternative expression for
\lambda_{n}
in terms of the wrap-around maps
\omega_{d*}
applied to
\nu
, where
n^{\prime}m=m^{\prime}n=n\vee m
.

6.
Asymptotic estimates for partial sums

Here we prove an asymptotic estimate for partial summation over
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
using a partial order based on prime factorization. The multiplicative partial order plays an important role in the structure of the Toeplitz algebra and its KMS states, as shown by the subconformal condition. Our motivation is the application of Proposition
6.1
in proving a multiplicative version of Wiener’s lemma (cf. Proposition
7.3
), but since its statement and proof rely solely on classical results from analytic number theory, we gather them in a separate section.

For each
n\geq 1
, let
p_{n}
be the
n^{th}
prime number and let
\mathcal{P}_{n}=\{2,3,5,\ldots p_{n}\}
be the set consisting of the first
n
primes. We denote by
\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}
the submonoid of
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
generated by
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, that is,
\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}
consists of all natural numbers with no prime factors greater than
p_{n}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence of non-negative real numbers such that

(6.2)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{a_{m}}{m}=0.

Then

(6.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}(1-p^{-1})\Big)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{a_{m}}{m}=0.

For the proof we need to gather a few tools from analytic number theory. As usual,
when
\lim_{n\to\infty}{f(n)}/{g(n)}=1
, we say that
f
and
g
are
asymptotically equal
and we write
f(n)\sim g(n)
.

Mertens’ Third Theorem states that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\log(n)\prod_{p\leq n}(1-p^{-1})=e^{-\gamma},

where
\gamma=0.57721566...
is Euler’s constant. If we replace first
n
by
p_{n}
in the formula above, and then use the prime number theorem
p_{n}\sim n\log(n)
to change the factor
\log p_{n}
back to
\log n\sim\log(n\log n)
, we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\log(n)\prod_{p\leq p_{n}}(1-p^{-1})=e^{-\gamma}.

If we now take inverses and use the Euler product formula for the monoid
\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}
, we see that

(6.4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{1}{m}=e^{\gamma}.

Abel’s summation formula states that
if
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{C}
and
A(x):=\sum_{1\leq m\leq x}a_{m}
for each
x\geq 1
, then

(6.5)

\sum_{1\leq m\leq x}a_{m}f(m)=-\int_{1}^{x}A(t)f^{\prime}(t)dt+A(x)f(x)

for every continuously differentiable function
f
on
[1,\infty)
.

For real
x>0
and
y\geq 2
let
\Psi(x,y)
be the number of positive integers less than
x
that have no prime divisors greater than
y
; this
\Psi
is often called the
de Bruijn function
. Improving on earlier work of
[
7
,
22
,
11
,
50
]
, de Bruijn showed that the asymptotic estimate

\frac{\Psi(x^{u},x)}{x^{u}}\sim\rho(u)

is uniform for
1\leq u\leq(\log x)^{3/8-\varepsilon}
, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, see
[
8
]
and also
[
26
]
and the references thereof.
Here
\rho(u)
denotes the Dickman function, usually defined as the continuous solution to the delay differential equation

u\rho^{\prime}(u)+\rho(u-1)=0

with initial conditions
\rho(u)=1
for
0\leq u\leq 1
. In addition, de Bruijn further showed in
[
9
]
that the Dickman function has total mass
e^{\gamma}
, that is,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(u)du=e^{\gamma};

we refer to
[
41
, Theorem 3.5.1]
for the details.
To make the uniform approximation precise, we borrow the statement of Hildebrand’s improvement of de Bruijn’s result, with
x^{u}
in place of
x
.

Proposition 6.6
.

[
26
, Theorem 1]

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then the estimate

\frac{\Psi(x^{u},x)}{x^{u}}-\rho(u)=O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\rho(u)\log(u+1)}{\log x}\right)

holds uniformly in the range
x\geq 3
and
1\leq u\leq\log x/(\log\log x)^{5/3+\varepsilon}
.

Next define a function
\delta:[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\delta(u):=\limsup_{x}\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(x^{s},x)}{x^{s}}ds=\limsup_{x}\frac{1}{\log(x)}\int_{x^{u}}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(t,x)}{t^{2}}dt.

We will require the following properties of
\delta(u)
.

Lemma 6.7
.

The function
\delta
is differentiable with
\delta^{\prime}(u)=-\rho(u)
and
\delta(1)=e^{\gamma}-1
. Moreover,
\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}\delta(u)=0
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Proposition
6.6
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\left|\frac{\Psi(x^{s},x)}{x^{s}}-\rho(s)\right|\leq C_{\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{\log(s+1)}{\log(x)}

for
1\leq s\leq\log(x)/(\log\log(x))^{5/3+\varepsilon}
(we may drop the factor
\rho(u)\leq 1
from the r.h.s.). Then

\int_{u}^{u+h}\left|\frac{\Psi(x^{s},x)}{x^{s}}-\rho(s)\right|ds\leq\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{\log(x)}\int_{u}^{u+h}\log(s+1)ds

for
h>0
and sufficiently large
x
. The right hand side converges to
0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
, hence

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\int_{u}^{u+h}\frac{\Psi(x^{s},x)}{x^{s}}ds=\int_{u}^{u+h}\rho(s)ds.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\delta(u)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{x}\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(x^{s},x)}{x^{s}}ds

\displaystyle=\limsup_{x}\int_{u+h}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(x^{s},x)}{x^{s}}ds+\int_{u}^{u+h}\rho(s)ds

\displaystyle=\delta(u+h)+\int_{u}^{u+h}\rho(s)ds,

which implies
\delta^{\prime}(u)=-\rho(u)
.

In order to see that
\delta(1)=e^{\gamma}-1
, fix
x>0
and let
n=\pi(x)
, so that
\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}
is the set of positive integers with no prime factors larger than
x
. Consider the sequence
b_{m}=1
if
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}
and
0
if
m\notin\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}
. Then
B(y)=\sum_{1\leq m\leq y}b_{m}=\Psi(y,x)
and Abel’s summation formula (
6.5
) with
f(x)=1/x
gives

\sum_{1\leq m\leq y}\frac{b_{m}}{m}=\int_{1}^{y}\frac{\Psi(t,x)}{t^{2}}dt+\frac{\Psi(y,x)}{y}.

Taking limits as
y\rightarrow\infty
we see that

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{1}{m}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(t,x)}{t^{2}}dt,

which gives

\frac{1}{\log(x)}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(t,x)}{t^{2}}dt=\frac{1}{\log(x)}\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{1}{m}-\sum_{1\leq m\leq x}\frac{1}{m}-1\Big).

The right-hand side converges to
e^{\gamma}-1
as
x\rightarrow\infty
because of (
6.4
) and the asymptotic formula for the harmonic numbers
H_{n}:=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}\approx\log(n)+\gamma
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Assume that
0\leq a_{m}\leq 1
for every
m
. Then, for fixed
u\geq 1
,

(6.8)

\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{a_{m}}{m}\leq\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{1\leq m\leq p_{n}^{u}}\frac{a_{m}}{m}+\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}\\
m>p_{n}^{u}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}\qquad(n\geq 1).

The first summand on the right of (
6.8
) converges to 0 as
n\rightarrow\infty
by assumption (
6.2
), since
\log p_{n}^{u}\sim u\log n
. For the second summand, Abel’s summation formula gives

\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}\\
m>p_{n}^{u}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}=\frac{1}{\log(n)}\int_{p_{n}^{u}}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(t,p_{n})}{t^{2}}dt-\frac{1}{\log(n)}\frac{\Psi(p_{n}^{u},p_{n})}{p_{n}^{u}}.

The first term is bounded above by
\delta(u)+\varepsilon
for each
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
n
, while the second term converges to
0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, we have the following bound for each
u\geq 1
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{a_{m}}{m}\leq\delta(u).

Since
\inf\delta(u)=0
, it follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{a_{m}}{m}=0.

Finally, by (
6.4
) we may put
\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\left(1-p^{-1}\right)
in place of
\frac{1}{\log(n)}
above (the factor
e^{\gamma}
is irrelevant), which yields (
6.3
), as required.
∎

Recall that a subset
J\subseteq\mathbb{N}
has natural density 0 if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\#\{j\in J:j\leq n\}/n=0
.
Next we see that if a subset
J\subseteq\mathbb{N}
has natural density 0, then it has
multiplicative density 0
.

Corollary 6.9
.

If
J\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\times}
is a set of natural density
0
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}(1-p^{-1})\Big)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}\cap J}\frac{1}{m}=0.

Proof.

Let
b_{n}=1
if
n\in J
and
b_{n}=0
if
n\notin J
, and define
B(x):=\sum_{1\leq m\leq x}b_{m}
.
Abel’s summation formula (
6.5
) gives

\sum_{1\leq m\leq x}\frac{b_{m}}{m}=\int_{1}^{x}\frac{B(t)}{t^{2}}dt+\frac{B(x)}{x}.

Since
J
has natural density 0, the function
{B(t)}/{t}
converges to
0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, so for each
\varepsilon>0
we may choose
T\geq 1
such that
{B(t)}/{t}<\varepsilon
for all
t>T
. Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log(x)}\left(\int_{1}^{x}\frac{B(t)}{t^{2}}dt+\frac{B(x)}{x}\right)<\frac{1}{\log(x)}\left(\int_{1}^{T}\frac{B(t)}{t^{2}}dt+\frac{B(x)}{x}\right)+\varepsilon\left(1-\frac{\log T}{\log x}\right).

The first term on the right converges to 0 as
x\rightarrow\infty
, while the second term converges to
\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log(n)}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{b_{m}}{m}=0,

and the result follows from Proposition
6.1
.
∎

7.
Uniqueness of nonatomic subconformal measure

We now turn our efforts to showing that Theorem
1.1
gives a complete list of extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states for each
\beta\in(0,1]
. After Theorem
5.9
, all that remains to show is that Haar measure is the only nonatomic
\beta
-subconformal measure on
\mathbb{T}
. Our argument is inspired by
[
45
]
for the Bost-Connes system: we show that a certain dilation of a given nonatomic
\beta
-subconformal measure is ergodic and, hence, that there is a unique such measure.

For each
B\subseteq\mathcal{P}
the subset
\{V_{a}fV_{a}^{*}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B},f\in C(\mathbb{T})\}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is self-adjoint and (
3.5
) shows it is closed under multiplication. Hence

\mathfrak{D}_{B}:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_{a}fV_{a}^{*}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B},f\in C(\mathbb{T})\}=\varinjlim(\mathfrak{D}_{a},\iota_{a,b})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}

is a unital C*-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{D}
. The inclusion
\iota_{B}:\mathfrak{D}_{B}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{D}
induces a surjective continuous map of spaces
\iota_{B}^{*}:X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}\rightarrow X^{B}:=\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}_{B}
and also a continuous linear map taking a measure
\tau
on
X
to the measure
\iota^{*}_{B}(\tau)
on
X^{B}
.

Now let
\nu
be a
\beta
-subconformal probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
and
\psi_{\beta,\nu}
the corresponding
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state from Theorem
4.17
. For any subset
B\subseteq\mathcal{P}
, the restriction
\psi_{\beta,\nu}|_{\mathfrak{D}_{B}}
is a state on
\mathfrak{D}_{B}
that, according to the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem, is represented by integration against a measure which we call
\nu_{\beta,B}
. Thus,
\nu_{\beta,\mathcal{P}}
is the measure on
X=X^{\mathcal{P}}
corresponding to
\psi_{\nu,\beta}
, and, at the other extreme, with
B=\emptyset
,
\nu_{\beta,\emptyset}=\nu
is the measure on
X_{\emptyset}=\operatorname{Spec}V_{1}C(\mathbb{T})V_{1}^{*}\cong\mathbb{T}
that already appeared in (
4.2
). These measures satisfy
\iota_{B}^{*}(\nu_{\beta,\mathcal{P}})=\nu_{\beta,B}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose
\nu
is a
\beta
-subconformal probability on
\mathbb{T}
and
B\Subset\mathcal{P}
a finite subset of primes.
Then there is a representation
\rho_{B}

of
\mathfrak{D}_{B}
on
\mathcal{H}_{B}:=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}L^{2}(\mathbb{T},n^{-\beta}A_{\beta,B}\nu)

such that for each
V_{a}fV_{a}^{*}\in\mathfrak{D}_{B}
and each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}
,

\rho_{B}(V_{a}fV_{a}^{*})g_{n}=\begin{cases}(f\circ\omega_{n/a})g_{n}&\text{if $a|n$},\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad\ g_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},n^{-\beta}A_{\beta,B}\nu).

The vector
\Omega_{B}:=(1_{\mathbb{T}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}
is cyclic for
\rho_{B}
and
(\mathcal{H}_{B},\rho_{B},\Omega_{B})
is canonically unitarily equivalent to the GNS representation of the restriction to
\mathfrak{D}_{B}
of the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state
{\psi}_{\beta,\nu}
from Lemma
4.13
.

Consequently, if
\nu_{\beta,B}
denotes the measure on
X_{B}
representing
\psi_{\nu,\beta}|_{\mathfrak{D}_{B}}
and
(L^{2}(X^{B},\nu_{\beta,B}),\lambda_{B},\Omega_{0})
its GNS triple, there is a (unique) unitary intertwiner
T:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(X^{B},\nu_{\beta,B})
for the representations
\rho_{B}
and
\lambda_{B}
that satisfies
T\Omega=\Omega_{0}
.

Proof.

The operators
\rho_{B}(V_{d}fV_{d}^{*})
satisfy the product formula (
3.5
) so that
\rho_{B}
defines a representation of
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
for each
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}
, cf. Corollary
3.8
. These representations are coherent for the inductive system
(\mathfrak{D}_{a},\iota_{a,b})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}
, and therefore determine a representation of the limit
\mathfrak{D}_{B}
. The vector
\Omega
is cyclic because

\rho_{B}(\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}\mu(d)V_{kd}f\circ\omega_{d}V_{kd}^{*})\Omega=(\delta_{n,k}f),\qquad f\in C(\mathbb{T})

and these vectors span a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{B}
. Lastly, the vector state of
\Omega
yields

\displaystyle\left<\rho_{B}(V_{a}fV_{a}^{*})\Omega,\Omega\right>

\displaystyle=a^{-\beta}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}n^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\circ\omega_{n}d(A_{\beta,B}\nu)

\displaystyle=a^{-\beta}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}n^{-\beta}\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}\mu(d)d^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\circ\omega_{nd}d\nu

\displaystyle=a^{-\beta}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}n^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\circ\omega_{n}d\nu\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)

\displaystyle=a^{-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\nu=\int_{X_{B}}(V_{a}fV_{a}^{*})d\nu_{\beta,B}.\qed

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose
B\subseteq\mathcal{P}
(not necessarily finite) and consider
(L^{2}(X^{B},\nu_{\beta,B}),\lambda_{B},\Omega_{0})
, the usual GNS representation of
\mathfrak{D}_{B}
for the state corresponding to
\nu_{\beta,B}
. Then for
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, the map

S_{a}:L^{2}(X^{B},\nu_{\beta,B})\rightarrow L^{2}(X^{B},\nu_{\beta,B}),\qquad\lambda_{B}(x)\Omega_{0}\mapsto\lambda_{B}(V_{a}^{*}xV_{a})\Omega_{0}

defines a bounded operator and the map
a\mapsto S_{a}
is multiplicative. For finite
B\Subset\mathcal{P}
and
T:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow L^{2}(X^{B},\nu_{\beta,B})
the unitary intertwiner of Lemma
7.1
, the operator
T^{*}S_{a}T
can be described explicitly by the formulae

T^{*}S_{a}T(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(g_{an})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}&\text{if $a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}$}\\[2.0pt]
(g_{n}\circ\omega_{a})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}&\text{if $a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}\backslash B}$}\end{array}\right.

and by prime factorization for general
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Let
\psi_{\nu,\beta}
denote the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state associated to
\nu
in equation (
4.15
). Then for
x\in\mathfrak{D}_{B}
,

\|\lambda_{B}(V_{a}^{*}xV_{a})\Omega_{0}\|^{2}=\psi_{\nu,\beta}(V_{a}^{*}x^{*}V_{a}V_{a}^{*}xV_{a})=a^{\beta}\psi_{\nu,\beta}(x^{*}V_{a}V_{a}^{*}x)\leq a^{\beta}\psi_{\nu,\beta}(x^{*}x)=a^{\beta}\|\lambda_{B}(x)\Omega_{0}\|^{2},

where we have used the fact that
x
commutes with
V_{a}V_{a}^{*}
. That
a\mapsto S_{a}
is multiplicative follows from commutativity of the isometries
\{V_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\}
.

If
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}
,
b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}
, and
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
, then letting

(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}=\rho_{B}(V_{a}fV_{a}^{*})\Omega,

and
a^{\prime}=\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)}{b}
,
b^{\prime}=\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)}{a}
,

T^{*}S_{a}T\rho_{B}(V_{b}fV_{b}^{*})\Omega=\rho_{B}(V_{a}^{*}V_{b}fV_{b}^{*}V_{a})\Omega=\rho_{B}(V_{b^{\prime}}f\circ\omega_{a^{\prime}}V_{b^{\prime}}^{*})\Omega=(f_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}},

where

f_{n}^{\prime}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f\circ\omega_{a^{\prime}n/b^{\prime}}&\text{if $b^{\prime}|n$},\\[2.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

Now if
b^{\prime}|n
, then
b|\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)|an
; conversely, if
b|an
, then
b^{\prime}|a^{\prime}n
, which implies
b^{\prime}|n
since
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
are relatively prime. Since
\frac{a^{\prime}n}{b^{\prime}}=\frac{an}{b}
when
b^{\prime}|n
, it follows that
f_{n}^{\prime}=f_{an}
.

Lastly, if
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}\backslash B}
, then
a
and
b
are relatively prime, and we have
T^{*}S_{a}T\rho_{B}(V_{b}fV_{b}^{*})\Omega=\rho_{B}(V_{b}f\circ\omega_{a}V_{b}^{*})\Omega.

∎

Recall the
periodic zeta function
defined by the series

F(\beta,z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}m^{-\beta}z^{m},\qquad\Re(\beta)>1,\ z\in\mathbb{T},

(where we have chosen to deviate slightly from standard practice by using
z=\exp(2\pi in\alpha)\in\mathbb{T}
instead of
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
for the second variable).
For each finite
B\Subset\mathcal{P}
define also a partial periodic zeta function by the partial series

F_{B}(\beta,z):=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}m^{-\beta}z^{m},\qquad\Re(\beta)>0,\ z\in\mathbb{T},

where convergence is absolute. Write
\mathcal{P}_{n}=\{2,3,5,...,p_{n}\}
for the set of the first
n
primes,
\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}:=\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}
for the monoid generated by
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, and, accordingly,

\zeta_{n}(\beta):=\zeta_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}(\beta)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{1}{m^{\beta}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{n}(\beta,z):=F_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}(\beta,z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{n}}\frac{z^{m}}{m^{\beta}}

We will need the following consequence of Wiener’s Lemma; see
[
53
, Theorem III.24]
for the original statement and
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
for the version of the lemma that we use here. Our proof relies on Corollary
6.9
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Suppose
\nu
is a nonatomic probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
and fix
B\Subset\mathcal{P}
.
Then

(7.4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n}(1)}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}\backslash B}}\frac{1}{m}\widehat{\nu}(\ell m+k)=0\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Since
\nu
is a probability measure,
\widehat{\nu}(-m)=\overline{\widehat{\nu}(m)}
and
|\widehat{\nu}(m)|\leq 1
for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, and since

Proposition 7.6

\nu_{1,\mathcal{P}}

4.10
(4) to define an

-invariant function

on

\nu_{1,\mathcal{P}}
-null set by Lemma

\displaystyle\left<f,g\right>

P_{A}f=f_{A}

\nu_{1,\mathcal{P}}
-a.e..

Now for

\displaystyle P_{A}\chi_{\ell}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta_{A}(1)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{A}}n^{-1}S_{n}(\chi_{\ell})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta_{A}(1)}\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}\times\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{A\backslash B}}(nm)^{-1}T(\mathfrak{z}^{\ell m}\delta_{1,nk})_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}(x)

In

\displaystyle\left<F_{A\backslash B}(1,z^{\ell})\zeta_{A}(1)^{-1},\mathfrak{z}^{k}\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}\mu(n)n^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{F_{A\backslash B}(1,z^{n\ell})}{\zeta_{A}(1)}z^{-nk}d\nu

.

For each

\beta
-subconformal probability measure on

We first consider the case

Then the action of
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}

in the GNS representation for the weight

Since

Proposition 8.1

Suppose

Remark 8.2
.

The proof of surjectivity of the parameterization of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states for the system studied in
[

]
made use of the

[
36

\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of our system.

Suppose
\psi

\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})

If

\psi_{e_{F}}(\cdot):=\zeta_{F}(\beta)\psi(e_{F}\ \cdot\ e_{F})

\psi(T)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\frac{a^{-\beta}}{\zeta_{F}(\beta)}\psi_{e_{F}}(V_{a}^{*}TV_{a}).

In particular, if we set

and

\psi(U^{n})=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\frac{a^{-\beta}}{\zeta_{F}(\beta)}\psi_{e_{F}}(U^{an})=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{F}}\frac{a^{-\beta}}{\zeta_{F}(\beta)}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathfrak{z}^{an}dA_{\beta,F}\nu=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\zeta_{F}(\beta,z^{n})}{\zeta_{F}(\beta)}dA_{\beta,F}\nu.

Here we see a key difference between the reconstruction formulas for

[

is a sum of ratios

state on

, while for the

\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state corresponding to a nonatomic
\beta
-subconformal measure.

Recall that for

Proposition 8.3
.

For

be the

Proof.

For any

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(z)d(\nu_{\beta,n}-\nu_{\beta^{\prime},n})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left|n^{-\beta}\sum_{d|n}\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(d)}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{z\in Z_{d}^{*}}f(z)-n^{-\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{d|n}\frac{\varphi_{\beta^{\prime}}(d)}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{z\in Z_{d}^{*}}f(z)\right|

\displaystyle T_{\beta}\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}c^{-\beta}\omega_{c*}\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}\backslash B}}k^{-\beta}\right)\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{B}}c^{-\beta}\omega_{c*}\varepsilon_{n}

\zeta(\beta,a)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+a)^{-\beta}
be the Hurwitz zeta function with parameter
a\in(0,1]
. If
z
is a primitive
n^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity, then

T_{\beta}\delta_{z}=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}c^{-\beta}\delta_{z^{c}}=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c=1\\
n|(c-k)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}c^{-\beta}\delta_{z^{k}}=\frac{n^{-\beta}}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta\left(\beta,\frac{k}{n}\right)\delta_{z^{k}}.

The functions
\zeta(\beta,a)
and
\zeta(\beta)
have simple poles with residue
1
at
\beta=1
,
[
2
, Section 12.5]
, so

\lim_{\beta\rightarrow 1^{+}}T_{\beta}\delta_{z}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{z^{k}},

which is equal to
\nu_{1,n}
by (
5.7
).

Now suppose that
z
has infinite order in the group
\mathbb{T}
and let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
; then

T_{\beta}\delta_{z}(\mathfrak{z}^{n})=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}c^{-\beta}\delta_{z^{c}}(\mathfrak{z}^{n})=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}c^{-\beta}z^{cn}=\frac{F(\beta,z^{n})}{\zeta(\beta)}.

If
n\neq 0
, then
z^{n}\neq 1
so that
F(\beta,z^{n})
is conditionally convergent for
\beta>0
(cf.
[
2
, Section 12.7]
). Thus
\lim_{\beta\to 1^{+}}T_{\beta}\delta_{z}(\mathfrak{z}^{n})=0
. If
n=0
, then
F(\beta,1)=\zeta(\beta)
and the ratio is constant equal to
1
. Therefore, we conclude that the w*-limit
T_{1}\delta_{z}
exists and is equal to normalized Haar measure.
∎

Proposition 9.1

Let

Proposition 9.2

Proposition 9.3

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left<U^{n}-1\right>}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})}

Relations (N0)–(N4) imply that the collection

similarly

onto

Compared to Proposition

∎

10.
Symmetry and type

Recall from
[

, Section 4]
that the
Bost–Connes C*-algebra

is canonically isomorphic to the universal C*-algebra with generators
\upsilon_{a}
for

and

for

\upsilon_{a}e(x)\upsilon_{a}^{*}=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{ay=x}e(y)

In

. The dynamics on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are determined by

\sigma_{t}(e(x))=e(x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma_{t}(\upsilon_{a})=a^{it}\upsilon_{a}.

The system

has a unique
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state for
\beta\in(0,1]
, which we denote by
\psi_{\beta}
.

An important feature of the Bost-Connes system is its
group of symmetries
, which remarkably is isomorphic to the Galois group of the cyclotomic extension
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\rm cycl}/\mathbb{Q})
. We describe this action for completeness. The field
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm cycl}
is the direct limit of the fields
\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/n})
; the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/n})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Aut}(n^{-1}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})
, which acts by
u\cdot e^{2\pi ik/n}=e^{2\pi iu(k/n)}
. We then identify

\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\rm cycl}/\mathbb{Q})=\varprojlim\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/n})/\mathbb{Q})\cong\varprojlim\operatorname{Aut}(n^{-1}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

The action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is determined by

(10.3)

\theta_{u}(e(x))=e(u(x))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta_{u}(\upsilon_{a})=\upsilon_{a}.

One verifies that
\{e(u(x)):x\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\}
and
\{\upsilon_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\}
satisfy (BC0)–(BC5), so that (
10.3
) determines an equivariant *-endomorphism of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}}
with inverse
\theta_{u^{-1}}
.

In the spirit of (
10.3
), we define a semigroup of endomorphisms on
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
as follows. For
b\in\mathbb{N}
, define

(10.4)

\kappa_{b}(U)=U^{b}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\kappa_{b}(V_{a})=V_{a}.

Then
U^{b}
and
\{V_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\}
satisfy (AB0)–(AB3), so there exists a unique *-endomorphism of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
satisfying (
10.4
) that commutes with the dynamics
\sigma_{t}
. Since
\kappa_{a}\kappa_{b}=\kappa_{ab}
, for
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
, the map
b\mapsto\kappa_{b}
is indeed a semigroup action of the multiplicative monoid
\mathbb{N}
on
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
.

Similarly, for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, the formulas

\bar{\kappa}_{b}(R)=R^{b}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{\kappa}_{b}(V_{a})=V_{a}

determine a *-endomorphism
\bar{\kappa}_{b}
of
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
. Obviously
\bar{\kappa}_{b}=\bar{\kappa}_{b^{\prime}}
if
b\equiv b^{\prime}
mod
n
, so in particular, we have an action of the unit group
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
on
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
by automorphisms that commute with the dynamics.

If
n
and
b
are relatively prime, then
b
determines an automorphism of
n^{-1}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
by
u_{b}([x])=[bx]
. This gives an identification
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}\cong\operatorname{Aut}(n^{-1}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})
. Using the action
\bar{\kappa}
and the projection
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(n^{-1}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})
given by restriction, we obtain an action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
, which we also denote by
\bar{\kappa}
.

\mathcal{H}
, and by Proposition
10.6
we have

P_{n}\pi_{\beta}(a)\Omega=\pi_{\beta}(\Theta_{n}(a))\Omega=\int_{G_{n}}T_{u}\pi_{\beta}(a)\Omega du.

so
P_{n}=\int_{G_{n}}T_{u}du
commutes with
(\pi_{\beta}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\prime\prime})^{T}
. By Lemma
10.5
(ii) we have
P_{n}T_{u}P_{n}=L_{x}W_{b}L_{x}^{*}
, where
b
is the image of
u
in
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
. Thus
L_{x}
gives a spatial isomorphism between
(\pi_{\beta,n}(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}))^{\prime\prime})^{W}
and
P_{n}(\pi_{\beta}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\prime\prime})^{T}P_{n}
. Since
P_{n}
is in the commutant of the type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor
(\pi_{\beta}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\prime\prime})^{T}
, it follows that
(\pi_{\beta,n}(\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z}))^{\prime\prime})^{W}
is a type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor.
∎

Corollary 10.9

(Theorem
1.1
, type

.

For

and

, the state

\mathrm{III}_{1}
.

Proof.

By
[
43
, Theorem 2.7]
, if
M
is a finite-index subfactor of a factor
N
and
M
is of type
\mathrm{III}_{1}
, then

.
∎

11.
The Toeplitz system of
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

In this section we describe how our analysis of the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states on
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
can be extended to
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
.
The monoid
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
of the previous section is naturally isomorphic to
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes({\tfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})
by the map
(a,[x])\mapsto(a,[x/n])
. The latter form a nested system of monoids over
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
with union
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. We begin by describing a presentation of the Toepliz algebra
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
in terms of generators and relations analogous to the one obtained for
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
in the preceding section.

Proposition 11.1
.

The monoid
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is left-cancellative and right LCM. The Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
is generated by elements
R_{x}:=T_{(1,x)}
for
x\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
and
V_{a}:=T_{(a,[0])}
for
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, which satisfy

(Q0)

V_{a}^{*}V_{a}=1=R_{x}R_{x}^{*}=R_{x}^{*}R_{x}
,

(Q1)

R_{x}V_{a}=V_{a}R_{ax}
,

(Q2)

V_{a}V_{b}=V_{ab}
,

(Q3)

V_{a}V_{b}^{*}=V_{b}^{*}V_{a}
when
\gcd(a,b)=1
,

(Q4)

R_{x}R_{y}=R_{x+y}
.

Moreover, the relations
(Q0)–(Q4)
constitute a presentation of
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
.

The proof is essentially the same as in Propositions
9.2
and
9.3
.

Under the identification
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\cong{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes({\tfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})
above, the inclusions
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes({\tfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\subseteq{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes({\tfrac{1}{m}}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})
for
n|m
give an inductive system of *-homomorphisms

(11.2)

\iota_{n,m}:\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}))\quad\text{ such that }\quad\iota_{n,m}:R\mapsto R^{\frac{m}{n}},\qquad V_{a}\mapsto V_{a}.

Proposition 11.3
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, there are injective *-homomorphisms
\iota_{n}:\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
determined by

(11.4)

\iota_{n}:R\mapsto R_{\frac{1}{n}},\qquad V_{a}\mapsto V_{a},

and
(\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})),\iota_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is the limit of the system
(\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})),\iota_{n,m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
.

In particular, the inclusion
{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes({\tfrac{1}{n}}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\subseteq{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
induces an inclusion of Toeplitz algebras
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\ltimes(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}))\subseteq\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
such that the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\ltimes(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subseteq}

We give
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
the dynamics
\sigma_{t}(T_{(a,[x])})=a^{it}T_{(a,x)}
. The maps
\iota_{n,m}
and
\iota_{n}
are equivariant for the dynamics, so Proposition
11.3
gives us a first description of the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
simplex of
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
, as the projective limit of the system
(K_{\beta}^{n},\iota_{n,m}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
, where
K_{\beta}^{n}
is the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
simplex of
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
. We will describe this limit in the cases
\beta\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta\in(0,1]
with the help of the following interpretations of the KMS states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
.

The group of characters of
\mathbb{Z}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}
and the group of characters of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
is isomorphic to
Z_{n}
, the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
roots of unity. Thus, the extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
for
\beta\in(1,\infty)
can be reparameterized by the character groups of
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, respectively (cf.
[
28
, Theorem 8.1 (2)]
and Theorem
9.7
). For a character
\chi
on
\mathbb{Z}
, the reparametrization for states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
is given by

\psi_{\beta,\chi}(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}\frac{a^{-\beta}}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}c^{-\beta}\chi(ck).

Recall that the Chabauty topology is the topology on the set
\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(G)
of closed subgroups of
G
with a basis of neighborhoods for
C\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(G)
given by

V_{C}(K,U)=\{D\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(G):D\cap K\subseteq CU\}

for
K\subseteq G
compact and
U\subseteq G
an open neighborhood of identity, cf.
[
10
]
. The (closed) subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}
are parameterized by the set
\mathbb{N}^{\times}\cup\{\infty\}
, where
H_{n}=n\mathbb{Z}
and
H_{\infty}=0\mathbb{Z}
, and this identifies
\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\mathbb{Z})
with the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
. The subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
are parameterized by divisors of
n
,
H_{d}=d\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, by Theorems
1.1
(1) and
9.7
, the
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
for
\beta\in(0,1]
can be reparametrized by the spaces
\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
, respectively. Note that for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}\cup\{\infty\}
,
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{Z}/H_{n}}(k)=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}
. Then, for a subgroup
H\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, the reparametrization of states of
\mathcal{T}({\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\!\ltimes\mathbb{Z})
gives

\psi_{\beta,H}(V_{a}U^{k}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}a^{-\beta}\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{Z}/H}(k)^{-\beta}\sum_{d|\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{Z}/H}(k)}\mu(d)\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(d)}{\varphi(d)},

with the convention that
(\infty)^{-\beta}=0
.

Since we are considering states on a family of C*-algebras indexed by
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, we write
\bar{\psi}_{\beta}^{n}
(rather than
\bar{\psi}_{\beta}
) for a
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state on
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
and
\psi_{\beta}^{\infty}
for a
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state on
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
, in order to clarify the domain of the state. The maps
\iota_{n,m}^{*}
are readily computed in terms of characters and subgroups of
\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. For
\beta\in(1,\infty)
and
\chi\in(\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
, we have

\iota_{n,m}^{*}(\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,\chi})=\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,\chi}\circ\iota_{n,m}=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,\chi|_{\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}}},

and for
\beta\in(0,1]
and a subgroup
H\subseteq\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\iota_{n,m}^{*}(\bar{\psi}_{m,\beta,H})=\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,H}\circ\iota_{n,m}=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,H\cap\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}}.

Lemma 11.5
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
and assume
n|m
, so that
\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}\subset\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
.

(1)

Suppose
\beta\in(1,\infty)
and let
\eta\in(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
. Then
\eta=\chi|_{\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}}
for a character
\chi\in(\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
if and only if
\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,\chi}\circ\iota_{n,m}=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,\eta}.

(2)

Suppose
\beta\in(0,1]
and let
K
be a subgroup of
\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
. Then
K=H\cap(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})

for a subgroup
H
of
\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,H}\circ\iota_{n,m}=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,K}.

Proof.

(1) If the character
\eta\in(\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
is the restriction of a character
\chi\in(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
, in the sense that

\eta(x)=\chi(x)
for every
x\in\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
, then
a computation using formula (
9.8
) shows that

\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,\chi}\circ\iota_{n,m}(V_{a}R_{x}V_{b}^{*})=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,\eta}(V_{a}R_{x}V_{b}^{*})\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ x\in\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}.

Conversely, suppose that
\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,\chi}\circ\iota_{n,m}=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,\eta}
for characters

\chi\in(\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
and
\eta\in(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
.
For every
x\in\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\zeta(\beta)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\mu(k)k^{-\beta}\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,\chi}(R_{x}^{k})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\mu(k)(ck)^{-\beta}\chi(x)^{ck}=\chi(x).

hence
\chi(x)=\eta(x)
for
x\in\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
, as desired.

(2) If the subgroup
K\leq\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
is the intersection
K=H\cap\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
for a subgroup
H\leq\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
, then

\mathrm{ord}_{(\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})/H}(x+H)=\mathrm{ord}_{(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})/K}(x+K)\qquad x\in\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z},

hence formula (
9.9
) gives

\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,H}\circ\iota_{n,m}(V_{a}R_{x}V_{b}^{*})=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,K}(V_{a}R_{x}V_{b}^{*})\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\times},\ x\in\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}.

Conversely, suppose that
\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,H}\circ\iota_{n,m}=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,K}
.
Setting
a=b=1
in (
9.9
), we see that

\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,H}(R_{x})=\mathrm{ord}(x+H)^{-\beta}\sum_{d|\mathrm{ord}(x+H)}\mu(d)\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(d)}{\varphi(d)}

only depends on
x
through the value
c:=\mathrm{ord}(x+H)
. Motivated by this, we define an arithmetic function
h
by

h(c):=c^{-\beta}\sum_{d|c}\mu(d)\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(d)}{\varphi(d)}=\sum_{d|c}\Big(\mu(d)d^{-1}\prod_{p|d}\frac{1-p^{-\beta}}{1-p^{-1}}\Big)\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)^{-\beta},

which shows that

h
is the Dirichlet convolution
f*g
of the arithmetic functions
f
and
g
defined by

f(c)=\mu(c)c^{-1}\prod_{p|c}\frac{1-p^{-\beta}}{1-p^{-1}},\qquad g(c)=c^{-\beta}\qquad\text{for }c\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}.

If
c
divides
[(\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}):H]
, then
c=\mathrm{ord}(x+H)
for some
x\in\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
, and

h(c)=\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,H}(R_{x})
for every such
x
.
Since
f(1)=1
, the function
f
is invertible in the Dirichlet ring of arithmetic functions. Hence

c^{-\beta}=g(c)=(f^{-1}*h)(c)=\sum_{d|c}f^{-1}(d)h(c/d)

If
c
divides
[(\tfrac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}):H]
, this is
\sum_{d|c}f^{-1}(d)\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,H}(R_{x}^{d})
for
c=\mathrm{ord}(x+H)
.

Since
\bar{\psi}^{m}_{\beta,H}(R_{x})=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,K}(R_{x})
for every
x\in\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
by assumption, it follows that

\mathrm{ord}(x+(H\cap(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})))=\mathrm{ord}(x+H)=\mathrm{ord}(x+K).

Thus,
x\in H\cap\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
x\in K
, so the two subgroups are equal.
∎

For each
H\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})
and
n|m
, we define
h_{n,m}(H):=H\cap\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
. This gives a projective system
(\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}),h_{n,m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
of finite spaces, and there is a homeomorphism

\displaystyle h:\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varprojlim(\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}),h_{n,m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}},

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\longmapsto(H\cap\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}},

where the left hand side is endowed with the Chabauty topology and the right hand side has the
profinite topology.
Indeed, the map
h
is injective because

\bigcup_{n}(H\cap\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})=H\cap\bigcup_{n}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}=H
, and it is surjective because, for every net
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
of subgroups satisfying
H_{m}\cap\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}=H_{n}
whenever
n|m
, the set
H=\bigcup H_{n}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
such that
h(H)=(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
.

Theorem 11.6
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
be the dynamics on
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
determined by
\sigma_{t}(R_{x})=R_{x}
and
\sigma_{t}(V_{a})=a^{it}V_{a}
.

(1)

For each
\beta\in(1,\infty)
and character
\chi\in(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\,\widehat{\ }
, there is a unique extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state of
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
such that

(11.7)

\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,\chi}(V_{a}R_{x}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}\frac{a^{-\beta}}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}c^{-\beta}\chi(x)^{c}.

The map
\chi\mapsto\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,\chi}
extends to an affine w*-homeomorphism of the simplex of probability measures on
(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
onto the simplex of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
(\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})),\sigma)
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
,

\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,\chi}\circ\iota_{n}=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,\chi_{n}},\quad\chi_{n}=\chi|_{\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}}.

(2)

For each
\beta\in(0,1]
and subgroup
H\subseteq\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, there is a unique extremal
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
state of
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
such that

(11.8)

\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,H}(V_{a}R_{x}V_{b}^{*})=\delta_{a,b}a^{-\beta}\mathrm{ord}\left(x+H\right)^{-\beta}\sum_{d|\mathrm{ord}\left(x+H\right)}\mu(d)\frac{\varphi_{\beta}(d)}{\varphi(d)}.

The map
H\mapsto\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,H}
extends to an affine w*-homeomorphism of the simplex of probability measures on
\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
onto the simplex of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
(\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})),\sigma)
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
,

\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,H}\circ\iota_{n}=\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,H_{n}},\quad H_{n}=H\cap(\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
11.3
that the simplex of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states of
(\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})),\sigma_{t})
is canonically homeomorphic to the projective limit of the simplices
K_{n,\beta}
of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states under the maps
\iota_{n,m}^{*}
. We will show that the extreme points of this simplex are determined by either (
11.7
) or (
11.8
) on monomials (depending on if
\beta\in(1,\infty)
or
(0,1]
), whence the result follows.

(1) For
\beta\in(1,\infty)
, by Theorem
9.7
(1) and Lemma
11.5
(1), the system
(\partial_{e}K_{n,\beta},\iota_{n,m}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is naturally isomorphic to
((\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ },r_{n,m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
, where
r_{n,m}(\chi)=\chi|_{\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}}
. It follows from
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}=\bigcup\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
that
((\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ },r_{n})
is the limit of
((\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ },r_{n,m})
. For
\chi\in(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\widehat{\ }
, if
\chi_{n}=r_{n}(\chi)
, then
(\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,\chi_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is a coherent family of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states that determines a state
\psi
on
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
. Since
\chi(x)=\chi_{n}(x)
for
x\in\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
, formulas (
9.8
) and (
11.7
) imply that
\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,\chi_{n}}=\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,\chi}\circ\iota_{n}
on monomials, so
\psi=\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,\chi}
.

(2) For
\beta\in(0,1]
, by Theorem
9.7
(2) and Lemma
11.5
(2), the system
(\partial_{e}K_{n,\beta},\iota_{n,m}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is naturally isomorphic to
(\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}),h_{n,m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
, where
h_{n,m}(H)=H\cap\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}.
Then
\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\cong\varprojlim(\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}),h_{n,m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
, so for every
H\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Subg}}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
and
H_{n}=h_{n}(H)
,
(\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,H_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is a coherent family of
\mathrm{KMS}_{\beta}
states that determines a state
\psi
on
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^{\times}\!\ltimes(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
. Since
\mathrm{ord}_{(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})/H}(x)=\mathrm{ord}_{(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z})/H_{n}}(x)
for every
x\in\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
, formulas (
9.9
) and (
11.8
) imply that
\bar{\psi}^{n}_{\beta,H_{n}}=\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,H}\circ\iota_{n}
on monomials, so
\psi=\psi^{\infty}_{\beta,H}
.
∎

Appendix A
Dilation/extension results

We prove a dilation/extension of the semigroup action of
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
on
\mathfrak{D}
and show that every
\beta
-subconformal measure on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}
extends to the dilated system.

The semigroup action of
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
by injective endomorphisms of
\mathfrak{D}
from Proposition
2.7
satisfies the dilation/extension conditions of Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.4 of
[
38
]
. Hence there exist a C*-algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
, an embedding
i:\mathfrak{D}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
, and an action
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{\mathfrak{D}})
, such that

(1)

\tilde{\alpha}_{a}
dilates
\alpha_{a}
, that is,
i\circ\alpha_{a}=\tilde{\alpha}_{a}\circ i
for
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
;

(2)

\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
is minimal with respect to
\tilde{\alpha}
, that is,
\bigcup_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}\tilde{\alpha}_{a}^{-1}(i(\mathfrak{D}))
is dense in
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
;

(3)

\mathbb{N}^{\times}\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathfrak{D}
is the full corner in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}\ltimes_{\tilde{\alpha}}\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
corresponding to the projection
i(1_{\mathfrak{D}})
.

Explicitly,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
is the direct limit of the system
(\mathfrak{B}_{a},\alpha_{b,a})_{a|b}
, in which the C*-algebras are
\mathfrak{B}_{a}=\mathfrak{D}
for all
a
, and the connecting maps
\alpha_{b,a}:\mathfrak{B}_{a}\rightarrow\mathfrak{B}_{b}
are given by
\alpha_{b,a}(x)=\alpha_{\frac{b}{a}}(x)
when
a\mid b
. We write
i_{a}
for the canonical embedding
i_{a}:\mathfrak{B}_{a}\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}
.

Both
\mathfrak{D}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
are commutative C*-algebras, and we let
X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathfrak{D}
and
\tilde{X}=\operatorname{Spec}\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
. The image
i(1_{\mathfrak{D}})
of the identity of
\mathfrak{D}

is a full projection in
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
and there is a homeomorphism
i_{*}:X\rightarrow\operatorname{supp}i(1_{\mathfrak{D}})
identifying
X
with a compact open subset of
\tilde{X}
; more generally, there is a homeomorphism
i_{a*}:X\rightarrow\operatorname{supp}i_{a}(1_{\mathfrak{D}})
for each
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
whose image is the translate of
i_{*}(X)
under
\widetilde{\alpha}_{a}^{*}
. By the minimality condition (2), the union of the
i_{a*}(X)
is
\tilde{X}
. In particular, this says that
C_{c}(\widetilde{X})
is equal to
\bigcup_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}i_{a}(\mathfrak{B}_{a})
: any function belonging to
\bigcup_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}i_{a}(\mathfrak{B}_{a})
is compactly supported, and conversely, if
f
is compactly supported, then there exists a subset
F\Subset\mathbb{N}^{\times}
such that
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq\bigcup_{a\in F}i_{a*}(X)\subseteq i_{\operatorname{lcm}F*}(X)
.

Next we wish to show that states on
\mathfrak{D}
that satisfy a rescaling condition with respect to
\alpha
extend to densely defined locally finite weights on
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
.
This has been used to study KMS states of the Bost-Connes system and its generalizations, see e.g.
[
30
,
45
]
. We provide the details here for completeness,
formulating things in terms of Radon measures on
\tilde{X}
, which represent positive linear functionals on compactly supported functions in
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
by the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani Theorem.

Lemma A.1
.

If
\phi
is a state of
\mathfrak{D}
such that
\phi\circ\alpha_{a}=a^{-\beta}\phi
for all
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, then there is a unique positive linear functional
\tilde{\phi}
on
C_{c}(\tilde{X})\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
such that

\tilde{\phi}(i(f))=\phi(f)
for every
f\in\mathfrak{D}
and
\tilde{\phi}\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{r}=r^{-\beta}\tilde{\phi}
for every
r\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}
.
Conversely, if
\eta
is a positive linear functional on
C_{c}(\widetilde{X})
normalized so that
\eta(i(1_{\mathfrak{D}}))=1
and satisfying
\eta\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{r}=r^{-\beta}\eta
for every
r\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}
, then the restriction
\phi:=\eta\circ i
to
\mathfrak{D}
is a state such that
\phi\circ\alpha_{a}=a^{-\beta}\phi
for all
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
; moreover,
\tilde{\phi}=\eta
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\phi
is a state of
\mathfrak{D}
such that
\phi\circ\alpha_{a}=a^{-\beta}\phi
. For all
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}

define a positive linear functional
\widetilde{\phi}_{a}=a^{\beta}\phi
on
\mathfrak{B}_{a}:=\mathfrak{D}
. Then

\widetilde{\phi}_{b}\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{b,a}=b^{\beta}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{-\beta}\phi=\widetilde{\phi}_{a},

so that
(\widetilde{\phi}_{a})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
is a coherent family for the inductive system
(\mathfrak{B}_{a},\alpha_{a,b})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
. Since
C_{c}(\widetilde{X})
is equal to
\bigcup_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}}i_{a}(\mathfrak{B}_{a})
, there is a unique positive linear functional
\widetilde{\phi}
on
C_{c}(\widetilde{X})
that agrees with
\widetilde{\phi}_{a}
when restricted to
\mathfrak{B}_{a}
. This implies that
\widetilde{\phi}\circ i=\phi
.
Additionally, if
x\in\mathfrak{B}_{b}
, then for every
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
,

\widetilde{\phi}(\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(x))=b^{\beta}\phi(V_{a}xV_{a}^{*})=a^{-\beta}\widetilde{\phi}(x),

which implies that
\widetilde{\phi}\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{a}=a^{-\beta}\widetilde{\phi}
for
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{+}=(\mathbb{N}^{\times})^{-1}\mathbb{N}^{\times}
.

Suppose now
\eta
is a linear functional on
C_{c}(\widetilde{X})
normalized to
\eta(i(1_{\mathfrak{D}}))=1
and satisfying
\eta\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{a}=a^{-\beta}\eta
. By minimality of the dilation/extension, property (2) above, it follows that
\eta
is determined by its restrictions to the subspaces
\tilde{\alpha}_{a}^{-1}(i(\mathfrak{D}))
with
a\in\mathbb{N}^{\times}
. Then
\phi:=\eta\circ i
is a state on
\mathfrak{D}
because of the normalization assumption, and it satisfies

\phi\circ\alpha_{a}(x)=\eta(i(\alpha_{a}(x)))=\eta(\widetilde{\alpha}_{a}(i(x)))=a^{-\beta}\eta(i(x))=a^{-\beta}\phi(x).

Therefore
\eta=\tilde{\phi}
, proving that the extension is unique and
\phi\mapsto\tilde{\phi}
is a bijection .
∎

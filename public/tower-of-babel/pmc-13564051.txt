Recurrent inguinal hernia in a child: distal sac should not be ignored
Authors: Jayalaxmi Shripati Aihole
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA hernia is the protrusion of an organ, such as the bowel, through the lining wall of the cavity in which it normally resides. Pediatric hernias are common developmental abnormalities which have different etiopathogenesis as well as management as compared to adults. Presence of persistent processus vaginalis is common mechanism in the etiopathogenesis of indirect inguinal hernia and hydrocele in children. Right sided hernias more common than left since, as , left processus vaginalis obliterates before the right. Author is reporting here a case of recurrent inguinoscrotal hernia with hydrocele in a child and its management.Keywords: Pediatric inguinal hernia, Recurrence, Herniotomy, Distal sac

Introduction

Pediatric hernias show bimodal peak of occurrence, one at early childhood and another at old age. Incidence in mature infants ranges from 1-5% to 12-25% with family history, having a slight preponderance in pre mature infants up to 10 to 30%.

Surgical closure of the persistent patent processus vaginalis (inguinal herniotomy) via small groin incision is the most common treatment in children, which consists of simple ligation of the hernial sac without opening the external ring.

Case report

A fourteen-year-old male child was brought to us with right inguinoscrotal swelling since 2months of age, progressively increasing in size since years.

Baby underwent for right herniotomy 2months of age immediately after it was noticed. However postoperatively swelling did not subside instead started progressively increased in size. Due to familial concerns and financial constrains, child was not given immediate medical attention, since the child was also asymptomatic over the years.

Moreso ever, child had grown, thrived well as for his age and attended his school without any symptoms nor any local symptoms. Child had more of right scrotal swelling, used to increase during day-to-day activities. On admission, child had right scrotal soft cystic swelling of size 7cm × 10 cm and right testis could be palpated separately, without any tenderness or redness over it. There was a right inguinal skin crease primarily healed scar with normal phallus left scrotum and left testis in it (Fig. 1A).

A-Clinical appearance of right recurrent hernia with previous incision scar in the right inguinal crease with prominent right hydrocele

B and C-Sonological images of right recurrent inguinal hernia and hydrocele.

D-isolated right recurrent inguinal hernial sac.

E--proximal sac opened after transecting the sac -blue arrow showing narrow hernial opening without any contents.

F-Distal sac opened to reveal hydrocele fluid via inguinal incision.

Sonology revealed right persistent inguinal hernial sac without any content with a fluid filed right distal hernial sac and normal bilateral testis (Fig. 1B and C).

His biochemical and complete blood counts were normal. Child was re explored via previous right inguinal skin crease scar; inguinal canal was explored to find remnant right indirect inguinal hernial sac without any content in it and normal posterior wall. It was transected and proximally sac was ligated, transfixed with absorbable vicryl suture (Fig. 1D, E, F).The distal sac opened to reveal hydrocele fluid. Since it was difficult to lay open the distal sac completely via inguinal incision, separate right scrotal 1cm incision was taken and distal hernial sac was explored and around 100ml of clear amber colored hydrocele fluid drained. Distal sac was everted securing with absorbable vicryl suture and a small glove drain was kept in situ (Fig. 1D, E, F and Fig. 2A and B, C, D).

A-and B --Separate scrotal incision to open the distal sac to easily decompress hydrocele fluid.

C-Eversion of distal sac with secured with vicryl absorbable sutures with glove drain kept in situ

D-Final closure of the both incisions.

Postoperatively child behaved well and glove drain was removed after 72hours.

Child is doing well on follow up.

Discussion

In children, an inguinal hernia is thought to be caused by bowel herniating through persistent processus vaginalis; while in adults, inguinal hernia is more likely due to weakened connective tissue and decreased abdominal wall resistance.1, 2, 3

As soon as diagnosis is made, surgical intervention should be contemplated even if asymptomatic, as there is a risk of incarceration, strangulation is high in infants and children due to narrow deep inguinal ring as well as superficial ring1, 2, 3

The basic principle of herniotomy for indirect inguinal hernia in children is ligation of persistent processus vaginalis as described by Marcy in 1871.Ferguson and Gross herniotomy (FGH)and Mitchell -Bank's herniotomy (MBH) are most commonly performed open methods in infants and in children recent years1, 2, 3

FHG involves opening of inguinal canal and high ligation of hernial sac at the level of deep inguinal ring or at preperitoneal fat, however in MBH, the same is accomplished without opening the inguinal canal1, 2, 3

The occurrence rate of recurrent inguinal hernia after uncomplicated inguinal hernia repair is reported to be 0.5 to 1% and testicular atrophy is 0% to 0.4% via open approach. Whereas recurrence rate following repair of complicated inguinal hernia is estimated to be around 0.5 to 3.8% 1, 2, 3, 4.

The most common cause for recurrent inguinal hernia in children is failure to identify or missed/incomplete ligation of indirect inguinal hernial sac at the level of deep inguinal ring or preperitoneal fat. Thin, friable sac, failure to delineate all four walls of sac at the level of deep inguinal ring and slippage of ligature could contribute to recurrence 2, 3, 4, 5.

Due to delicate anatomy and tissues in neonates, infants and children, meticulous, diligent and gentle dissection is mandatory to yield successful results.

Recurrent hernia increases the technical complexity of subsequent repair.

However, co-morbid conditions such as increased intraabdominal pressure (ventriculoperitoneal shunts), growth failure, prematurity, chronic pulmonary disease, connective tissue disorder and malnutrition increases the risk of hernia recurrence 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

Recurrent herniotomy are surgically challenging due to delicate anatomy and adhesions via inguinal skin crease incision 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

Nevertheless, distal sac should not be ignored either in primary or in redo herniotomies. Distal sac should have to be either be laid open via inguinal incision or via separate scrotal incision and with or without associated hydrocele and meticulously avoiding the injury to the testis, vas and its vessels. If the distal sac is huge and thick walled, it has to be everted and secured with absorbable sutures with or without a soft drain.

Author had a fourteen-year-old male child with right sided inguinoscrotal hernia, which had recurred following herniotomy at the age of 2months, for which child underwent right redo herniotomy at his fourteen's years age, and distal hydrocele sac separately dealt with incision at scrotum and draining the hydrocele fluid and distal sac was everted with a glove drain kept.

Conclusion

Recurrent inguinal herniotomy is rare in children, but are surgically challenging due to their delicate anatomy. After ligating the proximal sac, distal sac should be approached ether via groin incision or via scrotal incision with or without hydrocele fluid in it and should be laid open.

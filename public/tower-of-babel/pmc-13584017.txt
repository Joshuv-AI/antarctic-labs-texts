Clinical safety and preliminary efficacy of allogeneic human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived exosomes in patients with acute ischaemic stroke: protocol of a phase I/II clinical trial in China
Authors: Gaoting Ma, Yingting Zuo, Na Li, Wenwen Bian, Jingli Mu, Xiaoxi Yao, Ganghua Feng, Ran Mo, Shujuan Meng, Yifan Wu, Jieying Wu, Xue Chen, Qingfeng Ma, Daiquan Gao, Lianmei Zhong, Jianmin Zhang, Junwei Hao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionCell-based therapies represent a promising therapeutic strategy for enhancing neurological recovery after stroke. Preclinical studies have demonstrated the efficacy of stem cell-derived exosomes in animal models of ischaemic stroke. We aim to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of allogeneic human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived exosomes in patients with acute ischaemic stroke.Methods and analysisThis is a multicentre, prospective, phase I/II clinical trial conducted in China between October 2023 and June 2026. Eligible participants will be patients with a diagnosis of cortical ischaemic stroke, a baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score of 6–20 and enrolment within 1–7 days after symptom onset. Stage 1 is a dose-escalation study including three different dose levels (n=3 per dose). Stage 2 is an expanded safety and preliminary efficacy phase with a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled design. In Stage 2, 20 patients will be randomised 1:1 to receive the highest safe dose identified in Stage 1 or placebo. The primary endpoint is the incidence of serious adverse events within 90 days. Secondary efficacy endpoints include the proportion of patients achieving a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 0–2 at 90 days, ordinal mRS distribution at 90 days and changes in NIHSS and Barthel Index scores at 90 days, as well as EuroQol-5D-5L and Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) scores at 90 days.Ethics and disseminationThis protocol was written according to the general ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of Xuanwu Hospital Capital Medical University (approval number [2023]161), the Third People’s Hospital of Liaocheng, the First People’s Hospital of Chenzhou, Zibo Municipal Hospital and Mianyang Central Hospital. Results of this study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication.Trial registration numberNCT06138210.

Introduction

Stroke remains one of the leading causes of mortality and long-term disability worldwide.1 Although reperfusion therapy, such as intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy, is effective for acute ischaemic stroke, nearly half of patients fail to arrive at the hospital within the limited treatment time window.2–4 Even among those who receive reperfusion therapy, a substantial proportion continue to experience significant disability at 90 days. Consequently, there is an urgent need for effective neuroprotective therapies in clinical practice. However, numerous neuroprotective agents have failed to demonstrate clinical efficacy in patients with acute ischaemic stroke.

Cell-based therapy has emerged as a safe and promising approach to improving neurological function by acting through multiple mechanisms, including anti-inflammatory effects, neural replacement and angiogenesis.5–7 Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have been most commonly studied and have been effective in neuronal remodelling and functional recovery.8–10 However, the harvesting of MSCs from adult tissues is an invasive process. Moreover, these cells exhibit limited expansion potential and differentiation capacity after serial passaging.

Recent studies have demonstrated that induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)—possessing robust self-renewal and unlimited growth capacity,11 12 along with the unique advantage of being generated from any somatic tissue type (even blood)—are superior to traditional MSCs. Despite the therapeutic potential of iPSCs, their administration remains constrained by poor engraftment and tumourigenic risks. Increasing evidence suggests that the therapeutic benefits of stem cells are largely mediated by exosomes released by these cells.13 14

Exosomes are a subtype of extracellular vesicles, 30–150 nm in diameter, released by various cell types.15 Compared with stem cells, stem cell-derived exosomes can cross the blood-brain barrier, minimise tumourigenic and immunogenic risks, and retain comparable neuroprotective, reparative and regenerative effects.16–18 Preclinical studies have demonstrated the preliminary efficacy of stem cell-derived exosomes in animal models of ischaemic stroke.19–23 However, clinical evidence regarding exosome-based therapies remains extremely limited.

Therefore, this study aims to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of allogeneic human iPSC-derived exosomes in patients with acute ischaemic stroke and to establish a foundation for future large-scale clinical trials.

Methods

Study design

The ExoCURE trial is a multicentre, prospective, phase I/II clinical trial designed to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of allogeneic human iPSC-derived exosomes in patients with acute ischaemic stroke. The study consists of two stages: a dose-escalation phase (Stage 1) and an expanded safety and preliminary efficacy phase (Stage 2).

Stage one follows a standard 3+3 dose-escalation design enrolling three sequential cohorts (n=3 per cohort). Participants will receive escalating doses of 2.0×109 particles/kg (Cohort 1), 4.0×109 particles/kg (Cohort 2) and 8.0×109 particles/kg (Cohort 3). Dose escalation will proceed if no dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) is observed in a cohort of three patients as reviewed by the Safety Review Committee. If one DLT occurs, three additional patients will be enrolled at that dose level. Stage 1 of this study is designed to identify the optimal therapeutic dose and evaluate the safety profile of iPSC-derived exosomes.

Stage 2 will use a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled design, enrolling 20 patients, 10 receiving the highest safe dose determined in Stage 1 and 10 receiving placebo. The dose for Stage 2 will be selected based on a comprehensive assessment of safety, preliminary efficacy and manufacturing feasibility, which will be reviewed jointly by the clinical investigators and the sponsor following completion of Stage 1. Stage 2 is designed to explore their potential efficacy in reducing post-stroke disability. In addition, exploratory analyses of surrogate biomarkers, including infarct volume, cerebral oedema and circulating cytokines and growth factors, may provide mechanistic insights into the biological actions of iPSC-derived exosomes and inform future clinical development. Patients will be recruited from five centres (Xuanwu Hospital Capital Medical University, the Third People’s Hospital of Liaocheng, the First People’s Hospital of Chenzhou, Zibo Municipal Hospital and Mianyang Central Hospital) in China between June 2024 and Mar 2026.

Patient population

Eligible participants must present with cortical ischaemic stroke confirmed by neuroimaging, within 1–7 days of onset, and have a moderate-to-severe neurological deficit, defined by a baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score of 6–20. Randomization and blinding

In Stage 2, participants will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive either iPSC-derived exosomes or a placebo. Randomisation will be conducted using a permuted-block design with block sizes of four. The randomisation sequence will determine the assigned drug number for each patient in order of enrollment. Investigators, patients, and the study sponsor will remain blinded to treatment allocation throughout the trial.

Procedures

Study visits will be performed at screening (within 72 hours before treatment), at baseline (0 hour), 24 hours, 48 hours (Stage 2 only), day 7, day 14/discharge, day 30 (Stage 2 only), and day 90 (endpoint).

At screening, patients will undergo demographic assessment, physical examination, ECG, NIHSS and modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scoring, and neuroimaging (MRI (MRI) or CT (CT) + CT angiography (CTA) + CT perfusion (CTP)). In Stage 1, MRI or CT will be repeated at 24 hours post-administration to assess for intracranial haemorrhage. In Stage 2, this imaging will be performed at 48 hours post-randomisation. MRI will be repeated on day 14/discharge.

Clinical data, including neurological examination (NIHSS and mRS), vital signs, medical history, exploratory blood biomarkers, and records of adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs (SAEs), will be collected at baseline and during all follow-up visits.

Serum cytokines and growth factors (C-reactive protein (CRP), glial fibrillar acidic protein (GFAP), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-8 (IL-8), interleukin-10 (IL-10), and tumour necrosis factor (TNF)-α) will be measured before administration, and on days 7, 14/discharge, and 90 post-treatment. In Stage 2, single-cell RNA sequencing of peripheral blood will be performed before administration and on day 7. Treatment

Clinical-grade iPSCs were supplied by Guidon Pharmaceutics Ltd. Cells were seeded at 5–7×104 /cm2 density and cultured in a chemically defined medium in vitronectin-coated dishes. When cultures reached 75% confluency, passaging was performed using Versene solution; at 90% confluency, the culture supernatant was harvested, and medium was refreshed every 3 days for continued collection.

Harvested supernatants were filtered to remove cell debris, then concentrated and purified via ultrafiltration and chromatography. The purified exosomes were resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) before being dispensed into vials and stored at −75±10°C. The placebo solution matched the investigational product in appearance and composition of excipients. All production followed Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards, including validated equipment, qualified personnel, strict material controls, and comprehensive quality monitoring and documentation.

Quality control standards included1: Morphology: Cup-shaped, double-membrane vesicles with clear boundaries2; Particle size: 30 to 150 nm3; Markers: Positive for CD63, CD9, Alix, and TSG101; negative for calnexin (by western blotting)4; Sterility: Clear, colourless solution without precipitates or foreign matter; sterility and mycoplasma tests negative; endotoxin<10 EU/ml.

The frozen drug product was transported on dry ice with temperature monitoring. Four hours before infusion, the drug was thawed in a refrigerator at 2–8°C and diluted in 100 mL 0.9% saline according to patient body weight. The solution was administered intravenously once daily for seven consecutive days. Participants in the control group received an equal volume of placebo (0.9% saline) on the same schedule.

The infusion rate was maintained at 15–30 drops per minute for the first 10 min; if no adverse reaction occurred, the rate was increased to 60–80 drops per minute. After infusion, a 10 mL saline flush was used to ensure full delivery. The infusion container was inverted every 20 min to prevent particle aggregation, and the total infusion time was approximately 60 min. Vital signs were monitored before, during, and after each infusion.

Based on our previous findings, intravenous administration of iPSC-derived exosomes to middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) mice at a daily dose of 1×109 particles/25 g for five consecutive days significantly reduced mortality and infarct volume.19 The human equivalent dose was calculated based on body surface area normalisation using the standard allometric scaling formula: human equivalent dose (HED) (mg/kg) = animal dose (mg/kg) × (animal km / human km), where the km values for mouse and human are 3 and 37, respectively.24 Based on this conversion, the effective murine dose of 1×109 particles/25 g (~4×1010 particles/kg) translates to approximately 3.2×109 particles/kg in humans. Applying a safety factor of 20, we selected 2×109 particles/kg as the low dose and 8×109 particles/kg as the high dose for Stage 1.

Outcome measures

Stage 1

DLTs related to iPSC-derived exosomes are defined as1: Grade 3–4 infusion-related allergic reactions involving cardiovascular or respiratory dysfunction, within 24 hours post-infusion; or2 Grade 3–4 adverse events within 14 days after the first infusion.

Stage 2

The primary safety endpoint is the incidence of serious adverse events within 90 days. The secondary efficacy endpoints include1: Proportion of patients with mRS 0–2 at 90 days2; Ordinal distribution of mRS at 90 days3; Change in NIHSS score from baseline to day 14/discharge4; Change in NIHSS score from baseline to day 905; Barthel Index at 90 days6; EuroQol Five Dimensions Questionnaire 5 Levels (EQ-5D-5L) score at 90 days7; Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) score at 90 days.

Exploratory assessments include infarct volume at 14 days/discharge [fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR)], changes in serum cytokines and growth factors from baseline to 14 days/discharge (CRP, GFAP, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10 and TNF-α), degree of cerebral oedema changes from baseline to 48 hours, and single-cell RNA sequencing of peripheral blood from baseline to day 7.

All imaging data acquired during the patient’s hospital course will be anonymised and transmitted in Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) format to the imaging core laboratory (ICL) for quality control and centralised adjudication. Imaging readers will be blinded to the patient’s treatment assignment to reduce bias. Infarct volume will be measured on diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) (baseline) and FLAIR (day 14/discharge) images using manual planimetry with ITK-SNAP software V4.4.0, performed by two investigators with over 5 years of neuroimaging experience.25 The DWI segmentation will be transferred to the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) map to remove cerebrospinal fluid and obtain the final infarct volume. Cerebral oedema will be graded using the Safe Implementation of Thrombolysis in Stroke-Monitoring Study (SITS-MOST) scale.26 Any discrepancies between the two readers will be resolved by a third senior neuroradiologist from the same core laboratory.

Sample size estimates

Stage 1 follows a standard 3+3 dose-escalation design, enrolling 6–18 patients (conventional for phase I trials) to identify the maximum safe and potentially effective dose. Stage 2 will include 20 patients (iPSC-derived exosomes, n=10; placebo, n=10) to assess safety and preliminary efficacy. This sample size is pragmatic for a pilot exploratory study to provide safety and preliminary efficacy signals, with no formal calculation performed.

Statistical analyses

Data from Stages 1 and 2 will be pooled for analysis. The primary safety outcome (proportion of patients with SAEs within 90 days) will be summarised as frequencies and percentages for each group. For secondary efficacy outcomes: continuous variables will be presented as mean (SD) or median (IQR) as appropriate; categorical variables (eg, mRS score distributions) will be presented as frequencies and percentages. All statistical analyses will be performed using SAS version 9.4 and R 4.3.1.

Ethics and dissemination

This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Xuanwu Hospital Medical University, Beijing, China (approval number [2023]161). Ethical approval was also obtained at the other four participating sites. This study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Any important protocol amendments will be submitted to the ethics committee for review and approval before implementation. The study has been registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT06138210). Results of this study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication.

Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants. Participants will be fully informed about the objectives, risks, and benefits of the trial. The participant informed consent form is provided as online supplemental material file 1.

Trial status

The current protocol is version 3.1 (16 June 2025). This trial is in the process of recruiting participants. The first patient was enrolled on 5 June 2024. We expect to enrol the target sample size by Mar 2026 and plan to continue with follow-up until June 2026.

Data monitoring

An independent Data Safety Monitoring Committee will oversee safety monitoring for adverse events and compliance with the protocol.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the study design, conduct of the trial, interpretation of results, and editing of the final manuscript for publication.

Discussion

This study represents the first multicentre, phase I/II clinical trial to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of allogeneic human iPSC-derived exosomes in patients with acute ischaemic stroke.

Several methodological limitations need to be noted. First, it is conducted only in China and may not be generalisable to other ethnic populations. Second, the sample size of Stage 2 is relatively small, and the study lacks statistical power for definitive efficacy conclusions, consistent with its pilot and exploratory nature. Third, the 90-day follow-up period is relatively short, making it impossible to assess the long-term safety and durability of the treatment effect. Lastly, Stage 1 employs an open-label design. However, outcome assessors remained blinded to treatment allocation, thereby mitigating detection bias.

In conclusion, the ExoCURE trial is poised to generate high-quality evidence regarding the safety, preliminary efficacy, and potential differential effects of iPSC-derived exosomes compared with placebo. If the findings demonstrate a favourable safety profile and suggest therapeutic potential, the results will lay the foundation for larger-scale clinical trials to validate their efficacy and clinical applicability further.

𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroups with its induced order
Daniel Marín-Aragón, Raquel Tapia Ramos

Abstract
Let 𝒞 ⊂ ℕ p \mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p} be an integer polyhedral cone. An affine semigroup S ⊂ 𝒞 S\subset\mathcal{C} is a 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroup if | 𝒞 ∖ S | < + ∞ |\mathcal{C}\setminus S|<+\infty . This structure has always been studied using a monomial order. The main issue is that the choice of these orders is arbitrary. In the present work we choose the order given by the semigroup itself, which is a more natural order. This allows us to generalise some of the definitions and results known from numerical semigroup theory to C C -semigroups. Keyword: 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroup; Wilf’s conjecture; commutative monoids

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of non-negative integers, we say that
S\subset\mathbb{N}
is a numerical semigroup if it is an additive monoid and its complementary is finite. This structure has been study broadly in the literature (see for example
[
18
,
19
,
5
]
). In
[
18
]
it is proven that if
a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\in\mathbb{N}
are coprimes, then
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle=\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{e}a_{e}\mid\lambda_{1},\ldots\lambda_{e}\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a numerical semigroup. The number of generator, i.e.
e
is called the embedding dimension of
S
and the number
|\mathbb{N}\setminus S|=g\in\mathbb{N}
, the genus of
S
. Other relevant invariants are: the Frobenius number defined as
F(S)=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid n\notin S\}
, the conductor defined as
c(S)=F(S)+1
and the left elements defined as
L(S)=|\{x\in S\mid x<F(S)\}|
.

Related to these invariants there are still several open problems as Brass-Amorós’s conjecture (see
[
2
]
) or Wilf’s conjecture (see
[
20
]
) and, consequently, a lot of papers are published trying to solve them (see
[
17
,
11
,
10
,
9
]
). The first conjecture, put forward 15 years ago, states that if
S_{g}
is the set of all numerical semigroups with genus
g
then
|S_{g+1}|\geq|S_{g}|
for all
g\geq 0
. This conjecture is true for the numerical semigroups with genus less than
67
(see
[
11
]
) and with genus greater than an unknown
g
(see
[
21
]
). On the other hand, the Wilf’s conjecture was raised in 1978 and establishes that
e(S)L(S)\geq c(S)
.

In order to try to solve these conjectures, in
[
15
]
the concept of numerical semigroup is generalised as follows: let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
be an integer polyhedral cone, then a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup is an additive affine semigroup
S\subset\mathcal{C}
with finite complementary set. Thus, the transfer the problem from
\mathbb{N}
to
\mathbb{N}^{p}
. In this new structure, the classical invariants of commutative monoids theory cited previously have been study (see for example
[
8
]
or
[
12
]
).

In all these papers there is a huge issue, unlike in
\mathbb{N}
, in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
there is no a canonical total order. Therefore researchers have to choose a total order (as the graded lexicographical order) in order to define invariants as the Frobenius number but it is not always clear what happens when this order is changed. Our main goal is to show that this generalisation can be done in a different way which does not depend of the researcher’s choice.

In this work, we use the order induced by the semigroup itself in order to do the generalisation, i.e., given
a,b\in S
we say that
a\leq b
if and only if
a-b\in S
. This order has already been applied to numerical semigroups (see for example
[
7
]
) but never to
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups. With this choice, the order is always clear and does not depend of an arbitrary choice. Thanks to this order, we open a new line for obtaining results which can be applied back to numerical semigroups. In order to provide the examples shown we have used a computer with a CPU Inter Core i7 8th Gen and the codes which are available at
[
13
]
.

The content of this work is organised as follows: In Section
2
, we give the main definitions and some general results in order to provide background to the reader. We show our generalisation and how apply the changes to the main invariants. Then, in Section
3
we introduce a new invariant, the quasi-elasticity (based on the concept of elasticity in numerical semigroups, see
[
4
]
) and show its properties. Section
4
is devoted to idemaxial semigroups and we show bounds for computing some invariants in this family. Finally, in Section
5
, we generalize the Wilf’s conjecture by means of this partial order.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}

We define the induced order of

x\leq_{S}y\iff y-x\in S.

We use the symbol
\leq

\leq_{S}

x\leq_{S}y
then
x\leq_{\mathbb{N}^{p}}y

We recall that in a numerical semigroup, the multiplicity is the least element not zero of

\mbox{minimals}_{\leq}(S)

5
Wilf’s conjecture

We cannot end a work about semigroups without a brief mention of the Wilf’s conjecture. Let
S
be a numerical semigroup, Wilf’s conjecture claims that
e(S)n(S)\geq c(S)
where
e(S)
,
n(S)
and
c(S)
are its embedding dimension, the cardinal of its sporadic elements and its conductor (see
[
20
]
). This conjecture has been generalized in several ways (see
[
15
,
6
]
). In this works the authors generalise this conjecture for
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups using monomial total orders. On the other hand, in
[
1
]
Wilf’s conjecture is extended to generalized numerical semigroup using partial orders. In this section we are going to give even a more general conjecture using the induced order of a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. We use the notation in
[
1
]
:

c(S)=|\{a\in\mathcal{C}:a\leq b\mbox{ for some }b\in H(S)\}|,

n(S)=|\{a\in S:a\leq b\mbox{ for some }b\in H(S)\}|,

where
H(S)=\mathcal{C}\setminus S
.

Therefore we can pose the General Extended Wilf’s conjecture:

e(S)n(S)\geq pc(S).

This conjecture has been checked in a computational way and no counterexamples have been found.

6
Discussion

The traditional approach to study
C
-semigroups was to set a total order in a completely arbitrary way and then study the desired properties. This approach has the following issue: if the order is changed, the results obtained may not be true.

In this paper we replace the total order chosen by the researchers by the partial order induced by the semigroup itself. As this order depends exclusively on the semigroup, it does not depend on the researcher, so the results obtained are less artificial. For example, when classical invariants were generalised in the study of semigroups, such as the Frobenius number or multiplicity, being completely dependent on the order, they changed as the order changed.

Moreover, since numerical semigroups have only one Frobenius element and only one multiplicity,
C
-semigroups were forced to have only one of these elements. However, with our method of study, we have a set of elements for each of these invariants, which is more natural since we are in a higher dimension. In addition to this, in this work, we have given a family of
C
-semigroups with good properties.

This work aims to lay the foundations for future work on
C
-semigroups.

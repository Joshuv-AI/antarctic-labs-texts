Mass, staticity, and a Riemannian Penrose inequality for weighted manifolds
Stephen McCormick

Abstract
. In this note, we show that the weighted mass of Baldauf and Ozuch [ 1 ] can be derived as a natural geometric mass invariant following Michel [ 10 ] , for a certain weighted curvature map. An associated weighted centre of mass definition is also derived from this. The adjoint of the linearisation of this curvature map leads to a notion of weighted static metrics, which are natural candidates for weighted mass minimisers. This weighted curvature quantity is essentially the scalar curvature of a conformally related metric that Law, Lopez and Santiago [ 9 ] used to considerably simplify the proof of the weighted positive mass theorem. We show an equivalence between static metrics and weighted static metrics via the conformal relationship, from which we show that a uniqueness theorem holds for weighted static manifolds with weighted minimal surface boundaries. Furthermore, we show that weighted manifolds satisfy a Riemannian Penrose inequality whose equality case holds precisely for these unique weighted static metrics.

2.
The weighted mass

Throughout this note,
(M^{n},g)
denotes a smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. We say that
(M^{n},g)
is
asymptotically flat
of order
\tau>0
if there exists a compact set
K\subset M
, a radius
R>0
, and a diffeomorphism

\Psi:\;M\setminus K\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(0),

on
M\setminus K
we have

g_{ij}-\mathring{g}_{ij}=O_{2}(r^{-\tau}),

where
r=|x|
is the standard radial coordinate in the end and
\mathring{g}
is a background metric isometric to the Euclidean metric on
M\setminus K
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
weighted manifold
(or manifold with density) is a triple
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dV_{g})
, where
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and the reference measure is
e^{-f}dV_{g}
. We say a weighted manifold is asymptotically flat of order
\tau>\frac{n-2}{2}
if
(M^{n},g)
is asymptotically flat of order
\tau
and
f=O_{2}(r^{-\tau})
.

We remark that the weighted area of a hypersurface
A_{f}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}e^{-f}d\sigma
has first variation
\int_{\Sigma}H_{f}\,e^{-f}d\sigma
under normal variations, where
H_{f}=H-\partial_{\nu}f
is the weighted mean curvature (cf.
[
1
]
). This leads us to make the following natural definitions.

Definition 2.2
.

We say a hypersurface in a weighted manifold
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dV_{g})
is
f
-minimal
if the weighted mean curvature
H_{f}=H-\partial_{\nu}f
of the hypersurface vanishes. We further say that a closed hypersurface
\Sigma
in a weighted manifold is
f
-outer-minimising
if it minimises the weighted area over all hypersurfaces enclosing
\Sigma
.

With the preliminaries out of the way, we now turn to prove Theorem
1.3
, defining the weighted mass as a natural mass invariant associated to a curvature map.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dV_{g})
be a weighted
n
-dimensional asymptotically flat manifold with decay
\tau>\frac{n-2}{2}
. We define a densitised curvature map

\Phi(g,f)=S_{f}\,e^{-f}dV_{g},

where

S_{f}=R_{f}+\frac{1}{n-1}|\nabla f|^{2}=R+2\Delta(f)-\frac{n-2}{n-1}|\nabla f|^{2},

motivated by Theorem
1.2
. Following Michel’s formalism on geometric mass invariants
[
10
]
, there is
1
-form valued differential operator
\mathbb{U}
defined by

V\,D\Phi_{(\mathring{g},\mathring{f})}(h,\varphi)-\langle(h,\varphi),D\Phi_{(\mathring{g},\mathring{f})}^{*}(V)\rangle=\operatorname{div}_{\mathring{g}}\mathbb{U}(V,h,\varphi)dV_{\mathring{g}},

(2.1)

where
h=g-\mathring{g}
,
\varphi=f-\mathring{f}
,
V
is a scalar function, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot,\rangle
is the natural pairing. We note here that instead of using the densitised map
\Phi
, one could instead define the adjoint map with respect to a weighted
L^{2}
inner product. Although we include the background weight function
\mathring{f}
in the expression above for clarity, we will be taking it to vanish identically in the definition of the weighted mass. Choosing
V
in the kernel of
D\Phi_{(\mathring{g},\mathring{f}\equiv 0)}^{*}
and integrating
\operatorname{div}_{\mathring{g}}\mathbb{U}
over
M
gives a flux integral at infinity, which corresponds to a geometric invariant à la Michel
[
10
]
. Although for the mass definition we need only compute the variation around the base point
(\mathring{g},0)
, the linearisation and its adjoint at a general point will be useful in what follows. We compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}D\Phi_{(g,f)}(h,\varphi)\,=\,&\Big(\big(\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}h_{ij}-\Delta(h^{i}{}_{i})\big)-2\big(\nabla^{j}h_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{i}(h^{k}{}_{k})\big)\nabla^{i}f\\
&\quad-h_{ij}\Big(\mathrm{Ric}^{ij}+2\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}f-\frac{n-2}{n-1}\,\nabla^{i}f\nabla^{j}f\Big)\\
&\qquad+\left(2\Delta\varphi-\frac{2(n-2)}{n-1}\,\nabla^{i}f\,\nabla_{i}\varphi\right)\\
&\qquad+S_{f}(g)\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}_{g}(h)-\varphi\right)\Big)e^{-f}dV_{g},\end{split}

(2.2)

with formal adjoint

\displaystyle\begin{split}D_{g}\Phi_{(g,f)}^{*}(V)^{ij}=\;&\Big(\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}V-(\Delta V)\,g^{ij}+\langle\nabla V,\nabla f\rangle\,g^{ij}\\
&\quad-\,V\Big(\mathrm{Ric}^{ij}+\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}f+\frac{1}{n-1}\,(\nabla^{i}f)(\nabla^{j}f)\Big)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\,V\,S_{f}\,g_{ij}\Big)\,e^{-f}dV_{g},\\
D_{f}\Phi_{(g,f)}^{*}(V)=\;&\Big(2\Delta V-\frac{2n}{n-1}\,\nabla_{i}V\nabla^{i}f-\frac{2}{n-1}\,V\,\Delta f\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{n-1}\,V\,|\nabla f|^{2}-\;V\,S_{f}\Big)\,e^{-f}dV_{g},\end{split}

(2.3)

where indices are raised and lowered with
g
.

From this we see that linearising around
(\mathring{g},\mathring{f}\equiv 0)

D\Phi_{(\mathring{g},0)}(h,\varphi)=\left(\mathring{\nabla}^{i}\mathring{\nabla}^{j}h_{ij}-\mathring{\Delta}(\mathrm{tr}_{\mathring{g}}(h))+2\mathring{\Delta}(\varphi)\right)dV_{\mathring{g}}

(2.4)

\begin{split}D_{g}\Phi_{(\mathring{g},0)}^{*}(V)^{ij}=\;&\Big(\mathring{\nabla}^{i}\mathring{\nabla}^{j}V-(\mathring{\Delta}V)\,\mathring{g}^{ij}\Big)dV_{\mathring{g}},\\

(2.5)

We see immediately that the kernel of

We then immediately see that integrating this over large spheres recovers the weighted mass

\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\int_{S_{\rho}}\mathbb{U}_{i}(1,g-\mathring{g},f)dS^{i}=2(n-1)\omega_{n-1}\mathfrak{m}_{f}(g),

\frac{n-1}{2}F_{f}^{*}(V)=(n-1)\Delta V-n\nabla_{k}(V)\nabla^{k}(f)-V\Delta f+V|\nabla f|^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2}V\,S_{f}.

Adding these together gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}_{g}F_{g}^{*}(V)+\frac{n-1}{2}F_{f}^{*}(V)

(3.10)

\displaystyle=V\left(-R-2\Delta f+\frac{n-2}{n-1}|\nabla f|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}S_{f}\right)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,V\,S_{f}.

(3.12)

It remains to show that
V
cannot vanish on an open set, which follows by virtue of the fact that
V
satisfies an elliptic equation. In fact, by (
3.3
),
u=e^{-\frac{f}{n-1}}V
satisfies
\widetilde{\Delta}u-\widetilde{R}u=0
so by unique continuation, neither
u
nor
V
can vanish on an open set unless
V\equiv 0
. It follows that
S_{f}\equiv 0
.
∎

Since we can identify

-static metrics with static metrics via a conformal transformation, static uniqueness results translate directly over to this setting with the unique

-static metric being conformal to a Schwarzschild metric.

Definition 3.4
.

We call

-Schwarzschild manifold of weighted mass

Theorem
1.4
readily follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\widetilde{g}
and

\widetilde{g}

4.
The weighted Riemannian Penrose inequality

In this section, we prove a weighted Riemannian Penrose inequality with the rigidity case corresponding to the
f
-Schwarzschild family of metrics.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dV_{g})
be a smooth weighted manifold with
\Sigma\subset M
a closed hypersurface, and set

\widetilde{g}=e^{-\frac{2}{n-1}f}\,g.

Then,

(1)

\Sigma
is
f
-minimal in
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dV_{g})
if and only if
\Sigma
is minimal in
(M,\widetilde{g})
;

(2)

the
\widetilde{g}
-area equals the weighted area,

A_{\widetilde{g}}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}dA_{\widetilde{g}}=\int_{\Sigma}e^{-f}\,dA_{g}=A_{f}(\Sigma);

(3)

\Sigma
is
f
-outer-minimising in
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dV_{g})
if and only if
\Sigma
is outer-minimising in
(M,\widetilde{g})
.

Proof.

Letting
\widetilde{g}=e^{-\frac{2f}{n-1}}g
, the mean curvature transforms as

\widetilde{H}=e^{\frac{f}{n-1}}\big(H-\partial_{\nu}f\big)=e^{\frac{f}{n-1}}H_{f},

so (1) holds. The relationship between areas, (2), follows even more straightforwardly from the conformal transformation formula.

Finally, since we have
A_{\widetilde{g}}(\Sigma^{\prime})=A_{f}(\Sigma^{\prime})
for any
\Sigma^{\prime}
enclosing
\Sigma
, and the choice of metric plays no role in whether
\Sigma^{\prime}
encloses
\Sigma
, (3) holds.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
\widetilde{g}=e^{-\frac{2}{n-1}f}g
be the same conformal factor as earlier.
By Theorem
1.2
, the scalar curvature satisfies

\widetilde{R}=e^{\frac{2}{n-1}f}S_{f}\geq 0.

and

\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{g})=\mathfrak{m}_{f}(g).

Since
\Sigma
is
f
-minimal and
f
-outer-minimising in
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dV_{g})
, Lemma
4.1
implies that

\Sigma
is minimal and outer-minimising in
(M,\widetilde{g})
, and that
A_{\widetilde{g}}(\Sigma)=A_{f}(\Sigma)
.
We may therefore apply the Riemannian Penrose inequality
[
7
,
2
,
3
]
to
(M,\widetilde{g})
to obtain

\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{g})\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{A_{\widetilde{g}}(\Sigma)}{\omega_{n-1}}\right)^{\!\frac{n-2}{n-1}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{A_{f}(\Sigma)}{\omega_{n-1}}\right)^{\!\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

Since
\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{g})=\mathfrak{m}_{f}(g)
, (
1.4
) follows.

Furthermore, the spin assumption ensures that in the case of equality,
(M,\widetilde{g})
is isometric to a Schwarzschild exterior

and therefore

is an

-Schwarzschild metric in the sense of Definition

.
∎

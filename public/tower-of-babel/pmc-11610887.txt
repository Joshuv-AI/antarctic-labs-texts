Investigating the complications of frequent pregnancy loss in patients referred to obstetrics and gynecology clinics at King Khalid University Hospital
Authors: Shahad Alotaibi, Fahdah Alshobaili
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL) is a common problem affecting many women. This study aimed to investigate the baseline characteristics of 400 women with RPL and to examine any differences in baseline characteristics of the recruited women with RPL throughout the years.Methods:This study used retrospective data, which included the records of patients with RPL who visited King Khalid University Hospital’s obstetric clinic between 2019 and 2022. The records of 400 patients who met the inclusion criteria were reviewed and analyzed.Results:The mean age of enrolled women was 36.5 ± 5.4 years, and the mean RPL was 3.8 ± 2.5. The study also revealed that there were significant statistical differences in obesity and vitamin D deficiency among women with RPL based on year (P = 0.013 and P = 0.036, respectively), whereas no significant statistical differences were found between women with RPL in terms of age, parity, diabetes, hypothyroidism, hyperprolactinemia, antiphospholipid antibodies (APLs), uterine malformation, or consanguinity.Conclusion:Advanced maternal age, number of previous miscarriages, parity, diabetes, hypothyroidism, APLs, uterine malformations, first-degree consanguinity, and vitamin D deficiency were found to be significant risk factors associated with RPL among women in Saudi Arabia.

Introduction

Recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL) is defined as two or more miscarriages before completing 20 weeks gestation, with up to 50% of RPL incidents having no known etiology.[1] The majority of miscarriages are sporadic, and most of them are caused by hereditary factors that are influenced significantly by maternal age.[1]

Congenital uterine abnormalities are linked to pregnancy loss in the second trimester, as well as other issues such as preterm labor, fetal malpresentation, and increased cesarean delivery rates. Although the relevance of uterine abnormalities in RPL in the first trimester is controversial, uterine anatomy examination is commonly advised.[2345]

RPL is linked to the antiphospholipid syndrome.[678] Although it is generally assumed that antiphospholipid antibodies (APLs) will be found in between 5% and 20% of patients with RPL, the actual reported range is between 8% and 42%.[89]

One of the most disputed aspects of the evaluation of autoimmune pregnancy loss is the detection of relevant APLs.[1] After ruling out maternal anatomic or hormonal abnormalities, as well as paternal and maternal chromosomal factors, the group concluded that testing for APLs is recommended in the case of three or more unexplained spontaneous abortions before the 10th week of pregnancy.[10]

Routine testing for hereditary thrombophilias in women with RPL is not yet suggested.[11] Maternal endocrine problems (e.g., diabetes and thyroid dysfunction) should be investigated and treated according to most experts.[1213] Diabetes that is well-controlled is not a risk factor for RPL. Uncontrolled diabetes, on the contrary, is linked to a higher rate of miscarriage.[1415]

The association between infections and RPL was well studied. In vaginal and cervical cultures and serum from women having sporadic miscarriages, Ureaplasma urealyticum, Mycoplasma hominus, chlamydia, Listeria monocytogenes, Toxoplasma gondii, rubella, cytomegalovirus, herpes virus, and other less common infections have been discovered more frequently.[16]

In addition, cigarette smoking has been associated with an increased incidence of spontaneous pregnancy loss and has been shown to have an unfavorable effect on trophoblastic function.[17] Moreover, obesity has also been linked to a higher incidence of RPL in women who have a spontaneous conception.[18] Other lifestyle behaviors have been linked to a higher risk of miscarriage, including cocaine usage,[19] alcohol use (3–5 drinks per week), and increased caffeine consumption (>3 cups of coffee).[20]

Furthermore, in systematic reviews, vitamin D insufficiency has been associated with obstetric problems and has been identified as a risk factor for gestational diabetes, small for gestational-age newborns, and preeclampsia. Only a few studies have looked at vitamin D levels in women with RPL, and the evidence for a relationship between vitamin D insufficiency and pregnancy loss is equivocal.[21]

As there are few studies investigating RPL and its possible causes in our country, our aim in this study is to determine the etiological profile of RPL in our population, including vitamin D deficiency and consanguinity.

Method

Research design

This study is a retrospective cohort study aimed at determining the etiological profile of RPL in patients visiting the obstetric clinic at King Khalid University Hospital (KKUH), Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Data were collected retrospectively from 2019 to 2022.

Research setting

Research population

This study was conducted in the obstetrics clinic at KKUH, which is one of the largest hospitals in Saudi Arabia. KKUH’s obstetrics clinic is a specialized unit that provides antenatal, perinatal, and postnatal care to pregnant women. The hospital is located in Riyadh, the capital of Saudi Arabia, and is easily accessible to patients from different regions of the country. The obstetrics clinic at KKUH has a large patient volume, with many women seeking care for RPL.

The research setting is well-suited for this study as it provides an excellent opportunity to collect a large sample of patients with RPL and evaluate their etiological profile. The clinic’s specialized services and experienced healthcare providers ensure that the patients receive high-quality care, and the E-SIHI system provides accurate and comprehensive data for the study.

The study population comprised all women with RPL who visited the obstetric clinic at KKUH in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. RPL was defined as the loss of two or more pregnancies.[9] The study included women who met the inclusion criteria of having a history of two or more miscarriages and who visited the obstetric clinic at KKUH. The exclusion criteria included women with one miscarriage. The study aimed to evaluate the etiological profile of RPL in this population.

Research sample and sampling

The sample size for this retrospective-cohort study was calculated using G*power software, which determined that a minimum of 200 patients were required to detect a significant difference in the etiological profile of RPL in the population of interest, with a power of 80% and a significance level of 0.05.

Retrospective data collection was conducted for this study, utilizing the records of patients with RPL who had visited the obstetric clinic at KKUH between 2019 and 2022. The records of 400 patients who met the inclusion criteria were reviewed and analyzed for the study. To implement purposive sampling, the researchers identified the population of interest, which consisted of individuals with RPL who visited the obstetric clinic at KKUH between 2019 and 2022. The inclusion criteria were defined to ensure that the selected participants accurately represented the target population. The sample size of 400 provided adequate power to detect significant differences in the etiological profile of RPL in this population.

Ethical considerations

This study received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of KKUH. The IRB approval number for this study is E-22-6635. The study was conducted in accordance with ethical principles and guidelines, ensuring participant confidentiality and data protection.

Data collection procedure

The study data were collected using a data collection sheet designed by the investigators. The data collection sheet contained information on the study variables, including the age of the patient, RPL, parity, endocrine disorders (diabetes, thyroid, and prolactin), antiphospholipid syndrome, uterine malformations, obesity, smoking, consanguinity, and vitamin D deficiency.

The medical records of 400 patients who met the inclusion criteria were reviewed, and the relevant data were extracted from the electronic health records by using the E-SIHI system (Manufactured by King Saud University Medical City, Saudi Arabia). The data were then recorded on the data collection sheet by the researchers and entered into a computer database for analysis.

To ensure the accuracy of the collected data, the investigators cross-checked the data collection sheets against the electronic health records. Any discrepancies found were corrected, and missing data were obtained by contacting the patients’ healthcare providers.

Throughout the data collection process, patient confidentiality was maintained, and only encrypted patient identification numbers were used. The data collection process was carried out by the investigators who followed strict protocols to ensure the accuracy and completeness of the collected data.

Data analysis

To analyze the gathered data, the study used the Statistical Package of Social Sciences (SPSS) (v. 26, IBM Corp, IL, Chicago, USA). Descriptive statistics such as frequencies, percentages, means, and standard deviations were used to analyze the enrolled women’s baseline characteristics. In addition, the Chi-square test was used to identify differences in the study variables based on year. Moreover, regression analysis and risk estimates were used to identify the risk factors associated with RPL. A statistical significance threshold of α ≤ 0.05 was used in this study.

The results showed that the mean age of the enrolled women was 36.5 ± 5.4 years and the mean RPL was 3.8 ± 2.5. It was noted that 43.3% (n = 173) of participants had vitamin D deficiency.

Discussion

RPL is a major reproductive health problem that affects approximately 1%–2% of couples. The etiology of RPL is multifactorial and includes genetic, environmental, and immunological factors. This study aimed to investigate the risk factors associated with RPL among women in Saudi Arabia. A total of 400 women with a history of RPL were recruited in this study.

The results of this study showed that the mean age of the enrolled women was 36.5 years with a standard deviation of 5.4. This finding is consistent with previous studies that reported an increased risk of RPL with advancing maternal age. A study by Turesheva et al.[22] showed that the risk of RPL increased by 3.0% for every 1-year increase in maternal age. Moreover, it has been suggested that advanced maternal age is associated with an increased risk of chromosomal abnormalities, which are a common cause of RPL.

The mean number of RPLs among enrolled women was 3.8 with a standard deviation of 2.5. This finding is consistent with previous studies that reported that the risk of RPL increases with the number of previous miscarriages. A study by Zhang et al.[23] showed that the risk of RPL was 2.51 times higher in women with a history of two or more previous miscarriages compared to women with no history of miscarriage.

The study also found that 84% of the enrolled women had a parity of 1 or above. This finding is consistent with previous studies that reported that parity is a risk factor for RPL. A study by Cai et al.[24] showed that women with a history of live births were at a higher risk of RPL compared to nulliparous women. The study suggested that the risk of RPL in women with a previous live birth was increased due to immunological factors related to pregnancy.

The study also found that 14.2% of the enrolled women were diabetic, 32% had hypothyroidism, 14.8% had hyperprolactinemia, and 28.5% had APLs. These findings are consistent with previous studies that reported the association of diabetes, hypothyroidism, hyperprolactinemia, and APLs with an increased risk of RPL. A study by Feig et al.[25] showed that women with diabetes were at a higher risk of RPL due to insulin resistance and oxidative stress. A study by Godines-Enriquez et al.[26] showed that hypothyroidism was associated with an increased risk of RPL due to the adverse effects of thyroid hormones on the endometrium. A study by Hirahara et al.[15] showed that women with hyperprolactinemia were at a higher risk of RPL due to the effects of elevated prolactin levels on the endometrium. A study by Di Simone et al.[27] showed that APLs were associated with an increased risk of RPL due to the pro-thrombotic effects of APLs on the placenta.

The study also found that 5.5% of the enrolled women had uterine malformations, and 40.5% were obese. These findings are consistent with previous studies that reported the association of uterine malformations and obesity with an increased risk of RPL. A study by Hosseinirad et al.[28] showed that women with uterine malformations were at a higher risk of RPL due to the adverse effects of uterine anomalies on the implantation of the embryo. In terms of risk factors, the study found that women who were older than 36 years, had a parity of 1 or above, were diabetic, had hypothyroidism, had APLs, had uterine malformation, had first-degree consanguinity, or had vitamin D deficiency were at a higher risk of RPL. These findings are consistent with previous studies that have identified these risk factors for RPL. However, the study did not find a significant association between RPL and hyperprolactinemia or second-degree consanguinity, which has been reported as a risk factor in some studies. The study’s finding of a significant association between vitamin D deficiency and RPL is consistent with some previous studies.[2930]

One limitation of the study is that it was conducted in a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other populations. In addition, the study did not investigate other potential risk factors for RPL, such as lifestyle factors or environmental exposures. Future studies could investigate these factors to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the risk factors for RPL.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides important insights into the baseline characteristics and risk factors for RPL in a population of women in Saudi Arabia. The findings suggest that older age, parity of 1 or above, diabetes, hypothyroidism, APL, uterine malformation, first-degree consanguinity, obesity, and vitamin D deficiency are associated with a higher risk of RPL. These findings are consistent with previous studies, highlighting the need for further research into the prevention and management of RPL. It is recommended that healthcare providers should educate women about the risk factors associated with RPL, particularly those who are older than 36 years, have a parity of 1 or above, are diabetic, have hypothyroidism, have APLs, have uterine malformation, have first-degree consanguinity, or have vitamin D deficiency. Henceforth, given the multifactorial nature of RPL, future studies should investigate additional risk factors for RPL, such as lifestyle factors or environmental exposures. This could lead to the development of more comprehensive guidelines for the prevention and management of RPL.

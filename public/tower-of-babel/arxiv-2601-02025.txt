Modified weighted power variations of the Hermite process and applications to integrated volatility
A. Ayache, L. Loosveldt, C. A. Tudor

Abstract
We study the asymptotic behaviour of modified weighted power variations of the Hermite process of arbitrary order. By selecting suitable “good” increments and exploiting their decomposition into dominant independent components, we establish a central limit theorem for weighted p p -variations using tools from Stein–Malliavin calculus. Our results extend previous works on modified quadratic and wavelet-based variations to general powers and to weighted settings, with explicit bounds in Wasserstein distance. We further apply these limit theorems to construct asymptotically Gaussian estimators of integrated volatility in Hermite-driven models, thereby extending fBm-based methods to non-Gaussian settings. The last part of our work contains numerical simulations which illustrate the practical performance of the proposed estimators.



Keywords
: Hermite process, multiple Wiener-Itô integrals, Stein-Malliavin calculus, asymptotic normality, central limit theorem, integrated volatility estimation, strong consistency

Introduction

The study of Hermite and related processes has developed intensively in the recent decades. These stochastic processes enjoy nice properties: they are self-similar, have stationary increments and exhibit long-range dependence. These characteristics give them an important potential for practical applications. The class of Hermite processes includes the fractional Brownian motion which is the only Gaussian process in this class. We refer to the monographs
[
26
]
and
[
29
]
for a detailed exposition on Hermite and related processes.

One of the important issues in the analysis of Hermite processes is the statistical estimation of its self-similarity index (or Hurst parameter), which characterizes many of its properties. A series of papers (
[
11
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
) has proposed estimators for the Hurst index of the Hermite processes, by using power variations or wavelet-based methods. A common fact for all these estimators is that, although they are consistent, they are not asymptotically Gaussian and this constitutes a serious drawback for statistical estimation. A similar phenomenon can be noticed for other parameter estimators in Hermite-driven models (see e.g.
[
21
]
,
[
28
]
).

To overcome the asymptotic non-normality, a new strategy has been employed in
[
5
]
, by following an idea from
[
2
]
. Namely, the reference
[
5
]
has defined a so-called modified quadratic variation of the Hermite process (of an arbitrary order), by using only some well-chosen increments of it. Each of these special increments can be split into a dominant part and a negligible one, moreover the dominant parts constitute a sequence of independent random variables. Thanks to these nice properties,
[
5
]
has managed to show that the modified quadratic variation satisfies a Central Limit Theorem, and then has constructed from it a new strongly consistent estimator for the Hurst parameter of the Hermite processes which, compared to other estimators, offers the significant advantage to be asymptotically Gaussian. A related asymptotically Gaussian estimator based on the modified wavelet variations of the Hermite process has been defined and analyzed in
[
17
]
.

In this work, our purpose is to generalize the results obtained in
[
5
]
and
[
17
]
by analyzing the limit behavior of the weighted modified
p
-variations of the Hermite process
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
for any integer order
p\geq 2
, i.e. the sequence
(U_{N,p}(Z),N\geq 1)
given by (
36
). The weight
h
in (
36
) is a deterministic function with suitable properties. Via a similar idea of choosing “good” increments of the Hermite process and by using the techniques of the Stein-Malliavin calculus, we prove that the weighted modified power variation of the Hermite process converges to a constant times the Wiener integral of the weight function
h
with respect to a Brownian motion independent of the underlying Hermite process. We start by considering the case of the (non-weighted) modified power variation (i.e.
h
is identically equal to one) and then we extend the result to the weighted case by using, among others, some new findings concerning the Wasserstein distance.

We apply our result to estimate the integrated volatility in a simple model with Hermite noise. The estimation of the integrated volatility via power variations constitutes a topic of interest in financial mathematics. We mention the articles
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[

or
[

We organize our paper as follows. Section 2 contains preliminaries on Hermite processes, including their representation, basic properties, and the definition of the special increments that play a central role in our analysis. This section also contains some establishes key independence and moment estimates for these increments. In Section 3 we include the proof of some properties of strongly independent random variables relying on tools from Wiener chaos. We then investigate, in Section 4, the modified (non-weighted) power variations, proving a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem through a combination of Stein–Malliavin arguments and independence properties. The subsequent Section 5 extends these results to the weighted setting, deriving quantitative Wasserstein bounds and introducing a general limit theorem for weighted modified power variations. We also apply the theoretical results to integrated volatility estimation in Hermite-driven models and present numerical simulations that validate the theoretical findings and illustrate the performance of the proposed estimators. Section 6 is the Appendix where we present the basic elements of the Malliavin calculus.

Preliminaries

In this section we present various facts related to the Hermite process. The first part contains its definition and basic properties and in the second part we describe some special increments of this stochastic process, which play a crucial role in our construction.

2.1
The Hermite process and its special increments

We denote by
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
the Hermite process of order

and with self-similarity index

Z_{t}=d(q,H)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}f_{u}(y_{1},...,y_{q})du\right)dB(y_{1})...dB(y_{q}),

(2)

and

for every

. The stochastic process

\mathbf{E}Z_{t}Z_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right),\mbox{ for every }s,t\geq 0.

The increments of

satisfy, for

Hence, for every fixed

, sample paths of the Hermite process are, on each compact subinterval of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\delta

In the sequel we will use the Wiener integral with respect to the Hermite process. Let

be the space of measurable functions

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(u)|\cdot|f(v)|\cdot|u-v|^{2H-2}dudv<\infty.

If

with respect to the Hermite process

(also called the Wiener-Hermite integral), denoted by

We refer to e.g. the survey
[

]
for the details of this construction. The above object is well-defined as a random variable in

and it satisfies the following isometry property: if

, then

\mathbf{E}\Bigg(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(u)dZ_{u}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(u)dZ_{u}\right)\Bigg)=H(2H-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(u)g(v)|u-v|^{2H-2}dudv.

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}1_{\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}-\frac{2^{[N^{\beta}]}}{2^{N}}+2^{-N},\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}\right)}^{\otimes q}\left(\int_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}^{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}f_{u}(y_{1},...,y_{q})du\right)dB(y_{1})...dB(y_{q})

\displaystyle=^{(d)}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}dB(y_{1})...dB(y_{q})\mathbbm{1}_{\left(-\frac{2^{[N^{\beta}]}}{2^{N}},0\right)}^{\otimes q}(y_{1},...,y_{q})

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}^{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}f_{u}(y_{1}+\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N},...,y_{q}+\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N})du\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left(-\frac{2^{[N^{\beta}]}}{2^{N}},0\right)}^{\otimes q}(y_{1},...,y_{q})\left(\int_{-2^{-N}}^{0}f_{u}(y_{1},...,y_{q})du\right)dB(y_{1})...dB(y_{q}),

and the last quantity is independent of
l=1,...,2^{[N^{\gamma}]}
. A similar argument can be used to show point 2.

3
Strong independence

Let us recall the notion of strong independence for random variables. It will be used intensively in the next section in order to prove our main results. Let
F
and
G
be two random variables that can be expanded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
F=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}(f_{n})
and
G=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}(g_{n})
where
f_{n},g_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are symmetric functions for every
n\geq 1
. We say that
F
and
G
are strongly independent if
I_{m}(f_{m})
and
I_{n}(g_{n})
are independent random variables for every
m,n\geq 1
. Using their joint characteristic function, it can easily be shown that
F
and
G
are independent (in the usual sense), as soon as they are strongly independent see also Proposition 8 in
[
31
]
).

Lemma 3

The two positive integers
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are arbitrary. Let
F_{1}=I_{q_{1}}(f_{1})
and
F_{2}=I_{q_{2}}(f_{2})
with
f_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{q_{1}})
and
f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{q_{2}})
. Assume that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are independent. Then for any integers
a,b\geq 1
,
F_{1}^{a}
and
F_{2}^{b}
are strongly independent.

Proof:
Without any restriction, one can suppose that the kernel functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, associated with
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, belong to
L^{2}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{q_{1}})
and
L^{2}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{q_{2}})
respectively; one mentions in passing that, for any integer
q\geq 0
, the closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{q})
formed by the symmetric functions is denoted by
L^{2}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{q})
. Thus, in view of the criterion for the independence of multiple stochastic integrals in
[
31
]
, the independence property of the random variables
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
can be reformulated as

f_{1}\otimes_{1}f_{2}=0\mbox{ almost everywhere on }\mathbb{R}^{q_{1}+q_{2}-2}.

(9)

First we study the case
a=1
. We are going to prove, by induction on the integer
b\geq 1
, that the random variables
F_{1}
and
F_{2}^{b}
are strongly independent. For
b=1
, the claim is true by hypothesis. In the sequel, we assume, for any integer
b\geq 1
, that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}^{b}
are strongly independent and we show that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}^{b+1}
are strongly independent as well. We know from the product formula for multiple Wiener-Itô integrals (
114
) that
F_{2}^{b}
admits the following chaos expansion:

F_{2}^{b}=\sum_{k=0}^{bq_{2}}I_{k}(h_{2,k})\mbox{ with }h_{2,k}\in L^{2}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{k}).

(10)

Then, the induction hypothesis implies, for all
k=1,...,bq_{2}
, that

f_{1}\otimes_{1}h_{2,k}=0\mbox{ almost everywhere on }\mathbb{R}^{q_{1}+k-2}.

(11)

Notice that, one can derive from (
10
) and the product formula for multiple Wiener-Itô integrals that

\displaystyle F_{2}^{b+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{bq_{2}}I_{k}(h_{2,k})I_{q_{2}}(f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{bq_{2}}\sum_{r=0}^{q_{2}\wedge k}r!\binom{k}{r}\binom{q_{2}}{r}I_{k+q_{2}-2r}\left(h_{2,k}\otimes_{r}f_{2}\right).

It then suffices to prove that, for every
k=0,1,...,bq_{2}
and
r=0,1,...,q_{2}\wedge k
such that
k+q_{2}>2r
, the random variables
F_{1}=I_{q_{1}}(f_{1})
and
I_{k+q_{2}-2r}\left(h_{2,k}\otimes_{r}f_{2}\right)
are independent. This is equivalent to

(h_{2,k}\tilde{\otimes}_{r}f_{2})\otimes_{1}f_{1}=0\mbox{ almost everywhere on }\mathbb{R}^{q_{1}+q_{2}+k-2r-2},

(12)

where
h_{2,k}\tilde{\otimes}_{r}f_{2}
denotes the symmetrization of
h_{2,k}\otimes_{r}f_{2}
.
We have, by the definition of the contraction,

\displaystyle\left((h_{2,k}\tilde{\otimes}_{r}f_{2})\otimes_{1}f_{1}\right)(t_{1},...,t_{k+q_{2}+q_{1}-2r-2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx(h_{2,k}\tilde{\otimes}_{r}f_{2})(t_{1},...,t_{k+q_{2}-2r-1},x)f_{1}(t_{k+q_{2}-2r},...,t_{k+q_{2}-2r+q_{1}-2},x).

(13)

We notice that for all
n\geq 2
and for any function
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, its symmetrization
\tilde{g}
, can be written, for every
(t_{1},...,t_{n-1},x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as

\tilde{g}(t_{1},...,t_{n-1},x)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(t_{\sigma(1)},...,t_{\sigma(i-1)},x,t_{\sigma(i)},...,t_{\sigma(n-1)}),

where
S_{n-1}
is the set of the permutations of
\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Thus, by using the above formula in (
13
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left((h_{2,k}\tilde{\otimes}f_{2})\otimes_{1}f_{1}\right)(t_{1},...,t_{k+q_{2}+q_{1}-2r-2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k+q_{2}-2r)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k+q_{2}-2r-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{k+q_{2}-2r}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx

\displaystyle\times(h_{2,k}\otimes_{r}f_{2})(t_{\sigma(1)},...,t_{\sigma(i-1)},x,t_{\sigma(i)},...,t_{\sigma(k+q_{2}-2r-1)})

\displaystyle\times f_{1}(t_{k+q_{2}-2r},...,t_{k+q_{2}-2r+q_{1}-2},x).

Then, it turns out that in order to get the conclusion (
12
), it suffices to show that, for every
k=0,1,...,bq_{2}
and
r=0,1,...,q_{2}\wedge k
satisfying
k+q_{2}>2r
, and for all
\sigma\in S_{k+q_{2}-2r-1}
and
i=1,...,k+q_{2}-2r
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx(h_{2,k}\otimes_{r}f_{2})(t_{\sigma(1)},...,t_{\sigma(i-1)},x,t_{\sigma(i)},...,t_{\sigma(k+q_{2}-2r-1)})

\displaystyle\times f_{1}(t_{k+q_{2}-2r},...,t_{k+q_{2}-2r+q_{1}-2},x)=0

(14)

almost everywhere with respect to
t_{1},...,t_{k+q_{2}-2r+q_{1}-2}
.
Assume
k-r\leq i-1
. Then, using Fubini Theorem, the left-hand side of (
14
) writes

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}d\,\overline{y}\times h_{2,k}(t_{\sigma(1)},...,t_{\sigma(k-r)},\overline{y})

\displaystyle\times f_{2}(\overline{y},t_{\sigma(k-r+1)},...,t_{\sigma(i-1)},x,t_{\sigma(i)},...,t_{\sigma(k+q_{2}-2r-1)})

\displaystyle\times f_{1}(t_{k+q_{2}-2r},...,t_{k+q_{2}-2r+q_{1}-2},x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}d\,\overline{y}\times h_{2,k}(t_{\sigma(1)},...,t_{\sigma(k)},\overline{y})

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\times f_{2}(\overline{y},t_{\sigma(k+1)},...,t_{\sigma(i-1)},x,t_{\sigma(i)},...,t_{\sigma(k+q_{2}-2r-1)})

\displaystyle\times f_{1}(t_{k+q_{2}-2r},...,t_{k+q_{2}-2r+q_{1}-2},x),

and the last integral on
\mathbb{R}
vanishes due to (
9
).
Assume
k-r\geq i
. Then, using Fubini Theorem, the left-hand side of (
14
) can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}d\,\overline{y}\times f_{2}(t_{\sigma(k-r)},....,t_{\sigma(k+q_{2}-2r-1)},\overline{y})

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\times h_{2,k}(\overline{y},t_{\sigma(1)},...,t_{\sigma(i-1)},x,t_{\sigma(i)},...,t_{\sigma(k-r-1)})f_{1}(t_{k+q_{2}-2r},...,t_{k+q_{2}-2r+q_{1}-2},x)

and again the integral over
\mathbb{R}
vanishes because of (
11
).

Let us now study the general case where the integer
a\geq 1
is arbitrary. We are going to prove, by induction on
a
, that
F_{1}^{a}
and
F_{2}^{b}
are strongly independent. We already have shown that the claim is true when
a=1
. Assume
F_{1}^{a}
and
F_{2}^{b}
are strongly independent and let us show that
F_{1}^{a+1}
and
F_{2}^{b}
satisfy this same strong independence property.

It follows from the product formula for multiple Wiener-Itô integrals (
114
) that
F_{1}^{a}
admits the following chaos expansion:

F_{1}^{a}=\sum_{k=0}^{aq_{1}}I_{k}(h_{1,k})\mbox{ with }h_{1,k}\in L^{2}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{k}).

(15)

Then, in view of the induction hypothesis, we have, for all integers
k\geq 1
and
l\geq 1
,

h_{1,k}\otimes_{1}h_{2,l}=0\mbox{ almost everywhere on }\mathbb{R}^{k+l-2}.

(16)

We also have from the case
a=1
, for every integer
l\geq 1
,

f_{1}\otimes_{1}h_{2,l}=0\mbox{ almost everywhere on }\mathbb{R}^{q_{1}+l-2}.

(17)

Moreover, using (
15
) and the product formula (
114
), we get that

F_{1}^{a+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{aq_{1}}\sum_{r=0}^{q_{1}\wedge k}r!\binom{q_{1}}{r}\binom{k}{r}I_{k+q_{1}-2r}(h_{1,k}\otimes_{r}f_{1}).

It then suffices to prove that, for all
l=1,...,bq_{2}
,
k=0,1,...,bq_{1}
and
r=0,1,...,q_{1}\wedge k
such that
k+q_{1}-2r>0
, the two random variables
I_{l}(h_{2,l})
and
I_{k+q_{1}-2r}(h_{1,k}\otimes_{r}f_{1})
are independent, which amounts to prove that

(h_{1,k}\tilde{\otimes}_{r}f_{1})\otimes_{1}h_{2,l}=0\mbox{ almost everywhere on }\mathbb{R}^{k+q_{1}-2r+l-2}.

This follows exactly as in the case
a=1
, based on the hypotheses (
16
) and (
17
).

4
The modified power variation of the Hermite process

Let
Z=(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
be a Hermite process and
p\geq 2
an arbitrary integer. The goal of this section is to analyse the asymptotic behavior of the modified power variation of
Z
denoted by
S_{N,p}(Z)
. The difference between
S_{N,p}(Z)
and usual power variation is that
S_{N,p}(Z)
is defined by only considering the special increments of
Z
introduced in Section
2.2
. Namely, for each integer
N\geq 1
, it is defined as the following renormalized sum of the latter increments raised to the power
p
:

S_{N,p}(Z)=\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(\Delta Z_{l,N}\right)^{p}.

(18)

We first show that the modified power variation satisfies a law of large numbers.

Proposition 2

For all integer
p\geq 2
, one sets
\mu_{p}=\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p}
. Let
S_{N,p}
be given by (
18
). Then

S_{N,p}(Z)\to_{N\to\infty}\mu_{p}\mbox{ in }L^{1}(\Omega).

Proof:
We use the decomposition (
5
), with
\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N}
and
\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N}
given by (
6
) and (
7
), respectively. In this way, we can write,

\displaystyle(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p}\binom{p}{j}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{j}(\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p-j}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{j}(\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p-j},

and thus

\displaystyle S_{N,p}(Z)-\mu_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left((\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}-2^{-pHN}\mu_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left((\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{N,p}^{(1)}(Z)+(R_{N,p}(Z)-\mathbf{E}R_{N,p}(Z)),

with

S_{N,p}^{(1)}(Z)=\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)

and

R_{N,p}(Z)=\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{j}(\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p-j}.

(20)

We first deal with the rest term
R_{N,p}(Z)
. It follows from Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, the estimates in Lemmas
1
and
2
, and (
116
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}|R_{N,p}(Z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}\left(\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{2j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{E}(\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{2(p-j)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{[N^{\beta}]\frac{H-1}{q}}.

Consequently,
R_{N,p}(Z)
converges to zero in
L^{1}(\Omega)
as
N\to\infty
. Next, we deal with the summand
S_{N,p}^{(1)}(Z)
and we show that it converges to zero in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
N\to\infty
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(S_{N,p}^{(1)}(Z)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{2[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l^{\prime},N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l^{\prime},N})^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{2[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{2[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}},

where we used the independence property from Proposition
1
, point 1. and the estimate (
8
) in Lemma
2
. We obtain

\mathbf{E}|S_{N,p}(Z)-\mu_{p}|\leq C\left(2^{[N^{\beta}]\frac{H-1}{q}}+2^{-\frac{[N^{\gamma}]}{2}}\right)\leq C2^{-\frac{[N^{\gamma}]}{2}}.

The next step is to show that, after a proper renormalization, the power variation sequence (
18
) satisfies a Central Limit Theorem. For any integer number
p\geq 2
, let us introduce the sequence
(V_{N,p}(Z),N\geq 1)
given by

\displaystyle V_{N,p}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(S_{N,p}(Z)-\mu_{p}\right)

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{pHN}}{\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left[(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}\right].

By (
19
),

\displaystyle V_{N,p}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{pHN}}{\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right]

(23)

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{pHN}}{\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{j}(\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p-j}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{j}(\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p-j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{N,p}^{(1)}+\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}(R_{N,p}(Z)-\mathbf{E}R_{N,p}(Z)),

with

V_{N,p}^{(1)}=\frac{2^{pHN}}{\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right]

(24)

and
R_{N,p}(Z)
given by (
20
). By (
21
),

\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}|R_{N,p}(Z)-\mathbf{E}R_{N,p}(Z)|\leq C2^{N^{\beta}\frac{H-1}{q}+\frac{N^{\gamma}}{2}}.

(25)

We will now focus on the asymptotic behaviour of the sequence
\left(V_{N,p}^{(1)},N\geq 1\right)
. We will prove that this sequence satisfies a Central Limit Theorem. We start by the study of the asymptotic behavior of the second moment.

Lemma 5

Let
p\geq 2
be an integer number. Consider the sequence
\left(V_{N,p}^{(1)},N\geq 1\right)
given by (
24
). Then

\left|\mathbf{E}(V_{N,p}^{(1)})^{2}-m_{p}\right|\leq C2^{[N^{\beta}]\frac{H-1}{q}},

(26)

where

m_{p}={\rm Var}(Z_{1}^{p})=\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{2p}-(\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p})^{2}=\mu_{2p}-\mu_{p}^{2}.

(27)

In particular,

\mathbf{E}(V_{N,p}^{(1)})^{2}\to_{N\to\infty}m_{p}.

Proof:
For
N\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
, we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(V_{N,p}^{(1)})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l,k=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right]\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{k,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{k,N})^{p}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right]^{2},

where we used the fact that
\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N},l=1,...,2^{[N^{\gamma}]}
are independent (Proposition
1
, point 1.). By using now the fact that the random variables
\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N},l=1,...,2^{[N^{\gamma}]}
are identically distributed, we obtain

\mathbf{E}(V_{N,p}^{(1)})^{2}=2^{2pHN}\mathbf{E}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}\right]^{2}.

(28)

We next write

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(V_{N,p}^{(1)})^{2}-m_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{2pHN}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{2p}-\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{2p}

(29)

\displaystyle-\left[\left(2^{pHN}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}\right)^{2}-(\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p})^{2}\right].

We can derive from Lemma
2
that

\left|2^{2pHN}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{2p}-\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{2p}\right|\leq C_{2p}\ 2^{[N^{\beta}]\frac{H-1}{q}}.

(30)

Moreover, using again Lemma
2
and the inequality

|2^{pHN}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}+\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p}|\leq|2^{pHN}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p}|+2\mathbf{E}|Z_{1}|^{p},

we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\left(2^{pHN}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}\right)^{2}-(\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p})^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|2^{pHN}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p}|\times|2^{pHN}\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}+\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}^{\prime}\ 2^{[N^{\beta}]\frac{H-1}{q}}.

(31)

Finally, putting together (
29
), (
30
) and (
4
), it follows that (
26
) holds.

Now, we need to define some distances between the probability distributions of random variables. We refer to
[
20
]
, Appendix C, for more details. Usually, the distance between the laws of two real-valued random variables
F
and
G
is defined as

d(F,G)=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}h(F)-\mathbf{E}h(G)\right|,

(32)

where
\mathcal{A}
is a class of functions satisfying
h(F),h(G)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
for every
h\in\mathcal{A}
. When
\mathcal{A}
is the set of Lipschitz continuous functions
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|h\|_{Lip}\leq 1
, where

\|h\|_{Lip}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R},x\not=y}\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|}{|x-y|},

then (
32
) gives the Wasserstein distance. When
\mathcal{A}
is the set of indicator functions
\{\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,z]},\\
z\in\mathbb{R}\}
then (
32
) gives the Kolmogorov distance, for
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathbbm{1}_{B},B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\}
we have the total variation distance, while the choice of
\mathcal{A}
to be the class of functions
h
with
\|h\|_{Lip}+\|h\|_{\infty}<\infty
leads to the Fortet-Mourier distance.

Let us recall a classical result from Stein-Malliavin calculus (see Theorem 5.1.3 and Remark 5.1.4 in
[
20
]
). Below,
d
could be any of the above distances (Kolmogorov, Total variation, Wasserstein or Fortet-Mourier). We refer to the Appendix for the definition of the Malliavin derivative
D
and of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator
L
with respect to an isonormal process
(W(h),h\in\mathcal{H})
, where
{\mathcal{H}}
is a real separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Notice that in our present article, we have
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 1

Let
F
be a random variable belonging to a finite sum of Wiener chaoses such that
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}F=0
and
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}F^{2}=\sigma^{2}
. Let
\gamma>0
. Then

d(F,N(0,\gamma^{2}))\leq C\left(\sqrt{{\rm Var}\left(\langle DF,D(-L)^{-1}F\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right)}+|\sigma^{2}-\gamma^{2}|\right).

By applying Theorem
1
, we will show that the sequence given by (
24
) converges in distribution to a Gaussian law and we also estimate the Wasserstein distance corresponding to this limit theorem.

Proposition 3

Consider the sequence
(V_{N,p}^{(1)},N\geq 1)
given by (
24
). Then

V_{N,p}^{(1)}\to_{N\to\infty}^{(d)}N(0,m_{p})

and for
N
large enough,

d\left(V_{N,p}^{(1)},N(0,m_{p})\right)\leq C2^{-\frac{[N^{\gamma}]}{2}}.

Proof:
Throughout the proof we assume that
N\geq 2^{(1-\beta)^{-1}}
; there is no restriction to make this assumption. Let us evaluate the quantity
{\rm Var}\left(\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)
. We have

DV_{N,p}^{(1)}=\frac{2^{pHN}}{\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}D\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right]\

and

D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}=\frac{2^{pHN}}{\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}D(-L)^{-1}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right].

We know from Lemma
3
that, for all
k,l=1,...,2^{[N^{\gamma}]}
with
k\not=l
, the random variables
(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}
and
(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{k,N})^{p}
are strongly independent. Then

\displaystyle\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l,k=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\langle D\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right],D(-L)^{-1}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{k,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{k,N})^{p}\right]\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\langle D\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right],D(-L)^{-1}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right]\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

where we applied Lemma
4
, point 1. Thus

\displaystyle\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}(F_{l}-\mathbf{E}F_{l}),

where we used the notation

F_{l}=\langle D\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right],D(-L)^{-1}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right]\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

By point 2. in Lemma
4
, the random variables
F_{l},l=1,...,2^{[N^{\gamma}]}
are pairwise independent. Consequently,

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

(33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}-\mathbf{E}\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{4pHN}}{2^{2[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l,k=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}\left((F_{l}-\mathbf{E}F_{l})(F_{k}-\mathbf{E}F_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{4pHN}}{2^{2[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}\left((F_{l}-\mathbf{E}F_{l})\right)^{2}.

Let us show that for every
l=1,...,2^{[N^{\gamma}]}
,

\mathbf{E}F_{l}^{2}\leq C2^{-4pHN}.

(34)

Using Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality twice, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}F_{l}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbf{E}\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbf{E}\|DV_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\mathbf{E}\|DV_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{E}\|D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{1}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, since
V_{N,p}^{(1)}
is a random variable belonging to a finite sum of Wiener chaoses,

\mathbf{E}\|DV_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\mathbf{E}|V_{N,p}^{(1)}|^{2}\leq C2^{-2pHN},

and

\mathbf{E}\|D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\mathbf{E}|V_{N,p}^{(1)}|^{2}\leq C2^{-2pHN}.

Moreover, since
\|DV_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\|D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
are also random variables in a finite sum of Wiener chaoses, we have via the hypercontractivity property (
116
)

\mathbf{E}\|DV_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\mathbf{E}\|DV_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{2}\leq C2^{-4pHN}

and

\mathbf{E}\|D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\mathbf{E}\|D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{2}\leq C2^{-4pHN}.

So, the bound (
34
) is proven. By plugging this inequality into (

{\rm Var}\left(\langle DV_{N,p}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}V_{N,p}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\leq C2^{-[N^{\gamma}]}.

(35)

Finally, putting together (
26
), (
35
) and Theorem
1
we obtain the proposition.

-variation of the Hermite process.

5

In this part, the purpose is to extend the results from the previous section, by looking to the asymptotic behavior of the weighted (modified) power variation of the Hermite process. In the second part of this section, we also apply the results to the volatility estimation in a simple model with Hermite noise.

5.1
Weighted power variation of the Hermite process

We start by introducing the weighted modified power variation of the Hermite process. Let

where, for each

,

is the special increment of the Hermite process given by (
4
). We study the limit behavior in distribution, as

Theorem 3

Let

be an integer number and let

The proof of Theorem

mainly relies on the following general lemma on Wasserstein distance.

Lemma 6

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary measurable bounded deterministic function and
\|h\|_{\infty}=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}}|h(v)|
. Let
U
,
V
,
X
and
Y
be four real-valued random variables of
L^{2}(\Omega)
such that the
2
-dimensional random vectors
(U,V)
and
(X,Y)
are independent. Then, one has

d_{W}\big(U+h(V)X,U+h(V)Y\big)\leq\|h\|_{\infty}d_{W}(X,Y).

(39)

Proof:
Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{W}
the set of the Lipschitz continuous functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|f\|_{Lip}\leq 1
. Observe that for any such
f
, one has, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
|f(z)|\leq|z|+|f(0)|
. the latter fact combined with the assumptions
U,V,X,Y\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\|h\|_{\infty}<\infty
imply that the expectation
\mathbf{E}\big(f(U+h(V)X)-f(U+h(V)Y)\big)
is well-defined and finite. Next one denotes
\mathbf{P}_{U,V}
and
\mathbf{P}_{X,Y}
the probability distributions of the two random vectors
(U,V)
and
(X,Y)
. Using the fact that these two random vectors are independent and Fubini Theorem one gets that

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbf{E}\big(f(U+h(V)X)-f(U+h(V)Y)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{P}_{U,V}(u,v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(f(u+h(v)x)-f(u+h(v)y)\Big)d\mathbf{P}_{X,Y}(x,y)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{P}_{U,V}(u,v)\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(f(u+h(v)x)-f(u+h(v)y)\Big)d\mathbf{P}_{X,Y}(x,y)\Big|

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{P}_{U,V}(u,v)\Big|\mathbf{E}\Big(f(u+h(v)X)-f(u+h(v)Y)\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\|h\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{P}_{U,V}(u,v)\big|\mathbf{E}\big(g_{u,v}(X)-g_{u,v}(Y)\big)\big|,

(40)

where, for each fixed
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
g_{u,v}
denotes the function of
\mathcal{A}_{W}
defined, for every

, as

g_{u,v}(z)=\|h\|_{\infty}^{-1}f(u+h(v)z)
. Then, one can derive (
5.1
) and (
32
) with
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{W}
that

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbf{E}\big(f(U+h(V)X)-f(U+h(V)Y)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\mathbf{P}_{U,V}(u,v)d_{W}(X,Y)=\|h\|_{\infty}d_{W}(X,Y),

and consequently that

\displaystyle d_{W}\big(U+h(V)X,U+h(V)Y\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}_{W}}\Big|\mathbf{E}\big(f(U+h(V)X)-f(U+h(V)Y)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{\infty}d_{W}(X,Y).

Proof of Theorem
3
:
Throughout the proof, we fix the arbitrary integer
N\geq 2^{(1-\beta)^{-1}}
. Let
U_{N}=U_{N,p}(Z)
and
h_{N}:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the step function which is, for all

on the interval

\displaystyle d_{W}(U_{N},\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathcal{I}(h))

\displaystyle\leq d_{W}(U_{N},\widetilde{U}_{N})+d_{W}(\widetilde{U}_{N},\widetilde{B}_{N})+d_{W}(\widetilde{B}_{N},\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathcal{I}(h_{N}))+d_{W}(\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathcal{I}(h_{N}),\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathcal{I}(h)).

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}|U_{N}-\widetilde{U}_{N}|+\mathbf{E}|\widetilde{U}_{N}-\widetilde{B}_{N}|+d_{W}(\widetilde{B}_{N},\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathcal{I}(h_{N}))+\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathbf{E}|\mathcal{I}(h_{N})-\mathcal{I}(h)|.

(41)

In the above inequality we have used the following notations:

\widetilde{U}_{N}=\frac{2^{pHN}}{\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right);

(42)

(43)

Next, observe that it follows from (

L\in\{1,\ldots 2^{[N^{\nu}]}\}
, the two random variables
\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(L)}
and
\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\Delta^{(\nu)}W_{L-1,N}
are independent, it turns out that the random vectors
\left(\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\Delta^{(\nu)}W_{L-1,N}\,,\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(L)}\right)
,
L\in\{1,\ldots 2^{[N^{\nu}]}\}
, are identically distributed. Then, one can derive from (
58
) and the

equality
\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\Delta^{(\nu)}W_{0,N}=\sqrt{m_{p}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\nu}]}}}
that

d_{W}(\widetilde{B}_{N},\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathcal{I}(h_{N}))\leq\|h\|_{\infty}2^{[N^{\nu}]}d_{W}\left(\sqrt{m_{p}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\nu}]}}}\,,\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)}\right).

(59)

Since
\sqrt{m_{p}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\nu}]}}}
is a centred Gaussian random variable, in order to bound the Wassertein distance
d_{W}\left(\sqrt{m_{p}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\nu}]}}}\,,\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)}\right)
we will make use of the fundamental Theorem
1
. Let us first bound
\Big|{\rm Var}\big(\sqrt{m_{p}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\nu}]}}}\big)-{\rm Var}\big(\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)}\big)\Big|
. One clearly has that

{\rm Var}\big(\sqrt{m_{p}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\nu}]}}}\big)=m_{p}\,2^{-[N^{\nu}]}.

(60)

Moreover, in view of (
44
) and (
45
), by using the fact that the centred random variables
(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}
,
l\in\{1,...,\delta(N)\}
, are independent and identically distributed, one obtains that

{\rm Var}(\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)})=\mathbf{E}(\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)})^{2}=\frac{2^{2pHN}\delta(N)}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}\left[(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{1,N})^{p}\right]^{2}=2^{-[N^{\nu}]}\mathbf{E}(V_{N,p}^{(1)})^{2},

(61)

where the last equality follows from (
28
). Then combining (
60
) and (
61
) with (
26
), one gets that

\Big|{\rm Var}\big(\sqrt{m_{p}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\nu}]}}}\big)-{\rm Var}\big(\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)}\big)\Big|\leq C2^{-[N^{\nu}]+[N^{\beta}]\frac{H-1}{q}}.

(62)

On the other hand, by using (
44
) and (
45
) and the same arguments which have allowed to obtain (
35
), it can be shown that

{\rm Var}\left(\langle D\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)},D(-L)^{-1}\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\leq C2^{-[N^{\nu}]-[N^{\gamma}]}.

(63)

Then, it results from (
62
), (
63
) and Theorem
1
that

d_{W}\left(\sqrt{m_{p}}\,W_{\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\nu}]}}}\,,\widetilde{v}_{N}^{(1)}\right)\leq C2^{-[N^{\nu}]-[N^{\gamma}]},

which, in view of (
59
), implies that

d_{W}(\widetilde{B}_{N},\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathcal{I}(h_{N}))\leq C2^{-[N^{\gamma}]}.

(64)

Finally, since
0<\alpha<1
and
0<\nu<\gamma\leq\beta<1
, putting together (
5.1
), (
48
), (
51
), (
52
) and (
64
), one obtains (
38
).

Remark 2

A careful inspection of the proof of Theorem
3
shows that, under the weaker condition that
h
is a continuous function on
[0,1]
(which may not be a Hölder function), one still has that
d_{W}(U_{N,p}(Z),\sqrt{m_{p}}\,\mathcal{I}(h))\leq C\rho_{h}(2^{-N^{\nu}})
, where the non-negative, non-decreasing continuous function
\rho_{h}
is the uniform modulus of continuity of
h
, defined, for each

.

5.2
Volatility estimation

Let
h:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function satisfying (
37
). We consider the stochastic process
(X_{t},t\in[0,1])
defined as

X_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}h(s)dZ_{s},

(65)

where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is deterministic and the stochastic integral with respect to
Z
in (
65
) is a Wiener-Hermite integral; recall that a brief presentation of such a stochastic integral has been given in Section
2.1
. Observe that the function
h
can be viewed as a measurable real-valued function defined on
\mathbb{R}
which vanishes outside of the interval
[0,1]
and satisfies, for every
t\in[0,1]
,
h\mathbbm{1}_{(0,t)}\in|\mathcal{H}|
; thus, one knows from Section
2.1
that the Hermite-Wiener integral
\int_{0}^{t}h(s)dZ_{s}
is well-defined. One mentions in passing that the fact that, for all
t\in[0,1]
,
h\mathbbm{1}_{(0,t)}\in|\mathcal{H}|
is a straightforward consequence of the boundedness of the function
h
(see(
37
)). Indeed, since
\|h\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
H\in(1/2,1)
, one has, for each
t\in[0,1]
,

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}|h(u)|\cdot|h(v)|\cdot|u-v|^{2H-2}dudv\leq\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|u-v|^{2H-2}dudv<\infty.

Let us emphasize that the estimation of the integrated volatility
\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds
, with
p\geq 1
, constitutes a common topic in financial mathematics. When the noise
Z
in (
65
) is a semimartingale, there exists a huge literature on this topic. We refer, among many others, to
[
9
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
15
]
or
[
27
]
for several approaches to estimate the integrated volatility, even when it is assumed to be random. When the process
X
is observed at discrete times on the interval
[0,1]
, one of the most usual methods to do it is via a power variation of the process
X
. We will use a similar approach in the case of the Hermite noise in (
65
), but our power variation will be defined by using only some special increments of the process
X
given by (
65
). This will allow to prove that the power variation sequence defined below constitutes a consistent estimator for the integrated volatility and moreover, it satisfies a Central Limit Theorem after a proper renormalization. Related results obtained for the case when
Z
is the fractional Brownian motion can be found in
[
7
]
or
[
13
]
.

We start by defining the modified power variation of the process
X
. We set, for any integer numbers
p\geq 2
and
N\geq 1
,

S_{N,p}(X)=\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}(\Delta X_{l,N})^{p},

(66)

where, for
l=1,...,2^{[N^{\gamma}]}
,

\Delta X_{l,N}=X_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}-X_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}.

(67)

Thus, similarly to what has been done (
4
) and (
18
), we use the increments of
X
of length
2^{-N}
located at the points
\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}
of the unit interval
[0,1]
. The following result shows that the modified power variation of
X
provides a consistent estimator for the integrated volatility.

Proposition 4

Assume
h
satisfies (
37
) and consider the sequence
(S_{N,p}(X),N\geq 1)
given by (
66
) with an integer
p\geq 2
. Then, setting
\mu_{p}=\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p}
, one has

S_{N,p}(X)\to_{N\to\infty}\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds\mbox{ in }L^{1}(\Omega).

(68)

Proof:
As we already did it in the proof of Theorem
3
, we use an approach based on the approximation of the stochastic integral in (
65
) by Riemann sums inspired by the works
[
7
]
or
[
13
]
. Let
M
and
N
be two arbitrary integer numbers such that
2<2^{(1-\beta)^{-1}}\leq M\leq[N^{\gamma}]
. We decompose the difference
S_{N,p}(X)-\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds
into four terms as follows:

\displaystyle S_{N,p}(X)-\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds

(69)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}(\Delta X_{l,N})^{p}-\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}h\left(\frac{l}{2^{N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}\left(h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}-h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}\right)(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mu_{p}\frac{1}{2^{M}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle+\mu_{p}\left(\frac{1}{2^{M}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}-\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A_{N,p}+B_{N,M,p}+C_{N,M,p}+D_{M,p}.

Our next goal is to estimate from above each one of the latter four summands.

Estimation of
A_{N,p}
.
We use the triangle inequality and the inequality

|A^{p}-B^{p}|\leq 2^{p-2}\,p\left(|B|^{p-1}|A-B|+|A-B|^{p}\right),

(70)

which holds, for any
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
and for all integer
p\geq 2
. Thus, we obtain

|A_{N,p}|\leq C_{p}\left(|A_{N,p,1}|+|A_{N,p,2}|\right),

with

A_{N,p,1}=\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left|h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\right|^{p-1}|\Delta Z_{l,N}|^{p-1}\left|\Delta X_{l,N}-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\Delta Z_{l,N}\right|

and

A_{N,p,2}=\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left|\Delta X_{l,N}-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\Delta Z_{l,N}\right|^{p}.

Let
a\geq 2
be an arbitrary positive integer number, it follows from (
65
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left|\Delta X_{l,N}-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\Delta Z_{l,N}\right|^{a}=\mathbf{E}\left|\int_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}^{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}\left(h(s)-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\right)dZ_{s}\right|^{a}.

Since the Wiener integral of any function of
|\mathcal{H}|
with respect to
Z
is an element of the
q
th Wiener chaos, using the hypercontractivity property (
116
) and the isometry of the Wiener integral (
3
), one can derive from the previous equality that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left|\Delta X_{l,N}-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\Delta Z_{l,N}\right|^{a}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\mathbf{E}\left|\int_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}^{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}\left(h(s)-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\right)dZ_{s}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{a}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(C\int_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}^{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}\int_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}^{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}\left(h(u)-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\left(h(v)-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\right)|u-v|^{2H-2}dudv\right)^{\frac{a}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(2^{-2\alpha N}\int_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}^{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}\int_{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}^{\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}+2^{-N}}|u-v|^{2H-2}dudv\right)^{\frac{a}{2}},

where we used (
37
) for the last inequality. Consequently, we obtain

\mathbf{E}\left|\Delta X_{l,N}-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\Delta Z_{l,N}\right|^{a}\leq C2^{-\alpha Na}2^{-HNa}.

(71)

We will use Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality and (
71
) to bound the terms denoted by
A_{N,p,1}
and
A_{N,p,2}
. For
A_{N,p,1}
, we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}|A_{N,p,1}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mathbf{E}|\Delta Z_{l,N}|^{p-1}\left|\Delta X_{l,N}-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\Delta Z_{l,N}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(\mathbf{E}|\Delta Z_{l,N}|^{2p-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{E}\left|\Delta X_{l,N}-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\Delta Z_{l,N}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}2^{-(p-1)HN}2^{-HN}2^{-\alpha N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\alpha N}.

Regarding
A_{N,p,2}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}|A_{N,p,2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(\mathbf{E}\left|\Delta X_{l,N}-h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)\Delta Z_{l,N}\right|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}2^{-\alpha pN}2^{-HpN}\leq C2^{-\alpha pN}.

We then conclude that

\mathbf{E}|A_{N,p}|\leq C2^{-\alpha N}.

(72)

Estimation of
B_{N,M,p}
.
We notice that for every integer
l\in\left[(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1,k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}\right]
,

\left|\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}-\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right|\leq 2^{-M}.

(73)

Then, by (
37
) and the inequality (
70
),

\left|h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}-h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}\right|\leq C2^{-M\alpha},

for any integer number
p\geq 2
. It gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}|B_{N,M,p}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}\left|h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}-h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}\right|\mathbf{E}|\Delta Z_{l,N}|^{p}

(74)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}2^{-M\alpha}\mathbf{E}|\Delta Z_{l,N}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}2^{-M\alpha}2^{-pHN}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-M\alpha}.

Estimation of
C_{N,M,p}
.
Using the equalities
\mu_{p}=\mathbf{E}Z_{1}^{p}=2^{pHN}\mathbf{E}\left(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)
, for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,2^{[N^{\gamma}]}\}
, we can express this summand as

\displaystyle C_{N,M,p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{M}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}\frac{2^{pHN}2^{M}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}\left((\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)

(75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{N,M,p}^{(1)}+R_{N,M,p},

with

C_{N,M,p}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{2^{M}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}\frac{2^{pHN}2^{M}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})\right)

and

\displaystyle R_{N,M,p}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{M}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}\frac{2^{pHN}2^{M}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt\times\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{j}(\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p-j}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{j}(\check{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p-j}\right).

Since
h
is a bounded function on the interval
[0,1]
(see (
37
)), similarly to the proof of (
21
), it can be shown that

\mathbf{E}|R_{N,M,p}|\leq C2^{[N^{\beta}]\frac{H-1}{q}},

(76)

for all integers
N
and
M
with
2^{(1-\beta)^{-1}}\leq M\leq[N^{\gamma}]
. We next deal with
C_{N,M,p}^{(1)}
. We will compute its norm in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, in order to benefit from the independence property in Proposition
1
. We have, for all integers
M,N
with
2^{(1-\beta)^{-1}}\leq M\leq[N^{\gamma}]
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(C_{N,M,p}^{(1)})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2M}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}\frac{2^{2pHN}2^{2M}}{2^{2[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}\mathbf{E}\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{2^{2pHN}}{2^{2[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}\mathbf{E}\left((\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\tilde{\Delta}Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{1}{2^{2[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\sum_{l=(k-1)\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}+1}^{l=k\frac{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}{2^{M}}}1\leq C2^{-[N^{\gamma}]}.

Thus, using the latter inequality, Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, (
75
) and (
76
) we get that

\mathbf{E}|C_{N,M,p}|\leq C2^{-\frac{[N^{\gamma}]}{2}}.

(77)

Estimation of
D_{M,p}
.
We can write

D_{M,p}=\mu_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\int_{\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}}^{\frac{k}{2^{M}}}\left(h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}-h(s)^{p}\right)ds,

and by (
70
) and (
37
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}|D_{M,p}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\int_{\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}}^{\frac{k}{2^{M}}}\left|h\left(\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}\right)^{p}-h(s)^{p}\right|ds

(78)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{2^{M}}\int_{\frac{k-1}{2^{M}}}^{\frac{k}{2^{M}}}2^{-M\alpha}ds\leq C2^{-M\alpha}.

Finally, using (
69
), (
72
), (
74
), (
77
) and (
78
), we obtain, for all integer
M\geq 2^{(1-\beta)^{-1}}
,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{E}\left|S_{N,p}(X)-\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds\right|\leq C2^{-M\alpha}.

Thus, letting
M
goes to
+\infty
, it follows that

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{E}\left|S_{N,p}(X)-\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds\right|=\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{E}\left|S_{N,p}(X)-\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds\right|=0.

For every integers
p\geq 2
and
N\geq 1
, we set

V_{N,p}(X)=\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(S_{N,p}(X)-\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds\right),

(79)

where
S_{N,p}(X)
is given by (
66
). We recall that
h:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is a deterministic function satisfying (
37
). The next result shows that, as soon as the function
h
is smooth enough, the renormalized modified power variation
V_{N,p}(X)
converges in distribution to a Gaussian random variable which can be expressed, up to the multiplicative constant
\sqrt{m_{p}}
, as a Wiener integral of the function
h
.

Theorem 4

Let
p\geq 2
be an integer number. Consider the sequence
(V_{N,p}(X),N\geq 1)
given by (
79
) and assume that
h
satisfies (
37
) with
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
. Then

V_{N,p}(X)\to^{(d)}_{N\to\infty}\sqrt{m_{p}}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)dW_{s},

where
(W_{t},t\geq 0)
is a Wiener process and
m_{p}
is given by (
27
). Moreover, for each
\nu\in(0,\gamma)
, there is a constant
C
, which depends on
\nu
and
h
, such that, for all
N
large enough, one has

d_{W}\left(V_{N,p}(Z),\sqrt{m_{p}}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}dW_{s}\right)\leq C2^{-\alpha N^{\nu}}.

(80)

Proof:
We write

\displaystyle V_{N,p}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}(\Delta X_{l,N})^{p}-\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}(\Delta X_{l,N})^{p}-\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}-\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mu_{p}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\mu_{p}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}-\mu_{p}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)^{p}ds\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle T_{1,N,p}+T_{2,N,p}+T_{3,N,p}.

Let us estimate each of the three summands from above. First, we notice that

T_{1,N,p}=\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}A_{N,p},

where
A_{N,p}
is given by (
69

For

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\frac{2^{pHN}}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}\left((\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}-2^{-pHN}\mu_{p}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{2^{pHN}}{\sqrt{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}h\left(\frac{l}{2^{[N^{\gamma}]}}\right)^{p}\left((\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}-\mathbf{E}(\Delta Z_{l,N})^{p}\right).

-Hölder continuous,

\alpha

The desired bound for the Wasserstein distance follows from (

Remark 3

(
\mathcal{A}

\left(P_{j}:=\left\{0=x_{0}^{(j)}<x_{1}^{(j)}<\dots<x_{m}^{(j)}=t\right\}\right)_{j}

(86)

In view of (
84

would consist in considering a partition of

and computing from it the Riemann-Stieltjes sum appearing in the right-hand side of (

itself, obtained by using the simulation method which was introduced very recently in

4

Let us now concisely describe the latter simulation method.
It relies on the resolution parameter
J\in\mathbb{N}
, which corresponds to the level of approximation (see equation (
92
) below). Also, it relies on the two other parameters
a\in(1/2,1)
and

, which are needed to insure the rate of convergence (
92
) for the approximation procedure, see Section 3 in
[
4
]
and references to
[
3
]
therein. Having introduced these three parameters, let us fix some notations. We recall that
q\in\mathbb{N}
is the order of the Wiener chaos to which the Hermite process
Z
of Hurst parameter
H\in(1/2,1)
belongs. In the sequel we set
\delta=\frac{H-1}{q}+\frac{1}{2}
.
The univariate Meyer fractional scaling function
[
19
]
of order
\delta
is denoted by
\Phi_{\Delta}^{(\delta)}
and defined through its Fourier transform as

\widehat{\Phi}_{\Delta}^{(\delta)}(\xi)=\left(\frac{1-e^{-i\xi}}{i\xi}\right)^{\delta}\widehat{\phi}(\xi)~\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\text{ and }\widehat{\Phi}_{\Delta}^{(\delta)}(0)=1,

where
\phi
is a univariate Meyer scaling function
[
16
]

(g_{J,k}^{\phi})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the sequence of i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
Gaussian random variable defined, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, as

g_{J,k}^{\phi}:=2^{J/2}I_{1}\left(\phi(2^{J}\bullet-k)\right),

the Gaussian FARIMA
(0,\delta,0)
sequence
(Z_{J,\ell}^{(\delta)})_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
associated to
(g_{J,k}^{\phi})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is given, for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, by

Z_{J,\ell}^{(\delta)}:=g_{J,\ell}^{\phi}+\sum_{p=1}^{+\infty}\gamma_{p}^{(\delta)}g_{J,\ell-p}^{\phi},

where the coefficients
\gamma_{p}^{(\delta)}=\frac{\delta\,\Gamma(p+\delta)}{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(\delta+1)}
, for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
. We mention in passing that

can be simulated by using, for instance, a circulant matrix embedding procedure, which is an exact method
[
14

32
]

Our next goal is to introduce a piecewise linear stochastic process which approximates the Hermite process

. To this end, let us now consider the infinite set

Appendix: Multiple stochastic integrals and the Malliavin derivative

The basic tools from the analysis on Wiener space are presented in this section. We will focus on some elementary facts about multiple stochastic integrals. We refer to
[
22
]
or
[
20
]
for a complete review on the topic.

Consider
{\mathcal{H}}
a real separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
with its associated inner product
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\mathcal{H}}
, and
(B(\varphi),\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}})
an isonormal Gaussian process on a
probability space
(\Omega,{\mathfrak{F}},\mathbb{P})
, which is a
centred Gaussian family of random variables such that

\mathbf{E}\left(B(\varphi)B(\psi)\right)={\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
for every

\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Denote by
I_{q}
(
q\geq 1
) the
q
th multiple
stochastic integral with respect to
B
, which is an
isometry between the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}^{\odot q}

(symmetric tensor product) equipped with the scaled norm

\sqrt{q!}\,\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes q}}
and
the Wiener chaos of order
q
, which is defined as the closed linear
span of the random variables
H_{q}(B(\varphi))
where

\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}},\;\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}=1
and

H_{q}
is the Hermite polynomial of degree
q\geq 1
defined
by:

H_{q}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{q}}{q!}\exp\left(\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{q}}{{\mathrm{d}x}^{q}}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)\right),\;x\in\mathbb{R}.

(111)

For
q=0
,

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }I_{0}(x)=x\mbox{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(112)

The isometry property of multiple integrals can be written as follows : for
p,\;q\geq 0
,
f\in{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes p}}
and

g\in{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes q}}

\mathbf{E}\Big(I_{p}(f)I_{q}(g)\Big)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}q!\langle\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes q}}&&\mbox{if}\;p=q,\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\cr 0&&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

(113)

where
\tilde{f}
stands for the symmetrization of
f
. When
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(T)
, with
T
being an interval of
\mathbb{R}
, we have the following product formula: for
p,\;q\geq 0
,
f\in{{\mathcal{H}}^{\odot p}}
and

g\in{{\mathcal{H}}^{\odot q}}
,

\displaystyle I_{p}(f)I_{q}(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{p\wedge q}r!\binom{q}{r}\binom{p}{r}I_{p+q-2r}\left(f\otimes_{r}g\right),

(114)

where, for
r=0,...,p\wedge q
, the contraction
f\otimes_{r}g
is the function in
L^{2}(T^{p+q-2r})
given by

(f\otimes_{r}g)(t_{1},...,t_{p+q-2r})=\int_{T^{r}}f(u_{1},...,u_{r},t_{1},...,t_{p-r})g(u_{1},...,u_{r},t_{p-r+1},...,t_{p+q-2r})du_{1}...du_{r}.

(115)

An useful property of finite sums of multiple stochastic integrals is the hypercontractivity. Namely, for every fixed real number
p\geq 2
, there exists a universal deterministic finite constant
C_{p}
, such that, for any random variable
F
of the form
F=\sum_{k=0}^{n}I_{k}(f_{k})
with
f_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
, the following inequality holds:

\mathbf{E}|F|^{p}\leq C_{p}\left(\mathbf{E}F^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

(116)

We denote by
D
the Malliavin derivative operator that acts on
cylindrical random variables of the form
F=g(B(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,B(\varphi_{n}))
, where
n\geq 1
,

g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function with
compact support and
\varphi_{i}\in{{\mathcal{H}}}
, in the following way:

DF=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}(B(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,B(\varphi_{n}))\varphi_{i}.

The operator
D
is closable and it can be extended to
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
which denotes the closure of the set of cylindrical random variables with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
defined as

\|F\|_{1,2}^{2}:=\mathbf{E}|F|^{2}+\mathbf{E}\|DF\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

If
F=I_{p}(f)
, where
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot p}
with
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(T)
and
p\geq 1
, then

D_{\ast}F=pI_{p-1}\left(f(\cdot,\ast)\right),

where
"\cdot"
stands for
p-1
variables.

The pseudo inverse
(-L)^{-1}
of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator
L
is defined, for
F=I_{p}(f)
with
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot p}
and
p\geq 1
, by

(-L)^{-1}F=\frac{1}{p}I_{p}(f).

At last notice that in our work, we have
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
while the role of the isonormal process
(B(\varphi),\varphi\in\mathcal{H})
is played by the usual Wiener integral on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
associated with the Brownian motion
(B(y),y\in\mathbb{R})
.

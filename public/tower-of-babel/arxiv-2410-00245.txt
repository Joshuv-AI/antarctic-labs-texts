The Euler characteristic of 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} via Hodge integrals
Aitor Iribar Lopez

Abstract
. We prove the Harder-Siegel formula for the Euler characteristic of 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} via the intersection theory of ℳ ¯ g \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g} and a vanishing result for lambda classes on the boundary of the toroidal compactifications of 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} , recently proven by Canning, Molcho, Oprea and Pandharipande.



.

Introduction

The moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
is a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack of dimension
\binom{g+1}{2}
, usually denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{g}
. Over the complex numbers, this is the same as saying that
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is an orbifold, and it can be identified with the stack quotient of the Siegel upper half space by the group of symplectic matrices with integer coefficients

(1)

\mathcal{A}_{g}\cong[\mathcal{H}_{g}/\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})]\,.

The Gauss-Bonnet formula
[
Har71
]
of Harder shows that the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
can be obtained from the volume of a fundamental domain for the action of
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{H}_{g}
, which was known to Siegel
[
Sie36
]
:

Theorem 1
.

The Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is

(2)

\chi(\mathcal{A}_{g})=\zeta(-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot\zeta(1-2g)\,.

We employ the
logarithmic Gauss–Bonnet formula
to reduce the calculation of the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
to an intersection theoretic problem on the toroidal compactifications of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, which is solved via Hodge integrals and the Torelli map

On every toroidal compactification of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, there is a universal semiabelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{G}_{g}\to\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
with a zero section
s
. The
Hodge bundle
is a vector bundle of rank
g
defined by

\mathbb{E}_{g}=s^{*}\Omega_{\pi}\,,

and its Chern classes are denoted by
\lambda_{i}=c_{i}(\mathbb{E}_{g})
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
denote the moduli space of stable curves of genus
g
with
n
markings, and let

\pi:\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

be the universal curve, with sections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
corresponding to the markings. It is a smooth DM stack of dimension
3g-3+n
and it has a
Hodge bundle
, defined by

\mathbb{E}_{g}=\pi_{*}\omega_{\pi}.

We denote its Chern classes by
\lambda_{i}
also. On some toroidal compactifications there is a Torelli morphism

\operatorname{Tor}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}\,,

as shown in
[
Ale04
,
Nam76
]
, and
\operatorname{Tor}^{*}\mathbb{E}_{g}=\mathbb{E}_{g}
. Esnault and Viehweg showed in
[
EV02
]
that the
\lambda
classes satisfy
Mumford’s relation
:

(3)

(1+\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{g})(1-\lambda_{1}+\ldots+(-1)^{g}\lambda_{g})=c(\mathbb{E}_{g}\oplus\mathbb{E}^{\vee}_{g})=1\text{ in }\operatorname{CH}^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g})\,.

On
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
there are also line bundles
\mathbb{L}_{i}=s_{i}^{*}\omega_{\pi}
representing the cotangent space at the
i
-th marking, and its first Chern class is
\psi_{i}
. The
\psi
classes and the
\lambda
classes are part of the
tautological ring
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
(see
[
FP13
,
Pan18
]
for the definition). Integrals of the form

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\lambda_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots\lambda_{g}^{a_{g}}\psi_{1}^{b_{1}}\ldots\psi_{n}^{b_{n}}

are
Hodge integrals
, and they appear naturally in Gromov-Witten theory.

We will consider the locus
\mathcal{B}_{g}
of semiabelian varieties which are trivial extension of an abelian variety by a torus.
\lambda_{g}
is proportional to the class
[\mathcal{B}_{g}]
when we restrict ourselves to an open subset of
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
given by degenerations of torus rank at most
1

[
vdG99
,
EvdG05
]
. Then we compute the proportionality factor in two ways. The first is through the isomorphism
\mathcal{B}_{g}\cong\mathcal{A}_{g-1}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, and the second one is pulling back via the Torelli map. We will see that the formula for the Euler characteristic (
2
) follows from the evaluation of the Hodge integrals

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}\lambda_{g}\lambda_{g-1}\lambda_{g-2}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}}\frac{\lambda_{g}\lambda_{g-1}c(\mathbb{E}_{g}^{\vee})}{1-\psi}\,,

which had been computed by Faber and Pandharipande
[
FP00a
,
FP00b
]
.

To a polarization on an abelian variety of dimension
g
we can associate a list of numbers
\delta=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{g})
, where
d_{i}\,|\,d_{i+1}
, and there is a moduli stack of abelian varieties of dimension
g
together with a polarization of type
\delta
, denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{g,\delta}
. When
d_{i}=1
for all
i
, we recover
\mathcal{A}_{g}
. These moduli spaces are part of a tower of étale maps. By the degree calculations of
[
IL24
]
, the Euler characteristics of all the moduli spaces
\mathcal{A}_{g,\delta}
is determined:

Theorem 2
.

For a list of positive integers
\delta=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{g})
such that
d_{i}\,|\,d_{i+1}
for all
i
,

\chi(\mathcal{A}_{g,\delta})=\left(d_{g}^{2g-2}d_{g-1}^{2g-6}\ldots d_{1}^{-2g+2}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq g}\prod_{p\mid d_{j}/d_{i}}\frac{(1-p^{-2(j-i+1)})}{(1-p^{-2(j-i)})}\right)\chi(\mathcal{A}_{g}),

where the product is over primes
p
.

We will prove in Corollary
7
that the non-vanishing of
\chi(\mathcal{A}_{g})
implies the non-vanishing of
\lambda_{g-1}\ldots\lambda_{1}
on
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
. This result was first established in
[
vdG99
, Corollary 1.3]
using the geometry of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
over fields of positive characteristic.

Further directions

The main geometric input for this formula is that
\lambda_{g}
is proportional to
[\mathcal{B}_{g}]
on
\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\leq 1}

[
EvdG05
]
to express

in terms of boundary strata of the toroidal compactifications of

is

Note that
\mathcal{B}_{g}
is one of the two components of the closure of the
product locus

\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
in
\mathcal{A}^{\leq 1}_{g}
. In
[
COP24
]
, the authors compute
\operatorname{Tor}^{*}([\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{g-1}])
and prove that it is a tautological class on

in Lemma

, we think it is reasonable to expect the following:

Conjecture 1
.

Logarithmic Euler characteristic

If
\mathcal{Y}
is a smooth DM-stack of dimension
n
with a smooth compactification
\overline{\mathcal{Y}}
such that the complement
D
of
\mathcal{Y}
is a normal crossing divisor then the sheaf of meromorphic differentials having at most log poles along
D
is a vector bundle of rank
n
, denoted by
\Omega_{\overline{\mathcal{Y}}}(\operatorname{log}D)
and it is well-known that

\chi(\mathcal{Y})=(-1)^{n}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{Y}}}c_{n}(\Omega_{\overline{\mathcal{Y}}}(\operatorname{log}D))\,,

where
\chi
is the Euler characteristic (see
[
CMZ20
, Section 2]
for a proof). When
\mathcal{Y}
is
\mathcal{A}_{g}
and
\overline{\mathcal{Y}}
is one of its toroidal compactifications (constructed in
[
AMRT10
]
over the complex numbers, or
[
CF91
]
over the integers),
\Omega_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}(\operatorname{log}D)
is the
canonical extension
of
\Omega_{\mathcal{A}_{g}}
(see
[
CF91
, VI.4.1]
for details) so we have the following:

\Omega_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}(\operatorname{log}D)=\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{g}\,.

The top Chern class of
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{g}
can be computed by the Giambelli formula (
[
Ful84
, Example 14.5.1]
), and it equals

2^{g}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\lambda_{g}&0&0&\ldots&0\\
\lambda_{g-2}&\lambda_{g-1}&\lambda_{g}&\ldots&0\\
\lambda_{g-4}&\lambda_{g-3}&\lambda_{g-2}&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\ldots&\lambda_{1}\end{array}\right|=2^{g}\left(\lambda_{g}\lambda_{g-1}\ldots\lambda_{1}+\sum_{k=0}^{g-1}\underbrace{\lambda_{g}\ldots\lambda_{g-k+1}\lambda_{g-k}^{2}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=0\text{ by Mumford's}\\
\text{ relation }\eqref{eqn: Mumford}\end{subarray}}p_{k}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{g})\right)\,,

so

(4)

\chi(\mathcal{A}_{g})=(-1)^{\binom{g+1}{2}}2^{g}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{g}\,.

In particular, the integral on the right hand side does not depend on the toroidal compactification, and more generally any integral of
\lambda
classes is independent of the toroidal compactification, by choosing a common roof, and it makes sense on non-smooth toroidal compactifications considering Chern classes as operational Chow classes.

An abelian variety of dimension
1
is an elliptic curve, so

(5)

\chi(\mathcal{A}_{1})=-2\int_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}\lambda_{1}=-2\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}}\psi=\zeta(-1)\,.

The Hirzebruch-Mumford proportionality theorem

Consider the Lagrangian Grassmanian
\operatorname{LG}_{g}
, that parametrizes Lagrangian subspaces of dimension
g
inside a symplectic vector space of dimension
2g
. It is a smooth projective variety of dimension
\binom{g+1}{2}
. The universal Lagrangian subspace

\mathbb{S}_{g}\to\operatorname{LG}_{g}

defines a vector bundle of rank
g
, with Chern classes
x_{i}=c_{i}(\mathbb{S}_{g})
, and
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{g})
is the cotangent bundle to
\operatorname{LG}_{g}
. Mumford shows in
[
Mum77
]
that there is a constant
K(g)
such that for any
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}\lambda_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots\lambda_{g}^{a_{i}}=K(g)\int_{\operatorname{LG}_{g}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\,.

This constant has been determined in
[
vdG99
, Theorem 1.13]
by the Gauss-Bonnet formula:

\chi(\mathcal{A}_{g})=K(g)\chi(\operatorname{LG}_{g})\,.

An approach to prove the formula for
\chi(\mathcal{A}_{g})
could be to determine
K(g)
in an alternative way.

Van der Geer also shows that the assignment
\lambda_{i}\mapsto x_{i}
defines an isomorphism between the subring of
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
generated by the classes
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{g-1}
(also known as the
tautological ring
) and the cohomology ring of
\operatorname{LG}_{g-1}
. One of the steps of the proof given there relied on a characteristic
p
argument and the ampleness of
\lambda_{1}
. We give a different proof:

Corollary 7

(
[
vdG99
, Corollary 1.4]
)
.

\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{g-1}\neq 0
in
\operatorname{CH}_{g}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
.

Proof.

If it were
0
then

0=\epsilon_{\mathcal{A}_{g}}(\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{g-1})=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}}\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{g}=(-1)^{\binom{g+1}{2}}2^{-g}\chi(\mathcal{A}_{g}),

a contradiction.
∎

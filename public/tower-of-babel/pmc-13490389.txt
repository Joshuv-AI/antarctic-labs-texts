A hybrid heuristic-based multi-objective strategy for optimized charging and discharging in plug-in EV energy management
Authors: V. R. Rajan, D. Chandrakala, D. Rene Dev
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The paper will deal with the problem of optimal charging and discharging coordination of Plug-in Electric Vehicles in a local multi-energy system that will be conducted under uncertain conditions. The current practices are usually based on simplified assumptions and fail to coordinate various energy sources and resources effectively, which restricts their usage in real-life conditions. In order to eliminate these constraints, a hybrid optimization model that involves Rider Optimization and Osprey Optimization integration is suggested. The model developed will take into account both the economic and environmental goals, such as the cost of operation, profitability of the system, and minimization of carbon emission. The lack of certainty in the behavior and generation of the renewable is factored in by using probabilistic modeling, which allows the system to be more realistic. The suggested algorithm constraints global exploration and local exploitation to optimally search the energy scheduling strategies. The outcomes of the simulation display the assumption that the proposed method can deliver the enhanced performance of the operational level as it successfully organizes the activities of distributed energy resources and electric vehicle charging. The model offers computationally effective and flexible framework to be used in practical energy management applications in local multi-energy systems.

Introduction

The automotive sector is currently moving from traditional internal combustion engine vehicles to EVs1. The main explanation for this transformation is that traditional cars with combustion engines contribute significantly to CO2 emissions2. The resultant emission has the responsibility to increase the nuisance stages, particularly in large towns because the quantity of automobiles is much higher than in smaller towns3. Furthermore, growing CO2 levels are liable to rise in temperature and melting glaciers4. As a result, there’s an urgent requirement for a viable alternative to standard fuel-powered cars. EVs offer a practical opportunity to conventional gasoline-powered vehicles5. They produce less carbon dioxide and successfully reduce pollution in the air, particularly in large cities. As a result, EVs are poised to shape the next generation of travel, contributing to environmentally friendly growth6. However, the task of electrifying transportation is fraught with difficulties. The most difficult task is developing a good power management plan to manage the EV7.

At present, the electric vehicle’s range is restricted8. Nowadays, electric automobiles can drive 300–500 km on just one charge of the batteries, which is insufficient for future possibilities. The dimensions, number of power sources, and aesthetics associated with larger or several batteries are the key barriers to automobile technology9. As a result, having an effective power management plan is essential for trying to improve the range of EV travel in battery power10. This may be accomplished by optimizing a battery’s “State-of-Charge (SOC)” characteristic11. Batteries SOC has become a proportion that represents how full a battery is about its overall capability. An enhanced SOC characteristic shows more efficient use of electrical energy12. The SOC levels greater than 50% are desired at all times. While driving, batteries can be fully charged by applying regenerative brakes. An effective transfer of electricity could result in quicker charging of batteries and a better SOC pattern. With the goal of enhancing the SOC description, innovative and smart controllers must be developed.

With growing knowledge of protecting the environment and the desire to lead a sustainable lifestyle, EVs have steadily gained popularity among regular households as a safe and effective mode of transport. However, the indecision of random human behavior and the discomfort of charge render make it difficult to understand the charging demands of each EV, which may cause destabilization within the regional electrical system13. Other environmental elements like power pricing, Photovoltaic (PV), and the climate help to enhance the improbability of power management14. Due to these environmental elements, EV vehicle charging planning is critical in boosting energy conservation and smoothing potential peak demand. In particular, the conventional models have EV charging arranging issues like an optimization problem to maximize the profit of the owners by scheduling the “charging and discharging” procedures15. The traditional model-based study focuses on modeling the EV charging schedule challenge as a specific optimization challenge and addressing it using nonlinear software or adaptive programming methods16. Recently, an effective optimization technique has been used to charge the PEV with numerous unpredictable components. To summarize, such EV charging planning research utilizes an optimization framework and solves the issue under the supposition of a fully visible environment17. Yet, the accuracy of the model may have a significant impact on optimization performance. Therefore, we developed an effective optimization-based charging and discharging strategy to overcome the challenges in the existing models.

Recent progress in battery technology and power electronics has significantly improved the driving range and performance of electric vehicles, leading to their rapid adoption in modern transportation systems. As a result, Plug-in Electric Vehicles (PEVs) are increasingly integrated into power grids, which introduces new operational challenges related to charging coordination, system reliability, and environmental sustainability. While large-scale PEV deployment offers clear benefits in reducing fossil fuel dependency, unmanaged or poorly coordinated charging behavior can negatively impact grid stability and increase operational costs.

Most existing PEV charging and discharging strategies rely on simplified and deterministic assumptions regarding user behavior, energy demand, and system availability. In real-world scenarios, however, PEV arrival and departure times, charging duration, and user preferences are highly uncertain. Consequently, these strategies often fail to respond effectively to stochastic operating conditions, leading to suboptimal utilization of available resources. In addition, many approaches consider electrical energy in isolation, resulting in weak coordination among multiple energy carriers such as electricity, heat, and renewable sources within local energy systems. This lack of integrated energy management restricts flexibility and limits overall system efficiency.

Scalability is another critical issue associated with conventional PEV charging strategies. Centralized optimization-based methods, although effective for small-scale systems, become computationally expensive and difficult to implement as the number of vehicles and energy resources increases. Furthermore, many existing solutions focus primarily on minimizing energy costs without adequately addressing the tradeoff between operator profitability and carbon emission reduction. As a result, economic objectives are often prioritized at the expense of environmental performance, which undermines the sustainability goals of PEV integration.

Designing an effective charging and discharging strategy for PEVs also involves resolving several inherent contradictions. Enhancing user convenience through flexible and fast charging may cause peak load conditions and grid congestion, whereas strict grid-oriented charging control can reduce user satisfaction. Similarly, minimizing operational costs does not necessarily align with reducing greenhouse gas emissions, as lower-cost energy sources may not always be environmentally favorable. Moreover, centralized energy management systems provide coordinated decision-making but lack adaptability to real-time variations, while real-time or decentralized approaches offer responsiveness but struggle to maintain global optimality. Addressing these conflicting requirements remains a key challenge in PEV energy management.

The rising deployment of Plug-in Electric Vehicles has brought new possibilities and challenges to energy systems of the present day. Although electric vehicles can contribute to the flexibility of energy by controlling the charging and discharging of this energy, uncoordinated functioning might cause the overloading of the system, increase of the cost of operation, and the instability of the grid. The coordination of electric vehicle charging is even more important in local multi-energy systems, where electrical and thermal resources communicate with each other, and efficient and sustainable working of the system is important. Numerous energy management solutions have been devised to handle this issue. Nevertheless, most of the current solutions are based on deterministic models and fail to address the uncertainty represented by user behavior and the renewable energy production. Moreover, there are a number of papers that aim at the maximization of the electrical energy without the comprehensive exploration of the interplay between various energy carriers. This is absence of coordination and this diminishes the effectiveness of the whole system. Moreover, traditional optimization methods may be challenged by dealing with complex, nonlinear and multi-objective problems especially when it is necessary to be scaled and adaptable. The other critical limitation is the tradeoff of conflicting goals including, the cost minimization, profit maximization, and emission reduction.

The current practices often involve predetermined weighting schemes without considering the effect of these tools on system performance that limits the adaptability of decision-making. In addition, centralized optimization methods might not offer enough flexibility to dynamic and uncertain operating conditions. In order to overcome such challenges, the paper presents a hybrid-optimization-based energy management framework which would be based on integrating the Rider Optimization and Osprey Optimization. The HR-OOA strategy suggested will improve the exploration and exploitation capacities, and thus, complex multi-objective problems will be solved efficiently. The framework embraces uncertainty conscious model of the electric vehicle behaviour as well as the renewable generation and takes into account the multi-energy coordination into the system. Using the combination of computational power with higher quality of the solution, the method presented offers a realistic and flexible solution to the real-life energy management use scenarios.

In recent years, reinforcement learning–based energy management strategies have been explored to address uncertainty and real-time decision-making in PEV and microgrid applications. Advanced deep reinforcement learning techniques are capable of learning adaptive control policies for complex and nonlinear systems. However, these methods typically require large volumes of training data, extensive computational resources, and carefully designed reward functions to ensure convergence and stability. In addition, the black-box nature of deep learning models reduces transparency, which can limit their acceptance in practical energy management applications.

In contrast, the proposed HR-OOA provides a computationally efficient and interpretable alternative for practical local multi-energy system operation. By avoiding long training processes and relying on transparent population-based optimization, HR-OOA enables flexible, explainable, and effective coordination of PEV charging and discharging decisions while simultaneously improving operator profit and reducing carbon emissions.

Literature survey

Related works

In 2020, Di et al18. have proposed a complete multi-objective optimization approach for the electricity administration of a “Local multi-energy systems (LMES)” in the context of PEV, and the goal of combining LMES owner profit maximization alongside CO2 emission reduction. The LMES provided energy, warmth, and conditioning to a PEV-powered structure cluster that used to operate simultaneously in “Grid-to-Vehicle (G2V)” and “Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G)” phases. The issue was tackled by creating a Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) issue with stated modeling of connections among energy transporters, which included sending methods within the LMES and discovering the optimized charging and discharging methods of PEV so as to maximize the owner’s earnings, whereas simultaneously lowering CO2 emissions. The weighted average approach was utilized for representing the eco-environmental optimization issue, which was addressed and considered a group of workplaces in Italian as the end-users for the LMES using PEVs held by the staff of the workplaces. Because of the best possible collaboration between the numerous energy transporters within the LMES and the efficient handling of the variability gathered on both the demand and supply sides, the test outcomes showed the efficacy for the optimization structure to maximize the operator’s earnings and simultaneously lower CO2 emissions. Furthermore, the suggested model discovered that the PEV, serving as decentralized storage for energy, enabled the supply of emergency services while also supplementing solar generation to increase energy conservation. In comprehensively, most of the versatility gathered by PEV was put to the retail market to increase the owner’s revenue. The electrical energy released from PEV could be utilized for personal use via the LMES to reduce ecological effects via a carbon-free supply.

In 2014, Yu et al19. have presented a “Trip-oriented Energy Management Control (TEMC)” technique for PHEV. The suggested approach offered system optimization and monitoring methodologies to increase practical “Fuel Economy (FE)” in Energy Splitting PHEV design by improving the electrical distribution of demand among gas and battery power and balancing the energy breakdown among the physical and electric routes. A two-degree-of-freedom system simulation is developed to characterize the electric train’s actuator kinetics and power transmission features. The proposed model made three major improvements to PHEV electrical power management research: 1) the issue of optimum control had been resolved while taking into account both the irregularity of battery performance and the intricate nature of the PowerSplit construction; 2) an innovative trip-oriented consumption of energy preparing technique was suggested via an autonomous pattern-based software development approach; and 3) a control system with feedback was developed to realize the best possible energy usage procedure in practical applications. The suggested energy utilization control technique was demonstrated to enhance FE in Ford Getaway PHEVs.

In 2021, Suhail et al20. have presented the enhancement of a PHEV’s battery life utilizing a controller with fuzzy logic then an artificial fuzzy logic regulator using batteries current state as a decision variable, and then compared the efficacy of both scenarios. The power and engine condition of the battery were chosen as the inputs depending on their values, the controller of the fuzzy regulator decided the precise torque needed to be transformed to the energy that might be utilized for battery charging to control the forward gained value. To determine the forward gained value, a sophisticated fuzzy algorithm, and an innovative adaptive neuron fuzzy information regulator were utilized. To model the efficiency of the suggested structure, the Simulink platform was employed. This might prove useful in determining which kind of autonomous system to make use of to ensure the combination-powered vehicle’s energy effectiveness. The outcomes of both monitoring methods were contrasted, and the best microcontroller was chosen for plug-in electric car management of energy. The results showed that enhanced control approaches improved the efficiency and fuel consumption of hybridization electric automobiles.

In 2016, Tabari and Yazdani21 have suggested an “Energy Management Strategy (EMS)” to manage a PEV electrical system integrated “Direct Current (DC)” supply system. Using dual DC-DC converters and a centralized voltage-sourced adapter, the DC distribution network was designed to connect electric automobiles in a garage with the AC network. The suggested EMS regulated the electrical supply inside the direct-current system by employing a web-based limited optimization method. As a result, PEV users had the ability to charge or discharge the batteries according to the status of power and the anticipated trip intentions. The EMS provided PEV users with two exchanges of energy choices: 1) The rapid energy swap choice for users who want to reduce the time it takes to trade power; and 2) the best trading solution for the proprietors in order to either reduce charging costs or maximize earnings by releasing the energy they have stored. The suggested EMS managed both charging/discharging demands from PEV users within an effortless way while also taking into consideration the electrical consumption and electrical power restrictions of the AC grid to avoid under and over-voltage, including reverse energy flow concerns. To show the usefulness of the recommended EMS, different exchange of energy situations was performed.

In 2017, Wang and Liang22 have investigated on PHEV methods for energy administration using bilateral V2G. Initially, an economical strategy was taken from the standpoint of the prospective PHEV user. They used “Dynamic Programming (DP)” to solve the managing energy challenge in order to reduce everyday electrical costs. Yet, even with just a few repetitions, the "well-known" difficulty in resolving DP provided a computing problem. As a result, a state-independent four-threshold battery charging/discharging plan was developed using the probabilistic stocking principle, and mathematically established the optimum value of the suggested energy administration method. Then, a reverse repetition method was created to put into effect the stimulus regulations. Secondly, considering the standpoint of the transmission network manager, they attempted to reduce the highest load periods and flatter the whole load curve. To that purpose, they devised an ideal PHEV charging strategy and a resembling "water-filling" method for this kind of situation. Accurate PHEV battery designs, time-of-use power rates, and real-world residential demand information were all included in the V2G network design. The theoretical evaluation and demonstrations were critical to the eventual large-scale use of PHEVs in smart electricity systems.

In 2022, Chu et al23. have suggested a decentralized structure for PEV vehicle charging administration in an apartment complex outfitted with a solar and battery bank linked to a local converter using an efficient “Federated Deep Reinforcement Learning (FDRL)” algorithm. To begin, the PEV charge management structure was characterized as essentially an online charging facility that coordinates charging processes by exchanging public data with dispersed agents. Then, taking into account the heterogeneity of PEV charge nervousness, cell deterioration, and group cost, a PEV preference model was built. Following that, they suggested an attention-weighted federation soft-actor-critic technique that effectively sought the coordinated planning of the PEV battery charge in a manner that was distributed, with attention-based sharing of data for scaling and security of privacy. Lastly, an actual case investigation was done to verify the suggested approach’s efficacy and sustainability.

In 2021, Schmid et al24. have provided a series-production-ready energy administration method for concurrent PHEVs using “Pontryagin’s Minimum Principle (PMP)”. The PMP method made use of journey forecast data provided by a series-production navigational device. Conventional models produced two critical practical issues which were thoroughly tackled in this paper. First, the trip forecast offered clustered data from the navigational chart, including predicted typical speeds. Those changes significantly impacted the application of PMP because of referred to as discontinuities that develop anytime an engine ON/OFF instruction is changed. Second, the trip’s prognosis was inherently unreliable. Results indicated that a transparent PMP approach was insufficient and that a response was required. There were two approaches dealt with: one centered on repetitive optimization and another centered on an adaptable PMP strategy. The present research also proposed an original strategy for dealing with solitary controls that was easy to use in automobile practice while also being computationally effective. The approach produced results that were extremely comparable to a benchmark approach derived from a standalone PMP program that utilized data pertaining to the real trip. A study revealed an important opportunity for energy use reductions of 5.29% compared to a standard controlling technique.

In 2023, Ahmed et al25. have suggested an “Efficient Black Widow Optimization (EBWO)” technique in a commercial energy administration system using evolution technology to characterize varied charging scenarios. The suggested approach analyzed unpredictable, off-peak, highest point, and battery institute charge situations to achieve optimum vehicle-to-grid capability with regard to both expenses and consumption. A thorough schedule of charging instances was investigated to minimize power interruptions or situations with a substantial supply–demand imbalance. In addition, the suggested scheme concept minimized greenhouse gas emissions from the generating side in order to construct an environmentally friendly generating infrastructure that, in spite of specific nonlinear limitations, maximized the efficiency of fuel-based generation of energy.

Problem statement

Although a wide range of energy management and charging strategies for Plug-in Electric Vehicles has been reported in the literature, most existing approaches remain primarily descriptive and rely on simplified modeling assumptions that restrict their applicability under realistic operating conditions18–20. In particular, many methods are developed under deterministic assumptions, where vehicle arrival times, charging duration, and energy demand are assumed to be known in advance. Such formulations fail to capture the inherent randomness in user behavior and system dynamics, leading to reduced robustness when deployed in practical environments21. Furthermore, several studies employ fixed or predefined weighting factors in multi-objective optimization without conducting sensitivity analysis, which limits the adaptability of the solutions when operational priorities or system conditions change22.

In addition, a significant portion of existing research focuses on optimizing electrical energy alone, while neglecting coordinated interaction among multiple energy carriers within local energy systems23. This isolated treatment restricts the exploitation of cross-energy flexibility and weakens the system’s ability to balance economic and environmental objectives simultaneously. Moreover, conventional optimization and heuristic-based methods often struggle to achieve a consistent tradeoff between operator profitability and carbon emission reduction, particularly under uncertain and dynamically varying conditions24.

Therefore, this work addresses these limitations by formulating a hybrid optimization-based charging and discharging framework that explicitly incorporates multi-energy coordination, multi-objective tradeoff analysis, and uncertainty-aware decision-making. By leveraging a hybrid optimization strategy, the proposed approach aims to enhance scalability, robustness, and adaptability while improving economic performance and reducing environmental impact in Design and development of local multi-energy systems

Local multi-energy system scheme

The LMSE contains a number of conversion, storage, and generation systems. The LMES can transmit power, cooling and heat to construct clustering with the PEVs. Such latter are sectioned as the cluster based on the PEV’s characteristics and owners for the purpose of charging. The operator of LMES is considered as only the actor who has the responsibility for meeting the consumer needs consisting of the PEV’s management via the stations of charging. The owner can sell the collected flexibility via the dispersed systems and end-consumers to the market while operating PEV in the V2G module. The elaborated details about the flow of energy that occurs between the system are depicted in Fig. 1.Fig. 1Pictorial depiction for the flow of energy occurs between LMSE.

Pictorial depiction for the flow of energy occurs between LMSE.

The energy source is given using the grids, battery, PV as well as PEV. The energy is utilized to operate the “electric heat pumps” and fulfill the electrical loads of the clusters, which include the PEV’s charging demands. Additionally, the collected flexibility obtained from the PV, PEV, and “Combined heat and Power” (CHP) can be sold to the market. The heat energy obtained through the collected flexibility centered on the source of energy from boilers and CHP, the “electric heat pumps,” and heat space is also employed to satisfy the cooling loads’ clustering.

Parameter source and assumptions

The parameters used in the PEV operational model are selected based on a combination of established research practices and practical charging station norms. Battery capacity limits, SOC bounds, and power ratings are chosen to reflect typical values observed in commercially available electric vehicles and standard charging infrastructure. Arrival and departure times are modeled using assumed statistical distributions to capture the stochastic nature of user behavior, while initial SOC values are generated within realistic operating ranges. These assumptions enable a balanced representation of real-world uncertainty while maintaining computational tractability.

In addition to the operational constraints, battery degradation is considered by introducing a cost factor associated with charging and discharging energy. This term reflects the long-term impact of repeated cycling on battery health. The fact that the degradation effects are included in the model will deter overcharging and discharging processes and will encourage a more sustainable use of battery resources.

The uncertainty arising in renewable generation and user behavior is also factored in using probabilistic modeling of the important variables. Specifically, the changes in the power of the sun and the arrival and departure of PEVs are modeled with the help of reasonable assumptions based on probabilities. This will allow the proposed framework to account for the variability in the nature of system operation and enhance the strength of the scheduling decisions in the uncertain situations.

Computational complexity and stability discussion

Computational complexity analysis

The computational complexity of HR-OOA primarily depends on the population size and the number of iterations. At each iteration, all candidate solutions in the population are evaluated by the objective function and checked against system constraints. Since both the population size and iteration count are fixed and moderate, the algorithm exhibits linear growth in computational effort and remains suitable for practical local multi-energy system optimization.

Convergence behavior

The convergence of HR-OOA is guided by the complementary interaction between its global exploration and local exploitation mechanisms. During early iterations, the exploration-oriented component enables candidate solutions to search across a wide solution space, reducing the likelihood of premature convergence. As iterations progress, the exploitation-oriented component intensifies the search around promising regions, resulting in gradual refinement of solution quality. This cooperative behavior leads to smooth convergence trends observed in simulation results, where objective function values stabilize after a finite number of iterations.

Stability under different initializations

Algorithm stability is assessed by initializing the population with different random seeds and observing the consistency of convergence behavior. The results indicate that HR-OOA converges reliably to similar solution quality across multiple runs, demonstrating robustness against variations in initial population distribution. This stability is attributed to the hybrid search structure, which mitigates excessive sensitivity to initial conditions while maintaining sufficient diversity throughout the optimization process.

Parameter selection and tuning strategy

The parameters of the proposed HR-OOA algorithm are selected through a structured tuning process that balances solution quality, convergence speed, and computational stability. Initially, a trial-and-error approach is employed to identify feasible parameter ranges that ensure stable algorithm execution without premature divergence. This step helps eliminate extreme values that lead to oscillatory or stagnant search behavior.

Subsequently, sensitivity-based tuning is performed by varying one parameter at a time while keeping others fixed, allowing observation of its influence on convergence speed and objective function value. Parameters that significantly affect solution quality, such as population size and iteration count, are refined to achieve a tradeoff between computational effort and optimization performance. Finally, stability-driven tuning is applied to ensure consistent convergence across multiple simulation runs. Parameter values that yield low variance in objective outcomes are selected to enhance robustness under uncertain operating conditions.

Convergence rationale of HR-OOA

The convergence behavior of the proposed HR-OOA algorithm is governed by the complementary characteristics of its constituent optimization mechanisms. The Rider Optimization component emphasizes global exploration by enabling candidate solutions to traverse diverse regions of the search space. This behavior reduces the likelihood of premature convergence and enhances the algorithm’s ability to escape local optima during early iterations.

In contrast, the Osprey Optimization component focuses on local exploitation by intensifying the search around promising solution regions identified during the exploration phase. This targeted refinement improves solution accuracy and accelerates convergence once near-optimal regions are reached. By hybridizing these two mechanisms, HR-OOA dynamically balances exploration and exploitation throughout the optimization process. As a result, the algorithm exhibits faster convergence, improved stability, and enhanced solution consistency compared to standalone heuristic approaches.

Computational complexity and stopping criteria

The computational complexity of the HR-OOA algorithm primarily depends on the population size and the maximum number of iterations. For each iteration, all candidate solutions are evaluated based on the objective function and system constraints, resulting in a complexity that grows linearly with both population size and iteration count. This structure makes HR-OOA computationally efficient and scalable for practical energy management applications. The algorithm terminates when either the predefined maximum number of iterations is reached or when the improvement in objective value falls below a specified tolerance threshold, ensuring a balance between solution accuracy and execution time.

The weighting factors used in the multi-objective formulation represent the relative importance assigned to economic and environmental objectives. These weights are selected to reflect realistic operational priorities of local energy system operators, where cost minimization, profit maximization, and carbon emission reduction must be addressed simultaneously. Equal and non-equal weight combinations are examined to ensure that no single objective dominates the optimization process, allowing a balanced tradeoff among competing performance metrics.

The weighted sum method is adopted due to its simplicity, transparency, and computational efficiency in handling multiple conflicting objectives. By converting the multi-objective problem into a single aggregated objective, the optimization process remains tractable while enabling direct interpretation of tradeoffs among objectives. This approach is particularly suitable for practical energy management applications, where decision-makers often adjust priorities by modifying weight values rather than redesigning the optimization framework.

Multiple weight combinations are tested to observe their influence on the optimal scheduling decisions and system performance. This analysis provides insight into how changes in objective prioritization affect economic outcomes and environmental impact. The improved performance achieved by the proposed HR-OOA algorithm can be explained by the interaction between the optimization strategy and the formulation of the multi-objective function.

The objective function simultaneously considers economic and operational criteria, including the minimization of energy cost and the maximization of economic return for system owners. In the early stages of the search process, the algorithm emphasizes exploration of the solution space, allowing diverse scheduling patterns to be evaluated. As the search progresses, the algorithm gradually focuses on exploitation to refine promising solutions identified during the exploration phase. This adaptive search behavior enables the optimization process to effectively balance the competing objectives in the energy management problem. Consequently, the algorithm is able to identify energy scheduling strategies that reduce operational cost while improving the overall economic performance of the local multi-energy system. The probabilistic assumption of stochastic nature of the renewable generation and user behavior is used to represent the realistic operating conditions. The arrival and departure times of PEVs are generated based on assumed probability distributions to reflect realistic usage behavior.

Simulation setup and experimental configuration

To ensure reproducibility and fair performance comparison, a detailed simulation setup is defined for evaluating the proposed HR-OOA-based energy management strategy. The experiments are conducted on a local multi-energy system consisting of electrical, thermal, and renewable energy components coordinated with a fleet of Plug-in Electric Vehicles.

A fixed number of PEVs is considered in the simulation, representing a small-to-medium-scale charging environment. Vehicle arrival and departure times are generated using assumed probabilistic distributions to reflect realistic user behavior, with arrival times concentrated during typical commuting hours and departure times distributed accordingly. The initial state of charge of each PEV is randomly assigned within permissible operating bounds, ensuring variability in charging demand across vehicles.

The electrical load profile of the local energy system is predefined and remains consistent across all simulation runs to isolate the effect of the optimization algorithm. Photovoltaic generation capacity is selected to represent a rooftop or community-scale installation, with power output varying according to time-dependent solar availability. The energy storage system is modeled with a fixed capacity and power rating, allowing both charging and discharging operations within defined efficiency limits. The combined heat and power unit and boiler capacities are chosen to meet the thermal demand of the system under normal operating conditions, while the heat pump supplements thermal supply based on its coefficient of performance.

Market electricity prices are assumed to vary over time to capture peak and off-peak periods, encouraging economically efficient charging and discharging decisions. Carbon emission factors are associated with grid-supplied electricity and fuel-based generation units, enabling simultaneous evaluation of economic and environmental objectives.

For performance comparison, all benchmark algorithms are evaluated using the same system configuration, input data, and parameter settings, including identical population sizes, iteration limits, and stopping criteria where applicable. This unified experimental setup ensures that observed performance differences are attributable solely to algorithmic characteristics rather than external parameter variations.

The conceptual analyses of different vehicle-to-grid participation strategies have been done such as unidirectional charging and bidirectional energy exchange. Electric vehicles can only behave as loads in the unidirectional mode but they can also provide energy back to the system in the bidirectional mode. The bidirectional operation can provide greater flexibility in the system as it allows a maximum load to be reduced and better energy balancing. This enables electric vehicles to act as distributed energy resources which can help in better use of the existing energy in the multi-energy system of the locality.

Impact of weight selection on optimization outcomes

The selection of weighting factors has a noticeable influence on the optimization results obtained from the proposed framework. Increasing the weight assigned to cost-related objectives leads to lower operational expenses but may result in higher carbon emissions due to increased reliance on low-cost conventional energy sources. Conversely, assigning greater emphasis to emission reduction encourages higher utilization of renewable energy and storage resources, which improves environmental performance but may reduce short-term operator profit.

When profit-oriented weights are prioritized, the optimization favors charging and discharging strategies that enhance revenue opportunities, particularly through coordinated energy exchange and storage utilization. Balanced weight combinations produce intermediate solutions that achieve reasonable cost savings while maintaining acceptable emission levels. The sensitivity analysis confirms that the proposed approach responds smoothly to weight variations, demonstrating its flexibility and suitability for supporting diverse operational preferences in local multi-energy systems.

Conclusion

This study addressed the challenge of coordinating Plug-in Electric Vehicle charging and discharging within a local multi-energy system under uncertain operating conditions. The problem was formulated to simultaneously consider economic and environmental objectives while accounting for user behavior variability, multi-energy interactions, and system-level operational constraints. By moving beyond simplified and deterministic assumptions, the proposed framework provides a more realistic and practical approach to energy management in PEV-integrated systems.

To solve this complex multi-objective optimization problem, a Hybrid Rider-based Osprey Optimization Algorithm (HR-OOA) was developed. The effectiveness of HR-OOA lies in its ability to balance global exploration and local exploitation, enabling robust search performance and stable convergence across different operating scenarios. Unlike traditional optimization methods that suffer from scalability or rigidity, the hybrid structure allows HR-OOA to adapt efficiently to nonlinear, constrained, and uncertain system environments without requiring extensive training or excessive computational resources.

The authors of the paper reviewed an issue of coordinated charging and discharging of Plug-in Electric Vehicles in a local multi-energy system under uncertain operating situations. The study was intended to address the significant flaws of existing approaches, i.e., simplified modeling of the systems, a lack of coordination of energy sources as well as insufficient uncertainty control in user behavior and renewable production. To overcome these difficulties, a hybrid optimization framework that is based on the integration of Rider Optimization and Osprey Optimization was developed. The proposed HR-OOA strategy works well in terms of global search/local refinement that enables the discovery and successful resolution of the multi-objective energy management issue. The framework assists in coming up with improved operational decisions in the complex system environment as it follows both the economic and environmental objectives simultaneously. In practice, the proposed solution offers a flexible and computationally efficient model, which can be applied to the actual local energy systems. It is open to facilitate sustainable and reliable integration of the electric vehicles due to the fact that it permits uncertainty and accommodates fluctuating operational priorities. Overall, the paper demonstrates that hybrid optimization techniques have a future in the context of the enhancement of the work and performance of the existing system of energy management. The recommended structure can be integrated with the real-life energy management systems and can facilitate the process of making decisions in near real-time.

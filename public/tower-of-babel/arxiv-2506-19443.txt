From dual canonical bases to positroidal subdivisions
Jian-Rong Li and Ayush Kumar Tewari

Abstract
. The Grassmannian cluster algebra ℂ ⁡ [ Gr ⁡ ( k , n ) ] \mathbb{C}[\Gr(k,n)] admits a distinguished basis known as the dual canonical basis, whose elements correspond to rectangular semi-standard Young tableaux with k k rows and with entries in [ n ] [n] . We establish that each such tableau induces a positroidal subdivision of the hypersimplex Δ ⁡ ( k , n ) \Delta(k,n) via a map introduced by Speyer and Williams. For Gr ⁡ ( 2 , n ) \Gr(2,n) , we prove that non-frozen prime tableaux correspond precisely to the coarsest positroidal subdivisions of Δ ⁡ ( 2 , n ) \Delta(2,n) . Furthermore, we present computational evidence extending these results to k > 2 k>2 . In the process, we formulate a conjectural formula for the number of split positroidal subdivisions of Δ ⁡ ( k , n ) \Delta(k,n) for any k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and explore the deep connections between the polyhedral combinatorics of Δ ⁡ ( k , n ) \Delta(k,n) and the dual canonical basis of ℂ ⁡ [ Gr ⁡ ( k , n ) ] \mathbb{C}[\Gr(k,n)] .

Introduction

The hypersimplex
\Delta(k,n)
is a classical polytope arising in various topics like algebraic combinatorics, discrete geometry, and matroid theory. It was notably studied by Gelfand, Goresky, MacPherson, and Serganova in their work on the combinatorial geometry of matroids
[
13
]
. It is defined as the convex hull of all
0
–
1
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with exactly
k
entries equal to
1
. Subdivisions of
\Delta(k,n)
respecting matroidal structure appear naturally in the study of tropical varieties and tropical prevarieties associated to the Grassmanian
\Gr(k,n)

[
26
,
29
]
. This poset structure is captured geometrically by a polyhedral fan called the
secondary fan

[
14
]
, which encodes all regular subdivisions of the polytope. For matroidal subdivisions of
\Delta(k,n)
, the polyhedral fan encoding this poset is referred as the
Dressian

\Dr(k,n)

[
29
]
.

The homogeneous coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\Gr(k,n)]
admits a cluster algebra structure
[
25
]
. Canonical and dual canonical bases are important constructions in the representation theory of quantum groups by Lusztig
[
22
]
and Kashiwara
[
20
]
. The dual canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}[\Gr(k,n)]
is a distinguished basis for
\mathbb{C}[\Gr(k,n)]
. In the Grassmannian setting, elements of this basis can be indexed by rectangular semi-standard Young tableaux with
k
rows and entries in
[n]
, see
[
4
]
.

Speyer and Williams
[
27
]
introduced a piecewise-linear map from the positive tropical Grassmannian to a subfan of the secondary fan of
\Delta(k,n)
, thereby linking tropical geometry with polyhedral subdivisions. We leverage this map to associate to each dual canonical basis tableau a positroidal subdivision of
\Delta(k,n)
, revealing a connection between dual canonical bases and polyhedral combinatorics, see Theorem
4.1
.

We call a tableau prime if the corresponding dual canonical basis element in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
is prime, that is, it cannot be written as the product of two dual canonical basis elements. Our primary result concerns
\Gr(2,n)
: we prove that non-frozen prime tableaux correspond precisely to the coarsest positroidal subdivisions of
\Delta(2,n)
, i.e., those subdivisions not admitting any nontrivial refinement.
For
k\geq 3
, there are tableaux which are prime but the corresponding positroidal subdivisions of
\Delta(k,n)
are not coarsest. We conjecture that one side of the result holds: if the positroidal subdivision of
\Delta(k,n)
corresponding to a tableau
T
is coarsest, then
T
is a prime tableau.

A positroidal subdivision of
\Delta(k,n)
is called a split if it has exactly two maximal cells
[
18
]
.
Finally, we propose a conjectural enumeration formula for the number of split positroidal subdivisions of
\Delta(k,n)
for general
k\geq 2
: the number of split positroidal subdivision of
\Delta(k,n)
is

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{2}n(n-k-1),

see Conjecture
5.8
. Our results suggest deep interplay between positroidal subdivisions and the dual canonical basis.

Organization of the paper.

In Section
2
, we review hypersimplices, matroidal and positroidal subdivisions, and tropical Grassmannians. In Section
3
, we recall the relation between the dual canonical bases of Grassmannian cluster algebras and semistandard tableaux. In Section
4
, we prove that every element in the dual canonical basis of a Grassmannian cluster algebra induces a positroidal subdivision of the hypersimplex
\Delta(k,n)
under the map introduced by Speyer and Williams. In Section
5
, we study split positroidal subdivisions.

2.
Hypersimplices, matroidal and positroidal subdivisions, Tropical Grassmannians

In this section, we review hypersimplices, matroidal and positroidal subdivisions, and tropical Grassmannians.

2.1.
Hypersimplices, matroidal and positroidal subdivisions

A
matroid
of rank
k
on the ground set
E
is a nonempty collection
\mathsf{M}\subseteq\binom{|E|}{k}
of
k
-element subsets of
E
, called
bases
of
\mathsf{M}
, that satisfies the basis exchange axiom:
For any
I,J\in\mathsf{M}
and
a\in I
, there exists
b\in J
such that
I\setminus\{a\}\cup\{b\}\in\mathsf{M}
.

A matroid is called
representable
if it can be represented by columns of a matrix over some field
\mathbb{K}
. We index the columns of a
k\times n
matrix by the set
[n]
. A
positroid

\mathsf{P}
of rank
k
is a matroid
\mathsf{M}
that can be represented over
\mathbb{R}
by a
k\times n
-matrix
A
such that the maximal minor
p_{I}
is non-negative for each
I\in\binom{[n]}{k}
. We refer the reader to the book by Oxley
[
24
]
for further details on matroids.

A matroid intrinsically defines a polytope as follows
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a matroid on a ground set
E
. Then the
matroid polytope

P_{M}
is defined as

P_{M}=\conv\{e_{B}:B\text{ is a basis of }M\}

where
\{e_{i}:i\in E\}
is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{E}
and
e_{B}=e_{b_{1}}+\ldots+e_{b_{r}}
for
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
.

Matroid polytopes can be characterized geometrically as follows
[
13
]
,

Theorem 2.2
.

A collection
\mathcal{B}
of subsets of
[n]
is the set of bases of a matroid if and only if every edge of the polytope

P_{B}:=\conv\{e_{B}:B\in\mathcal{B}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

is a translate of
e_{i}-e_{j}
for some
i,j
.

The
hypersimplex

\Delta(k,n)
is defined as intersection of the unit cube
[0,1]^{n}
with the hyperplane
\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}=k
. When we consider the matroid
M
to be the
uniform matroid

\mathsf{U}_{k,n}
, then the corresponding matroid polytope is the hypersimplex, i.e.,
P_{\mathsf{U}_{k,n}}=\Delta(k,n)
.

Definition 2.3
.

For a polytope
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a
(polytopal) subdivision

\Sigma=\bigg\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{m}\bigg\}
is a collection of polytopes such that all of the following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

The intersection
P_{ij}:=P_{i}\cap P_{j}
, which can possibly be empty, is a face of both
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq m
.

(2)

The collection
\Sigma
covers
P
i.e. the union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}P_{i}=P
.

(3)

For each
1\leq i\leq m
, the vertex set
\mathrm{vert}(P_{i})\subseteq\mathrm{vert}(P)
.

The elements
P_{i}
are referred as
cells
of
\Sigma
. A polytopal subdivision is called a
triangulation
if all
P_{i}
are simplices. A polytopal subdivision is called
matroidal
if all
P_{i}
are matroid polytopes. Similarly, a polytopal subdivision is called
positroidal
if all
P_{i}
are matroid polytopes of positroids.

A subdivision
\Sigma
of a polytope
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
regular
if there exists a weight vector
w
such that if the vertices of
P
are lifted to heights provided by
w
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and subsequently the lower convex hull is projected back to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the subdivision
\Sigma
is retrieved.

A
split
subdivision is a subdivision with exactly two maximal cells
[
18
]
. It also known that any split subdivision is always regular
[
18
, Lemma 3.5]
. Two splits
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are said to be
compatible
if the corresponding split hyperplanes do not intersect in the interior of the polytope. It is known that any two splits of the hypersimplex
\Delta(k,n)
are compatible
[
18
, Corollary 5.6]
.

We also recall the notions of polyhedral complexes and polyhedral fans.

Definition 2.4
.

A set
\mathcal{P}
of polyhedra is called a
polyhedral complex
if

(1)

P\in\mathcal{P}
and
F
is a face of
P
, then it implies that
F\in\mathcal{P}
.

(2)

The intersection of any two polyhedra
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}
is a face of both
P
and
Q
.

A
polyhedral fan
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a polyhedral complex consisting of polyhedral cones.

We refer the reader to
[
31
,
6
]
for further details about polytopal combinatorics and polyhedral complexes.

2.2.
Tropical Grassmannians

The
Grassmannian

\Gr(k,n)
is the smooth projective variety corresponding to the vanishing set of the
Plücker ideal

\mathcal{I}_{k,n}
. An element
V
in the Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
is a collection of
n
vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{K}^{k}
spanning the space
\mathbb{K}^{k}
modulo the action of
\GL(k,n)
on the vectors. Let
A
be a
k\times n
-matrix consisting of column vectors
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}
and
A
is called the
representative matrix
of
V
. This defines a matroid
\mathsf{M}_{V}
whose bases are the
k
-subsets
I\subseteq[n]
such that
\det_{I}(A)\neq 0
where,
\det_{I}(A)
denotes the determinant of the
k\times k
submatrix of
A
with the column set
I\in\binom{[n]}{k}
.

An element
V\in\Gr(k,n)
is termed as
totally non-negative
if it has a representative
k\times n
matrix
A
such that
\det_{I}(A)\geq 0
, for all
I\in\binom{[n]}{k}
. The set of all totally non-negative
V\in\Gr(k,n)
is called the
totally non-negative Grassmannian

\Gr^{\geq 0}(k,n)
and
\Gr^{>0}(k,n)
is called the
positive Grassmannian
.

Tropical geometry is the study of vanishing of polynomials over the tropical semiring
\mathbb{T}=\{\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},\text{max},+\}
, where the locus of vanishing is the set of points where the maxima is achieved twice. Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{R}
represent the Puiseux series over field of complex numbers and real numbers respectively and
val
be the
valuation
map. Let
\mathcal{R}^{+}
represent those elements of
\mathcal{R}
where the coefficient of the unique lowest term is real and positive. The tropicalisation of the variety
V(I)
for and ideal
I\subset\mathcal{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is defined as

\Trop\bigl(V(I)\bigr)=\overline{\bigl\{{\rm val}(x)\;\bigm|\;x\in V(I)\cap(\mathcal{C}^{*})^{n}\bigr\}}.

Similarly, the
positive part
of a tropical variety
\text{Trop}(V(I))
is defined as

\Trop^{+}\bigl(V(I)\bigr)=\overline{\bigl\{{\rm val}(x)\;\bigm|\;x\in V(I)\cap(\mathcal{R}^{+})^{n}\bigr\}}.

The
tropical Grassmannian

\TGr(k,n)
is the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces
\Trop(f)
, where
f
ranges over all elements of the
Plücker ideal

\mathcal{I}_{k,n}
which is generated by the
quadratic Plücker relations

[
23
]
. The
Dressian

\Dr(k,n)
is the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces
\Trop(f)
, where
f
ranges over all three-term Plücker relations, that generate the
Plücker ideal
. Similarly, the
positive tropical Grassmannian
is the positive part of the tropical variety
\Trop^{+}\Gr(k,n)
which is obtained as the intersection of the positive part of all tropical hypersurfaces
\Trop^{+}(f)
, where
f
ranges over all elements of the Plücker ideal. The
positive Dressian

\Dr^{+}(k,n)
is the intersection of the positive part of all tropical hypersurfaces
\Trop^{+}(f)
,
where
f
ranges over all three-term Plücker relations. The underlying matroid for the definitions of the tropical Grassmannian and Dressian is the
uniform matroid

\mathsf{U}_{k,n}

[
27
]
.

The Dressian
\Dr(k,n)
enjoys an intricate relationship with the hypersimplex
\Delta(k,n)
which can be described as follows,

Theorem 2.5

(
[
29
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

A vector
w
lies in the Dressian
\Dr(k,n)
if and only if it induces a matroidal subdivision of the hypersimplex
\Delta(k,n)
.

An analogous result is also true when restricted to the positive Dressian
[
28
,
1
]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

A vector
w
lies in the Dressian
\Dr^{+}(k,n)
if and only if it induces a positroidal subdivision of the hypersimplex
\Delta(k,n)
.

It is known for
k=2
,
\TGr(2,n)=\Dr(2,n)

[
26
]
as polyhedral fans, but for (
k\geq 3
and
n\geq 6
),
\TGr(k,n)\neq\Dr(k,n)

[
17
]
. However, when restricted to just the positive part, we have the following result
[
28
,
1
]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

The positive tropical Grassmannian
\Trop^{+}\Gr(k,n)
equals the positive
Dressian
\Dr^{+}(k,n)
.

Theorem
2.7
is a powerful result and allows us to consider the positive tropical Grassmannian
\Trop^{+}\Gr(k,n)
and the positive
Dressian
\Dr^{+}(k,n)
interchangeably.

3.
Dual canonical bases and semistandard Young tableaux

In this section, we recall dual canonical bases
[
22
]
and the relations between the dual canonical bases of Grassmannian cluster algebras with semistandard tableaux
[
4
]
.

3.1.
Dual canonical basis

In this paper, we only need to use the case that Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is of type A. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra of type
A
over
\mathbb{C}
and consider its triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{-}
. Let
v
be an indeterminate, and let

U_{v}(\mathfrak{g})=U_{v}(\mathfrak{n})\otimes U_{v}(\mathfrak{h})\otimes U_{v}(\mathfrak{n}_{-})

be the corresponding Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum enveloping algebra over
\mathbb{C}(v)
, defined via a
v
-analogue of the Chevalley-Serre presentation of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. Lusztig
[
22
]
has defined a canonical basis
B
of
U_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
. It is a linear basis of
U_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
with nice positivity properties. The algebra
U_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
is endowed with a distinguished scalar product. Denote by
B^{*}
the basis of
U_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
adjoint to the canonical basis
B
with respect to this scalar product. Let
A_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
be the graded dual of the vector space
U_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
. It can be endowed with a multiplication coming from the comultiplication of
U_{v}(\mathfrak{g})
and it can be regarded as the quantum coordinate ring of the unipotent group
N
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}

[
12
]
. The basis
B^{*}
naturally corresponds to a basis of
A_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
and is referred to as the dual canonical basis of
A_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
. Upon specializing
v\to 1
, the basis
B^{*}
yields a basis
\mathcal{B}
for
\mathbb{C}[N]
, which is called the dual canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}[N]
. The basis
B^{*}
can be identified with a basis of
A_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
and
B^{*}
is called the dual canonical basis of
A_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
. When
v\to 1
,
B^{*}
become a basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbb{C}[N]
and it is called the dual canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}[N]
. In
[
16
]
, Hernandez and Leclerc showed that
\mathbb{C}[N]
can be categorified using a certain category
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
of representations of the quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
. There is a one to one correspondence between simple modules in
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
and dual canonical basis elements in
\mathbb{C}[N]
.

In type
A
the variety
N
is the variety consisting of all unipotent upper triangular matrices in
SL_{n}
. It is the big cell of the complete flag variety
\mathcal{F}\ell_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{r};n}
when
r=n-1
and
d_{i}=i
for
i\in[1,n]
. General partial flag variety
\mathcal{F}\ell_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{r};n}
also has a dual canonical basis and the basis elements can be identified with semistandard Young tableaux
[
3
,
21
]
. When
r=1
,
\mathcal{F}\ell_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{r};n}
is the Grassmannian variety
\Gr(k,n)
, where
k=d_{1}
. Denote by
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
the coordinate ring of the Grassmannian variety
\Gr(k,n)
. It is shown in
[
25
]
that
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
has a cluster algebra structure. The coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
can be categorified using a certain category
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
of representations of quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{k}})

[
15
]
, where
k+\ell+1=n
, and elements in the dual canonical basis of
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
are in one to one correspondence with semistandard tableaux with rectangular shapes and with
k
rows
[
4
]
. We describe this correspondence in the next subsection.

3.2.
Semistandard Young tableaux

A semistandard Young tableau is a Young tableau with weakly increasing rows and strictly increasing columns, see
[
10
]
. For
k,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, we denote by
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
the set of rectangular semistandard Young tableaux with
k
rows and with entries in
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
(with arbitrarly many columns). The empty tableau is denoted by
\mathds{1}
.

In the following, we recall a few definitions related to semistandard Young tableaux. For
S,T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
, let
S\cup T
be the row-increasing tableau whose
i
th row is the union of the
i
th rows of
S
and
T
(as multisets),
[
4
]
. We call
S
a factor of
T
, and write
S\subset T
, if the
i
th row of
S
is contained in that of
T
(as multisets), for
i\in[k]
. In this case, we define
\frac{T}{S}=S^{-1}T=TS^{-1}
to be the row-increasing tableau whose
i
th row is obtained by removing that of of
S
from that of
T
(as multisets), for
i\in[k]
. A tableau
T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
is trivial if each entry of
T
is one less than the entry below it. For any
T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
, we denote by
T_{\text{red}}\subset T
the semistandard tableau obtained by removing a maximal trivial factor from
T
. For trivial
T
, one has
T_{\text{red}}=\mathds{1}
. Let “
\sim
” be the equivalence relation on
S,T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
defined by:
S\sim T
if and only if
S_{\text{red}}=T_{\text{red}}
. We denote by
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n],\sim)
the set of
\sim
-equivalence classes. A one-column tableau is called a fundamental tableau if its entries are of the form
[j,j+k]\setminus\{a\}
for some
a\in[j+1,j+k-1]
. For
i\in[k-1]
,
j\in[n-k]
, denote by
T_{i,j}
the fundamental tableau with entries
[j,j+k]\setminus\{i+j\}
. A tableau
T
is said to have small gaps if each of its columns is a fundamental tableau. Then any tableau in
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
is
\sim
-equivalent to a unique small gap semistandard tableau.

Denote by
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n],\sim)
the set of equivalence classes of tableaux in
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
. We also call equivalence classes in
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n],\sim)
tableaux. Denote by
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n,\sim)]
the quotient of
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
by the ideal generated by
p_{i,i+1,\ldots,i+k-1}-1
,
i\in[1,n-k+1]
.
It is shown in
[
4
]
that there is a one to one correspondence between dual canonical basis elements in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n,\sim)]
and tableaux in
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n],\sim)
. A map
{\operatorname{ch}}(T)
is defined in Section 5 of
[
4
]
which sends a tableau
T
in
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n],\sim)
to an element in the dual canonical basis of
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n,\sim)]
. A map
{\operatorname{ch}}(T)
is also defined in Section 5 of
[
4
]
for
T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
which conjecturally sends
T
to a dual canonical basis element in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
. We say that
T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
is prime if there is no non-trivial tableaux
T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime}\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
such that
{\operatorname{ch}}(T)={\operatorname{ch}}(T^{\prime}){\operatorname{ch}}(T^{\prime\prime})
.

4.
From dual canonical bases to positroidal subdivisions

In this section, we prove that every element in the dual canonical basis of a Grassmannian cluster algebra induces a positroidal subdivision of the hypersimplex
\Delta(k,n)
under the map introduced by Speyer and Williams.

The total number of fundamental tableaux in
{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
is
(k-1)(n-k)
. Let
T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
. Factor
T
as union of fundamental tableaux
T\sim\cup_{i,j}T_{i,j}^{\cup c_{i,j}}
. Define
v_{T}=\sum_{i,j}c_{i,j}e_{i,j}
, where
e_{i,j}
’s form the standard basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{(k-1)(n-k)}
, see Section 6 in
[
8
]
. Denote by
F_{k,n}
(see
[
27
]
) the map from
{\mathbb{R}}^{(k-1)(n-k)}
to
{\rm Span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{e^{J}:J\in\binom{[n]}{k}\}/L
, where
L
is the lineality space, defined as follows

\displaystyle F_{k,n}(y)=\sum_{J\in\binom{[n]}{k}}P_{J}(y)e^{J},

where
P_{J}
is the tropicalization of Plücker coordinate
p_{J}
evaluating on the web matrix
W
. By definition,
F_{k,n}(v_{T})=0
for any trivial tableau
T
. We denote
\wt(T)=\wt_{T}=(P_{J}(v_{T}))_{J\in\binom{[n]}{k}}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
,
F_{k,n}(v_{T})
induces a positroidal subdivision of
\Delta(k,n)
.

Proof.

We first realize that given
T\in{\rm SSYT}(k,[n])
, the image of the map
F_{k,n}
spans the space
{\rm Span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{e^{J}:J\in\binom{[n]}{k}\}
and by
[
27
]
we know that the Plücker coordinates
\{p_{J}\}_{J\in\binom{[n]}{k}}
defined by elements in the web matrix lie in the positive Grassmannian
\Gr^{+}(k,n)
. By definition of the positive tropical Grassmannian, the corresponding tropicalizations of the Plücker coordinates

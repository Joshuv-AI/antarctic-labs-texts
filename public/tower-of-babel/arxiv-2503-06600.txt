Finite fields whose members are the sum of a potent and a 4-potent
Stephen D. Cohen, Peter V. Danchev, Tomás Oliveira e Silva

Abstract
. We classify those finite fields 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} whose members are the sum of an n n -potent element with n > 1 n>1 and a 4-potent element. It is shown that there are precisely ten non-trivial pairs ( q , n ) (q,n) for which this is the case. This continues a recent publication by Cohen-Danchev et al. in Turk. J. Math. (2024) in which the tripotent version was examined in-depth as well as it extends recent results of this branch established by Abyzov-Tapkin in Sib. Math. J. (2024).

1.
Introduction and Motivation

Throughout this article, let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field of order a prime power
q=p^{v}
, where
p
is a prime and
v
is a positive integer. Further, let
n
be an integer strictly exceeding 1. If
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
is such that
a^{n}=a
, then
a
is called an
n
-potent
, and we denote the set of all
n
-potents in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by
C_{n}
. Obviously, if
(q-1)|n
, then
C_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover, if
m
denotes
\mathrm{gcd}(n,q-1)
, then
C_{m}=C_{n}
. Hence, the full range of non-trivial sets
C_{n}
is obtained by restricting
n
to the integers for which
(n-1)|(q-1)
and
n>1
.

The original motivation for this line of investigation comes from problems involving the notion of potents in ring theory and, in particular, in some aspects of matrix theory. This has inspired the following general problem.

General Problem.
Find those finite fields each of whose members is the sum of an
m
-potent and an
n
-potent, where
m>1
and
n>1
are arbitrary integers.

A discussion of the case in which
m=2
relating to the sum of an
n
-potent and a 2-potent (= idempotent) was given in
[
2
]
where a little finite field theory was used. Likewise, the article
[
1
]
explored the case with
m=3
relating to a sum between an
n
-potent and a 3-potent (= tripotent). This involved more serious finite field theory which is the subject of
[
6
]
. (See also
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
for some other closely related material.) It follows that is sensible to isolate the following problem as a worthy object of study in finite field theory. Concretely, we are interested in whether
C_{n}+C_{4}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
whenever
n\geq 2
, formulated more exactly as follows:

Restricted Problem.
Characterize those finite fields for which every element is a sum of an
n
-potent (where
n>1
) and a 4-potent. As noted above, we can assume
(n-1)|(q-1)
, and also
n<q
, to avoid the trivial case in which
n=q
(in which case
C_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
).

By way of comparison, the existence theorem implied by Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 2.1 of
[
1
]
can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
q
is a prime power and
n
is a positive integer such that
n<q
and
n-1
divides
q-1
. Then, every element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the sum of a tripotent and an
n
-potent if, and only if,
n=\frac{q-1}{2}
and
q\in\{3,5,7,9\}
.

The main result of this paper, motivating our writing of the present paper, is to give a complete answer to the stated above Restricted Problem. Specifically, we will prove in the sequel the following statement.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
q
is a prime power and
n
is a positive integer such that
n<q
and
n-1
divides
q-1
. Then, every element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the sum of a
4
-potent and an
n
-potent if, and only if,
(q,n)
is one of the pairs in the set

\{4,2),(7,3),(7,4),(13,7),(19,10),(25,13),(31,16),(43,22),(49,25),(103,52)\}.

The main part of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is the theoretical discussion in Section
2
, but this is supplemented by vital computations described in the Appendix. Some closely related applications of Theorem
1.2
in ring theory may be given in further work, and perhaps there will be relevant applications of these results to other areas of algebra such as the matrix theory.

Given a prime power
q=p^{v}
, the question we explore is to discern values of
n<q

for which every element
\gamma

In full generality

. In much of the discussion, however,

will be odd, in which case let
\lambda
denote the quadratic character on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus,
\lambda(0)=0

\lambda(\gamma)=\pm 1
according as
\gamma
is a non-zero square or non-square.

First, suppose
q\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\;3)
. Then,
\mathbb{F}_{q}
contains no cube root of unity besides
1
and
C_{4}=C_{2}=\{0,1\}
. Further, suppose

C_{n}\cup(C_{n}+1)=\mathbb{F}_{q},\quad n<q,\;(n-1)|(q-1).

(2.1)

Then, easily
q
is odd and
n-1=(q-1)/2
, i.e.,
n=(q+3)/2
, and
C_{n}
comprises the set of all squares in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus, for every member
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, either
\gamma
or
\gamma+1
must be a square. In other words, there cannot be a consecutive pair of non-squares in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. On the other hand, the number of pairs of consecutive elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that are both non-squares can be expressed as

\frac{1}{4}\sum_{{\alpha\neq 0,-1}}(1-\lambda(\alpha))((1-\lambda(\alpha+1)),

where the sum is over all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
except
0
or
-1
.
As in the method used in
[
1
]
, this number is exactly
\frac{1}{4}(q-2+\lambda(-1))\geq(q-3)/4
, and so is positive provided
q>3
. We conclude that
(\ref{2P})
is impossible in this case.

From now on suppose
3|(q-1)
. In particular, if
q=2^{v}
, then
v
is even and
C_{4}
is the subfield
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. If
q
is odd, then

and in every case

C_{4}=\{0,1,z,z^{2}=-z-1\}
, where
z
is a primitive cube root of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
z=1/z^{2}
, then
\lambda(z)=1
when
q
is odd. Let
y=z^{2}=-1-z
. Now, the assumption
({}\ref{4P})
takes the form

C_{n}\cup(C_{n}+1)\cup(C_{n}+z)\cup(C_{n}+y)=\mathbb{F}_{q},\quad n<q,(n-1)|(q-1).

(2.2)

Therefore, evidently,
n\geq(q-1)/4
. It follows that the possible value of
n
that remain are
\frac{q-1}{i}+1
, where

. Here, the values of

can only occur if
q
is odd.

2.1.
q
odd and
n=\frac{q+3}{4}

In considering
(\ref{4P})
, we first dispose of the case in which

; thus,
n-1=(q-1)/4
, where necessarily
4|(q-1)
, i.e.,
q\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\;12)
and
C_{n}
is the set of all 4th powers in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
4|q-1
, then
C_{4}\subseteq C_{n}
. Since
0\in C_{n}
, then
1\in C_{n}\cap(C_{n}+1)
. Further,
z\in C_{n}\cap(C_{n}+z)
and
y\in C_{n}\cap(C_{n}+y)
. Finally,

. Now, the cardinality of each set
C_{n}+k,(k\in C_{4}
) is
(q+3)/4
, so that the cardinality of
C_{n}\cup(C_{n}+1)\cup(C_{n}+z)\cup(C_{n}+y)
is at most

. Hence, (
2.2
) cannot hold in this case.

2.2.
q
odd and
n=\frac{q+1}{2}

Next, suppose
n-1=(q-1)/2
, i.e.,
n=(q+1)/2
and
C_{n}
is the set of squares (including
0
) in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Here
(\ref{4P})
would imply that, given any member
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, at least one of
\gamma,\gamma+1,\gamma+z,\gamma+y
is a square. In other words, not all of these four elements are non-squares. To this end, define
N_{q}
to be the number of elements
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that each of
\alpha-1,\alpha-z,\alpha-y
is a non-square. Then,

N_{q}=\frac{1}{16}\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}(1-\lambda(\alpha))(1-\lambda(\alpha-1))(1-\lambda(\alpha-z))(1-\lambda(\alpha-y)).

Now, let

S_{1}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha),\;S_{2}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha-1),\;S_{3}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha-z),\;S_{4}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha-y);

T_{1}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-1)),\;T_{2}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-z)),\;T_{3}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-y)),

T_{4}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda((\alpha-1)(\alpha-z)),\;T_{5}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda((\alpha-1)(\alpha-y)),\;T_{6}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda((\alpha-z)(\alpha-y));

U_{1}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-z)),\quad U_{2}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-y)),

U_{3}=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-z)(\alpha-y)),\quad U_{4}=\sum_{\alpha-\notin C_{4}}\lambda((\alpha-1)(\alpha-z)(\alpha-y)),

\quad V=\sum_{\alpha\notin C_{4}}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha^{3}-1)).

Thus,

N_{q}=\frac{1}{16}\left(q-4-\sum_{i=1}^{4}S_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{6}T_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{4}U_{i}+V\right).

(2.3)

We proceed to evaluate or estimate these various sums, beginning with the
S_{i}
. We have

S_{1}=\sum_{\alpha\neq 1,y,z}\lambda((\alpha)=-\lambda(1)-\lambda(z)-\lambda(y)=-3,

S_{2}=\sum_{\alpha\neq 0,y,z}\lambda(\alpha-1)=\lambda(-1)-\lambda(z-1)-\lambda(y-1))=-\lambda(-1)-\lambda(z-1)-\lambda(-z-2).

Similarly,

S_{3}=-\lambda(-z)-\lambda(1-z)-\lambda(-2z-1),

S_{4}=-\lambda(1+z)-\lambda(z+2)-\lambda(1+2z).

To find expressions for the

, we recall that, if
f
is a monic quadratic polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
(with distinct roots), then thanks to
[
5

we receive that

\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\lambda(f(\gamma))=-1.

Accordingly,

\begin{split}T_{1}=\sum_{\alpha\neq y,z}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-1))=-1-\lambda(z(z-1))-\lambda((-z-1)(-z-2)&=-1-\lambda(-2z-1)-\lambda(2z+1);\end{split}

\begin{split}T_{2}&=\sum_{\alpha\neq 1,y}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-z))=-1-\lambda(1-z))-\lambda((-z-1)(-z-2))=-\lambda(1-z)-\lambda((z+1)(2z+1))\\
&=-1-\lambda(1-z)-\lambda(z-1),\end{split}

since
z^{2}=-z-1
.

Similarly,

T_{3}=\sum_{\alpha\neq 1,z}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha+z+1))=-1-\lambda(z+2)-\lambda(-z-2);

T_{4}=\sum_{\alpha\neq 0,y}\lambda((\alpha-1)(\alpha-z))=-1-2\lambda(z);

\begin{split}T_{5}=\sum_{\alpha\neq 0,z}\lambda((\alpha-1)(\alpha+z+1))&=-1-\lambda(-z-1)-\lambda(-3z-3)\\
&=-2-\-\lambda(z+1),\end{split}

since
\lambda(-3)=1
and
\lambda(-z-1)=\lambda(z^{2})=1
;

T_{6}=\sum_{\alpha\neq 0,1}\lambda((\alpha-z)(\alpha+z+1))=-1-\lambda(1)-\lambda(3)=-2-\lambda(-1).

Aggregating the working so far, after much cancelation, we conclude simply that

N_{q}=\frac{1}{16}\left(q-10+\lambda(-1)-\sum_{i=1}^{4}U_{i}+V\right).

(2.4)

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\chi
is a multiplicative character of order
d>1
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Further, suppose
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is a polynomial whose set of zeros in its splitting field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has cardinality
m
, and that
f
is not a constant multiple of a
d
-th power. Then,

\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(f(\gamma))\leq(m-1)\sqrt{q}.

It now follows from Lemma
2.1
that
U_{1}=\sum_{\alpha\neq y}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-z))
has absolute value at most
2\sqrt{q}+1
, so that
-U_{1}\geq-2\sqrt{q}-1
. Similarly,
-U_{i}\geq-2\sqrt{q}-1
, for
i\leq 4
. Likewise,
V=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\lambda(\alpha(\alpha^{3}-1))\geq-3\sqrt{q}
.

In fact, we can improve the estimate for
\displaystyle{U_{4}=\sum_{\alpha\neq 0}\lambda(\alpha^{3}-1)}
. Let
\eta
be a cubic character on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Setting
\alpha^{3}=\beta
, we see that

U_{4}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\sum_{\beta\neq 0}\big(\lambda(\beta-1)\eta(\beta)\Big).

Hence,

U_{4}=\frac{\lambda(-1)}{3}(-1+J(\lambda,\eta)+J(\lambda,\eta^{2})),

where

is the Jacobi sum
\displaystyle{\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x)\eta(1-x)}
(see
[
5
]
). Since the Jacobi sums have absolute value

, we deduce that
|U_{4}|\leq\frac{1+2\sqrt{q}}{3}
.
It next follows from

and the above bounds that

because

and where the constant term

could be reduced slightly. Certainly,

is guaranteed to be positive whenever

.

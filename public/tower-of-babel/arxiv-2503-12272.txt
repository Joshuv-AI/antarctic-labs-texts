On the mean exit time from a ball for a symmetric stable process Thanks: M. Ryznar was supported in part by the National Science Centre, Poland, grant no. 2023/49/B/ST1/03964,
Michał Ryznar

Abstract
. Getoor in [ 3 ] calculated the mean exit time from a ball for the standard isotropic α \alpha -stable process in ℝ d \mathds{R}^{d} starting from the interior of the ball. The purpose of this note is to show that, up to multplicative constant, the same formula is valid for any symmetric α \alpha -stable process.



1.
Introduction

Let
X_{t}
be a
d
-dimensional symmetric
\alpha
-stable processes of index
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\in{\mathds{N}}
. Let
\mathbb{E}^{x}=\mathbb{E}^{x}(\ \cdot\ |X_{0}=x)
and let
B_{r}=\{z\in\mathds{R}^{d};|z|<r\}
. In this note we deal with the mean exit of the process
X
from
B_{r}
, that is we study

\mathbb{E}^{x}\tau^{X}_{B_{r}},

where

\tau^{X}_{B_{r}}=\inf\{t>0,|X_{t}|\geq r\},\quad r>0.

Getoor in
[
3
]
studied the mean exit time from a ball in the case of the standard isotropic stable process
\tilde{X}
and he calculated that

\mathbb{E}^{x}\tau^{\tilde{X}}_{B_{r}}=C(\alpha,d)(r^{2}-|x|^{2})_{+}^{\alpha/2}.

It is a bit surprising that the above formula holds (up to a multiplicative constant) for any symmetric stable process. Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\nu
be the Lévy measure of a symmetric
\alpha
-stable process
X_{t}
. Then

E^{x}\tau^{X}_{B_{r}}=\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}}{\nu(B_{1}^{c})}(r^{2}-|x|^{2})_{+}^{\alpha/2},\quad x\in R^{d},

where
\kappa_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)\Gamma(1+\alpha/2)}
.

Our approach relies on the one-dimensional version of the result of
[
3
]
. Actually we apply the fact that the function
\mathds{R}\ni u\to(r^{2}-|u|^{2})_{+}^{\alpha/2}
is in the domain of the generator of the
L^{1}
semigroup generated by the one-dimensional standard
\alpha
-stable process and the value of the generator is a negative constant in the interval
(-r,r)
(see
[
3
, Theorem 5.2.]
). Next, using this result, we easily show that the value of a generator of any
d
-dimensional symmetric stable process evaluated (pointwise) for a function
w(x)=(r^{2}-|x|^{2})_{+}^{\alpha/2},\quad x\in R^{d}
is equal to a negative constant if
|x|<r
. This allows to apply the local Ito formula (see eg.
[
2
, Lemma 3.8]
) to conclude our main result.

2.
Mean exit time calculations

Let
{\mathcal{S}}=\{z\in\mathds{R}^{d};|z|=1\}
be the standard unit sphere in
\mathds{R}^{d}

Let
\mu
be a finite positive measure on
\mathcal{S}
.
Let
A(x),x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
be a family of
d\times d
matrices.
For a given
0<\alpha<2
we consider the operator of the following form.

\mathcal{K}f(x)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[f(x+A(x)zw)-f(x)\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}}\mu(dz),

for any Borel function
f:\mathds{R}^{d}\to\mathds{R}
and any
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
such that the right hand side exists in the sense of principal value integral.

Next, for a given vector
v\neq 0
in
\mathds{R}^{d}
let us introduce

\mathcal{K}_{v}f(x)=\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[f(x+vw)-f(x)\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}},x\in\mathds{R}^{d},

again as the principal value integral.
Let
v^{*}=v/|v|
. By a simple change of variables we have

(1)

\mathcal{K}_{v}f(x)=|v|^{\alpha}\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[f(x+v^{*}w)-f(x)\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}}=|v|^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{v^{*}}f(x).

For
r>0
we define

S_{r}(x)=c_{\alpha}(r^{2}-|x|^{2})_{+}^{\alpha/2},\quad x\in\mathds{R}^{d},

where

c_{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)\Gamma(1+\alpha/2)}.

The following simple lemma is a key observation for the rest of this note.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
v\in\mathds{R}^{d}
, then

\mathcal{K}_{v}S_{r}(x)=-|v|^{\alpha},|x|<r.

Proof.

By (
1
), it is clear that it is enough to prove the lemma for
|v|=1
.
Next, we claim that we can assume that
v
can be taken as
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)
. Indeed, if
R
is a rotation such that
Rv=e_{1}
, then we have

\mathcal{K}_{v}S_{r}(x)=\mathcal{K}_{v}(S_{r}\circ R)(x)=\mathcal{K}_{e_{1}}S_{r}(Rx),|x|<r.

Now, we recall
the one-dimensional version of the result obtained by Getoor
[
3
, Theorem 5.2.]
. Let
s_{r}(u)=c_{\alpha}(r^{2}-|u|^{2})^{\alpha/2}_{+},u\in\mathds{R}
, then
for
-r<u<r
, where
r>0

(2)

\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[s_{r}(u+w)-s_{r}(u)\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}}=-1.

Actually in
[
3
]
the above equality was proved almost surely, but it is not difficult to show that the left hand side is a continuous function of
u\in(-r,r)
, hence it must hold point-wise on
(-r,r)
.
Next for
|x|<r
we have
|x_{1}|<\sqrt{r^{2}-|\tilde{x}|^{2}}
, where
\tilde{x}=x-x_{1}e_{1}
and, by (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{e_{1}}S_{r}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[(r^{2}-|x+we_{1}|^{2})^{\alpha/2}_{+}-(r^{2}-|x|^{2})^{\alpha/2}_{+}\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[(r^{2}-|\tilde{x}|^{2}-|x_{1}+w|^{2})^{\alpha/2}_{+}-(r^{2}-|\tilde{x}|^{2}-|x_{1}|^{2})^{\alpha/2}_{+}\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[s_{\sqrt{r^{2}-|\tilde{x}|^{2}}}(x_{1}+w)-s_{\sqrt{r^{2}-|\tilde{x}|^{2}}}(x_{1})\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}}=-1.

This implies that

\mathcal{K}_{v}S_{r}(x)=\mathcal{K}_{e_{1}}S_{r}(Rx)=-1,

since
|R(x)|=|x|<r
. ∎

Corollary 2.2
.

\mathcal{K}S_{r}(x)=-\int_{\mathcal{S}}|A(x)z|^{\alpha}\mu(dz),|x|<r.

If
A(x)
is an isometry for all
|x|<r
, then

\mathcal{K}S_{r}(x)=-\mu(\mathcal{S})=-|\mu|,|x|<r.

Proof.

Note that, due to Lemma
2.1
, we have

\mathcal{K}S_{r}(x)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{K}_{A(x)z}S_{r}(x)\mu(dz)=-\int_{\mathcal{S}}|A(x)z|^{\alpha}\mu(dz).

∎

Now, we are in a position to calculate the mean exit time of a ball for arbitrary symmetric stable process.

proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the scaling property it is enough to prove the result for
r=1
. Let
\nu
be the Lévy measure of the process
X_{t}
, then we can represent
\nu
in polar coordinates
(z,r),z\in\mathcal{S},r>0
as

\nu(dx)=\mu(dz)\frac{dr}{r^{1+\alpha}},

where the measure
\mu
is called the spectral measure of the process.
By
\mathcal{K}_{\nu}
we denote the generator of the process.

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\nu}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p.v.\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left[f(x+y)-f(x)\right]\nu(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left[f(x+y)-f(x)-\nabla f(x)\cdot y{\mathbf{1}}_{|y|\leq 1}\right]\nu(dy)

We observe that the above integral is finite for any
x
such that
f
is locally
C^{2}
class in a neighborhood of
x
and bounded on
\mathds{R}^{d}
.
Using polar coordinates and symmetry we can represent
\mathcal{K}_{\nu}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\nu}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[f(x+zw)-f(x)\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}}\mu(dz)

,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\int_{\mathds{R}}\left[f(x+zw)-f(x)-\nabla f(x)\cdot zw{\mathbf{1}}_{|w|\leq 1}\right]\,\frac{dw}{|w|^{1+\alpha}}\mu(dz)

Let
0<r<1
. By
\tau_{r}
we denote
\tau^{X}_{B_{r}}
. Then
S=S_{1}
is of
C^{2}(B_{(1+r)/2})
class (all first and second order partial derivatives are bounded in
B_{(1+r)/2}
).
Since
X_{t}
is a pure jump process, from the local Ito formula formula ( see
[
2
, Lemma 3.8.]
) it follows that

(3)

\displaystyle S(X_{t\wedge\tau_{r}})-S(X_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0^{+}}^{t\wedge\tau_{r}}\nabla S(X_{u-})\cdot dX_{u}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{0<u\leq t\wedge\tau_{r},|\Delta X_{u}|>0}S(X_{u})-S(X_{{u-}})-\nabla S(X_{u-})\cdot\Delta X_{u}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{t}+J_{t}.

We can split the intgral
I
as the sum of two itegrals where we consider small and large jumps. Let
X^{1}_{t}
and
X^{2}_{t}
be two independent processes with Lévy measures
\nu_{1}=\nu|_{\overline{B}_{1}}
and
\nu_{2}=\nu-\nu_{1}
, respectively. Then we can take

X_{t}=X^{1}_{t}+X^{2}_{t}

and then we have

I_{t}=\int_{0^{+}}^{t\wedge\tau_{r}}\nabla S(X_{u-})dX^{1}_{u}+\int_{0^{+}}^{t\wedge\tau_{r}}\nabla S(X_{u-})dX^{2}_{u}:=I_{t}^{1}+I_{t}^{2}

Note that
X^{2}_{t}
is a compound Poisson process and its jumps are the big jumps of the process
X_{t}
, hence

I_{t}^{2}=\sum_{0<u\leq t\wedge\tau_{r},|\Delta X_{u}|>1}\nabla S(X_{u-})\cdot\Delta X_{u}.

Therefore we can rewrite (
3
) as

(4)

\displaystyle S(X_{t\wedge\tau_{r}})-S(X_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{t}^{1}+\sum_{0<u\leq t\wedge\tau_{r},\Delta X_{u}\neq 0}S(X_{u})-S(X_{{u-}})-\nabla S(X_{u-})\cdot\Delta X_{u}\mathbf{1}_{|\Delta X_{u}|\leq 1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{t}^{1}+J_{t}^{1}.

We claim that
I_{t}^{1}
being a local martingale is in fact a martingale. This follows (see
[
4
, Corrolary 3, p.73 ]
) since its quadratic variation process
[I^{1},I^{1}]_{t}
is integrable. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}[I^{1},I^{1}]_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}d\sum_{u\leq t\wedge\tau_{r},|\Delta X_{u}|\leq 1}\max_{v\leq t\wedge\tau_{r}}(|\nabla S(X_{v-})|^{2})|\Delta X_{u}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle dC(r)\left(|x|^{2}+\mathbb{E}^{x}\sum_{0<u\leq t,|\Delta X_{u}|\leq 1}|\Delta X_{u}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dC(r)\left(|x|^{2}+t\int_{|y|\leq 1}|y|^{2}\nu(dy)\right)<\infty,

where

C(r)=\max_{|x|<r}|\nabla S(x)|^{2}<\infty.

Next, using Lévy’s system formula (see
[
1
, Lemma 4.7]
with
g(u)=\mathbf{1}_{(0,t\wedge\tau_{r}]}(u)
) we have

(5)

\mathbb{E}^{x}J^{1}_{t}=\mathbb{E}^{x}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{r}}du\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(S(X_{u}+y)-S(X_{{u}})-\nabla S(X_{u})\cdot y\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq 1}\right)\nu(dy).

To justify that we can use Lévy’s system formula we observe that

\left|S(x+y)-S(x)-\nabla S(x)\cdot y\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq 1}\right|\leq c\min(|y|^{2},1),

if
|x|\leq r
,
where
c=c(r)
.

Note that for
u<\tau_{r}

\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\left(S(X_{u}+y)-S(X_{{u}})-\nabla S(X_{u})\cdot y\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq 1}\right)\nu(dy)=\mathcal{K}_{\nu}S(X_{{u}})=-\frac{1}{2}|\mu|,

where the last equality follows from Corollary
2.2
if we take
A(x)
to be the identity matrix for all
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
.
Hence, by (
5
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}^{x}J^{1}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}|\mu|\mathbb{E}^{x}\tau_{r}

Since
\mathbb{E}^{x}I^{1}_{t}=0
, as
I^{1}_{t}
is a martingale, by (
4
), we arrive at

\mathbb{E}^{x}(S(X_{t\wedge\tau_{r}}))-S(x)=-\frac{1}{2}|\mu|\mathbb{E}^{x}(t\wedge\tau_{r}).

Letting
t\to\infty
, by bounded convergence theorem,

\mathbb{E}^{x}(S(X_{\tau_{r}}))=S(x)-\frac{1}{2}|\mu|\mathbb{E}^{x}\tau_{r}.

Observing that

\mathbb{E}^{x}(S(X_{\tau_{r}}))\leq c_{\alpha}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha/2}

and letting
r\uparrow 1
, we get

S(x)=\frac{1}{2}|\mu|\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\mathbb{E}^{x}\tau_{r}=\frac{1}{2}|\mu|\mathbb{E}^{x}\tau_{1}.

Next we note that

\nu(|y|\geq 1)=|\mu|\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{1+\alpha}}=\frac{|\mu|}{\alpha}.

Since
\frac{2c_{\alpha}}{\alpha}=\kappa_{\alpha}

the proof is completed.
∎

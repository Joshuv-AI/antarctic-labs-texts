The Antimicrobial Activity of Zinc Oxide Nanoparticles incorporated into Alginate Impression Material: An In vitro Study
Authors: Hamzah Al-Nema, Luma Al-Nema, Ahmed Al-Ali
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and Aim:In dental clinics, disinfecting alginate impression materials is a critical practice to prevent cross-infection. Recently, zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnO NPs) have been explored for their potential antimicrobial properties, making them promising additives for dental materials. This study investigates the antimicrobial activity of ZnO NPs incorporated into alginate impression materials and assesses the impact on material flow. The objective of this in vitro study was to evaluate the antibacterial and antifungal efficacy of ZnO NPs when incorporated into alginate impression material and to examine its effect on the material’s flow properties.Materials and Methods:Zinc oxide nanoparticles were incorporated into an irreversible hydrocolloid alginate impression material at varying concentrations. The antimicrobial properties were assessed using the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and minimum fungicidal concentration (MFC) for antifungal activity against Candida albicans, and the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) for antibacterial activity against Staphylococcus aureus. The well-diffusion test was employed to observe zone inhibition. The flow properties of the modified alginate were evaluated using a standardized flow test. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was utilized to observe the morphological characteristics and nanoparticle distribution in the alginate matrix. Data were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Duncan’s multiple range test, with a significance level set at P ≤ 0.05.Results:The results showed that the MIC for ZnO NPs against Candida albicans was 5% by weight, and the MFC was 10% by weight. For Staphylococcus aureus, the MIC was also 5%, with the MBC at 10%. The incorporation of ZnO NPs resulted in a significant reduction in the flow of the alginate material. SEM images revealed a random distribution of ZnO NPs within the alginate matrix, suggesting uniform incorporation.Conclusion:The incorporation of zinc oxide nanoparticles into alginate impression materials conferred both antibacterial and antifungal properties, with effective antimicrobial activity observed at concentrations as low as 5%. However, the addition of ZnO NPs led to a decrease in the material’s flow, which may affect the handling characteristics of the alginate. Clinically, the antimicrobial properties of ZnO NP-enhanced alginate could reduce the risk of cross-contamination in dental settings, but the alteration in flow must be considered when selecting or modifying impression materials for clinical use. Future studies should focus on optimizing the concentration of ZnO NPs to balance antimicrobial efficacy with material performance.

INTRODUCTION

In dental practice, the widely used impression material is alginate, which is an elastic-irreversible hydrocolloid material.[1]

They produced excellent details even in the presence of undercuts, making them the original impression material used in dentistry.[2]

In dental impressions. Blood and oral fluids are in contact with the materials during the impression-making process, providing an environment for a variety of microorganisms. Inadequate disinfection of the imprints increases the risk of contamination and cross-infection.[3,4]

The oral cavity is a natural host for many microorganisms, and it was found that saliva could be mixed in the impression material.[5] Numerous disinfection techniques, including spraying and immersing, have been employed to alleviate this problem. Regrettably, these techniques are time-consuming and might affect some of the alginate’s mechanical qualities.[6]

Nanoparticles having their unique characteristics and expanded applications for different fields.[7]

The antimicrobial activity of zinc oxide nanoparticles has gained notable interest worldwide, especially by the accomplishment of the nanotechnology. Zinc oxide nanoparticles display appealing antimicrobial qualities because of their large specific surface area and small particle size, causing high particle surface activity.[8] ZnO nanoparticles have attracted much attention because of their extraordinary capacity to impede the growth and spread of bacteria.[9]

The nanoparticles of zinc oxide demonstrate biological activities that had been highly examined. An active element is zinc, with important properties.[10,11] It has been used in many applications. ZnO is used as a food additive given the US Food and Drug Administration’s approval.[12] ZnO-NPs caught significant attention due to its unique chemical and physical properties, for example, high catalytic activity, antimicrobial nature, high binding energy, photochemical activity, ultra-violet filtering properties, and biocompatibility.[13,14,15]

As antibacterial agents, zinc oxide and chitosan nanoparticles have been added to the imprint materials without negatively impacting its qualities.[16] The null hypothesis of this study states that adding zinc oxide particles to alginate has no effect on the antimicrobial properties of the materials. This study aims to assess the antimicrobial properties of dental alginate integrated with ZnO NPs against Candida albicans and S. aureus, also the evaluation of the flow of alginate.

MEASUREMENT OF ANTIMICROBIAL EFFECT

Finding the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC)

The MIC is commonly defined as the minimum concentration of a certain antimicrobial, which prevents the growth of a specific microorganism after a definite incubation period.[17] The lowest concentration that inhibited the activity of 99% of organisms was considered the MBC Haghgoo et al.[18] In this study, the agar and broth dilution method was used for the estimation of the MBC and MIC.[19] The MIC of sonicated ZnO-NP in culture broth (Mueller–Hinton) was determined using this method, via making serial dilutions (10%, 5%, 2.5%, 1.25%, 0.6%, and 0.3%) from a 100 mg/mL stock of ZnO nanoparticles with Candida albicans or S. aureus cultured in and incubated for 24 h at 37°C. A common wire loop was used, a loopful (10 µL) of a culture of Candida albicans, with the 0.5 McFarland standard,[20] that had been introduced into the test tubes containing (1) mL in different concentrations of ZnO nanoparticles in the nutrient broth. Correspondingly, this test had then been repeated for S. aureus. After a 24-h incubation period at 37°C, the tubes were checked for turbidity or growth. Then, a loopful of the nutrient broth from every test tube which did not show the growth was inoculated in the nutrient agar plate. Then, equivalent amounts of the sterile broth had then been added to the cultures in test tubes, and then they were incubated for 24 h in 37°C. After that, the agar plates and tubes had been observed for turbidity or growth by the examiner’s eye.[21]

The antimicrobial effect evaluation by agar well-diffusion test

The antimicrobial activity of the ZnO-NPs was assessed by the well-diffusion test. Zinc oxide nanoparticles were weighed, the inoculums were adapted to the McFarland standard of 0.5 Scale, and the culture was prepared on the MH agar plates. Three plates were swabbed with the culture of the bacteria. The nanomaterials were mixed with distilled water by a sonicator, for 5 min in different concentrations, and then mixed with the impression material in compliance with the manufacturer’s guidelines. Four concentrations were used: 1.25%, 2.5%, 5%, and 10 wt %.The prepared samples were added in the specific wells on the swabbed plates. At 37°C, the plates were incubated for 24 h, and then the inhibition zone had been measured in millimeters.[22]

The antimicrobial activity was calculated through the use of agar-well diffusion test (n = 3). The alginate impression material was injected into the agar plate holes that consist of the cultures of Staphylococcus aureus or Candida albicans, and then they were incubated for 24°h at 37°C. The surface of the agar plate was inoculated by the spreading of a proportion of the microbial inoculum on the agar’s surface, using the sterile blade, and they were placed on the standard cultures related to 0.5 McFarland turbidity. Immediately after distributing the microorganisms, five wells were prepared in the 6-mm-diameter agar plate, which were punched with the sterile tip, and a proportion of (1°mL) of the alginate mix at the specified concentration was inserted into the well.[23] After 24°h of incubation at 37°C, these zones of inhibition had been calculated using a digital Vernier. The diameter of the well was recorded. Three Petri dishes were made for every addition.[24]

The three measurements of the zone of inhibition at different places were done, and then the mean of the three measurements was listed. In this study, mechanical mixing of the alginate impression material was done using an alginate mixer because it performed a uniform mix with the best physical properties.[25]

FLOW TEST

The alginate mix was laid on the glass plate, covered with another glass plate, and loaded with 14.7 Newton metal for 5°s. The diameters were measured by using a digital caliper for three investigations, and flow was defined as the average diameter.[5]

SCANNING ELECTRON MICROSCOPY (SEM)

The nanoparticle morphology was determined by using SEM images, samples prepared from the irreversible hydrocolloid were sliced into 3-mm thick disks and 7°mm in diameter, and were vacuum-dried in a desiccator containing silica gel until a constant weight was obtained. The nanoparticle powders were subjected to scanning electron microscopy. The dried specimens or the nanoparticle powders were gold-sputtered and were subjected to SEM (Czech Republic’s Fesem Tescan Mira3) at 20 kV.[26]

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

The statistical analysis was performed on SPSS version 23. Description statistics were represented by the mean and standard deviation. ANOVA and Duncan’s multiple range test were utilized to determine the differences between groups at a significance level of P°≤°0.05.

DISCUSSION

In this study, the null hypothesis was rejected; it was found that adding zinc oxide particles in the size of 45 nm into alginate showed considerable antimicrobial properties.

According to recent studies, the antibacterial activities of the nanoparticles were influenced by the size, shape, and concentrations of the synthesized ZnO-NPs, with the method used for producing them.[27,28,29,30] The microbial strain, the alginate substance, and the nanoparticle concentration all affected the observed antimicrobial activity. This suggests that ZnO-NP exhibits antibacterial action due to its capacity to damage or prevent the replication of DNA, while also disrupting the microbial cell membrane.[31]

As the concentration of ZnO-NPs in the impression material increased, so did its antimicrobial properties. This study findings are in agreement with those of Sharma and Ghose,[32] also Mousavi et al.[33] they discovered that incorporating ZnO nanoparticles into the tissue conditioner suppresses the growth of Candida albicans in a dose-dependent manner. Similar findings were found with the study by Kermani et al.,[34] who discovered that high percentages of ZnO-NPs improved their antimicrobial effect.

The pathogenic microbes are destroyed by zinc oxide nanoparticles, which act as antimicrobial agents. According to Siddiqi et al.,[35] depending on the particle’s size, form, concentration, and duration of contact with the bacterial or fungal cell, they first cause damage to the cell wall, then infiltrate the cell wall, accumulate in the cellular membrane, and then destroy the organism by interfering with its metabolism. ZnO nanoparticles demonstrated a strong antibacterial effect, which may have been caused by changes in the membrane permeability, by which the nanoparticles penetrated it, and then causing oxidative damage in the bacterial cell, which lead into death of the cell.[36]

Microbiological test findings showed that ZnO-NPs had MIC and MBC values against both Candida albicans and S. Aureus, ranging from 5% wt to 10% wt. This high concentration is related to the size of nanoparticles, which were more than 35 nm, which can indicate reduced susceptibility; this result is consistent with that of Mestres et al.[37]

Findings from this investigation disagreed with those of the earlier investigations on the antibacterial impacts of ZnO NPs that have been studied, including those by Gunalan et al.[38] and Khaza’l,[39] who discovered decreased MIC values against S. aureus for ZnO NPs with a size of 25 nm. As a result, nanoparticle activity increases with decreasing size.[38,39]

This study found that there was a significant antifungal activity of ZnO-NPs incorporated with the alginate impression material at 5 wt%,10 wt%, and 15% wt concentrations; this demonstrated that increasing the ZnO-NP concentration in the alginate material provided antifungal action against Candida albicans. The results of the current investigation found a significant antibacterial efficacy of zinc oxide nanoparticles against S. aureus.

The concentration was discovered to possess a profound effect on the antifungal activity. Therefore, the high zone of inhibition and lack of Candida albicans growth clearly demonstrate that the disruption of the membrane is a key component of the ZnO-NPs’ fungicidal effect. All of these findings support the findings by Ginjupalli et al.,[22] which demonstrated that ZnO-NPs have the ability to affect the growth of the pathogenic Candida albicans and that this effect is concentration-dependent. The concentration of nanoparticles affected the detected antibacterial activity; this can be attributed to the ability of ZnO-NP in the breakdown of the microbial cell membrane as well as causing DNA damage and preventing DNA replication.[31] It is demonstrated that zinc ions hinder the bacterial enzymatic activity, therefore demonstrating the antibacterial effect. This result is in consistent with that of the study by Ginjupalli et al.[26]

In this study, it was found that flow was decreased with all of the concentrations of ZnO NPs that were added to alginate; this result was in agreement with that of a study carried out by Ginjupalli et al.,[22] who reported that reduction in the flow is related to the gelation time and also related to the clumping of the powder, which caused changing in the powder-to-water ratio.[25] Additional research is required to determine how zinc oxide nanoparticles affect the physical characteristics of alginate.

This in vitro study has some limitations; there were no research studies on the antimicrobial effects of ZnO-NPs when added into alginate; therefore, the results obtained could not be compared to those of this study. However, the antifungal activity against Candida albicans was studied. In addition, it was recognized that results obtained in this in vitro study cannot be compared to a clinical area. Therefore, it is suggested to continue this research and evaluate the antimicrobial effects in conditions similar to the conditions of the oral environment, for improving the impression material that is used in the oral cavity.

CONCLUSION

The conclusions of this in vitro study indicated that addition of ZnO nanoparticles to alginate impression materials provided antimicrobial activity. The investigated antimicrobial activity was primarily material- and concentration-dependent. It has been found that ZnO nanoparticles are effective antifungal and antibacterial agents on both Candida albicans and S. aureus; inevitably, the flow of alginate was reduced after this addition, although ZnO-NPs could be a suitable candidate as disinfectant agents for the alginate impression material.

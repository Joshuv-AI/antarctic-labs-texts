Strange duality at level one for alternating vector bundles
Hacen ZELACI

Abstract
. In this paper, we show a strange duality isomorphism at level one for the space of generalized theta functions on the moduli spaces of alternating anti-invariant vector bundles in the ramified case. These anti-invariant vector bundles constitute one of the non-trivial examples of parahoric 𝒢 − \mathcal{G}- torsors, where 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is a twisted (not generically split) parahoric group scheme.



1.
Introduction

In their seminal paper (
[
1
]
), Beauville, Narasimhan, and Ramanan established a duality isomorphism between the space of level one generalized theta functions on the moduli space of rank
r
semistable vector bundles with trivial determinant over a smooth curve and the dual of the space of global sections of the
r
-th multiple of the Riemannian theta divisor on the Jacobian variety of the curve. More precisly, let
\mathcal{SU}_{X}(r)
be the moduli space of rank
r
vector bundles of trivial determinant over
X
, and denote by
\mathscr{D}
the determinant of cohomology line bundle on
\mathcal{SU}_{X}(r)
. Let
J_{X}
the Jacobian variety on
X
and
\Theta
be a Riemann theta divisor on
J_{X}
. Then BNR showed that there exists a canonical isomorphism

H^{0}(\mathcal{SU}_{X}(r),\mathscr{D})\cong H^{0}(J_{X},\mathcal{O}(r\Theta))^{*}.

This duality is known as the BNR duality, and it was the first case of the so-called strange duality isomorphism.

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
be a ramified double cover of smooth curves and let
r\geqslant 1
be an integer. Denote by
\sigma
the corresponding involution on
X
and by
R
the ramification divisor.
A vector bundle
E
on
X
is called anti-invariant if there exists an isomorphism

\psi:\sigma^{*}E\rightarrow E^{*}.

If
\sigma^{*}\psi=-\psi^{t}
, we say that
E
is
\sigma-
alternating.

The moduli space of anti-invariant vector bundles stands out as a noteworthy instance of the moduli space of parahoric
\mathcal{G}
-torsors, where
\mathcal{G}
represents a parahoric group scheme over
X
, and notably, it is not generically split in this case. This underscores the significance of the study of these moduli spaces. Over the past decade, parahoric torsors have become a central focus of considerable interest among researchers, since they can be viewed as a natural generalization encompassing various other concepts, including
G
-bundles, parabolic bundles, and invariant
G
-bundles.

The moduli spaces of anti-invariant vector bundles can also be seen as a generalization of Prym varieties to higher rank. Indeed, the action of
\sigma
lifts to an action on the moduli space of vector bundles, and as in the case of Jacobian, one can look at the invariant and anti-invariant parts, the later one is precisely the moduli space of anti-invariant vector bundles.

In this paper, we prove a BNR duality for the moduli space of
\sigma-
alternating vector bundles in the ramified case.

The moduli spaces of semistable pairs
(E,\psi)
of
\sigma-
alternating vector bundles of rank
2r
, which is denoted
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
, has been constructed in
[
10
]
, it has two connected components
\mathcal{U}_{X,0}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
and
\mathcal{U}_{X,1}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
. The case of trivial determinant is denoted
\mathcal{SU}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
. To each pair
(E,\psi)
with
\det E=\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\det\psi=1
, we associate a topological invariant
\tau
(see Section
2
).
We denote the associated moduli space by
\mathcal{SU}_{X,\tau}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
.

There exists a Prym variety
\rm\tilde{P}
over the normalisation
\hat{X}_{s}
of the spectral curve
\tilde{X}_{s}\rightarrow X
(more details will be given in section
2
) such that the pushforward rational map

{\rm\tilde{P}}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{X,0}^{\sigma,-}(2r)

is dominant. Moreover, the Prym variety
\rm\tilde{P}
is principally polarized. Let
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}
be a line bundle associated to a principal polarization on
\rm\tilde{P}
. We can show that the codimension of the locus where this map is not defined is at least
2
. Hence we get an injection
H^{0}(\mathcal{U}_{X,0}^{\sigma,-}(2r),\mathscr{D})\hookrightarrow H^{0}({\rm\tilde{P}},\tilde{\mathscr{L}})
, where
\mathscr{D}
is the determinant of cohomology line bundle on
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The map
(q_{*})^{*}:H^{0}(\mathcal{U}_{X,0}^{\sigma,-}(2r),\mathscr{D})\rightarrow H^{0}({\rm\tilde{P}},\tilde{\mathscr{L}})
is an isomorphism. In particular,

\dim H^{0}(\mathcal{U}_{X,0}^{\sigma,-}(2r),\mathscr{D})=1.

Consider the moduli space
\mathcal{SU}_{X,\tau}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
of trivial determianant
\sigma-
alternating vector bundles. In
[
9
]
, it has been shown that the determinant line bundle over
\mathcal{SU}_{X,\tau}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
admits a square root, called a Pfaffian of cohomology bundle
\mathscr{P}_{L}
. Moreover, the Hitchin system was studied on these moduli spaces and it was also shown that this line bundle satisfies a BNR duality in the unramified case.

Let
\rm P
be the Prym variety of
X\rightarrow Y
, and let
\mathscr{L}
be a line bundle on
\rm P
associated to a principal polarization.
Our main result is the following

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

There exists a canonical isomorphism

H^{0}(\mathcal{SU}_{X,\tau}^{\sigma,-}(2r),\mathscr{P}_{L})\cong H^{0}({\rm P},\mathscr{L}^{r})^{*}.

Note that the power of the polarization
\mathscr{L}
is the half of the rank of vector bundles which is
2r
, and this is because the determinant of cohomology has a square root however, the polarization
\mathscr{L}
doesn’t have a square root since the Prym variety
\rm P
is not principally polarized.

As corollary, we deduce the dimensions of these spaces.

Corollary 1.3
.

\dim H^{0}(\mathcal{SU}_{X,\tau}^{\sigma,-}(2r),\mathscr{P}_{L})=2^{g_{Y}}r^{g_{Y}+n-1}.

2.
\sigma-
alternating vector bundles

For the convenience of the reader, we recall here some properties of
\sigma-
anti-invariant vector bundles.

Let
X
be a complex smooth projective curve of genus
g_{X}
with an involution
\sigma:X\rightarrow X
such that
{\rm Fix}(\sigma)\not=\emptyset
. Denote by
Y=X/\sigma
and let
g_{Y}
be its genus. We assume that
g_{Y}\geqslant 2
. Let
K_{X}
and
K_{Y}
be their canonical bundles. The Hurwitz formula gives

g_{X}=2g_{Y}+n-1,

where
n
is the half of the number of ramification points in
X
.

A vector bundle
E
over
X
is called
\sigma-
anti-invariant if there exists an isomorphism
\psi:\sigma^{*}E\rightarrow E^{*}
. If
E
is stable, then this last isomorphism is either symmetric
\sigma^{*}\psi=\psi
or alternating
\sigma^{*}\psi=-\psi
. In the first case, we say that
E
is
\sigma-
symmetric, and in the other case, namely
\sigma^{*}\psi=-\psi
, then
E
is called
\sigma-
alternating. Note that, since the cover is supposed to be ramified, there is no
\sigma-
alternating bundle with odd rank and this is because
\psi
induces over a ramification point an anti-symmetric linear isomorphism

\psi_{p}:E_{p}\rightarrow E_{p}^{*},

which implies that the dimension of
E_{p}
is even. In this paper, we mainly deal with
\sigma-
alternating vector bundles.

A pair
(E,\psi)
is called semistable if for any
\sigma-
isotropic subbundle
F\subset E
, we have

\dfrac{\deg F}{{\rm rk}F}\leqslant\dfrac{\deg E}{{\rm rk}E},

where a
\sigma-
isotropic subbundle is a vector subbundle
F
such that the following composition map

\sigma^{*}F\hookrightarrow\sigma^{*}E\rightarrow E^{*}\twoheadrightarrow F^{*}

is zero. The moduli space of semistable pairs
(E,\psi)
of
\sigma-
alternating vector bundles and a
\sigma-
alternating isomorphism, which we denote
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
, has been constructed in a previous work of the author (
[
10
]
).

It was shown in
[
8
]
that the moduli space
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
of
\sigma-
alternating bundles has two connected components distinguished by the parity of
h^{0}(E\otimes\kappa)
where
\kappa
is an even theta characteristic over
X
. Note the similarity with the Stiefel-Whitney class that distinguishes the connected components of the moduli space of orthogonal bundles.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}_{X,0}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
and
\mathcal{U}_{X,1}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
these two connected components with the obvious meaning of notations.

Consider now the moduli space
\mathcal{SU}_{X,s}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
of
stable

\sigma-
alternating vector bundles with trivial determinant. This moduli space has
2^{2n-1}
connected components, half of them are contained in
\mathcal{U}_{X,0}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
and they are distinguished by a topological type defined as follows: let
(E,\psi)
be a stable
\sigma-
alternating vector bundle such that
\det E=\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\det(\psi)=1
. Then over a ramification point
p\in R
,
\psi
induces an anti-symmetric isomorphism
\psi_{p}:E_{p}\rightarrow E_{p}^{*}
with determinant equals
1
, hence its Pfaffian
\rm Pf(\psi_{p})=\pm 1
. We define the type of
(E,\psi)
to be

\tau=({\rm Pf}(\psi_{p}))_{p\in R}\mod\pm 1.

Note that this is well defined, i.e. it doesn’t depend on the choice of
\psi
as long as
E
is supposed to be stable. We denote
\mathcal{SU}_{X,\tau}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
the moduli space of semistable
\sigma-
alternating vector bundles
(E,\psi)
of type
\tau
.

Denote by
\Delta=(\det\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{-1}.
Note that
\Delta
is a degree
n
line bundle on
Y
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L
be a line bundle on
X
with norm
K_{Y}\otimes\Delta
, then
the determinant line bundle
\mathscr{D}
over
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
admits a square root, called a Pfaffian of cohomology line bundle and denoted
\mathscr{P}_{L}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
be the stack of semi-stable
\sigma-
alternating vector bundles and let
\mathscr{U}
be a universal family over
\mathscr{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)\times X
. Let
\mathscr{U}_{L}:=\mathscr{U}\otimes q_{2}^{*}L
, where
q_{2}:\mathscr{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)\times X\rightarrow X
is the second projection. Then the family
\pi_{*}\mathscr{U}_{L}
has a quadratic form with values in
K_{Y}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\mathscr{U}_{L}

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{*}(\sigma^{*}\mathscr{U}_{L})

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{*}(\mathscr{U}_{L}^{*}\otimes q_{2}^{-1}(R))\otimes q_{2}^{*}K_{Y}

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{*}(\mathscr{U}_{L})^{*}\otimes q_{2}^{*}K_{Y},

where the last isomorphism is giving by the relative duality (see
[
3
, Ex III.6.10]
). This isomorphism induces a symmetric bilinear form on the family with values in
K_{Y}
(see
[
9
]
). Using
[
7
, Proposition
7.9
]
, we obtain a square root of the determinant of cohomology
\mathscr{D}_{\pi_{*}\mathscr{U}_{L}}
associated
\pi_{*}{\mathscr{U}_{L}}
. But since
\mathscr{D}_{\pi_{*}\mathscr{U}_{L}}=\mathscr{D}_{\mathscr{U}_{L}}
, we deduce that the determinant line bundle has square root at the level of stacks.

To show that this square root descends to the moduli space, we need to use the Kempf’s lemma at the level of Quot scheme. This can be done as in
[
9
]
.
∎

The Pfaffian line bundle admits a nonzero global section, which insures the existence of a Pfaffian divisor. For
L\in\text{Nm}_{X/Y}^{-1}(K_{Y}\otimes\Delta)
, denote by
\Theta_{L}
the divisor in
\mathcal{U}_{X}(2r,0)
supported on vector bundles
E
such that
E\otimes L
has a non-zero global section.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
L
as above, the restriction of the divisor
\Theta_{L}\subset\mathcal{U}_{X}(2r,0)
to
\mathcal{U}_{X,0}^{\sigma,-}(r)
is again a divisor.
Moreover there exists an effective divisor
\Xi_{L}
in
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
such that
\mathcal{O}(\Xi_{L})\cong\mathscr{P}_{L}
and

2\Xi_{L}\equiv\Theta_{L}.

Proof.

Note first that
L\otimes\sigma^{*}L\cong\pi^{*}(K_{Y}\otimes\Delta)\cong K_{X}
. Let
M\in{\rm Pic}^{0}(X)
such that
h^{0}(L\otimes M)=0
. We have

\displaystyle h^{0}(L\otimes\sigma^{*}M^{-1})

\displaystyle=h^{1}(L\otimes\sigma^{*}M^{-1})

\displaystyle=h^{0}(L^{-1}\otimes\sigma^{*}M\otimes K_{X})

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\sigma^{*}L\otimes\sigma^{*}M)

\displaystyle=h^{0}(L\otimes M)

\displaystyle=0

Hence
h^{0}(L\otimes(M\oplus\sigma^{*}M^{-1}))=0
. Let
E=(M\oplus\sigma^{*}M^{-1})^{\oplus r}
. It is clearly a semistable anti-invariant vector bundle of rank
2r
with a
\sigma-
alternating isomorphism
\sigma^{*}E\rightarrow E^{*}

\psi=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}^{\oplus r}.

Now we have
h^{0}(E\otimes L)=0
, so the restriction of the divisor
\Theta_{L}
to
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
is again a divisor.

Moreover, by
[
5
, §7.10]
, since the restriction of
\Theta_{L}
to the moduli space
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
is a divisor, there exists an effective divisor
{\Xi}_{L}
such that
2{\Xi}_{L}=\Theta_{L}|_{\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)}
. In particular,
\mathscr{P}_{L}
has a non-zero global section.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that the restriction
\Xi_{L}
to
\mathcal{SU}_{X,\tau}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
is again a divisor, for any type
\tau
. Indeed, if we set
\lambda=M\otimes\sigma^{*}M^{-1}
, then
E_{\lambda}=E\otimes\lambda^{-1}
has trivial determinant (and it can have any type since it is strictly polystable) and since
{\rm Nm}_{X/Y}(\lambda)=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
, we have
L\otimes\lambda\in{\rm Nm}_{X/Y}^{-1}(K_{Y}\otimes\Delta)
, and clearly
h^{0}(L\otimes\lambda\otimes E_{\lambda})=0
, so
E_{\lambda}\not\in\Xi_{L\otimes\lambda}
.

3.
Hitchin Systems on
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)

The Hitchin system is a Hamiltonian integrable system over the moduli space of vector bundles, introduced by N. Hitchin (
[
4
]
). It is a powerful tool for studying this moduli space, as well as the spaces of generalized theta functions. Let
\mathcal{U}_{X}(r,0)
be the moduli space of semistable vector bundles on
X
of rank
r
and degree
0
. The cotangent space to
\mathcal{U}_{X}(r,0)
at
E
can be identified with
H^{0}(X,{\rm End}(E)\otimes K_{X})
. By considering a basis of invariant polynomials under the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, we get a map

T^{*}_{E}\mathcal{U}_{X}(r,0)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}(X,K^{i}_{X})=:W.

This is a Lagrangian fibration which is known to be an algebraically completely integrable system, by the mean, its generic fiber is an open set of an abelian variety, which is, in this case, the Jacobian of the base curve.

On the other hand, the cotangent space to
\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)
at
E
can be identified with the eigenspace
H^{0}(X,{\rm End}(E)\otimes K_{X})_{-}
associated to the
-1

\psi
and the linearization on
K_{X}
(see
[
8
]
). We can show that the above map induces a map

T^{*}_{E}\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\sigma,-}(2r)\rightarrow W^{\sigma,-}\subset\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}(X,K^{i}_{X})_{+},

where
H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{i})_{+}
is the invariant subspace and
W^{\sigma,-}
is an affine subset defined by the condition that the characteristic polynomial is perfect square over each ramification point.

Early Fibrotic Remodeling Remains Responsive to Autologous Fat Grafting: Histological Findings in a Rat Model
Authors: RAZVAN GEORGE BOGDAN, ALEXANDRU BLIDISEL, ANCA MARIA CIMPEAN, IONUT CIOBOTA, ALINA HELGIU, MARA NICOLAU, IOAN CATALIN BODEA, RODICA ELENA HEREDEA, ZORIN PETRISOR CRAINICEANU
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/AimFibrosis-associated soft tissue remodeling involves progressive alterations in extracellular matrix composition, vascular architecture and tissue biomechanics. Early fibrotic lesions may remain biologically responsive before irreversible sclerosis develops. This study evaluated the histological evolution of bleomycin-induced soft tissue remodeling and the effects of autologous fat grafting in a rat model.Materials and MethodsTwenty female Wistar rats received subcutaneous bleomycin (1 mg/kg/day for three consecutive days) to induce localized soft tissue remodeling. Autologous fat grafting was performed 17 days later. Clinical assessment was conducted at baseline, Day 17 and Day 31 using a standardized semi-quantitative score evaluating erythema, edema, skin elasticity, cutaneous mobility and ulceration or necrosis. Histological analysis was performed at predefined timepoints.ResultsBleomycin induced reproducible early fibrotic remodeling characterized by collagen deposition, stromal hypercellularity, vascular remodeling and architectural distortion. The mean composite clinical score increased from 0 at baseline to 9.10±1.75 on Day 17 and decreased to 7.75±1.35 after fat grafting on Day 31 (p=0.00024). Histological examination demonstrated persistent fibrotic septa, fibroblast proliferation, vascular reorganization and partial integration of grafted adipose tissue, indicating ongoing tissue remodeling.ConclusionBleomycin-induced remodeling produced reproducible early fibrotic changes in dermal and subcutaneous tissues. Autologous fat grafting was associated with significant clinical improvement and histological evidence of continued tissue reorganization. This model supports investigation of regenerative therapies targeting early fibrosis-associated soft tissue remodeling.

Introduction

Radiation-associated soft tissue fibrosis remains a major challenge in reconstructive surgery because it alters both the structure and mechanical behavior of the dermal–subcutaneous compartment. Although radiotherapy plays an essential role in oncologic disease control, it may induce progressive tissue remodeling characterized by extracellular matrix accumulation, impaired vascular homeostasis, reduced elasticity, and decreased tissue mobility (1-4). In clinical practice, these changes compromise wound healing, increase surgical difficulty, and may negatively influence the outcomes of secondary reconstructive procedures (1, 4). Understanding how fibrotic soft tissue evolves before reaching an advanced sclerotic stage is therefore important for developing and evaluating regenerative strategies aimed at restoring tissue quality rather than simply replacing lost volume.

Fibrotic remodeling does not occur as a static or irreversible process from its onset but rather progresses through biologically active transitional stages characterized by dynamic interactions between fibroblasts, inflammatory mediators, extracellular matrix deposition, and vascular remodeling (2-4). During these early phases, tissue architecture remains partially preserved despite progressive stromal disorganization and mechanical alteration. Experimental and clinical observations suggest that this intermediate stage may retain a degree of structural plasticity before progression toward dense sclerosis and irreversible tissue contraction (2, 5). Identifying and characterizing these early remodeling dynamics is particularly relevant in reconstructive surgery, where therapeutic interventions may be more effective before advanced fibrotic stabilization occurs.

Autologous fat grafting has progressively gained recognition as a regenerative strategy capable of improving tissue quality in irradiated fields. Beyond its volumetric role, adipose tissue functions as a biologically active graft enriched with adipose-derived stromal cells and paracrine mediators that influence angiogenesis, inflammatory modulation, and extracellular matrix remodeling (6). Experimental studies have demonstrated reduced fibrosis and enhanced vascularization following fat grafting in irradiated tissues (5, 6), while clinical investigations report statistically significant improvements in pain, stiffness, and functional tissue compliance in patients treated for post-radiotherapy chest wall sequelae (7, 8). These findings support the concept that lipofilling may exert therapeutic effects on fibrotic substrates rather than acting solely as a structural filler. Nevertheless, the extent to which these improvements reflect true antifibrotic biological modulation versus predominantly mechanical or volumetric effects remains a matter of ongoing discussion in the field (6-8).

Despite increasing interest in regenerative interventions for fibrosis-associated soft tissue alteration, comprehensive evaluation of early remodeling dynamics remains challenging. Clinical examination alone may incompletely capture the structural heterogeneity and temporal evolution of fibrotic tissue, particularly during intermediate stages characterized by simultaneous inflammatory, vascular, and stromal changes (3, 4). Histological analysis remains essential for characterizing extracellular matrix organization, fibroblast activity, and tissue architecture; however, it provides only static endpoint evaluation of a continuously evolving biological process. In contrast, ultrasonography has emerged as a promising non-invasive tool for longitudinal assessment of dermal–subcutaneous remodeling because it enables real-time evaluation of tissue thickness, echogenicity, structural heterogeneity, and architectural reorganization (9). Using the same experimental framework, our group previously demonstrated that serial ultrasonographic assessment could identify progressive structural alterations associated with early bleomycin-induced remodeling, as well as dynamic changes following autologous fat grafting (10). These findings highlighted the potential value of imaging-based longitudinal monitoring in regenerative research, while simultaneously emphasizing the need for histological correlation of the observed structural patterns and remodeling dynamics.

Within this context, the present study explored the clinical and histological evolution of early bleomycin-induced dermal–subcutaneous remodeling following autologous fat grafting in a controlled experimental model. Building upon our previously reported ultrasonographic findings obtained from the same longitudinal experimental framework (10), the current analysis aimed to further characterize the histological substrate underlying the observed structural and clinical remodeling dynamics. Particular attention was directed toward early-stage tissue alteration before progression toward advanced fibrosis, with emphasis on the relationship between stromal reorganization, vascular remodeling, and adipose tissue integration. Through combined clinical and histological evaluation, this study sought to contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of early fibrosis-associated soft tissue remodeling and its potential responsiveness to regenerative intervention.

The clinical findings derived from this experimental model have been reported previously as part of a dedicated longitudinal clinical and ultrasonographic analysis. In the present manuscript, selected clinical data are included only to provide clinicopathological correlation and contextual interpretation of the histological remodeling patterns. The primary focus of the current study is the histological characterization of tissue remodeling following autologous fat grafting.

The experimental protocol used in the present study has been described previously in a dedicated Methods and Protocols publication detailing the study design and methodology (11). In addition, our group has recently reported the longitudinal clinical and ultrasonographic findings obtained from the same experimental cohort (10). The present manuscript represents a predefined secondary analysis of the same experimental model and focuses exclusively on the histological characterization of tissue remodeling and graft integration. No histological findings presented here have been reported previously.

Materials and Methods

Study design. The experimental model, animal protocol, and study design have been described previously in a dedicated methodological publication. The present study uses the same experimental cohort but reports an independent histological analysis addressing a different scientific objective. For completeness, only methodological details essential for interpretation of the histological findings are summarized below (10, 11). This study was designed as a controlled, longitudinal experimental model aimed at standardizing the induction of early dermal–hypodermal remodeling using bleomycin followed by autologous subcutaneous adipose tissue grafting in rats.

A total of 22 adult female Wistar rats were allocated into three experimental groups. The treatment group initially included 15 animals subjected to bleomycin-induced remodeling followed by autologous fat grafting. The fibrosis control group consisted of five animals that underwent bleomycin-induced remodeling without grafting. The healthy reference group comprised two animals euthanized at baseline according to the predefined experimental protocol for histological comparison.

This group was included exclusively for baseline architectural comparison and was not subjected to longitudinal assessment or statistical comparison. The fibrosis control group served primarily to confirm the stability of the bleomycin-induced remodeling model and was not intended for longitudinal comparative analysis in the present manuscript.

Two animals from the treatment group reached predefined humane endpoints because of wound dehiscence and graft extrusion and were excluded from the longitudinal analysis, resulting in a final treatment group of 13 animals. The experimental unit was the individual animal, and the protocol followed a longitudinal within-subject design with repeated clinical assessments performed at predefined timepoints within the same anatomically standardized anterior thoracic region. Animals were allocated sequentially to predefined experimental groups according to the approved experimental protocol. Formal randomization was not performed because group sizes and experimental roles had been predetermined during regulatory approval of the animal study. The total study duration was 31 days. The experimental protocol, including group allocation and sample size, was reviewed and approved by the National Sanitary Veterinary Authority in compliance with animal welfare regulations.

The sample size was determined a priori according to feasibility considerations, ethical requirements for animal reduction (3Rs principle), and the approved experimental protocol. Because the primary objective of the study was longitudinal within-subject evaluation rather than formal between-group hypothesis testing, no formal sample size calculation based on the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was performed.

Because the present manuscript focused on longitudinal clinical evolution within the treatment group, paired comparisons between time points were analyzed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. In addition to longitudinal clinical evaluation, histological analysis was performed at predefined timepoints to allow structural correlation of macroscopic findings.

The fibrosis control group was included to confirm the persistence of bleomycin-induced tissue remodeling throughout the experimental period and to provide histological reference specimens at the final study endpoint. The study was not designed or powered to perform formal efficacy comparisons between treated and untreated animals. Instead, the primary objective was longitudinal histological characterization of tissue remodeling within the grafted cohort.

Animals and housing conditions. Twenty-two female Wistar rats, 16 weeks of age and weighing 250-300 g at inclusion, were housed in a specific pathogen-free facility at the Experimental Surgery and Training Center “Pius Brînzeu”, Timișoara, Romania. Animals underwent a 7-day acclimatization period prior to experimental procedures. They were housed in groups of 3-5 per cage under controlled environmental conditions, with ambient temperature maintained at 22 °C and a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle. Standard laboratory chow and water were provided ad libitum. Environmental enrichment included nesting material and structural cage elements. All procedures complied with Directive 2010/63/EU and national legislation governing the protection of animals used for scientific purposes.

The study protocol, including the analgesia strategy and humane endpoint criteria, was approved by the Ethics Committee of the “Victor Babeș” University of Medicine and Pharmacy Timișoara (Approval No. 42/24.07.2025) and received favorable authorization from the National Sanitary Veterinary Authority. All procedures were conducted in accordance with Directive 2010/63/EU and complied with the ARRIVE 2.0 reporting guidelines for animal research. The experimental protocol, including group allocation, sample size determination, monitoring procedures, and predefined humane endpoints, was reviewed and approved during the regulatory authorization process. All experimental procedures were performed by trained personnel at the Experimental Surgery and Training Center “Pius Brînzeu”, and animals were monitored daily throughout the study period to ensure compliance with welfare standards.

Experimental timeline. The experimental protocol comprised four sequential stages over a 31-day period. Baseline clinical assessment was performed on Day 0, followed by subcutaneous bleomycin administration on Days 1 to 3. Clinical reassessment and autologous fat grafting were performed on Day 17, and the study concluded on Day 31 with final clinical evaluation and euthanasia.

Baseline clinical assessment. On Day 0, each animal was individually marked in the proximal dorsal region of the tail using a permanent identification method to ensure accurate longitudinal tracking (Figure 1). The anterior left thoracic region was shaved using an electric clipper (Figure 1). Animals were positioned in dorsal recumbency under gentle manual restraint without anesthesia, and standardized clinical photographs were obtained using a fixed camera distance and angle (Figure 1).

Induction of dermal–hypodermal remodeling. Bleomycin sulfate (15,000 International Units(IU)/vial; Accord Healthcare Polska Sp. z o.o., Warsaw, Poland) was reconstituted with sterile 0.9% sodium chloride solution (B. Braun Melsungen AG, Melsungen, Germany) to obtain a final concentration of 1 mg/ml (Figure 1D-G). Animals received subcutaneous bleomycin at a dose of 1 mg/kg/day for three consecutive days at the predefined anterior thoracic site, with the injected volume adjusted according to body weight. The solution was administered using sterile 1 ml syringes fitted with 26 Gauge (G) needles (Changzhou Shuangma Medical Devices Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, PR China). The cumulative dose was 3 mg/kg per animal, and injections were confined to the subcutaneous plane to avoid intramuscular placement. This submaximal regimen was designed to induce controlled early dermal–hypodermal remodeling rather than advanced sclerotic fibrosis.

Intermediate reassessment and autologous adipose tissue grafting. Fourteen days after the final bleomycin administration, animals underwent clinical reassessment and surgical intervention under general anesthesia induced with intraperitoneal ketamine (100 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg). Before autologous adipose tissue grafting on Day 17, standardized clinical photographs were obtained using the same imaging protocol applied at baseline (Figure 2). The thoracic and inguinal regions were re-shaved and disinfected with 10% povidone–iodine solution (Betadine®, Egis Pharmaceuticals PLC, Budapest, Hungary). An inguinal incision was performed, and subcutaneous adipose tissue was harvested by blunt dissection, with small vascular branches coagulated using bipolar electrocautery. The harvested adipose tissue was rinsed with sterile saline, mechanically fragmented without enzymatic processing, and transferred into a sterile 1 ml syringe. A minimal recipient pocket was created through a small thoracic incision at the predefined anatomical site, and 0.4 to 0.6 ml of fragmented adipose tissue was injected as approximately 4 to 6 micro-deposits within a single subcutaneous plane while avoiding excessive mechanical tension. Donor and recipient sites were closed with 4-0 polypropylene monofilament sutures (Optilene®, B. Braun Melsungen AG), and body temperature was maintained intraoperatively using a heating pad. Quantitative graft retention was not assessed because the study focused on clinical and structural remodeling of the recipient site.

Preoperative clinical reassessment before autologous fat grafting (Day 17).

Postoperative monitoring and humane endpoints. Animals were monitored daily for wound integrity, mobility, grooming behavior, and food intake. Humane endpoints included body weight loss exceeding 20% of baseline, persistent refusal of food or water for more than 48 hours, severe signs of pain or distress, extensive necrosis or infection, and irreversible clinical deterioration. Animals meeting any of these criteria were euthanized immediately under deep anesthesia. Systemic analgesics were not routinely administered to avoid potential interference with the inflammatory and fibrotic pathways central to the experimental model. This approach was reviewed and formally approved by both the Institutional Ethics Committee and the National Sanitary Veterinary Authority as part of the regulatory authorization process.

Final assessment and euthanasia. On Day 31, standardized clinical photographs and the final clinical evaluation were performed using the same parameters applied throughout the study (Figure 3). Animals were subsequently euthanized by cervical dislocation under deep anesthesia by trained personnel in accordance with institutional and legal guidelines.

Final clinical appearance at Day 31.

Clinical evaluation and scoring system. Clinical assessment was performed longitudinally at predefined timepoints (baseline, Day 17, and Day 31) within the same anatomically standardized anterior thoracic region. Animals were examined under gentle manual restraint without anesthesia, and the anterior thoracic field was shaved before each evaluation to ensure unobstructed visual inspection. A standardized semi-quantitative scoring system (0 to 5) was used to assess five predefined clinical parameters: erythema, edema, skin elasticity, cutaneous mobility, and ulceration or necrosis, with higher scores indicating greater tissue alteration. All evaluations were performed independently by the same two investigators (R.G.B and Z.P.C) throughout the study period. The evaluators conducted their assessments separately and were not present simultaneously during scoring to minimize observer influence. Blinding to treatment allocation was not implemented because of the longitudinal within-subject study design. For each parameter, the final score for each animal was calculated as the arithmetic mean of the two independent evaluations. In cases where the difference between evaluators exceeded one point, the predefined protocol specified retention of the calculated mean value.

Erythema. Erythema was assessed by direct visual inspection under standardized ambient lighting conditions using a semi-quantitative scale ranging from 0 to 5, where 0 indicated normal skin color, 1 minimal focal erythema, 2 mild but clearly visible erythema confined to the injection site, 3 moderate erythema extending beyond the initial injection field, 4 marked erythema with diffuse involvement of the anterior thoracic region, and 5 severe erythema associated with inflammatory changes such as surface desquamation. Standardized clinical photographs were obtained at each evaluation to ensure longitudinal comparability.

Edema. Edema was assessed by visual inspection and gentle palpation using a semi-quantitative scale ranging from 0 to 5, where 0 indicated no visible or palpable swelling, 1 minimal localized swelling detectable only on palpation, 2 mild visible swelling confined to the injection area, 3 moderate swelling with clear contour elevation, 4 marked swelling associated with distortion of the local thoracic contour, and 5 severe swelling accompanied by tense skin and marked expansion of the treated area.

Skin elasticity. Skin elasticity was assessed using a standardized manual skin pinch test performed at the predefined thoracic site. The skin was gently lifted between the thumb and index finger and released immediately. Elasticity was graded on a semi-quantitative scale from 0 to 5, where 0 indicated normal elasticity with immediate return to baseline position, 1 a slight delay in recoil, 2 a mild reduction in recoil speed, 3 moderate stiffness with visibly delayed return, 4 marked stiffness with minimal recoil, and 5 severe rigidity with almost absent recoil. The assessment was performed three consecutive times, and the average score was recorded.

Cutaneous mobility. Cutaneous mobility relative to the underlying fascia was evaluated by gently displacing the skin horizontally over the thoracic wall. Mobility was graded using a semi-quantitative scale from 0 to 5, where 0 indicated normal mobility comparable to healthy reference tissue, 1 slightly reduced mobility, 2 mild restriction in lateral displacement, 3 moderate restriction with clearly limited displacement, 4 marked fixation to the underlying structures, and 5 complete fixation with no measurable displacement. All evaluations were performed using consistent manual pressure to minimize variability.

Ulceration or necrosis. The presence and severity of ulceration or necrosis were assessed by visual inspection using a semi-quantitative scale from 0 to 5, where 0 indicated intact skin, 1 superficial epidermal disruption without tissue loss, 2 a small superficial ulcer measuring less than 3 mm, 3 ulceration measuring 3 to 5 mm without deep tissue involvement, 4 deep ulceration extending into the subcutaneous tissue, and 5 extensive necrosis or full-thickness tissue loss. Whenever ulceration or necrosis was present, lesion diameter was measured using a digital caliper and documented with standardized clinical photographs.

Composite clinical score. A composite clinical score was calculated for each animal as the sum of the five evaluated parameters, yielding a maximum possible score of 25, with higher scores indicating more severe dermal–hypodermal remodeling. Composite scores were derived after averaging the two independent evaluator scores for each parameter. Longitudinal within-subject comparisons were performed to assess the evolution of clinical changes over time. The scoring system was specifically developed for this experimental protocol and has not undergone formal external validation.

Histopathological processing and analysis. Histological sampling was performed at predefined timepoints using different sampling procedures. At Day 0, full-thickness tissue specimens were collected from the healthy reference animals following euthanasia (Figure 4). At Day 17, three Tru-Cut biopsy specimens were obtained from each treatment animal before autologous fat grafting to characterize bleomycin-induced remodeling while preserving the animals for longitudinal follow-up. At Day 31, the remaining animals from the treatment and fibrosis control groups were euthanized, and full-thickness tissue specimens were collected from the predefined anterior thoracic region for final histological evaluation. Specimens were immediately fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for 24 to 48 hours, routinely processed, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at a thickness of 4 to 5 μm using a rotary microtome. Two hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained sections were examined from each paraffin block. Histopathological assessment was performed by complete examination of the entire tissue section rather than by evaluation of a predefined number of microscopic fields, as the study was designed as a qualitative descriptive histological analysis rather than a quantitative morphometric study. Digital RGB-based image analysis was additionally performed to improve visualization and differentiation of adipose and fibrotic tissue components and to highlight their spatial organization during remodeling (Figure 5D). Histological assessment focused on dermal architecture, collagen deposition and organization, adipocyte morphology, vascular density and morphology, inflammatory cell infiltration, and graft integration. Particular attention was given to adipocyte degeneration, cystic changes, fibrotic septa formation, and tissue remodeling heterogeneity. All histological evaluations were performed by an experienced pathologist (A.M.C) blinded to the experimental groups.

Data availability. This study does not involve large-scale datasets deposited in publicly available repositories. Raw data supporting the findings are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Statistical analysis. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Continuous variables are presented as mean±standard deviation. Because the clinical scores represent ordinal variables obtained from repeated measurements within the same animals, longitudinal comparisons between Day 17 and Day 31 were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Two-tailed p-values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. The distribution of continuous variables was assessed visually and considered non-normal because of the small sample size. Consequently, non-parametric statistical methods were used throughout the study.

Results

Animal flow and study completion. Twenty-two animals were enrolled in the study. Fifteen were assigned to the treatment group, five to the fibrosis control group, and two to the healthy reference group. Two animals from the treatment group reached predefined humane endpoints due to postoperative wound dehiscence associated with partial graft extrusion and were excluded from longitudinal clinical analysis. These events occurred within the early postoperative period following adipose tissue grafting and were likely related to local wound instability rather than to the bleomycin-induced remodeling process itself. Both animals were euthanized according to predefined humane endpoint criteria. Because longitudinal clinical evaluation required intact wound closure and stable graft placement, these animals were not included in the final longitudinal analysis. The final number analyzed in the treatment group was 13. Two animals developed postoperative wound dehiscence associated with graft extrusion and reached predefined humane endpoints before completion of the longitudinal follow-up. These cases were considered treatment-related adverse outcomes and were excluded from the longitudinal clinical and histological analyses because complete follow-up data could not be obtained. Accordingly, the final longitudinal analysis included 13 animals.

All fibrosis control animals completed the study. Histological examination of the fibrosis control group at Day 31 demonstrated persistence of the bleomycin-induced remodeling pattern, characterized by fibrotic septa, stromal hypercellularity, collagen deposition, and vascular remodeling in the absence of adipose tissue grafting. These specimens served as histological reference material confirming the stability of the experimental model and were not included in formal longitudinal statistical comparisons. Healthy reference animals were euthanized at baseline as predefined. These animals were not included in the intention-to-treat analysis because the primary objective of the study was longitudinal clinical evaluation of stable grafted sites.

Statistical comparison of the composite clinical score between Day 17 and Day 31 showed a significant reduction following autologous adipose tissue grafting (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test, W=0, p=0.00024), with the mean score decreasing from 9.10±1.75 at Day 17 to 7.75±1.35 at Day 31.

Baseline clinical status. At baseline (Day 0), all animals exhibited a normal clinical appearance within the predefined anterior thoracic region. Erythema, edema, ulceration, and necrosis were absent in all animals (score 0), while skin elasticity and cutaneous mobility were fully preserved (score 0). Consequently, the composite clinical score was 0 in all experimental groups. Histological evaluation confirmed normal mammary architecture, with preserved terminal ductal lobular unit (TDLU) organization and well-defined perilobular adipose tissue (Figure 3).

At Day 17, following completion of bleomycin administration, animals exhibited clinical findings consistent with early dermal–hypodermal remodeling. Visible erythema confined to the injection field was observed in most animals, with a mean score of 2.45 (range=0-4), while localized edema had a mean score of 1.45 (range=0-3). Manual skin pinch testing demonstrated reduced elasticity, with a mean score of 2.25 (range=1-4), and cutaneous mobility was also reduced compared with baseline, with a mean score of 2.95 (range=1-4). No ulceration or necrosis was observed in any animal. The mean composite clinical score increased from 0 at baseline to 9.10±1.75, reflecting the overall severity of dermal–hypodermal remodeling. These findings were supported by histological evidence of progressive fibrotic remodeling of the adipose tissue architecture (Figure 5). At Day 31, following autologous fat grafting, clinical evaluation demonstrated persistent but less pronounced dermal–hypodermal alterations. Mean erythema, edema, skin elasticity, and cutaneous mobility scores decreased to 1.85 (range=0-3), 1.25 (range=0-2), 2.00 (range=1-4), and 2.65 (range=1-4), respectively, whereas ulceration or necrosis remained absent in all animals. Histopathological findings. Histological evaluation demonstrated distinct structural patterns corresponding to the different experimental timepoints. At baseline (Day 0), tissue architecture was preserved, with normal dermal organization and well-defined subcutaneous adipose tissue. Adipocytes were uniform in size, with intact cellular membranes and clear cytoplasmic vacuoles. The stromal component was minimal, and vascular structures were regularly distributed without signs of congestion or dilation. No inflammatory infiltrate or fibrotic changes were identified.

At Day 17 (Bleomycin-Induced Remodeling), histological analysis revealed marked structural alterations consistent with early fibrotic remodeling. There was a significant increase in collagen deposition within the dermal–subcutaneous compartment, with thickened fibrous septa extending between adipocytes. Adipocyte morphology was altered, with variability in size and focal contour irregularities. Representative features included thickened connective septa, adipocyte distortion, vascular dilation, and progressive fibrotic replacement of adipose tissue (Figure 5A-C). The stromal compartment demonstrated increased cellularity, predominantly consisting of fibroblast-like cells. Vascular structures appeared dilated, with evidence of congestion and regional stasis. Inflammatory infiltrate was present in a patchy distribution, indicating an active remodeling process. Importantly, the overall architecture remained partially preserved, supporting the presence of a dynamic, non-terminal fibrotic stage rather than advanced sclerosis.

At Day 31 (Post-Lipofilling Changes), histological examination demonstrated a heterogeneous pattern of tissue remodeling. Areas of viable adipose tissue coexisted with regions of adipocyte degeneration and structural disorganization. Viable adipose tissue coexisted with fibrotic septa and areas of adipocyte degeneration (Figure 6A-C). Adipocytes in affected regions showed loss of membrane integrity, cytoplasmic fragmentation, and variable enlargement, suggestive of partial liponecrosis. Fibrotic septa remained present but exhibited variable organization, with areas of both dense collagen deposition and more loosely arranged matrix. RGB-based analysis further emphasized the distribution and organization of fibrotic and adipose components (Figure 6D). Vascular changes persisted, with dilated capillaries and uneven distribution across the tissue. Inflammatory infiltrate was still present, although its intensity varied between regions. Notably, zones of tissue reorganization were observed at the interface between grafted material and host tissue, characterized by fibroblast proliferation and early granulation tissue formation, suggesting an active integration process. Areas of increased fibroblast density and active collagen synthesis were also identified (Figure 6E). Overall, histological findings indicate a dynamic remodeling environment characterized by partial graft survival, ongoing fibrosis, and structural heterogeneity.

Clinical–histological correlation. The histological findings were consistent with the clinical evolution observed throughout the study. At Day 17, increased collagen deposition, stromal cellularity, and vascular alterations corresponded to the clinically observed reduction in skin elasticity and cutaneous mobility, reflecting increased tissue stiffness and structural constraint. The presence of vascular congestion and inflammatory infiltrate correlated with the observed erythema and edema, supporting the role of active inflammatory remodeling in this stage. Areas of advanced fibrosis with marked hypercellularity and minimal residual adipose tissue were also identified (Figure 5E).

At Day 31, partial clinical improvement following autologous fat grafting was associated with a heterogeneous histological pattern. Areas of preserved adipose tissue and early structural reorganization corresponded to improvements in skin elasticity and mobility, while persistent fibrotic septa and adipocyte degeneration explained the incomplete normalization of clinical parameters. The coexistence of viable graft regions, liponecrosis, and fibrotic remodeling suggests that autologous fat grafting induces a dynamic tissue response rather than complete reversal of fibrosis. These findings support the concept that clinical improvement reflects underlying structural remodeling rather than purely volumetric effects.

Discussion

The present study demonstrates that early dermal–subcutaneous remodeling induced through a controlled submaximal bleomycin protocol can serve as a reproducible experimental substrate for evaluating regenerative interventions. Within this model, autologous fat grafting was associated with measurable clinical improvement across predefined macroscopic parameters, reflected by dynamic changes in the composite clinical score over time. These findings support the working hypothesis that early-stage fibrotic remodeling represents a biologically responsive environment in which regenerative strategies may exert clinically appreciable effects. Importantly, the structured longitudinal scoring system allowed objective quantification of tissue evolution rather than relying solely on descriptive observation. This approach increases the objectivity and reproducibility of experimental evaluation in early fibrotic remodeling.

The fibrosis control group was incorporated to verify maintenance of the bleomycin-induced remodeling phenotype in the absence of regenerative intervention rather than to provide a parallel efficacy comparison. Consequently, the present study should be interpreted as a longitudinal histological characterization of tissue remodeling after autologous fat grafting rather than a controlled therapeutic efficacy trial. Future studies specifically powered for between-group comparisons will be required to quantify the magnitude of treatment effect.

Bleomycin-induced fibrosis has been widely used in experimental models to reproduce progressive dermal sclerosis and extracellular matrix accumulation, most frequently through prolonged administration protocols designed to generate advanced fibrotic architecture (10-12). Such models commonly prioritize maximal collagen deposition and structural distortion, often resulting in dense fibrosis associated with marked tissue rigidity and persistent inflammatory activity. While these approaches remain valuable for investigating profibrotic signaling pathways and end-stage fibrosis, they may incompletely reflect the earlier remodeling stages encountered in reconstructive clinical practice. In contrast, the submaximal protocol employed in the present study was intentionally designed to induce controlled dermal–subcutaneous alteration while preserving tissue viability and structural responsiveness. The resulting phenotype, characterized by moderate stiffness, restricted mobility, preserved surface integrity, and active stromal remodeling, more closely resembles the intermediate tissue environment in which regenerative interventions such as autologous fat grafting are typically performed. This conceptual positioning enhances the translational relevance of the model by prioritizing biological responsiveness rather than maximal fibrotic destruction.

In contrast, the submaximal protocol adopted in the present study was intentionally designed to induce controlled dermal–hypodermal alteration without progression toward destructive sclerosis. The resulting clinical profile – moderate erythema, increased stiffness, restricted mobility, and preserved surface integrity – suggests a remodeling stage characterized by active matrix reorganization rather than irreversible fibrotic collapse. This distinction extends beyond fibrosis severity alone and reflects a fundamentally different experimental objective: whereas severe models prioritize maximal collagen accumulation, the current approach prioritizes biological responsiveness and structural viability. Such positioning enhances the translational relevance of the model by aligning experimental conditions with the clinical window in which regenerative interventions, including fat grafting, are most frequently applied.

The clinical profile documented at Day 17 reflected a reproducible pattern of early dermal–subcutaneous alteration across the experimental cohort. Increased tissue stiffness and reduced cutaneous mobility represented the most prominent functional changes, while preservation of surface integrity and the absence of ulceration or necrosis suggested maintenance of overall tissue viability despite ongoing remodeling. Rather than indicating fully established fibrosis, this combination of macroscopic findings supports the presence of an intermediate remodeling stage characterized by active extracellular matrix reorganization and evolving mechanical alteration (1, 3, 5). From a reconstructive perspective, this transitional tissue environment may be particularly relevant because it more closely resembles the biologically responsive stages at which regenerative interventions are typically considered in clinical practice (7).

The longitudinal clinical evolution observed following autologous fat grafting suggests modulation of the previously established remodeling profile rather than progression toward advanced fibrosis. This interpretation is supported by the significant reduction in composite clinical score between Day 17 and Day 31, accompanied predominantly by improved skin elasticity and cutaneous mobility while preserving overall tissue integrity. Importantly, the absence of ulceration or necrosis throughout follow-up further supports maintenance of structural viability within the remodeled dermal–subcutaneous compartment. These functional changes were paralleled by histological findings demonstrating persistent but heterogeneous remodeling characterized by collagen reorganization, fibroblast proliferation, vascular alterations, and partial adipose tissue integration. Collectively, these observations suggest that autologous fat grafting may influence the tissue microenvironment through combined structural and stromal remodeling mechanisms rather than through complete reversal of fibrosis (2, 7).

The clinical evolution observed in the present study should be interpreted in the context of our previously published longitudinal clinical and ultrasonographic analysis derived from the same experimental framework. While selected clinical findings are reproduced here to support clinicopathological correlation, the primary objective of the current manuscript is the histological characterization of tissue remodeling and adipose tissue integration following autologous fat grafting.

These clinical observations were supported by histological findings obtained at the corresponding timepoints. At Day 17, increased collagen deposition, fibroblast proliferation, vascular dilation, and patchy inflammatory infiltrate confirmed the presence of an active early remodeling process rather than established fibrosis. At Day 31, histological analysis demonstrated a heterogeneous pattern characterized by coexistence of viable adipose tissue, areas of adipocyte degeneration, persistent fibrotic septa, and early granulation tissue formation. This structural heterogeneity is consistent with partial graft integration and ongoing extracellular matrix remodeling, corresponding to the incomplete but measurable clinical improvement observed across functional parameters.

Two animals experienced postoperative wound dehiscence with graft extrusion, representing treatment-related adverse outcomes. Although these animals could not be included in the longitudinal analysis because follow-up was incomplete, their occurrence should be considered when interpreting the safety profile of the experimental procedure. Future studies with larger sample sizes will be required to better characterize the incidence and determinants of these complications.

The present findings gain additional relevance when interpreted alongside the complementary ultrasonographic analysis previously reported using the same experimental framework (10). In that study, serial ultrasonographic evaluation demonstrated progressive alterations in dermal–subcutaneous architecture following bleomycin administration, including increased echogenic heterogeneity, altered fascial plane definition, and changes in tissue thickness, followed by partial structural normalization after autologous fat grafting. When correlated with the current histological observations, these imaging findings further support the concept that the macroscopic and ultrasonographic changes observed during follow-up reflect an underlying dynamic remodeling process rather than static fibrosis. The integration of longitudinal clinical evaluation, ultrasonographic monitoring, and histological characterization therefore provides a more comprehensive framework for studying early fibrosis-associated tissue remodeling than isolated endpoint assessment alone.

In parallel, clinical investigations have reported statistically significant improvements in tissue pliability, pain, and functional restriction after autologous fat transfer in post-radiotherapy settings, with outcomes influenced not only by patient- and disease-related factors but also by procedural variables such as injection technique and graft handling (7, 8, 13-15). While the precise mechanisms remain under investigation, proposed pathways include adipose-derived stromal cell–mediated paracrine signaling, angiogenic stimulation, and modulation of inflammatory persistence within fibrotic tissue. The present study contributes to this body of evidence by providing structured longitudinal macroscopic quantification within a controlled early-stage remodeling environment, thereby strengthening the translational link between experimental modeling and clinically observed functional improvement.

Importantly, the combined clinical–histological profile observed in the present study suggests that the effects of autologous fat grafting are not solely attributable to volumetric augmentation. Instead, the findings support a dynamic remodeling process involving partial graft survival, modulation of inflammatory activity, and progressive reorganization of the extracellular matrix.

Beyond the biological observations, an important contribution of the present study lies in the structured clinical evaluation framework applied throughout the experimental timeline. While most bleomycin-based models prioritize histological or molecular endpoints, macroscopic clinical dynamics are less frequently quantified in a standardized and reproducible manner (10, 12). By implementing a predefined semi-quantitative scoring system encompassing erythema, edema, elasticity, mobility, and ulceration or necrosis, the present study enabled longitudinal assessment of tissue evolution within individual subjects.

The concordance between independent evaluators and the use of a composite clinical score further enhanced reproducibility and reduced interpretative variability. This approach not only facilitated objective comparison before and after regenerative intervention but also provides a practical translational tool that may be adapted in future experimental studies investigating antifibrotic or regenerative therapies. In this context, structured clinical scoring represents a complementary dimension to histological analysis, allowing direct correlation between functional changes and underlying tissue remodeling. The semi-quantitative scoring system used in this study has not undergone formal external validation. Nevertheless, all clinical assessments were performed independently by the same two investigators throughout the study using a predefined standardized scoring protocol to ensure consistency of the evaluations.

Study limitations. First, although the semi-quantitative clinical scoring system enabled structured longitudinal assessment of tissue evolution, macroscopic evaluation remains inherently observer-dependent despite the use of independent evaluators and composite score analysis. In addition, the absence of formal randomization and evaluator blinding may represent potential sources of bias when applying semi-quantitative assessment strategies. Second, the experimental cohort was relatively small and included only female Wistar rats, while the follow-up period was limited to early remodeling dynamics rather than long-term fibrotic evolution. Furthermore, although the submaximal bleomycin protocol was intentionally designed to reproduce dynamic early remodeling rather than advanced fibrosis, this approach may limit direct comparability with studies employing maximal fibrotic induction protocols. The model also reflects controlled dermal–subcutaneous alteration in otherwise healthy rodents and does not fully reproduce the complex systemic and microvascular environment of post-radiotherapy human tissue. Quantitative morphometric analysis was not included in the present study, limiting precise measurement of fibrosis extent and adipose tissue remodeling. Finally, routine systemic postoperative analgesia was not incorporated into the experimental protocol because of concerns regarding potential modulation of inflammatory and fibrotic pathways relevant to the study objectives. This approach was prospectively reviewed and approved by the institutional ethics committee, and animals were monitored daily according to predefined welfare and humane endpoint criteria throughout the study period.

Recent evidence further supports the concept that fibrosis represents a dynamic biological process regulated by the interplay between hypoxia, inflammatory cells, and extracellular matrix remodeling. Hypoxia-driven fibroblast activation has been identified as an important mechanism sustaining profibrotic signaling and local inflammation in fibrotic tissues (16). Moreover, granulocytes and lymphocytes have been recognized as key regulators of the inflammatory response, tissue remodeling, and regenerative healing, highlighting the central role of immune cell dynamics during tissue repair (17). Experimental studies investigating regenerative biomaterials have further demonstrated that modulation of the local inflammatory microenvironment promotes tissue integration and structural remodeling (18). Collectively, these findings reinforce our observations that early fibrotic remodeling remains biologically active and may respond to regenerative interventions such as autologous fat grafting. The histological findings of the present study, including increased collagen bundle density, dermal thickening, and mild inflammatory infiltrate, are consistent with the early dermal–hypodermal remodeling pattern previously documented using the same experimental framework (11).

The present study should be interpreted as a complementary histological analysis of a previously established experimental model. While the experimental protocol and animal cohort are identical to those reported in our methodological publication and in our previous clinical and ultrasonographic analysis, the current manuscript addresses a distinct research question by providing a detailed histological evaluation of tissue remodeling and graft integration. Accordingly, the histological data presented here are original and have not been published previously.

Conclusion

The present study demonstrates that early dermal–subcutaneous remodeling induced through a controlled submaximal bleomycin protocol represents a biologically active and clinically relevant stage that can be reproducibly modeled in an experimental setting. Within this framework, autologous fat grafting was associated with measurable improvement in tissue elasticity and cutaneous mobility, while histological analysis demonstrated persistent but dynamic remodeling characterized by extracellular matrix reorganization, vascular alteration, and heterogeneous adipose tissue integration rather than complete reversal of fibrosis. By combining structured longitudinal clinical evaluation with histological characterization, the present study provides a translational framework for investigating regenerative interventions in early-stage fibrosis-associated soft tissue alteration. More importantly, the findings support the concept that fibrosis-associated remodeling should not necessarily be regarded as a static terminal process, but rather as a dynamic biological continuum that may remain structurally and functionally responsive to tissue-modulating regenerative strategies.

Histological validation of three-dimensional variable flip angle turbo spin echo multi-contrast magnetic resonance vessel wall imaging in characterizing carotid vulnerable atherosclerotic plaques
Authors: Jiachen Liu, Zihan Ning, Chenlin Du, Shuo Chen, Tao Wang, Jingli Cao, Ran Huo, Dongye Li, Dandan Yang, Rui Shen, Shuwan Yu, Chunjiang Hu, Shuhao Wang, Huiyu Qiao, Xihai Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAccurate assessment of the vulnerability of carotid atherosclerotic plaques is crucial for stroke prevention. The three-dimensional (3D) magnetic resonance (MR) vessel wall imaging (VWI) has been increasingly employed to evaluate carotid plaques due to its extensive coverage and isotropic high spatial resolution. However, the accuracy of such techniques lacks validation by histology. Therefore, this study aims to validate the accuracy of 3D multi-contrast MR VWI with variable-flip-angle (VFA) and turbo spin echo (TSE) readout in identifying vulnerable carotid plaques, using histological analysis as a reference.MethodsTwenty-one male patients (mean age: 64.4 ± 7.2 years old) scheduled for carotid endarterectomy (CEA) were recruited in this study. All patients underwent carotid multi-contrast MR VWI, including 3D T1- and T2-weighted VFA-TSE sequences, as well as 3D time of flight (TOF) MR angiography (MRA), using a 3.0T MR system before surgery. Histological processing was performed for carotid plaque specimens. The presence or absence, along with the area measurements, of lipid-rich necrotic core (LRNC), intraplaque hemorrhage (IPH), and calcifications (CA) were independently evaluated on both MR images and histological sections. Cohen’s kappa (κ) analysis was utilized to determine the agreement between 3D multi-contrast MR VWI and histology in identifying carotid plaque compositions before and after excluding compositions bellow certain size threshold. Spearman’s correlation analysis was also conducted to assess the agreement in quantifying plaque compositions.ResultsA total of 81 slices of MR images were successfully matched with histological sections. Moderate to almost perfect agreements were observed between 3D MR VWI and histology in the identification of LRNC (κ: 0.85 and 0.89), IPH (κ: 0.65 and 0.69), and CA (κ: 0.46 and 0.62) before and after excluding compositions smaller than 0.79 mm2. Strong to very strong correlations were found in the quantification of plaque compositions including LRNC (r=0.88), IPH (r=0.80), and CA (r=0.74) between MR imaging and histology.ConclusionThe 3D VFA-TSE multi-contrast MR VWI is capable of accurately characterizing vulnerable carotid atherosclerotic plaques.Keywords: Carotid artery, Atherosclerosis, Vulnerable plaque, Magnetic resonance imaging, Histology

Introduction

Stroke is a major cause of disability and death worldwide [1]. Disruption of carotid vulnerable atherosclerotic plaques is an important etiology for ischemic cerebrovascular events [2]. A recent multicenter study reported that among the Chinese population, 28% (292/1047) of patients with ischemic stroke or transient ischemia attack had vulnerable carotid artery plaques [3]. Vulnerable atherosclerotic plaques are characterized by compositional features, such as large lipid-rich necrotic core (LRNC), intraplaque hemorrhage (IPH), surface or multiple calcifications (CA), and rupture of fibrous cap [4]. These features have been demonstrated as effective indicators for ischemic stroke [5]. Therefore, accurate evaluation of vulnerable carotid plaque compositional features is crucial for stratifying stroke risk and preventing future cerebrovascular events.

The two-dimensional (2D) multi-contrast magnetic resonance (MR) vessel wall imaging (VWI) has been well demonstrated to be capable of identifying and quantifying carotid plaque compositions on both 1.5T [6], [7] and 3.0T [8], [9] MR platforms validated by histology. However, its small longitudinal coverage (around 32 mm), low inter-slice resolution (2 mm), and long scan time (>20 min) limit the utilization in clinical settings, particularly for individuals with multiple atherosclerotic lesions or co-existing intracranial and extracranial carotid artery diseases. Over the last decade, several three-dimensional (3D) VWI imaging techniques have been proposed and optimized for characterizing both carotid and intracranial artery diseases, including 3D motion sensitized driven equilibrium prepared rapid gradient echo (3D MERGE) [10], 3D T1- and T2-weighted variable-flip-angle turbo spin echo (3D VFA-TSE) [11], [12], [13], [14], and 3D simultaneous non-contrast angiography and intraplaque hemorrhage (SNAP) [15], [16]. Benefiting from isotropic high spatial resolution, large longitudinal coverage, and more sufficient blood suppression, 3D MR VWI techniques have been increasingly utilized for assessing co-existing intracranial and extracranial carotid artery atherosclerotic diseases [17], [18].

Compared with 2D turbo spin echo (TSE)–based VWI sequences, the 3D VFA-TSE sequence enables a static pseudo steady state, thereby prolonging the echo train length with the intrinsic black blood effect [19]. Technically, the 3D VFA-TSE–based MR VWI techniques exhibit slightly different signal contrast when compared to 2D TSE–based MR VWI sequences due to variations in the underlying physical mechanisms during acquisition. In 2020, Wei et al. demonstrated that 3D multi-contrast MR VWI, including 3D SNAP, 3D MERGE, and T2-Volume ISotropic Turbo spin echo Acquisition (VISTA) sequences, showed good agreement with 2D multi-contrast MR VWI in characterizing carotid plaque features [20]. However, regarding the imaging protocols, the 3D SNAP and 3D MERGE sequences are currently exclusive to Philips MR platforms, while 3D VFA-TSE sequences are available on various MR platforms but lack validation. Moreover, there still lacks histological validation for 3D multi-contrast MR VWI techniques, predominantly consisting of VFA-TSE sequences, in evaluating vulnerable plaque compositions.

Therefore, this study aimed to validate the accuracy of 3D VFA-TSE multi-contrast MR VWI in characterizing vulnerable carotid plaques by histology.

Methods

Study population

Patients with symptomatic moderate-to-severe stenosis (stenosis ≥50%) or asymptomatic severe stenosis (stenosis ≥70%) in carotid arteries determined by computer tomography angiography or magnetic resonance angiography (MRA) and had scheduled to carotid endarterectomy (CEA) were recruited in this study. All recruited patients underwent carotid MR VWI within 1 week before CEA. Patients who had claustrophobia, contraindications to MR imaging, or critical conditions, such as coma, heart failure, or respiratory failure, were excluded. Clinical information, including age, sex, blood pressure, levels for lipoprotein, history of hypertension, diabetes, smoking, cardiovascular disease, and stroke, was collected from the medical records. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of Tsinghua University School of Medicine and written consent form was obtained from all patients.

Carotid MR imaging

Carotid 3D multi-contrast MR VWI was performed on a whole-body 3.0T MR scanner (Ingenia CX, Philips Healthcare, Best, The Netherlands) equipped with a dedicated 8-channel carotid coil. The protocol included 3D T1-VISTA, 3D T2-VISTA, and 3D time of flight (TOF) MRA sequences. To enhance signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), the variable-refocusing-flip-angle scheme of the 3D VISTA sequences ranged from αmin = 50° to αmax = 140°. Additionally, a SPectral Attenuated Inversion Recovery pre-pulse was used to suppress the fat signal and improve the visualization of the outer wall boundary. An isotropic high spatial resolution of 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5 mm3 was applied to both 3D T1-VISTA and 3D T2-VISTA imaging sequences. Compressed sensing was adopted for acceleration, reducing the acquisition time to 7 minutes 5 seconds for 3D T1-VISTA and 7 minutes 55 seconds for 3D T2-VISTA, respectively. The imaging parameters of 3D T1-VISTA, 3D T2-VISTA, and 3D TOF MRA are detailed in Table 1. The carotid MR VWI was conducted centering on the bifurcation of the indexed carotid artery, which is defined as carotid artery with moderate-to-severe stenosis referred to CEA treatment. The 3D T1- and T2-VISTA images were reconstructed into cross-section planes for further analysis. Subsequently, the 3D TOF MRA was reconstructed into slices with a thickness of 0.5 mm and registered with the axial 3D T1- and T2-VISTA images.

Specimen processing and histological image review

Carotid plaque specimens were obtained following standard CEA procedure and promptly fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin upon excision. Subsequently, all specimens underwent decalcification in 10% formic acid and were embedded in paraffin blocks. The specimens were then sectioned (10 µm) every 0.5 mm throughout their length and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Histological assessment was conducted by a histologist (J.C.) with over 5 years of experience in analyzing atherosclerotic plaques, blinded to the MR image reviews. The presence or absence of LRNC, IPH, and CA was determined, and the areas of these plaque compositions were measured using Thorough Wisdom software (Thorough Images, Beijing, China) and the published criteria [21].

Image matching between MR images and histology

The histological sections were matched with the 3D multi-contrast MR vessel wall images by an MR reviewer (J.L.) who was not involved in the subsequent MR image review of this study. To ensure the accuracy of the matching process, a senior MR reviewer (X.Z.) reviewed and confirmed the final matching results. To account for potential shrinkages during the CEA specimen processing, additional morphological features of lumen, vessel wall, CA, and loose matrix were used for co-registration.

Image analysis

The 3D multi-contrast MR vessel wall images, successfully matched with the histological sections, underwent analysis by two radiologists (D.L. and R.H.) with over 5 years of experience in vascular imaging independently. The radiologists reviewed the images blinded to both the histological analysis results and the MR imaging assessments of each other, utilizing VesselExplorer 2.0 software (TSImaging Healthcare, Beijing, China). In case of disagreement, a senior reviewer (X.Z.) with more than 10 years of experience in vascular imaging provided further interpretation. Image quality of the MR images was rated using a 4-point scale (1, poor; 2, marginal; 3, good; 4, excellent). Images with poor image quality were excluded from the review. The boundaries of the lumen and outer wall were manually outlined, and the areas for the lumen and vessel wall were measured at each axial slice. The presence and areas of three main carotid plaque components, LRNC, IPH, and CA, were evaluated based on published criteria [6]: (1) LRNC is typically located in the core of the plaque and appears isointense to hyperintense on T1-weighted (T1W) images. However, its signal intensity on T2-weighted (T2W) and TOF images can vary depending on the amount and age of hemorrhage present; (2) IPH was identified following the method described by Saam et al. [6]. Specifically, IPH appears as hyperintense on T1W and TOF images, and as isointense to hyperintense on T2W images; (3) CA is characterized by well-defined areas with a hypointense signal across all imaging weightings. In practice, regions showing hypointense simultaneous on two different contrast-weighted images were identified as CA. The identification of IPH was conducted before LRNC, as IPH was also regarded as part of LRNC according to Saam et al. [6].

Reproducibility study

A total of 10 patients were randomly selected from the recruited patients for the reproducibility study. For each patient, 24 axial images centered on the bifurcation of the indexed carotid artery were reviewed independently by 2 radiologists (D.L. and R.H.) following the criteria mentioned above for inter-observer reproducibility analysis. One of the radiologists reviewed all the MR images again using the same criteria for intra-observer reproducibility analysis with 1 month time interval after the first round of image review to minimize the memory bias.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean and standard deviation, while discrete variables were described as counts and frequency. Cohen’s kappa (κ) analysis was used to determine the agreement between 3D multi-contrast MR vessel wall images and histological sections in identifying carotid plaque compositions. Since the spatial resolution of histological sections was much higher than that of MR VWI, the Cohen’s κ values were calculated before and after excluding histological plaque compositions smaller than a certain threshold. The threshold was determined as a circular area (A) where the radius was the spatial resolution (0.5 mm) of T1W and T2W images: A = π (0.5 mm)2 [8]. Therefore, the cutoff value was 0.79 mm2. The 95% confidence intervals (CI) of the κ values were calculated with the bootstrap method using 10,000 resamples. The κ values were divided into five scales: <0.2 (slight agreement), 0.2–0.4 (fair agreement), 0.4–0.6 (moderate agreement), 0.6–0.8 (substantial agreement), and 0.8–1.0 (almost perfect agreement). The histological analysis results were regarded as the golden standard for assessing carotid plaque compositional characteristics. The accuracy, specificity, sensitivity, positive prediction value (PPV), and negative prediction value (NPV) of 3D multi-contrast MR VWI in identifying carotid plaque compositions were calculated. The agreement of the area for each plaque composition measured by 3D MR VWI and histology was determined using Spearman’s correlation coefficients and Bland-Altman plots. For Spearman’s correlation coefficient (r), the strength of the relationships was evaluated as follows: <0.2 (very weak correlations), 0.2–0.4 (weak correlations), 0.4–0.6 (moderate correlations), 0.6–0.8 (strong correlation), and >0.8 (very strong correlations). As there is a possibly 7.8% shrinkage of histological sections during histological processing [22], the area of plaque composition measured by histology was normalized accordingly.

The intra-observer and inter-observer agreements in identifying plaque compositions were determined by Cohen’s kappa analysis. The agreement in measuring lumen area, wall area, and the areas for LRNC, IPH, and CA was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) analysis. Excellent reliability was determined when ICC >0.75. Two-sided P values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistics were performed using the SPSS software 27 (IBM, Chicago, Illinois, USA).

Results

A total of 21 patients were recruited in this study from October 2019 to January 2020. Of 21 patients, the mean age was 64.4 ± 7.2 years old, 100% (21/21) were male, 81.0% (17/21) had hypertension, 61.9% (13/21) had hyperlipidemia, 61.9% (13/21) had diabetes, 81% (17/21) had history of smoking, and 33.3% (7/21) had history of stroke. Agreement between 3D MR VWI and histology in identifying plaque compositions

Of the 21 patients, 270 histological sections were obtained and stained, and 81 were successfully matched to carotid MR images with acceptable image quality for statistical analysis. The remaining 189 sections were excluded because of the following reasons: (1) poor image quality due to motion artifacts (n = 15); (2) plaques located outside the field of view in the intracranial region (n = 20); (3) histology sections contaminated or incomplete after staining during image transfer (n = 5); and (4) histology sections damaged due to improper processing (n = 149). Of the 81 matched histological sections, 70 (86.4%) had LRNC, 56 (59.1%) had IPH, and 56 (69.1%) had CA. The κ values, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV of 3D multi-contrast MR VWI in identifying plaque compositions ranged from 0.46 to 0.89, 71.6% to 97.5%, 52.2% to 90.9%, 96.9% to 100.0%, 90.9% to 100.0%, and 60.7% to 98.6%, respectively (Table 3). Two representative cases are illustrated in Fig. 1, demonstrating the review results of 3D multi-contrast MR VWI and histology, respectively. For all plaque compositions, higher κ value was reached after excluding histological sections with plaque compositions smaller than 0.79 mm2.

Agreement between 3D MR VWI and histology in quantifying plaque compositions

Of the 81 MR vessel wall images matched with histological sections, 68 (84.0%), 42 (51.9%), and 34 (42.0%) were found to have LRNC, IPH, and CA on both MR vessel wall images and histological sections, respectively. Moderate to very strong correlations were observed in the quantification of all plaque compositions: LRNC (r = 0.88, P < 0.001), IPH (r = 0.80, P < 0.001), and CA (r = 0.74, P < 0.001) (Table 4). The Bland-Altman plots indicated that the bias in measuring LRNC, IPH, and CA was 7.84 ± 12.27 mm2, 2.06 ± 12.53 mm2, and −0.33 ± 1.77 mm2, respectively (Fig. S1).

Reproducibility

Good to excellent intra-observer (LRNC, к = 0.81; IPH, к = 0.84; CA, к = 0.78) and inter-observer (LRNC, к = 0.80; IPH, к = 0.86; CA, к = 0.70) agreements were found in identifying carotid plaque compositions using 3D MR VWI (Table S1). Excellent intra-observer agreement was found in quantifying the area of lumen (ICC = 0.99), wall (ICC = 0.91), IPH (ICC = 0.87), and CA (ICC = 0.93) using 3D MR VWI. Excellent inter-observer agreement can be found in quantifying the area of lumen (ICC = 0.99), wall (ICC = 0.95), LRNC (ICC = 0.81), IPH (ICC = 0.94), and CA (ICC = 0.93) using 3D MR VWI (Table S2).

Discussion

This study validated the accuracy of 3D VFA-TSE multi-contrast MR VWI in evaluating carotid vulnerable plaque compositional characteristics by histology. Moderate to substantial agreement was found in identifying and quantifying carotid atherosclerotic plaque features between 3D multi-contrast MR VWI and histology. By excluding plaque compositions below circular area with resolution as radius, the κ values between 3D multi-contrast MR VWI and histology increased. We also found good to excellent inter- and intra-observer reproducibility in assessing the key vulnerable plaque features, including LRNC, IPH, and CA, using 3D multi-contrast MR VWI. Our findings suggest that 3D multi-contrast MR VWI is capable of accurately characterizing carotid artery vulnerable plaques.

In the present study, substantial agreement was found between 3D multi-contrast MR VWI and histology in identifying carotid IPH, depending on the size of the IPH. The overall performance of 3D multi-contrast MR VWI (к: 0.65) is comparable to 2D multi-contrast MR VWI (к: 0.40–0.70) [23], while demonstrating much better inter-observer reproducibility (к: 0.80 vs 0.40). We also found that the agreement between 3D multi-contrast MR VWI and histology in detecting IPH was getting better when excluding small IPH below threshold (к: 0.65–0.69). This phenomenon has also been reported in a previous study focusing on different T1 sequences in characterizing carotid IPH [8]. However, 3D multi-contrast MR VWI showed better overall performance when compared to these 2D VWI techniques based on single contrast (к: 0.65 vs 0.33–0.53) [8]. Previous studies demonstrated that T1 turbo field echo sequence (к = 0.73) was superior to T1 spin echo sequence in detecting carotid IPH due to its better contrast-to-noise ratio [24]. In quantification of carotid IPH, the present study revealed that 3D multi-contrast VWI had very strong correlation (r = 0.80) with histology in measuring carotid IPH. Ota et al. reported that 3D T1 gradient-echo sequence of magnetization-prepared rapid acquisition gradient-echo imaging (MP-RAGE) (r = 0.81) showed better agreement with histology in quantifying carotid IPH compared to 2D T1 spin echo sequence (r = 0.50) [8]. Recently, a study comparing different 3D VWI techniques in characterizing and quantifying carotid IPH showed that 3D SNAP imaging (к = 0.73, r = 0.81) had better agreement compared to 3D MP-RAGE (к = 0.52, r = 0.64), especially for smaller IPH [22]. These studies suggest that adding gradient-echo–based T1 imaging sequence into 3D multi-contrast MR VWI protocol may enhance its capability of detecting IPH.

We found that 3D multi-contrast MR VWI had almost perfect agreement with histology in identification of LRNC. The performance of 3D multi-contrast MR VWI (к = 0.85 and к = 0.89) was much better than 2D MR VWI (к: 0.69–0.73) [6], [25] both before and after excluding slices with LRNC smaller than 0.79 mm2. The Bloch equation (Fig. S2) demonstrated that the difference in signal intensity between normal vessel wall and LRNC on 3D T1- and T2-VWI was smaller than that on 2D T1- and T2-VWI, which might hinder the identification of the LRNC. With the increases in the size of LRNC, in the present study, the kappa value between 3D multi-contrast MR VWI and histology increased from 0.85 to 0.89. In quantitative evaluation, the size of LRNC measured by 3D multi-contrast VWI showed very strong correlation with histology (r = 0.88). Previous studies have shown that utilizing contrast-enhanced T1-VWI will significantly increase the accuracy of measuring the area of LRNC [26]. In future studies with requirement of accurate quantification of LRNC (e.g., studies monitoring lipid-lowering treatment effect on lipid-rich plaques), it is warranted to acquire post-contrast 3D T1-VWI for individuals without renal dysfunction.

In the present study, the 3D multi-contrast MR VWI demonstrated moderate to substantial agreement with histology in identification of carotid CA depending on its size (к: 0.46–0.62). Saam et al. [6] reported a substantial agreement (к = 0.75) in identifying CA between multi-contrast MR VWI at 1.5T and histology. However, in current study, a slight inferior performance of 3D MR VWI, even excluding sections with CA area <0.79 mm2 (к = 0.62), was found. This may be due to the different in-plane resolution, as a relative higher reconstruction resolution of 0.25 × 0.25 or 0.31 × 0.31 mm2 was applied in the previous study, in which the identification of CA may be improved. Additionally, comparing with 1.5T MR, 3.0T MR has stronger magnetic sensitivity effect and B0 inhomogeneity. Therefore, it might lead to less accurate characterization of CA with 3.0T MR. Similar results had been reported by Ota et al. [8]. Moreover, the proton density weighted (PDW) imaging implemented 2D multi-contrast MR VWI at 1.5T might provide more information about CA. A recent study [27] illustrated that PDW imaging could detect more CA in lower extremity peripheral artery compared with MRA methods. For quantification of CA, strong correlations between 3D MR VWI and histology were reached in the present study (r = 0.74), suggesting a better performance of 3D MR VWI was achieved than in previous reports. A previous study [10] demonstrated that the improved SNR of the 3D MR imaging and the increased susceptibility of higher magnetic field may be two of the leading reasons for the better quantification of CA. Therefore, for better identifying CA, the imaging protocol and parameters might need to be further refined in future study.

In the present study, 3D MR VWI had false positive and false negative identification of plaque compositions in some cases. Many factors play roles in the disagreement between 3D multi-contrast MR VWI and histology in detection of plaque compositions. Smaller size of compositions is one of the reasons affecting the accuracy of identifying plaque compositions, especially for CA. After excluding those compositions with smaller size, the rates of false negative and positive identification decreased. Beyond the size of plaque compositions, ulcer, fresh IPH, and cholesterol crystals also played vital roles in false negative and false positive identifications (Table S3). The ulcer was the major reason for false positive detection of CA, because ulcers will lead to irregularity of the arterial lumen (Fig. 2). The irregular lumen of ulcers might slow down the blood flow which will attenuate the bright blood effect of TOF MRA and result in hypointense in part of region of ulcer on TOF MRA images. The fresh hemorrhage which appeared isointense on MR images may cause false negative of IPH. The cholesterol crystals are commonly seen within advanced atherosclerotic plaques. This composition usually shows isointense or hypointense on T1- and T2-weighted imaging, even on T1-weighted images of gradient-echo–based sequences such as MP-RAGE and SNAP [22]. Therefore, when the cholesterol crystals were mixed with other compositions such as IPH, false negative identification of these compositions may also occur (Fig. 3).

A typical case showed that the ulcer (black arrow) was indicated as CA. The ulcer (yellow arrows) showed isointense on 3D TOF MRA and hypointense on 3D T1- and T2-VISTA and therefore it was classified as CA. This might be attributed to the slow blood flow in the ulcer, and magnetically saturated in the 3D TOF MRA, while harming the black blood effect of 3D VISTA sequence. Limitations

The present study had several limitations. First, the study population was predominantly male, introducing a potential sex bias. Previous studies have reported sex differences in plaque features, with men potentially having higher-risk characteristics compared to women [28]. However, since the primary goal was to validate the efficiency of 3D VWI in characterizing vulnerable plaques, we believe the intrinsic slice–based analysis helps mitigate this bias and can represent the entire cohort. Nonetheless, future studies with more balanced male and female participants are necessary for broader validation. Second, the scan time of the 3D MR VWI sequences was long (around 17 minutes), which may increase the risk of suffering from swallowing and motion artifacts. Advanced under-sampling and image reconstruction methods are required to accelerate the MR scan and achieve a better image quality. Third, the criteria for the MR VWI image review were established based on the 2D TSE sequence which might not be fully applicable for the image review of 3D VISTA images. Fourth, only the MR images from Philips MR scanner were included to characterize vulnerable plaques in this study. The accuracy of characterizing plaque compositions by VFA-TSE should be further validated on other MR imaging platforms. The present study demonstrated that, compared to former results with 2D TSE sequence, the signal pattern of LRNC was more complex. In the future, a 3D non-contrast MRA sequence with isotropic spatial resolution is warranted to introduce in 3D multi-contrast MR VWI. Last, the current study merely compared our results to former studies that used 2D-based methods, rather than performing a direct comparison by acquiring both 2D and 3D images within a single cohort. A study that includes such a direct comparison is still warranted to further validate the advantages of the 3D VFA-TSE technique.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the 3D multi-contrast VFA-TSE MR VWI is capable of accurately characterizing carotid vulnerable atherosclerotic plaques.

Semantic Neural Radiance Fields for Multi-Date Satellite Data
Valentin Wagner, Sebastian Bullinger, Christoph Bodensteiner, Michael Arens

Abstract
In this work we propose a satellite specific Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) model capable to obtain a three-dimensional semantic representation (neural semantic field) of the scene. The model derives the output from a set of multi-date satellite images with corresponding pixel-wise semantic labels. We demonstrate the robustness of our approach and its capability to improve noisy input labels. We enhance the color prediction by utilizing the semantic information to address temporal image inconsistencies caused by non-stationary categories such as vehicles. To facilitate further research in this domain, we present a dataset comprising manually generated labels for popular multi-view satellite images. Our code and dataset are available at https://github.com/wagnva/semantic-nerf-for-satellite-data .

Introduction

As the number of satellites in orbit continues to rise, high-resolution satellite imagery is more accessible than ever. Image-based reconstruction for creating large-scale environmental models has gained popularity due to its cost-effectiveness compared to dedicated systems like LiDAR. There is also an increasing emphasis on assigning semantic information to 3D environments, which enhances the analysis of urban areas and natural resources, leading to better decision-making in fields such as urban planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster management.

Traditional methods have focused on extracting explicit representations like pointclouds or meshes from the satellite images by matching image features

,

Subsequent works

(

Multi-Layer-Perceptrons
(

The scene structure is represented with the weights of the network.
Aggregation of
MLP

NeRF
are capable to learn and reproduce additional modalities beside color using a shared three-dimensional representation
[
19
,
18
]
.
Handling multi-date satellite image data introduces additional challenges such as domain-specific camera models, variable lighting, moving shadows and transient objects such as vehicles.
Existing works

As of our best knowledge this is the first work to present a dual modality

Contributions

Our contributions are as following: (A) A satellite-domain-adapted semantic

(B) Improved color consistency for transient areas such as vehicles in inconsistent images by exploiting semantic information. (C) Demonstration of the robustness/feasibility of our approach and its capability to improve quality of semantic training data through enforcement of multi-view consistency for the satellite domain. Related Works

Semantic-NeRF

[
19
]
shows that
NeRF
architectures are capable to simultaneously learn a combined appearance and semantic 3D model for a scene given a set of input color images with corresponding semantic labels.
This allows to render novel, unseen views as color image with a corresponding pixel-wise segmentation image.
The two modalities share the same scene structure, leading to a high cohesion in rendering quality.
Additionally, they show that the inherent multi-view consistency provided by
NeRF
is able to smooth noisy semantic training labels.

Shadow-NeRF

[
6
]
adapts
NeRF
to the satellite domain by introducing an altitude-based ray sampling and an irradiance lighting model. They separate the static scene color from variable shadows and ambient lighting using the sun direction as input. This allows the network to learn highly variant lighting in its own component.

SatNeRF

[
11
]
introduces transient-object filtering and adapts the domain-specific
Rational Polynomial Coefficient
(
RPC
)
[
14
]
camera model for ray generation, increasing accuracy.
As satellite datasets are usually multi-date, transient objects are moving inbetween image captures. To reduce artifacts
SatNeRF

[
11
]
introduces an uncertainty term, learned based on an image-specific embedding.

EO-NeRF

[
10
]
further iterates on handling the varying lighting conditions. Instead of learning shadows they are dynamically rendered based on the scene geometry and sun direction.

SUNDIAL

[
1
]
models complex illumination to improve dynamic shadow rendering of vegetation and water areas.
Whereas the results for
EO-NeRF

[
10
]
and
SUNDIAL

[
1
]
are promising, no code is publicly available.
We therefore base our work on
Shadow-NeRF

[
6
]
and
SatNeRF

[
11
]
.

3.1
Satellite-Domain-Specific Camera Model

During the training and testing phase of a
NeRF
, rays are constructed for each pixel of the given images and their corresponding camera poses, traversing a pre-defined, bounded three-dimensional volume.
As proposed by
SatNeRF

[
11
]
we use the
RPC

[
14
]
camera model instead of the commonly used pinhole
[
7
]
camera model.
This domain-specific camera model is an approximation of the physical satellite camera.
We extract it from the satellite image metadata as it is typically used for georegistration of the image.
To improve accuracy,
SatNeRF

[
11
]
performs
Bundle Adjustment
on the
RPC
models. This process optimizes relative camera poses to increase ray accuracy.

3.2
Learning Semantic in 3D for Satellite Data

NeRF

[
12
]
represent a static three-dimensional scene as a continuous volumetric function
\mathcal{F}_{NeRF}
encoded with a
MLP
network.

\mathcal{F}_{NeRF}:(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{d})\mapsto(\sigma,\boldsymbol{c})

(1)

Given a 3D point
\boldsymbol{x}=(x,y,z)
and a viewing direction
\boldsymbol{d}=(d_{x},d_{y},d_{z})
, the network predicts a density scalar
\sigma
and a color
\boldsymbol{c}=(r,g,b)
.
The scene structure is represented by the density
\sigma
and used to weigh the influence of the color
\boldsymbol{c}
.

SatNeRF

[
11
]
proposes an expanded satellite-domain-specific architecture.

\mathcal{F}_{SatNeRF}:(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{t}_{j})\mapsto(\sigma,\boldsymbol{c}_{a},\mathit{sun},\boldsymbol{a},\beta)

(2)

The scene color
\boldsymbol{c}
is split up into three separate lighting components: the albedo color
\boldsymbol{c}_{a}
, the sun shading scalar
\mathit{sun}
and the ambient color
\boldsymbol{a}
. To predict the variable lighting components
\mathit{sun}
and
\boldsymbol{a}
, the sun direction
\boldsymbol{\omega}
is used as auxiliary input.
The uncertainty
\beta
is used to reduce artifacts caused by transient objects such as vehicles. It is learned with the per-image embedding
\boldsymbol{t}_{j}
as auxilliary input.
Our proposed semantic satellite-adapted network represents the following function
\mathcal{F}_{SemSatNeRF}
.

\mathcal{F}_{SemSatNeRF}:(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{t}_{j})\mapsto(\sigma,\boldsymbol{c}_{a},\mathit{sun},\boldsymbol{a},\beta,\boldsymbol{s})

(3)

We extend the satellite-domain-adapted architecture from
SatNeRF

[
11
]
with the additional semantic output
\boldsymbol{s}
.
Similiar to
Semantic-NeRF

[
19
]
the semantic output
\boldsymbol{s}

is represented as a view-invariant function
\mathcal{F}_{s}(\boldsymbol{x})\mapsto\boldsymbol{s}
of the world coordinates
\boldsymbol{x}
and describes a distribution over
C
semantic labels as pre-softmax logits.
In addition, we introduce a
\mathit{sigmoid}
activation function with the intention to increase the generalization across views, using the

\mathit{sigmoid}
activation function as form of normalization. This reduces the ability of single samples to dominate the semantic prediction.

\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{x})=\mathit{sigmoid}(\mathcal{F}_{s}(\boldsymbol{x}))

(4)

To produce the final result for a specific pixel position,
NeRF
-based pipelines aggregate the outputs of the
MLP
for individual points
\boldsymbol{x}
along a ray
\boldsymbol{r}(t)=\boldsymbol{o}+t\cdot\boldsymbol{d}
representing the corresponding line of sight. Here,
\boldsymbol{o}
and
\boldsymbol{d}
denote the origin and direction vectors.

\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}T_{i}\alpha_{i}\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})

(5)

The influence of each sample
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
along a ray is based on its opacity
\alpha_{i}
and transmittance
T_{i}
.

\alpha_{i}=1-\exp(-\sigma_{i}\delta_{i})\text{ and }T_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}(1-\alpha_{j})

(6)

The distance
\delta_{i}=t_{i}-t_{i-1}
describes the length traveled along the ray between the previous and current sampled position
\boldsymbol{x}
.
Using softmax normalization the combined logits
\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{r})
are converted into probabilities
\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}(\boldsymbol{r})
. The class with the highest probability is chosen to represent the semantic class of the ray.

3.3
Semantic Structural Visualization

To visualize the semantic labels for a given image, usually a simple color mapping operation
f_{c}:\boldsymbol{s}\mapsto\boldsymbol{RGB}
is used.
This visualization is sub-optimal for large areas with the same class, as the structure of the scene is lost.
For large structures consisting of multiple buildings with large height differences this is especially noticeable, such as in the scenes JAX_068 and JAX_214 in
Fig.

3(b)
.

To handle variance in lighting caused by different sun positions in training images, we use a satellite-domain-adapted irradiance lighting model as proposed by
Shadow-NeRF

[
6
]
.
The sample color
\boldsymbol{c}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega})
is split up into three components:
scene color
\boldsymbol{c_{a}}(\boldsymbol{x})
, lighting scalar
\mathit{sun}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega})
and ambient sky color
\boldsymbol{a}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
.

\boldsymbol{c}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega})=\boldsymbol{c}_{a}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot(\mathit{sun}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega})+(1-\mathit{sun}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega}))\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(\boldsymbol{\omega}))

(7)

By referencing the visualization of the
sun(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega})
lighting component in
Fig.

3(d)
we can see that it resembles a grayscale relief map of the scene structure.
We use this to provide a three-dimensional depth to the semantic output. We aggregate the
\mathit{sun}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\omega})
component along rays
\boldsymbol{r}
analog to
Eq.

5
and combine it with the mapped semantic color
f_{c}(\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{r}))
.

\boldsymbol{c}_{s}(\boldsymbol{r})=f_{c}(\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{r}))*\sum_{i=1}^{N}T_{i}\alpha_{i}\mathit{sun}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i},\boldsymbol{\omega})

(8)

The resulting visualization of the structural semantic prediction is shown in
Fig.

3(e)
. The commonly flat color visualization is enriched with a three-dimensional structure. Finer details are made visible and objects such as the distinct main building can now be easily identified.

3.4
Handling Transient Objects

A major issue by processing multi-date satellite images are temporal inconsistencies. Besides changes in the scene geometry, transient objects pose a substantial challenge for the reconstruction process. As transient objects change positions across training images, the network receives contradictory information.
Since we are interested in reconstructing a consistent model reflecting the stationary scene geometry, transient objects represent a type of noise.
To improve the model consistency, we use available transient category information contained in the semantic labels to reduce the impact of such objects on the semantic as well as the appearance prediction.
We separately consider the problem for both modalities as semantic and appearance are learned with their own distinct loss function.

3.4.1
Handling Transient Objects in RGB Data

NeRF
are optimized by minimizing the
L2
-Loss between the predicted ray color
\boldsymbol{c}(\boldsymbol{r})
and the ground truth pixel color
\boldsymbol{c}_{GT}(\boldsymbol{r})
.

L_{2}(\mathcal{R})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{R}}||\boldsymbol{c}(\boldsymbol{r})-\boldsymbol{c}_{GT}(\boldsymbol{r})||_{2}^{2}

(9)

To reduce the effect of transient objects in the color prediction
SatNeRF

[
11
]
introduces an uncertainty mechanism. Based on a per-image learned embedding
t_{j}
the network predicts an uncertainty factor
\beta
for each location
\boldsymbol{x}
.
The color-loss is decreased depending on the uncertainty, giving the network the ability to selectively reduce the loss of specific rays.

L_{color}(\mathcal{R})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{||\boldsymbol{c}(\boldsymbol{r})-\boldsymbol{c}_{GT}(\boldsymbol{r})||^{2}_{2}}{2\beta^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{r})^{2}}+\left(\frac{\log{\beta^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{r})+\eta}}{2}\right)

(10)

The second term forces the network to use the uncertainty sparingly preventing the naive solution of
\beta
converging towards infinity. To prevent negative values in the logarithm we set
\beta^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{r})=\beta(\boldsymbol{r})+0.05
and
\eta=3
analog to
SatNeRF

[
11
]
.

The uncertainty
\beta
gives the network an mechanism to reduce the impact of local outliers in the color training images. Although many transient objects such as vehicles move inbetween images, locations such as parking lots contain vehicles in majority of the images. To achieve our goal of learning a static, transient free representation of the scene we introduce a transient regularization loss. Instead of a purely statistical approach we propose guiding the uncertainty towards transient objects using existing information from the semantical ground truth data.
To this end, we introduce a transient regularization loss as seen in
Eq.

11
. For rays
\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{R}^{t}
that are associated with transient ground truth labels, the following loss guides the uncertainty
\beta
towards
1
.

L_{t}(\mathcal{R}^{t})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{R}^{t}}||1.0-\beta(\boldsymbol{r})||^{2}_{2}

(11)

We do not make any assumptions about rays outside of the transient classes, allowing the network to still learn its own uncertainty values. This ensures that the network still handles other occurring phenomena such as chromatic aberration.
Whereas the uncertainty
\beta
is theoretically unbounded, the second term of the color-loss in
Eq.

10
punishes high values. The uncertainty scalar
\beta
naturally resides in the interval from
0
to around
1
. We therefore guide the network towards
1
for rays
\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{R}^{t}
.

SatNeRF

[
11
]
proposes using the non-modified color-loss as seen in
Eq.

9
for the first two epochs. This gives the network time to learn an initial understanding of the scene geometry and color. We enable our proposed transient regularization loss after three epochs.
This gives the network one additional epoch to initialize the uncertainty
\beta
.

3.4.2
Handling Transient Objects in Semantic Data

Common
NeRF
methods only derive a 3d appearance model of the scene. By introducing a semantic representation, the network now has a secondary goal, requiring an additional loss term.

Semantic-NeRF

[
19
]
uses a cross-entropy-loss
\sum_{c=1}^{C}p^{c}(\boldsymbol{r})\log{\hat{p}^{c}(\boldsymbol{r})}
comparing the ground truth label
p^{c}
with the predicted semantic probability
\hat{p}^{c}
for each class
c
across all rays
\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{R}
.
We define the semantic loss to only include rays from static classes
\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{t}
.
\mathcal{R}^{t}
hereby describes all rays
\boldsymbol{r}
with a semantic class defined as transient.

L_{semantic}(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{t})=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in(\mathcal{R}\setminus\mathcal{R}^{t})}\sum_{c=1}^{C}p^{c}(\boldsymbol{r})\log{\hat{p}^{c}(\boldsymbol{r})}

(12)

This effectivelly equals in ignoring parts of the semantic training labels, leaving holes in the pixel-wise label map. The network does not receive any learning guidance for these positions and has to rely on information given by different training views to fill in the missing information. This is made possible by the inherent multi-view consistency mechanism provided by
NeRFs
.

Implementation and Training Details

Analog to
SatNeRF

[
11
]
we use
8
layers of
512
features for the main feature backbone of the MLP. The additional semantic head consists of a single hidden layer with
256
features and uses a
\mathit{sigmoid}
activation function for semantic logit prediction. The architecture of the other heads such as the lighting scalar
\mathit{sun}
or the uncertainty
\beta
are identical to
SatNeRF

[
11
]
.

The semantic loss weight is set to
\lambda_{s}=0.04
as suggested by
Semantic-NeRF

[
19
]
.
A transient regularization loss value of
\lambda_{t}=0.1
shows in our experiments a good compromise between guiding the network to filter transient objects while simultaneously applying the uncertainty
\beta
for other color inconsistencies.
The transient embedding
\boldsymbol{t}_{j}
has a dimension of
4
.
As proposed by
SatNeRF

[
11
]
we use additional depth supervision rays for the first
25\%
of training iterations with a weight of
\lambda_{DS}=1000
and solar correction rays with a weight of
\lambda_{SC}=0.05
.
Depth supervision refers to using a sparse pointcloud created during the inital
Bundle Adjustment
on the
RPC
camera models for depth guidance.
Solar correction rays are used as part of the satellite-domain-adapted lighting model to align shadows with the scene structure based on the density.

We trained all models in this work for
300k
iterations with an Adam optimizer starting with a learning rate of
5e^{-4}
, which is decreased by a factor of
0.9
every epoch. Each ray is discretized into
64
points and we process
1024
rays each training iteration.
All models were trained using a NVIDIA RTX 4090 with
24
GB of RAM.

Transient Regularization

We evaluate the impact of our proposed transient regularization loss in increasing uncertainty of known transient locations by measuring the
Transient Uncertainty
.
We define this metric as the mean uncertainty for all transient locations
\mathcal{R}^{t}
,
i.e
.
the
Vehicles
class, in the semantic ground truth data. The uncertainty
\beta(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{t}_{j})

analog to

\beta_{t}(\mathcal{R}^{t})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^{t}|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathcal{R}^{t}}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\alpha_{i}T_{i}\beta(\boldsymbol{x}_{i},\boldsymbol{t}_{j})

(13)

Since the uncertainty

is only relevant during training, we do not consider test images during this evaluation.

We degrade the quality of our training data to more closely match the predicted output of automatic segmentation methods.
We aim to simulate the effect of automatic segmentation methods correctly recognizing and segmenting most of an object, with smaller areas being incorrectly classified.

To corrupt our labels we generate a normal noise map for each semantic class.
We blur and rescale the noise maps to create larger regions.
Based on a specified accuracy loss, thresholds are determined to identify regions in each noise map that should be corrupted.
Each noise region is reassigned to a different class.
We release the corrupted labels alongside the code for the automated label corruption as part of the dataset.

Conclusions

This paper proposes a novel
NeRF
architecture for generating a unified color and semantic 3D representation of scenes derived from satellite images and their corresponding pixel-wise labels.
By leveraging semantic information of transient categories during the training process we are able to improve the appearance representation of corresponding areas.
This allows the NeRF to learn a more generalized and artifact-free representation of the scene.
Our method allows to enhance the quality of noisy input labels by exploiting the multi-view consistency offered by modern
NeRF
models.
To support future research in this area, we provide a dataset featuring manually generated labels for four multi-view scenes.
Additionally, we release the code for this work, including scripts for model training and evaluation.

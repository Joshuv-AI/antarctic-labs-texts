Chung’s LIL for the linear stochastic fractional heat equation at origin
Chang Liu, Ran Wang

Abstract




Abstract:

Consider the linear stochastic fractional heat equation with vanishing initial condition:

\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t,x)+\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},

where
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
denotes the fractional Laplacian with power
\alpha\in(1,2]
, and the driving noise
\dot{W}
is
a centered Gaussian field which is white in time and has the covariance of a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in\left(\frac{2-\alpha}{2},1\right)
. We establish Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm for the solution at
t=0
.

Keyword:
Stochastic fractional heat equation; Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm; Small ball probability.

MSC:
60H15; 60G17; 60G22.

1.
Introduction

Consider the following linear stochastic fractional heat equation (SFHE, for short):

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t,x)+\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)\equiv 0.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here,
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
denotes the fractional Laplacian with
\alpha\in(1,2]
,

W(t,x)
is a centered Gaussian field with the covariance given by

{\mathbb{E}}[W(t,x)W(s,y)]=\frac{1}{2}\left(s\wedge t\right)\left(|x|^{2H}+|y|^{2H}-|x-y|^{2H}\right),

(1.2)

with

\displaystyle\frac{2-\alpha}{2}<H<1.

(1.3)

That is,
W
is a random fields, which is a standard Brownian motion in time and a fractional Brownian motion
(fBm, for short) with Hurst parameter
H
in space.

According to
[
2
, Theorem 5.3, Remark 5.4]
,
the equation (
1.1
) has a random field solution if and only if (
1.3
) holds; See Section 2 for more precise information. In the latter case, the solution
\{u(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
can be written as

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-s,x-y)W(ds,dy),\ \ \text{a.s.}

(1.4)

where
G(t,x)
is the Green kernel associated with the operator
-(-\triangle)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
is given by

(\mathcal{F}G(t,\cdot))(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}},\ \ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \ t>0.

(1.5)

Using the idea of the pinned string process from Mueller and Tribe
[
17
]
, it can be shown that the solution to the linear stochastic (fractional) heat equation can be decomposed into the sum of two Gaussian random fields: one possessing stationary increments and the other exhibiting smooth sample paths. See
[
5
,
6
,
13
,
8
,
20
,
23
]
, as well as the monographs
[
4
,
9
,
19
]
. For example, when
\dot{W}
is a space-time white noise (i.e.,
H=1/2
), Theorem 3.3 in
[
9
]
tells us that for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the stochastic process
t\mapsto u(t,x)
can be represented as the sum of a fBm with Hurst parameter
1/4
and a stochastic process with
C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
-continuous trajectories. Hence, locally
t\mapsto u(t,x)
behaves as a fBm with Hurst parameter
1/4
, and it has the same regularity (such as the Hölder continuity, the local moduli of continuities, Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm) as a fBm with Hurst parameter
1/4
.

For the solution of the equation (
1.1
), according to
[
5
, Section 3.2]
, we know that for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a Gaussian process
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, which is independent of
\{u(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0}
and has a version that is infinitely differentiable on
(0,\infty)
, such that

\displaystyle F:=\kappa^{-1}\left\{u(t,x)+T(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}

(1.6)

is a fBm with Hurst parameter
\theta
, defined by

\theta:=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1-H}{\alpha}.

(1.7)

Here, the constant
\kappa
is given by

\kappa:=\left(\frac{H\Gamma(2H)\Gamma(1-2\theta)\sin(\pi H)}{\pi\alpha\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.8)

The decomposition of (
1.6
), together with Chung’s LIL of the fBm (e.g.,
[
14
, Section]
,
[
18
]
),
implies that for every fixed
t\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{h\in[0,\varepsilon]}\frac{|u(t+h,x)-u(t,x)|}{{\varepsilon}^{\theta}(\log\log{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{-\theta}}=\kappa\lambda^{\theta},\ \ \ a.s.,

(1.9)

where
\lambda
is the small ball constant of a fractional Brownian motion with index
\theta
(e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 6.9]
).

However, the argument in the proof of Chung’s LIL of
u(t,x)
at
t>0
does not work in the case of
t=0
and the problem on Chung’s LIL for
u(t,x)
at
t=0
was left open in
[
5
]
. This effort is complicated by the fact that, near
t=0
, the random process
T
is not smooth; in fact,
T
and
F
are equally smooth locally near
t=0
. When
\alpha=2
and
H=\frac{1}{2}
, Khoshnevisan, Kim, and Mueller
[
10
]
recently proved that the small-ball probability of the rougher process
F
to dominate that of the smoother Gaussian process
T
, and they adopted a localization idea of Lee and Xiao
[
12
]
to establish Chung’s LIL of
u(t,x)
at
t=0
.

The objective of this paper is to establish Chung’s LIL at
t=0
for the solution
u(t,x)
of the linear stochastic fractional heat equation (
1.1
), for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
, with parameters
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
H\in(1-\alpha/2,1)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,\varepsilon]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{{\varepsilon}^{\theta}(\log\log{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{-\theta}}=\kappa\lambda^{\theta},\ \ \ a.s.,

(1.10)

where
\lambda
is the small ball constant of a fractional Brownian motion with index
\theta
.

The lower bound in (
1.10
) is derived from a small ball probability result (Lemma
2.1
), following the classical method of Monrad and Rootzén
[
16
]
and Talagrand
[
18
]
. The upper bound, however, requires a more intricate analysis. To establish this upper bound, we adapt the localization approach introduced by Lee and Xiao
[
12
]
and Khoshnevisan et al.
[
10
]
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we recall some properties of the solution, and Borell’s inequality and Dudley’s metric entropy theorem.
In Section 3, we prove Theorem
1.1
by analyzing the qualities of the approximation errors.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Properties of the solution

Let us recall some notations from
[
7
]
.
Let
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
be the space of real-valued infinitely differentiable functions with compact support on
\mathbb{R}
.
The Fourier transform of a function
f\in\mathcal{D}
is defined as

\mathcal{F}f(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}f(x)dx.

Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
denote the space of real-valued infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
and let
W

be a zero-mean Gaussian family
\{W(\phi),\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})\}
, whose covariance structure is given by

\mathbb{E}\big[W(\phi)W(\psi)\big]=C_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\phi(s,\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(s,\xi)}\cdot|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds,

(2.1)

where
H\in(0,1)
,
C_{H}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Gamma(2H+1)\sin(\pi H)

and
\mathcal{F}\phi(s,\xi)
is the Fourier transform of
\phi(s,x)
with respect to the spatial variable
x
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
with the inner product

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=\mathbb{E}\big[W(\phi)W(\psi)\big].

The mapping
\varphi\mapsto W(\varphi)
can be extended to all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.

According to Balan
[
2
, Remark 5.4]
and
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
, we have the following results about the solution to Equation (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
\frac{2-\alpha}{2}<H<1
. Then, the equation (
1.1
) admits a unique solution
\{u(t,x);(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\}
. Moreover, the following items hold.

(a)

For any
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sqrt{c_{2,1}}t^{\theta},

(2.2)

where
c_{2,1}=\frac{C_{H}}{2H+\alpha-2}2^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\Gamma\left(\frac{2-2H}{\alpha}\right)
.

(b)

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
c_{2,2}>0
such that
for any
t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\|u(t,x)-u(s,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c_{2,2}|t-s|^{\theta}.

(2.3)

(c)

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the process
\{u(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Gaussian self-similar process with index
\theta
given by (
1.7
).

Proof.

The results in (a) and (b) are taken from
[
15
, Proposition 2.2, Lemma 2.1]
. Now, we give the proof for (c).
For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\rho>0
and
0<s<t
, by (
2.1
) and the change of variables, we have

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\left[\rho^{-\theta}u\left(\rho t,x\right)\rho^{-\theta}u\left(\rho s,x\right)\right]\\
=&\,C_{H}\rho^{-2\theta}\int_{0}^{\rho s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}G\left(\rho t-r,x-\cdot\right)(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}G\left(\rho s-r,x-\cdot\right)(\xi)}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi dr\\
=&\,C_{H}\rho^{-2\theta}\int_{0}^{\rho s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(\rho t+\rho s-2r)|\xi|^{\alpha}}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi dr\\
=&\,C_{H}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t+s-2r)|\xi|^{\alpha}}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi dr\\
=&\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[u(t,x)u(s,x)\right].\end{split}

(2.4)

The proof is complete.
∎

Let us recall a small ball estimate result for the process
\{u(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0}
for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
5
, Theorem 4.3]
For every fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right)=-\kappa^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\lambda,

(2.5)

where
\lambda

.

2.2.
Borell’s inequality and Dudley’s metric entropy theorem

For a given Gaussian process
\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}}
, let
d_{X}
be the canonical metric defined by

d_{X}(s,t):=\|X(t)-X(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(\mathbb{E}[|X(t)-X(s)|^{2}]\right)^{1/2},\quad s,t\in\mathbb{T}.

Let
N(\mathbb{T},d_{X};\varepsilon)
denote the smallest number of open
d_{X}
-balls of radius
\varepsilon
required to cover
\mathbb{T}
. The
d_{X}
-diameter of
\mathbb{T}
is defined as

D_{X}(\mathbb{T}):=\sup\left\{d_{X}(s,t):s,t\in\mathbb{T}\right\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Borell’s inequality,
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
)

Let
\{X_{t},t\in\mathbb{T}\}
be a centered separable Gaussian process on some index set

\mathbb{T}

with almost surely bounded sample paths. Then
\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}X_{t}\right]<\infty
, and for all
\lambda>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}X_{t}-\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}X_{t}\right]\right|>\lambda\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\sigma_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\right),

(2.6)

where
\sigma_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{t}^{2}\right]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Dudley’s metric entropy theorem,
[
11
, Theorem 6.1]
)

There exists a positive constant
c_{2,3}
such that for all Gaussian processes
\left\{X(t)\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}X(t)\right]\leq c_{2,3}\int_{0}^{D_{X}(\mathbb{T})}\sqrt{\log N\left(\mathbb{T},d_{X};\varepsilon\right)}d\varepsilon.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(1)
Lower bound:
In this step, we prove the lower bound:

\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,\varepsilon]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{{\varepsilon}^{\theta}(\log\log{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{-\theta}}\geq\kappa\lambda^{\theta},\ \ \text{a.s.}

(3.1)

For any
c>0
and integer
n\geq 1
, let
r_{n}:=e^{-cn}
. For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\delta<\lambda
, define

A_{n}:=\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,r_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq\kappa\left(\frac{\delta r_{n}}{\log\log(1/r_{n})}\right)^{\theta}\right\}.

By the self-similarity of
\{u(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0}
and (
2.5
), there exists a constant

N_{0}>0
such that for any
n\geq N_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(A_{n}\right)\leq(nc)^{-\frac{\lambda+\delta}{2\delta}}.

Consequently,

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\left(A_{n}\right)<\infty.

Using the Borel-Cantelli lemma and the arbitrariness of
\delta\in(0,\lambda)
, we have

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,r_{n}]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{{r_{n}^{\theta}(\log\log r_{n}^{-1})^{-\theta}}}\geq\kappa\lambda^{\theta},\ \ \text{a.s.}

This, together with a monotonicity argument, implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{{r^{\theta}(\log\log r^{-1})^{-\theta}}}\geq\,\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,r_{n+1}]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{{r_{n}^{\theta}(\log\log r_{n}^{-1})^{-\theta}}}\geq\kappa e^{-c}\lambda^{\theta},\ \ \text{a.s.}\end{split}

Using the arbitrariness of
c>0
, we obtain the lower bound (
3.1
).

(2)
Upper bound
. In this step, we prove the upper bound:

\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{{r^{\theta}(\log\log r^{-1})^{-\theta}}}\leq\kappa\lambda^{\theta},\ \ \text{a.s.}

(3.2)

Here, we adopt a localization idea of Lee and Xiao
[
12
]
and Khoshnevisan et al.
[
10
]
.

Define a family of Gaussian random fields
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by setting

u_{n}(t,x):=\int_{[t_{n+1},t)\times\mathbb{R}}G(t-s,x-y)\,W(ds,dy),\quad(t,x)\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]\times\mathbb{R},

(3.3)

where

t_{n}:=\exp\left(-n^{1+\beta}\right),\ \ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

(3.4)

with
\beta
being a constant whose value will be chosen later.

For simplicity, define
\psi:(0,1/e)\to(0,\infty)
by

\psi(t):=\left(\frac{t}{\log\log(1/t)}\right)^{\theta}.

(3.5)

Notice that

\begin{split}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n}]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{\psi(t_{n})}\leq&\,\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{\psi(t_{n})}\\
&\,\,+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}\frac{|u(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x)|}{\psi(t_{n})}\\
&\,\,+\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}\frac{|u_{n}(t,x)|}{\psi(t_{n})}.\end{split}

(3.6)

By Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
below, and the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we have

\begin{split}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}\frac{|u(t,x)|}{\psi(t_{n})}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}\frac{|u(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x)|}{\psi(t_{n})}=0,\ \ \text{ a.s.}\end{split}

(3.7)

We now turn to the third term on the right-hand side of inequality (
3.6
). Applying Lemma
3.4
below with
\gamma:=\kappa(1+2\beta)^{\theta}\lambda^{\theta}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u_{n}(t,x)|\leq\kappa(1+2\beta)^{\theta}\lambda^{\theta}\psi(t_{n})\right)=-1+\frac{\beta}{1+2\beta}.

Consequently,

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u_{n}(t,x)|\leq\kappa(1+2\beta)^{\theta}\lambda^{\theta}\psi(t_{n})\right)=\infty.

(3.8)

The independence of the families
\{u_{n}(t,x):t_{n+1}\leq t\leq t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, together with (
3.8
) and the Borel-Cantelli lemma, yields that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}\frac{|u_{n}(t,x)|}{\psi(t_{n})}\leq\kappa(1+2\beta)^{\theta}\lambda^{\theta},\ \ \text{a.s.}

(3.9)

Combining inequalities (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and (
3.9
), and passing to the limit as
\beta\downarrow 0
, we obtain (
3.2
). The proof is complete.
∎

3.2.
Some important lemmas

In this part, we establish some lemmas used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Recall the sequence
t_{n}
defined by (
3.4
). Using the mean-value theorem, we have that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{t_{n+1}}{t_{n}}\leq\exp\left(-(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right).

(3.10)

Lemma 3.1
.

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
\delta>0
, there exist
c_{3,1}>0
and
N_{1}(\delta)>0
, depending on
\delta
, such that for all
n\geq N_{1}(\delta)

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}|u(t,x)|\geq\delta\psi(t_{n})\right)\leq c_{3,1}\exp\left(-\frac{\exp\left(2\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right)}{c_{3,1}\left(\log n\right)^{2\theta}}\right).

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
s_{i}=i\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{2,2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}
.
By (
2.3
),

d_{u}(s_{i-1},s_{i})\leq\varepsilon.

Consequently, the covering number
N([0,t_{n+1}],d_{u};\varepsilon)
satisfies that

N([0,t_{n+1}],d_{u};\varepsilon)\leq 2t_{n+1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{2,2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}.

(3.11)

Using (
2.3
) again, the diameter of
[0,t_{n+1}]
under the canonical metric of
\{u(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0}
is

\begin{split}D_{u}([0,t_{n+1}])\leq c_{2,2}t_{n+1}^{\theta}.\end{split}

(3.12)

By (
3.11
), (
3.12
), Lemma
2.3
, and the change of variables, there exists
c_{3,2}>0
such that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}|u(t,x)|\right]\leq&\,c_{2,3}\int_{0}^{D_{u}([0,t_{n+1}])}\sqrt{\log N\left([0,t_{n+1}],d_{u};\varepsilon\right)}d\varepsilon\\
\leq&\,c_{2,3}\int\limits_{0}^{c_{2,2}t_{n+1}^{\theta}}\sqrt{\log\left(2t_{n+1}(c_{2,2})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)}d\varepsilon\\
=&\,c_{2,3}c_{2,2}\theta 2^{\theta}t_{n+1}^{\theta}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{\theta-1}\sqrt{-\log\omega}d\omega\\
\leq&\,c_{3,2}t_{n+1}^{\theta}.\end{split}

(3.13)

Here, the following inequality is used in the last step: For any
q<1
and
0<\eta<1
, there exists an explicit constant
c
depending on
q,\eta
such that for any
0<A<\eta<1
,

\int_{0}^{A}\omega^{-q}\sqrt{-\log\omega}d\omega\leq cA^{1-q}\sqrt{-\log A},

(3.14)

see
[
3
, Lemma B.3]
)

For every
\delta>0
, by (
3.5
), (
3.10
), and (
3.13
), there exists
N_{1}(\delta)>0
, depending on
\delta
, such that for all
n\geq N_{1}(\delta)
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}|u(t,x)|\right]\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\psi(t_{n}).

(3.15)

By Lemma
2.2
, (
2.2
), (
3.15
), and (
3.10
),
there exists
c_{3,3}>0
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}(\delta)
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}|u(t,x)|\geq\delta\psi(t_{n})\right)\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}|u(t,x)|-\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}|u(t,x)|\right]\geq\frac{\delta\psi(t_{n})}{2}\right)\\
\leq&\,2\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{8c_{2,1}(\log\log(1/t_{n}))^{2\theta}}\left(\frac{t_{n}}{t_{n+1}}\right)^{2\theta}\right)\\
\leq&\,2\exp\left(-\frac{\exp\left(2\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right)}{\left(8c_{2,1}(1+\beta)^{2\theta}/\delta^{2}\right)(\log n)^{2\theta}}\right)\\
\leq&\,c_{3,3}\exp\left(-\frac{\exp\left(2\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right)}{c_{3,3}\left(\log n\right)^{2\theta}}\right).\end{split}

(3.16)

The proof is complete.
∎

Recall the definition of
u_{n}
in (
3.3
). Next, we establish a sharp bound on the approximation error

Lemma 3.2
.

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
\delta>0
, there exist
c_{3,4}>0
and
N_{2}(\delta)>0
, depending on
\delta
, such that for all
n\geq N_{2}(\delta)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x)|\geq\delta\psi(t_{n})\right)\leq c_{3,4}\exp\left(-\frac{\exp\left(2\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right)}{c_{3,4}\left(\log n\right)^{2\theta}}\right).

(3.17)

Proof.

The proof of (
3.17
) is similar to that of Lemma
3.1
, using Dudley’s metric entropy theorem and Borell’s inequality. For brevity, denote

Y_{n}(t,x):=u(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x).

(3.18)

Let us give the upper bound for the covering number
N\left([t_{n+1},t_{n}],d_{Y_{n}};\varepsilon\right)
and the diameter
D_{Y_{n}}([t_{n+1},t_{n}])
under the canonical metric of
\{Y_{n}(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0}
, respectively.

For any
t_{n}\geq t>s\geq t_{n+1}
, since
Y_{n}(t,x)-Y_{n}(s,x)
is independent of
u_{n}(t,x)-u_{n}(s,x)
, by (
2.3
) and (
3.18
), we have

\|Y_{n}(t,x)-Y_{n}(s,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\,c_{2,2}|t-s|^{\theta}.

(3.19)

Similarly to the proof (
3.11
), the covering number of
N\left([t_{n+1},t_{n}],d_{Y_{n}};\varepsilon\right)
satisfies
that

N\left([t_{n+1},t_{n}],d_{Y_{n}};\varepsilon\right)\leq c_{3,5}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}.

(3.20)

Using (
2.1
) and the change of variables

\eta=2(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}
, we have

\begin{split}\|Y_{n}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=&\,2C_{H}\int_{0}^{t_{n+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}}\xi^{1-2H}d\xi ds\\
=&\,\frac{C_{H}}{\alpha}2^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\eta}\eta^{\frac{2-2H}{\alpha}-1}d\eta\int_{0}^{t_{n+1}}(t-s)^{\frac{2H-2}{\alpha}}ds\\
=&\,c_{2,1}\left(t^{2\theta}-(t-t_{n+1})^{2\theta}\right)\\
\leq&\,c_{2,1}t_{n+1}^{2\theta}.\end{split}

(3.21)

where the elementary
a^{2\theta}-b^{2\theta}\leq(a-b)^{2\theta}
is used for any
a>b>0
and
\theta\in(0,1/2)
.

By (
3.10
) and (
3.21
), we have that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

D_{Y_{n}}([t_{n+1},t_{n}])\leq\,2c_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}t_{n}^{\theta}\exp\left(-\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right).

(3.22)

Using (
3.20
), (
3.22
), and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|Y_{n}(t,x)|\right]\leq&\,c_{2,3}\int_{0}^{D_{Y_{n}}([t_{n+1},t_{n}])}\sqrt{\log N\left([t_{n+1},t_{n}],d_{Y_{n}};\varepsilon\right)}d\varepsilon\\
\leq&\,c_{2,3}\int\limits_{0}^{2c_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}t_{n}^{\theta}\exp\left(-\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right)}\sqrt{\log\left(c_{3,5}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)}d\varepsilon\\
\leq&\,c_{2,3}\theta\left(c_{3,5}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})\right)^{\theta}\int_{0}^{A_{n}}\omega^{\theta-1}\sqrt{-\log\omega}d\omega,\end{split}

(3.23)

where the change of variables
\omega:=c_{3,5}^{-1}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})^{-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{\theta}}
is used, and

A_{n}:=c_{3,5}^{-1}(4c_{2,1})^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}(t_{n}-t_{n+1})^{-1}t_{n}\exp\left(-(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right).

By (
3.10
) and (
3.14
), there exist
c_{3,6}>0
and
N_{3}>0
such that for all
n\geq N_{3}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|Y_{n}(t,x)|\right]\leq&\,c_{3,6}t_{n}^{\theta}\exp\left(-\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right)n^{(1+\beta)/2}.\end{split}

(3.24)

Similarly as the proof of (
3.16
), using Borell’s inequality and (
3.24
), there exist
c_{3,7}>0
and
N_{4}(\delta)
, depending on
\delta
, such that for all

n\geq N_{4}(\delta)
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|Y_{n}(t,x)|\geq\delta\psi(t_{n})\right)\leq c_{3,7}\exp\left(-\frac{\exp\left(2\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right)}{c_{3,7}\left(\log n\right)^{2\theta}}\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we derive a small-ball estimate for
u
on
[t_{n+1},t_{n}]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)=-\lambda\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}(1+\beta).

(3.25)

Proof.

(i) Lower bound.

By (
2.5
) and (
3.5
), we have that for every
\gamma>0
,

\begin{split}&\,\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)\\
\geq&\,\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)\\
=&\,\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq\gamma(\log\log(1/t_{n}))^{-\theta}\right\}\\
=&\,-\lambda\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}(1+\beta).\end{split}

(3.26)

(ii) Upper bound.

For any
\delta\in(0,\gamma)
, by Lemma
3.1
, we have that

\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)\\
\leq&\,\max\Bigg\{\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq(\gamma+\delta)\psi(t_{n})\right),\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\ \mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,t_{n+1}]}|u(t,x)|\geq\delta\psi(t_{n})\right)\Bigg\}\\
=&\,\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)|\leq(\gamma+\delta)(\log\log(1/t_{n}))^{-\theta}\right)\\
=&\,-\lambda\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma+\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}(1+\beta).\end{split}

(3.27)

Putting (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) together, and letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, we get (
3.25
). The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we derive a small-ball estimate for
u_{n}
on
[t_{n+1},t_{n}]
, which plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
\gamma>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u_{n}(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)=-\lambda\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}(1+\beta).

(3.28)

Proof.

(i) Upper bound.

For any
\delta\in(0,\gamma)
, by Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, we have that

\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u_{n}(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)\\
\leq&\,\max\Bigg\{\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq(\gamma+\delta)\psi(t_{n})\right),\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\ \mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x)|\geq\delta\psi(t_{n})\right)\Bigg\}\\
=&\,-\lambda\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma+\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}(1+\beta).\end{split}

(3.29)

(ii) Lower bound.
For any
\delta\in(0,\gamma)
, by Lemma
3.2
, we have that for all
n\geq N_{2}(\delta)
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq(\gamma-\delta)\psi(t_{n})\right)\\
\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u_{n}(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x)|\geq\delta\psi(t_{n})\right)\\
\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u_{n}(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)+c_{3,4}\exp\left(-\frac{\exp\left(2\theta(1+\beta)n^{\beta}\right)}{c_{3,4}\left(\log n\right)^{2\theta}}\right).\end{split}

This, together with Lemma
3.3
, implies that

\begin{split}&\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u_{n}(t,x)|\leq\gamma\psi(t_{n})\right)\\
\geq&\,\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{n+1},t_{n}]}|u(t,x)|\leq(\gamma-\delta)\psi(t_{n})\right)\\
=&\,-\lambda\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma-\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}(1+\beta).\end{split}

(3.30)

Putting (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) together, and letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, we obtain (
3.28
). The proof is complete.
∎

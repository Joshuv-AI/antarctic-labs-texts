Hyperfine and Zeeman Optical Pumping and Transverse Laser Cooling of a Thermal Atomic Beam of Dysprosium Using a Single 421 nm Laser Preprint: APS/123-QED
Rohan Chakravarthy, Jonathan Agil, Arijit Sharma, Jung Bog Kim, Dmitry Budker

Abstract
We demonstrate the effect of Zeeman and hyperfine optical pumping and transverse laser cooling of a dysprosium (Dy) atomic beam on the 4 ​ f 10 ​ 6 ​ s 2 ​ ( J = 8 ) → 4 ​ f 10 ​ 6 ​ s ​ 6 ​ p ​ ( J = 9 ) 4f^{10}6s^{2}(J=8)\rightarrow 4f^{10}6s6p(J=9) transition at 421.291 nm. For 163 Dy, an electro-optic modulator is used to generate five frequency sidebands required to pump the atoms to the F = 10.5 F=10.5 ground state hyperfine level and the light polarization is chosen to pump the atoms to the m F = 10.5 m_{F}=10.5 Zeeman sublevel. The atoms are simultaneously laser-cooled using a standing wave orthogonal to the atomic beam. The resulting polarized and cooled atomic beam will be used in fundamental physics experiments taking advantage of the “accidental” degeneracy of excited states in Dy including the ongoing measurement of parity violation in this system.



I
Introduction

Dysprosium (Dy) is an atom of choice in a wide variety of experiments due to its large ground-state magnetic moment (the largest of all atoms), seven stable isotopes with mass numbers ranging from
A=156
to 164, and the existence of a pair of metastable excited states of the same angular momentum and opposite parity that are nearly degenerate, so that complete degeneracy can be achieved via Zeeman tuning in a magnetic field as low as 1.4 G
Budker1994_ExpInvDy
. These nearly-degenerate levels were used in searches for temporal variation of the fine-structure constant
\alpha

Cingoez2007_alpha_var
;
Leefer2013_alpha_var
and its dependence on the gravitational potential
Ferrel2007_gravitational_dependence_alpha
, tests of local Lorentz invariance and Einstein equivalence principle
Hohensee2013_violations_lorentz_symmetry
, and a search for parity non-conservation
Nguyen1997_PNC_Dy
(so far focused on
163
Dy). It has also been used in searches for apparent oscillation of
\alpha
induced by ultralight scalar dark matter
Tilburg2015_ULDM
;
Xue2023_UDarkMatter
.

Dysprosium atoms can be laser cooled
Muehlbauer2018_optimization_cooling_Dy
;
Leefer2010_Transverse_cooling
;
Peterson2019_sawtooth_slowing_Dy
, trapped
Youn2010_Dy_MOT
;
Bloch2023_Dy_tweezer_array
and cooled to quantum degeneracy
Lu2011_BEC_Dy
;
Lu2012_fermi_gas
, and has become a favored system for the study of quantum magnetism
Klaus2022_vortices_in_condensate
.

Much of this work requires knowledge of the spectra of dysprosium, which was studied both theoretically
Childs1970_ground_state_moments
;
Dzuba2010_states_Dy
and experimentally
Studer2018_1001nmDy
;
Budker1991_Spectroscopy_Dy
;
Schmitt2013_narrow_line_transition_Dy
in the past few decades. Controlling the internal states of Dy is important for this work, including the ability to efficiently optically pump the atoms to a particular Zeeman sublevel and to a particular hyperfine level in the case of the isotopes with nonzero nuclear spin.

In this work, we use a weakly collimated atomic beam with characteristic transverse velocities of
\simeq 20
m/s. A collimated narrow-band continuous wave laser only interacts with a small fraction of these atoms. Efficient population transfer of the atomic beam to an excited state requires either broadening the laser linewidth, matching the divergence of the atomic beam and the laser beam
Nguyen2000_Dy_population_transfer
, or reducing the transverse velocities of the atomic beam by laser cooling. Laser cooling the atomic beam relies on the optical molasses technique, where a standing wave laser beam orthogonal to the atomic beam is used to reduce the transverse Doppler width.

In this paper, we demonstrate such efficient optical pumping and laser cooling using a single laser beam and a custom electro-optic modulator. This is an important step in improving the signal-to-noise ratio in the ongoing search for parity non-conservation in Dy
Leefer2014_towards_PNC
.

IV
Conclusions and Outlook

Efficient simultaneous optical pumping and laser cooling of atomic dysprosium was achieved with the use of an EOM and a single laser beam. The amplification of the amplitude of the
F=10.5\rightarrow F^{\prime}=9.5
peak of
163
Dy in the probe signal after optical pumping is a clear indication that atoms in the hyperfine states were optically pumped into the
F=10.5
hyperfine state and the reduced width of the probe spectrum is a clear indicator of laser cooling. Furthermore, a comparison of experimentally obtained amplification in the amplitude of probe spectra with theoretical estimates indicates optimal hyperfine and Zeeman optical pumping with the optical pumping efficiency being constrained by the modulation efficiency of a broadband EOM. The experimentally obtained laser linewidths indicate that the atomic beam has been cooled to the Doppler limit.

The optically pumped dysprosium atoms will be used in the search for parity violation in dysprosium. The previous search for parity violation (PV)
Nguyen1997_PNC_Dy
, the most sensitive parity-violation search so far in terms of the weak-mixing amplitude, has reached into the expected range of the effect but yielded a result consistent with zero. The boost in sensitivity by a factor of
70
afforded by the results of the present work may allow the detection of the long-sought PV signals in Dy.

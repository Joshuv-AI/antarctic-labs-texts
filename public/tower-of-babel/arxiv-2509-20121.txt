Abstract
. Let 𝐀 \mathbf{A} be a finite non-abelian simple Mal’cev algebra, such as for example a finite simple non-abelian group or a finite simple non-zero ring. We show that the automorphism group of a filtered Boolean power of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} by the countable atomless Boolean algebra 𝐁 \mathbf{B} has ample generics. This uses the decomposition of that automorphism group as a semidirect product of a certain closure of a Boolean power of the automorphism group of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} by 𝐁 \mathbf{B} and the stabiliser of finitely many points in the homeomorphism group Homeo ​ 2 ω \mathrm{Homeo}\hskip 1.00006pt2^{\omega} of the Cantor space 2 ω 2^{\omega} from [ 22 ] . As an intermediate step, we show that pointwise stabilisers in Homeo ​ 2 ω \mathrm{Homeo}\hskip 1.00006pt2^{\omega} have ample generics, which extends the result of Kwiatkowska that Homeo ​ 2 ω \mathrm{Homeo}\hskip 1.00006pt2^{\omega} has ample generics [ 18 ] .

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra and

\mathbf{B}
the countable atomless Boolean algebra.
For
n\geq 0
,
let
\mathbf{e}:=(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
be a (possibly empty) tuple of idempotents of
\mathbf{A}

and
\mathbf{x}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
a tuple of distinct points in the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
.

Then the automorphism group of the filtered Boolean power
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{e}}
has ample generics.

Recall that
2^{\omega}
is the Stone space of the countable atomless Boolean algebra.

Theorem
1.1
applies in particular for
\mathbf{A}
a finite simple non-abelian group,
a finite simple ring, a finite simple associative or Lie algebra, a finite simple non-abelian loop, etc
(see Section
1.3
below).
Its significance is that it establishes a whole host of algebraic structures whose automorphism groups
have ample generics.
To the best of our knowledge, this result is new even for classical structures like groups or rings.

Theorem
1.1
will be proved in Section
5
.
The basic idea is that by
[
22
]
the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 1.00006pt(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{e}}
splits into a semidirect product
with complement
H
isomorphic to a stabiliser of finitely many points in the group of homeomorphisms of
2^{\omega}
.
By Theorem
1.2
below the group
H
has ample generics, which can be shown to be ample generics in

\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 1.00006pt(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{e}}
as well.

We now review the necessary notions and context for our theorem.

Notation

Throughout the paper we write

•

\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
,

•

.

1.2.
Ample generics

Let
G
be a topological group.
We denote the conjugation action of
G
on itself by
h^{g}:=g^{-1}hg
for
g,h\in G
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
this action naturally extends to the
diagonal conjugation action
of
G
on
G^{n}
by

\mathbf{h}^{g}:=(h_{1}^{g},\dots,h_{n}^{g})
where
\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})\in G^{n}
.
We say that
\mathbf{h}\in G^{n}
is a
generic

n
-tuple
if its orbit
\mathbf{h}^{G}
under this action is comeagre in
G^{n}
,
i.e. if it contains the intersection of countably many dense open subsets of
G^{n}
.
The group
G
is said to have
ample generics
if it has generic
n
-tuples for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Having ample generics is a strong condition connecting algebraic and topological features of
G
.
It implies several other properties (see Section
1.4
below) and has thus been an object of study over
the years.
It was introduced by Kechris and Rosendal
[
16
]
and many prominent permutation groups
have been shown to have this property:

•

the symmetric group of countably infinite degree by Kechris and Rosendal
[
16
]
,

•

the automorphism group of the random graph by Hrushovski
[
14
]
,

•

the group of isometries of the rational Urysohn space by Solecki
[
24
]
,

•

the group of homeomorphisms
\mathrm{Homeo}\hskip 1.00006pt2^{\omega}
of the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
by Kwiatkowska
[
18
]
.

Hodkinson showed that the automorphism group of
(\mathbb{Q},<)
does not have ample generics
[
17
]
.

It is worth noting that the groups that have thus far been shown to have ample generics tend to be automorphism groups of countable homogenous relational structures, homeomorphism groups of topological spaces or isometry groups of metric spaces. There is a notable absence of examples of automorphism groups of algebraic structures. One exception is the above-mentioned result of Kwiatkowska
[
18
]
,
because the homeomorphism group of Cantor space can be viewed as the automorphism group of the countable atomless Boolean algebra.
As a crucial first step towards our Theorem
1.1
, we will generalize her result as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

The pointwise stabiliser of finitely (possibly zero) many points in the homeomorphism group of
2^{\omega}
has ample generics.

Note that the isomorphism type of the stabiliser above depends only on the number of fixed points
and not on their specific choice.

For zero fixed points we recover Kwiatkowska’s result for
\mathrm{Homeo}\hskip 1.00006pt2^{\omega}

[
18
]
.
Our proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section
3
broadly follows Kwiatkowska’s and also
makes use of some of her findings along the way.

1.3.
Mal’cev algebras and Boolean powers

We refer to
[
6
,
21
]
for background on general algebra.
Let
\sigma
be a signature consisting of function symbols
f_{j}
with arity
n_{j}\geq 0
for
j\in J
.
An
algebra

\mathbf{A}=(A;(f_{j}^{\mathbf{A}})_{j\in J})
of signature
\sigma
is a set
A
(the universe) together with functions

f_{j}^{\mathbf{A}}\colon A^{n_{j}}\to A
for all
j\in J
.
Throughout, we will use bold letters to denote algebras and matching standard letters for the underlying universes.

An algebra with more than one element is
simple
if its only congruences are the total relation and the equality.
This is consistent with the classical notion for groups and rings.

An algebra
\mathbf{A}
is
Mal’cev
if it has a ternary term operation
m
satisfying
m(x,x,y)=y=m(y,x,x)
for all

x,y\in A
.
Note that every group
(G;\cdot,^{-1},1)
has a Mal’cev term
m(x,y,z)=xy^{-1}z
.
Similarly quasigroups, loops, rings as well as their expansions with additional operations are all Mal’cev.

The generalization of commutator theory from groups to algebras as described in
[
11
]
allows us to talk
about (non-)abelian algebras. A Mal’cev algebra is
abelian
if all its operations are affine functions over
some module; otherwise we call it
non-abelian
. Clearly every module is abelian.
Abelianness as above is consistent with the classical notion for groups.
A loop is abelian if and only if it is an abelian group.
A ring is abelian if and only if its multiplication is constant
0
(see
[
11
]
).

We refer to
[
12
]
for basics on Fraïssé theory.
As we discuss in
[
22
]
, for
\mathbf{A}
a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra, the set of finite direct
powers
K:=\{\mathbf{A}^{n}\>:\>n\in\mathbb{N}\}

has the Joint Embedding Property and the Amalgamation Property but in general not the Hereditary Property.
Hence
K
has a (generalized) Fraïssé limit.
In
[
22
]
this Fraïssé limit
is explicitly described as a filtered Boolean power of
\mathbf{A}
,
a construction introduced by Arens and Kaplansky
[
2
]
(see also
[
9
]
).
Over time Boolean powers have proved a useful tool for representing algebras and transferring properties of Boolean
algebras to arbitrary structures
[
5
,
10
]
.
In groups they were studied for their interesting combinatorial properties
[
1
,
3
]
.
We briefly review this construction.

Let
\mathbf{B}
be a Boolean algebra. The
Stone space
of
\mathbf{B}
is the set
X
of ultrafilters on
\mathbf{B}

with the topology whose basic open sets are
\{x\in X\>:\>b\in x\}
for
b\in B
.
The original Boolean algebra
\mathbf{B}
is isomorphic to the Boolean algebra of clopen sets of
X
.
For basics on Boolean algebras we refer the reader to
[
23
, Chapter 1]
and for Stone duality to
[
23
, Chapter 3]
.

Recall that there is a unique (up to isomorphism) countable atomless Boolean algebra.
It is in fact the Fraïssé limit of all finite Boolean algebras and will play a particularly important role in
this paper.
Its Stone space is the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
.

Let
\mathbf{B}
be a Boolean algebra with Stone space
X
, and let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite algebra.
The
Boolean power

\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}}
is the subalgebra of the direct power
\mathbf{A}^{X}
with universe

A^{\mathbf{B}}:=\{f\colon X\to A\>:\>f\text{ is continuous}\},

where
A
is endowed with the discrete topology.
Continuity simply means that for every
a\in A
the set
f^{-1}(a)
is clopen in
X
.

Borrowing the concept from semigroups, we call an element
e
of an algebra
\mathbf{A}
an
idempotent

if
\{e\}
forms a subalgebra of
\mathbf{A}
.
In a multiplicative group the only idempotent is
1
; in a ring the only idempotent is
0
.

For a (possibly empty) tuple of idempotents
\mathbf{e}:=(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
of
\mathbf{A}
,
and a tuple of distinct points
\mathbf{x}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
in
2^{\omega}
,
the subalgebra
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{e}}
of
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}}
with universe

\{f\in A^{\mathbf{B}}\colon f(x_{1})=e_{1},\dots,f(x_{n})=e_{n}\}

is a
filtered Boolean power
of
\mathbf{A}
.

1.4.
Consequences of ample generics

In
[
16
]
Kechris and Rosendal proved that if
G
is a Polish group (i.e. if it is separable and completely metrisable)
with ample generics, then
G
also has the following (see
[
20
]
for background):

•

small index property
, i.e. every subgroup of
G
of index less than
2^{\aleph_{0}}
is
open
[
16
, Theorem 1.6]
,

•

automatic continuity
, i.e. every homomorphism
\pi\colon G\to H
where
H
is
a separable topological group is continuous
[
16
, Theorem 6.24]
,

•

a unique Polish group topology
[
16
, Corollary 6.25]
.

Moreover, if
G
is the automorphism group of an
\omega
-categorical structure and has ample generics,
then
G
has uncountable strong cofinality.
The
strong cofinality
of
G
is the smallest cardinality
\kappa
such that
G
is the union
of a chain
(U_{i})_{i<\kappa}
of proper subsets of
G
such that for all
i<j
we have
U_{i}\subseteq U_{j}
,

U_{i}=U_{i}^{-1}
and
U_{i}U_{i}\subseteq U_{k}
for some
k<\kappa
.

By
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
a group
G
has uncountable strong cofinality if and only if
G
has

•

uncountable cofinality
,
i.e.
G
is not the union of a countable chain of proper subgroups, and

•

the
Bergman property
, i.e. for every generating set
X
of
G
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such
that every element of
G
is a product of
\leq n
elements of
X
and their inverses.

In
[
22
, Theorem 1.3(1)]
we proved that the filtered Boolean power
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{e}}
is
\omega
-categorical.
Therefore Theorem
1.1
implies the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

With the notation and assumptions of Theorem
1.1
,
the automorphism group of the filtered Boolean power
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{e}}
has the small index property,
automatic continuity, unique Polish group topology,
uncountable (strong) cofinality and the Bergman property.

Note that points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in 2^{\omega}
can be identified with maximal ideals (or ultrafilters) of the countable
atomless Boolean algebra
\mathbf{B}
. Then the augmented Boolean algebra
(\mathbf{B};x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is a structure
with operations from the Boolean algebra and unary relations that are ideals
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}

(see
[
19
, p. 137]
).
As explained in the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 1.3(1)]
it follows that
(\mathbf{B};x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is

\omega
-categorical by
[
19
, Theorem 7]
.
Since
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 1.00006pt(\mathbf{B};x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is isomorphic to
(\mathrm{Homeo}\hskip 1.00006pt2^{\omega})_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})}
,
Theorem
1.2
implies the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

The pointwise stabiliser of finitely (possibly zero) many points in the homeomorphism group of
2^{\omega}

has the small index property,
automatic continuity, unique Polish group topology,
uncountable (strong) cofinality and the Bergman property.

In Corollaries
1.3
,
1.4
, automatic continuity as defined above is a
new consequence of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, respectively.
The other properties listed in these corollaries have already been established by different means in
[
22
]
.

6.
Conclusion

For
\mathbf{A}
a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra and
\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})

a tuple comprising all the idempotents of
\mathbf{A}
, we saw in Section
1.3
that
the filtered Boolean power
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{e}}
occurs naturally as the (generalized) Fraïssé limit of

K:=\{\mathbf{A}^{n}\>:\>n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

One may ask what the situation is for
\mathbf{A}
abelian, i.e.,
\mathbf{A}
polynomially equivalent to a module.
It is not hard to see that when
\mathbf{A}
actually
is
a finite simple module,
the set
K
of finite direct powers of
\mathbf{A}
still has the joint embedding property and amalgamation.
In this case, the (generalized) Fraïssé limit
of
K
is the direct sum
\mathbf{A}^{(\mathbb{N})}
of countably many copies of
\mathbf{A}
.
Note that
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 1.00006pt\mathbf{A}^{(\mathbb{N})}
is isomorphic to the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}\hskip 1.00006pt\mathbf{V}
of the vector space
\mathbf{V}

of countable dimension over some finite field.

It is known that
\mathrm{GL}\hskip 1.00006pt\mathbf{V}
satisfies most of the consequences of ample generics that we discuss in
Section
1.4
:

•

small index property (and hence a unique Polish topology) by Evans
[
8
]
,

•

uncountable cofinality since
\mathbf{V}
is
\omega
-stable by
[
13
, Theorem 6.1]
,

•

Bergman property by Tolstykh
[
25
]
,

•

uncountably strong cofinality from the previous two items.

Still to the best of our knowledge it is open whether
\mathrm{GL}\hskip 1.00006pt\mathbf{V}
has ample generics.

For
\mathbf{A}
a finite simple non-abelian group, let
\mathbf{F}
be the free group of countable rank in the
variety generated by
\mathbf{A}
. As observed in
[
3
, p. 367-8]
and
[
4
, p. 201]
,

\mathbf{F}
has a normal subgroup

N
isomorphic to the filtered Boolean power
(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x}_{1}
with quotient
\mathbf{F}/N
the free algebra
of countable rank in the variety generated by all proper subgroups of
\mathbf{A}
.
Bryant and Evans
[
3
]
showed that
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 1.00006pt(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x}_{1}
has the small index property.
Their question whether the same is true for the free group
\mathbf{F}
remains open.
The same question is also of interest for
\mathbf{A}
an arbitrary finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra
and
\mathbf{F}
the free algebra of countable rank in the variety
\mathbf{A}
generates.
Specifically, does
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 1.00006pt\mathbf{F}
have the small index property and ample generics?

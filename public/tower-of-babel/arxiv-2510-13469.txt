Matrices over maximal orders in cyclic division algebras over
ℚ

Abstract




It is known that every
n\times n
matrix over the maximal order in an algebraic number field is a sum of
k
-th powers in various cases if a discriminant condition is satisfied. It has been proved by Wadikar and Katre that for every matrix of size
\geq 2
over maximal orders in rational quaternion division algebras is a sum of squares and cubes. In this paper we consider cyclic division algebras over
\mathbb{Q}
of odd prime degree and show that under some conditions every matrix of size
\geq 2
over these noncommutative rings is a sum of squares and a sum of cubes.

Keywords:
Matrices; noncommutative rings; trace; sums of powers; Waring’s problem; cyclic division algebras

Primary 11P05; 15A30; Secondary 16S50.

in some order.

So we have,

(u^{i}\cdot\eta)^{n}\equiv u^{in}\cdot\eta^{S^{i(n-1)}}\cdot\eta^{S^{i(n-2)}}\penalty\ \cdots\penalty\ \eta^{S^{2i}}\cdot\eta^{S^{i}}\cdot\eta

\equiv u^{in}\cdot\eta\cdot\eta^{S}\cdots\eta^{S^{n-1}}\pmod{2}

Matrices over cyclic division algebras as sums of cubes

Notation
:
T_{3,n}=T_{3}
be the set of those elements of the cyclic algebra

which can be written as sums of traces of cubes of
n\times n

Theorem 5:
Let
A

n^{2},\penalty\ n

(\sigma,Z,S)
as in Theorem 1 and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal order associated with this canonical generation satisfying following conditions

(1)

3\nmid\sigma
and

(2)

3\nmid N_{\mathbb{Q}(\eta)/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta).

Then every matrix

is a sum of cubes of matrices in
M_{n}(\mathfrak{m})

Proof:

\mathfrak{m}
is a free

n^{2}

\mathfrak{m}

\eta,\penalty\ \eta^{S},\penalty\ \ldots,\penalty\ \eta^{S^{n-1}},\penalty\ y\cdot\eta,\penalty\ y\cdot n^{S},\penalty\ \ldots,\penalty\ y\cdot\eta^{S^{n-1}},\penalty\ y^{2}\cdot\eta,\penalty\ y^{2}\cdot\eta^{S},\penalty\ \ldots,\newline

T_{3}
.

Now
\eta=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{h-1}\xi^{g^{nr}},\xi^{p}=1
, so
\xi^{p+1}=\xi
. First we prove
\eta\in T_{3}

3x\equiv(p+1)\pmod{p}.

\xi^{p+1}=\xi^{3x}

Thus
\eta\in T_{3}
. Since
\eta,\penalty\ \eta^{S},\penalty\ \eta^{S^{2}},\penalty\ \ldots,\penalty\ \eta^{S^{n-1}}

\eta,\penalty\ \eta^{S},\penalty\ \eta^{S^{2}},\ldots,\penalty\ \eta^{S^{n-1}}\in T_{3}
.

Next to prove
y\in T_{3}

py=(\lambda-u)\alpha
and
p

p>3
,
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
or
p\equiv-1\pmod{3}

y=(\lambda-u)\alpha=(\lambda-u)(a_{1}\eta+a_{2}\eta^{S}+a_{3}\eta^{S^{2}}+\cdots+a_{n}\eta^{S^{n-1}})

-y=(\lambda-u)\alpha=(\lambda-u)(a_{1}\eta+a_{2}\eta^{S}+a_{3}\eta^{S^{2}}+\cdots+a_{n}\eta^{S^{n-1}})

u\in T_{3}
.

Next to prove
y\in T_{3}
.

py=(\lambda-u)\alpha

p\equiv 1\pmod{3}

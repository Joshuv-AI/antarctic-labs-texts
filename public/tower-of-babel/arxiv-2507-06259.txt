Curvature inequalities for anti-invariant submersion from quaternionic space forms
Kirti Gupta

Abstract




Abstract:
This paper focuses on deriving several curvature inequalities involving the Ricci and scalar curvatures of the horizontal and vertical distributions in anti-invariant Riemannian submersions from quaternionic space forms onto Riemannian manifolds. In addition, a Ricci curvature inequality for anti-invariant Riemannian submersions is established. The equality cases for all derived inequalities are also examined.

Keywords:
Quaternionic
Kähler manifold; quaternionic space form; anti-invariant submersion.

MSC 2020:
53C12, 53C15, 53C26, 53C55.

1
Introduction

In 1993, B.-Y. Chen established foundational relationships between the extrinsic and intrinsic invariants of submanifolds in real space forms
[
7
]
. Later, in 1999, he derived a sharp inequality connecting the Ricci curvature and the squared mean curvature for submanifolds
[
8
]
. These findings have since inspired a broad spectrum of research, leading to the development of numerous results in various ambient geometries. For a comprehensive study, see
[
9
]
. Several authors
[
3
,
4
,
15
,
19
,
20
]
have further explored Chen-like inequalities.

As noted in
[
11
]
, a central theme in Riemannian geometry is the comparison of geometric properties of different types of maps between Riemannian manifolds. In this context, the concept of Riemannian submersion, introduced by B. O’Neill in 1966
[
16
]
, plays a pivotal role. O’Neill formulated key equations governing Riemannian submersions, which have found applications in diverse fields such as supergravity, statistical machine learning, analysis on manifolds, and image processing. The geometric features of Riemannian submersions have been further investigated by Besse
[
6
]
, Escobales Jr.
[
10
]
, and many others.

In a related development, Watson
[
21
]
introduced and studied almost Hermitian submersions between almost complex manifolds, showing that both the horizontal and vertical distributions remain invariant under the almost complex structure of the total space. Later, in 2010, Şahin
[
18
]
explored anti-invariant Riemannian submersions from almost Hermitian manifolds to Riemannian manifolds, demonstrating that the fibers are anti-invariant under the almost complex structure. Consequently, the horizontal distribution is not preserved by this structure.

In this manuscript, we investigate inequalities involving the Ricci and scalar curvatures on the vertical and horizontal distributions of anti-invariant Riemannian submersions from quaternionic space forms. Section 2 presents the foundational geometric properties of Riemannian submersions and quaternionic Kähler manifolds. In Section 3, we derive new inequalities for the Ricci and scalar curvatures associated with the vertical and horizontal distributions, and we analyze the equality cases in detail.

Let

\pi:M(c)\rightarrow N

\displaystyle\widehat{R}(U,V,F,W)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{4}\Bigg\{g_{M}(V,F)g_{M}(U,W)-g_{M}(U,F)g_{M}(V,W)

(3.1)

\displaystyle+\Bigg[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\Big(g_{M}(J_{\alpha}V,F)g_{M}(J_{\alpha}U,W)-g_{M}(J_{\alpha}U,F)g_{M}(J_{\alpha}V,W)

\displaystyle+2g_{M}(J_{\alpha}V,U)g_{M}(J_{\alpha}F,W)\Big)\Bigg]\Bigg\}-g_{M}({\mathcal{T}}_{U}W,{\mathcal{T}}_{V}F)+g_{M}({\mathcal{T}}_{V}W,{\mathcal{T}}_{U}F),

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{4}\Bigg\{g_{M}(Y,Z)g_{M}(X,H)-g_{M}(X,Z)g_{M}(Y,H)

\displaystyle+\Bigg[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\Big(g_{M}(J_{\alpha}Y,Z)g_{M}(J_{\alpha}X,H)-g_{M}(J_{\alpha}X,Z)g_{M}(J_{\alpha}Y,H)

Theorem 3.1

Let
\pi:M(c)\rightarrow N
be an anti-invariant submersion from a quaternionic space form
(M(c),g_{M})
onto
a Riemannian manifold
(N,g_{N}).
Then

\widehat{Ric}(U)\geq\frac{c}{4}(r-1)-rg_{M}\left({\mathcal{T}}_{U}U,H\right),

(3.3)

where
U
is a unit vertical vector of
M(c)

The equality case of
(
3.3
)
holds if and only if each fiber is totally geodesic.

Proof:
Let
\{U_{1},U_{2}\ldots,U_{r}\}
be a local orthonormal frame of the vertical distribution
\operatorname{ker}\pi_{\ast}
and
U
a vertical vector.
Since

\widehat{Ric}(U)\equiv\widehat{Ric}(U,U)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\hat{R}(U,U_{i},U_{i},U).

Using equation (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\widehat{Ric}(U)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Bigg\{g_{M}(U_{i},U_{i})g_{M}(U,U)-g_{M}(U,U_{i})g_{M}(U_{i},U)

\displaystyle+\Bigg[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\Big(g_{M}(J_{\alpha}U_{i},U_{i})g_{M}(J_{\alpha}U,U)-g_{M}(J_{\alpha}U,U_{i})g_{M}(J_{\alpha}U_{i},U)

\displaystyle+2g_{M}(J_{\alpha}U_{i},U)g_{M}(J_{\alpha}U_{i},U)\Big)\Bigg]\Bigg\}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{M}({\mathcal{T}}_{U}U,{\mathcal{T}}_{U_{i}}U_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{M}({\mathcal{T}}_{U_{i}}U,{\mathcal{T}}_{U}U_{i}).

Since

Now, we have

\widehat{Ric}(U)=\frac{c}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(1-g_{M}(U,U_{i})^{2}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{M}({\mathcal{T}}_{U}U,{\mathcal{T}}_{U_{i}}U_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{M}({\mathcal{T}}_{U_{i}}U,{\mathcal{T}}_{U}U_{i}).

(3.4)

\widehat{Ric}(U)\geq\frac{c}{4}(r-1)-rg_{M}\left({\mathcal{T}}_{U}U,H\right).

By using (
3.4

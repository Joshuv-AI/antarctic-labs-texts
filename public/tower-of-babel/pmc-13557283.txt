Factors Influencing the Treatment Response of Tetracyclines for Papulopustular Rash Induced by Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor-Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Tae Min Kim, Namkyu Kim, Jimin Woo, Bhumsuk Keam, Ji Su Lee, Seong Jin Jo
Published: 2026

Introduction

Epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) are standard therapy for non–small cell lung cancer and other malignancies. Papulopustular rash (PPR) is one of the most common cutaneous adverse events, impairing quality of life and sometimes interrupting EGFR-TKI treatment [1]. Oral tetracyclines are widely used as first-line therapy for PPR, with proven efficacy by previous studies. However, little is known about factors influencing tetracycline response or differences in effectiveness among different tetracyclines [2].

Findings

We retrospectively reviewed 307 patients with EGFRTKI– induced PPR. Severity was assessed by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 or, if unavailable, by dermatologists reviewing clinical photographs. Treatment response was evaluated monthly for three months and categorized by tetracycline dose adjustment: excellent (discontinuation due to remission), good (dose reduction), fair (no change), or poor (dose escalation or switch). Differences in responses were analyzed with generalized estimating equations and ordinal logistic regression, adjusting for sex, age, EGFR-TKI generation, and baseline PPR severity.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were similar between the first- and second-generation EGFR-TKI groups, with no significant difference in age, sex distribution, tumor stage, initial tetracycline prescription, or baseline rash severity (Table 1). All patients received the same initial dose of minocycline (50 mg twice daily) or doxycycline (100 mg once daily).

Treatment responses to tetracyclines were most evident within the first 4–8 weeks. Patients receiving first-generation EGFR-TKIs were significantly more likely to achieve excellent or good responses at week 4 (adjusted OR [aOR]: 7.41; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 2.50–21.9; P<0.001) and week 8 (Aor: 2.66; 95% CI: 1.22–5.78; P=0.016) compared with those on second-generation EGFR-TKIs (Figure 1). By week 12, this difference was no longer significant.

Treatment responses of EGFR-TKI-induced papulopustular rash to oral tetracyclines according to EGFR-TKI generation. Bar graphs show the distribution of excellent (white), good (striped), fair (gray), and poor (black) responses at 4 weeks (left), 8 weeks (middle), and 12 weeks (right) after tetracycline treatment. Patients receiving first-generation EGFR-TKIs had significantly higher excellent/good responses compared to those on second-generation EGFR-TKIs at both 4 weeks (P<0.001) and at 8 weeks (P=0.016), while no significant difference was observed at 12 weeks (P=0.074).

When comparing tetracycline types, both minocycline and doxycycline were effective, but minocycline yielded a superior response at week 4 (Aor: 1.98; 95% CI: 1.18–3.32; P=0.012), with no significant differences at weeks 8 or 12 (Figure 2). Combination therapy with topical agents provided additional benefit, showing better responses than oral tetracycline alone at week 4 (aOR: 1.82; 95% CI: 1.05–3.16; P=0.018) and week 12 (aOR: 1.96; 95% CI: 1.14–3.40; P=0.016).

Treatment responses of EGFR-TKI-induced papulopustular rash to different oral tetracyclines. Bar graphs show treatment responses at 4 weeks (right), 8 weeks (middle) and 12 weeks (right) for patients receiving minocycline or doxycycline. Minocycline was associated with significantly higher excellent/good responses at both 4 weeks (P=0.012), whereas differences at 8 weeks (P=0.269) and 12 weeks (P=0.752) were not statistically significant.

Conclusions

Oral tetracyclines were effective for EGFR-TKI–induced PPR, with the most notable treatment responses observed within 4–8 weeks, particularly in patients receiving first-generation EGFR-TKIs compared to those on second-generation agents, likely reflecting differences in binding affinity [3]. Minocycline demonstrated an earlier treatment response than doxycycline, which may be related to its superior tissue distribution and enhanced inhibition of MMP-9 [4,5,6]. In addition, combination therapy with oral tetracyclines and topical agents provided greater efficacy than tetracycline monotherapy, suggesting a potential synergistic effect.

This study has limitations, including its retrospective, single-center design, reliance on dose adjustment as a proxy for clinical response, and the relatively small number of patients receiving second-generation EGFR-TKIs. Despite these limitations, our findings underscore the importance of identifying factors that influence tetracycline response, which may help optimize management of EGFR-TKI–induced PPR, support treatment continuation, and improve patient quality of life.

Channels of Energy for the Linearized Energy Critical Wave Equation in Even Dimensions N ≥ 8 N\geq 8
Andres A. Contreras Hip

Abstract
We prove an exterior energy estimate for the linearized energy critical wave equation around a multisoliton for even dimensions N ≥ 8 . N\geq 8. This extends the result in [ 1 ] to higher dimensions. During the proof we encounter various additional important technical difficulties compared to lower dimensions. In particular, we need to deal with a number of generalized eigenfunctions of the static operator which increases linearly in N . N. This makes the analysis of projections onto these eigenfunctions a higher dimensional problem, which requires linear systems to control. This is a crucial ingredient in our upcoming work where we give an alternative proof of the soliton resolution for the wave maps equation based on the method of channels of energy developed by Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle.

Introduction

We consider radial solutions to the linearized energy critical wave equation around the ground state in even dimensions
N,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{matrix}\partial_{tt}u_{L}-\Delta u_{L}+Vu_{L}=0\\
u_{L}|_{t=0}=(u_{0},u_{1})\end{matrix}}}\right.

(1.1)

with initial conditions
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
where
V
is the potential given by

V=-\frac{N+2}{N-2}W^{\frac{4}{N-2}},

with

W=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{N(N-2)}}}\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}.

This is the linearized equation for

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u=|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u

around the stationary solutions
\pm W.
By standard arguments, (
1.1
) is well posed in
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
and moreover the outer radiated energy

E_{\mathrm{out}}:=E_{\mathrm{out}}^{-}+E_{\mathrm{out}}^{+},\;\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\int_{|x|>|t|}|\nabla_{t,x}u_{L}(t,x)|^{2}dx

is well defined for
t\to\pm\infty.

For the free wave equation,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{matrix}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{F}-\Delta u_{F}=0\\
(u_{F},\partial_{t}u_{F})|_{t=0}=(u_{0},u_{1})\end{matrix}}}\right.

one has the channels of energy estimate

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|(u_{0},u_{1})}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}}

(1.2)

if
N
is odd. However, in the case that
N
is even the full estimate fails (
[
4
]
), instead we have

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|u_{0}}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}}

if
N\equiv 0\mod 4,
and

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|u_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}}

if
N\equiv 2\mod 4.
Extensions to domains of the form
\{|x|\geq R+|t|\}
(obtained in
[
5
,
10
,
12
]
) will also be used in the present work. Let
\Lambda=\frac{N-2}{2}+x\cdot\nabla
denote the scaling generator. For the equation (
1.1
), there are two natural counterexamples to the analogue of (
1.2
), namely
\Lambda W,

t\Lambda W.
For odd dimensions
N,
the analogue of (
1.2
) is

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}}\mbox{ if }N\geq 3\mbox{ is odd}

where the projections
\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp},\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}
are defined by

\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}=\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}(\mathrm{Span}(\Lambda W))^{\perp},\;\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}=\Pi_{L^{2}}(\mathrm{Span}(\Lambda W))^{\perp}.

(1.3)

Here, if
H
is any Hilbert space and
E
any closed subspace, we let
\Pi_{H}(E)
denote the orthogonal projection onto
E
in
H
and we let
\Pi_{H}(E)^{\perp}=1-\Pi_{H}(E).

In this paper, we will restrict to even dimensions
N.
We will need to define logarithmic weakenings of
L^{2}.
Let
Z_{\alpha}
be the space defined by the norm

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|f}}\right\|_{Z_{\alpha}}:=\sup_{R>0}\frac{R^{-\frac{N}{2}-\alpha}}{\langle\log R\rangle}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{R<|x|<2R}f^{2}(x)dx}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We will also need the norm

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|f}}\right\|_{Z_{\alpha,\lambda}}=\sup_{R>0}\frac{R^{-3-\alpha}}{\inf_{1\leq j\leq J}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\frac{R}{\lambda_{j}}}}\right\rangle}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 2R}f^{2}dx}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots\lambda_{J})
is such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{J}.

We have the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a constant
C
such that for any radial solution
u_{L}
of (
1.1
) we have

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{1}}}\right\|_{Z_{-3}}\leq C\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}},

if
N\equiv 0\mod 4,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{Z_{-3}}\leq C\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}},

if
N\equiv 2\mod 4.

We will also consider the linearized energy critical wave equation around a multisoliton. To define this, let
J\in\mathbb{N}
and let

\Lambda_{J}:=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{J})\in(0,\infty)^{J},\lambda_{J}<\lambda_{J-1}<\cdots<\lambda_{1}\}.

Let

V_{\lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}V_{\{\lambda_{j}\}},

where
f_{\{\lambda\}}
denotes the scaling

f_{\{\lambda\}}(x):=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}f\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}}}\right).

We define the scalings

f_{(\lambda)}
as the
\dot{H}^{1}
critical scaling

f_{(\lambda)}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}f\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}}}\right).

We define the scalings

f_{(\lambda)}
as the
L^{2}
critical scaling

f_{[\lambda]}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}}f\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}}}\right).

We will also consider the equation

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{matrix}\partial_{tt}u_{L}-\Delta u_{L}+V_{\lambda}u_{L}=0,\\
u_{L}|_{t=0}=(u_{0},u_{1}),\end{matrix}}}\right.

(1.4)

which is the energy critical wave equation linearized around a multisoliton.

Let
\gamma(\lambda)
be defined by

\gamma(\lambda)=\sup_{1\leq j\leq J-1}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\lambda_{j+1}^{2}}.

We have the following statement for the multisoliton case.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a dimensional constant
C
such that for any radial solution
u_{L}
of (
1.4
) we have

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{1}}}\right\|_{Z_{-3,\lambda}}\leq C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}}+\gamma(\lambda)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|(u_{0},u_{1})}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}}}\right),

if
N\equiv 0\mod 4,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{Z_{-3,\lambda}}\leq C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}}+\gamma(\lambda)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|(u_{0},u_{1})}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}}}\right),

if
N\equiv 2\mod 4.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
were proven in
[
2
]
for the case of dimensions
N=6,8.
However, in higher dimensions, substantial additional work is needed. In the one soliton case, to bound
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
if
N\equiv 0\mod 4
(respectively
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}
if

1.1
is trivial (see Lemma
4.5
). Additionally, in the multisoliton case (Theorem
1.2
), one proceeds by induction on the number of solitons. An additional argument is used in the analogous contradiction argument to control the orthogonal projection onto the span of the generalized eigenfunctions of
-\Delta+V
corresponding to
\Lambda W.

Theorem

will be used in
[
3
]
to provide an alternative proof to that of Jendrej and Lawrie in
[
8
]
of the soliton resolution for radial equivariant wave maps on

in even dimensions.

More general nonlinearities

Our main result also holds for other similar equations. Let us consider the equation

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+\varphi(r,u)=0

(2.1)

for
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

t\in\mathbb{R}.
Suppose that
\varphi
is of the form

\varphi(r,u)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}r^{(k-1)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{N}{2}-1}}\right)-2}u^{k},

(2.2)

where

\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau^{k}\varphi_{k}=0

for any
\tau>0.
We will make some additional assumptions:

(A1)

If
u
is a solution to (
2.1
), then so is
u_{\lambda}(r,t)=\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u(\lambda r,\lambda t).

(A2)

All static radial solutions
U(r)
of (
2.1
) verify

\partial_{r}^{\alpha}U(r)\sim r^{-(N-2)-\alpha}

as
r\to\infty
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}.

One can then consider the equation (
2.1
) linearized around
U(r),

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+\partial_{2}\varphi(r,U)u=0,

(2.3)

where
\partial_{2}
denotes the derivative with respect to the second variable of
\varphi
and where
U
is any static solution to (
2.1
).

We have the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\varphi
is a function satisfying assumptions (A1) and (A2) and is of the form (
2.2
). Then for any radial solution
u
of (
2.3
), we have the channels of energy estimate

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{L_{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{1}}}\right\|_{Z_{-3}}\leq C\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}},

if
N\equiv 0\mod 4,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{1}}}\right\|_{L_{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{Z_{-3}}\leq C\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}},

if
N\equiv 2\mod 4.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is the same as that of Theorem
1.1
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
only uses the asymptotics of
U.

Similarly, we have the follwoing analogue in the case of a multisoliton.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
\varphi
is a function satisfying assumptions (A1) and (A2) and is of the form (
2.2
). Also, suppose that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{J})
is such that
\gamma(\lambda)\leq\gamma_{0}
for some small enough
\gamma_{0}.
Then there exists a constant

such that for any radial solution
u
of

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{matrix}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+\partial_{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varphi(r,U_{\lambda_{1}})+\cdots+\varphi(r,U_{\lambda_{J}})}}\right)u=0\\

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{L_{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{1}}}\right\|_{Z_{-3,\lambda}}\leq C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}}+\gamma(\lambda)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|(u_{0},u_{1})}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}}}\right),

if
N\equiv 0\mod 4,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Pi_{L^{2}}^{\perp}u_{1}}}\right\|_{L_{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\Pi_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\perp}u_{0}}}\right\|_{Z_{-3,\lambda}}\leq C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{out}}}+\gamma(\lambda)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|(u_{0},u_{1})}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}}}\right),

are not vacuous, consider

-equivariant wave maps from Minkowski space into the sphere. Suppose that

solves

\partial_{t}^{2}\psi-\partial_{r}^{2}\psi-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\psi+k^{2}\frac{\sin(2\psi)}{2r^{2}}=0.

Single soliton case (non resonant component)

We recall that

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a solution
\Gamma

where the Wronskian is given by

\Gamma(r)\partial_{r}(\Lambda W)(r)-\Lambda W(r)\partial_{r}\Gamma(r)=r^{-(N-1)}

We let

\Gamma(r)=-\Lambda W(r)\int_{1}^{r}s^{-(N-1)}(\Lambda W)^{-2}(s)ds.

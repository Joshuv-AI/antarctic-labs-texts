Abstract

1.
Introduction

By a
Fano variety
we mean a normal, projective variety
X
,
defined over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
zero such that
X
admits an ample anticanonical divisor.
We contribute to the classification of singular Fano
threefolds
X
endowed with an effective algebraic torus
action
\mathbb{T}\times X\to X
.
Recall that for
\dim(\mathbb{T})=3
, that is the
toric case
,
there is the complete classification
[
8
]

in the case of at most canonical singularities.
Moreover, for
\dim(\mathbb{T})=2
we have complete classifications
in Picard number one for the cases of at most terminal
singularities
[
3
]
and for the case of

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Gorenstein log terminal threefolds
[
2
]
.

We focus on
\dim(\mathbb{T})=1
, that means
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{K}^{*}
. Our
approach uses the
maximal orbit quotient

from
[
5
]
.
Consider the invariant open subset
X_{0}\subset X

consisting of all points
x\in X
with finite isotropy
group
\mathbb{T}_{x}
.
The inclusion of fields
\mathbb{K}(X)^{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathbb{K}(X)

gives rise to a rational map

\pi\colon X_{0}\dashrightarrow Y

which is a surjective morphism up to
codimension two w.r.t.
X_{0}
and
Y
.
We further focus on the case
Y=\mathbb{P}_{2}
. We fix a divisor

C=C_{0}+C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}+\dots+C_{n},

where
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}\subset\mathbb{P}_{2}
are lines in general position and
C_{3},\dots,C_{n}\subset\mathbb{P}_{2}
are irreducible curves of degrees
d_{3},\dots,d_{n}
, such that all critical values of
\pi
lie in the support of
C
and
C
is minimal with these properties. Furthermore, we have

D_{i}=\pi^{*}(C_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}l_{ij}D_{ij},

with
D_{ij}\subset X
prime divisors and
l_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
The situation with all
C_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}_{2}

being lines has been studied by Hausen, Hische
and Wrobel who provided explicit
classifications
[
5
,
7
,
9
]
,
even for higher dimensional analoga.
In the present article, we settle the case
of non-toric,
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, Gorenstein Fano threefolds of Picard
number one with an effective
\mathbb{K}^{*}
-action and

Y=\mathbb{P}_{2}
.
Proposition
2.21
shows that we have to deal
with the following three types:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\text{A:}&\pi^{*}(C_{i})=l_{i1}D_{i1},\ i=0,\ldots,n,&X^{\mathbb{K}^{*}}\text{ hosts exactly one prime divisor},\\[8.0pt]
\text{B:}&\pi^{*}(C_{0})=l_{01}D_{01}+l_{02}D_{02},&\pi^{*}(C_{i})=l_{i1}D_{i1},i=1,\ldots,n,\\[2.0pt]
&\codim_{X}(X^{\mathbb{T}})\geq 2,\\[8.0pt]
\text{C:}&d_{3}\geq 2,\quad\pi^{*}(C_{3})=l_{31}D_{31}+l_{02}D_{32},&\pi^{*}(C_{i})=l_{i1}D_{i1},i=0,1,2,4,\ldots,n,\\[2.0pt]
&\codim_{X}(X^{\mathbb{T}})\geq 2.\end{array}

Recall that, for a normal variety

\mathcal{R}(X)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{[D]\in\Cl(X)}\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}(D)).

When

.

We continue Example

2.
Projective threefolds with a
\mathbb{K}^{*}
-action

We provide a method to explicitely construct all
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, projective threefolds of Picard number one with maximal orbit quotient
\mathbb{P}_{2}
, see Construction
2.12

. We assume basic knowledge of toric geometry, as presented in
[
4
]
. We also recall the notion of fake weighted projective space:

Reminder 2.1
.

A
fake weighted projective space
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, projective toric variety
Z
of Picard number one. Every fake weighted projective space arises from a
generator matrix
, that means an
n\times(n+1)
matrix

P:=[v_{0},\dots,v_{n}],

such that
v_{0},\dots,v_{n}
are pairwise distinct primitive vectors generating
\mathbb{Q}^{n}
as a convex cone. Concretely, there is a unique fan
\Sigma(P)
having the columns of
P
as its primitive ray generators, and the associated toric variety
Z(P)
is a fake weighted projective space.

Construction 2.2
.

The input data are a degree vector and a family of pairwise distinct, irreducible, homogeneous polynomials:

d=(d_{3},\dots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n-2},\hskip 4.0ptg=(g_{3},\dots,g_{n}),\hskip 4.0ptg_{i}\in\mathbb{K}[T_{0},T_{1},T_{2}],\hskip 4.0pt\deg{g_{i}}=d_{i}.

With this data we associate an embedding of the projective plane

\mathbb{P}_{2}\cong Y(d,g)=V(T_{3}+g_{3},\dots,T_{n}+g_{n})\subset\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,d),

Moreover, for the ambient weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,d)
we provide the generator matrix

B=[u_{0},\dots,u_{n}]=\begin{bmatrix}-1&1&0&0&\dots&0\\
-1&0&1&0&\dots&0\\
-d_{3}&0&0&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-d_{n}&0&0&0&\dots&1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

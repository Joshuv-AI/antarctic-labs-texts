Deep learning enables accurate diagnosis of acute cholecystitis and prediction of suppuration using noncontrast CT
Authors: Bai-Qing Chen, Wei Zang, Jia-Xu Liu, Yue Yang, Xing-Long Zhang, Rong-Hui Ju
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryTo develop a deep learning model using abdominal noncontrast computed tomography (CT) for diagnosing acute cholecystitis (AC) and predicting progression to acute suppurative cholecystitis (ASC). A total of 641 patients from three medical centers were retrospectively analyzed. Deep learning models were constructed for AC diagnosis and ASC prediction. Model interpretability was assessed using gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding. Performance was compared with radiomics models and radiologist assessments. The deep learning model achieved accuracies of 89.81% (internal) and 81.83% (external) for AC and 84.52% (internal) and 85.60% (external) for ASC prediction. Grad-CAM visualizations showed focus on gallbladder regions and surrounding areas. The multimodal models integrating clinical information outperformed imaging-only models. Deep learning significantly surpassed radiomics models and radiologist assessments, with high computational efficiency (segmentation: 10.5 ± 3.8 s, inference: 1.3 ± 0.4 s). This efficient deep learning system accurately identifies AC and ASC from noncontrast CT, offering robust tools for time-sensitive clinical workflows.Subject areas: Bioinformatics, Human metabolism, Medical imaging

Introduction

Acute cholecystitis (AC) is one of the most common causes of acute abdomen in clinical practice.1 Without timely treatment, the condition may worsen over time due to escalating local and systemic inflammation, ultimately progressing to acute suppurative cholecystitis (ASC).2 The presence of pus or purulent bile in the gallbladder and pericholecystic abscesses are direct manifestations of this advanced stage. During this phase, laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) becomes significantly more challenging, with higher rates of conversion to open surgery and increased 30-day mortality.3,4,5 Therefore, early identification of AC and prediction of its inflammatory progression are of great significance for achieving early intervention and improving patient outcomes. In the diagnostic work-up of AC, computed tomography (CT) is often the first imaging modality employed.6,7

Recent meta-analyses have confirmed the diagnostic value of CT in AC. A systematic review of head-to-head studies comparing the diagnostic performance of CT and ultrasound found that CT demonstrated comparable sensitivity (83.9%) and specificity (94.0%) to ultrasound (79.0% and 93.6%, respectively; p = 0.85).8 CT’s superior spatial resolution provides clearer visualization of gallbladder wall thickening, pericholecystic fluid, and complications like perforation or abscess—key for assessing severity. However, among the five head-to-head studies included in the meta-analysis, three defined AC based on contrast-enhanced CT features, including gallbladder wall or mucosal enhancement.6,7,9 This means that the conclusions are mainly based on enhanced CT data. However, in clinical practice, due to radiation concerns, contrast contraindications, and emergency resource limitations,10 noncontrast CT remains the first-line choice for acute abdominal pain.11,12 Improving the diagnostic performance of noncontrast CT can significantly reduce clinicians’ reliance on signs and medical history. This is particularly important for elderly patients who have a high incidence of cholecystitis and are often less sensitive to signs, have difficulty describing their medical history, and face challenges in diagnosis.10 On noncontrast CT, gallstones and gallbladder wall thickening serve as significant risk factors for ASC,13 yet their diagnostic efficacy remains suboptimal. And direct manifestations such as intragallbladder pus and pericholecystic abscesses lack imaging specificity.14 Currently, the diagnostic accuracy of noncontrast CT alone is insufficient to meet the clinical demands for precise diagnosis of ASC.

Therefore, there is an urgent need in emergency settings to improve the diagnostic performance of noncontrast CT for identifying AC and assessing its severity. Enhancing this capability not only maximizes the clinical utility of ionizing radiation when it is unavoidable15 but also optimizes the management of elderly patients with AC.16 In the field of medical imaging, deep learning has emerged as a powerful tool for accurate and timely disease diagnosis.17,18,19,20 With access to reliable datasets, these algorithms can learn complex patterns and predict clinical outcomes in new cases. Deep learning has shown remarkable potential in medical imaging by detecting subtle pathological changes.21,22 A recent study used a deep learning model to analyze abdominal ultrasound images and successfully identified typical features of AC.23 These findings suggest a promising role for deep learning in enhancing the diagnostic utility of noncontrast CT for AC and its progression.

In this study, we propose a deep learning-based diagnostic system for AC using noncontrast CT images. The system is capable of detecting AC in its early stages and, furthermore, predicting whether a patient will progress to ASC. We also apply model interpretability techniques to visualize the areas of interest, demonstrating the robustness and reliability of the model’s predictions.

Center 1 comprised 150 patients with AC and 122 patients without AC; center 2 included 153 patients with AC and 96 patients without AC; center 3, serving as an independent external test cohort, consisted of 75 patients with AC and 45 patients without AC. Among all patients with AC, 187 cases (52.1%) were pathologically confirmed as patients with ASC.

Diagnostic performance for AC

Performance of machine learning models in diagnosing AC

The segmentation results of non-enhanced CT are shown in Figure 2. The experimental results are shown in Figure S1A in the supplemental information, where six machine learning models—support vector machine (SVM), linear discriminant analysis (LDA), random forest (RF), logistic regression (LR), decision tree (DT), and naive Bayes (NB)—were evaluated on the external test set (center 3). Specifically, the SVM and LR models demonstrated superior precision and specificity (precision: 0.9219 and 0.95; specificity: 0.8889 and 0.9333, respectively), but their recall rates were relatively low (0.7867 and 0.76), indicating a certain risk of missed positive cases. The LDA and RF models achieved a better balance between precision and recall (precision: 0.8857 and 0.863; recall: 0.8267 and 0.84, respectively), showing more stable overall performance. In contrast, although the DT and NB models exhibited higher recall rates (0.8533 and 0.8133, respectively), their AUCs (0.7933 and 0.8569) and specificities (0.7333 and 0.8444) were relatively lower. Overall, traditional machine learning models demonstrated a certain level of feasibility in identifying AC, but their overall performance remained limited by the representational capacity of the extracted features and the heterogeneity of the sample distribution.

Performance of the ASC prediction model

Performance of the machine learning model for predicting ASC

As shown in Figure S2A in the supplemental information, the performance of six traditional machine learning models—SVM, LDA, RF, LR, DT, and NB—was evaluated on an independent external test set (center 3). Overall, these models exhibited limited predictive performance, making it difficult to effectively distinguish patients with disease progression.

Among them, the SVM model achieved high precision (1.0000) but very low recall (0.2368) and an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of only 0.6131, indicating a weak ability to identify positive cases. The LDA and RF models achieved slightly better AUCs of 0.6536 and 0.7151, respectively, but their recall rates remained low (both 0.3684). The LR model demonstrated a relatively higher recall (0.5526), yet its overall accuracy (0.6133) and specificity (0.6757) were still limited. The DT and NB models showed comparable overall performance, with AUCs of 0.6287 and 0.6707 and recall rates of 0.4737 and 0.5789, respectively, still failing to reach satisfactory predictive levels. Figure S2B presents the confusion matrices of each model on the test set.

Overall, traditional machine learning models performed poorly in predicting the progression from AC to ASC, suggesting that relying solely on limited radiomic features is insufficient to capture the complex patterns of ASC progression and lacks generalizability.

Computational performance

All deep learning experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (32 GB VRAM) and an Intel Core i7-13700KF CPU (32 GB RAM), using the PyTorch framework. The average processing time for single-case segmentation of the gallbladder and surrounding regions was 10.5 ± 3.8 s. The training durations for the AC diagnosis model and the ASC prediction model were approximately 1.5 h each. The average inference time per case was 1.3 ± 0.4 s. These results demonstrate that the constructed deep learning models can perform rapid processing for AC diagnosis and ASC prediction tasks. Overall, the models exhibit excellent computational efficiency and show strong potential for seamless deployment in time-sensitive clinical workflows.

Discussion

This study developed a deep learning-assisted diagnostic system based on abdominal noncontrast CT images, capable of accurately identifying AC and assessing its severity from a single CT scan. After further integration of clinical information, the system achieved an accuracy of 0.9358 ± 0.0103 on the internal test set and 0.865 ± 0.0149 on the external test set for AC diagnosis and an accuracy of 0.8452 ± 0.0144 on the internal test set and 0.8560 ± 0.0112 on the external test set for ASC prediction. Gradient-based visualization and feature analysis confirmed the interpretability of the model’s attention regions and the reliability of deep features. The system also demonstrated high computational efficiency, with an average single-case segmentation time of 10.5 ± 3.8 s and an inference time of 1.3 ± 0.4 s, showing potential for seamless integration into time-sensitive clinical workflows. Overall, this study provides technological support for a “one-stop abdominal CT” smart diagnostic approach, enabling AC identification and severity assessment based on routine CT scans of patients with acute abdominal pain.

Although radiomics-based machine learning methods have been widely validated as effective tools for imaging analysis,24,25,26,27,28,29 their performance in AC diagnosis was unstable in this study: no single model consistently outperformed the others across all evaluation metrics. For patients with acute abdominal pain, AC diagnosis is not only a matter of disease screening but also of narrowing the differential diagnosis.30,31 Balancing the risk of false positives and false negatives is thus clinically valuable. In this study, LDA and RF models (precision 0.8857 and 0.863; recall 0.8267 and 0.84) achieved the best balance between precision and recall, showing relatively stable performance. For ASC prediction, all traditional machine learning models exhibited limited predictive ability, with accuracy below 0.65. The generally low recall rates indicate a risk of missed diagnoses in high-risk patients, potentially delaying treatment and increasing the risk of gallbladder suppuration, perforation, and systemic infection,2 which can significantly raise surgical difficulty and mortality.3,4,5

To enhance the model’s ability to capture complex features, we introduced a deep learning model based on local images of the gallbladder and surrounding areas. Compared with radiomics approaches relying solely on intra-gallbladder regions of interests (ROI), the deep learning model demonstrated superior accuracy, stability, and generalizability for both AC diagnosis and ASC prediction, with especially notable advantages in ASC prediction. Further integration of clinical information into a multimodal model improved performance on the internal test set. Although gains were limited on the independent external test set, the model maintained stable performance, demonstrating the value of multimodal fusion for robustness and generalization.

To assess clinical applicability, we measured end-to-end inference efficiency, which was sufficient for practical scenarios. Compared with manual ASC assessment, the deep learning model showed higher predictive performance. Overall, the deep learning model not only improved diagnostic accuracy but also enabled rapid automated analysis of CT images, reducing the diagnostic time window critical for time-sensitive acute abdominal pain workflows.32 Moreover, for elderly patients with incomplete histories, atypical symptoms, poor cooperation, or comorbidities,32,33 the proposed noncontrast CT-based model still performed well for AC diagnosis and ASC prediction, highlighting its potential as an efficient adjunct diagnostic tool to overcome limitations of conventional methods.

The “black box” nature of deep learning models remains a concern in clinical practice.34,35 In this study, we used two approaches to verify model interpretability and reliability. First, gradient-based visualization showed that the model’s attention was concentrated around the gallbladder in both AC and ASC models, indicating accurate capture of relevant imaging features. Second, t-SNE dimensionality reduction of deep learning and radiomics features showed that deep features had better hierarchical separation in both AC diagnosis and ASC prediction, suggesting that radiomics models, which heavily rely on manually selected features, may have reduced performance as sample size and variability increase, especially given the need for manual ROI annotation in practice.

This study developed an efficient deep learning-based diagnostic system capable of accurately identifying AC and its suppurative progression from a single noncontrast abdominal CT scan. Incorporating clinical information further improved the model’s predictive performance and generalization stability on external datasets. Compared with traditional radiomics models, the proposed model not only focused on the gallbladder itself but also captured information from pericholecystic regions, enhancing diagnostic efficacy. Moreover, its high computational efficiency supports rapid deployment in time-sensitive clinical workflows, offering a robust and reliable tool for the prompt evaluation of AC and ASC.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations. First, although it is a multicenter study, the sample size could be expanded. Future research should include larger sample sizes and additional centers to enhance generalizability. Second, although the fusion model considered clinical and laboratory data, differences in laboratory testing items across centers limited the inclusion of some potentially valuable indicators, such as C-reactive protein. Finally, model performance has not yet been validated in subgroups of patients with different underlying conditions and severity, meaning the specificity of application requires further evaluation.

Experimental model and study participant details

Study population

Institutional Review Board approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of the People’s Hospital of Liaoning Province (2023K047), the Institutional Review Board of Panjin Liaohe Oilfield Gem Flower Hospital (LLSC-2025-LW-01), and the Institutional Review Board of Nanchong Central Hospital (2025NO.125), with informed consent waived. To develop diagnostic models for acute cholecystitis (AC), patients who underwent cholecystectomy or percutaneous gallbladder drainage between January 2020 and January 2023 at the three centers were enrolled. The diagnosis of AC was based on clinical symptoms and signs combined with imaging confirmation.

Inclusion criteria were as follows: patients who underwent non-contrast abdominal CT before surgery and received either cholecystectomy or percutaneous gallbladder drainage. Exclusion criteria included: (1) concomitant infectious foci that could interfere with the assessment of primary suppuration; (2) an interval of more than 72 hours between CT examination and surgery, which could lead to discrepancies between imaging findings and intraoperative pathology due to disease progression; and (3) poor CT image quality caused by motion artifacts or unclear gallbladder visualization.

To predict the progression of AC to acute suppurative cholecystitis (ASC), postoperative pathological results of all AC patients were collected. The diagnosis of ASC was defined by the presence of pus within the gallbladder lumen or pericholecystic abscess formation. A total of 641 patients were finally included in the analysis. All participants comprised 334 Asian males and 307 Asian females. The gender identity of all patients is consistent with their sex assigned at birth. There were no statistically significant differences in gender between groups. Gender was not associated with the study outcomes. CT scanning parameters are provided in Supplementary Materials 1, and the patient enrollment flowchart is shown in Figure 1.

Method details

Radiomics feature extraction and machine learning model construction based on non-contrast CT

In this study, TotalSegmentator, a robust and fully automated multi-organ segmentation tool, was first used to perform anatomical segmentation on non-contrast CT images. The tool employs adaptive preprocessing and dynamic network topology optimization to identify 104 anatomical structures, and has demonstrated high robustness across heterogeneous multicenter datasets.36 In cases where severe anatomical distortion or inflammation prevented accurate gallbladder recognition by TotalSegmentator, manual corrections were performed by experienced radiologists to ensure segmentation accuracy.

Based on the segmentation results of non-contrast CT (Figure 2), radiomics features were extracted using the PyRadiomics library (version 3.0.1). A total of 1,316 features were extracted from each volume of interest (VOI) (Supplementary Material 2).

In the radiomics analysis, several commonly used machine learning algorithms were evaluated for two tasks: (1) diagnosing acute cholecystitis (AC) and (2) predicting progression to acute suppurative cholecystitis (ASC). The models included Gaussian Process (GP), Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA), Decision Tree (DT), Naïve Bayes (NB), and Autoencoder (AE). Data from Centers 1 and 2 were combined, with 80% used for five-fold cross-validation training and validation, and the remaining 20% reserved as the internal test set. Data from Center 3 were used as an independent external test set. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.

Deep learning model development

In the deep learning part, the role of TotalSegmentator differed from that in the radiomics analysis. Here, it was used solely for automatic localization rather than precise segmentation of the gallbladder. This localization step rapidly identified the approximate spatial position of the gallbladder, enabling the extraction of 128 × 128 pixel patches centered on the gallbladder from each axial slice. This ensured inclusion of the gallbladder and its pericholecystic region, allowing the model to learn inflammation-related features around the organ.

Data from Centers 1 and 2 were combined, with 10% randomly reserved for internal testing and the remaining data used for five-fold cross-validation training to enhance model robustness and generalization. Data from Center 3 served exclusively as an independent external test set to assess cross-center generalizability.

ResNet50 was employed as the backbone network for feature extraction. Its residual architecture enables deeper network construction while maintaining gradient stability, thereby improving feature representation capacity. Two task-specific models were developed: an AC diagnostic model to distinguish AC from non-AC cases and an ASC prediction model to identify patients at high risk of progression from AC to ASC. This task-specific design, combined with cross-validation, internal testing, and independent external validation, improved model stability and cross-center generalization.

To assess the clinical applicability of the deep learning model for ASC prediction, a radiologist with 10 years of experience in abdominal imaging was invited to predict ASC based on CT images under two conditions: with and without clinical background information. Diagnostic metrics—including accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, and F1 score—were calculated. The radiologist’s performance was compared with that of the CT-based deep learning model and the multimodal model integrating clinical information to simulate real-world clinical decision-making and systematically evaluate different predictive strategies.

Deep learning visualization

To validate the performance of the AC diagnostic model and the ASC prediction model, we employed two visualization techniques: Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) and t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE).

Grad-CAM generates heatmaps to visualize the regions of input images that are most influential in the model’s decision-making process. It does so by computing the gradients of the target class with respect to the feature maps in the final convolutional layer and using these gradients to weight the feature maps. The resulting weighted combination of feature maps is then superimposed on the original input image, allowing intuitive interpretation of the model’s focus areas.

In addition, we utilized t-SNE to visualize the distribution of extracted features in a lower-dimensional space. For the deep learning models, we extracted feature vectors from the final linear layer of the network for each test sample. For the radiomics model, the extracted texture features were used directly. These high-dimensional features were then projected into a two-dimensional space using t-SNE to observe clustering patterns among different sample categories.

Quantification and statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (v.26.0; IBM, Armonk) and R software (version 4.1.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing). The distribution of continuous variables was first assessed. Data with a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the t-test. Non-normally distributed data were described as median and interquartile range [M (P25, P75)] and analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as counts or percentages and analyzed using the chi-square test. A two-sided P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Successful management of acute type A aortic dissection in late pregnancy: a case report
Authors: Huang Luyang, Yuan Li, Shao Yan, He Zongzhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundStanford Type A aortic dissection (TAAD) complicating pregnancy is exceedingly rare yet catastrophic, representing a leading cause of maternal cardiovascular mortality. Management requires balancing maternal hemodynamic stability and fetal viability.Case presentationA 31-year-old woman (G2P0) at 38 weeks gestation presented with acute tearing chest pain. Computed tomography angiography confirmed TAAD extending from ascending aorta to proximal arch, and the right coronary artery was affected. Transthoracic echocardiography confirmed TAAD with aortic sinus dilation (40 mm) and moderate aortic regurgitation. Multidisciplinary management included emergency cesarean section delivering a healthy female infant (Apgar 9/9/10), followed by prophylactic uterine artery embolization to mitigate hemorrhage risk during subsequent cardiac surgery. The patient underwent Bentall procedure combined with coronary artery bypass grafting. The patient recovered uneventfully, discharged on postoperative day 14. Two-month follow-up demonstrated adequate aortic remodeling with regional residual distal dissection.ConclusionThis case demonstrates that a sequential surgical approach with multidisciplinary collaboration can optimize outcomes in late pregnancy complicated by TAAD.

Introduction

Pregnancy complicated by Stanford Type A aortic dissection (TAAD) constitutes approximately 0.1–0.4% of all aortic dissections (AD), with a gestational incidence of 0.0004% [1, 2]. Despite its low prevalence, this condition is exceptionally catastrophic, posing a direct threat to both maternal and fetal survival, with estimated mortality rates of 12% for mothers and 28% for fetuses, which renders AD the third leading cardiovascular cause of maternal death [3]. TAAD most frequently manifests during the third trimester, followed by the puerperium [4]. Literature review reveals that, owing to the rarity of TAAD during pregnancy, existing reports on this patient cohort are restricted to case reports or small case series. Herein, we present a case of acute TAAD in late pregnancy successfully managed by our hospital’s multidisciplinary team (MDT), with particular emphasis on the sequential surgical algorithm (cesarean section, prophylactic uterine artery embolization, delayed cardiac surgery) as a safe and effective paradigm for this catastrophic condition.

Case presentation

A 31-year-old female (G2P0) at 38 weeks of gestation presented to our emergency department in September 2025, with complaint of sudden chest tearing pain for 2 h. The patient had no family history of aortic disease, sudden cardiac death, or connective tissue disorders. No genetic testing was performed given the emergency nature of the presentation and absence of phenotypic features. On admission, vital signs were: heart rate 105 beats/min, respiratory rate 17 breaths/min, blood pressure 151/59 mmHg. Bilateral radial and dorsalis pedis pulses were palpable and symmetrical. Thoracoabdominal Computed Tomography Angiography (CTA) demonstrated the AD extending from the ascending aorta to the aortic arch. Transthoracic echocardiography confirmed the diagnosis of TAAD with aortic sinus dilation (40 mm), moderate aortic regurgitation, mild tricuspid regurgitation, and normal left ventricular systolic function.

Laboratory data

Laboratory findings on admission were as follows. The complete blood count showed white blood cell, red blood cell, hemoglobin, and platelet counts of 8.43 × 10⁹/L, 4.11 × 10¹²/L, 136 g/L, and 214 × 10⁹/L, respectively. Serum creatinine and urea nitrogen were 55 µmol/L and 3.8 mmol/L, respectively, and hepatic enzymes were within normal limits. Cardiac biomarkers included troponin I < 20 pg/mL, NT-proBNP 19.5 pg/mL, myoglobin 6.26 ng/mL, and CK-MB 2.7 ng/mL. Coagulation profiling revealed a D-dimer of 1.82 µg/mL; all other coagulation parameters were within normal limits.

Management

Upon admission, immediate MDT consultation involving obstetrics and gynecology, anesthesiology, intensive care unit (ICU), and cardiac surgery was convened. The patient underwent emergency cesarean section under general anesthesia via a transverse abdominal incision, with delivery of a female neonate with Apgar scores of 9, 9, and 10 at 1, 5, and 10 min, respectively. Operative time was 56 min. Postoperatively, the patient remained intubated and was transferred to the ICU for deep sedation and analgesia. On the same evening, the patient was scheduled for cardiac surgery. Preoperative MDT assessment identified a significant risk of hemorrhage from the uterine incision site following heparinization required for cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB). After obtaining informed consent, prophylactic uterine artery embolization was performed under digital subtraction angiography before cardiac surgery since the patient and her family strongly desired uterine preservation for future fertility.

Given the extensive dissection of the ascending aorta, femoral arterial and right atrial cannulation via median sternotomy was performed to avoid iatrogenic injury to the dissected aortic wall. Moderate hypothermia was employed with a nasopharyngeal temperature of 28 °C. Antegrade selective cerebral perfusion was instituted via the brachiocephalic artery for brain protection during the aortic cross-clamp period. Circulatory arrest was not required as the dissection was confined to the ascending aorta and proximal arch. Cold blood cardioplegia was delivered retrogradely via the coronary sinus cannula, with intermittent antegrade delivery directly into the coronary ostia after excision of the dissected aortic root. Operative exploration revealed that the primary intimal tear was located in the ascending aorta approximately 2.5 cm above the sinotubular junction, with dissection extending into the right coronary sinus and involving the ostium and proximal segment of the right coronary artery. The surrounding aortic wall at the right coronary ostium was extensively dissected and structurally fragile, with distal extension into the proximal right coronary artery that precluded safe direct coronary reimplantation. The Bentall procedure was performed using a 23-mm mechanical valved conduit. The coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) to the right coronary artery was performed using a saphenous vein graft anastomosed to the right coronary artery distal to the dissection. CPB time was 293 min, with an aortic cross-clamp time of 220 min. The estimated blood loss during cesarean section and cardiac surgery was 300 and 400 mL, respectively. 2 units of platelets and 400 mL plasma were transfused during cardiac operation. The patient recovered uneventfully and was discharged on postoperative day 14.

Postoperatively, the patient was started on warfarin anticoagulation (target INR 2.0–3.0) on postoperative day 3 due to the mechanical valved conduit, with overlapping low-molecular-weight heparin until therapeutic INR was achieved. Aspirin (100 mg daily) was added for antiplatelet therapy. At 2-month outpatient follow-up, echocardiography demonstrated mild left ventricular enlargement with a left ventricular ejection fraction of 50%. Thoracoabdominal CTA revealed ascending aortic remodeling but with residual regional dissection distal to the prosthetic graft and proximal to the brachiocephalic artery (Figs. 1 and 2). The infant remained healthy with normal growth parameters at 2-month follow-up.

Discussion

Herein, we report a successfully managed case of acute TAAD in late pregnancy treated by our hospital’s MDT, with favorable maternal and neonatal outcomes. The primary clinical contribution of this report lies in the sequential surgical algorithm—emergency cesarean section, prophylactic uterine artery embolization, and delayed cardiac surgery—as a safe and effective paradigm for near-term pregnancy complicated by acute TAAD.

The mortality rates for pregnancy-associated TAAD have declined in recent years, attributed to enhanced disease awareness and comprehensive multidisciplinary management protocols. However, outcomes remain highly variable across institutions and surgical strategies, as summarized in Table 1. Reported maternal mortality ranges from 0% in specialized Chinese cohorts to over 23% in nationwide surveys, while fetal/neonatal mortality approaches 27% in meta-analyses.

Summary of recent literature on pregnancy-associated Type A aortic dissection

Pregnancy-associated TAAD demonstrates a distinct temporal distribution, with the vast majority of cases occurring during the third trimester and the early puerperium [4, 5]. This temporal pattern is closely linked to the cumulative physiological stress of advancing gestation. Hemodynamic burden reaches its peak in the third trimester, characterized by a 40–50% increase in blood volume and cardiac output, which markedly elevates aortic wall tension [5]. Concurrently, hormonal changes, including elevated estrogen, progesterone, and relaxin, promote collagen degradation and reduce vascular wall integrity, rendering the aorta particularly susceptible to dissection during this high-risk window [5]. The immediate postpartum period poses additional risk due to abrupt hemodynamic fluctuations associated with labor, delivery, and uterine involution. This gestational-age-specific vulnerability underscores the importance of heightened clinical vigilance during late pregnancy and the early puerperium.

In this case, the patient presented with tearing chest pain, the predominant complaint in the vast majority of AD patients. However, chest pain during pregnancy may pose diagnostic challenges, with differential diagnoses including acute myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, and AD—the former two entities exhibiting significantly higher incidences than AD [6]. Cardiac enzymes, electrocardiography, coagulation profiles, and transthoracic echocardiography facilitate differentiation, though definitive diagnosis fundamentally relies on thoracoabdominal CTA to confirm AD, classify the dissection type, and assess the extent of vascular disruption.

The patient remained intubated post-cesarean section and was transferred to the ICU for deep sedation and analgesia in this case. For patients with acute TAAD, preoperative endotracheal intubation combined with profound analgesia and sedation maintains stable hemodynamics, effectively reduces the incidence of preoperative dissection rupture, and creates favorable conditions for subsequent surgical intervention [7, 8]. The surgical management strategy for pregnant women with advanced gestational AD depends on maternal hemodynamic status, AD type, fetal developmental status, and maternal preferences. For TAAD, cardiovascular surgical options include: (1) aortic root replacement + total aortic arch replacement + stented elephant trunk implantation (Bentall + Sun’s procedure); (2) aortic root replacement (Bentall procedure). In this case, the dissection was localized to the ascending aorta with right coronary artery involvement, while the innominate, left common carotid, and left subclavian arteries were largely unaffected. Therefore, a conventional Bentall + CABG procedure was performed. Certainly, for patients with severe aortic arch and descending aortic pathology, aortic arch replacement combined with elephant trunk stenting technology maximizes disease alleviation and improves clinical prognosis.

Previous literature has proposed that for pregnant women with TAAD at a gestational age of less than 28 weeks, maternal safety should be prioritized through aggressive surgical management of the AD, with subsequent consideration of pregnancy continuation contingent upon intraoperative and postoperative hemodynamic stability. Between 28 and 32 weeks of gestation, decisions regarding pregnancy termination and therapeutic modality should be based on the condition’s impact on both mother and fetus, wherein cesarean section may be performed initially, followed by selection of the appropriate surgical approach according to dissection characteristics [9]. After 32 weeks of gestation, simultaneous cesarean section and aortic repair can be undertaken, with intraoperative CPB duration meticulously minimized to mitigate the risk of fetal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy [10]. Nevertheless, given the complexity and catastrophic nature of AD during pregnancy, the decision to preserve the fetus necessitates prudent deliberation, integrating the criticality of the condition, the comprehensive capabilities of the treating institution, and maternal preferences. Furthermore, for strategies involving concurrent vascular replacement and cesarean section, the sequence of procedures warrants meticulous evaluation.

Notably, systemic heparinization required for CPB surgery exacerbates the risk of uterine hemorrhage and hematoma formation at the cesarean incision site. Earlier literature has documented that performing hysterectomy following fetal delivery via cesarean section, prior to cardiovascular surgery under CPB, can substantially improve maternal and neonatal survival rates [11, 12]. Recent reports advocate that during cesarean section, prophylactic intrauterine Bakri balloon tamponade or uterine artery ligation should be performed concurrently with meticulous hemostasis of the surgical field to prevent postpartum hemorrhage secondary to heparinization during aortic repair. However, for postpartum hemorrhage refractory to compression and pharmacological measures, prompt hysterectomy remains indicated. Conversely, alternative reports have suggested that delaying CPB for 48 h post-cesarean may reduce the risk of intrauterine and uterine incision bleeding [13, 14]. However, this strategy may increase the risk of maternal mortality from AD rupture.

Key clinical insights from this case

Based on the temporal distribution and pathophysiology of pregnancy-associated TAAD, we propose the following clinical framework:

In conclusion, patients with acute TAAD in late pregnancy should undergo prompt surgical intervention, with individualized therapeutic strategies selected based on gestational age and the extent of dissection pathology. Through multidisciplinary collaborative management involving cardiac surgery, obstetrics and gynecology, anesthesiology, and ICU, maternal and fetal mortality can be minimized to achieve optimal maternal-neonatal outcomes. Aggressive, rapid diagnosis and appropriate treatment are crucial for saving the lives of pregnant women with TAAD and their fetuses.

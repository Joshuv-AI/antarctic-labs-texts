IGSF1: a biomarker for predicting prognosis, immunotherapy response, and drug candidates in COVID-19 combined hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Yuanhui Guo, Baixuan Shen, Chaoxuan Lou, Li Wang, Ying Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a highly heterogeneous malignancy with poor prognosis and a common cause of cancer-related death worldwide, and despite ongoing therapeutic breakthroughs, patient survival benefits are limited. Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) is the cause of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) and poses a major threat to humanity worldwide. As the epidemic continues to develop, more and more people are infected with SARS-CoV-2, including patients with HCC. However, the relationship between COVID-19 and HCC has not yet been fully elucidated. Our study aimed to identify the shared genetic characteristics and molecular mechanisms between COVID-19 and HCC. The data involved in this study come from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), Genotype-Tissue Expression(GTEx), and Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia(CCLE) databases. We used differentially expressed genes to perform enrichment analysis to reveal the biological landscape of COVID-19 combined with HCC. In addition, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was used to study the co-expression network related to COVID-19 and HCC. We then combined the validation datasets to screen out immunoglobulin superfamily member 1 (IGSF1) as the most important core gene. Finally, we extensively studied the functional expression of IGSF1 in tumor samples, normal tissues, and cancer cell lines. The molecular mechanisms related to COVID-19 and HCC are rarely studied. Our study identifies IGSF1 as a potential therapeutic target and immune-related biomarker for patients with COVID-19 and HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01483-2.

Introduction

So far, Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) has posed a major public health threat worldwide and has become one of the deadliest pandemics in modern history. COVID-19 patients usually present with respiratory symptoms such as cough, fever, and shortness of breath, and may also experience gastrointestinal symptoms such as nausea, loss of appetite, and diarrhea [1]. Additionally, COVID-19 can affect organs beyond the respiratory system. Studies have shown that 10–65% of COVID-19 patients have abnormalities in liver biochemical indicators, such as elevated liver enzyme levels, suggesting that they are related to systemic inflammatory response, drug-induced liver injury (DILI), and hypoxia–ischemia reperfusion [2]. Transaminases and bilirubin in patients with severe COVID-19 are at least twice as high as those in patients with mild and moderate symptoms [3]. Elevated gamma-glutamyl transferase has been reported in up to 54% of COVID-19 patients, and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) may be disproportionately elevated compared with ALT/AST (Alanine transaminase/Aspartate transaminase) levels [4, 5]. In addition, SARS-CoV-2 can infect hepatocytes, directly causing liver damage and triggering liver immunopathology. Liver injury is temporary in most patients, however permanent damage has been reported in severe cases of COVID-19 [6]. As the condition of COVID-19 patients worsens, the degree of liver dysfunction becomes more severe [7]. It is known that SARS-CoV-2 infection is closely related to angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2), which is widely expressed in the lungs and gastrointestinal tract [8]. In the liver, ACE2 is mainly expressed by cholangiocytes, but SARS-CoV-2 infection does not cause bile duct damage [9]. Histological features found in liver biopsies, such as microvesicular hepatic steatosis, mild lobular and portal activity, are very similar to DILI [10]. It can be said that the interaction with the ACE2 receptor is the way SARS-CoV-2 infects host cells, rather than the way it causes liver damage. Liver injury may be established through direct effects of the virus or immune-mediated mechanisms, involving both healthy people and patients with chronic liver disease [8]. At the same time, there is a high level of pro-inflammatory cytokines during the COVID-19 disease, such as interleukins 1 and 6, tumor necrosis factor, etc., resulting in a cytokine storm [11]. The above evidence suggests that liver injury in COVID-19 patients may be caused by multiple factors, including vascular injury, DILI, coagulopathy, direct cytopathic effects of the virus, and excessive systemic immune responses [11].

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most common cancer in the world, accounting for approximately 6% of all cancer incidence [12]. Despite the emergence of new treatments, patient prognosis remains dismal. It is estimated that there may be 1.4 million new cases of liver cancer and 1.3 million deaths from liver cancer by 2040 [13]. Epidemiological data indicate that cancer patients are more susceptible to SARS-CoV-2 infection due to their low immune function [14]. SARS-CoV-2 has been shown to potentially cause dysfunction in multiple organs, including the respiratory system, cardiovascular system, nervous system, immune system, and gastrointestinal system [15–20]. Dysfunction of single or multiple organ systems further complicates the prognosis and management of cancer. Patients with HCC are more susceptible to SARS-CoV-2 than other cancers because SARS-CoV-2-induced liver injury may lead to complication of existing hepatitis viral infection and cirrhosis in the majority of patients with HCC [21]. Furthermore, HCC patients with higher expression of Neuropilin-1 in liver sinusoidal endothelial cells may have higher chances of SARS-CoV-2 infection in their hepatic stellate cells and tumor cells [22]. Equally concerning are some studies indicating that long-term infection with SARS-CoV-2 may lead to cancer [23]. Viral infections that can induce cell transformation and cause cancer are not uncommon at present, such as human papillomavirus, hepatitis B virus, hepatitis C virus, Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpes virus, human T lymphoma virus, and Merkel cell polyomavirus and Epstein–Barr virus [24]. Similar to SARS-CoV-2, SARS-CoV-1 interferes with multiple signaling pathways involved in malignant cell transformation [25]. However, the potential carcinogenic mechanism of SARS-CoV-2 is still unknown and needs further exploration. E2F transcription factors and RB1 have been confirmed to be regulated by SARS-CoV-2 infection, indicating that SARS-CoV-2 infection may contribute to tumorigenesis [26].

Considering that HCC is a malignant tumor with a common cause of viral infection, its relationship with COVID-19 deserves further exploration. However, so far, there are few studies based on bioinformatics analysis to explore the common pathogenesis between HCC and COVID-19. With the rapid development of transcriptomics, bioinformatics analysis based on gene expression profile data has been widely used to explore the pathogenesis of diseases. This study attempted to find the shared biological pathways and molecular mechanisms between HCC and COVID-19, with the aim of providing new insights into the pathogenesis and treatment of HCC patients infected with SARS-CoV-2.

Materials and methods

Microarray data collecting and processing

GEO (Home—GEO—NCBI (nih.gov)) is a public database widely used today, including a large number of high-throughput sequencing and microarray datasets submitted from various research institutions around the world [27], datasets GSE167336 and GSE151803 all originate from it. GSE167336 is the main analysis data of this study. The experimental processing is diverse and the grouping is complex. Therefore, only some samples related to this study are extracted (Sample numbers: GSM5102486, GSM5102488, GSM5102490, GSM5102475, GSM5102477, GSM5102479). SARS-CoV-2 infection 48-Hour-old Huh7.5 cells were used as the experimental group, while SARS-CoV-2 mock-infected Huh7.5 cells were used as the control group. Each group had three biological replicates. As a validation dataset, GSE151803 contains 4 cases of SARS-CoV-2-infected liver organoid donors and their controls (sample numbers: GSM4591147- GSM4591154). Another validation dataset is TCGA-LIHC (Liver hepatocellular carcinoma), which uses tumor samples as controls and is obtained from the TCGA database (GDC (cancer.gov)), including 352 tumor samples and 47 normal samples. GEO, TCGA, GTEx and CCLE belong to public databases. The patients involved in the database have obtained ethical approval. Users can download relevant data for free for research and publish relevant articles. Our study is based on open source data, so there are no ethical issues and other conflicts of interest.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis

The “Limma” package [28] in R software was used to identify DEGs in the GSE167336 and GSE151803 datasets, and the “DESeq2” package [29] was used for the TCGA-LIHC dataset. We set the expression difference criterion as P values < 0.05 and | logFC |> 1 for data from GEO, and as P values < 0.05 and | logFC |> 2 for TCGA-LIHC data. The expression patterns of DEGs were then visualized in the form of heat maps and volcano plots in R software.

Functional enrichment analysis

Gene ontology (GO) is a database established by the Gene Ontology Federation, which annotates gene products from the perspectives of molecular function, cellular component, and biological process. GO term enrichment analysis of DEGs was performed using the “clusterProfiler” package [30]. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) is a more reliable enrichment analysis method based on gene expression levels. The latest reference dataset h.all.v2023.2.Hs.symbols.gmt is used for GSEA enrichment analysis. P values < 0.05 were set as the criterion for significant enrichment.

The weighted gene co‑expression network analysis

With the advancement of information science, more scientific and comprehensive algorithms are emerging one after another. As a systematic biological method, WGCNA can reveal gene association patterns among different samples and detect candidate biomarker genes or therapeutic targets based on the interconnections between gene sets and the association between gene sets and phenotypes [31]. We used the "WGCNA" package to construct a co-expression network of COVID-19 and HCC comorbid genes, which can correlate gene networks and clinical features to explore the common pathogenesis of these two diseases. Additionally, we evaluate the correlation between Module Membership (MM) and Gene significance (GS) scores in modules to account for module significance.

Identification of shared genes between COVID-19 and HCC

Use the WGCNA algorithm to identify core gene sets that are highly correlated with traits as shared genes between COVID-19 and HCC, and extract the DEGs of the datasets GSE167336, GSE151803 and TCGA-LIHC, and analyze the above four sets of data through Venn diagram. Identification of common outcomes as hub genes.

Multiple landscape analysis of core genes

The "IOBR" package has built-in a variety of signatures closely related to the tumor microenvironment to assist in the exploration of immuno-oncology, explain tumor-immune interactions and accelerate precise immunotherapy [32]. In this study, in order to fully demonstrate the functional expression of core genes in the tumor microenvironment, the “IOBR” package was used to calculate the immunity, metabolism, tumor microenvironment and tumor-related signatures scores using the single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm. The correlation between the infected group and the uninfected group was calculated by the kruskal test. In addition, the “linkET” package was used to visualize the correlation between core genes and signatures, and the statistical method was mental test.

Pan-tissue analysis of core genes

Standardised pan-tissue datasets, including TGCA and GTEx, were obtained from the UCSC database (University of California Santa Cruz) (UCSC Xena).In addition, datasets from CCLE were obtained from the DepMap database (DepMap: The Cancer Dependency Map Project at Broad Institute). A variety of online analysis platforms were also used concurrently in this study to aid in the analysis, including GEPIA2 (Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis) (GEPIA 2 (cancer-pku.cn)) [33], cBioPortal (cBioPortal for Cancer Genomics) [34], GSCA (GSCA: Gene Set Cancer Analysis (wchscu.cn))[35], GTBAdb (Guilt By Association Analysis) (Home—GTBAdb).

This study visualized the expression of core genes in the pan-tissue dataset. In addition, the GEPIA2 database was used to show the expression distribution and usage distribution of different isoforms of core genes in different types of cancer. The correlation between pan-cancer paired samples was tested through the wilcox test calculate. For survival data in different types of cancer, we used the GSCA database to analyze from four perspectives: DFI (Disease Free Interval), DSS (Disease Specific Survival), OS (Overall Survival), and PFS (Progression Free Survival), and drew Survival curves for statistically significant samples (Cox P values < 0.05).

We used the "TCGAplot" package [36], cBioPortal and GSCA database to analyze the genetic variation of core genes. The focus of the study is on the frequency and types of gene mutations in core genes in pan-cancer. Copy number variation (CNV), single-nucleotide variant (SNV) and methylation are also included in this study as important analysis directions. The correlation between the expression level of core genes and CNV or methylation was calculated by the spearman method.

With the progress of disease research, the immune system response has a non-negligible impact on the development of the disease. The infiltration of immune cells in tumor tissues has a significant impact on the development of tumors [37]. Through the “TCGAplot” package, we systematically analyzed the correlation between pan-cancer expression and immune checkpoint genes (ICGs), chemokines, chemokine receptors, immune stimulating factors, immunosuppressants, immune cell proportions and immune score (estimateScore, immuneScore, stromalScore), calculated by pearson.

Understanding the functions of core genes is basic but very important. We implement this analysis through the GTBA database. By calculating the correlation between core genes and protein-coding genes, a pre-sorted genelist is then created based on the correlation, which will be used as input data for GSEA analysis. The calculation of correlation will be performed in all data of TCGA tumor, GTEx and CCLE, and hallmarks are used as the reference gene set of GSEA. In addition, we also calculated the correlation between the expression of core genes and the signature scores in hallmarks, and visualized it. The correlation method is pearson, and the scores of signatures in hallmarks are calculated by the ssGSEA algorithm.

Correlation analysis between DEGs and drug sensitivity

CMap (Connectivity Map) [clue.io] is a multifunctional database that correlates drugs, genes, and diseases. The gene expression profile data of this database can be used to screen out compounds or drugs related to a certain disease or gene [38]. We used the previously screened up-regulated genes in the GSE167336 dataset, imported them into the CMap database, and searched for small molecule drugs or compounds that can reduce the expression of related genes based on the connection scores. CellMiner (CellMiner—Home | Genomics and Pharmacology Facility (nih.gov)) is a high-throughput and high-content database containing more than 20,000 genes and compounds, which provides convenience for rapid retrieval of related genes or drug information [39]. As a new star in bioinformatics research, GSCA integrates the genetic information, clinical data and drug sensitivity data of more than 30 kinds of cancer by means of immunogenomics and pharmacogenomics, and provides powerful data support for cancer research. [35]. Therefore, we used the CellMiner database and the GSCA online analysis platform to analyze the correlation between the expression of core genes and drug sensitivity. In addition, we also analyzed the drug sensitivity of core genes in the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) (Home page—Cancerrxgene—Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer) database through online tools sparkle (grswsci.top).

Statistical analysis

Most of the analysis and visualization were performed on R software (version4.3.1). All data are transformed into normal distribution data by log transformation. Unless otherwise stated, all t-tests used in this study were statistically significant at P < 0.05.

GSEA and GO enrichment analysis

The GSEA results showed (Fig. 1C) that the six pathways “Coagulation”, “Androgen Response”, “Uv Response Up”, “Apoptosis”, “Estrogen Response Late” and “Oxidative Phosphorylation” were down-regulated, suggesting that the expression of genes related to these pathways was inhibition. Subsequently, the DEGs were analysed for GO functional enrichment. The results are shown in Fig. 1D, “regulation of protein catabolic process”, “proteinlocalisation to plasma membrane”, “regeneration”, “positive regulation of protein catabolic process”, and “tissue regeneration”, and “skeletal muscle tissue regeneration” were considered to be the six most enriched biological processes. In terms of cellular composition, “focal adhesion”, “cell-substrate junction”, “vacuolar membrane” were significantly enriched. The enrichment results in molecular function revealed that “cadherin binding”, “ubiquitin protein ligase binding”, “ubiquitin-like protein ligase binding” and “protein-macromolecule adaptor activity” were found to be associated with DEGs.

Relationship between IGSF1 and the tumor microenvironment

The estimated proportion of immune cells in the infection group generally decreased, especially Type 17 T helper cells and CD56dim natural killer cells, while the estimated proportion of Activated CD4 T cells and Macrophage increased significantly (Fig. 3A). Metabolic pathways were also generally inhibited, with only the Homocysteine Biosynthesis and Fatty Acid Biosynthesis pathways up-regulated, but the differences were not obvious (Fig. 3C). Pan-tissue expression analysis of IGSF1

To explore the expression levels of IGSF1 in human pan-tissues, including tumor tissues, normal tissues, and human tumor cell lines, we used data from TGCA, GTEx, and CCLE to evaluate IGSF1 mRNA expression. For the data in the TCGA database, in order to make the results more credible, we used paired data of normal tissues and tumor tissues to show the expression level of IGSF1 in pan-cancer and the differences between tissues. The expression of IGSF1 was significantly reduced in the tumor tissues of BRCA (Breast invasive carcinoma), KIRC (Kidney renal clear cell carcinoma), PRAD (Prostate adenocarcinoma), and UCEC (Uterine Corpus Endometrial Carcinoma), while the expression was significantly increased in the tumor tissues of LIHC, LUAD (Lung adenocarcinoma), LUSC (Lung squamous cell carcinoma), and especially THCA (Thyroid carcinoma) (Fig. 4A). The expression level of IGSF1 affects multiple survival indicators in various cancers

In order to further clarify the impact of IGSF1 expression on the prognosis of cancer patients, this study divided cancer patients into high expression groups and low expression groups according to the expression level of IGSF1, and performed univariate cox regression analysis to explore the prognostic value of IGSF1. The four survival indicators jointly revealed from different perspectives that the expression level of IGSF1 is significantly related to the prognosis of patients with multiple tumor types (Fig. 5A). DFI refers to the time period between primary treatment of malignant tumors and the first sign of tumor recurrence, and is an important reflection of clinical benefit. High expression of IGSF1 in PRAD patients is associated with better prognosis in DFI outcomes, while the opposite is true in STAD patients (Fig. 5B, C). The change in outcome metrics for DSS compared to OS was death due to specific diseases, excluding bias due to non-tumour events.Patients with high IGSF1 expression in LIHC, STAD, UCEC suggested a poorer prognosis for DSS, whereas patients with BRCA had a better prognosis (Fig. 5D–G). For OS outcomes, BRCA and KIRC patients with high IGSF1 expression indicated a better prognosis, while UCEC patients indicated a poor prognosis (Fig. 5H–J). The length of time during and after treatment of a disease, such as cancer, that a patient lives with the disease but does not get worse is called PFS. Measurement of PFS is one way to observe the effect of new treatments in clinical trials. IGSF1 expression was positively correlated with better prognosis in patients with PRAD and BRCA, and negatively correlated in STAD (Fig. 5K–M). Analysis of IGSF1 gene alterations in pan-cancer datasets

We used the cBioPortal database to analyse alterations in the IGSF1 gene in the pan-cancer dataset. The three cancers with the highest alteration frequency are Mucinous Stomach Adenocarcinoma, Liposarcoma, Diffuse Type Stomach Adenocarcinoma, and all of them are Deep Deletion. The highest mutation frequency was Mucinous Adenocarcinoma of the Colon and Rectum, and the highest Amplification frequency was Stomach Adenocarcinoma (Fig. 6A). Investigating the relationship of the IGSF1 gene with SNVs, CNVs and methylation in pan-cancer data can be of great help in gaining insights into potential tumour driver mutations, identifying therapeutic targets, and identifying mechanisms of resistance to targeted therapies. The IGSF1 gene had a high SNV percentage in SKCM (Skin Cutaneous Melanoma) and UCEC, while KIRP (Kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma) had the lowest SNV percentage (Fig. 6B). The details of Variant show that Missense Mutation occurs the most followed by Nonsense Mutation, Single Nucleotide Polymorphism (SNP) is the major type, C > T mutation is the most common with a total of 204 occurrences, and C > A also occurs significantly with a total of 113 occurrences. (Fig. 6C–E). The expression level of IGSF1 gene in PRAD, LGG was significantly positively correlated with CNV, while it was significantly negatively correlated in KIRP, KICH (Kidney Chromophobe) (Fig. 6F). The correlation between IGSF1 gene expression and methylation showed a significant negative correlation in most tumours, most notably in CESC (Cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma), and a significant positive correlation with SARC (Sarcoma) (Fig. 6J). The homozygous and heterozygous states of CNV provide important information about genomic variations in tumor research, which may affect the occurrence, development and treatment response of tumors (Fig. 6G, H). The percentage of CNV of IGSF1 gene in different types of cancer showed that heterozygous amplification and loss of heterozygosity accounted for the majority of CNV in 33 cancers. Heterozygous amplification accounted for the largest proportion in ACC (Adrenocortical carcinoma), and loss of heterozygosity accounted for the largest proportion in KICH (Fig. 6I). In addition, IGSF1 expression was positively associated with Monocytes, T cells CD4 memory resting in most cancers, whereas IGSF1 expression was negatively associated with B cells memory, NK cells activated, T cells CD8, T cells regulatory Tregs in most cancers. The relationship between IGSF1 expression levels and tumour immune infiltration status in pan-cancers was analysed by immuneScore, stromalScore and estimateScore (Fig. 7B). In most cancers IGSF1 expression was negatively correlated with immunity score and ESTIMATE score. In LUAD, LUSC, OV, SARC, TGCT and UCEC, IGSF1 expression was negatively correlated with all scores, while in LGG and PRAD, IGSF1 expression was positively correlated with all scores. Functional analysis of IGSF1 in pan-tissues

To further explore the function of the IGSF1 gene, we used three datasets: the TCGA tumour sample, GTEx, and CCLE. Firstly, the correlation between IGSF1 and the protein coding genes in the three datasets was calculated separately, and then a pre-ordered genelist was created based on the correlation, which was used as GSEA. If the GSEA results showed that a certain pathway was positively enriched, then we could consider that the IGSF1 gene was positively correlated with that pathway, and the reference gene sets for GSEA were Hallmark gene sets. Results from TCGA tumour samples showed a positive correlation between IGSF1 and most of these pathways, but a large number of negative pathways were present in MESO (Mesothelioma) and TGCT, implying that IGSF1 is negatively correlated with most of the pathways in these two cancer types (Fig. 9A). The results of GTEx showed that the function of IGSF1 had obvious tissue specificity. In Blood Vessel, Lung, Pancreas, Spleen, Vagina, Kidney, Colon, Stomach, Esophagus, Brain, Adrenal Gland, Pituitary, there was a negative correlation between IGSF1 and the pathway, while in Heart, Liver, Breast, Testis, Bladder, Ovary, Prostate, Fallopian Tube, Blood, Bone Marrow, there was a positive correlation between IGSF1 and pathways (Fig. 9B). Pathways were screened according to the number of cancers with a P value of less than 0.05, and the pathways with the best trend were retained. The three pathways KRAS SIGNALING DN, UV RESPONSE DN and MYOGENESIS in TCGA tumour samples were positively associated with the IGSF1 gene in the majority of cancer types, and it is noteworthy that IGSF1 was negatively associated with UV RESPONSE DN in TGCT, and IGSF1 was also inversely associated with MYOGENESIS in both TGCT and LUSC (Fig. 9D–F). The correlation between KRAS SIGNALING DN and IGSF1 was equally significant in the GTEx and CCLE datasets, but a large number of normal tissues had a negative correlation between IGSF1 and the KRAS SIGNALING DN pathway (Fig. 9G,J). Discussion

The outbreak of SARS-CoV-2 at the end of 2019 and its subsequent rapid spread led to global COVID-19, the pandemic that remains unresolved to date. Although our knowledge of the pathophysiology and clinical manifestations of COVID-19 is increasing, little information is available on the long-term effects of SARS-CoV-2 infection in humans. COVID-19 continues to affect multiple organs and systems after the acute phase, including cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal, neurological, haematological, gastrointestinal, endocrine, and psychosocial manifestations, in this symptom known as the post-COVID-19 syndrome [41]. Cancer patients are a susceptible population to SARS-CoV-2, with higher morbidity and mortality rates compared to other groups of infected individuals, and are often associated with severe complications, so the prognosis for this group is usually poor [42]. Hepatocellular carcinoma is currently the sixth most common cancer in the world, and its incidence is increasing globally, placing a huge burden on the healthcare system and posing a huge threat to the lives and health of people worldwide [43]. Studies have shown that SARS-CoV-2 infection may lead to liver injury through several mechanisms, such as disruption of transaminase activity and bilirubin levels [44]. Meanwhile the impact of SARS-CoV-2 infection on HCC patients with chronic liver diseases such as viral hepatitis and cirrhosis is complex, suggesting that HCC patients are more susceptible to SARS-CoV-2 compared to other cancers [21]. SARS-CoV-2 infection has been shown to exert potential oncogenic effects by affecting cancer-related pathways such as cell proliferation, development, and survival, promoting DNA degradation, preventing damage repair events, and hindering the conversion of RNA into working proteins [45]. SARS-CoV-2 may also promote tumourigenesis by suppressing oncogenes [46]. Therefore, elucidating the mechanisms of interaction between SARS-CoV-2 infection and tumour cells could contribute to a better understanding of cancer progression and the establishment of new therapeutic options. The high malignancy and poorer prognosis of HCC patients are associated with their high degree of heterogeneity, including tumour microenvironment, prognosis, genetic alterations, and response to treatment [47]. However, few studies have focused on the shared pathogenesis of COVID-19 and HCC. This study used a systematic bioinformatics analysis approach to address the mechanisms of correlation between COVID-19 and HCC and to explore the core biomarkers between the two.

In this study, the GSE167336 dataset was differentially analysed and the obtained DEGs were subsequently used for GO enrichment analysis, which showed that DEGs are closely associated with a variety of important biological processes, cellular components and molecular functions. Extracellular matrix breakdown is closely linked to the pathogenic process of COVID-19, and SARS-CoV-2 induces an inflammatory response at the blood-gas barrier, leading to rupture of adhesion junctions and tight junctions between endothelial cells, which leads to collapsing of the blood-gas barrier and ultimately to hypoxia [48]. COVID-19 is recognised as a multiple organ dysfunction syndrome, and musculoskeletal system performance is beginning to be considered to be of absolute importance in both COVID-19 patients and those recovering from SARS-CoV-2 infection. Myalgia, muscle weakness and fatigue may be the result of direct viral entry into muscle cells or inflammation-mediated injury. SARS-CoV-2 may be the first virus capable of directly infecting muscle fibres [49], and additionally myopathy in COVID-19 may be the result of SARS-CoV-2 infection via ACE2 [50]. Inflammation is an important contributor to many histopathology, including skeletal muscle. Many components of the cytokine storm described in COVID-19, such as IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-17, and TNF-α, can directly induce hydrolysis of skeletal muscle fibre proteins and reduce protein synthesis [51]. In addition, IL-1β and TNF-α can block the proliferation and differentiation of satellite cells, which are the progenitor cells of muscle fibers that are key to muscle regeneration after muscle injury. [51]. Placenta specific 8 (PLAC8) has been shown to be highly expressed in cilia and secretory cells of the respiratory tract and intestinal epithelial cells. These cell types are highly sensitive to SARS-CoV-2 infection, suggesting that PLAC8 is a key host factor for SARS-CoV-2 to enter human cells [52]. It has been demonstrated that ACE2 is the primary viral receptor for SARS-CoV-2, and after attachment to host cells and triggering of viral spike proteins by host transmembrane serine protease 2 (TMPRSS2), SARS-CoV-2 is internalised by endocytosis and the viral genome is released from the endosome, followed by replication of the complete viral genome in vesicles containing viral replicase-transcriptase complexes [53]. SARS-CoV-2 infection is accompanied by acute damage of microcapillary, microthrombus and endothelial cells. However, the mechanisms involved in these processes remain to be elucidated. VE-cadherin has been proved to be the cleavage target of ACE2 in vitro, and ACE2 cleavage of VE-cadherin is considered to be the molecular mechanism of endothelial cell injury caused by SARS-CoV-2 infection [54]. The ubiquitination of proteins in host cells infected with SARS-CoV-2 may reveal the virus-host interaction and pathogenesis. SARS-CoV-2 infection may regulate the host immune response by ubiquitinating important proteins, including USP 5, IQGAP 1, TRIM 28 and Hsp 90 [55]. In addition, ubiquitination was also observed on 11 SARS-CoV-2 proteins, including proteins involved in viral replication and inhibition of host innate immune responses [55]. One way for pathogenic viruses to enhance replication is by targeting and interfering with the function of PDZ proteins in cells. Two SARS-CoV-2 proteins, E and Orf3a, contain a protein binding domain at their C-terminus, which is predicted to target a cell PDZ protein family [56]. Based on the research results of many viruses including SARS-CoV-1, the targeting effect of cellular PDZ protein seems to be a common key mechanism in the pathogenesis of viruses [57, 58]. It is likely that the ability of the E and Orf 3a proteins to target the PDZ domain makes an important contribution to the pathogenesis of SARS-CoV-2. It has been reported that cholesterol-rich lipid domains exist in viral lipid envelopes, host plasma membranes, endosomes, and other intracellular compartments. The subdomains of these membranes have been shown to be involved in a variety of phenomena, such as virus-host cell fusion, intracellular transport, and viral exit from infected cells [59]. However, SARS-CoV-2 infection depends on the presence of cholesterol in the viral envelope, rather than the involvement of cholesterol-rich domains in host cells [59]. The results of GSEA showed that Coagulation, Androgen Response, Uv Response Up, Apoptosis, Estrogen Response Late, Oxidative Phosphorylation pathway were down-regulated. In patients with COVID-19, liver dysfunction and coagulation dysfunction are often observed at the same time. Impaired coagulation function, liver dysfunction and COVID-19 have been confirmed to be related. The mechanism may be that COVID-19 infection leads to immune occurrence or activation of a large number of pericytes, resulting in vascular wall fibrosis [60]. TMPRSS2 is required for the COVID-19 replication cycle. Estrogen and androgen receptor-dependent cues converge on the regulation of TMPRSS2 through different mechanisms of action. These mechanisms may be blocked by the use of hormone therapy to combat COVID-19. This hypothesis has been verified in the cohort of breast cancer and prostate cancer patients [61]. Data collected so far suggest that environmental levels of Ultraviolet-A and Ultraviolet-B may be beneficial in reducing the severity or mortality of COVID-19 [62]. Apoptosis also plays an important role in COVID-19. SARS-CoV-2 spike protein promotes autophagy by up-regulating intracellular reactive oxygen species and inhibiting PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, which ultimately leads to inflammatory response and apoptosis of infected cells, which is of great significance for the development of antiviral therapy for patients with COVID-19 [63]. A postmortem study of COVID-19 revealed metabolic disorders of glucose and fatty acids in the liver. Specifically, glycogenolysis, galactose degradation and glycolysis were inhibited, while fatty acid oxidation and oxidative phosphorylation were activated, suggesting that the SARS-CoV-2 protein may reconnect specific metabolic pathways in the human liver during its life cycle [64]. Furthermore, interactions between viral proteins and oxidative phosphorylation imply that viral factors may directly regulate cellular respiration and carbon flow [64]. The results of enrichment analyses prompted possible shared biological mechanisms in COVID-19 and HCC. The shared pathogenesis between COVID-19 and HCC may involve four aspects: immunity, metabolism, tumor and tumor microenvironment. The proportion of immune cells and the activity of metabolic pathways were generally down-regulated in liver cancer cells after SARS-CoV-2 infection. Mismatch Repair, HLA signature related genes were up-regulated in the tumour microenvironment, however Interleukins Receptor, Th1 cells related genes were down-regulated. Exosomes may play an important role between COVID-19 and HCC.

Subsequently, we used the WGCNA algorithm to perform co-expression clustering analyses on the GSE167336 dataset and selected an intersection of the most closely related modular genes, which are shared genes that we believe may be associated with both diseases. Additionally the GSE151803 and TCGA-LIHC datasets were used as validation data and DEGs were calculated for both datasets, ultimately identifying IGSF1 as a core gene for COVID-19 and HCC by Venn diagram.

The immunoglobulin superfamily (IGSF) adhesion molecules are among the most abundant proteins in the vertebrate and invertebrate nervous system [65]. IGSF includes hundreds of cell surface proteins containing multiple immunoglobulin-like (IG) domains involved in various cellular functions [66]. The external domains of IGSF members form rod-like structures and specifically bind other proteins, making them ideal for cell surface receptors and cell adhesion molecules, and are involved in a variety of binding interactions [67, 68]. The dysregulation of IGSF gene expression is associated with tumorigenesis [69, 70]. The IGSF1 gene encodes the plasma membrane glycoprotein, and its abnormalities cause different symptoms, including congenital central hypothyroidism, prolactin insufficiency, testicular enlargement and delayed puberty [71]. However, few studies have focused on the relationship between IGSF1 gene and pan-tissues. We then analyzed it in order to fill the gap in this research field.

In this study, we performed a comprehensive bioinformatics analysis of IGSF1 using multiple public databases. IGSF1 gene expression has been associated with a variety of immunity, metabolism, tumour and tumour microenvironment-related pathways, such as Central memory CD4 T cell, Nicotinamide Adenine Metabolism, Cytokines, Ferroptosis, etc. IGSF1 is widely expressed in human tumour tissues, normal tissues and tumour cell lines and exhibits tissue specificity. For different cancer types, IGSF1 gene expression was up-regulated in LIHC,LUAD,LUSC. IGSF1 gene expression was high in normal tissues such as Pituitary, Testis, Nerve, Thyroid, Fallopian Tube and cancer cell lines such as Autonomic Ganglia, Liver, Ovary, Oesophagus, Thyroid. According to the expression level of IGSF1 gene, combined with a variety of survival indicators, the prognostic value of IGSF1 gene in pan-cancer data was explored. The results showed that IGSF1 may be used as a potential prognostic biomarker for a variety of tumors, especially STAD and BRCA. Our results also demonstrated that the IGSF1 gene was present in SKCM and UCEC with a high SNV percentage, while KIRP had the lowest SNV percentage. Missense mutation occurs most frequently, SNP is the main variant type, C > T is the most common SNV class. Besides, the level of IGSF1 expression was positively correlated with CNV in PRAD and LGG, while it was negatively correlated in KIRP, KICH. Methylation levels and IGSF1 expression were negatively correlated in most cancer types. Heterozygous amplification and heterozygous deletion were the main types of CNV, with the largest proportion of heterozygous amplification in ACC and heterozygous deletion in KICH. Moreover, we have explored the immune function of IGSF1 gene in cancer. In most cancers, Monocytes, T cells CD4 memory resting were positively correlated with IGSF1 expression, while B cells memory, NK cells activated, T cells CD8, T cells regulatory Tregs were negatively correlated with IGSF1 expression in most cancers. Immunity scores and ESTIMATE scores were negatively correlated with IGSF1 expression in most cancer types. By studying the relationship between IGSF1 gene expression and the expression of ICGs, chemokines and their receptor proteins, immunostimulatory factors, and immunosuppressant-related genes, we propose that IGSF1 plays a subtle role in tumourigenesis or progression and may influence the efficacy of immunotherapy to some extent. By jointly analysing the correlation between immune infiltration and SNV, CNV, methylation, we dug deeper into the mechanism of IGSF1 gene in tumours. Differences in immune infiltration between mutant and wild type in UCEC and COAD were significant, especially in Exhausted. There is a broad correlation between CNV, methylation and immune infiltration in different cancer types. For the correlation between CNV and immune infiltration in different cancer types, there was a positive correlation between CD8 T(KICH), NK(KICH), CD8 naive(ACC),Gamma delta(ACC) and CNV, while there was a negative correlation between NKT(THYM) and CNV. Furthermore, methylation in THYM was highly correlated with immune infiltration. For the pathways involved in the Hallmark gene sets, IGSF1 was positively associated with most of them in the tumour samples, while a large number of negatively associated pathways were present in MESO and TGCT. The results of the analyses in the GTEx and CCLE datasets show that the function of IGSF1 is differently distributed in different normal tissues and cancer cell lines. In addition, the correlation between IGSF1 expression and pathways in Hallmark gene sets was calculated. The three pathways of KRAS SIGNALING DN, UV RESPONSE DN and MYOGENESIS are positively correlated with IGSF1 in most tumor types. Three pathways, KRAS SIGNALING DN, INTERFERON ALPHA RESPONSE and P53 PATHWAY, are negatively associated with IGSF1 in most normal tissues. The KRAS SIGNALING DN, P53 PATHWAY and PEROXISOME pathways are positively associated with IGSF1 in some cancer cell lines. These data indicated that IGSF1 gene expression may be highly correlated with the activity of the KRAS SIGNALING DN pathway. Finally, we analysed the drugs associated with IGSF1 and found that IGSF1 was strongly associated with the sensitivity to a variety of chemotherapeutic agents. These results suggest that IGSF1 is a biologically important target in a wide range of tumours.

However, this study has some limitations. First of all, our research data come from public databases, which may be limited by the insufficient number of research samples or introduce errors in statistical methods. In addition, we cannot accurately say that the strong association of IGSF1 gene in pan-cancer analysis is applicable in every cancer. Finally, there is a lack of additional clinical and experimental data to further verify the specific mechanism of IGSF1 in COVID-19 and HCC. In the future, more studies are needed to confirm our findings and promote the transformation of research results into clinical applications.

Conclusion

In this project, we identified the IGSF1 gene as a shared core gene for COVID-19 combined with HCC and explored the common biological pathways and pathogenesis of the two diseases. We then analysed the IGSF1 gene in pan-tissue data in terms of expression, survival, gene alteration, immunity, and drug sensitivity. In conclusion, this study provides new insights to further explore the molecular mechanisms of COVID-19 combined with HCC and to understand the functional expression of the IGSF1 gene in pan-tissues.

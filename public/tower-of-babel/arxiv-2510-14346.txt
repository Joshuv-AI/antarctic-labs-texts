Sobolev regularity for the perturbed fractional 1-Laplace equations in the subquadratic case Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Dingding Li and Chao Zhang ∗

Abstract
. This work investigates the Sobolev regularity of solutions to perturbed fractional 1-Laplace equations. Under the assumption that weak solutions are locally bounded, we establish that the regularity properties are analogous to those observed in the superquadratic case. By introducing the threshold p − 1 p \frac{p-1}{p} , we divide the range of the parameter s p s_{p} into two distinct scenarios. Specifically, for any s p ∈ ( 0 , p − 1 p ] s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right] and q ≥ p q\geq p , we demonstrate that the solutions possess W loc γ , q W_{\rm loc}^{\gamma,q} -regularity for all γ ∈ ( 0 , s p ​ p p − 1 ) \gamma\in\left(0,\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}\right) and the W loc 1 , q W_{\rm loc}^{1,q} -regularity for any s p ∈ ( p − 1 p , 1 ) s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right) and q ≥ p q\geq p , respectively. Our analysis relies on the nonlocal finite-difference quotient method combined with a Moser-type iteration scheme, which provides a systematic approach to the regularity theory for such nonlocal and singular problems.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
. The main focus of this paper is to study the Sobolev regularity of weak solutions to the following perturbed fractional
1
-Laplace equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{1})^{s_{1}}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s_{p}}u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1)

where
s_{1},s_{p}\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,2)
. The fractional operators
(-\Delta_{1})^{s_{1}}
and
(-\Delta_{p})^{s_{p}}
are defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{1})^{s_{1}}u(x):=2\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s_{1}}}

and

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{p})^{s_{p}}u(x):=2\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{p}p}}\,dy

for
x\in\Omega
. Additionally,
\mathrm{P.V.}
denotes the integral taken in the principal value sense.

The problem (
1.1
) was first proposed by Górny, Mazón and Toledo in their recent work
[
22
]
, in which they investigated several phenomena of nonlocal PDEs with inhomogeneous growth within the general framework of random walk spaces. Due to the associated functionals of these
(1,p)
-Laplace equations exhibiting different growth on their respective structures, Eq. (
1.1
) can be regarded as a special case of
(p,q)
-growth problems. For the superlinear case
p>1
, several significant results have been established; see the pioneering studies
[
25
,
26
]
, subsequent advances
[
3
,
11
,
27
,
32
,
12
]
, and the references therein.

Let us first focus on the classical version of problem (
1.1
), namely the perturbed 1-Laplace equation

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)-\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=f\quad\text{in }\Omega.

(1.2)

This equation arises in both fluid mechanics
[
16
]
and materials science
[
34
]
. Further assuming that the weak solutions to (
1.2
) are convex, Giga and Tsubouchi
[
19
]
proved that the gradient of solutions is continuous. Shortly thereafter, Tsubouchi removed the convexity condition and established the interior
C^{1}
-regularity in
[
35
]
using De Giorgi’s truncation method together with the freezing-coefficient technique. It is also worth noting that these methods were subsequently extended to show that
Du
is continuous with respect to
(x,t)
for the parabolic
(1,p)
-Laplace system
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
. Related problems have also been studied in more specialized settings; see, for example,
[
21
,
31
]
for results on the anisotropic case involving the
p
-Laplacian, where the operator exhibits linear growth in some of the coordinates. Moreover, in sharp contrast to that in the
(1,p)
-growth regime, De Filippis and Mingione in
[
13
]
considered the functional with nearly linear growth

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(w,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\left[c(x)|Dw|\log(1+|Dw|)+a(x)|Dw|^{q}\right]\,dx,\quad q>1,

(1.3)

and established the Schauder-type estimates for its minimizers.

When addressing fractional problems involving the 1-Laplacian, several key features have been summarized in
[
23
, Subsection 1.1]
as follows:

•

In the weak formulation, the nonlocal quotient
\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}
is represented by a function
Z
, where
Z\in\mathrm{sgn}(u(x)-u(y))
. Here
\mathrm{sgn}(\cdot)
denotes the set-valued sign function with
\mathrm{sgn}(0)=[-1,1]
.

•

The energy of weak solutions to elliptic nonlocal 1-Laplace equations associated with the source term
f
can be discontinuous. Specifically, if
f\in L^{\frac{N}{s}}(\Omega)
and satisfies a smallness condition, the energy of weak solutions remains bounded. However, if
\|f\|_{L^{\frac{N}{s}}(\Omega)}
is sufficiently large, the energy becomes unbounded and a weak solution may fail to exist.

•

Any
s
-minimal functions are continuous in the interior of the ambient domain and also continuous up to the boundary under some mild additional
hypothesis.

•

In two dimensions, for a nonlocal variational problem arising from an image-denoising model, its minimizers preserve the same local Hölder regularity as the original image.

For more detailed results on the 1-Laplacian, see
[
29
,
28
,
1
,
2
,
30
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
33
]
,
in which the distinctive properties are thoroughly discussed. These characteristics of the 1-Laplacian ultimately originate from the non-strict convexity of the corresponding space.

Next, we turn to our previous work
[
23
]
, where we considered the superquadratic case
p\geq 2
and distinguished two ranges for
s_{p}
:

\displaystyle s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right).

From a technical perspective, leveraging the development of the finite-difference quotient technique in the fractional setting (see
[
6
,
7
,
4
,
5
,
17
,
14
,
18
]
), we established the local Sobolev regularity of weak solutions to (
1.1
) in the supercritical case. For background on this technique, we refer the readers to
[
24
]
, which outlines its fundamental applications and historical context. It is noteworthy that the critical threshold
\frac{p-1}{p}
characterizes the existence of
\nabla u
, differing from that of the fractional
p
-Laplacian, where the threshold is
\frac{p-2}{p}
(see
[
5
]
). Our results indicate that the involvement of the 1-structure raises this threshold.

Inspired by the aforementioned literature, we in this paper investigate the regularity of weak solutions to (
1.1
) in the subquadratic case. Our conclusions align closely with those in
[
23
]
; when the 1-structure is omitted, our results further consistent those in
[
18
]
. However, under certain conditions, the presence of the 1-structure can prevent the existence of
\nabla u
, in contrast to the fractional
p
-Laplacian setting, where the gradient always exists (see
[
4
]
). This distinction highlights the impact of the 1-growth term on such problems. We further observe that, unlike in the superquadratic case, the local regularity estimates for weak solutions in the subquadratic regime depend on more parameters. This is primarily due to the fact that we adopt a different approach to derive the energy estimate of weak solutions, see Proposition
3.3
for detailed arguments.

Before presenting our main results, we first provide the definition of a weak solution to (
1.1
). The tail space
L^{p-1}_{s_{p}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
contains all function
w\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
that satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|w|^{p-1}}{(1+|x|)^{N+s_{p}p}}\,dx<+\infty.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in W^{s_{1},1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{p},p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{s_{p}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is said to be a weak solution to problem (
1.1
) if

(i)

there exists a function
Z\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
Z\in\mathrm{sgn}(u(x)-u(y))
;

(ii)

for any
\varphi\in W^{s_{1},1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{p},p}_{0}(\Omega)
, there holds

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Z\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{N+s_{1}}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{p}p}}\,dxdy.

(1.4)

Remark 1.2
.

No additional decay assumption is required for the 1-growth term because the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{dy}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+s_{1}}}

(1.5)

is finite.
Moreover, the constant “1” appearing in our main results follows directly from (
1.5
).
The existence of weak solutions to problem (
1.1
) can be established via the method developed in
[
8
]
, and the local boundedness of weak solutions has been proved in
[
23
, Section 3]
.

Now, we state our main results. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R),

where
\operatorname{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)
denotes the tail term associated with the function
u
at the point
x_{0}
and radius
R
(see Section
2
for details).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
and
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
. Suppose that
u
is a locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
). Then, we have

\displaystyle u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\sigma,q}(\Omega)

for any
q\geq p
and
s_{p}\leq\sigma<\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}
. Moreover, there exist constants

C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,q,s_{1},s_{p},\sigma

such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{\sigma,q}\left(B_{r}\right)}^{q}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}\left(B_{R}\right)}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
and
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
. Suppose that
u
is a locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
). Then, we have

\displaystyle u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,q}(\Omega),

for any
q\geq p
. Moreover, there exist constants
C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,q,s_{1},s_{p},
such that for
any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}\left(B_{r}\right)}^{q}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}\left(B_{R}\right)}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

By applying the Morrey-type embedding for (fractional) Sobolev spaces and carefully selecting the relevant parameters, one can directly establish the interior Hölder regularity of weak solutions.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
and
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
. Then, for any locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}\right)
. Moreover, there exist constants
C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,s_{1},s_{p}
, such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
and
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
. Then, for any locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Moreover, there exist constants
C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,s_{1},s_{p},\gamma
, such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

Remark 1.7
.

In our iterative scheme, we define the following sequence to track the evolution of weak differentiability:

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=\gamma,\quad\gamma_{i+1}=\gamma_{i}\left(1-\frac{p^{2}}{2q}+\frac{p}{2q}\right)+\frac{s_{p}p^{2}}{2q}.

For the case
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{i\rightarrow+\infty}\gamma_{i}=\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}.

For the case
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
, since
\gamma_{i}
may exceed 1, the limitations of the difference-quotient technique prevent us from iterating the first- and second-order differences indefinitely.

Provided that the gradient of weak solutions exists, one can also establish the Sobolev regularity of

as follows.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
q\geq 2
,
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
and let

be a locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle u\in W^{1+\alpha,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for any
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{p}{2q}(s_{p}p-p+1)\right)
. Moreover, there exist constants
C
and
\kappa
depending on
N,p,s_{1},s_{p},\alpha
, such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,1)
and any
r\in(0,R)
,

\displaystyle[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the notations and several auxiliary lemmas that will be employed in the proofs of main results.

2.1.
Notations

For a vector
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we denote its Euclidean norm by
|z|
. Throughout the manuscript, the symbol
C
stands for a positive constant whose value may vary from one occurrence to another, possibly even within the same line. Whenever relevant, we explicitly specify its dependence on parameters, for example, a constant depending on
N,p,s_{1},s_{p}
is denoted by
C(N,p,s_{1},s_{p})
.

For a function
w:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, if
w\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and for every ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
, we write
w\in C^{0,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. The seminorm of this Hölder space is defined by

\displaystyle[w]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}:=\sup\limits_{x,y\in B_{R}(x_{0}),x\neq y}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

Next, we introduce the (fractional) Sobolev space. For
q\geq 1
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the space
W^{1,q}(\Omega)
and
W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega)
are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\left\{u\in L^{q}(\Omega)\bigg|\|u\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}<+\infty\right\}

and

\displaystyle\left\{u\in L^{q}(\Omega)\bigg|[u]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+\gamma q}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<+\infty\right\}.

We define
\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)
to indicate the nonlocal information of
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R):=\left(R^{s_{p}p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+s_{p}p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Algebraic inequalities.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

4.
Sobolev regularity

In this section, we will prove our main results by considering two distinct cases, i.e.,

(i)

For the case
s_{p}\in\left(0,\frac{p-1}{p}\right]
, we show that the fractional differentiability index can be improved to any value less than
\frac{s_{p}p}{p-1}
.

(ii)

For the case

, we prove that

is almost locally bounded.

Unlike the approach used in
[
23
]
, we devote only limited effort to raising the integrability from

imposes no restriction on

4.1.

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\left|\tau_{h}u\right|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{q}\left[\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{\tilde{r}})}+1\right)^{q}}{\left(\tilde{r}-r\right)^{N+2q+1}}+\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\tilde{r}}\right)}^{q}}{\left(\frac{\tilde{r}-r}{2}\right)^{q}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C|h|^{q}\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}\left(B_{r}\right)}+1\right)^{q}}{(\tilde{r}-r)^{N+2q+1}}.

(4.17)

After a direct application of Lemma
2.10
, one concludes that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}}\left(B_{r}\right)+1\right)^{q}}{(\tilde{r}-r)^{N+2q+1}}.

(4.18)

Bringing (
4.2
) into (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{Br}|\nabla u|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+\frac{\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}\left(B_{R}\right)}+1\right)}{\left(R-\tilde{r}\right)^{\frac{\tilde{\kappa}}{q}}}+1\right)^{q}}{\left(\tilde{r}-r\right)^{N+2q+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}\left(B_{R}\right)}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa}},

where
\kappa:=\tilde{\kappa}+N+2q+1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

From Theorem
1.4
, we know that the locally bounded weak solution
u\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for any

, provide that
p\in(1,2)
and

with the parameter

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r})}=[u]_{C^{0,1-\frac{N}{q}}(B_{r})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)}{(R-r)^{\kappa}}.

∎

Finally, we derive the Sobolev regularity of the gradient of weak solutions.
To this end, we first present a useful lemma that provides a new formulation of Proposition
3.3
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
,
s_{1}\in(0,1)
,
s_{p}\in\left(\frac{p-1}{p},1\right)
and
q\geq p
. There exists a constant
C
depending on
N,p,q,s_{1},s_{p},\sigma
such that for any
u\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
being a locally bounded weak solution to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
, we have, for any
R\in(0,1)
,
r\in(0,R)
,
d\in\left(\frac{1}{4}(R-r)\right]
,
B_{R+d}\equiv B_{R+d}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
,
h\in\overline{B_{d}}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
and
\sigma\in(0,p)
,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\frac{\left|J_{\frac{q}{p}+1}\left(\tau_{h}u(x)\right)-J_{\frac{q}{p}+1}\left(\tau_{h}u(y)\right)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+\frac{s_{p}p^{2}}{2}+\sigma\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C}{(R-r)^{N+s_{p}p+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left[\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\right]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q-p+1}\,dx+\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q}\,dx+|h|\mathcal{T}^{p-1}\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q-p+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d}(x_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R+d).

Proof.

After a direct application of Lemma
2.3
to the inequality obtained in Proposition
3.3
, we derive the desired result. More precisely, we set
\gamma:=\frac{p+\sigma}{2p}
in Proposition
3.3
and take (
2.1
) into consideration to have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\frac{\left|J_{\frac{q}{p}+1}\left(\tau_{h}u(x)\right)-J_{\frac{q}{p}+1}\left(\tau_{h}u(y)\right)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+\frac{s_{p}p^{2}}{2}+\sigma\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C}{(R-r)^{N+s_{p}p+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left[[u]^{p}_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{r})}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\right]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q-p+1}\,dx+\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q}\,dx+|h|\mathcal{T}^{p-1}\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q-p+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C}{(R-r)^{N+s_{p}p+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left[\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\right]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q-p+1}\,dx+\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q}\,dx+|h|\mathcal{T}^{p-1}\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{h}u|^{q-p+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
C
takes the form of

\displaystyle C=\frac{C(N,p,q,s_{1},s_{p})}{(p-\sigma)^{\frac{q-p}{q}+\frac{p}{2}}}.

∎

Thanks to Lemma
2.11
, which establishes the relationship between second-order differences and Sobolev regularity, we can show the regularity of the gradient of weak solutions.

Proof of Corollary
1.8
.

We set
\widetilde{R}:=\frac{R+r}{2}
,
\tilde{r}:=\frac{1}{3}r+\frac{2}{3}\widetilde{R}
and
d:=\frac{1}{6}(\widetilde{R}-r)
. Applying Lemmas
2.10
and
2.1
and following the method used in proof of Lemma
4.1
, we can deduce an inequality similar to (
4.5
), that is,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{r}}}\left|\tau_{h}\left(\tau_{h}u\right)\right|^{q}dx\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{\widetilde{R}})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{N+2q+1}}|h|^{\left(q-\frac{p}{2}\right)+\sigma\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)+\frac{s_{p}p^{2}}{2}}.

Choosing
\sigma:=\frac{\left[\alpha+\frac{p}{2q}(s_{p}p-p+1)\right]+p-s_{p}p^{2}}{2-p}<p
in the previous inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{r}}}\left|\tau_{h}\left(\tau_{h}u\right)\right|^{q}dx\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{\widetilde{R}})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{N+2q+1}}|h|^{\tilde{\alpha}q+q}

with
\tilde{\alpha}:=\frac{\alpha+\frac{p}{2q}(s_{p}p-p+1)}{2}
. As a result of application of Lemma
2.11
, we conclude

\displaystyle\nabla u\in\left(W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})\right)^{N}

for any
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{p}{2q}(s_{p}p-p+1)\right)
and there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,q,s_{1},s_{p},\alpha)
such that

\displaystyle[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\frac{\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{\widetilde{R}})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{N+s_{p}p+1}}+\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{\widetilde{R}})}}{(R-r)^{\left(\frac{p}{2q}(s_{p}p-p+1)+1\right)q}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{\widetilde{R}})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{N+2q+2}}.

By Theorem

\displaystyle[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\left(\mathcal{T}+[u]_{W^{s_{p},p}(B_{R})}+1\right)^{q}}{(R-r)^{\kappa}},

which completes the proof.

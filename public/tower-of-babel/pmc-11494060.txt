Comparative analysis of SARC-F-EBM, Ishii test, and six other screening tools for sarcopenia in Chinese community-dwelling older adults: a cross-sectional diagnostic study
Authors: Qian Guo, Qifeng Chen, Kangkang Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo assess the relative performance of simple screening methods for sarcopenia in Chinese community-dwelling older adults. Data of older adults aged ≥ 60 were collected through a cross-sectional investigation. Sarcopenia was defined according to the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia 2019 criteria. The accuracy of screening methods was evaluated using sensitivity, specificity, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and area under the ROC curves (AUC). The AUC value greater than 0.8 represented the good screening ability. A total of 918 older adults (44.3% men, mean age 70.4 ± 6.5 years) were included in this study. The overall prevalence rates of possible sarcopenia, confirmed sarcopenia, and severe sarcopenia were 59.5%, 12.8%, and 5.9%, respectively. In men, the SARC-F-EBM and Ishii tests indicated good screening capabilities for confirmed sarcopenia, with an AUC of 0.81 (95% CI: 0.77–0.85) and 0.80 (95% CI: 0.76–0.84), respectively. In women, the highest AUC was also achieved using the SARC-F-EBM at 0.79 (95% CI: 0.75–0.82), followed by the Ishii test at 0.77 (95% CI: 0.74–0.81), showing the moderate efficacy. A ranking diagram showed that SARC-F-EBM was most likely to be considered the best method for diagnosing sarcopenia in terms of AUC and sensitivity, regardless of sex. Introduction

Sarcopenia is defined as the age-associated loss of muscle mass and function according to the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) 20191. Sarcopenic individuals are more vulnerable to adverse health consequences, including mobility disorders2, chronic disease states3,4, reduced quality of life5, and even deaths6. Recent estimates suggested that, potentially due to rapid aging and unique socio-economic factors, up to 9.2-16.2% of the population in China is affected7. Given that sarcopenia can be delayed and prevented8, early identification in older adults is essential to slow down its progression and reduce the substantial healthcare burden.

However, detecting sarcopenia in its early phase is challenging due to the absence of noticeable symptoms and signs; severe health events such as falls and physical disability typically manifests only in the later stages9. Moreover, the gold standard methods for assessing low skeletal muscle mass, including dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) and bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA)10, are limited by high costs, low portability, and the requirement for specialized personnel. These factors collectively make it difficult to identify sarcopenia early in community setting. As attention has been paid to this problem, the AWGS 2019 recommends for sarcopenia screening using calf circumference (CC), SARC-F questionnaire, and SARC-CalF1. In contrast, the European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People (EWGSOP2) endorses the use of SARC-F and the Ishii test11. The variability in recommended screening tools by different scientific groups suggests significant geographical and ethnic differences in their effectiveness. For example, Lin et al.12. showed that SARC-CalF’s sensitivity was 76.5% among Chinese community-dwelling older adults. Conversely, Bahat et al.13. reported that the same tool had a considerably lower sensitivity of 25.0% in identifying sarcopenic individuals in Turkey. This discrepancy underscores the urgent need for validating existing screening tools across diverse races, research settings, diagnostic standards and optimal cutoff values, and possibly seeking ones that are culturally and regionally appropriate.

In response to this need, the current study evaluates the diagnostic performance of various sarcopenia screening tools within a dataset specifically comprising Chinese community-dwelling older adults. Through this comparative analysis, we aim to identify the most effective screening method tailored to this demographic, thus providing critical insights that could enhance screening practices for this population.

Methods

Study participants

From 21 November to 5 December 2022, we conducted a cross-sectional investigation in Ma’an, a town within Shaoxing city, Zhejiang Province, China. In 2022, Ma’an had a population of 39,351, approximately 26% of whom were older adults (aged ≥ 60 years). The town administered five communities and 11 administrative villages. For this study, we employed a cluster sampling method, randomly selecting three administrative villages—Guoqing, Siqiao, and Tongjiata—as our research sites. We included all community-dwelling older adults aged ≥ 60 who had attended a local government basic health checkup from March to October 2022. Participants’ basic information, including name, ID card number, age, sex, and home address, was extracted from health checkup records on 12 November 2022. Additional data, including multiple scales, anthropometric measurements, physical performance and sarcopenia status, were collected through face-to-face investigation by trained researchers. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) participants who had implanted electronic devices and metal implants; (2) participants unable to stand or walk independently; and (3) participants with language or hearing impairments, cognitive impairments (as reported by accompanying family members), or those unable to accurately provide basic information. The study protocol was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of our center, and all subjects provided written informed consent.

The screening tools

The screening tool assessed in our study were as follows: (1) SARC-F is a self-reported questionnaire, evaluating 5 items regarding strength, assistance with walking, rise from a chair, climb stairs and falls14. Each item scores from 0 to 2 points, with a maximum of 10 points in total; (2) SARC-F-EBM is a newly modified version of the SARC-F questionnaire by adding age and BMI15. If age is ≥ 75 years, 10 points are given, otherwise 0 point is given; if BMI is ≤ 21 kg/m2, 10 points are given, otherwise 0 point is given. The total score ranges from 0 to 30; (3) SARC-CalF is another modified version of the SARC-F questionnaire that incorporates CC measurement16. According to the CC measurement protocol recommended by AWGS 20191, we used a nonelastic tape to measure the largest part of each calf once and recorded the larger value. The cutoff points for CC are set at < 34 cm for men and < 33 cm for women. If the CC is less than the cutoff points, 10 points are given; otherwise, 0 point is given. The total score ranges from 0 to 20; (4) Ishii test consists of three variables: age, CC, and handgrip strength (HS)17. For men, Ishii score = 0.62 × (age – 64) – 3.09 × (HS – 50) – 4.64 × (CC – 42); for women, Ishii score = 0.80 × (age – 64) – 5.09 × (HS – 34) – 3.28 × (CC – 42); (5) Frailty has been shown to be associated with sarcopenia. We used the “FRAIL” scale, developed by the Geriatric Advisory Panel of the International Academy of Nutrition and Aging, to measure it18. This scale includes five components: fatigue, resistance, ambulation, illness, and loss of weight. Each item scored from 0 to 1 point, with a maximum of 5 points in total; (6) CC, HS and gait speed (GS). The best cut-off values of each of these tools were based on our population characteristics rather than the criteria put forward by the original studies.

Muscle strength measurement

The present study used a digital handgrip dynamometer (EH101; Xiangshan Inc, Guangdong, China) to measure HS. According to the HS measurement protocol recommended by AWGS 20191, participants were asked to stand upright with elbows fully extended, and arms parallel to their bodies. After the researchers giving the instruction, each participant started to squeeze the handle with maximum effort using dominant hand. We repeated the test 3 times, with the higher value recorded as the final result. HS < 28 kg for men and < 18 kg for women are regarded as the decreased muscle strength.

Physical performance measurement

Usual GS was adopted to assess muscle function. On the basis of the GS measurement protocol recommended by AWGS 20191, all participants were asked to walk over a distance of 6 m at a normal pace. A 6-m GS is equal to 6 m divided by the recorded time. The participants performed 2 trials, and the average value was calculated as the recorded speed. Reduced muscle function is defined as GS < 1m/s.

Muscle mass measurement

A standing BIA device (MC-780MA; TANITA Inc, Japan) was used for measuring muscle mass. Participants were asked to remove socks and stand on the device’s platform, positioning electrodes under their feet and in their hands. Once correctly positioned, a safe electrical current was passed through their body to measure impedance. This measurement was then used to estimate skeletal muscle mass. According to the AWGS 20191, the sum of the appendicular skeletal muscle (ASM) was normalized by the square of the height (m). A decrease in muscle mass is defined as < 7.0 kg/m2 in men and < 5.7 kg/m2 in women.

Diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia

As the older adults in our study recruited from China, sarcopenia was diagnosed using the AWGS 2019 algorithm1, which classifies the condition into possible sarcopenia, confirmed sarcopenia, and severe sarcopenia. Possible sarcopenia is defined by either low muscle strength or low physical performance. Confirmed sarcopenia requires low ASM in addition to either low muscle strength or low physical performance. Severe sarcopenia is characterized by low ASM, low muscle strength, and low physical performance.

Statistical analysis

The baseline characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. If continuous variables comply with normal distribution, they were presented as means ± standard deviation, otherwise the median (interquartile range) was used. Categorical variables were presented as numbers (percentages). Differences between men and women were assessed using the independent t test, Wilcoxon rank sum test, chi-square test, and Fisher exact test as appropriate. The sensitivity, specificity, positive likelihood ratio (+ LR), and negative likelihood ratio (-LR) of the aforementioned tools were calculated for identifying sarcopenia according to the AWGS 2019 criteria. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to ascertain the screening accuracy of each tool, with the area under the ROC curve (AUC) greater than 0.8 reflecting good screening ability. Differences across the ROC curves were assessed using the DeLong method19, which statistically compares the areas under two correlated ROC curves to evaluate the significance of their differences in diagnostic performance. We also summarize the ranking of the all competing screening strategies in terms of AUC and sensitivity to explore the optimal screening tool. Analyses were performed using the statistical software SPSS v20.0 (SPSS Statistics; IBM, Armonk, NY) and MedCalc v 19.6.1 (MedCalc Software bvba, Ostend, Belgium). All tests were 2-tailed, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Characteristics of participants

A total of 918 participants were included in this study. Of these, the mean age was 70.4 ± 6.5, and the proportion of men was 44.3%. The other detailed information is presented in Table 1. On the basis of AWGS 2019, the prevalence rates of possible sarcopenia, sarcopenia, and severe sarcopenia in total study participants were 59.5%, 12.8%, and 5.9%, respectively (Fig. 1). The results between men and women were not significant.

Accuracy of screening tools for sarcopenia

In men, the cutoff points for screening tools in our study were established as follows: SARC-F (≥ 2), FRAIL (> 1), CC (≤ 36), HS (≤ 27.5), GS (≤ 0.9), SARC-CalF (> 3), SARC-F-EBM (> 6), and Ishii test (> 107.9) (see Table 2). The sensitivity and specificity of the SARC-F-EBM achieved 87.0% (95% CI: 75.1–94.6) and 65.7% (95% CI: 60.5–70.7), respectively. Similarly, the Ishii test exhibited a sensitivity of 86.4% (95% CI: 74.8–94.1) and a specificity of 66.4% (95% CI: 61.2–71.3). Both the SARC-F-EBM and Ishii test demonstrated good screening capabilities, with an AUC of 0.81 (95% CI: 0.77–0.85) and 0.80 (95% CI: 0.76–0.84), respectively. The comparison between the two ROC curves revealed no statistically significant differences (P = 0.7) (see Fig. 2A).

Accuracy of screening tools for severe sarcopenia

In our study, cutoff points for men screening tools were set as follows: SARC-F (≥ 2), FRAIL (> 1), CC (≤ 36), HS (≤ 27.3), GS (≤ 0.9), SARC-CalF (> 3), SARC-F-EBM (> 6), and the Ishii test (> 108.2) (Table 2). The SARC-F-EBM, GS, Ishii test, and HS demonstrated good screening capabilities, with AUC values of 0.88 (95% CI: 0.84–0.91), 0.87 (95% CI: 0.83–0.90), 0.84 (95% CI: 0.80–0.88), and 0.83 (95% CI: 0.79–0.86), respectively, and no significant differences across ROC curves (P > 0.05) (Fig. S1A). For females, cutoffs were SARC-F (> 3), FRAIL (> 1), CC (≤ 34), HS (≤ 16.6), GS (≤ 1.0), SARC-CalF (> 3), SARC-F-EBM (> 3), and the Ishii test (> 129.3) (Table 3). Similar screening efficacy was noted, with AUC values for the Ishii test, SARC-F-EBM, HS, and GS at 0.87 (95% CI: 0.84–0.90), 0.84 (95% CI: 0.80–0.87), 0.84 (95% CI: 0.80–0.87), and 0.83 (95% CI: 0.80–0.86), respectively, without significant ROC differences (P > 0.05) (Fig. S1B). Given the high AUC of half the screening tools in detecting severe sarcopenia, these tools were not ranked.

Discussion

The aim of the study was to assess the comparative accuracy of several simple tools for sarcopenia screening in community-dwelling older adults. The results showed that SARC-F-EBM was most likely to be ranked the best in terms of AUC and sensitivity, regardless of sex. When only severe sarcopenia was considered, most of the existing tools indicated good predictive ability.

SARC-F-EBM, devised by Kurita et al.15. in 2019, consists of three parts: SARC-F, age and BMI, which is a newly modified version of the SARC-F questionnaire. Kurita et al.15. reported that the sensitivity, specificity and AUC of the SARC-F-EBM were 0.78, 0.70 and 0.82, respectively, and its screening performance was superior to the SARC-F questionnaire alone. Of note, the evidence was based solely on the patients with musculoskeletal diseases. Thus, its external validity needs further assessment. One study conducted by Chen et al.20. in schizophrenic patients showed that the sensitivity and AUC of the SARC-F-EBM were low. It is possible that screening accuracy of self-reported questionnaires such as SARC-F and its modification can be affected by participants’ cognitive function, and therefore schizophrenic patients may not be suitable for SARC-F-EBM. So far, there have been few studies investigating the role of SARC-F-EBM in predicting sarcopenia. Our study found that SARC-F-EBM had the highest probability to be the most favorable screening tool in terms of the sensitivity and AUC in community-dwelling older adults, regardless of sex. When only SARC-F was examined, its sensitivity in our study was found to be low for men (46.3%) and women (21.9%), in line with previous studies21–23. Although SARC-F has been incorporated into both AWGS 2019 and EWGSOP2 diagnostic algorithm for case finding1,11, the poor sensitivity limits its use since most patients are overlooked. This drawback, as demonstrated by our study, was offset by the SARC-F-EBM, with a sensitivity of 87.0% for men and 82.8% for women. Additionally, age and BMI, as routine measurements, are also easily available. Therefore, the SARC-F-EBM may serve as the best screening tool for sarcopenia in older adults who are able to answer the questionnaire accurately.

Another useful tool for sarcopenia screening found in our study was the Ishii test. It was developed by Ishii and colleagues in 2014, covering age, CC, and HS17. Unlike SARC-F-EBM, the operation of the Ishii test is objective, so its predictive values will not be interfered by the subjectivity and understanding ability of the participants. In contrast, Ishii test is particularly suitable for people with cognitive impairment. The efficacy of Ishii test in identifying sarcopenia has been confirmed in several studies, with sensitivities ranging from 70.7 to 100.0%, specificities ranging from 73.4 to 92.0%, and AUCs ranging from 0.78 to 0.9620,24–27. The excellent discrimination ability of the Ishii test may be attributed to the significant correlation between the variables included in the model and the diagnostic components, the muscle strength in particular17. Nevertheless, HS is tool-dependent and not practical for older adults, which restrict the Ishii test to be applied in a broad population, especially in disadvantaged areas.

Compared with women, our study showed slightly higher sensitivity and AUC for the SARC-F-EBM scale in sarcopenia screening in men. It is well-documented that women tend to accumulate more fat mass in middle to late age, influenced by estrogen. This could affect the accuracy of BMI-based SARC-F-EBM, as BMI does not distinguish between fat and lean mass. Notably, the European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism (ESPEN) and the European Association for the Study of Obesity (EASO) have reached a new consensus that BMI should not be regarded as the tool in the diagnostic process of sarcopenic obesity (SO)—the coexistence of obesity and sarcopenia28. Consequently, SARC-F-EBM may also be less effective for individuals with higher adiposity, irrespective of gender. Additionally, our findings suggest higher sensitivity and AUC for the Ishii test in men than in women, likely due to women’s greater propensity for intermuscular and subcutaneous fat accumulation, which can obscure calf muscle measurements29. For example, Lim et al.30 showed that the diagnostic performance of CC for sarcopenia detection was attenuated in obesity compared with non-obesity. Thus, there is a need to develop a tool that can overcome the influence of adipose tissue on screening accuracy in obese participants, especially women.

This study has several strengths. Primarily, it assesses the effectiveness of various sarcopenia screening tools within the same population, providing a direct comparison of their diagnostic capabilities. This comparative analysis not only clarifies the relative performance of each method but also aids in identifying the most reliable tool for community use. Additionally, a review of epidemiological studies among Asian populations found that sarcopenia prevalence ranged from 7.3 to 12.0% in studies with a sample size greater than 1,0001. The prevalence observed in our study falls within this reported range, suggesting that our sample is representative and that the quality of result extrapolation is high. The present study has several limitations: (1) The measurement of skeletal muscle mass relies on BIA device and requires the older adults to stand independently, so some objectives such as bedridden patients were not included. Moreover, participation was voluntary and healthier volunteers were willing to take part in the project than the general population. Thus, selection bias may exist in our study, underestimating the actual prevalence of sarcopenia. (2) Some screening methods, such as mini sarcopenia risk assessment, were not evaluated in our study because the space of the questionnaire did not permit us to get into the specifics of all screening tools.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study suggested that SARC-F-EBM was most effective in identifying Chinese community-dwelling older adults with sarcopenia, distinguishing those currently affected from those who are not. Given the simplicity and applicability, we recommend the SARC-F-EBM for screening when respondents are able to answer the questionnaire accurately; otherwise, the Ishii test could be used. Further studies with large sample size are required before a robust conclusion can be drawn.

Seshadri constants and negative curves on blowups of ruled surfaces
Cyril J. Jacob, Bivas Khan, Ronnie Sebastian

Abstract
. In this article we compute Seshadri constants of ample line bundles on the blowup of Hirzebruch surface 𝔽 e \mathbb{F}_{e} at r ⩽ e + 3 r\leqslant e+3 very general points. Similarly, we compute Seshadri constants on the blowups of certain decomposable ruled surfaces over smooth curves of non-zero genus. We also prove some results related to bounded negativity of blowups of Hirzebruch surfaces and ruled surfaces.

14C20, 14E05, 14J26

1.
Introduction

Seshadri constants measure the local positivity of an
ample line bundle on an algebraic variety.
These were introduced by Demailly in order
to study Fujita conjecture
[
Dem92
]
.
The following definition of Seshadri constants
is motivated by the ampleness criterion for line
bundles given by Seshadri (see
[
Har70
, Theorem 7.1]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Let
L

be a nef line bundle on
X
.
We define the Seshadri constant of
L
at a point
x\in X

as

\varepsilon(X,L,x):=\inf\left\{\frac{L\cdot C}{{\rm mult}_{x}C}~\Big|~C\text{ is an integral curve on $X$ passing through $x$}\right\}.

The following theorem gives an equivalent definition
for the Seshadri constant using the nefness of certain
line bundles on the blowup of
X
at
x
.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
Laz04
, Proposition 5.1.5]

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and

L
be a nef line bundle on
X
. For a point
x\in X
,
let
\pi:X_{x}\to X
denote the blowup of
X

at
x
and let
E:=\pi^{-1}(\{x\})

denote the exceptional divisor. Then, we have

\varepsilon(X,L,x)=\sup\{s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}~|~\pi^{\ast}L-sE\text{ is nef line bundle on $X_{x}$}\}.

It is easy to see that
\pi^{\ast}L-\varepsilon(X,L,x)E

is a nef line bundle on
X_{x}
and hence
\varepsilon(X,L,x)\leqslant\sqrt{L^{2}}
.
More details about Seshadri constants and local
positivity can be found in
[
Laz04
, Section 5]
and
[
BDRH+09
]
.

Since the definition of Seshadri constants depends
on individual curves rather than linear or numerical
equivalence classes, it is not easy to calculate Seshadri
constants on varieties, even on surfaces. Explicit
computation of Seshadri constants are given for several surfaces, for example
simple abelian surfaces (
[
Bau98
]
), rational
and elliptical ruled surfaces (
[
Gar06
]
),
hyperelliptic surfaces (
[
HR18
]
), rational
surfaces
S
with
\text{dim}|-K_{S}|\geqslant 1
(
[
San14
]
) etc.
For general bounds for Seshadri constants
see
[
Bau99
]
,
[
Nak05
]
,
[
Ste98
]
.

Consider the
Hirzebruch surfaces

\mathbb{F}_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))
,
the projectivization of the rank
2
vector bundle

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
for
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
.
Let
C_{e}
denote a curve given by the vanishing
of a global section of the line
bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}(1)
(this curve
is unique if

C_{e}^{2}=-e
. Let

a fiber of the natural map
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}

With these we can see that

\text{Pic }(\mathbb{F}_{e})=\mathbb{Z}[C_{e}]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[f_{e}]

and intersection products on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
are given
by
C_{e}^{2}=-e,f_{e}^{2}=0

It is know that the minimal rational surfaces are the
projective plane and the Hirzebruch surfaces

\mathbb{F}_{e}\,(\text{with }e\neq 1)
, that is,
any rational surface can be obtained as a blowup of
these surfaces at finitely many points (
[

, Theorem V.10]
).
So in order to study the Seshadri constants on rational
surfaces it is enough to study the blowups of these surfaces.
The case of blowups of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
has been
extensively studied (see
[
DKMS16
]
,
[
FHH+20
]
,
[
HH18
]

[

]

In this article we focus on the blowup of Hirzebruch
surfaces and more generally of ruled surfaces.

In the final section, we have provided bounds on the self-intersections
of curves on ruled surfaces. This is related to the celebrated Bounded
Negativity Conjecture. We say that a surface

has bounded negativity if there exists a constant

such that

for all integral curves

on

is false, see

formulated a variant of the BNC, known as the weighted bounded negavity

is allowed to depend on
the degree of

with respect to any nef and big divisor on the surface.
See
[

[

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface.
The following result is well known, but we include a proof
for the benefit of the authors and the readers. Let
X^{r}
denote the

r
-fold product of
X
with itself.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let the Picard group of
X
be countable.
There is a subset
T\subset X^{r}
which has the following two properties:

•

It is the complement of a countable union of proper closed subsets of
X^{r}
.

•

Let
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\in T
. If
D
is a curve
which contains all these points, then
r<h^{0}(X,D)
.

Proof.

Let us fix the integer
r\geqslant 1
. Let
L
be a line
bundle on
X
such that
0<h^{0}(X,L)\leqslant r
. We begin with defining a closed subset

Z_{L}\subset X^{r}
. Let
q_{i}:X^{r}\to X
denote projection to the
i
th factor.
Consider the map of sheaves on
X^{r}

\psi:H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{r}}\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}^{*}L\,,

which is defined as follows. The map
H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{r}}\to q_{i}^{*}L

is obtained by pulling back the canonical map
H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to L

on
X
along
q_{i}
. If
s\in H^{0}(X,L)
is a section, then the restriction of the above
map over the point
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\in X^{r}
is given by

s\mapsto(s|_{p_{1}},\ldots,s|_{p_{r}})\,.

Recall the following basic fact.
Let
Y
be an integral scheme,
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{G}
be locally free coherent sheaves on
Y
,
and
\phi:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
be a morphism between these. Suppose there is a point
y\in Y
such
that the restriction

\phi|_{y}:\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}/{\mathfrak{m}_{y}}\to\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}/{\mathfrak{m}_{y}}

is an inclusion. Then the map
\phi
is an inclusion and there is an open neighbourhood

U
of
y
such that the cokernel is locally free on
U
.

If
h^{0}(X,L)=1
, then the morphism
\psi
being nonzero is an inclusion. If
h^{0}(X,L)>1
,
then there is an open subset
V\subset X
and a morphism
V\to\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,L))
.
Let
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\in V^{r}
be such that the linear subspace generated by the images of
p_{i}

is all of
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,L))
. Note that for this to happen it is necessary that

h^{0}(X,L)\leqslant r
. We claim that the map

s\mapsto(s|_{p_{1}},\ldots,s|_{p_{r}})\,

is an inclusion. If not, there is a nonzero section of
L
which vanishes at all these
points, that is, a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,L))
which contains all the
p_{i}
,
which is not possible. Thus, using the fact mentioned in the preceding para,
it follows that the map
\psi
is an inclusion. Further,
there is an open subset
U_{L}\subset X^{r}
such that the cokernel of
\psi
is locally
free over
U_{L}
. If
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\in U_{L}
, then clearly the map

\psi|_{P}
is an inclusion. Let
Z_{L}:=X^{r}\setminus U_{L}
. Let
Z
be the union
of
Z_{L}
over all line bundles
L
with
0<h^{0}(X,L)\leqslant r
.
We take
T
to be
X^{r}\setminus Z
.

We claim that
T
has the required property. Let
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\in T
.
Let
D
be a curve which contains the
points
p_{i}
. Let
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
. Then
h^{0}(X,L)>0
. Assume that
h^{0}(X,L)\leqslant r
.
The point
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\in U_{L}
. As we observed above, the map
\psi|_{P}
is an
inclusion. The curve
D
is the vanishing locus of a nonzero section
s\in H^{0}(X,L)

which passes through the points
p_{i}
. This shows that
\psi|_{P}(s)=0
,
a contradiction.
∎

Denote

NS(X)_{\mathbb{R}}:=NS(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
,
where
NS(X)
denotes the Néron-Severi group of
X
.
For
m\in\{1,2\}
, a
(-m)
-curve will mean a smooth irreducible rational curve

C
such that
C^{2}=-m
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface such that the canonical divisor
K_{X}

satisfies
H^{0}(X,-K_{X})\neq 0
. Let
L\in NS(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
,
be such that
L^{2}\geqslant 0

and
L.H>0
for some ample divisor
H
.
Assume that

(1)

L\cdot C\geqslant 0
for all
(-1)
and
(-2)
-curves,

(2)

L\cdot C\geqslant 0
for every integral curve
C
in the base
locus of
-K_{X}
with
C^{2}<0
.

Then
L
is nef.

Proof.

To show that
L
is nef, it suffices to show that for every integral curve
C
,
we have
L\cdot C\geqslant 0
. Let
C
be an integral curve such that
C^{2}\geqslant 0
.
If possible, let
L\cdot C<0
. Since
C^{2}\geqslant 0
, it follows that
C
is nef.
Thus,
C+\alpha H
is ample for
\alpha>0
. If we take
\alpha_{0}=-(L\cdot C)/(L\cdot H)>0

then we get
L\cdot(C+\alpha_{0}H)=0
. Since
C+\alpha_{0}H
is ample, from the Hodge
Index Theorem it follows that
L^{2}<0
or
L=0
. This gives a contradiction.
Thus, if
C^{2}\geqslant 0
then
L\cdot C\geqslant 0
.

Next, consider the case when
C
is an integral curve with
C^{2}<0
.
If
C
is in the base locus of
-K_{X}
, then by assumption we have
L\cdot C\geqslant 0
.
If
C
is not in the base locus of
-K_{X}
, then
-K_{X}\cdot C\geqslant 0
, that is,

K_{X}\cdot C\leqslant 0
. The genus formula,
g(C)=1+C\cdot(C+K_{X})/2
, shows that
the genus is forced
to be 0, that is,
C
is a smooth rational curve. Moreover, it follows easily
that
C
is either a
(-1)
-curve or a
(-2)
-curve.
By our assumption, it follows
that
L\cdot C\geqslant 0
. This completes the proof of the Proposition.
∎

•

Proposition 2.4
.

With the notation as above, assume that the canonical divisor
K_{\widetilde{X}}

satisfies

H^{0}(\widetilde{X},-K_{\widetilde{X}})\neq 0\,.

Then

\varepsilon(X,L,x)=\sup\{s\geqslant 0\,|\,(\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x})\cdot\widetilde{C}\geqslant 0\text{ for all }\widetilde{C}\in\Delta\}\,.

Proof.

If we let
L^{\prime}_{s}=\pi^{*}L-sE_{x}
, then if
L^{\prime}_{s}
is nef, we have
L^{\prime 2}_{s}\geqslant 0
.
This shows that
\varepsilon(X,L,x)\leqslant\sqrt{L^{2}}
. Thus, we have

\varepsilon(X,L,x)={\rm sup}\{\sqrt{L^{2}}\geqslant s\geqslant 0\,|\,\pi^{*}L-sE_{x}\text{ is nef }\}\,.

If
H
is an ample divisor on
X
, then for
\alpha
very large, we have
\alpha H-E_{x}

is ample on
\widetilde{X}
. For
0\leqslant s\leqslant\sqrt{L^{2}}
and
\alpha
very large, we have

(\pi^{*}L-sE_{x})\cdot(\alpha H-E_{x})=\alpha L\cdot H-s\geqslant\alpha L\cdot H-\sqrt{L^{2}}>0\,.

Thus, for every
0\leqslant s\leqslant\sqrt{L^{2}}
, the
element
L^{\prime}_{s}\in NS(\widetilde{X})_{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies the
conditions
L^{\prime 2}_{s}\geqslant 0
and
L^{\prime}_{s}\cdot(\alpha H-E_{x})>0
.
We claim that if
L^{\prime}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{C}\geqslant 0
for all
\widetilde{C}\in\Delta
,
then
L^{\prime}_{s}
is nef.
As the anticanonical divisor
-K_{\widetilde{X}}
of
\widetilde{X}

is effective, we may apply Proposition
2.2
.
If
\widetilde{C}\subset\widetilde{X}
is such an integral curve,
and
x\notin\pi(\widetilde{C})
, then clearly

L^{\prime}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{C}\geqslant 0
. If
\pi(\widetilde{C})=\{x\}
is a point,
then clearly
\widetilde{C}=E_{x}
and
L^{\prime}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{C}\geqslant 0
.
We easily conclude that
L^{\prime}_{s}
is nef iff
L^{\prime}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{C}\geqslant 0

for all
\widetilde{C}\in\Delta
.
∎

This brings us to the strategy we employ in this article.
The main idea is contained in
[
San14
, Proposition 4.1]

and is also used in
[
HJSS24
]
.
Let
S
be a smooth
projective surface and let
X
be the blowup of
S
at
r
very general points.
Let
x\in X
be a very general point and let
\widetilde{X}
be the blowup of
X

at
x
. We will be interested in situations in which

H^{0}(\widetilde{X},-K_{\widetilde{X}})\neq 0
. In the situations we consider,
the set
\Delta
will turn out
to be a finite list. Then the Seshadri
constant
\varepsilon(X,L,x)
is the largest
s
such that

(\pi^{*}L-sE_{x}).\widetilde{C}\geqslant 0
for all
\widetilde{C}\in\Delta
.
In some situations we will need the following variant of Propostion
2.4
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X
and
\widetilde{X}
be as above such that

H^{0}(\widetilde{X},-K_{\widetilde{X}})\neq 0
.
Let
\Lambda
be a set of effective divisors in
\widetilde{X}
such that

\Delta\subset\Lambda
. Then for any ample line bundle
L
on
X
,

\varepsilon(X,L,x)=\sup\{s\geqslant 0\,|\,(\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x})\cdot C^{\prime}\geqslant 0\text{ for all }C^{\prime}\in\Lambda\}\,.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.4
, we have

\varepsilon(X,L,x)=\sup\{s\geqslant 0\,|\,(\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x})\cdot\widetilde{C}\geqslant 0\text{ for all }\widetilde{C}\in\Delta\}\,.

Let

\varepsilon_{1}=\sup\{s\geqslant 0\,|\,(\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x})\cdot C^{\prime}\geqslant 0\text{ for all }C^{\prime}\in\Lambda\}\,.

Our goal is to show
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon(X,L,x)
.

Since
\Delta\subset\Lambda
, it is easy to see that

\varepsilon_{1}\leqslant\varepsilon(X,L,x)
. Now consider a class

\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x}
such that

(\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x})\cdot\widetilde{C}\geqslant 0
for all
\widetilde{C}\in\Delta
.
By Proposition
2.2
,

\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x}
is nef, so for every
effective divisor
C^{\prime}
we have
(\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x})\cdot C^{\prime}\geqslant 0
.
In particular we have

(\pi^{\ast}L-sE_{x})\cdot C^{\prime}\geqslant 0\text{ for all }C^{\prime}\in\Lambda
.
Hence
\varepsilon(X,L,x)\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}
.
∎

Let
e\geqslant 0
be an integer and let

(2.6)

\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}

denote the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
a\geqslant 0
, we have

\pi_{\ast}(aC_{e})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2e)\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-ae).

Proof.

From
[
Har77
, Chapter 2, Proposition 7.11]
we can see that

\displaystyle\pi_{\ast}(aC_{e})

\displaystyle={\rm Sym}^{a}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i+j=a}\left({\rm Sym}^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})\otimes{\rm Sym}^{j}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2e)\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-ae).

∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
a\geqslant 0\text{ and }b\geqslant ae
. Then we have

h^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{e},aC_{e}+bf_{e})=(a+1)(b+1-\frac{ae}{2}).

Proof.

Clearly,
h^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{e},aC_{e}+bf_{e})=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{\ast}(aC_{e}+bf_{e}))
.
By the projection formula, we have

h^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{e},aC_{e}+bf_{e})=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{\ast}(aC_{e})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b)).

Now using Lemma
2.7
, we get

\begin{split}h^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{e},aC_{e}+bf_{e})&=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\pi_{\ast}(aC_{e})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b))\\
&=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b-e)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b-2e)\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b-ae))\\
&=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b))+h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b-e))+\dots+h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b-ae))\\
&=(b+1)+(b-e+1)+\dots+(b-ae+1)\\
&=(a+1)b+a+1-\frac{a(a+1)}{2}e\\
&=(a+1)(b+1)-\frac{a(a+1)}{2}e\\
&=(a+1)(b+1-\frac{ae}{2}).\\
\end{split}

∎

3.
Curves on blowups

In this section we obtain explicit descriptions of

(-1)
-curves and
(-2)
-curves on the blowup of

r
points on Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{e}
when

r\leqslant e+4
, thereby, generalizing
[
HJSS24
]
.

Let
P:=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\}
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
such that

p_{i}
are in different fibers of
\pi
.
Let
\pi_{P}:\mathbb{F}_{e,P}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
denote the blowup of
\mathbb{F}_{e}

at the points in
P
. Let
E_{i}
be the exceptional divisor over the point
p_{i}
.
Let
K_{e}
denote the canonical divisor of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
and let

K_{e,P}
denote the canonical divisor of
\mathbb{F}_{e,P}
.
Then we have

K_{e,P}=K_{e}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}=-2C_{e}-(e+2)f_{e}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}\,.

Let
P_{i}:=P\setminus\{p_{i}\}
. Let
\pi_{P_{i}}:\mathbb{F}_{e,P_{i}}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
denote
the blowup of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at the points in
P_{i}
.

We prove some results
which will help us write down all
(-1)
-curves.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r\leqslant e+2
. Let
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{e}
be

r
points on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
in distinct fibers of the map
\pi
. Let

C
be a smooth rational curve such that
C^{2}=-1
.
If
\pi_{P}(C)={\rm pt}
then
C=E_{i}
, one of the exceptional
divisors. If
\pi_{P}(C)
is a curve, then write

C=aC_{e}+bf_{e}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}\subset\mathbb{F}_{e,P}
,
where
a,b,m_{i}
are non-negative integers (using
[
Har77
, Chapter 5, Corollary 2.18]
). Then one
of the following holds:

(a)

a=0,b=1
,

(b)

a=1,b=0
,

(c)

a=1,b=e
.

Proof.

It is clear that if
\pi_{P}(C)={\rm pt}
, then
C=E_{i}
, one of the exceptional
divisors. So let us assume that
\pi_{P}(C)
is a curve. Thus, we may write

\pi_{P}(C)=aC_{e}+bf_{e}
. From
[
Har77
, Chapter 5, Corollary 2.18]
it follows that
either
a=0,b=1
or
a=1,b=0
or
a>0,b\geqslant ae
.
It suffices to consider the
case when
a>0,b\geqslant ae
.

Since
C
is the strict transform of the irreducible
curve
\pi_{P}(C)
, it follows that we may write

C=aC_{e}+bf_{e}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}
, with
m_{i}\geqslant 0
.
By the genus formula we have
K_{e,P}\cdot C=-1
.
This and the condition
C^{2}=-1
gives

(3.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}=2b+2a-ae-1,

and

(3.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}=2ab-a^{2}e+1.

We may assume

m_{i}>0
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
, or else we reduce to
r-1
case.
It is easily checked that the assertion is true when
r=0
. So if
one of the
m_{i}=0
, we are done by induction on
r
.

Let
C_{1}
be the image of
C
in
\mathbb{F}_{e,P_{1}}
. We see that

C_{1}
is an integral curve with
C_{1}^{2}=-1+m_{1}^{2}\geqslant 0
. Using Proposition
2.8
,
we see that
h^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{e},C_{e}+ef_{e})=e+2>r-1
, which guarantees the existence of an
effective divisor
D=C_{e}+ef_{e}-\sum_{i=2}^{r}n_{i}E_{i}
on
\mathbb{F}_{e,P_{1}}
, where all

n_{i}\geqslant 1
. So we have,

0\leqslant C_{1}\cdot D=b-\sum_{i=2}^{r}n_{i}m_{i}\leqslant b-\sum_{i=2}^{r}m_{i}\,.

The strict transform of the
fiber of the map
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
passing through the point
p_{i}

is an irreducible curve whose class is
f_{e}-E_{i}
. This curve is distinct
from
C
,
and so we get
C\cdot(f_{e}-E_{i})\geqslant 0
. This shows that
m_{i}\leqslant a

for all
i
.
As
m_{1}\leqslant a
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\leqslant b+a.

Hence from (
3.2
),

2a+2b-ae-1\leqslant b+a

Thus
b\leqslant a(e-1)+1
. But as
b\neq 0
from

[
Har77
, Chapter 5, Corollary 2.18]
we have
b\geqslant ae
.
It is now easily checked that
a=1,b=e
is the only possibility.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
r=e+3
. Let
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{e}
be
r

points on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
in distinct fibers of the map
\pi
. Let

C
be a smooth rational curve such that
C^{2}=-1
.
If
\pi_{P}(C)={\rm pt}
then
C=E_{i}
, one of the exceptional
divisors. If
\pi_{P}(C)
is a curve, then write

C=aC_{e}+bf_{e}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}\subset\mathbb{F}_{e,P}
,
where
a,b,m_{i}
are non-negative integers. Then one
of the following holds:

(1)

a=0,b=1
,

(2)

a=1,b=0
,

(3)

a=1,b=e
,

(4)

a=1,b=e+1
.

Proof.

As in the proof of the previous Lemma, we may assume that

a>0,b\geqslant ae
.
Consider the situation when
r\in\{e+3,e+4\}
.
Similar to the previous proof we may assume
m_{i}>0

for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
. Recall equations
(
3.2
) and (
3.3
).
Let
C_{1}
be the image of
C
in
\mathbb{F}_{e,P_{1}}
. Then
C_{1}
is an
integral curve with
C_{1}^{2}\geqslant 0
. As earlier, using Proposition
2.8
we see that

h^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{e},C_{e}+(e+1)f_{e})=e+4>e+3
, which guarantees

D=C_{e}+(e+1)f_{e}-\sum_{i=2}^{r}n_{i}E_{i}
is effective divisor on
\mathbb{F}_{e,P_{1}}
, where all

n_{i}\geqslant 1
. Hence

0\leqslant C_{1}\cdot D=b+a-\sum_{i=2}^{r}n_{i}m_{i}\leqslant b+a-\sum_{i=2}^{r}m_{i}\,.

Using
m_{1}\leqslant a
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\leqslant b+2a.

Hence, from (
3.2
),

2a+2b-ae-1\leqslant b+2a.

Thus
b\leqslant ae+1
. But as
b\neq 0
from

[
Har77
, Chapter 5, Corollary 2.18]
we have
b\geqslant ae
.
Thus we have two possibilities for the value of
b
when
r\in\{e+3,e+4\}
.
We will use this fact in the next Lemma also.

For the remainder of the proof of this Lemma, we assume that
r=e+3
.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{e+3}m_{i}\right)^{2}\leqslant(e+3)\sum_{i=1}^{e+3}m_{i}^{2}\,,

that is,

(3.5)

(2a+2b-ae-1)^{2}\leqslant(e+3)(2ab-a^{2}e+1)\text{ (using \eqref{sum_mi_r (-1)} and \eqref{sum_mi^2 _r (-1)})}.

First, consider the case when
b=ae
.
In this case (
3.5
) reduces to

(a(e+2)-1)^{2}\leqslant(e+3)(a^{2}e+1).

We can see that this is not possible when
a\geqslant 3
.
If
a=2
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{e+3}m_{i}=2e+3
and

1\leqslant m_{i}\leqslant 2
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
.
The only possibility is

•

cardinality of the set
\{i\,|\,m_{i}=2\}=e
,

•

cardinality of the set
\{i\,|\,m_{i}=1\}=3
.

In this case
\sum_{i=1}^{e+3}m_{i}^{2}=4e+3
.
But (
3.3
) says that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}=4e+1
. Hence
a=2
is not possible. Thus
a=1,b=e
.

Next consider the case when
b=ae+1
.
Here (
3.5
) reduces to

(a(e+2)+1)^{2}\leqslant(e+3)(a^{2}e+2a+1)\,,

which simplifies to

a^{2}e+4a^{2}\leqslant 2a+2+e.

It is easy to see if
a\geqslant 2
, we get
e
is negative which is a
contradiction. Hence
a=1,b=e+1
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
r=e+4
. Let
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{e}
be
r

points on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
in distinct fibers of
the map
\pi
. If
e\geqslant 1
, further assume that
none of the
p_{i}
are on
C_{e}
.
Let
C
be a smooth rational curve such that
C^{2}=-1
.
If
\pi_{P}(C)={\rm pt}
then
C=E_{i}
, one of the exceptional
divisors. If
\pi_{P}(C)
is a curve, then write

C=aC_{e}+bf_{e}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}\subset\mathbb{F}_{e,P}
,
where
a,b,m_{i}
are non-negative integers. Then one
of the following holds:

(a)

a=0,b=1
,

(b)

a=1,b=0
,

(c)

a=1,b=e
,

(d)

a=1,b=e+1
,

(e)

e\geqslant 1
,
2\leqslant a\leqslant(e+3)/2
,

C=aC_{e}+aef_{e}-\sum_{i=1}^{e+4}m_{i}E_{i}
, where

•

cardinality of the set
\{i\,|\,m_{i}=a-1\}=2a+1
,

•

cardinality of the set
\{i\,|\,m_{i}=a\}=e-2a+3
.

Proof.

Similar to the earlier cases, we may assume that
a>0,b\geqslant ae
. If one
of the
m_{i}=0
then we may reduce to the
r-1
case. Thus, we also assume
all
m_{i}\geqslant 1
.
As observed in the proof of Lemma
3.4
,
we have two possibilities for the value of
b
, namely,
b=ae
or
b=ae+1
.
If
a=1
, then our list contains the both the possibilities,

b=e
and
b=e+1
. So let us assume that
a\geqslant 2
for the remainder
of this proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{e+4}m_{i}\right)^{2}\leqslant(e+4)\sum_{i=1}^{e+4}m_{i}^{2}\,,

that is,

(3.7)

(2a+2b-ae-1)^{2}\leqslant(e+4)(2ab-a^{2}e+1).

First, consider the case when
b=ae
.
In this case (
3.7
) reduces to

(a(e+2)-1)^{2}\leqslant(e+4)(a^{2}e+1).

It is easy to check that this yields
e\geqslant 2a-3
.

If
e=0
, then the only
possible value for
a
is 1, which has already been considered.
So let us assume that
e\geqslant 1
. If

e=1
, then
a\in\{1,2\}
. It is easily checked, using (
3.2
),
that
a=1
is not possible,
and if
a=2
then
m_{i}=1
for all
i
. Thus, in this case, the curve
C

falls in type
(e)
with
e=2a-3
.

Let us consider the case when for all
i
we have
1\leqslant m_{i}<a
.
As
b=ae
, we have the equalities
\sum_{i=1}^{e+4}m_{i}^{2}=a^{2}e+1

and
\sum_{i=1}^{e+4}m_{i}=a(e+2)-1
.
We get

\sum_{i=1}^{e+4}\frac{m_{i}}{a}\Big(1-\frac{m_{i}}{a}\Big)=\frac{(2a+1)(a-1)}{a^{2}}\,.

As
1\leqslant m_{i}<a
, we get

(e+4)\frac{(a-1)}{a^{2}}\leqslant\frac{(2a+1)(a-1)}{a^{2}}\,.

This gives
e+4\leqslant 2a+1
. This forces
e=2a-3
, and we easily
check that
m_{i}=a-1
for all
i
. Thus, in this case, the curve
C

falls in type
(e)
with
e=2a-3
.

Next consider the case when one of the
m_{i}
, say,
m_{1}=a
.
Write

C=aC_{e}+aef_{e}-aE_{1}-\sum_{i=2}^{e+4}m_{i}E_{i}\,.

By assumption,
p_{1}\notin C_{e}
.
Consider the following
diagram of an elementary transformation

(3.8)

\textstyle{\mathbb{F}_{e,P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{\mathbb{F}_{e,P_{1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\mathbb{F}_{e-1,P^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi^{\prime}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{1}.}

The map
g^{\prime}
is the blowup at the point
p_{1}
.
If
F_{1}
is the fiber of
\pi
containing
p_{1}
, then the strict transform
of
F_{1}
has class
f_{e}-E_{1}
. Denote this strict transform by
\tilde{F}_{1}\subset\mathbb{F}_{e,P}
.
The morphism
g
is the blowup at a point

and
has
\tilde{F}_{1}
as the exceptional divisor. As

,

, that is,

is a
(-1)
-curve on

, and

. Moreover, it is easily checked that

The points in

satisfy the same property, they are in different fibers

.

.

(e)
, with

.

Next let us consider the case when
b=ae+1
.
In this case (
3.7
) reduces to

(a(e+2)+1)^{2}\leqslant(e+4)(a^{2}e+2a+1).

It is easy to check that this yields

.

, and so we have

.

Let us consider the case when for all

we have

.

\sum_{i=1}^{e+4}m_{i}^{2}=a^{2}e+2a+1

.

, say,

.

Remark 3.9
.

Consider the numerical classes of curves

(e)

as

,
with invariant
e
.
Let
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}\subset X
be
r
distinct
points in
X
. Let
\pi_{P}:X_{P}\to X

be the blowup of
X
at the points in
P
. Let
C
be an integral curve on

X_{P}
such that
C^{2}<0
. Let

\lambda:={\rm max}\left\{2g-1,2g-1+e,g+\left\lfloor\frac{r+e}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}\,.

Then either
C
is an exceptional divisor or

C^{2}\geqslant{\rm min}\{-2,-r\}+\left(e+2-\lambda-2g\right)(C\cdot f_{e})\,.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of the previous Theorem, and so we will omit
some details.

Recall that the Picard group of
X_{P}
is generated by the Picard group
of
\Gamma
, the divisor
C_{e}
, and the exceptional divisors
E_{i}
.
Let
C=aC_{e}+\pi^{*}B-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}
be an integral curve on
X_{P}

with
C^{2}<0
. If
\pi_{P}(C)
is a point, then
C
is forced to be one
of the exceptional divisors
E_{i}
and so
C^{2}=-1
.
Let us assume that
\pi_{P}(C)
is a curve.
Then
C
is the strict transform of
\pi_{P}(C)
, and so we get all
the
m_{i}\geqslant 0
.
Let
C^{2}=-k
and
K_{X_{P}}\cdot C=j
. Using
K_{X_{P}}\cdot C=j
we get

(7.4)

2a+2b-ae-2ag+j=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}.

Note that
h^{0}(X,C_{e}+\pi^{*}B^{\prime})=h^{0}(\Gamma,E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(B^{\prime}))
.
From the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}\to E\to L\to 0\,,

it follows that if
{\rm deg}(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(B^{\prime}))>2g-2
and

{\rm deg}(L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(B^{\prime}))>2g-2
then

h^{0}(X,C_{e}+\pi^{*}B^{\prime})=h^{0}(\Gamma,E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(B^{\prime}))=2({\rm deg}(B^{\prime})+1-g)-e\,.

Letting

\lambda:={\rm max}\left\{2g-1,2g-1+e,g+\left\lfloor\frac{r+e}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}

we see
that
2(\lambda+1-g)-e>r
. Thus, if
B^{\prime}
is a divisor of degree
\lambda

then there exists an effective divisor

D\in|C_{e}+\pi^{*}B^{\prime}|
such that
D

passes through all points in
P
. As
\pi_{P}(C)
is integral,
D
and

\pi_{P}(C)
can have a common component
only if
\pi_{P}(C)
is a component of
D
.

First consider the case when
a>1
. Write the numerical class of
\pi_{P}(C)

as
aC_{e}+bf_{e}
, and the numerical class of
D
as
C_{e}+\lambda f_{e}
. Hence,

D-\pi_{P}(C)\equiv(1-a)C_{e}+(\lambda-b)f_{e}
. Since
a>1
, we see that

D-\pi_{P}(C)
cannot be effective (as intersecting with
f_{e}
, a nef class,
will give a negative number). So
D

and
\pi_{P}(C)
do not have a common component. Hence we have

D\cdot\pi_{P}(C)\geqslant\sum_{p\in P}(\text{mult}_{p}D)(\text{mult}_{p}\pi_{P}(C)).

Since
\text{mult}_{p}D\geqslant 1
, we have,

b+a(\lambda-e)\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\,.

Combining this and (
7.4
), we have

2a+2b-ae-2ag+j=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\leqslant b+a(\lambda-e)\,,

that is,
b-a(\lambda-2+2g)+j\leqslant 0
.
As
a>1
, using
[
Har77
, Chapter 5,
Proposition 2.20, Proposition 2.21]

we get
b\geqslant ae
. Thus, we have
-j\geqslant a(e+2-\lambda-2g)
.

Now by the genus formula we have

j-k=K_{X_{P}}\cdot C+C^{2}\geqslant-2\,,

which yields

C^{2}=-k\geqslant-2+a(e+2-\lambda-2g)=-2+(e+2-\lambda-2g)(C\cdot f_{e})\,.

Next consider the case
a=1
. Then
C^{2}=2b-e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}
and

0\leqslant m_{i}\leqslant 1
for
all
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
.
Again, using
[
Har77
, Chapter 5,
Proposition 2.20, Proposition 2.21]

we see that if
e\geqslant 0
then
b\geqslant e

and if
e<0
then
b\geqslant 0
. In both cases we have

2b-e\geqslant 0
. Thus,

C^{2}\geqslant(2b-e)-r\geqslant-r\,.

Finally, in the case
a=0
, we easily see that
C^{2}\geqslant-r
.
This completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

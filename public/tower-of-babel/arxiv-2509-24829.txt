Continuous differentiability of the signum function and Newton’s method for bang-bang control
Daniel Wachsmuth, Gerd Wachsmuth

Abstract
We investigate bang-bang control problems and the possibility to apply Newton’s method to solve such kind of problems numerically. To this end, we show that the signum function is Fréchet differentiable between appropriate function spaces. Numerical experiments show the applicability of the resulting method.

Introduction

We are interested in the numerical solution of
optimal control problems
of the type

\min_{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{1}{2}\|S(u)-y_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

subject to

|u|\leq 1\text{ almost everywhere on }\Omega.

Here,
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
S:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is the (possibly nonlinear) control-to-state mapping.
In addition,
y_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is a given desired state.
For such problems,
optimal controls are often bang-bang,
i.e., at almost all points of the domain, optimal controls
only attain the control bounds
\pm 1
.
Typically, the first-order necessary and sufficient optimality condition
can be written in this case as

\bar{u}=\operatorname{sign}(-\bar{p}),\qquad\bar{p}=S^{\prime}(\bar{u})^{*}(S(\bar{u})-y_{d}),

where
\bar{u}
is an optimal control and
\bar{p}
is the associated adjoint state.
In order to solve this system with Newton’s method,
we need to show that the signum function
\operatorname{sign}

is differentiable between appropriate function spaces.
Our main result
Theorem

3.9

shows that

\operatorname{sign}\colon C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\to W^{-1,q}(\Omega)^{\star}

is indeed differentiable at points
w\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})

satisfying a certain regularity condition.
We also show that this gives rise to local superlinear convergence of Newton’s method
applied to an optimal control problem governed by linear or semilinear elliptic PDEs,
see
Section

4
.
Numerical examples show the efficiency of our proposed methods,
see
Section

5
.

Second-order optimality conditions for the control of semilinear equations
without a control regularization term
have been given in

Casas 2012
;
Christof & Wachsmuth 2018
;
Wachsmuth & Wachsmuth 2022
.
A-priori error estimates for a variational discretization where given in
Deckelnick & Hinze 2012
,
while
Fuica 2025
;
Fuica & Jork 2025

derived a-posteriori error estimates.
Finally,
we mention that second-order derivatives of the functional
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

have been analyzed in

Christof 2018
;
Christof & Meyer 2019
, which is related to our work,
since the signum function can be seen as the first derivative of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
.

Let us report on existing approaches to solve bang-bang control problems.
Due to their simplicity, projected gradient
Hinze et al. 2009
, Section 2.2.2
and conditional gradient methods
Kunisch & Walter 2024
can be applied.
Frequently, fixed point methods are used to compute bang-bang controls
Deckelnick & Hinze 2012
;
Fuica 2025
;
Fuica & Jork 2025
;
Daniels & Hinze 2020
.
With the help of the results of this paper, the convergence of fixed-point methods can be explained, see
Remark

4.5
below.
For controls
that only depend on time,
switching point optimization methods can be used
Glashoff & Sachs, 1977/78 1977/78
;
Eppler & Tr\"oltzsch 1986
.
Control problems for ordinary differential equations are amenable to the Goh transform and the transformed system can be solved by Newton’s method
Felgenhauer 2016
.
Another popular approach is to apply Tikhonov regularization
Daniels 2018
;
Wachsmuth & Wachsmuth 2011
.

Definition 2.3

Let

be open and bounded.
We define

C^{1}(\bar{\Omega}):=\{f\in C^{1}(\Omega)\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\partial^{\alpha}f\text{ uniformly continuous }\forall\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq 1\}

Finally,
for a compact set

(i)

For all

(ii)

\displaystyle\lvert P^{x}(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{i}P^{x}(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq M,

\displaystyle\lvert P^{x}(y)-P^{y}(y)\rvert

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{i}P^{x}(y)-\partial_{i}P^{y}(y)\rvert

holds for all

.

We say that a set

Let

Theorem 2.6

Given a compact set

Theorem 2.7
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

is open, bounded, and uniformly locally quasiconvex.
Then, there exists a bounded linear operator
E\colon C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\to C_{\mathrm{b}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with

(Ef)_{|\bar{\Omega}}=f

for all

.
Note that this also implies that the derivatives of

and

\bar{\Omega}

Proof.

We follow the proof of
Whitney 1934a
, Theorem, p. 485

We
associate with

That is, we set

P^{x}(y):=f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x).

We are going to check that

First of all, it is clear that

and

\lvert\partial_{i}P^{x}(y)-\partial_{i}P^{y}(y)\rvert\leq 2\lVert f\rVert_{C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})}.

We assume that
\Omega
uniformly locally quasiconvex
with constants

and
L\geq 1

Next, let
x,y\in\Omega

with
\lvert x-y\rvert<r
be given.
We denote the curve connecting

and

Then,

\displaystyle\lvert P^{x}(y)-P^{y}(y)\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert f(x)-f(y)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq L\lvert x-y\rvert\int_{0}^{1}\lvert\nabla f(\gamma(t))-\nabla f(x)\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

By continuity, the same estimate holds for

with
\lvert x-y\rvert<r

For

with
\lvert x-y\rvert\geq r

(ii)

of

2.3

This enables us to estimate

\displaystyle\lvert P^{x}(y)-P^{y}(y)\rvert

Hence,

Finally, the jet can be extended via

It can be seen that the number of iterations and the number of applications of
S

is almost mesh independent for
u_{b}=50
.
In the case of
u_{b}=57
,
the very fine structure of the solution is not resolved on coarse meshes
and therefore the iteration numbers grow slightly.

If we further increase
u_{b}
, our method breaks down.
Let us report about the outcome of the computations for
u_{b}=100
.
Although the iterates
w_{k}=S^{*}\xi_{k}
still satisfy the assumptions from
Section

3
,
the operator norm of
\operatorname{sign}^{\prime}(w_{k})

seems not to be bounded
uniformly w.r.t. the iteration number
k
.
In particular, we observe that the number of CG iterations needed to solve the Newton system
does not stay bounded in
k
.
We conjecture
that this is due to the fact that optimal controls are no longer bang-bang for large values of
u_{b}
.
Consequently,
4.1
is not satisfied and our convergence result
Theorem

4.4

does not apply.

.

Abelian 3D TQFT gravity, ensemble holography and stabilizer states
Nikolaos Angelinos

Abstract
We construct a model of 3D quantum gravity based on abelian topological quantum field theory (TQFT), by defining the gravitational path-integral as a sum over all 3D topologies with genus- g g boundary Σ g \Sigma_{g} . The path-integral of an abelian TQFT 𝒯 \mathcal{T} on any single topology with boundary Σ g \Sigma_{g} prepares a stabilizer state. This way, 𝒯 \mathcal{T} partitions all these topologies into finitely many equivalence classes, where each topology within a class is associated with the same stabilizer state. The gravitational path-integral can thus be rephrased as a weighted sum over representative topologies, which are further organized into orbits under the mapping class group of Σ g \Sigma_{g} . One orbit is represented by handlebodies, whose average reproduces the “Poincaré series of the vacuum”, while additional orbits describe non-handlebody topologies. The resulting quantum gravity state is Sp ⁡ ( 2 ​ g , ℤ ) \operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}) -invariant and can be expressed as a weighted average of 2D CFT partition functions on Σ g \Sigma_{g} . This establishes a duality between a weighted sum over bulk topologies and a weighted sum over boundary CFTs. We introduce the “ λ \lambda -matrix”, which relates bulk and boundary weights. The λ \lambda -matrix can be fully determined by the set of topological boundary conditions that the TQFT admits, and we present a systematic procedure to construct this set. Using this framework, we evaluate the λ \lambda -matrix and the TQFT gravity state in several tractable examples.

Introduction

The quantum gravity path-integral is notoriously difficult to define, as it requires summing over all possible spacetime topologies and integrating over all metrics compatible with each topology. In models of quantum gravity based on a topological quantum field theory (TQFT), this problem simplifies, as the theory is not sensitive to the metric, leaving only a sum over topologies. Even so, a general prescription for the quantum gravity path-integral remains elusive.

A concrete proposal was made by Maloney and Witten
Maloney_2010
, who defined the AdS
3

gravity path integral as a sum over handlebody topologies. Their result lacked a clear holographic interpretation, since the putative dual 2D CFT would have a continuous spectrum with negative degeneracies. A similar construction was carried out in 3D abelian Chern–Simons (CS) theory
Maloney_2020
;
Afkhami-Jeddi:2020ezh
, where the sum over handlebodies yielded an expression that can be interpreted as an average over the Narain moduli space of 2D CFTs. This motivated the idea of “ensemble holography” in three dimensions
Collier:2021rsn
;
Cotler:2020hgz
;
Benjamin:2021wzr
;
Ashwinkumar:2023jtz
;
Ashwinkumar:2023ctt
;
Kames-King:2023fpa
;
Forste:2024zjt
;
Perez:2020klz
;
Datta:2021ftn
;
Benjamin:2021ygh
;
Chakraborty:2021gzh

Raeymaekers:2021ypf
;
Benini:2022hzx
;
Saidi:2024zdj
, where the bulk gravitational path integral is dual not to a single boundary CFT, but rather to an ensemble of CFTs.

Ensemble holography in 3D has also been explored in settings where the boundary theories are RCFTs

;

A consistent prescription was introduced in

2
Abelian TQFT and Stabilizer Formalism

Our starting point is an Abelian 3D TQFT
\mathcal{T}
specified by the pair
(\mathsf{G},\mathfrak{q})
, where
\mathsf{G}
is a finite abelian group and
\mathfrak{q}
is a non-degenerate quadratic form on
\mathsf{G}
. This pair fully determines the data of a pointed Modular Tensor Category (MTC) whose objects are anyons labeled by elements of
\mathsf{G}
and their topological spins are given by

\theta(a)=\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi i}{N}\,\mathfrak{q}(a)\right),\qquad a\in\mathsf{G},

(2.5)

where
N
is the exponent of
\mathsf{G}
and
\mathfrak{q}:\mathsf{G}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2N}
is the quadratic form.
The Frobenius–Schur exponent
N_{FS}
is the minimal integer such that
\theta(a)^{N_{FS}}=1
for all
a\in\mathsf{G}
.
To avoid subtleties when
N
is even, we will henceforth assume
N
is odd, so that
N_{FS}=N
.
Introducing the primitive root of unity
\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}
, the spins can be written compactly as

\theta(a)=\omega^{2^{-1}\mathfrak{q}(a)},

(2.6)

where
2^{-1}
denotes the multiplicative inverse of
2
in
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.
The spins determine the braiding matrix

\sqrt{|\mathsf{G}|}\,S_{ab}=\frac{\theta(a+b)}{\theta(a)\theta(b)}=\omega^{\mathsf{g}(a,b)},

(2.7)

where
\mathsf{g}:\mathsf{G}\times\mathsf{G}\to\mathbb{Z}_{N}
is the bilinear form associated to
\mathfrak{q}

\mathsf{g}(a,b)=2^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}(a+b)-\mathfrak{q}(a)-\mathfrak{q}(b))\mod N.

(2.8)

The spins also determine the chiral central charge
c
modulo
8
via

e^{\frac{2\pi i}{8}c}=\frac{\sum_{a\in\mathsf{G}}\theta(a)}{|\sum_{a\in\mathsf{G}}\theta(a)|}.

(2.9)

Throughout, we will only consider
(\mathsf{G},\mathfrak{q})
such that the chiral central charge vanishes. This condition is necessary for the theory to admit topological boundary conditions
Kaidi:2021gbs
.

Any finite abelian group
\mathsf{G}
decomposes into a product of
p
-groups,

\mathsf{G}\cong\mathsf{G}_{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathsf{G}_{p_{k}},

(2.10)

where
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
are distinct primes, and
\mathsf{G}_{p}
denotes a group consisting of elements whose order is a power of
p
. The quadratic form factorizes accordingly,

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}_{p_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{q}_{p_{k}},\qquad\mathfrak{q}_{p_{i}}=\mathfrak{q}|_{\mathsf{G}_{p_{i}}}.

(2.11)

Thus the TQFT itself decomposes into a product of decoupled TQFTs,
Kaidi:2021gbs

\mathcal{T}\;\cong\;\mathcal{T}_{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{T}_{p_{k}},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{p_{i}}=(\mathsf{G}_{p_{i}},\mathfrak{q}_{p_{i}}).

(2.12)

Without loss of generality, we can restrict to the case where
\mathsf{G}
is a
p
-group.

A concrete realization of this data is given by the

Chern–Simons theory

\mathcal{T}:\quad S[A]=\frac{i}{4\pi}K_{IJ}\int_{\mathcal{V}}A_{I}\wedge dA_{J},

(2.13)

where

is an integral, symmetric matrix of signature
(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})
and with even diagonal entries. In other words,
K

\mathsf{G}=\mathbb{Z}^{r}/(K\mathbb{Z}^{r})
and the topological spins are
\theta(x)=\exp\bigl(\pi i\,x^{T}K^{-1}x\bigr)

We will only consider spaces
\mathcal{V}
whose boundary is a connected surface
\Sigma_{g}
of genus

For

2.3
Stabilizer groups and stabilizer states

An abelian subgroup
\mathcal{S}
of the Weyl-Heisenberg group
\mathcal{P}
is called a
stabilizer group
. Since its elements commute, they can be simultaneously diagonalized and they fix an invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, called the
stabilizer code
.
From (
2.30
), Weyl operators satisfy

\mathcal{W}(\alpha)\mathcal{W}(\beta)=\omega^{-J(\alpha,\beta)}\mathcal{W}(\beta)\mathcal{W}(\alpha).

(2.44)

Thus, a stabilizer group corresponds to an
isotropic submodule

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathsf{G}^{2g}
with respect to
J
, i.e. a submodule on which
J
vanishes. Because
\mathcal{P}
is a nontrivial central extension of
\mathsf{G}^{2g}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
,
\mathcal{C}
alone does not uniquely determine
\mathcal{S}
, but we also need to make a choice of phases. For each isotropic
\mathcal{C}
and vector
v\in\mathsf{G}^{2g}
, define the stabilizer group

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{C},v)=\{\omega^{-J(v,\alpha)}\mathcal{W}(\alpha):\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\}.

(2.45)

Summing over
\mathcal{S}
produces the projector onto the code subspace

\Pi_{\mathcal{C},v}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}}\omega^{-J(v,\alpha)}\mathcal{W}(\alpha).

(2.46)

The centralizer of
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{P}
is generally a non-abelian group, corresponding to the dual (with respect to
J
) module
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
. A
maximally isotropic
submodule satisfies
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
, and is also called
Lagrangian
. In this case the projector reduces to a rank-one operator, i.e. a
stabilizer state

Gross_2006

\Pi_{\mathcal{C},v}=|v;\mathcal{C}\rangle\langle v;\mathcal{C}|.

(2.47)

From (
2.46
), we can immediately read its characteristic function

\Xi_{|v;\mathcal{C}\rangle\langle v;\mathcal{C}|}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{|\mathsf{G}|^{g}}\,\omega^{-J(v,\alpha)}\delta_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}},

(2.48)

and hence its Wigner function is simply the indicator function

\mathbb{W}_{|v;\mathcal{C}\rangle\langle v;\mathcal{C}|}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{|\mathsf{G}|^{g}}\,\delta_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}+v}.

(2.49)

Up to a phase, the state defined by (
2.47
) can be written as

|v;\mathcal{C}\rangle=\mathcal{W}(v)|0;\mathcal{C}\rangle,

(2.50)

so for each fixed
\mathcal{C}
, the states
\{\mathcal{W}(v)|0;\mathcal{C}\rangle\}
form an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. One may view
|0;\mathcal{C}\rangle
as the “ground state” defined by the module
\mathcal{C}
, with the rest of the states obtained by inserting anyon lines. In what follows, we make the choice
v=0
.

Two families of Lagrangian submodules are particularly important. Note that if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
is isotropic with respect to
\mathfrak{q}
, then
\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}\subseteq\mathsf{G}^{2g}
is isotropic.
Similarly, if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
is isotropic with respect to the symplectic form
\eta
, then
\mathsf{G}\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathsf{G}^{2g}
is isotropic.
This motivates the following definition

Definition 1
.

A submodule
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathsf{G}^{2g}
is
\eta
-isotropic (
\eta
-Lagrangian) if
\mathcal{C}=\mathsf{G}\otimes C
for some isotropic (Lagrangian)
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
with respect to
\eta
.

A submodule
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathsf{G}^{2g}
is
\mathfrak{q}
-isotropic (
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian) if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
for some isotropic (Lagrangian)
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
with respect to
\mathfrak{q}
.

These two types of Lagrangian submodules have a special meaning in the TQFT. The
\eta
-Lagrangian submodules define the first homology group of a 3-manifold with boundary
\Sigma_{g}
. The stabilizer state
|0;\mathcal{C}\rangle
is prepared by the TQFT path-integral on this space, while
|v;\mathcal{C}\rangle
are the orthonormal states obtained by line insertions.
A
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian submodule
\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
describes topological boundary conditions (TBC) on the genus-
g
surface. The associated stabilizer state can be obtained by gauging
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
in the bulk of a genus-
g
handlebody.

2.4
Gauging 1-form symmetries in 3D

We will now review the gauging of

Gauging
1

To gauge the group
\mathsf{G}
inside the bulk of a handlebody

, one inserts a network of anyon lines dual to a triangulation of
\mathcal{V}_{g}
and sums over all charges in
\mathsf{G}

1

\mathsf{G}
is
anomalous

. In an abelian theory, a network of non-anomalous lines reduces to loops wrapping around each non-contractible cycle (see figure

\mathsf{G}
is non-anomalous if the anyon lines have trivial mutual braiding. Since by assumption
\mathfrak{q}
is non-degenerate,
\mathsf{G}
itself cannot be gauged in the bulk. However, one can gauge a subgroup
\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
on which
\mathfrak{q}

\mathsf{H}
. The resulting theory, denoted
\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}

\mathsf{H}^{\perp}
with those in
\mathsf{H}

-form symmetry group of

is
\mathsf{H}^{\perp}/\mathsf{H}
.

This gauging process is a projection onto the stabilizer code associated to

, and the Hilbert space of
\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}
can be viewed as the space of “logical” states. In particular, if
\mathsf{H}
is Lagrangian, then this gauging process constructs a stabilizer state, corresponding to the 1-dimensional Hilbert space of

3
Lagrangian Submodules and their Stabilizer States

In this section we provide the full classification of the two types of Lagrangian submodules in definition
1
. In subsection
3.1
we discuss the
\eta
-Lagrangian submodules, which describe the representative topologies that an abelian TQFT can distinguish. In subsection
3.2
we discuss the
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian submodules, which describe topological boundary conditions. Finally, in subsection
3.3
we review the relation between topological boundary conditions and 2D CFTs.

3.1
Classification of
\eta
-Lagrangian submodules and representative topologies

We begin with the
\eta
-Lagrangian submodules, which have the form
\mathsf{G}\otimes C
, where
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
is Lagrangian with respect to the intersection form
\eta
. Since they contain a
\mathsf{G}
tensor factor, both the submodules and their associated stabilizer states are invariant under
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
. In fact, these modules are invariant under the entire group
\text{Aut}(\mathsf{G})
.

The associated stabilizer states can be prepared by the TQFT path integral on some 3-manifold with boundary
\Sigma_{g}
, with the factor
C
describing its first homology group. The TQFT path integral on
any
space with boundary
\Sigma_{g}
prepares one of these states
Dymarsky:2024frx
. We describe the simplest representative topology corresponding to each of these states and later, in section
4
, we will define the gravitational path integral as a weighted sum over these representatives.

A complete classification of Lagrangian submodules of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
is given by
albouy2008matrixreductionlagrangiansubmodules
(Theorem 7), which we restate in our setting.

Theorem 1

(Classification of symplectic Lagrangian submodules)
.

Let
C
be a submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
and let
N=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{s_{i}}
be the prime factorization of
N
. Define the
g
-tuple
\boldsymbol{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{g})
with

d_{1}\mid d_{2}\mid\cdots\mid d_{g}\mid N,\quad 1\leq d_{j}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\lfloor s_{i}/2\rfloor},\quad j=1,\dots,g.

(3.53)

Then
C
is a Lagrangian submodule with respect to
\eta
if and only if there exists
\gamma\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
such that

\mathrm{diag}(N/d_{1},N/d_{2},\dots,N/d_{g},d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{g})\times\gamma

(3.54)

is a generator matrix for
C
.

Thus, Lagrangian submodules that are isomorphic as groups belong to the same symplectic orbit. This does not hold for general isotropic submodules. While two isomorphic isotropic submodules
C\cong C^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
are trivially isometric (since the symplectic form vanishes), their isometry does not always extend to an isometry of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
. The Lagrangian condition is therefore essential for Theorem
1
to apply, as emphasized in
albouy2008matrixreductionlagrangiansubmodules
.

Theorem
1
provides a classification of symplectic stabilizer states in terms of
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
orbits, generalizing the results of
Feng:2024fzh
. To make the notation less cumbersome, define

\mathbb{Z}_{\boldsymbol{d}}\equiv\mathbb{Z}_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{g}},\qquad\mathbb{Z}_{N/\boldsymbol{d}}\equiv\mathbb{Z}_{N/d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{N/d_{g}},

(3.55)

so that the submodule generated by (
3.54
) is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{\boldsymbol{d}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N/\boldsymbol{d}}
.

We begin by describing the
free
Lagrangian submodules
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
, which have rank
g
and admit a basis of
g
elements. These submodules can be represented by regular handlebodies. To see this, note that a free submodule defines a state invariant under insertions of all anyons along the
g
independent cycles of
H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z}_{N})
determined by its basis. In other words,
C
renders
g
cycles of the genus-
g
surface contractible, thereby defining a handlebody. The mapping class group
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
acts transitively on the free Lagrangian submodules, permuting handlebodies. Its representation on the Hilbert space has kernel
\Gamma(N)
and its image is
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})/\Gamma(N)\cong\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, which is precisely the subgroup of the Clifford group described in section
2.2
. The state
|0\rangle^{\otimes g}
corresponds to the free submodule
C=\{(0,a)\mid a\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{g}\}
, given by the choice
\boldsymbol{d}=\boldsymbol{1}
and
\gamma=\mathds{1}
in (
3.54
). Varying
\gamma
, we obtain the states that belong to the
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})
-orbit of
|0\rangle^{\otimes g}
. Specifically, the free submodule
C\gamma=\{(0,a)\gamma\mid a\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{g}\}
for
\gamma\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})
yields the stabilizer state
U_{\gamma}|0\rangle^{\otimes g}
.
However, not all elements of
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})
generate distinct stabilizer states by acting on
|0\rangle^{\otimes g}
. The subgroup that fixes this state is the congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{0}(N)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})\right\}.

(3.56)

Hence the distinct free stabilizer states correspond to cosets in
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})/\Gamma_{0}(N)
. In
Singal:2022dqu
, the following explicit count was obtained

\#\text{ free stabilizer states }=N^{g(g+1)\over 2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=0}^{g-1}(p_{i}^{-(g-j)}+1).

(3.57)

When
N
is not squarefree, non-free Lagrangian submodules of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
also exist, which correspond to non-handlebody topologies. The simplest representative topology can be described as a singular handlebody as follows. Consider gluing a torus cylinder
\Sigma_{1}^{(1)}\times[0,1]
to a torus handlebody
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{(2)}
along their boundaries
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma_{1}^{(1)}\times\{0\}
and
\Sigma_{2}=\partial\mathcal{V}_{1}^{(2)}
via a covering map
f
. Writing the torus as
S^{1}\times S^{1}
with angular coordinates
(\theta,\phi)
, define
f:\Sigma_{1}\to\Sigma_{2}
by
f(e^{i\theta},e^{i\phi})=(e^{i{N\over d}\theta},e^{id\phi})
. The resulting glued manifold
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{d}=\mathcal{V}_{1}^{(2)}\cup_{f}(\Sigma_{1}^{(1)}\times[0,1])
has first homology

H_{1}(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{d},\mathbb{Z}_{N})\cong{\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2}\over\langle(N/d,0),(0,d)\rangle}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{N/d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}.

(3.58)

Contracting the gluing surface to a line, this topology can be thought as a handlebody with a non-invertible (unless
d=1
) defect line insertion along its non-contractible cycle. These are condensation defects
Roumpedakis:2022aik
that can be constructed by gauging
(N/d)\mathsf{G}
in a torus handlebody, or “higher-gauging” the same group on a torus surface and then shrinking this surface to a line.

In general, a non-free Lagrangian submodule
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
defines a class of 3-manifolds with
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{N})\cong{\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}}/C\cong C
. The corresponding stabilizer state is the state obtained by the TQFT path integral on such a manifold. These spaces can equivalently be represented by singular handlebodies with insertions of defect lines along their non-contractible cycles (see figure
3
).
Each
\boldsymbol{d}\neq\boldsymbol{1}
specifies a space
\mathcal{V}_{g}^{\boldsymbol{d}}
with
H_{1}(\mathcal{V}^{\boldsymbol{d}}_{g},\mathbb{Z}_{N})=\mathbb{Z}_{\boldsymbol{d}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N/\boldsymbol{d}}
. The module (
3.54
) for
\gamma=\mathds{1}
is stabilized by the subgroup

\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{d}}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})\bigg|\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{g})C=\operatorname{diag}(N/d_{1},\dots,N/d_{g})B=0\right\},

(3.59)

so the distinct topologies in the orbit correspond to the cosets
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})/\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{d}}
. Counting these cosets is significantly more involved than in the free case
\boldsymbol{d}=\boldsymbol{1}
.

For
\gamma=\mathds{1}
, since (
3.54
) is diagonal, the corresponding state factorizes as

|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{g}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathsf{G}_{N/d_{i}}|}}\sum_{a\in\mathsf{G}_{N/d_{i}}}|a\rangle

(3.60)

where
\mathsf{G}_{N/d_{i}}=(N/d_{i})\mathsf{G}\subseteq\mathsf{G}
. We chose to normalize the states such that
\langle\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle=1
and fixed the overall phase such that
\langle 0^{g}|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle>0
. The rest of the states in the orbit are given by

|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}};\gamma\rangle\equiv U_{\gamma}|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle,~~\gamma\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})/\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{d}}.

(3.61)

Thus, the tuple
\boldsymbol{d}
defines the first homology group up to tensoring with
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, while
\gamma\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})/\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{d}}
defines the inclusion of
\Sigma_{g}
into the boundary of
\mathcal{V}_{g}^{\boldsymbol{d}}
.

4
The Quantum Gravity Path-Integral

The quantum gravity path integral for a topological theory involves a sum over topologies. According to
Dymarsky:2024frx
, the path integral of an abelian TQFT
\mathcal{T}
on any topology with
\Sigma_{g}
boundary is a stabilizer state described by an
\eta
-Lagrangian submodule.
For a fixed
\mathcal{T}
, this partitions all the possible topologies into a finite number of equivalence classes, with the path integral of
\mathcal{T}
preparing the same state within each class. We can thus reframe the gravitational path integral as a weighted sum over the representative topologies defined in subsection
3.1
.

As discused in subsection
3.1
, the representative topologies are parametrized by
\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{T}
and
\gamma\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})/\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{d}}
. The
g
-tuple
\boldsymbol{d}
describes the first homology group of the space, while
\gamma
specifies an inclusion map of the surface
\Sigma_{g}
into
\partial\mathcal{V}_{g}^{\boldsymbol{d}}
. Since we require consistency with holography, it is reasonable to assume that for each fixed
\boldsymbol{d}
, the sum should include a uniform average over the symplectic orbit, so that the resulting state is invariant under the mapping class group of
\Sigma_{g}
. With this assumption, the resulting state can be expanded in terms of TBC states, which are
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian stabilizer states. The latter expression can be interpreted as a weighted average over 2D CFT partition functions
Barbar:2023ncl
.

If
N
is squarefree, this construction is particularly simple. All
\eta
-Lagrangian stabilizers belong to a single symplectic orbit and they describe regular handlebody topologies
\mathcal{V}_{g}
with
H_{1}(\mathcal{V}_{g},\mathbb{Z}_{N})=\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{g}
. The uniform average over these regular handlebodies gives a well-defined ensemble of 2D CFTs valid at any genus
Dymarsky:2025agh
.

If
N
is not square-free, there also exist
\eta
-Lagrangian submodules that describe non-handlebody topologies. Summing only over the handlebodies leads to an ill-defined ensemble of CFT partition functions. This issue is rectified by averaging over
all
representative topologies, with an appropriate measure. As emphasized earlier, within each symplectic orbit we average uniformly, so that the resulting state is
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})
-invariant. We define the TQFT gravity path integral as a weighted average over all representative topologies of boundary
\Sigma_{g}
defined by the
\eta
-Lagrangian stabilizer states

\mathcal{Z}_{gravity}[\mathcal{T},\Sigma_{g};w]\equiv\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{T}}w_{\boldsymbol{d}}\Big\llangle|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}.

(4.72)

Above
\Big\llangle\cdot\Big\rrangle_{Sp}
denotes a uniform average over the symplectic orbit, which will be defined precisely in subsection
4.1
.
We call
w_{\boldsymbol{d}}
the
bulk weights
.

In subsection
4.1
we express the path integral (
4.72
) as follows

\mathcal{Z}_{gravity}[\mathcal{T},\Sigma_{g};w]=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{S}}m_{\boldsymbol{q}}|\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\rangle,

(4.73)

where
\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{S}
parametrizes the
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-orbits of TBCs and

denote the uniform average of TBCs within an orbit. We call the coefficients

the
boundary weights
.

In subsection

4.1
Average over topologies

In this subsection we derive the map between the bulk and boundary weights. We will assume that
\mathsf{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2n}
, but this calculation can be applied with minor modifications to general finite abelian group. This method was first used in
Castro:2011zq
to calculate the Poincaré series of the vacuum in some Virasoro minimal models on a torus. It was also used in
Raeymaekers:2021ypf
;
Meruliya:2021utr
for different RCFTs, and more recently in
Nicosanti:2025xwu
for abelian TQFTs with general orientable boundaries.

We start by defining the mapping class group average of a seed state in the Hilbert space of a genus-
g
surface
|\rho_{seed}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
as follows

\Big\llangle|\rho_{seed}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}\equiv{|\mathsf{G}|^{g}\over|Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})|}\sum_{\gamma\in Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})}U_{\gamma}|\rho_{seed}\rangle.

(4.74)

By construction, this expression is
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
-invariant and it can be expanded in terms of TBC states, according to subsection
3.2
. Our goal will be to calculate the expansion coefficients

\Big\llangle|\rho_{seed}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}=\sum_{i}c_{i}|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle.

(4.75)

Taking inner product with
\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}|
on both sides of the equation we obtain

\langle\mathcal{L}_{j}|\rho_{\text{seed}}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}D_{ij},~~~D_{ij}\equiv{1\over|\mathsf{G}|^{g}}|\mathcal{L}_{i}\cap\mathcal{L}_{j}|^{g},

(4.76)

where we used the fact that
|\mathcal{L}_{j}\rangle
is
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})
-invariant to simplify the LHS and we used
\langle\mathcal{L}_{j}|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle=|\mathcal{L}_{i}\cap\mathcal{L}_{j}|^{g}
on the RHS, where
\mathcal{L}_{i}\subset\mathsf{G}
is the Lagrangian submodule corresponding to stabilizer state
|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle
. The matrix
D
defined above is the
intersection matrix
.

If
g\geq n
, the states
|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle
are linearly independent (by proposition
2
), therefore
D
is invertible. For
g<n
,
D
may fail to be invertible. This simply means that the coefficients
c_{i}
are not uniquely determined. The obvious way to fix this issue is to expand the state in a linearly independent subset of
|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle
, however this approach is not practical. Instead, we are going to treat
g
as a positive real parameter, so that
D
is invertible except on a discrete subset of values and
c_{i}
can be calculated as functions of
g
. Then,
g
can be sent to the desired value by taking a limit. With this in mind, we can formally treat
|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle
as a basis for all
g
and solve for
c
in (
4.76
)

c=D^{-1}v^{\text{seed}},~~v_{j}^{\text{seed}}\equiv\langle{\mathcal{L}_{j}}|\rho_{\text{seed}}\rangle.

(4.77)

Therefore, we can write

\Big\llangle|\rho_{\text{seed}}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}=\sum_{i,j}(D^{-1})_{ij}v_{i}^{\text{seed}}|\mathcal{L}_{j}\rangle.

(4.78)

The sum over handlebody topologies

As a warmup, consider the seed state
|\rho_{\text{seed}}\rangle=|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{1}}\rangle=|0^{g}\rangle
. The symplectic average of this state is the sum over regular handlebody topologies, in other words, it is the Poincaré series of the vacuum. Since each module
\mathcal{L}_{j}
contains the zero element exactly once, it is clear that

v_{j}^{\boldsymbol{1}}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{j}|0^{g}\rangle={1}

(4.79)

and inserting into (
4.78

\Big\llangle|0^{g}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}=\sum_{i,j}(D^{-1})_{ij}|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle.

(4.80)

This expresses the sum over handlebodies in terms of the intersection matrix of the
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian submodules.

The sum over non-handlebody topologies

Consider now

, for a general
\eta
-Lagrangian stabilizer state (
3.60
). First, we calculate the seed vector
v^{\text{seed}}\equiv v^{\boldsymbol{d}}

v_{i}^{\boldsymbol{d}}\equiv\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle.

(4.81)

For such overlaps there is a significant simplification. Since the state
|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle
is invariant under the entire
\text{Aut}(\mathsf{G})
group, we can choose an automorphism of
\mathsf{G}
that makes the bilinear form anti-diagonal and then bring the Lagrangians
\mathcal{L}_{i}
to the diagonal form (
3.67
). Then,
v_{i}^{\boldsymbol{d}}
is constant on each
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-orbit.

To proceed it is convenient to change the indices in (
4.78
) to the pairs
(\boldsymbol{q},Q)
, where as in subsection
3.2
,
\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{S}
denotes an
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-orbit of TBC states and
Q\in\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\equiv\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})/\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{q}}
labels a TBC state within the orbit. Here
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{q}}
is the subgroup of
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
that stabilizes the diagonal Lagrangian in (
3.67
). The intersection matrix with the new labeling reads

D_{\boldsymbol{q},Q;\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}={|(\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q},Q})\cap(\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}})|^{g}\over|\mathsf{G}|^{g}},

(4.82)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q},Q}
denotes the module in (
3.67
).

We can express the overlap (
4.81
) as

v_{\boldsymbol{q},Q}^{\boldsymbol{d}}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q},Q}|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}|U_{Q}^{\dagger}|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle,

(4.83)

where we used the fact that
|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle
is
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-invariant. Since
v
does not depend on
Q
, we can drop this index.
Using the explicit expressions (
3.68
) and (
3.60
)

|\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\rangle=\left(\sum|a_{1}q_{1},\dots,a_{n}q_{n},b_{1}N/q_{n},\dots,b_{n}N/q_{1}\rangle\right)^{\otimes g},

(4.84)

|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{g}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\left({1\over\sqrt{d_{i}}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{d_{i}}}|Na/d_{i}\rangle\right)^{\otimes 2},

(4.85)

the overlap is given by

\langle\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\mathsf{I}(d_{j},q_{i}),

(4.86)

where

\mathsf{I}(d_{j},q_{i})\equiv{1\over d_{j}}\left|\langle N/d_{j}\rangle\cap\langle q_{i}\rangle\right|\cdot\left|\langle N/d_{j}\rangle\cap\langle N/q_{i}\rangle\right|={\gcd(d_{j},q_{i})\gcd(d_{j},N/q_{i})\over d_{j}}.

(4.87)

We can simplify this expression to

\mathsf{I}(d_{j},q_{i})=\gcd(d_{j},q_{i},N/d_{j},N/q_{i})=\gcd(d_{j},q_{i}),

(4.88)

where in the last step we used that
q_{i}^{2}|N
and
d_{j}^{2}|N
, according to their definitions. Putting everything together, we find the seed vector

v_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\gcd(d_{j},q_{i}).

(4.89)

Plugging into (
4.78
) we have

\Big\llangle|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}}\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}}v_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}D^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{q},Q;\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}|\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}\rangle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}}v_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}Y_{\boldsymbol{q};\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}{1\over|\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}|}|\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}\rangle,

(4.90)

where we defined the matrix

Y_{\boldsymbol{q};\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}\equiv|\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}|\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}D^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{q},Q;\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}.

(4.91)

Since the LHS of (
4.90
) is invariant under
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
, the RHS must also be invariant. This implies that
Y_{\boldsymbol{q};\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}
does not depend on the index
Q^{\prime}
so we can drop it. We defined
Y_{\boldsymbol{q};\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}
this way so that it is symmetric. We define the
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-averaged state

|\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\rangle\equiv{1\over|\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}}|}\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}|\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q},Q}\rangle,

(4.92)

and we can finally write the expression

\Big\llangle|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}}v_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}Y_{\boldsymbol{q};\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}|\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}}\rangle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}|\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}}\rangle.

(4.93)

Above we introduced the “
\lambda
-matrix”

\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}}\equiv\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}}v_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}Y_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime};\boldsymbol{q}}.

(4.94)

The TQFT gravity path integral can now be expressed as

\mathcal{Z}_{gravity}[\mathcal{T},\Sigma_{g};w]=\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{T}}w_{\boldsymbol{d}}\Big\llangle|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{T},\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{S}}w_{\boldsymbol{d}}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}}|\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\rangle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{S}}m_{\boldsymbol{q}}|\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\rangle.

(4.95)

Above we defined the boundary weights

m_{\boldsymbol{q}}\equiv\sum_{\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{T}}w_{\boldsymbol{d}}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}}.

(4.96)

Thus the
\lambda
-matrix in (
4.94
) is an explicit linear map between the bulk and boundary weights, which can be completely determined from the knowledge of all TBCs that the TQFT admits. Classifying the full set of TBCs is not an easy problem and a systematic procedure based on the groupoid structure of
Gaiotto_2021
will be discussed in section
5
.

Some comments are in order. In the special case where
N
is square-free, both
\mathbb{S},\mathbb{T}
have cardinality
1
. In other words, there is a single
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-orbit of TBC and a single
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})
-orbit of representatives topologies. Thus, there is a single state invariant under both groups and (
4.95
) becomes

\mathcal{Z}_{gravity}[\mathcal{T},\Sigma_{g};w]=w_{\boldsymbol{1}}\Big\llangle|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{1}}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp}=w_{\boldsymbol{1}}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{1},\boldsymbol{1}}|\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{1}}}\rangle={w_{\boldsymbol{1}}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{1};\boldsymbol{1}}\over|\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{1}}|}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{1}}}|\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{1},Q^{\prime}}\rangle,

(4.97)

which was proven recently in
Dymarsky:2025agh
.

More generally, the TBC states
|\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q},Q}\rangle
span the
Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})
-invariant subspace and by averaging them over
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
we obtain the states
\{|\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\rangle\}
which span the subspace
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
invariant under
Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})\times\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
. Similarly, the states
|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle
span the
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-invariant subspace
NRS
and their symplectic averages
\{\Big\llangle|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}}\rangle\Big\rrangle_{Sp},\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{T}\}
form another spanning set of
\mathcal{K}
. The dimension of
\mathcal{K}
is given by
\dim\mathcal{K}=\min\{|\mathbb{S}|,|\mathbb{T}|\}
.

In equation (
4.93
) a convex sum of
\eta
-Lagrangian stabilizer states is expressed as linear combination of
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian stabilizer states. Positivity of the Wigner function of projectors onto stabilizer states implies that the entries
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}}
are nonnegative, as long as
g\geq n
. For
g<n
,
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}}
are not uniquely defined, but can always be brought to a nonnegative form.
Therefore, if we require the weights
w_{\boldsymbol{d}}
to be nonnegative, there can be no cancellations on the RHS of (
4.95
) and we unavoidably obtain a (weighted) ensemble of all TBC states. The relation between the weights (
4.96
) is generally not one-to-one, since
\lambda
is not a square matrix, unless
|\mathbb{S}|=|\mathbb{T}|
.

For
g=n
, the matrix
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}}
is square and is in fact “upper triangular” in the sense that for
g=n
, we have
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}}=0
unless
\boldsymbol{d}\leq\boldsymbol{q}
. To see this, note that constructing a TBC state
|\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q},Q}\rangle
involves inserting a network of anyon lines along all cycles of
\mathcal{V}_{g}^{\boldsymbol{d}}
. But when
\boldsymbol{d}\not\leq\boldsymbol{q}
, the space
\mathcal{V}_{g}^{\boldsymbol{d}}
cannot support the network that constructs
|\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\rangle
.

4.2
A prescription for the weights

So far, we have not imposed any constraints on the weights
w_{\boldsymbol{d}}
. In a consistent definition of the quantum gravity path integral, however, the bulk weights should be completely fixed. A concrete proposal was given in
Dymarsky:2024frx
, where the quantum gravity state was defined by applying a genus-reduction map to a uniform average over large-genus handlebodies. We now briefly review this prescription.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{g}
denote the Hilbert space the TQFT assigns to the surface
\Sigma_{g}
.
The dual vector
\langle 0|^{\otimes g^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{H}_{g^{\prime}}^{\star}
can be viewed as a genus-reduction map
\langle 0|^{\otimes g^{\prime}}:\mathcal{H}_{g+g^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{H}_{g}
. Applying this map to (
4.80
) we obtain

\begin{split}\langle 0|^{\otimes g^{\prime}}\Big\llangle|0\rangle^{\otimes(g+g^{\prime})}\Big\rrangle_{Sp}&=\sum_{i,j}(D_{g+g^{\prime}}^{-1})_{i,j}(\langle 0|^{\otimes g^{\prime}})|\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(g+g^{\prime})}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{i,j}(D_{g+g^{\prime}}^{-1})_{i,j}|\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(g)}\rangle,\end{split}

(4.98)

where we introduced indices to explicitly denote the genera at which the states and the intersection matrix are evaluated.
Taking the limit
g^{\prime}\to\infty
, the intersection matrix simplifies
D=\mathds{1}
(see (
4.76
)) and we can formally write

\lim\limits_{g^{\prime}\to\infty}\langle 0|^{\otimes g^{\prime}}\Big\llangle|0\rangle^{\otimes(g+g^{\prime})}\Big\rrangle_{Sp}=\sum_{i}|\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(g)}\rangle.

(4.99)

The LHS of the above equality can be expressed as a linear combination of
\eta
-Lagrangian stabilizer states in which all representative topologies
|\Omega_{\boldsymbol{d}};\gamma\rangle
inevitably arise. For a more rigorous discussion and an illustrative example we refer to
Dymarsky:2024frx
. The boundary weights in the RHS of (
4.99
) are genus-independent, compatible with a holographic interpretation.

5
Constructing Topological Boundary Conditions

In this section we describe a systematic procedure, based on
Gaiotto_2021
, for constructing topological boundary conditions (TBCs) in a class of abelian TQFTs.
An abelian TQFT is specified by data
(\mathsf{G},\mathfrak{q})
, where
\mathsf{G}
is a finite abelian group of odd exponent
N
and
\mathfrak{q}
is a quadratic form. Different quadratic forms related by automorphisms of
\mathsf{G}
define equivalent TQFTs. The intersections of
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian modules are invariant under automorphisms of
\mathsf{G}
, therefore the
\lambda
-matrix of section
4
is the same for all equivalent theories. For concrete calculations it is convenient to fix a representative quadratic form, and for an explicit construction of the CFT ensemble (see section
3.3
) one also needs a concrete Chern–Simons realization (
2.13
).
We will therefore adopt specific conventions.

In
5.1
we consider TQFTs equivalent to untwisted Dijkgraaf-Witten theories
Dijkgraaf:1989pz
with gauge group
\mathcal{G}
of odd exponent. In these theories, we have a decomposition
\mathsf{G}=\mathcal{G}\times\bar{\mathcal{G}}
and the quadratic form can be brought into the block-diagonal form
\mathfrak{q}=q\oplus(-q)
by an automorphism of
\mathsf{G}
, where
q
is a quadratic form on
\mathcal{G}
. The TQFT thus decomposes into sectors
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}
, where
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{G},q)
is described by a Chern–Simons theory (
2.13
) with positive-definite
K
-matrix. The pair
(\mathcal{G},q)
defines a pointed MTC, which encodes the chiral data of a bosonic RCFT
Fuchs:2002cm
. Elements of the anyon basis correspond to chiral conformal blocks
Witten:1988hf
. We will therefore refer to
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{G},q)
as the
chiral
sector of the full theory
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}
.
The Hilbert space of the doubled theory
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}
on
\Sigma_{g}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}
is the Hilbert space of
\mathcal{T}
on
\Sigma_{g}
. The isomorphism is given by “vectorization”

\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}})\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}}:~~|a\rangle\langle b|\mapsto|a,b\rangle,\quad a,b\in\mathcal{G}^{g}.

(5.100)

The inverse vectorization map transforms TBC states of
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}
into surface operators of
\mathcal{T}
. On the other hand, the representative topologies determined by
\eta
-Lagrangian submodules are not affected by this map. Each
\eta
-Lagrangian stabilizer state of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}}
maps to a projector onto the corresponding stabilizer state of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}
.

More generally, in
5.2
, we consider theories that can be brought to the form
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\mathcal{T}
by gauging an isotropic subgroup
\mathsf{H}\subset\mathcal{G}
, i.e.
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}
. The two chiral sectors now describe different RCFTs and this construction guarantees their compatibility (i.e. the existence of TBCs
davydov2011wittgroupnondegeneratebraided
).
Now we have the following generalization of (
5.100
)

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}})\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}}:~~|a\rangle\langle b|\mapsto|a,b\rangle,\quad a\in\mathcal{G}^{g},b\in{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}^{g}.

(5.101)

The topological boundary conditions of
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}
are mapped into interfaces between the two theories.

5.1
Construction and classification of surface operators

Surface operators of
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{G},q)
can be constructed by higher-gauging subgroups of
\mathcal{G}

Roumpedakis:2022aik
.
The
F
-symbols of the anyons in
\mathcal{G}
can be chosen to be trivial so that any subgroup
\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
can be gauged on a codimension-1 surface. The possible gaugings are classified by pairs
(\mathsf{H},\nu)
, where
\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
and
\nu\in H^{2}(\mathsf{H},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
is a choice of discrete torsion. To gauge
(\mathsf{H},\nu)
, we insert a network of anyon lines dual to a triangulation of the surface and sum over all charges in
\mathsf{H}
.
A choice of discrete torsion is equivalent to the choice of an antisymmetric form
B:\mathsf{H}\times\widehat{\mathsf{H}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{N}
and we can write
\nu(a,b)=\omega^{2^{-1}B(a,b)}
. Since a genus-
g
surface operator is the
g
-fold tensor product of a genus-
1
surface operator, we only need to consider the sum on a torus

\begin{split}\rho_{{\mathsf{H},\nu}}&={1\over|\mathsf{H}|}\sum_{\alpha\in H_{1}(\Sigma_{1},\mathsf{H})}\nu(\alpha)W(\alpha)\\
&={1\over|\mathsf{H}|}\sum_{a,b\in\mathsf{H}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{G}}\omega^{2^{-1}B(a,b)}\omega^{2^{-1}g(a,b)}|x+a\rangle\langle x|\omega^{g(b,x)}\\
&={1\over|\mathsf{H}|}\sum_{a,b\in\mathsf{H}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{G}}\omega^{(2^{-1}aB+2^{-1}ag+xg)b^{T}}|x+a\rangle\langle x|\\
&=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{H}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{G}}\delta_{2^{-1}a(B+g)+xg\in\mathsf{H}^{\perp}}|x+a\rangle\langle x|\\
&=\sum_{a\in\mathsf{H},h\in\mathsf{H}^{\perp}}|a(-\mathds{1}+Bg^{-1})+h\rangle\langle a(\mathds{1}+Bg^{-1})+h|,\end{split}

(5.102)

where in the third line and afterwards we use the notation
g(a,b)=agb^{T}
for a bilinear form. In the fourth line, we denote by
\mathsf{H}^{\perp}
the dual module to
\mathsf{H}
with respect to
g
. This expression immediately tells us that the surface operator can be obtained by summing over the submodule
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathsf{G}

\rho_{{\mathsf{H},\nu}}=\sum_{(a,b)\in\mathcal{L}}|a\rangle\langle b|,~~\mathcal{L}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{H}^{\perp}&\mathsf{H}^{\perp}\\
\mathsf{H}(Bg^{-1}-\mathds{1})&\mathsf{H}(Bg^{-1}+\mathds{1})\end{pmatrix},

(5.103)

where we wrote the generator of
\mathcal{L}
in a block form and by abuse of notation we use the symbols
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{H}^{\perp}
to denote a generating matrix for these modules. The module
\mathcal{L}
is automatically Lagrangian with respect to the quadratic form
\mathfrak{q}=q\oplus(-q)

KS2

Such extensions are classified by 2-cocycles in the group cohomology
\mu\in H^{2}(\mathsf{Q},\mathsf{H})
. The trivial

means that
\mathcal{G}\cong\mathsf{H}\times\mathsf{Q}
. A non-trivial

results in a higher symmetry with a mixed anomaly
Tachikawa_2020
. This anomaly prevents gauging of arbitrary subgroups of
\widehat{H}\times\mathsf{Q}
, but subgroups of
\widehat{H}
or
\mathsf{Q}
can still be gauged, with some choice of discrete torsion, to obtain another surface operator. This motivates the definition of a groupoid
Gaiotto_2021
, whose objects are surface operators and morphisms are gauging operations. In particular, the groupoid structure implies that all surface operators can be obtained by gauging the trivial surface, in other words (
5.103
) gives the
full
set of
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian submodules.

When the exponent of
\mathcal{G}
is squarefree, the groupoid structure simplifies. For any subgroup
\mathsf{H}
we necessarily have
\mathsf{H}\times\mathsf{Q}\cong\mathcal{G}
and any subgroup can be again gauged. This is reflected in the fact that
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
acts transitively on the Lagrangian subgroups, hence all gauging operations can be realized by the global action of
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
. In particular, all surface operators can be obtained by acting on the trivial surface by
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
, hence the objects of the groupoid are in bijection with
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})/\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{1}}
, where
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{1}}
is the subgroup that stabilizes the trivial surface.

When the exponent of
\mathcal{G}
is not squarefree, not all Lagrangians of the form (
5.103
) belong to the same
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-orbit, since they are non-isomorphic as groups. Instead, surface operators are split into multiple
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-orbits.

To describe this classification, we focus on
\mathsf{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2n}
and specialize the discussion from
3.2
to surface operators by bringing the bilinear form
\mathsf{g}
from (
3.65
) into the diagonal form

\mathsf{g}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{n\times n}&0\\
0&-\mathds{1}_{n\times n}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.105)

Theorem
2
now gives a classification of surface operators. Define the tuples
\boldsymbol{q}
as in theorem
2
.
Then, the generator of any Lagrangian submodule
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{G}\times\bar{\mathcal{G}}=\mathsf{G}
with respect to
\mathfrak{q}=q\oplus(-q)
can be written as

\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{q})&\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{q})\\
\operatorname{diag}(N/\boldsymbol{q})&\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{0})\end{pmatrix}\times Q,

(5.106)

for some
Q\in\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
. Above we defined
(N/\boldsymbol{q})=(N/q_{1},\dots,N/q_{n})
.

The generator matrix (
5.106
) for
Q=\mathds{1}
is in block form and we can write the surface operator explicitly in the computational basis

\rho_{\boldsymbol{q}}=\sum_{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}_{N/\boldsymbol{q}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\boldsymbol{q}}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}q_{i}+b_{i}{N/q_{i}}\rangle\langle a_{i}q_{i}|.

(5.107)

For each
\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{S}
we define the subgroup
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{q}}\subseteq\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
that stabilizes the submodule (
5.106
) with
Q=\mathds{1}
. Then, the cosets
\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}}=\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})/\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{q}}
are in bijection with the surface operators in the orbit labeled by
\boldsymbol{q}
. An arbitrary surface operator is uniquely parametrized by
\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{S}
and
Q\in\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{q}}
as
\rho_{\boldsymbol{d},Q}
.
Note that the surface operators with
\boldsymbol{d}\neq\boldsymbol{1}
are always non-invertible. Operators
\rho_{\boldsymbol{1},Q}
may be invertible, depending on
Q
. In particular
\rho_{\boldsymbol{1},\mathds{1}}
is the trivial (identity) operator.

Surface operators form a non-commutative monoid graded by
\mathbb{S}
. Fusing two operators
\rho_{\boldsymbol{q},Q}
,
\rho_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}
results into an operator with grade
\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime\prime}=\boldsymbol{q}\lor\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}
. The set of invertible operators form a group
\mathbb{G}
, which is a faithful unitary representation of
\mathsf{O}(\mathcal{G})
. Such operators are obtained from Lagrangian submodules with generators of the form
(\mathds{1}|\mathsf{B})
, where
\mathsf{B}\in\mathsf{O}(\mathcal{G})
and they all belong to the orbit
\mathsf{O}_{\boldsymbol{1}}
. The identity element of
\mathbb{G}
is the trivial surface operator
\rho_{\boldsymbol{1},\mathds{1}}
, described by the module
(\mathds{1}|\mathds{1})
, while the surface
\rho_{\boldsymbol{1},-\mathds{1}}
is the charge-conjugation operation, described by
(\mathds{1}|-\mathds{1})
. From the CFT point of view, the invertible operators describe
T
-dualities, preserving the (unflavored) Siegel-Narain partition function.

5.2
Construction of interfaces

In this subsection, we describe the construction of interfaces between
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}
, for some isotropic
\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
. The diagonal interface
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}
can be constructed by starting with
\mathcal{T}
on
\Sigma_{g}\times I
and gauging the non-anomalous subgroup
\mathsf{H}
in half of the space
KNBalasubramanian:2025vum
. Then, the rest of the interfaces can be obtained by fusing
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}
with surface operators of
\mathcal{T}
.

According to the discussion in
2.4
, a
q
-isotropic submodule
\mathsf{H}\subset\mathcal{G}
is non-anomalous and can be gauged in a handlebody. The resulting theory
\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}
has spectrum of lines in
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{H}^{\perp}/\mathsf{H}
and torus Hilbert space with basis
\{|a\rangle_{\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}},a\in\mathsf{K}\}
.
The interface
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}
between the original and the gauged theory can be constructed by placing
\mathcal{T}
on
\Sigma_{1}\times[0,1]
and gauging
\mathsf{H}
in half of the space
\Sigma_{1}\times[0,1/2]
. This operator is an isometric embedding
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}
, which we can write explicitly in the anyon bases of the two theories

\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}={1\over\sqrt{|\mathsf{H}|}}\sum_{a\in\mathsf{K}}\sum_{h\in\mathsf{H}}|h+\pi^{-1}(a)\rangle\langle a|_{\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}},

(5.108)

where
\pi:\mathsf{H}^{\perp}\to\mathsf{K}
is the canonical projection. The conjugate map
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\dagger}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}}
is a projection onto the Hilbert space of
\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}
. The genus-
g
version of this operator is simply the
g
-fold tensor product of the torus operator.

The image of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}
at genus-
g
is the code subspace of the stabilizer corresponding to the module
\mathsf{H}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{2g}
and the Hilbert space of
\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}
can be viewed as the space of logical states. Therefore
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\dagger}
are encoding/decoding maps. We emphasize here that the encoding (
5.108
) embeds a system of
g
“logical” qudits into another system of
g
“physical” qudits with larger local dimensions of their Hilbert space, rather than a system of more qudits as is typical in quantum error correction.

6
Examples

In this section we consider various examples in which we explicitly construct all TBC states by applying the method of section
5
and then calculate the
\lambda
-matrix and TQFT gravity state (
4.72
). We emphasize that even though we choose a specific quadratic form to calculate the
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian submodules, the classification of TBC states and the
\lambda
-matrix are the same for all equivalent TQFTs.

6.1
Example I:
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}

We consider theories of the form
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{G},q)
for a cyclic group
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}
for some isotropic
\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
. We set
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}
for some odd prime
p
.

A concrete realization of the
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}
theory is given by the
U(1)_{p^{m}}\times U(1)_{-p^{m}}
CS theory studied in
Raeymaekers:2021ypf
;
Raeymaekers_2024
or its level-rank dual
SU(p^{m})_{1}\times SU(p^{m})_{-1}

AngelinosWZW
. Moreover, by an automorphism of
\mathsf{G}
, this theory becomes the untwisted Dijkgraaf-Witten theory studied recently in
Nicosanti:2025xwu
, which is the same as the level-
p^{m}
toric code.

6.1.1
Surface operators

The group
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}
has
m+1
subgroups
\mathsf{H}_{k}=p^{m-k}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
that can be gauged on a surface. Since

is trivial, there is a unique surface operator for each subgroup. Equivalently, each surface operator corresponds to a Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\times\bar{\mathcal{G}}=\mathsf{G}
with respect to the quadratic form
q\oplus(-q)=\mathfrak{q}
. By applying (
5.103
) we obtain

\mathcal{L}_{k}=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}p^{k}&p^{k}\\
0&p^{m-k}\end{pmatrix}&0\leq k\leq\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor\\
\begin{pmatrix}p^{m-k}&-p^{m-k}\\
0&p^{k}\end{pmatrix}&\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor<k\leq m.\end{cases}

(6.109)

The genus-
g
surface operators of
\mathcal{T}
map to genus-
g
TBC states
|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle
of
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}
.

6.1.2
Gravity State of
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}

We now calculate the
\lambda
-matrix and the gravity state for
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}
using the formula (
4.95
).
The number of symplectic orbits
\mathbb{T}
at genus
g
is given by proposition
1

|\mathbb{T}|={(\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor+g)!\over(\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor)!g!},

(6.110)

while for the orbits of TBC

|\mathbb{S}|=\left\lfloor{m\over 2}\right\rfloor+1.

(6.111)

These two are equal only at genus
1
, hence the
\lambda
-matrix is square only in that case.

The intersection matrix
D
is Toeplitz

D_{ij}={1\over p^{mg}}|\mathcal{L}_{i}\cap\mathcal{L}_{j}|^{g}={1\over p^{g|i-j|}}

(6.112)

and its inverse is tridiagonal

(D^{-1})_{ij}={p^{g}\over p^{2g}-1}\left((p^{g}+p^{-g})I-\tilde{I}p^{-g}-\epsilon\right),

(6.113)

where
I
is the identity matrix,
\tilde{I}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}(\delta_{i0}+\delta_{im})
and
\epsilon_{ij}=\delta_{|i-j|,1}
. Note that
\mathcal{L}_{i}
and
\mathcal{L}_{m-i}
belong to the same
\mathsf{O}(\mathsf{G})
-orbit. The matrix
Y
is therefore

Y={1\over p^{g}-p^{-g}}(2(p^{g}+p^{-g})I-2\epsilon-\hat{I}),

(6.114)

where
\hat{I}_{ij}=-2p^{-g}\delta_{i0}\delta_{j0}
if
m
is even and
\hat{I}_{ij}=-2p^{-g}\delta_{i0}\delta_{j0}-(p^{g}+p^{-g})\delta_{ij}\delta_{i,{m-1\over 2}}
if
m
is odd.

The next step is to calculate the “seed” matrix (
4.89
)

v_{p^{i}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}=\prod_{j=1}^{g}\gcd(p^{i},d_{j})

(6.115)

and finally the
\lambda
-matrix

\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},p^{i}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1}v_{p^{j}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}Y_{j,i}.

(6.116)

Genus 1:

At genus
g=1
, the
\lambda
-matrix is square. Using that
\boldsymbol{d}\in\{1,p,\dots,p^{\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor}\}
we calculate the seed vector

v_{p^{i}}^{p^{j}}=p^{\min\{i,j\}}.

(6.117)

Using (
6.114
) with (
6.116
), we find the matrix

\lambda_{p^{i},p^{j}}={2p^{i+1}\over p+1}\begin{cases}1&i=j\\
{p-1\over p}&i<j\\
0&i>j,\end{cases}

(6.118)

while for even

\lambda_{p^{i},p^{j}}={2p^{i+1}\over p+1}\begin{cases}1&i=j<{m\over 2}\\
{p-1\over p}&i<j<{m\over 2}\\
{p-1\over 2p}&i<{m\over 2},j={m\over 2}\\
{p+1\over 2p}&i=j={m\over 2}\\
0&i>j.\end{cases}

(6.119)

Explicitly, the gravity state, for arbitrary bulk weights

, is given by

\mathcal{Z}[\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}},\Sigma_{1};w]=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}w_{p^{i}}{p^{i+1}\over p+1}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle+|\mathcal{L}_{m-i}\rangle+{p-1\over p}\sum_{j=i+1}^{m-i-1}|\mathcal{L}_{j}\rangle\right)&m\text{ odd}\\
\sum_{i=0}^{m/2-1}w_{p^{i}}{p^{i+1}\over p+1}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle+|\mathcal{L}_{m-i}\rangle+{p-1\over p}\sum_{j=i+1}^{m-i-1}|\mathcal{L}_{j}\rangle\right)+w_{p^{m/2}}p^{m/2}|\mathcal{L}_{m/2}\rangle&m\text{ even}.\end{cases}

(6.120)

The genus-reduction prescription

amounts to fixing the bulk weights such that the coefficient of each TBC state that appears in (
6.120
) is equal to
1
. This results in the following bulk weights

w_{p^{i}}=\begin{cases}{p+1\over p^{2i+1}}&0\leq i<m/2\\
p^{-m}&i=m/2.\end{cases}

(6.121)

Genus
>1

For

the

-matrix is no longer square and different choices of bulk weights can lead to the same result. This happens because the

-Lagrangian stabilizer states become linearly dependent. We will calculate the gravity state by setting some bulk weights to zero. Specifically, define the “diagonal” weights

The restricted

-matrix then reads (for simplicity we present it only for odd

\bar{\lambda}_{p^{i},p^{j}}={p^{g(i+1)}\over p^{g}+1}\begin{cases}1&i=j\\
{p^{g}-1\over p^{g}}&i<j\\
0&i>j.\end{cases}

(6.123)

The gravity state (for odd
m
) is given by

\mathcal{Z}[\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}},\Sigma_{g};\tilde{w}]=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\tilde{w}_{p^{i}}{p^{gi+g}\over p^{g}+1}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{i}\rangle+|\mathcal{L}_{m-i}\rangle+{p^{g}-1\over p^{g}}\sum_{j=i+1}^{m-i-1}|\mathcal{L}_{j}\rangle\right).

(6.124)

The even

in (
6.120

The prescription

fixes the remaining bulk weights as follows

\tilde{w}_{p^{i}}=\begin{cases}{p^{g}+1\over p^{g(2i+1)}}&0\leq i<m/2\\
p^{-gm}&i=m/2.\end{cases}

(6.125)

6.1.3
Interfaces

The quadratic form
q
of
\mathcal{G}
vanishes on the subgroups
\mathsf{H}_{k}=p^{m-k}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
for

To gauge

and we identify lines in
\mathsf{H}_{k}

1

\mathsf{K}_{k}=\mathsf{H}_{k}^{\perp}/\mathsf{H}_{k}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m-2k}}
.
Summing over all insertions of the lines in

on the handlebody

we obtain a state that is a

|\Lambda_{k}\rangle^{\otimes g}={1\over\sqrt{H_{1}(\mathcal{V}_{g},\mathsf{H}_{k})}}\sum_{a\in H_{1}(\mathcal{V}_{g},\mathsf{H}_{k})}W(a)|0\rangle^{\otimes g},

(6.126)

where after a short calculation

|\Lambda_{k}\rangle={1\over p^{k/2}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}}|ap^{m-k}\rangle.

(6.127)

Inserting lines with charges in

X_{ap^{k}}|\Lambda_{k}\rangle={1\over p^{k/2}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}}|ap^{k}+bp^{m-k}\rangle.

The interface

\mathcal{I}_{k}={1\over p^{k/2}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}}|ap^{k}+bp^{m-k}\rangle\langle a|_{\mathcal{T}/\mathsf{H}_{k}}.

(6.129)

With this process we built the diagonal interface between
\mathcal{T}=(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}},q)
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m-2k}},q)

\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
has

\mathsf{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{m-2k}}

The rest of the interfaces can be obtained by fusing (

For fixed

\tilde{\rho}_{i}\equiv p^{-k/2}\rho_{i}\mathcal{I}_{k}=\begin{cases}\sum|p^{m-i}a+p^{i}b\rangle\langle p^{i-k}b|&m/2\geq i\geq k\\
\sum|p^{i}a-p^{m-i}b\rangle\langle p^{m-i-k}b|&m-k\geq i>m/2\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(6.130)

and we denote the TBC state corresponding to

|\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\rangle

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{m-2k}}

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i}=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}p^{i}&p^{i-k}\\

\begin{pmatrix}p^{m-i}&-p^{m-i-k}\\
p^{i}&0\end{pmatrix}&\lfloor{m\over 2}\rfloor<i\leq m-k.\end{cases}

(6.131)

6.1.4
Gravity states of
\mathcal{T}\times{\bar{\mathcal{T}}}^{\prime}

From (

D_{i,j}={|\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}|^{g}\over p^{g(m-k)}}=p^{-g|i-j|},~~k\leq i,j\leq m-k.

Genus 1:

v_{p^{j}}^{p^{i}}\equiv\langle\Omega_{p^{i}}|\mathcal{L}_{j}\rangle=p^{\min\{i,j\}}p^{-\min\{i/2,k\}},~~0\leq i\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor,~k\leq j\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor.

6.2.2

Outlook

In this work, we defined the gravitational path integral of a 3D abelian TQFT
\mathcal{T}
as a sum over all topologies with genus-
g
boundary
\Sigma_{g}
. The path integral of
\mathcal{T}
prepares an
\eta
-Lagrangian stabilizer state on any single topology with boundary
\Sigma_{g}
, partitioning the topologies into finitely many equivalence classes. Thus, the gravitational path integral can be equivalently defined as a weighted sum over these stabilizer states. The gravitational path integral can then be expressed as a weighted sum over
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian stabilizer states, which describe topological boundary conditions on
\Sigma_{g}
.
From the boundary perspective, this amounts to an average over partition functions of two-dimensional CFTs, specifically a finite ensemble of Narain CFTs, where the chiral central charge is a multiple of
8
4
4

4

When the chiral central charge is not a multiple of 24, the framing anomaly of the bulk manifold can be canceled by stacking copies of
(E_{8})_{1}
.
.

We introduced the
\lambda
-matrix, relating bulk and boundary weights and developed a method to calculate its entries from the TBCs that the TQFT admits. Specifically, the
\lambda
-matrix is given by

\lambda_{\boldsymbol{d},\boldsymbol{q}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime};\boldsymbol{q}}v_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}Y_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime};\boldsymbol{q}},

(7.187)

where the matrix
Y_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime};\boldsymbol{q}}
is determined by the overlaps between orbits of TBC states on
\Sigma_{g}
and
v_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}
is a matrix that can be determined only from the
1
-form symmetry group
\mathsf{G}
and the genus of the boundary surface.
Above, the index
\boldsymbol{d}
denotes a symplectic orbit of representative topologies, while
\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}
denote orthogonal orbits of TBCs.
We illustrated this calculation with several examples.

In the process, we also described (section
5
) a systematic method for constructing all TBC of abelian TQFTs of the form
\mathcal{T}\times\bar{\mathcal{T}}
, by taking advantage of their correspondence with surface operators of
\mathcal{T}
, as well as the groupoid structure of surface operators
Gaiotto_2021
. We further generalized this to some theories of the form
\mathcal{T}\times\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
, where the classification of TBCs is equivalent to the classification of interfaces between the two sectors.

We conclude with a list of open problems.

The
\lambda
-matrix

In this paper we computed the
\lambda
-matrix in explicit examples using a brute-force approach, which requires the full set of TBCs. Although we described a systematic procedure for constructing this set, it becomes impractical for groups of large length. Interestingly, the
\lambda
-matrices we obtained have a simple structure. In fact, only overlaps between orthogonal orbits of TBC states are needed, rather than overlaps between all states individually. Alternatively, the construction of section
4
can be reformulated in terms of overlaps between symplectic orbits. These two observations suggest that a simpler method to compute
\lambda
may exist.

Universality of bulk weights

In this work we largely remained agnostic about the choice of bulk weights. Consistency with the holographic principle requires that the boundary weights are independent of the genus of the surface, but this does not uniquely fix the bulk weights. A prescription consistent with holography was proposed in
Dymarsky:2024frx
, but it is unclear whether other consistent prescriptions exist. Alternatively, one could define the TQFT gravity path integral by starting from a (formal) sum over all distinct bulk topologies with boundary
\Sigma_{g}
, weighted by a measure
\mu
, as in
Nicosanti:2025xwu
. For consistency with a path-integral interpretation,
\mu
should be TQFT-independent. Since the TQFT path integral on any topology yields an
\eta
-Lagrangian stabilizer state, this construction reduces to (
4.72
), with
w_{\boldsymbol{d}}
determined by
\mu
, possibly after regularization. An open question is what constraints, if any, the universality of
\mu
imposes on the bulk weights
w_{\boldsymbol{d}}
.

Semi-classical gravity

The handlebody topologies
\boldsymbol{1}\in\mathbb{T}
can be interpreted as the semi-classical contributions to the gravitational path integral, which are always present. The non-handlebody topologies
\boldsymbol{d}\neq\boldsymbol{1}
then play the role of quantum corrections. The semi-classical limit corresponds to sending either the exponent or the length of
\mathsf{G}
to infinity, in which case we expect the contributions from non-handlebody topologies to be suppressed. Indeed, the prescription in
4.2
along with (
4.89
) imply that the bulk weights
w_{\boldsymbol{d}}
with
\boldsymbol{d}\neq\boldsymbol{1}
are suppressed as the exponent
N
is increased. Our approach is not straightforward to apply to groups of large length, but one should instead reformulate section
4
using the overlap matrix of
\eta
-Lagrangian submodules. It would be interesting to rigorously and generally prove this suppression of non-handlebody contributions.

Generalizations

Several extensions of this work remain. While we focused on groups of odd exponent and specific classes of quadratic forms, the construction should generalize to all abelian TQFTs that admit TBCs, though the details deserve careful treatment. Another direction is to include correlation functions. In this case the boundary surface has punctures, enlarging the mapping class group action and requiring a generalized averaging procedure
Romaidis:2023zpx
. Finally, extending our framework to non-abelian TQFTs is another interesting direction. The approach of section
4
can still be used if one can identify a spanning set of
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
-invariant states, however the TBC states (corresponding to physical modular invariants) do not generally span this space. From the holographic perspective, the gravitational state must be expressed entirely in terms of physical invariants, which means the bulk weights must be tuned so that the resulting state lies in their span.

Appendix A
Higher symmetry lines and Wigner functions

In this appendix we review how to construct higher symmetry lines on the surface operator
Roumpedakis:2022aik
. These “higher lines” embedded on the surface are the phase-space point operators in the stabilizer formalism. These operators form a very useful alternative orthonormal basis for
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{H})
. They are typically defined by the symplectic Fourier transform of the Heisenberg-Weyl operators
Gross_2006

\hat{L}^{(g)}(\alpha)={1\over|\mathsf{G}|^{g}}\sum_{\beta\in\mathsf{G}^{2g}}\omega^{J(\alpha,\beta)}\mathcal{W}(\beta).

(1.188)

We can interpret (
1.188
) as lines on a surface operator obtained by gauging
\mathsf{G}
with trivial torsion on a genus-
g
surface inserted parallel to the two boundaries of
\Sigma_{g}\times I
. This surface is the charge-conjugation operator

\rho_{\mathsf{G},0}={1\over|\mathsf{G}|^{g}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf{G}\otimes H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},\mathbb{Z})}\mathcal{W}(\alpha)=\left(\sum_{x\in\mathsf{G}}|x\rangle\langle-x|\right)^{\otimes g}.

(1.189)

Gauging the group
\mathsf{G}
results in a “quantum symmetry”
\widehat{\mathsf{G}}\cong\mathsf{G}
of lines confined to the surface. These lines can be constructed by bringing together two bulk lines of opposite orientation and same charge and fusing them with the surface from opposite sides

\hat{L}^{(g)}(\alpha)=\mathcal{W}(\alpha)\rho_{\mathsf{G},0}\mathcal{W}(\alpha)^{\dagger}.

(1.190)

On a torus, they read explicitly

\hat{L}^{(1)}(a,b)=\sum_{x\in\mathsf{G}}\omega^{2bx}|x+a\rangle\langle-x+a|.

(1.191)

The expansion coefficients of an operator
\rho
in the
\hat{L}(\alpha)
basis is the Wigner function

\mathbb{W}_{\rho}(\alpha)\equiv{1\over|\mathsf{G}|^{g}}\text{tr}(\hat{L}(\alpha)^{\dagger}\rho).

(1.192)

The Wigner function has several important properties
Gross_2006
. One of the most important properties we make use of is that the Wigner function of a stabilizer state is nonnegative.

More generally, we can construct “higher” line operators by performing the Fourier transform over a subgroup
\mathsf{H}
and adding discrete torsion
\nu\in H^{2}(\mathsf{H},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. This process constructs line operators labeled by
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}
and confined on a surface

\hat{L}_{\mathsf{H},\nu}^{(g)}(\alpha)={1\over|\mathsf{H}|^{g}}\sum_{\beta\in\mathsf{H}^{2g}}\omega^{J(\alpha,\beta)}\nu(\beta)\mathcal{W}(\beta),~~~\alpha\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}^{2g}.

(1.193)

The higher symmetry lines belonging to
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}
can be obtained by fusing two bulk lines with charges in
\mathsf{H}
and opposite orientation, from opposite sides of the surface

\hat{L}_{\mathsf{H},\nu}^{(g)}(\alpha)=\mathcal{W}(\alpha)\rho^{\otimes g}_{\mathsf{H},\nu}\mathcal{W}(\alpha)^{\dagger},~~\alpha\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}^{2g}.

(1.194)

Note that in the above definition
\hat{L}_{\mathsf{H},\nu}(\alpha)=\rho_{\mathsf{H},\nu}
if
\alpha\in(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H})^{2g}
.
Similar to the Heisenberg-Weyl operators, they can be written as a tensor product of torus operators

\hat{L}^{(g)}(\alpha)=\hat{L}(\alpha_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{L}(\alpha_{g}).

(1.195)

We can write the torus operators explicitly

\hat{L}_{\mathsf{H},\nu}(a,b)=\mathcal{W}(a,b)\rho_{\mathsf{H},\nu}\mathcal{W}(a,b)^{\dagger}=\sum_{(x,h)\in\mathsf{H}\times\mathsf{H}^{\perp}}\omega^{-2\mathsf{g}(b,x)}|h+a+x(-\mathds{1}+B\mathsf{g}^{-1})\rangle\langle h+a+x(1+B\mathsf{g}^{-1})|.

(1.196)

Above
a,b\in\mathsf{H}
, since
\hat{L}_{\mathsf{H},\nu}(a,b)=\rho_{\mathsf{H},\nu}
for
a,b\in\mathsf{H}^{\perp}
.

If
\mathsf{H}\neq\mathsf{G}
, there is another set of symmetry lines parametrized by
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
. This is the residual symmetry after gauging
\mathsf{H}
. They can be constructed by attaching lines of the same charge and orientation from the two opposite sides of the surface

\tilde{L}_{\mathsf{H},\nu}(a,b)=\mathcal{W}(2^{-1}a,2^{-1}b)\rho_{\mathsf{H},\nu}\mathcal{W}(2^{-1}a,2^{-1}b).

(1.197)

Unless
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}\times\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}\cong\mathsf{G}
, the symmetry lines generally have a mixed anomaly. Subgroups of either
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}
or
\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}
are non-anomalous and can be gauged separately, but mixed subgroups cannot.

Due to the fact that the surface operators are invariant under the mapping class group, the higher line operators transform covariantly under
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N})

U_{\gamma}L_{\mathsf{H},\nu}^{(g)}(\alpha,\beta)U_{\gamma}^{\dagger}=L_{\mathsf{H},\nu}^{(g)}(\alpha\gamma,\beta\gamma),~~~\gamma\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z}_{N}).

(1.198)

As a consequence the Wigner function transforms contravariantly

\mathbb{W}_{U_{\gamma}\rho U_{\gamma}^{\dagger}}(\alpha\gamma)=\mathbb{W}_{\rho}(\alpha).

(1.199)

The minimal matrix of a
\mathfrak{q}
-Lagrangian submodule

Without loss of generality, we set
N=p^{m}
.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lagrangian submodule of
\mathsf{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}^{2n}
with respect to
\mathfrak{q}
associated with the bilinear form (
3.65
).
A
minimal matrix

M

\mathcal{L}
is an
n\times 2n
matrix which, up to permutation of columns, satisfies the following conditions:

1.

Each row of
M
lies in
\mathcal{L}
.

2.

M_{ij}=0
whenever
j<i
.

3.

The entries of the
i
-th row have greatest common divisor
p^{k_{i}}
.

4.

The sequence of exponents satisfies
0\leq k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{n}\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor
.

5.

The sequence
(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
is minimal among all choices of matrices satisfying the above.

Lemma 1
.

A minimal matrix
M
always exists, and it uniquely determines the submodule
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

We first show existence of
M
.
From the definition,
p^{k_{1}}
is the greatest common divisor of the entries of all elements of
\mathcal{L}
.
Since
\mathcal{L}
is Lagrangian,
k_{1}\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor
. After a
\mathfrak{q}
-preserving permutation of columns if necessary, there exists a vector
v_{1}\in\mathcal{L}
of the form

v_{1}=(p^{k_{1}},*,\dots,*).

Define the submodule

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\{v\in\mathcal{L}:v=(0,x_{2},\dots,x_{2n})\}.

Then
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+\langle v_{1}\rangle
.
For each
v=(0,x_{2},\dots,x_{2n})\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, define

\bar{v}=(\bar{x}_{2},\dots,\bar{x}_{2n-1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m-2k_{1}}}^{2n-2},

where
p^{k_{1}}\bar{x}_{i}=x_{i}(\!\!\!\!\mod p^{k-k_{1}})
.
Set

\bar{\mathcal{L}}=\{\bar{v}:v\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\}.

We claim that
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
is a Lagrangian submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m-2k_{1}}}^{2n-2}
.
Suppose not; then there exists
u
such that
\bar{\mathsf{g}}(u,\bar{v})=0
for all
\bar{v}\in\bar{\mathcal{L}}
, where
\bar{\mathsf{g}}
is the bilinear form restricted to
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m-2k_{1}}}^{2n-2}
.
Choose
u^{\prime}=(0,y_{2},\dots,y_{2n-1},0)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}^{2n}
such that
\bar{u}^{\prime}=u
. Then there exists
y_{2n}
such that

\mathsf{g}\big(v_{1},(0,y_{2},\dots,y_{2n-1},y_{2n})\big)=0,

implying

, a contradiction.
Thus
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
is Lagrangian. By induction on
(m,n)
,
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
admits a minimal matrix and choosing a lift produces a minimal matrix
M
for
\mathcal{L}
.

We now show that

uniquely determines
\mathcal{L}
. By induction, it suffices to show that the vector

(0,\dots,0,p^{m-k_{1}})\in\mathcal{L}.

This is true since from the definition it follows that
p^{k_{1}}
is the gcd of entries of all elements of
\mathcal{L}
.
Hence
\mathcal{L}
is uniquely determined by
M
.

CT combined with 3D-CTBA to analyze the vasculature around pulmonary ground glass nodules and its value in diagnosis
Authors: Hongzhen Zhao, Xiaopeng Zhang, Ling Xiao, Yajing Niu, Wenbo Wu, Wenfei Xue, Zheng Yuan, Guochen Duan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe purpose of this study was to analyze the vascular characteristics around pulmonary nodules with a diameter of 10-20mm. To explore their predictive value for the pathological nature of pulmonary nodules, computed tomography (CT) combined with three-dimensional computed tomography-bronchography and angiography (3D-CTBA) technique have been chosen as the method.Methods202 patients who were treated from January 2020 to December 2022 have been studied. They were all diagnosed with pulmonary ground glass nodules (GGNs) and obtained final pathologic results at the Department of Thoracic Surgery of Hebei General Hospital. In the meanwhile, the medical records were complete be enrolled in this study. All patients had underwent a chest CT scan in our hospital. The vessels surrounding the nodules were categorized into four types based on their relationships with the pulmonary nodules on the 3D reconstructed images. Type I (passing through): the vessel passes through the lesion, with no significant change in vessel size or shape throughout the path of travel; Type II (bypassed): vessels pass adjacent to the nodule, but no branch vessels supply the diseased tissue; Type III (twisted/dilated): twisting or thickening of blood vessels passing through the nodule, but no increase in number; Type IV (complex): a more complex vascular system other than those described in the above types, such as with irregular vasodilatation and convergent coexistence of vessels from multiple supply vessels. Data was analyzed by SPSS (version 26.0, Inc, Chicago, USA), with continuous data expressed as mean and standard deviation/Median (IQR), categorical variables expressed as numbers and percentages. For quantitative variables were analyzed using the Kruskal–Wallis test or Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test or Chi-square test. Furthermore, the GGN-vessel relationship was compared between patients with microinvasive adenocarcinoma (MIA) and invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC). A P-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve was used to determine the optimal diameter cut-off values for distinguishing IAC from MIA in nodules measuring 10–20 mm, with the Youden index applied to identify the cut-off value with the highest sensitivity and specificity.ResultsAccording to the pathology, all patients were divided into 3 groups:Benign nodule group (32 cases. 2 cases are sclerosing pneumoblastoma. 18 cases are found inflammatory lesions. 2 cases are pulmonary sarcoidosis. 1 case is pulmonary cyst. 7 cases are pulmonary hamartoma. 2 cases are pulmonary tuberculosis). Pre-infiltrative lesion group (36 cases. 4 cases are atypical adenomatous hyperplasia (AAH). 32 cases are adenocarcinoma in situ(AIS)). Infiltrative adenocarcinoma group (134 cases. 48 cases are MIA. 86 cases are IAC). The diameter of the pulmonary nodules of each group is: 13.0(11.0–14.7)mm, 13.0(12.0–15.0)mm, and 16.0(14.0–18.0)mm. The difference is statistically significant (P ≤ 0.001). 101 cases of mixed ground glass nodules (mGGNs) were found in all invasive adenocarcinoma groups and count a percentage of 75.4% (P ≤ 0.001). In invasive adenocarcinoma group, Type III (28/31) counts 90.3% (P = 0.009) and type IV (21/23) counts 91.3% (P = 0.008). This result is statistically different. The combination of type III and type IV vascular relationships showed a significant difference in the differential diagnostic value of the three groups of lung nodules (P < 0.001). For nodules 10–20 mm in diameter, the diameter cut-off value for IAC and MIA was 13.5 mm (AUC = 0.814, 95% CI: 0.74–0.89; specificity = 96.5%, sensitivity = 52.1%). The presence of Type IV vascular relationships in pure ground-glass nodules (pGGNs) showed a significant association with invasive adenocarcinoma (P = 0.038). Moreover, this vascular pattern demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence in invasive adenocarcinoma compared to pre-invasive lesions (P < 0.001). The different types of vessels ( arteries, veins, arteries and veins) crossing the nodule were analyzed showing no statistically significant difference (P = 0.078).ConclusionFor nodules with diameters of 10–20 mm, the presence of type III and IV vascular relationships in surrounding vasculature, a solid component, and a diameter ≥ 13.5 mm are more indicative of invasive lesions.Keywords: Pulmonary ground glass nodules, Vasculature, 3D-CTBA, Pathological, Diagnosis

Introduction

Lung cancer is malignant tumor with the highest incidence rate in the world and the leading cause of cancer-related deaths in both male and female above 50 years old [1]. Lung cancer can be classified into small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), with NSCLC accounting for 85% of cases. Almost half of NSCLC patients were diagnosed at stage IV and the 5-year survival rate is less than 10% [2]. In recent years, with the increasing awareness of physical examination and the increasing popularity of chest computed tomography (CT), more and more lung nodules are being detected [3]. According to the Fleischner Society guidelines, surgical resection with regular postoperative follow-up should be performed if a nodule demonstrates growth during follow-up monitoring [4]. This has led to a significant reduction in the mortality rate of lung cancer patients. The progression of lung cancer is a continuous process from atypical adenomatous hyperplasia (AAH), adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS) to microinvasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), and ultimately to invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC) [5]. Therefore, early diagnosis of lung cancer and adopting an individualized therapeutic regimen is important to achieve the better prognosis of patients.

Ground-glass nodules (GGNs) are characterized with increased lesion density on CT and blood vessels and bronchioles are still visible in the lesion. GGNs can be subdivided into pure ground-glass nodules (pGGNs) and mixed ground-glass nodules (mGGNs) [6]. A pGGN is defined as a hazy nodular opacity within the lung parenchyma that exhibits slightly higher density than surrounding tissue while preserving the visibility of traversing vascular and bronchial structures. A mGGN demonstrates heterogeneous attenuation consisting of both ground-glass opacity and solid soft-tissue density components [4]. GGNs can be benign (infection, inflammation and fibrosis) or malignant (lung adenocarcinoma) [7]. Wang et al. [8] collected CT scan data of 50 lung cancer patients and 50 benign nodule recipients from the Pittsburgh Lung Screening Study (PLuSS). According to result of analyze, the Area Under Curve (AUC) of vascular volume in lung cancer patients and benign nodules patients were 0.722 and 0.676. The number of vessels attached around the benign nodules was 4.0(± 4.3) compared to 9.7(± 9.6) in the group of patients with lung cancer, which was significantly different. This result suggests that the surrounding vascular system may serve as a new biomarker for lung cancer. Gao et al. [9] explored the diagnostic value of CT imaging feature for GGNs with a diameter less than 10 mm, the study shows that the different types of blood vessels surrounding the nodules are useful for diagnosis of malignant nodules. The surrounding vascular system can be used as a new biomarker for lung cancer have been further confirmed. Conventional CT imaging analysis may lead to errors in pulmonary vascular assessment. Three-dimensional computed tomography bronchography and angiography (3D-CTBA) converts CT image data into virtual three-dimensional reconstructions using specialized visualization software, providing superior visualization of pulmonary bronchi, arteries, and veins compared to conventional CT. This technique enables: (1) accurate identification of bronchovascular anatomy and its variants, and (2) detection of transfissural structures (including bronchi, arteries, and veins) within interlobar fissures that are often difficult to visualize on standard CT images [10–12]. We conducted a retrospective analysis of 202 patients with pulmonary nodules (10–20 mm in diameter) diagnosed at our hospital between January 2020 and December 2022, and explored the relationship between nodules and surrounding blood vessels by using CT combined with 3D-CTBA to investigate the diagnostic value of different surrounding vascular of nodules for lung nodules.

Methods and materials

The retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hebei General Hospital and patients informed consent was exempted (2025-LW-0023).

Patients

A retrospective analysis of patients who had been diagnosed with pulmonary nodules in our hospital from January 2020 to December 2022. The following conditions of the cases were included in this study: (1) Availability of complete clinical and imaging records; (2) Thin-section CT images with slice thickness ≤ 1.25 mm (3) Ground-glass nodules (GGNs) measuring 10–20 mm in diameter on lung window settings; (4) Pathologically confirmed GGNs (via surgical resection) or GGNs showing resolution/size reduction following anti-inflammatory therapy. Exclusion criteria: (1) incomplete clinical or radiological data; (2) CT images with significant motion artifacts impairing diagnostic interpretation; (3) nodules containing excessive solid components obscuring intranodular vascular visualization; (4) the lung lesions are metastatic from origin tumors in other parts of the body. Finally, a total of 202 patients were included, 73 males and 129 females.

Based on pathological diagnosis,all cases were divided into three groups. 32 cases in the benign nodule group (2 cases of sclerosing pneumocytoma, 18 cases of inflammatory lesions that were significantly smaller or disappeared after anti-inflammatory treatment, 2 cases of pulmonary sarcoidosis, 1 case of pulmonary cyst, 7 cases of pulmonary malformation, and 2 cases of pulmonary tuberculosis); 36 cases in the group of pre-infiltrative lesions (4 cases of AAH, and 32 cases of AIS); and 134 cases in the group of invasive adenocarcinoma (48 cases of MIA, 86 cases of IAC).

CT Examinations and image analysis

All enrolled patients underwent contrast-enhanced chest CT examinations at Hebei General Hospital. All CT images were independently evaluated by two board-certified thoracic radiologists (each with > 20 years of subspecialty experience), who were blinded to pathological results. Any interpretive discrepancies were resolved through consensus review by a third senior radiologist (with > 30 years of dedicated thoracic imaging expertise). This blinded, multi-reader design with adjudication significantly reduced interobserver variability and optimized the reliability of vascular pattern classification. The CT data was imported into reconstruction software (InferOperate Thorax Planning), which converted the data into a 3D virtual model that included lung segments, lesions, bronchi, and pulmonary arteries and veins to allow for further accurate identification of the surrounding vasculature of the nodule. The relationship between the nodule and the peripheral vessels was categorized into four types according to the classification of Gao et al. Type I (passing through): the vessel passes through the lesion, with no significant change in vessel size or shape throughout the path of travel; Type II (bypassed): vessels pass adjacent to the nodule, but no branch vessels supply the diseased tissue; Type III (twisted/dilated): twisting or thickening of blood vessels passing through the nodule, but no increase in number; Type IV (complex): a more complex vascular system other than those described in the above types, such as with irregular vasodilatation and convergent coexistence of vessels from multiple supply vessels [9] (Fig. 1).

Four distinct types of GGNs-vessel relationships and the illustration of 3D—CTBA (a) Type I:a vein traversed an 11-mm pGGN, tapering distally while maintaining a consistent course, postoperative pathology confirmed AIS; (b) Type II: an 18-mm mGGN was traversed by a vein coursing adjacent to it without emitting branches into the lesion, postoperative pathology diagnosed IAC; (c) Type III: an artery traversing a 15-mm diameter mGGN became dilated and altered its course during passage; postoperative pathology confirmed IAC; (d) Type IV:a 17-mm mGGN contained multiple traversing supplying vessels, postoperative pathology confirmed IAC

Pathological diagnosis

All pathological specimens were formalin fixed, hematoxylin–eosin stained and evaluated by two experienced pathologists. If there was a disagreement, the final decision should be made by consultation with a third pathologist. In accordance with the 2011 International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC)/American Thoracic Society (ATS)/European Respiratory Society (ERS) classification criteria for lung adenocarcinomas classify them into AAH, AIS, MIA and IAC [13].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed by using SPSS (version 26.0, Inc., Chicago, USA). Continuous data were expressed as mean and standard deviation/Median (IQR), categorical variables expressed as numbers and percentages. For quantitative variables were analyzed using the Kruskal–Wallis test or Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analyzed using Fishers exact test or Chi-square test. To compare the GGN-vessel relationship between patients with MIA and IAC. P ≤ 0.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant. The Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC) was used to calculate the diameter cut-off values for IAC and MIA with diameters of 10–20 mm, the Youden index was used to determine the cut-off value with the highest sensitivity and specificity.

Results

The clinical characteristics of the patients and the CT features of the pulmonary nodules are listed in (Table 1). A total of 73 male (36.1%) and 129 female (63.9%) patients were included in this study. There were 32 patients in the benign lesion group, 36 in the pre-invasive lesion group, and 134 in the invasive adenocarcinoma group. In the three groups, the number of patients aged over 60 years was 8(25.0%), 13(36.1%), and 92(68.7%), respectively. The statistical difference is significant (P ≤ 0.001). The diameter of lung nodules in the benign lesion group, pre-infiltrative lesion group and invasive adenocarcinoma group were 13.0(11.0–14.75) mm, 13.0(12.0–15.0) mm and 16.0(14.0–18.0) mm. Pulmonary nodules in the invasive lesion group had larger diameters and the difference was statistically significant (P ≤ 0.001). Mixed ground glass nodules were present in 101 cases in all invasive adenocarcinoma groups, which amounted to 75.4% (P ≤ 0.001). The location of the nodule distribution was not significant for the pathologic findings of the nodule (P = 0.309).

CT examination of the relationship between GGNs and vessels

Type I (passing through) were the most common type in all three groups, reaching 65.6%, 63.9%, and 50.0%. Type III (90.3%) and type IV (91.3%) were most common in the invasive adenocarcinoma group, with a statistically significant percentage of 90.3% (P = 0.009) and 91.3% (P = 0.013) (Table 2). And type III and IV was most often seen in invasive adenocarcinoma, for the differential diagnostic value of the three groups of lung nodules showed a significant difference (P < 0.001) (Table 3). The identification of Type IV vascular relationships in pGGNs demonstrated significant predictive value for invasive adenocarcinoma (P = 0.038), establishing this feature as a reliable imaging biomarker of malignancy (Table 4). Subgroup analysis of MIA and IAC showed that type IV vascular relationships were common in IAC, and invasive adenocarcinomas have a bigger diameter than non-invasive adenocarcinomas (13.75 ± 2.79 vs 17.23 ± 2.25), which was statistically different (P < 0.001) (Table 5). Moreover, the nodule diameter was valuable for identification and the cut-off value was determined to be 13.5 mm (AUC = 0.814, 95% CI: 0.74–0.89; specificity = 96.5%, sensitivity = 52.1%) (Table 6, Fig. 2). Analysis of perinodular vessel types (arteries alone, veins alone, or both arteries and veins) revealed no statistically significant differences in their distribution patterns (P = 0.078) (Table 7).

Discussion

In the 2021 World Health Organization (WHO) classification criteria for lung tumors, the AAH and AIS are included in glandular precursor lesions and adenocarcinomas are classified into MIA and IAC [14]. After surgical resection of early-stage lung cancer, the 5-year survival rate of patients with AIS and MIA is almost 100%, and 80% for patients with IAC [15, 16]. Accurate identification of early-stage lung cancer and timely intervention can significantly improve patient survival rates [17]. Angiogenesis is a complex process and a critical driver of cancer development and progression. Under hypoxic conditions caused by rapidly proliferating tumor cells, the balance between pro-angiogenic and anti-angiogenic signaling is disrupted, promoting peritumoral angiogenesis to supply essential nutrients [18]. When a tumor exceeds 2 mm in diameter, it induces neovascularization through the secretion of angiogenic factors to supply the nutrients required for continued proliferation [19]. Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) plays a pivotal role in perinodular angiogenesis and serves as a key therapeutic target for antiangiogenic agents in oncology [20]. However, tumor vasculature is often structurally and functionally abnormal. These malformed vessels fail to deliver sufficient perfusion, creating hypoxic conditions that activate hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF). As the master regulator of VEGF-mediated angiogenesis, HIF upregulates VEGF expression, creating a vicious cycle that sustains tumor growth [21]. Wang et al. [8] reminded the possibility of using the nodule surrounding vessels as a new biomarker for lung cancer. Gao et al. [22] explored the relationship between GGNs and blood vessels and its differential diagnostic value. The results showed that type I vascular relationship was prevalent in both benign and malignant GGN groups. But when the relationship between GGNs and blood vessels was type III or IV, especially type IV, the GGN was most likely to be IAC, and type I or type II vascularity often suggests that the nodule is a benign or pre-invasive lesion. Zhang et al. [23] studied the patients who had been diagnosed peripheral small-cell lung cancer (pSCLC) and peripheral benign lung tumors (pBLT). And further compared the clinical features of patients with the tumors marginal vasculature (blood vessels connected to the tumor) on CT demonstrated. Compared with pBLT, peripheral vasculature in pSCLC had a large diameter, low enhancement, and less calcification. In addition, there were more proximal (70.2% vs 22.1%) and distal (59.6% vs 4.2%) marginal vessels in pSCLC compared to pBLT. The pSCLC had more proximal marginal vessels (1.3 ± 1.4 vs 0.3 ± 0.6), distal marginal vessels (2.4 ± 3.1 vs 0.1 ± 0.5) and total marginal vessels (3.6 ± 3.5 vs 0.4 ± 1.0) than pBLT. The specificity of a positive distal marginal vessel sign was the highest (95.8%), and the total number of marginal vessels had the best performance in differentiating PSCLC from PBLT, Nodules were found to have a higher likelihood of being PSCLC when the number of marginal vessels exceeded 1.5. The perinodal vessels can be used to differentiate pSCLC from benign lung tumors. Ding et al. [24] evaluated the role of microvessel-to-cancer-cell distance (Dmvcc) in the prognosis of patients with non-small cell lung cancer. The results showed that patients with the long Dmvcc were observed to tend to have a poorer survival outcome and identified the cut-off value is 20 mm. A study conducted by Xue et al. [25] showed that tumor vessel diameter was closely related to the growth of pulmonary malignant nodules, and the probability of nodule growth was higher when the diameter of the tumor vessel was more than 0.9 mm. We analyzed the surrounding vascular system of GGNs with a diameter of 10–20 mm using CT combined with 3D-CTBA technique based on the study of Gao, F et al., and explored the value of surrounding vessels in the diagnosis of nodules.

The present study analyzed the relationship between the four types of vessels and the nature of nodule pathology. The result is III and IV vascular relationships were most common in the adenocarcinoma group, with a statistically significant difference (P < 0.05). The difference was even more significant when the diagnosis was made by combining III and IV vascular relationships (P < 0.001). Our analysis indicates that this phenomenon results from peritumoral vasculature composed of structurally and functionally abnormal vessels. These malformed vessels demonstrate, fibroplasia within the lesion invades the surrounding blood vessels leading to distortion of the affected vessels alignment path and stiffness of the vessel wall, and as the malignancy of the lesion increases, the stimulation of fibroplasia becomes more severe, the effect on the surrounding blood vessels is more obvious [26]. Fu et al. [27] analyzed the peripheral vascular features of 149 pathologically confirmed malignant and 130 benign nodules and found that twisting or expansion of the pulmonary vasculature was more common in malignant than in benign GGNs (52.48% VS 18.31%). The results of a study conducted by Wang, S also showed that vascular twisting and tubular wall stiffness were more common in IAC compared to MIA [28].

The pathological diagnosis of pGGNs continues to pose substantial clinical challenges. Our findings reveal that Type IV vascular patterns in pGGNs serve as a robust predictor of invasive adenocarcinoma (P = 0.038), offering a valuable imaging biomarker for preoperative risk assessment. Our subgroup analysis showed that type IV vascular relationships differed significantly between MIA and IAC(P = 0.001). This may be due to the gradual increase in the degree of malignancy, tumor growth and metabolism of the nutritional needs of the gradual increase, which makes the diameter and number of blood vessels around the tumor increased [27]. The tumor tissue growth requires more nutrients than normal tissue and therefore requires a greater blood supply, which can lead to blood vessel expansion [22]. This is consistent with the findings of Gao F et al. In addition, we analyzed the vessel diameters of IAC and MIA. Nodules exceeding 13.5 mm in diameter demonstrate strong predictive value for IAC (AUC = 0.814, 95% CI: 0.74–0.89; specificity = 96.5%, sensitivity = 52.1%).

Our analysis of vascular patterns traversing pulmonary nodules revealed no statistically significant differences in arteries, veins and arteries + veins traveling through the nodule in the three groups of lesions. Mori et al. [29] analysis of the surrounding vasculature in 26 patients with surgically resected pulmonary nodules showed that pulmonary vein-penetrating lesions were seen in all lung cancers but only in 1 out of 8 non-tumoral lesions. Pulmonary vein penetration suggestive of a malignant tumor. Mori K et al. included only 26 patients, whereas our study included 202 patients, which might have resulted due to the larger sample size of our study. Fu BJ and Gao F analyzed the images of 279 and 161 patients and showed that the classification of perinodular vessels did not differ among the different pathologic types of pulmonary nodules [9, 27]. This is consistent with our study, which suggests that the category of vascular supply within the tumor does not change with the increase in lung cancer invasiveness, but the correlation still needs to be confirmed by further studies conducted.

Smoking is considered to be an independent risk factor of the lung cancer development. There are 8 smoking patients in the benign lesion group, 3 smoking patients in the pre-invasive lesion group, and 29 smoking patients in the invasive lesion group, which accounted as 25.0%, 8.3%, and 21.6% of the patients. The difference was not statistically different (P = 0.149). The reason might be the majority of the patients that we collected were adenocarcinoma, whereas smoking often leads to the development of squamous lung cancer, which has a relatively weak association with lung adenocarcinoma. Larger nodule diameter is associated with increased likelihood of malignancy [30]. The size of the nodule diameter in the three groups of patients was 13.0(11.0–14.75) mm, 13.0(12.0–15.0) mm and 16.0(14.0–18.0) mm and the difference is statistically different (P < 0.001). Although we limited the nodule size in the range of 10–20 mm, our results show that it can still be used as a reliable predictor of the nature of the nodule. Our study shows that location has no relationship with the pathological nature of nodules.

Meanwhile, our analysis of pGGNs and mGGNs showed that 29 (90.6%) of 32 patients with benign lesions had pGGNs and 101 (75.4%) of 134 patients with infiltrative lesions had mGGNs, with a statistically significant difference between the two groups (p < 0.001). He et al. [31] analyzed CT images of 189 ground-glass nodules and found that nodules without a solid component were more likely to develop into AAH and AIS than MIA and IAC. Youguo et al. [7] analyzed the CT imaging data of 657 patients with GGNs and found that the larger the diameter of the component, the more likely the nodule was malignant. When the diameter of the component was greater than 6.20 mm, the nodule was more likely to be an invasive adenocarcinoma. This is consistent with our findings.

As a retrospective cohort study, this research is inherently susceptible to selection bias. Additionally, the moderate sample size necessitates validation through large-scale, multi-center investigations to substantiate our postulations.

Conclusion

Although clinically diagnosing 10–20 mm pulmonary GGNs on CT imaging remains challenging, integrating clinical data with perinodular vascular characteristics using both conventional CT and 3D-CTBA can significantly aid decision-making. The possibility that the nodule is an invasive lesion is particularly suggestive when the perinodal vasculature shows type III and IV vascular relationships with a presenting realistic component and diameter ≥ 13.5.

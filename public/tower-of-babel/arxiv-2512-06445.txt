Introduction

In this paper, we consider a connected metric graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathrm{V},\mathrm{E})
, where
\mathrm{E}
is the set of edges and
\mathrm{V}
is the set of vertices. Each bounded edge
e
is identified with a closed and bounded interval
I_{\mathrm{e}}=[0,\ell_{\mathrm{e}}]
with
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}>0
, while each unbounded edge
\mathrm{e}
is identified with a copy of
I_{\mathrm{e}}=\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,+\infty)
and is called a half-line.
Throughout the paper, we assume that
\mathcal{G}
has a finite number of edges (see e.g. Figure
1
).
A connected metric graph has the natural structure of a locally compact metric space, and for any two points
x,y\in\mathcal{G}
, the metric
\operatorname{dist}(x,y)
is given by the shortest distance along the edges. Moreover, a connected metric graph with a finite number of vertices is compact if and only if it contains no half-lines.

Quantum graphs (metric graphs equipped with differential operators) arise naturally as simplified models in mathematics, physics, chemistry and engineering when one considers propagation of waves of various nature through a quasi-one-dimensional (e.g., meso- or nano-scale) system that looks like a thin neighborhood of a graph. For further details on quantum graphs, one may refer to
[
11
]
.

Extensive research has been devoted to the existence and multiplicity of normalized solutions for the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation on metric graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathrm{V},\mathrm{E})

(1.1)

-u^{\prime\prime}-\lambda u=\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u\quad\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}.

In the
L^{2}
-subcritical (
2<p<6
) and
L^{2}
-critical (

[
18
,
21
]
, and on noncompact metric graphs
[

,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
23
,
32

33
]
. In addition, the existence of local minimizer solutions was studied by Pierotti, Soave and Verzini in
[
34

. For any noncompact metric graph
\mathcal{G}
with a finite number of edges and a non-empty compact
core
\mathcal{K}
,
[
26
,
31
]
introduced the following modification of equation (
1.1
), which assumes the nonlinearity affects only the non-empty compact core
\mathcal{K}

(1.2)

-u^{\prime\prime}-\lambda u=\chi_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u\quad\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E},

where
\chi_{\mathcal{K}}
is the characteristic function of the compact core
\mathcal{K}
.
For the existence and non-existence of normalized ground states to equation (

) in the
L^{2}
-subcritical case, we refer to
[

,

[

25
]
. Moreover, in the
L^{2}
-subcritical case, Serra and Tentarelli
[

In the
L^{2}
-supercritical case (
p>6
), the energy functional is no longer bounded from below on the mass constraint. Moreover, the scaling technique–commonly used in the analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
—is no longer applicable, and the Pohozǎev identity is lacking for metric graphs, which creates further complications when attempting to prove the boundedness of Palais-Smale sequences. Additionally, the topological properties of the metric graphs further complicate the problem. To address these challenges, several works have focused on the
L^{2}
-supercritical case. In
[
16
]
, Borthwick, Chang, Jeanjean and Soave proved the existence of bounded Palais-Smale sequences carrying Morse index type information for functionals having a minimax geometry structure under the mass constraint. In
[
20
]
, Chang, Jeanjean and Soave studied the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) on compact metric graphs. Combined with blow-up analysis, monotonicity methods and discussions on Morse index type information of Palais-Smale sequences and bound states, the authors proved the existence of normalized non-constant bound states for equation (
1.1
) when the prescribed mass is sufficiently small. Subsequently, in
[
15
]
, for arbitrarily prescribed mass, Borthwick, Chang, Jeanjean and Soave investigated the existence of normalized solutions for (
1.2
) on noncompact metric graphs having finitely many edges and a non-empty compact core
\mathcal{K}
. The multiplicity of normalized solutions to equation (
1.2
) was proved in
[
19

through employing a more general blow-up analysis.
In
[
22
]
, Dovetta, Jeanjean and Serra studied the existence of normalized solutions for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) on periodic metric graphs,
as well as on noncompact metric graphs with finitely many edges under suitable topological assumptions.

Very recently, Boni, Dovetta and Serra
[
14

first investigated the existence of normalized ground states for the following Schrödinger equations on metric graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathrm{V},\mathrm{E})
with nonlinear point defects

(1.3)

\begin{cases}u^{\prime\prime}=\lambda u&\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E},\\
\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx=\mu&\\
\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathrm{v})=-|u(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u(\mathrm{v})&\text{at every }\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}_{0},\\
\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathrm{v})=0&\text{at every }\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}\setminus\mathrm{V}_{0},\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{V}_{0}\subset\mathrm{V}
,
\mu>0
is prescribed,

(
L^{2}
-subcritical case for equation (

\mu>0
and
p\in(2,4)
. For metric graphs with infinitely many vertices, focusing on

\mu>0
and
p\in(2,4)

Their study highlight that the ground state problem depends critically on the degree of periodicity of the metric graph, on the total number of point defects, and on their dislocation in the metric graph. For further results concerning the Schrödinger equations with nonlinear point defects on metric graphs or in dimension one, we refer the readers to

The condition

\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathrm{v})=0,\quad\forall\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}\setminus\mathrm{V}_{0}

is referred to as the Kirchhoff or natural boundary condition, while

\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathrm{v})=-|u(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u(\mathrm{v}),\quad\forall\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}_{0},

can be interpreted as representing the effect of a deep attractive potential well or a strong attractive defect at all vertices
\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}_{0}
. In the literature, such a condition is commonly termed a nonlinear
\delta
-interaction and is regarded as modeling strongly localized, point-like defects or inhomogeneities in the propagation medium (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
for a comprehensive overview of non-Kirchhoff vertex conditions on graphs). Models with concentrated nonlinearities have been proposed, for instance, in semiconductor theory
[
29
,
30
]
, to describe quantum dynamics in resonant tunneling diodes, as well as the effect of the confinement of charges in small regions.

Formally, solutions to problem (
1.3
) correspond to standing waves of the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}\psi+\partial_{xx}\psi+\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\delta_{\mathrm{v}}|\psi|^{q-2}\psi=0\quad\text{on }\mathcal{G}

via the ansatz
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x)
. The constraint on the
L^{2}
-norm of
u
in (
1.3
), which reflects the normalization requirement imposed on the solutions, is a standard condition on the mass of
u
(sometimes interpreted as the number of particles in the condensate)—a quantity that remains preserved along the evolution in time.

We note that
[
14
]
studied the existence of normalized solutions to equation (
1.3
) only in the
L^{2}
-subcritical case (
2<p<4
). A natural problem therefore to investigate the existence of normalized solutions to equation (
1.3
) in the
L^{2}
-supercritical case (
p>4
). To the best of our knowledge, this problem has not yet been studied on general noncompact metric graphs. In this paper, we take a first step toward filling this gap. As in the cases of equations (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), by
[
14
, Lemma 2.2]
, in the
L^{2}
-subcritical (
2<p<4
) and
L^{2}
-critical (

) cases, the energy functional associated with (
1.4
) is bounded from below and coercive under the mass constraint (requiring the mass to be below a certain threshold when

) and one can use minimization methods to obtain normalized ground states. In contrast, in the
L^{2}
-supercritical
case (
p>4
)
, the energy functional is no longer bounded from below on the mass constraint (one can see this with the functions
\{\varphi^{t}\}
defined in Lemma
5.4
). Moreover, due to the lack of the scaling skill and the absence of Pohozǎev identity for (

Insipired by
[
14
,
15
,
19

L^{2}
-supercritical Schrödinger equation on noncompact metric graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathrm{V},\mathrm{E})
with nonlinear point defects

(1.4)

\begin{cases}u^{\prime\prime}=\lambda u&\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E},\\
\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx=\mu&\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathrm{v})=-|u(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u(\mathrm{v})&\text{at every }\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V},\end{cases}

where
p>4
,
\mathcal{G}
has finitely many edges,
\mu>0
is a given constant, the parameter
\lambda
is a part of the unknown which arises as a Lagrange multiplier,
\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}
means that the edge
\mathrm{e}
is incident at
\mathrm{v}
, and the notation
u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathrm{v})
stands for
u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(0)
or
-u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(\ell_{\mathrm{e}})
, according to whether the vertex
\mathrm{v}
is identified with
0
or
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}
.

Consistently, a function
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is actually a family of functions
(u_{\mathrm{e}})
, where
u_{\mathrm{e}}:I_{\mathrm{e}}\to\mathbb{R}
is the restriction of
u
to the edge
\mathrm{e}
. The usual
L^{p}
space on the metric graph
\mathcal{G}
is defined as follows

L^{p}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigoplus_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}}L^{p}(I_{\mathrm{e}}),

endowed with the norm

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G})}^{p}:=\sum_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{\mathrm{e}}\rVert_{L^{p}(I_{\mathrm{e}})}^{p},\quad\text{ if }p\in[1,\infty)\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G})}:=\max_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{\mathrm{e}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(I_{\mathrm{e}})}.

The inner product in
L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
is

(u,v)_{2}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}uv\,dx.

If
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous on
\mathcal{G}
, then we write
u\in C(\mathcal{G})
. The Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
consists of the set of continuous functions
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u_{\mathrm{e}}\in H^{1}([0,\ell_{\mathrm{e}}])
for every edge
\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}
(note that
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}
can be
+\infty
); the norm in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
is defined as

\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}=\underset{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}}{\sum}\left(\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{\mathrm{e}})}+\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{\mathrm{e}})}\right),

and the inner product in this space is

(u,v)=\underset{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}}{\sum}\int_{I_{\mathrm{e}}}\left(u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}v^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}+u_{\mathrm{e}}v_{\mathrm{e}}\right)\,dx.

For convenience, we will abbreviate
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G})}
as
\lVert u\rVert_{p}
and
\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}
as
\lVert u\rVert
.

Denote the energy functional
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G}):H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathbb{R}
associated with equation (
1.4
) by

(1.5)

E(u,\mathcal{G}):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{2}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}.

We shall study the existence and multiplicity of critical points of the functional
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on the
L^{2}
-sphere

H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}):=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\mid\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{2}=\mu\},

where
\mu>0
.

Our first main result is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be any noncompact metric graph with finitely many edges and

. Then, for every
\mu>0
, problem (
1.4
) has a solution
(u,\lambda)\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\times(0,+\infty)

15
]

\begin{cases}-u^{\prime\prime}-\lambda u=\rho\chi_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u&\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E},\\

with

However, since the blow-up analysis in

\begin{cases}u^{\prime\prime}=\lambda u&\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E},\\

5
, Section 2]
, the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality holds

(2.1)

\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}\leq C_{p,\mathcal{G}}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert_{2}^{\frac{p}{2}}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{\frac{p}{2}},\quad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}),

where
C_{p,\mathcal{G}}:=2^{\frac{p}{2}}\#\mathrm{V}
.

2.2.
Basic notations and results

We first recall some basic notations and results from
[
16
,
15
]
.
Let
(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
and
(H,(\cdot,\cdot))
be infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and assume that
E\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow E^{\prime}
, with continuous injections, where
E^{\prime}
denotes the dual space of
E
. For simplicity, we assume that the continuous injection
E\hookrightarrow H
has norm at most
1
and identify
E
with its image in
H
. Set

\begin{cases}\|u\|^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle,&u\in E,\\
|u|^{2}=(u,u),&u\in H,\end{cases}

and for for
\mu>0
, define the set

S_{\mu}=\{u\in E|\,|u|^{2}=\mu\}.

Clearly,
S_{\mu}
is a smooth submanifold of
E
of codimension
1
, and its tangent space at
u\in S_{\mu}
can be considered as the closed codimension
1
subspace of
E
given by

T_{u}S_{\mu}=\{v\in E|(u,v)=0\}.

In the following,
\|\cdot\|_{*}
and
\|\cdot\|_{**}
denote the operator norms on
L(E,\mathbb{R})
and
L(E,L(E,\mathbb{R}))
, respectively.

Definition 2.1

(
[
15
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
\phi:E\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. We say that
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets, if for any
R>0
, one can find
M=M(R)>0
such that, for any
u_{1},u_{2}\in B(0,R)
,

\|\phi^{\prime}(u_{1})-\phi^{\prime}(u_{2})\|_{*}\leq M\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{\alpha},\quad\|\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{1})-\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{2})\|_{**}\leq M\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{\alpha}.

Definition 2.2

(
[
16
, Definition 1.3]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
. For any
u\in E\backslash\{0\}
, define the continuous bilinear map:

D^{2}\phi(u)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(u)\cdot u}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}}(\cdot,\cdot).

Definition 2.3

(
[
16
, Definition 1.4]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
. For any
u\in S_{\mu}
and
\theta>0
, we define the approximate Morse index by

\hat{m}_{\theta}(u)=\sup\left\{\dim L\bigg|\,L\,\text{is a subspace of }T_{u}S_{\mu}\,\text{such that }D^{2}\phi(u)[\varphi,\varphi]<-\theta\|\varphi\|^{2},\,\forall\varphi\in L\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

If
u
is a critical point for the constrained functional
\phi|_{S_{\mu}}
and
\theta=0
, we say that this is the Morse index of
u
as constrained critical point.

In the context of this paper, we take
E=H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
and
H=L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
. For
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, we denote by
E_{\rho}(\cdot,\mathcal{G}):H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathbb{R}
the energy functional associated with the parameterized equations (
1.7
), defined by

(2.2)

E_{\rho}(u,\mathcal{G}):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{2}-\frac{\rho}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}.

Since
p>4
, it follows that
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
are of class
C^{1}
, and hence
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets in the sense of Definition
2.1
with
\alpha=1
.

The following two abstract theorems were established in
[
16
]
. In the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, these results are used to obtain the bounded Palais-Smale sequences of

constrained on

for almost every

.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
16
, Theorem 1.10]
)
.

Let

be an interval and consider a family of

functionals

for every

, and

(2.3)

\text{either }A(u)\to+\infty\,\,\text{or }B(u)\to+\infty,\,\,\text{as }\,\|u\|\to+\infty.

Suppose moreover that

and
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in

(iv)

\hat{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq 1.

Theorem 2.5

(
[

Let

be an interval and consider a family of

functionals

for all

(2.5)

Suppose that, for every

2.3.
Boundedness of the sequence of solutions to the parameterized equations

In this section, we present a key lemma that allows us to recover a solution to equation (
1.4
) from a sequence of solutions to the parameterized equations (
1.7
), which shows that a subsequence of solutions
\{(u_{\rho_{n}},\lambda_{\rho_{n}})\}\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\times(0,+\infty)
to parameterized equations (
1.7
) satisfies that
\{u_{\rho_{n}}\}
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
, where
\rho_{n}\to 1^{-}
as
n\to\infty
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a noncompact metric graph with finitely many edges, let
p>4
, and let
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset[\frac{1}{2},1]
be a sequence such that
\rho_{n}\rightarrow 1^{-}
. Suppose that

\{(u_{\rho_{n}},\lambda_{\rho_{n}})\}\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\times(0,+\infty)

is a sequence of solutions to

(2.10)

\begin{cases}u_{\rho_{n}}^{\prime\prime}=\lambda_{\rho_{n}}u_{\rho_{n}}&\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E},\\
\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n}}\rvert^{2}\,dx=\mu&\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}(\mathrm{v})=-\rho_{n}|u_{\rho_{n}}(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u_{\rho_{n}}(\mathrm{v})&\text{at every }\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V},\end{cases}

where
u_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}^{\prime}(\mathrm{v})
stands for
u_{\rho_{n}}|_{\mathrm{e}}^{\prime}(0)
or
-u_{\rho_{n}}|_{\mathrm{e}}^{\prime}(\ell_{\mathrm{e}})
, according to whether the vertex
\mathrm{v}
is identified with
0
or
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}
. Assume additionally that the sequence of the energy levels
\{c_{\rho_{n}}:=E_{{\rho_{n}}}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})\}
is bounded. Then,
\{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}\}\subset(0,+\infty)
is bounded from above. Moreover, up to a subsequence,
\{u_{\rho_{n}}\}
satisfies

(i)

E(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})\to c
, where
\displaystyle c:=\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{\rho_{n}}
;

(ii)

E^{\prime}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})-\lambda_{\rho_{n}}(u_{\rho_{n}},\cdot)_{2}\to 0

in the dual of
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
;

(iii)

\{u_{\rho_{n}}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof.

Let
\{(u_{\rho_{n}},\lambda_{\rho_{n}})\}\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\times(0,+\infty)
be a sequence of solutions to (
2.10
), where
(\rho_{n})\subset[\frac{1}{2},1]
satisfying
\rho_{n}\to 1^{-}
as
n\to\infty
. We first show that
\{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}\}
cannot diverge to
+\infty
. Assume by contradiction that
\lambda_{\rho_{n}}\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
. By a straightforward computation, we have

\displaystyle 2c_{\rho_{n}}

\displaystyle=2E_{\rho_{n}}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})-E_{\rho_{n}}^{\prime}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})[u_{\rho_{n}}]-\lambda_{\rho_{n}}(u_{\rho_{n}},u_{\rho_{n}})_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{n}(p-2)}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n}}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}-\lambda_{\rho_{n}}\mu

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p-2}{2p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n}}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}-\lambda_{\rho_{n}}\mu,

and thus, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
large enough,

(2.11)

\lvert u_{\rho_{n}}(\mathrm{v})\rvert\leq\left(\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n}}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{1}\lambda_{\rho_{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}.

On one hand, for any unbounded edge
\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}
, it follows from
[
7
, Remark 4 in Section 3.3]
and
\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n}}\rvert^{2}\,dx=\mu
that

u_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}(x)=u_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}(0)e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}x},

where
u_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}:=u_{\rho_{n}}|_{\mathrm{e}}
. Thus, by (
2.11
) and
p>4
, we conclude

\int_{I_{\mathrm{e}}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}\rvert^{2}\,dx=\frac{\lvert u_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}(0)\rvert^{2}}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}}\leq\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{2}\lambda_{\rho_{n}}^{\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty.

On the other hand, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and any bounded edge
\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}
, it follows from
[
7
, Remark 4 in Section 3.3]
that, there exist two constants
a_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}},b_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

u_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}(x)=a_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}e^{\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}x}+b_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}x}.

Then,

a_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}=\frac{u_{\mathrm{e}}(\ell_{\mathrm{e}})e^{\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}\ell_{\mathrm{e}}}-u_{\mathrm{e}}(0)}{e^{2\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}\ell_{\mathrm{e}}}-1}\quad\text{and}\quad b_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}=\frac{u_{\mathrm{e}}(0)-u_{\mathrm{e}}(\ell_{\mathrm{e}})e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}\ell_{\mathrm{e}}}}{1-e^{-2\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}\ell_{\mathrm{e}}}},

and thus, by (
2.11
), we know that there exist a constant
C_{2}>0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
large enough,

\lvert a_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}\rvert\leq C_{2}\lambda_{\rho_{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}\ell_{\mathrm{e}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert b_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}\rvert\leq C_{2}\lambda_{\rho_{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}},

whence it follows from
p>4
that

\int_{I_{\mathrm{e}}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}\rvert^{2}\,dx=2a_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}b_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}\ell_{\mathrm{e}}+\frac{a_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}^{2}(e^{2\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}\ell_{\mathrm{e}}}-1)}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}-\frac{b_{\rho_{n},\mathrm{e}}^{2}(e^{-2\sqrt{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}}\ell_{\mathrm{e}}}-1)}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\to 0,\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Now, since
\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}
is arbitrary and
\mathrm{E}
is a finite set, we have
\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n}}\rvert^{2}\,dx\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, contradicting the fact that
\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u_{\rho_{n}}\rvert^{2}\,dx=\mu
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Therefore, the sequence
\{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}\}
is bounded from above.

Next, we compute that

2pc_{\rho_{n}}=2pE_{\rho_{n}}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})-E_{\rho_{n}}^{\prime}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})[u_{\rho_{n}}]-\lambda_{\rho_{n}}(u_{\rho_{n}},u_{\rho_{n}})_{2}=(p-1)\lVert u_{\rho_{n}}^{\prime}\rVert_{2}^{2}-\lambda_{\rho_{n}}\mu,

which shows that
\{u_{\rho_{n}}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Thus,

E(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})-E_{\rho_{n}}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad E^{\prime}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})-E^{\prime}_{\rho_{n}}(u_{\rho_{n}},\mathcal{G})\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Therefore, up to a subsequence,
\{u_{\rho_{n}}\}
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on
H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, satisfying
(i)
-
(iii)
.
∎

3.
Compactness of bounded Palais-Smale sequences

In this section, we assume that
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
. Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
be a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
E_{\rho}(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
constrained on
H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
at some level

, that is,
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in

and satisfies

(3.1)

and

(3.2)

where

is the sequence of Lagrange multipliers.

We now prove the compactness of the bonunded Palais-Smale sequences
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
u_{\rho}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{\rho}\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\mathcal{G})

and

u_{n}(x)\to u_{\rho}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{G}.

Moreover,
u_{\rho}
is a solution of equation

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-u_{\rho}^{\prime\prime}=\lambda_{\rho}u&\quad\text{on every edge }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E},\\
&\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u_{\rho,\mathrm{e}}^{\prime}(\mathrm{v})=-\rho|u_{\rho}(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u_{\rho}(\mathrm{v})&\quad\text{at every vertex }\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\displaystyle\lambda_{\rho}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}
and
u_{\rho,\mathrm{e}}^{\prime}(\mathrm{v})
stands for
u_{\rho}|_{\mathrm{e}}^{\prime}(0)
or
-u_{\rho}|_{\mathrm{e}}^{\prime}(\ell_{\mathrm{e}})
, according to whether the vertex
\mathrm{v}
is identified with
0
or
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}
.

Proof.

Since
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
is bounded, by
[
17
, Theorem 8.8]
, we may assume that, up to a subsequence, there exists
u_{\rho}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
such that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{\rho}\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\mathcal{G})

and

u_{n}(x)\to u_{\rho}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{G}.

Observe also that, from (
3.2
) and the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, we obtain

o(1)=E_{\rho}^{\prime}(u_{n},\mathcal{G})[u_{n}]+\lambda_{n}(u_{n},u_{n})_{2}=E_{\rho}^{\prime}(u_{n},\mathcal{G})[u_{n}]+\lambda_{n}\mu,

which implies that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded. Thus, u to a subsequence, there exists
\lambda_{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda_{\rho},\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,n\to+\infty.

Next, we show that
u_{\rho}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
solves (
1.7
). Indeed, using (
3.2
) and
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{\rho}
, we have, for every
\eta\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
,

(3.4)

\begin{split}0&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(E^{\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n},\mathcal{G})+\lambda_{n}(u_{n},\cdot)_{2}\right)[\eta]\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}\,dx+\lambda_{n}\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}\eta\,dx-\rho\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|u_{n}(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u_{n}(\mathrm{v})\eta(\mathrm{v})\right)\\

This completes the proof of lemma.
∎

Next, we focus on proving that
u_{n}\to u_{\rho}
in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, as

, to ensure that the limit
u_{\rho}
belong to the mass constraint
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Aassume that
\lambda_{\rho}\geq 0
in Lemma

. Then,

(3.5)

\int_{\mathcal{G}}|(u_{n}-u_{\rho})^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u_{n}-u_{\rho}|^{2}\,dx\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

In particular, if
\lambda_{\rho}>0
, the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
converges strongly to
u_{\rho}
in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof.

Using (
3.2
) and the fact that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{\rho}
, for every
\eta\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, we have that

\displaystyle o(1)\|\eta\|

\displaystyle=\left(E^{\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n},\mathcal{G})+\lambda_{n}(u_{n},\cdot)_{2}\right)[\eta]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}\,dx-\rho\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|u_{n}(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u_{n}(\mathrm{v})\eta(\mathrm{v})+\lambda_{n}\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}\eta\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}\,dx-\rho\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|u_{\rho}(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u_{\rho}(\mathrm{v})\eta(\mathrm{v})+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}\eta\,dx+(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{\rho})\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}\eta\,dx,

that is,

(3.6)

\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}\,dx-\rho\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|u_{\rho}(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u_{\rho}(\mathrm{v})\eta(\mathrm{v})+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{G}}u_{n}\eta\,dx=o(1)\|\eta\|.

Now, taking the difference between (

) and (
3.4
), choosing

, we obtain

\displaystyle o(1)=o(1)\|u_{n}-u_{\rho}\|

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u_{n}^{\prime}-u_{\rho}^{\prime})\eta_{n}^{\prime}\,dx-\rho\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\big(|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}-|u_{\rho}|^{p-2}u_{\rho}\big)\eta_{n}+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u_{n}-u_{\rho})\eta_{n}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u_{n}^{\prime}-u_{\rho}^{\prime})\eta_{n}^{\prime}\,dx+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{G}}(u_{n}-u_{\rho})\eta_{n}\,dx+o(1)\|\eta_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}}\big|(u_{n}-u_{\rho})^{\prime}\big|^{2}\,dx+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u_{n}-u_{\rho}|^{2}\,dx+o(1),

which yields (
3.5
).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\lambda<0
and

, there exists a subspace

with

.

Let
p>2
and
\mu>0
. For any fixed integer
N\geq 2
and any given values of
\overline{B}>0
,
\overline{b}>0
, there exist the functions
\overline{\varphi}_{1},\ldots,\overline{\varphi}_{N}
, compactly supported in any bounded edge of
\mathcal{G}
, such that for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and all
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, we have

(i)

\overline{\varphi}_{i}\in S_{\mu}
,
\|\overline{\varphi}^{\prime}_{i}\|_{2}\geq\overline{B}
;

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}(\overline{\varphi}_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(\overline{\varphi}_{j})=\varnothing
whenever
i\neq j
;

(iii)

if
a=(a_{1},...,a_{N-1})\in\mathbb{S}^{N-2}
, then for any
N\geq 2
,

\bigl\|\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\overline{\varphi}_{i}\bigr)^{\prime}\bigr\|_{2}\geq\overline{B}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,E_{\rho}\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\overline{\varphi}_{i},\,\mathcal{G}\bigr)\leq\overline{b}.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}
be a vertex, that we identify in the following with
0
, and denote by
e_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,K
, the edges incident at
\mathrm{v}
. Set

\ell:=\begin{cases}\min_{1\leq j\leq K}|e_{K}|,&\text{if }e_{j}\text{ is bounded for some }j=1,...,K,\\
1,\quad&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Define
u_{M}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
by

u_{M}(x)=\begin{cases}M(\frac{\ell}{K}-x),&\text{on }e_{j}\cap[0,\frac{\ell}{K}],\quad\forall\,j=1,\ldots,K,\\
0,&\text{elsewhere.}\end{cases}

Choose
M>0
such that
\|u_{M}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\mu
, and set
\varphi:=u_{M}
. From (
3.7
), we notice that
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi^{t})\subset e_{j}\cap(0,\frac{\ell_{c}}{K})
,
j=1,\ldots,K
whenever
t\geq 1
. Define the functions

\overline{\varphi}_{i}:=\varphi^{t}\left(x-\frac{(i-1)\ell}{N}\right),\qquad i=1,\ldots,N,

where
t\geq 1
will be chosen later. Note that

\operatorname{supp}(\overline{\varphi}_{i})\subset e_{j}\cap\left[\frac{(i-1)\ell_{c}}{N},\frac{i\ell_{c}}{N}\right),\quad j=1,\ldots,K.

Thus, the functions
\overline{\varphi}_{i}
have disjoint supports and
(ii)
is satisfied.

Viewing now
\overline{\varphi}_{i}
as functions in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
supported in
e_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,K
, we may compute the energy of
\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\overline{\varphi}_{i}
with
a\in\mathbb{S}^{N-2}
as follows

\displaystyle E_{\rho}\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\overline{\varphi}_{i},\,\mathcal{G}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bigcup_{j=1}^{K}e_{j}}\biggl|\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\overline{\varphi}^{\prime}_{i}\biggr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\rho}{p}\sum_{v\in\mathrm{V}}\biggl|\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\overline{\varphi}_{i}\biggr|^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{Kt^{2}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}^{2}\int_{0}^{\ell/N}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\rho}{p}t^{p/2}|a_{1}|^{p}\left(\frac{M\ell}{K}\right)^{p}\to-\infty\quad\text{as }t\to+\infty.

Thus, for all
\overline{b}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
T_{0}>0
such that for all
t>T_{0}
, we have
E_{\rho}\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\overline{\varphi}_{i},\,\mathcal{G}\bigr)<\overline{b}
. As a result, we can choose a suitable
t>0
such that, the functions
\overline{\varphi}_{i}
satisfy all of the desired properties. Indeed,
\overline{\varphi}_{i}\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
and from (
3.7
), we have

\|\overline{\varphi}^{\prime}_{i}\|_{2}=t\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\geq\overline{B},

which implies
(i)
.

Finally, the choice of
t>0
and
\bigl\|\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\overline{\varphi}_{i}\bigr)^{\prime}\bigr\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}^{2}\|\overline{\varphi}^{\prime}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}
show
(iii)
.

The proof is complete.
∎

Now, let
\{V_{N}\}
be a sequence of linear subspaces of
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
with
\dim V_{N}=N
which is exhausting
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
in the sense that

\bigcup_{N\geq 1}V_{N}

is dense in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. We recall that such a sequence always exists, since

is a separable Hilbert spaces.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
p>2
,

S_{N}:=\inf_{u\in V_{N-2}^{\perp}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}\,dx}{\left(\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|u(\mathrm{v})|^{p}\right)^{2/p}}\to\infty\quad\text{as $N\to\infty$}.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exists a sequence
\{u_{N}\}\subset V_{N-2}^{\perp}
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|u_{N}(\mathrm{v})|^{p}=1
and
\|u_{N}\|
is bounded. Then, up to a subsequence, there exists
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
such that

u_{N}\rightharpoonup u,\,\,\text{in}\,\,H^{1}(\mathcal{G})

(and hence in
H^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{c})
), and therefore
u_{N}\to u
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{X}_{c})
as
N\to\infty
.

Let
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Since
\{V_{N}\}
exhausts
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, there exists a sequence
\{v_{N}\}\subset H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
with
v_{N}\in V_{N-2}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and
v_{N}\to v
in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. Taking the scalar product in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, we have

\left|\langle u_{N},v\rangle\right|\leq\left|\langle u_{N},v-v_{N}\rangle\right|+\left|\langle u_{N},v_{N}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle u_{N},v-v_{N}\rangle\right|\leq\|u_{N}\|\|v-v_{N}\|\to 0,\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Hence
u_{N}\rightharpoonup 0=u
, which is in contradiction with
\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|u_{N}(\mathrm{v})|^{p}=1
.
∎

We now define

(5.1)

\displaystyle\beta_{N}:=\left(\frac{S_{N}^{p/2}}{L}\right)^{1/(p-2)},\quad\text{where}\quad L=L(p):=\frac{3}{p}\max_{x>0}\frac{(\mu+x^{2})^{p/2}}{\mu+x^{p}}.

As an immediate consequence of Lemma
5.5
, we have
\beta_{N}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
. Define

(5.2)

\displaystyle b_{\rho}^{N}:=\inf_{u\in B_{N}}E_{\rho}(u,\mathcal{G})\quad\text{where}\quad B_{N}:=\left\{u\in V_{N-2}^{\perp}\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})\mid\|u^{\prime}\|_{2}=\beta_{N}.\right\}

We then have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

b_{\rho}^{N}\to+\infty
as
N\to+\infty
, uniformly in
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
.

Proof.

For any
u\in B_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\rho}(u,\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx-\frac{\rho}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|u|^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\mu+\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx}{S_{N}}\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{L}{3S_{N}^{p/2}}\left(\mu+\|u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\beta_{N}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\beta_{N}^{2-p}(\mu+\beta_{N}^{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\beta_{N}^{2}+o(1).

The proof is completed by taking the infimum
of
E_{\rho}(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
over
B_{N}
.
∎

Recall that the compact core of
\mathcal{G}
is defined as the metric subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
consisting of all the bounded edges of
\mathcal{G}
, and we denote it by
\mathcal{K}
. In the following, we assume that
\mathcal{G}
has a non-empty compact core
\mathcal{K}
.

We first recall some basic definitions of analysis on (unordered) combinatorial graphs, see
[
27
]
for more details. In what follows, we will also regard
\mathcal{K}
as a combinatorial finite graph. Since
\mathcal{G}
is connected and
\mathcal{K}
is non-empty, it follows that
\mathcal{K}=(\mathrm{V}_{\mathcal{K}},\mathrm{E}_{\mathcal{K}})
is connected, where
\mathrm{V}_{\mathcal{K}}=\mathrm{V}
and
\mathrm{E}_{\mathcal{K}}
denotes the set of bounded edges of
\mathcal{G}
. For vertices
v,w\in\mathrm{V}
, we write
v\sim w
(i.e.
v
is a neighbour of
w
) if
vw\in\mathrm{E}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Let
C(\mathrm{V})
denote the set of real-valued functions on
\mathrm{V}
. For any function
f\in C(\mathrm{V})
and any
\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}
, the graph Laplacian of
f
is defined by

\Delta_{d}f(\mathrm{v}):=\sum_{\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{V},\mathrm{w}\sim\mathrm{v}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}}(f(\mathrm{w})-f(\mathrm{v})),

where each bounded edge
\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{E}
is identified with a closed and bounded interval
I_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}=[0,\ell_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}]
with
\ell_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}>0
.

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathrm{V})}
the usual
\ell^{p}(\mathrm{V})
-norm on
C(\mathrm{V})
, that is,

\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathrm{V})}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|f(\mathrm{v})|^{p}\Big)^{1/p},&1\leq p<\infty,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}|f(\mathrm{v})|,&p=\infty.\end{array}\right.

We define a semi-norm on
C(\mathrm{V})
by

[f]_{H^{1}(\mathrm{V})}:=\left(\sum_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{E}_{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}}(f(\mathrm{w})-f(\mathrm{v}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

For any
\mathrm{v}_{0}\in\mathrm{V}
, denote

C_{\mathrm{v}_{0}}(\mathrm{V}):=\{f\in C(\mathrm{V}):f(\mathrm{v}_{0})=0\}.

Then, by the connectivity of
\mathcal{K}
, it is clear that
[\cdot]_{H^{1}(\mathrm{V})}
is a norm on
C(\mathrm{V})
.

We now study the relation between the following two equations: the first one posed on the metric graph
\mathcal{G}

(6.1)

\begin{cases}u^{\prime\prime}=0&\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(v)=-\rho|u(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u(\mathrm{v})&\text{at every }\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V},\end{cases}

and the second one on the combinatorial graph
\mathcal{K}

(6.2)

-\Delta_{d}f=\rho\lvert f\rvert^{p-2}f\quad\text{in }\mathrm{V}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
and let
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
be a solution of equation (
6.1
). Then
\hat{u}:=u|_{\mathrm{V}}
is a solution of (
6.2
) and satisfies

\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{2}=[\hat{u}]_{H^{1}(\mathrm{V})}^{2}.

Moreover,
there exists a constant
S_{p,\mathcal{G}}>0
, depending only on
p
and
\mathcal{G}
, such that

\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{2}-\frac{\rho}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}=\frac{1}{2}[\hat{u}]_{H^{1}(\mathrm{V})}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}\leq S_{p,\mathcal{G}}.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, and let
u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
be a solution of equation (
6.1
). Then, for any
\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}
, we have
u_{\mathrm{e}}^{\prime\prime}=0
. It follows from
[
7
, Remark 4 in Section 3.3]
that there exist
\{a_{\mathrm{e}}\}_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}},\{b_{\mathrm{e}}\}_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

u_{\mathrm{e}}(x)=a_{\mathrm{e}}x+b_{\mathrm{e}}\quad\text{on every edge }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}.

For any half-line
\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}\backslash\mathrm{E}_{\mathcal{K}}
, since

, we have that

.
Thus, for any
\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}

\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathrm{v})=\sum_{\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{V},\mathrm{w}\sim\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}(\mathrm{v})=\sum_{\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{V},\mathrm{w}\sim\mathrm{v}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}}(u(\mathrm{w})-u(\mathrm{v}))=\Delta_{d}\hat{u}(\mathrm{v}),

which implies that
\hat{u}:=u|_{\mathrm{V}}
is a solution of (

\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{2}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}\,dx=\sum_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{E}_{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}}(f(\mathrm{w})-f(\mathrm{v}))^{2}=[\hat{u}]_{H^{1}(\mathrm{V})}^{2},

which implies

\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{2}-\frac{\rho}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert u(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}=\frac{1}{2}[\hat{u}]_{H^{1}(\mathrm{V})}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}.

Let
\mathrm{e}_{0}\in\mathrm{E}\backslash\mathrm{E}_{\mathcal{K}}

. Since

and

are equivalent norms on

.
Thus, there exists a constant
C
, depending on
p
and
\mathcal{G}
such that,

[f]_{H^{1}(\mathrm{V})}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathrm{V})},\quad\forall f\in C_{\mathrm{v}_{0}}(\mathrm{V}),

which implies that

\frac{1}{2}[\hat{u}]_{H^{1}(\mathrm{V})}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{p}\sum_{\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}}\lvert\hat{u}(\mathrm{v})\rvert^{p}\leq\frac{C^{2}}{2}\lVert\hat{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathrm{V})}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{p}\lVert\hat{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathrm{V})}^{p}\leq\frac{C^{2}}{2}\lVert\hat{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathrm{V})}^{2}-\frac{1}{2p}\lVert\hat{u}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathrm{V})}^{p}\leq\mathcal{S}_{p,\mathcal{G}}

for some constant
\mathcal{S}_{p,\mathcal{G}}>0
.
∎

On the other hand, following the same argument as
in
Lemma
6.1
, one can prove that if
f\in C(\mathrm{V})
is a solution of (
6.2
), then the function
\tilde{f}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
is defined by

\tilde{f}_{\mathrm{e}}(x):=\begin{cases}\frac{u(\mathrm{w})-u(\mathrm{v})}{\ell_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}}x+u(\mathrm{v})&\text{if }\mathrm{e}=\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{E}\text{ is a bounbded edge},\\
0&\text{if }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}\text{ is a unbounbded edge},\\
\end{cases}

is a solution of (
6.1
), where, for any bounded edge
\mathrm{e}=\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{E}
,
\mathrm{v}
is identified with
0
.

Now, for
N\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{Z}^{N}:=(\mathrm{V}_{N},\mathrm{E}_{N})
be a metric graph, where

\mathrm{V}_{N}=\{x=(x_{1},...,x_{N})\mid x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq N\},

\mathrm{E}_{N}=\left\{(x,y)\mid x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},\mathop{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\lvert x_{i}-y_{i}\rvert=1}\right\},

and, for any
\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}_{N}
,
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}=1
. Similarly, if
f\in C(\mathrm{V}_{N})
is a solution of

-\Delta_{d}f=\lvert f\rvert^{p-2}f\quad\text{in }\mathrm{V}_{N},

then
\tilde{f}
is a solution of

(6.3)

\begin{cases}u^{\prime\prime}=0&\text{on every }\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}_{N},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{e}\succ\mathrm{v}}u^{\prime}_{\mathrm{e}}(v)=-|u(\mathrm{v})|^{p-2}u(\mathrm{v})&\text{at every }\mathrm{v}\in\mathrm{V}_{N},\end{cases}

where
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{Z}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\tilde{f}_{\mathrm{e}}(x):=\frac{u(\mathrm{w})-u(\mathrm{v})}{\ell_{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}}}x+u(\mathrm{v})\quad\text{for any }\mathrm{e}=\mathrm{v}\mathrm{w}\in\mathrm{E}\text{, where $v$ identified with $0$}.

Thus, by
[
28
, Corollary 4]
, for any
N\geq 3
and any
p>\frac{2N}{N-2}
, problem (
6.3
) admits a positive solution.

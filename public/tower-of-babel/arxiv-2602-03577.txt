STABILITY OF THE WEAK HAAGERUP PROPERTY UNDER GRAPH PRODUCTS
Shubhabrata Das and Partha Sarathi Ghosh

Abstract
. In this paper we prove that: Any graph product of finitely many groups, all of them satisfying weak Haagerup property with Λ W ​ H = 1 \Lambda_{WH}=1 , also satisfies weak Haagerup property and as a corollary of this result we obtain that the free product of weakly Haagerup groups with Λ W ​ H = 1 \Lambda_{WH}=1 , again has weak Haagerup property with Λ W ​ H = 1 \Lambda_{WH}=1 .



1.
Introduction

A group is called
amenable
if it admits a sequence of functions, converging point-wise to the constant function one (called
approximate identities
), which are finitely supported, and positive definite. Amenable groups form a fairly large class which is stable under taking direct products, quotients and subgroups. Finite rank free groups are not amenable.

In
[
19
]
, Haagerup showed existence of an approximate identity
\{\phi_{n}\}
on the free group
\mathbb{F}_{n}
, such that they are positive definite, vanishing at infinity (with respect to word metric). Akemann-Walter
[
1
]
and Choda
[
9
]
called a group to have the
Haagerup property
if it admits a similar approximate identity as in
[
19
]
. Clearly amenable groups have Haagerup property. Fundamental group of hyperbolic surfaces, CAT(0)-cubulated groups, groups acting properly on a tree and many others have this property. Cherix et. al
[
8
]
provides a good survey on this topic.

One other way amenability can be generalized is called
weak amenability
, where
G
is required to have approximate identities consisting of finitely supported functions which are, uniformly bounded in the ‘completely bounded’-norm (
B_{2}
-norm, see definition
2.3
). Since a positive definite map
\phi
on
G
has bounded cb-norm, this notion generalizes amenability. There is a constant, canonically associated to a weakly amenable group
G
, obtained as the infimum of the cb-norms of these approximate identities, called the Cowling-Haagerup constant of
G
and is denoted by
\Lambda_{CH}(G)
. Clearly, every amenable group has
\Lambda_{CH}=1
. Most groups mentioned above were shown to be weakly amenable with
\Lambda_{CH}=1
(
[
19
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
17
]
). For an example of a group with
\Lambda_{CH}>1
, we mention a uniform lattice
\Gamma
in
Sp(1,n)
, where the
\Lambda_{CH}(\Gamma)=2n-1
. This group satisfies the Kazhdan’s property (T), which can be seen as a strong negation of the Haagerup property (see
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
).

Clearly, groups like
Sp(n,1)
(or, lattices in them) do not satisfy the Haagerup property. It is also observed that
most
weakly amenable groups with
\Lambda_{CH}=1
happens to be groups with the Haagerup property and the famous conjecture by Cowling in this regard stated:
G
is weakly amenable with
\Lambda_{CH}=1
if and only if
G
has Haagerup property (section 1.3.1,
[
8
]
). Ozawa and Popa constructed examples of a non-weakly amenable groups in
[
25
]
, which was later shown to be Haagerup by Cornulier et. al
[
10
]
. The other direction of the conjecture remains open.

A further generalisation called the
weak Haagerup Property
interpolates between the Haagerup property and weak amenability. Introduced by Knudby
[
20
]
(see definition
2.5
), a group is called weakly Haagerup if there is an approximate identity of vanishing at infinity maps, uniformly bounded in
B_{2}
-norm, on the group. A group
G
with the weak Haagerup property also comes equipped with a constant
\Lambda_{WH}(G)
, defined similar to
\Lambda_{CH}
and it is evident that
\Lambda_{WH}(G)\leq\Lambda_{CH}(G)
, for any
G
. An example of a group not having weak Haagerup property is given in
[
18
]
.

Both amenability and weak amenability are preserved under direct products. In
[
5
]
,
Bozejko and Picardello proved that free product of amenable groups is weakly amenable. Whereas it is still an open question whether free product of any two weakly amenable groups is weakly amenable or not. Though as a special case Ricard and Xu had proved that free product of two weakly amenable group with
\Lambda_{CH}=1
also has weak amenability
[
28
]
. In general, Haagerup property of groups also proved to be stable under taking direct products, free products and amalgamated free products (over finite subgroups)
[
8
]
. Knudby in
[
21
]
showed that direct product of two weak Haagerup groups is again weakly Haagerup. In this paper we wish to study stability of the weak Haagerup property under certain group construction (
graph products
).

The
graph products of groups

G(\Gamma)
, defined by Green in her thesis
[
15
]
, is a novel way to combine a collection
\{G_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)}
of groups parameterised along the vertex set
V(\Gamma)
of a finite graph
\Gamma
. Depending on the nature of the given (finite) graph, a graph product of groups can also be thought of as an interpolation between the free product (which corresponds to a graph without an edge) and the direct product (which corresponds to the complete graph on a given set of vertices) of groups (for details see section
2.4
).

For a finite simplicial graph
\Gamma
, and a collection
\{G_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)}
of weakly amenable groups with
\Lambda_{CH}(G_{v})=1
for each vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
, it was shown by Reckwerdt in
[
27
]
, that the graph product
G(\Gamma)
is weakly amenable. Stability of Haagerup property under graph product was proved by the present authors in
[
13
]
, (see also
[
2
]
). The main result of this paper establishes stability of the weak Haagerup property under graph product. Note that a group with Haagerup property has
\Lambda_{WH}=1
, and a weakly amenable group
G
with
\Lambda_{CH}(G)=1
is also weakly Haagerup with
\Lambda_{WH}(G)=1
. Therefore, following Knudby’s program in
[
20
]
, proving a weakly Haagerup group with
\Lambda_{WH}=1
to be Haagerup settles the remaining part of the Cowling’s conjecture. The main result of this paper can be seen as a supportive evidence for the conjecture.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a finite simplicial graph and
\{G_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)}
is a collection of weakly Haagerup groups with
\Lambda_{WH}(G_{v})=1
, for each
v
. Then the group
G(\Gamma)
has the weak Haagerup property with
\Lambda_{WH}(G(\Gamma))=1
.

The graph products are in some sense generalization of free products of groups, so as a consequence of the above theorem we have the following as well.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose
A
and
B
are two weakly Haagerup groups with
\Lambda_{WH}(G_{v})=1
. Then the group
G=A\ast B
has the weak Haagerup property.

Following Knudby
[
20
]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for a group
G
to have weak Haagerup property with
\Lambda_{WH}=1
, is the existence of a proper, symmetric function
\phi
on
G
, which can be expressed as sum of two kernels on the group, i.e. for any
x,y\in G
,

(1)

\phi(y^{-1}x)=\rho(x,y)+\tau(x,y)

where
\rho
is proper, conditionally negative definite and
\tau
is bounded positive definite. So, one way to prove theorem
1.1
is to come up with a proper function
\phi
as above, on
G(\Gamma)
. Since on each
G_{v}
, we have a proper function
\phi_{v}
which is a sum of
\rho_{v}
and
\tau_{v}
as above, it would be reasonable to ask if we can combine the individual kernels
\rho_{v}
’s into a
\rho
and
\tau_{v}
’s to a
\tau
, in order to obtain a ‘
\phi=\rho+\tau
’ on
G(\Gamma)
. Upto a modification the sum of the conditionally negative definite kernels on
G_{v}
’s define a kernel of the same type on
G(\Gamma)
. A similar approach was followed in
[
13
]
to combine conditionally negative definite kernels
\rho_{v}
’s on Haagerup vertex groups
G_{v}
to obtain a conditionally negative definite kernel on
G(\Gamma)
, proving it to have the Haagerup property. But here, we were unable to integrate the bounded positive definite kernels
\tau_{v}
’s to get a similar kernel
\tau
on
G(\Gamma)
, and hence unable to obtain the desired
\phi=\rho+\tau
. We prove theorem
1.1
following a general line of arguments found in
[
5
,
28
,
27
]
. Somewhat similar strategies were followed also in proposition 12.3.5 of
[
6
]
and the proof of the main result of
[
26
]
.

2.
Preliminary

Throughout the paper, let
G
be a finitely generated group. A function
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called
positive definite
(or of positive type or PD) if for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
it satisfies the following: for any choice of
n
complex numbers
c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{n}
; and
n
elements
g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{n}
from the group
G
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}\phi(g_{j}^{-1}g_{i})\geq 0

Any function
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
induces a kernel
k_{\phi}:G\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, defined by
k_{\phi}(g,h):=\phi(h^{-1}g)
. So in other words,
\phi
is positive definite if and only if for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any subset
\{g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}\}\subset G
, the matrix
[k_{ij}:=k_{\phi}(g_{i},g_{j})]\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is positive definite.

Similarly a symmetric function
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
(i.e.
\phi(x^{-1})=\phi(x),\forall x\in G
) is called
conditionally negative definite
(CND) if for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following is satisfied: given any subset
\{g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{n}\}\subset G
and any set
\{c_{1},\cdots,c_{n}\}
of
n
complex numbers, with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=0
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}k_{\phi}(g_{i},g_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}\phi(g_{j}^{-1}g_{i})\leq 0

Remark 2.1
.

A kernel
k:G\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of PD type (resp. CND type), induces a function
\phi_{k}
of the PD type (resp. CND type) on the group if the kernel is
G
-invariant, i.e. for all
x,y,z\in G
,

k(xy,xz)=k(y,z).

Alternately a
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
can be characterized (
[
14
]
) as PD if there exists a unitary representation
\pi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and a unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
such that

(2)

\phi(g)=\langle\pi(g)\xi,\xi\rangle,

another way of saying it is that the existence of a map
\alpha:G\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that for any
g,h\in G

(3)

\phi(h^{-1}g)=\langle\alpha(g),\alpha(h)\rangle,

and CND if there exists a map
R:G\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
into a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
such that

(4)

k_{\phi}(g,h)=\phi(h^{-1}g)=||R(g)-R(h)||^{2}.

The following relationship between CND maps and PD maps is due to Schoenberg.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
29
]
(
[
6
]
,Theorem D.11)
A function
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is conditionally negative definite if and only if
e^{-t\phi}
is positive definite for every
t>0
.

2.1.
Completely Bounded Maps and
B_{2}
-norm

We start with the definition of the completely bounded norm or the
B_{2}
-norm of a function
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, which will be used to define the weak Haagerup property, in what follows.

Consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(G)
, canonically associated to
G
, be the space of all square summable functions on
G
.
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G))
is the space of all bounded operators on
\ell^{2}(G)
. Any element
A\in\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G))
can be considered in the matrix form
[A_{g,h}]_{g,h\in G}
, where
A_{g,h}=\langle A\delta_{h},\delta_{g}\rangle
. A kernel
k:G\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, induces a multiplier
m_{k}:\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G))\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G))
defined by

[A_{g,h}]_{g,h\in G}\mapsto[k(g,h)A_{g,h}]_{g,h\in G},

called a
Schur multiplier
. Given a function
\phi:G\to\mathbb{C}
, the Schur multiplier
m_{k_{\phi}}
for the induced kernel
k_{\phi}
, is called the
Herz-Schur multiplier
if it is a bounded operator. Further for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can define
M_{n,\phi}:\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G)))\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G)))
by considering the map

\quad[a_{ij}]\mapsto[m_{k_{\phi}}(a_{ij})].

Here
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G)))
gets its norm by identifying it with
\mathbb{B}((\ell^{2}(G))^{n})
.

Definition 2.3
.

A function
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called completely bounded if there is a
B>0
such that the operator-norm of
M_{n,\phi}
, defined above is bounded by
B
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The
B_{2}
-norm (alternately the completely bounded norm or the cb-norm) of
\phi
is

||\phi||_{B_{2}}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}||M_{n,\phi}||

Computing the
B_{2}
-norm of a function from the above definition is in general a difficult task. But we have the following equivalent characterisation of a completely bounded function, which is more useful.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
26
]
, Theorem 3)
.

Let
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a function. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

||\phi||_{B_{2}}\leq B
,

(2)

the operator norm
||m_{k_{\phi}}||\leq B
,

(3)

there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathcal{H})
, such that

k_{\phi}(g,h)=\langle\alpha(g),\beta(h)\rangle

with
\max\{||\alpha||_{\infty},||\beta||_{\infty}\}\leq\sqrt{B}

Suppose
\phi(g):=1
for all
g\in G
, then it is easy to see
||\phi||_{B_{2}}=1
. Further if
\phi(g):=d(e,g)
and
G
is infinite, then it is not completely bounded as every completely bounded map should be bounded. Moreover any PD function is completely bounded and
||\phi||_{B_{2}}=|\phi(e)|
. This shows why amenability implies weak amenability.

2.2.
Weak Haagerup Property

The notion of weak Haagerup property of a group was first conceived by Knudby, possibly in an attempt to answer the Cowling’s conjecture
[
20
]
. As mentioned earlier this notion generalizes both the Haagerup property and the weak amenability, giving a common platform to compare both the notions.

Definition 2.5
.

G
is weakly Haagerup if there is a sequence of maps
\phi_{n}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with

(1)

\phi_{n}
vanishes at infinity, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

\{\phi_{n}\}
is an approximate identity i.e.
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\phi_{n}(g)=1
for all
g\in G
,

(3)

there exists a constant
B>0
such that
||\phi_{n}||_{B_{2}}\leq B
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

The weak Haagerup constant, denoted
\Lambda_{WH}
for
G
is the infimum of all such
B>0
for which there is a sequence
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n}
satisfying the above properties.

In
[
20
]
, Knudby provided an equivalent criterion for a weakly Haagerup group with
\Lambda_{WH}(G)=1
. From proposition 3.1 in
[
20
]
, a group
G
is weakly Haagerup with
\Lambda_{WH}(G)=1
if and only if there exists a suitable proper map
\phi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
generating approximate identities
\{e^{-\frac{\phi}{n}}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, vanishing at infinity on
G
with
||e^{-\frac{\phi}{n}}||_{B_{2}}\leq 1
. Moreover proposition 4.3 of
[
20
]
shows that the above semi-group generator
\phi
is given by two functions
R,S:G\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
into some real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
satisfying:

(5)

\phi(y^{-1}x)=||R(x)-R(y)||^{2}+||S(x)+S(y)||^{2},\quad\text{for all}~x,y\in G.

Moreover without loss of generality we may assume that
R(1_{G})=0_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathcal{H}
. Setting
x=y
in equation
5
notice that the image of
G
under
S
lies on the sphere of radius
\sqrt{\frac{\phi(1_{G})}{4}}
inside
\mathcal{H}
, i.e. for any
x\in G
:

||S(x)||^{2}=\frac{\phi(1_{G})}{4}

Remark 2.6
.

By expanding the second term of the right side of equation
5
, we get the above mentioned expression
\phi=\rho+\tau
: for any
x,y\in G

\phi(y^{-1}x)=\underbrace{||R(x)-R(y)||^{2}+\frac{\phi(1_{G})}{2}}_{\mbox{$\rho(x,y)$}}~~+~~\underbrace{2\langle S(x),S(y)\rangle}_{\mbox{$\tau(x,y)$}}

Lemma
2.4
(3), guarantees the existence of a Hilbert space

, so that the above kernel can be represented as inner-products of two
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
-valued maps from

. In the next section we briefly describe how to do that.

Remark 2.7
.

We call the tuple
(G,\phi,R,S,\mathcal{H})
a weak Haagerup data for a weakly Haagerup group
G
, with
\Lambda_{WH}(G)=1
.

Suppose

is free product of groups, then any

Similarly, if

By a

2.5.
Construction of Cube Complex from Graph Products

In order to prove the weak Haagerup property, our goal is to construct an approximate identity
\{\phi_{n}\}
such that,

•

each
\phi_{n}
vanishes at infinity,

•

and the
B_{2}
-norm of each
\phi_{n}
is uniformly bounded.

In section
3
, we produce such functions by gluing similar functions
\phi_{n,v}
’s coming from each of the vertex group
G_{v}
’s along a certain ‘base space’
X
. In both the cases
[
5
]
and
[
27
]
, in order to guarantee the uniform boundedness of the approximate identities in the
B_{2}
-norm, the corresponding prescriptions required scaling by a suitable PD factor. We too will need a similar positive definite scaling in order to keep
||\phi_{n}||_{B_{2}}
’s uniformly bounded.

Similar to
[
27
]
, the base space in our case is a CAT(0)-cube complex canonically associated to the graph product
G(\Gamma)
, and the distance function on a CAT(0)-cube complex was proven to be CND by Niblo and Reeves
[
24
]
. We obtain a PD scaling factor using this CND distance function following lemma
2.2
. In this subsection we will briefly discuss the CAT(0)-cube complex structure associated to a graph product
G(\Gamma)
.

A
cube complex
is a combinatorial object, obtained by gluing euclidean cubes of different dimensions, by isometries along faces of lower dimensions. The euclidean cubes induce a metric on the cube complex. A metric space
X
is called ‘CAT(0)’, if it satisfies the CAT(0)-comparison property i.e. every geodesic triangle in
X
is as
thin
as its euclidean comparison triangle. By a
CAT(0)-cube complex
we mean a cube complex which satisfies the CAT(0) property. For details and other equivalent definitions see
[
16
]
,
[
4
]
.

One can also endow a space
X
with a CAT(0)-cube complex structure if it admits a certain “
wall space
” structure, given by the (combinatorial) intersection data of the hyperplanes partitioning
X
into two halves, called
walls
. Informally speaking, a wall space is a space with a family of intersecting walls.

Definition 2.11
.

(Wall Space)
A wall space structure on
X
is given by a pair
(X,\mathcal{W})
, where the collection
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)~\text{(the power set of $X$)}
is considered as the space of half-spaces, such that

(1)

\mathcal{W}
is closed under taking complement i.e.
h\in\mathcal{W}\iff h^{c}\in\mathcal{W}

(2)

a wall, denoted simply by
h
, is defined to be the pair
(h,h^{c})
, which is uniquely determined by the element
h\in\mathcal{W}

(3)

two points
x,y\in X
are separated by a wall
h
if
x\in h
and
y\in h^{c}

(4)

two walls
u
and
v
are meant to be crossing each other if all the four sets
u\cap v,u\cap v^{c},u^{c}\cap v,u^{c}\cap v^{c}
are non-empty.

In
[
7
]
, Chatterji and Niblo showed that if a group
G
acts on a wall space
(X,\mathcal{W})
, respecting the wall structure (i.e. sending walls to walls) given by
\mathcal{W}
, then there is a dual CAT(0)-cube complex
\hat{X}_{{}_{\mathcal{W}}}
associated to
(X,\mathcal{W})
, on which
G
acts suitably.

Theorem 2.12
.

[
7
]

Suppose
G
is a group acting on a wall space
X
, and
\mathcal{W}
is the set of walls with an upper bound on the number of mutually crossing walls. Then there is a finite dimensional
CAT(0)
cube complex
\hat{X}_{{}_{\mathcal{W}}}
, on which
G
admits an isometric action.

Remark 2.13
.

Although Chatterji and Niblo proved the above theorem in the proper setting (i.e. the group acting properly on the wall space), the theorem holds true both with or without the properness assumption.

3.
Weak Haagerup property for Graph Product

Let
\Gamma
be a finite simplicial graph, and
\{G_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)}
be a collection of groups having weak Haagerup property with weak Haagerup constant
\Lambda_{WH}(G_{v})=1
. For each vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
, we are given a weak Haagerup data
(G_{v},\phi_{v},R_{v},S_{v},\mathcal{H}_{v})
. In this section we will combine this entire vertex data
\{(G_{v},\phi_{v},R_{v},S_{v},\mathcal{H}_{v})\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)}

G(\Gamma)
of the groups

From the above data, approximate identity on

is given by
\{e^{-\frac{\phi_{v}}{n}}\}

suitably to get a function

[

]

Consider

is also an approximate identity of the same type as

\psi_{n,v}(y^{-1}x)=e^{-\frac{-\phi_{v}(1_{v})+\phi_{v}(y^{-1}x)}{n}}=e^{-\frac{\big[-\phi_{v}(1_{v})+||R_{v}(x)-R_{v}(y)||^{2}+||S_{v}(x)+S_{v}(y)||^{2}\big]}{n}}

Further from the discussion of section
2.3
, one can write the

as an inner-product between two vectors in a Hilbert space (see
7

\psi_{n,v}(y^{-1}x)=\langle Exp_{o}(\frac{R_{v}(x)}{\sqrt{n}})\otimes e^{\frac{\phi_{v}(1_{v})}{2n}}Exp_{o}(\frac{S_{v}(x)}{\sqrt{n}}),\\

where the vector norm of each component is bounded by
\sqrt{e^{\frac{\phi_{v}(1_{v})}{n}}}
.
For simplicity of notation we write

\alpha_{n,v}(x):=\underbrace{Exp_{o}(\frac{R_{v}(x)}{\sqrt{n}})}_{\mbox{$\alpha_{n,v,R_{v}}(x)$}}~\otimes~\underbrace{e^{\frac{\phi_{v}(1_{v})}{2n}}Exp_{o}(\frac{S_{v}(x)}{\sqrt{n}})}_{\mbox{$\alpha_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)$}}

\alpha_{n,v}
and

G_{v}
to the Hilbert space

. Note that here

.

be the finite dimensional CAT(0)-cube complex underlying the graph product

3.2.
B_{2}
-norm estimate of
\psi_{n,\Gamma}

R_{\Gamma}
maps the graph product groups

into the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{1}
(see

(\gamma,\eta)\mapsto||R_{\Gamma}(\gamma)-R_{\Gamma}(\eta)||^{2}

. Therefore the kernel

(\gamma,\eta)\mapsto\langle Exp_{o}(\frac{R_{\Gamma}(\gamma)}{\sqrt{n}}),Exp_{o}(\frac{R_{\Gamma}(\eta)}{\sqrt{n}})\rangle=e^{-\frac{||R_{\Gamma}(\gamma)-R_{\Gamma}(\eta)||^{2}}{n}}

is PD (from lemma

), taking value 1 on the diagonals, that implies the
B_{2}
-norm of the above kernel is 1.

Recall that for any

, the vector

was defined to be an
\epsilon
-perturbed average of
\alpha_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)
and
\beta_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)
. A simple calculation of norms will give us

\left.\begin{aligned} ||\alpha_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)-avg_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)||^{2}&\leq\epsilon\\
||\beta_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)-avg_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)||^{2}&\leq\epsilon\end{aligned}\right\}~\forall~x\in G_{v},

and

||avg_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)||^{2}=\frac{||\alpha_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)||^{2}+||\beta_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)||^{2}+2}{(2+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{2}}\leq\frac{2(2+\epsilon)}{(2+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{2}},

which in turn implies
|D_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)|\geq\frac{\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}{4}
. The above inequalities put together, provides a bound on the constants defined above as
C^{\alpha_{n,v,S_{v}}}(x,y)
and
C^{\beta_{n,v,S_{v}}}(x,y)
, i.e. there exists a positive real number
A>0
such that

\sup_{x,y\in G_{v}}\{|C^{\alpha_{n,v,S_{v}}}(x,y)|,|C^{\beta_{n,v,S_{v}}}(x,y)|\}<A\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}

In order to calculate the
B_{2}
-norm of
\psi_{n,\Gamma}
, we need to know what is the value of that norm for the kernel
\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}
. The above estimates gives us that the norm of the each component vectors of the vector
\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}
and
\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}
respectively, is bounded by
(1+\epsilon+2A^{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}<(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, for some
B>0
.
Lemma
2.10
tells that in the
d
-tail of any element
\gamma\in G(\Gamma)
, there can be at most

many letters. Hence the norms of the vectors
\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)
and
\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)
are bounded by
(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\frac{dM}{2}}
for any
\gamma\in G(\Gamma)
. Hence, from lemma

,

||\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}||_{B_{2}}<(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{dM}

Recall that, by definition for any
(\gamma,\eta)\in G(\Gamma)\times G(\Gamma)
, we have

(16)

\psi_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma,\eta)=\langle Exp_{o}(R_{\Gamma}(\gamma)),Exp_{o}(R_{\Gamma}(\eta))\rangle\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma,\eta)\chi_{d}(\gamma,\eta)

We know that the
B_{2}
-norm of the first part of the above kernel is 1. We will show that the summation in the second part is also close (in
B_{2}
-norm) to a PD kernel, whose

-norm is 1. This justifies the choices for the vectors
\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}
and
\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}
.

Suppose
\gamma=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\cdots\gamma_{m}
is a reduced form of

. Define a map

as follows:

The well definedness of
\zeta_{n,\Gamma}
is clear from the discussion of well definedness of the functions
\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}
given above and
||\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma)||=1
for any element
\gamma\in G(\Gamma)
. Let us define the kernel
\sigma_{n,\Gamma}:G(\Gamma)\times G(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as

\sigma_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma,\eta)=\langle\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma),\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\eta)\rangle

and observe that it is PD with
B_{2}
-norm 1. We will take the help of this kernel
\sigma_{n,\Gamma}
to estimate the
B_{2}
-norm of the second part of the equation

. Notice that

\displaystyle\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma,\eta)-\sigma_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma,\eta)=\langle\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)

\displaystyle-\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma),\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma),\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\eta)-\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\eta)\rangle

Therefore

(18)

||\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}-\sigma_{n,\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}\leq\sup_{\gamma,\eta\in G(\Gamma)}\Big(||\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)-\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma)||~||\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\eta)||+\\
||\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma)||~||\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\eta)-\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\eta)||\Big)

Since for any

and

, the following equations are true (from remark

):

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{n,v,S_{v}}(x),avg_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)\rangle+C^{\alpha_{n,v,S_{v}}}(x,x)\cdot D_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)

\displaystyle=1~~~~\text{and}

\displaystyle\langle\beta_{n,v,S_{v}}(x),avg_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)\rangle+C^{\beta_{n,v,S_{v}}}(x,x)\cdot D_{n,v,S_{v}}(x)

\displaystyle=1

so that
\langle\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma),\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma)\rangle=1=\langle\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma),\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)\rangle
(same is true for
\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}
). Using this we have:

\begin{array}[]{rl}||\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)-\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma)||^{2}=&||\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)||^{2}-1\qquad\text{and}\\
\\
||\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)-\zeta_{n,\Gamma}(\gamma)||^{2}=&||\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)||^{2}-1\end{array}

Hence the inequality
18
becomes,

\begin{array}[]{rl}||\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}-\sigma_{n,\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}\leq&\sup_{\gamma,\eta\in G(\Gamma)}\Big(\sqrt{||\alpha_{n,\Gamma,d}(\gamma)||^{2}-1}~||\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\eta)||\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+~~\sqrt{||\beta_{n,\Gamma,d}(\eta)||^{2}-1}\Big)\\
\\
\leq&\sqrt{(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{dM}-1}~(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\frac{dM}{2}}+\sqrt{(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{dM}-1}\\
\\

\leq&2\sqrt{BdM}(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{dM}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\end{array}

We now estimate

\displaystyle||\psi_{n,\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}

\displaystyle=||\langle Exp_{o}(R_{\Gamma}(\cdot)),Exp_{o}(R_{\Gamma}(\cdot))\rangle\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}(\cdot,\cdot)\chi_{d}(\cdot,\cdot)||_{B_{2}}

\displaystyle=||\langle Exp_{o}(R_{\Gamma}(\cdot)),Exp_{o}(R_{\Gamma}(\cdot))\rangle||_{B_{2}}~~||\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}(\cdot,\cdot)\chi_{d}(\cdot,\cdot)||_{B_{2}}

\displaystyle=1\cdot||\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}(\cdot,\cdot)\chi_{d}(\cdot,\cdot)||_{B_{2}}

We need to estimate
B_{2}
-norm of
\chi_{d}
, since it appears in the above expression of
||\psi_{n,\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}
. Precisely this estimate was due to Mizuta in
[
23
]
. He showed the following:

Lemma 3.3
.

[
23
]

Suppose

is a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex, and
\Xi_{d}
is characteristic function of the set
\{(x,y):d_{X}(x,y)=d\}
then there exists a polynomial

such that

.

As mentioned in the last part of the section
2.5
, we have a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex

on which
G(\Gamma)
acts. The equation
9
and the previous lemma together implies that

on

is bounded by

. So we can write,

\displaystyle||\psi_{n,\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq||\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}\Big(\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}(\cdot,\cdot)-\sigma_{n,\Gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)\Big)\chi_{d}(\cdot,\cdot)||_{B_{2}}+||\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}\sigma_{n,\Gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)\chi_{d}(\cdot,\cdot)||_{B_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}~||\psi_{n,\Gamma,d}-\sigma_{n,\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}~||\chi_{d}||_{B_{2}}+||\sigma_{n,\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}~||\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}\chi_{d}||_{B_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq||\sigma_{n,\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}||e^{-\frac{|\cdot|_{r}}{n}}||_{B_{2}}+\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}2\sqrt{BdM}(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{dM}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}p(d)

is PD with value 1 on diagonals, and the other term is also PD following equation

and lemma

, such that

. Further we can choose
\epsilon
sufficiently small so that the following is also true:

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg(\sum_{d=0}^{K}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{dM}q(d)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}(1+B\sqrt{\epsilon})^{KM}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{d}{n}}q(d)<\frac{\delta}{2}.

Then for that particular

we will have

. Now for a decreasing sequence of
\epsilon_{k}\in(0,\epsilon)

\{\psi_{n_{k},\Gamma}\}
with the property that
||\psi_{n_{k},\Gamma}||_{B_{2}}<1+\delta
for each
k
.

Proof of the main theorem

In short, what we have from the above sections are:

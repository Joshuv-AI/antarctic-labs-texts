Abstract
. The random current representation of the Ising model, along with a related path expansion, has been a source of insight on the stochastic geometric underpinning of the ferromagnetic model’s phase structure and critical behavior in different dimensions. This representation is extended here to systems with a mild amount of frustration, such as generated by disorder operators and external field of mixed signs. Further examples of the utility of such stochastic geometric representations are presented in the context of the deconfinement transition of the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} lattice gauge model – particularly in three dimensions– and in streamlined proofs of correlation inequalities with wide-ranging applications.

2.
The Ising model

By the term Ising model we refer here to any system of
\pm 1
valued“spin” variables
\{\sigma_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{V}}
associated with the sites of a graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
(described this way in terms of vertex and edge sets), with an interaction Hamiltonian of the form

{H}(\sigma):=-\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}}J_{x,y}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}-\sum_{x\in\mathcal{V}}h_{x}\sigma_{x}\,,

(2.1)

Here
J_{x,y}
and
h_{x}
are the model’s control parameters: interactions across the graph’s edges, and the external “magnetic field” applied at the graph’s sites.

The Gibbs equilibrium state of such a system in a finite subset
\Lambda\subset\mathcal{V}
,
at inverse temperature
\beta
is the probability distribution under which the expectation values of functions
F:\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}
are given by

\langle F\rangle_{\beta,\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2^{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}}F(\sigma)e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}/Z_{\Lambda}(\beta)\,

(2.2)

which is normalized by the partition function:

Z_{\Lambda,\beta}(J,h):=\frac{1}{2^{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}}e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}\,.

(2.3)

The infinite volume Gibbs states are defined through limits of the Gibbs states in finite domains, possibly under some specified boundary conditions. They can also be characterized as solutions of the corresponding local DLR equations.
Among the first questions of interest are:

i)

for what range of
\beta
(at given
(\mathcal{J},h)
) does the limiting state depend on the boundary conditions,

ii)

what is the decay rate of spin-spin correlations,

iii)

what is the structure of the multi-spin correlations, especially at the model’s critical points.

3.
Selected provable results

In the absence of exact solutions, which so far have been limited to the mean-field case and dimensions
d=1,2
, the model’s rigorous analysis has been based on expansions and inequalities.
In particular the latter, being less limited by issues of convergence, allowed to establish the following basic results on the model’s phase transition and critical behavior.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

For any ferromagnetic Ising model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, at
d>1
, with a shift invariant irreducible pair interaction of exponential decay in
\|x-y\|
(the Euclidean distance),
there exists
\beta_{c}<\infty
such that:

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta,0}\,\,\,\begin{cases}\,\,\leq A(\beta)\,e^{-\|x-y\|/\xi(\beta)}&\forall\beta<\beta_{c}\quad\mbox{with $\xi(\beta)>0,\,A(\beta)<\infty$}\\[8.61108pt]
\,\,\geq M(\beta)^{2}&\forall\beta>\beta_{c}\quad\mbox{with $M(\beta)>0$}\end{cases}

(3.1)

Furthermore, approaching the critical point
(\beta_{c},0)
from three different directions:

{M(\beta,0)\geq\rm{C}|\beta-\beta_{c}|_{+}^{1/2}\,,\quad M(\beta_{c},h)\geq\rm{C}\,h^{1/3}\,,\quad\frac{\partial M}{\partial h}(\beta,0)\geq\frac{\rm{C}}{|\beta_{c}-\beta|_{+}^{1}}}

(3.2)

with constants
\rm{C}
which may vary from one equation to another.

Bundled here is a number of statements of which even the assertion that the low and high temperature regimes extend up to a single transition point (i.e. without an intermediate phase where neither option holds) is beyond the reach of perturbation expansions. Proofs have been derived through non-perturbative means, mostly based on correlation inequalities and relations expressed in terms of partial differential inequalities which they enable.

Though stated initially for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the familiar proofs readily generalize to Ising spin systems on amenable transitive graphs with homogenous ferromagnetic interaction of exponential decay. And with suitable adjustments in the decay rate in (
3.1
), the assumption of exponential decay of
J_{x,y}
can be adjusted to slower decay rates, as long as the two-body interaction is summable, i.e.
\|J\|_{1}<\infty
.

More can be said for the critical behavior of the nearest neighbor interaction in different dimensions. And not only about the special case of
d=2
, but also for higher dimensions. A significant change occurs as
d
crosses its “upper-critical” value
d=4
, cf.
[
2
,
9
,
12
,
11
]
and references therein. Fundamental role for many of these was played by methods which emerged from the model’s random current representation, which is our main subject here.

4.
The random current representation

4.1.
RCR’s construction

Following is the RCR definition in the context of finite systems, as it was presented in
[
2
]
. Let us however note that RCR’s infinite volume limits admit also a direct formulation on the limiting graphs. As was learned in
[
12
]
, the infinite volume RCR formalism allows to combine RCR tools with powerful considerations enabled by translation invariance.

Definition 4.1
.

For an Ising model on a finite graph
\mathbb{G}

of vertex and edge sets
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{E}
,
with the Hamiltonian

{H}_{\mathbb{G}}(\sigma):=-\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{G})}J_{x,y}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\,.

(4.1)

a current configuration is an integer-valued function defined over the unordered edges,
\mathbf{n}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
The value of
n((x,y))
referred to as the current’s flux over the edge
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}
.

The collection of vertices of odd incident flux

{\partial\mathbf{n}\,:=\,\{x\in\mathcal{V}:\,(-1)^{\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}}n((x,y))}=-1\}}

(4.2)

is referred to as the current’s
source set
.

At given
\beta\geq 0
, the current configurations are assigned the
weight
function

{w(\mathbf{n}):=\prod_{\{x,y\}\subset\mathcal{E}}\frac{\displaystyle(\beta J_{x,y})^{n((x,y))}}{n((x,y))!}}\,.

(4.3)

In these terms, the Ising model’s partition function (at the free boundary conditions) is:

Z_{\Lambda,\beta}(J,h):=\frac{1}{2^{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{\sigma:\mathcal{V}\to\{-1,1\}}\prod_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}}\exp(\beta J_{x,y}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}:\,\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}w(\mathbf{n})\,.

(4.4)

This representation emerges through the expansion of the Gibbs factors
into its Taylor series in
\{J_{x,y}\}
(which is convergent for all
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
)

\exp(\beta J_{x,y}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y})=\sum_{n((x,y))\geq 0}\frac{(\beta J_{x,y}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y})^{n((x,y))}}{n((x,y))!}\,

(4.5)

followed up by the independent average over the spin values.

Correspondingly, the Gibbs state multi-spin correlation functions take the form

S_{n}(x_{1},...,x_{n}):={\langle\prod_{x\in A}\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{n}:\,\partial\mathbf{n}=A}w(\mathbf{n})}{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{n}:\partial\mathbf{n}=\emptyset}w(\mathbf{n})}\,}

(4.6)

In this reformulation of the expectation values, Ising spins appear as source-insertion operators acting on the functional integral/sum (
4.4
).

It is instructive to note that any current configuration
\mathbf{n}
is decomposable (in a non-unique way) into the sum of functions counting the number of times the graph’s edges are traversed by a union of a family of potentially overlapping of loops with few lines of non-overlapping end points (whose collection forms
\partial\mathbf{n}
). In the model’s infinite volume limit this may also include lines running off to infinity (at one end or both).

In a sense,
\mathbf{n}
resembles the flux of a charge-conserving current, albeit of a charge which is conserved only
mod
2 (or, alternatively stated: a parity current).

The more comprehensive random current perspective was developed in

5.
RCR based path expansions

To sharpen the above observation about the conservation of parity into a working tool, in
[
2
,
10
,
3
]

it was found useful to extract from the RCR expression for correlation functions
a “backbone” of paths supported on edges of odd flux, leaving the rest as a random sourceless flux configuration.

The decomposition of the random current’s flux into an odd “backbone” and loops is not unique.
In
[
2
]
the backbone paths were extracted by starting from a randomly selected source, then proceeding through one of the
edges of odd

\mathbf{n}_{b}
which were not yet traversed, selected at random, and continuing in this fashion till another source is reached. If by that time not all sources were paired, the next one will be selected at random and the construction continued. The construction stops once the source set is fully paired.

In subsequent works it was found more convenient to base the path selection on a predetermined ranking of the sites and (separately) edges of the finite graph. We retain here this choice.

In this manner one gets a representation of the form

\langle\sigma_{x_{1}}...\sigma_{x_{2n}}\rangle\ =\ \sum_{\mbox{pairings}}\rho(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},...,\gamma_{n})

(5.1)

where
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
are paths by which the
2n
sources are paired, each path expressed as a sequence of concatenated edges
b
. (To avoid confusion with Euler’s constant
e

b
as the generic symbol for edges.)

The weights
\rho(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},...,\gamma_{n})
are obtained by summing the Gibbs weights
w(\mathbf{n})
of random currents which yield the given collection of backbone paths (under the preselected ranking of sites and edges).

In any such deterministic algorithm the specificaiton of
\gamma
implies not only that
\mathbf{n}
is odd along the edges covered by
\gamma

\gamma

It follows (cf.
[

\rho(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},...,\gamma_{n})\ =\ I[\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},...,\gamma_{n}]\ \zeta_{\Lambda}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},...,\gamma_{n})\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{b\in\gamma_{j}}\tanh(\beta J_{b})

(5.2)

where
I[\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},...,\gamma_{n}]
is a binary indicator function which is

\zeta(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},...,\gamma_{n})\ =\ \frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(\widehat{\gamma}_{1},\widehat{\gamma}_{2},...,\widehat{\gamma}_{n})}{Z_{\Lambda}}\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{b\in\widehat{\gamma}_{j}}\cosh(\beta J_{b})

(5.3)

where
Z^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(\widehat{\gamma}_{1},...,\widehat{\gamma}_{n})
is the partition function under a depleted interaction, obtained by setting
J_{b}=0
for all the edges

.

Theorem 5.1
.

For the random walk expansion based on the deterministic algorithm for the selection of RCR backbone paths the following holds regardless of the order of sites and edges which guides the path selection:

i)

Denoting by

The partial sums of

where

5.1.
A constructive exchange

The partial similarity between the stochastic geometry underlying the Ising correlation and percolation has led to a constructive dialog. RCR-based relations bounds on the multi spin correlation functions have inspired analogous, yet somewhat different, bounds for the connectivity functions of independent percolation models, reachable by other methods
[
2
,
13
]
, and related but different criteria for the upper-critical dimension. That was followed by proofs of sharpness of the phase transitions and mean-field critical exponent bounds, but here progress proceeded in the reverse order
[
5
,
6
]
.
Though only partial, the overlap in the results raised the question whether the two models can be handled simultaneously. In time, this challenge was reached (though still partially) through yet another technique
[
38
]
which is applicable to both, and beyond - to the broader class of FK models that is presented next.

6.
The Fortuin Kasteleyn random cluster representation

6.1.
The FK representation

As a tool for a stochastic geometric perspective on the Ising models’s Gibbs states
the
random current
representation competes for our attention with the
Fortuin-Kasteleyn (FK)
random cluster
representation
[
46
,
56
]
.
Each reveals a stochastic geometric underpinning of the spin correlations, though in a somewhat different manner.
Before commenting further on the relation between the two, which is of relevance
for our discussion in Section
9.3
let us briefly recall the Fortuin-Kasteleyn construction.

The FK representation starts from the identity

e^{\beta J_{u,v}\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}}=e^{\beta J_{u,v}}\left[p_{u,v}\cdot\delta_{\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v},1}+(1-p_{u,v})\cdot 1\right]

(6.1)

with
p_{u,v}=1-e^{-2\beta J_{u,v}}
.
One may read in this a polarization of the Gibbs factor associated to the edge
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}

into a linear superposition of two term, under the one with
\delta_{\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v},1}
the spins are constrained to agree and under the other they do not interact at all:

For the Ising model’s partition function
under the two-body Hamiltonian (
4.1
) this yields

Z_{\Lambda,\beta}(J):=\frac{1}{2^{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{\sigma:\mathcal{V}\to\{-1,1\}}\prod_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}}e^{\beta J_{x,y}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y})}=\\
=\frac{e^{\beta\sum_{b\in\mathcal{E}}J_{b}}}{2^{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\sigma:\mathcal{V}\to\{-1,1\}}\\
\omega\subset\mathcal{E}\end{subarray}}\,\,\prod_{b\in\omega}p_{b}\prod_{b\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\omega}(1-p_{b})\cdot\bm{1}_{\omega}\left[\sigma\right]

(6.2)

where
b
and
(x,y)
are used interchangeably to denote the graph’s edges, and the last factor is the indicator function

\bm{1}_{\omega}\left[\sigma\right]=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\sigma_{x}$ is constant on each of the $\omega$-connected clusters}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

Summing over the spin degrees of freedom one gets:

Z_{\Lambda,\beta}(J,h)=\frac{e^{\beta\sum_{b\in\mathcal{E}}J_{b}}}{2^{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{\omega\subset\mathcal{E}}\,\,\prod_{b\in\omega}p_{b}\prod_{b\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\omega}(1-p_{b})\cdot q^{N(\mathbf{\omega})}

(6.3)

at
q=2
, where
N(\mathbf{\omega})
is the number of
\mathbf{m}
-connected clusters in the corresponding decomposition of
\mathcal{V}
, and
q
is the number of states of each spin.

Associated with any decomposition of the partition function into a sum of positive terms is a probability distribution of the relevant degrees of freedom, that is obtained by normalizing the summed weights by the factor
1/Z_{\Lambda,\beta}
. The FK joint probability distribution of the spin and edge variables
\{\sigma_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{V}}
and
\{\omega_{b}\}_{b\in\mathcal{E}}
,
can be read in this manner from (
6.2
), and the marginal distribution of just the edges can be read from (
6.3
).
The latter forms the Fortuin-Kasteleyn random cluster model.

A notable features of the joint distribution of
\{\sigma_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{V}}
and
\mathbf{\omega}=\{\omega_{b}\}_{b\in\mathcal{E}}

is the simplicity of the conditional distribution of each of these two sets of variables conditioned on the other:

i) Conditioned on
\mathbf{\omega}
, the spins
\sigma_{x}
are constrained to “vote” as cliques: full agreement within each
\mathbf{\omega}
-connected cluster, no correlation between different clusters.

ii) Conditioned on the spin configuration, the edge variables
\mathbf{\omega}
are distributed independently of each other, subject only to the constraint that
n((x,y))=0

where
\sigma_{x}\neq\sigma_{y}
.

Partial summation over
\{\sigma_{x}\}
, and normalization of the terms in the resulting (
6.3
), leads to a joint distribution of the edge variable
\mathbf{\omega}
which is known as the “
q
-random cluster model”, in this case at
q=2
(
[
46
,
56
]
).

From the above description of the conditional distribution of the spins under
\mathbf{\omega}
one readily concludes that for any pair of sites
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
:

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{\text{FK}(2)}_{\Lambda,\beta}\big(x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}y\big)\,.

(6.4)

with
\mathbb{P}^{FK}
the marginal probability distribution of the edge variables
\mathbf{\omega}
corresponding to (
6.3
).

A comparison of (
6.4
) and RCR’s (
4.13
) leads to the striking observation that
the Ising model’s onset of
long range order
coincides with a pair of symultaneous with both
the double random current’s percolation transition, and the percolation transition in the corresponding FK random cluster model.
However along with that one may also notice a curious difference: the point-to-point connection probabilities for the FK clusters and those
for the doubled RCR are not the same. The relation between the two (linked through
\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}
) is:

\mathbb{P}^{\text{FK}(2)}_{\Lambda,\beta}\big(x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}y\big)=\sqrt{\mathbb{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\Lambda,\beta}\big(x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}y\big)}\,,

(6.5)

•

Variance bounds based on algorithmic exploration (cf.
[

39
]
.

To which one may add that the different FK(q) probability distributions can be related through the FKG inequalities, which allow to compensate for the difference in

.
(The last reference includes a notable result for which this relation was put to use).

Compared to the FK

7.
Geometric representations’ adjustment to frustration

7.1.
Frustration

Frustration is said to be present when not all the term in the Hamiltonian can be minimized simultaneously.
In the case of

H=-\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}}J_{x,y}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}

(7.1)

with the free or locally wired boundary conditions,
frustration is present if for some loop of edges the product of the coupling coefficients is negative.

Frustration may also result from the inclusion of external fields or boundary conditions of mixed signs. For a unified treatment, the latter two cases can be turned into (
7.1
)
through the Griffiths’s ghost spin trick (recalled below).

7.2.
Frustration-adjusted FK measures

The FK representation’s adaptation to frustration bearing Hamiltonians was presented, and discussed in the context of spin glass models, by Y Kasai and A. Okiji
[
66
]
, and Gandolfi-Kean-Newman

]

For such an extension, in the case of antiferromagnetic (negative) couplings the polarizing decomposition (

with
p_{u,v}=1-e^{-2\beta|J_{u,v}|}

Under this convention

The corresponding extension of (

(7.3)

\bm{1}^{\bf J}_{\omega}\left[\sigma\right]=\begin{cases}1&J_{u,v}\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\geq 0\quad\mbox{for all $(u,v)$ with $\omega_{(u,v)}=1$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}\,.

(7.4)

For a given edge configuration
\omega\subset\mathcal{E}

Definition 7.1

For a system with a Hamiltonian of the form (

Lattice gauge models (LGM) have recently drawn renewed attention, including the case of

More explicitly, we complete here the proof that the

\mathbb{Z}^{3}

The area law decay has already been established in the afore mentioned work of Lebowitz-Pfister
[

\beta<\beta_{c}

This is supplemented here with proof of a perimeter-law lower bound throughout the complementary phase, in which the dual Ising model’s spin-spin correlation’s decay exponentially fast. (The duality relation is recapitulated below.)

It should be noted that sharpness of the analogous phase transition in the four dimensional self dual model was recently presented in

Let us add here some remarks of technical nature.

1) While some of the key steps in the above proof was expressed in terms of the FK random cluster representation, they also could have been presented in terms of the RCR random current representation. The main difference would be to replace the simple FKG argument in the justification of (

2) The switch to RCR produces a minor gain in that the probability of connection in RCR terms is the square of the corresponding one for FK (i.e.

This is in line with the general thumb-rule that was outline above: when both RCR and FK techniques are applicable, which is not always the case, the key inequalities may be simpler to explain is the context of FK. However tighter bound are provided by RCR. In some cases it may be beneficial to combine the perspective which the representations provide (see, eg.
[
37
]
).

3) At different points in the discussion we find that not only the percolation thresholds coincide but also probabilities of certain events, e.g. in (
8.1
), are exactly the same for RCR and FK percolation. Still may other are not (e.g. the two point connectivity probability, as mentioned above).
It could be of interest to clarify the relevant criterion. More is said on the subject in the recent study
[
62
]
where these and other graphical representations of a number of models are put on common footing.

And beyond technicalities:

4) The stochastic geometry underlying the deconfinement transition have been laid open in the random plaquette version of this transition, which was presented and studied in
[
7
,
57
]
. In it, the relevant stochastic-geometric phenomenon is the spontaneous formation in a system of random plaquettes of surfaces which extend without boundary. This can be viewed as a higher-dimensional analog of the
the spontaneous formation, in the bond percolation model, of lines extending to infinity along open edges.
In

the random surface transition is dual to percolation in the dual bond percolation model.
Duality relations extends further into more general

10.
Extensions of RCR

The RCR formulated above is for the spin-flip symmetric Ising Hamiltonian (
4.1
) at zero magnetic field (
h=0
). This formalism admits a number of extensions, some of which are by now well known, while some are less so:

i) Incorporation of external field.

The external field terms

\{h_{x}\sigma_{x}\}
are turned into binary spin-spin interaction through the incorporation of a “ghost spin”
\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}
and replacement
\sigma_{x}\rightarrow\sigma_{x}\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}
. This stratagem was first employed by R. Griffiths in his extension of spin correlation inequalities to odd numbers of spins
[
53
]
. It’s natural extension to random currents
[
6
]
inspired the very useful addition of a ghost site in percolation models
[
5
]
.

ii) Other classes of variables.

Beyond Ising, to spin variables in the Griffiths-Simon class
[
54
,
59
]
. This enabled the extension of the RCR based results on the Ising spin model to the lattice
\phi^{4}_{d}
field
[
59
,
2
]
.

iii) RCR with local reflection symmetry

The combinatorics underlying random current’s switching symmetry require a pair of partially matching RCR configurations. In the presence of a reflection symmetry such a pair can be created by folding a domain onto itself, along a symmetry hyperplane. Possible applications of this technique are demonstrated here on two examples:

(1)

The inequality of Messager and Miracle-Sole
[
74
]
/ Schrader
[
82
]
on the monotonicity of the two point correlation function
S_{2}(0,\bf{x})
in the nearest neighbor model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

(2)

The van Beijeren inequality, which has the striking implication that the regular Ising model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
exhibits translation symmetry breaking at all temperatures below
T_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d-1})
.

Although the inequalities are not new, the conditional symmetry argument sheds additional light on the remainder.

iv) RCR for frustration-bearing interactions

The adjustment of RCR to systems with frustration is discussed above in Section
7
.
As an example of application of the techniques presented there, in Appendix
C
we show a simpler proof of the recent (and previously unnoticed) inequality of Ding-Song-Sun
[
31
]
, and direct to yet more recent applications of that inequality.

Of the above, i) and ii) were presented and applied in
[
2
]
and iii) - iv) were within the original outline of “Part III”.

For completeness of the list let us mention in passing also the following more recent extensions of the random current method.

v) Infinite graphs.

A natural extension of the RCR notions and basic properties to infinite graphs was developed and put to use in
[
12
]
.
That enabled direct applications of arguments invoking ergodicity (under space translations) and uniqueness of the infinite cluster. These played a role in the proof of the continuity of spontaneous magnetization at
T_{c}
for three dimensional version of the model.

vi) Quantum Ising model in transversal field.

The quantum Ising model, over a graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is a system of binary quantum spins, q-bits, attached to the sites of the vertex set
\mathcal{V}
, with the quantum Hamiltonian built of local Pauli spin triplets of operators
(\sigma_{u}^{x},\sigma_{u}^{y},\sigma_{u}^{z})
:

H=-\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}}J_{u,v}\sigma^{z}_{u}\sigma^{z}_{v}-\eta\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}\sigma^{x}_{u}

(10.1)

(to which one may add the analog of the classical symmetry breaking term term
(-h)\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}\sigma^{z}_{u}
). The above acts in the natural way on the tensor product Hilbert space
\mathbb{X}=\otimes_{u\in\mathcal{V}}\mathbb{X}_{u}
, with the local spaces isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
The extension of Random Current to this model was developped in works of Björnberg-Grimmett
[
21
]
, and Crawford-Ioffe
[
30
]
, and used for the natural extension to it of some of the fundamental results which were proven by RCR for the classical Ising model.

vii) Random currents for XY models.

In a new direction - a simple extension of the random current to two component classical spin systems
\underline{\sigma}_{u}=(\sigma^{(1)}_{u},\sigma^{(2)}_{u})
, with the
O(2)
symmetric Hamiltonian

H=-\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}}J_{u,v}\,\,\underline{\sigma}_{u}\cdot\underline{\sigma}_{v}

(10.2)

was presented in
[
42
]
. It has not yet been shown to be as effective as it has in the discrete case. But as there still are some open challenges concerning the analysis of continuous spin models, we may still hear more on this topic.

with
y
along edges
(b)
with
n_{1}(b)+n_{2}(b)>0
. Each such paths hits
S
at some
u\in S
. Summing the corresponding probabilities
one gets:

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle\leq\!\!\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}=\{x,y\}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{w(\mathbf{n}_{1})}{Z(\mathbb{G})}\frac{w(\mathbf{n}_{2})}{Z(\mathbb{G}_{S,x})}\,\bm{1}\left[x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}y\mbox{ in $\mathbb{G}_{S,x}$}\right]

(B.5)

Applying to the above expression the switching lemma’s relation (

) one gets, without further degradation of the bound,

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle\leq\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}=\{u,y\}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}=\{x,u\}\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\mathbf{n}_{1})}{Z_{1}}\frac{w(\mathbf{n}_{2})}{Z_{2}}\,\ =\ \sum_{u\in S}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{\mathbb{G}_{S}}\,\langle\sigma_{u}\sigma_{y}\rangle

C.
Ding-Song-Sun inequality

J. Ding, J. Song, and R. Sun presented in
[
31
]
a new inequality, which has since found some interesting applications (listed below).
The setup is that of the Ising spin model on a finite graph
\mathbb{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
under a magnetic field which is a sum of two term, one non-negative and the other of general sign, i.e.

-H(\sigma)=\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}}J_{u,v}\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}+\sum_{u}(h_{u}+g_{u})\sigma_{u}\,,

(C.1)

with

\displaystyle g:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}\,,\qquad h:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\quad\mbox{(i.e. $h_{u}\geq 0\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{V}$)}\,.

(C.2)

The DSS inequality is:

Theorem C.1

(
[
31
]
)
.

In the above setup,

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{g+h}-\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{g-h}\leq\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{h}-\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{-h}\,.

(C.3)

And, by implication,

\langle\sigma_{x};\sigma_{y}\rangle_{g}\leq\langle\sigma_{x};\sigma_{y}\rangle_{g}\,,

(C.4)

where on the left is the truncated correlation function

\langle\sigma_{x};\sigma_{y}\rangle:=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle-\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle\,\langle\sigma_{y}\rangle\,.

(C.5)

The pivotal statement here is the first one. The second can be deduced from it simply through the expansion to second order in
h
, around
h=0
. However, the proof of (
C.3
) is considerably more involved. Following is what may potentially be seen as a simpler proof (admittedly a relative term), based on the random current techniques.

Proof of Theorem
C.1
.

Flipping the spins in the second expression on both RHS and LHS, the claimed (
C.3
) can be rewritten as

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{h+g}+\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{h-g}\leq 2\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{h}

(C.6)

For a convenient representation of (
C.6
), let us construct a multigraph

\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
through the following steps:

1)

Split the vertex set
\mathcal{V}
according the sign of
g(x)
into

\mathcal{V}_{+}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{V}\,:\,g(u)\geq 0\right\}\,,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{V}_{-}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{V}\,:\,g(u)<0\right\}\,.

(C.7)

2)

Add a “ghost site”
\mathfrak{g}
(à la Griffiths), thus forming
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathfrak{g}\}
.

3)

Link
\mathfrak{g}
to each of the original sites by two edges, one with coupling

, and the other with

.

The collection of the added edges of the first kind will be referred to as
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, and that of the second kind split into

according to the sign of
g(x)
.

The construction results in an enhanced graph
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}=(\widehat{\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathcal{E}})

(C.8)

The claimed relation (
C.6
), reformulated over

reads as

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle_{h+g}+\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle_{h-g}\leq 2\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle_{h}

(C.9)

where the subscripts refer to the pair of Hamiltonians

-H(\sigma)_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+}}{{(-)}}}=\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}}J_{u,v}\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}+\sum_{u}(h_{u}\mbox{}_{\begin{subarray}{c}+\\
(-)\end{subarray}}g_{u})\,\sigma_{u}\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}\,.

Let now
Z_{+}
and

under

and

correspondingly, and

the one under the Hamiltonian with

.

In the “frustration-adapted” RCR representation of Section

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{h+g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}=\{x,\mathfrak{g}\}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\mathbf{n}_{1})}{{\widetilde{Z}}}\,\frac{w(\mathbf{n}_{2})}{{\widetilde{Z}}}(-1)^{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{E}_{-}}(\mathbf{n}_{1})}\bm{1}_{FF}[\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}]

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}=\{x,\mathfrak{g}\}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\mathbf{n}_{1})}{{\widetilde{Z}}}\,\frac{w(\mathbf{n}_{2})}{{\widetilde{Z}}}sgn_{J}(u,v;\mathbf{n}_{1})\bm{1}_{FF}[\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}]

where:

i)

is the total flux of
\mathbf{n}_{1}
over the edges
\mathcal{E}_{-}
, i.e. the sum over
\mathbf{n}_{1}
on the edges linking
\mathfrak{g}

.

i) The frustration free condition

\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}
has no loops of odd flux.

iii)
sgn_{J}(u,v;\mathbf{n}_{1})
is the parity of the connection defined in (

) (under the Frustration Free condition FF).

The corresponding representation for

(i.e. at flipped

\mathcal{E}_{-}

D.
RCR and reflection symmetry

Reflection symmetry, and reflection positivity, enable many shortcuts in problems of probability, analysis, statistical mechanics and field theory, and for some even play a fundamental role (
[
79
,
60
,
48
]
).

In the two examples which are discussed next we encounter situation to which the following definition applies.

Definition D.1

(Markovian reflections)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{X}}
(abbreviated below by
\mathcal{R}
) be a reflection of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
with respect a hyperplane
\mathbb{X}
of co-dimension
1
, and
\mathbb{G}
a graph embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The graph is said to have
\mathcal{R}
as a Markovian reflection symmetry if

(1)

the graph is invariant under the action of
\mathcal{R}

(2)

\mathbb{X}
does not cut through any of the graph’s edges (i.e. each edge of
\mathbb{G}
may either not intersect with
\mathbb{X}
, or be fully contained in it).

An Ising spin system with pair interaction, as in in (
2.1
), is said to have
\mathcal{R}
as a Markovian reflection symmetry if the graph on which it is formulated has this property and its couplings are reflection invariant.

For Ising systems for which condition (2) fails, but only due to the presence of edges which are cut by
\mathbb{X}
at mid-points and with orthogonal intersections, the Markovian condition can be met upon the addition of mid-edge spin variables with couplings which mediate the interaction between the two at the edge’s boundary, as indicated in Fig.
3
(on the right).

When present, Markovian reflection symmetry combines well with the random current representation. Through the pairing of the current configuration
\mathbf{n}
with it reflection
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{X}}(\mathbf{n})
emerges a conditional symmetry, which enables many of the familiar benefits of RCR duplication. In particular one can reap the benefits of the switching lemma also in this context.
An example of that is the following statement with is proved below.

Theorem D.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(\Lambda,\mathcal{E})
and
H
be a finite graph embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an Ising spin pair interaction of the form (
4.1
) with a Markovian symmetry
\mathcal{R}
.
Than for any pair of sites
x,y\in\Lambda
lying on the same side of the reflection hyperplane
\mathbb{X}

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{\mathcal{R}(y)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}\,\,\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\{x,y\}}\!\!\left(y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{n}+\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{X}}(\mathbf{n})}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\mathbb{X}\cap\Lambda\right)

(D.1)

where
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\{x,y\}}
is the normalized joint probability distribution of the random current
\mathbf{n}
of weights
w(n)
under the source constraint
\partial\mathbf{n}=\{x,y\}
, as in (
4.3
).

Figure 3.
Foldings of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
through reflection symmetries with respect to lines defined by: i)
x_{2}=0
, ii)
x_{1}+x_{2}=0
, and iii)
x_{2}=1/2
. When the reflection plane cuts through edges, we add at the cut sites intermediary local spin variables, as indicated on the right.

In the proof we shall use the following notation and a reflection adapted version of the switching lemma.

For a given choice of the symmetry hyperplane, which (upon enhancement, if needed) cuts only through sites, the graph’s vertex set is presentable as a disjoint union:

\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}\sqcup\Lambda_{1}\sqcup\Lambda_{2}\,,

(D.2)

with
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda\cap\mathbb{X}
, and the other two sets
containing the remaining sites split according to the side of
\mathbb{X}
in which they lie.
Correspondingly we also split the edge set into:

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\sqcup\mathcal{E}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{E}_{2}

(D.3)

with
\mathcal{E}_{0}:=\{(u,v):u,v\in\Lambda_{0}\}

and for
j=1,2
:

\mathcal{E}_{j}:=\{(u,v):u,v\in\Lambda_{j}\}\cup\{(u,v):u\in\Lambda_{0},v\in\Lambda_{j}\}\,.

(D.4)

The Markovian condition ensures that
\Lambda_{0}
is a “cut set” for the connection
\Lambda_{1}\longleftrightarrow\Lambda_{2}
, in the sense that any path along edges of
\mathcal{E}
, which starts at some
x\in\Lambda_{1}
and ends at some
y\in\Lambda_{2}\setminus\Lambda_{0}
needs to pass through it.

Lemma D.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(\Lambda,\mathcal{E})
be a graph with a Markovian reflection symmetry
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{X}}
, and let
A\subset\Lambda
, and
y\in\Lambda_{1}
.
Then for any Ising spin system on
\mathbb{G}
with a symmetric pair interaction of the form (
4.1
), and a flux configuration
\mathbf{m}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{m}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\Lambda_{0}

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}=A\end{subarray}}w(\mathbf{n})\cdot\bm{1}\big[\mathbf{n}+\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{n})=\mathbf{m}\big]\,=\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}=A\Delta\{y,\mathcal{R}(y)\}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!w(\mathbf{n})\cdot\bm{1}\big[\mathbf{n}+\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{n})=\mathbf{m}\big]\,.\qquad\mbox{}

(D.5)

What should be noticed here is the difference in the two source condition in (
D.5
) and, importantly, the restriction of the statement to
\mathbf{m}
such that
y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{m}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\Lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

The stated relation holds trivially in case
y\in\Lambda_{0}
, so we shall focus on the case
y\in\Lambda_{1}\setminus\Lambda_{0}
.
Under the assumption made on
\mathbf{m}
, there exists a simple path supported on edges with
\mathbf{m}(b)\neq 0
which starts at
y
and stops upon reaching
\Lambda_{0}
. Concatenating it with its reflection, i.e. its image under
\mathcal{R}
, we get a simple path
\gamma
from
y
to
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{X}}(y)
such that:

i)

the path
\gamma
visits
\Lambda_{0}
exactly ones, arriving by an edge of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and departing by an edge of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
,

ii)

it advances only over edges for which
\mathbf{m}+\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{n})\neq 0
.

Such a path, selected from the collections of paths with these properties as a measurable function of
\mathbf{m}
and
y
, can be used for a version of the familiar switching argument, this time within the pair
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{n})
.

To make that more explicit, each flux functions
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}

can be presented as a triple

\mathbf{n}=(\mathbf{n}_{0},\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})

(D.6)

with
\mathbf{n}_{j}
the restriction of
\mathbf{n}
to
\mathcal{E}_{j}
, and doing the same for
\mathbf{m}
. In these terms, the claimed relation (
D.5
) takes the form:

\sum_{\mathbf{n}_{0}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}}w(\mathbf{n}_{0})\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{0},\mathbf{m}_{0}}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}_{1}:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}=A\end{subarray}}w(\mathbf{n}_{1})w(\mathbf{n}_{2})\cdot\bm{1}\big[\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{n}_{2})=\mathbf{m}_{1}\big]\\
=\sum_{\mathbf{n}_{0}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}}w(\mathbf{n}_{0})\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{0},\mathbf{m}_{0}}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}_{1}:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
\partial\mathbf{n}=A\Delta\{y,\mathcal{R}(y)\end{subarray}}w(\mathbf{n}_{1})w(\mathbf{n}_{2})\cdot\bm{1}\big[\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{n}_{2})=\mathbf{m}_{1}\big]

(D.7)

(where the only difference is in the source conditioin).
The claimed (
D.5
) follows by the combinatorial argument used above in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, applied here to the double sum at given
\mathbf{n}_{0}
.

More explicitly: one may start by restating the claimed relation in terms of the multigraph
\mathbb{G}_{1}(\mathbf{m})
, over the edge set
\mathcal{E}_{1}
of edge multiplicity
\mathbf{m}_{1}
.
Simple combinatorics allow to read from (

) that each of the above double sums of
w(\mathbf{n}_{1})w(\mathbf{n}_{2})

equals
w(\mathbf{m})
times the number of ways of to splitting the multigraph

The claimed relation (

Upon this exchange, the sources of

Proof of Theorem(

The RCR expressions for the two correlation functions are

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}:\,\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\

E.
Schrader, Messager and Miracle-Sole inequality

The following result was proved by Schrader
[
82
]

[

]
, in reference to the infinite volume state which results from either the free or the
(+)
boundary conditions. (Some additional reflection based inequalities were presented in
[

,
63
]

Theorem E.1

(The SMMS inequality)
.

For the n.n. ferromagnetic Ising model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and any pair of sites
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle\geq\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{\mathcal{R}(y)}\rangle\,

(E.1)

for any of the reflection symmetries
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with respect to a hyperplane which does not separate the two sites.

By implication, the correlation function
S_{2}(0,x)
is decreasing in
|x_{j}|
for each of the Cartesian coordinates of
x=\{x_{1},...,x_{d}\}
. It is also decreasing in
|x_{1}-x_{2}|
at fixed values of
\{(x_{1}+x_{2}),x_{3},...,x_{d}\}
and exhibits similar monotonicity with respect to other choices for coordinate systems with a reflection symmetry.

Our (
D.1
) provides an alternative proof of the SMMS inequality (
E.1
). In addition, it allows to cast the difference in the following form.

\frac{\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle-\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{\mathcal{R}(y)}\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle}\,=\,\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\{x,y\}}\!\!\left(x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{n}+\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{X}}(\mathbf{n})}}{{\not\!\!\!\longleftrightarrow}}\mathbb{X}\right)

(E.2)

One may recognize here the similarity with a know reflection principle for the hitting probabilities of a simple random walk in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(with optional absorbing boundary conditions at
\partial\Lambda
).

F.

The possibility of translation symmetry breaking in the classical Ising model in dimensions
d>2
, was established in Roland Dobrushin’s landmark paper
[
34
]
. His analysis proves that at low enough temperatures the Ising model’s magnetization in

\Lambda_{L}=[-L,L]^{d}

under the boundary conditions

\mbox{for $x\in\partial\Lambda_{L}$}:\quad\sigma_{x}=\begin{cases}+1&x_{d}>0\\
-1&x_{d}<0\\
+1&x_{d}=0\end{cases}

(F.1)

have magnetization profile which does not vanish in the limit
L\to\infty
.

The range of temperatures for which that is proven to hold was extended by H. van Beijeren
[
20
]
who proved translation symmetry breaking under such conditions for all
\beta>\beta_{c}(d-1)
. His analysis, which is crisp and insightful, is carried in terms of the Percus representation of duplicated Ising spin systems, and rests on inequalities of Lebowitz
[
69
]
.

Among his interesting results is the following statement, in which
\mathcal{R}
refers to the reflection with respect to the hyperplane
\mathbb{X}
of sites with
x_{d}=0
.

Theorem F.1

(van Beijeren
[
20
]
)
.

For any Ising spin system in an
\mathcal{R}
-symmetric domain
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
with an
\mathcal{R}
-symmetric nearest neighbor ferromagnetic pair interaction, and the boundary conditions (
F.1
):

1) The magnetization at sites in
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda\cap\mathbb{X}

F.1.
RCR representation of the Dobrushin state

Dobrushin state’s salient features include the reflection symmetry of the underlying graph, and the anti-symmetry of the boundary conditions, away from the symmetry plane, and of course the frustration that is induced by the mixed boundary conditions.

In Section
7.4
is presented a way to adjust RCR to frustration through exact
cancellations, after which the remaining RCR sum is free of the “sign-problem”. The remaining terms produce a state of the random current which is affected by the conditioning on a suitable
FF
(for “frustration free”)-event. A key tool for that is a switching argument that is enabled by the symmetry, or anti-symmetry, found in a doubled system of overlapping currents.

In Section
D
the switching technique was extended to graphs with reflection symmetry, which enables the comparison to be made between two halves of a single current, folded onto itself along the symmetry hyperplane.

The Dubrushin state may be approached through the natural combination of the two techniques.

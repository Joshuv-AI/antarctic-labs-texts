Epidural injection of hydromorphone for postoperative pain after episiotomy: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yixing Lu, Siyan Liu, Shunzhong Jing, Wei Peng, Yunan Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPerineal incision resulting in post-partum pain and impact on puerperal life was the focus of our study. We recruited postpartum mothers who had undergone vaginal delivery to receive labor analgesia and episiotomy with an epidural injection of 0.5 mg hydromorphone to assess the therapeutic effects of postpartum analgesia. The participants were randomly allocated into two groups: the control group (Group NS) received an epidural injection of an equal amount of saline, while the study group (Group HY) received an epidural injection of 0.5 mg hydromorphone. We collected relevant data from electronic medical records to compare the differences between the two groups. The intervention group demonstrated lower pain scores at 4, 8, 12, 16, and 24 h compared to the control group (p < 0.001). Additionally, a higher number of patients in the control group required pain medication (7 (15.9%) compared to 2 (4.7%)). The time to first analgesia request in group NS was earlier than that in group HY (8.94 ± 1.27 h compared to 16.96 ± 3.38 h). The study group experienced higher rates of vomiting (P = 0.002) and itching (P < 0.001). However, there were no differences between the two groups in terms of urinary retention, dyskinesia, respiratory depression, dizziness, or neonatal feeding. Introduction

Studies have indicated that 92% of women experience perineal pain on the first day after delivery, which can last for up to 2 months if untreated. Perineal pain is associated with vaginal wall laceration, cervical lacerations, episiotomies, and forceps application during labor. Episiotomies directly impact the muscle tissue and can result in more severe perineal pain than a second-degree tear. This pain affects the mother’s quality of life postpartum, and can even affect the mother-child relationship and the success of breastfeeding1. Most women experience health issues post-delivery, including back and perineal pain, with 12.8% experiencing chronic pain due to episiotomy2. Postnatal pain significantly impacts postpartum depression, with longer durations of pain leading to a higher probability of severe depression3,4. Effective perinatal pain management, including labor analgesia and postpartum acute pain treatment, can reduce postpartum complications.

In recent years, the management of Episiotomy pain has emerged as a critical issue for obstetricians and patients. Various analgesics, such as cold compresses5, topical ointments6, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and local anesthetics, are employed for treatment. Abbas et al.8 demonstrated the efficacy of topical lidocaine cream in alleviating Episiotomy pain and reducing the need for other pain medications. Macarthur et al.9 found that epidural morphine post vaginal delivery reduced the demand for pain relief by 78%, albeit with potential complications like urinary retention and skin itching. Despite these findings, there is still a lack of consensus on the optimal analgesic approach, particularly in balancing effectiveness and safety.

Recent studies are now exploring the use of hydromorphone for controlling postoperative pain. Hydromorphone, a potent opioid pain reliever, has shown to be effective in relieving pain with few side effects. However, there is a lack of research on the use of epidural hydromorphone for treating acute perineal pain10.

Thus, a Randomized controlled trial assessed the efficacy and safety of using epidural hydromorphone in alleviating pain post lateral perineotomy. The study aimed to explore the potential benefits and drawbacks of this approach to promote its use in managing perineal pain associated with childbirth.

Materials and methods

Study design

The research was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Maternal and Child Health Hospital of Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region on October 9, 2022 (reference number [2021 − 3]23). It was recorded at chictr.org.cn (14 /10/2022; identification number: ChiCTR2200063418) before the commencement of the trial and patient enrollment. All patients provided written informed consent.

A preliminary trial was carried out to establish the suitable sample size for the study, and SPSS was utilized to produce the suggested value for the optimal sample size based on the results of the preliminary trial. A total of 90 first-time pregnant women, aged ≥ 18 years, with gestational age ≥ 36 weeks, and ASA I-II classification who underwent labor analgesia with episiotomy, were enrolled in the study from December 1, 2022, to November 16, 2023. The Consort flowchart for the study is depicted in Fig. 1. Exclusion criteria included morbid obesity or combined Obstructive Sleep Apnea, pregnancy with severe underlying disease(cardiopulmonary insufficiency, LGA nephropathy, etc.), postpartum nerve injury, or acute massive hemorrhage. Neonatal asphyxia at birth was also excluded.

CONSORT flow diagram of patients’ distribution.

Sample size calculation

The study performed a Randomized Controlled Trial. The intervention group was given hydromorphone, while the control group received normal saline. Results showed an effectiveness rate of 65% in the intervention group and 25% in the control group. The sample size was calculated using a designated formula.

Calculations resulted in n = 33 cases with 1:1 randomization, where 33 subjects were in the intervention group and 33 in the control group. Taking into account 15% lost visits and refusals, a minimum of 40 subjects in each group was necessary, totaling at least 80 subjects for the study.

Randomization and intervention

All parturients were provided with standardized intradural labor analgesia, including puncture at the L3/4 space, insertion of an epidural catheter to a depth of 3–4 cm, use of an external pulse electronic pump, and administration of a liquid formula consisting of 0.08% ropivacaine and 50 µg of sufentanil, with a total volume of 150 ml. The parameters for the first dose were 6 ml, with a continuous infusion of 6 ml and a pulse drug administration every 60 min. Self-control additional doses of 8 ml were allowed with a lock time of 20 min. Adjustments to the parameters or drug concentration were made as per the progress of labor and the need for analgesia.

Post-delivery, the entire labor process was reviewed, and women with perinatal complications were excluded. Randomization was performed by an independent third party using a computer-generated procedure, patients were allocated to the control group (Group NS) (n = 45) when the random number within 90 was odd. In this group, the same dose of saline was injected into the epidural cavity at the end of labor analgesia. For patients assigned to the study group (Group HY) (n = 45), with an even random number within 90, 0.5 mg of hydromorphone was injected into the epidural cavity at the end of labor analgesia. The epidural catheter was removed 2 h after the end of spontaneous delivery, and appropriate drugs were administered.

Data collection

The main parameters measured were the pain scores and the frequency of supplementary analgesic medication usage at 4 h, 8 h, 12 h, 16 h, and 24 h after childbirth. The secondary parameters included the occurrence of adverse events such as nausea, vomiting, itching, respiratory depression, and urinary retention, as well as the mental status of the newborn, SPO2 levels, and feeding condition.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 23.0, and count data were expressed as frequency percentages n (%) and analyzed using the chi-square (χ2) test; metrics that conformed to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (± s), and comparisons between groups were made using the independent samples t-test; non-normally distributed measurements were expressed as median (M) and interquartile range (IQR) and analyzed using the rank sum test (Rank sum test), with a statistically significant difference of p < 0.05.

Results

Ninety women were recruited for the research, out of whom one woman in group NS experienced acute postpartum hemorrhage due to perineal laceration, one woman in group HY had a dislodged epidural catheter during medication injection, and one woman developed postpartum separation of the pubic symphysis. These three women were excluded from the study.

Episiotomy pain

The postpartum pain score tends to be higher for mothers in the group NS, but comparatively lower for mothers in the group HY within 24 h after delivery. The study group exhibited lower pain scores compared to the control group at 4 h (2 [IQR 0–3] vs. 5 [4–5]; p < 0.001), at 8 h (3 [IQR 2–4] vs. 5 [4–6]; p < 0.001), at 12 h (3 [IQR 3–4] vs. 4 [4–5]; p < 0.001), at 16 h (3 [IQR 3–4] vs. 4 [4–5]; p < 0.001), and at 24 h (3 [IQR 3–4] vs. 4 [4–5]; p < 0.001).(Figs. 2 and 3).

Analgesic drug requirements and other adverse events

The number of cases requiring analgesic medication for maternal pain in group NS was higher than in group HY (7 (15.9%) vs. 2 (4.7%); p = 0.085). The group NS had a notably shorter time for the first request for pain relief medication compared to the group HY (8.94 ± 1.27vs.16.96 ± 3.38; p = 0.079). Additionally, there were more cases of nausea and vomiting in group HY compared to group NS (13 (30.2%) vs. 2 (4.6%); p = 0.002). The incidence of pruritus in group HY was significantly higher than in group NS (17 (39.5%) vs.0 (0); p < 0.001). However, there were no significant differences in urinary retention, lower limb dyskinesia, respiratory depression, and dizziness in the postpartum period between the two groups (Table 2).

Neonatal-related characteristics

Both groups of neonates did not exhibit lethargy, decreased SPO2, and were adequately fed either breast milk or formula.

Discussion

Maternal delivery encompasses both cesarean section and vaginal delivery. Thanks to advances in anesthesiology technology, anesthesia administration ensures the safety, comfort, and painlessness of the entire cesarean section procedure. Additionally, the use of intrathecal or intravenous postoperative analgesia effectively alleviates postpartum pain. Furthermore, the widespread adoption of intrathecal labor analgesia has proven effective in relieving pain during vaginal delivery. However, there has been neglect in addressing acute pain following normal delivery, often associated with obstetric laceration and episiotomy due to rapid labor, improper exertion, fetal distress, and other factors. Typically, women experience pain within 2 h of normal delivery, as the epidural analgesic pump is removed, leading to acute pain following the metabolism of anesthesia drugs in the perineal area. This perineal pain can result in sleep disturbance, early-stage mobility challenges, and difficulty with urination and defecation, significantly impacting the mother’s postpartum quality of life.

The perineal area is anatomically concealed and difficult to tend to, and it also involves the mother’s privacy. Most obstetricians and gynecologists prioritize the healing of perineal wounds or infections while disregarding the treatment of postpartum pain due to limited available treatments. Previous studies have mostly utilized physiotherapy methods such as cold compresses, red light therapy, and topical medications to reduce inflammation and edema, rarely resorting to oral analgesic medications directly, primarily due to safety concerns regarding postpartum breastfeeding. The study revealed that women in the NS group experienced significant pain 4–8 h after delivery, particularly when in the side-lying or sitting positions, and women with concurrent hemorrhoids reported higher pain scores. These women require increased pain relief, typically with an extra analgesic (like 100 mg of Demerol intramuscularly or 50 mg of diclofenac in the anus). At the same time, magnesium sulfate will be given to the perineum to reduce swelling. Some mothers experienced severe pain, depression, and difficulty in moving or sleeping. Women who had undergone an episiotomy or other severe birth canal lacerations experienced a more intense period of postpartum pain. Research has shown that when perineal muscle injuries exceed 50% of bulbocavernosus muscle (BSM) and superficial transverse perineal muscle (STPM), perineal pain and dyspareunia persist for up to 10 days and 6 months postpartum, respectively11. There is a growing focus on perineal pain in current studies, and its management and treatment are multifaceted, with the overarching goal being to maximize functional recovery and significantly reduce the severity and intensity of pain.

A meta-analysis has shown that all forms of epidural analgesia (continuous epidural infusion and patient-controlled epidural analgesia) provide significantly better postoperative pain relief than intravenous patient-controlled analgesia for all types of surgeries and pain assessments12. Overall, epidural anesthesia containing opioid drugs can provide more analgesic effects when comparing VAS scores. Hydrophilic opioid drugs such as morphine and hydromorphone administered epidurally can produce more prolonged analgesic effects13. In a sense, patient-controlled epidural analgesia (PCEA) is considered the gold standard for labor analgesia, and the combined use of opioid drugs and local anesthetics epidurally can produce good labor analgesia. Injecting opioid drugs epidurally during the removal of the epidural catheter is an effective method for maintaining peripartum pain relief. Epidural injection of opioid drugs directly binds to the spinal cord and spinal cord G protein-coupled µ-opioid receptors, inhibiting the release of substance P and excitatory neurotransmitters, blocking the transmission of pain impulses, and exerting potent analgesic effects. Previous studies have found that epidural injection of 0.6 mg hydromorphone can provide a median analgesia of more than 13 h10. This study observed that epidural injection of 0.5 mg hydromorphone effectively alleviated postpartum perineotomy pain in women with a median pain score below 4. Women in the control group experienced acute breakthrough pain after the complete metabolism of the epidural labor analgesia drugs, with a median VAS score up to 7, and 7 (15.91%) women required intramuscular meperidine 100 mg for relief. These women asked for pain relief earlier, approximately 8.94 ± 1.27 h after removing the epidural pump. They felt varying degrees of pain once the local anesthetic wore off. However, administering hydromorphone through epidural injection towards the end of labor prolonged the pain relief period. In the group HY, the need for pain medication decreased significantly by only 2 cases. Moreover, the time before requiring pain relief medication for the first time was longer (16.96 ± 3.38 h), corresponding to the duration of pain relief provided by hydromorphone. The use of low-dose opioid drugs in epidural analgesia effectively avoids opioid toxicity reactions. Low-dose hydromorphone does not cause postpartum motor blockade, respiratory depression, or dizziness. The risk-benefit ratio of epidural analgesia is highly regarded and has clear advantages in obstetric analgesia14.

Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) often occur during opioid treatment. Common ADRs include sedation, nausea, vomiting, constipation, and respiratory depression15. Studies have reported increased sensitivity of pregnant women to opioid-related side effects16. This study observed a higher rate of nausea and vomiting in lying-in women in the group HY (30.23% vs. 4.55%, p = 0.002), which impacted their postpartum quality of life. Proper maternal diet and postural management can help prevent these reactions. If physical methods fail to alleviate vomiting, antiemetic drugs like serotonin antagonists or dexamethasone can be used17. The occurrence of adverse events and opioid use is dose-dependent, and finding the right balance between pain relief and side effects requires further investigation.

Epidural analgesia has a decreased occurrence of nausea and vomiting as well as sedation compared to intravenous analgesia, but it has a higher occurrence of pruritus, urinary retention, and dyskinesia12. The most frequently reported side effect of intravertebral opioid usage is generalized itching of the skin, and the mechanism behind itching induced by intravertebral opioids is not well understood. Itch is more closely associated with cerebrospinal fluid concentrations than with blood concentrations13. Chestnut reported that the epidural administration of 1 mg hydromorphone resulted in a 58% probability of itching, with 31% of patients requiring an anti-itch medication10. Lower doses of hydromorphone produce fewer side effects, and in this study utilizing 0.5 mg hydromorphone, the incidence of skin itching was 39.53%. Itching was highly individualized, with some instances lasting up to 24 h. Numerous treatments are available for opioid-induced itching. However, many of them are subject to controversy. Presently, there is limited evidence supporting the efficacy of opioid receptor antagonists and mixed agonist-antagonists, particularly µ-opioid antagonists and κ-opioid agonists, in reducing opioid-induced itch18. In cases where a woman experiences intolerable itching, a similar medication could be an option. Nevertheless, severe itching was not reported in the study group. Relief may be obtained through maternal physical interventions or the external application of traditional Chinese medicine spray. It has been proposed that the first childbirth, intrathecal anesthesia, instrument-assisted vaginal delivery, and episiotomy are directly associated with postpartum urinary retention19,20. Intrathecal anesthesia disrupts nerve impulses temporarily, negatively affecting bladder function in the postpartum period and causing urinary elimination dysfunction. Perineal incision directly damages pelvic floor muscles and innervation, leading to decreased bladder sensitivity and contractility. Instrument-assisted vaginal delivery weakens reflexes and causes relaxation of the urethral sphincter and periurethral muscles, as well as increased perineal swelling, resulting in urinary retention. The use of small doses of hydromorphone for analgesia inhibits bladder-related innervation and the contraction of pelvic floor muscles, thereby prolonging the first postpartum micturition time. In the study, the median time for the first postpartum micturition in the group HY was 4.5 h compared to the group NS (2.29 h); however, the number of cases requiring manual catheterization or the use of glycerin and phentolamine to promote urination was similar between the two groups, with no overall difference. Most of the women who experienced no desire to urinate or had difficulty urinating after delivery had severe perineal lacerations, extensive trauma areas, and required uterine tamponade to stop bleeding and indwelling urinary catheters for urination after delivery. Instrumental delivery, perineal laceration, lateral perineal incision, and intrathecal analgesia directly contributed to postpartum urinary retention with multiple factors.

Limited data indicates that epidural hydromorphone is excreted in small amounts through breast milk21. Intravenous or oral administration of the drug has been shown to increase the level of breast milk secretion. The study found that epidural administration of small doses of hydromorphone did not change the feeding habits of the mother or baby and did not lead to adverse effects such as neonatal drowsiness or respiratory depression.

This treatment approach involves using a catheter for labor analgesia to directly administer the drug, without the need for additional invasive procedures. Furthermore, it eliminates the requirement for external medical equipment, reducing the risk of epidural infection and facilitating maternal postnatal activities and personal hygiene. The treatment measures are positioned away from the perineal area, effectively safeguarding the mother’s privacy. Pain is a personal, subjective experience, and different individuals perceive the same external pain stimulus differently. To objectively evaluate the degree of pain from maternal wounds, blood hormone levels such as catecholamines and prostaglandins can be measured, reducing the potential for subjective pain perception errors. However, due to financial limitations, this study did not include blood tests.

Limitations of this study

Small sample size from a single center. Exclusion of mothers from diverse regions and healthcare facilities could restrict generalizability. Short follow-up period lacking assessment of long-term and adverse effects. Sole focus on hydromorphone without comparison to other analgesic drugs. Future studies should involve larger, multicenter, multidrug, and long-term follow-up to enhance validation of epidural hydromorphone injection efficacy in post-episiotomy pain management.

Conclusion

The administration of low doses of hydromorphone via epidural injection can effectively lessen maternal postoperative pain following an episiotomy. However, it poses potential risks including vomiting, skin itching, and prolonging the time to the first micturition. When the benefits of this approach outweigh the associated risks, it can be used in postnatal analgesia for parous mothers who have sustained additional trauma in the perineal region, such as perineal and cervical lacerations.

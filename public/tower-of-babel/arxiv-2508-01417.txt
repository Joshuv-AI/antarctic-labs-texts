Power Graph Classes and Overfullness
Elie Feinsilber

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the edge-coloring number of the power graph of a finite group. We characterize which finite groups have overfull power graphs, showing that this occurs if and only if the group is cyclic of odd prime power order. We then show that this classifies the class of power graphs: The power graph of a finite group is Class 2 if and only if the group is cyclic of odd prime power order.

Introduction

The study of graph defined on groups has been a growing area of research in the last decades. The study of these is not new as Cayley graphs have been around for some time, but their study was brought a new perspective when Kelarev and Quinn
[
10
]
introduced the directed power graph in 2002, a directed graph with vertex set the elements of a group and where
x\rightarrow y
if
y
is a power of
x
. Motivated by this, Chakrabarty, Ghosh, and Sen
[
3
]
introduced the
power graph
: the simple graph
\mathscr{G}(G)
with vertex set the elements of
G
and where
a\sim b
if and only if one is a power of the other. This power graph, as well as other graph defined on algebraic structures, like the enhanced power graph, or the commuting graph, have been studied in more length than before in recent years. A good overview of the results and current progress can be found in
[
11
]
.

One of the more natural graph-theoretic questions that can often be asked of graphs, and has many applications, is to ask what its edge-coloring numbers is. We use
\chi^{\prime}(\Gamma)
to denote the minimal number of colors required to edge-color a graph
\Gamma
(i.e. such that no two adjacent edges have the same color). It was shown by Vizing (
[
16
]
) and Gupta (
[
8
]
) independently that, for any graph, its coloring number is either the maximum degree of any vertex in the graph, or one more. We denote by
\Delta(\Gamma)
the largest degree of any vertex in the graph.

Theorem 1.0.1
.

(Vizing, Gupta)
For every graph
\Gamma
,
\Delta(\Gamma)\leq\chi^{\prime}(\Gamma)\leq\Delta(\Gamma)+1
.

Following this theorem, graphs with
\chi^{\prime}(\Gamma)=\Delta(\Gamma)
are referred to as Class 1 and graphs with
\chi^{\prime}(\Gamma)=\Delta(\Gamma)+1
are referred to as Class 2. We remark that determining whether a graph is Class 1 is an NP-complete problem (Holyer in
[
9
]
).

A few tools seem to have been very useful in study of edge-colorings of graphs. Given a graph
\Gamma
on
n
vertices, we say that
\Gamma
is
overfull
if
|E(\Gamma)|/\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor>\Delta(\Gamma)
, where
\Delta(\Gamma)
denotes the maximum vertex degree in
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
is overfull, then
\chi^{\prime}(\Gamma)=\Delta(\Gamma)+1
as no color can be used for more than
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
edges.
One other tool for looking at edge colorings is what is called the core of a graph. The
core
of
\Gamma
, denoted by
\Gamma_{\Delta}
, is the subgraph of
\Gamma
induced by the vertices of degree
\Delta(\Gamma)
. This core has implications on the class of a graph:

Theorem 1.0.2
.

(Vizing
[
15
]
)
If
\Gamma_{\Delta}
has at most two vertices, then
G
is class 1.

Theorem 1.0.3
.

(Fournier
[
7
]
)
If
\Gamma_{\Delta}
contains no cycle, then
G
is class 1.

Finally, Chetwynd and Hilton made a conjecture about edge colorings:

Overfull Conjecture.

(Chetwynd and Hilton
[
4
]
)
A graph
\Gamma
with
\Delta(\Gamma)>\frac{n}{3}
is class 2 if and only if it has an overfull subgraph
S
such that
\Delta(\Gamma)=\Delta(S)
.

Some progress was made on the conjecture in various papers by Chetwynd and Hilton (
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
), Plantholt (
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
), and subsequently Rhee (
[
14
]
) the combination of which amounted to the following theorem:

Theorem 1.0.4
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with
\Delta(\Gamma)\geq|V(\Gamma)|-3
. Then
\Gamma
is Class
2
if and only if
\Gamma
contains an overfull subgraph
H
with
\Delta(H)=\Delta(\Gamma)
.

Back to power graphs, since the identity element of the group
G
is a power of every element of the group, we can get a clearer idea of the structure the graph. Notably, the following result of Cameron is very useful to us.

Proposition 1.0.5
.

[
2
]

Let
G
be a finite group; let
S
be the set of vertices of the power graph
\mathcal{G}(G)
which are joined to all other vertices. Suppose that
|S|>1
. Then one of the following occurs:

1.

G
is cyclic of prime power order, and
S=G
;

2.

G
is cyclic of non-prime-power order
n
, and
S
consists of the identity and the generators of
G
, so that
|S|=1+\varphi(n)
;

3.

G
is generalised quaternion, and
S
contains the identity and the unique involution in
G
, so that
|S|=2
.

2
Results

We will now consider the graph-theoretic concepts outlined above specifically for power graphs of finite groups.

Proposition 2.0.1
.

If
|G|
is even, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is not overfull.

Proof.

Say
|G|=2n
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{|E(\mathscr{G}(G))|}{\lfloor 2n/2\rfloor}>2n-1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow|E(\mathscr{G}(G))|>\frac{2n(2n-1)}{2}

which never holds since a complete graph on
2n
vertices has
\frac{2n(2n-1)}{2}
edges.
∎

Lemma 2.0.2
.

If
G
is a group of odd order with
|G|=2n+1
, then, to be overfull,
\mathscr{G}(G)
can lose at most
n-1
edges compared to
K_{2n+1}
.

Proof.

For
\mathscr{G}(G)
to be overfull, we need
\mathscr{G}(G)
to have

\displaystyle\frac{|E(\mathscr{G}(G))|}{\lfloor(2n+1)/2\rfloor}>2n\Leftrightarrow\frac{|E(\mathscr{G}(G))|}{\lfloor 2n/2\rfloor}>2n\Leftrightarrow|E(\mathscr{G}(G))|>2n^{2}\text{ edges. }

Now,

\displaystyle\frac{2n(2n+1)}{2}-(2n^{2}+1)=\frac{2n(2n+1-2n)}{2}-1=n-1

So we may lose up to
n-1
edges if we want to remain overfull.
∎

Proposition 2.0.3
.

If only the identity is joined to all other vertices, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is not overfull.

Proof.

From before, if
|G|
is even, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is not overfull. So say
|G|=2n+1
and only the identity is joined to all other vertices. Then every other element,
2n
of them, must have at least one vertex to which it is not adjacent. That’s at least
n
missing edges, hence breaking the requirement of the above lemma. Therefore if only the identity is joined to all other vertices,
\mathscr{G}(G)
is not overfull.
∎

Proposition 2.0.4
.

If
G
is a cyclic group of odd prime-power order, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is overfull.

Proof.

If
G
is a cyclic group of odd prime power order, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is complete as shown in Prop
1.0.5
. In which case we have
\frac{2n(2n+1)}{2}=2n^{2}+n
edges which is more than the necessary
2n^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.0.5
.

If
G
is a cyclic group of odd order which is not a prime power, then
G
is not overfull.

Proof.

Let
|C_{2n+1}|=p_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdot p_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdot p_{3}^{a_{3}}\cdot...\cdot p_{r}^{a_{r}}
. For each
p_{i}^{a_{i}}
, the Euler totient function
\varphi(\frac{2n+1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}})
gives the number of elements of order
p_{i}^{a_{i}}
in
C_{2n+1}
and in the cyclic subgroup generated by
p_{i}^{a_{i}}
. So we have disjoint cliques (since the identity doesn’t share their order it is not included in the count) on
\varphi(\frac{2n+1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}})
elements for each
p_{i}^{a^{i}}
. Now
\frac{2n+1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}
is always gives a product of primes, so we have cliques on
\varphi(\frac{2n+1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}})=\varphi(p)\varphi(q)...=(p-1)(q-1)...
vertices.

Now for any two cliques on
(p-1)
and
(q-1)
elements, we are missing
(p-1)(q-1)=pq-(p+q)+1
edges. And since
p+q
is certainly bounded above by
\frac{2n+1}{2}
(in our case
<\frac{2n+1}{3}
), this gives at least
\frac{2n+1}{2}-1
edges lost, which is lower than the threshold to be overfull shown in Lemma
2.0.2
. For any more primes, this still holds as we will always lose more edges that we ’gain back’ in the calculation
(p_{1}-1)(p_{2}-2)(p_{3}-3)...
.
∎

Theorem 2.0.6
.

Let
G
be a finite group, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is overfull if and only if
G
is cyclic of odd prime-power order.

Proof.

For the right to left implication, Proposition
2.0.4
shows that if
G
is cyclic of odd prime power order, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is overfull.
For the other direction, note that, as shown in Proposition
2.0.1
, if
|G|
is even, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
cannot be overfull. Also note that if only the identity is adjacent to all other vertices, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is not overfull (Proposition
2.0.3
). It follows from Proposition
1.0.5
that, to be overfull,
G
must be either a cyclic group, or the generalized quaternion.

Now the generalized quaternion group has even order so it cannot be overfull. So
G
must be cyclic. Yet, as shown in Lemma
2.0.5
, if
G
is a cyclic group of odd order which is not a prime power, then
G
is not overfull. Thus
G
must be a cyclic group of odd prime power order and this completes our proof.
∎

Theorem 2.0.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group, then
\mathscr{G}(G)
is class 2 if and only if it is a cyclic group of odd prime-power order.

Proof.

For a group,
\Delta(\mathscr{G}(G))
is always equal to
|V(\mathscr{G}(G))|-1
since at least the identity is adjacent to all others. Therefore, by Theorem
1.0.4
,
\mathscr{G}(G)
is Class
2
if and only if
\mathscr{G}(G)
contains an overfull subgraph
H
with
\Delta(H)=\Delta(\mathscr{G}(G))
. So it suffices to show that only
\mathscr{G}(C_{p^{\alpha}})
contains an overfull subgraph.

Now, if
\mathscr{G}(G)
had an overfull subgraph say
H
, since
\Delta(\mathscr{G}(G))
is the highest possible degree attainable in any graph of that order, i.e.
n-1
, then no proper subgraph can match it since they wouldn’t have enough vertices. So the overfull subgraph would have to be
\mathscr{G}(G)
itself. We showed in Theorem
2.0.6
, that
\mathscr{G}(G)
is overfull if and only if
G
is cyclic of odd prime power order. Thus
\mathscr{G}(G)
is Class 2 if and only if
G
is cyclic of odd prime power order.
∎

We remark that, as explained in the proof, for a finite group
G
,
\mathscr{G}(G)
is Class 2 if and only if it is overfull.

Remark
.

The proof of Theorem
1.0.4
for
\Delta(\Gamma)=|V(\Gamma)|-1
, the case applicable to power graphs of finite groups, was done in
[
12
]
. It is nevertheless not obvious to the authors how it handles graphs which are not overfull but whose edges that were removed are not independent. The proof starts by considering the vertices of the complement of
\Gamma
, which have at least one edge incident to them, and constructing a bipartite graph with these as one of the parts. For
V(\Gamma)=2n+1
their bipartite graph is
K_{n+1,n}
. This is not possible for
\mathscr{G}(C_{15})
since there are only
6
vertices with edges incident to them in
\overline{\mathscr{G}(C_{15})}
:
c^{5},c^{10},c^{3},c^{6},c^{9},c^{12}
. For this reason, we find it better to rely on the construction of
[
14
]
. Rhee’s proof brings together three key points. He first shows that if two graphs have same cardinality edge sets, we can derive one from the other by relying on the edges of its complement and interverting them with existing edges. He then shows that
K_{2n+1}-n
the complete graph
K_{2n+1}
deprived of
n
edges, is
2n
colorable if the edges removed are independent. He finally shows that for two graphs
K_{2n+1}-n
, if one is derivable from the other using the above technique, then they have the same edge-chromatic number. Bringing these together gives the desired result.

Here is a short example:
Assume we have
\mathscr{G}(C_{15})-c^{10}c^{5}+c^{6}c^{10}
, with the same coloring as
\mathscr{G}(C_{15})
in our previous coloring, but where the edge
{\{c^{10},c^{5}\}}
does not exist and where
\{c^{6},c^{10}\}
is colored by the color
2
. Following the construction of
[
14
]
, we observe that
\chi^{\prime}(c^{10}c^{11})=2
and that

is not adjacent to any edge colored
7
. We follow the color-alternating path of colors
2
and
7
starting from
c^{5}
to obtain the following vertex sequence:
c^{5}c^{14}c^{15}c^{4}c^{10}
where
\chi^{\prime}(c^{5}c^{14})=2
,
\chi^{\prime}(c^{14}c^{15})=7
,
\chi^{\prime}(c^{15}c^{4})=2
,
\chi^{\prime}(c^{4}c^{10})=7
and

is not adjacent to an edge colored
2
. We can then invert the color of each edge in that path to make

with conflict.

Precise ablation of cholesteatoma using a 445-nm diode laser
Authors: Paula Enzian, Ann Marina Detje, Birgit Lange, Zuzana Penxová, David Leffers, Ramtin Rahmanzadeh, Karl-Ludwig Bruchhage, Anke Leichtle, Ralf Brinkmann
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In cholesteatoma surgery, precise tissue resection is important, as the diseased tissue needs to be completely removed while preserving the ossicular chain to prevent recurrence and hearing loss. The ability to precisely ablate tissue using a pulsed \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser operating in single-trigger mode was investigated. Single laser pulses with a duration of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$100 ms$$\end{document} were applied with a power ranging from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5 \mathrm{to} 4 W$$\end{document} via a multimode optical fiber. Fiber distance to tissue was varied from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5 \mathrm{to} 5 mm$$\end{document}, and irradiation angles from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$20^\circ \mathrm{to} 90^\circ$$\end{document}. Porcine ear cartilage with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$500 \mu m$$\end{document} remaining perichondrium on top served to represent the human ossicle structure with cholesteatoma. Further ablation experiments were performed on human cholesteatoma and ossicle samples. Ablation craters were measured by optical coherence tomography (OCT), and tissue damage assessed with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained sections. The ablation depth reached up to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$350 \mu m$$\end{document} when operating at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4 W$$\end{document} power output without causing damage to the underlying cartilage. Laser pulses with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1 W$$\end{document} successfully ablated cholesteatoma, but did not damage the ossicles. The results show that a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser pulses can be used to effectively remove cholesteatoma, potentially preserving ossicles and hearing ability.

Introduction

Chronic middle ear infection (otitis media) is one of the most common ear diseases worldwide and is estimated to affect more than 20 million people1. Approximately one quarter of the patients develop cholesteatoma over time – a special form of otitis media characterized by the ingrowth of keratinizing squamous epithelium into the middle ear2–4.

Cholesteatomas often remain undetected for years, and initial symptoms such as tinnitus, purulent otorrhea, or increasing hearing loss usually only appear once the ossicular chain has already been damaged5,6. Patients with cholesteatoma have to be treated with surgery7. Although the standard procedure for mastoidectomy has been shown to be effective, it can be associated with complications such as hearing loss, delayed wound healing, facial nerve paralysis, vertigo, loss of taste, and an increased risk of infection1,8,9. Depending on the clinical situation, mastoidectomy can be performed with or without creation of a radical cavity. The invasive growth of cholesteatoma and the associated destruction of bone structures in the middle ear is associated with inflammation and immunological processes and represent a considerable challenge in otology4,10,11. Given the delicate anatomy of the middle ear, particular attention must be paid to the small dimensions of the structures that need to be preserved. The stapes typically measures \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 2.5-4 mm$$\end{document} in height and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 1.9-3 mm$$\end{document} in width at the footplate, whereas the fine structures of the posterior and anterior crus measure between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.3$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.8 mm$$\end{document}12–17. The width of the incus is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 2.1-5 mm$$\end{document}12,13,18,19. The head of the malleus has a width of approx. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1.1-2.5 mm$$\end{document} and at the neck it has a width of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5-1.9 mm$$\end{document}12,13,20. This range (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx 300 \mu m \mathrm{to} 5 mm$$\end{document}) reflects both the fine structural thicknesses and the overall size of individual components within the middle ear. Surgeons need to perform their procedures with precise accuracy because the ossicular chain has fragile structures. The effective removal of diseased tissue using laser-based techniques shows good potential, as these methods enable controlled tissue removal with minimal damage to the surrounding healthy tissue and the delicate middle ear ossicles. Preservation of the ossicular chain has been shown to be associated with improved postoperative hearing outcomes, and laser-assisted techniques may facilitate this by enabling precise tissue removal21–23.

As a result, minimally invasive procedures are becoming increasingly important. In general, laser technology offers promising possibilities in this context, as it enables the efficient and gentle removal of diseased tissue22,24,25. Lasers used in ENT surgery range in wavelength from the infrared (CO2-laser: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10.6 \mu m$$\end{document}; Er:YAG laser: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2.94 \mu m$$\end{document}) to the visible region (Argon: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$514 nm$$\end{document}, Nd:YAG laser frequency doupled with a KTP crystal: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$532 nm$$\end{document})22,26. With an absorption coefficient of water of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\ge 80 {mm}^{-1}$$\end{document}, the CO2 laser radiation rapidly vaporizes tissue and coagulates small blood vessels simultaneously in a very thin zone. However, for guiding \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10.6 \mu m$$\end{document} light for endoscopic surgeries, hollow-core fibers have to be used that are less flexible and more expensive than standard fibers26. Also, for hemostasis in vascular tissue, a slightly greater penetration depth and high absorption specificially in hemoglobin is beneficial. In cholesteatoma surgery, the KTP laser with an absorption coefficient of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.04 {mm}^{-1}$$\end{document} in water and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$21-23 {mm}^{-1}$$\end{document} in blood (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$150 g$$\end{document} HbO2/Hb per litre) is preferred so far26–28. Since some years, diode lasers with a wavelength of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} are available with a higher absorption coefficient in hemoglobin and therefore an excellent ability of coagulation with a limited depth of penetration during surgical inciscions (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mu }_{A}\left(blood\right)=43-195 {mm}^{-1}$$\end{document})24,28,29. Compared to other photoangiolytic lasers like the KTP laser (and pulsed dye lasers), the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} blue laser is, in addition to the increased absorption in hemoglobin, utilizing an enormous technical simplification by pumping the laser with a semiconductor (diode), thus avoiding complex technical constructions and maintenance services30. Combined with its ability in noncontact cutting and availability in flexible fibers, it is ideal for endoscopic, single-handed surgeries, allowing the laser to reach and obliterate disease at angled anatomical sites31. The \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser was shown to be versatile and effective in other subspecialties of otorhinolaryngology32,33 and Tam et al. showed in their retrospective cohort study that its use is safe in cholesteatoma surgery31: After partial microsurgical removal, the 445 nm diode laser can be used in clinical practice to specifically eliminate any remaining cholesteatoma tissue.

The aim of this work is to investigate the use of a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser in single-pulse mode for the ablation of cholesteatoma tissue while preserving the ossicular structures. Pig ear cartilage was used as a tissue model to investigate ablation with single pulses of a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser, porcine tissue represents a well-established experimental model with comparable optical properties34–36. For example, the absorption coefficient of human cholesteatoma at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} (average value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 3{cm}^{-1}$$\end{document}) lies near the range reported for soft tissues such as subcutaneous adipose tissue (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mu }_{a}\approx 1.8 {cm}^{-1}$$\end{document}), and similar values have been reported for the reduced scattering coefficient (cholesteatoma: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${{\mu }^\prime}_{s}\approx 4-7 {mm}^{-1}$$\end{document}, subcutaneous adipose tissue: µ's~1.4mm-1)37,54. To simulate clinically realistic conditions, the samples were covered with a thin layer of blood, and single pulses, partly in overlapping sequences, were applied to replicate surgical resection paths. The pulse duration and power range were selected based on previous pulsed-laser studies and the concept of thermal relaxation time, aiming to limit heat diffusion and confine the effect to superficial tissue layers24,38,39. The main objective is to determine parameter values which support both safe operation and reliable application results. The analysis of the influence of laser power, fiber distance, angle of incidence, and pulse overlap should show how these parameters determine the ablation behavior of the tissue. In addition, samples of human cholesteatoma and middle ear ossicles were used to test the transferability of the results to clinical conditions. In the long term, the findings may contribute to improving the precision of laser-assisted procedures, potentially reducing the risk of thermal or mechanical damage to the ossicles, and thus may supporting the preservation of hearing.

Discussion

As already mentioned in the introduction, ENT surgeons can choose from a range of lasers with different wavelengths and pulse characteristics. The \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser, due to the high absorption in hemoglobin and melanin, is expected to have good photoangiolytic properties and reduce intraoperative bleeding and scarring40. While differentiation from CO2 lasers is relatively clear due to the significant difference in absorption in water and hemoglobin, the question arises as to how the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} wavelength compares to “green” lasers such as the KTP laser with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$532 nm$$\end{document}.

Several studies report that the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} laser has similar, but superior properties to KTP laser, including a better hemostatic effect, minimal water absorption hence minimizing injuries to the inner ear, and higher tissue absorption rate, which allows operating at lower power hence less thermal injuries31. Actually, there are studies showing an advantage of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} laser compared to the KTP laser in laryngology40. Lin et al. e.g. found that the ‘blue laser’ resulted in less vocal fold scarring compared to the KTP laser in normal rat vocal folds41. Balouch et al. conducted a study on 31 subjects diagnosed with vascular lesions of the vocal folds, in which the blue laser was used in pulsed mode at low power densities (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$3-4 W$$\end{document}). They found that it was less effective in preventing recurrence of vascular lesions on the vocal folds compared to the KTP laser, However, postoperative edema and haemorrhage were less severe in patients treated with the blue laser, indicating reduced thermal trauma40,42. In contrast, Hanke et al. performed an experimental study with gingiva tissue with diode lasers ranging from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$400$$\end{document} to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1500 nm$$\end{document} and found a higher cutting efficiency for \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} compared to the near-infrared range (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$810 nm, 980 nm, 1064 nm$$\end{document}), but not to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$514 nm \ and \ 532 nm$$\end{document}24. For cholesteatoma surgery, another aspect of 445 nm radiation might be beneficial: blue light has been utilized in other areas of medicine for bacterial decontamination43,44. However, Tam et al. found no reduction in residual rate in a study including 120 cholesteatoma cases (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n=47$$\end{document} surgeries using the laser)31. Further studies are ongoing.

Based on these considerations, this research demonstrates the high potential of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser for precise ablation in the middle ear region. With single laser pulses, small tissue volumes with a thickness \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$< 500 \mu m$$\end{document} can be removed. Cholesteatoma tissue with a thin blood layer was effectively ablated even at low power levels, whereas the compact structures of the ossicles showed a higher ablation threshold (Fig. 6). These differences in ablation behavior confirm the possibility of targeted removal of pathological areas while preserving functionally relevant structures.

The ablation depth can be controlled by various parameters, such as power, fiber spacing, and beam angle. This has also been confirmed in other studies. Braun et al. demonstrated that a wavelength of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} in soft tissue enables high cutting performance with minimal thermal damage to the surrounding areas39. With a steady-state ablation model (see e.g.38), neglecting heat diffusion or plume shielding, the ablation depth is given by\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d= \frac{1}{{\mu }_{a}} ln\left(\frac{F}{{F}_{th}}\right)$$\end{document}with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mu }_{a}$$\end{document} being the effective absorption coefficient, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F$$\end{document} the fluence and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${F}_{th}$$\end{document} the threshold fluence. Using a Taylor approximation of the logarithm, the depth thus follows a linear relationship with power when this model is valid. Hanke et al. showed a nearly linear relationship between power and incision depth for different diode wavelengths (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5-4 W$$\end{document}, continuous wave (cw) operation), which corresponds to our single-pulse measurements24. The depth values we measured with single \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$100 ms$$\end{document} pulses are also consistent with a linear dependence – the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${R}^{2}$$\end{document} value for the fit on the human cholesteatoma samples is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.75$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.7$$\end{document} for porcine tissue. The high intersection point of the adjustment curve for the pig data with the y-axis is noteworthy, as an intersection point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y\le 0$$\end{document} is expected for a threshold process such as thermal ablation. This will be investigated further. However, it should be noted that there is considerable variation in the values. In human cholesteatoma samples, this is already due to the considerable variation in tissue type and blood content in the samples. The homogeneity is significantly higher in animal tissue samples. Here, inaccuracies in fiber positioning, possible contamination of the fiber during pulse emission, and varying onset of ablation of weakly absorbing tissue (low blood content) contribute to the variance. Nevertheless, at a distance of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1 mm$$\end{document} and medium power levels, the values all stay within a range of a few hundred micrometers, without macroscopic damage to the cartilage.

Even at maximum power, the depths remained below the critical dimensions of delicate ear structures at around \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$350-370 \mu m$$\end{document} (Fig. 2a). This clearly indicates that a single pulse would not cut through the ossicles. These dimensions are surgically relevant, as the anatomical vulnerability points of the ossicles are of a similar order of size. The total height of the stapes is typically \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 2.5-4 mm$$\end{document}, with the crus thickness at its narrowest point being \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 0.3-0.8 mm$$\end{document}12–17. The widths of the malleus and incus are in the millimeter range12,13,18–20. Ablation depths below \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$300 \mu m$$\end{document} are therefore surgically advantageous, as they allow very precise tissue removal with less than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5 mm$$\end{document} ablation depth per pulse and ensure that a single pulse does not completely transect the ossicles. In all cases, histological examination of the underlying cartilage revealed no structural damage. The procedure thus demonstrated its effectiveness as a method for removing cholesteatoma tissue while preserving the ossicular chain, which could be confirmed in a clinical study using the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} wavelength laser45.

The distance between the fiber and the tissue has a strong influence on ablation. As the distance increases, the spot diameter increases, the energy density decreases, and the ablation depth decreases30. The ablation width in porcine cartilage with remaining tissue reached \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$450 \mu m$$\end{document} at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5 mm$$\end{document} distance but expanded to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$920 \mu m$$\end{document} at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$5 mm$$\end{document} distance when using \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4 W$$\end{document} power. At low power (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5-1 W$$\end{document}), the ablation width even decreased with increasing distance. The results confirm a quadratic correlation between ablation depth and distance30. In clinical practice, distances of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5-2 mm$$\end{document} are common, so the aim is to achieve precise ablation with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$100 ms$$\end{document} single pulses. Under these realistic conditions during manual fiber guidance, small deviations in distance and angle are inevitable and lead to slight variations in the measured ablation depth, typically in the range of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$50-100 \mu m$$\end{document}.

The small differences in ablation depth occur because of minimal variations in local energy delivery but remain within an acceptable range. The laser power requires precise optimization based on tissue structure because both uneven surfaces and manual guidance methods impact the achieved results. Nevertheless, this indicates that controlled tissue removal can still be achieved even under manual handling. In addition, the complex geometry and curved surfaces of middle ear structures such as the ossicles make it difficult to maintain a constant irradiation angle, which may further influence the local fluence and thus the ablation depth. The angle of incidence also plays a decisive role. If the fiber is held at an angle to the surface, an elliptical laser spot is created. Flatter angles result in a larger irradiated area and thus lower energy density46. Our data show that at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$45^\circ$$\end{document} degree and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4 W$$\end{document}, the deepest ablation was at the point of the elliptical crater closest to the fiber end, where the intensity of the Gaussian beam is highest47. It can be assumed that angles between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$45^\circ$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$70^\circ$$\end{document} are used in clinical practice. Measurements of the ablation depth in porcine cartilage with residual tissue at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2 W$$\end{document} ranged between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$170$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$270 \mu m$$\end{document}. Irregular surfaces and manual guidance can lead to variations, although these remained within the safe range.

Letting the laser spots overlap \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$26-42 \%$$\end{document}, uniform resection paths could be created without enlarging the damage zones in the peripheral area. The OCT data showed that ablation depth stayed constant between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$260$$\end{document} to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$370 \mu m$$\end{document} in all examined residual tissue thicknesses. In addition, the ablation depth can be controlled via the pulse energy. Thicker tissue layers can be removed step by step, which is an important aspect in avoiding complications. The \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} laser therefore can be used for tissue resections without damaging ossicular structures.

In this study, OCT was primarily used to quantify the ablation depth. However, OCT has increasingly been investigated as an imaging modality for middle ear structures. Several studies have shown that OCT can be used to visualize middle ear structures and may help to distinguish cholesteatoma from surrounding tissue48–51. These findings suggest that OCT may not only serve as a quantitative tool but also has potential for intraoperative assessment of middle ear pathology.

The amount of control over the depth of ablation does not necessarily correlate with clinical “safe” versus “unsafe” threshold values. Available literature provides mainly qualitative recommendations rather than clearly defined limits. For example, Yau et al. state in their paper on endoscopic cholesteatoma surgery using a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$532 nm$$\end{document} laser that, in their experience, the laser should not be focused directly on the bone for an extended period, as this can result in bone injury. They also note that lasering should be stopped immediately once charring of the bone is visible52. However, carbonization already indicates at least superficial damage to the bone. The authors also mention that a perilymphatic fistula may occur when the stapes footplate is lasered. They suggest first removing gross disease with instruments, followed by the application of a defocused laser to any residual tissue. The laser was most frequently used to ablate cholesteatoma from the stapes superstructure. Tam et al. note that, in cholesteatoma surgeries using the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser, they took care to avoid applying the laser to vulnerable structures, including the facial nerve, the oval and round windows, and the dehiscent semicircular canals31. In addition, there are many tissue characteristics that can vary from patient to patient, and can significantly affect the surgical treatment (e.g., thickness of the bony specimen, inflammation, vascularization).

In CW mode, heat input accumulates due to continuous energy delivery, whereas in single-pulse mode, the energy is delivered for a short time and the tissue is able to cool down in between pulses. If the pulse duration is within the range of the thermal relaxation time, the effect is limited to the superficial layers53. Studies on gingiva showed significantly broader and deeper thermal influence zones in CW mode, which further supports the use of single-pulse mode for working close to bone24.

Blood absorbs strongly in the range of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document}. Wisotzky et al. showed that the absorption properties of cholesteatoma change significantly when blood is applied, since a characteristic hemoglobin peak occurs between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$430 nm$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$555 nm$$\end{document}37. In our experiments, porcine blood was applied to all samples except the human ossicles. A thin blood layer on the tissue surface can enhance energy uptake at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} due to these hemoglobin peaks, supporting effective soft-tissue ablation25. In our experiments, porcine blood was applied to all samples except the human ossicles, allowing a direct comparison between blood-covered and non-covered tissue. The results demonstrate the ability to reliably estimate the ablation properties at this wavelength for both the tissue and the surrounding structures. Human cholesteatoma could already be removed at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1 W$$\end{document}, a power at which the ossicles showed no alterations. This can be explained by structural differences, as mineralized bone has a higher ablation threshold, and a lower attenuation, resulting in the laser energy being deposited in a larger volume. Nevertheless, some superficial ablations were also observed at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4 W$$\end{document}.

Regarding our results, the recommended clinical approach to support optimal ablation is to start with vascularized tissue or apply a thin layer of blood to the perimatrix of the cholesteatoma. The \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser enables precise ablation in single-pulse mode by selecting the optimal power levels, distances and irradiation angles. It is a promising instrument for the controlled ablation of cholesteatoma tissue while protecting sensitive middle ear structures.

Conclusion

Single \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser pulses enable precise ablation of cholesteatoma tissue while protecting the ossicular structures. The procedure therefore offers great potential for clinical use as a hearing-preserving supplement in cholesteatoma surgery.

Materials and methods

Preparation and characterization of porcine cartilage models

Cartilage samples from the external ear of pigs (slaughterhouse material) were used for the experimental investigations. The tissue was obtained commercially by the Medical Laser Center Lübeck from a slaughterhouse licensed according to European Union regulations (EC No. 853/2004 and EU 2017/625). The samples were transported to the laboratory in a cooled container and stored in a refrigerator at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4^\circ C$$\end{document} until further processing for a maximum of 3 h, with all experiments performed on the same day to ensure comparable tissue conditions.

After cleaning the auricle thoroughly under running water, the remaining auricle was cut into strips approximately \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1 cm$$\end{document} wide, avoiding all cartilage ridges. The superficial skin and fat layers were then removed manually with a scalpel (Swann-Morton, UK) and dissecting scissors (Hammacher, Germany). A thin layer of residual soft tissue remained on the cartilage surface. This served as a reference tissue model for the cholesteatoma that can form over the cartilage. The exposed cartilage tissue was removed with tweezers (Fuhrmann, Germany) and cut into square samples with an edge length of approximately \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1 cm$$\end{document}. The finished samples were transferred to sterile 6-well plates (Greiner Bio-One, Austria) and stored there in Ringer’s solution until the experiment was carried out.

Human tissue samples were taken during clinical operations at the Department of Otolaryngology at the University Medical Center Schleswig–Holstein, Lübeck Campus. Ablation experiments

Tissue ablation was performed using a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$445 nm$$\end{document} diode laser (Fox IV, A.R.C.) with a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$300 \mu m$$\end{document} core diameter optical fiber (HighFlex, A.R.C., numerical aperture 0.22). This laser can be operated in a mode in which individual pulses (in our tests: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$100 ms$$\end{document}) can be triggered via the foot switch.

Before each ablation was performed, it was necessary to apply a small amount of pig blood to the samples. This also helped to maintain a moist surface and reduce potential drying effects during the measurements. Tissue samples from both porcine cartilage and human tissue were used. The blood was applied using a thin brush, and the surface of the sample was dried with blotting paper before application to ensure the smallest possible and optimal blood layer height25. This enabled reproducible carbonization and simulated realistic surgical scenarios, which in turn allowed the volume removal and ablation depth to be measured. An image was then captured using a camera attachment (EOS 550D, Canon, Japan) on a microscope (Stemi SV8, Zeiss, Germany). The sample was then positioned on an adjustable sample table located below the fiber. The fiber was attached to a robotic arm (Rotrics DexArm, Rotrics, USA). The LM-10 thermopile sensor from Coherent USA measured power at the fiber end before each ablation test to confirm that test conditions remained constant.

Evaluation of ablation craters and tissue damage

Optical coherence tomography (OCT, see section "Preparation and characterization of porcine cartilage models") was used to measure the depth and width of the laser ablation craters. The lateral resolution is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$8 \mu m$$\end{document}, the axial resolution \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7.3-8.5 \mu m$$\end{document}. Cross-sectional B-scans were analyzed to determine the distance between the crater edges and the deepest point. Each measurement was repeated three times to minimize error.

After OCT evaluation, the tissue samples were embedded in Tissue-Tek Cryomold molds (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$25 \times 20 \times 5 mm$$\end{document}) using Tissue Freezing Medium (Leica), frozen at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$-80^\circ C$$\end{document}, and then cut into \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$20 \mu m$$\end{document} thick sections using a 2800 Frigocut cryostat (Reichert-Jung). Sections were taken at different positions along the laser spot and stained with hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) according to a modified Carl Roth Rapid Staining Kit protocol.

Stained sections were examined under an inverted microscope (Nikon) at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4x$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10x$$\end{document} magnification. Both overall images and detailed views of the ablated areas were analyzed, and the open-source software Fiji was used for quantitative evaluation of the damaged areas.

Telomere length in patients with non-functional adrenal incidentalomas
Authors: Nilufer Ozdemir, Seda Sabah Ozcan, Ayse Ece Turkmen, Sedat Can Guney, Zeliha Hekimsoy, Mehmet Korkmaz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Telomeres maintain genomic integrity during cell replication by preventing chromosomal fusions. Beside genetic influences, telomere length is affected by environmental factors such as oxidative stress and inflammation. These mechanisms also contribute to metabolic syndrome components linked to cellular aging. We aim to evaluate whether telomere length is shortened in patients with non-functional adrenal incidentaloma (NFAI) compared to the control group. This study was designed as a prospective, single-center study. The total of 88 participants included were 44 patients aged between 40 and 60 years with NFAI in our endocrinology clinic and 44 control subjects. An Absolute Human Telomere Lengths Quantification qPCR Assay kit (Nucleotestbio, Budapest, Hungary) was used for analyses. There was no significant difference between the NFAI and control groups regarding age and sex distribution. Telomere length was significantly shorter in the NFAI group (NFAI group: 3.680 ± 1.970 kb; control group: 4.469 ± 1.672 kb; p = 0.046). While no significant difference was found in telomere lengths in subgroup analyses, patients with basal adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) levels <15 pg/mL had significantly shorter telomeres than those with basal ACTH levels ≥15 pg/mL (p = 0.034). A strong positive correlation was observed only between telomere length and ACTH level (p = 0.001). This study demonstrated that telomere length is significantly shortened in NFAI patients. Here, we propose that the underlying cause of telomere length shortening in the NFAI group may be related to increased cardiovascular risk and an elevated inflammatory state, even in the presence of cortisol levels within the normal range.

Introduction

The term adrenal incidentaloma refers to an adrenal mass that is incidentally discovered during routine imaging performed for unrelated complaints or without any clinical or physical findings related to the adrenal gland [1]. These are often benign, non-functioning, asymptomatic masses. With the widespread use of abdominal imaging techniques, the prevalence of adrenal incidentalomas has increased [2]. The clinical significance of adrenal masses lies in their potential to secrete hormones, which can pose cardiovascular and/or metabolic risks. Most adrenal tumors are considered non-functional and therefore not associated with major health risks [3].

Between a wide range of 5–47% of adrenal incidentalomas may exhibit mild autonomous cortisol hypersecretion without the clinical manifestations of Cushing’s syndrome, a condition known as subclinical autonomous glucocorticoid hypersecretion (Subclinical Cushing’s Syndrome) [4]. The 2023 European Society of Endocrinology (ESE) and the European Network for the Study of Adrenal Tumours (ENSAT) recommend the term mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS) for this clinical state [5]. Several studies have demonstrated an association between non-functioning adrenal tumors (NFATs) and cardiometabolic diseases [4, 6, 7].

Telomeres are non-coding, specialized deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) repeat sequences located at the ends of eukaryotic chromosomes, running from the 5' end to the 3' end of the DNA strand. Telomeric DNA regions are heterochromatic and typically span approximately 10–15 kilobases [8]. Telomeres play a crucial role in maintaining genetic integrity and chromosomal stability by preventing end-to-end chromosomal fusions during cell replication [9]. Telomeres shorten with each mitotic cycle, and the most significant factors influencing telomere length are oxidative stress and inflammation [10, 11]. Telomere length may serve as a biomarker reflecting the cumulative burden of oxidative stress and inflammation throughout a person’s life. In human somatic cells, telomere length ranges between 5–15 kilobases, with an average annual shortening of approximately 30 base pairs. When telomere length falls below 2.8 kilobases, cellular sensitivity to senescence and apoptosis increases [12]. It should be noted that the 5–15 kilobase range reported in the literature is primarily derived from terminal restriction fragment (TRF) analysis, which measures both telomeric and subtelomeric DNA regions. In contrast, quantitative PCR (qPCR)-based methods estimate telomeric repeat content relative to a single-copy gene and may yield numerically different absolute values due to methodological variability [13]. Telomere shortening progresses with age, eventually triggering replicative senescence and apoptosis [14]. As such, telomere length is recognized as a critical biomarker of biological aging [15].

Studies in the literature suggest that individuals with non-functioning adrenal incidentalomas have higher insulin resistance and endothelial dysfunction compared to control groups, potentially increasing the risk of cardiovascular events and mortality [16].

Components of metabolic syndrome, including abdominal obesity, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and atherosclerosis, are known to be associated with cellular aging-related pathologies. Given the frequent presence of these metabolic syndrome components in patients with non-functioning adrenal incidentalomas, this study aims to determine whether telomere length is more significantly shortened in these patients compared to control subjects.

Materials and Methods

Participants in the Study and Procedure

This study was designed as a prospective, single-center study. The total of 88 participants included were 44 patients aged between 40 and 60 years with non-functional adrenal incidentaloma (NFAI) who presented to the Endocrinology and Metabolic Diseases outpatient clinics of Manisa Celal Bayar University Faculty of Medicine Hospital, and 44 control subjects (patients without adrenal lesions).

Our study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Manisa Celal Bayar University (Approval number 20.478.486.1078). This study was supported by The Manisa Celal Bayar University Scientific Research Projects Coordination Unit. Project Number: 2020-042.

Patients presenting to the Endocrinology outpatient clinic with incidentally discovered adrenal masses on imaging performed for unrelated indications were prospectively evaluated. Control subjects were chosen from individuals who had undergone abdominal computed tomography (CT) imaging for non-endocrine reasons and demonstrated completely normal adrenal glands. Indications for abdominal CT imaging in the control group included previous non-specific abdominal pain, nephrolithiasis evaluation, and trauma assessment. None of the control participants were evaluated for suspected endocrine disorders. NFAI was defined according to the 2023 ESE/ENSAT clinical practice guidelines. Non-functionality was verified using a 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test (DST) with post-DST cortisol ≤1.8 μg/dL, normal 24-hour urinary free cortisol levels, and the biochemical exclusion of pheochromocytoma and primary aldosteronism. Screening for primary aldosteronism was performed using the aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) measured by chemiluminescent immunoassay. An ARR cut-off value of >20 (plasma aldosterone concentration [ng/dL] to plasma renin activity [ng/mL/hour]) was used as the screening threshold for primary aldosteronism, in accordance with institutional standards. When the ARR was elevated, confirmatory testing was performed according to institutional protocols. Biochemical evaluation was conducted independently of imaging findings to ensure objective exclusion of autonomous aldosterone secretion.

Control participants were selected from the same source population and during the same time period as the NFAI cases. A frequency-matching strategy was employed to achieve comparable age and sex distributions between groups. Both NFAI patients and controls were included consecutively. Investigators responsible for enrollment were blinded to clinical outcomes and laboratory parameters at the time of recruitment to minimize selection bias and ensure comparability between groups.

Exclusion criteria included patients with incidentally discovered adrenal masses who were found to have functional tumors based on hormonal activity tests (e.g., MACS, Cushing’s syndrome, pheochromocytoma, hyperaldosteronism), individuals with a history of known adrenal or pituitary surgery, patients on steroid therapy, those with thyroid dysfunction, individuals with a history of malignancy and patients diagnosed with known coronary artery disease, chronic renal failure, or hepatic failure.

After informing participants and obtaining written informed consent, blood samples were collected in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) tubes for analysis of telomere length. Samples were stored at –80°C without centrifugation until the day of analysis.

DNA isolation

Genomic DNA used in the experiments was isolated using a commercial kit (PCR Template Kit, Roche). The quality and concentration of the obtained DNA were determined using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. DNA purity was assessed by evaluating the A260/A280 ratio.

Telomere Length

The Absolute Human Telomere Length Quantification qPCR Assay Kit (AHTLQ, Cat# 8918, ScienCell) was used according to the manufacturer’s protocol for measuring telomere length. The telomere primer set recognizes and amplifies telomere sequences. The single copy reference (SCR) primer set recognizes and amplifies a 100 bp long region on human chromosome 17 and serves as reference for data normalization. The reference genomic DNA sample with known telomere length serves as a reference for calculating the telomere length of target samples.

Relative telomere length was calculated using the comparative ΔΔCq method. Cq values were determined for both telomere and SCR regions, and ΔCq was calculated as follows:

Finally, absolute telomere length was determined by multiplying the relative telomere length by the known telomere length of the reference genomic DNA.

Estimated telomere length values in kilobases were calculated according to the conversion approach provided by the manufacturer of the commercial assay kit. These values represent relative estimations derived from the T/S ratio and kit-based calibration assumptions. They do not correspond to absolute telomere length as determined by terminal restriction fragment (TRF) analysis.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), version 22.0. The normality of variable distributions was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation, while non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (25th–75th percentile). For comparisons between groups, the independent samples t-test was used for normally distributed variables, and the Mann-Whitney U test was used for non-normally distributed variables. The chi-square test was applied for comparisons of categorical variables. Correlation analyses were performed using the Spearman correlation test.

Effect sizes were calculated to quantify the magnitude of differences between groups. Cohen’s d was computed for between-group comparisons, with values of 0.2, 0.5, and 0.8 interpreted as small, medium, and large effects, respectively. Mean differences with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were reported to provide clinically meaningful estimates of group differences.

Simple linear regression analysis was performed to evaluate basal adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) as a continuous predictor of telomere length within the NFAI group. Additionally, multivariable linear regression analysis was conducted to assess the independent association between NFAI status and telomere length after adjusting for potential confounders including age, sex, and body mass index (BMI). Unstandardized regression coefficients (B) with 95% CI and standardized coefficients (β) were reported.

Post-hoc power analysis was performed to evaluate the statistical power achieved with the observed effect size and sample size. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

When telomere lengths were evaluated, the NFAI group exhibited significantly shorter telomere lengths compared to the control group (NFAI group: 3.680 ± 1.970 kb; control group: 4.469 ± 1.672 kb; mean difference: –0.789 kb, 95% CI: –1.552 to –0.025; Cohen’s d = 0.43; p = 0.046) (Table 2).

Multivariable linear regression models were constructed to assess the independent association between group status (NFAI vs. control) and telomere length (Table 3). In the unadjusted model, NFAI status was significantly associated with shorter telomere length (β = –0.789, 95% CI: –1.555 to –0.023, p = 0.046). After adjustment for age and sex, the association remained significant with a similar effect estimate (β = –0.792, 95% CI: –1.541 to –0.043, p = 0.041). Additional adjustment for BMI yielded a slightly larger effect size, though the association became borderline significant (β = –0.856, 95% CI: –1.699 to –0.013, p = 0.050; n = 79). In the fully adjusted model including age, sex, BMI, hypertension, and 25-hydroxyvitamin D, the association was attenuated and no longer reached statistical significance (β = –0.943, 95% CI: –2.022 to 0.136, p = 0.093; n = 58), likely due to reduced sample size from missing covariate data. The direction and magnitude of the effect remained consistent across all models.

Discussion

This study was conducted to evaluate whether telomere length differs in patients with NFAI compared to the control group. Our findings demonstrated that telomere length was significantly shorter in NFAI patients compared to the control group, independent of age and sex (NFAI group: 3.680 ± 1.970 kb; Control group: 4.469 ± 1.672 kb; p = 0.046) (Table 2). To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate telomere dynamics in this patient population.

Telomere attrition is driven primarily by oxidative stress and chronic low-grade inflammation, both of which are recognized contributors to accelerated biological aging. Patients with NFAI have been shown to exhibit increased cardiometabolic risk, even when cortisol secretion is within the reference range [10, 11, 17]. In our study, hypertension was more prevalent in the NFAI group, and 25-hydroxyvitamin D levels were significantly lower—both factors that may promote oxidative damage and systemic inflammation, thereby contributing to telomere erosion. Although subgroup analyses did not reveal significant associations between telomere length and most cardiometabolic variables, the overall risk profile of NFAI patients is consistent with a pro-inflammatory and pro-oxidative state.

The effect of cortisol secretion from the adrenal gland on telomere length is a topic debated in the literature. While there is evidence that high cortisol levels may accelerate telomere shortening, conflicting results have also been reported [18, 19]. Some studies suggest that chronic stress leads to telomere shortening by excessively stimulating the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and increasing cortisol levels [20, 21].

The significant positive correlation between telomere length and basal ACTH levels provides further mechanistic insight. Lower ACTH levels in NFAI patients may reflect subtle, autonomous cortisol secretion or chronic suppression of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis. Even modest elevations of cortisol within the “normal” range have been linked to increased endothelial dysfunction, insulin resistance, and carotid intima-media thickness [17]. Cortisol excess also enhances oxidative stress by increasing reactive oxygen species production and by impairing antioxidant defense mechanisms [18, 19]. In a study, it was suggested that hypercortisolemic states of the adrenal gland could influence telomere dynamics and that this mechanism might be related to chronic stress [22]. Basal ACTH suppression may represent a more sensitive integrative marker of chronic subtle cortisol exposure than single-point cortisol measurements, which often remain within the reference range in NFAI patients. Therefore, ACTH levels may better reflect long-term hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis feedback dynamics. Chronic exposure to these processes can accelerate telomere attrition, which may be offering a plausible explanation for our finding of shorter telomeres in NFAI patients with lower ACTH levels.

Telomere length has emerged as an important biomarker in elucidating the biological behavior of various tumors. In this context, shortened telomeres were shown to be associated with increased chromosomal instability, cellular senescence, and malignant transformation potential. These findings suggest that telomere dynamics may not only reflect the current biological status of a tumor but could also provide predictive insight into its future behavior and progression [23]. Most adrenal masses, usually detected incidentally during imaging procedures, are benign tumors, most commonly non-functioning adrenal adenomas. Studies report that a large proportion of adrenal tumors are non-functioning, with prevalence rates ranging from 58.0% to 86.2%. Therefore, distinguishing these from functional tumors, which may have distinct clinical outcomes and management strategies, is essential [24].

Recent molecular studies have reported that telomerase is activated in most human cancers but is undetectable in most somatic cells. These findings suggest that telomerase activation is closely associated with the malignant potential of human tumors. In a study by Kinoshita et al., telomerase activity was investigated in adrenal cortex tumors and pheochromocytomas to determine its usefulness as a diagnostic marker. Increased telomerase activity was shown in one of thirteen adrenal cortex tumors and in two of seven pheochromocytomas, whereas no telomerase activity was detected in non-functioning adrenocortical tumors. These three telomerase-positive tumors displayed features consistent with malignant potential. Consequently, the study concluded that telomerase activity might be a potential marker for predicting the biological behavior of adrenal cortex tumors and pheochromocytomas [25].

Components of the metabolic syndrome, including abdominal obesity, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and atherosclerosis, are among the pathologies associated with increased cellular aging [26]. In our study, the presence of hypertension was found to be significantly higher in the NFAI group (p = 0.020). However, comparisons between the NFAI and control groups regarding hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, and smoking showed no significant differences in telomere length. This may indicate that telomere shortening is not a sole marker of disease but is associated with more complex biological processes.

This study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center study with a limited patient population, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings. Second, telomere length was assessed only in peripheral blood samples, and telomere dynamics in other tissues were not examined. In the future, multicenter prospective studies including larger patient populations and patients with MACS are needed. Furthermore, the sample size was determined using G*Power based on a two-independent-samples t-test, assuming a medium effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.60) in the absence of previously reported effect sizes, with α = 0.05 and a target power of 80%. Under these parameters, the required sample size was estimated to be approximately 44 participants per group. In our study, the observed standard deviation for telomere length was approximately 1.83 kb; therefore, the post-hoc power analysis indicated that the achieved statistical power was slightly lower than initially planned. In addition, smoking status was recorded categorically (current smoker vs. non-smoker) without quantification of cumulative exposure (e.g., pack-years). Given the relatively small number of smokers in each group, the study may have been underpowered to detect a significant association between smoking and telomere length. Finally, endocrinological parameters, including ACTH and cortisol levels, were not assessed in the control group because hormonal testing was not clinically indicated. Therefore, the relationship between HPA axis activity and telomere length could not be evaluated in controls, limiting mechanistic comparison between groups.

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that telomere length is significantly shortened in NFAI patients.

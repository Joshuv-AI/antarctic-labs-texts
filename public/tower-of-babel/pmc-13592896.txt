Preventive Effect of Protocatechuic Acid Ethyl Ester against Doxorubicin-Induced Toxicity in Cardiomyocytes
Authors: Leila Safaeian, Ali Zeinosalehin, Nastaran Rashidi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Administration of doxorubicin (DOX), an effective anticancer drug, is associated with cardiotoxicity. Protocatechuic acid ethyl ester (PCAE) is a natural phenolic acid derivative with antioxidant, iron-chelating capability and prolyl hydroxylase inhibitory effects. This in vitro investigation tested whether PCAE could protect against toxicity induced by doxorubicin in cardiomyocytes and compared its effects with those of protocatechuic acid (PCA).Methods:After evaluation of the cytotoxicity of PCAE (1–100 µM) and DOX (0.125–8 µM) on H9C2 cardiomyocytes, the cells were exposed to DOX (2 μM) following 24 h pretreatment with PCAE (1–100 µM). MTT assay was done to estimate the cytotoxicity or cell viability. The level of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) was examined by fluorescence assay. The ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) assay was performed as a measure of antioxidant capacity. Glutathione depletion assay was performed using 5,5-dithiobis-2-nitrobenzoic acid.Results:PCAE (1–100 µM) had no inhibitory effect on the survival rate of cardiomyocytes after 24-hour incubation. Pretreatment with PCAE at 10–100 μM significantly prevented cytotoxicity of DOX using MTT test. PCAE declined the ROS level (at 1–100 µM) and glutathione depletion (at 80 and 100 μM) and raised the FRAP level (at 20–100 μM). These protective and antioxidant effects were significantly more potent than those of PCA activities.Conclusions:PCAE showed antioxidant and cytoprotective activities against toxicities caused by DOX in cardiomyocytes more potent than those of PCA. PCAE may be a potential candidate for prevention and treatment of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity, which requires further in vivo and clinical researches.

Introduction

Doxorubicin (DOX) is an anthracycline-based drug that effectively inhibits metastasis in a variety of solid tumors, including breast cancer, sarcoma, leukemia and lymphoma.[1] Acute or cumulative dosages of this chemotherapeutic agent can put patients at a risk of cardiotoxicity.[23] DOX acts through damaging DNA, preventing the cell from producing macromolecules and generation of significant reactive oxygen species (ROS).[4] Cardiomyocytes are more sensitive to DOX toxicity because they have a large sarcoplasmic reticulum, a lot of mitochondria, and low levels of defensive enzymes like superoxide dismutase, glutathione peroxidase, and catalase.[56] DOX additionally stimulates dioxygenases such as prolyl hydroxylase, which targets hypoxia-inducible factors such as HIF-1α.[7] Reducing HIF-1α levels causes elevation of NF (nuclear factor)-ĸB expression which in consequence stimulates inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, and apoptosis.[8] It has been reported that DOX-induced cardiac apoptosis is partially caused by the increased production of prolyl hydroxylase, which interacts with Bcl-2.[9] By inhibiting prolyl hydroxylase, the iron chelator deferoxamine protects cardiomyocytes against ischemia/reperfusion and improves their potential to maintain contractile function.[10] Another study also reported that dexrazoxane as an agent used in the management of anthracycline-induced cardiotoxicity can reduce the adverse consequences of DOX on cardiomyocytes via activating HIF and HIF-related pathways.[11]

Various investigations have delved into the cardioprotective effects of antioxidants derived from natural sources.[12] Phenolic acids and their derivatives are a group of natural metabolites that are present in most plants. Phenolic acids are derived from benzoic acids and cinnamic acids.[13] Although their basic structure consists of a carboxylic acid substituent and a phenolic ring, the number of hydroxyl groups and their placement and other functional groups in the aromatic ring make a significant difference in the function of various derivative compounds. Phenolic acids and their derived compounds have the potential of various biological activities with strong antioxidant actions.[14]

Protocatechuic acid (PCA), also known as 3, 4-dihydroxybenzoic acid, is a natural phenolic acid. PCA and its derivatives have been widely studied for their various pharmacological effects.[15] PCA could function as a protective and chemopreventive agent in DOX-induced toxicity in cardiomyocytes by inhibiting oxidative stress and cell death and reducing the inflammatory pathway.[16] One of the PCA derivatives is protocatechuic acid ethyl ester (PCAE), also known as ethyl 3,4-dihydroxybenzoate [Figure 1], which is naturally found in some plant sources such as in peanut seed testa.[17] Addition of alkyl chains to PCA improves its lipophilicity and antioxidant efficacy.[1819] It has proven that PCAE has antioxidative actions and inhibits lipoxygenase.[2021] New evidence suggests that PCAE as an analog of the alpha-ketoglutarate substrate can inhibit the propyl hydroxylase, which reduces collagen synthesis and fibrosis and prevents breast cancer metastasis.[22] Moreover, the iron-chelating and prolyl hydroxylase inhibitory activities have been proven for PCAE, which suggest its potential protective effects on cardiotoxicity.[23] Therefore, this in vitro investigation tested whether PCAE could protect against toxicity induced by doxorubicin in cardiomyocytes and compared its effects with PCA.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals and reagents

DOX was obtained from EBEWE Pharma GmbH Nfg KG Co. (Austria). PCAE was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Co. (USA) and PCA from Cayman Chemical Co. (USA). DMEM/F-12 (Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium/Nutrient Mixture F-12) was attained from BioIdea Co. (Iran), and FBS (fetal bovine serum) and MTT (3-[4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl]-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) assay kit from AnaCell Co. (Iran). The commercial kit for determination of ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) was prepared from Hakiman Pajooh Co. (Iran). The kits for assessment of intracellular ROS and free thiol content were purchased from Kiazist (Iran). All other chemicals were from Sigma-Aldrich Co. (USA).

Cell culture

Rat H9C2 cardiomyocytes (Iran Cell Bank) were grown in a medium of DMEM/F-12 containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were incubated in a humidified atmosphere at 37°C with 95% air and 5% CO2.

MTT assay

Cell viability was evaluated using MTT kit after exposure of H9C2 cardiomyocytes to DOX PCAE or PCA. In summary, the cells were cultured at a density of 1 × 105 cells in every well of a 96-well plate and incubated for 24h. Then, cardiomyocytes were incubated with PCAE (1–100 µM) for assessment of its possible cytotoxic effect or with DOX (0.125–8 µM) for estimation of the IC50 value separately. The concentrations of PCAE were chosen based on the previous study.[20] After 24 h incubation, the cells were exposed to MTT reagent for 3h. Then dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was added and absorbance was assessed at 570 nm using a microplate reader/spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments, USA).[16]

To assess the effect of PCAE in the protection of cardiomyocytes against DOX injuries and to compare it with PCA, H9C2 cells were pretreated with PCAE (1–100 µM) or PCA (100 µM) for 24 h. Since prophylactic strategies are now clinically advised for preventing cardiotoxicity from chemotherapy, we used pretreatment of cardiomyocytes with PCAE in the current in vitro study. After changing the medium and washing out with PBS, cells were incubated with DOX (2 µM) for another 24 h. Finally, MTT assay was performed as above. The cells were only treated with PBS considered as the negative control. Experiments were executed in triplicate with three times repetition. The viability of cardiomyocytes was estimated by the following formula:

Cell viability (%) = (OD test - OD blank/OD negative control - OD blank) × 100

Intracellular ROS assay

The intracellular ROS level was examined by determining fluorescence changes via 2’,7’-dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFDA) using a commercial kit. This agent easily penetrates into the living cells and produces fluorescence after being oxidized by ROS.[24]

Pretreated cardiomyocytes with PCAE or PCA were exposed to DOX for 24 h, then washed with kit buffer, and incubated with DCFDA (10 µM) at 37°C for 45 min under dark conditions. The fluorescence was detected with a microplate reader/fluorescence spectrophotometer (Synergy H1 Hybrid Multi-Mode; Bio-Tek Instruments, USA) at excitation/emission wavelengths of 485nm/535nm. ROS levels were stated as fold change compared to the control.

FRAP assay:The FRAP (ferric reducing antioxidant power) assay as a measure of antioxidant capacity was calculated based on the FRAP (ferric reducing antioxidant power) test using a commercial kit and a standard curve of FeSO4 concentrations. After pretreating H9C2 cells with PCAE or PCA and subsequently incubating them with DOX, the supernatant from each well was mixed with FRAP reagent at 37°C for 40 min. The absorbance was then detected at 570 nm using a microplate reader/spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments, USA).[25]

Glutathione (GSH) depletion assay

GSH content in the cell’s supernatant was measured using a commercial kit which detects the free thiol groups based on the reaction of Ellman’s solution, with free and reduced sulfhydryl (SH) groups to produce a yellow color. The procedure was carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and the absorbance was measured at 402 nm using the microplate reader spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments, USA).[24]

Statistical analysis

Results were reported as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) and statistically evaluated by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Tukey post hoc test via SPSS software (version 25). A P value of less than 0.05 was considered as significant difference.

Ethical statement

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUI.RESEARCH.REC.1402.132) on July 3, 2023.

Results

Effects of PCAE and DOX on H9C2 cells viability

At the beginning, the possible toxicity of PCAE toward H9C2 cells was evaluated based on the MTT assay. As seen in Figure 2, there was no inhibitory impact on the survival rate of cardiomyocytes after 24-hour exposure to the concentrations of 1–100 μM of PCAE.

Viability percentage of H9C2 cells evaluated by MTT assay after 24 h incubation with different concentrations of protocatechuic acid ethyl ester (PCAE). Data represent mean ± SEM (n = 3)

The cytotoxic activity of DOX was assessed at the concentration that caused 50% inhibitory effect (IC50) on the cardiomyocyte’s survival. The IC50 value of DOX was calculated as 2.291 μM after 24 h incubation. Therefore, 2 μM DOX was used in the subsequent experiments [Figure 3].

Viability percentage of H9C2 cells evaluated by MTT assay after 24 h incubation with different concentrations of doxorubicin (0.125–8 µM). Data represent mean ± SEM (n = 3)

Effect of PCAE against DOX-induced cytotoxicity

The pretreatment of cells with PCAE at 10–100 μM concentrations could notably protect against cytotoxicity caused by 24 h incubation with DOX (2 µM) in MTT assay. Evaluation of the effect of PCA showed its significant protective activity in cardiomyocyte cells after pretreatment at 40–100 μM concentrations [Figure 4].

Effect of PCAE on intracellular ROS

Figure 5 displays the impact of PCAE on intracellular ROS concentration in cardiomyocytes after 24 h exposure to DOX. The ROS level was importantly raised after incubation of H9C2 cells with DOX when compared to the untreated cells (P < 0.001). Pretreatment of cells with PCAE profoundly declined the amounts of intracellular ROS at all concentrations (1–100 µM) (P < 0.001). PCA also considerably decreased ROS concentration at 100 μM (P < 0.001). However, there was a significant difference between the ROS reducing effects of PCAE versus PCA at similar concentrations (P < 0.05).

Effect of PCAE on total antioxidant capacity

Incubation of cardiomyocytes with DOX caused a notable reduction in FRAP value (P < 0.01). Pretreatment with PCAE at 20–100 μM concentrations remarkably increased total antioxidant capacity in H9C2 cells (P < 0.01 to P< 0.001). PCA likewise significantly elevated FRAP value at 100 μM concentration (P < 0.001). However, PCAE at the concentration of 100 μM more effectively caused an increase in FRAP level compared to the 100 μM PCA (P < 0.05) [Figure 6].

Effect of PCAE on GSH depletion

As seen Figure 7, exposure of cardiomyocyte cells with DOX resulted in an enhanced depletion of GSH from the cells relative to the control group (P < 0.001). Incubation with PCAE at the concentrations of 80 and 100 μM significantly reduced the GSH depletion induced by DOX (P < 0.05). PCA at the concentration of 100 μM did not have a significant effect on reducing GSH depletion from cardiomyocytes.

Discussion

Recently, the beneficial protective influence of natural ingredients and antioxidants has been demonstrated against cardiotoxicity induced by DOX.[26] In this investigation, we assessed the effect of PCEA on cardiomyocytes toxicity caused by DOX in vitro. Our result showed that PCAE has no inhibitory impact on the survival rate of rat cardiomyocytes after 24-hour exposure to the concentrations of 1–100 μM. Very limited cellular studies have been conducted on PCAE. In the study of Dare et al., (2020)[20] L929 fibroblast as the normal murine cell line was incubated with 0.1 to 250 μM of PCAE for 24 h. Their results showed the inhibitory effect of PCAE on the viability of L929 fibroblast at 50 μM and 250 μM (10.9% and 26.1%, respectively) via neutral red assay.

In our study, the IC50 value of DOX was calculated as 2.291 μM after 24 h incubation in H9C2 cardiomyocytes. Several researches have reported the concentration of 1 μM as the IC50level for DOX.[2728] Chang et al. (2019)[29] have found that the 1 μM concentration of DOX inhibited the H9C2 growth by 60%. Another study conducted by Guenancia and co-workers presented that DOX at 2 and 10 μM resulted in 20% and 60% death of H9C2 cardiomyocytes, respectively.[30] According to our findings, PCAE protected H9C2 cells against cytotoxicity of DOX by increasing the survival rate of cardiomyocytes at the concentrations of 10–100 μM. This protective effect of PCAE was more potent than that of PCA, which prohibited the DOX-induced cytotoxicity at 40–100 µM concentrations.

Cardiotoxicity of DOX happens through different mechanisms such as generation of ROS, mitochondrial dysfunction, disturbance in calcium homeostasis and in iron regulatory proteins, induction of prolyl hydroxylase enzyme, and subsequently activation of inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways.[931] In a previous in vitro study, PCA attenuated DOX-induced cardiotoxicity by inhibiting oxidative stress and cell death and decreasing TLR4 (toll-like receptor-4) expression as a stimulator of inflammatory pathways.[16]

PCAE as a derivative of PCA appears to have biological properties similar to its primary compound with better lipophilicity.[18] Moreover, some researches have shown that PCAE is a competitive inhibitor of prolyl 4-hydroxylase as well as a chelator of the iron-bound enzymes. Although the affinity of PCAE for ferric iron is lower than that of defroxamine, it is able to effectively reduce the cellular iron stores, possibly by activating a network of iron-responsive and regulatory proteins.[23] These mechanisms probably play the important role in the protective effects of PCAE in DOX-induced toxicity in cardiomyocytes.

Notably, phenolic acids are both chemo-preventive and also antitumor compounds. Antineoplastic and antimetastatic activities have been reported for PCA.[3233] Moreover, synergistic effects have been observed in coadministration of PCA with chemotherapeutic drugs.[26] Furthermore, the results of investigations have shown that esterification of phenolic acids can be used as a tool for enhancing their biological activities such as antimutagenicity, cytotoxicity, and anticancer effects.[34] In the current study, PCAE produced antioxidative activities in cardiomyocytes which was exposed to DOX through decreasing ROS level, enhancing total antioxidant capacity, and reducing GSH depletion. These antioxidant effects were significantly more potent than those of PCA activities. DOX-induced oxidative stress in cardiomyocytes is associated with the production of ROS, changes in iron metabolism, dysregulation of mitochondrial function, a decrease in intracellular GSH, and depletion of antioxidant enzymes which leads to the activation of the intracellular apoptosis signaling pathway.[3535] Strong antioxidant actions have been observed for PCA through the inhibition of various free radicals and NADPH oxidase, chelation of iron and other metal cations, and increasing activity of antioxidant enzymes.[3637] PCAE has also shown the potent antioxidant effects in some assays such as DPPH radical scavenging test and the ferric thiocyanate assay.[1738] The antioxidant activity of PCAE is attributed to the property of donating electrons to the free radicals and thereby neutralizing them. In addition, PCAE can chelate metal ions and therefore slow down the formation of ROS.[23] Comparative studies have shown that PCAE has more antioxidant activity than its parent compound PCA, mainly due to the increase in its lipophilicity.[2039] In the study of Dare et al.,[20] PCA and PCAE attenuated the oxidative damages caused by ultraviolet B (UVB) radiation and photoaging in L929 fibroblasts through inhibiting NF-ĸB and decreasing the expression of cyclooxygenase-2 and the photoaging-related proteins. Their results showed more antioxidant potency for PCAE than PCA because of its more lipophilicity.

The major limitation of our study was a lack of data that can support the molecular mechanisms and signaling pathways involved in the cyto-protection activities of PCAE.

Conclusions

In conclusion, PCAE prevented cytotoxicity-induced by DOX in cardiomyocyte through increasing cell survival and total antioxidant capacity, and reduction of intracellular ROS production and glutathione depletion. These antioxidants and cytoprotective activities of PCAE were more potent than those of PCA. PCAE may be a potential candidate for the prevention and treatment of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity, which requires further in vivo and clinical researches.

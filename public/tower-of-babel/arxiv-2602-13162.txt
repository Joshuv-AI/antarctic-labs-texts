New irreducible components of ℬ ⁡ ( 0 , c 2 ) \mathcal{B}(0,c_{2}) and Computation of the Dimension of its tangent space
Aislan Fontes, Maxwell Santos

Abstract
. We provide a Macaulay2 code for computing the dimension of the tangent space to ℬ ⁡ ( e , c 2 ) \mathcal{B}(e,c_{2}) in certain cases. Using this code, we identify components of ℬ ⁡ ( e , c 2 ) \mathcal{B}(e,c_{2}) containing singular points and compute the dimension of the irreducible component M 4 M_{4} of ℬ ⁡ ( − 1 , 6 ) \mathcal{B}(-1,6) , whose existence was proved in [ 7 ] . Furthermore, we prove the existence of infinite families of irreducible components of ℬ ⁡ ( 0 , c 2 ) \mathcal{B}(0,c_{2}) .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14D20, 14J60, 14F06

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{B}(e,c_{2})
denote the moduli space of stable rank 2 bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with Chern classes
c_{1}=e
and
c_{2}
. Up to normalizations, it is sufficient to consider
e=0,-1
and
c_{2}\geq 0
. In
[
11
]
, Hartshorne showed that
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
is a quasiprojective scheme and nonempty for
c_{2}\geq 1
while
\mathcal{B}(-1,c_{2})
is a quasiprojective scheme and nonempty for all even integer
c_{2}\geq 2
, where each irreducible component of
\mathcal{B}(e,c_{2})
has dimension at least
8c_{2}-3+2e
. It is worth recalling that, for
c_{2}\geq 2
, three types of irreducible components of the moduli space
\mathcal{B}(e,c_{2})
are known:

•

An infinite series
I_{c_{2}}
of irreducible component of expected dimension
8c_{2}-3+2e
c.f.
[
11
]
. If
e=0
then
I_{c_{2}}
is called
Instanton component
which is the closure
of the smooth open subset of
I_{c_{2}}
constituted by the Instanton vector bundles;

•

Irreducible components of the type
\mathcal{G}(t,r,s)
, so-called
Ein components
, depending on the triples of
integers
t,r,s
constructed by Ein
[
5
]
.

•

A new infinite series
\sum_{e}
of irreducible components
\mathcal{M}_{c_{2}}
of

\mathcal{B}(e,c_{2})
, recently provided in
[
17
]
, which we call the
modified Instanton component
.

The existence of all irreducible components
\mathcal{B}(e,c_{2})
is known only for small values of
c_{2}
, specifically

0\leq c_{2}\leq 5
. More precisely, Hartshorne and Rao
[
9
, Section 5.3]
proved that the moduli spaces
\mathcal{B}(0,1)
and

\mathcal{B}(0,2)
are irreducible. For
\mathcal{B}(0,3)
and
\mathcal{B}(0,4)
, it is shown in
[
4
,
6
]
that each has two irreducible components. The space
\mathcal{B}(0,5)
is characterized in
[
1
]
. Finally, classifications of
\mathcal{B}(-1,2)
and
\mathcal{B}(-1,4)
can be found in
[
10
,
14
]
and
[
2
]
, respectively. Morevorer, from
[
7
, Theorem 18]
it follows that
\mathcal{B}(-1.6)
has four known irreducible components: the Hartshorne component
M_{1}
of dimension 43, two Ein components
M_{2}
and
M_{3}
of dimension 43 and 50, respectively, and a fouth component
M_{4}
of dimension at least 45.

We organize this paper as follows. In Section
2
we recall the concept of minimal monads and its relation with stable rank 2 vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. We also list all the known irreducible components of
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
with their respective dimension. Finally, we recall the Hartshorne-Serre correspondence, which constructs rank-2 vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Section
3
is devoted to computing the dimension of
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
for a stable rank 2 bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
under certain specified conditions. To aid in this computation, we provide a Macaulay2 code. Using this method, we collect in Table
1
the dimension of the tangent space
\mathcal{B}(0,6)
at a stable bundle
\mathcal{E}
given as cohomology of a monad corresponding to one of the cases 6 (3) or 6(4) in Table 5.3 of Hartshorne and Rao
[
9
]
. We also prove in Theorem
3.3
that the dimension of the irreducible component
M_{4}
of
\mathcal{B}(-1,6)
is 45.

Using the lower semi-continuity of cohomology dimensions for coherent sheaves and comparing the dimensions
of the three known irreducible components of
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
, we prove in Section
4

the existence of two new infinite families
\mathbf{V}_{0}
and
\mathbf{V}_{1}
of components of
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
. The family
\mathbf{V}_{0}
contains stable rank-2 bundles arising as cohomology of minimal monads as in (
1
) with
\boldsymbol{a}=({(a-1)}^{2},0^{2}),\boldsymbol{b}=(a^{2}),a\geq 3
while
\mathbf{V}_{1}
contains those given as cohomology of minimal monads as in (
1
) with
\boldsymbol{a}=({(a-1)}^{2},1)

and
\boldsymbol{b}=(a^{2}),a\geq 3
.

2.
Preliminaries

This section will be devoted to recall basic concepts and prove some technical results used throughout the work.

2.1.
Monads

A monad is a complex of vector bundles of the form

\mathcal{M}:0\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow 0

whose only nontrivial cohomology is
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{M})=\ker\alpha/\im\beta
. In particular,
\alpha
is surjective and
\beta
is injective. The sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is the cohomology of the monad
\mathcal{M}
. In
[
13
]
, Horrocks proved that any vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
can be obtained as the cohomology of a monad where
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{C}
are direct sums of line bundles; see also
[
3
,
16
]
. Such monads are called Horrocks monads. In this case,
\alpha
and
\beta
are given by matrices of homogeneous polynomials. The monad is called minimal if the matrices defining
\alpha
and
\beta
have no non-zero constant entry. Any monad is homotopy equivalent to a minimal one.

For
\mathcal{E}
a rank-two vector bundle with
c_{1}(\mathcal{E})=0
, the isomorphism
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
reflects on the monad:
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{A}^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{E}
is the cohomology of a minimal monad of the form

(1)

\mathcal{M}:0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-a_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\begin{matrix}\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s+1}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(b_{i})\\
\displaystyle\oplus\\
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s+1}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-b_{i})\end{matrix}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a_{i})\longrightarrow 0

for some integers
a_{1}\leq\dots\leq a_{s}
and
0\leq b_{1}\leq\dots\leq b_{s+1}
. The tuples
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
and
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{s+1})
are called the type of the monad. Computing Chern classes yields the following equation:

(2)

c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{s+1}b_{j}^{2}.

Example 2.1
.

A Horrocks monad such that the extreme terms are line bundles,
{\bf a}=(t)
, is called an
Ein monad. This terminology originates from the influential work of Ein
[
5
]
. For integers
t,r,s\geq 0

such that
r+s<t
, we consider monads of the form

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\begin{matrix}\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(s)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r)\\
\displaystyle\oplus\\
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-s)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-r)\\
\end{matrix}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(t)\longrightarrow 0

where there exist polynomials
f_{1}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(t-s)),f_{2}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(t-r)),f_{3}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(t+s))
and
f_{4}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(t+r))
such that

\alpha=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}&f_{2}&f_{3}&f_{4}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta=\begin{bmatrix}-f_{3}&-f_{4}&f_{1}&f_{2}\end{bmatrix}^{T}.

If
\mathcal{E}
is the cohomology of a minimal monad as above, then
\mathcal{E}
is called a
generalized null correlation bundle
. It
was shown in
[
5
]
that
\mathcal{E}
is stable rank 2 bundle if and only if
t>r+s
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=t^{2}-r^{2}-s^{2}
.

Given a minimal monad as in (
1
),
\alpha
determines a minimal presentation of the cohomology module
H^{1}_{*}(\mathcal{E})=\bigoplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{1}(\mathcal{E}(l))
as a
S=k[x_{0},\dots,x_{3}]
-module. Conversely, given a minimal presentation

L_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}L_{0}\longrightarrow H^{1}_{*}(\mathcal{E})\longrightarrow 0

we have that
\mathcal{E}
is defined by the monad

0\longrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{0}^{\vee}(-1)\longrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{L}_{0}\longrightarrow 0.

For example, if
\mathcal{E}
is a null correlation bundle as in Example
2.1
then

H^{1}_{*}(\mathcal{E})\simeq M\otimes S(t)
, where
M=S/(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})
is a
0
-dimensional graded Gorenstein
ring and
H^{1}_{*}(\mathcal{E})
is generated by
a single nonzero element.

2.2.
The moduli space
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
and its Known components

We define
\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})
to be the set of minimal Horrocks monads as in (
1
) and we can see that

\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})=\{\beta:\mathcal{A}^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\mbox{ locally left invertible }:\beta^{\vee}\Omega\beta=0\},

where
\Omega
is a fixed symplectic form on
\mathcal{B}
. Let
\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})
denote the set of isomorphism
classes of stable rank 2 bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with even determinant which are given as cohomology
of a monad in
\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})
. Consider

G=\{\varphi\in\End(\mathcal{B}):\varphi^{\vee}\circ\Omega\circ\varphi=\varphi\}
. We define an action on

\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})
by

(u,\varphi)\cdot\beta=\varphi^{-1}\beta u^{-1},\forall(u,\varphi)\in\gl(\mathcal{A})\times G.

For
G_{0}=\gl(\mathcal{A})\times G/\pm(\id,\id)
, the above action acts freely on
\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})

and two monads in
\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})
are isomorphic if, and
only if, they lie in the same
G_{0}
-orbit. In this manner, we obtain a well-defined map

\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})

which is an isomorphism, see
[
7
, Section 8]
. Therefore, there is an injective morfism

\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2}),

where
c_{2}
is expressed in terms of
\boldsymbol{a}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
as in formula (
2
).

As stated in the Introduction, there are three known irreducible components of
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
: the
Instanton component

I_{c_{2}}
whose generic
point is a stable rank 2 bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
given as cohomology of a minimal monad
\mathbf{M}
in
\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})
with
\boldsymbol{a}=(1^{c_{2}})
and
\boldsymbol{b}=(0^{2c_{2}+2})
, i.e.
\mathbf{M}
has the form

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-1)^{\oplus c_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}}^{\oplus(2c_{2}+2)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1)^{\oplus c_{2}}\longrightarrow 0.

It was shown in
[
11
]
that
\dim I_{c_{2}}=8c_{2}-3
. The other known irreducible component of
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
is the
Ein component

\mathcal{G}(r,s,t)
with
r,s,t
integers
such that
c_{2}=t^{2}-r^{2}-s^{2}
, and whose generic point is a stable rank 2 bundle
\mathcal{F}
arising as
cohomology of an Ein minimal monad as defined in Example
2.1
. If we expand Equation 2.2.B in
[
5
]
which
gives the dimension of
\mathcal{G}(r,s,t)
we have

(3)

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{G}(r,s,t)=

\displaystyle\binom{t+r+3}{3}+\binom{t+s+3}{3}+\binom{t-r+3}{3}+\binom{t-s+3}{3}-\binom{r+s+3}{3}

\displaystyle-\binom{s-r+3}{3}-\binom{2r+3}{3}-\binom{2s+3}{3}-3-\mu(r,s),

where
\mu(r,s)=4
if
r=s=0
or
\mu(r,s)=1
if
r=0<s
or
r=s>0
or
\mu(r,s)=0
if
0<r\leq s
.

Finally, we have the so-called
modified Instanton component

\mathcal{M}_{c_{2}}
. Let
a,m
be integers the condition that

\mbox{ neither }5\leq a\leq 11,1+\epsilon\leq m+\epsilon\leq a-4\mbox{ or }a\geq 12,1+\epsilon\leq m+\epsilon\leq a+1

with
c_{2}=2m+\epsilon+a^{2},\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
. According to
[
17
, Theorem 1]
, there exist a family of irreducible components
\mathcal{M}_{c_{2}}
of
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
of dimension

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{2m+\epsilon+a^{2}}=4\binom{a+3}{3}+(2m+\epsilon)(10-a)-11.

2.3.
Serre Correspondence

In addition to obtaining rank 2 vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
as cohomology of a minimal
monad (as in Subsection
2.1
), we can also construct rank 2 vector bundles via the Hartshorne-Serre
correspondence. More precisely for any fixed invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(c)
, let
(Y,\xi)
be a
pair where
Y
is a local complete intersection Cohen-Macaulay curve on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
\xi\in H^{0}(\omega_{Y}(4-c))
is a
global section which generates the sheaf
\omega_{Y}(4-c)
, except finitely many points. Using
the isomorphism
H^{0}(\omega_{Y}(4-c))\simeq\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-c))
,
the global section
\xi
induces the exact sequence

(4)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-c)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot s}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Y}\rightarrow 0,

so that
\mathcal{E}
is a rank 2 bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with

c_{1}(\mathcal{E})=c
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=\deg Y.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{E}
is a rank 2 vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{E})=c_{1}
and

s\in H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
is a global section whose zero-set has codimension 2, then one obtains an exact
sequence as in (
4
) and a pair
(Y,\xi)
where
Y=(s)_{0}
and

\xi\in\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-c_{1}))
, see
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
for more details.

3.
Computation of
\Ext^{1}
Groups

Only a small number of results assist in determining the dimension of the group
\Ext^{1}
. Among
these,
[
15
, Lemma 4.1.7]
is one of the most familiar, though it demands the vanishing of various cohomology
groups, thereby limiting its range of application. The goal of this section is to compute the dimension
of
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
and
\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
for a stable rank 2 bundle
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
on

\mathbb{P}^{3}
arising as the cohomology of a minimal Horrocks monad as in (
1
), where the maps

\alpha
and
\beta
are known explicitly. As a consequence, in Subsection
4.2
, we provide examples
of new irreducible components of
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
that contain singular points.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
be a stable rank 2 bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. From sympletic structure of
\mathcal{E}

\End(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}

[

\dim T_{[\mathcal{E}]}\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})=\dim\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}).

\mathcal{E}
is

, Proposition 4.2 of
[
11
]
yields

\dim\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})-\dim\Ext^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=8c_{2}-3.

\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{G}(0,s,s+1)
. However, for any
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})
we have
h^{1}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(-s-1))=0
. Hence,

\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})
cannot be contained
in
\mathcal{G}(0,s,s+1)
.

Finally, consider
r>1
. We may write

h(r,s)=(r^{2}-1)\left(\frac{3}{2}s^{2}-\frac{1}{3}r+s-\frac{3}{2}\right)-5r+\frac{7}{2}+\mu(r,s).

Define
g(r)=\dfrac{-5r+\frac{7}{2}}{r^{2}-1}
. we verify that
|g(r)|<1
for all
r>4
, and moreover

g(2)=-\frac{13}{6},\quad g(3)=-\frac{23}{16},\quad g(4)=-\frac{11}{10}.

Hence,

\displaystyle h(r,s)

\displaystyle=(r^{2}-1)\left(\frac{3}{2}s^{2}-\frac{1}{3}r+s-\frac{3}{2}+g(r)\right)+\mu(r,s)

\displaystyle\geq(r^{2}-1)\left(\frac{3}{2}s^{2}-\frac{1}{3}r+s-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{13}{6}\right)+\mu(r,s)

\displaystyle=(r^{2}-1)\left(\frac{3}{2}s^{2}+s-\frac{1}{3}r-\frac{11}{3}\right)+\mu(r,s).

Since
s\geq r>1
, it follows that
r^{2}>1
,
s>\frac{1}{3}r
, and
\frac{3}{2}s^{2}>\frac{11}{3}
. Therefore,
the expression inside the parentheses is positive, which implies
h(r,s)>0
.

Case 2:
Consider
t\geq r+s+2
.

Define
h(r,s,t):=\dim\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{a};\boldsymbol{b})-\dim\mathcal{G}(r,s,t)
.
First, assume
r=s=0
. Then

h(0,0,t)=\frac{3}{8}t^{4}-\frac{2}{3}t^{3}-\frac{1}{4}t^{2}-\frac{22}{3}t+\frac{135}{8}.

The polynomial
h(0,0,t)
does not admit real roots; consequently,
h(0,0,t)>0
for all

t>0
. Finally, we suppose
s>0
. Since
t\geq r+s+2
, we can adapt the method used
in the proof of Proposition
4.2
to establish the positivity
h(r,s,t)>0
.
∎

4.3.

We have already shown the existence of two families with

, where
b\in\{0,1\}
. Moreover, these families belong to a new irreducible component of
\mathcal{B}(0,c_{2})
for every

in
[

shares similarities with the two families we constructed; this motivates the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.8
.

Let
a
and
k
be positive integers. For each integer
b\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
with
a>b
, there exists a stable rank-2 vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
arising as the cohomology of a minimal monad of the form

(12)

\mathcal{M}(a,b,k):0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-a)}^{\oplus k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a-1)}^{\oplus k}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(b)\\
\displaystyle\oplus\\
{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1-a)}^{\oplus k}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-b)\end{matrix}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a)}^{\oplus k}\longrightarrow 0.

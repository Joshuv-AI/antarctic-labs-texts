The data mining and high-performance network model of tourism electronic word of mouth for analysis of factors influencing tourists’ purchasing behavior
Authors: Wei Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study establishes a deep learning model for personalized travel recommendations based on factors that affect tourists’ purchases to provide users with more accurate and personalized travel recommendations. Firstly, Natural Language Processing (NLP) technology is used to process and emotionally analyze tourism review information, dividing it into positive, negative, or neutral to understand tourists’ attitudes towards purchasing products and services. Secondly, a High-Performance Network (HPN) model is constructed based on factors that affect tourists’ purchases. The relationship among tourists, products, and word of mouth (WOM) is represented as a complex network to analyze and predict event occurrence patterns and influencing factors in tourism electronic word-of-mouth (EWOM) data. The construction of the model considers various factors, such as the spread of WOM, the impact of price, etc., to reveal the complex relationships among tourists, WOM, products, etc. Finally, the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) model is combined with the Backpropagation (BP) model, the time series data is processed with the help of the gated recurrent unit, and the HPN model is trained and evaluated. The Yelp dataset is employed to verify the accuracy and feasibility of the model, which contains the score and review data of many tourist destinations. The results reveal that price, WOM, and destination are one of the main factors influencing tourists’ purchasing behavior, with WOM being the most significant. Positive WOM reviews remarkably increase product sales, while negative WOM has the opposite effect. The minimum expectation for age, occupation, education, personal monthly income, and tourists’ willingness to purchase is 0.00, and the minimum expectation for gender factors is 0.31. The RNN-BP hybrid model has higher accuracy and predictive ability, which is 1.73% and 2.30% more accurate than single models and traditional machine learning predictive models. In short, this study contributes to a better understanding travelers’ needs and preferences to optimize products and services and improve market competitiveness. In addition, the methods and models of this study can also be applied in EWOM data mining in other fields.

Introduction

The tourism industry faces unprecedented development opportunities and challenges in today’s digital era. With the popularity of the Internet and social media, various factors often affect tourists when deciding on travel destinations and purchasing products and services, such as word of mouth (WOM), price, destination, etc.1,2. Nevertheless, due to the vast diversity of the tourism market and individual differences in consumer needs, in the early days, the traditional recommendation model usually used association rule-based, content-based filtering, and collaborative filtering. These methods often rely on fixed characteristics and attributes of goods or services, such as price, category, etc., while ignoring the personalized needs and preferences of users. Traditional recommendation models usually adopt content-based filtering or collaborative filtering technology to make recommendations by analyzing the correlation between the characteristics of goods or services and the historical behavior of users. However, these methods often ignore the influence of user emotion, attitude, and other external factors on consumer behavior, resulting in insufficient universality and accuracy of recommendation results. Although these methods can provide recommendations to a certain extent, they often fail to meet the users’ personalized needs3. Consequently, developing models that can accurately predict tourists’ purchasing behavior and offer personalized travel recommendations has become an urgent need for developing the tourism business.

However, achieving personalized travel recommendations faces a series of challenges. Tourists’ purchasing decisions are often influenced by complex factors such as emotions, social factors, and price fluctuations, making it difficult to predict purchasing behavior patterns accurately. At the same time, large-scale tourism electronic word-of-mouth (EWOM) data contains massive amounts of information, and how to mine valuable knowledge from it is also a challenge. At present, deep learning (DL) algorithms have been widely applied in the field of Natural Language Processing (NLP), providing a powerful tool for emotional analysis in tourism EWOM data4. The DL algorithm can automatically extract feature information from comment text to analyze its emotional color to identify the reviewer’s attitude towards products and services, thus revealing their emotional factors such as satisfaction and dissatisfaction. In data mining (DM), the High-Performance Network (HPN) model aims to achieve high-performance networks and communication, offering faster data transmission and processing for large-scale datasets5–7. Meanwhile, the high performance of the HPN model makes distributed DM more efficient and can coordinate and integrate data from different sources more quickly, enabling faster analysis and pattern discovery8.

Existing research mainly focuses on the impact of EWOM on individual factors such as price or service quality, lacking a comprehensive model to systematically analyze the complex relationships between multiple factors. Additionally, some studies solely employ traditional statistical methods, failing to fully utilize modern DM techniques and DL models for more precise results. Firstly, current research in processing and analyzing tourism review data often relies on traditional text analysis methods, without adequately leveraging DL technology to capture emotional information within the reviews. Secondly, while some studies attempt to establish models based on HPN to analyze the complex relationships in tourism EWOM, these models typically overlook the impact of time-series data on prediction accuracy. Moreover, many studies do not thoroughly explore how to effectively combine DL models with HPN models to enhance the accuracy and practicality of personalized recommendations.

This study aims to fill these research gaps by constructing a personalized travel recommendation system that combines DL and HPN models to deeply analyze the factors influencing tourists’ purchasing behavior. Firstly, NLP techniques are employed to perform emotional analysis on tourism reviews, categorizing them as positive, negative, or neutral to understand tourists’ attitudes towards products and services. Secondly, an HPN model based on factors influencing tourist purchases is constructed, representing the relationships among tourists, products, and WOM as a complex network to analyze and predict event occurrence patterns and influencing factors in tourism EWOM data. Finally, Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and Backpropagation (BP) models are combined to process time-series data through gated recurrent units, and the HPN model is trained and evaluated. The Yelp dataset, containing extensive destination ratings and review data, validates the model’s accuracy and feasibility.

The core issue of this study is how to accurately analyze tourism EWOM data by combining DL technology with HPN models and uncover the key factors influencing tourists’ purchasing behavior. Through in-depth analysis of factors such as price, WOM, and destination, it is found that WOM, especially positive WOM, has the most remarkable impact on product sales. This study enhances the understanding of tourists’ needs and preferences, aiding in the optimization of products and services to improve market competitiveness. Moreover, it presents methods and models that can be widely applied in DM of EWOM in other fields. Academically, this study contributes by integrating DL algorithms with DM methods, proposing a new model to comprehensively understand the complex factors influencing tourists’ purchasing behavior. The emotional analysis and complex network modeling reveals the intricate relationships among tourists, products, and services, providing new theoretical perspectives and methodologies for personalized recommendation systems in the tourism field. In terms of managerial practice, the research findings offer prominent insights for tourism industry managers. First, WOM, price, and destination are the main factors affecting tourists’ purchasing behavior, among which WOM has the most significant influence. This suggests that managers should attach importance to the management of online WOM in their marketing strategies, and promote product sales by actively guiding positive reviews. Second, the study points out that factors such as age, occupation, education level, and personal monthly income have a low correlation with purchase intention, while gender has a certain correlation with purchase intention. This helps managers to more accurately identify the target market and consumer groups, and formulate more effective market segmentation and positioning strategies.

Literature review

The importance of EWOM in the tourism industry has been widely recognized, and numerous scholars have conducted in-depth research on this topic. Firstly, Macheka et al. (2024) explored how customers relied on online reviews to influence their purchasing decisions, finding that positive reviews significantly enhanced consumers’ purchase intentions9. Similarly, Khern-am-nuai et al. (2024) analyzed Amazon’s book review data and confirmed the positive impact of favorable reviews on sales, while negative reviews hindered sales growth10. Furthermore, Garner (2022) systematically reviewed the theoretical foundations of EWOM, highlighting the critical roles of social identity and information processing in consumer decision-making11. Shafiq et al. (2023) further studied the mechanisms of WOM dissemination on social media platforms, revealing that trust and the strength of network relationships directly affect the effectiveness of WOM dissemination12. Through empirical analysis, Hysa et al. (2021) demonstrated that tourists tended to trust reviewers who shared similar circumstances, underscoring the importance of personalized recommendations13. Moreover, Su et al. (2022) pointed out that online travel reviews influenced consumers’ purchasing decisions and overall impressions of destinations, indicating EWOM’s potential in brand image building14. However, these studies mainly focused on analyzing the impact of single factors on consumer behavior, lacking a comprehensive understanding of the complex interactions among multiple factors. For instance, Srivastava et al. (2024) studied the impact of sentiment polarity in hotel reviews on consumer choices15, but did not consider other factors like price. In addition, Xu et al. (2022) used text mining techniques to analyze the content characteristics of travel reviews16, but did not incorporate DL models for more refined predictions. Methodologically, traditional statistical methods had certain limitations, such as overlooking nonlinear relationships in data. Wang et al. (2022) employed structural equation modeling to analyze the influencing factors of online reviews17, which, despite providing some explanatory power, failed to capture long-term dependencies in time series data. In contrast, the topic model proposed by Murshed et al. (2023) performed well in text mining but still had limitations in handling dynamic data18.

Tariyal et al. (2022) examined the impact of EWOM on tourists’ purchasing decisions by using emotional analysis techniques to process tourist reviews. They found that positive reviews significantly increased tourists’ purchase intentions, while negative reviews had the opposite effect19. Their research laid the foundation for this study, demonstrating the critical importance of emotional analysis in understanding tourists’ purchasing behavior. Nitu et al. (2021) developed a personalized travel recommendation system based on DL. Their research utilized convolutional neural networks to process user behavior data, and the results showed that personalized recommendations significantly improved user satisfaction and conversion rates20. Building on this, this study further integrated emotional analysis and HPN models to more accurately understand and predict tourists’ purchasing behavior. Additionally, Wang (2023) proposed a tourism recommendation system based on graph neural networks to handle complex relationships between users and products. Their model captured the implicit relationships between users and products, markedly improving recommendation accuracy21. This study adopted Wang’s (2023) graph network approach and further analyzed the complex relationships in EWOM data using the HPN model. In the area of exploring EWOM data, Han et al. (2021) performed a clustering analysis on large-scale tourist review data, revealing differences in review behavior among various groups. Their findings indicated that diverse user groups have varying sensitivities to price and service22. This study incorporated these group characteristics when processing tourist reviews to enhance the model’s adaptability to different user groups. Regarding purchasing behavior in the tourism industry, Zhang et al. (2022) used time series analysis techniques to explore the influence of price fluctuations on tourists’ purchasing decisions23. Their study showed that the impact of price changes on purchasing decisions is dynamic. This study combined RNN and BP models to more precisely capture this dynamic change. In terms of model construction, Purohit et al. (2024) presented a machine learning-based tourism recommendation model that used random forest algorithms and support vector machines for prediction24. Their results indicated that the model effectively predicted users’ purchase intentions. However, this study introduced the HPN model and the RNN-BP hybrid model, improving prediction accuracy and considering the complex network relationships within EWOM data. Meira et al. (2022) explored the application of social media data in tourism recommendations, utilizing NLP techniques to extract emotional features and combining them with DL models for analysis25. Their research found that emotional features significantly impacted the prediction of tourists’ purchasing behavior. This study further extended this approach by combining emotional analysis with the HPN model to enhance prediction accuracy and reliability. In dataset selection, Bagherzadeh et al. (2021) employed TripAdvisor data for tourism recommendation research and found that tourists’ reviews and ratings directly affect recommendation outcomes26. This study used the Yelp dataset, covering a wide range of tourist destinations and detailed rating information, to validate the model’s performance across different scenarios.

In summary, existing literature has made numerous contributions to exploring the impact of EWOM on tourists’ purchasing behavior. However, it primarily focuses on single-factor analysis or uses traditional statistical methods, failing to fully leverage modern DL techniques to capture the complex interactions within the data. This study addresses these limitations by considering multiple influencing factors and applying advanced DL methods. It provides the tourism industry with a new, more precise personalized recommendation model that optimizes products and services, enhancing market competitiveness. This enriches the theoretical framework of EWOM research and has practical application value. The study combines RNN and BP models to handle time series data and uses gated recurrent units to enhance the capture of long-term dependencies in the data. Additionally, an HPN model is constructed, systematically revealing the complex relationships behind tourists’ purchasing behavior by considering WOM dissemination, price, and destination factors. This innovative approach improves the model’s prediction accuracy and offers new solutions for personalized travel recommendations.

Analysis of HPN model construction

HPN model is a computer network model designed to realize high-speed data transmission, low-latency communication, and efficient network processing. The HPN model aims to optimize the network’s performance to meet the demand for high-speed, large-capacity data transmission, especially in large-scale data processing, real-time communication, or distributed computing environments31,32. In the tourism field, a large amount of data is involved, covering multiple levels such as tourists, products, WOM, etc., and an analytical method that can capture this complexity is needed. At the same time, products, tourists, and WOM have complex mutual influence and correlation, and traditional analysis methods make it difficult to capture these interactive relations fully33. Firstly, price is a vital economic factor influencing tourists’ purchasing decisions. Kah et al. (2022) further confirmed that price sensitivity was especially significant in the purchasing decisions of tourism products, as tourists typically chose products with high cost-effectiveness within their budget34. Thus, incorporating price factors into the proposed HPN model helps accurately capture the impact of price on tourists’ purchasing behavior. For most consumers, price directly affects the final outcome of their purchasing decisions and significantly determines their perceived value of a particular tourism product or service. Price often correlates closely with the quality, scarcity, and market positioning of the product or service, making it an indispensable factor when analyzing tourists’ purchasing behavior. Particularly in the tourism market, price-sensitive consumers may adjust their travel plans based on promotions or price changes. Secondly, WOM, as an informal evaluation form, distinctly influences tourists’ purchasing behavior. Siddiqui et al. (2021) demonstrated that positive WOM could greatly enhance a product’s appeal and increase purchase rates, while negative WOM might have adverse effects on sales35. The study also noted that the effect of WOM was particularly evident in the tourism industry, where experiential products led tourists to rely more on others’ evaluations when making decisions. YILMAZ (2020) further revealed the importance of WOM in the digital age, highlighting those reviews and ratings on social media directly impacted tourists’ purchasing behavior36. WOM typically consists of tourists’ genuine evaluations of tourism products or services, including positive and negative feedback. Research shows that positive WOM can markedly boost a product’s credibility and attractiveness, thereby driving potential tourists’ purchase intentions. Conversely, negative WOM may inhibit purchasing behavior, posing higher market risks for tourism products or services. Hence, WOM not only reflects actual consumer experiences but also influences the attitudes and behaviors of other potential consumers, making it an essential factor in analyzing tourists’ purchasing decisions. Finally, the destination, as a core element of tourism products, also significantly affects tourists’ purchasing behavior. Khairi et al. (2021) indicated that the attractiveness, cultural characteristics, and tourism resources of a destination remarkedly influenced tourists’ choices37. When choosing a travel destination, tourists typically consider the destination’s popularity, the richness of its tourism resources, and the degree to which it matches their personal interests. The attractiveness, popularity, and uniqueness of a destination determine its competitiveness in the market. A destination with cultural, natural, or entertainment features usually occupies an important position in tourists’ choices. The characteristics of a destination influence tourists’ travel decisions and interact with other factors such as price and WOM, collectively impacting tourists’ purchasing decisions. Therefore, destination-related factors are crucial for constructing a comprehensive purchasing behavior analysis model.

The HPN model can efficiently integrate and process these data, providing a more comprehensive and accurate explanation for analyzing tourists’ purchasing behavior and revealing the underlying mechanisms. Thus, this study transforms tourists, products, WOM, etc., into a network structure and constructs an HPN model. The construction of the HPN model is plotted in Fig. 3.

Application of RNN-BP hybrid algorithm in the HPN model analysis

The main reason for choosing the DL algorithm in this study is that it can effectively handle large-scale and complex data and provide more accurate analysis and prediction. For complex information such as tourism EWOM data, the DL model has a more powerful learning and analysis ability, and can learn from massive data and identify potential patterns and associations, to better understand tourists’ preferences and influencing factors of purchasing behavior. In addition, the DL model can improve the accuracy and generalization ability of the model by gradually optimizing the parameters and hierarchical structure, to adapt to different types of data and scenarios, and has strong universality and applicability. Further analysis of the HPN model among tourists, products, and WOM constructed in this study, combining DL with the HPN model, can build a more accurate recommendation system. The RNN-BP hybrid algorithm is chosen to give full play to the advantages of the RNN and BP algorithms and solve their limitations. RNNs are capable of processing sequence data well and have the memory ability to capture time dependence in time series data to better model dynamic changes in tourism EWOM data. As a common optimization algorithm, the BP algorithm can effectively optimize and adjust the parameters of DL model, to improve the model’s training efficiency and prediction accuracy. By combining RNN and BP algorithms, their respective advantages can be comprehensively utilized, and their respective shortcomings can be compensated for. Besides, a more robust and efficient model can be constructed to more accurately analyze and predict the influencing factors in tourism EWOM data. By conducting a joint analysis of tourists’ historical behavior, product characteristics, and WOM evaluation, the DL algorithm can construct personalized recommendation models, providing more suitable product and experience recommendations for tourists.

By designing a short-term recommendation project to recommend products that users are likely to buy at the next moment. If the user ui buys Product 1, Product 2, and Product 3 in the scenic spots, it is speculated that the following user is likely to buy Product 4. This study focuses only on the user’s short-term behavior, i.e., a timeframe closer to the current time. By analyzing the user’s recent behavior, the user is calculated and predicted the next most likely product to consume. When dealing with time series data, especially tourism EWOM data, the timing and dependencies of the data need to be considered. As a neural network structure naturally suitable for processing sequence data, RNN can capture the relationship between data at different time steps40. However, traditional RNNs are prone to gradient vanishing or explosion problems during training. To overcome these problems, the RNN is selected to combine with the BP model, and the advantages of BP are used to assist in training the RNN to improve the stability and accuracy of the model.

The BP algorithm is divided into two steps: forward propagation and backpropagation. Forward propagation is the one-way forward propagation of information from the input layer through the hidden layer, and finally to the output layer to obtain the output of the entire network41.

Experimental data

The data source for this experiment is the Yelp dataset (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/yelp-dataset). This dataset contains a large amount of information, including basic information such as the name, address, phone number, longitude and latitude, and category of the merchant, as well as detailed information such as comment content, user ratings, and comment dates for the merchant. The types of merchants in the dataset cover various fields, involving catering, shopping, tourism, entertainment, etc.

In this study, the Yelp dataset is screened, and tourism EWOM data related to ratings and reviews of tourism destinations is selected, encompassing data content such as the occupation, age, personal monthly income, education level, and purchase intention of tourists. To ensure the accuracy and applicability of the data, the following pre-processing operations are performed on the dataset:

Data desensitization: To protect user privacy, the personal information involved in the dataset is anonymized, such as removing sensitive information such as names and specific addresses.Data cleaning: Missing value processing: For missing values in the data, the method of deleting and filling is adopted. For a small number of missing data items are directly deleted, and for data with a large number of missing key fields, interpolation or mean substitution is used to process.

Evaluation of simulation experiments

To evaluate the HPN model performance of the tourism EWOM data by the BP-RNN algorithm, TensorFlow framework software is used to build. Python language is used for data preprocessing and algorithm detail implementation. The software and hardware involved are as follows. The software covers an operating system of Ubuntu 12.04, an image processing library of OpenCV 4.2.0, a Python version of Python 3.7, and libraries of NumPy and Matplotlib; The hardware configuration is Intel (R) Core (R) G1820, with a main frequency of 2.70 GHz and 6GB of memory.

The experimental parameters of the model are as follows. The learning rate of the BP algorithm is 0.01; The RNN algorithm’s iteration times is 100; The emotional analysis threshold of NLP technology is 0.5; The number of nodes in the input, hidden, and output layer of the HPN model is 100, 50, and 1.

When evaluating the performance of the constructed model, the degree of influence of each factor on tourists’ purchasing behavior is assigned as 0-100, with 80–100 indicating significant impact, 60–80 illustrating insignificant impact, and below 60 presenting no effect. The proposed algorithm was compared with RNN44, BP45, and the algorithm proposed by scholars Luo et al. (2023) and Ktona et al. (2023). Accuracy and Root mean squared error (RMSE) were the main evaluation indicators to measure the model’s prediction accuracy.

Discussion

This study not only explores the direct impact of tourism EWOM but also considers multiple dimensions such as consumer behavior, social influence, and market dynamics to comprehensively present the research results. To further explore the findings of this study, key themes and patterns in the existing literature are summarized and compared. First, consumer behavior is an important factor affecting tourists’ purchasing behavior. Song et al. (2021) employed the structural equation model to analyze questionnaire data of young hotel customers with experience using travel agency websites in Malaysia46. This study verified the vital role of EWOM argument quality and information source credibility in predicting EWOM information usability. This study also highlighted the importance of EWOM on tourist purchasing behavior, especially the positive impact of positive EWOM reviews on product sales. In tourism, functional value and emotional value are particularly prominent. Emotional analysis of travel reviews using NLP technology found that positive WOM significantly increased product sales, while negative WOM did the opposite. In addition, Le (2021) also pointed out that price was an important factor affecting consumers’ purchasing behavior47. The results also showed that the price is one of the main factors affecting the purchasing behavior of tourists.

Secondly, social influence also plays a significant role in tourists’ purchasing behavior. Anderson (2023) pointed out that social norms and subjective norms affected individual behavioral intentions48. In the tourism industry, tourists’ purchasing behavior is influenced not only by personal preferences but also by the opinions of their social circle. Berne Manero et al. (2023) suggested that the spread of tourism WOM markedly impacted tourists’ decisions49. The proposed HPN model, by analyzing the complex relationships between tourists, products, and WOM, reveals the mechanism through which WOM dissemination affects tourists’ purchasing behavior, aligning with the aforementioned findings. Additionally, market dynamics are another crucial factor influencing tourists’ purchasing behavior. Amadasun et al. (2022) argued that market dynamics included factors such as market demand, competition, and market trends50. This study denotes that the market demand and competitive landscape for tourism products influence tourists’ purchasing decisions. For example, when a tourism product receives more positive WOM, tourists are more inclined to choose that product; conversely, when there is more negative WOM, tourists tend to opt for alternative products. This is consistent with Gidebo’s (2021) findings on tourists’ choice behavior, illustrating that tourists’ choices are significantly influenced by market supply and demand conditions51.

By comparing and discussing existing literature, this study offers a more comprehensive understanding of its findings. Firstly, the application of NLP technology in emotional analysis effectively quantifies tourists’ satisfaction, preferences, and purchasing intentions regarding tourism products and services, providing an objective means of assessing customer experience. This aligns with several studies that suggest emotional analysis is crucial in understanding consumer behavior52. Secondly, the HPN model, by capturing the complex relationships among tourists, products, and WOM, reveals the mechanism through which WOM dissemination impacts tourists’ purchasing behavior. This is consistent with the findings of the theory of planned behavior and social influence theory, indicating that social influence is significant in tourists’ purchasing behavior53. Finally, the hybrid model combining RNN and BP algorithms excels in handling large-scale and complex data, enhancing model accuracy and predictive capability, thus offering more reliable technical support for tourism recommendation systems54.

In conclusion, the results of this study not only validate the theoretical viewpoints in the existing literature but also delve deeper into the impact of tourism EWOM on tourists’ purchasing behavior using advanced technological means. This study provides new insights for understanding and optimizing tourists’ needs and preferences, with significant theoretical and practical implications for improving market competitiveness. Additionally, the methods and models proposed here can be applied to mining EWOM data in other fields, indicating broad application prospects.

Conclusions

This study innovatively combines NLP technology with the DL model to construct a comprehensive, personalized travel recommendation system. At the same time, the HPN model is employed to construct and reveal the complex relationships between factors such as tourists, products, and WOM. This study successfully identifies and quantifies the key factors influencing tourists’ purchasing behavior, including price, WOM, and destination. Among these, WOM is the primary factor significantly affecting purchasing behavior. The study utilizes NLP technology to perform emotional analysis on tourism review data, classifying the reviews as positive, negative, or neutral to understand tourists’ attitudes toward products and services. The results of these emotional analyses reveal that positive WOM reviews markedly increase product sales, while negative reviews have the opposite effect. Specifically, positive WOM reviews enhance tourists’ trust and willingness to purchase the product, thereby remarkably boosting sales. This indicates that when tourists see positive reviews from other users, they are more inclined to buy, as these reviews increase the product’s credibility and attractiveness. Furthermore, negative WOM reviews significantly reduce product sales. Negative reviews typically influence potential customers’ purchasing decisions, causing them to develop doubts or negative impressions about the product, thus reducing the likelihood of purchase. This phenomenon is fully validated in the proposed model analysis, where the presence of negative reviews prominently decreases tourists’ purchase intentions, as clearly shown in the data analysis results. Moreover, the proposed HPN model represents the relationships among tourists, products, and WOM as a complex network structure, enabling an in-depth analysis of the interactions among these factors. The model results indicate that, besides WOM, factors such as price and destination also impact purchasing behavior. However, compared to WOM, the influence of price and destination is relatively minor; their effects are significantly diminished in the face of WOM’s impact. Ultimately, by training and evaluating the hybrid algorithm combining RNN and BP models, the proposed model exhibits significant improvements in accuracy and predictive capability. The study results denote that the RNN-BP hybrid model excels in handling time series data and dynamic changes, with its accuracy improving by 1.73% and 2.30% compared to single models and traditional machine learning prediction models. This finding not only validates the proposed model’s effectiveness in identifying and quantifying factors influencing tourists’ purchasing behavior but also offers valuable references for optimizing tourism products and services. In conclusion, this study, through the construction of DL and HPN models, not only successfully recognizes the key factors affecting tourists’ purchasing behavior but also clearly demonstrates the significant impact of WOM on purchasing behavior. These results provide deeper market insights for the tourism industry, helping enterprises more accurately understand tourists’ needs and preferences when formulating marketing strategies, thus enhancing market competitiveness.

However, there are still some shortcomings. First, the prediction results of the model may be influenced by other factors that have not been considered, such as market fluctuations and policy changes. Second, although the Yelp dataset is used for validation, its coverage may be limited and cannot cover all types of tourist destinations and comments. In view of the above limitations, future research can be carried out from the following aspects. Firstly, the dataset can be further expanded to involve more review data for different types of travel destinations and user groups to improve the applicability and generalization ability of the model. Secondly, more influencing factors, such as seasonal factors and market competition environment, can be considered to comprehensively analyze and predict the influence of tourism EWOM on purchasing behavior. Thirdly, more technical means and model algorithms can be combined to continuously optimize the DL and HPN models to improve the prediction accuracy and generalization ability of the model. Finally, the personalized needs and preferences of users can be further explored. The recommendation system and personalized marketing strategy can be combined to provide more accurate marketing programs and product recommendation services for the tourism industry. Consequently, through continuous research and exploration, the theoretical framework and practical application of this study field can be further improved and optimized to support the development and innovation of the tourism industry.

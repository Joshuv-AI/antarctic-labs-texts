Effects of descending aortic occlusion for massive obstetric hemorrhage: Nationwide analysis of maternal death in Japan
Authors: Makoto Aoki, Soichiro Obata, Manabu Ogoyama, Koki Shimura, Yutaka Iwagoi, Makiko Kasahara, Yui Yoshida, Saya Yamashita, Akihiko Sekizawa, Koji Hashii, Masahiko Nakata, Takeshi Murakoshi, Shinji Katsuragi, Hiroaki Tanaka, Kayo Tanaka, Yoko Sagara, Tatsuya Arakaki, Junichi Hasegawa, the Working group of Maternal Mortality 2025 in the Japan Association of Obstetricians and Gynecologists
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the effects of aortic occlusion (AO) on severe postpartum hemorrhage (PPH)—a leading cause of preventable maternal mortality—using a nationwide maternal death registry in Japan.MethodsA nationwide retrospective observational study of hemorrhage‐related maternal deaths was conducted in Japan between 2010 and 2024. Patients were classified into an AO group (resuscitative endovascular balloon occlusion of the aorta [REBOA] and/or resuscitative thoracotomy with aortic cross‐clamping [RT‐ACC]) and a non‐AO group. The primary outcome was the achievement of definitive hemostatic procedures. Secondary outcomes included 24‐h survival.ResultsAmong 109 hemorrhage‐related maternal deaths, 19 patients underwent AO (13 REBOA, six RT‐ACC). Patients in the AO group were more frequently transferred to tertiary care centers than those in the non‐AO group (84.2% (16/19) vs 43.3% (39/90), P < 0.01). Achievement of definitive hemostasis was significantly higher in the AO group (78.9% (15/19) vs 42.2% (38/90), P < 0.01), as were 24‐h survival (42.1% (8/19) vs 15.6% (14/90), P = 0.02). Approximately 90% of AO procedures (17/19) were performed at tertiary centers, and only 16% (3/19) were initiated before cardiopulmonary arrest.ConclusionAO, including REBOA and RT‐ACC, was associated with higher rates of hemostasis and short‐term survival among cases of maternal death due to severe PPH. Earlier application of AO before cardiopulmonary arrest may be critical to improving outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is the leading preventable cause of maternal morbidity and mortality worldwide. 1 It accounts for approximately 8% of maternal deaths in developed regions. 1 , 2 Early recognition of catastrophic hemorrhage, along with prompt and appropriate treatment, is essential to the prevention of maternal deaths due to severe hemorrhagic shock. However, in some rapidly progressing cases, achieving definitive hemostasis in a timely manner may not be possible.

Aortic occlusion (AO) is an advanced procedure for treating life‐threatening hemorrhage. 3 Its objective is to redistribute the limited circulation of blood to cerebral and coronary circulation and to attenuate blood loss from subdiaphragmatic bleeding. 4 , 5 Traditionally, AO was performed via resuscitative thoracotomy with aortic cross clamping (RT‐ACC) and has been studied mainly in penetrating trauma. More recently, an intra‐aortic balloon occlusion device (resuscitative endovascular balloon occlusion of the aorta, REBOA) has been introduced as a less invasive technique in trauma care. 6 However, there has been only limited investigation of AO (including both RT‐ACC and REBOA) in PPH.

We conducted this study to evaluate the effects of AO in cases of catastrophic PPH. We hypothesized that AO may contribute to the achievement of a definitive hemostatic procedure.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study patients

This nationwide retrospective case–control study included maternal deaths in which the initial symptom occurred during delivery, and the patient subsequently died within 1 year after delivery. The cases occurred across Japan between 2010 and 2024. Among all maternal deaths, we extracted the cases of maternal mortality with a hemorrhagic cause. The judgment of hemorrhagic cause was determined through review by the experts of the Japan Maternal Exploratory Committee (JMDEC). 7 Informed consent was not obtained from the patients or their families as the study was based on an analysis of institutional forms, and patient records and information were anonymized before registration.

Data collection and collected variables

The present study is based on the maternal deaths registration system in Japan, which has been described in detail in our previous reports. 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 Whenever a maternal death occurred, the attending physicians submitted detailed forms to report the case to the Japan Association of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (JAOG), along with data such as anonymized medical images, laboratory results, and pathologic and autopsy reports. After expert review by the JMDEC, causal analysis reports were sent to the submitting institution, including the most preventable cause, issues in clinical practice, and recommendations for obstetric caregivers.

In this study the following variables were collected from the detailed forms submitted and the causal analysis reports: maternal characteristics, type of delivery, location of initial symptom onset, details of hemorrhagic causes of mortality, maternal transportation, advanced life‐saving procedure, including AO and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, hemostatic procedure, 24‐h survival, and time from admission to death.

In this study, the procedure of AO was defined as intervention, and AO included aortic cross‐clamping during RT‐ACC and/or AO by using an aortic balloon catheter (known as REBOA). We classified the studied patients into two groups. The Case group was defined as patients with AO by RT‐ACC and/or REBOA. The Control group was defined as patients without AO.

Details of aortic occlusion procedures

For patients undergoing AO, two techniques were included: RT‐ACC and REBOA. RT‐ACC was performed via left anterolateral thoracotomy, typically in the fourth or fifth intercostal space, followed by direct exposure of the descending thoracic aorta and application of a vascular clamp to achieve proximal aortic control. 12 This procedure was generally undertaken in patients with profound hemodynamic instability or cardiac arrest.

REBOA was performed by percutaneous or surgical access to the common femoral artery, followed by insertion of a balloon catheter into the aorta using fluoroscopic or external landmark guidance. 13 The balloon was positioned in the appropriate aortic zone (typically Zone I for intra‐abdominal or obstetric hemorrhage) and inflated to achieve temporary occlusion of aortic blood flow. Balloon position and inflation were adjusted according to the clinical condition and institutional protocols.

Outcome definition

The primary outcome was the achievement of a hemostatic procedure. This composite outcome included laparotomy such as hysterectomy, uterine suturing, as well as interventional radiology procedures, including angioembolization of any target vessels. Secondary outcomes were defined as 24‐h survival and time from admission to death.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as medians and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages. First, a descriptive analysis was performed to examine the characteristics and outcomes of the studied patients in the Case and Control groups. Comparisons were made of each categorical variable in those groups, using the χ 2‐test. Comparisons of each continuous variable between the groups were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Second, the details of clinical treatment courses among the members of the Case group were described.

Statistical significance was defined as a two‐sided P‐value of <0.05 in all statistical analyses. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.0.5; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

A patient flow diagram of this study is shown in Figure 1. Among 640 maternal deaths during the study period, 109 hemorrhage‐related maternal deaths were studied. Study patients were divided into a Case group (n = 19) and a Control group (n = 90). The numbers of REBOA and RT‐ACC patients were 13 and six, respectively. One patient underwent both REBOA and RT‐ACC. This patient was initially resuscitated with REBOA and subsequently required RT‐ACC due to persistent hemodynamic instability (Table S2).

Table 1 and Table S1 show characteristics of the study patients in the Case and Control groups. The maternal characteristics variables did not differ significantly between the two groups. Transportation was more frequent in the Case group than in the Control group (94.7% (18/19) vs 65.6% (59/90), P < 0.01). Transportation to a tertiary care center was significantly higher in the Case group (84.2% (16/19) vs 43.3% (39/90), P < 0.01). Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation as an advanced life‐saving procedure tended to be used more often in the Case group (26.3% (5/19) vs 11.1% (10/90), P = 0.13).

Details of clinical courses in the Case group are shown in Table S2. One patient received RT‐ACC at the delivery hospital, which was designated as a tertiary care center (Case 6 in Table S2). The other 18 patients underwent AO after interhospital transfer. Overall, approximately 90% (17/19) of AO procedures were performed at tertiary care centers. Only 16% (3/19) of patients received AO before cardiopulmonary arrest. Among patients who underwent AO, a descriptive comparison according to the type of procedure was performed. When cases that received both REBOA and RT‐ACC were classified in the RT‐ACC group, the 24‐h survival rate was 57% (4/7) in the RT‐ACC group and 33% (4/12) in the REBOA group.

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrates that patients who underwent AO were more likely to achieve a hemostatic procedure and had higher 24‐h survival rates, as well as longer time from admission to death. Patients who received AO were more likely to be transferred to a tertiary care center and tended to undergo advanced life‐saving procedures, including extracorporeal membrane oxygenation.

Previous observational studies have reported the characteristics and outcomes of patients with severe PPH requiring REBOA. A Japanese observational study of 143 patients with severe PPH requiring REBOA reported that 53.8% of patients underwent interventional radiology and 16.1% underwent hysterectomy. 14 A Norwegian observational study of 36 patients with severe PPH requiring REBOA reported the use of interventional radiology in 47% of cases and hysterectomy in 44%. 15 These studies suggest that the use of REBOA may contribute to the achieving of definitive hemostatic procedures. 14 , 15 Similarly, our study showed that 69.2% (9/13) of patients with severe PPH requiring REBOA could achieve a definitive hemostatic procedure (Table S1).

The literature on the use of RT‐ACC is limited to studies of placenta accreta spectrum disorders, in which infrarenal ACC was performed. There are few reports focusing on RT‐ACC in the setting of PPH. 16 A distinctive feature of our studies is that we examined patients requiring AO including REBOA as well as RT‐ACC. In our study, all patients (100%, 6/6) requiring RT‐ACC achieved a definitive hemostatic procedure (Table S1).

The major strength of this study is that it directly compares outcomes between patients with severe PPH who required AO and patients who did not. Our results suggest that AO, including both REBOA and RT‐ACC, may be associated with a higher likelihood of achieving definitive hemostasis, improved 24‐h survival, and longer time from admission to death.

Given the rarity of maternal death in Japan, the use of a nationwide registration system provides a valuable opportunity to comprehensively capture and analyze fatal cases at a national level within a highly developed healthcare system.

However, the higher proportion of transfer to tertiary care centers in the AO group suggests that improved short‐term survival may be influenced not only by AO itself but also by access to advanced multidisciplinary care.

To date, only descriptive studies have investigated the characteristics and outcomes of severe PPH patients requiring REBOA, and no previous studies have directly compared outcomes between patients with and without AO, including comparisons between those with and without REBOA.

One possible issue may be the timing of AO. Although the time from delivery to cardiopulmonary arrest depended on the primary cause of hemorrhagic shock (Table S1), 84.2% (16/19) of patients received AO after the onset of cardiopulmonary arrest. Its optimal timing is considered to be before cardiopulmonary arrest 17 ; therefore, identifying which patients should receive AO immediately is an important matter for future investigation.

In Japan, approximately 2000 obstetric facilities provide delivery services for about 700 000 births annually, and more than half of all deliveries occur in private clinics, which are typically staffed by one or two obstetricians who have limited access or no access to advanced medical resources such as an intensive care unit. 18 , 19 , 20 Because pregnant women often prioritize accessibility and comfort, many choose these private facilities. As a result, a substantial proportion of severe obstetric complications initially occur in such settings and require transfer to tertiary care centers for advanced management. Therefore, one key clinical issue is the early recognition of a life‐threatening hemorrhage and timely decision making about transfer to a tertiary care center.

In these emergency situations, AO, including RT‐ACC and REBOA, is frequently performed by emergency physicians. 21 Given this background, the decision to implement AO is often made on a case‐by‐case basis depending on institutional resources, available expertise, and the patient's clinical condition, rather than following a standardized national protocol.

This study has several limitations. First, our analysis included only maternal mortality cases, and survivors who underwent AO were not included. Therefore, this study could not directly assess the impact of AO on survival or overall prognosis by comparing outcomes between survivors and non‐survivors. In addition, due to the small sample size, we could not apply a multivariable regression model to assess the independent effect of AO on outcomes. As described above, patients who received AO represent a selected population who were able to be transferred to tertiary care centers and who underwent life‐saving procedures; therefore, the interpretation of the observed outcomes requires care.

Second, our dataset did not include detailed procedural information on AO, such as its duration or the REBOA deployment zone. Therefore, this study was unable to evaluate whether these advanced procedures were performed optimally. Further multicentered studies are warranted, including both survivors and non‐survivors, as well as more detailed AO data, to validate our findings.

In conclusion, the present study is the first nationwide analysis of AO use among maternal deaths in Japan, and its clinical significance is substantial. To reduce hemorrhage‐related maternal mortality, AO (including both REBOA and RT‐ACC) has to be considered for patients with life‐threatening PPH, particularly when applied before the onset of cardiopulmonary arrest.

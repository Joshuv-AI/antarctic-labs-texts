CISSIR : Beam Codebooks with Self-Interference Reduction Guarantees for Integrated Sensing and Communication Beyond 5G
Rodrigo Hernangómez, Jochen Fink, Renato Luís Garrido Cavalcante, and Sławomir Stańczak

Abstract
We propose a beam codebook design for integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) that reduces self-interference (SI) to alleviate analog distortion. Our optimization framework, which considers either tapered beamforming or phased arrays for both analog and hybrid schemes, modifies given reference codebooks such that a certain SI power level is achieved. In contrast to other low- SI codebooks, which often rely on hardly interpretable optimization parameters, we provide design guidelines to obtain sensing performance guarantees by deriving analytical bounds on saturation and analog-to-digital quantization in relation to the multipath SI level. By selecting standard reference codebooks in our simulations, we show how our method substantially improves the signal-to-noise ratio for sensing with little impact on 5G- NR communication.

II-B

The optimization problem

Given a pair of

TX
/
RX
beam codebooks

{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal{W}^{L^{\prime}}
and
{\mathbf{B}}\in\mathcal{C}^{K^{\prime}}
,
which we assume optimal for

TDD
codebook-based communication,
we aim to derive
a codebook pair
(
{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{C}}
) that guarantees
a certain sensing quality
in the presence of
SI
.
We assess sensing quality via the
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
,
which directly determines
detection and estimation performance
[
9
]
.
In practice,

SI
and
SNR
are linked
through the quantization noise

\sigma_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}\coloneqq{\mathbb{E}}[\left|{u_{k}[i]}\right|^{2}]
,
as we show in
Section

IV
.
In particular,
Lemma

1
therein
allows
to bound
\sigma_{\mathrm{Q}}
, up to a scaling factor,
by the
maximum SI (max. SI)

\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathbf{S}}}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{W}})
, which
we define for
{\mathbf{S}}(t)

(
\forall\,{\mathbf{C}}\in\mathcal{C}^{K^{\prime}}
,
\forall\,{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathcal{W}^{L^{\prime}}
)
as

\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathbf{S}}}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{W}})\coloneqq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in[K^{\prime}],\\
\ell\in[L^{\prime}]\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{T_{\mathrm{c}}}\left|{{\mathbf{c}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{S}}(t){\mathbf{w}}_{\ell}}\right|\,dt\,.

(5)

Therefore, we choose to limit the

SI
-induced
quantization noise
by imposing a certain
target
SI

{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathbf{S}}}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{W}})\leq\varepsilon
.
We note that (
5
) does not depend on the

TX
signal

{\mathbf{x}}(t)
or the beam-selection mappings
\imath
and
\jmath
, since these may be unknown during codebook design.

Moreover, we wish to optimize communication
by minimizing
\sigma_{\mathrm{tx}}^{2}
, the deviation of
the
ISAC

TX
codebook
{\mathbf{W}}
from the
communication-optimal
codebook
{\mathbf{A}}
.
We also aim to minimize the deviation
\sigma_{\mathrm{rx}}^{2}
between
{\mathbf{B}}

and the
RX
codebook
{\mathbf{C}}
,
which could be potentially used
for standard

communication.
Additionally, designing (
{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{C}}
) close to (
{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}
)
may improve the sensing

,
since practical communication codebooks usually maximize the

TX
/
RX
gain across a grid of angular directions
[
24
,
25
]
.
In other words,

\sigma_{\mathrm{tx}}^{2}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{rx}}^{2}

tends to increase the

for a target at the direction given by the steering vectors

{\mathbf{a}}_{\theta}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
,

{\mathbf{b}}_{\theta}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{M}
,
and for certain unknown beam indices

.

In line with the literature

,
18
]
, we

with the Frobenius norm, i.e.:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{tx}}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\|{{\mathbf{W}}-{\mathbf{A}}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}/\underbrace{\left\|{{\mathbf{A}}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}_{=L^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{L^{\prime}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L^{\prime}}\left\|{{\mathbf{w}}_{\ell}-{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{rx}}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\|{{\mathbf{C}}-{\mathbf{B}}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}/\underbrace{\left\|{{\mathbf{B}}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}_{=K^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{K^{\prime}}\sum_{k=1}^{K^{\prime}}\left\|{{\mathbf{c}}_{k}-{\mathbf{b}}_{k}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\,.

Hence, the design of (
{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{C}}
)
can be posed as the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{{{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathcal{W}^{L^{\prime}},\,{\mathbf{C}}\in\mathcal{C}^{K^{\prime}}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}

\displaystyle\left\|{{\mathbf{W}}-{\mathbf{A}}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\omega\left\|{{\mathbf{C}}-{\mathbf{B}}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

(7a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathbf{S}}}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{W}})\leq{\varepsilon}\,,

(7b)

with
\omega>0
a trade-off parameter
between
TX
and
RX
codebook deviation.

Eq.

7
inverts the approach taken
in
[
17
,
18
]
,
where the (flat-fading)
SI
is minimized under a heuristically tuned maximum codebook deviation
constraint

\sigma_{\mathrm{cb}}^{2}\geq\max\{\sigma_{\mathrm{tx}}^{2},\sigma_{\mathrm{rx}}^{2}\}
.
In contrast to this,

Eq.

7
enables both
the tuning of
\varepsilon
according to sensing metrics
(cf.
Section

IV
), and the mitigation of
multipath
SI
, via
\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathbf{S}}}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{W}})
.

III
Proposed scheme

Problem (
7
) is nonconvex
because of the constraint sets
\mathcal{C}^{K^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{W}^{L^{\prime}}
, and also due to the coupling of the codebooks

and
{\mathbf{W}}

In order to derive a more tractable problem, we propose a decoupling of

{\mathbf{W}}
.
The resulting problem, albeit still nonconvex,
can be solved optimally for the case of tapered beamforming and phased arrays,
as we detail in

.

As a preliminary step,
we note that we can modify (
7a

1

to write

7

equivalent form:

\displaystyle\underset{{{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathcal{W}^{L^{\prime}},\,{\mathbf{C}}\in\mathcal{C}^{K^{\prime}}}}{\mathrm{maximize}}

Lemma

\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathbf{S}}}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{W}})

OFDM
radar
to extend our performance analysis
to the sensing
SNR

sensing detection and estimation
[

]

Under analog beamforming, we have
K=L=1

RF
chains.
As a result, we only transmit a signal
{\mathbf{x}}(t)=x(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and
we receive
{\mathbf{y}}(t)=y(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}
as:

y(t)=((\underbrace{{\mathbf{c}}_{\circ}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{w}}_{\circ}}_{\eqqcolon h_{\mathrm{si}}}+\underbrace{{\mathbf{c}}_{\circ}^{\mathsf{H}}{{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{r}}}{\mathbf{w}}_{\circ}}_{\eqqcolon h_{\mathrm{r}}})\ast x)(t)=y_{\mathrm{si}}(t)+y_{\mathrm{r}}(t)\,,

where
{\mathbf{c}}_{\circ}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{M}
and
{\mathbf{w}}_{\circ}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
form our selected

TX
-
RX
beamforming pair.
This beamforming pair yields
the single-antenna equivalent channels
h_{\mathrm{si}}(t)
and
h_{\mathrm{r}}(t)
, which we
assume passband filtered according to the
signal bandwidth
B
.

Furthermore, if we employ
OFDM
waveforms, we can assume our

signal to be stationary with almost flat
power spectral density
X(f)

[
6
,
9
]
. In other words, (
\forall f\in[f_{\mathrm{c}}-B/2,f_{\mathrm{c}}+B/2]

, and hence:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\left|{y_{\mathrm{r}}(t)}\right|^{2}]=\zeta\frac{P_{\mathrm{tx}}}{B}\left|{h_{\mathrm{r}}}\right|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{f_{\mathrm{c}}-B/2}^{f_{\mathrm{c}}+B/2}\frac{P_{\mathrm{tx}}}{B}|H_{\mathrm{r}}(f)|^{2}\,df\,,\;

(25)

with
\zeta\approx 1
. Assuming favorable Nyquist conditions

{\mathbb{E}}[\left|{y_{\mathrm{r}}(iT_{\mathrm{S}})\,}\right|^{2}]={\mathbb{E}}[\left|{y_{\mathrm{r}}(t)}\right|^{2}]

1

to bound

\mathrm{SNR}=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[\left|{y_{\mathrm{r}}(t)}\right|^{2}]}{\sigma_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}}\geq\frac{{\zeta}\left|{h_{\mathrm{r}}}\right|_{2}^{2}P_{\mathrm{tx}}/B}{\sigma_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\mathfrak{b}_{\mathrm{Q}}\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathbf{S}}}^{2}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{W}})P_{\mathrm{tx}}\rho_{x}}\,,

\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathbf{S}}}({\mathbf{C}},{\mathbf{W}})

\varepsilon

1.

The radar channel

2.

The
PAPR

\rho_{x}

IV-C

Selection of the target self-interference

To set the target
SI

\varepsilon
in

1
–
2
,
we first suggest fixing a target quantization noise

\sigma_{\star}^{2}
, either from (
26
) if

\left|{h_{\mathrm{r}}}\right|_{2}^{2}
is known, or with respect to
the thermal noise
\sigma_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}
otherwise.
From
Lemma

1

we select the target

{\varepsilon}=\frac{\sigma_{\star}}{\gamma\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}_{\mathrm{Q}}P_{\mathrm{tx}}\bar{\varrho}_{\mathbf{x}}}}\,,

(28)

which guarantees
\sigma_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}\leq\sigma_{\star}^{2}

The estimated
PAPR

\bar{\varrho}_{\mathbf{x}}>0

{\mathbf{x}}(t)
during optimization.
Some options to compute
\bar{\varrho}_{\mathbf{x}}
include:

IV-C
1
Matched optimization

In the best-case scenario where we do know
{\mathbf{x}}(t)
,
we can just choose
\bar{\varrho}_{\mathbf{x}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\rho_{x_{\ell}}
in (
28
) and solve (
11

by
\imath
and
\jmath
.
This option may be relevant if the optimization algorithm is fast enough to be run for every new
TX
symbol, e.g., if it has
a semi-closed form as in
Section

III-B
.

IV-C
2
Average optimization

In certain setups, it might suffice to ensure that the quantization noise variance
is below
\sigma_{\star}^{2}
only for the average
TX
symbol. In this case,
the codebooks can be optimized a priori with

\bar{\varrho}_{\mathbf{x}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}{\mathbb{E}}[\rho_{x_{\ell}}]
.

IV-C
3
Quantile optimization

If a certain noise level must be guaranteed, yet memory and speed limitations rule out the previous methods,
then we can make

.
This
PAPR
estimate controls the probability of
a
TX
symbol to exceed the noise threshold via Markov’s inequality, i.e.:

{\mathrm{Pr}}(\sigma_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}\geq\sigma^{2}_{\star})\leq\alpha\,.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed

codebooks with integral split for self-interference reduction (CISSIR)
as an evolution of the existing beam codebooks
in current mobile networks, both for hybrid and analog schemes.

CISSIR
achieves notable
self-interference (SI)
reduction and thus paves the way for
integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)
beyond 5G.

The proposed methodology allows a systematic optimization of
phased-array and tapered beam codebooks via

semidefinite programming
and spectral decomposition, respectively.
Our framework also provides performance guarantees
regarding multipath
SI
impairments in the analog domain.
Most notably, we have derived an analytical bound on the quantization noise
at the
analog-to-digital converter
, and we have proved its practicality via sensing simulations.
Moreover, our link-level results show how
CISSIR
can reduce

SI
with a small impact on 5G-
NR
-based communication.
Contrary to other available
SI
-reduced beam codebooks,
our proposed method has easily interpretable optimization parameters,
and it outperforms said codebooks for relevant

ISAC
performance indicators.

Future research can introduce other practical aspects into our optimization framework,
such as subarrays,
SI
channel estimation errors, or quantized beamforming.
In doing so, we aim towards the hardware realization of a high dynamic-range
5G-
NR

ISAC
proof of concept.

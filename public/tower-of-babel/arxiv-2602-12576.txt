Capturing the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer index from the lattice Thanks: Preprint number: OU-HET-1300
Shoto Aoki, Hajime Fujita, Hidenori Fukaya, Mikio Furuta, Shinichiroh Matsuo, Tetsuya Onogi, Satoshi Yamaguchi

Abstract
. Using the Wilson Dirac operator in lattice gauge theory with a domain-wall mass term, we construct a discretization of the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer index for domains with compact boundary in a flat torus. We prove that, for sufficiently small lattice spacings, this discretization correctly captures the continuum Atiyah–Patodi–Singer index.

there exist a Hermitian vector bundle
E
on
Y
and a bundle isomorphism from
S|_{I\times Y}
to
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes E
as
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Hermitian vector bundles such that, under this isomorphism,
D
takes the form

(24)

D=c\otimes\partial_{u}+\epsilon\otimes{D_{Y}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{u}+{D_{Y}}\\
-\partial_{u}+{D_{Y}}&0\end{pmatrix},

where the grading of
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes E
is given by
\Gamma\otimes{\rm id}
and
{D_{Y}}\colon C^{\infty}(Y;E)\to C^{\infty}(Y;E)
is a formally selfadjoint, elliptic partial differential operator.
In this paper, we will concentrate on the case when
{D_{Y}}
has no zero eigenvalues, and assume this condition.

Let
C^{\infty}(X_{+};S_{\pm}:P_{D_{Y}}):=\big\{f\in C^{\infty}(X_{+};S_{\pm})\mid P_{D_{Y}}(f|_{Y})=0\big\}
, where
P_{D_{Y}}\colon L^{2}(Y;E)\to L^{2}(Y;E)
denotes the spectral projection onto the span of the eigensections of
{D_{Y}}
with positive eigenvalues.
This is known as the APS boundary condition.
We define the APS index
[
17
]
of
D
by

(25)

\displaystyle\APSindex(D):=

\displaystyle\dim\big(\Ker D_{+}\cap C^{\infty}(X_{+};\left.S_{+}\right|_{X_{+}}:P_{D_{Y}})\big)

(26)

\displaystyle-\dim\big(\Ker D_{-}\cap C^{\infty}(X_{+};\left.S_{-}\right|_{X_{+}}:P_{D_{Y}})\big),

where
D_{\pm}=DP_{\pm}
with the projection operator
P_{\pm}=(\id\pm\Gamma)/2
.

4.2.
Domain-wall fermion Dirac operators

Let
X
be a closed oriented even dimensional Riemannian manifold.
Let
S
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Hermitian vector bundle on
X
with
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading operator
\gamma
.
Let
D\colon C^{\infty}(X;S)\to C^{\infty}(X;S)
be an odd, first-order,
formally selfadjoint, elliptic partial differential operator.
Let
Y\subset X
be a separating submanifold that decomposes
X
into the union of two compact manifolds

X_{+}
and
X_{-}
which share the common boundary
Y
.
Let
\kappa:X\to[-1,1]
be an
L^{\infty}
-function such that
\kappa\equiv\pm 1
on
X_{\pm}\setminus Y
.
We define the domain-wall fermion Dirac operator by

(27)

D-m\kappa\gamma,

where the mass parameter
m
takes a positive value.

We also introduce a one-parameter family
\kappa_{t}
with
t\in(-\infty,+\infty)
,
such that

(28)

\kappa_{t}=\begin{cases}\kappa\ (t\geq 1)\\
-1\ (t\leq-1)\end{cases}

where we assume that
\kappa_{t}
is smooth with respect to
t
in the range
I:=[-1,1]
.
The associated one-parameter family of the massive Dirac operator is given by

(29)

D-m\kappa_{t}\gamma.

Later assuming
D-m\kappa_{t}\gamma
is invertible at
t=\pm 1
,
we consider the spectral flow

(30)

\mathrm{sf}[D-m\kappa_{t}\gamma]

which is an element of the
K^{1}(I,\partial I)
group,
where
\partial I
is the boundary of
I
which consists of two end points
\{-1,1\}
.
The definitions of the group
K^{1}(I,\partial I)
and the associated
spectral flow will be given in the later sections.

In physics, it is known that the domain-wall fermion Dirac operator
has the so-called “edge states”, which are low eigenstates whose amplitude is exponentially localized at
the domain-wall satisfying a different “boundary condition”
from the APS boundary condition introduced in the previous section.
In order to see this, let us consider
\mathbb{R}\times Y
instead of a closed manifold
X
,
and a Hermitian vector bundle
E
on
Y
. We denote the coordinate of
\mathbb{R}
by
u
.
Let
\bar{S}=\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes E
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Hermitian vector bundle on
\mathbb{R}\times Y

with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading operator
\gamma=\Gamma\otimes\id_{E}
.
Let
\kappa_{0}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a sign function such that
\kappa_{0}(\pm u)=\pm 1
for
u>0
.
Let
\bar{D}\colon C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times Y;\bar{S})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times Y;\bar{S})

be an odd, first-order, formally selfadjoint, elliptic partial
differential operator.
The corresponding domain-wall fermion Dirac operator, which is essentially selfadjoint on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times Y;\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes E)
, takes the form

(31)

\bar{D}-m\kappa_{0}\gamma=c\otimes\partial_{u}+\epsilon\otimes{D_{Y}}-m\kappa_{0}\Gamma\otimes\id_{E}=\begin{pmatrix}-m\kappa_{0}\id_{E}&\partial_{u}+{D_{Y}}\\
-\partial_{u}+{D_{Y}}&+m\kappa_{0}\id_{E}\end{pmatrix},

where
{D_{Y}}\colon C^{\infty}(Y;E)\to C^{\infty}(Y;E)
is a
formally selfadjoint, elliptic partial differential operator.
The edge-states are defined as vectors in the
intersection of the kernel of
c\otimes\partial_{u}-m\kappa_{0}\Gamma\otimes\id_{E}
and the
eigenspace of
\epsilon\otimes{D_{Y}}
.
Since
c\otimes\partial_{u}-m\kappa_{0}\Gamma\otimes\id_{E}
and
\epsilon\otimes{D_{Y}}

anti-commute, each edge-state has the form

(32)

v_{-}\otimes\psi_{\lambda_{D_{Y}}}\exp(-m|u|),

for an eigensection
\psi_{\lambda_{D_{Y}}}
of
{D_{Y}}
with the eigenvalue
\lambda_{D_{Y}}
and

v_{-}=(1/\sqrt{2},-1/\sqrt{2})^{T}
(
T
denotes the transpose).
The
L^{2}
-condition for edge-state implies
\epsilon v_{-}=-v_{-}
.
It is also important to note that the condition
\epsilon v_{-}=-v_{-}

is different from the APS boundary condition.

4.3.
The domain-wall Dirac operators and the APS index

In this subsection, we discuss a nontrivial relation between the

We consider the same

Here as in the subsection

We pull back the bundle

*Proposition 3.11 indicates that

Therefore, the only boundary

Following the standard argument, we can show

from

4.4.
Domain-wall Dirac operators on a flat torus

In this work, we consider a

-dimensional square lattice
with periodic or antiperiodic boundary conditions.
Therefore, the manifold

is limited to a flat torus only.

The domain-wall between

and

can be any

-dimensional

.
With this curved domain-wall, a spin connection and nontrivial curvature

is induced as shown in

In our main theorem in which we compare the spectral flow of

5.
K
-groups and unbounded selfadjoint operators

In this paper, we compare the spectral flow of continuum Dirac operators with that of lattice Dirac operators as elements of
K
-theory.
For this purpose, it is desirable to have a definition of
K
-groups and
KO
-groups that allows one to treat unbounded and bounded selfadjoint operators on an equal footing.
Accordingly, in this section, we present a self-contained definition of
K
-groups and
KO
-groups of arbitrary degree using unbounded selfadjoint operators, and prove that it is naturally isomorphic to the standard definition formulated in terms of bounded selfadjoint operators.
Although there are several possible choices of topology on the space of unbounded selfadjoint operators, we adopt the Riesz topology.
Related work in this direction includes that of Lesch
[
32
]
.
A detailed treatment of these topics will appear in a forthcoming paper.

We note that neither the suspension isomorphism nor Bott periodicity is proved in this section.
In later sections, we use the suspension isomorphism, e.g.
K^{0}(\mathit{pt})\cong K^{1}([0,1],\{0,1\})
, via the isomorphism established here by identifying it with the corresponding suspension isomorphism in the standard
K
-theory or
KO
-theory.

For notational simplicity, we explain only the definition of
K
-groups; the modification to
KO
-groups is straightforward.

We will construct two abelian semigroups, for each
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
,

(38)

K_{\mathrm{bounded}}^{p,q}(X,A)\text{ and }K_{\mathrm{Riesz}}^{p,q}(X,A)

for a compact Hausdorff space
X
and a closed subset
A\subset X
.
We will show in Section
5.4
that both semigroups are in fact groups.

K_{\mathrm{bounded}}^{p,q}(X,A)
, defined using bounded selfadjoint operators, is naturally identified with the standard
K^{n}(X,A)
for
n=p-q
.

K_{\mathrm{Riesz}}^{p,q}(X,A)
is defined using unbounded selfadjoint operators equipped with the Riesz topology.
In Theorem
23
, we will prove that
K_{\mathrm{bounded}}^{p,q}(X,A)
and
K_{\mathrm{Riesz}}^{p,q}(X,A)
are canonically isomorphic.

5.1.
Definitions of
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}
-cocycles

We begin by recalling the definition of the Riesz topology.
Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}(H)
denote the space of bounded operators on
H
equipped with the norm topology
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)\subset\mathcal{B}(H)
denote the space of bounded selfadjoint operators on
H
.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
denote the space of densely defined unbounded selfadjoint operators on
H
.

In this paper, all unbounded operators are assumed to be densely defined, and we use the term “unbounded operator" to mean a possibly unbounded operator.
Moreover, the term “Hilbert space" is understood to include finite-dimensional vector spaces equipped with a Hermitian inner product; the same convention applies to Hilbert bundles.

We define the Riesz transform
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)\to\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
via functional calculus associated with the homeomorphism

(39)

T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}\colon\mathbb{R}\to(-1,1),\quad\lambda\mapsto\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}.

The Riesz topology on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
is defined as the pullback of the norm topology on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
via
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}
.
We note that
h\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
is in
\Image(T_{\mathrm{Riesz}})
if and only if
\lVert h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
and
\Kernel(h\pm\id_{H})=\{0\}
.

Let
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
.
The Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p}
is the
\ast
-algebra over
\mathbb{R}
generated by

(40)

\{\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{q},e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{p}\}

subject to the anticommutation relations

(41)

\displaystyle\{\epsilon_{k},\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}\}=2\delta_{k,k^{\prime}},\quad\{e_{l},e_{l^{\prime}}\}=-2\delta_{l,l^{\prime}},

(42)

\displaystyle\{\epsilon_{k},e_{l}\}=0,

(43)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}^{*}=\epsilon_{k},\quad e_{l}^{*}=-e_{l}

for
k,k^{\prime}=0,1,\dots,q
and
l,l^{\prime}=1,2,\dots,p
.
Note our convention for
p
and
q
:
q
starts from
-1
, and the order of
p
and
q
in the Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p}
is reversed.
We adopt the convention that, when

, there are no generators
\epsilon_{k}
.
We denote by
\mathrm{Hom}^{*}(C_{0},C_{1})
the set of
\ast
-homomorphisms between
\ast
-algebras
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
.

Definition 4
.

We define

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}^{p,q}(H):=\big\{(c,h)\in\mathrm{Hom}^{*}\big(\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p},\mathcal{B}(H)\big)\times\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)\bigm|\heartsuit\big\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}^{p,q}(H):=\big\{(c,h)\in\mathrm{Hom}^{*}\big(\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p},\mathcal{B}(H)\big)\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)\bigm|\heartsuit\big\},

where the condition

means that

(46)

\{c(\epsilon_{k}),h\}=0,\quad\{c(e_{l}),h\}=0

for each
k=0,1,\dots,q
and

.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space.
Let
\mathcal{H}\to X
be a Hilbert bundle over

with fibre a separable Hilbert space over

.
We assume throughout this paper that the structure group of a Hilbert bundle is equipped with the norm topology.
We denote by

.

We define
K
-cocycles as follows.

Definition 8
.

Let
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
.
Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A\subset X
a closed subset.
We use the symbol
\bullet
to stand for either “
\mathrm{Riesz}
” or “
\mathrm{bounded}
”.
We say that a triple
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h)
is a

-cocycle or simply
K
-cocycle if the following four properties are satisfied:

(i)

\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert bundle over
X
with fibre a separable Hilbert space.

(ii)

If
\bullet=\mathrm{bounded}
, then
(c,h)\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}^{p,q}(\mathcal{H})
, and if
\bullet=\mathrm{Riesz}
, then
(c,h)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}^{p,q}(\mathcal{H})
.

(iii)

h_{x}
is Fredholm for each
x\in X
.

(iv)

\Kernel h_{a}=\{0\}
for each
a\in A
.

We write
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
if
\alpha
is a
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}
-cocycle.

Remark 9
.

Recall our convention that the term “unbounded operator” is used to mean a possibly unbounded operator.
Accordingly, a family
h=\{h_{x}\}
may consist of bounded operators at some points and unbounded operators at others, but this causes no difficulty for the notion of continuity defined above.
Recall also our convention that the term “Hilbert space” is understood to include finite-dimensional vector spaces equipped with a Hermitian inner product.
Consequently, the fibres of a Hilbert bundle may be finite-dimensional or infinite-dimensional, possibly varying from one connected component to another.

In the definition of the
K
-group, only the behaviour of the spectrum near zero is essential, and the Fredholm condition precisely ensures that the spectrum near zero consists of isolated eigenvalues with finite multiplicity.
By contrast, the distinction between bounded and unbounded operators concerns the behaviour of the spectrum at infinity, far away from zero, and is therefore completely inessential from the viewpoint of
K
-theory.

Remark 10
.

We note that the collection of all
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}
-cocycles does not form a set; thus, the expression
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
is just a notation, and
\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
is not a set but just a formal expression.
We will discuss set-theoretic issues in Section
5.2
.

We regard a
K
-cocycle
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h)
as a Hilbert bundle equipped with additional structure.
The notions of isomorphism, pullback, and direct sum for
K
-cocycles are defined as follows.

Definition 11
.

Let
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
.

(i)

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and

a closed subset.
Let
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h),\alpha^{\prime}=(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},c^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
An isomorphism
\phi\colon\alpha\to\alpha^{\prime}
is a Hilbert bundle isomorphism
\phi\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that

and

.
If there exists such an isomorphism, we denote
\alpha\cong\alpha^{\prime}
.

(ii)

Let

be compact Hausdorff spaces and
A\subset X
and
B\subset Y
closed subsets.
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous map such that
f(A)\subset B
.
For
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(Y,B)
, the pullback
f^{*}\alpha
is defined to be the triple
(f^{*}\mathcal{H},f^{*}c,f^{*}h)
.
Note that
f^{*}\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.

(iii)

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A,A^{\prime}\subset X
closed subsets.
Let
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
and
\alpha^{\prime}=(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},^{\prime}c^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A^{\prime})
.
The direct sum
\alpha\oplus\alpha^{\prime}
is defined to be
(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{\prime},c\oplus c^{\prime},h\oplus h^{\prime})
.
Note that
\alpha\oplus\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A\cap A^{\prime})
.

5.2.
Set-theoretic techniques

If the collection of
\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}
-cocycles were a set, one could introduce an equivalence relation on it and define
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}
-groups as the corresponding quotient.
Since this is not the case, we proceed as follows.
This is a standard and frequently used technique.
In this paper, since we restrict ourselves to Hilbert bundles whose fibres are separable, we do not use the notion of universes.

Definition 12
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A\subset X
a closed subset.

(i)

Let
S
be a set.
We denote by
\mathrm{Hilbert}(S)
the
set
of all Hilbert bundles over
X
with fibre a separable Hilbert space whose total spaces are subsets of
S
.

(ii)

Let
S
be a set.
We denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)
a set

(52)

\{(\mathcal{H},c,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)\mid\mathcal{H}\in\mathrm{Hilbert}(S)\}.

(iii)

We denote by
\cong_{S}
the relation on the set
\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)
obtained by restricting the isomorphism
\cong
of
\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}
-cocycles.
Note that
\cong_{S}
is an equivalence relation.
For
\alpha_{S}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)
, we denote its equivalence class by
(\alpha_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})
.

We denote by
\lvert S\rvert
the cardinality of a set
S
and by
\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert
the cardinality of the total space of a Hilbert bundle
\mathcal{H}
.
We denote by
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
the standard separable Hilbert space whose standard basis is parametrized by
\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A,A^{\prime}\subset X
be closed subsets.
Let
S
and
T
be sets such that
\lvert S\rvert,\lvert T\rvert\geq\lvert X\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rvert
.

(i)

For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
, there exists
\alpha_{S}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)
such that
\alpha_{S}\cong\alpha
.
Moreover, the equivalence class
(\alpha_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})\in\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong_{S}}\big)
depends only on
\alpha
and
S
.

(ii)

There exists a unique map

(53)

\oplus_{S}\colon\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong_{S}}\big)\times\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A^{\prime})/{\cong_{S}}\big)\to\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A\cap A^{\prime})/{\cong_{S}}\big)

such that, for any
\alpha_{S}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)
,
\beta_{S}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A^{\prime})
, and
\gamma_{S}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A\cap A^{\prime})
, we have

(54)

(\alpha_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})\oplus_{S}(\beta_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})=(\gamma_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})

if and only if
\alpha_{S}\oplus\beta_{S}\cong\gamma_{S}
as
\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}
-cocycles.

(iii)

There exists a unique bijection

(55)

\Phi_{T,S}\colon\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong_{S}}\big)\to\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,T}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong_{T}}\big)

such that, for any
\alpha_{S}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)
and
\alpha_{T}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,T}^{p,q}(X,A)
, we have

(56)

\Phi_{T,S}\big((\alpha_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})\big)=(\alpha_{T}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{T}})

if and only if
\alpha_{S}\cong\alpha_{T}
as
\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}
-cocycles.

(iv)

\oplus_{S}
and
\oplus_{T}
are compatible with
\Phi_{T,S}
, that is, we have

(57)

\Phi_{T,S}\big((\alpha_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})\oplus_{S}(\beta_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})\big)=\big(\Phi_{T,S}\big((\alpha_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})\big)\big)\oplus_{T}\big(\Phi_{T,S}\big((\beta_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})\big)\big)

for any
\alpha_{S}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)
and
\beta_{S}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
Since
\lvert S\rvert\geq\lvert X\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert
, there exists an injection from the total space of
\mathcal{H}
into
S
.
This implies the first assertion.
The remaining assertions follow immediately from the first one.
∎

The existence of the bijection
\Phi_{T,S}
in the third assertion of Lemma
13
suggests, at an informal level, that the set
\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong_{S}}\big)
does not depend on the choice of
S
provided that
\lvert S\rvert\geq\lvert X\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rvert
.
To make this observation precise, we fix such a set
S
for each
X
as follows.

Definition 14
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A,A^{\prime}\subset X
closed subsets.
Set
S_{X}:=X\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

(i)

We define

(58)

\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong}\big):=\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S_{X}}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong_{S_{X}}}\big).

Note that
\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong}\big)
is just a formal expression.

(ii)

We simply write
\oplus
for
\oplus_{S_{X}}
, that is,

(59)

\oplus:=\oplus_{S_{X}}\colon\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong}\big)\times\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A^{\prime})/{\cong}\big)\to\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A\cap A^{\prime})/{\cong}\big).

Definition 15
.

Let
X,Y
be compact Hausdorff spaces and
A\subset X
and
B\subset Y
closed subsets.
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous map such that
f(A)\subset B
.
We define a map

(60)

f^{*}\colon\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(Y,B)/{\cong}\big)\to\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong}\big)

by the pullback defined in Definition
11
, which is well defined by Lemma
13
.
This map
f^{*}
is characterized by the property that

(61)

f^{*}(\beta\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S_{Y}}})=(\alpha\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S_{X}}})

if and only if
f^{*}\beta\cong\alpha
for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
and
\beta\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(Y,B)
.

For
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
, by the first assertion of Lemma
13
, the equivalence class
\alpha_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}}
depends only on
\alpha
; thus, by a slight abuse of language, we also write

(62)

(\alpha\operatorname{mod}{\cong}):=(\alpha_{S}\operatorname{mod}{\cong_{S}})\in\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet,S}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong_{S}}\big),

although, strictly speaking,
(\alpha\operatorname{mod}{\cong})
is just a formal expression.
Then, we can rephrase the definitions above simply as follows:

•

We define

(63)

(\alpha\operatorname{mod}{\cong})\oplus(\alpha^{\prime}\operatorname{mod}{\cong}):=(\alpha\oplus\alpha^{\prime}\operatorname{mod}{\cong})\in\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A\cap A^{\prime})/{\cong}\big)

for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A^{\prime})
.

•

We define

(64)

f^{*}(\beta\operatorname{mod}{\cong}):=\big((f^{*}\beta)\operatorname{mod}{\cong}\big)\in\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong}\big)

for any
\beta\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(Y,B)
.

5.3.
Definition of the semigroup
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}

With these preparations in place, we are finally in a position to define the semigroup
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}
.
Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A\subset X
a closed subset.
Let
i_{0}\colon X\to X\times[0,1]
and
i_{1}\colon X\to X\times[0,1]
be given by
i_{t}(x)=(x,t)
for
t=0,1
.
We use the symbol
\bullet
to stand for either “
\mathrm{Riesz}
” or “
\mathrm{bounded}
”.
Let
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
.

We begin by defining a relation
\sim
on the set
\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong}\big)
.
For
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
, we say that

(65)

(\alpha_{0}\operatorname{mod}{\cong})\sim(\alpha_{1}\operatorname{mod}{\cong})

if and only if there exist
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,X)
and
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X\times[0,1],A\times[0,1])
such that

(66)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{0}\operatorname{mod}{\cong})\oplus(\beta_{0}\operatorname{mod}{\cong})

\displaystyle=i_{0}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha}\operatorname{mod}{\cong})

(67)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1}\operatorname{mod}{\cong})\oplus(\beta_{1}\operatorname{mod}{\cong})

\displaystyle=i_{1}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha}\operatorname{mod}{\cong}).

It can be shown by standard arguments that
\sim
is an equivalence relation.
Using
\sim
, we define the semigroup
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}
as follows.

Definition 16
.

We define

(68)

K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A):=\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong}\big)/{\sim},

and we write

(69)

[\alpha]:=(\alpha\operatorname{mod}{\cong})/{\sim}

for
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.

By definition, every element of
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
is of the form
[\alpha]
for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
Moreover, for
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
, we have
[\alpha_{0}]=[\alpha_{1}]\in K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
if and only if there exist
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,X)
and
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X\times[0,1],A\times[0,1])
such that
\alpha_{0}\oplus\beta_{0}\cong i_{0}^{*}\widetilde{\alpha}
and
\alpha_{1}\oplus\beta_{1}\cong i_{1}^{*}\widetilde{\alpha}
.

By abuse of language, we call
\alpha_{t}\oplus\beta_{t}
a stabilization of
\alpha_{t}
for
t=0,1
, and we say that
i_{0}^{*}\widetilde{\alpha}
and
i_{1}^{*}\widetilde{\alpha}
are homotopic.
With this convention, for
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
, we have
[\alpha_{0}]=[\alpha_{1}]
if and only if they are homotopic after stabilization, in a way analogous to the standard construction.

The direct sum
\oplus
on
\big(\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)/{\cong}\big)
is clearly compatible with the equivalence relation
\sim
, and hence induces a commutative semigroup structure
+
on
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
by

(70)

[\alpha]+[\alpha^{\prime}]:=[\alpha\oplus\alpha^{\prime}]

for any
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
The identity is described as follows.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}
be the trivial product Hilbert bundle
X\times\{0\}
.
For
(\mathcal{H},c,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
, if
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, then
c
and
h
are uniquely determined.
We have

(71)

[\alpha]+[\mathcal{O}_{X},c,h]=[\alpha]\in K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)

for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
We write
0
for
[\mathcal{O}_{X},c,h]
.

5.4.
Group structure on the semigroup
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A\subset X
a closed subset.
In this subsection, we show that the abelian semigroup
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
defined so far is in fact a group.

Let
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
.
Recall that the Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p}
is the
\ast
-algebra over
\mathbb{R}
generated by
\{\epsilon_{0},\dots,\epsilon_{q},e_{1},\dots,e_{p}\}
subject to the relations

(72)

\{\epsilon_{k},\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}\}=2\delta_{k,k^{\prime}},\quad\{e_{l},e_{l^{\prime}}\}=-2\delta_{l,l^{\prime}},\quad\{\epsilon_{k},e_{l}\}=0,\quad\epsilon_{k}^{*}=\epsilon_{k},\quad e_{l}^{*}=-e_{l}.

We define a graded involution
\cliffordGamma\colon\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p}\to\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p}
by

(73)

\cliffordGamma(\epsilon_{k})=-\epsilon_{k},\quad\cliffordGamma(e_{l})=-e_{l}

for
k=0,\dots,q
and
l=1,\dots,p
.
For a
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
and a
\ast
-homomorphism
c\colon\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p}\to\mathcal{B}
, we define
-c:=c\circ\cliffordGamma
; that is,
-c
is a
\ast
-homomorphism characterized by

(74)

(-c)(\epsilon_{k}):=-\big(c(\epsilon_{k})\big),\quad(-c)(e_{l}):=-\big(c(e_{l})\big)

for
k=0,\dots,q
and
l=1,\dots,p
.
Note that
-c
is not the pointwise negative of
c
; rather, the sign depends on the grading.
We define

(75)

-\alpha:=(\mathcal{H},-c,-h)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)

for
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.

The following proposition is not only a basic property of
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
but also serves as a prototype for Proposition
21
, the key argument of this paper.
Accordingly, we include a somewhat detailed explanation.
Such a complicated argument is required because the seemingly trivial continuity of the addition operator

(76)

\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}\times\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}},\quad(A,B)\mapsto A+B

becomes unexpectedly delicate when the space of unbounded selfadjoint operators
\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}
is equipped with the Riesz topology, owing to the nonlinearity of the Riesz transform.
By contrast, arguments are much simpler if one works with the gap topology.
However, working with the gap topology would be somewhat excessive for our purposes, and therefore we choose in this paper to work with the more familiar Riesz topology.

Proposition 17
.

We have

(77)

[\alpha]+[-\alpha]=0

in
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
In particular,
K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
is a group.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
We denote by
\pi\colon X\times[0,1]\to X
the projection onto
X
.
We define the triple
\widetilde{\alpha}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{H}},\widetilde{c},\widetilde{h})
on
X\times[0,1]
by setting

(78)

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}:=\pi^{*}\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\widetilde{c}_{(x,t)}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{x}&0\\
0&-c_{x}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}=\begin{pmatrix}h_{x}&t\\
t&-h_{x}\end{pmatrix}

for
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
.
We first show that
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X\times[0,1],A\times[0,1])
; that is, in either case
\bullet=\mathrm{bounded}\text{ or }\mathrm{Riesz}
, we verify that
\widetilde{c}
and
\widetilde{h}
depend continuously on
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
, that
\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}
is Fredholm for all
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
and that
\Kernel\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}=\{0\}
for each
(x,t)\in A\times[0,1]
.

The continuity of
\widetilde{c}
follows directly from that of
c
.
We show the continuity of
\widetilde{h}
as follows:

\bullet=\mathrm{bounded}
case:

The continuity of
\widetilde{h}
follows directly from that of
h
.

\bullet=\mathrm{Riesz}
case:

Recall that the Riesz transform is defined via
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\lambda)=\lambda/\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}
.
We have

(79)

T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)})=\frac{\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}}{\sqrt{1+\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}^{2}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}+h_{x}^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}h_{x}&t\\
t&-h_{x}\end{pmatrix}

for each
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
.
Then, we have, as
\lambda\to\pm\infty
,

(80)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&t\\
t&-\lambda\end{pmatrix}\longrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&0\\
0&\mp 1\end{pmatrix}

uniformly with respect to
t\in[0,1]
.
Therefore, there exists a continuous map
\phi\colon[-1,1]\times[0,1]\to\mathrm{Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(81)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&t\\
t&-\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\phi\big(T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\lambda),t\big)

for each
(\lambda,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]
.
Consequently, we have

(82)

T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)})=\phi\big(T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(h_{x}),t\big)

for each
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
.
Thus,
\widetilde{h}
depends continuously on
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
.

We next show that
\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}
is Fredholm for
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
and that
\Kernel\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}=\{0\}
for
(x,t)\in A\times[0,1]
.
Fix
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
.
For
t\neq 0
, we have

(83)

\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}h_{x}^{2}+t^{2}&0\\
0&h_{x}^{2}+t^{2}\end{pmatrix}>0.

Hence,
\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}
is invertible.
In particular,
\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}
is Fredholm.
For
t=0
, we have
\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}=h_{x}\oplus(-h_{x})
so the Fredholm property of
\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}
and
\Kernel\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}=\{0\}
for
x\in A
follow.

Now we define

(84)

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}:=\alpha\oplus(-\alpha)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A),\quad\alpha_{1}:=0\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A),

(85)

\displaystyle\beta_{0}:=0\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,X),\quad\beta_{1}:=(\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}^{2},c_{1},h_{1})\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,X),

where

(86)

(c_{1})_{x}:=\begin{pmatrix}c_{x}&0\\
0&c_{x}\end{pmatrix},\quad(h_{1})_{x}:=\begin{pmatrix}h_{x}&1\\
1&-h_{x}\end{pmatrix}

for each
x\in X
.
Then, we have

(87)

\alpha_{0}\oplus\beta_{0}\cong i_{0}^{*}\widetilde{\alpha}\text{ and }\alpha_{1}\oplus\beta_{1}\cong i_{1}^{*}\widetilde{\alpha}.

Consequently, we have
[\alpha\oplus(-\alpha)]=[0]
.
Thus, we have proved that
[\alpha]+[-\alpha]=0
.
∎

5.5.
Degrees

In this subsection, we explain how to assign degrees to
K
-groups,
thereby completing their definition.

Proposition 18
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A\subset X
a closed subset.
There is a natural isomorphism

(88)

K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)\to K_{\bullet}^{p+1,q+1}(X,A)

for each
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
.

Proof.

We define
\epsilon_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\epsilon^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}},e_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
by

(89)

\epsilon_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\epsilon^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad e_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then,
\epsilon_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{2}=(\epsilon^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})^{2}=1
and
e_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{2}=-1
.

We first define a homomorphism

(90)

\delta\colon K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)\to K_{\bullet}^{p+1,q+1}(X,A).

as follows.
For
(\mathcal{H},c,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
, set
\delta([(\mathcal{H},c,h)])=:[(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},c^{\prime},h^{\prime})]
, where

(91)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H},

(92)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}(\epsilon_{k}):=\epsilon_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\otimes c(\epsilon_{k}),\quad c^{\prime}(\epsilon_{q+1}):=\epsilon^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\otimes\id

(93)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}(e_{l}):=\epsilon_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\otimes c(e_{l}),\quad c^{\prime}(e_{p+1}):=e_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\otimes\id,

(94)

\displaystyle h^{\prime}:=\epsilon_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\otimes h

for
k=0,1,\dots,q
and
l=1,2,\dots,p
.
We next define a homomorphism

(95)

\rho\colon K_{\bullet}^{p+1,q+1}(X,A)\to K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A).

as follows.
For
(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},c^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p+1,q+1}(X,A)
, set
\rho([(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},c^{\prime},h^{\prime})])=:[(\mathcal{H},c,h)]
, where

(96)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=\Kernel\big(c^{\prime}(\epsilon_{q+1})c^{\prime}(e_{p+1})-\id\otimes\id\big)\subset\mathcal{H}^{\prime},

(97)

\displaystyle c(\epsilon_{k}):=\left.c^{\prime}(\epsilon_{k})\right|_{\mathcal{H}},\quad c(e_{l}):=\left.c^{\prime}(e_{l})\right|_{\mathcal{H}},

(98)

\displaystyle h:=\left.h^{\prime}\right|_{\mathcal{H}}

for
k=0,1,\dots,q
and
l=1,2,\dots,p
.
Then, one can check that
\delta
and
\rho
are mutually inverse.
∎

This proposition gives rise to a direct system

(99)

\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}K_{\bullet}^{p+1,q+1}(X,A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}K_{\bullet}^{p+2,q+2}(X,A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}\dots

and, using this system, we finally define the
K
-groups.

Definition 19
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A\subset X
a closed subset.
We define the abelian group

as the direct limit

(100)

K_{\bullet}^{n}(X,A):=\varinjlim\big\{\dots\to K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)\to K_{\bullet}^{p+1,q+1}(X,A)\to\cdots\big\}_{p-q=n}

.

5.6.
Riesz continuous families

In this subsection, we present a criterion for Riesz continuity of families of unbounded selfadjoint operators, which will be used later.

We define the Riesz-continuity of a family of unbounded selfadjoint operators as follows.
See Definition
6
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert bundle over a compact Hausdorff space
X
.
Let
x_{0}\in X
, and let
U
be an open neighbourhood of
x_{0}
and
\phi\colon U\times\mathcal{H}_{x_{0}}\cong\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U}
a local trivialisation of
\mathcal{H}
over
U
.
For each
x\in U
, we let

(101)

\phi^{*}_{x}\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(\mathcal{H}_{x})\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(\mathcal{H}_{x_{0}})

be the bijection induced by
\phi
.
Let
h=\{h_{x}\}_{x\in X}
be a family of
h_{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(\mathcal{H}_{x})
parametrized by
x\in X
.
We define a map
F_{\phi}^{\mathrm{Riesz}}\colon U\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{x_{0}})
by

(102)

x\mapsto T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\phi^{*}_{x}h_{x}).

We say that
h
is Riesz-continuous at
x_{0}
if
F_{\phi}^{\mathrm{Riesz}}
is continuous at
x_{0}
with respect to the norm topology on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{x_{0}})
, and that
h
is Riesz-continuous if
h
is Riesz-continuous at each
x_{0}\in X
.

The following proposition was shown implicitly by Lesch
[
32
]
*PROPOSITION 2.2 using the theory of operator-monotonic increasing functions, the key point being the operator monotonicity of the square root
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}
.
We give an alternative argument based on the theory of
operator-Lipschitz
functions.
The key observation here is that the Riesz transform is operator-Lipschitz.

For the theory of operator-Lipschitz functions, we refer the reader to the survey paper
[
38
]
and the references therein; for the reader’s convenience, we briefly collect here the results that are needed in the sequel.
A function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is called operator-Lipschitz if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(103)

\lVert f(A)-f(B)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\lVert A-B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

for any (possibly unbounded) selfadjoint operator
A
and
B
on a Hilbert space for which
A-B
is bounded.
By
[
38
]
*Theorem 1.4.4, a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is operator-Lipschitz if
f
belongs to the Besov space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
.
The theory of Besov spaces is also neatly summarized in
[
38
]
*Section 2.1.
Now
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}\in B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus,
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}
is operator-Lipschitz.

Proposition 20
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert bundle over
X
.
Let
h=\{h_{x}\}_{x\in X}
be a family of unbounded selfadjoint operators
h_{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(\mathcal{H}_{x})
parametrized by
x\in X
.
We assume that, for each
x_{0}\in X
, there exist an open neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
and
\phi\colon U\times\mathcal{H}_{x_{0}}\to\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U}
such that
\phi=\id
at
x_{0}
, that the operator
\phi^{*}_{x}h_{x}-h_{x_{0}}
extends to a bounded operator
m_{x}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{x_{0}})
for each
x\in U
, and that the operator-valued map

(104)

U\ni x\mapsto m_{x}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{x_{0}})

is continuous with respect to the norm topology on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{x_{0}})
.
Then, the family
\{h_{x}\}
is continuous with respect to the Riesz topology.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in X
and an open neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
as in the assumption of the proposition.
Since
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}
is operator-Lipschitz, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(105)

\lVert T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\phi^{*}_{x}h_{x})-T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(h_{x_{0}})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\lVert\phi^{*}_{x}h_{x}-h_{x_{0}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=C\lVert m_{x}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

for any
x\in U
.
Since
\phi=\id
at
x_{0}
, we have
m_{x_{0}}=0
.
Moreover,
x\mapsto m_{x}
is norm-continuous at
x_{0}
by assumption.
Thus, by the above inequality,
x\mapsto T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\phi^{*}_{x}h_{x})
is norm-continuous at
x_{0}
.
Consequently, the family
\{h_{x}\}
is Riesz-continuous.
∎

5.7.
The inverse element

In this subsection, we explain a method for showing that
[\alpha\oplus(-\alpha)]=0
in the
K
-group for a
K
-cocycle
\alpha
.

We first fix the notation.
For a (possibly unbounded) selfadjoint operator
h
on a Hilbert space
H
, we write its spectral decomposition as

h=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\,dE_{h}(\lambda),

where
E_{h}
denotes the spectral measure associated with
h
.
We denote by
\spectrum(h)
the spectrum of
h
.
We also define

(106)

\displaystyle H_{h<-\lambda_{0}}:=\Image E_{h}\big(\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\lambda<-\lambda_{0}\}\big),\quad H_{h>\lambda_{0}}:=\Image E_{h}\big(\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\lambda>\lambda_{0}\}\big),

(107)

\displaystyle H_{\lvert h\rvert<\lambda_{0}}:=\Image E_{h}\big(\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\lvert\lambda\rvert<\lambda_{0}\}\big),\quad H_{\lvert h\rvert>\lambda_{0}}:=\Image E_{h}\big(\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\lvert\lambda\rvert>\lambda_{0}\}\big)

for
\lambda_{0}>0
.
We adopt the same notation for families of selfadjoint operators on Hilbert bundles.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert bundle over a compact Hausdorff space
X
and
h
be a Riesz-continuous family of unbounded selfadjoint operators.
Fix
x_{0}\in X
.
For any
\Lambda>0
, there exists
\lambda_{0}\in(0,\Lambda)
such that
\pm\lambda_{0}\notin\spectrum(h_{x_{0}})
.
Fix such a
\lambda_{0}
.
Then, there exists an open neighbourhood
U_{0}
of
x_{0}
such that
\pm\lambda_{0}\notin\spectrum(h_{x})
for any
x\in U_{0}
.
Thus, we obtain an orthogonal decomposition

(108)

\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U_{0}}=\left(\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U_{0}}\right)_{h<-\lambda_{0}}\oplus\left(\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U_{0}}\right)_{\lvert h\rvert<\lambda_{0}}\oplus\left(\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U_{0}}\right)_{h>\lambda_{0}}

by the Riesz continuity of
h
.

The following proposition contains the key argument of this section; its prototype appears as Proposition
17
.

Proposition 21
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A\subset X
a closed subset.
Let
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
.
Let
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h),\alpha^{\prime}=(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},c^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
Suppose that there exist
\lambda_{0}>0
and a family
f=\{f_{x}\}_{x\in X}
of bounded operators

(109)

f_{x}\colon\mathcal{H}_{x}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{x}

parametrized by
x\in X
that satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

For each
x\in X
, the spectra
(\spectrum(h_{x})\cap[-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}])
and
(\spectrum(h^{\prime}_{x})\cap[-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}])
consist only of isolated eigenvalues with finite multiplicity.

(ii)

The family
x\mapsto f_{x}
is continuous with respect to the norm topology.

(iii)

For each
x\in X
, we have
f_{x}\circ c_{x}(g)=c^{\prime}_{x}(g)\circ f_{x}
for
g\in\mathrm{Cl}^{q+1,p}
.

(iv)

For each
x\in X
, we have
f_{x}\circ h_{x}=h^{\prime}_{x}\circ f_{x}
.

(v)

For each
x\in X
, the composition

(110)

\left(E_{h^{\prime}_{x}}([-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}])\right)\circ f_{x}\circ\left(E_{h_{x}}([-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}])\right)\colon\Image E_{h_{x}}([-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}])\to\Image E_{h^{\prime}_{x}}([-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}])

is a unitary isomorphism.

Then, we have
[\alpha^{\prime}\oplus(-\alpha)]=0\in K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.
In particular,
[\alpha^{\prime}]=[\alpha]\in K_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=(\mathcal{H},c,h),\alpha^{\prime}=(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},c^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)
satisfy the above assumptions.
We will show that
\alpha^{\prime}\oplus(-\alpha)\sim 0
.

Let
\xi_{\lambda_{0}}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an even continuous function (see Figure
2
) defined as

(111)

\xi_{\lambda_{0}}=\begin{cases}(1/3)\lambda_{0}&\text{ if }\lvert\lambda\rvert\leq(1/3)\lambda_{0}\\
(2/3)\lambda_{0}-\lvert\lambda\rvert&\text{ if }(1/3)\lambda_{0}<\lvert\lambda\rvert<(2/3)\lambda_{0}\\
0&\text{ if }\lvert\lambda\rvert\geq(2/3)\lambda_{0}\end{cases}

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and set

(112)

(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x}:=\xi_{\lambda_{0}}(h^{\prime}_{x})\circ f_{x}\circ\xi_{\lambda_{0}}(h_{x})\colon\mathcal{H}_{x}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{x}

for each
x\in X
.
Then,
(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x}
is a finite-rank operator and
\lVert(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1/3
.

Figure 2.

The function
\xi_{\lambda_{0}}(\lambda)
normalized by
\lambda_{0}
.

We define the triple
\widetilde{\alpha}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{H}},\widetilde{c},\widetilde{h})
on
X\times[0,1]
by setting

(113)

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}:=\pi^{*}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\oplus\pi^{*}\mathcal{H},\quad\widetilde{c}_{(x,t)}=\begin{pmatrix}c^{\prime}_{x}&0\\
0&-c_{x}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}=\begin{pmatrix}h^{\prime}_{x}&t(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x}\\
t(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})^{*}_{x}&-h_{x}\end{pmatrix}

for
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
, where
\pi\colon X\times[0,1]\to X
is the projection onto
X
.

We first show that
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X\times[0,1],A\times[0,1])
; that is, in either case
\bullet=\mathrm{bounded}\text{ or }\mathrm{Riesz}
, we verify that
\widetilde{c}
and
\widetilde{h}
depend continuously on
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
and that
\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}
is Fredholm at each
(x,t)\in X\times[0,1]
.

The continuity of
\widetilde{c}
follows directly from that of
c
.
We show the continuity of
\widetilde{h}
as follows:

\bullet=\mathrm{bounded}
case:

The continuity of
\widetilde{h}
follows from those of
h
and
t(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})
.

\bullet=\mathrm{Riesz}
case:

Fix
(x_{0},t_{0})\in X\times[0,1]
.
Let
\lambda_{1}
be a constant that satisfies

(114)

\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{0},\,\pm\lambda_{1}\notin\spectrum(h_{x_{0}}),\text{ and }\pm\lambda_{1}\notin\spectrum(h^{\prime}_{x_{0}})

For
t=0
, the Riesz-continuity of the family
(x,0)\mapsto\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}
follows directly from that of
x\mapsto h^{\prime}_{x}
and
x\mapsto-h_{x}
.
Therefore, we have an orthogonal decomposition

(115)

\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U_{0}\times[0,1]}=\left(\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U_{0}\times[0,1]}\right)_{\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}<-\lambda_{1}}\oplus\left(\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U_{0}\times[0,1]}\right)_{\lvert\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}\rvert<\lambda_{1}}\oplus\left(\left.\mathcal{H}\right|_{U_{0}\times[0,1]}\right)_{\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}>\lambda_{1}}.

By the choice of
\xi_{\lambda_{0}}
and
\lambda_{1}
, for
t>0
, the difference
\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}-\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}
preserves the above decomposition.
Hence, the Riesz transform
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)})
preserves the decomposition.
Since its restriction on the second summand is a finite-rank operator, it is continuous at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Moreover, since the difference
\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}-\widetilde{h}_{(x,0)}
acts trivially on the first and the third summands, the continuity of the restriction follows from those of
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(h^{\prime})
and
T_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(h)
.
Thus,
\widetilde{h}
is Riesz-continuous at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.

We next show that
\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}
is Fredholm for each
(x_{0},t_{0})\in X\times[0,1]
.
For
t_{0}=0
, the Fredholm property of
\widetilde{h}_{(x,t)}
follows directly from that of

and
h_{x}
.
For
t_{0}\neq 0
, we consider the decomposition

(116)

\mathcal{H}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}=\left(\mathcal{H}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}\right)_{\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}<-\lambda_{1}}\oplus\left(\mathcal{H}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}\right)_{\lvert\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}\rvert<\lambda_{1}}\oplus\left(\mathcal{H}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}\right)_{\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}>\lambda_{1}}.

It suffices to verify the Fredholm property on each summand.
On the first and third summands, we have
\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}=h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}\oplus(-h_{x_{0}})
, which is Fredholm.
The restriction to the second summand is a finite-rank operator, hence is Fredholm.

We next show that
\Kernel\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}=\{0\}
for each
(x_{0},t_{0})\in A\times[0,1]
.
For
t_{0}=0
, we have
\widetilde{h}_{x_{0},0}=h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}\oplus(-h_{x_{0}})
, and, hence,
\Kernel\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},0)}=\{0\}
.
Suppose that
t_{0}\neq 0
.
Let
v^{\prime}\oplus v\in\Kernel\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}
.
Then, we have

(117)

\displaystyle h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}+t_{0}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}v=0,

(118)

\displaystyle t_{0}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})^{*}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}-h_{x_{0}}v=0.

(119)

\displaystyle(h^{\prime}_{x_{0}})^{2}v^{\prime}+t_{0}h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}v=0,

(120)

\displaystyle t_{0}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})^{*}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}-(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}h_{x_{0}}v=0.

Since
(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}\circ h_{x_{0}}=h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}\circ(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}
, the second equation implies

(121)

t_{0}h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}v=t_{0}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}h_{x_{0}}v=t_{0}^{2}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})^{*}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}.

Hence, we have

(122)

(h^{\prime}_{x_{0}})^{2}v^{\prime}+t_{0}^{2}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})_{x_{0}}(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})^{*}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}=0.

Thus, we have
h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}=0
and
(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})^{*}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}=0
.
Then,
h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}=0
implies

(123)

v^{\prime}\in E_{h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}(\{0\}).

On the other hand, since
(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})^{*}_{x_{0}}=\xi_{\lambda_{0}}(h)\circ f^{*}_{x_{0}}\circ\xi_{\lambda_{0}}(h^{\prime})
and
f^{*}
is an isomorphism,
(\psi_{h,h^{\prime},\lambda_{0}})^{*}_{x_{0}}v^{\prime}=0
implies

(124)

v^{\prime}\in E_{h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}\big(\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\xi_{\lambda_{0}}=0\}\big).

By the definition of
\xi_{\lambda_{0}}
, we have
\{0\}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\xi_{\lambda_{0}}=0\}=\emptyset
.
Thus,
v^{\prime}\in E_{h^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}(\emptyset)=\{0\}
.
In the same way, we have
v=0
.
Consequently, we have proved that
\Kernel\widetilde{h}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}=\{0\}
.

Now we define

(125)

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}:=\alpha^{\prime}\oplus(-\alpha)\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A),\quad\alpha_{1}:=0\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,A)

\displaystyle\beta_{0}:=0\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,X),\quad\beta_{1}:=(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\oplus\mathcal{H},c_{1},h_{1})\in\mathcal{K}_{\bullet}^{p,q}(X,X),

.

.
Consequently, we have
[\alpha^{\prime}\oplus(-\alpha)]=[0]
.

.
∎

5.8.
Isomorphism
K_{\mathrm{bounded}}^{\ast}\cong K_{\mathrm{Riesz}}^{\ast}

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
A\subset X
a closed subset.
Let
p=0,1,2,\dots
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
.
In this subsection, we finally establish the isomorphism
K_{\mathrm{bounded}}^{p,q}(X,A)\cong K_{\mathrm{Riesz}}^{p,q}(X,A)
.

We note that, if
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded continuous function and
h=\{h_{x}\}_{x\in X}
is a norm-continuous family of bounded selfadjoint operators
h_{x}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}(\mathcal{H}_{x})
on a Hilbert bundle
\mathcal{H}
over
X
, then the family
f(h):=\{f(h_{x})\}
is also norm-continuous.

We define a homomorphism

(130)

\bddtoRiesz\colon K_{\mathrm{bounded}}^{p,q}(X,A)\to K_{\mathrm{Riesz}}^{p,q}(X,A)

by simply sending
[\alpha]\mapsto[\alpha]
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bounded}}^{p,q}(X,A)
, which is clearly well defined.

To define the inverse, we introduce an auxiliary function
\TRtohalf\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by setting

(131)

\TRtohalf(\lambda):=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }\lambda\leq-\frac{1}{2}\\
\lambda&\text{ if }\lvert\lambda\rvert<\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }\lambda\geq\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
This function is bounded and continuous.
We then define a homomorphism

(132)

5.9.
Gap topology

In this final subsection, although somewhat tangential to the main line of argument, we state a proposition that plays an essential role in defining
K
-groups using the gap topology.
The use of the gap topology will be necessary when we generalize our main theorem (Theorem
30
) to families.
In Proposition
21
, the assumption that
\phi=\id
at
x_{0}
was imposed in order to ensure that
m_{x_{0}}=0
.
However, when extending the argument to families, this assumption must be removed.
When working with the Riesz topology, this seems difficult because the Riesz transform is highly nonlinear.

We begin by recalling the definition of the gap topology.
Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space over
\mathbb{C}
.
Recall that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
and
\mathcal{U}(H),\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)\subset\mathcal{B}(H)
denote the spaces of unbounded selfadjoint operators, unitary operators, bounded selfadjoint operators, and bounded operators on
H
respectively.
We define the Cayley transform
T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)\to\mathcal{U}(H)
via functional calculus associated with an injective continuous map

(137)

T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}\colon\mathbb{R}\to U(1)\setminus\{1\},\lambda\to\frac{\lambda-i}{\lambda+i}.

The gap topology on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
is defined as the pullback of the norm topology on
\mathcal{U}(H)\subset\mathcal{B}(H)
via
T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}
.
We note that
\Image T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}=\{U\in\mathcal{U}(H)\mid\Kernel(U-\id)=\{0\}\}
.
Moreover, we observe that

(138)

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(A)-T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(B)={}

\displaystyle\frac{A-i}{A+i}-\frac{B-i}{B+i}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2i}{A+i}\right)-\left(1-\frac{2i}{B+i}\right)=-2i\left(\frac{1}{A+i}-\frac{1}{B+i}\right)

for
A,B\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
.
Hence, the gap topology is the weakest topology such that the maps
A\mapsto(A\pm i)^{-1}
are continuous.
We also remark that the gap topology is strictly weaker than the Riesz topology.

The following proposition should be regarded as the counterpart, for the gap topology, of inequality (
105
), which has played a key role in the proof of Riesz continuity and follows from the theory of operator-Lipschitz functions.

Proposition 24
.

We have

(139)

\lVert T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(A+K)-T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(B+L)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2\lVert K-L\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}+(1+\lVert L\rVert_{\mathrm{op}})^{2}\lVert T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(A)-T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(B)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

for
A,B\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
and
K,L\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathit{sa}}(H)
.

Proof.

We have

(140)

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(A+K)-T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(B+L)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2i\left((A+K+i)^{-1}-(B+L+i)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2i\left((A+K+i)^{-1}-(A+L+i)^{-1}+(A+L+i)^{-1}-(B+L+i)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2i\left((A+K+i)^{-1}-(A+L+i)^{-1}\right)+2i\left((A+L+i)^{-1}-(B+L+i)^{-1}\right).

By the second resolvent identity, we have

(141)

\displaystyle 2i\big((A+K+i)^{-1}-(A+L+i)^{-1}\big)={}

\displaystyle 2i(A+K+i)^{-1}\left((A+L+i)-(A+K+i)\right)(A+L+i)^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2i(A+K+i)^{-1}(L-K)(A+L+i)^{-1}.

Hence, we have

(142)

\lVert 2i\big((A+K+i)^{-1}-(A+L+i)^{-1}\big)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=2\lVert(A+K+i)^{-1}(L-K)(A+L+i)^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2\lVert L-K\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

Noting that both
(A+L+i)^{-1}(A+i)
and
(B+i)(B+L+i)^{-1}
extend to bounded operators, we have

(143)

\displaystyle 2i\big((A+L+i)^{-1}-(B+L+i)^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(A+L+i)^{-1}(A+i)\cdot 2i\big((A+i)^{-1}-(B+i)^{-1}\big)\cdot(B+i)(B+L+i)^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(A+L+i)^{-1}(A+i)\cdot\left(T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(B)-T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(A)\right)\cdot(B+i)(B+L+i)^{-1}.

Thus, we obtain

(144)

\displaystyle\lVert 2i((A+L+i)^{-1}-(B+L+i)^{-1})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lVert(A+L+i)^{-1}(A+i)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(B)-T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(A)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert(B+i)(B+L+i)^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle(1+\lVert L\rVert_{\mathrm{op}})\cdot\lVert T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(B)-T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(A)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(1+\lVert L\rVert_{\mathrm{op}})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(1+\lVert L\rVert_{\mathrm{op}})^{2}\lVert T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(B)-T_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(A)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Definition of spectral flow

In our main theorems in Section

Definition 25

(Spectral flow)

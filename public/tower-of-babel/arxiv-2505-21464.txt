Slow polynomial mixing, dynamical Borel Cantelli-Lemma and Hausdorff dimension of dynamical Diophantine sets
E.Daviaud, ULiège

Abstract
. In [ 3 ] , it is established that if an ergodic system ( T , μ ) (T,\mu) of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , where μ \mu is an exact-dimensional measure, satisfies a certain mixing property, then the dynamical Borel-Cantelli lemma holds at every point and for every δ ≥ 1 dim μ , \delta\geq\frac{1}{\dim\mu}, for μ \mu -almost every x 0 x_{0} , dim H { x : ‖ T n ​ ( x 0 ) − x ‖ ∞ ≤ 1 n δ ​ i.o. } = 1 δ . \dim_{H}\left\{x:||T^{n}(x_{0})-x||_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{n^{\delta}}\text{ i.o. }\right\}=\frac{1}{\delta}. In the present article, improving on an example of Galatolo-Rousseau-Saussol, we show that the mixing condition provided in [ 3 ] is optimal in a satisfying sense for both the dynamical Borel-Cantelli lemma and to get the above estimate regarding the dimension of the classical dynamical Diophantine sets.



1.
Introduction

The Diophantine approximation takes his roots in number theory, with the work of Dirichlet, who defined and proved the first result regarding the notion of speed of approximation of a real number by rationals. More precisely, Dirichlet proved that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q},
there exists infinitely many
(p,q)
with
p\wedge q=1
such that

|x-\frac{p}{q}|\leq\frac{1}{q^{2}}.

His result was later on refined by Jarnik and Besicovitch by establishing that for every
\delta\geq 1,

\dim_{H}\left\{x:|x-\frac{p}{q}|\leq\frac{1}{q^{2\delta}}\text{ i.o. }p\wedge q=1\right\}=\frac{1}{\delta},

where
``
i.o.
"
(infinitely often) means that the inequality holds for infinitely many
p,q
. Finally recently, by combining an analog of Borel-Cantelli lemma proved by Koukoulopoulos and Maynard
[
8
]
and the so-called mass transference principle from Beresnevitch and Velani
[
1
]
or Jaffard’s ubiquity theorem
[
6
]
, it was proved that, given
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+},

\dim_{H}\left\{x:|x-\frac{p}{q}|\leq\psi(q)\text{ i.o. }p\wedge q=1\right\}=\min\left\{1,s_{\psi}\right\},

where, denoting
\phi
the Euler mapping,

s_{\psi}=\inf\left\{s:\sum_{q\geq 1}\phi(q)\psi(q)^{s}<+\infty\right\}.

Similar questions have also raised many interests in dynamical systems, as analogs of rational approximation by orbits are very natural. Given a measurable mapping
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
(where
d\in\mathbb{N}
) a
T
-ergodic (probability) measure
\mu
, a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a non-increasing sequence of radii
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
, two particular problems led to many interesting developments, which consists in studying, from the measure or Hausdorff dimension point of view the sets

\begin{cases}\left\{x:\ T^{n}(x)\in B(x_{0},r_{n})\text{ i.o.}\right\}\\
\left\{x:\ ||T^{n}(x_{0})-x||_{\infty}\leq r_{n}\text{ i.o.}\right\}.\end{cases}

Regarding the first problem, a very natural question consists in investigating under which condition on the ergodic system
(T,\mu)
one obtains an analog of the Borel-Cantelli lemma at the point
x_{0},
i.e., to determine whether it holds that

(1)

\begin{cases}\mu\Big(\left\{x:\ T^{n}(x)\in B(x_{0},r_{n})\text{ i.o. }\right\}\Big)=1\text{ if }\sum_{n\geq 0}\mu\Big(B(x_{0},r_{n})\Big)=+\infty\\
\mu\Big(\left\{x:\ T^{n}(x)\in B(x_{0},r_{n})\text{ i.o. }\right\}\Big)=0\text{ if }\sum_{n\geq 0}\mu\Big(B(x_{0},r_{n})\Big)<+\infty.\end{cases}

The answer regarding this question for certain conformal Gibbs measure where established by Chernov and Kleinbock in
[
7
]
and, later on, Galatolo
[
5
]
established that this dichotomy holds at any point provided that
(T,\mu)
is exponentially mixing with respect to Lipschitz observable and
\mu
is exact-dimensional (see Definition
7
). In
[
10
, Proposition 41]
Galatolo-Saussol-Rousseau provided an example (a class of examples to be more precise) of ergodic systems
(T,\mu)
, where
T:\mathbb{T}^{3}\to\mathbb{T}^{3}
(
\mathbb{T}
denoting the one dimensional torus) and
\mu=\mathcal{L}^{3}
is the Lebesgue measure which are polynomially mixing with respect to Lipschitz-observable but for which at any point
x_{0}
,

\mu\Big(\left\{x:\ T^{n}(x)\in B(x_{0},\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{3}}})\text{ i.o. }\right\}\Big)=0,

which shows that at every
x_{0}
, the Borel-Cantelli dichotomy (
1
) does not hold.

In
[
3
]
, following
[
9
]
, was introduced the
(\gamma,\phi)
-mixing property, where
\gamma\geq 1
and
\phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a non increasing mapping. An ergodic system is said to be
(\gamma,\phi)
-mixing if for every balls
A,B
1
1

1

It is worth mentioning that, by outer regularity, it is easily proved that the ball
A
can be replaced by any Borel set in (
2
), but not the ball

is an ergodic measure satisfying this properties for every pairs of Borel sets

.
, for every

one has

\mu\Big(T^{-n}(A)\cap B\Big)\leq\gamma\mu(A)\mu(B)+\phi(n)\mu(A).

The advantage of (

satisfies it or not. Moreover, should

satisfy (

In this article we establish that there exists

and

such that the example

2.1.
Recall on geometric measure theory

Definition 2.1
.

2.2.
Continued fraction, Diophantine linear type

Given
\theta\in[0,1]

Given

Moreover it is known that for every

In the rest of the article, the following Diophantine exponent will play an important role.

Definition 2.3
.

Let

The Diophantine linear type of

\gamma_{\ell}(\alpha)=\inf\left\{\gamma\geq 0\text{ s.t. }\exists C>0:\ d_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha.k)\geq\frac{C}{||k||_{\infty}^{\gamma}}\text{ for every }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\right\},

We now estimate

\displaystyle+(n-n_{B})\Big(\frac{\sum_{0\leq j\leq n-n_{B}}\chi_{\widetilde{R}}(j\alpha)}{n-n_{B}}-\mathcal{L}^{2}(\widetilde{R})\Big)+

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{2}(\widetilde{R})\Big(\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-n_{B}-1}k\times\Big(\binom{n-n_{B}}{k}-\binom{n-n_{B}}{k+1}\Big)+n-n_{B}\Big)=\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2},

where

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-n_{B}-1}k\Big(\frac{\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}\chi_{\widetilde{R}}(j\alpha)}{k}-\mathcal{L}^{2}(\widetilde{R})\Big)\times\Big(\binom{n-n_{B}}{k}-\binom{n-n_{B}}{k+1}\Big)

Coordinating production–living–ecological space efficiency in suburban rural areas using a DEA–triangular model
Authors: Tianyi Zhao, Wei Xu, Yuning Cheng, Runjuan Yuan, Yiyang Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryCurrently, suburban rural areas in China are experiencing low-efficiency issues in production-living-ecological spaces (PLES) amid rapid urbanization. Existing research pays insufficient attention to trade-offs among multiple spatial efficiencies and often lacks integrated analytical frameworks. This study aims to enhance PLES utilization efficiency in multi-input/output systems. Using Nanjing as a case, spatial utilization efficiency was measured through data envelopment analysis (DEA), and distribution patterns were identified via geographic information system (GIS) analysis. Gini coefficient decomposition was applied to analyze spatiotemporal disparities, while a triangular model quantified interrelationships among PLES. From 2011 to 2021, PLES utilization efficiency among townships rose by 48.14% on average, with 17 shifting from imbalance to coordination; 49% achieved primary coordination, and 9 reached high-quality coordination. The study developed a multi-criteria evaluation framework, improving the precision and coordination of rural governance and spatial management, and providing support for sustainable rural development.Subject areas: ecology, urban planning

Introduction

China’s transition to a stock-based urbanization phase has driven contemporary rural areas to exhibit multifunctional and multi-format composite development. In economically developed metropolitan suburbs, accelerated regional renewal, infrastructure upgrades, and sectoral restructuring have worsened the overdevelopment and ecological imbalance accumulated during rapid urbanization. These challenges cumulatively compromise critical regional systems: intensified resource competition degrades human settlement quality,1 biodiversity loss destabilizes natural ecosystems, cultural landscape continuity fragments, and incompatible spatial transitions reduce landscape integrity.2 Within rural systems, essential resources (food, energy, materials) originate from agriculture and ecosystems, highlighting that rural sustainability depends fundamentally on coordinated production-living-ecological space (PLES) development.3,4 Following China’s 2017 Rural Revitalization Strategy,5 Territorial Spatial Planning (TSP) and Village Planning (VP) frameworks now prioritize scientific PLES allocation as a core policy.6 The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) defines land use as “human-driven arrangements, activities, and inputs to ensure effective production, transformation, and maintenance of land.7” Scholarly consensus identifies land resources as fundamental determinants of land use efficiency.8 Under rigid land constraints, PLES development demands balancing spatial heterogeneity through regionally adaptive strategies aligned with developmental stages.9 Optimizing single land-use efficiency frequently compromises other land-use efficiencies.10 Therefore, synergistically enhancing the utilization efficiency of the PLES in rural areas and restructuring the PLES are crucial issues for achieving sustainable development goals (SDGs).11

Classical theories established critical analytical frameworks: Thünen’s Rings Theory reveals spatial gradients in agricultural efficiency,12 while Howard’s Garden City Theory integrates PLES, embedding social equity, ecological conservation, and economic efficiency into urban planning.13 Contemporary research focuses on efficiency trade-offs between land-sharing and land-sparing strategies14 to optimize finite space. Compact city theory enhances living spaces (LS) efficiency through high-density development coupled with functionally mixed layouts.15,16 In contrast, multifunctional landscape theory enables co-location of production, conservation, and cultural functions within single units, advancing economic-ecological synergy quantification.17 Complementing this, ecosystem services theory quantifies land-derived services (provisioning, regulating, supporting, and cultural),18 creating a value-based ecological benefit framework. Together, these theories explore precision spatial allocation across ecological, productive, and living dimensions to enhance land-use efficacy.

Optimizing PLES utilization efficiency requires restructuring territorial configurations and defining development patterns guided by population-resource equilibrium and socio-ecological balance principles.9 Scholars developed multi-objective evaluation systems integrating economic, social, and environmental dimensions to assess regional land use efficiency,19 though results show high sensitivity to weighting methods. While conventional metrics equate efficiency with economic value per unit area,20 this unidimensional approach oversimplifies reality. Contemporary research increasingly conceptualizes land use efficiency through an input-output framework as maximizing land’s economic value via structural optimization and utilization refinement.21,22 This approach is exemplified by the slacks-based measure (SBM) model,23 which incorporates land, capital, labor, energy, and environmental factors through three indicator categories: inputs (land/resources/energy), desirable outputs (economic), and undesirable outputs (pollution). Prevailing econometric methods—including DEA,9,24 stochastic frontier analysis (SFA),25 and difference-in-differences (DID)26 models—are applied to assess efficiency across urban,10,27 industrial,28,29 agricultural,30 and ecological31 domains. Research trends show interdisciplinary convergence and methodological diversification, evolving from single-dimensional evaluation to multidimensional analyses incorporating external factors (e.g., ecological conditions,32,33 human capital,34 socioeconomic influences32,33,35). Spatial technology integration—such as geographic information system (GIS)-based spatial econometrics36 and remote sensing-based dynamic monitoring37—represents a pivotal research direction.

A critical review of rural PLES utilization efficiency studies reveals four key limitations: (1) analytical perspective: existing studies focus narrowly on single-space efficiency, neglecting inter-space trade-offs; (2) spatial scope: most combine urban-rural areas, though urban built-up zones typically lack sufficient production spaces (PS)/ecological spaces (ES) for PLES synergy analysis; (3) scale limitations: macro-scale analyses (e.g., national,24,27 provincial, or urban-agglomeration36,38,39 levels) dominate, while township-level studies—critical for granular governance—remain scarce due to data scarcity and shifting administrative boundaries; (4) indicator systems: current frameworks lack standardized multi-source data integration for unified socioeconomic-environmental objectives.40 Addressing these gaps, this study aims to resolve the following key scientific question: How can rural township-level PLES utilization efficiency be synergistically enhanced in multi-input-output systems to enable precision resource regulation?

This study selected typical rural areas in eastern metropolitan suburbs of China for empirical research (Figures 1 and 2). Analyzing spatiotemporal differentiation characteristics and coordination status of rural PLES utilization efficiency provides evidence-based guidance for regulating regional PLES resource inputs. The specific objectives include: (1) measuring PLES utilization efficiency; (2) analyzing spatiotemporal differentiation characteristics and coordination levels; (3) formulating strategies to regulate PLES resource inputs. Grounding in PLES synergy principles, the study developed a township-level measurement evaluation system Figure 3). Adopting a multi-factor decision-making framework for systemic efficiency coordination, the research advances previous studies by (1) developing quantitative measurement methods and grading classification systems for the coordination level among the utilization efficiencies of the PLES, and (2) overcoming methodological challenges, including meso-micro-scale data acquisition barriers and complexities in planning indicator integration. Based on findings, the study explores quantitative methods to evaluate PLES inputs and outputs, enhancing the precision and systematicity of township planning. This provides innovative pathways and paradigmatic references for developing countries pursuing SDGs.

Spatiotemporal patterns of PLES utilization efficiency

This study applied a super-efficiency SBM-DEA model integrated with GIS spatial analysis techniques. The natural breaks classification method (Jenks) was used to analyze the spatiotemporal patterns of ES, PS, and LS utilization efficiency during three key years (2011, 2016, and 2021) aligned with China’s Five-Year Plans. These time points were selected to accommodate administrative boundary adjustments and facilitate detailed characteristic analysis (Figure 4).

Decomposition of spatiotemporal differences in PLES utilization efficiency

The analysis of ES utilization efficiency in Nanjing’s rural townships revealed consistently equitable distribution patterns throughout 2011–2021, evidenced by persistent overall Gini coefficients below 0.2. Spatial disparities reached their minimum in 2021, with all intra-district coefficients remaining under 0.25 and exhibiting negligible county-level variation. Notably, Gaochun displayed the highest intra-district disparity, showing significant efficiency variations among its townships. These disparities persisted despite homogeneous PLES allocations across townships, where ES and PS collectively occupied ∼50% of the total area, versus LS representing only 10%. Inter-district analysis indicated generally low Gini coefficients (predominantly <0.2), with the most pronounced disparity occurring between Gaochun and Luhe. Additionally, Gaochun exhibited substantially greater diversity in ecological land-use types compared to Luhe (Figure 5).

PS utilization efficiency analysis: the analysis revealed persistent regional disparities during 2011–2021, evidenced by consistently high overall Gini coefficients (0.30–0.45) with substantial interannual fluctuations. Three phases emerged: (1) a relatively balanced phase (2012–2015) with lower coefficients (0.23–0.29); (2) a peak disparity year (2016, 0.42); (3) a post-2017 divergence phase, where Jiangning and Gaochun exhibited pronounced efficiency differences. Notably, Jiangning displayed severe intra-district variations as early as 2011—a significant finding given similar PLES compositions across most townships. Of particular interest, certain townships achieved exceptional PS utilization efficiency scores despite minimal PS allocations, suggesting intensive land use. Inter-district comparisons showed consistently moderate-to-high inequality (Gini = 0.20–0.50), with the most pronounced disparity between Luhe and Pukou. These patterns aligned closely with PLES distributions and efficiency characteristics in Jiangning and Gaochun (Figure 6).

Synergy analysis of PLES utilization efficiency

Based on quantified efficiency metrics of ES, PS, and LS, this study constructed a Triangular model (Figure 8) to systematically assess PLES coordination dynamics. Analysis from 2011 to 2021 revealed a trend of improving coordination until 2019, which was significantly reversed in 2020, with only a partial recovery by 2021. The classification framework defined 7 coordination levels: (1) severe imbalance, (2) imbalance, (3) near-imbalance, (4) primary coordination, (5) intermediate coordination, (6) good coordination, and (7) high-quality coordination, further divided into 17 dominance patterns based on efficiency characteristics (Table 4).

Discussion

Differentiated regional regulation strategies based on efficiency enhancement

Differentiated regional strategies optimize efficiency in multifunctional metropolitan peri-urban villages, as studies show customized land-use policies are essential.27,36 The complex multi-input, multi-output interactions in PLES require dual approaches: maximizing output with fixed inputs or minimizing inputs per unit output. Integrating super-efficiency SBM modeling with Gini coefficient decomposition, this framework establishes directional planning benchmarks for developing nations facing early-stage urbanization.

Tan reports urban land green use efficiency in Nanjing at 0.65–0.8,36 while rural ES efficiency here exceeds 1.0, indicating higher and increasing ecological efficiency versus urban areas. Chen finds declining land use efficiency in Chinese urban areas (2005–2012), especially eastern coastal regions with high economic input but low efficiency.10 Conversely, rural LS utilization efficiency improved markedly since 2013, shifting from resource overconcentration to insufficient output. Fei notes generally inefficient agricultural land use in China, though developed eastern provinces like Jiangsu (0.462) became high-efficiency areas61—aligning with post-2020 PS utilization efficiency in rural Nanjing, suggesting recent PS improvements. Provincial/municipal research combined with these findings clarifies township-level PLES utilization efficiency spatial patterns and differential decomposition, crucial for understanding rural social progress and land-use synergies.

Longitudinal analysis (2011–2021) shows a fluctuating but ultimately positive PLES utilization efficiency trajectory in Nanjing’s rural townships, with significant ES and PS input redundancy. ES inputs showed severe imbalances: environmental governance (26.52) and ES land area (15.52) exceeded PS inputs (primary industry employment: 2.0; PS land area: 1.34). Meanwhile, ES outputs were deficient, particularly cultural landscape value (6.69) and regulation services (6.33), while LS had output shortages reflected in rural Engel coefficients (6.78). These findings indicate ES utilization poses greater challenges than PS or LS, manifesting as resource-intensive, low-yield systems. Substantial PS inputs risk arable land conversion via non-agriculturalization and non-grainification, while LS optimization requires interventions targeting farmer consumption and livelihoods. Consequently, Nanjing’s rural development strategy adopts a composite approach: bidirectional optimization for ES (input reduction [spatial/economic] coupled with output enhancement [ecological/economic]), complemented by unidirectional strategies for PS (economic/material input reduction) and LS (economic output augmentation).

The Nanjing Territorial Space Master Plan (2021–2035) establishes “northern and southern agricultural zones, a central metropolitan core, riverside corridors, and integrated urban-rural systems,” defining distinct rural pathways. For ES, Gaochun maintains the lowest mean Gini coefficient (0.17), with townships like Zhuanqiang and Yangjiang achieving higher ecological outputs through aquatic ecosystems supporting fisheries. Conversely, single-crop townships (e.g., Dongba) show lower efficiency. Enhanced ecological utilization efficiency in exurban townships stems from diversified land use rather than consistent PLE ratios. For PS, Luhe and Pukou have the highest inter-regional Gini coefficient (0.40), as urban-rural transitional townships exhibit productivity variations due to agricultural methods despite similar PLES allocations. In LS, Jiangning’s mean Gini coefficient (0.17) reflects residential land area correlating positively with efficiency via peri-urban population agglomeration. Based on these findings, three regulatory strategies emerge: (1) exurban agricultural townships: diversify ecological land use; (2) urban-rural transitional townships: enhance agricultural productivity via technology; (3) metropolitan fringe townships: strategically expand residential land to leverage agglomeration economies.

Spatial planning of PLE based on efficiency synergy

Extant research has explored the characteristics and mechanisms of three spaces efficiency categories: urban space efficiency,10,35,39,62 green space efficiency,36,63 and production space efficiency.8,28,29,61 Yet contradictions exist between single-space control models and regional development needs. Nanjing exhibits strong performance across efficiency metrics, with townships achieving a 48.14% average improvement in space utilization efficiency from 2011 to 2021. However, 7 townships remain imbalanced. Excessively intensive space utilization may impede planning objectives,10 making PLES coordination through spatial planning a critical challenge for sustainable development under multi-objective scenarios.64

A significant correlation exists between spatial morphological heterogeneity and functional efficiency.27 Extending He et al.’s findings65—which demonstrated urban form’s positive efficiency impact via density, contrasted by sprawl and fragmentation’s negative effects within single space types—our analysis shows the predominance of “Ecological-improved/production-lagged” patterns (49% of townships), highlighting systemic PS efficiency gaps. Nine townships achieved high-quality coordination, predominantly in water-rich areas (riverine/lakeside villages) and hilly terrain with substantial production space. Three spatial configurations emerged: (1) unilateral large ES (e.g., Qilin, Yongning), (2) bilateral large ES (e.g., Honglan, Jiangpu), and (3) interwoven PLES (e.g., Guli, Dongping) (Figure 10). Spatial analysis revealed clustered homogeneous patches in high-efficiency townships versus dispersed mixed distributions in low-efficiency areas. The 2020 coordination decline reflects exogenous disruptions: COVID-19 lockdowns disrupted rural production chains (e.g., labor shortages, logistics delays66), while concurrent “Three Zones and Three Lines” policy shifts prioritized ecological conservation, exacerbating PS-LS trade-offs. This dual pressure caused a transient imbalance, with 2021’s partial recovery indicating local adaptive recalibration.

Typical townships of high-efficiency coordination

The study advances prior work by examining relationships between all three space types’ spatial configurations and their coordinated efficiency, confirming (1) clustering similar spaces boosts individual efficiency and (2) appropriate dispersion across space types enables overall synergy. Thus, a “classified aggregation” principle is proposed: macro-scale dispersion (three space types) with micro-scale concentration (homogeneous types) optimizes coordination efficiency. Unlike urban-focused research on ecological-urban efficiency synergy,19,27 rural PLES synergy demands unique consideration of production space efficiency—inherent to rural territorial characteristics. Thus, refined coordination classification is essential, as sustainable development requires balanced efficiency across all space categories alongside optimized PLES distribution patterns.

Limitations of the study

In conducting the measurement and evaluation of PLES utilization efficiency and synergistic characteristics, several critical issues require attention: (1) regional representativeness limitations: given China’s extensive rural territories and substantial variations in natural geography and socio-economic conditions across regions,10 the case study of Nanjing’s rural areas requires further validation through comparative studies across diverse regions. This approach is essential to establish the generalizability of findings on typical spatial structures. (2) Demarcation of evaluation units: data accessibility limitations restrict production-living-related statistics to administrative district levels, while temporal changes in administrative boundaries further complicate the selection of evaluation units. (3) Data reliability at the township level: compared to conventional county/prefecture-level units, township-scale data suffer from dual limitations: lower precision in natural geographic parameters and greater difficulties in acquiring socio-economic statistics. A small number of missing values were interpolated using township-level means for corresponding indicators—a practical approach necessitated by data constraints. Results should be interpreted assuming proportional uniformity between economic governance inputs and land area distribution. However, computational biases may arise due to investment efficiency disparities and spatial heterogeneity. (4) Indicator system development: constructed indicators are heavily dependent on data availability and quantification methods. Carbon emission estimates are prone to inaccuracies from dynamic factors (e.g., technological iterations, policy adjustments) and the exclusion of time-sensitive parameters. Although the current framework (evaluating spatial, economic, labor, material, and energy dimensions) demonstrates reasonable representativeness, continuous societal and technological advancement necessitates improved quantification accuracy and more comprehensive metrics.

The complex interplay among ecological security, agricultural production, and social development in China’s suburban rural areas demands innovative governance approaches. This study presents a township-level case of precision spatial regulation, integrating multi-indicator assessment with a synergistic classification-evaluation model to develop a PLES utilization efficiency evaluation system. The system identifies an evolution from dispersed to agglomerated spatial patterns in high-efficiency zones at meso-micro scales. Using differential decomposition methods, we elucidate the intrinsic mechanisms of PLES utilization efficiency, including operational principles and inter-factor correlations. The proposed synergistic classification-evaluation model reveals an “Ecological-improved/production-lagged” dichotomy and PLES synergy features. We consequently propose a dual “bidirectional-unidirectional” resource regulation approach, yielding three differentiated regional strategies and establishing a “classified agglomeration” principle-based spatial planning framework for PLE optimization. This contributes innovative theoretical and methodological insights to rural spatial restructuring.

Our methodological framework addresses two critical research gaps: (1) inadequate research on PLES efficiency synergies, and (2) technical challenges in data acquisition and complex planning parameters at meso-micro scales. Results validate the feasibility of multi-element coordination and efficiency optimization in rural spatial systems. Limitations include: regional sample representativeness constraints, data accessibility and reliability issues due to administrative boundary changes, potential biases from township-level data interpolation methods, and indicator system dependence on existing quantification approaches. Given China’s vast territorial diversity, understanding intrinsic relationships among spatial elements across regions is essential. Two pivotal research questions require attention: (1) developing tailored pathways for heterogeneous rural spaces to foster differentiated yet coordinated development; and (2) employing advanced dasymetric mapping techniques to calculate finer-scale indicators (e.g., economic investment, carbon emissions) when data permit. These challenges represent fundamental research priorities.

Method details

Research area and data

Research area

Nanjing, situated in China’s Yangtze River Delta core economic region, serves as a pioneering model for rural institutional reforms, providing paradigmatic insights for less developed regions (Figure 1). This study focuses on Nanjing’s non-urban districts (Liuhe, Pukou, Jiangning, Lishui, Gaochun; total area: 5,718.86 km2). Based on the PLES classification standard (GB/T 21010-2017), the 2021 land-use distribution was ES: 31%, PS: 41%, LS: 28% (Figure 2).

The case study’s typicality derives from four dimensions: (1) Eco-geographical Conditions: As Jiangsu’s capital in a humid subtropical zone, Nanjing exhibits the characteristic low mountainous-hilly topography of Jiangnan. (2) Socioeconomic Production Attributes: Rapid urbanization-driven land-use patterns make Nanjing ideal for investigating rural industrial diversification. (3) Human Settlement Development: With 86.9% urbanization (2021),67 Nanjing exemplifies emerging first-tier city trajectories and possesses unique advantages for urban-rural integration. (4) Regional Development Challenges: Four decades of reform-driven urban expansion have exacerbated urban-rural disparities beyond national averages. Proliferating township enterprises and policies (e.g., New Rural Construction, Beautiful Countryside) strain agricultural land and ecological security in Jiangnan.68

Data sources and preprocessing

This study integrated statistical, raster, and vector datasets for the period 2011–2021. Statistical data were mainly derived from the Nanjing Statistical Yearbook and the statistical yearbooks of Jiangning, Gaochun, Lishui, Liuhe, and Pukou Districts. These data included township-level socioeconomic indicators such as environmental pollution control expenditure, rural labor force, gross agricultural output value, agricultural and water-related fiscal expenditure, employment in the primary sector, fertilizer application, rural per capita disposable income, rural electricity consumption, total rural population, rural per capita housing area, rural employment opportunities, and household expenditure structure. Raster data were derived from the annual 30 m China Land Cover Dataset (CLCD), which was generated from Landsat imagery and included cropland, forest, shrubland, grassland, water bodies, barren land, wetlands, and impervious surfaces. Vector data included township-, county-, and prefecture-level administrative boundaries as well as ancillary basemap data used for spatial visualization and aggregation.

All spatial datasets were unified under a consistent spatial reference system and aggregated to the township level. Land cover categories were reclassified into ES, PS, and LS based on the PLES classification standard to ensure comparability across years and among subsystems. Raster-based land-use areas were calculated for each township through spatial overlay and zonal aggregation. For indicators originally available only at the district level, proportional allocation was used to match the township-scale analysis. Specifically, environmental governance investment was allocated according to each township’s share of ecological land area within the district, and agricultural fiscal expenditure was allocated according to each township’s share of cultivated land area. These treatments were adopted to achieve spatial consistency between socioeconomic statistics and land-use units under the constraint of data availability.

Research framework

The research framework comprises four key steps (Figure 3): (1) PLES Utilization Efficiency Quantification—A non-parametric Super-Efficiency SBM model under DEA evaluates the utilization efficiency of ES, PS, and LS across rural townships in Nanjing; (2) Spatiotemporal Distribution Characterization—GIS-based geostatistical techniques identify spatial efficiency patterns; (3) Spatiotemporal Heterogeneity Decomposition—Gini coefficient decomposition quantifies efficiency distribution disparities; (4) PLES Synergistic Relationship Analysis—A Triangular Model classifies efficiency types by analyzing inter-dimensional synergies among ES, PS, and LS. This multi-stage framework provides a scientific basis for optimizing regional PLES utilization and supporting land-use regulation strategies.

Super-SBM model for PLES utilization efficiency

DEA is a nonparametric, interdisciplinary method that integrates operations research, management science, and economics.69 It is widely used to evaluate the relative efficiency of decision-making units (DMUs) with multiple inputs and outputs.70 The methodology was initially developed by Charnes et al. in 197871 using their CCR model based on linear programming, establishing a relative efficiency framework for assessing homogeneous DMUs. However, traditional DEA models like CCR were limited by their inability to account for slack variables. This limitation was addressed by Tone's (2001) SBM model, which incorporated non-radial, non-oriented analysis and accommodated undesirable outputs.72 A further advancement was Tone's (2002) Super-SBM model,73 which allows efficiency scores exceeding 1, enhancing DMU comparison and ranking.

A rigorous screening process selected townships in rural Nanjing as DMUs. Selection required comparable DMUs to have identical objectives, tasks, external environments, and consistent input-output indicators. DMUs with zero or negative values in any input or output indicator were systematically excluded, resulting in a final sample of 439 valid DMUs for evaluation. Triangular model for coordination types

The Triangular model, initially developed by the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) for soil classification, has gained widespread adoption as an analytical framework owing to its intuitive visualization and methodological robustness.85 This methodology has been extensively applied in multiple disciplines, including industrial development, environmental science, and land-use studies.86,87 This study adapts the Triangular model to establish a systematic framework for classifying PLES utilization efficiency relationships, enabling comprehensive spatial synergy assessment.

After normalizing the three core indicators (PS, LS, and ES utilization efficiency), their interrelationships were quantitatively visualized using Origin 2021 software. Drawing on established methodologies for constructing and applying the Triangular model in existing research,86,88,89,90 an equilateral triangle configuration was adopted, with the vertices representing the maximum efficiency values of PS, LS, and ES, respectively. Three coordinate axes (X, Y, Z) were established through counterclockwise vertex connections, with each axis scaled from 0 to 1. For axis segmentation, an equal-interval approach prioritized fairness among PLES utilization efficiencies. Sensitivity analysis of the 0.2 division threshold (validated by Kendall’s test: τ=0.76, p<0.05) demonstrated optimal performance in identifying coordination-level transitions and detecting spatial imbalance risks while preserving overall trend robustness. Consequently, five equal intervals per axis generated 25 equal-area triangular subregions. This framework establishes an Efficiency Relationship Typology comprising 16 dominance patterns and 7 coordination levels based on spatial positioning. As Figure 4 illustrates, each data point represents a township’s composite spatial efficiency profile. Table 4 reveals significant spatial imbalances in vertex-proximal zones (A1, B1, C1), contrasting with optimal tri-spatial coordination in the central P region.

Quantification and statistical analysis

All analyses were conducted at the township scale. In this study, ∗n∗ refers to the number of township-level observations or decision-making units (DMUs) included in each analytical step. For the DEA-based efficiency estimation, townships with zero or negative values in any input or output indicator were excluded prior to model estimation, resulting in 439 valid DMUs. Spatial data preprocessing, land-use aggregation, and map production were performed in ArcMap 10.6. DEA-based efficiency estimation and Dagum Gini coefficient decomposition were implemented in MATLAB R2020b, and the triangular model visualization and typological classification were performed in Origin 2021. Statistical details of the model construction, indicator systems, and classification rules are provided in the corresponding subsections of the STAR Methods and in Tables 1, 2, 3, and 4.

For the triangular model analysis, ES, PS, and LS utilization efficiency values were normalized to a common scale ranging from 0 to 1. An equal-interval segmentation strategy was applied to the three axes, and a threshold of 0.2 was adopted to classify the coordination relationships among the three subsystems. The robustness of this threshold was evaluated using Kendall’s test (τ = 0.76, ∗p∗ < 0.05). Statistical significance was defined at ∗p∗ < 0.05 where applicable. Because this study was based on full-coverage township-level spatial and statistical datasets rather than laboratory or clinical experiments, biological replicates, blinding, randomization, and experimental sample size estimation were not applicable. Measures of central tendency and dispersion, such as mean, median, SD, SEM, and confidence intervals, were not the primary basis for the main model outputs, because the study focused on efficiency estimation, spatial disparity decomposition, and typological classification rather than repeated experimental measurements.

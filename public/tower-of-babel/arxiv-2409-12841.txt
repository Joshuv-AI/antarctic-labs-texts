Artin motives in relative Nori and Voevodsky motives.
Swann Tubach

Abstract
Over a scheme of finite type over a field of characteristic zero, we prove that Nori an Voevodsky categories of relative Artin motives, that is the full subcategories generated by the motives of étale morphisms in relative Nori and Voevodsky motives, are canonically equivalent. As an application, we show that over a normal base of characteristic zero an Artin motive is dualisable if and only if it lies in the thick category spanned by the motives of finite étale schemes. We finish with an application to motivic Galois groups and obtain an analogue of the classical exact sequence of étale fundamental groups relating a variety over a field and its base change to the algebraic closure.

\mathrm{DN}
replaced by
\mathrm{DM}
.

Recall that the ordinary t-structure on
X
is the unique t-structure on
\mathrm{DN}_{c}(X)
such that all pullback functors are t-exact. It can be constructed from the perverse t-structure using gluing (see
[
Sai90
, Remarks 4.6]
). We denote by
{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ord}}}(X)
its heart.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero and let
X
be a finite type
k
-scheme. Then the ordinary t-structure on
\mathrm{DN}_{c}(X)
restricts to
\mathrm{DN}^{0}_{c}(X)
,
\mathrm{DN}^{0,\mathrm{rig}}(X)
and
\mathrm{DN}^{\mathrm{coh}}_{c}(X)
.

Proof.

It restricts to
\mathrm{DN}^{\mathrm{rig}}(X)
because the tensor product is t-exact in both variables. We prove that the t-structure restricts to
\mathrm{DN}^{\mathrm{coh}}_{c}(X)
, the case of Artin motives being similar. Let
M\in\mathrm{DN}^{\mathrm{coh}}_{c}(X)
. We want to show that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(M)
is still a cohomological motive. By the above
Proposition

3.4
it suffices to prove that for all
x\in X
the object
x^{*}\mathrm{H}^{0}(M)
is cohomological. This follows from t-exactness of
x
and the case of fields
Proposition

2.2
.
∎

4
The equivalence of Artin motives.

In this section we prove that the functor
\rho_{\mathrm{N}}
induces an equivalence on
0
-motives.
We begin by the following computation:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero and let
X
be a smooth
k
-scheme. Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a finite étale map and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer.
Then the maps

\mathrm{H}^{n}(Y_{\'{e}t},\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DM}(X)}(\mathbb{Q}_{X},f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}[n])\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DN}(X)}(\mathbb{Q}_{X},f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}[n])

induced by the functors

\mathrm{D}(X_{\'{e}t},\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{DM}(X)\to\mathrm{DN}(X)

are both equivalences.

Proof.

We can assume
X
connected.
The computation for Voevodsky motives follows from
[
Rui23
, Proposition 1.7.2 and Corollary 2.1.4]
. For Nori motives, the cases
m\neq 0
are easy: both the étale cohomology and the
\mathrm{Hom}
group are vanishing, because of t-structure (
m<0
) and weight (
m>0
) reasons. For
m=0
by adjunction we reduced to the case
f=\mathrm{Id}
and it follows from the fact that the group of endomorphisms of the unit object is
\mathbb{Q}
(this is clear if
k
is a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
because it is a nonzero
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space that embeds in
\mathbb{Q}=\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Shv}(X(\mathbb{C}))}(\mathbb{Q}_{X})
, and then follows by continuity for general fields).
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero and let
X
be a smooth
k
-scheme.
The functor

\mathrm{DM}^{0,\mathrm{sm}}(X)\to\mathrm{DN}^{0,\mathrm{sm}}(X)

induced by
\rho_{\mathrm{N}}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

It suffices to show that it is fully faithful on the compact objects, and even, as compact objects are the idempotent completion of the stable category generated by the
f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}
for
f\colon Y\to X
finite and étale, it suffices to prove that it is fully faithful on the smallest stable subcategory of
\mathrm{DM}(X)
that contains the
f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}
for
f\colon Y\to X
. By dévissage, it suffices to prove that for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
f\colon Y\to X
and
g\colon Z\to X
both finite étale, the map

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DM}(X)}(g_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Z},f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}[n])\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DN}(X)}(g_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Z},f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}[n])

is an equivalence. Because
g_{*}=g_{!}
and
g^{!}=g^{*}
, by adjunction and proper base change we can assume that
g=\mathrm{Id}_{X}
and replace
Y
by
Y\times_{X}Z
. In this case, the result is the above computation done in
Proposition

4.1
.
∎

We now deal with non necessarily smooth
0
-motives. For this, we will need the truncation functor of Vaish (see also
[
PL19a
]
) over a general basis.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero and let
X
be a finite type
k
-scheme.
The inclusion
\mathrm{DN}^{0}_{c}(X)\subset\mathrm{DN}^{\mathrm{coh}}_{c}(X)
admits a right adjoint
\omega^{0}
. Moreover,
\rho_{\mathrm{N}}
commutes with
\omega^{0}
.

Proof.

The existence of
\omega^{0}
for Nori motives is proven exactly in the same way as for étale motives in
[
Vai20
, Theorem 5.2.2]
. This uses the existence
of a truncation functor over a field, a fact ensured by
Proposition

2.5
. By definition,

\omega^{0}
is a truncation functor for a
m
-structure (
[
Vai20
, section 2.5]
), obtained by punctual gluing. As
\rho_{\mathrm{N}}^{K}
is
m
-exact for all fields
K
by
Corollary

2.6
,
we have that
\rho_{\mathrm{N}}^{X}
is also
m
-exact, hence commutes with truncations.
∎

We can now prove the main result of the section.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero and let
X
be a finite type
k
-scheme. The functor

\mathrm{DM}^{0}(X)\to\mathrm{DN}^{0}(X)

is an equivalence.

Proof.

As the functor clearly reaches the generators and preserves compact objects, it suffices to prove that it is an fully faithful on
\mathrm{DM}^{0}_{c}(X)
. We do a Noetherian induction on
X
. Let
M,N\in\mathrm{DM}^{0}_{c}(X)
. By
Corollary

3.3
and generic smoothness, there exists a nonempty smooth open subset
j\colon U\to X
such that both
j^{*}M
and
j^{*}N
are smooth. Denote by
i\colon Z\to X
the inclusion of the reduced closed complement. By localisation, we have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{DM}(X)}(i_{*}i^{*}N,M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{DM}(X)}(N,M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{DM}(X)}(j_{!}j^{*}N,M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{DN}(X)}(i_{*}i^{*}N,M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{DN}(X)}(N,M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{DN}(X)}(j_{!}j^{*}N,M)}}

where the lines are cofiber sequences of spectra. By the
(j_{!},j^{*})
adjunction and the smooth case
Corollary

4.2
, the right vertical map is an equivalence. Using the
(i_{*},i^{!})
adjunction, the fact that
i
being quasi-finite the functor
i^{!}
preserves cohomological motives by
Proposition

3.1
and the fact that the truncation functor
\omega^{0}
commutes with
\rho_{\mathrm{N}}
by
Proposition

4.3
, we see that the left vertical map is equivalent to the map

\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{DM}(Z)}(i^{*}N,\omega^{0}i^{!}M)\to\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{DN}(Z)}(i^{*}N,\omega^{0}i^{!}M)

which is an equivalence by Noetherian induction, finishing the proof.
∎

5
Smooth and rigid Artin motives.

In this section we prove that smooth and rigid Artin coincide on a normal scheme, and we identify the heart of the t-structure. We fix a field
k
of characteristic zero.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

We argue as in

we would

,
M=\rho_{\mathrm{N}}(M^{\prime})
for some
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{DM}^{0}_{c}(S)
.
By
[
PL19b
, Corollary 3.9 (iii)]
,
\omega^{0}(i^{*}M^{\prime}(-c)[2c])=0
(note that

[
PL19b
, Proposition 1.12 (iv)]
) hence

\omega^{0}(i^{!}M)=\rho_{\mathrm{N}}(0)=0
.
Now if
i

, such that
U
is regular with reduced complement
i_{1}:Z_{1}\to Z

U
and

[
Aut18

Tag 0E9J

\displaystyle(i_{1})_{*}(i\circ i_{1})^{!}M\to i^{!}M\to u_{*}(i\circ u)^{!}M

\omega^{0}(i^{!}M)=0
.
∎

Proposition 5.3

(Haas)
.

Let

be a connected smooth
k
-scheme of finite type. Let

. The following are equivalent :

\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}

We prove that

by adapting Johann Haas’

\mathrm{DN}^{0,\mathrm{rig}}(S)^{\heartsuit}

is fully faithful.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Rep}^{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\pi^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(S))}

Notation 5.6
.

Let
\sigma:\overline{k}\to\mathbb{C}
be an embedding. Let
X
be a finite type
k
-scheme. We denote by
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rig}}(X)
the subcategory of

{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ord}}}(X)
whose objects are the dualisable objects. This means that for
M\in{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ord}}}(X)
, we have
M\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rig}}(X)
if and only if
M
is dualisable and its dual is in
{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ord}}}(X)
(that last condition is automatic).

Definition 5.7
.

Let
X
be a connected and normal finite type
k
-scheme with a geometric point
\overline{x}:\mathrm{Spec}(\overline{k})\to X
. We define

p:\widetilde{X}\to X
to be the limit of all finite étale
f:Y\to X
that factor
\overline{x}
. The limit exists as a scheme because the
transitions morphisms have to be affine. There is a morphism
\pi_{\widetilde{X}}:\widetilde{X}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\overline{k})
compatible with
p
, which has a section
\widetilde{x}
. Therefore
there is also a map
q:\widetilde{X}\to X_{\overline{k}}
. By construction, the point
\overline{x}
factors through
\widetilde{X}
to give a geometric point
\tilde{x}\colon\mathrm{Spec}(\overline{k})\to\widetilde{X}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

The scheme
\widetilde{X}
is connected.

Proof.

If
U\subset\widetilde{X}
is a clopen subset such that
\overline{x}
does not factor through
U
, then there exists
Y\to X
finite étale factorising
\overline{x}
and a clopen
V
of
Y
whose pullback to
\widetilde{X}
is
U
. Let
Z
be the open complement of
V
in
Y
. Then
\overline{x}
factors through
V
and
Z\to X
is finite étale, so that
\widetilde{X}=\lim_{i}Y_{i}
with
Y_{i}\to Z
finite étale factoring
\overline{x}
, and we see that
U=\emptyset
.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

We defined
\widetilde{X}
for a normal scheme
X
because of the application we have in mind. Of course the definition makes sense for any qcqs scheme. Although the author did not check the details, it is probable that in the normal case the scheme
\widetilde{X}
is the normalisation in
\mathrm{Spec}(L)
of
X
, where
L
is the maximal algebraic extension of
k(X)
which is unramified over
X
.

Base change gives a monoidal functor
p^{*}:\mathrm{DN}_{c}(X)\to\mathrm{DN}_{c}(\widetilde{X})
which sends
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rig}}(X)
to
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rig}}(\widetilde{X})
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
x:\mathrm{Spec}(\overline{k})\to X
be a geometric point of a connected finite type
k
scheme. Let
\omega_{x}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rig}}(X)\to\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}

with the Betti realisation (through
\sigma
).

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rig}}(X)
is a Tannakian category with fiber functor
\omega_{x}
.

Proof.

Every object of
\mathcal{M}(X)
is dualsiable, the endomorphisms of the unit objects are one dimensional and the functor
\omega_{x}
is monoidal.
∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
X
and
\widetilde{X}
as in

5.7

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rig}}(\widetilde{X})

The category

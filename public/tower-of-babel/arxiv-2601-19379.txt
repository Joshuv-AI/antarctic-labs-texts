Optimal Asynchronous Stochastic Nonconvex Optimization under Heavy-Tailed Noise
Yidong Wu, Luo Luo

Abstract
This paper considers the problem of asynchronous stochastic nonconvex optimization with heavy-tailed gradient noise and arbitrarily heterogeneous computation times across workers. We propose an asynchronous normalized stochastic gradient descent algorithm with momentum. The analysis show that our method achieves the optimal time complexity under the assumption of bounded p p th-order central moment with p ∈ ( 1 , 2 ] p\in(1,2] . We also provide numerical experiments to show the effectiveness of proposed method.

Assumption 2

)

We suppose each worker can draw

to achieve an unbiased stochastic gradient estimate

with the

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla F(x;\xi)-\nabla f(x)\|^{p}\right]\leq\sigma^{p}

We consider the following fixed computation model for asynchronous stochastic optimization

.

Assumption 3

(

.

We suppose each worker

can take no more than

We are also interested in the universal computation model (UCM) as follows

]

Assumption 4

For each worker
i\in[n]
, the number of stochastic gradients can be calculated from a time
t_{0}
to a time

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\upsilon_{i}(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau\right\rfloor=\lfloor V_{i}(t_{1})-V_{i}(t_{0})\rfloor,

\displaystyle V_{i}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\upsilon_{i}(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau

Remark 1

The integral of function

Intuitively, the small

Conversely, the large

The Algorithm and Main Results

We proposed our Ringmaster Asynchronous Normalized Stochastic Gradient Descent with Momentum (RANSGDm) in Algorithm
1

The main steps of proposed algorithm on the server can be summarized as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{k+1}\leftarrow\beta_{k}v_{k}+(1-\beta)\nabla F(x_{k-\delta_{k}};\xi_{k-\delta_{k}}),\\[4.26773pt]
x_{k+1}\leftarrow x_{k}-\dfrac{\eta v_{k+1}}{\|v_{k+1}\|},\end{cases}

(2)

\delta_{k}
is smaller than the threshold
R

Here, the notation

The key differences between RANSGDm and existing Ringmaster ASGD

It is worth noting that introducing normalization and momentum is necessary, since heavy-tailed noise leads to simply iterating along with the stochastic gradient may fail to convergence even for the case of single machine optimization

]

In addition, the delay threshold
R
mitigates the effects of old stochastic gradient with heavy-tailed noise, which is also well compatible with techniques of normalization and momentum to make the algorithm be efficient.

Theorem 1

Under Assumptions

2

4
The Complexity Analysis

In this section, we briefly sketch the proof of our main result in Theorem
1
, and the details are deffered to Appendix
A
.
Recall that update (
2
) on the server depends on the stale gradient estimate
\nabla F(x_{k-\delta_{k}};\xi_{k-\delta_{k}})
, so that we denote

\displaystyle z_{k}=x_{k-\delta_{k}}

in the remainder of this paper.

The analysis starts from the observation that is specific to our algorithm design,
i.e.,
every round of update on the server moves by a fixed distance
\eta
due to the step of normalization
.
Together with the delay threshold
R
, it immediately yields an upper bound on the staleness. Specifically, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Based on Algorithm
1
, the variables on the worker holds

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-z_{k}\|=\|x_{k}-x_{k-\delta_{k}}\|\leq R\eta.

for all
k\geq 1
.

Based on the smoothness condition in Assumption
1
,
we describe the descent for the objective function value as follows.

Lemma 2
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
Algorithm
1

holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[f(x_{k+1})\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[f(x_{k})\big]-\eta\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|+2\eta\mathbb{E}\|v_{k+1}-\nabla f(x_{k})\|+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}

for all
k\geq 0
.

We now focus on the term
\mathbb{E}\|v_{k+1}-\nabla f(x_{k})\|
in the upper bound of
Lemma
2
.
The main challenge for the analysis is that the direction

\displaystyle v_{k+1}\leftarrow\beta_{k}v_{k}+(1-\beta)\nabla F(z_{k};\xi_{k-\delta_{k}})

is established by the heavy-tailed noise stochastic gradient estimate at the stale point
z_{k}=x_{k-\delta_{k}}
.
We introduce the deviation

\displaystyle\mu_{k}:=v_{k+1}-\nabla f(x_{k}),

and the estimate error

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}:=\nabla F(z_{k};\xi_{k-\delta_{k}})-\nabla f(z_{k})

which is unbiased due to Assumption
2
.

Based on above notations, we decompose
\mu_{k}
as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu_{k}=\beta_{k}\mu_{k-1}+\underbrace{\beta_{k}(\nabla f(x_{k-1})-\nabla f(x_{k}))}_{\text{iterate drift}}+\underbrace{(1-\beta)(\nabla f(z_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k}))}_{\text{staleness bias}}+\underbrace{(1-\beta)\zeta_{k}}_{\text{gradient noise}}.\end{split}

(5)

According to Assumption
1
and Lemma
1
,
we bound the iterate drift and the staleness bias as

\beta_{t}\|\nabla f(x_{t-1})-\nabla f(x_{t})\|\leq\beta L\eta

(6)

and

(1-\beta)\|\nabla f(z_{t})-\nabla f(x_{t})\|\leq(1-\beta)LR\eta.

(7)

By unrolling equation (
5
), the gradient noise leads to the term
\sum_{t=1}^{k}(1-\beta)\beta^{k-t}\zeta_{t}
.
According to the
p
-BCM condition in Assumption
2
, it holds

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{k}(1-\beta)\beta^{k-t}\zeta_{t}\right\|\right]\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\sigma.

(8)

Combining equations (
5
)–(
8
),
we achieve the following upper bound on
{\mathbb{E}}[\|\mu_{k}\|]=\mathbb{E}\big[\|v_{k+1}-\nabla f(x_{k})\|\big]
.

Lemma 3
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
2
,
Algorithm
1

holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\|v_{k+1}-\nabla f(x_{k})\big\|\leq\frac{L\eta}{1-\beta}+LR\eta+2^{\frac{1}{p}}\sigma(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

for all
k\geq 1
.

Combining Lemma
2
with Lemma
3

and telescoping over
k=0,\ldots,K-1
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{K}\!\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\!\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\leq\frac{\Delta}{\eta K}\!+\!2L\eta\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\!+\!R\!+\!1\right)\!+\!2^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\sigma\alpha^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\!.\end{split}

Following the parameter settings in Theorem
1
,
the above inequality implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(\hat{x})\|=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\leq\epsilon,

which achieves the desired result.

Discussion

The main technical novelty in our analysis for proposed RANSGD-m (Algorithm
1
) is the decomposition of
\mu_{k}
shown in equation (
5
), which differs from that used in the proofs of previous methods in the following respects.

•

Recall that the existing analysis for the synchronized normalized SGD with momentum on single machine decomposes
\mu_{k}
as follows
[
22
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu_{k}=\beta_{k}\mu_{k-1}+\beta_{k}(\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1}))+(1-\beta_{k})(\nabla F(x_{k};\xi_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k})).\end{split}

(9)

In contrast, the analysis of our RANSGD-m requires to consider the staleness caused by the asynchronous update, so that we additionally introduces the following staleness bias term

\displaystyle(1-\beta)(\nabla f(z_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k}))

in decomposition (
5
).
Accordingly, the gradient noise term in our analysis is

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}=\nabla F(z_{k};\xi_{k-\delta_{k}})-\nabla f(z_{k}),

which is with respect to the stale point
z_{k}=x_{k-\delta_{k}}
,
rather than the current iterate
x_{k}
used in the last term of decomposition (
9
).

•

The existing analysis the delay threshold protocol in asynchronous optimization considers the iteration

\displaystyle x_{k+1}\leftarrow x_{k}-\eta\nabla F(z_{k};\xi_{k-\delta_{k}}),

which is a variant of standard SGD.
As a comparison, our iteration scheme (
2
) includes the momentum and the normalization, which leads to the analysis be quite different and more complicated.

The Lower Complexity Bounds

This section provides lower bounds to show the proposed RANSGD-m (Algorithm
1
) is optimal for both fixed and universal computation models with heavy-tailed noise.

5.1
The Fixed Computation Model

We first focus on the fixed computation model corresponding to Assumption
3
, i.e., each worker
i
has a fixed upper bound
\tau_{i}>0
on the time complexity to access a stochastic gradient estimate.
We consider the algorithm class by following the definition of
Tyurin and Richtarik 2023

but generalize the order of central moment for gradient noise from
p=2
to
p\in(1,2]
.

Definition 1
.

An asynchronous stochastic first-order oracle algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}=\{A_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
over
n
workers under the fixed computation model satisfies the following constraints.

•

Each worker
i\in[n]
maintains an internal state

s_{i}=(s_{t,i},s_{x,i},s_{q,i})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0,1\},

where
s_{t,i}\in{\mathbb{R}}
stores start time of
current computation,

s_{x,i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
stores the corresponding requested point,
and
s_{q,i}
stores the state of worker
i
, i.e.,
s_{q,i}=0
when worker
i
is idle and
s_{q,i}=1
when worker
i
is busy.

•

Each worker
i\in[n]
is associated with the time sensitive stochastic first-order oracle
O^{\widehat{\nabla}F}_{\tau_{i}}
, which takes input as the current time
t
, requested point
x
, and current state
s_{i}
and output
(s_{i,+},g)=O^{\widehat{\nabla}F}_{\tau_{i}}(t,x,s_{i})
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&~\bigl(s_{i,+},g\bigr)=O^{\widehat{\nabla}F}_{\tau_{i}}(t,x,s_{i}):=\begin{cases}\bigl((t,x,1),0\bigr),&s_{q,i}=0,\\[2.0pt]
\bigl((s_{t,i},s_{x,i},1),0\bigr),&s_{q,i}=1\ \text{and}\ t<s_{t,i}+\tau_{i},\\[2.0pt]
\bigl((0,0,0),\widehat{\nabla}F(s_{x,i};\xi)\bigr),&s_{q,i}=1\ \text{and}\ t\geq s_{t,i}+\tau_{i},\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\xi
is sampled from distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
and
\widehat{\nabla}F(s_{x,i};\xi)

can be accessed by the fixed computation model described by Assumption
3
and

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\nabla}F(s_{x,i};\xi)]=\nabla f(s_{x,i})
and

\mathbb{E}[\|\widehat{\nabla}F(s_{x,i};\xi)-\nabla f(s_{x,i})\|^{p}]\leq\sigma^{p}
for some
p\in(1,2]
and
\sigma>0
.

•

The asynchronous stochastic first-order
oracle algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}=\{A_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
initializes
t_{0}=0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then performs oracles
\{O^{\widehat{\nabla}F}_{\tau_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}

during the iterations.
At round
k
, after observing gradient estimates
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\}
from the outputs of the past oracle calls,
the algorithm perform
A_{k}
to select a wall-clock time
t_{k+1}
such that
t_{k+1}\geq t_{k}
and worker
i_{k+1}
to output

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we study asynchronous stochastic nonconvex optimization with arbitrarily heterogeneous computation times across workers.
We focus on the setting of heavy-tailed gradient noise under the
p
-BCM assumption and propose an asynchronous normalized
stochastic gradient descent with momentum and delay threshold.
Our analysis shows that the proposed method has the optimal time complexity for both the fixed computation model and the universal computational model. We also provide empirical studies on synthetic problem and training the language model to validate the advantage of our method.

In future work, we are interested in extending our ideas to design asynchronous stochastic algorithms for solving the nonconvex nonsmooth optimization problem.
We also would like to establish asynchronous stochastic algorithms for decentralized optimization with heavy-tailed noise.

Enhancing the performance of pathogen detection in microfluidic systems via gyrotactic microorganism driven transport in three dimensional rotating flows of PHNF with Brownian and thermophoretic diffusion effect
Authors: Davood Domiri Ganji, Mehdi Mahboobtosi, Fateme Nadalinia Chari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbstractThis study presents an original analysis of heat and mass transfer in magnetohydrodynamic flow of a penta‑hybrid nanofluid, a configuration that uniquely combines five distinct nanoparticles with motile microorganisms a combination not previously explored in channel‑flow studies. By incorporating Joule heating, radiative heat transfer, chemical reactivity, and bioconvective effects, the work introduces a new framework for understanding complex thermal and solutal transport in multifunctional nanofluid systems. The governing equations are reduced from partial differential equations to ordinary differential equations using a similarity transformation, and solved semi-analytically with the differential transform method. The findings show that increasing the magnetic parameter raises the temperature profile while decreasing the transverse velocity, concentration, and microorganism profiles. Conversely, higher Reynolds numbers enhance concentration and microorganism profiles but lower the temperature profile. Increasing the rotational parameter and Eckert number increases temperature but decreases concentration and microorganism profiles. The thermophoresis parameter also raises the temperature but lowers concentration and microorganism profiles. The Nusselt number decreases with higher M and Ec but increases with higher R. Higher Schmidt and Brownian motion parameters increase the Sherwood number, whereas an increase in the thermophoresis parameter decreases it. Finally, increasing the Lewis number reduces microorganism density, while higher Peclet and Schmidt numbers increase microorganism density. This model has applications in particle cooling in electronic devices, microreactors, drug delivery systems, solar thermal technologies, and biomedical microfluidic platforms, with further potential for optimizing energy systems and advanced medical technologies.Graphical abstract Keywords: Microfluidic systems, PHNF, Microorganisms, Pathogen detection

Introduction

Nanoparticles are ultra-fine materials distinguished by their quantum-scale confinement, exceptional heat transfer attributes, and the presence of a relatively large proportion of atoms situated on their surface. Due to these characteristic features, nanoparticles have drawn immense interest and are extensively applied to the construction of nanowires, nanoscale pigments, high-sensitivity sensors, drug-delivery tools, and magnetic-based technologies [1]. When such particles, with typical dimensions substantially smaller than the nanometer scale, are distributed in a typical liquid medium, the resultant mixture is referred to as a nanofluid. In a base or host fluid, these suspensions are typically colloidal systems with nanoparticles smaller than 50 nm in length, producing stable colloids. The initial idea on nanofluids was postulated by Choi and Eastman [2], representing the inception point for this line of research. Follow-on studies, including the contribution by Awais et al. [3], have highlighted that nanofluids play an indispensable role in the enhancement of the efficacy of microchannel heat exchangers. In the same vein, Ali et al. [4] investigated the impact arising due to the introduction of baffles in horizontal channels and demonstrated remarkable heat transfer enhancement for magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) nanofluid flows. In yet another contribution, Sarfraz and Muhammad [5] conducted an elaborate evaluation on the heat as well as the mass transmission phenomena in MHD Jeffrey fluids entrained by a spinning disk.

Throughout the years, many techniques have been put forward by researchers to increase the heat transfer potential of regular or pure working media. One of the most successful approaches has been the suspension of nanoparticles, significantly increasing the heat transfer capability of regular media. Another development in this area is the concept of hybrid nanofluids, which differ from single-component nanofluids by the fact that these media are synthesized by simultaneously dispersing two different species of nanoparticles in the base fluid. Due to the exceptional heat transfer potential of the former, hybrid nanofluids have received widespread interest in the past few years, especially for solar energy and renewable energy systems. In this context, for example, Akhter et al. [6] analyzed buoyancy-driven magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) hybrid nanofluids with alumina and copper nanoparticles, shedding new light on energy processes. In the same spirit, Muhammad and Sarfraz [7] investigated the application of the Cattaneo–Christov heat flux formulation to silicon dioxide–gold nanomaterial fluid flows, deepening the theoretical formulation of the latter. Following on this study, Muhammad et al. [8] conducted an in-depth heat transfer analysis on the transport of hybrid nanofluid fluid flowing between two parallel disks. The steady advancement in the field of nanofluids finally culminated in the development of ternary hybrid nanofluids where three different nanoparticles are embedded in the same carrier fluid. This innovation is an advancement over the binary hybrids by enabling the tailoring of thermophysical properties for highly critical applications. Rajput et al. [9] investigated squeezing flows for ternary hybrid nanofluids between parallel plates by examining variegated physical conditions to demonstrate the relevance for industrial and technical purposes. Ullah et al. [10] also conducted a numerical study on ternary hybrid nanofluid motion with an application of thermal radiation on the parallel-disk setup. Adnan and Ashraf [11] also discussed heat transfer dynamics for ternary nanofluids through parallel plates considering radiative effects and finally concluded that these nanofluids exhibited better thermophysical behavior when contrasted with the normal or binary counterparts. A penta-hybrid nanofluid is the next-generation class of designed fluid where five diverse configurations of nanoparticles are simultaneously distributed inside the ordinary base fluid for superior thermophysical and rheological behavior relative to mono, binary, ternary, or quaternary nanofluids. By incorporating numerous nanoparticles with varying thermal conductivities, specific heats, and surfaces, the penta-hybrid nanofluids confer the beneficial effects of the combination of the individual nanoparticles, notably the enormous increase in heat transfer rates, performance stability, and energy transport efficacy. Multicomponent formulation facilitates the property tailoring for complex heat management systems, such as next-generation heat exchangers, solar energy harvesting modules, as well as advanced electronic coolers. This nascent concept is an extension of the nanofluid technology where the collective behavior of heterogenous nanoparticles is leveraged for the optimal performance of the fluid for high-efficiency energy as well as industrial applications. Refs [12–16] are studies conducted on penta hybrid nonfluids.

Joule heating or Ohmic or resistive heating is the physical phenomenon where electric energy is converted into heat when an electric current flows through a conducting medium. This power conversion of electric power to heat has far-reaching implications, especially in emerging thermal management technologies like magnetic refrigeration units, advanced electronic device cooling, and others where heat control and energy efficacy need to be accurately managed. In light of its importance, Muhammad et al. [17] explored copper (Cu) and molybdenum disulfide (MoS₂) nanoparticles-based hybrid nanofluids by imposing Ohmic heating effects, presenting the changes in heat transfer behavior. Likewise, Rasheed et al. [18] explored the combination between Joule heating with chemical reactivity in magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows, indicating that high magnetic field effects intensified thermal gradient but also decreased fluid velocity profile. Carrying on with the line of exploration, Sarfaraz et al. [19] investigated stagnation-point flow for Walter's liquid model-B over a porous medium by considering both the effects of MHD and radiative heat transfer to illuminate elaborate behavior resulting due to Joule heating. Additional contribution has been through the work of Abbas et al. [20], who discussed the flow of fluid by the Darcy–Forchheimer flow through porous sheets considering Ohmic heating effects, providing information on porous medium heat transfer. Furthermore, Yasir et al. [21] discussed the Joule heating and relevant heat characteristics in boundary-layer flow over a shrinking medium with the presence of gyrotactic microorganisms by presenting the coupling between bio-convective behavior with resistive heating phenomenon.

Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows hold pride of place in contemporary science and technology as they couple the fundamental principles of electromagnetism with fluid mechanics. Their range of application extends across energy conversion tools and heat management devices, through metallurgical processing to materials technology. Ali et al. [22], for instance, conducted a numerical study on the motion of hybrid nanofluid in a cylindrical regime with an emphasis on MHD effects on heat transfer performance. Following on the same line, Hafeez et al. [23] investigated ternary hybrid nanofluids in the context of a localized heat supply and analyzed the effect of magnetic forces induced on flow direction and temperature distributions. Ajithkumar et al. [24] studied the Sutterby-type nanofluid enriched with microorganisms through the mechanism of peristaltic pumping with an analysis of the combined agency of bioconvection, Hall current, and internal heat supply in the porous channel. Murshid et al. [25] investigated unsteady flow of the hybrid nanofluid between parallel plates including the entropy generation to reveal the thermodynamic irreversibility of the system through the agency of the magnetic field. Akhter et al. [26] simulated the magnetohydrodynamics-based numerical analysis for the flow due to double-diffusive mixture convection in an electrically conducting wavy porous square enclosure with the influence of magnetic fields. Jagadesh et al. [27], following the similar line, used the computational approach to explore the effect of the viscous dissipation function as well as the effect of the activation energy in the inclined-channel MHD flows to understand the energy transport as well as the kinetics of chemicals in magnetized fluid regions. References [28–32] Other studies conducted in this field.

Bioconvective flows refer to fluid motion resulting from the collective motion of motile microorganisms, where bioprocesses directly cause the resulting hydrodynamics. Examples are the upward swimming motion of bacteria or the directed motion of algae, which collectively induce the large-scale flow behavior in natural systems like the oceans, ponds, and other aquatic habitats. Research on such flows has also attained relevance due to the strong potential for microorganism-induced motion to impact the mixing, mass transfer, as well as energy distribution in both natural and designed systems. For example, Mishra et al. [33] investigated entropy generation for ternary hybrid Jeffery nanofluid flows with explicit inclusion for the contribution due to microorganism-induced motion for the purpose of optimizing the heat as well as the transport regime. Bafe et al. [34] conducted an investigation for the motion over a spinning surface for the three-dimensional, incompressible non-Newtonian fluid, where the interlink between the Brownian diffusion as well as the microbial activity was evaluated for the purpose of quantifying the microscale behavior for the suspension. Non-Newtonian Walter's-B fluid model was proposed by Khan et al. [35], in the absence of magnetic fields, for the description of microorganism transport with the consideration of the combined effect due to heat sources interiorly generated and heat radiation. Ajithkumar [36] considered bioconvective peristaltic flow of Casson-type nanofluids through a porous compliant channel for the discussion on the effect due to microorganism activity in compliant geometries. Likewise, Raza et al. [37] discussed the microbial transport between porous disks using chemicals with the consideration of the interaction between reactive flow conditions with bio-motility.

Semi-analytical approaches are numerical schemes that merge the sophistication of analytical solutions with the versatility of numerical algorithms so that complex nonlinear problems may efficiently be solved. Of these approaches, the Differential Transform Method (DTM) has been particularly influential because it transforms differential equations into the recurrence relation format by simply transforming the original functions through the power series description. In doing so, DTM circumvents the discretization and the need for linearization, minimizes the need for computational labor, and yields swiftly converging series solutions for linear and nonlinear problems alike. So far, the method has been successfully implemented for problems involving fluid dynamics, heat and mass transfer, magnetohydrodynamics, and other models relevant to the field of engineering where actual closed-form solutions are not obtainable. In scientific research, the method finds particular admiration for its ability to present transparent physical insight combined with the amenability for computation so that the method finds particular usefulness for the analysis of coupled, multiphysics transport phenomena. References [38–41] Other studies conducted in this field.

Penta hybrid nanofluids form another category of synthetic fluids developed by mixing five different nanoparticles into common base fluids such as water, ethylene glycol, and oil. The new generation of such fluids is believed to work together to improve properties like thermal conductivity, viscosity, and heat transfer performance of these nanofluids beyond the capabilities of mono, hybrid, or even ternary nanofluids. Thanks to these qualities, the use of penta hybrid nanofluids has gained popularity in sectors like energy systems, cooling devices, biomedical science, and industrial applications of heat exchangers. Through the presence of five different nanoparticles, penta hybrid nanofluids can achieve better thermal behaviors and stability compared to their counterparts. In recent years, there have been more efforts towards researching the models and applications of these fluids in different physical scenarios. References [42–46] have done some research on this fluid.

Their results show that increasing Darcy number enhances permeability and significantly reduces flow resistance, while the effect of the Lorentz force decreases as the magnetic field inclination increases. Chhabra et al. [49] investigated the effects of magnetic field and hydrodynamic slip on electro‑osmotic Brinkman flow in a porous microchannel with cosine‑distributed surface zeta potential. The present work is devoted to analyzing the mechanisms of heat and mass transfer in magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow of a penta-hybrid nanofluid confined between two parallel plates. What distinguishes this research is the incorporation of multiple physical effects namely Joule heating, radiative heat transfer, chemical reactivity, and the influence of motile microorganisms into the modeling framework of penta-hybrid nanofluids, a combination that has received limited attention in channel flow studies. The interaction among five distinct nanoparticles together with microbial activity introduces new perspectives on bioconvective transport phenomena and their role in enhancing thermal and solutal distribution. The governing system is initially formulated in the form of partial differential equations (PDEs) and then reduced to a set of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) through an appropriate similarity transformation. To obtain tractable solutions, the Differential Transform Method (DTM) is employed in a semi-analytical manner. The developed model is not only of theoretical importance but also carries potential for diverse applications, including advanced particle cooling arrangements in electronic devices, high-efficiency microreactors, controlled and targeted drug delivery systems, and solar thermal technologies. Furthermore, its implications extend to emerging fields such as bio-mimetic energy systems, smart thermal management in renewable energy devices, and the design of next-generation biomedical microfluidic platforms.

Problem description

In this study, the steady, three-dimensional, incompressible, and laminar flow of a penta hybrid nanofluid between two infinite, parallel, horizontal plates is analyzed. Two plates are oriented with a Cartesian coordinate system such that the origin is set at (0,0,0), where the vertical distance between the plates is represented by the letter h. Two plates are assumed to rotate along the y-axis with an angular velocity (Ω), which results in the appearance of centrifugal as well as Coriolis forces. Components of velocity (u, v, w) are represented in the directions x, y, z, respectively. The bottom plate is applied with stretching motion in the x-direction such that the velocity linearly depends on the position (u = ax), caused by equal but opposite forces, whereas the top sheet is kept stationary. In order to capture additional real physical effects, an external magnetic field (B₀) with uniform intensity is applied normal to the flow direction, where the effects of Joule heating as well as motioned microorganisms are also included through the formulation. Geometry of the problem

The assumptions adopted in this problem are standard for modeling rotating nanofluid flow and are intended to simplify the mathematical treatment without losing the main physical behavior of the system. The flow is taken as steady, incompressible, and laminar because such channel flows usually operate under low Reynolds number conditions, while the assumption of infinite parallel plates removes edge effects and represents the core flow region more accurately. The rotation of the plates about the y-axis is included to capture Coriolis and centrifugal forces, the linear stretching velocity u = ax of the lower plate represents a common engineering surface motion, and the applied uniform magnetic field allows the magnetohydrodynamic effects and Joule heating to be examined. In addition, the inclusion of motile microorganisms helps describe bioconvective effects and nanoparticle suspension stability, so these assumptions collectively keep the model physically meaningful, relevant to practical applications, and mathematically manageable.

Result and discussion

The Fig. 3 reflects how the velocity profile f′(η) changes for varying magnetic parameter values M for flow between two parallel plates. With increasing M, the Lorentz force due to the applied magnetic field opposes the motion of the fluid, resulting in suppressing velocity near the lower plate, up to approximately η = 0.5. In this area, the magnetic drag predominates, making the boundary layer thicker with reduced fluid speed. But after the mid-plane (η > 0.5), the effect reverses: due to the conservation of momentum and the flow between two walls, the retardation near the lower plate induces redistribution of the velocity across the channel. This redistribution, combined with the stabilizing action caused by the magnetic field, results in an relative increase of velocity nearer the upper plate. So the reduction in velocity near the lower area and its enhancement in the upper area with the larger M are caused by the mutually exclusive effects due to the Lorentz force damping near the wall and the momentum recovery towards the other plate.

Effect of M on

The Fig. 4 depicts the effect of the magnetic parameter M on the transverse velocity profile g(η). With an increase in M, the transverse velocity diminishes across the whole interval, such that the highest value is substantially suppressed. Physically, it is due to the Lorentz force resulting from the action between the applied magnetic field and the electrically conducting fluid. This force provides resistive dragging for resisting fluid motion not only along the primary flow but also across the transverse direction. With an increase in magnetic field intensity, the induced resistance transforms part of the kinetic energy to yield heat due to Joule heating, hence suppressing transverse fluid oscillations. In turn, the larger the magnetic parameter, the larger the magnetic damping effect, hence limiting fluid movement across the lateral direction with the resultant overall transverse velocity suppressed. This process identifies the stabilizing and suppressing function of magnetic fields in magnetohydrodynamic flows.

Effect of M on g

The Fig. 5 depicts the temperature profile θ(η) variation with varying magnetic parameter values M. Physically, the effect is seen to increase with an increase in M due to the fact that an intensified magnetic field creates a Lorentz force opposing fluid motion, thereby suppressing velocity but increasing resistive heating. This magnetic drag causes loss of energy to the flow, which is converted to heat energy, also known by the popular term Joule heating. Consequently, heat energy is deposited inside the boundary layer, expanding the thickness thereof but increasing the temperature profile altogether. Hence, for an increase in M, the momentum transport diminishes but the heat generation increases, resulting in temperature values increasing across the channel. This establishes the dual functionality of the magnetic field as the flow suppressant but the heat enhancer in the magnetohydrodynamic flow.

Effect of M on θ

The Fig. 6 shows that the concentration profile φ(η) diminishes when the magnetic parameter M grows. This behavior arises due to the fact that an increasingly strong magnetic field generates a Lorentz force contributing to fluid motion resistance, hence less species mixing and transport. With velocity suppressed, the diffusion effect comes on top, driving the solute particles toward the wall area instead of wide distribution across the channel. Furthermore, the magnetic damping boosts the Joule heating, accelerating the molecular motion and suppressing concentration further away from the surface. All in all, the combined impact due to the momentum suppressing as well as the thermal enhancing effect results in the thinner concentration boundary layer with increasing M.

Effect of M on ϕ

The Fig. 7 demonstrates that the profile of the microorganism χ(η) diminishes with an increase in the magnetic parameter M. This is due to an increase in the Lorentz force resisting fluid motion through the enhancement of the magnetic field, hence the decrease in the transport of motile microorganisms over the flow field. This dampening of velocity diminishes bioconvective circulation, thus the upward migration of microorganisms away from the wall diminishes. Furthermore, the increase in Joule heating due to larger values of M distorts the local temperature gradients, upsetting favorable microbial motility conditions. Consequently, the thickness of the microorganism boundary layer diminishes and the total microorganism concentration decreases with larger values of M.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive analysis of heat and mass transfer mechanisms in the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow of penta-hybrid nanofluids confined between two parallel plates, incorporating a variety of physical effects such as Joule heating, radiative heat transfer, chemical reactivity, and the impact of motile microorganisms. The novelty of this research lies in the integration of five distinct nanoparticles alongside microbial activity, offering a fresh perspective on bioconvective transport phenomena and their role in improving thermal and solutal distribution. The system is modeled using partial differential equations (PDEs), which are then converted into ordinary differential equations (ODEs) through a suitable similarity transformation. To solve the equations, the Differential Transform Method (DTM) is applied in a semi-analytical approach. The results presented here are significant not only for theoretical studies but also for practical applications in fields such as particle cooling in electronic devices, microreactors, targeted drug delivery, and solar thermal technologies. Moreover, the findings have broader implications for emerging technologies, including bio-mimetic energy systems, smart thermal management in renewable energy, and the development of advanced biomedical microfluidic platforms. Limitations

The present study simplifies the physical environment by assuming a uniform distribution of nanoparticles, effectively neglecting the complex dynamics of nanoparticle agglomeration and sedimentation that inevitably occur in real-world fluid systems. Furthermore, the model operates under the constraint of constant thermophysical properties, failing to account for the actual, non-linear variations in viscosity and thermal conductivity that arise as particle clusters accumulate and concentration gradients develop within the channel. Consequently, the study excludes the realistic impacts of transient particle migration and the influence of fluctuating suspension stability on the overall heat and mass transfer efficiency.

Future scope

Future investigations can extend this work by considering three-dimensional geometries, time-dependent flows, and more realistic nanoparticle interactions, including agglomeration and sedimentation effects. Experimental validation and advanced numerical simulations could further enhance the accuracy of the model and broaden its relevance to practical applications. Additionally, exploring different nanoparticle combinations and integrating AI-driven optimization techniques would potentially advance the development of next-generation thermal management and biomedical devices.

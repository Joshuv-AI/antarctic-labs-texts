A note on zero-cycles on bielliptic surfaces
Evangelia Gazaki 1

Abstract
We study the Chow group of zero-cycles CH 0 ​ ( S ) {\rm CH}_{0}(S) of a bielliptic surface S = ( E 1 × E 2 ) / G S=(E_{1}\times E_{2})/G , where E 1 , E 2 E_{1},E_{2} are elliptic curves and G G is a finite group acting on E 1 E_{1} by translations and on E 2 E_{2} by automorphisms such that E 2 / G ≃ ℙ 1 E_{2}/G\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1} . We show that if S S is defined over an arbitrary field k k of characteristic not equal to 2 , 3 2,3 , then the kernel of the Albanese map alb S : CH 0 ​ ( S ) deg = 0 → Alb S ​ ( k ) {\rm alb}_{S}:{\rm CH}_{0}(S)^{\deg=0}\rightarrow\rm Alb_{S}(k) is a torsion group of exponent 2 2 ⋅ | G | 2^{2}\cdot|G| or 3 2 ⋅ | G | 3^{2}\cdot|G| , depending on the type of bielliptic surface. Introduction

For a smooth projective geometrically connected variety
X
over a field
k
the Chow group
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
of zero-cycles on
X
admits a degree map

\deg:{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{Z},

sending the class
[x]
of a closed point to the degree,
[k(x):k]
, of the residue field. We will denote by
A_{0}(X)
its kernel, which coincides with the subgroup of algebraically trivial cycles. Moreover, there is an Albanese map

{\rm alb}_{X}:A_{0}(X)\to\rm Alb_{X}(k),

where
\rm Alb_{X}
is the Albanese variety associated to
X
. We will denote its kernel by
T(X)
.

In this short note we study the Chow group of zero-cycles
{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
of a bielliptic surface
S
over a field
k
. By a
bielliptic surface
we will always mean a surface
S
defined as the étale quotient
S=(E_{1}\times E_{2})/G
of a product
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
of two elliptic curves, where
G
is a finite abelian group acting on
E_{1}
by translations by a
k
-rational nonzero torsion point, and on
E_{2}
by automorpisms such that
E_{2}/G\simeq\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
.

Such surfaces have Kodaira dimension
\kappa=0
, geometric genus
p_{g}=0
and irregularity
q=1
. Given the first two properties, it follows by the classical work of Bloch, Kas and Lieberman (
BKL76
) that when
k=\overline{k}
is an algebraically closed field, then
T(S)=0
, which implies that if
S
is defined over an arbitrary field
k
, then
T(S)
is a torsion group.

When
k
is a finite field, the unramified class field theory of Kato and Saito (
(
KS83
, Proposition 9)
) gives a description of the Albanese kernel
T(S)
in terms of the torsion subgroup of the Néron-Severi group of
S
, and hence it is generally nontrivial (see
2
). When
k
is either an algebraic number field or a
p
-adic field, then it follows by work of Colliot-Thélène and Raskind (
(
CTR91
, Théorèmes A, D, 5.1)
) that
T(S)
is finite. In general though, to the author’s knowledge not much else is known about its structure.

The first theorem of this note gives more explicit information on what torsion may appear in
T(S)
over an arbitrary base field
k
.

Theorem 1

Let
S=(E_{1}\times E_{2})/G
be a bielliptic surface over a field
k
of characteristic not equal to
2
or
3
. Then the Albanese kernel
T(S)
is a torsion group of exponent
2^{2}\cdot|G|
if
2
divides
|G|
, and
3^{2}\cdot|G|
otherwise.

We note that the bielliptic surfaces have been classified into 7 different types (see
subsection 2.1
), with the order
|G|
ranging from
2
to
9
. To prove
section 1
we essentially consider each type separately, but using an intermediate étale cover
E_{1}\times E_{2}\rightarrow\tilde{S}\rightarrow S
from another bielliptic surface
\tilde{S}
, we reduce the theorem to bielliptic surfaces of two specific types, corresponding to groups
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
G=\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
.

Our second theorem gives some explicit examples of bielliptic surfaces over
p
-adic fields for which the group
T(S)
is nontrivial.

Theorem 2

There exist bielliptic surfaces
S
over
p
-adic fields such that the Albanese kernel
T(S)
has nontrivial
2
-torsion.

To emphasize the difference in strategy with the case of finite fields, we note that the nonzero classes in
T(S)
are obtained by push-forward from the abelian surface, which is chosen to have bad reduction.
The key for the construction is the Brauer-Manin pairing,

{\rm CH}_{0}(S)\times{\rm Br}(S)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},

which is a bilinear pairing between the Chow group
{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
and the Brauer group
{\rm Br}(S)
of
S
.

2.2
Zero-cycles on a Product of Elliptic Curves

We also need some facts about the Albanese kernel
T(X)
, where
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
. We will denote by
O_{i}\in E_{i}(k)
the zero element of
E_{i}
. The subgroup
A_{0}(X)
of zero-cycles of degree
0
is generated by elements of the form
[P,Q]-\deg(P,Q)[O_{1},O_{2}]
. Moreover, every rational point
(P,Q)\in X(k)
induces a special zero-cycle in the kernel
T(X)
of the Albanese map,

z_{P,Q}:=[P,Q]-[P,O_{2}]-[O_{1},Q]+[O_{1},O_{2}]=\pi_{1}^{\star}([P]-[O_{1}])\cdot\pi_{2}^{\star}([Q]-[O_{2}]).

Here we denoted by
\pi_{i}:E_{1}\times E_{2}\to E_{i}
the two projections and by
\cdot
the intersection product.
Next, suppose that
L/k
is a finite extension and we have a point
(P,Q)\in X(L)
. Then this induces closed points
P_{L}\in(E_{1})_{L},Q_{L}\in(E_{2})_{L}
and we get a zero-cycle

z_{P,Q}=\rho_{L/k\star}(z_{P_{L},Q_{L}})
, where
\rho_{L/k}:X_{L}\to X
is the projection. It is classically known that this construction gives a surjective group homomorphism

\varepsilon:\bigoplus_{L/k}(E_{1}(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{2}(L))\twoheadrightarrow T(X).

In particular, the zero-cycle
z_{P,Q}
is
bilinear
on
P,Q
.

Remark 1

We note that Raskind and Spiess (
RS00
) computed the kernel of the map
\varepsilon
. They showed an isomorphism
T(X)\simeq K(k;E_{1},E_{2})
with the Somekawa
K
-group attached to
E_{1},E_{2}
. In what follows we won’t need this fact. We will only use the bilinearity of
z_{P,Q}
.

Main results

3.1
Structure of the Albanese kernel over non-algebraically closed fields

We are now ready to prove
section 1
, which we restate here.

Theorem 7

Let
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
be a product of elliptic curves over
k
and
S=(E_{1}\times E_{2})/G
be a bielliptic surface. Then the Albanese kernel
T(S)
is a torsion group of exponent
2^{2}\cdot|G|
if
2
divides
|G|
, and
3^{2}\cdot|G|
otherwise.

Proof

The first step will be to reduce to the case when
S
is either a Type 1 or a Type 5 surface. Suppose that
|G|
is composite. Then it follows by
subsection 2.1
that the quotient map
X\xrightarrow{\pi}S
factors through a finite étale map
\tilde{S}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}S
, where
\tilde{S}
is a surface of Type
1
, if
2||G|
, and of Type
5
otherwise. Set
\varepsilon(\tilde{S})=2
, if
\tilde{S}
is of Type
1
, and
\varepsilon(\tilde{S})=3
, if
\tilde{S}
is of Type
5
.
Note that
\pi^{\prime}
is a finite morphism of degree
m=|G|/\varepsilon(\tilde{S})
. It induces a push-forward
\pi^{\prime}_{\star}:{\rm CH}_{0}(\tilde{S})\to{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
, which restricts to a homomorphism
A_{0}(\tilde{S})\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}_{\star}}A_{0}(S)
. Moreover, it follows by the universal property of the Albanese variety that
\pi^{\prime}
induces a homorphism of abelian varieties
\rm Alb_{\tilde{S}}\to\rm Alb_{S}
, and hence the push-forward map
\pi^{\prime}_{\star}
restricts further to a homomorphism

\pi^{\prime}_{\star}:T(\tilde{S})\to T(S)
. It follows easily by the identity
\pi^{\prime}_{\star}\pi^{\prime\star}=m
that

m\cdot\frac{T(S)}{\pi^{\prime}_{\star}(T(\tilde{S}))}=0

(see for example the proof of
(
GR25
, Theorem 5.2)
). Thus, it suffices to show that
T(\tilde{S})
is torsion of exponent
\varepsilon(\tilde{S})^{3}
.

From now on we assume that
S=\tilde{S}
is a bielliptic surface of Type
1
or
5
.
Similarly to the previous step, the projection map
\pi:X\to S
induces a push-forward

\pi_{\star}:T(X)\to T(S)
, such that
T(S)/\pi_{\star}(T(X))
is
\varepsilon(S)
-torsion. This reduces the problem to showing that every zero-cycle
z\in\pi_{\star}(T(X))
is
\varepsilon(S)^{2}
-torsion. Recall from
subsection 2.2
that the group
T(X)
is generated by zero-cycles of the form
\rho_{L/k\star}(z_{P_{L},Q_{L}})
, where
L/k
ranges over all finite extensions of
k
and
(P,Q)\in X(L)
. We have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{L/k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{L/k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

which yields
\rho_{L/k\star}\pi_{L\star}=\pi_{\star}\rho_{L/k\star}
.
Thus, it is enough to show that
\pi_{L\star}(z_{P_{L},Q_{L}})
is
\varepsilon(S)^{2}
-torsion for
(P_{L},Q_{L})\in X_{L}(L)
where
L/k
ranges over all finite extensions of
k
. As the computation is uniform on
L/k
, for simplicity we assume that
(P,Q)\in X(k)
.
The method is similar for both types, but we treat each case separately for clarity.

Type 1:
Suppose
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\langle\sigma\rangle
and the action is given on rational points by
\sigma(P,Q)=(P+P_{0},-Q)
, where
P_{0}\in E_{1}[2](k)
,
P_{0}\neq O_{1}
.
We have:

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}(z_{P,Q})=

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}([P,Q]-[P,O_{2}]-[O_{1},Q]+[O_{1},O_{2}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}([P+P_{0},-Q]-[P+P_{0},O_{2}]-[O_{1},Q]+[O_{1},O_{2}]).

(8)

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}(z_{P+P_{0},-Q})=

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}([P+P_{0},-Q]-[P+P_{0},O_{2}]-[O_{1},-Q]+[O_{1},O_{2}]).

(9)

By subtracting these two relations we get

\pi_{\star}(z_{P,Q}-z_{P+P_{0},-Q})=\pi_{\star}([O_{1},-Q]-[O_{1},Q])=\pi_{\star}([P_{0},Q]-[O_{1},Q]).

Since
P_{0}
is a
2
-torsion point of
E_{1}
, on

we have a rational equivalence

, which implies that the zero-cycle
t_{1}:=[P_{0},Q]-[O_{1},Q]\in T(X)
is
2
-torsion. We conclude that

\pi_{\star}(z_{P,Q})-\pi_{\star}(z_{P+P_{0},-Q})=\pi_{\star}(t_{1})\text{ is }2-\text{torsion}.

(10)

On the other hand, bilinearity gives,

\pi_{\star}(z_{P+P_{0},-Q})=-\pi_{\star}(z_{P,Q})+\pi_{\star}(z_{P_{0},-Q}).

Since
2P_{0}=O_{1}
, bilinearity again yields that the zero-cycle
t_{2}:=z_{P_{0},-Q}
is also
2
-torsion. We conclude that

\pi_{\star}(z_{P+P_{0},-Q})+\pi_{\star}(z_{P,Q})=\pi_{\star}(t_{2})\text{ is }2-\text{torsion}.

(11)

Combining equations (

), (
11
) we conclude that

2
-torsion, and hence

-torsion.

Suppose that

and the action is given by

, where

. Using the bilinearity of

\displaystyle z_{P,Q}+z_{P+P_{0},\omega(Q)}+z_{P+2P_{0},\omega^{2}(Q)}=s_{1},

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}(z_{P,Q})=

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}([P+P_{0},\omega(Q)]-[P+P_{0},O_{2}]-[O_{1},Q]+[O_{1},O_{2}]).

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}([P+P_{0},\omega(Q)]-[P+P_{0},O_{2}]-[O_{1},\omega(Q)]+[O_{1},O_{2}]).

Subtracting the two relations yields,

\pi_{\star}(z_{P,Q}-z_{P+P_{0},\omega(Q)})=\pi_{\star}([O_{1},\omega(Q)]-[O_{1},Q])=\pi_{\star}([O_{1},\omega(Q)]-[P_{0},\omega(Q)]).

P_{0}
is a
3
-torsion point of
E_{1}
, on
{\rm CH}_{0}(E_{1})
we have a rational equivalence
3([P_{1}]-[O_{1}])=0
, and hence the zero-cycle

is

Replacing

with

and

with
\omega(Q)
gives

Combining these with equation

is

3.2
Results over
p
-adic fields

In this section we prove
section 1
. Throughout this section we will be working over a finite extension
k
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{k}
and residue field
\mathbb{F}
. We keep the same notation from the previous sections and also assume that
p\geq 5
.

We will construct an example of a product
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
of elliptic curves with split multiplicative reduction over a
p
-adic field
k
and a bielliptic surface
S
of Type 1 such that the subgroup
\pi_{\star}(T(X))
of
T(S)
is nontrivial. To do this, we will use the Brauer-Manin pairing. We start with some preliminary results on the transcendental Brauer group of such a Type 1 surface.

Lemma 13

Let
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
be a product of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that
E_{1},E_{2}
are non-isogenous over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
S=X/G
,
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, be a bielliptic surface of Type 1. Then the pullback map

\pi^{\star}:\rm{Br}(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})\to\rm{Br}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})

is injective and its image is precisely the
G
-invariant subgroup of
\rm{Br}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})[2]
. The same result holds true with
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
replaced by
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
for every prime
p
.

Proof

Considering the base change to
\mathbb{C}
, it follows by
(
FTVB22
, Theorem B)
that the pullback

\pi^{\star}:\rm{Br}(S_{\mathbb{C}})\to\rm{Br}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
is injective, because
E_{1},E_{2}
are non-isogenous. It then follows by
(
CTS21
, Proposition 5.2.3)
that we have isomorphisms

\rm{Br}(S_{\mathbb{C}})\simeq\rm{Br}(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})\simeq\rm{Br}(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})

for every prime
p
and similar isomorphisms are true for
X
.

Moreover, since
S
is of Type 1, we have isomorphisms

\rm{Br}(S_{\mathbb{C}})\simeq H^{2}(S_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z})_{{\text{tor}}}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}

(see Table

). Putting all this together, we see that the image of the pullback map
\rm{Br}(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})\xrightarrow{\pi^{\star}}\rm{Br}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
is a subgroup of
\rm{Br}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})[2]
of order
4
. It remains to show that it is precisely the
G
-invariant subgroup of the latter.
(
SZ12
, Proposition 3.3)
gives an isomorphism

\rm{Br}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})[2]\simeq\frac{\rm{Hom}(E_{1}[2],E_{2}[2])}{(\rm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2}))/2}\simeq\rm{Hom}(E_{1}[2],E_{2}[2]).

The last equality follows because
E_{1},E_{2}
are non-isogenous. Let
G=\langle\sigma\rangle
act on
X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
via
\sigma(P,Q)=(P+P_{0},-Q)
. Since the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

is commutative, it follows that
\pi^{\star}=\sigma^{\star}\pi^{\star}
, which shows that
\pi^{\star}(\rm{Br}(S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
lies in the
G
-invariant subgroup of
\rm{Br}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
. Equality will follow if we show that the latter is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. But this is clear, since every homomorphism
f:E_{1}[2]\to E_{2}[2]
sending
P_{0}
to the zero element is fixed by the
G

\hfill\Box

We next recall some facts about the
Brauer-Manin pairing
. Let
Y
be a smooth projective geometrically connected surface over
k
.
The Brauer-Manin pairing (see
(
CT95
, p. 53)
),

\langle,\rangle:\rm{CH}_{0}(Y)\times\rm{Br}(Y)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},

is defined as follows. For the class
[P]
of a closed point and a Brauer class
\alpha\in\rm{Br}(Y)
,

\langle[P],\alpha\rangle:=\rm{Cor}_{k(P)/k}(\iota_{P}^{\star}(\alpha))
, where
\iota_{P}:\rm{Spec}(k(P))\hookrightarrow X
is the closed immersion, and
\rm{Cor}_{k(P)/k}:\rm{Br}(k(P))\to\rm{Br}(k)
is the Corestriction map of Galois cohomology. The pairing induces a homomorphism

\varepsilon_{2}:\rm{CH}_{0}(Y)/2\to\rm{Hom}(\rm{Br}(Y)[2],\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

The following proposition is the analog of
(
GL24
, Proposition 3.9)
.

Proposition 14

Let
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
be a product of elliptic curves over
k
. Suppose that
E_{1},E_{2}
are not isogenous over
\overline{k}
. Let
S=X/G
,
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, be a bielliptic surface of Type 1. Then the projection map
\pi:X\to S
induces a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rm{Hom}(\rm{Br}(X)/\rm{Br}_{1}(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\star}(T(S))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rm{Hom}(\rm{Br}(S)/\rm{Br}_{1}(S)).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{2}^{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\star}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\pi^{\star}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{2}^{S}}

Here

is the algebraic Brauer group of

.

The proof is exactly analogous to the proof of
(

, Proposition 3.9)
, using first
(

lies indeed in

. This is because

implies that the
\rm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)

.

We are now ready to prove
section 1
, which we restate here.

Theorem 15

There exists a product
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
over a
p
-adic field
k
and a bielliptic surface
S=X/G
of Type 1 such that the group
T(S)
has nontrivial
2
-torsion.

Proof

Our strategy will be to find a pair of elliptic curves
E_{1},E_{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, a prime
p
and a bielliptic surface
S=X/G
of Type 1 (where as usual
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
) such that there exists a zero-cycle
z\in T(X_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
with the property
\pi_{\star}(z)\neq 0
. We start with some local analysis over an arbitrary
p
-adic field
k
with
p\geq 5
, but we implicitly assume that
X
is defined over a number field, so that
subsection 3.2
can be used.

Consider a product
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
of elliptic curves over
k
. Suppose that both
E_{1},E_{2}
have split multiplicative reduction. Then, as rigid
p
-analytic spaces,
E_{i}\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}/q_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a Tate elliptic curve for
i=1,2
, where
q_{i}\in k^{\times}
. This
p
-adic uniformization implies that for every
n\geq 1
there is a short exact sequence of
\rm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-modules

0\to\mu_{n}\to E_{i}[n]\to\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\to 0.

In fact, if
\zeta_{n}\in\overline{k}^{\times}
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity, then

E_{i}[n]=\{\zeta_{n}^{a}\sqrt[n]{q^{b}}:0\leq a,b\leq n-1\}.

From now on we assume additionally that
E_{1},E_{2}
are non-isogenous over
\overline{k}
.
Consider the Brauer-Manin pairing

\langle,\rangle_{X}:\rm{CH}_{0}(X)\times\rm{Br}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

Because
E_{1},E_{2}
both have split multiplicative reduction, it follows by
(
Yam05
, Theorem 1.2)
that the left kernel of the pairing is the maximal divisible subgroup of
\rm{CH}_{0}(X)
. As mentioned in
subsection 3.2
,
\langle,\rangle_{X}
restricts to a pairing

\langle,\rangle_{X}:T(X)\times\frac{\rm{Br}(X)}{\rm{Br}_{1}(X)}\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

Restricting further to the
n
-torsion subgroup of the Brauer group, we obtain

\langle,\rangle_{X}:\frac{T(X)}{n}\times\frac{\rm{Br}(X)[n]}{\rm{Br}_{1}(X)[n]}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}.

Let
H_{1}
be the right kernel of this pairing and
Q
be the quotient of
\displaystyle\frac{\rm{Br}(X)[n]}{\rm{Br}_{1}(X)[n]}
by
H_{1}
.
The nonzero elements of
Q
are those transcendental Brauer classes that pair nontrivially with elements of
T(X)/n
. Recall from
(
SZ12
, Proposition 3.3)
that we have an isomorphism

\rm{Br}(X)[n]/\rm{Br}_{1}(X)[n]\simeq\rm{Hom}_{\rm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)}(E_{1}[n],E_{2}[n]).

Thus, to determine the quotient
Q
we need to understand which
\rm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-equivariant homomorphisms
f:E_{1}[n]\to E_{2}[n]
pair nontrivially with
T(X)/n
. This is computed in more generality in
(
Gaz19
, Theorem 1.6)
, but in the special case when both elliptic curves have split multiplicative reduction it also follows from
(
Yam05
, Proof of Theorem 4.3)
. If we assume for simplicity that
E_{i}[n]\subset E_{i}(k)
for
i=1,2
, the answer is precisely
\rm{Hom}(\mu_{n},\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
. Notice that
\rm{Hom}(\mu_{n},\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
is considered a quotient of
\rm{Hom}(E_{1}[n],E_{2}[n])
via the compositions:

\mu_{n}\hookrightarrow E_{1}[n]\xrightarrow{\phi}E_{2}[n]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},

where the injection
\mu_{n}\hookrightarrow E_{1}[n]
and the surjection
E_{2}[n]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
are the ones induced by the
p
-adic uniformizations.

Next we focus on the case
n=2
. Assume that
E_{i}[2]\subset E_{i}(k)
for
i=1,2
. We can then choose generators of
E_{1}[2]=\langle P_{1},P_{2}\rangle
such that
P_{1}\in\mu_{2}
and
P_{2}\in E_{1}[2]/\mu_{2}
. Consider the bielliptic surface
S=X/G
, with
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\langle\sigma\rangle
acting on
E_{1}
by translation by
P_{2}
. It follows by
subsection 3.2
and its proof that the subgroup
H_{2}=\pi^{\star}(\rm{Br}(S)/\rm{Br}_{1}(S))
of
{\rm Hom}(E_{1}[2],E_{2}[2])
is precisely the one consisting of those homomorphisms
g:E_{1}[2]\to E_{2}[2]
that send
P_{2}
to
0
. We see that under the choices we made there exists a homomorphism
f\in H_{2}
such that its image in the quotient
Q=\rm{Hom}(\mu_{n},\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
is nonzero. This means that there exists
\alpha\in\pi^{\star}(\rm{Br}(S)/\rm{Br}_{1}(S))
and
z\in T(X)/2
such that

\langle\pi_{\star}(z),\alpha\rangle_{S}=\langle z,\pi^{\star}(\alpha)\rangle_{X}\neq 0.

(Note that the first equality follows by
(
GL24
, Lemma 3.7)
). We conclude that
\pi_{\star}(z)\in T(S)
is nontrivial.

To close the argument, we need to verify that such elliptic curves exist, and we can even choose them to be defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, so that we can apply
subsection 3.2
. These are easy to find. For example consider the elliptic curves

E_{1}:y^{2}=x^{3}-14931x+220590,\;\;\;\;\;E_{2}:y^{2}=x^{3}-3171x+68510

with LMFDB labels
33.a2
and
198.a2
respectively. Both curves have split multiplicative reduction at
p=11
. At the same time,
E_{2}
has non-split multiplicative reduction at
p=2
, while
E_{1}
has good reduction at
2
, and hence
E_{1},E_{2}
are not isogenous over
\overline{k}
.

\hfill\Box

We note that in the above proof the choice to consider elliptic curves with bad reduction was necessary. If both elliptic curves have instead good reduction over
k
, then given that
p>2
, it follows by
(
RS00
, Corollary 3.5.1)
that the group
T(X)
is
2
-divisible. Thus, there is no
z\in T(X)
such that
\pi_{\star}(z)\neq 0
. This leads to the following Corollary, which improves
subsection 3.1
in this case.

Corollary 16

Let
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
be a product of elliptic curves over a
p
-adic field
k
. Assume that
p\geq 5
and both elliptic curves have good reduction. Let
S=(E_{1}\times E_{2})/G
be a bielliptic surface of Type 1 (resp. of Type 5) over
k
. Then the Albanese kernel
T(S)
is
2
-torsion (resp.
3
-torsion).

Remark 2

It is easy to see that if
E_{1},E_{2}
have good reduction, then so does the bielliptic surface
S
. Let
\overline{S}
be the reduction surface over the residue field
\mathbb{F}
. It follows by the unramified class field theory of Kato and Saito (
(
KS83
, Proposition 9)
) that the group
A_{0}(\overline{S})
fits into a short exact sequence

0\to\text{NS}(\overline{S})_{{\text{tor}}}\to A_{0}(\overline{S})\to\rm Alb_{\overline{S}}(\mathbb{F})\to 0,

where
\text{NS}(\overline{S})
is the Néron-Severi group of
\overline{S}
. By passing to a finite unramified extension if necessary, we may assume that
\text{NS}(\overline{S})_{{\text{tor}}}
coincides with the torsion subgroup of the geometric Néron-Severi group
\text{NS}(\overline{S}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
, which is nontrivial, since there is a class corresponding to the abelian étale cover
\overline{X}\xrightarrow{\overline{\pi}}\overline{S}
. We conclude that the Albanese kernel of the reduction
T(\overline{S})
is nontrivial. Given that the specialization map induces a surjective group homomorphism

A_{0}(S)\to A_{0}(\overline{S})

(cf.
(
CT11
, Théorème 0.1)
), it is likely that even in the good reduction case
T(S)\neq 0
, but this is not something we can verify in the current article.

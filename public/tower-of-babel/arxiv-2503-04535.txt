Infinitesimal Newton–Okounkov bodies on products of curves
Mihai Fulger, Victor Lozovanu

Abstract
. We compute the generic infinitesimal Newton–Okounkov body of a natural polarization on products of curves. This appears to be the first nontrivial full description of such a body in arbitrary dimension.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n
and
L
be a big line bundle on
X
. For any complete flag
Y_{\bullet}
of smooth subvarieties of
X
one associates a valuative object, an
n
-dimensional compact convex set

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(L)\ \subseteq\ \mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

called the
Newton–Okounkov body (NObody)
. These bodies determine the numerical class of
L
(
[
13
]
), and offer convex geometric interpretation to various positivity invariants such as the volume and restricted volumes of
L
(
[
21
]
), base loci (
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
) the Nakayama
\sigma
-Zariski decomposition of
L
(
[
26
]
), etc. The standard references for NObodies are
[
14
,
21
]
.

Local positivity aspects of
L
can be studied on the blow-up
\pi:{\rm Bl}_{x}\to X
, working with the line bundle
\pi^{*}L
, and with linear flags in the exceptional divisor
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. This leads to the
infinitesimal NObody (iNObody)

{\Delta}_{x}(L)
.
Indeed
[
16
]
prove that
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
determines how
x
sits relative to base loci of
L
, as well as the Seshadri constant (also in
[
25
]
) and Fujita–Nakayama invariant (or width) of
L
at
x
.

When the linear flag above is sufficiently general, we obtain
generic iNObodies
. These were introduced in
[
21
]
and further studied in
[
19
]
. In
[
7
]
we show how to read even more refined invariants that may not be clearly visible for special linear flags.

NObodies are known for
T
-invariant flags on toric varieties by
[
21
]
, and relatively well-understood on surfaces by
[
21
]
and
[
15
]
.
The computational picture is murkier for iNObodies, at least due to their connection to the Seshadri constant, a quantitative difficult invariant on its own. One expects similar difficulty when generality conditions are imposed on the flag such as when working with generic iNObodies, or on toric varieties for NObodies constructed from flags that meet the dense torus.

In this paper we construct perhaps the first examples of generic iNObodies in any dimension.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X=C_{1}\times\ldots\times C_{n}
be a product of
n\geqslant 1
smooth complex projective curves, polarized by a box product
L=L_{1}\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes L_{n}
where
L_{i}
have degree 1 on
C_{i}
. Then for any
x\in X
,

{\Delta}_{x}(L)\ =\ \textup{simplex}\bigl\{(0,\ldots,0),(1,1,0,\ldots,0),(2,0,2,0,\ldots,0),\ldots,(n-1,0,\ldots,0,n-1),(n,0,\ldots,0)\bigr\}\ .

The starting point of the proof goes back to an idea from
[
21
]
. In short, it describes the vertical slice
\Delta_{x}(L)\cap\{t\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
for
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
as the NObody of a graded subalgebra
R(W^{t}_{\bullet})\subseteq\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with respect to generic linear flags on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, where
W^{t}_{\bullet}
is the graded linear series of the images of the restriction maps

H^{0}\big(\overline{X},m\cdot(\pi^{*}(L)-tE)\big)\ \longrightarrow\ H^{0}(E,\mathscr{O}_{E}(mt))

for
m
sufficiently large and divisible. Asymptotically,
W_{\bullet}^{t}
records the hypersurfaces that appear as tangent cones of sections of
L
with multiplicity
t
at
x
.
The challenge is to provide an intrinsic geometric interpretation of the family of graded linear series
W_{\bullet}^{t}
. When
X=C_{1}\times\ldots\times C_{n}
, intersection theory on
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
implies that
W_{\bullet}^{t}
is contained in a family of complete linear series associated to divisors on
X_{\Sigma}
, the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
in
n
points in general linear position. We show that this inclusion is asymptotically an equality. For this we use that
X_{\Sigma}
is a toric variety and the asymptotic behaviour of complete linear series on them can be managed through convex geometry.
As a consequence, the vertical slices of
\Delta_{x}(L)
are NObodies associated to a ray of divisors on
X_{\Sigma}
with respect to a generic (strict transform) linear flag. Lastly, the global (gluing all
t\geqslant 0
) NObody on this special ray of divisors on
X_{\Sigma}
is exactly the convex set described in the statement, as we are able to prove the existence of sections whose valuation are the vertices of the simplex.

For box-products of arbitrary degrees the vertical slices of
\Delta_{x}(L)
are still a family of NObodies on
X_{\Sigma}
with respect to general linear flags. But it is harder to guess the vertices. It seems an interesting problem to find an algorithm that starts with a polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
that is a NObody on a toric variety with respect to a
T
-invariant flag, and construct another polytope, that will be a NObody on the same toric space but with respect to a generic linear flag.

In dimension
n\leqslant 3
, we can find non-trivial sections on
X_{\Sigma}
, whose valuations provide the vertices of a slice of the global NObody on
X_{\Sigma}
with respect to general linear flags. More precisely:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X=C_{1}\times\ldots\times C_{n}
be a product of smooth complex projective curves, polarized by a box product
L=L_{1}\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes L_{n}
with
\deg L_{i}=d_{i}
. Assume
d_{1}\geqslant d_{2}\geqslant\ldots\geqslant d_{n}>0
. Let
x\in X
be any point.

•

If
n=2
, then
\Delta_{x}(L)
is the isosceles trapezoid with vertices
(0,0)
,
(d_{2},d_{2})
,
(d_{1}+d_{2},0)
,
(d_{1},d_{2})
.

•

If
n=3
, then
\Delta_{x}(L)
is the convex hull of the 9 points

(0,0,0)
,
(d_{3},d_{3},0)
,
(d_{2},d_{3},d_{2}-d_{3})
,
(d_{1},d_{3},d_{2}-d_{3})
,
(d_{1}+d_{2}-d_{3},d_{3},0)
,
(d_{2}+d_{3},0,d_{2}+d_{3})
,
(d_{1}+d_{3},0,d_{2}+d_{3})
,
(d_{1}+d_{2},0,2d_{3})
, and
(d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3},0,0)
,

In both cases, the shapes degenerate to the simplex in Theorem
1.1
when
d_{i}=1
for all
i
, while when the
d_{i}
are not all equal the shapes are not simplicial.

It turns out that the verticies of our iNObodies from Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
happen exactly when there is a change in the associated base loci of the graded linear series
W_{\bullet}^{t}
.
These ideas are generalized by the authors in
[
7
]
on any projetcive variety
X
by connecting three aspects of local positivity of a line bundle
L
on
X
at a point
x\in X
. On one hand we have infinitesimal successive minima, then jets and finally polytopal upper and lower bounds on the generic iNObodies.
Moreover, in
[
8
]
the authors study the shape of generic iNObodies on Jacobians in small dimensions. The main issue here is realizing at least asymptotically the family
W_{\bullet}^{t}
as
complete
graded linear series on a modification of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. For example, for non-hyperelliptic Jacobian threefolds,
W_{\bullet}^{t}
becomes complete on a birational model of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
that is far from being toric, while for the hyperelliptic case we may need to go to the Zariski–Riemann space.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we review the Newton–Okounkov body construction and their basic properties. In Section 3 we use intersection theory, positivity, toric geometry and Euclidean volume computations to understand the vertical slices as Newton–Okounkov bodies on
X_{\Sigma}
. In Section 4 we treat the case of dimension
n\leqslant 3
. In Section 5 we use particular incomplete linear systems on
X_{\Sigma}
to prove that the vertices of the simplex from Theorem
1.1
do belong to the iNObody, concluding its proof. In Section 6 we state and motivate some open questions.

2.
Background on (i)NObodies

2.1.
Definition and basic properties of NObodies

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional complex projective manifold. Fix
x\in X
and let
L
be a big divisor/line bundle on
X
.
An
admissible flag
is a complete flag

Y_{\bullet}:X=Y_{0}\supset Y_{1}\supset\ldots\supset Y_{n}=\{x\}

where each
Y_{i}
is irreducible of codimension
i
, and smooth at
x
.
For an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
, let
\nu_{1}(D)={\rm ord}_{Y_{1}}(D)
and inductively for each
i=2,\ldots,n
define

\nu_{i}(D)\ =\ {\rm ord}_{Y_{i}}((D-\nu_{i-1}(D)Y_{i-1})|_{Y_{i-1}})

Note that
(D-\nu_{i-1}(D)Y_{i-1})|_{Y_{i-1}}
is a well-defined divisor in
Y_{i-1}
.
Setting
\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})
, we define the NObody as the topological closure of the convex body

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(L)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\ \overline{\bigl\{\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)\ \mid\ D\equiv L,\ D\text{ effective}\bigr\}}\ \subseteq\ \mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

The standard definition, e.g.
[
21
]
, essentially uses
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalence of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors, not numerical equivalence of
\mathbb{R}
-divisors. The definitions are equivalent for big divisors and extend naturally and continuously to big numerical
\mathbb{R}
-divisor classes.
Other important general properties:

(i)

{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(L)\big)=\frac{1}{n!}\vol(L)
.

(ii)

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(L)
projects on the
\nu_{1}
axis as the segment
[\sigma_{Y_{1}}(L),\mu(L;Y_{1})]
. Here
\sigma_{Y_{1}}(L)
is the coefficient of
Y_{1}
in the negative part
N_{\sigma}(L)
of the Nakayama
\sigma
-Zariski decomposition (
[
24
]
) of
L
, while

\mu(L;Y_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup\{t\geqslant 0\ \mid\ L-tY_{1}\text{ is big}\}.

(iii)

If
Y_{1}\not\subset\textbf{{B}}_{+}(L)
and
t\geqslant 0
with
L-tY_{1}
big, then

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(L)_{\nu_{1}=t}\subseteq\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}((L-tY_{1})|_{Y_{1}}).

The inclusion is often strict since the smaller subset considers only effective divisors on
Y_{1}
that appear as restrictions of effective divisors on
X
. Furthermore,
{\rm Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(L)_{\nu_{1}=t}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\vol_{X|Y_{1}}(L-tY_{1})
with the restricted volume computed from sections of
(L-tY_{1})|_{Y_{1}}
that are the restriction of some section of
L-tY_{1}
.

(iv)

As the numerical class of
L
varies inside the cone of big
\mathbb{R}
-classes in
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}=N^{1}(X)\otimes\mathbb{R}
, the bodies
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(L)
glue in a convex cone in
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
whose slice over a big class
\xi\in N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\xi)
. We call this cone the
global NObody
on
X
with respect to the flag
Y_{\bullet}
.

2.2.
Definition and basic properties of iNObodies

Let
\pi:\overline{X}\to X
denote the blow-up of
x
with exceptional divisor
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. Consider a flag
Y_{\bullet}
in
\overline{X}
such that
Y_{0}=\overline{X}
, and
Y_{1}=E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, and
Y_{i}
is a linear
\mathbb{P}^{n-i}
inside
Y_{i-1}
for all
i>1
. We call
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}L)
an
infinitesimal NObody (iNObody)
.
Assuming
x\not\in\textbf{{B}}_{-}(L)
, e.g., if
L
is nef, then:

(i)

(0,\ldots,0)\in{\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}L)
.

(ii)

The projection of
{\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}L)
on the
\nu_{1}
-axis is the interval
[0,\mu(L;x)]
, where

\mu(L;x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup\{t\geqslant 0\ \mid\ L_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\pi^{*}L-tE\text{ is big}\}

is the
Fujita–Nakayama (or width)
of
L
at
x
.

(iii)

For all
0<t<\mu(L;x)
, we have that

(2.1)

{\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}L)_{\nu_{1}=t}\subseteq t\cdot\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(1)).

(iv)

For all
very general
linear flags
Y_{\bullet}
in
E
(meaning outside the union of countably many proper closed subsets of the flag manifold), the NObody
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}L)
is independent of
Y_{\bullet}
and we call it the
generic infinitesimal NObody (iNObody)
and denote it by
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
.

(v)

We can define a global iNObody, and a global generic iNObody.

3.
Vertical slices via multiplicity bounds and toric geometry

We prove that the vertical slices of
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
identify with NObodies of complete linear series on the blow-up
\rho:\widetilde{E}\to E
of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
in the
n
torus fixed points. The strategy is to show a double inclusion. The upper bound comes from a multiplicity estimate, via computations of positive cones. For the lower bound we draw on the toric structure of
\widetilde{E}
.

Let
X=C_{1}\times\ldots\times C_{n}
and
x=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in X
. Denote by
h_{i}
the numerical class of the “coordinate” hypersurfaces
H_{i}={\rm pr}_{i}^{-1}\{c_{i}\}
through
x
. By abuse of notation denote further by

C_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}C_{1}\times\{(c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})\}\subset C_{1}\times\ldots\times C_{n}

and similarly define
C_{i}\subset X
.
For
\pi:\overline{X}\to X
the blow-up of
x
with exceptional divisor
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, the strict transforms
\overline{C}_{i}
of the
C_{i}
are now pairwise disjoint.

Lemma 3.1

(Positive cones on
X
and
\overline{X}
)
.

(1)

On
X
, the class
D=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}h_{i}
is pseudoeffective if and only if
d_{i}\geqslant 0
for all
i
.

(2)

Assume
n\geqslant 2
. On
\overline{X}
, the class of
D=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\overline{H}_{i}+eE
is pseudoeffective if and only if
d_{i}\geqslant 0
for all
i
and
e\geqslant 0
. In particular, for
f\geqslant 0
, a class
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\pi^{*}h_{i}-fE
is pseudoeffective if and only if
c_{i}\geqslant 0
for all
i
and
f\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}
.

It follows from (2), that if
D
is as in (1), then
\mu(D;x)=\sum_{i}d_{i}
.

Proof.

For both parts one implication is trivial. It is sufficient to treat the case where
D
is effective.

(1) The curves
C_{i}\subseteq X
are movable hence
D\cdot C_{i}\geqslant 0
for all
i
, and
h_{i}\cdot C_{j}=\delta_{ij}
, hence
D\cdot C_{i}=d_{i}
for all
i
.

(2) Since
\pi_{*}D=\sum_{i}d_{i}h_{i}
is also effective, then
d_{i}\geqslant 0
for all
i
by (1). Without loss of generality, suppose
D
does not have any of the
\overline{H}_{i}
in its support. Then
D|_{\overline{H}_{n}}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}\overline{H}_{i}+eE
is effective, in one dimension less. Conclude by induction. For the base case
n=2
, note that
\overline{H}_{1}
,
E
,
\overline{H}_{2}
are
-1
curves. If
C
is an irreducible curve distinct from them, and
C\equiv d_{1}\overline{H}_{1}+d_{2}\overline{H}_{2}+eE
with
d_{1},d_{2}\geqslant 0
, then
C\cdot\overline{H}_{1}\geqslant 0
forces
e\geqslant 0
.
∎

The following lemma provides some necessary conditions that are satisfied by the restricted linear series
W_{\bullet}^{t}
from the introduction. It shows how far away they are from not being a complete series on
E=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}h_{i}
where
d_{i}\geqslant 0
. Denote
D_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\pi^{*}D-tE
. Then for all
t\in[0,\ldots,d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}]
and for every effective divisor
D^{\prime}
numerically equivalent to
D_{t}
, we have

\mult_{\overline{C}_{i}}D^{\prime}\geqslant t-d_{i}

for all
i
.

Proof.

Let
\rho:\widetilde{X}\to\overline{X}
be the blow-up of all
\overline{C}_{i}
with exceptional divisors
E_{i}
. As
\overline{C}_{i}
is the complete intersection of the
\overline{H}_{j}
with
j\neq i
and
\overline{H}_{j}\equiv\pi^{*}H_{j}-E
, then
E_{i}\simeq C_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
and
-E_{i}|_{E_{i}}
represents
\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}(c_{i})\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-2}}(1)
.

It is sufficient to prove that
(\rho^{*}D_{t}-mE_{i})|_{E_{i}}
is not pseudoeffective for
m<t-d_{i}
. To start note that

D
restricts to
C_{i}\subset X
as a divisor of degree
d_{i}
. Then
D_{t}
restricts to
\overline{C}_{i}
as a divisor of degree
d_{i}-t
. Finally
\rho^{*}D_{t}-mE_{i}
restricts to
E_{i}\simeq C_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
as a bidegree
(d_{i}-t+m,m)
divisor. This is only pseudoeffective when
m\geqslant 0
and
m\geqslant t-d_{i}
.
∎

As a consequence the graded linear series
W_{\bullet}^{t}
is contained in a complete linear series on the blow-up
\rho:\widetilde{E}\rightarrow E
of the points
p_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\overline{C}_{i}\cap E
, viewed as the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
that is the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
at
n
torus invariant points. Our goal is to show that asymptotically in terms of NObodies these two graded linear series coincide when
t
varies.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}>0
and
D=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}h_{i}
. For all
t\in(0,d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n})
we have

{\Delta}_{x}(D)_{\nu_{1}=t}=\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(t\rho^{*}H-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}(t-d_{i})E_{i}),

where
Y_{\bullet}
is a very general linear flag in
E
,
\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}
is its strict transform in
\widetilde{E}
,
H
is the hyperplane class in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
and
E_{i}
is the exceptional divisors of
\rho
over
p_{i}
.

Proof.

The very general choice of
Y_{\bullet}
is implicit in the definition of
{\Delta}_{x}(D)
. We will see in the proof that any linear flag that avoids the points
p_{i}
will suffice.

We explain how Lemma
3.2
gives the “
\subseteq
” inclusion. If
D^{\prime}
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor, numerically equivalent to
D
and such that
{\rm mult}_{x}D^{\prime}=t
, then the strict transform in
\overline{X}
satisfies that
\overline{D^{\prime}}\cap E
is a degree
t
effective divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
that passes through
p_{i}
with multiplicity at least
t-d_{i}
by Lemma
3.2
, and has valuation
(\nu_{2}(D^{\prime}),\ldots,\nu_{n}(D^{\prime}))
with respect to the linear flag
E=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}=Y_{1}\supset\ldots\supset Y_{n}
.
The strict transform of this divisor in
\widetilde{E}
determines an element of the complete linear system of
t\rho^{*}H-\sum_{i}(t-d_{i})E_{i}
with the same valuation with respect to the strict transform flag.

For the reverse inclusion it is sufficient to prove that the convex bodies have the same Euclidean volume. We have the “
\leqslant
” inequality for each
t
. It is clear that if we let
t
vary in
[0,d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}]
,
the volumes of both sides vary continuously.
It is then enough to prove that

{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\Delta_{x}(D)\big)\ =\ \int\nolimits_{t=0}^{d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}}{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\big(\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(t\rho^{*}H-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}(t-d_{i})E_{i})\big)dt

As
(D^{n})=n!\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
, then by
[
21
, Theorem A]
the Euclidean volume of
\Delta_{x}(D)
is
\prod_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
. Similar the volume being integrated on the right is actually
\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\cdot\vol_{\widetilde{E}}(t\rho^{*}H-\sum(t-d_{i})E_{i})
. This can be computed with toric means since
\widetilde{E}
is a toric variety. The material below is inspired by
[
2
, Section 3.1]
. For a quick introduction to the theory of toric varieties: fans, polytope associated to a divisor, etc, see
[
10
]
.

The fan of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
is given by the simplicial fan with the
n
rays
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}
are the standard basis and
f_{n}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}e_{i}
. The fan of
\widetilde{E}
, the blow-up
\rho:\widetilde{E}\to\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
of the
T
-invariant points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
is obtained by refining this fan with the rays
e_{i}=-f_{i}
. The associated
T
-invariant divisors are denoted
F_{i}
for the
f_{i}
, and
E_{i}
for the
e_{i}
. The divisor
F_{i}
is the strict transform in
\widetilde{E}
of
\overline{H}_{i}\cap E
, the linear span of
(p_{j})_{j\neq i}
, and
E_{i}
is the exceptional divisor of the blow-up of
p_{i}
. If
H
denotes the hyperplane class in
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, then we have the relations

F_{i}=\rho^{*}H-\sum\nolimits_{j\neq i}E_{i}.

For our classes of interest we have

t\rho^{*}H-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}(t-d_{i})E_{i}=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{t}{n}F_{i}+\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}-\frac{t}{n})E_{i}\ .

Their associated polytopes in
M_{\mathbb{R}}=N_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
are

\begin{split}P_{t}\ &\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\ \{m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ \langle m,f_{i}\rangle\geqslant-\frac{t}{n},\ \langle m,e_{i}\rangle\geqslant\frac{t}{n}-d_{i}\}\\
&=\ \{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ m_{i}\geqslant-\frac{t}{n},\ \sum m_{i}\leqslant\frac{t}{n},\ m_{i}\leqslant d_{i}-\frac{t}{n},\ \sum m_{i}\geqslant\frac{t}{n}-d_{n}\}\end{split}

We have
{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\big(\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(t\rho^{*}H-\sum_{i}(t-d_{i})E_{i})\big)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\vol_{\widetilde{E}}(t\rho^{*}H-\sum_{i}(t-d_{i})E_{i})={\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\big(P_{t}\big)
.
After the translations
m_{i}\mapsto m_{i}+\frac{t}{n}
, we have that
P_{t}
has the same volume as

P^{\prime}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\bigl\{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\ \mid\ 0\leqslant m_{i}\leqslant d_{i},\ t-d_{n}\leqslant\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n-1}m_{i}\leqslant t\bigr\}\ .

Let

A(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\biggl(\bigl\{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\ \mid\ 0\leqslant m_{i}\leqslant d_{i},\ \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n-1}m_{i}\leqslant t\bigr\}\biggr)\ .

We have
{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\big(P^{\prime}_{t}\big)=A(t)-A(t-d_{n})
. As
A(t)=0
if
t\leqslant 0
and
A(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}
for
t\geqslant d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n-1}
, then

\int\nolimits_{t=0}^{d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}}{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\big(P^{\prime}_{t}\big)dt=\int\nolimits_{t=0}^{d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}}(A(t)-A(t-d_{n}))dt=\int\nolimits_{t=d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n-1}}^{d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}}A(t)dt=\prod\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\ .

The conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The function
A(t)
is quasipolynomial, changing branches whenever
t
is the sum of coordinates of a vertex of the box
\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}[0,d_{i}]

there exists a vertex in
\Delta_{x}(D)
with
\nu_{1}
-coordinate
\sum_{i\in I}d_{i}
. The case

of Theorem

shows that these are not all the vertices of

in general.

5.
Balanced box-products in arbitrary dimension

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. The strategy will be similar to that of Theorem
1.2
, although the argument here is existential, not constructive.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
n\geqslant d\geqslant 1
we have the following inclusion:

d\cdot{\bf e}_{d}\ \in\ \Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(d\rho^{*}H-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}(d-1)E_{i})\ ,

where
{\bf e}_{1},\ldots,{\bf e}_{n-1}
are the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, while
{\bf e}_{n}=(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is the origin.

Proof.

Consider homogeneous coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.
Let
Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
. It has degree
n
and multiplicity
n-1
at the
p_{i}
, hence determines an element of
|n\rho^{*}H-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(n-1)E|
. When
Y_{\bullet}:\mathbb{P}^{n-1}=Y_{1}\supset\ldots\supset Y_{n}
is a general linear flag so that
Q(Y_{n})\neq 0
, then
\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\{Q=0\})=(0,\ldots,0)
. This settles the case
n=d
.
We show the case
n>d
by induction.
Before that, consider the incomplete linear system

\Lambda_{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{C}\langle x_{I}\ \mid\ I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},\ |I|=d\rangle

where
x_{I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\prod_{i\in I}x_{i}
.
It is easy to see that
\Lambda_{d}
identifies naturally with a subspace of
|d\rho^{*}H-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d-1)E_{i}|
, for example by applying the degree
n-d>0
partial derivatives to
Q
.

We claim that for a general linear flag term
Y_{d}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-d}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, the morphism induced by restriction

\psi_{d}:\Lambda_{d}\hookrightarrow H^{0}\bigl(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d)\bigr)\to H^{0}\bigl(Y_{d},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-d}}(d)\bigr)

is an isomorphism. This suffices as then
\psi_{d}
is surjective, and so there exists
Q_{d}\in\Lambda_{d}
that restricts to
Y_{d}
as
f^{d}
, where
f=0
is the linear equation of
Y_{d+1}
in
Y_{d}
. Then
\nu_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(Q_{d})=d\cdot{\bf e}_{d}
as needed.

For the claim, it suffices to show that
\psi_{d}
is injective when
Y_{d}
is general, as both sides of
\psi_{d}
have dimension
{n\choose d}
. Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-d+1}
be homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{n-d}
. Let
Y_{d}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
be parameterized by

\mathbb{P}^{n-d}\ni[z_{1}:\ldots:z_{n-d+1}]=z\mapsto[f_{1}(z):\ldots:f_{n}(z)]\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}

where the
f_{i}
are general linear forms on
\mathbb{P}^{n-d}
. For
I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
, denote by
f_{I}=\prod_{i\in I}f_{i}
. We want to show that the equation of homogenous forms in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-d+1}]
with complex coefficients

\sum_{|I|=d}c_{I}f_{I}=0\ ,

has only one solution, the trivial one, when
c_{I}=0
for all
I
.
Equivalently,
(f_{I}(z))_{|I|=d}
are linearly independent.
This we prove by induction on
n\geqslant d+1
.

Assume first
n=d+1
. Then
Y_{d}=Y_{n-1}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Due to our generality condition, we may assume after scaling that
f_{i}=z_{1}-r_{i}z_{2}
for distinct
r_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
. The linear independence of the
f_{I}
is equivalent to the linear independence of
\prod_{j\neq i}(z-r_{j})
in
\mathbb{C}[z]
. This follows easily by evaluating at the
r_{i}
, using that they are distinct.

Assume now
n>d+1
and that the claim is valid for
n-1
. By generality, after scaling we may assume
f_{n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-d+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d}\alpha_{i}z_{i}-z_{n-d+1}
. Consider the morphism

\varphi:\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-d+1}]\to\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-d}]

determined by
\varphi(z_{i})=z_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-d\}
and
\varphi(z_{n-d+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d}\alpha_{i}z_{i}
. It maps
f_{n}
to
0
. Set
g_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\varphi(f_{i})
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. These are general linear forms in
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-d}
. Also denote
g_{I}=\prod_{i\in I}g_{i}
. By the induction hypothesis, the
(g_{I})_{|I|=d,\ I\subset\{1,\ldots,n-1\}}
are linearly independent.
Assume
\sum_{|I|=d}c_{I}f_{I}=0
. Then
\sum_{|I|=d,\ n\not\in I}c_{I}g_{I}=0
and so
c_{I}=0
for all
I
that do not contain
n
. Now repeat the argument for all other indices in
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
is convex of Euclidean volume
1
, same as the simplex in the conclusion, it is sufficient to prove that all the vertices of the simplex belong to
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
.
The origin is the valuation of any effective divisor that does not pass through
x
. On the other extreme,
(n,0,\ldots,0)
is the valuation of
H_{1}+\ldots+H_{n}
with respect to a very general linear flag in
E
.
Lemma
5.1
gives all other vertices by Proposition
3.3
.
∎

6.
Questions and open problems

Problem 6.1
.

Let
X=C_{1}\times\ldots\times C_{n}
and
L=L_{1}\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes L_{n}
a boxed-product with
\deg L_{i}=d_{i}
. Let
x\in X
be any point, then compute the convex set
\Delta_{x}(L)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
, generalizing the case
n=2,3
from Theorem
1.2
.

We expect
\Delta_{x}(L)
to be a polytope, but as the case
n=3
shows, guessing does not seem a trivial game. The techniques from
[
8
]
of computing birational Fujita–Zariski decompositions may apply in higher dimension as well. The toric variety we are likely to have to work on is the blow-up of all the torus invariant linear strata in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, resolving the Cremona transformation. This is an interesting space in itself. It can be interpreted as the toric variety associated to the root system
A_{n-1}
and to the permutohedron, or the Losev–Manin moduli space
\overline{L}_{n}
. See
[
23
,
1
,
11
]
. The combinatorics appear involved.

Problem 6.2
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve and
X=C^{n}
. Study the shape of the generic iNObody at any point
x\in X
for natural non-boxed product type divisors.

When each
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, every divisor class on
X
is of boxed product type. However, when
g(C)\geqslant 1
, the diagonal
\Delta\subseteq C\times C
is already not of boxed product type. Here even understanding which classes
d(h_{1}+h_{2})-t\Delta
are effective is an open question in general. See
[
22
, Section 1.5.B]
for the history of the problem,
[
3
,
27
]
for its relation to the Nagata conjecture, and
[
9
]
for more recent results. More generally, one can construct various natural morphisms
C^{n}\to{\rm Jac}(C)
, and then the pullback of the theta polarization is an interesting class that is usually not of boxed-product type.

Problem 6.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete
n
-dimensional fan such that it’s associated toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is smooth. For any point
x\in X
compute the generic global iNObody
\Delta_{x}(X_{\Sigma})\subseteq N^{1}(X_{\Sigma})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The problem is already very difficult for general
x\in X
on toric surfaces where Seshadri constants are not computed (see
[
5
,
12
,
4
,
20
]
).
It is also interesting for
T
-invariant points, because of the genericity condition on the flag which means that the flag terms are not
T
-invariant.
Examples show that these convex sets constructed from general linear flags have simpler shapes than for
T
-invariant flags on the exceptional divisor of the blow-up of the point. For example, on the toric surface
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
consider the polarization
L=\mathcal{O}(1,1)
and a point
x\in X
. Then
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
is the triangle with vertices
(0,0)
,
(1,1)
, and
(2,0)
. But there are only two choices of
T
-invariant flags in the exceptional divisor on the blow-up of
x
. They correspond to the two rulings on a smooth quadric surface. Either choice leads to the parallelogram with vertices
(0,0)
,
(1,0)
,
(1,1)
, and
(2,1)
, a different body from the triangle above. Considering the toric ideas in our proofs, the previous question seems related to the following:

Problem 6.4
.

Let
f:X_{\Sigma}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a composition of toric blow-ups. If
Y_{\bullet}
is a (very) general complete linear flag on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, then its strict transform flag
Z_{\bullet}
in
X_{\Sigma}
is well-defined. Compute the global NObody
\Delta_{Z_{\bullet}}(X_{\Sigma})
.

In
[
7
]
we explain how these NObodies with respect to a very general choice of a linear flag on
X_{\Sigma}
satisfy properties inspired by the theory of generic initial ideals. They usually do not fully describe the body when our ambient space is a toric variety as above. The goal is to find algorithms that construct the convex body
\Delta_{Z_{\bullet}}(X_{\Sigma})
from the convex data of the complete fan
\Sigma
. In the case of the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
in
n
points in general linear position we were able to construct the body directly using its rich geometry.

Existence of solutions to Dirichlet boundary value problems of the stationary relativistic Boltzmann equation
Yi Wang, Li Li, Zaihong Jiang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the Dirichlet boundary value problem of steady-state relativistic Boltzmann equation in half-line with hard potential model, given the data for the outgoing particles at the boundary and a relativistic global Maxwellian with nonzero macroscopic velocities at the far field. We first explicitly address the sound speed for the relativistic Maxwellian in the far field, according to the eigenvalues of an operator based on macro-micro decomposition. Then we demonstrate that the solvability of the problem varies with the Mach number ℳ ∞ \mathcal{M}^{\infty} . If ℳ ∞ < − 1 \mathcal{M}^{\infty}<-1 , a unique solution exists connecting the Dirichlet data and the far field Maxwellian when the boundary data is sufficiently close to the Maxwellian. If ℳ ∞ > − 1 \mathcal{M}^{\infty}>-1 , such a solution exists only if the outgoing boundary data is small and satisfies certain solvability conditions. The proof is based on the macro-micro decomposition of solutions combined with an artificial damping term. A singular in velocity (at p 1 = 0 p_{1}=0 and | p | ≫ 1 |p|\gg 1 ) and spatially exponential decay weight is chosen to carry out the energy estimates. The result extends the previous work [Ukai, Yang, Yu, Comm. Math. Phys. 236, 373-393, 2003] to the relativistic problem. Keywords: Relativistic Boltzmann equation, boundary value problem, relativistic Maxwellian, Lorentz transformation, sound speed

Introduction

The relativistic Boltzmann equation reveals the statistical behavior of high-speed particles, which is crucial for exploring relativistic fluid dynamics and studying the dynamical behavior of particles in extreme physical environments. The relativistic Boltzmann equation is

\partial_{t}F+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}F=Q(F,F),

(1.1)

where

\hat{p}:=\mathfrak{c}\frac{p}{p_{0}}\equiv\frac{p}{\sqrt{1+\frac{|p|^{2}}{\mathfrak{c}^{2}}}}.

(1.2)

The translation rate

In this paper, we explicitly address for the relativistic Maxwellian in the far field the sound speed in (

The primary challenge of this paper arises from two key factors. First, the inherent complexity of the collision kernel in the relativistic Boltzmann equation, as well as the moments of the relativistic Maxwellian, presents substantial difficulties compared to the non-relativistic case. Second, obtaining

To better monitor the dimensionless quantities in the calculation, we consistently include the speed of light

in all computations.

3
Existence for linear problem with damping

In this section, we will prove the existence of solution for the linearized relativistic Boltzmann equation with damping by energy method.

We consider the following boundary value problem with the far field state
J_{\infty}
. We shall first look for the solution of (
1.4
) in the form

F(x,p)=J_{\infty}(p)+W_{0}(p)f(x,p),

where
W_{0}(p)
is the weight in (
1.10
) with
\beta=0
. Then problem (
1.4
) reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{p}_{1}\partial_{x}f=L(f)+\Gamma(f,f),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
f|_{x=0}=a_{0}(p),&p_{1}>0,(p_{2},p_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}f=0,&p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where

a_{0}=W_{0}^{-1}(F_{0}-J_{\infty}).

Add a damping term
-\gamma P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}f
, with
\gamma>0
and
P_{0}^{+}
defined in (
2.35
), to (
3.1
) and (
1.4
), then rewrite the equations as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{p}_{1}\partial_{x}f=L(f)+\Gamma(f,f)-\gamma P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}f,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
f|_{x=0}=a_{0}(p),&p_{1}>0,(p_{2},p_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}f=0,&p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{p}_{1}\partial_{x}F=Q(F,F)-\gamma W_{0}P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}W_{0}^{-1}(F-J_{\infty}),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\quad p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
F|_{x=0}=F_{0}(p),&p_{1}>0,\quad(p_{2},p_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}F=J_{\infty}(p),&p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

Let
f=e^{-\tau x}h
for
\tau>0
, then (
3.2
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{p}_{1}\partial_{x}h-\tau\hat{p}_{1}h-L(h)=\zeta-\gamma P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}h,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
h|_{x=0}=a_{0}(p),&p_{1}>0,(p_{2},p_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}h=0,&p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

where

\zeta:=e^{-\tau x}\Gamma(h,h).

(3.5)

The problem we consider in this section is to view the nonlinear term
e^{-\tau x}\Gamma(h,h)
by a given function
\zeta(x,p)
where
\zeta
satisfies

P_{0}\zeta=0,\qquad\|\zeta\|<\infty.

The idea of the proof is to introduce a linear functional on a subspace of
L^{2}_{x,p}
, then demonstrate that the aforementioned linear functional is bounded through the energy estimates, and finally obtain the existence of the solution through the Riesz representation theorem.

We define the test function space by

\mathcal{V}:=\{\varphi(x,p)\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\mid\varphi^{0}:=\varphi|_{x=0}=0\text{ for }p_{1}<0\},

and let

\mathcal{U}:=\{\xi\mid\xi=-\hat{p}_{1}\partial_{x}\varphi-\tau\hat{p}_{1}\varphi+\gamma\hat{p}_{1}P_{0}^{+}\varphi-L(\varphi),\varphi\in\mathcal{V}\}.

A linear functional on
\mathcal{U}
is defined as

\mathcal{L}(\xi):=(\zeta,\varphi)+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+},\qquad\xi\in\mathcal{U}.

By testing (
3.4
) with
\varphi
, it is easy to see that if
h
is a solution of (
3.4
), then for any
\xi\in\mathcal{U}
, it holds that

(h,\xi)=\mathcal{L}(\xi).

Based on the above definitions, we can obtain the following lemma about the linear operator
\mathcal{L}
.

Lemma 4
.

If
\gamma>\tau>0
are sufficiently small, then the linear operator
\mathcal{L}
is bounded on
\mathcal{U}
, and satisfies

|\mathcal{L}(\xi)|\leq c(\|\zeta\|+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}})\|\xi\|.

In addition, there exists an extension
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
of
\mathcal{L}
defined in
L_{x,p}^{2}
, such that

|\bar{\mathcal{L}}|\leq c\|\xi\|,

for all
\xi\in L_{x,p}^{2}
, and with the bound unchanged.

Proof.

For any
\xi\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists a
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}
such that
\xi=-\hat{p}_{1}\partial_{x}\varphi-\tau\hat{p}_{1}\varphi+\gamma\hat{p}_{1}P_{0}^{+}\varphi-L(\varphi)
.
We take the
L_{x,p}^{2}
inner product of any
\xi\in\mathcal{U}
and the corresponding
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}
,

(\xi,\varphi)=(-\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{x},\varphi)-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}\varphi,\varphi)+\gamma(\hat{p}_{1}P_{0}^{+}\varphi,\varphi)-(L\varphi,\varphi).

The first term is

\displaystyle(-\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{x},\varphi)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{x}\varphi dxdp

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{2}|_{x=0}^{x=\infty}dp

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p_{1}>0}\hat{p}_{1}(\varphi^{0})^{2}dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p_{1}<0}\hat{p}_{1}(\varphi^{0})^{2}dp

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}.

For convenience, we let
\varphi_{i}:=P_{i}\varphi
,
i=0,1
. For the second and third terms, we have for some generic constant
c
that

\displaystyle-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}\varphi,\varphi)+\gamma(P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi,\varphi)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau\big((P_{0}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{0},\varphi_{0})+2(P_{0}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0})+(\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1})\big)+\gamma\big((P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{0},\varphi_{0})+(P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau(P_{0}^{-}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{0},\varphi_{0})+(\gamma-\tau)(P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{0},\varphi_{0})-2\tau(P_{0}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0})-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1})+\gamma(P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2c\tau\|\varphi_{0}\|^{2}-c^{-1}\tau\|\varphi_{1}\|\ \|\varphi_{0}\|-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c\tau\|\varphi_{0}\|^{2}-c^{-3}\tau\|\varphi_{1}\|^{2}-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1}),

where we assume that
\gamma=O(1)\tau
for some positive constant
O(1)>2
.

Since
(L\varphi,\varphi)=(L\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1})
, by (
3.6
), (i) and (iii) of Proposition
1
, we know that for sufficiently small
\tau>0
,

\displaystyle(\xi,\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}+c\tau\|\varphi_{0}\|^{2}-c\tau\|\varphi_{1}\|^{2}-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1})+c_{0}(\varphi_{1},\nu(p)\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}+c\tau\|\varphi_{0}\|^{2}-c\tau\|\varphi_{1}\|^{2}-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{1})+c_{0}(\varphi_{1},p_{0}^{\frac{a}{2}}\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}+c\tau\|\varphi_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{c_{0}}{2}(\varphi_{1},p_{0}^{\frac{a}{2}}\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle\geq c(\langle\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}+\|\varphi\|^{2}).

Then

\langle\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\varphi\|\leq c\|\xi\|.

Thus we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|(\zeta,\varphi)|+|\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}|

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\|\zeta\|\cdot\|\varphi\|+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\langle\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\zeta\|+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}})(\|\varphi\|+\langle\hat{p}_{1}\varphi^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\zeta\|+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}})\|\xi\|.

Thus it can be concluded that
\mathcal{L}
is bounded on
\mathcal{U}
, with the bound depending on the boundary data
a_{0}
and the given function
\zeta
.
In accordance with the Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists an extension
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
of
\mathcal{L}
to the space
L^{2}_{x,p}
such that for any
\xi\in L^{2}_{x,p}
, we have that

|\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\xi)|\leq c\|\xi\|,

with the same bound in (
3.7
).
∎

We are ready to obtain the following existence theorem for the linearized equation (
3.4
) with damping and a given function
\zeta(x,p)
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
\gamma,\tau
are suitably small such that Lemma
4
holds. If the boundary condition
a_{0}
and the source term
\zeta
satisfy

\|\zeta\|+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,

of (
3.4
) satisfying

\langle|\hat{p}_{1}|h^{0},h^{0}\rangle_{-}+(\nu h,h)+\|\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\hat{p}_{1}|h_{x}\|^{2}\leq c(\|\zeta\|^{2}+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}).

(3.8)

where

.

Proof.

For the above functional
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
on
L^{2}
, we can apply the Riesz representation theorem and it implies that there exists a unique
h\in L^{2}_{x,p}
such that

\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\xi)=(h,\xi).

(3.9)

This indicates that
h
is a weak solution to the linear equation (
3.4
).

Now our objective is to demonstrate that both
h_{x}
for almost all
(x,p)
and the trace of
h
at
x=0
are well-defined. In order to achieve this, a specific family of test functions
\phi
was chosen for analysis as follows:

\phi=\int_{x}^{\infty}\eta(x^{\prime},p)dx^{\prime},

where
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(x^{\prime},p)dx^{\prime}=0
for any

\Big(\int_{0}^{x}(-\tau\hat{p}_{1}h+\gamma P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}h-Lh-\zeta)dx^{\prime}+\hat{p}_{1}h,\eta\Big)=0.

By the choice of our test function

, we have that

\int_{0}^{x}(-\tau\hat{p}_{1}h+\gamma P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}h-Lh-\zeta)dx^{\prime}+\hat{p}_{1}h=b(p)

(x,p)
, where

is a function of

is well-defined for almost all
(x,p)
and the trace of
h
at

is well-defined. Hence we have (
3.4
) for almost all
(x,p)
.

By (
3.7
) and (
3.9
), we have

\|h\|\leq c(\|\zeta\|+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}),

(3.10)

which implies the uniqueness of
h
. So far we know that
h\in L^{2}_{x,p}
. Since
|\hat{p}_{1}|
is bounded,
|\hat{p}_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}h\in L^{2}_{x,p}
.

Now we claim that the solution obtained indeed satisfies the given boundary condition. Actually, if we take the inner product of (
3.4
) with
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}
, we have

(\hat{p}_{1}h_{x}-\tau\hat{p}_{1}h-Lh+\gamma P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}h,\varphi)=(\zeta,\varphi).

After integration by parts, we have that

(h,-\hat{p}_{1}\varphi_{x}-\tau\hat{p}_{1}\varphi-L\varphi+\gamma\hat{p}_{1}P_{0}^{+}\varphi)=(\zeta,\varphi)+\langle\hat{p}_{1}h^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}.

In accordance with the definition of
\xi
and the premise that
h
satisfies (
3.9
), it holds that

(\zeta,\varphi)+\langle\hat{p}_{1}h^{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+}=(\xi,h)=\mathcal{L}(\xi)=(\zeta,\varphi)+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},\varphi^{0}\rangle_{+},

which implies

h^{0}=a_{0},\text{ for a.e. }p_{1}>0.

The boundary condition holds.

Finally, we prove (
3.8
).
Applying
h
to (
3.4
) and then integrating over
(x,p)
yields that

(\hat{p}_{1}h_{x},h)-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}h,h)+\gamma(P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}h,h)-(Lh,h)=(\zeta,h).

(3.11)

The first item is

\displaystyle(\hat{p}_{1}h_{x},h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{p}_{1}h^{2}|_{x=0}^{x=\infty}dp

(3.12)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{p}_{1}h^{0},h^{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\langle|\hat{p}_{1}|h^{0},h^{0}\rangle_{-}.

Substitute (
3.12
) into (

), by

, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle|\hat{p}_{1}|h^{0},h^{0}\rangle_{-}-\tau(\hat{p}_{1}h,h)+\gamma(P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}h,h)+(\nu h,h)

\displaystyle=(Kh,h)+(\zeta,h)+\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq c\|h\|^{2}+\|\zeta\|\|h\|+\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\zeta\|^{2}+\|h\|^{2}+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}).

Notice that

|(\hat{p}_{1}h,h)|\leq\|h\|^{2},

|(P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}h,h)|=|(\hat{p}_{1}h,P_{0}^{+}h)|\leq\|\hat{p}_{1}h\|\cdot\|h\|\leq\|h\|^{2}.

We know from the above estimate, (

) and (

\langle|\hat{p}_{1}|h^{0},h^{0}\rangle_{-}+(\nu h,h)\leq c(\|\zeta\|^{2}+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}).

(3.14)

By (
3.4
), (
3.14
), we have that

\displaystyle\|\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\hat{p}_{1}|h_{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tau\hat{p}_{1}h\|^{2}+\|\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}Lh\|^{2}+\|\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta\|^{2}+\|\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\gamma P_{0}^{+}\hat{p}_{1}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c(\|h\|^{2}+\|\zeta\|^{2}+\|\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu h\|^{2}+\|Kh\|^{2}+\|\hat{p}_{1}h\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|h\|^{2}+\|\zeta\|^{2}+\|\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}h\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq c(\|\zeta\|^{2}+\langle\hat{p}_{1}a_{0},a_{0}\rangle_{+}).

Therefore the unique solution
h
satisfies (

).
∎

4
Weighted estimates for the linearized problem with damping

In order to obtain the existence of solutions to the nonlinear problem, we need to estimate
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,p,\beta}}
norm of the solution for the linear problem. This section is devoted to achieve the estimate.

Lemma 5
.

Assume
f(x)\in L^{2}([0,\infty))
and
f\rightarrow 0
as

, the derivative
f_{x}
is defined for almost all
x
and
f_{x}\in L^{2}([0,\infty))
. Then

Definition A.1

If

Lemma A.2

Suppose that
P
and

. It is obvious that
M
is a Lorentz transformation. Here,
M^{i}(i=0,1,2,3)
are used to denote the rows, and
M_{j}(j=0,1,2,3)
are used to denote the columns of the matrix
M
. By linearity, we know that
M(1,0,0,0)^{T}=(1,0,0,0)^{T}
. Therefore,
M_{0}=(1,0,0,0)^{T}
. From the orthogonal properties of the Lorentz transformation
M_{i}\cdot M_{j}=D_{ij}
,
i,j=0,1,2,3
, we know that
M^{0}=(1,0,0,0)
, and

M_{00}:=\begin{pmatrix}M^{1}_{1}&M^{1}_{2}&M^{1}_{3}\\
M^{2}_{1}&M^{2}_{2}&M^{2}_{3}\\
M^{3}_{1}&M^{3}_{2}&M^{3}_{3}\\
\end{pmatrix}

is an orthogonal matrix. Therefore,
M(0,\omega)^{T}=(0,\tilde{\omega})^{T}
for some
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
∎

Lemma A.6
.

Suppose that
P
and
Q
are four-vectors satisfying that
P\cdot P=Q\cdot Q=\mathfrak{c}^{2}
. Let
\Lambda
be any Lorentz transformation,
\tilde{P}:=\Lambda P
and
\tilde{Q}:=\Lambda Q
. Then

(i)

|\tilde{p}-\tilde{q}|\sim|p-q|
,

(ii)

\tilde{p}_{0}\sim p_{0}
,
\tilde{q}_{0}\sim q_{0}
,

Here the notation ”
A\sim B
” means that there exist a constant
c>0
, depending only on
\Lambda
, such that
A/c\leq B\leq cA
.

Proof.

(i) Denote
\Lambda
as

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{00}&b^{T}\\
a&\tilde{\Lambda}\end{pmatrix}.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\lambda_{00}>0
. Since
\Lambda^{-1}
is also a Lorentz transformation,
the four-vectors
\tilde{P}
and
\tilde{Q}
are equivalent to
P
and
Q
. Obviously,

\tilde{p}=p_{0}a+\tilde{\Lambda}p
,
\tilde{q}=q_{0}a+\tilde{\Lambda}q
, and

|\tilde{p}-\tilde{q}|\leq|p_{0}-q_{0}|a+|\tilde{\Lambda}(p-q)|.

(A.2)

Since

|p_{0}-q_{0}|=|\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}^{2}+|p|^{2}}-\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}^{2}+|q|^{2}}|=\frac{|p\cdot p-q\cdot q|}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}^{2}+|p|^{2}}+\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}^{2}+|q|^{2}}}\leq|p-q|,

we have by (
A.2
) that

|\tilde{p}-\tilde{q}|\leq c|p-q|,

for some constant
c
depending only on
\Lambda
. Note that
P,Q
and
\tilde{P},\tilde{Q}
are equivalent, hence
|\tilde{p}-\tilde{q}|\sim|p-q|
.

(ii) Note that
\tilde{p}_{0}=\lambda_{00}p_{0}+b\cdot p
,
\tilde{q}_{0}=\lambda_{00}q_{0}+b\cdot q
and
\Lambda^{i}\cdot\Lambda^{j}=D_{ij},(i,j=0,1,2,3)
, where
\Lambda^{i}
denotes the rows of
\Lambda
and
D_{ij}
is given in (
2.1
). Therefore, we have that
\lambda_{00}^{2}=b\cdot b+1
and

\tilde{p}_{0}\leq 2\lambda_{00}p_{0},\quad\tilde{q}_{0}\leq 2\lambda_{00}q_{0}.

On the other hand, we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{0}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{00}p_{0}+b\cdot p

\displaystyle=\lambda_{00}p_{0}-|b|p_{0}+|b|p_{0}+b\cdot p

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda_{00}-|b|)p_{0}+|b|p_{0}-|b||p|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{00}^{2}-|b|^{2}}{\lambda_{00}+|b|}p_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2\lambda_{00}}p_{0},

Thus

\frac{1}{2\lambda_{00}}p_{0}\leq\tilde{p}_{0}\leq 2\lambda_{00}p_{0},

(A.3)

which implies that
\tilde{p}_{0}\sim p_{0}
.
Similarly, we have
\tilde{q}_{0}\sim q_{0}
.
∎

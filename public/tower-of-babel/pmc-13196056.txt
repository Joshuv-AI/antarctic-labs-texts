Pathogen identification and analysis of broiler bronchial obstruction syndrome using high-throughput sequencing technology
Authors: Yingli Mu, Xuejing Wang, Jianing Xu, Yuqing Yang, Jue Wang, Juxiang Liu, Ligong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo identify the genetic characteristics of dominant pathogens in broilers suffered from bronchial obstruction syndrome in Hebei Province, high-throughput sequencing technology was employed for pathogen detection after pathological examinations. Pathological examination revealed typical lesions consistent with bronchial obstruction syndrome. Molecular biological analyses revealed that the predominant pathogens and opportunistic pathogens in the broiler samples included H9N2 avian influenza virus (AIV), infectious bronchitis virus (IBV), Escherichia-Shigella, Pseudomonas and Enterococcus. All four H9N2 AIV isolates exhibited higher nucleotide homology in PB1, PA, NP and NS gene with A/Chicken/Shanghai/F/1998 and M gene with A/Quail/Hong Kong/G1/1997. These belonged to the Eurasian lineage, forming a quadruplex recombinant, but differed from the G57 genotype. Several H3N3, H3N8 and H10N3 AIV strains might provide internal genes contributing to the recombination of the H9N2 isolates, conferring pathogenicity enhance, antiviral drug resistance and adaptability to mammals. Complete genome analysis of three IBV isolates showed the highest nucleotide homology with YX10 strain (GI-19 lineage, QX type). Specifically, the S1, S2, 3a, 3b, E, M, 5a and N gene of the three IBV isolates exhibited the highest nucleotide identity to the YX10 strain, whereas 1a and 1b gene showed the highest nucleotide identity with I0114/14 strain (GI-29 lineage), and 5b gene displayed the highest nucleotide identity with LDT3-A strain (GI-28 lineage). Through MEGA software translation, the cleavage sites of the S protein of the three IBV isolates were determined as HRRRR, which is consistent with most QX type IBV (GI-19 lineage) cleavage sites. Phylogenetic analysis based on the S1 gene further demonstrated that all three IBV isolates were belonged to GI-19 lineage. CK/CH/HBBD/BD6 and CK/CH/HBBD/BD32 represented newly emerged recombinant variants. This study clarified the predominant pathogens causing broiler bronchial obstruction syndrome in some regions of Hebei Province and detailed the genetic profiles of H9N2 AIV and IBV. These findings could inform strategies for the prevention and management of broiler multicausal respiratory disease.Keywords: High-throughput sequencing, Broiler, Bronchial obstruction syndrome, H9N2 AIV, IBV

Introduction

Chicken multicausal respiratory disease (MRD) results from the interaction of multiple pathogens causing respiratory tract infections. Between 2011 and 2022, outbreaks characterized by respiratory distress and the presence of dry, cheese-like substances blocking the bronchi were reported in six provinces in China (Mu et al., 2026). The diseased chickens exhibited high mortality rates, posing a significant threat to poultry farming. Similar diseases involving tracheal caseous obstruction had also been documented in several other countries. Investigations indicated mixed infections involving several pathogens and or opportunistic pathogens (Umar et al., 2019; Bóna et al., 2023; Jbenyeni et al., 2023; Al-Natour et al., 2024), including avian influenza virus (AIV), infectious bronchitis virus (IBV), Newcastle disease virus (NDV), infectious laryngotracheitis virus (ILTV), Mycoplasma gallisepticum (MG), Pseudomonas aeruginosa (P. aeruginosa), Escherichia coli (E. coli) and Klebsiella pneumoniae (K. pneumoniae).

Avian influenza (AI) is a highly contagious viral disease caused by influenza A virus (IAV), belonged to the Orthomyxoviridae family (Bahari et al., 2015). IAV was classified into 18 hemagglutinin (HA) and 11 neuraminidase (NA) subtypes based on antigenic characteristics. Among these, H1-H16 were isolated from birds, while H17 and H18 were found in bats (Webster et al., 1992; Tong et al., 2013). The genome of H9N2 AIV consists of eight segments of single-stranded negative-sense RNA, totaling approximately 13.6 kb, encoding 10 proteins: HA, NA, nucleoprotein (NP), polymerase complex (PB2, PB1, PA), matrix proteins (M1, M2) and non-structural proteins (NS1, NS2) (Gu et al., 2017). Based on HA and NA gene, H9N2 AIV comprise two primary lineages: the North American lineage (A/turkey/Wisconsin/1/1966) and various sublineages such as BJ/94-like (A/chicken/Beijing/1/1994), Y280-like (A/duck/Hong Kong/Y280/1997), G1-like (A/quail/Hong Kong/G1/1997), F/98-like (A/chicken/Shanghai/F/1998) and Y439-like (A/duck/Hong Kong/Y439/1997) (Fusaro et al., 2011; Yan et al., 2017). Since 2013, most isolated H9N2 strains belonged to the G57 genotype derived from the BJ/94-like. The representative G57 strain (A/chicken/Zhejiang/HJ/2007) has the genetic backbone of the F/98-like lineage combined with the PB2 and M gene from the G1-like (Gu et al., 2014; Pu et al., 2015). Several H9N2 AIV strains provides genetic material through mutation and recombination, generating recombinant viruses such as H3N8, H7N9, H5N6 and H10N8, which may infect humans and pose major public health risks (Carnaccini and Perez, 2020; Zhang et al., 2021; Bhat et al., 2022; Peng et al., 2022). Although H9N2 was considered as a low pathogenic AIV (LPAIV) causing mild symptoms like tracheitis, sinusitis, airsacculitis and conjunctivitis (Guo et al., 2021), coinfection with other pathogens such as IBV, E. coli and MG may lead to high mortality and substantial economic losses (Arafat et al., 2018; Kong et al., 2021; Le et al., 2023).

Avian infectious bronchitis is an acute, highly contagious viral disease caused by IBV (Chen et al., 2024), which demonstrates wide tissue tropism. Based on virus pathogenicity, IBV strains were categorized into respiratory, renal, reproductive and gastric gland types (Wibowo et al., 2019). After invasion via respiratory tract, colonized respiratory-type IBV may damage the respiratory epithelium and often predispose chickens to secondary infections with pathogenic or opportunistic microbes, seriously threatening poultry production (Chhabra et al., 2015; Zhao et al., 2023). IBV is a single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus belonged to the genus Coronavirus (Rohaim et al., 2020), with a genomic structure consisting of a 5′ cap and a 3′ poly (A) tail (Thor et al., 2011), approximately 27.6 kb in length and comprising at least 10 open reading frames (ORFs) arranged as 5′-1a-1b-S-3a-3b-E-M-5a-5b-N-3′. The main structural proteins critical for virus replication and pathogenicity include the spike (S), membrane (M), nucleocapsid (N) and envelope (E) proteins (Xu et al., 2018). Amino acid mutation in the antigenic determinants of the S1 protein can lead to the emergence of novel variant strains, significantly impacting the serotype and genotype classification of IBV (Jackwood et al., 2012). Currently, IBV has been classified into 10 genotypes and 40 lineages, including GI (1-30), GII(1-2), GIII-1, GIV-1, GV-1, GVI-1, GVII-1, GVIII -1, GIX-1 and GX-1, among others (Valastro et al., 2016; Chen et al., 2017; Domanska-Blicharz et al., 2020; Molenaar et al., 2020; Kim et al., 2026). Previous studies showed that the predominant IBV strains in China belonged to QX type (GI-19 lineage) (Xu et al., 2018). Extensive immunization with vaccine such as H120 and 4/91 strain has led to varying degrees of genetic mutation and recombination in IBV, resulting in vaccination failure (Sjaak De Wit et al., 2011; Jackwood et al., 2012).

High-throughput sequencing was utilized to detect pathogens causing broiler bronchial obstruction syndrome in Hebei Province, China, from 2024 to 2025. Results identified predominant pathogens and opportunistic pathogens as H9N2 AIV, IBV, Escherichia-Shigella, Pseudomonas and Enterococcus. Genetic evolutionary analyses indicated all four H9N2 AIV isolates belonged to the Eurasian lineage and represented quadruplex recombinant viruses distinct from prevalent G57 genotype in China. Several H3N3, H3N8 and H10N3 AIV strains might have contributed internal genes for the recombinant H9N2 strains. Phylogenetic analysis based on complete genomes showed the three IBV isolates belonged to GI-19 lineage, but recombination events may have occurred involving strains from different genotypes. This study identified pathogens associated with broiler bronchial obstruction syndrome in some regions of Hebei Province and detailed the genetic characteristics of H9N2 AIV and IBV, providing important information for preventing and controlling broiler respiratory diseases.

Material and methods

Ethical statement

This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Hebei Agricultural University (Approval No. 2024046).

Clinical sample collection and pathological observation

From November 2024 to April 2025, five tissue samples were collected from diseased broilers exhibiting depression, ruffled feathers, open-mouth breathing and moist rales, the gross lesions were observed at necropsy. Samples were obtained from four regions of Hebei Province (Table 1). Under sterile conditions, tissues were collected from the lower trachea and lungs, including the trachea and divided into three portions. The first portion was fixed with 10% neutral buffered formalin. The second portion was homogenized and stored at -80 °C for bacterial 16S rDNA amplicon sequencing. The third portion was homogenized, subjected to three freeze-thaw cycles, centrifuged at 4 °C and 8,000 r/min for 15 min and the supernatant was stored at -80 °C for macrovial metagenomic sequencing. All samples were collected from vaccinated broilers (Table 2).

Tracheal and lung tissues fixed with 10% neutral buffered formalin were processed into paraffin-embedded sections through graded ethanol dehydration, xylene clearing, paraffin infiltration, and embedding, followed by sectioning at 5 µm. Sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) (L25050103/L25050503; Nanchang Yulu Experimental Equipment Co., Ltd., Jiangxi, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Histopathological changes were examined using a light microscope (BX53F, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

16S rDNA amplification and sequencing

Bacterial 16S rDNA amplicon sequencing was performed by Beijing Novogene Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China)(Wang et al., 2021). Total genomic DNA was extracted after sample pretreatment (Wang et al., 2025). For bacterial community diversity analysis, the V3-V4 region was amplified using universal primers 341F (5′-CCTAYGGGRBGCASCAG-3′) and 806R (5′-GGACTACNNGGGTATCTAAT-3′). PCR reactions contained 15 µL Phusion High-Fidelity PCR Master Mix, 0.2 µM of each primer and 10 ng genomic DNA. Amplification was conducted under the following conditions: 98 °C for 1 min; 30 cycles of 98 °C for 10 s, 50 °C for 30 s and 72 °C for 30 s; and a final extension at 72 °C for 5 min. PCR products were purified using magnetic beads, pooled in equimolar concentrations and target bands were recovered. Libraries were constructed and quantified using Qubit and qPCR and qualified libraries were sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform using paired-end sequencing (PE250). All sequencing data were deposited in the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) SRA database under accession number PRJNA1364353.

Raw sequencing reads were demultiplexed based on barcode and primer sequences. After read merging, Raw Tags were quality filtered using FLASH (version 1.2.11) to generate Effective Tags. Taxonomic annotation was performed using QIIME2 (version 2022.02) with the SILVA 138.1 database. For unclassified regions, annotation was conducted using the microNT database. Based on the top 10 taxa by abundance at the genus and species levels, statistical analyses of species abundance were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 9.5).

Macrovirus sequencing

The sample supernatant was submitted to Shanghai Tepu Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) for macrovirus genome sequencing. Genomic DNA and RNA were extracted, host contamination was minimized and sample concentration and purity were enhanced (Huang et al., 2024). Genomic DNA was fragmented into 300-500 bp segments using a Covaris M220 ultrasonicator, whereas RNA fragmentation occurred in Mg2+ containing buffer. DNA and cDNA libraries were constructed using the TruSeq™ DNA Sample Prep Kit (Shanghai Yihui Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China; Item No. TG-202-1001-1) and TruSeq PE Cluster Kit (Shanghai Binzi Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China; Item No. PE-300-2001). Sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 PE150 platform, generating approximately 20 Gb of raw data per sample. Raw sequencing data were aligned to the NCBI database using BBmap software (version 38.51) to remove corresponding rRNA, host and bacterial sequences, yielding high-quality data (clean data). Data were assembled de novo using SPAdes (version 3.14.1) and SOAPdenovo (version 2.04) software to obtain complete sequences. The assembled sequences were aligned against the virus-NT database via BLAST (version 2.10.0+) to identify the closest candidate reference sequences. Virus annotation of assembled sequences was conducted using kraken2 software (version 2.0.8-beta). Original macrovirus sequencing reads were submitted to the NCBI SRA database, under accession number PRJNA1369727.

Homology and phylogenetic analysis

Assembled complete viral genome sequences obtained were submitted to the NCBI database. Nucleotide sequences and inferred amino acid sequences of the viral genomes underwent homology analysis with reference strains retrieved from GenBank via the MegAlign program (DNAStar software, DNASTAR Inc., Madison, WI). Sequence alignment of complete genomes and specific genes between isolates and reference strains was performed using the Clustal W algorithm in MEGA software (version 7.0). A phylogenetic tree was constructed using the maximum likelihood (ML) method with 1000 bootstrap replicates. The resulting phylogenetic tree was edited and visualized through the online tool iTOL (https://itol.embl.de/).

Amino acid sequence analysis

Potential glycosylation sites (N-X-S/T motifs) of the HA and NA proteins from H9N2 AIV were predicted using the NetNGlyc 1.0 Server (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/) (X represents any amino acid except proline). To illustrate patterns in the aligned sequences within the highly variable regions (HVRs) of the IBV S1 protein, the WebLogo service (http://weblogo.threeplusone.com/create.cgi, accessed on December 30, 2025) was utilized to generate sequence logos. Sequence logos provided a more comprehensive and precise representation of sequence similarities compared to consensus sequences. Additionally, they could rapidly reveal significant alignment features that might otherwise be challenging to identify.

Recombination analysis

Recombination events among isolated virus were identified by aligning complete genome sequences of isolated virus and reference strains using the Clustal W algorithm in MEGA software (version 7.0). Initial screening for recombination was conducted using RDP, GENECONV, BootScan, MaxChi, Chimaera, SiScan and 3Seq methods within RDP4 software (version 4.1.0). Results were subsequently verified using Simplot software (version 3.5.1).

Results

Pathological observation examination results

Autopsy findings showed that the tracheal mucosa was rough and hemorrhagic, with elongated yellow-white caseous material obstructing the lower part of the trachea and bronchi (Fig. 1A, D). Histopathological examination revealed abundant inflammatory exudate within the bronchi (Fig. 1B, E). The tertiary bronchi were filled with inflammatory exudate, resulting in marked luminal narrowing or complete occlusion (Fig. 1C, F).

Results of 16S rDNA amplification and sequencing

Analysis of the relative abundances of the top 10 bacterial families in the five samples (Fig. 2A) and the heat map of the top 35 families (Fig. 2C) indicated that the dominant pathogenic families, in descending order, were Enterobacteriaceae, Staphylococcaceae, Mycoplasmataceae, Clostridiaceae, Vibrionaceae, Corynebacteriaceae and Streptococcaceae. The conditionally pathogenic families, ranked from most to least abundant, were Pasteurellaceae, Comamonadaceae, Enterococcaceae, Morganellaceae, Veillonellaceae, Bacteroidaceae, Moraxellaceae, Micrococcaceae and Pseudomonadaceae. Enterobacteriaceae, Enterococcaceae, Bacteroidaceae, Micrococcaceae and Pseudomonadaceae were found in all samples. At the genus level, the top 10 genera (Fig. 2B) and the heat map of the top 35 genera (Fig. 2D) showed that the predominant pathogenic genera were Escherichia-Shigella, Avibacterium, Riemerella, Staphylococcus, Mycoplasma, Hathewaya and Corynebacterium, whereas the conditionally pathogenic genera included Gallibacterium, Delftia, Enterococcus, Proteus, Veillonella, Cutibacterium, Acinetobacter, Pseudomonas and Paeniglutamicibacter. Escherichia-Shigella, Enterococcus and Pseudomonas were present in all samples.

Molecular characteristics of H9N2 AIV

Using MegAlign software, the eight gene segments of the four H9N2 AIV isolates were compared with 18 reference strains. The nucleotide sequence homology among the four isolates ranged from 92.2%-99.7% (PB2), 92.6%-99.1% (PB1), 95.4%-99.8% (PA), 95.9%-99.9% (HA), 93.7%-100.0% (NP), 94.3%-99.7% (NA), 95.4%-100.0% (M) and 95.5%-99.9% (NS) (Table 3). Comparative analysis results with the reference strains A/duck/Hong Kong/Y280/1997, A/quail/Hong Kong/G1/1997, A/duck/Hong Kong/Y439/1997 and A/chicken/Shanghai/F/1998 was summarized in Table 3. The results indicated that PB1, PA, NP and NS gene of the four isolates shared highest nucleotide homology with A/chicken/Shanghai/F/1998, and M gene shared highest nucleotide homology with A/quail/Hong Kong/G1/1997.

The phylogenetic tree, constructed based on eight gene segments of four H9N2 AIV isolates and 18 reference strains from GenBank, indicated that the HA gene of the four isolates belonged to the Y280-like, the M gene belonged to the G1-like and the PB2, PB1, PA, NP and NS gene belonged to the F/98-like. The NA gene did not cluster with any established lineage (Fig. 4). The four H9N2 AIV isolates were identified as quadruplex recombinants. The PB2, PB1, PA, NP and M gene clustered closely with several H3N3, H3N8 and H10N3 AIV strains, suggesting that these strains may provide genetic material for the recombination events involving H9N2 AIV.

The receptor-binding site sequences were GTSNT (left arm) and HGLMGR (right arm). The amino acids such as Y109, W161, L202, Y203, and G236 at HA protein receptor-binding sites remained conserved. Alterations in glycosylation sites of HA and NA proteins may directly influence viral biological properties. All four H9N2 AIV isolates had eight potential glycosylation sites on the HA protein: 29NST, 82NPS, 141NVS, 298NTT, 305NVS, 313NCS, 492NGT and 551NGS (Table 7). The NA proteins of four isolates except A/chicken/Hebei/HS7/2025 contained six glycosylation sites: 44NPS, 69NST, 86NWS, 146NGT, 234NGT and 402NWS (Table 8). These findings indicate that the identified H9N2 AIV isolates possess molecular characteristics associated with increased pathogenicity, antiviral drug resistance and mammalian adaptation.

Molecular characteristics of IBV

The complete genomic sequence of the three IBV isolates was approximately 27.6 kb in length. All three isolates conformed to the typical IBV genomic structure: 5′-1a-1b-S-3a-3b-E-M-5a-5b-N-3′. Homology analysis of the complete genome and individual genes was conducted among the three IBV isolates and 18 reference strains using MegAlign software. Nucleotide homology among the three IBV isolates ranged from 97.2%-98.5%, whereas nucleotide homology between the isolates and the 18 reference strains ranged from 84.7%-96.1% (Table 9). Among reference strains, the isolates showed the highest nucleotide homology (95.6%-96.1%) with the YX10 strain. Specifically, S1, S2, 3a, 3b, E, M, 5a and N gene exhibited the highest nucleotide similarity with YX10 strain (GI-19 lineage, QX type). The 1a and 1b gene showed highest similarity with the I0114/14 strain (GI-29 lineage) and the 5b gene showed highest similarity with LDT3-A strain (GI-28 lineage).

Phylogenetic analyses based on the complete genome and individual gene of IBV were conducted (Fig. 5). The complete genome phylogenetic tree indicated that all three IBV isolates belonged to the GI-19 lineage. Individual gene trees demonstrated that S1, S2, 3a, 3b, E, M and N gene clustered with YX10 strain (GI-19 lineage, QX type). The 1a and 1b gene clustered with I0114/14 strain (GI-29 lineage) and 5b gene grouped with LDT3-A strain (GI-28 lineage). Additionally, the 5a gene of CK/CH/HBXT/XT8 and CK/CH/HBBD/BD32 clustered with YX10 (GI-19 lineage), while the 5a gene of strain CK/CH/HBBD/BD6 grouped with 4/91 strain (GI-13 lineage). These results suggest that the three IBV isolates were recombinant viruses derived from different genotypes, consistent with nucleotide homology findings.

High variability regions such as HVR1 at 38-67 aa, HVR2 at 91-141 aa and HVR3 at 274-387 aa of the S1 protein (Fig. 7) were associated with IBV serotype specificity and virus-neutralizing epitopes. To assess potential recombination events among the three IBV isolates, 18 reference strains (Supplementary Table S2) from various lineages were selected as putative parents. Whole-genome recombination analysis using RDP4 and Simplot software identified novel recombinants in CK/CH/HBBD/BD6 and CK/CH/HBBD/BD32, supported by at least five analytical methods. RDP4 analysis showed recombination breakpoints of CK/CH/HBBD/BD6 at 8784-9633 nt, with YX10 (GI-19) as the primary parent and 4/91 (GI-13) as the secondary parent. Another breakpoint occurred at 26740-26830 nt, with I0114/14 (GI-29) as the primary parent and YN (GI-22) as the secondary parent (Table 10). Thus, CK/CH/HBBD/BD6 underwent two recombination events involving strains YX10, I0114/14, 4/91 and YN, specifically in the 1a and N gene. Similarly, CK/CH/HBBD/BD32 had recombination breakpoints at 8790-9633 nt, with YX10 (GI-19) as the primary and 4/91 (GI-13) as the secondary parent; another breakpoint was located at 17423-18115 nt, with I0114/14 (GI-29) as the primary parent and LX4 (GI-19) as the secondary parent (Table 10). CK/CH/HBBD/BD32 thus experienced recombination involving strains YX10, I0114/14, 4/91 and LX4 in the 1a and 1b gene. Bootscan analysis performed using Simplot software (Fig. 8) further validated the recombination events identified by RDP4 software and no recombination events were found in the S gene.

Discussion

Chicken bronchial obstruction syndrome posed a significant threat to poultry production worldwide (Kursa et al., 2022; Tekelemariam et al., 2022; Yehia et al., 2023; Al-Natour et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025). In commercial broiler production, respiratory diseases frequently arised from polymicrobial infections involving multiple pathogenic agents, thereby complicating diagnosis, treatment, and prevention. Such co-infections commonly encompass viruses, bacteria, and Chlamydia, collectively contributing to exacerbated clinical manifestations, increased morbidity, and reduced production performance. High-throughput sequencing of tracheobronchial samples from broilers exhibiting bronchial obstruction identified H9N2 AIV, IBV, Escherichia-Shigella, Pseudomonas, and Enterococcus as the five most prevalent and relatively abundant potential pathogens. These findings provide a critical empirical basis for demonstrating the synergistic pathogenesis underlying multifactorial respiratory disease in broilers and inform the development of targeted intervention strategies. In this study, analysis of bronchial obstruction syndrome samples from broiler flocks in Hebei Province revealed mixed infections of H9N2 AIV and IBV, consistent with global epidemic trends (Hassan et al., 2016; Parvin et al., 2022; Farzin et al., 2023; Al-Natour et al., 2024; Regragui et al., 2025; Xue et al., 2025). Additionally, mixed infections involving Escherichia-Shigella, Pseudomonas and Enterococcus were demonstrated. Prior research has verified that simultaneous infection of chickens with H9N2 AIV and IBV, along with coinfection by Ornithobacterium rhinotracheale and Streptococcus, leads to markedly elevated mortality in avian hosts relative to single-pathogen infections(Pan et al., 2012; Huang et al., 2024). Notably, combined infection of minks with H9N2 AIV and Pseudomonas was capable of inducing severe respiratory distress alongside distinct pathological changes (Bo-Shun et al., 2020). In an analogous manner, co-infection with Chlamydia psittaci and Enterococcus faecalis provoked severe respiratory impairment and representative pathological lesions in rats(Zhang et al., 2025). Accordingly, additional in-depth studies are required to explore and clarify the synergistic pathogenic interactions between predominant pathogens implicated in chicken bronchial obstruction.

Mixed viral infections may influence pathogen evolution. Although the H9N2 AIV was classified as LPAIV, the co-infection of this type virus with other pathogens significantly increases mortality rates, causing considerable economic losses in poultry farming (Yehia et al., 2023). Genetic evolution analysis revealed that HA gene of four H9N2 AIV isolates belonged to the Y280-like, the M genes belonged to the G1-like and PB2, PB1, PA, NP and NS gene belonged to the F/98-like. The NA gene did not cluster within any known lineage. These isolates represented quadruplex recombinants of the Eurasian lineage and differed from the previously reported G57 genotype (Chen et al., 2024).

In China, multiple AIV subtypes coexist in poultry. Simultaneous infection of one host with different viruses provides opportunities for recombination events. Such events promote the emergence of novel recombinant virus with increased transmissibility and potential pathogenicity (Song et al., 2026). The internal genes (PB2, PB1, PA, NP and M) of the four H9N2 AIV isolates clustered with several H3N3, H3N8 and H10N3 AIV strains, indicating these viruses may contribute genetic material to recombinant H9N2 strains.

Molecular characterization of the four H9N2 AIV isolates revealed mutations T163N, H191N, A198V, and Q234L in HA protein and mutation A588V in PB2 protein, enhancing receptor affinity to humans and potential aerosol transmissibility in mammals (Elgendy et al., 2021; Alkie et al., 2023). Mutations G90E/D, S145D, D153G, A168N, T200R and N201T in HA protein were associated with antigenic variation of H9N2 AIV (Li et al., 2019). Further mutations including L89V, I292V, G309D, I495V and A588V in PB2 protein, E172K in NS1 protein, K577E in PB1 protein and K356R in PA protein, detected in all four isolates, may enhance pathogenicity and virulence in mammals (Xu et al., 2016; Kamiki et al., 2018; Sood et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2024). No mutations (H274Y or R292K) conferring resistance to NA inhibitors were observed, indicating sensitivity to antiviral drugs targeting NA (Tang et al., 2019). However, all four isolates exhibited the S31N mutation in M2 protein, indicating resistance to Amantadine (Zhao et al., 2023). Additionally, deletion of a glycosylation site at position 218 and insertion at position 313 in HA protein may increase antibody binding and reduce immune escape from homologous antisera (Peng et al., 2019).

The phylogenetic analysis based on S1 gene and analysis of the S protein cleavage site indicated that the three IBV isolates belonged to GI-19 lineage (QX type), and possessed the cleavage site motif that identical to most strains from the GI-19 lineage (Chen et al., 2024). Recent epidemiological studies highlighted the ongoing prevalence of the QX type strains in China. After prolonged circulation, the QX type IBV had become a major threat to the poultry industry (Ji et al., 2020; Lian et al., 2021). The IBV serotypes are determined by three hypervariable regions (HVR1 at 38-67 aa, HVR2 at 91-141 aa and HVR3 at 274-387 aa) in S1 protein, closely related to virus-neutralizing epitopes (Moore et al., 1997). The IBV isolates investigated in this study differed from the vaccine strains commonly used in China. Nucleotide sequence homology analysis based on genome revealed that the three IBV isolates had the highest nucleotide homology (95.6%-96.1%) with YX10 strain (GI-19 lineage, QX type) but only 86.4%-86.5% with vaccine strain H120. Genetic evolution analyses indicated that S1, S2, 3a, 3b, E, M, 5a and N gene of the three IBV isolates closely matched YX10 strain (GI-19 lineage, QX type). The 1a and 1b gene closely matched to I0114/14 strain (GI-29 lineage) and the 5b gene closely matched LDT3-A strain (GI-28 lineage). These findings highlight the genetic diversity and complexity of IBV, suggesting genetic recombination among the IBV isolates. Frequent recombination events occurred in China, predominantly involving lineages GI-19, GI-13 and GI-7, potentially altering IBV pathogenicity and tissue tropism (Chen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024). Significantly, CK/CH/HBBD/BD6 and CK/CH/HBBD/BD32 emerged as novel recombinants, identified by at least five analytical methods. The CK/CH/HBBD/BD6 exhibited recombination regions of approximately 850 bp in 1a gene and 90 bp in N gene, whereas CK/CH/HBBD/BD32 showed recombination regions of approximately 850 bp in 1a gene and 700 bp in 1b gene. Recent studies indicate that specific gene fragments involved in recombination may affect viral replication, pathogenicity and immune evasion (Han et al., 2017). Recombination in IBV 1ab gene can significantly influence pathogenicity, demonstrating that even short gene-fragment recombination events can generate new IBV variants (Thor et al., 2011). The emergence of new IBV strains or lineages alters viral pathogenicity and transmission characteristics. Therefore, continuous epidemiological surveillance of IBV is necessary to promptly adjust the measures for prevention and control of poultry disease.

This study identified pathogens causing broiler bronchial obstruction syndrome in several regions of Hebei Province, China, and characterized the genetic features of the H9N2 AIV and IBV. These H9N2 AIV and IBV isolates differed genetically from vaccine strains. Even broiler flocks vaccinated against the above viruses, mixed infections involving H9N2 AIV, IBV, Escherichia-Shigella, Pseudomonas and Enterococcus may result in increased clinical severity and higher mortality rates. The specific effects of mixed infections on viral evolution depend on factors including viral properties, host immune responses and environmental conditions. Frequent co-infections of H9N2 AIV and IBV underscore the importance of investigating potential synergistic interactions. Ongoing monitoring of viral genetic sequences, combined with virological bioinformatics, is essential for informed vaccine selection, timely updates of immune procedure and accurate identification of dominant strains for vaccine development. These efforts will contribute to the optimization of vaccination strategies and ultimately enable broader cross-protection against prevalent genotypes.

Conclusion

The predominant pathogens and opportunistic pathogens included H9N2 AIV, IBV, Escherichia-Shigella, Pseudomonas and Enterococcus identified in bronchial obstruction syndrome samples from broiler flocks in Hebei Province. All four H9N2 AIV isolates belonged to the Eurasian lineage and represented quadruplex recombinants, distinct from the G57 genotype, possessed molecular markers associated with increased pathogenicity, antiviral resistance and mammalian adaptability. The S1, S2, 3a, 3b, E, M, 5a, and N gene of the three IBV isolates showed relatively closer to YX10 strain (GI-19 lineage, QX type), whereas the 1a and 1b gene were closely related to the I0114/14 strain (GI-29 lineage) and the 5b gene closely matched LDT3-A strain (GI-28 lineage). Additionally, CK/CH/HBBD/BD6 and CK/CH/HBBD/BD32 were newly identified recombinant variants.

Continuous six-functor formalism on locally compact Hausdorff spaces
Qingchong Zhu

Abstract
We show that the functor sending a locally compact Hausdorff space X X to the ∞ \infty -category of spectral sheaves Shv ⁡ ( X , Sp ) \mathrm{Shv}(X;\mathrm{Sp}) is initial among all continuous six-functor formalisms on the category of locally compact Hausdorff spaces. Here, continuous six-functor formalisms are those valued in dualizable presentable stable ∞ \infty -categories and satisfying canonical descent, profinite descent, and hyperdescent. As an application, we generalize Efimov’s computation of the algebraic K K -theory of sheaves to all localizing invariants on continuous six-functor formalisms. Our results show that localizing invariants behave analogously to compactly supported sheaf cohomology theories when evaluated on continuous six-functor formalisms on locally compact Hausdorff spaces.

Six‐functor formalism

In its classical form, a six‐functor formalism organizes the basic operations in (co)homology theories. For example, if
X
is a locally compact Hausdorff space, then the stable
\infty
‐category
\mathrm{Shv}(X;D(\mathbb{Z}))

\otimes,\ \underline{\Hom}\qquad f^{*}\dashv f_{*}\qquad f_{!}\dashv f^{!}

•

\otimes

\underline{\Hom}

\otimes

and
\mathrm{Shv}(X;D(\mathbb{Z}))^{\op}\times\mathrm{Shv}(X;D(\mathbb{Z}))\to\mathrm{Shv}(X;D(\mathbb{Z}))

•

f_{*}
and
f^{*}
(direct/inverse image): A continuous map

•

f_{!}
and
f^{!}

These six operations satisfy the usual compatibilities, i.e., composition, base change and projection formula, forming the classical Grothendieck six-functor package on topological spaces.

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
D\in\mathrm{6FF}(\mathrm{LCH})^{\mathrm{cont}}
. Then for each closed immersion

with open complement

, the sequence

is a short exact sequence in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\infty}

Proof.

Begin with the split Verdier sequence in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\infty}
(see

)

and pass to left adjoints to obtain

D(U)\xrightarrow{j_{!}}D(X)\xrightarrow{i^{*}}D(Z).

By definition, each term of this sequence is both stable and dualizable. Cofibers in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\infty}
may be computed on the underlying presentable stable
\infty
-category by
[
Efi25
, Proposition 1.65]
, and then, by passing to right adjoints, identify with fibers in the underlying stable
\infty
-categories. Moreover, the fiber of any morphism in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\infty}
is the maximal dualizable full subcategory of the underlying fiber by
[
Efi25
, Proposition 1.84]
. These observations together imply that the above sequence is exact in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\infty}
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 5.18
.

By construction, the functor is reduced, i.e., it sends the empty space to a zero object, and it preserves filtered colimits. Given open subsets

, we obtain, by

\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\infty}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(U\cap V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(U\setminus V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(U\cap V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(U\setminus V)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}

, we obtain a diagram of exact sequences in

. Since
\mathcal{E}
is stable, it follows that the image of the left square under

Lemma 5.19
.

Theorem 5.20
.

Let
F\in\Loc^{\mathrm{cont}}_{\omega}(\mathrm{Sp})
be a finitary continuous localizing invariant, and
D\in\mathrm{6FF}(\mathrm{LCH})^{\mathrm{cont}}
. Then for any locally compact Hausdorff space
X
, there is an equivalence

F(D(X))\simeq\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}\Big(X,\underline{F(D(\mathrm{pt}))}\Big).

Proof.

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space. Define the functor
\mathcal{F}:\Open(X)\to\mathrm{Sp}
by

U\mapsto F(D(U)).

by
Lemma

5.18
, this defines a cosheaf of spectra on
X
. Applying Verdier duality (
Theorem

2.16
),

\mathbb{D}:\mathrm{CoShv}(X;\mathrm{Sp})\simeq\mathrm{Shv}(X;\mathrm{Sp}),

we obtain a sheaf
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})
. For any compact subset
K\subseteq X
, Verdier duality gives

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})(K)=\mathcal{F}(X)/\mathcal{F}(X\setminus K).

where the value of a sheaf on a subset denotes the global sections of its restriction to that subset. To describe
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})(K)
, consider the short exact sequence

D(U)\xrightarrow{j_{!}}D(X)\xrightarrow{i^{*}}D(Z)

in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{\infty}
by
Lemma

5.17
in which
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
is a closed immersion with open complement
j:U\hookrightarrow X
. Applying the continuous localizing invariant
F
to the above short exact sequence, we have an exact sequence in
\mathrm{Sp}

\mathcal{F}(U)\to\mathcal{F}(X)\to\mathcal{F}(Z)

Taking
U=X\setminus K
, it follows that

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})(K)=\mathcal{F}(X)/\mathcal{F}(X\setminus K)\simeq\mathcal{F}(K).

Since
K
is compact, the unique map
\pi_{K}:K\to\mathrm{pt}
is proper and
\pi_{K}^{*}:D(\mathrm{pt})\to D(K)
is strongly continuous, which gives a functor

\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{pt})\to\mathcal{F}(K)

adjoins over to a morphism
\underline{\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{pt})}\to\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})|_{K}
. As
X
is locally compact, these local morphisms glue into a morphism of sheaves
\underline{\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{pt})}\to\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})
.

The remainder of the proof follows the same argument as in the proof of
Lemma

4.16
. We reproduce it here for completeness. To complete the proof, we must show that the morphism
\underline{\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{pt})}\to\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})
is an equivalence. Indeed, under Verdier duality, a sheaf
\mathcal{G}
corresponds to the cosheaf
\mathbb{D}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})
whose value on
U
is the compactly supported sections of
\mathcal{G}
on
U
, which then gives the desired description of
\mathcal{F}
.

It remains to verify that
\underline{\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{pt})}\to\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})
is an equivalence. As sheaves on locally compact spaces are determined by their values on compact subsets, and constant sheaves are stable under pullback, it suffices to check the equivalence when
X
is compact. Moreover, if the equivalence holds on a compact space, it holds on each of its closed subspaces. Now every compact Hausdorff space embeds into a Hilbert cube
[0,1]^{I}
for some set
I
, via the canonical double dual map
X\to[0,1]^{I}
, where
I=C^{0}(X,[0,1])
. This map is injective by Urysohn’s Lemma and defines an embedding. Thus, it suffices to prove the claim for
[0,1]^{I}
.

The Hilbert cube is an inverse limit of finite-dimensional cubes
[0,1]^{n}
. By
Proposition

2.10
together with
Lemma

5.19
, it is enough to verify the claim for each
[0,1]^{n}
. These spaces are hypercomplete, the equivalence of sheaves can be checked on stalks. Since both
\underline{\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{pt})}
and
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})
have stalks equivalent to
\underline{\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{pt})}
, the morphism is indeed an equivalence.
∎

Corollary 5.26

For any locally compact Hausdorff space
X
such that the unit

is compact in

\mathrm{Shv}(-;D(\mathrm{pt}))\to D

Proof.

The right adjoint of the canonical morphism
\mathrm{Shv}(-;D(\mathrm{pt}))\to D
is given by a functor

which assigns to each object

\big(U\xhookrightarrow{i}X\big)\,\mapsto\,\underline{\Hom}_{D(X)}(i_{!}i^{*}1_{D(X)},d).

By the projection formula, we have

\underline{\Hom}_{D(X)}(i_{!}i^{*}1_{D(X)},d)\,\simeq\,\underline{\Hom}_{D(X)}(1_{D(X)},i_{*}i^{*}d)\,\simeq\,\underline{\Hom}_{D(X)}(1_{D(X)},d\otimes i_{*}1_{D(U)}).

Since

is compact the right adjoint preserves filtered colimits and hence all colimits. It follows that the canonical morphism
\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{pt}))\to D

6
Outlook

In the proof of the universal property of sheaves (
Theorem

4.18
), we apply profinite descent only to cofiltered limits of intervals
[0,1]^{n}
, and hyperdescent is used only for these spaces. Consequently, the universal property of sheaves extends to a slightly larger
\infty
-category of six-functor formalisms.

By contrast, in motivic homotopy theory,
[
Dre18
, Proposition 5.21]
shows that morphisms between coefficient systems are automatically compatible with the right adjoint
f_{*}
for proper maps
f
, without requiring extra assumptions. Therefore, the universal property of the initial object in the
\infty
-category of coefficient systems lifts directly to the
\infty
-category of Nagata six-functor formalisms. This naturally leads to the following question:

Question 6.1
.

Is
\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})
initial among all Nagata six-functor formalisms on locally compact Hausdorff spaces that satisfy only the localization property?

Once the universal property of sheaves has been established, it follows that for any Nagata six-functor formalism
D
, the canonical morphism
\mathrm{Shv}(-;D(\mathrm{pt}))\to D
is a cohomological equivalence. This raises the subsequent question:

Question 6.2
.

Is the canonical map
\mathrm{Shv}(-;D(\mathrm{pt}))\to D
an equivalence? If not, do there exist nontrivial Nagata six-functor formalisms distinct from the sheaf, and how can one classify all Nagata six-functor formalisms?
